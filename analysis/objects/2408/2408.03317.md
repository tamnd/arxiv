<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label {L:dist to pisom}
Suppose that $P$ and $Q$ are orthogonal projections such that $\|P-Q\| = \sin\theta < 1$ for $0 \le \theta < \frac\pi2$.
Let $U$ be the partial isometry in the polar decomposition of $QP$.
Then $\|U-P\| = 2 \sin\frac\theta2 < \sqrt2$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
If $P \wedge Q = R \ne 0$, we can replace $P$ and $Q$ by $P-R$ and $Q-R$.
This does not affect the problem unless $P=Q$, which is a trivial case.
Let $P' = (P \vee Q) - P$. Then $P'QP$ is injective on $PH$ and has range dense in $P'H$.
There is no loss in assuming that $P+P'=I$.

Let $C = (PQP)^{1/2}$ and $S = (1-C^2)^{1/2}$.
The Halmos model \cite{Halmos} for two subspaces in generic position (i.e., $P\wedge Q = 0$ and $P \vee Q = I$) 
shows that $P$, $Q$ and $U$ are unitarily equivalent to
\[
 P = \begin{bmatrix}I&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}
 \quad
 Q = \begin{bmatrix}C^2&CS\\CS&S^2\end{bmatrix} 
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 U = \begin{bmatrix}C&0\\ S&0\end{bmatrix} .
\]
Then since the two columns of $P-Q$ have orthogonal ranges,
\[
 \| P - Q \| = \Big\| \begin{bmatrix}S^2&-CS\\-CS&-S^2\end{bmatrix} \Big\| = \| S^2 ( S^2 +C^2) \|^{1/2} = \|S\| .
\]
So $\|S\| = \sin\theta$. Thus $C = (1-S^2)^{1/2} \ge \cos\theta I$ and $\| I - C \| = 1-\cos\theta$.
Therefore
\[
 \|U-P\| = \Big\| \begin{bmatrix}C-I&0 \\ S&0\end{bmatrix} \Big\| = \| (C-I)^2 + S^2 \|^{1/2} = \sqrt{\| 2(I-C)\|} .
\]
Hence $\|U-P\| = \sqrt{2(1-\cos\theta)} = 2 \sin \frac\theta2$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label {T:two pairs proj}
Let $P_1$ and $P_2$ $($respectively $Q_1$ and $Q_2)$ be pairwise orthogonal projections on a separable Hilbert space.
Suppose that 
\[
 \| P_1 - Q_1 \|<1 \quad\text{and}\quad  \| P_2 - Q_2 \| < 1 . 
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{rank} (P_1+P_2)^\perp = \operatorname{rank} (Q_1+Q_2)^\perp .
\]
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We may suppose that $ \operatorname{rank} (P_1+P_2)^\perp \le \operatorname{rank} (Q_1+Q_2)^\perp$.
If both are infinite, there is nothing to prove.
Thus we may assume that $ \operatorname{rank} (P_1+P_2)^\perp$ is finite.
Form the polar decomposition 
\[
 Q_iP_i = U_i (P_iQ_iP_i)^{1/2} \quad\text{for}\   i=1,2 .
\]
Then $U = U_1+U_2$ is a partial isometry of $(P_1+P_2)H$ onto $(Q_1+Q_2)H$.
Thus $U$ is left semi-Fredholm since it has finite nullity and closed range.
However by the lemma,
\begin{align*}
 \| U - (P_1+P_2) \|^2 &= \big\| \big[ (U_1-P_1)P_1\ \ \ (U_2-P_2)P_2  \big]  \big\|^2 \\&
 \le \|U_1-P_1\|^2  + \|U_2-P_2\|^2 < 4 
\end{align*}
because the two terms in the $1 \times 2 $ matrix have orthogonal domains.
If $\operatorname{rank} (Q_1+Q_2)^\perp = \infty$, then $\pi(U)$ would be a proper isometry, and thus $\sigma_e(U) = \overline{{\mathbb{D}}}$ is the closed unit disc.
If $\operatorname{rank} (Q_1+Q_2)^\perp < \infty$, then $\pi(U)$ is unitary.
If also $\operatorname{ind} U \ne 0$, then $\sigma_e(U) = \overline{{\mathbb{T}}}$ is the unit circle.
In either case,
\[
 \|U-(P_1+P_2)\|_e = \|\pi(U - I) \| = 2 .
\]
Hence $\operatorname{ind} U = 0$, which means  $\operatorname{rank} (P_1+P_2)^\perp = \operatorname{rank} (Q_1+Q_2)^\perp$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- cor-1: body_md -->
\label {C:closest proj}
If $P$, $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ are projections with $Q_1 < Q_2$, then 
\[
\max\{ \|P - Q_i \| : i=1,2 \} = 1 .
\]
<!-- end cor-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
If this result were false, we could have $\|P - Q_i \| < 1$ for $i=1,2$.
Using $P$, $P^\perp$, $Q_1$ and $Q_2^\perp$ in Theorem~\ref{T:two pairs proj}, we would obtain
\[
 0 = \operatorname{rank}(P+P^\perp)^\perp = \operatorname{rank}(Q_1+Q_2^\perp)^\perp = \operatorname{rank} (Q_2 - Q_1) > 0.
\]
This contradiction establishes the result.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label {P:dist betw projs}
Let $P$ and $Q$ be projections on a Hilbert space ${\mathcal{H}}$.
Then 
\[
 \| P - Q \| = \max \big\{ \|PQ^\perp\|, \| P^\perp Q \| \big\} .
\]
Moreover if $\|P-Q\|<1$, then $\|PQ^\perp\| = \| P^\perp Q \|$.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
First of all, $P-Q = PQ^\perp - P^\perp Q$ is the difference of two operators with pairwise orthogonal domains and ranges.
Thus 
\[
 \| P - Q \| = \max \big\{ \|PQ^\perp\|, \| P^\perp Q \| \big\} .
\]

We make use of the general form for a pair of projections \cite{Halmos}. 
There is a canonical decomposition ${\mathcal{H}} = {\mathcal{H}}_{00} \oplus {\mathcal{H}}_{10} \oplus {\mathcal{H}}_{01} \oplus {\mathcal{H}}_{11} \oplus {\mathcal{H}}_2 \oplus {\mathcal{H}}_2$
and positive injective operators $C,S \in {\mathcal{B}}(H_2)$ such that $C^2+S^2 = I$ and
\[
 P = 0 \oplus I \oplus 0 \oplus I \oplus \begin{bmatrix} I & 0 \\0 & 0\end{bmatrix} 
 \ \text{ and } \ 
 Q = 0 \oplus 0 \oplus I \oplus I \oplus \begin{bmatrix} C^2 & CS \\CS & S^2\end{bmatrix} .
\]
Any of the spaces ${\mathcal{H}}_{ij}$ and ${\mathcal{H}}_2$ can be vacuous.
It follows that 
\[
 P Q^\perp = 0 \oplus I \oplus 0 \oplus 0 \oplus \begin{bmatrix} S^2 & -CS \\0 & 0\end{bmatrix} 
 \ \text{ and } \ 
 P^\perp Q = 0 \oplus 0 \oplus I \oplus 0 \oplus \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\CS & S^2\end{bmatrix} .
\]
Now 
\[
 \left\| \begin{bmatrix} S^2 & -CS \\0 & 0\end{bmatrix} \right\| =
 \left\| \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\CS & S^2\end{bmatrix} \right\| =
 \| S^2 ( S^2+C^2) \|^{1/2} = \|S\|. 
\]

If $\| P - Q \| < 1$, then ${\mathcal{H}}_{10}$ and ${\mathcal{H}}_{01}$ must be vacuous.
Hence 
\[
 \|PQ^\perp\| = \| P^\perp Q \| = \|S\| .
\]
However if either ${\mathcal{H}}_{10}$ or ${\mathcal{H}}_{01}$ is non-trivial, then $\|PQ^\perp\| = 1$ or $\|PQ^\perp\| = 1$, respectively.
If say ${\mathcal{H}}_{10} \ne \{0\}$ and ${\mathcal{H}}_{01} = \{0\}$, then $\|PQ^\perp\|=1$ and $\|P^\perp Q\| = \|S\|$.
So if $\|S\|<1$, the two norms differ. It is similar if ${\mathcal{H}}_{10} = \{0\}$ and ${\mathcal{H}}_{01} \ne \{0\}$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label {T:close nests}
Suppose that ${\mathcal{M}}$ and ${\mathcal{N}}$ are two nests on ${\mathcal{H}}$ such that $d({\mathcal{M}}, {\mathcal{N}}) = \gamma < 1$.
Then there is a unique order isomorphism $\theta : {\mathcal{M}} \to {\mathcal{N}}$ such that $\| \theta - {\operatorname{id}} \| = \gamma$.
Moreover $\theta$ preserves dimension.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For each $M\in {\mathcal{M}}$, there is an $N \in {\mathcal{N}}$ with $\| P_M - P_N \| \le \gamma$.
By Corollary~\ref{C:closest proj}, the subspace $N$ is unique.
Define $\theta(M) = N$.
Reversing the roles of ${\mathcal{M}}$ and ${\mathcal{N}}$ shows that $\theta$ is a bijection.
It is evident that this map must preserve order, so $\theta$ is an order isomorphism.

Suppose that $M_1 < M_2$, $P_i := P_{M_i}$ and $Q_i = P_{\theta(M_i)}$ for $i=1,2$.
Then $\|P_1 - Q_1\| < 1$, $\| P_2^\perp - Q_2^\perp\| = \|P_2 - Q_2 \| < 1$, $P_1$ and $P_2^\perp$ are orthogonal 
and $Q_1 < Q_2$, so that $Q_1$ and $Q_2^\perp$ are orthogonal.
Hence by Theorem~\ref{T:two pairs proj}, 
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{rank}(P_2-P_1) &= \operatorname{rank} (P_1+P_2^\perp)^\perp \\& = \operatorname{rank} (Q_1+Q_2^\perp)^\perp = \operatorname{rank}(Q_2-Q_1) . 
\end{align*}
This means that $\theta$ preserves dimension.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- cor-2: body_md -->
Suppose that $\theta : {\mathcal{M}} \to {\mathcal{N}}$ is an order isomorphism of two nests on separable Hilbert space.
If 
\[
 \|\theta-{\operatorname{id}}\| := \sup_{M\in{\mathcal{M}}} \| P_{\theta(M)} - P_M \| = \gamma < 1 ,
\]
then there is an invertible operator $S$ 
such that $SM = \theta(M)$ for all $M \in {\mathcal{M}}$.
If $\gamma < \frac12$, one can take $\|S-I\| < 2\gamma$.
If $\gamma \ge \frac12$, one can take $\|S-I\| < 2 + \varepsilon$ for any $\varepsilon>0$.
<!-- end cor-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{T:close nests}, $\theta$ preserves dimension.
Hence by the Similarity theorem \cite{Dav_sim}, $\theta$ is implemented by a similarity. 
(See the introduction for some discussion of this.)
When $\gamma < \frac12$, \cite{DavNestAlgs}*{Lemma 18.3} shows that one can choose $S$ so that $\| S - I \| < 2 \gamma$.
For $\gamma \ge \frac12$, one can take $S = U+K$ with $U$ unitary and $\|K\| < \varepsilon < \frac12$.
Hence 
\[
 \|S-I \| \le \| U \| + \| K \| + \| I \|  < 2 + \varepsilon < 5 \gamma . \qedhere
\]

<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label {L:lower bound}
Let ${\mathcal{M}}, {\mathcal{N}}$ be nests on ${\mathcal{H}}$. For any $M\in{\mathcal{M}}$ and $N\in{\mathcal{N}}$,
\[
 d({\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}}), {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) \ge \| P_M^\perp P_{N_+} \| \, \|P_M P_N^\perp \| .
\]
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $\varepsilon > 0$.
We may choose a unit vector $\eta \in N^\perp$ so that $\|P_M \eta \| > \|P_M P_N^\perp \| - \varepsilon$.
Likewise choose a unit vector $\zeta \in N_+$ so that $\| P_M^\perp \zeta \| > \| P_M^\perp P_{N_+} \| - \varepsilon$.
The rank one operator $\zeta \eta^*$ lies in ${\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})$ and has norm 1.
Therefore
\begin{align*}
 d({\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}}), {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) &\ge d( \zeta \eta^*, {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}})) \ge \| P_M^\perp \zeta \eta^* P_M \| \\&
 = \| P_M^\perp \zeta \| \,  \|P_M \eta \| > \| P_M^\perp P_{N_+} \| \, \|P_M P_N^\perp \| - 2\varepsilon. 
\end{align*}
As $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary, the result follows.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- cor-3: body_md -->
\label {C:lower bound}
Let ${\mathcal{M}}, {\mathcal{N}}$ be nests on ${\mathcal{H}}$. For any $M\in{\mathcal{M}}$ and $N\in{\mathcal{N}}$,
\[
 d({\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}}), {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) \ge \| P_M^\perp P_N \| \, \|P_M P_N^\perp \| .
\]
<!-- end cor-3 -->
<!-- thm-4: body_md -->
\label {T:dist algs}
Let ${\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{N}}$ be nests on separable Hilbert space ${\mathcal{H}}$.
If 
\[
 d({\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}}), {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) < 1 ,
\]
then $d({\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{N}}) < 1$; and so ${\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}})$ and ${\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})$ are similar.
<!-- end thm-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
For the contrapositive, we suppose that $d({\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{N}}) = 1$ and show that $d({\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}}), {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) = 1$.

There are two cases to consider. In the first case, there are projections $M_i\in{\mathcal{M}}$ and ${\mathcal{N}}_i \in {\mathcal{N}}$
such that $\| P_{M_i} - P_{N_i} \| = t_i < 1$ for $i\ge1$ with $\sup t_i = 1$.
In this case, Proposition~\ref{P:dist betw projs} shows that $\| P_{M_i}^\perp P_{N_i} \| = \| P_{M_i} P_{N_i}^\perp \| = t_i$.
Thus by Corollary~\ref{C:lower bound},
\[
 1 \ge d({\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}}), {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) \ge \sup_{i\ge 1} \| P_{M_i}^\perp P_{N_i} \| \, \| P_{M_i} P_{N_i}^\perp \| = \sup_{i\ge1} t_i^2 = 1.
\]

In the second case, there is a projection in one nest at distance 1 from the other.
Interchanging the two nests if required, we may suppose that there is $M \in {\mathcal{M}}$ such that $\| P_M - P_N \| = 1$ for all $N\in {\mathcal{N}}$.
Let 
\[
 \delta = \sup_{N\in {\mathcal{N}}} \min \big\{ \| P_M^\perp P_N \| , \|P_M P_N^\perp \| \big\} .
\]
Again Corollary~\ref{C:lower bound} and Proposition~\ref{P:dist betw projs} show that $d({\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}}), {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) \ge \delta$.
Hence if $\delta = 1$, we are done.

So we assume that $\delta < 1$.
If $\| P_M^\perp P_N \| \le \delta$, the same holds for all $N'<N$ in ${\mathcal{N}}$.
Define $N_0 = \bigvee \{ N : \| P_M^\perp P_N \| \le \delta \}$.
There are $N_i$ increasing to $N_0$ with $\| P_M^\perp P_{N_i} \| \le \delta$.
Since $P_{N_i}$ converges in the strong operator topology to $P_{N_0}$, we have $\| P_M^\perp P_{N_0} \| \le \delta$.
That is, $N_0$ is the largest element of ${\mathcal{N}}$ with this property. Hence $\|P_M P_{N_0}^\perp \| = 1$.

If $N>N_0$, then $\| P_M^\perp P_N \| = 1$, and thus $ \|P_M P_N^\perp \| \le \delta$.
Recall that $N_{0+} = \bigwedge \{ N \in {\mathcal{N}} : N > N_0 \}$.
Arguing as above, we obtain $ \|P_M P_{N_{0+}}^\perp \| \le \delta$.
Thus $\| P_M^\perp P_{N_{0+}} \| = 1$. This shows that $P_{N_{0+}} > P_{N_0}$.
Now an application of Lemma~\ref{L:lower bound} shows that 
\[
 1 \ge d({\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}}), {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) \ge \| P_M^\perp P_{N_{0+}} \| \, \|P_M P_{N_0}^\perp \| = 1 . \qedhere
\]
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- ex-1: body_md -->
Let $\frac1{\sqrt2} \le s < 1$ and set $c = \sqrt{1-s^2}$. Let ${\mathcal{H}} = {\mathbb{C}}^2$.  
Let $M= {\mathbb{C}} \begin{sbmatrix} 1\\0 \end{sbmatrix}$ and $N = {\mathbb{C}} \begin{sbmatrix} c\\s \end{sbmatrix}$
and define nests ${\mathcal{M}} = \big\{ \{0\}, M , {\mathcal{H}} \big\}$ and ${\mathcal{N}} = \big\{ \{0\}, N , {\mathcal{H}} \big\}$.
Then ${\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}})$ is the algebra of $2\times2$ upper triangular matrices in the standard basis.
Using the calculation in Lemma~\ref {L:dist to pisom}, 
\[
 d({\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{N}}) = \| P_M - P_N \| = s < 1 .
\]

For $0 \le a \le 1$, consider $T = \begin{bmatrix} a & 1-a^2 \\ 0 & -a \end{bmatrix}$ in ${\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}})$.
One sees that
\[
 \|T\| = \left\| \begin{bmatrix} a & 1-a^2 \\ 0 & a \end{bmatrix} \right\|
 = \left\| \begin{bmatrix} 0 & a \\ a & 1-a^2 \end{bmatrix} \right\| = 1
\]
because the last matrix, which is self-adjoint, has characteristic polynomial $\lambda^2 - (1-a^2) \lambda - a^2 = (\lambda - 1)(\lambda + a^2)$.
We compute 
\begin{align*}
 d(T, {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) &= \| P_N^\perp T P_N \| = \big| \big\langle  T \begin{sbmatrix} c\\s \end{sbmatrix}, \begin{sbmatrix} s\\ -c \end{sbmatrix}  \big\rangle \big|     
 = 2acs + (1-a^2) s^2 .
\end{align*}
A bit of calculus shows that we should choose $a = \frac c s$, which yields
\[
 d(T, {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) = 2c^2 + s^2 - c^2 = c^2 + s^2 = 1.
\]
Therefore $d({\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}}), {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) = 1$.

<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
In the example above, when $0 < s < \frac1{\sqrt2}$, we can choose $a=1$ to see that $d({\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}}), {\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{N}})) \ge 2cs$.
For very small $s$, this shows that the constant 2 in Lance's estimate cannot be improved.
