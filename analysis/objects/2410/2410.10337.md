<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a simple undirected graph with minimal degree at least two.  
    For two directed edges $e,f$, we say that one can transition from $e$ to $f$ if $h(e)=t(f)$ and $e' \neq f$ and denote this relation by $e \rightarrow f$.
    The Non-Backtracking Random-Walk (NBRW) on $G$ is a Markov chain on the state space $\vec{E}$ with the transition matrix $\Pi_G$ defined by:
    \begin{equation*}
        \pi_G(e,f) = \begin{cases}
             \frac{1}{\operatorname{outdeg}(e)}, & \mbox{ if }e \rightarrow f\\
             0,                    & \mbox{otherwise}
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation*}
    We regard the state $e=(u,v)$ as being at $v$ coming from $u$. 
    Let $B$ denote the non-backtracking adjacency matrix, which is an $\vec{E}$ by $\vec{E}$ 0-1 matrix where $B_{e,f}=1$ if $e \rightarrow f$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The definition generalizes to allow for multiple edges and self loops in $G$, where a self loop can be either a whole-loop or a half-loop. 
    Each edge, except for the half-loops, gives rise to two edges in $\vec{E}$ each being the inverse of the other.  
    A half-loop in $G$ gives rise to a single edge in $\vec{E}$, which is its own inverse.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- claim-1: body_md -->
For any graph $G=(V,E)$ with minimal degree at least two, 
    the uniform edge distribution $\nu_s$ defined by $\nu_s(e) = 1/|\vec{E}|$ for all $e \in \vec{E}$ is stationary for the Markov chain $\Pi_G$.
<!-- end claim-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
\begin{eqnarray*}
        (\nu_s \Pi_G)_f 
            &=& \sum_{e \in \vec{E}} \nu_s(e) \pi_G(e,f) = \frac{1}{|\vec{E}|} \sum_{e \in \vec{E}} \pi_G(e,f)\\
            &=& \frac{1}{|\vec{E}|} \sum_{e: e \rightarrow f} \frac{1}{\operatorname{outdeg}(e)} = \frac{1}{|\vec{E}|} = \nu_s(f).
    \end{eqnarray*}
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- claim-2: body_md -->
\label{claim:glover2021non}
    The NBRW Markov chain on $G$ as well as the non-backtracking adjacency matrix $B$ are irreducible iff $G$ is connected with $\operatorname{mindeg}(G) \geq 2$ and $\operatorname{maxdeg}(G) > 2$.
<!-- end claim-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
To prove that the condition on $G$ is required, assume that the NBRW on $G$ is irreducible. 
    The condition $\operatorname{mindeg}(G) \geq 2$ must hold since the walk is well defined. 
    In addition, $G$ must be connected, because otherwise one can find edges $e, f \in \vec{E}$ with no non-backtracking path from $e$ to $f$. 
    It remains to exclude the possibility that $G$ is connected with $\operatorname{mindeg}(G) = \operatorname{maxdeg}(G) = 2$, namely that $G$ is a cycle. 
    Indeed, in this case, for any $e \in \vec{E}$, there is no non-backtracking path from $e$ to $e'$.
    
    To prove that the condition on $G$ implies irreducibility, 
    we first argue that it suffices to find a path $P_{h,h'}$ from some edge $h$ to its inverse $h'$ to imply irreducibility.
    Indeed, given any pair of edges $e,f \in \vec{E}$, because $G$ is connected 
    there is a non-backtracking path $P_1$ connecting $e$ to either $h$ or $h'$ and 
    there is a non-backtracking path $P_2$ connecting $h$ or $h'$ to $f$. 
    Therefore, there is a non-backtracking path from $e$ to $f$ formed by concatenating $P_1$, possibly $P_{h,h'}$ or its inverse, and $P_2$.

    To conclude, observe that the condition $\operatorname{mindeg}(G) \geq 2$ implies the existence of some non-backtracking cycle $C = e_1, e_2, \ldots, e_\ell$. 
    As the graph is connected with $\operatorname{maxdeg}(G) > 2$, there must be a vertex $v \in C$ with $\deg(v)>2$,
    and we may assume that $t(e_1)=h(e_\ell)=v$, as depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:vertex_deg3}.
    \begin{figure}[h]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.3\textwidth,clip,trim=1mm 1mm 1mm 1mm]{Figure2_deg3-vertex_d.png}
        \caption{A cycle with a vertex of degree more than two.}
        \label{fig:vertex_deg3}
    \end{figure}
    Since $\deg(v)>2$ there is some edge $e$ with $h(e)=v$ distinct from $e_1'$ and $e_\ell$. Then the path $e,e_1, e_2, \ldots, e_\ell,e'$ is a path from $e$ to $e'$, implying the Markov chain irreducibility.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Given a finite connected graph $G$ with minimal degree at least 2, let:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{G},r}(v)$ denote the radius $r$ ball around the vertex $v$ in $\tilde{G}$, 
        \item $\Omega_{e,\ell}$ be the set of length $\ell$ non-backtracking walks in $G$ starting from $e \in \vec{E}$,
        \item $\Omega_\ell = \cup_{e \in \vec{E}} \Omega_{e,\ell}$ be the set of all length $\ell$ non-backtracking walks in $G$.
    \end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemma:rho_Perron}
    Given an NB-irreducible graph $G$, the following definitions for $\rho(G)$ are equivalent:        
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \rho(G) 
            &=& \lim_{r \rightarrow \infty} |\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{G},r}(v)|^{1/r} 
            =   \lim_{\ell \rightarrow \infty} |\Omega_{e,\ell}|^{1/\ell}
            =   \lim_{\ell  \rightarrow \infty} |\Omega_\ell|^{1/\ell} \\
            &=& \lim_{\ell \rightarrow \infty} \left( \nu_s \ B^\ell \ \overline{1} \right)^{1/\ell}
            = \rho(B),
    \end{eqnarray*}
    where $B$ denotes the non-backtracking adjacency matrix, $\rho(B)$ is the largest (Perron) eigenvalue of $B$, and $\overline{1}$ is the all-ones vector.
    Equality holds regardless of the choice of $v \in V(\tilde{G})$ and $e \in \vec{E}$, where applicable.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{proposition:Gelfand}
    Let $\| \cdot \|$ be a matrix norm on $M_n$ and let $A\in M_n$.
    Then $\rho(A)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|A^k\|^{1/k}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
The average growth rate $\Lambda(G)$ is the rate predicted by the stationary distribution $\nu_s$ of the NBRW:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambda}
    \Lambda(G) = \Bigl( \prod_{e \in \vec{E}} \operatorname{outdeg}(e) \Bigr)^{1/{|\vec{E}|}} = \prod_{v \in V(G)} \Bigl( \deg(v)-1 \Bigr)^{\deg(v)/{|\vec{E}|}}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lemma:Xbeta_lb}
    \begin{equation*}
        \mathbb{E}_{\Omega_\ell}[X_\beta] \geq \left( \prod_{e \in \vec{E}} \beta(e) \right)^{\frac{\ell}{|\vec{E}|}}
    \end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
\begin{eqnarray*}
        \mathbb{E}_{\Omega_\ell}[X_\beta] 
        &=& \sum_{\omega \in \Omega_\ell} \mu[\omega] X_\beta(\omega) 
        \geq \exp\left( \mathbb{E}_{\Omega_\ell}[\log X_\beta]\right)\\
                                                          
        &=& \exp\left( \frac{\ell}{|\vec{E}|} \sum_{e \in \vec{E}} \log \beta(e)\right)
        = \left( \prod_{e \in \vec{E}} \beta(e) \right)^\frac{\ell}{|\vec{E}|},        
    \end{eqnarray*}
    where the inequality is Jensen's inequality, and the equality before the last follows by linearity of expectation and the fact that the marginal distribution of $e_i$ is $\nu_s$ for all $i$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma:ModifiedPiPerronLowerBound}
    For an NB-irreducible graph $G$, define the modified NBRW Markov chain $\Pi_\beta$ by:
    \begin{equation*}
        (\Pi_\beta)_{e,f} = (\Pi_G)_{e,f} \cdot \beta(e).
    \end{equation*}
    Then the Perron eigenvalue of $\Pi_\beta$ satisfies:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:ModifiedPiPerronLowerBound}
        \rho(\Pi_\beta) 
        =    \lim_{\ell \rightarrow \infty} \left( \mathbb{E}_{\Omega_\ell}[X_\beta] \right)^\frac{1}{\ell} 
        \geq \left( \prod_{e \in \vec{E}} \beta(e) \right)^\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}.
    \end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
\begin{eqnarray*}
         \nu_s \left( \Pi_\beta \right)^{\ell} \overline{1} 
         &=& \sum_{\omega = (e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_\ell) \in \Omega_\ell} \mu[\omega] \ \prod_{i=0}^{\ell-1} \beta(e_i) 
         = \mathbb{E}_{\Omega_\ell}[X_\beta].
    \end{eqnarray*}
    Therefore by Proposition~\ref{proposition:Gelfand},
    \begin{equation*}
         \rho(\Pi_\beta) 
         = \lim_{\ell \rightarrow \infty} \left( \nu_s \ ( \Pi_\beta )^\ell \ \overline{1} \right)^{1/\ell}
         =   \lim_{\ell \rightarrow \infty} \mathbb{E}_{\Omega_\ell}[X_\beta]^{1/\ell}.
    \end{equation*}
    The result follows by applying Lemma~\ref{lemma:Xbeta_lb} to lower bound the right hand side.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:rho_ge_lambda}
     $\rho \geq \Lambda$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:Rl}
    $\Lambda = 2^{\mathbb{E}[R_{\ell}/\ell]}$ and $\log_2 \rho(B) = \lim_{\ell \rightarrow \infty} \frac{1}{\ell} \log_2 \mathbb{E}[2^{R_\ell}]$.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By definition, $R_\ell = \log_2(X_\beta)$ with $\beta(e) = \operatorname{outdeg}(e)$. 
    Therefore, by the last two equalities in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lemma:Xbeta_lb}, we have $\mathbb{E}[R_\ell] = \mathbb{E}[\log_2 X_\beta] = \ell \log_2 \Lambda$, which is the first equality.
    
    For the second equality, we use Lemma~\ref{lemma:ModifiedPiPerronLowerBound} with $\beta(e) = \operatorname{outdeg}(e)$, 
    yielding that $\log_2 \rho(B) = \log_2 \lim_{\ell \rightarrow \infty} \left( \mathbb{E}[X_\beta] \right)^\frac{1}{\ell} = \lim_{\ell \rightarrow \infty} \frac{1}{\ell} \log_2 \mathbb{E}[2^{R_\ell}]$.

\label{lemma:bounded}

    $0<c_1\le c_2$, depending only on $G$, such that

    \qquad\text{for all $\ell\ge 1$ and $\omega\in\Omega_\ell$.}
    \end{equation}

    $|R_\ell(\omega)-\ell\log_2\Lambda(G)|\le\max(\log_2 c_2,-\log_2 c_1)$ for all

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lemma:suspended_implies_rho_eq_Lambda}
    If the NB-irreducible graph $G$ satisfies the suspended path condition, then
    \eqref{eq:bounded} holds, and in particular $\rho(G) = \Lambda(G)$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Given a graph $G$ satisfying the suspended path condition and some non-backtracking walk $\omega = (e_0,\ldots,e_\ell)\in \Omega_\ell$, 
    let $i_1 < \cdots < i_s$ be the set of indices $i<\ell$ with $\operatorname{outdeg}(e_i) > 1$.
    If $s>0$, then $\omega$ can be expressed as the concatenation $P_0 P_1 \cdots P_s$, where $P_j = (e_{i_j+1},e_{i_j+2},\ldots,e_{i_{j+1}})$ for $j=1,\ldots,s-1$ are suspended paths, 
    $P_0 = (e_0,\ldots,e_{i_1})$ and $P_s = (e_{i_s+1},\ldots,e_\ell)$ are partial suspended paths.
    Then:
    \begin{eqnarray}
        F(\omega) 
        &=& \prod_{i=0}^{\ell-1} \operatorname{outdeg}(e_i) 
         =  \prod_{j=0}^{s-1} \operatorname{outdeg}(P_j)                                \nonumber \\
        &=& \operatorname{outdeg}(P_0)^\frac{1}{2} \left[ \prod_{j=1}^{s-1} \operatorname{indeg}(P_j)^\frac{1}{2} \operatorname{outdeg}(P_j)^\frac{1}{2} \right] \operatorname{indeg}(P_s)^\frac{1}{2}                               \nonumber \\
        &=& \operatorname{outdeg}(P_0)^\frac{1}{2} \operatorname{indeg}(P_s)^\frac{1}{2} \cdot \Lambda(G)^{\ell+1-|P_0|-|P_s|}
        = \Theta(1) \cdot \Lambda(G)^{\ell},                              \label{eq:outdegree_prod_approx}
    \end{eqnarray}
    where the third equality uses the fact that $\operatorname{outdeg}(P_j) = \operatorname{indeg}(P_{j+1})$, 
    the fourth equality uses the suspended path condition, 
    and where $\Theta(1)$ denotes a factor that is upper and lower bounded by positive constants that depend on $G$ but not on $\ell$ or $\omega$.
    
    Otherwise, $s=0$ and $F(\omega)=1$. As $\ell$ is bounded by the maximal length of a suspended path, it follows that $F(\omega) = \Theta(1) \cdot \Lambda(G)^{\ell}$.
    
    In both cases we get $\rho(G) = \Lambda(G)$ by Lemma~\ref{lemma:bounded}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{theorem:Nussbaum}
    For $0 \leq t \leq 1$, assume that $M_t$ is an irreducible non-negative matrix such that $(M_t)_{i,j} = (U_{i,j})^{1-t} (V_{i,j})^t$ for all $i,j$.
    Then:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:Nussbaum_logconvexity}
        \rho(M_t) \leq \rho(U)^{1-t} \rho(V)^t,
    \end{equation}
    where equality holds for some $0 < t <1$ 
    if and only if there exists a positive diagonal matrix $D$ and a constant $k > 0$ such that
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:Nussbaum_condition}
        U = k D^{-1} V D.
    \end{equation}
    Furthermore, if \eqref{eq:Nussbaum_condition} holds then \eqref{eq:Nussbaum_logconvexity} holds with equality for all $t$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:potential}
    Let $G$ be an NB-irreducible graph such that $\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)$. Then there
    exists a function $\varphi:\vec{E}\to\mathbb{R}$ such that
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:potential}
        \varphi(f)=\varphi(e)+\log\Lambda(G)-\log\operatorname{outdeg}(e)\qquad\text{for all } e,f \in \vec{E} \text{ with } e\to f.
    \end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Given an NB-irreducible graph $G$ such that  $\rho(G) = \Lambda(G)$, let the matrix $M_t$ be defined as above.
    Then $\rho(M_t)=\rho(G)^t$ for all $0 \leq t \leq 1$, by Lemma~\ref{lemma:Mt_eq_Lambda_t},
    and therefore by Theorem~\ref{theorem:Nussbaum} we have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:P_diagsim_B}
    \Pi_G=kD^{-1}BD
    \end{equation}
    where $k>0$ is constant, and $D$ is a diagonal matrix with positive diagonal entries.
    Let $\lambda = \log k$ and define $\varphi:\vec{E} \to \mathbb{R}$ by 
    $\varphi(e) = \log D_{e,e} $.

    For any pair of edges $e,f$ such that $e \rightarrow f$, we have $B_{e,f} = 1$  and
    $(\Pi_G) _{e,f} = \operatorname{outdeg}(e)^{-1} = \operatorname{indeg}(f)^{-1}$. 
    Taking $\log$ of the $e,f$ entry of equation \eqref{eq:P_diagsim_B} yields:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:Nsbm_ef}
        -\log\operatorname{outdeg}(e) = \lambda -\varphi(e)+\varphi(f).
    \end{equation}
    Summing equation \eqref{eq:Nsbm_ef} over all $e \rightarrow f$ pairs with weight $\operatorname{outdeg}(e)^{-1}$
    yields:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:sum_NsbmoverEdges}
        -|\vec{E}| \log \Lambda = |\vec{E} | \cdot\lambda -\sum_{e}\varphi(e)+\sum _{f}\varphi(f) 
        = |\vec{E} | \cdot\lambda.
    \end{equation}
    Hence, $\lambda = -\log \Lambda$. Plugging $\lambda$ into \eqref{eq:Nsbm_ef} yields equation \eqref{eq:potential}, as claimed.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:spc-nec}
    If an NB-irreducible graph $G$ satisfies $\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)$, then it
    satisfies the suspended path condition \eqref{eq:suscond}.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Given an NB-irreducible graph $G$ such that $\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)$, let $\varphi$ be the function given by Lemma~\ref{lem:potential}.
    For a vertex $v \in V(G)$, denote its incoming and outgoing edges by $\vec{E}_{\mathrm{in}}(v) = h^{-1}(v)$ and $\vec{E}_{\mathrm{out}}(v) = t^{-1}(v)$.
    
    Then, for any pair of edges $e \in \vec{E}_{\mathrm{in}}(v)$ and $f \in \vec{E}_{\mathrm{out}}(v)$ with $f\neq e'$, we have $e \rightarrow f$ and therefore by Lemma~\ref{lem:potential}
    \[
        \varphi(f)=\varphi(e)+\log\Lambda(G)-\log(\deg(v)-1).
    \]
    If $\deg(v) \ge 3$, then for any $e_1,e_2 \in \vec{E}_{\mathrm{in}}(v)$, there is an edge $f \in \vec{E}_{\mathrm{out}}(v)$ so that both $e_1 \rightarrow f$ and $e_2 \rightarrow f$, implying that $\varphi(e_1)=\varphi(e_2)$.
    Therefore, $\varphi$ is constant on $\vec{E}_{\mathrm{in}}(v)$, and by a similar argument, it is constant on $\vec{E}_{\mathrm{out}}(v)$ as well. 
    We denote these values by $\varphi_{\mathrm{in}}(v)$ and $\varphi_{\mathrm{out}}(v)$ respectively and obtain the following identity, for any $v \in V(G)$ with $\deg(v) \ge 3$:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:in_out_potential}
            \varphi_{\mathrm{out}}(v)=\varphi_{\mathrm{in}}(v)+\log\Lambda(G)-\log(\deg(v)-1).
    \end{equation}

    Let $P = (e_0,\dots,e_{\ell-1}) \in \mathcal{S}$, let $u = t(e_0)$ and $w = h(e_{\ell-1})$, where $u,w$ are not necessarily distinct vertices. 
    By definition, $\deg(u),\deg(w)\ge 3$ and $\operatorname{outdeg}(e_i)=1$ for $i<\ell-1$.
    Applying equation \eqref{eq:potential} repeatedly along $P$ and along its inverse $P^{-1}$ gives:
    \begin{align*}
        \varphi_{\mathrm{in}}(w) &= \varphi_{\mathrm{out}}(u) + (\ell-1) \log\Lambda(G),\\
        \varphi_{\mathrm{in}}(u) &= \varphi_{\mathrm{out}}(w) + (\ell-1) \log\Lambda(G).
    \end{align*}
    Summing the two equations yields
    \[
        0 = \left[\varphi_{\mathrm{out}}(u) - \varphi_{\mathrm{in}}(u)\right] + \left[\varphi_{\mathrm{out}}(w) - \varphi_{\mathrm{in}}(w)\right] + 2(\ell-1) \log\Lambda(G).
    \]
    Applying \eqref{eq:in_out_potential} results in
    \[
        0 = 2\log\Lambda(G) - \log(\deg(u)-1)  - \log(\deg(w)-1) + 2(\ell-1) \log\Lambda(G),
    \]
    i.e. $(\deg(u)-1)(\deg(w)-1) = \Lambda(G)^{2\ell}$, which is the suspended path condition \eqref{eq:suscond} since $\operatorname{indeg}(P)=\deg(u)-1$ and $\operatorname{outdeg}(P)=\deg(w)-1$.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:realxedSuspended}
    For an NB-irreducible graph $G$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda-g}
        \Lambda(G) = \prod_{P\in \mathcal{S}}g(P)^{|P|/|\vec{E}|}.
    \end{equation}
    Consequently, $\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)$ if and only if $g$ is constant on $\mathcal{S}$, in
    which case the common value is $\Lambda(G)$.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Since $\mathcal{S}$ partitions $\vec{E}$, we have $\sum_{P\in \mathcal{S}}|P|=|\vec{E}|$, and since all
    edges of a suspended path except the last one have out-degree one,
    $\prod_{e\in\vec{E}}\operatorname{outdeg}(e)=\prod_{P\in \mathcal{S}}\operatorname{outdeg}(P)$. As $P\mapsto P^{-1}$ is
    a bijection of $\mathcal{S}$ with $\operatorname{outdeg}(P^{-1})=\operatorname{indeg}(P)$, we also have
    $\prod_{P\in \mathcal{S}}\operatorname{outdeg}(P)=\prod_{P\in \mathcal{S}}\operatorname{indeg}(P)$. Therefore
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \Lambda(G) 
            &=& \prod_{e \in \vec{E}} {\operatorname{outdeg}(e)}^\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}
             =  \prod_{P \in \mathcal{S}} {\operatorname{outdeg}(P)}^\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\\
            &=& \prod_{P \in \mathcal{S}} \Bigl[ \operatorname{outdeg}(P)\operatorname{indeg}(P) \Bigr] ^ \frac{1}{2|\vec{E}|}
             =  \prod_{P \in \mathcal{S}} {g(P)} ^ \frac{|P|}{|\vec{E}|}. 
    \end{eqnarray*}
    If $\rho=\Lambda$ then $g\equiv\Lambda$ by Theorem~\ref{theorem:main}. Conversely,
    if $g\equiv\alpha$ on $\mathcal{S}$, then as \eqref{eq:lambda-g} is a weighted geometric average of $g(P)$, we must have $\alpha = \Lambda$, so the suspended path condition
    holds, and $\rho=\Lambda$ by Theorem~\ref{theorem:main}.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{lemma:two_cycles_variance}
    Given an NB-irreducible graph $G$ with $\rho > \Lambda$, there exist two non-backtracking cycles $C_1$ and $C_2$ with $|C_1| = |C_2|$, 
    a common edge in $C_1 \cap C_2$, but different average out-degree:
    \begin{equation*}
        \left[ \prod_{e \in C_1} \operatorname{outdeg}(e) \right]^{\dfrac{1}{|C_1|}} < \left[ \prod_{e \in C_2} \operatorname{outdeg}(e) \right]^{\dfrac{1}{|C_2|}}.
    \end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Since $\rho>\Lambda$, Theorem~\ref{theorem:main} implies that the cycle condition does not hold.
    Therefore, there is a cycle $C$ with $\prod_{e \in C} \operatorname{outdeg}(e) \neq \Lambda(G)^{|C|}$.
    We only prove the claim for the case
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{e \in C} \log_2 \operatorname{outdeg}(e) < \log_2 \Lambda(G),    
    \end{equation}
    as proof for the other case is virtually identical.
    Let $f$ be some edge in $C$.
    Then, the first step is to find a second cycle $C'$ with a strictly larger average log out-degree such that $f \in C'$.
    Indeed, since $\mathbb{E}[R_\ell] = \ell \log_2 \Lambda$, then for any $\ell$, there is some specific walk $\omega=(e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_\ell) \in \Omega_\ell$ with $R_\ell(\omega) \geq \ell \log_2 \Lambda$.

    whose length is bounded by some constant $l_0$ that depends only on $G$. 
    Therefore, $C' = P_1 \omega P_2$ is a cycle with $f \in C'$.
    Furthermore, 
    \begin{equation*}
               \frac{1}{|C'|} \sum_{e \in C'} \log_2 \operatorname{outdeg}(e)
        \geq    \frac{1}{\ell + 2l_0} R_\ell(\omega)
        \geq   \frac{\ell}{\ell + 2l_0} \log_2\Lambda.
    \end{equation*}
    By taking a sufficiently large $\ell$, one can ensure that:
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{e \in C} \log_2 \operatorname{outdeg}(e) < \frac{1}{|C'|} \sum_{e \in C'} \log_2 \operatorname{outdeg}(e).    
    \end{equation}
    We end the construction by looping through $C$ and $C'$ the required amount of times to obtain the cycles $C_1$ and $C_2$, whose length is  the LCM of $|C|$ and $|C'|$,
    meeting the requirements of the lemma.

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lemma:variance_lb}
    If $G$ is an NB-irreducible graph with $\rho > \Lambda$ then there is a constant $c=c(G)$ so that $\operatorname{var}[R_\ell] \geq c \cdot \ell$ for all $\ell$.
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
In the proof of Lemma~\ref{lemma:variance_ub} we derive an exact formula~\eqref{eq:exact_normalized_variance} for $\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\operatorname{var}[R_\ell]/\ell$.
    As it is unclear how to directly prove this limit is non-zero if $\rho>\Lambda$, we resort to cruder techniques in the following proof.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Given an NB-irreducible graph $G$ with $\rho > \Lambda$,
    by Lemma~\ref{lemma:two_cycles_variance} 
    there are two cycles $C_1$ and $C_2$ of the same length $\ell_0=|C_1| = |C_2|$, 
    with different average out-degree, and with some edge $f$ in their intersection.
    Recall that:
    \begin{equation*}
        \operatorname{var}[R_\ell] = \mathbb{E}_{\omega \in \Omega_\ell}\left[ {(R_\ell(\omega) - \ell \log_2\Lambda)}^2\right].
    \end{equation*}
    In order to lower-bound the variance, we use the exposure method. 
    We expose some of the edges of $\omega = (e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_\ell)$
    and argue that the conditional expectation of ${(R_\ell(\omega) - \ell \log_2\Lambda)}^2$ given the exposed edges is $\Omega(\ell)$ with high probability.
    Define the mapping $\phi:\vec{E}\to\mathbb{N}$, where $\phi(e)$ is the length of the shortest NB-path from $e$ to $f$ if $e \neq f$ and $\phi(f) = \ell_0$.
    Also, let $\ell_{\max}$ be the maximum of $\phi(e)$ on $e \in \vec{E}$.
    We build the partial exposure mapping $\chi$ from $\{0,\ldots,\ell\}$ to $\vec{E}$ by the following randomized procedure:
    
    \begin{center}
        \fbox{\begin{minipage}{7 cm}
        \begin{algorithmic}
            \State $\chi \gets $ the empty mapping
            \State $i \gets 0$
            \While{$i < \ell$}
                \State Expose $e_i$ and set $\chi(i) \gets e_i$
                \State $i \gets i + \phi(e_i)$
            \EndWhile
            \State Expose $e_\ell$ and set $\chi(\ell) \gets e_\ell$
        \end{algorithmic}
        \end{minipage}}        
    \end{center}
    
    The domain of $\chi$ is the set of exposed edge indices, $i_1=0 < i_2 < \cdots < i_k=\ell$.
    Therefore, conditioned on $\chi$, one may write $R_\ell$ as the sum of independent random variables:
    \begin{equation*}
        R_\ell = \sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1} \log_2 \operatorname{outdeg}(e_i) = \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} R(i_{j+1}-i_j,e_{i_j},e_{i_{j+1}}),
    \end{equation*}
    where $R(\ell',e',e'')$ is the random variable counting the number of random bits used by a length $\ell'$ NBRW, conditioned on it beginning at $e'$ and ending at $e''$.
    Therefore:
    \begin{equation*}
        \operatorname{var}[R_\ell | \chi]
           = \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \operatorname{var}\!\left[R(i_{j+1}-i_j,e_{i_j},e_{i_{j+1}})\right]
           \geq |I| \cdot \operatorname{var}\!\left[R(\ell_0,f,f)\right],
    \end{equation*}
    where $I = I(\chi) = \{i \in  \operatorname{dom}(\chi) :\, i+\ell_0 \in \operatorname{dom}(\chi) \mbox{ and } \chi(i) = \chi(i+\ell_0) = f\}$.
    As the number of exposed edges is lower bounded by $\ell/\ell_{\max} = \Omega(\ell)$,
    it follows that $|I|$ is lower bounded by a binomial random variable $X \sim B(\ell/(2\ell_{\max}),p_{\min})$, 
    where $p_{\min}$ is a positive constant defined as the minimum of $(\Pi^{\phi(e)})_{e,f} \cdot (\Pi^{\ell_0})_{f,f}$ on $e \in \vec{E}$. 
    Therefore, $\mathbb{E}[|I|] \geq \mathbb{E}[X] = c_1 \ell$, where $c_1 = p_{\min}/(2 \ell_{\max})$.
    The Chernoff bound on the lower tail of $X$ yields $\Pr[|I| \leq c_1 \ell/2] \leq \Pr[X \leq c_1 \ell/2] \leq \exp[-c_1 \ell/2]$, which is $o(1)$ as $\ell$ grows.

    Recall that by our assumption on the cycles $C_1, C_2$, the variance of $R(\ell_0,f,f)$ is some positive constant $c_2$.
    Therefore:
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \operatorname{var}[R_\ell]
        &=& \mathbb{E}\!\left[{(R_\ell(\omega) - \ell \log_2\Lambda)}^2\right]
        = \sum_\chi \mathbb{E}\!\left[{(R_\ell(\omega) - \ell \log_2\Lambda)}^2 \Big\vert \chi\right] \cdot \Pr[\chi] \\
        &\geq& \sum_\chi |I(\chi)| \cdot \operatorname{var}\!\left[R(\ell_0,f,f)\right] \cdot \Pr[\chi] \\
        &\geq& \frac{c_1 \ell}{2} \cdot \operatorname{var}\!\left[R(\ell_0,f,f)\right] \cdot \Pr\!\left[|I| \geq \frac{c_1 \ell}{2}\right] \\
        &\geq& \frac{c_1 c_2 \ell}{2} \cdot \Pr\!\left[|I| \geq \frac{c_1 \ell}{2}\right]
        = \Omega(\ell).
    \end{eqnarray*}

\label{lemma:variance_ub}
    If $G$ is an NB-irreducible graph then there is a constant $c=c(G)$ so that $\operatorname{var}[R_\ell] \leq c \cdot \ell$ for all $\ell$.
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
We define $R'_\ell = R_\ell - \ell \log_2\Lambda = \sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1} R'_{\ell,i}$, 
    where $R'_{\ell,i}$ are dependent, zero mean, identically distributed random variables with $R'_{\ell,i}(\omega) = \log_2 \operatorname{outdeg}(e_i) - \log_2\Lambda$.
    Therefore:
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \operatorname{var}[R_\ell] 
        &=& \operatorname{var}[R'_\ell]
        =   \mathbb{E}\!\left[ {(R'_\ell)}^2\right] 
        =   \sum_{i,j=0}^{\ell-1} \mathbb{E}\!\left[ R'_{\ell,i} R'_{\ell,j}\right] \\
        &=& \ell \cdot \mathbb{E}\!\left[ {(R'_{\ell,0})}^2\right] + 2 \sum_{\Delta=1}^{\ell-1} (\ell-\Delta) \cdot \mathbb{E}\!\left[ R'_{\ell,0} R'_{\ell,\Delta}\right],
    \end{eqnarray*}
    where in the last step we use the fact that the NBRW starting from the stationary distribution 
    $\nu_s$ is time-invariant.

    Let $f$ denote a column vector where $f_e = \log_2 \operatorname{outdeg}(e) - \log_2\Lambda$ and note that $\sum_e f_e = 0$. 
    Recall that $\Pi=\Pi_G$ is the NBRW transition matrix.
    Then:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized_variance}
        \frac{\operatorname{var}[R_\ell]}{\ell} = \frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}  \cdot f^T f + \frac{2}{|\vec{E}|} \sum_{\Delta=1}^{\ell-1} (1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell})  \cdot f^T \Pi^\Delta f.            
    \end{equation}
    Let $J$ be the Jordan normal form of $\Pi$, written as the direct sum of its blocks $J=J_1 \oplus J_2 \ldots \oplus J_k$, 
    where block $J_i$ corresponds to the eigenvalue $\lambda_i$.
    Assume further that $\lambda_1 = 1 \geq |\lambda_2| \geq \cdots \geq |\lambda_k|$ and that $\Pi = Q^{-1} J Q$.
    Then:
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \frac{\operatorname{var}[R_\ell]}{\ell} 
        &=& - \frac{1}{|\vec{E}|} \cdot f^T f + \frac{2}{|\vec{E}|} f^T Q^{-1} \left( \sum_{\Delta=0}^\ell (1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell}) \cdot  J^\Delta  \right) Q f\\
        &=& - \frac{1}{|\vec{E}|} \cdot f^T f + \frac{2}{|\vec{E}|} f^T Q^{-1} \left[\bigoplus_{i=1}^k \left( \sum_{\Delta=0}^\ell (1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell}) \cdot  J_i^\Delta  \right) \right] Q f  \\
        &=& - \frac{1}{|\vec{E}|} \cdot f^T f + \frac{2}{|\vec{E}|} f^T Q^{-1} \left[0 \oplus \bigoplus_{i=2}^k \left( \sum_{\Delta=0}^\ell (1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell}) \cdot  J_i^\Delta  \right) \right] Q f,  
    \end{eqnarray*}
    where in the last equality  we can replace $J_1$ by the scalar zero. This is justified because the leftmost column of $Q^{-1}$ is a multiple of the all ones vector, orthogonal to $f^T$.
    The next step is to employ the following identity, holding for any matrix $A$ provided that $I-A$ is invertible:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix_power_sum}
        \sum_{\Delta=0}^\ell (1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell}) \cdot A^\Delta = \frac{1}{\ell} \cdot (A^{\ell+1} - A)(I-A)^{-2} + (I-A)^{-1}.
    \end{equation}
    As $I-J_i$ is invertible for all $i>1$, it follows that the normalized variance can be written as:
    \begin{align}
        \frac{\operatorname{var}[R_\ell]}{\ell} = & -\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|} \cdot f^T f + \\
        &\frac{2}{|\vec{E}|} f^T Q^{-1} \left[0 \oplus \bigoplus_{i=2}^k \left( \frac{1}{\ell} \cdot (J_i^{\ell+1} - J_i)(I-J_i)^{-2} + (I-J_i)^{-1} \right) \right] Q f. \nonumber     
    \end{align}
    The matrices $J_i^{\ell+1}$ are upper triangular Toeplitz, 
    where $(J_i^{\ell+1})_{u,v} = \lambda_i^{\ell+1-v+u} \binom{\ell+1}{v-u}$ for $u \leq v$. 
    Therefore, if $|\lambda_i| < 1$, all entries of the matrix $J_i^{\ell+1}$ are bounded in absolute value by some constant that is a function of $G$ only.
    Otherwise, if $|\lambda_i|=1$, then necessarily $\lambda_i$ has algebraic multiplicity of one (see~\cite{horn2012matrix}, Corollary~8.4.6), so $J_i$ is a scalar and  $|J_i^{\ell+1}|=1$.
    It follows that the normalized variance converges when $\ell\to\infty$:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:exact_normalized_variance}
        \lim_{\ell\to\infty} \frac{\operatorname{var}[R_\ell]}{\ell} 
        = - \frac{1}{|\vec{E}|} \cdot f^T f + \frac{2}{|\vec{E}|} f^T Q^{-1} \left[0 \oplus \bigoplus_{i=2}^k (I-J_i)^{-1} \right] Q f.
    \end{equation}
    As the right-hand side does not depend on $\ell$ this concludes the proof.

\label{lemma:k4_minus_edge_variance}
        Given the probability space $\Omega_\ell$ of the length $\ell$ NBRW on $K_4$ minus an edge, starting from the stationary distribution, the random variable  
        $R_\ell(\omega) = \sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1} \log_2 \operatorname{outdeg}(e_i)$, for $\omega=(e_0,\ldots,e_\ell) \in \Omega_\ell$ satisfies:

<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
As in Lemma~\ref{lemma:variance_ub}, we define $R'_\ell = \sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1} R'_{\ell,i}$, 
    where $R'_{\ell,i}$ are random variables with $R'_{\ell,i}(\omega) = \log_2 \operatorname{outdeg}(e_i) - \log_2\Lambda$.
    Therefore:
    \begin{equation*}
        \frac{\operatorname{var}[R_\ell]}{\ell} = - \mathbb{E}\!\left[ {(R'_{\ell,0})}^2\right] + 2 \sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell} (1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell}) \cdot \mathbb{E}\!\left[ R'_{\ell,0} R'_{\ell,\Delta}\right].
    \end{equation*}

    In order to avoid 10x10 matrices, we exploit the fact that $K_4$ minus an edge has only three edge types.
    Then, the stationary distribution, the NBRW transition matrix, and the vector $f$ with $f_e = \log_2 \operatorname{outdeg}(e) - \log_2\Lambda$, 
    with row and column order of: $uu$, $vu$ and $uv$, are as follows:
    \begin{equation*}
        \nu_s = \begin{pmatrix} 1/5 \\ 2/5 \\ 2/5 \end{pmatrix}, \,
        \Pi = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1/2 & 0 & 1/2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \,
        f = \begin{pmatrix} 2/5 \\ 2/5 \\ -3/5\end{pmatrix}.
    \end{equation*}
    Using $N_s$ to denote the matrix with diagonal $\nu_s$ we write:
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{\operatorname{var}[R_\ell]}{\ell} = - f^T N_s f + 2 \sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell} (1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell})  \cdot f^T N_s \Pi^\Delta f.
    \end{equation}
    We next observe that the matrix $\Pi$ is diagonalizable, $\Pi = Q^{-1} D Q$, with:
    \begin{equation*}
        Q = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & 1 & 1 \\ i & -1-i & 1 \\ -i & -1+i & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \,
        D = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i}{2} \end{pmatrix},
    \end{equation*}
    where the first row of $Q$ is the left Perron eigenvector, equal to $\nu_s$ up to scaling.
    In addition, the right Perron eigenvector, which is the first column of $Q^{-1}$, is necessarily the all one vector up to scaling.
    Therefore, $(f^T N_s Q^{-1})_1 = 0$, implying that $f^T N_s Q^{-1} D = f^T N_s Q^{-1} E$, where $E$ is the matrix obtained by zeroing the first diagonal entry of $D$.
    Plugging this into the variance expression yields:
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \frac{\operatorname{var}[R_\ell]}{\ell} 
        &=& - f^T N_s f + 2 \sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell} (1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell}) \cdot f^T N_s Q^{-1} D^\Delta Q f \\
        &=& - f^T N_s f + 2 f^T N_s Q^{-1} \left( \sum_{\Delta=0}^\ell (1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell}) \cdot E^\Delta \right) Q f.
    \end{eqnarray*}
    Using~\eqref{eq:matrix_power_sum}, the normalized variance expression converges to:
                                                                                                       
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \lim_{\ell\to\infty} \frac{\operatorname{var}[R_\ell]}{\ell} 
        &=& -f^T N_s f + 2 f^T N_s Q^{-1} \left( \lim_{\ell\to\infty} \sum_{\Delta=0}^\ell \frac{\ell-\Delta}{\ell} \cdot E^\Delta \right) Q f \\
        &=& -f^T N_s f + 2 f^T N_s Q^{-1} (I-E)^{-1} Q f.
    \end{eqnarray*}
    Plugging in the numbers, yields $2/125$ on the right hand side, as claimed.
