<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Let $m>1+\frac d2$ and the metric on $\mathcal D^m$ be induced by a Fourier multiplier $\mathcal{L}$.
If the initial velocity $v_0$ has Sobolev regularity $m+k$ for $k\geq1$,
the numerical approximation of bandlimiting the right-hand sides in \eqref{eqn:EPDiffIntro} converges to the true solution as the bandlimit $R$ tends to infinity.
Moreover, for $k\geq2$ the error tends to zero at rate $R^{1-k}$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
Let $m>1+\frac d2$ and the metric on $\mathcal D^m$ be strong, twice differentiable, and induced by some $\mathcal{L}=B^*B+\bar\mathcal{L}$,
where $\bar\mathcal{L}$ is continuous from $H^m$ to $H^{1-m}$ and $H^m\times\mathcal D^m\ni(v,\phi)\mapsto[B(v\circ\phi^{-1})]\circ\phi\in L^2$ is differentiable.
If the initial velocity $v_0$ has Sobolev regularity $m+k$ for $k\geq1$,
then so does the velocity $v_t$ for all $t$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $m>\frac d2+1$.
The group of \emph{Sobolev diffeomorphisms} (on the torus) of Sobolev regularity $m$ is
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal D^m=\{\phi\in H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{T}^d)\,|\,\phi^{-1}\in H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{T}^d),\,\phi\text{ preserves orientation}\}
\end{equation*}
with group product $(\phi,\psi)\mapsto\phi\circ\psi$.
The subgroup formed by the connected component (in $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{T}^d)$) of the identity is denoted $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:SobolevMetric}
Let $(\!(\cdot,\cdot)\!)_{H^m}$ be an inner product of $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ with induced norm $\vvvert\cdot\vvvert_{H^m}$ equivalent to the standard $H^m$-norm, then
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:metric}
g_\phi(v,w)=(\!(v\circ\phi^{-1},w\circ\phi^{-1})\!)_{H^m}
\qquad\text{for }
\phi\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m,\,v,w\in T_\phi\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m
\end{equation}
is the induced \emph{right-invariant Riemannian Sobolev metric} on $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$.\footnote{ 
Note that in contrast to most standard textbooks on Riemannian geometry
we do not impose any smoothness requirements on a Riemannian metric, but simply take it to be a collection of inner products -- one on each tangent space to the Riemannian manifold.
Depending on the choice of $(\!(\cdot,\cdot)\!)_{H^m}$, \eqref{eqn:metric} will of course entail \emph{some} regularity of the map $\phi\mapsto g_\phi$, but this is ignored for now. 
}
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
The \emph{Riemannian distance} $\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m}$ on $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ induced by the right-invariant Riemannian Sobolev metric is given by
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m}(\chi,\psi)^2
=\inf_{\substack{\phi:[0,1]\to\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m\\\phi_0=\chi,\,\phi_1=\psi}}E[\phi]
\end{equation*}
for the \emph{path energy}
\begin{equation*}

\end{equation*}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:Regulcomposition}
Let $m>\frac d2+1$ and $0\leq s\leq m$.
Let $\varepsilon>0$ small enough such that $\mathrm{id}+\overline{B_\varepsilon(0)}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal D}_{\mathrm{id}}^m$. There exists $C>0$ such that
\begin{equation*}
\|f\circ\phi\|_{H^s}\leq(1+C\|\phi-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})\|f\|_{H^s}
\qquad\text{for all }
\phi\in\mathrm{id}+\overline{B_\varepsilon(0)},\ f\in H^s(\mathbb{T}^d).
\end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
First note that by Sobolev embedding we have $\|\phi-\mathrm{id}\|_{C^1}\leq c\|\phi-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}$ for some constant $c>0$ (which of course depends on the chosen inner product on $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$).
Thus, in particular $\|D\phi-\mathrm{I}\|_{C^0}\leq c\varepsilon$ for all $\phi\in\mathrm{id}+\overline{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}$,
and $\varepsilon$ can indeed be chosen small enough such that $\det D\phi>0$ everywhere and therefore $\mathrm{id}+\overline{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal D}_{\mathrm{id}}^m$.

The proof is by induction in $s$.
Without loss of generality we consider the standard norm $\|f\|_{H^s}^2=\sum_{j=0}^s\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}|D^jf|^2\,\mathrm{d} x$.
For $s=0$ we have
\begin{multline*}
\|f\circ\phi\|_{H^0}^2
=\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}|f\circ\phi|^2\,\mathrm{d} x
=\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}|f|^2\det D(\phi^{-1})\,\mathrm{d} x\\
\leq\min_{x\in\mathbb{T}^d}\frac1{\det D\phi(x)}\|f\|_{H^0}^2
\leq[(1+cL\|\phi-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})\|f\|_{H^0}]^2,
\end{multline*}
where $L$ is the Lipschitz constant of the smooth map $A\mapsto\sqrt{1/\det A}$ on the closed ball of radius $c\varepsilon$ around the identity matrix $\mathrm{I}$.
Now let $s>0$.
We have $D(f\circ\phi)=Df\circ\phi\, D\phi$.
By the induction hypothesis $\|Df\circ\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}\leq(1+\kappa\|\phi-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})\|Df\|_{H^{s-1}}$ for some $\kappa>0$.
Furthermore,
\begin{multline*}
\|Df\circ\phi\, D\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}
\leq\|Df\circ\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}+\|Df\circ\phi(D\phi-\mathrm{I})\|_{H^{s-1}}\\
\leq\|Df\circ\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}(1+\hat K\|D\phi-\mathrm{I}\|_{H^{m-1}})
\leq\|Df\circ\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}(1+K\|\phi-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})
\end{multline*}
for some $\hat K,K>0$ by \cite[Lemma 2.3]{InKaTo13} or \cref{thm:BeHo21} (essentially since $H^{m-1}(\mathbb{T}^d)$ is a Banach algebra).
In summary,
\begin{align*}
&\,\quad\|f\circ\phi\|_{H^s}^2\\
&=\|f\circ\phi\|_{H^0}^2+\|D(f\circ\phi)\|_{H^{s-1}}^2\\
&\leq(1+cL\|\phi-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})^2\|f\|_{H^0}^2+(1+K\|\phi-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})^2\|Df\circ\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}^2\\
&\leq(\!1\!+\!cL\|\phi\!-\!\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\!)^2\|f\|_{H^0}^2\!+\!(\!1\!+\!K\|\phi\!-\!\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\!)^2(\!1\!+\!\kappa\|\phi\!-\!\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\!)^2\|Df\|_{H^{s\!-\!1}}^2\\
&\leq(1+C\|\phi-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})^2\|f\|_{H^s}^2
\end{align*}
for some $C>0$ (depending on $c,L,K,\kappa$ and $\varepsilon$).

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:normEstDeform}
Let $m>\frac{d}{2}+1$, $0\leq s\leq m$, and $I=[0,1]$.
There exists a constant $C>0$ such that for all $v\in L^1(I;H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d))$ with flow $\phi:I\to\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$, $\phi_0=\mathrm{id}$, it holds
\begin{enumerate}
\item
$\|\phi_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\leq\exp(C\|v\|_{L^1(I;H^m)})-1$ for all $t\in I$,
                             
                                                                                                                          
\item\label{enm:composition}
$\|f\circ\phi_t\|_{H^s}\leq\exp(C\|v\|_{L^1(I;H^m)})\|f\|_{H^s}$ for all $t\in I$, $f\in H^s(\mathbb{T}^d)$,
\item\label{enm:adjointMapEstimate}
if $s\leq m-1$, then $\|\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t}w\|_{H^s}\leq\exp(C\|v\|_{L^1(I;H^m)})\|w\|_{H^s}$ for all $t\in I$, $w\in H^s(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
First note that                                                                                                                                                    
             
we may consider the flow $\phi_t$ in $\widetilde{\mathcal D}_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ instead of $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ since (with our notation conventions from the previous section) this does not change any of the estimates.

Next consider $v$ with $(1+C\varepsilon)\|v\|_{L^1(I;H^m)}<\varepsilon$ for $\varepsilon$ and $C$ from \cref{thm:Regulcomposition}.
We show $\|\phi_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\leq\varepsilon$ for all $t\in I$.
Indeed, let $T\in I$ be the smallest time such that either $\|\phi_T-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}=\varepsilon$ or $T=1$, then for $t<T$ we have
\begin{multline}\label{eqn:distFromId}
\|\phi_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}
\leq\int_0^t\|v_r\circ\phi_r\|_{H^m}\mathrm{d} r\\
\leq(1+C\varepsilon)\int_I\|v_r\|_{H^m}\mathrm{d} r
=(1+C\varepsilon)\|v\|_{L^1(I;H^m)}<\varepsilon.
\end{multline}
The last inequality remains strict even after taking the liminf as $t\to T$,
while $\liminf_{t\to T}\|\phi_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}$ can be bounded below by $\|\phi_T-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}$
(due to the weak lower semi-continuity of the norm and the uniform convergence $\phi_t\to\phi_T$, which implies $\phi_t-\mathrm{id}\to\phi_T-\mathrm{id}$ weakly in $H^m$ due to $\phi_T-\mathrm{id}\in H^m$).
Thus we must have $T=1$ and therefore $\|\phi_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\leq\varepsilon$ for $t\leq1$.

From now on, $v$ is an arbitrary velocity field.
We split it into $N$ time segments $v^j=v|_{[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}$ (extended to $I$ by zero)
with $0=t_0<\ldots<t_N=1$ chosen such that $\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\|v_t\|_{H^m}\mathrm{d} t=\|v\|_{L^1(I;H^m)}/N<\frac\varepsilon{1+C\varepsilon}$.
Let us denote by $\phi^j$ the flow of $v^j$, then by the above we have $\|\phi^j_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\leq\varepsilon$, and furthermore
\begin{equation*}
\phi_t=\phi^N_t\circ\cdots\circ\phi^2_t\circ\phi^1_t.
\end{equation*}

\begin{enumerate}
\item
Abbreviate $V_N\!=\!(1\!+\!C\varepsilon)\|v\|_{L^1(I;H^m)}/N$.
From \eqref{eqn:distFromId} we know $\|\phi^j_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\leq V_N$ for all $j$.
By induction in $n$ we have $\|\phi^{j+n-1}_t\circ\cdots\circ\phi^j_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\leq(1+CV_N)^n-1$ for all $j$ (assuming without loss of generality $C\geq1$):
Indeed, the case $n=1$ is trivial, and for $n>1$, using \cref{thm:Regulcomposition} we have
\begin{align*}
&\|\phi^{j+n-1}_t\circ\cdots\circ\phi^j_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\\
\leq&\|(\phi^{j+n-1}_t\circ\cdots\circ\phi^{j+1}_t-\mathrm{id})\circ\phi^j_t\|_{H^m}+\|\phi^j_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\\
\leq&(1+CV_N)\|\phi^{j+n-1}_t\circ\cdots\circ\phi^{j+1}_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}+\|\phi^j_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\\
\leq&(1+CV_N)[(1+CV_N)^{n-1}-1]+V_N
\leq(1+CV_N)^n-1.
\end{align*}
Thus, $\|\phi_t-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\leq(1+CV_N)^N\to_{N\to\infty}\exp(C(1+C\varepsilon)\|v\|_{L^1(I;H^m)})$.
       
                                                                 
         
                   
                                
                                                   
                                                         
                 
                                                                                                                                           
                                                                                                                                                                                  
                   
                                
                           
             
                                                                             
                 
\item
From $\|f\circ\phi_t^N\circ\cdots\circ\phi_t^1\|_{H^s}=\|(\cdots(f\circ\phi_t^N)\circ\cdots\circ\phi_t^2)\circ\phi_t^1\|_{H^s}$ it follows
by inductively applying \cref{thm:Regulcomposition} that
$\|f\circ\phi_t\|_{H^s}\leq\|f\|_{H^s}\prod_{j=1}^N(1+C\|\phi_t^j-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})\leq\|f\|_{H^s}(1+CV_N)^N\to_{N\to\infty}\|f\|_{H^s}\exp(C(1+C\varepsilon)\|v\|_{L^1(I;H^m)})$.
\item
We will show $\|\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^j}w\|_{H^s}\leq(1+\kappa V_N)\|w\|_{H^s}$ for all $j$ and some constant $\kappa>0$,
from which the result will follow exploiting $\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t}w=\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^N}\cdots\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^1}w$ and letting $N\to\infty$ as before. Indeed,
\begin{align*}
\|\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^j}w\|_{H^s}
&\leq(1+C\|(\phi_t^j)^{-1}-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})\|D\phi_t^jw\|_{H^s}\\
&=(1+C\|(\phi_t^j)^{-1}-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})\|(D\phi_t^j-\mathrm{I})w+w\|_{H^s}\\
&\leq(1+C\|(\phi_t^j)^{-1}-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})(c\|D\phi_t^j-\mathrm{I}\|_{H^m}+1)\|w\|_{H^s},
\end{align*}
using \cref{thm:BeHo21} with constant $c>0$ in the last inequality and \cref{thm:Regulcomposition} in the first.
The latter is allowed since $(\phi_t^j)^{-1}$ is the flow of $[0,t]\ni r\mapsto-v^j(t-r)$ \cite{Tr95a} so that by \eqref{eqn:distFromId} we also have $\|(\phi_t^j)^{-1}-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m}\leq V_N<\varepsilon$.
Consequently, with some $\tilde c>0$ we have $\|\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^j}w\|_{H^s}\leq(1+CV_N)(1+\tilde cV_N)\|w\|_{H^s}$, as desired.
\qedhere
\end{enumerate}

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:continuityAdjointAdjointMap}
Let $m>\frac d2+1$, $0\leq s<m$, and $\phi_n\to\phi$ in $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ as $n\to\infty$.
Then $w_n\to w$ weakly in $H^s(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ implies
$w_n\circ\phi_n\to w\circ\phi$ and $\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_n}w_n\to\mathrm{Ad}_\phi w$ both weakly in $H^s(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$.
As a consequence, the map $(\rho,\phi)\mapsto\mathrm{Ad}_\phi^*\rho$ is continuous from $H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\times\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ into $H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By \cref{thm:normEstDeform}\eqref{enm:composition} $w_n\circ\phi_n$ is bounded, hence contains a weakly converging subsequence (for simplicity still indexed by $n$).
We show that the weak limit is $w\circ\phi$ independent of the chosen subsequence so that actually the whole sequence converges weakly.
To this end let $\eta$ be smooth or at least $H^m$-regular, then
\begin{equation*}
\langle\eta,w_n\circ\phi_n-w\circ\phi\rangle
=\langle\eta,(w_n-w)\circ\phi_n+w\circ\phi_n-w\circ\phi\rangle
\mathop\to_{n\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\eta,(w_n-w)\circ\phi_n\rangle
\end{equation*}
due to the continuity of the composition. Next, by the transformation rule we have
\begin{equation*}
\langle\eta,(w_n-w)\circ\phi_n\rangle
=\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\det D\phi_n^{-1}\,\eta\circ\phi_n^{-1}\cdot(w_n-w)\,\mathrm{d} x
\mathop\to_{n\to\infty}0
\end{equation*}
since $\eta\circ\phi_n^{-1}\to\eta\circ\phi^{-1}$ strongly in $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ (by continuity of inversion in $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ and composition with $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ \cite[Lemmas 2.7-2.8]{InKaTo13}),
$\det D\phi_n^{-1}\to\det D\phi^{-1}$ strongly in $H^{m-1}(\mathbb{T}^d)$
(since $\phi\mapsto\phi^{-1}$ is continuous on $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$,
$\psi\mapsto D\psi$ is continuous from $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ to $H^{m-1}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$,
and taking the pointwise determinant is continuous from $H^{m-1}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$ into $H^{m-1}(\mathbb{T}^d)$ since $H^{m-1}$ forms a Banach algebra \cite[Lemma 2.16]{InKaTo13}),
and thus $\det D\phi_n^{-1}\,\eta\circ\phi_n^{-1}\to\det D\phi^{-1}\,\eta\circ\phi^{-1}$ strongly in $H^{m-1}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ by \cref{thm:BeHo21}.

Likewise, $D\phi_nw_n$ converges weakly to $D\phi w$ in $H^s(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ as the product of a strongly (in $H^{m-1}$) and a weakly (in $H^s$) converging sequence
so that $\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_n}w_n$ converges weakly to $\mathrm{Ad}_\phi w$ by $\phi_n^{-1}\to\phi^{-1}$ in $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ and the previous.

Finally, suppose $\mathrm{Ad}_\phi^*\rho$ were not continuous in $\phi$ and $\rho$.
Then there exist $C>0$ as well as sequences $\phi_n\to\phi$ in $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$, $\rho_n\to\rho$ in $H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ and  $w_n$ in $H^s(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ with $\|w_n\|_{H^s}\leq 1$ such that
\begin{equation*}
C
<\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_n}^*\rho_n-\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi}^*\rho,w_n\rangle
=\langle\rho_n,\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_n}w_n\rangle-\langle\rho,\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi}w_n\rangle.
\end{equation*}
However, due to the boundedness of $w_n$ we can extract a subsequence (for simplicity again indexed by $n$) with $w_n\to w$ weakly in $H^s(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$.
Then along this subsequence the right-hand side of the above converges to $\langle\rho,\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi}w\rangle-\langle\rho,\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi}w\rangle=0$, yielding a contradiction.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:smoothMetric}
Let $m>\frac d2+1$, $0\leq l\leq m$.
If $\mathcal{L}=\bar\mathcal{L}+B^*B$ with $\bar\mathcal{L}:H^{m-l}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\to H^{l-m}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ bounded linear
and $B=\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_kD^k$ a differential operator of order $m$ with coefficient tensors $a_i$ of $W^{l,\infty}$-regularity,
then the metric \eqref{eqn:metric} is $l$ times continuously differentiable.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:exampleMetrics}
The result of course applies to the standard example $\mathcal{L}=1+(-\Delta)^m$ with the choice $\bar\mathcal{L}=0$ and $B=(1,\Delta^{m/2})$ (for even $m$) or $(1,\nabla\Delta^{(m-1)/2})$ (for odd $m$),
but one may just as well introduce non-local operators or spatially inhomogeneous weights,
e.g.\ by choosing $\bar\mathcal{L} u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{\mathrm{dist}(x,y)^{d+2s}}\omega(x,y)\,\mathrm{d} y$ for some $s\in(0,1)\cap(0,m-l)$ and a bounded weight $\omega(x,y)$
or by choosing $\bar\mathcal{L} u=-\mathrm{div}(\chi_A\nabla u)$ for the characteristic function $\chi_A$ of some measurable $A\subset\mathbb{T}^d$ (as long as $l\leq m-1$).
In particular the latter choice may be of interest in applications, modelling an enhanced energy dissipation (which is one possible physical interpretation of the path energy) in region $A$,
e.g.\ due to a low permeability porous structure.
The resulting metric is not smooth, but only $m-1$ times continuously differentiable (as one can for instance easily verify for the concrete choice $d=1,m=2,A=[0,\frac12]$).

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We check differentiability in the global chart $\widetilde{\mathcal D}_{\mathrm{id}}^m\subset\mathrm{id}+H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$. We have
\begin{multline*}
g_\phi(v,w)
=\langle\bar\mathcal{L}(v\circ\phi^{-1}),w\circ\phi^{-1}\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^d} B(v\circ\phi^{-1})\cdot B(w\circ\phi^{-1})\,\mathrm{d} x\\
=\langle\bar\mathcal{L}(v\circ\phi^{-1}),w\circ\phi^{-1}\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^d} B(v\circ\phi^{-1})\circ\phi\cdot B(w\circ\phi^{-1})\circ\phi\,\det D\phi\,\mathrm{d} x.
\end{multline*}
Now $(w,\phi)\mapsto w\circ\phi^{-1}$ is $l$ times differentiable as a map from $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\times\widetilde{\mathcal D}_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ to $H^{m-l}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$.
Indeed, it is linear in $w$ so that the map and all its derivatives with respect to $\phi$ are $C^\infty$-smooth in $w$ as long as they are continuous in $w$.
Thus it remains to check differentiability with respect to $\phi$.

Using the formulas
\begin{equation*}
\partial_\phi\phi^{-1}(u)=-D\phi^{-1}\,u\circ\phi^{-1},
\qquad
\partial_\phi(v\circ\phi^{-1})(u)=-Dv\circ\phi^{-1}\,D\phi^{-1}\,u\circ\phi^{-1}
\end{equation*}
we first show inductively that $\partial_\phi^l(v\circ\phi^{-1})(u_1,\ldots,u_l)$ is a sum of products of (potentially multiple factors) $D\phi^{-1}$
with (potentially zero or higher order) derivatives of $v$ and $u_1,\ldots,u_l$ (all composed with $\phi^{-1}$) and (potentially multiple, first or higher order) derivatives of $D\phi^{-1}$.
Moreover, these derivatives amount to an overall order of $l$ in each product, at most $l-1$ of which can act on $D\phi^{-1}$.
Indeed, this holds for $l=1$, and if it holds for $l$,
then applying another time $\partial_\phi$ in some direction $u_{l+1}$ leads by the product rule again to a sum of terms,
each one resulting from differentiating one factor of the previous products with respect to $\phi$.
If this factor is $D^jw\circ\phi^{-1}$ for $w$ being one of $v,u_1,\ldots,u_l$, then $$\partial_\phi[D^jw\circ\phi^{-1}](u_{l+1})=-D^{j+1}w\circ\phi^{-1}\,D\phi^{-1}\,u_{l+1}\circ\phi^{-1}$$
so that the total order of derivatives of $v,u_1,\ldots,u_{l+1},D\phi^{-1}$ indeed was increased by one (from $l$ to $l+1$).
If the factor is $D^j(D\phi^{-1})$, then $$\partial_\phi[D^j(D\phi^{-1})](u_{l+1})=-D^{j+1}(D\phi^{-1}\,u_{l+1}\circ\phi^{-1}),$$
which by the product rule and the same arguments as above increases the total order of derivatives of $v,u_1,\ldots,u_{l+1},D\phi^{-1}$ again by one
(and the highest occurring order of differentiating $D\phi^{-1}$ at most by one), as desired.

In summary, for $l\geq1$ we have that $\partial_\phi^l(v\circ\phi^{-1})(u_1,\ldots,u_l)$ is a sum of products
with one factor in each of $H^{m-n_1},\ldots,H^{m-n_k}$ (the differentiated $v$ and $u_j$), one factor in each of $H^{m-1-n_{k+1}},\ldots,H^{m-1-n_K}$ (the differentiated $D\phi^{-1}$)
as well as (potentially multiple) factors in $H^m$ (undifferentiated $u_j$) and $H^{m-1}$ (the factors $D\phi^{-1}$),
where $n_1,\ldots,n_K\geq1$ with $n_1+\ldots+n_K=l$ and $n_{k+1},\ldots,n_K\leq l-1$.
Now \cref{thm:BeHo21} implies that as soon as one function has Sobolev regularity larger than $\frac d2$, the product of two functions has the minimum Sobolev regularity of the two.
So assume first $m-n_1,\ldots,m-n_k,m-1-n_{k+1},\ldots,m-1-n_K>\frac d2$, then the product has Sobolev regularity $\min\{m-n_1,\ldots,m-n_k,m-1-n_{k+1},\ldots,m-1-n_K\}\geq m-l$.
Otherwise, if at least one factor has Sobolev regularity no larger than $\frac d2$, all factors with Sobolev regularity larger than $\frac d2$ can be ignored for the regularity of the product.
So assume without loss of generality that $m-n_1,\ldots,m-n_k,m-1-n_{k+1},\ldots,m-1-n_K\leq\frac d2$ (if this only holds for a subset, then reindex and redefine $k,K$ correspondingly).
Furthermore, we may assume $k=1$ without loss of generality (since for fixed $n_1,\ldots,n_K$ this represents the case of overall lowest Sobolev regularity).
Then, by iteratively applying \cref{thm:BeHo21} (starting with the most regular function and multiplying one after another the functions in the order of decreasing Sobolev regularity)
we obtain that the product has Sobolev regularity $m-n_1-\ldots-n_K+(K-1)(m-1-\frac d2)-\epsilon$ for any $\epsilon>0$ (note that \cref{thm:BeHo21} also holds for fractional exponents).
Due to $m>1+\frac d2$ and $n_1+\ldots+n_K\leq l$ this implies that the product has Sobolev regularity $m-l$.
Hence in total, $\partial_\phi^l(v\circ\phi^{-1})(u_1,\ldots,u_l)\in H^{m-l}$, where the dependence on $\phi,v,u_1,\ldots,u_l\in H^m$ is continuous;
consequently,
                   
                   
                                                         
        
                                                                                  
                 
                                                                                                                                           
                                                                                                                                  
                                                                                                                                                                         
                                                                                                                                                             
                                                                                                                                                                                          
                                                                                       
the $l$th derivative of $v\circ\phi^{-1}$ in $\phi$ is a bounded $l$-linear map from $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)^l$ into $H^{m-l}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$, which depends continuously on $\phi\in\widetilde{\mathcal D}_{\mathrm{id}}^m$.

Since the dual pairing is smooth (even bilinear), the map $(v,w,\phi)\mapsto\langle\bar\mathcal{L}(v\circ\phi^{-1}),w\circ\phi^{-1}\rangle$ is $l$ times differentiable
from $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\times H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\times\widetilde{\mathcal D}_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ into the reals.

Further, the map $\phi\mapsto\det D\phi$ is smooth from $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ into $C^0(\mathbb{T}^d)$ as the composition of a $d$-degree polynomial with a linear operator.
Now, abbreviate $\bar Dw=D(w\circ\phi^{-1})\circ\phi$ (we drop the dependence on $\phi$ in the notation), then $D^k(w\circ\phi^{-1})\circ\phi=\bar D^kw$.
Since
\begin{equation*}
\bar Dw
=[Dw\circ\phi^{-1}\,D\phi^{-1}]\circ\phi
=Dw(D\phi)^{-1},
\end{equation*}
the map $(w,\phi)\mapsto\bar Dw$ is infinitely smooth from $H^s(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\times\widetilde{\mathcal D}_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ into $H^{s-1}(\mathbb{T}^d)^{d\times d}$
(even though $\phi\mapsto\phi^{-1}$ is not smooth in $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$,
since matrix inversion and pointwise multiplication is).
By iteration, $(w,\phi)\mapsto\bar D^mw$ is smooth from $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\times\widetilde{\mathcal D}_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ into $L^2(\mathbb{T}^d)^{d\times\cdots\times d}$.
Finally, $\phi\mapsto a_i\circ\phi$ is $l$-times differentiable from $\widetilde{\mathcal D}_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ into tensor fields of $L^\infty$-regularity
so that $(u,\phi)\mapsto B(u\circ\phi^{-1})\circ\phi$ is $l$ times differentiable from $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\times\widetilde{\mathcal D}_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ into $L^2(\mathbb{T}^d)$-tensors.
Since pointwise product and integration are smooth as long as they are bounded, $(u,v,\phi)\mapsto g_\phi(u,v)$ is $l$ times differentiable.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:GuRaRuWi23}
Let $m>1+\frac d2$ and the Riemannian metric \eqref{eqn:metric} on $\mathcal D^m$ be induced by $\mathcal{L}=B^*B+\bar\mathcal{L}$ with $\bar\mathcal{L}:H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\to H^{1-m}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ bounded linear
and $B=\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_kD^k$ a differential operator of order $m$ with coefficient tensors $a_i$ of $W^{0,\infty}$-regularity.
Then geodesics in $\mathcal D^m$ with velocity $v$ and momentum $\rho$ satisfy the weak PDE
\begin{equation*}
0=\int_0^1\!\!-2\langle\bar\mathcal{L} v_t, Dv_t\eta_t\rangle + 2\langle\rho_t,\dot\eta_t+D\eta_tv_t\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}|Bv_t|^2\mathrm{div}\eta_t+2Bv_t\cdot{\mathcal U}(v_t,\eta_t)\,\mathrm{d} x\,\mathrm{d} t
\end{equation*}
for all smooth $\eta:[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d$, where $${\mathcal U}(v_t,\eta_t)=\frac\mathrm{d}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\left[B(v_t\circ(\mathrm{id}+\epsilon\eta_t)^{-1})\circ(\mathrm{id}+\epsilon\eta_t)\right]_{\epsilon=0}\in H^0((0,1)\times\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d).$$
Its strong form is \eqref{eqn:EPDiff}.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:regularityPreservationLDDMM}
Let $m>\frac d2+1$, $1\leq k\leq m$, and the metric \eqref{eqn:metric} satisfy the conditions of \cref{thm:smoothMetric} for $l=2$ (thus be twice differentiable).
Let $\phi_t$ be a geodesic in $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ with $\phi_0=\mathrm{id}$, and let $v_t\in H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ be its velocity and $\rho_t=\mathcal{L} v_t\in H^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ its momentum.
If $\rho_0\in H^{-m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ or equivalently $v_0\in H^{m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$, then
\begin{equation*}
\rho_t=\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^{-1}}^*\rho_0\in H^{-m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)
\quad\text{and }v_t\in H^{m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)
\qquad\text{for all }t>0.
\end{equation*}
Moreover, $t\mapsto(\rho_t,v_t)$ is continuous into $H^{-m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\times H^{m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
First note that by \cref{thm:normEstDeform}\eqref{enm:adjointMapEstimate} the adjoint map with respect to any $\psi\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ represents a bounded isomorphism
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{Ad}_\psi:H^{m-k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\to H^{m-k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d).
\end{equation*}
Indeed, $\mathrm{Ad}_{\psi}^{-1}=\mathrm{Ad}_{\psi^{-1}}$ satisfies the same boundedness properties as $\mathrm{Ad}_\psi$ due to $\psi^{-1}\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$.
Next simply define
\begin{equation*}
\rho_t=\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^{-1}}^*(\rho_0)\in H^{k-m}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d),
\qquad
v_t=\mathcal{R}\rho_t=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\rho_t\in H^{m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d),
\end{equation*}
which by the previous is well-defined. For $w$ smooth we then obtain
\begin{multline*}
\langle\dot\rho_t,w\rangle
=\tfrac\mathrm{d}{\mathrm{d} t}\langle\rho_0,\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^{-1}}w\rangle
=\langle\rho_0,(D\phi_t)^{\!-1}Dw\circ\phi_t\dot\phi_t-(D\phi_t)^{\!-1}\!D\dot\phi_t(D\phi_t)^{\!-1}w\circ\phi_t\rangle\\
                                                                                          
=\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t}^*\rho_t,\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^{-1}}(Dwv_t-Dv_tw)\rangle
=\langle\rho_t,Dwv_t-Dv_tw\rangle,
\end{multline*}
which is still well-defined due to $v_t\in H^{m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$.
However, this is exactly the weak form of the geodesic ODE \eqref{eqn:EPDiff} which by \cref{thm:GuRaRuWi23} rigorously characterizes the geodesics.
Since the geodesic ODE is uniquely solvable by the regularity of the metric, $\rho_t$ must be the corresponding momentum.
The continuous time dependence finally follows from \cref{thm:continuityAdjointAdjointMap} and the continuity of the geodesic in $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:higherRegularity}
We formulated our result for $k\leq m$ since in that range the momentum $\rho_t$ stays a distribution.
However, the argument extends to $m<k\leq m+\frac d2$
(and the result is known to hold for $k>m+\frac d2$, see later).
Indeed, by \cref{thm:regularityPreservationLDDMM} we already know $\rho_t=\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^{-1}}^*\rho_0$,
and it remains to show that $\rho_0\in H^{-m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ implies $\rho_t\in H^{-m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ for all $t$.
Now let $w:\mathbb{T}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d$ be smooth, then
\begin{align*}
\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\rho_t\cdot w\,\mathrm{d} x
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\rho_0\cdot(D\phi_t)^{-1}w\circ\phi_t\,\mathrm{d} x
=\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\rho_0\circ\phi_t^{-1}\cdot D\phi_t^{-1}w\,\det D\phi_t^{-1}\,\mathrm{d} x\\
&\leq\|w\|_{H^{m-k}}\|\det D\phi_t^{-1}\,(D\phi_t^{-1})^T\rho_0\circ\phi_t^{-1}\|_{H^{k-m}}\\
&\lesssim\|w\|_{H^{m-k}}\|\det D\phi_t^{-1}\|_{H^{m-1}}\|D\phi_t^{-1}\|_{H^{m-1}}\|\rho_0\circ\phi_t^{-1}\|_{H^{k-m}},
\end{align*}
where the last line follows from \cref{thm:BeHo21}, using $m-1>\frac d2\geq k-m$.
Since the determinant is a degree $d$ polynomial and $H^{m-1}$ is a Banach algebra (cf.\ \cref{thm:BeHo21}),
we have $\|\det D\phi^{-1}\|_{H^{m-1}}\lesssim\|D\phi^{-1}\|_{H^{m-1}}^d\lesssim(1+\|\phi^{-1}-\mathrm{id}\|_{H^m})^d$.
Furthermore, $\|\rho_0\circ\phi_t^{-1}\|_{H^{k-m}}\lesssim c(\phi_t^{-1})\|\rho_0\|_{H^{k-m}}$ by \eqref{eqn:regularityComposition}
so that overall
\begin{equation*}
\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\rho_t\cdot w\,\mathrm{d} x
\leq C(\phi_t^{-1})\|\rho_0\|_{H^{k-m}}\|w\|_{H^{m-k}}
\end{equation*}
with a finite constant $C(\phi_t^{-1})$ depending only on $\phi_t^{-1}\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$.
Thus, $\rho_t\in H^{k-m}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ as desired.

For $k>m+\frac d2$ it was already shown in \cite[Thm.\,4.1]{MiPr10} by direct estimates
that the solution to the geodesic ODE \eqref{eqn:EPDiff} stays in $H^{k-m}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ if $\rho_0$ is.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:weakMetrics}
\Cref{thm:regularityPreservationLDDMM,rem:higherRegularity} solve a conjecture of \cite{MiPr10}:
The article considers right-invariant Sobolev metrics \eqref{eqn:metric} of order $m$ on $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^{m+k}$ with $k>0$.
Those are known as weak Riemannian metrics since they generate a topology on the tangent spaces $T_\phi\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^{m+k}=H^{m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$
with respect to which the tangent space is not complete.
As a consequence, the resulting Riemannian manifold is not metrically complete,
but nevertheless the geodesic equation and the Riemannian exponential map may still be well-defined.
Indeed, \cite[Thm.\,4.1]{MiPr10} shows that for a smooth metric and $k>m+\frac d2$ the Riemannian exponential $\exp_\phi^{m,k}:T_\phi\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^{m+k}\to\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^{m+k}$ is a local diffeomorphism\footnote{In fact, the proof of the diffeomorphism property in \cite[Thm.\,4.1]{MiPr10} does not require smoothness of the metric,
but just solvability of the geodesic ODE and differentiable dependence of its solution on the initial data, which holds as soon as the metric is three times differentiable.},
and this is conjectured to be true for any $k\geq0$.
\Cref{thm:regularityPreservationLDDMM,rem:higherRegularity} confirm this conjecture:
Indeed, they show that the restriction of $\exp_\phi^{m,0}$ from $T_\phi\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ to $T_\phi\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^{m+k}$ has range in $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^{m+k}$
(recall that by \cite{BrVi17} the flow of a $H^{m+k}$-regular velocity lies in $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^{m+k}$),
thus $\exp_\phi^{m,k}$ is nothing else but this restriction and therefore well-defined.
If the metric \eqref{eqn:metric} is three times differentiable, it is even a local diffeomorphism,
which follows from the Inverse Function Theorem (noting that differentiability of the metric on $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ implies differentiability on $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^{m+k}$).

\label{thm:FourierDecay}
Let $k\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $w:[0,1]\to H^k(\mathbb{T}^d)$ be continuous, then $\|({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})w_t\|_{H^k}\to0$ as $R\to\infty$, uniformly in $t\in[0,1]$.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
The Lie bracket $[v,w]$ satisfies the three characterizing properties of a Lie bracket:
bilinearity, antisymmetry, and the Jacobi identity.
However, as discussed before, it is not closed in $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$, i.e.\ $v,w\in H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ does not imply $[v,w]\in H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$.
In contrast, the approximate Lie bracket $[V,W]_R$ is closed in $H^m_R$,
but it is not a legitimate Lie bracket as it does not satisfy the Jacobi identity.
This is due to a principal obstruction observed by Omori in \cite{Om78}:
There exists no Lie algebra $H^m_R$ with finite Fourier support and correct Lie bracket.
                                                                                       
                                                                                                                             
                                                 
Let us quickly adapt the argument from \cite{Om78} to our setting, for simplicity restricting to dimension $d=1$:
Since the Lie bracket is closed, as soon as the Lie algebra $H^m_R$ contains trigonometric functions of two different nonzero frequencies,
then by repeatedly applying the Lie bracket $[\cdot,\cdot]$ one obtains trigonometric functions of arbitrarily high frequencies, which therefore must belong to $H^m_R$.
It does not help to drop the condition on $H^m_R$ of finite Fourier support, either:
There cannot exist any Hilbert Lie algebra containing the constant function and trigonometric functions of arbitrarily high frequencies.
Indeed, due to $[[1,\sin(2\pi\xi x)],1]=[2\pi\xi\cos(2\pi\xi x),1]=(2\pi\xi)^2\sin(2\pi\xi x)$ the Lie bracket cannot be bounded in any norm on $H^m_R$.
The only exception are the spaces $H^m_R=\mathrm{span}(1,\sin(2\pi\xi x),\cos(2\pi\xi x))$ with exactly one frequency $\xi\in\mathbb{Z}$;
on those, the standard Lie bracket is closed so that $H^m_R$ is a true Lie algebra.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:productsInB}
Let $m>\frac d2+1$ and let $\mathcal{L}$ satisfy the conditions from \cref{thm:smoothMetric} for $l=1$.
Then                                                                             
\begin{equation*}
\|w^TD\mathcal{L} w\|_{H^{-m-1}}\lesssim\|w\|_{H^m}^2
\qquad\text{for all }w\in H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d).
\end{equation*}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
For simplicity we assume $B=a_mD^m$ (additional lower derivatives are treated analogously).
It is straightforward to see $\partial_{x_i}B^*h=B^*\partial_{x_i}h+(D^m)^*[(\partial_{x_i}a_m^*)h]$ for any function $h$,
where $a_m^*$ is the adjoint of the tensor $a_m$.
Thus, for $g\in H^{m+1}(\mathbb{T}^d)$ we have
\begin{equation*}
\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\!\!gw^T\partial_{x_i\!}\mathcal{L} w\,\mathrm{d} x
\!=\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\!\!gw\cdot\partial_{x_i\!}(\bar\mathcal{L} w)\,\mathrm{d} x
+\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\!\! \partial_{x_i\!}Bw\cdot B(gw)+D^m(gw)(\partial_{x_i\!}a_m^*)\cdot Bw\,\mathrm{d} x.
\end{equation*}
The first term is clearly bounded in absolute value by a constant times $\|gw\|_{H^m}\|\partial_{x_i}\bar\mathcal{L} w\|_{H^{-m}}\lesssim\|w\|_{H^m}^2\|g\|_{H^m}$ (recall that $H^m$ is a Banach algebra).
Furthermore, by the product rule $B(gw)$ equals $(Bw)g$ plus a sum $S$ of products of derivatives of $w$ and $g$, each summand with $m$ derivatives in total, at least one of them on $g$.
Thus,
\begin{align*}
\int_{\mathbb{T}^d} \partial_{x_i}Bw\cdot B(gw)\,\mathrm{d} x
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^d} g\,\partial_{x_i}\tfrac{|Bw|^2}2\,\mathrm{d} x
+\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}S\cdot\partial_{x_i}Bw\,\mathrm{d} x\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\tfrac{|Bw|^2}2\partial_{x_i}g\,\mathrm{d} x
+\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}S\cdot\partial_{x_i}Bw\,\mathrm{d} x.
\end{align*}
The first integral is bounded by $\|Bw\|_{H^0}^2\|\partial_{x_i}g\|_{W^{0,\infty}}\lesssim\|w\|_{H^m}^2\|g\|_{H^{m+1}}$,
the second by $\|\partial_{x_i}Bw\|_{H^{-1}}\|S\|_{H^1}\lesssim\|w\|_{H^m}^2\|g\|_{H^{m+1}}$ (using \cref{thm:BeHo21}).
Similarly,
\begin{multline*}
\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}D^m(gw)(\partial_{x_i}a_m^*)\cdot Bw\,\mathrm{d} x\right|
\leq\|Bw\|_{H^0}\|\partial_{x_i}a_m\|_{W^{0,\infty}}\|D^m(gw)\|_{H^0}\\
\lesssim\|w\|_{H^m}\|gw\|_{H^m}
\lesssim\|w\|_{H^m}^2\|g\|_{H^m}.
\end{multline*}
Hence, $\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^d} gw^T\partial_{x_i}\mathcal{L} w\,\mathrm{d} x\right|\lesssim\|w\|_{H^m}^2\|g\|_{H^{m+1}}$ for all $g\in H^{m+1}(\mathbb{T}^d)$ and all coordinates $i$
so that the result follows from $w^TD\mathcal{L} w=\sum_{i=1}^dw^T\partial_{x_i}\mathcal{L} w$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:convergence}
Let $m>\frac d2+1$ and $\mathcal{L}$ be a Fourier multiplier satisfying the conditions from \cref{thm:smoothMetric} for $l=2$ (so that geodesics are well-posed in $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$).
Let $v_t$ be the velocity of a geodesic in $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{id}}^m$ and $V_t$ the solution of \eqref{eqn:LDDMMapproximateLie} with $V_0=\mathcal{T}_{R} v_0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
If $v_0\in H^{m+1}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$, then $\|V_t-v_t\|_{H^m}\to_{R\to\infty}0$ uniformly in $t\in[0,1]$.
\item
If $v_0\in H^{m+k}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ for $k>1$, then
\begin{equation*}
\|V_t-v_t\|_{H^m}\leq Ce^{C\|v_0\|_{H^{m+1}}e^{C\|v_0\|_{H^m}}}\|v_0\|_{H^{m+k}}R^{1-k}
\end{equation*}
for all $t\in[0,1]$ with a constant $C>0$ independent of $v_0$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Extend $\widetilde\mathrm{ad}$ to $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\times H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ by $\widetilde\mathrm{ad}=\mathcal{T}_{R}\mathrm{ad}$, which is still antisymmetric.
Let $e_t=V_t-v_t$.
We have
\begin{equation*}
\dot\rho_t=-\mathrm{ad}_{v_t}^*\rho_t,\qquad
\dot P_t=-\mathcal{T}_{R}\widetilde\mathrm{ad}_{V_t}^*P_t.
\end{equation*}
We obtain
\begin{multline*}
\frac\mathrm{d}{\mathrm{d} t}\frac{\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}^2}2
=\langle e_t,\mathcal{L}\dot e_t\rangle
=\langle\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t,\mathcal{T}_{R}\mathcal{L}\dot e_t\rangle
+\langle({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})e_t,({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})\mathcal{L}\dot e_t\rangle\\
=\langle\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t,\mathcal{L}\dot e_t\rangle
+\langle({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t,({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})\dot\rho_t\rangle,
\end{multline*}
where we exploited $({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})e_t=-({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t$.
We estimate the first term as follows,
\begin{multline*}
\langle\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t,\mathcal{L}\dot e_t\rangle
=\langle\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t,\mathrm{ad}_{v_t}^*\rho_t-\mathcal{T}_{R}\widetilde\mathrm{ad}_{V_t}^*P_t\rangle\\
=\langle[V_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t]_R,P_t\rangle-\langle[v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t],\rho_t\rangle
=\langle[V_t-v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t],P_t\rangle+\langle[v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t],P_t-\rho_t\rangle\\
=-\langle[({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t],P_t\rangle+(\!([v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t],\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t)\!)_{H^m}-(\!([v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t],({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t)\!)_{H^m},
\end{multline*}
exploiting $[\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t]=0$ and therefore $[e_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t]=[({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})e_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t]=-[({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t]$.
Using that $H^m$ is a Banach algebra as well as \cref{thm:consistencyTruncatedFourier} and \eqref{eqn:normEstimateTruncation} we next estimate
\begin{align*}
\|[({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t]\|_{H^m}
&=\|D\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t\,({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t-D({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t]\|_{H^m}\\
&\lesssim\|({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m}}\|\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t\|_{H^{m+1}}+\|({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}\|\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t\|_{H^m}\\
&\lesssim\tfrac{\|({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}}RR\|\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t\|_{H^{m}}+\|({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}\|\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t\|_{H^{m}}\\
&\leq2\|({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}\|\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t\|_{H^{m}}.
\end{align*}
Therefore, exploiting \cref{thm:normPreservation} we have
\begin{multline*}
-\langle[({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t],P_t\rangle
\leq\|[({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t]\|_{H^m}\|P_t\|_{H^{-m}}\\
\lesssim\|({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}\vvvertV_t\vvvert_{H^m}
=\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}\vvvertV_0\vvvert_{H^m}\|({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}\\
\leq\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m}\|({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}.
\end{multline*}
Furthermore, an integration by parts yields
\begin{align*}
(\!([q,u],w)\!)_{H^m}
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^d} (\mathcal{L} w)^T(Du\,q-Dq\,u)\,\mathrm{d} x\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^d} \mathcal{L} w\cdot u\,\mathrm{div} q+u^TD(\mathcal{L} w)q+(\mathcal{L} w)^TDq\,u\,\mathrm{d} x
                                                  
\end{align*}
for any $u\in H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ and $q,w\in H^{m+1}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$.
Therefore we have
\begin{multline*}
(\!([v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t],\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t)\!)_{H^m}\\
=-\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\!\!\!\mathcal{L}\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t\!\cdot\!\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t\mathrm{div} v_t\!+\!\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t^TD(\mathcal{L}\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t)v_t\!+\!(\mathcal{L}\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t)^T\!Dv_t\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t\,\mathrm{d} x
\lesssim\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}^2\|v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}
\end{multline*}
by \cref{thm:productsInB} as well as
\begin{align*}
&-(\!([v_t,\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t],({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t)\!)_{H^m}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^d}\!\!\!\mathcal{L}({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\!\cdot\! \mathcal{T}_{R} e_t\mathrm{div} v_t\!+\!\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t^TD(\mathcal{L}({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t)v_t\!+\!(\mathcal{L}({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t)^T\!Dv_t\mathcal{T}_{R} e_t\,\mathrm{d} x\\
&\lesssim\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}\|v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}\|({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}.
\end{align*}
Summarizing,
\begin{multline}\label{eqn:ODEestimate}
\frac\mathrm{d}{\mathrm{d} t}\frac{\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}^2}2
\lesssim\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m}\|({\mathcal{I}}\!-\!\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}
+\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}^2\|v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}\\
+\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}\|v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}\|({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}
+\|({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}\|({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})\dot v_t\|_{H^{m-1}},
\end{multline}
where the last summand is an upper bound for $\langle({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t,({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})\dot\rho_t\rangle$.
(Note that if we had substituted $\langle({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t,({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})\dot\rho_t\rangle=\langle({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t,-\mathrm{ad}_{v_t}^*\rho_t\rangle=\langle[v_t,({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t],\rho_t\rangle$,
the smallness of the term from two Fourier truncations rather than one would no longer be obvious.)
By assumption and \cref{thm:regularityPreservationLDDMM}, $\|v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}$ is uniformly bounded.
Furthermore, from \cref{thm:rhsRegularity} we see
\begin{equation*}
\|\dot v_t\|_{H^{m-1}}
\lesssim\vvvertv_t\vvvert_{H^m}^2
=\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m}^2.
\end{equation*}
Since $t\mapsto v_t\in H^{m+1}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ is continuous by \cref{thm:regularityPreservationLDDMM},
$\|({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}$ tends to zero uniformly in $t$ as $R\to\infty$ due to \cref{thm:FourierDecay}.
Since also $e_0\to0$ in $H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$, the Bihari--LaSalle inequality implies $e_t\to0$ as $R\to\infty$, uniformly in $t$, which proves the first claim.

For the second claim                   
                   
                          
                                     
                                                                                              
                                                                                                                                       
                                                                                                                                                        
                                                
                 
                                                        
      
we collect the occurring powers of $R$; we start with
\begin{equation*}
\vvverte_0\vvvert_{H^m}
=\vvvert({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_0\vvvert_{H^m}
\lesssim R^{-k}\|v_0\|_{H^{m+k}}.
\end{equation*}
Furthermore, denoting by $\phi_t$ the flow of $v_t$, we have
\begin{multline*}
\|({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})v_t\|_{H^{m+1}}
\lesssim R^{1-k}\|v_t\|_{H^{m+k}}
\lesssim R^{1-k}\|\rho_t\|_{H^{-m+k}}
=R^{1-k}\|\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^{-1}}^*\rho_0\|_{H^{-m+k}}\\
                                                               
\leq R^{1-k}\exp(C\|v\|_{L^1(I;H^m)})\|\rho_0\|_{H^{-m+k}}
=R^{1-k}\exp(C\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m})\|v_0\|_{H^{m+k}},
\end{multline*}
where in the first step we used \cref{thm:consistencyTruncatedFourier},
in the second $\|\rho_t\|_{H^{-m+k}}=\|\mathcal{L} v_t\|_{H^{-m+k}}\lesssim\|v_t\|_{H^{m+k}}$,
in the third \cref{thm:regularityPreservationLDDMM},
in the fourth \cref{thm:normEstDeform}\eqref{enm:adjointMapEstimate} to estimate the operator norm of $\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t^{-1}}$,
and in the last step we used that the norm $\vvvertv_t\vvvert_{H^m}$ of the velocity is constant along a geodesic.
Similarly,
\begin{multline*}
\|({\mathcal{I}}-\mathcal{T}_{R})\dot v_t\|_{H^{m-1}}
\lesssim R^{-k}\|\dot v_t\|_{H^{m+k-1}}
\lesssim R^{-k}\|\rho_t\|_{H^{-m+k}}\vvvertv_t\vvvert_{H^m}\\
\leq R^{-k}\exp(C\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m})\|v_0\|_{H^{m+k}}\vvvertv_t\vvvert_{H^m}
=R^{-k}\exp(C\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m})\|v_0\|_{H^{m+k}}\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m}
\end{multline*}
using \cref{thm:rhsRegularity} in the second step. In summary, \eqref{eqn:ODEestimate} turns into
\begin{align*}
\frac\mathrm{d}{\mathrm{d} t}\frac{\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}^2}2
&\lesssim\|v_0\|_{H^{m+1}}\exp(2C\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m})\cdot\\
&\quad\left(\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}^2+R^{1-k}\|v_0\|_{H^{m+k}}\sqrt{\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}^2}+R^{-2k+1}\tfrac{\|v_0\|_{H^{m}}\|v_0\|_{H^{m+k}}^2}{\|v_0\|_{H^{m+1}}}\right)\\
                                                                                                       
                                                                             
&\lesssim\|v_0\|_{H^{m+1}}\exp(2C\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m})\left(\vvverte_t\vvvert_{H^m}^2+R^{2-2k}\|v_0\|_{H^{m+k}}^2\right)
\end{align*}
via Young's inequality,
which together with the estimate for $\vvverte_0\vvvert_{H^m}$ yields the desired estimate via the Gronwall inequality.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:convergenceGenericRegularity}
Let the conditions of \cref{thm:convergence} hold, and let $r\in o(\log R)$.
Given $v_0\in H^m(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$, let $V^r\in\mathcal{T}_{R} H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ denote the solution of \eqref{eqn:LDDMMapproximateLie} for initial condition $V^r_0=\mathcal{T}_{r}v_0$.
Then $\|V^r_t-v_t\|_{H^m}\to0$ uniformly in $t\in[0,1]$ as $R\to\infty$.

Moreover, if $v_0\in H^{m+1}(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ and the metric \eqref{eqn:metric} is three times differentiable,
then $\|V^r_t-v_t\|_{H^m}\lesssim C(\|v_0\|_{H^{m+1}})r$ for all $t\in[0,1]$ for a constant depending only on $\|v_0\|_{H^{m+1}}$.

<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Denote by $v^r$ the geodesic with initial velocity $v^r_0=V^r_0=\mathcal{T}_{r}v_0$.
We can estimate
\begin{equation*}
\|V^r_t-v_t\|_{H^m}
\leq\|V^r_t-v^r_t\|_{H^m}+\|v^r_t-v_t\|_{H^m}.
\end{equation*}
By \cref{thm:convergence}, for $k\geq2$ we have
\begin{align}
\|V^r_t-v^r_t\|_{H^m}
&\leq Ce^{C\|v_0^r\|_{H^{m+1}}e^{C\|v_0^r\|_{H^m}}}\|v_0^r\|_{H^{m+k}}R^{1-k}\nonumber\\
&\leq Ce^{Cr\|v_0\|_{H^{m}}e^{C\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m}}}r^k\|v_0\|_{H^{m}}R^{1-k},\label{eqn:rR_estimate}
\end{align}
which for $r\in o(\log R)$ tends to zero as $R\to\infty$.
Likewise, $\|v^r_t-v_t\|_{H^m}\to0$ uniformly in $t\in[0,1]$ due to $\|v^r_0-v_0\|_{H^m}\to0$ and the continuity of the Riemannian exponential map.

If the metric is three times differentiable, the Riemannian exponential is even differentiable so that $\|v^r_t-v_t\|_{H^m}\leq C(\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m})\|v^r_0-v_0\|_{H^m}\leq\tilde C(\|v_0\|_{H^{m+1}})r$
for constants $C(\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m}),\tilde C(\|v_0\|_{H^{m+1}})$ depending only on the norm of $v_0$.
Furthermore, the right-hand side of \eqref{eqn:rR_estimate} tends to zero faster than $R^{\frac32-k}$ (with a constant depending on $\vvvertv_0\vvvert_{H^m}$),
which implies the second claim.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
The arguments hold verbatim also when replacing the domain $\mathbb{T}^d$ by $\mathbb{R}^d$ and interpreting $\mathcal{T}_{R}$ as the truncation operator on continuous Fourier space.
However, this is of course of little numerical relevance since the resulting truncated ODE \eqref{eqn:geodesicEquationLDDMMFourier} is still infinite-dimensional.

In certain situations the geodesic ODE on $\mathbb{R}^d$ can actually be reduced to the geodesic ODE on $\mathbb{T}^d$.
Indeed, an integral of \eqref{eqn:EPDiff} with momentum $\rho$, velocity $v$ and flow $\phi$ is given by $\rho_t=\mathrm{Ad}_{\phi_t}^*\rho_0$,
from which one can quickly see that if $\rho_0$ has compact support, then so does $\rho_t$ for all $t\in[0,1]$ \cite[Sec.\,4.3.1]{MiTrYo06}.
By rescaling and shifting coordinates we may assume $\rho_t$ to stay supported within $[0,1)^d$.
Hence, by appropriately choosing the operator $\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{T}^d}$ (the Riesz isomorphism to be used on the torus; the subscript distinguishes it from the original operator $\mathcal{R}$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$)
one obtains the identical evolution of $\rho_t$ on $\mathbb{T}^d$ as on $\mathbb{R}^d$:
Operator $\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{T}^d}$ would be the composition of an extension (of $\rho_t$) from $[0,1)^d$ to $\mathbb{R}^d$ by zero
(in Fourier space this would mean to interpret the discrete Fourier sum as a sum of Dirac measures and convolving this measure with the Fourier transform $\hat\chi$ of the characteristic function $\chi$ of $[0,1)^d$),
an application of $\mathcal{R}$ (which then yields $v_t$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$),
a restriction (of $v_t$) to $[0,1)^d$ (in Fourier space another convolution with $\hat\chi$),
and a reinterpretation as periodic function (by the Poisson summation formula, the discrete Fourier coefficients would just be the sampling of the continuous ones).
Unfortunately, our error analysis only applies in this setting if $\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{T}^d}$ is a Fourier multiplier, which for natural choices (such as $\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{L}^{-1}$ with $\mathcal{L}=(1-\Delta)^m$) is not the case.

For initial momentum of unbounded support the situation of course becomes even more involved.
For a numerical approximation one would need to periodize momentum or velocity (typically via the Poisson summation formula)
in addition to truncating in Fourier space and then analyze the error inflicted by both.
A possible direction would be to truncate $\rho_0$ in space and investigate the resulting error,
after which in a second step the above reduction from $\mathbb{R}^d$ to $\mathbb{T}^d$ is applied.
