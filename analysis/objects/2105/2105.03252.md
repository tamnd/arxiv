<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:semi-cat}
  Recall that a \emph{semi-category} is like a category, but lacks
  identity morphisms. A semi-category is \emph{thin} if there is at
  most one morphism between any pair of objects. Thus a small thin
  semi-category is the same thing as a set $\kappa$ (the set of
  objects) equipped with a transitive relation
  $\_<\_ \subseteq \kappa\times\kappa$ (the existence-of-a-morphism
  relation). Given such a $(\kappa,{<})$, a \emph{diagram}
  $D:\kappa\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$ in a category $\mathcal{C}$ is by definition a semi-functor
  from $\kappa$ to $\mathcal{C}$: thus $D$ maps each $i\in\kappa$ to a
  $\mathcal{C}$-object $D_i$, each pair $(j,i)$ with $j<i$ to a $\mathcal{C}$-morphism
  $D_{j,i}: D_j\rightarrow D_i$, and these morphisms satisfy
  $D_{j,i}\circ D_{k,j} = D_{k,i}$ for all $k<j<i$ in $\kappa$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:size}
  A \emph{size} is a small thin
  semi-category $(\kappa,{<})$ that is
  \begin{itemize}

  \item \emph{directed}: every finite subset of $\kappa$ has an upper
    bound with respect to $<$; specifically, we assume we are given a
    distinguished element $0^s\in\kappa$ and a binary operation
    $\_\mathbin{\sqcup^s}\_:\kappa\times\kappa\rightarrow\kappa$ satisfying
    $\forall i,j\in\kappa.\; i< {i \mathbin{\sqcup^s} j}
    \;\wedge\; j < {i\mathbin{\sqcup^s} j}$

  \item \emph{well-founded}: for all $K\subseteq \kappa$, if
    $\forall i\in\kappa.(\forall j< i.\; j \in K)
    \Rightarrow i\in K$, then $K = \kappa$.
    \qedhere
  \end{itemize}
  Note that the directedness property in particular gives a successor
  operation $\mathop{\uparrow^s} : \kappa\rightarrow\kappa$ on the elements of a size,
  defined by $\mathop{\uparrow^s} i \triangleq i\mathbin{\sqcup^s} i$ and satisfying
  $\forall i\in\kappa.\; i < \mathop{\uparrow^s} i$. (We do not need a successor
  that also preserves $<$, although the sizes constructed in the next
  section have one that does so.)

\label{rem:wf-rec}
  Since we are working constructively, the well-foundedness property
  of a size is stated in a suitably positive form; classically, it is
  equivalent to the non-existence of infinite descending chains for
  $<$. Well-foundedness of $<$ allows one to define size-indexed
  families by \emph{well-founded
    recursion}~\cite[section~6.3]{TaylorP:prafm}: given a size
  $\kappa$ and a $\kappa$-indexed family of sets

  $(f_i:(\prod_{j<i}A_j)\rightarrow
  A_i)_{i\in\kappa}$ we get a family of elements
  $(a_i\in A_i)_{i\in\kappa}$, uniquely defined by the
  requirement
  $\forall i\in\kappa.\; a_i=
  f_i((a_j)_{j<i})$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:infl-iter}
  Given an endofunctor $F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$ on a cocomplete category $\mathcal{C}$, for
  each size $\kappa$ an inflationary iteration of $F$ over $\kappa$
  exists (and is unique).
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Given $i\in\kappa$, say that a diagram $D:{\downarrow}(i)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$ is an
  inflationary iteration of $F$ \emph{up to} $i$ if for all $j<i$,
  $D_j = \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{k<j}F(D_k)$ and
  $\forall k<j.\; D_{k,j}= \mathrm{c}^{F\circ D}_{k,j}$.  Note that given such
  a diagram, for any $j<i$ we have that $D|_j:{\downarrow}(j)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$ is
  an inflationary iteration of $F$ up to $j$. Using well-founded
  induction for $<$, one can prove that
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:upto-unique}
    \forall i\in\kappa,\;\text{any two inflationary iterations of $F$
      up to $i$ are
      equal}
  \end{equation}
  Then one can use well-founded recursion for $<$
  (\cref{rem:wf-rec}) to define for each $i\in\kappa$ an
  inflationary iteration of $F$ up to $i$,
  $D^{(i)}:{\downarrow}(i)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$. If $j<i$, then
  $D^{(j)}$ and $D^{(i)}|_j$ are both inflationary
  iterations of $F$ up to $j$ and so are equal by
  \eqref{eq:upto-unique}. From this it follows that
  \[
    (D_i \triangleq \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{j<i}
    F(D^{(i)}_j))_{i\in\kappa} \;\wedge\;
    (D_{j,i} \triangleq \mathrm{c}^{F\circ
      D^{(i)}}_{j,i})_{j,i\in\kappa\mid
      j<i}
  \]
  defines an inflationary iteration of $F$. (Furthermore, since any
  such restricts to an up-to-$i$ inflationary iteration, uniqueness
  follows from \eqref{eq:upto-unique}.)

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:infl-iter}
  Suppose $\mathcal{C}$ is a cocomplete category, $F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$ is an
  endofunctor and there is a size $\kappa$ such that $F$ preserves
  colimits of diagrams $\kappa\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$. Then $F$ has an initial algebra
  whose underlying $\mathcal{C}$-object is the colimit
  $\mu F = \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}\mu_i F$ of the inflationary iteration
  $\mu_i F$ (\cref{def:infl-iter}) of $F$ over $\kappa$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By \cref{lem:infl-iter} there is an inflationary iteration of
  $F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$ over $\kappa$; call it $D:\kappa\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$ and define
  $\mu F \triangleq \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}D_i$. For each $i\in\kappa$, as in
  \cref{def:size} we have $\mathop{\uparrow^s} i\in\kappa$ with
  $i < \mathop{\uparrow^s} i$ and hence a $\mathcal{C}$-morphism
  \[
    \iota_i \triangleq
    \left(F(D_i)\xrightarrow{\iota_{i,\mathop{\uparrow^s} i}}
      D_{\mathop{\uparrow^s} i} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{inc}^D_{\mathop{\uparrow^s} i}}
      \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}D_i = \mu F\right)
  \]
  By \eqref{eq:iota-props}, $(\iota_i)_{i\in\kappa}$ is a
  cocone under the diagram $F\circ D : \kappa\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$ and so induces
  $\hat{\iota}: \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}F(D_i) \rightarrow \mu F$.  Then
  since $F$ preserves the colimit of $D$, we get a morphism
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:iota-def}
    \iota \triangleq \left(F(\mu F) = F(\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}D_i) \cong
     \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa} F(D_i) \xrightarrow{\hat{\iota}}
     \mu F\right)
  \end{equation}
  Therefore $\mu F$ has the structure of an $F$-algebra. To see that
  it is initial, suppose we are given $a:F(A)\rightarrow A$. We have to show
  that there is a unique $F$-algebra morphism
  $(\mu F, \iota)\rightarrow (A,a)$.

  If $h:\mu F \rightarrow A$ is such an algebra morphism, that is
  $h\circ\iota = a\circ F(h)$, then by definition of $\iota$ in
  \eqref{eq:iota-def} it follows that the associated cocone
  $(h_i \triangleq h \circ \mathrm{inc}^D_i:D_i\rightarrow
  A)_{i\in\kappa}$ satisfies
  $h_{\mathop{\uparrow^s} i}\circ \iota_{i,\mathop{\uparrow^s} i}= a\circ
  F(h_i)$. From this, using the directedness property of sizes,
  we get
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:upto-morphism}
    \forall i\in\kappa.\forall j<i.\; 
    h_i\circ \iota_{j,i} = a\circ F(h_i\circ
    D_{j,i})
  \end{equation}
  So if $h$ and $h'$ are both $F$-algebra morphisms
  $(\mu F, \iota)\rightarrow (A,a)$, one can prove by well-founded induction
  for $<$, using \eqref{eq:iota-ext} and \eqref{eq:upto-morphism},
  that
  $\forall i\in\kappa.\; h\circ \mathrm{inc}^D_i =
  h'\circ\mathrm{inc}^D_i$ and hence that $h=h'$.

  So it just remains to prove that there is such an $h$. It suffices
  to construct a cocone $(h_i:D_i\rightarrow A)_{i\in\kappa}$ satisfying
  \eqref{eq:upto-morphism} and then take $h$ to be the morphism given
  by the universal property of the colimit; for then we have
  $\forall i\in\kappa.\; h_{\mathop{\uparrow^s} i}\circ \iota_{i,\mathop{\uparrow^s} i} =
  a\circ F(h_i)$ and hence $h\circ\iota = a\circ F(h)$, as required.

  For each $i\in\kappa$, say that a morphism $h':D_i\rightarrow A$ is an
  \emph{up-to-$i$ algebra morphism} if
  $\forall j<i.\;h'\circ\iota_{j,i} = a\circ F(h'\circ D_{j,i})$
  (\emph{cf}.~\eqref{eq:upto-morphism}). Given such a morphism, then
  for any $j<i$, $h'\circ D_{j,i}:D_j\rightarrow A$ is an up-to-$j$ algebra
  morphism. From this it follows by well-founded induction for $<$
  that any two up-to-$i$ algebra morphisms are equal. A well-founded
  recursion for $<$ allows one to construct an up-to-$i$ algebra
  morphism $h_i:D_i\rightarrow A$ for each $i\in\kappa$; and the uniqueness
  of up-to algebra morphisms implies that $h_j=h_i\circ D_{j,i}$ when
  $j<i$. Thus $(h_i)_{i\in\kappa}$ is the required cocone satisfying
  \eqref{eq:upto-morphism}.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:filtered}
  A \emph{signature} (also known as a
  \emph{container}~\cite{abbott2005containers,GambinoN:welftd}) is
  specified by a set $A\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ and an $A$-indexed family of sets
  $B\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}^A$. We write $\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}} \in \ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}_1$ for the large set of all
  such signatures.  Given $\Sigma=(A,B)\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$, we say that a size
  $(\kappa,{<})$ is \emph{$\Sigma$-filtered} if for all $a\in A$ and
  every function $f:B(a) \rightarrow \kappa$, there exists $i\in\kappa$ with
  $\forall x\in B(a).\; f(x) < i$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:plump-size}
  There is a $\Sigma$-filtered size $(\kappa_\Sigma , {<})$ for every
  signature $\Sigma$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Given a signature $\Sigma=(A,B)$, we extend it to a
  signature $(A',B')$ by adding fresh nullary and binary operation
  symbols. Thus $A' \triangleq A\uplus\{n,b\}$ and $B'\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}^{A'}$
  satisfies $B'(a) \triangleq B(a)$ for $a\in A$, $B'(n)\triangleq \emptyset$
  and $B'(b)\triangleq \{0,1\}$. Let set $\kappa_\Sigma$ be the W-type
  $W_{(A',B')}$ and let $<$ be the plump order given by
  \eqref{eq:plump}. As noted above, $<$ is transitive and well-founded
  and has upper bounds for any arity-indexed family and hence in
  particular it is $\Sigma$-filtered. It just remains to see that it
  is directed (\cref{def:size}). Since $A'$ contains the
  nullary operation symbol $n$, $\kappa_\Sigma$ contains
  $0^s\triangleq \sup_n\emptyset$; and given $i,j\in\kappa_\Sigma$,
  letting $f:B'(b)=\{0,1\}\rightarrow \kappa_\Sigma$ map $0$ to $i$ and $1$
  to $j$, then $i\mathbin{\sqcup^s} j \triangleq \sup_b f$ is an upper bound for $i$ and
  $j$ with respect to $<$.

<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{def:sig-sum}
  Suppose $\Sigma_c=(A_c,B_c)$ is a family of signatures indexed by
  the elements $c$ of some set $C$. Then the \emph{signature sum}
  $\bigoplus_{c\in C}\Sigma_c$ is the signature $(A,B)$ where
  $A\triangleq \sum_{c\in C}A_c = \{(c,a) \mid c\in C \wedge a \in A_c\}$
  and $B\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}^A$ maps each $(c,a)$ to the set $B_c(a)$.  As a
  special case when $I=\{0,1\}$, we have the \emph{binary sum}
  $\Sigma_0\oplus\Sigma_1$. There is also an \emph{empty signature}
  $0=(\emptyset,\emptyset)$ which acts as a unit for $\oplus$ up to
  isomorphism (for a suitable notion of signature morphism).

\label{rem:sig-sum}

  is also $\Sigma_c$-filtered for each $c\in C$. Conversely, given a
  single signature $\Sigma$, if a size is $\Sigma$-filtered, it is
  also $(\bigoplus_{c\in C}\Sigma)$-filtered.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:sized-closure}
  Suppose that  $\mathcal{C}$,$\mathcal{D}$ and $\mathcal{E}$ are cocomplete categories.
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item\label{item:0} Any cocontinuous functor $\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}$ is sized.

  \item\label{item:2} Identity functors are sized. If $F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}$ and
    $G:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}$ are sized, so is their composition
    $G\circ F: \mathcal{C}\rightarrow \mathcal{E}$.
    
  \item\label{item:3} The terminal functor $\mathcal{C}\rightarrow 1$ and the
    projection functors $\pi_1:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow \mathcal{C}$ and
    $\pi_2:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow \mathcal{C}$ are sized; if $F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow \mathcal{D}$ and
    $G:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow \mathcal{E}$ are sized, then so is $\langle F, G\rangle:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow
    \mathcal{D}\times \mathcal{E}$.

  \item\label{item:4} For any $X\in\mathcal{C}$ the constant functor $1\rightarrow \mathcal{C}$
    with value $X$ is sized.
  \end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For part~\ref{item:0}, if $F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}$ is cocontinuous, then it is
  $\Sigma$-sized for any $\Sigma$ and in particular for the empty
  signature.

  The first sentence of part~\ref{item:2} follows from
  part~\ref{item:0}. If $F$ is $\Sigma$-sized and $G$ is
  $\Sigma'$-sized, then $F$ and $G$ both preserve colimits over any
  $\Sigma\oplus\Sigma'$-filtered size, because such a size is also
  $\Sigma$-{} and $\Sigma'$-filtered. The composition $G\circ F$
  preserves such a colimit because $F$ and $G$ do. Therefore $G\circ F$
  is  $\Sigma\oplus\Sigma'$-sized.
  
  For part~\ref{item:3} we use the fact that colimits in a product
  category are computed componentwise. Thus the terminal and
  projection functors are sized by part~\ref{item:0}; and if
  $F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow \mathcal{D}$ is $\Sigma$-sized and  
  $G:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow \mathcal{E}$ is $\Sigma'$-sized, then $\langle F, G\rangle$ is
  $\Sigma\oplus\Sigma'$-sized.

  For part~\ref{item:4}, note that each size $\kappa$ is directed and
  hence in particular is a connected semi-category; therefore
  $\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa} X$ is canonically isomorphic to $X$. So the
  constant functor with value $X$ is $\Sigma$-sized for any $\Sigma$
  and in particular for the empty signature.

<!-- axiom-1: body_md -->
\label{axi:wisc}
  A (possibly large) \emph{cover} of a set $X\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ is a surjective
  function $f:Y\twoheadrightarrow X$ with $Y\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}_1$.  An indexed
  family\relax{We will refer to elements of $\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$ as
    \emph{families} rather than \emph{signatures} when we are not
    thinking of them as collections of operation symbols of
    set-valued arity.}  $(E_c)_{c\in C} \in \ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$ is a \emph{wisc} for
  $X\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ if for any cover $f:Y\twoheadrightarrow X$, there exist $c\in C$ and
  $g : E_c \rightarrow Y$ such that $f\circ g$ is surjective.  The [\textsc{wisc}]{}
  axiom\relax{For simplicity and following \cite{StreicherT:reamc},
    we have given the axiom just for a pair of universes,
    $(\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}_0,\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}_1)$; more generally one can ask for the property
    to hold for any pair $(\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}_m,\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}_n)$.}  states that for every
  $X\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ there exists a family $(E_c)_{c\in C}\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$ that is a
  wisc for it.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:fam-size}
  Suppose [\textsc{wisc}]{} holds and that $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{D}$ are cocomplete
  categories.  If $(F_x:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D})_{x\in X}$ is a family of
  sized functors indexed by a set $X\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$, then there exists a
  signature $\Sigma\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$ such that $F_x$ is $\Sigma$-sized for all
  $x\in X$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Consider the large set
  $S \triangleq \sum_{x\in X}\{\Sigma'\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}} \mid \text{$F_x$ is a
    $\Sigma'$-sized functor}\}$ in $\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}_1$. By assumption on $F$, the
  first projection $\pi_1:S\rightarrow X$ is a large\relax{This proof,
    as well as that for \cref{lem:wisc}, illustrates the need for a
    wisc property that quantifies over large covers of small sets.}
  cover of $X$. By [\textsc{wisc}]{} there is some surjection $e:X'\twoheadrightarrow X$ in
  $\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ and a function $\Sigma':X'\rightarrow \ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$ so that for all
  $x'\in X'$, the functor $F_{e(x')}$ is $\Sigma'_{x'}$-sized; and
  since $e$ is surjective this implies that each $F_x$ is
  $\Sigma'_{x'}$-sized for some $x'\in X'$. Consider the signature
  $\Sigma \triangleq \bigoplus_{x'\in X'}\Sigma'_{x'}$ from
  \cref{def:sig-sum}. By \cref{rem:sig-sum}, each $F_x$ is
  $\Sigma$-sized.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:colim}
  Suppose that [\textsc{wisc}]{} holds, $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{D}$ are cocomplete categories,
  $\mathbf{C}$ is a small category and that $F:\mathbf{C}\times\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}$ is a
  functor. If for some signature $\Sigma\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$ the functor $F(c,\_)$
  is $\Sigma$-sized for each $c\in\mathbf{C}$, then
  $\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{c\in\mathbf{C}}F(c,\_):\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}$ is also $\Sigma$-sized. More
  generally, if each $F(c,\_)$ is sized, then so is
  $\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{c\in\mathbf{C}}F(c,\_)$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
If $F(c,\_):\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}$ is $\Sigma$-sized for all $c\in\mathbf{C}$ and
  $\kappa$ is a $\Sigma$-filtered size, then each $F(c,\_)$ preserves
  colimits of all diagrams $\kappa\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$. Thus given such a diagram
  $D:\kappa\rightarrow\mathcal{C}$, we have a canonical isomorphism
  $F(c,\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}D_i) \cong \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}F(c, D_i)$,
  natural in $c$. Taking the colimit over $c\in\mathbf{C}$ and writing
  $F' \triangleq \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{c\in\mathbf{C}}F(c,\_)$, we have
  $
    F'(\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}D_i) =
    \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{c\in\mathbf{C}}F(c,\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}D_i) \cong
    \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{c\in\mathbf{C}}\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa} F(c,D_i)
  $.
  Since colimits commute with each other, it follows that the
  canonical morphism
  $F'(\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}D_i)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}F'(D_i)$ is an
  isomorphism. Therefore $F'$ is $\Sigma$-sized. The last sentence of
  the theorem follows by \cref{lem:fam-size}.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:mu}
  Suppose [\textsc{wisc}]{} holds and that $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{D}$ are cocomplete
  categories.  If $F:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}$ is sized, then there is a
  function $X\mapsto \mu Y.F(X,Y)$ assigning to each $X\in\mathcal{C}$ an
  initial algebra $\mu Y.F(X,Y)$ for the functor $F(X,\_):\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}$.
  The induced functor $\mu Y.F(\_,Y):\mathcal{C}\rightarrow \mathcal{D}$ is sized. \qedhere
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Suppose $F:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}$ is $\Sigma$-sized. It follows from
  \cref{prop:sized-closure} and \cref{rem:sig-sum} that for each
  $X\in\mathcal{C}$, the functor $F_X \triangleq F(X,\_):\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}$ is
  $\Sigma$-sized. Therefore by \cref{thm:infl-iter,thm:sized-endof},
  the function
  $X\mapsto \mu Y.F(X,Y)\triangleq \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in \kappa_\Sigma}\mu_i F_X$
  is the required function mapping each $X\in\mathcal{C}$ to an initial algebra
  for $F(X,\_)$. Since each $\mu_i F_X$ is
  $\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{j<i}F(X, \mu_j F_X)$, it follows by well-founded induction
  on $i\in\kappa_\Sigma$ that each $\mu_i F_X$ is $\Sigma$-sized,
  using \cref{thm:colim} (taking $\mathbf{C}$ to be the category generated
  by the thin semi-category ${\downarrow}(i)$). Then by
  \cref{thm:colim} again (taking $\mathbf{C}$ to be the
  category generated by $\kappa$) we have that
  $\mu Y.F(\_,Y) = \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in \kappa_\Sigma}\mu_i F_{\_}$ is
  $\Sigma$-sized.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:wisc}
  Assuming [\textsc{wisc}]{} holds, then for every family of sets
  $(X_i)_{i\in I}\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$ there exists a family
  $(E_c)_{c\in C}\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$ that is a wisc for each set $X_i$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Consider $S \triangleq \sum_{i\in I}\{W\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}} \mid \text{$W$ is a wisc
    for $X_i$}\} \in \ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}_1$. By [\textsc{wisc}]{}, the first projection
  $\pi_1:S\rightarrow I$ is a large cover of $I$. Since there is a wisc for
  $I$, it follows that there is some surjection $e:J\twoheadrightarrow I$ in
  $\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ and a function $W:J\rightarrow \ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$ so that for all $j\in J$,
  $W_j$ is a wisc for $X_{e(j)}$. Consider the signature sum
  $W\triangleq\bigoplus_{j\in J}W_j\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$ as in \cref{def:sig-sum}. Thus
  writing $(C,E)$ for $W$ and $(C_j,E_j)$ for each $W_j$, we have
  $C\triangleq \sum_{j\in J} C_j \in \ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ and $E\in \ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}^C$ is the
  function mapping each $(j,c)\in \sum_{j\in J} C_j$ to $E_j(c)$. Then
  we claim that $W\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Sig}}$ is a wisc for each set $X_i$. For,
  given any cover $f:Y\twoheadrightarrow X_i$, since $e:J\twoheadrightarrow I$ is a surjection,
  there exists $j\in J$ with $e(j)= i$; then since $W_j=(C_j,E_j)$ is a
  wisc for $X_{e(j)} = X_i$, there exists $c\in C_j$ and
  $g:E_j(c)\rightarrow Y$ such that $f\circ g$ is surjective. So there exists
  $(j,c)\in C$ and $g:E(j,c) = E_j(c) \rightarrow Y$ such that $f\circ g$ is
  surjective. Therefore $W=(C,E)$ does indeed have the wisc property for
  $X_i$.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:product}
  Suppose that $\mathcal{C}$ is a cocomplete category.  Assuming [\textsc{wisc}]{}
  holds, if $(F_x:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}})_{x\in X}$ is a family of sized functors
  indexed by some set $X\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$, then the functor
  $\prod_{x\in X}F_x : \mathcal{C}\rightarrow\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ given by taking products in
  $\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ is also sized.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
By \cref{lem:fam-size}, there exists a signature $\Sigma$ so that
  each functor $F_x$ is $\Sigma$-sized. However, we need a bigger
  signature than $\Sigma$ in order to prove that $\prod_{x\in X}F_x$
  is sized. Using [\textsc{wisc}]{}, let $W = (E_c)_{c\in C}$ be a wisc for
  $X$. Then using \cref{lem:wisc}, let $W' = (E'_{c'})_{c'\in C'}$ be
  a wisc for the sets in the family
  $(\ker p)_{c\in C, p: E_c\twoheadrightarrow X}$, where
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:product-0}
    \ker p \triangleq \{(d_1,d_2)\in E_c\times E_c \mid p(d_1) = p(d_2)\}
  \end{equation}
  We claim that the functor $F'\triangleq\prod_{x\in X}F_x$ is
  $\Sigma'$-sized when $\Sigma' = \Sigma \oplus W \oplus W'$ (using
  the signature sum from \cref{def:sig-sum}).

  If $D:\kappa\rightarrow \mathcal{C}$ is a diagram on a $\Sigma'$-filtered size
  $\kappa$, then by \cref{rem:sig-sum}, each $F_x$ is $\Sigma'$-sized
  and so we have a canonical isomorphism
  $\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa} F_x(D_i) \cong F_x(\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}
  D_i)$. Taking the product over $x\in X$, we get
  $\prod_{x\in X} \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa} F_x(D_i) \cong \prod_{x\in X}
  F_x(\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa} D_i) = (\prod_{x\in X}
  F_x)(\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa} D_i)$.  So it just remains to show that the
  canonical function
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:product-1}
    \textstyle
     \operatorname{\mathrm{can}}_{F,D} : \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}\left(\left(\prod_{x\in X}
        F_x\right) D_i\right) = \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}\prod_{x\in X}
    F_x(D_i) \rightarrow \prod_{x\in X}\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}F_x(D_i)
  \end{equation}
  is an isomorphism, that is, both an injection and a surjection. The
  summand $W$ in $\Sigma'$ ensures that $\kappa$ has upper bounds for
  $E_c$-indexed families for any $c\in C$; and the $W'$ summand
  ensures the same for $E'_{c'}$-indexed families, for any
  $c' \in C'$. The first kind of upper bound, together with the wisc
  property of $W$, comes into play in proving that $\operatorname{\mathrm{can}}_{F,D}$ is
  injective; and both kinds of upper bounds and the wisc property of
  $W$ and $W'$ come into play in proving that $\operatorname{\mathrm{can}}_{F,D}$ is
  surjective.

  To prove that $\operatorname{\mathrm{can}}_{F,D}$ is injective and surjective we use the
  fact that the colimit in $\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ of a directed diagram
  $D:\kappa\rightarrow\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ can be described explicitly as the quotient
  $(\sum_{i\in\kappa}D_i)/{\approx}$ where the equivalence relation
  $\approx$ identifies $(i,d),(i',d') \in \sum_{i\in\kappa}D_i$ if
  there is some $j\in\kappa$ with $i<j$, $i'<j$ and
  $D_{i,j}(d) = D_{i',j}(d')$. We will write $[i,d]_{\approx}$ for the
  ${\approx}$-equivalence class of $(i,d)\in
  \sum_{i\in\kappa}D_i$. Then the function in \cref{eq:product-1}
  satisfies for all $i\in \kappa$ and $f\in \prod_{x\in X} F_x(D_i)$
  \[
  \operatorname{\mathrm{can}}_{F,D}[i, f]_{\approx} = \lambda x\in X.\; [i,f(x)]_{\approx}
  \]
  To see that $\operatorname{\mathrm{can}}_{F,D}$ is injective, suppose we also have
  $i'\in \kappa$ and $f'\in \prod_{x\in X} F_x(D_i)$ satisfying
  $\forall x \in X.\; [i,f(x)]_{\approx} = [i',f'(x)]_{\approx}$; we
  wish to prove that $(i,f)\approx(i',f')$. By definition of $\approx$
  we have
  $\forall x\in X.\exists j\in\kappa.\; i < j \;\wedge\; i' < j
  \;\wedge\; F_x(D_{i,j})(f\,x) = F_x(D_{i',j})(f'x)$. Since
  $W = (E_c)_{c\in C}$ is a wisc for $X$, there exist $c\in C$, a
  surjection $p:E_c\twoheadrightarrow X$ and a function $q:E_c\rightarrow \kappa$ so that
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:product-2}
    \forall z\in E_c.\; i< q(z) \;\wedge\; i'< q(z) \;\wedge\;
    F_x(D_{i,q(z)})(f(p\,z)) = F_x(D_{i',q(z)})(f'(p\,z))
  \end{equation}
  Since $W$ is a summand in $\Sigma'$ and $\kappa$ is a
  $\Sigma'$-filtered size, there is an $<$-upper bound $j\in\kappa$
  for $q:E_c\rightarrow \kappa$; and since $\kappa$ is directed, we can
  assume $i<j$ and $i'<j$. So from \eqref{eq:product-2} and
  surjectiviy of $p$ we deduce that
  $\forall x\in X.\; F_x(D_{i,j})(f\,x) = F_x(D_{i',j})(f'x)$, which implies
  $(i,f)\approx (i',f')$. Therefore the function
  $\operatorname{\mathrm{can}}_{F,D}$ in \eqref{eq:product-1} is indeed injective.

  To see that $\operatorname{\mathrm{can}}_{F,D}$ is also surjective, suppose we have
  $g\in\prod_{x\in X}\operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{i\in\kappa}F_x(D_i)$. Since
  \[\textstyle
    \forall x\in X.\exists(i,d)\in \sum_{i\in\kappa}F_x(D_i).\; g(x) =
    [i,d]_{\approx}
  \]
  and $W$ is a wisc for $X$, there exists some $c\in C$,
  $p:E_c\twoheadrightarrow X$ and
  $\langle q_1,q_2\rangle \in \prod_{z\in E_c}
  \sum_{i\in\kappa}F_{p(z)}(D_i)$ so that
  $\forall z\in E_c.\; g(p\,z) =[q_1(z),q_2(z)]_{\approx}$. Then since
  $W$ is a summand in $\Sigma'$ and $\kappa$ is a $\Sigma'$-filtered
  size, there is an $<$-upper bound $j\in\kappa$ for
  $q_1:E_c\rightarrow \kappa$. So we have
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:product-3}
    \forall z\in E_c.\; g(p\,z) = [j, q'(z)]_{\approx}
 \end{equation}
 where $q'\in\prod_{z\in E_c}F_{p(z)}(D_j)$ is
 $q'(z)\triangleq F_{p(z)}(D_{q_1(z),j})(q_2(z))$. It follows that the
 relation $\Phi\subseteq \sum_{x\in X} F_x(D_j)$ given by
 $\Phi(x,d) \triangleq \exists z\in E_c.\; x = p(z) \;\wedge\; d = q'(z)$
 is total (because $p$ is surjective); and were it also single-valued,
 it would determine a function $f\in\prod_{x\in X} F_x(D_j)$ which by
 virtue of \eqref{eq:product-3} would satisfy $\operatorname{\mathrm{can}}_{F,D}[j,f] =
 g$. However, we need to increase $j$ to get this single-valued
 property. Recall that $W'$ is a wisc for the kernel
 \eqref{eq:product-0} of $p:E_c\twoheadrightarrow X$.  If $(z_1,z_2)\in \ker p$,
 then by \eqref{eq:product-3}
 $[j,q'(z_1)]_{\approx} = g(p\,z_1) = g(p\,z_2) =
 [j,q'(z_2)]_{\approx}$. Therefore we have
 \[
   \forall (z_1,z_2)\in\ker p.\exists k\in \kappa.\; j < k \;\wedge\;
   F_{p(z_1)}(D_{q_1(z_1),k})(q_2(z_1)) = F_{p(z_2)}(D_{q_1(z_2),k})(q_2(z_2))
 \]
 So since $W'=(E_{c'})_{c'\in C'}$ is a wisc for $\ker p$ and $\kappa$
 has $E_{c'}$-indexed upper bounds for any $c'\in C'$ and is directed,
 it follows that there exists $c'\in C'$, 
 $\langle p_1,p_2\rangle:E_{c'}\twoheadrightarrow \ker p$ and $k\in\kappa$ with
 $j<k$ and
 \begin{equation}
   \label{eq:product-4}
   \forall z''\in E_{c'}. F_{p(p_1\,z'')}(D_{q_1(p_1 z''),k})(q_2(p_1\,z'')) =
   F_{p(p_2\,z'')}(D_{q_1(p_2 z''),k})(q_2(p_2\,z''))
 \end{equation}
 Now if we let $q''\in\prod_{z\in E_c}F_{p(z)}(D_k)$ be
 $q''(z)\triangleq F_{p(z)}(D_{q_1(z),k})(q_2(z))$, then from
 \eqref{eq:product-3} we have
 \begin{equation}
   \label{eq:product-5}
    \forall z\in E_c.\; g(p\,z) = [k, q''(z)]_{\approx}
  \end{equation}
  Let the relation $\Phi'\subseteq \sum_{x\in X} F_x(D_k)$ be
  $\Phi'(x,d) \triangleq \exists z\in E_c.\; x = p(z) \;\wedge\; d =
  q''(z)$.  It is total because $p$ is surjective; but it is also
  single-valued because if $\Phi'(x,d) \wedge \Phi'(x,d')$, then
  $d = q''(z_1) \wedge d' = q''(z_2)$ for some $(z_1,z_2)\in \ker p$,
  so that there exists $z''\in E_{c'}$ with
  $p_1(z'')=z_1 \wedge p_2(z'') = z_2$ and hence
  $d=q''(z_1) = q''(z_2) = d'$ by \eqref{eq:product-4}. Therefore
  $\Phi'$ is the graph of a function $f\in\prod_{x\in X} F_x(D_k)$;
  and by virtue of \eqref{eq:product-5} we have
  $\forall x\in X.\; g(x) = [k,f(x)]_{\approx}$, so that
  $g = \operatorname{\mathrm{can}}_{F,D}[k,f]$. Thus $\operatorname{\mathrm{can}}_{F,D} $ is indeed surjective.

\label{exa:sym-cont}
  The \emph{symmetric containers} of Gylterud~\cite{GylterudHR:symc}
  generalize ordinary signatures by replacing the set of operation
  symbols by a groupoid $\mathbf{A}$ and the arity function by a
  functor $B:\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$. The associated endofunctor
  $S_{\mathbf{A},B}:\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}\rightarrow\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ maps each set $X\in\ensuremath{\mathbf{Set}}$ to the
  colimit
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:symm-cont}
    S_{\mathbf{A},B}(X) \triangleq \operatorname{\mathrm{colim}}_{a\in\mathbf{A}} X^{B(a)}
  \end{equation}
  Applying \cref{thm:sized-endof}, \cref{prop:sized-closure} and
  \cref{thm:product} we have that any topos with universes satisfying
  [\textsc{wisc}]{} has initial algebras for symmetric containers.

  In fact these initial algebras are special cases of
  QW-types~\cite{PittsAM:quoitq}: they can be seen as sets of terms
  quotiented by the symmetries given by the groupoid structure on the
  arguments of an operation symbol. So their existence in toposes with
  [\textsc{wisc}]{} follows from the results of that paper. However, the
  construction here in terms of a colimit of an inflationary iteration
  gives a simpler description than for the general case of a QW-type.
