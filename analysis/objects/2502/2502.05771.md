<!-- con-1: body_md -->
Let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group, and let $\varphi\in\operatorname{IBr}(G)$. Then
$$|L_{\varphi}|\leq|Q:Q'|,$$
where $Q$ is a vertex for $\varphi$ (in the sense of Green).

<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{CLN}
Let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group with $p>2$, and $\varphi\in\operatorname{IBr}(G)$.
If $\varphi$ has a normal vertex $Q$, then $|L_{\varphi}|\leq|Q:Q'|$.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- con-2: body_md -->
Let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group with $p>2$, and let $\varphi\in\operatorname{IBr}(G)$ with vertex $Q$.
If $\delta$ is a linear character of $Q$, then
$$|L_\varphi(Q,\delta)| \le |{\bf N}_G(Q):{\bf N}_G(Q,\delta)|,$$
where $L_{\varphi}(Q,\delta)$ denotes the set of those lifts of $\varphi$ with vertex pair $(Q,\delta)$,
and ${\bf N}_G(Q,\delta)$ is the set of elements of ${\bf N}_G(Q)$ that stabilize $\delta$.
<!-- end con-2 -->
<!-- thma-1: body_md -->
\label{A}
Let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group for some odd prime $p$,
let $\varphi\in\operatorname{IBr}(G)$ have vertex $Q$, and let $\delta\in\operatorname{Irr}(Q)$ be a linear character.
Suppose that $N$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ such that
$Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(N)$ and $\delta$ extends to $N$.
Write $T=N{\bf N}_G(Q,\delta)$. Then the following hold.

{\rm (1)} For any $\chi\in L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$,
each irreducible constituent of $\chi_N$ is factorable.

{\rm (2)} Each Brauer character $\eta\in I_\varphi(T|Q)$
has a unique lift $\tilde\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(T|Q,\delta)$.
Furthermore, $\tilde\eta^G$ lies in $L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$,
and the map $\eta\mapsto\tilde\eta^G$
defines a bijection $I_\varphi(T|Q)\to L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$.

{\rm (3)} $|L_\varphi(Q,\delta)|\le |{\bf N}_G(Q):{\bf N}_G(Q,\delta)|$.
<!-- end thma-1 -->
<!-- cora-1: body_md -->
\label{B}
Let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group with $p>2$, and
suppose that $\varphi\in\operatorname{IBr}(G)$ has vertex $Q$.
If $K\vartriangleleft G$ is a $p'$-subgroup such that $KQ\vartriangleleft G$,
then
$$|L_\varphi(Q,\delta)|\le |{\bf N}_G(Q):{\bf N}_G(Q,\delta)|$$
for each $\delta\in\operatorname{Irr}(Q)$.
<!-- end cora-1 -->
<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{prod}
Let $G$ be a $\pi$-separable group,
and suppose that $\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)$ are $\pi$-special and $\pi'$-special,
respectively. Then $\alpha\beta$ is irreducible.
Also, if $\alpha\beta=\alpha'\beta'$,
where $\alpha'$ is $\pi$-special and $\beta'$ is $\pi'$-special,
then $\alpha=\alpha'$ and $\beta=\beta'$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{ext}
Let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group with $P\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(G)$,
and let $\theta\in\operatorname{Irr}(P)$.
Then $\theta$ extends to $G$ if and only if $\theta(x)=\theta(y)$
whenever $x,y\in P$ are conjugate in $G$.
In this case, $\theta$ has a unique $p$-special extension to $G$.
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
This follows by Theorem 3.6 and Corollary 3.15 of \cite{I2018} with $\pi=\{p\}$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
\label{I5.2}
Let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group with $p>2$, and
suppose that $\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)$ is a lift.
If $\chi=\beta^G$, where $\beta$ is a $p'$-special character of some subgroup $W$
with $p'$-index in $G$, then $\chi$ is also $p'$-special.
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
As mentioned above, we see that $\beta$ is $p$-rational, which clearly implies that $\chi$ is also $p$-rational. Then $\chi$ lies in $B_{p'}(G)$ by the comments after Theorem 5.2 of \cite{I2018} (with $p'$ in place of $\pi$).
Note that $\chi(1)=|G:W|\beta(1)$, which is a $p'$-number, and
thus $\chi$ is $p'$-special by Theorem 4.2 of \cite{I2018}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lem-4: body_md -->
\label{CL1.2}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, and let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group.
Suppose that $\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)$ is a lift of $\varphi\in\operatorname{IBr}(G)$.
Then all the vertex pairs for $\chi$ are linear and conjugate in $G$.
<!-- end lem-4 -->
<!-- lem-5: body_md -->
\label{a}
Let $G$ be a group with $T\le G$ and let $\varphi\in\operatorname{IBr}(G)$.
Then the number of irreducible Brauer characters of $T$
inducing $\varphi$ is lass than or equal to $|G:T|$.
<!-- end lem-5 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Suppose that $\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_n\in\operatorname{IBr}(T)$ are different irreducible Brauer characters inducing $\varphi$. Then each $\eta_i(1)=\varphi(1)/|G:T|$ and
$\varphi_T=a_1\eta_1 +\cdots+a_n\eta_n +\Delta$,
where $a_i$ are some integers and $\Delta$ is either a Brauer character of $T$ or zero.
Now $\varphi(1)\geq n(\varphi(1)/|G:T|)$, and the result follows.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lem-6: body_md -->
\label{WJ}
Let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group, and let $Q$ be a $p$-subgroup of $G$.
Suppose that $\delta$ is a linear character of $Q$ that is $G$-stable.
Then restriction to $p$-regular elements defines a natural bijection $\operatorname{Irr}(G|Q,\delta)\rightarrow\operatorname{IBr}(G|Q)$.
<!-- end lem-6 -->
<!-- lem-7: body_md -->
\label{stab}
Let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group with $N\vartriangleleft G$,
and suppose that $Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(N)$ and $\delta\in\operatorname{Irr}(Q)$.
Assume also that $\delta$ extends to $N$ and is $H$-stable,
where $H$ is a subgroup of $G$ containing $Q$.
Then $\delta$ is $NH$-stable,
and the unique $p$-special extension of $\delta$ to $N$
is $NH$-invariant.
<!-- end lem-7 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Observe that $Q$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $N\cap H$,
and so $H={\bf N}_H(Q)(N\cap H)$ by the Frattini argument.
This implies that $NH=HN={\bf N}_H(Q)N$.
To show that $\delta$ is $NH$-stable,
we need to prove that $\delta(x)=\delta(y)$ whenever $x,y\in Q$ are
conjugate in $NH$. Now we can write $y=x^{hn}=(x^h)^n$ for some $h\in {\bf N}_H(Q)$
and $n\in N$,
and thus $x^h$ and $y$ are elements of $Q$ that are conjugate in $N$.
It follows by Lemma \ref{ext} that $\delta(x^h)=\delta(y)$.
Furthermore, since $x$ and $x^h$ are conjugate in $H$,
we have $\delta(x)=\delta(x^h)$ because $\delta$ is $H$-stable.
This proves that $\delta(x)=\delta(y)$, and hence $\delta$ is $NH$-stable.

By Lemma \ref{ext} again, there is a unique $p$-special character $\hat\delta$ of $N$ that is an extension of $\delta$.
For any element $h\in {\bf N}_H(Q)$, we see that $h$ fixes $\delta$ if and only if
$h$ fixes $\hat\delta$. Since $\delta$ is ${\bf N}_H(Q)$-invariant
and $NH={\bf N}_H(Q)N$, it follows that $\hat\delta$ is $NH$-invariant,
as required.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{A}
Let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group for some odd prime $p$,
let $\varphi\in\operatorname{IBr}(G)$ have vertex $Q$, and let $\delta\in\operatorname{Irr}(Q)$ be a linear character.
Suppose that $N$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ such that
$Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(N)$ and $\delta$ extends to $N$.
Write $T=N{\bf N}_G(Q,\delta)$. Then the following hold.

{\rm (1)} For any $\chi\in L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$,
each irreducible constituent of $\chi_N$ is factorable.

{\rm (2)} Each Brauer character $\eta\in I_\varphi(T|Q)$
has a unique lift $\tilde\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(T|Q,\delta)$.
Furthermore, $\tilde\eta^G$ lies in $L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$,
and the map $\eta\mapsto\tilde\eta^G$
defines a bijection $I_\varphi(T|Q)\to L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$.

{\rm (3)} $|L_\varphi(Q,\delta)|\le |{\bf N}_G(Q):{\bf N}_G(Q,\delta)|$.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
(1) By definition, there is a nucleus $(W,\gamma)$ for $\chi$,
such that $\gamma\in\operatorname{Irr}(W)$ is factorable that induces $\chi$, $Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(W)$ and $(\gamma_p)_Q=\delta$. In particular, $\delta$ extends to $W$,
so it is $W$-stable.
Since $\gamma$ is factorable,
we can write $\gamma=\alpha\beta$, where $\alpha=\gamma_p$ and $\beta=\gamma_{p'}$.

We claim that $\alpha$ extends to a $p$-special character of $NW$.
To see this, observe that $Q$ is also a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $NW$
and $\delta$ is $NW$-stable by Lemma \ref{stab}.
It follows from Lemma \ref{ext} that $\delta$ has a unique $p$-special extension
to $NW$, say $\tilde\delta$.
Furthermore, note that $\tilde\delta$ is linear because $\delta$ is,
so $\tilde\delta_W$ is the unique $p$-special extension of $\delta$ to $W$.
This forces $\tilde\delta_W=\gamma_p=\alpha$ by the uniqueness of such an extension,
as claimed.

Next we need to show that $\beta^{NW}$ is $p'$-special.
Writing $\xi=\gamma^{NW}$, we see that $\xi^G=\gamma^G=\chi$,
and thus $\xi$ is irreducible.
Note that
$$\xi=(\alpha\beta)^{NW}=(\tilde\delta_W\beta)^{NW}
=\tilde\delta\beta^{NW},$$
so $\beta^{NW}$ is also irreducible.
Furthermore, it is clear that $\xi$ is a lift because $\chi$ is,
and we deduce that
$\xi^0=\tilde\delta^0(\beta^{NW})^0=(\beta^{NW})^0$.
This proves that $\beta^{NW}$ is a lift, and since
$\beta^{NW}(1)=|NW:W|\beta(1)$ is a $p'$-number,
it follows by Lemma \ref{I5.2} that $\beta^{NW}$ is $p'$-special.

Now we have proved that $\xi=\tilde\delta\beta^{NW}$ is factorable,
and hence an irreducible constituent of $\xi_N$ is also factorable.
Recall that  $\xi^G=\chi$, so $\chi_N$ has an irreducible constituent
that is factorable. By Clifford's theorem,
we know that each irreducible constituent of $\chi_N$ is factorable.

(2) By Lemma \ref{stab} again, we see that $\delta$ is $T$-stable (with $\operatorname{N}_G(Q,\delta)$ in place of $H$), and hence by Lemma \ref{WJ},
each $\eta\in I_\varphi(T|Q)\subseteq \operatorname{IBr}(T|Q)$ has a unique lift $\tilde\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(T|Q,\delta)$.
Observe that
$$(\tilde\eta^G)^0=(\tilde\eta^0)^G=\eta^G=\varphi,$$
so $\tilde\eta^G\in L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$.
It follows that $\eta\mapsto\tilde\eta^G$ is a well defined map from $I_\varphi(T|Q)$ to $L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$.

Let $\hat\delta\in\operatorname{Irr}(N)$ be the unique $p$-special extension of $\delta$ to $N$
(see Lemma \ref{ext}). We will prove that for any $\chi\in L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$,
there exists a $p'$-special character $\alpha$ of $N$
such that $\alpha\hat\delta$ is a factorable irreducible constituent of $\chi_N$,
and induction defines a bijection $\operatorname{Irr}(T|\alpha\hat\delta)\to \operatorname{Irr}(G|\alpha\hat\delta)$.

Actually, since $\chi_N$ has an irreducible constituent that is factorable by part (1),
we can choose a nucleus $(W,\gamma)$ for $\chi$
with $N \le W$, and such that $\gamma$ is factorable with $\gamma^G=\chi$, $Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(W)$ and $(\gamma_p)_Q=\delta$ (see the construction of
nuclei given at Page 2763 of \cite{N2002}).
Let $\theta$ be an irreducible constituent of $\gamma_N$.
Then $\theta$ is factorable, and thus $\theta_p=\gamma_p$ because $\gamma_p(1)=\delta(1)=1$.
This proves that $\theta_p$ is a $p$-special extension of $\delta$ to $N$,
so $\hat\delta=\theta_p$ by Lemma \ref{ext} again.
Writing $\alpha=\theta_{p'}$, we see that $\theta=\alpha\hat\delta$, which is factorable lying under $\chi$.
Furthermore, it follows from Lemma \ref{stab} that $\hat\delta$ is invariant in
$T=N{\bf N}_G(Q,\delta)$, and by Lemma \ref{prod},
we conclude that the inertial group $I$ of $\alpha\hat\delta$ in $G$ is contained in $T$.
Applying the Clifford correspondence, we deduce that induction defines two bijections:
$$\operatorname{Irr}(I|\alpha\hat\delta)\to\operatorname{Irr}(T|\alpha\hat\delta) \;\text{and}\; \operatorname{Irr}(I|\alpha\hat\delta)\to\operatorname{Irr}(G|\alpha\hat\delta),$$
which implies that induction also defines a bijection
$\operatorname{Irr}(T|\alpha\hat\delta)\to \operatorname{Irr}(G|\alpha\hat\delta)$, as required.

Now we establish the surjectivity of our map.
Let $\chi\in L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$ be as above.
Then there exists a character $\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(T|\alpha\hat\delta)$ that induces $\chi$.
Observe that $N\vartriangleleft T$ and $\alpha\hat\delta$ is factorable lying under $\psi$,
so similar reasoning shows that
there exists a normal nucleus $(U,\mu)$ for $\psi$ with $N\le U$,
and such that $\mu$ is factorable and induces $\psi$.
Since $\psi$ is a lift and $p>2$, we know that $\mu_p$ must be a linear character.
It follows that $(\mu_p)_N=\theta_p=\hat\delta$, and hence $(\mu_p)_Q=\hat\delta_Q=\delta$.
Choose a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ of $U$ containing $Q$,
and let $\lambda=(\mu_p)_P$.
Then by definition, $(P,\lambda)$ is a vertex for $\psi$ and $\lambda_Q=\delta$.
Now $\psi^G=\chi$, and thus $(P,\lambda)$ is also a vertex for $\chi$.
By the uniqueness, we see that $(P,\lambda)$ and $(Q,\delta)$ are conjugate in $G$.
This shows that $P=Q$ and $\lambda=\delta$,
and hence $(Q,\delta)$ is also a vertex for $\psi$.
Furthermore, note that $(\psi^0)^G=(\psi^G)^0=\chi^0=\varphi$, so $\psi^0\in\operatorname{IBr}(T)$.
It is easy to see that $Q$ is a vertex for $\psi^0$
because $\delta$ is linear (see Theorem 4.6(d) of \cite{C2010} for example),
and hence $\psi^0\in I_\varphi(T|Q)$.
This proves that our map is surjective.

Finally, we establish the injectivity of the map.
Let $\zeta,\eta\in I_\varphi(T|Q)$ induce the same character
$\chi=\tilde\zeta^G=\tilde\eta^G\in L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$,
where $\tilde\zeta$ and $\tilde\eta$ are the lifts in $\operatorname{Irr}(T|Q,\delta)$ of $\zeta$ and $\eta$, respectively.
Reasoning as before (with $T$ in place of $G$),
there exist $p'$-special characters $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ of $N$
such that $\alpha_1\hat\delta$ and $\alpha_2\hat\delta$ are factorable irreducible constituents of
$\tilde\zeta_N$ and $\tilde\eta_N$, respectively.
Of course both $\alpha_1\hat\delta$ and $\alpha_2\hat\delta$ lie under $\chi$,
and thus they are conjugate in $G$ by Clifford's theorem.
Since $G=N\operatorname{N}_G(Q)$ by the Frattini argument, we can write $\alpha_1\hat\delta=(\alpha_2\hat\delta)^x$
for some $x\in \operatorname{N}_G(Q)$,
which forces $\alpha_1=(\alpha_2)^x$ and $\hat\delta=\hat\delta^x$ by Lemma \ref{prod}.
In particular, the element $x$ fixes $\hat\delta_N=\delta$ and thus $x\in\operatorname{N}_G(Q,\delta)\le T$.
This shows that $\tilde\zeta_N$ and $\tilde\eta_N$ have a
common irreducible constituent $\alpha_1\hat\delta$,
so $\tilde\zeta, \tilde\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(T|\alpha_1\hat\delta)$.
Similar reasoning implies that induction defines a bijection
$\operatorname{Irr}(T|\alpha_1\hat\delta)\to\operatorname{Irr}(G|\alpha_1\hat\delta)$,
and hence $\tilde\zeta=\tilde\eta$.
Since $\zeta=\tilde\zeta^0=\tilde\eta^0=\eta$,
we conclude that our map is injective.
This proves (2).

(3) Note that $|I_\varphi(T|Q)|\leq|G:T|$ by Lemma \ref{a}.
Also, it is easy to see that
$$|G:T|=|N\operatorname{N}_G(Q):N\operatorname{N}_G(Q,\delta)|=|{\bf N}_G(Q):{\bf N}_G(Q,\delta)|.$$
By part (2), we deduce that $|L_\varphi(Q,\delta)|=|I_\varphi(T|Q)|\leq |{\bf N}_G(Q):{\bf N}_G(Q,\delta)|$.
The proof is now complete.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- cor-1: body_md -->
Let $G$ be a $p$-solvable group with $p>2$, and
suppose that $\varphi\in\operatorname{IBr}(G)$ has vertex $Q$.
If $K\vartriangleleft G$ is a $p'$-subgroup such that $KQ\vartriangleleft G$,
then $|L_\varphi(Q,\delta)|\le |{\bf N}_G(Q):{\bf N}_G(Q,\delta)|$ for each $\delta\in\operatorname{Irr}(Q)$.
In particular, we have $|L_\varphi|\le|Q:Q'|$.
<!-- end cor-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We may assume that $L_\varphi(Q,\delta)$ is nonempty.
Then $\delta$ is a linear character of $Q$ by Lemma \ref{CL1.2}.
Writing $N=KQ$, we see that $Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(N)$ and $\delta$ extends to $N$.
Now Theorem A applies, and the result follows.
