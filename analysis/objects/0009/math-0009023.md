We will call $\{{\mathbf X}({t}): {t\in T}\}$ a  $q$-Gaussian (non-commutative) process indexed by $T$

Since $\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z}$ are bounded random variables, to prove (\ref{LR-c}) we need only to verify
that for arbitrary polynomials $P,Q$,
$$
E\left(P(\tilde{X})\tilde{Y} Q(\tilde{Z})\right)=E\left(P (\tilde{X})(a\tilde{X}+b\tilde{Z}) Q (\tilde{Z})\right).$$

 This is equivalent to
                            
$${\mathbb E}\left(P({\mathbf X}){\mathbf Y} Q({\mathbf Z})\right)={\mathbb E}\left(P ({\mathbf X})(a{\mathbf X}+b{\mathbf Z}) Q ({\mathbf Z})\right),
$$
               
see  (\ref{C-ver}).
The latter follows from (\ref{LR3}) and (\ref{E nc}),  proving (\ref{LR-c}).
               

To prove (\ref{QV-c}), we  verify
that for arbitrary polynomials $P,Q$ we have

$$E\left(P(\tilde{X})\tilde{Y}^2 Q(\tilde{Z})\right)=E\left(P (\tilde{X})(A\tilde{X}^2+B\tilde{X}\tilde{Z}+C\tilde{Z}^2+D) Q (\tilde{Z})\right).$$

By definition  (\ref{C-ver}), this is equivalent to

\begin{equation}\label{QV-nc}
{\mathbb E}\left(P({\mathbf X}){\mathbf Y}^2 Q({\mathbf Z})\right)={\mathbb E}\left(P ({\mathbf X})(A{\mathbf X}^2+B{\mathbf X}{\mathbf Z}+C{\mathbf Z}^2+D) Q ({\mathbf Z})\right).
\end{equation}
It suffices to show that (\ref{QV-nc}) holds true when
$P=H_n$ and $Q=H_m$ are the $q$-Hermite polynomials defined by (\ref{q-H}).
Formula (\ref{QV3}) implies that the left-hand side of (\ref{QV-nc}) is given by
$$ c{\mathbb E}(H_n({\mathbf X})H_m({\mathbf Z}))+
a^2{\mathbb E}(H_n({\mathbf X}){\mathbf X}^2H_m({\mathbf Z}))+b^2 {\mathbb E}(H_n({\mathbf X}){\mathbf Z}^2H_m({\mathbf Z}))$$
$$

$$
and the right-hand side becomes
$$

+$$
$$B {\mathbb E}(H_n({\mathbf X}){\mathbf X}{\mathbf Z} H_m({\mathbf Z}))+ D{\mathbb E}(H_n({\mathbf X})H_m({\mathbf Z})).
$$
Using formulas from Lemma \ref{switch} we can see that both sides are zero, except when $m=n$

\noindent

\end{equation}

\noindent
{\it Case $m=n-2$}:

$$
(a^2+2ab \langle f, h \rangle+b^2\langle f, h \rangle^2)\langle f, h \rangle^{n-2}[n]_q!=(A+B\langle f, h \rangle+C\langle f, h \rangle^2)\langle f, h \rangle^{n-2}[n]_q!.
$$
This equation is satisfied whenever
\begin{equation}\label{ABC 2}
A+B\langle f, h \rangle+C\langle f, h \rangle^2=a^2+2ab \langle f, h \rangle+b^2\langle f, h \rangle^2.
\end{equation}

\noindent {\it Case $m=n$}:

On both sides of Eq. (\ref{QV-nc}) we factor out

(This is allowed since we are after sufficient conditions only!)
We get
$$
\left(\langle f, h \rangle (a^2+b^2)([n+1]_q+[n]_q)+ab \left(\langle f, h \rangle^2[n+1]_q+

Now we use $[n+1]_q=1+q[n]_q$. Suppressing the correction to

\left((1+q)(A+C)\langle f, h \rangle+B(q\langle f, h \rangle^2+1)\right)
[n]_q+D\langle f, h \rangle,

where $c+\dots$  denotes the suppressed constant term corrections.

This equation holds true when  the coefficients at $[n]_q$ match, which gives

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{q-Bell}
Suppose that $(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})$ is a classical version of $q$-Gaussian
$({\mathbf X},{\mathbf Y},{\mathbf Z}):=({\mathbf X}_f,{\mathbf X}_g,{\mathbf X}_h)$, where $f,h\in{\mathcal H}$ are linearly independent,
and $-1\leq q<1$.
If either \begin{equation}\label{sub-M}
\langle f, g\rangle \langle g, h \rangle \leq \langle f, h\rangle \mbox{ and }  0<\langle f, h \rangle<1,
\end{equation}
or $\langle f, h\rangle=0$, or $q=-1$, then inequality
(\ref{Bell}) holds true.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since the case $q=-1$ is well known, we restrict our attention to the case
 $-1<q<1$.
Our starting point is expression (\ref{QV-c}).
A computation shows that
 the conditional variance $Var(\tilde{Y}|\tilde{X},\tilde{Z}):=E(\tilde{Y}^2|\tilde{X},\tilde{Z})-\left(E(\tilde{Y}|\tilde{X},\tilde{Z})\right)^2$ is as follows.
\begin{eqnarray} \label{foo1}
Var(\tilde{Y}|\tilde{X},\tilde{Z})=&1-a^2-b^2-
ab\langle f, h \rangle \frac{(1+q)(1-\langle f, h \rangle^2)+2(1-q)}{1-q\langle f, h \rangle^2}
-&\\ \nonumber
&\frac {ab\left(1-q\right)}
{1-q\langle f, h \rangle^2}\left(\tilde{Z}{\langle f, h \rangle-\tilde{X}}\right )\left ({\langle f, h \rangle}\tilde{X}-\tilde{Z}\right).
&
\end{eqnarray}
The right-hand side of this expression must be non-negative over the support of $\tilde{X},\tilde{Z}$.
It is known, see  \cite[Lemma
8.1]{Bryc98} or \cite[Theorem 1.10]{BKS97}, that $\tilde{X},\tilde{Z}$ have the joint
probability density function $f(x,z)$ with respect to the product of marginals $\nu_q$.
Moreover, $f$ is
 defined for all $-2/\sqrt{1-q}\leq x,z\leq 2/\sqrt{1-q}$ and from
its explicit product expansion we can see that
 $$f(x,z)\geq \prod_{k=0}^\infty \frac{(1-\langle f, h \rangle^2 q^k)}{
(1+\langle f, h \rangle q^{k})^4}$$
 is  strictly positive. In particular, the right-hand side of (\ref{foo1}) must be non-negative
when evaluated at $\tilde{X}=\sqrt{2}/\sqrt{1-q},\tilde{Z}=-\sqrt{2}/\sqrt{1-q}$.


Using formulas (\ref{a}), (\ref{b}) with the above values of $\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}$
we get the rational expression for the conditional variance which can be written as follows.
\begin{eqnarray} \label{foo2}
&\left(1-q\langle f, h \rangle^2\right)\left(1-\langle f, h \rangle\right)^2Var\left(\tilde{Y}|\tilde{X},\tilde{Z}\right)=& \\ \nonumber
&
(1-\langle f, h \rangle)^2\left(1-q\langle f, h \rangle^2+(1+q)\langle f, h \rangle\langle f, g \rangle\langle g, h \rangle\right)-&
\\ \nonumber &\left(\langle f, g \rangle-\langle g, h \rangle\right)^2(1+\langle f, h \rangle^2) .
&
\end{eqnarray}
Therefore
$$(1-\langle f, h \rangle)^2\left(1-q\langle f, h \rangle^2+(1+q)\langle f, h \rangle\langle f, g \rangle\langle g, h \rangle\right)
\geq $$
$$\left(\langle f, g \rangle-\langle g, h \rangle\right)^2(1+\langle f, h \rangle^2).$$

Since the assumptions imply that $1-q\langle f, h \rangle^2+(1+q)\langle f, h \rangle\langle f, g \rangle\langle g, h \rangle\leq 1+\langle f, h \rangle^2$, this implies
$(1-\langle f, h \rangle)^2 \geq \left(\langle f, g \rangle-\langle g, h \rangle\right)^2$, proving (\ref{Bell}).
