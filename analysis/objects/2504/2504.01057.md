<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{LemA}
The diagram (\ref{EquA}) in $\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D}$, where $0\in\mathbf{0}$ and $1\in\mathbf{1}$, is a short exact sequence if and only if $m_1$ is an isomorphism, $m_2$ is a monomorphism, $e_1$ is an epimorphism and $e_2$ is an isomorphism.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
First, we make some general observations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item[(G)] $(f_1,f_2)$ is a monomorphism/epimorphism if and only if so are each $f_i$.
\end{itemize}
Now, suppose (\ref{EquA}) is a short exact sequence. (G) together with (E1-2) leave us to show that $m_1$ is a split epimorphism and symmetrically, $e_2$ is a split monomorphism. Thanks to commutativity of the right-hand side square below and (E4), we get a commutative triangle on the left:
$$
\xymatrix@=30pt{(X,0)\ar[r]^-{(m_1,m_2)} & (C,D)\ar[r]^-{(e_1,e_2)} & (1,Y)\\ & (C,0)\ar[ul]^-{(u'_1,\operatorname{id}_{0})}\ar[u]_-{(\operatorname{id}_{C},m_2)}\ar[r]_-{(e_1,\operatorname{id}_{0})} & (1,0)\ar[u]_-{(\operatorname{id}_{1},e_2m_2)}} $$
Then $m_1u'_1=\operatorname{id}_{C}$, proving that $m_1$ is a split epimorphism. That $e_2$ is a split monomorphism can be proved similarly (with a dual-symmetric argument).

For the converse, suppose all the assumptions stated for $m_i,e_i$ in the lemma hold. It is not difficult to see that since $m_1$ and $e_2$ are isomorphisms, without loss of generality we can assume that they are actually identity morphisms. So the sequence (\ref{EquA}) becomes the top line of the following diagram:
$$
\xymatrix@=30pt{(C,0)\ar[r]^-{(\operatorname{id}_{C},m_2)} & (C,D)\ar[r]^-{(e_1,\operatorname{id}_{D})} & (1,D)\\ & (U_1,U_2)\ar[u]_-{(u_1,u_2)}\ar[r]_-{(e_1u_1,d)}\ar@{-->}[ul]^-{(u_1,d)} & (1,0)\ar[u]_-{(\operatorname{id}_{1},m_2)}} $$
(E1-2) hold by (G) and we already know (E3) holds. To prove (E4), suppose $(e_1,\operatorname{id}_{D})\circ (u_1,u_2)$ factors through an object in $\mathcal{Z}$. Without loss of generality, we can assume that this object is $(1,0)$. This gives the rest of the above diagram of solid arrows. Now, $(u_1,u_2)$ indeed factors through $(\operatorname{id}_{C},m_2)$ as required in (E3): look at the dashed arrow in the diagram above. The proof of (E5) is similar.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{TheA}
$(\mathbb{C}\times_{} \mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}\times \mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\times\mathbb{D})$ is a pretorsion theory if and only if every morphism $C\to 1$ in $\mathbb{C}$ with $1\in \1$ is an epimorphism and every morphism $0\to D$ in $\mathbb{D}$ with $0\in \0$ is a monomorphism. That is, if and only if $\mathbb{D}$ is quasi-pointed and $\mathbb{C}$ satisfies the dual condition.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Suppose $(\mathbb{C}\times \mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\times\mathbb{D})$ is a pretorsion theory. Then any object $(C,D)$ is part of a short exact sequence, which by Lemma~\ref{LemA} can be written out as
$$\xymatrix@=30pt{(C,0)\ar[r]^-{(\operatorname{id}_{C},m_2)} & (C,D)\ar[r]^-{(e_1,\operatorname{id}_{D})} & (1,D)}$$
where $m_2\colon C\to D$ is a monomorphism and $e_1\colon C\to 1$ is an epimorphism. To prove the converse, we can apply Lemma~\ref{LemA} again and use the above sequence for each $(C,D)$. Thanks to the discussion at the start of this section, this would conclude the proof.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The property that every morphism going out from an initial object $0$ is a monomorphism, under the presence of a terminal object $1$, is equivalent to the property that the unique morphism $0\to 1$ is a monomorphism. This is a useful generalisation of pointedness in categorical algebra, first emphasized in \cite{Bourn01} (see also \cite{GJ17}).
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ExaA}
Let $\mathbb{C}=\mathbf{Set}^\mathsf{op}$ and $\mathbb{D}=\mathbf{Set}$. We can think of objects $(X,Y)$ in $\mathbf{Set}^\mathsf{op}\times \mathbf{Set}$ as sets having two types of elements: \emph{positive} elements (elements of $Y$) and \emph{negative} elements (elements of $X$). To emphasize this intuition, we can write $Y-X$ for $(X,Y)$. Theorem~\ref{TheA} is applicable here, since $\varnothing\to S$ is an injective function, for any set $S$. The torsion objects in the corresponding pretorsion theory are \emph{negative sets}, i.e., sets having only negative elements, while torsion-free objects are \emph{positive sets}, i.e., sets having only positive elements. The short exact sequence that every object $Y-X$ fits into identifies the ``subset'' $\varnothing-X$ of $Y-X$ consisting of negative elements, and the subset $Y-\varnothing$ of $X-Y$ consisting of positive elements:
$$\xymatrix{\varnothing-X\ar[r] & Y-X\ar[r] & Y-\varnothing}$$

		{#5}\ifxXo{\arrow[u,"{\,{\scalebox{1.2}{$#4$}}}"']}\fi
	\end{cd}\xspace
 & \to &  
	                                                  
	\begin{cd}*[(h^T,h^F)][5]

is an equivalence of categories. See \cite{FFG21} for the constructions of the canonical functors $T\:\mathbb{C}\to \mathcal{T}$ and $F\:\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{F}$ that induce $\Gamma$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathbb{D}$ be a quasi-pointed category and $\mathbb{C}$ be a category satisfying the dual condition. The pretorsion theory $(\mathbb{C}\times_{} \mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}\times \mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\times\mathbb{D})$ (of \textrm{Theorem}~\ref{TheA}) is rectangular.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The canonical functor $\Gamma$ associated to $(\mathbb{C}\times_{} \mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}\times \mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\times\mathbb{D})$ is
    \begin{fun}
	\Gamma & \: & \mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{D} & \longrightarrow & \mathbb{C}\times \mathbf{0}\times_{} \mathbf{1}\times\mathbb{D} \\[1ex]
    && (X,Y) & \to & ((X,0),(1,Y))
\end{fun}
This is clearly an equivalence of categories, as $\Gamma=T'\times_{} F'$ with  $T'\:X\mapsto (X,0)$ and $F'\:Y\mapsto (1,Y)$, and both $T'$ and $F'$ are equivalences.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{LemmaZ}
For any category $\mathbb{C}$, the triple $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\mathcal{J})$ is a pretorsion theory if and only if $\mathcal{J}$ is a full replete epireflective subcategory of $\mathbb{C}$. If $\mathcal{J}$ is equivalent to the terminal category, then $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\mathcal{J})$ is rectangular.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
It is known that if we have a pretorsion theory $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\mathcal{J})$ then $\mathcal{J}$ is a full replete epireflective subcategory of $\mathbb{C}$, see \cite[Corollary 3.4]{FFG21}. Taking the torsion free part of objects of $\mathbb{C}$ gives the epireflection.

    We prove that if $J\:\mathcal{J}\subseteq \mathbb{C}$ is a full replete epireflective subcategory of $\mathbb{C}$ then $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\mathcal{J})$ is a pretorsion theory. Call $L\:\mathbb{C}\to \mathcal{J}$ the epireflection. Of course both $\mathbb{C}$ and $\mathcal{J}$ are full replete subcategories of $\mathbb{C}$. The ideal of null morphisms is given by $\mathbb{C}\cap \mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}$, so every morphism landing into an object of $\mathcal{J}$ is null. It remains to show that every object $X\in \mathbb{C}$ sits in the middle of an appropriate short exact sequence. But given $X\in \mathbb{C}$, the epireflection produces an epimorphism $\eta_X\:X\to J(L(X))$, with $L(X)\in \mathcal{J}$. So consider the sequence
    $$X 
	\ext@arrow 0099\arrowfill@\Relbar\Relbar\DOTSB\protect\Relbar\protect\joinrel\Relbar{}{\operatorname{id}_{X}} X\xrightarrow[]{\eta_X}J(L(X))$$
    Since $\eta_X$ is null, $\operatorname{id}_{X}$ is a kernel of $\eta_X$. We show that $\eta_X$ is the cokernel of $\operatorname{id}_{X}$. Given any null morphism $m\:X\to M$, we have that $m$ factors as
    $X\xrightarrow[]{m^1} J(A)\xrightarrow[]{m^2}M$ with $A\in \mathcal{J}$. As $\eta_X$ gives a universal arrow, there exists a unique $v\:L(X)\to A$ such that
    \begin{cd}[4][6]
        X \ar[r,"{\eta_X}"]\ar[rd,bend right,"{m^1}"'] \& J(L(X))\ar[d,"J(v)"] \\
        \& J(A)
    \end{cd}
    Whence $m^2\circ J(v)$ is such that $m^2\circ J(v)\circ \eta_X=m$. Since $\eta_X$ is epi, $m^2\circ J(v)$ is the unique morphism with this property. We thus conclude that the sequence above is short exact.

    Now, assume that $\mathcal{J}$ is equivalent to the terminal category, and consider the canonical functor
\begin{fun}
	\Gamma & \: & \mathbb{C} & \longrightarrow & \mathbb{C}\times_{} \mathcal{J} \\[1ex]

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{LemmaW}
For any category $\mathbb{D}$, the triple $(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{I},\mathbb{D})$ is a pretorsion theory if and only if $\mathcal{I}$ is a full replete monocoreflective subcategory of $\mathbb{D}$. If $\mathcal{I}$ is equivalent to the terminal category, then $(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{I},\mathbb{D})$ is rectangular.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{LemD}
Let $\mathbb{C}$ be a category with terminal object and let $\1$ be the subcategory of terminal objects in $\mathbb{C}$. The triple $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\mathbf{1})$ is a pretorsion theory if and only if every morphism in $\mathbb{C}$ to the terminal object is an epimorphism. Such pretorsion theories are rectangular.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
$\1$ is of course a full replete subcategory of $\mathbb{C}$, by uniqueness of the terminal object up to iso. If every morphism in $\mathbb{C}$ to the terminal object is an epimorphism, then the unique epimorphisms $X\to 1$, for a fixed chosen $1\in \1$, give an epireflection of $\mathbb{C}$ into $\1$. Indeed, terminal objects are unique up to a unique isomorphism. By \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{LemmaZ}, $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\1)$ is a rectangular pretorsion theory.
    
    If $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\1)$ is a pretorsion theory, then by \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{LemmaZ} $\1$ is epireflective in $\mathbb{C}$. So for every $X\in \mathbb{C}$ there exists an epimorphism $\eta_X\:X\to 1$ with $1\in \1$. Whence every morphism in $\mathbb{C}$ to the terminal object is an epimorphism.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{TheB}
Consider a non-empty family $\mathbb{C}=(\mathbb{C}_i)_{i\in I}$ of non-empty categories and a family $(\mathcal{T}_i,\mathcal{F}_i)_{i\in I}$ of pairs of categories. The triple
\begin{equation}\label{EquB}(\Pi\mathbb{C},\Pi\mathcal{T},\Pi\mathcal{F})=\left(\prod_{i\in I}\mathbb{C}_i,\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{T}_i,\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{F}_i\right)\end{equation}
is a pretorsion theory if and only if each $(\mathbb{C}_i,\mathcal{T}_i,\mathcal{F}_i)$ is a pretorsion theory. Moreover, $(\Pi\mathbb{C},\Pi\mathcal{T},\Pi\mathcal{F})$ is rectangular if and only if each $(\mathbb{C}_i,\mathcal{T}_i,\mathcal{F}_i)$ is rectangular.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Firstly, we remark that when either of the two conditions hold, each $\mathcal{T}_i,\mathcal{F}_i$ are non-empty. So without loss of generality we may assume that these categories are non-empty from the onset. 
Under this assumption, each $\mathcal{T}_i,\mathcal{F}_i$ are full replete subcategories of $\mathbb{C}_i$ if and only if $\Pi\mathcal{T},\Pi\mathcal{F}$ are full replete subcategories of $\Pi\mathbb{C}$.

We have:
$$\Pi\mathcal{T}\cap \Pi\mathcal{F}=\Pi_{i\in I}(\mathcal{T}_i\cap \mathcal{F}_i).$$
This implies that a morphism is null in $\Pi\mathbb{C}$ if and only if it is null in each $\mathbb{C}_i$. So null-morphisms in $\Pi\mathbb{C}$ are \emph{component-wise}. This clearly guarantees that (T1) holds for $(\Pi\mathcal{T},\Pi\mathcal{F})$ if and only if it holds for each $(\mathcal{T}_i,\mathcal{F}_i)$. To guarantee the same for (T2) it suffices to show that short exact sequences in $\Pi\mathbb{C}$ are also component-wise (an analogue, and in fact a generalisation of Lemma~\ref{LemA}). It is easy to see that if each 
$$\xymatrix{T_i\ar[r]^-{m_i} & C_i\ar[r]^-{e_i} & F_i}$$
is a short exact sequence, then so is 
$$\xymatrix{(T_i)_{i\in I}\ar[r]^-{(m_i)_{i\in I}} & (C_i)_{i\in I}\ar[r]^-{(e_i)_{i\in I}} & (F_i)_{i\in I}.}$$
Conversely, suppose the above is a short exact sequence. To show that the previous sequence is also short exact for some $i\in I$, consider a morphism $u_i\colon U_i\to C$ such that $e_iu_i$ is null. Define $U_j=T_j$ when $j\neq i$ and $u_j=m_j$ when $j\neq i$. Then the composite $(e_i)_{i\in I}(u_i)_{i\in I}$ is null. This results in a factorisation of $(u_i)_{i\in I}$ through $(m_i)_{i\in I}$, and hence a factorisation of $u_i$ through $m_i$. By the fact that being a monomorphism is a component-wise property and duality, we obtain the desired: that the sequence at $i$ shown above is short exact.

Next, we prove that rectangularity is preserved and reflected under products. It is easy to see that the functor $K\colon \Pi\mathbb{C}\to \Pi\mathcal{T}\times \Pi\mathcal{F}$ that assigns to each objects its torsion and torsion-free part can be built component-wise using similar functors at each $K_i$. In fact, we have a commutative diagram
$$\xymatrix{\Pi\mathbb{C}\ar[r]^-{K}\ar[rd]_-{\Pi_{i\in I}(K_i)\;\;\;} & \Pi\mathcal{T}\times \Pi\mathcal{F}\ar[d]^-{L}\\ & \Pi_{i\in I}(\mathcal{T}_i\times \mathcal{F}_i)}$$
where $L$ is an isomorphism. So $K$ will be an equivalence if and only if each $K_i$ is an equivalence.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lemmakercoker}
    Let $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ be a pretorsion theory. Given a kernel $k\:k(X)\to X$ of $\operatorname{id}_{X}$, we have that $k(X)\in \mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$. Analogously, given a cokernel $c\:X\to c(X)$ of $\operatorname{id}_{X}$, we have that $c(X)\in \mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $k\:k(X)\to X$ be a kernel of $\operatorname{id}_{X}$. Then $k$ needs to be a null morphism, so $k$ factors as
$$k(X)\xrightarrow[]{j}Z\xrightarrow[]{k'}X$$
with $Z\in \mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$. Since $\operatorname{id}_{X}\circ k'$ is null, there exists a unique morphism $v\:Z\to k(X)$ such that $k\circ v=k'$. Then $v\circ j=\operatorname{id}_{}$, because this holds after composing with the mono $k$. So $k(X)$ is a retract of $Z\in \mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$, and by \cite[Corollary 2.8]{FFG21} we have $k(X)\in \mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$.

Analogously, we can prove that $c(X)\in \mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{LemE}
Let $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ be a rectangular pretorsion theory where $\mathbb{C}$ is a non-empty category. Then all objects in $\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F}$ are isomorphic to an object $Z$ which is terminal in $\mathcal{T}$ and initial in $\mathcal{F}$. In particular, the ideal of null morphisms is generated by one object $Z$. Moreover $\mathcal{F}$ is quasi-pointed and $\mathcal{T}$ satisfies the dual condition.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Consider an object $Z\in\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F}$ (such $Z$ exists by Remark 2.9(a) in \cite{FFG21}). The canonical functor $\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{F}$ maps $Z$ to an object in $\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{F}$ that is isomorphic to $(Z,Z)$. Since this functor is an equivalence, we obtain a bijection
$$\mathsf{hom}(Z,Z)\cong \mathsf{hom}((Z,Z),(Z,Z))$$
given by the mapping $z\mapsto (z,z)$. This forces $\mathsf{hom}(Z,Z)$ to be a singleton. Indeed, given $(z'\:Z\to Z, z''\:Z\to Z)$, there exists a unique $z\:Z\to Z$ such that $(z,z)=(z',z'')$. Thus $\mathsf{hom}(Z,Z)$ only contains the identity of $Z$. Now, consider another object $Z'$ in the intersection $\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F}$. By the canonical equivalence $\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{F}$, there must be an object $C$ in $\mathbb{C}$ with the torsion and torsion-free parts given by 
$T^C=Z$ and $F^C=Z'$. This forces isomorphisms $Z\cong C\cong Z'$, because identities give kernels and cokernels of null morphisms. Whence all objects in $\mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$ are isomorphic to $Z$.

We prove that $Z$ is terminal in $\mathcal{T}$ and initial in $\mathcal{F}$. Given an object $T\in\mathcal{T}$, consider its reflection $r^T\:T\to F^T$ of $T$ to its torsion-free part $F^T$. Since we always choose $\ell^T=\operatorname{id}_{T}$ (see the Notations subsection), $r^T$ is a cokernel of $\operatorname{id}_{T}$. By \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{lemmakercoker}, $F^T\in \mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$ and thus $F^T\cong Z$. In other words, for every $T\in \mathcal{T}$ we have a morphism $T\to Z$ that is a cokernel of $\operatorname{id}_{T}\colon T\to T$ in $\mathbb{C}$. As a consequence, it is also a cokernel of $\operatorname{id}_{T}$ in $\mathcal{T}$ and in particular an epi in $\mathcal{T}$. Moreover, since $\mathsf{hom}(Z,Z)$ is a singleton, there can only be one morphism $T\to Z$, proving that $Z$ is a terminal object in $\mathcal{T}$. Dually, $Z$ is an initial object in $\mathcal{F}$ such that all morphisms going out from $Z$ in $\mathcal{F}$ are mono in $\mathcal{F}$, which completes the proof.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{defequiv}
    By an \emph{equivalence} 
$(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})\approx (\mathcal{D},\mathcal{T}',\mathcal{F}')$
of pretorsion theories we mean an equivalence $\mathcal{C}\approx\mathcal{D}$ of categories which preserves torsion and torsion-free objects.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lemmaequiv}
    Let $\mathbb{C}$ and $\mathbb{D}$ be categories equipped with an ideal of null morphisms. Every equivalence of categories $\Lambda\:\mathbb{C}\approx \mathbb{D}:\Lambda'$ that preserves null morphisms preserves also kernels and cokernels, and thus also short exact sequences.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
We can assume that the equivalence $\Lambda\:\mathbb{C}\approx \mathbb{D}:\Lambda'$ is an adjoint equivalence. Consider a composite of morphisms
    $$T\xrightarrow[]{l}X\xrightarrow[]{r}F.$$
    Assume that $\ell$ is the kernel of $r$. We prove that $\Lambda(\ell)$ is the kernel of $\Lambda(r)$. So consider a morphism $m\:M\to \Lambda(X)$ such that $\Lambda(r)\circ m$ is a null morphism. By assumption, also $\Lambda'(\Lambda(r))\circ \Lambda'(m)$ is a null morphism, whence also
    $$\Lambda'(M)\xrightarrow[]{\Lambda'(m)}\Lambda'(\Lambda(X))\cong X\xrightarrow[]{r} F$$
    is a null morphism. Since $\ell$ is the kernel of $r$, there exists a unique morphism $v\: \Lambda'(M)\to X$ such that
    \begin{cd}[5][5]
        \Lambda'(M) \ar[r,"\Lambda'(m)"]\ar[d,"v"']\& \Lambda'(\Lambda(X)) \ar[d,iso]\\
        T \ar[r,"\ell"']\& X
    \end{cd}
Whence the morphism $w$ given by the composite $M\cong \Lambda(\Lambda'(M))\xrightarrow[]{\Lambda(v)} \Lambda(T)$ is such that $\Lambda(l)\circ w=m$, thanks to the triangular identity of the adjoint equivalence. Since $\Lambda(l)$ is mono, because every equivalence preserves limits, $w$ is the unique morphism with such property. We thus conclude that $\Lambda(\ell)$ is the kernel of $\Lambda(r)$.

Analogously, we have that $\Lambda$ preserves cokernels.
<!-- end proof-12 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{propequivpretors}
    Equivalences of pretorsion theories $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})\approx (\mathbb{D},\mathcal{T}',\mathcal{F}')$, as defined in \textrm{Definition}~\ref{defequiv}, coincide with internal equivalences in the $2$-category $\mathbf{PTor}$ of pretorsion theories.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Every equivalence of categories $\Lambda\:\mathbb{C}\approx \mathbb{D}\:\Lambda'$ that preserves torsion and torsion-free objects also preserves null morphisms, which are generated by $\mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$. By \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{lemmaequiv}, $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda'$ then also preserve short exact sequences. It is straightforward to conclude.
<!-- end proof-13 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{corolltransfer}
    Let $\Lambda\:\mathbb{C}\approx \mathbb{D}\:\Lambda'$ be an equivalence of categories and let $(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{T}',\mathcal{F}')$ be a pretorsion theory. Then there exists a pretorsion theory $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ such that $\Lambda$ lifts to an equivalence of pretorsion theories.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Define $\mathcal{T}$ to be the full subcategory of $\mathbb{C}$ on the objects of the essential image of 
    $$\mathcal{T}'\subseteq \mathbb{D}\xrightarrow[]{\Lambda'}\mathbb{C},$$
    and analogously define $\mathcal{F}$ using $\mathcal{F}'\subseteq \mathbb{D}\xrightarrow[]{\Lambda'}\mathbb{C}$. Equivalently, $\mathcal{T}=\Lambda^{-1}(\mathcal{T}')$ and $\mathcal{F}=\Lambda^{-1}(\mathcal{F}')$. Then of course $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ are replete, and both $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda'$ preserve torsion and torsion-free objects. As a consequence, $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda'$ also preserve null morphisms, and every morphism in $\mathbb{C}$ from an object of $\mathcal{T}$ to an object of $\mathcal{F}$ is null. Finally, given $X\in \mathbb{C}$, we want to construct an appropriate short exact sequence with $X$ in the middle. We consider $\Lambda(X)$ and its associated short exact sequence
    $$T^{\Lambda(X)}\xrightarrow[]{\ell}\Lambda(X)\xrightarrow[]{r}F^{\Lambda(X)}$$
    in $\mathbb{D}$. Since $\Lambda'$ is an equivalence of categories that preserves null morphisms, by \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{lemmaequiv} it preserves short exact sequences. We thus obtain a short exact sequence
$$\Lambda'(T^{\Lambda(X)})\xrightarrow[]{\Lambda'(\ell)}\Lambda'(\Lambda(X))\cong X\cong \Lambda'(\Lambda(X))\xrightarrow[]{\Lambda'(r)}\Lambda'(F^{\Lambda(X)}).$$
And we conclude that $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is a pretorsion theory such that $\Lambda$ lifts to an equivalence of pretorsion theories (also thanks to \textrm{Proposition}~\ref{propequivpretors}).
<!-- end proof-14 -->
<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{corequivtf}
    Let $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ be a rectangular pretorsion theory, and consider its associated equivalence $\Gamma\:\mathbb{C}\to \mathcal{T}\times_{}\mathcal{F}$. Then $$(\mathcal{T}\times_{}\mathcal{F},\hspace{0.1ex}\mathcal{T}\times_{}(\mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}),(\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F})\times_{} \mathcal{F})$$
    is a pretorsion theory equivalent to $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ via $\Gamma$.
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
By \textrm{Corollary}~\ref{corolltransfer}, there exists a pretorsion theory $(\mathcal{T}\times_{}\mathcal{F},\mathcal{T}'',\mathcal{F}'')$ such that $\Gamma$ lifts to an equivalence of pretorsion theories. By the proof of \textrm{Corollary}~\ref{corolltransfer}, $\mathcal{T}''$ is given by the full subcategory of $\mathcal{T}\times_{}\mathcal{F}$ on the objects of the essential image of $\mathcal{T}\subseteq \mathbb{C}\xrightarrow[]{\Gamma} \mathcal{T}\times_{} \mathcal{F}$. For every $T\in \mathcal{T}$, $\Gamma(T)=(T^T,F^T)\cong (T,Z)$ for fixed chosen $Z\in \mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$ such that all objects in $\mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$ are isomorphic to $Z$, by \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{LemE} and its proof. So $\mathcal{T}''=\mathcal{T}\times_{} \mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}=\mathcal{T}\times_{}\mathbf{Z}$, where $\mathbf{Z}$ is the full subcategory of $\mathbb{C}$ given by the objects isomorphic to $Z$. Analogously, $\mathcal{F}''=\mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}\times_{} \mathcal{F}=\mathbf{Z}\times_{} \mathcal{F}$.
<!-- end proof-15 -->
<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{theorcharactcart}
For a pretorsion theory $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ where $\mathcal{C}$ is a non-empty category, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is a rectangular pretorsion theory.

\item[(b)] $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is equivalent to a pretorsion theory of the form $(\mathbb{C}\times_{} \mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}\times \mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\times\mathbb{D})$ where $\mathbb{D}$ is a quasi-pointed category and $\mathbb{C}$ is a category satisfying the dual condition, as described in Theorem~\ref{TheA}.

\item[(c)] $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is equivalent to a product of pretorsion theories of the form $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\1)$ and $(\mathbb{D},\0,\mathbb{D})$ described in Corollaries~\ref{LemD} and \ref{LemC}, where $\mathbb{D}$ is a quasi-pointed category and $\mathbb{C}$ is a category satisfying the dual condition.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
First, let us note that (b)$\Rightarrow$(c) is obvious and (c)$\Rightarrow$(a) holds by Theorem~\ref{TheA}. It then suffices to prove
(a)$\Rightarrow$(b). Suppose $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is a rectangular pretorsion theory. By \textrm{Corollary}~\ref{corequivtf}, the canonical equivalence $\Gamma\:\mathcal{C}\to \mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{F}$ lifts to an equivalence of pretorsion theories between $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ and $$(\mathcal{T}\times_{}\mathcal{F},\hspace{0.1ex}\mathcal{T}\times_{}(\mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}),(\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F})\times_{} \mathcal{F}).$$
By \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{LemE}, all objects in $\mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$ are isomorphic to an object $Z$ which is terminal in $\mathcal{T}$ and initial in $\mathcal{F}$. Moreover, again by \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{LemE}, $\mathcal{F}$ is quasi-pointed and $\mathcal{T}$ satisfies the dual condition. So $(\mathcal{T}\times_{}\mathcal{F},\hspace{0.1ex}\mathcal{T}\times_{}(\mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}),(\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F})\times_{} \mathcal{F})$ is in fact of the form described in Theorem~\ref{TheA}.
<!-- end proof-16 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
A {\itshape rectangular torsion theory} is a rectangular pretorsion theory $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ where $\mathbb{C}$ is a pointed category, i.e.\ has zero object $0$, and $\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F}=\0$, where $\0$ is the subcategory of zero objects of $\mathbb{C}$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{proppointedcase}
    For a pretorsion theory $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ where $\mathbb{C}$ is a non-empty category, the following conditions are equivalent:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[(a)] $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is a rectangular torsion theory.
        \item[(b)] $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is equivalent to a product of pretorsion theories $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\0)$ and $(\mathbb{D},\0,\mathbb{D})$ described in Corollaries~\ref{LemD} and \ref{LemC}, where both $\mathbb{C}$ and $\mathbb{D}$ are pointed categories.
    \end{itemize}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
We prove $(a) 
	\ext@arrow 0055{\Rightarrowfill@}{}{}(b)$. By \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{LemE}, all objects in $\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F}$ are isomorphic to an object $Z$ which is terminal in $\mathcal{T}$ and initial in $\mathcal{F}$. Since $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is a rectangular torsion theory, $Z$ needs to be a zero object $0$ of $\mathbb{C}$. But then $0\in \mathcal{T}\subseteq \mathbb{C}$, whence it needs to be an initial object of $\mathcal{T}$. And we had already proved that $0$ is terminal in $\mathcal{T}$. So $0$ is a zero object in $\mathcal{T}$, and analogously also a zero object in $\mathcal{F}$. We conclude by \textrm{Theorem}~\ref{theorcharactcart}.

    We now prove $(b) 
	\ext@arrow 0055{\Rightarrowfill@}{}{}(a)$. Of course, by \textrm{Theorem}~\ref{theorcharactcart}, the product $(\mathbb{C}\times_{} \mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}\times \mathbf{0},\mathbf{0}\times\mathbb{D})$ of $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\0)$ and $(\mathbb{D},\0,\mathbb{D})$ is a rectangular pretorsion theory. We then have that $(0,0)$ is a zero object of $\mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{D}$, because it is a zero object componentwise. So $\mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{D}$ is pointed. Finally, notice that $(\mathbb{C}\times_{} \0) \cap (\0\times_{} \mathbb{D})=\0\times_{} \0$, which coincides with the subcategory of zero objects of $\mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{D}$.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{theorcharactcartpointed}
For a torsion theory $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is a rectangular torsion theory.

\item[(b)] $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is equivalent to a torsion theory of the form $(\mathbb{C}\times_{} \mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}\times \mathbf{0},\mathbf{0}\times\mathbb{D})$ where $\mathbb{D}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ are pointed categories.

\item[(c)] $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is equivalent to a product of torsion theories of the form $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\0)$ and $(\mathbb{D},\0,\mathbb{D})$.
\end{itemize}

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{propGJpointed}
Every torsion theory is a torsion theory in the sense of \cite{GJ20}. A torsion theory in the sense of \cite{GJ20} that is a rectangular pretorsion theory is automatically a rectangular torsion theory.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Of course, every torsion theory in our sense is a torsion theory in the sense of \cite{GJ20}. Let $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ be a pretorsion theory in the sense of \cite{GJ20} which is rectangular. We thus have kernels $k(X)\xrightarrow[]{k}X$ and cokernels $X\xrightarrow[]{c}c(X)$ of all identities $\operatorname{id}_{X}$. We prove that $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is a torsion theory. By \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{lemmakercoker}, $k(X)$ and $c(X)$ need to be objects in $\mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$. But by \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{LemE}, all objects in $\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F}$ are isomorphic to a fixed object $Z$ which is terminal in $\mathcal{T}$ and initial in $\mathcal{F}$. So both $k(X)$ and $c(X)$ are isomorphic to $Z$. We prove that $Z$ is a zero object in $\mathbb{C}$. By the argument above, for every $X\in \mathbb{C}$, there exists a morphism $k\:Z\to X$ which is the kernel of $\operatorname{id}_{X}$. Let $r$ be another morphism $Z\to X$. Since $\operatorname{id}_{}\circ r$ is null, there exists a unique morphism $w\:Z\to Z$ such that $k\circ w=r$. But by the proof of \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{LemE}, the only morphism from $Z$ to $Z$ is the identity. So that $k=r$. We conclude that $Z$ is initial in $\mathbb{C}$. Analogously, $Z$ is terminal in $\mathbb{C}$, as it gives the cokernels of all identities.
<!-- end proof-18 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
Let $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ be a rectangular pretorsion theory. We showed in the proof of \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{LemE} that $\mathcal{T}$ has cokernels of all identities, and $\mathcal{F}$ has kernels of all identities. These are given by some fixed $Z$ such that all objects in $\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F}$ are isomorphic to $Z$ and such that $Z$ is terminal in $\mathcal{T}$ and initial in $\mathcal{F}$. However, $\mathbb{C}$ only has kernels and cokernels of all identities when it is pointed, thanks to \textrm{Proposition}~\ref{propGJpointed}.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
A rectangular pretorsion theory $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is called {\itshape symmetrical} if it is equivalent to a product of pretorsion theories $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\1)$ and $(\mathbb{D},\0,\mathbb{D})$ described in Corollaries~\ref{LemD} and \ref{LemC}, where both $\mathbb{C}$ and $\mathbb{D}$ are quasi-pointed and both satisfy the dual condition.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
We call {\itshape bi-quasi-pointed} those categories that are quasi-pointed and also satisfy the dual condition.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rembiquasipointed}
    A category is bi-quasi-pointed if and only if it has initial object 0 and terminal object 1, and the unique morphism $0\to 1$ is both mono and epi. Of course, every pointed category is bi-quasi-pointed.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{propcharactsymm}
    For a rectangular pretorsion theory $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ where $\mathbb{C}$ is a non-empty category, the following conditions are equivalent:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[(a)] $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is symmetrical.
        \item[(b)] $\mathbb{C}$ is bi-quasi-pointed.
    \end{itemize}
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
We prove $(a) 
	\ext@arrow 0055{\Rightarrowfill@}{}{}(b)$. Assume that $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is equivalent to a product of pretorsion theories $(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\1)$ and $(\mathbb{D},\0,\mathbb{D})$ where both $\mathbb{C}$ and $\mathbb{D}$ are bi-quasi-pointed. The pair $(0_{\mathbb{C}},0_{\mathbb{D}})$ with $0_{\mathbb{C}}$ initial in $\mathbb{C}$ and $0_{\mathbb{D}}$ initial in $\mathbb{D}$ is initial in $\mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{D}$. Analogously, $(1_{\mathbb{C}},1_{\mathbb{D}})$ is terminal in $\mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{D}$. The unique morphism $(0_{\mathbb{C}},0_{\mathbb{D}})\to (1_{\mathbb{C}},1_{\mathbb{D}})$ is both mono and epi, because it is so componentwise.

    We now prove $(b) 
	\ext@arrow 0055{\Rightarrowfill@}{}{}(a)$. By the proof of \textrm{Theorem}~\ref{theorcharactcart}, $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is equivalent to the product of pretorsion theories $(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F})$ and $(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})$, and we know that $\mathcal{F}$ is quasi-pointed and $\mathcal{T}$ satisfies the dual condition. By assumption, $\mathbb{C}$ is bi-quasi-pointed. We show that $0\in \mathcal{T}$ and $1\in \mathcal{F}$. By \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{LemE}, all objects in $\mathcal{T}\cap \mathcal{F}$ are isomorphic to an object $Z$ which is terminal in $\mathcal{T}$ and initial in $\mathcal{F}$. Consider the short exact sequence $T^0\xrightarrow[]{\ell} 0\xrightarrow[]{r}F^0$ associated to $0$. The morphism $r$ is the unique morphism from $0$ to $F^0$, so it needs to coincide with the composite $0\xrightarrow[]{r^1} Z\xrightarrow[]{r^2} F^0$, where $r^1$ is given by the fact that $0$ is initial in $\mathbb{C}$ and $r^2$ by the fact that $Z$ is initial in $\mathcal{F}$. Then $r$ is null, whence $\ell$ needs to be an isomorphism (as identities give kernels of null maps). So $0\in \mathcal{T}$. Analogously, $1\in \mathcal{F}$. We thus have that $\mathcal{T}$ has initial object given by $0$ and terminal object given by $Z$. By the proof of \textrm{Lemma}~\ref{LemE}, all morphisms in $\mathcal{T}$ going into $Z$ are epi. Moreover, all morphisms in $\mathcal{T}$ going out from $0$ are mono, because this is true in $\mathbb{C}$ by assumption. So $\mathcal{T}$ is bi-quasi-pointed. Analogously, $\mathcal{F}$ is bi-quasi-pointed. And we conclude.
<!-- end proof-19 -->
<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{propcatcarttors}
    Every rectangular torsion theory is a symmetrical rectangular pretorsion theory. Moreover, the full sub-2-category of $\mathbf{PTor}_{\operatorname{R}}$ on rectangular torsion theories coincides with the full sub-2-category of $\mathbf{PTor}_{\operatorname{SR}}$ of rectangular torsion theories.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Every pointed category is bi-quasi-pointed, by \textrm{Remark}~\ref{rembiquasipointed}, so every rectangular torsion theory is symmetrical thanks to \textrm{Proposition}~\ref{propcharactsymm}. Moreover, every 1-cell in $\mathbf{PTor}_{\operatorname{R}}$ between rectangular torsion theories preserves the zero object. Indeed, it needs to preserve torsion objects and torsion-free objects, whence also the objects in the intersection $\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F}=\0$. So every every 1-cell in $\mathbf{PTor}_{\operatorname{R}}$ between rectangular torsion theories is a 1-cell in $\mathbf{PTor}_{\operatorname{SR}}$.

We first prove that $M$ is a (strict) 2-monad. If $\mathbb{C}$ is a bi-quasi-pointed category, also $\mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{C}$ is such, with $0=(0,0)$ and $1=(1,1)$. The unique map from $(0,0)$ to $(1,1)$ is componentwise mono and epi, and thus mono and epi. If $G$ preserves $0$ and $1$ then also $G\times_{} G$ preserves $0$ and $1$. It is straightforward to see that $M$ is a $2$-functor. We define the unit $\eta\:\operatorname{Id}_{} 
	\ext@arrow 0055{\Rightarrowfill@}{}{}M$ to have component on $\mathbb{C}$ given by the diagonal functor $\eta_\mathbb{C}\:\mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{C}$. Of course $\eta_\mathbb{C}$ preserves $0$ and $1$, and $\eta$ is 2-natural. We then define the multiplication $\mu\:M^2 
	\ext@arrow 0055{\Rightarrowfill@}{}{}M$ to have component on $\mathbb{C}$ given by the projection functor
$$\mu_\mathbb{C}=\pi_{1,4}=\pi_1\times_{}\pi_2\:(\mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{C})\times_{}(\mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{C})\to \mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{C}$$
on the first and fourth components. Of course $\mu_C$ preserves $0$ and $1$. It is straightfoward to show that $\mu$ is a $2$-natural transformation. The unit axioms of $2$-monad 
\begin{cd}[4][6]
    M \ar[rd,bend right=20,equal]\ar[r,Rightarrow,"{M\eta}"]\& M^2 \ar[d,Rightarrow,"{\mu}"]\& M \ar[l,Rightarrow,"{\eta M}"'] \ar[ld,bend left=20,equal]\\
    \& M
\end{cd}
are satisfied because for every $X,Y\in \mathbb{C}\times_{}\mathbb{C}$ we have
$$\pi_{1,4}((X,X),(Y,Y))=(X,Y)=\pi_{1,4}((X,Y),(X,Y))$$ (and analogously for morphisms). The other axiom
\begin{cd}[5.5][5.5]
    M^3 \ar[r,Rightarrow,"{M \mu}"] \ar[d,Rightarrow,"{\mu M}"']\& M^2 \ar[d,Rightarrow,"{\mu}"] \\
    M^2 \ar[r,Rightarrow,"{\mu}"']\& M
\end{cd}
is satisfied because for every $(X_1,\ldots,X_8)$ we have $$\pi_{1,4}((X_1,X_4),(X_5,X_8))=(X_1,X_8)=\pi_{1,4}((X_1,X_2),(X_7,X_8)).$$
So $M$ is a $2$-monad.

        
We now prove that the 2-category $\mathbf{PTor}_{\operatorname{SR}}$ is 2-equivalent over $\mathbf{BiQPointCat}$ to the 2-category $
	             
	\ensuremath{\IfBooleanT{+}{\mathbf{Ps}\mbox{-}}{#3}\mbox{-}\mathbf{\IfBooleanT{#2}{Co}Alg}}
{M}$ of pseudo-algebras of $M$. Notice that we have a forgetful $2$-functor $\mathbf{PTor}_{\operatorname{SR}}\to \mathbf{BiQPointCat}$, thanks to \textrm{Proposition}~\ref{propcharactsymm}.

We start showing that every symmetrical rectangular pretorsion theory $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ gives a pseudo-algebra for $M$. Let $\Gamma\: \mathbb{C} \to \mathcal{T} \times_{} \mathcal{F}\:\Gamma'$ be the adjoint equivalence of categories associated to $(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ (it can always be made into an adjoint equivalence). We construct a pseudo-algebra
$$(\mathbb{C},Q\: \mathbb{C} \times_{} \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}, Q_\mu\: Q \circ (Q \times_{} Q) \Rightarrow Q \circ \pi_{1,4}, Q_\eta\: \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{C}} \Rightarrow Q \circ \eta_{\mathbb{C}}).$$ We define the functor $Q\: \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C} \times_{} \mathbb{C}$ as the composite 
$$\mathbb{C} \times_{} \mathbb{C} \xrightarrow[]{\Gamma \times_{} \Gamma} (\mathcal{T} \times_{} \mathcal{F}) \times_{} (\mathcal{T} \times_{} \mathcal{F}) \xrightarrow[]{\pi_{1,4}} \mathcal{T} \times_{} \mathcal{F} \xrightarrow[]{\Gamma'} \mathbb{C}.$$
So $Q$ sends $(X,Y)\in \mathbb{C} \times_{} \mathbb{C}$ to $\Gamma'(T^X, F^Y)$. Notice that $Q$ preserves $0$ and $1$. Indeed, $Q(0,0)=\Gamma'(0,Z)\cong 0$, because $\Gamma'(0,Z)$ sits in a short exact sequence $0\xrightarrow[]{l} \Gamma'(0,Z) \xrightarrow[]{r} Z$ and $r$ is null so $l$ needs to be an isomorphism. Analogously $Q(1,1)= \Gamma'(Z,1)\cong 1$. 
We then define $Q_\mu\: Q \circ (Q \times_{} Q) \Rightarrow Q \circ \pi_{1,4}$ as the pasting
\begin{eqD*}

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{remlimitsinpoincat}
    It is folklore that one can always formally adjoin a zero object to any category. More precisely, $\mathbf{PointCat}$ is 2-equivalent to the 2-category of strict algebras and pseudo-morphisms for the $2$-monad $K$ on $\mathbf{Cat}$ that sends a category $\mathbb{C}$ to the category constructed by formally adjoining a zero object to $\mathbb{C}$. As a consequence,
                   
                                                  
    $\mathbf{PointCat}$ has all PIE limits, and in particular all bilimits, created by the forgetful $2$-functor into $\mathbf{Cat}$.
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- corollary-7: body_md -->
\label{corlimitscarttors}
    $\mathbf{Tor}_{\operatorname{R}}$ has all bilimits, created by the forgetful $2$-functor $\mathbf{Tor}_{\operatorname{R}}\to \mathbf{PointCat}$ and thus calculated in $\mathbf{Cat}$ (thanks to \textrm{Remark}~\ref{remlimitsinpoincat}).

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
The recipe for constructing weak products (also called biproducts) of rectangular torsion theories given by \textrm{Corollary}~\ref{corlimitscarttors} coincides with the recipe for products described in \textrm{Theorem}~\ref{TheB}. Exactly as the recipe for weak products also works in the setting of general pretorsion theories and gives products, one could try to take the recipes for bilimits given by \textrm{Corollary}~\ref{corlimitscarttors} and see if they also work in the general setting.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
For an object $e\colon X\to Y$ in $\mathcal{E}$, a diagram
$$\xymatrix{A\ar[r]^-{a}\ar[d]_-{} & X\ar[r]^-{be}\ar[d]^-{e} & B\ar[d]^-{\operatorname{id}_{B}} \\ 0\ar[r] & Y\ar[r]_{b} & B }$$
is an exact sequence in $\mathcal{E}$ if and only if $a$ is a kernel of $e$ while $e$ is a cokernel of $a$, and, $b$ is an isomorphism.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- theorem-9: body_md -->
\label{TheD}
$(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is a torsion
theory if and only if every object of $\mathcal{E}$ is a cokernel in $\mathbb{X}$ and has a kernel in $\mathbb{X}$. When $\mathbb{X}$ has binary products, this torsion theory is rectangular if and only if every object of $\mathcal{E}$ is a product projection in $\mathbb{X}$.
<!-- end theorem-9 -->
<!-- theorem-10: body_md -->
\label{TheE}
The class of product projections in a pointed category having binary products is a torsion theory class if and only if the category has normal projections, and, if and only if it is a rectangular torsion theory class.
<!-- end theorem-10 -->
<!-- theorem-11: body_md -->
\label{TheF}
The class of split epimorphisms in a pointed regular category is a torsion theory class if and only if the category is normal, and, if and only if the class of regular epimorphisms is a torsion theory class.
<!-- end theorem-11 -->
<!-- theorem-12: body_md -->
\label{TheG}
The class of split epimorphisms in a pointed regular category is a rectangular torsion theory class if and only if every split epimorphism is a product projection. The class of regular epimorphisms is a rectangular torsion theory class if and only if every regular epimorphism is a product projection.
