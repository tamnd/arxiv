\rm
A characterisation of $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}$ is closely related to a problem raised in \cite{Langlands} by R.Langlands that was resolved by M.Borovoi, P.Deligne, J.Milne, K.Shih and others. (We only need the case of the product of Shimura curves.)
A key outcome of the theory is the proof of existence and uniqueness of {\bf canonical models} of Shimura varieties.

Recall that a Shimura variety is given by a Shimura datum $(G,X)$, where $X$ is a complex manifold and $G$ is a reductive linear algebraic group such that $G({\mathbb R})$ acts on $X.$
The corresponding Shimura variety is defined as
                                                                      
$$\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_K:=G({\mathbb Q})\backslash(X\times G(\mathbb{A}_f)/K)$$
where $\mathbb{A}_f$ is the ring of finite adeles and $K$ runs through the family of open compact subgroups of $G(\mathbb{A}_f).$ For each $K,$ the set $\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_K$ of double cosets naturally possesses the structure of a complex quasi-projective algebraic variety.  The canonical model, which we also denote $\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_K$, is a model for $\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_K$ over an explicit number field $E(G,X)$. For $X=\mathbb{H}^{\pm}:= {\mathbb C}\setminus {\mathbb R}$ and $G=\mathrm{GL}_2$ 
we get the family of modular curves $\mathrm{Sh}(G,X)_K$ defined over $\mathbb{Q}$. We refer to \cite{Milne0} for a general survey and \cite{Milne1} for the case of Shimura curves. 

 Denote
$$\mathrm{S}:=\mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb Q})\backslash (\mathbb{H}^{\pm}\times \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f)),$$
{\em the covering of canonical Shimura curves},
and $\Delta$ the subgroup of matrices of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f)$ of the form $\left(\begin{array}{ll}
a\ 0\\ 0\ 1
\end{array}\right),$ $a\in \hat{{\mathbb Z}}^\times.$ Then there is a holomorphic covering map $\mathrm{S}\to \tilde{\mathbb{H}}$ which represents $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}$ as  $$\tilde{\mathbb{H}}\cong \mathrm{S}/\Delta= \mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb Q})\backslash (\mathbb{H}^{\pm}\times \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f))/\Delta.$$
This essentially reduces the study of $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}$ to the study of
$\mathrm{S}.$

\rm In our analysis it is important to distinguish the full structure on $\mathrm{S}$ as a complex manifold with infinitely many connected components, which we name $\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Full},$ and the purely algebraic structure  $\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure},$ which is just a set split into connected components with the action of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f)$ on it. 

It is clear that a CM-point on the $\mathbb{Y}(N)$ comes from a point on $\mathrm{S}$  fixed by a $g\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f)$ which is not in the centre of $\mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb Q})$ (the centre  of $\mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb Q})$ acts trivially on $\mathrm{S}$). \item[(i)] $[\tau,a]$ is a fixed point of some non-central  element $g\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f)$ if and only if $\tau\in \mathbb{H}$ is quadratic and $g\in a^{-1} G_\tau a$;
                                          

                                     
                                          

\item[(ii)] if $q\tau=\tau$ then $[\tau,a]$ is a fixed point of $q$ iff there is   $q'\in G_\tau$ such that $a qa^{-1}= q'$ iff $a\in N_\tau ;$


\item[(iii)] $[\tau,aK]$ are special points on $\mathrm{S}_K$ if and only if  $\tau\in \mathbb{H}$ is quadratic.

\end{itemize}

\end{array}\right),\ \  q'=\left( \begin{array}{ll}
\ s'\ mt'\\ -t'\ \ s'
\end{array}\right)
$$

$$q=a\left( \begin{array}{ll}
s\ mt\\ -t\ s
\end{array}\right)a^{-1}= \left( \begin{array}{ll}
s\ 0\\ 0\ s
\end{array}\right)+a\left( \begin{array}{ll}
0\ mt\\ -t\ 0
\end{array}\right)a^{-1}
$$
and thus 
$$a\left( \begin{array}{ll}

\end{array}\right)\mbox{ where } s''=s'-s.

\rm\label{orbits} {\bf Lemma.} {\em Given a quadratic $\tau\in \mathbb{H},$ the Hecke orbit $\mathrm{S}_\tau$ can be represented as $$\mathrm{S}_\tau= \mathrm{S}_{\sqrt{-n}}$$ for some 
 positive square-free integer $n,$ and this representation is unique.}
 
 {\bf Proof.} By definition $\tau= a\sqrt{-n}+b$ for some  $a,b\in {\mathbb Q}$ and a  positive square-free integer $n.$ Then
 $\tau= r \sqrt{-n}$ for $r=\left( \begin{array}{ll}
 a\ b\\
 0\ 1
\end{array}\right),$ that is $\tau \in  \mathrm{S}_{\sqrt{-n}}.$  

In order to prove uniqueness consider the assumption that
$[\sqrt{-m},g_1]=[\sqrt{-n},g_2]$ for a square-free positive integer $m$ and $g_1,g_2\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f).$ This is equivalent to 
$[\sqrt{-m},1]=[\sqrt{-n},g],$ for $g=g_2g_1^{-1}.$  By (\ref{qak}) the latter is equivalent to 
$$(\sqrt{-m},1)=(q\sqrt{-n},qg),\mbox{ for some }q\in \mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb Q}),$$
that is $\sqrt{-m}=q\sqrt{-n}.$ This is possible only if $m=n.$ 

The uniqueness of representation follows. $\Box$

The definition of level structure assumes that $\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}\,\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure} $ preserves the equivalence relations (\ref{qak}) and   commutes with the action of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f),$ that is (\ref{autg}) holds and, for $q\in \mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb Q}),$ 
\begin{equation} \label{aut0} q\tau=q\Longrightarrow [\tau, qa]^\sigma=[\tau,a]^\sigma.\end{equation} 
Also, condition (iii) of \ref{definitionS} imposes the condition for arbitrary $\tau_1,\tau_2\in \mathbb{H}$ and arbitrary $a_1,a_2\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f),$
$$  [\tau_1,a_1], [\tau_2,a_2]\in \mathrm{S}^\mu \Rightarrow
  [\tau_1,a_1]^\sigma, [\tau_2,a_2]^\sigma\in \mathrm{S}^{\mu^\sigma}.$$
This implies for $E$ and $E_K$ defined in \ref{Fact2.6}(iii)
\begin{equation} \label{components} E_K(s_1,s_2) \Leftrightarrow E_K(s_1^\sigma,s_2^\sigma);\ \ \ E(s_1,s_2) \Leftrightarrow E(s_1^\sigma,s_2^\sigma).\end{equation}



 
On $\mathrm{S}\setminus \bigcup_{\tau\in \mathrm{CM}}\mathrm{S}_\tau$ the action of 
$\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f)/Z({\mathbb Q})$ is free and hence the only condition on automorphisms of the $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f)/Z({\mathbb Q})$-set is the condition (\ref{components}).
 
 So we concentrate on the case that $\tau$ is quadratic, in which case
 $\mathrm{S}_\tau= \mathrm{S}_{\sqrt{-n}}$ for some 
 positive square-free integer $n$.

Note that   $\mathrm{S}_\tau$ is invariant under   $\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}.$ Indeed, $s\in \mathrm{S}_\tau$ if and only if $s=a^{-1} *\tau$ for some $a\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f),$ which is equivalent by \ref{specpoints}(i) to the condition that $s$ is  fixed by a $g\in a^{-1} G_\tau a.$ But $g*s=s$  implies $g*s^\sigma=s^\sigma$  by (\ref{autg}). 

 Also, by \ref{specpoints} $\mathrm{S}_\tau=\mathrm{S}_\tau(\mathrm{CM}),$ that is the orbit consists of special points.
 
 We study $\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_\tau,$ the substructure of $\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}$ on the set   $\mathrm{S}_\tau,$ $\tau$ quadratic.

\rm \label{rtsProp} {\bf Proposition.}{\em For a quadratic $\tau,$ for any
$\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_\tau$ 
                                                                
there is 
$r=r_\tau(\sigma)\in N_\tau $ such that 
 \begin{equation}\label{rsig} [\tau, a]^\sigma:= [\tau, r a],\mbox{ for all }a\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f). \end{equation}

Conversely, every $r\in  N_\tau $ determines a $\sigma=\sigma_{r,\tau}\in  \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_\tau$ by the formula {\rm (\ref{rsig})} and 
\begin{equation} \label{rsig2} \sigma_{r,\tau}=\sigma_{r',\tau} \Leftrightarrow
r'\cdot r^{-1}\in G_\tau.
\end{equation}
}
 

 
 {\bf Proof.}
Suppose 
$$[\tau, 1]^\sigma=[\tau, r_\tau(\sigma)], \mbox{ for some } r_\tau(\sigma)\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f),$$
and hence, for any $a\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f),$
 \begin{equation}\label{rts} [\tau, a]^\sigma=[\tau, 1\cdot a]^\sigma=a^{-1}\cdot [\tau,1]^\sigma= [\tau, r_\tau(\sigma)\cdot a].\end{equation}
 
\medskip


We claim that  $r_\tau(\sigma)\in N_\tau .$
                                      
Indeed, let $\tau$ be the fixed point of $q\in  \mathrm{GL}_2^+({\mathbb Q}),$ $q\notin Z({\mathbb Q}).$ Then by \ref{specpoints} $[\tau,1]$ is a fixed point of $q$ and $q^{-1}$ and so 
$$[\tau,q]=[\tau,1]\mbox{ and } [\tau, r_\tau(\sigma)\cdot q]=  
[\tau,r_\tau(\sigma)].$$ 

Hence $[\tau,r_\tau(\sigma)]$ is a fixed point of $q$ and thus by \ref{specpoints}(ii) $r_\tau(\sigma)\in N_\tau .$



Conversely, if $r\in N_\tau$ then for each $q\in G_\tau\setminus Z({\mathbb Q})$ there is $q'\in G_\tau,$ 
$$[\tau, r]=[\tau, q'r]=[\tau,rq]=q* [\tau,r].$$ 
                                                                                                                              
 
Define  the map $\sigma_{r,\tau}: \mathrm{S}_\tau\to \mathrm{S}_\tau,$  a partial transformation of $\mathrm{S},$   by setting
for each $a\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f)$ 
$$[\tau,a]^{\sigma_{r,\tau}}:= [\tau,ra].$$
Then $\sigma_{r,\tau}$ trivially satisfies (\ref{autg}). Also  (\ref{aut0}) is satisfied   because $rq=q'r$. This proves that $\sigma_{r,\tau}$ acts as an automorphism on $\mathrm{S}_\tau$ and $r=r_\tau(\sigma_{r,\tau}).$
 

                                
                                      
                                                                       
Now note that by (\ref{qak})
$[\tau, r]=[\tau,1],$ for $r\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f),$ holds  if and only if 
$r\in G_\tau.$ This proves (\ref{rsig2}). 
 $\Box$

$$N_{\tau} /G_{\tau}\cong\mathrm{Aut}\, \mathrm{S}_{\tau}^\mathrm{Full}=\mathrm{Aut}\, \mathrm{S}_{\tau}^\mathrm{Pure}\cong \mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb Q}(\tau)^\mathrm{ab}:{\mathbb Q})$$

 (iii) For any $g\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f),$
 $$g^\sigma=g.$$

Compare the conclusion of \ref{rtsProp} with \cite{Milne0}, 12.8 describing the action on the special points $\mathrm{S}_\tau$  of the canonical model by

-c\ nd\\ -d\ \ c

 \cite{MilneCC}. Lemma 3.2  of that paper states in particular

\rm \label{TT1} {\bf Proposition.}
 

  
 
 
 

(i) {\em  For all $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_{\bar{\tau}}:$

  $\bar{r}_{\bar{\tau}}(\sigma)\in \prod_i N_{\tau_i} $ and
   $\bar{r}_{\bar{\tau}}(\sigma)$ can be represented as
    $\langle r_1,\ldots, r_n\rangle=\bar{r}_{\bar{\tau}}(\sigma)$    
   so that, for some $q_1,\ldots, q_n\in {\mathbb Q}_+,$ 
$$q_1\cdot\det r_1=\ldots =q_n\cdot\det r_n=\mu(\sigma)\in \hat{{\mathbb Z}}^{\times}.$$

Moreover, $\mu(\sigma)=1$ if and only if all the components $\mathrm{S}^\lambda$ of $\mathrm{S}$ are invariant under $\sigma,$ and $\mu(\sigma)$
 runs through all values $\mu\in \hat{{\mathbb Z}}^{\times}$ as $\sigma$ runs in $\mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Full}_{\bar{\tau}}.$
}

\medskip

(ii) {\em For any $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_{\bar{\tau}}$
one can choose a representative $\langle r_1,\ldots,r_n\rangle$ of $\bar{r}_{\bar{\tau}}(\sigma)$ and $q\in {\mathbb Q}_+$
so that, for some partition $\{ 1,\ldots,n\}=\{ i_1,\ldots, i_k\}\dot{\cup} \{ i_{k+1},\ldots, i_n\},$
the following hold:
\begin{equation}\label{riri2}  \begin{array}{lll}\det r_{i_1}=\ldots=\det r_{i_k} \mbox{ and }r_{i_1}\in T_{\tau_{i_1}},\ldots,r_{i_k}\in T_{\tau_{i_k}},\\
\det r_{i_{k+1}}=\ldots=\det r_{i_n} \mbox{ and }r_{i_{k+1}}\notin T_{\tau_{i_{k+1}}},\ldots,r_{i_n}\notin T_{\tau_{i_n}},\\
\det r_{i_1}=\ldots=\det r_{i_k}=q\det r_{i_{k+1}}=\ldots=q\det r_{i_n}
\end{array}
\end{equation}
 }

(iii) 
{\em Given $\langle r_1,\ldots,r_n\rangle\in \prod_i N_{\tau_i} $ and   $q\in {\mathbb Q}_+$ suppose there exists  a partition $\{ 1,\ldots,n\}=\{ i_1,\ldots, i_k\}\dot{\cup} \{ i_{k+1},\ldots, i_n\}$ satisfying
(\ref{riri2}). 
Then 
  there exists $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_{\bar{\tau}}$
such that $\langle r_1,\ldots,r_n\rangle$ is a representative of $\bar{r}(\sigma).$ }

\medskip









{\bf Proof.} (i) By the preservation of connected components property (\ref{propE}), given $\sigma$ there is a $\mu$ such that 
for each $\tau_i:= \sqrt{-m_i},$
$$[\tau_i, r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)]\in \mathrm{S}^\mu, \mbox{ and hence } \det r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)=q_i^{-1}\cdot\mu, \ \mu\in \hat{{\mathbb Z}}^\times, \mbox{ some } q_i\in {\mathbb Q}_+.$$

This proves the first statement of (i). For the second statement
                                                                                          
               
 use the fact that $\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb Q}^\mathrm{ab}:{\mathbb Q})$ acts faithfully and transitively on the set of components $\mathrm{S}^\mu$ of $\mathrm{S}$ (see \cite{Milne1}, 3.4) so for each $\mu$ there is a $\sigma$ and $\bar{r}_{\bar{\tau}}(\sigma)$ such that  $\bigwedge_i \det r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)= q_i \mu.$ 
 
 
(ii) In matrix terms 
we have $$r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)=r_i=\left( \begin{array}{ll}\ a_i\ \ \ b_im_i\\ 
-\epsilon_i b_i\ \ \epsilon a_i
\end{array}\right),\ \ \ \det r_i=\epsilon_i  (a_i^2 + b_i^2m_i)=  q_i^{-1}\mu,\ ( a_i,b_i\in \mathbb{A}_f)$$
where $\epsilon_i$ is $+1$ if  $r_i\in T_{\sqrt{-m_i}}$  and $-1,$ if 
$r_i\in N_{\sqrt{-m_i}}\setminus T_{\sqrt{-m_i}}.$



As a result we have the equalities
\begin{equation}\label{q1} \epsilon_i q_i (a_i^2+b_i^2m_i)= \mu = \epsilon_j q_j (a_j^2+b_j^2m_j);
                                                                                      
\ i,j=1,\ldots,n.\end{equation}


Assume that $\epsilon_i=\epsilon_j.$ 
Then the equality between the algebraic expressions takes place for 
some $a_i,b_i,a_j,b_j\in {\mathbb R},$ not both $a_i,b_i$ zero, as well. By the Hasse principle, there are $s_i,t_i\in {\mathbb Q},\ \ i=1,\ldots, n$

\begin{equation} \label{q2}\epsilon_i q_i (s_i^2+t_i^2m_i)=\epsilon_j q_j (s_j^2+t_j^2m_j).\end{equation}


Consider, for $i=1,\ldots,n,$ the  matrices with rational entries,
 $$g_i=\left( \begin{array}{ll}\ s_i\ t_im_i\\ 
 -t_i\ \  s_i
\end{array}\right),$$
which by definition belongs to $G_{\sqrt{-m_i}}.$

By dividing the algebraic expressions of (\ref{q1}) on respective expressions of (\ref{q2}) we get
$$(a_i^2+b_i^2m_i)(s_i^2+t_i^2m_i)^{-1}=(a_j^2+b_j^2m_j)(s_j^2+t_j^2m_j),$$
and recalling the formula for $\det r_i$  
we have  $$\det r_i g_i^{-1}=\epsilon_i(a_i^2+b_i^2m_i)(s_i^2+t_i^2m_i)^{-1}=\epsilon_j(a_j^2+b_j^2m_j)(s_j^2+t_j^2m_j)^{-1}=
\det r_jg_j^{-1}.$$
Choose  representatives for $\bar{r}_{\bar{\tau}}(\sigma)$ modulo $G_{\bar{\tau}}$ as 
$$r_i:= r_i g_i^{-1}$$

Suppose $\epsilon_{i_1}=\ldots=\epsilon_{i_k}=1$ and $\epsilon_{i_{k+1}}=\ldots=\epsilon_{i_n}=-1.$

We get, for some $q\in {\mathbb Q}_+,$ from above that
$$\begin{array}{lll}\det r_{i_1}=\ldots=\det r_{i_k} \mbox{ and }r_{i_1}\in T_{i_1},\ldots,r_{i_k}\in T_{i_k}\\
\det r_{i_{k+1}}=\ldots=\det r_{i_n} \mbox{ and }r_{i_{k+1}}\notin T_{i_{k+1}},\ldots,r_{i_n}\notin T_{i_n}\\
\det r_{i_1}=\ldots=\det r_{i_k}=q\det r_{i_{k+1}}=\ldots=q\det r_{i_n}
\end{array}
$$ 

(iii) The condition $\bar{r}=\langle r_1,\ldots,r_n\rangle\in \prod_i N_{\tau_i} $
implies that $\bar{r}$ determines a transformation of $\mathrm{S}_{\bar{\tau}}$ which satisfies (\ref{autg}) and (\ref{aut0}). The conditions (\ref{riri2}) implies that $\bar{r}$ satisfies (\ref{propE}). Thus the transformation belongs to $\mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_{\bar{\tau}}.$
$\Box$

 Hence

\rm\label{C} {\bf The structure $(\mathrm{S}_\approx,  \tilde{\mathrm{G}}).$}
The action of $\tilde{\mathrm{G}}$ on $\mathrm{S}_\approx$ induces

 {\em Special points.} 
It is immediate from \ref{4.3(c)} and \ref{specpoints} that 
  the fixed points $s$ of  elements of   $g\in \tilde{\mathrm{G}}$ in $\mathrm{S}_\approx$ can be represented as $s=[\tau,a\cdot \Delta],$ $\det a\in {\mathbb Q}_+,$
  for $\tau$ quadratic such that 
                                       
$g*[\tau,a]=[\tau,a].$ 
                                                       

                                                                                                                                        
                                   

Respectively, the special points of  $\mathrm{S}_{\approx_{K}}$ are of the form $[\tau,a\cdot\Delta\cdot K],$ for $\tau\in \mathbb{H}$ quadratic.

\medskip
       
    
{\em 4-point relations between special points} (induced by the equivalence relation $E,$ see \ref{definitionS}(iii)). 

For each quadratic $\tau_1, \tau_2$                                                                  
define the 4-ary relation $R_{\tau_1,\tau_2}(s_1,s_2,t_1,t_2)$ on $\mathrm{S}_\approx:$
$$R_{\tau_1,\tau_2}(s_1,s_2,t_1,t_2)\Leftrightarrow $$
$$ \Leftrightarrow \bigwedge_{i=1,2}s_i=[\tau_i,a_i]_\approx \ \& \ t_i=[\tau_i,b_i]_\approx\ \& \
\exists r_1\in N_{\tau_1}, r_2\in N_{\tau_2}\ $$ $$ \det r_1=\det r_2\in \Delta \ \& \
\bigwedge_{i=1,2} b_i=r_i\cdot a_i\cdot \det r_i^{-1}
$$
where we assume that the representation of the $s_i$ and $t_i$ is canonical and $\det r_i$ takes its values in $\Delta$ via the isomorphism $\Delta\cong \hat{{\mathbb Z}}^\times.$ Note that we may always assume
that $\tau_1=\sqrt{-m_1}$ and $\tau_2=\sqrt{-m_2}$ for positive square-free integers.

Now set 
$$R(s_1,s_2,t_1,t_2)\equiv \bigvee R_{\sqrt{-m_1},\sqrt{-m_2}}(s_1,s_2,t_1,t_2)$$
where $m_1,m_2$ run through square-free positive integers.
\medskip

(a) {\em Relation  $R$ is invariant under $\mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_\approx.$   } 
Indeed, by \ref{Autapprox} any $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_\approx$ is induced by the unique $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}.$ The latter acts on special points by
$$[\tau_i,a_i]\mapsto [\tau_i,r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)\cdot a_i]; \ \
 [\tau_i,b_i]\mapsto [\tau_i,r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)\cdot b_i],$$
where $r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)\in N_{\tau_i},$ $\det r_{\tau_1}(\sigma)=\det r_{\tau_2}(\sigma).$  Respectively, in terms of  $\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_\approx$ the result of the application of $\sigma$ are
 $[\tau_i,a'_i]_\approx$ and $[\tau_i,b'_i]_\approx$ where
 $$a'_i=r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)\cdot a_i\cdot \det r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)^{-1}; \ \
 b'_i=r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)\cdot b_i\cdot \det r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)^{-1}$$
 Assuming $R_{\tau_1,\tau_2}(s_1,s_2,t_1,t_2),$ for $s_i=[\tau_i,a_i]_\approx\ \& \ t_i=[\tau_i,b_i]_\approx$ we get by definition
 $$b'_i=(r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)\cdot r_i\cdot r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)^{-1})\cdot a'_i\cdot \det (r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)\cdot r_i\cdot r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)^{-1})^{-1} $$
 and $r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)\cdot r_i\cdot r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)\in N_{\tau_i}.$
 Thus $R_{\tau_1,\tau_2}(s_1^\sigma,s_2^\sigma,t_1^\sigma,t_2^\sigma)$ holds.
 
 (b) {\em  For any special $s_1=[\tau_1,a_1]_\approx,\ s_2=[\tau_2,a_2]_\approx$ and any $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}$ it holds
$R(s_1,s_2, s_1^\sigma,  s_2^\sigma)$, where $s^\sigma$ is the  induced action.}

Indeed, we have
$$\sigma: [\tau_i,a_i]_\approx\mapsto [\tau_i,r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)\cdot a_1]_\approx$$
and in terms of canonical representatives
 $$\sigma: [\tau_i,a_i]\mapsto [\tau_i,r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)\cdot a_1\cdot \det r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)^{-1}]$$
 as required by $R_{\tau_1,\tau_2}.$ $R(s_1, s_1^\sigma, s_2, s_2^\sigma)$ follows.

(c) {\em For any special $s_1=[\tau_1,a_1]_\approx,\ s_2=[\tau_2,a_2]_\approx,$

$R(s_1,s_2, t_1,  t_2)$ iff there is  $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}$ such that $t_1=s_1^\sigma$ and $t_2=s_2^\sigma.$}

Proof. (b) proves one direction. In the opposite direction use the fact that by definition 
$$t_i=[\tau_i, r_i\cdot a_i\cdot \det r_i^{-1}]_\approx=[\tau_i, r_i\cdot a_i]_\approx$$
where $\det r_1=\det r_2 \in \Delta.$ By \ref{Cor316} there is $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}$ such that
$r_i=r_{\tau_i}(\sigma)$ modulo ${\mathbb Q}_+.$ Using the Hasse principle as in the proof of \ref{TT1}(ii) we can make this an equality. This proves the claim.

\rm \label{Gapprox} {\bf Theorem}. {\em The structure $\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_\approx$ 
 is bi-interpretable with the structure $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^\mathrm{Pure},$ and $\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}_\approx(\mathrm{CM})$ is bi-interpretable with $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^\mathrm{Pure}(\mathrm{CM}).$
 
                                                                                                 
                                                                     
                                                                   
                                                      
  }

{\bf Proof.} By definition, see (\ref{defSapprox}), we need to prove                                                                            
that  any automorphism of $\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}$ induces an automorphism of $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^\mathrm{Pure}$ and, conversely, any automorphism of  $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^\mathrm{Pure}$
 can be lifted to an automorphism of 
$\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}.$ The former is proved in \ref{specpointsapprox}. So we need to show that any  
$\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\,\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^\mathrm{Pure}$ can be lifted to an automorphism $\rho$ of 
$\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}.$ 

Claim. {\em Any 
$\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\,\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^\mathrm{Pure}(\mathrm{CM})$ can be lifted to an automorphism $\rho$ of 
$\mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}(\mathrm{CM}).$ }

Proof of Claim. Consider special points
$s_1,\ldots,s_n,\ldots\in \mathrm{S}_\approx$ with representatives \linebreak
$[\tau_1, a_1],\ldots,[\tau_1, a_n],\ldots\in \mathrm{S},$
$$\sigma: s_i\mapsto t_i,\ \ t_i=[\tau_i,b_i]_\approx, \mbox{ canonical representatives}.$$ 

 
                                                                      
                     
Since $\sigma$ preserves $R,$ we get 
$$\vDash  R(s_1,s_i,t_1,t_i), \mbox{ for }i=1,...,n,...$$
and hence
$$b_i=r_i\cdot a_i\cdot \det r_i^{-1},\ r_i\in N_{\tau_i}, \ \det r_1=\det r_i, \mbox{ for }i=1,...,n...$$
By  \ref{Cor316} there is a $\rho\in  \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}(\mathrm{CM})$ such that $r_i=r_{\tau_i}(\rho),$ $i=1,...,n...$,  and hence
$$s_i^\sigma=t_i=[\tau_i,b_i \Delta]=[\tau_i,a_i\Delta]^\rho=s_i^\rho,\ \ i=1,...,n... $$
Claim proved.
                                                                                                  
 
Note that the last argument in the proof of the claim also shows that $\bigwedge_i R(s_1,s_i,t_1,t_i)$ implies
the existence of $\sigma: s_i\mapsto t_i.$                                               

\medskip

Now let $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \tilde{\mathbb{H}}^\mathrm{Pure}$ and $\sigma^\mathrm{CM}$ be its restriction to  $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}(\mathrm{CM}).$ By the claim there is $\rho^\mathrm{CM}\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}(\mathrm{CM})$ whose restriction to $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}(\mathrm{CM})$ is $\sigma^\mathrm{CM}.$ 

Since special points are Zariski dense in the components $\mathrm{S}^\mu_{K(N)},$ $\rho^\mathrm{CM}$ determines the unique bijective map  
$\mathrm{S}^\mu\to \mathrm{S}^{\rho^\mathrm{CM}(\mu)}$ on the set of components of $\mathrm{S}.$ More precisely, 
$$\rho^\mathrm{CM}(\mu)=\mu\cdot \lambda_{\rho}$$
where $\lambda_{\rho}=\det r_\tau(\rho^\mathrm{CM})\in \hat{{\mathbb Z}}^\times$ is obtained from the property (\ref{rsig}) of automorphisms acting on CM-points.

Consider an arbitrary $s\in \mathrm{S}_\approx.$ It follows from the definition of $\approx$ that for each component  $\mathrm{S}^\mu$ there is a unique 
$x\in \mathrm{S}^\mu$ such that $x_\approx=s.$ Call this element $x(s,\mathrm{S}^\mu).$ 
By construction any element of $\mathrm{S}$ has the form $x(s,\mathrm{S}^\mu)$ for some
$s\in \mathrm{S}_\approx$ and a component $\mathrm{S}^\mu$ of $\mathrm{S}.$


Consider the element $\mathbf{t}\in \mathrm{GL}_2^+({\mathbb Q})$ and the set $\mathbf{t}^\Delta$  definable in $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^\mathrm{Pure}$ by definition. 
 Elements $\mathbf{t}^\mu$ of $\mathbf{t}^\Delta$ can be identified with curves $C^\mu_{\mathbf{t}/\approx}$ on $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}\times \tilde{\mathbb{H}}$ (see \ref{specpointsapprox}A(b)) and there is a one-to-one correspondence
between elements $\mathbf{t}^\mu$ of  $\mathbf{t}^\Delta$ and components $\mathrm{S}^\mu$ of $\mathrm{S}$   given by
$$C^\mu_{\mathbf{t}}=C_{\mathbf{t}}\cap (\mathrm{S}^\mu\times \mathrm{S}^\mu)$$
(see \ref{specpointsapprox}A).

Clearly, $$\rho^\mathrm{CM}: \mathbf{t}^\mu\mapsto \mathbf{t}^{ \rho^\mathrm{CM}(\mu)}=\sigma(\mathbf{t}^\mu)=\mathbf{t}^{ \sigma(\mu)},\ \ \mathbf{t}^{ \rho^\mathrm{CM}(\mu)}=\mathbf{t}^{\mu\cdot \lambda_\rho}=\mathbf{t}^{\mu\cdot \lambda_\sigma}.$$
for $\sigma$ and $\rho^\mathrm{CM}$  as above. This gives us an extension of the action of $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \tilde{\mathbb{H}}^\mathrm{Pure}$ to components of $\mathrm{S},$ $$\sigma: \mathrm{S}^\mu \to \mathrm{S}^{\mu\cdot \lambda_\sigma}.$$ 


Define the map $$\rho: \mathrm{S}\to \mathrm{S}; \ \ x(s,\mathrm{S}^\mu)^\rho:= x(s^\sigma, \mathrm{S}^{\mu\cdot \lambda_\sigma}).$$
This is the lift of $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \tilde{\mathbb{H}}^\mathrm{Pure}$ to a transformation  $\rho$ of $\mathrm{S}$ and by definition the restriction of $\rho$ to the CM-substructure is $\rho^\mathrm{CM}$   above.

By construction $\rho$ respects the splitting of $\mathrm{S}$ into components. 

In order to prove that $\rho\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}$
it remains 
to prove that $\rho$ respects the action by elements of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f).$

Consider $h\in \Delta'({\mathbb Q}_+)\cdot \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f).$
By definition \ref{specpointsapprox}A(b)) via the action on $\mathrm{S}^\mu$  $h$ induces on $\tilde{\mathbb{H}}$ the transformation
$h^\mu.$ This means that
$$h\cdot x(s,\mathrm{S}^\mu)=x(h^\mu\cdot s,\mathrm{S}^\mu)$$
and thus
$$(h\cdot x(s,\mathrm{S}^\mu))^\rho=x(h^\mu\cdot s,\mathrm{S}^\mu)^\rho=
x((h^{\mu}\cdot s)^\sigma, \mathrm{S}^{\mu\cdot \lambda_\sigma})=x(h^{\mu\cdot \lambda_\sigma}\cdot s^\sigma, \mathrm{S}^{\mu\cdot \lambda_\sigma})=$$ $$=h\cdot x(s^\sigma, \mathrm{S}^{\mu\cdot \lambda_\sigma})=
h\cdot x(s,\mathrm{S}^\mu)^\rho$$
which proves that any such $h$ is invariant under $\rho.$


Next consider the action of an element $d$ of $\Delta(\mathbb{A}_f)$ on 
$x(s,\mathrm{S}^\mu).$ Since any $a\in \mathbb{A}_f$ can be represented as $a=q\cdot \nu,$ for $q\in {\mathbb Q}_+$ and $\nu\in \hat{{\mathbb Z}}^\times,$
$d$ can be represented as 
$d=\mathbf{d}_q\cdot \mathbf{d}_\nu.$ Thus, the action by $d$ is invariant under $\rho$ if and only if the action by $\mathbf{d}_\nu$ is.                

We have by construction
$$\mathbf{d}_\nu\cdot x(s,\mathrm{S}^\mu)=x(s,\mathrm{S}^{\mu\cdot\nu})$$

Thus
$$(\mathbf{d}_\nu\cdot x(s,\mathrm{S}^\mu))^\rho= x(s,\mathrm{S}^{\mu\cdot\nu}))^\rho=x(s^\sigma,\mathrm{S}^{\mu\cdot\nu\cdot \lambda_\sigma})=\mathbf{d}_\nu\cdot x(s^\sigma,\mathrm{S}^{\mu\cdot \lambda_\sigma})=\mathbf{d}_\nu\cdot x(s,\mathrm{S}^{\mu})^\rho$$
which proves that the actions by $\mathbf{d}_\nu$ and by $d$ on $\mathrm{S}$ are invariant under $\rho.$

Since any element of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f)$ can be represented in the form
$d\cdot h$ where $d\in \Delta(\mathbb{A}_f)$ and $h\in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{A}_f)$
we proved that $\rho$ satisfies (\ref{autg}) and thus $\rho\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}.$ $\rho$ is the lifting of $\sigma\in \mathrm{Aut}\, \tilde{\mathbb{H}}^\mathrm{Pure}$ to  $\mathrm{Aut}\, \mathbf{S}^\mathrm{Pure}.$

This completes the proof of the theorem. $\Box$

\rm \label{RemCor} {\bf Remark/Corollary.} 
Consider the embedding 
$$(\mathbb{H},\mathrm{G})\subset (\tilde{\mathbb{H}},\tilde{\mathrm{G}}),$$
where $\mathrm{G}\cong\mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb Q})\cap \tilde{\mathrm{G}}=\mathrm{GL}_2^+({\mathbb Q}).$

1. 
Clearly, under this embedding special points in $\mathbb{H}$ are just quadratic imaginary points. 

The action of  individual elements $\mathbf{d}'_q\in \Delta'({\mathbb Q}_+),$  $\mathbf{d}_q\in \Delta({\mathbb Q}_+)$ and the scalar matrices $q\cdot \mathbf{I}$ are definable. 

The scalar matrices act trivially, as identity.


 
2. More generally, the action of $h\in \mathrm{GL}_2^+({\mathbb Q})$ depends on the choice of an isomorphism   
$\phi_\mu: \mathrm{G}\to \mathrm{GL}_2^+({\mathbb Q}),$ for   $\mu\in \hat{{\mathbb Z}}^\times,$ see \ref{specpointsapprox} A(b). However, on $\mathbb{H}$ only
$h^\mu$ for $\mu=\pm 1$ corresponds to a non-empty curve. Thus $h$ defines (in the natural $L_{\omega_1,\omega}$-language) the two-component curve $C_h\cup C_h^{-1},$ where  $C_h^{-1}$ is the graph
of $\mathbf{d}_{-1}\cdot h\cdot \mathbf{d}_{-1}$
   and the choice of the component depends on $\phi_\mu.$ (Note that if
   $h=\left( \begin{array}{ll}
a\ b\\ c\ d
\end{array}\right)$   then $\mathbf{d}_{-1}\cdot h\cdot \mathbf{d}_{-1}=\left( \begin{array}{ll}
 a\ -b\\ -c\ \ d
\end{array}\right).$)

\medskip

3. The 4-point relation $R(s_1,s_2,s'_1,s'_2)$ on $\mathbb{H}$ for
$s_i=p_i+q_i\sqrt{-m_i},$ $s'_i=p'_i+q'_i\sqrt{-m_i},$ $p_i,q_i,p'_i,q'_i\in {\mathbb Q},$ $q_i,q'_i\in {\mathbb Q}_+,$ holds if and only if $$q_1=q'_1 \ \& \ q_2=q'_2\ \& \ ((p_1=p'_1 \ \& \ p_2=p'_2) \vee (p_1=-p'_1 \ \& \ p_2=-p'_2))$$
                                                                                       
                                                                         
   
 Thus in $\mathbb{H}:$
   $$R(s_1,s_2,s'_1,s'_2)\ \Leftrightarrow\ \langle s'_1,s'_2\rangle =\langle s_1,s_2\rangle \ \vee \ \langle s'_1,s'_2\rangle =\langle -s^c_1,-s^c_2\rangle$$ 
where $-s^c$ is the application of complex conjugation to $-s.$
\medskip

4. Respectively, on $\mathbb{Y}(N)=\Gamma(N)\backslash \mathbb{H}=\tilde{\Gamma}(N)\backslash \tilde{\mathbb{H}}$ the indiscernible curves $C^\mu_{h,K(N)}$
are conjugated by elements of the group $\Delta/\Delta(N),$ or equivalently, by $\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb Q}(\zeta_N):{\mathbb Q}).$ The union  $\bigcup_\mu C^\mu_{h,K(N)}$ is a (reducible) algebraic curve over ${\mathbb Q}.$ 

The $j_N$-image of the 4-point relations $R(s_1,s_2,s'_1,s'_2)$ is a relation on CM-points  of $\mathbb{Y}(N),$  call it  $R_N.$
                                
                                            
 
 The respective set of pairs (called type of $\langle j_N(s_1),j_N(s_2)\rangle$ in Model Theory), 
$$\mathrm{tp}_N(\langle j_N(s_1),j_N(s_2)\rangle):=\{ \langle y_1,y_2\rangle\in \mathbb{Y}(N)\times \mathbb{Y}(N): R_N(j_N(s_1),j_N(s_2),y_1,y_2)\}$$
is, by \ref{specpointsapprox} E(c), a $\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb Q}$-orbit of the pair, a finite Zariski set.

5. Given $g\in \tilde{\mathrm{G}}$ the graph of $g,$ $\mathsf{graph}\, g\subset \tilde{\mathbb{H}}\times \tilde{\mathbb{H}},$ has a point in $\mathbb{H}\times \mathbb{H}$ if and only if $g\in \mathrm{GL}_2^+({\mathbb Q}).$

$\tau'=q\cdot \tau$ and 
$[q\cdot\tau,q\cdot g^{-1}\Delta]=[q\cdot\tau,\Delta],$  for some $q\in \mathrm{GL}_2^+({\mathbb Q}).$  That is
$q\cdot g^{-1} \in \Delta$ which can only happen if $q\cdot g=1,$ since
$\det q\cdot g\in {\mathbb Q}_+.$
