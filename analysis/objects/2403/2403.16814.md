<!-- propositionintro-1: body_md -->
\label{PRO:1}
    Let $X$ be a compact complex balanced manifold of balanced cone $\mathcal{B}_X$ and $\mathcal{E} \rightarrow X$ a torsion-free coherent sheaf. Then, for all compact $K \subset \mathcal{B}_X$, there is a finite family $\mathcal{S}_1,\ldots,\mathcal{S}_p \subset \mathcal{E}$ with for all $k$, $0 < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}_k) < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})$ such that for all $[\Theta] \in K$, $\mathcal{E}$ is $[\Theta]$-stable (resp. $[\Theta]$-semi-stable) if and only if for all $k$, $\mu_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{S}_k) < \mu_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E})$ (resp. $\mu_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{S}_k) \leqslant \mu_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E})$).
<!-- end propositionintro-1 -->
<!-- theoremintro-1: body_md -->
\label{THE:2}
    The set $\mathcal{C}_s^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E})$ (resp. $\mathcal{C}_{ss}^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E})$) of balanced classes of metrics $[\Theta]$ with respect to which $\mathcal{E}$ is stable (resp. semi-stable) is locally an open (resp. closed) convex polyhedral cone.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{REM:Formule c}
When it exists, $c$ is entirely determined by the topology of $E$, the real Dolbeault class $[\Theta] \in H^{n - 1,n - 1}(X,\mathbb{R})$ and the volume of $X$. Indeed, by Chern-Weil theory, we have,
$$
c_\Theta = \frac{2\pi c_1(E) \cup [\Theta]}{\mathrm{rk}(E)\mathrm{Vol}_\Theta(X)},
$$
by integrating over $X$ the trace of the HYM equation.

<!-- propositionintro-2: body_md -->
\label{PRO:Kobayashi--Hitchin}
    If $\mathcal{E} = (E,\overline{\partial}_E,h)$ is a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle of positive rank, then it is $[\Theta]$-polystable if and only if it admits a $\Theta$-HYM operator $\overline{\partial}$ in the gauge orbit of $\overline{\partial}_E$. In this case, it is unique up to a unitary gauge transformation and the decomposition of $\mathcal{E}$ in a direct sum of stable bundles is always both $\overline{\partial}$-holomorphic and $h$-orthogonal, making its components $\Theta$-HYM.
<!-- end propositionintro-2 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Here, we fix $h$ and we find $\overline{\partial}$ in the gauge orbit of $\overline{\partial}_E$ so the Chern connection associated to $(\overline{\partial},h)$ is $\Theta$-HYM. It is also possible to fix $\overline{\partial}_E$ and to find a metric $k$ such that the Chern connection associated to $(\overline{\partial}_E,k)$ is $\Theta$-HYM. In this case, there is a map, $f : (E,\overline{\partial}_E,k) \rightarrow (E,\overline{\partial},h)$ which is both a biholomorphism and an isometry.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
In the literature about balanced manifolds, the balanced cone usually contains elements of the Bott-Chern cohomology groups instead of the real Dolbeault ones, where the boundary morphisms are $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\partial\overline{\partial}$ instead of $d$. Here, we only need to work modulo $d$. Since $\partial\overline{\partial} = \frac{1}{2}(\partial - \overline{\partial})d$, the real Dolbeault cohomology groups are naturally quotients of the Bott-Chern ones. Therefore, this article's balanced cone can be seen as a projection of the classical balanced cone on a quotient space.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{LEM:Identités de Kähler}
    The following Kähler identities hold on the space $\Omega^1(X,E)$,
    \begin{align*}
        \Lambda_\Theta\partial & = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\overline{\partial}^*,\\
        \Lambda_\Theta\overline{\partial} & = -\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\partial^*,
    \end{align*}
    and the following hold on the space of smooth sections $\Omega^0(X,E)$,
    \begin{align*}
        \Delta_{\Theta,\partial} & = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\Lambda_\Theta\overline{\partial}\partial,\\
        \Delta_{\Theta,\overline{\partial}} & = -\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\Lambda_\Theta\partial\overline{\partial},\\
        \Delta_{\Theta,\partial} + \Delta_{\Theta,\overline{\partial}} & = \Delta_{\Theta,\nabla} = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\Lambda_\Theta(\overline{\partial}\partial - \partial\overline{\partial}),\\
        \Delta_{\Theta,\partial} - \Delta_{\Theta,\overline{\partial}} & = \Lambda_\Theta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_\nabla,
    \end{align*}
    where $F_\nabla = \nabla^2 = \overline{\partial}\partial + \partial\overline{\partial}$ is the curvature form of $\nabla$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The proof is the same as the standard proof of Kähler identities, but only on sections and $1$-forms. Indeed, if $\alpha$ is a $(0,1)$ form and $s$ a $0$-form with values in $E$,
\begin{align*}
    \left< \Lambda_\Theta\overline{\partial}\alpha,s \right>_\Theta & = \int_X h(\Lambda_\Theta\overline{\partial}\alpha,s)\mathrm{Vol}_\Theta\\
    & = \int_X h(\overline{\partial}\alpha,s) \wedge \Theta\\
    & = \int_X dh(\alpha,s) \wedge \Theta + \int_X h(\alpha,\partial s) \wedge \Theta\\
    & = \int_X h(\alpha,-J\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\partial s) \wedge \Theta \textrm{ because } d\Theta = 0,\\
    & = \left< \alpha,-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\partial s \right>_\Theta.
\end{align*}
We obtain the second identity. The first one is similar. We deduce that, on smooth sections,
$$
\Delta_{\Theta,\partial} = \partial\partial^* + \partial^*\partial = \partial^*\partial = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\Lambda_\Theta\overline{\partial}\partial,
$$
and similarly,
$$
\Delta_{\Theta,\overline{\partial}} = \overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^* + \overline{\partial}^*\overline{\partial} = \overline{\partial}^*\overline{\partial} = -\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\Lambda_\Theta\partial\overline{\partial}.
$$
The equality $\Delta_{\Theta,\nabla} = \Delta_{\Theta,\partial} + \Delta_{\Theta,\overline{\partial}}$ always hold by definition even if we don't assume $d\Theta = 0$. The third equality involving Laplacians is the sum of the two first and the fourth one is their difference.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{PRO:Noyau Laplacien}
    On smooth sections, $\Delta_{\Theta,\nabla}$ verifies $\ker(\Delta_{\Theta,\nabla}) = \ker(\nabla)$ is the space of $\nabla$-parallel sections and if moreover $\Lambda_\Theta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_\nabla = 0$, then $\ker(\Delta_{\Theta,\nabla}) = \ker(\overline{\partial})$ is the space of $\overline{\partial}$-holomorphic sections.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The first equality is a direct consequence of the fact that $\Delta_{\Theta,\nabla} = \nabla^*\nabla$ on smooth forms. The second one comes from Lemma \ref{LEM:Identités de Kähler}, which implies that $\Delta_{\Theta,\nabla} = \Delta_{\Theta,\partial} + \Delta_{\Theta,\overline{\partial}}$ and if $\Lambda_\Theta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_\nabla = 0$, $\Delta_{\Theta,\partial} = \Delta_{\Theta,\overline{\partial}}$ hence $\Delta_{\Theta,\nabla} = 2\Delta_{\Theta,\overline{\partial}} = 2\overline{\partial}^*\overline{\partial}$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{PRO:Calcul dPsi(0)}
    For any $h$-unitary connection $\nabla$, the map,
    $$
    \Psi : \left\{ \begin{array}{rcl}
		\displaystyle \Omega^0(X,\mathrm{End}_H(E,h)) & \longrightarrow & \displaystyle \Omega^0(X,\mathrm{End}_H(E,h))\\
		\displaystyle s & \longmapsto & \displaystyle \Lambda_\Theta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^s \cdot \nabla}
	\end{array} \right.,
    $$
    verifies $d\Psi(0) = \Delta_{\Theta,\nabla}$ (on $\mathrm{End}(E)$). Notice that since $\Delta_{\Theta,\nabla}$ is a real operator, it preserves $\mathrm{End}_H(E,h)$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We have for all smooth Hermitian sections $s$ of $\mathrm{End}(E)$,
$$
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^s \cdot \nabla = \nabla + \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^s\overline{\partial}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s}) + \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s}\partial(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^s),
$$
hence,
$$
F_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^s \cdot \nabla} = F_\nabla + \nabla\alpha_s + \alpha_s \wedge \alpha_s,
$$
where $\alpha_s = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^s\overline{\partial}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s}) + \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s}\partial(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^s) \in \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\Omega^1(X,\mathrm{End}_H(E,h))$. We have $\alpha_0 = 0$ and if $v \in T_s\Omega^0(X,\mathrm{End}_H(E,h)) = \Omega^0(X,\mathrm{End}_H(E,h))$,
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}|_{s = 0}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^s\overline{\partial}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s})) \cdot v = \frac{\partial}{\partial s}|_{s = 0}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^s) \cdot v\overline{\partial}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-0}) + \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^0\overline{\partial}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}|_{s = 0}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s}) \cdot v\right) = -\overline{\partial} v.
$$
Hence $\frac{\partial}{\partial s}|_{s = 0}\alpha_s \cdot v = \partial v - \overline{\partial} v$ symmetrically. Since $\alpha_s \wedge \alpha_s$ is quadratic and $\alpha_0 = 0$, its derivative at $0$ vanishes thus,
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}|_{s = 0}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^s \cdot \nabla} = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\nabla(\partial v - \overline{\partial} v) = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}(\overline{\partial}\partial - \partial\overline{\partial})v.
$$
After applying $\Lambda_\Theta$, by Lemma \ref{LEM:Identités de Kähler}, we deduce that $d\Psi(0) = \Delta_{\Theta,\nabla}$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
We define the $[\Theta]$-slope $\mu_{[\Theta]}$ of a non-zero torsion-free coherent sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ as
    $$
    \mu_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E}) = \frac{c_1(\mathcal{E}) \cup [\Theta]}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})}.
    $$
    Following Mumford \cite{Mumford}, we say that,
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $\mathcal{E}$ is $[\Theta]$-stable if for all coherent sub-sheaves $\mathcal{S} \subset \mathcal{E}$ with $0 < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}) < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})$,
        $$
        \mu_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{S}) < \mu_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E}).
        $$
        \item $\mathcal{E}$ is $[\Theta]$-semi-stable if for all coherent sub-sheaves $\mathcal{S} \subset \mathcal{E}$ with $0 < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}) < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})$,
        $$
        \mu_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{S}) \leqslant \mu_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E}).
        $$
        \item $\mathcal{E}$ is $[\Theta]$-polystable if $\mathcal{E}$ is a direct sum of stable coherent sheaves of the same $[\Theta]$-slope.
    \end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{PRO:FJH}
    If $\mathcal{E}$ is a $[\Theta]$-semi-stable vector bundle, then it admits a Jordan-Hölder filtration, \textit{i.e.} a filtration by coherent sub-sheaves $0 \subsetneq \mathcal{F}_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathcal{F}_m = \mathcal{E}$ such that for all $k$, $\mu_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{F}_k) = \mu_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E})$ and each $\mathcal{G}_k = \mathcal{F}_k/\mathcal{F}_{k - 1}$ is torsion-free and $[\Theta]$-stable. Moreover, the graded object,
    $$
    \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E}) = \bigoplus_{k = 1}^m \mathcal{G}_k,
    $$
    is unique up to isomorphism outside of a codimension $2$ sub-analytic space of $X$. In particular, its reflexive closure $\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E})^{\lor\lor}$ is unique up to isomorphism.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
This is a standard result. See for example \cite[Theorem 1.6.7, Corollary 1.6.10]{Huybrechts_Lehn}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
When $\mathcal{E}$ is locally free \textit{i.e.} a vector bundle, it is said to be \textit{sufficiently smooth} if its graded object $\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E})$ can be chosen to be locally free too, or equivalently, each $\mathcal{G}_i$ can be chosen to be locally free.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
When $F \subset E$ are smooth vector bundles, the Hermitian metric $h$ on $E$ gives rise to a diffeomorphism between $E/F$ and $F^\bot$. In other words, any exact sequence of smooth vector bundles splits. In particular, $\mathcal{E}$ and $\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E})$ have the same smooth structure when $\mathcal{E}$ is a sufficiently smooth vector bundle.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
$\mathcal{E}$ is $[\Theta]$-polystable if and only if it is $[\Theta]$-semi-stable and $\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E}) \cong \mathcal{E}$.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{LEM:Finitude locale}
    Let $V$ be a finite dimensional real vector space and $D \subset V$ a non-empty discrete subset. Let $U \subset V^\lor$ be an open subset of the dual of $V$ and such that for all $\varphi \in U$, $\varphi(D) \subset \mathbb{R}$ is bounded from above. Then, for all compact $K \subset U$, there is a finite set $F \subset D$ that may depend on $K$ such that for all $\varphi \in K$, $\sup(\varphi(D)) = \max(\varphi(F))$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
\ \\
\vspace{1mm}
\noindent\textbf{Step 1 :} For all real number $a$, for all $K \subset U$ compact, the set $F_{K,a} = \bigcup_{\varphi \in K} \{\varphi \geqslant a\} \cap D$ is finite.
\vspace{1mm}

Indeed, $F_{K,a} \subset D$ is discrete so if it is infinite, then it is unbounded. In this case, there is a sequence $(v_m)$ of elements of $F_{K,a}$ such that $\left\| v_m \right\| \rightarrow +\infty$ where $\left\| \cdot \right\| = \sqrt{\left< \cdot,\cdot \right>}$ is any Euclidean norm on $V$. Up to extracting, $\frac{v_m}{\left\| v_m \right\|} \rightarrow v_\infty \in V$ is in the unit sphere. For all fixed $m$, $v_m \in \bigcup_{\varphi \in K} \{\varphi \geqslant a\}$ so there is a $\varphi_m \in K$ such that $\varphi_m(v_m) \geqslant a$. Up to extracting again, $\varphi_m \rightarrow \varphi_\infty \in K$.

Let $\epsilon > 0$ and $\varphi = \varphi_\infty + \epsilon\left< v_\infty,\cdot \right>$. Since $U$ is open, for $\epsilon$ small enough, $\varphi \in U$. For all integer $m$,
\begin{align*}
    \varphi(v_m) & = \varphi_\infty(v_m) + \epsilon\left< v_\infty,v_m \right>\\
    & = \varphi_m(v_m) + \mathrm{O}(\left\| \varphi_m - \varphi_\infty \right\|\left\| v_m \right\|) + \epsilon\left< \frac{v_m}{\left\| v_m \right\|} + \mathrm{o}(1),v_m \right>\\
    & \geqslant a + \epsilon\left\| v_m \right\| + \mathrm{o}(\left\| v_m \right\|)\\
    & \underset{m \rightarrow +\infty}{\longrightarrow} +\infty.
\end{align*}
This contradicts the boundedness from above of $\varphi(D)$. It proves that $F_{K,a}$ is indeed finite. In particular, when $K = \{\varphi\}$ is a singleton and $a = \sup(\varphi(D)) - 1$, we obtain that for all $\varphi \in U$, the set,
$$
\{\varphi \geqslant \sup(\varphi(D)) - 1\} \cap D,
$$
is finite. In particular, it proves that the $\sup$ is reached (and it is reached finitely many times).

\vspace{1mm}
\noindent\textbf{Step 2 :} The map $\varphi \mapsto \max(\varphi(D))$ is continuous on $U$.
\vspace{1mm}

Let $\varphi_0 \in U$ and $K \subset U$ be a compact set such that $\varphi_0 \in K^o$. Let $v \in D$ such that $\max(\varphi_0(D)) = \varphi_0(v)$ and $a = \max(\varphi_0(D)) - 1$. Clearly, $v \in F_{K,a}$ and when $\varphi$ is close enough to $\varphi_0$, then $\varphi \in K$ and $\varphi(v) \geqslant a$. Thus,
$$
\max(\varphi(D)) \geqslant \varphi(v) \geqslant a,
$$
so the $w \in D$ that verify $\max(\varphi(D)) = \varphi(w)$ belong to $F_{K,a}$. In other words, for all $\varphi$ close enough to $\varphi_0$, $\max(\varphi(D)) = \max(\varphi(F_{K,a}))$.

Since $F_{K,a}$ is finite, $\varphi \mapsto \max(\varphi(F_{K,a}))$ is continuous at $\varphi_0$, proving the wanted result.

\vspace{1mm}
\noindent\textbf{Step 3 :} Conclusion.
\vspace{1mm}

Let $K \subset U$ be an arbitrary compact set. By the continuity result of step 2,
$$
a = \min_{\varphi \in K}(\max(\varphi(D))),
$$
is well defined and by the result of step 1,
$$
F = F_{K,a} = \bigcup_{\varphi \in K} \{\varphi \geqslant a\} \cap D,
$$
is finite. For all $\varphi \in K$, if $v \in D$ is such that $\max(\varphi(D)) = \varphi(v)$, then $\varphi(v) \geqslant a$ so $v \in F$. We deduce that $\sup(\varphi(D))$ is reached by an element in the finite set $F$, which is the wanted result.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{COR:Finitude locale}
    For all torsion-free coherent sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ and all compact $K \subset \mathcal{B}_X$, there is a finite family $\mathcal{S}_1,\ldots,\mathcal{S}_p$ of coherent sub-sheaves of $\mathcal{E}$ with for all $k$, $0 < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}_k) < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})$ such that for all $[\Theta] \in K$, $\mathcal{E}$ is $[\Theta]$-stable (resp. $[\Theta]$-semi-stable) if and only if for all $k$, $l_{\mathcal{S}_k}([\Theta]) > 0$ (resp. $l_{\mathcal{S}_k}([\Theta]) \geqslant 0$).
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $V = H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})$. By Serre duality, $V^\lor$ is naturally identified with $H^{n - 1,n - 1}(X,\mathbb{R})$ thus we can see $\mathcal{B}_X$ as an open subset of $V^\lor$. Let,
$$
D = \left\{\frac{c_1(\mathcal{S})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S})}\middle|\mathcal{S} \subset \mathcal{E}, 0 < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}) < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})\right\}.
$$
The first Chern classes belong to the lattice $H^2(X,\mathbb{Z})$ hence,
$$
D \subset \frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})!}H^2(X,\mathbb{Z}),
$$
is discrete. For each fixed $[\Theta] \in \mathcal{B}_X$, $\{c \cup [\Theta]|c \in D\} \subset \mathbb{R}$ is bounded from above. This is a standard fact uses to build Harder-Narasimhan filtrations (see for example \cite[Lemma 5.7.16]{Kobayashi}).

Therefore, we can use Lemma \ref{LEM:Finitude locale} which tells us that for all compact $K \subset \mathcal{B}_X$, there is a finite set $F \subset D$ such that for all $[\Theta] \in K$,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Sup atteint}
    \sup\{c \cup [\Theta]|c \in D\} = \max\{c \cup [\Theta]|c \in F\}.
\end{equation}
Let $F = \{c^1,\ldots,c^p\}$ and for all $k$, $\mathcal{S}_k \subset \mathcal{E}$ with $0 < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}_k) < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})$ such that $\frac{c_1(\mathcal{S}_k)}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}_k)} = c^k$. This tuple fits. Indeed, when $[\Theta] \in K$,
\begin{align*}
    \mathcal{E} \textrm{ is $[\Theta]$-stable } & \Leftrightarrow \forall \mathcal{S} \subset \mathcal{E}, 0 < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}) < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}) \Rightarrow \frac{c_1(\mathcal{S})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S})} \cup [\Theta] < \frac{c_1(\mathcal{E})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})} \cup [\Theta]\\
    & \Leftrightarrow \sup\{c \cup [\Theta]|c \in D\} < \frac{c_1(\mathcal{E})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})} \cup [\Theta] \textrm{ because the $\sup$ is reached,}\\
    & \Leftrightarrow \max\{c \cup [\Theta]|c \in F\} < \frac{c_1(\mathcal{E})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})} \cup [\Theta] \textrm{ by (\ref{EQ:Sup atteint}),}\\
    & \Leftrightarrow \forall 1 \leqslant k \leqslant p, l_{\mathcal{S}_k}([\Theta]) > 0.
\end{align*}
The same equivalence holds with weak inequalities.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{THE:Structures cônes}
    On a balanced manifold $X$, for all torsion-free coherent sheaf $\mathcal{E}$, the stable and semi-stable cones satisfy the following properties,
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $\mathcal{C}_s^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E})$ and $\mathcal{C}_{ss}^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E})$ are locally polyhedral convex cones.
        \item If there is a coherent $\mathcal{S} \subset \mathcal{E}$ such that $0 < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}) < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})$ and $\frac{c_1(\mathcal{S})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S})} = \frac{c_1(\mathcal{E})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})}$, then $\mathcal{C}_s(\mathcal{E}) = \mathcal{C}_s^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E}) = \emptyset$.
        \item If there is no coherent $\mathcal{S} \subset \mathcal{E}$ such that $0 < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}) < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})$ and $\frac{c_1(\mathcal{S})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S})} = \frac{c_1(\mathcal{E})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})}$, then $\mathcal{C}_{ss}(\mathcal{E})^o = \mathcal{C}_s(\mathcal{E})$ and $\mathcal{C}_{ss}^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E})^o = \mathcal{C}_s^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E})$.
        \item If $\mathcal{C}_s^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E}) \neq \emptyset$, $\overline{\mathcal{C}_s(\mathcal{E})} \cap \{\Theta > 0\} = \mathcal{C}_{ss}(\mathcal{E})$ and $\overline{\mathcal{C}_s^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E})} \cap \mathcal{B}_X = \mathcal{C}_{ss}^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E})$.
        \item For all $[\Theta_0] \in \mathcal{C}_{ss}^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E})$, if $[\Theta] \in \mathcal{B}_X$ is close enough to $[\Theta_0]$, $[\Theta] \in \mathcal{C}_s^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E})$ (resp. $[\Theta] \in \mathcal{C}_{ss}^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E})$) if and only if for all $\mathcal{S} \subset \mathcal{E}$ such that $l_{\mathcal{S}}([\Theta_0]) = 0$, $l_{\mathcal{S}}([\Theta]) > 0$ (resp. $l_{\mathcal{S}}([\Theta]) \geqslant 0$).
    \end{itemize}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
By Corollary \ref{COR:Finitude locale}, if $K \subset \mathcal{B}_X$ is compact, there is an integer $p$ and a $p$-tuple $\mathcal{S}_1,\ldots,\mathcal{S}_p \subsetneq \mathcal{E}$ of coherent sheaves such that for all $k$, $0 < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}_k) < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})$ and,
$$
\mathcal{C}_s^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E}) \cap K = \bigcap_{k = 1}^p \{l_{\mathcal{S}_k} > 0\} \cap K, \qquad \mathcal{C}_{ss}^{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{E}) \cap K = \bigcap_{k = 1}^p \{l_{\mathcal{S}_k} \geqslant 0\} \cap K.
$$
It gives then local polyhedral structures.

Moreover, since the pairing $H^{n - 1,n - 1}(X,\mathbb{R}) \times H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R}) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is non-degenerate by Serre duality, for each $\mathcal{S}$,
$$
l_{\mathcal{S}} = 0 \Leftrightarrow \frac{c_1(\mathcal{S})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S})} = \frac{c_1(\mathcal{E})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})}.
$$
From here, the results about the finite dimensional cones follow from basic topology and convex geometry, and the results about the infinite dimensional cones follow from the continuity of the projection onto $H^{n - 1,n - 1}(X,\mathbb{R})$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{LEM:Gr(Gr)}
    Let $\mathcal{E}$ be a torsion-free coherent sheaf and $K \subset \mathcal{B}_X$ compact and convex. By Theorem \ref{THE:Structures cônes}, $C = \mathcal{C}_{ss}(\mathcal{E}) \cap K$ is a convex polyhedral cone. Thus for all $[\Theta] \in C$, there is a unique face $F_{[\Theta]} \subset C$ such that $[\Theta]$ belongs to the relative interior of $F_{[\Theta]}$. If $[\Theta_1]$ and $[\Theta_2]$ are in $C$ and $F_{[\Theta_1]} \subset F_{[\Theta_2]}$, then $\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_2]}(\mathcal{E})$ is $[\Theta_1]$-semi-stable and,
    $$
    \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_1]}(\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_2]}(\mathcal{E})) \cong \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_1]}(\mathcal{E}).
    $$
    In particular, if $F_{[\Theta_1]} = F_{[\Theta_2]}$, $\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_1]}(\mathcal{E}) \cong \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_2]}(\mathcal{E})$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{S}_1,\ldots,\mathcal{S}_p \subset \mathcal{E}$ be a finite family of coherent sheaves given by Corollary \ref{COR:Finitude locale} such that $C = \bigcap_{k = 1}^p \{l_{\mathcal{S}_k} \geqslant 0\} \cap K$. We may assume that the $l_{\mathcal{S}_k}$ are pairwise distinct. Up to rearranging the $\mathcal{S}_k$, we may also assume that the faces $F_{[\Theta_2]}$ and $F_{[\Theta_1]} \subset F_{[\Theta_2]}$ are defined by the equations,
$$
F_{[\Theta_1]} = \bigcap_{k = 1}^j \{l_{\mathcal{S}_k} = 0\} \cap \bigcap_{k = j + 1}^p \{l_{\mathcal{S}_k} \geqslant 0\} \cap K, \qquad F_{[\Theta_2]} = \bigcap_{k = 1}^i \{l_{\mathcal{S}_k} = 0\} \cap \bigcap_{k = i + 1}^p \{l_{\mathcal{S}_k} \geqslant 0\} \cap K,
$$
for some $0 \leqslant i \leqslant j \leqslant p$. Moreover, since $[\Theta_1]$ (resp. $[\Theta_2]$) belongs to the relative interior of $F_{[\Theta_1]}$ (resp. $F_{[\Theta_2]}$), we have $l_{\mathcal{S}_k}([\Theta_1]) > 0$ when $k > j$ (resp. $l_{\mathcal{S}_k}([\Theta_2]) > 0$ when $k > i$).

Let,
$$
0 \subsetneq \mathcal{F}_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathcal{F}_m = \mathcal{E},
$$
be a Jordan-Hölder filtration for $\mathcal{E}$ with respect to $[\Theta_2]$. We have,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Expression gradué}
    \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_2]}(\mathcal{E}) = \bigoplus_{k = 1}^m \mathcal{G}_k,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{G}_k = \mathcal{F}_k/\mathcal{F}_{k - 1}$. By definition, for all $k$, $l_{\mathcal{F}_k}([\Theta_2]) = 0$. Since the $\mathcal{F}_k$ destabilise $\mathcal{E}$ with respect to $[\Theta_2] \in K$, there must exist an integer $1 \leqslant q \leqslant p$ such that $\frac{c_1(\mathcal{F}_k)}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F}_k)} = \frac{c_1(\mathcal{S}_q)}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}_q)}$. The equality $l_{\mathcal{F}_k}([\Theta_2]) = l_{\mathcal{S}_q}([\Theta_2]) = 0$ implies that $q \leqslant i$. In particular, $q \leqslant j$ so for all $k$, $l_{\mathcal{F}_k}([\Theta_1]) = 0$. It implies that each $\mathcal{F}_k$ is $[\Theta_1]$-semi-stable by $[\Theta_1]$-semi-stability of $\mathcal{E}$. Therefore, for all $k$, $l_{\mathcal{G}_k}([\Theta_1]) = 0$ and $\mathcal{G}_k$ is $[\Theta_1]$-semi-stable as a quotient of $[\Theta_1]$-semi-stable sheaves of the same $[\Theta_1]$-slope.

It means that the $\mathcal{F}_k$ form a filtration of $\mathcal{E}$ by semi-stable sub-sheaves with torsion-free semi-stable quotients with respect to $[\Theta_1]$. Let us consider a maximal refinement of this filtration with for all $1 \leqslant k \leqslant m$,
$$
\mathcal{F}_{k - 1} = \mathcal{F}_{k - 1,0} \subsetneq \mathcal{F}_{k - 1,1} \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathcal{F}_{k - 1,p_k} = \mathcal{F}_k = \mathcal{F}_{k,0}.
$$
Then, the $\mathcal{F}_{k,q}$ form a Jordan-Hölder filtration of $\mathcal{E}$ with respect to $[\Theta_1]$ thus on the one hand,
$$
\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_1]}(\mathcal{E}) = \bigoplus_{k = 1}^m\bigoplus_{q = 1}^{p_k} \mathcal{F}_{k - 1,q}/\mathcal{F}_{k - 1,q - 1},
$$
and on the other hand, for each $k$, the $\mathcal{F}_{k - 1,q}/\mathcal{F}_{k - 1}$ form a Jordan-Hölder filtration of $\mathcal{G}_k = \mathcal{F}_k/\mathcal{F}_{k - 1}$ with respect to $[\Theta_1]$ so, by (\ref{EQ:Expression gradué}),
$$
\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_1]}(\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_2]}(\mathcal{E})) = \bigoplus_{k = 1}^m \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_1]}(\mathcal{G}_k) = \bigoplus_{k = 1}^m\bigoplus_{q = 1}^{p_k} \mathcal{F}_{k - 1,q}/\mathcal{F}_{k - 1,q - 1}.
$$
It proves the lemma.

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
When $W$ is a space of functions, we denote by $A(W)$ its completion with respect to an $A$ norm.

Let $\Pi_\bot : \Omega_0^0(X,\mathrm{End}_H(E,h)) \rightarrow \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}^\bot$ be the orthogonal projection on $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}^\bot$ with respect to $\left< \cdot,\cdot \right>_0$ and,
$$
\Psi : \left\{ \begin{array}{rcl}
		\displaystyle U \times B \times \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}^\bot & \longrightarrow & \displaystyle \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}^\bot\\
		\displaystyle (\varepsilon,b,s) & \longmapsto & \displaystyle \Pi_\bot(\Lambda_\varepsilon\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^s \cdot \overline{\partial}_b} - c_\varepsilon\mathrm{Id}_E)
	\end{array} \right..
$$
By Lemma \ref{LEM:Applications lisses}, $\Psi$ is smooth when we endow the starting $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}^\bot$ with a $\mathcal{C}^2$ norm, the arrival one with a $\mathcal{C}^0$ norm and $U$ with a $\mathcal{C}^0$ norm. And by Proposition \ref{PRO:Calcul dPsi(0)},
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}_{|s = 0}\Psi(0,0,s) = \Pi_\bot\Delta_0,
$$
and $\Delta_0$ is a symmetric semi-definite positive operator and it is continuous from $\mathcal{C}^2(\Omega_0^0(X,\mathrm{End}_H(E,h)))$ to $\mathcal{C}^0(\Omega_0^0(X,\mathrm{End}_H(E,h)))$. According to Proposition \ref{PRO:Noyau Laplacien}, the kernel of the Laplacian $\Delta_0$ (thus its cokernel) is $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}$. In particular, $\Pi_\bot\Delta_0 = \Delta_0$ and,
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}_{|s = 0}\Psi(0,0,s) = \Delta_0 : \mathcal{C}^2(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}^\bot) \rightarrow \mathcal{C}^0(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}^\bot),
$$
is an isomorphism.

By the implicit function theorem, up to shrinking $U \times B$, there is a unique smooth $\sigma : \mathcal{C}^0(U) \times B \rightarrow \mathcal{C}^2(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}^\bot)$ such that $\sigma(0,0) = 0$ and for all $(\varepsilon,b) \in \mathcal{C}^0(U) \times B$, $\Psi(\varepsilon,b,\sigma(\varepsilon,b)) = 0$. When we set $\tilde{\Phi}(\varepsilon,b) = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,b)} \cdot \overline{\partial}_b - \overline{\partial}_0$, the first point is immediate and the third point is verified. For the second one, notice that for all $u \in K$ and all $(\varepsilon,b) \in U \times B$
\begin{align*}
    \Psi(\varepsilon,b,u^*\sigma(\varepsilon,u \cdot b)u) & = \Pi_\bot(\Lambda_\varepsilon\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{u^*\sigma(\varepsilon,u \cdot b)u} \cdot \overline{\partial}_b} - c_\varepsilon\mathrm{Id}_E)\\
    & = \Pi_\bot(\Lambda_\varepsilon\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{u^*\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,u \cdot b)} \cdot \overline{\partial}_{u \cdot b}u} - c_\varepsilon\mathrm{Id}_E)\\
    & = \Pi_\bot(u^*(\Lambda_\varepsilon\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,u \cdot b)} \cdot \overline{\partial}_{u \cdot b}} - c_\varepsilon\mathrm{Id}_E)u) \textrm{ by (\ref{EQ:Action gauge unitaire}),}\\
    & = 0.
\end{align*}
By uniqueness of $\sigma$, it implies that $u^*\sigma(\varepsilon,u \cdot b)u = \sigma(\varepsilon,b)$. Therefore,
$$
\overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon,u \cdot b} = \overline{\partial}_0 + \tilde{\Phi}(\varepsilon,u \cdot b) = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,u \cdot b)} \cdot \overline{\partial}_{u \cdot b} = u\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,b)}u^*u\overline{\partial}_bu^* = u(\overline{\partial}_0 + \tilde{\Phi}(\varepsilon,b))u^* = u\overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon,b}u^*.
$$
It proves the second point. For the fourth point, we have for all $b$,
\begin{align*}
    \Psi(0,b,0) & = \Pi_\bot(\Lambda_0\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{\overline{\partial}_b} - c_0\mathrm{Id}_E)\\
    & = \Pi_\bot(\Lambda_0\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{\overline{\partial}_0} + \Lambda_0\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\nabla_0(\Phi(b) - \Phi(b)^*) + \Lambda_0\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}(\Phi(b) - \Phi(b)^*) \wedge (\Phi(b) - \Phi(b)^*) - c_0\mathrm{Id}_E)\\
    & = \Pi_\bot(\partial_0^*\Phi(b) + \overline{\partial}_0^*\Phi(b)^*) + \mathrm{o}(b) \textrm{ by (\ref{EQ:dbar_0 HYM}) and Lemma \ref{LEM:Identités de Kähler}.}
\end{align*}
Therefore, $\frac{\partial}{\partial b}_{|b = 0}\Psi(0,b,0) = \Pi_\bot(\partial_0^*d\Phi(0) + \overline{\partial}_0^*d\Phi(0)^*)$. Now, recall that the image of $d\Phi(0)$ is the space $V$ of $(0,1)$-harmonic forms. In particular, it is included in the kernel of $\partial_0^*$. Similarly, $d\Phi(0)^*$ takes values in $\ker(\overline{\partial}_0^*)$ because $\nabla_0$ is unitary. Thus $\frac{\partial}{\partial b}_{|b = 0}\Psi(0,b,0) = 0$. Therefore, when we differentiate the equality $\Psi(0,b,\sigma(0,b))$ at $b = 0$,
$$
0 = \frac{\partial}{\partial b}|_{b = 0}\Psi(0,b,0) + \frac{\partial}{\partial s}|_{s = 0}\Psi(0,0,s)\frac{\partial}{\partial b}|_{b = 0}\sigma(0,b) = \Delta_0\frac{\partial}{\partial b}|_{b = 0}\sigma(0,b).
$$
Since $\frac{\partial}{\partial b}|_{b = 0}\sigma(0,b)$ belongs to $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}^\bot$ where $\Delta_0$ is injective, we have $\frac{\partial}{\partial b}|_{b = 0}\sigma(0,b) = 0$ hence,
\begin{align*}
    \tilde{\Phi}(0,b) & = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(0,b)} \cdot \overline{\partial}_b - \overline{\partial}_0\\
    & = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(0,b)} \cdot \overline{\partial}_0 + \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(0,b)}\Phi(b)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-\sigma(0,b)} - \overline{\partial}_0\\
    & = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(0,b)}\overline{\partial}_0(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-\sigma(0,b)}) + \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(0,b)}\Phi(b)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-\sigma(0,b)} \textrm{ by (\ref{EQ:Action de gauge}),}\\
    & = \Phi(b) + \mathrm{o}(b).
\end{align*}
We deduce the fourth point and we showed along the way that $\frac{\partial}{\partial b}|_{b = 0}\sigma(0,b) = 0$. All we have left to show is that the $\sigma(\varepsilon,b)$ are smooth as sections when $\varepsilon \in U$ and that $\sigma$ has the wanted regularity. The first part follows from elliptic regularity. For the second one, we know by Lemma \ref{LEM:Applications lisses} that,
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}|_{s = 0}\Psi(0,0,s) = \Delta_0 : (\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k},\left\| \cdot \right\|_{'\!A}) \rightarrow (\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k},\left\| \cdot \right\|_{A'}),
$$
is an isomorphism. Therefore, we can apply the implicit function theorem with these norms and we obtain a smooth map $\tilde{\sigma} : A'(U') \times B' \rightarrow {'\!A}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}^\bot)$ which verifies $\Psi(\varepsilon,b,\tilde{\sigma}(\varepsilon,b)) = 0$.

By uniqueness of $\sigma$, $\sigma_{|U'} = \tilde{\sigma}$ is smooth from $(U',\left\| \cdot \right\|_{A'}) \times B'$ to $(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}^\bot,\left\| \cdot \right\|_{'\!A})$.

\label{PRO:Inégalité norme b}
    Up to shrinking $U$ and $B$, for all $\varepsilon \in U$ and $b \in \overline{\mathcal{O}}$, $\left\| b \right\|^2 \leqslant C(\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|)$ for some positive constant $C$ independent from $\varepsilon$ and $b$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
By density, it is enough to verify it for $b \in \mathcal{O}$.

\vspace{1mm}
\noindent\textbf{Step 1 :} Finding an orthogonal decomposition of $E$ where $\Phi(b)$ is strictly upper triangular.
\vspace{1mm}

Recall from the beginning of Section \ref{SEC:Résulats analytiques} that,
$$
\overline{\partial}_E - \overline{\partial}_0 = \sum_{1 \leqslant i < j \leqslant m} \gamma_{ij},
$$
where $\gamma_{ij} \in \Omega^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{Hom}(G_j,G_i))$. If $t > 0$ and $g_t = \sum_{k = 1}^m t^k\mathrm{Id}_{G_k}$, we have $g_t \cdot \overline{\partial}_0 = \overline{\partial}_0$ so,
$$
g_t \cdot \overline{\partial}_E - \overline{\partial}_0 = g_t(\overline{\partial}_E - \overline{\partial}_0)g_t^{-1} = \sum_{1 \leqslant i < j \leqslant m} t^{i - j}\gamma_{ij} \underset{t \rightarrow +\infty}{\longrightarrow} 0.
$$
In other words, we can find Dolbeault operators $\overline{\partial}$ in the gauge orbit of $\overline{\partial}_E$ arbitrarily close to $\overline{\partial}_0$. By property of the Kuranishi slice, we can find elements $b' \in \mathcal{O}$ arbitrarily close to $0$. By Hilbert--Mumford criterion \cite[Theorem 12.4]{GRS}, there is a $\xi \in \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\frak{k}$ such that,
$$
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{t\xi} \cdot b \underset{t \rightarrow +\infty}{\longrightarrow} 0.
$$
Since $\Phi$ is $G$-equivariant, $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{t\xi} \cdot \Phi(b) \underset{t \rightarrow +\infty}{\longrightarrow} 0$. $\xi$ is a Hermitian holomorphic global section of $\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E}_0)$. By the spectral theorem, we can can diagonalise it as,
$$
\xi = \sum_{k = 1}^p \lambda_k\mathrm{Id}_{G_{b,k}},
$$
where the $\lambda_1 < \cdots < \lambda_p$ are its increasing eigenvalues and the $G_{b,k}$ are its eigenspaces. They are sub-bundles of $\mathcal{E}_0$ and they are pairwise orthogonal. Since $\mathcal{E}_0$ is $[\Theta_0]$-polystable, it implies that the $[\Theta_0]$-slope of each $G_{b,k}$ equals the $[\Theta_0]$-slope of $\mathcal{E}_0$. In the orthogonal decomposition,
$$
\Omega^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}(E)) = \bigoplus_{1 \leqslant i,j \leqslant p} \Omega^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{Hom}(G_{b,j},G_{b,i})),
$$
we can decompose $\Phi(b)$ as,
$$
\Phi(b) = \sum_{1 \leqslant i,j \leqslant p} \gamma_{ij,b}.
$$
We have,
$$
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{t\xi} \cdot \Phi(b) = \sum_{1 \leqslant i,j \leqslant p} \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{(\lambda_i - \lambda_j)t}\gamma_{ij,b} \underset{t \rightarrow +\infty}{\longrightarrow} 0.
$$
We deduce that for all $i \geqslant j$, $\gamma_{ij,b} = 0$ so $\Phi(b)$ is strictly upper triangular. In particular, if we set, $F_{b,k} = \bigoplus_{i = 1}^k G_{b,i}$, each $F_{k,b}$ is preserved by $\overline{\partial}_b$ so,
$$
\mathcal{F}_{k,b} = (F_{k,b},\overline{\partial}_{E|F_{k,b}}),
$$
is a holomorphic sub-bundle of $\mathcal{E}_b = (E,\overline{\partial}_b)$.

\vspace{1mm}
\noindent\textbf{Step 2 :} Computing the scalar product between $\nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(b)$ and each $a_S = \frac{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}}{\mathrm{rk}(S)}\mathrm{Id}_S - \frac{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}}{\mathrm{rk}(S^\bot)}\mathrm{Id}_{S^\bot} \in \frak{k}$.
\vspace{1mm}

Let $\mathcal{S} \subset \mathcal{E}_b$ be a sub-bundle with $0 < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{S}) < \mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})$ and $S$ its smooth structure. Let $\mathrm{Id}_S$ defined as the orthogonal projection on $S$ and $\mathrm{Id}_{S^\bot} = \mathrm{Id}_E - \mathrm{Id}_S$ the orthogonal projection on $S^\bot$. Then, in the decomposition $E = S \oplus S^\bot$, we can write $\overline{\partial}_b = \overline{\partial}_{b|S} + \beta_{S,b}$. $\beta_{S,b}$ is the second fundamental form of $S$. and by \cite[Equation (1.6.12)]{Kobayashi},
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Matrice par blocks courbure}
    \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_b = \begin{pmatrix} \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_S - \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} \beta_{S,b} \wedge \beta_{S,b}^* & \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\partial_b\beta_{S,b} \\ -\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\overline{\partial}_b\beta_{S,b}^* & \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{S^\bot} - \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\beta_{S,b}^* \wedge \beta_{S,b} \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $F_b$ is the curvature of the Chern connection associated to $\overline{\partial}_b$ on $E$, $F_S$ is some curvature form on $\mathcal{S}$ and $F_{S^\bot}$ is some curvature form on $\mathcal{E}_b/\mathcal{S}$ (they depend on $b$). By Chern-Weil theory,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Chern-Weil}
    \int_X \mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_S) \wedge \Theta_\varepsilon = 2\pi\mathrm{rk}(S)\mu_{[\varepsilon]}(S), \qquad \int_X \mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{S^\bot}) \wedge \Theta_\varepsilon = 2\pi\mathrm{rk}(E/S)\mu_{[\varepsilon]}(E/S),
\end{equation}
and for any $(0,1)$-form $\beta$, $\displaystyle \int_X \mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\beta^*\wedge\beta) \wedge \Theta_\varepsilon = \left\| \beta \right\|_\varepsilon^2$ and $\displaystyle \int_X \mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\beta\wedge\beta^*) \wedge \Theta_\varepsilon = -\left\| \beta \right\|_\varepsilon^2$. We can now compute that,
\begin{align*}
    \left< \nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_b),a_S \right>_\varepsilon & = \left< \Lambda_\varepsilon F_b + \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} c_\varepsilon\mathrm{Id}_E,a_S \right>_\varepsilon\\
    & = \int_X \mathrm{tr}\left(\Lambda_\varepsilon\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_b\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(S)}\mathrm{Id}_S - \frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(S^\bot)}\mathrm{Id}_{S^\bot}\right)\right)\mathrm{Vol}_\varepsilon\\
    & = \frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(S)}\int_X \mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_b\mathrm{Id}_S) \wedge \Theta_\varepsilon - \frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(S^\bot)}\int_X \mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_b\mathrm{Id}_{S^\bot}) \wedge \Theta_\varepsilon\\
    & = \frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(S)}\int_X \mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_S - \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} \beta_{S,b}\wedge\beta_{S,b}^*) \wedge \Theta_\varepsilon - \frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(S^\bot)}\int_X \mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{S^\bot} - \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\beta_{S,b}^*\wedge\beta_{S,b}\mathrm{Id}_{S^\bot}) \wedge \Theta_\varepsilon \textrm{ by (\ref{EQ:Matrice par blocks courbure}),}\\
    & = 2\pi\mu_{[\varepsilon]}(S) + \frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(S)}\left\| \beta_{S,b} \right\|_\varepsilon^2 - 2\pi\mu_{[\varepsilon]}(E/S) + \frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(S^\bot)}\left\| \beta_{S,b} \right\|_\varepsilon^2 \textrm{ by (\ref{EQ:Chern-Weil}),}\\
    & = -2\pi l_{\mathcal{S}}([\varepsilon]) + \left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(S)} + \frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(S^\bot)}\right)\left\| \beta_{S,b} \right\|_\varepsilon^2.
\end{align*}

\vspace{1mm}
\noindent\textbf{Step 3 :} Inequality with $\nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}$.
\vspace{1mm}

Applying step 2 with the particular case $\mathcal{S} = \mathcal{F}_{k,b} \subset \mathcal{E}$ with $k < p$, we obtain,
$$
\beta_{S,b} = \mathrm{Id}_{F_{k,b}}\overline{\partial}_{b|F_{k,b}^\bot} = \sum_{i \leqslant k \leqslant j} \gamma_{ij,b}.
$$
Moreover, all the $l_{\mathcal{S}} : H^{n - 1,n - 1}(X,\mathbb{R}) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ are continuous thus,
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k = 1}^p \left< \nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_b),a_{F_{k,b}} \right>_\varepsilon & = \sum_{k = 1}^p -2\pi l_{\mathcal{F}_{k,b}}([\varepsilon]) + \left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(F_{k,b})} + \frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(F_{k,b}^\bot)}\right)\left\| \sum_{i \leqslant k \leqslant j} \gamma_{ij,b} \right\|_\varepsilon^2 \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant \sum_{k = 1}^p C_3\sum_{i \leqslant k \leqslant j} \left\| \gamma_{ij,b} \right\|_0^2 - C_4\left\| [\varepsilon] \right\| \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant C_3\left\| \Phi(b) \right\|_0^2 - C_4\left\| [\varepsilon] \right\| \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant \frac{C_3}{C_1}\left\| b \right\|^2 - C_4\left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|, \label{EQ:Borne 1}
\end{align}
where $C_3$ and $C_4$ are positive constants that only depend on $\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})$, $\max_{1 \leqslant k \leqslant p}\left\{\left\| l_{\mathcal{F}_{k,b}} \right\|\right\}$,\\
$\sup_{\varepsilon \in U}\{\mathrm{Vol}_\varepsilon(X)\}$ (which is finite up to shrinking $U$) and the fact that the $\left\| \cdot \right\|_\varepsilon$ are uniformly equivalent. Clearly, it exists a constant $C_5$ independent from $\varepsilon$ and $b$ such that for all $k$ and $\varepsilon$, $\left\| a_{F_{k,b}} \right\|_\varepsilon \leqslant C_5$. Let for all $\varepsilon$, $\Pi_\varepsilon$ be the orthogonal projection on $\frak{k}$ with respect to $\left< \cdot,\cdot \right>_\varepsilon$. We have,
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k = 1}^p \left< \nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_b),a_{F_{k,b}} \right>_\varepsilon & = \sum_{k = 1}^p \left< \Pi_\varepsilon\nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_b),a_{F_{k,b}} \right>_\varepsilon \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant C_5\sum_{k = 1}^p \left\| \Pi_\varepsilon\nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_b) \right\|_\varepsilon \nonumber\\
    & = pC_5\left\| \Pi_\varepsilon\nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_b) \right\|. \label{EQ:Borne 2}
\end{align}
We deduce from (\ref{EQ:Borne 1}) and (\ref{EQ:Borne 2}) that $\left\| b \right\|^2 = \mathrm{O}\!\left(\left\| \Pi_\varepsilon\nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|\right)$ where the $\mathrm{O}$ is independent from $\varepsilon$ and $b$. From now on, all the asymptotic expansions are with respect to $(\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0},b) \rightarrow (0,0)$.

\vspace{1mm}
\noindent\textbf{Step 4 :} Conclusion.
\vspace{1mm}

By Proposition \ref{PRO:Tranche déformée}, we have $\nu_\varepsilon(b) = \nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon,b})$ with $\overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon,b} = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,b)} \cdot \overline{\partial}_b$ for some smooth,
$$
\sigma : \mathcal{C}^0(U) \times B \rightarrow \mathcal{C}^2(\Omega_0^0(X,\mathrm{End}_H(E,h))),
$$
which verifies $\sigma(0,0) = 0$ and $\frac{\partial}{\partial b}|_{b = 0}\sigma(0,b) = 0$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne sigma}
    \left\| \sigma(\varepsilon,b) \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^2} = \mathrm{O}(\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + \left\| b \right\|^2).
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\Delta_0$ takes values in $\frak{k}^\bot$ (for $\left< \cdot,\cdot \right>_0$) hence $\Pi_0\Delta_0 = 0$. Therefore, the map,
$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{rcl}
		\displaystyle (U,\left\| \cdot \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}) \times B \times (\Omega^0(X,\mathrm{End}_H(E,h)),\left\| \cdot \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^2}) & \longrightarrow & \displaystyle \frak{k}\\
		\displaystyle (\varepsilon,b,s) & \longmapsto & \displaystyle \Pi_\varepsilon\Delta_{\varepsilon,\nabla_b}s
	\end{array} \right.,
$$
vanishes at each $(0,0,s)$. By Lemma \ref{LEM:Applications lisses}, it is smooth hence,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne Delta}
    \left\| \Pi_\varepsilon\Delta_{\varepsilon,\nabla_b}s \right\|_\varepsilon = \mathrm{O}((\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + \left\| b \right\|)\left\| s \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^2}).
\end{equation}
Using the fact that $\nu_\varepsilon$ takes values in $\frak{k}$,
\begin{align*}
    \left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b) - \Pi_\varepsilon\nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_b) \right\|_\varepsilon & = \left\| \Pi_\varepsilon(\nu_\varepsilon(b) - \nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_b)) \right\|_\varepsilon\\
    & = \left\| \Pi_\varepsilon(\nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,b)} \cdot \overline{\partial}_b) - \nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_b)) \right\|_\varepsilon\\
    & = \left\| \Pi_\varepsilon\Delta_{\varepsilon,\nabla_b}\sigma(\varepsilon,b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \mathrm{O}(\left\| \sigma(\varepsilon,b) \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^2}^2) \textrm{ by Proposition \ref{PRO:Calcul dPsi(0)},}\\
    & = \mathrm{O}((\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + \left\| b \right\|)\left\| \sigma(\varepsilon,b) \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^2}) + \mathrm{O}(\left\| \sigma(\varepsilon,b) \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^2}^2) \textrm{ by (\ref{EQ:Borne sigma}) and (\ref{EQ:Borne Delta}),}\\
    & = \mathrm{O}(\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}\left\| b \right\| + \left\| b \right\|^3).
\end{align*}
Finally,
$$
\left\| b \right\|^2 = \mathrm{O}\!\left(\left\| \Pi_\varepsilon\nu_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\overline{\partial}_b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|\right) = \mathrm{O}(\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}\left\| b \right\| + \left\| b \right\|^3 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|),
$$
so,
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\left\| b \right\|^2}{\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|} & = \mathrm{O}\!\left(\frac{\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}\left\| b \right\| + \left\| b \right\|^3 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|}{\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|}\right)\\
    & = \mathrm{O}\!\left(1 + \frac{\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}\left\| b \right\|}{\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|}\right) + \mathrm{o}\!\left(\frac{\left\| b \right\|^2}{\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|}\right)\\
    & = \mathrm{O}\!\left(1 + \frac{\left\| b \right\|}{\sqrt{\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|}}\right) + \mathrm{o}\!\left(\frac{\left\| b \right\|^2}{\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|}\right)
\end{align*}

    Up to shrinking $U$, for all $\varepsilon \in U$, if we choose $b_\varepsilon(0)$ close enough to $0$, $b_\varepsilon$ is defined for all non-negative times, $t \mapsto \left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) \right\|_\varepsilon$ decreases and $\left\| b_\varepsilon(t) \right\| = \mathrm{O}\!\left(\sqrt{\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}}\right)$ uniformly with respect to $t$.

For all $t$ where the flow is defined, whatever is the choice of the starting point,
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) \right\|_\varepsilon^2\right) & = \left< d\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t))(-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} L_b\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t))),\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) \right>_\varepsilon\\
    & = -\Omega_\varepsilon(L_b\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)),\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} L_b\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t))) \textrm{ by the moment map property,}\\
    & \leqslant 0 \textrm{ by Lemma \ref{LEM:Omega positive}}.
\end{align*}
It proves that $t \mapsto \left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) \right\|_\varepsilon$ decreases while the flow is defined. In particular, by Proposition \ref{PRO:Inégalité norme b},
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Inégalité norme flux}
    \left\| b_\varepsilon(t) \right\|^2 \leqslant C(\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|) \leqslant C(\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0)) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|)
\end{equation}
Let $r > 0$ such that the closed ball of centre $0$ and radius $r$ is included in $B$. Assume, up to shrinking $U$, that for all $\varepsilon \in U$,
$$
\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\| \leqslant \frac{r^2}{2C}, \qquad \left\| \nu_\varepsilon(0) \right\|_\varepsilon \leqslant \frac{r^2}{4C}.
$$
Then choose $b_\varepsilon(0)$ close enough to $0$ so $\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0)) \right\|_\varepsilon \leqslant 2\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(0) \right\|_\varepsilon \leqslant \frac{r^2}{2C}$. It implies by (\ref{EQ:Inégalité norme flux}) that $\left\| b_\varepsilon(t) \right\| \leqslant r$ so $b_\varepsilon$ stays in a compact set included in $B$. By the finite time explosion theorem, the flow is defined at all time. Moreover,
$$
\left\| b_\varepsilon(t) \right\|^2 \leqslant C(\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0)) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|) \leqslant C(2\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(0) \right\|_\varepsilon + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + \left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|) = \mathrm{O}(\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}).
$$

$\varphi_\varepsilon$ is defined intrinsically in the sense that it doesn't depend on the choice of the starting point $b_\varepsilon(0)$. Since we have moreover a uniform bound on the norm of $b_\varepsilon(t)$ given by Proposition \ref{PRO:Flot défini}, it is enough to verify the wanted equality at $t = 0$ and the uniform bound follows.

\noindent\textbf{Step 1 :} Derivative of the Hermitian part of a gauge transformation.
\vspace{1mm}

For all $t$ near $0$, by (\ref{EQ:dbar flux}), we have $\overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(t)} = \tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)^{-1} \cdot \overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} = \tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)^{-1}f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1} \cdot \overline{\partial}_E$ hence $f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(t)} = f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)$. In particular, if we set $k = f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} \cdot h$, we have,
$$
f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(t)} \cdot h = f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1} \cdot k.
$$

Let us write the polar decomposition of $f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}$ with respect to $k$ as $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s(t)/2}u(t)$ where $u(t)$ is the unitary part (on the right) of $f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}$. In particular,
$$
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s(t)} = f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}(f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1})^\dagger,
$$
where $\cdot^\dagger$ is the adjoint with respect to $k$. $s$ is smooth with respect to any Sobolev norm and we have $s(0) = 0$ because $\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(0) = \mathrm{Id}_E$ so,
\begin{align*}
    -s(t) & \underset{t \rightarrow 0}{=} \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s(t)} - \mathrm{Id}_E + \mathrm{o}(t)\\
    & \underset{t \rightarrow 0}{=} f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1\dagger}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)^\dagger f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^\dagger - \mathrm{Id}_E + \mathrm{o}(t)\\
    & \underset{t \rightarrow 0}{=} (f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon'(0)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1} + f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1\dagger}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon'(0)^\dagger f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^\dagger)t + \mathrm{o}(t)\\
    & \underset{t \rightarrow 0}{=} 2\Re_k(f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon'(0)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1})t + \mathrm{o}(t),
\end{align*}
where $\Re_k$ is the Hermitian part with respect to $k$. We deduce that,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:s'(0)}
    s'(0) = -2\Re_k(f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon'(0)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Now, recall that for all $t$, $\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t) = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0))}g_\varepsilon(t)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-\sigma(\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(t))}$. Therefore, since $\left\| b_\varepsilon(0) \right\| \underset{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0}{=} \mathrm{o}(1)$, we have,
\begin{align*}
    \tilde{g}_\varepsilon'(0) & \ =\ \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0))}g_\varepsilon'(0)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-\sigma(\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0))} - \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0))}d\exp(-\sigma(\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)))\frac{\partial}{\partial b}|_{b = b_\varepsilon(0)}\sigma(\varepsilon,b)b_\varepsilon'(0)\\
    & \underset{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0}{=} g_\varepsilon'(0) + \mathrm{o}(g_\varepsilon'(0)) + \mathrm{o}(b_\varepsilon'(0)) \textrm{ because $\sigma(0,0) = 0$ and $\frac{\partial}{\partial b}|_{b = 0}\sigma(0,b) = 0$,}\\
    & \underset{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0}{=} \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0)) + \mathrm{o}(\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0))) + \mathrm{o}(L_{b_\varepsilon(0)}\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0)))\\
    & \underset{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0}{=} \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0)) + \mathrm{o}(\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0))).
\end{align*}
We deduce from this equality and (\ref{EQ:s'(0)}) that,
\begin{align}
    f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}s'(0)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} & \ =\ -2f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}\Re_k(f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon'(0)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1})f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} \nonumber\\
    & \ =\ -2\Re_h(\tilde{g}_\varepsilon'(0)) \textrm{ because } k = f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} \cdot h, \nonumber\\
    & \underset{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0}{=} -2\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0)) + \mathrm{o}(\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0))). \label{EQ:f-1 s'(0) f}
\end{align}

\noindent\textbf{Step 2 :} Relation between curvatures.
\vspace{1mm}

Since $f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(t)} \cdot h = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s(t)/2}u(t) \cdot k = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s(t)/2} \cdot k$, we have, by (\ref{EQ:Chasles}),
$$
\varphi_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) - \varphi_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0)) = M_{\varepsilon,\overline{\partial}_E}(h,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s(t)/2} \cdot k) - M_{\varepsilon,\overline{\partial}_E}(h,k) = M_{\varepsilon,\overline{\partial}_E}(k,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s(t)/2} \cdot k).
$$
Moreover, if we call $\partial_{E,k}$ the $(1,0)$ part of the Chern connection associated with $(\overline{\partial}_E,k)$ and we set $\partial_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} = f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1} \circ \partial_{E,k} \circ f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}$, we can compute that the connection $\nabla_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} = \partial_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} + \overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}$ is unitary with respect to $h$. Thus, by uniqueness, $\nabla_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}$ is the Chern connection associated with $(\overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)},h)$. Let $\nabla_{E,k} = \partial_{E,k} + \overline{\partial}_E$ be the associated connection. Notice that
$$
\nabla_{E,k} = f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} \circ \nabla_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} \circ f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}.
$$
These connections are conjugated. It is due to the fact that $f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} : (E,\overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)},h) \rightarrow (E,\overline{\partial}_E,k)$ is both holomorphic and unitary.

Therefore,
$$
F_{\overline{\partial}_E,k} = \nabla_{E,k} \circ \nabla_{E,k} = f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} \circ \nabla_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} \circ \nabla_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)} \circ f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1} = f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}F_{\overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)},h}f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}.
$$
Thus,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Contractions des courbures conjuguées}
    \Lambda_\varepsilon\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{\overline{\partial}_E,k} - c_\varepsilon\mathrm{Id}_E = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0))f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}.
\end{equation}

\vspace{1mm}
\noindent\textbf{Step 3 :} Conclusion.
\vspace{1mm}

Finally, by (\ref{EQ:Dérivée Donaldson}), when $\varepsilon \rightarrow 0$,
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\partial}{\partial t}|_{t = 0}\varphi_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) & \ =\ \int_X \mathrm{tr}\left(s'(0)(\Lambda_\varepsilon\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}} F_{\overline{\partial}_E,k} - c_\varepsilon\mathrm{Id}_E)\right)\mathrm{Vol}_\varepsilon\\
    & \ =\ \int_X \mathrm{tr}\left(f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}s'(0)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0))\right)\mathrm{Vol}_\varepsilon \textrm{ by (\ref{EQ:Contractions des courbures conjuguées}),}\\
    & \underset{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0}{=} \int_X \mathrm{tr}\left((-2\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0)) + \mathrm{o}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0))))\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0))\right)\mathrm{Vol}_\varepsilon \textrm{ by (\ref{EQ:f-1 s'(0) f}),}\\
    & \underset{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0}{=} -2\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(0) \right\|_\varepsilon^2 + \mathrm{o}(\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(0) \right\|_\varepsilon^2).
\end{align*}

Assume first of all that $\mathcal{E}$ is $[\Theta_\varepsilon]$-stable. Let for all $t$, $f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(t)} \cdot h = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s(t)/2} \cdot h$ for some $s(t)$ Hermitian with respect to $h$. We may assume that for all $t$,
$$
\int_X \mathrm{tr}(s(t))\mathrm{Vol}_0 = 0.
$$
Then, by the inequality (\ref{EQ:Simpson}) and Proposition \ref{PRO:phi décroissante}, for all $t$,
$$
\left\| s(t) \right\|_\varepsilon \leqslant C_{1,\varepsilon} + C_{2,\varepsilon}M(h,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s(t)/2} \cdot h) = C_{1,\varepsilon} + C_{2,\varepsilon}\varphi_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) \leqslant C_{1,\varepsilon} + C_{2,\varepsilon}\varphi_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(0)).
$$
It means that $t \mapsto s(t)$ is $L^2$ bounded. We have by definition,
$$
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-s(t)} = f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1}(f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)f_{\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0)}^{-1})^*,
$$
thus $t \mapsto \tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)$ and $t \mapsto \tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)^{-1}$ are $L^2$ bounded. We also have,
$$
g_\varepsilon(t) = \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-\sigma(\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(0))}\tilde{g}_\varepsilon(t)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon(t))},
$$
thus $t \mapsto g_\varepsilon(t)$ and $t \mapsto g_\varepsilon(t)^{-1}$ are bounded too (for any norm since they lie in a finite dimensional vector space). Therefore, we can find a sequence $t_m \rightarrow +\infty$ such that $g_\varepsilon(t_m) \rightarrow g_\varepsilon \in G$ is invertible and in particular,
$$
b_\varepsilon(t_m) \rightarrow b_{\infty,\varepsilon} = g_\varepsilon \cdot b_\varepsilon(0) \in \mathcal{O}.
$$
Let $l = \nu_\varepsilon(b_{\infty,\varepsilon}) \in \frak{k}$.

Since the norm of the moment map decreases by Proposition \ref{PRO:Flot défini}, $\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) \right\|_\varepsilon \rightarrow \left\| l \right\|_\varepsilon$. By Proposition \ref{PRO:phi décroissante}, up to shrinking $U$,
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) \underset{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0}{=} -2\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) \right\|_\varepsilon^2 + \mathrm{o}(\left\| \nu_\varepsilon(b_\varepsilon(t)) \right\|_\varepsilon^2) \leqslant -\left\| l \right\|_\varepsilon^2 \textrm{ for $t$ large enough},
$$
and inequality (\ref{EQ:Simpson}) implies that $\varphi$ is bounded from below hence $l = 0$.

In the semi-stable case, we have by Theorem \ref{THE:Structures cônes} that $\varepsilon = \lim_{m \rightarrow +\infty} \varepsilon_m$ for some $\varepsilon_m$ such that $\mathcal{E}$ is $[\Theta_{\varepsilon_m}]$-stable. The $b_{\infty,\varepsilon_m}$ remain in a compact set included in $B$ so, up to extraction, we may build $b_{\infty,\varepsilon} \in B \cap \overline{\mathcal{O}}$ as $\lim_{m \rightarrow +\infty} b_{\infty,\varepsilon_m}$. The bound on the norm of $b_{\infty,\varepsilon}$ is given by Proposition \ref{PRO:Inégalité norme b}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{THE:Famille de connections HYM}
    Let $(X,\Theta_0)$ be a compact balanced manifold and $\mathcal{E}$ a $[\Theta_0]$-semi-stable sufficiently smooth holomorphic vector bundle. Assume that $\mathcal{C}_s(\mathcal{E}) \neq \emptyset$. There is a $\mathcal{C}^0$ open neighbourhood $U$ of $0$ in the space of closed $(n - 1,n - 1)$ forms and a family $(\overline{\partial}_\varepsilon)_{\varepsilon \in U|\Theta_\varepsilon \in \mathcal{C}_{ss}(\mathcal{E})}$ of integrable Dolbeault operators on $E$ such that for all $\varepsilon$, $\overline{\partial}_\varepsilon$ is $\Theta_\varepsilon$-HYM and $(E,\overline{\partial}_\varepsilon) \cong \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_\varepsilon]}(\mathcal{E})$. Moreover,
    $$
    \left\| \overline{\partial}_\varepsilon - \overline{\partial}_0 \right\|_A = \mathrm{O}\!\left(\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{A'} + \sqrt{\left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|}\right).
    $$
    In particular, $\overline{\partial}_\varepsilon \underset{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow} \overline{\partial}_0$ for the $\mathcal{C}^\infty$ topology on $U$ and on the space of Dolbeault operators.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Let for all $\varepsilon \in U$ such that $\mathcal{E}$ is $[\Theta_\varepsilon]$-semi-stable, $\overline{\partial}_\varepsilon = \overline{\partial}_0 + \tilde{\Phi}(\varepsilon,b_{\infty,\varepsilon})$ where $b_{\infty,\varepsilon}$ is given by Proposition \ref{PRO:Limite b_varepsilon}. The HYM condition is fulfilled and when $\Theta_\varepsilon$ is in the stable cone, $b_{\infty,\varepsilon} \in \mathcal{O}$ so,
$$
(E,\overline{\partial}_\varepsilon) \cong \mathcal{E} \cong \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_\varepsilon]}(\mathcal{E}).
$$
When $\Theta_\varepsilon$ is only in the semi-stable cone, by \cite[Theorem 4]{Buchdahl_Schumacher}\footnote{In this paper, the results are proven in the Kähler case. However, in the third point of their conclusion and in the remark following \cite[Proposition 3.2]{Buchdahl_Schumacher}, the authors notice that the only obstruction for it to work on any compact complex manifold equipped with a Gauduchon metric is that in this case, the slope might not be of topological nature. In the balanced case, the slope is of topological nature since it only depends on the first Chern class of the bundle and not on the choice of its representative.},
$$
(E,\overline{\partial}_\varepsilon) \cong (E,\overline{\partial}_0 + \Phi(b_{\infty,\varepsilon})) \cong \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_\varepsilon]}(\mathcal{E}).
$$

By smoothness of $\sigma$ given by Proposition \ref{PRO:Tranche déformée} and the bound on the norm of $b$ given by Proposition \ref{PRO:Limite b_varepsilon}, we have, when $\varepsilon \rightarrow 0$,
\begin{align*}
    \left\| \overline{\partial}_\varepsilon - \overline{\partial}_0 \right\|_A & = \left\| \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,b_{\infty,\varepsilon})} \cdot (\overline{\partial}_0 + \Phi(b_{\infty,\varepsilon})) - \overline{\partial}_0 \right\|_A\\
    & = \left\| \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,b_{\infty,\varepsilon})}\overline{\partial}_0(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-\sigma(\varepsilon,b_{\infty,\varepsilon})}) + \boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\sigma(\varepsilon,b_{\infty,\varepsilon})}\Phi(b_{\infty,\varepsilon})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-\sigma(\varepsilon,b_{\infty,\varepsilon})} \right\|_A \textrm{ by (\ref{EQ:Action de gauge}),}\\
    & = \mathrm{O}(\left\| \sigma(\varepsilon,b_{\infty,\varepsilon}) \right\|_{'\!A}) + \mathrm{O}(\left\| \Phi(b_{\infty,\varepsilon}) \right\|_A)\\
    & = \mathrm{O}(\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{A'} + \left\| b_{\infty,\varepsilon} \right\|^2) + \mathrm{O}(\left\| b_{\infty,\varepsilon} \right\|)\\
    & = \mathrm{O}\!\left(\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{A'} + \left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + \sqrt{\left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|}\right)\\
    & = \mathrm{O}\!\left(\left\| \varepsilon \right\|_{A'} + \sqrt{\left\| [\varepsilon] \right\|}\right).
\end{align*}

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{THE:Gamma continue}
    The function,
    $$
    \Gamma_\mathcal{E} : \left\{ \begin{array}{rcl}
		\displaystyle \underline{\mathcal{C}}_{ss}(\mathcal{E}) & \longrightarrow & \displaystyle \mathcal{H}(E)/\mathcal{G}\\
		\displaystyle \Theta & \longmapsto & \displaystyle \{\overline{\partial} \in \mathcal{H}(E)|(E,\overline{\partial}) \cong \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E}) \textrm{ and $\overline{\partial}$ is $\Theta$-HYM}\}
	\end{array} \right.,
    $$
    is well-defined and continuous.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
For all $\Theta \in \underline{\mathcal{C}}_{ss}(\mathcal{E})$, $\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E})$ is a $[\Theta]$-polystable vector bundle so Proposition \ref{PRO:Kobayashi--Hitchin} tells us that the set,
$$
\{\overline{\partial} \in \mathcal{H}(E)|(\mathcal{E},\overline{\partial}) \cong \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E}) \textrm{ and $\overline{\partial}$ is $\Theta$-HYM}\},
$$
is exactly one $\mathcal{G}$-orbit so $\Gamma_\mathcal{E}$ is well defined and indeed takes values in $\mathcal{H}(E)/\mathcal{G}$. Let $K \subset \mathcal{B}_X$ be a convex compact subset. It is enough to show continuity on the polyhedral cone $C = \underline{\mathcal{C}}_{ss}(\mathcal{E}) \cap K$ for all such $K$ and we may assume that $C \neq \emptyset$. We have to consider three cases.

First case : $\mathcal{C}_s(\mathcal{E}) \cap C \neq \emptyset$. In this case, continuity of $\Gamma$ at each point $\Theta_0$ is a consequence of Theorem \ref{THE:Famille de connections HYM}.

Second case : It exists $\Theta_0 \in C$ such that $F_{[\Theta_0]}$ is the whole cone $C$ and $\mathcal{E}$ is $[\Theta_0]$-polystable. In this case, let us write,
$$
\mathcal{E} = \bigoplus_{k = 1}^m \mathcal{E}_k,
$$
with the $\mathcal{E}_k$ $[\Theta_0]$-stable with the same $[\Theta_0]$-slope. For all $\Theta \in \underline{\mathcal{C}}_{ss}(\mathcal{E})$, each $\mathcal{E}_k$ is $[\Theta]$-semi-stable and have the same $[\Theta]$-slope as $\mathcal{E}$ because $[\Theta] \in F_{[\Theta_0]}$. It implies that,
$$
\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E}) = \bigoplus_{k = 1}^m \mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E}_k).
$$
Moreover, any $\Theta$-HYM connection on $\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E})$ makes this decomposition holomorphic and induces a $\Theta$-HYM connection on each $\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta]}(\mathcal{E}_k)$. Therefore, it makes sense to write that $\Gamma_\mathcal{E} = \sum_{k = 1}^m \Gamma_{\mathcal{E}_k}$. The fact that each $\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}_k}$ is continuous is the first case because for all $k$, $\Theta_0 \in \mathcal{C}_s(\mathcal{E}_k) \cap C$.

Third case : General case. $C$ is assumed to be non-empty so there is a $\Theta_0$ such that $F_{[\Theta_0]} = C$. Thus, for all $\Theta \in \underline{\mathcal{C}_{ss}}(\mathcal{E})$, $F_{[\Theta]} \subset F_{[\Theta_0]}$. Therefore, a consequence of Lemma \ref{LEM:Gr(Gr)} is that,
$$
\Gamma_\mathcal{E} = \Gamma_{\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_0]}(\mathcal{E})},
$$
and $\mathrm{Gr}_{[\Theta_0]}(\mathcal{E})$ is a $[\Theta_0]$-polystable vector bundle. We conclude thanks to the second case.
