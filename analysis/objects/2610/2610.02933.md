<!-- question-1: body_md -->
\label{q:main}
Is the normalized minimizer constructed in \cite{HLW2024} a ground state of the
stationary problem, and is the level $m_a(c)$ a sharp threshold separating
scattering from blow-up among data of mass $c$?
<!-- end question-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:level}
Let $N\ge3$, $a>a_{\mathrm{H}}$, and $0<b<2$, and assume that \eqref{eq:range} holds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] For every $c>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:levelformula}
m_a(c)=K_{\alpha,N,b}\;\mathcal{C}_a^{-\frac{4}{N\alpha+2b-4}}\;c^{-\gamma}.
\end{equation}
In particular $c\mapsto c^{\gamma}m_a(c)$ is constant, and $m_a$ is real
analytic and strictly decreasing on $(0,\infty)$, with $m_a(c)\to\infty$ as
$c\downarrow0$ and $m_a(c)\to0$ as $c\to\infty$.
\item[(ii)] The constant $\mathcal{C}_a$ is attained, and $m_a(c)$ is attained for every
$c>0$. A function $u\in V(c)$ satisfies $E_a(u)=m_a(c)$ if and only if it is an
optimizer of \eqref{eq:GNintro}. Consequently the set of minimizers of $m_a(c)$
is $\{W^{t_W}:W\ \text{an optimizer with}\ \|W\|_{L^{2}}=c\}$, where $t_W>0$ is
the unique dilation parameter for which $W^{t_W}\in V(c)$.
\item[(iii)] Every minimizer $u_c$ of $m_a(c)$ is a ground state of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationary}
\mathcal{L}_a u+\omega u=|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u
\end{equation}
at the frequency
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omegac}
\omega_c=\Lambda_{\alpha,N,b}\,\mathcal{C}_a^{-\frac{4}{N\alpha+2b-4}}\;
c^{-\frac{4\alpha}{N\alpha+2b-4}},\qquad
\Lambda_{\alpha,N,b}:=\frac{\sigma_c}{\alpha+2}\Big(\frac{\alpha+2}{\beta}\Big)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-2}},
\end{equation}
that is, $S_{\omega_c}(u_c)\le S_{\omega_c}(v)$ for every nontrivial solution $v\inH^1_a$ of
\eqref{eq:stationary}, where $S_\omega:=E_a+\frac{\omega}{2}\mathcal{M}$.
Conversely, every ground state $v$ of \eqref{eq:stationary}, at any frequency
$\omega>0$, is a minimizer of $m_a(\|v\|_{L^{2}})$, and $\omega=\omega_{\|v\|_{L^2}}$.
The map $c\mapsto\omega_c$ is a decreasing bijection of $(0,\infty)$ onto itself.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\rm\label{rmk:limiting}
The case $a=0$ is covered by Theorem \ref{thm:level}. The case $b=0$ is excluded,
and the proof shows exactly where $b>0$ is needed.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item\emph{The case $a=0$ and $0<b<2$.} Then $\mathcal{L}_a=-\Delta$, and Theorem
\ref{thm:level} applies without change. The constant $\mathcal{C}_0$ is the sharp
constant of Farah \cite{Farah2016}, and \eqref{eq:levelformula} gives the
normalized level of the inhomogeneous equation without potential in terms of it.
For $N\ge3$, the positive radial solution of \eqref{eq:ellipticQ} with $a=0$ is
unique \cite{Yanagida1991,GenoudStuart2008}. By Theorem \ref{thm:equiv}, the
conditions $E_a(u_0)<m_a(c)$ and $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)\gtrless0$, with $a=0$, are equivalent to
the scale-invariant conditions used for this equation in
\cite{Farah2016,FarahGuzman2017,FarahGuzman2020,Campos2021,MiaoMurphyZheng2021,CamposCardoso2022,CFGM2022,Murphy2022}.
Hence the global existence, blow-up and scattering results proved there hold
below $m_0(c)$, in the corresponding ranges of $(N,\alpha,b)$.

\item\emph{The case $b=0$.} In Theorem \ref{thm:level}, the hypothesis $b>0$ is
used only through the compactness property of Lemma \ref{lem:weighted}(b), that
is, only for the attainment of $\mathcal{C}_a$. Part (i) holds for $b=0$ and every
$a>a_{\mathrm{H}}$, since its proof uses only the finiteness of $\mathcal{C}_a$ and the scaling
relations \eqref{eq:scalingAB}. Parts (ii) and (iii) hold for $b=0$ whenever
$\mathcal{C}_a$ is attained. Three situations occur.
If $a=b=0$, then $\mathcal{C}_0$ is attained by the ground state of Weinstein
\cite{Weinstein1983} and its translates. Theorem \ref{thm:level} then reduces to
classical facts on normalized solutions \cite{Jeanjean1997}, the dichotomy below
the level is that of
\cite{HolmerRoudenko2008,DHR2008,AkahoriNawa2013,Guevara2014,FXC2011}, and the
strong instability is due to Berestycki and Cazenave \cite{BerestyckiCazenave1981}.
If $b=0$ and $a_{\mathrm{H}}<a<0$, then $\mathcal{C}_a>\mathcal{C}_0$, and $\mathcal{C}_a$ is attained
\cite{KMVZ2017,MNN2021}. Parts (ii) and (iii) remain valid, and the ground state is
unique up to phase by \cite{MNN2021}.
If $b=0$ and $a>0$, the picture changes. Since $\|u\|_{\dot H^1_a}$ increases with $a$,
we have $\mathcal{C}_a\le\mathcal{C}_0$. Translating Weinstein's ground state $Q_0$ to
infinity, we get $J\big(Q_0(\cdot-y)\big)\to\mathcal{C}_0$ as $|y|\to\infty$,
because $\int|Q_0(x-y)|^2|x|^{-2}\,dx\to0$. Hence $\mathcal{C}_a=\mathcal{C}_0$. This constant
is not attained. Indeed, an optimizer $W$ would satisfy
$\mathcal{C}_0=J(W)<J_0(W)\le\mathcal{C}_0$, where $J_0$ denotes the quotient
\eqref{eq:GNquotient} with $a=0$. Consequently, $m_a(c)=m_0(c)$ for every $a\ge0$,
and, by the argument used in the proof of part (ii), $m_a(c)$ is not attained for
$a>0$. The proof of Theorem \ref{thm:equiv} still applies, with $Q$ replaced by
$Q_0$, and it gives exactly the conditions \eqref{eq:KMVZ} with $a\wedge0=0$. This
explains the appearance of $a\wedge0$ in \cite{KMVZ2017}. In this case there is no
minimizer, and the sharpness statement of Theorem \ref{thm:dichotomy} has no
counterpart in the form given there.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\rm \label{rmk:sharphyp}
The assumptions $N\ge3$ and \eqref{eq:range} are essential for the result in its
present form. We briefly discuss the corresponding borderline and complementary
cases. The case $b=0$ is considered in Remark \ref{rmk:limiting}, while the
critical coupling $a=a_{\mathrm{H}}$ is treated in Subsection \ref{ssec:hardy}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{The mass-critical case $\alpha=\frac{4-2b}{N}$.} In this case
$\beta=2$, and therefore the exponent of $\mathcal{C}_a$ in \eqref{eq:levelformula} is not defined. By
\eqref{eq:scalingAB}, the fibering map reduces to
$h_u(t)=t^{2}E_a(u)$, and hence it has no interior maximum. Proposition
\ref{prop:masscritical} shows that $V(c)=\emptyset$ for $c<c^*$, while
$m_a(c)=0$ for $c\ge c^*$, where $c^*$ is given by \eqref{eq:cstar}. Thus,
parts (i)--(iii) no longer hold in the stated form, and the relevant threshold
is determined by the mass alone. Moreover,
$c^*=\|Q\|_{2}$, where $Q$ is the ground state from
\cite[Lemma~3.2]{CamposGuzman2021}. This agrees with
\cite[Theorem~1.2]{CamposGuzman2021}.

\item \emph{The mass-subcritical case $0<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{N}$.} Here
$\beta<2$. The unique critical point $t_u$ of $h_u$ is a strict global
minimum, and $E_a<0$ on $V(c)$. Consequently,
$m_a(c)=\inf_{S(c)}E_a$. The computation in Lemma \ref{lem:fibering} and the
proofs of parts (i) and (ii) therefore remain unchanged. In particular,
\eqref{eq:levelformula} still holds, now with $K_{\alpha,N,b}<0$ and
$\gamma<0$. Hence $m_a(c)<0$, $m_a(c)\to0$ as $c\downarrow0$, and
$m_a(c)\to-\infty$ as $c\to\infty$. The minimizers are again precisely the
optimizers of \eqref{eq:GN} with mass $c$ that belong to $V(c)$. In this
regime, $m_a(c)$ is the minimum of the energy on the sphere $S(c)$. This is
the setting of orbital stability considered in \cite{CazenaveLions1982}, rather
than that of a dynamical threshold. Moreover, every solution is global; see
\cite{Suzuki2016,CamposGuzman2021}.

\item \emph{The energy-critical case
$\alpha=\frac{4-2b}{N-2}$.} In this case $\sigma_c=0$ and $\gamma=0$, and
\eqref{eq:GN} becomes the Hardy--Sobolev type inequality
$B(u)\le\mathcal{C}_a A(u)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}$. This inequality is invariant under the
scaling $u\mapsto\lambda^{\frac{N-2}{2}}u(\lambda\cdot)$, which preserves
$A$ and $B$ but not the mass. Consequently, the level given by
\eqref{eq:maxvalue} is independent of $c$. At the same time, the compactness
property in Lemma \ref{lem:weighted}(b) is lost, since optimizing sequences may
either concentrate or spread out.

The argument used in part (ii) shows that $m_a(c)$ is attained for one, and
equivalently for every, $c>0$, if and only if \eqref{eq:GN} admits an optimizer
in $\dot H^1_a$ that belongs to $L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^N)$. The natural threshold is then given by
the energy of such an optimizer, independently of the mass, as in
Kenig and Merle \cite{KenigMerle2006} and, for $a=0$, Guzm\'an and Murphy
\cite{GuzmanMurphy2021}. The Cauchy theory is given in \cite{Kim}; see also
Table~\ref{tab_LWP}. For normalized solutions at the Sobolev-critical exponent,
we refer to \cite{Soave2020b,JJLV2022}. If
$\alpha>\frac{4-2b}{N-2}$, the functional $B$ is no longer controlled on
$H^1_a$, and consequently $\mathcal{C}_a=+\infty$.

\item \emph{The dimension.} The assumption $N\ge3$ enters through the Hardy
inequality \eqref{eq:hardy}, which yields \eqref{eq:equivnorm} and the
identification $H^1_a=H^1$. It also enters through the Sobolev embedding used in
Lemma \ref{lem:weighted} and through the Cauchy theory. For $N=2$, we have
$a_{\mathrm{H}}=0$, and \eqref{eq:hardy} fails. When $a>0$, the form domain of $\mathcal{L}_a$ is
then a proper subspace of $H^1(\mathbb{R}^2)$, and \eqref{eq:range} becomes
$\alpha>2-b$. For $N=1$, the form domain of $\mathcal{L}_a$ is
$H^1_0(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})$, so the problem decouples into two problems on the
half-lines.

The variational argument in Section \ref{sec:variational} uses only
\eqref{eq:GN}, \eqref{eq:scalingAB}, and the compactness statement in
Lemma \ref{lem:weighted}(b). We therefore expect that it extends to $N=2$ and
$a>0$, with $H^1_a$ replaced by the form domain of $\mathcal{L}_a$. The corresponding
dynamical results would, however, require an appropriate Cauchy theory in this
setting.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:dichotomy}
Let $N\ge3$, $a>a_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $0<b<2$. Assume that \eqref{eq:range} and Assumption~\ref{ass:cauchy} hold. Let $u_0\inH^1_a(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:below}
E_a(u_0)<m_a(c),\qquad c:=\|u_0\|_{L^{2}} .
\end{equation}
Then $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)\ne0$, the sign of $\mathcal{Q}_a(u(t))$ does not depend on $t$, and the
following alternative holds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)>0$, the solution of \eqref{eq:main} is global. If
moreover \eqref{eq:dynrange} holds, it scatters in $H^1_a(\mathbb{R}^{3})$: there exist
$u_\pm\inH^1_a(\mathbb{R}^{3})$ such that $\|u(t)-e^{-it\mathcal{L}_a}u_\pm\|_{H^1_a}\to0$ as
$t\to\pm\infty$.
\item[(ii)] If $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)<0$ and $u_0\in\Sigma$, the solution blows up in finite
time in both time directions, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blowup}
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}|x|^{2}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx
\le 8\beta\big(E_a(u_0)-m_a(c)\big)<0
\end{equation}
on the whole lifespan.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, the condition \eqref{eq:below} is sharp: for every $c>0$ there is
$u_0\in V(c)\cap\Sigma$ with $E_a(u_0)=m_a(c)$ whose solution is the global,
non-scattering standing wave $e^{i\omega_ct}u_c$.

\rm \label{rmk:negenergy}
Since $m_a(c)>0$, Theorem \ref{thm:dichotomy} applies to every $u_0\ne0$ with
$E_a(u_0)\le0$. For such data, \eqref{eq:conserved} and \eqref{eq:Qdef} give
$\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)=\betaE_a(u_0)-\frac{\beta-2}{2}\|u_0\|_{\dot H^1_a}^{2}<0$. Hence every
$u_0\in\Sigma\setminus\{0\}$ with $E_a(u_0)\le0$ blows up in finite time in both
time directions. This is the criterion of Glassey \cite{Glassey1977}; see
\cite{Farah2016} for $a=0$ and \cite{Dinh2021} for $b=0$. Conversely, part (i)
concerns only data of positive energy, since $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)>0$ forces
$0<E_a(u_0)<m_a(c)$ (Lemma \ref{lem:invariance}). The substance of Theorem
\ref{thm:dichotomy} thus lies in the range $0<E_a(u_0)<m_a(c)$, where the energy
alone does not determine the sign of $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:equiv}
Let $N\ge3$, $a>a_{\mathrm{H}}$, and $0<b<2$. Assume that \eqref{eq:range} holds, and let $Q=Q_{a,b}$  be a ground state solution of \eqref{eq:ellipticQ}.
Then for every
$u_0\inH^1_a\setminus\{0\}$ of mass $c=\|u_0\|_{L^{2}}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equivE}
E_a(u_0)<m_a(c)
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\mathcal{M}(u_0)^{\sigma}E_a(u_0)<\mathcal{M}(Q)^{\sigma}E_a(Q),
\end{equation}
and, when these conditions hold,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equivQ}
\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)>0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\|u_0\|_{L^{2}}^{\sigma}\|u_0\|_{\dot H^1_a}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\sigma}\|Q\|_{\dot H^1_a},
\end{equation}
with $\sigma=\frac{1-s_c}{s_c}$ as in \eqref{eq:sigma}. If moreover
$E_a(u_0)>0$, the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:equivE} can be written in the
familiar form
$\mathcal{M}(u_0)^{1-s_c}E_a(u_0)^{s_c}<\mathcal{M}(Q)^{1-s_c}E_a(Q)^{s_c}$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\rm 
In the potential-well theory of Payne and Sattinger \cite{ps}, the region below
the ground-state level is divided by the sign of a functional into a stable set
and an unstable set, and the two conditions are used together. Theorem
\ref{thm:equiv} gives this combined form at once:
\begin{multline*}
\Big[E_a(u_0)<m_a(c)\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)>0\Big]\\
\iff
\Big[\mathcal{M}(u_0)^{\sigma}E_a(u_0)<\mathcal{M}(Q)^{\sigma}E_a(Q)\ \text{and}\
\|u_0\|_{2}^{\sigma}\|u_0\|_{\dot H^1_a}<\|Q\|_{2}^{\sigma}\|Q\|_{\dot H^1_a}\Big].
\end{multline*}
The same equivalence holds with $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)<0$ on the left and
$\|u_0\|_{2}^{\sigma}\|u_0\|_{\dot H^1_a}>\|Q\|_{2}^{\sigma}\|Q\|_{\dot H^1_a}$ on the right.
This last inequality is strict. Indeed, when $E_a(u_0)<m_a(c)$ and
$\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)<0$, the proof of Lemma \ref{lem:coercivity}(i) gives
$\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}\|u_0\|_{\dot H^1_a}^{2}>m_a(c)$, that is,
$\|u_0\|_{\dot H^1_a}^{2}>A_*(c)$, with $A_*(c)$ given by \eqref{eq:Astar}. The separate
formulation of Theorem \ref{thm:equiv} is slightly stronger, since
\eqref{eq:equivE} requires no sign condition. The energy condition must be
written with $m_a(c)$, or equivalently with the factor $\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}$. The
condition $E_a(u_0)<E_a(Q)$ alone is not invariant under \eqref{eq:scaling}.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\rm \label{rmk:exponent}
Both sides of \eqref{eq:equivE} and of \eqref{eq:equivQ} are invariant under
\eqref{eq:scaling}, as they should be. The exponent $\sigma$ equals $1$ only when
$s_c=\frac12$, which in the three-dimensional cubic case means $b=0$. The
threshold conditions in \cite[Theorem~1.1]{HLW2024} are written with the exponent
$1$, as in \eqref{eq:HR}, also when $b>0$. In that literal form, such conditions
cannot be sufficient for scattering. Indeed, if $Q$ solves \eqref{eq:ellipticQ},
then $Q_\omega(x):=\omega^{\frac{2-b}{2\alpha}}Q(\sqrt\omega\,x)$ solves
\eqref{eq:stationary}, and one checks that
$\mathcal{M}(Q_\omega)E_a(Q_\omega)=\omega^{1-2s_c}\mathcal{M}(Q)E_a(Q)$ and
$\|Q_\omega\|_{L^2}\|Q_\omega\|_{\dot H^1_a}=\omega^{\frac{1-2s_c}{2}}\|Q\|_{L^2}\|Q\|_{\dot H^1_a}$.
When $s_c>\frac12$ and $\omega>1$, both products are strictly below those of
$Q$, yet $e^{i\omega t}Q_\omega$ does not scatter. The conditions must therefore
be read in the scale-invariant form $\mathcal{M}^{1-s_c}E_a^{s_c}$, as in
\cite{FarahGuzman2017,MiaoMurphyZheng2021,CamposGuzman2021}. This is how we use
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{HLW2024}; see Proposition \ref{prop:scatcrit} and the
discussion following it. Theorem \ref{thm:dichotomy} is free of this ambiguity,
because $m_a(c)$ has the correct homogeneity by construction.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:instability}
Let $N\ge3$, $a>a_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $0<b<2$, assume \eqref{eq:range} and Assumption
\ref{ass:cauchy}, and let $u_c$ be a minimizer of $m_a(c)$. Then
$u_c\in\Sigma$, and the standing wave $e^{i\omega_ct}u_c$ is strongly unstable.
Moreover the destabilizing data can be taken on the same mass sphere: for every
$\varepsilon>0$ there is $v_0\in S(c)\cap\Sigma$ with $\|v_0-u_c\|_{H^1_a}<\varepsilon$,
$E_a(v_0)<m_a(c)$ and $\mathcal{Q}_a(v_0)<0$, whose solution blows up in finite time.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:monotone}
Let $N\ge3$, $0<b<2$ and $0<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{N-2}$. Then the map $a\mapsto\mathcal{C}_a$
is strictly decreasing on $\big(-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4},\infty\big)$. If moreover

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:sigma}
Let $N\ge3$, $0<b<2$ and $\frac{4-2b}{N}<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{N-2}$, and let
$s_c,\beta,\sigma_c,\gamma$ be as in \eqref{eq:sc} and \eqref{eq:betasig}. Then
$\beta>2$, $\sigma_c>0$, $\beta+\sigma_c=\alpha+2$, $0<s_c<1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmaid}
\frac\gamma2=\frac{\sigma_c}{\beta-2}=\frac{1-s_c}{s_c},
\qquad
\gamma+2=\frac{2\alpha}{\beta-2}=\frac{4\alpha}{N\alpha+2b-4}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The inequality $\alpha>\frac{4-2b}{N}$ is equivalent to $N\alpha+2b>4$, that is,
to $\beta>2$; the inequality $\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{N-2}$ is equivalent to
$(N-2)\alpha<4-2b$, that is, to $\sigma_c>0$. The same two inequalities say that
$s_c=\frac N2-\frac{2-b}{\alpha}$ lies in $(0,1)$, because $s_c>0$ reads
$N\alpha>2(2-b)$ and $s_c<1$ reads $(N-2)\alpha<2(2-b)$. Adding the definitions of
$\beta$ and $\sigma_c$ gives
$\beta+\sigma_c=\frac{N\alpha+2b+4-2b-(N-2)\alpha}{2}=\alpha+2$. For the first
identity in \eqref{eq:sigmaid} we multiply numerator and denominator of
$\frac{1-s_c}{s_c}$ by $2\alpha$ and obtain
\[
\frac{1-s_c}{s_c}=\frac{2\alpha-N\alpha+2(2-b)}{N\alpha-2(2-b)}
=\frac{4-2b-(N-2)\alpha}{N\alpha+2b-4}=\frac{\sigma_c}{\beta-2},
\]
which equals $\frac\gamma2$ by \eqref{eq:betasig}. Finally
$\gamma+2=\frac{2\sigma_c+2(\beta-2)}{\beta-2}=\frac{2(\alpha+2)-4}{\beta-2}
=\frac{2\alpha}{\beta-2}$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:weighted}
Let $N\ge3$, $0<b<2$ and $0<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{N-2}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] There is $C>0$ such that $B(u)\le C\|u\|_{H^1}^{\alpha+2}$ and
\[
\big|B(u)-B(v)\big|\le C\big(\|u\|_{H^1}+\|v\|_{H^1}\big)^{\alpha+1}\|u-v\|_{H^1}
\]
for all $u,v\in H^1(\mathbb{R}^N)$. Moreover, $B$ is of class $C^1$ on $H^1(\mathbb{R}^N)$, and
\[
B'(u)h=(\alpha+2)\operatorname{Re}\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u\,\bar h\,dx,
\qquad u,h\in H^1(\mathbb{R}^N).
\]
\item[(b)] If $u_n\rightharpoonup u$ weakly in $H^1(\mathbb{R}^N)$, then $B(u_n)\to B(u)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $\alpha(N-2)<4-2b$, we have $(\alpha+2)\frac{N}{N-b}<\frac{2N}{N-2}$. Indeed,
this inequality is equivalent to $(\alpha+2)(N-2)<2(N-b)$, that is, to
$\alpha(N-2)<4-2b$. We may therefore fix $r\in(1,\frac Nb)$ so close to $\frac Nb$
that $p:=(\alpha+2)r'<\frac{2N}{N-2}$, where $r'$ is the conjugate exponent. Note
that $p>2$. Then $|x|^{-b}\in L^{r}(B_1)$, and H\"older's inequality followed by
the Sobolev embedding gives
\[
\int_{|x|<1}\frac{|w|^{\alpha+2}}{|x|^{b}}\,dx\le\big\||x|^{-b}\big\|_{L^{r}(B_1)}
\|w\|_{L^{p}(B_1)}^{\alpha+2}\lesssim\|w\|_{H^1}^{\alpha+2},
\qquad
\int_{|x|\ge1}\frac{|w|^{\alpha+2}}{|x|^{b}}\,dx\le\|w\|_{\alpha+2}^{\alpha+2}
\lesssim\|w\|_{H^1}^{\alpha+2},
\]
where we used $2<\alpha+2<\frac{2N}{N-2}$ in the second estimate. Thus
$\|w\|_{L^{\alpha+2}(|x|^{-b}dx)}\lesssim\|w\|_{H^1}$, which is the first claim in
(a). For the second, we use
$\big||u|^{\alpha+2}-|v|^{\alpha+2}\big|\le(\alpha+2)(|u|^{\alpha+1}+|v|^{\alpha+1})|u-v|$
and H\"older's inequality with exponents $\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+1}$ and $\alpha+2$
for the measure $|x|^{-b}dx$. We obtain
$|B(u)-B(v)|\le(\alpha+2)\big(B(u)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}+B(v)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha+2}}\big)
\|u-v\|_{L^{\alpha+2}(|x|^{-b}dx)}$, and the claim follows from the first bound.
The differentiability of $B$ is a routine consequence of the same estimates
applied to the map $z\mapsto|z|^{\alpha+2}$, whose derivative is locally
H\"older continuous; see \cite[Chapter~3]{Cazenave2003} or \cite{Farah2016}.

For (b), let $u_n\rightharpoonup u$ in $H^1$. Then
$M_0:=\sup_n\|u_n\|_{H^1}+\|u\|_{H^1}<\infty$. Put $w_n:=u_n-u$. For $R\ge1$,
applying the argument above on $B_R$ and on its complement, we get
\[
\int\frac{|w_n|^{\alpha+2}}{|x|^{b}}\,dx
\le\big\||x|^{-b}\big\|_{L^{r}(B_R)}\|w_n\|_{L^{p}(B_R)}^{\alpha+2}
+R^{-b}\|w_n\|_{\alpha+2}^{\alpha+2}
\le C_R\|w_n\|_{L^{p}(B_R)}^{\alpha+2}+CR^{-b}M_0^{\alpha+2}.
\]
Given $\varepsilon>0$ we choose $R$ with $CR^{-b}M_0^{\alpha+2}<\varepsilon$. Since
$p<\frac{2N}{N-2}$, the Rellich--Kondrachov theorem gives $w_n\to0$ in
$L^{p}(B_R)$, so the first term tends to zero. Hence
$\|w_n\|_{L^{\alpha+2}(|x|^{-b}dx)}\to0$, and the difference estimate used in (a)
yields $B(u_n)\to B(u)$.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:GN}
Let $N\ge 3$, $a>-\frac{(N-2)^2}{4}$, $0<b<2$ and $0<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{N-2}$,
and let $\beta$ and $\sigma_c$ be defined as in \eqref{eq:betasig}. Define the
sharp Gagliardo--Nirenberg constant by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GNquotient}
\mathcal{C}_a:=\sup_{u\inH^1_a(\mathbb{R}^N)\setminus\{0\}}J(u),
\qquad
J(u):=\frac{B(u)}
{A(u)^{\beta/2}\|u\|_{2}^{\sigma_c}},
\end{equation}
where $A(u)$ and $B(u)$ are given by \eqref{eq:ABm}. Then $0<\mathcal{C}_a<\infty$, and the
supremum in \eqref{eq:GNquotient} is attained. Consequently, the sharp
Gagliardo--Nirenberg inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GN}
B(u)\le \mathcal{C}_a\,A(u)^{\beta/2}\|u\|_{2}^{\sigma_c},
\qquad u\inH^1_a(\mathbb{R}^N),
\end{equation}
holds, and $\mathcal{C}_a$ is the optimal constant.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Note first that $\beta+\sigma_c=\alpha+2$, $\beta>0$ and $\sigma_c>0$ for every
$\alpha$ in the stated range. By Lemma \ref{lem:weighted}(a),
$B(w)\le C\big(\|\nabla w\|_2^{2}+\|w\|_2^{2}\big)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}$ for all
$w\in H^1$. Given $u\ne0$, we apply this to $w=u^{t}$ and use
\eqref{eq:scalingAB} together with $\|\nabla u^t\|_2^2=t^2\|\nabla u\|_2^2$. We get
\[
t^{\beta}B(u)\le C\big(t^{2}\|\nabla u\|_2^{2}+\|u\|_2^{2}\big)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}.
\]
The choice $t^{2}=\|u\|_2^{2}/\|\nabla u\|_2^{2}$ gives
$B(u)\le 2^{\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}C\,\|\nabla u\|_2^{\beta}\|u\|_2^{\sigma_c}$, and
\eqref{eq:equivnorm} turns this into \eqref{eq:GN} with
$\mathcal{C}_a\le2^{\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}C\lambda_a^{-\beta/2}<\infty$. Positivity is obvious.

For the existence of an optimizer, we use the two-parameter invariance of $J$. For
$\theta>0$ and $t>0$, we have $J(\theta u^{t})=J(u)$, because the numerator and the
denominator of $J$ are both homogeneous of degree $\alpha+2$ in $u$, and because
$t^{\beta}B/(t^{2}A)^{\beta/2}=B/A^{\beta/2}$ by \eqref{eq:scalingAB}. Let $(u_n)$
be a maximizing sequence. Replacing $u_n$ by $\theta_nu_n^{t_n}$ with
$\theta_n=\|u_n\|_2^{-1}$ and $t_n=\big(\mathcal{M}(u_n)/A(u_n)\big)^{1/2}$, we may
assume $\|u_n\|_2=1$ and $A(u_n)=1$. Then $(u_n)$ is bounded in $H^1$, and after
extraction $u_n\rightharpoonup u$ weakly in $H^1$. By Lemma
\ref{lem:weighted}(b), we have $B(u)=\lim B(u_n)=\lim J(u_n)=\mathcal{C}_a>0$, so $u\ne0$. The
functional $A$ is a continuous nonnegative quadratic form on $H^1$, hence convex
and weakly lower semicontinuous, so $A(u)\le1$; likewise $\|u\|_2\le1$.
Consequently $J(u)\ge B(u)=\mathcal{C}_a$, and $u$ is an optimizer.

<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:cauchy}
For every $u_0\inH^1_a(\mathbb{R}^{N})$, there are $T_{\pm}=T_{\pm}(u_0)\in(0,\infty]$ and a
unique maximal solution
\[
u\in C\big((-T_-,T_+);H^1_a(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)\cap C^1\big((-T_-,T_+);H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)
\]
of \eqref{eq:main}. This solution conserves the mass and the energy
\eqref{eq:conserved} and depends continuously on the data. Moreover, the blow-up
alternative holds: if $T_{+}<\infty$, then
$\lim_{t\uparrow T_{+}}\|u(t)\|_{\dot H^1_a}=\infty$, and similarly backwards in time.

map $t\mapsto\||x|u(t)\|_{2}$ is continuous, and the virial identity
\eqref{eq:virial1} below holds.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:variance}
Let $N\ge3$, $a>a_{\mathrm{H}}$, $0<b<2$ and $0<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{N-2}$, let $I\ni0$ be an
interval, and let $u\in C(I;H^1_a(\mathbb{R}^N))$ solve \eqref{eq:main} in
$H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^N)$ with $u(0)=u_0\in\Sigma$. Then $u\in C^1(I;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^N))$,
$|x|u\in C(I;L^2(\mathbb{R}^N))$, and $V(t):=\int|x|^2|u(t,x)|^2\,dx$ is of class $C^1$ on
$I$ with $V'(t)=4\operatorname{Im}\int\bar u\,x\cdot\nabla u\,dx$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The operator $\mathcal{L}_a$ is bounded from $H^1_a$ to $H^{-1}$. By Lemma
\ref{lem:weighted}(a), the map
$u\mapsto|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u=\frac{1}{\alpha+2}B'(u)$ is continuous from $H^1$
to $H^{-1}$. The equation therefore gives
$\partial_tu=-i\big(\mathcal{L}_a u-|x|^{-b}|u|^\alpha u\big)\in C(I;H^{-1})$, so that
$u\in C^1(I;H^{-1})$.

For $\varepsilon>0$, put $\psi_\varepsilon(x):=|x|^2e^{-\varepsilon|x|^2}$. This is a smooth bounded
function with bounded gradient. Since
$\nabla\psi_\varepsilon=2x(1-\varepsilon|x|^2)e^{-\varepsilon|x|^2}$ and
$\sup_{s\ge0}|1-s|e^{-s/2}=1$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psieps}
|\nabla\psi_\varepsilon|\le2\psi_\varepsilon^{1/2},
\end{equation}
uniformly in $\varepsilon$. Multiplication by $\psi_\varepsilon$ is bounded on
$H^1$. Let $f_\varepsilon(t):=\int\psi_\varepsilon|u(t)|^2\,dx$. A computation of difference
quotients shows that $f_\varepsilon$ is of class $C^1$ on $I$, with
\[
f_\varepsilon'(t)=2\operatorname{Re}\langle\partial_tu,\psi_\varepsilon u\rangle_{H^{-1},H^1}.
\]
We have
\[
\langle\mathcal{L}_a u,\psi_\varepsilon u\rangle=\int\psi_\varepsilon|\nabla u|^2\,dx
+a\int\psi_\varepsilon\frac{|u|^2}{|x|^{2}}\,dx+\int\bar u\,\nabla\psi_\varepsilon\cdot\nabla u\,dx ,
\]
and $\langle|x|^{-b}|u|^\alpha u,\psi_\varepsilon u\rangle=\int\psi_\varepsilon|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha+2}\,dx$
is real. Hence $f_\varepsilon'(t)=2\operatorname{Im}\int\bar u\,\nabla\psi_\varepsilon\cdot\nabla u\,dx$, and
by \eqref{eq:psieps} and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, we get
$|f_\varepsilon'(t)|\le4f_\varepsilon(t)^{1/2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_2$. For $\delta>0$, this gives
$\big|\frac{d}{dt}(f_\varepsilon+\delta)^{1/2}\big|\le2\|\nabla u(t)\|_2$. Integrating and
letting $\delta\downarrow0$, we obtain
\[
f_\varepsilon(t)^{1/2}\le\||x|u_0\|_2+2\Big|\int_0^t\|\nabla u(s)\|_2\,ds\Big| .
\]
Since $\psi_\varepsilon\uparrow|x|^2$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the monotone convergence
theorem gives $u(t)\in\Sigma$, with a bound on $\||x|u(t)\|_2$ that is locally
uniform in $t$.

Next, we have
$f_\varepsilon(t)=f_\varepsilon(0)+2\int_0^t\operatorname{Im}\int\bar u\,\nabla\psi_\varepsilon\cdot\nabla u\,dx\,ds$.
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, we have $\nabla\psi_\varepsilon\to2x$ pointwise and
$|\nabla\psi_\varepsilon|\le2|x|$. Moreover, $|x||u||\nabla u|$ is integrable on
$[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^N$ by the bound just proved. The dominated convergence theorem
gives
\[
V(t)=V(0)+4\int_0^t\operatorname{Im}\int\bar u\,x\cdot\nabla u\,dx\,ds,
\]
so $V$ is continuous. For $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^N)$, the map
$t\mapsto\int|x|u(t)\bar\varphi\,dx$ is continuous, and $\||x|u(t)\|_2$ is locally
bounded. Hence $|x|u$ is weakly continuous with values in $L^2$. Together with the
continuity of $V(t)=\||x|u(t)\|_2^2$, this gives $|x|u\in C(I;L^2)$. The map
$s\mapsto\operatorname{Im}\int\bar u\,x\cdot\nabla u\,dx$ is then continuous, and we infer that
$V\in C^1(I)$ with the stated derivative.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:virial}
Let $N\ge3$, $0<b<2$, $a>a_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $0<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{N-2}$. Let $u_0\in\Sigma$,
and let $u$ be the maximal solution of \eqref{eq:main} given by the first part of
Assumption \ref{ass:cauchy}. Then $V(t):=\int|x|^{2}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx$ is of class
$C^{2}$ on $(-T_-,T_+)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virial1}
V'(t)=4\operatorname{Im}\int\bar u\,x\cdot\nabla u\,dx,
\qquad
V''(t)=8\,\mathcal{Q}_a\big(u(t)\big).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The first identity in \eqref{eq:virial1} is Lemma \ref{lem:variance}. The second
one is stated for \eqref{eq:main}, with a general weight, in
\cite[Lemma~3.4]{CamposGuzman2021}. We give the computation for the weight
$|x|^2$, because the exact coefficients matter here (see Remark
\ref{rmk:virialtypo}). We write \eqref{eq:main} as $i\partial_tu+\Delta u=G$ with
$G:=\frac{a}{|x|^{2}}u-|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u$, and we follow the classical
computation \cite[Chapter~6]{Cazenave2003}. Since $\bar uG$ is real, we have
$\partial_t|u|^2=-2\,\mathrm{div}\,\operatorname{Im}(\bar u\nabla u)$. For a smooth real weight
$w$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virialgeneral}
\frac{d}{dt}\Big(2\operatorname{Im}\int\bar u\,\nabla w\cdot\nabla u\,dx\Big)
=\int\Big(-\Delta\Delta w\,|u|^{2}+4\operatorname{Re}(\partial_j\bar u\,\partial_k u)\,\partial_{jk}w
+2\nabla w\cdot\{G,u\}_P\Big)\,dx,
\end{equation}
with $\{f,g\}_P:=\operatorname{Re}(f\nabla\bar g-g\nabla\bar f)$ and summation over repeated
indices. For $w=|x|^{2}$, we have $\Delta\Delta w=0$, $\partial_{jk}w=2\delta_{jk}$
and $\nabla w=2x$, so the first two terms give $8\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}$. For the
potential, we have $\{\frac{a}{|x|^{2}}u,u\}_P=-a|u|^{2}\nabla(|x|^{-2})=2a|u|^{2}\frac{x}{|x|^{4}}$,
hence $2\nabla w\cdot\{\frac{a}{|x|^{2}}u,u\}_P=8a\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}$. Thus the
inverse-square potential contributes with the same coefficient as the kinetic
term, which is the analytic expression of its scale invariance. For the
nonlinear part, we write $W:=|x|^{-b}$ and $h:=W|u|^{\alpha+2}$. Then
$\{-W|u|^{\alpha}u,u\}_P=|u|^{2}\nabla\big(W|u|^{\alpha}\big)$. Separating the
derivative that falls on $W$ from the one that falls on $|u|$, we find
\[
2\nabla w\cdot\big\{-W|u|^{\alpha}u,\,u\big\}_P
=\frac{4\alpha}{\alpha+2}\,x\cdot\nabla h
+\frac{8}{\alpha+2}\,|u|^{\alpha+2}\,x\cdot\nabla W .
\]
Since $x\cdot\nabla W=-bW$ and $\int x\cdot\nabla h=-N\int h$, the integral of the
last expression equals
\[
-\Big(\frac{4N\alpha}{\alpha+2}+\frac{8b}{\alpha+2}\Big)\int h\,dx
=-\frac{4(N\alpha+2b)}{\alpha+2}\int\frac{|u|^{\alpha+2}}{|x|^{b}}\,dx .
\]
Collecting the three contributions, we obtain
\[
V''(t)=8\|u(t)\|_{\dot H^1_a}^{2}-\frac{4(N\alpha+2b)}{\alpha+2}\int\frac{|u(t)|^{\alpha+2}}{|x|^{b}}\,dx
=8\mathcal{Q}_a(u(t))
\]
by \eqref{eq:Qdef}. This computation is first performed for regular solutions.
It extends to the solutions considered here by the regularization argument of
\cite[Section~6.5]{Cazenave2003}, combined with the continuous dependence
on the data and with Lemma \ref{lem:variance}; see also
\cite[Lemma~3.4]{CamposGuzman2021} and \cite{Dinh2021}. The singular terms cause no
difficulty, since $|x|^{-2}|u|^2$ and $|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha+2}$ are integrable by
\eqref{eq:hardy} and Lemma \ref{lem:weighted}.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:fibering}
Let $u\inH^1_a(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}$ and set $h_u(t):=E_a(u^{t})$ for $t>0$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hu}
h_u(t)=\frac{t^{2}}{2}A(u)-\frac{t^{\beta}}{\alpha+2}B(u),
\qquad
t\,h_u'(t)=\mathcal{Q}_a(u^{t})=t^{2}A(u)-\frac{\beta t^{\beta}}{\alpha+2}B(u),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:master}
E_a(u^{t})=\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}\,t^{2}A(u)+\frac1\beta\,\mathcal{Q}_a(u^{t}),
\qquad t>0.
\end{equation}
The map $h_u$ has exactly one critical point,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tu}
t_u=\Big(\frac{(\alpha+2)A(u)}{\beta B(u)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\beta-2}}\in(0,\infty),
\end{equation}
which is a strict global maximum. The map $h_u$ increases strictly on $(0,t_u)$
and decreases strictly on $(t_u,\infty)$, with $h_u(t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$ and
$h_u(t)\to-\infty$ as $t\to\infty$. Equivalently, $\mathcal{Q}_a(u^{t})>0$ for $t<t_u$,
$\mathcal{Q}_a(u^{t_u})=0$ and $\mathcal{Q}_a(u^{t})<0$ for $t>t_u$. In particular $t_u$ is the only
$t>0$ with $u^{t}\in V(\|u\|_{2})$, and $t_u<1$ if and only if $\mathcal{Q}_a(u)<0$, while
$t_u>1$ if and only if $\mathcal{Q}_a(u)>0$. Finally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maxvalue}
\max_{t>0}E_a(u^{t})=h_u(t_u)
=K_{\alpha,N,b}\,\frac{A(u)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-2}}}{B(u)^{\frac{2}{\beta-2}}}
=K_{\alpha,N,b}\,J(u)^{-\frac{2}{\beta-2}}\,\|u\|_{2}^{-\gamma},
\end{equation}
with $J$ as in \eqref{eq:GNquotient} and $K_{\alpha,N,b}$ as in \eqref{eq:K}.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The formula for $h_u$ follows from \eqref{eq:EQAB} and \eqref{eq:scalingAB}.
Differentiating it, we get $t h_u'(t)=t^{2}A-\frac{\beta t^{\beta}}{\alpha+2}B$,
which is $\mathcal{Q}_a(u^{t})$ by the same two relations. Identity \eqref{eq:master} is a
matter of elimination. Subtracting $\frac1\beta\mathcal{Q}_a(u^{t})$ from $E_a(u^{t})$
removes the term containing $B(u)$ and leaves
$\frac{t^{2}}{2}A-\frac{t^{2}}{\beta}A=\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}t^{2}A$.

Since $A(u)>0$ and $B(u)>0$ for $u\ne0$, and $\beta>2$ by Lemma \ref{lem:sigma},
the equation $h_u'(t)=0$ reads $t^{\beta-2}=\frac{(\alpha+2)A}{\beta B}$ and has
the single positive root \eqref{eq:tu}. The sign of $h_u'(t)$ is the sign of
$\frac{(\alpha+2)A}{\beta B}-t^{\beta-2}$, which is positive for $t<t_u$ and
negative for $t>t_u$. This gives the monotonicity, the strict maximality of $t_u$ and the
statements about the sign of $\mathcal{Q}_a(u^{t})$. The behaviour at $0$ and at infinity
is read off \eqref{eq:hu}, again because $\beta>2$. Taking $t=1$ in the sign
analysis, we obtain the last equivalences.

It remains to compute the maximum. By \eqref{eq:master} and $\mathcal{Q}_a(u^{t_u})=0$, we
have $h_u(t_u)=\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}t_u^{2}A$. Inserting \eqref{eq:tu}, we obtain
\[
h_u(t_u)=\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}\Big(\frac{\alpha+2}{\beta}\Big)^{\frac{2}{\beta-2}}
A^{1+\frac{2}{\beta-2}}B^{-\frac{2}{\beta-2}}
=K_{\alpha,N,b}\,\frac{A^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-2}}}{B^{\frac{2}{\beta-2}}}.
\]
We write $B=J(u)A^{\beta/2}\|u\|_{2}^{\sigma_c}$. Then the powers of $A$ cancel:
\[
\frac{A^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-2}}}{B^{\frac{2}{\beta-2}}}
=\frac{A^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-2}}}
{J(u)^{\frac{2}{\beta-2}}A^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-2}}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2\sigma_c}{\beta-2}}}
=J(u)^{-\frac{2}{\beta-2}}\|u\|_{2}^{-\gamma},
\]
by the definition \eqref{eq:betasig} of $\gamma$. This is \eqref{eq:maxvalue}.

\label{lem:minimax}
For every $c>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimax}
m_a(c)=\inf_{u\in S(c)}\ \max_{t>0}\ E_a(u^{t}).
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
If $u\in V(c)$, then $t_u=1$ by Lemma \ref{lem:fibering}, so
$E_a(u)=\max_{t>0}E_a(u^{t})$, which is at least the right-hand side of

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
By Lemma \ref{lem:minimax}, formula \eqref{eq:maxvalue} and Lemma
\ref{lem:GNrestrict}, we get
\[
m_a(c)=\inf_{u\in S(c)}K_{\alpha,N,b}J(u)^{-\frac{2}{\beta-2}}c^{-\gamma}
=K_{\alpha,N,b}\,c^{-\gamma}\Big(\sup_{u\in S(c)}J(u)\Big)^{-\frac{2}{\beta-2}}

\]
where we used that $s\mapsto s^{-\frac{2}{\beta-2}}$ is continuous and decreasing

the stated form.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:EL}
Let $W$ be an optimizer of \eqref{eq:GN}. Then $W$ is a weak solution of
\[
\mathcal{L}_a W+\frac{\sigma_c A(W)}{\beta\,\mathcal{M}(W)}\,W
=\frac{(\alpha+2)A(W)}{\beta\,B(W)}\,|x|^{-b}|W|^{\alpha}W .
\]
In particular, if in addition $\mathcal{Q}_a(W)=0$, then $W$ solves \eqref{eq:stationary}
with $\omega=\frac{\sigma_cA(W)}{\beta\mathcal{M}(W)}>0$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
The functionals $A$ and $\mathcal{M}$ are smooth on $H^1$, and $B$ is of class $C^1$ by
Lemma \ref{lem:weighted}(a). All three are positive at $W\ne0$. Hence $\log J$ is
differentiable at $W$ in the real sense. Since $W$ maximizes $J$ on the open set
$H^1\setminus\{0\}$, this derivative vanishes at $W$. Thus, for every $h\in H^1$,
we have
\[
\frac{B'(W)h}{B(W)}-\frac{\beta}{2}\,\frac{A'(W)h}{A(W)}-\frac{\sigma_c}{2}\,
\frac{\mathcal{M}'(W)h}{\mathcal{M}(W)}=0 .
\]
With $A'(W)h=2\operatorname{Re}\langle W,h\rangle_a$, $\mathcal{M}'(W)h=2\operatorname{Re}\int W\bar h$ and
$B'(W)h=(\alpha+2)\operatorname{Re}\int|x|^{-b}|W|^{\alpha}W\bar h$, multiplying by
$\frac{A(W)}{\beta}$, we get
\[
\operatorname{Re}\Big(\langle W,h\rangle_a+\frac{\sigma_cA(W)}{\beta\mathcal{M}(W)}\int W\bar h\Big)
=\frac{(\alpha+2)A(W)}{\beta B(W)}\operatorname{Re}\int|x|^{-b}|W|^{\alpha}W\bar h .
\]
Replacing $h$ by $ih$, we obtain the same identity for the imaginary parts. This
is the weak form of the equation. If $\mathcal{Q}_a(W)=0$, then
$A(W)=\frac{\beta}{\alpha+2}B(W)$ by \eqref{eq:EQAB}, and the coefficient on the
right-hand side equals $1$.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:leastaction}
Let $\omega>0$ and let $v\inH^1_a(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}$ satisfy \eqref{eq:nehari}.
This is the case, in particular, for every nontrivial solution of
\eqref{eq:stationary}. Then $S_\omega(v)=\Theta_{\alpha}B(v)$ with
$\Theta_{\alpha}:=\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha+2)}>0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bbound}
B(v)\ \ge\ B_\omega:=
\Big[\mathcal{C}_a\Big(\frac{\beta}{\alpha+2}\Big)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}
\Big(\frac{\sigma_c}{(\alpha+2)\omega}\Big)^{\frac{\sigma_c}{2}}\Big]^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}}.
\end{equation}
Equality holds in \eqref{eq:Bbound} if and only if $v$ is an optimizer of
\eqref{eq:GN} with $\mathcal{Q}_a(v)=0$, and in that case
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ABmsol}
A(v)=\frac{\beta}{\alpha+2}B(v),\qquad
\mathcal{M}(v)=\frac{\sigma_c}{(\alpha+2)\,\omega}B(v),\qquad
S_\omega(v)=\Theta_{\alpha}\,B(v).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
By \eqref{eq:nehari}, we have
$S_\omega(v)=\frac12\big(A(v)+\omega\mathcal{M}(v)\big)-\frac{B(v)}{\alpha+2}
=\big(\frac12-\frac1{\alpha+2}\big)B(v)=\Theta_\alpha B(v)$. Put
$x:=A(v)/B(v)$ and $y:=\omega\mathcal{M}(v)/B(v)$, so that $x,y>0$ and $x+y=1$. Then
$A(v)^{\beta/2}\mathcal{M}(v)^{\sigma_c/2}=\omega^{-\sigma_c/2}x^{\beta/2}y^{\sigma_c/2}
B(v)^{\frac{\beta+\sigma_c}{2}}$, and $\beta+\sigma_c=\alpha+2$. Inserting this in
\eqref{eq:GN} and dividing by $B(v)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}$, we get
\[
B(v)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\le\mathcal{C}_a\,\omega^{-\frac{\sigma_c}{2}}\,x^{\frac\beta2}y^{\frac{\sigma_c}{2}}.
\]
The function $x\mapsto\frac\beta2\log x+\frac{\sigma_c}{2}\log(1-x)$ is strictly
concave on $(0,1)$ and attains its maximum only at $x=\frac{\beta}{\beta+\sigma_c}
=\frac{\beta}{\alpha+2}$. Therefore
$x^{\beta/2}y^{\sigma_c/2}\le\big(\frac{\beta}{\alpha+2}\big)^{\beta/2}
\big(\frac{\sigma_c}{\alpha+2}\big)^{\sigma_c/2}$, and raising the resulting
inequality to the power $-\frac2\alpha$, which reverses it, we obtain
\eqref{eq:Bbound}. Equality holds exactly when \eqref{eq:GN} is an equality for $v$
and $x=\frac{\beta}{\alpha+2}$; the latter means $A(v)=\frac{\beta}{\alpha+2}B(v)$,
that is, $\mathcal{Q}_a(v)=0$. In that case $\omega\mathcal{M}(v)=B(v)-A(v)=\frac{\sigma_c}{\alpha+2}B(v)$,
which completes \eqref{eq:ABmsol}.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:coercivity}
Let $N\ge3$, $a>a_{\mathrm{H}}$, $0<b<2$, assume \eqref{eq:range}, and let $c>0$ and
$u\in S(c)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If $\mathcal{Q}_a(u)<0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coerneg}
\mathcal{Q}_a(u)<\beta\big(E_a(u)-m_a(c)\big).
\end{equation}
\item[(ii)] If $\mathcal{Q}_a(u)>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coerpos}
0<\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}\,\|u\|_{\dot H^1_a}^{2}<E_a(u).
\end{equation}
\item[(iii)] If $E_a(u)<m_a(c)$, then $\mathcal{Q}_a(u)\ne0$.
\item[(iv)] If $\mathcal{Q}_a(u)>0$ and $E_a(u)\le(1-\eta)m_a(c)$ for some $\eta\in(0,1)$,
then, with $A_*(c)$ as in \eqref{eq:Astar},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unifcoer}
\|u\|_{\dot H^1_a}^{2}<(1-\eta)A_*(c)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathcal{Q}_a(u)\ge\Big(1-(1-\eta)^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}\Big)\|u\|_{\dot H^1_a}^{2}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Assertion (iii) is immediate: $\mathcal{Q}_a(u)=0$ places $u$ in $V(c)$, and we get
$E_a(u)\ge m_a(c)$ by \eqref{eq:mc}. Assertion (ii) is \eqref{eq:master} at $t=1$,
namely $E_a(u)=\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}A(u)+\frac1\beta\mathcal{Q}_a(u)$, together with
$\mathcal{Q}_a(u)>0$ and $A(u)>0$.

For (i), let $t_u$ be the maximum point of the fibering map given by Lemma
\ref{lem:fibering}. Since $\mathcal{Q}_a(u)<0$, we have $t_u<1$. Since $u^{t_u}\in V(c)$,
we have $E_a(u^{t_u})\ge m_a(c)$. On the other hand, \eqref{eq:master} at $t=t_u$
gives $E_a(u^{t_u})=\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}t_u^{2}A(u)$. Combining the two and using
$t_u<1$ and $m_a(c)>0$, we obtain
\[
\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}A(u)=\frac{E_a(u^{t_u})}{t_u^{2}}\ \ge\ \frac{m_a(c)}{t_u^{2}}\ >\ m_a(c).
\]
Writing \eqref{eq:master} at $t=1$ as
$\mathcal{Q}_a(u)=\beta\big(E_a(u)-\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}A(u)\big)$ we obtain
$\mathcal{Q}_a(u)<\beta\big(E_a(u)-m_a(c)\big)$.

For (iv), assertion (ii) gives $\frac{\beta-2}{2\beta}A(u)<E_a(u)\le(1-\eta)m_a(c)$.
This is the first inequality in \eqref{eq:unifcoer}, by the definition of
$A_*(c)$. For the second one, we use \eqref{eq:GN} and the second expression in
\eqref{eq:Astar}, which says that
$\frac{\beta\mathcal{C}_a}{\alpha+2}c^{\sigma_c}=A_*(c)^{-\frac{\beta-2}{2}}$. We get
\[
\mathcal{Q}_a(u)=A(u)-\frac{\beta}{\alpha+2}B(u)\ge A(u)-\frac{\beta\mathcal{C}_a}{\alpha+2}c^{\sigma_c}A(u)^{\frac\beta2}
=A(u)\Big(1-\Big(\frac{A(u)}{A_*(c)}\Big)^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}\Big),
\]
and the bracket is at least $1-(1-\eta)^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}$.

\label{lem:invariance}
Let $N\ge3$, $a>a_{\mathrm{H}}$, $0<b<2$, assume \eqref{eq:range} and the first part of
Assumption \ref{ass:cauchy}. Let
$u_0\inH^1_a\setminus\{0\}$ satisfy \eqref{eq:below}, and let $u$ be the maximal
solution. Then $\mathcal{Q}_a(u(t))\ne0$ for every $t\in(-T_-,T_+)$, and the sign of
$\mathcal{Q}_a(u(t))$ is constant. If $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)>0$, the solution is global,
$0<E_a(u_0)<m_a(c)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unifbound}
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|u(t)\|_{\dot H^1_a}^{2}\le\frac{2\beta}{\beta-2}E_a(u_0)<A_*(c),
\qquad
\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathcal{Q}_a(u(t))}{\|u(t)\|_{\dot H^1_a}^{2}}\ge1-\Big(\frac{E_a(u_0)}{m_a(c)}\Big)^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}>0 .
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)<0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unifneg}
\mathcal{Q}_a(u(t))<\beta\big(E_a(u_0)-m_a(c)\big)<0
\qquad\text{for all }t\in(-T_-,T_+).
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
By conservation of mass and energy, we have $u(t)\in S(c)$ and
$E_a(u(t))=E_a(u_0)<m_a(c)$ for every $t$ in the maximal interval. Lemma
\ref{lem:coercivity}(iii) gives $\mathcal{Q}_a(u(t))\ne0$. The map $t\mapsto\mathcal{Q}_a(u(t))$ is
continuous, because $u\in C((-T_-,T_+);H^1_a)$ and $A$ and $B$ are continuous on

\eqref{eq:coerneg} to $u(t)\in S(c)$, we get \eqref{eq:unifneg}.

<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
By Assumption \ref{ass:cauchy}, Lemma \ref{lem:invariance} applies. Hence
$\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)\ne0$, and the sign of $\mathcal{Q}_a(u(t))$ is conserved. If $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)>0$, the
solution is global by Lemma \ref{lem:invariance}.

\emph{The scattering regime.} Assume $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)>0$ and \eqref{eq:dynrange}. By
Theorem \ref{thm:equiv}, hypothesis \eqref{eq:below} and $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)>0$ are exactly
the two conditions of Proposition \ref{prop:scatcrit}. Hence the solution
scatters.

\emph{The blow-up regime.} Assume $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)<0$ and $u_0\in\Sigma$. By Assumption
\ref{ass:cauchy} the solution stays in $\Sigma$, the variance
$V(t)=\int|x|^{2}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx$ is of class $C^{2}$, and $V''(t)=8\mathcal{Q}_a(u(t))$ by
Lemma \ref{lem:virial}. Inserting \eqref{eq:unifneg}, we get \eqref{eq:blowup}:
\[
V''(t)\le8\beta\big(E_a(u_0)-m_a(c)\big)=:-2\delta<0,
\qquad t\in(-T_-,T_+).
\]
Integrating twice, we obtain $V(t)\le V(0)+V'(0)\,t-\delta t^{2}$ on the maximal
interval.
The right-hand side becomes negative for $|t|$ large, while $V(t)\ge0$. Hence
$T_+<\infty$ and $T_-<\infty$, and by the blow-up alternative
$\|u(t)\|_{\dot H^1_a}\to\infty$ as $t\uparrow T_+$ and as $t\downarrow-T_-$.

\emph{Sharpness.} Let $u_c$ be a minimizer of $m_a(c)$, which exists by Theorem
\ref{thm:level}(ii). Then $u_c\in V(c)$, $E_a(u_c)=m_a(c)$, $u_c\in\Sigma$ by
Proposition \ref{prop:decay} below, and $e^{i\omega_ct}u_c$ solves
\eqref{eq:main} by Theorem \ref{thm:level}(iii). It belongs to $C(\mathbb{R};H^1_a)\cap C^1(\mathbb{R};H^{-1})$. By the
uniqueness contained in Assumption \ref{ass:cauchy}, it is the solution with data
$u_c$. This solution is
global but does not scatter. Indeed, put $r:=\frac{2N}{N-1}$, so that $(4,r)$ is
admissible and $H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^N)\subset L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^N)$. Suppose that
$\|u(t)-e^{-it\mathcal{L}_a}u_+\|_{H^1_a}\to0$ as $t\to\infty$ for some $u_+\inH^1_a$. Then
there is $T>0$ with
$\|e^{-it\mathcal{L}_a}u_+\|_{r}\ge\|u_c\|_{r}-\|u(t)-e^{-it\mathcal{L}_a}u_+\|_{r}\ge\frac12\|u_c\|_{r}>0$
for $t\ge T$, so that
$\|e^{-it\mathcal{L}_a}u_+\|_{L^4_tL^r_x([T,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^N)}=\infty$. This contradicts
Proposition \ref{prop:strichartz} with $F=0$. The same argument applies as
$t\to-\infty$. Therefore the strict inequality in \eqref{eq:below} cannot be
replaced by a non-strict one.

\rm \label{rmk:comparison}
Three features of this argument are worth pointing out. First, the dichotomy is
exhaustive without further hypotheses, because the case $\mathcal{Q}_a(u_0)=0$ is excluded by
the definition of the level itself. Second, the blow-up part uses no information
on the ground state beyond the number $m_a(c)$; in particular it does not use the
uniqueness of $Q$, which to our knowledge is open when $a\ne0$ and $b\ne0$. Third,
\eqref{eq:unifneg} is uniform in time with an explicit constant. Solving
$V(0)+V'(0)t-\delta t^{2}=0$, we obtain the bound
\[
\max\{T_+,T_-\}\ \le\
\frac{\big|V'(0)\big|+\sqrt{V'(0)^{2}+4\delta V(0)}}{2\delta},
\qquad \delta=4\beta\big(m_a(c)-E_a(u_0)\big),
\]
on the blow-up times. This upper bound deteriorates as the energy approaches the
level, like $\delta^{-1}$ in general and like $\delta^{-1/2}$ when $V'(0)=0$. It
says nothing about the actual blow-up time of solutions close to the level.

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:decay}
Let $N\ge3$, $a>a_{\mathrm{H}}$, $0<b<2$ and $0<\alpha<\frac{4-2b}{N-2}$, and let
$v\inH^1_a(\mathbb{R}^{N})$ be a weak solution of \eqref{eq:stationary} with $\omega>0$. Put
$\kappa:=\min\{\lambda_a,\omega\}$, with $\lambda_a$ as in \eqref{eq:equivnorm}.
There is $R\ge1$, depending on $v$, such that for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\frac12\sqrt\kappa]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decay}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}e^{2\varepsilon|x|}\big(|\nabla v|^{2}+|v|^{2}\big)\,dx
\ \le\ C_\varepsilon\,\frac{e^{2\varepsilon R}}{\kappa}\,B(v),
\end{equation}
with $C_\varepsilon$ depending only on $\varepsilon$. In particular $v\in\Sigma$.
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,\frac12\sqrt\kappa]$, and for $n\in\mathbb{N}$ let
$\theta_n(x):=e^{\varepsilon\min\{|x|,n\}}$. This is a bounded Lipschitz function with
$|\nabla\theta_n|\le\varepsilon\theta_n$ almost everywhere, so $\theta_n^2v\in H^1$ is an
admissible test function in Definition \ref{def:weak}. We use the pointwise identity
$\operatorname{Re}\big(\nabla v\cdot\nabla(\theta_n^{2}\bar v)\big)=|\nabla(\theta_nv)|^{2}-|\nabla\theta_n|^{2}|v|^{2}$,
valid for real $\theta_n$. With $w_n:=\theta_nv$, the real part of the weak
formulation becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:katoineq}
A(w_n)+\omega\|w_n\|_{2}^{2}
=\int|\nabla\theta_n|^{2}|v|^{2}\,dx+\int\frac{|v|^{\alpha}|w_n|^{2}}{|x|^{b}}\,dx .
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:equivnorm} the left-hand side is at least $\kappa\|w_n\|_{H^1}^{2}$.
The first term on the right is at most $\varepsilon^{2}\|w_n\|_{2}^{2}\le\frac\kappa4\|w_n\|_{H^1}^2$.
For the second one, we split the integral at a radius $R\ge1$. On $\{|x|<R\}$, we
use $\theta_n\le e^{\varepsilon R}$ to bound it by $e^{2\varepsilon R}B(v)$. On $\{|x|\ge R\}$, we
use
$|x|^{-b}\le1$, H\"older's inequality with exponents $\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}$ and
$\frac{\alpha+2}{2}$, and the Sobolev embedding $H^1\subset L^{\alpha+2}$, which
holds since $2<\alpha+2<\frac{2N}{N-2}$. We get
\[
\int_{|x|\ge R}\frac{|v|^{\alpha}|w_n|^{2}}{|x|^{b}}\,dx
\le\|v\|_{L^{\alpha+2}(|x|\ge R)}^{\alpha}\|w_n\|_{\alpha+2}^{2}
\le C_S^2\,\|v\|_{L^{\alpha+2}(|x|\ge R)}^{\alpha}\|w_n\|_{H^1}^{2}.
\]
Since $v\in L^{\alpha+2}$, we can choose $R\ge1$, depending only on $v$, $\kappa$
and $C_S$, so that $C_S^2\|v\|_{L^{\alpha+2}(|x|\ge R)}^{\alpha}\le\frac\kappa4$.
Collecting the estimates, we obtain
$\kappa\|w_n\|_{H^1}^{2}\le\frac\kappa2\|w_n\|_{H^1}^{2}+e^{2\varepsilon R}B(v)$, that
is, $\|\theta_nv\|_{H^1}^{2}\le\frac2\kappa e^{2\varepsilon R}B(v)$, uniformly in $n$.
As $n\to\infty$, $\theta_n v\to e^{\varepsilon|x|}v$ and
$\nabla(\theta_n v)\to\nabla(e^{\varepsilon|x|}v)$ almost everywhere. Fatou's lemma
gives $e^{\varepsilon|x|}v\in H^1$ with
$\|e^{\varepsilon|x|}v\|_{H^1}^{2}\le\frac2\kappa e^{2\varepsilon R}B(v)$. Since
$e^{\varepsilon|x|}\nabla v=\nabla(e^{\varepsilon|x|}v)-\varepsilon\frac{x}{|x|}e^{\varepsilon|x|}v$, we
obtain \eqref{eq:decay}. Finally, $|x|^{2}\le C_\varepsilon e^{2\varepsilon|x|}$, so
$|x|v\in L^{2}$.

Let $u_c$ be a minimizer of $m_a(c)$. Then $u_c\in V(c)$, $E_a(u_c)=m_a(c)$, and

\eqref{eq:scalingAB}. Second, $v_0^{(t)}\in\Sigma$, since

<!-- remark-15: body_md -->
\rm \label{rmk:instabmeaning}
The instability reflects the minimax nature of the construction. Along its
dilation fibre the normalized ground state sits at the top of a mountain pass, and
the two slopes of the pass are the two sides of the dichotomy in Theorem
\ref{thm:dichotomy}. Spreading the ground state out ($t<1$) produces data below
the level with $\mathcal{Q}_a>0$. These data are global, and under \eqref{eq:dynrange} they
scatter by Theorem \ref{thm:dichotomy}(i). Concentrating it ($t>1$) produces data
below the level with $\mathcal{Q}_a<0$, which blow up when they have finite variance. The standing
wave $e^{i\omega_ct}u_c$ lies on the boundary between the two regimes, and
$m_a(c)$ is the height of that boundary on the sphere $S(c)$. For the classical
equation, this mechanism goes back to Berestycki and Cazenave
\cite{BerestyckiCazenave1981}; see also \cite{LeCoz2008,Ohta2018,Dinh2021CVEE}
for related results with potentials.

\label{prop:masscritical}

while for $c\ge c^{*}$ the set $V(c)$ is nonempty and $E_a\equiv0$ on it, so that
$m_a(c)=0$. If, in addition, Assumption \ref{ass:cauchy} holds for these
parameters, then every solution of \eqref{eq:main} with $\|u_0\|_{2}<c^{*}$ is

$(\alpha+2)(N-2t)<2(N-b)$, that is, to $(\alpha+2)\frac{N}{N-b}<\frac{2N}{N-2t}$. As
in the proof of Lemma \ref{lem:weighted}, we can therefore choose $r\in(1,\frac Nb)$
with $2<(\alpha+2)r'\le\frac{2N}{N-2t}$. Moreover $\alpha+2\le\frac{2N}{N-2t}$,
because $t>\frac{\beta}{\alpha+2}>\frac{N\alpha}{2(\alpha+2)}$. Splitting $B(u)$ at
$|x|=1$ and using H\"older's inequality and the embedding
$H^{t}(\mathbb{R}^N)\subset L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^N)$, $2\le q\le\frac{2N}{N-2t}$, we get
$B(u)\le C\big(\|(-\Delta)^{t/2}u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}$.
We apply this to $u^{s}$, for which $B(u^s)=s^{\beta}B(u)$ and
$\|(-\Delta)^{t/2}u^{s}\|_2^{2}=s^{2t}\|(-\Delta)^{t/2}u\|_2^{2}$, and we choose
$s^{2t}=\|u\|_2^{2}/\|(-\Delta)^{t/2}u\|_2^{2}$. We get
\[
B(u)\le C'\,\big\|(-\Delta)^{t/2}u\big\|_{2}^{\frac{\beta}{t}}\,\|u\|_{2}^{\alpha+2-\frac{\beta}{t}} .
\]
Inserting \eqref{eq:frank}, we obtain
\[
B(u)\le C'\kappa_{N,t}^{-\frac{\beta}{2t}}\,\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{H}}(u)^{\frac\beta2}\,
\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{(1-t)\beta}{t}+\alpha+2-\frac{\beta}{t}}
=C'\kappa_{N,t}^{-\frac{\beta}{2t}}\,\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{H}}(u)^{\frac\beta2}\,\|u\|_{2}^{\sigma_c},
\]
since $\frac{(1-t)\beta}{t}-\frac\beta t=-\beta$ and $\alpha+2-\beta=\sigma_c$.
Thus $\mathcal{C}_{\sharp}<\infty$.

For $a>a_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $u\ne0$, we have
$A(u)=\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{H}}(u)+(a-a_{\mathrm{H}})\int|u|^2|x|^{-2}\ge\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{H}}(u)>0$, hence $J(u)$ is at most the
quotient in \eqref{eq:GNsharp}, and $\mathcal{C}_a\le\mathcal{C}_{\sharp}$. The inequality is strict, because
$\mathcal{C}_a<\mathcal{C}_{a'}\le\mathcal{C}_{\sharp}$ for any $a'\in(a_{\mathrm{H}},a)$, by Proposition
\ref{prop:monotone}. On the other hand, for fixed $u\ne0$, $A(u)\downarrow\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{H}}(u)>0$
as $a\downarrowa_{\mathrm{H}}$, so $J(u)$ converges to the quotient in \eqref{eq:GNsharp}.
Hence $\liminf_{a\downarrowa_{\mathrm{H}}}\mathcal{C}_a$ is at least that quotient for every $u$, that
is, at least $\mathcal{C}_{\sharp}$. Together with the monotonicity this shows $\mathcal{C}_a\uparrow\mathcal{C}_{\sharp}$.
The statement on $m_a(c)$ follows from \eqref{eq:levelformula}.
