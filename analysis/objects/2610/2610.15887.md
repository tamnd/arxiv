\label{thmC}
Let $N\subseteq M$ be modules over a commutative ring $R$. For every multiplicative set $S\subseteq R$,
\[
S^{-1}(\operatorname{rad} N)=\operatorname{rad}(S^{-1}N),\qquad
S^{-1}(\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad} N)=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}(S^{-1}N).
\]
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] A submodule $N\subseteq M$ is semiprime, respectively real, if and only if $N_\mathfrak m\subseteq M_\mathfrak m$ has that property for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$.
\item[(b)] For $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$ quasi-coherent on a Noetherian scheme, semiprimeness and reality are each detected at all stalks (equivalently at all closed stalks). Moreover,
\[
(\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G)_x=\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal G_x),\qquad
(\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G)_x=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}(\mathcal G_x),
\]
with the radicals on the right taken inside $\mathcal F_x$ over $\mathcal O_{X,x}$.
\end{enumerate}
No finite-generation or coherence assumption is required.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:subcrit}
An $R$-module $Q$ is prime if and only if $Q\neq0$ and $\operatorname{Ann}_R(L)=\operatorname{Ann}_R(Q)$ for every nonzero submodule $L\subseteq Q$. It is semiprime if and only if $L\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_R(L)Q$ implies $L=0$ for every submodule $L\subseteq Q$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The submodule conditions imply the elementwise ones by taking $L=Rq$. Conversely, for $0\neq q\in L$, primeness gives $\operatorname{Ann}_R(Q)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_R(L)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_R(q)=\operatorname{Ann}_R(Q)$, while semiprimeness excludes $q\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(L)Q\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_R(q)Q$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:primesheaf}
An object $Q$ is \emph{prime} if $Q\neq0$ and $\langle L\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}=\langle Q\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}$ for every nonzero subobject $L\subseteq Q$, following Smith \cite[Definition~4.3]{Smith}, Pappacena \cite[Definition~6.2]{Pap} and Kanda \cite[Definition~4.1]{Kanda22}. Equivalently, $Q\neq0$ and $q_L$ is an isomorphism for every nonzero subobject $L\subseteq Q$.

We call $Q$ \emph{semiprime} if $q_L|_L:L\to Q_{\langle L\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}}$ is nonzero for every nonzero $L\subseteq Q$. A quasi-coherent subsheaf is prime or semiprime when its quotient is the corresponding object of $\operatorname{QCoh}X$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:categoricalsemiprime}
An object $Q$ is semiprime if and only if $q_L|_L$ is a monomorphism for every subobject $L\subseteq Q$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Suppose $Q$ is semiprime and $K:=\ker(q_L|_L)\neq0$. Since $\langle K\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}\subseteq\langle L\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}$, the map $q_K$ factors through $q_L$. Its restriction to $K$ is therefore zero, contradicting semiprimeness. The converse is immediate.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:catdecomposition}
In a Grothendieck category, every subdirect product of prime objects is semiprime. Conversely, if every nonzero semiprime object contains a prime subobject, every semiprime object is a subdirect product of prime objects; see also \cite{Cnc}. For a locally Noetherian scheme $X$, every nonzero object of $\operatorname{QCoh}X$ contains a prime subobject \cite[Proposition~9.10]{Kanda22}. Hence every semiprime quasi-coherent sheaf is a subdirect product of prime quasi-coherent sheaves. The results below give the explicit fibre description, localization criteria and real decomposition beyond this abstract separation theorem.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $Q\subseteq\prod_iQ_i$ with all $Q_i$ prime, hence semiprime, let $L\subseteq Q$, put $\mathcal C=\langle L\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}$, and let $L_i$ be the image of $L$ in $Q_i$. As $\langle L_i\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}\subseteq\mathcal C$, the map $L_i\to(Q_i)_{\langle L_i\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}}$ factors through $L_i\to(Q_i)_{\mathcal C}$, which is therefore a monomorphism by Lemma~\ref{lem:categoricalsemiprime}. Since $\prod_i(Q_i)_{\mathcal C}\in\mathcal C$, the map $Q\to\prod_i(Q_i)_{\mathcal C}$ factors through $q_L$, and its restriction to $L$ is the monomorphism $L\to\prod_iL_i\to\prod_i(Q_i)_{\mathcal C}$. Hence $q_L|_L$ is a monomorphism, and $Q$ is semiprime.

Conversely, subobjects of a semiprime $Q$ are semiprime: for $L\subseteq Y\subseteq Q$, the map $L\to Q_{\langle L\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}}$ factors through $Y_{\langle L\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}}$. Let $L\subseteq Q$ be a prime subobject, $\mathcal C=\langle L\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}$ and $K_0=\ker q_L$, so $L\cap K_0=0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:categoricalsemiprime}. By Zorn's lemma and exactness of filtered colimits, choose $P\supseteq K_0$ maximal with $P\cap L=0$. Then $Q/P$, a quotient of $Q_{\mathcal C}$, lies in $\mathcal C$. If $0\neq K/P\subseteq Q/P$, maximality gives $K\cap L\neq0$, and $K\cap L$ embeds into $K/P$. Nonzero subobjects of prime objects are prime \cite[Proposition~4.2(1)]{Kanda22}, so $\mathcal C=\langle K\cap L\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}\subseteq\langle K/P\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}\subseteq\mathcal C$; thus $Q/P$ is prime. Let $Y$ be the intersection of all such $P$. If $Y\neq0$, it is semiprime and contains a prime subobject $L$; the corresponding $P$ satisfies $L\subseteq Y\subseteq P$ and $P\cap L=0$, a contradiction. Hence $Q\to\prod Q/P$ is a monomorphism.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:realsheaf}
A quasi-coherent subsheaf $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$ is \emph{real} if the only totally isotropic quasi-coherent subsheaf of $(\mathcal F/\mathcal G)^{\oplus n}$ is zero for every $n\ge1$. A submodule or quasi-coherent subsheaf is \emph{real prime} or \emph{real maximal} if it is real and prime, or real and maximal, respectively. A module or quasi-coherent sheaf is \emph{semireal} if it has a nonzero real quotient.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:closprop}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Submodules and direct products (hence direct sums) of semiprime or real modules have the same property.
\item[(b)] Inverse images and intersections of semiprime or real submodules have the same property.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For submodules, inclusions carry each self-annihilating expression or vanishing tensor sum to the ambient module; for products, the coordinate projections detect each such expression or sum. Thus (a) holds. For (b), a homomorphism $f:M\to M'$ induces $M/f^{-1}(N')\hookrightarrow M'/N'$, and $M/\bigcap_iN_i$ embeds into $\prod_iM/N_i$; apply (a).

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:sheafaffine}
Let $X$ be Noetherian and $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$ quasi-coherent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\mathcal G$ is semiprime if and only if $\Gamma(U,\mathcal G)$ is a semiprime submodule of $\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)$ on every affine open $U$, if and only if this holds on an affine open cover.
\item[(b)] The same equivalences hold for reality. Every real subsheaf is semiprime.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Put $\mathcal Q=\mathcal F/\mathcal G$; on an affine $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$, $\Gamma(U,\mathcal Q)=\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)/\Gamma(U,\mathcal G)$. The implications from an affine cover to the global conditions follow by checking sections on that cover. For the converse implications, we turn a counterexample on one affine open into a subsheaf forbidden by the global definition, using the extension fact above.

(a) Suppose $\mathcal G$ is semiprime, let $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$ be affine, $M=\Gamma(U,\mathcal Q)$ and $q\in\operatorname{Ann}_A(q)M$. Extend $Aq\subseteq M$ to a coherent subsheaf $\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal Q$ with $\Gamma(U,\mathcal E)=Aq$. As $\mathcal E$ is coherent, $\mathcal I(\mathcal E)=\operatorname{Ann}\mathcal E$, and $\Gamma(U,\operatorname{Ann}\mathcal E)=\operatorname{Ann}_A(Aq)=\operatorname{Ann}_A(q)$. Hence
\[
q\in Aq\cap\operatorname{Ann}_A(q)M=\Gamma\bigl(U,\ \mathcal E\cap\mathcal I(\mathcal E)\mathcal Q\bigr).
\]
This is the module obstruction $L\cap\operatorname{Ann}_A(L)M$ for $L=Aq$, and semiprimeness in the form \eqref{eq:sheafcrit} gives $\mathcal E\cap\mathcal I(\mathcal E)\mathcal Q=0$. Thus $q=0$, and $\Gamma(U,\mathcal G)$ is a semiprime submodule of $\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)$.

Conversely, suppose that $\Gamma(U_i,\mathcal Q)$ is semiprime for every member $U_i=\operatorname{Spec} A_i$ of an affine open cover, and let $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal I(\mathcal L)\mathcal Q$. On $U_i$, the sections of $\mathcal I(\mathcal L)\mathcal Q$ are $\Gamma(U_i,\mathcal I(\mathcal L))\Gamma(U_i,\mathcal Q)$, and $\Gamma(U_i,\mathcal I(\mathcal L))$ kills $\Gamma(U_i,\mathcal L)$. Hence every $q\in\Gamma(U_i,\mathcal L)$ satisfies $q\in\operatorname{Ann}_{A_i}(q)\Gamma(U_i,\mathcal Q)$, so $q=0$. Thus $\mathcal L=0$.

(b) Suppose $\mathcal G$ is real. On an affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$, put $Q=\Gamma(U,\mathcal Q)$ and suppose $\sum_i q_i\otimes q_i=0$ in $Q\otimes_A Q$. The cyclic submodule $Aq\subseteq Q^n$ generated by $q=(q_1,\dots,q_n)$ is totally isotropic, since $b_n(aq\otimes cq)=ac\sum_iq_i\otimes q_i=0$; let $\mathcal L_U\subseteq\mathcal Q^{\oplus n}|_U$ be the corresponding coherent subsheaf. Extend it to a coherent $\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal Q^{\oplus n}$. The extension need not remain totally isotropic outside $U$. Let
\[
\mathcal B:=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal E\xrightarrow{b_n}\mathcal Q\otimes\mathcal Q\bigr)
\]
measure this failure; it is coherent, being a quotient of the coherent sheaf $\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal E$, although $\mathcal Q\otimes\mathcal Q$ need not be coherent. Set $\mathcal J:=\operatorname{Ann}\mathcal B$. Since $\mathcal B|_U=0$, we have $\mathcal J|_U=\mathcal O_U$. Consequently $\mathcal L:=\mathcal J\mathcal E$ agrees with $\mathcal L_U$ on $U$, while its pairing vanishes globally: locally, $b_n(je\otimes j'e')=jj'\,b_n(e\otimes e')=0$, because $\mathcal J\mathcal B=0$. Reality gives $\mathcal L=0$, so $q_i=0$ for all $i$. Thus $Q$ is real.

Conversely, suppose the quotient modules on the members of an affine open cover are real and $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal Q^{\oplus n}$ is totally isotropic. On a member $U$, a section $q=(q_1,\dots,q_n)$ of $\mathcal L$ satisfies $b_n(q\otimes q)=\sum_i q_i\otimes q_i=0$ in $\Gamma(U,\mathcal Q\otimes\mathcal Q)=\Gamma(U,\mathcal Q)\otimes\Gamma(U,\mathcal Q)$, hence is zero. Thus $\mathcal L=0$.

Finally, real modules are semiprime: if $q=\sum_ja_jy_j$ with $a_jq=0$, then $q\otimes q=\sum_jy_j\otimes a_jq=0$, so $q=0$. Hence part (a) shows that every real subsheaf is semiprime.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:modfibres}
For $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec} R$, put $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=\operatorname{Frac}(R/\mathfrak p)$ and define the \emph{fibre} $M(\mathfrak p):=M\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. The \emph{fibre map} is the natural $R$-linear map $M\to M(\mathfrak p)$, $m\mapsto m(\mathfrak p):=m\otimes1$. For a submodule $N\subseteq M$, define $N(\mathfrak p)$ as the image of $N\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ in $M(\mathfrak p)$.
For a subspace $W\subseteq M(\mathfrak p)$, define the \emph{point kernel}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modulepointkernel}
P_{\mathfrak p,W}=\{m\in M:m(\mathfrak p)\in W\}.
\end{equation}
The \emph{saturation} of $N+\mathfrak p M$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modulesaturation}
K(N,\mathfrak p):=\{m\in M:sm\in N+\mathfrak p M\text{ for some }s\notin\mathfrak p\}.
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:fibreid}
Let $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec} R$ and $N\subseteq M$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Reduction modulo $\mathfrak p$ and localization give
\[
M(\mathfrak p)\cong(M/\mathfrak p M)\otimes_{R/\mathfrak p}\kappa(\mathfrak p)\cong M_\mathfrak p/\mathfrak p M_\mathfrak p.
\]
\item[(b)] The image of $N$ in the fibre is
\[
N(\mathfrak p)=\operatorname{span}_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\{n\otimes1:n\in N\}
=\frac{N_\mathfrak p+\mathfrak p M_\mathfrak p}{\mathfrak p M_\mathfrak p}.
\]
\item[(c)] Taking inverse images gives the saturation, and for $N=0$ the kernel of the fibre map:
\[
P_{\mathfrak p,N(\mathfrak p)}=K(N,\mathfrak p),\qquad
\ker\bigl(M\to M(\mathfrak p)\bigr)=K(0,\mathfrak p)=\{m\in M:sm\in\mathfrak p M\text{ for some }s\notin\mathfrak p\}.
\]
\item[(d)] If $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak m$ is maximal, then $\kappa(\mathfrak m)=R/\mathfrak m$, $M(\mathfrak m)=M/\mathfrak m M$, the fibre map is the quotient map, $N(\mathfrak m)=(N+\mathfrak m M)/\mathfrak m M$, and
\[
K(N,\mathfrak m)=P_{\mathfrak m,N(\mathfrak m)}=N+\mathfrak m M.
\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
(a) The ideal $\mathfrak p$ acts trivially on $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, and localization commutes with quotients. (b) The tensor image is spanned by the $n\otimes1$; in $M_\mathfrak p/\mathfrak p M_\mathfrak p$ it is the image of $N_\mathfrak p$. (c) By (b), $m(\mathfrak p)\in N(\mathfrak p)$ means $m/1\in N_\mathfrak p+\mathfrak p M_\mathfrak p$, equivalently $sm\in N+\mathfrak p M$ for some $s\notin\mathfrak p$. (d) Here $R/\mathfrak p$ is already a field; apply (c).

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:primequot}
Let $P$ be a proper submodule of an $R$-module $M$ and $\mathfrak p:=(P:M)$. Then $P$ is a prime submodule if and only if $\mathfrak p$ is a prime ideal and $M/P$ is a torsion-free $R/\mathfrak p$-module \cite[Lemma~1]{THS}. In this case $P=K(P,\mathfrak p)$, and
\[
P\text{ is a real submodule}\iff\mathfrak p\text{ is a real ideal}\iff\kappa(\mathfrak p)\text{ is a real field}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Torsion-freeness of $M/P$ over $R/\mathfrak p$ says that $rm\in P$ and $m\notin P$ imply $r\in\mathfrak p$, i.e.\ $rM\subseteq P$; given $P\neq M$, this is primeness of $P$. It remains to see that $\mathfrak p$ is a prime ideal if $P$ is a prime submodule: $\mathfrak p\neq R$, and if $ab\in\mathfrak p$ and $b\notin\mathfrak p$, choose $m$ with $bm\notin P$; then $a(bm)\in P$ gives $a\in\mathfrak p$. If $sm\in P+\mathfrak p M=P$ with $s\notin\mathfrak p$, then $m\in P$ by torsion-freeness; so $P=K(P,\mathfrak p)$.

Put $Q=M/P$ and $A=R/\mathfrak p$, so that $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$. If $Q$ is real and $\sum_i a_i^2=0$ in $A$, choose $0\neq q\in Q$. Then $\sum_i(a_iq)\otimes(a_iq)=0$, so every $a_iq=0$; torsion-freeness gives $a_i=0$. Thus $A$ is real. Clearing denominators shows that $A$ is real if and only if $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is real.

Conversely, suppose $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is real and $\sum_iq_i\otimes q_i=0$ in $Q\otimes_R Q$. In $V=Q\otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak p)$, applying $\lambda\otimes\lambda$ for any $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-linear functional $\lambda:V\to \kappa(\mathfrak p)$ gives $\sum_i\lambda(q_i\otimes1)^2=0$. Hence every functional vanishes on each $q_i\otimes1$, so these vectors are zero. Since $Q\to V$ is injective by torsion-freeness, all $q_i=0$. Thus $Q$ is real.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:simple}
For a submodule $P\subsetneq M$ the following are equivalent: \textup{(i)} $P$ is a maximal submodule; \textup{(ii)} $M/P$ is simple; \textup{(iii)} $M/P\cong R/\mathfrak m$ for some maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. In this case $\mathfrak m=(P:M)=(P:x)$ for every $x\in M\setminus P$, and $P$ is a prime submodule.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
(i)$\Leftrightarrow$(ii) is the correspondence between submodules of $M$ containing $P$ and submodules of $M/P$. A simple module is generated by each of its nonzero elements $\bar x$, so it is isomorphic to $R/\operatorname{Ann}_R(\bar x)$; and $R/I$ is simple if and only if $I$ is a maximal ideal. This gives (ii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii). In (iii), $M/P$ is a one-dimensional $R/\mathfrak m$-vector space, so every nonzero element has annihilator $\mathfrak m=\operatorname{Ann}_R(M/P)$; that is, $(P:x)=(P:M)=\mathfrak m$ for $x\notin P$, and $P$ is a prime submodule.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:modclass}
Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $M$ any $R$-module.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $(\mathfrak p,W)\mapsto P_{\mathfrak p,W}$ is a bijection from pairs with $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec} R$ and $W\subsetneq M(\mathfrak p)$ onto the prime submodules of $M$. Its inverse sends $P$ to $\mathfrak p=(P:M)$ and $W=P(\mathfrak p)$.
\item[(b)] This bijection restricts to real prime submodules by requiring $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ real.
\item[(c)] It restricts to maximal submodules by requiring $\mathfrak p$ maximal and $W$ a hyperplane, and to real maximal submodules by additionally requiring $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ real.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
(a) Put $P=P_{\mathfrak p,W}$. The map $M\to M(\mathfrak p)/W$, $m\mapsto m(\mathfrak p)+W$, has kernel $P$ and nonzero image, since the values $m(\mathfrak p)$ span $M(\mathfrak p)$ and $W$ is proper. Thus $M/P$ embeds as a nonzero submodule of $M(\mathfrak p)/W$. It is therefore torsion-free over $R/\mathfrak p$; hence $\operatorname{Ann}_R(M/P)=\mathfrak p$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:primequot} shows that $P$ is prime. Clearly $P(\mathfrak p)\subseteq W$. For the reverse inclusion, every $w\in W$ has the form $m(\mathfrak p)/\bar s$ with $s\notin\mathfrak p$. Then $m(\mathfrak p)=\bar s w\in W$, so $m\in P$. Thus $P(\mathfrak p)=W$.

Conversely, let $P$ be prime and $\mathfrak p=(P:M)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:primequot} and Lemma~\ref{lem:fibreid}(c) give
\[
P=K(P,\mathfrak p)=P_{\mathfrak p,P(\mathfrak p)}.
\]
The subspace $P(\mathfrak p)$ is proper because $M/P$ is nonzero and torsion-free over $R/\mathfrak p$, hence embeds into its nonzero fibre $M(\mathfrak p)/P(\mathfrak p)$. The two constructions are therefore inverse.

(b) Lemma~\ref{lem:primequot} says precisely that a prime submodule with annihilator $\mathfrak p$ is real if and only if $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is real.

(c) By Lemma~\ref{lem:simple}, a maximal $P$ is prime and $M/P\cong R/\mathfrak m$ for the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(P:M)$. Thus its quotient is one-dimensional over $R/\mathfrak m$. Conversely, for maximal $\mathfrak m$, the fibre map $M\to M(\mathfrak m)=M/\mathfrak m M$ is surjective, so
\[
M/P_{\mathfrak m,W}\cong M(\mathfrak m)/W.
\]
This quotient is simple exactly when $W$ is a hyperplane; by (b), a maximal submodule is real exactly when its maximal residue field is real. No finite-generation assumption is required. This proves Theorem~\ref{thmD}(a).

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:primequotients}
For a prime $\mathfrak p$ and $W\subsetneq M(\mathfrak p)$, put $P=P_{\mathfrak p,W}$. Since $\mathfrak p M\subseteq P$ and $P(\mathfrak p)=W$, localization and passage to the fibre give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primefibrequotient}
(M/P)_\mathfrak p\cong(M/P)\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)\cong M(\mathfrak p)/W.
\end{equation}
The natural map $M/P\hookrightarrow M(\mathfrak p)/W$ need not be surjective: for $R=M=\mathbb Z$, $\mathfrak p=(0)$ and $W=0$ it is $\mathbb Z\hookrightarrow\mathbb Q$. For maximal $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak m$, it is an isomorphism.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:primesections}
Let $\mathcal Q$ be a prime quasi-coherent sheaf on $X$. There is a point $\eta\in X$ such that, for every affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$, $\Gamma(U,\mathcal Q)\neq0$ if and only if $\eta\in U$; in this case $\Gamma(U,\mathcal Q)$ is a prime $A$-module with annihilator $\mathfrak p_\eta$. Thus prime subsheaves have prime or whole-module sections on affine opens.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$, $M=\Gamma(U,\mathcal Q)$ and $0\neq q\in M$. Since $A$ is Noetherian, $\widetilde{Aq}$ is coherent. By the extension fact in Section~\ref{sec:basic}, choose a coherent $\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal Q$ with $\mathcal E|_U=\widetilde{Aq}$. Since $\mathcal E\neq0$, the prime criterion in \eqref{eq:sheafcrit} gives $\mathcal I(\mathcal Q)=\mathcal I(\mathcal E)=\operatorname{Ann}\mathcal E$, and $\Gamma(U,\operatorname{Ann}\mathcal E)=\operatorname{Ann}_A(q)$. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Ann}_A(q)=\Gamma(U,\mathcal I(\mathcal Q))
\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_A(M)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_A(q).
\]
Thus $M$ is prime whenever it is nonzero, with prime annihilator $\mathfrak p_U=\Gamma(U,\mathcal I(\mathcal Q))$. Let $\eta_U$ correspond to $\mathfrak p_U$ and let $Z_U$ be its reduced closure in $X$.

Let $\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal Q$ be the subsheaf of local sections annihilated by $\mathcal I_{Z_U}$. Since $\mathcal I_{Z_U}$ is coherent, $\mathcal T$ is quasi-coherent. Moreover, $\mathcal T|_U=\mathcal Q|_U\neq0$, because $\mathcal I_{Z_U}|_U=\widetilde{\mathfrak p_U}$ annihilates $\mathcal Q|_U$. Primeness therefore gives
\[
\mathcal I_{Z_U}\subseteq\mathcal I(\mathcal T)=\mathcal I(\mathcal Q).
\]
For another affine $U'$ with nonzero sections, taking sections gives $\Gamma(U',\mathcal I_{Z_U})\subseteq\mathfrak p_{U'}$, hence $\eta_{U'}\in Z_U$; symmetrically $\eta_U\in Z_{U'}$. The closures, and hence their generic points, coincide. Call the common point $\eta$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:primequot}, $M$ is torsion-free over $A/\mathfrak p_U$, so it embeds into $M_{\mathfrak p_U}=\mathcal Q_\eta\neq0$. Every affine open containing $\eta$ therefore has nonzero sections.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:primeclass}
Let $x\in X$ and $W\subseteq\mathcal F(x)$ be a $\kappa(x)$-subspace.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\mathcal P_{x,W}$ is quasi-coherent. Its sections over $U$ are the sections $s$ with $s(x)\in W$ if $x\in U$, and all sections of $\mathcal F$ otherwise. Moreover, $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal P_{x,W}$ if and only if $\mathcal G(x)\subseteq W$. On an affine $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$ containing $x\leftrightarrow\mathfrak p$, with $M=\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)$ and $N=\Gamma(U,\mathcal G)$,
\[
\Gamma(U,\mathcal P_{x,\mathcal G(x)})=P_{\mathfrak p,N(\mathfrak p)}=K(N,\mathfrak p).
\]

\item[(b)] The assignment $(x,W)\mapsto\mathcal P_{x,W}$ is a bijection from pairs with $W\subsetneq\mathcal F(x)$ onto the prime quasi-coherent subsheaves of $\mathcal F$. Its inverse sends $\mathcal P$ to the point $x$ attached to $\mathcal F/\mathcal P$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:primesections} and $W=\mathcal P(x)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
(a) On any affine open containing $x$, the target is the sheaf associated to the $A$-module $V=\mathcal F(x)/W$, with scalars acting through $A\to\kappa(x)$; on an affine open not containing $x$, it is zero. Thus the target and the kernel are quasi-coherent. The section description follows from the map to the fibre. For $W=\mathcal G(x)$, its module of sections is $P_{\mathfrak p,N(\mathfrak p)}=K(N,\mathfrak p)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:fibreid}(c). The containment assertion follows from the fibre map.

(b) Let $W\subsetneq\mathcal F(x)$, and let $\mathcal Q\subseteq(j_x)_*V$, $V=\mathcal F(x)/W$, be the image of $\mathcal F\to(j_x)_*V$; it is nonzero, as its stalk at $x$ is $V$. We show that $\mathcal Q\cong\mathcal F/\mathcal P_{x,W}$ is prime with attached point $x$. Let $Z$ be the reduced closure of $x$; then $\mathcal I_Z(j_x)_*V=0$. For $0\neq\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal Q$, choose an affine $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$ with nonzero sections. Necessarily $x\in U$, and every nonzero section is a vector in $V$ with annihilator $\mathfrak p_x$. It remains nonzero at $x$, since $A\setminus\mathfrak p_x$ acts invertibly on $V$; hence $\mathcal L$ has nonzero sections on every affine neighbourhood of $x$. Any quasi-coherent ideal sheaf $\mathcal J$ killing $\mathcal L$ therefore satisfies $x\in V(\mathcal J)$, hence $\mathcal J\subseteq\mathcal I_Z$: on affines containing $x$ this follows from $\Gamma(U,\mathcal J)\subseteq\mathfrak p_x$, and on opens not containing $x$ we have $\mathcal I_Z=\mathcal O_X$, since every open meeting $Z$ contains its generic point $x$. Thus $\mathcal I(\mathcal L)=\mathcal I_Z$, and \eqref{eq:sheafcrit} proves primeness. The point attached to $\mathcal Q$ is $x$, since nonzero sections embed into their localization at $\mathfrak p_x$.

Conversely, let $\mathcal Q=\mathcal F/\mathcal P$ be prime, with attached point $x$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:primesections}. On each affine $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$ containing $x$, $\Gamma(U,\mathcal P)$ is a prime submodule of $\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)$ with annihilator $\mathfrak p_x$. Theorem~\ref{thm:modclass}(a) identifies it with the inverse image of $\mathcal P(x)$ in the fibre at $x$. On affines not containing $x$, $\mathcal Q$ has zero sections, so $\mathcal P$ equals $\mathcal F$. These affine descriptions give $\mathcal P=\mathcal P_{x,\mathcal P(x)}$.

Finally, $\mathcal P_{x,W}(x)=W$, because $\mathcal F_x\to\mathcal F(x)/W$ is surjective. Together with recovery of the attached point, this proves uniqueness of the pair.

\label{prop:realprimesheaf}
The correspondence $(x,W)\mapsto\mathcal P_{x,W}$ restricts to a bijection between pairs with $\kappa(x)$ real and $W\subsetneq\mathcal F(x)$ a $\kappa(x)$-subspace, and the real prime subsheaves of $\mathcal F$. Thus reality restricts the point, with no additional condition on $W$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop:primeclass}, the quotient $\mathcal F/\mathcal P_{x,W}$ is prime with attached point $x$. On an affine $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$ containing $x$, its sections are prime with annihilator $\mathfrak p_x$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:primesections}); the module classification in Theorem~\ref{thm:modclass}(b) says they are real if and only if $\kappa(x)=\operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak p_x)$ is real. On affine opens not containing $x$, the quotient is zero. Proposition~\ref{prop:sheafaffine}(b) proves the equivalence, and the classification gives the last assertion.

\label{lem:maxsheaf}
Let $x\in X$ be closed and $W\subseteq\mathcal F(x)$ a subspace.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The stalks of $\mathcal P_{x,W}$ are $\mathcal F_y$ for $y\neq x$, and the inverse image of $W$ in $\mathcal F_x$ for $y=x$. Moreover,

\mathcal F/\mathcal P_{x,W}\cong(j_x)_*(\mathcal F(x)/W).

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:maxsheaf}
Let $\mathcal F$ be quasi-coherent. The correspondence of Proposition~\ref{prop:primeclass} restricts to a bijection between pairs $(x,H)$ with $x$ closed and $H\subsetneq\mathcal F(x)$ a hyperplane, and the maximal subsheaves of $\mathcal F$. A maximal subsheaf $\mathcal P_{x,H}$ is real if and only if $\kappa(x)$ is real.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal M$ be maximal, so $\mathcal Q=\mathcal F/\mathcal M$ is nonzero and simple. Every quasi-coherent sheaf on a Noetherian scheme is a union of coherent subsheaves \cite[Tag~0GN6]{Stacks}; simplicity therefore makes $\mathcal Q$ coherent. Choose a closed point $x$ in its support. Nakayama's lemma gives $\mathcal Q(x)\neq0$, and a nonzero linear form on this fibre gives a surjection $\mathcal Q\to(j_x)_*\kappa(x)$ with quasi-coherent kernel. Simplicity makes this map an isomorphism. The composite $\mathcal F(x)\to\mathcal Q(x)\to\kappa(x)$ has a hyperplane kernel $H$, and $\mathcal M=\mathcal P_{x,H}$. Conversely, every such kernel is maximal by Lemma~\ref{lem:maxsheaf}(b). Uniqueness follows from the prime classification, and Proposition~\ref{prop:realprimesheaf} gives the reality criterion.

Proposition~\ref{prop:primeclass} proves the prime classification, including quasi-coherence and uniqueness of the pair. Proposition~\ref{prop:realprimesheaf} restricts the point to a real residue field. Proposition~\ref{prop:maxsheaf} restricts the pair to a closed point and a hyperplane.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:Kproper}
Let $N$ be a submodule of an $R$-module $M$ and $\mathfrak p$ a prime ideal of $R$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $\mathfrak p\not\supseteq(N:M)$, then $K(N,\mathfrak p)=M$.
\item[(b)] If $M/N$ is finitely generated, then $K(N,\mathfrak p)\neq M$ if and only if $\mathfrak p\supseteq(N:M)$.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:jac}
Let $P$ be a prime submodule of an $R$-module $M$, $\mathfrak p:=(P:M)$, and let $\mathcal M$ be a set of maximal ideals of $R$ containing $\mathfrak p$ such that $\mathfrak p=\bigcap_{\mathfrak m\in\mathcal M}\mathfrak m$. If $M/P$ is a torsionless $R/\mathfrak p$-module (e.g.\ if $M/P$ is finitely generated as an $R$-module, by Lemmas~\ref{lem:primequot} and~\ref{lem:embed}), then
\[
P=\bigcap_{\mathfrak m\in\mathcal M}(P+\mathfrak m M).
\]
Consequently, $P$ is an intersection of maximal submodules $P'$ with $(P':M)\in\mathcal M$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Put $D:=R/\mathfrak p$ and $Q:=M/P$. Let $x\in M\setminus P$ with image $q\neq0$ in $Q$. Choose $\varphi:Q\to D$ with $\varphi(q)\neq0$, and $c\in R$ with $\bar c=\varphi(q)$; then $c\notin\mathfrak p$, so there is $\mathfrak m\in\mathcal M$ with $c\notin\mathfrak m$. Since $\varphi(\mathfrak m Q)=\mathfrak m\varphi(Q)\subseteq\mathfrak m D=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak p$ and $\varphi(q)=\bar c\notin\mathfrak m/\mathfrak p$, we get $q\notin\mathfrak m Q$, i.e.\ $x\notin P+\mathfrak m M$. Hence $P=\bigcap_{\mathfrak m\in\mathcal M}(P+\mathfrak m M)$. For any $\mathfrak m$, linear functionals on the $R/\mathfrak m$-vector space $M/(P+\mathfrak m M)$ separate its nonzero vectors. Their nonzero composites $M\to R/\mathfrak m$ are surjective, with maximal kernels of annihilator $\mathfrak m$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:simple}. Thus each proper $P+\mathfrak m M$ is an intersection of such kernels, proving the consequence.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:empty}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:jac}, $P+\mathfrak m M=M$ can occur, equivalently $\mathfrak m(M/P)=M/P$ or $K(P,\mathfrak m)=M$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:fibreid}(d). If $M/P$ is finitely generated, this never happens, by Lemma~\ref{lem:Kproper}(b), since $\mathfrak m\supseteq\mathfrak p=(P:M)$. The same holds if $R$ is Noetherian. Indeed, let $D:=R/\mathfrak p$, $\bar\mathfrak m:=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak p$, and, using torsionlessness, identify $M/P$ with a $D$-submodule of $D^I$. If $\bar\mathfrak m(M/P)=M/P$, then the image $J_i$ of $M/P$ under the $i$-th projection is an ideal of $D$ with $\bar\mathfrak m J_i=J_i$; as $J_i$ is finitely generated, $(1+a)J_i=0$ for some $a\in\bar\mathfrak m$ \cite[Corollary~2.5]{AM}, and $1+a\neq0$; since $D$ is a domain, $J_i=0$. As this holds for all $i$, $M/P=0$, a contradiction.

Without these hypotheses, $P+\mathfrak m M=M$ can occur. Let $A:=\mathbb R[x^{1/2^n}:n\ge0]$, viewed as a ring of functions on $[0,\infty)$, and for $t\ge0$ let $\mathfrak m_t$ be the kernel of the evaluation $x^{1/2^n}\mapsto t^{1/2^n}$. The $\mathfrak m_t$ are real maximal ideals of $A$ with $\bigcap_{t\ge0}\mathfrak m_t=0$, as a nonzero polynomial in $x^{1/2^n}$ cannot vanish on $[0,\infty)$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:jac} with $R:=A$, $\mathcal M:=\{\mathfrak m_t:t\ge0\}$, $M:=\mathfrak m_0$ and $P:=0$. Since $A$ is a domain and $\mathfrak m_0\neq0$, the submodule $P$ is prime with $(P:M)=0$, and $M/P=\mathfrak m_0$ is torsionless over $A/(P:M)=A$, being an $A$-submodule of $A$. But $\mathfrak m_0=\mathfrak m_0^2$. Indeed, every $f\in\mathfrak m_0$ lies in $\mathbb R[y]$ for some $y=x^{1/2^N}$ and satisfies $f(0)=0$, so $f=yg$ with $g\in\mathbb R[y]$; with $z:=x^{1/2^{N+1}}$ we get $f=z\cdot(zg)\in\mathfrak m_0^2$, as $z,zg\in\mathfrak m_0$. Hence $P+\mathfrak m_0M=M$. As all $\mathfrak m_t$ are real, this also happens for real maximal ideals.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:Q}
(a) Over the Jacobson ring $\mathbb Z$, the module $\mathbb Q$ is torsion-free but not torsionless \cite[Remark~4.65(e)]{Lam}, so $0$ is a prime submodule; but $\mathbb Q$ has no maximal submodule, since $\mathbb Q/P\cong\mathbb Z/p$ would force $P\supseteq p\mathbb Q=\mathbb Q$ (cf.\ \cite[Examples~2.4(ii)]{MSS}).

(b) \emph{Freeness does not suffice.} Let $\pi:\mathbb Z^{(\mathbb N)}\to\mathbb Q$ be a surjection and $P:=\ker\pi$. Then $\mathbb Z^{(\mathbb N)}/P\cong\mathbb Q$ is a prime module, so $P$ is a prime submodule of a free module over a Jacobson ring, but by (a) no maximal submodule contains $P$. Thus freeness of $M$ cannot replace torsionlessness of $M/P$ in Theorem~\ref{thmA}(a). (Conversely, if $R$ is not a Jacobson ring, then no nonzero free module is Jacobson \cite[Examples~2.4(i)]{MSS}.)

(c) \emph{A torsionless module which is not finitely generated.} The $\mathbb Z$-module $\mathbb Z^\mathbb N$ is torsion-free and torsionless (by \cite[Remark~4.65(c)]{Lam}), so $0$ is a prime submodule and an intersection of maximal submodules by Theorem~\ref{thm:jac}. It is not a submodule of a free module, since subgroups of free abelian groups are free and $\mathbb Z^\mathbb N$ is not free by the Baer--Specker theorem \cite[Chapter~3, Theorem~8.2]{Fuchs}; so the embedding argument of \cite[Lemma~2.8]{MSS} does not apply to it.

(d) \emph{Torsionlessness is not necessary.} Let $p$ be a prime and $Q:=\mathbb Z[1/p]$. Then $0$ is a prime submodule and $Q/qQ\cong\mathbb Z/q$ for all primes $q\neq p$, while $pQ=Q$; so $0=\bigcap_{q\neq p}qQ$ is an intersection of maximal submodules. However, $\operatorname{Hom}_\mathbb Z(Q,\mathbb Z)=0$ (the image of $1$ under a homomorphism $\varphi$ is divisible by all powers of $p$, so $\varphi(1)=0$, and then $p^k\varphi(p^{-k})=0$, so $\varphi=0$, as $\mathbb Z$ is torsion-free), so $Q$ is not torsionless.

(e) \emph{A real torsion-free module with vanishing closed fibres.} Torsionlessness cannot be weakened to torsion-freeness in Theorem~\ref{thmA}(b), and finite generation cannot be dropped in Theorem~\ref{thmB}(b). Let $R=\mathbb R[x]$ and $M=\mathbb R(x)$. Then $M$ is a real module: multiplication $M\times M\to\mathbb R(x)$, $(v,w)\mapsto vw$, is $R$-bilinear, so it induces an $R$-linear map $\mu:M\otimes_RM\to\mathbb R(x)$ with $\mu(\sum_im_i\otimes m_i)=\sum_im_i^2$. If $\sum_im_i\otimes m_i=0$, then $\sum_im_i^2=0$ in the real field $\mathbb R(x)$, so all $m_i=0$. Thus $\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}0=0$, and $M$ is semireal, as $M\neq0$. But $M$ has no maximal submodules at all, since $\mathfrak m M=M$ for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ of $R$; in particular $M(a)=0$ for every $a\in\mathbb R$. Thus both the closed-fibre radical formula and the semireality test following Theorem~\ref{thm:modfibre} fail without finite generation. As in (b), the kernel of a surjection from a free $R$-module onto $\mathbb R(x)$ is a real prime submodule of a free module which is contained in no maximal submodule.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:fgreal}
Let $A$ be a finitely generated algebra over a real field $k$. Then every real prime ideal of $A$ is an intersection of real maximal ideals.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Let $\mathfrak p$ be a real prime ideal and $g\notin\mathfrak p$. Set $D:=A/\mathfrak p$ and order $\operatorname{Frac}D$. Lang's theorem \cite[Chapter~XI, \S3, Theorem~3.1, p.~457]{Lang}, applied to the finitely generated $k$-algebra $D[1/\bar g]$, gives a $k$-homomorphism
\[
\varphi:D[1/\bar g]\longrightarrow k^{\mathrm{rc}},
\]
where $k^{\mathrm{rc}}$ is a real closure for the ordering induced on $k$; see also \cite[p.~111]{Dubois} and \cite[II.2, (2.4), p.~52]{FG}. The image $\varphi(D)$ is a finitely generated algebraic $k$-algebra and a domain, hence a field; it is real, being contained in $k^{\mathrm{rc}}$. Thus the kernel of $A\to D\to\varphi(D)$ is a real maximal ideal containing $\mathfrak p$ but not $g$, since $\varphi(\bar g)$ is invertible. Intersecting these ideals gives $\mathfrak p$.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:fgreal}
Let $A$ be a finitely generated algebra over a field $k$.

(a) If $k$ is not real, then $A$ has no real prime ideals: if $-1=\sum_ic_i^2$ in $k$, then $1^2+\sum_ic_i^2=0$ lies in every ideal, so a real ideal contains $1$. Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:fgreal}, every finitely generated algebra over a field is real Jacobson.

(b) If $k$ is real closed, the real maximal ideals of $A$ are the $\mathfrak m_a$ with $a\in V_A(k)$: by Zariski's lemma \cite[Proposition~7.9]{AM} each residue field $A/\mathfrak m$ is a finite extension of $k$, and a real closed field has no proper real algebraic extension \cite[I.1, Theorem~1.29]{FG}; see also \cite[II.3, Theorem~3.4]{FG}.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Put $\mathfrak p=(P:M)$ and take all maximal ideals containing $\mathfrak p$ in (a), or all real maximal ideals containing $\mathfrak p$ in (b). The ring hypothesis says their intersection is $\mathfrak p$; in (b), $\mathfrak p$ is real by Lemma~\ref{lem:primequot}. Theorem~\ref{thm:jac} expresses $P$ as an intersection of maximal submodules with annihilators in this family, which are real in (b) by Theorem~\ref{thm:modclass}(c). For finitely generated prime quotients, Lemmas~\ref{lem:primequot} and~\ref{lem:embed} supply torsionlessness. Hence the corresponding ring property implies that every finitely generated module has the corresponding Jacobson property; the converse follows by taking $M=R$.

\label{cor:maxreal}

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:ann}
Let $M$ be a finitely generated $R$-module with annihilator $I$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $M$ is Jacobson if and only if $R/I$ is a Jacobson ring.
\item[(b)] $M$ is real Jacobson if and only if $R/I$ is a real Jacobson ring.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
In (a) use all prime and maximal ideals and submodules; in (b) use only the real ones. If $R/I$ has the indicated property, the annihilator of each prime submodule $P$ contains $I$ and is an intersection of the indicated maximal ideals. Theorem~\ref{thm:jac} and Lemma~\ref{lem:embed} express $P$ as an intersection of the corresponding maximal submodules.

Conversely, let $\mathfrak p\supseteq I$ be a prime ideal of the indicated type. Lemma~\ref{lem:Kproper}(b) makes $K(0,\mathfrak p)$ proper, and Theorem~\ref{thm:modclass} makes it prime with annihilator $\mathfrak p$, and real in (b). Write it as an intersection of the corresponding maximal submodules. Taking annihilators expresses $\mathfrak p$ as an intersection of the corresponding maximal ideals. The quotient correspondence for ideals (Section~\ref{sec:basic}) proves the ring property.

<!-- proposition-9: body_md -->
\label{prop:sheafseparation}
Let $X$ be of finite type over a field and $\mathcal F$ quasi-coherent. Every prime subsheaf $\mathcal P\subsetneq\mathcal F$ with coherent quotient is an intersection of maximal quasi-coherent subsheaves containing it. If $\mathcal P$ is real prime, it is an intersection of real maximal subsheaves.
<!-- end proposition-9 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
Write $\mathcal P=\mathcal P_{\eta,W}$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:primeclass}. On an affine $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$ containing $\eta$, its sections $P\subseteq M=\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)$ are prime with finitely generated quotient, hence torsionless over $A/(P:M)$ by Lemmas~\ref{lem:primequot} and~\ref{lem:embed}. The ring $A$ is Jacobson, and real Jacobson by Proposition~\ref{prop:fgreal} and Remark~\ref{rem:fgreal}(a). In the real case, $P$ is real by Proposition~\ref{prop:sheafaffine}(b). Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:jac} to all maximal ideals containing $(P:M)$, or all real maximal ideals in the real case. Thus $P$ is an intersection of the corresponding maximal submodules of $M$.

By the classification, these are precisely the $P_{\mathfrak m,H}$ with $P(\mathfrak m)\subseteq H$ a hyperplane, restricting to real residue fields in the real case. Their residue fields are finite over $k$ by Zariski's lemma, so their points are closed in $X$ \cite[Tag~01TF, applied to $X\to\operatorname{Spec} k$]{Stacks}. They therefore define global maximal subsheaves containing $\mathcal P$. An affine chart not containing $\eta$ misses its closure and has $\mathcal P|_U=\mathcal F|_U$; no closed point there imposes a condition. Lemma~\ref{lem:kernelint}, grouping hyperplanes over each point, assembles the affine intersections into the required global intersection.

This proves Theorem~\ref{thmA}(c).

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:modfibre}
Let $M$ be an $R$-module and $S\subseteq\operatorname{Spec} R$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For every $N\subseteq M$,
\[
\operatorname{rad}_S N=\bigcap_{\substack{P\supseteq N,\ P\text{ prime}\\(P:M)\in S}}P.
\]
\item[(b)] $S=\operatorname{Spec} R$ gives $\operatorname{rad}_S=\operatorname{rad}$, and $S=\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{re}}R$ gives $\operatorname{rad}_S=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}$. If $M$ is finitely generated, $S=\operatorname{Max}(R)$ gives $\operatorname{rad}_S=\operatorname{rad}$ when $R$ is Jacobson, and $S=\operatorname{Max}_{\mathrm{re}}(R)$ gives $\operatorname{rad}_S=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}$ when $R$ is real Jacobson.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
(a) By definition,
\[
\operatorname{rad}_S N=\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in S}P_{\mathfrak p,N(\mathfrak p)}.
\]
Fix $\mathfrak p\in S$. Theorem~\ref{thm:modclass}(a) identifies the primes containing $N$ with annihilator $\mathfrak p$ as the $P_{\mathfrak p,W}$ with $N(\mathfrak p)\subseteq W\subsetneq M(\mathfrak p)$. If $N(\mathfrak p)$ is proper, $P_{\mathfrak p,N(\mathfrak p)}$ is the smallest of these primes, so it is their intersection. If $N(\mathfrak p)=M(\mathfrak p)$, there are no such primes; their empty intersection and $P_{\mathfrak p,N(\mathfrak p)}$ both equal $M$. Intersecting over $\mathfrak p\in S$ proves (a) and also gives
\[
\operatorname{rad}_S N=\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in S}K(N,\mathfrak p).
\]
Thus $\operatorname{rad}_S N$ is semiprime and contains $\operatorname{rad} N$. If $S\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{re}}R$, all the primes in (a) are real by Theorem~\ref{thm:modclass}(b), so $\operatorname{rad}_S N$ is real and contains $\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad} N$.

(b) The first two equalities follow from (a) and the prime-decomposition theorems \cite[Theorems~A and~B]{Cdec}. Suppose now that $M$ is finitely generated and $R$ is Jacobson. By \cite[Theorem~A]{Cdec}, $\operatorname{rad} N$ is an intersection of prime submodules containing $N$, and Theorem~\ref{thmA}(a) expresses each of these as an intersection of maximal submodules. Each such maximal submodule is prime with maximal annihilator by Theorem~\ref{thm:modclass}(c), so it occurs in the intersection defining $\operatorname{rad}_{\operatorname{Max}(R)}N$ in (a). Hence $\operatorname{rad}_{\operatorname{Max}(R)}N\subseteq\operatorname{rad} N$, while (a) gives the reverse inclusion. The same argument with \cite[Theorem~B]{Cdec} and Theorem~\ref{thmA}(b) proves the real equality over a real Jacobson ring. In these closed cases the radical is also the intersection of the maximal, resp.\ real maximal, submodules containing $N$.

If $R$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra, Zariski's lemma \cite[Proposition~7.9]{AM} identifies all maximal ideals with the $\mathfrak m_a$, $a\in V_R(k)$, when $k$ is algebraically closed; Remark~\ref{rem:fgreal}(b) identifies the real maximal ideals with these $\mathfrak m_a$ when $k$ is real closed. Thus the corresponding closed-fibre conditions read $f(a)\in N(a)$ for every $a\in V_R(k)$. In each case, the Galois bijection identifies semiprime, resp.\ real, submodules with realizable families on the indicated points.
<!-- end proof-25 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
Take $S=\operatorname{Max}(R)$ in (a) and $S=\operatorname{Max}_{\mathrm{re}}(R)$ in (b). By Theorem~\ref{thm:modfibre}(b), $\operatorname{rad}=\operatorname{rad}_S$, resp.\ $\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}=\operatorname{rad}_S$, and by definition $\operatorname{rad}_S N=\{f\in M:f(\mathfrak m)\in N(\mathfrak m)\text{ for all }\mathfrak m\in S\}$; this is the membership criterion. The maximality and bijection statements are the Galois identities for this $S$.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:modproper}
Let $N\subseteq M$, $Q=M/N$ and $S=\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{re}}R$. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.30\linewidth}@{\hspace{0.04\linewidth}}p{0.65\linewidth}@{}}
(i) $Q$ is semireal;  & (iv) there is a real prime submodule $P\subset M$ containing $N$; \\[3pt]
(ii) $Q(\mathfrak p)\neq0$ for some $\mathfrak p\in S$; & (v) $N(\mathfrak p)\neq M(\mathfrak p)$ for some $\mathfrak p\in S$;  \\[3pt]
(iii) $\operatorname{rad}_S 0\neq Q$;  & (vi) $\operatorname{rad}_S N\neq M$;
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
If $Q$ is finitely generated, these are also equivalent to
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.30\linewidth}@{\hspace{0.04\linewidth}}p{0.65\linewidth}@{}}
(vii)  $R/\operatorname{Ann}_R(Q)$ is semireal; & (viii) $(N:M)$  is contained in a real prime ideal.
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
If $Q$ is finitely generated and $R$ is real Jacobson, one may take $S=\operatorname{Max}_{\mathrm{re}}(R)$ instead. For this choice of $S$, ``prime'' in (iv) may be replaced by ``maximal''.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
For any $S\subseteq\operatorname{Spec} R$, right exactness of tensoring with $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ gives $Q(\mathfrak p)=M(\mathfrak p)/N(\mathfrak p)$; thus
\[
\operatorname{rad}_S N\neq M
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Q(\mathfrak p)\neq0\text{ for some }\mathfrak p\in S
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N(\mathfrak p)\neq M(\mathfrak p)\text{ for some }\mathfrak p\in S.
\]
Indeed, the last condition means that some $f\in M$ fails a fibre condition defining $\operatorname{rad}_S N$. Thus (ii), (v) and (vi) are equivalent. Since $S=\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{re}}R$, Theorem~\ref{thm:modfibre}(b) gives $\operatorname{rad}_S0=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}_Q0$. Semireality of $Q$ means that it has a nonzero real quotient, equivalently $\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}_Q0\neq Q$, proving (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii). Applying the display to $0\subseteq Q$ gives (ii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii). By Theorem~\ref{thm:modfibre}(a) and Theorem~\ref{thm:modclass}(b), $\operatorname{rad}_S N$ is the intersection of the real prime submodules containing $N$; this intersection is proper exactly when there is such a submodule. Hence (iv)$\Leftrightarrow$(vi).

Since $\operatorname{Ann}_R(Q)=(N:M)$, condition (vii) is equivalent to (viii) by the real prime criterion for semireal rings. If $Q$ is finitely generated, Lemma~\ref{lem:Kproper}(b) gives $Q(\mathfrak p)\neq0$ exactly when $(N:M)\subseteq\mathfrak p$, proving (ii)$\Leftrightarrow$(viii). If $R$ is also real Jacobson, every real prime is contained in a real maximal ideal; applying the same lemma shows that closed real fibres suffice. For $S=\operatorname{Max}_{\mathrm{re}}(R)$, condition (v) gives $N(\mathfrak m)\subsetneq M(\mathfrak m)$ for some $\mathfrak m\in S$. Choose a hyperplane $H$ containing $N(\mathfrak m)$; then $P_{\mathfrak m,H}$ is a real maximal submodule containing $N$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:modclass}(c). Conversely, a real maximal submodule containing $N$ gives such a proper fibre, and is real prime. A nonzero finitely generated $Q$ also has a nonzero closed fibre over any ring: choose a maximal ideal containing its annihilator. These observations prove the proper-fibre assertions following Theorem~\ref{thmB}.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{sec:directional}
A \emph{directional point} of $M$ is a pair $(\mathfrak p,H)$ with $H\subset M(\mathfrak p)$ a hyperplane; write $\operatorname{Dir}M$ for their set. The corresponding $P_{\mathfrak p,H}$ are the fibrewise maximal primes (compare \cite[Corollaries~9 and~11]{THS}). For $S\subseteq\operatorname{Spec} R$, define
\[
V_S(N):=\{(\mathfrak p,H)\in\operatorname{Dir}M:\mathfrak p\in S,\ N(\mathfrak p)\subseteq H\},
\qquad
J(T):=\bigcap_{(\mathfrak p,H)\in T}P_{\mathfrak p,H},
\]
where $T$ is a set of directional points over $S$.

Every fibre subspace is the intersection of the hyperplanes containing it, with empty intersection $M(\mathfrak p)$. Thus $V_S(N)$ carries exactly the same information as $\Phi_S(N)$, and taking inverse images gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radSdir}
J(V_S(N))=\Psi_S\Phi_S(N)=\operatorname{rad}_S N.
\end{equation}
The $\Phi_S$--$\Psi_S$ correspondence therefore becomes an inclusion-reversing bijection between submodules fixed by $\operatorname{rad}_S$ and directional zero sets. The former are the semiprime, resp.\ real, submodules for the corresponding choices of $S$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:modfibre}.

For $M=R^n$, a hyperplane is the kernel of a nonzero form $\lambda_v(f(\mathfrak p))=\sum_i v_i f_i(\mathfrak p)$, so directional vanishing recovers the formalism of \cite{C22a,C13}. For $M=R$, there is one direction over each point, giving the usual ideal correspondence.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:sheaffibre}
Let $\mathcal F$ be quasi-coherent on a Noetherian scheme $X$ and $S\subseteq X$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For every quasi-coherent $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$,
\[
\operatorname{rad}_S\mathcal G=\bigcap_{\substack{\mathcal P\supseteq\mathcal G,\ \mathcal P\text{ prime}\\\text{attached point in }S}}\mathcal P.
\]
\item[(b)] $S=X$ gives $\operatorname{rad}_S=\operatorname{rad}$, and $S=X_{\mathrm{re}}$ gives $\operatorname{rad}_S=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}$. If $\mathcal F$ is coherent and $X$ is of finite type over a field, $S=X_{\mathrm{cl}}$ gives $\operatorname{rad}_S=\operatorname{rad}$, and $S=X_{\mathrm{re}}\cap X_{\mathrm{cl}}$ gives $\operatorname{rad}_S=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-28: body_md -->
(a) By definition,
\[
\operatorname{rad}_S\mathcal G=\bigcap_{x\in S}\mathcal P_{x,\mathcal G(x)}.
\]
Fix $x\in S$. Proposition~\ref{prop:primeclass} identifies the primes containing $\mathcal G$ and attached to $x$ as the $\mathcal P_{x,W}$ with $\mathcal G(x)\subseteq W\subsetneq\mathcal F(x)$. If $\mathcal G(x)$ is proper, $\mathcal P_{x,\mathcal G(x)}$ is the smallest of these primes, so it is their intersection. If $\mathcal G(x)=\mathcal F(x)$, there are no such primes; their empty intersection and $\mathcal P_{x,\mathcal G(x)}$ both equal $\mathcal F$. Intersecting over $x\in S$ proves (a), as in the module argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:modfibre}(a). More explicitly, on $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$, put $M=\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)$ and $N=\Gamma(U,\mathcal G)$, and identify $S\cap U$ with a subset of $\operatorname{Spec} A$. Lemma~\ref{lem:kernelint} makes $\operatorname{rad}_S\mathcal G$ quasi-coherent with affine sections $\operatorname{rad}_{S\cap U}N$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:modfibre}(a), these submodules are semiprime, and real if $S\subseteq X_{\mathrm{re}}$. The affine criteria of Proposition~\ref{prop:sheafaffine}(a),(b) therefore make $\operatorname{rad}_S\mathcal G$ semiprime, resp.\ real, so it contains $\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G$, resp.\ $\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G$.

(b) For $S=X$ or $S=X_{\mathrm{re}}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:kernelint} and Theorem~\ref{thm:modfibre}(b) identify the affine sections with the corresponding module radical. Thus, on $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$, with $M=\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)$ and $N=\Gamma(U,\mathcal G)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sheafaffineradicals}
(\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G)|_U=\widetilde{\operatorname{rad} N},\qquad
(\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G)|_U=\widetilde{\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad} N}.
\end{equation}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:sheafaffine}, these intersections are semiprime, respectively real. Every such quasi-coherent supersheaf $\mathcal H\supseteq\mathcal G$ has affine sections containing $\operatorname{rad} N$, respectively $\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad} N$, by the same proposition. Hence $\mathcal H$ contains the corresponding intersection, proving minimality. They are coherent when $\mathcal F$ is coherent, since $X$ is Noetherian. Together with (a), this proves the unrestricted prime-intersection decompositions.

Suppose now that $\mathcal F$ is coherent and $X$ is of finite type over a field. Every prime quotient of $\mathcal F$ is coherent, so Theorem~\ref{thmA}(c) expresses each prime subsheaf as an intersection of maximal subsheaves. By Proposition~\ref{prop:maxsheaf}, these are primes attached to closed points, hence they occur in (a) for $S=X_{\mathrm{cl}}$. Substituting these intersections in the unrestricted prime decomposition gives $\operatorname{rad}_{X_{\mathrm{cl}}}\mathcal G\subseteq\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G$; (a) gives the reverse inclusion. The same argument with real primes and real maximal subsheaves gives $\operatorname{rad}_{X_{\mathrm{re}}\cap X_{\mathrm{cl}}}\mathcal G=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G$. In these closed cases the radicals are also the intersections of the maximal, resp.\ real maximal, subsheaves containing $\mathcal G$. Over a real closed base field, the closed real points are precisely the rational points by Remark~\ref{rem:fgreal}(b).

In each case, the Galois identities identify semiprime, resp.\ real, subsheaves with realizable families on the indicated points. The radical is the largest quasi-coherent subsheaf with the same fibre images as $\mathcal G$ there; its sections over an open $U$ are the $s$ with $s(x)\in\mathcal G(x)$ for all $x\in S\cap U$, by Proposition~\ref{prop:primeclass}(a).

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
\label{rem:reduction}
Let $X$ be Noetherian and $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$ quasi-coherent. A local section of a semiprime quasi-coherent sheaf vanishing in every fibre on its domain is zero, since $\operatorname{rad}_X0=\operatorname{rad}0=0$; for real sheaves, real points suffice. Since quasi-coherent subsheaves of semiprime (resp.\ real) sheaves are semiprime (resp.\ real), every morphism $\mathcal F\to\mathcal E$ to a semiprime (resp.\ real) quasi-coherent sheaf that kills $\mathcal G$ factors uniquely through $\mathcal F/\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G$ (resp.\ $\mathcal F/\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G$). Indeed, its kernel is semiprime (resp.\ real) and contains $\mathcal G$, hence also the corresponding radical. For $\mathcal F=\mathcal O_X$ and $\mathcal G=\mathcal I$, the ordinary quotient is the structure sheaf of the reduced induced closed subscheme on $V(\mathcal I)$ \cite[Tag~01J3]{Stacks}. The factorization property is a sheaf analogue of \cite[Tag~0356]{Stacks}; in general, the correspondence uses realizable families of fibre subspaces in place of closed subsets (Theorem~\ref{thm:sheaffibre}).

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:denom}
Let $Q$ be an $R$-module and $S\subseteq R$ a multiplicative set.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $x\in Q$, $s\in S$ and $x/s\in\operatorname{Ann}_{S^{-1}R}(x/s)\,S^{-1}Q$, then $\sigma x\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(\sigma x)\,Q$ for some $\sigma\in S$.
\item[(b)] If $x_1,\dots,x_k\in Q$, $s\in S$ and $\sum_i(x_i/s)\otimes(x_i/s)=0$ in $S^{-1}Q\otimes_{S^{-1}R}S^{-1}Q$, then $\sum_i\sigma x_i\otimes\sigma x_i=0$ in $Q\otimes_RQ$ for some $\sigma\in S$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-30: body_md -->
(a) We clear denominators twice: first in an expression of $x/s$ as a linear combination whose coefficients annihilate $x/s$, and then in the annihilation relations themselves. By hypothesis,
\[x/s=\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^l(a_j/t_j)(y_j/u_j)\qquad\text{with }a_j\in R,\ y_j\in Q,\ t_j,u_j\in S\text{ and }(a_j/t_j)(x/s)=0.\]

\emph{The linear combination.} Put $t:=\prod_jt_ju_j\in S$ and $z_j:=\bigl(\prod_{i\neq j}t_iu_i\bigr)y_j\in Q$. Then $x/s=(\sum_ja_jz_j)/t$ in $S^{-1}Q$, so there is $v\in S$ with $v(tx-s\sum_ja_jz_j)=0$, i.e.\ $vtx=\sum_ja_j(vsz_j)$.

\emph{The annihilation relations.} $(a_j/t_j)(x/s)=0$ means $a_jx/(t_js)=0$, so $w_ja_jx=0$ for some $w_j\in S$. Put $w:=\prod_jw_j\in S$.

\emph{Conclusion.} Let $\sigma:=wvt\in S$. Multiplying $vtx=\sum_ja_j(vsz_j)$ by $w$ and using the relations $w_ja_jx=0$, we get
\[\sigma x=\textstyle\sum_j(wa_j)(vsz_j),\qquad (wa_j)\,\sigma x=\sigma\bigl(\prod_{i\neq j}w_i\bigr)(w_ja_jx)=0,\]
so $wa_j\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(\sigma x)$ and $\sigma x\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(\sigma x)Q$.

(b) Under the isomorphism above, the hypothesis says $(\sum_ix_i\otimes x_i)/s^2=0$ in $S^{-1}(Q\otimes Q)$, so $u\sum_ix_i\otimes x_i=0$ for some $u\in S$. Then $\sigma:=u$ works, since $\sum_iux_i\otimes ux_i=u\cdot u\sum_ix_i\otimes x_i$.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{lem:contr}
Let $S\subseteq R$ be a multiplicative set and $L$ a semiprime (resp.\ real) $S^{-1}R$-submodule of $S^{-1}M$. Then its contraction $L^c:=\{m\in M:\ m/1\in L\}$ is a semiprime (resp.\ real) submodule of $M$.
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-31: body_md -->
Put $Y:=S^{-1}M/L$, a semiprime (resp.\ real) $S^{-1}R$-module. We use two facts. First, $Y$ is also semiprime (resp.\ real) as an $R$-module: for $x\in Y$ we have $\operatorname{Ann}_R(x)Y\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{S^{-1}R}(x)Y$, as the image of $\operatorname{Ann}_R(x)$ in $S^{-1}R$ annihilates $x$; and the natural map $Y\otimes_RY\to Y\otimes_{S^{-1}R}Y$ sends $\sum_ix_i\otimes x_i$ to $\sum_ix_i\otimes x_i$. Second, submodules of semiprime (resp.\ real) modules are semiprime (resp.\ real) by Proposition~\ref{prop:closprop}(a). Now $m+L^c\mapsto m/1+L$ embeds $M/L^c$ into $Y$ by definition of $L^c$, so $M/L^c$ is a semiprime (resp.\ real) module.

<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{prop:loc}
Let $N$ be a submodule of an arbitrary $R$-module $M$ and $S\subseteq R$ a multiplicative set.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $M$ is a semiprime (resp.\ real) module, then the $S^{-1}R$-module $S^{-1}M$ is semiprime (resp.\ real).
\item[(b)] If $M_\mathfrak m$ is a semiprime (resp.\ real) $R_\mathfrak m$-module for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, then $M$ is a semiprime (resp.\ real) module.
\item[(c)] $S^{-1}(\operatorname{rad} N)=\operatorname{rad}(S^{-1}N)$ and $S^{-1}(\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad} N)=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}(S^{-1}N)$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, a submodule $N$ of $M$ is semiprime (resp.\ real) if and only if $N_\mathfrak p$ is a semiprime (resp.\ real) submodule of $M_\mathfrak p$ for every prime ideal $\mathfrak p$, if and only if this holds for every maximal ideal.
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-32: body_md -->
(a) Suppose $M$ is a semiprime module and $x/s\in\operatorname{Ann}_{S^{-1}R}(x/s)S^{-1}M$. Lemma~\ref{lem:denom}(a) gives $\sigma\in S$ with $\sigma x\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(\sigma x)M$, so $\sigma x=0$ and hence $x/s=0$. Suppose $M$ is a real module. Finitely many elements of $S^{-1}M$ can be written with a common denominator, say as $x_i/s$. If $\sum_i(x_i/s)\otimes(x_i/s)=0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:denom}(b) gives $\sigma\in S$ with $\sum_i\sigma x_i\otimes\sigma x_i=0$, so $\sigma x_i=0$ and $x_i/s=0$ for all $i$.

(b) We use twice that if $z\in M$ and $z/1=0$ in $M_\mathfrak m$ for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, then $z=0$ \cite[Lemma~2.8(a)]{Eis}. Suppose all $M_\mathfrak m$ are semiprime and let $q\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(q)M$, say $q=\sum_ja_jy_j$ with $a_jq=0$. For every maximal $\mathfrak m$ we have $q/1=\sum_j(a_j/1)(y_j/1)$ with $a_j/1\in\operatorname{Ann}_{R_\mathfrak m}(q/1)$, so $q/1=0$ in $M_\mathfrak m$. Hence $q=0$. Suppose all $M_\mathfrak m$ are real and $\sum_iq_i\otimes q_i=0$ in $M\otimes M$. Its image $\sum_i(q_i/1)\otimes(q_i/1)$ in $M_\mathfrak m\otimes_{R_\mathfrak m}M_\mathfrak m\cong(M\otimes M)_\mathfrak m$ vanishes, so $q_i/1=0$ for all $\mathfrak m$, and $q_i=0$.

(c) Let $L:=\operatorname{rad}(S^{-1}N)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:contr}, $L^c$ is a semiprime submodule of $M$ containing $N$, so $\operatorname{rad} N\subseteq L^c$ and hence $S^{-1}(\operatorname{rad} N)\subseteq S^{-1}(L^c)\subseteq L$. Conversely, $S^{-1}M/S^{-1}(\operatorname{rad} N)\cong S^{-1}(M/\operatorname{rad} N)$ is semiprime by (a), as $M/\operatorname{rad} N$ is semiprime, so $S^{-1}(\operatorname{rad} N)$ is a semiprime submodule containing $S^{-1}N$, and therefore $L\subseteq S^{-1}(\operatorname{rad} N)$. The same argument applies to $\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}$.

The last statement follows by applying (a) and (b) to $M/N$, as $(M/N)_\mathfrak p=M_\mathfrak p/N_\mathfrak p$.

<!-- proposition-11: body_md -->
\label{prop:modlocalclass}
Let $P\subseteq M$, put $Q=M/P$, and write $\operatorname{Supp}Q=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec} R:Q_\mathfrak p\neq0\}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $P$ is prime if and only if $\operatorname{Supp}Q$ has a smallest element $\mathfrak p$ under inclusion and $P_\mathfrak q$ is prime in $M_\mathfrak q$ for every $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Supp}Q$. In this case $\mathfrak p=(P:M)$ and $\operatorname{Supp}Q=V(\mathfrak p)$. If $Q$ is finitely generated, the support condition is equivalent to being nonempty and irreducible.
\item[(b)] $P$ is maximal if and only if $\operatorname{Supp}Q=\{\mathfrak m\}$ for one point $\mathfrak m$ and $P_\mathfrak m$ is maximal in $M_\mathfrak m$. The point $\mathfrak m$ is necessarily maximal.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-11 -->
<!-- proof-33: body_md -->
Recall that a nonzero module $Q$ is prime precisely when $ax=0$ implies $x=0$ or $aQ=0$.

(a) Suppose $Q$ is prime and put $\mathfrak p=\operatorname{Ann}_R Q$. This ideal is prime: if $abQ=0$ but $bQ\neq0$, apply the criterion to a nonzero element $bx$ to get $aQ=0$. The same criterion says that $Q$ is torsion-free over $R/\mathfrak p$. If $\mathfrak q\not\supseteq\mathfrak p$, an element of $\mathfrak p\setminus\mathfrak q$ kills $Q$ and becomes invertible in $R_\mathfrak q$, so $Q_\mathfrak q=0$. If $\mathfrak q\supseteq\mathfrak p$, every element outside $\mathfrak q$ acts injectively on $Q$, so $Q\to Q_\mathfrak q$ is injective. Moreover, $Q_\mathfrak q$ is torsion-free over $R_\mathfrak q/\mathfrak p R_\mathfrak q$: if $(a/s)(x/t)=0$ with $a\notin\mathfrak p$, then $uax=0$ for some $u\notin\mathfrak q$, and $ua\notin\mathfrak p$ forces $x=0$. Thus $Q_\mathfrak q$ is prime and $\operatorname{Supp}Q=V(\mathfrak p)$.

Conversely, let $\mathfrak p$ be the smallest support prime. We first show that $Q\to Q_\mathfrak p$ is injective. Suppose $0\neq x\in Q$ maps to zero. Choose a maximal ideal $\mathfrak q$ containing $\operatorname{Ann}_R(x)$; then $x/1\neq0$ in $Q_\mathfrak q$, so $\mathfrak q$ belongs to the support and $\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q$. Some $s\notin\mathfrak p$ satisfies $sx=0$. Primeness of $Q_\mathfrak q$ gives $sQ_\mathfrak q=0$. Since $\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q$, inverting all elements outside $\mathfrak p$ in $Q_\mathfrak q$ gives $Q_\mathfrak p$; but $s$ becomes invertible there, forcing $Q_\mathfrak p=0$, a contradiction.

Now let $ax=0$ with $x\neq0$. Injectivity gives $x/1\neq0$ in the prime module $Q_\mathfrak p$, so $(a/1)Q_\mathfrak p=0$. Injectivity then gives $aQ=0$. Hence $Q$ is prime. The forward implication identifies its annihilator and support as claimed.

If $Q$ is finitely generated, its support equals $V(\operatorname{Ann}_R Q)$. Indeed, $Q_\mathfrak q=0$ means that each of finitely many generators is killed by an element outside $\mathfrak q$; their product kills $Q$. This closed set has a smallest prime exactly when it is nonempty and irreducible.

(b) If $P$ is maximal, $Q$ is isomorphic to $R/\mathfrak m$ for a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, giving the stated support and simple localization. Conversely, $\mathfrak m$ is the smallest support prime and $Q_\mathfrak m$ is simple, hence prime. By (a), $\mathfrak m=\operatorname{Ann}_R Q$ and $V(\mathfrak m)=\{\mathfrak m\}$, so $\mathfrak m$ is maximal. Since $\mathfrak m Q=0$, every element outside $\mathfrak m$ already acts invertibly on $Q$; thus $Q\to Q_\mathfrak m$ is an isomorphism. Hence $Q$ is simple and $P$ maximal.

\item[(a)] $S^{-1}M$ is a semireal $S^{-1}R$-module if and only if $M$ has a real prime submodule $P$ with $(P:M)\cap S=\varnothing$.

\label{prop:stalk}
Let $X$ be Noetherian and $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$ quasi-coherent.
$\mathcal G$ is a semiprime (resp.\ real) subsheaf of $\mathcal F$ if and only if $\mathcal G_x$ is a semiprime (resp.\ real) submodule of $\mathcal F_x$ for every $x\in X$, if and only if this holds for every closed point $x$.

\[
(\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G)_x=\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal G_x),\qquad

By the last statement of Proposition~\ref{prop:loc}, $N$ is a semiprime (resp.\ real) submodule of $M$ if and only if all $N_\mathfrak p$ are. <!-- proposition-14: body_md -->
\label{prop:primestalk}
Let $X$ be Noetherian and $\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal F$ quasi-coherent with coherent quotient $\mathcal Q=\mathcal F/\mathcal P$. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\mathcal P$ is prime.
\item[(b)] $\operatorname{Ann}\mathcal Q=\mathcal I_Z$ for an integral closed subscheme $Z\subseteq X$, and $\mathcal Q$ is torsion-free as an $\mathcal O_Z$-module (on affine opens of $Z$, equivalently at its stalks).
\item[(c)] $\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal Q$ is nonempty and irreducible, and $\mathcal P_x$ is prime in $\mathcal F_x$ whenever $\mathcal Q_x\neq0$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-14 -->
<!-- proof-36: body_md -->
Coherence gives
\[
(\operatorname{Ann}\mathcal Q)_x=\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}(\mathcal Q_x),
\qquad \operatorname{Supp}\mathcal Q=V(\operatorname{Ann}\mathcal Q).
\]
These follow on affine opens from finite generation and localization of annihilators.

(a)$\Rightarrow$(b). Let $\eta$ be the point attached to $\mathcal Q$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:primesections}, and give $Z=\overline{\{\eta\}}$ its reduced structure. On every affine $U=\operatorname{Spec} A$ meeting $Z$, the module $\Gamma(U,\mathcal Q)$ is prime with annihilator $\mathfrak p_\eta$, hence torsion-free over $A/\mathfrak p_\eta$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:primequot}; on affines disjoint from $Z$ it is zero. These descriptions give (b).

(b)$\Rightarrow$(a). Any nonzero quasi-coherent $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal Q$ is coherent and torsion-free over $\mathcal O_Z$. Its generic stalk is nonzero, so its sections on every nonempty affine open of $Z$ are nonzero and torsion-free over the coordinate domain. Thus $\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal O_Z}\mathcal L=0$, giving $\mathcal I(\mathcal L)=\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal L=\mathcal I_Z=\mathcal I(\mathcal Q)$. The criterion \eqref{eq:sheafcrit} proves primeness.

(b)$\Rightarrow$(c). The support is $Z$, hence nonempty and irreducible. At each $x\in Z$, the stalk $\mathcal Q_x$ is nonzero and torsion-free over the domain $\mathcal O_{Z,x}=\mathcal O_{X,x}/\mathcal I_{Z,x}$, with annihilator $\mathcal I_{Z,x}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:primequot} gives primeness of $\mathcal P_x$.

(c)$\Rightarrow$(b). Put $\mathcal I=\operatorname{Ann}\mathcal Q$. On the support, $\mathcal I_x$ is prime by Lemma~\ref{lem:primequot}; elsewhere it is $\mathcal O_{X,x}$. Thus $\mathcal I$ is radical, and the closed subscheme $Z=V(\mathcal I)$ is reduced with nonempty irreducible underlying space, hence integral. The same lemma makes every stalk of $\mathcal Q$ on $Z$ torsion-free over $\mathcal O_{Z,x}$, proving (b).

<!-- remark-11: body_md -->
\label{rem:primesheaf}
Semiprimeness is local (Proposition~\ref{prop:stalk}), but primeness is not: for coherent quotients it additionally requires nonempty irreducible support (Proposition~\ref{prop:primestalk}(c)).

Primeness cannot be checked on stalks alone, or separately on an affine open cover. For $X=\operatorname{Spec} k[t]$, $\mathcal F=\mathcal O_X$ and $\mathcal P=\widetilde{(t(t-1))}$, the stalks at $(t)$ and $(t-1)$ are maximal ideals and all other stalks equal $\mathcal F_x$. Nevertheless, the quotient corresponds to $k[t]/(t(t-1))\cong k\oplus k$, which is semiprime but not prime. Both restrictions to the affine cover $D(t)\cup D(t-1)$ are prime; the missing condition is irreducibility of the support. The stalk condition in Proposition~\ref{prop:primestalk}(c) is not automatic: for $X=\operatorname{Spec} k[u,v]/(uv)$, the zero subsheaf of $\mathcal O_X$ is semiprime, but its stalk at the origin is not prime because the reduced local ring has zero divisors.

\label{cor:maxstalk}

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:sheafsemireallocal}
Let $X$ be Noetherian and $\mathcal Q$ quasi-coherent. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\mathcal Q$ is semireal, i.e.\ it has a nonzero real quasi-coherent quotient.
\item[(b)] $\Gamma(U,\mathcal Q)$ is a semireal $\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_X)$-module for some affine open $U\subseteq X$.

\end{enumerate}

<!-- example-4: body_md -->
\label{ex:projective}
Let $X=\mathbb P^2_\mathbb R$, $\mathcal F=\mathcal O_X(2)^2$, $Z=V(x_0^2+x_1^2)$ and $p=[0:0:1]$. Define
\[
\Theta=\begin{pmatrix}x_0&-x_1\\x_1&x_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H&=\operatorname{im}(\Theta:\mathcal O_X(1)^2\to\mathcal F),\\
\mathcal G&=\operatorname{im}(\Theta^2:\mathcal O_X^2\to\mathcal F).
\end{aligned}
\]
The adjugate identities and nonzero determinants give injectivity, so $\mathcal H\cong\mathcal O_X(1)^2$ and $\mathcal G\cong\mathcal O_X^2$. We shall prove
\[
\mathcal G\subsetneq\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G=\mathcal H\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G=\mathcal I_p\mathcal F\subsetneq\mathcal F.
\]

\emph{Geometry and affine description.} The conic $Z$ is integral over $\mathbb R$, with unique singular and real point $p$. Over $\mathbb C$ it splits into the conjugate lines $V(x_0\pm ix_1)$, meeting at $p$. Its complement of $p$ is
\[
Z\setminus\{p\}=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R[s,t]/(1+s^2)\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[t],
\qquad (s,t)=(x_1/x_0,x_2/x_0).
\]
Thus its other closed points have residue field $\mathbb C$, and its generic point $\eta$ has residue field $\mathbb C(t)$.

On $U_j=D_+(x_j)=\operatorname{Spec} A_j$, trivialize $\mathcal O_X(k)$ by $x_j^k$. Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_0&=\mathbb R[s,t], &(s,t)&=(x_1/x_0,x_2/x_0), &w_0&=1+is,\\
A_1&=\mathbb R[r,h], &(r,h)&=(x_0/x_1,x_2/x_1), &w_1&=r+i,\\
A_2&=\mathbb R[u,v], &(u,v)&=(x_0/x_2,x_1/x_2), &w_2&=u+iv.
\end{aligned}
\]
Identify $A_j^2$ with $B_j:=A_j\otimes_\mathbb R\mathbb C$ via $(a,b)\mapsto a+ib$. The maps become multiplication by $w_j$ and $w_j^2$, so the sections of $\mathcal H$ and $\mathcal G$ are $w_jB_j$ and $w_j^2B_j$. Moreover,
\[
B_0/(w_0)\cong\mathbb C[t],\qquad
B_1/(w_1)\cong\mathbb C[h],\qquad
B_2/(w_2)\cong\mathbb C[v],
\]
with $s\mapsto i$, $r\mapsto-i$ and $u\mapsto-iv$. Their annihilators over $A_j$ are $(1+s^2)$, $(r^2+1)$ and $(u^2+v^2)$: in the last case, divisibility of a real polynomial by $u+iv$ also implies divisibility by $u-iv$, hence by their product. These quotients are torsion-free over the coordinate domains of $Z$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:primestalk}(b), $\mathcal H$ is prime, hence semiprime.

\emph{Radicals and fibre images.} At any point $x\in U_j$, the fibre matrix is
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}a&-b\\b&a\end{pmatrix},\qquad
(a,b)=(x_0/x_j,x_1/x_j)(x).
\]
It is invertible outside $Z$ and zero at $p$. On $Z\setminus\{p\}$, $a^2+b^2=0$ and $a\neq0$, so $B$ has rank $1$ and $B^2=2aB$. Hence $\mathcal G(x)=\mathcal H(x)$ at every point. Since $\mathcal H$ is semiprime, Theorem~\ref{thm:sheaffibre}(b) gives
\[
\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G=\operatorname{rad}\mathcal H=\mathcal H.
\]
Among real closed points, only $p$ has $\mathcal G(x)\neq\mathcal F(x)$, and $\mathcal G(p)=0$. Theorem~\ref{thm:sheaffibre}(b) therefore gives $\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G=\mathcal I_p\mathcal F$. The resulting dimensions of images in $\mathcal F(x)$, over $\kappa(x)$, are:
\begin{center}\small\renewcommand{1.3}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
point $x$ & $\mathcal G(x)$ & $(\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G)(x)$ & $(\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G)(x)$ & $\mathcal F(x)$ \\ \hline
$x\notin Z$ & $2$ & $2$ & $2$ & $2$ \\
$x\in Z\setminus\{p\}$ & $1$ & $1$ & $2$ & $2$ \\
$x=p$ & $0$ & $0$ & $0$ & $2$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The last two rows, together with $w_j^2B_j\subsetneq w_jB_j$, prove the strict inclusions above. Equality of the first two columns illustrates Theorem~\ref{thm:sheaffibre}: a subsheaf and its semiprime radical have the same images in every fibre.

These images need not be the intrinsic fibres $\mathcal E_x/\mathfrak m_x\mathcal E_x$. At $p$, the intrinsic fibres of $\mathcal G$ and $\mathcal H$ have dimension $2$, whereas that of $\mathcal I_p\mathcal F$ has dimension $4$, since $(\mathcal I_p\mathcal F)_p=\mathfrak m_p^{\oplus2}$ and $\mathfrak m_p=(u,v)\mathcal O_{X,p}$. Thus $\mathcal H_p\subsetneq(\mathcal I_p\mathcal F)_p$ although both images in $\mathcal F(p)$ vanish.

\emph{Sections.} Let $S_k$ denote the real homogeneous forms of degree $k$ and put $w=x_0+ix_1$, so $w_j=w/x_j$. Identifying pairs of forms with $S_k\otimes_\mathbb R\mathbb C$, we obtain the following table; the last column includes dimensions over $\mathbb R$.
\begin{center}\small\renewcommand{1.3}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
sections over & $U_0$ & $U_1$ & $U_2$ & $X$ \\ \hline
$\mathcal G$ & $w_0^2B_0$ & $w_1^2B_1$ & $w_2^2B_2$ & $w^2\mathbb C$ \ ($2$) \\
$\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G$ & $w_0B_0$ & $w_1B_1$ & $w_2B_2$ & $w(S_1\otimes\mathbb C)$ \ ($6$) \\
$\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G$ & $B_0$ & $B_1$ & $(u,v)B_2$ & $\{f\in S_2\otimes\mathbb C:\ f(p)=0\}$ \ ($10$) \\
$\mathcal F$ & $B_0$ & $B_1$ & $B_2$ & $S_2\otimes\mathbb C$ \ ($12$)
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The global dimensions follow from $\mathcal G\cong\mathcal O_X^2$, $\mathcal H\cong\mathcal O_X(1)^2$, and $\dim S_2=6$, with vanishing at $p$ imposing one condition on each component. Since $\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=\mathbb R$ is a real field, every subspace of $\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)$ is a real, hence semiprime, $\mathbb R$-submodule, so both module radicals of $\Gamma(X,\mathcal G)$ equal $\Gamma(X,\mathcal G)$ itself; they do not recover the sections of the sheaf radicals, of dimensions $6$ and $10$.

For every open $U\subseteq X$, the supports of the quotients give
\[
(\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G)|_U\neq\mathcal F|_U\iff U\cap Z\neq\varnothing,\qquad
(\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G)|_U\neq\mathcal F|_U\iff p\in U.
\]
In particular, removing $p$ makes the real radical the whole sheaf, while the ordinary radical remains proper.

\emph{Multiplicities.} Fibre images do not distinguish $\mathcal G$ from $\mathcal H$, but quotient lengths do. At $\eta$, the local ring $D=\mathcal O_{X,\eta}=(A_2)_{(\pi)}$ is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $\pi=u^2+v^2$. The matrices $\Theta$ and $\Theta^2$ have unit entries $u$ and $u^2-v^2\equiv2u^2\pmod\pi$, and determinants $\pi$ and $\pi^2$. Row and column operations over $D$ therefore give
\[
(\mathcal F/\mathcal G)_\eta\cong D/(\pi^2),\qquad
(\mathcal F/\mathcal H)_\eta\cong D/(\pi).
\]
The lengths over the respective local rings are:
\begin{center}\small\renewcommand{1.3}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
quotient & support & length at $\eta$ & length at $p$ \\ \hline
$\mathcal F/\mathcal G$ & $Z$ & $2$ & $\infty$ \\
$\mathcal F/\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G$ & $Z$ & $1$ & $\infty$ \\
$\mathcal F/\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G$ & $\{p\}$ & $0$ & $2$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $\infty$ means no finite composition series: the first two quotients have one-dimensional support at $p$, while the third is the skyscraper $\mathbb R^2$. The ordinary radical removes multiplicity along $Z$; the real radical leaves a quotient supported only at $p$.

\emph{Direct witnesses.} The failures of semiprimeness of $\mathcal G$ and reality of $\mathcal H$ also follow from the intrinsic definitions. For $\mathcal Q=\mathcal F/\mathcal G$, the adjugate identity gives $\mathcal I_Z^2\mathcal Q=0$, but $\mathcal L=\mathcal I_Z\mathcal Q\neq0$, since $u^2+v^2\notin(u+iv)^2B_2$. Thus $\mathcal I_Z\mathcal L=0$ and $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal I_Z\mathcal Q$, violating \eqref{eq:sheafidealcrit}. For $\mathcal T=\mathcal F/\mathcal H$, the image of $(x_0,x_1):\mathcal T(-1)\to\mathcal T^{\oplus2}$ is nonzero and totally isotropic, as its tensor pairing factors through multiplication by $x_0^2+x_1^2$, which kills $\mathcal T$; so $\mathcal H$ is not real by Definition~\ref{def:realsheaf}.

\emph{Prime and maximal intersections.} Since $\mathcal G(\eta)=\mathcal H(\eta)$, $\mathcal F/\mathcal H$ is torsion-free on $Z$, and $(\mathcal F/\mathcal H)_\eta$ is killed by $\mathfrak m_\eta$, and since $\mathcal I_p\mathcal F$ consists of the sections vanishing at $p$, we have
\[
\mathcal H=\mathcal P_{\eta,\mathcal G(\eta)},\qquad
\mathcal I_p\mathcal F=\mathcal P_{p,0}.
\]
These are the smallest prime and real prime subsheaves containing $\mathcal G$. The maximal subsheaves over $\mathcal G$ are $\mathcal P_{p,L}$ for lines $L\subset\mathbb R^2$, and $\mathcal P_{x,\mathcal G(x)}$ for non-real closed $x\in Z$. Intersecting all of them gives $\mathcal H$; intersecting just the real ones gives $\mathcal I_p\mathcal F$.

Finally, for $\mathcal F_\infty=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal G_\infty=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}\mathcal G$, the direct-sum formulas \eqref{eq:radcoproduct} on affine opens give
\[
\operatorname{rad}\mathcal G_\infty=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}\mathcal H,\qquad
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad}\mathcal G_\infty=\mathcal I_p\mathcal F_\infty.
\]
This retains the chart distinctions and gives a real prime quotient at $p$ with infinite-dimensional fibre, illustrating the intersection results for noncoherent quasi-coherent sheaves.

<!-- question-2: body_md -->
\label{q:effective}
The radicals admit iterative descriptions, which suggest an effective version of the Nullstellens\"atze. For a submodule $N$ of an $R$-module $M$, let $N^{(1)}$ be generated by all $m\in M$ with $m\in(N:m)M$, and let $N^{[1]}$ be generated by all $m_i$ occurring in finite families with $\sum_i\bar m_i\otimes\bar m_i=0$ in $(M/N)\otimes_R(M/N)$. Starting with $N^{(0)}=N^{[0]}=N$, iterate these operations:
\[
N^{(k+1)}=(N^{(k)})^{(1)},\qquad
N^{[k+1]}=(N^{[k]})^{[1]}.
\]
For arbitrary modules,
\[
\operatorname{rad} N=\bigcup_{k\ge0}N^{(k)},\qquad
\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad} N=\bigcup_{k\ge0}N^{[k]}.
\]
The ordinary formula for finitely generated $M$ is \cite[Theorem~2.7]{Ary}. For $M=R$, the operations adjoin the $r$ with $r^2\in N$, respectively the $r_i$ with $\sum_i r_i^2\in N$.

For finitely generated modules, Marcelo and Rodr\'iguez characterize the prime radical as the degree-one part of an ideal radical in the symmetric algebra \cite[Corollary~2.4 and Proposition~3.2]{MR}; Section~3.2 of their paper uses this to compute generators for submodules of polynomial free modules. The question here concerns bounds on the iterative constructions above.

For finitely generated modules over Noetherian rings both chains stabilize, but the number of steps is not bounded in terms of $R$ and $M$ alone: for $R=M=\mathbb R[x]$ and $N=(x^{2^h})$ one has
\[
N^{(j)}=N^{[j]}=(x^{2^{h-j}})\qquad(0\le j\le h),
\]
so reaching $\operatorname{rad} N=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\text{-}rad} N=(x)$ takes exactly $h$ steps. For $R=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ and $M=R^m$, are there effective bounds on the number of steps in terms of $n$, $m$, the number of generators of $N$ and their degrees, in the spirit of the effective Nullstellensatz?
