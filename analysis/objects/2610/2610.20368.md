<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:bijection}
The construction \eqref{eq:v1}--\eqref{eq:vL}, from a graph monomial to a
final node, and the rank data, from a final node to a graph monomial, are
well defined and inverse to each other.  They give a bijection
\begin{equation}
V_L\longleftrightarrow {\cal S},
\qquad
T\;\longmapsto\;(a,d_2,\ldots,d_L)\;\longmapsto\;
m_T=(C_1)^a(C_2)^{d_2-1}\cdots(C_L)^{d_L-1},
\label{eq:bijection}
\end{equation}
and in particular \(|{\cal S}|=D_L=\dim{\cal A}_L\).
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
\emph{The construction \eqref{eq:v1}--\eqref{eq:vL} is well defined.}  The
bound on \(a\) in \eqref{eq:Mset} guarantees that \(R_1\) has an
\((a+1)\)-st element.  For \(i\ge2\), \(v_{i-1}\) was chosen in
\(R_{i-1}\subseteq S^{(i-1)}_{[d_i,\ldots,d_L]}\), and by
\eqref{eq:SLm1}--\eqref{eq:Si} this membership means that
\(R_i(v_{i-1};d_{i+1},\ldots,d_L)\) has at least \(d_i\) elements.

\emph{The rank data are well defined and give a graph monomial.}  At each
step, \(v_i(T)\) is the \(d_i\)-th element of \(R_i\), so \(R_i\) has at least
\(d_i\) elements, which says that \(v_{i-1}(T)\) lies in the admissible set
used at the next step to the left.  At the last step \(v_1(T)\in R_1\), so
\(R_1=S^{(1)}_{[d_2,\ldots,d_L]}\) is non-empty, the tuple
\((d_2,\ldots,d_L)\) is admissible, and \(a\le|R_1|-1\); thus \(m_T\in{\cal S}\).

\emph{The two maps are inverse.}  At each layer both directions use the same
admissible set, since \(R_i\) depends only on \(v_{i-1}\) and on
\(d_{i+1},\ldots,d_L\).  Starting from a tuple, the construction chooses
\(v_i\) as the \(d_i\)-th element of \(R_i\) (the \((a+1)\)-st for \(i=1\)), and
reading off the position of \(v_i\) in the same \(R_i\) returns \(d_i\) (resp.\
\(a+1\)); reading from right to left, each set is recovered from the integers
already returned.  Conversely, starting from \(T\), the construction applied to
its rank data chooses, layer by layer from the left, the nodes
\(v_1(T),\ldots,v_L(T)\).  Hence the maps are mutually inverse, and the second
arrow in \eqref{eq:bijection} simply records the rank data as the exponent
vector \((a,d_2-1,\ldots,d_L-1)\).  Finally \(|V_L|=D_L\), and \(D_L=\dim{\cal A}_L\)
since the final nodes label the projector basis of \({\cal A}_L\).
<!-- end proof-1 -->

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:final-layer-excised}
Let \(\Gamma\) be the degeneracy graph, with \(L\geq2\), and let
\(1\le d\le d_{\max}\), where \(d_{\max}\) is the largest number of children of
a node of \(V_{L-1}\).
The final-layer-excised graph \(\Gamma_{[d]}\) is the graph with \(L-1\) layers obtained
from \(\Gamma\) by
\begin{itemize}
\item deleting the final layer \(V_L\);
\item keeping at layer \(L-1\) only the nodes of \(S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}\), i.e.\ the
  nodes with at least \(d\) children;
\item keeping at each layer \(i<L-1\) only the nodes having a descendant in
  \(S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}\).
\end{itemize}
At each layer the surviving nodes are relabelled \(1,2,\ldots\), keeping
their relative order.  This preserves which nodes are siblings and the order
among siblings.  We identify each surviving node with the node of \(\Gamma\)
it comes from, so that sets of nodes of \(\Gamma_{[d]}\) may be compared with
sets of nodes of \(\Gamma\).  The final layer of \(\Gamma_{[d]}\) is
\(S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}\).

\label{lem:restrict}
Let \(1\le d\le d_{\max}\).  For \(1\le i\le L-2\) and all \(d_{i+1},\ldots,d_{L-1}\),
\begin{equation}
S^{(i)}_{[d_{i+1},\ldots,d_{L-1},\,d]}
=
S^{(i)}_{[d_{i+1},\ldots,d_{L-1}]}(\Gamma_{[d]}).
\label{eq:Srestrict}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for \(1\le i\le L-1\),
\begin{equation}
R_i(v_{i-1};d_{i+1},\ldots,d_{L-1},d)
=
R_i(v_{i-1};d_{i+1},\ldots,d_{L-1})(\Gamma_{[d]}),
\label{eq:Rrestrict}
\end{equation}
where for \(i=L-1\) the right-hand side is the final-layer set
\(R_{L-1}(v_{L-2})(\Gamma_{[d]})=\operatorname{Ch}_{L-1}(v_{L-2})\cap S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}\) of
\eqref{eq:RL}.

\(S^{(L-2)}_{[d_{L-1},d]}\) exactly when
\(|\operatorname{Ch}_{L-1}(u)\cap S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}|\ge d_{L-1}\).  The set
\(\operatorname{Ch}_{L-1}(u)\cap S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}\) is the set of children of \(u\) in
\(\Gamma_{[d]}\), which lie in its final layer, so this is the condition
\(u\in S^{(L-2)}_{[d_{L-1}]}(\Gamma_{[d]})\).  (Such a \(u\) has a descendant
in \(S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}\), so it is a node of \(\Gamma_{[d]}\).)  The inductive step is the same argument one layer to the left, using \eqref{eq:Srestrict} at layer
\(i+1\).  Equation \eqref{eq:Rrestrict} follows by intersecting with
\(\operatorname{Ch}_i(v_{i-1})\), using \eqref{eq:R1}--\eqref{eq:Ri}.

\label{cor:factor}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:newton}
Let \(f\) be a function on \(V_L\).  There are unique numbers
\(\Delta_{q,1}f,\ldots,\Delta_{q,n}f\), the Newton coefficients of \(f\), such
that
\begin{equation}
f(\xi_k)=\sum_{d=1}^{k}\pi_{q,d}(\xi_k)\,\Delta_{q,d}f,
\qquad 1\le k\le n ,
\label{eq:newtoninterp}
\end{equation}
and \(\Delta_{q,d}f\) depends only on the values of \(f\) at the first \(d\)
children of \(q\).  The \(n\times n\) matrix \(W_q\) with entries
\((W_q)_{k,d}=\pi_{q,d}(\xi_k)\), the Newton--Vandermonde matrix of
\(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n\), is the Newton evaluation matrix of the
one-layer graph formed by the children of \(q\), with their eigenvalues of
\(C_L\), and
\begin{equation}
\det W_q=\prod_{1\le k<m\le n}(\xi_m-\xi_k).
\label{eq:fact2}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Since \(\pi_{q,d}(\xi_k)=0\) for \(k<d\), the matrix \(W_q\) is lower
triangular, with diagonal entries \(\pi_{q,k}(\xi_k)=\prod_{m<k}(\xi_k-\xi_m)\),
which are non-zero because siblings have distinct eigenvalues.  The equations
\eqref{eq:newtoninterp} form the triangular system with matrix \(W_q\), so
they have a unique solution, and their first \(d\) equations determine
\(\Delta_{q,1}f,\ldots,\Delta_{q,d}f\).  The determinant of \(W_q\) is the
product of its diagonal entries.  Finally, the graph Newton polynomials of the
one-layer graph formed by the children of \(q\) are the \(\pi_{q,d}(C_L)\), as
in the one-layer example of Section~\ref{sec:evalNewton}, so \(W_q\) is its
Newton evaluation matrix.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:degree}
Let \(1\le d\le n\).  Suppose that \(f(\xi_k)=h(\xi_k)\), \(1\le k\le d\), for
a polynomial \(h\) of degree at most \(e\), and let \(c\) be the coefficient of
\(\xi^{e}\) in \(h\) (possibly zero).  Then
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{q,d}f=
\begin{cases}
0, & d-1>e,\\
c, & d-1=e.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:fact1}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
In both cases \(e\le d-1\).  Since \(\pi_{q,1},\ldots,\pi_{q,e+1}\) are monic of
degrees \(0,\ldots,e\), we can write \(h=\sum_{d'=1}^{e+1}a_{d'}\,\pi_{q,d'}\),
with \(a_{e+1}=c\).  Evaluating at \(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_d\) and comparing with the
first \(d\) equations of \eqref{eq:newtoninterp}, which determine
\(\Delta_{q,1}f,\ldots,\Delta_{q,d}f\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:newton}, gives
\(\Delta_{q,d'}f=a_{d'}\) for \(d'\le e+1\) and \(\Delta_{q,d'}f=0\) for
\(e+1<d'\le d\).
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The proof is by induction on the number of layers.  Each step excises the
final layer: the Newton expansion acts among the children of the nodes of the
current penultimate layer, and the diagonal blocks are the Newton evaluation
matrices of the graphs \(\Gamma_{[d]}\), which have one layer fewer.
Unwinding the induction therefore excises the layers \(L,L-1,\ldots,2\) in
turn, each step being applied to every graph produced by the previous one,
until one-layer graphs remain, whose Newton evaluation matrices are
triangular.

For \(L=1\), \(R_1=V_1\), the graph Newton polynomials
are the ordinary Newton polynomials, and the matrix is triangular with the
stated diagonal.

Let \(L\ge2\).  By Corollary~\ref{cor:factor}, both the rows (points \(T'\))
and the columns (polynomials \(N_T\)) of the matrix are labelled by pairs
\((q,d)\) with \(q\in S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}\).  For each \(q\in V_{L-1}\), expand the
rows belonging to the children of \(q\) in the Newton basis of their
eigenvalues, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:newton}, and let \({\cal N}'\) be the matrix of
Newton coefficients: its entry in row \((q,d)\) and column \(T\) is
\({\cal N}'_{(q,d),T}=\Delta_{q,d}N_T\), the \(d\)-th Newton coefficient of the
column \(T'\mapsto N_T(T')\) on the children of \(q\).  Then
\begin{equation}
{\cal N}=W{\cal N}',
\label{eq:NPfactor}
\end{equation}
where \(W\) is block diagonal, with one block for the children of each
\(q\in V_{L-1}\), and the block for \(q\) is the one-layer Newton--Vandermonde
matrix \(W_q\) of Lemma~\ref{lem:newton}.  By \eqref{eq:fact2},
\(\det W=\prod_q\det W_q=\prod_{q}\prod_{k<m}(\xi_m-\xi_k)\).

Consider the entry \({\cal N}'_{(q,d),T}\), where \(T\) is the final node labelled \((q',d')\).  On the children of \(q\), the eigenvalues of
\(C_1,\ldots,C_{L-1}\) are those of the path to \(q\), so by \eqref{eq:Nfactor}
\(N_T\) restricts to a polynomial in \(\xi\) of degree at most \(d'-1\), whose
coefficient of \(\xi^{d'-1}\) is \(N_{q'}(\Gamma_{[d']})(q)\); this value may
vanish, in which case the degree is smaller.  By the degree property,
Lemma~\ref{lem:degree},
\begin{equation}
{\cal N}'_{(q,d),T}=0 \quad (d>d'),
\qquad
{\cal N}'_{(q,d),T}=N_{q'}(\Gamma_{[d]})(q)={\cal N}(\Gamma_{[d]})_{q,q'}\quad (d=d').
\end{equation}
Ordering rows and columns by \(d\), the matrix \({\cal N}'\) is block triangular.  The
diagonal block for \(d\) has rows and columns labelled by \(S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}\),
the final layer of \(\Gamma_{[d]}\), and is the Newton evaluation matrix of
\(\Gamma_{[d]}\).

The diagonal blocks are labelled by the values of \(d\) for which
\(S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}\) is non-empty.  Since these sets decrease as \(d\) increases,
these values are \(d=1,\ldots,d_{\max}\), where \(d_{\max}\) is the largest
number of children of a node of \(V_{L-1}\): there are \(d_{\max}\) diagonal
blocks.  The block for \(d\) has size \(|S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}|\), the number of nodes
of \(V_{L-1}\) with at least \(d\) children.  Thus, if the numbers of children
of the nodes of \(V_{L-1}\) are arranged as a partition of \(D_L\), the sizes of
the diagonal blocks are the parts of the conjugate partition.  Hence, by
\eqref{eq:NPfactor}, and since the block triangular form of \({\cal N}'\) is
reached by a simultaneous reordering of rows and columns,
\begin{equation}
\det{\cal N}=\det W\,\det{\cal N}'
=\prod_{d=1}^{d_{\max}}\det{\cal N}(\Gamma_{[d]})
\ \times\prod_{q\in V_{L-1}}\ \prod_{\substack{b<b'\\ b,b'\in\operatorname{Ch}_L(q)}}
\bigl(x^{(L)}_{b'}-x^{(L)}_{b}\bigr).
\label{eq:detNblocks}
\end{equation}
Each diagonal block contributes its determinant \(\det{\cal N}(\Gamma_{[d]})\)
exactly once.  The product over \(q\in V_{L-1}\) acts only on the second
factor, which is \(\det W\) by \eqref{eq:fact2}, with one Vandermonde factor
\(\det W_q\) for each set of siblings in \(V_L\).

By induction,
\(\det{\cal N}(\Gamma_{[d]})=\prod_{q\in S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}}{\cal N}(\Gamma_{[d]})_{q,q}\).
On the other hand, by \eqref{eq:Nfactor}, for \(T\) the \(d\)-th child of
\(q\),
\begin{equation}
{\cal N}_{T,T}={\cal N}(\Gamma_{[d]})_{q,q}\prod_{\substack{w<T\\ w\in\operatorname{Ch}_L(q)}}
\bigl(x^{(L)}_T-x^{(L)}_w\bigr).
\end{equation}
Since the final nodes \(T\) correspond to the pairs \((q,d)\) with
\(q\in S^{(L-1)}_{[d]}\) (Corollary~\ref{cor:factor}), the product over all
\(T\in V_L\) of the first factor is \(\prod_{d}\det{\cal N}(\Gamma_{[d]})\), and
the product of the second factor is the second factor of
\eqref{eq:detNblocks}.  This proves \eqref{eq:diagonalproduct}.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:divdiff}
The Newton coefficients have a classical closed form: \(\Delta_{q,d}f\) is the
divided difference of \(f\) over the first \(d\) children of \(q\)
\cite{deBoor,StoerBulirsch},
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{q,d}f
=\sum_{k=1}^{d}
\frac{f(\xi_k)}{\prod_{m\le d,\,m\ne k}(\xi_k-\xi_m)} ,
\label{eq:divdiff}
\end{equation}
so that \(W_q^{-1}=Q_q\), the matrix with entries
\((Q_q)_{d,k}=\prod_{m\le d,\,m\ne k}(\xi_k-\xi_m)^{-1}\) for \(k\le d\) and
\(0\) for \(k>d\).  For the first few values of \(d\),
\begin{align}
\Delta_{q,1}f&=f(\xi_1),\nonumber\\
\Delta_{q,2}f&=\frac{f(\xi_2)-f(\xi_1)}{\xi_2-\xi_1},\nonumber\\
\Delta_{q,3}f&=\frac{f(\xi_1)}{(\xi_1-\xi_2)(\xi_1-\xi_3)}
+\frac{f(\xi_2)}{(\xi_2-\xi_1)(\xi_2-\xi_3)}
+\frac{f(\xi_3)}{(\xi_3-\xi_1)(\xi_3-\xi_2)} ,
\label{eq:divdiff123}
\end{align}
and in general they can be computed recursively: writing
\(f[\xi_j,\ldots,\xi_{j+d-1}]\) for the divided difference over the
consecutive children from the \(j\)-th to the \((j+d-1)\)-th, with
\(f[\xi_k]=f(\xi_k)\), one has \(\Delta_{q,d}f=f[\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_d]\) and, for
\(d\ge2\),
\begin{equation}
f[\xi_j,\ldots,\xi_{j+d-1}]
=\frac{f[\xi_{j+1},\ldots,\xi_{j+d-1}]-f[\xi_j,\ldots,\xi_{j+d-2}]}
{\xi_{j+d-1}-\xi_j}.
\label{eq:divdiffrec}
\end{equation}
For \(d=2\) this is the difference quotient in \eqref{eq:rowopexample}.  The
closed form is not needed in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:newtondet}; it is
used in Section~\ref{sec:inverse-evaluation}, where \({\cal N}\) is inverted.

\label{rem:L3}
Let \(D=(2,3,4)\).  Node \(1\in V_1\) has one child, node \(1\in V_2\), which

vanish at \(T_0\).  The polynomial with monomial \(C_2\) (rank data
\(d_2=2,d_3=1\), for which only node \(2\in V_1\) is admissible) is
\(C_2-x^{(2)}_{w}\) with \(w\) the first child of node \(2\in V_1\); for

\(2\in V_1\).  The
polynomial with monomial \(C_3\) (rank data \(d_2=1,d_3=2\), for which only
node \(1\in V_1\) is admissible) is \(C_3-x^{(3)}_{w'}\) with \(w'\) the first
child of node \(1\in V_2\); for generic eigenvalues it vanishes at \(T_0\) only if \(T_0\)
descends from node \(1\in V_1\).  Both cannot hold.  We return to this
example in Remark~\ref{rem:nested}.

\(3\): the only Newton expansion with more than one term is that of the rows
of \(T_1,T_2\), and the diagonal blocks are the evaluation matrices of

have \(2,1,1\) children, so there are \(d_{\max}=2\) blocks, of sizes \(3\) and

induction step once more to \(\Gamma_{[1]}\) gives

\det{\cal N}=\bigl(x^{(3)}_{T_2}-x^{(3)}_{T_1}\bigr)
\bigl(x^{(1)}_2-x^{(1)}_1\bigr)\bigl(x^{(2)}_3-x^{(2)}_2\bigr).
\end{equation}

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:vdm}
Corollary~\ref{cor:vdm} is also the form in which the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:newtondet} produces the determinant.  When the recursion has
reached a graph whose final layer is layer \(i\), labelled by the tuple
\((d_{i+1},\ldots,d_L)\) as in the paragraph ``Where the factors arise'' of
Section~\ref{sec:general} (for \(i=L\) this is \(\Gamma\) itself), the children
of \(v_{i-1}\) in that graph form the set \(R_i(v_{i-1};d_{i+1},\ldots,d_L)\),
by Lemma~\ref{lem:restrict} applied repeatedly.  In that graph,
\(v_{i-1}\) plays the role of the node \(q\in V_{L-1}\) in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:newtondet}, and the tuple \((d_{i+1},\ldots,d_L)\) records the
values of \(d\) chosen at the successive steps of the recursion,
\(\Gamma\to\Gamma_{[d_L]}\to\bigl(\Gamma_{[d_L]}\bigr)_{[d_{L-1}]}\to\cdots\).
For \(i\ge2\), \(\operatorname{Vdm}_{R_i(v_{i-1};d_{i+1},\ldots,d_L)}(x^{(i)})\)
is then the determinant \eqref{eq:fact2} of the matrix \(W_{v_{i-1}}\) for
these children in the factorisation \eqref{eq:NPfactor} of the Newton
evaluation matrix of that graph; for \(i=1\) it is the determinant of the
Newton evaluation matrix of that graph, which has one layer.  Every factor in
\eqref{eq:KRdetVdm} is therefore the determinant of a one-layer
Newton--Vandermonde matrix.

The simplest special case is a two-fold tensor product.  Let \(L=2\), with
\(m=D_1\) nodes in \(V_1\), suppose that every node of \(V_1\) has the same
number \(n\) of children, and that for each \(k\) the \(k\)-th children of all
the nodes of \(V_1\) have the same eigenvalue \(y_k\) of \(C_2\).  The joint
eigenvalues then form the product set
\(\{x^{(1)}_1,\ldots,x^{(1)}_m\}\times\{y_1,\ldots,y_n\}\), and \({\cal A}_2\) is
the tensor product of the algebras generated by \(C_1\) and by \(C_2\).  The
graph monomials are \(C_1^aC_2^b\), \(0\le a<m\), \(0\le b<n\), and, with
rows in path order and columns in the order of the bijection
\eqref{eq:bijection}, \({\cal M}\) is the Kronecker product of the Vandermonde
matrices of the two sets of eigenvalues.  In \eqref{eq:KRdetVdm} every
\(R_1(d_2)\), \(1\le d_2\le n\), is all of \(V_1\), and every
\(R_2(v_1)=\operatorname{Ch}_2(v_1)\) carries the eigenvalues \(y_1,\ldots,y_n\), so that
\begin{equation}
\det{\cal M}=\Bigl(\operatorname{Vdm}_{V_1}\bigl(x^{(1)}\bigr)\Bigr)^{n}
\Bigl(\prod_{1\le k<l\le n}(y_l-y_k)\Bigr)^{m},
\end{equation}
the determinant of a Kronecker product.  Degeneracy graphs are much more
general: the numbers of children, and the eigenvalues of the children, vary
from node to node.  The evaluation matrix \({\cal M}\) is then not a Kronecker
product, and the Newton evaluation matrix \({\cal N}\) is in general not
triangular in any ordering (Remark~\ref{rem:L3}), but the determinant keeps
the form \eqref{eq:KRdetVdm} of a product of Vandermonde determinants.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rem:nested}
A node \(u\in V_i\) is admissible for continuation data
\((d_{i+1},\ldots,d_L)\) exactly when this tuple is a point of \({\cal Y}(u)\).
Hence the condition of Remark~\ref{rem:nontri}, that every admissible set be
an initial segment of the children, holds when \({\cal Y}(u)\supseteq{\cal Y}(u')\) for
all siblings \(u<u'\).  For \(L=2\) the Young diagrams of the nodes of \(V_1\)
are the intervals \(\{1,\ldots,p_{1,a}\}\), and nesting is the condition
\(p_{1,1}\ge p_{1,2}\ge\cdots\) of Remark~\ref{rem:nontri}.  In the graph of
Remark~\ref{rem:L3}, the Young diagrams of the two nodes of \(V_1\) are those
of the partitions \((1,1)\) and \((2)\) (Figure~\ref{fig:L3ferrers}).  They are
not nested in either order, which is why, for generic eigenvalues, no
labelling makes the evaluation matrix triangular there.  Their overlap is one cube, the exponent of
\(x^{(1)}_2-x^{(1)}_1\) found in Remark~\ref{rem:L3}.

Proposition~\ref{prop:overlap} is a reformulation of the count

these Young diagrams, including the operations on them induced by grafting and

For the graph of Figure~\ref{fig:L2}, write
\(x_k=x^{(1)}_k\) and \(y_k=x^{(2)}_{T_k}\).  The graph monomials are
\(1,C_1,C_2\) and the Newton polynomials are \(N_{T_1}=1\),
\(N_{T_2}=C_1-x_1\), \(N_{T_3}=C_2-y_2\), so that, with rows and columns in
the order \(T_1,T_2,T_3\),
\begin{equation}
U=\begin{pmatrix}1&-x_1&-y_2\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}  The final nodes
are \(T_1=(1,1)\), \(T_2=(2,1)\), \(T_3=(2,2)\) and \(d_{\max}=2\).  We compute
the column of \({\cal N}^{-1}\) for \(T_3\), and hence \(P_{T_3}\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(T_3\) is the second child of node \(2\), so \(g(1,1)=g(2,1)=0\) and
  \(g(2,2)=1/(y_3-y_2)\).  This is the difference quotient in
  \eqref{eq:rowopexample}.
\item The entries of \({\cal N}'\) above the diagonal blocks are
  \({\cal N}'_{(q,1),T_3}=N_{T_3}(\text{first child of }q)\), equal to \(y_1-y_2\) for
  \(q=1\) and \(0\) for \(q=2\): the top right entry of \eqref{eq:E23after}.
\item For \(d=2\), the diagonal block is the \(1\times1\) matrix \((1)\) of
  \(\Gamma_{[2]}\), so \(c_{T_3}=g(2,2)=1/(y_3-y_2)\).  For \(d=1\),
  \begin{equation}
  h_1(1)=0-(y_1-y_2)\,c_{T_3}=-\frac{y_1-y_2}{y_3-y_2},\qquad
  h_1(2)=0 ,
  \end{equation}
  and the diagonal block is the \(2\times2\) Newton--Vandermonde matrix of
  \(\Gamma_{[1]}\) in \eqref{eq:E23after}, whose inverse is the matrix of
  divided differences: \(c_{T_1}=h_1(1)\) and
  \(c_{T_2}=\bigl(h_1(2)-h_1(1)\bigr)/(x_2-x_1)\).
\end{enumerate}
By \eqref{eq:projector-Newton-expansion},
\begin{equation}
P_{T_3}=\frac{(C_2-y_2)-(y_1-y_2)+(y_1-y_2)\dfrac{C_1-x_1}{x_2-x_1}}{y_3-y_2}
=\frac{(C_2-y_2)-(y_1-y_2)\,\dfrac{C_1-x_2}{x_1-x_2}}{y_3-y_2}.
\end{equation}
The same steps for \(T_1\) give \(g(1,1)=1\) and all other \(g=0\), hence
\(c_{T_3}=0\), \(c_{T_1}=1\), \(c_{T_2}=-1/(x_2-x_1)\) and
\(P_{T_1}=(C_1-x_2)/(x_1-x_2)\).  Finally
\(P_{T_2}=1-P_{T_1}-P_{T_3}\).  These are the expansions
\eqref{eq:projector-monomial-expansion} of the three projectors in the
monomial basis \(1,C_1,C_2\).
