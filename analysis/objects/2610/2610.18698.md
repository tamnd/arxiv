<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:rejection}
Let $L_Q=\lceil(1-Q)^{-1}\rceil$ and $H_Q=(1-Q)^{-L_Q}$. Repeatedly draw $J\sim\operatorname{Geom}(r)$ and accept $J$ with probability $k_J(A,Q)/H_Q$. The accepted value has law \eqref{eq:NB}; the procedure terminates almost surely. Each acceptance probability uses only a finite product.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $(A;Q)_n\leq1$,
\[
k_n(A,Q)\leq\frac1{(Q;Q)_\infty}.
\]
Moreover,
\begin{align*}
\log\frac1{(Q;Q)_\infty}
&=\sum_{m\geq1}\frac{Q^m}{m(1-Q^m)}
\leq\frac{-\log(1-Q)}{1-Q}
\leq L_Q[-\log(1-Q)].
\end{align*}
Thus $k_n\leq H_Q$. The probability of proposing and accepting $n$ is
$(1-r)r^n k_n/H_Q$, which normalizes to \eqref{eq:NB}. The acceptance probability per trial is
$(1-r)K_{A,Q}(r)/H_Q>0$, proving almost-sure termination.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:bulk-kernel}
Given an admissible valley $\kappa$, draw $G\sim G_{ab}(xy)$ independently and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-size}
m=\lvert \lambda\rvert+\lvert \mu\rvert-\lvert \kappa\rvert+G.
\end{equation}
Then sample a partition $\nu$ according to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-shape}
\mathbb P(\nu\mid\lambda,\mu,\kappa,G)=
\frac{\mathbf 1_{\{\lvert \nu\rvert=m\}}u_a(\nu/\lambda)v_b(\nu/\mu)}
{S_m^{a,b}(\lambda,\mu)}.
\end{equation}
Let $T_{a,b}^{x,y}(\kappa,\nu\mid\lambda,\mu)$ denote the
probability that the above procedure produces $\nu$ from $\kappa$,
with the outer partitions $\lambda,\mu$ fixed. For an admissible
input $\kappa$, put
\[
g=|\nu|+|\kappa|-|\lambda|-|\mu|.
\]
Then
\[
T_{a,b}^{x,y}(\kappa,\nu\mid\lambda,\mu)
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{c_g^{ab}(xy)^g}{C_{ab}(xy)}
\frac{u_a(\nu/\lambda)v_b(\nu/\mu)}
     {S_{|\nu|}^{a,b}(\lambda,\mu)},
& g\geq0 \text{ and } c_g^{ab}>0,\\[8pt]
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:bulk-transport}
The kernel in Definition~\ref{def:bulk-kernel} satisfies
\begin{align}
&\sum_\kappa V_b(\lambda/\kappa;y)U_a(\mu/\kappa;x)
 T_{a,b}^{x,y}(\kappa,\nu\mid\lambda,\mu)\nonumber\\
&\hspace{35mm}=\frac{U_a(\nu/\lambda;x)V_b(\nu/\mu;y)}{C_{ab}(xy)}.
\label{eq:bulk-balance}
\end{align}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix $\nu$ and put $m=\lvert \nu\rvert$. The sums over $\kappa$ below are restricted to partitions satisfying
$v_b(\lambda/\kappa)u_a(\mu/\kappa)>0$.

If $u_a(\nu/\lambda)v_b(\nu/\mu)=0$, both sides of
\eqref{eq:bulk-balance} vanish by the definition of $T_{a,b}^{x,y}$.
Otherwise, $S_m^{a,b}(\lambda,\mu)>0$ by \eqref{eq:Sbulk}.

For such an input $\kappa$, put
\[
g_\kappa=m+\lvert \kappa\rvert-\lvert \lambda\rvert-\lvert \mu\rvert.
\]
The corresponding monomial satisfies
\[
y^{\lvert \lambda\rvert-\lvert \kappa\rvert}
x^{\lvert \mu\rvert-\lvert \kappa\rvert}(xy)^{g_\kappa}
=
x^{m-\lvert \lambda\rvert}y^{m-\lvert \mu\rvert}.
\]
Using the explicit formula for $T_{a,b}^{x,y}$ and homogeneity,
we obtain
\begin{align*}
&\sum_\kappa
V_b(\lambda/\kappa;y)U_a(\mu/\kappa;x)
T_{a,b}^{x,y}(\kappa,\nu\mid\lambda,\mu)\\
&\quad=
\frac{U_a(\nu/\lambda;x)V_b(\nu/\mu;y)}
     {C_{ab}(xy)S_m^{a,b}(\lambda,\mu)}
\sum_\kappa
v_b(\lambda/\kappa)u_a(\mu/\kappa)c_{g_\kappa}^{ab}\\
&\quad=
\frac{U_a(\nu/\lambda;x)V_b(\nu/\mu;y)}
     {C_{ab}(xy)}.
\end{align*}
Here coefficients with negative indices are zero, and the last
equality follows from \eqref{eq:bulk-coeff-id}.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:boundary-kernel}
For a right reflection, let $h=v$ and $c=c_r$; for a left reflection, let $h=u$ and $c=c_l$. Given the reflected boundary partition $\kappa$, its neighboring interior partition $\eta$, and the parameter $x$ \emph{before} reflection, draw

\mathbb P(\nu\mid\eta,\kappa,B)=

\label{lem:local-global}

and $B_e(\kappa)$ be the nonnegative local weights of the original
and reduced models, respectively. Suppose that, for each $e$,
a Markov kernel $T_e$ satisfies

=
\frac{A_e(\nu)}{f},
\]
where $f>0$ is independent of $e$.

\[
Z_A=\sum_{e,\nu}H(e)A_e(\nu),
\qquad
Z_B=\sum_{e,\kappa}H(e)B_e(\kappa)
\]
are finite and positive. Then the update that keeps $e$ fixed
and applies $T_e$ to the local variables transports the normalized
measure with weights $H(e)B_e(\kappa)$ to the normalized measure
with weights $H(e)A_e(\nu)$.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->

and using the stochasticity of $T_e$, we obtain

Z_A
=
f\sum_{e,\kappa}H(e)B_e(\kappa)
  \sum_\nu T_e(\kappa,\nu)
=
fZ_B.
\]

\frac{H(e)B_e(\kappa)}{Z_B}\,T_e(\kappa,\nu)

=
\frac{H(e)A_e(\nu)}{Z_A},
\]

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:period}
After a plus or minus letter has visited both boundaries in the
order described above and returned to its starting slot, the
ordering of the labelled letters is the same as before, and the
travelling letter has recovered its original sign. Every
opposite-sign swap encountered during this procedure is a
reduction of the form
\[
+,-\ \longrightarrow\ -,+.
\]
The parameter carried by the travelling letter is multiplied by
$u^2v^2$. Consequently, after all $N$ labelled letters have been
processed once, the sorted labelled word is restored, with every
parameter multiplied by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-def}
r=(uv)^2.
\end{equation}
One period uses $2N$ reflections and exactly $2N(N-1)$ adjacent
swaps.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Consider a minus letter in slot $j$. Since the word is sorted,
moving it to the left boundary crosses only minus letters. The
left reflection changes its sign to $+$ and multiplies its
parameter by $u^2$.

The letter then moves from the left boundary to the right boundary.
Whenever it crosses a letter of the opposite sign, the adjacent
pair is
\[
+,-\ \longrightarrow\ -,+.
\]
All remaining swaps are between two plus letters. At the right
boundary, the second reflection changes the travelling letter
back to $-$ and multiplies its parameter by $v^2$.

On the return from the right boundary to slot $j$, every
opposite-sign swap again has the form
\[
+,-\ \longrightarrow\ -,+,
\]
and all remaining swaps are between two minus letters.

The three parts of the route use
\[
(j-1)+(N-1)+(N-j)=2(N-1)
\]
adjacent swaps. The other labelled letters never swap with one
another, so their relative order is unchanged. When the travelling
letter returns to slot $j$, the full ordering is therefore restored.
The two reflections restore its original sign and multiply its
parameter by $u^2v^2$.

The argument for a plus letter is the same with left and right
interchanged. Hence processing all $N$ labelled letters uses
$2N$ reflections and exactly $2N(N-1)$ adjacent swaps, and
multiplies every parameter by $u^2v^2$.

\label{thm:TV}

\leq Cr^K+\rho(\lvert \Lambda\rvert>M).
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:TV-limit}
\lim_{\substack{K\to\infty\\ M\to\infty}}
\left\lVert \widehat\pi_{K,M}-\pi_w\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=0.
\end{equation}

For a partition sequence
$\boldsymbol\lambda=(\lambda^{(0)},\ldots,\lambda^{(N)})
\in\mathbb{Y}^{N+1}$,  define its total skew degree
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degree}
D(\boldsymbol\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^N
\left|\lvert \lambda^{(i)}\rvert-\lvert \lambda^{(i-1)}\rvert\right|.
\end{equation}
By homogeneity, its weight under uniform edge scaling is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scale-weight}
W_s(\boldsymbol\lambda)=s^{D(\boldsymbol\lambda)}W_1(\boldsymbol\lambda).
\end{equation}
On the allowed support, consecutive partitions differ by adding or
removing a horizontal or vertical strip.
If $D(\boldsymbol\lambda)=0$, every consecutive pair has the same size,
so each such strip is empty and
$\lambda^{(0)}=\cdots=\lambda^{(N)}$.
The converse is immediate. Therefore the $D=0$ contribution is exactly $\mathfrak Z(z)$. Since $\mathcal Z_1<\infty$, dominated convergence gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z-continuity}
\mathcal Z_s\longrightarrow\mathcal Z_0\qquad(s\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
Moreover, for $0\leq s\leq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z-linear}
0\leq\mathcal Z_s-\mathcal Z_0
=\sum_{D(\boldsymbol\lambda)\geq1}s^{D(\boldsymbol\lambda)}W_1(\boldsymbol\lambda)
\leq s(\mathcal Z_1-\mathcal Z_0).
\end{equation}

Let $E_M$ be the event that every partition is equal and their common size is at most $M$. Under $\xi_s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:event-prob}
\xi_s(E_M)=\frac{\mathfrak Z_M(z)}{\mathcal Z_s},\qquad
\xi_s(\,\cdot\mid E_M)=\rho_M.
\end{equation}
For any probability measure $\mu$ and event $E$ of positive probability,
$\left\lVert \mu-\mu(\cdot\mid E)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=1-\mu(E)$. This follows by splitting the half-sum defining total variation into $E$ and its complement. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-TV}
\left\lVert \xi_s-\rho_M\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=1-\frac{\mathfrak Z_M(z)}{\mathcal Z_s}.
\end{equation}
Both $\rho_M$ and $\xi_{r^K}$ are processed using the same reverse
updates $\mathcal R_K$. The total variation error after these updates
is at most the error before them; see
\cite[Proposition~3(d)]{RobertsRosenthal2004}.
Using \eqref{eq:exact-replay}, \eqref{eq:output-law}, and
\eqref{eq:initial-TV}, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \|\widehat\pi_{K,M}-\pi_w\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
  &=\|\rho_M\mathcal R_K-\xi_{r^K}\mathcal R_K\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\\
  &\le\|\rho_M-\xi_{r^K}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\\
  &=1-\frac{\mathfrak Z_M(z)}{\mathcal Z_{r^K}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:TV-exact}.

Finally,
\begin{align*}
1-\frac{\mathfrak Z_M(z)}{\mathcal Z_s}
&=\frac{\mathcal Z_s-\mathcal Z_0}{\mathcal Z_s}
 +\frac{\mathcal Z_0-\mathfrak Z_M(z)}{\mathcal Z_s}\\
&\leq s\left(\frac{\mathcal Z_1}{\mathcal Z_0}-1\right)
 +\left(1-\frac{\mathfrak Z_M(z)}{\mathfrak Z(z)}\right).
\end{align*}
The final parenthesis is $\rho(\lvert \Lambda\rvert>M)$. This proves \eqref{eq:TV-rate}; the seed tail tends to zero because $\rho$ is a probability measure. Almost-sure termination was established after Algorithm~\ref{alg:sampler}.

  =\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{1-A^n}{1-Q^n}\frac{y^n}{n}

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:expected-running-time}
Fix $q,t,u,v,x_0\in(0,1)$ and suppose that $0<x_i\le x_0$ for every letter.
Let $N\ge1$ and $K,M\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$.

at unit cost. If $\mathcal T_{K,M}$ is the total operation count, including
history construction and initial sampling, then
\[

  \le C_0\left[(K+1)(N+1)^2e^{c_0N}
    +(M+1)^3e^{c_p\sqrt M}\right],
  \qquad c_p=\pi\sqrt{2/3},
\]

and multiply every edge parameter by $e^\delta$.
The partition-function estimate in Appendix~B, applied to these
modified parameters, gives
\[
  \widetilde Z\le A e^{BN^2},
\]
with $A,B>0$ depending only on the fixed parameters.

At any stage of the reverse reconstruction, let
$\nu(\boldsymbol\lambda)$ be the probability that the sampler has
partition sequence $\boldsymbol\lambda$, and let
$W(\boldsymbol\lambda)$ be its unnormalized weight for the current word.
Then
\[
  \nu(\boldsymbol\lambda)\le W(\boldsymbol\lambda).
\]
Indeed, initially, a constant sequence $(\Lambda,\ldots,\Lambda)$
with $\lvert \Lambda\rvert\le M$ has probability equal to its terminal
weight divided by $\mathfrak Z_M(uv)\ge1$; all other sequences have
probability zero. Each reverse update transports the current
unnormalized weight to the next unnormalized weight divided by a
scalar factor at least one. Thus the inequality is preserved
throughout the reconstruction.

\[
  S\le \lvert \lambda^{(0)}\rvert+D(\boldsymbol\lambda).

\[
  \widetilde W(\boldsymbol\lambda)
  =W(\boldsymbol\lambda)
    e^{\delta(\lvert \lambda^{(0)}\rvert+D(\boldsymbol\lambda))}
  \ge W(\boldsymbol\lambda)e^{\delta S}.

\[
  \mathbb E_\nu[e^{\delta S}]
  =\sum_{\boldsymbol\lambda}
     \nu(\boldsymbol\lambda)e^{\delta S}
  \le\sum_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\widetilde W(\boldsymbol\lambda)
  =\widetilde Z
  \le Ae^{BN^2}.
\]

size $m\le S$. For a same-sign update, every candidate has size
at most the larger of the two fixed outer partition sizes,
which is also at most $S$. By \eqref{eq:partition-count},
the number of candidates is therefore at most
\[
  \sum_{n=0}^{S}p(n)\le(S+1)e^{c_p\sqrt S}.
\]
Although $S$ is random, the exponential-moment bound proved above

Using \eqref{eq:branching-products} and

$O(S+1)$ box factors. Each factor contains a fixed number of powers
with exponents at most $S$. Evaluating these powers by repeated
multiplication takes $O(S+1)$ operations per factor, giving
$O((S+1)^2)$ operations per candidate. Thus one shape draw costs at most
$C_1(S+1)^3e^{c_p\sqrt S}$, including candidate generation,

H\"older's inequality gives
\[
  \mathbb E_\nu[(S+1)^3e^{c_p\sqrt S}]
  \le C_2e^{a^2(N+1)/(4\delta)}

  \le C_3e^{c_0N}.

$C_4L_Ke^{c_0N}$.

$C_5(M+1)^3e^{c_p\sqrt M}$.

partition, and scalar sampling cost $O(N+L_K)$ in expectation, as shown
in Section~5.2. Finally,
$L_K\le\lfloor N^2/4\rfloor+2KN^2$ proves the claim.

$\mathfrak Z(z)$. Fix $z<\eta<1$ and repeat independent trials:

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->

=|\log(1-tw)-\log(1-qw)|
\leq\frac{|q-t|}{1-\vartheta}q^a t^\ell.

\leq\frac{d(\lambda)}{(1-q)(1-t)}.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:finiteZ}
Under \eqref{eq:regime}, $1\leq Z_w<\infty$ for every finite labelled word and every pair of boundary types in $\mathcal C$.

Any nonzero branching coefficient in \eqref{eq:branching-products} is a product of $b$-ratios over subsets of cells. Therefore
\[
u_a(\lambda/\mu),\ v_a(\lambda/\mu)
\leq\exp\bigl(\gamma(q,t)(\sqrt{\lvert \lambda\rvert}+\sqrt{\lvert \mu\rvert})\bigr).
\]
The same bound applies to $R$ after conjugation and parameter exchange, since $\gamma(q,t)=\gamma(t,q)$. Likewise, each nonzero $\beta_c(\lambda)$ and $\tau_c(\lambda)$ is at most $e^{\gamma(q,t)\sqrt{\lvert \lambda\rvert}}$.

\[

\]
For fixed sizes there are at most $\prod_i p(m_i)$ partition sequences. Also $m_i\leq m_0+D$. The signs of the word and the values of $m_0,d_1,\ldots,d_N$ determine all sizes; invalid size sequences can be discarded. Using $v^{m_N}\leq1$ and $x_*=\max_i x_i<1$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z-majorant}

u^{m_0}x_*^D\exp\bigl(A\sqrt{m_0+D}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $A=(N+1)(2\gamma(q,t)+\pi\sqrt{2/3})$. Choose

$A\sqrt n\leq\delta n+A^2/(4\delta)$, the right side of \eqref{eq:Z-majorant} is at most
\[
\frac{\exp(A^2/(4\delta))}{(1-ue^\delta)(1-x_*e^\delta)^N}<\infty.
\]
