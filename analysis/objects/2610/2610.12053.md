<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:green-transform}
Set $g_o=g(o,o)$ and
\begin{equation}
 h(x)=\frac{g(o,x)}{g_o}=H(x,o),\qquad
 \widehat c_{xy}=c_{xy}h(x)h(y).
 \label{eq:green-transform}
\end{equation}
The network $(T,\widehat c)$ is recurrent. For every vertex $x$,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_o(x)^{-2}
 =1+\frac1{g_o}\sum_{e\in[o,x]}\frac1{\widehat c_e}.
 \label{eq:green-resistance}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_o(\xi_n)\downarrow0\qquad\text{for every }\xi\in\partial T.
 \label{eq:ray-correlation-decay}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The function $h$ is positive, equals one at $o$, and is harmonic
off $o$. For $x\ne o$, the transition probabilities of the
transformed network satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \widehat P(x,y)=P(x,y)\frac{h(y)}{h(x)}\qquad(y\sim x).
 \label{eq:transformed-transition}
\end{equation}
Up to its first visit to $o$, this is the original walk conditioned
to hit $o$. Every departure from $o$ therefore returns almost surely,
which proves recurrence.

Fix an edge from $p=x^-$ to $x$. The voltage
formula and the Green function killed on hitting $p$ give
\begin{equation}
 H(x,p)=\frac{c_{px}}{c_{px}+C_x^+}=\frac{h(x)}{h(p)},\qquad
 g(x,x)-\frac{g(p,x)^2}{g(p,p)}=\frac1{c_{px}+C_x^+}.
 \label{eq:edge-hitting-green}
\end{equation}
Both identities in \eqref{eq:edge-hitting-green} follow by finite
network exhaustion, including when $C_x^+=0$. Since
$g(p,x)=H(x,p)g(p,p)$, division yields
\begin{equation}
 \frac{g(x,x)}{h(x)^2}-\frac{g(p,p)}{h(p)^2}
 =\frac1{c_{px}h(p)h(x)}=\frac1{\widehat c_{px}}.
 \label{eq:green-increment}
\end{equation}
Summing \eqref{eq:green-increment} along $[o,x]$ and using
$g(x,x)/h(x)^2=g_o\rho_o(x)^{-2}$ proves
\eqref{eq:green-resistance}.
Every infinite ray has infinite total resistance in a recurrent
network: otherwise the unit flow supported on that ray would have
finite energy, contradicting the electrical transience criterion
\cite[Theorem~2.11]{LP}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:green-resistance} therefore gives
\eqref{eq:ray-correlation-decay}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:branching}
For any cable point $a$, conditional on $X(a)$, the fields in the components of
$\widetilde T\setminus\{a\}$ are independent. Along a geodesic directed
away from $a$, with parameter $t=-\log\rho(a,\cdot)^2$, the field
has the law of the stationary diffusion, restricted to the range of
this parameter,
\begin{equation}
 dX_t=\sqrt{2X_t}\,dB_t+(\alpha-X_t)\,dt,\qquad
 X_0\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha,1).
 \label{eq:CIR}
\end{equation}
Here $B$ is a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion, independent
of $X_0$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For $0<q\le1$, let $Q_q(z,\cdot)$ be the transition law of
\eqref{eq:CIR} after time $-\log q$, started at $z\ge0$, and write
$Q_qf(z)=\int f(y)Q_q(z,dy)$. Its Laplace transform is
\begin{equation}
 Q_q(e^{-\lambda\,\cdot})(z)
 =[1+(1-q)\lambda]^{-\alpha}
   \exp\left\{-\frac{qz\lambda}{1+(1-q)\lambda}\right\},\qquad\lambda\ge0.
 \label{eq:CIR-LT}
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $Y$ solves the stochastic differential equation
\begin{equation}
 dY_s=\sqrt{2Y_s}\,dB_s+\alpha\,ds,
 \label{eq:BESQ}
\end{equation}
driven by a standard Brownian motion, then $2Y$ is a squared-Bessel
process of dimension $2\alpha$ and the time change
\begin{equation}
 X_t=e^{-t}Y_{e^t-1}
 \label{eq:bessel-time-change}
\end{equation}
solves \eqref{eq:CIR} in law, with the same initial value.
Formula~\eqref{eq:CIR-LT} follows from the squared-Bessel
transition transform; see~\cite[Sections~4.1--4.2]{Lupu18}.
Integration against $\operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha,1)$ shows that this law is invariant.

Fix finitely many points, including $a$. Their spanning tree is
the union of the geodesics joining these points, and we add its branch
points to the finite set. By \eqref{eq:multiplicative}, the normalized
Green matrix $R$ on this enlarged finite set has entries given by
products of correlations across the edges of its spanning tree.
Starting at $a$ with law $\operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha,1)$, use the kernels $Q_q$
independently on the branches, where $q$ is the squared correlation
across an edge. We verify that the resulting field has transform
$\det(I+RD)^{-\alpha}$.

Let $l$ be a leaf with parent $p$, and put $q=R_{pl}^2$ and
$A=1+(1-q)\lambda_l$. Let $R_0$ and $D_0$ be the matrices
with the coordinate $l$ deleted, and let $e_p$ be the coordinate
vector at $p$ in the remaining space. By \eqref{eq:CIR-LT},
integrating out $X(l)$ multiplies the transform by $A^{-\alpha}$
and replaces $\lambda_p$ by $\lambda_p+q\lambda_l/A$. The same
recursion holds for the determinant: subtracting $\sqrt q$ times
row $p$ from row $l$ and eliminating the leaf coordinate gives
\begin{equation}
 \det(I+RD)
 =A\det\left(I+R_0\left[
        D_0+\frac{q\lambda_l}{A}e_pe_p^{\mathsf T}\right]\right).
 \label{eq:leaf}
\end{equation}
Induction in \eqref{eq:leaf} proves the transform identity, and
\eqref{eq:normalized-laplace} identifies the finite-dimensional laws.
Continuity on the cables then identifies the fields on countable
dense subsets, and hence their laws as continuous functions.
The independent construction on the branches proves the conditional
independence assertion.

\label{prop:two-point}
Let $u\ne v$ be cable points and $0<\alpha<1$. Then
\begin{equation}
 p_\alpha(u,v)=I_{\rho(u,v)^2}(1-\alpha,\alpha).
 \label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
More precisely, for $q=\rho(u,v)^2$ and $z>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_\alpha^T(u\leftrightarrow v\mid X(u)=z)
 =F_{\alpha,q}(z):=\frac1{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}
       \int_0^{zq/(1-q)}r^{-\alpha}e^{-r}\,dr.
 \label{eq:conditional-connection}
\end{equation}
With $F_{\alpha,q}(0)=0$, this function is nonnegative, increasing
and concave on $[0,\infty)$. If
$\beta=2(1-\alpha)$, there are constants
$0<c_\alpha\le C_\alpha<\infty$, depending only on $\alpha$, such that
\begin{equation}
 c_\alpha\rho(u,v)^\beta\le p_\alpha(u,v)
 \le C_\alpha\rho(u,v)^\beta.
 \label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
The bounds in \eqref{eq:power} also hold for $u=v$. For $\alpha\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_\alpha^T(\widetilde T\text{ is a single loop cluster})=1.
 \label{eq:full-connection}
\end{equation}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:branching} and \eqref{eq:positive-path}, the

diffusion \eqref{eq:CIR}, started at $z$, avoids zero up to time
$-\log q$. By \eqref{eq:bessel-time-change}, this is the probability

$T_0=\inf\{s\ge0:Y_s=0\}$. The squared-Bessel hitting-time formula gives
\begin{equation}
 T_0\overset d=\frac{z}{G_{1-\alpha}},
 \qquad G_{1-\alpha}\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(1-\alpha,1);
 \label{eq:hit-zero}
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:concavity}
Let $\alpha>0$ and let $k\ge1$ be an integer. If
$Z\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha,1)$ and $f_1,\ldots,f_k$ are bounded
nonnegative increasing concave functions on $[0,\infty)$, then
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E\prod_{i=1}^k f_i(Z)
 \le\frac{\mathbb E Z^k}{(\mathbb E Z)^k}\prod_{i=1}^k\mathbb E f_i(Z).
 \label{eq:concavity}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For any $f=f_i$, concavity and nonnegativity at zero imply that $f(z)/z$ is
decreasing for $z>0$. Under the size-biased law
$dQ=Z\,d\mathbb P/\mathbb E Z$, it has nonpositive covariance with every
increasing function $\psi$ for which the terms are integrable.
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E[f(Z)\psi(Z)]\le\frac{\mathbb E f(Z)}{\mathbb E Z}\mathbb E[Z\psi(Z)].
 \label{eq:size-biased-comparison}
\end{equation}
Apply \eqref{eq:size-biased-comparison} successively to
$f_1,\ldots,f_k$. At the $i$th step,
$\psi$ is a product of the remaining increasing nonnegative factors
and $Z^{i-1}$, so it is increasing. Gamma moments ensure
integrability. This yields \eqref{eq:concavity}.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:quasi}
Let $0<\alpha<1$ and write $p(x)=p_\alpha(o,x)$ for $x\in V$.
For $x,y\in V$ and $a=x\wedge y$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_\alpha^T(o\leftrightarrow x,\ o\leftrightarrow y)
 \le K_\alpha\frac{p(x)p(y)}{p(a)},\qquad
 K_\alpha=\frac{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)}{\alpha^2}.
 \label{eq:quasi}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Suppose first that $o,x,y$ lie in three different components of
$\widetilde T\setminus\{a\}$. For $v\in\{o,x,y\}$, set
$f_v(z)=F_{\alpha,\rho(a,v)^2}(z)$. Conditional on $Z=X(a)$,
the three events $\{a\leftrightarrow v\}$ are independent with
increasing concave probabilities $f_v(Z)$ by
\eqref{eq:conditional-connection}. Their simultaneous
occurrence is precisely the joint event in \eqref{eq:quasi}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:concavity}, with
$\mathbb E Z^3/(\mathbb E Z)^3=K_\alpha$, gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_\alpha^T(o\leftrightarrow x,\ o\leftrightarrow y)
 \le K_\alpha p_\alpha(o,a)p_\alpha(a,x)p_\alpha(a,y).
 \label{eq:three-connections}
\end{equation}
Two increasing functions of the same scalar variable have
nonnegative covariance. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 p(x)=\mathbb E[f_o(Z)f_x(Z)]
 \ge p_\alpha(o,a)p_\alpha(a,x),
 \label{eq:pair-positive-correlation}
\end{equation}
and the same inequality holds with $y$ in place of $x$. Substitution
in \eqref{eq:three-connections} proves \eqref{eq:quasi}. If $a=o$,
apply \eqref{eq:concavity} to $f_x$ and $f_y$; the moment constant
$(\alpha+1)/\alpha$ is at most $K_\alpha$.
If $a=x$ or $a=y$, the connection events are nested and the bound
holds with constant one.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:capacity-criterion}
Let $0<\alpha<1$ and $\beta=2(1-\alpha)$. If
\begin{equation}
 \inf_\Pi\sum_{x\in\Pi}\rho_o(x)^\beta=0,
 \label{eq:zero-cutset-sum}
\end{equation}
there is almost surely no unbounded cluster.
If $\mathcal E_\beta(\mu)<\infty$ for some unit
flow, then
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_\alpha^T(o\leftrightarrow\infty)
 \ge \frac{c_\alpha}{K_\alpha\mathcal E_\beta(\mu)}>0.
 \label{eq:survival}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
An infinite root cluster contains a ray and so meets every cutset.
The union bound and \eqref{eq:power} imply
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_\alpha^T(o\leftrightarrow\infty)
 \le C_\alpha\sum_{x\in\Pi}\rho_o(x)^\beta.
 \label{eq:cutset-first-moment}
\end{equation}
Under \eqref{eq:zero-cutset-sum}, taking the infimum in
\eqref{eq:cutset-first-moment} proves that the root cluster is bounded
almost surely.
Every unbounded cluster contains a vertex. If one occurred with
positive probability, countability would give a fixed vertex $u$
with positive probability of belonging to one. The events
$\{o\leftrightarrow u\}$ and $\{u\leftrightarrow\infty\}$ are
increasing in the Poisson soup, and are positively associated.
The Poisson Harris inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_\alpha^T(o\leftrightarrow\infty)
 \ge p_\alpha(o,u)\mathbb P_\alpha^T(u\leftrightarrow\infty).
 \label{eq:root-transfer}
\end{equation}
Since $p_\alpha(o,u)>0$, \eqref{eq:root-transfer} rules out an
unbounded cluster at any fixed vertex, and hence anywhere in $\widetilde T$.

To prove \eqref{eq:survival}, we give the weighted second-moment
argument directly; see~\cite[Section~5.3 and Theorem~5.19]{LP}.
Fix a unit flow $\mu$ with $\mathcal E_\beta(\mu)<\infty$ and set
\begin{equation}
 Z_n=\sum_{|x|=n}\frac{\mu(x)}{p_\alpha(o,x)}
             \mathbf 1_{\{o\leftrightarrow x\}}.
 \label{eq:weighted-connection-count}
\end{equation}
Flow conservation gives $\mathbb E_\alpha^T Z_n=1$. By
\eqref{eq:quasi} and \eqref{eq:power},
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_\alpha^T[Z_n^2]
 &\le K_\alpha\sum_{|x|=|y|=n}
       \frac{\mu(x)\mu(y)}{p_\alpha(o,x\wedge y)}\\
 &\le\frac{K_\alpha}{c_\alpha}
       \sum_{|x|=|y|=n}\mu(x)\mu(y)\rho_o(x\wedge y)^{-\beta}
 \le\frac{K_\alpha}{c_\alpha}\mathcal E_\beta(\mu).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:connection-second-moment}
\end{equation}
For the last inequality, if $\xi\in\partial T_x$ and
$\eta\in\partial T_y$, then
$\delta_G(\xi,\eta)\le\rho_o(x\wedge y)$. This also holds when
$x=y$, so the diagonal terms are included in the energy bound.
Let $A_n$ be the event that the root cluster meets generation $n$.
Since $\{Z_n>0\}\subseteq A_n$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_\alpha^T(A_n)\ge\mathbb P_\alpha^T(Z_n>0)
 \ge\frac{(\mathbb E_\alpha^T Z_n)^2}{\mathbb E_\alpha^T[Z_n^2]}
 \ge\frac{c_\alpha}{K_\alpha\mathcal E_\beta(\mu)}.
 \label{eq:generation-second-moment}
\end{equation}
By connectedness, local finiteness and properness, the events $A_n$
decrease to $\{o\leftrightarrow\infty\}$. Letting $n\to\infty$
proves \eqref{eq:survival}.

\label{lem:frostman}

 \label{eq:frostman}

$B_{\delta_G}(\xi,t)$ is $\partial T_{\xi_n}$, where $n$ is the first index

proves \eqref{eq:green-boundary-dimension}.

infimum is downward closed, so $\delta>0$. The max-flow/min-cut

 \label{eq:flow-correlation-bound}
\end{equation}

\eqref{eq:ray-correlation-decay}. Thus every countable cover of
the boundary by sets of diameter less than one has sum of

\end{equation}

 \label{eq:energy-integral-bound}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:energy-integral-bound} into
\eqref{eq:energy-along-rays} proves \eqref{eq:frostman}.

\label{lem:pruning}
The tree $T^\infty$, with its inherited conductances, is transient,

\begin{equation}

 \label{eq:pruning}

energy. Thus minimizing the energy over extensions of a function

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:GW-capacity}
For $\mathbf P_\infty$-almost every tree,
\eqref{eq:ray-capacity-condition}, and hence
\eqref{eq:capacity-condition}, holds.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Since $W>0$ almost surely, choose $b>0$ so small that, with
$p=\mathbf P_\infty(W<b)$,
\begin{equation}
 a=\bar f'(p)=\mathbf E_\infty[Yp^{Y-1}]<1.
 \label{eq:bad-branch-mean}
\end{equation}
This is possible because $p\downarrow0$ as $b\downarrow0$ and
$\bar f'(p)\to\bar\nu(1)<1$. The derivative is finite for
$p<1$, even when the mean is infinite. At $p=0$ the $Y=1$ term
has value one.

Call a downward step $x\to y$ bad if every other child $z$ of
$x$ has $W_z<b$. Let $N_n$ be the number of length-$n$ paths of
bad steps from the root of an ordinary tree with offspring $Y$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf E_\infty N_n=a^n.
 \label{eq:bad-path-count}
\end{equation}
Conditional on $Y=k$, sum over the first child. The other $k-1$
descendant trees satisfy $W<b$ independently with probability $p$,
and are independent of the path count in the chosen child's tree.
Thus $\mathbf E_\infty N_n=\mathbf E_\infty[Yp^{Y-1}]\mathbf E_\infty N_{n-1}$,
with $N_0=1$. Tonelli's theorem applies without a finite mean.

An infinite path of bad steps would imply $N_n\ge1$ for every
$n$, an event of probability zero by \eqref{eq:bad-path-count}.
Apply this at every nonroot vertex. Every infinite ray
therefore has infinitely many vertices with an off-ray branch
of conductance at least $b$. This proves
\eqref{eq:ray-capacity-condition}; apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:ray-capacity}.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:terminal}
There is an $\mathcal F_x$-measurable $U_x\in[0,1]$ such that
\begin{equation}
 U_xW_x\le\theta_T(x)\le U_x.
 \label{eq:conditional-flow-comparison}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\kappa=\mathbf E_\infty W>0$, then, for every
$A\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots\}$ and $n\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf E_\infty\sum_{|x|=n}\theta_T(x)\mathbf 1_{\{Y_x\in A\}}
       \le\kappa^{-1}\mathbf P_\infty(Y\in A).
 \label{eq:harmonic-domination}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Suppose $x$ exists and remove its strict descendants, obtaining a
network $S$. If $S$ is transient, write
$A_x=g_S(o,x)$ and $R_x=g_S(x,x)\ge1$. The latter bound follows
because $x$ is a leaf and its parent edge has resistance one. The
voltage equation at $x$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \theta_T(x)=\frac{A_xC_x^+}{1+R_xC_x^+}
       =U_x\frac{R_xC_x^+}{1+R_xC_x^+},\qquad U_x=\frac{A_x}{R_x}.
 \label{eq:terminal-current}
\end{equation}
Here $U_x$ is the probability of hitting $x$ from $o$ in $S$, so
$U_x\le1$. Since $R_x\ge1$, the last fraction lies between
$C_x^+/(1+C_x^+)=W_x$ and one.

If $S$ is not transient, it consists only of the finite path $[o,x]$:
any side branch would contain an almost surely transient descendant
tree. In this case all the unit current goes through $x$, so set
$U_x=1$. If $x$ does not exist, set $U_x=0$. This proves
\eqref{eq:conditional-flow-comparison} in every case.

Independence of the descendant tree and $\mathcal F_x$ now gives
\begin{align}
 \mathbf E_\infty[\theta_T(x)\mid\mathcal F_x]&\ge\kappa U_x,\nonumber\\
 \mathbf E_\infty[\theta_T(x)\mathbf 1_{\{Y_x\in A\}}\mid\mathcal F_x]
 &\le U_x\mathbf P_\infty(Y\in A)
 \le\kappa^{-1}\mathbf P_\infty(Y\in A)
                 \mathbf E_\infty[\theta_T(x)\mid\mathcal F_x].
 \label{eq:conditional-mark}
\end{align}
Sum over the countable collection of labels at depth $n$ and use
Tonelli's theorem and conservation of flow.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:harmonic-decay}
There is a deterministic $c>0$, depending only on the law of $Y$,
such that almost surely
\begin{equation}
 \max_{|x|=n}\theta_T(x)\le e^{-cn}
       \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n.
 \label{eq:harmonic-decay}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
For an ordinary descendant tree with $Y$ children, let $W_i$ be
the conductance through its $i$th child, including the initial unit
edge, and put
\begin{equation}
 B=\sum_{i=1}^Y\left(\frac{W_i}{C}\right)^2,
 \qquad C=\sum_{i=1}^YW_i,\qquad
 \delta=\mathbf E_\infty[W^2(1-B)].
 \label{eq:split-loss}
\end{equation}
Every $W_i$ is positive. Hence $B\le1$, with strict inequality when
$Y\ge2$, and $0<\delta<1$. At a nonroot vertex $x$, let $B_x$
be the corresponding sum of squared current fractions to its children.
By \eqref{eq:conditional-flow-comparison} and independence from $\mathcal F_x$,
\begin{align}
 \mathbf E_\infty[\theta_T(x)^2(1-B_x)\mid\mathcal F_x]
 &\ge U_x^2\mathbf E_\infty[W^2(1-B)]\nonumber\\
 &=\delta U_x^2
 \ge\delta\mathbf E_\infty[\theta_T(x)^2\mid\mathcal F_x].
 \label{eq:conditional-loss}
\end{align}
Let $H_n=\sum_{|x|=n}\theta_T(x)^2$. The identity
$H_{n+1}=\sum_{|x|=n}\theta_T(x)^2B_x$ and
\eqref{eq:conditional-loss} imply
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf E_\infty H_{n+1}\le(1-\delta)\mathbf E_\infty H_n\quad(n\ge1),\qquad
 \mathbf E_\infty H_n\le(1-\delta)^{n-1},
 \label{eq:flow-second-moment}
\end{equation}
since $H_1\le1$. No moment of the root law is used. Choose $c>0$
with $e^{2c}(1-\delta)<1$. Markov's inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf P_\infty\{\max_{|x|=n}\theta_T(x)>e^{-cn}\}
 \le e^{2cn}(1-\delta)^{n-1}.
 \label{eq:flow-tail}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is summable, so Borel--Cantelli proves the claim.

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:truncated-bound}
For every $M$ with $m_M>1$, almost surely
\begin{equation}
 D_G(T)\ge\frac{\log m_M}{\log(M+1)}.
 \label{eq:truncated-bound}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
Such a retained tree survives with positive probability, and its
branching number on survival is $m_M$ by~\cite[Corollary~5.10]{LP}.
For $0<s<\log m_M/\log(M+1)$, \eqref{eq:branching-number}
and max-flow/min-cut give a unit flow $\mu$ on a surviving retained
tree rooted at $v$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \mu(x)\le A(M+1)^{-s|x|_v}
 \label{eq:retained-flow}
\end{equation}
for some finite $A$, where $|x|_v$ is the depth from $v$.

Every retained vertex has degree at most $M+1$ in the ambient
pruned tree $T$.
Writing $H_T$ for hitting probabilities in that tree, for adjacent
retained vertices $x,y$,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_T(x,y)=\sqrt{H_T(x,y)H_T(y,x)}
 \ge\frac1{\sqrt{\deg_T(x)\deg_T(y)}}\ge\frac1{M+1}.
 \label{eq:retained-rho}
\end{equation}
Here the first inequality uses the probability of an immediate step.
Multiplicativity of normalized Green functions along a tree path gives
$\rho_T(v,x)\ge(M+1)^{-|x|_v}$. Consequently
$\mu(x)\le A\rho_T(v,x)^s$. Extend the flow along $[o,v]$ with mass
one and set it to zero on all unused branches. Increasing its constant
over this finite initial path gives $\mu(x)\le A'\rho_o(x)^s$
throughout $T$. Every cutset then has weight at least $1/A'$.

It remains to find a surviving retained tree. Let $a_M>0$ be the
probability of the required event at an ordinary vertex, including
$Y_v\le M$. Under \eqref{eq:leafless-law}, the generation size $L_n$
tends to infinity almost surely. Indeed it is nondecreasing, and a
finite eventual value would require a fixed positive number of
individuals to reproduce with exactly one child forever, an event
of probability zero since $\mathbf P_\infty(Y=1)<1$. Before revealing offspring
at generation $n$, the descendant trees there are independent, so
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf P_\infty(\text{no suitable vertex anywhere})
       \le\mathbf E_\infty(1-a_M)^{L_n}\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:find-subtree}
\end{equation}
The limit follows by bounded convergence. Apply the flow bound and
intersect the resulting events over the countably many eligible $M$.
