\label{ex:leftzero}
  Let $S_\ell$ be the semigroup on $\{1,2\}$ given by left projection,
  $x*y=x$; associativity holds because $(x*y)*z=x=x*(y*z)$. Under any
  assignment a word evaluates to the value of its leftmost variable, so
  $S_\ell\models u\approx v$ exactly when $u$ and $v$ begin with the same
  variable. The identity $xy\approx x$ is a basis: it rewrites any word to its
  first variable, after which the two sides of an identity of
  $\mathrm{Id}(S_\ell)$ coincide. Note that $S_\ell$ has no zero element, since
  $1*2=1$ and $2*1=2$, and is thus not nilpotent, yet it is still finitely
  based. \autoref{sec:proof} prints the Lean proof of this example.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:variety-inclusion}
Let $S,T$ be finite semigroups and let $B_T$ be a finite identity basis of $T$.
Then
\[
  \mathsf{V}(S)\subseteq\mathsf{V}(T)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\models B_T.
\]
Consequently, this inclusion is decidable by finite evaluation of $B_T$ on $S$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $V(S)$ is the smallest variety containing $S$, the inclusion
  $V(S)\subseteq V(T)$ holds if and only if $S\in V(T)$. As $B_T$ is an identity
  basis of $T$, this is equivalent to $S\models B_T$. Each identity in $B_T$
  involving $k$ variables can be checked on all $|S|^k$ assignments. Since $S$
  and $B_T$ are finite, this gives a terminating decision procedure.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
