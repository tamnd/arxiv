\label{lem:ccp-mixed-fractional} Let  {$0< \alpha < 1$, $\beta > 0$} and $\{u_k\}\subset\mathcal D^{\alpha,2}_\beta(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ be a bounded sequence such that $u_k\rightharpoonup u$ weakly in $\mathcal D^{\alpha,2}_\beta(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ and $u_k\to u$ a.e. in $\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$. Set $w_k:=u_k-u$, and assume that, up to a subsequence,
\[
|w_k|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dx\,dy\stackrel{*}{\rightharpoonup}\nu
\quad\text{and}\quad
\big[D_{x,\alpha}w_k+(1+\beta)^2|x|^{2\beta}|\nabla_yw_k|^2\big]\,dx\,dy
\stackrel{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu
\]
weakly-$*$ in $\mathcal M(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$, for some nonnegative finite measures $\mu$ and $\nu$, where $D_{x,\alpha}$ is the fractional $x$-energy \eqref{eq:density}. Define the concentration masses at \(\infty\) by
\begin{align*}
\mu_\infty&:=\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}\setminus B_R}\big[D_{x,\alpha}w_k
+(1+\beta)^2|x|^{2\beta}|\nabla_yw_k|^2\big]\,dx\,dy, \\
\nu_\infty&:=\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}\setminus B_R}|w_k|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dx\,dy,
\end{align*}
where $B_R=B_d(0,R)$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{minq}
\|\nu\|^{2/2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\le S_{\alpha,\beta}(n,m)^{-1}\|\mu\|,
\qquad
\nu_\infty^{2/2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\le S_{\alpha,\beta}(n,m)^{-1}\mu_\infty,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{align}
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|u_k|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dx\,dy
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|u|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dx\,dy+\|\nu\|+\nu_\infty,
\label{eq:Lp-balance}\\
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathcal E_{\alpha,\beta}(u_k)
&=\mathcal E_{\alpha,\beta}(u)+\|\mu\|+\mu_\infty .
\label{eq:E-balance}
\end{align}
If, in addition, $u=0$, $\nu\ne0$, and equality holds in the first inequality of \eqref{minq}, then $\nu$ is concentrated at a single point: there exist $z_0\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$ and $\nu_0>0$ such that $\nu=\nu_0\delta_{z_0}$. Finally, $\nu$ is concentrated on the degenerate hyperplane
\[
\Sigma:=\{(0,y):y\in\mathbb{R}^m\},
\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad
\nu\big(\mathbb{R}^{n+m}\setminus\Sigma\big)=0 ;
\]
in particular, $\nu$ cannot be a Dirac mass at a point $z_0=(x_0,y_0)$ with $x_0\ne0$.

\qquad

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The rescaling \eqref{eq:rescaled-sequence-alphabeta} involves translations in the $y$-variable only, in accordance with the invariance group \eqref{scaling}. <!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:energy-comparison} Let $u$ be measurable on $\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$ and let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$ be measurable. Writing $\Omega_y:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n:(x,y)\in\Omega\}$, one has
\[
\int_\Omega D_{x,\alpha}u\,dz
=[u]^2_{x,\alpha;\Omega}
+\frac12\int_{\mathbb{R}^m}\int_{\Omega_y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\Omega_y}
\frac{|u(x,y)-u(\xi,y)|^2}{|x-\xi|^{n+2\alpha}}\,d\xi\,dx\,dy .
\]
In particular
\[
[u]^2_{x,\alpha;\Omega}\ \le\ \int_\Omega D_{x,\alpha}u\,dz\ \le\ [u]^2_{x,\alpha},
\]
and both inequalities are strict in general.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The proof follows easily from Tonelli's theorem by writing \(\int_\Omega D_{x,\alpha}u\,dz\) slice-wise in \(y\) and splitting \(\mathbb R^n=\Omega_y\cup(\mathbb R^n\setminus\Omega_y)\).
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
The quantity $\int_\Omega D_{x,\alpha}u\,dz$ depends on the values of $u$ outside $\Omega$ and is therefore not intrinsic to $\Omega$, whereas $[u]_{x,\alpha;\Omega}$ is. For this reason the local spaces below are defined through $[\,\cdot\,]_{x,\alpha;\Omega}$, while $\nabla_{\mathcal L}u$ and the quantity $\|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}$ are used only for functions defined on all of $\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$; Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-comparison} relates the two. When $u$ has compact support inside $\Omega$, however, a bound in the opposite direction is available, as the following corollary shows.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:support-comparison} Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$ be open and let $u$ be measurable on $\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$ with $\operatorname{supp}u$ a compact subset of $\Omega$. Then
\[
[u]^2_{x,\alpha}\ \le\ 2\int_\Omega D_{x,\alpha}u\,dz,
\qquad\text{and consequently}\qquad
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u|^2\,dz\ \le\ 2\int_\Omega|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u|^2\,dz .
\]
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since \(u=0\) on \(\mathbb R^{n+m}\setminus\Omega\), fractional seminorm is controlled by twice the energy density integrated over \(\Omega\), by Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-comparison}; the \(y\)-gradient term is supported in \(\Omega\), which gives the second estimate.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
If $2\beta<n$, then $|x|^{-\beta}\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, so that for every compact $K\subset\Omega$
\[
\int_K|\nabla_yu|\,dz
\le\big\||x|^\beta\nabla_yu\big\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\,
\big\||x|^{-\beta}\big\|_{L^2(K)}<+\infty ;
\]
hence $\nabla_yu\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ and the slice-wise weak gradient coincides a.e.\ with the distributional $y$-gradient on $\Omega$. For $2\beta\ge n$ this need not hold across the degeneracy set $\Sigma:=\{(x,y):x=0\}$, and the two notions still agree a.e.\ on $\Omega\setminus\Sigma$. In what follows $\nabla_yu$ always denotes the slice-wise gradient, which is defined for a.e.\ $x$ and needs no integrability across $\Sigma$. Local integrability of $\nabla_yu$ in $(x,y)$ jointly is used only in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:poincare-cell}, on a set where $|x|$ is bounded below, and in the density argument below, where it follows from \eqref{eq:dens-gradbdd}.
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
Let $\Omega\subseteq\Omega'\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$ be measurable and let $u\in\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}(\Omega')$. Then all three terms of the norm are monotone in the domain,
\[
\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega')},
\qquad
\big\||x|^\beta\nabla_yu\big\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le\big\||x|^\beta\nabla_yu\big\|_{L^2(\Omega')},
\qquad
[u]_{x,\alpha;\Omega}\le[u]_{x,\alpha;\Omega'},
\]
the last because $\chi_\Omega(x,y)\chi_\Omega(\xi,y)\le\chi_{\Omega'}(x,y)\chi_{\Omega'}(\xi,y)$ pointwise in \eqref{eq:seminorm}. If moreover the slices $\Omega^{x}$ are open for a.e.\ $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$, then $u|_\Omega\in\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}(\Omega)$ with $\|u\|_{\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}(\Omega)}\le\|u\|_{\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}(\Omega')}$, the slice-wise condition of \eqref{eq:H-space} being inherited because a compact subset of $\Omega^{x}$ is a compact subset of $\Omega'^{\,x}$.

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
\label{rem:Hloc-restricts} If $u\in\mathcal H^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ then $u|_\Omega\in\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}(\Omega)$ for every bounded open $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$, so the inequalities of Section~\ref{sec:poincare} apply to $u$ on every anisotropic cylinder. Indeed $u\in L^2(\Omega)$ and the slice-wise condition hold by hypothesis, while \eqref{eq:grushin-gradient} and Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-comparison} give
\[
[u]^2_{x,\alpha;\Omega}\ \le\ \int_\Omega D_{x,\alpha}u\,dz
\ \le\ \int_\Omega|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u|^2\,dz<+\infty,
\qquad
(1+\beta)^2\!\int_\Omega|x|^{2\beta}|\nabla_yu|^2dz\le\int_\Omega|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u|^2dz .
\]
In particular, since every set of type $V_R$ defined in \eqref{eq:VR} is bounded, $\nabla_{\mathcal L}u\in L^2(V_R)$ for every $z_0$ and every $R>0$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:lip-stable} Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$ be measurable with open slices, let $F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be $L$-Lipschitz with $F(0)=0$, and let $u\in\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}(\Omega)$. Then $F\circ u\in\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}(\Omega)$ and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lip-stable}
|F\circ u|\le L\,|u|\ \text{ a.e.},
\qquad
[F\circ u]_{x,\alpha;\Omega}\le L\,[u]_{x,\alpha;\Omega},
\qquad
\big|\nabla_y(F\circ u)\big|\le L\,\big|\nabla_yu\big|\ \text{ a.e.\ in }\Omega .
\end{equation}
In particular, for every $0\le t_1<t_2$ the truncation $T^{t_2}_{t_1}[u]:=\min\{\max\{0,u-t_1\},\,t_2-t_1\}$ and $u_\pm:=\max\{\pm u,0\}\in \mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}(\Omega)$ and satisfy \eqref{eq:lip-stable} with $L=1$; moreover
\begin{equation*}
\nabla_y T^{t_2}_{t_1}[u]=\nabla_yu\,\chi_{\{t_1<u<t_2\}}
\qquad\text{a.e.\ in }\Omega .
\end{equation*}
All of this holds verbatim for $u\in\mathcal H^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$, applied on each bounded open $\Omega$ via Remark~\ref{rem:Hloc-restricts}. Finally, if $u$ is defined on all of $\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$ then the \emph{unrestricted} density \eqref{eq:density} and the Grushin gradient \eqref{eq:grushin-gradient} obey the same bound pointwise,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lip-stable-density}
D_{x,\alpha}(F\circ u)\le L^2\,D_{x,\alpha}u
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\big|\nabla_{\mathcal L}(F\circ u)\big|\le L\,\big|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u\big|
\qquad\text{a.e.\ in }\mathbb{R}^{n+m},
\end{equation}
and both are unchanged by the addition of a constant to $u$.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Since $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})\subset\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ and the latter is complete, it suffices to approximate an arbitrary $u\in\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ in the energy norm by test functions.


Now, for $T>0$, put $F_T(t):=\max\{-T,\min\{t,T\}\}$ and $u^T:=F_T\circ u$. Then $t\mapsto t-F_T(t)$ is $1$-Lipschitz and vanishes at $0$, so by  {Lemma~\ref{lem:lip-stable}} applied to it,
\[
\big|(u-u^T)(x,y)-(u-u^T)(\xi,y)\big|\le|u(x,y)-u(\xi,y)|,
\qquad
\nabla_y(u-u^T)=\nabla_yu\,\mathbf 1_{\{|u|>T\}}\ \text{ a.e.}
\]
Both right-hand sides are square integrable against the respective measures, and both left-hand sides tend to $0$ a.e.\ as $T\to\infty$ because $|u|<\infty$ a.e. Dominated convergence therefore gives
\[
\|u^T-u\|_{\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}}\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{as }T\to\infty .
\]
Hence we can assume from now on that $u\in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$.

Next, fix $\eta\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ with $0\le\eta\le1$, $\eta\equiv1$ on $B_1^n\times B_1^m$ and $\eta\equiv0$ outside $B_2^n\times B_2^m$, and for $R\ge1$ set
\[
\eta_R(x,y):=\eta\big(\delta_{1/R}(x,y)\big)=\eta\Big(\frac xR,\frac y{R^{\alpha+\beta}}\Big),
\qquad u_R:=\eta_Ru .
\]
Then $u_R\in\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}$, $u_R$ is bounded with compact support, and we claim $\|u_R-u\|_{\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}}\to0$.

Since $0\le\eta_R\le1$ and $\eta_R\to1$ pointwise, dominated convergence gives $\|u_R-u\|_{L^2}\to0$. For the weighted gradient, write
\[
|x|^\beta\nabla_y(u_R-u)=(\eta_R-1)\,|x|^\beta\nabla_yu+|x|^\beta u\,\nabla_y\eta_R .
\]
The first term tends to $0$ in $L^2$ by dominated convergence. For the second, $|\nabla_y\eta_R|\le\|\nabla_y\eta\|_\infty R^{-(\alpha+\beta)}$, and $\nabla_y\eta_R$ is supported in $\{|x|\le2R\}$, whence
\begin{equation*}
|x|^\beta|\nabla_y\eta_R|\le\|\nabla_y\eta\|_\infty(2R)^\beta R^{-(\alpha+\beta)}=CR^{-\alpha},
\end{equation*}
so that $\||x|^\beta u\nabla_y\eta_R\|_{L^2}\le CR^{-\alpha}\|u\|_{L^2}\to0$.

For the fractional seminorm set $w_R:=u_R-u=(\eta_R-1)u$ and decompose
\[
w_R(x,y)-w_R(\xi,y)
=\big(\eta_R(x,y)-1\big)\big(u(x,y)-u(\xi,y)\big)
+u(\xi,y)\big(\eta_R(x,y)-\eta_R(\xi,y)\big),
\]
so that $[w_R]_{x,\alpha}^2\le I_R+J_R$ with
\[
I_R:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^m}\!\!\iint_{\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^n}
\frac{|\eta_R(x,y)-1|^2\,|u(x,y)-u(\xi,y)|^2}{|x-\xi|^{n+2\alpha}}\,dx\,d\xi\,dy,
\]
and 
\[
J_R:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|u(\xi,y)|^2\,K_R(\xi,y)\,d\xi\,dy,
\]
where $K_R(\xi,y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}|x-\xi|^{-n-2\alpha}|\eta_R(x,y)-\eta_R(\xi,y)|^2\,dx$. The integrand of $I_R$ tends to $0$ pointwise and is dominated by $4|u(x,y)-u(\xi,y)|^2|x-\xi|^{-n-2\alpha}\in L^1$, so $I_R\to0$. For $J_R$ we use $|\eta_R(x,y)-\eta_R(\xi,y)|\le\min\{1,\|\nabla_x\eta\|_\infty|x-\xi|/R\}$ to get, uniformly in $(\xi,y)$,
\begin{equation*}
K_R(\xi,y)\!\le \! C\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\frac{\min\{1,|h|^2/R^2\}}{|h|^{n+2\alpha}}\,dh
\!=\!C\Big(R^{-2}\!\!\int_{|h|<R}\!\!|h|^{2-n-2\alpha}dh+\!\!\int_{|h|\ge R}\!\!|h|^{-n-2\alpha}dh\Big)
\!=\!\frac{C(n,\alpha)}{R^{2\alpha}},
\end{equation*}
both integrals being finite precisely because $0<\alpha<1$. Hence $J_R\le C R^{-2\alpha}\|u\|_{L^2}^2\to0$, and the claim follows.

Now, let $v\in\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}\cap L^\infty$ have compact support, let $\varsigma\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^m)$ be a standard mollifier and put $v^\sigma:=v*_y\varsigma_\sigma$, the convolution acting in the $y$-variable only. Then $v^\sigma$ has compact support and

\emph{(a)} $\|v^\sigma-v\|_{L^2}\to0$;

\emph{(b)} since the weight $|x|^{2\beta}$ does not depend on $y$, it commutes with $*_y$, so $|x|^\beta\nabla_yv^\sigma=\big(|x|^\beta\nabla_yv\big)*_y\varsigma_\sigma \to|x|^\beta\nabla_yv$ in $L^2$;

\emph{(c)} writing $\tau_\zeta v(x,y):=v(x,y-\zeta)$ and using Minkowski's integral inequality,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlocal_y_density}
[v^\sigma-v]_{x,\alpha}\le\int_{\mathbb{R}^m}\varsigma_\sigma(\zeta)\,[\tau_\zeta v-v]_{x,\alpha}\,d\zeta
\le\sup_{|\zeta|\le\sigma}[\tau_\zeta v-v]_{x,\alpha}\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
because $[\,\cdot\,]_{x,\alpha}$ is (half) the $L^2\big(\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^m\big)$ norm of $(x,\xi,y)\mapsto|x-\xi|^{-(n+2\alpha)/2}\big(v(x,y)-v(\xi,y)\big)$, on which $\tau_\zeta$ acts as a translation, and translations are continuous on $L^2$.

Thus $\|v^\sigma-v\|_{\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}}\to0$. Since $v\in L^\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dens-gradbdd}
\|\nabla_yv^\sigma\|_{L^\infty}=\|v*_y\nabla\varsigma_\sigma\|_{L^\infty}
\le\|\nabla\varsigma_\sigma\|_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^m)}\|v\|_{L^\infty}<\infty ,
\end{equation}
so that $\nabla_yv^\sigma\in L^\infty$ with compact support, hence $\nabla_yv^\sigma\in L^2(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ even without the weight.

Finally, let $g:=v^\sigma$ be as in defined above, let $\varrho\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ be a standard mollifier and set $g^\varepsilon:=g*_x\varrho_\varepsilon$. Then $g^\varepsilon\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$. Indeed, it is compactly supported, smooth in $x$ by convolution, smooth in $y$ because $\partial_y^\theta$ commutes with $*_x$ and $g$ is smooth in $y$, and all mixed derivatives are bounded and continuous. Moreover $\|g^\varepsilon-g\|_{L^2}\to0$, and $[g^\varepsilon-g]_{x,\alpha}\to0$ exactly as in \eqref{eq:nonlocal_y_density}, translations now acting in $x$. Finally, let $\mathcal K$ be a compact set containing the supports of all $g^\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\le1$, and put $\Lambda:=\sup_{(x,y)\in\mathcal K}|x|^{2\beta}<\infty$. Since $\nabla_yg^\varepsilon=(\nabla_yg)*_x\varrho_\varepsilon$ and, by \eqref{eq:dens-gradbdd}, $\nabla_yg\in L^2(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$, we obtain
\[
\big\||x|^\beta\nabla_y(g^\varepsilon-g)\big\|_{L^2}
\le\Lambda^{1/2}\,\big\|(\nabla_yg)*_x\varrho_\varepsilon-\nabla_yg\big\|_{L^2}
\longrightarrow0 .
\]
Hence $\|g^\varepsilon-g\|_{\mathcal H_{\alpha,\beta}}\to0$.

For all $v,v'\in\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^m$,

\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v+v')\;\le\;C_{\triangle}(\gamma)\,\big(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v)+\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v')\big),

where

\;=\;

1, & \gamma\ge1,\\[2pt]

\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The constant $C_{\triangle}(\gamma)$ is sharp for every $\gamma>0$. Consequently, $d_{\alpha,\beta}$ is a metric on $\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^m$ if and only if $\gamma=\alpha+\beta\ge1$.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Write $v=(x,y)$, $v'=(x',y')$ and $p:=2\gamma$. Then
\[
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)=\big(|x|^{p}+\big(|y|^{1/\gamma}\big)^{p}\big)^{1/p}
=\big\|\big(|x|,|y|^{1/\gamma}\big)\big\|_{\ell^p(\mathbb{R}^2)},
\]
where for $0<p<1$ the right-hand side is the $\ell^p$ quasi-norm. Since this expression is nondecreasing in each of its two nonnegative entries, the triangle inequalities $|x+x'|\le|x|+|x'|$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $|y+y'|\le|y|+|y'|$ in $\mathbb{R}^m$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qt-first}
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v+v')\;\le\;\big\|\big(|x|+|x'|,\,(|y|+|y'|)^{1/\gamma}\big)\big\|_{\ell^p(\mathbb{R}^2)}.
\end{equation}

Suppose first that $\gamma\ge1$. Then $t\mapsto t^{1/\gamma}$ is concave on $[0,\infty)$ and vanishes at $0$, hence subadditive, so $(|y|+|y'|)^{1/\gamma}\le|y|^{1/\gamma}+|y'|^{1/\gamma}$. Since $p\ge2$, Minkowski's inequality in $\ell^p(\mathbb{R}^2)$ applied to the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:qt-first} yields $\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v+v')\le\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v)+\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v')$.

Suppose next that $\tfrac12\le\gamma<1$. By convexity of $t\mapsto t^{1/\gamma}$,
\[
(s+s')^{1/\gamma}\le2^{1/\gamma-1}\big(s^{1/\gamma}+s'^{1/\gamma}\big),\qquad s,s'\ge0,
\]
and trivially $|x|+|x'|\le2^{1/\gamma-1}(|x|+|x'|)$. Inserting both bounds into~\eqref{eq:qt-first} and using the homogeneity of the norm together with Minkowski's inequality, which is available because $p\ge1$, we obtain
\[
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v+v')
\le2^{1/\gamma-1}\big\|\big(|x|,|y|^{1/\gamma}\big)+\big(|x'|,|y'|^{1/\gamma}\big)\big\|_{\ell^p(\mathbb{R}^2)}
\le2^{1/\gamma-1}\big(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v)+\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v')\big).
\]

Suppose finally that $0<\gamma<\tfrac12$, so that $p<1$. Set $a:=|x|^{p}$, $a':=|x'|^{p}$, $b:=|y|^{2}$, $b':=|y'|^{2}$, so that $r:=\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v)^{p}=a+b$ and $r':=\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v')^{p}=a'+b'$. Raising \eqref{eq:qt-first} to the power $p$ gives
\[
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v+v')^{p}\le(|x|+|x'|)^{p}+(|y|+|y'|)^{2}
=\big(a^{1/p}+a'^{1/p}\big)^{p}+\big(b^{1/2}+b'^{1/2}\big)^{2}.
\]
As $1/p>1$, the $\ell^{1/p}$-norm on $\mathbb{R}^2$ is dominated by the $\ell^1$-norm, so $\big(a^{1/p}+a'^{1/p}\big)^{p}\le a+a'$; moreover $\big(b^{1/2}+b'^{1/2}\big)^{2}=b+b'+2\sqrt{bb'}\le2(b+b')$. Hence $\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v+v')^{p}\le2(r+r')$. On the other hand, convexity of $t\mapsto t^{1/p}$ gives $r^{1/p}+r'^{1/p}\ge2^{1-1/p}(r+r')^{1/p}$, that is, $\big(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v)+\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v')\big)^{p}\ge2^{p-1}(r+r')$. Combining the two estimates,
\[
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v+v')^{p}\le2^{2-p}\big(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v)+\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v')\big)^{p},
\]
and taking $p$-th roots yields the claim, since $2^{(2-p)/p}=2^{1/\gamma-1}$. This proves~\eqref{eq:C0-def}.

For sharpness, the choice $v'=0$ shows that no constant smaller than $1$ is admissible. If $0<\gamma<1$, take $v=v'=(0,y)$ with $y\neq0$; then
\[
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v+v')=|2y|^{1/\gamma}=2^{1/\gamma-1}\cdot2|y|^{1/\gamma}
=2^{1/\gamma-1}\big(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v)+\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v')\big),
\]
so equality holds and $C_{\triangle}(\gamma)$ cannot be decreased.

Finally, $d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,z')=\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(z-z')$ is symmetric, since $\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(-v)=\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(v)$, and vanishes only for $z=z'$. Writing $z-z''=(z-z')+(z'-z'')$, the case $\gamma\ge1$ above is precisely the triangle inequality for $d_{\alpha,\beta}$. If $\gamma<1$, the points $z=0$, $z'=(0,y)$, $z''=(0,2y)$ with $y\neq0$ satisfy, by the equality case just exhibited, $d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,z'')=2^{1/\gamma-1}\big(d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,z')+d_{\alpha,\beta}(z',z'')\big) >d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,z')+d_{\alpha,\beta}(z',z'')$, so the triangle inequality fails and $d_{\alpha,\beta}$ is not a metric.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:AB-def}
A:=\frac a\rho\in[0,1],\qquad B:=\frac b{\rho^{\gamma}}\in[0,1],
\qquad\text{so that}\qquad A^{2\gamma}+B^{2}=\frac{a^{2\gamma}+b^{2}}{\rho^{2\gamma}}=1 .
\end{equation}
From $a<r$ and $b<r^{\gamma}$ we also get $\rho^{2\gamma}=a^{2\gamma}+b^{2}<2r^{2\gamma}$, hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rho-bounds}
\rho<\kappa r\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rho^{\gamma}<\kappa^{\gamma}r^{\gamma}=\sqrt2\,r^{\gamma}.
\end{equation}

We first record the exact offset between $z_0$ and $z_0^{*}$. By definition $s\rho=Mr$, hence also $s^{\gamma}\rho^{\gamma}=(Mr)^{\gamma}$; since $z_0^{*}-z_0=-\delta_s(z-z_0)=(-s\Delta x,-s^{\gamma}\Delta y)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:offset-coords}
|\xi_0^{*}-\xi_0|=s\,a=s\rho A=Mr\,A,
\qquad
|\eta_0^{*}-\eta_0|=s^{\gamma}b=s^{\gamma}\rho^{\gamma}B=(Mr)^{\gamma}B .
\end{equation}
Likewise $z_0^{*}-z=-\big((1+s)\Delta x,\ (1+s^{\gamma})\Delta y\big)$, so that, using $(1+s)a=(\rho+Mr)A$ and $(1+s^{\gamma})b=\big(\rho^{\gamma}+(Mr)^{\gamma}\big)B$,
\begin{equation*}

=(1+s)^{2\gamma}a^{2\gamma}+(1+s^{\gamma})^{2}b^{2}
=A^{2\gamma}\big(\rho+Mr\big)^{2\gamma}+B^{2}\big(\rho^{\gamma}+(Mr)^{\gamma}\big)^{2}.
\end{equation*}

\big(\rho+Mr\big)^{2\gamma}<(Mr)^{2\gamma}\Big(1+\frac{\kappa}{M}\Big)^{2\gamma}
\le(Mr)^{2\gamma}\Theta(M)^{2\gamma},

\end{equation*}

\end{equation}

\begin{equation*}

\end{equation*}
the last inequality being the second condition in \eqref{eq:M-offset-conditions}.

\emph{Case 2: $B\ge\kappa^{-\gamma}=2^{-1/2}$.} Since $t\mapsto t^{\gamma}$ is increasing, \eqref{eq:d-upper} gives
\begin{equation*}

The requirement $\Theta(M)\le\tfrac32$ amounts to the two conditions $M\ge2\kappa$ and $M\ge\big(\sqrt2/((3/2)^{\gamma}-1)\big)^{1/\gamma}$, the first of which is implied by $M\ge4\kappa=2^{2+\frac1{2\gamma}}$; hence \eqref{eq:M-offset} is exactly the maximum of the three surviving constraints. For every $\gamma>0$ one has $(3/2)^{\gamma}>1$ and $(3/4)^{\gamma}<1$ strictly, so all three are finite.

The idea is to join each point of the level set $\{|u|>t\}$ to a cylinder outside the support of $u$, where the average of $u$ vanishes, by a dyadic chain of cylinders. The Poincar\'e inequality along the chain then gives a cylinder around the point on which the energy is large, and the level set is covered by such cylinders with the Vitali lemma.

Fix $0<r\le R_0$, $ \mathcal{C}_R =  \mathcal{C}_R(z_0)$ and split $\mathcal J^{ \mathcal{C}_R}_{\alpha,2}|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u|=J_r|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u|+J^r|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u|$, where

\left(\int_{V_R}|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u|^2\,dw\right)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\end{equation}

\end{equation*}

\int_{V_R}|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u|^2\,dw\notag\\
&=C(\alpha, \beta,n,m)\,t^{-2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}
\left(\int_{V_R}|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u|^2\,dw\right)^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}/2}.
\label{eq:E1_bound}
\end{align}
By~\eqref{eq:J_Rbound}, $ \mathcal{C}_{R,t}\cap E_2=\emptyset$ once $t$ exceeds $C(\alpha,\beta)R^{\alpha-Q/2}\big(\int_{V_R}|\nabla_{\mathcal L}u|^2\,dw\big)^{1/2}$; for smaller $t$,

\label{eq:E2_bound}

\end{equation}
Adding \eqref{eq:E1_bound} and \eqref{eq:E2_bound} gives \eqref{eq:main_weak_type_riesz}.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:vanishing-constant} Let $u\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ and let $R_0>0$ be such that $\mathrm{supp}(u)\subset\mathcal{C}_{R_0}$. For $R\ge R_0$ define
\[
g_R(c):=\int_{\mathcal{C}_R}|u-c|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dz,\qquad c\in\mathbb{R}.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item for every $R\ge R_0$, $g_R$ attains its infimum over $\mathbb{R}$ at some point $c_R\in\mathbb{R}$;
\item $g_R(c_R)\le M:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|u|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dz$ for every $R\ge R_0$;
\item $c_R\to0$ as $R\to\infty$;
\item $\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{R_0}}|u-c_R|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dz=M$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
If $u\equiv0$ the statement is trivial (with $c_R\equiv0$, $M=0$), so assume $u\not\equiv0$, and in particular $M\in(0,\infty)$ (finite because $u$ is bounded with compact support).

\smallskip\noindent Fix $R\ge R_0$. The function $c\mapsto|u(z)-c|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}$ is convex for each fixed $z$ (as $2^*_{\alpha,\beta}>1$), so $g_R$ is convex, hence continuous, on $\mathbb{R}$. Moreover $g_R$ is coercive: by the reverse triangle inequality in $L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}(\mathcal{C}_R)$,
\begin{align*}
g_R(c)^{1/2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}& =\|u-c\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}(\mathcal{C}_R)} \\
& \ge\big|\,\|c\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}(\mathcal{C}_R)}-\|u\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}(\mathcal{C}_R)}\,\big| \\
& =\big|\,|c|\,|\mathcal{C}_R|^{1/2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}-M^{1/2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,\big|\xrightarrow[|c|\to\infty]{}\infty,
\end{align*}
using $\|u\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}(\mathcal{C}_R)}=\|u\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}=M^{1/2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}$ (valid since $u\equiv0$ outside $\mathcal{C}_{R_0}\subset\mathcal{C}_R$) and $|\mathcal{C}_R|<\infty$. A continuous coercive function on $\mathbb{R}$ attains its infimum, proving (i).

\smallskip\noindent\emph{(ii)} Since $c=0$ is an admissible competitor and $u\equiv0$ on $\mathcal{C}_R\setminus\mathcal{C}_{R_0}$,
\[
g_R(c_R)=\min_{c\in\mathbb{R}}g_R(c)\le g_R(0)=\int_{\mathcal{C}_R}|u|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dz
=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{R_0}}|u|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dz=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|u|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dz=M.
\]

\smallskip\noindent\emph{(iii)} Since $u\equiv0$ on $\mathcal{C}_R\setminus\mathcal{C}_{R_0}$ for $R\ge R_0$,
\[
g_R(c_R)=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{R_0}}|u-c_R|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dz+|c_R|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\big(|\mathcal{C}_R|-|\mathcal{C}_{R_0}|\big)
\ \ge\ |c_R|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\big(|\mathcal{C}_R|-|\mathcal{C}_{R_0}|\big).
\]
Combined with (ii), this gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cR-bound}
|c_R|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\le\frac{M}{|\mathcal{C}_R|-|\mathcal{C}_{R_0}|}.
\end{equation}
Since, $|\mathcal{C}_R|-|\mathcal{C}_{R_0}|=C(n,m)(R^Q-R_0^Q)\to\infty$ as $R\to\infty$, so the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:cR-bound} tends to $0$, whence $c_R\to0$.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{(iv)} Consider the auxiliary function
\[
h(c):=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{R_0}}|u-c|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dz,\qquad c\in\mathbb{R}.
\]
Since $u$ is bounded on $\mathcal{C}_{R_0}$ (say $|u|\le M_0$ there) and $|\mathcal{C}_{R_0}|<\infty$, dominated convergence shows $h$ is continuous at $c=0$: for $|c|\le1$, $|u-c|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\le(M_0+1)^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\mathbf1_{\mathcal{C}_{R_0}}\in L^1(\mathcal{C}_{R_0})$, and $|u(z)-c|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\to|u(z)|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}$ pointwise as $c\to0$, so by dominated convergence $h(c)\to h(0)=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{R_0}}|u|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dz=M$ as $c\to0$. Since $c_R\to0$ by (iii), continuity of $h$ at $0$ gives
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{R_0}}|u-c_R|^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}\,dz=\lim_{R\to\infty}h(c_R)=h(0)=M.\qedhere

Write $Q:=Q_{\alpha,\beta}$ and $\Lambda:=\sup_k\mathcal E_{\alpha,\beta}(u_k)^{1/2}<\infty$. To prove the theorem, we use the Riesz-Kolmogorov criterion given in Theorem $4.26$ of \cite{brezis_book}.

We begin with the uniform bound on the set $\mathcal A$. Theorem~\ref{thm:global} give $\|u_k\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}\le C\Lambda$ for every $k$, so that, by H\"older's inequality and $\tfrac12-\tfrac1{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}=\tfrac{\alpha}{Q}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rk-L2bound}
\|u_k\|_{L^2(\mathcal A)}\le\|u_k\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}\,|\mathcal A|^{\alpha/Q}
\le C(\mathcal A,\Lambda).
\end{equation}
Now, note that any general translation on $\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$ can be factored as $\tau_{(h,\zeta)}=\tau^x_h\circ\tau^y_\zeta$ where $\tau^x_h$ and $\tau^y_\zeta$ are translations in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $\mathbb{R}^m$ respectively. So, it enough to prove uniform convergence of the translations separately in $x$ and $y$ variable. Moreover, since the space $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ is dense in $\mathcal D^{\alpha,2}_\beta(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ with respect to $\mathcal E_{\alpha,\beta}(\cdot)^{1/2}$, it is enough to prove the translation estimates for $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$.

Let $v\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$. First we prove the continuity of translations in the $x$-variable. To this end, write $\tau^x_hv(x,y):=v(x-h,y)$ and take the Fourier transform in $x$ and applying Plancherel for a.e.\ fixed $y$, together with the identification of Gagliardo seminorm in Fourier terms, we get
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\|\tau^x_hv-v\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}^2
& =\int_{\mathbb{R}^m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\big|e^{-i\xi\cdot h}-1\big|^2|\widehat v(\xi,y)|^2
\,d\xi\,dy \\
& \leq  \int_{\mathbb{R}^m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\min\{4,|\xi|^2 |h|^2\}|\widehat v(\xi,y)|^2
\,d\xi\,dy \\
& \leq  \int_{\mathbb{R}^m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} 4^{1-\alpha}|h|^{2\alpha}|\xi|^{2\alpha}|\widehat v(\xi,y)|^2 \,d\xi\,dy \\
& \leq C(n,\alpha)\,|h|^{2\alpha} [v]_{x,\alpha}^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
To estimate the translations in the $y$-variable we split at a level $\sigma\in(0,1]$. The set $\{|x|\ge\sigma\}$ is invariant under $y$-translations and carries $|x|^{-\beta}\le\sigma^{-\beta}$, so with $\tau^y_\zeta v(x,y):=v(x,y-\zeta)$, we obtain
\[
\|\tau^y_\zeta v-v\|_{L^2(\{|x|\ge\sigma\})}
\le|\zeta|\,\|\nabla_yv\|_{L^2(\{|x|\ge\sigma\})}
\le|\zeta|\,\sigma^{-\beta}\,\big\||x|^{\beta}\nabla_yv\big\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})} .
\]
Let $\mathcal A_1:=\{z:\operatorname{dist}(z,\mathcal A)\le1\}$; for $|\zeta|\le1$ both $v$ and $\tau^y_\zeta v$ are supported, over $\mathcal A$, in $E_\sigma:=\{|x|<\sigma\}\cap\mathcal A_1$, whose measure is at most $C(\mathcal A)\sigma^n$, so that
\[
\|\tau^y_\zeta v-v\|_{L^2(\{|x|<\sigma\}\cap\mathcal A)}
\le2\|v\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}}|E_\sigma|^{\frac12-\frac1{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}}
\le C(\mathcal A)\,\|v\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}}\,\sigma^{\frac{\alpha n}{Q}} .
\]
Adding the two contributions and invoking Theorem~\ref{thm:global},
\begin{equation*}
\|\tau^y_\zeta v-v\|_{L^2(\mathcal A)}
\le C\Big(|\zeta|\,\sigma^{-\beta}+\sigma^{\frac{\alpha n}{Q}}\Big)
\mathcal E_{\alpha,\beta}(v)^{1/2},
\qquad 0<\sigma\le1,\ |\zeta|\le1,
\end{equation*}
with $C=C(n,m,\alpha,\beta,\mathcal A)$. Given $\varepsilon>0$ one now fixes $\sigma$ with $C\sigma^{\alpha n/Q}\Lambda<\varepsilon/2$ and then $|\zeta|$ with $C|\zeta|\sigma^{-\beta}\Lambda<\varepsilon/2$; the resulting threshold depends on $\varepsilon$ and $\Lambda$, not on $k$. Thus, translations in $y$ are uniformly continuous.

Therefore, by Theorem $4.26$ of \cite{brezis_book} the family $\{u_k\}$ has a subsequence converges in $L^2(\mathcal A)$, say to $u$.

For $\delta\in(2,2^*_{\alpha,\beta})$ write $\tfrac1\delta=\tfrac{1-\theta}2+\tfrac{\theta}{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}$ with $\theta\in(0,1)$; then
\[
\|u_k-u_j\|_{L^\delta(\mathcal A)}
\le\|u_k-u_j\|_{L^2(\mathcal A)}^{1-\theta}
\|u_k-u_j\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}^{\theta}
\le  C \Lambda^{\theta}
\|u_k-u_j\|_{L^2(\mathcal A)}^{1-\theta}\to0,
\]
so the same subsequence is Cauchy in $L^\delta(\mathcal A)$, while for $1\le\delta<2$ the claim follows from the case $\delta=2$ and H\"older's inequality on the bounded set $\mathcal A$.

\label{lem:ccp-cutoff} Let $\{v_k\}\subset\mathcal D^{\alpha,2}_\beta(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ be bounded with $v_k\to0$ a.e.\ in $\mathbb{R}^{n+m}$, and set
\[
d\mu_k:=\Big[D_{x,\alpha}v_k+(1+\beta)^2|x|^{2\beta}|\nabla_yv_k|^2\Big]dx\,dy .
\]
Let $\psi\in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ satisfy $0\le\psi\le1$, let $\nabla\psi$ be bounded and supported in a compact set $\mathcal K\subset\{|x|\le R_{x}\}\times\{|y|\le R_{y}\}$ for some $R_{x}, R_{y} >0$, and assume that $\psi(\cdot,y)$ is constant on $\{|x|>R_{x}\}$ for every $y\in\mathbb{R}^m$. Then for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $C_\varepsilon=C_\varepsilon(\beta)>0$, independent of $k$, such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ccp-cutoff}
\mathcal E_{\alpha,\beta}(\psi v_k)
\le(1+\varepsilon)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}\psi^2\,d\mu_k+C_\varepsilon\,\mathcal{R}_k(\psi),
\qquad
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{R}_k(\psi)=0,
\end{equation}
where, with $K_\psi( \xi,y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\frac{|\psi(x,y)-\psi(\xi,y)|^2}{|x-\xi|^{n+2\alpha}}\,dx$,
\[
\mathcal{R}_k(\psi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|x|^{2\beta}|\nabla_y\psi|^2|v_k|^2\,dx\,dy
+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|v_k(\xi, y)|^2K_\psi(\xi,y)\,d\xi\,dy .
\]
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
By Young's inequality, we obtain pointwise $$|\nabla_y(\psi v_k)|^2\le(1+\varepsilon)\psi^2|\nabla_yv_k|^2+C_\varepsilon|v_k|^2|\nabla_y\psi|^2.$$ Likewise, splitting
\[
\psi(x,y)v_k(x,y)-\psi(\xi,y)v_k(\xi,y)
=\psi(x,y)\big(v_k(x,y)-v_k(\xi,y)\big)+v_k(\xi,y)\big(\psi(x,y)-\psi(\xi,y)\big)
\]
and applying Young's inequality before integrating in $(x,\xi,y)$ gives
\[
[\psi v_k]_{x,\alpha}^2
\le(1+\varepsilon)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}\psi^2D_{x,\alpha}v_k\,dx\,dy
+C_\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|v_k|^2K_\psi\,d\xi\,dy .
\]
Adding the two estimates yields first term in \eqref{eq:ccp-cutoff}.


\smallskip

Now, we show that $\mathcal{R}_k(\psi) \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$. For the first term we note that, as $\beta\ge0$, the function $|x|^{2\beta}|\nabla_y\psi|^2$ is bounded and supported in the compact set $\mathcal K$. Thus we obtain $$\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|x|^{2\beta}|\nabla_y\psi|^2|v_k|^2\le C\int_{\mathcal K}|v_k|^2\to0,$$ where Theorem \ref{lem:rellich} gives $v_k\to0$ in $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$. For the second term, we first estimate $K_\psi$. Since $\psi$ is smooth with bounded gradient and $0\le\psi\le1$, the mean value theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ccp-Kdecay0}
K_\psi(\xi,y)\le\|\nabla_x\psi\|_\infty^2\int_{|h|<1}|h|^{2-n-2\alpha}dh
+\int_{|h|\ge1}|h|^{-n-2\alpha}dh=:\Gamma_0<\infty ,
\end{equation}
both integrals converging because $0<\alpha<1$. If $|y|>R_{y}$ then $\nabla\psi(\cdot,y)\equiv0$, so $\psi(\cdot,y)$ is constant and $K_\psi(\cdot,y)\equiv0$. Finally, if $|\xi|>2R_{x}$ then $\psi(x,y)-\psi(\xi,y)$ vanishes for $|x|>R_{x}$ by hypothesis, while for $|x|<R_{x}$ we have $|x-\xi|\ge|\xi|/2$ there, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ccp-Kdecay}
K_\psi(\xi,y)\le\int_{|x|\le R_{x}}\frac{dx}{|x-\xi|^{n+2\alpha}}\le\frac{C(n)\,R_{x}^n}{|\xi|^{n+2\alpha}} .
\end{equation}
Now, fix $\Lambda>2R_{x}$ and split
\[
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|v_k|^2K_\psi
=\int_{\{|\xi|\le\Lambda,\,|y|\le R_{y}\}}|v_k|^2K_\psi
+\int_{\{|\xi|>\Lambda,\,|y|\le R_{y}\}}|v_k|^2K_\psi=:\mathrm{I}_k+\mathrm{II}_k .
\]
By \eqref{eq:ccp-Kdecay0} and $v_k\to0$ in $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$, we deduce $$\mathrm{I}_k\le\Gamma_0\int_{\{|\xi|\le\Lambda,|y|\le R_{y}\}}|v_k|^2\to0$$ as $k\to\infty$, for each fixed $\Lambda$. \\
For $\mathrm{II}_k$ use \eqref{eq:ccp-Kdecay} and H\"older's inequality with exponents $2^*_{\alpha,\beta}/2$ and $2^*_{\alpha,\beta}/(2^*_{\alpha,\beta}-2)$; since
\[
q:=(n+2\alpha)\frac{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}-2}=(n+2\alpha)\frac{Q_{\alpha,\beta}}{2\alpha}>n,
\]
we obtain, with $C$ independent of $k$ and $\Lambda$,
\[
\mathrm{II}_k\le CR_{x}^n\|v_k\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}}^2
\bigg(\int_{\{|\xi|>\Lambda,\,|y|\le R_{y}\}}|\xi|^{-q}\,d\xi\,dy\bigg)^{\frac{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}-2}{2^*_{\alpha,\beta}}}
\le C(R_{x},R_{y})\,\Lambda^{-\theta},
\]
where $\theta:=n+2\alpha-\frac{2\alpha n}{Q_{\alpha,\beta}}$ which is positive since $Q_{\alpha,\beta}>2\alpha$. Hence $\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int|v_k|^2K_\psi\le C(R_{x},R_{y})\Lambda^{-\theta}$ for every $\Lambda>2R_{x}$, and letting $\Lambda\to\infty$ gives the claim.

c_0\le|x|\le3c_0\qquad\text{for every }(x,y)\in\mathcal A .
\end{equation}
Fix $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\mathcal A)$ with $0\le\chi\le1$ and $\chi\equiv1$ on a smaller neighbourhood $\mathcal A'\Subset\mathcal A$ of $z_0$, and set $\varphi_k:=\chi w_k$. Then $\{\varphi_k\}$ is bounded in the \emph{non-degenerate} space $\mathcal D^{\alpha,2}_0(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$, whose energy is that of the same family of operators with the parameter $\beta$ replaced by $0$,
\[
\mathcal E_{\alpha,0}(v)=[v]_{x,\alpha}^2
+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|\nabla_yv|^2\,dx\,dy .
\]
Indeed, by \eqref{eq:frozen-weight},
\[
\|\nabla_y\varphi_k\|_{L^2}
\le\|\chi\nabla_yw_k\|_{L^2}+\|w_k\nabla_y\chi\|_{L^2}
\le c_0^{-\beta}\big\||x|^{\beta}\nabla_yw_k\big\|_{L^2}
+\|\nabla_y\chi\|_{\infty}\|w_k\|_{L^2(\mathcal A)},
\]
which is bounded uniformly in $k$ by \eqref{eq:rk-L2bound}, while $[\chi w_k]_{x,\alpha}$ is bounded by the localization inequality established in the first part of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:ccp-cutoff}, together with $\|K_\chi\|_\infty<\infty$ and the boundedness of $\{w_k\}$ in $L^2(\mathcal A)$.

However, the homogeneous dimension attached to $(\alpha,0)$ is $Q_{\alpha,0}=n+\alpha m$, and $Q_{\alpha,0}>2\alpha$ always holds. Hence Theorem~\ref{thm:global} with $\beta=0$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:frozen-sobolev}
\|v\|_{L^{2^*_{\alpha,0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}^2
\le S_{\alpha,0}(n,m)^{-1}\,\mathcal E_{\alpha,0}(v),
\qquad
2^*_{\alpha,0}=\frac{2Q_{\alpha,0}}{Q_{\alpha,0}-2\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Since $\beta>0$ and $m\ge1$ force $Q_{\alpha,0}=n+\alpha m<n+(\alpha+\beta)m=Q$, and $t\mapsto2t/(t-2\alpha)$ is strictly decreasing on $(2\alpha,\infty)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exponent-gap}
2^*_{\alpha,\beta}<2^*_{\alpha,0} .
\end{equation}
Consequently $\{\varphi_k\}$ is bounded in $L^{2^*_{\alpha,0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})$ by \eqref{eq:frozen-sobolev}, and $\varphi_k\to0$ a.e.\ because $w_k\to0$ a.e. Since \eqref{eq:exponent-gap} places $2^*_{\alpha,\beta}$ strictly below $2^*_{\alpha,0}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:vitali-upgrade} applies with $r=2^*_{\alpha,\beta}$ and $q=2^*_{\alpha,0}$ and gives $\int_{\mathcal A}|\chi w_k|^{2^*}\,dz\to0$. Because $\chi\equiv1$ on $\mathcal A'$ this yields $\int_{\mathcal A'}|w_k|^{2^*}\,dz\to0$, and testing the weak-$*$ convergence $|w_k|^{2^*}dz\stackrel{*}{\rightharpoonup}\nu$ against $\psi\in C_c(\mathcal A')$ with $\psi\uparrow\mathbf1_{\mathcal A'}$ gives $\nu(\mathcal A')=0$. Covering $\mathbb{R}^{n+m}\setminus\Sigma$ by countably many such $\mathcal A'$ completes the proof.
