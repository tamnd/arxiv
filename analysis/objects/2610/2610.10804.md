<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:local_contractibility}
    The space~$\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(M,\xi)\right)$ is locally contractible.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix the inclusion~$\iota$ as our preferred embedding. As shown in \cite[Example 2.5.11]{Geiges}, the Legendrian submanifold~$K$ has a neighbourhood in~$M$ that is contactomorphic to a neighbourhood of the zero section of~$\left(J^1(K), \xi_{jet}\right)$, the space of~$1$-jets on~$K$. The zero section corresponds to the Legendrian submanifold~$K$. 
    Any Legendrian embedding sufficiently close to~$\iota$ will have image lying in this model neighbourhood. Consequently, it is enough to study the case where~$(M,\xi)$ is~$(J^1(K),\xi_{jet})$ and~$\iota$ is the zero section. We must prove that this there is a contractible neighbourhood around~$\iota$.

    Fix a Legendrian embedding~$e$ which is~$C^1$-close to~$\iota$ in~$\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(J^1(K),\xi_{jet})\right)$. In particular,~$e(K)$ is~$C^1$-close to~$K$ as Legendrian submanifolds of~$J^1(K)$.~$C^1$-closeness guarantees that~$e(K)$ is the graph of some section of the 1-jet bundle \cite[Proof of Theorem 6.2.3]{Banyaga}.
    We can now appeal to a result due to Lycagin \cite[1.2.4]{Lycagin} (see also \cite[Theorem 6.2.1]{Banyaga}
    ), which states that a section of the 1-jet space~$J^1(K)$ is Legendrian for~$\xi_{jet}$ if and only if it is the 1-jet of a function~$f\colon K \to \mathbb{R}$, with~$f$ being~$C^2$-close to the zero function.
    In particular, accounting for a small reparametrisation of~$e$ to match this section, a Legendrian embedding that is sufficiently close to~$\iota$ corresponds to a map that is close to~$(\operatorname{id},0)$ in~$\operatorname{Diff}(K) \times C^{\infty} (K,\mathbb{R})$, where~$0$ is the constantly vanishing function corresponding to the zero section. 
    We can reverse the process, starting with some~$(\phi, f') \in \operatorname{Diff}(K) \times C^{\infty}(K, \mathbb{R})$ where~$\phi$ is~$C^1$-close to~$\operatorname{id}$ and~$f'$ is~$C^2$-close to~$0$, and obtaining a Legendrian embedding~$C^1$-close to~$\iota$. These two processes are inverses to each other.

    Hence we have a homeomorphism from a neighbourhood~$U_{\iota}$ of~$\iota$ to a neighbourhood~$V_{(\operatorname{id},0)}$ of~$(\operatorname{id},0)$. Now,~$\operatorname{Diff}(K)$ is locally contractible, and the space of~$C^{\infty}$ functions from~$K$ to~$\mathbb{R}$ is convex. Hence, there is a (potentially smaller) contractible neighbourhood of~$(\operatorname{id},0)$ contained in~$V_{(\operatorname{id},0)}$. This gives the desired result once we pull-back via the homeomorphism.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:parametrised_fiber_bundle}
    Let~$(M, \xi)$ be a contact 3-manifold, and let~$K$ be an embedded Legendrian knot. Fix a Legendrian embedding~$\gamma \in \operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(M, \xi)\right)$ of~$K$ into~$M$. Then the map
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        p \colon \operatorname{Cont}_c(M, \xi) & \to & \operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(M, \xi)\right) \\
        \phi & \mapsto & \phi \circ \gamma
    \end{eqnarray*}
    is a locally trivial fibration with fibre~$\operatorname{Cont}_c^{\gamma(K)}(M, \xi)$, the subgroup of contactomorphisms of~$(M, \xi)$ (with compact support) which fix~$\gamma(K)$ \emph{pointwise}. 

    In particular, the parametrised Legendrian embedding space~$\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(M, \xi)\right)$ is~$\operatorname{Cont}_c(M, \xi)$-locally retractile.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We prove that~$\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K, (M, \xi)\right)$ is~$\operatorname{Cont}_c(M, \xi)$-locally retractile, which allows us to invoke the Cerf--Palais criterion, \emph{Lemma \ref{lem:properties of G-locally retractile spaces}(\ref{lemitem:Cerf-Palais criterion})}. 
    
    Moving in~$\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K, (M, \xi)\right)$ by precomposition with contactomorphisms, start with~$K$ viewed as a submanifold of~$M$ and take~$\gamma$ to be the inclusion embedding~$\iota \in \operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(M, \xi)\right)$. 

    Pick a contractible neighbourhood~$U$ of~$\iota$ in~$\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K, (M, \xi)\right)$, which we can do by \emph{Lemma \ref{lem:local_contractibility}}.
    For any embedding~$e \in U$, take a path~$\{e_t\}_{t \in [0, 1]}$ in~$U$ connecting~$e_0 \coloneqq \iota$ to~$e_1 \coloneqq e$. Define a time-dependent vector field~$X^e = \{X_t^e\}_{t \in [0, 1]}$ by
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        X_t^e \colon e_t(K) & \to & TM \\
        e_t(\theta) & \mapsto & \odv{}{s}_{s = t}\!\left(e_s(\theta)\right).
    \end{eqnarray*}

    Define a family of Hamiltonian functions~$\{H^e_t\}_{t \in [0, 1]}$ by~$H^e_t = \alpha(X^e_t)$, where~$\alpha$ is the 1-form associated to~$(M, \xi)$. Precisely, 
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        H^e_t \colon e_t(K) & \to & \mathbb{R} \\
        e_t(\theta) & \mapsto & \alpha\!\left(X^e_t\!\left(e_t(\theta)\right)\right).
    \end{eqnarray*}

    Following the proof of (\cite{Geiges}, Theorem 2.6.2), we can extend the family~$\{H^e_t\}_{t \in [0, 1]}$ to a family~$\{\widetilde{H}^e_t\}_{t \in [0, 1]}$ of compactly supported smooth functions, such that each~$\widetilde{H}^e_t \colon M \to \mathbb{R}$ has Hamiltonian vector field~$\widetilde{X}^e_t$ (that is,~$\widetilde{H}^e_t = \alpha(\widetilde{X}^e_t)$). For each~$t \in [0, 1]$, the restriction of~$\widetilde{X}^e_t$ and~$\widetilde{H}^e_t$ to~$e_t(K)$ will agree with~$X^e_t$ and~$H^e_t$ respectively. We now have a time-dependent contact vector field~$\widetilde{X}^e = \{\widetilde{X}^e_t\}_{t \in [0, 1]}$ defined on all of~$M$. Since~$U$ is contractible, we have a contractible choice of paths~$\{e_t\}$, so we can assume this assignment varies smoothly in~$e \in U$. Integrating, we obtain a family of contactomorphisms~$\{\widetilde{\varphi}^e_t\}_{t \in [0, 1]}$ of~$(M, \xi)$ such that for each~$t \in [0, 1]$ we have
    \[\widetilde{\varphi}^e_t \circ e_0 = e_t.\]
    
    We aim to use this (time-dependent) flow to define a local section~$U \to \operatorname{Cont}(M, \xi)$ which takes~$e = e_1$ to~$\varphi^e_1$. We would like to use an exponential map (mimicking the work of \cite{PalaisLocalTriviality60}), but such is ill-defined for time-dependent vector fields. We therefore take a detour via the canonical time-independent vector field~$\overline{X}^e$ associated to~$\widetilde{X}^e$, for which we can define an exponential map. 

    Concretely, define the time-independent vector field
    \begin{align*}\tag{$\ast$}\label{eq:time_dep_to_time_indept}
        \overline{X}^e \colon [0, 1] \times M & \to T\!\left([0, 1] \times M\right)\\
        (t, x) & \mapsto \!\left(1, \widetilde{X}^e_t(x)\right).
    \end{align*}
    We will view the time-dependent flow~$\{\widetilde{\varphi}^e_t\}_{t \in [0, 1]}$ as a flow of the time-independent vector field~$\overline{X}^e$ by the following trick. Define
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \overline{\varphi}^e \colon D \subseteq \mathbb{R} \times \!\left([0, 1] \times M\right) & \to & [0, 1] \times M \\
        \!\left(s, (t, x)\right) & \mapsto & \overline{\varphi}^e_s(t, x) \coloneqq \!\left(s+t, \widetilde{\varphi}^e_{s+t}\!\left(\left(\widetilde{\varphi}^e_t\right)^{-1}(x)\right)\right),
    \end{eqnarray*}
    where~$D$ is the flow domain on which this map makes sense. Now,~$\overline{\varphi}^e$ is the time-independent flow of~$\overline{X}^e$ if and only if~$\widetilde{\varphi}^e$ is the time-dependent flow of~$\widetilde{X}^e$ (that is,~$\overline{\varphi}^e$ is the unique solution of the time-independent initial value problem if and only if~$\widetilde{\varphi}^e$ is the unique solution of the time-dependent initial value problem). 

    By definition, for any~$(t, x) \in [0, 1] \times M$, we have 
    \[\operatorname{Exp}_{(t, x)}\!\left(s \cdot \overline{X}^e(t, x)\right) = \overline{\varphi}^e_s\!\left(t, x\right) = \!\left(s+t, \widetilde{\varphi}^e_{s+t}\!\left(\left(\widetilde{\varphi}^e_t\right)^{-1}(x)\right)\right).\]
    In particular, taking~$t = 0$ and~$x = e_0(\theta)$ for~$\theta \in S^1$, we have
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \operatorname{Exp}_{\left(0, e_0(\theta)\right)}\!\left(s \cdot \overline{X}^e(0, e_0(\theta))\right) & = & \!\left(s, \widetilde{\varphi}^e_{s}\!\left(\left(\widetilde{\varphi}^e_0\right)^{-1}\!\left(e_0(\theta)\right)\right)\right) \\
        \implies \operatorname{Exp}_{\left(0, e_0(\theta)\right)}\!\left(s \cdot \!\left(1, \widetilde{X}^e_0\!\left(e_0(\theta)\right)\right)\right) & = & \!\left(s, \widetilde{\varphi}^e_{s}\!\left(e_0(\theta)\right)\right) \\
        & = & \!\left(s, e_s(\theta)\right)
    \end{eqnarray*}
    and in particular
    \[\operatorname{Exp}_{\left(0, e_0(\theta)\right)}\!\left(\overline{X}^e_0\!\left(e_0(\theta)\right)\right) = \!\left(1, e_1(\theta)\right).\]

    Let~$N$ be a compact neighbourhood of~$e_0(K)=\iota(K)$ in~$M$ on which the time-dependent flow~$\{\widetilde{\varphi}^e_t\}_{t \in [0, 1]}$ is supported. Write~$X_N(TM)$ for the space of vector fields on~$M$ supported on~$N$ and write~$X_N\!\left(T\!\left([0, 1] \times M\right)\right)$ for the space of vector fields on~$[0, 1] \times M$ which are supported on~$[0, 1] \times N$. 
    
    We define the map 
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \overline{S} \colon U & \to & X_N(T([0,1]\times M)) \\
        e & \mapsto & \overline{X}^e
    \end{eqnarray*}
    as the composition of the above Hamiltonian map and the assignment in (\ref{eq:time_dep_to_time_indept}). Since the choice of~$\widetilde{X}^e$ varied smoothly in~$e$, we have that~$\overline{S}$ is smooth.
    Define the map
    \[\overline{E} \colon X_N\!\left(T\!\left([0, 1] \times M\right)\right) \to \operatorname{Diff}([0, 1] \times M)\]
    by~$\overline{E}(\overline{Y})(s, x) = \operatorname{Exp}_{(0, x)}(s \cdot \overline{Y}(0, x))$ where this assignment is a diffeomorphism, and smoothed off to the identity elsewhere. 

    Let~$\operatorname{LPDiff}([0, 1] \times M)$ be the subset of~$\operatorname{Diff}([0, 1] \times M)$ consisting of level-preserving diffeomorphisms; that is, diffeomorphisms~$g$ such that for each~$s \in [0, 1]$, we have~$g(\{s\} \times M) \subseteq \{s\} \times M$. Define the map
    \[\pi_2 \colon \operatorname{LPDiff}([0, 1] \times M) \to \operatorname{Diff}(M)\]
    by, for any~$g \in \operatorname{LPDiff}([0, 1] \times M)$,
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \pi_2(f) \colon M & \to & M \\
        x & \mapsto & p_2(g(1, x)),
    \end{eqnarray*}
    where~$p_2 \colon [0, 1] \times M \to M$ is the projection. 
    
    We are now ready to define our local section as
    \[\sigma \coloneqq \pi_2 \circ \overline{E} \circ \overline{S} \colon U \to \operatorname{Cont}_c(M, \xi).\]
    We first show that this is well-defined. Let~$e \in U$; we need~$\left(\overline{E} \circ \overline{S}\right)(e)$ to be a level-preserving diffeomorphism of~$[0, 1] \times M$ such that the time-1 diffeomorphism is actually a contactomorphism. Let~$(s, x) \in [0, 1] \times M$; now
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \left(\left(\overline{E} \circ \overline{S}\right)(e)\right)(s, x) & = & \overline{E}\left(\overline{X}^e\right)(s, x) \\
        & = & \operatorname{Exp}_{(0, x)}\!\left(s \cdot \overline{X}^e(0, x)\right) \\
        & = & \overline{\varphi}^e_s(0, x) \\
        & = & \left(s, \widetilde{\varphi}^e_s\!\left((\widetilde{\varphi}^e_0)^{-1}(x)\right)\right) \in \{s\} \times M.
    \end{eqnarray*}
    This verifies both requirements, since the~$\widetilde{\varphi}^e_s$ are contactomorphisms. Hence~$\sigma$ is a well-defined map. Furthermore from this calculation, we have, for any~$\theta \in S^1$,
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        (p \circ \sigma)(e)(\theta) & = & \left(\sigma(e) \circ \iota\right)(\theta) \\
        & = & (\sigma(e))(e_0(\theta)) \\
        & = & \widetilde{\varphi}^e_1\!\left((\widetilde{\varphi}^e_0)^{-1}(e_0(\theta))\right) \\
        & = & \widetilde{\varphi}^e_1(e_0(\theta)) = e(\theta),
    \end{eqnarray*}
    so~$p \circ \sigma = \operatorname{id}_U$. It is clear that~$\sigma$ is continuous, since~$\overline{S}$ was smooth. Hence~$\sigma$ is a local section of~$p$ near~$\iota \in \operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K, (M, \xi)\right)$. 
    
    We conclude that~$\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(M, \xi)\right)$ is~$\operatorname{Cont}_c(M, \xi)$-locally retractile. We can now apply the Cerf-Palais criterion to conclude that~$p$ is a locally trivial fibration, as required.

    \[\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(M, \xi)\right)\to \operatorname{LSub}\!\left(K,(M, \xi)\right)\]
    is trivial over~$\widehat{V}_{\iota}$. We split the proof into six steps, following \cite[13.11]{MichorManifoldsDiffMappings80}.
    
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Define~$s_{\iota}: \widehat{V}_{\iota} \to \operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(M, \xi)\right)$ by~$s_{\iota}=(u\big|_{{V_{\iota}}})^{-1}$.         \item The fibres of~$u$ over~$\widehat{V}_{\iota}$ meet~$V_{\iota}$ in exactly one point by \emph{Lemma \ref{lem:prep for param_to_unparam prop in contact setting} (i)}.         \[(\rho\big|_{\operatorname{Diff} S^1 \times V_{\iota}})^{-1} \eqqcolon (\gamma_{\iota},\delta_{\iota}) \colon u^{-1}(\widehat{V}_{\iota}) \to \operatorname{Diff}(S^1) \times V_{\iota}.\]
        Hence we have maps~$\gamma_{\iota} \colon u^{-1}(\widehat{V}_{\iota}) \to \operatorname{Diff}(S^1)$ and~$\delta_{\iota} \colon u^{-1}(\widehat{V}_{\iota}) \to V_{\iota}$ such that for any element~$j \in u^{-1}(\widehat{V}_{\iota})$ we have
        \[\delta_{\iota}(j)\circ \gamma_{\iota}(j) = \rho(\gamma_{\iota}(j),\delta_{\iota}(j))=j\]
        and, since~$\delta_{\iota}(j) \in V_{\iota}$, we have~$p_K \circ \delta_{\iota}(j) = \iota$.
        \item \textbf{Claim.}~$\gamma_{\iota}$ is smooth. 
        \begin{proof}
            We have 
            \begin{eqnarray*}
                \iota \circ \gamma_{\iota}(j) & = & \!\left(p_K \circ \delta_{\iota}(j)\right) \circ \gamma_{\iota}(j) \\
                & = & p_K(j) \circ \!\left(\delta_{\iota}(j) \circ \gamma_{\iota}(j)\right) = p_K\circ j,
            \end{eqnarray*}
            by step (2). Hence~$\gamma_{\iota}(j) = \rho(\cdot,\iota)^{-1}\circ (p_K)_*(j)$, which means~$\gamma_{\iota} = \rho(\cdot,\iota)^{-1}\circ (p_K)_*$, and the latter is smooth. 
        \end{proof}
        \item \textbf{Claim.}~$\delta_{\iota}$ is smooth.
        \begin{proof}
            We have~$\delta_{\iota}(j) \circ \gamma_{\iota}(j)= j$, so~$\delta_{\iota}(j)=j \circ \gamma_{\iota}(j)^{-1}$ and ~$\delta_{\iota}=\rho \circ(inv \circ \gamma_{\iota}, \operatorname{id})$, which is smooth (here we denote by~$inv$ the map that takes a diffeomorphism of~$S^1$ to its inverse).
        \end{proof}
        \item By the above steps,~$\rho$ is a diffeomorphism. This is our trivialization map.
        \item \textbf{Claim.} The map~$s_{\iota}: \widehat{V}_{\iota} \to V_{\iota}$ constructed in step (1) is continuous (and hence a homeomorphism onto its image).
        \begin{proof}
            For~$\hat{y} \in \widehat{V}_{\iota}$ we have~$$ \{ s_{\iota}(\hat{y})\} = \delta_{\iota}(u^{-1}(\hat{y}))$$ by construction. Let~$T \subset V_{\iota}$ be open. Then by step (4),~$\delta_{\iota}^{-1}(T)$ is open in~$u^{-1}(\widehat{V}_{\iota})$, which in turn is open in~$\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(M,\xi)\right)$ , so 
            \[u^{-1}(s_{\iota}^{-1}(T))=u^{-1}(u(T))=\delta_{\iota}^{-1}(T)\]
            is open in~$\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(M,\xi)\right)$. Now by the definition of quotient topology,~$s_{\iota}^{-1}(T)$ is open in~$\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K,(M,\xi)\right)$. 
        \end{proof}
    \end{enumerate}

    We thus have constructed our local trivializations.

    \[\begin{tikzcd}\tag{$\dagger$}\label{eq:long_exact_sequence_fibration}
	   \vdots && \\
	   {\pi_1\!\left(\operatorname{Diff}\!\left(S^1\right), \operatorname{id}\right)} & {\pi_1\!\left(\operatorname{LEmb}_{(p_0, v_0)}\!\left(K,S^3\right), \iota\right) \times \pi_1\!\left(\operatorname{U}(2), \operatorname{id}\right)} & {\pi_1\!\left(\mathcal{LE}(\iota), \iota(K)\right) } \\
	   && {0 \xleftarrow{g_\ast} \faktor{\mathbb{Z}}{2\mathbb{Z}},}
	   \arrow["{d_2}"', from=1-1, to=2-1]
	   \arrow["{g_1}", from=2-1, to=2-2]
	   \arrow["{h_1}", from=2-2, to=2-3]
	   \arrow["{d_1}", from=2-3, to=3-3]
    \end{tikzcd}\]
    where we have used functoriality of~$\pi_1$ in the middle term.

    \begin{claim*}
       ~$\text{im }(g_1) \subseteq \pi_1\!\left(\operatorname{U}(2),\operatorname{id}\right)$.
    \end{claim*}

    \begin{proof}[Proof of Claim.]
        By definition of the action of~$\pi_1(\operatorname{Diff}(S^1),\operatorname{id})$ on~$\pi_1(\operatorname{LEmb}_{(p_0,v_0)}\!\left(K,S^3\right),\iota)$, a loop of diffeomorphisms of~$S^1$ reparametrises the long knots. However, these are pointed knots, so in each of these diffeomorphisms, the image of~$0$ is always~$p_0$. Thus the loop of diffeomorphisms maps to a loop of reparametrisations of the interval. By the obvious `straightening', this is a null-homotopic loop of long Legendrian knots.
    \end{proof}

    Hence we can extract the following exact sequence:
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         
    \[\begin{tikzcd}[cramped]\tag{$\ddagger$}\label{eq:extracted exact sequence}
	   0 & {\pi_1\!\left(\operatorname{LEmb}_{(p_0, v_0)}\!\left(K, \ensuremath{\!\left(S^3, \xi_{std}\right)}\right)\right)} & {\pi_1\!\left(\mathcal{LE}(\iota), \iota(K)\right)} & {\pi_0\!\left(\operatorname{Diff}\!\left(S^1\right)\right)} & 0.
	   \arrow[from=1-1, to=1-2]
	   \arrow["{\overline{h}_1}", from=1-2, to=1-3]
	   \arrow["{d_1}", from=1-3, to=1-4]
	   \arrow[from=1-4, to=1-5]
    \end{tikzcd}\]
    
    We now unpack the sequence (\ref{eq:long_exact_sequence_fibration}). The connecting homomorphism~$d_1$ is defined as follows. A Legendrian motion~$f = \{f_t\} \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{L}\!\left(K, \ensuremath{\!\left(S^3, \xi_{std}\right)}\right)}$ lifts to a path~$\{\widetilde{f}_t\}$ in~$\operatorname{LEmb}\!\left(K, \ensuremath{\!\left(S^3, \xi_{std}\right)}\right)$. The endpoint of this path can be written~$\widetilde{f}_1 = f_1 \circ \phi_1$, for~$\phi_1 \in \operatorname{Diff}\!\left(S^1\right)$. Then~$d_1(f)$ is the class~$[\phi_1] \in \pi_0\!\left(\operatorname{Diff}\!\left(S^1\right)\right) \cong \faktor{\mathbb{Z}}{2\mathbb{Z}}$. Hence if a motion corresponds to a Legendrian inversion of~$K$, then it maps to the generator of~$\pi_0\!\left(\operatorname{Diff}\!\left(S^1\right)\right)$ under~$d_1$. 

    Thus if~$K$ is not Legendrian invertible, then~$\ker(d_1) = \pi_1\!\left(\mathcal{LE}(\iota), \iota(K)\right)$. Hence from (\ref{eq:extracted exact sequence}),~$\overline{h}_1$ is both injective and surjective, and we have an isomorphism
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:L motion group iso to pi1 of long embeddings}
        \pi_1\!\left(\operatorname{LEmb}_{(p_0, v_0)}\!\left(K, \ensuremath{\!\left(S^3, \xi_{std}\right)}\right)\right) \cong \pi_1\!\left(\mathcal{LE}(\iota), \iota(K)\right).
    \end{equation}

    If on the other hand~$K$ is Legendrian invertible, let~$\{f_t\}$ be a motion given by some isotopy which inverts~$K$, obtained by looking at the trace of submanifolds. Under~$d_1$ this maps to the generator of~$\pi_0\!\left(\operatorname{Diff}\!\left(S^1\right)\right)$, so now by (\ref{eq:extracted exact sequence}) we have  that~$\pi_1\!\left(\mathcal{LE}(\iota), \iota(K)\right)$ is an extension of~$\pi_1\!\left(\operatorname{LEmb}_{(p_0, v_0)}\!\left(K, \ensuremath{\!\left(S^3, \xi_{std}\right)}\right)\right)$ by~$\faktor{\mathbb{Z}}{2\mathbb{Z}}$. Moreover, a splitting is given by mapping the generator of~$\faktor{\mathbb{Z}}{2\mathbb{Z}}$ to any motion realising an inversion of~$K$.

    Finally, in \cite{Min-Fernandez,Fernandez-Aguinaga-Presas} it is shown that there is a homotopy equivalence \begin{equation}\label{eq:homotopy_equivalence_loop_space_contactomorphism}
        \Omega\!\left(\operatorname{LEmb}_{(p_0,v_0)}\!\left(K,S^3\right),\iota\right) \simeq \operatorname{Cont}^{\partial}(C(K),\xi|)
    \end{equation} 
    where~$\Omega$ denotes the pointed loop space, and where~$\operatorname{Cont}^{\partial}(C(K),\xi|)$ is the space of contactomorphisms of the Legendrian knot exterior (with contact structure induced by~$\xi$) that fix the boundary. Putting this together with the identification~$\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}\!\left(K, \ensuremath{\!\left(S^3, \xi_{std}\right)}\right)} \cong \pi_1\!\left(\mathcal{LE}(\iota), \iota(K)\right)$ from \emph{Theorem \ref{thm:legendrian motion group as pi_1 of embedding space}} gives an isomorphism
    \[\ensuremath{\pi_0\!\left(\operatorname{Cont}^\partial\!\left(C(K), \xi_\vert\right)\right)} \cong \ensuremath{\mathcal{L}\!\left(K, \ensuremath{\!\left(S^3, \xi_{std}\right)}\right)},\]
    which is (a) from the statement.
    
    Similarly in the case of~$K$ invertible, we obtain
    \[\ensuremath{\pi_0\!\left(\operatorname{Cont}^\partial\!\left(C(K), \xi_\vert\right)\right)}\rtimes \faktor{\mathbb{Z}}{2\mathbb{Z}} \cong \ensuremath{\mathcal{L}\!\left(K, \ensuremath{\!\left(S^3, \xi_{std}\right)}\right)},\]
    which is (b).
