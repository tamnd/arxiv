Beyond the approximation of the trajectory functions themselves,
optimal control problems also require accurate approximations of
their derivatives. Here, we discuss the relationship between the
derivative of a truncated Fourier expansion and the truncated
Fourier expansion of the derivative, and examine how the
Krylov--Lanczos correction improves the approximation of derivatives.

Since the truncated Fourier expansion $S_N[f](t)=f_N(t)$
is a finite linear combination of trigonometric functions,
its derivative can be computed analytically by differentiating
term by term:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prelim_fourier_derivative}
\dot{f}_N(t)=
\sum_{n=1}^{N}
\omega_n
\Bigl(
-a_n(f)\sin(\omega_n t)
+b_n(f)\cos(\omega_n t)
\Bigr),
\qquad t\in[0,T].
\end{equation}

Suppose that $f\in C^1([0,T])$ satisfies the endpoint
compatibility condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kl_F_periodic_zero}
f(T)=f(0).
\end{equation}
Then, differentiation commutes with the truncated Fourier
projection, that is,
\begin{equation*}
\frac{d}{dt}S_N[f](t)
=
\dot{f}_N(t)
=
S_N[\dot{f}](t).
\end{equation*}
Indeed, integration by parts shows that the Fourier
coefficients of $\dot{f}$ are $\omega_n b_n(f)$ and
$-\omega_n a_n(f)$, while $a_0(\dot{f})=0$.

However, when $f(T)\neq f(0)$, this property no longer
holds. In this case, integration by parts introduces
additional boundary terms, and differentiating the
truncated Fourier expansion of $f$ does not yield the
truncated Fourier expansion of $\dot{f}$. Hence, special care is needed when approximating derivatives.

In the case of Krylov--Lanczos approximation for the remainder $F=f-P_q$ (satisfies
the endpoint compatibility conditions up to order $q-1$).  Consequently, for $q\geqslant1$, we have
\begin{equation*}
\frac{d}{dt}S_N[F](t)
=
S_N[\dot{F}](t).
\end{equation*}
Differentiating the Krylov--Lanczos approximation
\eqref{eq:kl_final_approx}, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kl_final_derivative}
\dot{I}_{N,q}^{\mathrm{KL}}(t)=
\dot{F}_N(t)
+
\frac{A_0}{T}
+
\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}A_k B_{k-1}(t),
\end{equation}
where the last sum is understood to be empty when $q=1$.

We now state the corresponding convergence estimates for the derivatives. Let $f\in C^{q+1}([0,T])$,
with $q\geqslant2$. Since
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kl_derivative_error_identity}
\dot{f}(t)
-
\dot{I}_{N,q}^{\mathrm{KL}}(t)
=
\dot{F}(t)-\dot{F}_N(t),
\end{equation}
and the corrected remainder satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kl_derivative_periodicity}
\dot{F}^{(j)}(T)
=
\dot{F}^{(j)}(0),
\qquad
j=0,1,\ldots,q-2,
\end{equation}
the standard Fourier approximation estimates applied
to $\dot{F}\in C^q([0,T])$ yield
\begin{equation*}
\left\|
\dot{f}
-
\dot{I}_{N,q}^{\mathrm{KL}}
\right\|_{L^2[0,T]}
=
\mathcal{O}\left(
\frac{1}{N^{q-\frac12}}
\right),\qquad
\max_{t\in[0,T]}
\left|
\dot{f}(t)
-
\dot{I}_{N,q}^{\mathrm{KL}}(t)
\right|
=
\mathcal{O}\left(
\frac{1}{N^{q-1}}
\right),
\qquad N\to\infty.
\end{equation*}

Thus, the Krylov--Lanczos correction not only improves
the approximation of the original function but also
provides improved convergence rates for its derivatives.
