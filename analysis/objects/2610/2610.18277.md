<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
  Let $m\ge3$ be odd, and let $W$ be a graphon with $t(K_{2},W)=p\ge2/3$.
  Then,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main}
    t(C_{m},W)\gep^{m}-p(1-p)^{m-1}.
  \end{equation}
  Equality holds if and only if $W$ is equal to $W_k$ up to relabelling for some integer $k\ge3$, or $W\equiv1$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:commonality}
  Let $m\ge3$ be odd, and let $W$ be a graphon with $t(K_{2},W)=p\ge2/3$.
  Then, with $P_{m-1}$ the path with $m-1$ edges,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-commonality}
    t(C_{m},W)+t(C_{m},1-W)\ge p^m+(1-p)^m+\bigl[t(P_{m-1},1-W)-(1-p)^{m-1}\bigr].
  \end{equation}
  Furthermore, equality holds if and only if $W$ is $p$-regular.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:step-reduction}
  It suffices to prove the inequalities in \Cref{thm:main,thm:commonality} for graphons that
  are constant on each rectangle $I_a\times I_b$, where
  $I_1,\ldots,I_N$ partition $[0,1]$ into intervals of length $1/N$
  and $N$ ranges over all positive integers.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:half-norm}
  Every eigenvalue of $A$ lies in $[-1/2,1/2]$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Throughout this proof, vector coordinates and matrix entries refer to the standard basis of $\mathbb R^N$, indexed by the partition intervals $I_1,\ldots,I_N$.
  In these coordinates,
  \[
    0\le (M_U)_{ab}=\frac{U_{ab}}{N}\le\frac1N=(M_1)_{ab},
    \qquad 1\le a,b\le N,
  \]
  so $0\le M_U\le M_1$ entrywise.
  Let $f\in\mathcal E_0$ with $\left\lVert f\right\rVert=1$, and define its coordinatewise
  positive and negative parts by
  \[
    (f_+)_i=\max\{f_i,0\},
    \qquad
    (f_-)_i=\max\{-f_i,0\}.
  \]
  Both vectors have nonnegative entries, $f=f_+-f_-$, and $|f|=f_++f_-$.
  Since $e^{\mathsf T}f=0$,
  \[
    e^{\mathsf T}f_+=e^{\mathsf T}f_-=:a,
    \qquad
    2a=e^{\mathsf T}|f|\le\left\lVert e\right\rVert\left\lVert f\right\rVert=1.
  \]
  Expanding $f=f_+-f_-$ gives
  \[
    f^{\mathsf T}M_Uf
    =f_+^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_+
     -f_+^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_-
     -f_-^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_+
     +f_-^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_-.
  \]
  Since $f_+$, $f_-$, and $M_U$ have nonnegative entries,
  $f_+^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_-$ and $f_-^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_+$ are nonnegative.
  Dropping these terms gives
  \[
    f^{\mathsf T}M_Uf
    \le f_+^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_++f_-^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_-
      \le2a^2\le\frac12,
  \]
  where the second inequality uses the entrywise comparison $M_U\le M_1$
  in the standard coordinates, the nonnegative entries of $f_+$ and $f_-$,
  and the identities $f_+^{\mathsf T}M_1f_+=f_-^{\mathsf T}M_1f_-=a^2$.  Similarly,
  $f_+^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_+$ and $f_-^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_-$ are nonnegative,
  so dropping these terms gives
  \[
    f^{\mathsf T}M_Uf
    \ge-f_+^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_- - f_-^{\mathsf T}M_Uf_+
      \ge-2a^2\ge-\frac12.
  \]
  Here the second inequality again uses the entrywise comparison $M_U\le M_1$
  in the standard coordinates and the nonnegative entries of $f_+$ and $f_-$,
  together with $f_+^{\mathsf T}M_1f_-=f_-^{\mathsf T}M_1f_+=a^2$.
  Since $f^{\mathsf T}Af=f^{\mathsf T}M_Uf$, the Rayleigh--Ritz
  characterisation of the eigenvalues of the symmetric map $A$ proves the
  claim.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:spectral-moments}
  The measure $\mu$ is independent of the choice of orthonormal eigenvectors
  within each eigenspace. For every integer $j\ge0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
    \operatorname{supp}\mu\subseteq[-1/2,1/2],
    \qquad
    g^{\mathsf T}A^jg=\int\lambda^j\,\mathrm d\mu(\lambda),
    \qquad
    \mu(\mathbb R)=\left\lVert g\right\rVert^2.
  \end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By the spectral decomposition of $A$,
  \[
    g^{\mathsf T}A^jg
    =\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\lambda_i^j\left\lVert P_ig\right\rVert^2
    =\int\lambda^j\,\mathrm d\mu(\lambda).
  \]
  Taking $j=0$ gives the total mass identity, and the support bound follows from \Cref{lem:half-norm}.
  The sum of weights at each eigenvalue is the squared norm of the projection of $g$ onto its eigenspace, so $\mu$ is independent of the eigenbasis.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:cycle-excursions}
  For every odd $m\ge3$,

    t(C_{m},U)

We first prove \eqref{eq:cycle-U}.
  In \eqref{eq:trace-projection-expansion}, the word consisting only of $P$ gives $q^m$, while the word consisting only of $Q$ gives $\operatorname{Tr}(A^m)$.

  We now consider the remaining words, which contain both $P$ and $Q$.
  For a mixed word with $r$ cyclic $Q$-blocks, choose a boundary where $Q$ is followed by $P$ and rotate the word to start with $P$ and end with $Q$, which does not change the trace.
  For $1\le i\le r$, write the lengths of the $i$th $P$-block and the following $Q$-block as $\alpha_i+1$ and $\beta_i+1$, respectively, with $\alpha_i,\beta_i\ge0$.
  To evaluate the corresponding trace, use the block forms in \eqref{eq:block-PQ} and \eqref{eq:block-UW} to obtain
  \[
    Q M_U=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\g&A\end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    (Q M_U)^{\beta_i+1}P
    =\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\A^{\beta_i}g&0\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  The second identity follows by induction on $\beta_i$: right-multiplication by $P$ gives the case $\beta_i=0$, and left-multiplication by $Q M_U$ multiplies the lower-left block by $A$.
  The block forms also give $P M_U P=qP$, and left-multiplication of the second identity by $P M_U$ yields
  \[
    P M_U(Q M_U)^{\beta_i+1}P
    =(g^{\mathsf T}A^{\beta_i}g)P.
  \]
  Using $P M_U P=qP$, the pair of blocks, ending at the next $P$, satisfies
  \[
    (P M_U)^{\alpha_i+1}(Q M_U)^{\beta_i+1}P
    =q^{\alpha_i}(g^{\mathsf T}A^{\beta_i}g)P.
  \]
  By cyclicity and $P^2=P$, appending a final $P$ does not change the trace.
  Multiplying the block factors and using $\operatorname{Tr}(P)=1$ gives the length condition and trace formula
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:excursion-length-weight}
    \sum_{i=1}^r(\alpha_i+\beta_i)=m-2r,
    \qquad
    \operatorname{Tr}(R_1M_UR_2M_U\cdots R_mM_U)
    =q^{\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_r}
     \prod_{i=1}^rg^{\mathsf T}A^{\beta_i}g.
  \end{equation}

  We count pairs consisting of a mixed word with positions labelled $1,\ldots,m$ and $r$ cyclic $Q$-blocks, together with a marked boundary where $Q$ is followed by $P$.
  Each word has exactly $r$ choices of the mark.
  Conversely, an ordered tuple $(\alpha_1,\beta_1,\ldots,\alpha_r,\beta_r)$ of nonnegative integers satisfying the length condition in \eqref{eq:excursion-length-weight}, together with the labelled position $j$ of the first $P$ after the mark, determines exactly one marked word: place the successive $P$- and $Q$-blocks of the specified lengths cyclically from $j$.
  Each tuple therefore gives $m$ marked words, distinct even for periodic words because their marks occupy different labelled positions.
  These two constructions are mutually inverse, and by cyclicity and \eqref{eq:excursion-length-weight}, the trace weight depends only on the tuple.
  Summing the weights in these two ways gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:U-r-excursions}
      \Bigg(
        r\!\!\!\!
        \sum_{\substack{R_1\cdots R_m\in\{P,Q\}^m\\
                       \text{both $P$ and $Q$ occur}\\
                       R_1\cdots R_m\text{ has }r\text{ cyclic $Q$-blocks}}}
        \operatorname{Tr}(R_1M_U\cdots R_mM_U)\Bigg)
      =
      \Bigg(
        m \!\!\!\!
        \sum_{\substack{\alpha_1,\beta_1,\ldots,
                          \alpha_r,\beta_r\ge0\\
                          \sum_{i=1}^r(\alpha_i+\beta_i)=m-2r}}
        q^{\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_r}
        \prod_{i=1}^rg^{\mathsf T}A^{\beta_i}g\Bigg).
  \end{equation}
  Dividing \eqref{eq:U-r-excursions} by $r$ gives the factor $m/r$.
  By \eqref{eq:moments} and the definition \eqref{eq:h-def},
  \begin{align*}
    &\sum_{\substack{\alpha_1,\beta_1,\ldots,\alpha_r,\beta_r\ge0\\
                     \sum_{i=1}^r(\alpha_i+\beta_i)=m-2r}}
      q^{\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_r}
      \prod_{i=1}^rg^{\mathsf T}A^{\beta_i}g\\
    &\quad=\int\!\cdots\!\int
      \sum_{\substack{\alpha_1,\beta_1,\ldots,\alpha_r,\beta_r\ge0\\
                     \sum_{i=1}^r(\alpha_i+\beta_i)=m-2r}}
      q^{\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_r}
      \lambda_1^{\beta_1}\cdots\lambda_r^{\beta_r}
      \prod_{i=1}^r\,\mathrm d\mu(\lambda_i)\\
    &\quad=\int\!\cdots\!\int
      h_{m-2r}(q^{\times r},\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)
      \prod_{i=1}^r\,\mathrm d\mu(\lambda_i).
  \end{align*}
  Summing over $r$ and adding $q^m+\operatorname{Tr}(A^m)$ proves \eqref{eq:cycle-U}.

  For $M_W$, \eqref{eq:block-UW} replaces
  $(q,g,A)$ by $(p,-g,-A)$.  The two crossing signs cancel in each block,
  while
  \[
    (-g)^{\mathsf T}(-A)^{\beta_i}(-g)
    =(-1)^{\beta_i}g^{\mathsf T}A^{\beta_i}g.
  \]
  Hence $q$ becomes $p$, every $\lambda_i$ becomes $-\lambda_i$, and
  $\operatorname{Tr}(A^m)$ becomes $-\operatorname{Tr}(A^m)$.  This proves \eqref{eq:cycle-W}.

\label{lem:pge23-dirichlet-moment}
  Let $k\ge1$ and $j\ge0$ be integers, let $x_1,\ldots,x_k\in\mathbb R$, and
  let $(\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k)$ be uniformly distributed on the simplex
  \[
    \Theta_i\ge0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^k\Theta_i=1.
  \]
  Equivalently, this vector has the $\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1)$
  distribution.
  Then the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial $h_j$, defined in
  \eqref{eq:h-def}, admits the representation
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-dirichlet-moment}
    h_j(x_1,\ldots,x_k)
    =\binom{j+k-1}{k-1}
    \mathbb E\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^k\Theta_i x_i\right)^j\right].
  \end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Write $S=\sum_{i=1}^k\Theta_i x_i$. The multinomial theorem gives
  \[
    S^j=\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^k\Theta_i x_i\Bigr)^j
    =\sum_{\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_k=j}
    \frac{j!}{\alpha_1!\cdots\alpha_k!}
    \prod_{i=1}^k\Theta_i^{\alpha_i}x_i^{\alpha_i},
  \]
  where the sum is over nonnegative integer exponents.
  The Dirichlet moments satisfy
  \[
    \mathbb E\left[\prod_{i=1}^k\Theta_i^{\alpha_i}\right]
    =\frac{(k-1)!\,\alpha_1!\cdots\alpha_k!}{(j+k-1)!},
    \qquad \alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_k=j.
  \]
  Taking expectations therefore gives
  \begin{align*}
    \mathbb E\left[S^j\right]
    &=\sum_{\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_k=j}
    \frac{j!}{\alpha_1!\cdots\alpha_k!}
    \cdot\frac{(k-1)!\,\alpha_1!\cdots\alpha_k!}{(j+k-1)!}
    \prod_{i=1}^k x_i^{\alpha_i}\\
    &=\frac{j!\,(k-1)!}{(j+k-1)!}
    \sum_{\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_k=j}\prod_{i=1}^k x_i^{\alpha_i}
    =\frac{h_j(x_1,\ldots,x_k)}{\binom{j+k-1}{k-1}}.
  \end{align*}
  Multiplying by $\binom{j+k-1}{k-1}$ proves
  \eqref{eq:pge23-dirichlet-moment}.

  Setting all $r$ variables equal in the definition \eqref{eq:h-def}
  of the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial $h_j$ gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-h-diagonal}
    h_j(\lambda,\ldots,\lambda)=\binom{j+r-1}{r-1}\lambda^j,
  \end{equation}
  the binomial coefficient being the number of monomials of degree $j$ in $r$ variables.

  It remains to express the terms of $P_{m,r}$ through
  $h_j(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)$.
  The generating function of the $h_d$ is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-h-generating}
    \sum_{d\ge0}h_d(y_1,\ldots,y_k)z^d
    =\prod_{i=1}^k\frac{1}{1-y_iz}.
  \end{equation}
  Thus, the $k$ equal variables and the remaining ones give separate factors:
  \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \sum_{d\ge0}h_d(a^{\times k},b_1,\ldots,b_r)z^d
      &=\frac{1}{(1-az)^k}\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{1}{1-b_iz}\\
      &=\Bigl(\sum_{s\ge0}\binom{s+k-1}{k-1}a^sz^s\Bigr)
        \Bigl(\sum_{j\ge0}h_j(b_1,\ldots,b_r)z^j\Bigr).
    \end{aligned}
  \]
  Comparing the coefficients of $z^d$ gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-h-split}
    h_d(a^{\times k},b_1,\ldots,b_r)
    =\sum_{j=0}^d
    \binom{d-j+k-1}{k-1}a^{d-j}h_j(b_1,\ldots,b_r).
  \end{equation}
  Apply this identity to the three terms of $P_{m,r}$: to the first two
  with $k=r$ and $a=p$ or $q$, and to the third with $k=r+1$ and $a=q$.
  Since $h_j$ is homogeneous of degree $j$,
  $h_j(-\lambda_1,\ldots,-\lambda_r)=(-1)^jh_j(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)$.
  Therefore,
  \begin{align}
    P_{m,r}(q;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)
    ={}&\frac {m}{r}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n-j+r-1}{r-1}
    \bigl((-1)^jp^{n-j}+q^{n-j}\bigr)
    h_j(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r) \notag\\
    &-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1-j+r}{r}q^{n-1-j}
    h_j(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r). \label{eq:pge23-P-in-h}
  \end{align}

  Write the expansion \eqref{eq:pge23-P-in-h} as
  \[
    P_{m,r}(q;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)
    =\sum_{j=0}^nc_j\,h_j(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r),
  \]
  where $c_j$ denotes the combined coefficient of $h_j$; it does not
  depend on the $\lambda_i$.
  Setting $\lambda_1=\cdots=\lambda_r=\lambda$ and using
  \eqref{eq:pge23-h-diagonal},
  \begin{equation*}
    \widetilde P_{m,r}(q,\lambda)
    =\sum_{j=0}^nc_j\binom{j+r-1}{r-1}\lambda^j.
  \end{equation*}
  On the other hand, \Cref{lem:pge23-dirichlet-moment}, applied with
  $k=r$ and $x_i=\lambda_i$, gives
  \begin{align*}
    P_{m,r}(q;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)
    &=\sum_{j=0}^nc_j\binom{j+r-1}{r-1}\mathbb E\left[S^j\right]\\
    &=\mathbb E\left[\sum_{j=0}^nc_j\binom{j+r-1}{r-1}S^j\right]\\
    &=\mathbb E\left[\widetilde P_{m,r}(q,S)\right],
  \end{align*}
  the last equality being $\widetilde P_{m,r}$ with $\lambda$ replaced by $S$.

  For the second claim, suppose that the $\lambda_i$ lie in
  $[-1/2,1/2]$. Then, every convex combination $S$ lies in the same
  interval, so the right-hand side of
  \eqref{eq:pge23-dirichlet-identity} is at least
  $\min_{\lambda\in[-1/2,1/2]}\widetilde P_{m,r}(q,\lambda)$.
  Taking the minimum over $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r$ shows that the
  minimum of $P_{m,r}$ on $[-1/2,1/2]^r$ is at least this diagonal minimum.
  Conversely, setting $\lambda_1=\cdots=\lambda_r=\lambda$ attains each
  diagonal value, so the minimum of $P_{m,r}$ on $[-1/2,1/2]^r$ is at most
  the diagonal minimum. The two minima are therefore equal.

\label{lem:pge23-beta}
  Let $m\ge3$ be odd, let $r$ be an integer satisfying
  $1\le r\le(m-1)/2$, and set $n=m-2r$.
  Let $X\sim\operatorname{Beta}(r,r)$ and define
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-rho}
    \rho_{n,m}(u)
    =\frac {m}{n}\bigl(u^n+(1-u)^n\bigr)-u^{n-1},
    \qquad u\in\mathbb R.
  \end{equation}
  Then, for every $q,\lambda\in\mathbb R$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-beta-second}
    \widetilde P_{m,r}(q,\lambda)
    =\binom{n+2r-1}{2r-1}\frac {n}{r}
    \mathbb E\left[
    X^n\rho_{n,m}\left(q+\lambda\frac{1-X}{X}\right)
    \right].
  \end{equation}
  If $\lambda>0$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-beta-integral}
    \widetilde P_{m,r}(q,\lambda)
    =\frac{\Gamma(m)}{r\Gamma(n)\Gamma(r)^2}\,
    \lambda^{n+r}
    \int_0^\infty
    \frac{s^{r-1}}{(\lambda+s)^m}\rho_{n,m}(q+s)\,\mathrm d s.
  \end{equation}

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
By \Cref{lem:pge23-beta-representation} with $j=n$,
  \[
    h_n(a^{\times r},b^{\times r})
    =\binom{n+2r-1}{2r-1}\mathbb E\left[\bigl(aX+b(1-X)\bigr)^n\right].
  \]
  Applying \eqref{eq:pge23-h-generating} with $r$ copies each of $a$ and $b$ and differentiating with respect to $a$ gives
  \[
    \frac{\partial}{\partial a}
    \sum_{d\ge0}h_d(a^{\times r},b^{\times r})z^d
    =rz(1-az)^{-(r+1)}(1-bz)^{-r}.
  \]
  Comparing coefficients of $z^n$ yields
  \[
    \frac{\partial}{\partial a}h_n(a^{\times r},b^{\times r})
    =r\,h_{n-1}(a^{\times(r+1)},b^{\times r}).
  \]
  The expectation $\mathbb E\left[\bigl(aX+b(1-X)\bigr)^n\right]$ is a polynomial in $a,b$, since $X$ is bounded and its distribution does not depend on these parameters.
  Differentiating term by term gives
  \[
    \frac{\partial}{\partial a}\mathbb E\left[\bigl(aX+b(1-X)\bigr)^n\right]
    =n\,\mathbb E\left[X\bigl(aX+b(1-X)\bigr)^{n-1}\right].
  \]
  Combining the two derivative identities gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-beta-h-deriv}
    h_{n-1}(a^{\times(r+1)},b^{\times r})
    =\binom{n+2r-1}{2r-1}\frac {n}{r}
    \mathbb E\left[X\bigl(aX+b(1-X)\bigr)^{n-1}\right].
  \end{equation}

  Set $p=1-q$ and
  \[
    V_X=qX+\lambda(1-X),
    \qquad
    W_X=pX-\lambda(1-X),
    \qquad
    V_X+W_X=X.
  \]
  Apply \Cref{lem:pge23-beta-representation} to the two cycle terms
  in the definition \eqref{eq:Pmr} of $P_{m,r}$, with
  $\lambda_1=\cdots=\lambda_r=\lambda$, and
  \eqref{eq:pge23-beta-h-deriv} to the path term. This gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-beta-first}
    \widetilde P_{m,r}(q,\lambda)
    =\binom{n+2r-1}{2r-1}\frac {n}{r}
    \left[
      \frac {m}{n}\mathbb E\left[V_X^n+W_X^n\right]
      -\mathbb E\left[XV_X^{n-1}\right]
    \right].
  \end{equation}
  Since $X\in(0,1)$ almost surely, write
  \[
    u_X=\frac{V_X}{X}=q+\lambda\frac{1-X}{X},
    \qquad
    V_X=Xu_X,
    \qquad
    W_X=X(1-u_X).
  \]
  The two expectations in \eqref{eq:pge23-beta-first} therefore
  combine into the expectation of
  \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \frac {m}{n}\bigl(V_X^n+W_X^n\bigr)-XV_X^{n-1}
      &=X^n\left[
        \frac {m}{n}\bigl(u_X^n+(1-u_X)^n\bigr)-u_X^{n-1}
      \right]\\
      &=X^n\rho_{n,m}(u_X).
    \end{aligned}
  \]
  This proves \eqref{eq:pge23-beta-second}.

  We now derive \eqref{eq:pge23-beta-integral}.
  For $\lambda>0$, the probability density function of the Beta distribution gives
  \[
    \mathbb E\left[X^n\rho_{n,m}\left(q+\lambda\frac{1-X}{X}\right)\right]
    =\frac{\Gamma(2r)}{\Gamma(r)^2}
    \int_0^1 x^{n+r-1}(1-x)^{r-1}
    \rho_{n,m}\left(q+\lambda\frac{1-x}{x}\right)\,\mathrm d x.
  \]
  We now make the substitution
  \[
    s=\lambda\frac{1-x}{x},
    \qquad
    x=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+s},
    \qquad
    1-x=\frac{s}{\lambda+s},
    \qquad
    \,\mathrm d x=-\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda+s)^2}\,\mathrm d s.
  \]
  The endpoints $x=1$ and $x\to0^+$ correspond to $s=0$ and
  $s\to\infty$, respectively. Reversing the limits and using $n+2r=m$,
  we obtain the transformed weight
  \[
    \left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+s}\right)^{n+r-1}
    \left(\frac{s}{\lambda+s}\right)^{r-1}
    \frac{\lambda}{(\lambda+s)^2}
    =\frac{\lambda^{n+r}s^{r-1}}{(\lambda+s)^m}.
  \]
  Hence the expectation equals
  \[
    \frac{\Gamma(2r)}{\Gamma(r)^2}\lambda^{n+r}
    \int_0^\infty
    \frac{s^{r-1}}{(\lambda+s)^m}\rho_{n,m}(q+s)\,\mathrm d s.
  \]
  Finally,
  \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \binom{m-1}{2r-1}\frac {n}{r}
      \frac{\Gamma(2r)}{\Gamma(r)^2}
      &=\frac{(m-1)!}{(2r-1)!\,n!}\cdot\frac {n}{r}
        \cdot\frac{(2r-1)!}{((r-1)!)^2}\\
      &=\frac{\Gamma(m)}{r\Gamma(n)\Gamma(r)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
  \]
  Substituting into \eqref{eq:pge23-beta-second} proves
  \eqref{eq:pge23-beta-integral}.

\label{lem:pge23-laplace-gamma}

  $1\le r\le(m-1)/2$, and set $n=m-2r$.

  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-gamma-expectation}
    \widetilde P_{m,r}(q,\lambda)
    =\binom{m-r-1}{r}

  \end{equation}

  \eqref{eq:pge23-beta-integral} and exchanging the two integrals, we obtain

    &=\frac{\Gamma(m)}{r\Gamma(n)\Gamma(r)^2}\,\lambda^{n+r}
      \int_0^\infty s^{r-1}\rho_{n,m}(q+s)
      \left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(m)}\int_0^\infty t^{m-1}e^{-t(\lambda+s)}\,\mathrm d t\right)

  \eqref{eq:pge23-laplace-kernel} gives

    \int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^\infty
    s^{r-1}t^{m-1}e^{-t(\lambda+s)}\bigl|\rho_{n,m}(q+s)\bigr|\,\mathrm d t\,\mathrm d s

    =\binom{m-r-1}{r}.
  \]
  This proves the second identity in the lemma.

    \qquad v=q+xY-\frac12=\delta(zY-1).

  The identity becomes
  \[
    kI_k(b)+kbI_{k-1}(b)=I_{k+1}(b)+(b-r)I_k(b),
  \]
  so rearranging gives the moment recurrence
  \begin{equation*}
    I_{k+1}(b)=(r+k-b)I_k(b)+kbI_{k-1}(b).
  \end{equation*}
  Taking $k=2j-1$ and using $I_{k+1}=F$,
  $I_k=-F'/(2j)$, and $I_{k-1}=F''/(2j(2j-1))$ gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-gamma-ODE}
    bF''(b)+(b-r-2j+1)F'(b)-2jF(b)=0.
  \end{equation}

  \smallskip
  \noindent\emph{Reduction to a single value of $b$.}
  First observe that
  \[
    H(0)=(r+j)\mathbb E Y^{2j}>0,
    \qquad
    H(b)=(r+4j)b^{2j}+O(b^{2j-1})
    \qquad(b\to\infty),
  \]
  so $H$ is positive at both ends of $[0,\infty)$.
  If $H$ were negative somewhere, the maximal interval of negativity containing that point
  would have a first zero, where $H'\le0$, and a later zero, where
  $H'\ge0$.

  To locate these endpoints, we next determine the sign of $H'$ at a zero of $H$.
  Differentiating \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-H} and then eliminating $F''$ by \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-ODE}, we obtain
  \[
    H'(b)=\left(r+j+\frac{3}{2}\right)F'(b)+\frac{3b}{2}F''(b)
    =3jF(b)+\left(\frac{5r}{2}+4j-\frac{3b}{2}\right)F'(b).
  \]
  Let $b_0>0$ be a zero of $H$, so that $H(b_0)=0$. By \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-H}, we have
  \[
    F'(b_0)=-\frac{2(r+j)}{3b_0}F(b_0).
  \]
  Define
  \begin{equation*}
    b_*:=\frac{(r+j)(5r+8j)}{3(r+4j)}.
  \end{equation*}
  Substituting the expression for $F'(b_0)$ into the formula for $H'$
  gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-gamma-crossing}
    \frac{H'(b_0)}{F(b_0)}
    =(r+4j)\frac{b_0-b_*}{b_0}.
  \end{equation}
  Since $F(b_0)=\mathbb E(Y-b_0)^{2j}>0$, we distinguish three cases using
  \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-crossing}.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item If $b_0<b_*$, then $H'(b_0)<0$.
    \item If $b_0>b_*$, then $H'(b_0)>0$.
    \item If $b_0=b_*$, then $H(b_*)=H(b_0)=0$ and $H'(b_*)=0$.
    Differentiating the formula for $H'$ above and using
    \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-ODE} gives
    \[
      H''(b_*)=\frac{r+4j}{b_*}F(b_*)>0.
    \]
    Thus $b_*$ is a strict local minimum of $H$ and cannot be an endpoint
    of an interval on which $H$ is negative.
  \end{enumerate}
  The first endpoint of the interval of negativity would therefore lie below $b_*$
  and the second above it,
  so this interval would contain $b_*$ and give $H(b_*)<0$.
  To rule out this possibility, it remains only to prove
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-gamma-Hbstar}
    H(b_*)\ge0.
  \end{equation}

  \smallskip
  \noindent\emph{An integral representation at $b_*$.}
  We first write $H(b_*)$ as an integral.
  Since $F'(b)=-2j\mathbb E(Y-b)^{2j-1}$, \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-H} gives
  \[
    H(b_*)=(r+j)\mathbb E(Y-b_*)^{2j}-3jb_*\mathbb E(Y-b_*)^{2j-1}
    =(r+j)\,\mathbb E\bigl[(Y-b_*)^{2j-1}(Y-b_*-c)\bigr],
  \]
  where $c=\frac{3jb_*}{r+j}$.
  Expanding the expectation as an integral and substituting $x=y-b_*$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-gamma-H-integral}
    H(b_*) =\frac{r+j}{\Gamma(r) e^{b_*}}\int_{-b_*}^{\infty}
    x^{2j-1}(x-c)(x+b_*)^{r-1}e^{-x}\,\mathrm d x.
  \end{equation}

  The integrand changes sign, so this representation does not immediately
  establish positivity. We therefore seek a more useful representation by
  integration by parts. To obtain the factor
  $x^{2j-1}(x+b_*)^{r-1}e^{-x}$ upon differentiation, set
  \begin{equation*}
    G(x)=x^{2j}(x+b_*)^re^{-x},
    \qquad -b_*\le x<\infty,
  \end{equation*}
  so that
  \[
    G'(x)=x^{2j-1}(x+b_*)^{r-1}e^{-x}
    \bigl(-x^2+(r+2j-b_*)x+2jb_*\bigr).
  \]
  The choice of $b_*$ makes $c=3jb_*/(r+j)$ a root of this quadratic.
  Thus, the factor $x-c$ in the signed integrand of
  \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-H-integral} also appears in $G'$, up to sign.
  More precisely, the quadratic factors as $(c-x)(x+d)$, where
  \[
    d=\frac{2jb_*}{c}=\frac{2(r+j)}{3}.
  \]
  A direct calculation gives
  \[
    b_*-d=\frac{r(r+j)}{r+4j}>0,
  \]
  so $-d$ lies in the interval of integration.
  Thus
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-gamma-Gprime}
    G'(x)=x^{2j-1}(x+b_*)^{r-1}e^{-x}(c-x)(x+d),
  \end{equation}
  and the integrand of \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-H-integral} is $-G'(x)/(x+d)$, whence
  \[
    \frac{\Gamma(r)e^{b_*}}{r+j}H(b_*)
    =-\int_{-b_*}^{\infty}\frac{G'(x)}{x+d}\,\mathrm d x.
  \]
  The factor $x+d$ in \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-Gprime} cancels the denominator,
  so $G'(x)/(x+d)$ extends continuously to $x=-d$.
  To integrate by parts across this point, we use $G(x)-G(-d)$ as a
  primitive of $G'(x)$. Since $G'(-d)=0$, Taylor's theorem gives
  $G(x)-G(-d)=O((x+d)^2)$ as $x\to-d$. Thus
  $(G(x)-G(-d))/(x+d)$ tends to zero, while
  $(G(x)-G(-d))/(x+d)^2$ extends continuously to $x=-d$.

  Integration by parts on either side of $-d$ therefore yields
  \begin{align}
    &-\int_{-b_*}^{\infty}\frac{G'(x)}{x+d}\,\mathrm d x\notag\\
    &\quad=\left[-\frac{G(x)-G(-d)}{x+d}\right]_{-b_*}^{-d}
    +\left[-\frac{G(x)-G(-d)}{x+d}\right]_{-d}^{\infty}
    +\int_{-b_*}^{\infty}\frac{G(-d)-G(x)}{(x+d)^2}\,\mathrm d x\notag\\
    &\quad=\frac{G(-d)}{b_*-d}
    +\int_{-b_*}^{\infty}\frac{G(-d)-G(x)}{(x+d)^2}\,\mathrm d x,
    \label{eq:pge23-gamma-ibp-max}
  \end{align}
  where the evaluations at $-d$ are understood by continuity, and we used
  $G(-b_*)=0$ and $G(x)\to0$ as $x\to\infty$.

  Since $G(-d)=d^{2j}(b_*-d)^re^{d}>0$ and $b_*-d>0$, the term
  $G(-d)/(b_*-d)$ is positive. If $G(x)\le G(-d)$ for every
  $x\ge-b_*$, the integral is nonnegative as well, giving $H(b_*)>0$.
  It therefore suffices to prove that $G(x)\le G(-d)$ for every $x\ge-b_*$.

  \smallskip
  \noindent\emph{Comparison of the two local maxima.}
  By \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-Gprime}, the two nonzero local maxima occur at
  $-d$ and $c$.
  Their ratio is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-gamma-max-ratio}
    \frac{G(c)}{G(-d)}
    =\left(\frac {c}{d}\right)^{2j}
    \left(\frac{b_*+c}{b_*-d}\right)^r e^{-(c+d)}.
  \end{equation}
  Let $t=j/r>0$, and set
  \[
    A(t)=\frac{3t(5+8t)}{2(1+t)(1+4t)},
    \qquad
    B(t)=\frac{(1+4t)(5+8t)}{3(1+t)}.
  \]
  The logarithm of \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-max-ratio}, divided by $r$, is
  \[
    L(t)=2t\log A(t)+\log B(t)
      -\frac{32t^2+25t+2}{3(1+4t)}.
  \]
  After division by $r$, the parameters $r$ and $j$ occur only through
  their ratio $t$. Proving $L(t)<0$ for every $t>0$ therefore establishes
  the maximum comparison uniformly in $r$ and $j$.

  \smallskip
  \noindent\emph{Proof of the scalar bound $L(t)<0$.}
  Direct differentiation gives
  \begin{align}
    L'(t)
    &=2\log A(t)
    -\frac{(32t^2+16t-7)(32t^2+25t+2)}
    {3(t+1)(4t+1)^2(8t+5)},\label{eq:pge23-gamma-Lprime}\\
    L''(t)
    &=-\frac{(32t^2+25t+2)
    (128t^4+224t^3+120t^2-28t-25)}
    {t(t+1)^2(4t+1)^3(8t+5)^2}.
    \label{eq:pge23-gamma-Lsecond}
  \end{align}
  In \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-Lsecond}, the denominator and the factor
  $32t^2+25t+2$ are positive for $t>0$. The initial minus sign therefore
  makes the sign of $L''$ opposite to that of the quartic
  $P(t)=128t^4+224t^3+120t^2-28t-25$.
  The coefficients of $P$ change sign once, so by Descartes' rule of signs $P$ has at most one positive root.
  Since $P(0)=-25<0$ and $P(t)\to\infty$, it has exactly one positive
  root $t_0$. Consequently, $L'$ increases on $(0,t_0)$ and decreases on
  $(t_0,\infty)$.

  We now determine the sign of $L'$ near $0$, at $1$, and at $3/2$
  to locate its two zeros.
  We will use the elementary bounds $\log2<7/10$ and
  $\log13<13/5$. The positive terms in the exponential series give
  \[
    e^{7/10}>\sum_{\ell=0}^{3}\frac{(7/10)^\ell}{\ell!}>2,
    \qquad
    e^{13/5}>\sum_{\ell=0}^{6}\frac{(13/5)^\ell}{\ell!}>13,
  \]
  which prove both bounds by taking logarithms.
  As $t\downarrow0$ the argument $A(t)$ tends to $0$ while the rational term of \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-Lprime} tends to $-14/15$, so $L'(t)\to-\infty$.
  At $t=1$ we have $A(1)=39/20$ and the rational term equals $2419/1950$, so the bound
  \[
    \log x\ge\frac{2(x-1)}{x+1}\qquad(x\ge1)
  \]
  gives $2\log A(1)\ge\frac{76}{59}$ and
  \[
    L'(1)\ge\frac{76}{59}-\frac{2419}{1950}
    =\frac{5479}{115050}>0.
  \]
  At $t=3/2$ we have $A(3/2)=153/70$ and the rational term equals $19847/12495$; concavity of the logarithm at $x=2$ gives
  \[
    \log\frac{153}{70}\le\log2+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{153}{70}-2\right)
    =\log2+\frac{13}{140},
  \]
  so with $2 \log 2 < 7/5$,
  \[
    L'\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)
    <\frac{7}{5} + \frac{13}{70}-\frac{19847}{12495}
    =-\frac{67}{24990}<0.
  \]

  Since $L'$ first increases and then decreases, these signs show that it
  has exactly two roots: some $t_1\in(0,1)$ with $L''(t_1)>0$, and some
  $t_2\in(1,3/2)$ with $L''(t_2)<0$.
  Thus, $L$ decreases on $(0,t_1)$, increases on $(t_1,t_2)$, and
  decreases on $(t_2,\infty)$. Its supremum over $(0,\infty)$ is therefore
  the larger of $L(t_2)$ and its limit at $0$.
  The latter is negative,
  \[
    \lim_{t\downarrow0}L(t)=\log\frac{5}{3}-\frac{2}{3}<0,
  \]
  and $t_2$ lies in $[1,3/2]$, so it remains to control $L$ on $[1,3/2]$.
  By concavity of $\log$,
  \[
    \log A\le\log2+\frac{A-2}{2},
    \qquad
    \log B\le\log13+\frac{B-13}{13}.
  \]
  Using $\log2<7/10$ and $\log13<13/5$, exact simplification gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pge23-gamma-L-rational}
    L(t)<
    \frac{3(96t^3-191t^2-16t+46)}
    {130(t+1)(4t+1)}.
  \end{equation}
  On $[1,3/2]$ we have $96t-191<0$ and $t^2\ge1$, so the cubic numerator is at most $(96t-191)-16t+46=80t-145\le-25$, and $L(t)<0$ there as well.

  Together with the preceding description of the supremum, this proves
  $L(t)<0$ for every $t>0$.

  \smallskip
  \noindent\emph{Completion of the moment bound.}
  The ratio \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-max-ratio} is therefore smaller than $1$,
  so $G$ attains its maximum at $-d$. Thus, the integrand in
  \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-ibp-max} is nonnegative.
  The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-ibp-max} is then positive,
  giving $H(b_*)>0$. The earlier analysis of the zeros of $H$ shows that
  if $H$ were negative anywhere, then $H(b_*)<0$. Thus, $H(b)\ge0$ for
  every $b>0$, which is \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-H} and hence implies
  \eqref{eq:pge23-gamma-moment2}.
