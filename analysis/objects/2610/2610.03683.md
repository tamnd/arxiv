<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:samplers}
The \emph{heat-bath sampler} and the \emph{regularised heat-bath sampler} associated
with $\sigma$ are the quantum Markov semigroups
with Schr\"odinger-picture generators
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:generators}
 \begin{align}
  \mathcal L_*^{\mathrm{HB}}
    &=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}(\mathcal P_{i*}-\operatorname{id}),
       \label{eq:generator-heatbath}\\
  \mathcal L_*^{\mathrm{RHB}}
    &=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}(\mathbb{E}_{i*}-\operatorname{id}).
       \label{eq:generator-regularised-heatbath}
 \end{align}
\end{subequations}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Under the assumptions above, both generators in \eqref{eq:generators}
are primitive and KMS symmetric with invariant state $\sigma$.
The heat-bath generator $\mathcal L_*^{\mathrm{HB}}$ is quasi-local in
the sense of Proposition~\ref{loc:heatbath-quasilocal}.
There is a constant $C_\beta<\infty$, independent of $n$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mlsi}
 D(\rho\Vert\sigma)\le C_\beta\log n\,\operatorname{EP}^a(\rho),
 \qquad a\in\{\mathrm{HB},\mathrm{RHB}\},
\end{equation}
for every faithful state $\rho$. In particular,
$\alpha_{\rm MLSI}(\mathcal L_*^a)\ge c_\beta/\log n$ at every fixed
positive temperature.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:mixing}
For either sampler, all initial states $\rho$, and $t\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-decay}
 D(e^{t\mathcal L_*^a}\rho\Vert\sigma)
 \le e^{-c_\beta t/\log n}D(\rho\Vert\sigma).
\end{equation}
For $0<\epsilon<1$, the relative-entropy and trace-norm mixing times satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixing}
 \max\{t_{{\rm mix},D}^a(\epsilon),t_{\rm mix}^a(\epsilon)\}
 \le C_\beta'\log n\,[\log n+\log\epsilon^{-1}].
\end{equation}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{pre:properties}
The maps $\mathcal P_{S*}$ are channels preserving $\sigma$. Their adjoints
are KMS self-adjoint with spectrum in $[0,1]$. The limit $\mathbb{E}_S$ is
the $\sigma$-preserving conditional expectation onto an algebra
$\mathcal N_S\subseteq\mathcal A_{S^c}$ and is the KMS orthogonal
projection onto that algebra. In particular, both generators in
\eqref{eq:generators} are primitive, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy-comparison}
 0\le-\Gamma_\sigma^{1/2}\mathcal L_T^{\mathrm{HB}}\Gamma_\sigma^{-1/2}
   \le-\Gamma_\sigma^{1/2}\mathcal L_T^{\mathrm{RHB}}\Gamma_\sigma^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
The inequalities are in the operator order on Hilbert--Schmidt space.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The formula for $\mathcal P_{S*}$ is a composition of completely positive
maps. Writing $C=S^c$, cyclicity and partial trace give
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}[\mathcal P_{S*}(\rho)]
 =\operatorname{Tr}[\sigma_C\sigma_C^{-1/2}\rho_C\sigma_C^{-1/2}]
 =\operatorname{Tr}[\rho],\qquad \mathcal P_{S*}(\sigma)=\sigma.
\]
Thus it is a channel preserving $\sigma$. Its adjoint is
\[
 \mathcal P_S(X)=\mathbf1_S\otimes\Gamma_{\sigma_{S^c}}^{-1}
             \bigl(\operatorname{Tr}_S[\Gamma_\sigma(X)]\bigr).
\]
Putting $T_X=\operatorname{Tr}_S[\Gamma_\sigma(X)]$, we have
\[
 \langle X,\mathcal P_S X\rangle_{\mathrm{KMS}}
 =\operatorname{Tr}[T_X^\dagger\Gamma_{\sigma_{S^c}}^{-1}(T_X)]\ge0.
\]
Polarisation gives KMS self-adjointness, and unital positivity bounds
the spectral radius by one. Thus the spectrum lies in $[0,1]$, and
the powers converge to the orthogonal projection onto the eigenvalue-one
space.

If $X$ is fixed, Schwarz gives
$\mathcal P_S(X^\dagger X)-X^\dagger X\ge0$. Its expectation in the faithful
state $\sigma$ is zero, so equality holds. The same applies to
$XX^\dagger$. The equality case of Schwarz places $X$ in the
multiplicative domain of $\mathcal P_S$: for every $Y$,
$\mathcal P_S(XY)=X\mathcal P_S(Y)$ and $\mathcal P_S(YX)=\mathcal P_S(Y)X$.
The fixed space is consequently an algebra, and the limiting map is
bimodular over it. Every fixed point belongs to $\mathcal A_{S^c}$
by the formula for $\mathcal P_S$.

For a single site, the intersection of these algebras over all sites
is scalar. Positivity of the individual KMS forms shows that the
kernel of either sum of forms is precisely this intersection. Thus
both generators are primitive with invariant state $\sigma$.
Finally $1-\lambda\le1-\mathbf1_{\{1\}}(\lambda)$ for
$0\le\lambda\le1$. Applying this to each $\mathcal P_i$ proves
\eqref{eq:energy-comparison}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{cmlsi:local}
Every regularised heat-bath update generator $\mathbb{E}_S-\mathrm{id}$ satisfies CMLSI with
rate at least one. More precisely, for
$\tau=(\mathbb{E}_{S*}\otimes\mathrm{id}_M)(\rho)$,
\begin{equation}\label{cmlsi:rhb}
 \operatorname{EP}_{(\mathbb{E}_S-\mathrm{id})\otimes\mathrm{id}_M}(\rho)
 =D(\rho\Vert\tau)+D(\tau\Vert\rho)
 \ge D(\rho\Vert\tau).
\end{equation}
The usual spatial conditional relative entropy is bounded by this
fixed-algebra relative entropy:
\begin{equation}\label{cmlsi:conditional-comparison}
 \begin{aligned}
 D_S(\rho\Vert\sigma\otimes\rho_M)
 &:=D(\rho\Vert\sigma\otimes\rho_M)
     -D(\rho_{S^cM}\Vert\sigma_{S^c}\otimes\rho_M)\\
 &\le D(\rho\Vert\tau).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here the complement of $S$ includes the memory $M$.
For both the heat-bath and regularised heat-bath updates, $Q_S=\mathcal P_S$
or $Q_S=\mathbb{E}_S$, one also has
\begin{equation}\label{cmlsi:spatial}
 \operatorname{EP}_{(Q_S-\mathrm{id})\otimes\mathrm{id}_M}(\rho)
 \ge D_S(\rho\Vert\sigma\otimes\rho_M).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix a faithful reference $\Omega=\sigma\otimes\nu_M$, with $\nu_M>0$.
In the finite-dimensional decomposition of the conditional-expectation
algebra, the invariant states have blocks
$\omega_{j,L}\otimes\theta_{j,R}$, where the right factors
$\theta_{j,R}$ are fixed. Hence
$\log\tau-\log\Omega$ belongs to the expectation algebra, and
$\rho-\tau$ has zero trace pairing with it. This is the conditional
expectation chain rule; see \cite[Appendix A, equation (A.1)]{BardetCapelRouze2021NoncommutingAT}.
Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\bigl[(\rho-\tau)(\log\rho-\log\Omega)\bigr]
 =\operatorname{Tr}\bigl[(\rho-\tau)(\log\rho-\log\tau)\bigr],
\]
which proves \eqref{cmlsi:rhb}. Equivalently, the semigroup is explicit:
\[
 e^{t(\mathbb{E}_{S*}-\mathrm{id})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_M(\rho)
 =e^{-t}\rho+(1-e^{-t})\tau.
\]
Convexity of relative entropy gives contraction of $D_{\mathbb{E}}$ by $e^{-t}$,
also for singular states on the support of their memory marginal.

For any channel $Q_*$ preserving $\Omega$, direct expansion gives
\[
 \operatorname{EP}_{Q-\mathrm{id}}(\rho)
 =D(\rho\Vert\Omega)-D(Q_*\rho\Vert\Omega)
                              +D(Q_*\rho\Vert\rho).
\]
For $Q_* = \mathcal P_{S*}\otimes\mathrm{id}_M$, data processing
under the Petz recovery channel yields
\[
 D(Q_*\rho\Vert\Omega)
 \le D(\rho_{S^cM}\Vert\sigma_{S^c}\otimes\nu_M).
\]
The same inequality holds for every positive power of this channel,
and then for its limit $\mathbb{E}_{S*}\otimes\mathrm{id}_M$ by continuity.
For that limit the same trace-pairing identity gives the chain rule
\[
 D(\rho\Vert\Omega)=D(\rho\Vert\tau)+D(\tau\Vert\Omega).
\]
Combining it with the preceding data-processing bound proves
\eqref{cmlsi:conditional-comparison}.
Dropping the last nonnegative term proves \eqref{cmlsi:spatial},
since the difference of the two relative entropies is independent
of $\nu_M$. Faithful approximation gives the usual extended-value
statement for singular $\rho$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
A uniform local CMLSI relative to the exact fixed algebra of
$\mathcal P_i$ cannot replace the conditional-gap argument. For example,
on two qubits let $\sigma_\varepsilon\propto
e^{-\varepsilon Z_1Z_2}$, where $X_i,Z_i$ are Pauli matrices and
$\varepsilon>0$. Its second-site marginal is $\mathbf1/2$, and direct
evaluation of the Petz formula gives
\[
 \mathcal P_1(\mathbf1\otimes X_2)
       =\operatorname{sech}(\varepsilon)(\mathbf1\otimes X_2),
 \qquad \mathbb{E}_1(\mathbf1\otimes X_2)=0.
\]
By KMS symmetry, $V=\Gamma_{\sigma_\varepsilon}(\mathbf1\otimes X_2)$
is the corresponding Schr\"odinger eigenvector. For small real $u$,
$\rho_u=\sigma_\varepsilon+uV$ is a faithful density matrix and
$\mathbb{E}_{1*}\rho_u=\sigma_\varepsilon$. The second-order expansion of
relative entropy at its faithful minimum gives
\[
 \lim_{u\to0}
 \frac{\operatorname{EP}_{\mathcal P_1-\mathrm{id}}(\rho_u)}
      {D(\rho_u\Vert \mathbb{E}_{1*}\rho_u)}
       =2[1-\operatorname{sech}(\varepsilon)]\longrightarrow0
       \quad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
\]
Indeed the numerator is $(1-\operatorname{sech}\varepsilon)u$
times the derivative of $D(\rho_u\Vert\sigma_\varepsilon)$.
Thus even an ordinary local MLSI constant can vanish within a family
of uniformly bounded interactions. The ambient conditional gap and
the global sum of site updates are different statements.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{cmlsi:scope}
The spatial comparison \eqref{cmlsi:spatial} for a single heat-bath update
is not, by itself, a CMLSI relative to its fixed-point algebra:
its right-hand side need not equal $D_{\mathbb{E}_S}(\rho)$. Likewise,
\eqref{cmlsi:rhb} for each regularised heat-bath update does not supply the
factorisation needed for a complete inequality for their sum.
The defect-removal argument used below proves ordinary MLSI.
It centres observables around the scalar expectation
$X\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}[\sigma X]\mathbf1$ and uses a primitive
spectral gap, as in \cite[Lemma 2.3 and Theorem 2.4]{norms}.
After tensoring with an untouched memory, the fixed algebra is
$\mathbf1\otimes\mathcal B(\mathcal H_M)$ and this scalar-centred
gap vanishes: every nonzero $\mathbf1\otimes Z$ with
$\operatorname{Tr}[\nu_MZ]=0$ is fixed. Thus that proof cannot be
amplified verbatim. A memory-uniform conditional defect-removal
argument would be needed to conclude the same global CMLSI rate;
that rate remains unproved in the complete setting. Global CMLSI
with weaker volume dependence follows separately from the GNS gap,
as shown in Corollary~\ref{cmlsi:global}. The distinction between
primitive hypercontractivity and the conditional norm setting is
discussed in \cite[Section 2.2]{norms}.

\label{stat:weak-at}
We fix the interaction parameters and $\beta>0$ as in
Section~\ref{sec:model}.  There are constants $L_\beta,B_\beta,a_\beta>0$
such that, for every integer $L\geq L_\beta$, we can choose a cover
$\mathcal G=\{A_i\}_i\cup\{B_j\}_j$, illustrated in
Figure~\ref{fig:summary-cover}, with the following properties.
If $n\geq4L$, every member is an interval of length between $L$ and $6L$,
each site belongs to at most two members, and
$|\mathcal G|\leq n/L+2$.  If $n<4L$, we take $\mathcal G=\{\Lambda\}$.
For every state $\rho$,
\begin{equation}
 D(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 \leq\sum_{S\in\mathcal G}D_S(\rho\Vert\sigma)
       +B_\beta n e^{-a_\beta L}.
 \label{stat:weak-at-equation}
\end{equation}
When $n<4L$, the choice $\mathcal G=\{\Lambda\}$ gives
$D_\Lambda(\rho\Vert\sigma)=D(\rho\Vert\sigma)$, so the additive
error can be omitted and the inequality becomes an equality.
In particular, a constant
$K_\beta$ can be chosen so that $L=\lceil K_\beta\log n\rceil$ makes
the error at most one for every $n\ge2$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{stat:entropy-identities}
If $A\cup B=\Lambda$, we set $X=\Lambda\setminus A$ and
$Y=\Lambda\setminus B$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 D(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 \leq D_A(\rho\Vert\sigma)+D_B(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 +\left\|\log\sigma_{XY}-\log\sigma_{X}-\log\sigma_{Y}\right\|_\infty.
 \label{stat:two-set}
\end{equation}
For pairwise disjoint nonempty sets $A_1,\ldots,A_m$, we set
$A=\bigcup_i A_i$ and $X=\Lambda\setminus A$, and we define
\begin{equation}
 K_\sigma(A):=(m-1)\log\sigma+\log\sigma_{X}
                    -\sum_{i=1}^m\log\sigma_{A_i^c}.
 \label{stat:multipartite-defect}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 D_A(\rho\Vert\sigma)\leq\sum_{i=1}^mD_{A_i}(\rho\Vert\sigma)
                       +\left\|K_\sigma(A)\right\|_\infty.
 \label{stat:multipartite}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For the first statement, data processing and entropy subadditivity give
\begin{align*}
 D(\rho\Vert\sigma)-D_A(\rho\Vert\sigma)-D_B(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 &\leq-D(\rho_{XY}\Vert\sigma_{XY})
        +D(\rho_{X}\Vert\sigma_{X})
        +D(\rho_{Y}\Vert\sigma_{Y})\\
 &=-I_\rho(X:Y)
    +\operatorname{Tr}\bigl[\rho\bigl(\log\sigma_{XY}-\log\sigma_{X}
                                  -\log\sigma_{Y}\bigr)\bigr],
\end{align*}
which implies \eqref{stat:two-set}.
For the second statement, we set $A_{1:j}=\bigcup_{i\leq j}A_i$ and
$C_j=\Lambda\setminus A_{1:j}$.  Expanding and telescoping gives
\begin{align}
 D_A(\rho\Vert\sigma)-\sum_iD_{A_i}(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 &=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho K_\sigma(A)]
     -\sum_{j=2}^m I_\rho(A_{1:j-1}:A_j\mid C_j).
 \label{stat:entropy-remainder}
\end{align}
By strong subadditivity, each conditional mutual information
$I_\rho(A_{1:j-1}:A_j\mid C_j)$ is nonnegative.  Dropping these
terms from the right-hand side of \eqref{stat:entropy-remainder}
and using that $\rho$ is a state gives
\[
 D_A(\rho\Vert\sigma)-\sum_{i=1}^m D_{A_i}(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 \leq \operatorname{Tr}[\rho K_\sigma(A)]
 \leq \|\rho\|_1\left\|K_\sigma(A)\right\|_\infty
 =\left\|K_\sigma(A)\right\|_\infty,
\]
which is \eqref{stat:multipartite}.  For $m=1$, we have $A=A_1$
and $X=A_1^c$, so $K_\sigma(A)=0$ and the sum of conditional mutual
informations in \eqref{stat:entropy-remainder} is empty.  Thus
\eqref{stat:multipartite} is an equality in this case.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{stat:multiplicative-alternative}
For the two-set estimate \eqref{stat:two-set}, we could instead use
the superadditivity result of
\cite[Theorem 1]{CapelLuciaPerezGarcia2018Superadditivity} and its
quasi-factorisation reformulation
\cite[Theorem 5]{CapelLuciaPerezGarcia_ConditionalRelativeEntropy_2018}.
With $X=A^c$ and $Y=B^c$, we set
\[
 h(\sigma_{XY})
 :=(\sigma_X\otimes\sigma_Y)^{-1/2}\sigma_{XY}
                    (\sigma_X\otimes\sigma_Y)^{-1/2}
                      -\mathbf1_{XY}.
\]
The superadditivity estimate reads
\[
 D(\rho_X\Vert\sigma_X)+D(\rho_Y\Vert\sigma_Y)
 \le\bigl(1+2\|h(\sigma_{XY})\|_\infty\bigr)
                  D(\rho_{XY}\Vert\sigma_{XY}).
\]
By data processing and the definition of $D_A,D_B$, this gives,
whenever $\|h(\sigma_{XY})\|_\infty<1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{stat:two-set-multiplicative}
 D(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 \le\frac{D_A(\rho\Vert\sigma)+D_B(\rho\Vert\sigma)}
              {1-2\|h(\sigma_{XY})\|_\infty}.
\end{equation}
Here the defect enters multiplicatively. It measures the same
departure from a product state as the additive logarithmic defect
in \eqref{stat:two-set}. More precisely, a comparison
$e^{-\varepsilon}\sigma_X\otimes\sigma_Y\le\sigma_{XY}
\le e^{\varepsilon}\sigma_X\otimes\sigma_Y$ implies
\[
 \|\log\sigma_{XY}-\log\sigma_X-\log\sigma_Y\|_\infty
       \le\varepsilon,
 \qquad \|h(\sigma_{XY})\|_\infty\le e^\varepsilon-1.
\]
Thus the same product-comparison estimates control both defects,
with comparable bounds for small $\varepsilon$. We retain the
additive form \eqref{stat:two-set}, since the multipartite
decomposition and the buffered entropy--energy comparison below
introduce further additive errors in any case.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{stat:connected-mcmi-relative}
In the setting above, if $\delta_\beta(\ell)<1$, then
\begin{equation}
 H_\sigma(X:Z\mid Y)
 \leq a_\beta(\ell)-4\log(1-\delta_\beta(\ell)).
 \label{stat:connected-operator-bound}
\end{equation}
Consequently, there are $C_\beta,c'_\beta,\ell'_\beta>0$, independent of
$|\Lambda|$, such that
\begin{equation}
 H_\sigma(X:Z\mid Y)\leq F_\beta(\ell):=C_\beta e^{-c'_\beta \ell}

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We compare the Gibbs state $\sigma$ of the original Hamiltonian
$H_\Lambda$ with the approximating state
$\widetilde\sigma=\widetilde W/\operatorname{Tr}[\widetilde W]$ constructed from the
truncated Hamiltonian $H_0$ and the local corrections $T_{12},T_{45}$.
The aim is to compare their MCMIs while keeping track of the additive
error introduced by replacing each logarithmic marginal.
We write $\delta=\delta_\beta(\ell)$.  Since
\eqref{stat:global-relative-approximation} holds for every vector,
\[
 (1-\delta)W\leq\widetilde W\leq(1+\delta)W.
\]
Since the partial trace is positive, writing
$W_V=\operatorname{Tr}_{V^c}[W]$ and $\widetilde W_V=\operatorname{Tr}_{V^c}[\widetilde W]$, we have
\begin{equation}
 (1-\delta)W_V\leq\widetilde W_V\leq(1+\delta)W_V
 \qquad\text{for every }V\subseteq\Lambda.
 \label{stat:marginal-relative-order}
\end{equation}

By operator monotonicity of the logarithm,
\[
 \log(1-\delta)I
 \leq\log\widetilde W_V-\log W_V
 \leq\log(1+\delta)I.
\]
Since $\log(1+\delta)\leq-\log(1-\delta)$, it follows that
\begin{equation}
 \|\log\widetilde W_V-\log W_V\|_\infty\leq-\log(1-\delta).
 \label{stat:relative-log-comparison}
\end{equation}

The normalising constants cancel in the four-logarithm MCMI expression.
The triangle inequality therefore gives
\begin{align*}
 H_\sigma(X:Z\mid Y)
 &\leq H_{\widetilde\sigma}(X:Z\mid Y)
   +\sum_{V\in\{XYZ,Y,XY,YZ\}}
     \|\log W_V-\log\widetilde W_V\|_\infty\\
 &\leq a_\beta(\ell)-4\log(1-\delta_\beta(\ell)).
\end{align*}
This proves \eqref{stat:connected-operator-bound}.

For sufficiently large $\ell$, $\delta_\beta(\ell)\leq1/2$ and
$-\log(1-\delta_\beta(\ell))\leq2\delta_\beta(\ell)$.
Absorbing the polynomial prefactor into the exponential proves
\eqref{stat:connected-mcmi-exponential}, with
$c'_\beta=\min\{b_\beta/2,1/c_\beta\}$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{stat:expansional-estimates}
For consecutive intervals $U,V,W$, we set
\[
 E_{U,V}:=e^{-\beta H_{UV}/2}
               e^{\beta(H_U+H_V)/2}.
\]
These operators are called \emph{expansionals}.
There are constants $Q\geq1$ and $b'_\beta,c'_\beta>0$, and a
decaying function $r_\beta(t)\leq b'_\beta e^{-c'_\beta t}$, such that
\begin{align}
 \|E_{U,V}\|_\infty,\ \|E_{U,V}^{-1}\|_\infty&\leq Q,
 \label{stat:mix-foundation-expansional-norm}\\
 \|E_{U,VW}-E_{U,V}\|_\infty&\leq r_\beta(|V|).
 \label{stat:mix-foundation-expansional-locality}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
These follow from \cite[Proposition~3.2 and Corollary~3.4]{bcp}.
Writing $E_{U,V}(z)=e^{-zH_{UV}}e^{z(H_U+H_V)}$, the inverse is
controlled by $E_{U,V}(z)^{-1}=E_{U,V}(-z)^*$ for real $z$.
The two expansionals share the same truncation on
collars of radius less than $|V|$.  Their factorial tails imply the
displayed exponential bound.

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We combine Lemmas~\ref{stat:local-indistinguishability}
and~\ref{stat:expansional-estimates} to obtain a comparison in relative
operator order.  Both estimates also apply after folding, which keeps
the local dimension a fixed power of two.

The geometry has two stages.  We first compare the marginals on $U$
for the consecutive chains $UV_1V_2W$ and $UV_1V_2$: the buffer
$V=V_1V_2$ separates $U$ from the region $W$ that we remove, as
illustrated in Figure~\ref{stat:folded-gap-removal}(a).
We then apply this comparison to the folded chain in
Figure~\ref{stat:folded-gap-removal}(c), where the retained region is
$X\cup Z$, the buffer is $Y_1\cup Y_3$, and the removed region is
$Y_2$.  In the original chain, this removes the middle of the separator
and leaves two independent intervals.

We fix $U,V,W$ and split $V=V_1V_2$, with $V_1$ adjacent to $U$ and both
pieces of length at least $(|V|-1)/2$.  We set
\[
 F_{U,V}=E_{U,V}^{*}E_{U,V},\qquad
 T_U^{UV}=\operatorname{Tr}_V[\sigma^V F_{U,V}],\qquad
 T_U^{UVW}=\operatorname{Tr}_{VW}[\sigma^{VW}F_{U,VW}].
\]
The superscript records the region on which the operator before the
partial trace is defined, while the subscript records the retained
region. Thus $T_U^{UV}$ and $T_U^{UVW}$ both act on $U$, but arise
from operators on $UV$ and $UVW$, respectively. This follows the
same convention as the Gibbs marginals $(\sigma^{UV})_U$ and
$(\sigma^{UVW})_U$.
Weighted partial trace is unital and completely positive, since
$\operatorname{Tr}_V[\sigma^V F]=\operatorname{Tr}_V[(\sigma^V)^{1/2}F(\sigma^V)^{1/2}]$.  Hence
\eqref{stat:mix-foundation-expansional-norm} gives
\begin{equation}
 Q^{-2}\mathbf1\leq T_U^{UV},T_U^{UVW}\leq Q^2\mathbf1.
\label{stat:positive-boundary-bounds}
\end{equation}
Moreover, multiplication of the exponentials gives the exact identity
\[
 F_{U,V}=e^{\beta(H_U+H_V)/2}
          e^{-\beta H_{UV}}e^{\beta(H_U+H_V)/2},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}_V[e^{-\beta H_{UV}}]
 =Z_V e^{-\beta H_U/2}T_U^{UV}e^{-\beta H_U/2}.
\]
Here $Z_V=\operatorname{Tr}[e^{-\beta H_V}]$. With $S=e^{-\beta H_U/2}$, both reduced Gibbs states have the
same outside factors:
\begin{equation}
 (\sigma^{UV})_U=\frac{ST_U^{UV}S}{\operatorname{Tr}[ST_U^{UV}S]},\qquad
 (\sigma^{UVW})_U=\frac{ST_U^{UVW}S}{\operatorname{Tr}[ST_U^{UVW}S]}.
\label{stat:positive-boundary-marginals}
\end{equation}

Next, for any two expansionals bounded by $Q$,
\[
 \|E^*E-\widetilde E^*\widetilde E\|_\infty
 \leq2Q\|E-\widetilde E\|_\infty.
\]
We localise both $F_{U,VW}$ and $F_{U,V}$ to $F_{U,V_1}$.
By hypothesis, $r_\beta(t)\leq b'_\beta e^{-c'_\beta t}$, and hence the two
localisation errors sum to at most $4Qr_\beta(|V_1|)$.
For the remaining difference, we set
$D=(\sigma^{VW})_{V_1}-(\sigma^V)_{V_1}$.  Trace duality gives
\[
 \|\operatorname{Tr}_{V_1}[D F_{U,V_1}]\|_\infty
 \leq\|D\|_1\|F_{U,V_1}\|_\infty.
\]
Combining \eqref{stat:mix-foundation-li} for $V_1,V_2,W$ with the two
localisation errors yields
\begin{equation}
 \|T_U^{UVW}-T_U^{UV}\|_\infty
 \leq4Qr_\beta(|V_1|)+Q^2b_\beta e^{-c_\beta|V_2|}
 =:\omega_\beta(|V|)
 \leq b''_\beta e^{-c''_\beta|V|}.
\label{stat:positive-boundary-difference}
\end{equation}
We set $\epsilon=Q^2\omega_\beta(|V|)$.  Positivity and
\eqref{stat:positive-boundary-bounds} imply
\[
 (1-\epsilon)T_U^{UV}\leq T_U^{UVW}\leq(1+\epsilon)T_U^{UV}.
\]
For $\epsilon<1$, congruence by $S$ and normalisation give
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}(\sigma^{UV})_U
 \leq(\sigma^{UVW})_U\leq
 \frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}(\sigma^{UV})_U.
\label{stat:proved-strong-li}
\end{equation}
Thus the comparison factors are $e^{\pm s_\beta(|V|)}$, where
$s_\beta(|V|)=\log[(1+\epsilon)/(1-\epsilon)]
\leq4\epsilon\leq Ce^{-a|V|}$ for sufficiently large $|V|$.

We split $Y=Y_1Y_2Y_3$ so that $|Y_1|=|Y_3|=\lfloor|Y|/3\rfloor$.
The large-gap threshold ensures that $Y_2$ exceeds the interaction
range.  Removing it leaves two noninteracting intervals, and hence
\[
 \sigma^{XY_1\sqcup Y_3Z}
 =\sigma^{XY_1}\otimes\sigma^{Y_3Z}.
\]
We fold the chain through the midpoint of $Y_2$, as shown in
Figure~\ref{stat:folded-gap-removal}(b)--(c).  Paired sites become a
single site, the two outer regions $X,Z$ form an outer interval, the
two equal collars $Y_1,Y_3$ form its buffer, and $Y_2$ forms the inner
interval.  The folded chain has doubled local qubit number and bounded
nearest-neighbour strength; shorter legs can be padded with decoupled
spins.  Thus the preceding relative-order estimate applies to this chain too, with constants depending
only on the original fixed parameters.  This is the folding geometry
used in \cite{BergamaschiChen2025FastMixing}.

We begin by proving that we can lower bound the conditional Dirichlet form in terms of the on-site conditional variance as:
	\begin{align}
		\mathcal E_S^a(X) \geq \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in S} \operatorname{Var}_i(X) \,,\label{eq:4.2}
	\end{align}
	both for the heat-bath and the regularised sampler. Since $\mathcal E_S^a(X)  = \sum_{i\in S} \mathcal E_i^a(X) $, this is equivalent to proving:
	\begin{align}
		\operatorname{Re}
		\langle X,-\mathcal L_i^a(X)\rangle_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}\geq \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Var}_i(X) \qquad a\in\{\mathrm{HB},\mathrm{RHB}\}.\label{eq:4.3}
	\end{align}
	Let us start with the case of the heat-bath sampler $\mathcal L_i^{\mathrm{HB}} := -(\operatorname{id}-\mathcal P_i)$.  First, notice that by applying the Kadison--Schwarz inequality to $\mathcal P_i$ it follows that:
	\[
	\|\mathcal P_iX\|_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}^2 = \operatorname{Tr}[\sigma \mathcal P_i(X)^\dagger\mathcal P_i(X)]
	\le\operatorname{Tr}[\sigma\mathcal P_i(X^\dagger X)]
	=\operatorname{Tr}[\sigma X^\dagger X]
	=\|X\|_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}^2,
	\]
	where we implicitly used the invariance $\mathcal P_{i*}(\sigma) = \sigma$. Then, Eq.~\eqref{eq:4.3} follows from the inequality:
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		2\operatorname{Re}\langle X,(\operatorname{id}-\mathcal P_i)X\rangle_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}
		&=\|X-\mathcal P_iX\|_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}^2
		+\|X\|_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}^2
		-\|\mathcal P_iX\|_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}^2\\
		&\ge\|X-\mathcal P_iX\|_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}^2
		\ge\operatorname{Var}_i(X),
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	where we used $(\|X\|_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}^2
	-\|\mathcal P_iX\|_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}^2)\ge0$, and the last inequality follows from $\mathcal P_iX\in\mathcal A_{i^c}$. This proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:4.2} in the case of the heat-bath sampler.  For the regularised generator the argument is even simpler, since $(\operatorname{id} - \mathbb E_i)$ is a GNS orthogonal projection. Then, it follows that:

\label{gap:global-memory}
	Theorem~\ref{gap:conditional} directly implies a lower bound on the KMS spectral gap:
	\begin{equation}\label{gap:global-spectral}
		\lambda_{\mathrm{KMS}}(\mathcal L_\Lambda^a)\ge\gamma_\beta.\qquad a\in\{\mathrm{HB},\mathrm{RHB}\}
	\end{equation}
	Indeed, since $\mathcal L_\Lambda^a$ is KMS self-adjoint, its spectrum is real. Then, for any eigenoperator $X$ it holds that $-\mathcal L_\Lambda^a X=\lambda X$ (with
	$\lambda\ne0$), which implies that 
	\[
	\lambda\|X\|_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}^2
	=\mathcal E_\Lambda^a(X)
	\ge\gamma_\beta\|X\|_{\mathrm{GNS},\sigma}^2,

		&\ge\frac{\lambda_{\rm GNS}(\mathcal L)}{C_{\rm cb}(E_\sigma)},

		\alpha_{\rm CMLSI}(\mathcal L)
		&\ge\frac{\lambda_{\rm GNS}(\mathcal L)}

		\quad\text{if $\mathcal L$ is GNS symmetric}.
		\label{cmlsi:symmetric-gns}

First, let us report the following result from~\cite[Proposition 3.2]{bcp}: for $X$ supported on an interval $I$ and $|z|\le2$,
	\begin{equation}\label{loc-input-imaginary}
		\left\|e^{zK}Xe^{-zK}\right\|_\infty\le G^{|I|}\left\|X\right\|_\infty,\qquad
		\left\|(\operatorname{id}- E^{\mathrm{tr}}_{I[m]})e^{zK}Xe^{-zK}\right\|_\infty
		\le C G^{|I|}\frac{G^m}{(\lfloor m/r_0\rfloor+1)!}\left\|X\right\|_\infty.
	\end{equation}

	Then, define $K_0=K_D+K_{D^c}$, so that $F_D(z) := e^{zK} e^{-zK_0}$.
	The perturbation $(K-K_0)$ is a bounded sum of finite-range terms
	supported at no more than two cuts. Also introduce 
	$B(z)=e^{zK_0}(K-K_0)e^{-zK_0}$. Thanks to Eq.~\eqref{loc-input-imaginary}, there exist constants $\tilde{G}$ and $\tilde C$ such that
	\[
		\|B(z)\|_\infty\leq \tilde{G},\qquad\qquad \|(\operatorname{id} -  E^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathcal B_D[m]})B(z)\|_\infty \leq \varepsilon_m := \tilde{C} \frac{\tilde{G}^m}{(\lfloor m/r_0\rfloor+1)!}.
	\]
	Moreover, for any $0\le s\le1$,
	the expansional satisfies
	$\frac{d}{ds}F_D(sz)=zF_D(sz)B(sz)$, $F_D(0)=\mathbf1$. Then, by applying Grönwall’s inequality together with the bound on $\|B(z)\|_\infty$, we get $\|F_D(z)\| \leq e^{|z|\tilde{G}}$. The same argument can also be made for $ F_D(z)^{-1}$, since $\frac{d}{ds}F_D(sz)^{-1}=-zB(sz)F_D(sz)^{-1}$.  This proves the first inequality in Eq.~\eqref{loc-boundary-cocycle-tail} with $G = e^{2 \tilde{G}}$.

	At this point, let us introduce the notation $B_m(s) :=  E^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathcal B_D[m]}B(sz)$, and define the operator $F_m(s)$ to solve the differential equation $\frac{d}{ds}F_m(s)=zF_m(s)B_m(s)$, $F_m(0)=\mathbf1$. Since conditional expectations are contractive, $\|B_m(s)\|_\infty\leq \tilde{G}$, and  therefore $\|F_m(s)^{\pm1}\|_\infty \leq G$. For the inverse, use $\frac{d}{ds}F_m(s)^{-1}=-zB_m(s)F_m(s)^{-1}$ and $F_m(0)^{-1}=\mathbf1$. Writing $D_m(s)=\left\|F_D(sz)-F_m(s)\right\|_\infty$, integration of the differential equation gives
	\begin{align*}
		D_m(s)
		&\le |z|\int_0^s\!\bigl(\tilde G D_m(t)+G\varepsilon_m\bigr)\,dt\\
		&\le2\tilde G\int_0^s D_m(t)\,dt+2G\varepsilon_m.
	\end{align*}
	Then, applying Grönwall’s inequality one more time gives that $\|F_D(sz)-F_m(s)\|_\infty$ decays as $\varepsilon_m$. Then, we can use this to bound the right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{loc-boundary-cocycle-tail}:
	\begin{align}
		\left\|(\operatorname{id}- E^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathcal B_D[m]})F_D(z)\right\|_\infty = \left\|(\operatorname{id}- E^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathcal B_D[m]})(F_D(z)-F_m(1))\right\|_\infty \leq \tilde{G}' \varepsilon_m,
	\end{align}
	where we implicitly used the fact that $ E^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathcal B_D[m]}(F_m(1))=F_m(1)$, and that conditional expectations are contractive.

	X_k[X_{k+1},A]+Z_k[Z_{k+1},A].
	\]
	Since $[H,A]\in\mathcal N_1$ acts trivially on site $k$, linear
	independence of $\mathbf1_k,X_k,Z_k$ forces both commutators to vanish.
	Thus $A$ also acts trivially on site $k+1$. Induction from $k=1$
	to $n-1$ proves the claim.

	For any channel $T_*$ supported on $\{1\}[b]$ with $b<n-1$,
	its adjoint satisfies $T(Z_n)=Z_n$. Trace-norm duality therefore gives
	\[
	\|\mathbb{E}_{1*}-T_*\|_\diamond
	\ge\|\mathbb{E}_1(Z_n)-T(Z_n)\|_\infty
	=\|\operatorname{Tr}[\sigma Z_n]\mathbf1-Z_n\|_\infty\ge1.
	\]
	Hence no approximation error tending to zero with $b$, uniformly
	in the chain length, is possible for this regularised update or
	its generator term $\mathbb{E}_{1*}-\operatorname{id}$. Taking infinitely many iterations
	can therefore destroy the uniform locality of the original update.

\label{lem:superposition}
	Assume that $S$ is a nonempty interval and $\eta\ge0$.
	Let $\Pi$ be a Hilbert--Schmidt orthogonal projection such that
	$D_S(vv^\dagger\Vert\sigma)\le\eta$ for every unit vector

We use a construction akin to the one used in \cite{superpositions}.
		First suppose $0<x:=\left\|(1-\Pi)\psi\right\|_2^2<1$.
		Let us decompose any unit Hilbert--Schmidt amplitude $\psi$ as
	$\psi=\sqrt{1-x}\,v+\sqrt x\,w$, where $v,w$ are
		orthogonal unit amplitudes, chosen as $v=\Pi\psi/\sqrt{1-x}$ and $w=(1-\Pi)\psi/\sqrt x$, with $v\in\operatorname{Ran}\Pi$, and
	$x=\left\|(1-\Pi)\psi\right\|_2^2$. Introduce a purifying space $E$, and a qubit register $F$, and define
	\[
	|\Omega\rangle=\sqrt{1-x}\,|0\rangle_F|v\rangle_{\Lambda E}
	+\sqrt x\,|1\rangle_F|w\rangle_{\Lambda E},
	\]

		Here $E\simeq\mathcal H_\Lambda$, and both vectors use the same canonical vectorisation $|a\rangle_{\Lambda E}:=\sum_j a|j\rangle_\Lambda\otimes|j\rangle_E$ in a fixed orthonormal basis. Thus
		\[
		\operatorname{Tr}_E[|a\rangle\langle b|]=ab^\dagger,
		\qquad \langle a|b\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}[a^\dagger b].
		\]
		This preserves both the coherence $vw^\dagger$ and the orthogonality of $v,w$; in particular, $\Omega_F=(1-x)|0\rangle\langle0|+x|1\rangle\langle1|$.

	Define $\Omega_{\Lambda F} = \operatorname{Tr}_E[|\Omega\rangle\langle \Omega|]$ and $\Omega_F = \operatorname{Tr}_{\Lambda}[\Omega_{\Lambda F}]$. Let us explicitly expand the conditional relative entropy:
	\begin{align}
		D_S(\Omega_{\Lambda F}\Vert\sigma\otimes\Omega_F)
		= D_S(\Omega_\Lambda || \sigma) + I(S:F|S^c)_{\Omega_{\Lambda F}}\,, \label{eq:63}
	\end{align}
	where we have introduced the conditional mutual information. Since the latter satisfies a data processing inequality, for any channel of the form  $(\operatorname{id}_\Lambda\otimes\mathcal{N}_F)$ we have:
	\begin{align}
		D_S((\operatorname{id}_\Lambda\otimes\mathcal{N}_F)(\Omega_{\Lambda F})\Vert\sigma\otimes\mathcal{N}_F(\Omega_F))\leq D_S(\Omega_{\Lambda F}\Vert\sigma\otimes\Omega_F).\label{memory-data-processing}
	\end{align}

	\begin{align}

	\end{align}
	This implies that bounding the conditional entropy in Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.6} is enough to prove the claim.

	\[
	D_S(\Omega_{\Lambda F}\Vert\sigma\otimes\Omega_F)=- S(\Omega_{\Lambda F})+S(\Omega_{S^c F})
	-\operatorname{Tr}\bigl[\Omega_{\Lambda}(\log\sigma-\log\sigma_{S^c})]
	\]

	\[

	\le(1-x)D_S(vv^\dagger\Vert\sigma)
	+xD_S(ww^\dagger\Vert\sigma)+h(x).

		\begin{align*}
		D_S(\omega\Vert\sigma)
		&=-S(\omega)+S(\omega_{S^c})
		-\operatorname{Tr}[\omega(\log\sigma-\mathbf1_S\otimes\log\sigma_{S^c})]\\
		&\le |S|\log d+\|\log\sigma-\mathbf1_S\otimes\log\sigma_{S^c}\|_\infty\\
		&\le c_\beta(|S|+1)=M_S.
		\end{align*}
		For the last inequality, use $K=\beta H_\Lambda$ and the mean-force identity to write the logarithmic difference as $-(K-K_{S^c})-\log Z_S\,\mathbf1-V_{S^c}$. Here $\|K-K_{S^c}\|_\infty\le\beta J|S|$, $|\log Z_S|\le(\log d+\beta J)|S|$, and $\|V_{S^c}\|_\infty$ is bounded independently of the chain. Enlarging $c_\beta$ gives the stated $M_S$. Applying this to $\omega=ww^\dagger$ proves the claim, including singular $ww^\dagger$: only the faithful reference states occur inside logarithms.

		Finally, notice that nonnegativity of binary relative entropy gives
		$h(x)\le x\log\varepsilon^{-1}+\log(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}$. Restoring $x=\left\|(1-\Pi)\psi\right\|_2^2$ gives the claim for $0<x<1$. For positive $X$ with
$\|X\|_{2,\sigma}=1$, differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac d{dt}\log\|P_tX\|_{q(t),\sigma}\right|_{t=0^+}
       =\alpha\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X)-\mathcal E_{\mathrm{KMS}}(X)\le\delta.
 \label{app:norm-derivative}
\end{equation}
Here is a justification that also covers singular inputs and the
supremum over $X$.  The positive unit $L_2(\sigma)$ sphere is compact.
For $Y_t=\Gamma_\sigma^{1/q(t)}(P_tX)$,
\[
 Y'_0=\Gamma_\sigma^{1/2}(\mathcal L(X))
       -\frac\alpha4\{(\log\sigma)Y_0+Y_0\log\sigma\}.
\]
On every bounded scalar interval $x\ge0$, the expansion
$x^{2+h}=x^2+h x^2\log x+O(h^2)$ is uniform for $h\downarrow0$:
$x^{2+h}(\log x)^2$ stays bounded, with its value at zero defined
by continuity.  Together with continuity of $x^2\log x$, this proves
a uniform first-order expansion of $\operatorname{Tr}[Y_t^{q(t)}]$ and hence
\eqref{app:norm-derivative} uniformly on that sphere.

We first take the supremum over positive inputs.  Since the remainder
in the preceding expansion is uniform, there is a function
$r(t)\geq0$ with $r(t)=o(t)$ such that
\[
 \log\|P_tX\|_{q(t),\sigma}\leq\delta t+r(t)
 \qquad\text{for every }X\geq0\text{ with }\|X\|_{2,\sigma}=1.
\]
Thus, if we denote the norm restricted to positive inputs by
\[
 N_+(t):=\sup_{\substack{X\geq0\\\|X\|_{2,\sigma}=1}}
                  \|P_tX\|_{q(t),\sigma},
\]
then $N_+(t)\leq e^{\delta t+r(t)}$.

We next show that the same bound holds for arbitrary inputs.
For fixed $t$, we write $q=q(t)$ and pass to the ordinary Schatten
norms using the map
\[
 \Phi_t:=\Gamma_\sigma^{1/q}\circ P_t\circ\Gamma_\sigma^{-1/2}.
\]
Each factor is completely positive.  Moreover,
$\|\Phi_t\|_{2\to q}=\|P_t\|_{2\to q,\sigma}$, and
\[
 \|\Phi_t(B)\|_q\leq N_+(t)\|B\|_2
 \qquad(B\geq0),
\]
because $X=\Gamma_\sigma^{-1/2}(B)$ is positive and
$\|X\|_{2,\sigma}=\|B\|_2$.
For an arbitrary matrix $Z$, the polar decomposition gives
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}|Z^\dagger|&Z\\ Z^\dagger&|Z|\end{pmatrix}\geq0.
\]
Complete positivity therefore implies
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \Phi_t(|Z^\dagger|)&\Phi_t(Z)\\
  \Phi_t(Z)^\dagger&\Phi_t(|Z|)
 \end{pmatrix}\geq0.
\]
Writing $A=\Phi_t(|Z^\dagger|)$ and $B=\Phi_t(|Z|)$, positivity

contraction $K$.  Schatten H\"older and the positive-input bound yield
\begin{align*}
 \|\Phi_t(Z)\|_q
 &\leq\|A^{1/2}\|_{2q}\|K\|_\infty\|B^{1/2}\|_{2q}\\
 &\leq\|A\|_q^{1/2}\|B\|_q^{1/2}\\
 &\leq N_+(t)\,
       \bigl\||Z^\dagger|\bigr\|_2^{1/2}
       \bigl\||Z|\bigr\|_2^{1/2}
 =N_+(t)\|Z\|_2.
\end{align*}
The last equality uses that $Z$, $|Z|$, and $|Z^\dagger|$ have the
same singular values.  Taking the supremum over arbitrary $Z$ shows

\end{equation}

\label{tightening:norm-to-entropy}
Let $\Phi$ be a unital completely positive map with
$\Phi_*(\sigma)=\sigma$. If $\|\Phi\|_{2\to p,\sigma}\leq1$ for
some $p>2$, then every state $\rho$ satisfies
\[
 D(\Phi_*\rho\Vert\sigma)\leq\frac2pD(\rho\Vert\sigma).
\]
Consequently, if $\|P_t\|_{2\to 2+2ct,\sigma}\leq1$ for all
sufficiently small $t>0$ and some $c>0$, then
\[
 \operatorname{EP}(\rho)\geq cD(\rho\Vert\sigma),\qquad
 D(P_{t*}\rho\Vert\sigma)\leq e^{-ct}D(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 \quad(t\geq0).
\]

We set $p'=p/(p-1)$ and write the KMS adjoint as
\[
 \Phi^\#=\Gamma_\sigma^{-1}\Phi_*\Gamma_\sigma.
\]
Weighted H\"older duality gives
\begin{align*}
 \|\Phi^\#(X)\|_{2,\sigma}
 &=\sup_{\|Y\|_{2,\sigma}=1}
       |\langle\Phi^\#(X),Y\rangle_{\mathrm{KMS}}|\\
 &=\sup_{\|Y\|_{2,\sigma}=1}
       |\langle X,\Phi(Y)\rangle_{\mathrm{KMS}}|
 \le\|X\|_{p',\sigma}.
\end{align*}
At the other endpoint, $\Gamma_\sigma\Phi^\#=\Phi_*\Gamma_\sigma$
and trace-norm contraction of $\Phi_*$ imply
\[
 \|\Phi^\#(X)\|_{1,\sigma}
 =\|\Phi_*(\Gamma_\sigma(X))\|_1
 \le\|\Gamma_\sigma(X)\|_1=\|X\|_{1,\sigma}.
\]
Interpolating these bounds, for $0\le s\le1$ we obtain
\[
 \|\Phi^\#(X)\|_{b_s,\sigma}\le\|X\|_{a_s,\sigma},
 \qquad
 \frac1{a_s}=1-s+\frac{s}{p'}=1-\frac{s}{p},\qquad
 \frac1{b_s}=1-s+\frac{s}{2}=1-\frac{s}{2}.
\]
In particular,
\[
 a_0=b_0=1,\qquad a'_0=\frac1p,\qquad b'_0=\frac12.
\]
For $f_\rho=\Gamma_\sigma^{-1}(\rho)$, we have
$\Phi^\#(f_\rho)=f_{\Phi_*\rho}$, so
\[
 \log\|f_{\Phi_*\rho}\|_{b_s,\sigma}
 \le\log\|f_\rho\|_{a_s,\sigma}.
\]
Both sides vanish at $s=0$.

For faithful $\rho$, we write
\[
 A(u)=\Gamma_\sigma^{1/u-1}(\rho),\qquad
 F(u)=\operatorname{Tr}[A(u)^u],\qquad
 \log\|f_\rho\|_{u,\sigma}=\frac1u\log F(u).
\]
At $u=1$ we have $A(1)=\rho$, $F(1)=1$, and
\[
 A'(1)=-\tfrac12\bigl((\log\sigma)\rho+\rho\log\sigma\bigr).
\]
Differentiating under the trace gives
\begin{align*}
 F'(1)
 &=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\log\rho]+\operatorname{Tr}[A'(1)]\\
 &=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\log\rho]-\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\log\sigma]
 =D(\rho\Vert\sigma).
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\[
 \left.\frac d{du}\log\|f_\rho\|_{u,\sigma}\right|_{u=1}
 =D(\rho\Vert\sigma).
\]
The same calculation applies to $\Phi_*\rho$: if $\rho\ge\varepsilon\sigma$
for some $\varepsilon>0$, positivity and invariance give
$\Phi_*\rho\ge\varepsilon\sigma$. Differentiating the interpolated
norm inequality at $s=0$ yields
\[
 \frac12D(\Phi_*\rho\Vert\sigma)
 \le\frac1pD(\rho\Vert\sigma).
\]

Approximation by $(1-\varepsilon)\rho+\varepsilon\sigma$ and
continuity of relative entropy extend the channel inequality to all states.

Applying this estimate with $\Phi=P_t$ and $p=2+2ct$ gives, for
all sufficiently small $t>0$,
\[
 D(P_{t*}\rho\Vert\sigma)\le\frac{1}{1+ct}D(\rho\Vert\sigma).
\]
Differentiating at $t=0^+$ and using \eqref{eq:ep} gives
$\operatorname{EP}(\rho)\ge cD(\rho\Vert\sigma)$. The admissible time interval
need not be uniform in the system size.

Applying this inequality along $\rho_t=P_{t*}\rho$ gives
\[
 \frac d{dt}D(\rho_t\Vert\sigma)
 =-\operatorname{EP}(\rho_t)\le-cD(\rho_t\Vert\sigma),\qquad
 D(\rho_t\Vert\sigma)\le e^{-ct}D(\rho\Vert\sigma).
\]
Finally, approximation by $(1-\varepsilon)\rho+\varepsilon\sigma$
extends the entropy contraction to all states. Its right derivative
at zero gives the same entropy-production inequality, with the usual
extended value allowed for $\operatorname{EP}$.

 \mathcal N_i&=\bigoplus_j\mathcal B(\mathcal H_{j,L})\otimes\mathbf1_{j,R},

The $P_j$ are the central block projections; $q_j>0$ and

$\mathbb{E}_i$ the identity on each left factor and a state functional on its
