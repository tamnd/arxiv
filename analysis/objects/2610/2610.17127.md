<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:app-fw}
Suppose $\Phi$ is monotone on $K$ and $C^1$ near $K$, and its Jacobian
at a VI solution $x^*$ is coercive in directions parallel to the exposed
face $F_*:=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{s\in K}\langle \Phi(x^*),s\rangle$, in the sense of
\eqref{eq:face-coercivity}. Then $x^*$ is unique and there
is a finite constant
$C_{\rm FW}=C_{\rm FW}(K,\Phi,\alpha,\beta)$ such that every trajectory of
\eqref{eq:FW-intro} satisfies
\[
 \lVert x_T-x^*\rVert\leq C_{\rm FW}T^{-\min\{\alpha,1\}}\qquad(T\geq1).
\]
The constant is independent of the starting point and all vertex tie-breaking
choices. In
particular, every fixed $\alpha\geq1$ gives an $O(T^{-1})$ distance rate.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:schedule-characterization}
Fix a deterministic schedule with $0<\gamma_k\leq1$, $\gamma_k\to0$,
and $\sum_k\gamma_k=\infty$. Choose $n\geq1$ such that $\gamma_k<1$
for all $k\geq n$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schedule-normalization}
 \rho_n=1,\qquad \rho_{k+1}=\frac{\rho_k}{1-\gamma_k},\qquad
 w_k=\rho_{k+1}\gamma_k,\qquad
 q_k=\max\{\gamma_k,\rho_k^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}
On the fixed instance $K=[0,1]^2$, $\Phi(\xi,\eta)=(\xi-1/2,\eta)$,
there are a starting point, exact vertex selections, and a constant
$c>0$, depending only on the schedule and $n$, such that
\[
 \Delta_k:=\max\{\lVert x_k-x^*\rVert,\lVert x_{k+1}-x^*\rVert\}
 \geq c q_k\qquad\text{for all sufficiently large }k.
\]
This trajectory is not $o(k^{-1})$. Consequently, the $O(k^{-1})$
distance guarantee in Theorem~\ref{thm:app-fw} cannot be improved to
$o(k^{-1})$ uniformly over problems by any such prescribed schedule.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:unique}
A solution $x^*$ satisfying \eqref{eq:face-coercivity} is the unique VI
solution whenever $\Phi$ is monotone on $K$ and differentiable at $x^*$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Suppose $y$ is another solution and put $h=y-x^*$. The two VI inequalities
and monotonicity imply
\[
 0\leq\langle \Phi(x^*),h\rangle\leq\langle \Phi(y),h\rangle\leq0.
\]
Thus $y\in F_*$ and $h\in T_*$. Monotonicity along the segment
$x^*+th$, $0\leq t\leq1$, makes
$t\mapsto\langle \Phi(x^*+th),h\rangle$ nondecreasing. Its endpoint values are both
zero, so it is identically zero. Differentiating at $t=0$ gives
$0=\langle D\Phi(x^*)h,h\rangle\geq\mu_*\lVert h\rVert^2$, hence $y=x^*$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:uniform-fw}
Let $K$ be nonempty, compact, and convex, and let $\Phi$ be $C^1$ on a
neighborhood of $K$ and monotone on $K$. If $\mathcal S$ is the VI solution
set, then
\[
 \sup_{(x_k)\in\mathfrak X}\operatorname{dist}(x_k,\mathcal S)\longrightarrow0.
\]
If $\mathcal S=\{x^*\}$, this is uniform convergence to $x^*$. If also $K$
is a polytope, there is a common $k_0=k_0(K,\Phi,(\gamma_k))$ such that all
vertex-returning trajectories in $\mathfrak X$ satisfy
\[
 s_k\in\operatorname{vert}(F_*)\qquad(k\geq k_0),\qquad
 F_*:=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{s\in K}\langle \Phi(x^*),s\rangle.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The proof has three steps: upper semicontinuity of the inclusion,
a backward-complete limit trajectory argument for uniform attraction,
and exclusion of vertices outside the exposed face.

Define the Frank--Wolfe gap and the differential-inclusion map by
\[
 V(x):=\max_{s\in K}\langle \Phi(x),x-s\rangle,
 \qquad
 \mathcal G(x):=\mathcal B(\Phi(x))-x.
\]
The map $\mathcal G$ is upper semicontinuous with nonempty compact convex
values.  Indeed, if $x_j\to x$, $g_j\to g$, and
$g_j=s_j-x_j\in\mathcal G(x_j)$, compactness permits passage to
$s_j\to s\in K$, and the inequalities
$\langle \Phi(x_j),s_j\rangle\leq\langle \Phi(x_j),v\rangle$ pass to the limit for every
$v\in K$.  Thus $s\in\mathcal B(\Phi(x))$ and $g=s-x\in\mathcal G(x)$.

Hough proves that $V$ is continuous, that $V^{-1}(0)=\mathcal S$, and that
every solution $x(\cdot)$ of
\[
 \dot x(t)\in\mathcal G(x(t))
\]
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hough-gap-decay}
 V(x(t))\leq e^{-(t-r)}V(x(r))
 \qquad(r\leq t)
\end{equation}
\cite[Lemma~2(a)--(b)]{Hough2026}. More precisely, part~(b) is stated
for $K$-valued solutions of $\dot x\in\mathcal B(\Phi(x))-x$, so no projected
extension is needed here. Applying it to the time-shifted solution
$\tau\mapsto x(r+\tau)$ gives the displayed estimate for arbitrary $r\leq t$.

A proof of the gap-decay estimate \eqref{eq:hough-gap-decay} is given in
Appendix~\ref{sec:gap-decay}.

Suppose that the asserted uniform convergence fails.  Then there are
trajectories $(x_k^j)\in\mathfrak X$, integers $k_j\to\infty$, and
$\delta>0$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(x_{k_j}^j,\mathcal S)\geq\delta.
\]
Set $\sigma_0:=0$ and $\sigma_k:=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma_i$, and let $w_j$ be the
piecewise affine interpolation satisfying $w_j(\sigma_k)=x_k^j$.  Every
$w_j$ takes values in $K$ and is $\operatorname{diam}(K)$-Lipschitz.  Shift
the interpolation by defining
\[
 W_j(t):=w_j(t+\sigma_{k_j}),
 \qquad t\in[-\sigma_{k_j},\infty).
\]
On each compact interval, the paths $W_j$ are uniformly bounded and
equi-Lipschitz. Let $\widehat W_j(t)$ denote the left endpoint iterate and
$\ell_j(t)$ its iteration index. For almost every $t$,
\[
 \dot W_j(t)\in\mathcal G(\widehat W_j(t)).
\]
On every fixed compact interval $I$, the smallest index $\ell_j(t)$ tends
to infinity: for any fixed $m$, $\sigma_{k_j}+\inf I>\sigma_m$ eventually.
Since $\gamma_k\to0$, the interpolation error vanishes uniformly:
\[
 \sup_{t\in I}\lVert W_j(t)-\widehat W_j(t)\rVert\to0.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:appendix-di-compactness}, applied on $K$ to
$f_j=W_j$, $\widehat f_j=\widehat W_j$, and $G=\mathcal G$, therefore
gives a subsequence converging locally uniformly on $\mathbb R$ to
a complete trajectory $W$ with
$\dot W\in\mathcal B(\Phi(W))-W=\mathcal G(W)$ almost everywhere.

Let $M:=\max_{x\in K}V(x)$.  Applying \eqref{eq:hough-gap-decay} on
$[-T,0]$ gives
\[
 V(W(0))\leq e^{-T}V(W(-T))\leq e^{-T}M
 \qquad(T>0).
\]
Hence $V(W(0))=0$ and $W(0)\in\mathcal S$.  On the other hand,
$W_j(0)=x_{k_j}^j$ and local uniform convergence imply
$\operatorname{dist}(W(0),\mathcal S)\geq\delta$, a contradiction.  This
proves the first assertion. If the solution is unique, distance to
$\mathcal S$ equals distance to $x^*$, giving the second.

It remains to prove identification when the solution is unique and $K$ is
a polytope. If $F_*=K$, the assertion is automatic. Otherwise put
\[
 g_*:=\min_{v\in\operatorname{vert}(K)\setminus F_*}
       \langle \Phi(x^*),v-x^*\rangle>0.
\]
For $x$ close enough to $x^*$, continuity gives
\[
 \langle \Phi(x),v-x^*\rangle\geq g_*/2
 \qquad\forall v\in\operatorname{vert}(K)\setminus F_*.
\]
Choose a common $k_0$ after which every trajectory is in that neighborhood.
A selected vertex outside $F_*$ would satisfy
\[
 0<g_*/2\leq\langle \Phi(x_k),s_k-x^*\rangle\leq0,
\]
since $x^*$ is a feasible comparator in the oracle inequality. This is
impossible. Therefore every selected vertex lies in $F_*$ after $k_0$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:weighted}
Let $U$ be a nonempty finite subset of a finite-dimensional Euclidean
space $E$, with $0\in\operatorname{conv} U$, and let $A\colon E\to E$ be linear with
$\langle Ah,h\rangle\geq\mu\lVert h\rVert^2$ for all $h\in E$ and some $\mu>0$.
There is a constant $C_w=C_w(E,U,A)\geq1$ such that every
finite or infinite recursion
\[
 z_{k+1}=z_k+w_ku_k,\qquad u_k\in U,\qquad 0\leq w_k\leq W,
 \qquad
 \langle Az_k,u_k\rangle\leq\min_{u\in U}\langle Az_k,u\rangle+B,
\]
with $W,B\geq0$, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-bound}
 \sup_k\lVert z_k\rVert\leq C_w(W+\lVert z_0\rVert+B).
\end{equation}
The constant is independent of $W$, $B$, the initial state, the number of updates,
and the admissible choices.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:absorb}
Fix $m>N$. Suppose $y_{k+1}=y_k+w_ku_k$, $u_k\in U$, and $w_k\geq0$
for $N\leq k<m$, and the score tolerance at each such step is at most
$b+\eta\lVert y_k\rVert$, where $b,\eta\geq0$ and $C_w\eta<1$. Put
$W_m:=\max_{N\leq k<m}w_k$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-absorb}
 \max_{N\leq k\leq m}\lVert y_k\rVert
 \leq\frac{C_w\bigl(W_m+\lVert y_N\rVert+b\bigr)}
 {1-C_w\eta}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Put $M_m:=\max_{N\leq k\leq m}\lVert y_k\rVert$. Every score tolerance
for $N\leq k<m$ is at most $b+\eta M_m$. Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted} gives
\[
 M_m\leq C_w(W_m+\lVert y_N\rVert+b)+C_w\eta M_m.
\]
Subtracting $C_w\eta M_m$ from both sides and dividing by the positive
number $1-C_w\eta$ proves the bound. If $W_m=0$, the sequence is
constant and the bound is immediate.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The prescribed schedule satisfies the hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-fw}, and the solution is unique by
Lemma~\ref{lem:unique}. There is therefore a common identification index
$N_0\geq1$ such that all subsequent selected vertices lie in $F_*$.
We use the rescaling factors defined for $k\geq1$ in
Section~\ref{sec:rescale}.

Set $U_*:=\operatorname{vert}(F_*)-x^*$, so $0\in\operatorname{conv} U_*$, and use
$z_k=\rho_k(x_k-x^*)$. If $U_*=\{0\}$, \eqref{eq:rescale} makes $z_k$
constant after $N_0$, and $\lVert x_k-x^*\rVert=O(k^{-\alpha})$, which suffices.
Otherwise put $R_*:=\max_{u\in U_*}\lVert u\rVert>0$. Let $P_*$ be the orthogonal
projection onto $T_*$, and write
\[
 y_k=P_*z_k,\qquad q=z_k-y_k,\qquad
 A_*=P_*D\Phi(x^*)|_{T_*},\qquad c=P_*D\Phi(x^*)q.
\]
Every increment $w_k(s_k-x^*)$ belongs to $T_*$, so $q$ is constant after
$N_0$. By \eqref{eq:face-coercivity},
$\langle A_*h,h\rangle\geq\mu_*\lVert h\rVert^2$ for $h\in T_*$. Thus $A_*$ is invertible
and $\lVert A_*^{-1}\rVert\leq\mu_*^{-1}$. Define
\[
 d=A_*^{-1}c,\qquad \widetilde y_k=y_k+d,\qquad u_k=s_k-x^*.
\]
Then $\widetilde y_{k+1}=\widetilde y_k+w_ku_k$. The Taylor remainder
\[
 r_k:=\rho_kP_*\bigl(\Phi(x_k)-\Phi(x^*)
              -D\Phi(x^*)(x_k-x^*)\bigr)
\]
obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-remainder}
 \lVert r_k\rVert\leq\omega(\lVert x_k-x^*\rVert)\lVert z_k\rVert,\qquad \omega(t)\to0.
\end{equation}
A common nondecreasing modulus $\omega$ follows by taking the supremum of
the normalized Taylor remainder on $K\cap B(x^*,t)$.

For $u\in U_*$, $\langle \Phi(x^*),u\rangle=0$. Multiplying the oracle inequality
by $\rho_k>0$ therefore gives
\[
 \langle A_*\widetilde y_k+r_k,u_k\rangle
 =\min_{u\in U_*}\langle A_*\widetilde y_k+r_k,u\rangle.
\]
Comparing with a minimizer of the linearized score yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-oracle-error}
 \langle A_*\widetilde y_k,u_k\rangle
 \leq\min_{u\in U_*}\langle A_*\widetilde y_k,u\rangle
       +2R_*\lVert r_k\rVert.
\end{equation}
The substitution $\widetilde y_k=y_k+A_*^{-1}c$ gives
$A_*\widetilde y_k=A_*y_k+c$. The oracle tolerance in
\eqref{eq:boundary-oracle-error} therefore depends only on the Taylor
remainder $r_k$.

Let $C_{w,*}$ be the constant in Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted} for
$(T_*,U_*,A_*)$. Choose $\epsilon>0$ with $2C_{w,*}R_*\epsilon<1/2$.
Uniform attraction supplies a common $N\geq N_0$ after which the Taylor
modulus is at most $\epsilon$. Put
\[
 D_K:=\operatorname{diam}(K),\quad L_*:=\lVert D\Phi(x^*)\rVert,\quad
 Q_N:=\rho_ND_K(1+L_*/\mu_*).
\]
Uniformly over the family,
\[
 \lVert y_N\rVert,\lVert q\rVert\leq\rho_ND_K,\quad
 \lVert c\rVert\leq L_*\rho_ND_K,\quad
 \lVert d\rVert\leq(L_*/\mu_*)\rho_ND_K.
\]
Hence $\lVert \widetilde y_N\rVert\leq Q_N$ and
$\lVert z_k\rVert\leq\lVert \widetilde y_k\rVert+Q_N$ for $k\geq N$.
Fix any index $m>N$ and set
\[
 Y_m:=\max_{N\leq k\leq m}\lVert \widetilde y_k\rVert,\qquad
 W_m:=\max_{N\leq k<m}w_k.
\]
The tolerance is at most $2R_*\epsilon(Y_m+Q_N)$. Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:absorb} with $b=2R_*\epsilon Q_N$ and
$\eta=2R_*\epsilon$ gives
\[
 Y_m\leq\frac{C_{w,*}(W_m+Q_N+2R_*\epsilon Q_N)}
                   {1-2C_{w,*}R_*\epsilon}.
\]
The coefficient in this bound is independent of $m$.
The schedule formulas in Section~\ref{sec:rescale} imply
\[
 W_m=O(1+m^{(\alpha-1)_+}),\qquad \rho_m\asymp m^\alpha,
\]
where $a_+:=\max\{a,0\}$. Adding $Q_N$ and dividing by $\rho_m$ gives
$\lVert x_m-x^*\rVert=O(m^{-\min\{\alpha,1\}})$.
The diameter bound covers the finite prefix after enlarging the constant.
The constants may depend on the instance and schedule but are uniform
over starting points and vertex selections.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:general-schedules}
Under the assumptions on $K$ and $\Phi$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:app-fw}, use any
fixed deterministic schedule with $0<\gamma_k\leq1$, $\gamma_k\to0$, and
$\sum_k\gamma_k=\infty$ in the exact vertex iteration. Choose $n\geq1$ so that
$\gamma_k<1$ for $k\geq n$, and define
\[
 \rho_n=1,\qquad \rho_{k+1}=\frac{\rho_k}{1-\gamma_k},\qquad
 w_k=\rho_{k+1}\gamma_k,\qquad W_m=\max_{n\leq k<m}w_k.
\]
There is a finite constant $C$, depending only on the problem, the fixed
schedule, and $n$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-schedule-rate}
 \lVert x_m-x^*\rVert\leq C\frac{W_m}{\rho_m}\qquad(m>n),
\end{equation}
uniformly over starting points and vertex tie-breaking. If the weights are
eventually nondecreasing, equivalently if eventually
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schedule-weight-monotonicity}
 \gamma_{k+1}\geq\frac{\gamma_k}{1+\gamma_k},
\end{equation}
then $\lVert x_m-x^*\rVert=O(\gamma_m)$. In particular, this holds for
$\gamma_k=c/(k+b)^a$ with $0<a<1$, $c,b>0$, and $b^a\geq c$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The rescaling gives $z_{k+1}=z_k+w_k(s_k-x^*)$ exactly.
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-fw} supplies uniform identification and a common index
$N\geq n$ where the Taylor modulus is small. Applying the same
decomposition, change of coordinates, and Lemma~\ref{lem:absorb} as in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:app-fw} gives $\lVert z_m\rVert=O(1+W_m)$;
the singleton-face case gives a constant $z_m$.
Since $W_m\geq w_n>0$, we have $1+W_m\leq(1+w_n^{-1})W_m$; dividing by
$\rho_m$ and enlarging $C$ for the finite prefix proves
\eqref{eq:general-schedule-rate}. Moreover,
\[
 \frac{w_{k+1}}{w_k}
 =\frac{\gamma_{k+1}}{\gamma_k(1-\gamma_{k+1})}.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:schedule-weight-monotonicity} is precisely eventual
nondecrease of the weights, in which case $W_m=O(w_{m-1})$ and
\[
 \frac{W_m}{\rho_m}=O(\gamma_{m-1})=O(\gamma_m),\qquad
 \gamma_{m-1}\leq\frac{\gamma_m}{1-\gamma_m}
\]
for all sufficiently large $m$. For the stated power schedule,
$\gamma_{k+1}^{-1}-\gamma_k^{-1}
=c^{-1}((k+b+1)^a-(k+b)^a)\to0$, so the condition holds eventually.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:affine-harmonic}
Suppose $\alpha=\beta=1$ and $\Phi(x)=Lx+a$ is monotone on $K$ and
satisfies \eqref{eq:face-coercivity}. Then the exact-oracle conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:app-fw}, including boundary solutions, follows using only
the exact unit-step cycling bound of \cite[Theorem~1, $B=0$]{RobustCycling}.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Uniform vertex-face identification is independent of the cycling theorem.
After its common index $k_0\geq1$, use the decomposition and shift from the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:app-fw}. Here $\rho_k=k$, $w_k=1$, and $r_k=0$ identically, so
\[
 \widetilde y_{k+1}=\widetilde y_k+u_k,\qquad
 u_k\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{u\in U_*}\langle A_*\widetilde y_k,u\rangle.
\]
Let $C_*$ be the unit-step cycling constant. Exact cycling bounds
$\widetilde y_k$ by $C_*(1+\lVert \widetilde y_{k_0}\rVert)$.
The initial shifted state, the shift $d$, and the normal component $q$ are
uniformly bounded in terms of $k_0D_K$ and $\lVert L\rVert/\mu_*$. Hence $z_k$ is
uniformly bounded. The case $U_*=\{0\}$ is immediate because $z_k$ is
constant after $k_0$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:dual-growth}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:app-fw}, there is $\lambda>0$
such that, for every $s\in K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-growth}
 \langle \Phi(s),s-x^*\rangle\geq\lambda\lVert s-x^*\rVert^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for any Lipschitz constant $L$ of $\Phi$ on $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-gap-bound}
 0\leq G(x)\leq\langle \Phi(x^*),x-x^*\rangle
                 +\frac{L^2}{4\lambda}\lVert x-x^*\rVert^2\qquad\forall x\in K.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The singleton case is immediate. Write $a=\Phi(x^*)$ and
$H(s)=\langle \Phi(s),s-x^*\rangle$. Monotonicity and the VI give
$H(s)\geq\langle a,s-x^*\rangle\geq0$. In fact $H(s)>0$ for $s\ne x^*$:
if equality held, $h=s-x^*$ would belong to $T_*$, while the
nondecreasing function $t\mapsto\langle \Phi(x^*+th),h\rangle$ would have zero
endpoint values and strictly positive derivative at zero, a contradiction.

If \eqref{eq:radial-growth} failed, compactness and this strict positivity
would give $s_j\to x^*$ with $s_j\ne x^*$ and
$H(s_j)/\lVert s_j-x^*\rVert^2\to0$. Pass to a subsequence so that
$v_j=(s_j-x^*)/\lVert s_j-x^*\rVert\to v$, with $\lVert v\rVert=1$.
The polyhedral cone $\operatorname{cone}(K-x^*)$ is closed, so $v$ lies
in this cone. Also $\langle a,v\rangle=0$, since
$0\leq\langle a,v_j\rangle\leq H(s_j)/\lVert s_j-x^*\rVert\to0$.
Thus $x^*+tv\in F_*$ for some $t>0$, and $v\in T_*$.
Differentiability at $x^*$ now gives
\[
 \frac{H(s_j)}{\lVert s_j-x^*\rVert^2}
 =\frac{\langle a,s_j-x^*\rangle}{\lVert s_j-x^*\rVert^2}
    +\langle D\Phi(x^*)v_j,v_j\rangle+o(1)
 \geq\mu_*+o(1),
\]
a contradiction. This proves \eqref{eq:radial-growth}.

For $x,s\in K$, put $e=x-x^*$ and $h=s-x^*$. Then
\[
 \langle \Phi(s),x-s\rangle
 =\langle a,e\rangle+\langle \Phi(s)-a,e\rangle-H(s)
 \leq\langle a,e\rangle+L\lVert h\rVert\lVert e\rVert-\lambda\lVert h\rVert^2
 \leq\langle a,e\rangle+\frac{L^2}{4\lambda}\lVert e\rVert^2.
\]
Maximizing over $s$ proves \eqref{eq:dual-gap-bound}.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:gap-distance}
For exact-oracle trajectories under Theorem~\ref{thm:app-fw}, put
$r=\min\{\alpha,1\}$ and $s=\min\{\alpha,2\}$. Uniformly over starts
and vertex selections for the fixed instance and schedule,
\[
 V(x_k)=O(k^{-r}),\qquad G(x_k)=O(k^{-s}),\qquad
 \lVert x_k-x^*\rVert^2=O(k^{-2r}).
\]
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let $D_K=\operatorname{diam}(K)$ and let $L$ be a Lipschitz constant for
$\Phi$ on $K$. The VI and Lipschitz continuity give
\[
 V(x)\leq(LD_K+\lVert \Phi(x^*)\rVert)\lVert x-x^*\rVert.
\]
Indeed, split $\langle \Phi(x),x-s\rangle$ into
$\langle \Phi(x)-\Phi(x^*),x-s\rangle$,

 =\bigl(\xi-\tfrac12-\eta,\ \xi-\tfrac12+\eta+\tfrac14\bigr),

\label{prop:simplex-oracle}
For $d\geq2$, let

 K_d=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,\ldots,e_d\}\subset\mathbb{R}^d,\qquad
 u_d=d^{-1}(1,\ldots,1),\qquad \Phi_d(x)=x-u_d,

$t+1\geq(4\varepsilon+1/d)^{-1}$. Taking $d\geq\varepsilon^{-1}$
gives a lower bound $(5\varepsilon)^{-1}-1$ on vertex-LMO calls.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:appendix-di-compactness}
Let $X\subset E$ be compact, and let
$G\colon X\rightrightarrows E$ be upper semicontinuous with nonempty compact
convex values.  Let $I=[a,b]$, and suppose that $f_n\colon I\to X$ are
absolutely continuous with a common Lipschitz constant.  Suppose there are
measurable maps $\widehat f_n\colon I\to X$ such that
\[
 \sup_{t\in I}\lVert f_n(t)-\widehat f_n(t)\rVert\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(n\to\infty),
 \qquad
 \dot f_n(t)\in G(\widehat f_n(t))
 \quad\text{for a.e. }t\in I.
\]
Then there is a subsequence $(f_{n_j})_{j\geq1}$ and an absolutely continuous
function $f\colon I\to X$ such that
\[
 \sup_{t\in I}\lVert f_{n_j}(t)-f(t)\rVert\longrightarrow0
 \qquad (j\to\infty)
\]
and
\[
 \dot f(t)\in G(f(t))
 \quad\text{for a.e. }t\in I.
\]
More generally, suppose that $f_n$ and $\widehat f_n$ are defined on intervals
$I_n$ that exhaust $\mathbb{R}$, in the sense that every compact interval is contained
in $I_n$ for all sufficiently large $n$, and that the preceding assumptions
hold on every compact interval, with the approximation error tending to zero
as $n\to\infty$.  Then there is a subsequence $(f_{n_j})_{j\geq1}$ and a
solution $f\colon\mathbb{R}\to X$ such that $f_{n_j}\to f$ uniformly on every compact
interval.

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Arzel\`a--Ascoli gives indices $(n_j)$ for which $f_{n_j}$ converges uniformly.
We pass to this subsequence and suppress the subscript $n_j$ in the remainder
of the proof.  The derivatives
$g_n:=\dot f_n$ are bounded in $L^2(I;E)$ by the common Lipschitz constant, so,
after another subsequence, they converge weakly to some $g$.  Passing to the
limit in
\[
 f_n(t)-f_n(s)=\int_s^t g_n(r)\,dr
\]
shows that $f(t)-f(s)=\int_s^t g(r)\,dr$ and hence $g=\dot f$ almost
everywhere.

It remains to pass to the inclusion.  For $p\in E$, let
$h_p(x):=\max_{v\in G(x)}\langle p,v\rangle$.  Upper semicontinuity and compactness of
the values imply that $h_p$ is upper semicontinuous.  For $\varepsilon>0$
define its $\varepsilon$-enlargement by
\[
 h_p^\varepsilon(x):=\sup\left\{
 h_p(y):y\in X,\ \lVert y-x\rVert\leq\varepsilon
 \right\}.
\]
The function $h_p^\varepsilon$ is upper semicontinuous, hence measurable.
Indeed, if $x_\ell\to x$, pass to a subsequence realizing the upper limit
and choose $y_\ell\in X$ within $\varepsilon$ of $x_\ell$ and within
$1/\ell$ of the defining supremum.  A further convergent subsequence of
$(y_\ell)$ and upper semicontinuity of $h_p$ give
$\limsup_\ell h_p^\varepsilon(x_\ell)\leq h_p^\varepsilon(x)$.

Fix $\varepsilon>0$.  Since $\widehat f_n\to f$ uniformly, for all
sufficiently large $n$ and almost every $t\in I$,
\[
 \langle p,g_n(t)\rangle
 \leq h_p(\widehat f_n(t))
 \leq h_p^\varepsilon(f(t)).
\]
Consequently, for every nonnegative bounded measurable function $\varphi$,
weak convergence yields
\[
 \int_I\varphi(t)\langle p,g(t)\rangle\,dt
 =\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_I\varphi(t)\langle p,g_n(t)\rangle\,dt
 \leq\int_I\varphi(t)h_p^\varepsilon(f(t))\,dt.
\]
It follows that
$\langle p,g(t)\rangle\leq h_p^\varepsilon(f(t))$ almost everywhere.  Apply this
conclusion with $p$ in a fixed countable dense subset of $E$ and with
$\varepsilon$ in the positive rationals.  Outside one null set, all these
inequalities hold simultaneously.  Moreover, upper semicontinuity of $h_p$
and compactness of $X$ imply
\[
 h_p^\varepsilon(x)\downarrow h_p(x)
 \qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
\]
Indeed, $h_p^\varepsilon(x)\geq h_p(x)$, while maximizers corresponding to
any sequence $\varepsilon_\ell\downarrow0$ converge to $x$ and upper
semicontinuity gives the reverse inequality in the limit.
Thus $\langle p,g(t)\rangle\leq h_p(f(t))$ for all $p$ in the countable dense set and
almost every $t$.  Continuity in $p$ extends the inequalities to every
$p\in E$, and the support-function characterization of a closed convex set
gives $g(t)\in G(f(t))$ almost everywhere.

For intervals exhausting $\mathbb R$, apply the preceding argument on
$[-m,m]$ and take a diagonal subsequence.  This is the finite-dimensional
convergence theorem for differential inclusions; see
\cite[Theorem~1.1.4]{AubinCellina1984}.
