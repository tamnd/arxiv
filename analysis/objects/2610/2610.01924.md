<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:width-bound-argument}
    Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $\mathbb{F}_q$ of dimension $g$ and $H \subseteq A(\mathbb{F}_q)$
    with $|H|$ larger than the width of the Hasse--Weil interval. Then $|A(\mathbb{F}_q)| = n$,
    where $n$ is the unique multiple of $|H|$ in the Hasse--Weil interval.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The generalized width of the Hasse--Weil interval is
    \[
        (\sqrt{q} + 1)^{2g} - (\sqrt{q} - 1)^{2g} \leq 4^g \cdot q^{g - 1/2}
    \]
    and the lower bound of the Hasse--Weil interval is
    $(\sqrt{q} - 1)^{2g}$.
    Thus, for any subgroup $H \subseteq A(\mathbb{F}_q)$ such that $|H|$ is
    larger than the width of the Hasse--Weil interval, there is only one unique multiple $n$ of $|H|$
    in the Hasse--Weil interval. But this must then be the size of $A(\mathbb{F}_q)$, as $|A(\mathbb{F}_q)|$ is in the Hasse--Weil
    interval, and a multiple of $|H|$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:poonen en co}
	If $A$ is an extremal abelian variety over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$, then $A$ is superspecial.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By \cite[Theorem 5.3]{max_abvars}, a maximal abelian variety $A$ over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$
    is isomorphic to a product of maximal, hence supersingular, elliptic curves over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$.
    Thus, $A$ is superspecial. The analogous argument holds for minimal abelian varieties.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:probability}
    Let $A$ be an abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_q$ with characteristic polynomial of Frobenius
    $\varphi_A(x) = x^4 + ax^3 - bx^2 + qax + q^2$ with group structure
    \[
            A(\mathbb{F}_q) \cong \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}_{n_0} \times \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}_{n_1} \times \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}_{n_2} \times \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}_{n_3},
    \]
    where $n_{i+1} \mid n_i$. Write $\varphi_A(1- x)$ as $\lambda_0 + \lambda_1 x + \lambda_2 x^2 + \lambda_3 x^3 + x^4$.
    Then,
    \[
        n_3 \mid \lambda_3, \quad n_2 n_3 \mid \lambda_2, \quad n_1 n_2 n_3 \mid \lambda_1, \quad n_0 n_1 n_2 n_3 \mid \lambda_0.
    \]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:lam-in-terms-phi}
    Over $\mathbb{F}_q$, given $\varphi_A(x) = x^4 + ax^3 - bx^2 + qax + q^2$, then
    \begin{align*}
        \lambda_0 &= q^2 + 1 + a(q+1) - b,\\
        \lambda_1 &= -a(q + 1) - 2(a - b + 2),\\
        \lambda_2 &= 3a - b + 6,\\
        \lambda_3 &= -(a + 4).
    \end{align*}

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:supersingular_orders}
	Let $A$ be an abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Then $A$ is supersingular if and only if
	its characteristic polynomial of Frobenius is of the form
	\[
		\varphi_A(x) = x^4 - b p x^2 + p^2, \qquad b \in \{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\}.
	\]
	Consequently, the possible orders of supersingular abelian surfaces over $\mathbb{F}_p$ are
	\[
		|A(\mathbb{F}_p)| \  =  \ \begin{cases}
			\quad (p+1)^2 & \text{if } b = -2			\\
			\quad p^2 + p + 1 & \text{if } b = -1		\\
			\quad p^2 + 1 & \text{if } b = 0			\\
			\quad p^2 - p + 1 & \text{if } b = 1		\\
			\quad (p-1)^2 & \text{if } b = 2
		\end{cases}
	\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The result on $\varphi_A(x)$ follows from \cite{howe2009}. The possible orders are then derived from $|A(\mathbb{F}_p)| = \varphi_A(1)$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:supersingular-type}
	Let $A$ be a supersingular abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
	When $\varphi_A(x) = x^4 - b p x^2 + p^2$, we say that $A$ is \emph{of type $b$}.
	Furthermore, when $n = |A(\mathbb{F}_p)|$, i.e., $n = p^2 - bp + 1$ for $b \in \{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2 \}$,
	then $n$ is a \emph{supersingular order}.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm: possible group structures of rational jacobians}
	Let $A /\mathbb{F}_p$ be a supersingular abelian surface with $p > 5$.
	\begin{enumerate}
		\par If $b \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$, then the group structure is cyclic and:
		\begin{enumerate}
			\par if $b = -1$, then $A(\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/(p^2 + p + 1)\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}},$
			\par if $b = \phantom{-}0$, then  $A(\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/(p^2 + 1)\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}},$
			\par if $b = \phantom{-}1$, then  $A(\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/(p^2 - p + 1)\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}},$
		\end{enumerate}
		\par if $b = \pm 2$, then the group structure is of rank at least 2 and:
		\begin{enumerate}
			\par if $b = -2$ and $p \equiv 1 \bmod 4$, then $A(\mathbb{F}_p) \cong (\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/(p+1)\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}})^2$,

We denote the Weil polynomial for $A$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$
	by $\varphi_p(x)$
	and over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ by $\varphi_q(x)$. The goal is to use  \Cref{lem:supersingular_orders} and show that $a_p =0$ and $p \mid c_p$.

	As $|A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})| = n^2$ we get from $\varphi_q(1) = n^2$ that
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
		a_q(p^2 + 1) + c_q = 2p^2.
	\end{equation}

	By $\varphi_p(x) = \prod (x - \alpha_i)$ and $\varphi_q(x) = \prod (x - \alpha_i^2)$,
	we have that $\varphi_p(x)\varphi_p(-x) = \varphi_q(x^2)$.
	Expanding this out and comparing coefficients, we
	find that $a_q = 2c_p - a_p^2$ and $c_q = c_p^2 - 2 a_p^2 p + 2p^2$.
	This allows us to rewrite \Cref{eq:A} as
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:B}
		a_p^2 = \frac{c_p^2 + 2c_p(p^2 + 1)}{(p+1)^2},
	\end{equation}
	and so from $a_q = 2c_p - a_p^2$, by expressing everything in terms of $c_p$,

	we get
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:C}
		a_q = \frac{c_p(4p - c_p)}{(p+1)^2}.
	\end{equation}
	First we bound $c_p$. From the Weil bound in \Cref{eq:upper-bound-b} we have
	$c_p \leq 2p + \frac{1}{4} a_p^2$.
	Together with \Cref{eq:B}, we obtain
	\[ c^2_p - 2c_p(p^2+4p+1) + 8p(p+1)^2 \geq 0.\]
	Solving the quadratic in $c_p$, we get that $c_p \leq 4p$ or $c_p \geq 2(p^2 + 2p + 1)$. The latter is not possible due to the Weil bounds, so we find that
	$c_p \leq 4p$.

	Furthermore, the right-hand side of \Cref{eq:B} should be positive,
	which implies that $c_p^2+2c_p(p^2+1) \geq 0$.
	Writing as $c_p(c_p+2p^2+2) \geq 0$, and noting that
	$c_p + 2p^2 + 2 \geq 0$ by \Cref{eq:lower-bound-b}, we have that $c_p \geq 0$.
	Thus, we know $0 \leq c_p \leq 4p$.
	Using these values for $c_p$ in \Cref{eq:C}, and noting that $a_q$ is an integer, we have $a_q \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$. We now show that only $a_q = 0$ is possible.
	\begin{itemize}
		\par[$a_q = 1$:] From $(p+1)^2 = c_p (4p - c_p)$, we get $2 \mid c_p$.
		But then $1 = a_q = 2c_p - a_p^2$ cannot have a solution, as this requires $a_p$ odd
		and $a_p^2 \equiv 3 \bmod 4$, which is impossible.
		\par[$a_q = 2$:] From $2(p+1)^2 = c_p (4p - c_p)$, we get $4 \mid c_p$.

	\end{itemize}

	$c_p = 0$, so that $a_p = 0$ (from $a_q = 2c_p - a_p^2$), as the case $c_p = 4p$
	forces $a_p^2 = 8p$, which can only happen for $p = 2$.

		\par $b = \phantom{-}0$: $A[\pi - p] = A^t(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})^{\rho}$ and $A[\pi + p] = A^t(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})^{\rho'}$.

		\par $b = \phantom{-}1$: $A[\pi - p] = A^t(\mathbb{F}_{p^3})^{\rho}$ and $A[\pi + p] = A(\mathbb{F}_{p^3})$.

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Since $A[m] = \bigoplus_{\ell^e \mid m} A[\ell^e]$ and every eigenspace decomposes accordingly, the index
  of $H$ is the product of the local indices $[A[\ell^e] : H \cap A[\ell^e]]$ over the prime powers
  $\ell^e \mid m$. For a given prime factor $\ell$, suppose that $1, -1, p, -p$ are pairwise distinct modulo $\ell$.
  By \Cref{lem:phi splits mod n} we have
  $\varphi(x) \equiv \prod_\lambda (x - \lambda) \bmod \ell^e$, and these factors are pairwise coprime
  in $(\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^e\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}})[x]$ because the differences $\lambda - \lambda'$ are units.
  Thus, $A[\ell^e] = \bigoplus_\lambda \ker(\pi - \lambda)$, where each summand is
  free of rank 1 over $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^e\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}$. This implies that the local index at $\ell$ is 1.
  The only cases where this fails, i.e., $1, -1, p, -p$ are \emph{not} pairwise distinct modulo $\ell$, are:
  $b = 0$ and $\ell = 2$; $b = \pm 1$ and $\ell = 3$; or $b = \pm 2$, where the eigenvalues $\pm 1$ and $\pm p$
  agree (in some order) modulo every $\ell \mid m$.

  Consider first the case $b = \pm 2$.
  By \Cref{lem:eigenspace fields} we have $p \equiv \pm 1 \bmod m$, so $A[\pi - p]$ and $A[\pi + p]$
  coincide, in some order, with $A[\pi - 1] = A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ and $A[\pi + 1] = A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)^\rho$; thus
  $H = A(\mathbb{F}_p) + A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)^\rho$. As $A(\mathbb{F}_p) \cap A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)^\rho = A[2](\mathbb{F}_p)$, we have
  \[
	|H| = \frac{|A(\mathbb{F}_p)| \cdot |A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)|}{|A[2](\mathbb{F}_p)|} = \frac{|A(\mathbb{F}_p)|^2}{|A[2](\mathbb{F}_p)|}.
  \]
  Substituting the three
  group structures of \Cref{thm: possible group structures of rational jacobians} yields the three indices.

  Consider the case $b = 0$, where $m = p^2 + 1$ and $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is cyclic by
  \Cref{thm: possible group structures of rational jacobians}. As $p^2 \equiv 1 \bmod 8$
	we get $m \equiv 2 \bmod 8$, so $2 \mid m$ and the $2$-Sylow subgroup of $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/2\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}$. All

  $\ker(\pi - 1) \cap A[2] = A[2](\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/2\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}$. So, $H \cap A[2] \cong \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/2\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}$ with local

  likewise $\ker(\pi + 1) \cap A[3] \cong A^t[3](\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/3\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}$.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{lem:almost symplectic basis}
  Let $A$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ be a supersingular abelian surface of exponent $m$,
  and  let $P \in A[\pi -1], Q \in A[\pi + 1], R \in A[\pi - p], S \in A[\pi + p]$ be eigenpoints in $A[m]$.

  $\zeta = e_m(D, D')$, and derive constraints on $\zeta$ using

       e_m( \pi(D), \pi(D') ) = \zeta^p \text{ and } e_m( \pi(D), \pi(D') ) = e_m( \lambda D, \lambda' D') = \zeta^{\lambda \cdot \lambda'}.

  to obtain $\zeta_{R, S}^{p+1} = 1$.

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:trace-pairing}
Let $A/\mathbb{F}_p$ be a supersingular abelian surface of type $b = \pm 2$, and let
$n = p \mp 1$. Then $A(\mathbb{F}_p) \subseteq A[n] \subseteq A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$, and
\[
  (\pi + 1)\,A[n] = A(\mathbb{F}_p), \qquad (\pi - 1)\,A[n] = A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)^\rho .
\]
Moreover, for all $R \in A[n]$, $Q \in A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ and $S \in A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)^\rho$,
\[
  e_n\big((\pi + 1)R, Q\big) = e_n(R, Q)^{p+1},
  \qquad
  e_n\big((\pi - 1)R, S\big) = e_n(R, S)^{-(p+1)} .
\]
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The exponent of $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ divides $n$, so
	$A(\mathbb{F}_p) \subseteq A[n]$; the same holds for $A^t$, which is again of type $b = \pm 2$. By~\Cref{rem:torsion-in-Fpt},
	$A[n] \subseteq A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$ and $(\pi - 1)(\pi + 1) = \pi^2 - 1 = 0$ on $A[n]$.
	On $A[n]$, the kernel of $\pi - 1$ is $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$. The kernel of $\pi + 1$ is $A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)^\rho$ that is of order $n^2$, so $(\pi + 1)A[n]$ has order $n^4/n^2 = n^2$. It is contained in
	$\ker(\pi - 1) = A(\mathbb{F}_p)$, thus $(\pi + 1)A[n] = A(\mathbb{F}_p)$. Similarly,
	$(\pi - 1)A[n] = A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)^\rho$.

	Moreover,
	$e_n(\pi R, \pi T) = e_n(R, T)^p$ for all $R, T \in A[n]$. If $Q \in A(\mathbb{F}_p)$, then $e_n(\pi R, Q) = e_n(\pi R, \pi Q) = e_n(R, Q)^p$, and multiplying by
	$e_n(R, Q)$ gives the first identity. For $S \in A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)^\rho$, we have $\pi S = -S$ giving the second identity.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:minmax-test}
    \Cref{alg:minmax-test} runs in $O(\log p)$ time and returns \textbf{\upshape true}
    for any minimal or maximal abelian variety over $\mathbb{F}_q$. For any non-extremal variety of dimension $g$, \Cref{alg:minmax-test}
    returns \textbf{\upshape true} with probability at most $O(p^{-1})$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
This follows directly from \Cref{lem:width-bound-argument}. We detail the maximal case; the minimal
    case follows similarly.
    For the subgroup $H = A[p+1](\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$, we get that $|H|$ divides $(p+1)^{2g}$,
    and thus if $|H|$ is larger than $4gp^{2g-1}$, then $|A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})| = (p+1)^{2g}$ and $A$ is maximal,
    and therefore superspecial.
    So, for any non-maximal $A$, the subgroup $H$ is smaller than $O(4gp^{2g-1})$. The probability of sampling a point $P \in A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$ such that $[p+1]P = \mathbf{0}$ is equal to the probability of sampling a point in $H$. In the non-maximal case we therefore have that the probability is
    \[
        \frac{|H|}{|A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})|} \leq \frac{4gp^{2g - 1}}{(p - 1)^{2g}} = O(p^{-1}).
    \]

<!-- lemalg-3: body_md -->
\label{alg:superspecial-test}
    Let $\mathcal{J}$ be a Jacobian of dimension $g$ of a curve $C$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ with $q = p$ or $p^2$. Let $\operatorname{Aut}(C)$ be the geometric automorphism group of $C$ (i.e., automorphisms defined over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$).
    For each $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(C)$:
    \begin{itemize}
        \par Let $\mathcal{J}^\sigma$ be the Jacobian of the twist $C^\sigma$ of $C$ by $\sigma$ over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$.
        \par Sample $P \xleftarrow{\$} \mathcal{J}^\sigma(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$.
        \par If $[p]P = \pm P$, return \textbf{\upshape true}.
    \end{itemize}
    If no $\mathcal{J}^\sigma$ passes the $[p]P = \pm P$ test, return \textbf{\upshape false}.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:superspecial-test}
    Let $p > g + 1$. Then
    \Cref{alg:superspecial-test} runs in $O(g \log p)$ time and returns \textbf{\upshape true}
    for any superspecial Jacobian. For any non-superspecial Jacobian of dimension $g$, \Cref{alg:superspecial-test}
    returns \textbf{\upshape true} with probability at most $O((g-1) \cdot p^{-1})$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
By \Cref{thm:minmax-test}, the probability of passing the $[p]P = -P$ test
    for any non-maximal Jacobian
    is bounded by $O(p^{-1})$.
    By Hurwitz's automorphisms theorem, we get $|\operatorname{Aut}(C)| \leq 84(g - 1)$, as long as
    $p > g + 1$~\cite{roquette}\footnote{
    \textcite{roquette} shows this result for all curves except one, but the exceptional curve is itself a superspecial curve.}
    and we can bound the number of twists we have to visit by the size of $\operatorname{Aut}(C)$
    by \Cref{lem:twists}.
    Each test costs $O(\log p)$ group operations, giving a total cost of $O(g \log p)$. Furthermore, if the $[p]P = -P$
    test succeeds on any twist $\mathcal{J}^\sigma$,
    then $\mathcal{J}$ is superspecial and a union bound over the twists gives the stated probability $O((g-1) \cdot p^{-1})$.
    The analogous argument holds replacing maximal with minimal and $[p]P = -P$ with $[p]P = P$.

<!-- lemma-17: body_md -->
\label{lem:prob-p+1}
    Let $A$ be an abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Let $P$ be a random point on $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$.
    The probability that $[p+1]P = \mathbf{0}$ is
    \begin{itemize}
        \par 1, if $A$ is supersingular of type $b = -2$,
        \par $O(p^{-1/2})$, otherwise.
    \end{itemize}
<!-- end lemma-17 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Let $m = p+1$.
    The probability to sample a point $P$ with $[m]P = \mathbf{0}$ equals
    \[
        \mathcal{P} := \frac{|A(\mathbb{F}_p)[m]|}{|A(\mathbb{F}_p)|}.
    \]
    Let $h_0, \ldots, h_3 \in \ensuremath{\mathbb{N}}$ be such that $A(\mathbb{F}_p)[m] \cong \mathbb{Z}_{h_0} \times \mathbb{Z}_{h_1} \times \mathbb{Z}_{h_2} \times \mathbb{Z}_{h_3}$ and $h_{i+1} \mid h_i$.
    From \Cref{lem:weil-pairing},
    $h_3 \mid h_2 \mid 2$,  and we have $h_1 \mid h_0 \mid p+1$ and $h_1 \mid \lambda_0$, $h_1 \mid \lambda_1$.
    Since  $\lambda_0 = (p+1)(p-1+a_p) + 2-b_p$ and $ \lambda_1 = -a_p(p+1) -2a_p -2(2-b_p)$, this implies $h_1 \mid 2a_p$.

    Let $W = 8\sqrt{p}(p+1)$ denote the width of the
    Hasse--Weil interval over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
    If $h_0 h_1 \leq W$, then $|A(\mathbb{F}_p)[m]| = h_0h_1h_2h_3 \leq 4W$, and therefore
    \[
    \mathcal{P} \leq \frac{4W}{(\sqrt{p}-1)^4} = \frac{32\sqrt{p}(p+1)}{(\sqrt{p}-1)^4} = O\big(p^{-1/2}\big).
    \]
    Suppose instead that $h_0 h_1 > W$.
    If $a_p \neq 0$, then $h_1 \leq 2|a_p| \leq 8\sqrt{p}$ by~\Cref{eq:bound-a}, so that
    $h_0 h_1 \leq 8\sqrt{p}(p+1) = W$, a contradiction. Hence, $a_p = 0$.
    Now $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{h_0} \times \mathbb{Z}_{h_1}$,
    whose order exceeds $W$ and divides $(p+1)^2$. By \Cref{lem:width-bound-argument}, $|A(\mathbb{F}_p)| = (p+1)^2$ and so $b_p = -2p$. Therefore, $A$ is supersingular of type $b = -2$ and $\mathcal{P} = 1$.

<!-- lemma-18: body_md -->
\label{lem:prob-p-1}
    Let $A$ be an abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
    The probability that
    $[p-1]P = \mathbf{0}$ for a random $P \in A(\mathbb{F}_p)$
    and $[p-1]Q = \mathbf{0}$ for a random $Q \in A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)$,
    is
    \begin{itemize}
        \par 1, if $A$ is supersingular of type $b = 2$,
        \par $O(p^{-1/2})$, otherwise.
    \end{itemize}
<!-- end lemma-18 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Let $G = A[p-1](\mathbb{F}_p)$ and $H = A^t[p-1](\mathbb{F}_p)$ with twisting isomorphism $\rho: A^t \to A$.
    Then $S = \langle G, H^\rho \rangle$
    is a subgroup of $A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$ whose order divides $\#A[p-1] = (p-1)^4$.
    By the width-bound argument, if $|S| \geq 8 (p^3 + p)$, then $|A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})| = (p-1)^4$,
    which implies $A$ is minimal, and hence superspecial by \Cref{lem:poonen en co}.
    In particular, it is of type $b = 2$
    as this is the only $\mathbb{F}_p$-type to lift to a minimal abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$.
    Thus, in the non-supersingular case we have $|S| \leq 8 (p^3 + p)$.
    Since $G$ and $H$ share only the 2-torsion, of size at most 16, and $|S| = |G| \cdot |H|/|G \cap H|$, then
    $|G| \cdot |H| \leq 16 \cdot |S| \leq 2^7 \cdot p^3 + 2^7 \cdot p$, so at
    least one of $|G|$ or $|H|$ is smaller than $2^{7/2} \cdot p^{3/2} + \epsilon$.

\label{lem:prob-p2-p-1}
    Let $A$ be an abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and
    let $n = p^2 - bp + 1$ with $b \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$.
    The probability that
    $[n]P = \mathbf{0}$ for a random $P \in A(\mathbb{F}_p)$
    and $[n]Q = \mathbf{0}$ for a random $Q \in A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)$,

        \par $O(p^{-1/2})$, otherwise.

<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Let $H = A[n](\mathbb{F}_p)$ and $G = A^t[n](\mathbb{F}_p)$. These groups do not share points
    beyond the 2-torsion. We again look at $S = \langle H, G^\rho \rangle$,
    where $\rho$ is the twisting isomorphism.
    Let $\gcd(n, p^2 -1) = d \in \{1, 2, 3\}$. By \Cref{lem:weil-pairing},

    and $A^t$ with probability $O(p^{-1/2})$, similarly as in the proof of~\Cref{lem:prob-p-1}.

    Although cryptographically such torsion occurs with negligible probability,
    their exclusion ensures that \Cref{alg:supersingularity_test} never misclassifies a supersingular surface.\footnote{

  $G^t = A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)^\rho = A[\pi + 1]$.

  $e_n((\pi + 1)R, Q) = 1$ for all $R \in A[n]$ and $Q \in G$, and
  $e_n((\pi - 1)R, S) = 1$ for all $R \in A[n]$ and $S \in G^t$. Since $(\pi + 1)A[n] = G$ and
  $(\pi - 1)A[n] = G^t$, both $G$ and $G^t$ are isotropic.

    \textbf{(iii)} Since $G$ is isotropic of order $n^2$ in $A[n]$, we have that $G = G^{\perp}$, and similarly for $G^t$. Thus, a point $P \in G$ lies in the left kernel if and only if $P \in G^t$, i.e., $P \in G \cap G^t = A[2](\mathbb{F}_p)$. By symmetry, the right kernel is $A^t[2](\mathbb{F}_p)$.

    \textbf{(iv)} Write $H = \langle P_1, P_2 \rangle \subseteq G$ and
    $H^t = \langle Q_1^\rho, Q_2^\rho \rangle \subseteq G^t$.
    The image of $M$ is the image of the homomorphism
    $H \to \mathrm{Hom}(H^t, \mu_n)$, $P \mapsto e_n(P, \cdot)$.
    This is a composition of maps $H \to \mathrm{Hom}(G^t, \mu_n)$ with the restriction
    $\mathrm{Hom}(G^t, \mu_n) \to \mathrm{Hom}(H^t, \mu_n)$. The first map has kernel $H \cap A[2](\mathbb{F}_p) = H[2]$ by \textbf{(iii)}. The order of the kernel is therefore at most $4$ (since $H$ is generated by two elements), and the image has order at least $|H|/4$.
    The restriction map has kernel of order $[G^t : H^t] = n^2/|H^t|$. Hence,
    $|\mathrm{im}\, M| \geq |H| \cdot |H^t| / (4n^2)$.

<!-- remark-10: body_md -->
\label{rem:2-rank from f}
  Which of the three cases in \textbf{(i)} of \Cref{lem: perfect pairings on type b = 2} and
  \Cref{lem: trivial pairings on type b = -2} applies is governed by the $2$-rank of $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ alone:
  by the index formula of \Cref{lem:decomposition of Jn}, the three group structures listed there are
  precisely those of $2$-rank $2$, $3$ and $4$, giving index $4$, $8$ and $1$ respectively.
  When $A$ is the Jacobian of a genus-$2$ curve $C : y^2 = f(x)$, this rank is read off directly
  from the factorization type of $f$ by \Cref{lem: factoring fx gives 2 rank}, so the case distinction
  costs a single polynomial factorization rather than a group-structure computation.
  Moreover, \Cref{thm: possible group structures of rational jacobians} forces the $2$-rank to be at
  least $2$ when $b = \pm 2$, so a factorization type of $f$ of $2$-rank $0$ or $1$ already certifies
  that $\mathcal{J}$ is \emph{not} supersingular of type $b = \pm 2$.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:conclusive-testing-non-cyclic}

  Let $A$ be an abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Write
  $W = 8\sqrt{p}(p+1)$, the width of the Hasse--Weil interval over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
  \begin{itemize}
    \par Let $n = p-1$, and let $P_1, P_2 \in A[n](\mathbb{F}_p)$ and $Q_1, Q_2 \in A^t[n](\mathbb{F}_p)$. If $e_n(P_1, P_2)$ and $e_n(Q_1, Q_2)$ have orders $k, k'$ with $k^2, k'^2 > W$, then $A$ is supersingular of type $b = 2$.
    \par  Let $n = p + 1$, let $\zeta$ be a primitive $n$-th root of unity, and let $P_1, P_2 \in A[n](\mathbb{F}_p)$ and $Q_1, Q_2 \in A^t[n](\mathbb{F}_p)$. Let $\zeta_{i,j} = e_n(P_i, Q_j^\rho)$, where $\rho$ is the twisting isomorphism. If the matrix $M = (\log_\zeta \zeta_{i,j})_{i,j}$ over $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ satisfies $|\mathrm{im}\, M| > W$, then $A$ is supersingular of type $b = -2$.
  \end{itemize}
  Conversely, let $A$ be supersingular of type $b = \pm 2$ and let $n = p \mp 1$.
  For $p$ large enough, there exist $P_1, P_2 \in A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ and $Q_1, Q_2 \in A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)$
  satisfying the corresponding condition above.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
First let $n = p - 1$. If $e_n(P_1, P_2)$ has order $k$ then
$|\langle P_1, P_2 \rangle| \geq k^2 > W$. Moreover, $|\langle P_1, P_2 \rangle|$ divides $n^2$, so $|A(\mathbb{F}_p)| = (p-1)^2$ by~\Cref{lem:width-bound-argument}. The same argument applied to $Q_1, Q_2$ gives $|A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)| = (p-1)^2$. It follows that $A$ is supersingular of type $b = 2$.

Now let $n = p + 1$. We have that $|\mathrm{im}\, M| \leq |\langle P_1, P_2 \rangle|$ and, as the images of $M$ and $M^T$ have the same size, we have $|\mathrm{im}\, M| \leq |\langle Q_1^\rho, Q_2^\rho \rangle| = |\langle Q_1, Q_2 \rangle|$.
Both orders divide $n^2 = (p+1)^2$ and (by hypothesis) $|\mathrm{im}\, M| > W$, so~\Cref{lem:width-bound-argument} gives $|A(\mathbb{F}_p)| = |A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)| = (p+1)^2$.

For the converse, note that by \Cref{thm: possible group structures of rational jacobians}, each of the three possible group structures
of $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ contains a subgroup generated by two elements of order at least $n^2/4$,
and the same holds for $A^t(\mathbb{F}_p) \cong A(\mathbb{F}_p)$. Moreover,
$A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ contains a point of order at least $n/2$.
Let $A$ be of type $b = 2$, so $n = p - 1$, and let $Q \in A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ be of maximal order
$k_0 \in \{n, n/2\}$. By \Cref{lem: perfect pairings on type b = 2}\textbf{(ii)},
there is $P \in A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ with
$e_n(P, Q)$ of order $k = k_0/2 \geq n/4$, and similarly on $A^t$ of order $k' \geq n/4$.
Now $k^2 \geq \left(\tfrac{p-1}{4}\right)^2 > W$ for $p > 2^{18}$.
A similar argument holds for type $b = -2$.
If moreover
$A(\mathbb{F}_p) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^2$,
which by \Cref{rem:2-rank from f} can be read off from the 2-rank of $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$,
then any bases give $k = k' = n/2$ for $b = 2$, resp. $|\mathrm{im}\,M| \ge n^2/4$ for $b = -2$
by \Cref{lem: trivial pairings on type b = -2}\textbf{(iv)},
and the conditions hold for $p > 2^{10}$.

The converse statement ensures that such a test passes for supersingular surfaces.

\label{lem:b0}
  Let $A$ be an abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_p$ of type $b = 0$
  and let $m = p^2 + 1$ be the exponent of $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$.

  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \par The subgroup generated by the four eigenspaces
    $A[\pi - 1] \subseteq A(\mathbb{F}_p)$,
    $A[\pi + 1] \subseteq A^t(\mathbb{F}_p)^\rho$,
    $A[\pi - p],
    A[\pi + p] \subseteq A^t(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})^{\rho'}$
    generate a subgroup of index 8 of the
    full $m$-torsion $A[m] \subseteq A(\mathbb{F}_{p^4})$.
    \par Let $P \in A[\pi - 1]$ and $Q \in A[\pi + 1]$, then $e_m(P, Q)$ is trivial.
    \par For any generators $P, Q, R, S$ of the four eigenspaces (see above), the pairings $e_m(P,R)$ and $e_m(Q,S)$ have order exactly $m/2$.
  \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-22 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
\textbf{(i)} follows from~\Cref{thm: possible group structures of rational jacobians} and~\Cref{lem:decomposition of Jn}.
  Write $m=2m'$ with $m'$ odd (possible as $p^2\equiv1\bmod 8$). As in the proof of~\Cref{lem:decomposition of Jn}, $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is cyclic and every eigenspace meets $A[2]$ in
  $A[2](\mathbb{F}_p)=\langle T\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
  \textbf{(ii)} By~\Cref{lem:almost symplectic basis}, $e_m(P,Q)\in\mu_2$, and its value is
  $e_2([m']P,[m']Q)=e_2(T,T)=1$.
  \textbf{(iii)} The same computation gives $e_2([m']P,[m']R)=1$, so $e_m(P,R)$ has order
  dividing $m'$. For the converse, note that $[A[m]:H]=8$ is a power of $2$, so
  $H\supseteq A[m']$ and the four eigenspaces restrict to a basis of
  $A[m']\cong(\mathbb{Z}/m'\mathbb{Z})^4$. By~\Cref{lem:almost symplectic basis} the pairings
  $e_m(P,Q),e_m(P,S),e_m(Q,R),e_m(R,S)$ all have order dividing $2$, hence are trivial
  on $m'$-torsion. The Gram matrix of $e_{m'}$ on this basis therefore has only the
  entries $e_{m'}(P,R)$ and $e_{m'}(Q,S)$ off the diagonal, and non-degeneracy of
  $e_{m'}$ forces both to be primitive. Thus, $e_m(P,R)$ has order exactly $m'=m/2$, and likewise for $e_m(Q,S)$.

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor:conclusive-testing-cyclic}
  Let $A$ be an abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_p$, let $b \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$, let
  $n = p^2 - bp + 1$. Let
  $P \in A[n](\mathbb{F}_p)$, $Q \in A^t[n](\mathbb{F}_p)$ and
  $R, S \in A[n]$,\footnote{In practice, $R$ and $S$ are taken in the eigenspaces as in \Cref{lem:eigenspace fields}, so that they are defined (possibly on a twist) over $\mathbb{F}_{p^k}$, where $k$ is the embedding degree of $n$, and all pairing values lie in $\mu_n \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{p^k}^\times$.} and suppose that
  $\zeta_1 = e_n(P, R)$ and $\zeta_2 = e_n(Q^\rho, S)$ have orders $k_1$ and $k_2$ with
  \(
  k_1 k_2 > 8 \gcd(n,2)\cdot(p^3 + p).
  \)
  Then $A$ is supersingular of type $b$.

    Conversely, let $A$ be supersingular of type $b \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$ and $p > 2^7$, and let
  $P \in A[\pi - 1]$, $Q^\rho \in A[\pi + 1]$, $R \in A[\pi - p]$ and $S \in A[\pi + p]$ be generators
  of the four eigenspaces. Then these points satisfy the above condition.
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
We have
$k_1 \mid \mathrm{ord}(P)$ and $k_2 \mid \mathrm{ord}(Q)$, so the subgroup $H = \langle P, Q^\rho \rangle \subseteq A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$ satisfies
\[
  |H| = \frac{\mathrm{ord}(P)\,\mathrm{ord}(Q)}{|\langle P \rangle \cap \langle Q^\rho \rangle|}
      \geq \frac{k_1 k_2}{\gcd(2, n)} > 8(p^3 + p),
\]
which is the width of the Hasse--Weil interval over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$. Moreover, $|H|$ divides $n^2$, so~\Cref{lem:width-bound-argument} gives $|A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})| = n^2$. As $A$ is defined over
$\mathbb{F}_p$,~\Cref{thm:supersingular-orders} shows that $A$ is supersingular of type $b$.

For the converse, $A(\mathbb{F}_p)$ has exponent $n$. If $b = 0$, then $k_1 = k_2 = n/2$ by~\Cref{lem:b0}, and the condition
$n^2/4 > 16(p^3 + p)$ holds for $p \geq 67$. If
$b = \pm 1$, then $n$ is odd and $k_1, k_2 \geq n/3$ by~\Cref{lem:b1} and~\Cref{lem:bm1},
and $n^2/9 > 8(p^3 + p)$ holds for $p \geq 79$.
<!-- end proof-23 -->
<!-- remark-12: body_md -->
In \Cref{cor:conclusive-testing-cyclic}, the points $R$ and $S$ only serve as witnesses: a pairing $e_n(P, R)$ of order $k_1$ certifies that $P$ has order at least $k_1$, and similarly for $Q$. Supersingularity then follows from the width bound on $\langle P, Q^\rho\rangle$. Such witnesses cannot be found in $A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$, as pairings between points of $A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$ take values in $\mu_n \cap \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^\times = \mu_{\gcd(n, p^2 - 1)}$, which has order at most 3. This is why we pass to $\mathbb{F}_{p^k}$, where non-degeneracy of $e_n$ guarantees that suitable witnesses exist.

<!-- lemalg-4: body_md -->
\label{alg:conclusive-extremal-surfaces}
  Let $A$ be an abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$, and $g \in \mu_{p+1} \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^*$
  a fixed generator of $\mu_{p+1}$. Repeat the following steps until termination.
  \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0mm]
    \par {\upshape Sample 4 random points $P_1, \ldots, P_4 \xleftarrow{\$} A(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})$}
    \par {\upshape If $[p+1]P_i \neq \mathbf{0}_A$ for any $i$, return \textbf{false}}
    \par {\upshape Compute $\zeta_{i,j} = e_{p+1}(P_i, P_j)$ and set $Z = (\log_g(\zeta_{i,j}))$ as a matrix over $\mathbb{Z}$}
    \par {\upshape Compute the invariant factors $d_i$ of the Smith normal form of $Z$}
    \par {\upshape Set $N_Z= \prod_{i=1}^{4} \frac{p+1}{\gcd(d_i, p+1)}$. If $N_Z > 8p^3 + 8p$, return \textbf{true}. Otherwise, repeat.}
  \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemalg-4 -->
<!-- lemma-25: body_md -->
\Cref{alg:conclusive-extremal-surfaces} returns \textbf{\upshape true} only for maximal supersingular
  abelian surfaces over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$, and \textbf{\upshape false} otherwise.
<!-- end lemma-25 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
If $[p+1]P_i \neq \mathbf{0}_A$ for any $i$, then $A$ is clearly non-maximal.
  Then, when the four points $P_i$ span a subgroup $H$
  with $|H| > 8p^3 + 8p$ by the width-bound argument on the Hasse--Weil interval, we
  have that $A$ must have order $(p+1)^4$, i.e., maximal. We can measure the size of $H$
  using the image of $Z$ as a linear map.
  This can be done effectively\footnote{
    See https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/3252961/image-of-a-linear-transformation-with-a-matrix-in-mathbb-z-m.
  }
  using the invariants $d_1, \ldots, d_4$ of the Smith normal form of $Z$ as a matrix over $\mathbb{Z}$:
  the image of $Z$ is then of size $\prod \frac{p+1}{\gcd(d_i, p+1)}$.
<!-- end proof-24 -->
<!-- remark-13: body_md -->
Given a prime $p$, it is theoretically possible to derive the expected number of iterations
  required in \Cref{alg:conclusive-extremal-surfaces}.
  Experimental data for primes of the form $p = 2^{k} \cdot 5 - 1$
  shows that we succeed on the first try with probability over $99 \%$ already from
  $k = 18$. Thus, in practical applications, \Cref{alg:conclusive-extremal-surfaces}

<!-- remark-14: body_md -->
We have written \Cref{alg:conclusive-extremal-surfaces} with simplicity in mind,
  however, theoretically, as \Cref{alg:conclusive-extremal-surfaces} samples random points, it may never terminate
  as the algorithm could sample low-order points over and over with
  exponentially small probability.
  This may be resolved by deterministically sampling points that span an increasingly larger
  subgroup consistently.
  For example, if $A$ is a Jacobian $\mathcal{J}$, the deterministic sampling of divisors reduces to deterministically
  sampling points $(x_0, y_0)$ on the associated hyperelliptic curve $C$, which can be done by sampling
  $x_0 = \omega^n$ for a generator $\omega$ of $k^*$ and increasing $n$.
  However, in practice, such an algorithm is tedious to work with,
  and does not significantly speed-up validation in practical situations.
