<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:intro-tail}
Let $p\ge7$ be prime.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] Every multiple of $p^{p+3+2r_p}$ is an integer group
determinant of $C_p\times C_p$.
\item[\textup{(b)}] Suppose that $p\nmid h_p^+$. If $m$ is norm-free
and $N$ is an integer with $p+3\le N\le2p-2$ and $N<p+3+2r_p$, then
$\pm p^Nm\notin S(C_p\times C_p)$.
\item[\textup{(c)}] Consequently, if $p\nmid h_p^+$ and $r_p\le(p-5)/2$,
then $e(p)=p+3+2r_p$. Both hypotheses hold if $p<2^{31}$. The second
holds whenever $p$ is regular or has fewer than $(p-7)/4$ irregular
pairs.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:intro-p5}
Let $p\ge7$ be a prime with $p\nmid h_p^+$. Then $p^{p+5}\mathbf Z\subseteq
S(C_p\times C_p)$ if and only if every irregular pair $(p,2i)$
satisfies $3i>p-1$. The smallest prime for which this fails is $p=103$,
whose only irregular pair is $(103,24)$. For this prime
$r_{103}=5$ and $e(103)=116$; thus every multiple of $103^{116}$ occurs,
whereas $\pm103^N\notin S(C_{103}\times C_{103})$ for
$106\le N\le115$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:intro-least}
Let $p\ge11$ be prime. Every nonzero $p$-divisible integer group
determinant $\Theta$ of $C_p\times C_p$ satisfies $|\Theta|\ge p^{p+5}$.
Equality occurs if every irregular pair $(p,2i)$ satisfies $3i>p-1$, in
particular if $p$ is regular. If $p\nmid h_p^+$, then this condition is
also necessary for equality.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For every regular prime $p\ge7$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-whole-set}
 S(C_p\times C_p)=\mathcal U_p
 \cup\{\pm p^{p+3}m:m\in\mathcal C_{2,p}\}
 \cup\{\pm p^{p+4}m:m\in\mathcal C_{23,p}\}
 \cup p^{p+5}\mathbf Z.
\end{equation}
Membership in $\mathcal C_{2,p}$ and $\mathcal C_{23,p}$ is decided by a
finite computation, and every accepted allocation yields explicit integer
group coefficients realizing the corresponding determinant.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:intro-primes}
Let $p\ge11$ be a regular prime and let $\ell\ne p$ be a rational
prime. Then $\pm p^{p+3}\ell\notin S(C_p\times C_p)$. Moreover,
$\pm p^{p+4}\ell\in S(C_p\times C_p)$ if and only if
$\ell\equiv1\pmod p$ and one (equivalently, every) prime ideal
$\mathfrak q$ above $\ell$ is principal with
\[
 d_3(\mathfrak q)=d_5(\mathfrak q)=\cdots=d_{p-6}(\mathfrak q)=0
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (d_{p-4}(\mathfrak q),d_{p-2}(\mathfrak q))\ne(0,0).
\]
These primes have natural density $(p+1)/(hp^{(p-3)/2})$ among all
rational primes.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:profiles}
Let $v\in\mathbf Z^G$ with $\Theta(v)\ne0$ and $p\mid\Theta(v)$. Then $p\mid s$
and every $f_\delta$ lies in $\mathfrak p$. If $L=0$, then
$v_p(\Theta(v))\ge2p+3$. If $L\ne0$, then after a linear change of
coordinates $v_\mathfrak p(f_b)=1$ for every $b\in\mathbf F_p$, the exceptional valuation
$e=v_\mathfrak p(f_\infty)$ is at least two, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile-valuation}
 v_p(\Theta(v))=v_p(s)+p+e.
\end{equation}
In particular, a determinant of exact valuation $p+3$ has profile
$(v_p(s);e)=(1;2)$, and a determinant of exact valuation $p+4$ has
profile $(2;2)$ or $(1;3)$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Every character value is congruent to $s$ modulo $\mathfrak p$. If $p\nmid s$,
then all character values and their norms are prime to $p$, and so is
$\Theta(v)$, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence $p\mid s$, and every
$f_\delta$ lies in $\mathfrak p$. Since
$\zeta^k\equiv1-k\varpi\pmod{\mathfrak p^2}$ and $s\in\mathfrak p^{p-1}\subseteq\mathfrak p^2$, the
value of the character $(i,j)\mapsto\zeta^{\alpha i+\beta j}$ is
congruent to $-\varpi L(\alpha,\beta)$ modulo $\mathfrak p^2$. If $L=0$, all

while $v_\mathfrak p(f_\infty)\ge2$, and~\eqref{eq:valuation}

because $v_p(s)\ge1$ and $e\ge2$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:reconstruct}
Let $c_\delta\colon\mathbf F_p\to\mathbf Z$, $\delta\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)$, be integer vectors with
common sum $s$. They are the ray vectors of some $v\in\mathbf Z^G$ if and only
if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reconstruction-congruence}
 c_\infty(j)+\sum_{b\in\mathbf F_p}c_b(i+bj)\equiv s\pmod p
 \qquad\text{for every }(i,j)\in\mathbf F_p^2.
\end{equation}
In that case $v$ is unique, its augmentation is $s$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integer-recovery}
 v_{ij}=\frac{c_\infty(j)+\sum_{b\in\mathbf F_p}c_b(i+bj)-s}{p}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For the rays of a given $v$, the numerator of~\eqref{eq:integer-recovery}
before subtracting $s$ counts the cell $(i,j)$ once on each of the
$p+1$ lines through it and every other cell exactly once. It therefore
equals $pv_{ij}+s$, which proves necessity and uniqueness. Conversely,
the congruences make~\eqref{eq:integer-recovery} integral. When we sum this
formula over the line in direction $\delta$ indexed by $t$, the term
$c_\delta$ contributes $pc_\delta(t)$, each of the other $p$ directions
contributes $s$, and the $p$ subtractions of $s$ cancel these
contributions; hence the reconstructed vector has the prescribed rays.
Summing over all $p^2$ cells gives the augmentation
$((p+1)ps-p^2s)/p=s$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:transform}
Let $1\le e<p$ and $z\in R$, and let $H$ be the transform of $\varpi^ez$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transform}
 \sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p}\overline{\varrho_e(z)}(t)\exp(tT)=T^{p-1}+H(T).
\end{equation}
If $\overline{\varrho_e(z)}(t)=\sum_{d=0}^{p-1}\gamma_dt^d$ as a polynomial
function on $\mathbf F_p$ and $h_n=[T^n]H$ denotes the coefficient of $T^n$ in
$H$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
 \gamma_{p-1}=0,\qquad
 \gamma_{p-1-n}=-n!\,h_n\ (1\le n\le p-2),\qquad
 \gamma_0=1+h_{p-1}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Represent $\varpi^ez$ modulo $p\mathfrak p$ by a polynomial in $\varpi$ without
constant term. Expanding this polynomial in powers of $\zeta$ gives a
vector $(a_t)_{t\in\mathbf F_p}$ of sum zero with $\sum_ta_t\exp(tT)=H(T)$. Adding the all-ones vector makes the sum equal to $p$ and
adds $\sum_t\exp(tT)=T^{p-1}$, because the power sums $\sum_tt^n$ vanish
in $\mathbf F_p$ for $0\le n\le p-2$ and $(p-1)!=-1$ in $\mathbf F_p$. This
proves~\eqref{eq:transform}. Comparing constant coefficients gives
$\gamma_{p-1}=0$. For $1\le n\le p-2$, the coefficient of $T^n$ on the
left of~\eqref{eq:transform} is $\sum_{t,d}\gamma_dt^{d+n}/n!$; it
involves only the nonvanishing power sum with $d+n=p-1$ and equals
$-\gamma_{p-1-n}/n!$; at $n=p-1$ the
remaining term is $-\gamma_0$ divided by $(p-1)!=-1$. These are the
formulas~\eqref{eq:gamma}.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:moments}
Suppose that $p\mid s$, and put $\tau=s/p\bmod p$. Integer ray vectors
$c_\delta$ of common sum $s$ with evaluations $f_\delta\in\mathfrak p$ satisfy the
reconstruction congruences~\eqref{eq:reconstruction-congruence} if and
only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degree}
 \deg h_n\le n\quad\text{and}\quad h_{\infty,n}=[b^n]h_n
 \qquad(1\le n\le p-2),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant}
 \sum_{b\in\mathbf F_p}h_{p-1}(b)+h_{\infty,p-1}=-\tau.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
First let all rays have sum $p$, and use~\eqref{eq:gamma}. Regard
the reconstruction sum in~\eqref{eq:reconstruction-congruence} as a
polynomial function of $(X,Y)=(i,j)$. For $1\le d\le p-2$, its
homogeneous part of degree $d$ is
\[
 \sum_{b\in\mathbf F_p}\gamma_d(b)(X+bY)^d+\gamma_{\infty,d}Y^d.
\]
Its coefficients vanish exactly when $\sum_bb^k\gamma_d(b)=0$ for
$0\le k<d$ and $\gamma_{\infty,d}=-\sum_bb^d\gamma_d(b)$, because the
binomial coefficients involved are nonzero modulo $p$. Taking power sums
for $k=0,1,\dots,d-1$ successively shows that the first conditions say
that $\gamma_d(b)$ has degree at most $p-1-d$, and the last condition
identifies $\gamma_{\infty,d}$ with the coefficient of $b^{p-1-d}$. With
$n=p-1-d$ and after cancelling the common factor $-n!$, these
are the conditions~\eqref{eq:degree}. There is no ray term of degree
$p-1$, since $\gamma_{p-1}=0$, and polynomial functions on $\mathbf F_p^2$ of degree at most $p-1$ in each
variable are determined by their values, so this accounts for every
nonconstant term.

Changing the sum of every ray from $p$ to $s$ adds $s/p-1$ to each
entry. At sum $p$ the $p+1$ constant terms contribute
$1+\sum_bh_{p-1}(b)+h_{\infty,p-1}$ modulo $p$, and the adjustments
contribute $(p+1)(s/p-1)\equiv\tau-1$. Since $s\equiv0\pmod p$, the
remaining congruence is~\eqref{eq:constant}.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:units}
Let $p\ge5$ be prime.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] Every $u\in R^\times$ satisfies $c_j(u)=0$ for each
odd $j$ with $3\le j\le p-2$.
\item[\textup{(ii)}] For $1\le a\le p-1$, the circular unit
$u_a=(1-\zeta^a)/(1-\zeta)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-expansion}
 \log\frac{u_a(T)}a=\frac{a-1}{2}\,T+
 \sum_{i=1}^{M}\kappa_i(a^{2i}-1)T^{2i}.
\end{equation}
\item[\textup{(iii)}] The image in $(R/pR)^\times$ of the group $C$
generated by $\zeta$ and the $u_a$ is
\[
 \widehat C=\mathbf F_p^\times\cdot\exp\Bigl(\mathbf F_p T\oplus
 \bigoplus_{i\notin\mathcal I_p}\mathbf F_p T^{2i}\Bigr).
\]
\item[\textup{(iv)}] $\mathcal I_p^{E}\subseteq\mathcal I_p$ and
\[
 \widehat E=\mathbf F_p^\times\cdot\exp\Bigl(\mathbf F_p T\oplus
 \bigoplus_{i\notin\mathcal I_p^{E}}\mathbf F_p T^{2i}\Bigr).
\]
\item[\textup{(v)}] If $p\nmid h_p^+$, then $\widehat E=\widehat C$ and
$\mathcal I_p^{E}=\mathcal I_p$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, if $p$ is regular, then
$\widehat E=\mathbf F_p^\times\cdot\exp(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbf F_p}\{T,T^2,T^4,\ldots,T^{p-3}\})$; two
residues prime to $\mathfrak p$ then differ by the residue of a global unit if and
only if their coordinates $c_3,c_5,\ldots,c_{p-2}$ agree.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
(i) Every unit of $R$ is a power of $\zeta$ times a real unit; see
\citet[Lemma~2]{ConradKummer}. Complex conjugation replaces $T$ by $-T$,
so the normalized logarithm of a real unit is even, and $\zeta$
contributes only the linear term $T$.

(ii) We have $u_a(T)=(\exp(aT)-1)/(\exp T-1)=aE(aT)/E(T)$, so the
expansion~\eqref{eq:unit-expansion} follows from the
series~\eqref{eq:uniformizer} applied to $E(aT)$ and $E(T)$.

(iii) Since $u_a\equiv a\pmod\mathfrak p$ and $1+T\mathbf F_p[T]/(T^{p-1})$ has exponent
$p$, the residue of $u_a^p$ is the constant $a$; hence
$\mathbf F_p^\times\subseteq\widehat C$. By~(ii), the logarithms of the residues
of $\zeta$ and $u_a$ span $\mathbf F_p T$ together with the vectors
$\sum_i\kappa_i(a^{2i}-1)T^{2i}$, so the logarithmic part of
$\widehat C$ is contained in the displayed space, the coordinates with
$i\in\mathcal I_p$ being zero. For the reverse inclusion consider the square
matrix $(a^{2i}-1)_{1\le i\le M,\ 2\le a\le M+1}$. It is invertible over
$\mathbf F_p$: a vanishing linear combination $\sum_i\alpha_i(a^{2i}-1)$ of its
rows gives a polynomial $\sum_{i=1}^{M}\alpha_i(X^i-1)$ of degree at
most $M$ with the $M+1$ roots $1,2^2,\ldots,(M+1)^2$, which are distinct because
$M+1=(p-1)/2$; hence every $\alpha_i$ is zero. Deleting the rows with $i\in\mathcal I_p$ and multiplying
each remaining row by $\kappa_i\ne0$ preserves the independence of the
rows. Hence the units $u_2,\ldots,u_{M+1}$ realize every vector of
coordinates $(c_{2i})_{i\notin\mathcal I_p}$, and a power of $\zeta$ adjusts
the linear coordinate.

(iv) By~(i), the logarithmic part of $\widehat E$ is contained in
$\mathbf F_p T\oplus\bigoplus_i\mathbf F_p T^{2i}$. It is a subgroup of an $\mathbf F_p$-vector
space, hence a subspace, and it is stable under the automorphisms
$\sigma_t$. Since the characters $t\mapsto t^j$ for $1\le j\le p-2$ are
distinct, the projection
$\pi_j=(p-1)^{-1}\sum_tt^{-j}\sigma_t$ maps $\sum_kx_kT^k$ to $x_jT^j$
and preserves this subspace. Hence the subspace is the direct sum of the
lines $\mathbf F_p T^j$ for which some element has a nonzero coordinate $x_j$.
It contains $T$, and by the definition of $\mathcal I_p^{E}$ some unit has a
nonzero coordinate $c_{2i}$ exactly when $i\notin\mathcal I_p^{E}$. Together with $\mathbf F_p^\times\subseteq\widehat E$ this gives the
displayed description. By~(iii), every $i\notin\mathcal I_p$ lies outside
$\mathcal I_p^{E}$.

(v) Let $C^+$ be the group generated by $-1$ and the real circular units
$\zeta^{(1-a)/2}u_a$ with $1<a<p/2$, where $(1-a)/2$ is read modulo $p$;
these generate the circular units of
$\mathbf Q(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})$~\cite[Lemma~8.1]{Washington1997}.
The index $[E^+:C^+]$ of $C^+$ in the unit group $E^+$ of
$\mathbf Q(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})$ equals $h_p^+$~\cite[Theorem~8.2]{Washington1997}.
As in the proof of~(i), every unit of $R$ is a power of $\zeta$ times a
real unit, so $R^\times=\langle\zeta\rangle E^+$;
since $C$ contains $\langle\zeta\rangle C^+$ (note $-1=\zeta u_{p-1}$),
every $u\in R^\times$ satisfies $u^{h_p^+}\in C$. The $p$-group
$1+T\mathbf F_p[T]/(T^{p-1})$ has exponent $p$, and $p\nmid h_p^+$, so raising to
the power $h_p^+$ is bijective on it. Hence the image of $u$ in this
$p$-group lies in the image of $C$, and since $\widehat C$ also contains
the constants $\mathbf F_p^\times$, we obtain $\widehat E=\widehat C$. Comparing~(iii) and~(iv) gives
$\mathcal I_p^{E}=\mathcal I_p$.

For a regular prime, $\mathcal I_p=\varnothing$, so (iv) gives the last
assertion.

\label{lem:ideals}
Suppose that $p$ is regular and let $h=|\operatorname{Cl}(R)|$. For an integral ideal
$I$ prime to $\mathfrak p$, choose a generator $y$ of $I^h$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ideal-defects}
 d_j(I)=h^{-1}c_j(y)\in\mathbf F_p\qquad(j=3,5,\ldots,p-2).
\end{equation}
We call the $d_j(I)$ the \emph{defects} of $I$. They are well defined,
additive and Galois equivariant, and they equal $c_j(z)$ when $I=zR$. For a prime ideal $\mathfrak q$ above a
rational prime $\ell\ne p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frobenius}
 (\ell^j-1)\,d_j(\mathfrak q)=0\qquad(j=3,5,\ldots,p-2).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The class number $h$ annihilates the class group, so $I^h$ is principal,
and every generator of $I^h$ is prime to $\mathfrak p$. Regularity makes $h$ invertible
modulo $p$. Two generators differ by a unit, whose odd coordinates
vanish by Lemma~\ref{lem:units}(i). Multiplying generators of $I^h$ and
$J^h$ proves additivity, and applying $\sigma_t$ to a generator proves
equivariance. If $I=zR$, we may choose $y=z^h$. Finally, the Frobenius
automorphism $\sigma_\ell$ fixes $\mathfrak q$, so equivariance gives
$\ell^jd_j(\mathfrak q)=d_j(\mathfrak q)$.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:parity}
Suppose that $e\ge3$ and $q=0$. Then $h_n(b)=0$ for every $b\in\mathbf F_p$ and
every even $n\le p-1$, and $h_{\infty,n}=0$ for every even $n\le p-2$.

<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The linear coefficient of $\log G_b$ is $q(b)=0$, and its odd
coefficients of degree $3$ to $p-2$ vanish by~\eqref{eq:generic-log}.
Thus $\log G_b$ is even modulo $T^{p-1}$, and so is $G_b$. Hence
$h_n(b)=\lambda[T^{n-1}]G_b=0$ for even $n\le p-1$. The moment
conditions then give $h_{\infty,n}=[b^n]h_n=0$ for even $n\le p-2$. If
$e\le p-2$, the transform $H_\infty=(1-\exp T)^ez_\infty$ has $T$-adic
order exactly $e$, so $h_{\infty,e}\ne0$ and $e$ is odd.

Now let $e\le p-4$, so that $e$ is odd. Consider the relative series
$J=H_\infty/(h_{\infty,e}T^e)$ modulo $T^{p-e}$. Its coefficient of
$T^k$ is $h_{\infty,e+k}/h_{\infty,e}$. For odd $k\le p-2-e$, the index
$e+k$ is even and at most $p-2$, so this coefficient vanishes. Hence
$J=J_0+\mu T^{p-1-e}$, where $J_0$ is even and
$\mu=h_{\infty,p-1}/h_{\infty,e}$; here $p-1-e$ is odd because $e$ is odd. Since
$p-1-e$ is the highest retained degree, the coefficient of
$T^{p-1-e}$ in $\log J$ equals $\mu$. On the other hand, this
coefficient is an odd coordinate of degree $p-1-e\ge3$, so it vanishes
by~\eqref{eq:relative-log} and Lemma~\ref{lem:units}(i). Therefore
$h_{\infty,p-1}=0$. Also $h_{p-1}(b)=\lambda[T^{p-2}]G_b=0$ because
$G_b$ is even. The constant moment condition~\eqref{eq:constant} now gives
$s/p\equiv0\pmod p$.

\label{lem:obstruction}
Suppose that $e=2r+1\le p-2$ and $q=0$. Then every $i\in\mathcal I_p^{E}$ with

\[
 \Lambda_i(b)=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}

$\Lambda_i$ is not identically zero on $\mathbf F_p$. This contradiction shows

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:construction}
Let $r\ge1$ be admissible for $\mathcal I_p^{E}$, with $2r+1\le p-2$. Then
$p^{p+3+2r}\mathbf Z\subseteq S(C_p\times C_p)$. If $r$ is admissible for

$\nu\in U$; in particular $1\notin U$, because $2r+1\le p-2$.
Every $\nu\in U$ satisfies $\nu\le M<p$. Let
\[
 P(X)=1+X+\sum_{\nu\in U}c_\nu X^\nu\in\mathbf F_p[X],
\]
where the coefficients $c_\nu$ are chosen recursively, in increasing
order of $\nu$, so that $[X^\nu]\log P=0$ for every $\nu\in U$. This is possible because $[X^\nu]\log P$ equals $c_\nu$ plus
an expression in the coefficients of lower degree, and the denominators
in the logarithm are smaller than $p$.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-data}
 H_b(T)=T\,P(b^{2r+1}T^{2r})\pmod{T^p}\quad(b\in\mathbf F_p),\qquad
 H_\infty(T)=T^{2r+1},\qquad s=p^2n.

least three, and its coefficient of $T^{2i}$ is $-(2r+1)\kappa_i=0$ for
$i\in\mathcal I$. Hence there is a unit $z_\infty$ with residue
$w_\infty$, again circular when $\mathcal I=\mathcal I_p$. By the
definition~\eqref{eq:H}, the values $f_b=\varpi z_b$ and
$f_\infty=\varpi^{2r+1}z_\infty$ have the transforms~\eqref{eq:tail-data}.

with evaluations $f_\delta$. By

factorization~\eqref{eq:factor} gives

\label{thm:tail}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:operator}
Let $2\le L\le p-1$, let $q\in\mathbf F_p$, and prescribe odd coordinates $d_j$
for $3\le j<L$. Set
\[
 K_{q,d}(T)=\exp\Bigl(qT+\sum_{\substack{3\le j<L\\ j\text{ odd}}}d_jT^j\Bigr)
 \pmod{T^L}.
\]
For given \emph{raw even coefficients} $x_{2i}$ with $2i<L$, define
$E_0=1$ and, recursively,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-recurrence}
 E_{2i}=x_{2i}-\sum_{0\le j<i}E_{2j}\,[T^{2i-2j}]K_{q,d}.
\end{equation}
Then put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-operator}
 \mathcal A_L(q,d;x)=K_{q,d}(T)\sum_{0\le2i<L}E_{2i}T^{2i}\pmod{T^L}.
\end{equation}
The same formulas define the operator over $\mathcal B$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:affine-triangular}
The series $\mathcal A_L(q,d;x)$ is the unique series modulo $T^L$ with
constant coefficient one, first logarithmic coordinate $q$, odd
logarithmic coordinates $d_j$ for $3\le j<L$, and raw even coefficients
$x_{2i}$. For fixed
$q$ and $d$, each of its coefficients is an affine function of the
$x_{2i}$: the coefficient of degree $2i$ is $x_{2i}$, and the
coefficient of degree $2i+1$ depends only on $x_2,\ldots,x_{2i}$.
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
All factorials and logarithmic denominators that occur are smaller than
$p$, so the truncated exponential and logarithm are inverse bijections
between $T\mathbf F_p[T]/(T^L)$ and $1+T\mathbf F_p[T]/(T^L)$, and similarly over
$\mathcal B$. A series with the prescribed odd logarithmic coordinates,
divided by $K_{q,d}$, has an even logarithm and is therefore even.
Conversely, every even series with constant coefficient one has an even
logarithm. Hence the series with the prescribed logarithmic data are
exactly the products $K_{q,d}E$ with $E$ even and $E_0=1$. Matching the
raw coefficient of degree $2i$ determines $E_{2i}$ and
gives~\eqref{eq:affine-recurrence}, so by induction $E_{2i}$ is affine in
$x_2,\ldots,x_{2i}$. The coefficient of degree $2i+1$ of the product
uses only $E_0,\ldots,E_{2i}$. This proves uniqueness and the triangular
dependence. The argument is valid over $\mathcal B$; it says nothing
about degrees in $b$, and the degree conditions are imposed explicitly
below.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:placed}
Let $p\ge7$ be regular, let $e\in\{2,3\}$, and let the principal ideals
and the nonzero augmentation $s$ be as fixed above. There exist integer
group coefficients with augmentation $s$ and character values
\[
 f_b=\varpi y_bu_b\quad(b\in\mathbf F_p),\qquad
 f_\infty=\varpi^ey_\infty u_\infty,
\]
where all $u_b,u_\infty$ are global units and all generic transforms have
leading coefficient one, if and only if $\mathcal L_2(D,d_\infty)$ accepts when
$e=2$, or $\mathcal L_3(D,d_\infty,s/p\bmod p)$ accepts when $e=3$. An
accepted system yields the integer coefficients constructively, and
their determinant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-determinant}
 s\,p^{p+e}\prod_{b\in\mathbf F_p}\operatorname{N}(I_b)\,\operatorname{N}(I_\infty).
\end{equation}

that an accepted branch yields compatible transforms. Finally we lift
the transforms to character values and integer coefficients.

Suppose first that the integer coefficients exist. The translation
leading to~\eqref{eq:affine-q-normal} preserves the prescribed data and
the augmentation. The local law~\eqref{eq:local-law} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:affine-triangular} put the generic transforms in the
form~\eqref{eq:affine-generic}, and the moment bounds for the odd
coefficients give exactly the polynomial spaces~\eqref{eq:affine-unknowns}.
The other generic degree bounds give~\eqref{eq:affine-generic-equations},
and the exceptional coefficients of degree at most $p-2$ must equal the
corresponding $t_n$.

Let $e=2$. The exceptional leading coefficient is $t_2=a\ne0$.

give~\eqref{eq:affine-cubic}, so $a$ belongs to one of the branches and
$t_3=aB$. The series $H_\infty/(aT^2)$ has first logarithmic coordinate
$B$, odd logarithmic coordinates $d_{\infty,j}$ for $3\le j\le p-4$,
and raw even coefficients $t_{2i+2}/a$ for $1\le i\le M-1$. By the
uniqueness in Lemma~\ref{lem:affine-triangular}, its truncation modulo
$T^{p-3}$ is the series~\eqref{eq:affine-r2-Q}, and matching its
remaining odd coefficients gives~\eqref{eq:affine-r2-special}. Hence
the branch accepts.

Conversely, a solution of a branch for $e=2$ gives all generic
transforms and, through degree $p-2$, the exceptional series
$aT^2Q_\infty$. Its coefficients agree with the required
values $t_n$: in degrees two and three by~\eqref{eq:affine-r2-leading},
in higher even degrees by the raw inputs of~\eqref{eq:affine-r2-Q}, and
in higher odd degrees by~\eqref{eq:affine-r2-special}; in degree one
both vanish. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-free-constant}
 h_{\infty,p-1}=-\tau-\sum_{b\in\mathbf F_p}h_{p-1}(b)
\end{equation}
and add $h_{\infty,p-1}T^{p-1}$ to $aT^2Q_\infty$. In the relative series

local law still holds. All nonconstant moment conditions and the
constant moment condition now hold, for every value of $\tau$.

nonprincipal prime ideals nor needs a trivial class group.

\emph{principal} if every $I_\delta$ is principal. Its
defect data are those of~\eqref{eq:ideal-defects}; we abbreviate the
whole collection $(D(b)_{b\in\mathbf F_p},d_\infty)$ by $D$, so that $\mathcal L_2(D)$
and $\mathcal L_3(D,\tau)$ denote the tests of

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:scalar}
Let $p\ge7$ be regular, let $h=|\operatorname{Cl}(R)|$, and let $m>0$ be prime to
$p$. Then $p^{p+3}m\in S(G)$ if and only if some principal allocation
for $m$ passes $\mathcal L_2(D)$. Also, $p^{p+4}m\in S(G)$ if and only if some
principal allocation passes $\mathcal L_2(D)$ or $\mathcal L_3(D,m\bmod p)$. The
same criteria hold for $-p^{p+3}m$ and $-p^{p+4}m$, and every accepted
allocation yields explicit integer group coefficients. Moreover, put
\[
 M'=\frac{(p+1)(p-3)}{2}-1.
\]
In both criteria it suffices to consider allocations that place at most
$h^{p+1}p^{M'}-1$ prime ideals above each rational prime $\ell$,
independently of $v_\ell(m)$; when $h=1$, at most $(p-1)M'$ prime ideals
above each $\ell$ suffice.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Suppose that $p^{p+3}m$ or $p^{p+4}m$ occurs. Lemma~\ref{lem:profiles}
puts the character values into a critical profile, and removing their
powers of $\mathfrak p$ leaves principal cofactor ideals. By~\eqref{eq:factor},
the norms of these ideals and the part of $s$ prime to $p$ account
for $m$. A prime ideal above $\ell$ uses $\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)$ factors of $\ell$,
so the cofactor ideals form a principal allocation. The changes of
coordinates used in Section~\ref{sec:systems} permute the rays and
conjugate the ideals, and the set of allocations is stable under both
operations. By Theorem~\ref{thm:placed}, the allocation therefore passes
$\mathcal L_2$ at valuation $p+3$, and $\mathcal L_2$ or $\mathcal L_3$ at valuation $p+4$.

For the converse, and to identify the augmentation residue, let
$Q_0=\prod_b\operatorname{N}(I_b)\,\operatorname{N}(I_\infty)$ for a principal allocation. Then
$Q_0\mid m$, and $Q_0\equiv1\pmod p$ because
$\ell^{\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)}\equiv1\pmod p$ for every allocated factor. If $\mathcal L_2$
accepts, take $s=pm/Q_0$ at valuation $p+3$ or $s=p^2m/Q_0$ at valuation
$p+4$; this test does not depend on $\tau$. If $\mathcal L_3$ accepts, take
$s=pm/Q_0$, so that $s/p\equiv m\pmod p$. Choosing generators of the
principal ideals $I_\delta$ and applying Theorem~\ref{thm:placed} gives the
desired determinant. Negating all coefficients negates the
determinant, which proves the assertions about signs.

For the bound, record for each ray ideal its ideal class, its
$(p-3)/2$ odd defects if the ray is generic, and its $(p-5)/2$ visible
odd defects if the ray is exceptional. These data lie in the finite
abelian group
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state-group}
 \mathsf H=\operatorname{Cl}(R)^{p+1}\times\mathbf F_p^{M'},\qquad|\mathsf H|=h^{p+1}p^{M'}.
\end{equation}
For a fixed rational prime $\ell$, call the element of $\mathsf H$
contributed by a conjugate of $\mathfrak q_\ell$ placed in a given ray a
\emph{block}. An allocation above $\ell$ is a word in these blocks, and
a shortest word representing a given element of $\mathsf H$ has no two
equal prefix sums: otherwise the nonempty subword between them sums to
zero and could be deleted. Its length is therefore at most
$|\mathsf H|-1$. Replacing an allocation by such a shorter word preserves
the ideal class and the visible defects of every ray ideal, does not increase the norm
used, and leaves the tests unchanged; the saved factors are absorbed in
$s$, and the residue of $s/p$ is preserved because their norms are
congruent to one modulo $p$. When $h=1$, the blocks lie in the vector
space $\mathbf F_p^{M'}$. If we choose a basis of their span among the blocks,
then every attainable vector is a combination of at most $M'$ basis vectors with
coefficients in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$, and therefore uses at most $(p-1)M'$
blocks. If the original allocation is shorter, we keep it.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:prime-support}
Let $p\ge7$ be regular and let $m>0$ be prime to $p$. If an allocation
for $m$ passes $\mathcal L_2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-support}
 \sum_{\substack{\ell\mid m\\ \ell^3\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)}}
 \Bigl\lfloor\frac{v_\ell(m)}{\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)}\Bigr\rfloor\ \ge\ p-6.
\end{equation}
In particular, this inequality is necessary for
$\pm p^{p+3}m\in S(G)$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
If $\mathcal L_2$ accepts, then $D_3$ has degree six by~\eqref{eq:affine-cubic},
so $D_3(b)\ne0$ for at least $p-6$ values of $b\in\mathbf F_p$. By additivity of the defects, every generic ray with
$D_3(b)\ne0$ contains an allocated prime ideal $\mathfrak q$ with
$d_3(\mathfrak q)\ne0$, and then $\ell^3\equiv1\pmod p$ for the prime
$\ell$ below $\mathfrak q$, by the Frobenius relation~\eqref{eq:frobenius}.
Each allocated ideal lies in exactly one ray, and at most
$\lfloor v_\ell(m)/\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)\rfloor$ ideals above $\ell$ are
allocated. This proves~\eqref{eq:prime-support}, and the last assertion
follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar}.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{thm:prime-cofactors}
Let $p\ge11$ be regular and let $\ell\ne p$ be a rational prime. Then
$\pm p^{p+3}\ell\notin S(G)$. Moreover, $\pm p^{p+4}\ell\in S(G)$ if
and only if $\ell\equiv1\pmod p$ and a prime ideal $\mathfrak q$ above
$\ell$ is principal with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-defect-criterion}
 d_3(\mathfrak q)=d_5(\mathfrak q)=\cdots=d_{p-6}(\mathfrak q)=0
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \bigl(d_{p-4}(\mathfrak q),d_{p-2}(\mathfrak q)\bigr)\ne(0,0).
\end{equation}
The condition does not depend on the prime ideal chosen above $\ell$,
and every admitted prime has an explicit realization.
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
For a prime cofactor the left side of~\eqref{eq:prime-support} is at
most one, whereas $p-6>1$. Hence no allocation passes $\mathcal L_2$, at either
critical valuation, and it remains to analyze $\mathcal L_3$ at valuation
$p+4$.

An allocation for $\ell$ is empty unless $\ell\equiv1\pmod p$, in which
case it places at most one prime ideal $\mathfrak q$ above $\ell$ into
one ray, and $\mathfrak q$ must then be principal. The empty allocation
cannot pass $\mathcal L_3$. Indeed, after the normalization $q=0$ the generic
relative series $H_b/T$ have even logarithms, so every even coefficient
of $H_b$ vanishes; in particular $h_4=0$, so $h_{\infty,4}=[b^4]h_4=0$
and the linear logarithmic coordinate of the exceptional relative series
vanishes as well. Its odd coordinates also vanish, because the
allocation is empty, so $H_\infty/T^3$ is even. All coefficients of
degree $p-1$ are then zero, which contradicts the constant moment
condition, since then $s=p\ell$ and $\tau\equiv\ell\not\equiv0\pmod p$.

Suppose first that $\mathfrak q$ is placed in a generic ray $b_0$. Write
$d_j=d_j(\mathfrak q)$ and $\mathbf 1_{b_0}(b)=1-(b-b_0)^{p-1}$, the indicator function of $b_0$, so
that
$D_j=d_j\mathbf 1_{b_0}$ and all exceptional defects vanish. The canonical
polynomial $\mathbf 1_{b_0}$ has degree $p-1$. With $q=0$, the first even
generic coefficient is $h_4=D_3$, and its degree bound forces $d_3=0$.
More generally, if the odd defects below an odd index $j$ vanish, then
$H_b/T$ is the product of an even series with constant coefficient one
and a series whose first odd term is $D_jT^j$, so $h_{j+1}=D_j$. For
$j=3,5,\ldots,p-4$ the required degree of $h_{j+1}$ is at most
$j+1\le p-3<p-1$, and induction gives $d_3=\cdots=d_{p-4}=0$. The only
remaining even generic coefficient is $h_{p-1}=d_{p-2}\mathbf 1_{b_0}$.
The exceptional series again has vanishing linear and odd logarithmic
coordinates, so $h_{\infty,p-1}=0$. All of $\ell$ is now allocated, so
$s=p$ and $\tau=1$, and the constant moment condition requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-finite-top}
 d_{p-2}=-1.
\end{equation}
In particular this coordinate is nonzero. Galois conjugation multiplies
it by $t^{p-2}=t^{-1}$, so every nonzero value can be moved to the
value in~\eqref{eq:prime-finite-top}.

If instead $\mathfrak q$ is placed at infinity, every generic odd defect
vanishes. The generic transforms are odd, and all exceptional
coefficients of even degree up to $p-3$ must vanish. Put
$a=h_{\infty,3}\ne0$. The relative series $H_\infty/(aT^3)$ then has
vanishing coefficients in the odd degrees $1,3,\ldots,p-6$, and so does
its logarithm. Thus $d_3=\cdots=d_{p-6}=0$, and in the highest visible
odd degree the raw coefficient equals $d_{p-4}$. The constant moment
condition reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-exceptional-top}
 1+a\,d_{p-4}=0,
\end{equation}
which is possible only if $d_{p-4}\ne0$; the coordinate $d_{p-2}$ is
invisible at infinity. The two placements together give exactly the
necessary condition~\eqref{eq:prime-defect-criterion}.

For sufficiency we exhibit compatible data. If
$\beta=d_{p-4}(\mathfrak q)\ne0$, place $\mathfrak q$ at infinity, put
$a=-\beta^{-1}$, and prescribe
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-exceptional-packet}
 H_b=T+ab^3T^3,\qquad H_\infty=aT^3+a\beta T^{p-1},\qquad s=p.
\end{equation}
The generic relative logarithms are even. The exceptional relative
series is $1+\beta T^{p-4}$, whose only visible odd logarithmic term is
$\beta T^{p-4}$. The nonconstant moment conditions hold, because $h_1=1$
and $h_3=ab^3$ have the required degrees, their coefficients
$[b^n]h_n$ equal $h_{\infty,n}$, and all other coefficients through
degree $p-2$ vanish; the constant
moment condition holds because $a\beta=-1$.

If $d_{p-4}=0$ and $d_{p-2}\ne0$, replace $\mathfrak q$ by its conjugate
under $\sigma_t$ with $t=-d_{p-2}$, so that its last defect becomes
$-1$, and place this conjugate in the ray $b_0=0$. For an arbitrary
$a\in\mathbf F_p^\times$, prescribe
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-finite-packet}
 H_b=T+ab^3T^3-\mathbf 1_0(b)T^{p-1},\qquad H_\infty=aT^3,\qquad s=p.
\end{equation}
The generic relative logarithm has no odd term below degree $p-2$, and
its coefficient of $T^{p-2}$ is $-\mathbf 1_0(b)$: dividing by $1+ab^3T^2$
does not change this top term at the retained precision. The exceptional
relative series is constant. The nonconstant moment conditions are as
in~\eqref{eq:prime-exceptional-packet}, and the top coefficients sum to
$-1$ because $\sum_b\mathbf 1_0(b)=1$, so the constant moment condition
also holds.

In both cases the local law~\eqref{eq:local-law} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:units} lift the series with exactly the prescribed
principal cofactor ideal, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:moments}
and~\ref{lem:reconstruct} produce integer group coefficients; the
explicit lift is~\eqref{eq:affine-unit-recovery}--\eqref{eq:affine-ray-lift}.
The augmentation is $p$, the generic valuations are one, the exceptional
valuation is three, and the cofactor norms multiply to $\ell$, so the
determinant is $p^{p+4}\ell$; negation gives the negative value.
Finally, Galois conjugation multiplies each $d_j$ by $t^j\ne0$ and
preserves principality, and all prime ideals above a split prime $\ell$
are conjugate. Hence the criterion does not depend on the chosen ideal.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{lem:prime-ray-class}
For a regular prime $p\ge7$, the map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-ray-isomorphism}
 \operatorname{Cl}_{pR}(K)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(R)\times\mathbf F_p^{(p-3)/2},\qquad
 [I]_{pR}\longmapsto\bigl([I],d_3(I),d_5(I),\ldots,d_{p-2}(I)\bigr),
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism. It is equivariant for $\sigma_t$, which acts as usual
on $\operatorname{Cl}(R)$ and by multiplication by $t^j$ on the coordinate $d_j$. In
particular, $|\operatorname{Cl}_{pR}(K)|=hp^{(p-3)/2}$.
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
A principal ideal generated by an element congruent to one modulo $pR$
has vanishing defects, so the map is well defined; additivity
and equivariance follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:ideals}. If the image of
$[I]_{pR}$ is zero, then $I=zR$ and every odd coordinate of $z$
vanishes. Lemma~\ref{lem:units} provides a global unit $u$ with the same
residue as $z$, so $z/u\equiv1$ modulo $pR$ and $[I]_{pR}=0$. Thus the
map is injective. The exact sequence~\cite[Chapter~V, Theorem~1.7]{MilneCFT}
\[
 R^\times\longrightarrow(R/pR)^\times\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}_{pR}(K)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(R)\longrightarrow0
\]
and the order $(p-1)p^{(p-1)/2}$ of the unit image in Lemma~\ref{lem:units}
give $|\operatorname{Cl}_{pR}(K)|=h\,(p-1)p^{p-2}/\bigl((p-1)p^{(p-1)/2}\bigr)
=hp^{(p-3)/2}$, which is the order of the target. Hence the map is an
isomorphism.

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{thm:prime-density}
For a regular prime $p\ge11$, the rational primes $\ell\ne p$ with
$\pm p^{p+4}\ell\in S(G)$ have natural density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-density}
 \frac{p+1}{hp^{(p-3)/2}}
\end{equation}
in the set of all rational primes, and Dirichlet density equal to the
same value.
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
Let $L$ be the ray class field of $K$ modulo $pR$. Class field theory
identifies $A=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ with $\operatorname{Cl}_{pR}(K)$ through the Artin map~\cite[Chapter~V, Theorems~3.5--3.6 and Corollary~3.7]{MilneCFT}. The
modulus and its group of principal ideals generated by elements
congruent to one are stable under $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf Q)$, so conjugation carries
the Artin kernel to itself, and uniqueness in the class field
correspondence gives $\sigma(L)=L$ for every automorphism extending an
element of $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf Q)$. Thus $L/\mathbf Q$ is Galois, and its group $\Gamma$
has order $(p-1)hp^{(p-3)/2}$. Conjugation on the abelian normal subgroup
$A$ factors through $\Gamma/A=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf Q)$ and corresponds, under the
Artin map, to the action in Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-ray-class}; no
splitting of the extension of groups is needed.

Let $\mathcal C\subset A$ consist of the classes with trivial ordinary
class, vanishing defects through degree $p-6$, and last two coordinates
not both zero. This set has $p^2-1$ elements. The Galois action
preserves it, acting on the last pair by
$(x,y)\mapsto(t^{p-4}x,t^{p-2}y)=(t^{-3}x,t^{-1}y)$, so $\mathcal C$ is
stable under conjugation in $\Gamma$. Every rational prime $\ell\ne p$
is unramified in $L$, and its Frobenius lies in $A$ exactly when $\ell$
splits in $K$, that is, when $\ell\equiv1\pmod p$. In that case the
Frobenius conjugacy class of $\ell$ is the orbit of the Artin symbol of
a prime $\mathfrak q\mid\ell$, which has residue degree one. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:prime-cofactors}, the admitted primes are exactly
those whose Frobenius lies in $\mathcal C$. The Chebotarev density
theorem for a conjugation-stable subset~\cite[Chapter~V, Theorem~3.23 and Remark~3.26(d); Chapter~VIII,
Theorem~7.4]{MilneCFT} gives the density
\[
 \frac{|\mathcal C|}{|\Gamma|}=\frac{p^2-1}{(p-1)hp^{(p-3)/2}}
 =\frac{p+1}{hp^{(p-3)/2}}.
\]
The factor $p-1$ accounts for the splitting of $\ell$ in $K$; no further
factor arises from the choice of $\mathfrak q$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:prime-size}
Let $p\ge11$ be regular. Every prime $\ell$ with
$\pm p^{p+4}\ell\in S(G)$ satisfies
\[
 \ell>\frac{p^{p-4}}{2^{p-1}}.
\]
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:prime-cofactors}, $\ell\equiv1\pmod p$ and a prime
ideal $\mathfrak q\mid\ell$ has a generator $y$ with
$c_3(y)=\cdots=c_{p-6}(y)=0$. Choose an integer $k$ with
$k\equiv c_1(y)\pmod p$ and put $w=\zeta^{-k}y$. Then $c_1(w)=0$ and
$c_j(w)=c_j(y)$ for $j\ge2$. Let $\overline w$ denote the complex
conjugate of $w$. Complex conjugation fixes residues modulo $\mathfrak p$ and
replaces $T$ by $-T$, so $\overline w(0)=w(0)$ and
$c_j(\overline w)=(-1)^jc_j(w)$. Hence the residue of $w/\overline w$
modulo $pR$ is
\[
 \exp\Bigl(2\sum_{j\ \mathrm{odd}}c_j(w)T^j\Bigr)
 =1+2c_{p-4}(w)T^{p-4}+2c_{p-2}(w)T^{p-2},
\]
and therefore $w-\overline w\in\mathfrak p^{p-4}$. Since $\ell$ splits completely
in $K$, the ideal $\mathfrak q$ differs from its complex conjugate, so
$w\ne\overline w$. Thus $w-\overline w$ is a nonzero element of
$\mathfrak p^{p-4}$, and $p^{p-4}$ divides $\operatorname{N}(w-\overline w)$. On the other
hand, for every complex embedding $\sigma$ of $K$ we have
$\sigma(\overline w)=\overline{\sigma(w)}$, so
$|\sigma(w-\overline w)|\le2|\sigma(w)|$. Taking the product over the
$p-1$ embeddings gives $\operatorname{N}(w-\overline w)\le2^{p-1}\operatorname{N}(w)=2^{p-1}\ell$.
Hence $p^{p-4}\le2^{p-1}\ell$, and equality is impossible because the
left side is odd.
