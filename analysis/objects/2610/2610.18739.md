<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{assum:v3-primitive-finite-window}
For the main primitive result, let
$\bm b\in C^3(\mathbb R^d;\mathbb R^d)$ and
$\sigma\in C^3(\mathbb R^d;\mathbb R^{d\times q})$. Here
$\|\cdot\|_F$ denotes the Frobenius norm. Assume that, for all
$\bm x,\bm y\in\mathbb R^d$,
\begin{equation}
 \|\bm b(\bm x)-\bm b(\bm y)\|\leq L_b\|\bm x-\bm y\|,\qquad
 \|\sigma(\bm x)-\sigma(\bm y)\|_F\leq L_\sigma\|\bm x-\bm y\|.
\label{eq:v3-primitive-global-lipschitz}
\end{equation}
Since the coefficients are $C^1$, this condition implies
\[
 \sup_{\bm x}\|D\bm b(\bm x)\|<\infty,
 \qquad
 \sup_{\bm x}\sum_j\|D\sigma_j(\bm x)\|^2<\infty.
\]
Assume also, for some $\kappa\geq0$,

\begin{equation}
 \|D^2\bm b(\bm x)\|+\sum_j\|D^2\sigma_j(\bm x)\|
 \leq C \langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{\kappa}.
\label{eq:v3-primitive-second-derivative-growth}
\end{equation}
Let $V\in C^2(\mathbb R^d)$ with $V\geq0$ and suppose, for some $v\geq0$, that
\begin{equation}
 \|\nabla V(\bm x)\|+\|\nabla^2V(\bm x)\|
 \leq C \langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{v}.
\label{eq:v3-primitive-lyapunov-growth}
\end{equation}


For some $p\geq v+\kappa+2$, we assume\footnote{More generally, it is
enough for the right-hand side to have polynomial growth of any order no
larger than $p$; we use the self-weighted form below for simplicity.}
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb{E}[\|\bm H_{k+1}\|^p\mid\mathcal F_k]
 \leq M \langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_k\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p}.
\label{eq:v3-primitive-oracle-p-moment}
\end{equation}

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main-rate-transfer}
Under Assumption~\ref{assum:v3-primitive-finite-window}, for each fixed
$T>0$ and indices $m\leq n\leq N_T(m)$ satisfying
$\mathbb{E}\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_m\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p}<\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\left|\mathbb{E}V(\bm X_n)
-\mathbb{E}\!\left[P_{t_m,t_n}V(\bm X_m)\right]\right|
\leq C_T\mathbb{E}\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_m\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p}
\int_{t_m}^{t_n}\alpha(s)\,ds.
\label{eq:main-rate-transfer-window-comparison}
\end{equation}
If, in addition,
$M_p:=\sup_{k\geq0}\mathbb{E}\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_k\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p}<\infty$ (or equivalently
\(\sup_{k\geq0}\mathbb E\|\bm X_k\|^p<\infty\)) and the
associated SDE satisfies, for deterministic nonnegative locally
integrable functions $\lambda$ and $g$ with
$0\leq\lambda(t)\leq\bar\lambda<\infty$,
\[
\mathcal L_tV(\bm x)\leq-\lambda(t)V(\bm x)+g(t).
\]
With
$R(s,t)=\exp\{-\int_s^t\lambda(r)\,dr\}$, for every $n\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}V(\bm X_n)
\leq R(0,t_n)\mathbb{E}V(\bm X_0)
+\int_0^{t_n}R(s,t_n)\{g(s)+C_T\alpha(s)\}\,ds,
\label{eq:v3-first-order-transfer-bound}
\end{equation}
where $C_T<\infty$ is independent of $n$ and may depend on the fixed-window
and moment constants, including $M_p$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:foster-lyapunov-stability-transfer}
Suppose the stochastic approximation and associated SDE satisfy
Assumption~\ref{assum:v3-primitive-finite-window} with moment exponent
$p\geq2$. Let $\Phi\in C^2(\mathbb R^d)$ satisfy $\Phi\geq1$ and, for a
constant $C_\Phi<\infty$,
\[
\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^p\leq C_\Phi\Phi(\bm x),\qquad
\|\nabla\Phi(\bm x)\|\leq C_\Phi\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p-1},\qquad
\|\nabla^2\Phi(\bm x)\|\leq C_\Phi\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p-2}.
\]
Assume also that $\mathbb E\Phi(\bm X_0)<\infty$ and that, for some
$\gamma>0$, $K\geq0$, and finite index $N_0$, the following bound holds
uniformly for $t\geq t_{N_0}$ and $\bm x\in\mathbb R^d$:
\[
\mathcal L_t\Phi(\bm x)\leq-\gamma\Phi(\bm x)+K.
\]
There is a
sufficiently small $\bar\alpha>0$, depending only on the constants in these
assumptions, such that if there is a finite index $N\geq N_0$ for which
$\alpha_n<\bar\alpha$ for every $n\geq N$, then
\[
\sup_{n\geq0}\mathbb E\Phi(\bm X_n)<\infty,
\qquad
M_p:=\sup_{n\geq0}\mathbb E\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_n\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^p<\infty.
\]

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:power-lifting-sde-stability}
In applications, the natural SDE Lyapunov function often controls a
lower-order moment than the one required by
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-rate-transfer}. For example, if $W$ is quadratic, so
that $1+W(\bm x)\asymp\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^2$, but a $p$-th moment is required,
we use the power-lifting trick and set
\[
\Phi(\bm x)=(1+W(\bm x))^{p/2}.
\]
More generally, if
$1+W(\bm x)\asymp\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p_0}$, we take
$\Phi(\bm x)=(1+W(\bm x))^{p/p_0}$. Under the standing regularity
assumptions, the generator chain rule shows that a Foster--Lyapunov bound
for $W$ gives a corresponding bound for $\Phi$ once the stepsizes are
sufficiently small. Since $\Phi(\bm x)\asymp\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^p$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:foster-lyapunov-stability-transfer} then gives the
required $p$-th moment bound. A short verification of this power-lifting step
is given in Lemma~\ref{lem:power-lifting-sde-stability}.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:v3-global-lipschitz}
The global Lipschitz condition, together with
$\alpha_{\max}<\infty$, also ensures that the associated SDE is well posed
for every restart. Indeed, global Lipschitzness gives linear growth, and the
time-inhomogeneous diffusion coefficient
$\sqrt{\alpha(t)}\,\sigma(\bm x)$ is uniformly Lipschitz in $\bm x$ and has
linear growth. The standard existence and uniqueness theorem for SDEs
therefore gives a unique nonexplosive strong solution on every finite
interval; see \citet[Theorem~5.2.1]{oksendal2003stochastic}.

The global Lipschitz condition is a convenient sufficient condition rather
than a minimal one. It gives uniformly bounded linear coefficients in the
first variational equation and places the later stochastic-flow estimates in
their standard setting. We do not pursue weaker local conditions here.
Likewise, the linear growth used in the one-step comparison below is
convenient rather than essential: polynomially growing coefficients can be
handled under nonexplosion and sufficiently strong fixed-window polynomial
moment bounds.

\label{lem:window-linear-energy}
Consider an adapted $\mathbb R^d$-valued process $\bm Y$ satisfying

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:window-linear-energy-sde}
 d\bm Y_t=(A_t\bm Y_t+\bm f_t)\,dt
 +\sum_j(\Sigma_{j,t}\bm Y_t+\bm g_{j,t})\,dB_t^j,
\end{equation}
where $A_t,\Sigma_{j,t}\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ and
$\bm f_t,\bm g_{j,t}\in\mathbb R^d$ are progressively measurable
coefficient processes, and $B^j$ are real-valued Brownian motions.
For matrices, $\|\cdot\|$ denotes the Euclidean operator norm.
Suppose, almost surely,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:window-linear-energy-coefficient-bounds}
 \langle \bm y,A_t\bm y\rangle\leq L\|\bm y\|^2
 \quad\text{for }\bm y\in\mathbb R^d,\qquad
 \sum_j\|\Sigma_{j,t}\|^2\leq L_1^2.
\end{equation}

Then, for every $p\geq2$ and every fixed $S>0$, whenever $t-s\leq S$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:window-linear-energy-estimate}
 \mathbb{E}\sup_{s\leq r\leq t}\|\bm Y_r\|^p
 \leq C^Y_{p,S}\left\{
 \mathbb{E}\|\bm Y_s\|^p+
 \int_s^t\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm f_r\|^p+
             \left(\sum_j\|\bm g_{j,r}\|^2\right)^{p/2}\right]dr
 \right\}.
\end{equation}

Here $C^Y_{p,S}$ may depend on the fixed constants $L$ and $L_1$, but not on
an operator-norm bound for $A_t$.

\label{lem:window-two-bound-taylor}
Suppose $u\in C^2(\mathbb R^d)$ and, for $0\leq\gamma_1\leq\gamma_2$,

\begin{equation}
 \|\nabla u(\bm x)\|\leq C_1 \langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{\gamma_1},\qquad
 \|\nabla^2u(\bm x)\|\leq C_2 \langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{\gamma_2}.
\label{eq:window-taylor-derivative-growth}
\end{equation}
Set $\gamma_3 = \max\{2,\gamma_1+1\}$. Then, for $\bm z \in \mathbb R^d$,
\begin{equation}
 |u(\bm x+\bm z)-u(\bm x)-\nabla u(\bm x)^\top\bm z|
 \leq C_3\{\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{\gamma_2}\|\bm z\|^2+\|\bm z\|^{\gamma_3}\}.
\label{eq:window-taylor-deterministic-remainder}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if
$\mathbb{E}[\|\bm H\|^{\gamma_3}\mid\mathcal F]\leq M \langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{\gamma_4}$,
 and $\bm x$ is $\mathcal F$-measurable, then

\begin{equation}
 \left|\mathbb{E}[u(\bm x+h\bm H)\mid\mathcal F]-u(\bm x)
       -h\nabla u(\bm x)^\top\mathbb{E}[\bm H\mid\mathcal F]\right|
 \leq C_4 h^2 \langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{\gamma_5}\, ,
\label{eq:window-taylor-conditional-bound}
\end{equation}
for $0<h\leq\alpha_{\max}$ and
where
\(
\gamma_5
=
\max\{
\gamma_2+ {2\gamma_4}/{\gamma_3},
\gamma_4
\}.
\)

In particular, if $\bm x$ is $\mathcal F$-measurable and, for some
$p\geq\gamma_2+2$,
\[
\mathbb E[\|\bm H\|^p\mid\mathcal F]\leq M_p\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p},
\]
then, for $0<h\leq\alpha_{\max}$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Big|\,\mathbb E[u(\bm x+h\bm H)\mid\mathcal F]
&-u(\bm x)\\
&-h\nabla u(\bm x)^\top\mathbb E[\bm H\mid\mathcal F]\,\Big|
\leq C_5h^2\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{\gamma_2+2}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:window-taylor-conditional-p-moment-bound}
\end{equation}
Here $C_5$ depends only on $C_1,C_2$, the derivative exponents
$\gamma_1,\gamma_2$, $p$, $M_p$, and $\alpha_{\max}$.

\label{thm:finite-window-sde-rate-transfer}

$\mathbb{E}V(\bm X_0)<\infty$. Assume positive stepsizes with
$\alpha_{\max}:=\sup_n\alpha_n<\infty$ and $\sum_n\alpha_n=\infty$, and suppose that the restarted SDE

locally integrable,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-window-sde-generator}
\mathcal L_tV(\bm x)\leq-\lambda(t)V(\bm x)+g(t).
\end{equation}

approximation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-window-sde-approximation}

\leq
\mathbb E\!\left[P_{t_m,t_n}V(\bm X_m)\right]
+\epsilon_m\mathbb E V(\bm X_m)
+\int_{t_m}^{t_n}\rho(s)\,ds,
\qquad m\leq n\leq N_T(m),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

$R_j=R(t_{m_j},t_{m_{j+1}})$. Suppose that

\end{equation}

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:stepsize-rate-inheritance}
Suppose that, at the diffusion times $t_n$, a nonnegative sequence satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stepsize-rate-inheritance-envelope}
E_n\leq e^{-\lambda t_n}E_0+C\int_0^{t_n}
e^{-\lambda(t_n-s)}\alpha(s)^\delta\,ds,
\qquad \lambda,\delta>0.
\end{equation}
For constant stepsizes $\alpha_n=h>0$, this gives
\[
E_n\leq e^{-\lambda hn}E_0+
\frac{C}{\lambda}h^\delta(1-e^{-\lambda hn})
\leq e^{-\lambda hn}E_0+\frac{C}{\lambda}h^\delta.
\]
Thus a fixed step gives convergence to an $O(h^\delta)$ neighbourhood,
not convergence to zero. For
$\alpha_n=a/(n+n_0)^\vartheta$, where $a,n_0>0$ and $0<\vartheta<1$,
integral comparison yields
\[
t_n=\frac{a}{1-\vartheta}
\bigl\{(n+n_0)^{1-\vartheta}-n_0^{1-\vartheta}\bigr\}+O(1),
\]
so $e^{-\lambda t_n}\leq C_0e^{-c(n+n_0)^{1-\vartheta}}$ with
$c=\lambda a/(1-\vartheta)$. Moreover,
\[
\int_0^{t_n}e^{-\lambda(t_n-s)}\alpha(s)^\delta\,ds
=O(\alpha_n^\delta)
=O((n+n_0)^{-\vartheta\delta}).
\]
Indeed, on $s\geq t_n/2$ the piecewise-constant stepsize is
$O(\alpha_n)$, whereas the contribution from $s<t_n/2$ is exponentially
small. Consequently,
\[
E_n=O\!\left(e^{-c(n+n_0)^{1-\vartheta}}
+(n+n_0)^{-\vartheta\delta}\right).
\]
At the harmonic boundary, $\alpha_n=a/(n+n_0)$ and
$t_n=a\log(n+n_0)+O(1)$. Comparing the forcing with
$\alpha(s)^\delta\asymp e^{-\delta s/a}$ gives
\[
E_n=\begin{cases}
O(n^{-\delta}),&\lambda a>\delta,\\
O(n^{-\delta}\log n),&\lambda a=\delta,\\
O(n^{-\lambda a}),&\lambda a<\delta.
\end{cases}
\]
The threshold is specific to this harmonic boundary: for $0<\vartheta<1$,
contraction is stretched exponential in iteration time and eventually
dominates polynomial forcing, while in the harmonic case contraction and
forcing decay polynomially and their exponents compete.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We first control the finite initial segment. Integrating the gradient
of $\Phi$ along the line from $\bm 0$ to $\bm x$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(\bm x)
&=\Phi(\bm 0)+\int_0^1\nabla\Phi(\theta\bm x)^\top\bm x\,d\theta\\
&\leq\Phi(\bm 0)+C_\Phi\|\bm x\|
\int_0^1\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \theta\bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p-1}\,d\theta
\leq C\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The polynomial translation inequality and the primitive oracle bound
then give, for every $k$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_{k+1}\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^p\mid\mathcal F_k]
&\leq C_p\left(\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_k\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^p+
\alpha_k^p\mathbb E[\|\bm H_{k+1}\|^p\mid\mathcal F_k]\right)\\
&\leq C_p(1+M\alpha_k^p)\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_k\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^p.
\end{aligned}
\]
The coercive bound in the proposition and
$\mathbb E\Phi(\bm X_0)<\infty$ first give
$\mathbb E\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_0\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^p<\infty$. Since every $\alpha_k$ is finite,
induction in the conditional estimate therefore gives
$\mathbb E\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_k\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^p<\infty$ for every $0\leq k\leq N$.
The polynomial upper bound on $\Phi$ gives
$\mathbb E\Phi(\bm X_k)<\infty$ on this finite prefix, in particular at
$k=N$. Thus no smallness condition is needed for the first $N$ steps.

We now start the Taylor argument at an index $n\geq N$ and write
$h=\alpha_n$. Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives
\[
\Phi(\bm X_n+h\bm H_{n+1})
=\Phi(\bm X_n)+h\nabla\Phi(\bm X_n)^\top\bm H_{n+1}
+h^2\int_0^1(1-\theta)\,
\bm H_{n+1}^\top\nabla^2\Phi(\bm X_n+\theta h\bm H_{n+1})
\bm H_{n+1}\,d\theta.
\]
The polynomial translation inequality
$\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x+\bm y\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{r}\leq C_r(\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{r}+\|\bm y\|^r)$,
for $r\geq0$, and the Hessian bound imply that the conditional expectation
of the absolute remainder is at most
\[
C h^2\left[
\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_n\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p-2}
\mathbb E(\|\bm H_{n+1}\|^2\mid\mathcal F_n)
+h^{p-2}\mathbb E(\|\bm H_{n+1}\|^p\mid\mathcal F_n)
\right].
\]
Conditional Jensen's inequality and the primitive $p$th-moment bound
give
\[
\mathbb E(\|\bm H_{n+1}\|^2\mid\mathcal F_n)
\leq\left(\mathbb E(\|\bm H_{n+1}\|^p\mid\mathcal F_n)\right)^{2/p}
\leq C\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_n\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{2}.
\]
Choose $\bar\alpha\leq1$. Since $h<\bar\alpha$, the remainder is
therefore bounded by
$Ch^2\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_n\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p}$. Using
$\mathbb E[\bm H_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]=\bm b(\bm X_n)$ and
$\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^p\leq C_\Phi\Phi(\bm x)$, we obtain
\[
\mathbb E[\Phi(\bm X_{n+1})\mid\mathcal F_n]
\leq\Phi(\bm X_n)
+h\bm b(\bm X_n)^\top\nabla\Phi(\bm X_n)
+Ch^2\Phi(\bm X_n).
\]

The global Lipschitz bound on $\sigma$ implies
$\|\sigma(\bm x)\|_F\leq C\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle$. Hence
\[
|A(\bm x):\nabla^2\Phi(\bm x)|
\leq C\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{2}\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p-2}
\leq C\Phi(\bm x).
\]
For $n\geq N$, we have $t_n\geq t_N\geq t_{N_0}$ and
$\alpha(t_n)=\alpha_n=h$. The tail generator inequality therefore yields
\[
\bm b(\bm x)^\top\nabla\Phi(\bm x)
=\mathcal L_{t_n}\Phi(\bm x)
-\frac h2 A(\bm x):\nabla^2\Phi(\bm x)
\leq-\gamma\Phi(\bm x)+K+Ch\Phi(\bm x).
\]
Substitution into the Taylor estimate gives
\[
\mathbb E[\Phi(\bm X_{n+1})\mid\mathcal F_n]
\leq\bigl(1-\gamma h+Ch^2\bigr)\Phi(\bm X_n)+Kh.
\]
Decrease $\bar\alpha$ if necessary so that
$C\bar\alpha\leq\gamma/2$ and $\bar\alpha\leq2/\gamma$. Then for
$n\geq N$,
\[
\mathbb E[\Phi(\bm X_{n+1})\mid\mathcal F_n]
\leq\left(1-\frac{\gamma h}{2}\right)\Phi(\bm X_n)+Kh,
\qquad 0\leq1-\frac{\gamma h}{2}\leq1.
\]
Taking expectations, set $u_n=\mathbb E\Phi(\bm X_n)$ and
$L=2K/\gamma$. For $n\geq N$,
\[
u_{n+1}\leq\left(1-\frac{\gamma\alpha_n}{2}\right)u_n
+\frac{\gamma\alpha_n}{2}L.
\]
The coefficient lies in $[0,1]$, so induction from $N$ gives
$u_n\leq\max\{u_N,L\}$ for every $n\geq N$. Combining this tail bound
with the finite prefix yields
\[
\sup_{n\geq0}\mathbb E\Phi(\bm X_n)
\leq\max\left\{\max_{0\leq k\leq N}\mathbb E\Phi(\bm X_k),
\frac{2K}{\gamma}\right\}<\infty.
\]
Finally, the one-sided coercive bound gives
$M_p:=\sup_{n\geq0}\mathbb E\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm X_n\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p}
\leq C_\Phi\sup_{n\geq0}\mathbb E\Phi(\bm X_n)<\infty$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:power-lifting-sde-stability}
Let $W\in C^2(\mathbb R^d)$ satisfy $W\geq0$, and let
$2\leq p_0\leq p$. Suppose, for positive finite constants $c_-$, $c_+$,
and $C_W$, that
\[
c_-\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p_0}\leq1+W(\bm x)
\leq c_+\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p_0},\qquad
\|\nabla W(\bm x)\|\leq C_W\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p_0-1},\qquad
\|\nabla^2W(\bm x)\|\leq C_W\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p_0-2}.
\]
Assume that $\sigma$ is globally Lipschitz, as in
Assumption~\ref{assum:v3-primitive-finite-window}, and that on a tail
\[
\mathcal L_tW(\bm x)\leq-\lambda_0W(\bm x)+K_0
\]
for some $\lambda_0>0$ and finite $K_0$. Set $r=p/p_0$ and
$\Phi=(1+W)^r$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(a)] $\Phi(\bm x)\asymp\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^p$;
\item[\rm(b)] for a finite constant $C$,
\[
\|\nabla\Phi(\bm x)\|\leq C\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p-1},\qquad
\|\nabla^2\Phi(\bm x)\|\leq C\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p-2};
\]
\item[\rm(c)] there are $c,C,\bar\alpha>0$ such that, wherever the
lower-order Foster--Lyapunov inequality holds and $\alpha(t)\leq\bar\alpha$,
\[
\mathcal L_t\Phi(\bm x)\leq-c\Phi(\bm x)+C.
\]
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The two-sided growth assumption gives
$\Phi\asymp\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle^{p}$. Differentiation yields
\[
\nabla\Phi=r(1+W)^{r-1}\nabla W,\qquad
\nabla^2\Phi=r(1+W)^{r-1}\nabla^2W
+r(r-1)(1+W)^{r-2}\nabla W\nabla W^\top.
\]
The growth assumptions give the bounds in (b). For $1\leq r<2$,
the negative exponent $r-2$ in the Hessian's second term is bounded using
the lower coercivity bound on $1+W$. The generator chain rule gives
\[
\mathcal L_t\Phi
=r(1+W)^{r-1}\mathcal L_tW
+\frac{\alpha(t)}2r(r-1)(1+W)^{r-2}
\|\sigma^\top\nabla W\|^2.
\]
Writing $z=1+W$, the assumed drift bound gives
$r z^{r-1}\mathcal L_tW\leq-r\lambda_0z^r+Cz^{r-1}
\leq-c_0z^r+C$ by Young's inequality. Global Lipschitzness gives
$\|\sigma(\bm x)\|_F\leq C\langle\mkern-4.5mu\langle \bm x\rangle\mkern-4.5mu\rangle$. Combining this with the
gradient bound and the lower coercivity bound shows
\[
(1+W)^{r-2}\|\sigma^\top\nabla W\|^2\leq C(1+W)^r=C\Phi.
\]
Consequently,
$\mathcal L_t\Phi\leq-c_0\Phi+C+C\alpha(t)\Phi$. Choosing
$\bar\alpha$ small enough to absorb the last term proves (c).
