<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:polynomial}
Let \(k\ge2\), \(r\ge A_k\), and \(M\ge1\) be integers. There is
a nonempty \(\mathbb Q\)-defined Zariski open subset
\(U_{k,r,M}\subset\mathscr A_r\) with the following property.
For every extension \(L/K\) of characteristic-zero fields, every
\(\sigma\in U_{k,r,M}(K)\), every \(n\ge1\), and every ordered
tuple of pairwise distinct nonconstant polynomials
\[
 p_1,\ldots,p_k\in L[t_1,\ldots,t_n],\qquad
 \deg p_i\le M,
\]
the compositions \(\sigma(p_1),\ldots,\sigma(p_k)\) are linearly
independent over \(L\).

Moreover, \(U_{k,r,M}^0:=U_{k,r,M}\cap\mathscr A_r^0\) is nonempty.
In particular it gives the same conclusion when both the composing
polynomial and all the inputs vanish at the origin.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:joint}
The \emph{joint degree} of \((p_1,\ldots,p_k)\) is
\(\deg f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^k}(1)\). Equivalently, write
\[
 f=[Q:P_1:\cdots:P_k]
\]
using homogeneous polynomials of one degree without a common zero. That
degree is the joint degree, and \(p_i=P_i/Q\).
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:rational}
The open set in Theorem~\ref{thm:polynomial} can be chosen so that,
in addition, it separates every tuple of pairwise distinct nonconstant
rational functions \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\in L(t)\) of joint degree at
most \(M\), for every extension \(L/K\) as above. Its intersection
with \(\mathscr A_r^0\) is nonempty.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:identifiability}
Let $\mathbf{d} = (d_0, \dots, d_L)$ with $L \ge 2$, let $D = \max(d_0, \dots, d_{L-1})$, and let
\[
  r \ \ge\ A_{2D} \ =\ 10 D^2 -9D + 4.
\]
There is a nonempty Zariski open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^0[u]_{\le r}$ such that, for every
$\sigma \in U$, the weights $W$ in architecture $\mathbf{d}$ are identifiable if and only if
$W$ is not a subnetwork. 
Moreover if the architecture that has layer-specific activations $\sigma_i$ with $r_i := \deg \sigma_i$ for $1\leq i \leq L-1$ then the same holds if $r_i \geq A_{2d_i}$.
In particular, for each $i$ there is a nonempty open zariski sets $U_i \subset \mathbb{R}^0[u]_{\leq r_i}$ such that for any choice $(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_{L-1}) \in U_1 \times \dots \times U_{L-1}$, the weights $W$ are identifiable if and only if $W$ is not subnetwork.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:incidence}
Fix \(k,r,M\). Call a geometric point of \(\Sigma_r\) bad if
it admits a nontrivial scalar relation among \(k\) compositions.
The inputs are required to be distinct nonconstant rational
functions of joint degree at most \(M\). The bad locus is the
image of a morphism \(\mathscr I\to\Sigma_r\) of finite-type
\(\mathbb Q\)-schemes.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
In a nonzero relation, delete the terms with zero coefficient and
divide by one of the remaining coefficients. We may then assume
that the relation has \(s\) terms, with all \(\lambda_i\ne0\)
and \(\lambda_s=1\). A one-term relation is impossible, since
composition of a nonconstant rational function with a nonconstant
polynomial is nonconstant. Thus \(2\le s\le k\).
After deleting coordinates, cancel the common homogeneous factor
in \((Q,P_1,\ldots,P_s)\). The resulting joint degree is at least
one and at most the original joint degree.

For \(2\le s\le k\) and \(1\le D\le M\), let
\(Q,P_1,\ldots,P_s\) be homogeneous polynomials of degree \(D\)
in two variables, without a common zero, taken up to a common
nonzero scalar. Let \(Q\ne0\), let no \(P_i\) be proportional
to \(Q\), and let \(P_i\ne P_j\) for \(i\ne j\).
These conditions define an open subset of a projective space.
Indeed, the common-zero locus is the proper image of a closed
incidence over \(\mathbb P^1\); the other excluded conditions are
given by coefficient equalities or minors.

Adjoin \((\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{s-1})\in(\mathbb G_m)^{s-1}\),
put \(\lambda_s=1\), and impose the coefficient equations of
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^s\lambda_i
       \sum_{j=0}^r a_jP_i^jQ^{r-j}=0.
\]
The finite disjoint union of these incidences over \(s,D\) is a
finite-type \(\mathbb Q\)-scheme \(\mathscr I\). Its image is the
bad locus in the statement. To see the reverse inclusion when
\(s<k\), complete the tuple by distinct translates \(p_1+c\)
and assign zero coefficients to the added terms. Over a geometric
field this is possible, and the joint degree remains \(D\).
The image is constructible by Chevalley's theorem
\cite[Tag 054K]{stacks}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:smooth-incidence}
The morphism \(\mathscr Y_s\to\Sigma_r\) is smooth. The projection
\(Y_a\to\Lambda_s\) is smooth along \(Z=0\).
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
On \(Z=1\), the defining equation is
\[
 \Phi(a,x,\lambda)=\sum_i\lambda_i\sigma_a(x_i)=0.
\]
Its spatial derivatives are \(\lambda_i\sigma_a'(x_i)\), and
its derivatives in the scalar coordinates are \(\sigma_a(x_j)\)
for \(j<s\). If all these derivatives vanished, then
\(\sigma_a(x_1)=\sigma_a'(x_1)=0\). This contradicts squarefreeness.
Here \(s\ge2\), so the scalar coordinate \(\lambda_1\) is
available.

At \(Z=0\), the spatial homogeneous derivatives include
\[
 ra_r\lambda_iX_i^{r-1},\qquad 1\le i\le s.
\]
At least one is nonzero. Euler's identity shows that the homogeneous
differential annihilates the radial direction on the hypersurface;
it therefore gives a nonzero cotangent vector on \(\mathbb P^s\).
The relative spatial differential is consequently surjective there.
These computations prove both assertions by the relative Jacobian
criterion.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:horizontal}
The sequence of global sections
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathrm H^0(C,f^*T_{Y_a})
 \longrightarrow\mathrm H^0(C,f^*T_{\mathscr Y_s})
 \xrightarrow{d\pi}T_a\Sigma_r\longrightarrow0
\]
is exact. Any \(L\)-linear section of its last map induces a
splitting of the pulled-back tangent sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-sequence}
 0\longrightarrow f^*T_{Y_a}
 \longrightarrow f^*T_{\mathscr Y_s}
 \xrightarrow{d\pi}\mathcal O_C^{r+1}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The sequence \eqref{eq:horizontal-sequence} is exact by
Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-incidence}. Taking global sections is left
exact, and \(\mathrm H^0(C,\mathcal O_C)=L\). It remains to prove that
\(d\pi:\mathrm H^0(C,f^*T_{\mathscr Y_s})\to T_a\Sigma_r\) is surjective.

After shrinking \(\mathscr H\) around \(b\), we may assume that
\(\mathscr H\) is smooth. The universal homogeneous coordinates
define an evaluation morphism
\[
 \operatorname{ev}:C\times\mathscr H\longrightarrow\mathscr Y_s,
 \qquad\pi\circ\operatorname{ev}=q\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathscr H},
\]
whose restriction to \(C\times\{b\}\) is \(f\).
Indeed, the coordinates have no common zero, and their common
scalar ambiguity does not affect the projective morphism.

Differentiating this family at \(b\), with \(C\) fixed, gives
\[
 T_b\mathscr H\longrightarrow\mathrm H^0(C,f^*T_{\mathscr Y_s}),
\]
whose composition with \(d\pi\) is \(dq_b\). Since \(dq_b\)
is surjective, so is \(d\pi\) on global sections.

Choose an \(L\)-linear section
\[
 H_0:T_a\Sigma_r\longrightarrow\mathrm H^0(C,f^*T_{\mathscr Y_s}).
\]
Extending \(H_0\) by \(\mathcal O_C\)-linearity gives
\[
 H:T_a\Sigma_r\otimes_L\mathcal O_C\longrightarrow f^*T_{\mathscr Y_s},
 \qquad c\otimes g\longmapsto gH_0(c).
\]
Then \(d\pi\circ H=\operatorname{id}\), so \(H\) splits
\eqref{eq:horizontal-sequence}. The splitting is regular on all
of \(C\), since the images of \(H_0\) are global sections.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:defect}
We have \(\tau\le(s-1)D\). If \(\ell=s-1\), then \(\Delta=0\).
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-incidence}, the spatial differential
alone is surjective at target infinity. Hence \(\Delta\)
is disjoint from \(E\).

Write \(H(e_j)=(u_j,v_j,e_j)\) in the ambient tangent bundle.
Tangency to the universal equation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-lift}
 \mu_W(u_j,v_j)=-M_j,
 \qquad M_j=\sum_i\lambda_iP_i^jQ^{r-j}.
\end{equation}
Thus every \(M_j\in\mathrm H^0(C,\mathcal O_C(rD))\) vanishes along
\(\Delta\), with its full multiplicities.
The Vandermonde matrix \((p_i^j)_{0\le j<s,1\le i\le s}\)
is invertible over \(L(t)\). Since \((\lambda_i)\ne0\),
some moment \(\sum_i\lambda_ip_i^\nu\) is nonzero for
\(0\le\nu\le s-1\). Its numerator
\[
 \widehat S_\nu=\sum_i\lambda_iP_i^\nu
       \in\mathrm H^0(C,\mathcal O_C(\nu D))
\]
is a nonzero section. Since \(M_\nu=Q^{r-\nu}\widehat S_\nu\)
and \(\Delta\) is disjoint from \(E\), we obtain
\[
 \Delta\le\operatorname{div}(\widehat S_\nu),
 \qquad\tau\le\nu D\le(s-1)D.
\]
Here we use \(r\ge s-1\), so that \eqref{eq:moment-lift}
is available for \(j=\nu\).

If \(\ell=s-1\), then \(W=L^{s-1}\) and \(\mu_W\) is
the full normal map in \eqref{eq:normal-pullback}. It is
surjective, so \(\Delta=0\).

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:frame}
Assume \(r\ge s\). On the locus of distinct affine coordinates,
the following fields span the tangent space to
\(\mathscr Y_s\to\Lambda_s\):
\begin{alignat}{3}
 &\delta\sigma=z^jT_x,&\qquad &\delta x_i=0,
       &\quad&0\le j\le r-s;\label{eq:frame-A}\\
 &\delta\sigma=S_i z^j-S_jz^i,&&\delta x_l=0,
       &&0\le i<j\le s-1;\label{eq:frame-B}\\
 &\delta\sigma=R_m:=-z^m\sigma_a'(z)+T_xQ_m,
       &&\delta x_i=x_i^m,
       &&0\le m\le s-1.\label{eq:frame-C}
\end{alignat}
Here \(Q_0=Q_1=0\), and for \(m\ge2\), \(Q_m\) cancels only
the terms of \(-z^m\sigma_a'\) of degree strictly greater than
\(r\). Each coefficient of the first two families has spatial
degree at most \(s\). The same is true for
\eqref{eq:frame-C} when \(m\le2\); for \(m\ge3\), its
coefficient degrees are at most \(s+m-2\).
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For \eqref{eq:frame-A}, every weighted evaluation at the nodes
vanishes. For \eqref{eq:frame-B}, it is
\(S_iS_j-S_jS_i=0\). Their degree bounds follow immediately.

For \(m\ge2\), write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truncated-quotient}
 Q_m(z)=\sum_{u=0}^{m-2}q_u z^{r+m-1-s-u}.
\end{equation}
The smallest exponent is \(r+1-s\ge0\). Top-term cancellation
determines its coefficients successively by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 q_u=(r-u)a_{r-u}-\sum_{j=1}^u b_jq_{u-j},
                  \qquad 0\le u\le m-2.
\end{equation}
Induction gives \(\deg_xq_u\le u\). The product \(b_jq_u\)
has degree at most \(j+u\), so every coefficient of \(R_m\)
has degree at most \(s+m-2\). The spatial coefficients have
degree \(m\), which satisfies the same bound. For \(m=0,1\),
the coefficients of \(R_m\) have spatial degree zero and the spatial
coefficients have degree at most one. In every case
\(\deg_zR_m\le r\).

Tangency of \eqref{eq:frame-C} is the polynomial identity
\[
 \delta\Phi
  =\sum_i\lambda_i\bigl(\sigma_a'(x_i)x_i^m+R_m(x_i)\bigr)
  =\sum_i\lambda_iT_x(x_i)Q_m(x_i)=0.
\]
No inverse of a difference of nodes occurs in these fields.

To prove spanning, first prescribe a spatial variation. The vectors
\((x_i^m)_{i=1}^s\), \(0\le m\le s-1\), form a basis at
distinct nodes by the Vandermonde determinant. Subtract a combination
of \eqref{eq:frame-C} to leave zero spatial variation. The remaining
coefficient variation \(A(z)\) satisfies
\(\sum_i\lambda_iA(x_i)=0\). Divide \(A\) by the monic
polynomial \(T_x\). Its quotient is accounted for by
\eqref{eq:frame-A}, and its remainder has degree less than \(s\).

On this remainder space, the weighted-evaluation functional has
coordinates \((S_0,\ldots,S_{s-1})\). They are not all zero,
again by Vandermonde and the nonzero scalar vector. If \(S_\nu\ne0\),
the vectors \(S_\nu e_j-S_je_\nu\), \(j\ne\nu\), span its
kernel. These are among \eqref{eq:frame-B}, up to sign.
This proves spanning of the entire zero-scalar tangent space.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:field-sequence}
There are \(h\) tangent fields with pole bounds
\(w_1,\ldots,w_h\), where \(\sum_iw_i\le sh+e_s\),
which give an exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:field-sequence}
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_C(2)\oplus
   \bigoplus_{i=1}^h\mathcal O_C(-w_iE)
   \longrightarrow\mathscr B\longrightarrow\mathscr R\longrightarrow0
\end{equation}
with \(\mathscr R\) torsion. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:RR-length}
 0\le\operatorname{length}_L\mathscr R
   =\left(s+1-r+\sum_iw_i\right)D+\tau-2.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The affine translation field \(\partial/\partial x_i\) extends
to a global vector field on \(\mathbb P^s\), induced by
\(X_i\mapsto X_i+\varepsilon Z\). The coefficient directions
are global on \(\Sigma_r\). A polynomial of spatial degree
at most \(w\), evaluated at the \(p_i\), has pole divisor
at most \(wE\). Each field in Lemma~\ref{lem:frame} therefore
pulls back to a regular section of the ambient tangent bundle
twisted by \(wE\). Its image under the hypersurface
differential vanishes away from \(E\), hence everywhere.
It is thus a section of \(f^*T_{\mathscr Y_s}(wE)\).

Apply the regular projection \(P\) defined after
Lemma~\ref{lem:horizontal}. The original field has zero scalar
component, and its horizontal part has scalar component in
\(W\otimes_L\mathcal O_C\). Its projection consequently lies in
\(\mathrm H^0(C,\mathscr B(wE))\).

These projected sections span \(\mathscr B\) at the generic point
\(\eta\) of \(C\). Indeed, for \(v\in\mathscr B_\eta\), choose a
coefficient vector \(c\in L(t)^{r+1}\) such that \(H(c)\) has the opposite scalar
component. Then \(v+H(c)\) has zero scalar component.
Lemma~\ref{lem:frame} expresses it as a combination of the
displayed fields, since the \(p_i\) are distinct. Applying
\(P\) recovers \(v\).

The source differential has zero scalar component and gives
\[
 df:\mathcal O_C(2)\longrightarrow\mathscr B.
\]
It is generically nonzero because \(f\) is nonconstant and
the characteristic is zero. Choose \(h\) projected fields
which, together with \(df\), form a basis at the generic point.
The resulting map in \eqref{eq:field-sequence} is generically
an isomorphism. Its kernel is zero because its source is locally
free, and its cokernel \(\mathscr R\) is torsion. This also covers
\(h=0\), when only \(df\) is needed. The chosen fields satisfy
\(\sum_iw_i\le sh+e_s\).

Twist \eqref{eq:field-sequence} by \(\mathcal O_C(-1)\).
Riemann-Roch and \eqref{eq:kernel-chi} give
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{length}_L\mathscr R
 &=\chi(\mathscr B(-1))-2+D\sum_iw_i\\
 &=\left(s+1-r+\sum_iw_i\right)D+\tau-2.
\end{align*}
Twisting preserves the length of a torsion sheaf, so this proves
\eqref{eq:RR-length}.

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Suppose that the bounded-joint-degree bad locus is dense.
Section 2 supplies a relation in a dominating family, with
\(2\le s\le k\) and \(D\ge1\). The sequence \(A_s\) is
increasing, since \(A_{s+1}-A_s=5s-2>0\), and \(A_s\ge s\).
Thus \(r\ge A_k\) meets the hypotheses of the preceding lemmas.

If \(\ell=s-1\), then \(h=2s-3\) and \(\tau=0\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:defect}. Proposition~\ref{prop:field-sequence}
gives
\begin{align*}
 0\le\operatorname{length}_L\mathscr R
 &\le\bigl(s+1-r+s(2s-3)+e_s\bigr)D-2\\
 &=(A_s-r)D-2<0.
\end{align*}
If \(\ell\le s-2\), then \(h\le2s-4\) and
\(\tau\le(s-1)D\). The same proposition gives
\begin{align*}
 0\le\operatorname{length}_L\mathscr R
 &\le\bigl(2s-r+s(2s-4)+e_s\bigr)D-2\\
 &=(A_s-1-r)D-2<0.
\end{align*}
Both cases are impossible. The closure of the bad image is
therefore proper in \(\Sigma_r\).
Let \(Z_{k,r,M}\) be the scheme-theoretic image of
\(\mathscr I\to\Sigma_r\).
Its support is this closure, since the morphism is of finite type
\cite[Tag 01R5]{stacks}. Define
\[
 U_{k,r,M}=\Sigma_r\setminus Z_{k,r,M}.
\]
The incidence and its scheme-theoretic image are defined over
\(\mathbb Q\), so \(U_{k,r,M}\) is a nonempty open in \(\mathscr A_r\).
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empty-incidence}
 \mathscr I\times_{\Sigma_r}U_{k,r,M}=\varnothing.
\end{equation}

Let \(K\) be a field of characteristic zero. Its prime subfield
is \(\mathbb Q\), and
\[
 \mathscr A_{r,K}:=\mathscr A_r\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q}\operatorname{Spec} K
       =\operatorname{Spec} K[a_0,\ldots,a_r].
\]
The base change \(U_{k,r,M,K}\subset\mathscr A_{r,K}\) is open
\cite[Tag 01JY]{stacks}. More explicitly, a principal open
\(D(g)\subset\mathscr A_r\) becomes the principal open defined by
the same polynomial \(g\) over \(K\), since
\[
 \mathbb Q[a_0,\ldots,a_r]_g\otimes_{\mathbb Q}K
       \cong K[a_0,\ldots,a_r]_g.
\]
The nonempty open \(U_{k,r,M}\) contains such a \(D(g)\) with
\(g\ne0\). Its image in \(K[a_0,\ldots,a_r]\) is nonzero,
so \(U_{k,r,M,K}\) is nonempty. It has \(K\)-points because
\(K\) is infinite.

For any extension \(L/K\), base change of
\eqref{eq:empty-incidence} gives an empty incidence over
\(U_{k,r,M,L}\). A relation over \(L\) with a composing polynomial
in \(U_{k,r,M}(K)\) would give an \(L\)-point of this
incidence. This proves the asserted exclusion over every such
extension.

It remains to show that the open meets \(\mathscr A_r^0\).
For any constant \(c\), let
\[
 (T_c\sigma)(z)=\sigma(z+c).
\]
The identity
\[
 \sum_i\lambda_i\sigma(p_i)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_i\lambda_i(T_c\sigma)(p_i-c)=0
\]
shows that the geometric bad locus is translation invariant.
The input shift preserves joint degree: it changes generated
coordinates by the invertible linear transformation
\[
 [Q:P_1:\cdots:P_k]
   \longmapsto[Q:P_1-cQ:\cdots:P_k-cQ].
\]
It also preserves distinctness and nonconstancy. Translations
preserve \(\Sigma_r\), so its bad-image closure and its good
complement are invariant.

Choose a geometric point \(\sigma\in U_{k,r,M}\), and choose a
root \(\alpha\) of \(\sigma\) over an algebraic closure.
Then \(T_\alpha\sigma\in U_{k,r,M}\) and
\((T_\alpha\sigma)(0)=0\). Consequently
\(U_{k,r,M}\cap\mathscr A_r^0\) is a nonempty relative open
defined over \(\mathbb Q\). This assertion places no origin condition
on the rational inputs. Since \(\mathscr A_r^0\cong\mathbb A^r_{\mathbb Q}\),
the same principal-open argument shows that this intersection
has a nonempty base change, with rational points, over every
characteristic-zero coefficient field.

For univariate polynomial inputs of degrees at most \(M\), the
generated map has joint degree at most \(M\). Thus
Theorem~\ref{thm:rational} gives the assertion over every field
extension, and also the nonempty origin slice.

For multivariate inputs, choose a vector \(v\in L^n\) such that
restriction along \(t\mapsto tv\) preserves the positive degrees
of every \(p_i\), and preserves every nonzero difference
\(p_i-p_j\). Such vectors form a nonempty open: for each
nonconstant polynomial or nonconstant difference, it suffices to
avoid the zero set of its highest nonzero homogeneous part; a
nonzero constant difference imposes no condition. The field \(L\)
is infinite. Restriction therefore gives distinct nonconstant
univariate inputs of degree at most \(M\). Any scalar relation
among the original compositions restricts to the same scalar
relation among these compositions, which is impossible.

This line passes through the origin, so it also preserves
\(p_i(0)=0\). Moreover, the ordinary separation property on
\(U_{k,r,M}\cap\mathscr A_r^0\) already applies to such inputs;
there is no need to preserve their origin values during the
translation argument for the composing polynomial.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The proof bounds the degree threshold independently of \(M\), but
does not bound the degree of a relation at an individual exceptional
composing polynomial. Properness of each closed set
\(Z_{k,r,M}\) does not imply properness of the closure of their
union. The distinction between an open for each finite \(M\)
and one open for all input degrees is therefore retained.
