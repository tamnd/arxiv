\label{main3}
For $\lambda>{\rm e}^{\pi}$, the quaternionic Hopf surface $H_\lambda$ admits a hyperhermitian metric $h$ satisfying the soliton equation 
\begin{equation}
{\rm Ric}_I(h)^{+}=h-\mathcal{L}_{V}(h)\,,\quad  \mathcal{L}_{V}(h)\neq 0\,, 
\end{equation}
where $V=-\frac12 \theta^{\sharp}$, and $\theta$ is the Lee form of $g_H$. 
Moreover, if $g_t$ is a long-time solution of \eqref{nflow} on $H_\lambda$ such that 
$$
c=\frac{2\,\mathrm{Vol}(g_H)}{\displaystyle\int_{H_\lambda}\mathrm{tr}_{g_H}g_0\,d\mu_{g_H}}\,,\quad  \mathcal{L}_V(g_0)=0\,,
$$ 
and satisfying one of the following two conditions
\begin{enumerate}
\item[1.] $C^{-1}g_H\leq g_t\leq C g_H$ for every $t\in [0,\infty)$ and a uniform constant $C$;

\vspace{0.1cm}
\item[2.] $\mathcal{L}_{IV}g_0=0$;
\end{enumerate}
then $g_t$ converges smoothly to an HKT--Einstein metric as $t\to \infty$.

The metric $g$ satisfies the HKT--Einstein equation

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
In \cite{OPS03} it is proved that $g$ is an HKT metric with potential $1$-form $n\tilde \theta$, in other words
\[
\omega=\frac{n}{2}\left(d(I\tilde \theta)+d^c_J(K\tilde \theta)\right)\,.
\]
In our conventions, \cite[Proposition 2.6(b)]{FG26} says
\[
dI\theta=\frac{1}{2n-1}\mathrm{Ric}_I(\omega)\,,
\]
where $\theta$ is the Lee form of $g$. We claim that $\theta=\frac{n}{2n-1}\tilde \theta$. Assuming the claim we deduce
\[
\omega=\frac{2n-1}{2}\left(d(I \theta)+d^c_J(K\theta)\right)
=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{Ric}_I(\omega)-J\mathrm{Ric}_I(\omega)\right)
=\mathrm{Ric}_I(\omega)^-\,,
\]
as desired, so it only remains to prove that $\theta=\frac{n}{2n-1}\tilde \theta$. From the construction of $g$ it is not difficult to see that
\[
\Omega^{n}=\frac{n^n}{2}\tilde \Omega^{n}\,,
\]
therefore, we deduce $\alpha_{\Omega}=\alpha_{\tilde \Omega}$, where $\alpha_\Omega$ is the $(1,0)$-form such that $\partial \bar \Omega^n=\alpha_\Omega \wedge \bar \Omega^n$ and analogously for $\alpha_{\tilde \Omega}$. On the other hand, since $\Omega$ is HKT $\alpha_{\Omega}=(2n-1)\theta^{1,0}$ (see \cite[Lemma 2.2]{BGV23}), whereas locally $\tilde \Omega=\mathrm{e}^f\hat \Omega$, where $\hat \Omega$ is the $(2,0)$ form associated to a local hyperk\"ahler metric, hence
\[
\alpha_{\tilde \Omega}\wedge \bar{\tilde \Omega}^n
=\partial \bar{\tilde \Omega}^n
=\partial (\mathrm{e}^{nf}\bar{\hat \Omega}^n)
=n\partial f \wedge \mathrm{e}^{nf}\bar{\hat \Omega}^n
=n\partial f \wedge \bar{\tilde \Omega}^n
\]
implying $\alpha_{\tilde \Omega}=n\partial f=n\tilde \theta^{1,0}$.

<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{Gauduchon}
For every hyperhermitian metric $h$ on $(U,I,J,K)$ we have
\[
\Delta_h f+|\partial f|_h^2\leq 0\,.
\]
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The conformal change formula for the Levi-Civita connection is
\[
\nabla^{\tilde g}_X Y
=
\nabla^{\hat g}_X Y
+\frac12\bigl(
df(X)Y+df(Y)X
-\hat g(X,Y)\mathrm{grad}_{\hat g}f
\bigr).
\]
Since the Lee form $\tilde \theta=df$ is parallel, we have $\nabla^{\tilde g}df=0$. It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{Hessian}
\mathrm{Hess}_{\hat g}f
=
df\otimes df-\frac12|df|_{\hat g}^2\hat g\,.
\end{equation}
Fix a point and choose a $\hat g$-orthonormal frame $e_i, Ie_i,Je_i, Ke_i$, $i=1,\ldots,n$, adapted to $(I,J,K)$ which diagonalizes $h$ relative to $\hat g$.
By hyperhermitianity, the eigenvalue of $h$ is the same on the four vectors of each block; denote it by $\lambda_i>0$. Set
\[
r_i=
df(e_i)^2+df(Ie_i)^2+df(Je_i)^2+df(Ke_i)^2\,.
\]
Tracing \eqref{Hessian} gives
\[
\begin{split}
\mathrm{tr}_{h}\mathrm{Hess}_{\hat g}f&=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{1}{\lambda_i}\left(
\mathrm{Hess}_{\hat g}f(e_i,e_i)
+\mathrm{Hess}_{\hat g}f(Ie_i,Ie_i)+
\mathrm{Hess}_{\hat g}f(Je_i,Je_i)
+\mathrm{Hess}_{\hat g}f(Ke_i,Ke_i)\right)
\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{r_i-2|df|_{\hat g}^2}{\lambda_i}\,.
\end{split}
\]
The associated $\hat g$-unitary frame of type $(1,0)$ is
\[
Z_{2i-1}=\frac{e_i-\mathrm{i}Ie_i}{\sqrt{2}},
\qquad
Z_{2i}=\frac{Je_i-\mathrm{i}Ke_i}{\sqrt{2}}.
\]
Extend $\mathrm{Hess}_{\hat g}f$ complex-bilinearly to $TU\otimes \mathbb{C}$. Since $\hat g$ is K\"ahler with respect to $I$, for every $Z,W\in T_I^{1,0}U$ we have $ (\partial\bar\partial f)(Z,\bar W) = \mathrm{Hess}_{\hat g}f(Z,\bar W) $. Hence
\[
\Delta_h f =\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{r_i-2|df|_{\hat g}^2}{\lambda_i}, \qquad
|\partial f|_h^2 =\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{r_i}{\lambda_i}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Delta_h f+|\partial f|_h^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n\frac{r_i-|df|_{\hat g}^2}{\lambda_i} = -\sum_{i=1}^n
\frac{\sum_{j\neq i}r_j}{\lambda_i}
\leq 0\,. \qedhere 
\]

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{Alesker}
Let $\hat g,h$ be hyperhermitian metrics on $(U,I,J,K)$, where $h$ is HKT. Take $I$-holomorphic coordinates $(z^1,\ldots,z^{2n})$ around a point $p\in M$ satisfying the following assumptions: 
\begin{itemize}
\item $J_p$ takes its standard form with respect to $(z^1,\ldots,z^{2n})$;


\vspace{0.1cm}
\item $J_{i,k}^{\bar s}(p)=J_{i,\bar k}^{\bar s}(p)=J_{\bar j,k}^r(p)=J_{\bar j, \bar k}^r(p)=0$;


\vspace{0.1cm}
\item $
\hat g_{i\bar j}(p)=\delta_{ij}$, $
h_{i\bar j}(p)=\lambda_i\delta_{ij}$, $
\lambda_{2a-1}=\lambda_{2a}>0$.
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
\sum_{i,k=1}^{2n}
\frac{|h_{k\bar k,i}|^2}{\lambda_i\lambda_k}
\leq
2\sum_{i,j,r=1}^{2n}
\frac{|h_{i\bar j,r}|^2}{\lambda_i\lambda_j} \quad \mbox{ at }p\,.
\]
Consequently, if $\hat{g}$ is hyperk\"ahler
\[
\frac{|\partial\mathrm{tr}_{\hat g}h|_h^2}{\mathrm{tr}_{\hat g}h}
\leq
2\hat g^{r\bar s}h^{i\bar b}h^{a\bar j}
(\nabla^{\hat g}_{\bar s}h)_{a\bar b}
(\nabla^{\hat g}_r h)_{i\bar j}  \quad \mbox{ at }p\,.
\]

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since the Obata connections is torsion-free, we can take $I$-holomorphic geodesic coordinates for it around $p$, then we may assume that these coordinates satisfy the assumptions of the proposition, see e.g. \cite[Remark 2.19]{DS23}. Hyperhermitianity of $h$ implies, at $p$,
\[
h_{i\bar j}
=J_i^{\bar s}J_{\bar j}^r h_{r \bar s},
\qquad
h_{i\bar j,k}
=J_i^{\bar s}J_{\bar j}^r h_{r \bar s,k}\,.
\]
                                        
    
                                  
        
                                    
    
Let $\Omega$ be the $(2,0)$-form associated with $h$. Since $h$ is HKT, we obtain at $p$
\[
0=(\partial \Omega)_{ij\ell}=\Omega_{ij,\ell}+\Omega_{j\ell,i}+\Omega_{\ell i,j}=(h_{i\bar q}J^{\bar q}_j)_{,\ell}+(h_{j\bar q}J^{\bar q}_\ell)_{,i}+(h_{\ell\bar q}J^{\bar q}_i)_{,j}=h_{i\bar q,\ell}J^{\bar q}_j+h_{j\bar q,i}J^{\bar q}_\ell+h_{\ell\bar q,j}J^{\bar q}_i\,.
\]
Now, multiplying by $J^\ell_{\bar k}$ and choosing $j=k$ we obtain, in particular
\[
h_{k\bar k,i}
=
h_{i\bar q,\ell}J^{\bar q}_kJ^{\ell}_{\bar k}+h_{i\bar k,k}\qquad \mbox{at }p\,.
\]
    
               
   
                                
    
               
    
                   
      
         
                    
                     
         
    

Write
\[
B_b=\{2b-1,2b\},
\qquad
\mu_b=\lambda_{2b-1}=\lambda_{2b},
\qquad b=1,\ldots,n\,,
\]
and set $k=s_b:=2b-1$. We then get
\[
|h_{s_b\bar s_b,i}|^2
\leq
2\left(
|h_{i\bar q,\ell}J^{\bar q}_{s_b}J^{\ell}_{\overline{s_b}}|^2+|h_{i\bar s_b,s_b}|^2\right)=2\sum_{j\in B_b}
|h_{i\bar j,j}|^2\qquad \mbox{at }p\,.
\]
Now, observe that, for $a\neq b$ after relabelling the indices
\[
\sum_{\substack{i\in B_a\\j\in B_b}}
|h_{j\bar i,i}|^2=
\sum_{\substack{i\in B_a\\j\in B_b}}
|h_{p\bar q,i}J^p_{\bar i}J^{\bar q}_j|^2=\sum_{\substack{i\in B_a\\j\in B_b}}
|h_{i\bar j, r}J^{ r}_{\bar i}|^2\qquad \mbox{at }p\,.
\]
We then infer
\[
\sum_{\substack{i\in B_a}}|h_{s_b\bar s_b,i}|^2+\sum_{\substack{j\in B_b}}|h_{s_a\bar s_a,j}|^2\leq 2\sum_{\substack{i\in B_a\\j\in B_b}}
(|h_{i\bar j,j}|^2+
|h_{j\bar i,i}|^2)
\leq
2\sum_{r=1}^{2n}\sum_{\substack{i\in B_a\\j\in B_b}}
|h_{i\bar j,r}|^2\qquad \mbox{at }p\,.
\]
Since the left-hand side is symmetric in $a$ and $b$ we deduce
\begin{equation}\label{eq:X}
\sum_{\substack{i\in B_a}}|h_{s_b\bar s_b,i}|^2+\sum_{\substack{j\in B_b}}|h_{s_a\bar s_a,j}|^2\leq \sum_{r=1}^{2n}\left( \sum_{\substack{i\in B_a\\j\in B_b}}
|h_{i\bar j,r}|^2+\sum_{\substack{i\in B_b\\j\in B_a}}
|h_{i\bar j,r}|^2\right)\qquad \mbox{at }p\,.
\end{equation}
On the other hand, for $a=b$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Y}
\sum_{i\in B_a}|h_{s_a\bar s_a,i}|^2\leq \sum_{r=1}^{2n}|h_{s_a\bar s_a,r}|^2=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{2n} \sum_{i,j\in B_a}|h_{i\bar j,r}|^2\qquad \mbox{at }p\,.
\end{equation}
We then deduce, using \eqref{eq:X} and \eqref{eq:Y}, at $p$
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i,k=1}^{2n}
\frac{|h_{k\bar k,i}|^2}{\lambda_i\lambda_k}
&=2\sum_{a=1}^n\sum_{i=1}^{2n}
\frac{|h_{s_a\bar s_a,i}|^2}{\lambda_i\mu_a}\\
&=
2\sum_{a=1}^n\frac{1}{\mu_a^2}\sum_{i\in B_a}|h_{s_a\bar s_a,i}|^2
+
2\sum_{a<b}\frac{1}{\mu_a\mu_b}\left(\sum_{\substack{i\in B_a}}|h_{s_b\bar s_b,i}|^2+\sum_{\substack{j\in B_b}}|h_{s_a\bar s_a,j}|^2\right)\\
&\leq
\sum_{a=1}^n\frac{1}{\mu_a^2}\sum_{r=1}^{2n} \sum_{i,j\in B_a}|h_{i\bar j,r}|^2
+
2\sum_{a<b}\frac{1}{\mu_a\mu_b}\sum_{r=1}^{2n}\left( \sum_{\substack{i\in B_a\\j\in B_b}}
|h_{i\bar j,r}|^2+\sum_{\substack{i\in B_b\\j\in B_a}}
|h_{i\bar j,r}|^2\right)\\
&\leq
2\sum_{a,b=1}^n\frac{1}{\mu_a\mu_b}\sum_{r=1}^{2n}\sum_{\substack{i\in B_a\\j\in B_b}}
|h_{i\bar j,r}|^2=
2\sum_{i,j,r=1}^{2n}
\frac{|h_{i\bar j,r}|^2}{\lambda_i\lambda_j}\,.
\end{aligned}
\]
                                 
    
        
   
                                   
                
    
     
    
        
   
                                      
                    
        
        
   
                                      
                                   
    
                              
    
                     
    
                                                             
      
    
               
    
                                            
    
         
                                      
                    
                                      
                                                          
                     
    
                                                       
                 
    
                           
    
               
    
                            
    
                                                              
    
        
   
                                   
               
                
    
                                                          
                        
    
                 
                   
                                              
    
                               
   
                                               
       
                              
   
                                          
                                       
                      
                                               
               
    
This proves the first assertion. 

Finally, when $\hat g$ is hyperk\"ahler its first derivatives vanish at $p$, and weighted Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\partial\mathrm{tr}_{\hat g}h|_h^2=
\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\frac1{\lambda_i}
\left|\sum_{k=1}^{2n} h_{k\bar k,i}\right|^2=
\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\frac1{\lambda_i}
\left|\sum_{k=1}^{2n} \sqrt{\lambda_k} \frac{h_{k\bar k,i}}{\sqrt \lambda_k}\right|^2\leq
\left(\sum_{k=1}^{2n}\lambda_k\right)
\sum_{i,k=1}^{2n}
\frac{|h_{k\bar k,i}|^2}{\lambda_i\lambda_k}\,.
\end{aligned}
\]
Now the second assertion follows from the first and the fact that at $p$, partial and covariant first derivatives with respect
to $\hat g$ agree, so that
\[
\sum_{i,j,r}
\frac{|h_{i\bar j,r}|^2}{\lambda_i\lambda_j}
=
\hat g^{r\bar s}h^{i\bar b}h^{a\bar j}
(\nabla^{\hat g}_{\bar s}h)_{a\bar b}
(\nabla^{\hat g}_r h)_{i\bar j}\,. \qedhere
\]

\label{rem}
{\em Our result does not naturally extend to the \lq\lq general\rq\rq  \,case without the assumption of the existence of a background LCHK metric. For instance the crucial inequality \eqref{B-team} does not hold in general if we replace the hyperk\"ahler metric with an HKT metric. A counterexample is the following: 

Work on a neighbourhood of the origin in
$\mathbb H^2\simeq\mathbb C^4$ and set 
\[
a(z)=|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2\,,
\qquad
b(z)=|z_3|^2+|z_4|^2\,.
\]
Consider the background metric
\[
\omega=\omega_0+\left(i \partial \bar \partial(a(z)b(z))\right)^-\,,
\]
where $\omega_0$ is the standard metric on $\mathbb{C}^4$. In our conventions
$$
Jd\bar z_1=dz_2,\qquad Jd\bar z_2=-dz_1,\qquad Jd\bar z_3=dz_4,\qquad Jd\bar z_4=-dz_3\,,
$$
and so, the matrix associated to $\omega$ is 
\[
\mathrm{Id}+ \begin{pmatrix} b(z)&0& \dfrac{\bar z_1z_3+\bar z_4z_2}{2}& \dfrac{\bar z_1z_4-\bar z_3z_2}{2}\\[3mm] 0&b(z)& \dfrac{\bar z_2z_3-\bar z_4z_1}{2}& \dfrac{\bar z_2z_4+\bar z_3z_1}{2}\\[3mm] \dfrac{\bar z_3z_1+\bar z_2z_4}{2}& \dfrac{\bar z_3z_2-\bar z_1z_4}{2}& a(z)&0\\[3mm] \dfrac{\bar z_4z_1-\bar z_2z_3}{2}& \dfrac{\bar z_4z_2+\bar z_1z_3}{2}& 0&a(z)\end{pmatrix}
\]
which is positive definite on a sufficiently small neighbourhood of the origin. Clearly, at the origin $g_{i\bar j}=\delta_{i\bar j} $ and $ g_{i\bar j,k}=0 $.

For $t>1$, define
\[
\varphi_t=(t-1)a(z)+(t^{-1}-1)b(z)-a(z)b(z).
\]
Then $ \omega_{\varphi_t} := \omega+(i\partial \bar \partial \varphi_t)^- $ has the matrix expression $
\mathrm{diag}(t,t,t^{-1},t^{-1})$, which is positive definite. In particular, since $\det(g_{\varphi_t})=1$ is constant in $t$, writing $F=-\frac12\log\det(g_{i\bar j})$, the associated $(2,0)$ form $\Omega_{\varphi_t}$ solves
\[
\Omega_{\varphi_t}^{2}=\mathrm{e}^F\Omega^2\,, \qquad \text{for every }t>1\,,
\]
where $\Omega$ is the $(2,0)$ form associated to $g$. At the origin,
\[
\mathrm{tr}_g g_{\varphi_t}
=\mathrm{tr}_{g_{\varphi_t}}g
=2(t+t^{-1})\,, \qquad \partial\mathrm{tr}_g g_{\varphi_t}=0\,, \qquad \Delta_{\varphi_t}\mathrm{tr}_g g_{\varphi_t} = -4(t^2+t^{-2})\,.
\]
Consequently, at the origin
\[
\frac{\Delta_{\varphi_t}\mathrm{tr}_g g_{\varphi_t}}{\mathrm{tr}_g g_{\varphi_t}} =-2\frac{t^2+t^{-2}}{t+t^{-1}} \longrightarrow-\infty \qquad\text{as }t\to\infty\,,
\]
whereas
\[
\frac{1+\mathrm{tr}_{g_{\varphi_t}}g}{\mathrm{tr}_g g_{\varphi_t}}
= 1+\frac{1}{2(t+t^{-1})}
\]
remains uniformly bounded, thus violating the inequality \eqref{B-team}.
}

{\em The standard metric $g_H$ is, up to rescaling, the unique
HKT--Einstein metric on $H_\lambda$ invariant under
both $V$ and $IV$. Indeed, write such a metric as
${\rm e}^f g_H$. The invariance implies that $f$ descends
to the round unit sphere $S^2$, and the HKT--Einstein equation
${\rm Ric}_I({\rm e}^f g_H)^+=c\,{\rm e}^f g_H$ becomes
\[
\Delta_{S^2}f+c\,{\rm e}^f=1.
\]
Integration shows that $c>0$, so, after rescaling the metric,
we may assume $c=1$. Then
$\int_{S^2}{\rm e}^f\,dA=4\pi$, and
\cite[Theorem 1.1]{Lin00}, applied with $\alpha=4\pi$,
$\gamma=0$, and $\varphi=(f-\bar f)/(4\pi)$, where $\bar f$
denotes the average of $f$, implies that $f$ is constant.
The equation then yields $f\equiv0$.}
