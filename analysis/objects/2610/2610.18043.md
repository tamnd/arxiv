\protect\hypertarget{prp-profile-equiv}{}\label{prp-profile-equiv}

(First-update profile equivalence.) Let \(F\) be twice continuously
differentiable and strictly convex in \(\beta_0\) for every \(\beta\),
and let \(\tilde F(\beta) = \min_{\beta_0} F(\beta_0, \beta)\). Suppose
the exact strategy has driven \(\beta_0\) to the conditional minimizer
\(\beta_0^{(t)} = \arg\min_{\beta_0} F(\beta_0, \beta^{(t)})\), and
suppose \(H_{00} > 0\) and \(\tilde H_{jj} > 0\) there. Then the
\emph{first} coefficient update of the next pass uses the gradient
\(\partial_j F(\beta_0^{(t)}, \beta^{(t)}) = \partial_j \tilde F(\beta^{(t)})\),
so its update direction agrees with the corresponding coordinate
direction for \(\tilde F\) at \(\beta^{(t)}\). Its diagonal step size
remains \(1/H_{jj}\). Because \(\tilde H_{jj} \leq H_{jj}\) by
Equation~\ref{eq-schur-diag}, this step size is no larger than
\(1/\tilde H_{jj}\), the step size for \(\tilde F\)'s local quadratic
model along \(e_j\).

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let \(\beta_0^\star(\beta)\) be the conditional minimizer near
\(\beta^{(t)}\). It exists at \(\beta^{(t)}\) by assumption and is
unique by strict convexity. Since \(H_{00} > 0\) there, the implicit
function theorem applied to \(\partial_0 F = 0\) makes \(\beta_0^\star\)
differentiable locally. Thus
\(\tilde F(\beta) = F(\beta_0^\star(\beta), \beta)\) near
\(\beta^{(t)}\). Differentiating through \(\beta_0^\star\) and using the
stationarity condition
\(\partial_0 F(\beta_0^\star(\beta), \beta) = 0\), \[
  \partial_j \tilde F(\beta)
  = \partial_j F(\beta_0^\star(\beta), \beta)
    + \partial_0 F\big(\beta_0^\star(\beta), \beta\big)\partial_j \beta_0^\star(\beta)
  = \partial_j F\big(\beta_0^\star(\beta), \beta\big).
\] At the conditional minimizer, \(\partial_0 F = 0\), so the
intercept's dependence on \(\beta\) contributes nothing to the
derivative. Evaluating at \(\beta_0^{(t)} = \beta_0^\star(\beta^{(t)})\)
gives the gradient equality. For the step size,
\(\tilde H_{jj} = H_{jj}(1 - \rho_{0j}^2) \leq H_{jj}\), so
\(1/H_{jj} \leq 1/\tilde H_{jj}\)~(Equation~\ref{eq-schur-diag}). The
update therefore uses a step size no larger than the profiled local
model's \(1/\tilde H_{jj}\). This local comparison alone does not
guarantee descent of \(\tilde F\) after a finite update.

\protect\hypertarget{prp-frozen-block-rate}{}\label{prp-frozen-block-rate}

\emph{(Frozen two-block rate.)} Let \(q = H_{00}/L_0 \in (0,1]\). On the
frozen quadratic, suppose \(H\) is positive definite and each pass first
minimizes over the active coefficient block and then updates the
intercept. The Newton and gradient strategies contract the intercept
error by \[
  r_{\mathrm{N}} = \kappa, \qquad r_{\mathrm{G}} = 1 - q(1 - \kappa),
\] respectively. Exact coefficient-block minimization erases the
incoming coefficient error, so the only potentially nonzero eigenvalue
of each pass is its intercept contraction factor. For fixed
\(0 < \kappa < 1\) and nonzero initial intercept error, let
\(T_{\mathrm{G}}(\varepsilon)\) and \(T_{\mathrm{N}}(\varepsilon)\)
count passes to reduce its magnitude by a factor \(\varepsilon\). Then
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-frozen-block-rate}{
  \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \frac{T_{\mathrm{G}}(\varepsilon)}{T_{\mathrm{N}}(\varepsilon)}
  = \frac{\log \kappa}{\log \left( 1 - q(1 - \kappa) \right)}
  \longrightarrow \frac{1}{q}
  = \frac{L_0}{H_{00}} \quad \text{as } \kappa \to 1.
}\end{equation} For fixed \(q < 1\), this asymptotic ratio diverges as
\(\kappa \to 0\). At a fixed tolerance, however, Newton needs only one
pass once \(\kappa \leq \varepsilon\), and the gradient pass count stays
finite. These are different orders of limits. At \(\kappa = 0\), Newton
solves the decoupled quadratic in one pass. When \(q = 1\), the two
strategies coincide for every \(\kappa\).
