<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:product_haar_povm_covariance}
For every \(W\in U(d)\),
\begin{equation}
    E^{(n)}(d(W\vec u))
    =
    W^{\otimes n}E^{(n)}(d\vec u)W^{\otimes n\dagger},
    \label{eq:product_haar_povm_covariance}
\end{equation}
where \(W\vec u=(Wu_1,\ldots,Wu_n)\).  Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
    p_{W\phi}^{(n)}(W\vec u)
    =
    p_\phi^{(n)}(\vec u)
    \label{eq:product_haar_likelihood_covariance}
\end{equation}
with respect to the product Haar measure.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For one copy, Haar invariance gives \(d(Wu)=du\), and
\(\lvert Wu\rangle\langle Wu\rvert=W\lvert u\rangle\langle u\rvert W^\dagger\).  Hence
\begin{equation*}
    E(d(Wu))
    =
    W E(du) W^\dagger.
\end{equation*}
Taking tensor products over the \(n\) independent copies gives
\eqref{eq:product_haar_povm_covariance}.  The likelihood covariance
\eqref{eq:product_haar_likelihood_covariance} follows immediately from
\eqref{eq:local_product_haar_likelihood_density} and the unitary invariance of
inner products.

<!-- fact-1: body_md -->
\label{fact:part_swap}
When taking the partial trace over a subsystem $k$ of an $n$-partite operator $A$ multiplied by a transposition $S_{k,n+1}$, the SWAP operator effectively substitutes the external $(n+1)$-th basis states directly into the $k$-th tensor slot of $A$. Formally, we have,
\begin{align*}
&\langle x | \operatorname{Tr}_{1:n} \left[ S_{k,n+1} (A \otimes I_{n+1})  \right] | y \rangle\\
 =& \sum_{j_{\setminus k}} \langle j_1 \dots x \dots j_n | A | j_1 \dots y \dots j_n \rangle,
\end{align*}
where $x$ and $y$ are inserted exactly at the $k$-th position of the bra and ket, respectively, and the sum runs over all basis indices except the $k$-th.
<!-- end fact-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
See Appendix \ref{proof_fact:part_swap} for the proof.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:m_k_perm}
For a joint measurement operator $O_n = |u_1 \dots u_n\rangle\langle u_1 \dots u_n|$ and the fully symmetric projector $\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}^{(n)}$, the cross-correlation operator $K_{k,\vec{u}}$ can be expressed in terms of the permanents of the minors of the Gram matrix $G$ (where $G_{a,b} = \langle u_a | u_b \rangle$),
\begin{equation*}
K_{k,\vec{u}} = \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{j=1}^n \operatorname{Perm}(G(k|j)) |u_j\rangle\langle u_k|,
\end{equation*}
where $G(k|j)$ denotes the minor of $G$ obtained by completely removing the $k$-th row and $j$-th column.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
See Appendix \ref{proof_lem:m_k_perm} for the proof.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
After compression of the product Haar-POVM outcome effect to the symmetric
subspace, the arbitrary-effect identity of the companion paper~\cite{CompanionGlobal} gives the abstract
form
\[
    \hat\rho_n(\vec u)=\frac{I+n\rho_{E_{\vec u}}}{n+d}.
\]
The calculation in this section identifies the normalized one-particle marginal
\(\rho_{E_{\vec u}}\) explicitly as
\(\widetilde\rho_{\mathrm{marg}}(\vec u)\).  Thus the abstract identity
and the local permanent calculation have distinct roles: the former determines
the universal identity-regularized form, whereas the latter evaluates the
outcome-dependent marginal itself.  The Gram-minor permanent coefficients are the concrete product-Haar-POVM
realization of this general effect marginal.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:marginal_psd}
The fully summed cross-correlation operator $K_{\vec{u}}$ is a positive semi-definite operator. Consequently, its trace-normalized counterpart $\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{marg}}(\vec{u})$ satisfies the requirements of a valid quantum density operator.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Recall the explicit expression
\begin{equation}
    K_{\vec u}
    =
    \frac{1}{n!}
    \sum_{k=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^n
    \operatorname{Perm}(G(k\mid j))
    \lvert u_j\rangle\langle u_k\rvert.
    \label{eq:psd_proof_K_explicit_recall}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
    V:
    \mathbb C^n\to\mathcal H,
    \qquad
    V\lvert e_i\rangle=\lvert u_i\rangle,
    \label{eq:psd_proof_synthesis_map}
\end{equation}
where \(\{\lvert e_i\rangle\}_{i=1}^n\) is the standard basis of \(\mathbb C^n\).
Define the permanent-weight matrix \(C_{\vec u}\) on \(\mathbb C^n\) by
\begin{equation}
    (C_{\vec u})_{j,k}
    :=
    \frac{1}{n!}\operatorname{Perm}(G(k\mid j)).
    \label{eq:psd_proof_C_def}
\end{equation}
Then \eqref{eq:psd_proof_K_explicit_recall} is exactly the factorization
\begin{equation}
    K_{\vec u}
    =
    V C_{\vec u} V^\dagger .
    \label{eq:psd_proof_K_factorization}
\end{equation}
It remains to show that \(C_{\vec u}\ge0\).

For each \(j\), let
\begin{equation}
    \lvert U_{\setminus j}\rangle
    :=
    \bigotimes_{\substack{i=1\\ i\ne j}}^n \lvert u_i\rangle,
    \label{eq:psd_proof_leave_one_out_vector}
\end{equation}
where the tensor factors are kept in their original order after deleting the
\(j\)-th entry.  Set
\begin{equation}
    \lvert W_j\rangle
    :=
    \Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}^{(n-1)}\lvert U_{\setminus j}\rangle
    \in \mathcal H^{\otimes(n-1)}.
    \label{eq:psd_proof_projected_leave_one_out}
\end{equation}
Consider the Gram matrix
\begin{equation}
    L_{j,k}
    :=
    \langle W_k\mid W_j\rangle.
    \label{eq:psd_proof_L_def}
\end{equation}
By construction, \(L\) is positive semidefinite.  Expanding the symmetric
projection gives
\begin{align}
    L_{j,k}
    &=
    \left\langle
        U_{\setminus k}
    \middle|
        \Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}^{(n-1)}
    \middle|
        U_{\setminus j}
    \right\rangle 
    \notag\\
    &=
    \frac{1}{(n-1)!}
    \sum_{\substack{\sigma\in S_n:\\ \sigma(k)=j}}
    \prod_{m\ne k}\langle u_m\mid u_{\sigma(m)}\rangle
    \notag\\
    &=
    \frac{1}{(n-1)!}\operatorname{Perm}(G(k\mid j)).
    \label{eq:psd_proof_L_perm_identity}
\end{align}
Here the second line identifies a permutation of the reduced ordered lists with
a permutation \(\sigma\in S_n\) satisfying \(\sigma(k)=j\).  Comparing
\eqref{eq:psd_proof_C_def} and \eqref{eq:psd_proof_L_perm_identity}, we obtain
\begin{equation}
    C_{\vec u}
    =
    \frac{1}{n}L.
    \label{eq:psd_proof_C_equals_L_over_n}
\end{equation}
Therefore \(C_{\vec u}\ge0\).  The factorization
\eqref{eq:psd_proof_K_factorization} then implies \(K_{\vec u}\ge0\).
Finally,
\[
    \widetilde\rho_{\mathrm{marg}}(\vec u)
    =
    \frac{K_{\vec u}}{n\operatorname{Tr}[O_n\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}^{(n)}]}
\]
has unit trace by \eqref{eq:tr_K_u}.  Hence it is a positive semidefinite
trace-one operator, i.e. a density operator.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:pointwise_covariance_quantum_local}
Let \(W\in U(d)\) and write \(W\vec u=(Wu_1,\ldots,Wu_n)\).  Then
\begin{equation}
    \hat\rho_n(W\vec u)
    =
    W\hat\rho_n(\vec u)W^\dagger.
    \label{eq:pointwise_covariance_quantum_local}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The Gram matrix of \(W\vec u\) has entries
\(\langle Wu_a\mid Wu_b\rangle=\langle u_a\mid u_b\rangle\), and hence is the
same as the Gram matrix of \(\vec u\).  Therefore all permanent weights in
Lemma~\ref{lem:m_k_perm} are unchanged by the simultaneous rotation of all
outcomes.  The rank-one operators transform as
\begin{equation*}
    \lvert Wu_j\rangle\langle Wu_k\rvert
    =
    W\lvert u_j\rangle\langle u_k\rvert W^\dagger.
\end{equation*}
Thus \(K_{W\vec u}=WK_{\vec u}W^\dagger\), and the normalization factor
\(\operatorname{Tr}[O_n\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}^{(n)}]=\operatorname{Perm}(G)/n!\) is unchanged.  Hence
\begin{equation*}
    \widetilde\rho_{\mathrm{marg}}(W\vec u)
    =
    W\widetilde\rho_{\mathrm{marg}}(\vec u)W^\dagger.
\end{equation*}
Substituting this into
\(\hat\rho_n=(n\widetilde\rho_{\mathrm{marg}}+I)/(n+d)\) gives
\eqref{eq:pointwise_covariance_quantum_local}.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:local_posterior_mean_bayes_act}
For product Haar-POVM data and the Haar prior, the unique Bayes act for quantum
relative-entropy loss is the posterior mean
\begin{equation}
    \hat\rho_n(\vec u)
    =
    \mathbb E[\rho_\Phi\mid\vec u].
    \label{eq:local_posterior_mean_bayes_act}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, it has the identity-regularized posterior form
\begin{equation}
    \hat\rho_n(\vec u)
    =
    \frac{n\widetilde\rho_{\mathrm{marg}}(\vec u)+I}{n+d}.
    \label{eq:posterior_mean_evaluated_as_quantum_local_main}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The Bayes-act statement is the fixed-observation Bayes identity in Section~\ref{sec:bayes_tools_p2}
applied to the product Haar-POVM \(E^{(n)}\), with the outcome \(x\) equal to
the complete sequence \(\vec u\).  The additional content here is the explicit
Haar-integral and one-particle-marginal calculation, which evaluates that
posterior mean and gives exactly
\eqref{eq:posterior_mean_evaluated_as_quantum_local_main}.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:local_decision_rule_minimax_product_haar_povm_data}
\label{thm:product_haar_povm_minimax_optimality}
Within the class \(\mathcal D_n\) of decision rules based on product
Haar-POVM outcomes, the local posterior predictive state is
minimax for quantum relative-entropy loss:
\begin{equation}
    \sup_\psi R_{\hat\rho_n}(\psi)
    =
    \inf_{\delta\in\mathcal D_n}
    \sup_\psi R_\delta(\psi).
    \label{eq:product_haar_povm_minimax_optimality}
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:product_haar_povm_covariance}, the product Haar-POVM model is
covariant.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:pointwise_covariance_quantum_local}, the local posterior predictive state is pointwise covariant.  Proposition~\ref{prop:local_posterior_mean_bayes_act}
shows that it attains the Haar-Bayes value among all decision rules in
\(\mathcal D_n\).

The minimax conclusion is the specialization to decision rules based on the product Haar-POVM outcomes of the
Hunt--Stein averaging argument in the compact-group averaging argument: for a
covariant observation model and a covariant decision rule, the frequentist risk
is constant on the unitary orbit of pure states, hence its Haar-Bayes risk equals
its worst-case risk.  Therefore the Haar-Bayes value and the minimax value
coincide within \(\mathcal D_n\), and the local posterior predictive state is
decision-rule minimax.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The corollary compares only decision rules that map the observed product Haar-POVM outcome sequence to a density matrix.  The product measurement is specified in advance.  Thus this is not a minimax statement over all quantum
measurements on \(\rho_\psi^{\otimes n}\), and it does not include collective
measurements or other POVMs in the optimization.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:finite_sample_complexity}
Let $d\ge2$ and $0<\epsilon\le1$, and set $C_d=d^2+d-2$.  If
\begin{equation}
 n\ge \frac{4C_d}{\epsilon}
 \log\!\left(\frac{4C_d(d+1)}{\epsilon}\right).
\label{eq:explicit_finite_sample_condition}
\end{equation}
then $R_{n,d}^{\rm loc}\le\epsilon$.  In particular, this gives the rigorous
finite-sample scaling
\begin{equation}
 n=O\!\left(\frac{d^2}{\epsilon}
 \log\frac{d}{\epsilon}\right).
 \label{eq:quadratic_d_sample_complexity}
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Since $(n+d)/(n+d-1)\le2$, it is enough that
$2C_d\log(n+d)/n\le\epsilon$.  Put
$A=2C_d/\epsilon$.  The right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:explicit_finite_sample_condition} is $2A\log(2A(d+1))$.
Let $n_0=2A\log(2A(d+1))$.  Since $A\ge4$ and $d\ge2$, we have
$n_0\ge A$.  The function
$x\mapsto x/A-\log(x+d)$ is increasing for $x\ge A$, because its derivative
is $A^{-1}-(x+d)^{-1}>0$.  Moreover,
\begin{align*}
 \frac{n_0}{A}=2\log(2A(d+1))
& \ge \log\bigl(2A\log(2A(d+1))+d\bigr)\notag\\
 &=\log(n_0+d),
\end{align*}
where the inequality follows from
$2A(d+1)^2\ge 2A\log(2A(d+1))+d$, valid for $A\ge4$ and $d\ge2$.
Hence $\log(n+d)\le n/A$ for every $n\ge n_0$, and the desired inequality
follows.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:fixed_mesh_selected_mle}
For every fixed $k\ge k_0$ and every $\theta\in\Theta$,
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt n\,\operatorname{Log}_\theta\widehat\theta_{n,k}
 \Longrightarrow N(0,I_{\theta,k}^{-1}),
 \label{eq:selected_mle_asymptotic_normality}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 n\,\mathbb E_\theta
 \bigl[1-F(\widehat\theta_{n,k},\theta)\bigr]
 \longrightarrow a_k(\theta).
 \label{eq:fixed_mesh_pointwise_risk}
\end{equation}
Moreover, for some finite constant $C_k$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \mathbb E_\theta
 \bigl[1-F(\widehat\theta_{n,k},\theta)\bigr]
 \le \frac{C_k}{n}
 \label{eq:fixed_mesh_risk_domination}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large $n$.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 n\int_\Theta
 \mathbb E_\theta\bigl[1-F(\widehat\theta_{n,k},\theta)\bigr]
 \,\nu(d\theta)
 \longrightarrow\overline a_k.
 \label{eq:fixed_mesh_haar_averaged_risk}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The preceding regularity statement places the fixed-$k$ model directly within
ordinary finite-alphabet MLE theory.  It gives consistency and
\eqref{eq:selected_mle_asymptotic_normality}.  In Fubini--Study normal
coordinates,
\[
 1-F(\operatorname{Exp}_\theta v,\theta)
 =g_{{\rm FS},\theta}[v,v]+O(\|v\|^4).
\]
The standard quadratic-risk expansion therefore gives
\eqref{eq:fixed_mesh_pointwise_risk}.

For completeness, only a domination estimate, not a parameter-uniform risk
expansion, is needed.  Compact identifiability gives a uniform
Kullback--Leibler separation outside any fixed neighborhood of the true
parameter.  Finite-alphabet concentration then localizes every selected global
MLE to that neighborhood with probability at least $1-Ce^{-cn}$, uniformly in
$\theta$.  Within the neighborhood, the score expansion, bounded scores from
\eqref{eq:fixed_mesh_uniform_support}, and the uniform inverse-Fisher bound from
\eqref{eq:fisher_recovery_form_bound} give mean squared geodesic error
$O_k(n^{-1})$.  Since infidelity is locally bounded by a constant times squared
Fubini--Study distance and is globally at most one,
\eqref{eq:fixed_mesh_risk_domination} follows.  Dominated convergence now gives
\eqref{eq:fixed_mesh_haar_averaged_risk}.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:small_cell_spanning}
Let $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{d^2}$ be rays whose projectors span the Hermitian
matrices.  There are pairwise disjoint Borel neighborhoods
$B_1,\ldots,B_{d^2}$, each of positive $\nu$-measure and with boundary of
$\nu$-measure zero, such that the
operators
\[
 A_\ell:=\int_{B_\ell}\rho_v\,\nu(dv),
 \qquad 1\le \ell\le d^2,
\]
span the Hermitian matrices.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Choose pairwise disjoint sufficiently small geodesic balls centered at the
distinct rays $\xi_\ell$, with radii chosen so that their boundaries have zero
invariant measure.  Continuity of $v\mapsto\rho_v$ gives
\[
 \frac{A_\ell}{\nu(B_\ell)}\longrightarrow\rho_{\xi_\ell}
\]
as the radius of $B_\ell$ tends to zero.  In a finite-dimensional vector
space, the set of ordered $d^2$-tuples that form a basis is open.  After
shrinking the balls, the normalized operators
$A_\ell/\nu(B_\ell)$ therefore still span the Hermitian matrices.  Multiplying
each member by the positive scalar $\nu(B_\ell)$ does not change their linear
span.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:nested_continuity_refinements}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a finite family of Borel cells in the compact metric
probability space $(\Theta,d_{\rm FS},\nu)$.  Assume that the cells partition
$\Theta$ up to a set of $\nu$-measure zero, that every cell has positive
$\nu$-measure, and that every cell boundary has $\nu$-measure zero.  For every
$\delta>0$, there is a finite refinement $\mathcal C'$ with the same
properties such that every cell of $\mathcal C'$ has diameter at most
$4\delta$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Choose a finite $\delta$-net $x_1,\ldots,x_N$ in $\Theta$.  For each $r$, choose
a radius $t_r\in(\delta,2\delta)$ for which
$\nu(\partial B(x_r,t_r))=0$.  Such radii exist because the boundaries of balls
with distinct radii are disjoint, so only countably many radii can have
positive boundary measure.  The balls $B(x_r,t_r)$ still cover $\Theta$.
Disjointize this cover by setting
\[
 D_1=B(x_1,t_1),~
 D_r=B(x_r,t_r)\setminus\bigcup_{a<r}B(x_a,t_a),
~ 2\le r\le N.
\]
Every $D_r$ has boundary contained in the finite union of the chosen sphere
boundaries, and every nonempty $D_r$ has diameter at most $2t_r<4\delta$.
Intersecting the $D_r$ with the cells of $\mathcal C$ gives a finite family of
Borel sets whose boundaries have $\nu$-measure zero, because they are
contained in the union of the old cell boundaries and the chosen sphere
boundaries.  Retain only the intersections having positive $\nu$-measure.  The finite
union $N$ of the omitted intersections satisfies $\nu(N)=0$.  Consequently,
the retained cells cover $\Theta$ except for a set of $\nu$-measure zero.
Each retained cell lies in one $D_r$, so its diameter is less than
$4\delta$, and it lies in one parent cell, so the family is a refinement in
the measure-zero convention stated above.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:spanning_persists_refinement}
If the cell operators of $\mathcal C_k$ span the Hermitian matrices and
$\mathcal C_{k+1}$ refines $\mathcal C_k$, then the cell operators of
$\mathcal C_{k+1}$ also span the Hermitian matrices.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Because the refinement is understood up to sets of $\nu$-measure zero, each parent cell $C_{k,j}$ is the
disjoint union of its child cells of positive $\nu$-measure up to a set of $\nu$-measure zero.
Therefore
\[
 A_{k,j}=\sum_{\ell:\,C_{k+1,\ell}\subset C_{k,j}}A_{k+1,\ell}.
\]
Thus every parent-cell operator belongs to the linear span of the child-cell
operators.  The latter span therefore contains the span of the former.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:spanning_implies_identifiability}
If $\{A_{k,j}\}_j$ spans the Hermitian matrices, then the map
$\theta\mapsto q_{\theta,k}$ is globally identifiable.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Suppose $q_{\theta,k}(j)=q_{\theta',k}(j)$ for every $j$.  By
\eqref{eq:recovering_mesh_model},
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\!\left[(\rho_\theta-\rho_{\theta'})A_{k,j}\right]=0
 \qquad\text{for every }j.
\]
Since the $A_{k,j}$ span the Hermitian matrices, the Hermitian operator
$\rho_\theta-\rho_{\theta'}$ is orthogonal in the Hilbert--Schmidt inner product
to every Hermitian matrix.  Hence it is zero.  Equality of the rank-one
projectors implies $\theta=\theta'$ as rays.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:mesh_score_conditional_expectation}
For every $k$, $\theta$, and $w$,
\begin{equation}
 S_{\theta,w}^{(k)}(J_k)
 =\mathbb E_\theta[S_{\theta,w}(V)\mid J_k]
 \quad P_\theta\text{-almost surely}.
 \label{eq:mesh_score_as_conditional_expectation}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
For a cell $C_{k,j}$, the bounded derivative in
\eqref{eq:score_times_density_derivative} justifies differentiation under the
cell integral and gives
\begin{align*}
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}q_{\theta_t,k}(j)\right|_{t=0}
& =\int_{C_{k,j}}\partial_w p_\theta(v)\,\nu(dv) \\
& =\int_{C_{k,j}}p_\theta(v)S_{\theta,w}(v)\,\nu(dv).
\end{align*}
Dividing by the positive number $q_{\theta,k}(j)$ yields
\[
 S_{\theta,w}^{(k)}(j)
 =\frac{\int_{C_{k,j}}p_\theta(v)S_{\theta,w}(v)\,\nu(dv)}
        {\int_{C_{k,j}}p_\theta(v)\,\nu(dv)},
\]
which is precisely the conditional mean of the fine score on that cell.

<!-- fact-2: body_md -->
\label{fact:trace_swap_identity}
Let \(A\) be an operator on \(\mathcal H^{\otimes n}\), and let
\(S_{k,n+1}\) be the swap between the \(k\)-th tensor factor and an additional
\((n+1)\)-th factor.  Then
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{Tr}_{1,\ldots,n+1}
    \left[
        (A\otimes I)S_{k,n+1}
    \right]
    =
    \operatorname{Tr}_{1,\ldots,n}[A].
    \label{eq:trace_swap_identity}
\end{equation}
<!-- end fact-2 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
It is enough by linearity to verify the identity for simple tensor operators
\(A=A_1\otimes\cdots\otimes A_n\).  Since the swap exchanges the \(k\)-th and
\((n+1)\)-th tensor factors, the full trace factorizes as
\begin{align}
&\quad\operatorname{Tr}\left[(A\otimes I)S_{k,n+1}\right]\nonumber\\
    &\qquad\qquad\quad=
    \left(\prod_{i\ne k}\operatorname{Tr}A_i\right)
    \operatorname{Tr}_{k,n+1}\left[(A_k\otimes I)S_{k,n+1}\right].\nonumber
\end{align}
For the last two factors,
\(\operatorname{Tr}_{k,n+1}[(A_k\otimes I)S_{k,n+1}]=\operatorname{Tr}A_k\),
which follows immediately by expanding in an orthonormal basis.  Hence the
right-hand side is \(\prod_i\operatorname{Tr}A_i=\operatorname{Tr}A\).  Linearity
gives the claim for every operator \(A\) on \(\mathcal H^{\otimes n}\).
