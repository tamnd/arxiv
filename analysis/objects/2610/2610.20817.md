<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{T:main}
Let $A$ and $B$ be finite ordered sets.
If $A^A\cong B^B$, then $A\cong B$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{L:cancellation}
Let $P$, $Q$, and $R$ be finite ordered sets.
If $P\times R\cong Q\times R$, then $P\cong Q$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\operatorname{DirInd}$ denote the set of isomorphism types of connected,
nontrivial, directly indecomposable ordered sets. For every
$D\in\operatorname{DirInd}$, introduce an indeterminate $x_D$.
Encode a~connected ordered set $K$ by the monomial
\[
   \operatorname{m}_K=\prod\{x_D^{\operatorname{mult}_D(K)}\mid D\in\operatorname{DirInd}\},
\]
where $\operatorname{mult}_D(K)$ is the multiplicity of $D$ in the direct decomposition
of $K$. In particular, $\operatorname{m}_{\mathbf{1}}=1$.
For a~finite ordered set $X$, let $\operatorname{Comp}(X)$ denote the set of connected components of $X$.
Define
\[
   \operatorname{p}_X=\sum\{\operatorname{m}_K\mid K\in\operatorname{Comp}(X)\}.
\]
Isomorphic components are counted separately in this sum.
The uniqueness of connected components and Hashimoto's Refinement
Theorem imply that $\operatorname{p}_X=\operatorname{p}_{X'}$ if and only if $X\cong X'$.
The components of a~product are the products of components, so
$\operatorname{p}_{X\times Y}=\operatorname{p}_X\operatorname{p}_Y$.
Thus the hypothesis gives $\operatorname{p}_P\operatorname{p}_R=\operatorname{p}_Q\operatorname{p}_R$.
Only finitely many indeterminates occur in these polynomials, and
$\operatorname{p}_R\neq0$.
Cancellation in the polynomial ring over $\mathbb Z$ gives
$\operatorname{p}_P=\operatorname{p}_Q$, and therefore $P\cong Q$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The canonical isomorphism $(P\times Q)^Y\cong P^Y\times Q^Y$
sends a~map constant on each component of $Y$ to a~pair of maps
with the same property, and conversely. Since the components of
a~product are the products of components, it restricts to the
isomorphism in \eqref{E:C-product}.
Similarly, restriction gives $P^{Y+Z}\cong P^Y\times P^Z$ and
identifies the selected components, proving \eqref{E:C-sum}.

For \eqref{E:C-iteration}, use the currying isomorphism
\[
   P^{Y\times Z}\longrightarrow(P^Y)^Z,
   \qquad f\longmapsto\bigl(z\mapsto(y\mapsto f(y,z))\bigr).
\]
Let $f$ lie in $\operatorname{Con}(P,Y\times Z)$, and choose a~comparability
path from $f$ to a~map $g$ constant on every component of
$Y\times Z$. These components are the products of components
of $Y$ and $Z$. Thus every section $y\mapsto g(y,z)$ is constant
on each component of $Y$, and the curried map of $g$ is constant
on each component of $Z$.
For fixed $z$, evaluation of the curried path gives a~path in
$P^Y$ ending at this section of $g$. Every section along the path
therefore belongs to $\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)$. Hence the entire curried path
lies in $\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)^Z$ and ends at a~map constant on each component
of $Z$. The curried map of $f$ belongs to
$\operatorname{Con}(\operatorname{Con}(P,Y),Z)$.

Conversely, let $h$ lie in $\operatorname{Con}(\operatorname{Con}(P,Y),Z)$.
Choose a~path in $\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)^Z$ from $h$ to a~map that is constant
on each component $Z_j$ of $Z$, with value $h_j\in\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)$.
For each $j$, choose a~path in $P^Y$ from $h_j$ to a~map $g_j$
constant on every component of $Y$. This path lies in
$\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)$. Change the value on one $Z_j$ at a~time along its
chosen path, keeping it constant throughout $Z_j$.
Each resulting map on $Z$ is order-preserving, since distinct
components have no comparabilities. There are finitely many
components, so these paths concatenate to a~finite path ending
at the map with value $g_j$ on $Z_j$. After uncurrying, its
endpoint is constant on every component of $Y\times Z$.
Thus the uncurried map of $h$ belongs to $\operatorname{Con}(P,Y\times Z)$.
The currying isomorphism therefore restricts to
\eqref{E:C-iteration}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{L:height}
For the ordered sets $P$ and $Y$,
\begin{equation}\label{E:height-exponent}
   \operatorname{height}\bigl(\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)\bigr)=\operatorname{height}(P^Y)=|Y|\operatorname{height}(P).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Along a~chain in $P^Y$, the value at a~fixed element of $Y$ can
increase strictly at most $\operatorname{height}(P)$ times.
Every strict step changes at least one value.
This gives the upper bound $|Y|\operatorname{height}(P)$.

For the reverse inequality, choose a~chain
$p_0<\cdots<p_h$ in $P$, where $h=\operatorname{height}(P)$, and a~linear extension of $Y$.
Starting with the constant map $p_0$, change the values from $p_{j-1}$
to $p_j$ one at a~time, in reverse order of this linear extension,
for $j=1,\dots,h$.
At each stage of a~transition, the elements already changed form
an upper set of $Y$. Since $p_{j-1}<p_j$, every intermediate map
is therefore order-preserving.
This gives a~chain of length $|Y|h$, starting at a~constant map,
and therefore lying in $\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{L:normal}
Let $D$ be a~connected, nontrivial, directly indecomposable ordered set.
There are ordered sets $E_D$ and $F_D$, unique up to isomorphism, such that
$E_D$ is absolutely $\operatorname{Con}$-indecomposable, $F_D$ is connected,
and
\begin{equation}\label{E:normal}
   D\cong\operatorname{Con}(E_D,F_D).
\end{equation}
For a~connected ordered set $Y$, the ordered set $\operatorname{Con}(D,Y)$
is connected, nontrivial, and directly indecomposable, and
\begin{equation}\label{E:normal-exponent}
   E_{\operatorname{Con}(D,Y)}\cong E_D,
   \qquad
   F_{\operatorname{Con}(D,Y)}\cong F_D\times Y.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Existence follows from \cite[Theorem~2(4)]{FarBC}, which restates
McKenzie's result. Lemma~8 of the same paper shows that $F_D$ is connected.

To verify uniqueness, suppose that also
$D\cong\operatorname{Con}(E',F')$, where $E'$ is absolutely $\operatorname{Con}$-indecomposable
and $F'$ is connected.
By \cite[Theorem~4]{FarBC}, there are connected ordered sets
$H$, $U$, $V$, and $Z$ such that
\begin{align*}
 E_D&\cong\operatorname{Con}(H,U),\\
 E'&\cong\operatorname{Con}(H,V),\\
 F_D&\cong V\times Z,\\
 F'&\cong U\times Z.
\end{align*}

Absolute $\operatorname{Con}$-indecomposability gives $U\cong V\cong\mathbf{1}$ and
$E_D\cong E'\cong H$. Hence $F_D\cong F'\cong Z$.

For connected $Y$, the connectedness and direct
indecomposability of $\operatorname{Con}(D,Y)$ follow from
\cite[Lemma~9(1)]{FarBC}. Constant maps embed $D$ into $\operatorname{Con}(D,Y)$,
so this ordered set is nontrivial.
Finally, \eqref{E:C-iteration} gives
\[
   \operatorname{Con}(D,Y)
      \cong\operatorname{Con}\bigl(\operatorname{Con}(E_D,F_D),Y\bigr)
      \cong\operatorname{Con}(E_D,F_D\times Y).
\]
The product $F_D\times Y$ is connected.
The uniqueness just proved therefore gives
\eqref{E:normal-exponent}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{D:Lambda}
Let $P$ be a~connected ordered set, with the direct
decomposition \eqref{E:direct}.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{E:Lambda}
   \operatorname{Tow}(P)=F_{D_1}+\cdots+F_{D_k}.
\end{equation}
For $P=\mathbf{1}$, this is the empty sum: $\operatorname{Tow}(\mathbf{1})=\varnothing$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{L:Lambda}
Let $P$ and $Q$ be connected ordered sets, and let
$Y$ be an ordered set.
Then
\begin{align}
   \operatorname{Tow}(P\times Q)
      &\cong\operatorname{Tow}(P)+\operatorname{Tow}(Q),\label{E:Lambda-product}\\
   \operatorname{Tow}\bigl(\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)\bigr)
      &\cong Y\times\operatorname{Tow}(P).\label{E:Lambda-exponent}
\end{align}
If $P$ is nontrivial, then $\operatorname{Tow}(P)$ is an ordered set and
\begin{equation}\label{E:Lambda-height}
   \operatorname{height}\bigl(\operatorname{Tow}(P)\bigr)<\operatorname{height}(P).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The factors of $P\times Q$ are the factors of $P$ together with those
of $Q$, proving \eqref{E:Lambda-product}.

Write $P\cong\prod\{D_j\mid 1\leq j\leq k\}$ as in \eqref{E:direct}, and let
$Y=Y_1+\cdots+Y_r$ be the decomposition of $Y$ into connected components.
By \eqref{E:C-product} and \eqref{E:C-sum},
\[
   \operatorname{Con}(P,Y)\cong
      \prod\{\operatorname{Con}(D_j,Y_i)\mid 1\leq i\leq r,\ 1\leq j\leq k\}.
\]
Each factor is connected and directly indecomposable by
Lemma~\ref{L:normal}.
The same lemma gives
\[
   \operatorname{Tow}\bigl(\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)\bigr)
      \cong\sum\{F_{D_j}\times Y_i\mid 1\leq i\leq r,\ 1\leq j\leq k\}
      \cong Y\times\operatorname{Tow}(P),
\]
proving \eqref{E:Lambda-exponent}.
The empty product and empty sum cover the case $P=\mathbf{1}$.

Now assume that $P$ is nontrivial.
Then $k\geq1$.
For each factor $D_j$, the ordered set $E_{D_j}$ is connected and
nontrivial, and therefore $\operatorname{height}(E_{D_j})\geq1$.
By Lemma~\ref{L:height},
\[
   \operatorname{height}(F_{D_j})<|F_{D_j}|
      \leq |F_{D_j}|\operatorname{height}(E_{D_j})
      =\operatorname{height}(D_j)\leq\operatorname{height}(P).
\]
The height of $\operatorname{Tow}(P)$ is the maximum of the heights of its
summands, proving \eqref{E:Lambda-height}.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{T:ordering}
There is a~linear ordering $\preceq$ of the isomorphism types of
connected ordered sets with the following properties.
For connected ordered sets $P$, $Q$, and $R$, and
an ordered set $Y$,
\begin{align*}
   P\prec Q &\quad\text{implies that}\quad
      P\times R\prec Q\times R,\\
   P\prec Q &\quad\text{implies that}\quad
      \operatorname{Con}(P,Y)\prec\operatorname{Con}(Q,Y).
\end{align*}
Both implications also hold with $\preceq$ in place of $\prec$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{L:lex}
Let $C$ and $N$ be chains, possibly infinite, and let $\operatorname{F}$ be a~set of functions
from $C$ to $N$ such that any two functions differ at only finitely
many elements of $C$.
For distinct $f,g\in\operatorname{F}$, let $\operatorname{did}(f,g)$ be the largest element
at which they differ.
Declare $f<g$ if $f(\operatorname{did}(f,g))<g(\operatorname{did}(f,g))$.
This defines a~linear ordering of $\operatorname{F}$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Comparability and asymmetry follow from the fact that $N$ is a~chain.
To verify transitivity, let $f<g<h$ and put
\[
   c=\max\{\operatorname{did}(f,g),\operatorname{did}(g,h)\}.
\]
The three functions agree above $c$.
At $c$, we have $f(c)\leq g(c)\leq h(c)$, with at least one strict
inequality.
Hence $\operatorname{did}(f,h)=c$ and $f<h$.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Lemma~\ref{L:lex}, now applied to multiplicity functions on
$(\operatorname{DirInd},\leq_2)$, shows that $\preceq$ is a~linear ordering of
isomorphism types.
Equation~\eqref{E:vectors} shows that multiplication preserves
strict comparisons.

Suppose that $Y$ is connected.
By \eqref{E:C-product}, the factors of $\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)$ are obtained by
replacing each factor $D$ of $P$ by $\operatorname{Con}(D,Y)$, with the same
multiplicity.
These are directly indecomposable factors by Lemma~\ref{L:normal}.
By \eqref{E:prime-order}, distinct factors remain distinct and their
ordering is preserved.
Therefore, the largest factor at which the multiplicities differ
is replaced by the largest such factor after exponentiation.
Its multiplicities are unchanged.
This proves that $P\prec Q$ implies $\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)\prec\operatorname{Con}(Q,Y)$.

For an arbitrary $Y$, write $Y=Y_1+\cdots+Y_r$ as a~sum
of connected components.
If $P\prec Q$, the connected case gives
$\operatorname{Con}(P,Y_i)\prec\operatorname{Con}(Q,Y_i)$ for every $i$.
For $0\leq j\leq r$, put
\[
   \operatorname{Z}_j=\prod\{\operatorname{Con}(Q,Y_i)\mid 1\leq i\leq j\}
         \times\prod\{\operatorname{Con}(P,Y_i)\mid j+1\leq i\leq r\},
\]
with an empty product interpreted as $\mathbf{1}$.
The factors other than the $j$th one have a~connected
product. Compatibility with multiplication therefore gives
$\operatorname{Z}_{j-1}\prec \operatorname{Z}_j$ for $1\leq j\leq r$.
By transitivity, $\operatorname{Z}_0\prec \operatorname{Z}_r$, and \eqref{E:C-sum} gives
$\operatorname{Con}(P,Y)\prec\operatorname{Con}(Q,Y)$.
The non-strict implications follow because both constructions
preserve isomorphisms.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{D:T}
Let $\operatorname{Max}(X)$ be a~$\preceq$-greatest member of $\operatorname{MaxInt}(X)$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{L:exponent-intervals}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be ordered sets, and let
$Y=Y_1+\cdots+Y_r$ be the decomposition of $Y$ into connected components.
The members of $\operatorname{MaxInt}(X^Y)$ are, up to isomorphism, precisely the
ordered sets
\begin{equation}\label{E:exponent-intervals}
   I_1^{Y_1}\times\cdots\times I_r^{Y_r},
   \qquad I_i\in\operatorname{MaxInt}(X)\quad(1\leq i\leq r).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let $[f,g]\in\operatorname{MaxInt}(X^Y)$ and put $h=\operatorname{height}(X)$.
Applying the coordinatewise upper bound from the proof of
Lemma~\ref{L:height}, we obtain
\[
   |Y|h=\operatorname{height}([f,g])
      \leq\sum\{\operatorname{height}([f(y),g(y)])\mid y\in Y\}
      \leq |Y|h.
\]
Thus every interval $[f(y),g(y)]$ has height $h$.
A~chain of maximum length in this bounded interval contains both
endpoints. Hence its bottom is a~minimal element of $X$ and its top
is a~maximal element of $X$; otherwise that chain could be extended
in $X$.
For comparable $y,z\in Y$, the elements $f(y)$ and $f(z)$ are
comparable minimal elements, so they are equal.
Similarly, $g(y)=g(z)$.
Hence $f$ and $g$ are constant on each $Y_i$, with values $a_i$
and $b_i$, respectively.
Setting $I_i=[a_i,b_i]$, restriction to the components $Y_i$ gives
an order-isomorphism
\[
   [f,g]\cong\prod\{I_i^{Y_i}\mid 1\leq i\leq r\}.
\]
This proves \eqref{E:exponent-intervals}.

Conversely, choose $I_i=[a_i,b_i]\in\operatorname{MaxInt}(X)$ for every $i$.
The maps with constant values $a_i$ and $b_i$ on $Y_i$ are the
endpoints of an interval of the form \eqref{E:exponent-intervals}.
By Lemma~\ref{L:height} and \eqref{E:height-product}, its height is
$\sum\{|Y_i|h\mid 1\leq i\leq r\}=|Y|h$.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{L:T}
For ordered sets $X$ and $Y$,
\begin{align}
   \operatorname{Max}(X\times Y)&\cong \operatorname{Max}(X)\times \operatorname{Max}(Y),\label{E:T-product}\\
   \operatorname{Max}(X^Y)&\cong \operatorname{Max}(X)^Y.\label{E:T-exponent}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Every interval in $X\times Y$ is a~product $I\times J$ of intervals
in $X$ and $Y$.
By \eqref{E:height-product}, it has maximum height if and only if
$I\in\operatorname{MaxInt}(X)$ and $J\in\operatorname{MaxInt}(Y)$.
For such intervals, Theorem~\ref{T:ordering} gives
\[
   I\times J\preceq \operatorname{Max}(X)\times \operatorname{Max}(Y).
\]
The right-hand side is itself an interval of maximum height.
This proves \eqref{E:T-product}.

For \eqref{E:T-exponent}, write $Y=Y_1+\cdots+Y_r$ as above.
By Lemma~\ref{L:exponent-intervals}, every maximum-height interval
in $X^Y$ has the form \eqref{E:exponent-intervals}, up to isomorphism.
Since $I_i$ and $\operatorname{Max}(X)$ are bounded, \eqref{E:bounded} and
Theorem~\ref{T:ordering} give
\[
   I_i^{Y_i}=\operatorname{Con}(I_i,Y_i)
      \preceq\operatorname{Con}(\operatorname{Max}(X),Y_i)=\operatorname{Max}(X)^{Y_i}.
\]
Taking products, we see that every such interval is at most
\[
   \prod\{\operatorname{Max}(X)^{Y_i}\mid 1\leq i\leq r\}\cong\operatorname{Max}(X)^Y
\]
in the ordering $\preceq$.
The latter is also an interval of maximum height, obtained by
taking $I_i=\operatorname{Max}(X)$ for every $i$.
This proves \eqref{E:T-exponent}.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{P:reconstruction}
Let $A$ be an ordered set of positive height, and let
$E\cong A^A$.
The constructions
\begin{align*}
   U&=\operatorname{Tow}(\operatorname{Max}(E)),\\
   V&=\operatorname{Max}(U),\\
   W&=\operatorname{Max}(\operatorname{Tow}(V))
\end{align*}
are well-defined.
There is a~connected ordered set $L$, unique up to
isomorphism, such that
\begin{equation}\label{E:recover-L}
   V\cong W\times L.
\end{equation}
Moreover, $L\cong \operatorname{Max}(A)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{E:recover-A}
   U\cong\operatorname{Tow}(L)\times A.
\end{equation}
Thus $A$ is the unique finite ordered set, up to isomorphism,
that satisfies \eqref{E:recover-A}.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $L_0=\operatorname{Max}(A)$ and $R=\operatorname{Tow}(L_0)$.
Since $\operatorname{height}(A)\geq1$, the interval $L_0$ is nontrivial.
Lemma~\ref{L:Lambda} shows that $R$ is an ordered set, so we can set
$S=\operatorname{Max}(R)$.

By Lemma~\ref{L:T} and the boundedness of $L_0$,
\[
   \operatorname{Max}(E)\cong L_0^A=\operatorname{Con}(L_0,A).
\]
Applying $\operatorname{Tow}$ and using \eqref{E:Lambda-exponent}, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{E:U}
   U\cong R\times A.
\end{equation}
Thus $V$ is defined.
Applying $\operatorname{Max}$ to \eqref{E:U} gives
\begin{equation}\label{E:V}
   V\cong S\times L_0.
\end{equation}
Consequently, by \eqref{E:Lambda-product},
\begin{equation}\label{E:Lambda-V}
   \operatorname{Tow}(V)\cong\operatorname{Tow}(S)+R.
\end{equation}
The sum in \eqref{E:Lambda-V} contains $R$, so $W$ is defined.

If $S=\mathbf{1}$ up to isomorphism, then $\operatorname{Tow}(S)=\varnothing$.
Otherwise, the height inequality \eqref{E:Lambda-height} gives
\[
   \operatorname{height}(\operatorname{Tow}(S))<\operatorname{height}(S)=\operatorname{height}(R).
\]
Every interval in a~disjoint sum lies in one summand.
If $\operatorname{Tow}(S)=\varnothing$, the right-hand side of
\eqref{E:Lambda-V} is just $R$. Otherwise, its maximum-height
intervals cannot lie in $\operatorname{Tow}(S)$, by the strict height inequality.
In either case, they are precisely the maximum-height intervals of $R$.
It follows that
\[
   W=\operatorname{Max}(\operatorname{Tow}(V))\cong \operatorname{Max}(R)=S.
\]
Together with \eqref{E:V}, this proves
$V\cong W\times L_0$.

Lemma~\ref{L:cancellation} shows that any
$L$ satisfying \eqref{E:recover-L} is isomorphic to $L_0$.
In particular, $L$ is connected, so $\operatorname{Tow}(L)$ is defined.
Therefore, $\operatorname{Tow}(L)\cong R$, and \eqref{E:U}
is exactly \eqref{E:recover-A}.
Finally, cancellation of $\operatorname{Tow}(L)$ in \eqref{E:recover-A}
proves the uniqueness of $A$.

Suppose that $A$ is an antichain, with $|A|=n\geq1$.
Then $A^A$ is an antichain with $n^n$ elements.
Since $B$ embeds into $B^B$ by constant maps, $B$ is also an antichain.

The function $n\mapsto n^n$ is strictly increasing on the positive

Let $E=A^A\cong B^B$.

\[
   \operatorname{Tow}(L)\times A\cong U\cong\operatorname{Tow}(L)\times B.
\]

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
We call an exponential term $t$ \emph{reconstructing}, if, for finite ordered sets $A$ and $B$,
\[
   t(A)\cong t(B)\quad\text{implies that}\quad A\cong B.
\]

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{L:iteration-roots}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be finite ordered sets, and let $k\geq1$.
If $X^{\times k}\cong Y^{\times k}$, then $X\cong Y$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Use the polynomials from the proof of Lemma~\ref{L:cancellation}.
The hypothesis gives $\operatorname{p}_X^k=\operatorname{p}_Y^k$, and hence
\[
   (\operatorname{p}_X-\operatorname{p}_Y)\sum\{\operatorname{p}_X^{k-1-j}\operatorname{p}_Y^j\mid 0\leq j\leq k-1\}=0.
\]
The sum is nonzero, since $\operatorname{p}_X$ and $\operatorname{p}_Y$ are nonzero polynomials
with nonnegative coefficients. The polynomial ring is an integral
domain, so $\operatorname{p}_X=\operatorname{p}_Y$, and therefore $X\cong Y$.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{L:iteration-der}
For ordered sets $X$ and $Y$,
\begin{align}
   \operatorname{Der}(X\times Y)&\cong\operatorname{Der}(X)+\operatorname{Der}(Y),
      \label{E:iteration-der-product}\\
   \operatorname{Der}(X^Y)&\cong\operatorname{Der}(X)\times Y.
      \label{E:iteration-der-exponent}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-9 -->

The first identity follows from \eqref{E:T-product} and
\eqref{E:Lambda-product}. For the second, \eqref{E:T-exponent} gives
$\operatorname{Max}(X^Y)\cong\operatorname{Max}(X)^Y$. Since $\operatorname{Max}(X)$ is bounded,
\eqref{E:bounded} and \eqref{E:Lambda-exponent} yield
\[
   \operatorname{Tow}(\operatorname{Max}(X)^Y)
      =\operatorname{Tow}(\operatorname{Con}(\operatorname{Max}(X),Y))
      \cong\operatorname{Tow}(\operatorname{Max}(X))\times Y.
\]

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{T:iteration-reconstruction}
Fix integers $d\geq1$, $r_i\geq0$, and $s_i\geq1$, for
$1\leq i\leq d$. For a~finite ordered set $A$, define
\begin{align}
   E_0(A)&=A,\notag\\
   D_i(A)&=A^{\times r_i}\times E_{i-1}(A)^{\times s_i},\notag\\
   E_i(A)&=A^{D_i(A)}\qquad(1\leq i\leq d).
      \label{E:iteration-family}
\end{align}
For finite ordered sets $A$ and $B$,
\[
   E_d(A)\cong E_d(B)\quad\text{implies that}\quad A\cong B.
\]
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
We show how to recover $A$ from $E_d(A)$, with $d$, $r_i$, and $s_i$
fixed. All determinations are up to isomorphism.

Suppose that $A$ is an antichain of size $n\geq1$. Then all
$D_i(A)$ and $E_i(A)$ are antichains. Put $e_i(n)=|E_i(A)|$. We have
\[
   e_0(n)=n,\qquad
   e_i(n)=n^{\,n^{r_i}e_{i-1}(n)^{s_i}}.
\]
Induction shows that every $e_i$ is strictly increasing on the positive
integers. Indeed, its exponent is positive and nondecreasing, and its
base is strictly increasing. Moreover, constant maps embed $A$ into
each $E_i(A)$ for $i\geq1$. Thus $E_d(A)$ is an antichain if and only
if $A$ is an antichain, and in this case $e_d(n)$ determines $n$ and
therefore $A$.

Now assume that $\operatorname{height}(A)>0$, and abbreviate $E_i(A)$ and $D_i(A)$
to $E_i$ and $D_i$. Put
\[
   L=\operatorname{Max}(A),\qquad R=\operatorname{Tow}(L),\qquad S=\operatorname{Max}(R).
\]
The ordered set $L$ is nontrivial, so Lemma~\ref{L:Lambda}
ensures that $R$ and $S$ are defined. Also,
\begin{equation}\label{E:iteration-height-drop}
   \operatorname{height}(S)=\operatorname{height}(R)<\operatorname{height}(L).
\end{equation}
Every $E_i$ has positive height. Since $s_i\geq1$, every $D_i$ also
has positive height.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Recovering $L$.}
Put $Q_i=\operatorname{Max}(E_i)$ for $0\leq i\leq d$, so $Q_0=L$.
For $1\leq i\leq d$, \eqref{E:iteration-der-exponent} gives
\begin{equation}\label{E:iteration-Q}
   \operatorname{Tow}(Q_i)=\operatorname{Der}(E_i)\cong R\times D_i.
\end{equation}
Consequently, \eqref{E:T-product} yields
\begin{equation}\label{E:iteration-V}
   V_i =\operatorname{Red}(Q_i)
      \cong S\times L^{\times r_i}\times Q_{i-1}^{\times s_i}.
\end{equation}
In particular, each $V_i$ is connected and nontrivial.
For $i\geq2$, applying \eqref{E:Lambda-product} and then
\eqref{E:iteration-Q} gives
\begin{equation}\label{E:short}
   \operatorname{Tow}(V_i)
      \cong\operatorname{Tow}(S)+r_iR+s_i(R\times D_{i-1}).
\end{equation}
The last summands have height
$\operatorname{height}(R)+\operatorname{height}(D_{i-1})>\operatorname{height}(R)$.
If $S\cong\mathbf{1}$, then $\operatorname{Tow}(S)=\varnothing$; otherwise,
\[
   \operatorname{height}(\operatorname{Tow}(S))<\operatorname{height}(S)=\operatorname{height}(R).
\]
Thus every interval of maximum height lies in one of the $s_i$ copies of
$R\times D_{i-1}$. It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{E:iteration-descent}
   \operatorname{Red}(V_i)\cong\operatorname{Max}(R\times D_{i-1})\cong V_{i-1}
   \qquad(2\leq i\leq d).
\end{equation}

Set $k=r_1+s_1$, so $k\geq1$. Equation~\eqref{E:iteration-V} gives
\begin{align*}
   V_1&\cong S\times L^{\times k},\\
   \operatorname{Tow}(V_1)&\cong\operatorname{Tow}(S)+kR.
\end{align*}
The same height comparison shows that $\operatorname{Red}(V_1)\cong S$.
Together with \eqref{E:iteration-descent}, this proves
\begin{equation}\label{E:iteration-recover-L}
\begin{aligned}
   \operatorname{Red}^d(Q_d)&\cong S\times L^{\times k},\\
   \operatorname{Red}^{d+1}(Q_d)&\cong S.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here the superscripts on $\operatorname{Red}$ denote composition. Every application
is defined: its argument is $Q_d$ or one of the nontrivial ordered
sets $V_i$. No application to $S$ is required.

Both ordered sets on the left of \eqref{E:iteration-recover-L} are determined
by $E_d$. Cancel the factor $S$, using
Lemma~\ref{L:cancellation}, to recover $L^{\times k}$. Then
Lemma~\ref{L:iteration-roots} recovers $L$. We now also know
$R=\operatorname{Tow}(L)$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Recovering $A$.}
By \eqref{E:iteration-Q},
\[
   \operatorname{Der}(E_d)\cong R\times D_d.
\]
Cancel the known factor $R$ to recover $D_d$.
For $2\leq i\leq d$, Lemma~\ref{L:iteration-der} gives
\begin{align}
   \operatorname{Der}(D_i)
      &\cong r_iR+s_i\operatorname{Der}(E_{i-1})\notag\\
      &\cong r_iR+s_i(R\times D_{i-1})\notag\\
      &\cong R\times(r_i\mathbf{1}+s_iD_{i-1}).
         \label{E:iteration-recover-D}
\end{align}
Once $D_i$ is known, cancel $R$ in
\eqref{E:iteration-recover-D} to determine
$K_i\cong r_i\mathbf{1}+s_iD_{i-1}$.
For a~connected isomorphism type $T$, let $c_T(X)$ denote the
number of components of $X$ of type $T$. Then
\[
   c_T(K_i)=
   \begin{cases}
      r_i+s_i c_{\mathbf{1}}(D_{i-1}),&T\cong\mathbf{1},\\
      s_i c_T(D_{i-1}),&T\ncong\mathbf{1}.
   \end{cases}
\]
Consequently,
\[
   c_T(D_{i-1})=
   \begin{cases}
      \bigl(c_{\mathbf{1}}(K_i)-r_i\bigr)/s_i,&T\cong\mathbf{1},\\
      c_T(K_i)/s_i,&T\ncong\mathbf{1}.
   \end{cases}
\]
These are nonnegative integers by \eqref{E:iteration-recover-D}.
Thus all component multiplicities of $D_{i-1}$ are determined,
including its singleton multiplicity. There is no need to identify
which individual singleton components of $K_i$ were contributed
by $r_i\mathbf{1}$. The uniqueness of connected components now
determines $D_{i-1}$ up to isomorphism.

Continuing in this way, we recover
\[
   D_1\cong A^{\times(r_1+s_1)}=A^{\times k}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{L:iteration-roots} now recovers $A$.
For $d=1$, the last step follows directly after recovering $D_d$;
no descent through \eqref{E:iteration-recover-D} is needed.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{C:iteration-towers}
For a~finite ordered set $A$, define
\begin{align*}
   F_1(A)&=A,& F_{m+1}(A)&=F_m(A)^A,\\
   G_1(A)&=A,& G_{m+1}(A)&=A^{G_m(A)},\\
   H_0(A)&=A,& H_{m+1}(A)&=H_m(A)^{H_m(A)}.
\end{align*}
For every fixed $m\geq1$, each of $F_m(A)$, $G_m(A)$, and $H_m(A)$
determines $A$ up to isomorphism.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
For $m=1$, both $F_1(A)$ and $G_1(A)$ equal $A$. Now let $m\geq2$
and put
$k=m-1$. By the exponential laws,
\begin{align*}
   F_m(A)&\cong A^{A^{\times k}},\\
   F_m(A)^{\times k}&
      \cong\bigl(A^{\times k}\bigr)^{A^{\times k}}.
\end{align*}
Thus $F_m(A)$ determines $A^{\times k}$ by
Theorem~\ref{T:main}, and then determines $A$ by
Lemma~\ref{L:iteration-roots}. This is also the case $d=1$,
$r_1=0$, $s_1=k$ of Theorem~\ref{T:iteration-reconstruction}.

For $G_m$ with $m\geq2$, take $d=m-1$, $r_i=0$, and $s_i=1$ in
Theorem~\ref{T:iteration-reconstruction}. Finally, apply
Theorem~\ref{T:main} successively $m$ times to recover $A$ from
$H_m(A)$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{P:iteration-substitution}
If $s$ and $t$ are reconstructing exponential terms, then the term
obtained by substituting $t$ for every occurrence of $x$ in $s$ is
reconstructing.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
The value of the substituted term at $A$ is $s(t(A))$. Therefore,
\[
   s(t(A))\cong s(t(B))
      \quad\text{implies that}\quad t(A)\cong t(B)
      \quad\text{implies that}\quad A\cong B.
\]
All the ordered sets to which reconstruction is applied are finite.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{C:iteration-four}
Every exponential term with at most four occurrences of $x$ is
reconstructing.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
One occurrence is immediate, and two occurrences are covered by
Theorem~\ref{T:main}. With three occurrences, the two terms are
$(x^x)^x$ and $x^{(x^x)}$, both covered by
Corollary~\ref{C:iteration-towers}.
With four occurrences, the five values at $A$ are
\[
\begin{gathered}
   \bigl((A^A)^A\bigr)^A,\\
   \bigl(A^{(A^A)}\bigr)^A,\\
   (A^A)^{(A^A)},\\
   A^{((A^A)^A)},\\
   A^{(A^{(A^A)})}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The first, third, and fifth are $F_4(A)$, $H_2(A)$, and $G_4(A)$,
respectively. The second is isomorphic to the third, since
\[
   \bigl(A^{(A^A)}\bigr)^A
      \cong A^{(A^A)\times A}
      \cong A^{A\times(A^A)}
      \cong(A^A)^{(A^A)}.
\]
For the fourth, take $d=2$, $r_1=r_2=0$, $s_1=2$, and $s_2=1$
in Theorem~\ref{T:iteration-reconstruction}. Then
$E_1(A)\cong(A^A)^A$ and $E_2(A)\cong A^{((A^A)^A)}$.
