<!-- head-1: body_md -->
Section~\ref{sec:examples} collects our examples, old and new, and
describes each with the same three tools: displacement operators, a
stabilizer generator matrix $T$, and a logical matrix.
The columns of $T$ form a basis of all lifted labels of the stabilizers,
including the periods of rotor angles and qudit labels.
With the lifted symplectic form $J$, the integer matrix
$\varTheta=T^{\intercal}JT$ records the commutation phases of these
columns.
The code dimension is then $K=|\operatorname{Pf}\varTheta|$ for every such
system and every stabilizer lattice, with or without torsion
(Lemma~\ref{lem:full_lift_pfaffian}).
The columns of the logical matrix $T\varTheta^{-1}$ generate the logical
displacements.
Single-factor examples include qubit and qudit stabilizer codes such as
the toric code, GKP codes, and rotor GKP codes.
They also include an intrinsically non-CSS two-rotor code, which no
generalized Clifford operator maps to a CSS code, and homological rotor
codes with finite homology.
Two-factor examples include the oscillator--qudit LCA codes of
Ref.~\cite{yr64-ypxt}, a new hybrid Golay code, and two new families,
which couple a rotor to an oscillator and to a qudit, respectively.
We build the hybrid Golay code from partially lifted binary and
quaternary Golay codes, which we also introduce.
The resulting code couples twelve oscillators intrinsically to twelve qubits.
Its stabilizer lattice has torsion, so the code lies outside the LCA
construction.
A minimal oscillator--rotor code encodes a ququart in a lattice that no
symplectic automorphism of $G$ splits into separate oscillator and rotor
lattices.
We prove that every one-oscillator--one-rotor lattice code of square-free
logical dimension does split, so dimension four is the first possible
instance of intrinsic oscillator--rotor coupling.
In particular, every ideal lattice stabilizer state of this system is
symplectically equivalent to a product state.
In contrast, a new rotor--qudit code couples a rotor intrinsically to a
qudit of dimension $d$ already at $K=d$.
A related rotor--qudit stabilizer state is not mapped to any product
state by any generalized Clifford operator.
Examples with several factor types include mixed-qudit codes with unequal
or composite local dimensions, which we relate to concatenated GKP codes.
A torsion-extended code places a stabilizer lattice with torsion on an
oscillator, a rotor, and a qubit.
This lattice couples all three systems in its defining coordinates, but a
generalized Clifford operator maps the code to a product of a GKP code and
two stabilizer states.
In contrast, a torsion-free CSS lattice on the same three systems encodes a
ququart that no generalized Clifford operator decomposes along any
bipartition (Proposition~\ref{prop:hybrid-intrinsic-nondecoupling}).

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:squarefree-oscillator-rotor-decoupling}
Every isotropic lattice for one oscillator and one rotor whose logical
dimension $K$ is square-free is symplectically decomposable.
Equivalently, the logical dimension $K$ of an isotropic lattice in this
system that is not symplectically decomposable is divisible by the square
of a prime.
In particular, $K\geq4$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:oscillator-rotor-normal-form}, $K=mN$ with
$m,N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}$.  A prime dividing both $m$ and $N$ would have its
square dividing $K$.  Square-freeness therefore implies
$\gcd(m,N)=1$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:oscillator-rotor-coprime-splitting} applies.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:oscillator-rotor-stabilizer-states}
Every ideal lattice stabilizer state of one oscillator and one rotor is
symplectically decomposable.  More precisely, a generalized Clifford
operator maps it to a tensor product of an ideal oscillator
stabilizer state and an ideal rotor stabilizer state.  All lattice codes
with $K=2$ or $K=3$ are also symplectically decomposable.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
This follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:squarefree-oscillator-rotor-decoupling},
since $K=1,2,3$ are square-free, together with the generalized Clifford
implementation of Proposition~\ref{prop:symplectic_implementer}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:oscillator-rotor-nondecoupling}
For every $\alpha>0$ and $b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}$, the lattice
$D_{\alpha,b}$ of Sec.~\ref{ex:osc-rotor} is not symplectically
decomposable.  Thus four is the smallest logical dimension permitting
intrinsic coupling in this system.

\label{prop:rotor-qudit-nondecoupling}
For every $d\geq2$, the lattice $D=\langle w_1,w_2\rangle$ of
Sec.~\ref{ex:rotor-qudit} is not symplectically decomposable.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:rotor-qudit-state}
For every $d\geq2$, let $z=(0,-\tfrac1{d^2}|0,1)$ be the label of the
logical operator $\overline{Z}$ of Sec.~\ref{ex:rotor-qudit}.
The lattice $D'=\langle w_1,z\rangle$, obtained by adding $\overline{Z}$ to the

It is not symplectically decomposable, and no generalized

\end{equation}

\label{prop:hybrid-intrinsic-nondecoupling}
The lattice $D_{\mathrm{int}}$ is not symplectically decomposable for any
bipartition of the oscillator, the rotor, and the qubit.
In particular, no generalized Clifford operator maps the code to a
tensor product of codes on complementary sets of these systems.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:phase_triv}
For every isotropic lattice $D\subset G$, there is a function
$c\colon D\to\mathbb T$, with $c(0)=1$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase_coboundary}
 \beta(w_1,w_2)=\frac{c(w_1+w_2)}{c(w_1)c(w_2)},
 \qquad w_1,w_2\in D.
\end{equation}
A multiplier of the form~\eqref{eq:phase_coboundary}, for a phase function
$c\colon D\to\mathbb T$ with $c(0)=1$, is called a \emph{coboundary}.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilde_displacement}
 \widetilde{\mathbf D}(w):=c(w)\mathbf D(w)
\end{equation}
defines a unitary representation of $D$ on $L^2(M)$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Isotropy makes $\beta|_{D\times D}$ symmetric, hence a coboundary
by~\cite[Lemma 7.2]{kleppner1965multipliers}.
Then Equation~\eqref{eq:ccr_semi_displacement_general} gives
$\widetilde{\mathbf D}(w_1)\widetilde{\mathbf D}(w_2)
=\widetilde{\mathbf D}(w_1+w_2)$; unitarity follows from
$|c(w)|=1$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:stab_group}
For an isotropic lattice $D$ and a phase function $c$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:phase_coboundary}, the \emph{lattice stabilizer group} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stabgroup}
 \mathcal S_D:=\{\widetilde{\mathbf D}(w):w\in D\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:rieffel_bundle_decomposition}
The evaluation map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rieffel_fiberization_map}
 \mathcal U_D^0:\mathscr S(M)\to\Gamma(\mathscr E_D),
 \qquad (\mathcal U_D^0\xi)(\chi)=[\xi]_\chi,
\end{equation}
extends to a unitary Bloch--Floquet transform
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rieffel_direct_integral}
 \mathcal U_D:L^2(M)\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}
 \int_{\widehat D}^{\oplus}E_{D,\chi}\,d\chi.
\end{equation}
For $a\in \mathrm C^*(D)$, $w\in D$, and $\xi\in L^2(M)$,
\begin{align}
 (\mathcal U_D(a\cdot\xi))(\chi)
 &=\widehat a(\chi)(\mathcal U_D\xi)(\chi),
 \label{eq:rieffel_fiberwise_action}\\

\label{lem:full_lift_pfaffian}
Let $M={\mathbb{R}}^p\times{\mathbb{Z}}^q\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{{\boldsymbol{c}}}$ with $p,q,k\geq0$, and put
$C_{{\boldsymbol{c}}}:=c_1\cdots c_k$.

Eq.~\eqref{eq:full_lift_covering}, with lifted coordinates
$(x,\xi\,|\,n,\theta\,|\,a,b)$.

\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{per}}:=\ker\pi
 =\{0\}^{2p+q}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^q\times{\boldsymbol{c}}\,{\mathbb{Z}}^k
 \times{\boldsymbol{c}}\,{\mathbb{Z}}^k.
 \label{eq:full_lift_periods}
\end{equation}

\emph{full lift}.
By part~(i) below, $\Lambda=T{\mathbb{Z}}^{2(p+q+k)}$ for an invertible
\emph{stabilizer generator matrix} $T$.

Eq.~\eqref{eq:symplectic-form}, and call
$T_{\mathrm{dual}}:=T\varTheta^{-1}$ the \emph{logical matrix}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $\Lambda$ is a full-rank lattice in ${\mathbb{R}}^{2(p+q+k)}$ with
 $\Lambda_{\mathrm{per}}\subseteq\Lambda\subseteq\widetilde G$ and
 $\Lambda/\Lambda_{\mathrm{per}}\cong D$.
 Conversely, every full-rank lattice between $\Lambda_{\mathrm{per}}$

 \item $D$ is isotropic if and only if $\varTheta$ is an integer matrix.
 \item The covolume is
 $|G/D|_\mu=|\det T|/C_{{\boldsymbol{c}}}=|\operatorname{Pf}\varTheta|$.
 In particular, $K=|\det T|/C_{{\boldsymbol{c}}}=|\operatorname{Pf}\varTheta|$
 when $D$ is isotropic.
 \item The symplectic dual
 $\Lambda^\perp:=\{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2(p+q+k)}:v^{\intercal}J\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}\}$

 induces an isomorphism
 ${\mathbb{Z}}^{2(p+q+k)}/\varTheta{\mathbb{Z}}^{2(p+q+k)}\cong D^\perp/D$, and
 $\rho\bigl(\pi(T_{\mathrm{dual}}\ell),\pi(T_{\mathrm{dual}}\ell')\bigr)
 =e^{-2\pi i\,\ell^{\intercal}\varTheta^{-1}\ell'}$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:full_lift_gkp_concatenation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:full_lift_pfaffian} extends the concatenation picture of
Sec.~\ref{ex:mixed-qudit} to every configuration
group $M={\mathbb{R}}^p\times{\mathbb{Z}}^q\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{{\boldsymbol{c}}}$.
We keep each oscillator and replace each rotor and each qudit by one
oscillator.
The $i$th qudit is encoded in the square GKP code with
stabilizers $\mathbf D(\radical"270370{c_i},0)$ and $\mathbf D(0,\radical"270370{c_i})$, via
$\ket{j}\mapsto\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\ket{(j+c_im)/\radical"270370{c_i}}_{\mathrm{osc}}$.
A rotor is encoded in the oscillator states fixed by the single
displacement $\mathbf D(0,1)=e^{2\pi i\hat x}$, via
$\ket{n}_{\mathrm{rot}}\mapsto\ket{n}_{\mathrm{osc}}$.
This constraint confines position to the integers.
Rotor angular momentum thus becomes oscillator position, and the rotor
angle becomes oscillator momentum modulo one.
The resulting injective encoding
$V\colon\mathscr S'(M)\to\mathscr S'({\mathbb{R}}^{p+q+k})$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 V\,\mathbf D(\pi(v))=\mathbf D(Rv)\,V,
 \qquad
 R:=I_{2p}\oplus I_{2q}\oplus{\boldsymbol{c}}^{-1/2}\oplus{\boldsymbol{c}}^{-1/2},
 \label{eq:full_lift_concatenation}
\end{equation}
for every lift $v\in\widetilde G$.
Let $J_{\mathrm{osc}}^{(r)}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I_r\\-I_r&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
and let $J_{\mathrm{std}}:=J_{\mathrm{osc}}^{(p)}\oplus J_{\mathrm{osc}}^{(q)}
\oplus J_{\mathrm{osc}}^{(k)}$ be the standard form of $p+q+k$
oscillators.
Then $R^{\intercal}J_{\mathrm{std}}R=J$.
Likewise, $R^{\intercal}J'_{\mathrm{std}}R=J'$, where $J'_{\mathrm{std}}$ is
obtained from $J_{\mathrm{std}}$ by replacing its negative entries by zero.
The inner stabilizers $\mathbf D(Rv)$ with $v\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{per}}$,
together with the encoded stabilizers $c(\pi(v))\mathbf D(Rv)$ with
$v\in\Lambda$, generate the concatenated stabilizer group.
By the second identity and Eq.~\eqref{eq:lifted_cocycle_bicharacter}, the
phases $c(\pi(v))$ trivialize the oscillator cocycle on $R\Lambda$.
The label lattice of this group is the rescaled full lift $R\Lambda$, a GKP
lattice with symplectic Gram matrix $\varTheta$.
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:full_lift_concatenation}, the GKP code of $R\Lambda$
with phases $c(\pi(v))$ contains $V(\mathcal C)$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:code_dimension}, applied to $M$ and to
${\mathbb{R}}^{p+q+k}$, gives both spaces the dimension
$|\det RT|=|\det T|/C_{{\boldsymbol{c}}}=K$.
Hence the two codes coincide, and
$(R\Lambda)^\perp/R\Lambda=R\Lambda^\perp/R\Lambda
\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{2(p+q+k)}/\varTheta{\mathbb{Z}}^{2(p+q+k)}$.
Conversely, an isotropic full-rank lattice in ${\mathbb{R}}^{2(p+q+k)}$
that contains $R\Lambda_{\mathrm{per}}$ lies in
$(R\Lambda_{\mathrm{per}})^\perp=R\widetilde G$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:full_lift_pfaffian}, it equals $R\pi^{-1}(D)$ for a
unique isotropic lattice $D\subset G$.
The case $p=q=0$ is the concatenation of Sec.~\ref{ex:mixed-qudit}.
For split rotor lattices, the case $p=k=0$ inverts the oscillator-to-rotor
construction of Sec.~\ref{ex:rotor-gkp} with $B_0={\mathbb{Z}}^q$.

The correspondence identifies ideal codespaces, stabilizer groups, and
logical displacement groups.
It does not identify error sets.
Indeed, an oscillator displacement $\mathbf D(v)$ with
$v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2(p+q+k)}\setminus R\widetilde G$ fails to commute with the inner
stabilizers.
It also does not identify Clifford groups.
Every $W\in\mathrm{GL}(2(p+q+k),{\mathbb{Z}})$ with $W^{\intercal}\varTheta W=\varTheta$
gives a Gaussian symplectic map $RTWT^{-1}R^{-1}$ that preserves
$R\Lambda$.
However, $TWT^{-1}$ descends to an element of
$\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{sp}}(D)$ exactly when it maps
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{per}}$ into itself.
For example, the two-mode GKP code of the ququart lattice $D_{\alpha,b}$
of Sec.~\ref{ex:osc-rotor} has a Gaussian logical Fourier gate.
In the basis $(w_1,\ldots,w_4)$ of its full lift, this gate corresponds to
\[
 W=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&1\\2&1&0&2\\2&0&1&-2\\-1&0&0&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
which maps the logical classes $[z_X]\mapsto-[z_Z]$ and
$[z_Z]\mapsto[z_X]$ but does not preserve $\Lambda_{\mathrm{per}}$.
In contrast, no element of $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{sp}}(D_{\alpha,b})$
implements a logical Fourier gate (Sec.~\ref{subsec:clifford_examples}).

\label{prop:symplectic_implementer}
Every $S\in\operatorname{Sp}(G,\rho)$ admits a continuous phase function
$a_S:G\to\mathbb T$ and a unitary $\mathcal U_S$ on $L^2(M)$ such that

\begin{equation}

 =a_S(x)\mathbf D(Sx),
 \qquad x\in G.
 \label{eq:clifford_implementer}
\end{equation}
For a fixed phase function $a_S$, the implementer $\mathcal U_S$ is unique
up to an overall scalar phase.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Symplecticity makes
$\delta_S(x,y):=\beta(Sx,Sy)/\beta(x,y)$ a continuous symmetric cocycle.
Thus $G\times\mathbb T$, with multiplication
$(x,t)(y,u)=(x+y,tu\delta_S(x,y))$, is an abelian LCA group.
By the Pontryagin character-extension theorem, the identity character
on $\{0\}\times\mathbb T$ extends to a continuous character
$h(x,t)=b(x)t$ of this group. Its multiplicativity gives
$b(x)b(y)=\delta_S(x,y)b(x+y)$, so $a_S=b^{-1}$ satisfies
\[
 a_S(x+y)=a_S(x)a_S(y)\delta_S(x,y).
\]
Consequently $(x,t)\mapsto(Sx,a_S(x)t)$ is an automorphism of the
Heisenberg group with multiplier $\beta$.
The Segal--Shale--Weil construction supplies its unitary implementer
and continuous Schwartz preservation
\cite[Chap.~I, \S\S10--12]{weil1964unitaires}.
Uniqueness up to scalar phase follows from irreducibility of the
displacement representation.

$2w(z)<d_w(D)$.  If $d_w(D)=\infty$, the right-hand side is finite,
which is again a contradiction.  Thus the chosen class is $z+D$.

<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{prop:fe_coefficients}
The map $\varphi\mapsto(\varphi_{k,n,w})$,
$\varphi_{k,n,w}:=\langle e_{k,n,w},\varphi\rangle$, is a topological
isomorphism of $\mathscr S(M)$ onto the space of rapidly decreasing sequences
indexed by $\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^p\times\mathbb Z^q\times\mathbb Z_{{\boldsymbol{c}}}$.  Its Fr\'echet
topology is equivalently given by the seminorms
\begin{equation}
 p_R(\varphi):=\sup_{k,n,w}\omega_{k,n}^{\,R}\,|\varphi_{k,n,w}|,
 \qquad R\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}.
 \label{eq:fe_coefficient_seminorms}
\end{equation}
The coefficient map $u\mapsto(u_{k,n,w})$, with
$u_{k,n,w}:=\langle u,e_{k,n,w}\rangle$, identifies the continuous anti-dual
$\mathscr S'(M)$ with the sequences satisfying
\begin{equation}
 |u_{k,n,w}|\leq C\,\omega_{k,n}^{\,R_0}
 \qquad\text{for some }C>0,\ R_0\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}.
 \label{eq:fe_distribution_growth}
\end{equation}
The corresponding functional is uniquely given by the absolutely
convergent series
$\langle u,\varphi\rangle=\sum_{k,n,w}u_{k,n,w}\overline{\varphi_{k,n,w}}$.

<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
For a single slice $\varphi(\cdot,n,w)\in\mathscr S(\mathbb R^p)$ the
equivalence between the seminorms \eqref{eq:schwartz_seminorms} and the
weighted Hermite seminorms is \cite[Sec.~2.2, Eqs.~(12)--(13) and

every seminorm \eqref{eq:schwartz_seminorms}, because the $q_R$-seminorms of

Continuity of $u$ gives $|\langle u,\varphi\rangle|\leq Cp_{R_0}(\varphi)$ for

Hermite series converge in $\mathscr S(M)$, so the coefficients determine $u$.

\label{thm:main_dual}
Let $Z\in\mathscr T_n$ have rational entries. Then there exist $g\in\mathrm{SO}(n,n\mid\mathbb Z)$ and a finite abelian group ${\mathbb{Z}}_{{\boldsymbol{c}}}$ such that, for every $\varTheta\in\mathscr T_n$ with $\varTheta-Z$ invertible, there are embedding maps $T,T_{\mathrm{dual}}:L^*\to H^*$ for the symplectic Gram matrices $\varTheta$ and $-g\cdot\varTheta$, respectively. Here $M={\mathbb{R}}^p\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{{\boldsymbol{c}}}$, $H^*={\mathbb{R}}^p\times{\mathbb{R}}^{*p}\times{\mathbb{R}}^k\times{\mathbb{R}}^{*k}$, and
\[
T_{\mathrm{dual}}({\mathbb{Z}}^n)=T({\mathbb{Z}}^n)^\perp.
\]
Moreover, writing $\widetilde T=\gamma\circ T$ for the continuous projection of $T$, the construction determines $\widetilde T$, up to left multiplication by a $J_{\textsc{cv}}$-symplectic matrix, through
\begin{equation}
\widetilde T^{\intercal}J_{\textsc{cv}}\widetilde T=\varTheta-Z.
\label{eq:projected-embedding-identity}
\end{equation}
