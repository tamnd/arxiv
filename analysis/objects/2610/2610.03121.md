<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:dn-triple}
  Let $n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$. A positive integral $D(n)$-triple is a set
  $\{a,b,c\}\subset \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ of three pairwise distinct integers such
  that $ab+n$, $ac+n$ and $bc+n$ are all squares in $\mathbb{Z}$. In this
  paper the square $0$ is allowed.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Some treatments of Diophantine $m$-tuples exclude or separate
  zero-square cases; we include them, while negative integers are not
  squares in $\mathbb{Z}$. This affects the bounded enumeration for $n=-3$
  but not nonsingularity once $nabc(a-b)(a-c)(b-c)\ne0$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:assoc-curve}
  Let $n\in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$ and let
  $a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ be pairwise distinct. Then the smooth projective
  closure of the affine cubic \eqref{eq:Eabcn} defines an elliptic
  curve $E_{a,b,c}^{(n)}/\mathbb{Q}$. Under the substitution $X = abc\,x$,
  $Y = abc\,y$ it has the integral Weierstrass model
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:integral-model}
    Y^{2} \;=\; X^{3} + n(ab+ac+bc)\,X^{2}
            + abc\,n^{2}(a+b+c)\,X + (abc)^{2}n^{3}
  \end{equation}
  The discriminant of the displayed Weierstrass model
  \eqref{eq:integral-model} is
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:discriminant}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{mod}}(E_{a,b,c}^{(n)})
    =16(abc)^{2}n^{6}(a-b)^{2}(a-c)^{2}(b-c)^{2}
  \end{equation}
  The non-trivial rational $2$-torsion points of $E_{a,b,c}^{(n)}$ on this
  model are
  \[
    (-bcn,0),\qquad (-acn,0),\qquad (-abn,0);
  \]
  hence
  $E_{a,b,c}^{(n)}(\mathbb{Q})[2] \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Expanding $(ax+n)(bx+n)(cx+n)$ and substituting $X=abc\,x$,
  $Y=abc\,y$ yields~\eqref{eq:integral-model}. The roots of the cubic
  in $X$ are $-bcn$, $-acn$, $-abn$, with pairwise differences
  $cn(a-b)$, $bn(a-c)$, $an(b-c)$, so the model discriminant is
  \[
    16\{cn(a-b)\}^{2}\{bn(a-c)\}^{2}\{an(b-c)\}^{2},
  \]
  which is~\eqref{eq:discriminant}. Since $nabc\ne 0$ and $a,b,c$ are
  pairwise distinct, this discriminant is nonzero; hence the cubic
  is nonsingular and defines an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$. The same
  three roots give the stated nontrivial rational $2$-torsion
  points.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:legendre-twist}
  Let $n\in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$, let $1\le a<b<c$ be integers, and let
  \[
    E=E_{a,b,c}^{(n)}:\quad
    Y^{2}=(X+bcn)(X+acn)(X+abn)
  \]
  be the integral model associated with
  $y^{2}=(ax+n)(bx+n)(cx+n)$. Let $p$ be an odd prime with
  \[
    p\nmid abc\,n(a-b)(a-c)(b-c),
  \]
  (equivalently, the displayed integral model has nonsingular
  reduction at $p$), and let $\overline E_p/\mathbb{F}_p$ be the reduction.
  Let $\chi_p$ denote the quadratic character of $\mathbb{F}_p$, extended
  by $\chi_p(0)=0$. Define
  \[
    \lambda=\frac{b(c-a)}{c(b-a)}\in \mathbb{F}_p\setminus\{0,1\},
    \qquad
    \delta=cn(b-a)\in \mathbb{F}_p^{\times},
  \]
  and let $L_{\lambda}:v^{2}=u(u-1)(u-\lambda)$ be the corresponding
  Legendre curve over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Then the change of variables
  \[
    u=\frac{X+bcn}{cn(b-a)},\qquad
    Z=\frac{Y}{cn(b-a)}
  \]
  identifies $\overline E_p$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ with
  \[
    C_{\lambda,\delta}:\quad
    Z^{2}=\delta\,u(u-1)(u-\lambda).
  \]
  For an elliptic curve $C/\mathbb{F}_p$, write $a_p(C)=p+1-\#C(\mathbb{F}_p)$. The
  curve $C_{\lambda,\delta}$ is the quadratic twist of $L_\lambda$
  by $\delta$. If $\delta$ is a square, it is $\mathbb{F}_p$-isomorphic to
  $L_\lambda$; if $\delta$ is a nonsquare, it is the twist by the
  nontrivial quadratic character. At exceptional $j$-invariants,
  that twist may still be $\mathbb{F}_p$-isomorphic to $L_\lambda$.
  In either case,
  \[
    a_p(\overline E_p)=\chi_p(\delta)\,a_p(L_{\lambda}),
  \]
  hence
  \[
    \#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_p)
    =
    p+1-\chi_p(\delta)\,a_p(L_{\lambda}).
  \]
  In particular, with $a,b,c$ fixed and $p$ as above, the dependence
  on $n$ among values with $p\nmid n$ enters only through
  $\chi_p(n)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The hypotheses on $p$ make $b-a$, $c-a$, $b-c$, $c$, and $n$
  nonzero in $\mathbb{F}_p$, so $\delta\in\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ and $\lambda\ne 0$.
  If $\lambda=1$, then $b(c-a)=c(b-a)$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$, hence $a(b-c)=0$,
  contradicting the good-reduction hypothesis; thus
  $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_p\setminus\{0,1\}$.

  Setting $X=-bcn+\delta u$ and $Y=\delta Z$ with $\delta=cn(b-a)$
  gives, over $\mathbb{F}_p$,
  \[
    X+bcn=\delta u,\qquad
    X+acn=\delta(u-1),\qquad
    X+abn=\delta(u-\lambda)
  \]
  The reduced equation becomes
  $\delta^2 Z^{2}=\delta^{3}u(u-1)(u-\lambda)$, and division by
  $\delta^2$ yields
  \[
    Z^{2}=\delta\,u(u-1)(u-\lambda),
  \]
  which is $C_{\lambda,\delta}$. The substitution extends to the
  projective closures by sending the point at infinity of
  $\overline E_p$ to that of $C_{\lambda,\delta}$, giving the
  asserted $\mathbb{F}_p$-isomorphism.

  Counting points on $C_{\lambda,\delta}$ gives
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    \#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_p)
    &=p+1+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_p}
      \chi_p\!\bigl(\delta u(u-1)(u-\lambda)\bigr)\\
    &=p+1+\chi_p(\delta)
      \sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_p}\chi_p\!\bigl(u(u-1)(u-\lambda)\bigr),
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  since $\delta\in\mathbb{F}_p^\times$. For the Legendre curve,
  \[
    a_p(L_{\lambda})
    =-\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_p}\chi_p\!\bigl(u(u-1)(u-\lambda)\bigr),
  \]
  so the trace and point-count formulae follow. Finally,
  $\chi_p(\delta)=\chi_p(c(b-a))\chi_p(n)$, so with $a,b,c,p$ fixed
  the remaining dependence on $n$ among values with $p\nmid n$ is
  through $\chi_p(n)$; this concerns the displayed family only and
  does not assert that $\{a,b,c\}$ remains a $D(n)$-triple as $n$
  varies.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:forced-cofactor}
  Let $n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$, let $\{a,b,c\}$ be a positive
  integral $D(n)$-triple, and let $p$ be an odd
  prime of good reduction for $E_{a,b,c}^{(n)}$, with reduction
  $\overline E_p/\mathbb{F}_p$. Then
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $4 \mid \#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_{p})$;
    \item $\overline E_p$ is non-anomalous.
  \end{enumerate}
  If moreover $p \ge 11$, then $\#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_{p}) > 4$ by
  Hasse's bound, so with $q$ the largest prime divisor of
  $\#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_{p})$ and $h=\#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_{p})/q$ one has
  $h \ge 4$. The primes $p=3,5,7$ are not excluded from~(i)
  or~(ii); they are excluded only from this cofactor lower bound.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since $p$ is odd, Equation~\eqref{eq:four-divides} gives~(i). If
  $\#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_{p}) = p$, then $4 \mid p$, impossible for odd
  $p$, giving~(ii). For $p \ge 11$, Hasse's bound gives
  $\#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_{p}) \ge p + 1 - 2\sqrt{p} > 4$. Set
  $N=\#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_{p})$ and let $q$ be the largest prime
  divisor of $N$. If $q=2$, then $N$ is a power of $2$, and $N>4$
  forces $N\ge 8$, hence $h=N/2\ge 4$. If $q$ is odd, then
  $4\mid N/q=h$, so again $h\ge 4$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:h4-criterion}
  With the notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:legendre-twist} and $p\ge 11$, the
  reduction $\overline E_p/\mathbb{F}_p$ has cofactor
  $h(\overline E_p/\mathbb{F}_p)=4$ (relative to the largest prime divisor of
  $\#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_p)$) if and only if
  \[
    \frac{p+1-\chi_p(\delta)\,a_p(L_{\lambda})}{4}
  \]
  is prime ($2$ included). If $\overline E'_p$ denotes the
  quadratic twist of $\overline E_p$ by any nonsquare in
  $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$, then
  $h(\overline E'_p/\mathbb{F}_p)=4$ if and only if
  \[
    \frac{p+1+\chi_p(\delta)\,a_p(L_{\lambda})}{4}
  \]
  is prime ($2$ included).
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:legendre-twist},
  $\#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_p)=p+1-\chi_p(\delta)\,a_p(L_{\lambda})$. The
  displayed quotient is an integer because the split model has
  three distinct rational points of order $2$. Since $p\ge11$,
  Hasse's bound gives $\#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_p)>4$. If the cofactor is
  $4$, then the quotient is its largest prime divisor. Conversely,
  if $\#\overline E_p(\mathbb{F}_p)=4r$ with $r$ prime, then $r$ is the
  largest prime divisor, including when $r=2$, so the cofactor is $4$.

  The quadratic twist by a nonsquare has the opposite Frobenius trace
  and order $p+1+\chi_p(\delta)\,a_p(L_{\lambda})$. Explicitly, for
  any nonsquare $d\in\mathbb{F}_p^\times$, the twist by $d$ of
  $C_{\lambda,\delta}:Z^2=\delta\,u(u-1)(u-\lambda)$ is represented
  by $Z^2=d\delta\,u(u-1)(u-\lambda)$. The points $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$,
  $(\lambda,0)$, $O$ are still $\mathbb{F}_p$-rational, so the same
  $2$-torsion argument applies; the twist order is divisible by $4$
  and greater than $4$ for $p\ge 11$, giving the second condition.
