<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
There is a constant \(c_E>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large
\(X\),
\[
 \#\left\{p\le X:
 \begin{array}{l}
 p\text{ prime},\quad p\equiv5\pmod8,\\
 n_p\text{ squarefree},\quad \Omega(n_p)\le2
 \end{array}\right\}
 \ge c_E\frac{X}{(\log X)^2}.
\]
The primes counted may be chosen so that every prime divisor of
\(n_p\) is at least \(X^{1/8}\). Here \(\Omega(n)\) counts prime
factors with multiplicity, with \(\Omega(1)=0\).
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:content}
Every squarefree ideal \(\mathfrak a\) has a unique factorization
\[
 \mathfrak a=(c)\mathfrak b,
\]
where \(c=c(\mathfrak a)\) is a positive odd squarefree rational integer,
\((c)\) and \(\mathfrak b\) are coprime, and \(N\mathfrak b\) is squarefree.
Moreover,
\[
 d(\mathfrak a)=cN\mathfrak b,\qquad N\mathfrak a=c^2N\mathfrak b.
\]
If \(\mathfrak a\mid(\alpha)\), then
\(c\mid\gcd(\Re\alpha,\Im\alpha)\).
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
At a split odd prime, include \(\ell\) in \(c\) if both prime
ideals above \(\ell\) divide \(\mathfrak a\); if exactly one of them
divides \(\mathfrak a\), place that prime ideal in \(\mathfrak b\).
An inert prime ideal \((\ell)\), of norm \(\ell^2\), contributes
\(\ell\) to \(c\) when it divides \(\mathfrak a\).
The possible single factor \((1+i)\) is placed in \(\mathfrak b\).
Unique factorization of ideals proves all the assertions.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:content-tail}
For fixed \(C>0\) and integer \(J\ge0\), there is a constant
\(L_J\) such that, for \(X\ge3\) and \(Z\ge1\),
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathcal O,\ 0<N\alpha\le CX\\
                  \gcd(\Re\alpha,\Im\alpha)>Z}}
       \tau(N\alpha)^J
 \ll_{C,J} XZ^{-1/2}(\log X)^{L_J}.
\]
The greatest common divisor is positive, including when one
coordinate is zero.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For \(R\ge1\), a disk of radius \(R\) contains \(O(R^2)\)
nonzero Gaussian integers. Hence the number of elements in
the indicated sum is at most
\[
 \sum_{Z<g\le\sqrt{CX}}
       \#\{\alpha:0<N\alpha\le CX,\ g\mid\alpha\}
 \ll_C X\sum_{g>Z}g^{-2}\ll_C X/Z.
\]
Write \(r_2(n)=\#\{\alpha\in\mathcal O:N\alpha=n\}\) for \(n\ge1\).
The sum-of-two-squares formula gives \(r_2(n)\le4\tau(n)\).
For any fixed nonnegative integer \(t\), it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divisor-moment}
 \sum_{0<N\alpha\le CX}\tau(N\alpha)^t
 \le4\sum_{n\le CX}\tau(n)^{t+1}
 \ll_{C,t}X(\log X)^{L'_t}.
\end{equation}
One may use \(\tau(n)^k\le\tau_{2^k}(n)\) and the elementary
bound \(\sum_{n\le U}\tau_j(n)\ll_j U(1+\log U)^{j-1}\).
Here \(U\ge1\), and \(\tau_j(n)\) counts ordered \(j\)-tuples
of positive integers with product \(n\).
The first inequality follows prime by prime by encoding the
exponent levels of a \(k\)-tuple of divisors by the subsets
containing each level. Cauchy's inequality now proves the lemma.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:transfer}
For \(X\ge3\), let \(m_X(\alpha)\) be a nonnegative weight on
nonzero Gaussian integers, supported on \(N\alpha\le CX\), with
\[
 m_X(\alpha)\ll\tau(N\alpha)^M
\]
for fixed \(C>0\) and a nonnegative integer \(M\). Suppose
\[
 A_X(\mathfrak a):=\sum_{\mathfrak a\mid(\alpha)}m_X(\alpha)
       =Y_X\gamma_X(\mathfrak a)+R_X(\mathfrak a),
\]
where \(0\le Y_X\ll X(\log X)^M\), \(\gamma_X(\mathfrak a)\ge0\), and
\[
 \gamma_X(\mathfrak a)\ll\frac{\tau(N\mathfrak a)^{B_0}}{N\mathfrak a}
\]
for a fixed nonnegative integer \(B_0\).
Assume that for every \(A>0\) some
\(B=B(A)\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ideal-hypothesis}
 \sum_{N\mathfrak a\le\sqrt X/(\log X)^B}|R_X(\mathfrak a)|
 \ll_A\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}.
\end{equation}
For squarefree positive integers \(d\), put
\[
 A_X[d]=\sum_{d\mid N\alpha}m_X(\alpha),\qquad
 g_X(d)=\mu(d)\sum_{d(\mathfrak a)=d}
                \mu_{\mathcal O}(\mathfrak a)\gamma_X(\mathfrak a),
\]
where the inner sum is over squarefree ideals.
Then for every \(A>0\) and integer \(J\ge0\) some \(B=B(A,J)\)
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer-conclusion}
 \sum_{d\le\sqrt X/(\log X)^B}\mu(d)^2\tau(d)^J
       |A_X[d]-Y_Xg_X(d)|
 \ll_{A,J}\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}.
\end{equation}
Constants may depend on the fixed parameters in the hypotheses.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
First we upgrade \eqref{eq:ideal-hypothesis} to include any
fixed weight \(\tau(N\mathfrak a)^J\). For \(1\le Q\le\sqrt X\),
Cauchy's inequality gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{N\mathfrak a\le Q}\tau(N\mathfrak a)^J|R_X(\mathfrak a)|
 &\le
 \left(\sum_{N\mathfrak a\le Q}|R_X(\mathfrak a)|\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_{N\mathfrak a\le Q}\tau(N\mathfrak a)^{2J}|R_X(\mathfrak a)|\right)^{1/2}.
\end{align*}
For the second factor use \(|R_X|\le A_X+Y_X\gamma_X\).
The number of ideal divisors of \((\alpha)\) is at most
\(\tau(N\alpha)^2\), and \(N\mathfrak a\mid N\alpha\) if
\(\mathfrak a\mid(\alpha)\). Interchanging sums and using
\eqref{eq:divisor-moment} therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{N\mathfrak a\le Q}\tau(N\mathfrak a)^{2J}A_X(\mathfrak a)
 \ll\sum_{0<N\alpha\le CX}\tau(N\alpha)^{M+2J+2}
 \ll X(\log X)^{C_1}.
\]
There are at most \(\tau(n)\) Gaussian ideals of norm \(n\).
The main-term contribution is consequently bounded by
\[
 Y_X\sum_{n\le Q}\frac{\tau(n)^{2J+B_0+1}}n
 \ll X(\log X)^{C_2}.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:ideal-hypothesis} with a larger logarithmic
saving proves its divisor-weighted form.

For squarefree \(d\), local inclusion--exclusion gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-transfer}
 \mathbf 1_{d\mid N\alpha}
 =\mu(d)\sum_{d(\mathfrak a)=d}
              \mu_{\mathcal O}(\mathfrak a)\mathbf 1_{\mathfrak a\mid(\alpha)}.
\end{equation}
At a split prime this is \eqref{eq:split-union}; at an inert
or ramified prime there is a single ideal-divisibility condition.
Multiplication over the prime factors of \(d\) proves
\eqref{eq:exact-transfer}. Thus
\[
 A_X[d]-Y_Xg_X(d)
 =\mu(d)\sum_{d(\mathfrak a)=d}\mu_{\mathcal O}(\mathfrak a)R_X(\mathfrak a).
\]

Set \(Z=(\log X)^L\) and \(D=\sqrt X/(\log X)^B\).
For terms with \(c(\mathfrak a)\le Z\) and \(d(\mathfrak a)\le D\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:content} gives \(N\mathfrak a\le DZ\).
Since \(\tau(d(\mathfrak a))\le\tau(N\mathfrak a)\), the weighted ideal estimate
bounds their total contribution after increasing \(B\) by \(L\).

For the terms with \(c(\mathfrak a)>Z\), bound the remainder by its
counting and main-term parts. For the former, interchange sums.
Every contributing \(\alpha\) has
\(\gcd(\Re\alpha,\Im\alpha)>Z\).
There are at most \(\tau(N\alpha)^2\) incident ideals, and each
satisfies \(\tau(d(\mathfrak a))^J\le\tau(N\alpha)^J\). Hence
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{\substack{d(\mathfrak a)\le D\\c(\mathfrak a)>Z}}
       \tau(d(\mathfrak a))^J A_X(\mathfrak a)
 &\ll
 \sum_{\substack{0<N\alpha\le CX\\
                  \gcd(\Re\alpha,\Im\alpha)>Z}}
       \tau(N\alpha)^{M+J+2}\\
 &\ll XZ^{-1/2}(\log X)^{C_3}
\end{align*}
by Lemma~\ref{lem:content-tail}.
For the main term, write \(\mathfrak a=(c)\mathfrak b\) and \(n=N\mathfrak b\).
The hypotheses and the bound for the number of ideals of norm
\(n\) imply
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{\substack{d(\mathfrak a)\le D\\c(\mathfrak a)>Z}}
       \tau(d(\mathfrak a))^J\gamma_X(\mathfrak a)
 &\ll \sum_{c>Z}\frac{\tau(c)^{J+2B_0}}{c^2}
           \sum_{n\le D/c}\frac{\tau(n)^{J+B_0+1}}n\\
 &\ll Z^{-1/2}(\log X)^{C_4}.
\end{align*}
Indeed, for fixed \(t\),
\[
 \sum_{c>Z}\frac{\tau(c)^t}{c^2}
 \le Z^{-1/2}\sum_{c\ge1}\frac{\tau(c)^t}{c^{3/2}}
 \ll_t Z^{-1/2}.
\]
The main-term tail after multiplication by \(Y_X\) is therefore
\(\ll XZ^{-1/2}(\log X)^{M+C_4}\).
Choose \(L\) sufficiently large for both tails, then \(B\)
sufficiently large for the small-content estimate.
This proves \eqref{eq:transfer-conclusion}.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:first-distribution}
For a squarefree positive integer \(d\), let
\[
 A_d(X)=2\#\{p\le X:p\text{ prime},\
                   p\equiv1\pmod4,\ d\mid n_p\}.
\]
Define a multiplicative function \(g\) on squarefree integers by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 g(2)=\frac12,\qquad
 g(\ell)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac2{\ell-1}-\frac1{(\ell-1)^2},
       &\ell\equiv1\pmod4,\\[4pt]
 \displaystyle\frac1{\ell^2-1},&\ell\equiv3\pmod4.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For every \(A>0\) and integer \(J\ge0\), some \(B=B(A,J)\)
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-distribution}
 \sum_{d\le\sqrt X/(\log X)^B}\mu(d)^2\tau(d)^J
       |A_d(X)-g(d)\operatorname{Li}(X)|
 \ll_{A,J}\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Take the weight \(m_X(\alpha)\) to be the indicator that
\(\alpha=(\pi-1)/b\) for \(\pi\in\mathcal P\), \(N\pi\le X\).
Its support consists of distinct nonzero Gaussian integers
with \(N\alpha\ll X\).
The divisibility condition \(\mathfrak a\mid(\alpha)\) is equivalent to
\(\pi\equiv1\pmod{b\mathfrak a}\). Equation~\eqref{eq:ray-bv} and
the preceding deletion estimate give
\eqref{eq:ideal-hypothesis} with
\[
 Y_X=\operatorname{Li}(X),\qquad \gamma(\mathfrak a)=\frac4{\Phi(b\mathfrak a)}
       \ll\frac{\tau(N\mathfrak a)^2}{N\mathfrak a}.
\]
The factor \(Nb=8\) is absorbed in the logarithmic cutoff.
For odd ideals, \(\gamma(\mathfrak a)=1/\Phi(\mathfrak a)\); adjoining
a single factor \((1+i)\) multiplies the density by \(1/2\).
The local identity \eqref{eq:exact-transfer} therefore yields
\eqref{eq:density}. Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} proves the result.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:switching}
For every \(A>0\), there is \(B=B(A)>0\) such that, with
\(Q=\sqrt X/(\log X)^B\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:switched-ideal}
 \sum_{N\mathfrak a\le Q}\max_{(\alpha,\mathfrak a)=1}
 \left|
 \#\{\omega\in\mathcal W_X:\omega\equiv\alpha\pmod\mathfrak a\}
 -\frac{\#\{\omega\in\mathcal W_X:(\omega,\mathfrak a)=1\}}{\Phi(\mathfrak a)}
 \right|
 \ll_A\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For an even ideal, both counts in \eqref{eq:switched-ideal}
are zero, so we consider odd moduli.
Choose \(\delta=(\log X)^{-d_0}\), with \(d_0\) to be fixed.
Partition \([z,y)\) into the intervals
\[
 I_T=[T,\min\{(1+\delta)T,y\}),\qquad
 T=z(1+\delta)^k,\qquad k\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0},\quad T<y.
\]
Their number satisfies \(H\ll(\log X)^{d_0+1}\).
In the block \(N\pi_3\in I_T\), replace the product cutoff
by \(8N(\pi_1\pi_2)T\le X\).
Let \(\mathcal W'_X\) be the union of the resulting sets.
Representations remain unique, and
\[
 \mathcal W_X\subseteq\mathcal W'_X,\qquad
 \mathcal W'_X\setminus\mathcal W_X
 \subseteq\{\omega:X<N\omega\le(1+\delta)X\}.
\]
For every ideal \(\mathfrak a\) and every residue class, the number
of lattice points in this annulus is
\[
 O\left(\frac{\delta X}{N\mathfrak a}
       +\frac{\sqrt X}{\sqrt{N\mathfrak a}}+1\right).
\]
This follows by counting points in the two disks using the
area and perimeter bounds for a rotated square lattice of
covolume \(N\mathfrak a\).
Summing over \(N\mathfrak a\le Q\) by
\eqref{eq:elementary-ideal-sums} gives
\[
 O(\delta X\log X+\sqrt{XQ}+Q).
\]
The change in the coprime main term is at most
\(O((\delta X+\sqrt X)(\log X)^8)\).
Thus replacing \(\mathcal W_X\) by \(\mathcal W'_X\) costs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-error}
 O((\delta X+X^{3/4})(\log X)^8)
\end{equation}
for \(Q\le\sqrt X\). The boundary is estimated for the union
as a whole, so this error is not multiplied by \(H\).

Fix a block \(I_T\). Let \(a(\alpha)\) and \(c_T(\beta)\)
be the indicators, respectively, of
\[
 \alpha=\pi_3\in\mathcal P,\quad N\pi_3\in I_T,
\]
and
\[
 \beta=\pi_1\pi_2,\quad
 \pi_1,\pi_2\in\mathcal P,\quad
 y<N\pi_2<N\pi_1,\quad N\beta\le X/(8T).
\]
The factorization defining \(\beta\) is unique. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energies}
 \sum_\alpha|a(\alpha)|^2\ll T,\qquad
 \sum_\beta|c_T(\beta)|^2\ll X/T.
\end{equation}
Character orthogonality bounds the sum of discrepancies of
this block by
\[
 4\sum_{\substack{N\mathfrak a\le Q\\\mathfrak a\ {\rm odd}}}
       \frac1{\Phi(\mathfrak a)}
       \sum_{\substack{\chi\bmod\mathfrak a\\\chi\ne\chi_0}}
       |A_\mathfrak a(\chi)C_\mathfrak a(\chi)|,
\]
where the character sums use \(a\) and \(c_T\).
The factor four accounts for the units in
\eqref{eq:switched-set}; \(b\) is invertible modulo \(\mathfrak a\).
Induce each character from its primitive conductor \(\mathfrak f\)
and write \(\mathfrak a=\mathfrak f\mathfrak m\).
Since \(\Phi(\mathfrak f\mathfrak m)\ge\Phi(\mathfrak f)\Phi(\mathfrak m)\), enlarging
the positive summation range gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:induced-sum}
 4\sum_{\substack{N\mathfrak m\le Q\\\mathfrak m\ {\rm odd}}}\frac1{\Phi(\mathfrak m)}
  \sum_{\substack{N\mathfrak f\le Q\\\mathfrak f\ {\rm odd}}}\frac1{\Phi(\mathfrak f)}
  \sum_{\substack{\chi\bmod\mathfrak f\ {\rm primitive}\\\chi\ne\chi_0}}
       |A_\mathfrak m(\chi)C_\mathfrak m(\chi)|.
\end{equation}
Here
\[
 A_\mathfrak m(\chi)=\sum_{(\alpha,\mathfrak m)=1}a(\alpha)\chi(\alpha),
 \qquad
 C_\mathfrak m(\chi)=\sum_{(\beta,\mathfrak m)=1}c_T(\beta)\chi(\beta).
\]
Extension of a primitive character by zero already accounts
for coprimality with \(\mathfrak f\); coprimality of \(\mathfrak f\) and
\(\mathfrak m\) is not required.

Put \(R=(\log X)^r\). For \(N\mathfrak f\le R\), at most four
primary primes occurring in \(a\) can divide \(\mathfrak m\), since
their norms are at least \(X^{1/8}\) and \(N\mathfrak m\le X^{1/2}\).
Therefore, uniformly in \(\mathfrak m\),
\[
 A_\mathfrak m(\chi)=\sum_\alpha a(\alpha)\chi(\alpha)+O(1).
\]
Use \eqref{eq:small-character} and
\(|C_\mathfrak m(\chi)|\ll X/T\).
The number of primitive characters modulo \(\mathfrak f\) is at most
\(\Phi(\mathfrak f)\), and there are \(O(R)\) ideals of norm at most
\(R\). The small-conductor contribution to
\eqref{eq:induced-sum} is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-conductors}
 \ll R(\log X)^8
       \left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^C}+\frac Xz\right).
\end{equation}

For \(U<N\mathfrak f\le2U\), \(U\ge R\), Cauchy's inequality,
\eqref{eq:large-sieve}, and \eqref{eq:energies} give,
uniformly in \(\mathfrak m\),
\begin{align*}
 &\sum_{U<N\mathfrak f\le2U}\frac1{\Phi(\mathfrak f)}
       \sum_{\chi\bmod\mathfrak f}^{*}
           |A_\mathfrak m(\chi)C_\mathfrak m(\chi)|\\
 &\quad\ll
 \frac1U\bigl((U^2+T)T\bigr)^{1/2}
          \bigl((U^2+X/T)X/T\bigr)^{1/2}\\
 &\quad\ll
 \sqrt X\left(U+\sqrt T+\sqrt{X/T}+\frac{\sqrt X}{U}\right).
\end{align*}
Summing dyadic intervals up to \(Q\) and then summing
\(1/\Phi(\mathfrak m)\) bounds the large-conductor part by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:large-conductors}
 \ll\sqrt X(\log X)^9
       \left(Q+\sqrt y+\sqrt{X/z}+\frac{\sqrt X}{R}\right).
\end{equation}
After multiplication by \(\sqrt X\), the middle two
terms have sizes \(X^{2/3}\) and \(X^{15/16}\).

Given \(A>0\), one possible choice is
\[
 d_0=A+20,\qquad r=A+d_0+20,\qquad
 B=A+d_0+20,\qquad C=A+d_0+r+20.
\]
With these choices, \eqref{eq:boundary-error} and the sums
over \(H\ll(\log X)^{d_0+1}\) blocks of
\eqref{eq:small-conductors} and \eqref{eq:large-conductors}
are \(O_A(X/(\log X)^A)\).
The fixed powers of \(\log X\) multiplying \(X^{7/8}\),
\(X^{15/16}\), and smaller powers are absorbed for large \(X\).
This proves \eqref{eq:switched-ideal}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:coprimality}
For every fixed integer \(J\ge0\) and \(Q\le\sqrt X\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coprimality-removal}
 \sum_{\substack{N\mathfrak a\le Q\\\mathfrak a\ {\rm odd}}}
 \frac{\tau(N\mathfrak a)^J}{\Phi(\mathfrak a)}
 \#\{\omega\in\mathcal W_X:(\omega,\mathfrak a)\ne1\}
 \ll_J X^{7/8}(\log X)^{C_J}.
\end{equation}
Consequently the ideal-divisibility counts of \(1+\omega\)
have the form
\[
 B_X(\mathfrak a)=|\mathcal W_X|\gamma_B(\mathfrak a)+R_B(\mathfrak a),\qquad
 \gamma_B(\mathfrak a)=
 \begin{cases}
 1/\Phi(\mathfrak a),&\mathfrak a\text{ odd},\\
 0,&\mathfrak a\text{ even},
 \end{cases}
\]
and satisfy \eqref{eq:ideal-hypothesis}.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Each \(\omega\) has three odd prime ideal factors, all of norm
at least \(z\). For each such \(\mathfrak l\),
\[
 \sum_{\substack{N\mathfrak a\le Q\\\mathfrak l\mid\mathfrak a}}
       \frac{\tau(N\mathfrak a)^J}{\Phi(\mathfrak a)}
 \ll_J\frac1{N\mathfrak l}
       \sum_{N\mathfrak m\le Q}\frac{\tau(N\mathfrak m)^J}{\Phi(\mathfrak m)}
 \ll_J\frac{(\log X)^{C_J}}{N\mathfrak l}.
\]
Indeed, writing \(\mathfrak a=\mathfrak l\mathfrak m\) changes \(\Phi\) by a factor
at least \(N\mathfrak l/2\) and the divisor weight by a bounded
factor. The remaining sum is a harmonic divisor moment.
Summing first over \(\omega\), using \(|\mathcal W_X|\ll X\),
proves \eqref{eq:coprimality-removal}.

For an odd ideal, \(\mathfrak a\mid(1+\omega)\) is the reduced
residue condition \(\omega\equiv-1\pmod\mathfrak a\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:switching} gives the main term
\(\#\{\omega:(\omega,\mathfrak a)=1\}/\Phi(\mathfrak a)\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:coprimality-removal} with \(J=0\)
replaces it by \(|\mathcal W_X|/\Phi(\mathfrak a)\).

\label{prop:second-distribution}
Define a multiplicative squarefree density \(g_B\) by
\[
 g_B(2)=0,\qquad g_B(\ell)=g(\ell)\quad(\ell\text{ odd}).
\]
For every \(A>0\) and integer \(J\ge0\), some \(B=B(A,J)\)
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-distribution}
 \sum_{d\le\sqrt X/(\log X)^B}\mu(d)^2\tau(d)^J
 \left|\#\{\omega\in\mathcal W_X:d\mid N(1+\omega)\}
                -g_B(d)|\mathcal W_X|\right|
 \ll_{A,J}\frac{X}{(\log X)^A}.
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Combining \eqref{eq:chen-inequality}, \eqref{eq:W1},
\eqref{eq:W2}, \eqref{eq:W3}, and
\eqref{eq:switched-mass} gives
\[
 G(X)\ge
 \left(\frac{e^\gamma}{4}
              \left(\log\frac32-c\right)-o(1)\right)
                   \operatorname{Li}(X)V(z)
 \gg\frac{X}{(\log X)^2}
\]
by \eqref{eq:positive-constant} and
\eqref{eq:sieve-product}.
The error in \eqref{eq:squareful-error} is smaller than
this lower bound. Each rational prime contributes two
primary Gaussian primes, so division by two gives the
asserted quantitative lower bound.

All counted \(n_p\) are \(z\)-rough, hence odd.
For each counted prime, choose a primary Frobenius eigenvalue
\(\pi=a+vi\).
The primary congruence implies
\[
 a\text{ odd},\qquad v\text{ even},\qquad a+v\equiv1\pmod4.
\]
By \eqref{eq:cm-norm},
\[
 n_p=\frac{(a-1)^2+v^2}{8}.
\]
If \(v\equiv0\pmod4\), both squares in the numerator
vanish modulo \(16\), so \(n_p\) is even.
If \(v\equiv2\pmod4\), the numerator is \(8\pmod{16}\),
so \(n_p\) is odd. Thus oddness is equivalent to
\(v\equiv2\pmod4\), equivalently
\(p=a^2+v^2\equiv5\pmod8\).
The roughness assertion is part of the definition of \(G(X)\).

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:group}
For the primes counted in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, every
prime divisor of \(n_p\) is congruent to \(1\pmod4\), and
\[
 E(\mathbf F_p)\simeq
       \mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z\times\mathbf Z/(4n_p)\mathbf Z.
\]
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The integer \(n_p\) is an odd squarefree Gaussian norm,
so every prime factor splits in \(\mathbf Z[i]\).
For the group structure, write
\(E(\mathbf F_p)\simeq\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z\times
\mathbf Z/e\mathbf Z\), with \(d\mid e\);
see \cite[p.~816]{IwaniecJimenezCM}.
The four points of \(E[2]\) are rational, hence \(2\mid d\).
Since \(d^2\mid8n_p\) and \(n_p\) is odd and squarefree,
one has \(d=2\) and \(e=4n_p\).
