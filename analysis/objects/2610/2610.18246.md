\label{thm:A}
For every $d\geq0$, every closed-point lift $z$, and $0\leq a\leq d+1$,
\[
 I_a=\{x\in\mathcal O:
   \Theta_{d,z}(xy)\in p^aW(k)\text{ for every }y\in\mathcal O\}.
\]
If $e_{d,z}\in M_{d,z}$ is the specialized Euler class, the target map induces a ring isomorphism
\[
 \mathcal O/I_{d+1}\simeq M_{d,z}/(p e_{d,z}).
\]

\end{equation}

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:2.4}
Put $\phi^d:k\to k$ and $\sigma_d=W(\phi^d):W\to W$. By \eqref{eq:2.14} and uniqueness of the coefficient lift,
\[
 t_d(c)=s_d(\sigma_d(c))\qquad(c\in W).
\]
Consequently $t_d(cx)=s_d(\sigma_d(c))t_d(x)$: the target map is $\sigma_d$-semilinear for the source coefficient structure. Perfectness makes $\sigma_d$ an automorphism, and $\sigma_d(p)=p$.

Traces, determinants, duals and source base change use the source structure unless explicitly stated otherwise. A source-$W$-linear functional $\ell$ satisfies $\ell(t_d(cx))=\sigma_d(c)\ell(t_d(x))$; applying $\sigma_d^{-1}$ to its values restores $W$-linearity in $x$. This transport preserves $p$-divisibility and module lengths; compare \cite[Corollary~3.49]{BSY}. Here $\sigma_d$ always denotes the coefficient automorphism, distinct from BSY's source projection and from the Witt transfer $\sigma$ of Section~\ref{sec:4}.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:3.1}
For every $d\geq0$, reduction of $t_d$ induces a surjective ring map
\[
 \overline t_d:\overline{\mathcal O}\twoheadrightarrow R_d,
 \qquad \ker(\overline t_d)=\overline I_{d+1}.
\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Surjectivity is \cite[Proposition~8.1]{RezkWitt}. The $d$th ghost at $k$ is
\[
 g_d:\mathbb W_E(k)\longrightarrow R_d,\qquad
 (a_m)_m\longmapsto(\kappa_d^{\mathrm{tr}}\otimes1)(a_{p^d}),
\]
where the tensor product uses $\epsilon$. Equations \eqref{eq:2.10} and \eqref{eq:2.18} give $g_d(\iota(c))=t_d(c)\otimes1=\overline t_d(c\bmod p)$. Applied to the perfect $k$-algebra $k$, \cite[Proposition~8.8]{RezkWitt} gives $\ker(g_d)=p\mathbb W_E(k)+\mathbb I_{d+1}(k)$. Transport through $\iota$ and then reduction of the domain modulo $p$ yield
\[
 \ker(\overline t_d)=(p\mathcal O+I_{d+1})/p\mathcal O=\overline I_{d+1}.
\]

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:3.2}
Let $f:B\to C$ be a continuous homomorphism of $p$-adically complete and separated rings. If $B/pB\to C/pC$ is surjective, then $f$ is surjective.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For $c\in C$, choose recursively $b_r\in B$ so that
\[
 c-f\!\left(\sum_{i<r}p^ib_i\right)\in p^rC\quad(r\geq0).
\]
To pass from $r$ to $r+1$, divide this error by $p^r$ in $C$ and lift its reduction modulo $p$ to $B$. The sum $b=\sum_{r\geq0}p^rb_r$ converges in $B$; continuity and separation of $C$ give $f(b)=c$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:3.3}
For every $d\geq0$ and every continuous local $W$-algebra map $z:\mathcal O\to W$ reducing to the closed point, $\tau_{d,z}$ is surjective and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.6}
 M_{d,z}\simeq\mathcal O/J_{d,z},\qquad
 J_{d,z}=\operatorname{Fitt}^{\mathcal O}_0(M_{d,z}),
\end{equation}
where the $\mathcal O$-module structure is the target action through $\tau_{d,z}$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.7}
 J_{d,z}/pJ_{d,z}\xrightarrow{\sim}\overline I_{d+1},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.8}
 J_{d,z}\cap p\mathcal O=pJ_{d,z}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Both $\mathcal O=W[[u_1,\ldots,u_{n-1}]]$ and the finite free $W$-module $M_{d,z}$ are $p$-adically complete and separated. Every ring map preserves powers of $p$, so $\tau_{d,z}$ is also continuous for these topologies. Proposition~\ref{prop:3.1} and Lemma~\ref{lem:3.2} give its surjectivity. If $j_1,\ldots,j_r$ generate $J_{d,z}$, the presentation $\mathcal O^r\xrightarrow{(j_1\ \cdots\ j_r)}\mathcal O\to M_{d,z}\to0$ gives the Fitting equality in \eqref{eq:3.6}.

\[
 J_{d,z}/pJ_{d,z}\simeq(J_{d,z}+p\mathcal O)/p\mathcal O

which proves \eqref{eq:3.7}.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
For every commutative $\mathcal O$-algebra $A$, there is a natural additive homomorphism

such that, for $a=(a_m)_m\in\mathbb W_E(A)$,

\end{equation}
and, for $r\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:4.7}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:4.2}
Let $a\geq1$, $r\geq a-1$, and $N=p^r$. If
\[
 F(X)=1+\sum_{m\geq1}b_mX^m\in1+XW(k)[[X]]
\]
satisfies $b_m\in pW(k)$ for $1\leq m<p^a$, then
\[
 N[X^N]\log F(X)\in p^aW(k).
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Put $B=F-1$. Since $B(0)=0$, only $1\leq j\leq N$ contributes to $[X^N]$ in
\[
 N\log(1+B)=\sum_{j\geq1}(-1)^{j+1}\frac NjB^j.
\]
Consider a term
\[
 \frac Nj b_{m_1}\cdots b_{m_j},
 \qquad m_i\geq1,\quad m_1+\cdots+m_j=N.
\]
Let $\ell=\#\{i:m_i<p^a\}$ and $v=v_p(j)$. The product contributes at least $\ell$ factors of $p$, while $N/j$ has valuation $r-v$. Thus the term has valuation at least $r-v+\ell$.

If $\ell=j$, then $j-v_p(j)\geq1$, giving $r-v+\ell\geq r+1\geq a$. If $\ell<j$, some $m_i\geq p^a$, hence $r\geq a$, and
\[
 p^r\geq(j-\ell)p^a+\ell,
 \qquad j\leq p^{r-a}+\ell.
\]
Thus $v\leq r-a+\ell$: otherwise
\[
 j\geq p^{r-a+\ell+1}>p^{r-a}+\ell,
\]
a contradiction. The strict inequality uses $p\geq2$ and $2^{\ell+1}\geq\ell+2$. Again $r-v+\ell\geq a$, so every contribution lies in $p^aW(k)$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:4.3}
For $y\in\mathcal O$, put
\[
 \eta_y=\mathbb W_E(z)(P(y))=(\eta_{y,m})_{m\geq0}\in\mathbb W_E(W(k)).
\]
If $a\geq0$ and $y\in I_a$, then
\[
 \eta_{y,m}\in p\bigl(E^0B\Sigma_m\otimes_{\mathcal O,z}W(k)\bigr)
 \qquad(0<m<p^a).
\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $\pi:W(k)\to k$ be reduction. Since $\pi\circ z=\epsilon$, functoriality and \eqref{eq:2.10} give
\[
 \mathbb W_E(\pi)(\eta_y)=\mathbb W_E(\epsilon)(P(y))=\iota(y).
\]
On the $m$th coefficient module $N_m:=E^0B\Sigma_m\otimes_{\mathcal O,z}W(k)$, this is
\[
 N_m\longrightarrow E^0B\Sigma_m\otimes_{\mathcal O,\epsilon}k
 \simeq N_m/pN_m.
\]
For $y\in I_a$, the components of $\iota(y)$ with $0<m<p^a$ vanish by definition of the Witt filtration. Hence $\eta_{y,m}\in pN_m$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:4.4}
Let $a\geq1$ and $r\geq a-1$. Then
\[
 \boxed{\Theta_{r,z}(I_a)\subseteq p^aW(k).}

For $y\in I_a$, use $\eta_y$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:4.3} and write

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:5.1}
Let $R$ be a finite projective commutative algebra over a ring $A$ and let $\ell:R\to A$ have perfect pairing $(x,y)\mapsto\ell(xy)$. Let $I\subset R$ be an ideal such that $R/I$ is finite projective over $A$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:5.3}
 \operatorname{Ann}_R(I)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}_A(R/I,A),
 \qquad a\longmapsto(\overline b\mapsto\ell(ab)).
\end{equation}
If multiplication by $c\in R$ induces an isomorphism $R/I\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Ann}_R(I)$, the functional $\overline b\mapsto\ell(cb)$ is perfect on $R/I$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The displayed functional is independent of the lift $b$ because $aI=0$. Conversely, precompose a functional on $R/I$ with $R\to R/I$ and use perfectness of $\ell$ to represent it by a unique $a\in R$. For $i\in I$ and $r\in R$, its value on $ir$ is zero, so $\ell((ai)r)=0$ for every $r$. Perfectness implies $ai=0$. This proves \eqref{eq:5.3}; composing it with multiplication by $c$ proves the last assertion.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->

 \mathcal O_{\bar k}\otimes_{\mathcal O}E^0BQ
 \xrightarrow{\sim}E_{\bar k}^0BQ

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:7.1}
Suppose $n>1$. Put $U=\widehat{\mathcal O_{(p)}}$, and let $R$ be the completion of a strict henselization of $U$. After compatible trivializations of the etale factors, there is an exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:7.1}
 0\longrightarrow\mu_{p^\infty}\longrightarrow\mathcal G_R
 \xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }Q:=(\mathbf Q_p/\mathbf Z_p)^{n-1}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Fix $d\geq0$. Regard finite subgroups $K\subset Q$ as constant group schemes. For $|K|=p^j$, $0\leq j\leq d$, put $a=d-j$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:7.2}
 q_a:\mathcal G_R\longrightarrow\mathcal G':=\mathcal G_R/\mu_{p^a},
 \qquad \rho:\mathcal G'\longrightarrow Q,\qquad \rho q_a=\pi.
\end{equation}
The sheaf of group-homomorphism sections over $K$, defined for $R$-algebras $S$ by
\[
 X_K(S)=\{\sigma:K_S\longrightarrow\mathcal G'_S:
                 \rho_S\sigma=i_{K,S}\},
 \qquad i_{K,S}:K_S\hookrightarrow Q_S,
\]
is a torsor under the Cartier dual $K^D=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(K,\mathbf G_m)$.
It is represented by a finite locally free affine $R$-scheme of degree $p^j$.
Writing $C_K=\mathcal O(X_K)$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:7.4}
 \mathcal O_d\otimes_{\mathcal O}R
 \simeq\prod_{j=0}^d\prod_{\substack{K\subset Q\\|K|=p^j}}C_K.
\end{equation}
No splitting of \eqref{eq:7.1} is assumed.

<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Choose auxiliary deformation parameters $v_1,\ldots,v_{n-1}$ and a coordinate $X$ such that $\mathcal O\simeq W(k)[[v_1,\ldots,v_{n-1}]]$ and $[p]_G(X)\equiv v_1X^p\pmod{(p,X^{p+1})}$, as in \cite[Section~2.1]{StapletonTranschromatic}. Since $n>1$, $v_1\notin(p)$, so the map to $U$ factors through
\[
 \mathcal O\longrightarrow L_1:=\widehat{\mathcal O[v_1^{-1}]}_{(p)}
 \longrightarrow U.
\]

Over $L_1$, the formal part has height one and the etale quotient has height $n-1$ \cite[Propositions~2.3, 2.4 and~2.7]{StapletonTranschromatic}; the same holds over $R$. The identity component is taken over the $p$-adic formal base. Its geometric special fibre is multiplicative by \cite[Theorem~IV]{LazardFormalGroups}, so the Cartier dual of each finite torsion level has etale special fibre. If $B$ is the finite locally free $R$-algebra of such a dual, base change of relative differentials gives $\Omega_{B/R}\otimes_R R/pR=0$. The module $\Omega_{B/R}$ is finite over $R$, so Nakayama's lemma gives $\Omega_{B/R}=0$. Finite flatness and finite presentation therefore make $B$ etale over $R$. The ring $R$ is a complete strictly henselian discrete valuation ring, and $\mathcal O_{(p)}\to R$ is faithfully flat with uniformizer $p$. Its finite etale group schemes are constant. Compatible trivializations of the quotient and the height-one dual give \eqref{eq:7.1}.

We first work over any $R/p^vR$-algebra $S$, with $v\geq1$. A rank-$p^d$ subgroup $H\subseteq\mathcal G_S[p^d]$ has open and closed fibres $H_b$ over $b\in Q[p^d]$, each finite locally free over $S$. Partition $\operatorname{Spec}(S)$ into open and closed pieces where their ranks are fixed. On each piece the positive-rank labels form a subgroup $K$: the corresponding fibres have sections fppf locally, and multiplication and inversion give closure. Translation by such a section identifies each fibre with $H_0$. Hence $H\to K_S$ is faithfully flat, $|K|=p^j$ and $\operatorname{rank}(H_0)=p^{d-j}$.

Put $a=d-j$. We claim that $H_0=\mu_{p^a,S}$, including for nonreduced $S$. Its Cartier dual $H_0^D$ is finite locally free. On a geometric fibre over an algebraically closed field $\kappa$ of characteristic $p$, the coordinate algebra of $\mu_{p^d}$ is $\kappa[T]/(T^{p^d})$, whose unique quotient of dimension $p^a$ is $\kappa[T]/(T^{p^a})$. Thus the subgroup on that fibre is $\mu_{p^a}$, and its dual is the etale cyclic group $\mathbf Z/p^a$. The relative differentials of $H_0^D$ therefore vanish on every geometric fibre, thus vanish by local Nakayama; finite flatness makes $H_0^D$ etale over $S$. The dual inclusion gives a map $(\mathbf Z/p^d)_S\to H_0^D$ that is surjective on geometric fibres. Etale locally both groups are constant, so this map is finite etale and surjective. Its kernel is an open and closed subgroup of $(\mathbf Z/p^d)_S$ of rank $p^{d-a}$. The unique subgroup of that order in $\mathbf Z/p^d$ is $p^a(\mathbf Z/p^d)$, so the kernel is $(p^a(\mathbf Z/p^d))_S$. Dualizing this standard cyclic quotient proves the claimed equality of embedded subgroup schemes.

Under \eqref{eq:7.2}, $H/\mu_{p^a,S}$ gives a section over $K_S$. Conversely, a section $\sigma$ gives $H=q_a^{-1}(\sigma(K_S))$, finite locally free of rank $p^a|K|=p^d$. Its $p^j$-multiple lies in $\mu_{p^a,S}$, so it is killed by $p^d$. These constructions are inverse and commute with base change.

For the torsor assertion, let $S$ be an arbitrary $R$-algebra. Multiplication by $p^a$ identifies $\ker(\rho)=\mu_{p^\infty}/\mu_{p^a}$ with $\mu_{p^\infty}$. Two sections over $K_S$ differ by an element of
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(K,\mu_{p^\infty})
 =\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(K,\mathbf G_m)=K^D.
\]
Sections exist fppf locally. Write $K=\bigoplus_i\mathbf Z/p^{\lambda_i}$. Since $\rho$ is surjective as an fppf sheaf, after an fppf cover of $\operatorname{Spec}(S)$ each generator lifts to a section $g_i$ of $\mathcal G'$. Its multiple $p^{\lambda_i}g_i$ lies in the kernel. Multiplication by $p^{\lambda_i}$ on $\mu_{p^\infty}$ is fppf surjective, therefore after a further cover choose a kernel section $h_i$ with the same multiple. There are finitely many generators, so common fppf cover suffices for these choices. The lifts $g_i-h_i$ satisfy the cyclic relations and, by commutativity, define a group-homomorphism section over $K$. It follows that $X_K$ is a $K^D$-torsor over $R$. Effective affine descent and descent of finite local freeness represent it by an affine scheme of degree $|K|$ \cite[Lemmas~35.37.1 and~35.23.32]{StacksDescent}.

The correspondence over all $R/p^vR$-algebras, including nonreduced ones, gives compatible isomorphisms of representing algebras modulo $p^v$. Both sides of \eqref{eq:7.4} are finite $R$-modules, thus $p$-adically complete; taking inverse limits proves the formula. For $K=0$, the section is unique and $C_K=R$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:7.2}
Let $R$ be a commutative local ring, $K$ a finite abelian group of order $m$, and $C$ the algebra of an fppf torsor under $K^D=\operatorname{Spec} R[K]$, where $R[K]$ is the group algebra. There is a grading
\[
 C=\bigoplus_{\chi\in K}C_\chi,\qquad C_0=R,\qquad
 C_\chi\otimes_R C_\psi\xrightarrow{\sim}C_{\chi+\psi},
\]
by invertible (rank-one locally free) $R$-modules, with the last map given by multiplication. For the degree-zero projection $\pi_0:C\to R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:7.8}
 \beta(x,y):=\pi_0(xy)\ \text{is perfect},\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}_{C/R}(xy)=m\beta(x,y).
\end{equation}
When $R$ is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $p$ and $m=p^j$, all $m$ Smith exponents of its trace map are $j$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Write the coaction as $\alpha(c)=\sum_\chi c_\chi\otimes z^\chi$, where $z^\chi$ is the group-algebra basis, with comultiplication $z^\chi\mapsto z^\chi\otimes z^\chi$. Counit and coassociativity give $c=\sum_\chi c_\chi$ and $\alpha(c_\chi)=c_\chi\otimes z^\chi$. Coefficient uniqueness gives the direct-sum grading, and multiplicativity of $\alpha$ makes it an algebra grading.

After a faithfully flat extension trivializing the torsor, this is the usual grading of $R[K]$. Descent makes $C$ finite locally free of rank $m$. Its graded summands are projective, and their formation commutes with base change. Their rank-one property and the isomorphisms $R\to C_0$ and $C_\chi\otimes_R C_\psi\to C_{\chi+\psi}$ therefore descend.

Multiplication by $x\in C_\chi$ sends $C_\psi$ to $C_{\chi+\psi}$. For $\chi\ne0$ its matrix in homogeneous bases has zero diagonal; for $x\in C_0=R$ its trace is $mx$. Thus $\operatorname{Tr}_{C/R}=m\pi_0$. The isomorphisms $C_\chi\otimes_R C_{-\chi}\to R$ make $\beta$ perfect, proving \eqref{eq:7.8} without division by $m$. Over a DVR the trace matrix is $p^j$ times an invertible matrix.

 }
\end{equation}

For every $d\geq0$, the functional
\[
 \lambda_d^\omega=p^{-d}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal O_d/\mathcal O}(h_d-)
\]
is integral and perfect by Proposition~\ref{prop:5.2}, since $h_d=\xi_de_d$. Now set $d=a+b$. Base change of $\lambda_b^\omega$ along $t_a$ gives a perfect $\mathcal O_a$-linear functional on $C=C_{a,b}$:
\[
 \lambda_{C/\mathcal O_a}^\omega(x\otimes y)
 =x\,t_a(\lambda_b^\omega(y)).
\]
Source linearity of $\lambda_b^\omega$ makes this respect the tensor relation. Composing with $\lambda_a^\omega$ gives
\[
 \lambda_C^\omega(x\otimes y)
 =\lambda_a^\omega\bigl(x\,t_a(\lambda_b^\omega(y))\bigr).
\]
The perfect pairings defined by these functionals give the isomorphisms
\[
 C\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_a}(C,\mathcal O_a)
 \xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(C,\mathcal O).

The first sends $z$ to $\lambda_{C/\mathcal O_a}^\omega(z-)$; the second composes with $\lambda_a^\omega$. Their composite proves perfectness of $\lambda_C^\omega$. Trace transitivity and \eqref{eq:8.11} give
\[
 \lambda_C^\omega(z)
 =p^{-d}\operatorname{Tr}_{C/\mathcal O}(\phi_{a,b}(h_d)z).
\]

\begin{equation}\label{eq:8.21}
 \lambda_d^\omega(\mathfrak t(z)b)
 =\lambda_C^\omega(z\phi_{a,b}(b))
 \qquad(z\in C,\ b\in \mathcal O_d).
\end{equation}
Applying \eqref{eq:8.21} to $\phi_{a,b}(b_0)z$ and to $z,b_0b$ gives $\mathfrak t(\phi_{a,b}(b_0)z)=b_0\mathfrak t(z)$ by perfectness, proving $\mathcal O_d$-linearity.

After inverting $p$, ordinary trace transitivity identifies the right side of \eqref{eq:8.21} with $\lambda_d^\omega(\operatorname{Tr}_{C[1/p]/\mathcal O_d[1/p]}(z)b)$. Thus $\mathfrak t$ extends this relative trace; $p$-torsion-freeness of $\mathcal O_d$ makes the extension unique. In particular we see that this map is independent of $\omega$.

Put $w=\mathfrak t(1)$. Trace transitivity gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal O_d/\mathcal O}(w)=r_a r_b$. Proposition~\ref{prop:3.1} makes $R_d=\mathcal O_d/\mathfrak m\mathcal O_d$ a local Artinian algebra of source $k$-dimension $r_d$ with residue field $k$. \[
 r_d\overline w=r_a r_b\quad\text{in }k.
\]
The rank series \eqref{eq:5.1} gives $r_a\equiv r_b\equiv r_d\equiv1\pmod p$, so $\overline w=1$. Thus $w$ is a unit. By $\mathcal O_d$-linearity, $\mathfrak t(\phi_{a,b}(b))=bw$; this proves the retraction and surjectivity.

The functional paired with $\mathcal T_a(x)\otimes\mathcal T_b(y)$ is $z\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{C/\mathcal O}((x\otimes y)z)$, by trace transitivity in $\mathcal O\to\mathcal O_a\to C$. Restricting along $\phi_{a,b}$ and using the relative trace proves \eqref{eq:8.18}. For any $b\in\mathcal O_d$, surjectivity and \eqref{eq:8.3} give $z=\sum_i x_i\otimes y_i$ with $\mathfrak t(z)=b$. Thus $\mathcal T_d(b)=\sum_i\mathcal T_a(x_i)\mathcal T_b(y_i)$, proving the reverse inclusion in \eqref{eq:8.19}; the forward inclusion is already \eqref{eq:8.18}.

Suppose $n\geq2$. <!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:9.1}
There is a unit $u_m\in \mathcal O_{\ell}^{\times}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:9.3}
 \rho_{d,m}(e_d)=u_m p^m e_{\ell}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-29: body_md -->
Choose an oriented coordinate $X$ on $G$, and let $f_H(X)$ be the monic polynomial cutting out a finite subgroup divisor $H$. Its constant term is zero because $H$ contains the identity. For $H$ of rank $p^r$, its Euler class is the pullback of $e_r$ under its classifying map; it agrees with $f_H'(0)$ up to a unit \cite[proof of Theorem~9.2]{StricklandSymmetric}, with coefficient extension as in Remark~\ref{rem:5.3}. For the trivial subgroup, $f_H(X)=X$ and $e_0=1$.

Let $K$ be the universal subgroup of order $p^\ell$ over $\mathcal O_\ell$. Strickland's inverse-image construction gives
\[
 f_{p^{-m}K}(X)=v(X)f_K([p^m](X)),
 \qquad v(X)\in \mathcal O_\ell[[X]]^\times
\]
\cite[Definition~12.8]{StricklandSubgroups}. Differentiating at the identity gives
\[
 f_{p^{-m}K}'(0)=v(0)\,p^m f_K'(0).
\]
Here $f_K(0)=0$ eliminates the term involving $v'(0)$, and $[p^m]'(0)=p^m$ since integer multiplication acts by that integer on the tangent line. The factor $v(0)$ and the two Euler comparison factors are units, giving \eqref{eq:9.3}.

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{thm:9.2}
Let $m\geq0$, $d\geq nm$ and $\ell=d-nm$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:9.5}
 \boxed{
 \{c\in\mathcal O:c\varepsilon_m\in \mathcal O_d\}
 =p^{(n-1)m}\mathcal O.
 }
\end{equation}
Moreover, if
\[
 D_{d,m}=\mathcal O_d/\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal O_d}(K_m),
\]
then $D_{d,m}$ is finite free over $\mathcal O$ and there is a canonical fibre-product presentation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:9.6}
 \boxed{
 \mathcal O_d\simeq
 \mathcal O_{\ell}
 \times_{\mathcal O_{\ell}/p^{(n-1)m}\mathcal O_{\ell}}
 D_{d,m}.
 }
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-30: body_md -->
\emph{Step 1: identify the annihilator by duality.} Use the perfect functionals of Proposition~\ref{prop:5.2} at indices $d$ and $\ell$. There is a unique $\eta_m\in \mathcal O_d$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:9.8}
 \lambda_d(\eta_m b)=\lambda_{\ell}(\rho_{d,m}(b))
 \qquad(b\in \mathcal O_d).
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:5.1}, applied to $K_m$ with finite free quotient $\mathcal O_\ell$, gives the isomorphism
\[
 \operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal O_d}(K_m)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O,s_\ell}(\mathcal O_{\ell},\mathcal O),
 \qquad
 a\longmapsto(\rho_{d,m}(b)\mapsto\lambda_d(ab)).
\]
Since every functional on $\mathcal O_\ell$ is $\lambda_\ell(x-)$ and $x$ lifts through $\rho_{d,m}$, equation~\eqref{eq:9.8} identifies its representing element with a multiple of $\eta_m$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:9.9}
 \operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal O_d}(K_m)=\eta_m\mathcal O_d.
\end{equation}
The surjection $\rho_{d,m}$ splits as a map of finite free source $\mathcal O$-modules. Its dual injection therefore has direct-summand image, which the perfect pairing identifies with $\operatorname{Ann}(K_m)$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 2: compute the generic projector.} Put $q=(n-1)m$. Since the subgroup rings are local (Section~\ref{sec:2}), lift $u_m$ to a unit $\widetilde u_m\in\mathcal O_d^\times$. After inverting $p$, $\rho_{d,m}$ identifies $\varepsilon_m\mathcal O_d[1/p]$ with $\mathcal O_\ell[1/p]$. Trace on this product factor, formula~\eqref{eq:5.4} and Lemma~\ref{lem:9.1} give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lambda_d(\widetilde u_m^{-1}p^q\varepsilon_m b)
 &=p^{q-d}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal O_\ell/\mathcal O}
       (u_m^{-1}\rho_{d,m}(e_d)\rho_{d,m}(b))\\
 &=p^{-\ell}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal O_\ell/\mathcal O}
       (e_\ell\rho_{d,m}(b))
 =\lambda_\ell(\rho_{d,m}(b)),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where traces and functionals are extended to $\mathcal O[1/p]$ and $q-d+m=-\ell$. Perfectness persists under this base change, so comparison with \eqref{eq:9.8} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:9.10}
 \eta_m=\widetilde u_m^{-1}p^{(n-1)m}\varepsilon_m
 \qquad\text{in }\mathcal O_d[1/p].
\end{equation}
Its integral left side proves $p^q\varepsilon_m\in\mathcal O_d$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 3: prove that the scalar denominator is exact.} If $c\varepsilon_m\in\mathcal O_d$ for $c\in\mathcal O$, it annihilates $K_m$: this holds after inverting $p$ and descends by $p$-torsion-freeness. Thus it lies in \eqref{eq:9.9}. Applying $\rho_{d,m}$ and using \eqref{eq:9.10} gives $c\in p^q\mathcal O_\ell$. The algebra $\mathcal O_\ell$ is finite free of positive rank, hence faithfully flat; ideal contraction gives $c\in p^q\mathcal O$, proving \eqref{eq:9.5}.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 4: recover the integral algebra by gluing.} The intersection $K_m\cap\operatorname{Ann}(K_m)$ vanishes after inverting $p$, hence vanishes integrally by $p$-torsion-freeness. Thus $b\mapsto(\rho_{d,m}(b),[b])$ is injective. Equations~\eqref{eq:9.9} and~\eqref{eq:9.10} give
\[
 \rho_{d,m}(\operatorname{Ann}(K_m))=p^q\mathcal O_\ell,
\]
making $\beta$ well-defined. For a pair $(x,[b])$ with matching reductions, choose $a\in\operatorname{Ann}(K_m)$ with $\rho_{d,m}(a)=x-\rho_{d,m}(b)$; then $b+a$ lifts the pair. This proves \eqref{eq:9.6}. The direct-summand statement in Step~1 makes $D_{d,m}$ finite projective, hence free, over local $\mathcal O$.

<!-- corollary-8: body_md -->
\label{cor:9.3}
Assume $n=2$. By \eqref{eq:5.7} and the invariant-factor classification of finite abelian groups, each geometric generic subgroup of order $p^d$ has a unique type (abstract group isomorphism class)
\[
 H_a\simeq\mathbf Z/p^{d-a}\oplus\mathbf Z/p^a,
 \qquad 0\leq a\leq L:=\lfloor d/2\rfloor.
\]
A zero exponent denotes the trivial factor. Let $\pi_a\in \mathcal O_d[1/p]$ be the idempotent selecting subgroups of type $H_a$. Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \min\{s\in\mathbf Z_{\geq0}:p^s\pi_a\in \mathcal O_d\}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 a+1,&a<L,\\
 a,&a=L.
 \end{cases}
 }
\]
In particular, the cyclic projector has exact denominator $p$ whenever $d\geq2$.
<!-- end corollary-8 -->
<!-- proof-31: body_md -->
For $0\leq a\leq L$, the earlier projector $\varepsilon_a$ selects subgroups containing $G[p^a]$, with $\varepsilon_0=1$. Since $d\geq2a$, Theorem~\ref{thm:9.2} gives
\[
 \min\{s\in\mathbf Z_{\geq0}:p^s\varepsilon_a\in \mathcal O_d\}=a.
\]
Containment of $G[p^a]$ is equivalent to the smaller invariant exponent being at least $a$: precisely then $H[p^a]\subseteq G[p^a]$ has the same order $p^{2a}$ and equals it. Thus
\[
 \pi_a=\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_{a+1}\quad(a<L),
 \qquad
 \pi_L=\varepsilon_L.
\]
For $a<L$, also $a+1\leq L$, so multiplication by $p^{a+1}$ makes both terms integral. If $p^a\pi_a$ were integral, then $p^a\varepsilon_{a+1}=p^a\varepsilon_a-p^a\pi_a$ would be integral, contradicting its exact denominator $p^{a+1}$. The final projector is $\varepsilon_L$.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:10.1}
The map $W[T]\to M_{d,c}$ sending $a\in W$ to $\sigma_d(a)$ and $T$ to $u_{d,c}$ induces an isomorphism of rings
\[
 W[T]/(F_{d+1,c}(T))\xrightarrow{\sim}M_{d,c}.
\]
It is $\sigma_d$-semilinear for the usual coefficient actions. Consequently
\[
 J_{d,c}=(F_{d+1,c}(u))\subset W[[u]].
\]
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-32: body_md -->
Write $M=M_{d,c}$, $x=u_{d,c}$, $N=N_{d+1}$, $\sigma=\sigma_d$ and $F=F_{d+1,c}$. By \eqref{eq:10.3}, $1,x,\ldots,x^{N-1}$ is a $W$-basis: the matrix of these vectors in any $W$-basis is invertible modulo $p$, hence has unit determinant. Put $\chi(T)=\det_W(T\operatorname{id}_M-m_x)$. Reduction of multiplication by $x$ is the nilpotent shift on this power basis. Since $\sigma(p)=p$ and $F=\sigma^{-1}(\chi)$,

\[
 \chi(T)\equiv F(T)\equiv T^N\pmod p.
\]
Thus $F$ is distinguished: it is monic and all lower coefficients lie in $pW$.

Cayley--Hamilton \cite[Chapter~XIV, \S~3, Theorem~3.1]{LangAlgebra} gives $\chi(m_x)=0$; applying it to $1$ gives $\chi(x)=0$. Evaluation with coefficient action $\sigma$ therefore induces $W[T]/(F)\to M$. Monicity gives the domain the basis $1,T,\ldots,T^{N-1}$, which maps to the power basis of $M$. Since $\sigma$ is an automorphism, this is a ring isomorphism, $\sigma$-semilinear for the usual coefficient actions.

The relation $\chi(x)=0$ gives $x^N\in pM$, hence $(p,x)^NM\subseteq pM$. Thus $\tau_{d,c}$ is continuous from the $(p,u)$-adic topology to the $p$-adic topology of the complete module $M$, and power-series evaluation is the limit of polynomial evaluations. Distinguished Weierstrass division over $W$ \cite[Chapter~IV, \S~9, Theorem~9.1]{LangAlgebra} writes every $g\in W[[u]]$ uniquely as $g=F(u)h+r$, with $h\in W[[u]]$, $r\in W[u]$ and $\deg r<N$. Since $\tau_{d,c}(F(u))=0$ and the powers of $x$ form a basis, $\tau_{d,c}(g)=\tau_{d,c}(r)=0$ if and only if $r=0$. Therefore $J_{d,c}=(F(u))$.
