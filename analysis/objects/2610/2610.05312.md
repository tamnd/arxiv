<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:weak}
A finite-energy renormalized weak solution of
\eqref{eq:system} with data \eqref{eq:data} is a triple
\((\rho,u,H)\), with \(m=\rho u\), having the regularity in
\eqref{eq:regularity} below, satisfying \(\operatorname{div} H=0\) and the
following identities. All test functions are smooth, periodic
in space, and compactly supported in time in \([0,\infty)\).
For scalar \(\phi\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-mass}
 \int_0^\infty\!\!\int_{\mathbb T^2}
       (\rho\phi_t+m\cdot\nabla\phi)\,\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dt
       +\int_{\mathbb T^2}\rho_0\phi(0)\,\mathrm dx=0.
\end{equation}
For vector fields \(\varphi,\psi\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-momentum}
\begin{aligned}
0={}&\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_{\mathbb T^2}
 \bigl[m\cdot\varphi_t+(\rho u\otimes u):\nabla\varphi
                  +\rho^\gamma\operatorname{div}\varphi
                  -\mu\nabla u:\nabla\varphi\\
 &\qquad
 -(\mu+\rho^\beta)(\operatorname{div} u)(\operatorname{div}\varphi)
                  -\mathbb M(H):\nabla\varphi\bigr]\,\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dt
       +\int_{\mathbb T^2}\rho_0u_0\cdot\varphi(0)\,\mathrm dx,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-magnetic}
 \int_0^\infty\!\!\int_{\mathbb T^2}
 \bigl[H\cdot\psi_t+(H\otimes u-u\otimes H):\nabla\psi
                     -\nu\nabla H:\nabla\psi\bigr]\,\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dt
 +\int_{\mathbb T^2}H_0\cdot\psi(0)\,\mathrm dx=0.
\end{equation}
For every \(b\in C^1([0,\infty))\) with compactly supported
derivative,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renormalized}
 \int_0^\infty\!\!\int_{\mathbb T^2}
 \bigl[b(\rho)\phi_t+b(\rho)u\cdot\nabla\phi
          -(\rho b'(\rho)-b(\rho))(\operatorname{div} u)\phi\bigr]\,\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dt
 +\int_{\mathbb T^2}b(\rho_0)\phi(0)\,\mathrm dx=0.
\end{equation}
Finally, the energy inequality \eqref{eq:energy-main} holds
for the canonical time representatives specified below.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Assume \(\mu,\nu>0\), \eqref{eq:parameters}, and
\eqref{eq:data}. There exists a global finite-energy
renormalized weak solution of \eqref{eq:system}.
For every \(T<\infty\) and every fixed \(1\le p<\infty\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regularity}
\begin{gathered}
 \rho\ge0,\qquad
 \rho\in L^\infty(0,T;L^p(\mathbb T^2))\cap C([0,T];L^p(\mathbb T^2)),\\
 u,H\in L^2(0,T;H^1(\mathbb T^2)),\qquad
 \sqrt\rho\,u\in L^\infty(0,T;L^2(\mathbb T^2)),\\
 H\in C([0,T];L^2(\mathbb T^2))\cap L^\infty(0,T;L^p(\mathbb T^2)),\\
 \rho^{\beta/2}\operatorname{div} u\in L^2((0,T)\times\mathbb T^2).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For every \(1<r<2\), the momentum has a representative
\(m\in C_{\mathrm{weak}}([0,T];L^r(\mathbb T^2))\).
The density, momentum, and magnetic field attain the prescribed
initial data in these time topologies.

With these representatives, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy-definition}
\begin{aligned}
 E(t)&=\int_{\mathbb T^2}\left[
       \mathcal K(\rho(t),m(t))
          +\frac{\rho(t)^\gamma}{\gamma-1}
          +\frac{|H(t)|^2}{2}\right]\,\mathrm dx,\\
 \mathfrak d(t)&=\int_{\mathbb T^2}\left[
      \mu|\nabla u|^2+(\mu+\rho^\beta)|\operatorname{div} u|^2
                          +\nu|\nabla H|^2\right](t)\,\mathrm dx.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy-main}
                E(t)+\int_0^t\mathfrak d(s)\,\mathrm d s\le E(0)
                    \qquad\text{for every }t\ge0.
\end{equation}
There is also a common full-measure set \(S\subset(0,\infty)\)
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy-start}
 E(t)+\int_s^t\mathfrak d(\tau)\,\mathrm d\tau\le E(s),
       \qquad s\in S\cup\{0\},\quad t\ge s.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-kinetic}
 E(t)\longrightarrow E(0),\qquad
 \frac{m(t)}{\sqrt{\rho(t)}}\longrightarrow\sqrt{\rho_0}\,u_0
                      \quad\text{strongly in }L^2(\mathbb T^2)
                      \quad(t\downarrow0),
\end{equation}
where the quotient is defined to be zero on the vacuum set.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:scope}
No smallness assumption is imposed on the initial data.
The weak-existence theorem alone does not assert a pointwise upper or positive lower
bound for the limiting density, uniqueness of general weak
solutions, or global existence of strong solutions.
Strong continuity in a given finite \(L^p\) space uses a higher
fixed moment; no estimate uniform as \(p\to\infty\) is involved.
The starting times in \eqref{eq:energy-start} are not enlarged
to all times, and no global energy equality is claimed.
The proof treats the full interval $\beta>1$ directly. With
$B=\max\{\beta,2\}$, the density-moment exponent in
\eqref{uni:sublinear-ode} remains strictly below one.
The fixed-parameter closure \eqref{fix:density-closure} compares
the powers $1+3B/8$ and $(B+2)/3$ with $B$; both are strictly
smaller because $B\ge2$. Thus the artificial quadratic viscosity
controls the lower-$\beta$ range, while the original viscosity
supplies the dominant power when $\beta>2$.
The bounded multipliers in \eqref{comp:quotient-sources} require
only $\beta>1$. In particular, $\beta=4/3$ and $\beta=2$ are
included in the weak theorem, without endpoint limiting arguments.

\label{thm:weak-strong}
Let $\beta>1$ and $\gamma>1$.
Suppose $(\rho,u,H)$ is a finite-energy renormalized weak solution in
Definition~\ref{def:weak}, and $(r,U,B)$ solves the same equations in
\eqref{eq:strong-class}. If their initial density, momentum, and magnetic

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:strong}
Fix $\mu,\nu>0$, $\beta>1$, $\gamma>\Gamma_*(\beta)$, and $R>0$.
There is $m_0=m_0(\mu,\nu,\beta,\gamma,R)$ with the following property.
If $m\ge m_0$ and $\rho_0,u_0,H_0\in H^2(\mathbb T^2)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-data}
 \int_{\mathbb T^2}\rho_0\,\mathrm dx=m,\qquad \operatorname{div} H_0=0,\qquad
 m^{(\gamma-3)/2}\|\rho_0-m\|_{H^2}
       +\|u_0\|_{H^2}+\|H_0\|_{H^2}\le R,
\end{equation}
then \eqref{eq:system} has a unique global strong solution in
\eqref{eq:strong-class}, with $(r,U,B)$ replaced by $(\rho,u,H)$.
There is $C_R$, independent of $m$ and time, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-uniform}
\begin{gathered}
 \tfrac34m\le\rho(t,x)\le\tfrac54m,\\
 \sup_{t\ge0}\left[
 m^{(\gamma-3)/2}\|\rho(t)-m\|_{H^2}
           +\|u(t)\|_{H^1}+\|H(t)\|_{H^1}\right]\le C_R.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The uniform $H^2$ bounds for $u,H$ may depend on the fixed value of $m$.
Every weak solution in Definition~\ref{def:weak} with these same data
coincides with this strong solution on every finite interval.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:strong-scope}
The number $R$ is arbitrary and is fixed before $m$ is chosen. Thus the
velocity and magnetic norms need not be small. For $\gamma>3$, however,
\eqref{eq:scaled-data} requires an absolutely small density perturbation,
$\|\rho_0-m\|_{H^2}\le Rm^{-(\gamma-3)/2}$; the absence of an upper
bound on $\gamma$ must not be separated from this hypothesis.
For $\gamma<3$ the same condition even allows an additive density
perturbation of order $m^{(3-\gamma)/2}$, which is still small relative
to $m$. Nonzero mean magnetic fields are allowed.

In particular, for fixed $r_0,u_0,H_0\in H^2$ with $\int r_0=0$,
$\operatorname{div} H_0=0$, and $\rho_0=m+r_0$, the theorem applies when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-additive-range}
 1<\beta<2,\qquad \Gamma_*(\beta)<\gamma\le3.
\end{equation}
If $r_0=0$, it applies to every $\gamma>\Gamma_*(\beta)$.
The lower threshold is $5-2\beta$ for $1<\beta\le4/3$ and
$\beta+1$ for $\beta\ge4/3$.
Its minimum is $7/3$, attained at $\beta=4/3$, but the theorem does not
include equality. For example, $\beta=33/25$ and $\gamma=12/5$ satisfy
both $\beta<4/3$ and $\gamma<5/2$. These are sufficient conditions from the present
estimates, not claimed necessary or optimal conditions for the equations.
Figure~\ref{fig:parameter-regions} summarizes these parameter ranges,

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{pre:weighted-poincare}
Let \(\rho\ge0\), \(0<M_-\le\int\rho\le M_+\), and
\(\lVert \rho\rVert_\kappa\le K\) for some fixed \(\kappa>1\).
For every \(1\le q<\infty\) and \(v\in H^1(\mathbb T^2)\),
\begin{equation}\label{pre:weighted-poincare-est}
 \lVert v\rVert_q\le
 C_{q,\kappa,M_-,M_+,K}
       \bigl(\lVert \sqrt\rho\,v\rVert_2+\lVert \nabla v\rVert_2\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular, no positive lower bound for \(\rho\) is required.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Writing \(M=\int\rho\), we have
\[
 M\lvert \langle v\rangle\rvert
 \le \lvert \int\rho v\,\mathrm dx\rvert
       +\lvert \int\rho(v-\langle v\rangle)\,\mathrm dx\rvert
 \le \sqrt M\lVert \sqrt\rho\,v\rVert_2
       +\lVert \rho\rVert_\kappa\lVert v-\langle v\rangle\rVert_{\kappa'}.
\]
Since \(\kappa'<\infty\), \eqref{pre:sobolev} controls the last norm.
Another application of \eqref{pre:sobolev} proves the assertion.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{pre:commutator}
Let \(\mathcal R_{ij}=\partial_i\partial_j\Delta^{-1}\), and set
\([v,\mathcal R_{ij}]f=v\mathcal R_{ij}f-\mathcal R_{ij}(vf)\).
If \(1<p,q<\infty\) and \(1/r=1/p+1/q<1\), then
\begin{equation}\label{pre:commutator-est}
 \lVert \nabla[v,\mathcal R_{ij}]f\rVert_r
 \le C_{p,q}\lVert \nabla v\rVert_p\lVert f\rVert_q.
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{pre:friedrichs}
Suppose \(z\in L^\infty_tL^p_x\), \(u\in L^2_tH^1_x\), and
\(2<p<\infty\). For a periodic spatial mollifier \(J_\ell\), write
\(z_\ell=J_\ell*z\). Then, with \(1/r=1/2+1/p\),
\begin{equation}\label{pre:friedrichs-est}
 c_\ell=\operatorname{div}(uz_\ell)-J_\ell*\operatorname{div}(uz)
       \longrightarrow0\quad\hbox{in }L^2_tL^r_x.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\(\lVert c_\ell\rVert_{L^2_tL^r_x}
 \le C\lVert \nabla u\rVert_{L^2_{t,x}}\lVert z\rVert_{L^\infty_tL^p_x}\).
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
In the distributional convolution formula,
\[
 c_\ell(x)=z_\ell(x)\operatorname{div} u(x)
 +\int\nabla J_\ell(h)\cdot
             \bigl(u(x)-u(x-h)\bigr)z(x-h)\,\mathrm d h.
\]
The fundamental theorem of calculus and H\"older's inequality give
the stated uniform bound. For smooth \(u\), spatial translation
continuity of \(z\) shows that the integral converges to
\(-z\operatorname{div} u\), whereas the first term converges to \(z\operatorname{div} u\).
Their sum therefore converges to zero in \(L^2_tL^r_x\).
Approximation of \(u\) in \(L^2_tH^1_x\), followed by the uniform
bound, proves \eqref{pre:friedrichs-est}. This is the commutator
underlying renormalization \cite{DiPernaLions1989}. It concerns fixed
limiting functions \(z,u\), not strong convergence between distinct
approximate solutions.

\label{pre:wente}
For smooth periodic \(v=(v_1,v_2)\) and \(\phi\),
\begin{equation}\label{pre:wente-est}
 \lvert \int\phi\det\nabla v\,\mathrm dx\rvert
 \le C\lVert \nabla\phi\rVert_2\lVert \nabla v\rVert_2^2.
\end{equation}
For \(v,\phi\in H^1\), the left-hand side is understood as the

\]
The Jacobian has zero mean. Integration by parts against

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{pre:bkm}
For each fixed \(q>2\) and \(v\in W^{2,q}(\mathbb T^2;\mathbb R^2)\),

 \lVert \nabla v\rVert_\infty
 \le C_q\bigl(\lVert \operatorname{div} v\rVert_\infty+\lVert \operatorname{curl} v\rVert_\infty\bigr)

A periodic dyadic decomposition and the div--curl representation

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{uni:uniform-moments}
Let \(T<\infty\). Suppose solutions of \eqref{uni:mixed-system}
exist on \([0,\tau]\), where \(\tau\le T\), and their initial data obey
\begin{equation}\label{uni:initial-bounds}
 0<M_-\le\int\rho_0\,\mathrm dx\le M_+,\qquad
 \lVert \rho_0\rVert_\infty+\lVert u_0\rVert_{H^1}+\lVert H_0\rVert_{H^1}\le K.
\end{equation}
Then, for every fixed \(1\le p<\infty\),
\begin{equation}\label{uni:moment-bound}
 \sup_{0\le t\le\tau}
      \bigl(\lVert \rho(t)\rVert_p+\lVert H(t)\rVert_p\bigr)\le C_{p,T}.
\end{equation}
The constants depend only on the indicated initial bounds,
\(\mu,\nu,\beta,\gamma,T\), and the fixed exponent. They are
independent of \(\delta\), of \(\tau\le T\), of the density maximum
during the evolution, and of a common positive density lower bound.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Write \(d=\operatorname{div} u\), \(\omega=\operatorname{curl} u\), \(M=\int\rho_0\,\mathrm dx\), and
\[
 E(t)=\int\left(\frac12\rho|u|^2
                 +\frac{\rho^\gamma}{\gamma-1}
                 +\frac12|H|^2\right)\,\mathrm dx,\qquad
 e_\delta(t)=\int\left(a_\delta d^2+\mu\omega^2
                               +\nu|\nabla H|^2\right)\,\mathrm dx.
\]
The kinetic and magnetic work terms cancel. The periodic identity
\(\lVert \nabla u\rVert_2^2=\lVert d\rVert_2^2+\lVert \omega\rVert_2^2\) identifies
the viscous dissipation exactly, and yields
\begin{equation}\label{uni:energy}
 E(t)+\int_0^t e_\delta(s)\,\mathrm d s=E(0),\qquad
 \lVert \nabla u(t)\rVert_2^2\le C_\mu e_\delta(t).
\end{equation}
In particular \(E(0)\) is bounded uniformly by
\eqref{uni:initial-bounds}. Applying Lemma~\ref{pre:weighted-poincare}
with \(\kappa=\gamma\) gives, for every fixed finite \(q\),
\begin{equation}\label{uni:velocity}
 \lVert u(t)\rVert_q\le C_q(1+\lVert \nabla u(t)\rVert_2).
\end{equation}
Only the basic pressure energy is used here.

\medskip\noindent\textit{Magnetic moments.}
The induction equation is
\(H_t+u\cdot\nabla H=H\cdot\nabla u-Hd+\nu\Delta H\).
For a fixed \(q\ge2\), testing by \(|H|^{q-2}H\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{uni:magnetic-test}
\begin{aligned}
 &\frac1q\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm dt}\int|H|^q\,\mathrm dx
 +\nu\int|H|^{q-2}|\nabla H|^2\,\mathrm dx
   +\nu(q-2)\int|H|^{q-2}|\nabla|H||^2\,\mathrm dx\\
 &=\int|H|^{q-2}H_iH_j\partial_j u_i\,\mathrm dx
       -\left(1-\frac1q\right)\int d|H|^q\,\mathrm dx.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Put \(Z=|H|^{q/2}\). The dissipation controls
\(4\nu(q-1)q^{-2}\lVert \nabla Z\rVert_2^2\); the right-hand side is at most
\(C_q\lVert \nabla u\rVert_2\lVert Z\rVert_4^2\).
Using \eqref{pre:gn}, including its low-frequency term, and Young's
inequality, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{uni:magnetic-moments}
 \frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm dt}\lVert H\rVert_q^q
   +c_{q,\nu}\lVert \nabla Z\rVert_2^2
 \le C_{q,\nu}(1+\lVert \nabla u\rVert_2^2)\lVert H\rVert_q^q,\qquad
 \sup_{t\le\tau}\lVert H(t)\rVert_q\le C_{q,T}.
\end{equation}
The last estimate follows from \eqref{uni:energy} and
\(H_0\in H^1\hookrightarrow L^q\). In particular, magnetic moments
have been obtained without any higher density moment.

\medskip\noindent\textit{A transported scalar.}
Define zero-mean potentials by
\begin{equation}\label{uni:potentials}
 -\Delta\xi=\operatorname{div}(\rho u),\qquad
 -\Delta\eta=\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}(\rho u\otimes u-H\otimes H),
\end{equation}
and introduce the full effective flux and its primitive
\begin{equation}\label{uni:flux-primitive}
 \mathcal B=a_\delta d-P(\rho)-\frac12|H|^2,\qquad
 \Lambda_\delta(r)=2\mu\log r+\frac{r^\beta-1}{\beta}
                                  +\frac\delta2(r^2-1).
\end{equation}
Taking the divergence of the momentum equation gives
\(\xi_t+\eta+\mathcal B-\langle \mathcal B\rangle=0\).
Since \(r\Lambda_\delta'(r)=a_\delta(r)\), the continuity equation
implies \(D_u\Lambda_\delta(\rho)=-a_\delta d\), where
\(D_u=\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla\). Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{uni:transport}
 D_u(\Lambda_\delta(\rho)-\xi)+P(\rho)+\frac12|H|^2
       =\eta-u\cdot\nabla\xi-\langle \mathcal B\rangle.
\end{equation}
Both the magnetic pressure and the fluid pressure on the left
are nonnegative. No modification of the continuity equation
has been made.

\medskip\noindent\textit{A fixed density moment.}
Fix an integer \(m\ge1\), and set
\begin{equation}\label{uni:indices}
\begin{gathered}
 k=2m,\quad \epsilon=k^{-1},\quad p=k\beta+1,\quad
 s=\frac p\beta=\frac{kp}{p-1}>2,\\
 y=\lVert \rho\rVert_p,\quad
 W=(\Lambda_\delta(\rho)-\xi)_+,\quad
 I=\int\rho W^k\,\mathrm dx,\quad f=I^{1/k}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The continuity equation and the \(C^1\) chain rule for the positive
part to the power \(k\) give
\begin{equation}\label{uni:positive-test}
 \frac1k I'
 \le\int\rho W^{k-1}|\eta-u\cdot\nabla\xi|\,\mathrm dx
       +|\langle \mathcal B\rangle|\int\rho W^{k-1}\,\mathrm dx.
\end{equation}
H\"older's inequality, first in the measure \(\rho\,\mathrm dx\) and then
with spatial exponents \(p,p'\), uses the identity
\begin{equation}\label{uni:weighted-holder}
 \left(\int\rho|G|^k\,\mathrm dx\right)^{1/k}
       \le y^\epsilon\lVert G\rVert_{kp'}
       =y^\epsilon\lVert G\rVert_s.
\end{equation}
To include times when \(I=0\), let \(f_\zeta=(I+\zeta)^{1/k}\),
\(\zeta>0\). Then \eqref{uni:positive-test} yields
\begin{equation}\label{uni:f-derivative}
 f_\zeta'
 \le \left(\frac I{I+\zeta}\right)^{(k-1)/k}
       \left[y^\epsilon\lVert \eta-u\cdot\nabla\xi\rVert_s
                         +M^\epsilon|\langle \mathcal B\rangle|\right]
 \le y^\epsilon\lVert \eta-u\cdot\nabla\xi\rVert_s
                         +M^\epsilon|\langle \mathcal B\rangle|.
\end{equation}

Choose the fixed, finite exponents
\begin{equation}\label{uni:elliptic-indices}
\begin{gathered}
 q=\frac{2p}{\beta-1},\qquad r=\frac{2p}{\beta+1};\\
 \frac1s=\frac1p+\frac2q,\quad
 \frac1r=\frac1p+\frac1q,\quad
 \frac1s=\frac1q+\frac1r.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here \(\beta>1\) ensures \(q<\infty\), and all singular-integral
exponents are strictly between one and infinity. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{uni:eta-xi}
\begin{aligned}
 \lVert \eta\rVert_s
 &\le C_s\bigl(y\lVert u\rVert_q^2+\lVert H\rVert_{2s}^2\bigr),\\
 \lVert u\cdot\nabla\xi\rVert_s
 &\le\lVert u\rVert_q\lVert \nabla\xi\rVert_r
 \le C_r\lVert u\rVert_q\lVert \rho u\rVert_r
 \le C_r y\lVert u\rVert_q^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{uni:velocity} and \eqref{uni:magnetic-moments}
therefore bound their sum by
\(C_{m,T}[y(1+e_\delta)+1]\).
The spatial mean of the flux is controlled separately:
\begin{equation}\label{uni:mean-flux}
 |\langle \mathcal B\rangle|
 \le\left(\int a_\delta d^2\,\mathrm dx\right)^{1/2}
        \left(\int a_\delta\,\mathrm dx\right)^{1/2}
        +\int(P(\rho)+|H|^2/2)\,\mathrm dx
 \le C\bigl[1+\sqrt{e_\delta}(1+y^{B/2})\bigr].
\end{equation}
Indeed \(p=k\beta+1>B\), so that \(\delta\le1\) and finite measure give
\(\int a_\delta\le2\mu+y^\beta+y^2\le C(1+y^B)\).
The pressure integral is controlled by energy for every
\(\gamma>1\); no comparison between \(\gamma\) and \(\beta\) is needed.
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{uni:scalar-ode-input}
 f_\zeta'\le C_{m,T}(1+e_\delta)
                 (1+y^{\max\{1+\epsilon,B/2\}}).
\end{equation}

\medskip\noindent\textit{Coercivity and closure.}
For \(r\ge2\),
\(\Lambda_\delta(r)\ge c_\beta r^\beta\), where
\(c_\beta=(1-2^{-\beta})/\beta\). Since
\(\Lambda_\delta(\rho)\le W+|\xi|\), splitting at \(\rho=2\) and
using \eqref{uni:weighted-holder} gives
\begin{equation}\label{uni:coercivity-first}
 \int\rho^p\,\mathrm dx\le C_m\left(1+I+\int\rho|\xi|^k\,\mathrm dx\right),\qquad
 y^{\beta+\epsilon}\le C_m(1+f+y^\epsilon\lVert \xi\rVert_s).
\end{equation}
Set \(q_0=2s/(s+2)\in(1,2)\); then
\(1/q_0=1/2+1/s\). The finite critical Sobolev estimate and
the kinetic energy bound imply
\begin{equation}\label{uni:xi-lower-power}
 \lVert \xi\rVert_s
 \le C_s\lVert \xi\rVert_{W^{1,q_0}}
 \le C_s\lVert \rho u\rVert_{q_0}
 \le C_s\lVert \sqrt\rho u\rVert_2\lVert \sqrt\rho\rVert_s
 \le C_{m,T}\lVert \rho\rVert_{s/2}^{1/2}
 \le C_{m,T}y^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The last step uses \(1<s/2=p/(2\beta)<p\).
The term \(y^{\epsilon+1/2}\) is strictly lower order than
\(y^{\epsilon+\beta}\). Absorbing it yields
\begin{equation}\label{uni:coercivity}
 y^{\beta+\epsilon}\le C_{m,T}(1+f)
                         \le C_{m,T}(1+f_\zeta).
\end{equation}
Combining this with \eqref{uni:scalar-ode-input}, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{uni:sublinear-ode}
 \begin{gathered}
 f_\zeta'\le C_{m,T}(1+e_\delta)(1+f_\zeta)^{\alpha_m},
 \\
 \alpha_m=\frac{\max\{1+1/(2m),B/2\}}{\beta+1/(2m)}<1.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The strict inequality follows from \(1<\beta\) and
\(B/2=\max\{\beta/2,1\}<\beta\); no upper bound on \(\beta\)
is used.
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{uni:integrated-ode}
 (1+f_\zeta(t))^{1-\alpha_m}
 \le(1+f_\zeta(0))^{1-\alpha_m}
   +C_{m,T}(1-\alpha_m)\left(T+\int_0^\tau e_\delta\,\mathrm dt\right).
\end{equation}
The initial term is uniform: \((\Lambda_\delta(\rho_0))_+\)
is bounded by \eqref{uni:initial-bounds}, whereas
\(\lVert \xi_0\rVert_\infty\le C\lVert \rho_0u_0\rVert_4\le C\).
In particular the negative logarithm at small density does not
enter this upper bound. Taking \(\zeta\downarrow0\) proves the
uniform \(L^{2m\beta+1}\) density bound. Given any finite target
exponent, one chooses a fixed larger \(m\) and uses the finite
measure of \(\mathbb T^2\). No bound uniform as \(m\to\infty\) is asserted.

We give the construction and the estimates needed for the continuation
argument. For a prescribed density \(r\), write
\[
 \mathcal L_r z=\mu\Delta z+
       \nabla\bigl((\mu+\lambda_\delta(r))\operatorname{div} z\bigr).
\]
First consider
\begin{equation}\label{fix:linear}
 r z_t-\mathcal L_r z=f,\qquad
 c\le r\le C,\qquad
 \sup_t(\|r\|_{H^2}+\|r_t\|_{H^1})\le N.
\end{equation}
Let \(P_m\) be a symmetric Fourier projection. The finite-dimensional
mass matrix is positive definite. Its inverse also has the following
bound, uniform in \(m\):
\begin{equation}\label{fix:mass-inverse}
 P_m(rq)=P_m g,\quad q=P_mq
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \|q\|_2\le c^{-1}\|g\|_2,\qquad
 \|q\|_{H^1}\le C_N\|g\|_{H^1}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, testing successively with \(q\) and \((1-\Delta)q\) leaves only
the additional term \(\int\nabla r\cdot\nabla q\,q\). It satisfies
\[
 \left|\int\nabla r\cdot\nabla q\,q\right|
 \le C_N\|q\|_2^{1/2}\|q\|_{H^1}^{3/2}
 \le\varepsilon\|q\|_{H^1}^2+C_{N,\varepsilon}\|q\|_2^2.
\]
Applied to the projected equation, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{fix:linear-time}
 \|z_{m,t}\|_{H^1}
 \le C_N\bigl(\|z_m\|_{H^3}+\|f\|_{H^1}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here \(\|\mathcal L_r z\|_{H^1}\le C_N\|z\|_{H^3}\);
the coefficient terms use \(L^4\times L^4\) and \(L^2\times L^\infty\).

Testing the projected equation with \(z+\Delta^2z\) retains the weighted
inertia. Its highest-order part is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{fix:inertial-identity}
 \int rz_t\Delta^2z
 =\frac12\frac d{dt}\int r|\Delta z|^2
       -\frac12\int r_t|\Delta z|^2
 +2\int(\nabla r\cdot\nabla z_t)\Delta z
       +\int(\Delta r)z_t\Delta z .
\end{equation}
Put \(Z_j=\|z\|_{H^j}\). The last two terms are bounded, by
\eqref{fix:linear-time}, by
\[
 C_N(Z_3+\|f\|_{H^1})Z_2^{1/2}Z_3^{1/2}
 \le\varepsilon Z_3^2+
       C_{N,\varepsilon}(Z_2^2+\|f\|_{H^1}^2).
\]
The \(r_t\) term is bounded by \(C_NZ_2Z_3\).
For \(b=\mu+\lambda_\delta(r)\) and \(d_z=\operatorname{div} z\), the other
highest-order contribution is
\[
 \int b|\Delta d_z|^2+
 \int\bigl(2\nabla b\cdot\nabla d_z+(\Delta b)d_z\bigr)\Delta d_z.
\]
Its second integral is bounded by \(C_NZ_2^{1/2}Z_3^{3/2}\).
The shear term supplies \(\mu\|\nabla\Delta z\|_2^2\).
The forcing requires only \(H^1\), since
\(\int f\Delta^2z=-\int\nabla f\cdot\nabla\Delta z\).
Together with the zeroth-order estimate, this proves
\begin{equation}\label{fix:linear-H2}
 \mathcal E_2'+c_N Z_3^2
 \le C_N\mathcal E_2+C_N\|f\|_{H^1}^2,\qquad
 \mathcal E_2=\int r(|z|^2+|\Delta z|^2)\asymp Z_2^2.
\end{equation}
The energy-equivalence constants depend on the density interval, not on
its \(H^2\) norm. The same argument with \(z-\Delta z\), using first the
\(L^2\) part of \eqref{fix:mass-inverse}, proves
\begin{equation}\label{fix:linear-H1}
 \mathcal E_1'+c_N\|z\|_{H^2}^2
 \le C_N\mathcal E_1+C_N\|f\|_2^2,\qquad
 \mathcal E_1=\int r(|z|^2+|\nabla z|^2).
\end{equation}
Galerkin compactness gives the linear solution; the same energy
estimates give its uniqueness.

Start with heat extensions \(u^0,H^0\) of the initial data and iterate
\begin{equation}\label{fix:iteration}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\rho^{n+1}_t+\operatorname{div}(\rho^{n+1}u^n)=0,\\
 &\rho^{n+1}u^{n+1}_t-\mathcal L_{\rho^{n+1}}u^{n+1}=f^n,\\
 &f^n=-\rho^{n+1}u^n\cdot\nabla u^n-\nabla P(\rho^{n+1})
                      +\operatorname{div}\mathbb M(H^n),\\
 &H^{n+1}_t-\nu\Delta H^{n+1}
       =\operatorname{div}(u^n\otimes H^n-H^n\otimes u^n).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
All iterates have the prescribed initial data. The last tensor is
antisymmetric, so its double divergence vanishes and
\(\operatorname{div} H^{n+1}=0\).
On a fixed \(H^2\) ball, the two parabolic right-hand sides are bounded
in \(L^\infty H^1\). The transport estimates give
\begin{equation}\label{fix:transport-ball}
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\rho^{n+1}\|_{L^\infty(0,t;H^2)}
 &\le\|\rho_0\|_{H^2}
          \exp\bigl(C\sqrt t\,\|u^n\|_{L^2(0,t;H^3)}\bigr),\\
 \|\rho_t^{n+1}\|_{L^\infty(0,t;H^1)}&\le C_N .
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The characteristic formula keeps the density in
\([c_0/2,2C_0]\) after reducing \(t\).
Choose first an \(H^2\) ball with strict margin over the initial weighted
energies, then a ball for the \(L^2H^3\) norms, and finally a small time.
In \eqref{fix:linear-H2} the initial energy is multiplied by a Gronwall
factor that can be kept below \(2\); after absorption the shear and
magnetic dissipation retain fixed positive coefficients.
Consequently this order of choices preserves both balls.
It also bounds \(u_t^n,H_t^n\) in \(L^2H^1\).

For completeness, the contraction does not require the entire
\(L^2H^3\) norm of the initial heat layer to be small.
Write \(R^{n+1}=\rho^{n+1}-\rho^n\), \(U^{n+1}=u^{n+1}-u^n\),
and \(Z^{n+1}=H^{n+1}-H^n\). Since
\[
 R^{n+1}_t+\operatorname{div}(u^nR^{n+1})
       =-\operatorname{div}(\rho^n U^n),\qquad R^{n+1}(0)=0,
\]
the \(H^1\) transport estimate implies
\begin{equation}\label{fix:density-difference}
 \|R^{n+1}\|_{L^\infty H^1}^2
       \le Ct\|U^n\|_{L^2H^2}^2.
\end{equation}
The velocity difference has the new density as its inertia coefficient,
and its additional source terms are
\[
 -R^{n+1}u_t^n+
 \nabla\bigl((\lambda_\delta(\rho^{n+1})
                 -\lambda_\delta(\rho^n))\operatorname{div} u^n\bigr).
\]
Their \(L^2\) norm is bounded by
\[
 C\|R^{n+1}\|_{H^1}
                  (\|u_t^n\|_{H^1}+\|u^n\|_{H^3}).
\]
For example, the differentiated coefficient terms use respectively
\(L^2\times L^\infty\), \(L^4\times L^4\times L^\infty\), and
\(L^4\times L^4\), not an \(H^1\)-to-\(L^\infty\) embedding.
The differences of the remaining sources are bounded in \(L^2\) by
\(C(\|R^{n+1}\|_{H^1}+\|U^n\|_{H^1}+\|Z^n\|_{H^1})\).
Thus \eqref{fix:linear-H1} and \eqref{fix:density-difference} give
\(\mathfrak D_{n+1}\le Ct\mathfrak D_n\), where
\(\mathfrak D_n\) is the squared \(L^\infty H^1\cap L^2H^2\)
difference norm of \(u^n,H^n\).
The small factor is the time integral in
\eqref{fix:density-difference}. Contraction, weak compactness in the
higher norms, and the equations give \eqref{fix:local-class}.
Strong \(H^2\) continuity follows from the linear energy identities
and the \(H^2\) transport estimate. The same difference argument proves
uniqueness and the asserted uniform restart time.

\begin{equation}\label{comp:notation}
 m_n=\rho_nu_n,\qquad d_n=\operatorname{div} u_n,\qquad

\end{equation}

for every fixed finite $k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{comp:moments}
 \sup_n\lVert \rho_n\rVert_{L^\infty L^k}<\infty.
\end{equation}
Assume also the uniform energy bounds
\begin{equation}\label{comp:energy}
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_n\bigg\{&\sup_{0\le t\le T}

 &+\int_{Q_T}\Big[|\nabla u_n|^2+|\nabla H_n|^2

                         \bigg\}<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Suppose the initial densities satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{comp:initial}
 0<c_0\le\rho_{0n}\le C_0,\qquad
 \rho_{0n}\longrightarrow\rho_0
       \quad\text{in every finite }L^k(\mathbb T^2).
\end{equation}
Then, along a subsequence, there exist $\rho,u,H$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{comp:conclusion}
 \rho_n\longrightarrow\rho
       \quad\text{strongly in every finite }L^k(Q_T),

\end{equation}

\begin{aligned}
 \rho_n^\gamma&\longrightarrow\rho^\gamma
                  &&\text{in every finite }L^k(Q_T),\\
 \rho_n^\beta d_n&\longrightarrow\rho^\beta d
                  &&\text{in }\mathcal D'(Q_T),\\

                  &&\text{in }L^2(Q_T).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{comp:flux}
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{comp:density}, let
$b\in C^1([0,\infty))$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{comp:b-condition}
 \lVert b\rVert_\infty+\lVert k_b\rVert_\infty<\infty,\qquad

\end{equation}

                    \quad\text{in }\mathcal D'(Q_T).

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Put $b_n=b(\rho_n)$ and $K_n=k_b(\rho_n)d_n$. The exact nonlinear
continuity equation is
\begin{equation}\label{comp:b-continuity}
 \partial_tb_n+\operatorname{div}(b_nu_n)=-K_n.
\end{equation}
In view of \eqref{comp:b-condition}, $K_n$ is bounded in $L^2(Q_T)$.
Hence $b_n$ is compact in $L^2H^{-\eta}$ for $0<\eta<1$. The argument
in \eqref{comp:product-identification} gives
$b_nu_n\rightharpoonup\overline b\,u$ in $L^2L^2$. Writing
$K_n\rightharpoonup\overline K$ in $L^2(Q_T)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{comp:b-limit}
 \partial_t\overline b+\operatorname{div}(\overline b\,u)=-\overline K.
\end{equation}
Neither $\overline b=b(\rho)$ nor
$\overline K=k_b(\rho)\operatorname{div} u$ is used here.

Let $\Delta^{-1}$ be the inverse of the negative-spectrum Laplacian on
mean-zero functions, with its zero mode set to zero, and define
\begin{equation}\label{comp:potential}
 \Phi_n=\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{div} m_n,\qquad
 \mathcal R_{ij}=\partial_i\partial_j\Delta^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The multiplier $\mathcal R_{ij}$ is self-adjoint and has symbol
$\xi_i\xi_j/|\xi|^2$ at nonzero frequencies. The order $-1$ of the
potential, together with \eqref{comp:strong-negative}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{comp:potential-compactness}
\begin{aligned}
 &\Phi_n\to\Phi\quad\text{in }L^2H^{2/5}\subset L^2L^2,\\
 &\Phi_n\text{ bounded in }L^\infty W^{1,4/3},\qquad
   \partial_t\Phi_n\text{ bounded in }L^2L^{8/7}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Fix $\psi\in C_c^\infty(Q_T)$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{comp:commutator-definition}
 C_{n,i}=\sum_j[u_{n,j},\mathcal R_{ij}](\psi b_n),\qquad
 [v,\mathcal R]f=v\mathcal Rf-\mathcal R(vf).
\end{equation}
The nonendpoint derivative commutator estimate
of \cite{CoifmanMeyer1978} gives
\begin{equation}\label{comp:commutator-bound}
 \lVert \nabla[v,\mathcal R_{ij}]f\rVert_{3/2}
       \le C\lVert \nabla v\rVert_2\lVert f\rVert_6.
\end{equation}
The zero-order term is controlled by H\"older's inequality and the
$L^q$ boundedness of $\mathcal R_{ij}$. Thus $C_n$ is bounded in
$L^2W^{1,3/2}\hookrightarrow L^2H^{3/5}$. No derivative of $b_n$ is
required. Strong negative-space convergence of $b_n$ identifies both
products in \eqref{comp:commutator-definition}, so
\begin{equation}\label{comp:commutator-limit}
\begin{aligned}
 C_n&\rightharpoonup C\quad\text{in }L^2H^{3/5},\\
 C_i&=\sum_j[u_j,\mathcal R_{ij}](\psi\overline b),\qquad
 \int_0^T\langle m_n,C_n\rangle\,\mathrm dt\longrightarrow
       \int_0^T\langle m,C\rangle\,\mathrm dt.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For instance, testing $u_n\mathcal R(\psi b_n)$ transfers the difference
$b_n-\overline b$ to a strong $H^{-\eta}$ factor paired with a bounded
$H^1$ factor. The last convergence in \eqref{comp:commutator-limit}
uses \eqref{comp:strong-negative}, not strong convergence of $C_n$.

Define the magnetic potential
\begin{equation}\label{comp:magnetic-potential}
 \mathcal A_n=M_n-\langle M_n\rangle
                    -\sum_{i,j}\mathcal R_{ij}(H_{n,i}H_{n,j}).
\end{equation}
It converges strongly in $L^1L^2$ by
\eqref{comp:magnetic-compactness}. Applying $\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{div}$ to the
momentum equation yields the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{comp:potential-equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \partial_t\Phi_n+\mathcal R_{ij}(m_{n,i}u_{n,j})+\mathcal A_n
       &=-F_n+\langle F_n\rangle
     +e_n-\langle e_n\rangle,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where repeated indices are summed. Testing with $\psi b_n$, integrating
in time by means of \eqref{comp:b-continuity}, and using self-adjointness
of $\mathcal R_{ij}$ give
\begin{equation}\label{comp:localized-identity}
\begin{aligned}
 \int_{Q_T}\psi b_nF_n
 ={}&\int_{Q_T}\bigl[\Phi_nb_n(\psi_t+u_n\cdot\nabla\psi)
                    -m_n\cdot C_n-\psi\Phi_nK_n\bigr]\\
    &+\int_{Q_T}\psi b_n(e_n-\langle e_n\rangle-\mathcal A_n)
       +\int_0^T\langle F_n\rangle\Big(\int_{\mathbb T^2}\psi b_n\,\mathrm dx\Big)\,\mathrm dt.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here and in similar formulas the unmarked space-time integrals use
$\,\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dt$. In particular, the source term has the negative sign displayed
in \eqref{comp:localized-identity}. The commutator sign follows from
$\partial_j\Phi_n=\mathcal R_{ij}m_{n,i}$.

Every contribution in \eqref{comp:localized-identity} has a justified limit.
The time-cutoff and transport terms use strong $\Phi_n$ in $L^2L^2$
and weak convergence of $b_n$ and $b_nu_n$. The commutator term uses
\eqref{comp:commutator-limit}. Crucially, the actual source converges to
$-\int_{Q_T}\psi\Phi\overline K$, by strong $\Phi_n$ and weak $K_n$ in
$L^2(Q_T)$. The magnetic term uses strong $L^1L^2$ convergence of
$\mathcal A_n$ and the uniform bound for $b_n$.

The mean term requires a separate time compactness argument. The function
$B_{n,\psi}(t)=\int_{\mathbb T^2}\psi b_n\,\mathrm dx$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{comp:mean-time}
 B_{n,\psi}'=\int_{\mathbb T^2}
       (\psi_tb_n+\nabla\psi\cdot b_nu_n-\psi K_n)\,\mathrm dx.
\end{equation}
It is bounded in $H^1(0,T)$ and therefore converges strongly in
$C([0,T])$ along a subsequence. This is sufficient to pair it with the
weak $L^2$ limit of $\langle F_n\rangle$. Finally, the artificial-stress contribution
$\int_{Q_T}\psi b_n(e_n-\langle e_n\rangle)$ vanishes by \eqref{comp:small-stress}.

To identify the resulting expression, one must also justify the time
integration by parts at the limit. The appropriate spaces are
\begin{equation}\label{comp:limit-time-spaces}
\begin{aligned}
 &\Phi\in L^2W^{1,8/7},\qquad \Phi_t\in L^2L^{8/7},\\
 &\overline b\in L^2L^8,\qquad
       \overline b_t\in L^2W^{-1,8}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\overline b u\in L^2L^8$ and $\overline K\in L^2L^2$; moreover,
$L^2\hookrightarrow W^{-1,8}$ is dual to
$W^{1,8/7}\hookrightarrow L^2$. Regularizing the fixed limit functions
in these dual spaces proves
\begin{equation}\label{comp:limit-time-product}
 -\int_{Q_T}\psi\overline b\Phi_t
 =\int_{Q_T}\bigl[\Phi\overline b(\psi_t+u\cdot\nabla\psi)
       +\psi\overline b u\cdot\nabla\Phi-\psi\Phi\overline K\bigr].
\end{equation}
The spatial products are integrable since
$u\cdot\nabla\Phi\in L^2L^{8/7}$ and
$\Phi\overline K\in L^2L^{4/3}$. No $L^2H^1$ estimate for $\Phi$ is
being assumed. Apply \eqref{comp:limit-time-product} to the limit of
\eqref{comp:potential-equation}. It reproduces precisely the limit of
the right-hand side of \eqref{comp:localized-identity}, including the
same term $-\int\psi\Phi\overline K$. The result is
$\int\psi\overline F\,\overline b$, proving
\eqref{comp:flux-factorization}. A countable dense family of tests,
followed by the uniform bounds, provides a common subsequence.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{ws:relative-theorem}
Let \(\beta>1\) and \(\gamma>1\). Suppose that
\((\rho,u,H)\) satisfies Definition~\ref{def:weak} on \([0,T]\)
and has positive total mass. Let \((r,U,B)\) solve the same
system with
\begin{equation}\label{ws:strong-class}
\begin{gathered}
 0<c_T\le r\le C_T,\qquad \operatorname{div} B=0,\qquad
 r,U,B\in C([0,T];H^2),\\
 U,B\in L^2(0,T;H^3),\qquad
 U_t,B_t\in L^2(0,T;H^1),\qquad r_t\in C([0,T];H^1).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The initial states need not coincide. There is a nonnegative
\(G_T\in L^1(0,T)\) such that, for every \(t\in[0,T]\),
\begin{equation}\label{ws:relative-estimate}
 \mathcal F(t)+\frac12\int_0^t\mathcal D(s)\,\mathrm d s
 \le\mathcal F(0)+\int_0^tG_T(s)\mathcal F(s)\,\mathrm d s.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{ws:gronwall}
 \mathcal F(t)\le\mathcal F(0)
                  \exp\left(\int_0^tG_T(s)\,\mathrm d s\right).
\end{equation}
The coefficient can depend on fixed finite weak-density moments,
total mass, the reference density interval, and reference norms.
It does not depend on a weak-density pointwise bound or gradient.

\(\mathfrak d=D\asymp m^{\beta-1}\), this gives
\begin{equation}\label{sl:initial-layer-flux}
\begin{aligned}
 \int_0^{T_0}G^2\,\mathrm dt
 &\le C_*\left(m^{\beta+1}+m^2t_L+m+m^{2-\gamma}\right)\\
 &\le C_*\left(m^{\beta+1}+m^{3-\beta}\log m
                           +m+m^{2-\gamma}\right)
 \le C_*m^{\beta+1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In the last inequality,
\(m^{3-\beta}\log m=o(m^{\beta+1})\) because \(\beta>1\),
while \(\gamma>\beta+1\). This prefix estimate does not presume
existence up to \(t_L\).

Use \(\|q\|_{H^1}^2\le Mm^{1-\gamma}\) on the prefix. On
\([t_L,T]\), when nonempty, use \eqref{sl:staged-profile-input}
and the full-time budget \(\int_0^TG^2\,\mathrm dt\le C_*m^3\) from
\eqref{sl:flux-budgets}. Thus
\[
 \frac1m\int_0^T(\delta_\rho^2+\|q\|_r^2)G^2\,\mathrm dt
 \le C_{r,*}\left(m^{\beta+1-\gamma}
                             +m^{a_*+2-\gamma}\right).
\]
Both exponents are strictly negative. Importantly, the full-time
\(m^3\) budget has only been paired with the improved tail bound,
not with the coarse initial-layer bound.

For \(\beta>2\), multiply \eqref{sl:profile-input} by \(G^2/m\)
and apply separately the weighted and unweighted estimates in
\eqref{sl:flux-budgets}. Their contributions are respectively
\(C_{r,*}m^{\beta+1-\gamma}\) and
\(C_{r,*}m^{\beta+2-s-\gamma}\). Writing
\(a=\gamma-\beta>1\) makes the
two exponents \(1-a<0\) and \(2-s-a<0\), since \(s>1\).
This proves \eqref{sl:feedback-budget} in both branches.

Now apply Lemma~\ref{sl:mixed}, \eqref{sl:low-profile}, the Hodge identity
\(\|\nabla u\|_2^2=\|d\|_2^2+\|\omega\|_2^2\), and the basic kinetic
bound. Lemmas~\ref{sl:magnetic} and~\ref{sl:finite-p} give the last
assertion, replacing the history integral by the larger full integral
in \eqref{sl:feedback-budget}. The smallness assumption of
Lemma~\ref{sl:finite-p} follows already from the coarse bound
\(\|q\|_{H^1}^2\le Mm^{1-\gamma}\).
All dependence on the high bootstrap heights is multiplied by a
strictly negative power of \(m\). Thus these implications do not
introduce a constant-level circular dependence.

\label{sg:high-proposition}
Fix
\[
 1<\beta\le2,\qquad \gamma>\beta+1,\qquad a\le1,
\]
and choose \(h\) so that
\begin{equation}\label{sg:h-interval}
 h>\beta,\qquad a+h+\beta+1-2\gamma<0,
 \qquad h<3\beta+2\gamma-2 .
\end{equation}
Choose fixed finite \(P\ge4\) and \(p\ge P\) so that
\begin{equation}\label{sg:exponent-choices}
 \kappa_P:=2+\frac4P<\min\{2\beta,h+2-\beta\},
 \qquad \frac{h+1}{p+1}<\beta-1 .
\end{equation}
Suppose a smooth solution on \([0,T]\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{sg:bootstrap-assumptions}
 \langle \rho\rangle=m,\quad
 \|\rho/m-1\|_\infty\le\delta_0,\quad
 \sup_{t\le T}\|u(t)\|_{H^1}^2\le V,\quad
 \sup_{t\le T}\|\omega(t)\|_p\le W,\quad
 \sup_{t\le T}\mathcal A_1(t)\le Mm ,
\end{equation}
where \(\delta_0>0\) is fixed sufficiently small.
Suppose additionally that, for some \(0\le s\le T\),
\begin{equation}\label{sg:improved-input}
 \mathcal A_1(t)\le C_a m^a\qquad(s\le t\le T),
\end{equation}
with \(C_a\) independent of \(m,s,T\). The coarse bound
\(\mathcal A_1\le Mm\) remains available in all other estimates.
Use the basic energy and magnetic estimates in the following explicit
form:
\begin{equation}\label{sg:lower-inputs}
\begin{gathered}
 \int_0^T\|\nabla u\|_2^2\,\mathrm dt\le C_Rm,\qquad
 \int_0^T\|d\|_2^2\,\mathrm dt\le C_Rm^{1-\beta},\\
 \int_0^T\bigl(\|\nabla K_H\|_2^2+\|K_H\|_4^2\bigr)\,\mathrm dt
       \le C_*(V,R)m .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
These are lower-order inputs, not assumptions on high derivatives
of the magnetic forcing. For a fixed sufficiently small
\(\varepsilon_0>0\), and then sufficiently large \(m\),
\(\mathcal A_{\mathsf T}\asymp\mathcal A_1\), and on \([s,T]\),
\begin{equation}\label{sg:joint-sharp}
\begin{aligned}
 Y'+c\bigl(\mathscr D_S+\mathscr B_\rho+m^{-h}Z^2\bigr)
 \le{}& C_*\bigl[
       m^{2-\beta}\|\nabla u\|_4^4
       +m^{\kappa_P-h}\|\nabla u\|_2^{\kappa_P}\\
 &\hspace{17mm}
       +(m^{2-h}+m^{e_P})\|d\|_2^2\bigr]+\mathcal M(t),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where
\[
 Z=\|\nabla^2\omega\|_2,\qquad
 e_P=\frac{P(2-h)-\beta}{P-1},\qquad
 \mathcal M(t)\ge0,\qquad
 \int_0^T\mathcal M(t)\,\mathrm dt\le C_*m^{1-\beta}.
\]
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{sg:joint-simplified}
 Y'+c\bigl(\mathscr D_S+\mathscr B_\rho+m^{-h}Z^2\bigr)
       \le C_*m^{2-\beta}+\mathcal M(t).
\end{equation}
With \(k(h)=\max\{3-2\beta,\beta+1-h,1-\beta\}<1\),
\begin{equation}\label{sg:dynamic-conclusion}
\begin{aligned}
 Y_h(t)&\le e^{-cD(t-s)}Y_h(s)+C_*m^{k(h)},\\
 \|q(t)\|_{H^1}^2
       &\le Cm^{-\gamma}e^{-cD(t-s)}Y_h(s)
                                      +C_*m^{k(h)-\gamma}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(C_*\) may also depend on \(a,h,C_a\). All constants and
thresholds are independent of \(s,T\). For any prescribed finite
collection of admissible pairs \((a,h)\), the correction parameter
\(\varepsilon_0\) and the finite spatial exponents can be chosen
in common.

\label{sg:full-time-remark}
In this conditional remark the constants are denoted by $C_*$;
they become data-dependent constants $C_R$ after the bootstrap heights
are fixed in Section~\ref{sec:strong-closure}.
Suppose the lower-order bootstrap has additionally supplied
\[
 \int_0^T\|\nabla\omega\|_2^2\,\mathrm dt\le C_Rm.
\]
Then, on any interval \([s,T]\) on which the improved input and
weight conditions of Proposition~\ref{sg:high-proposition} hold,
the sharp inequality \eqref{sg:joint-sharp}, rather than
its constant-source simplification, yields
\[
 \int_s^T
       (\mathscr D_S+\mathscr B_\rho+m^{-h}Z^2)\,\mathrm dt
                         \le C\,Y_h(s)+C_*m^{3-\beta}.
\]
In particular this is bounded by \(C_*m^{3-\beta}\) whenever
\(Y_h(s)\le C_*m\), since \(\beta\le2\). This includes the first
stage starting at zero, with \(Y_h(0)\le C_Rm\).
Indeed
\(\int\|\omega\|_4^4\le
C\sup\|\omega\|_2^2\int\|\nabla\omega\|_2^2\le C_*m\),
while
\(\int\|d\|_4^4\le
C\sup\|d\|_{H^1}^2\int\|d\|_2^2\le C_*m^{1-\beta}\).
Hodge gives \(\int\|\nabla u\|_4^4\le C_*m\).
The transport remainder costs \(m^{\kappa_P+1-h}\),
which is smaller than \(m^{3-\beta}\) by
\eqref{sg:exponent-choices}; the compression remainders
cost \(m^{3-h-\beta}+m^{e_P+1-\beta}\), also smaller.
One must not obtain this conclusion by integrating the
constant source in \eqref{sg:joint-simplified}.

\label{sg:superquadratic}
Suppose \(\beta>2\) and \(\gamma>\beta+1\). Define
\begin{equation}\label{sg:super-parameters}
 a=\gamma-\beta,\qquad
 s=\min\{\beta-1,a\}>1,\qquad h=2s,\qquad
 \mathfrak d=\min\{D,\Gamma\}\asymp m^s.
\end{equation}
Choose fixed finite \(P\ge4\) and \(p\ge P\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{sg:super-exponents}
 \kappa_P=2+\frac4P<2s,
 \qquad \frac{h+1}{p+1}<s.
\end{equation}
Under \eqref{sg:bootstrap-assumptions} and \eqref{sg:lower-inputs},
the energy \(Y\) in \eqref{sg:modified-energy}, with this value of
\(h\), is equivalent to \(\mathcal A_1+\mathcal V_h\), and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{sg:super-sharp}
\begin{aligned}
 Y'+c\bigl(\mathscr D_S+\mathscr B_\rho+m^{-h}Z^2\bigr)
 \le{}&C_*\bigl[m^{2-\beta}\|\nabla u\|_4^4
       +m^{\kappa_P-h}\|\nabla u\|_2^{\kappa_P}\\
 &\hspace{16mm}+(m^{2-h}+m^{e_P})\|d\|_2^2\bigr]
       +\mathcal M(t),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(e_P=[P(2-h)-\beta]/(P-1)\), and
\begin{equation}\label{sg:super-magnetic}
 \mathcal M\ge0,\qquad
 \int_0^T\mathcal M\,\mathrm dt
       \le C_*(m^{1-\beta}+m^{1-h}).
\end{equation}
If \(Y(0)\le C_Rm\), then
\begin{equation}\label{sg:super-dynamic}
\begin{aligned}
 Y(t)&\le C_Rm e^{-c\mathfrak d t}+C_*m^{2-s},\qquad
 \|q(t)\|_{H^1}^2
   \le C_Rm^{1-\gamma}e^{-c\mathfrak d t}
                                      +C_*m^{2-s-\gamma}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{sg:super-full-time}
 \int_0^T\bigl(\mathscr D_S+\mathscr B_\rho+m^{-h}Z^2\bigr)\,\mathrm dt
       \le C_*m.
\end{equation}
All constants and thresholds are independent of \(T\).

We retain the exact symbols in \eqref{sg:frequency-parameters},
rather than replacing the multiplier by a scalar. At every nonzero
torus frequency,
\begin{equation}\label{sg:super-coercivity}
 \alpha(\xi)\ge c\min\{D,\Gamma\}=c\mathfrak d,
 \qquad
 \mathscr D_S+\mathscr B_\rho
       \ge c\mathfrak d\mathcal A_1.
\end{equation}
The second statement follows from \eqref{sg:energy-equivalence} and
the Poincare inequality for \(f\); indeed the flux part has the
stronger rate \(D\ge\mathfrak d\). The multiplier bound
\(\alpha(\xi)\le c_s|\xi|/2\) and the density recovery
\eqref{sg:q-small} are unchanged, so the corrected energy remains
coercive with a fixed small \(\varepsilon_0\).

\emph{Acoustic errors and pressure positivity.}
Use the exact identities \eqref{sg:exact-high-identity} and
\eqref{sg:cross-identity}. The algebraic estimates
\eqref{sg:forcing-bound}--\eqref{sg:quadratic-density-strain},
\eqref{sg:commutator-budget}, and
\eqref{sg:diffusion-cross}--\eqref{sg:cross-physical} do not require
\(h>\beta\) or \(K\to\infty\). Their absorption now uses
\(\mathfrak d\), not \(D\). To make this change explicit, the
coefficients multiplying \(\mathcal A_1\), apart from the
differentiated solenoidal strain, are bounded by a fixed multiple of
\begin{equation}\label{sg:super-error-coefficients}
\begin{aligned}
 &m^{h-2\beta+1-2\gamma}+m^{-(\beta-1)/3}
       +m^{\beta-\gamma}+m^{-(\beta-1)/(p-1)}\\
 &\quad +m^{(h+1)/(p+1)}
       +m^{-(\beta-1)\theta/(2-\theta)}
       +m^{\beta+1-2\gamma}+m^{1-\beta}+B_q+B_q^2,
       \qquad \theta=2/p.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Each is \(o(\mathfrak d)\). For the only positive exponent that
needs a choice of \(p\), use \eqref{sg:super-exponents}. For the
shear term, \(h=2s\), \(s\le\beta-1\), and \(\gamma>\beta+1\)
give \(h-2\beta+1-2\gamma-s<0\). The diffusion cross term has
relative exponent \(\beta+1-2\gamma-s<0\). All the other displayed
coefficients either have negative powers or tend to zero by
\eqref{sg:q-small}.

The genuine pressure calculation
\eqref{sg:cross-pressure}--\eqref{sg:pressure-positive} remains
valid also when \(K\) tends to zero or stays bounded, because
\[
 (\delta_\rho+B_q)\sqrt{1+K^2}
       \le C_*(m^{(1-\gamma)/2}+m^{1-\beta})\longrightarrow0.
\]
In estimating the diffusion cross term, the coefficient left after
paying \(\mathscr B_\rho\) is exactly of order
\(\Gamma m^{-\gamma}\asymp m^{-\beta}\); hence it is paid by
\(\mathscr D_S\) regardless of the size of \(K\). The resolvent
commutator uses \(\Gamma/K=c_s\) and the bounds already displayed
before \eqref{sg:commutator-raw}, which hold for every \(K>0\).
The adverse inverse-viscosity cross terms contain
\(\delta_\rho K\) or \(B_qK\), both tending to zero by the same
estimate. Thus no fast acoustic gap has been assumed in this step.

\emph{Differentiated density strain without a cutoff.}
Let \(E_q=m^\gamma\|\nabla q\|_2^2\). For any of its principal
tensor contractions, the finite-exponent interpolation used in
\eqref{sg:high-density-estimate} gives directly
\[
 |\mathcal G|\le C_p\|\omega\|_p^{1/2}Z^{1/2}E_q.
\]
Here all density frequencies are retained. Twice applying Young's
inequality yields
\begin{equation}\label{sg:super-all-frequency}
\begin{aligned}
 |\mathcal G|
 &\le\delta\mathfrak d E_q
       +C_{\delta,p}\mathfrak d^{-1}\|\omega\|_p ZE_q
 \le\delta\mathscr B_\rho+\delta m^{-h}Z^2
       +C_{\delta,p}m^h\mathfrak d^{-2}\|\omega\|_p^2E_q^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since \(E_q\le C_*m\), the last term divided by
\(\mathfrak d E_q\) is bounded by
\(C_*m^{h+1}\mathfrak d^{-3}\le C_*m^{1-s}=o(1)\).
The genuine coefficient \(B(\rho)\) is bounded in \(L^\infty\),
so this estimate includes the variable-coefficient principal
contractions. The cubic terms generated by
\(\nabla w=A(\nabla q+\sigma q\otimes q)\) are integrated once
against \(\nabla v\), exactly as in the calculation preceding
\eqref{sg:full-differentiated-strain}. They cost
\(C_p\|\omega\|_p(B_q+B_q^2)E_q=o(\mathfrak d)E_q\).
Combining these estimates proves
\begin{equation}\label{sg:super-acoustic}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal A_{\mathsf T}'+c\mathfrak d\mathcal A_{\mathsf T}
      +c\mathscr D_S+c\mathscr B_\rho
 \le{}&\delta m^{-h}Z^2+C_\delta m^{2-\beta}\|\nabla u\|_4^4
 +C_\delta m^{-\beta}
           (\|\nabla K_H\|_2^2+B_q^2\|K_H\|_4^2).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\emph{Palinstrophy, including the baroclinic term.}
Apply the original test \eqref{sg:palinstrophy-identity} with the
weight \(m^{1-h}\). The transport and compression estimates
\eqref{sg:pal-transport}--\eqref{sg:pal-compression} hold with the

\[
 \delta m^{-h}Z^2+C_*m^{-h}\|q\|_8^4
 \le\delta m^{-h}Z^2
       +C_*m^{1-h-2\gamma-s}\mathscr B_\rho,
\]
using \(\|q\|_8^4\le C_*m^{1-\gamma}\|\nabla q\|_2^2\) and

For the baroclinic remainder retain the second line of
\eqref{sg:pal-baroclinic}, but pay its last term from the flux
dissipation, rather than from a putative rate \(D\) on the density:
\begin{equation}\label{sg:super-baroclinic}
\begin{aligned}
 m^{\beta-2h}\|q\|_4^4\|f\|_2^2
 &\le C_*m^{\beta-2h+2-2\gamma}\|f\|_2^2
 \le C_*m^{2-2h-2a}\mathscr D_S=o(\mathscr D_S).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This uses \(\|f\|_2\le C\|\nabla f\|_2\), and is valid even when
\(h\le\beta\). Finally \eqref{sg:pal-magnetic} gives its original
coefficient \(m^{-h}\). Adding these five remainder estimates to
\eqref{sg:super-acoustic} proves \eqref{sg:super-sharp}, with
\[
 \mathcal M=C_*(m^{-\beta}+m^{-h})
          (\|\nabla K_H\|_2^2+B_q^2\|K_H\|_4^2).
\]
The input \eqref{sg:lower-inputs} and \(B_q^2=o(1)\) imply
\eqref{sg:super-magnetic}. Neither of its two powers is discarded.

\emph{The dynamic estimate and the full-time budget.}
As before,
\(m^{-h}Z^2\ge c(V)m^{h-2}\mathcal V_h^2\). Completing the
square with the actual acoustic rate gives
\[
 c(V)m^{h-2}\mathcal V_h^2
       \ge c_1\mathfrak d\mathcal V_h
                         -C_*\mathfrak d^2m^{2-h}
       \ge c_1\mathfrak d\mathcal V_h-C_*m^2.
\]
The potential estimate \eqref{sg:potential-H1}, Hodge, and the
fixed finite-exponent vorticity bound give
\(\|\nabla u\|_4+\|d\|_2\le C_*\). Moreover
\(\kappa_P-h<0\), \(2-h<0\), and \(e_P\le2-h\).
Thus \eqref{sg:super-sharp} implies
\[
 Y'+c\mathfrak dY\le C_*m^2+\mathcal M.
\]
Variation of constants gives
\[
 Y(t)\le C_Rm e^{-c\mathfrak d t}
       +C_*(m^{2-s}+m^{1-\beta}+m^{1-h}).
\]
Both magnetic powers are at most \(2-s\), since
\(s\le\beta-1\) and \(h=2s\). This proves the first estimate in
\eqref{sg:super-dynamic}; \eqref{sg:q-small} gives the second.

For the full-time estimate, do not integrate the constant source
\(m^2\). The additional lower-order budget, Hodge, and
\eqref{sg:potential-H1} give
\(\int\|\nabla u\|_4^4\,\mathrm dt\le C_*m\), exactly by the two
interpolations in Remark~\ref{sg:full-time-remark}. Also
\(\int\|\nabla u\|_2^{\kappa_P}\,\mathrm dt\le C_*m\).
Integrating \eqref{sg:super-sharp}, including its initial energy,
therefore costs at most a fixed multiple of
\[
 m+m^{3-\beta}+m^{\kappa_P+1-h}
       +m^{3-h-\beta}+m^{e_P+1-\beta}
       +m^{1-\beta}+m^{1-h}\le C m.
\]
Here \(\beta>2\), \(h>2\), and \(\kappa_P<h\) are precisely
the inequalities needed for the last comparison. This proves
\eqref{sg:super-full-time}.
