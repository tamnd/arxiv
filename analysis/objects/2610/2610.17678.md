<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:stability}
Let the uniqueness assumption, Assumption~\ref{assum:infsup}, hold.  For every $f_1\in L^{2}(\Omega)$,
${\bf f}_2\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}$, $\phi\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma_D)$ and $\psi\in\Hm00(\Gamma_N)$,
problem \eqref{weak} has a unique solution $u$.  With
$\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}:=-(A\nabla u+{\bf b} u+{\bf f}_2)$, the pair $(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$},u)$ is the unique
solution of \eqref{fos} in $H(\operatorname{div};\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stability}
\|u\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\|_{H(\operatorname{div};\Omega)}\ \le\
C_{S}\big(\|f_1\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\bf f}_2\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\psi\|_{\Hm00(\Gamma_N)}
+\|\phi\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}\big).
\end{equation}
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $u_\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)$ with $\operatorname{tr}^{g}_{\Gamma_D}(u_\phi)=\phi$.  By
\eqref{continuity_a}, \eqref{infsup} and the Ne\v{c}as--Babu\v{s}ka theorem
there is a unique $u_0\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega)$ with
$a(u_0,v_0)=\ell(v_0)-a(u_\phi,v_0)$ for all $v_0\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega)$, and
$\beta_a\|u_0\|_{1,\Omega}\le\|f_1\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\bf f}_2\|_{0,\Omega}
+\|\psi\|_{\Hm00(\Gamma_N)}+C_{con}\|u_\phi\|_{1,\Omega}$.  Thus $u:=u_0+u_\phi$ is the
unique solution of \eqref{weak}, and the infimum over $u_\phi$ and
\eqref{eq:quotient_inf} bound $\|u\|_{1,\Omega}$ as in \eqref{eq:stability}.
Testing \eqref{weak} with $v_0\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ gives
$\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}=f_1-\mbox{\boldmath$\beta$}\cdot\nabla u-\mu u$, and \eqref{inparts} with
$\gamma=\Gamma_N$ gives $\operatorname{tr}^{d}_{\Gamma_N}(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\cdot{\bf n})=\psi$, so $(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$},u)$
solves \eqref{fos}; the bounds for $\|\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\|_{0,\Omega}$ and
$\|\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\|_{0,\Omega}$ follow from the two equations in $\Omega$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- cor-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:residual_rep}
Let the uniqueness assumption, Assumption~\ref{assum:infsup}, hold, let
$(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v)\in H(\operatorname{div};\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)$, and set
$$
R_1:=A^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}+A\nabla v+{\bf b} v),\quad
R_2:=\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}+\mbox{\boldmath$\beta$}\cdot\nabla v+\mu v.
$$
Then $(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v)$ is the unique solution of \eqref{fos} with data $f_1=R_2$,
${\bf f}_2=-A^{\frac{1}{2}}R_1$, $\phi=\operatorname{tr}^{g}_{\Gamma_D}(v)$ and $\psi=\operatorname{tr}^{d}_{\Gamma_N}(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}\cdot{\bf n})$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual_stability}
\|v\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}\|_{H(\operatorname{div};\Omega)}
\ \le\ C_{S}\Big(\big(1+\Lambda_1^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\big(\|R_1\|_{0,\Omega}+\|R_2\|_{0,\Omega}\big)
+\|\operatorname{tr}^{g}_{\Gamma_D}(v)\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}+\|\operatorname{tr}^{d}_{\Gamma_N}(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}\cdot{\bf n})\|_{\Hm00(\Gamma_N)}\Big).
\end{equation}
<!-- end cor-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The data are admissible: $\operatorname{tr}^{g}_{\Gamma_D}(v)\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma_D)$ by
\eqref{eq:quotient_lift} and $\operatorname{tr}^{d}_{\Gamma_N}(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}\cdot{\bf n})\in\Hm00(\Gamma_N)$ by
\eqref{eq:hdiv_trace}.  Substitution shows that
$(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v)$ solves \eqref{fos} with these data, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:stability} gives \eqref{eq:residual_stability}.

<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:continuity}
There is a constant $C_2$ depending only on $\Lambda_0$, $\Lambda_1$,
$\|{\bf b}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}$, $\|\mbox{\boldmath$\beta$}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}$ and
$\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}$ with $J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v;0)\le C_2\,|\!|\!|(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v)|\!|\!|^{2}$ for all $(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v)\in\Sigma\times H^{1}(\Omega)$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The two volume terms are bounded by the triangle inequality and the bounds on
the coefficients, and the boundary terms of $J(\cdot;0)$ and $|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|$
coincide.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:inverseD}
There is $C$, depending only on shape regularity, $k$ and $d$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverseD}
\|g\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}\le C\Big(\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_D}h_F^{-1}\|g\|_{0,F}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}
\qquad\forall g\in W^{D}_{h}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $L_hg\in V_h$ take the Lagrange nodal values of $g$ at the nodes on
$\overline{\Gamma_D}$ and the value zero at all other nodes.  Then
$\operatorname{tr}^{g}_{\Gamma_D}(L_hg)=g$.  For a node $z$ on
a face $F\in{\mathcal{E}}_D$, $|g(z)|^{2}\le Ch_F^{1-d}\|g\|^{2}_{0,F}$, and on every
element $K$, $\|L_hg\|^{2}_{0,K}+h_K^{2}\|\nabla L_hg\|^{2}_{0,K}\le
Ch_K^{d}\sum_z|g(z)|^{2}$, the sum running over the nodes of $K$ on
$\overline{\Gamma_D}$.  Elements and faces sharing a node have comparable diameters
and the node patches overlap finitely, so
$\|L_hg\|^{2}_{1,\Omega}\le C\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_D}(h_F+h_F^{-1})\|g\|^{2}_{0,F}$, and
since $h_F\le1$ the right-hand side is at most
$2C\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_D}h_F^{-1}\|g\|^{2}_{0,F}$.  Finally,
$\|g\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}\le\|L_hg\|_{1,\Omega}$ by \eqref{eq:quotient_lift}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:threshold}
Let the uniqueness assumption, Assumption~\ref{assum:infsup}, hold.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normequiv}
C_1|\!|\!|(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v)|\!|\!|^{2}\ \le\ J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v;0)\ \le\ C_2|\!|\!|(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v)|\!|\!|^{2}
\qquad\forall(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v)\in\Sigma\times V^{D}_{\ast},
\end{equation}
with $C_1,C_2>0$ independent of the mesh size.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The upper bound is Lemma~\ref{lem:continuity}.  For the lower bound, the
functional is $J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v;0)=\|R_1\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|R_2\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}
+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_D}h_F^{-1}\|v\|_{0,F}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}\cdot{\bf n}\|_{0,\Gamma_N}^{2}$, with
$R_1,R_2$ as in Corollary~\ref{lem:residual_rep}.  Since
$\operatorname{tr}^{g}_{\Gamma_D}(v)\in W^{D}_{h}$, \eqref{eq:inverseD} and \eqref{eq:embedN}
bound the two boundary norms in \eqref{eq:residual_stability} by
$CJ(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v;0)^{1/2}$, so $\|v\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}\|_{H(\operatorname{div};\Omega)}\le
CJ(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v;0)^{1/2}$.  The boundary terms of $|\!|\!|(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v)|\!|\!|^{2}$ are
terms of $J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v;0)$.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:where_discrete}
The only discrete ingredient in Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold} is the space $V^{D}_{\ast}$ of \eqref{def:Vh}, whose
Dirichlet trace is restricted to $W^{D}_{h}$.  That restriction serves a single
purpose: to apply the inverse inequality \eqref{eq:inverseD}, which bounds the
fractional norm $\|\operatorname{tr}^{g}_{\Gamma_D}(v)\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}$ by the weighted $L^{2}$
penalty and holds on the finite-dimensional trace space.  On $\Gamma_N$ the
corresponding step is the embedding \eqref{eq:embedN}, valid for every
$q\in L^{2}(\Gamma_N)$, so neither the flux nor the interior values of the scalar
are restricted.  The functional itself is defined on $\Sigma\times H^{1}(\Omega)$; the
restriction to $V^{D}_{\ast}$ belongs to the proof of the lower bound.
<!-- end rem-1 -->
<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:aposteriori}
Let the uniqueness assumption, Assumption~\ref{assum:infsup}, hold, let the
data satisfy Assumption~\ref{assum:data}, let $(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$},u)$ be the solution of
\eqref{fos}, and let $Q_h$ satisfy \eqref{def:Qh}.
Then, for all $(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v)\in\Sigma\times V^{D}_{\ast}$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:reliability}
c\,[J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v;f_1,{\bf f}_2,\phi,\psi)^{1/2}+\|\phi-Q_h\phi\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}]
&\le|\!|\!|(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},u-v)|\!|\!|\\
\nonumber
&\le C\,[J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v;f_1,{\bf f}_2,\phi,\psi)^{1/2}+\|\phi-Q_h\phi\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}],
\end{align}
with constants $c,C>0$ depending on $C_Q$ but independent of the mesh size.
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $e:=(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},u-v)$ and $\eta:=J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v;f_1,{\bf f}_2,\phi,\psi)^{1/2}$.
Since $(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$},u)$ solves \eqref{fos} and the residuals are affine,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta_identity}
\eta^{2}=J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$},v-u;0),
\end{equation}
so $\eta\le C_2^{1/2}|\!|\!| e|\!|\!|$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:continuity}.  Since
$\operatorname{tr}^{g}_{\Gamma_D}(v)\in W^{D}_{h}$, $Q_h\phi-v=Q_h(\phi-v)\in W^{D}_{h}$, and
\eqref{eq:inverseD} and \eqref{def:Qh} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qh_discrete}
\|Q_h\phi-v\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}\le C\Big(\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_D}h_F^{-1}\|\phi-v\|^{2}_{0,F}\Big)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
For the upper bound we apply \eqref{eq:residual_stability} to $e$: by
\eqref{eq:eta_identity} its volume residuals are at most $\sqrt2\,\eta$, by
\eqref{eq:embedN} its Neumann trace $\psi-\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}\cdot{\bf n}$ is at most $C\eta$ in
$\Hm00(\Gamma_N)$, and by \eqref{eq:Qh_discrete} its Dirichlet trace $\phi-v$ is at
most $\|\phi-Q_h\phi\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}+C\eta$ in $H^{1/2}(\Gamma_D)$; the boundary terms
of $|\!|\!| e|\!|\!|^{2}$ are terms of $\eta^{2}$.  For the lower bound,
\eqref{eq:quotient_lift} and \eqref{eq:Qh_discrete} give
$\|\phi-Q_h\phi\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}\le\|\operatorname{tr}^{g}_{\Gamma_D}(u-v)\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}+\|Q_h\phi-v\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}\le
C|\!|\!| e|\!|\!|$.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- cor-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:best_approx}
Let the uniqueness assumption, Assumption~\ref{assum:infsup}, hold, let the
data satisfy Assumption~\ref{assum:data}, and let $(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$},u)$ be the solution
of \eqref{fos}.  Then, for all $(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v)\in\Sigma\times V^{D}_{\ast}$,
$$
c\,[J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v;f_1,{\bf f}_2,\phi,\psi)^{1/2}+\delta_h(\phi)]
\le|\!|\!|(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},u-v)|\!|\!|
\le C\,[J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},v;f_1,{\bf f}_2,\phi,\psi)^{1/2}+\delta_h(\phi)].
$$
<!-- end cor-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The upper bound follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:aposteriori} with $Q_h=S_h$ and
\eqref{eq:SZ_quasiopt}.  The lower bound follows from \eqref{eq:eta_identity}, Lemma~\ref{lem:continuity} and $\delta_h(\phi)\le\|\phi-v\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}\le\|u-v\|_{1,\Omega}\le|\!|\!|(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$},u-v)|\!|\!|$,
by \eqref{eq:quotient_lift}.

<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:pythagoras}
Let $\psi_h:=\Pi_k\psi$.  For every $\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h\in\Sigma_h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pythagoras}
\|\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h\cdot{\bf n}-\psi\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma_N}
=\|\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h\cdot{\bf n}-\psi_h\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma_N}+\|\psi-\psi_h\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma_N},
\end{equation}
so that, over $\Sigma_h\times V_h$, $J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h,v_h;f_1,{\bf f}_2,\phi,\psi) =J(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h,v_h;f_1,{\bf f}_2,\phi,\psi_h) +\|\psi-\psi_h\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma_N}$, the last term being independent of $(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h,v_h)$.  In particular the two
minimization problems have the same solution.
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
On each face $F\in{\mathcal{E}}_N$ we write
$\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h\cdot{\bf n}-\psi=(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h\cdot{\bf n}-\psi_h)+(\psi_h-\psi)$.  The first summand
belongs to $P_k(F)$ because $\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h\cdot{\bf n}|_F\in P_k(F)$, and the second is
$L^{2}(F)$-orthogonal to $P_k(F)$, so the cross term vanishes.  Summing over
the faces gives \eqref{eq:pythagoras}.

<!-- thm-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:apriori}
Let the uniqueness assumption, Assumption~\ref{assum:infsup}, hold, let the
data satisfy Assumption~\ref{assum:data}, and let $(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$},u)$ be the solution
of \eqref{fos} and $(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_h,u_h)$ the solution of \eqref{eq:ls_min}.  Let
$0<s\le k+1$, and assume $u\in H^{1+s}(\Omega)$, $\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\in H^{s}(\Omega)^{d}$ and
$\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\in H^{s}(\Omega)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:apriori}
\|u-u_h\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_h\|_{H(\operatorname{div};\Omega)}
\le C\,h_{\max}^{s}\big(|u|_{1+s,\Omega}+|\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}|_{s,\Omega}+|\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}|_{s,\Omega}\big),
\end{equation}
with $C$ independent of the mesh size.
<!-- end thm-4 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let $z_h:=(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_h,u_h)$, $z_h^{\ast}:=(\Pi^{RT}_h\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$},I_hu)\in\Sigma_h\times V_h$
and $J(\cdot):=J(\cdot;f_1,{\bf f}_2,\phi,\Pi_k\psi)$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:pythagoras}, $z_h$ minimizes $J$ over $\Sigma_h\times V_h$, so
$J(z_h-z_h^{\ast};0)^{1/2}\le J(z_h)^{1/2}+J(z_h^{\ast})^{1/2}\le2J(z_h^{\ast})^{1/2}$,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:apriori_step1}
\|u_h-I_hu\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_h-\Pi^{RT}_h\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\|_{H(\operatorname{div};\Omega)}
\le2\sqrt2\,C_1^{-1/2}J(z_h^{\ast})^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Since $(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$},u)$ solves \eqref{fos} and $\Pi^{RT}_h\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\cdot{\bf n}=\Pi_k\psi$
on $\Gamma_N$ by \eqref{eq:RT_moment}, the Neumann term of $J(z_h^{\ast})$
vanishes, its Dirichlet term is $\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_D}h_F^{-1}\|u-I_hu\|^{2}_{0,F}$,
and its volume terms are at most
$C\big(\|\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}-\Pi^{RT}_h\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\|_{H(\operatorname{div};\Omega)}+\|u-I_hu\|_{1,\Omega}\big)^{2}$.
By \eqref{eq:RT_approx} and \eqref{eq:SZ_approx},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:apriori_step2}
J(z_h^{\ast})^{1/2}
\le Ch_{\max}^{s}\big(|u|_{1+s,\Omega}+|\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}|_{s,\Omega}+|\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}|_{s,\Omega}\big),
\end{equation}
and the triangle inequality with \eqref{eq:RT_approx}, \eqref{eq:SZ_approx},
\eqref{eq:apriori_step1} and \eqref{eq:apriori_step2} gives \eqref{eq:apriori}.
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- rem-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:zero_defect}
The a priori estimate is stated in $H(\operatorname{div};\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)$, the norm of
the problem, and not in $|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|$.  The Dirichlet term of
$|\!|\!|(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_h,u-u_h)|\!|\!|^{2}$ is of the same order, by
\eqref{eq:SZ_approx}, \eqref{eq:apriori_step1} and \eqref{eq:apriori_step2},
but by Lemma~\ref{lem:pythagoras} the Neumann term is at least
$\|\psi-\Pi_k\psi\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma_N}$, which for $s>\frac12$ is in general only of
order $h_{\max}^{2s-1}$; $|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|$ is the norm of the a posteriori
estimate.  The unweighted $L^{2}(\Gamma_N)$ penalty, although stronger than the
natural $\Hm00(\Gamma_N)$ norm, enters the proof only through
$\|\Pi^{RT}_h\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\cdot{\bf n}-\Pi_k\psi\|_{0,\Gamma_N}$, which vanishes, so
\eqref{eq:apriori} is optimal.
<!-- end rem-2 -->
<!-- lem-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:oscD}
Let $Q_h$ satisfy \eqref{def:Qh}.  For every $\phi\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma_D)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oscD_bound}
\|\phi-Q_h\phi\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}
+\Big(\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_D}h_F^{-1}\|\phi-Q_h\phi\|^{2}_{0,F}\Big)^{1/2}
\le C\inf_{w_h\in W^{D}_{h}}\Big\{\|\phi-w_h\|_{1/2,\Gamma_D}
+\Big(\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_D}h_F^{-1}\|\phi-w_h\|^{2}_{0,F}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\},
\end{equation}
with $C$ depending only on $C_Q$, shape regularity, $k$ and $d$.
<!-- end lem-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $w_h\in W^{D}_{h}$.  Since $Q_hw_h=w_h$,
$\phi-Q_h\phi=(\phi-w_h)-Q_h(\phi-w_h)$ with $Q_h(\phi-w_h)\in W^{D}_{h}$.  By
\eqref{def:Qh}, the weighted $L^{2}$ norm of $Q_h(\phi-w_h)$ is at most $C_Q$
times that of $\phi-w_h$, and by \eqref{eq:inverseD} and \eqref{def:Qh} its
$H^{1/2}(\Gamma_D)$ norm is at most a constant times the same quantity.  The
triangle inequality gives \eqref{eq:oscD_bound}, since $w_h$ is arbitrary.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- cor-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:oscD}
Let the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:aposteriori} hold, and let

\label{rem:overpenalize}
Let the uniqueness assumption, Assumption~\ref{assum:infsup}, hold, let the
data satisfy Assumption~\ref{assum:data}, let the regularity of

By \eqref{eq:pythagoras_N} and \eqref{eq:pythagoras_D}, $z_h:=(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_h,u_h)$

<!-- rem-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:three_roles}
Three questions that are often discussed together are logically distinct.
\emph{(i) Stability asks for a lower threshold.}  For the present boundary
residuals the sharp thresholds are
$h_F^{-1}$ on $\Gamma_D$ and $1$ on $\Gamma_N$.  \emph{(ii) Approximation asks whether
the penalized defect is actually being approximated.}  If a compatible comparison annihilates that defect, its weight is irrelevant:
``large weight times zero is zero.''  Theorem~\ref{rem:overpenalize} proves this
for all $\alpha\ge1$ and $\beta\ge0$; on $\Gamma_D$ only the approximation of the
datum in $W^{D}_{h}$ remains.  The same mechanism is familiar in discontinuous
Galerkin methods.  In the weakly over-penalized symmetric interior-penalty
method of Brenner--Owens--Sung \cite{BOS:08}, the projected scalar jump is
penalized more strongly than in the standard SIP scaling, yet optimal energy
and $L^{2}$ convergence are retained.  \emph{(iii) Conditioning asks for the
algebraic price of that choice.}  A penalty may be harmless for convergence and still make the stiffness matrix
poorly scaled.
<!-- end rem-4 -->
<!-- rem-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:nn_penalty}
The mechanism is not specific to finite elements.  On $\Gamma_N$ the unweighted
$L^{2}$ penalty controls the natural norm of every square-integrable residual,
by \eqref{eq:embedN}, so the weight $1$ suffices for every trial class.  In
every least-squares minimization, a weight above the stability threshold can
be increased without changing the error bound whenever the trial class
contains an accurate comparison function that meets the boundary data, because
the penalty multiplies zero.  The boundary residuals of this paper can also be
used in the neural-network method FOSLS-deRhaNN \cite{Zhang:26}.

\label{lem:disc_companion}
There are linear maps ${\mathcal{C}}_h^u:V_h^{\rm b}\longrightarrow V_h$ and ${\mathcal{C}}_h^\sigma:\Sigma_h^{\rm b}\longrightarrow\Sigma_h$ with constants depending only on shape regularity, $k$, and $d$, such that
\begin{align}
\label{eq:scalar_companion}
 &\|v_h-{\mathcal{C}}_h^u v_h\|_{1,h}^2
 +\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_D}h_F^{-1}
   \|v_h-{\mathcal{C}}_h^u v_h\|_{0,F}^2
 \ \lesssim\
 \sum_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_I}h_F^{-1}\|[v_h]\|_{0,F}^2,\\
\label{eq:flux_companion}
 &\|\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h-{\mathcal{C}}_h^\sigma\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h\|_{H(\operatorname{div},h)}^2
 \ \lesssim\
 \sum_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_I}h_F^{-1}
 \|[\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h\cdot{\bf n}_F]\|_{0,F}^2,
\end{align}
and the flux companion preserves the normal trace on boundary faces,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flux_companion_boundary}
 ({\mathcal{C}}_h^\sigma\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h)\cdot{\bf n}
 =\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h\cdot{\bf n}
 \qquad\text{on every }F\subset\partial\Omega.
\end{equation}

\label{prop:disc_sharp}
Let $\Omega=(0,1)^{d}$, let $\Gamma_D$ be the union of the interiors of the faces

\end{equation}

\emph{The flux jump.}  Let $w$ be the continuous piecewise linear function on
$[0,1]$ with $w(0)=w(1)=0$ and slope $m_j:=\frac{M-1}{2}-j$ on
$(j/M,(j+1)/M)$, $j=0,\dots,M-1$; the slopes have mean zero, so $w(1)=0$
indeed.  Put $v_h(x):=w(x_1)$ and $\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h:=-\nabla v_h$.  The meshes resolve

modulus $|m_{i-1}-m_i|=1$ on the planes, each of area one.  Hence

$\|\nabla v_h\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}=\frac1M\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}m_j^{2}=\frac{M^{2}-1}{12}$.

\le C\omega_u h_{\max}\|\nabla g\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}$, whereas
$\|v_h\|_{0,\Omega}\to\|g\|_{0,\Omega}>0$.  Inserting $({\bf 0},v_h)$ into
\eqref{eq:disc_sharp} gives $\omega_u\ge c\,h_{\max}^{-1}$.

\label{thm:disc_apriori}
Let the uniqueness assumption, Assumption~\ref{assum:infsup}, hold, let the
data satisfy Assumption~\ref{assum:data}, let $(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$},u)$ be the solution of
\eqref{fos}, and assume the regularity of Theorem~\ref{thm:apriori} for some
$0<s\le k+1$.  Then

 \|u-u_h^{\rm d}\|_{1,h}
 +\|\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_h^{\rm d}\|_{H(\operatorname{div},h)}
  \le C\,h_{\max}^{s}

\end{equation}
with $C$ independent of the mesh size.

\label{rem:disc_overpen}
The interior weights $h_F^{-1}$ in \eqref{def:Jdisc} may be replaced by any
weights $\omega_{u,F},\omega_{\sigma,F}$ with
$\omega_{u,F},\omega_{\sigma,F}\ge c\,h_F^{-1}$ for a fixed $c>0$, for instance
$h_F^{-\rho}$ with $\rho\ge1$, provided the same weights are used in the norm
\eqref{def:normdisc}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:disc_normequiv} then holds with a lower
constant depending on $c$ only, because its proof uses the jump terms only
through the companion bounds of Lemma~\ref{lem:disc_companion}, which involve
$h_F^{-1}$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:disc_apriori} holds with the same right-hand side
and a constant depending on $c$ only, the same constant if $c\ge1$, because the comparison pair has zero interior jumps
by \eqref{eq:disc_zero_jumps}, so the weights multiply zero.  Theorem~\ref{thm:disc_apost} holds with the
same weights in $\eta_{\rm d}$ and in the norm, by the same argument.
Over-penalization of the interior jumps therefore leaves the convergence rate
unchanged.

\label{rem:disc_weaker}
Let the interior weights $h_F^{-1}$ in \eqref{def:Jdisc} and
\eqref{def:normdisc} be replaced by weights $\omega_{u,F},\omega_{\sigma,F}>0$,
and put
$\kappa_h:=\min_{F\in{\mathcal{E}}_I}\min\{\omega_{u,F},\omega_{\sigma,F}\}\,h_F$.  The
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:disc_apriori} uses the norm equivalence only after
the bound
$$
J_{\rm dis}(z_h-z_h^\ast;0)^{1/2}\le2J_{\rm dis}(z_h^\ast;f_1,{\bf f}_2,\phi,\Pi_k\psi)^{1/2}
\le Ch_{\max}^{s}\big(|u|_{1+s,\Omega}+|\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}|_{s,\Omega}+|\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}|_{s,\Omega}\big),
$$
whose right-hand side does not depend on the interior weights, because
$z_h^\ast$ has no jumps.  Hence $z_h-z_h^\ast$ is of order $h_{\max}^{s}$ in the
norm induced by the functional, for every choice of interior weights.  The norm
equivalence is needed to pass from this norm to the norm of
\eqref{eq:disc_apriori}.

If $\kappa_h\le1$, every interior term of the functional is at least $\kappa_h$
times the corresponding term of \eqref{def:Jdisc}, so the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:disc_normequiv} holds with the constant $\kappa_hC_{\rm d}$ for the
broken volume norms.  The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:disc_apriori} then gives
\eqref{eq:disc_apriori} with its right-hand side multiplied by
$1+\kappa_h^{-1/2}$.  For $\omega_{u,F}=\omega_{\sigma,F}=h_F^{-\rho}$ with
$\rho<1$ on quasi-uniform meshes, $\kappa_h\simeq h_{\max}^{1-\rho}$ and the
resulting bound is of order $h_{\max}^{s-(1-\rho)/2}$; for constant weights it
is of order $h_{\max}^{s-1/2}$.  In the setting of
Proposition~\ref{prop:disc_sharp} the lower constant of the norm equivalence is
indeed of order $\kappa_h$ for constant weights, so the factor cannot be removed
from the norm equivalence.

With a constant weight $\omega_\sigma$, the upper bound of
\eqref{eq:disc_apost} fails for the pair $(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h,v_h)$ in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:disc_sharp}.  For the data $f_1=0$,
${\bf f}_2={\bf 0}$, $\phi=0$ and $\psi=0$ the exact solution is zero, the error of
$(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h,v_h)$ is $(\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_h,v_h)$ itself, and
$\eta_{\rm d}^{2}=\omega_\sigma(M-1)$ is at most $6\omega_\sigma/(M+1)$ times
the square of the middle term of \eqref{eq:disc_apost}.  We therefore use the
weight $h_F^{-1}$: it is the weakest weight for which the norm equivalence
holds, and with it the a priori estimate \eqref{eq:disc_apriori} holds without
a mesh-dependent factor and the error equivalence \eqref{eq:disc_apost}
holds for every discrete pair.

<!-- rem-8: body_md -->
\label{rem:cr_clean}
The Crouzeix--Raviart space carries the built-in continuity
$\int_F[v_h]\,ds=0$, $F\in{\mathcal{E}}_I$, which the standard Crouzeix--Raviart method
uses in place of a jump penalty.  The least-squares method of
\cite{LiangZhang:23}, with $H(\operatorname{div})$-conforming fluxes and Crouzeix--Raviart
scalars, was designed in the same spirit: its functional contains only the
element-wise residuals.  For general, possibly indefinite, equations its
analysis uses discrete stability for sufficiently fine meshes
\cite{LiangZhang:23}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:disc_normequiv} concerns completely
unconstrained broken spaces, while the Crouzeix--Raviart space carries a
face-moment constraint, so its zero-penalty stability is a different endpoint.
