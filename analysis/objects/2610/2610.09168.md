<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:tdesign}
Let ${\cal B}=\{B_1,\ldots,B_B\}$ be a
$t$-$(N,n,\lambda_t)$ design. Consider the sampling plan that selects
one block uniformly from ${\cal B}$ and takes all units in the selected
block as the sample. Then, for every $s=1,\ldots,t$ and every set of
$s$ distinct units $i_1,\ldots,i_s$,
\[
\pi_{i_1\cdots i_s}
=
P(i_1,\ldots,i_s\in S)
=
\frac{n^{(s)}}{N^{(s)}}
=
\frac{\binom ns}{\binom Ns},
\]
where
\[
a^{(s)}=a(a-1)\cdots(a-s+1)
\]
denotes the falling factorial. Consequently, all inclusion
probabilities through order $t$ are identical to those under SRSWOR
of size $n$ from a population of size $N$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix $s\leq t$ and a set $C$ of $s$ distinct population units.
Let $\lambda_s$ denote the number of blocks containing $C$.
The defining property of a $t$-design implies that $\lambda_s$ does
not depend on the particular choice of $C$.

Count pairs $(C',B_j)$ for which $C'$ is an $s$-subset of the
population and $C'\subset B_j$. Counting first over the $B$ blocks
gives
\[
B\binom ns.
\]
On the other hand, there are $\binom Ns$ possible choices of $C'$,
each contained in exactly $\lambda_s$ blocks. Hence
\[
B\binom ns
=
\lambda_s\binom Ns,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{\lambda_s}{B}
=
\frac{\binom ns}{\binom Ns}.
\]
Since a block is selected uniformly,
\[
\pi_{i_1\cdots i_s}
=
\frac{\lambda_s}{B}
=
\frac{\binom ns}{\binom Ns},
\]
which is precisely the corresponding inclusion probability under
SRSWOR.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:counterexample}
There exists a sequence of finite populations ${\cal P}_N$ with
\[
N=q^2+q+1,\qquad n=q+1,\qquad q\to\infty,
\]
and, on each population, a projective-plane BIBD sampling plan and
SRSWOR of size $n$, such that the following properties hold.

\begin{enumerate}
\item
The two sampling plans have exactly the same first- and second-order
inclusion probabilities:
\[
\pi_i=\frac{n}{N},
\qquad
\pi_{ij}=\frac{n(n-1)}{N(N-1)},\qquad i\ne j.
\]

\item
Under the BIBD sampling plan, the sample total $T_{\rm BIBD}$
converges in distribution to a centered standard exponential random
variable:
\[
T_{\rm BIBD}\xrightarrow{d}E-1,
\qquad E\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1).
\]

\item
Under SRSWOR, the sample total $T_{\rm SRS}$ is asymptotically
standard normal:
\[
T_{\rm SRS}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1).
\]
\end{enumerate}

In particular, the two sample totals have exactly the same expectation
and variance for every $q$, while their limiting distributions are
different.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For each $q$, let $\mbox{\bf w}$ be the centered exponential-quantile vector
defined above and choose a permutation $\tau_q$ satisfying
\[
\max_{i\in[N]}|y_{i\tau_q}|=o(1).
\]
Define the finite population by
\[
\mbox{\bf y}_q=\frac1qA^\top\tau_q(\mbox{\bf w}).
\]

By construction,
\[
A\mbox{\bf y}_q=\tau_q(\mbox{\bf w}).
\]
Since the BIBD sampling plan selects each of its $N$ blocks with
probability $1/N$, the distribution of its sample total is the
empirical distribution of the coordinates of $\tau_q(\mbox{\bf w})$, which
is identical to that of $\mbox{\bf w}$. The empirical distribution of
$\mbox{\bf w}$ converges to the distribution of $E-1$, where
$E\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1)$. Hence
\[
T_{\rm BIBD}\xrightarrow{d}E-1.
\]

The population is centered, and
\[
\|\mbox{\bf y}_q\|^2
=
\frac1q\|\mbox{\bf w}\|^2.
\]
Since
\[
\frac1N\|\mbox{\bf w}\|^2\longrightarrow1,
\]
we have
\[
\frac1N\|\mbox{\bf y}_q\|^2
=
\frac{1}{qN}\|\mbox{\bf w}\|^2
\sim\frac1q.
\]
The variance of the SRSWOR sample total is therefore
\[
\begin{split}
\mbox{\sc var}(T_{\rm SRS})
&=
n^2\left(\frac1n-\frac1N\right)V_N^2\\
&=
n\left(1-\frac nN\right)V_N^2,
\end{split}
\]
where
\[
V_N^2=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^Ny_i^2.
\]
Using $n=q+1$, $N=q^2+q+1$, and
$N^{-1}\|\mbox{\bf w}\|^2\to1$, it follows that
\[
\mbox{\sc var}(T_{\rm SRS})\longrightarrow1.
\]

Moreover, the permutation $\tau_q$ was chosen so that
\[
\max_{i\in[N]}|y_{i\tau_q}|=o(1).
\]
Together with the preceding variance calculation, this satisfies the
finite-population Lindeberg condition for SRSWOR. Hence
\[
\frac{T_{\rm SRS}}
{\{\mbox{\sc var}(T_{\rm SRS})\}^{1/2}}
\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1).
\]
Since $\mbox{\sc var}(T_{\rm SRS})\to1$, Slutsky's theorem gives
\[
T_{\rm SRS}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1).
\]

Finally, the two sampling plans have identical first- and second-order
inclusion probabilities by Theorem~\ref{thm:tdesign}. Therefore,
for the same finite population, their sample totals have exactly the
same expectation and variance for every $q$. This completes the
proof.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
