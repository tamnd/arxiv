<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:optimal_LSIB_scalar}
    A linear encoder \(T=kX+N\) with $N \sim S\alphaS(1)$ independent of $X$ and 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:optimal_LSIB_scalar}
        |k|=\begin{cases}
            0, & \beta<\beta^c,\\\displaystyle \frac1{\gamma_X}\left(\frac{\beta\rho^\alpha-1}{1-\rho^\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}, & \beta\ge\beta^c,
        \end{cases}\quad \beta^c:=\rho^{-\alpha} > 1,
    \end{equation}
     is an optimal encoder for the LSIB.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Consider $T = k X + N$ with \(N \sim S\alphaS(\gamma_N)\), and define $Z \sim S\alphaS(1)$. Then, the mutual information quantities are given by
    \begin{align}
         &I(X;T)\nonumber\\
         =\,&h(T)-h(T\,|\,X) \nonumber\\
         =\,&h(kX+N)-h(N)\nonumber\\
=\,&h(Z)+\frac1\alpha\log\left(|k|^\alpha\gamma_X^\alpha+\gamma_N^\alpha\right)-h(Z)-\log(\gamma_N) \label{eq:stabpropuse}\\
=\,&\frac1\alpha\log(1+\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}),\label{eq:compression_rate_scalar}
\end{align}
and
\begin{align}
        &I(Y;T)\nonumber\\
        =\,&h(T)-h(T\,|\,Y)\nonumber\\
        =\,&h(kX+N)-h(kA+N)\nonumber\\
        =\,&h(Z)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(|k|^\alpha\gamma_X^\alpha+\gamma_N^\alpha\right)-h(Z)\nonumber\\
        &\qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad -\frac{1}{\alpha}\log(|k|^\alpha\gamma_A^\alpha+\gamma_N^\alpha)\label{eq:stabpropuse1}\\
=\,&\frac1\alpha\log\left(\frac{1+|k|^\alpha\gamma_X^\alpha\gamma_N^{-\alpha}}{1+|k|^\alpha\gamma_X^\alpha\gamma_N^{-\alpha}(1-\gamma_Y^\alpha\gamma_X^{-\alpha})}\right)\nonumber\\
        =\,&\frac1\alpha\log\left(\frac{1+\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}}{1+\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}(1-\rho^\alpha)}\right),\label{eq:relevant_information_scalar}
    \end{align}
    where we used equation~\eqref{eq:stabilityproperty} to write equations~\eqref{eq:stabpropuse} and~\eqref{eq:stabpropuse1}. Since equations~\eqref{eq:compression_rate_scalar} and~\eqref{eq:relevant_information_scalar} are given in terms of \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}\), we minimize \(\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}):=\mathcal L(T)=I(X;T)-\beta I(Y;T)\) over \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}\ge0\)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
    by taking the first derivative 
and setting it to zero 
\begin{align}
\frac{1-\beta}{1+\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}}
+\frac{\beta(1-\rho^\alpha)}
{1+\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}(1-\rho^\alpha)}
&=0, \nonumber\\
\frac{1+\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}}
{1+\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}(1-\rho^\alpha)}
&=\frac{\beta-1}
{\beta(1-\rho^\alpha)}.
\label{eq:stationary}
\end{align}
Solving for \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}\) yields
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}^*
=\frac{\beta\rho^\alpha-1}
{1-\rho^\alpha},
\label{eq:stationary_snr}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}^*\ge0\) denotes the point satisfying the
stationary equation whenever such a point exists.

To determine when a stationary point exists, define the left-hand
side of~\eqref{eq:stationary} as \(R(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha})\),
   
          
              
                         
   
 which is strictly increasing over
\(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}\ge0\), with range
\(\mathcal D=[1,(1-\rho^\alpha)^{-1})\). Hence, whenever a
solution to~\eqref{eq:stationary} exists, it is unique.

When \(\beta< \beta^c \, (=\rho^{-\alpha})\), the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:stationary} satisfies
\(
\tfrac{\beta-1}{\beta(1-\rho^\alpha)}
\notin\mathcal D.
\)
Thus, no stationary point exists. In this case,
\(d\mathcal L/d\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}>0\) for all \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}\ge0\), and therefore
\(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}=0\) is the unique global minimizer.

When \(\beta\ge\beta^c\), the value \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}^*\) in
\eqref{eq:stationary_snr} is a stationary point. To determine
its optimality, rewrite the derivative as
\begin{equation*}
\frac{d\mathcal L}{d\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}}
= \frac{1}{\alpha}
\frac{1-\beta\rho^\alpha
+\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}(1-\rho^\alpha)}
{(1+\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha})\bigl[1+\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}(1-\rho^\alpha)\bigr]}.
\end{equation*}

The denominator is positive for \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}\ge0\), while the numerator
is strictly increasing in \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}\) and vanishes uniquely at
\(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}=\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}^*\). Hence, for \(\beta>\beta^c\), \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}^*\) is the unique global minimizer.
   
                            
                                 
      
                            
                             
   
                                                       
At
\(\beta=\beta^c\), we have \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}^*=0\), and the derivative is
strictly positive for every \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}>0\), so \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}=0\) is again
the unique global minimizer.

Finally, one can achieve the optimal \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}\) by choosing \(\gamma_N=1\) and the appropriate value of \(k\). 
    
We point out that \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}^*\) is a strictly increasing function of \(\beta\) when \(\beta\ge\beta^c\), which is expected: the more weight we give to relevant information preservation as opposed to information compression, the more \(\mathrm{snr}_{\alpha}\) we require to preserve the relevant information.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{th:suboptimal}
    Consider $(X,Y)$ two jointly $\alpha$-stable RVs as in~\eqref{eq:channelmodel}. When \(\beta>\beta^c=\rho^{-\alpha}\), any linear encoder \(T=kX+N\) with \(k\neq0\) and \(N\simS\alphaS(\gamma_N)\) independent of \(X\) is strictly suboptimal for the general IB problem, i.e. it cannot achieve \(\min_{p_{T|X}}I(X;T)-\beta I(Y;T)\).
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $X$ is symmetric, we assume, Without Loss of Generality (WLoG), \(k>0\). An optimal encoder with conditional law \(p_{T|X}\) for the general IB problem satisfies the stationary condition given in~\cite{tishby2000informationbottleneckmethod}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selfconsistent}
      p_{T|X}(t| x)=\frac{p_T(t)}{Z(x,\beta)}\exp\!\left\{\!-\beta
      D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{Y|X}(y|x)\,\|\,p_{Y|T}(y| t))\right\},
    \end{equation}
    where $Z(x,\beta)$ is the normalization term. Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that the linear encoder \(T=kX+N\) is optimal, and satisfies~\eqref{eq:selfconsistent}. Define for any \(x_1\neq x_2\), the function
    \begin{align}
        \xi(t)&:=\log p_N(t-kx_1)-\log p_N(t-kx_2) -\beta\int_\mathbb R\Delta(y) \log p_{Y|T}(y|t)\,dy, \label{eq:Xi}
    \end{align}
    where
    \begin{eqnarray*}
    \Delta(y)&:=&p_{Y|X}(y|x_1)-p_{Y|X}(y|x_2)\\
&=&p_Y(y)\left[\frac{p_A(x_1-y)}{p_X(x_1)}-\frac{p_A(x_2-y)}{p_X(x_2)}\right].
    \end{eqnarray*}
    
We show in Lemma~\ref{lem:stationarity} in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, that if the linear encoder satisfies~\eqref{eq:selfconsistent}, then 
the function $\xi_{\mathrm{lin,opt}}(t)$ must be constant in $t$, and hence \(\xi'_{\mathrm{lin,opt}}(t)=0\) for all \(t\). However, this is contradicted by Lemma~\ref{lem:stationarity-asymptotics} in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} which yields the following asymptotic result for the linear encoder
    \begin{equation}
    \lim_{t\to\infty}t^{2}\xi_{\mathrm{lin}}'(t)
                                                                              
      =(1+\alpha)k(x_1-x_2)\bigl[\beta\rho^\alpha-1\bigr]\nonumber
    \end{equation}
    which is nonzero since \(\alpha, k >0\), \(x_1\neq x_2\), and \(\beta>\beta^c=\rho^{-\alpha}\). Therefore, the linear encoder fails the necessary condition for optimality among all channels \(p_{T|X}(t|x)\) and is therefore strictly suboptimal.\footnote{Note that, for the Gaussian case, \(\xi_{\mathrm{lin}}'(t)=k(x_1-x_2)[1/\sigma_N^2-\beta\rho^2/\sigma_M^2]\) where \(\sigma_M^2:=k^2\sigma_N^2+\sigma_A^2\). Using the optimal linear encoder of Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal_LSIB_scalar}, \(\sigma_M^2=2\beta\rho^2\) and \(\sigma_N^2=2\gamma_N^2=2\), so \(\xi'_{\rm lin}(t)=0\) for all \(t\) and thus satisfies the necessary condition for optimality.}

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:gap_bound}
    At a fixed compression rate \(I(X;T)=R\), the relevant information loss $\ell(R)$ due to the optimal stable linear encoder is upperbounded by
    \begin{equation*}
        \ell(R) \leq \frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left[\frac{1+s(1-\rho^\alpha)}{(1-\rho^\alpha)(1+s)}\right]
    \end{equation*}
    where \(s:=e^{\alpha R}-1\).

<!-- property-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:series_expansion}
The density of \(X\simS\alphaS(\gamma)\) admits the following asymptotic series expansion (\(x\to+\infty\)) (see \cite{NolanJohnP2020USDM},\cite{zolotarev_one-dimensional_1986}):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:expansion}
p_X(x)\sim\frac1\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n!}\Gamma(n\alpha+1)\sin\bigl(\tfrac{n\pi\alpha}{2}\bigr)\gamma^{n\alpha}x^{-n\alpha-1}.
\end{equation}
                                                                                                               
                 
                                                                                                                                         
               
The asymptotic series expansion \((x\to+\infty)\) of the \(k^{\rm th}\) derivative of the density, $k \geq 1$, is
\begin{equation*}
p_X^{(k)}(x)\sim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_{k,n}x^{-n\alpha-k-1},
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
c_{k,n}:=\frac{(-1)^{k}}\pi\frac{(-1)^{n+1}\gamma^{n\alpha}}{n!}\,\Gamma(n\alpha+k+1)\,\sin\!\Big(\frac{n\pi\alpha}{2}\Big).
\end{equation*}
<!-- end property-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The expansion in~\eqref{eq:expansion} is obtained from the Fourier representation of the density function given in equation~\eqref{eq:charsym}
\begin{align*}
p_X(x)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-\gamma^\alpha|\theta|^\alpha}e^{-i\theta x}\,d\theta\\
&=\frac1{\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-\gamma^\alpha \theta^\alpha} \cos(\theta x)\,d\theta.
\end{align*}

Differentiating the Fourier representation in~\eqref{eq:charsym}
under the integral sign $k$ times, which is justified by dominated
convergence, yields
\begin{align}
p_X^{(k)}(x)
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
(-i\theta)^k e^{-\gamma^\alpha|\theta|^\alpha}
e^{-i\theta x}\,d\theta \nonumber\\
&=\frac{1}{\pi}\Re\left\{
i^k\int_0^\infty
r^k e^{-\gamma^\alpha r^\alpha}e^{irx}\,dr
\right\}.
\label{eq:pk-fourier}
\end{align}

Let
\[
f(z):=z^k e^{-\gamma^\alpha z^\alpha}.
\]
Fix
\[
0<\psi_0<
\min\left\{\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}\right\},
\]
and take $z^\alpha$ on its principal branch. Then $f$ is analytic in
$0<\arg z<\psi_0$ and continuous up to the boundary away from the
origin. For $z=Re^{i\psi}$, $0\le\psi\le\psi_0$, and $x>0$,
\begin{align}
\left|e^{ixz}\right|
&=e^{-xR\sin\psi}\le 1,
\label{eq:bd1}\\
|f(z)|
&=R^k e^{-\gamma^\alpha R^\alpha\cos(\alpha\psi)}
\nonumber\\
&\le
R^k e^{-\gamma^\alpha R^\alpha\cos(\alpha\psi_0)},
\label{eq:bd2}
\end{align}
where $\cos(\alpha\psi_0)>0$.

To account for the branch point at the origin when $\alpha$ is
noninteger, consider first the truncated sector contour consisting of
the segment $[\varepsilon,R]$, the arc
$\{Re^{i\psi}:0\le\psi\le\psi_0\}$, the ray from
$Re^{i\psi_0}$ to $\varepsilon e^{i\psi_0}$, and the inner arc
$\{\varepsilon e^{i\psi}:0\le\psi\le\psi_0\}$. By Cauchy's integral
theorem,
\[
\oint f(z)e^{ixz}\,dz=0.
\]
Using~\eqref{eq:bd1}--\eqref{eq:bd2}, the contribution of the outer
arc satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\left|
\int_0^{\psi_0}
iRe^{i\psi}f(Re^{i\psi})
e^{ixRe^{i\psi}}\,d\psi
\right|
\le
\psi_0 R^{k+1}
e^{-\gamma^\alpha R^\alpha\cos(\alpha\psi_0)}
\to0
\end{equation*}
as $R\to\infty$. The contribution of the inner arc is
$O(\varepsilon^{k+1})$ and therefore vanishes as
$\varepsilon\to0$. Hence,
\begin{equation}
\int_0^\infty f(r)e^{ixr}\,dr
=
e^{i\psi_0}
\int_0^\infty
f(re^{i\psi_0})
e^{ixre^{i\psi_0}}\,dr.
\label{eq:RHS}
\end{equation}

We next evaluate the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:RHS} using the
generalized Watson lemma~\cite{Wyman1964Laplace}, \cite{wong_asymptotic_1989}. Around the origin,
\[
f(r)
=
\sum_{n=0}^\infty
\frac{(-1)^n\gamma^{n\alpha}}{n!}
r^{n\alpha+k}
=
\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n r^{\lambda_n-1},
\]
where
\[
a_n:=\frac{(-1)^n\gamma^{n\alpha}}{n!},
\qquad
\lambda_n:=n\alpha+k+1.
\]
Along the rotated ray,
\[
e^{i\psi_0}f(re^{i\psi_0})
=
\sum_{n=0}^\infty
a_n e^{i\lambda_n\psi_0}r^{\lambda_n-1}.
\]
Moreover,
\(
e^{ixre^{i\psi_0}}
=
e^{-z r}\), where \(z:=-ixe^{i\psi_0}\),
and
\[
|\arg z|
=
\frac{\pi}{2}-\psi_0<\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
The conditions of the generalized Watson lemma therefore hold, and
as $x\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
\int_0^\infty f(r)e^{ixr}\,dr
&\sim
\sum_{n=0}^\infty
a_n e^{i\lambda_n\psi_0}
\Gamma(\lambda_n)z^{-\lambda_n}
\nonumber\\
&=
\sum_{n=0}^\infty
a_n\Gamma(\lambda_n)
x^{-\lambda_n}e^{i\lambda_n\pi/2}.
\label{eq:watson-k}
\end{align}

Substituting~\eqref{eq:watson-k} into~\eqref{eq:pk-fourier} gives
\[
p_X^{(k)}(x)
\sim
\frac{1}{\pi}
\sum_{n=0}^\infty
a_n\Gamma(\lambda_n)x^{-\lambda_n}
\Re\left\{
i^k e^{i\lambda_n\pi/2}
\right\}.
\]
Since
\begin{align*}
i^k e^{i\lambda_n\pi/2}
&=
e^{ik\pi/2}
e^{i(n\alpha+k+1)\pi/2}\\
&=
(-1)^k i e^{in\alpha\pi/2},
\end{align*}
we have
\[
\Re\left\{
i^k e^{i\lambda_n\pi/2}
\right\}
=
(-1)^{k+1}
\sin\left(\frac{n\pi\alpha}{2}\right).
\]
The $n=0$ term therefore vanishes, and
\begin{equation*}
p_X^{(k)}(x) \sim
\!\sum_{n=1}^\infty
\frac{(-1)^{n+k+1}\gamma^{n\alpha}}{\pi n!}
\Gamma(n\alpha+k+1)
\sin\left(\frac{n\pi\alpha}{2}\right)
x^{-n\alpha-k-1},
\end{equation*}
as $x\to+\infty$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:score}
    Let \(X\simS\alphaS(\gamma)\). Define the \emph{score} function for the density of \(X\) as \(s_X(u):=\frac{d}{du}\log p_X(u)=\frac{p_X'(u)}{p_X(u)}\).
    The function \(\zeta_X(u):=s_X(u)+\frac{1+\alpha}{u}\) satisfies, for \(|u|\ge1\),
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi-bounds}
\begin{aligned}
|\zeta_X(u)|
&\le C_X |u|^{-1-\alpha}, \qquad
|\zeta_X'(u)|
\le C_X |u|^{-2-\alpha},\\
|\zeta_X''(u)|
&\le C_X |u|^{-3-\alpha},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
for some \(C_X<\infty\). Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-exp}
\begin{aligned}
s_X(u)
&=-\frac{1+\alpha}{u}
+O(|u|^{-1-\alpha}),\\
s_X'(u)
&=\frac{1+\alpha}{u^2}
+O(|u|^{-2-\alpha}),\\
s_X''(u)
&=-\frac{2(1+\alpha)}{u^3}
+O(|u|^{-3-\alpha}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
    Moreover, \(\sup_u|s_X(u)|<\infty\) and \(|s_X(u)|\le C/|u|\) for \(|u|\ge1\).

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
From Property~\ref{prop:series_expansion},
    \begin{equation*}
        p_X(u)=c_{0,1}u^{-\alpha-1}(1+r_0(u))
    \end{equation*}
    where \(r_0(u):=O(u^{-\alpha})\) as \(u\to\infty\), and 
    \begin{equation*}
        p_X^{(k)}(u)=c_{k,1}u^{-\alpha-k-1}(1+r_k(u))
    \end{equation*}
    where \(r_k(u)=O(u^{-\alpha})\) as \(u\to\infty\). Therefore, the score function
    \begin{align*}
        s_X(u)=\frac{p_X'(u)}{p_X(u)}&=\frac{c_{1,1}}{c_{0,1}}\,u^{-1}\cdot\frac{1+r_1}{1+r_0}\\
        &=-\frac{1+\alpha}{u}(1+O(u^{-\alpha}))\\
        &=-\frac{1+\alpha}u+O(u^{-1-\alpha}).
    \end{align*}
    For the derivative of the score function, first note that
    \begin{align*}
        \frac{p^{(k)}(u)}{p(u)}&=\frac{c_{k,1}}{c_{0,1}}\,u^{-k}\cdot\frac{1+r_k}{1+r_0}\\
        &=(-1)^k\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}(\ell+\alpha)\,u^{-k}(1+O(u^{-\alpha}))\\
        &=(-1)^k\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}(\ell+\alpha)\,u^{-k}+O(u^{-k-\alpha}).
    \end{align*}
    Consequently,
    \begin{align*}
        s_X'(u)&=\frac{p_X''(u)}{p_X(u)}-s_X^2(u)\\
        &=\frac{(2+\alpha)(1+\alpha)}{u^2}-\frac{(1+\alpha)^2}{u^2}+O(u^{-2-\alpha})\\
        &=\frac{1+\alpha}{u^2}+O(u^{-2-\alpha})
    \end{align*}
    and similarly
    \begin{align*}
        s_X''(u)&=\frac{p^{(3)}(u)}{p(u)}-3\frac{p''(u)}{p(u)}s_X(u)+2s_X^3(u)\\
        &=-\frac{2(1+\alpha)}{u^3}+O(u^{-3-\alpha}).
    \end{align*}
    Therefore, \(\zeta_X(u)=s_X(u)+\frac{1+\alpha}{u}=O(u^{-1-\alpha})\), \(\zeta_X'(u)=s_X'(u)-\frac{1+\alpha}{u^2}=O(u^{-2-\alpha})\), and \(\zeta_X''(u)=s_X''(u)+\frac{2(1+\alpha)}{u^3}=O(u^{-3-\alpha})\). The \(O\)-bounds hold for sufficiently large \(u> u_0\). Over the interval \([1,u_0]\), positivity and smoothness of \(p_X(\cdot)\) makes \(s_X(u)=\tfrac{p_X'(u)}{p_X(u)}\in C^\infty\). So, \(\zeta_X^{(k)}(u)\), for all $k \ge 0$, is continuous, and therefore bounded on $|u| \geq 1$. For \(u<-1\), it also holds true since \(\zeta_X^{(k)}(u)\) is either an odd or even function, so \(|\zeta_X^{(k)}(u)|=|\zeta_X^{(k)}(-u)|\). Finally,
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $|s_X(u)|\le \tfrac{C}{|u|}$ for $|u|\ge1$: from $|s_X|\le\frac{1+\alpha}{|u|}+C_X|u|^{-1-\alpha}\le\frac{1+\alpha+C_X}{|u|}$ on $|u|\ge1$.
        \item $\sup_u|s_X|<\infty$: bounded by the previous estimate for $|u|\ge1$, and by continuity on $[-1,1]$.
    \end{itemize}

The function \(\Delta(y)\)  satisfies
\begin{align}
\textnormal{(P1)}\quad&
\int_{\mathbb R}\Delta(y)\,dy=0,
\label{eq:P1}\\
\textnormal{(P2)}\quad&
\int_{\mathbb R}y\Delta(y)\,dy
=\rho^\alpha(x_1-x_2).
\label{eq:P2}
\end{align}
Moreover, for some
\(C=C(x_1,x_2,\alpha,\gamma_Y,\gamma_A)\),

\label{eq:P3}
\end{equation}

\begin{align}
\int_{|y|>R}|\Delta(y)|\,dy
&=O(R^{-1-2\alpha}), \label{eq:tail0}\\
\int_{|y|>R}|y||\Delta(y)|\,dy
&=O(R^{-2\alpha}). \label{eq:tail1}
\end{align}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:stationarity}
Let \(M:= T - k Y = kA+N\) be the effective noise in the induced channel \(Y\to T\) for the optimal stable linear encoder. For \(\beta>0\), suppose that
\(p_{T|X}(t|x)\) achieves the optimum IB and satisfies
\eqref{eq:selfconsistent}. Then, for every \(x_1\neq x_2\),
the function \(\xi(t)\) in~\eqref{eq:Xi} is constant in \(t\),
and
\begin{equation*}
\xi_{\mathrm{lin,opt}}'(t) \equiv 0,
\end{equation*}
where 
\begin{equation}
\xi_{\mathrm{lin}}'(t)=s_N(t-kx_1)-s_N(t-kx_2) -\beta\!\int_{\mathbb R}
\Delta(y)s_M(t-ky)\,dy. \label{eq:Xiprime}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Taking logarithms in~\eqref{eq:selfconsistent} and expanding
the divergence gives
\begin{align}
&\log p_{T|X}(t|x)\nonumber\\
&=\log p_T(t)
+\beta\!\int p_{Y|X}(y|x)
   \log p_{Y|T}(y|t)\,dy
\nonumber\\
&-\beta\!\int p_{Y|X}(y|x)
   \log p_{Y|X}(y|x)\,dy
-\log Z(x,\beta).
\label{eq:IB-log}
\end{align}
The last two terms are independent of \(t\).

The first integral is absolutely convergent since at \(|y|\to\infty\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log p_{Y|T}(y|t)
&=\log p_Y(y)+\log p_M(t-ky)-\log p_T(t)\\
&=O(\log|y|),
\end{aligned}
\]
while
\(p_{Y|X}(y|x)= \frac{p_A(x-y)p_Y(y)}{p_x(x)} = O(|y|^{-2-2\alpha})\).

Evaluating~\eqref{eq:IB-log} at \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) and
subtracting shows that \(\xi(t)\) given in equation~\eqref{eq:Xi} is constant, since
\(p_{T|X}(t|x_i)=p_N(t-kx_i)\), for $i = 1,2$. Furthermore,
\(
\partial_t\log p_{Y|T}(y|t)
=s_M(t-ky) - s_T(t),
\)
where $s_M(\cdot)$ and $s_T(\cdot)$ are the score functions of $p_M$ and $p_T$ respectively. Differentiation under the integral sign follows by dominated
convergence because \(\sup_u|s_M(u)|<\infty\) and
\(\Delta\in L^1(\mathbb R)\) by~\eqref{eq:P3}.
The \(s_T(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}} \Delta(y) \, dy\) term vanishes by~\eqref{eq:P1}, while
\(\partial_t\log p_N(t-kx_i)=s_N(t-kx_i)\).
This proves~\eqref{eq:Xiprime}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:stationarity-asymptotics}

For the linear encoder $T = kX + N$ given in Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal_LSIB_scalar}, the function $\xi(t)$ given in equation~\eqref{eq:Xi} satisfies
    \begin{equation}
    \lim_{t\to\infty}t^{2}\xi_{\mathrm{lin}}'(t)
                                                                              
      =(1+\alpha)k(x_1-x_2)\bigl[\beta\rho^\alpha-1\bigr].
    \end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop:score},
\(s_N(u)=\zeta_N(u)-(1+\alpha)/u\). Hence
\begin{align}
&s_N(t-kx_1)-s_N(t-kx_2)
\nonumber\\
&=
\zeta_N(t-kx_1)-\zeta_N(t-kx_2)
-(1+\alpha)
\left(
\frac{1}{t-kx_1}-\frac{1}{t-kx_2}
\right)
\nonumber\\
&=
\zeta_N(t-kx_1)-\zeta_N(t-kx_2)
-\frac{(1+\alpha)k(x_1-x_2)}{t^2}
+O(t^{-3})\nonumber
\end{align}
as \(t\to\infty\). By the mean value theorem,
\[
\zeta_N(t-kx_1)-\zeta_N(t-kx_2)
=k(x_2-x_1)\zeta_N'(\eta_t)
\]
for some \(\eta_t\) between \(t-kx_1\) and \(t-kx_2\).
Hence, equation~\eqref{eq:Phi-bounds} gives
\(\zeta_N'(\eta_t)=O(t^{-2-\alpha})=o(t^{-2})\),
\begin{equation}
s_N(t-kx_1)-s_N(t-kx_2) =
-\frac{(1+\alpha)k(x_1-x_2)}{t^2}
+o(t^{-2}).
\label{eq:encoder-asymptotic}
\end{equation}


Next we consider the term \(\int_{\mathbb R}
\Delta(y)s_M(t-ky)\,dy\). To this end, choose \(\delta\in\left(\frac{1}{1+2\alpha},1\right)\). Let \(J(t):=t^\delta\) 
and define, within this proof,
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_t&:=\{|y|\le J(t)\}, \qquad
C_t:=\{|t-ky|\le 1\},\\
B_t&:=\{|y|>J(t)\}\setminus C_t.
\end{aligned}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(t\), these sets partition
\(\mathbb R\).

\paragraph*{Inner region $A_t$}
Since \(k\tfrac{J(t)}{t}\to0\), for sufficiently large \(t\) and
\(y\in A_t\), any point \(\eta_{y,t}\) between \(t\) and
\(t-ky\) satisfies
\(\tfrac12t\le\eta_{y,t}\le2t\). Thus
\[
s_M(t-ky)
=s_M(t)-ky\,s_M'(t)
+\frac{k^2y^2}{2}s_M''(\eta_{y,t}).
\]
Using~\eqref{eq:score-exp},
\[
s_M(t)=O(t^{-1}),\quad
s_M'(t)=\frac{1+\alpha}{t^2}+O(t^{-2-\alpha}),
\]
and
\(\sup_{y\in A_t}|s_M''(\eta_{y,t})|=O(t^{-3})\).
Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\int_{A_t}\Delta(y)s_M(t-ky)\,dy
=s_M(t)\int_{A_t}\Delta(y)\,dy - k s_M'(t)\int_{A_t}y\Delta(y)\,dy
+R_t,
\label{eq:inner-decomp}
\end{equation}
where
\[
|R_t|
\le Ct^{-3}\int_{A_t}y^2|\Delta(y)|\,dy.
\]

By~\eqref{eq:P1} and~\eqref{eq:tail0},
\[
\int_{A_t}\Delta(y)\,dy
=O(J(t)^{-1-2\alpha}),
\]
so the first term in~\eqref{eq:inner-decomp} is
\(O(t^{-1-\delta(1+2\alpha)})=o(t^{-2})\).

Likewise, \eqref{eq:P2} and~\eqref{eq:tail1} give
\[
\int_{A_t}y\Delta(y)\,dy
=\rho^\alpha(x_1-x_2)+O(t^{-2\delta\alpha}),
\]
and hence
\begin{align*}
&-k s_M'(t)\int_{A_t}y\Delta(y)\,dy
\nonumber\\
&\quad=
-\frac{(1+\alpha)k\rho^\alpha(x_1-x_2)}{t^2}
+O(t^{-2-\alpha})
+O(t^{-2-2\delta\alpha}).
\label{eq:inner-main}
\end{align*}

Finally, equation~\eqref{eq:P3} yields
\[
\int_{A_t}y^2|\Delta(y)|\,dy
=
\begin{cases}
O(1), & \alpha>\frac12,\\
O(\log t), & \alpha=\frac12,\\
O(t^{\delta(1-2\alpha)}), & \alpha<\frac12.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus \(R_t=o(t^{-2})\), and
\begin{equation*}
\int_{A_t}\Delta(y)s_M(t-ky)\,dy
=
-\frac{(1+\alpha)k\rho^\alpha(x_1-x_2)}{t^2}
+o(t^{-2}).
\label{eq:inner-result}
\end{equation*}

\paragraph*{Outer region $B_t \cup C_t$}
Since \(k>0\) WLoG, We obtain
\[
C_t=\left[\frac{t-1}{k},\frac{t+1}{k}\right],
\qquad |C_t|=\frac{2}{k}.
\]

On \(C_t\), \(y=\tfrac{t}{k}+O(1)\), so
\(|\Delta(y)|=O(t^{-2-2\alpha})\).  Since \(\delta<1\), \(C_t\subset\{|y|>J(t)\}\) for large \(t\), and 
\[
\int_{C_t}\Delta(y)s_M(t-ky)\,dy
=O(t^{-2-2\alpha})=o(t^{-2}),
\]
where we  used Proposition~\ref{prop:score}
\[
|s_M(u)|\le \frac{C}{|u|},\quad |u|\ge1,
\qquad
\sup_u|s_M(u)|<\infty.
\]
On \(B_t\cap\{|y|\le \tfrac{t}{2k}\}\),
\(|t-ky|\ge t/2\), and therefore
\begin{align*}
&\left|
\int_{\substack{B_t\\ |y|\le t/(2k)}}
\Delta(y)s_M(t-ky)\,dy
\right|
\nonumber\\
&\quad\le
\frac{2C}{t}\int_{|y|>J(t)}|\Delta(y)|\,dy
=O(t^{-1-\delta(1+2\alpha)})
=o(t^{-2}).
\label{eq:outer-near}
\end{align*}

Finally, on \(B_t\cap\{\tfrac{t}{2k}\}\),
\(|t-ky|>1\). With \(w=ky-t\), the contribution is bounded by
\begin{equation*}
\frac{C}{k}
\int_{\substack{|w|>1\\ |w+t|>t/2}}
\frac{
\left|\Delta\!\left((w+t)/k\right)\right|
}{|w|}\,dw .
\label{eq:outer-w}
\end{equation*}
For \(1<|w|\le t/2\),
\(|w+t|\ge t/2\), so this part is
\[
O(t^{-2-2\alpha}\log t)=o(t^{-2}).
\]
For \(|w|>\tfrac{t}{2}\), the condition \(|w+t|>\tfrac{t}{2}\) implies either
\(w>\tfrac{t}{2}\) or \(w<-3\tfrac{t}{2}\), and hence
\(|w+t|\ge\frac{|w|}{3}\). Thus the remaining part is
\[
O\!\left(\int_{|w|>t/2}|w|^{-3-2\alpha}\,dw\right)
=O(t^{-2-2\alpha})
=o(t^{-2}).
\]
Combining the inner and outer contributions, we obtain
\begin{equation}
    \int_{\mathbb R}\Delta(y)s_M(t-ky)\,dy =
-\frac{(1+\alpha)k\rho^\alpha(x_1-x_2)}{t^2}
+o(t^{-2}).
\label{eq:decoder-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
\xi_{\mathrm{lin}}'(t)
= s_N(t-kx_1)-s_N(t-kx_2) - \beta\!\int_{\mathbb R}
\Delta(y)s_M(t-ky)\,dy,\nonumber
\end{equation}
using \eqref{eq:encoder-asymptotic} and \eqref{eq:decoder-asymptotic} gives
\begin{align}
t^2\xi_{\mathrm{lin}}'(t)&=-(1+\alpha)k(x_1-x_2) + \beta(1+\alpha)k\rho^\alpha(x_1-x_2)+o(1)\nonumber\\
    &=(1+\alpha)k(x_1-x_2)[\beta\rho^\alpha-1]+o(1)\nonumber,
\end{align}
which completes the proof.
