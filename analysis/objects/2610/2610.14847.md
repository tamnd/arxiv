<!-- mainthm-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal C$ be a slightly degenerate braided fusion category with fermion $\psi$, and fix a minimal nondegenerate extension $\mathcal C\subset\mathcal E$. The restriction homomorphism $r\colon\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal E)\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal C)$ of $\mathbb C$-linear locality-respecting braided autoequivalences fits into the central short exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central-exact}
	1\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2\xrightarrow{\;1\mapsto[\alpha_\psi]\;}\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal E)\xrightarrow{\;r\;}\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal C)\longrightarrow1,
\end{equation}
where $[\alpha_\psi]$ is the fermion-attachment class. In particular, $r$ is surjective, $\ker r=\{[\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal E,\mathrm{id}_\psi],[\alpha_\psi]\}$, and every class in $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal C)$ has exactly two lifts, which differ by $[\alpha_\psi]$. If $\mathcal E$ is unitary, the same central short exact sequence exists with $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}$ replaced by $\operatorname{Aut}^{\mathrm u}_{\mathrm{LR}}$, the groups of unitary locality-respecting autoequivalences modulo unitary equivalence.

<!-- convention-1: body_md -->
\label{conv:coherence}
All monoidal categories are understood in the usual weak, not necessarily strict, sense. We write
\begin{equation*}
	a_{X,Y,Z}\colon (X\otimes Y)\otimes Z\longrightarrow X\otimes(Y\otimes Z), \quad l_X\colon\mathbf 1\otimes X\longrightarrow X, \quad r_X\colon X\otimes\mathbf 1\longrightarrow X
\end{equation*}
for their associator and left and right unit constraints (unitors). These are part of the data and satisfy the pentagon and triangle axioms. A full monoidal subcategory carries the inherited constraints. We use superscripts to distinguish categories when necessary.

By Mac Lane's coherence theorem~\cite[Thm.~2.9.2 and Rem.~2.9.3]{EGNO:2015}, between two bracketings of a word in objects, possibly with copies of $\mathbf 1$ inserted, there is a unique isomorphism composed of tensor products of $a$, $l$, $r$, their inverses and identities. We call it the canonical isomorphism. When we omit $\otimes$ and canonical isomorphisms in a diagram, an object stands for a word, in any bracketing. An arrow $usv\to utv$ labelled $f\colon s\to t$, where $u$ and $v$ are possibly empty words, stands for the canonical isomorphism from the given bracketing of $usv$ to one in which $s$ is a single factor, followed by $\mathrm{id}_u\otimes f\otimes\mathrm{id}_v$, followed by the canonical isomorphism to the given bracketing of $utv$. By coherence and naturality of $a$, $l$ and $r$, this morphism does not depend on the bracketing in which $s$ is a single factor. Hence whether such a diagram commutes does not depend on the bracketings chosen for its objects.
<!-- end convention-1 -->
<!-- convention-2: body_md -->
\label{conv:graded}
If $\mathcal E=\mathcal E_0\oplus\mathcal E_1$ as abelian categories~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi1.3]{EGNO:2015}, we call the objects of $\mathcal E_0\cup\mathcal E_1$ homogeneous. Linear functors, natural transformations and tensor structures are specified on homogeneous objects and morphisms and extended by additivity. Since every object is a direct sum of homogeneous ones, morphisms between $\mathcal E_0$ and $\mathcal E_1$ vanish, and all functors involved are additive, with $\otimes$ additive in each variable. Naturality, the monoidal axiom and the braiding compatibility need only be checked on homogeneous objects.
<!-- end convention-2 -->

Unitary fusion categories carry a positive $\dagger$-structure, unitary braided fusion categories also have unitary braiding, and unitary tensor functors are $\dagger$-functors with unitary tensor structure. However, unitarity plays no role in the proofs, and it enters only through the statement of the Main Theorem for the groups $\operatorname{Aut}^{\mathrm u}_{\mathrm{LR}}$ of unitary classes, which is needed for the comparison with~\cite{Bulmash:2021ryq} and which we deduce from the general case.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:transport}
Let $(F,J^F,\iota^F)\colon\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ be a tensor functor, let $G\colon\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ be a functor, and let $\theta\colon F\Rightarrow G$ be a natural isomorphism. There is a unique tensor structure on $G$ making $\theta$ monoidal, namely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport}
\begin{aligned}
	J^G_{X,Y} &=\theta_{X\otimes Y}J^F_{X,Y}
       (\theta_X^{-1}\otimes\theta_Y^{-1}),\\
       \iota^G&=\theta_\mathbf 1\iota^F.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
If the categories are braided and $F$ is braided, then $G$ is braided as well.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The formulas are forced by the defining conditions~\eqref{eq:monoidal-nat}, cf.~\cite[Def.~2.4.8 and Rem.~2.4.9]{EGNO:2015}. We substitute~\eqref{eq:transport} into the monoidal axiom~\eqref{eq:monoidal-axiom} and the unit conditions~\eqref{eq:functor-units} for $(G,J^G,\iota^G)$. By naturality of $\theta$ and of the constraints of $\mathcal B$, the components of $\theta$ cancel, and both conditions reduce to those for $F$. The braidedness condition follows similarly from~\eqref{eq:braided-functor} and naturality of the braiding.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:faithful}
Let $A,B$ be nonzero objects of a fusion category.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $g\in\operatorname{End}(B)$ satisfies $\mathrm{id}_A\otimes g=0$ or $g\otimes\mathrm{id}_A=0$, then $g=0$.
\item If $f\in\operatorname{End}(A)$ and $g\in\operatorname{End}(B)$ satisfy $f\otimes\mathrm{id}_B=\mathrm{id}_A\otimes g$, then there is $\lambda\in\mathbb C$ such that $f=\lambda\mathrm{id}_A$ and $g=\lambda\mathrm{id}_B$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We choose a left dual of $A$, with evaluation $\mathrm{ev}_A\colon A^*\otimes A\to\mathbf 1$. By the rigidity axioms, $\mathrm{ev}_A\neq0$. By semisimplicity and simplicity of $\mathbf 1$, there is therefore a section $s\colon\mathbf 1\to A^*\otimes A$ with $\mathrm{ev}_A s=\mathrm{id}_\mathbf 1$. For $h\in\operatorname{End}(A\otimes B)$, set
\begin{equation*}
	\pi(h):=l_B(\mathrm{ev}_A\otimes\mathrm{id}_B)a^{-1}_{A^*,A,B}(\mathrm{id}_{A^*}\otimes h)a_{A^*,A,B}(s\otimes\mathrm{id}_B)l_B^{-1}.
\end{equation*}
By naturality of the constraints, for $f\in\operatorname{End}(A)$ and $g\in\operatorname{End}(B)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pi-cancel}
	\pi(\mathrm{id}_A\otimes g)=g,\qquad\pi(f\otimes\mathrm{id}_B)=\lambda_f\mathrm{id}_B,\qquad\lambda_f:=\mathrm{ev}_A(\mathrm{id}_{A^*}\otimes f)s\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbf 1)=\mathbb C.
\end{equation}

(1) If $\mathrm{id}_A\otimes g=0$, then $g=\pi(\mathrm{id}_A\otimes g)=0$ by~\eqref{eq:pi-cancel}. The case $g\otimes\mathrm{id}_A=0$ follows in the same way from a right dual of $A$, applied to $\operatorname{End}(B\otimes A)$.

(2) Applying $\pi$ to $f\otimes\mathrm{id}_B=\mathrm{id}_A\otimes g$, we get $\lambda_f\mathrm{id}_B=g$ by~\eqref{eq:pi-cancel}. So $(f-\lambda_f\mathrm{id}_A)\otimes\mathrm{id}_B=0$, and $f=\lambda_f\mathrm{id}_A$ by (1), applied with the roles of $A$ and $B$ exchanged. Hence (2) holds with $\lambda=\lambda_f$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:mu}
Let $\psi$ be a fermion in a braided fusion category and let
$e\colon\psi\otimes\psi\to\mathbf 1$ be an isomorphism. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:e-assoc}
l_\psi(e\otimes\mathrm{id}_\psi)
=r_\psi(\mathrm{id}_\psi\otimes e)a_{\psi,\psi,\psi},
\qquad e\circ c_{\psi,\psi}=-e.
\end{equation}
Set $\psi^0:=\mathbf 1$, $\psi^1:=\psi$ and define
$\mu_{p,q}\colon\psi^p\otimes\psi^q\to\psi^{p+q}$, with exponents modulo two, by
\[
\mu_{0,q}=l_{\psi^q},\qquad \mu_{p,0}=r_{\psi^p},\qquad \mu_{1,1}=e.
\]
The two definitions of $\mu_{0,0}$ agree since $l_\mathbf 1=r_\mathbf 1$. These maps satisfy
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:mu-props}
\begin{align}
\mu_{p+q,r}(\mu_{p,q}\otimes\mathrm{id})
&=\mu_{p,q+r}(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mu_{q,r})a_{\psi^p,\psi^q,\psi^r},\label{eq:mu-assoc}\\
\mu_{q,p}c_{\psi^p,\psi^q}&=(-1)^{pq}\mu_{p,q}.\label{eq:mu-comm}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The braided equivalence $\langle\psi\rangle\simeq\mathbf{sVect}$ transports the usual
associative multiplication on the two fermion powers to maps of this form,
with some nonzero $e_0\colon\psi\otimes\psi\to\mathbf 1$.
Every other $e$ is a scalar multiple of $e_0$. In the only associativity
identity with three nonzero exponents, each side contains $e$ exactly once,
so it remains valid after this rescaling. Cases involving a zero exponent
are the unit axioms and naturality of $l,r$. The braiding identity~\eqref{eq:mu-comm} follows from $c_{\psi,\psi}=-\mathrm{id}$ and the identities $c_{\mathbf 1,X}=r_X^{-1}l_X$ and $c_{X,\mathbf 1}=l_X^{-1}r_X$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:grading}
For every simple $X\in\mathcal E$ there is $p(X)\in\mathbb Z/2$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parity}
\begin{aligned}
	M_{X,\psi}&:=c_{\psi,X}\circ c_{X,\psi}=(-1)^{p(X)}\,\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes\psi},\\
	\text{equivalently}\quad M_{\psi,X}&:=c_{X,\psi}\circ c_{\psi,X}=(-1)^{p(X)}\,\mathrm{id}_{\psi\otimes X}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
If $Z$ is a simple summand of $X\otimes Y$, then $p(Z)=p(X)+p(Y)$. Let $\mathcal E_i$ be the full subcategory of direct sums of simple objects of parity $i$. Then $\mathcal E=\mathcal E_0\oplus\mathcal E_1$ is a faithful $\mathbb Z/2$-grading with $\mathcal E_0=\mathcal C=\langle\psi\rangle'$, and $\mathcal C'=\langle\psi\rangle$. Every braided autoequivalence $F$ of $\mathcal E$ with $F(\psi)\cong\psi$ satisfies $F(\mathcal E_i)\subseteq\mathcal E_i$.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $X$ be simple. Since $\psi$ is invertible, $X\otimes\psi$ and $\psi\otimes X$ are simple, so both double braidings in~\eqref{eq:parity} are scalars. Since $M_{\psi,X}=c_{X,\psi}\,M_{X,\psi}\,c_{X,\psi}^{-1}$, the two scalars agree, and we write
\begin{equation*}
	M_{X,\psi}=\lambda_X\,\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes\psi},\qquad M_{\psi,X}=\lambda_X\,\mathrm{id}_{\psi\otimes X}.
\end{equation*}
By~\cite[Lemma~8.22.1(i)]{EGNO:2015}, if $M_{U,W}=\lambda\,\mathrm{id}_{U\otimes W}$ and $M_{V,W}=\mu\,\mathrm{id}_{V\otimes W}$, then $M_{U\otimes V,W}=\lambda\mu\,\mathrm{id}_{(U\otimes V)\otimes W}$. For $U=V=\psi$ and $W=X$, we get $M_{\psi\otimes\psi,X}=\lambda_X^2\,\mathrm{id}$. Since $\psi\otimes\psi\cong\mathbf 1$ and $M_{\mathbf 1,X}=\mathrm{id}$, we have $\lambda_X^2=1$ by naturality of the braiding. Hence $\lambda_X=(-1)^{p(X)}$ for a unique $p(X)\in\mathbb Z/2$. For $W=\psi$, $U=X$ and $V=Y$, we get $\lambda_Z=\lambda_X\lambda_Y$ for every simple summand $Z$ of $X\otimes Y$ by naturality of the braiding, that is, $p(Z)=p(X)+p(Y)$.

Since $\psi\in\mathcal Z_2(\mathcal C)$, we have $\langle\psi\rangle\subseteq\mathcal C'$. As $\mathcal E$ is nondegenerate, $\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal C)\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal C')=\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal E)=2\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal C)$~\cite[Cor.~8.21.7]{EGNO:2015}, so $\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal C')=2=\operatorname{FPdim}(\langle\psi\rangle)$ and $\mathcal C'=\langle\psi\rangle$ by~\cite[Prop.~6.3.3]{EGNO:2015}. By the double centralizer theorem~\cite[Cor.~8.21.2]{EGNO:2015}, $\mathcal C=\mathcal C''=\langle\psi\rangle'$. A simple object has parity $0$ if and only if it centralizes $\psi$, so $\mathcal E_0=\langle\psi\rangle'=\mathcal C$. If $\mathcal E_1=0$, then $\psi$ centralizes $\mathcal E$, so $\psi\in\mathcal Z_2(\mathcal E)=\mathbf{Vect}$, which contradicts $c_{\psi,\psi}=-\mathrm{id}$. Hence $\mathcal E_1\neq0$, and since $\mathbf 1\in\mathcal E_0$, the grading is faithful.

Let $F$ be a braided autoequivalence of $\mathcal E$ with $F(\psi)\cong\psi$, and let $X$ be simple. Applying~\eqref{eq:braided-functor} for $F$ twice, we get $F(c_{\psi,X}c_{X,\psi})\,J^F_{X,\psi}=J^F_{X,\psi}\,c_{F(\psi),F(X)}c_{F(X),F(\psi)}$. Hence the monodromy of $F(X)$ with $F(\psi)\cong\psi$ is $\lambda_X$, that is, $p(F(X))=p(X)$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:LR}
A \emph{locality-respecting} (LR) autoequivalence of $(\mathcal B,\psi)$ is a pair $(F,\varphi)$, where $F$ is a braided autoequivalence of $\mathcal B$ and $\varphi\colon F(\psi)\to\psi$ is an isomorphism satisfying the \emph{local-vertex condition}~\cite[Def.~3.5, Eq.~(3.1)]{Galindo-Venegas-Ramirez:2025}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertex}
	e\circ(\varphi\otimes\varphi)=F(e)\circ J^F_{\psi,\psi}\colon F(\psi)\otimes F(\psi)\to\mathbf 1 .
\end{equation}
Here $F$ is unit-normalized.
This condition does not depend on the choice of $e$: replacing $e$ by $se$, $s\in\mathbb C^\times$, multiplies both sides by $s$, because $F$ is linear. A \emph{morphism of pairs} $(F,\varphi)\to(G,\varphi')$ is a monoidal natural isomorphism $\eta\colon F\Rightarrow G$ with $\varphi'\circ\eta_\psi=\varphi$. Since $\eta$ is invertible, $\eta^{-1}$ is a morphism of pairs $(G,\varphi')\to(F,\varphi)$, so every morphism of pairs is an isomorphism. The \emph{composite} of pairs is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compose-pairs}
	(F,\varphi)\cdot(G,\varphi'):=(F\circ G,\ \varphi\circ F(\varphi')).
\end{equation}
We write $[F,\varphi]$ for the isomorphism class of $(F,\varphi)$ and $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal B)$ for the set of classes. In the unitary setting, $F$, $\varphi$ and $\eta$ are required to be unitary, and the resulting set is
denoted $\operatorname{Aut}^{\mathrm u}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal B)$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:LR-meaning}
Since $\langle\psi\rangle\simeq\mathbf{sVect}$, condition~\eqref{eq:vertex} is precisely the condition that $\varphi$ extend uniquely to a monoidal natural isomorphism
\begin{equation*}
	\widehat\varphi\colon F|_{\langle\psi\rangle}\Rightarrow \mathrm{Id}_{\langle\psi\rangle},\qquad\widehat\varphi_{\mathbf 1}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbf 1},\quad\widehat\varphi_\psi=\varphi.
\end{equation*}
Indeed, on simple objects the only nontrivial instance of the tensor condition in~\eqref{eq:monoidal-nat} for $\widehat\varphi$ is at $(\psi,\psi)$. There $\widehat\varphi_{\psi\otimes\psi}=e^{-1}F(e)$ by naturality of $\widehat\varphi$ at $e$, so the condition reads $e^{-1}F(e)J^F_{\psi,\psi}=\varphi\otimes\varphi$, which is~\eqref{eq:vertex}. Thus an LR pair records a \emph{chosen} monoidal trivialization on the fermion subcategory.

Moreover, if $\eta\colon F\Rightarrow G$ is a monoidal natural isomorphism and $(G,\varphi')$ is an LR pair, then $(F,\varphi'\circ\eta_\psi)$ is an LR pair, and $\eta$ is an isomorphism between these pairs by Definition~\ref{def:LR}. Indeed, by~\eqref{eq:vertex} for $(G,\varphi')$, the two conditions in~\eqref{eq:monoidal-nat} for $\eta$, and naturality of $\eta$ at $e$, we have
\begin{equation*}
	e\circ(\varphi'\eta_\psi\otimes\varphi'\eta_\psi)=G(e)\,J^G_{\psi,\psi}\,(\eta_\psi\otimes\eta_\psi)=G(e)\,\eta_{\psi\otimes\psi}\,J^F_{\psi,\psi}=\eta_\mathbf 1\,F(e)\,J^F_{\psi,\psi}=F(e)\,J^F_{\psi,\psi}.
\end{equation*}
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:LR-group}
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $F$ is a braided autoequivalence with $F(\psi)\cong\psi$, then there exists an isomorphism $\varphi_0\colon F(\psi)\to\psi$ satisfying~\eqref{eq:vertex}, and the only isomorphisms satisfying this condition are $\varphi_0$ and $-\varphi_0$. In the unitary setting, if $F$ and $e$ are unitary, then $\varphi_0$ is unitary.
\item Composition~\eqref{eq:compose-pairs} induces a group structure on $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal B)$.
\item Put $\check\Upsilon:=[\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal B,-\mathrm{id}_\psi]$. Then
$[F,-\varphi]=[F,\varphi]\cdot\check\Upsilon=\check\Upsilon\cdot[F,\varphi]$,
and $\check\Upsilon^2=1$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
(1) Fix an isomorphism $\omega\colon F(\psi)\to\psi$. Since $\operatorname{End}(\psi)=\mathbb C$, every isomorphism $F(\psi)\to\psi$ is $s\omega$ for a unique $s\in\mathbb C^\times$. Both sides of~\eqref{eq:vertex} for $\omega$ are isomorphisms $F(\psi)\otimes F(\psi)\to\mathbf 1$, and $\operatorname{Hom}(F(\psi)\otimes F(\psi),\mathbf 1)\cong\operatorname{End}(\mathbf 1)=\mathbb C$, so $F(e)\circ J^F_{\psi,\psi}=\lambda\,e\circ(\omega\otimes\omega)$ for a unique $\lambda\in\mathbb C^\times$. Hence $s\omega$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:vertex} if and only if $s^2=\lambda$, and the solutions are $\pm\varphi_0$ with $\varphi_0:=\sqrt\lambda\,\omega$. For $\psi\otimes\psi=\mathbf 1$ and $e=\mathrm{id}$ this is~\cite[Prop.~3.6(1),(3)]{Galindo-Venegas-Ramirez:2025}. If $F$ and $e$ are unitary and $\varphi$ is a solution, then $\varphi\otimes\varphi=e^{-1}F(e)J^F_{\psi,\psi}$ is unitary. Writing $\varphi^\dagger\varphi=c\,\mathrm{id}_{F(\psi)}$ with $c>0$, we get $c^2=1$, so $c=1$ and $\varphi$ is unitary.

(2) By Remark~\ref{rem:LR-meaning}, an LR pair is a braided autoequivalence equipped with a chosen monoidal trivialization on $\langle\psi\rangle$. Composing these trivializations gives
\begin{equation*}
	FG(\psi)\xrightarrow{\,F(\varphi')\,}F(\psi)\xrightarrow{\,\varphi\,}\psi.
\end{equation*}
Hence $(FG,\varphi F(\varphi'))$ is again an LR pair.

We next check that the product is independent of representatives. Suppose $\eta\colon(F,\varphi)\Rightarrow(\widetilde F,\widetilde\varphi)$ and $\theta\colon(G,\varphi')\Rightarrow(\widetilde G,\widetilde\varphi')$ are isomorphisms of pairs. Their horizontal composite
\begin{equation*}
	(\eta*\theta)_X=\eta_{\widetilde G(X)}\circ F(\theta_X)\colon FG(X)\longrightarrow\widetilde F\widetilde G(X)
\end{equation*}
is a monoidal natural isomorphism. By naturality of $\eta$ and the marking conditions for $\eta,\theta$, we have	
\begin{equation*}
	\widetilde\varphi\,\widetilde F(\widetilde\varphi')\,(\eta*\theta)_\psi=\widetilde\varphi\,\eta_\psi\,
  F(\widetilde\varphi'\theta_\psi)=\varphi F(\varphi').
\end{equation*}
Thus it is an isomorphism of the composite pairs, so the product
descends to classes.

Composition is associative since both bracketings of three pairs have tensor functor $FGH$ and marking $\varphi F(\varphi')FG(\varphi'')$. The identity is $[\mathrm{Id},\mathrm{id}_\psi]$.

It remains to construct inverses. We choose a unit-normalized tensor quasi-inverse $G$ of $F$ and a monoidal isomorphism $\epsilon\colon FG\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}$. Since $FG\cong\mathrm{Id}$ monoidally, $FG$ is braided, and since $F$ is braided and faithful, $G$ is braided. Moreover, $G(\psi)\cong GF(\psi)\cong\psi$ by $\varphi$ and $GF\cong\mathrm{Id}$. By (1), choose a marking $\varphi'$ making $(G,\varphi')$ an LR pair. Both $\epsilon_\psi$ and $\varphi F(\varphi')$ are valid markings for $FG$, by Remark~\ref{rem:LR-meaning} and closure under composition. By (1), they differ by at most a sign. Replacing $\varphi'$ by $-\varphi'$ if necessary therefore makes $\epsilon$ an isomorphism
\begin{equation*}
	(F,\varphi)(G,\varphi')\cong(\mathrm{Id},\mathrm{id}_\psi).
\end{equation*}
Thus every class has a right inverse. A monoid in which every element has a right inverse is a group. Indeed, if $ab=1$, choose $c$ with $bc=1$. Then $a=a(bc)=(ab)c=c$, so $ba=bc=1$.

(3) By~\eqref{eq:compose-pairs}, $(F,\varphi)(\mathrm{Id},-\mathrm{id}_\psi)=(F,-\varphi)=(\mathrm{Id},-\mathrm{id}_\psi)(F,\varphi)$, since $F(-\mathrm{id}_\psi)=-\mathrm{id}_{F(\psi)}$. For $(F,\varphi)=(\mathrm{Id},-\mathrm{id}_\psi)$, we get $\check\Upsilon^2=[\mathrm{Id},\mathrm{id}_\psi]=1$.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:restriction}
Let $\mathcal C\subset\mathcal E$ be a minimal nondegenerate extension. By Lemma~\ref{lem:grading}, every braided autoequivalence $F$ of $\mathcal E$ with $F(\psi)\cong\psi$ preserves $\mathcal C$, and so does a quasi-inverse, which also fixes $\psi$ up to isomorphism. Hence $F$ restricts to a braided autoequivalence $F|_\mathcal C$ of $\mathcal C$. The \emph{restriction homomorphism} is
\begin{equation*}
	r\colon\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal E)\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal C),\qquad [F,\varphi]\mapsto[F|_\mathcal C,\varphi].
\end{equation*}
Thus $[F,\varphi]\in\ker r$ if and only if there is a morphism of pairs $(F|_\mathcal C,\varphi)\to(\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal C,\mathrm{id}_\psi)$, that is, a monoidal natural isomorphism $\eta\colon F|_\mathcal C\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal C$ with $\eta_\psi=\varphi$. The same definitions apply to $\operatorname{Aut}^{\mathrm u}_{\mathrm{LR}}$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:unitary}
Let $\mathcal B$ be a unitary braided fusion category with fermion $\psi$, and let $e$ be unitary. Then the forgetful map $\operatorname{Aut}^{\mathrm u}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal B)\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal B)$ is bijective, and $\operatorname{Aut}^{\mathrm u}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal B)$ is a group under~\eqref{eq:compose-pairs}. If $\mathcal C\subset\mathcal E$ is a unitary minimal nondegenerate extension, then the bijections for $\mathcal B=\mathcal E$ and $\mathcal B=\mathcal C$ commute with the restriction maps, and hence identify the unitary and linear restriction homomorphisms.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $(F,\varphi)$ be an LR autoequivalence of $\mathcal B$. By~\cite[Thm.~5]{Reutter:2023}, there is a monoidal natural isomorphism $\eta\colon F\Rightarrow F'$ onto a unitary monoidal autoequivalence $F'$. It is braided, because $J^{F'}$ is transported from $J^F$ along $\eta$ (Section~\ref{subsec:tensor-functors}). We write $\varphi':=\varphi\circ\eta_\psi^{-1}$. By Remark~\ref{rem:LR-meaning}, $(F',\varphi')$ is an LR autoequivalence and $\eta$ is a morphism of pairs $(F,\varphi)\to(F',\varphi')$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:LR-group}(1), $\varphi'$ is unitary. Hence the forgetful map is surjective. A morphism of pairs between unitary pairs is a monoidal natural isomorphism between unitary monoidal equivalences, hence unitary by~\cite[Thm.~2]{Reutter:2023}. Hence the forgetful map is injective. It is multiplicative, since composites of unitary pairs are unitary, so $\operatorname{Aut}^{\mathrm u}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal B)$ is a group. Restriction to $\mathcal C$ maps unitary pairs to unitary pairs, so the bijections commute with $r$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:BB}
In~\cite{Barkeshli:2014cna,Bulmash:2021hmb,Bulmash:2021ryq} a symmetry is described in a skeletal gauge. It consists of a permutation $\rho$ of the simple labels and $U$-symbols $U_\rho(a,b;c)$, the matrices in chosen bases of the fusion-space maps $V^{ab}_c\to V^{\rho(a)\rho(b)}_{\rho(c)}$, $v\mapsto\rho(v)\circ J^\rho_{a,b}$, where $V^{ab}_c=\operatorname{Hom}(a\otimes b,c)$.

A natural isomorphism between two symmetries with the same permutation is, by Schur's lemma~\cite[Prop.~1.8.4]{EGNO:2015}, a family of scalars $\gamma_a$. It changes the $U$-symbols by the ratio of the $\gamma$'s~\cite[Eq.~(22)]{Bulmash:2021ryq}.

Bulmash and Barkeshli require $U_\rho(\psi,\psi;\mathbf 1)=1$~\cite[Eq.~(65)]{Bulmash:2021ryq}, and they allow only natural isomorphisms admitting a presentation with $\gamma_\psi=1$~\cite[Eq.~(77)]{Bulmash:2021ryq}. In a skeletal model $F(\psi)=\psi$, and the dictionary is as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item $U_F(\psi,\psi;\mathbf 1)=1$ is \eqref{eq:vertex} with $\varphi=\mathrm{id}_\psi$;
\item a natural isomorphism with $\gamma_\psi=1$ is a morphism of pairs
$(F,\mathrm{id}_\psi)\to(G,\mathrm{id}_\psi)$.
\end{itemize}
More generally, we write $\varphi=t\,\mathrm{id}_\psi$ with $t\in\mathbb C^\times$ in a skeletal model and then transport $F$ along the natural isomorphism whose component at $\psi$ is $\varphi$ and whose other simple components are identities. By Lemma~\ref{lem:transport} and Remark~\ref{rem:LR-meaning}, we obtain an isomorphic LR pair $(F^\gamma,\mathrm{id}_\psi)$, for which $U_{F^\gamma}(\psi,\psi;\mathbf 1)=1$. Thus the dictionary covers every LR pair. In particular, every LR pair $(F,-\mathrm{id}_\psi)$ is isomorphic to a pair $(F^\gamma,\mathrm{id}_\psi)$. Here $F^\gamma$ is $F$ transported along the natural isomorphism with $\gamma_\psi=-1$ and $\gamma_a=1$ for $a\neq\psi$. Accordingly, the natural isomorphisms $\Upsilon_\psi$ and $\check\Upsilon_\psi$ of \cite[Eqs.~(72)--(73)]{Bulmash:2021ryq} correspond to the classes $[\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal C,-\mathrm{id}_\psi]$ and $[\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal E,-\mathrm{id}_\psi]$.

Consequently $\operatorname{Aut}^{\mathrm u}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal B)$ is the group denoted $\operatorname{Aut}_{LR}(\mathcal B)$ in~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi IV.C]{Bulmash:2021ryq} and the fermionic topological symmetry group $\operatorname{Aut}_f(\mathcal B)$ of~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi III.A]{Aasen:2021vva}, which is defined by the same two conditions~\cite[Eqs.~(104) and~(107)]{Aasen:2021vva}, in both cases restricted to complex-linear symmetries. The map $r$ is the restriction map of~\cite[Eq.~(83)]{Bulmash:2021ryq}.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:normalized}
A pair $(\Phi,\mathrm{id}_\psi)$ is \emph{normalized} if $\Phi$ is a braided autoequivalence of $\mathcal E$ whose restriction to $\mathcal C$ is the identity tensor functor. That is, $\Phi(b)=b$, $\Phi(f)=f$, $J^\Phi_{b,b'}=\mathrm{id}$ and $\iota^\Phi=\mathrm{id}_\mathbf 1$ for all objects $b,b'$ and morphisms $f$ of $\mathcal C$. The local-vertex condition~\eqref{eq:vertex} with $\varphi=\mathrm{id}_\psi$ then holds automatically, since $e$ is a morphism of $\mathcal C$ and hence $\Phi(e)\circ J^\Phi_{\psi,\psi}=e=e\circ(\mathrm{id}_\psi\otimes\mathrm{id}_\psi)$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:normalization}
Every class in $\ker r$ is represented by a normalized pair. If $(\Phi,\mathrm{id}_\psi)$ is normalized, then $\Phi(\mathcal V)\subseteq\mathcal V$, and $\Phi|_\mathcal V$ with the module structure
\begin{equation*}

\end{equation*}

Let $[F,\varphi]\in\ker r$. We construct a normalized pair $(\Phi,\mathrm{id}_\psi)$ with $[\Phi,\mathrm{id}_\psi]=[F,\varphi]$. By Definition~\ref{def:restriction}, there is a monoidal natural isomorphism $\eta\colon F|_\mathcal C\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal C$ with $\eta_\psi=\varphi$. We define a functor $\Phi\colon\mathcal E\to\mathcal E$ and a natural isomorphism $\Theta\colon F\Rightarrow\Phi$ on homogeneous objects (Convention~\ref{conv:graded}) by
\begin{equation*}
	\Phi(b)=b,\quad\Theta_b=\eta_b\quad(b\in\mathcal C),\qquad\Phi(X)=F(X),\quad\Theta_X=\mathrm{id}_{F(X)}\quad(X\in\mathcal V),
\end{equation*}
and on a morphism $f\colon U\to U'$ between homogeneous objects $U,U'$ of $\mathcal E$ by $\Phi(f)=\Theta_{U'}\,F(f)\,\Theta_U^{-1}$, so that $\Theta$ is natural by construction. Here $\Phi(f)$ is the unique morphism with $\Theta_{U'}\,F(f)=\Phi(f)\,\Theta_U$, by~\cite[Lem.~1.5.10]{Riehl:2016} applied to $F(f)$ and the isomorphisms $\Theta_U$ and $\Theta_{U'}$. By this uniqueness, $\Phi(gf)=\Phi(g)\Phi(f)$ for $g\colon U'\to U''$ and $\Phi(\mathrm{id}_U)=\mathrm{id}_{\Phi(U)}$, so $\Phi$ is a functor. \node (tr) at (4.4,0) {$F(X\otimes Y)$};

Hence $\Phi|_\mathcal V$, with the module structure $s$, is a left $\mathcal C$-module autoequivalence of $\mathcal V$.

\label{prop:module-type}
Let $(\Phi,\mathrm{id}_\psi)$ be normalized. There is a unique $a\in\{\mathbf 1,\psi\}$ such that $\Phi|_\mathcal V\cong R_a$ as left $\mathcal C$-module functors, where $\Phi|_\mathcal V$ carries the module structure of Lemma~\ref{lem:normalization}.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:normalization} and Corollary~\ref{cor:morita}(1), there are $a\in\mathcal C$
and a module natural isomorphism $t\colon\Phi|_\mathcal V\Rightarrow R_a$. Using the module structure $s^{R_a}_{b,X}=a^{-1}_{b,X,a}$,~\eqref{eq:module-nat} reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t-module}
	t_{b\otimes X}\circ J^\Phi_{b,X}=a^{-1}_{b,X,a}(\mathrm{id}_b\otimes t_X)\qquad(b\in\mathcal C,\ X\in\mathcal V).
\end{equation}

\label{rem:twist-argument}
If $\mathcal E$ is pseudo-unitary, the transparency of $a$ in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:module-type} can also be seen using twists, that is, topological spins. First, $a$ is invertible. Indeed, $R_a\cong\Phi|_\mathcal V$ is an autoequivalence of $\mathcal V$, so it permutes the simple objects of $\mathcal V$. Summing over them, we get $\sum_X\operatorname{FPdim}(X)^2=\sum_X\operatorname{FPdim}(X\otimes a)^2=\operatorname{FPdim}(a)^2\sum_X\operatorname{FPdim}(X)^2$, so $\operatorname{FPdim}(a)=1$ and $a$ is invertible~\cite[Exercise~4.5.9]{EGNO:2015}. For a simple object $Y$ of $\mathcal E$, let $m_a(Y)$ be the monodromy scalar of $Y$ with $a$, that is, $c_{a,Y}\circ c_{Y,a}=m_a(Y)\,\mathrm{id}_{Y\otimes a}$.

Since $\mathcal E$ is pseudo-unitary, it has a unique spherical structure with $d_Y=\operatorname{FPdim}(Y)$ for simple $Y$~\cite[Prop.~9.5.1]{EGNO:2015}, and we let $\theta$ be the corresponding ribbon structure~\cite[(8.35), Prop.~8.10.12]{EGNO:2015}. It satisfies the balancing identity~\cite[Def.~8.10.1]{EGNO:2015}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:balancing}
\theta_{U\otimes V}=(\theta_U\otimes\theta_V)\circ c_{V,U}\circ c_{U,V}\qquad(U,V\in\mathcal E).
\end{equation}
For simple $Y$ we write $\theta_Y$ also for the scalar by which $\theta$ acts on $Y$. A braided autoequivalence $F$ of $\mathcal E$ preserves $\theta$. Indeed, it transports the spherical structure to one with the same dimensions $\operatorname{FPdim}(F(Y))=\operatorname{FPdim}(Y)$~\cite[Prop.~4.5.7]{EGNO:2015}, hence to itself by this uniqueness, and it preserves the Drinfeld isomorphism $u$~\cite[(8.30)]{EGNO:2015}, which is built from the braiding and the duality. Hence $\theta_{F(Y)}=\theta_Y$ for simple $Y$.

Let $X$ be a simple vortex. Since $\Phi(X)\cong X\otimes a$, we get $\theta_{X\otimes a}=\theta_X$. Since $a$ is invertible, $X\otimes a$ is simple (Section~\ref{subsec:fermions}), so all terms of the balancing identity at $(U,V)=(X,a)$ are scalar multiples of $\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes a}$, and it reads $\theta_{X\otimes a}=\theta_X\,\theta_a\,m_a(X)$. Hence $m_a(X)=\theta_a^{-1}$ for every simple vortex $X$.

Now let $b\in\mathcal C$ be simple. Since $b\otimes X\neq0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:faithful}(1), it has a simple summand $Y$, and $Y$ is a vortex because $\mathcal C\otimes\mathcal V\subseteq\mathcal V$. By~\cite[Lemma~8.22.1(i)]{EGNO:2015} and naturality of the braiding, as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:grading}, $m_a(Y)=m_a(b)\,m_a(X)$. Since $X$ and $Y$ are simple vortices, $m_a(X)=m_a(Y)=\theta_a^{-1}$, so $\theta_a^{-1}=m_a(b)\,\theta_a^{-1}$ and $m_a(b)=1$. Thus every simple object of $\mathcal C$ has trivial monodromy with $a$, that is, $a\in\mathcal Z_2(\mathcal C)$. We note that the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:module-type} is purely braided and needs no ribbon structure.

\label{rem:rigidity-cocycle}
In the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rigidity}, the tensor structure $J'$ is trivialized in two steps, which we phrase in terms of the group cohomology of $\mathbb Z/2$ with coefficients in $\mathbb C^\times$. For a homogeneous object $U\in\mathcal F$, we write $p(U)\in\{0,1\}$ for its degree, and we add degrees modulo $2$. Since the grading is faithful, every degree is the degree of a nonzero object, and tensor products of nonzero objects are nonzero by Lemma~\ref{lem:faithful}(1).
\begin{enumerate}
\item By~\eqref{eq:J-trivial-1},~\eqref{eq:J-trivial-2} and~\eqref{eq:J-scalar}, we have $J'_{U,V}=\omega(p(U),p(V))\,\mathrm{id}_{U\otimes V}$ for homogeneous $U,V\in\mathcal F$, where $\omega(g,h):=\lambda^{gh}$ for $g,h\in\{0,1\}$. A priori, the components $J'_{X,Y}$ with $X,Y\in\mathcal M$ are arbitrary automorphisms of $X\otimes Y$, and the essential input is that, by~\eqref{eq:four-odd} and Lemma~\ref{lem:faithful}(2), they are all equal to $\lambda\,\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes Y}$ for a single $\lambda\in\mathbb C^\times$. Let $U,V,W\in\mathcal F$ be nonzero and homogeneous of degrees $g,h,k$. Since $F'$ is the identity functor, the monoidal axiom~\eqref{eq:monoidal-axiom} for $F'$ at $(U,V,W)$ reads
\begin{equation*}
	a_{U,V,W}\,J'_{U\otimes V,W}\,(J'_{U,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_W)=J'_{U,V\otimes W}\,(\mathrm{id}_U\otimes J'_{V,W})\,a_{U,V,W}.
\end{equation*}
The objects $U\otimes V$ and $V\otimes W$ are homogeneous of degrees $g+h$ and $h+k$, so the left-hand side is $\omega(g+h,k)\,\omega(g,h)\,a_{U,V,W}$ and the right-hand side is $\omega(g,h+k)\,\omega(h,k)\,a_{U,V,W}$. Cancelling $a_{U,V,W}$ and using $(U\otimes V)\otimes W\neq0$, we get
\begin{equation*}
	\omega(h,k)\,\omega(g,h+k)=\omega(g+h,k)\,\omega(g,h).
\end{equation*}
By faithfulness of the grading, this identity holds for all $g,h,k\in\mathbb Z/2$, which is the $2$-cocycle condition for $\omega$. Since $\omega$ is normalized, that is, $\omega(0,h)=\omega(g,0)=1$, the condition is automatic unless $g=h=k=1$, where it reads $\lambda=\lambda$. So $J'$ is given by the normalized $2$-cocycle $\omega$ on $\mathbb Z/2$.
\item For $\tau\colon\mathbb Z/2\to\mathbb C^\times$, let $\gamma\colon F'\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal F$ be the natural isomorphism with $\gamma_U=\tau(p(U))\,\mathrm{id}_U$ for homogeneous $U$. For homogeneous $U,V$ of degrees $g,h$, the tensor condition $\gamma_{U\otimes V}J'_{U,V}=\gamma_U\otimes\gamma_V$ in~\eqref{eq:monoidal-nat} reads $\tau(g+h)\,\omega(g,h)\,\mathrm{id}_{U\otimes V}=\tau(g)\,\tau(h)\,\mathrm{id}_{U\otimes V}$. Hence it holds for all $U,V$ if and only if $\omega=\delta\tau$, where $(\delta\tau)(g,h)=\tau(g)\,\tau(h)\,\tau(g+h)^{-1}$. Since $\iota^{F'}=\mathrm{id}_\mathbf 1$, the unit condition reads $\tau(0)=1$, and it follows from $\omega=\delta\tau$ because $\omega(0,0)=1$ and $(\delta\tau)(0,0)=\tau(0)$. So $\gamma$ is monoidal if and only if $\omega=\delta\tau$. For $\tau(0)=1$ and $\tau(1)=t$, we have $(\delta\tau)(g,h)=t^{2gh}$, so $\omega=\delta\tau$ if and only if $t^2=\lambda$. Every normalized $2$-cocycle on $\mathbb Z/2$ is of the form $(g,h)\mapsto\lambda^{gh}$ with $\lambda\in\mathbb C^\times$, and $\lambda$ has a square root in $\mathbb C^\times$, so we recover $H^2(\mathbb Z/2;\mathbb C^\times)=0$.
\end{enumerate}

\label{def:alpha}
On homogeneous objects and morphisms, we define
\begin{equation*}
	\alpha(X):=\begin{cases}X,&p(X)=0,\\
	X\otimes\psi,&p(X)=1,
	\end{cases}\qquad\alpha(f):=\begin{cases}
	f,&p(X)=p(X')=0,\\
	f\otimes\mathrm{id}_\psi,&p(X)=p(X')=1
	\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
for $f\colon X\to X'$, and extend by additivity. We first define a tensor structure on the functor $\widetilde\alpha\colon X\mapsto X\otimes\psi^{p(X)}$, where it is given by a single formula for all parities. Since $\mathcal E$ is not assumed to be strict, $\widetilde\alpha(b)=b\otimes\mathbf 1$ is only isomorphic to $b$ for $b\in\mathcal C$, so we then transport this structure to $\alpha$, which is the identity on $\mathcal C$. On morphisms, $\widetilde\alpha(f)=f\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\psi^{p(X)}}$, and we put $\widetilde\iota=r_\mathbf 1^{-1}\colon\mathbf 1\to\mathbf 1\otimes\mathbf 1$. For $p=p(X)$, $q=p(Y)$, we abbreviate $P=\psi^p$ and $Q=\psi^q$. We define
\begin{equation*}
	K_{X,Y}\colon (X\otimes P)\otimes(Y\otimes Q) \longrightarrow (X\otimes Y)\otimes\psi^{p+q}
\end{equation*}
by the following composite, read from right to left:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K}
\begin{aligned}
	K_{X,Y}:={}&(\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes Y}\otimes\mu_{p,q})\circ a^{-1}_{X,Y,P\otimes Q}\circ(\mathrm{id}_X\otimes a_{Y,P,Q})\\
	&\circ\bigl(\mathrm{id}_X\otimes(c_{P,Y}\otimes\mathrm{id}_Q)\bigr)\circ(\mathrm{id}_X\otimes a^{-1}_{P,Y,Q})\circ a_{X,P,Y\otimes Q}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus $K$ moves the attached $P$ past $Y$ and then multiplies $P$ and $Q$. The natural isomorphism
\begin{equation*}
	\vartheta_X\colon\widetilde\alpha(X)\longrightarrow\alpha(X),\qquad \vartheta_X=\begin{cases}
	r_X,&p(X)=0,\\
	\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes\psi},&p(X)=1,
	\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
transports this structure to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J-alpha}
	J^\alpha_{X,Y}:=\vartheta_{X\otimes Y}\circ K_{X,Y}\circ(\vartheta_X^{-1}\otimes\vartheta_Y^{-1}),\qquad \iota^\alpha:=\vartheta_\mathbf 1\circ\widetilde\iota=\mathrm{id}_\mathbf 1.
\end{equation}
We call $(\alpha,J^\alpha,\iota^\alpha)$ \emph{fermion attachment}. In particular, since $p(\psi)=0$, we have $\alpha(\psi)=\psi$.

\end{enumerate}

\end{equation}
is connected \'etale of dimension $d$~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi3.2]{Davydov:2011dqx}. By standard condensation formulas, the category $\mathcal K=\mathcal D_A^0$ of local $A$-modules~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi3.5]{Davydov:2010kfz} is nondegenerate, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:surj-residual}
	\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal K)=4,\qquad [\mathcal K]=[\mathcal D]=0
\end{equation}
in the Witt group~\cite[Cors.~3.30, 3.32 and Prop.~5.4]{Davydov:2010kfz}.

The objects $g_1=\psi\boxtimes\mathbf 1$ and $g_2=\mathbf 1\boxtimes\psi$ centralize $A$, because $\psi$ centralizes $\mathcal C$. Their free modules $h_i=g_i\otimes A$ are therefore local~\cite[Lem.~3.15]{Davydov:2010kfz}, and since the free-module functor from the centralizer of $A$ to $\mathcal D_A^0$ is braided~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi3.4--3.5]{Davydov:2010kfz}, each $h_i$ satisfies $h_i\otimes_A h_i\cong\mathbf 1$ and $c_{h_i,h_i}=c_{g_i,g_i}=-\mathrm{id}$. Moreover, $h_1$ and $h_2$ are nontrivial because $g_i$ would be a summand of $A$ if $g_i\otimes A\cong A$, whereas a summand $X^*\boxtimes F(X)$ with a trivial factor has $X\cong\mathbf 1$ and is therefore $\mathbf 1\boxtimes\mathbf 1$. Hence $h_1$ and $h_2$ are fermions in $\mathcal K$. So $\langle h_1\rangle\simeq\mathbf{sVect}$, and $\mathcal K$, being nondegenerate of dimension $4=2\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathbf{sVect})$, is a minimal nondegenerate extension of $\mathbf{sVect}$. By injectivity of the Witt-class map for such extensions~\cite[Thm.~5.17 and the nonunitary discussion in \ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi5.3]{Lan:2016rcq}, we identify $\mathcal K$ with the toric code using~\eqref{eq:surj-residual}. In particular, $h_1\cong h_2$ is its unique fermion.

Since the toric code is pointed, we can choose a nontrivial invertible boson $b\in\mathcal K$, namely $e$ or $m$~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi5.2, Eq.~(41)]{Kitaev:2005hzj}. The subcategory $\{\mathbf 1,b\}$ is Tannakian, and it equals its own centralizer because that centralizer has dimension $4/2=2$~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi2.2, Eq.~(5)]{Davydov:2011dqx}, so $\mathbf 1_{\mathcal K}\oplus b$ is a Lagrangian algebra in $\mathcal K$~\cite[Rem.~4.7]{Davydov:2010kfz}. It lifts to a connected \'etale algebra $L=A\oplus U(b)$ in $\mathcal D$~\cite[Prop.~3.16]{Davydov:2010kfz}, where $U$ is the functor that forgets the $A$-module structure. Since $b$ is invertible, $\operatorname{FPdim}(U(b))=\operatorname{FPdim}(A)=d$~\cite[proof of Lem.~3.5]{Davydov:2011dqx}, so $L$ has dimension $2d$ and is Lagrangian in $\mathcal D$.

The parity grading of Lemma~\ref{lem:grading} on each factor gives $\mathcal D$ a $(\mathbb Z/2)^2$-grading with $A\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{rev}}\boxtimes\mathcal C$ in degree $(0,0)$. Since $b$ is a simple $A$-module, $U(b)$ is homogeneous, say of degree $(p,q)$. Its monodromies with $h_1,h_2$, induced from those with $g_1,g_2$, are $(-1)^p,(-1)^q$, both of which equal $-1$ because $h_1\cong h_2$ is the fermion $\varepsilon$ of the toric code, which braids by $-1$ with $e$ and $m$~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi5.2]{Kitaev:2005hzj}. Hence $(p,q)=(1,1)$. Consequently $L$ has $L_{0,0}=A$ as an algebra and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:surj-factor-intersections}
	L\cap(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{rev}}\boxtimes\mathbf 1)=\mathbf 1\boxtimes\mathbf 1=L\cap(\mathbf 1\boxtimes\mathcal E).
\end{equation}

By the classification of connected \'etale algebras in a product~\cite[Thm.~3.6 and Prop.~3.7]{Davydov:2011dqx}, $L$ is therefore isomorphic to the graph algebra $A_H$ of a braided autoequivalence $H$ of $\mathcal E$, because the factor algebras are trivial by~\eqref{eq:surj-factor-intersections} and, since $L$ is Lagrangian, both graph subcategories are the whole factors. The isomorphism $L\cong A_H$ preserves the grading, so it restricts to an algebra isomorphism $A_F=L_{0,0}\cong(A_H)_{0,0}$. The summands of $A_H$ in degree $(0,0)$ are the objects $X^*\boxtimes H(X)$ with $X,H(X)\in\mathcal C$. Their total dimension equals $\operatorname{FPdim}(A_F)=d=\sum_{X\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal C)}\operatorname{FPdim}(X)^2$, so $H(\mathcal C)\subseteq\mathcal C$. Hence $H(\mathcal C)=\mathcal C$, because $H$ maps the finitely many simple objects of $\mathcal C$ injectively to simple objects of $\mathcal C$. Since $\mathcal C$ is closed under tensor products, $(A_H)_{0,0}$ is the graph algebra of $H|_{\mathcal C}$, so
\begin{equation*}
	A_{H|_{\mathcal C}}\cong A_F\qquad\text{as algebras}.
\end{equation*}
By~\cite[Thm.~3.6]{Davydov:2011dqx}, applied in $\mathcal C^{\mathrm{rev}}\boxtimes\mathcal C$, graph algebras of non-isomorphic braided autoequivalences are non-isomorphic. Hence there is a monoidal natural isomorphism $\eta\colon H|_{\mathcal C}\Rightarrow F$.

Given an LR pair $(F,\varphi)$, we take $H$ unit-normalized and put $\widetilde\varphi=\varphi\circ\eta_\psi$. By Remark~\ref{rem:LR-meaning}, $(H|_{\mathcal C},\widetilde\varphi)$ is an LR pair and $\eta$ is an isomorphism of pairs $(H|_{\mathcal C},\widetilde\varphi)\to(F,\varphi)$. Condition~\eqref{eq:vertex} for $(H,\widetilde\varphi)$ reads $e\circ(\widetilde\varphi\otimes\widetilde\varphi)=H(e)\circ J^H_{\psi,\psi}$, which is the same equation as for $(H|_{\mathcal C},\widetilde\varphi)$. Hence $(H,\widetilde\varphi)$ is an LR pair of $\mathcal E$, and
\begin{equation*}
	r[H,\widetilde\varphi]=[H|_{\mathcal C},\widetilde\varphi]=[F,\varphi].
\end{equation*}
The unitary statement follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:unitary}.

\label{rem:surjectivity-literature}
The existence statement of Proposition~\ref{prop:surjectivity} also follows by combining earlier results. We fix the embedding $\iota\colon\mathcal C\hookrightarrow\mathcal E$. Hence there is a minimal nondegenerate extension $\mathcal P$ of $\mathbf{sVect}$ such that
\begin{equation*}
	(\mathcal E,\iota\circ F)\simeq(\mathcal E,\iota)\star\mathcal P
\end{equation*}
as marked extensions. Here $\star$ is the relative product of~\cite[Lem.~4.11 and \ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi5.1]{Lan:2016rcq}, obtained by condensing the algebra $\mathbf 1\boxtimes\mathbf 1\oplus\psi\boxtimes\psi$ in $\mathcal E\boxtimes\mathcal P$. \begin{equation*}
	[\mathcal E]=[\mathcal E\star\mathcal P]=[\mathcal E]+[\mathcal P],\qquad [\mathcal P]=0.
\end{equation*}
The Witt-class map on minimal extensions of $\mathbf{sVect}$ is injective~\cite[Thm.~5.17 and \ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi5.3]{Lan:2016rcq}, so $\mathcal P$ is the toric-code extension $\mathcal Z(\mathbf{sVect})$, which is the unit of the group of minimal nondegenerate extensions of $\mathbf{sVect}$~\cite[Thm.~4.20]{Lan:2016rcq}. Choosing the direction of this equivalence, we obtain $H\colon\mathcal E\to\mathcal E$ with a monoidal isomorphism $H\circ\iota\Rightarrow\iota\circ F$. Transporting the fermion marking as in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:surjectivity}, we obtain a preimage under $r$ of every LR class $[F,\varphi]$.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rem:obstruction}
The Main Theorem determines the coefficients of the second-layer obstruction of~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi VI.A]{Bulmash:2021ryq}, which obstructs lifting a symmetry action on $\mathcal C$ to an action on $\mathcal E$. Let a group $G_b$ act on $\mathcal C$ in the sense of~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi VI.A]{Bulmash:2021ryq}, with classes $[\rho_g]\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal C)$ satisfying $[\rho_g][\rho_h]=[\rho_{gh}]$. By the Main Theorem, each $[\rho_g]$ has a lift $[\check\rho_g]\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal E)$, but the lifts need not satisfy the group law. The failure is measured by
\begin{equation*}
	o_2(g,h)=[\check\rho_{gh}][\check\rho_h]^{-1}[\check\rho_g]^{-1}\in\ker r.
\end{equation*}
Writing $g\cdot m=[\check\rho_g]\,m\,[\check\rho_g]^{-1}$ for $g\in G_b$ and $m\in\ker r$, which lies in $\ker r$ since $\ker r$ is normal, we have $o_2(gh,k)\,o_2(g,h)=o_2(g,hk)\,\big(g\cdot o_2(h,k)\big)$ for $g,h,k\in G_b$ by associativity, so $o_2$ is a $2$-cocycle for this action~\cite[Eqs.~(84) and~(89)]{Bulmash:2021ryq}. A priori, its class lies in $H^2(G_b;\ker r)$, which is a group cohomology group only if $\ker r$ is Abelian, since only then is conjugation by the lifts a well-defined action of $G_b$ on $\ker r$. This is the standing assumption of~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi VI.A]{Bulmash:2021ryq}, and even then the coefficients may be twisted. By the Main Theorem, $\ker r\cong\mathbb Z/2$. Hence $\ker r$ is Abelian, and the conjugation action of $G_b$ on $\ker r$ is trivial, because $\mathbb Z/2$ has no nontrivial automorphisms. Thus $H^2(G_b;\ker r)=H^2(G_b;\mathbb Z/2)$ is group cohomology with trivial coefficients, and $o_2$ defines a class in it. This class vanishes exactly when the lifts can be chosen to form a homomorphism $G_b\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{LR}}(\mathcal E)$. The same identification of coefficient groups applies to the modified obstruction in~\cite[Eqs.~(98)--(100)]{Bulmash:2021ryq}; its vanishing means that the lift classes can be chosen to satisfy the modified multiplication law in~\cite[Eq.~(99)]{Bulmash:2021ryq}.

\label{prop:G1}
Let $\mathcal C\subset\mathcal E$ be a minimal nondegenerate extension.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\pi_1(\mathrm{Mext},\mathcal E)=\{[\![\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal E,\mathrm{id}]\!],[\![\alpha,\mathrm{id}]\!]\}\cong\mathbb Z/2$.
\item The homomorphism $\pi_1(\mathrm{Mext},\mathcal E)\to\ker r$,
$[\![F,\eta]\!]\mapsto[F,\eta_\psi]$, is an isomorphism.
\item The monoidal natural automorphisms of $\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal E$ that restrict to the
identity on $\mathcal C$ are exactly $\mathrm{id}$ and the grading automorphism
$\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon_X=(-1)^{p(X)}\mathrm{id}_X$. Thus the automorphism group of
$\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal E$ in $\mathbf{Ex}_{br}(\mathbb Z/2,\mathcal C)$ is $\mathbb Z/2$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
\label{rem:G1-vs-A}
Proposition~\ref{prop:G1} distinguishes two levels of symmetry data. In the unitary setting, we can view $\mathcal C$ as the original fermionic anyon sector and $\mathcal V$ as the vortex sector introduced by gauging fermion parity.

At the first level, $\pi_1(\mathrm{Mext},\mathcal E)$ classifies braided autoequivalences of $\mathcal E$ whose action on $\mathcal C$ is coherently identified with the identity. There are exactly two classes, represented by the identity and by fermion attachment $\alpha$, which attaches $\psi$ to every vortex. By Proposition~\ref{prop:G1}(2), this group is isomorphic to $\ker r$. A class $[\![F,\eta]\!]$ includes a monoidal natural isomorphism $\eta\colon F|_\mathcal C\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal C$, which records how the transformed anyons in $\mathcal C$ are identified with the original ones, compatibly with fusion. A class $[F,\varphi]\in\ker r$ retains only the fermion identification $\varphi=\eta_\psi$, and requires only that a compatible $\eta$ exist. So we obtain a surjection $[\![F,\eta]\!]\mapsto[F,\eta_\psi]$ by forgetting the other components of $\eta$. It is injective because both groups have exactly two elements, by Proposition~\ref{prop:G1}(1) and the Main Theorem.

At the second level, by Proposition~\ref{prop:G1}(3), the only monoidal natural automorphism of $\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal E$ other than the identity that is trivial on $\mathcal C$ is
\begin{equation*}
	\varepsilon_X=\begin{cases}\mathrm{id}_X, & X\in\mathcal C,\\ -\mathrm{id}_X, & X\in\mathcal V.\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
The common vortex sign is compatible with fusion because two vortices fuse into $\mathcal C$, so their two signs multiply to $+1$. This automorphism leaves every anyon label unchanged and measures an ambiguity in the identification of symmetry actions. In particular, if $\xi\colon F\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}_\mathcal E$ is a monoidal natural isomorphism with $\xi|_\mathcal C=\mathrm{id}$, then the only other such isomorphism is $\varepsilon\circ\xi$. The automorphisms $\mathrm{id}$ and $\varepsilon$ form $\pi_2$ of the 2-groupoid $\mathbf{Ex}_{br}(\mathbb Z/2,\mathcal C)$ at $\mathcal E$.

Thus the two copies of $\mathbb Z/2$ live at different levels. The group $\pi_1$ classifies symmetry actions, whereas $\pi_2$ consists of automorphisms of the identity action, and only $\pi_1$ enters the group extension~\eqref{eq:central-exact} of the Main Theorem. The abstract values $\pi_1=\pi_2=\mathbb Z/2$ are recorded in~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi3]{Johnson-Freyd:2026galois}. In Proposition~\ref{prop:G1}, we give explicit representatives and identify $\pi_1$ with the LR kernel.

\label{rem:DN}
The braided Picard description packages the even and vortex sectors, their fusion, and its coherence data. By~\cite[Thm.~8.18]{Davydov-Nikshych:2021}, $\mathbf{Ex}_{br}(\mathbb Z/2,\mathcal C)$ is 2-equivalent to the 2-groupoid of braided monoidal 2-functors $\mathbb Z/2\to\underline{\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}}_{br}(\mathcal C)$.   \pi_1=\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal Z_2(\mathcal C))=\{\mathbf 1,\psi\},
  \qquad \pi_2=\mathbb C^\times \ .
\]

Proposition~\ref{prop:G1} instead computes the homotopy groups of the \emph{mapping 2-groupoid at $\mathcal E$}, both of which are $\mathbb Z/2$.

The explicit proof explains the distinction. Proposition~\ref{prop:module-type} gives the two module types $R_{\mathbf 1}$ and $R_\psi$, corresponding to identity and fermion attachment. After reduction to the identity module type, Lemma~\ref{lem:rigidity} leaves only a common odd--odd tensorator $\lambda\,\mathrm{id}$. Rescaling the vortex identifications by $t$ changes $\lambda$ to $\lambda/t^2$. Thus $t^2=\lambda$ removes the scalar, while the two choices $\pm t$ differ by $\varepsilon$. The residual $\mathbb Z/2$ therefore describes compatible identifications,
rather than additional symmetry classes.

This interpretation is supplementary; the kernel proof uses only~\cite[\ifmmode\mathsection\else\textsection\fi8.1, Eq.~(8.5)]{Davydov-Nikshych:2021}
through Lemma~\ref{lem:normalization}.

\label{lem:square-sign}
For homogeneous $X,Y$ of parities $p,q$,
\[
\beta_{X\otimes Y}\circ J^{\alpha^2}_{X,Y}
=(-1)^{pq}(\beta_X\otimes\beta_Y).
\]

<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Label the first and second attached powers in $\alpha^2(X)$ by
$\psi^p_{(1)}$ and $\psi^p_{(2)}$, and similarly for $Y$.
For an even object these are formal unit factors, inserted and
removed by the $r$-maps in~\eqref{eq:J-alpha}. By
\eqref{eq:composite},
\[
J^{\alpha^2}_{X,Y}
=\alpha(J^\alpha_{X,Y})\circ J^\alpha_{\alpha(X),\alpha(Y)}.
\]
Expanding~\eqref{eq:J-alpha}, the second attachment moves
$\psi^p_{(2)}$ past $Y$ and $\psi^q_{(1)}$, while the first
attachment moves $\psi^p_{(1)}$ past $Y$. The hexagon axioms
combine the two crossings past $Y$ into the crossing of
$\psi^p\otimes\psi^p$ past $Y$. This crossing disappears upon
contraction by $\mu_{p,p}$: the precise identity is
\[
r_Y\circ(\mathrm{id}_Y\otimes\mu_{p,p})
\circ c_{\psi^p\otimes\psi^p,Y}
=l_Y\circ(\mu_{p,p}\otimes\mathrm{id}_Y).
\]
It follows from naturality of the braiding and
$c_{\mathbf 1,Y}=r_Y^{-1}l_Y$.

The remaining difference from separate contraction on $X$ and
$Y$ is the crossing of $\psi^p_{(2)}$ past
$\psi^q_{(1)}$. By supercommutativity~\eqref{eq:mu-comm} and associativity~\eqref{eq:mu-assoc} of $\mu$, the resulting four-power contraction equals $(-1)^{pq}$ times separate contraction of the
two $p$-powers and the two $q$-powers. By the pentagon and triangle axioms, the intervening reassociations agree with those in $\beta_X\otimes\beta_Y$. Hence the stated equality of morphisms from $\alpha^2(X)\otimes\alpha^2(Y)$ to $X\otimes Y$ holds.
