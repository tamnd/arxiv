<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
There exists an $SO(3)\times\mathbb{Z}_2^T\times\mathbb{Z}_2^R$-invariant, normal matrix product state $\psi$ for any triple of indices $(\sigma_H,\sigma_T,\sigma_R)\in\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$ with injectivity length $l\leq 8$.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
Let $\sigma_H$ be the hidden-order index defined above for the MPS tensor associated to the spin $(\alpha,\beta)$ defining a normal MPS; then
\begin{equation}\sigma_H=(-1)^{2\alpha}=(-1)^{2\beta}\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For each choice of $\alpha,\beta$ let $\mathbb{E}(X)$ be the primitive normalized transfer operator, and let $R,L$ be its respective left and right fixed points. Let $$Z=\mathrm{diag}(S_{\alpha}^z\otimes \mathbbm{1}_4,S_{\beta}^z\otimes\mathbbm{1}_4),\quad V(\theta)=e^{-i\theta Z}$$
then \begin{equation}
e^{-i\theta i}A^i=V(\theta)A^iV(\theta)^{-1},\quad V(2\pi)=(-1)^{2\alpha}=(-1)^{2\beta}
\end{equation}
Defining ${\mathcal L}_V(X)=VX$, the transfer operator with a twist is $\mathbb{E}_{\theta}=\mathcal{L}_{V(\theta)}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{L}_{V(\theta)^{-1}}$. On sites $0,...,N$ with $N=2l+1$ with $\Delta=\frac{2\pi}{N}$ we have
\begin{equation}
\omega(V_l)=(-1)^{2\alpha}\mathrm{tr}\left[L(\mathbb{E}\mathcal{L}_{V(\Delta)})^N(R)\right]
\end{equation}
The leading simple eigenvalue $\lambda(\Delta)$ of $\mathbb{E}\mathcal{L}_{V(\Delta)}$ has the properties \begin{equation}
\lambda(0)=1,\quad \lambda'(0)=-i\mathrm{tr}(LZR)=0
\end{equation}
with the last equality following from $z$-rotation invariance of the fixed point, and so $\lambda(\Delta)^N\to 1$ and thus $\omega(V_l)\to\sigma_H=(-1)^{2\alpha}=(-1)^{2\beta}$.

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Define \begin{equation}j=\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0
\end{bmatrix}, \quad p=-\sigma_H\sigma_R
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
J&=\begin{cases}
\mathbbm{1}_4,& \sigma_H\sigma_T=1,\\
\mathrm{diag}(j,j), & \sigma_H\sigma_T=-1
\end{cases},\\
K&=\begin{cases}
\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1,-1),& \sigma_H\sigma_R=1,\\
\mathrm{diag}(j,-j), & \sigma_H\sigma_R=-1
\end{cases}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
We note $JK=KJ$.
The entries of $A$ lie in $\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{30},i,\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3},\sqrt{5},\sqrt{7}]$ with denominators not divisible by the prime $1009$ which is the smallest prime $p$ where all the prime roots have a integer solution $\mod p$. This fact can be worked out from the rational Racah formula, the proof of which we elide, as one can also square each term in the Clebsch-Gordan tensors to see this. We map the matrices under the homomorphism to $\mathbb{F}_{1009}$ given by \begin{equation}
\begin{split}
i&\mapsto 469, \quad \sqrt{2}\mapsto 439,\quad \sqrt{3}\mapsto 149,\\
\sqrt{5}&\mapsto 244, \quad \sqrt{7}\mapsto 45
\end{split}
\end{equation}
We choose random matrices with coefficients in the set $\{-3,-2,...,2,3; -3i,-2i,...,2i,3i\}$:
\begin{align*}
 Z_1&=\begin{pmatrix}2&3&3&0\\3&3&3&-3\\0&1&-2&-1\\1&2&1&-2\end{pmatrix}
 +i\begin{pmatrix}1&3&-2&0\\-1&3&-3&0\\3&0&-3&2\\3&3&3&-1\end{pmatrix},\\[4pt]
 Z_2&=\begin{pmatrix}2&3&1&3\\1&-2&0&1\\-1&1&-1&3\\-2&1&-3&-3\end{pmatrix}
 +i\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\2&0&-1&3\\3&-1&-2&-2\\-2&0&1&1\end{pmatrix},\\[4pt]
 Z_3&=\begin{pmatrix}3&3&0&1\\-3&-2&3&3\\-3&0&0&1\\-3&0&-1&-2\end{pmatrix}
 +i\begin{pmatrix}2&1&-2&2\\0&-2&1&0\\0&-1&3&-2\\3&-2&2&3\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align*}and let \begin{equation}
X_k=Z_k+J\overline{Z}_kJ^{-1},\quad k=1,2,3
\end{equation}
Then an explicit injective solution is given by defining the multiplicity matrices:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
M_{\alpha,\alpha}&=X_1-pKX_1^TK^{-1},\\
M_{\beta,\beta}&=X_2-pKX_2^TK^{-1}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
M_{\alpha,\beta}&=X_3,\\
M_{\beta,\alpha}&=p\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha+1}{2\beta+1}}KX_3^TK^{-1}
\end{split}
\end{equation} and $C^i_{\alpha,\beta}$ etc. determined by the desired $\sigma_H$.
