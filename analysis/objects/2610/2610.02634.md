<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{th:MREpdeexistence}
For a given $f\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$  and $B\in L^\infty(\Omega)$, there exists an unique solution of \eqref{eq:cplxPDE} $u\in H^1(\Omega).$ Furthermore, the following stability estimate is satisfied
\[
\|u\|_{H^1(\Omega)} \leq C_1 \|f\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)},
\]
where $C_1$ is a constant dependent on $\|B\|_2$. Furthermore, if $B$ is uniformly Lipschitz continuous and $\partial\Omega\in C^2$, then we have 
$u\in H^2(\Omega)$ and 
\[
\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)} \leq C_2 \|f\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)},
\]
where $C_2$ is a constant dependent on $\|B\|_2$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{th:inverse}
Let $B(x)\in C^{0,1}(\bar{\Omega})\cap H^{n/2+4+\epsilon}(\bar{\Omega}),$ with $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$. Then for $I = I_n = \dfrac{1}{2}n(n+3)$, corresponding to the $I$ boundary conditions $(f^j)_{1\leq j \leq I}$, the solutions $(u^j)_{1\leq j \leq I}$ of \eqref{eq:cplxPDE} uniquely determine $B(x),$ where $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$. Furthermore, we have the following estimate
\[
\|B\|_{W^{0,\infty}(\Omega)} \leq D \|(u^1,\cdots,u^I)\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)}.
\]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The aforementioned Theorem \ref{th:inverse} implies that in one dimension, two pairs of boundary and interior datum guarantees unique recovery of $B$, while in two dimensions, five pairs of boundary and interior datum guarantees unique recovery of $B$. However, these are not optimal estimates as demonstrated in \cite[Section 4]{Bal2012}. Specifically, one isotropic coefficient $B(x)$ can be reconstructed from one measurement in any dimensions.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{proposition3}
The objective functional $J$ given in \eqref{eq:cplxcostfn}
is sequentially weakly lower semicontinuous (w.l.s.c.) on $L^2(\Omega)$;
that is, if
$
B_k\rightharpoonup B
\quad\text{weakly in }L^2(\Omega),
$
then
\[
J(B)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}J(B_k).
\]
Moreover, $J$ is coercive on $L^2(\Omega)$, in the sense that
\[
\|B_k\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\to\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
J(B_k)\to\infty,
\]
and $J$ is Fr\'echet differentiable.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The coercivity property of $J$ follows from the presence of the terms $\|B_i\|_2$. For proving the w.l.s.c property, we first note that $J$ are not convex in $B$. However, $U_{ad}$ is convex and closed and also weakly sequentially compact.
This is because if $\{B^n\}\in U_{ad}$, then $\|B^n\|_{L^2}\leq D$ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$. Thus, the sequence is uniformly bounded. Since $L^2(\Omega)$ is a Hilbert space, there is a  subsequence $\{B^{n_k}\}$ such that $B^{n_k}\rightharpoonup B\in L^2(\Omega)$. Also, we have that $\|B\|_{L^2}\leq \lim\inf\|B^n\|_{L^2}\leq D$. Thus, $B\in U_{ad}$. Next, we consider the set 
\[
U_{\eta} = \lbrace B \in U_{ad}: J(B) \leq \eta \rbrace.
\]
Since $J$ is continuous, the set $U_{\eta}$ is closed for all $\eta \in \mathbb{R}$. Thus, $U_{\eta}$ is a closed subset of a weakly sequentially compact space $U_{ad}$ and is weakly sequentially closed for all $\eta\in\mathbb{R}$ \cite{Beer1992}.
This implies that $J$ is weakly sequentially lower semi-continuous. Finally, the Fr\'echet differentiability of $J$ follows from similar arguments as given in \cite{Annunziato2013}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{th:existence}
Let
$
f^j\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega),~ j=1,\ldots,I.
$
Denote $u^j(B)$ as the unique solution of \eqref{eq:cplxPDE_multi}, for every $B\in U_{ad}$ and each prescribed Dirichlet
boundary datum $f^j$, which satisfies the stability estimate
of Theorem~\ref{th:MREpdeexistence}. Let $J$ be the multiple-data objective functional defined in
\eqref{eq:cplxcostfn}. Then there exists
$B^*\in U_{ad}$
and an associated collection of states
$\mathbf{u}^*=(u^{1,*},\ldots,u^{I,*})$
such that each $u^{j,*}=u^j(B^*)$ solves
\eqref{eq:cplxPDE_multi} with boundary datum $f^j$, and
\[
J(\mathbf{u}^*,B^*)
=
\min_{B\in U_{ad}}J(\mathbf{u}(B),B),
\]
where
\[
\mathbf{u}(B)
=
(u^1(B),\ldots,u^I(B)).
\]
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since all terms in the objective functional are nonnegative,
\[
J(\mathbf{u}(B),B)\geq 0,
\]
and hence the infimum of $J$ over $U_{ad}$ is finite. Let
$\{B^m\}\subset U_{ad}$ be a minimizing sequence; that is,
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}
J(\mathbf{u}(B^m),B^m)
=
\inf_{B\in U_{ad}}
J(\mathbf{u}(B),B).
\]
By the definition of $U_{ad}$,
\[
\|B^m\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\leq D
\]
for every $m$, and therefore $\{B^m\}$ is bounded in
$L^2(\Omega;\mathbb{C})$. Since $L^2(\Omega;\mathbb{C})$ is a
Hilbert space, there exists a subsequence, again denoted by
$\{B^m\}$, and some $B^*\in L^2(\Omega;\mathbb{C})$ such that
\[
B^m\rightharpoonup B^*
\qquad\text{weakly in }L^2(\Omega;\mathbb{C}).
\]
Moreover, $U_{ad}$ is closed and convex and is therefore weakly
closed. Consequently,
\[
B^*\in U_{ad}.
\]
For each $j=1,\ldots,I$, let
\[
u^{j,m}=u^j(B^m)
\]
denote the solution of \eqref{eq:cplxPDE_multi} corresponding to
$B^m$ and the fixed boundary datum $f^j$. By the stability estimate
of Theorem~\ref{th:MREpdeexistence}, the sequence
$\{u^{j,m}\}_m$ is bounded in $H^2(\Omega)$. Since $I$ is finite, a
diagonal subsequence may be selected such that, for every
$j=1,\ldots,I$,
\[
u^{j,m}\rightharpoonup u^{j,*}
\qquad\text{weakly in }H^2(\Omega).
\]
By the Rellich--Kondrachov compactness theorem,
\[
H^2(\Omega)\Subset H^1(\Omega),
\]
under the regularity assumptions on $\Omega$, and hence
\[
u^{j,m}\to u^{j,*}
\qquad\text{strongly in }H^1(\Omega).
\]
In particular,
\[
u^{j,m}\to u^{j,*}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\nabla u^{j,m}\to\nabla u^{j,*}
\qquad\text{strongly in }L^2(\Omega).
\]
We next show that $u^{j,*}$ satisfies the state equation corresponding
to $B^*$. For any $v\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, the weak formulation
satisfied by $u^{j,m}$ is
\[
\int_\Omega
\big((1+i)+B^m\big)
\nabla u^{j,m}\cdot\nabla v\,dx
-
\int_\Omega
\rho\omega^2u^{j,m}v\,dx
=0.
\]
For the second term, strong convergence in $L^2(\Omega)$ gives
\[
\int_\Omega
\rho\omega^2u^{j,m}v\,dx
\longrightarrow
\int_\Omega
\rho\omega^2u^{j,*}v\,dx.
\]
For the coefficient-dependent term, write
\[
\begin{aligned}
&
\int_\Omega
B^m\nabla u^{j,m}\cdot\nabla v\,dx
-
\int_\Omega
B^*\nabla u^{j,*}\cdot\nabla v\,dx
\\
&\quad =
\int_\Omega
B^m
\big(\nabla u^{j,m}-\nabla u^{j,*}\big)
\cdot\nabla v\,dx
+
\int_\Omega
(B^m-B^*)
\nabla u^{j,*}\cdot\nabla v\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $\{B^m\}$ is bounded in $L^2(\Omega)$,
$\nabla u^{j,m}\to\nabla u^{j,*}$ strongly in $L^2(\Omega)$, and
$\nabla v\in L^\infty(\Omega)$, the first term on the right tends to
zero. Furthermore,
\[
\nabla u^{j,*}\cdot\nabla v\in L^2(\Omega),
\]
so the weak convergence
$B^m\rightharpoonup B^*$ in $L^2(\Omega)$ implies that the second
term also tends to zero. Consequently,
\[
\int_\Omega
B^m\nabla u^{j,m}\cdot\nabla v\,dx
\longrightarrow
\int_\Omega
B^*\nabla u^{j,*}\cdot\nabla v\,dx.
\]
Together with the strong convergence of $\nabla u^{j,m}$, this yields
\[
\int_\Omega
\big((1+i)+B^*\big)
\nabla u^{j,*}\cdot\nabla v\,dx
-
\int_\Omega
\rho\omega^2u^{j,*}v\,dx
=0
\]
for every $v\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$.

Now, the trace operator is continuous from $H^1(\Omega)$ to
$H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$. Since
\[
u^{j,m}=f^j
\qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega
\]
for every $m$ and
$u^{j,m}\to u^{j,*}$ in $H^1(\Omega)$, it follows that
\[
u^{j,*}=f^j
\qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega.
\]
Hence $u^{j,*}$ is the solution of the state equation corresponding
to $B^*$ and the boundary datum $f^j$, for each $j=1,\ldots,I$.

Finally, by the sequential weak lower semicontinuity of the objective
functional,
\[
\begin{aligned}
J(\mathbf{u}^*,B^*)
&\leq
\liminf_{m\to\infty}
J(\mathbf{u}(B^m),B^m)
\\
&=
\inf_{B\in U_{ad}}
J(\mathbf{u}(B),B).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $B^*\in U_{ad}$, the reverse inequality follows from the
definition of the infimum. Therefore,
\[
J(\mathbf{u}^*,B^*)
=
\min_{B\in U_{ad}}
J(\mathbf{u}(B),B),
\]
and $(\mathbf{u}^*,B^*)$ is a minimization pair.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
The first order optimality system corresponding to the solution of \eqref{eq:cplxcostfn}-\eqref{eq:cplxPDE_multi} is the same as the first order optimality system corresponding to the solution of \eqref{eq:costfn}-\eqref{eq:PDE}.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->

Since the cost functional \( J(u, B_1, B_2) \) is real-valued, and optimization must be performed over real-valued functionals, we define the Lagrangian as  \begin{equation}\label{eq: cplxlagr}
\mathcal{L} = J(u, B_1, B_2) + \operatorname{Re}\left\{ \langle F(u, A), \bar{q} \rangle_C \right\} 
\end{equation}   
where \(F(u,B) = F_1(u,B) + iF_2(u,B)\) denotes the solution of the complex-valued PDE \eqref{eq:cplxPDE_multi}, \( q = q_1 + iq_2 \) is the corresponding complex-valued adjoint variable, and $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle_C$ is the complex inner product in $L^2(\Omega)$. Simplifying \eqref{eq: cplxlagr}, we obtain:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cplxlagr2}
\mathcal{L} = J(u, B_1, B_2) +  \langle F_1(u, B), q_1 \rangle + \langle F_2(u, B), q_2 \rangle,
\end{equation}
where $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle$ is the standard inner product in $L^2(\Omega)$.
Using the Gatueaux derivative, the adjoint partial differential equations are given as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cplxadjPDE}
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
\nabla \cdot \left( (1+B_1)\nabla q_1 - (1+B_2) \nabla q_2 \right) + \rho\omega^2 q_1 &= -\alpha(u_1 - u_1^\delta), \\[1.5ex]
\nabla \cdot \left( (1+B_1)\nabla q_2 + (1+B_2) \nabla q_1 \right) + \rho\omega^2 q_2 &= -\alpha(u_2 - u_2^\delta),
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{equation}
for all \(q_1,\, q_2 \in H_f^1(\Omega)\).
In a similar way, the optimality conditions are the given as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cplxopti}
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial B_1} &= \beta_1B_1 - \nabla u_1\cdot\nabla q_1 - \nabla u_2\cdot\nabla q_2, \\[1.5ex]
\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial B_2}&= \beta_2B_2 + \nabla u_2\cdot\nabla q_1 - \nabla u_1\cdot\nabla q_2,
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{equation}
On the other hand, if we separate \(F(u,B)\) into its real and imaginary parts and combine them in a system of PDEs, then the Lagrangian we obtain \(\mathcal{L}\) is given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:systlagr}
\mathcal{L} = J(u, B_1, B_2) +  \langle F_1(u, B), q_1 \rangle + \langle F_2(u, B), q_2 \rangle.
\end{equation}
As the Lagrangian obtained in \eqref{eq:systlagr} is identical to equation \eqref{eq:cplxlagr2}, therefore the adjoint equations \(\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial u_1}\) and \(\dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial u_2}\) obtained are same as \eqref{eq: cplxlagr}. Similarly, the optimality conditions in this case are same as \eqref{eq:cplxopti}.
