<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:ah-main}
Let $\mathcal P=(A_i,\phi_i)$ be a unital inductive system of finite
direct sums of homogeneous corners over connected compact metrizable
spaces, with ordinary globally diagonal maps as in
Definition~\ref{def:ah}. Suppose that $A=\varinjlim\mathcal P$ is simple.
For the mean dimension $\mathrm{mdim}(A_i, \phi_i)$ of
\cite[Definition 3.6]{NiuAH}, recalled in Definition~\ref{def:gamma},
\[
 \operatorname{rc}(A)=\frac12\mathrm{mdim}(A_i, \phi_i)
 \qquad\text{in }[0,\infty].
\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:actions-main}
Let $(X, \Gamma)$ be a free and minimal dynamical system, where $\Gamma$ is a discrete amenable group, and $X$ is a compact metrizable space. If $(X, \Gamma)$ has URP, then
\[
 \operatorname{rc}(C(X)\rtimes\Gamma) \geq \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{mdim}(X,\Gamma).
\]
If $(X, \Gamma)$ also has COS, then
\[
 \operatorname{rc}(C(X)\rtimes\Gamma)
 =\frac12\operatorname{mdim}(X,\Gamma)
 \qquad\text{in }[0,\infty].
\]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:weighted-coordinate-compression}
Let $\mathcal I$ be a finite set, let $d_i\geq 0$ be integers, and let
$q\geq 0$ be an integer.  There is a complex linear map
\[
 C:\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal I}\mathbb C^{d_i}\longrightarrow
 \mathbb C^q
\]
whose restriction to each coordinate subspace
$\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda}\mathbb C^{d_i}$ with
$\sum_{i\in\Lambda}d_i\leq q$ is injective.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For each such $\Lambda$ with positive total dimension, failure of
injectivity is a proper determinantal algebraic subset of the space of
all linear maps to $\mathbb C^q$: it is proper because that particular
coordinate subspace has dimension at most $q$.  There are only finitely
many subsets $\Lambda$.  A finite union of proper algebraic subsets
cannot exhaust this complex vector space.  Any map outside the union
works.  Zero-dimensional coordinate subspaces impose no condition; in
particular the assertion for $q=0$ is immediate.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{input:compression}
Let $p,n\geq1$ be integers, put $t=2p+1$, and choose $L,\mathcal H$ as above. Let $Y$ be compact metrizable, $0<a<1/16$, and $f:Y\to C_t^n$ continuous. Suppose there is one continuous complex bundle monomorphism
\[
 \mathcal E_f\longrightarrow
 \mathcal T(f,Z_{an})\times\mathbb C^q,
 \qquad q\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0},\quad q<2pn,
\]
on the entire compact sparse-parameter locus. Then there is an integer $u\geq1$ and a continuous map $G:Y^u\to C_t^{un}$ such that at every point at least $(a/8)un$ slots are on $\partial C_t$, and each original boundary slot of $f^{\times u}$ is preserved as an entire vector. The cited theorem allows $u$ arbitrarily large; only existence is needed here.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:packets}
Let $S$ be as above, fix $t\geq1$, and put $g=\lfloor(m+1)/(t+1)\rfloor$. There are continuous maps $\mathfrak f_j:S\to C_t$, $1\leq j\leq g$, with this property: given a continuous $y:Y\to S$ and continuous $v_j:Y\to C_t$ satisfying
\[
 v_j(z)=\mathfrak f_j(y(z))\quad\text{when }\mathfrak f_j(y(z))\in\partial C_t,
\]
there is a continuous $\widetilde y:Y\to S$ with vertex support contained in the original vertex support and satisfying
\[
 \dim\operatorname{car}(\widetilde y(z))\leq
 m-|\{j:v_j(z)\in\partial C_t\}|.
\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Fix a boundary-preserving homeomorphism $\kappa:\Delta^t\to C_t$. Let $c_\ell$ be the sum of vertex coordinates of color $\ell$. Split the first $(t+1)g$ colors into packets, with packet weight vector $w_j$ and total mass $M_j$. Set $b=(1/(t+1),\ldots,1/(t+1))$, and choose $0<\tau<1/(4(m+1))$. The continuous simplex vectors
\[
 q_j=\begin{cases}w_j/M_j,&M_j\geq\tau,\\
 b+(w_j-M_jb)/\tau,&M_j\leq\tau
 \end{cases}
\]
give $\mathfrak f_j=\kappa\circ q_j$.

Choose $\eta:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ continuous, zero on $[0,\tau]$ and one on $[2\tau,1]$. Replace packet $j$ by the unnormalized vector $\eta(M_j)M_j\kappa^{-1}(v_j)$ and leave leftover colors unchanged. Let $\widehat c_\ell$ be the resulting color weights, with sum $Z$. Then $Z\geq1-2g\tau>0$. If an original color is absent, its new weight is zero: when the packet survives, $M_j>\tau$ and its original normalized vector is on the simplex boundary, so the boundary-preservation assumption fixes that vector.

For a vertex $v$ of color $\ell$, define its new coordinate to be
\[
 \widetilde\lambda_v=\begin{cases}
 \lambda_v\widehat c_\ell/(c_\ell Z),&c_\ell>0,\\
 0,&c_\ell=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
At $c_\ell=0$ this is continuous: $\lambda_v/c_\ell\leq1$, whereas $\widehat c_\ell\to0$ and $Z$ is bounded below. The new coordinates sum to one and use only original vertices, so they define a point of $S$. Each boundary output removes at least one color from its packet, or the packet is discarded entirely. The packets are disjoint and each color occurs at most once in a carrier, giving the asserted bound.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:ah}
An ordinary diagonal corner AH presentation is a unital inductive
system $\mathcal P=(A_i,\phi_{i,i+1})$ with
\[
 A_i=\bigoplus_{s=1}^{h_i}
       p_{i,s}M_{r_{i,s}}(C(X_{i,s}))p_{i,s},
 \qquad A=\varinjlim_i A_i.
\]
Here $h_i,r_{i,s}$ are positive integers, $X_{i,s}$ is a nonempty
compact connected metrizable space, and $p_{i,s}$ is a continuous
projection of nonzero constant rank $n_{i,s}$.  Write $E_{i,s}$ for
its range bundle.  Injectivity of the connecting maps is not assumed.

For every adjacent pair of stages and every target summand there is
a finite occurrence list of continuous eigenmaps, each with a specified
source summand, and a global unitary bundle identification
\[
 E_{i+1,k}\cong
 \bigoplus_{s=1}^{h_i}\ \bigoplus_{a=1}^{m_{i,i+1}^{s,k}}
       (\lambda_{i,i+1}^{s,k,a})^*E_{i,s},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{i,i+1}^{s,k,a}:X_{i+1,k}\longrightarrow X_{i,s}.
\]
Under this identification, the connecting map is the direct sum of
the operators $f_s(\lambda_{i,i+1}^{s,k,a}(x))$.  In particular,
there are no extra multiplicity bundles or twisting projections.
This is the ordinary diagonal class of \cite{NiuAH}, as distinguished
from the generalized diagonal maps in its Section~7.

We fix these adjacent occurrence lists and obtain the lists for
$\phi_{i,j}$ by composition and concatenation along all paths.
Occurrences are retained separately even when their eigenmaps agree.
Thus for $j\geq i$ the target bundle $E_{j,k}$ is globally identified
with the direct sum of
$(\lambda_{i,j}^{s,k,a})^*E_{i,s}$, where
$1\leq a\leq m_{i,j}^{s,k}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ah-rank-identity}
 n_{j,k}=\sum_{s=1}^{h_i}m_{i,j}^{s,k}n_{i,s}.
\end{equation}
The identity map at a stage has one identity occurrence for each
summand.  The bundle identifications for later maps are those induced
by the adjacent identifications.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:ah-cover-monotonicity}
For a fixed stage $i$ and cover $\alpha$, the finite numbers
\[
 b_j(\alpha)=\max_{1\leq k\leq h_j}
      \frac{\mathcal D(\alpha_{i,j,k})}{n_{j,k}},
 \qquad j\geq i,
\]
form a nonincreasing sequence.  Consequently
$d_i(\alpha)=\lim_{j\to\infty}b_j(\alpha)$ exists in $[0,\infty)$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For $\ell\geq j\geq i$, coherence of the occurrence lists gives
\[
 \alpha_{i,\ell,t}
  =\bigvee_{k=1}^{h_j}\ \bigvee_{a=1}^{m_{j,\ell}^{k,t}}
      (\lambda_{j,\ell}^{k,t,a})^{-1}\alpha_{i,j,k}.
\]
Subadditivity and the pullback inequality yield
\[
 \mathcal D(\alpha_{i,\ell,t})
 \leq\sum_k m_{j,\ell}^{k,t}
             \mathcal D(\alpha_{i,j,k})
 \leq b_j(\alpha)\sum_km_{j,\ell}^{k,t}n_{j,k}
 =b_j(\alpha)n_{\ell,t}.
\]
Taking the maximum over $t$ proves $b_\ell(\alpha)\leq b_j(\alpha)$.
The sequence is nonnegative and bounded by $b_i(\alpha)<\infty$.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:gamma}
Following \cite[Definition~3.6]{NiuAH}, put
\[
 \gamma_i(\mathcal P)=\sup_{\alpha}d_i(\alpha)
  =\sup_{\alpha}\lim_{j\to\infty}
       \max_k\frac{\mathcal D(\alpha_{i,j,k})}{n_{j,k}},
 \qquad
 \gamma(\mathcal P)= \mathrm{mdim}(A_i, \phi_{i, i+1})=\sup_i\gamma_i(\mathcal P).
\]
The supremum is over the finite open covers just described.  The
normalizing number is the rank of the unit projection, not the size
$r_{j,k}$ of its ambient matrix algebra.  These quantities may be
infinite, although $d_i(\alpha)$ is finite for each fixed cover.

The sequence $\gamma_i(\mathcal P)$ is nondecreasing: the propagated
tuple $(\alpha_{i,i+1,k})_k$ is a cover at stage $i+1$, and its later
propagations are exactly those of $\alpha$.  Hence equivalently
$\gamma(\mathcal P)=\lim_i\gamma_i(\mathcal P)$.  We retain the
presentation in the notation; no invariance under changing the
presentation is assumed in the definition.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:rank-growth}
If the unital limit $A$ is simple and infinite dimensional, then
\[
 \min_{1\leq s\leq h_i}n_{i,s}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
This conclusion does not require injectivity of the connecting maps.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Suppose otherwise.  There are arbitrarily late stages with a summand
of rank at most some fixed integer $N$.  Passing to a subsequence of
those stages, the summand ranks have one constant value
$n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$.  Choose a point in each such summand and an
identification of its fiber with $\mathbb C^n$.  Evaluation gives a
unital homomorphism $\rho_j:A_j\to M_n$.

For each fixed $i$, all sufficiently late such evaluations restrict
to unital homomorphisms $\rho_j\phi_{i,j}:A_i\to M_n$.
The space $H_i=\operatorname{Hom}_1(A_i,M_n)$ is compact in the
pointwise norm topology: it is a closed subset of the product of
the compact matrix balls
$\prod_{a\in A_i}\{z\in M_n:\|z\|\leq\|a\|\}$, since linearity,
multiplicativity, preservation of adjoints, and preservation of the
unit are closed conditions.  Each $H_i$ is nonempty by the late
evaluations.  In the compact product $\prod_i H_i$, the connecting
relations
\[
 \rho_i=\rho_{i+1}\phi_{i,i+1}
\]
have the finite intersection property: any finitely many relations
are satisfied by restricting one sufficiently late evaluation, with
arbitrary elements of $H_i$ chosen at the remaining stages.
There is therefore a compatible family of unital homomorphisms
$\rho_i:A_i\to M_n$.  It induces a unital homomorphism
$\rho:A\to M_n$ by the universal property of the inductive limit.
Simplicity makes $\rho$ injective, which is impossible for an
infinite-dimensional algebra mapping into $M_n$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:finite-limit}
If the simple unital limit of an ordinary diagonal presentation is
finite dimensional, then
\[
 A\cong M_N,\qquad \gamma(\mathcal P)=0,
 \qquad \operatorname{rc}(A)=0.
\]
No injectivity of the connecting maps is required.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
A finite-dimensional simple unital algebra is a full matrix algebra.
Fix a stage $i$ and a cover $\alpha=(\alpha_s)_s$.  The
finite-dimensional representation $\phi_{i,\infty}:A_i\to M_N$
decomposes as a finite direct sum of point-evaluation representations.
For each source summand let $S_s\subset X_{i,s}$ be its finite set
of evaluation points; this set can be empty.  If $S_s$ is nonempty,
choose pairwise disjoint open neighborhoods of its distinct points,
each contained in a member of $\alpha_s$, and let $U_s$ be their
union.  Choose $f_s:X_{i,s}\to[0,1]$ continuous, zero on $S_s$
and one on $X_{i,s}\setminus U_s$.  If $S_s$ is empty, take
$f_s=1$.  The central positive element
\[
 f=\bigoplus_s f_sp_{i,s}\in A_i
\]
has image zero in $A$.  The inductive-limit norm formula gives
$\|\phi_{i,j}(f)\|\to0$, also for noninjective systems.

For all sufficiently late $j$ this norm is less than $1/2$.
The diagonal description then implies
\[
 f_s(\lambda_{i,j}^{s,k,a}(x))<1/2
 \qquad(x\in X_{j,k})
\]
for every eigenmap occurrence.  There are consequently no occurrences
from a source summand with $S_s=\varnothing$.  Every remaining
eigenmap has its range in $U_s$.  Since $X_{j,k}$ is connected,
that range lies in one of the pairwise disjoint neighborhoods making
up $U_s$, and hence in one member of $\alpha_s$.  Its pullback
cover therefore contains the whole set $X_{j,k}$ as a member.
The joined cover $\alpha_{i,j,k}$ also contains $X_{j,k}$, so
$\mathcal D(\alpha_{i,j,k})=0$ for every target summand at all
sufficiently late stages.  Thus $d_i(\alpha)=0$ for every $i$ and
$\alpha$, proving $\gamma(\mathcal P)=0$.

Finally, in matrix algebras Cuntz comparison of positive elements is
exactly comparison of their ranks.  The unique normalized trace on
$M_N$, extended with unnormalized matrix traces to amplifications,
has dimension function $\operatorname{rank}(a)/N$.  The strict
trace inequality therefore implies Cuntz subequivalence, and
$\operatorname{rc}(M_N)=0$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:ah-frames}
Let $P\in M_d(C(X))$ be a continuous projection of constant rank
$n>0$, and let $E=\operatorname{ran}P$.  For every integer
$b\geq0$ and every $D\geq db$, the bundle $E^{\oplus D}$
contains a continuously framed trivial subbundle of rank $b$.
Equivalently, there is a continuous matrix
\[
 J:X\longrightarrow M_{Dd,b}(\mathbb C),\qquad
 J(x)^*J(x)=1_b,\quad P(x)^{\oplus D}J(x)=J(x).
\]
Consequently finitely many source blocks and finitely many prescribed
frame ranks can be accommodated in one common external amplification.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
If $e_1,\ldots,e_d$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb C^d$, the section
\[
 \sigma(x)=\frac1{\sqrt n}
       (P(x)e_1,\ldots,P(x)e_d)\in E_x^{\oplus d}
\]
is continuous and has norm one, since
\[
 \sum_{a=1}^d\|P(x)e_a\|^2=\operatorname{Tr}(P(x))=n.
\]
Place $b$ copies of this section in disjoint groups of $d$ summands.
They form an orthonormal frame in $E^{\oplus db}$; adding
zero coordinates gives the stated frame in any larger amplification.
For $b=0$ the assertion is vacuous.  A maximum of the finitely many
required amplification sizes gives a common choice.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:ah-lift}
Let $a\in M_{D_0}(A_j)_+$ and $b\in M_{D_1}(A_j)_+$.  If their
images satisfy $a\precsim b$ in $M_\infty(A)$, then for every
$\epsilon>0$ and every prescribed index $h_0\geq j$ there are
$h\geq h_0$ and a rectangular matrix
\[
 V\in M_{D_1,D_0}(A_h)
\]
such that
\[
 \big\|V^*\phi_{j,h}(b)V-\phi_{j,h}(a)\big\|<\epsilon.
\]
The statement does not require injective connecting maps.  Once this
inequality holds, it continues to hold after pushing the same witness
to any later stage.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Denote the canonical maps into the limit by $\iota_i$.  Cuntz
subequivalence gives a rectangular $v\in M_{D_1,D_0}(A)$ with
\[
 \|v^*\iota_j(b)v-\iota_j(a)\|<\epsilon/3.
\]
One may obtain this rectangular matrix by taking the source and target
corners of a witness in a common matrix amplification, with $a$ and
$b$ padded by zeros.  Approximate $v$ by $\iota_l(v_l)$ for some
$l\geq j$ and $v_l\in M_{D_1,D_0}(A_l)$, sufficiently closely that
\[
 \|\iota_l(v_l)^*\iota_j(b)\iota_l(v_l)-\iota_j(a)\|
 <2\epsilon/3.
\]
Put $c_l=v_l^*\phi_{j,l}(b)v_l-\phi_{j,l}(a)$.  The
inductive-limit norm formula, valid also for noninjective systems, is
\[
 \|\iota_l(c_l)\|=\lim_{h\to\infty}\|\phi_{l,h}(c_l)\|.
\]
Choose $h\geq\max\{l,h_0\}$ with the last norm less than
$\epsilon$ and set $V=\phi_{l,h}(v_l)$.  Contractivity of all
subsequent connecting maps proves the final assertion.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:ah-embedding}
Let $r\geq0$ be a comparison bound for $A$.  Fix $p\geq1$,
$t=2p+1$, and $0<a<1/16$, and use the torus data
$L,\mathcal H,Q$ of Theorem~\ref{input:compression}.  At source stage
$j$, fix integers $g_s,R_s\geq0$ and continuous packet maps
\[
 F_{s,\ell}:X_s\longrightarrow C_t=[-1,1]^t,
 \qquad 1\leq\ell\leq g_s,
\]
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ah-source-trace-gap}
 \frac{R_s-pg_s}{n_s}>r\qquad\text{for every source block }s.
\end{equation}
There is a witness stage $h\geq j$, which may be required to lie
beyond any prescribed index, with the following property.  For each
block $k$ of $A_h$, put
\[
 N_k=\sum_s m_{j,h}^{s,k}g_s,
 \qquad q_k=\sum_s m_{j,h}^{s,k}R_s.
\]
For $N_k\geq1$, form $f_k:X_{h,k}\to C_t^{N_k}$ by concatenating
\[
 F_{s,\ell}\circ\lambda_{s,k,\alpha},
 \qquad (s,\alpha,\ell),\quad
 1\leq\alpha\leq m_{j,h}^{s,k},\quad1\leq\ell\leq g_s,
\]
in a fixed occurrence order.  Then there is one continuous complex
bundle monomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ah-entire-locus-embedding}
 \mathcal E_{f_k}\longrightarrow
 \mathcal T(f_k,Z_{aN_k}(t,N_k))\times
             \mathbb C^{\,q_k+2p\lceil aN_k\rceil}.
\end{equation}
Here the source has rank $pN_k$, and the monomorphism is defined
simultaneously on the entire sparse torus locus.  Thus
Theorem~\ref{input:compression} applies with $Y=X_{h,k}$, $n=N_k$,
and $f=f_k$ whenever
\[
 q_k+2p\lceil aN_k\rceil<2pN_k.
\]
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
\emph{Positive elements with a uniform tracial gap.}
Choose a tubular neighborhood $U$ of $L$ in $(-1,1)^t$, a retraction
$\pi_L:U\to L$, and a continuous function
$\zeta:C_t\to[0,1]$ with compact support in $U$, equal to one on a
neighborhood of $L$.  Put $\widetilde Q=Q\circ\pi_L$ on $U$ and
extend $\zeta\widetilde Q$ by zero to $C_t$.  Compactness gives
$\delta>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ah-nearby-projection}
 y\in L,\quad \|u-y\|_\infty<\delta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 u\in U,\quad\zeta(u)=1,\quad
 \|\widetilde Q(u)-Q(y)\|<1/2.
\end{equation}
In particular, $\widetilde Q(u)$ is injective on $\mathcal H_y$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:ah-frames} gives common positive amplification sizes $D_0,D_1$
and continuous isometric frames $J_{0,s}$ and $J_{1,s}$ of ranks
$2pg_s$ and $R_s$ in $E_{j,s}^{\oplus D_0}$ and
$E_{j,s}^{\oplus D_1}$, respectively.  Define
\begin{align*}
 (P_0)_s(x)&=J_{0,s}(x)
       \operatorname{diag}_{\ell=1}^{g_s}
       \bigl(\zeta(F_{s,\ell}(x))
                          \widetilde Q(F_{s,\ell}(x))\bigr)
       J_{0,s}(x)^*,\\
 (P_1)_s(x)&=J_{1,s}(x)J_{1,s}(x)^*.
\end{align*}
The first expression uses the zero extension just specified; an empty
diagonal is zero.  Thus $P_0\in M_{D_0}(A_j)_+$ is a contraction,
and $P_1\in M_{D_1}(A_j)$ is a projection.  Their fiber ranks satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(P_0)_s(x)\leq pg_s,
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}(P_1)_s(x)=R_s.
\]
For a normalized trace $\tau$ on $A$, write its restriction to $A_j$
using component weights $t_s\geq0$, $\sum_s t_s=1$, and probability
measures $\mu_s$ on the blocks of positive weight.  The fiber trace
is normalized by $n_s$.  Functional calculus and bounded convergence
therefore give
\[
 d_\tau(P_0)\leq\sum_s t_s\frac{pg_s}{n_s},
 \qquad d_\tau(P_1)=\sum_s t_s\frac{R_s}{n_s}.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:ah-source-trace-gap} implies
$d_\tau(P_0)+r<d_\tau(P_1)$ for every $\tau$.  The comparison bound
gives $P_0\precsim P_1$ in the limit.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:ah-lift},
choose one stage $h$ and one rectangular witness $V$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ah-finite-stage-witness}
 \|V^*\phi_{j,h}(P_1)V-\phi_{j,h}(P_0)\|<1/2.
\end{equation}
There is one such inequality in $M_{D_0}(A_h)$, valid on all its
blocks and all their points; separate witnesses are not chosen for
different sparse supports.

\emph{The finite-dimensional matrix in one target block.}
Fix $k$ with $N_k\geq1$.  Under the diagonal identification, pull back
every column of $J_{0,s}$ and $J_{1,s}$ along every occurrence
$\lambda_{s,k,\alpha}$.  Transporting these columns to the target
unit bundles gives continuous isometric frames
\[
 J_{0,k}:\mathbb C^{2pN_k}\longrightarrow
                  E_{h,k}^{\oplus D_0},\qquad
 J_{1,k}:\mathbb C^{q_k}\longrightarrow
                  E_{h,k}^{\oplus D_1}.
\]
Different occurrences give orthogonal coordinate groups, even when
the underlying maps coincide.  Writing $f_{k,\nu}$ for the slots of
$f_k$, these frames satisfy
\begin{align*}
 \phi_{j,h}(P_0)_k(x)
 &=J_{0,k}(x)\operatorname{diag}_{\nu=1}^{N_k}
       \bigl(\zeta(f_{k,\nu}(x))\widetilde Q(f_{k,\nu}(x))\bigr)
       J_{0,k}(x)^*,\\
 \phi_{j,h}(P_1)_k(x)&=J_{1,k}(x)J_{1,k}(x)^*.
\end{align*}
Let $V_k(x)$ be the block matrix of the single witness, and set
\[
 W_k(x)=J_{1,k}(x)^*V_k(x).
\]
This takes values in the fixed space $\mathbb C^{q_k}$.  Whenever
$\phi_{j,h}(P_0)_k(x)\xi=\xi$, Equation~\eqref{eq:ah-finite-stage-witness}
gives the simultaneous estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ah-simultaneous-injectivity}
 \|W_k(x)\xi\|^2\geq\tfrac12\|\xi\|^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed the square of the left-hand norm is
$\langle V_k(x)^*\phi_{j,h}(P_1)_k(x)V_k(x)\xi,\xi\rangle$.
The estimate holds on the entire direct sum of the selected
eigenvalue-one ranges.

\emph{Alignment on the entire torus locus.}
Put $N=N_k$ and
\[
 \mathcal T_k=\mathcal T(f_k,Z_{aN}(t,N)),\qquad
 y_\nu=f_{k,\nu}(x)-z_\nu\in L.
\]
Choose a continuous $\chi:[-2,2]^t\to[0,1]$ with $\chi(0)=1$ and
support contained in $\{z:\|z\|_\infty<\delta\}$.  On
$\mathcal E_{f_k}=\bigoplus_\nu\mathcal H_{y_\nu}$ define
\[
 \Xi_{(x,z)}(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_N)
 =J_{0,k}(x)
   \bigl(\chi(z_\nu)(\zeta\widetilde Q)(f_{k,\nu}(x))
                                      \eta_\nu\bigr)_{\nu=1}^N.
\]
This formula is continuous everywhere, since $\zeta\widetilde Q$
is a continuous matrix on the whole cube.  If $\chi(z_\nu)>0$,
Equation~\eqref{eq:ah-nearby-projection} applies to
$u=f_{k,\nu}(x)$ and $y=y_\nu$.  Thus $\zeta(u)=1$ and
$\widetilde Q(u)$ injects $\mathcal H_{y_\nu}$ into the corresponding
projection range.  The image of $\Xi_{(x,z)}$ consequently lies in
the eigenvalue-one subspace of $\phi_{j,h}(P_0)_k(x)$.
Distinct slots occupy distinct frame coordinates.  Therefore
\[
 \Phi_1(x,z)=W_k(x)\Xi_{(x,z)}
\]
is continuous and has the exact fiber kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ah-first-map-kernel}
 \ker\Phi_1(x,z)
 =\bigoplus_{\nu:\chi(z_\nu)=0}\mathcal H_{y_\nu}.
\end{equation}
Here Equation~\eqref{eq:ah-simultaneous-injectivity} prevents
cancellation between distinct nonzero slot contributions.

\emph{One fixed map for all sparse exceptions.}
Using the fixed inclusions $\mathcal H_{y_\nu}\subset\mathbb C^{2p}$, put
\[
 \Psi_{(x,z)}(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_N)
     =\bigl((1-\chi(z_\nu))\eta_\nu\bigr)_{\nu=1}^N
       \in(\mathbb C^{2p})^N.
\]
Every possibly nonzero output group has $z_\nu\ne0$, since
$\chi(0)=1$.  The range is consequently supported on at most
$\lfloor aN\rfloor\leq H:=\lceil aN\rceil$ groups.  There is a
single complex linear map
\[
 D:(\mathbb C^{2p})^N\longrightarrow\mathbb C^{2pH}
\]
injective on every coordinate subspace supported on at most $H$
groups.  This is Lemma~\ref{lem:weighted-coordinate-compression} with every coordinate weight equal to $2p$ and target dimension $2pH$.


Set $\Phi_2=D\Psi$.  Both $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$ are continuous in
$(x,z)$ on the whole locus, including changes of sparse support.
If a vector is killed by $\Phi_1$, its components lie only in slots
with $\chi(z_\nu)=0$, by Equation~\eqref{eq:ah-first-map-kernel}.

 T_k&=\prod_{s=1}^{h_j}\prod_{a=1}^{m_{j,h}^{s,k}}S_s,

\end{equation}

We first turn the pointwise carrier bound into a fixed finite target
complex.  Put
\[
 B_k=\left\lfloor(d-2\delta)u_kn_{h,k}\right\rfloor.

Let $P_k$ be the union of all product-face cells of $T_k^{u_k}$ of
dimension at most $B_k$.  Every face of such a cell has no larger
dimension, so this union is a finite subcomplex.  Its dimension is at
most $B_k$, and the carrier assumption gives
$\mathcal R_k(X_{h,k}^{u_k})\subseteq P_k$.  The complex $P_k$ depends only on
the fixed stage-$h$ type and its chosen compression, not on any later

$\dim T_k\leq C_k$.  These finite complexes and their products may be
triangulated without increasing dimension.

 b_k=q_ku_k+r_k,\qquad q_k\geq0,\quad 0\leq r_k<u_k.

Order the $b_k$ eigenmaps from $X_{l,v}$ to $X_{h,k}$ arbitrarily.

of $r_k$ maps.  On every complete group use the continuous map

 \Phi_{l,v}:X_{l,v}\longrightarrow

Factors with exponent zero are omitted.  This is a finite polyhedron,

 &\leq \sum_k q_k(d-2\delta)u_kn_{h,k}
       +\sum_k r_kC_k\\
 &\leq (d-2\delta)\sum_k b_kn_{h,k}
       +\sum_k (u_k-1)C_k\\
 &= (d-2\delta)n_{l,v}+E.

The second inequality uses $d-2\delta>0$.  The last equality is the

$\gamma(\mathcal P)=\infty$ excludes every finite comparison bound,
and hence forces $\operatorname{rc}(A)=\infty$.

We adapt the proof of \cite[Theorem~6.2]{NiuAH} to the
noninjective presentations allowed here by verifying the two
hypotheses of \cite[Lemma~6.1]{NiuAH}.  The finite-dimensional case follows
from Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-limit}, and the assertion is automatic if
$\gamma(\mathcal P)=\infty$.  Suppose therefore that $A$ is infinite
dimensional and $\gamma=\gamma(\mathcal P)<\infty$.

\emph{Finite-stage local approximants.}
Fix a finite subset $F\subset A$ and positive tolerances $\eta$ and
$\zeta$.  Approximate $F$ by the images of a finite set $F_i\subset A_i$
at some stage $i$.  Include the unit in $F_i$.  In the ambient matrix
realization of each source corner choose a componentwise finite open
cover $\alpha$ controlling, to an arbitrarily small tolerance, the
variation of these matrices and of the source unit projections.
The tolerance may depend on the finite maximum of the norms of the
chosen lifts; no norm bound on their images is substituted for that
maximum.
By Definition~\ref{def:gamma} and cover-rate monotonicity, a
sufficiently late stage $j$ satisfies
\[
 \max_k\frac{\mathcal D(\alpha_{i,j,k})}{n_{j,k}}
     <\gamma+\zeta.
\]

Carry out the construction in
\cite[Proof of Theorem~4.3]{NiuAH} inside this finite stage, before
mapping it to $A$.  For clarity, for each target block use its
diagonal corner realization
\[
 E_{j,k}\cong\bigoplus_{s,a}
                   (\lambda_{i,j}^{s,k,a})^*E_{i,s}
 \subset X_{j,k}\times\mathbb C^{R_k},
 \qquad R_k=\sum_s m_{i,j}^{s,k}r_{i,s}.
\]
In these coordinates both the images of $F_i$ and the target unit
projection have small matrix variation on every member of
$\alpha_{i,j,k}$.  This statement concerns the diagonal realization;
it does not assert small variation after a varying bundle unitary
has transported the matrices to a different realization.

Refine $\alpha_{i,j,k}$ by a cover of order
$\mathcal D(\alpha_{i,j,k})$, and use a subordinate partition of
unity to map $X_{j,k}$ into its finite nerve $\Delta_k$.
Writing $\Omega_k$ for the compact image of this map, pullback of
matrix functions gives a subalgebra
$M_{R_k}(C(\Omega_k))\subset M_{R_k}(C(X_{j,k}))$.
The partition-of-unity averages of the finitely many matrices
belong to this subalgebra and approximate those matrices in norm.
The average of the target unit projection can be cut spectrally at
$1/2$ to obtain a projection $e_k$ in this subalgebra arbitrarily
close to the target unit projection.  A unitary close to the identity
in the \emph{full ambient matrix algebra} identifies these two
projections.  Conjugating the $e_k$-corner by that unitary, and then
transporting it back by the prescribed bundle identification, gives
a unital subalgebra $C_j\subset A_j$ with
\[
 C_j\cong\bigoplus_k
       e_kM_{R_k}(C(\Omega_k))e_k,
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}(e_k)=n_{j,k},
 \qquad \dim\Omega_k\leq\mathcal D(\alpha_{i,j,k}).
\]
It approximates $\phi_{i,j}(F_i)$ as closely as desired.  The rank
statement holds at every point of $\Omega_k$, since every such
point is the image of a point of $X_{j,k}$ and the pulled-back
projection is unitarily equivalent to the unit of rank $n_{j,k}$.
The original approximation of $F$ can therefore be chosen so that
the image $C=\phi_{j,\infty}(C_j)$ approximates $F$ within $\eta$.

\emph{Quotient images do not increase the dimension ratio.}
The homomorphism $C_j\to C$ need not be injective.  Every ideal in
a homogeneous corner corresponds to an open subset of its spectrum.
Consequently its quotient is the same corner restricted to a closed
subset.  Omitting zero summands, there are compact sets
$K_k\subset\Omega_k$ with
\[
 C\cong\bigoplus_k
       (e_k|_{K_k})M_{R_k}(C(K_k))(e_k|_{K_k}).
\]
This is a unital subalgebra of $A$.  Its surviving unit ranks are
still $n_{j,k}$ and
$\dim K_k\leq\dim\Omega_k$.  The homogeneous-corner comparison
estimate of \cite[Corollary~5.2]{TomsComparison}, also used in
\cite[Proof of Theorem~6.2]{NiuAH}, gives
\[
 \operatorname{rc}(C)
 \leq\max_k\frac{\dim K_k}{2n_{j,k}}
 <\frac\gamma2+\frac\zeta2.
\]
For zero-dimensional spectra the same bound is zero, since the
homogeneous corner is AF.  Thus the local approximation condition
of \cite[Lemma~6.1]{NiuAH} holds with $r=\gamma/2$.  The quotient
spectra need not be connected; the displayed comparison estimate
uses their finite covering dimension and their constant unit ranks.

\emph{Projections with nearly constant normalized traces.}
Fix one source stage $i$ and put
$N_i=\max_s n_{i,s}$.  For any $u\in[0,1]$ and any late stage $j$,
order the eigenmap occurrences in each target block.  The corresponding
pullback unit bundles have positive ranks at most $N_i$ and total
rank $n_{j,k}$.  Select a prefix of these occurrences whose total
rank is the largest prefix rank not exceeding $u n_{j,k}$.
Its rank differs from $u n_{j,k}$ by at most $N_i$; for $u=1$ select
all occurrences.  Projection onto the selected summands, transported
by the global diagonal bundle identification, is a continuous
projection $z_j\in A_j$.  Uniformly over all normalized fiber traces,
\[
 \left|\tau_x(z_j)-u\right|
 \leq\frac{N_i}{\min_k n_{j,k}}
 \longrightarrow0
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-growth}.  The same estimate holds for every
trace on $A$ after mapping $z_j$ to the limit, because its restriction

\emph{The output packing estimate.}

is an integer, so its floor gives

The weaker exceptional-count assumption is useful when an allocation

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:cover-bound}
Under the free minimal URP hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:actions-main}, every finite comparison bound $r$ for $A$ satisfies $d_\mathcal U\leq2r$ for every finite open cover $\mathcal U$.

It suffices to consider $r>0$: if zero is a comparison bound, every
positive $r$ is also a comparison bound, and we let $r\downarrow0$.
We argue by contradiction. We first fix the parameters and rank
estimates, then construct the simultaneous bundle embedding and
reconstruct a subordinate cover of smaller order.

\subsection{Parameters and quantitative estimates}\label{sec:actions-parameters}

Let $\mathcal U$ be a finite open cover and write
\[
 d=\lim_F\frac{\mathcal D(\mathcal U^F)}{|F|}>2r,
 \qquad M=\max\{1,\mathcal D(\mathcal U)\}.
\]
Here the limit is the Ornstein--Weiss cover rate, and $r\geq0$ is a
finite comparison bound.  Choose $p\geq1$ and then $0<a<1/16$ such that,
with $t=2p+1$ and $c=d/(t+1)$,
\[
 pc>r,
 \qquad G=(1-11a)pc-r>0.
\]
The following constants are fixed before either tiling is selected:
\begin{align*}
 \kappa=b&=\frac{a}{128},
 &\Delta&=\frac{ac}{32},
 &\tau&=\frac{G}{4(r+G)},\\
 \varepsilon&=\min\left\{\frac c4,
                  \frac{ac}{4096},\frac{\kappa c}{16}\right\},
 &c_-&=c-\varepsilon,
 &c_+&=c+\varepsilon,\\
 \eta&=\min\left\{\frac{\kappa}{4},\frac{ac}{4096M}\right\},
 &\delta_{\mathrm{out}}&=\min\left\{\frac a{512},
                                      \frac{ac}{4096M}\right\},
 &\delta_{\mathrm{prop}}&=\frac a{512}.
\end{align*}
In particular $c_->0$, $\kappa,b<a/64$, and $0<\tau\leq1/4$.
Fix also an Ornstein--Weiss invariance test ensuring
\begin{equation}\label{eq:urp-ow-reserve}
 \frac{\mathcal D(\mathcal U^E)}{|E|}>d-\frac\Delta2
\end{equation}
for every finite nonempty set $E$ passing that test.  This test depends
only on $\mathcal U$ and the already fixed $\Delta$.

\paragraph{Primary shapes and their integer ranks.}
Choose an exact finite-shape primary tiling with shapes $F_s$ so invariant
and so large that
\[
 \left|\frac{\mathcal D(\mathcal U^{F_s})}{|F_s|}-d\right|
       \leq\frac\varepsilon2,
 \qquad
 |F_s|\geq
 \max\left\{\frac4\varepsilon,\frac{4096}{apc}\right\}.
\]
Put $m_s=\mathcal D(\mathcal U^{F_s})$,
\[
 g_s=\left\lfloor\frac{m_s+1}{t+1}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad R_s=\lfloor(2-10a)pg_s\rfloor.
\]
The preceding requirements give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:urp-primary-densities}
 c_-\leq\frac{g_s}{|F_s|}\leq c_+,
 \qquad \frac{m_s}{|F_s|}\leq d+\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{align*}
 \frac{R_s-pg_s}{|F_s|}
 &\geq(1-10a)p(c-\varepsilon)-\frac1{|F_s|}\\
 &\geq r+G+apc-\frac{apc}{4096}-\frac{apc}{4096}
 >r+G.
\end{align*}
Thus all floor errors are absorbed before the dynamical castle is chosen. Fix the colored primary nerves $\varphi_s:X\to S_s$ for $\mathcal U^{F_s}$, of dimensions at most $m_s$, and their packet maps $F_{s,j}:X\to C_t$ now. Fix also a one-site nerve $\varphi_0:X\to S_0$ for $\mathcal U$.

\paragraph{Macro shapes and padding.}
Having fixed the primary tiling, choose an exact macro tiling with shapes
$E_1,\ldots,E_L$ satisfying the allocation lemma's inner-boundary estimate
\[
 |\partial_H E_l|\leq\eta|E_l|,
 \qquad H=\bigcup_sF_sF_s^{-1},
\]
and the additional requirements
\[
 \mathcal D(\mathcal U^{E_l})\leq(d+\varepsilon)|E_l|,
 \qquad |E_l|\geq\frac4{\kappa c}.
\]
Set
\[
 n_l=\lceil c_+|E_l|\rceil,
 \qquad B_l=(d+\varepsilon+M\eta)|E_l|+1.
\]
Fix fallback nerves $\varphi_l^{\mathrm{fb}}:X\to S_l^{\mathrm{fb}}$ for $\mathcal U^{E_l}$ with dimensions at most $\mathcal D(\mathcal U^{E_l})$. For any pattern $\lambda$ furnished by the allocation below, let
$h_{l,\lambda}$ be the number of real packet slots in its disjoint primary
tiles.  The allocation lemma and \eqref{eq:urp-primary-densities} give
\[
 c_-(1-\eta)|E_l|\leq h_{l,\lambda}
      \leq c_+|E_l|\leq n_l.
\]
Consequently its number of padded slots satisfies
\begin{align*}
 \frac{n_l-h_{l,\lambda}}{n_l}
 &\leq\frac{2\varepsilon+c\eta+|E_l|^{-1}}{c_+}\\
 &\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{c}+\eta+\frac{1}{c|E_l|}
 \leq\frac{\kappa}{8}+\frac{\kappa}{4}+\frac{\kappa}{4}
 <\kappa.
\end{align*}
Padded slot functions are fixed vectors in $\partial C_t$.  On the torus
test locus a padded slot therefore has nonzero sparse parameter.  Give each padded slot effective weight zero. On the torus test locus it is already included in the sparse-parameter exception budget $an$, by the weaker condition in Proposition~\ref{prop:allocated-bundle-embedding}. Padding is charged separately in the covering-order estimate below.

\paragraph{Closed exceptional sets are chosen last.}
All $n_l$ and $B_l$ are now fixed finite constants.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:urp-closed-budget}
 \theta_l=\min\left\{
 \frac{b c_+(1-\delta_{\mathrm{out}})}{4L n_l},
 \frac{ac}{4096L B_l}\right\}>0.
\end{equation}
Only at this point invoke the allocation lemma, with primary sliced-tower
mass greater than $1-\tau$, and with compactly supported masks $w_l$ whose
exceptional closed sets $C_l\supset\{w_l<1\}$ satisfy
\[
 \sup_{\mu\in M_\Gamma(X)}\mu(C_l)<\frac{\theta_l}{2}.
\]
The uniform closed-set orbit estimate then supplies an invariance test,
depending on these already fixed $C_l$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:urp-bad-count}
 \#\{g\in F:gx\in C_l\}\leq\theta_l|F|
 \quad(x\in X, 1\leq l\leq L)
\end{equation}
whenever $F$ passes that test.  Thus no exceptional-set tolerance is
chosen before the possibly large macro costs are known.

\paragraph{The strict tracial gap and the witness.}
Let $B_s$ be the sliced primary bases and arrange
\[
 \sum_s|F_s|\mu(B_s)>1-\tau
 \quad(\mu\in M_\Gamma(X)).
\]
For the positive elements encoding the primary packet bundles, their
target-minus-source dimension functions satisfy
\begin{align*}
 d_\mu(P_1)-d_\mu(P_0)
 &\geq\sum_s(R_s-pg_s)\mu(B_s)\\
 &>(r+G)(1-\tau)=r+\frac{3G}{4}>r.
\end{align*}
Cuntz comparison therefore gives one finite-propagation witness.  Its
finite support $K$ is fixed before the input window $F$ is chosen.

\paragraph{Input windows and source ranks.}
Choose $F$ after that witness.  In addition to
\eqref{eq:urp-bad-count} and \eqref{eq:urp-ow-reserve}, require that full
macro tiles contained in $F$ leave at most
$\delta_{\mathrm{out}}|F|$ uncovered sites and that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:urp-propagation-budget}
 \left|\left(\bigcup_sF_s\right)KF\right|
     \leq(1+\delta_{\mathrm{prop}})|F|.
\end{equation}
Let $q_l$ be the number of those macro tiles of type $l$, and set
$n_F=\sum_lq_ln_l$.  Their disjointness gives the useful explicit bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:urp-source-bounds}
 c_+(1-\delta_{\mathrm{out}})|F|
 \leq n_F
 \leq\left(c_++\frac{\kappa c}{4}\right)|F|
 \leq c_+\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{4}\right)|F|.
\end{equation}
The first term in \eqref{eq:urp-closed-budget}, together with
\eqref{eq:urp-bad-count}, ensures that the total number of slots in bad
macro tiles is at most
\[
 |F|\sum_l n_l\theta_l
 \leq\frac b4c_+(1-\delta_{\mathrm{out}})|F|
 \leq\frac b4 n_F\leq b n_F.
\]
The second term ensures that their additional join cost is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:urp-join-budget}
 |F|\sum_lB_l\theta_l\leq\frac{ac}{4096}|F|.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{A uniform strict target-rank margin.}
Disjointness of the active primary tiles and
\eqref{eq:urp-propagation-budget} bound the witness's output rank by
\[
 (2-10a)pc_+(1+\delta_{\mathrm{prop}})|F|.
\]
The fixed linear compression map and sparse exceptional map therefore
use total target rank at most
\[
 \left\lceil(2-10a)pc_+(1+\delta_{\mathrm{prop}})|F|\right\rceil
       +2p\lceil(a+b)n_F\rceil.
\]
Require also
\[
 |F|\geq
 \frac{2(1+2p)}{apc_+(1-\delta_{\mathrm{out}})}.
\]
After division by the source rank $pn_F$, the two ceiling errors are
then at most $a/2$.  The normalized target rank is consequently at most
\begin{align}\label{rank-control}
 &(2-10a)\frac{1+\delta_{\mathrm{prop}}}
                   {1-\delta_{\mathrm{out}}}
       +2(a+b)+\frac a2 \nonumber \\
 &\leq (2-10a)\left(1+\frac a{128}\right)
              +2a+\frac a{64}+\frac a2
 <2-7a<2.
\end{align}
The positive rank margin is independent of $F$ and of the
compression product exponent.

\paragraph{Covering-order saving with a fixed reserve.}
For a good macro tile, the uncompressed primary nerve factors and its
one-site hole factors cost at most
$(d+\varepsilon+M\eta)|E_l|$.  Each real boundary slot reduces this
carrier dimension by one.  A fallback macro nerve costs at most
$(d+\varepsilon)|E_l|$, so $B_l$ bounds the extra cost of the join at a
bad macro tile.  Cover uncovered input sites by one-site nerve factors.
If torus compression in $u$ copies supplies $(a/8)u n_F$ boundary slots,
discarding padding and all slots in bad macro tiles still leaves at least
\[
 (a/8-\kappa-b)u n_F
\]
real boundary slots in good macro tiles.  Hence the resulting cover
subordinate finite target complex has normalized dimension at most
\begin{align*}
 d+\varepsilon+M\eta+M\delta_{\mathrm{out}}
       +\frac{ac}{4096}
 -(a/8-\kappa-b)c_+(1-\delta_{\mathrm{out}}).
\end{align*}
The four positive error terms sum to at most $ac/1024$.  Furthermore
\[
 (a/8-\kappa-b)c_+(1-\delta_{\mathrm{out}})
 =\frac{7a}{64}c_+(1-\delta_{\mathrm{out}})
 >\frac{ac}{16}.
\]
Thus, for every permitted compression exponent $u$, the cover-rate upper
bound is strictly less than
\begin{equation}\label{eq:urp-final-reserve}
 d-\frac{63ac}{1024}<d-\Delta.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The Ornstein--Weiss lower bound for every product exponent.}
For the left action used here, window translates are right translates.
Choose $c_1,\ldots,c_u$ such that the windows $Fc_j$ are pairwise disjoint,
and put $F_u=\bigcup_jFc_j$.  Every fixed left-invariance test passed by $F$ is also passed by $F_u$. In particular, for a finite test set $Q$ containing $e$,
\[
 |QF_u\setminus F_u|\leq u|QF\setminus F|.
\]
For each individual test element $k$,
\[
 |kF_u\mathbin{\triangle}F_u|
 \leq\sum_j|kFc_j\mathbin{\triangle}Fc_j|
 =u|kF\mathbin{\triangle}F|.
\]
The same observation applies to finite test sets.  Therefore
\eqref{eq:urp-ow-reserve} yields
\[
 \frac{\mathcal D(\mathcal U^{F_u})}{|F_u|}>d-\frac\Delta2
 \qquad\hbox{for every }u\geq1,
\]
contradicting the subordinate-cover bound
\eqref{eq:urp-final-reserve}.  The final invariance and cardinality
requirements on $F$ are all imposed after the finite witness support is
known; increasing the invariance or cardinality of $F$ does not alter any
previously fixed constants, masks, packet maps, or ranks.

Use the primary colored nerves $\varphi_s$, packet maps $F_{s,j}$, one-site nerve $\varphi_0$, and fallback nerves $\varphi_l^{\mathrm{fb}}$ fixed at the indicated stages above. Every zero-parameter slot with effective weight less than one is consequently in a macro with $w_l<1$. \subsection{Reconstruction of the whole orbit cover}
Choose $c_1,\ldots,c_u\in\Gamma$ such that $Fc_b$ are pairwise disjoint, and put $F_u=\bigsqcup_bFc_b$. Such choices exist because $\Gamma$ is infinite: at each stage only finitely many new translates are forbidden. Pull $\mathcal G$ back along

Write its replacement slots as $v_{b,l,c,\nu}(x)$. In a macro with positive weight, a real slot $\nu$ labeled $(s,q,j)$ has original value $F_{s,j}(qcc_bx)$. The preservation conclusion just stated therefore gives
\[
 F_{s,j}(qcc_bx)\in\partial C_t
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 v_{b,l,c,\nu}(x)=F_{s,j}(qcc_bx).

This supplies exactly the boundary-preservation hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:packets} for each primary nerve factor on its open pattern region. The boundary-slot lower bound also holds at every $\iota_u(x)$, and will supply the carrier-dimension saving below.

For each macro pattern $\lambda$ define the finite polyhedron
\[
 A_{l,\lambda}=
 \prod_{(s,q)\in\mathcal P_{l,\lambda}}S_s
 \ \times\prod_{q\in J_{l,\lambda}}S_0,
 \qquad A_l=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_l}A_{l,\lambda}.
\]
The primary coordinates before reconstruction are $\varphi_s(qcc_bx)$; the hole coordinates are $\varphi_0(qcc_bx)$. Reconstruct each primary factor using its assigned replacement packets. The resulting point in $A_{l,\lambda}$ is continuous on its positive-mask pattern region, and every positive vertex coordinate still implies membership in the corresponding original orbit-cover member.

Join this pattern point to the unchanged fallback point $\varphi_l^{\mathrm{fb}}(cc_bx)$ in
\[
 P_l=A_l*S_l^{\mathrm{fb}},
\]
giving the $A_l$ branch weight $w_l(cc_bx)$ and the fallback branch weight $1-w_l(cc_bx)$. This defines a continuous map into $P_l$ even where the pattern changes: the bounded coordinates of the entire $A_l$ branch are multiplied by a weight tending to zero. At weight one the fallback branch disappears. Include the unchanged one-site factors $\varphi_0(qc_bx)$ for $q\in J_F$. Taking the product over all $b,l,c$ and these outside sites defines a continuous map
\[
 \Phi:X\longrightarrow P_u,
\]
where $P_u$ is a finite polyhedron.

We verify subordination on the whole inverse image of each target
neighborhood. At any point of $\Phi(X)$, choose in each macro factor a branch of positive weight. If it is the pattern branch, its component $\lambda$ is locally fixed; choose a positive vertex in each primary and hole factor. The primary cover members describe all sites of $F_sq$, and the hole factors describe the complementary sites, together covering $E_l$ exactly. Preservation of vertex support ensures the same memberships throughout the inverse image of a neighborhood on which those vertices remain positive. If the fallback branch is chosen, a positive vertex in its unchanged nerve specifies a member of $\mathcal U^{E_l}$ on that whole inverse image. Make the analogous choices for the outside one-site factors. The finitely many assigned members combine to one member of $\mathcal U^{F_u}$. Intersecting the positive-coordinate neighborhoods proves the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:refinement}.

For completeness, the dimension estimate is an estimate of a fixed finite target subcomplex. Equip $P_u$ with the cell structure formed from products and joins of simplex faces in its factors. For a macro of type $l$ with weight one, its carrier dimension is at most
\[
 \sum_{(s,q)\in\mathcal P_{l,\lambda}}m_s
       +m_0|J_{l,\lambda}|-s_{b,l,c}(x),
\]
where $s_{b,l,c}(x)$ counts its real boundary replacement packets. The expression before the saving is at most $(d+\varepsilon+M\eta)|E_l|$. For a macro with weight less than one, the product-join carrier bound is at most the baseline plus $m(E_l)+1$, which is at most the baseline plus $B_l$. This bound also covers weight zero, when only the fallback branch remains.

At most $\kappa un_F$ boundary slots can be padded slots in good macros, and at most $bun_F$ can be in bad macros. The remaining real boundary slots in good macros total at least
\[
 (a/8-\kappa-b)un_F.
\]
The estimates \eqref{eq:urp-join-budget} and \eqref{eq:urp-source-bounds}, together with the outside-site cost $M\delta_{\mathrm{out}}u|F|$, therefore bound every image carrier by
\begin{align*}
 u|F|\bigl(d+\varepsilon+M\eta+M\delta_{\mathrm{out}}
       +ac/4096\bigr)
 -(a/8-\kappa-b)un_F
 <u|F|(d-\Delta),
\end{align*}
as computed in \eqref{eq:urp-final-reserve}. There are finitely many cells, and the faces of a cell never have larger dimension. Thus $\Phi(X)$ lies in the union of all cells of dimension at most $\lfloor u|F|(d-\Delta)\rfloor$, a finite subcomplex of that dimension. Triangulate this subcomplex without raising its dimension. Lemma~\ref{lem:refinement} yields
\[
 \frac{\mathcal D(\mathcal U^{F_u})}{|F_u|}\leq d-\Delta.
\]
On the other hand, $F_u$ passes the Ornstein--Weiss lower test because $F$ does, giving \eqref{eq:urp-ow-reserve} for $F_u$. This is a contradiction.

Theorem~\ref{thm:cover-bound} proves $d_\mathcal U\leq2r$ for every finite comparison bound $r$ and every finite open cover $\mathcal U$. If comparison bounds exist, taking their infimum proves the first assertion of Theorem~\ref{thm:actions-main}; if none exist, its right-hand side is infinite and the assertion is automatic. In particular, infinite mean dimension excludes every finite comparison bound. Under COS, the upper bound in  \cite[Theorem 4.8]{Niu} completes the equality in the extended nonnegative reals.
