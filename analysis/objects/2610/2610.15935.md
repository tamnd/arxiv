\label{prop:limit}
Suppose that, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), the Lindeberg condition holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lindeberg}
 L_n(\varepsilon)
 :=\frac{1}{V_n}\sum_{k=1}^n
 \mathbb E\!\left[Y_{n,k}^2
 \mathbf 1_{\{|Y_{n,k}|>\varepsilon\sqrt{V_n}\}}\right]
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
If \(a_n\ge0\) and \(a_n/\sqrt{V_n}\to b\in[0,\infty)\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limit}
 \mathbb P(M_n\le a_n)\longrightarrow 2\Phi(b)-1.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx}
 \mathbb P(M_n\le a_n)
 =\operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{a_n}{\sqrt{2V_n}}\right)+o(1).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Parametrize the sums by accumulated variance:
\[
 t_{n,k}=\frac{1}{V_n}\sum_{j=1}^k\sigma_{n,j}^2,
 \qquad t_{n,0}=0,\qquad t_{n,n}=1.
\]
Increments with zero variance may be removed. For every
\(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \max_k\frac{\sigma_{n,k}^2}{V_n}
 \le\varepsilon^2+L_n(\varepsilon).
\]
Thus the largest gap between consecutive times tends to zero.
The functional Lindeberg theorem, on this deterministic variance
clock, gives convergence to \(B\) of the process that linearly
interpolates the points
\((t_{n,k},S_{n,k}/\sqrt{V_n})\); see \cite{durrett}, Section~8.2,
for the martingale formulation. Independence makes the conditional
variances deterministic and reduces the tail condition to
\eqref{eq:lindeberg}.

The maximum of the interpolation is \(M_n/\sqrt{V_n}\).
Since the maximum is a continuous functional on continuous paths
with the uniform norm, it converges in distribution to
\(\sup_{0\le t\le1}B_t\). The distribution function of this maximum
is continuous, including at zero, and is given by \eqref{eq:correct}
at \(t=1\), extended to \(a=0\). This proves \eqref{eq:limit}.
Continuity of \(\Phi\) then gives \eqref{eq:approx}.
