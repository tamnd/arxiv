<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{structural}
For any unit vector $\xi$ one has $\langle E{\bf b}\xi,\xi\rangle=\int_{\pi_\xi} D{\bf b}_{y,\xi}\,d{\mathscr H}^{k-1}(y)$, namely
$$
\int\phi(x)\, d\langle E{\bf b}\xi,\xi\rangle(x)=\int_{\pi_\xi}\int_{\Omega_{y,\xi}}\phi(y+s\xi)\,dD{\bf b}_{y,\xi}(s)\,d{\mathscr H}^{k-1}(y)
$$
for any bounded Borel function $\phi$ with compact support. Analogously, 
$$
\langle E^a{\bf b}\xi,\xi\rangle=\int_{\pi_\xi} D^a{\bf b}_{y,\xi}\,d{\mathscr H}^{k-1}(y), 
\qquad
|\langle E^a{\bf b}\xi,\xi\rangle|=\int_{\pi_\xi} |D^a{\bf b}_{y,\xi}|\,d{\mathscr H}^{k-1}(y),
$$
$$
\langle E^s{\bf b}\xi,\xi\rangle=\int_{\pi_\xi} D^s{\bf b}_{y,\xi}\,d{\mathscr H}^{k-1}(y),
\qquad
|\langle E^s{\bf b}\xi,\xi\rangle|=\int_{\pi_\xi} |D^s{\bf b}_{y,\xi}|\,d{\mathscr H}^{k-1}(y).
$$
Finally, 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sliceJ1}
\int_{\pi_\xi}{\mathscr H}^{0}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt
\vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits J_{{\bf b}_{y,\xi}}\,d{\mathscr H}^{k-1}(y)\leq{\mathscr H}^{k-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt
\vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits J_{{\bf b}}
\end{equation}
and ${\bf b}\in SBD(\Omega)$ implies $({\bf b})_{y,\xi}\in SBV(\Omega_{y,\xi})$ for ${\mathscr H}^{k-1}$-a.e. $y\in\pi_\xi$.

\label{def:Cauchy}
Given $\bar\rho\in L^1\cap L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$, we say that $\rho_t$ solves the initial value problem

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem_negative_times}
{\rm A standard procedure, that we will also adopt in the final sections of the paper, consists in the extension of ${\bf b}$ to negative
times, setting ${\bf b}_t=0$ and $\rho_t=0$ for all $t\in (-\infty,0)$. In this case, Definition~\ref{def:Cauchy} can be formulated by saying that 
for all $\phi\in C^1_c(\mathbb{R}^d)$ the map $t\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\phi\rho_t\,dx$
is a $SBV_{\rm loc}$ function in $(-\infty,T)$, with distributional derivative
$$
\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\phi\bar\rho\,dx\biggr)\,\delta_0+\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}{\bf b}_t\cdot\nabla\phi\rho_t\,dx\biggr){\mathscr L}^{1}.
$$

It will also be convenient to formulate \eqref{eq:CE} when a nonzero right hand side is involved, possibly a measure $\theta$ in
$(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^d$ with finite total variation on bounded sets. 
In this case, extending both ${\bf b}$ and $\rho$ to $0$ for $t\in (0,T)^c\times\mathbb{R}^d$, an equivalent formulation
involves the space-time vector field
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spacetime}
{\bf B}(t,x)=\bigl(1,{\bf b}_t(x)\bigr)
\end{equation}
as follows: 
\begin{equation}
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}\rho_t+{\rm div}({\bf b}_t\rho_t)}=\theta&\text{in $(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^d$;}\cr\cr
\rho_0=\bar\rho
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
holds if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rep_div_BB}
D\cdot (\rho{\bf B})=\bar\rho{\mathscr H}^{d}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt
\vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits\{t=0\}+\theta-\rho_T{\mathscr H}^{d}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt
\vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits\{t=T\}
\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^d$,}
\end{equation}
where from now on $D\cdot {\bf C}$ stands for the space-time divergence of a $(d+1)$-dimensional vector field ${\bf C}$.
}

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:disinte}
Let $X$, $\mathcal A$ be Polish topological spaces, let $\mathcal F$ be a $\sigma$-algebra containing ${\mathscr B}(\mathcal A)$ 
and let $f:X\to\mathcal A$ be a (${\mathscr B}(X)-\mathcal F$)-measurable map. \\
Then, for every nonnegative and $\sigma$-finite measure $\mu$ in $X$  such that $f_\#\mu$ is $\sigma$-finite
there exist a weakly$^*$ measurable family of measures $\mu_a\in {\mathscr M}_+\bigl(X\bigr)$, $a\in\mathcal A$ , concentrated on $f^{-1}(a)$ 
and unique up to sets of negligible $f_\#\mu$-measure, such that
$\mu=\int_{{\mathcal A}}\mu_a\,df_\#\mu$,
namely 
$$
\int_X\phi(x)\,d\mu(x)=\int_{{\mathcal A}}\int_X\phi(x)\,d\mu_a(x)\,d f_\#\mu(a)
$$
for any Borel function $\phi:X\to [0,\infty]$.

By our assumption on $\mathcal F$, the map $f$ is also Borel. Then the result follows by standard
disintegration theorem in Polish (or even Lusin) topological spaces, see for instance \cite[Theorems~10.4.10 and Example~10.4.11]{Bogachev},
after a decomposition of $X$ in countably many Borel sets with finite $\mu$-measure.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:Kellerer}
Let $X$, $Y$ be Polish spaces and $\mu_X\in{\mathscr P}\bigl(X\bigr)$, $\mu_Y\in{\mathscr P}\bigl(Y\bigr)$. Let $C\subseteq X\times Y$ be a Borel set. Then
$$
\sup\left\{\Sigma(C):\ \Sigma\in\Gamma(\mu_X,\mu_Y)\right\}=\min\biggl\{\mu_X(A)+\mu_Y(B):\ {\bf 1}_A(x)+{\bf 1}_B(y)\geq {\bf 1}_C(x,y)\biggr\},
$$
where $\Gamma(\mu_X,\mu_Y)$ consists of all $\Sigma\in{\mathscr P}\bigl(X\times Y\bigr)$ having $\mu_X$ and $\mu_Y$ as marginals, while
the minimization in the right hand side is among all Borel sets $A\subseteq X$, $B\subseteq Y$.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Let $\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x):\mathbb{R}^d\to C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^d)$ be a Borel map. We say that $\mathbb{X}$ is a Regular Lagrangian Flow relative to ${\bf b}$ if:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\mathbb{X}(t,x)=x+\int_0^t{\bf b}_s(\mathbb{X}(s,x))\,ds$ for all $t\in [0,T]$, for ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$; 
\item[(ii)] there exists a constant $C>0$ such that $\mathbb{X}(t,\cdot)_\#{\mathscr L}^{d}\leq C{\mathscr L}^{d}$ for all $t\in [0,T]$.
\end{itemize}
The least $C$ in (ii) is called compressibility constant of $\mathbb{X}$ and denoted by $C(\mathbb{X})$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
{\rm Already the case of Sobolev vector fields illustrates the necessity of conditions additional to (i) in order to
recover some form of uniqueness. After the first counterexamples provided in \cite{DL_89} (for ``almost Sobolev'' vector fields, as those in the fractional Sobolev space $W^{s,1}$, $s<1$)
in \cite{Kum_2024}   a continuous, Sobolev and divergence-free vector field ${\bf b}$ is built such that at least two distinct
solutions start from a set of full measure of initial points $x$
(improving an earlier construction in \cite{BCD_2021}, see also \cite{PiSo}). By the uniqueness theorem of RLF, only a suitable selection of these grants the bounded compression (ii), in this specific case 
even the measure-preserving property. The critical integrability exponent of $|\nabla{\bf b}_t|$ for this counterexample is exactly $d$: remarkably,
as soon as $|\nabla{\bf b}_t|\in L^p$ with $p>d$, it is proved in \cite{CC21} that uniqueness in the conventional sense holds for ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-almost every
initial condition $x$, if one works with the continuous representative of ${\bf b}_t$ granted by the Sobolev embedding.
}

\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] for ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$, $\eta_x$ is concentrated on absolutely continuous solutions to \eqref{eq:ODE} starting at $x$; 
\item[(ii)] denoting ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\int\eta_x\,d{\mathscr L}^{d}(x)\in{\mathscr M}_+\bigl(C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^d)\bigr)$, there exists a constant $C>0$ such that $({\sf ev}_t)_\#{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\leq C{\mathscr L}^{d}$ for all $t\in [0,T]$.
\end{itemize}
The least $C$ in (ii) is called compressibility constant of ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ and denoted by $C({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})$.

{\rm  A beautiful example in \cite{Pap_2025} shows somehow the necessity to deal with {\it generalized} flows in the absence of sufficient smoothness: for any
 integer $k\geq 1$ and $\alpha\in (0,1)$ a divergence-free vector field ${\bf b}\in C^k_t(C^{0,\alpha}_x)$ is built such that there is no measure-preserving
 selection of characteristics. In this case the superposition principle obviously grants existence of a GRLF, even a measure-preserving one 
 as in Brenier-Schnirelman's theory of generalized incompressible Euler equations (see for instance \cite{AF} and the references therein). 
 More generally, as suggested by the author, without smoothness
 assumptions it is not clear if existence of a RLF for a divergence-free vector field implies that the RLF is measure-preserving.
 
 Still in connection with the impossibility to provide well-behaved selections of characteristics, the well-known example \cite{Depauw_03} of a bounded, divergence-free vector field in $L^1_{\rm loc}\bigl((0,1];BV(\mathbb{T}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)\bigr)$ for which forward uniqueness of \eqref{eq:CE} fails even in the class $\mathcal R_+$
 has been revisited in \cite{Pit_2024}, proving existence and uniqueness of the corresponding GRLF ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$. This GRLF, due to an instantaneous branching at $t=0$, does not have any RLF counterpart, namely a selection of trajectories, indeed the failure of forward uniqueness of \eqref{eq:CE} corresponds to the conditional
 probabilities ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_x$ not being Dirac masses.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:invariant}{\rm
The bounded compression condition is useful to show that the notions of RLF and GRLF are invariant in the Lebesgue
equivalence class of ${\bf b}$. Indeed, if $\tilde{{\bf b}}$ is a modification of ${\bf b}$ in a ${\mathscr L}^{d+1}$-negligible Borel set $M$, we know
that for ${\mathscr L}^{1}$-a.e. $t\in (0,T)$ the set $\{\gamma:\ (t,\gamma(t))\in M\}$ is ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$-negligible, to be precise, for all $t\in (0,T)$
such that the $t$-section $M_t$ of $M$ is ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-negligible. Then, Fubini's theorem gives that for ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$-a.e. $\gamma$
the curve $\gamma(t)$ belongs to $M_t$ for a ${\mathscr L}^{1}$-negligible set of times, whence the invariance of condition (i) follows.}

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
{\rm In order to have an axiomatization of flows invariant under modifications ${\bf b}$ in ${\mathscr L}^{d+1}$-negligible sets, the
$L^\infty$ bound could be simply replaced by a non-quantitative bound $\mathbb{X}(t,\cdot)_\#{\mathscr L}^{d}\ll{\mathscr L}^{d}$ for
all (or even ${\mathscr L}^{1}$-almost all) $t\in (0,T)$, see the argument in Remark~\ref{rem:invariant}. In this connection, on the Eulerian side, in the last few years,
in the class of Sobolev vector fields, many efforts have been devoted to the search of the optimal range of exponents $(p,q)$ (with
$q$ local summability of $\nabla {\bf b}_t$, $p$ local summability of $\rho_t$) ensuring local well
posedness of \eqref{eq:CE}. As soon as $\frac 1p +\frac 1 q<1+\frac 1{dp}$, well posedness in the
class of nonnegative $\rho$'s is ensured by \cite{BCD_2021}, and it is an open problem for signed solutions
(the restriction to nonnegative solutions
comes from the use of the superposition principle, Theorem~\ref{thm:superpo}); on the other hand, the counterexamples built in \cite{BCK}
(see also \cite{MoSa_2020}) show that when $\frac 1p +\frac 1 q>1+\frac 1{dp}$ uniqueness fails.

At the abstract level, it would be interesting to explore a different axiomatization of RLF and GRLF with a quantitative $L^q$ condition,
$q<\infty$, on the time marginals (the main difficulty is due to the fact that $L^q$ bounds on the flow map are not stable under
composition).

\label{lem:Borel}
Let $K\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ be compact and let $\mathbb{Y}:K\to C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^d)$ be continuous and injective. The collection made by all pairs $(x,y)\in K\times K$

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:main} Under assumption \eqref{eq:growth}, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] there exists at most one GRLF ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\int\eta_x\,d{\mathscr L}^{d}(x)$;
\item[(b)] for any nonnegative initial datum $\rho_0\in L^1\cap L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$, \eqref{eq:CE} has at most one solution $\rho_t\in\mathcal R_+$ starting from $\rho_0$.
\end{itemize}
Moreover, any of these conditions implies that, if there exists a GRLF, then
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\eta_x$ is a Dirac mass for ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$;
\item[(ii)] the unique solution $\rho_t$ to \eqref{eq:CE} in the class ${\mathcal R}_+$ is representable as $({\sf ev}_t)_\#(\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\rho_0(x){\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_x\,dx)$;
\item[(iii)] ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\eta_x\,dx$ is concentrated on a forward nonbranching Borel family $\Gamma\subseteq C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^d)$.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
$(a)\Rightarrow(b)$. Since any solution $\rho_t$ in this class has a representation \eqref{eq:Lag1} via the superposition principle, it suffices to
check that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}}$ is uniquely determined by ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$. Indeed, we can represent ${\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}}$ as $\int\rho_0(x)\sigma_x\,d{\mathscr L}^{d}(x)$, with $\sigma_x$ concentrated
probability measures on absolutely continuous solutions to the ODE \eqref{eq:ODE} starting at $x$. As $\|\rho_t\|_\infty$ is uniformly bounded, one also has
$({\sf ev}_t)_\#{\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}}\leq C{\mathscr L}^{d}$ for all $t\in [0,T]$. Now, assume by contradiction that $\sigma_x\neq\eta_x$ in a set with positive $\rho_0{\mathscr L}^{d}$-measure, 
so that there exists $\tau>0$ such that ${\mathscr L}^{d}(F_\tau)>0$, with
$$
F_\tau=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^d: \rho_0(x)\geq\tau,\,\,\sigma_x\neq\eta_x\}.
$$
If we define
$$
\tilde\eta_x:=
\begin{cases}
\eta_x & \text{if $x\in\mathbb{R}^d\setminus F_\tau$};
\\
\sigma_x & \text{if $x\in F_\tau$}
\end{cases}
$$
we obtain a different GRLF $\tilde\eta$ with $C(\tilde{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}})\leq C({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}})+C/\tau$, a contradiction. This proves uniqueness of ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$
and the representation of solutions to \eqref{eq:CE} in (ii). 
\smallskip
 
$(b)\Rightarrow(a)$. See Theorem~5.4 of \cite{Am_2004} (or Theorem~3.10 of \cite{AmCri_2014}). The strategy of the proof is to show that any GRLF $\eta_x$ is a Dirac
mass for ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ (thus proving (i) as well), arguing by contradiction and, in particular, using the set of points $x$ where this is not the case to build two
distinct solutions to \eqref{eq:CE} in the class $\mathcal R_+$ and with the same initial condition.

\smallskip

Now, let us prove (iii). Since $\eta_x=\delta_{\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x)}$ for ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$, it suffices to show the existence of a 
 ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-negligible Borel set $\mathcal N\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ such that the family of curves $\{\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^d\setminus\mathcal N}$ 
is forward nonbranching. Let now $\mu_d$ be the standard Gaussian in $\mathbb{R}^d$ (or any other probability measure mutually absolutely 
continuous with ${\mathscr L}^{d}$); it suffices to show the existence for
any $\epsilon>0$ of a Borel set $\mathcal N_\epsilon\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ such that  $\mu_d(\mathcal N_\epsilon)<2\epsilon$ and such that
the family of curves $\{\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^d\setminus\mathcal N_\epsilon}$ is forward nonbranching, as the $\mu_d$-negligible set
$\mathcal N=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathcal N_{2^{-k}}$ gives the result.

Now, fix $\epsilon>0$ and a compact set $K\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ with $\mu_d(\mathbb{R}^d\setminus K)<\epsilon$ and
$x\mapsto\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x)$ is continuous. Following a strategy similar to \cite{Bia_Bo_2020}, we will apply Theorem~\ref{thm:Kellerer} with $X=Y=\mathbb{R}^d$, $\mu_X=\mu_Y=\mu_d$ 
and
$$
C=\left\{(x,y)\in K\times K: (\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x),\mathbb{X}(\cdot,y))\in G\right\},
$$
where $G$ is the forward branching set in \eqref{eq:fwd_branching}.
Thanks to Lemma~\ref{lem:Borel} and the continuity of $x\mapsto\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x)$ on $K$, $C$ is a Borel set. The ``switching'' operator
$$
{\sf Sw}(\gamma,\tilde\gamma)(s):=
\begin{cases}
\gamma(s) &\text{if $s\in [0,t_{(\gamma,\tilde\gamma)}]$};\cr\cr
\tilde\gamma(s) &\text{if $s\in [t_{(\gamma,\tilde\gamma)},T]$},
\end{cases}
$$
where $t_{(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})}>0$ is the first time of intersection, is well defined on $G$ and
maps $(\gamma,\tilde\gamma)$ to a curve ${\sf Sw}(\gamma,\tilde\gamma)\neq\gamma$. 

Theorem~\ref{thm:Kellerer} and \eqref{eq:Ketterer1} grant that
either there exists $\Sigma\in{\mathscr P}\bigl(\mathbb{R}^d\times\mathbb{R}^d\bigr)$ having $\mu_d$ as marginals with $\Sigma(C)>\epsilon$, or there exist Borel sets $A,\,B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$
with $\mu_d(A)+\mu_d(B)>2-\epsilon$ and $(A\times B)\cap C=\emptyset$. If the second possibility occurs, we may take
$\mathcal N_\epsilon=\mathbb{R}^d\setminus (K\cap A\cap B)$, as
$$
\mu_d(A\cap B)\geq\mu_d(A)+\mu_d(B)-\mu_d(A\cup B)>1-\epsilon.
$$
To conclude the proof, it suffices to show that the first possibility does not occur. If such $\Sigma$ exists, write $\hat{\Sigma}={\bf 1}_C\Sigma$
and $\hat{\Sigma}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\hat{\Sigma}_x\,d\mu(x)$, with $\mu=\rho{\mathscr L}^{d}$, the marginal of $\hat{\Sigma}$ with respect to the first factor
and $\hat{\Sigma}_x\in{\mathscr P}\bigl(K\bigr)$. As $\mu(\mathbb{R}^d)=\Sigma(C)>0$, we have 
${\mathscr L}^{d}(F_\tau)>0$ for some $\tau>0$, with $F_\tau=\{\rho>\tau\}$. Now, we may replace $\eta_x=\delta_{\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x)}$ by 
$$
\tilde\eta_x:=
\begin{cases}
{\sf Sw}(\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x),\mathbb{X}(\cdot,y))_\#\hat{\Sigma}_x &\text{if $x\in F_\tau$;}\cr\cr
\eta_x & \text{if $x\in\mathbb{R}^d\setminus F_\tau$.}
\end{cases}
$$
It is clear that $\tilde\eta_x\neq\eta_x$, as ${\mathscr L}^{d}(F_\tau)>0$ and 
${\sf Sw}(\gamma,\tilde\gamma)\neq\gamma$ whenever $(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\in G$.
It is not difficult to show that still $\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ has bounded compression, whence the contradiction. 

Let us prove, indeed, that $\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ has bounded compression; for any Borel set $E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ and any $t\in [0,T]$
we can estimate from above 
$$
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}\bigl(\{\gamma:\ \gamma(t)\in E\}\bigr)\leq C({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}){\mathscr L}^{d}(E)+
\int_{F_\tau}\hat{\Sigma}_x\bigl(\{y:\ {\sf Sw}(\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x),\mathbb{X}(\cdot,y))(t)\in E\}\bigr)\,dx.
$$
By the very definition of ${\sf Sw}$, the second addendum in the right hand side can be estimated from above by
\begin{equation}
{\mathscr L}^{d}\bigl(\{x\in F_\tau:\ \mathbb{X}(t,x)\in E\}\bigr)+\int_{F_\tau}\hat{\Sigma}_x\bigl(\{y:\ \mathbb{X}(t,y)\in E\}\bigr)\,dx.
\end{equation}
The first term can be estimated once more with $C({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}){\mathscr L}^{d}(E)$; the second term, recalling that
$\mu_d\leq C_d{\mathscr L}^{d}$ and $\hat{\Sigma}\leq\Sigma$, by
$$
\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\hat{\Sigma}_x\bigl(\{y:\ \mathbb{X}(t,y)\in E\}\bigr)\,d\mu_d(x)\leq
\frac{1}{\tau}\mu_d(\{y:\mathbb{X}(t,y)\in E\})\leq\frac{C_d}{\tau}C({\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}){\mathscr L}^{d}(E),
$$
concluding the proof.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:main}
Assume  that any of the equivalent conditions in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} holds  and that there exists a GRLF. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] the RLF $\mathbb{X}$ exists and is unique, and ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_x=\delta_{\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x)}$ for ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$;
\item[(b)] there exists a ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-negligible Borel set $\mathcal N\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ such that 
the family of curves $\{\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^d\setminus\mathcal N}$ is forward nonbranching;
\item[(c)] If the same conditions
hold for $\tilde{{\bf b}}_t:=-{\bf b}_{T-t}$ then ${\mathscr L}^{d}\ll ({\sf ev}_T)_\#{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\mathbb{X}(T,\cdot)_\#{\mathscr L}^{d}$, 
and there exists a ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-negligible Borel set $\mathcal N\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ such that 
the family of curves $\{\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^d\setminus\mathcal N}$ is disjoint in $[0,T]$.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Statements (a) and (b) are an immediate consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. In order to prove (c), if
we call $\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\int\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_y\,d{\mathscr L}^{d}(y)$ the unique GRLF relative to $\tilde{\bf b}$, it is clear that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}'=j_\#\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ yields a solution of \eqref{eq:CE} with vector field ${\bf b}$,
where 
$$
j:C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^d)\to C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^d)\quad\text{is the time inversion, $j(\gamma)(t):=\gamma(T-t)$},
$$
as the bounded compression property at any time $t\in [0,T]$ and the property of being concentrated on solutions are preserved by this transformation.
In particular ${{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}}'=\tilde\rho_T\mathcal{L}^{d}(y)\otimes{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}}_y$, where ${\sf ev}_{T\#}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\tilde\rho_T\mathcal{L}^{d}$.
This implies that for ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-a.e. $y$ the measure $\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_y$ is concentrated on
the curves $\mathbb{X}(\cdot,x)$ such that $y=\mathbb{X}(T,x)$, as ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ is concentrated on this family of curves.  Since, in addition, 
$\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_y=\delta_{\mathbb{Y}(\cdot,y)}$ has to be a Dirac mass for ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-a.e. $y\in\mathbb{R}^d$, 
we obtain the existence of a Borel set $Y_0\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ of full ${\mathscr L}^{d}$-measure such that for all $y\in Y_0$ there
exists only one $x(y)$ such that $y=\mathbb{X}(T,x(y))$ and more generally $\mathbb{Y}(T-t,y)=\mathbb{X}(t,x(y))$ for all $t\in [0,T]$.  

As $x(y)=\mathbb{Y}(T,y)$, $y\in Y_0$, thanks to Lusin's theorem (applied for instance with the finite reference measure $\gamma_d$, mutually absolutely
continuous with ${\mathscr L}^{d}$)
we can assume with no loss of generality that $Y_0$ is the union of compact sets $K_i$ such that $\mathbb{Y}(T,\cdot)$ is 
continuous on $K_i$; it follows that $X_0=\mathbb{Y}(T,Y_0)$ is a Borel set. Now we can prove that ${\mathscr L}^{d}\ll \mathbb{X}(T,\cdot)_\#{\mathscr L}^{d}$,
more precisely $\mathbb{X}(T,\cdot)_\#{\mathscr L}^{d}\geq (C(\tilde{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}}))^{-1}{\mathscr L}^{d}$. Indeed, for any compact set $E\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ one has
\begin{eqnarray*}
{\mathscr L}^{d}\bigl(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^d:\ \mathbb{X}(T,x)\in E\}\bigr)&\geq& {\mathscr L}^{d}\bigl(\{x\in X_0:\ \mathbb{X}(T,x)\in E\}\bigr)\\
&=&{\mathscr L}^{d}\bigl(\mathbb{Y}(T,\cdot)(Y_0\cap E)\bigr)\\
&\geq&\frac 1{C(\tilde{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}})}{\mathscr L}^{d}\bigl(\{y:\ \mathbb{Y}(T,y)\in\mathbb{Y}(T,\cdot)(Y_0\cap E)\}\bigr)\\&\geq&\frac 1{C(\tilde{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}})}{\mathscr L}^{d}(Y_0\cap E)\bigr)
=\frac 1{C(\tilde{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}})}{\mathscr L}^{d}(E).
\end{eqnarray*}
Eventually, by repeating the argument symmetrically, we may take as $\mathcal N$ the union $\mathcal N_1\cup\mathcal N_2$, where $\mathcal N_1$ and $\mathcal N_2$ are
the Lebesgue negligible sets associated to $\mathbb{X}$ and $\mathbb{Y}$ respectively.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rep_div_BB_1}
D\cdot (\rho{\bf B})=\bar\rho{\mathscr H}^{d}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt
\vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits\{t=0\}-\rho_T{\mathscr H}^{d}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt
\vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits\{t=T\}
\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^d$.}
\end{equation}
In particular Proposition~\ref{pbasic}  gives 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:agreement}
{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}(\rho {\bf B},\nu_\Sigma)+{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}(\rho {\bf B},-\nu_\Sigma)=0\qquad
\text{${\mathscr H}^{d}$-a.e. on $\Sigma$}
\end{equation}
for any countably ${\mathscr H}^{d}$-rectifiable set $\Sigma\subseteq (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^d$. In addition, \eqref{goodac} gives:
\begin{equation*}
D^a\cdot (h(\rho){\bf B})=(h(\rho)-\rho h'(\rho))D^a\cdot {\bf B}\qquad\forall h\in C^1(\mathbb{R}).
\end{equation*}
Choosing $h_\epsilon(z)=\sqrt{\epsilon^2+(z^+)^2}-\epsilon\in C^1(\mathbb{R})$ and using the uniform control on the singular parts 
of $D \cdot (h_\epsilon(\rho){\bf B})$ provided by
\eqref{eq:verygoodac}, by approximation we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noac}
D^a\cdot (\rho^+{\bf B})=0.
\end{equation}

On the other hand, for any countably ${\mathscr H}^{d}$-rectifiable set $\Sigma\subseteq (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^d$, the chain rule for traces and Proposition~\ref{pbasic} give that for any $h\in C^1(\mathbb{R})$ and $R>0$, if we set $\Sigma_R:=\Sigma \cap ((0,T) \times B_R(0))$,

\begin{eqnarray}\label{eq:goodsigma1}
&&D^s\cdot (h(\rho){\bf B})(\Sigma_R)=\int_{\Sigma_R }({\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}(h(\rho){\bf B},\nu_\Sigma)+{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}(h(\rho){\bf B},-\nu_\Sigma))\,d{\mathscr H}^{d}=\\
&&\int_{\Sigma_R} {\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},\nu_\Sigma)  h\left(\frac{{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}(\rho{\bf B},\nu_\Sigma)}{{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},\nu_\Sigma)}\right)+
{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},-\nu_\Sigma)  h\left(\frac{{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}(\rho{\bf B},-\nu_\Sigma)}{{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},-\nu_\Sigma)}\right)\,d{\mathscr H}^{d}.\nonumber
\end{eqnarray}
Passing to the limit with the same approximating sequence $h_\varepsilon$ as before, we can set $h(z)=z^+$, notice that \eqref{eq:agreement} shows that points where ${\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},\nu_\Sigma)$ and
${\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},-\nu_\Sigma)$ have different sign give no contribution to the integral. Because of \eqref{eq:onesideddiv1}, the case when both
are negative can be ruled out, so that 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:goodsigma2}
D^s\cdot (\rho^+{\bf B})(\Sigma_R)=\int_{\Sigma_R \cap \{{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},\nu_\Sigma),\,{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},-\nu_\Sigma)\geq 0\}} |{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}(\rho{\bf B},\pm\nu_\Sigma)|\,d{\mathscr H}^{d}.
\end{equation}

Now, Proposition~\ref{precti} grants that ${\mathscr L}^{1}\mid_{[0,T]}\otimes |E^s{\bf b}_t|$ is concentrated on $J_{\bf B}$ and \eqref{eq:verygoodac} grants that the same
is true for $D^s\cdot (\rho^+{\bf B})$. Hence, we may choose as $\Sigma$ the set $J_{\bf B}\cap (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^d$ to obtain from \eqref{eq:noac}, \eqref{eq:goodsigma2}
and Lemma~\ref{lem:cylindrical} (choosing $R=R_i$ and sending $i \to \infty$)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:goodsigma3}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\rho_T^+\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\bar\rho^+\,dx=\int_{\{{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},\nu_\Sigma),\, {\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},-\nu_\Sigma)\geq 0\}
\cap (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^d}|{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}(\rho{\bf B},\pm\nu_\Sigma)|\,d{\mathscr H}^{d}.
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\rho\in {\mathcal R}_+$, by mass conservation the right hand side must vanish. Hence, we may apply
\eqref{eq:goodsigma2} to the difference $\rho=\rho^1-\rho^2$, with $\rho^i\in {\mathcal R}_+$ solving \eqref{eq:CE_initial} with
the same initial datum $\bar\rho$, to obtain from Lemma~\ref{lem:cylindrical} again
\begin{eqnarray*}
\int (\rho^1_T-\rho^2_T)^+\,dx&=&\int_{\{{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},\nu_\Sigma),\,{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},-\nu_\Sigma)\geq 0\}\cap (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^d}
\hskip -2cm |{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}((\rho^1-\rho^2){\bf B},\pm\nu_\Sigma)|\,d{\mathscr H}^{d}\\
&\leq&
\int_{\{{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},\nu_\Sigma),\,{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}({\bf B},-\nu_\Sigma)\geq 0\}\cap (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^d}
\hskip -2cm |{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}(\rho^1{\bf B},\pm\nu_\Sigma)|+|{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}(\rho^2{\bf B},\pm\nu_\Sigma)|\,d{\mathscr H}^{d}=0.
\end{eqnarray*}
Exchanging the roles of $\rho^1$ and $\rho^2$ and repeating this argument with any intermediate time $t\in (0,T)$ gives the result.

\label{lem:cylindrical}
If $\rho\in\mathcal R_+$ satisfies $D\cdot (\rho{\bf B})=\mu$ with $\mu$ finite in bounded subsets of $(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^d$, 
then there exist $R_i\to \infty$ such that

$$

Let $C=\sup_{s\in [0,T]}\|\rho_s\|_\infty$.
From \eqref{eq:rep_div_BB}, for all $R>0$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:seraop}
\int_{B_R}\rho_T\,dx-\int_{B_R}\bar\rho\,dx-D\cdot (\rho{\bf B})\bigl((0,T)\times B_R\bigr)=
\int_{(0,T)\times\partial B_R}{\rm Tr}^{{\rm in}}(\rho {\bf B},\nu_{(0,T)\times\partial B_R})\,d{\mathscr H}^{d}.
\end{equation}
where the normal is chosen to be the inner one to the cylinder $(0,T)\times B_R$.
Now, the growth condition \eqref{eq:growth} ensures 

\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
\lim_{i\to\infty}&2^{2-i}\int_{2^i}^{2^{i+1}}\int_{(0,T)\times\partial B_R}\rho|{\bf B}|\,d{\mathscr H}^{d}\,dR\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{2^i}^{2^{i+1}}\int_{(0,T)\times\partial B_R}\rho\frac{|{\bf B}|}{1+|x|}\,d{\mathscr H}^{d}\,dR\\
&\leq \lim_{i\to\infty} \int_{2^i}^{2^{i+1}}\int_{(0,T)\times\partial B_R}\rho\,d{\mathscr H}^{d}\,dR+ \int_{2^i}^{2^{i+1}}\int_{(0,T)\times\partial B_R}\rho\frac{|{\bf b}|}{1+|x|}\,d{\mathscr H}^{d}\,dR\\

\end{split}
\end{equation*}
where the last term goes to 0 by dominated convergence.

to conclude that the term in the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:seraop} with $R=R_i$ is infinitesimal as $i\to\infty$.

\label{defLagRep}
Assume that the vector field $\rho{\bf B}=(\rho,\rho{\bf b})$ satisfies $D\cdot (\rho{\bf B})=\mu\in{\mathscr M}\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\bigr)$.

\rho{\bf B}{\mathscr L}^{d+1}=\int_\Upsilon(\mathrm{Id}, \gamma)_\#\left((1,\gamma') {\mathscr L}^{1}\right)\,d{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(\gamma) 
\end{equation}
and
\begin{align}
\mu & =\int_\Upsilon\left[\delta_{(t_\gamma^-,\gamma(t_\gamma^-))}(d t, d x)-\delta_{(t_\gamma^+,\gamma(t_\gamma^+))}(d t, d x)\right] \, d{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(\gamma), \label{eq:concancellazioni}\\
|\mu| & =\int_\Upsilon\left[\delta_{(t_\gamma^-,\gamma(t_\gamma^-))}(d t, d x)+\delta_{(t_\gamma^+, \gamma(t_\gamma^+))}(d t, d x)\right] \, d{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(\gamma).\label{eq:nocancellazioni}
\end{align}

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{extended_lagrangian}
Let $\rho\in\mathcal {R}^{\mathcal{I}}_+$ for some $\mathcal{I}$ bounded open interval, then any vector field $\rho{\bf B}$ satisfying $D\cdot (\rho{\bf B})\in{\mathscr M}\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\bigr)$ admits a Lagrangian representation.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
In this proof we shall follow the language and notation of \cite{ARV}.
The 1-current  $S=\rho{\bf B}{\mathscr L}^{d+1}$ has locally finite mass, furthermore $\partial S\in{\mathscr M}\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\bigr)$, which means that $S$ is a locally normal current. By \cite[Theorem 1.4]{ARV} it is possible to decompose the 1-current $S$ as the sum of a cyclic current $S_c$
and an acyclic current $S_a$ in such a way that 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:decompositionmasscyclicacyclicm}
        \rho|{\bf B}|{\mathscr L}^{d+1}=\|S\|=\|S_a\|+\|S_c\|,
    \end{equation}
where $\|\cdot\|$ denotes the mass measure of a current. From \eqref{eq:decompositionmasscyclicacyclicm} we obtain that there exist 
$\rho_a , \rho_c \in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})$ nonnegative such that $\rho=\rho_a + \rho_c$, $S_a =\rho_a {\bf B}$ and $S_c =\rho_c {\bf B}$.
Next we show that $S$ is acyclic or, equivalently, that  $\rho_c=0$. 
    
Indeed, since $S_c$ is a cycle, one has $D\cdot(\rho_c{\bf B})=0$ in $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$,
hence $\rho_c$ is a nonnegative solution of the continuity equation with initial datum 0.
In particular by \eqref{eq:weak1} we obtain 
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\phi\rho_{c,t}\,dx=\int_0^t\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}{\bf b}_\tau\cdot\nabla\phi\rho_{c,\tau}\,dx\, d\tau
\quad\text{for all $\phi\in C^1_c(\mathbb{R}^d)$, $ t\geq0$.}$$
Fix $r>0$ and consider for $R\geq r$, $\phi_R\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb{R}^d)$ such that $0\leq\phi_R\leq 1$, $\phi_R\mid_{B_R(0)}= 1$ and 
$(1+|x|)|\nabla\phi_R(x)|\leq C$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ for some fixed constant $C>0$ independent of $R$. These conditions grant, for all $t>0$,
\begin{eqnarray*}
\int_{B_r (0)}\rho_{c,t}\,dx&\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\phi_R\,\rho_{c,t}\,dx\leq
\int_0^t\biggl|\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}{\bf b}_s\cdot\nabla\phi_R\rho_{c,s}\,dx\biggr|\,ds\\
&\leq& C\|\rho\|_\infty\int_0^T \int_{\{\phi_R<1\}} \frac{|{\bf b}^1_s|}{1+|x|}\,dx\,ds+
C\int_0^T \|\frac{|{\bf b}^2_s|}{1+|x|}\|_\infty\int_{\{\phi_R<1\}} \rho_s\,dx\,ds,
\end{eqnarray*}
with the last term going to 0 as $R\to\infty$ by dominated convergence, implying that $\rho_c =0$.
We can then apply \cite[Theorem 1.4]{ARV} and find 
a positive Borel measure ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ over the space $\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})$ of the open ended curves $\sigma(s)$ with 
locally finite length in $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$ (namely their intersection with any bounded set of $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$ has finite length)
such that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$-almost every curve $\sigma$ is injective and
\begin{align}
&S=\int_{\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})} S_\sigma d{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(\sigma), \label{decomp} \\
&\|S\|=\int_{\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})} \|S_\sigma\| d{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(\sigma),\label{masseq}\\
&\partial S=\int_{\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})} \partial S_\sigma d{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(\sigma), \label{eq:ARVboundary}\\
& \|\partial S\|=\int_{\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})} \|\partial S_\sigma\| d{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(\sigma).\label{eq:ARVvariationboundaryeq} 
\end{align}
where $S_\sigma$ denotes the current with locally finite mass in $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$ canonically induced by $\sigma\in \Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})$.
Testing $S$ against $\varphi\frac{{\bf B}}{|{\bf B}|}$ for any $\varphi\in C_c(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})$ nonnegative, it is possible to show that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$-a.e. curve $\sigma$ has the property that $\sigma'(s)$ is a nonnegative multiple of $\rho{\bf B}(\sigma(s))=\rho(\sigma(s))(1,{\bf b}(\sigma(s))$ and $\rho(\sigma(s))>0$ for ${\mathscr L}^{1}$-a.e. $s$ such that $|\sigma'(s)|>0$ (see for instance \cite{PhdBonicatto}).
Hence, up to a reparametrization, ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$-almost every curve $\sigma$ can be represented as $(t,\gamma(t))$, with $\gamma$ a solution of \eqref{eq:ODE} in an interval $(t_\gamma^- ,t_\gamma^+)\subseteq \mathcal{I}$. 

From now on, with a slight abuse, we will see ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ as a measure
on the curves $\gamma$, noticing that $S_\sigma=S_{(t,\gamma)}$. The fact that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ is concentrated on curves of locally finite length
in $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$ implies that, for ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$-a.e. curve $\gamma$, the possibly infinite limits $|\gamma(t_\gamma^+)| := \lim_{t \uparrow t_\gamma^+} |\gamma(t)| \quad \text{and} \quad |\gamma(t_\gamma^-)| := \lim_{t \downarrow t_\gamma^-} |\gamma(t)|$
exist.

Finally, we check that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ is indeed concentrated on curves of finite length.
Testing $S$ against $\frac{1}{|{\bf b}|(1+|x|)}(0,{\bf b})$, with $\tau_\gamma=(t_\gamma^++t_\gamma^-)/2$ we find 
\begin{align}
&\int\left|\log\left(\frac{1+|\gamma(t_\gamma^{\pm})|}{1+|\gamma(\tau_\gamma)|}\right)\right|\,d{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(\gamma)\leq\int\int_{t^-_{\gamma}}^{t^+_{\gamma}} \frac{|\dot\gamma(s)|}{1+|\gamma(s)|}ds\,d{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(\gamma)=\int\int_{t^-_{\gamma}}^{t^+_{\gamma}} \frac{|{\bf b}(\gamma(s))|}
{(1+|\gamma(s)|)}ds\,d{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(\gamma)\nonumber \\
&= S\left(\frac{1}{|{\bf b}|(1+|x|)}(0,{\bf b})\right)\leq
\|\rho\|_\infty \left\|\frac{|{\bf b}^1|}{1+|x|}\right\|_{L^1((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^d)}+\int_0^T\left\|\frac{|{\bf b}^2_s|}{1+|x|}\right\|_\infty\int\rho_s\,dx ds
<\infty.
\end{align}
It follows that the first integral is finite, hence ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ is concentrated on curves of finite length.
We conclude by noting that ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ is a Lagrangian representation.
Indeed since ${\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}$ is supported on curves of finite length, \eqref{rhoBBrep} follows from \eqref{decomp} whereas \eqref{eq:ARVboundary} and \eqref{eq:ARVvariationboundaryeq} written in terms of $\gamma$ yield \eqref{eq:concancellazioni} and \eqref{eq:nocancellazioni} respectively.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:meas}
Let $\Delta\subseteq\Upsilon$ be a $\sigma$-compact untangled family. Then 
the map $\gamma\mapsto\pi_\Delta(\gamma):=\sigma_{[\gamma]}\in\Upsilon_0$ is Borel. In addition, the map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defP}
P_\Delta:  \mathscr{G}(\Delta)\mapsto\Upsilon_0
\end{equation}
which maps $(t,x)\in\mathrm{Graph\,}\gamma$ with $\gamma\in\Delta$ to $\sigma_{[\gamma]}$, is Borel.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let us write $\Delta=\cup_p \Delta_p$ with $\Delta_p\subseteq \Delta_{p+1}$ compact, $p\geq 0$. Notice that
compactness of $\Delta_p$ grants a positive lower bound on the length of the intervals of curves $\gamma\in\Delta_p$. We denote by ${\bf J}_k$ the operator
associating to a pair $(\gamma,\tilde\gamma)\in\Delta\times\Delta$ with 
intersections of the domains having length larger than $2^{-k}$ the
new curve obtained by gluing the two curves. Since $\Delta$ is untangled, this construction is possible.

We now define recursively $\Delta_p^{(i)}$ as follows: $\Delta_p^{(0)}=\Delta_p$ and $\Delta_p^{(i+1)}$ is
obtained by applying the gluing ${\bf J}_i$ operator to admissible pairs of curves in $\Delta_p^{(i)}$ and $\Delta_p$.
It is not difficult to prove by induction on $i$ that all $\Delta_p^{(i)}$ are compact (here the lower bound on the length
of intersection plays a role), and that the graphs of all curves $\sigma_{[\gamma]}$
are finite or countable unions of graphs of curves in $\bigcup_{p,i}\Delta_p^{(i)}$.

Now, we fix $q$, $N$ with $N>q$ and we consider the map $\pi_{q,N}:\Delta_q\to\Upsilon$ which associates to a curve $\gamma\in \Delta_q$ the
curve whose graph is the union of the graphs of all curves in
$\bigcup_{p,i=0}^N \Delta_p^{(i)}$ containing $\gamma$ as subcurve. 
Thanks to the compactness of the domain and of the class of curves chosen to build $\pi_{q,N}(\gamma)$, this set-valued map is 
upper semicontinuous, hence Borel (see for instance \cite[Theorem II.20 and Corollary III.3]{Castaing}). It is also clear from
the construction that the graphs of $\pi_{q,N}(\gamma)$ are non decreasing with respect to $N$. 

Now, if we view $\pi_{q,N}$ as an $\Upsilon_0$-valued map, it is  Borel as well and it is clear that
$\pi_{q,N}(\gamma)$ converge as $N\to\infty$ to $\pi_\Delta(\gamma)=\sigma_{[\gamma]}$. This proves that the restriction
of the map $\gamma\mapsto \sigma_{[\gamma]}$ to $\Delta_q$ is Borel, and since $q$ is arbitrary we obtain that the map $\pi_\Delta$
is Borel on $\Delta$.

Finally, to prove that the map $P_\Delta$ is Borel we follow a similar argument, considering the $\Upsilon$-valued approximations
\begin{equation}
P_{q,N}:  \bigcup_{\gamma \in \Delta_q} \mathrm{Graph\,}\gamma\mapsto\Upsilon
\end{equation}

which maps $(t,x)\in\mathrm{Graph\,}\gamma$ with $\gamma\in\Delta_q$ to the curve $P_{q,N}(t,x)$ whose
graph is the union of all graphs in $\bigcup_{p,i=0}^N \Delta_p^{(i)}$ 
containing $(t,x)$.
To check that $P_{q,N}$ is Borel it is sufficient to show
(\cite[Theorem~III.2]{Castaing}) that  
$$
P_{q,N}^{-1}\bigl(\{\gamma: \gamma\cap U\neq\emptyset\}\bigr)
$$
is a Borel set for any open set $U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$. This set coincides with $$\mathscr{G}(\Delta_q)\cap\bigcup_{\sigma \in\bigcup_{p,i=0}^N \Delta_p^{(i)}\,\textit{s.t.}\,\mathrm{Graph\,}\sigma\cap U\neq\emptyset}\mathrm{Graph\,}\sigma$$ which is $\sigma$-compact, hence Borel.

    \pi_P(\gamma) := P(t,x)\quad\text{where $x=\gamma(t)$ for some $t\in (t_\gamma^-,t_\gamma^+)$, $\gamma\in\Delta$}
    \end{equation}

<!-- proposition-9: body_md -->
\label{prop:chainrule}
Let $h\in C^1(\mathbb{R})$,  ${\bf b}$ be such that all Lagrangian representations related to ${\bf b}$ are untangled and $\rho\in\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{I}}_+$ for some bounded open interval $\mathcal{I}$. Let ${\bf m}$, $(\sigma_a)_{a \in \mathcal{A}}$, $(w_a)_{a \in \mathcal{A}}$, $(\mu_a)_{a \in \mathcal{A}}$ be associated to the minimal partition via characteristics $P_{min}$ for $\rho{\bf B}$. Let also $u \in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})$ be such that $u_a w_a \in BV_{\rm loc}(I_a)$ for $\mathbf{m}$-a.e. $a \in \mathcal{A}$, and denote $\mu_a':=D(u_a w_a)$. \\
 Then, for $\mathbf{m}$-a.e. $a \in \mathcal{A}$ the functions $u_a$ and $h(u_a)w_a$ belong to $BV_{\rm loc}(I_a)$ and
$D(h(u_a) w_a)$ is representable by
    \begin{align}
        &\sum_i \,[h(u_a(t_i^+))w_a(t_i^+)-h(u_a(t_i^-))w_a(t_i^-)]\delta_{t_i}+h'(\tilde{u}_a) (Du_a)^{\rm cont}\tilde{w}_a+h(\tilde{u}_a)(Dw_a)^{\rm cont}
        \label{eq:mu_a_h} \\
        &=\sum_i \, [h(u_a(t_i^+))w_a(t_i^+)-h(u_a(t_i^-))w_a(t_i^-)]\delta_{t_i}+h'(\tilde{u}_a)(\mu'_a)^{\rm cont}+(h(\tilde{u}_a)-\tilde{u}_ah'(\tilde{u}_a))\mu_a^{\rm cont},\nonumber
    \end{align}
where $\{t_i\}_i$ are jump points either of $u_a$ or of $w_a$.
<!-- end proposition-9 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
As $P_{min}$ is a minimal partition for $\rho{\bf B}$, we have $\inf_{J_a}\tilde{w}_a>0$ on any compact sub-interval $J_a$ of $I_a$, thus 
Vol'pert's Chain Rule and the Leibniz formula for the products of locally bounded $BV$ functions (see e.g. \cite[Theorem~3.96, Example~3.97]{AFP_00})
yield that $w_a^{-1} \in BV_{\rm loc}(I_a)$ and $u_a=w_a^{-1}(u_aw_a)\in BV_{\rm loc}(I_a)$ as well.
The formula \eqref{eq:mu_a_h} is then a simple consequence of the chain rule applied to $h(u_a)$ and of the Leibniz rule
applied to $h(u_a)$ and $w_a$.\qedhere

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{chain_BB}
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:chainrule} one has 
$$
D\cdot(h(u)\rho{\bf B}\mathscr{L}^{d+1})=\int (\mathrm{Id},\sigma_a)_\# D(h(u_a)w_a) \, d\mathbf{m}(a),
$$
for any $h \in C^1(\mathbb{R})$, with $D(h(u_a)w_a)$ representable as in \eqref{eq:mu_a_h}.
