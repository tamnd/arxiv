<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p>0$, and let $d\geq 2$. Then $\operatorname{PCF}_d\subseteq\mathscr{P}_{d}$ is a countable union of subvarieties, each of dimension at most $\lfloor d/p\rfloor$, and this bound is attained by infinitely many components in all cases except $(p, d)=(2, 2)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:pointwise}
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p>0$ and let $f\in k[z]$ be a PCF polynomial of degree $d\geq 2$. Then $f$ is conjugate to a polynomial in $\mathscr{P}_{d}$, and every conjugate of $f$ in $\mathscr{P}_{d}$ has coefficients generating an extension of $\mathbb{F}_p$ of transcendence degree at most $\lfloor d/p\rfloor$. Moreover, there are infinitely many pairwise non-conjugate examples of PCF polynomials $f$ defined over fields of positive characteristic witnessing this bound in all cases except $(p, d)=(2, 2)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:char2}
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $2$, and let \[f(z)=z^d+a_{d-1}z^{d-1}+\cdots+a_1z\in k[z]\] be a PCF polynomial. Then exactly one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $d$ is odd and $f(z)=zH(z)^2$ for a monic polynomial $H$, and so in particular $f$ is defined over the field $\mathbb{F}_2(a_1, a_3,  ..., a_{d-2})$;
\item $d$ is odd and $f$ is not of the above form, and every coefficient of $f$ is algebraic over the field
$\mathbb{F}_2\!\left(a_2, a_4, \ldots, a_{d-1}\right)$; or
\item  $d$ is even and for some $1\leq e<d$ every coefficient of $f$ is algebraic over the field
$\mathbb{F}_2\!\left(a_2, a_4, \ldots, a_{d-2}, a_e\right)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- question-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{M}_d$ be the moduli space of rational functions of degree $d\geq 2$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and let $f\in\mathcal{M}_d$ be a PCF point over some extension of $\mathbb{F}_p$. How large can the transcendence degree of the field of moduli of $f$ be? What is the largest transcendence degree that occurs for maps $f$ with arbitrarily large critical orbits?
<!-- end question-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:valcrit}
Let $L/K$ be an extension of fields and let $\alpha\in L$. Then $\alpha$ is algebraic over $K$ if and only if $|\alpha|\leq 1$ for every non-archimedean absolute value on $L$ that restricts to the trivial absolute value on $K$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
One direction is familiar. If $\alpha\neq 0$ is algebraic over $K$, let $P(T)\in K[T]$ be its minimal polynomial. For any non-archimedean absolute value $|\cdot|$ on $L$ restricting trivially to $K$, the assumption $|\alpha|>1$ would give $0=|P(\alpha)|=|\alpha|^{\deg(P)}\neq 0$ by the ultrametric inequality, and hence a contradiction. So we have $|\alpha|\leq 1$ for every non-archimedean absolute value on $L$ that extends the trivial absolute value on $K$.

On the other hand, suppose that $\alpha$ is transcendental over $K$, so that $K(\alpha)\cong K(T)$ is just the field of rational functions in one variable over $K$. The familiar absolute value
\[|g_0(T)/g_1(T)|:=\exp\left(\deg(g_0)-\deg(g_1)\right)\]
for polynomials $g_i$
induces an absolute value on $K(\alpha)$ which restricts trivially to $K$, and has $|\alpha|=e>1$. By a theorem of Chevalley (see for example \cite[Theorem~1, p.~107]{Ribenboim}) this absolute value can be extended to $L$, proving the contrapositive of the other direction of the claim.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:escape}
Let $|\cdot|$ be a nonarchimedean absolute value on a field containing the coefficients of
\[
f(z)=z^d+a_{d-1}z^{d-1}+\cdots+a_0,
\]
(where $d\geq 2$) and set
\[
R_f=\max\left\{1,\; |a_j|^{1/(d-j)}: 0\leq j< d\right\}.
\]
If $|\alpha|>R_f$ then $|f^k(\alpha)|=|\alpha|^{d^k}\to\infty$ as $k\to\infty$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $|z|>R_f$ then for each $0\leq j<d$ we have $|z|^{d-j}>|a_j|$, whence $|a_jz^j|<|z|^d$. By the ultrametric inequality $|f(z)|=|z|^d$. Since $|f(z)|=|z|^d>|z|>R_f$, the claim iterates.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:boundPCF}
Let  $k$ be  an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, and let \[f(z)=z^d+a_{d-1}z^{d-1}+\cdots+a_1z\in k[z]\] be a PCF polynomial with $p\mid d$. Then either $f$ is inseparable, or else the coefficients of $f$ are algebraic over $F=\mathbb{F}_p(a_e, a_p, ..., a_{d-p})$,
where $e$ is the maximal index with $p\nmid e$ and $a_e\neq 0$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $|\cdot|$ be an absolute value of $k$ which restricts trivially to $F$, suppose that $f$ is separable, and write $f(z)=H(z^p)+G(z)$, where $G\neq 0$ contains no monomial of degree divisible by $p$. We have
\[f'(z)=G'(z)=ea_e\prod_{i=1}^{e-1} (z-c_i),\]
with $e=\deg(G)$
and so (comparing coefficients)
\[ja_j = (-1)^{e-j} ea_e \sigma_{e-j}(c_1, ..., c_{e-1}),\]
where $\sigma_n$ is the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $n$. For $j$ with $p\nmid j$ and $\rho =\max\{1,  |c_1|, ..., |c_{e-1}|\}$, we immediately obtain from $|a_e|=1$ that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aj}|a_j|\leq \rho ^{e-j}.\end{equation}
If $\rho>1$ and $c$ is a critical point with $|c|=\rho$, then~\eqref{eq:aj} gives $|G(c)|\leq \rho^e$, while the triviality of $|\cdot|$ on $F$ gives
\[|f(c)-G(c)|=|H(c^p)|=|c|^d=\rho^d>\rho^e,\]
and hence $|f(c)|=\rho^d$. But Lemma~\ref{lem:escape} with~\eqref{eq:aj} and the fact that $f$ is PCF gives $|f(c)|\leq \rho$, contradicting $\rho>1$.

It must be that $\rho\leq 1$, and so $|c_i|\leq 1$ for all $i$. But then~\eqref{eq:aj} gives $|a_j|\leq 1$ for all $j$ with $p\nmid j$. Since $|\cdot|$ was an arbitrary absolute value whose restriction to $F$ was trivial, Lemma~\ref{lem:valcrit} allows us to conclude that $a_j$ is algebraic over $F$ for all $j$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:degen}
Suppose that $p\nmid d$. For every PCF polynomial $f\in \mathscr{P}_{d}$ there is a polynomial  $g\in \mathscr{N}_{d}$ whose coefficients generate a field of the same transcendence degree as those of $f$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Now suppose that \[f(z)=z^d+a_{d-1}z^{d-1}+\cdots +a_1 z\] is PCF, and let $L=\mathbb{F}_p(a_1, ..., a_{d-1}, c_1, ..., c_{d-1})$ where $f'(z)=d\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}(z-c_i)$. Since the $c_i$ are algebraic over the field generated by the coefficients of $f$, the transcendence degree of $L$ is the same as the transcendence degree of the coefficient field.

In $L[t]$, let $A_j=t^{d-j}a_j$ and $C_i=t c_i$ and let \[R=\mathbb{F}_p[t, A_1, ..., A_{d-1}, C_1, ..., C_{d-1}]\subseteq L[t]\] be the Rees algebra, which is finitely generated as an algebra over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and has fraction field $L(t)$. Let $\mathfrak{q}\supseteq tR$ be a minimal prime containing $tR$, and let $E$ be the field of fractions of $R/\mathfrak{q}$. Because $t$ is nonzero and not a unit, the principal ideal theorem shows that $\mathfrak{q}$ has height 1. Since $R$ is finitely generated  over $\mathbb{F}_p$, we have $\dim(R/\mathfrak{q})+\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{q})=\dim R$ which is the transcendence degree of $L(t)$ (see \cite[Theorem~A and Corollary~13.4]{Eisenbud})), so $E$ has transcendence degree one less, or in other words the transcendence degree of $L$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.  We will show that there is a $g\in \mathscr{N}_{d}$ whose coefficients generate a field over which $E$ is algebraic.

Set \[F(z)=z^d+A_{d-1}z^{d-1}+\cdots +A_1 z\in R[z].\] A direct computation shows that $F'(z)=d\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}(z-C_i)$.


Let $Y_{i, m}=t^{d^m}f^m(c_i)$ for all $m$, including $Y_{i, 0}=C_i$. Note that, since
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ynot}Y_{i,m+1} = Y_{i,m}^{d} +   \sum_j A_jt^{(d-j)(d^m-1)}Y_{i,m}^{j},\end{equation}
we have by induction $Y_{i, m}\in R$. Also, a calculation gives $Y_{i, 1}=F(C_i)$, and then an induction with~\eqref{eq:Ynot} gives $Y_{i, m}\equiv F(C_i)^{d^{m-1}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ tR)$ for all $m\geq 1$.

 Since $f$ is PCF, there exists for each $i$ an $m_i>n_i$ such that $f^{m_i}(c_i)=f^{n_i}(c_i)$, and so \[Y_{i, m_i}=t^{d^{m_i}}f^{m_i}(c_i)=t^{d^{m_i}}f^{n_i}(c_i)=t^{d^{m_i}-d^{n_i}}Y_{i, n_i}\in tR\subseteq  \mathfrak{q}.\]
In other words, if $\overline{x}$ denotes the image of $x\in R$ in the residue field $E$, we have $\overline{F}(\overline{C_i})^{d^{m_i-1}}=0$ for all $i$, and hence $\overline{F}(\overline{C_i})=0$. This shows that $g=\overline{F}\in \mathscr{N}_{d}(E)$. But $R$ is generated over $\mathbb{F}_p$ by $t\in\mathfrak{q}$ and the $A_i$ and $C_j$, so $E$ is generated over $\mathbb{F}_p$ by the $\overline{A_i}$ and $\overline{C_j}$. Since the $\overline{C_j}$ are algebraic over the $\overline{A_i}$, the $\overline{A_j}$ on their own generate a subfield of $E$ over which $E$ is algebraic.

We have constructed an example defined over some field $E$, but it is worth noting that we could take our $g$ to be defined over the same algebraically closed field $k$ as $f$. Specifically, if $x_1, ..., x_\tau$ is a transcendence basis of $E/\mathbb{F}_p$, and $y_1, ..., y_\tau\in\mathbb{F}_p(a_1, ..., a_{d-1})\subseteq k$ are algebraically independent, then the map $x_i\to y_i$ extends to an embedding $\sigma:E\to k$, since $k$ is algebraically closed, and $\sigma(g)\in \mathscr{N}_{d}(k)$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:abc}
Let $A$, $B$, $C$ be polynomials with coefficients in $k$ such that $AB=C$, and $A$ is monic, and $B$ (equivalently $C$) is nonzero. Then the coefficients of $A$ and $B$ are algebraic over the coefficients of $C$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Suppose that $B$ has leading coefficient $B_n$. The roots of $C$ are algebraic over the field generated by the coefficients of $C$, so the roots of $A$ and $B$ are algebraic over this field too. But the coefficients of $A$ are contained in the field generated by the roots, so $A$ is defined over some algebraic extension of the field generated by the coefficients of $C$. The same is true for the coefficients of the monic polynomial $B/B_n$. But since $A$ is monic, $B_n$ is also the leading coefficient of $C$ and hence is in the field generated by the coefficients of $C$, so the coefficients of $B$ are algebraic over that field too.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:phifinite}
Fix integers $t\geq 1$ and $r_1, ..., r_t\geq 1$, set $R=r_1+\cdots +r_t$, assume $p\nmid R$, and set $\ell=\lfloor R/p\rfloor$. Define polynomials \[b_0, ..., b_{R-1}, c_0, ..., c_{t-2} \in \mathbb{F}_p[\alpha_1, ..., \alpha_t]\] by equating coefficients of powers of $T$ in
\[B(T):=T^R+b_{R-1}T^{R-1}+\cdots +b_0=\prod_{i=1}^t (T-\alpha_i)^{r_i}\]
and
\[C(T):=RT^{t-1}+c_{t-2}T^{t-2}+\cdots +c_0 =  \sum_{i=1}^{t}r_i\prod_{j\neq i}(T-\alpha_j).\]
Then 
             
                                                      
                                                                                               
   
                
each $\alpha_i$ is integral over $\mathbb{F}_p[b_0, b_p, ..., b_{\ell p}, c_0, ..., c_{t-2} ]$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Note that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deriv}
B'(T)=C(T)\prod_{i=1}^t(T-\alpha_i)^{r_i-1}
\end{equation}
over $\mathbb{F}_p[\alpha_1, ..., \alpha_t]$.
We are claiming that $\mathbb{F}_p[\alpha_1, ..., \alpha_t]$ is finitely generated as a module over the subring $\mathbb{F}_p[b_0,b_p,\ldots,b_{\ell p},c_0,\ldots,c_{t-2}]$. Since the $b_i$ and $c_j$ are homogeneous polynomials of the $\alpha_n$,  it suffices to show that the only solution to
\[b_{0}=b_p=\cdots = b_{\ell p} = c_0 = \cdots = c_{t-2}=0\] in $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ is at $\alpha_1=\cdots=\alpha_t=0$.

Suppose that for some value of $\alpha_1, ..., \alpha_t\in \overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ we have $b_0=\cdots =b_{\ell p}=0$ and $c_0=\cdots =c_{t-2}=0$, so in particular $C(T)=RT^{t-1}$ (with $R\neq 0$). Write $S=\{i :\alpha_i=0\}$ and set $s=\sum_{i\in S}r_i$. Note that
$\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}(B)=s$ and so in particular $b_s\neq 0$, where $b_R:=1$ in the case that $s=R$. Since $b_{ip}=0$ for all $i$ we have $p\nmid s$ and $s\geq 1$. The product rule for the derivative with~\eqref{eq:deriv} then gives
\begin{equation*}s-1=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}B'(T)=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0} C + \sum_{i\in S}(r_i-1)=t-1+s-\# S.\end{equation*}
We have $\# S = t$ and so $\alpha_i=0$ for all $i$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:twocases}
Let $f\in \mathscr{N}_{d}$ and assume $p\nmid d$. Then the coefficients of $f$ are algebraic over the coefficients of either $f^{[0]}$ or $f^{[r]}$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Now, let $f\in \mathscr{N}_{d}$ over some algebraically closed extension $k/\mathbb{F}_p$. We may write $f(z)=H(z)^p B(z)$ with
\[H(z)=z^M+h_{M-1}z^{M-1}+\cdots + h_0=\prod_{i=1}^M (z-\gamma_i)\]
and
\[B(z)=z^R+b_{R-1}z^{R-1}+\cdots+ b_0,\]
such that $B$ has no root of multiplicity $p$ or greater. Set $R=\ell p + r$, with $0\leq r < p$.
Note that $R\equiv d\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)$, and in particular $\deg(B)\geq 1$. We will show that the coefficients of $f$ are algebraic over $F=\mathbb{F}_p(b_0, b_p, ..., b_{\ell p}, \gamma_1, ..., \gamma_M)$. Note that this field is generated by $\ell+1+M=n+1$ elements over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and so has transcendence degree at most $n+1$. But in fact
\[0=f(0)=(-1)^Mb_0\gamma_1^p\cdots \gamma_M^p\]
implies that one of the claimed generators is 0, and so the transcendence degree of $F/\mathbb{F}_p$ is at most $n$.


We may write $B(z)=\prod_{i=1}^t(z-\alpha_i)^{r_i}$ with $t\geq 1$, $r_i\geq 1$, and $\alpha_i\in k$ distinct. Write also $C(z)= \sum_{i=1}^{t}r_i\prod_{j\neq i}(z-\alpha_j)$ and note that
\[f'(z)=H(z)^pC(z)\prod_{i=1}^t (z-\alpha_i)^{r_i-1}.\]
Since $f\in \mathscr{N}_{d}$, and since $C$ and $B$ are relatively prime,  the roots of $C$ must be among the roots of $H$. Consequently, the coefficients $c_0, ..., c_{t-2}$ of $C(z)$ are contained in $F$, and so by Lemma~\ref{lem:phifinite} the roots $\alpha_1, ..., \alpha_t$ of $B$ are algebraic over $F$. But the coefficients of $f$ are in the extension of $\mathbb{F}_p(\alpha_1, ..., \alpha_t, \gamma_1, ..., \gamma_M)$ which we have just shown to be finite over $F$ so our first claim is demonstrated.

Write $H(z)^p=\tilde{H}(z^p)$, so the coefficients of $\tilde{H}$ are $h_0^p, ..., h_M^p$. Note that the roots of $H$ are algebraic over the coefficients of $H$, and hence over the coefficients of $\tilde{H}$, and so $F$ is an algebraic extension of field generated by the coefficients of $\tilde{H}$ and those of $B^{[0]}$. If $B^{[0]}\neq 0$, then $f^{[0]}=\tilde{H}B^{[0]}$ gives us that the coefficients of $\tilde{H}$ and $B^{[0]}$ are algebraic over the coefficients of $f^{[0]}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:abc}. If, on the other hand, $B^{[0]}=0$, we have just shown that the coefficients of $f$ are algebraic over the field generated by the coefficients of $\tilde{H}$. For any $j$ with $f^{[j]}\neq 0$, the relation $f^{[j]}=\tilde{H}B^{[j]}$ gives that the coefficients of $\tilde{H}$ are algebraic over the coefficients of $f^{[j]}$. Note that $f^{[r]}\neq 0$ as $b_R=1$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:fofr}
Suppose that $p\nmid d$. The coefficients of any PCF polynomial $f\in \mathscr{P}_{d}$ generate an extension of $\mathbb{F}_p$ of transcendence degree at most $n$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
From Lemma~\ref{lem:degen}, it suffices to prove that the coefficients of any $f\in \mathscr{N}_{d}$ generate an extension of transcendence degree at most $n$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:twocases}, the coefficients of $f$ are algebraic over the coefficients of either $f^{[0]}$ or $f^{[r]}$. Since $f(0)=0$, the polynomial $f^{[0]}$ is a sum of at most $n$ monomials, and so in this case we have the coefficients of $f$ algebraic over an extension of $\mathbb{F}_p$ by $n$ or fewer coefficients of $f$. The polynomial $f^{[r]}$ is monic of degree $n$, so $f^{[r]}$ has at most $n$ coefficients not already in $\mathbb{F}_p$. Again, we have the coefficients of $f$ algebraic over a field generated by at most $n$ of these coefficients.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:ptw}
For every $d\geq 2$ with $(p, d)\neq (2, 2)$, there exist infinitely many  PCF $f\in \mathscr{P}_{d}$, no two related by a conjugacy composed with a field automorphism, such that the coefficients of $f$ generate an extension of $\mathbb{F}_p$ of transcendence degree  exactly $\lfloor d/p\rfloor$.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Suppose first that $d=np+r$ with $r\neq 0$ and $n\geq 1$, let $\zeta, u_2, ..., u_n$ be algebraically independent over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and let $K=\overline{\mathbb{F}_p(\zeta, u_2, ..., u_n)}$. Over $K[T]$, let
\[F_T(z)=(z-\zeta)^{np}+Tz^{p-r}+\sum_{i=2}^n u_iz^{ip-r},\]
and $f_T(z)=z^rF_T(z)$
so that
\[f'_T(z)=rz^{r-1}(z-\zeta)^{np}.\]
Note that $f_T$ is PCF if and only if the orbit of $\zeta$ is finite, and note also that since $u_2, ..., u_n, (-\zeta)^{np}$ are among the coefficients of $f$, the subfield of $K$ generated by the coefficients after specializing to any $T=u_1$ algebraic over this field still has transcendence degree $n$.

For $P_m(T)=F_T(f^{m-1}_{T}(\zeta))$ we claim that choosing $u_1\in K$ with $P_m(u_1)=0$ will cause $\zeta$ to have exact preperiod $m$ under $f_{u_1}$. Certainly, $P_m(u_1)=0$ implies $f^m_{u_1}(\zeta)=0$, by definition. On the other hand, $f^{m-1}_{u_1}(\zeta)=0$ implies $P_m(u_1)=F_{u_1}(0)=(-\zeta)^{np}\neq 0$, a contradiction, so it must be that $\zeta$ has exact preperiod $m$ under $f_{u_1}$. It remains to show that $P_m(T)$ is nonconstant, so that we may choose such a $u_1$. One can confirm directly that $P_1(T)$ has degree 1 in $T$.  Note that for any non-constant $Q(T)\in K[T]$, we have $\deg_T(F_T(Q))=np\deg_T(Q)$ unless possibly there is cancellation between the terms $Q^{np}$ and $T Q^{p-r}$. For these to be of the same degree, it must be that $((n-1)p+r)\deg(Q)=1$ and hence $\deg(Q)=n=r=1$, so $d=p+1$. If we are not in this case, $P_m(T)$ is non-constant in $T$ for all $m\geq 1$, (indeed $\deg(P_m)=npd^{m-2}$ for $m\geq 2$) so we may choose $u_1\in K$ with $P_m(u_1)=0$. Note that the polynomials $f_{u_1}(z)\in K[z]$ are PCF and pairwise non-conjugate for different $m$, as the preperiods of the critical orbits are invariant under conjugacy and field automorphisms.

The case $\deg(Q)=1$ and $d=p+1$ occurs only once. Here we have $f^0_T(\zeta)=\zeta$, $f_T(\zeta)= T\zeta^{p}+O(1)$, where $O(1)$ is a term in $K$, and so
\[P_2(T)=\zeta^{p^2-p}(\zeta^p+1)T^p-\zeta^p,\]
showing that the polynomial has the expected degree despite the potential for cancellation in the leading coefficient.

In the case $d=np$ with $n\geq 2$, we consider the similar family
\[f_T(z)=z^d+\lambda z^{p-1}(z-\zeta)^{(n-1)p}+Tz^p+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1} u_j z^{pj}\]
with
\[f'(z)=-\lambda z^{p-2}(z-\zeta)^{(n-1)p},\]
so again $f$ is PCF if and only if $\zeta$ is preperiodic for $f$. We take $\lambda, \zeta, u_2, .., u_{n-1}$ to be algebraically independent and solve for $u_1$. Note that the coefficients of $f$ include $\lambda$, $\lambda(-\zeta)^{(n-1)p}, u_2, ..., u_{n-1}$ which are algebraically independent, and specializing at $T=u_1$ algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_p(\lambda, \zeta, u_2, ..., u_{n-1})$ gives a polynomial whose coefficients generate a field of transcendence degree $n$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

Let
\[F_T(z)=z^{d-p+1}+\lambda (z-\zeta)^{(n-1)p}+T z+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1} u_j z^{pj-p+1},\]
and let $P_m(T)=F_T(f^{m-1}_T(\zeta))$. As above, $f_T(z)=z^{p-1}F_T(z)$, so $\zeta$ has tail length exactly $m$ for $f_{u_1}(z)$ whenever $P_m(u_1)=0$. It suffices to show that the $P_m$ are nonconstant, but we check that for $m\geq 2$ we have
\[\deg(P_m)=(d-p+1)d^{m-2},\]
while $\deg(P_1)=1$.
The roots of the $P_m$ produce infinitely many pairwise non-conjugate PCF polynomials with $n$ algebraically independent coefficients.

We are left with the cases $d\leq p$. For $d<p$ we simply need to exhibit infinitely many pairwise non-conjugate PCF maps, which we can do by considering the family $f_T(z)=z^d+Tz^{d-1}=z^{d-1}(z+T)$. The critical points are $\zeta=-(d-1)T/d$ and, if $d\geq 3$, the fixed point $z=0$. Letting $P_m(T)=f_T^{m-1}(\zeta)+T$, we have $P_1(T)=T/d$ and $\deg(P_m)=d^{m-1}$ for $m\geq 2$, while $\operatorname{ord}_T(P_m)=1$ for all $m\geq 1$. So for each $m\geq 2$ the polynomial $P_m$ has a nonzero root $u_1\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$. Since $f_{u_1}$ vanishes only at $0$ and $-u_1$, the equation $P_m(u_1)=0$ says that $f_{u_1}^{m-1}(\zeta)=-u_1\neq 0$ and $f_{u_1}^m(\zeta)=0$, and any earlier zero in the orbit of $\zeta$ would force $f_{u_1}^{m-1}(\zeta)=0$. So $\zeta$ has preperiod exactly $m$ under $f_{u_1}$, while the only other critical point, when there is one, is fixed.

For $d=p\neq 2$ let $\lambda$ be transcendental over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and use $f_T(z)=z^p+\lambda z^2+Tz$. Here $\zeta=-T/(2\lambda)$ is the unique critical point. Write $f_T(z)=zF_T(z)$, so that $F_T(z)=z^{p-1}+\lambda z+T$, and let $P_m(T)=F_T(f^{m-1}_T(\zeta))$. One confirms that $\deg(P_m) = (p-1) p^{m-1}$ and $\operatorname{ord}_T(P_m)=1$ for all $m\geq 1$, so each $P_m$ has a nonzero root $u_1$, algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_p(\lambda)$. For such a root we have $f^m_{u_1}(\zeta)=0$, while $f^{m-1}_{u_1}(\zeta)=0$ would give $P_m(u_1)=F_{u_1}(0)=u_1\neq 0$. So $\zeta$ has preperiod exactly $m$ under $f_{u_1}$, and the coefficients $\lambda$ and $u_1$ of $f_{u_1}$ generate a field of transcendence degree exactly $1=\lfloor d/p\rfloor$. These specializations again have distinct critical portraits for distinct $m$.

In the case $d=p=2$, the polynomial $f(z)=z^2+tz$ is the generic point of $\mathscr{P}_{2}$ and is PCF.

The equivalence of Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:pointwise} is essentially by definition.

 If $V$ is an irreducible component of the PCF locus in $\mathscr{P}_{d}$ defined over an algebraically closed field $k$, then the function field $k(V)$ has transcendence degree $\dim(V)$ over $k$, and the coordinate functions $a_{d-1}, ..., a_1$ on $V$ give a PCF polynomial $f(z)=z^d+a_{d-1}z^{d-1}+\cdots +a_1z$ over $k(V)$, and so \[\dim(V)=\operatorname{trdeg}_k k(a_1, ..., a_{d-1})\leq \operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb{F}_p} \mathbb{F}_p(a_1, ..., a_{d-1})\leq n\] by Theorem~\ref{thm:pointwise}.
 
 
  On the other hand, let $f(z)\in\mathscr{P}_{d}$ be a PCF polynomial whose coefficients generate an extension $F$ of $\mathbb{F}_p$ of transcendence degree $s$. Since $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_p$, the field $F\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ has transcendence degree $s$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$, and it is the function field of an irreducible subvariety $W$ of $\mathscr{P}_{d}$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ of dimension $s$, namely the closure of the point whose coordinates are the coefficients of $f$. As $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ is algebraically closed, $W$ is geometrically irreducible, so $V=W\times_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}}k$ is an irreducible subvariety of $\mathscr{P}_{d}$ over $k$ of dimension $s$. The coordinates of the generic point of $V$ satisfy exactly the same polynomial relations over $\mathbb{F}_p$ as the coefficients of $f$, and being PCF is detected by such relations (the equations defining the $Z_{m, m'}$ above), so the generic point of $V$ is PCF and $V$ is contained in the PCF locus.

 Now take, for each $m\geq 2$, the polynomial $f_m$ constructed in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:ptw} (excluding the case $d=p=2$), whose coefficients generate a field of transcendence degree $n$ and which has a critical point of exact preperiod $m$, every other critical point being fixed. The construction above gives a subvariety $V_m\subseteq\mathscr{P}_{d}$ over $k$ of dimension $n$ with PCF generic point. Since the critical orbit relations are also polynomial relations over $\mathbb{F}_p$ in the coefficients, the generic point of $V_m$ has maximal critical preperiod exactly $m$, and so $V_m\neq V_{m'}$ for $m\neq m'$. Indeed the $V_m$ are pairwise non-conjugate, as conjugacy preserves critical preperiods. Finally, the dimension bound prevents $V_m$ from being properly contained in any larger subvariety of the PCF locus, so each $V_m$ is a component.

In the case of even $d$, Lemma~\ref{lem:boundPCF} already gives an explicit subset of the coefficients of $f$ over which the others are algebraic, for a PCF polynomial of degree $d$. So we consider just the case of odd $d$. A key relation holds here, namely
\[f(z)-zf'(z)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_{2i}z^{2i}.\]
First, suppose that $a_{2i}=0$ for all $i$. Then $f(z)=zH(z)^2$ for some polynomial $H$, and we are in the first case of the theorem. Suppose otherwise that some $a_{2i}\neq 0$, and let $j$ be the maximal such index. Then for any absolute value $|\cdot|$ on the field generated by the coefficients and critical points of $f$ trivial on $F=\mathbb{F}_p(a_2, a_4, ..., a_{d-1})$, if we have a $f'(c)=0$ with $|c|>1$ then
\[|f(c)|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^n a_{2i}c^{2i}\right|=|c|^{2j}>|c|.\]
Taking $|c|$ maximal, and arguing as in the escape-rate section, we contradict $f$ being PCF. So it must be that $|c|\leq 1$ for every critical point of $f$, and since $|\cdot|$ was arbitrary we see that the critical points of $f$ are algebraic over $F$. But
\[\prod_{i=1}^{d-1} (z-c_i)=f'(z)=z^{d-1}+a_{d-2}z^{d-3}+\cdots + a_1,\]
and so the odd-index coefficients of $f$ are contained in the field generated by the critical points. We have that the coefficients of $f$ are algebraic over $F$ as claimed.
