<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:sextic-lower}
Fix a finite set \(S_0\) of prime ideals containing the
primes above \(6\). Let \(0<\delta<3/11\) and \(\varepsilon>0\).
For \(A,B\ge2\) satisfying
\[
                    B^{16/11+\delta}\le A\le B^{2-\delta},
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-lower}
 \Xi_{6,S_0}(A,B)\gg_{\delta,\varepsilon,S_0}
                         (AB)^{-\varepsilon}A^{5/6}B^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:balanced-cutoff}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(X\ge2\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:balanced-lower}
                    \Xi_{6,S_0}(X,X)
                        \gg_{\varepsilon,S_0}X^{17/16-\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:primitive-columns}
Fix \(0<\delta<3/11\), the profiles \(W,w\), and the initial
exclusion set \(S_0\). There is a fixed finite enlargement
\(S\supseteq S_0\), obtained by including all primes up to
a sufficiently large norm cutoff, such that, for all
sufficiently large \(B\) and all \(A\) satisfying
\[
               B^{16/11+\delta}\le A\le B^{2-\delta},
\]
there is a nonzero vector \(\lambda\), supported on squarefree
\(b\in I(S)\) with \(q_b\le B\), for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-row-witness}
 \|\lambda\|_2^2\ll B^{-1},\qquad
 \sum_{m\in I(S)}w(m/\sqrt A)
      \left|\sum_b\lambda_bJ_6(m,b)\right|^2
      \gg A^{5/6}B^{1/3}\|\lambda\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
The constants depend only on \(\delta\) and the fixed data.
The row sum is over all ideals in \(I(S)\), evaluated at
their primary generators.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:row-transfer}
Fix \(S\) and the row profile \(w\) as in
Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}. Let \(0<\delta<1/3\)
and \(\varepsilon>0\). For \(A,B\ge2\) in
\[
               B^{4/3+\delta}\le A\le B^{2-\delta},
\]
suppose a nonzero vector \(\lambda\), supported on
squarefree \(b\in I(S)\) with \(\mathrm N b\le B\), satisfies
\[
 \sum_{m\in I(S)}w(m/\sqrt A)
      \left|\sum_b\lambda_bJ_6(m,b)\right|^2
                \ge cA^{5/6}B^{1/3}\|\lambda\|_2^2,
\]
with fixed \(c>0\). A further row weight between
zero and one may be retained in this hypothesis.
Then
\[
            \Xi_6(A,B)\gg_{\delta,\varepsilon,c,S}
                      (AB)^{-\varepsilon}A^{5/6}B^{1/3}.
\]
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix \(\delta,\varepsilon,S_0\). Proposition~\ref{prop:primitive-columns}
chooses a finite enlargement \(S\), independent of
\(A,B\), and supplies, for sufficiently large \(B\),
a nonzero squarefree-column vector with the lower moment
\eqref{eq:all-row-witness}.
The same \(\delta\) is valid in Proposition~\ref{lem:row-transfer}:
indeed, \(\delta<3/11<1/3\) and \(16/11>4/3\).
That proposition gives \eqref{eq:main-lower} for \(S\).
The inclusion \eqref{eq:fixed-submatrix} gives the
assertion for the original fixed set \(S_0\).
The constants attached to the enlarged set are allowed
to depend on \(\delta\) and \(S_0\). If \(B\) is below
the construction threshold, then \(A\le B^{2-\delta}\)
is bounded as well. The unit row and column give
\(\Xi_{6,S_0}(A,B)\ge1\), so decreasing the implied
constant covers this remaining range.

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The full cutoff allows columns of length \(X^\theta\)
with \(1/2<\theta<11/16\). The limiting exponent is
\[
 \sup_{1/2<\theta<11/16}
         \left(\frac56+\frac\theta3\right)=\frac{17}{16}.
\]
To retain fixed margins, take \(A=X\), \(B_0=X^\theta\),
with \(\theta=11/16-\tau\) and \(0<\tau<3/16\).
Both \(1/\theta-16/11\) and \(2-1/\theta\) are positive.
Choose
\[
 0<\delta_0<\min\{3/11,1/\theta-16/11,2-1/\theta\}.
\]
Then \(B_0^{16/11+\delta_0}\le X\le B_0^{2-\delta_0}\),
so Theorem~\ref{thm:sextic-lower} applies for sufficiently
large \(X\). Column inclusion and the theorem with loss
exponent \(\varepsilon_0>0\) give
\[
 \Xi_{6,S_0}(X,X)\ge\Xi_{6,S_0}(X,B_0)
 \gg X^{17/16-\tau/3-(1+\theta)\varepsilon_0}.
\]
Choose \(\tau\) first and then \(\varepsilon_0\) so that
\(\tau/3+(1+\theta)\varepsilon_0<\varepsilon\).
The bounded range again follows from
\(\Xi_{6,S_0}(X,X)\ge1\) by decreasing the implied constant.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:uniform-gram}
There is \(c_*>0\), depending only on the fixed data and
not on \(\sigma,\mathfrak d,\mathbf e\), such that, for
\(q/L\) sufficiently large relative to the fixed data,
\(q\ge L\ge1\), and \(X=L^2/q\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-gram}
\begin{split}
 \mathcal G_{\mathfrak d,\mathbf e}(q,L)
 ={}&c_*q^{5/6}L^{-2/3}
       \prod_{p\mid\mathfrak d}
       \frac{q_p^{-j_p/6}}{(1+q_p^{-1})^2}\\
 &+O_{\varepsilon,\rm fixed}\left(
 (qLD)^\varepsilon\frac qL
      \left(1+X^{1/12}+\frac{DX^2}{L}\right)\right),
 \qquad j_p=(-e_p\bmod6)\in\{0,\ldots,5\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), the implied constant is uniform in the moving ideal
\(\mathfrak d\), the exponent vector, and the ray class.
For the complementary range \(q\ge L\ge1\) and
\(L^2/q\le1\), one has the uniform bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:short-column-bound}
 \mathcal G_{\mathfrak d,\mathbf e}(q,L)
       \ll_{\rm fixed}q/L.
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:local-correlation}
Let \((n_1,n_2)=1\), and let \(p^c\parallel C\), with
\(P=q_p\). The local factor of \(L_C(n_1,n_2;k)\),
when \(p\nmid n_1n_2\), is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-correlation}
 L_{p^c}
 =P^{c-1}\chi_p(n_1/n_2)^c
 \begin{cases}
 P-1,&p\mid k,\\
 -1,&p\nmid k,\ 6\nmid c,\\
 P-2,&p\nmid k,\ 6\mid c.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
If \(p\) divides exactly one residual column, the value is
\(P^{c-1}(P-1)1_{6\mid c}1_{p\nmid k}\).
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Reduction modulo \(p\) leaves a field correlation
with \(P^{c-1}\) lifts. In the unit case with \(p\nmid k\),
the substitution \(y=(k/n_1)t\) reduces it to
\(\chi_p(n_1/n_2)^c\sum_{z\in(\mathcal O/p)^\times,\ z\ne1}\chi_p(z)^c\).
If \(p\mid k\), the two unit variables are proportional,
giving \(P-1\) terms. If exactly one residual column is
divisible by \(p\), the congruence determines the variable
with unit coefficient. Its contribution is zero unless
\(p\nmid k\); the remaining character sum vanishes
unless \(6\mid c\), when it has \(P-1\) terms.
This proves the local formulas in
\cite[Lemma 13.3]{qr}, with their nonunit branches.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:ideal-count}
For a squarefree ideal \(\mathfrak d\) prime to \(S\), let
\(g_{S\mathfrak d}(\mathfrak a)=
\prod_{p\mid\mathfrak a,\ p\notin S\mathfrak d}(1-q_p^{-1})\).
For every \(Y>0\), uniformly in \(\mathfrak d\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-ideal-count}
 \sum_{\mathrm N\mathfrak a\le Y}g_{S\mathfrak d}(\mathfrak a)
 =\frac{\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s)}
          {\zeta_K^{S\mathfrak d}(2)}Y+O_K(Y^{1/2}).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The elementary ideal count in this
quadratic field is
\(\#\{\mathfrak a:\mathrm N\mathfrak a\le Y\}
=r_KY+O_K(Y^{1/2})\), with
\(r_K=\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s)\).
Expand \(g\) as
\(\sum_{\mathfrak b\mid\mathfrak a,(\mathfrak b,S\mathfrak d)=1}
\mu(\mathfrak b)/\mathrm N\mathfrak b\).
The main term is
\(r_KY\sum_{(\mathfrak b,S\mathfrak d)=1}
\mu(\mathfrak b)/(\mathrm N\mathfrak b)^2\), which gives
the stated reciprocal zeta factor. Completing this
sum costs \(O_K(1)\), and the counting errors are bounded by
\[
 Y^{1/2}\sum_{\mathfrak b}(\mathrm N\mathfrak b)^{-3/2}
       \ll_K Y^{1/2}.
\]
Dropping the coprimality condition in this absolute
bound makes its independence of \(\mathfrak d\) explicit.
For \(Y<1\) the same bound is immediate.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:short-columns}
For \(q\ge L\ge1\) and \(L^2\le q\), the family
\eqref{eq:punctured-polynomial} satisfies
\(\mathcal G_{\mathfrak d,\mathbf e}(q,L)\ll_{\rm fixed}q/L\),
uniformly in \(\mathfrak d,\mathbf e,\sigma\).
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Opening \(G(s,-m)\) gives reduced additive fractions
with moduli \(q_s\le L\). Distinct reduced fractions
with denominators \(s,t\) differ, modulo \(\mathcal O\),
by a nonzero lattice numerator divided by \(st\).
Since \(|st|\le L\), their separation on the complex
torus is \(\gg L^{-1}\). The total squared
coefficient mass is at most
\[
  L^{-2}\sum_{q_s\asymp L}O(\varphi(s)/q_s)\ll L^{-1}.
\]
Poisson summation and the packing bound for these
separated torus frequencies give a frequency-Gram
row sum \(O(q+L^2)\). More explicitly, the kernel is
bounded by
\[
 q\sum_{k\in\mathcal O}
       (1+\sqrt q\,|k+\alpha-\beta|)^{-M},
\]
and summing over \(L^{-1}\)-separated \(\beta\) gives
\(O(q+L^2)\) for \(M>4\).
Schur's test therefore gives
\((q+L^2)/L\ll q/L\).
The exclusions and moving character are bounded coefficient
restrictions; all imprimitive moduli retain their unit
numerators in this argument.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Suppose first that \(X=L^2/q\ge1\).
For each \(h\mid\mathfrak s\) in \eqref{eq:fixed-row-projector},
put \(Q=q/\mathrm N h\). Taking \(q/L\) sufficiently large relative
to the fixed data puts every \(Q/L\) in the range of
\eqref{eq:unrestricted-gram}; also \(X_Q=(\mathrm N h)X\ge1\).
Apply that formula to each term of the finite projector.
The main coefficients combine to
\eqref{eq:positive-gram-constant}, and the moving local
factors are unchanged because \(h\) is supported on \(S\).
The finitely many errors give \eqref{eq:uniform-gram},
uniformly in \(D,\mathbf e,\sigma\).
If \(X\le1\), Lemma~\ref{lem:short-columns} gives
\eqref{eq:short-column-bound} directly.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:column-projection}
With the fixed data and range of

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:prime-power-gauss}
Let \(p\notin S\), \(P=q_p\), and \(a\ge1\).
If \(6\nmid a\), then
\[
 G(p^a,-m)=0\quad\text{unless }v_p(m)=a-1.
\]
For \(m=p^{a-1}u\), \(p\nmid u\), one has
\[
 G(p^a,-m)=P^{(a-1)/2}\tau^-_{p,a}\chi_p(u)^{-a},
 \qquad
 \tau^-_{p,a}=P^{-1/2}
                 \sum_{x\bmod p}\chi_p(x)^a e(-x/p),
 \qquad |\tau^-_{p,a}|=1.
\]
If \(6\mid a\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:principal-gauss}
       G(p^a,-m)
          =P^{a/2}1_{p^a\mid m}
                     -P^{a/2-1}1_{p^{a-1}\mid m}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The multiplicative character modulo \(p^a\) depends
only on the reduction modulo \(p\). Write a residue
as \(x=x_0+py\), with \(x_0\bmod p\) and
\(y\bmod p^{a-1}\). Additive orthogonality in \(y\)
shows that the sum vanishes unless \(p^{a-1}\mid m\).
If \(m=p^{a-1}u\), this inner sum contributes
\(P^{a-1}\), leaving
\[
 G(p^a,-m)=P^{a/2-1}
       \sum_{x_0\bmod p}\chi_p(x_0)^a e(-ux_0/p).
\]

Suppose first that \(6\nmid a\). The character
\(\chi_p^a\) is nonprincipal. If \(p\mid u\), its
complete sum is zero; thus a nonzero contribution
requires \(v_p(m)=a-1\). For \(p\nmid u\), changing
variables by multiplication by \(u\) gives the factor
\(\chi_p(u)^{-a}\) and the normalized Gauss sum
\(\tau^-_{p,a}\). The finite-field Gauss-sum identity
gives \(|\tau^-_{p,a}|=1\), proving the first formula.

If \(6\mid a\), the character equals one on units
and zero at zero. The remaining additive sum is
\(P-1\) when \(p\mid u\), and \(-1\) otherwise.
Multiplying by \(P^{a/2-1}\), and retaining the
initial condition \(p^{a-1}\mid m\), gives
\eqref{eq:principal-gauss}. This also recovers the

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:higher-factor-reduction}
Fix \(0<\delta<1/3\), \(\rho>0\), and \(S\). Suppose
\(A,B\ge2\) and \(B^{4/3+\delta}\le A\le B^{2-\delta}\).
Put \(Z=AB\), \(M=A^{5/6}B^{1/3}\), and \(g=\delta/18\).
For \(1\le T\le Z\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:squarefree-row-transfer}
 \Psi_6(A,B)\ll_{\rho,\delta,S} Z^\rho
       \left[T\Xi_6(A,B)+M(T^{-2/3}+Z^{-g})\right].
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
For sixth-power-free \(u\), factor uniquely
\[
                   u=a_1a_2^2a_3^3a_4^4a_5^5
\]
into pairwise coprime squarefree ideals.
In dyadic ranges \(\mathrm N a_j\asymp F_j\), \(j\ge2\), put
\(F=\prod_{j=2}^5F_j^j\), \(S_1=\prod_{j=2}^5F_j\).
The product \(F\) consumes row length, while \(S_1\)
counts the possible choices of higher factors. Their different
roles determine which averaging direction is effective.
For a coefficient vector of squared norm one,
fixing the higher factors and averaging \(a_1\)
allows at most \(O(S_1)\) choices of those factors.
Their product twists the column coefficients, and
the remaining squarefree row length is at most
\(A/F\). Thus the normalized block contribution
has the upper bound
\[
                         U_1\ll S_1\Xi_6(A/F,B).
\]
There is a second upper bound for the same block.
Fix \(a_1,a_3,a_4,a_5\) and average \(a_2\).
There are at most \(O((A/F)S_1/F_2)\) choices of
the fixed variables.
Since \(J_6(a_2,b)^2=J_3(a_2,b)\), the second bound is
\[
                 U_2\ll\frac AF\frac{S_1}{F_2}\Xi_3(2F_2,B).
\]
In both cases the fixed factors only twist or delete
column coefficients, without increasing their norm.
The remaining row restrictions may be dropped by
positivity. All collision zeros are retained.

We use the better estimate in two ranges. Inserting
the sextic upper bound into \(U_1\), and using
\(F\ge S_1^2\), gives
\[
 U_1\ll Z^\rho\left(
       A/S_1+BS_1+MS_1^{-2/3}
       +A^{1/3}B^{5/6}S_1^{1/3}\right).
\]
Consequently, for \(T<S_1\le A^{1/3}\), this is at most
\[
 Z^\rho\left(
 A/T+MT^{-2/3}+A^{1/3}B+A^{4/9}B^{5/6}\right).
\]
For \(S_1>A^{1/3}\), insert the cubic estimate into
\(U_2\). The three terms are bounded using
\[
 \frac{S_1}{F}\le S_1^{-1},\qquad
 \frac{S_1}{FF_2}\le S_1^{-2},\qquad
 \frac{S_1}{FF_2^{1/3}}\le S_1^{-4/3}.
\]
The result is at most
\[
                  Z^\rho\left(A+A^{1/3}B+A^{5/9}B^{2/3}\right).
\]
The switch at \(S_1=A^{1/3}\) reflects two different
ways of counting rows. Below it, the number of fixed
higher factors is small enough for the sextic estimate.
Above it, averaging the squared factor \(a_2\) turns
the problem into a cubic one and gives a stronger bound.
The displayed product inequalities follow by comparing
the exponent of each \(F_j\); we take dyadic lower
endpoints \(F_j\ge1\), including the unit ideal in
the first interval.
There are \(O(\log^4(2A))\) dyadic blocks.
Replacing an actual product of higher factors by its
dyadic lower endpoints changes a cutoff only by an
absolute constant.
The higher-factor head \(S_1\le T\) has
\(O(T\log^3(2T))\) tuples and is bounded by
that number times \(\Xi_6(A,B)\). Here we keep the
actual fixed factors rather than enlarging the row
cutoff by a dyadic constant: their product has norm
at least one, so the remaining row cutoff is at most \(A\).

The terms that do not decay with \(T\), divided by \(M\),
satisfy the following bounds:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{term}&\text{upper bound after division by }M\\ \hline
 A&B^{-\delta/6}\\
 A^{1/3}B&B^{-\delta/2}\\
 A^{4/9}B^{5/6}&B^{-1/54-7\delta/18}\\
 A^{5/9}B^{2/3}&B^{-1/27-5\delta/18}.
\end{array}
\]
For the first row use \(A\le B^{2-\delta}\);
for the other three use \(A\ge B^{4/3+\delta}\).
Since \(Z\le B^3\), all are \(O(Z^{-g})\) with
\(g=\delta/18\). Absorbing the logarithmic dyad counts
and the head count into an arbitrarily small power of \(Z\)
proves \eqref{eq:squarefree-row-transfer}. In the estimates
above, use a smaller positive exponent than the prescribed
\(\rho\) to leave room for these logarithms.
