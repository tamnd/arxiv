<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:coverage}
Without auxiliary calls, the best accuracy under $N\le n$ with prior $r$ is $V_n(r)$, and every stopping rule has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped}
 \mathbb P_r(\mathrm{acc}=1,N\le n)\le V_n(r).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:exact}
The infimum of $B_{\mathrm{aux}}$ over learners and auxiliary mechanisms with $N\le n$ almost surely and $\mathbb P(\mathrm{acc}=1)\ge s$ equals $\mathcal R_n(q,s)$. It is attained by a single auxiliary observation before any verification, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 \mathcal R_n(q,s)=\sup_{r:\,q\ll r}\big\{\mathrm{kl}_+\big(s\,\|\,V_n(r)\big)-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|r)\big\}.
\end{equation}
Under an arbitrary stopping rule, $B_{\mathrm{aux}}\ge\mathcal R_n\big(q,\mathbb P(\mathrm{acc}=1,N\le n)\big)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
Build a reference law $\widetilde P$ with prior $r$, the same learner and the same exact verifications, but with every auxiliary kernel replaced by its posterior predictive $\overline K_t$. Verification transitions are identical under the two laws, so they cancel from the likelihood ratio, and over complete histories
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathKL}
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\,\|\,\widetilde P)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|r)+B_{\mathrm{aux}} .
\end{equation}
Under $\widetilde P$ every auxiliary observation can be simulated from the history, so \cref{prop:coverage} gives $\widetilde P(\mathrm{acc}=1,N\le n)\le V_n(r)$, and data processing to that event, as in the fundamental inequality of \citet{garivier2019explore}, yields the following (\cref{app:selective}).
\begin{lemma}[Selective-channel converse]\label{lem:selective}
For every protocol and every reference prior $r$ with $q\ll r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selective}
 \mathrm{kl}_+\big(\mathbb P(\mathrm{acc}=1,N\le n)\,\|\,V_n(r)\big)\leD_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|r)+B_{\mathrm{aux}} .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma} Optimizing $r$ makes the bound exact: \eqref{eq:dual} is the rate--distortion dual of \citet{csiszar1974extremum} written in the coordinates of a reference prior (\cref{app:duality}). For achievability, send an optimal list, verify all but its last entry, and output the last entry if none is accepted.

For unique answers, uniform $q$ and $n+1<M$, the frontier is the list-Fano value $\mathrm{kl}_+(s\,\|\,(n+1)/M)$ \citep{sakai2020}, so each optimally placed bit halves the verification budget. For $q=(0.8,0.1,0.1)$ and $n=1$, zero error costs $\mathcal R_n(q,1)=0.2$ bits: list answer~1 together with answer~2 or~3, each with probability $1/2$ (\cref{cor:zero} in \cref{sec:zero}); the value at $r=q$ is only $\log_2(1/0.9)=0.152$. With overlapping acceptance sets, taking $r=q$ in~\eqref{eq:dual} gives only the lower bound $\mathrm{kl}_+(s\|V_n(q))$, and the optimal $r$ need not be uniform even when $q$ is (\cref{sec:zero}). The theorem prices an optimally \emph{designed} source; a fixed source with the same mutual information can be far less useful (\cref{sec:channels}): as in the comparison of experiments \citep{blackwell1953equivalent}, no single number such as mutual information ranks sources for every task.\looseness=-1

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:calls}
Call sources \emph{binary} when each auxiliary observation takes two values.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Every protocol has $B_{\mathrm{aux}}\le\mathbb E\sum_t\log_2|\mathcal Z_t|$ over its auxiliary observations. With exact verification and binary sources, any protocol with $N\le n$ almost surely and $\mathbb P(\mathrm{acc}=1)\ge s$ therefore makes $\mathbb E Q\ge\mathcal R_n(q,s)$ calls, whatever its source mechanisms or order.
\item[(ii)] For unique answers the bound is tight to an additive constant. For every $s$, designed binary sources that ignore target history, with no shared randomness, support a source-first protocol with $N\le n$, $\mathbb P(\mathrm{acc}=1)\ge s$, a bounded number of calls and
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E Q&<\mathcal R_n(q,s)+1+h_2(s)+2s\\
 &\le\mathcal R_n(q,s)+1+\log_2 5 .
\end{align*}
At zero error, sources that are deterministic functions of the world give $\mathbb E Q<\mathcal R_n(q,1)+2$, and no constant below $1$ is possible.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:general}
No constant suffices for general acceptance sets, for any $n$. Let $n=0$, let the responses be the independent sets of a graph $G$ on $[M]$, and let $f_\theta(I)=\mathbf 1\{\theta\in I\}$. Every zero-error protocol with binary sources has $\mathbb E Q\ge H_\chi(G,q)$, the least entropy of a colouring of $G$, while $\mathcal R_n(q,1)$ is the graph entropy $H_{\mathrm K}(G,q)$ of \citet{korner1973coding}; their difference is unbounded. Pairing each world with an index that an accepted response must match extends the gap to every $n$. The excess is at most logarithmic: sources that are deterministic functions of the world and the request achieve $\mathbb E Q\le\mathcal R_n(q,s)+\log_2(\mathcal R_n(q,s)+1)+5$ for every $s$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:interleave}
Against a given set of sources, interleaving can save an unbounded number of expected calls, even with exact verification. For unique answers, $q=(1-\epsilon,\epsilon/K,\dots,\epsilon/K)$, $n=1$, $s=1$ and sources that return the $\log_2K$ bits ($K\ge4$ a power of two) of an index in which world~1 shares the index of world~2, an interleaved protocol makes $\epsilon\log_2K$ expected calls and every source-first protocol at least $(1-\epsilon)\log_2K$. Interleaving can also divide $\mathbb E Q$ by an unbounded factor: for $q=(1-2p,p,p)$ with $0<p\le1/4$, $n=1$ and $s=1$, an interleaved protocol makes $\mathbb E Q=2p=\mathcal R_n(q,1)$ calls, while every source-first protocol makes $\mathbb E Q\ge1$. Against designed sources and unique answers, however, \cref{thm:calls} bounds the saving by $1+\log_2 5<3.33$ calls, and by $2$ at zero error.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:fixed}
With exact verification and fixed sources, any interleaved protocol with $Q\le b$ and $N\le n$ can be replaced, with the same source mechanisms, by a source-first protocol: all its source calls, then all its verifications. The replacement has at least the same success probability and obeys the same caps, including a total cap $Q+N\le C$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixedvalue}
 \mathsf A_{b,n}:=\sup\mathbb P(\mathrm{acc}=1)=\sup_{\sigma:\,Q\le b}\mathbb E\,V_n\big(q(\cdot\mid Z_\sigma)\big).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:noisy}
Let a verification of $y$ return a bit drawn from a binary-input kernel $G(\cdot\mid f_\Theta(y))$, independent of the history, and let $V^G_n(r)$ be the best value of $\mathbb P_r(\mathrm{acc}=1,N\le n)$ without auxiliary information.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The converse survives in two forms: $\mathrm{kl}_+\big(\mathbb P(\mathrm{acc}=1,N\le n)\,\|\,V^G_n(r)\big)\leD_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|r)+B_{\mathrm{aux}}$ for every $r$ with $q\ll r$, and, with $N\le n$ almost surely and $\mathbb P(\mathrm{acc}=1)\ge s$, $B_{\mathrm{aux}}\ge\mathcal R_0(q,s)-n\,\operatorname{cap}(G)$, where $\operatorname{cap}(G)$ is the capacity of $G$.
\item[(ii)] Neither reduction survives. Source-first protocols can need an unbounded factor more information than interleaved ones: for one flipped query at zero error, $1$ bit against $h_2(\epsilon)$ (\cref{thm:noise}). At equal call caps, take a uniform binary answer $A$, let the verifier flip its reply with probability $\epsilon$, and let two fixed sources report $\mathbf 1\{A\ne r\}$ for $r=0,1$, each missing a true $1$ with probability $\eta$. With one call and one verification, interleaving reaches error $\epsilon\eta$, while every source-first protocol has error at least $\min\{\eta/2,\epsilon(1+\eta)/2\}$, which the best one attains. At $\epsilon=\eta$ the ratio is $1/(2\eta)$, so \cref{thm:fixed} fails.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:rank}
For arbitrary adaptive interleaving,
\begin{align}
 \mathsf A_{b,n}&=\min\{1,(n+1)2^{-k+\rho_b}\},\label{eq:rankrect}\\
 \mathsf A_C&=\max_{0\le b\le\min(C,J)}\min\{1,(C-b+1)2^{-k+\rho_b}\}.\label{eq:ranktotal}
\end{align}
A fixed maximizing subset followed by enumeration of the compatible targets attains both. Neither outcome-adaptive source selection nor adaptive budget allocation helps.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:qsp}
For every source budget $b$, with $(x)_+=\max\{x,0\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qsp}
 \rho_b=\max_{U}\ \min\{t(U),\,(b-\dim U)_+\}.
\end{equation}
After a polynomial-time reduction, all budgets and maximizing subsets are computed in $\mathcal N_h\operatorname{poly}(J,k+h)$ field operations, where $\mathcal N_h=\sum_{u=0}^h{h\brack u}_2=2^{O(h^2)}$ counts the subspaces of $\mathbb F_2^h$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:waste}
In a uniform linear bank, take zero error with $n$ verifications, $n+1<2^k$, and let $\kappa=\lceil k-\log_2(n+1)\rceil=\lceil\mathcal R_n(q,1)\rceil$. A hard source cap $b$ suffices if and only if $b\ge b_n$, where
\[
 b_n=\kappa+u(\kappa),\qquad u(\kappa)=\min\{\dim U:\ t(U)\ge\kappa\},
\]
over the subspaces $U$ of \cref{thm:qsp} ($b_n=\infty$ if $\rho_J<\kappa$). The same $b_n$ is the least expected number of calls of a source-first protocol. The excess over the rounded-up information price, $b_n-\kappa=u(\kappa)\le h$, is the number of calls an optimal subset spends on nuisance directions, and it vanishes exactly when an optimal subset resolves one new target dimension per call.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:zero}
The minimum auxiliary information for zero error and $n$ target queries is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero}
 \mathcal R_n(q,1)=\min_{a\in\mathcal A_n}\sum_\theta q_\theta\log_2(1/a_\theta).
\end{equation}
For unique answers, let $\ell=\min(n+1,M)$. If $\ell<M$, choose $\tau>0$ so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clip}
 a_i^*=\min\{1,q_i/\tau\},\qquad \sum_i a_i^*=\ell.
 \quad\text{Then}\quad
 \mathcal R_n(q,1)=\sum_{q_i<\tau}q_i\log_2(\tau/q_i).
\end{equation}
If $\ell=M$, the price is zero.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:noise}
For error probability at most $\delta\in[0,\epsilon]$, the minimum interleaved auxiliary information is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noisyafter}
 B_{\mathrm{inter}}(\epsilon,\delta)=h_2(\epsilon)-h_2(\delta).
\end{equation}
Let $v_\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon)$ be the unique solution of
$h_2(v)+(\epsilon-v)h_2'(v)=1$. If all auxiliary observations must precede the query, the minimum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noisybefore}
 B_{\mathrm{upfront}}(\epsilon,\delta)=
 \begin{cases}
 1-h_2(\delta),&0\le\delta\le v_\epsilon,\\
 (\epsilon-\delta)h_2'(v_\epsilon),&v_\epsilon\le\delta\le\epsilon.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In particular, zero error costs $h_2(\epsilon)$ versus one bit. With the strictly positive error tolerance $\delta=\epsilon^2$, the interleaved cost tends to zero and the upfront cost tends to one as $\epsilon\to0$.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
A single noisy reply contributes at most $1-h_2(\epsilon)$ bits, whereas final error $\delta$ requires at least $1-h_2(\delta)$ total bits. Query first, then optimally describe the remaining Bernoulli-$\epsilon$ error: the elementary binary rate--distortion test channel attains the difference. For an upfront source $Z$, set $v=\min\{P(A=1\mid Z),P(A=0\mid Z)\}$. Optimal error after the query is $\min(v,\epsilon)$. By symmetry, any distribution over $v\in[0,1/2]$ is realizable with mean prior $1/2$. Maximizing $\mathbb E h_2(v)$ subject to $\mathbb E\min(v,\epsilon)\le\delta$ gives the upper concave envelope of $h_2(v)$ and the point $(\epsilon,1)$, hence~\eqref{eq:noisybefore}. Appendix~\ref{app:noise} proves the constructions, the tangent condition, and the limiting separation.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:learnlinear}
Suppose training contexts supply the target answer and all $J$ source labels, drawn independently and uniformly from an unknown rank-$\ell$ subspace of $\mathbb F_2^{k+J}$ with target rank $k$, and let $\ell_0\ge\ell$ be a declared bound. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sample}
 m\ge\ell_0+\lceil\log_2(1/\delta)\rceil ,
\end{equation}
then with probability at least $1-\delta$ the span of $m$ contexts is the whole subspace, and QSP applied to it returns optimal policies for every budget, with nuisance dimension $h=\ell-k$.
