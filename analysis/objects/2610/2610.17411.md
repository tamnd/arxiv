<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:SG}
Every \(\mathrm{II}_1\) factor with separable predual is generated, as a von Neumann algebra, by two self-adjoint elements.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let \(A\) be a separable unital \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}-algebra such that $\partial\mathrm{T}(A)$ is nonempty, compact, and zero-dimensional. Assume that \textup{(C)} (\eqref{eq:comparison}) holds. Then
\[
A=\ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(g_1,g_2)
\]
for two ordinary elements \(g_1,g_2\in A\).
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{gr:main}
Let \(A\) be a separable unital \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}-algebra such that $\partial\mathrm{T}(A)$ is nonempty, compact, and zero-dimensional. Assume that $A$ has real rank zero, and assume that \textup{(PC)} (\eqref{eq:PC}) holds. Then $A$ is singly generated, and its single generators form a norm-dense \(G_\delta\) subset of \(A\). Moreover,
\[
\operatorname{gr}(A)\leq1,
\]
with equality when \(A\) is noncommutative, where $\operatorname{gr}$ is Thiel's generator rank in \cite{ThielGR}.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:polar}
Let \(A\) be a separable unital \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}-algebra such that
\(\partial\mathrm{T}(A)\) is nonempty, compact, and zero-dimensional.
Assume that \(A\) satisfies \textup{(C)}.
Suppose there is a unitary \(u\in A\) with the following properties.
For \(\tau\in\partial\mathrm{T}(A)\), put
\[
M_\tau=\pi_\tau(A)'',\qquad u_\tau=\pi_\tau(u),\qquad
D_\tau=\ensuremath{\mathrm{W}^*}(u_\tau),
\]
and let \(\mathbb E_{D_\tau}:M_\tau\to D_\tau\) be the trace-preserving
conditional expectation. For every \(\tau\in\partial\mathrm{T}(A)\),
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{u-cond-1} the spectral distribution of \(u\) with respect
  to \(\tau\) is atomless and has full support on \(\mathbb T\); and
\item\label{u-cond-2} for every \(a\in A\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-compression}
\lim_{t\downarrow0}
\sup_{\substack{p\in D_\tau\ {\rm projection}\\
                 0<\tau(p)\leq t}}

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:separated-fibres}
Let \(A\) be separable and unital, with
\(\varnothing\neq K=\partial\mathrm{T}(A)\) compact and zero-dimensional.
Let \(C\subseteq A\) be a unital sub-\(\ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}\)-algebra such that
\[
\pi_\tau(C)''=\pi_\tau(A)''\quad(\tau\in K),
\]
and suppose that \(\tau\mapsto\tau|_C\) is injective on \(K\).
Then, for every \(z\in A_\mathrm{sa}\) with \(\left\|z\right\|\leq1\) and
every \(\eta>0\), there is \(c\in C_\mathrm{sa}\) such that
\[
\left\|c\right\|\leq1,\qquad \left\|z-c\right\|_{2,K}<\eta.
\]
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Each restricted trace is extremal, because its GNS von Neumann
algebra is the factor \(\pi_\tau(A)''\). Thus the restriction
map identifies \(K\) with a compact subset \(K_C\) of
\(\partial\mathrm{T}(C)\). Let \(F_C\) be the closed Bauer face generated
by \(K_C\), and complete \(C\) relative to \(F_C\).
The inclusion into \(\mathcal M_K\) extends to an injective
\(*\)-homomorphism
\[
\Phi:\mathcal M_{C,F_C}\longrightarrow\mathcal M_K.
\]
Indeed, the uniform \(2\)-seminorms on \(C\) agree, and bounded
uniformly \(2\)-Cauchy sequences give the extension. Unit-ball
density makes it contractive; equality of the \(2\)-norms makes
it injective and hence isometric.

By \cite[Theorem 3.37]{TracialComplete}, the source contains
\(\mathrm{C}(K_C)\) centrally. Its function corresponding to
\(f\in \mathrm{C}(K)\) has scalar image \(f(\tau)1\) in every factor
fibre. The family of extended traces on \(\mathcal M_K\) is
faithful, so its image is the ambient central function \(f\).
In particular, the range of \(\Phi\) contains every central
clopen projection.

For each \(\tau\in K\), the Kaplansky density theorem in each fibre and
Lemma~\ref{lem:lift} give a self-adjoint contraction
\(c_\tau\in C\) with \(\left\|z-c_\tau\right\|_{2,\tau}<\eta/4\).
The inequality with \(\eta/2\) holds on a neighborhood of
\(\tau\). Choose a subordinate finite clopen partition
\(V_1,\ldots,V_L\), let \(p_\ell=1_{V_\ell}\), and choose the
corresponding contractions \(c_\ell\). Then
\[
d=\sum_\ell p_\ell c_\ell\in\Phi(\mathcal M_{C,F_C}),
\qquad \left\|d\right\|\leq1,
\qquad \left\|z-d\right\|_{2,K}<\eta/2.
\]
Approximate the self-adjoint contractive preimage of \(d\)
by a self-adjoint contraction from \(C\), using
\cite[Proposition 3.28]{TracialComplete} and quotient lifting.
An error smaller than \(\eta/2\) proves the assertion.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{gr:lem:projection-pair}
Using Theorem~\ref{thm:SG}, every finite factor $M$ with separable
predual has a projection $P$ and a self-adjoint contraction $D$ such
that $\ensuremath{\mathrm{W}^*}(P,D)=M$.  If $M\neq\mathbb C$, one may require
$0<P<1$.  For $M=\mathbb C$, take $P=1$ and $D=0$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For $M=\mathrm{M}_d(\mathbb C)$, $d\geq2$, choose distinct
$t_1,\ldots,t_d\in[0,1]$, let $D=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_d)$,
and let $P$ be the rank-one projection onto
$d^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1)$.  Spectral calculus for $D$ gives each diagonal
matrix unit, and $e_{ii}Pe_{jj}=d^{-1}e_{ij}$ gives all matrix units.

Suppose instead that $M$ is type $\mathrm{II}_1$.  Splitting its identity into
two equivalent projections identifies $M$ with $\mathrm{M}_2(N)$, where $N$ is
a type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor with separable predual.  Theorem~\ref{thm:SG}
gives $u,v\in N_\mathrm{sa}$ with $\ensuremath{\mathrm{W}^*}(u,v)=N$.  Affine rescaling allows us
to assume $3/5\leq u,v\leq4/5$.  Set
\[
 s=(u-u^2)^{1/2},\qquad
 P=\begin{pmatrix}1-u&s\\s&u\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D=\begin{pmatrix}v&0\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since $s$ commutes with $u$ and $s^2=u-u^2$, we have
$P=P^*=P^2$.  Both $P$ and $1-P$ are nonzero, and $D$ is a
self-adjoint contraction.  The separated spectra of $v$ and $1/2$
recover $e_{11}$ and $e_{22}$ from $D$.  Moreover, $s$ is positive
and invertible, so the polar part of $e_{11}Pe_{22}$ is $e_{12}$.
These matrix units, the $e_{22}$ compression of $P$, and the
$e_{11}$ compression of $D$ recover $u$ and $v$ in the first corner.
They therefore generate $\mathrm{M}_2(N)$ as a von Neumann algebra.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{gr:lem:join}
Let $f_1,\ldots,f_s$ be nonzero orthogonal projections summing to
the identity of a finite factor $M$.  Suppose a von Neumann
subalgebra $E\subseteq M$ contains $f_jMf_j$ for every $j$ and a
nonzero element of $f_jMf_{j+1}$ for every $j<s$.  Then $E=M$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Fix a nonzero element of $f_jMf_{j+1}$ lying in $E$, and let
$v\in E$ be its polar part.  Its initial projection
$q=v^*v\leq f_{j+1}$ is nonzero, and $vv^*\leq f_j$.
Finite-factor comparison supplies finitely many partial isometries
$t_i\in f_{j+1}Mf_{j+1}$ such that
\[
 t_i^*t_i\leq q,\qquad \sum_i t_it_i^*=f_{j+1}.
\]
Indeed, partition $f_{j+1}$ into finitely many projections whose
traces are at most the trace of $q$, and compare each of them with
$q$.  Such a partition exists by the elementary dimension theory
of matrix and type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factors.  For $y\in f_jMf_{j+1}$,
\[
 y=\sum_i (yt_iv^*)(vt_i^*).
\]
Here $yt_iv^*\in f_jMf_j\subseteq E$ and $vt_i^*\in E$, proving
$f_jMf_{j+1}\subseteq E$.  Taking adjoints gives the reverse corner.

To obtain every remaining corner, join successively larger diagonal
corners.  More explicitly, if $F=f_1+\cdots+f_j$ and
$FMF\subseteq E$, the already recovered nonzero element of
$f_jMf_{j+1}\subseteq FMf_{j+1}$ and the same argument give
$FMf_{j+1}\subseteq E$.  Hence
$(F+f_{j+1})M(F+f_{j+1})\subseteq E$.  Induction gives $E=M$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{gr:common-labels}
Fix distinct real numbers $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_L$, a number
$C\geq0$, and $\rho>0$.  There are an integer $N\geq1$ and
pairwise distinct nonzero real labels
\[
 \Lambda_\ell=\{\lambda_{\ell,k,+},\lambda_{\ell,k,-}:1\leq k\leq N\},
 \qquad 1\leq\ell\leq L,
\]
such that
\begin{equation}\label{gr:eq:labels}
 |\lambda-\alpha_\ell|<\rho\quad(\lambda\in\Lambda_\ell),
 \qquad
 R=\left|\bigcup_{\ell=1}^L\Lambda_\ell\right|=2LN,
\end{equation}
with the following property.  Let $M$ be any finite factor with
separable predual, let $e_1,\ldots,e_L$ be orthogonal projections
in $M$ summing to $1$, and let $B_0\in M_\mathrm{sa}$ satisfy
$\left\|B_0\right\|\leq C$.  Some $e_\ell$ may be zero.  There are
$H,B\in M_\mathrm{sa}$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item
 $H=\sum_{\ell,k,\sigma}\lambda_{\ell,k,\sigma}e_{\ell,k,\sigma}$,
 where the projections are orthogonal and
 $\sum_{k,\sigma}e_{\ell,k,\sigma}=e_\ell$;
\item
 $\left\|H-\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell e_\ell\right\|<\rho$ and
 $\left\|B-B_0\right\|<\rho$;
\item $\ensuremath{\mathrm{W}^*}(H,B)=M$.
\end{enumerate}
Zero projections in the refined partition are allowed.  The integer
$N$ may be chosen using only $C$ and $\rho$; all labels are fixed
using only $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_L,C,\rho$.  In particular, neither
the labels nor $R$ depends on $M$, the projection partition, a target
element, or a tracial approximation tolerance.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
If $C>0$, choose $N>8C/\rho$ and partition $[-C,C]$ into $N$
nonempty Borel intervals $I_k$ of diameter $2C/N<\rho/4$.
Allocate every common endpoint to just one interval.  Choose
$\mu_k\in I_k$.  If $C=0$, take $N=1$, $I_1=\{0\}$, and
$\mu_1=0$.  Choose the $2LN$ labels in advance, all distinct and
nonzero, with each $\Lambda_\ell$ contained in a sufficiently small
neighborhood of $\alpha_\ell$ to satisfy
\eqref{gr:eq:labels}.  The neighborhoods for different $\ell$ can
be chosen disjoint, because the $\alpha_\ell$ are distinct.

For each nonzero $e_\ell$, use Borel functional calculus in its
corner to set
\[
 f_{\ell,k}=1_{I_k}(e_\ell B_0e_\ell).
\]
These projections sum to $e_\ell$ and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{gr:eq:diagonal-bound}
 \left\|f_{\ell,k}B_0f_{\ell,k}-\mu_kf_{\ell,k}\right\|<\rho/4
\end{equation}
whenever $f_{\ell,k}\neq0$.  If $e_\ell=0$, set all
$f_{\ell,k}=0$.  Every nonzero corner $f_{\ell,k}Mf_{\ell,k}$
is a finite factor.  By Lemma~\ref{gr:lem:projection-pair}, choose
a projection $P_{\ell,k}$ and a self-adjoint contraction
$D_{\ell,k}$ generating this corner.  If it is scalar, take
$P_{\ell,k}=f_{\ell,k}$ and $D_{\ell,k}=0$.  Set
\[
 e_{\ell,k,+}=P_{\ell,k},\qquad
 e_{\ell,k,-}=f_{\ell,k}-P_{\ell,k},
\]
and set these projections to zero for zero corners.  Define $H$
using the predetermined labels and this refined partition.  Its
norm estimate follows directly from \eqref{gr:eq:labels}.

Choose $0<\gamma<\rho/4$.  Replace each nonzero diagonal
compression of $B_0$ onto $f_{\ell,k}$ by
\[
 \mu_kf_{\ell,k}+\gamma D_{\ell,k},
\]
retaining all off-diagonal compressions.  By
\eqref{gr:eq:diagonal-bound}, this block diagonal change has norm
less than $\rho/2$.  Enumerate the nonzero $f_{\ell,k}$ as
$f_1,\ldots,f_s$.  Whenever a successive compression
$f_jBf_{j+1}$ is zero, insert a nonzero element of arbitrarily small
norm in that corner and its adjoint in the reverse corner.  Each
such corner is nonzero because $M$ is a factor.  There are finitely
many insertions, so their total norm can be made less than
$\rho/2$.  They do not alter diagonal compressions.  The resulting
self-adjoint $B$ satisfies $\left\|B-B_0\right\|<\rho$ and
$f_jBf_{j+1}\neq0$ for every $j<s$.

Spectral calculus for $H$ recovers every nonzero
$e_{\ell,k,\sigma}$, because the labels are all distinct, and
therefore recovers $f_{\ell,k}$ and $P_{\ell,k}$.  Compression of
$B$ then recovers
\[
 D_{\ell,k}
 =\gamma^{-1}\bigl(f_{\ell,k}Bf_{\ell,k}-\mu_kf_{\ell,k}\bigr).
\]
Thus $\ensuremath{\mathrm{W}^*}(H,B)$ contains every full diagonal corner and every
successive nonzero link.  Lemma~\ref{gr:lem:join} proves that it
equals $M$.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:baire-condition}
\left\|z_j-P(a,b)\right\|_2<1/k.
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Use the uniform completion
$\mathcal M_A=\mathcal M_K$ and canonical map $\iota$ described in
Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. Its extended traces form a faithful
family and its canonical central functions act fiberwise as
scalars. Compactness of $K$ identifies
$\operatorname{Aff}(\mathrm{T}(A))$ with $\mathrm{C}(K,\mathbb R)$ by restriction.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{A generic first coordinate separates the traces.}
Consider the bounded real-linear map
\[
 E:A_\mathrm{sa}\longrightarrow \mathrm{C}(K,\mathbb R),\qquad
 E(a)(\tau)=\tau(a).
\]
It is an open surjection.  Indeed, a continuous real function on $K$
extends to a continuous affine function on $\mathrm{T}(A)$, and
\cite[Proposition 2.7]{TracialComplete} gives, for every such function
$f$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, an $a\in A_\mathrm{sa}$ satisfying
$E(a)=f$ and $\left\|a\right\|\leq\left\|f\right\|_\infty+\varepsilon$.

Fix a compatible metric $\rho$ on $K$.  For $m\geq1$, let $V_m$
consist of those $f\in \mathrm{C}(K,\mathbb R)$ such that
\[
 f(\tau)\neq f(\sigma)
 \quad\text{whenever }\rho(\tau,\sigma)\geq1/m.
\]
This set is open: the indicated pairs form a compact subset of
$K\times K$, and the continuous function
$|f(\tau)-f(\sigma)|$ has a positive minimum there when that subset
is nonempty.  It is also dense.  Given $f$ and a prescribed uniform
error, choose a finite clopen partition on whose cells both the
oscillation of $f$ and the diameter for $\rho$ are sufficiently
small, with every diameter less than $1/m$.  Approximate $f$ by a
function constant on each cell, choosing the finitely many constants
distinct by arbitrarily small perturbations.  The resulting function
belongs to $V_m$.  Thus
\[
 S=\bigcap_{m\geq1}E^{-1}(V_m)
\]
is a norm-dense $G_\delta$ subset of $A_\mathrm{sa}$; its elements have
injective trace-evaluation functions.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{A generic pair generates every fiber.}
Choose a countable norm-dense sequence $(z_j)$ in the self-adjoint
unit ball of $A$.  Fix a countable collection $\mathcal P$ of
self-adjoint noncommutative $*$-polynomials in two self-adjoint
variables, with rational real and imaginary coefficients, which is
dense among such polynomials on every norm-bounded set of pairs.
For $j,k\geq1$, let $U_{jk}\subseteq A_\mathrm{sa}^2$ consist of the pairs
$(a,b)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bauer-local-polynomials}
 \text{for every }\tau\in K\text{ there is }P\in\mathcal P
 \text{ with }\left\|z_j-P(a,b)\right\|_{2,\tau}<1/k.
\end{equation}
Each $U_{jk}$ is norm open.  For a pair satisfying
\eqref{eq:bauer-local-polynomials}, continuity in $\tau$ gives a
neighborhood on which any chosen polynomial witness still works.
Compactness supplies finitely many witnesses.  Equivalently, for
some $P_1,\ldots,P_L\in\mathcal P$,
\[
 \max_{\tau\in K}\min_{1\leq\ell\leq L}
 \left\|z_j-P_\ell(a,b)\right\|_{2,\tau}<1/k.

persists under small norm perturbations.

\]

polynomial approximation.  The same pair and polynomial have error

 \left\|K_0-\iota(b)\right\|<r/4.
\]
Moreover, the fiber of $(H_0,K_0)$ at any $\tau\in V_\ell$ is the
fiber of $(a_\ell,b_\ell)$, so its $P_\ell$ error is less than

symmetrizing the bounded approximants.  Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial}
applies uniformly over the fibers, since its estimate depends only
on the polynomial and the operator-norm bound.  Thus these
approximants can be chosen so that the error in each of the finitely
many $P_\ell$ evaluations is less than $1/(2k)$ uniformly on $K$.

norm neighborhood.  This proves density of $U_{jk}$.

$\mathbb E_{Q_\tau}:M_\tau\to Q_\tau$ therefore fixes these elements, hence all of
$\pi_\tau(A)$ by norm density and rescaling.  Consequently
$Q_\tau=M_\tau$.  This proves the first two assertions.

Put \(C=\ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(1,x,y)\). The restricted traces are distinct because
their values on \(x\) are distinct, and the fibrewise generation
has just been proved. Lemma~\ref{lem:separated-fibres} gives the
contractive uniform tracial approximation assertion.

\label{lem:uniform-storage}
Let $A$ be unital, $\mathrm{T}(A)\ne\varnothing$, and suppose positive elements
satisfy simultaneous comparison

 \qquad \sum_{i\ge1}\alpha_i<\tfrac14.

\end{equation}

       +\sqrt{\frac{3\lambda_i}{\mu_j}}\right)

\end{equation}

 (1-F_i)r_i=\theta_i(H)(H-\lambda_i)r_i,

 \left\|F_i r_j\right\|\le R\sqrt{3\lambda_i/\lambda_j},\qquad

\end{equation}

 \le(2R+\eta_i)\eta_i\le(2R+1)\eta_i=\rho_i/4.
\]

required estimate.

\label{gr:fixed-label-approximation}
Let $A$ be separable, unital, and of real rank
zero, and suppose $K=\partial\mathrm{T}(A)$ is nonempty, compact, and
zero-dimensional.  Fix
\[
 a_0=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\alpha_\ell p_\ell\in A_{\mathrm{sa}},

where the $\alpha_\ell$ are distinct and the $p_\ell$ form a nonzero
orthogonal projection partition of $1$.  Fix also
$b_0\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}$ and $\eta>0$.
There are pairwise disjoint finite sets of nonzero real labels
$\Lambda_\ell$, with total cardinality $R$, such that for every
$x\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}$ and $\delta>0$ there are $h,b\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}$
and a self-adjoint polynomial $F$ satisfying
\[
 \|h-a_0\|<\eta,\qquad \|b-b_0\|<\eta,\qquad
 \|x-F(h,b)\|_{2,\mathrm{T}(A)}<\delta.
\]
Moreover, $b\in\mathcal S$, and $h$ is invertible, with a spectral
decomposition using labels in $\Lambda_\ell$ on subprojections of
$p_\ell$.  In particular, $h$ has at most $R$ spectral values.
The sets $\Lambda_\ell$ and $R$ depend only on $a_0,b_0,\eta$, and
are independent of $x$ and $\delta$.

Apply Lemma~\ref{gr:common-labels} with the scalar labels
$\alpha_\ell$, norm bound $\|b_0\|$, and norm tolerance $\eta/8$.
This fixes the sets $\Lambda_\ell$ and their total cardinality $R$.
At each $\tau\in K$, the lemma gives $H_\tau,B_\tau\in(M_\tau)_{\mathrm{sa}}$
with

 \ensuremath{\mathrm{W}^*}(H_\tau,B_\tau)=M_\tau,\qquad
 \|H_\tau-\pi_\tau(a_0)\|<\eta/8,\qquad
 \|B_\tau-\pi_\tau(b_0)\|<\eta/8.

The spectral projections of $H_\tau$ with labels in

The same assertion applies to matrix and scalar factors.

approximation give a self-adjoint polynomial $P_\tau$ with

<!-- lemma-17: body_md -->
\label{gr:uniform-norm-encoding}
Let $A$ be a unital $\ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}$-algebra of real rank zero with
$\mathrm{T}(A)\ne\varnothing$.  Suppose that projections in $A$ satisfy
\[
  \bigl[\tau(p)<\tau(q)\text{ for every }\tau\in \mathrm{T}(A)\bigr]
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\precsim q.
\]
Here $\precsim$ denotes Murray--von Neumann subequivalence when its
arguments are projections.  Let
\[
  h=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_jp_j\in A_{\mathrm{sa}},
  \qquad b\in A_{\mathrm{sa}},
\]
where the $p_j$ are nonzero orthogonal projections summing to $1$, and
the labels $\lambda_j$ are distinct and nonzero.  Suppose that a projection
$c\in A$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  r(r+2)\tau(c)<1\qquad(\tau\in \mathrm{T}(A)).
  \label{gr:encoding-capacity}
\end{equation}
For every $y=y^*\in cAc$ and every $\rho,\nu>0$, there exist
$h',b'\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}$ such that
\[
  \|h'-h\|<\rho,\qquad \|b'-b\|<\rho,
  \qquad \operatorname{dist}\bigl(y,\ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(h',b')\bigr)<\nu.
\]
Moreover, $h'$ is invertible and $h\in \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(h',b')$.
<!-- end lemma-17 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
We first compress $y$ into a projection commuting with $h$.
Hereditary subalgebras of a real-rank-zero algebra have approximate
identities consisting of projections; see \cite{BrownPedersen}.
For each $j$, choose a projection
$Q_j\in\operatorname{Her}(p_jcp_j)$ so that, with $Q=\sum_jQ_j$,
\begin{equation}
  d:=QyQ\quad\text{satisfies}\quad\|y-d\|<\nu.
  \label{gr:compression-error}
\end{equation}
Indeed, a projection approximate identity in
$\operatorname{Her}(p_jcp_j)$ satisfies
\[
  \|(p_j-Q_j)c\|^2
  =\|(p_j-Q_j)(p_jcp_j)(p_j-Q_j)\|\longrightarrow0.
\]
As there are only finitely many $j$, this gives
$\|(1-Q)c\|\longrightarrow0$.  Since $y=cyc$,
\[
  \|y-QyQ\|\le2\|y\|\,\|(1-Q)c\|,
\]
which proves the required choice.  If $Q=0$, then $\|y\|<\nu$ and
we may take $h'=h$, $b'=b$.  The original $h$ is invertible, so all
conclusions hold in this case.  We henceforth assume $Q\ne0$.

Each $Q_j$ is Murray--von Neumann subequivalent to $c$.
To see this, a projection in $\operatorname{Her}(p_jcp_j)$ is Cuntz
subequivalent to $p_jcp_j$, and $p_jcp_j$ is Cuntz subequivalent to $c$.
For projections, Cuntz subequivalence agrees with Murray--von Neumann
subequivalence.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
  \tau(Q)=\sum_j\tau(Q_j)\le r\tau(c)
  \qquad(\tau\in \mathrm{T}(A)).
  \label{gr:Q-trace}
\end{equation}

We next construct a buffer projection containing $Q$.  Put
\[
  z_j=(p_j-Q_j)bQ.
\]
Choose a projection $f_j\in\operatorname{Her}(z_jz_j^*)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
  \|(p_j-Q_j-f_j)z_j\|<\frac{\rho}{4r}.
  \label{gr:buffer-corner-error}
\end{equation}
A projection approximate identity for $\operatorname{Her}(z_jz_j^*)$
converges on $z_j$ in norm, so this is possible.  If $z_j=0$, take
$f_j=0$.  In all cases $f_j\le p_j-Q_j$.  Also $f_j\precsim Q$:
indeed, $f_j$ is Cuntz subequivalent to $z_jz_j^*$, this positive element
is Cuntz equivalent to $z_j^*z_j$, and
$0\le z_j^*z_j\le\|b\|^2Q$.

Set
\[
  E_j=Q_j+f_j\le p_j,\qquad E=\sum_jE_j.
\]
Then $E$ is a projection, $Q\le E$, and $Eh=hE$.  Equation
\eqref{gr:buffer-corner-error} gives
\begin{equation}
  \|Qb(1-E)\|=\|(1-E)bQ\|
  \le\sum_j\|(p_j-Q_j-f_j)z_j\|<\rho/4.
  \label{gr:buffer-error}
\end{equation}
Its trace cost does not depend on this accuracy:
\begin{equation}
  \tau(E)\le\tau(Q)+r\tau(Q)=(r+1)\tau(Q).
  \label{gr:buffer-trace}
\end{equation}
Together with \eqref{gr:encoding-capacity} and \eqref{gr:Q-trace}, this
implies
\[
  \tau(Q)+\tau(E)\le(r+2)\tau(Q)
  \le r(r+2)\tau(c)<1\qquad(\tau\in \mathrm{T}(A)).
\]
Thus $\tau(Q)<\tau(1-E)$ for every trace.  Projection comparison gives
a projection $P_0\le1-E$ with $P_0\sim Q$.

The projection $P_0$ need not commute with $h$.  We replace it by an
equivalent projection that does.  Real rank zero implies that $V(A)$
is a refinement monoid \cite[Lemma~2.3]{AraPardo}.
Apply finite refinement to
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{j=1}^{r}[Q_j]+[1-E-P_0]
  =[1-E]=\sum_{k=1}^{r}[p_k-E_k].
  \label{gr:refinement-equality}
\end{equation}
This yields actual orthogonal decompositions
\begin{equation}
  Q_j=\sum_{k=1}^{r}Q_{jk},\qquad
  p_k-E_k=\sum_{j=1}^{r}P_{jk}+R_k,
  \qquad Q_{jk}\sim P_{jk},
  \label{gr:refinement-decompositions}
\end{equation}
with zero summands permitted.  We justify the passage from classes to
actual subprojections.  If a projection $q\in A$ satisfies
$[q]=\sum_{\ell}x_{\ell}$ in $V(A)$, choose matrix-projection
representatives of the $x_{\ell}$.  A partial isometry implementing the
equivalence of their direct sum with $q$ transports the summands to
orthogonal subprojections of $q$.  These subprojections belong to the
corner $qAq$.  Apply this separately to every row and column of the
refinement matrix for \eqref{gr:refinement-equality}.  The resulting
matching subprojections have equal classes.  Equivalence of two
projections in $A$, initially implemented in a matrix algebra, is
implemented in $A$ itself: compress the implementing partial isometry
to their initial and final matrix corners.  This proves
\eqref{gr:refinement-decompositions} without cancellation.

Put
\[
  P_k=\sum_jP_{jk},\qquad P=\sum_kP_k.
\]
Then $P\le1-E$, $Ph=hP$, and $P\perp Q$.
Adding partial isometries from $P_{jk}$ to $Q_{jk}$ gives
$w\in QAP$ with
\begin{equation}
  w^*w=P,\qquad ww^*=Q,\qquad wP_{jk}w^*=Q_{jk}.
  \label{gr:transport}
\end{equation}
Orthogonality of the initial and final projections makes the sum a
partial isometry.

Choose $0<\delta_0<\rho/4$ and define
\begin{equation}
  b'=b-QbP-PbQ+\delta_0(w+w^*).
  \label{gr:bridge-coordinate}
\end{equation}
This element is self-adjoint and satisfies $Qb'P=\delta_0w$.  Moreover,
\[
  \|b'-b\|\le2\|QbP\|+2\delta_0
  \le2\|Qb(1-E)\|+2\delta_0<\rho.
\]
It remains to make $Q$, $P$, and the stored element spectrally

  L=\max\{1,\|d\|\},\qquad e=L^{-1}w^*dw\in(PAP)_{\mathrm{sa}}.
\]

  h'=h+\sum_{j,k}(3s+t_{jk})Q_{jk}+6sP+se,

\end{equation}

  \subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{r}\bigl(\lambda_k+[5s,7s]\bigr).
  \label{gr:storage-spectral-bands}
\end{equation}

disjoint.  They all avoid zero.  Thus $h'$ is invertible, and
$1\in \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(h')$ even with the convention that a unit is not
automatically adjoined.  Continuous functional calculus recovers every
$Q_{jk}$ and the aggregate projection $P$ from $h'$; it therefore also

  \qquad P_k=\sum_jP_{jk}\in D,
  \qquad hP=\sum_k\lambda_kP_k\in D.
\]
Consequently,
\[
  e=s^{-1}(Ph'P-hP-6sP)\in D,
  \qquad d=Lwew^*\in D.

Equation \eqref{gr:compression-error} proves
$\operatorname{dist}(y,D)<\nu$.  Finally,
\[
  h=h'-\sum_{j,k}(3s+t_{jk})Q_{jk}-6sP-se\in D.
\]
This last recovery uses both coordinates; no assertion that
$h\in \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(h')$ is needed.

the projection choices above remain available.  If $A=\mathbb C$,
condition \eqref{gr:encoding-capacity} forces $c=0$, which is the
already handled trivial case.

$a,b,x\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there are
$a',b'\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}$ such that

\[
  U_{j,m}=\bigl\{(a,b)\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}^2:

\[
  r(r+2)\tau(c)\le R(R+2)\tau(c)<1/2<1.
\]

Only now, after $F$ is fixed, choose $0<\rho<\eta$ so that
\[
  \|F(h',b')-F(h,b)\|<\varepsilon/4
\]
whenever $\|h'-h\|,\|b'-b\|<\rho$.
Lemma~\ref{gr:uniform-norm-encoding}, with $\nu=\varepsilon/4$, gives
such a pair $h',b'$, with $h'$ invertible, and an element
$y'\in \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(h',b')$ satisfying $\|y-y'\|<\varepsilon/4$.
Invertibility of $h'$ ensures that $1\in \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(h',b')$.
Thus $F(h',b')$ belongs to this algebra even if $F$ has a constant term.
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{dist}\bigl(x,\ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(h',b')\bigr)
  &\le \|x-F(h,b)-y\|
       +\|F(h,b)-F(h',b')\|+\|y-y'\|\\
  &<3\varepsilon/4<\varepsilon.
\end{align*}
Also
\[
  \|h'-a\|<3\eta<\varepsilon,
  \qquad \|b'-b_0\|<2\eta<\varepsilon.
\]
Lemma~\ref{gr:density-criterion} now proves that the generating
self-adjoint pairs and the single generators form norm-dense
$G_\delta$ sets.  The generator-rank characterization for unital
algebras \cite[Theorem~3.4]{ThielGR} yields $\operatorname{gr}(A)\le1$.
If $A$ is noncommutative, it cannot be generated by one self-adjoint
element.  Hence $\operatorname{gr}(A)\ne0$, and
$\operatorname{gr}(A)=1$.

\label{fg:lem:extraction}
Let \(u\) be a unitary with spectrum \(\mathbb T\), and let
\(y=\sum_j\psi_j b_j\psi_j\), where \(b_j=b_j^*\in A\).

\qquad
\phi_i e_h^{1/2}=0\quad(h\neq i).
\end{equation}

\qquad \sup_i\left\|r_i\right\|<\infty.

\]

\begin{equation}\label{fg:eq:extraction}
\phi_i h d_{S_i}=\alpha_i r_i\qquad(i\geq1).
\end{equation}
In particular, \(r_i,G,y\in \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(1,u,h)\).

<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
The uniform bound on \(r_i\) proves norm convergence. We examine
the three types of terms in the compression.

For the background, \eqref{fg:eq:core-background} makes
\(\phi_i\psi_j=0\) unless \(j=\ell_i\), while
\eqref{fg:eq:far-source} gives \(\psi_{\ell_i}d_{S_i}=0\). Thus
\begin{equation}\label{fg:eq:background-zero}
\phi_i y d_{S_i}=0.
\end{equation}

For the ordinary witness terms, the disjoint range cutoffs give
\[
\phi_i r_h d_{S_i}=0\quad(h\neq i).
\]
For \(h=i\), \eqref{fg:eq:range-cutoffs} and \eqref{fg:eq:plateau} give
\begin{equation}\label{fg:eq:own-witness}
\phi_i r_i d_{S_i}=r_i.
\end{equation}

For an adjoint term, write
\begin{equation}\label{fg:eq:adjoint-expression}
\phi_i r_h^*d_{S_i}
=\phi_i\kappa_{S_h}v_h^*e_h^{1/2}d_{S_i}.
\end{equation}
If \(\ell_h\notin S_i\), the rightmost product is zero by
\eqref{fg:eq:core-source}. If \(\ell_h\in S_i\), the assignment for
witness \(i\) gives \(\ell_i\to\ell_h\).
The membership \(\ell_i\in S_h\) would give the reverse arrow
\(\ell_h\to\ell_i\), which is impossible. Therefore
\(\ell_i\notin S_h\), and the leftmost product in
\eqref{fg:eq:adjoint-expression} is zero. This proves
\begin{equation}\label{fg:eq:adjoint-zero}
\phi_i r_h^*d_{S_i}=0\qquad\text{for every }h,
\end{equation}
including \(h=i\).

The identities prove \eqref{fg:eq:extraction} for finite sums and then,
by norm convergence, for the series. Both compression factors belong
to \(\ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(u)\), so division by the nonzero scalar \(\alpha_i\)
recovers \(r_i\) inside \(\ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}(1,u,h)\). The series recovers \(G\),
and subtraction recovers \(y=h-G\).

<!-- lemma-27: body_md -->
\label{lem:free-word-bound}
Let \((M,\tau)=(M_0,\tau_0)*(M_1,\tau_1)\) be a free product of
finite von Neumann algebras with faithful normal tracial states.
If \(a_j\in M_{i_j}\), \(\tau(a_j)=0\), and
\(i_j\ne i_{j+1}\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:free-word-bound}
\left\|a_1\cdots a_n\right\|
\leq (n+1)\prod_{j=1}^n\left\|a_j\right\|_2
 +\sum_{k=1}^n\left\|a_k\right\|\prod_{j\ne k}\left\|a_j\right\|_2.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-27 -->
<!-- proof-35: body_md -->
We use the creation, diagonal, and annihilation decomposition on the
free-product Hilbert space.
Let \(P_i\) be the projection onto the reduced tensors whose first
letter comes from \(L^2(M_i)\ominus\mathbb C1\). For a centered
\(a\in M_i\), left multiplication decomposes as
\[
 a=C(a)+\Delta(a)+A(a),\qquad
 C(a)=P_i aP_i^\perp,\quad
 \Delta(a)=P_i aP_i,\quad A(a)=P_i^\perp aP_i.
\]
Creation prepends the vector \(a\), so
\(\left\|C(a)\right\|=\left\|a\right\|_2\). Annihilation is the adjoint of
creation by \(a^*\), so traciality gives
\(\left\|A(a)\right\|=\left\|a\right\|_2\); also
\(\left\|\Delta(a)\right\|\leq\left\|a\right\|\).
If two adjacent letters belong to different factors, the products
\(AC\), \(A\Delta\), \(\Delta C\), and \(\Delta\Delta\)
vanish, since \(P_0P_1=0\). Thus the expansion of
\(a_1\cdots a_n\) has only the \(n+1\) terms consisting of
creations followed by annihilations, and the \(n\) terms with one
diagonal operator between creations and annihilations. Taking norms
and summing gives \eqref{eq:free-word-bound}.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:free-product-compression}
Let \((A,\tau)=(A_0,\tau_0)*(B,\sigma)\), where the two
unital \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^*}-algebras have faithful tracial states. Identify their
GNS images with subalgebras of \(M=\pi_\tau(A)''\), and put
\(M_0=\pi_{\tau_0}(A_0)''\). Let \(D\subseteq M_0\) be a
unital von Neumann subalgebra. If, for every \(a_0\in A_0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:base-compression}
\lim_{t\downarrow0}
\sup_{\substack{p\in D\ {\rm projection}\\0<\tau_0(p)\leq t}}
\left\|p\bigl(a_0-\mathbb E_D^{M_0}(a_0)\bigr)p\right\|=0,
\end{equation}
then, for every \(a\in A\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-compression}
\lim_{t\downarrow0}
\sup_{\substack{p\in D\ {\rm projection}\\0<\tau(p)\leq t}}
\left\|p\bigl(a-\mathbb E_D^M(a)\bigr)p\right\|=0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-36: body_md -->
The GNS closure \(M\) is the von Neumann free product of
\(M_0\) and \(M_1=\pi_\sigma(B)''\), with their normal trace
extensions. Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:free-word-bound} applies also when
the endpoint letters involve projections in \(D\subseteq M_0\).
Uniqueness of trace-preserving conditional expectations gives
\(\mathbb E_D^M=\mathbb E_D^{M_0}\circ\mathbb E_{M_0}^M\).
In particular, its restriction to \(M_0\) is
\(\mathbb E_D^{M_0}\). The algebraic free product is spanned by
\(A_0\) and the mixed reduced words
\[
 w=c_0b_1c_1\cdots b_mc_m,\qquad m\geq1,
\]
where \(b_j\in B\) and \(c_j\in A_0\), all \(b_j\) and all
interior \(c_j\) are centered, and the endpoints may be arbitrary,
including \(1\). Freeness gives \(\mathbb E_{M_0}^M(w)=0\),
hence \(\mathbb E_D^M(w)=0\).

Put \(n=2m+1\) and
\(K_w=\prod_{j=0}^m\left\|c_j\right\|\prod_{j=1}^m\left\|b_j\right\|\).
For a projection \(p\in D\), let \(s=\tau(p)\) and write
\[
 pc_0=z_0+\alpha1,\qquad c_mp=z_m+\beta1,
 \qquad \tau(z_0)=\tau(z_m)=0.
\]
Centering is an orthogonal projection in \(L^2\), and traciality
gives
\[
 \left\|z_0\right\|_2\leq\sqrt{s}\left\|c_0\right\|,\quad
 \left\|z_m\right\|_2\leq\sqrt{s}\left\|c_m\right\|,\quad
 |\alpha|\leq s\left\|c_0\right\|,\quad |\beta|\leq s\left\|c_m\right\|,
\]
while \(\left\|z_0\right\|\leq2\left\|c_0\right\|\) and
\(\left\|z_m\right\|\leq2\left\|c_m\right\|\).
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:free-word-bound} to the word with both
endpoints centered. Each of its \(2n+1\) operator summands
has at least one endpoint measured in \(L^2\), because at most
one letter is measured in operator norm. Each summand is therefore
bounded by \(2K_w\sqrt{s}\). The two terms with one scalar
endpoint are bounded by \(2K_ws\) each, and the term with two
scalar endpoints is bounded by \(K_ws^2\), using the ordinary
product norm estimate. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-word-compression}
 \left\|pwp\right\|\leq(4n+7)K_w\sqrt{\tau(p)}.
\end{equation}
This estimate also covers words that begin or end in \(B\), by
taking the corresponding \(c_j=1\).

For \(a\in A\), choose a finite sum \(a'=a_0+\sum_j w_j\)
of this form with \(\left\|a-a'\right\|<\eta\). Contractivity gives
\[
 \left\|p(a-\mathbb E_D^M(a))p\right\|
 \leq2\eta+
 \left\|p(a_0-\mathbb E_D^{M_0}(a_0))p\right\|
 +\sum_j\left\|pw_jp\right\|.
\]
The last two terms tend uniformly to zero as \(\tau(p)\to0\),
by \eqref{eq:base-compression} and
\eqref{eq:mixed-word-compression}. Letting \(\eta\to0\)
proves \eqref{eq:product-compression}.

<!-- lemma-28: body_md -->
\label{fg:lem:one-trace}
Let \(A\) be unital with a trace \(\tau\), and suppose it has
comparison by \(\tau\) as in \eqref{eq:factor-comparison}.
If \(A\) contains a \(\tau\)-Haar unitary, then
\(\mathrm{T}(A)=\{\tau\}\).
<!-- end lemma-28 -->
<!-- proof-37: body_md -->
Fix \(\rho\in \mathrm{T}(A)\), and let \(\nu\) be the spectral
distribution of the Haar unitary \(u\) for \(\rho\). An open
arc \(I\) of normalized length \(t\in(0,1)\) is the positivity
set of a continuous positive function \(f_I\). Thus
\[
\mathrm{d}_\tau(f_I(u))=t,\qquad \mathrm{d}_\rho(f_I(u))=\nu(I).
\]
Rotation averaging gives
\(\int_{\mathbb T}\nu(zI)\,dm(z)=t\). Hence some rotation
has measure at most \(t\), and some has measure at least \(t\).

For \(a\in A_+\), put \(s=\mathrm{d}_\tau(a)\in[0,1]\). If \(t>s\),
compare \(a\) with a rotated arc cutoff of \(\rho\)-dimension
at most \(t\); then \(\mathrm{d}_\rho(a)\leq t\). If \(t<s\), compare
a rotated cutoff of \(\rho\)-dimension at least \(t\) with
\(a\); then \(\mathrm{d}_\rho(a)\geq t\). Letting \(t\) approach \(s\),
with the evident one-sided argument at \(0\) and \(1\), gives
\(\mathrm{d}_\rho(a)=\mathrm{d}_\tau(a)\).
Applying this equality to all cutdowns and using
\[
\rho(a)=\int_0^{\left\|a\right\|}\mathrm{d}_\rho((a-t)_+)\,dt
\]
gives \(\rho(a)=\tau(a)\) for all positive \(a\), hence
\(\rho=\tau\).
