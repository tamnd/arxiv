<!-- question-1: body_md -->
\label{linear control}
Let $ u$ be a smooth $k$-admissible solution of \eqref{complex Hessian equation 1} for $k=2,\cdots,n-1$. Does there exists a uniform constant $C$ for which \eqref{lambda and nabla} holds\,?
<!-- end question-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{main thm}
Let $ u$ be a smooth $k$-admissible solution of \eqref{complex Hessian equation 1}  for $k=2,\cdots,n-1$. Suppose that the bisectional curvature of $(M,\omega)$ and the oscillation of  $u$ on $M$ satisfy the following inequality
\begin{equation}\label{curvature}
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}\mathrm{osc}_{M}u<\frac{1}{2(2+\log 2)} \approx  0.2173. 
\end{equation} 
Then, there exists a constant $C$ that depends on $n$, $k$, $h$, $\mathrm{osc}_{M}u$, and $(M,\omega)$  such that the inequality \eqref{lambda and nabla} is satisfied.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
We notice that the upper bound $\frac{1}{2(2+\log 2)}$ is not sharp in general, whereas we will not pursue it in this work.  It is crucial to observe that the quantity $\mathcal{R}_{\omega}\mathrm{osc}_{M}u$ remains unchanged under scaling transformations of the metric $\omega$. Hence, removing the dependence of $\mathrm{osc}_{M}u$  in inequality \eqref{curvature} offers a promising prospect. Suppose this can be achieved, particularly if 
\begin{equation}\label{curvature new}
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}<\frac{1}{2(2+\log 2)}
\end{equation}
and we consequently derive \eqref{lambda and nabla}, then by rescaling the metric $\omega$, the newly formulated condition in \eqref{curvature new} will be satisfied, thereby comprehensively resolving Question \ref{linear control}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{h closes 1}
Let $ u$ be a smooth $k$-admissible solution of \eqref{complex Hessian equation 1}  for $k=2,\cdots,n-1$. Assume that the following inequality holds for a certain uniform constant $\epsilon_{0}$:
\begin{equation}
\|h-1\|_{L^{1}(M)}< \epsilon_{0}\,.   
\end{equation}
Then, there exists a constant $C$ that depends on $n$, $k$, $h$, $\mathrm{osc}_{M}u$, and $(M,\omega)$  such that the inequality \eqref{lambda and nabla} is satisfied.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{gradient estimate} Under the same assumptions as in either Theorem  \ref{main thm} or Theorem \ref{h closes 1}. Then, there exists a  constant $C$ that depends on $n$, $k$, $h$, $\mathrm{osc}_{M}u$, and $(M,\omega)$ such that 
\begin{equation}
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\Delta_{\omega}  u\|_{L^{\infty}} \leq  C.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The interior $W^{2,p}$ estimate for the Dirichlet problem of the real Monge-Amp\`ere equation 
\[
\det (u_{ij})=h
\]
was derived in the seminal work of Caffarelli \cite{Caf}, under the condition that $h$ is a small perturbation of $1$ (i.e. $\|h-1\|_{L^{\infty}} < \epsilon$ for some small constant $\epsilon$). Cheng and Xu \cite{CX23} subsequently obtained an analogous result for the complex Monge-Amp\`ere equation. Theorem \ref{gradient estimate} can be regarded as a global counterpart of these interior estimates, proving global $C^2$ estimates for complex Hessian operators when $h$ is close to $1$ in an $L^1$ sense.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{lower bound of L Q}
At $x_{0}$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{prop}
\begin{split}
C_{0}\Big(\frac{1}{K}+\frac{\mathcal{F}}{\lambda_{1}}\Big)+\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}\mathcal{R}_{\omega}\mathcal{F}
& \geq 
G+B + \varphi'F^{i\overline{i}}\Big(|u_{i\overline i}|^{2}+\sum_{p=1}^{n}|u_{ip}|^{2}\Big)  \\[1mm]
& \qquad
+\varphi'' F^{i\overline{i}}|(|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2})_{i}|^{2}+
\psi'L(u)+\psi'' F^{i\overline{i}}|u_{i}|^{2}\,,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where the terms $G$, $B$ and $\mathcal{F}$ are given by
\[
\begin{split}
G &= -\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Lambda\lambda_{1}}} F^{i\overline{j},k\overline{l}}u_{i\overline{j}1} u_{k\overline{l}\overline{1}}\,,\\[2mm]
B &= -F^{i\overline{i}}\frac{|(\lambda_{1})_{i}|^{2}}{2\sqrt{\Lambda\lambda_{1}}\lambda_{1}}\,,\\[2mm]
\mathcal{F} &= \sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{i\overline{i}}\,.
\end{split}
\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lower bound of L lambda1}
At $x_{0}$, we have
\[
L(\lambda_{1}) \geq 
-F^{i\overline{j},k\overline{l}}u_{i\overline{j}1} u_{k\overline{l}\overline{1}}
-\mathcal{R}_{\omega}\lambda_{1}\mathcal{F}-C_{0}\mathcal{F}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For completeness, the comprehensive proof outlined in \cite{Sze} is presented here. The renowned formulas for computing the derivatives of the eigenvalue  $\lambda_1$  at a diagonal matrix possessing distinct eigenvalues are given below (refer to \cite{Gerhardt,Spruck} for further details):
\begin{equation}\label{derivatives of lambda}
\begin{split}
\lambda_{1}^{pq}& =\delta_{1p}\delta_{1q}\,,\\
\lambda_{1}^{pq,rs}& =(1-\delta_{1p})\frac{\delta_{1q}\delta_{1r}\delta_{ps}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{p}}+(1-\delta_{1r})\frac{\delta_{1s}\delta_{1p}\delta_{rq}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{r}}\,,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{1}^{pq}$ represents the derivative with respect to the entry located at the $p$-th row and $q$-th column. Let us define a matrix
\[
A_{pq}=\delta_{pq}+g^{p\overline{i}}u_{q\overline{i}}\,.
\]
It follows from \eqref{derivatives of lambda} along with the K\"ahlerity of $\omega$ that
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\lambda_{1,i}&=\lambda_{1}^{pq}(A_{pq})_{i}=u_{1\overline{1}i}\,,\\
\lambda_{1,i\overline{i}}&=\lambda_{1}^{pq,rs}(A_{pq})_{i}(A_{rs})_{\overline i}+\lambda_{1}^{pq}(A_{pq})_{i\overline{i}}\\
&=g_{1\overline{1}i\overline i}+u_{1\overline{1}i\overline i}+\sum_{p>1}\frac{|u_{p\overline 1i}|^{2}+|u_{1\overline{p}i}|^{2}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{p}}\,.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
By commuting the covariant derivatives, we have
\begin{equation}\label{nonnegative 1}
\begin{split}
u_{1\overline{1}i\overline i}&=u_{i\overline i1\overline{1}}+(u_{1\overline{1}}-u_{i\overline i})R_{1\overline 1i\overline{i}}\\
&=u_{i\overline i1\overline{1}}+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{i})R_{1\overline 1i\overline{i}}\\
&\geq  u_{i\overline i1\overline{1}}+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{i})\inf_{l\neq 1}R_{1\overline 1l\overline{l}}\\
&\geq  u_{i\overline i1\overline{1}}-\mathcal{R}_{\omega}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{i})\,.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{AA}
\begin{split}
F^{i\overline i}\lambda_{1,i\overline{i}}\geq  &F^{i\overline i}u_{i\overline i1\overline{1}}-\mathcal{R}_{\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{i\overline i}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{i})+F^{i\overline i}g_{1\overline{1}i\overline i}\,.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Applying $e_{1}e_{\overline{1}}$  to the equation \eqref{complex Hessian equation 1}, we see that
\begin{equation*}
F^{i\overline i}(g_{i\overline{i}1\overline{1}}+u_{i\overline{i}1\overline{1}})+F^{i\overline{j},k\overline{l}}u_{i\overline{j}1} u_{k\overline{l}\overline{1}}=(\sqrt[k]{h})_{1\overline{1}}\,.
\end{equation*}
Inserting it into \eqref{AA}, we infer that
\begin{equation*} 
\begin{split}
F^{i\overline i}\lambda_{1,i\overline{i}}\geq  &-F^{i\overline{j},k\overline{l}}u_{i\overline{j}1} u_{k\overline{l}\overline{1}}-\mathcal{R}_{\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{i\overline i}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{i})+F^{i\overline i}(g_{1\overline{1}i\overline i}-g_{i\overline{i}1\overline{1}})+(\sqrt[k]{h})_{1\overline{1}}\\
\geq  & -F^{i\overline{j},k\overline{l}}u_{i\overline{j}1} u_{k\overline{l}\overline{1}}-\mathcal{R}_{\omega}\lambda_{1}\mathcal{F}-C_{0}\mathcal{F}\,.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Here, we have used the facts that $\mathcal{F}\geq  1$ and the homogeneity of $F$ in the last inequality. Indeed,  at the extremal point of $Q$, it can be deduced from   \eqref{Fik} and the Newton-Maclaurin inequality that
\begin{equation}\label{F dayu 1}
\mathcal{F}=\frac{1}{k}\sigma_{k}(\lambda)^{\frac{1-k}{k}}\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\partial \sigma_{k}(\lambda)}{\partial \lambda_i}=\frac{n-k+1}{k}\sigma_{k}(\lambda)^{\frac{1-k}{k}}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda)\geq  1\,.
\end{equation}
This completes the proof of the lemma.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lower bound of L de u}
At $x_{0}$, we have
\begin{equation*}
L(|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2})\geq  F^{i\overline{i}}\Big(|u_{i\overline i}|^{2}+\sum_{p=1}^{n}|u_{ip}|^{2}\Big)-C_{0}K-\mathcal{R}_{\omega}K^{2}\mathcal{F}.
\end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
A direct calculation yields that
\begin{equation*} 
L(|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2})=   F^{i\overline{i}}\Big(|u_{i\overline i}|^{2}+\sum_{p=1}^{n}|u_{ip}|^{2}\Big)+\sum_{p=1}^{n}F^{i\overline i}(u_{pi\overline i}u_{\overline p}+u_{\overline pi\overline i}u_{p})\,.
\end{equation*}
Therefore, we are left to show
\begin{equation}\label{reduced}
\sum_{p=1}^{n}F^{i\overline i}(u_{pi\overline i}u_{\overline p}+u_{\overline pi\overline i}u_{p})\geq  -C_{0}K-\mathcal{R}_{\omega}K^{2}\mathcal{F}\,.  
\end{equation}
By commuting the covariant derivatives,
\[
u_{pi\overline i}=u_{i\overline ip}+\sum_{q=1}^{n}u_{q}R_{i\overline ip\overline{q}}\,, \qquad u_{\overline pi\overline i}=u_{i\overline i\overline p}\,.
\]
Applying $e_{p}$ and $e_{\overline p}$ to the equation \eqref{complex Hessian equation 1}, it follows that
\begin{equation*}
F^{i\overline i}u_{i\overline{i}p}=(\sqrt[k]{h})_{p}\,, \qquad F^{i\overline i}u_{i\overline{i}\overline p}=(\sqrt[k]{h})_{\overline p}\,.
\end{equation*}
Thus, we infer that
\begin{equation}\label{nonnegative 2}
\begin{split}
\sum_{p=1}^{n}F^{i\overline i}(u_{pi\overline i}u_{\overline p}+u_{\overline pi\overline i}u_{p})
&= 2\sum_{p=1}^{n}\mathrm{Re}\Big((\sqrt[k]{h})_{p}u_{\overline{p}} \Big)+F^{i\overline{i}}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}u_{\overline{p}}u_{q}R_{i\overline ip\overline{q}}\\
&\geq  -C_{0}K-\mathcal{R}_{\omega}K^{2}\mathcal{F}\,.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
This establishes the desired inequality \eqref{reduced}, thereby concluding the proof of the lemma.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\cite[Theorem 1]{GPT23}
Let $u$ and $\hat{u}$ be solutions of the following complex Hessian equations
\begin{equation*} 
\sigma_{k}(\omega_{u})=h\,, \qquad \sigma_{k}(\omega_{\hat{u}})=\hat{h}
\end{equation*}
 on a compact K\"ahler manifold $M$ with $\sup_{M}(u-\hat{u})=\sup_{M}(\hat{u}-u)$, where $h$ and $\hat{h}$ are two given non-negative functions on $M$ with 
\begin{equation*}
\int_{M}h\omega^{n}=\int_{M}\hat{h}\omega^{n}=\int_{M}\omega^{n}\,, \qquad \|h\|_{L^{q}}+\|\hat{h}\|_{L^{q}}\leq  Q 
\end{equation*}
for some constants $q>\frac{n}{k}$ and $Q$. Then, there exists a constant $\hat{C}$ depends on $n$, $k$, $\omega$, $Q$, and $q$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{stability}
\|u-\hat{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq  \hat{C}\|h-\hat{h}\|_{L^{1}}^{\hat{\delta} }\,, \qquad \hat{\delta} :=\frac{1}{(n+1)\frac{q-1}{q-\frac{n}{k}}+3}\,.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Without loss of generality, we may assume $\mathcal{R}_{\omega}> 0$, as in the case where $\mathcal{R}_{\omega}=0$  is trivially handled. Let $\hat{u}$ be a constant function defined as $\frac{1}{2}(\sup_{M}u+\inf_{M}u)$ and set $\hat{h}=1$ in the equation  $\sigma_{k}(\omega_{\hat{u}})=\hat{h}$.

It is straightforward to verify the following relationship
\[\sup_{M}(u-\hat{u})=\sup_{M}(\hat{u}-u)\,. \]
Therefore, we infer that
\begin{equation*}
\|u-\hat{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\sup_{M}(u-\hat{u})=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{osc}_{M}u\,.
\end{equation*}
It can be deduced from \eqref{stability} that
\begin{equation}\label{stability 2}
\mathrm{osc}_{M}u\leq  2\hat{C}\|h-1\|_{L^{1}}^{\hat{\delta} }\,.
\end{equation}
Here, $\hat{C}$ is a positive constant depending on $n$, $k$, $\omega$, $q$, and $\|h\|_{L^q}$. To derive \eqref{curvature} from \eqref{stability 2}, it suffices to ensure that 
\begin{equation*}
2\hat{C}\mathcal{R}_{\omega} \|h-1\|_{L^{1}}^{\hat{\delta} }< \frac{1}{2(2+\log 2)}.
\end{equation*}
In simpler terms, this translates to 
\[
 \|h-1\|_{L^{1}}<\Big(\frac{1}{4(2+\log 2)\mathcal{R}_{\omega}\hat{C}} \Big)^{(n+1)\frac{q-1}{q-\frac{n}{k}}+3} 
\]
where the right-hand side represents our desired threshold $\epsilon_{0}$. This completes our proof of Theorem \ref{h closes 1}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{gradient}
Suppose that there is a uniform constant $C_{0}$ such that
\[
\|u\|_{L^\infty}\leq  C_{0}\,, \qquad \|\Delta_{\omega}  u \|_{L^\infty}\leq  C_{0}\Big( \|\nabla  u \|_{L^\infty}+1 \Big)\,.
\]
Then, there exists a uniform bound $C'$ with
\begin{equation}\label{gradient estimate }
\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty} \leq  C'\,.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The proof in the hypercomplex setting has already been established by Gentili and the first-named author in \cite{GZb}.  Interpolation theory, as outlined in section 6.8 of  \cite{GT}, demonstrates that for any constant $ \epsilon>0 $ and $\alpha$ within the range of $ 0<\alpha<1 $, there is a positive constant  $ C(\epsilon,\alpha)$  such that
\[
\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\leq  C(\epsilon,\alpha) \|u\|_{L^\infty}+\epsilon\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq  C(\epsilon,\alpha) C_{0}+\epsilon \|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\,.
\]
By selecting $ q=\frac{2n}{1-\alpha}>2n $ and  applying  to Morrey's inequality along with  elliptic $ L^q $-estimates for the Laplacian, we obtain the following inequality:
\[
\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq  C_{1} \|u\|_{W^{2,q}}\leq  C_{2}\Big( \|u\|_{L^q}+\|\Delta_{\omega}  u\|_{L^q} \Big)\leq  C_{2}\Big(\|u\|_{L^\infty}+\|\Delta_{\omega}  u\|_{L^\infty}\Big)\,.
\]
Here, $ C_{1} $ and $C_{2}$ are positive constants that depend only on $ \alpha $.

Combining all the preceding inequalities, we conclude that
\[
\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\leq  C(\epsilon,\alpha) C_0+\epsilon C_{0}C_{2}\big( 2+\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty} \big)\,. 
\]
From this inequality, we can deduce the gradient estimate \eqref{gradient estimate } by selecting $\epsilon$ small enough to ensure that $\epsilon<(C_{0}C_{2})^{-1} $. Now, we can select 
\[ 
C'=\frac{ C_{0}}{1-\epsilon C_{2}C_{0}}\big(C(\epsilon,\alpha)+2\epsilon C_{2}\big)
\]
as a candidate for the desired upper bound of $\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}$. This completes the proof of proposition.
