Let $\mathbf{V}$ be the quadratic space over $F=\mathbb{Q}$ with the underlying space $\{X\in M_2(\mathbb{Q}): \tr(X)=0\}$ and quadratic form $Q(X)=-\det X$ of signature (2,1). The group $\operatorname{PSL}_2$ acts by isometries on $\mathbf{V}$ by the formula
    \[
        g\cdot X = gX g^{ - 1}, \hspace{1cm} g\in \operatorname{PSL}_2,\; X\in \mathbf{V},
    \]
    which gives an embedding of algebraic groups $\operatorname{PSL}_2\subset \mathbf{G}$. We have $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb{R}) = G^0$, and the standard arithmetic subgroup $\Gamma:=\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$. An isometry between the Poincar\'e upper half-plane and $\mathcal{H}$ is given by
    \[
        \{z =x + yi\in \mathbb{C}: x\in \mathbb{R}, y\in \mathbb{R}_{ > 0}\} \cong \mathcal{H}, \hspace{1cm} z\mapsto  \frac{1}{y}\begin{pmatrix} - x & |z|^2 \\ - 1 & x\end{pmatrix},
    \]
    and this intertwines the usual action of $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb{R})$ and that of $G^0$. We have an isomorphism of varieties
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:pli}
        \mathbb{P}^1\cong\operatorname{Iso}_\mathbf{V},\hspace{1cm} (a:b)\mapsto \operatorname{Span} \begin{pmatrix} -ab & a^2 \\ - b^2 & ab\end{pmatrix} 
    \end{equation}
    which is equivariant for the action of $\operatorname{PSL}_2$ on the left side and $\mathbf{G}$ on the right side.
    
    Let $\gamma\in \Gamma$. The element $\gamma$ stabilizes a geodesic line if and only if $|\tr\gamma|>2$, that is, if $\gamma$ is hyperbolic. In that case, let $a,\tilde a\in \mathbb{R}$ be the two fixed points of $\gamma$ and
    $\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{-1}$ the corresponding eigenvalues of $\gamma$, when acting on $\mathbb{Q}^2$, for the eigenvectors $(a,1)$ and $(a',1)$ respectively.\footnote{Technically $\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon^{-1}$ are only defined up to sign.} By \eqref{eq:pli} we see that the two isotropic lines in $V$ fixed by $\gamma$ are spanned by $u:=(\begin{smallmatrix}

let $K$ be an imaginary quadratic field and $\mathcal{O}_K$ its ring of integers. let $\mathbf{V}$ be the quadratic space over $F=\mathbb{Q}$ with underlying space and quadratic form 
    \[
        \{X\in M_2(K): X+\overline{X}^a=0\}, \hspace{1cm} Q(X) = -\det X
    \]
    ($x^a$ denotes the adjugate of $x$). The signature is then $(3,1)$. The group $\Res_{K / \mathbb{Q}}\operatorname{PSL}_2$ acts by isometries on $\mathbf{V}$ by $g\cdot X = gX \overline{g}^{ - 1}$ and this gives an embedding $\Res_{K / \mathbb{Q}}\operatorname{PSL}_2\subset \mathbf{G}$. We have $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb{C})=G^0$, and the Bianchi group $\Gamma:=\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$. One can also give an isometry between the Poincar\'e upper half-space and $\mathcal{H}$ by
    \[
        \{z =x + yj\in \mathbb{H}: x\in \mathbb{C}, y\in \mathbb{R}_{ > 0}\} \cong \mathcal{H}, \hspace{1cm} z\mapsto  \frac{1}{y}\begin{pmatrix} - x & |z|^2 \\ - 1 & x\end{pmatrix},
    \]
    and this intertwines the action of $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ by quaternionic linear fractional transformations and that of $G^0$. We have an isomorphism of varieties
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:rpli}
        \Res_{K /\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{P}^1\cong\operatorname{Iso}_\mathbf{V},\hspace{1cm} (\alpha:\beta)\mapsto \operatorname{Span} \begin{pmatrix} - \alpha \overline{\beta} & |\alpha|^2 \\ - |\beta|^2 & \overline{\alpha}\beta\end{pmatrix} .
    \end{equation}
    which is equivariant for the action of $\Res_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\operatorname{PSL}_2$ on the left and $\mathbf{G}$ on the right.

    Now let $\gamma\in \Gamma:=\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$, $t=\tr\gamma$, and assume that $|t|>2$.                                                                          
    Let $\alpha,\tilde \alpha\in \mathbb{C}$ be the two fixed points of $\gamma$ and $\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon^{-1}$ the corresponding eigenvalues for $(\alpha,1)$ and $(\alpha',1)$. Then $\alpha$, $\tilde \alpha$, and $\varepsilon$ belong to the quadratic extension $L=K(\sqrt{\delta})$ of $K$, where $\delta=t^2-4$. By \eqref{eq:rpli} the isotropic lines in $V$ fixed by $\gamma$ are then spanned by
    \[
        u: =\begin{pmatrix}
        -\alpha & |\alpha|^2 \\ -1 & \overline{\alpha}
    \end{pmatrix}, \hspace{.5cm} \tilde u : =\begin{pmatrix}
        -\tilde \alpha & |\tilde \alpha|^2 \\ -1 & \overline{\tilde \alpha}
    \end{pmatrix},
    \]
    and it is not hard to compute that the corresponding eigenvalues of $\gamma$, when acting on $V$, are $\lambda=|\varepsilon|^2$ and $\lambda^{-1}=|\varepsilon|^{-2}$. Moreover, a direct computation gives $|\varepsilon|^2+|\varepsilon|^{-2}=(|t|^2+|\delta|) / 2$ so $F' = \mathbb{Q}(|\delta|)$.

    Thus $F'=\mathbb{Q}$ if and only if $|\delta|\in \mathbb{Q}$. If that is the case, then $\overline{L}=L$ and so $L$ is a biquadratic extension with imaginary quadratic subfields $K$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\delta})$ and real quadratic subfield $E'=\mathbb{Q}(|\epsilon|^2)$. By another similar calculation  $\varepsilon / \overline{\varepsilon}+\overline{\varepsilon} / \varepsilon = (|t|^2-|\delta|) /2$, so $\varepsilon / \overline{\varepsilon}$ is a $4$th or $6$th root of unity, as those are the only numbers of absolute value $1$ belonging to quadratic number fields.                                                                                                                                                          
        
    
    If $|\delta|\not\in \mathbb{Q}$, then $E'=\mathbb{Q}(|\varepsilon|^2)$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\varepsilon / \overline{\varepsilon})$ are (Galois-conjugate) quartic ATR number fields. Their common Galois closure is $M:=\mathbb{Q}(|\varepsilon|^2,\varepsilon / \overline{\varepsilon}) = E' K = L\overline{L}$ which is a dihedral number field of degree $8$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prp:tce}

This essentially follows from the corresponding analogous result for the universal forms $\varphi^0$, $\psi^0$, and $\widetilde \psi^0$. Away from the origin of $\mathbb{R}^m$ we can swap the exterior deivative with the integral in the definition of $\widetilde \psi^0$ and hence \eqref{eq:ute} implies $d\widetilde\psi^0 = \varphi^0$. Pulling back by $u(X)$ this implies that $d\widetilde\psi^0(X) = \varphi^0(X)$ away from $\mathcal{D}_X$.

Now whenever $\mathcal{D}_X$ is empty the transgression equation just follows by Stokes theorem. When $X_1$,\ldots, $X_m$ are linearly dependent there is also nothing to prove by Lemma \ref{lem:bpl}(c). The remaining case is when $Q(X)$ is positive definite. Here we emply the following lemma, which we will prove after finishing the current proof. 

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tfb}
    If $Q(X)$ is positive definite then $\mathcal{D}_X\to \mathcal{D}\xrightarrow{u(X)} \mathbb{R}^m$ is a trivial fiber bundle.
\end{lemma}


We only need the fact that $u(X)$ is a fiber bundle, as this allows us to use the projection formula for integration along the fibers. The local integrability of $\widetilde\psi^0(X)$ now follows from \eqref{eq:utf} and the integrability of $\omega$ on $S^{m-1}$. Letting $f=u(X)_\ast \eta\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^m)$ we then have to show that
\[
    -\int_{\mathbb{R}^m} df \wedge \widetilde\psi^0 = \int_{\mathbb{R}^m} f\varphi^0 - f(0).
\]
Again we use the transgression equation, in the form 
\[
    \varphi^0 - t^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{ - \pi r^2t} \nu = -d\left(\int_1^t (t')^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{ - \pi r^2 t'} \frac{dt'}{t'} \rho\right).
\]
Integrating against $f$ and applying Stokes theorem we get 
\[
    \int_{\mathbb{R}^m} f\varphi^0 - \int_{\mathbb{R}^m}ft^{\frac{m}{2}} e^{ - \pi r^2t} \nu = \int_{\mathbb{R}^m}df\wedge \int_1^t (t')^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{ - \pi r^2 t'} \frac{dt'}{t'} \rho.
\]
Finally we take $t\to\infty$. The second term converges to $f(0)$ from the classic fact that $t^{m/2}e^{-\pi r^2t}$ converges towards the delta distribution at $0$. For the right-hand side we have
\[
    \int_{\mathbb{R}^m}df\wedge \int_t^\infty (t')^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{ - \pi r^2 t'} \frac{dt'}{t'} \rho = \int_{\mathbb{R}^m}df\wedge\int_{r^2t}^\infty (t')^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{ - \pi t'} \frac{dt'}{t'} \omega

which converges to $0$ by dominated convergence.

(ii): Suppose that $a\equiv  1 \bmod \mathfrak{n}_v$. To show that $(\begin{smallmatrix}a & \\ & \prescript{t}{}a^{ - 1}\end{smallmatrix})$ acts trivially, we check that $a-1$ acts trivially on $\mathbf{L}_v^\# / \mathbf{L}_v$, and that $\chi_v(\det a)=1$. 

The first part follows from the fact that $\mathbf{L}_v$ also is the dual lattice to $\mathbf{L}_v^\#$. The second part requires some case work. First let us suppose that the residue characteristic of $F_v$ is odd. In that case, if $\mathfrak{n}_v=\mathcal{O}_v$ then $\mathbf{L}_v$ must be unimodular and $(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathbf{V}}{2}}d=1$. On the other hand, if $\mathfrak{n}_v\neq \mathcal{O}_v$ then $\det a$ must be a square. Hence we are left with the case that the residue characteristic is $2$. Now by adapting the argument in \cite[Chapter 15, 4.4.]{MR1662447} it follows that $(\mathbf{L},Q)$ is isomorphic to a direct sum of rank $1$ and rank $2$ quadratic lattices
\[
    q_i (x) =D_i x^2, \hspace{1cm} q_j(x,y) =A_jx^2 + B_jxy + C_jy^2,
\]
where $D_i,A_j,B_j,C_j\in \mathfrak{d}_v^{-1}$. Let $D_j = B_j^2-4A_jC_j$. The levels of $q_i$ and $q_j$ are $4D_i\mathfrak{d}$ and $D_j\mathfrak{d}^2$, respectively, and their discriminants are $2D_i$ and $-D_j$, respectively. Thus
\[
    (-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathbf{V}}{2}}d = \prod_i D_i \prod_j D_j,\hspace{1cm} \mathfrak{n}_v = \bigcap_i (4D_i\mathfrak{d}) \cap\bigcap_j (D_j\mathfrak{d}^2).
\]
If $\mathfrak{n}_v\subsetneqq 4\mathcal{O}_v$, then $\det(a)$ must be a square, so it suffices to consider the case in which $4\mathcal{O}_v\subset\mathfrak{n}_v$. Thus $D_i\mathfrak{d}_v = 4\mathcal{O}_v$ for all $i$, and $4\mathcal{O}_v\subset D_j\mathfrak{d}_v^2$ for all $j$. Since there is an even number of the $q_i$'s, we see that $d\in (F_v^\times)^2\mathcal{O}_v^\times$. If $\mathfrak{n}_v = 4\mathcal{O}_v$, this would imply that $\chi_v(\det a)=1$ by \cite[Proposition 21.]{MR4864833}. Thus there are none of the $q_i$'s, and $4\mathcal{O}_v\subsetneqq D_j\mathfrak{d}_v^2$ for all $j$. This implies that $2\notin B_j\mathfrak{d}_v$, and hence 
\[
    D_j \in B_j^2(1+4B_j^{-2}\mathfrak{d}_v^{-2}) = B_j^2 (1+4 D_j^{ - 1}\mathfrak{d}_v^{ - 1})
    \subset
    B_j^2 (1+4 \mathfrak{n}_v^{ - 1}),
\]
for all $j$. It follows that $\chi_v(\det a)=1$ again by \cite[Proposition 21.]{MR4864833}

If $b\equiv 0 \bmod \mathfrak{n}_v$, it follows from the definition of $\mathfrak{n}_v$ that $(\begin{smallmatrix}1 & b\\ & 1\end{smallmatrix})$ acts trivially. The congruence subgroup $\ker (\operatorname{Sp}_m(\mathcal{O}_v)\to \operatorname{Sp}_m(\mathcal{O}_v / \mathfrak{n}_v))$ is generated by upper and lower triangular matrices \cite[Proposition 13.2.]{MR244257}, so (ii) finally follows from these observations.

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
\label{rem:igc}
    Suppose that $s:(\Gamma_Y\backslash c_Y)\times \mathbb{R}\to M$ is a smooth map with $s(-,0)$ equal to $c(Y)$, and such that $s$ and $c(Y)$ are transverse. For $\varepsilon>0$ let $s_\varepsilon$ be the restriction of $s$ to $(\Gamma_Y\backslash c_Y)\times [0,\varepsilon]$, and $c_\varepsilon$ the restriction of $s$ to $(\Gamma_Y\backslash c_Y)\times\{\varepsilon\}$. Then $\hat{c}(Y)$ is also represented by the spark $\alpha(Y,\widetilde{\psi}^0)-(-1)^{mm'}\delta_{s_\varepsilon}$, where $\delta$ denotes the delta current. For generic $\varepsilon$, $c_\varepsilon$ and $c(Y')$ are disjoint, hence $\hat{c}(Y)\star \hat{c}(Y')$ is given by 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adp}
    \int_M \alpha(Y',\varphi^0)\wedge \alpha(Y,\widetilde{\psi}^0) -( - 1)^{m}
    \int_{s_\varepsilon} \alpha(Y',\varphi^0)
    +( - 1)^{m}
    \int_{c_\varepsilon} \alpha(Y',\widetilde{\psi}^0).
\end{equation}
Now by Stokes theorem and lemma \ref{lem:bpl}(e) the difference between $\varsigma(Y,Y')$ and \eqref{eq:adp} is simply
\begin{equation}
    ( - 1)^m\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{s_\varepsilon} \alpha(Y't^{\frac{1}{2}},\varphi^0).
\end{equation}
We saw in Section \ref{sec:3} that this type of expression encodes exactly the rational part of the linking numbers.
