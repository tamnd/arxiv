<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
For a confidence level $\tau\in(0,1)$, the $\tau$-expectile of the portfolio loss $L_{x}$ under a distribution $F$ is the unique solution $e^{F}_{\tau}(L_{x})$ of
\begin{equation}
\tau\,\mathbb{E}_{F}\bigl[(L_{x}-e^{F}_{\tau}(L_{x}))^{+}\bigr]
-(1-\tau)\,\mathbb{E}_{F}\bigl[(L_{x}-e^{F}_{\tau}(L_{x}))^{-}\bigr]=0,
\label{eq:expectile-def}
\end{equation}
where $(\cdot)^{+}=\max\{0,\cdot\}$ and $(\cdot)^{-}=\max\{0,-\,\cdot\}$. Equivalently, $e^{F}_{\tau}$ is the minimizer of the asymmetric quadratic loss
$\mathbb{E}_{F}[\tau((L_{x}-e)^{+})^{2}+(1-\tau)((L_{x}-e)^{-})^{2}]$.
\label{sec:expectile}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A conditional risk mapping $\rho_{t_{1},t_{2}}(\cdot)$ is separable if it decomposes into a sum of single-period components,
\begin{equation}
\rho_{t_{1},t_{2}}\bigl(-w_{t_{1},t_{2}}\bigr)
=\sum_{i=t_{1}+1}^{t_{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\rho_{i}(-w_{i}\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t_{1}}\bigr],
\label{eq:separable}
\end{equation}
where $-w_{t_{1},t_{2}}$ denotes the investor's loss from $t_{1}$ to $t_{2}$.
\label{def:separable}
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:timeconsistency}
For $\tau\in[\tfrac12,1)$ and $\lambda\in(0,1)$, the per-period mapping
\[
\rho_{t}(X_{t+1})=(1-\lambda)\,\mathbb{E}_{F_{t}}\bigl(-\xi_{t}^{\top}x_{t}\bigr)
+\lambda\,e^{F_{t}}_{\tau}\bigl(-\xi_{t}^{\top}x_{t}\bigr)
\]
is monotone and coherent. Consequently the objective \eqref{eq:objective} is separable in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:separable}, the multi-period program decouples across periods, and sequential re-optimization at each rebalancing date is time consistent.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:nonrep}
For $\tau\in(\tfrac12,1)$ the expectile $e_{\tau}$ is coherent and law invariant but not comonotone additive. Consequently $e_{\tau}$ is not a spectral risk measure and admits no representation as a single mixture
$e_{\tau}(\cdot)=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{CVaR}_{\alpha}(\cdot)\,d\mu(\alpha)$
for any probability measure $\mu$ on $[0,1)$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
On $M(\Xi)\times M(\Xi)$ the order-$p$ Wasserstein distance is
\begin{equation}
W_{p}(F_{1},F_{2})=\Bigl(\inf_{\pi\in M(\Xi^{2})}
\int_{\Xi^{2}}\lVert \xi_{1}-\xi_{2} \rVert^{p}\,d\pi(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\Bigr)^{1/p},
\label{eq:wasserstein}
\end{equation}
where $F_{1},F_{2}\in M(\Xi)$ are the marginals of $\xi_{1},\xi_{2}$ and $\pi$ is a coupling.
\label{def:wasserstein}
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Under an $\ell_{\infty}$ ground metric, the associated dual norm is $\ell_{1}$. Since $\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{1}\equiv1$ on the simplex \eqref{eq:simplex}, the term $\lVert x_t \rVert_{*}$ becomes constant and the robust adjustment does not distinguish among feasible allocations. In contrast, under an $\ell_{1}$ ground metric, the associated dual norm is $\ell_{\infty}$ and $\lVert x_t \rVert_{\infty}$ remains decision dependent. Propositions~\ref{prop:degeneracy} and~\ref{prop:norm} make the consequences precise, and Section~\ref{sec:h2} tests them.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:beta}
Since $e^{F}_{\tau}$ is not
an expectation, the supremum of the sum in \eqref{eq:model-a} does not in general equal the sum of the suprema. With the mean pinned at $-\beta_{t}$ on
$\mathcal{P}_{t}(\beta_{t}):=\mathcal{P}_{t}(F_{t,N_{t}})\cap\{F:\mathbb{E}_{F}(\xi_{t}^{\top}x_{t})=\beta_{t}\}$, however, the decomposition is exact:
\begin{equation}
\max_{F_{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{t}(\beta_{t})}
\Bigl[(1-\lambda)(-\beta_{t})+\lambda e^{F_{t}}_{\tau}\Bigr]
=-(1-\lambda)\beta_{t}+\lambda\max_{F_{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{t}(\beta_{t})}e^{F_{t}}_{\tau}
\bigl(-\xi_{t}^{\top}x_{t}\bigr).
\label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
The remainder of the derivation rests on \eqref{eq:split}.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:supsup}
The supremum-form representation does not directly resolve the ambiguity problem for two reasons. First, the outer level of the model in Section~\ref{sec:trilevel} involves minimization with respect to $x_t$. Interchanging the relevant operators therefore leads to a $\min_x\max_{\gamma}$ formulation. This results in a semi-infinite optimization problem over a continuum of values of $\gamma$. Its solution generally requires either discretization or a cutting-plane procedure. Consequently, the resulting formulation no longer preserves the exact linear programming representation established in Theorem~\ref{thm:reformulation}. Second, the inner problem includes a mean equality constraint within the ambiguity ball as described in Section~\ref{sec:trilevel}. The closed-form characterization of worst-case used for the interchange in \citet{liu2022inf} is not directly applicable in the presence of this additional constraint. The envelope formulation developed in Section~\ref{sec:envelope} addresses these limitations by introducing a single scalar variable together with one binding constraint that characterizes its value.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:fixedpoint}
Let $\tau\in[\tfrac12,1)$ and $\kappa_{\tau}=(2\tau-1)/(1-\tau)$. For any $F$ with
$\mathbb{E}_{F}|L|<\infty$,
\begin{equation}
e^{F}_{\tau}(L)=\mathbb{E}_{F}[L]+\kappa_{\tau}\,\mathbb{E}_{F}\bigl[(L-e^{F}_{\tau}(L))^{+}\bigr].
\label{eq:fixedpoint}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:envelope}
Let $\mathcal{P}$ be a set of probability measures on $\Xi$ with
$\sup_{F\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{F}\lVert \xi \rVert<\infty$, and let $\tau\in(\tfrac12,1)$. Define
\begin{equation}
\Psi(q):=\sup_{F\in\mathcal{P}}\Bigl[\mathbb{E}_{F}[L]+\kappa_{\tau}\,\mathbb{E}_{F}(L-q)^{+}\Bigr]-q .
\label{eq:Psi}
\end{equation}
Then $\Psi$ is continuous and strictly decreasing on $\mathbb{R}$, possesses a unique root $q^{\star}$, and
\begin{equation}
\sup_{F\in\mathcal{P}}e^{F}_{\tau}(L)=q^{\star}=\min\bigl\{q\in\mathbb{R}:\Psi(q)\leq0\bigr\}.
\nonumber
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:twopiece}
For fixed $x_{t}\in\mathcal{X}$ and $q_{t}\in\mathbb{R}$, the integrand appearing in \eqref{eq:Psi} is
\begin{equation}
h_{t}(\xi_{t}):=-\xi_{t}^{\top}x_{t}+\kappa_{\tau}\bigl(-\xi_{t}^{\top}x_{t}-q_{t}\bigr)^{+}
=\max\bigl\{-k_{1}\xi_{t}^{\top}x_{t}+c_{1}q_{t},\;
-k_{2}\xi_{t}^{\top}x_{t}+c_{2}q_{t}\bigr\},
\label{eq:twopiece}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
k_{1}=1,\qquad k_{2}=1+\kappa_{\tau},\qquad c_{1}=0,\qquad c_{2}=-\kappa_{\tau},\qquad
\Delta:=k_{2}-k_{1}=\kappa_{\tau} .
\nonumber
\end{equation}
In particular $k_{2}>k_{1}\geq0$ for every $\tau\in(\tfrac12,1)$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
In the mean-CVaR inner problem of \citet{wang2026multi}, $k_{1}=1-\lambda$, $k_{2}=(1-\alpha+\lambda\alpha)/(1-\alpha)$ and $\Delta=\lambda/(1-\alpha)$. In contrast, the slope gap $\Delta$ in the present formulation is the endogenous constant $\kappa_{\tau}$ determined by the expectile level rather than the exogenous tail-inflation factor $1/(1-\alpha)$. Proposition~\ref{prop:sensitivity} shows that this distinction also affects the marginal cost of robustness.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:inherit}
The function $\gamma_{t,2}^{0}$ in \eqref{eq:gamma2} will coincide with that arising under an $\ell_{\infty}$ ground metric. The change in the ground metric affects the expression only through the factor $w_{t}$, thus analysis of Section~\ref{sec:beta} is unaffected by the choice of ground metric.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rem:slope}
Differentiating $q=D_{t}(q,\beta_{t})$ implicitly, and using the envelope identity $\partial D_{t}/\partial\beta=\gamma_{t,1}
=a_{t,1}-1$ together with $\partial D_{t}/\partial q=-\kappa_{\tau} P$, where
$P:=\mathbb{P}_{F^{\star}}(L_{x_{t}}>q_{t})$ is evaluated under the worst-case measure, gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{dq_{t}}{d\beta_{t}}=\frac{a_{t,1}-1}{1+\kappa_{\tau} P},
\qquad
\frac{dV_{t}}{d\beta_{t}}=-(1-\lambda)+\frac{\lambda\,(a_{t,1}-1)}{1+\kappa_{\tau} P}.
\label{eq:slope}
\end{equation}
The affine expression is recovered only in the degenerate case $\kappa_{\tau} P=0$.
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:betaL}
The function $D_{t}(q,\cdot)$ is concave and non-increasing on
$[\beta_{t,L},\beta_{t,U}]$. Consequently $\gamma_{t,1}=\partial D_{t}/\partial\beta\leq0$, so $a_{t,1}\leq1$, and by \eqref{eq:slope}
\begin{equation}
\frac{dV_{t}}{d\beta_{t}}\ \leq\ -(1-\lambda)\ <\ 0
\qquad\text{for every admissible }a_{t,1}.
\label{eq:slope-negative}
\end{equation}
The intermediate maximization is therefore attained at $\beta_{t}^{\star}=\beta_{t,L}$ for all parameter values.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:reformulation}
Let $\tau\in(\tfrac12,1)$, $\lambda\in(0,1)$, $\rho_{t}>0$, and let $F_{t}\in M(\Xi)$ satisfy \eqref{eq:moment}. The optimal value of Model
\eqref{eq:model-a}--\eqref{eq:model-c} equals the minimum of the optimal values of the four programs \emph{EX-A1}, \emph{EX-A2}, \emph{EX-B1} and \emph{EX-B2} stated below, and the corresponding multi-period optimal allocations are obtained from their solutions. Each program is linear in $(x_{t},q_{t},s_{t,i},w_{t})$ for fixed $a_{t,1}$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
\label{rem:w}
The constraints \eqref{eq:EXA1-f} impose only $w_t\ge x_{t,i}$ and leave $w_t$ free to exceed $\|x_t\|_\infty$. In Case~A this is immaterial: the objective \eqref{eq:EXA1-a}, the constraints \eqref{eq:EXA1-b} and \eqref{eq:EXA1-e} penalize larger $w_t$, so the constraints bind and $w_t^\star=\|x_t\|_\infty$. In Case~B the constraints \eqref{eq:EXB-c} increases $w_t$
and
\(
w_t^\star=\max\Bigl\{\|x_t\|_\infty,\ \bigl|\hat{\mu}_t^{\top}x_t-r_t\bigr|/\rho_t\Bigr\},
\)
with equality to $\|x_t\|_\infty$ precisely on the region where the Case~B condition \eqref{eq:caseB} holds. Outside that region the large $w_t$ increases $q_t$ through \eqref{eq:EXB-b} and so penalizes the allocation. Each branch is thus exact on its own case region and conservative elsewhere, which is all the minimization over the four branches requires.
<!-- end remark-7 -->
<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
\label{rem:no-bisection}
The constraints \eqref{eq:EXA1-b} and \eqref{eq:EXB-b} represents the fixed point of Theorem~\ref{thm:envelope} as inequalities rather than equalities. This is exact because $q_t$ enters the objective with the strictly positive coefficient $\lambda$. Hence, the minimization selects the smallest feasible value of $q_t$, at which the corresponding constraint holds with equality. This value coincides with the fixed point $q_t^\star$ and therefore no outer bisection procedure is required. Since $q_t$ appears in
\eqref{eq:EXA1-d} only with coefficient $-\kappa_\tau$, the system remains linear in $(q_t,s_{t,i})$.
<!-- end remark-8 -->
<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
\label{rem:caseB}
In Case~B the selected endpoint $\beta_{t,L}=r_t$ does not depend on $x_t$, so $-(1-\lambda)r_t$ is an additive constant and $\lambda$ leaves the argmin: the program reduces to worst-case expectile minimization subject to \eqref{eq:EXB-c}. The share of rebalancing dates in Case~A therefore determines whether the mean--risk trade-off was active at all and should be reported alongside performance. Under the $\ell_1$ ground metric the Case~A condition \eqref{eq:caseA} is easy to satisfy for diversified portfolio than $\ell_\infty$ ground metric, since
$\|x_t\|_\infty\le\|x_t\|_1=1$. Hence, the mean--risk trade-off remains relevant over a larger region of the feasible set.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:sensitivity}
Let
\begin{equation}
q_{t}^{\star}(\rho_{t}):=\sup_{F_{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{t}(F_{t,N_{t}})}
e^{F_{t}}_{\tau}(L_{x_{t}}),
\qquad
S_{t}(\rho_{t}):=\bigl\{i:\hat{L}_{t,i}>q_{t}^{\star}(\rho_{t})\bigr\},
\qquad
m_{t}:=|S_{t}(\rho_{t})|,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
so that $m_{t}/N_{t}=\hat{P}_{t}(q_{t}^{\star})$. Then $q_{t}^{\star}$ is non-decreasing in $\rho_{t}$, $m_{t}$ is non-increasing in $\rho_{t}$, and on any interval over which $S_{t}$ is constant $q_{t}^{\star}$ is exactly affine in $\rho_{t}$ with
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial q_{t}^{\star}}{\partial\rho_{t}}
=\frac{(1+\kappa_{\tau})\,\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}}{1+\kappa_{\tau}\,m_{t}/N_{t}}.
\label{eq:sensitivity}
\end{equation}
Consequently $q_{t}^{\star}$ is piecewise affine, non-decreasing and convex in $\rho_{t}$. By contrast, the worst-case CVaR $\alpha\in(0,1)$ on the same ambiguity set satisfies
\begin{equation}
\sup_{F_{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{t}(F_{t,N_{t}})}\mathrm{CVaR}^{F_{t}}_{\alpha}(L_{x_{t}})
=\mathrm{CVaR}^{F_{t,N_{t}}}_{\alpha}(L_{x_{t}})
+\frac{\rho_{t}\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}}{1-\alpha},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho_{t}}\sup_{F_{t}}\mathrm{CVaR}^{F_{t}}_{\alpha}(L_{x_{t}})
=\frac{\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}}{1-\alpha},
\label{eq:cvar-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
which is constant in $\rho_{t}$ and invariant to the loss distribution.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:degeneracy}
Suppose $\mathrm{sp}_{t}(x_{t})>0$ and define
\begin{equation}
\rho^{\mathrm{crit}}_{t}(x_{t}):=
\frac{\mathrm{sp}_{t}(x_{t})}{(1+\kappa_{\tau})\,\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}}
=\frac{\max_{i}\hat{L}_{t,i}-\bar{L}_{t}}{(1+\kappa_{\tau})\,\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}}>0 .
\label{eq:rhocrit}
\end{equation}
Then the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\rho_{t}\geq\rho^{\mathrm{crit}}_{t}(x_{t})$ if and only if
$q_{t}^{\star}\geq\max_{i}\hat{L}_{t,i}$, equivalently
$\hat{P}_{t}(q_{t}^{\star})=0$, in which case the second affine piece of
\eqref{eq:twopiece} is inactive at every sample point.
\item[(ii)] On that region the worst-case expectile is available in closed form,
\begin{equation}
q_{t}^{\star}=(1+\kappa_{\tau})\rho_{t}\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}-\hat{\mu}_{t}^{\top}x_{t},
\qquad
\frac{\partial q_{t}^{\star}}{\partial\rho_{t}}=(1+\kappa_{\tau})\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*},
\label{eq:degenerate-q}
\end{equation}
the second identity being the $m_{t}=0$ endpoint of \eqref{eq:sensitivity} and the
maximum attainable value of that derivative.
\item[(iii)] If $\rho_{t}<\rho^{\mathrm{crit}}_{t}(x_{t})$ then
$\hat{P}_{t}(q_{t}^{\star})\geq1/N_{t}>0$ and
$\overline{(\hat{L}_{t}-q_{t}^{\star})^{+}}>0$.
\end{enumerate}
If instead $\mathrm{sp}_{t}(x_{t})=0$, all sample losses coincide,
$\rho^{\mathrm{crit}}_{t}(x_{t})=0$, and \eqref{eq:degenerate-q} holds for every
$\rho_{t}\geq0$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- remark-10: body_md -->
\label{rem:hcm}
Proposition~\ref{prop:degeneracy} admits a direct connection with an existing univariate result. Fix $x_{t}$ and map $\xi\mapsto-\xi^{\top}x_{t}$. The image is a scalar reference distribution $G$ with
$\mu_{G}=-\hat{\mu}_{t}^{\top}x_{t}$ and $\operatorname{ess\,sup}G=\max_{i}\hat{L}_{t,i}$,
and since $\xi\mapsto\xi^{\top}x_{t}$ is Lipschitz with modulus $\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}$ the image of
$\mathcal{P}_{t}(F_{t,N_{t}})$ is contained in the scalar $1$-Wasserstein ball around $G$ of
radius $\varepsilon:=\rho_{t}\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}$. 
Using $1+\kappa_{\tau}=\tau/(1-\tau)$, which corresponds to the parameter $\beta$ in \citet{hu2024extreme}, part~(i) of Proposition~\ref{prop:degeneracy} becomes $\operatorname{ess\,sup}G\leq\mu_{G}+\varepsilon(1+\kappa_{\tau})$ while \eqref{eq:degenerate-q} gives the value
$\mu_{G}+\varepsilon(1+\kappa_{\tau})$.
These expressions coincide with the condition and the corresponding worst-case value in Theorem~2(i) of \citet{hu2024extreme}. 
The contribution of Proposition~\ref{prop:degeneracy} therefore lies not in the scalar identity itself but in its dependence on the portfolio allocation. In particular,
$\rho^{\mathrm{crit}}_{t}$ depends on $x_t$ through both $\mathrm{sp}_{t}(x_{t})$ and $\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}$. This decision dependence provides the basis for the ground-metric comparison in Proposition~\ref{prop:degeneracy}.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:nominal}
Let $\rho_{t}=0$ for every $t$ and let $\lambda\in(0,1)$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\mathcal{P}_{t}(F_{t,N_{t}})=\{F_{t,N_{t}}\}$, the interval
\eqref{eq:betabounds} collapses to the single point

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:norm}
The dual norm $\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}$ enters the worst-case value through the term
$(1+\kappa_\tau)\rho_{t}\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}$ and the degeneracy threshold \eqref{eq:rhocrit} through $\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{*}^{-1}$. Consequently the ground metric in \eqref{eq:wasserstein} determines the implicit regularisation induced by robustification:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\ell_{\infty}$ ground metric (dual $\ell_{1}$): on the simplex
$\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{1}\equiv1$, the robust term is the constant $(1+\kappa_\tau)\rho_{t}$, and $\rho_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is identical for every allocation. Robustification exerts no first-order penalty on concentration, and past the threshold the program reduces to maximization of the sample mean;
\item[(ii)] $\ell_{1}$ ground metric (dual $\ell_{\infty}$): the penalty
$(1+\kappa_\tau)\rho_{t}\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{\infty}$ is a maximum-weight concentration penalty, minimized at equal weights, and remains linear upon introducing $w_{t}\geq x_{t,i}$; $\rho^{\mathrm{crit}}_{t}$ scales with $\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{\infty}^{-1}$;
\item[(iii)] $\ell_{2}$ ground metric: the penalty $(1+\kappa_\tau)\rho_{t}\lVert x_{t} \rVert_{2}$ is of ridge type and the program becomes a second-order cone program.
\end{enumerate}

Fix $x$, write $L=L_x$ and
$g(q;F):=\mathbb{E}_F[L]+\kappa_\tau\,\mathbb{E}_F(L-q)^+-q$, so that
$\Psi=\sup_{F\in\mathcal{P}}g(\cdot\,;F)$.

\emph{(i) A slope bound uniform in $F$.} For each $F\in\mathcal{P}$ the map
$q\mapsto\mathbb{E}_F(L-q)^+$ is non-increasing and $1$-Lipschitz, so for $q_1<q_2$
\[
-(1+\kappa_\tau)(q_2-q_1)\ \le\ g(q_2;F)-g(q_1;F)\ \le\ -(q_2-q_1),
\]
the two bounds being independent of $F$. Each $g(\cdot\,;F)$ is therefore strictly decreasing, with unique root $e^F_\tau(L)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:fixedpoint}; and since the bounds are uniform, the
pointwise supremum $\Psi$ inherits both the $(1+\kappa_\tau)$-Lipschitz continuity and the strict decrease at rate at least one. Finiteness of $\Psi$ follows from $|L|\le\|\xi\|\,\|x\|_*$ and $(L-q)^+\le|L|+|q|$ together with $\sup_{F\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_F\|\xi\|<\infty$. As $\Psi(q)\to+\infty$ for $q\to-\infty$ and $\Psi(q)\to-\infty$ for $q\to+\infty$, continuity and
strict monotonicity give a unique root $q^\star$.

\emph{(ii) Identification of the root.} For every $F\in\mathcal{P}$ we have
$g(q^\star;F)\le\Psi(q^\star)=0$, and since $g(\cdot\,;F)$ is strictly decreasing with root $e^F_\tau(L)$ this forces $e^F_\tau(L)\le q^\star$; hence
$\sup_{F}e^F_\tau(L)\le q^\star$. Conversely, fix $q<q^\star$. Strict monotonicity gives $\Psi(q)>0$, so by definition of the supremum there is $F\in\mathcal{P}$ with $g(q;F)>0$ and therefore $e^F_\tau(L)>q$. Letting $q\uparrow q^\star$ yields the reverse inequality, so $\sup_{F}e^F_\tau(L)=q^\star$, and $q^\star=\min\{q:\Psi(q)\le0\}$ because $\Psi$ is continuous and strictly decreasing. The supremum is attained whenever $\mathcal{P}$ is weakly compact and
$F\mapsto\mathbb{E}_F[L]+\kappa_\tau\mathbb{E}_F(L-q^\star)^+$ is upper semicontinuous, which holds for the \eqref{eq:ambiguityset} under \eqref{eq:moment}.

$h_t(\xi)=\max\{-k_1\xi^{\top}x_t+c_1q_t,\,-k_2\xi^{\top}x_t+c_2q_t\}$ with

In Case~A the selected endpoint is extreme and Slater fails, but $D_t(q,\cdot)$ is concave by Lemma~\ref{lem:betaL} and the optimal transport value is continuous in the mean constraint, so the dual value is continuous at the endpoint and the substitution remains exact.

\emph{(ii) Factorization of $\gamma_{t,2}^\star$.} Under the $\ell_1$ ground metric the dual norm is $\ell_\infty$, so \eqref{eq:dual-d} read $|a_{t,1}|w_t\le\gamma_{t,2}$ and

\emph{(i) The robust fixed point.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:twopiece} the integrand $h_t$ in \eqref{eq:Psi} is a maximum of two affine functions of $\xi_t$ with gradients $-x_t$ and $-(1+\kappa_\tau)x_t$, hence Lipschitz with modulus $(1+\kappa_\tau)\|x_t\|_*$. Without the mean constraint the dual \eqref{eq:dual-d} at $\gamma_{t,1}=0$ reads $\gamma_{t,2}\ge(1+\kappa_\tau)\|x_t\|_*$, in agreement with $\gamma_{t,2}^0(1)=1+\kappa_\tau$, and the dual objective is increasing in $\gamma_{t,2}$; the minimum is attained at $\gamma_{t,2}^\star=(1+\kappa_\tau)\|x_t\|_*$, so that by Theorem~\ref{thm:envelope} the quantity $q_t^\star$ is the unique fixed point of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G}

=(1+\kappa_\tau)\rho_t\|x_t\|_*+\bar{L}_t+\kappa_\tau\overline{(\hat{L}_t-q)^+}.
\end{equation}
Since $G$ is non-decreasing in $\rho_t$ and non-increasing in $q$, while $\Psi=G-q$ is strictly decreasing, $q_t^\star$ is non-decreasing in $\rho_t$; hence $S_t$ shrinks and $m_t$ is non-increasing. Holding $S_t$ fixed gives
$\overline{(\hat{L}_t-q)^+}=N_t^{-1}\sum_{i\in S_t}(\hat{L}_{t,i}-q)$, and $q_t^\star=G(q_t^\star)$ rearranges to
\begin{equation}
q_t^\star\Bigl(1+\tfrac{\kappa_\tau m_t}{N_t}\Bigr)
=(1+\kappa_\tau)\rho_t\|x_t\|_*+\bar{L}_t+\frac{\kappa_\tau}{N_t}\sum_{i\in S_t}\hat{L}_{t,i},\nonumber
\end{equation}
which is affine in $\rho_t$; differentiating yields \eqref{eq:slope}. As $m_t$ is non-increasing in $\rho_t$ the slope is non-decreasing, so $q_t^\star$ is convex.

\emph{(ii) The CVaR comparison.} By the Rockafellar--Uryasev representation \citep{rockafellar2000optimization}, $\mathrm{CVaR}^F_\alpha(L_{x_t})=\min_q\{q+(1-\alpha)^{-1}\mathbb{E}_F(L_{x_t}-q)^+\}$.
