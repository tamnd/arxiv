<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:Bregular}
A bounded domain \(\Omega\Subset\mathbb C^n\) is called \emph{B-regular} if for every
\(\psi\in C(\partial\Omega)\) there exists
\(u\in\mathrm{PSH}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline\Omega)\) such that
\(u|_{\partial\Omega}=\psi\).
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:convexifiable}
Let \(\Omega\Subset\mathbb C^n\), \(n\ge2\), be B-regular and convexifiable, and
let $f$ define a reduced divisor on a neighborhood of $\overline\Omega$, with $A=(f)|_\Omega$.  Then
\[
 G_{A,\Omega}\in C(\Omega,[-\infty,0]).
\]
Moreover, with \(v_j=P_\Omega\min\{-\log|f|,j\}\),
\[
 v_j\uparrow\widetilde G_A\quad\text{on }\Omega\setminus A,
 \qquad
 \left(\sup_jv_j\right)^*=\widetilde G_A\quad\text{on }\Omega.
\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:Bregular-convexifiable}
For a bounded convex domain, Sibony's results imply B-regularity when the boundary contains no nonconstant analytic disc; conversely, B-regularity excludes such discs by the maximum principle.  Thus the two conditions are equivalent in the convex case; see \cite{Sibony87}.  Since B-regularity and boundary analytic discs are preserved by biholomorphisms extending across the closures, the same characterization holds for convexifiable domains.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:sectorial}
Let $f$ define a reduced divisor on a neighborhood of $\overline\Omega$, and put $A=(f)|_\Omega$.  The pair $(\Omega,A)$ is said to admit a
\emph{sectorial inward deformation}, abbreviated \emph{SID}, if there are neighborhoods
$U$ of $\overline\Omega$ and $V$ of $A\cap\partial\Omega$, constants
$0<\theta<\pi/2$, $t_0,c,C>0$, and a holomorphic map
\[
 S:U\times\Delta_{t_0}\longrightarrow\mathbb C^n,
 \qquad S_\tau(z):=S(z,\tau),
\]
such that $S_0=\mathrm{id}$ and, for every $\tau\in\Gamma_{\theta,t_0}$,
\begin{align}
 S_\tau(\Omega)&\subset\Omega,\label{eq:sector-selfmap}\\
 \sup_{z\in\overline\Omega}|S_\tau(z)-z|&\le C|\tau|,\label{eq:sector-displacement}\\
 \operatorname{dist}(S_\tau(z),\partial\Omega)&\ge c|\tau|,
 \qquad z\in V\cap\overline\Omega.\label{eq:sector-depth}
\end{align}
In addition, at every singular boundary point of the divisor,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-escape}
 p\in\operatorname{Sing}(A)\cap\partial\Omega
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \tau\longmapsto f(S_\tau(p))\ \hbox{is not identically zero near }0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:sector-geometry}
Three features of the SID condition are useful.  First, no point of $\Omega$ is required to be fixed.  A submean argument will select a nearby moving center on a smaller sector.  Second, the depth estimate \eqref{eq:sector-depth} is imposed only near $A\cap\partial\Omega$; singular points of $A$ contained in a compact subset of $\Omega$ are treated by ordinary local Weierstrass preparation.  Third, local invertibility follows automatically from $D_zS_0=I$ on a neighborhood of the compact set $\overline\Omega$: the maps $S_\tau$ are locally biholomorphic near $\overline\Omega$ for all sufficiently small $|\tau|$.  At regular boundary points of $A$ the insertion is transverse to the divisor and does not use \eqref{eq:finite-escape}; that condition is needed only at singular boundary points, where the parameter $\tau$ itself becomes the Weierstrass variable.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:arkiv-sector}
The relation with \cite[Theorem~2.2]{Dieu05} is complementary rather than one of formal inclusion.  The radial hypothesis there is imposed only on a relative neighborhood of $A\cap\partial\Omega$ and is combined with holomorphic convexity and a boundary regularity condition on the complementary part of $\partial\Omega$; it need not produce a global holomorphic self-map of $\Omega$ as required by SID.  In particular, \cite[Corollary~2.9]{Dieu05} applies to bounded pseudoconvex strongly star-shaped domains and finite unions of complex hyperplanes through the star center, without assuming convexifiability or B-regularity.  Conversely, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} allows a general reduced hypersurface, but at each singular boundary point it requires finite-order escape.  For the homogeneous divisor in \cite{Dieu05}, radial dilation about the common intersection point preserves the divisor; hence at a singular boundary point $p$ one has $f(tp)=0$ for all $t$, and this radial family fails \eqref{eq:finite-escape}.  Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:main} directly recovers the radial situation of \cite{Dieu05} only when that radial motion extends to a global SID with the quantitative depth estimate and the finite-order escape condition is satisfied.  Neither theorem therefore subsumes the other.  On bounded convex B-regular domains, and more generally after a convexifying biholomorphism, Theorem~\ref{thm:convexifiable} nevertheless recovers the corresponding homogeneous hyperplane examples by using the contraction from Proposition~\ref{thm:geometry} toward a point outside $A$, rather than the dilation through the common intersection point.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $\Omega\Subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\ge2$, be a domain.  Let $f\not\equiv0$ be holomorphic on an open neighborhood $U_f$ of $\overline\Omega$ and assume that the divisor $(f)$ is reduced on $U_f$.  Suppose that $\{f=0\}\cap\Omega\ne\varnothing$, and put $A=(f)|_\Omega$.  Assume that the pair $(\Omega,A)$ admits an SID in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:sectorial}.  Multiplying $f$ by a nonzero constant if necessary, assume $\|f\|_{\overline\Omega}\le1$.  Then, with
$v_j=P_\Omega\min\{-\log|f|,j\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SID-limit}
 v_j\nearrow\widetilde G_A\quad\text{on }\Omega\setminus A,
 \qquad
 \left(\sup_jv_j\right)^*=\widetilde G_A\quad\text{on }\Omega.
\end{equation}
If, in addition, $\Omega$ is B-regular, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PB-limit}
 PB_{\phi_j}\nearrow\widetilde G_A\quad\text{on }\Omega\setminus A,
 \qquad
 \left(\sup_jPB_{\phi_j}\right)^*=\widetilde G_A,
\end{equation}
and
\[
 G_{A,\Omega}\in C(\Omega,[-\infty,0]).
\]

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:KN-sectorial}
Put
\[
 P(z):=|z|^8+\frac{15}{7}|z|^2\operatorname{Re}z^6,
 \qquad
 \rho(z,w):=|w|^2+P(z)+|z|^{10}-1,
\]
and
\[
 \Omega_{\rm KN}:=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb C^2:\rho(z,w)<0\}.
\]
The bound $P(z)\ge-\frac87|z|^8$, together with the term $|z|^{10}$, shows that $\Omega_{\rm KN}$ is bounded.  Moreover,
\[
 \rho_{z\bar z}=16|z|^6+15\operatorname{Re}z^6+25|z|^8
 \ge |z|^6+25|z|^8,
 \qquad \rho_{w\bar w}=1,
\]
so $\Omega_{\rm KN}$ is pseudoconvex.  For the real flow of $X=(-z,-4w)$,
\[
 d\rho(X)=-8(\rho+1)-2|z|^{10},
\]
which is strictly negative on $\partial\Omega_{\rm KN}$.  Hence the boundary is smooth; it is real analytic by the defining equation.  Also
\[
 \rho(e^{-s}z,e^{-4s}w)
 =e^{-8s}\rho(z,w)-(1-e^{-8s})
 +(e^{-10s}-e^{-8s})|z|^{10},
 \qquad s>0.
\]
Thus the real flow stays in the domain and tends to the origin, so $\Omega_{\rm KN}$ is connected.  The Levi form is positive definite on the boundary whenever $z\ne0$; hence the weakly pseudoconvex boundary set is contained in
$\{(0,w):|w|=1\}$ and has vanishing two-dimensional Hausdorff measure.
Sibony's criterion therefore gives B-regularity \cite{Sibony87}.

Set
\[
 S_\tau(z,w):=(e^{-\tau}z,e^{-4\tau}w).
\]
A uniform Taylor estimate on a fixed boundary collar, together with compactness of its complement, gives constants $\theta,t_0,c,C>0$ such that, for $\tau\in\Gamma_{\theta,t_0}$,
\[
 S_\tau(\Omega_{\rm KN})\subset\Omega_{\rm KN},\qquad
 \operatorname{dist}(S_\tau(\Omega_{\rm KN}),\partial\Omega_{\rm KN})\ge c|\tau|,
 \qquad
 \|S_\tau-\mathrm{id}\|_{\overline\Omega_{\rm KN}}\le C|\tau|.
\]

At $p=(0,1)$, with $\eta=2(w-1)$, the boundary equation becomes
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\eta+P(z)+|z|^{10}+\frac14|\eta|^2=0.
\]
Its degree-eight tangential part is the classical Kohn--Nirenberg polynomial \cite{KN73}.  For completeness, the local supporting obstruction can be seen directly.  If a smooth holomorphic supporting hypersurface through $p$ existed, its tangent
space would have to be the complex tangent space of $\partial\Omega_{\rm KN}$
at $p$; otherwise the restriction of the defining function to the
hypersurface would change sign.  Hence, in the coordinates $(z,\eta)$, it
would be a graph $\eta=h(z)$ with $h(0)=0$ and $h'(0)=0$.  If the first nonzero term of $h$ has degree smaller than eight, its harmonic real part has a negative direction and dominates the defining equation.  If its degree is greater than eight, or if $h\equiv0$, a negative direction of $P$ already forces the graph into the domain.  In the remaining case
$h(z)=cz^8+O(z^9)$, take
\[
 \theta_k=\frac{\pi}{6}+\frac{k\pi}{3},\qquad 0\le k\le5.
\]
Then $P(re^{i\theta_k})=-\frac87r^8$, while
$\sum_{k=0}^5\operatorname{Re}(ce^{8i\theta_k})=0$; hence for at least one
$k$ the degree-eight contribution from $\operatorname{Re}h$ is nonpositive.
The terms $|z|^{10}$ and $|\eta|^2/4$ have higher order, so the graph again
enters $\Omega_{\rm KN}$, a contradiction.  Thus there is no smooth local
holomorphic supporting hypersurface at $p$.  Since local convexifiability
would pull a complex supporting hyperplane back to such a smooth hypersurface,
$\Omega_{\rm KN}$ is not locally convexifiable at $p$, and hence is not
convexifiable.

The SID theorem also applies to a divisor with a genuine singular boundary point.  For $\lambda>0$ let
\[
 f_\lambda(z,w):=(w-1)^2-\lambda z^2,
 \qquad A_\lambda:=(f_\lambda)|_{\Omega_{\rm KN}}.
\]
The extending zero set is reduced and has its only singular point at $(0,1)$.  It meets the domain, since for sufficiently small $t>0$,
\[
 \rho(t,1-\sqrt\lambda\,t)
 =-2\sqrt\lambda\,t+\lambda t^2+\frac{22}{7}t^8+t^{10}<0.
\]
Finally,
\[
 f_\lambda(S_\tau(0,1))=(e^{-4\tau}-1)^2,
\]
which has order two at $\tau=0$.  Thus $(\Omega_{\rm KN},A_\lambda)$ admits an SID, while $\Omega_{\rm KN}$ is not convexifiable.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:obstacle-disc}
Let $\Omega\Subset\mathbb C^n$ be a domain.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obstacle}
 \widetilde G_A=P_\Omega\phi
 =\sup\{u:u\in\mathrm{PSH}(\Omega),\ u\le-\log|f|\}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, for $z\in\Omega\setminus A$,
\begin{align}
 G_{A,\Omega}(z)
 &=\inf_{g\in\mathcal A(\Omega,z)}H_A(g),\label{eq:discG}\\
 \widetilde G_A(z)
 &=\inf_{g\in\mathcal A(\Omega,z)}
   \int_{\mathbb T}-\log|f(g(\zeta))|\,d\sigma(\zeta),\label{eq:discU}
\end{align}
where $d\sigma(e^{it})=dt/(2\pi)$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $G_{A,\Omega}\le0$, we have $\widetilde G_A\le-\log|f|=\phi$.  Conversely, if $u\in\mathrm{PSH}(\Omega)$ and $u\le\phi$, then
\[
 u+\log|f|\le0,
 \qquad
 \nu_{u+\log|f|}\ge\nu_{\log|f|}=\nu_A.
\]
Hence $u+\log|f|\le G_{A,\Omega}$ by \eqref{eq:defG}, and therefore $u\le\widetilde G_A$.  This proves \eqref{eq:obstacle}.

The preceding comparison is first read on $\Omega\setminus A$.  Both $u$ and the
plurisubharmonic extension of $\widetilde G_A$ satisfy
\[
 u(p)=\limsup_{\Omega\setminus A\ni z\to p}u(z),\qquad
 \widetilde G_A(p)=\limsup_{\Omega\setminus A\ni z\to p}\widetilde G_A(z),
 \qquad p\in A,
\]
by the submean property (equivalently, by removability across the analytic
hypersurface).  Hence $u\le\widetilde G_A$ extends across $A$, and so does
\eqref{eq:obstacle}.

Formula \eqref{eq:discG} is the cited theorem.  If $F=f\circ g$, Jensen's formula gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb T}-\log|F|\,d\sigma
 =-\log|F(0)|
 +\sum_{F(a)=0}\operatorname{ord}_a(F)\log|a|.
\]
Here $F(0)\ne0$ and $F$ extends past $\overline\mathbb D$.  Its boundary
zeros are finite and cause only integrable logarithmic
singularities.  Jensen's formula follows first when there are
no boundary zeros, and the general case follows by applying
it on circles of radii tending to $1$.  Notice the sign:
$\log|a|<0$ for every zero inside the disc.
Taking the infimum over $g\in\mathcal A(\Omega,z)$ and using \eqref{eq:discG} gives \eqref{eq:discU}.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:qub}
Following \cite[Definition 2.4]{DLH26}, with the terminology adapted from
Nilsson--Wikstr\"om \cite{NW21}, a function $u\in\mathrm{PSH}(\Omega)$ is called
quasi upper bounded if there are upper bounded functions
$u_j\in\mathrm{PSH}(\Omega)$ such that $u_j\nearrow u$ outside a pluripolar
subset of $\Omega$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:PB-trunc}
Assume in addition that $\Omega$ is B-regular.  Then, for every $t>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PB=canonical}
 PB_{\phi_t}=v_t=P_\Omega\phi_t
     =P_\Omega\min\{\widetilde G_A,t\}
 \quad\hbox{on }\Omega.
\end{equation}
In particular, $PB_{\phi_s}\le PB_{\phi_t}$ for $0<s<t$, and
\[
 PB_{\phi_t}|_{\partial\Omega}=\phi_t|_{\partial\Omega}
 \nearrow -\log|f|\big|_{\partial\Omega}
\]
in the extended-real sense.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By B-regularity, $PB_{\phi_t}$ is the unique continuous maximal
plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega$ with boundary value $\phi_t$.
Since
\[
 -\phi_t=\max\{\log|f|,-t\}\in\mathrm{PSH}(\Omega),
\]
the function $PB_{\phi_t}-\phi_t$ is plurisubharmonic and has boundary

$PB_{\phi_t}\le v_t=P_\Omega\phi_t$.  Conversely, if
$u\in\mathrm{PSH}(\Omega)$ and $u\le\phi_t$, then its boundary upper limit is
at most $\phi_t|_{\partial\Omega}$.  Hence $u$ is one of the competitors
in the boundary envelope \eqref{eq:PB-def}, and therefore

$v_t\le PB_{\phi_t}$.  Thus $PB_{\phi_t}=v_t$, and
\eqref{eq:canonical-trunc} gives the last equality in
\eqref{eq:PB=canonical}.  The remaining assertions follow from the
monotonicity of $\phi_t$.

\label{rem:PB-defect}
We record the possible regularization defect of the Perron--Bremermann limit.  Under the B-regularity assumption in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the
pointwise limit
\[
 \sup_{j\ge1}PB_{\phi_j}
\]
is lower semicontinuous because the functions $PB_{\phi_j}$ are
continuous and increasing.  Formula \eqref{eq:PB-limit} says that this
limit already equals $\widetilde G_A$ on $\Omega\setminus A$, while on
$A$ one may have to take the upper regularization:
\[
 \Bigl(\sup_{j\ge1}PB_{\phi_j}\Bigr)^*=\widetilde G_A.
\]
Thus any possible pointwise defect is confined to the pluripolar set
$A$.  At a point $p\in A\cap\partial\Omega$ one has
$\phi_j(p)=j$, so the boundary values tend to $+\infty$; this does not
assert that $\widetilde G_A(z)\to+\infty$ as $z\to p$.  At
$p\in\partial\Omega\setminus A$, the boundary data eventually stabilize
to $-\log|f(p)|$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:Dirichlet} gives the corresponding
boundary limit of $\widetilde G_A$.

\label{cor:Dirichlet}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem \ref{thm:convexifiable}, the function $\widetilde G_A$ is locally bounded and maximal on $\Omega$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MAzero}
 (dd^c\widetilde G_A)^n=0
\end{equation}
in the Bedford--Taylor sense.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundaryphi}
 \lim_{\Omega\ni z\to p}\widetilde G_A(z)=-\log|f(p)|,
 \qquad p\in\partial\Omega\setminus A.
\end{equation}
Among quasi upper bounded maximal plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega$ which are bounded from below and have the boundary values \eqref{eq:boundaryphi} outside an $\Omega$-pluripolar set, $\widetilde G_A$ is unique.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By Proposition \ref{prop:obstacle-disc}, $\widetilde G_A\ge0$, and it is locally bounded above near $A$ by \cite[Proposition 3.2]{LS99}.  On $\Omega\setminus A$, the function $G_{A,\Omega}$ is maximal by \cite[Theorem 3.3]{LS99}, while $\log|f|$ is pluriharmonic.  Hence $\widetilde G_A$ is maximal on $\Omega\setminus A$.  A locally bounded plurisubharmonic function which is maximal outside a pluripolar set is maximal everywhere; see \cite[Chapter 4]{Klimek}.  This gives \eqref{eq:MAzero}.

Let $p\in\partial\Omega\setminus A$.  Since $\widetilde G_A\le\phi$,
\[

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:geometry}
Let $D\Subset\mathbb C^n$, $n\ge2$, be a bounded convex domain and let
$\Phi$ be biholomorphic from a neighborhood of $\overline D$ onto a
neighborhood of $\overline\Omega$, where $\Omega=\Phi(D)$.  For every reduced divisor $A=(f)|_\Omega$, the pair $(\Omega,A)$ admits an SID.  Moreover, $D$ is B-regular if and only if $\Omega$ is B-regular.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Choose $z_*\in\Omega\setminus A$, put $w_*:=\Phi^{-1}(z_*)$, and write
\[
 d:=\operatorname{dist}(w_*,\partial D)>0,
 \qquad
 R:=\sup_{w\in D}|w-w_*|.
\]
In convex coordinates set
\[
 \mathcal S_\tau(w):=(1-\tau)w+\tau w_*.
\]
For real $0<t<1$, convexity gives
\[
 B(\mathcal S_t(w),td)\subset D,
 \qquad w\in D.
\]
Write $\tau=t+is$.  Then
\[
 |\mathcal S_\tau(w)-\mathcal S_t(w)|\le |s|R.
\]
Choose a fixed angle $0<\theta<\pi/2$ so small that
$R\tan\theta<d/2$.  If $|\arg\tau|<\theta$ and
$t=\operatorname{Re}\tau>0$, then
$|s|R<td/2$.  Hence
\[
 \mathcal S_\tau(w)\in
 B(\mathcal S_t(w),td)\subset D.
\]
Moreover, using the same interior ball and the $1$-Lipschitz property of the
distance function,
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(\mathcal S_\tau(w),\partial D)
 \ge td-|s|R\ge \frac d2t\ge c_0|\tau|
\]
with a constant $c_0>0$ depending only on $d$, $R$, and $\theta$.  Thus
$\mathcal S_\tau(D)\subset D$ throughout this sector (after restricting to
$|\tau|<t_0$ only to remain inside the fixed neighborhood on which the
transport by $\Phi$ is defined).  Also
\[
 \sup_{w\in D}|\mathcal S_\tau(w)-w|\le R|\tau|.
\]
Choose a neighborhood $U_D$ of $\overline D$ compactly contained in the domain of $\Phi$ and so small that, after decreasing $t_0$, $\mathcal S_\tau(U_D)$ remains in the domain of $\Phi$ for every $|\tau|<t_0$.  Transporting the family by $\Phi$ gives a holomorphic map on a full parameter disc,
\[
 S_\tau:=\Phi\circ\mathcal S_\tau\circ\Phi^{-1}
 \quad\text{on }U:=\Phi(U_D),\qquad |\tau|<t_0.
\]
Because $\Phi$ and $\Phi^{-1}$ extend holomorphically across the closures, the preceding inclusion, depth, and displacement estimates persist on $\Omega$ after changing the constants.

It remains to check finite-order escape at a singular boundary point $p\in A\cap\partial\Omega$.  Put $x=\Phi^{-1}(p)$ and $F=f\circ\Phi$.  If
\[
 \tau\longmapsto F((1-\tau)x+\tau w_*)
\]
vanished identically near $0$, the identity theorem, applied on a connected complex neighborhood of the real segment $[0,1]$ on which the expression is defined, would give $F(w_*)=0$.  This contradicts $z_*\notin A$.  Hence Definition~\ref{def:sectorial} is satisfied.

Finally, B-regularity is invariant under a biholomorphism extending across the closures.  Indeed, continuous boundary data pull back under $\Phi$, a continuous plurisubharmonic extension on one domain composes with the inverse biholomorphism to give such an extension on the other, and the converse follows by symmetry.

\label{prop:Reinhardt-SID}
Let $\Omega\Subset\mathbb C^n$ be a bounded Reinhardt domain. Assume that
$\Omega=\{\rho<0\}$ near $\partial\Omega$, where $\rho$ is a $C^1$
defining function satisfying
\[
 d\rho_z(-z)\le -c_0<0,\qquad z\in\partial\Omega.
\]
Assume also that $\Omega$ is hyperconvex and that $\partial\Omega$ contains
no nonconstant analytic disc. Let $f$ be holomorphic and define a reduced divisor on a neighborhood of $\overline\Omega$, put $A=(f)|_\Omega$, and assume $A\ne\varnothing$, and suppose that
\[
 \tau\longmapsto f(e^{-\tau}p)
 \quad\hbox{is not identically zero near }0
\]
for every $p\in\operatorname{Sing}(A)\cap\partial\Omega$. Then
$(\Omega,A)$ admits an SID via
\[
 S_\tau(z):=e^{-\tau}z,
\]
and
\[
 G_{A,\Omega}\in C(\Omega,[-\infty,0]).
\]
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
By \cite[Proposition~3.1]{DDH05}, the Reinhardt assumptions imply that
$\Omega$ is B-regular. By continuity of $d\rho_z(-z)$, after shrinking to a
fixed boundary collar one has
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\rho(e^{-t}z)\le -\frac{c_0}{2}
\]
for all sufficiently small $t>0$ as long as the orbit remains in that collar.
Hence the real contractions $S_t(z)=e^{-t}z$ map $\Omega$ into itself for
all sufficiently small $t>0$.  If $L$ bounds $|\nabla\rho|$ on the chosen
collar, the defining-function decrease and
$|\rho(q)|\le L\,\operatorname{dist}(q,\partial\Omega)$ show that points coming from the
collar have boundary depth at least $c_1t$ for some $c_1>0$; points outside
the collar remain a fixed positive distance from the boundary for small $t$.
Because $\Omega$ is Reinhardt, multiplication by $e^{-is}$ preserves
$\Omega$ and its Euclidean boundary distance. Thus for
$\tau=t+is$ in a sufficiently small fixed sector,
$S_\tau=e^{-is}S_t$ satisfies the self-map and depth estimates of
Definition~\ref{def:sectorial}. The displacement estimate follows from
$|e^{-\tau}-1|=O(|\tau|)$ on the bounded set $\overline\Omega$. The final
assumption is exactly the finite-order escape condition. The conclusion now
follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\label{lem:root-selection}
Let $\Delta\subset\mathbb D$ be a simply connected smoothly bounded source domain
whose boundary contains an open arc $J'\subset\mathbb T$, and assume
$0\notin\overline\Delta$.  Let $J\Subset J'$ be a closed arc of positive length, let
$\chi$ be holomorphic and nowhere zero on $\Delta$, continuous up to a
neighborhood of $J'$ in $\overline\Delta$, and assume $|\chi|=1$ on $J'$.
Fix $\beta\in\mathbb D^*$.  Given $\eta>0$, after choosing an arbitrarily thin
one-sided collar of $J$ in $\Delta$, for every sufficiently large integer
$N$ one can select solutions
\[
 z_{N,1},\ldots,z_{N,\ell_N}
\]
of $z^N\chi(z)=\beta$ in that collar such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-selection-count}
 \ell_N\le 2N|J|
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-selection-weight}
 \sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_N}\log|z_{N,\ell}|
 \le |J|\log|\beta|+\eta.
\end{equation}
The selected solutions may be taken in pairwise disjoint root cells of the
construction in the proof of \cite[Lemma~3.2]{DDF12}.

\label{lem:source-preimage}

source domain whose boundary contains an open arc $J'\supset J$, with
$0\notin\overline\Delta$.  Let $\chi$ be holomorphic and nowhere zero on

 a_N:C_N\longrightarrow B

\label{def:cartan-pair}
Let $S$ be a complex manifold.  A pair $(K_0,K_1)$ of compact subsets of $S$
is called a \emph{Cartan pair} if $K_0$, $K_1$, $K_0\cup K_1$, and
$K_0\cap K_1$ are Stein compacta and
\[
 \overline{K_0\setminus K_1}\cap
 \overline{K_1\setminus K_0}=\varnothing.
\]
Here a Stein compact is a compact set admitting a basis of open Stein
neighborhoods.  We also use the same terminology when one of the two
source sets has finitely many components: a pair $(D_0,D_1)$ of
relatively compact smoothly bounded open sets in the source plane is
called a \emph{smoothly bounded Cartan pair} when
$(\overline D_0,\overline D_1)$ is a Cartan pair in this sense.  In
the construction below, $D_0$ is connected and $D_1$ is a finite
disjoint union of source discs.  The source pair is chosen with the
additional separation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-separation}
 \overline{D_0\setminus D_1}\cap
 \overline{D_1\setminus D_0}=\varnothing;
\end{equation}
this is the separation imposed in
\cite[proof of Theorem~3.1]{DDF12}.

\label{lem:cartan-additive}
Let $D_0,D_1$ be a smoothly bounded Cartan pair in the
source plane satisfying \eqref{eq:source-separation},
$0\in D_0\setminus\overline{D_1}$, and
$D_0\cup D_1=\mathbb D$.  Suppose that
$h$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline\mathbb D$ and that
$g:D_1\to\mathbb C^n$ is holomorphic and continuous on $\overline{D_1}$.
There is a constant $C>0$ depending only on the fixed pair such that,
if
\[
 \|g-h\|_{\overline{D_0\cap D_1}}\le\epsilon,
\]
then there is $H\in\mathcal O(\mathbb D,\mathbb C^n)\cap
C(\overline\mathbb D,\mathbb C^n)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:additive-Cartan-estimate}
 H(0)=h(0),\qquad
 \|H-h\|_{\overline{D_0}}+
 \|H-g\|_{\overline{D_1}}\le C\epsilon.
\end{equation}
If both $h(\overline{D_0})$ and $g(\overline{D_1})$
lie in $\Omega$, the same construction gives $H(\overline\mathbb D)
\subset\Omega$ whenever $C\epsilon$ is smaller than their
distances from $\partial\Omega$.

Write $\delta=g-h$ on $D_0\cap D_1$.  By
\eqref{eq:source-separation}, there is a fixed smooth function
$\lambda$ on the source plane such that $0\le\lambda\le1$,
$\lambda=0$ near $\overline{D_0\setminus D_1}$, and
$\lambda=1$ near $\overline{D_1\setminus D_0}$.
Thus the derivatives of $\lambda$ within $D_0\cup D_1$ occur
only in the overlap, including when $D_1$ has several components.
On $D_0\cap D_1$ put
\[
 v=(\bar\partial\lambda)\delta,
\]
and extend $v$ by zero outside the overlap.  This is a bounded,
compactly supported $(0,1)$-form on the source plane, and
$\|v\|_\infty\le\|\bar\partial\lambda\|_\infty\epsilon$.
Its Cauchy--Green transform
\[
 u(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb C}
         \frac{v(\xi)}{z-\xi}\,dA(\xi)
\]
is continuous, satisfies $\bar\partial u=v$ in the distribution
sense, and obeys $\|u\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb C)}\le C_1\epsilon$.
The integral estimate uses only the fixed bounded support of $v$
and the local integrability of $|z-\xi|^{-1}$; in particular,
$C_1$ does not depend on derivatives of $\delta$.

On $D_0$ extend $\lambda\delta$ by zero across
$D_0\setminus D_1$ and put $a=\lambda\delta-u$.
On $D_1$ extend $(\lambda-1)\delta$ by zero across
$D_1\setminus D_0$ and put $b=(\lambda-1)\delta-u$.
The extended functions $\lambda\delta$ on $D_0$ and
$(\lambda-1)\delta$ on $D_1$ are continuous and have distributional
$\bar\partial$-derivative $v$: their cutoff factors are constant on
neighborhoods of the portions where the overlap ends inside the union.
Since $\bar\partial u=v$, it follows that
$\bar\partial a=\bar\partial b=0$ in the sense of distributions.  Weyl's
lemma therefore shows that $a$ and $b$ are holomorphic.  On the
overlap, $a-b=\delta=g-h$.  Consequently the two definitions
\[
 H=h+a-a(0)\quad\text{on }D_0,\qquad
 H=g+b-a(0)\quad\text{on }D_1
\]
agree and give a holomorphic map on $\mathbb D$.  It extends
continuously to $\overline\mathbb D$, and $H(0)=h(0)$.
The bound for $u$, together with $|\lambda|\le1$ and
$|a(0)|\le C_1\epsilon$, proves
\eqref{eq:additive-Cartan-estimate} with a constant depending
only on the pair and the chosen cutoff.  The final assertion
follows by comparing this bound with the stated positive
distances to the boundary of $\Omega$.

The source-side construction follows the proof of
\cite[Theorem~3.1, especially (3.9)--(3.11)]{DDF12}.  The additional point
is that the target intersections with the divisor must be recovered by a
Rouch\'e argument.  The order in which the parameters are chosen is kept
explicit.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 1: The target zeros are separated first, and then local Rouch\'e margins are fixed.}
Let $\beta_{k,s}$ be the distinct zeros of $Q_k$ in $\mathbb D$, with
multiplicities $\mu_{k,s}$.  Around every $\beta_{k,s}$ choose pairwise disjoint open discs
$B_{k,s}\Subset\mathbb D^*$ so small that $Q_k$ has no zero on
$\partial B_{k,s}$ and no zero in $B_{k,s}$ other than
$\beta_{k,s}$.  We also require the logarithmic smallness condition in
Lemma~\ref{lem:source-preimage}; importantly, this extra restriction depends
only on $\beta_{k,s}$ and is therefore fixed before the source cutoff
$\chi_k$ is chosen.  Finally, the discs are chosen so small that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-cell-budget}
 \sum_s \mu_{k,s}\omega(B_{k,s})<\frac{\tau}{32}
 \qquad (k=1,\ldots,m).
\end{equation}

$h$ of a thin source neighborhood of $I_k$ lies compactly in $V_k$.
By shrinking $I_k$ once more, uniformly for $z$ in that source
neighborhood and $\xi\in\partial B_{k,s}$,
\[

 <\frac12\min_{\partial B_{k,s}}|Q_k|.
\]

final arcs $J_k\Subset I_k$ and before the large integer $N$ are chosen.

These margins depend on the fixed target discs and old-zero

$J_k^0$, equals a negative constant $-M$ on
$\partial\Delta_k\setminus J_k'$, and is smoothly interpolated on the two

harmonic-measure estimate in \cite[proof of Theorem~3.1]{DDF12} makes

\noindent\emph{Step 4: Rouch\'e's theorem transfers the source roots to divisor intersections.}

form of Lemma~\ref{lem:source-preimage} applies simultaneously to all of
them.  By taking $N$ still larger, all these source discs lie in the thin
source neighborhood on which the Rouch\'e margin from Step~1 is valid.  Since $Q_k$ has the single zero

$Q_k\circ a_k$ has exactly $\mu_{k,s}$ zeros in
$C_{k,s,\ell}$, counted with multiplicity.  Rouch\'e's theorem therefore

\[

independently of the number and lengths of the later arcs.  After increasing $N$ once more, summing

\noindent\emph{Step 5: Additive gluing and radial regularization preserve the retained weights.}

on $D_0$ and the map $g_N=q_k(h,a_k)$ on each component $\Delta_k$

\[
 0<\Psi_j\le C_5+\log(1/\eta_j)=O(\log j)
 \qquad\text{on }D_j.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:sectorial-B},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sectorial-uninserted}
 \int_{D_j}\Psi_j\,d\sigma
 =O\!\left(\frac{\log j}{j}\right)=o(1).
\end{equation}

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 4: Arc insertion globalizes the local corrections.}
Choose a sequence $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$.  For each fixed $j$, first remove arbitrarily short arcs around the finitely many boundary zeros of $f\circ h_j$ so that the integral of $\Psi_j^+$ over the removed arcs is at most $\varepsilon_j$.  On the remaining part of $B_j\setminus D_j$, the function $\Psi_j$ is continuous.

For every point $\zeta$ in this remaining set, choose one of the
insertion families from Step~3 and a sufficiently small relatively open
neighborhood of $\zeta$ contained in $G_j$ on which this same family remains
admissible.  First fix all target zero contours for that family.  Apply the preparatory part of Lemma~\ref{lem:arc}, with the common
tolerance $\tau=\varepsilon_j$, to obtain a boundary neighborhood
$I_\zeta$ on which the target contours and their Rouch\'e margins remain
valid.  Closed arcs centered at $\zeta$, contained in
$I_\zeta\cap G_j$, and sufficiently short that the oscillation of $\Psi_j$
is at most $\varepsilon_j$, form a Vitali cover of the remaining set.  Thus
every selected arc is contained in $B_j\setminus D_j$.  The one-dimensional
Vitali theorem gives a countable pairwise disjoint subfamily covering the
remaining set up to a null set.  Since $\Psi_j^+\in L^1(\mathbb T)$, retain finitely many arcs so that the integral over the discarded tail is at most $\varepsilon_j$, and shrink them slightly to give positive mutual separation.  Absolute continuity of the integral allows the shrinking loss to be absorbed into $\varepsilon_j$.  Denote the resulting arcs by $J_{j,k}$ and their centers by $\zeta_{j,k}$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sectorial-vitali}
 \left|
 \sum_k |J_{j,k}|\Psi_j(\zeta_{j,k})
 -\int_{B_j\setminus D_j}\Psi_j\,d\sigma
 \right|=o(1).
\end{equation}

Let $W_{j,k}$ denote the logarithmic weight of the target roots in the local
family chosen at $\zeta_{j,k}$.  The Step~1 data in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:arc} have already been fixed above.  Execute Steps~2--5 of
that proof simultaneously for this finite family, taking its remaining
error tolerance equal to $\varepsilon_j$.  This gives
$H_j\in\mathcal A(\Omega,p_j)$ such that
\[
 H_A(H_j)
 \le H_A(h_j)+\sum_k|J_{j,k}|W_{j,k}+o(1).
\]
Thus neither the source Cartan pair nor the harmonic cutoff is chosen until
after all target contours and the final arcs have been fixed.
For the absolute insertions, $W_{j,k}\le-\Psi_j(\zeta_{j,k})$.  The singular relative insertions have the additional loss $C_0$, whose total contribution is at most $C_0\sigma(B_j)=o(1)$ by \eqref{eq:sectorial-B}.  The regular relative insertions have loss $O(\log j)$ on a set of length $O(1/j)$, and hence also contribute $o(1)$.  Combining these bounds with \eqref{eq:sectorial-vitali} and the uninserted estimate \eqref{eq:sectorial-uninserted} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sectorial-recovery}
 H_A(H_j)
 \le H_A(h_j)-\int_{B_j}\Psi_j\,d\sigma+o(1).
\end{equation}
The order of choices is important: at each fixed $j$, all target contours are fixed before the source Cartan pair and harmonic cutoffs in Lemma~\ref{lem:arc}, and the large root-distribution integer is chosen last.  Thus no Rouch\'e margin is required to be uniform in a parameter selected later.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 5: Jensen's formula is used at the moving center.}
Since $h_j(0)=p_j$ and $p_j\notin A$, Jensen's formula gives
\[
 \phi(p_j)+H_A(h_j)
 =\int_\mathbb T-\log|f(h_j)|\,d\sigma.
\]
On $\mathbb T\setminus B_j$ the integrand is at most
$\phi_j(g_j)+\eta_j$, while on $B_j$ one has
$-\log|f(h_j)|-\Psi_j=\phi_j(g_j)$.  Combining these identities with \eqref{eq:sectorial-recovery} and \eqref{eq:sectorial-nearmin} gives
\[
 \phi(p_j)+H_A(H_j)
 \le v_j(p)+o(1).
\]
The Riesz-envelope formula \eqref{eq:discG} therefore yields
\[
 \widetilde G_A(p_j)\le v_j(p)+o(1).
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:sectorial-center},
\[
 \widetilde G_A(p)\le v_j(p)+o(1).
\]
Since $v_j\le\widetilde G_A$, we obtain
$v_j(p)\to\widetilde G_A(p)$ for every $p\in\Omega\setminus A$; monotonicity gives the full increasing limit.

Put $w:=\sup_jv_j$.  Since $v_j\le\widetilde G_A$ and
$\widetilde G_A$ is locally bounded above, $w^*\in\mathrm{PSH}(\Omega)$ and
$w^*\le\widetilde G_A$.  The pointwise convergence just proved gives
$w=\widetilde G_A$ on $\Omega\setminus A$.  Both $w^*$ and
$\widetilde G_A$ are locally bounded above plurisubharmonic extensions of
the same function across the analytic hypersurface $A$; removability and
uniqueness of the extension therefore give
\[
 w^*=\widetilde G_A\quad\text{on }\Omega.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:SID-limit} without any B-regularity assumption.

Assume now that $\Omega$ is B-regular.  Proposition~\ref{prop:PB-trunc}
gives $v_j=PB_{\phi_j}$, hence \eqref{eq:PB-limit}.  The increasing limit of
the continuous functions $v_j$ is lower semicontinuous on
$\Omega\setminus A$, while $\widetilde G_A$ is plurisubharmonic and therefore
upper semicontinuous; thus $\widetilde G_A$ is continuous there.  Finally,
\cite[Proposition~3.2]{LS99} gives local boundedness above near $A$, and so
\[
 G_{A,\Omega}=\log|f|+\widetilde G_A\longrightarrow-\infty
 \qquad\text{as }z\to A.
\]
This proves extended-real continuity.
