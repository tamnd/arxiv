<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A persistence module indexed by $I$ is a covariant functor $ V : I \longrightarrow \textbf{Vect}_{\mathbb{K}}$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
The persistence diagram $dgm_p(\mathcal{F}_f)$ of a filtration $\mathcal{F}_f$ induced by a function $f$ is the multiset
		of points $(a, b)\in \overline{\mathbb{R}}^2$ (extended real plane), each taken with its corresponding multiplicity, representing the birth time $``a"$ and death time $``b"$ of a $p$-dimensional homology class in the associated persistence module $V$. By convention, every point on the diagonal $\Delta=\{(t,t) ~|~ t\in \overline{\mathbb{R}}\}$ is included with infinite multiplicity.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $X$ and $Y$ be two persistence diagram and $\Pi$ denote the set of all bijections $\pi:X\rightarrow Y$.  Consider the distance between the two points $x=(x_1,x_2)$ and $y=(y_1,y_2)$ in $L_\infty$-norm $||x-y||_\infty=\max\{|x_1-y_1|,|x_2-y_2|\}$ with the assumption that $\infty-\infty=0.$ The bottleneck distance between two diagrams $X$ and $Y$ is $d_B(X,Y)=\inf\limits_{\pi\in\Pi}\sup\limits_{x\in X}||x-\pi(x)||_\infty$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{S}
		{\normalfont
			Let $K$ be a simplicial Complex and $f,g:K\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ two monotonic functions. For each dimension $p$, the bottleneck distance between the persistence diagram $X=Dgm_p(\mathcal{F}_f)$ and $Y=Dgm_p(\mathcal{F}_g)$ is bounded from above by the $L_{\infty}$-distance between the functions, $d_B(X,Y)\leq ||f-g||_{\infty}$.}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Let $X$ be a finite $T_0$ space. The order complex $\mathcal{S}(X)$ of $X$ is the simplicial complex whose simplices are the nonempty chains of $X$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
Let $X$ be a finite space and $x,y\in X$. The furtherness $\Psi(x,y)$ of $x$ from $y$ is the smallest whole number $k$ such that there exist a nested sequence $(U_j)_{j\geq0}$ of open sets around $x$ such that $y\in U_k$.\par
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{2.10}
		{\normalfont Let $X$ be a finite $T_0$ space. Then, $\Psi(x,y)=|U_y\backslash U_x|, \forall x,y\in X.$}
<!-- end remark-1 -->

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{A}
		Let $X$ be a finite $T_0$ space and $x\in X$. Then
		\begin{itemize}
			\item[$(i)$] $[0,\infty)\in dgm(V_x)$ with multiplicity one.
			\item[$(ii)$] If $X$ has $m$ minimal points which are not comparable with $x$, then $m$ new classes appear at level 1.
			\item[$(iii)$] No new class appear after level 1.
		\end{itemize}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $a$ be a minimal point of $X$. Then, either $a\leq x$ or $x$ is not comparable with $a$. Thus, by Remark \ref{2.10}, we get either $\Psi(x,a)=0$ or $\Psi(x,a)=1$.\par 
		First, suppose $\Psi(x,a)=0$. At level $0$, we know that  $\mathcal{S}(U_x)$ appears. A class corresponding to the connected component of $x$ appears in $dgm(V_x)$. As $U_x$ is connected, $\mathcal{S}(U_x)$ is connected. So, just one class $[0,-)$ appears at level 0 in $dgm(V_x)$. By elder rule, if any class merge with this class at any level, this class $[0,-)$ will go on, and merged class will end at that level. Therefore, this class will have infinite persistence. Hence $[0,\infty) \in dgm(V_x)$ and has multipicity 1.\par 
		
		If $\Psi(x,a)=1$, then $a$ is  not comparable with $x$. In this case, a new connected component will appear at level 1 corresponding to $a$. Note that $X_x^0= U_x$ is the collection all those points such that furtherness of $x$ from those points is 0. If $t\in U_x$ is comparable with $a$, then $\Psi(t,a)=0 \implies \Psi(x,a)\leq \Psi(a,t)+\Psi(t,a)=0 \implies x$ is comaparable with $a$, a contradiction. So, $X_x^0= U_x$ does not contain any point which is comparable with $a$. Thus, there is no vertex in $\mathcal{S}(X_x^0)$ that share an edge with $a$ in $\mathcal{S}(X)$. So, at level 1, when $a$ appears, it does not share any edge with the vertices of level 0. Therefore, it forms a new connected component at level 1. A new class $[1,n_a)$ appeares in $dgm(V_x)$ corresponding to $a$, where $n_a\in \mathbb{N}\cup \{\infty\}$ and $n_a>1$. Hence, if there are $m$ minmal points in $X$ not comparable with $x$,  then $m$ new classes appear at level 1.\par 
		After level 1, if any point $y$ appears, then $y$ is not a minimal point and it is comparable to at least one of the minimal points appeared in level 0 or 1. So, if $y$ appears in $X_x^t, t>1$, then $y$ appears with an edge joining $y$ with an earlier appeared vertex (corresponding to a minimal point) in $S(X_x^t)$. Thus, no new connected component created corresponding to $y$, and hence, no new class starts from $t$ in $dgm(V_x)$, for $t>1$.

\label{D}
		Let $X$ be a finite $T_0$ space and $x\in X$. Let $b$ be a minimal point of $X$ not comparable with $x$. If a class born at level 1 in $dgm(V_x)$ corresponding to $b$, dies at level $n_b<\infty,$ then there is an element in $X_x^{n_b}$ which is comparable with both $b$ and another minimal point of $X$. Moreover, if no such element exists then $n_b=\infty$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $\{a_1,a_2,...,a_k\}$ be the collection of minimal points of $X$, which are not comparable to $x$. By Theorem \ref{A}, for each $a_i, 1\leq i\leq k$, we have a class $[1,n_{a_i})$ in $dgm(V_x)$, where $n_{a_i}\in \mathbb{N}\cup \{\infty\}$. Note that, we have a component in $S(X_x^1)$ corresponding to each $a_i$  and  one component containing $x$, $1\leq i\leq k$. So,  we have $k+1$ components at level 1.\par 
		
		Next, we observe that $n_{a_j}= \min \{ t ~|~ y\in X_x^t \text{ such that } y \text{ is comparable with both }$ $a_i \text{ and } a_j,\text{ for	some } 0\leq i< j\leq k\}, 1\leq j\leq k$, where $a_0$ is a minimal point comparable with $x$.\par 
		
		Notice that if a point $y$ enters  $X_x^2$ which is comparable with both $a_i \text{ and } a_j,\text{ for }\\
		 \text{ some } 0\leq i < j\leq k,$ then there will be an edge between $y$ and $a_i$ along with an edge between $y$ and $a_j$ in $\mathcal{S}(X_x^2)$. So, two components of $\mathcal{S}(X_x^1)$ merge into one component of $\mathcal{S}(X_x^2)$ due to the entrance of $y$. If both $i$ and $j$ are non-zero then one class  corresponding to $a_i$ or $a_j$ ends and one persists. So, in this case, WLOG, we may take  $[1,n_{a_j})=[1,2)\in dgm(V_x)$.
		Even if  $i=0$, then by elder rule the class corresponding to $a_0$ will persist and other will end. So, $[1,n_{a_j})=[1,2)\in dgm(V_x)$.
		If no such $y$ enters $X_x^2$, then no class ends at level 2. In this case $[1,2)\notin dgm(V_x)$.\par 
		
		We repeat this process untill a point $y$ appears in $X_x^t$, for some $t\geq 2$, where $y$ is comparable with both $a_i$ and $a_j$, for some $0\leq j \leq k$. So, we can say that the class corresponding to minimal point $a_j, 1\leq j\leq k,$ ends at $n_{a_{j}}$, where $n_{a_{j}}$ is the earliest level $t$ such that $y$  enters $X_x^t$, and $y$ is comparable with both $a_i$ and $a_j$, for some $0\leq i \leq k$.  If no such $y$ enters $X_x^t$, $\forall t\geq 1$, then $n_{a_j}=\infty.$

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{M'}
    Let $X$ be a finite $T_0$ space and $\sim$ be a relation as defined in Remark \ref{M}. Then for $x,y\in X$, we have $x\sim y$ if and only if $dgm(V_x)=dgm(V_y)$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
First, assume that $x\sim y$. By Remark \ref{F}, $|dgm(V_x)|=|dgm(V_y)|$. We have  $\{\Psi(x,t)~|~  t\in \zeta_X^x\} = \{\Psi(y,t)~|~  t\in \zeta_X^y\}$ as multisets. If $[1,n_a^x)\in dgm(V_x)$ such that $n_a^x<\infty$, where $a$ is a minimal point not comparable with $x$, then $n_a^x= \Psi(x,t)$, for some $t\in \zeta_X^x$.  Therefore, $n_a^x\in \{\Psi(x,t)~|~  t\in \zeta_X^x\} = \{\Psi(y,t)~|~  t\in \zeta_X^y\}$. Hence $n_a^x= \Psi(y,t') =n_b^y$, for some $t'\in  \zeta_X^y$ and some  minimal point $b$ not comparable with $y$. Consequently, $[1,n_a^x)= [1,n_b^y)\in dgm(V_y)$. Thus, every class of finite persistence in $dgm(V_x)$ is also present in $dgm(V_y)$. Note that the classes of infinite persistence in a persistence diagram are corresponding to the connected components of $\mathcal{S}(X)$. So, $dgm(V_x)\subseteq dgm(V_y)$. Similarly, we get $dgm(V_y)\subseteq dgm(V_x)$. Hence, $dgm(V_x) =  dgm(V_y)$.\par
      Now, let $dgm(V_x)=dgm(V_y)$. Again, by Remark \ref{F}, $x$ and $y$ have the same number of minmial points in $X$ that are  not comparable with them. Consider $t\in \zeta_X^x$, then $\Psi(x,t)= n_a^x$, for some minimal point $a$ not comparable with $x$. Thus, $[1, n_a^x)\in dgm(V_x) =dgm (V_y) \iff [1, n_a^x)=[1, n_b^y),$ for some minimal point $b$ not  comparable with $y$. Therefore, $\Psi(x,t)= n_a^x= n_a^y =\Psi(y,t')$, for some $t'\in \zeta_X^y$. Hence, The multisets $\{\Psi(x,t)~|~  t\in \zeta_X^x\}$ and  $\{\Psi(y,t)~|~  t\in \zeta_X^y\}$ are equal. Therefore, $x\sim y$.

As $x\sim z$, we get $|dgm(V_x)|=|dgm(V_z)|$, and  as multisets $\{\Psi(x,t)~|~ t\in \zeta_X^{x}\} = \{\Psi(z,t)~|~ t\in\zeta_X^z \}$. As $x\leq y\leq z$, by Theorem \ref{K}, $|dgm(V_z)|\leq |dgm(V_y)|\leq |dgm(V_x)|$. Therefore, $|dgm(V_z)| = |dgm(V_y)|= |dgm(V_x)|$.\par 
	Let $\mathcal{A}_x, \mathcal{A}_y$ and $\mathcal{A}_z$ be the collections of minimal points of $X$ not comparable with $x,y$ and $z$, respectively. So, $|\mathcal{A}_x|=|\mathcal{A}_y|=|\mathcal{A}_z|$. Clearly,  $\mathcal{A}_z\subseteq \mathcal{A}_y \subseteq \mathcal{A}_x$. This gives that $\mathcal{A}_z= \mathcal{A}_y =\mathcal{A}_x$. We know that $\zeta_X^x$ is the collection of those cone points of $X$, which are responsible to end a class in $dgm(V_x)$. For some $a\in \mathcal{A}_x$, if $n_a^x<\infty$, then $n_a^x\in \{\Psi(x,t)~|~ t\in \zeta_X^x\}$ and $[1,n_a^x)\in dgm(V_x)$. Therefore, $\{n_a^x~|~ a\in \mathcal{A}_x\}= \{\Psi(x,t)~|~ t\in \zeta_X^x\} ~\cup~ \{\infty$ with multiplicity $k-1\}$, where $k$ is the number of connected components of $\mathcal{S}(X)$. As $x\sim z$, $\{\Psi(x,t)~|~ t\in \zeta_X^x\}=\{\Psi(z,t)~|~ t\in \zeta_X^z\} \implies \{n_a^x~|~ a\in \mathcal{A}_x\}=\{n_a^z~|~ a\in \mathcal{A}_z\}$. By Theorem \ref{E},  for every $a\in \mathcal{A}_z$, we have $n_a^x\leq n_a^y\leq n_a^z$.  Consequently, $\{n_a^x~|~ a\in \mathcal{A}_x\}=\{n_a^y~|~ a\in \mathcal{A}_y\}=\{n_a^z~|~ a\in \mathcal{A}_z\}$. Hence, $\{\Psi(x,t)~|~ t\in \zeta_X^x\}=\{\Psi(y,t)~|~ t\in \zeta_X^y\}=\{\Psi(z,t)~|~ t\in \zeta_X^z\}$. Thus, $x\sim y\sim z$.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{M''}{\normalfont
		Let $X_0$ be the quotient space of a finite space $X$ and $x,y\in X$. We define a relation on $X$ by setting $x\sim' y$ if and only if $[x]$ and  $[y]$ are related by the relation defined in Remark \ref{M}. By Theorem \ref{M'}, it is equivalent to say that $x\sim' y \iff dgm(V_{[x]})=dgm(V_{[y]})$.\par 
		Notice that the quotient map $p:X\rightarrow X/$$\sim'$ factors as $p=\pi\circ q$, where $q: X\rightarrow X_0$ is the quotient map, and $\pi: X_0\rightarrow X_0/$$\sim$ is the quotient map induced by the equivalence relation $\sim$ defined in Remark \ref{M}. Thus, the quotient space $X/$$\sim'$ can be obtained by first passing to the $T_0$-quotient $X_0= X/$$\sim_0$, and then quotienting by $\sim$. Therefore, $X/$$\sim'$ and $X_0/$$\sim$ are topologically identical, as both quotient spaces have the same cardinality and the same topology. Consequently, $X/$$\sim'$ $= X_0/$$\sim$. Thus, the quotient space $X/$$\sim'$  is completely determined by the homotopically equivalent $T_0$ quotient space $X_0$. Furthermore, if $X$ is a $T_0$ space, then the equivalence relation $\sim'$ coincides with $\sim$.}
