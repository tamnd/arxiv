\label{thm:L4-cont}

\[

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:energy}
Let $u$ be a strong solution of \eqref{eq:LLG-intro}. Then for every $0\le t_0<t_1<T_{\ast}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy-id}
E\bigl(u(t_1)\bigr)+a\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\int_{\mathbb R^2}|\tau(u)(t,x)|^2\,dxdt
=E\bigl(u(t_0)\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular, $E(u(t))$ is nonincreasing.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By the strong-solution regularity $u-Q\in C([0,T_{\ast});H^2)$ and $\partial_tu\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,T_{\ast});H^1)$, the pairings, integrations by parts, and chain rule below are justified. By integration by parts,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}E(u(t))
=
\int_{\mathbb R^2}\nabla u:\nabla u_t\,dx
=-\int_{\mathbb R^2}\Delta u\cdot u_t\,dx.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:tension} and $u_t\cdot u=0$, we get
\[
-\Delta u\cdot u_t
=-(\tau(u)-|\nabla u|^2u)\cdot u_t
=-\tau(u)\cdot u_t.
\]
Substituting \eqref{eq:LLG-tau}, and observing that
\[
\tau(u)\cdot (u\times \tau(u))=0,
\]
we obtain
\[
\frac{d}{dt}E(u(t))=-a\int_{\mathbb R^2}|\tau(u)|^2\,dx.
\]
Integrating in time gives \eqref{eq:energy-id}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:L4-by-Lap}
For every $u:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb S^2$, one has the pointwise identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uDelta}
|\nabla u|^2 = -u\cdot \Delta u,
\end{equation}
and consequently
\begin{equation*}
|\nabla u|^4 \le |\Delta u|^2
\qquad\text{a.e. on }\mathbb R^2.
\end{equation*}
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L4-by-Delta-int}
\left\lVert \nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^4\le \left\lVert \Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The first formula is exactly the second identity in \eqref{eq:constraint1}. By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
|\nabla u|^4=|u\cdot \Delta u|^2\le |u|^2|\Delta u|^2=|\Delta u|^2.
\]
Integrating yields \eqref{eq:L4-by-Delta-int}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:L2-relative}
Fix $Q\in\mathbb S^2$. For every strong solution $u$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L2-growth}
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert u(t)-Q\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2
\le 2aE\bigl(u(t)\bigr)
\le 2aE\bigl(u_0\bigr).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every $t\in[0,T_{\ast})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L2-growth-int}
\left\lVert u(t)-Q\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2
\le
\left\lVert u_0-Q\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2+4aE(u_0)t.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By the strong-solution regularity $u-Q\in C([0,T_{\ast});H^2)$ and $\partial_tu\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,T_{\ast});H^1)$, the function $t\mapsto \|u(t)-Q\|_{L^2_x}^2$ is absolutely continuous, and the pairings and integrations by parts below are justified. Set $v:=u-Q$. Then
\[
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2
=
\int_{\mathbb R^2}v\cdot u_t\,dx
=:J_1+J_2+J_3,
\]
where
\[
J_1=a\int v\cdot \Delta u,
\qquad
J_2=a\int |\nabla u|^2 v\cdot u,
\qquad
J_3=b\int v\cdot (u\times \Delta u).
\]
For the first term, integration by parts gives
\[
J_1=-a\int |\nabla u|^2\,dx=-2aE(u(t)).
\]
For the second term, using $|u|=|Q|=1$, we have $v\cdot u =1-Q\cdot u\le 2$, whence
\[
J_2\le 2a\int |\nabla u|^2\,dx=4aE(u(t)).
\]
The last term is eliminated by the divergence form:
\[
J_3
=-b\int Q\cdot (u\times \Delta u)
=-b\int Q\cdot \partial_j(u\times \partial_j u)
=0.
\]
Combining these estimates yields
\[
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert u-Q\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2
\le 2aE(u(t)).
\]
Energy monotonicity then gives \eqref{eq:L2-growth} and \eqref{eq:L2-growth-int}.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:lady}
For every $f\in H^1(\mathbb R^2)$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lady}
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^2\le C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^2_x}\left\lVert \nabla f\right\rVert_{L^2_x}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $g:=|f|^2$. The two-dimensional Sobolev embedding $W^{1,1}(\mathbb R^2)\hookrightarrow L^2(\mathbb R^2)$ gives
\[
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^2=\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^2_x}\le C\left\lVert \nabla g\right\rVert_{L^1_x}.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\nabla g=\nabla(|f|^2)=2 f\cdot \nabla f,
\]
and hence, by H\"older's inequality,
\[
\left\lVert \nabla g\right\rVert_{L^1_x}\le 2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^2_x}\left\lVert \nabla f\right\rVert_{L^2_x}.
\]
Combining the two inequalities gives
\[
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^2\le C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^2_x}\left\lVert \nabla f\right\rVert_{L^2_x}.
\]
This is \eqref{eq:lady}.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:derived-GN}
Assume $u-Q\in H^3(\mathbb R^2)$. Then there exists an absolute constant $C>0$ such that
\begin{align}
\left\lVert \nabla^2 u\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^2
&\le C\left\lVert \Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x}\left\lVert \nabla\Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x},
\label{eq:nabla2-L4}
\\
\left\lVert \Delta u\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^2
&\le C\left\lVert \Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x}\left\lVert \nabla\Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x},
\label{eq:Delta-L4}
\\
\left\lVert \nabla u\right\rVert_{L^6_x}^3
&\le C\left\lVert \nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^2\left\lVert \Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x}.
\label{eq:nabla-L6}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For \eqref{eq:nabla2-L4}, apply Lemma \ref{lem:lady} to $f=\nabla^2u$ and obtain
\[
\left\lVert \nabla^2 u\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^2
\le C\left\lVert \nabla^2 u\right\rVert_{L^2_x}\left\lVert \nabla^3 u\right\rVert_{L^2_x}.
\]
On the whole space, Plancherel's identity gives
\[
\left\lVert \nabla^2 u\right\rVert_{L^2_x}=\left\lVert \Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x},
\qquad
\left\lVert \nabla^3 u\right\rVert_{L^2_x}=\left\lVert \nabla\Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x},
\]
and hence \eqref{eq:nabla2-L4} follows.

For \eqref{eq:Delta-L4}, apply Lemma \ref{lem:lady} directly to $f=\Delta u$:
\[
\left\lVert \Delta u\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^2
\le C\left\lVert \Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x}\left\lVert \nabla\Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x}.
\]
This is \eqref{eq:Delta-L4}.

For \eqref{eq:nabla-L6}, first use the two-dimensional Gagliardo--Nirenberg interpolation inequality

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^6_x}\le C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^{2/3}\left\lVert \nabla f\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^{1/3},

\]

\[

\le C\left\lVert \nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^2\left\lVert \Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:nabla-L6}.

\label{lem:pointwise-expansion}
Let $u$ be sufficiently smooth. For every $j,k\in\{1,2\}$, one has
\begin{align}
\partial_j\bigl(u\times \partial_j u\bigr)
&=
u\times \partial_{jj}u,
\label{eq:cross-div-exact}
\\
\partial_k\partial_j\bigl(u\times \partial_j u\bigr)
&=
(\partial_k u)\times \partial_{jj}u
+
u\times \partial_k\partial_{jj}u,
\label{eq:cross-diff-exact}
\\
\partial_k\bigl(|\nabla u|^2u\bigr)
&=
2\sum_{m=1}^2
\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u\bigr)u
+
|\nabla u|^2\partial_k u.
\label{eq:cubic-diff-exact}
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{align}
\left\lvert \partial_k\partial_j(u\times \partial_j u)-u\times \partial_k\partial_{jj}u\right\rvert
&\le C\left\lvert \nabla u\right\rvert\left\lvert \nabla^2u\right\rvert,
\label{eq:point-cross}
\\
\left\lvert \nabla\bigl(|\nabla u|^2u\bigr)\right\rvert
&\le C\left\lvert \nabla u\right\rvert\left\lvert \nabla^2u\right\rvert+C\left\lvert \nabla u\right\rvert^3.
\label{eq:point-cubic}
\end{align}

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The first formula follows directly from the product rule and antisymmetry:
\[
\partial_j(u\times \partial_j u)
=
(\partial_j u)\times(\partial_j u)+u\times \partial_{jj}u
=
u\times \partial_{jj}u.
\]
Applying $\partial_k$ to this formula gives
\[
\partial_k\partial_j(u\times \partial_j u)
=
(\partial_k u)\times \partial_{jj}u+u\times \partial_k\partial_{jj}u,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:cross-diff-exact}.

For \eqref{eq:cubic-diff-exact}, write first
\[
|\nabla u|^2=\sum_{m=1}^2 \partial_m u\cdot \partial_m u.
\]
Then
\[
\partial_k(|\nabla u|^2)
=
2\sum_{m=1}^2 \partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u,
\]
and the product rule gives
\[
\partial_k\bigl(|\nabla u|^2u\bigr)
=
\partial_k(|\nabla u|^2)\,u+|\nabla u|^2\partial_k u
=
2\sum_{m=1}^2
\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u\bigr)u
+
|\nabla u|^2\partial_k u.
\]

Finally, \eqref{eq:cross-diff-exact} immediately implies
\[
\left\lvert \partial_k\partial_j(u\times \partial_j u)-u\times \partial_k\partial_{jj}u\right\rvert
=
\left\lvert (\partial_k u)\times \partial_{jj}u\right\rvert
\le C\left\lvert \nabla u\right\rvert\left\lvert \nabla^2u\right\rvert,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:point-cross}. Meanwhile \eqref{eq:cubic-diff-exact} gives
\[
\left\lvert \partial_k\bigl(|\nabla u|^2u\bigr)\right\rvert
\le
2\sum_{m=1}^2
\left\lvert \partial_m u\right\rvert\,\left\lvert \partial_{km}u\right\rvert\,\left\lvert u\right\rvert
+
|\nabla u|^2\left\lvert \partial_k u\right\rvert.
\]
Since $|u|=1$, summing over $k=1,2$ gives \eqref{eq:point-cubic}.

\label{lem:lap-expansion}
Let $u$ be sufficiently smooth. Then
\begin{align}
\sum_{j=1}^2\Delta\partial_j\bigl(u\times \partial_j u\bigr)
&=
u\times \Delta^2 u
+
2\sum_{j,k=1}^2
(\partial_k u)\times \partial_{kjj}u,
\label{eq:cross-laplacian-exact}
\\
\Delta\bigl(|\nabla u|^2u\bigr)
&=
|\nabla u|^2\Delta u
+
4\sum_{k,m=1}^2
\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u\bigr)\partial_k u
\notag\\
&\quad
+
2\sum_{m=1}^2
\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_m\Delta u\bigr)u
+
2\sum_{k,m=1}^2
|\partial_{km}u|^2 u.
\label{eq:cubic-laplacian-exact}
\end{align}

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We first prove \eqref{eq:cross-laplacian-exact}. By \eqref{eq:cross-div-exact},
\[
\partial_j\bigl(u\times \partial_j u\bigr)=u\times \partial_{jj}u.
\]
Apply the Laplacian to this identity and, for each $k\in\{1,2\}$, use the second-order product rule
\[
\partial_{kk}(v\times w)
=
(\partial_{kk}v)\times w
+
2(\partial_k v)\times (\partial_k w)
+
v\times \partial_{kk}w,
\]
with $v=u$ and $w=\partial_{jj}u$. We get
\[
\Delta\partial_j(u\times \partial_j u)
=
\sum_{k=1}^2
\Bigl(
(\partial_{kk}u)\times \partial_{jj}u
+
2(\partial_k u)\times \partial_{kjj}u
+
u\times \partial_{kkjj}u
\Bigr).
\]
Now sum over $j=1,2$. The first class of terms satisfies
\[
\sum_{j,k=1}^2(\partial_{kk}u)\times \partial_{jj}u
=
\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^2\partial_{kk}u\Bigr)\times
\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^2\partial_{jj}u\Bigr)
=
\Delta u\times \Delta u
=
0,
\]
whereas the third class becomes
\[
\sum_{j,k=1}^2 u\times \partial_{kkjj}u
=
u\times \Delta^2 u.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:cross-laplacian-exact} follows.

We next prove \eqref{eq:cubic-laplacian-exact}. By \eqref{eq:cubic-diff-exact},
\[
\partial_k\bigl(|\nabla u|^2u\bigr)
=
2\sum_{m=1}^2
\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u\bigr)u
+
|\nabla u|^2\partial_k u.
\]
Differentiating once more in $k$ and summing, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\Delta\bigl(|\nabla u|^2u\bigr)
&=
2\sum_{k,m=1}^2
\partial_k\Bigl[\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u\bigr)u\Bigr]
+
\sum_{k=1}^2\partial_k\bigl(|\nabla u|^2\partial_k u\bigr)
\\
&=
2\sum_{k,m=1}^2
\partial_k\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u\bigr)u
+
2\sum_{k,m=1}^2
\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u\bigr)\partial_k u
\\
&\quad
+
\sum_{k=1}^2\partial_k(|\nabla u|^2)\partial_k u
+
|\nabla u|^2\Delta u.
\end{align*}
Now expand the derivatives in the first two terms:
\[
\partial_k\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u\bigr)
=
|\partial_{km}u|^2+\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{kkm}u,
\]
and
\[
\partial_k(|\nabla u|^2)
=
2\sum_{m=1}^2\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u.
\]
Substitution and collection of terms give
\begin{align*}
\Delta\bigl(|\nabla u|^2u\bigr)
&=
2\sum_{k,m=1}^2|\partial_{km}u|^2u
+
2\sum_{m=1}^2
\Bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \sum_{k=1}^2\partial_{kkm}u\Bigr)u
\\
&\quad
+
4\sum_{k,m=1}^2
\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u\bigr)\partial_k u
+
|\nabla u|^2\Delta u
\\
&=
2\sum_{k,m=1}^2|\partial_{km}u|^2u
+
2\sum_{m=1}^2
\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_m\Delta u\bigr)u
\\
&\quad
+
4\sum_{k,m=1}^2
\bigl(\partial_m u\cdot \partial_{km}u\bigr)\partial_k u
+
|\nabla u|^2\Delta u,
\end{align*}
which is \eqref{eq:cubic-laplacian-exact}.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:low-freq}
Let $u$ be a strong solution of \eqref{eq:LLG-intro}, let $I=(T_0,T_1)\subset[0,T_{\ast})$, and let $k\in\mathbb Z$. Then
\begin{align}
\left\lVert P_{<k}\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb R^2)}
&\le C|I|^{1/4}2^{k/2}E(u_0)^{1/2},
\label{eq:low-L4-scalar}
\\
\Biggl(\sum_{j<k}\left\lVert P_j\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb R^2)}^2\Biggr)^{1/2}
&\le C|I|^{1/4}2^{k/2}E(u_0)^{1/2}.
\label{eq:low-L4-ell2}
\end{align}
In particular, if $I=\mathcal I_{k_0}(T_{\ast};c_0)$ is a self-similar time window, then
\begin{align}
\left\lVert P_{<k_0+M}\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_{t,x}(\mathcal I_{k_0}(T_{\ast};c_0)\times\mathbb R^2)}
&\le C c_0^{1/4}2^{M/2}E(u_0)^{1/2},
\label{eq:low-self-scalar}
\\
\Biggl(\sum_{j<k_0+M}\left\lVert P_j\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_{t,x}(\mathcal I_{k_0}(T_{\ast};c_0)\times\mathbb R^2)}^2\Biggr)^{1/2}
&\le C c_0^{1/4}2^{M/2}E(u_0)^{1/2}.
\label{eq:low-self-ell2}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We first prove \eqref{eq:low-L4-scalar}. By H\"older's inequality in the time variable,
\[
\left\lVert P_{<k}\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb R^2)}
\le |I|^{1/4}\sup_{t\in I}\left\lVert P_{<k}\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^4_x}.
\]
The low-frequency Bernstein inequality gives
\[
\left\lVert P_{<k}\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^4_x}
\le C2^{k/2}\left\lVert P_{<k}\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}
\le C2^{k/2}\left\lVert \nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}.
\]
Combining this with energy monotonicity,
\[
\sup_{t\in I}\left\lVert \nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}
\le (2E(u_0))^{1/2},
\]
yields \eqref{eq:low-L4-scalar}.

We next prove \eqref{eq:low-L4-ell2}. For each $j<k$, the same H\"older and Bernstein inequalities give
\[
\left\lVert P_j\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb R^2)}^2
\le C|I|^{1/2}2^j\sup_{t\in I}\left\lVert P_j\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2.
\]
Therefore
\begin{align*}
\sum_{j<k}\left\lVert P_j\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb R^2)}^2
&\le C|I|^{1/2}\sum_{j<k}2^j\sup_{t\in I}\left\lVert P_j\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2
\\
&\le C|I|^{1/2}2^k\sup_{t\in I}\sum_{j<k}\left\lVert P_j\nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2
\\
&\le C|I|^{1/2}2^k E(u_0).
\end{align*}
Taking the square root gives \eqref{eq:low-L4-ell2}.

If $I=\mathcal I_{k_0}(T_{\ast};c_0)$ and $k$ is replaced by $k_0+M$, then
\[
|I|^{1/4}2^{(k_0+M)/2}
\le c_0^{1/4}2^{-k_0/2}2^{k_0/2}2^{M/2}=c_0^{1/4}2^{M/2}.
\]
Substituting this into the preceding estimates yields \eqref{eq:low-self-scalar} and \eqref{eq:low-self-ell2}.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Lemma~\ref{lem:low-freq} says that, on a self-similar time window, the $L^4$ size of the low-frequency part $P_{<k+M}\nabla u$ is completely determined by the energy and the geometric parameters $c_0,M$, independently of the terminal complexity of the solution. The frequency decomposition used here is deliberately elementary: only Bernstein inequalities and the energy bound enter.

\label{prop:H2-main}
Let $u$ be a smooth solution of \eqref{eq:LLG-intro}, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:YZ-def}
Y(t):=\left\lVert \Delta u(t)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2,
\qquad
Z(t):=\left\lVert \nabla\Delta u(t)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2.
\end{equation}
Then there exists a constant $C_{a,b}>0$, depending only on $a,b$, such that for a.e. $t\in(0,T_{\ast})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H2-main}
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}Y(t)+\frac a2 Z(t)
\le C_{a,b}\left\lVert \nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^4 Y(t).
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Write the divergence form \eqref{eq:LLG-div} as
\[
\partial_t u-a\Delta u
=
b\,\partial_j(u\times \partial_j u)+a|\nabla u|^2u.
\]
Apply $\Delta$ to both sides and take the $L^2$ pairing with $\Delta u$. Since
\begin{align}
\int_{\mathbb R^2}\Delta u\cdot \Delta \partial_t u\,dx
&=
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert \Delta u\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2
=
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}Y(t),
\notag
\\
-a\int_{\mathbb R^2}\Delta u\cdot \Delta^2u\,dx
&=
a\int_{\mathbb R^2}|\nabla\Delta u|^2\,dx
=
aZ(t),
\notag
\end{align}
we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:start-H2}
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}Y(t)+aZ(t)=bI_1(t)+aI_2(t),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
I_1(t)&:=\int_{\mathbb R^2}\Delta\partial_j(u\times \partial_j u)\cdot \Delta u\,dx,
\notag

I_2(t)&:=\int_{\mathbb R^2}\Delta\bigl(|\nabla u|^2u\bigr)\cdot \Delta u\,dx.
\notag
\end{align}

\medskip
\noindent Step 1: exact simplification of the precession term $I_1$.

By \eqref{eq:cross-laplacian-exact} in Lemma \ref{lem:lap-expansion},
\[
\Delta\partial_j(u\times \partial_j u)
=
u\times \Delta^2 u
+
2\sum_{k=1}^2
(\partial_k u)\times \partial_k\Delta u.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:I1-first-split}
I_1(t)=I_{1,1}(t)+2I_{1,2}(t),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
I_{1,1}(t)
&:=
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\bigl(u\times \Delta^2 u\bigr)\cdot \Delta u\,dx,
\notag
\\
I_{1,2}(t)
&:=
\sum_{k=1}^2
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\bigl((\partial_k u)\times \partial_k\Delta u\bigr)\cdot \Delta u\,dx.
\label{eq:I12-def}
\end{align}
For $I_{1,1}$, first use the scalar triple product identity
\[
(a\times b)\cdot c =-(a\times c)\cdot b
\]
to rewrite the integrand as
\[
\bigl(u\times \Delta^2u\bigr)\cdot \Delta u
=
-\bigl(u\times \Delta u\bigr)\cdot \Delta^2u.
\]
Then integrate by parts in $x_k$ to get
\begin{align}
I_{1,1}(t)
&=
-\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\bigl(u\times \Delta u\bigr)\cdot \Delta^2u\,dx
\notag\\
&=
\sum_{k=1}^2
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\partial_k\bigl(u\times \Delta u\bigr)\cdot \partial_k\Delta u\,dx
\notag\\
&=
\sum_{k=1}^2
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\bigl((\partial_k u)\times \Delta u\bigr)\cdot \partial_k\Delta u\,dx
+
\sum_{k=1}^2
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\bigl(u\times \partial_k\Delta u\bigr)\cdot \partial_k\Delta u\,dx
\notag\\
&=
\sum_{k=1}^2
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\bigl((\partial_k u)\times \Delta u\bigr)\cdot \partial_k\Delta u\,dx,
\label{eq:I11-after-parts}
\end{align}
because
\[
\bigl(u\times \partial_k\Delta u\bigr)\cdot \partial_k\Delta u=0.
\]
On the other hand, the same scalar triple product identity rewrites \eqref{eq:I12-def} as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:I12-triple}
I_{1,2}(t)
=
-\sum_{k=1}^2
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\bigl((\partial_k u)\times \Delta u\bigr)\cdot \partial_k\Delta u\,dx.
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:I11-after-parts} and \eqref{eq:I12-triple} back into \eqref{eq:I1-first-split} gives the exact identity
\begin{equation*}
I_1(t)
=
-\sum_{k=1}^2
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\bigl((\partial_k u)\times \Delta u\bigr)\cdot \partial_k\Delta u\,dx.
\end{equation*}
Equivalently, this step may be written as the following pointwise divergence identity:
\begin{equation*}
\Bigl[\sum_{j=1}^2\Delta\partial_j\bigl(u\times \partial_j u\bigr)\Bigr]\cdot \Delta u
+
\sum_{k=1}^2
\bigl((\partial_k u)\times \Delta u\bigr)\cdot \partial_k\Delta u
=
\sum_{k=1}^2
\partial_k\Bigl[\bigl(u\times \partial_k\Delta u\bigr)\cdot \Delta u\Bigr].
\end{equation*}
Thus
\begin{equation*}
|I_1(t)|
\le
C\int_{\mathbb R^2}
|\nabla u|\,|\Delta u|\,|\nabla\Delta u|\,dx.
\end{equation*}
By H\"older's inequality and \eqref{eq:Delta-L4},
\begin{align}
|I_1(t)|
&\le
C\left\lVert \nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^4_x}
\left\lVert \Delta u(t)\right\rVert_{L^4_x}
\left\lVert \nabla\Delta u(t)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}
\notag\\
&\le
C\left\lVert \nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^4_x}
\bigl(Y(t)^{1/4}Z(t)^{1/4}\bigr)

\notag\\
&=
C\left\lVert \nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^4_x}Y(t)^{1/4}Z(t)^{3/4}.
\notag
\end{align}
We apply Young's inequality to the last term. Let
\[
A:=Z(t)^{3/4},
\qquad
B:=\left\lVert \nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^4_x}Y(t)^{1/4}.
\]
Then $AB\le \varepsilon A^{4/3}+C_\varepsilon B^4$, namely
\[
\left\lVert \nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_x}Y^{1/4}Z^{3/4}
\le

+
C_\varepsilon \left\lVert \nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^4Y.

\varepsilon=\frac{a}{8(1+|b|)},

\frac a8 Z(t)

C_{a,b}\left\lVert \nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^4Y(t).

\le

\label{cor:Gronwall}
Let $u$ be a maximal strong solution of \eqref{eq:LLG-intro}. If on some interval $I=(T_0,T_1)\subset[0,T_{\ast})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-def}
M_I^4:=\int_{T_0}^{T_1}\left\lVert \nabla u(t)\right\rVert_{L^4_x}^4\,dt<\infty,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H2-bound-I}
\sup_{t\in[T_0,T_1]}\left\lVert \Delta u(t)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2
\le \left\lVert \Delta u(T_0)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2\exp\bigl(C_{a,b}M_I^4\bigr),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H3-time-int}
\int_{T_0}^{T_1}\left\lVert \nabla\Delta u(t)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2\,dt
\le C_{a,b}\left\lVert \Delta u(T_0)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2\exp\bigl(C_{a,b}M_I^4\bigr).
\end{equation}
Consequently, together with Lemma~\ref{lem:L2-relative}, one has
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{t\in[T_0,T_1]}\left\lVert u(t)-Q\right\rVert_{H^2}
\le C\Bigl(
\left\lVert u(T_0)-Q\right\rVert_{H^2},\,E(u_0),\,a,\,b,\,M_I,\,T_1-T_0\Bigr).
\end{equation*}

By Lemma \ref{lem:strong-H2-transfer}, for every $t\in[T_0,T_1]$,

\label{lem:degree-energy}
Let $v\in H_Q^1(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb S^2)$. Then

<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
We first prove \eqref{eq:degree-energy}. By $|v|=1$, $|a\times b|\le |a|\,|b|$, and the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality
\[
ab\le \frac{a^2+b^2}{2}\qquad (a,b\ge 0),
\]
we have pointwise
\[
\bigl|v\cdot(\partial_1 v\times \partial_2 v)\bigr|
\le |v|\,|\partial_1 v\times \partial_2 v|
\le |\partial_1 v|\,|\partial_2 v|
\le \frac12\bigl(|\partial_1 v|^2+|\partial_2 v|^2\bigr).
\]
Integrating over $\mathbb R^2$ yields
\[
4\pi\,\bigl|\operatorname{deg}(v)\bigr|
\le
\frac12\int_{\mathbb R^2}|\nabla v|^2\,dx
=E(v),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:degree-energy}.

We next explain why the degree is preserved along an $H_Q^2$ continuous path. The first part has already shown that, whenever $v\in H_Q^1$, the degree is meaningful and satisfies \eqref{eq:degree-energy}. To prove conservation along a time-continuous trajectory, we further use the two-dimensional embedding $H^2(\mathbb R^2)\hookrightarrow L^\infty(\mathbb R^2)$ to control the zeroth-order difference terms in $\mathcal J(v)-\mathcal J(w)$. Set
\[
\mathcal J(w):=w\cdot(\partial_1 w\times \partial_2 w).
\]
For any $v,w\in H_Q^2(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb S^2)$, the identity
\begin{align*}
\mathcal J(v)-\mathcal J(w)
={}&
(v-w)\cdot(\partial_1 v\times \partial_2 v)
+w\cdot\bigl((\partial_1 v-\partial_1 w)\times \partial_2 v\bigr)
\\
&
+w\cdot\bigl(\partial_1 w\times (\partial_2 v-\partial_2 w)\bigr)
\end{align*}
and H\"older's inequality give
\begin{align*}
\left\lVert \mathcal J(v)-\mathcal J(w)\right\rVert_{L^1_x}
\le{}&
\left\lVert v-w\right\rVert_{L^\infty_x}\left\lVert \nabla v\right\rVert_{L^2_x}^2
\\
&+\left\lVert \nabla(v-w)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}\left\lVert \nabla v\right\rVert_{L^2_x}
+\left\lVert \nabla w\right\rVert_{L^2_x}\left\lVert \nabla(v-w)\right\rVert_{L^2_x}.
\end{align*}
By the two-dimensional Sobolev embedding $H^2(\mathbb R^2)\hookrightarrow L^\infty(\mathbb R^2)$, the right-hand side is controlled by $\left\lVert v-w\right\rVert_{H^2_x}$. Therefore, if $v_n\to v$ in $H_Q^2$, then $\operatorname{deg}(v_n)\to \operatorname{deg}(v)$. \qquad\text{strongly in }H^1(K_\rho),
\label{eq:bubble-strong-away}
\\
x_{j,n} &\to a_{\ell(j)},
\label{eq:bubble-center-limit}
\\
U(t_n,x_{j,n}+\lambda_{j,n}y)
&\to \omega_j(y)
\qquad\text{strongly in }C^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^2),
\label{eq:bubble-rescaled}
\\
\frac{\lambda_{i,n}}{\lambda_{j,n}}
+\frac{\lambda_{j,n}}{\lambda_{i,n}}
+\frac{|x_{i,n}-x_{j,n}|}{\lambda_{i,n}+\lambda_{j,n}}
&\longrightarrow \infty
\qquad (i\neq j),
\label{eq:bubble-orthogonality}

E\bigl(U(t_n)\bigr)
&\longrightarrow
E(u_\ast)+\sum_{j=1}^J E(\omega_j).
\label{eq:bubble-energy-split}
\end{align}

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:maximal-strong-singular}
Let $u$ be a maximal strong solution of \eqref{eq:LLG-intro}, and suppose
\[
0<T_{\ast}<\infty.
\]
Then for every $T<T_{\ast}$,
\[
u\in \mathcal W([0,T]\times\mathbb R^2),
\qquad
u\in C^\infty((0,T)\times\mathbb R^2;\mathbb S^2),
\]
and $T_{\ast}$ is a finite singularity time in the sense above.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:strong-in-weakclass}, for every $T<T_{\ast}$,
\[
u\in \mathcal W([0,T]\times\mathbb R^2).
\]
Moreover, since
\[
u-Q\in C([0,T_{\ast});H^2)\cap L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,T_{\ast});H^3),
\qquad
\partial_tu\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,T_{\ast});H^1),
\]
substitution into the divergence form \eqref{eq:LLG-div} shows that the right-hand side belongs to $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,T_{\ast});H^1)$. Standard quasilinear parabolic regularization and bootstrap then give
\[
u\in C^\infty((0,T_{\ast})\times\mathbb R^2;\mathbb S^2).
\]

It remains to prove that $T_{\ast}$ is indeed a finite singularity time. Suppose the contrary. Then there exist $\delta>0$ and
\[
\widetilde u\in \mathcal W([0,T_{\ast}+\delta)\times\mathbb R^2)
\cap C^\infty((0,T_{\ast}+\delta)\times\mathbb R^2;\mathbb S^2)
\]
such that $\widetilde u=u$ on $(0,T_{\ast})\times\mathbb R^2$. By positive-time parabolic regularization, on every compact time interval $[s_1,s_2]\Subset(0,T_{\ast}+\delta)$ one has
\[
\widetilde u-Q\in C([s_1,s_2];H^2(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb R^3)),
\qquad
\sup_{s\in[s_1,s_2]}\left\lVert \widetilde u(s)-Q\right\rVert_{H^2}<\infty.
\]
Choose $\varepsilon\in(0,\delta/4)$ and set $t_0=T_{\ast}-\varepsilon$. Then
\[
\widetilde u(t_0)=u(t_0)\in H_Q^2(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb S^2),
\qquad
\sup_{s\in[t_0,T_{\ast}+\varepsilon]}\left\lVert \widetilde u(s)-Q\right\rVert_{H^2}<\infty.
\]
The local strong-solution theory gives a unique $H_Q^2$ strong solution $v$ with initial value $u(t_0)$, defined on some interval $[t_0,t_0+\eta)$; the boundedness above allows the standard continuation argument to extend $v$ along $\widetilde u$ step by step, at least until $T_{\ast}+\varepsilon$. On the other hand, $u$ and $\widetilde u$ coincide on $(t_0,T_{\ast})$, and $v$ and $\widetilde u$ have the same initial value. Hence, by uniqueness of strong solutions,
\[
v=\widetilde u=u
\qquad\text{on }[t_0,T_{\ast})\times\mathbb R^2,
\]
and $v$ continues $u$ beyond $T_{\ast}$. This contradicts the maximality of $T_{\ast}$. Therefore $T_{\ast}$ must be a finite singularity time.

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:degree-splitting}
In the notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:bubble-degree-input}, set
\[
d_j:=\operatorname{deg}(\omega_j)\in \mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}
\qquad (1\le j\le J).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bubble-degree-split}
\operatorname{deg}(u_0)
=
\operatorname{deg}(u_\ast)+\sum_{j=1}^J d_j,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bubble-energy-lower}
\lim_{n\to\infty}E\bigl(U(t_n)\bigr)
\ge
4\pi\,\bigl|\operatorname{deg}(u_\ast)\bigr|
+
4\pi\sum_{j=1}^J |d_j|.
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Write
\[
u_n:=U(t_n),
\qquad
\mathcal J(v):=v\cdot(\partial_1 v\times \partial_2 v).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:degree-energy}, for every $n$,
\[
\operatorname{deg}(u_n)=\operatorname{deg}(u_0),
\qquad
|\mathcal J(v)|\le \frac12|\nabla v|^2.
\]

\smallskip
\noindent Step 1: convergence of Jacobians away from singular points.
Fix $0<\rho<\rho_0$ and set
\[
K_\rho:=\mathbb R^2\setminus \bigcup_{\alpha=1}^N B(a_\alpha,\rho).
\]
By \eqref{eq:bubble-strong-away},
\[
u_n-u_\ast\to 0\qquad\text{strongly in }H^1(K_\rho).
\]
Therefore
\begin{align}
\bigl\|\partial_1 u_n\times \partial_2 u_n-\partial_1 u_\ast\times \partial_2 u_\ast\bigr\|_{L^1(K_\rho)}
&\le \left\lVert \partial_1(u_n-u_\ast)\right\rVert_{L^2(K_\rho)}\left\lVert \partial_2u_n\right\rVert_{L^2(K_\rho)}
\nonumber\\
&\quad+\left\lVert \partial_1u_\ast\right\rVert_{L^2(K_\rho)}\left\lVert \partial_2(u_n-u_\ast)\right\rVert_{L^2(K_\rho)}
\to0.
\label{eq:cross-away-conv}
\end{align}
Thus the sequence
\[
g_n:=\bigl|\partial_1 u_n\times \partial_2 u_n\bigr|
\]
is uniformly integrable in $L^1(K_\rho)$. Since also $u_n\to u_\ast$ in $H^1(K_\rho)$, after passing to a subsequence we may assume $u_n\to u_\ast$ a.e. on $K_\rho$; Vitali's theorem then gives
\begin{equation*}
\int_{K_\rho}|u_n-u_\ast|\,\bigl|\partial_1 u_n\times \partial_2 u_n\bigr|\,dx\to0.
\end{equation*}
Combining this with \eqref{eq:cross-away-conv}, we get
\begin{align}
\int_{K_\rho}\mathcal J(u_n)\,dx-\int_{K_\rho}\mathcal J(u_\ast)\,dx
={}&
\int_{K_\rho}(u_n-u_\ast)\cdot(\partial_1u_n\times\partial_2u_n)\,dx
\nonumber\\
&+\int_{K_\rho}u_\ast\cdot\Bigl[(\partial_1u_n\times\partial_2u_n)-(\partial_1u_\ast\times\partial_2u_\ast)\Bigr]dx
\nonumber\\
\longrightarrow{}0.
\label{eq:jacobian-away}
\end{align}

\smallskip
\noindent Step 2: diagonal extraction of bubble balls.
Fix the above $\rho$. Choose an increasing sequence $R_m\uparrow\infty$ such that, for every $j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rm-tail-choice}
E\bigl(\omega_j;\mathbb R^2\setminus B(0,R_m)\bigr)
+
\int_{\mathbb R^2\setminus B(0,R_m)}|\mathcal J(\omega_j)|\,dy
\le 2^{-m}.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:bubble-rescaled}, for each $m$ one can choose $N_m$ such that, whenever $n\ge N_m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rescaled-C1-choice}
\sum_{j=1}^J
\left\lVert u_n(x_{j,n}+\lambda_{j,n}\cdot)-\omega_j\right\rVert_{C^1(B(0,R_m))}
\le 2^{-m}.
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:bubble-center-limit}--\eqref{eq:bubble-orthogonality} and increasing $N_m$ if necessary, we may also require that for all $n\ge N_m$,
\begin{equation*}
B(x_{j,n},R_m\lambda_{j,n})\subset B(a_{\ell(j)},\rho),
\qquad
B(x_{i,n},R_m\lambda_{i,n})\cap B(x_{j,n},R_m\lambda_{j,n})=\varnothing\qquad (i\ne j).
\end{equation*}
Take an increasing subsequence $n_m\ge N_m$ and relabel it by $n$. Put
\[
R_n:=R_m,
\qquad
B_{j,n}:=B(x_{j,n},R_n\lambda_{j,n}).
\]
Then $R_n\uparrow\infty$, and \eqref{eq:rescaled-C1-choice}, \eqref{eq:Rm-tail-choice} imply
\begin{align}
E(u_n;B_{j,n})
&=E\bigl(u_n(x_{j,n}+\lambda_{j,n}\cdot);B(0,R_n)\bigr)
\longrightarrow E(\omega_j),
\label{eq:bubble-ball-energy-conv}
\\
\int_{B_{j,n}}\mathcal J(u_n)\,dx
&=
\int_{B(0,R_n)}\mathcal J\bigl(u_n(x_{j,n}+\lambda_{j,n}y)\bigr)\,dy
\longrightarrow
\int_{\mathbb R^2}\mathcal J(\omega_j)\,dy
=
4\pi d_j.
\label{eq:jacobian-bubble}
\end{align}

\smallskip
\noindent Step 3: smallness of the neck energy.
Define the neck region
\[
\Gamma_{n,\rho}
:=
\Bigl(\bigcup_{\alpha=1}^N B(a_\alpha,\rho)\Bigr)
\setminus
\Bigl(\bigcup_{j=1}^J B_{j,n}\Bigr).
\]
By disjointness and the decomposition,
\begin{equation*}
E(u_n)
=
E(u_n;K_\rho)
+
\sum_{j=1}^J E(u_n;B_{j,n})
+
E(u_n;\Gamma_{n,\rho}).
\end{equation*}
Using \eqref{eq:bubble-strong-away}, \eqref{eq:bubble-ball-energy-conv}, and \eqref{eq:bubble-energy-split}, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{n\to\infty}E(u_n;\Gamma_{n,\rho})
=
E\Bigl(u_\ast;\bigcup_{\alpha=1}^N B(a_\alpha,\rho)\Bigr).
\end{equation*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-jacobian-bound}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\left|
\int_{\Gamma_{n,\rho}}\mathcal J(u_n)\,dx
\right|
\le
E\Bigl(u_\ast;\bigcup_{\alpha=1}^N B(a_\alpha,\rho)\Bigr).
\end{equation}

\smallskip
\noindent Step 4: splitting of the degree.
By the region decomposition,
\begin{align}
4\pi\,\operatorname{deg}(u_0)
&=4\pi\,\operatorname{deg}(u_n)
=\int_{\mathbb R^2}\mathcal J(u_n)\,dx
\nonumber\\
&=
\int_{K_\rho}\mathcal J(u_n)\,dx
+
\sum_{j=1}^J\int_{B_{j,n}}\mathcal J(u_n)\,dx
+
\int_{\Gamma_{n,\rho}}\mathcal J(u_n)\,dx.
\notag
\end{align}
Letting $n\to\infty$ and using \eqref{eq:jacobian-away}, \eqref{eq:jacobian-bubble}, and \eqref{eq:neck-jacobian-bound}, we get
\begin{equation*}
4\pi\,\operatorname{deg}(u_0)
=
\int_{K_\rho}\mathcal J(u_\ast)\,dx
+
4\pi\sum_{j=1}^J d_j
+
\varepsilon_\rho,
\qquad
|\varepsilon_\rho|
\le
E\Bigl(u_\ast;\bigcup_{\alpha=1}^N B(a_\alpha,\rho)\Bigr).
\end{equation*}
Finally let $\rho\downarrow0$. Since $u_\ast\in H_Q^1(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb S^2)$,
\[
\int_{K_\rho}\mathcal J(u_\ast)\,dx
\longrightarrow
\int_{\mathbb R^2}\mathcal J(u_\ast)\,dx
=4\pi\operatorname{deg}(u_\ast),
\qquad
E\Bigl(u_\ast;\bigcup_{\alpha=1}^N B(a_\alpha,\rho)\Bigr)\to0.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:bubble-degree-split} holds.

\smallskip
\noindent Step 5: the energy lower bound.
By \eqref{eq:bubble-energy-split} and Lemma~\ref{lem:degree-energy},
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}E(u_n)
=
E(u_\ast)+\sum_{j=1}^J E(\omega_j)
\ge
4\pi\,\bigl|\operatorname{deg}(u_\ast)\bigr|
+
4\pi\sum_{j=1}^J |d_j|,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:bubble-energy-lower}.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:deg0-8pi-barrier}
Let $u$ be a maximal strong solution of \eqref{eq:LLG-intro}, and assume
\[
0<T_{\ast}<\infty,
\qquad
\operatorname{deg}(u_0)=0.
\]
Then necessarily
\begin{equation}\label{eq:8pi-barrier}
E(u_0)\ge 8\pi.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
By Lemma \ref{lem:degree-energy}, for every $t\in[0,T_{\ast})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deg-const-before-T}
\operatorname{deg}(u(t))=\operatorname{deg}(u_0)=0.
\end{equation}
On the other hand, Proposition~\ref{prop:maximal-strong-singular} implies that $u$ is smooth on $(0,T_{\ast})$ and that $T_{\ast}$ is a finite singularity time in the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:bubble-degree-input}. Thus one may choose $t_n\uparrow T_{\ast}$, a limit map $u_\ast$, and harmonic bubbles $\omega_1,\dots,\omega_J$ such that \eqref{eq:bubble-energy-split} holds. Set
\[
d_j:=\operatorname{deg}(\omega_j)\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}.
\]
By Lemma \ref{lem:degree-splitting} and \eqref{eq:deg-const-before-T},
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{deg}(u_\ast)=-\sum_{j=1}^J d_j.
\end{equation*}
Using energy monotonicity and \eqref{eq:bubble-energy-lower} in Lemma \ref{lem:degree-splitting}, we get
\begin{align}
E(u_0)
&\ge \lim_{n\to\infty}E\bigl(u(t_n)\bigr)
\label{eq:energy-lower-deg0}
\\
&\ge
4\pi\,\bigl|\operatorname{deg}(u_\ast)\bigr|
+
4\pi\sum_{j=1}^J |d_j|
\nonumber\\
&=
4\pi\Bigl(\Bigl|\sum_{j=1}^J d_j\Bigr|+\sum_{j=1}^J |d_j|\Bigr).
\nonumber
\end{align}
Since every $d_j\neq0$ and $J\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integer-lower-bound}
\Bigl|\sum_{j=1}^J d_j\Bigr|+\sum_{j=1}^J |d_j|\ge 2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $\sum_j d_j\neq0$, then the left-hand side is at least $1+1=2$; if $\sum_j d_j=0$, then because not all $d_j$ are zero, necessarily $\sum_j |d_j|\ge2$. Substituting \eqref{eq:integer-lower-bound} into \eqref{eq:energy-lower-deg0} gives
\[
E(u_0)\ge 8\pi,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:8pi-barrier}.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:deg0-analytic-div}
Let $u$ be a maximal strong solution of \eqref{eq:LLG-intro}, and assume
\[
0<T_{\ast}<\infty,\qquad \deg(u_0)=0.

Then, besides \eqref{eq:8pi-barrier}, for every $T_0\in[0,T_{\ast})$ and every dyadic index $k_\ast\in\mathbb Z$,
\begin{align}
\int_{T_0}^{T_{\ast}}\int_{\mathbb R^2}|\nabla u(t,x)|^4\,dxdt&=\infty,\label{eq:deg0-L4-div}\\
\left\lVert P_{\ge k_\ast}\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_{t,x}((T_0,T_{\ast})\times\mathbb R^2)}&=\infty.\label{eq:deg0-uv-div}
\end{align}

Inequality \eqref{eq:8pi-barrier} has already been proved in Proposition~\ref{prop:deg0-8pi-barrier}. If for some $T_0<T_{\ast}$ one had

\]
then Theorem~\ref{thm:L4-cont} would immediately imply that $u$ can be continued beyond $t=T_{\ast}$, contradicting maximality. Hence \eqref{eq:deg0-L4-div} holds. Similarly, if for some $k_\ast$ one had

\left\lVert P_{\ge k_\ast}\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^4_{t,x}((T_0,T_{\ast})\times\mathbb R^2)}<\infty,
\]
then Theorem~\ref{thm:uv-tail-cont} would again give a continuation contradiction, and therefore \eqref{eq:deg0-uv-div} holds.

Assume the contrary, namely
\[

\]
Then Corollary~\ref{cor:deg0-analytic-div} gives
\[
E(u_0)\ge 8\pi,

\int_{T_0}^{T_{\ast}}\int_{\mathbb R^2}|\nabla u|^4=\infty,

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
The relation between the result of this section and the first four sections can be summarized as follows: the first four sections provide the analytic continuation mechanism at a finite terminal time, while this section shows that, in the degree-zero sector with energy below $8\pi$, the geometric carrier that would trigger a finite-time singularity--a nontrivial bubble tree--does not exist. Therefore the $8\pi$ improvement is not the product of a new $H^2$ differential inequality, but the combination of the strong-solution framework of this paper with bubble quantization under the degree-zero constraint.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
The degree-zero hypothesis is not a superficial decoration. Known $1$-equivariant finite-time blow-up is built around the lowest-energy nonconstant steady state. Consequently, one cannot expect a uniform general threshold, valid across all topological sectors, that is strictly larger than $4\pi$. In contrast, the key to the improvement to $8\pi$ in this section is that degree zero rules out the cheapest singularity mechanism consisting of ``one bubble core of degree $\pm1$ plus a constant background.'' For a related $1$-equivariant blow-up construction, see \cite{XuZhao}.
