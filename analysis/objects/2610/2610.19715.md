<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:OBgenieK}
The single-user capacity $C_k$ of the interference channel in \eqref{eq:Kuserapprox} at high SNR is characterized by
\begin{align} \label{eq:capLBUB}
C_k^{\mathrm{high\!-\!SNR}} &= \log\biggl(\frac{P_{k}}{2\sigma_k^2}\biggl[1+\biggl(\sum_{\substack{w=1\\w \neq k}}^K P_w \sum_{m=-M}^M c_{k,w}^m\biggr)^2\biggr]\! \biggr),
\end{align}
for all $k \in \{1,2,\ldots,K\}$. The collection of the single-user high-SNR upper bounds on $C_k$ in \eqref{eq:capLBUB} for all $k \in \{1,2,\ldots,K\}$ constitute the following outer bound:
\begin{align} \label{eq:OBregion}
\mathcal{C}_{K\textrm{-cap-reg}}^{\mathrm{high\!-\!SNR}} 
   &\subseteq 
   \bigg\{ (R_1,R_2,\ldots,R_K) \;\bigg|\;
   R_k \leq C_k^{\mathrm{high\!-\!SNR}}, \notag\\
   &\phantom{wwwwwwwwwwwww}
   \;\forall k \in \{1,2,\ldots,K\}
   \bigg\}.
\end{align}

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\textbf{Interpretation of Achievability and Input Distributions:} Throughout this paper, the term \emph{achievable} refers to rates attained within the first-order RP channel model under average power constraints. As will be clear from the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:OBgenieK}, Gaussian inputs arise as the optimal choice for the user of interest since the genie-aided outer bound establishes that constant-amplitude interferer signaling is extremal. Under this provably optimal interferer structure, the effective channel reduces to a linear additive-noise model within the RP approximation, for which Gaussian inputs are optimal in the standard information-theoretic sense. The resulting rate expressions thus characterize the single-user capacity corner points of the RP channel under average power constraints.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:prelog}
In the symmetric scaling regime $P_1\asymp P_2\asymp\cdots\asymp P_K\asymp P$, the expression
in~\eqref{eq:capLBUBfinite} (or \eqref{eq:capLBUB}) grows as
\[
C_k
\sim \log\!\bigl(P_k P^2\bigr)
=3\log P+O(1),
\]
so the capacity pre-log can reach $3$ under the first-order RP model. Such superlinear pre-log factors have been reported previously for dispersionless single-channel RP models (e.g., \cite[Sec.~VI]{vannucci2002rp},\cite[Sec.~3.1 and 5]{keykhosravi2019accuracy}) and reflect the fact that first-order RP is not strictly energy preserving, leading to an artificial power gain when all channels scale simultaneously. Thus, our capacity characterizations should be interpreted within the RP approximation; more complete models, including signal-noise interactions and higher-order terms, are expected to moderate the asymptotic slope. Nevertheless, our numerical results in Section~\ref{sec:num} show that the derived bounds closely track achievable rates over the validated $0$-$5$ dBm regime.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:allhelpsone}
Consider the $K$-user interference channel described in~\eqref{eq:Kuserapprox}.  
\noindent For each $k$, let $\mathbf{r}^{(k)}=(r^{(k)}_1,r^{(k)}_2,\ldots,r^{(k)}_K)\in\mathbb{R}_+^K$ be the rate vector with
\[
r^{(k)}_j =
\begin{cases}
C_k, & j=k,\\[4pt]
0, & j\neq k.
\end{cases}
\]

\noindent Define the \emph{all-helps-one} region as the convex hull of these $K$ extreme points:
\begin{align} \label{eq:AHOregion}
\mathcal{R}_{\text{AHO}}
&\triangleq \mathrm{Conv}\big\{\mathbf{r}^{(1)},\mathbf{r}^{(2)},\ldots,\mathbf{r}^{(K)}\big\} \\
&= \Big\{ \mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}_+^K \;\Big|\; \mathbf{R}=\sum_{k=1}^K \lambda_k \mathbf{r}^{(k)},\;
\lambda_k\ge 0,\; \sum_{k=1}^K \lambda_k = 1 \Big\}. \nonumber
\end{align}

\noindent Then the region $\mathcal{R}_{\text{AHO}}$ is achievable, i.e.,
\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}_{K\textrm{-cap-reg}} \supseteq \mathcal{R}_{\text{AHO}}.
\end{align}

\noindent Equivalently, letting $\lambda_k$ denote the fraction of time during which user-$k$ is the ``helped'' user, the region can be written coordinate-wise as
\begin{align*}
R_k \le \lambda_k \, C_k, \quad k=1,2,\ldots,K,\quad
\lambda_k\ge 0,\;\sum_{k=1}^K \lambda_k = 1.
\end{align*}

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
AHO should be interpreted as an information-theoretic characterization of long-term rate trade-offs, rather than a specific transmission protocol. In practice, the associated time sharing may occur over longer timescales (than symbol-level coordination), such as transmission epochs, wavelength reconfiguration, or network-level scheduling.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The achievability proof for $\mathcal{R}_{\text{AHO}}$ proceeds as follows. Fix $k \in \{1,2,\ldots,K\}$ and suppose that all interferer transmissions are chosen as\footnote{The constant-amplitude interferer assignment in \eqref{eq:constamp} cannot be strictly enforced for the first and last $M$ symbols, incurring an $O(M/n)$ rate penalty that vanishes as $n\to\infty$, so the expression \eqref{eq:capLBUBfinite} holds as a capacity result.}
\begin{align}
|x_{w}[i-m]|^2 = P_w, 
\quad \forall \; w \neq k, \; \forall \; m, \; \text{and} \; \forall \; i > m, \label{eq:constamp}
\end{align}
i.e., users $w \neq k$ employ constant-amplitude signaling. This choice turns out to be optimal in terms of maximizing the achievable rate of user-$k$, since it precisely achieves the single-user upper bound \eqref{eq:capLBUBfinite} in Proposition~\ref{thm:OBgenieKfinite}. Indeed, under this condition, the channel reduces to the following memoryless single-user model (with time indices omitted for brevity):  
\begin{align}
Y_{k} &= X_{k}\biggl(1+j \sum_{\substack{w=1\\w \neq k}}^K P_w \sum_{m=-M}^M c_{k,w}^m \biggr)+N_{k}, \label{eq:shamai}
\end{align}
which is a complex AWGN channel with average input power constraint $P_k$, whose capacity is well known. Hence, the achievable rate is given by \eqref{eq:capLBUBfinite}. Repeating this construction for each user yields $K$ corner points of the achievable rate region. Their convex hull, obtained by time sharing, defines the \emph{all-helps-one} inner bound for \eqref{eq:Kuserapprox}. This construction should be viewed as an information-theoretic characterization of trade-offs under non-cooperating users, rather than a simultaneous multi-user coding scheme. A numerical comparison with TIN and the outer bound of Prop.~\ref{thm:OBgenieKfinite} is provided in Section~\ref{sec:num}.

The analysis above employs continuous-valued (Gaussian) inputs for analytical tractability within the first-order RP model, whereas practical optical systems use discrete modulation formats. While \eqref{eq:capLBUBfinite} pertains to continuous signaling, the key qualitative conclusions, namely the extremality of constant-amplitude interferer signaling and its advantage over TIN, are expected to extend to sufficiently rich discrete constellations with similar amplitude statistics.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
Under symmetric operation with $K$ users, each user acts as the active user for a fraction $1/K$ of the time. Consequently, the throughput of the AHO scheme is the average symmetric rate $C_k/K$, where the factor $1/K$ accounts for the power expended while a user serves as a helper. Thus, the helper power is reflected through the averaging over time (see Fig.~\ref{fig:succ_vs_tin_vs_aho}).

For comparison, conventional time-division multiple access (TDMA) silences all interfering users, rendering the channel linear and yielding a symmetric rate of \smash{$\frac{1}{K}\log\!\left(1+\frac{P_k}{2\sigma_k^2}\right)$}. In contrast, AHO employs constant-amplitude helper signals whose XPM contributions increase the effective SNR of the active user, resulting in a symmetric throughput of $C_k/K$.

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:extreme}
Fix user $k$ and blocklength $n$. For each interferer $w\neq k$,
let
\[
\mathcal{F}_w :=\Bigl\{\underline z_w\in\mathbb{R}_+^{\,n}: \tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf 1^\top\underline z_w=P_w\Bigr\},
\qquad \mathcal{F}:=\prod_{w\neq k}\mathcal{F}_w ,
\]
and define $\underline z:=(\underline z_w)_{w\neq k}$. Consider the quadratic form
\[
Q(\underline z):=\underline z^{\mathsf T}C\underline z,
\]
where $C\succeq 0$ is the symmetric matrix shown above. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Q$ is convex on $\mathcal{F}$.
\item Any maximizer of $Q$ over $\mathcal{F}$ can be chosen at an extreme point of $\mathcal{F}$, i.e., for each $w\neq k$ there exists $j_w\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$ such that
\[
\underline z_w = nP_w\,\mathbf e_{j_w},
\]
\end{enumerate}
where \(\mathbf{e}_j\) is the \(j\)-th standard basis vector of \(\mathbb{R}^n\). In particular, $Q$ achieves its supremum over $\mathcal{F}$ at such an extreme allocation.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:diag-opt}
Fix a user $k$ and blocklength $n$, and consider the effective single-user channel given by
\[
Y_k^n = H_k X_k^n + N_k^n,
\]
where $H_k=\operatorname{diag}(h_{k,1},h_{k,2},\ldots,h_{k,n})$ and $N_k^n\sim\mathcal{CN}0,\Sigma_k)$ with $\Sigma_k=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{k,1}^2,\sigma_{k,2}^2,\ldots,\sigma_{k,n}^2)$. For Gaussian inputs $X_k^n\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,K_k)$ under the constraint $\operatorname{Tr}(K_k)\le nP_k$, 
\[
I(X_k^n;Y_k^n)
=\log\det\!\big(I_n+\Sigma_k^{-1/2}H_k K_k H_k^{\!H}\Sigma_k^{-1/2}\big)
\]
is maximized by a diagonal covariance matrix $K_k^\star$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $D_k := H_k^{\!H}\Sigma_k^{-1}H_k=\operatorname{diag}(d_{k,1},d_{k,2},\ldots,d_{k,n})$ with $d_{k,i}\ge 0$. Then we have
\[
I(X_k^n;Y_k^n)
=\log\det\!\big(I_n + D_k^{1/2} K_k D_k^{1/2}\big).
\]
Since $D_k^{1/2}K_kD_k^{1/2}$ is Hermitian positive semidefinite, Hadamard’s inequality implies
\[
\det\!\big(I_n + D_k^{1/2} K_k D_k^{1/2}\big)
\le \prod_{i=1}^n \big(1+d_{k,i}(K_k)_{ii}\big),
\]
with equality if and only if $D_k^{1/2} K_k D_k^{1/2}$ is diagonal. Hence, correlation across symbols (off-diagonal entries of $K_k$) cannot increase $I(X_k^n;Y_k^n)$. Replacing $K_k$ by its diagonal part preserves the trace constraint and does not decrease the mutual information, so an optimal diagonal covariance $K_k^\star$ exists.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:log_approx}
Fix user $k$ and blocklength $n$. Define the per-symbol powers
$P_{k,i}:=\mathbb{E}[|X_k[i]|^2]$ and
\[
S_k[i]:= \sum_{\substack{w=1\\ w\neq k}}^{K} |x_w[i-m]|^2 \sum_{m=-M}^{M} c_{k,w}^m.
\]
Let the per-symbol SNRs and its blockwise lower envelope be 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-def}
\gamma_{k,i}:= \frac{P_{k,i}}{2\sigma_k^2}\bigl(1+S_k[i]^2\bigr),
\qquad
\underline{\gamma}_k := \min_{1\le i\le n}\gamma_{k,i}.
\end{equation}
Then the following bound holds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:avg-log-gap}
0 \le \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \bigl[\log(1+\gamma_{k,i})-\log\gamma_{k,i}\bigr]
\le \frac{1}{\underline{\gamma}_k}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, along any sequence of power allocations $\{P_{k,i}\}$ such that $\underline{\gamma}_k\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:avg-log-replace}
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \log(1+\gamma_{k,i}) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \log\gamma_{k,i} + o(1).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For any $\gamma>0$,
\[
\log(1+\gamma)-\log\gamma =\log\!\Big(1+\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big) \le \frac{1}{\gamma}.
\]
Hence, for each $i$,
\[
0\le \log(1+\gamma_{k,i})-\log\gamma_{k,i} \le \frac{1}{\gamma_{k,i}} \le \frac{1}{\underline{\gamma}_k}.
\]
Averaging over $i=1,2,\ldots,n$ yields \eqref{eq:avg-log-gap}. If $\underline{\gamma}_k\to\infty$, then $1/\underline{\gamma}_k\to0$, which gives \eqref{eq:avg-log-replace}.
