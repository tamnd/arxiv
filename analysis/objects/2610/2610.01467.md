At $p_7$, $p_8$ and $p_9$, respectively, the pairs $(L_0,L_1)$ are $(1,1)$, $(1,2)$ and $(1,3)$ in the corresponding homogeneous coordinate normalizations. Since $\ell=\bar\ell\pi$, the assertion follows.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:global-pencil}
The base locus of the pencil spanned by $L_0,L_1$ is a zero-dimensional scheme of length $36$. On every smooth fiber disjoint from the base locus, $\ell=L_1/L_0$ defines a morphism of degree $72$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $B=\{L_0=L_1=0\}\cap X$. Proposition~\ref{prop:three-labels} and the base-point-freeness quoted above show that $B$ is disjoint from $F$. If $B$ contained a curve $D$, then $D$ would be disjoint from $F$, hence $D\cdot F=0$ and $D$ would be vertical for $\alpha$. All smooth fibers are irreducible, and the three singular fibers are irreducible and reduced \cite[long version, Proposition~5(iii)]{CKY}; hence the support of $D$ would be a full fiber $F_t$. A hyperplane divisor containing $F_t$ would have the form $H=F_t+R$ with $R$ effective. Since $H$ is ample, this would give
\[
36=H^2=H\cdot F_t+H\cdot R\geq 72,
\]
a contradiction. Thus the two hyperplane divisors meet properly. Their intersection scheme has length $H^2=36$. On a fiber avoiding $B$, their restrictions have no common zero and define a map of degree $H\cdot F_t=72$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:two-real-arcs}
Let $c_+$ and $c_-$ be transported along the two real arcs from $O$ to $b$, using conjugation-equivariant transport. The two real fixed points of each $c_\pm$ lie on one oval of $F(\mathbb R)$, and the ovals obtained for $c_+$ and $c_-$ are different.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Cut the real base circle at $O$. The connectedness of $X(\mathbb R)$ and the decomposition of the cut real fibration into a saddle component and a product annulus follow from \cite[Theorem~1 and Corollary~2]{AYreal}; the unique real saddle is also described in \cite{SS}. Each regular real fiber has two circles. The saddle component is diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{RP}^2\setminus(D^2\sqcup D^2)$. The saddle component has one boundary circle on either side. In the real Morse model, the two fixed points of the local vanishing circle lie on the regular level circle of this saddle component; the two local arcs join outside the Morse chart to form that circle. Equivariant transport along either arc carries the fixed points to the corresponding boundary circle of the cobordism. The boundary-gluing permutation is the transposition for two components by \cite[Theorem~1]{AYreal}: otherwise the reglued real surface would be disconnected. Hence the boundary circles of the saddle component on the two sides are identified at $O$ with different ovals of $F(\mathbb R)$.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:crossing}
Let $\pi:F\to G$ be a cyclic degree-three branched cover with deck generator $S$ and branch set $D\subset G$. Let $c\subset F$ be an oriented embedded circle disjoint from $\pi^{-1}(D)$. After a small perturbation, assume $\beta=\pi|_c$ is an immersion with transverse double points and no triple points. At a double point $\beta(t)=\beta(u)$, $t<u$, let
\[
c(u)=S^{j(t,u)}c(t),\qquad j(t,u)\in\{1,2\},
\]
and let $\varepsilon(t,u)$ be the local intersection sign of the ordered tangent vectors $(\dot\beta(t),\dot\beta(u))$ on $G$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossing}
[c]\cdot S_*[c]
=\sum_{\substack{t<u\\\beta(t)=\beta(u)}}
\begin{cases}
-\varepsilon(t,u),&j(t,u)=1,\\
\phantom{-}\varepsilon(t,u),&j(t,u)=2.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Here the loop $\beta|_{[t,u]}$ is based at the double point. Our monodromy convention lifts it from $c(t)$ to $S^{j(t,u)}c(t)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
At a double point the two lifts are distinct because $c$ is embedded; hence $j$ is $1$ or $2$. If $j=1$, $c$ meets $S(c)$ at $c(u)=S(c(t))$. The ordered tangents of $(c,S(c))$ project to $(\dot\beta(u),\dot\beta(t))$, so the intersection sign is $-\varepsilon$. If $j=2$, then $c(t)=S(c(u))$ and the ordered projected tangents are $(\dot\beta(t),\dot\beta(u))$, giving $+\varepsilon$. The two occupied sheets form a pair in a three-element cyclic set. This pair and its translate have one element in common, so the double point accounts for exactly one intersection of $c$ with $S(c)$. Conversely, every intersection of $c$ and $S(c)$ projects to a double point of $\beta$. Summing the local signs proves \eqref{eq:crossing}.

Interchanging $t$ and $u$ reverses the local crossing sign and replaces $j$ by $-j$ modulo three. The summand is unchanged, as required.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:real-parity}
Suppose $F$ and $G$ have compatible real involutions $C$ and $J$, with $CSC=S^{-1}$, and $c$ is $C$-invariant as an unoriented circle. Choose a compatible generic perturbation in Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing}. Then
\[
[c]\cdot S_*[c]\equiv
\#\{\text{$J$-fixed double points of $\beta$}\}\pmod2.
\]
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The involution $J$ preserves the set of double points of $\beta$. Nonfixed double points occur in pairs, each of whose two summands is odd modulo two. A fixed double point contributes one modulo two. Theorem~\ref{thm:crossing} gives the claim. At an unbranched real point of $G$, the involution on the three-sheet fiber fixes exactly one sheet, since it conjugates $S$ to $S^{-1}$. The two lifts on the $C$-invariant curve $c$ form a $C$-stable set of two distinct sheets. They cannot both be fixed, so $C$ exchanges them.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:real-translate}
Under the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:real-parity}, suppose also that $c$ meets $R_1$ transversely and avoids the fixed points of $S$. Then
\[
[c]\cdot S_*[c]\equiv [c]\cdot[R_1]\pmod2.
\]
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The real involution $C$ exchanges $R_1$ and $R_2$. If $z\in c\cap R_1$, then $S C z=z$, so $Cz=S^2z$ is a distinct point of $c\cap R_2$. The pair $z,Cz$ projects to one double point fixed by the involution on $G$. Conversely, at a fixed projected double point the two branches are exchanged by $C$; of its two lifts on $c$, exactly one lies in $R_1$. The result follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:real-parity} and the mod-two intersection count.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:reflection-loci}
For distinct $i,j\in\{0,1,2\}$, the curves $R_i=\operatorname{Fix}(S^iC)$ and $R_j=\operatorname{Fix}(S^jC)$ meet precisely at $p_7,p_8,p_9$, and they meet transversely there. In particular,
\[
[R_i]\cdot[R_j]=1\pmod 2.
\]
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
A point fixed by both $S^iC$ and $S^jC$ is fixed by their product $S^{i-j}$, hence by $S$. Conversely, the three fixed points of $S$ are real and are fixed by every $S^iC$. In a local holomorphic coordinate at any such point, $S$ acts by a nontrivial cube root of unity and $C$ is a reflection. The fixed real lines of $S^iC$ are distinct for distinct $i$, so the intersections are transverse. There are three of them, giving the stated mod-two intersection.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:trace}
For the product $M=T_{v_0}T_{v_2}T_{v_1}$ in this cyclic order, acting on $H_1(F;\mathbb Q)$,
\[
\operatorname{tr}(M)=38-3m^2-m^3,
\qquad
\det(zI-M)=(z-1)^{36}
\bigl[z^2-(2-3m^2-m^3)z+1\bigr].
\]
For the reverse product $T_{v_0}T_{v_1}T_{v_2}$, replace $m$ by $-m$ in these formulas.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Set $N_i(x)=(v_i\cdot x)v_i$. Then $T_{v_i}=I+N_i$, $\operatorname{tr}N_i=0$, and for $i\ne j$, $\operatorname{tr}(N_iN_j)=-(v_i\cdot v_j)^2=-m^2$. The trace of $N_0N_2N_1$ is $-m^3$. Expanding the product proves the trace formula. Its difference from the identity has rank at most three, so the eigenvalue $1$ has algebraic multiplicity at least $35$. For a symplectic matrix of dimension $38$ this multiplicity is even, hence at least $36$. The other two eigenvalues have sum $2-3m^2-m^3$ and product one, which gives the displayed polynomial.

\label{prop:single-trace}
Let $T=T_{[c]}$ act on $H_1(F;\mathbb Q)$ by $T(x)=x+([c]\cdot x)[c]$. Then
\[
m=1+\operatorname{tr}(S_*T).
\]
Moreover,
\[
m^2=38-\operatorname{tr}(T S_*T S_*^{-1}).
\]
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The three fixed points of $S$ give a Lefschetz number of $3$, hence $\operatorname{tr}(S_*|H_1(F;\mathbb Q))=-1$. The operator $S_*T-S_*$ has rank one: it sends $x$ to $([c]\cdot x)S_*[c]$, so its trace is $[c]\cdot S_*[c]=m$. For the second identity, expand the two transvections $T_{[c]}T_{S_*[c]}$. Each rank-one term has trace zero and their product has trace $-m^2$.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:schreier}
Let
\[
A=qp^{-1},\qquad B=pqp^{-2},\qquad D_i=p^idp^{-i}\quad(i=0,1,2),
\]
and use $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$. Then $K$ has the presentation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schreier}
[A,B]=D_0(BD_2B^{-1})(BA D_1A^{-1}B^{-1}).
\end{equation}
If $\rho:\Delta\to\operatorname{Mod}(F)$ is the orbifold monodromy representation with $\rho(d)=T_c$, the three peripheral factors in \eqref{eq:schreier} are positive Dehn twists, with the indicated transport conjugations.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Use $1,p,p^2$ as coset representatives for $K$ in $\Delta$. Reidemeister--Schreier rewriting gives the stated generators. To check the relation explicitly, put $L=pq$. Since $q=Ap$ and $B=pAp^{-1}$, the relation $q^3=1$ implies
\[
L=Bp^2,\qquad L^2=BAp,\qquad L^3=[B,A].
\]
The relation $pqrd=1$ gives $r=L^{-1}d^{-1}$, so $r^3=1$ is equivalent to $(dL)^3=1$. Expanding this last word yields
\[
(dL)^3=D_0(BD_2B^{-1})(BA D_1A^{-1}B^{-1})[B,A].
\]
Since $[B,A]=[A,B]^{-1}$, this gives \eqref{eq:schreier}. The resulting group is a genus-one surface group with three peripheral generators, as its covering interpretation also shows. Applying $\rho$ sends each conjugate of $d$ to a conjugate of the positive twist $T_c$.

(AS)^3=1,\qquad (SAS T_c)^3=1.

<!-- proposition-9: body_md -->
\label{prop:symmetric-pencil}
The base scheme of this pencil has length $36$. It consists of $24$ reduced points and six points of length two, the latter being the fixed points of $\sigma$ outside $F$. A general member $D_t$ is smooth. It has genus $28$, and $\alpha|_{D_t}:D_t\to E$ has degree $72$ and total ramification degree $54$. For a general parameter, the six fixed points outside $F$ and the three nodes contribute nine simple ramification points. The remaining ramification has degree $45$ and is arranged in $15$ free $\sigma$-orbits, counted with multiplicity. Moreover, $D_t/\langle\sigma\rangle$ has genus $8$.

The intersection number of two bicanonical divisors is $(2K_X)^2=36$. <!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:product-cover}
Let $H_0,H_1\in|2K_X|$ be general divisors meeting transversely at $36$ points, and let $\pi:Y\to X$ be the blowup of these points. The resolved pencil $h:Y\to\mathbb P^1$ and the Albanese map give a finite map
\[
f=(\alpha\pi,h):Y\longrightarrow E\times\mathbb P^1
\]
of degree $72$. If $A=E\times\{\mathrm{pt}\}$ and $B=\{\mathrm{pt}\}\times\mathbb P^1$, the ramification divisor $R$ and its pushforward branch cycle satisfy
\[
R\equiv5\pi^*K_X-\sum_{i=1}^{36}E_i,
\qquad f_*R\equiv180A+54B.
\]
Here $e(Y)=39$ and $\operatorname{sign}(Y)=-35$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
The bicanonical system is very ample \cite{BY}, so a general pair has $(2K_X)^2=36$ distinct transverse base points, all away from the three singular fibers. Put $D=\pi^*(2K_X)-\sum E_i$, the class of a fiber of $h$, and write $F$ also for the pullback of a smooth Albanese fiber. Then $f^*A=D$, $f^*B=F$, and $D\cdot F=72$. Every Albanese fiber of $X$ is irreducible \cite{CKY}. The strict transform of a smooth fiber containing $k$ base points has degree $72-k\geq36$ under $h$, since these base points are transverse and have multiplicity one. A singular fiber contains no base points and has degree $72$. Each exceptional curve maps with degree one under $h$. These are all curves contracted by $\alpha\pi$, and none is contracted by $h$. The proper map $f$ is therefore finite, with degree $D\cdot F=72$.

Since $K_{E\times\mathbb P^1}=-2A$ and $K_Y=\pi^*K_X+\sum E_i$, the ramification formula gives $R=K_Y-f^*K_{E\times\mathbb P^1}=5\pi^*K_X-\sum E_i$. By the projection formula,
\[
(f_*R)\cdot B=R\cdot F=180,\qquad
(f_*R)\cdot A=R\cdot D=5(2K_X^2)-36=54.
\]
These intersections give the asserted numerical class of the branch cycle. Finally blowing up $36$ points increases the Euler characteristic by $36$ and decreases the signature by $36$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:vanishing-span}
The homology classes of all monodromy translates of the three nodal vanishing cycles span $H_1(F;\mathbb Q)$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Let $E^\circ$ be the complement of the three critical values, and let $W\subset H_1(F;\mathbb Q)$ be the invariant span of the vanishing classes under the monodromy of $E^\circ$. Put $K=\ker(\pi_1(X)\to\pi_1(E))$. Van Kampen for the nodal fibration identifies
\[
H_1(K;\mathbb Q)\cong H_1(F;\mathbb Q)/W.
\]
This is finite dimensional, and the conjugation action of $\pi_1(E)\cong\mathbb Z^2$ on it is well defined.

We use the presentation of $\pi_1(X)$ computed by Cartwright, Koziarz, and Yeung \cite{CKY}. In their generators $a_1,a_2,a_3$, the Albanese character sends them to $(1,3)$, $(-2,1)$, and $(-1,-1)$, respectively. Let $\mathcal J(s,t)$ be the Fox matrix of their thirteen relators, with these images substituted. Direct calculation gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal J(s,t)=
\begin{cases}
1,&(s,t)=(1,1),\\
2,&(s,t)\in(\mathbb C^*)^2\setminus\{(1,1)\}.
\end{cases}
\]
Here is a short exact check of the second assertion. Number the relators as in the presentation file accompanying \cite{CKY}. Take the minors in columns $1,2$ and row pairs $(1,2),(1,3),(2,3),(1,4)$. After dividing by $st-1$ and clearing Laurent monomials, their polynomial factors have Gr\"obner basis $(s^4,t)$ in $\mathbb Q[s,t]$. Thus the rank is two when $st\ne1$. On $st=1$, the numerators of the minors in columns $1,3$ and row pairs $(1,2),(1,3)$, together with the minor in columns $2,3$ and rows $(1,2)$, have greatest common divisor $s-1$. The rank at $(1,1)$ follows by direct substitution. A script performing these calculations is included in the ancillary verification file.

For a nontrivial character $\chi$ of $\mathbb Z^2$, the degree-one boundary map of the presentation complex has rank one, so the Fox rank gives $H_1(X;\mathbb C_\chi)=0$. The homology five-term sequence for $1\to K\to\pi_1(X)\to\mathbb Z^2\to1$, with these twisted coefficients, then shows that the $\chi$-coinvariants of $H_1(K;\mathbb C)$ vanish. For the trivial character, the same sequence, together with the surjection $H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\to H_2(\pi_1(X);\mathbb Q)$, gives
\[
H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow H_2(E;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow
H_1(K;\mathbb Q)_{\mathbb Z^2}\longrightarrow H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow H_1(E;\mathbb Q).
\]
The first arrow is onto: a bicanonical curve maps to $E$ with positive degree. The last arrow is an isomorphism. Hence the ordinary coinvariants vanish as well. If $H_1(K;\mathbb C)$ were nonzero, its finite-dimensional commuting $\mathbb Z^2$-action would have a one-dimensional character quotient. This contradicts the vanishing just proved for every character. Therefore $H_1(K;\mathbb Q)=0$, as required.

The presentation of Cartwright, Koziarz, and Yeung gives generators
$a_1,a_2,a_3$ of $\pi_1(X)$ with images in
$\pi_1(E)\cong\mathbb Z^2$ given by
\[
v_1=(1,3),\qquad v_2=(-2,1),\qquad v_3=(-1,-1).
\]
Their pairwise determinants are $7,2,3$. Hence they generate
$\mathbb Z^2$, and $\alpha_*$ is onto. The kernel of the map
$\mathbb Z^3\to\mathbb Z^2$ with columns $v_1,v_2,v_3$ is generated
by $(-3,2,-7)$: this vector is primitive, and
$-3v_1+2v_2-7v_3=0$.

Let $\widetilde E\to E$ be the universal cover and
$\widetilde X=X\times_E\widetilde E$. Then
$\pi_1(\widetilde X)=K$. Over the complement of the critical values
the map $\widetilde X\to\widetilde E$ is a surface bundle. Around each
node, filling the puncture kills a peripheral lift, and the Lefschetz
thimble kills the transported vanishing cycle. Since
$\widetilde E$ is contractible, van Kampen applied over an exhaustion
by disks gives
\[
K\cong\pi_1(F)\big/
\big\langle\!\big\langle
\text{all transported nodal vanishing cycles}
\big\rangle\!\big\rangle.
\]
Thus $\pi_1(F)\to K$ is onto. Paths to the lifts of the critical values
include paths winding around the handle loops of $E$.

Let $N$ be the normal closure of the four elements in the statement.
They lie in $K$, so $N\subseteq K$. The quotient $\pi_1(X)/N$ is
abelian, hence a quotient of
$\mathbb Z^3/\langle(-3,2,-7)\rangle$. The map from this last group
to $\mathbb Z^2$ defined by $v_1,v_2,v_3$ is an isomorphism.
Therefore the map $\pi_1(X)/N\to\mathbb Z^2$ induced by $\alpha_*$
is an isomorphism. Its kernel is $K/N$, and $N=K$.
