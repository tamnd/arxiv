\label{defofdecreasing}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:countable-diff-entropy}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be compact subshifts over a finite alphabet. If
$X\triangle Y$ is at most countable, then
\[
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(Y).
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We prove that $h_{\mathrm{top}}(Y)\geq h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)$; the reverse
inequality follows by interchanging $X$ and $Y$. If
$h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)=0$, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, by the
variational principle, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is an ergodic
$\sigma$-invariant probability measure $\mu$ supported on $X$ with
$h_\mu(\sigma)>h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)-\varepsilon>0$ (after reducing
$\varepsilon$ if necessary). A positive-entropy ergodic measure is
nonatomic, and hence $\mu(X\triangle Y)=0$. Since $\mu(X)=1$, it follows
that $\mu(Y)=1$. As $Y$ is closed and shift-invariant, $\mu$ is also an
invariant measure for $\sigma|_Y$, and therefore
\[
h_{\mathrm{top}}(Y)\geq h_\mu(\sigma)>h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)-\varepsilon.
\]
Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives the desired inequality.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Take $K=1$ and $Q=(a,b)$ in Raith's notation. Suppose first that $a<b$.
The selected completion is continuous on $I\setminus(a,b)$, since any
retained critical endpoint is approached only from its selected side.
Its finite-time survivor sets, and hence $S_f(a,b)$, are compact.
Labelling the retained left and right components by $1$ and $2$ gives a
continuous itinerary map. Density of $C(f)$ makes this map injective:
two distinct points with the same itinerary would leave the interval
between them free of precritical points. Thus it conjugates the completed
survivor system to a compact subshift $X_H$.

Let $R_Y$ be Raith's compact survivor system in the split space, and let
$Y_H$ be its image under the two-branch itinerary map. This map is
continuous because the two sides of $c$ are separated. The same
expansivity argument shows that a fibre can contain only the two split
copies of a single interval point. This finite-to-one factor therefore
preserves entropy, so Raith's survivor entropy equals
$h_{\mathrm{top}}(Y_H)$.
Outside the partition and hole endpoints and their preimages, the two
systems have the same itineraries and the same hole-avoidance condition.
The exceptional points form a countable set, since each branch is
strictly monotone. Consequently, $X_H\triangle Y_H$ is at most countable,
and Lemma~\ref{lem:countable-diff-entropy} gives
\[

=h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_H)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(Y_H).
\]
For $a=b=c$, Raith's completion has the full kneading space $\Omega(f)$;

the entropy $h_{\mathrm{top}}(f)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(S_f(c,c))$.

Thus the two entropy conventions agree for all the holes under

$[c,1)$, including right-continuity at $b=c$ and at the empty hole.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{po1}
 Let \(g:S^1\to S^1\) be a topologically expansive
 continuous orientation-reversing covering of degree \(-2\).
 Then \(g\) has exactly three fixed points.
For each fixed point \(x_0\), there exists a unique
orientation-preserving homeomorphism
\(\phi:S^1\to S^1\) such that
$
\phi(x_0)=0$ and $
\phi\circ g=m_{-2}\circ\phi.$
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Since $g$ is locally orientation reversing, each fixed point is isolated
and has index $+1$. The Lefschetz number $L(g)=1-\deg(g)=3$ therefore
gives exactly three fixed points.

Fix one, $x_0$, and let $y$ be its other preimage. The counterclockwise
arcs $I_1=[x_0,y]$ and $I_2=[y,x_0]$ define itineraries
$k(x)=\epsilon_0\epsilon_1\cdots$, with $\epsilon_i=j$ when
$g^i(x)\in I_j$, using one-sided itineraries at partition boundaries.
At $x_0$ these are $(12)^\infty,(21)^\infty$, with interval addresses
$0,1$; at $y$ they are $\ell_0:=1(12)^\infty,r_0:=2(21)^\infty$,
both with address $1/2$. Further preimages have the two itineraries
$w\ell_0,wr_0$, again with equal addresses. Thus
$\phi(x):=[k(x)]$ is well defined and continuous. The counterclockwise
labeling and alternating order make $\phi$ monotone of degree $1$,
and~\eqref{eq:negative-address-shift} gives
$\phi(x_0)=0$ and $\phi\circ g=m_{-2}\circ\phi$.

Each fibre $J_z:=\phi^{-1}(z)$ is a proper closed arc or a point,
and $g(J_z)\subset J_{m_{-2}(z)}$. If $g|_{J_z}$ were not injective,
a strictly decreasing lift would give $g(J_z)=S^1$, a contradiction.
Every point of $J_{m_{-2}(z)}$ has two preimages in
$J_z\cup J_{z+1/2}$, one in each fibre by injectivity. Hence
$g:J_z\to J_{m_{-2}(z)}$ is a homeomorphism.
If $J_z$ is nontrivial and $z$ is eventually periodic, an iterate
restricts to a positively expansive homeomorphism of a nontrivial
closed interval, which is impossible. Otherwise its forward fibres
are pairwise disjoint and their lengths tend to zero; two distinct
points in a sufficiently late fibre then remain within an
expansiveness constant forever. Thus every fibre is a singleton and
$\phi$ is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism.

Any other normalized orientation-preserving conjugacy $\psi$ must
send $y$ to $1/2$ and $I_1,I_2$ to $[0,1/2],[1/2,1]$, respectively.
It therefore preserves itineraries, so $\psi(x)=[k(x)]=\phi(x)$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:address-injectivity}
Let $(k_-,k_+)$ be decreasing-admissible. Then
$0<A\leq1/2\leq B<1$, and $A=B$ if and only if
$(k_-,k_+)=(\ell_0,r_0)$. If $A<B$, the restriction of $\pi_{-2}$ to
$\Omega(k_-,k_+)$ is injective and strictly order-preserving.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The address map is non-decreasing in the alternating order, as follows by
comparing the first differing digit with the largest possible tail.
It satisfies
\[
\pi_{-2}(1\omega)=\frac{1-\pi_{-2}(\omega)}2,
\qquad
\pi_{-2}(2\omega)=\frac{2-\pi_{-2}(\omega)}2.
\]
Its unique addresses of $0$ and $1$ are $(12)^\infty$ and
$(21)^\infty$, respectively. Thus $A=0$ would make $\sigma(k_-)$ the
largest sequence, and $B=1$ would make $\sigma(k_+)$ the smallest,
contradicting $\sigma(k_-)\prec\sigma(k_+)$. The first symbols give
$A\leq1/2\leq B$, with equality $A=B$ precisely for
$(k_-,k_+)=(\ell_0,r_0)$.

To check injectivity, suppose that distinct sequences $u,v$ have the same
address.  Write \(u=w\alpha\) and \(v=w\beta\), where \(w\) is
their maximal common prefix. The prefix \(w\) acts on interval addresses
by an injective affine inverse branch \(F_w\), thus
\(\pi_{-2}(u)=\pi_{-2}(v)\) implies
\(\pi_{-2}(\alpha)=\pi_{-2}(\beta)\).  The two remaining sequences have the form
$1u'$ and $2v'$. The displayed identities then force
$\pi_{-2}(u')=0$ and $\pi_{-2}(v')=1$. Hence
$
\{u,v\}=\{w\ell_0,wr_0\}.
$
Since $\ell_0$ is the largest sequence beginning with $1$ and $r_0$ is
the smallest sequence beginning with $2$, we have
$
k_-\preceq\ell_0\prec r_0\preceq k_+.
$
When $A<B$, at least one of $\ell_0,r_0$ lies in $(k_-,k_+)$.
The shift-invariance of $\Omega(k_-,k_+)$ therefore prevents both
$w\ell_0$ and $wr_0$ from belonging to it. This proves injectivity,
and strict order preservation follows from monotonicity.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:nested}
Let \(J_0=(A,B)\) be the open arc not containing \(0\), where
\(A=[k_-]\) and \(B=[k_+]\), and suppose that \(k_-\) and \(k_+\)

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The decreasing-admissibility conditions give
\[
m_{-2}^{n}(A),\,m_{-2}^{n}(B)\notin J_0
\qquad\text{for every }n\geq0.
\]

Suppose that \(J_a\) and \(J_b\) intersect and that neither is
contained in the other. Without loss of generality, assume that
\(a<b\). The map \(m_{-2}^{a}\) sends \(J_a\) homeomorphically
onto \(J_0\), while it sends \(J_b\) onto a connected component of
\(m_{-2}^{-(b-a)}(J_0)\). Since \(J_a\) and \(J_b\) overlap but
neither contains the other, the open arc \(m_{-2}^{a}(J_b)\)
meets \(J_0\) but is not contained in \(J_0\). It must therefore
contain either \(A\) or \(B\) in its interior. Since
\(m_{-2}^{b-a}\) maps \(m_{-2}^{a}(J_b)\) homeomorphically onto
\(J_0\), the remaining \(b-a\) iterates send this endpoint into
the interior of \(J_0\), contradicting the conditions above.
Hence, whenever \(J_a\) and \(J_b\) intersect, one is contained
in the other.

Now suppose that \(J_a\cap J_b=\varnothing\). Since all the
components under consideration avoid \(0\), we may cut the circle
at \(0\) and regard them as open intervals in an ordinary interval.
Consequently, if their closures meet, then they meet at exactly
one point:
\[
\overline{J_a}\cap\overline{J_b}=\{x\}.
\]
If \(a=b\), two distinct components of \(m_{-2}^{-a}(J_0)\)
cannot share an endpoint, since that endpoint would have to be
mapped simultaneously to \(A\) and \(B\). Hence, after interchanging
\(J_a\) and \(J_b\) if necessary, we may assume that \(a<b\).

Since \(x\) is an endpoint of both components, set
\[
E:=m_{-2}^{a}(x)\in\{A,B\},
\qquad
F:=m_{-2}^{b}(x)\in\{A,B\}.
\]
Then
$
F=m_{-2}^{b-a}(E).
$
 Every shift of $k_-$ and $k_+$ belongs to
$\Omega(k_-,k_+)$. Since $A,B\in(0,1)$, a circle return to either
endpoint is also an equality of interval addresses.
Lemma~\ref{lem:address-injectivity} converts it into the corresponding
equality of symbolic sequences.
The two intervals approach the common endpoint $x$ from opposite sides.
Consequently, the iterate $m_{-2}^{b-a}$ preserves this endpoint pairing when
$b-a$ is even and reverses it when $b-a$ is odd: in the first case the two
endpoint images are the opposite endpoints of $J_0$, so $E\ne F$, whereas in
the second case they are the same endpoint, so $E=F$.
If \(a\) and \(b\) have the same parity, then \(b-a\) is even and
\(E\neq F\). Hence \(m_{-2}^{b-a}\) maps one endpoint of \(J_0\)
to the other, which is forbidden by the even part of
the decreasing-admissibility condition. If \(a\) and \(b\) have opposite parity, then
\(b-a\) is odd and \(E=F\). Hence \(m_{-2}^{b-a}\) fixes an
endpoint of \(J_0\), which is forbidden by the odd part of
the decreasing-admissibility condition. Both cases are impossible, so the closures are
disjoint.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:blow-up-completion}
Let $f\in\mathrm{EDLM}$. Identifying the endpoints of $\widetilde I$
gives a circle $\widetilde S^1$ carrying a continuous, positively
expansive, orientation-reversing covering $\widetilde f$ of degree $-2$.
In cut interval coordinates, its exterior branches $\widetilde f_-$
and $\widetilde f_+$ satisfy

\pi\circ\widetilde f_\pm=f\circ\pi
\quad\text{on their respective domains outside }J_0,
\]
with endpoint values
\[
\pi\bigl(\widetilde f_-(e_-)\bigr)=f(c-),\qquad
\pi\bigl(\widetilde f_+(e_+)\bigr)=f(c+).

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
First suppose that $(k_-,k_+)\ne(\ell_0,r_0)$.
The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:necessity} shows that every realized
itinerary and each of its shifts avoids $(k_-,k_+)$. In the nonfull

say of length $p$. Each point of $C_k$ is determined by its length-$k$

we therefore have
\[
\#C_k\leq(2^p-1)^q2^r.
\]
Thus any $\max\{1,(2^p-1)^{1/p}\}<\gamma<2$ makes $L$ finite.
Since $C(f)$ is dense, the cumulative inserted lengths give the
ordered interval and continuous collapse described above; the
complement of the interiors of the inserted intervals has length zero.

$J_{f(x)}$. These maps extend by order to the two exterior branches.
For an inserted target fibre $J_y$ lying in the image of one exterior
branch, the inverse branch gives a unique $x$ with $f(x)=y$; if
$y\in C_j$, then $x\in C_{j+1}$. Thus the inserted fibres meeting a
domain interval $[u,v]$ in that branch correspond bijectively, in
reverse order, to the inserted fibres meeting
$[\widetilde f(v),\widetilde f(u)]$. At $e_-$ use the right endpoint
of the fibre over $f(c-)$, and at $e_+$ the left endpoint of the
fibre over $f(c+)$; this includes partial endpoint fibres and gives
the prescribed one-sided values continuously.

the image interval has no additional positive-length part. Since each
fibre $J_x$ is mapped affinely onto $J_{f(x)}$, including its partial
endpoint pieces, for every $u<v$ on one exterior branch we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\widetilde f(v)-\widetilde f(u)\right|
&=\sum_{x\in C(f)\setminus\{c\}}
  \left|\widetilde f\bigl([u,v]\cap J_x\bigr)\right|\\
&=\gamma\sum_{x\in C(f)\setminus\{c\}}
  \left|[u,v]\cap J_x\right|
=\gamma(v-u).
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently, both exterior branches are affine with slope
$-\gamma$. Since $f(0)=1$ and $f(1)=0$, they are
\[
\widetilde f_-(t)=L-\gamma t,\qquad
\widetilde f_+(t)=\gamma(L-t).
\]

After identifying $0$ and $L$, take the lift $F$ equal to
$\widetilde f_-$ on $[0,e_-]$ and to $\widetilde f_+-L$ on $[e_+,L]$,
and interpolate affinely across $J_0$. Its absolute slope on $J_0$ is
\[
s=2L-\gamma(L-1)=\gamma+(2-\gamma)L>\gamma>1.
\]
Thus $F(0)=L$, $F(L)=-L$, and extension by
$F(t+L)=F(t)-2L$ gives a continuous strictly decreasing lift of degree
$-2$. All its pieces expand lengths by a factor at least $\gamma>1$,
so the induced circle covering is positively expansive.

full branches and the required exterior branch correspondence.
The full inverse-cylinder partitions have mesh tending to zero,
because their boundaries include the dense precritical set of $f$.
Matching these ordered partitions with those of $m_{-2}$ gives an
orientation-preserving conjugacy, hence positive expansivity.

\label{lem:dlm-realization}
Let
\[
k_-\in1\{1,2\}^{\mathbb N},\qquad k_+\in2\{1,2\}^{\mathbb N}.
\]
Suppose that $(k_-,k_+)$ is weak-admissible and
$\sigma(k_-)\prec\sigma(k_+).$
Then there exists a decreasing Lorenz map
\(g\in\mathrm{DLM}\) whose kneading invariants are $(k_-,k_+)$ and whose

$
\Omega(g)=\Omega(k_-,k_+).
$

Put $\Omega=\Omega(k_-,k_+)$. This is a compact shift-invariant space
whose topology agrees with the alternating order, with least and greatest
elements $e_0=(12)^\infty$ and $e_1=(21)^\infty$. Write
$\Omega_i=\{\omega\in\Omega:\omega_1=i\}$. Weak-admissibility gives
$\max\Omega_1=k_-$ and $\min\Omega_2=k_+$, so $k_-,k_+$ are adjacent.

\noindent\textup{(i)}
Choose a countable dense set $D_0\subseteq\Omega$ containing
$e_0,e_1,k_-,k_+$ and both members of every adjacent pair. Such pairs
are countable: each is determined by its maximal common finite prefix
as the facing endpoints of the corresponding symbol cylinders. Set
\[
D=\bigcup_{m,n\geq0}\sigma^{-m}\bigl(\sigma^n(D_0)\bigr),
\]
with all preimages taken in $\Omega$. Since the shift has at most two
preimages, $D$ is countable; it is closed under shifts and allowed
preimages, so $\omega\in D\Longleftrightarrow\sigma(\omega)\in D$.
Choose a probability measure $\mu$ concentrated on $D$, with positive
mass at every element of $D$, and set
\[
L(\omega)=\mu\{\nu\in\Omega:\nu\prec\omega\},\qquad
R(\omega)=\mu\{\nu\in\Omega:\nu\preceq\omega\},\qquad
I_\omega=[L(\omega),R(\omega)].
\]
For $x\in(0,1)$, compactness and right-continuity of $R$ give a least
$\omega_x$ with $R(\omega_x)\geq x$. If $\omega_x\ne e_0$, minimality gives
$
L(\omega_x)=\sup_{\nu\prec\omega_x}R(\nu)\leq x\leq R(\omega_x);
$
if $\omega_x=e_0$, the left inequality follows from $L(e_0)=0$.
Thus $x\in I_{\omega_x}$, and $0\in I_{e_0}$, $1\in I_{e_1}$ prove coverage
of $[0,1]$. For $\omega\prec\nu$, density of $D$ gives
$R(\omega)\leq L(\nu)$, with equality exactly when the labels are adjacent.

adjacent endpoints; they are nondegenerate exactly for $\omega\in D$.

\[

\bigcup_{\omega\in\Omega_1}I_\omega=[0,c],\quad
\bigcup_{\omega\in\Omega_2}I_\omega=[c,1],

$2\eta\in\Omega_2\Longleftrightarrow2\eta\succeq k_+$.

\[
\sigma(\Omega_1)=\{\eta\in\Omega:\eta\succeq\sigma(k_-)\},\qquad
\sigma(\Omega_2)=\{\eta\in\Omega:\eta\preceq\sigma(k_+)\}.

these pieces form strictly decreasing bijections
\[
G_1:[0,c]\longrightarrow[L(\sigma(k_-)),1],\qquad
G_2:[c,1]\longrightarrow[0,R(\sigma(k_+))],
\]
which are homeomorphisms because their domains and ranges are compact

Since $\sigma(e_0)=e_1$ and $\sigma(e_1)=e_0$, we have $g(0)=1$, $g(1)=0$.

g(c-)=L(\sigma(k_-))<R(\sigma(k_+))=g(c+).
\]
Thus $g\in\mathrm{DLM}$.

branch-limit identities at $c$, these inequalities hold for every shift.
Hence every realized itinerary belongs to $\Omega$, proving
$\Omega(g)=\Omega(k_-,k_+)$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lelele11}
Let $T_{-2}$ have a hole $(a,b)$ with $0\leq a\leq 1/2$, and keep
$a$ fixed while $b$ varies in $(1/2,1)$. Let $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}=(v_1v_2\cdots)$ be the

    \item if $n$ is even, $\mathbf{b}'\succ\mathbf{b}$ and $\Omega(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = \Omega(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}')$;
    \item if $n$ is odd, $\mathbf{b}'\prec\mathbf{b}$ and $\Omega(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}') \setminus \Omega(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b})$ is countable.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
The proof is independent of the numerical value of $a$.  Indeed, every
lower boundary sequence begins with $1$, whereas the first violating shift
$\sigma^n(\mathbf{b})$ begins with $2$.  Thus this violating shift lies above the
fixed lower boundary and enters the forbidden interval $(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$ for every
fixed $a\leq 1/2$.  The remaining comparisons use only the common prefix
$v_1\cdots v_n$, its parity, and the fact that $\mathbf{a}$ is kept fixed.
\par
\rm (1) We proceed by contradiction. Suppose $\mathbf{b}'$ is not self-admissible. Then
there exists a minimal $r<n$ such that $\sigma^r(\mathbf{b}')\prec\mathbf{b}'$, which means
$
(v_{r+1}\cdots v_n v_1\cdots v_r)  \prec (v_1\cdots v_n).
$ and $v_{r+1}=2$.
If $v_{r+1}\cdots v_n \prec v_1\cdots v_{n-r}$, then comparing the
original sequence $\mathbf{b}$ at the same shift gives $\sigma^r(\mathbf{b})\prec\mathbf{b}$,
contradicting the minimality of $n$. Hence we must have
$v_{r+1}\cdots v_n = v_1\cdots v_{n-r}$, and we split the proof into
two cases based on the parity of $r$. Here we only give the proof for odd
$n$; the case of even  $n$ is entirely analogous.

\emph{Case 1: $r$ is even.} Then $n-r$ is odd (since $n$ is odd).
By the equality $v_{r+1}\cdots v_n = v_1\cdots v_{n-r}$ and the parity
condition, we obtain $v_1\cdots v_r \succ v_{n-r+1}\cdots v_n$.
If $v_{n-r+1}=2$, then $\sigma^{n-r}(\mathbf{b})\prec\mathbf{b}$, contradicting the
minimality of $n$. If $v_{n-r+1}=1$, then using the structure of $\mathbf{b}$ and $v_{n+1}=2$,
we get $\sigma^r(\mathbf{b})=(v_{r+1}\cdots v_{n}v_{n+1}\cdots)\prec\mathbf{b}=(v_1\cdots v_{n-r}v_{n-r+1}\cdots)$, again a contradiction.

\emph{Case 2: $r$ is odd.} Then $n-r$ is even, giving
$v_1\cdots v_r \prec v_{n-r+1}\cdots v_n$. By the assumption that $\sigma^n(\mathbf{b}) \prec \mathbf{b}$, we have $v_{n+1}\cdots v_{n+r}
\preceq v_1\cdots v_r$.
Consequently,
\[
v_{r+1}\cdots v_n v_{n+1}\cdots v_{n+r}
\preceq v_1\cdots v_{n-r}v_1\cdots v_r
\prec v_1\cdots v_{n-r}v_{n-r+1}\cdots v_n,
\]
which yields $\sigma^r(\mathbf{b})\prec\mathbf{b}$, contradicting the minimality of $n$. Thus $\mathbf{b}'$ must be self-admissible.

\par (2) Let $n$ be even. We first prove that $\mathbf{b}'\succ\mathbf{b}$.
Write $\mathbf{b} = v_1\cdots v_n \sigma^n(\mathbf{b})$. Since $\mathbf{b}' = (v_1\cdots v_n)^\infty$,
both sequences agree on the first $n$ symbols. Let $k\ge 1$ be the smallest
integer such that the $k$-th blocks of length $n$ differ, i.e.,
\[
v_{(k-1)n+1}\cdots v_{kn} \neq v_1\cdots v_n
\quad\text{for } \mathbf{b}' \text{ and } \mathbf{b}.
\]
Such a $k$ exists because $\mathbf{b}'\neq\mathbf{b}$. The condition
$\sigma^n(\mathbf{b})\prec\mathbf{b}$ together with the minimality of $k$ implies that
the $k$-th block of $\mathbf{b}$ is strictly smaller than the corresponding block
of $\mathbf{b}'$ in the alternating order. Since $n$ is even, comparing sequences
block-by-block is compatible with the alternating lexicographic order,
and we obtain $\mathbf{b}'\succ\mathbf{b}$.
\par Next we prove that $\Omega(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = \Omega(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}')$ when $n$ is even.
Since $\mathbf{b}'\succ\mathbf{b}$, clearly $\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}')\subseteq\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$.
For the reverse inclusion, suppose that
$w\in\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\setminus\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}')$. Let $k\geq0$ be the
first time such that
$
u:=\sigma^k(w)\in[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}').
$
Put
\[
p:=v_1\cdots v_n,\qquad T:=\sigma^n,\qquad
q:=T(\mathbf{b})=\sigma^n(\mathbf{b}).
\]
Since $n$ is even, deleting the common prefix $p$ preserves the
alternating order. Moreover, $q\prec\mathbf{b}$ and $T(\mathbf{b}')=\mathbf{b}'$. Every
sequence in $[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}')$ has the prefix $p$, and hence
\[
q=T(\mathbf{b})\preceq T(u)\prec T(\mathbf{b}')=\mathbf{b}'.
\]

We claim that some iterate $T^j(u)$ belongs to $(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$. If
$T(u)\prec\mathbf{b}$, then $T(u)\succeq q$. Since $q$ starts with $2$,
$T(u)$ also starts with $2$, whereas $\mathbf{a}$ starts with $1$. Therefore
$
\mathbf{a}\prec T(u)\prec\mathbf{b}.
$
If $T(u)\succeq\mathbf{b}$, then $T(u)\in[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}')$, and the same argument
can be repeated.

Suppose that no iterate enters $(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$. Then
$
T^j(u)\in[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}')$ for every $j\geq0.
$
Each $T^j(u)$ begins with $p$. Thus every block of $u$ of length $n$ is
equal to $p$, and consequently
$
u=p^\infty=\mathbf{b}',
$
which contradicts $u\in[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}')$. Hence there exists $j\geq1$ such
that $T^j(u)\in(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$. It follows that
$
\sigma^{k+jn}(w)=T^j(u)\in(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}),
$
contradicting $w\in\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$. Therefore
$
\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\subseteq\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}').
$
Together with the inclusion proved above, this gives
$
\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}').
$
\par (3) Let $n$ be odd.  As in the proof of (2), we verify that
$\mathbf{b}'\prec\mathbf{b}$, and hence

\(w\in\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}')\setminus\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\). There exists a minimal $k\ge 0$
such that $\sigma^k(w)\in[\mathbf{b}',\mathbf{b})$, and set $u=\sigma^k(w)$. If
$u=\mathbf{b}'$, then $w$ is a preimage of $\mathbf{b}'$. Otherwise,
$\mathbf{b}'\prec u\prec\mathbf{b}$. As $\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}'$ share the prefix $v_1\cdots v_n$ and
$u$ lies strictly between them, $u$ also begins with $v_1\cdots v_n$.
Since $n$ is odd, $\sigma^n$ reverses the alternating order on sequences
with a common prefix of length $n$. Applying $\sigma^n$ to
$\mathbf{b}'\prec u\prec\mathbf{b}$ yields
\[
\mathbf{b}'=\sigma^n(\mathbf{b}')\succ\sigma^n(u)\succ\sigma^n(\mathbf{b}),
\]
which indicates that

\label{lem:self-admissible-adjust}
Let $\mathbf{a}\in1\{1,2\}^{\mathbb N}$ and
$\mathbf{b}=(v_1v_2\cdots)\in2\{1,2\}^{\mathbb N}$ be self-admissible.
Suppose that there exists a least $n\ge1$ such that
$\sigma^n(\mathbf{b})\succ\mathbf{a}$ and $v_{n+1}=1$, and put
$p=v_1\cdots v_n$ and $\mathbf{v}'=p^\infty$. Then $\mathbf{v}'$ is self-admissible.
Moreover, the following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{it:self-adjust-even} If $n$ is even, then $\mathbf{v}'\succ\mathbf{b}$ and
$
\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}')=\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}).
$
\item\label{it:self-adjust-odd} If $n$ is odd, then $\mathbf{v}'\prec\mathbf{b}$,
$\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\subseteq\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}')$, and
\[
\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}')\setminus\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
=\bigl\{\omega\in\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}'):\
\sigma^k(\omega)=\mathbf{v}'\text{ for some }k\ge0\bigr\}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, $\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}')\triangle\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$ is at most
countable in either case.

The self-admissibility of $\mathbf{v}'$ follows from the same periodization
argument as in Lemma~\ref{lelele11}; we record the short adaptation.
Assume that $\mathbf{v}'$ is not self-admissible and choose the least
$1\le r<n$ such that $\sigma^r(\mathbf{v}')$ starts with $2$ and
$\sigma^r(\mathbf{v}')\prec\mathbf{v}'$. If the comparison is decided before the
common block $v_1\cdots v_{n-r}$ is exhausted, the same comparison for
$\mathbf{b}$ gives $\sigma^r(\mathbf{b})\prec\mathbf{b}$, contradicting the
self-admissibility of $\mathbf{b}$. Otherwise,
$v_{r+1}\cdots v_n=v_1\cdots v_{n-r}$. If $n-r$ is odd, the remaining
comparison gives $\sigma^{n-r}(\mathbf{b})\prec\mathbf{b}$ when
$v_{n-r+1}=2$. When $v_{n-r+1}=1$, the assumptions give
\[
\sigma^r(\mathbf{b})\prec v_{r+1}\cdots v_n\mathbf{a}
=v_1\cdots v_{n-r}\mathbf{a}
\preceq v_1\cdots v_{n-r}\sigma^{n-r}(\mathbf{b})=\mathbf{b},
\]
where $\sigma^{n-r}(\mathbf{b})\preceq\mathbf{a}$ follows from the minimality of $n$.
If $n-r$ is even, the comparison forces $v_{n-r+1}=2$ and directly gives
$\sigma^r(\mathbf{b})\prec\mathbf{b}$.
Every alternative contradicts the self-admissibility of $\mathbf{b}$.
Therefore $\mathbf{v}'$ is self-admissible.

For the survivor spaces, put $\mathbf q=\sigma^n(\mathbf{b})$. Then
\[
\mathbf{b}=p\mathbf q,\qquad \mathbf{v}'=p\mathbf{v}',\qquad
\mathbf{a}\prec\mathbf q\prec\mathbf{v}',
\]
where the last inequality holds because $\mathbf q$ starts with $1$
and $\mathbf{v}'$ starts with $2$. Deleting the common prefix $p$ preserves
the alternating order when $n$ is even and reverses it when $n$ is odd.
Thus $\mathbf{v}'\succ\mathbf{b}$ in the even case and $\mathbf{v}'\prec\mathbf{b}$ in the odd case.

$\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}')\subseteq\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$. If
$\omega\in\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\setminus\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}')$, some shift
$u$ lies in $[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{v}')$. Hence $u$ begins with $p$ and
$\mathbf q\preceq\sigma^n(u)\prec\mathbf{v}'$. If
$\sigma^n(u)\prec\mathbf{b}$, then $\sigma^n(u)\in(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$, since
$\mathbf q\succ\mathbf{a}$, contradicting survival. Otherwise
$\sigma^n(u)\in[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{v}')$, and iteration gives the same conclusion
for every $n$-shift of $u$. Every aligned length-$n$ block of $u$ is
therefore $p$, so $u=p^\infty=\mathbf{v}'$, contradicting $u\prec\mathbf{v}'$. Thus
$\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}')=\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$.

\par\emph{Odd $n$.} Monotonicity gives
$\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\subseteq\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}')$. If
$\omega\in\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}')\setminus\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$, some shift
$u$ lies in $[\mathbf{v}',\mathbf{b})$. Unless $u=\mathbf{v}'$, deleting the common

$\mathbf q\prec\sigma^n(u)\prec\mathbf{v}'$, hence
$\sigma^n(u)\in(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}')$, a contradiction. Thus every such $\omega$
is a shift-preimage of $\mathbf{v}'$. Conversely, every surviving
shift-preimage of $\mathbf{v}'$ fails for $(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$ because
$\mathbf{a}\prec\mathbf{v}'\prec\mathbf{b}$. Therefore
\[
\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}')\setminus\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
=\bigl\{\omega\in\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}'):
\sigma^k(\omega)=\mathbf{v}'\text{ for some }k\ge0\bigr\},
\]
and this set is at most countable.

\label{newlemma1}
Let $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ be self-admissible.
Then there exists a weak-admissible pair $(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})$ such that
$\Omega(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\triangle \Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$ is at most countable.

<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Start with $(\mathbf{a}_0,\mathbf{b}_0)=(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$. Whenever the current right
boundary fails to survive, choose its least violating time $n$.
Self-admissibility forces $\sigma^n(\mathbf{b}_j)$ to start with $1$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:self-admissible-adjust} replaces the pair by
\[
\mathbf{a}_{j+1}=\mathbf{a}_j,\qquad \mathbf{b}_{j+1}=(\mathbf{b}_j|_n)^\infty.
\]
Repeat with the left boundary fixed until the right boundary survives.
If the left boundary still fails to survive, adjust it by the symmetric
lemma, keeping the right one fixed and returning to right adjustment
whenever it ceases to survive. Stop when both boundaries survive.

Each adjustment preserves self-admissibility and changes the survivor
space by at most a countable set. The procedure is finite: the first
adjustment of either boundary makes it periodic, and a subsequent
adjustment of a boundary of least period $q$ occurs at a least violating
time $1\leq r<q$. Otherwise periodicity gives an earlier violation
modulo $q$; a multiple of $q$ returns the boundary itself and cannot
be a violating time. Its new period is at most $r<q$, so each boundary can be
adjusted only finitely often, even when the adjustments alternate.
The final pair $(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})$ is weak-admissible, and the finite union
of the successive symmetric differences is countable. If every
adjustment occurs at an even first-violating time,
Lemma~\ref{lem:self-admissible-adjust}\textup{(\ref{it:self-adjust-even})}
and its symmetric counterpart give the stronger equality
$\Omega(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$.

\label{prop:boundary-return-plateaux}

L=
\begin{cases}
w\mathbf{a},&k\text{ even},\\
w^\infty,&k\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}

$\eta\succ\mathbf{b}$ excludes $\mathbf{b}$, so $P(\mathbf{b})$ cannot extend to the
right of $\mathbf{b}$.

Since $\mathbf{a}\prec L\prec\mathbf{b}$, monotonicity gives
$S\subseteq\Omega(\mathbf{a},L)$. If $k$ is even, then
$\sigma^k(L)=\mathbf{a}$ and all later shifts are shifts of $\mathbf{a}$;
if $k$ is odd, then $\sigma^k(L)=L$. It remains to check
$1\leq j<k$. Let $w=\mathbf{b}|_k=w_1\cdots w_k$.
For such $j$, the sequences $\sigma^j(L)$ and
$\sigma^j(\mathbf{b})$ share the prefix $w_{j+1}\cdots w_k$. Define
\[
J_j=
\begin{cases}
\bigl(\sigma^j(L),\sigma^j(\mathbf{b})\bigr],&j\text{ even},\\
\bigl[\sigma^j(\mathbf{b}),\sigma^j(L)\bigr),&j\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
The common-prefix cylinder implies that every
$x\in J_j$ has this prefix. Deleting it gives
\[
\sigma^{k-j}(x)\in
\begin{cases}
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}],&k\text{ even},\\
[\mathbf{a},L),&k\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

force $\sigma^{k-j}(x)=\sigma^k(\mathbf{b})$. On the other hand, both
$x$ and $\sigma^j(\mathbf{b})$ begin with the common word
$w_{j+1}\cdots w_k$. After this common prefix is deleted, the tail
of $x$ is $\sigma^{k-j}(x)=\sigma^k(\mathbf{b})$, while the tail of
$\sigma^j(\mathbf{b})$ is also $\sigma^k(\mathbf{b})$. Hence the two sequences have
the same prefix and the same tail, so
\[
x=(w_{j+1}\cdots w_k)\sigma^k(\mathbf{b})=\sigma^j(\mathbf{b}).
\]
Therefore $J_j\cap S=\{\sigma^j(\mathbf{b})\}$.

give $h_{\mathrm{top}}(g)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(S_f(a,b))$, including the
critical-endpoint and empty-hole cases.
If no adjustment is needed, the realization is exact:
$\Omega(g)=\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$.

<!-- proposition-13: body_md -->
\label{prop:edlm-null}
Fix \(f\in\mathrm{EDLM}\) and {\(0<a\le c\)}. Let \(E_f(a)\)
denote the set of {\(b\in[c,1)\) at which the map
\(t\mapsto S_f(a,t)\) is not locally constant relative to \([c,1)\)}.
Define \(E_{-2}(m)\) analogously as the bifurcation set of
\(T_{-2}\) {for right endpoints in \([1/2,1)\)}, with fixed
left endpoint $m:=\pi_{-2}\bigl(\tau_f(a-)\bigr)$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_f(a)&=\{b\in[c,1):b\in S_f(a,b)\}\\
&\subseteq C(f)\cup\Phi_f^{-1}\bigl(E_{-2}(m)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(C(f)\) is the countable set consisting of the critical point
and its preimages, as in Definition~\ref{defofdecreasing}.
In particular, if the pullback condition
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{Leb}\!\left(
\Phi_f^{-1}\bigl(E_{-2}(m)\bigr)\right)=0
\tag{P}\label{eq:pullback-null}
\end{equation*}
holds, then \(\operatorname{Leb}(E_f(a))=0\).

<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
{
We first prove the characterization relative to the parameter interval
\([c,1)\). Suppose \(b\in S_f(a,b)\). If \(a<b\), then for every
\(t>b\) sufficiently close to \(b\), the point \(b\) belongs to the
hole \((a,t)\), and hence \(b\notin S_f(a,t)\). Thus \(b\in E_f(a)\).
In particular, when \(b=c>a\) and \(c\in S_f(a,c)\), the same argument
applies with the right-completed convention at \(c\). If \(a=b=c\),
Remark~\ref{re-edlm-empty-hole} gives
\(S_f(c,c)=[0,1]\), whereas \(S_f(c,t)\) omits the nonempty interval
\((c,t)\) for every \(t>c\); hence \(c\in E_f(c)\).

Conversely, suppose \(b\notin S_f(a,b)\). We first record a
finite-escape observation. For a nonempty hole \(J=(u,v)\) with
\(u\le c\le v\), put \(D_J=I\setminus J\), using the selected
one-sided completion if \(c\) is an endpoint of \(J\).
The resulting restriction \(f:D_J\to I\) is continuous: either
\(c\notin D_J\), or \(D_J\) approaches \(c\) only from the selected
side. Define the finite-time survivor sets by
\[
K_0(J)=D_J,\qquad
K_{n+1}(J)=\{x\in D_J:f(x)\in K_n(J)\}.
\]
These sets are compact by induction, and \(K_n(J)\) consists exactly
of the points avoiding \(J\) for times \(0,\ldots,n\).
Consequently, if a point escapes by time \(r\), a neighbourhood of
that point is disjoint from \(K_r(J)\). Nearby points therefore also
escape by time \(r\), although their first escape times may differ.

First let \(b>c\), and let \(r\ge1\) be the first time at which
the completed orbit of \(b\) enters \((a,b)\). Choose
\[
\max\{c,f^r(b)\}<d<b,\qquad J=(a,d).
\]
The orbit of \(b\) also enters \(J\) at time \(r\), so
\(b\notin K_r(J)\). Choose \(\delta>0\) such that
\[
U=(b-\delta,b+\delta)\subset(d,1)\setminus K_r(J),
\]
and set \(V=(b-\delta/2,b+\delta/2)\).
This argument also covers \(a=c\) when the orbit reaches \(c\)
before escaping: a nearby orbit crossing to the right of \(c\)
enters \(J\), while one remaining on the left follows the continuous
left branch. No continuity of the full iterate \(f^r\) across these
two possibilities is required.
For every \(t\in V\), we have \(J\subset(a,b)\cap(a,t)\) and
\[
(a,b)\triangle(a,t)
\subseteq[\min\{b,t\},\max\{b,t\}]\subset U.
\]
Every point of \(U\) escapes into the common hole \(J\) by time
\(r\). Thus every orbit surviving either \((a,b)\) or \((a,t)\)
avoids \(U\). The holes coincide outside \(U\), and their critical
endpoint conventions agree, so
\[
S_f(a,t)=S_f(a,b),\qquad t\in V.
\]
Thus \(b\notin E_f(a)\).

It remains to consider \(b=c\). The case \(c\in S_f(a,c)\) was
handled in the first part of the proof. If \(c\notin S_f(a,c)\),
then \(a<c\). Put \(J=(a,c)\) and let \(r\ge1\) be the first
escape time of the right-completed orbit of \(c\). Since
\(c\notin K_r(J)\), choose \(\delta>0\) with \(c+\delta<1\)
such that \(U=[c,c+\delta)\) is disjoint from \(K_r(J)\).
Every point of \(U\) escapes into \(J\) by time \(r\) under the
right completion. For \(c<t<c+\delta/2\), its orbit either follows
this escape itinerary or first reaches \(c\), which already belongs
to the enlarged hole \((a,t)\). Thus survivors of either hole avoid
\(U\). Since the holes coincide outside \(U\),
\(S_f(a,t)=S_f(a,c)\) for \(t\in[c,c+\delta/2)\).
Hence \(c\notin E_f(a)\).
Therefore
\[
E_f(a)=\{b\in[c,1):b\in S_f(a,b)\}.
\]}

Now let \(b\in E_f(a)\setminus C(f)\). By the characterization
just proved, \(b\in S_f(a,b)\), and its orbit avoids \(c\).
Its itinerary \(\mathbf{b}=\tau_f(b)=\tau_f(b+)\) is therefore unique and,
by order preservation along the surviving orbit, belongs to
\(\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\). Put
$
\epsilon:=\Phi_f(b)=\pi_{-2}\bigl(\tau_f(b+)\bigr).
$
Monotonicity of the address map implies that the addresses of all
shifts of \(\mathbf{b}\) avoid \((m,\epsilon)\).
No such shift can equal \(\ell_0=1(12)^\infty\) or
\(r_0=2(21)^\infty\): the kneading bounds
\(k_-\preceq\ell_0\prec r_0\preceq k_+\) would force it to equal
\(k_-\) or \(k_+\), respectively. By strict order preservation of
itineraries at distinct points, this would make the orbit of \(b\)
reach \(c\), contrary to \(b\notin C(f)\).
Thus the address orbit avoids \(1/2\), and the address map intertwines
all its iterates with \(T_{-2}\). Consequently,
\(\epsilon\in S_{-2}(m,\epsilon)\).
Applying the self-survival characterization just proved to
\(T_{-2}\), with left boundary \(m\), we obtain
$
\epsilon\in E_{-2}(m).
$
Hence
\[
E_f(a)\subseteq C(f)\cup\Phi_f^{-1}\bigl(E_{-2}(m)\bigr).
\]
The exceptional set is needed because, at a precritical parameter,
the upper sequence used by \(\Phi_f\) need not be the sequence selected
by the surviving critical-endpoint completion. Each first-preimage
level of \(c\) is finite, so \(C(f)\) is countable.
Condition~(P) therefore implies \(\operatorname{Leb}(E_f(a))=0\).

<!-- example-5: body_md -->
\label{ex:edlm-linear-pullback}
Fix $1<\beta_1,\beta_2\leq2$ and set $c=1/2$. Define
\[
f(x)=f_{\beta_1,\beta_2}(x):=
\begin{cases}
1-\beta_1x,&0\le x<c,\\
\beta_2(1-x),&c<x\le1.
\end{cases}
\]
The slopes are $-\beta_1$ and $-\beta_2$, and
$f(0)=1$, $f(1)=0$. The critical values are
\[
f(c-)=1-\frac{\beta_1}{2},\qquad
f(c+)=\frac{\beta_2}{2},
\]
which lie in $[0,1]$ and give an upward jump of size
$(\beta_1+\beta_2-2)/2>0$.
Put $\lambda=\min\{\beta_1,\beta_2\}>1$.
If a nonempty open interval $J$ avoided all preimages of $c$, every
iterate would be affine on $J$, with
\[
1\geq |f^n(J)|\geq\lambda^n|J|
\]
for all $n$, a contradiction. Thus the precritical set is dense and
$f\in\mathrm{EDLM}$.

Fix $0<a<c$, and put
\[
\mathbf{a}=\tau_f(a-),\qquad
m=\pi_{-2}(\mathbf{a}),\qquad
\Phi(b)=\pi_{-2}\bigl(\tau_f(b+)\bigr),\quad c\le b<1.
\]
Here $m=m_{\beta_1,\beta_2}$ and $\Phi=\Phi_{\beta_1,\beta_2}$.
We verify condition~\eqref{eq:pullback-null} directly.

First, every fixed hole $H=(a,b)$ with $a<c<b<1$ has a
Lebesgue-null survivor set. To prove this, put
$S=S_f(a,b)$ and $\delta=\min\{c-a,b-c\}>0$.
If $\operatorname{Leb}(S)>0$, choose a density point
$x\in S\cap(0,1)$ and an arbitrarily small open interval
$J\subset(0,1)$ centred at $x$.
Let $n$ be the first time that $f^n(J)$ contains $c$;
such a time exists  by topological expansivity.
Since $f^j(J)$ avoids $c$ for $0\leq j<n$, the map
$f^n$ is affine and injective on $J$. Its slope is
$(-1)^n\beta_1^{n_1}\beta_2^{n_2}$, where $n_i$ counts the visits
to branch $i$ before time $n$, and $n_1+n_2=n$.
Its image $I=f^n(J)$ contains both $c$ and
$f^n(x)\notin(a,b)$. If $f^n(x)\leq a$, then
$(a,c)\subset I$; otherwise $f^n(x)\geq b$ and
$(c,b)\subset I$. Hence $|I\cap H|\geq\delta$.
Every point of $J$ whose $n$th image lies in $H$ escapes.
Because $f^n$ is affine on $J$ and $|I|\leq1$,
\[
\frac{|J\setminus S|}{|J|}
\geq\frac{|I\cap H|}{|I|}
\geq\delta.
\]
This contradicts the density-point property as $J$ shrinks to $x$,
so $\operatorname{Leb}(S)=0$.

The single parameter $b=c$ has Lebesgue measure zero. Now let
$b\in\Phi^{-1}(E_{-2}(m))\setminus\{c\}$, and write
$\epsilon=\Phi(b)$ and $\mathbf{b}=\tau_f(b+)$.
The self-survival characterization in
Proposition~\ref{prop:edlm-null}, applied to $T_{-2}$, gives
$\epsilon\in S_{-2}(m,\epsilon)$.
Here we use only that characterization, which does not assume (P).
Since this survivor orbit avoids $1/2$, its negative-doubling
itinerary is unique and equals $\mathbf{b}$, as $\pi_{-2}(\mathbf{b})=\epsilon$.
By Proposition~\ref{proptwoholes}, $\mathbf{b}\in\Omega(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$
and there is a point $x\in S_f(a,b)$ with itinerary $\mathbf{b}$.
Strict order preservation of one-sided itineraries at distinct
points forces $x=b$.

Finally, set $d_N=c+(1-c)/(N+1)$ for $N\geq1$.
For each such $b>c$, some $N$ satisfies $d_N<b$.
Since $(a,d_N)\subset(a,b)$, the preceding argument gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-bifurcation-transfer}
\Phi^{-1}\bigl(E_{-2}(m)\bigr)
\subseteq\{c\}\cup\bigcup_{N\geq1}S_f(a,d_N).
\end{equation}
The singleton and all the survivor sets on the right are null by the
preceding arguments.
Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{Leb}\!\left(
\Phi_{\beta_1,\beta_2}^{-1}
\bigl(E_{-2}(m_{\beta_1,\beta_2})\bigr)\right)=0,
\]
which is condition~\eqref{eq:pullback-null}.
Thus Corollary~\ref{coco3} applies for every $1<\beta_1,\beta_2\leq2$
and every $0<a<1/2$.
