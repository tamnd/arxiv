<!-- campanaconj-1: body_md -->
Let $(X,D)$ be a smooth projective Campana orbifold over a number
field $k$, with simple normal crossings boundary and finite integral
orbifold multiplicities. Then $(X_{\overline{k}},D_{\overline{k}})$ is special if
and only if there exist a finite extension $k'/k$, a finite set
$S'\supseteq\Omega_{k'}^\infty$, and a proper integral model over
$\mathcal O_{S'}$ with Zariski-dense $S'$-Campana points in $X_{k'}$.
<!-- end campanaconj-1 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:line}
Let $P_i$ be distinct closed points of $\mathbf P^1_k$, and let
\[
 D=\sum_{i\in I}\left(1-\frac1{n_i}\right)P_i,
 \qquad 2\leq n_i<\infty.
\]
Suppose that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-fano-degree}
 \sum_{i\in I}\left(1-\frac1{n_i}\right)[k(P_i):k]<2.
\end{equation}
Then $(\mathbf P^1_k,D)$ satisfies Campana weak approximation for every
fixed finite set $S\supseteq\Omega_k^\infty$ and every fixed proper
flat integral model. More precisely,
global $S$-Campana points outside $\operatorname{Supp} D$ are dense in the product
of the local Campana spaces.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:hyperplanes}
Let $n\geq2$, and let $E_1,\ldots,E_{n+3}\subset\mathbf P^n_k$ be
distinct $k$-hyperplanes in general position. Set
\[
 D=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{n+3}E_i,\qquad
 U=\mathbf P^n_k\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n+3}E_i.
\]
Fix a finite set $S\supseteq\Omega_k^\infty$, and let $\mathscr E_i$ be
the closure of $E_i$ in $\mathbf P^n_{\mathcal O_S}$. Then the
$S$-Campana points in $U(k)$ are dense in the product of the local
Campana spaces. In particular, $(\mathbf P^n_k,D)$ satisfies Campana weak
approximation.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:quadrics}
Let $n\geq2$, and let $C_1,C_2\subset\mathbf P^n_k$ be smooth
$k$-quadric hypersurfaces meeting transversely. Set
\[
 D=\frac12(C_1+C_2),\qquad
 U=\mathbf P^n_k\setminus(C_1\cup C_2).
\]
For  any finite set $S\supseteq\Omega_k^\infty$,  the
$S$-Campana points in $U(k)$ are dense in the product of the local
Campana spaces. In particular, $(\mathbf P^n_k,D)$ satisfies Campana weak
approximation.
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- terminology-1: body_md -->
Write $\mathcal O_S$ for the ring of $S$-integers in $k$; the same notation
with $S'$ refers to the corresponding ring in the specified extension
field. Write $\Omega_k$ for the places of $k$, $\Omega_k^\infty$ for the

\label{lem:open}
The space $\mathcal C_v(X,D)$ is open in $X(k_v)$, and
$X^\circ(k_v)\cap\mathcal C_v(X,D)$ is dense in $\mathcal C_v(X,D)$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
At a finite place, reduction of sections modulo a fixed power of
$\mathfrak m_v$ is locally constant for the $v$-adic topology.
Near the reduction of a section, choose finitely many generators
of the ideal of each $\mathscr D_{i,\lambda}$. Its intersection multiplicity
is the minimum of their valuations. A finite minimum is locally
constant; near a common zero all these valuations exceed any fixed
bound. There are only finitely many local components, and all
weights are finite, so the Campana conditions are open. The assertion
is immediate at places of $S$. Finally, a proper closed subset of a smooth geometrically integral
$k_v$-variety contains no nonempty $v$-adic open subset.
Applying this to $\operatorname{Supp} D$ proves the density assertion.

\label{prop:pointed-quadrics-arithmetic}
Let $Y\subset\mathbf P^{m+2}_k$ be a smooth geometrically integral
intersection of two quadrics, with $m\geq2$ and $P_0\in Y(k)$.
 Then
$\overline{Y(k)}=Y(\mathbf A_k)^{\operatorname{Br}(Y)}$, where closure is taken in
the usual adelic topology (the product topology since $Y$ is proper).
If $m=2$, the group $\operatorname{Br}(Y)/\operatorname{Br}(k)$ is finite and killed by $2$. If $m\geq3$, then
$\operatorname{Br}(Y)=\operatorname{Br}(k)$ and $Y$ satisfies ordinary weak approximation.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $m=2$, then $Y$ is a del Pezzo surface of degree $4$.
The density statement is \cite[Theorem~0.1]{SS}, and
$\operatorname{Br}(Y)/\operatorname{Br}(k)$ is isomorphic to $0$, $\mathbf Z/2$ or $(\mathbf Z/2)^2$
by \cite[Remark~1.2(1)]{VAV}. 

For $m=3$, the Brauer--Manin approximation statement follows from
\cite[Theorem~1]{CTS92}; for $m\geq4$, ordinary weak approximation
is just \cite[Theorem~3.11]{CTSSD}. We explain why the former statement
also gives ordinary weak approximation. 

For every $m\geq3$,
the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem gives $\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{\overline{k}})=\mathbf Z[\mathcal O_Y(1)]$ and
$H^2_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(Y_{\overline{k}},\mu_r)=\mathbf Z/r$, generated by the hyperplane
class. In the Kummer sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{\overline{k}})/r
 \longrightarrow H^2_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(Y_{\overline{k}},\mu_r)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Br}(Y_{\overline{k}})[r]\longrightarrow0,
\]
the first map is therefore an isomorphism. Thus
$\operatorname{Br}(Y_{\overline{k}})=0$. The hyperplane class is defined over $k$, so
Hochschild--Serre gives
\[
 \operatorname{Br}(Y)/\operatorname{Br}(k)\hookrightarrow
 H^1(k,\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{\overline{k}}))=H^1(k,\mathbf Z)=0.
\]
Hence
$\operatorname{Br}(Y)=\operatorname{Br}(k)$, and global reciprocity gives
$Y(\mathbf A_k)^{\operatorname{Br}(Y)}=Y(\mathbf A_k)$.

\label{lem:residue}
Let $R$ be the valuation ring of a nonarchimedean local field $K$
of odd residue characteristic, with residue field $\mathbf F$. Let
$\mathcal M$ be smooth, separated and of finite type over $R$.
If $P,Q\in\mathcal M(R)$ reduce to a locally closed subvariety
$A\simeq\mathbf A^r_{\mathbf F}$ of $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$, then
$\beta(P)=\beta(Q)$ for every $\beta\in\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal M_K)[2]$.
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By \cite[Corollary~2.5 and Proposition~5.1]{Bright},  we have
\[
 \partial:\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal M_K)[2]\longrightarrow
 H^1(\mathcal M_{\mathbf F},\mathbf Z/2),\qquad
 \partial_K(\beta(P))=\bar P^*(\partial\beta).
\]
The smoothness of $\mathcal M$ over $R$ makes its special fibre a regular
divisor; if it is disconnected, the residue map is taken on each
component. The local residue map is an isomorphism
$\partial_K:\operatorname{Br}(K)[2]\xrightarrow{\sim}H^1(\mathbf F,\mathbf Z/2)$,
since $R$ is henselian and $\operatorname{Br}(\mathbf F)=0$ for a finite field.
By Kummer theory,
$H^1(\mathbf A^r_{\mathbf F},\mathbf Z/2)=H^1(\mathbf F,\mathbf Z/2)$, since the only
units of $\mathbf F[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$ are constants and its Picard group
is zero. Thus $(\partial\beta)|_A$ is constant, and its evaluations
at $\bar P$ and $\bar Q$ agree. Injectivity of $\partial_K$ then
gives $\beta(P)=\beta(Q)$ in $\operatorname{Br}(K)$.

\label{prop:pointed-cover}
There is a geometrically Galois finite morphism
$\pi:Y\to\mathbf P^1_k$, with $Y\simeq\mathbf P^1_k$, branched precisely over
$Z$. Its deck group scheme
\[
 G=\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathbf P^1_k}(Y)
\]
is finite \'etale over $k$, with $G(\overline{k})=H$. If
$R_j=(\pi^{-1}(Q_j))_{\mathrm{red}}$ and $Y_U=\pi^{-1}(U_\Delta)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullback-boundary}
 \pi^*Q_j=e_jR_j,
\end{equation}
and $Y_U\to U_\Delta$ is a left $G$-torsor.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Choose $b\in U_\Delta(k)$ and $y_b\in\bar\pi^{-1}(b)(\overline{k})$.
Write $\Gamma_k=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)$. For $\sigma\in\Gamma_k$, the
conjugate cover is again universal for the same orbifold, since
$\Delta$ is defined over $k$. Uniqueness of the universal cover,
and the simply transitive action of $H$ on the fibre above $b$,
give a unique isomorphism of pointed covers
\[
 \phi_\sigma:{}^\sigma(\bar Y,\bar\pi,y_b)
       \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }(\bar Y,\bar\pi,y_b).
\]
Consequently $\phi_{\sigma\tau}=\phi_\sigma\circ{}^\sigma\phi_\tau$.
The cover and the point are defined over a finite extension. Taking
a finite Galois extension containing their fields of definition,
uniqueness makes these maps a finite descent datum. Effective descent
yields $\pi:Y\to\mathbf P^1_k$ and $y_b\in Y(k)$. Since $Y$ is smooth
projective of geometric genus zero, $Y\simeq\mathbf P^1_k$.

The induced action on $H$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deck-galois-action}
 \rho_\sigma(h)=\phi_\sigma\circ{}^\sigma h\circ\phi_\sigma^{-1}
 \qquad(h\in H).
\end{equation}
The cocycle identity for $\phi$ shows that $\rho$ is a continuous
$\Gamma_k$-action by group automorphisms. Finite groups with such
action correspond to finite \'etale $k$-group schemes
\cite[Tag~04JI]{Stacks}. Let $G$ correspond to $(H,\rho)$. The
action on $\bar Y$ descends with it. Equivalently, for a $k$-scheme
$T$, one has $G(T)=\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf P^1_T}(Y_T)$; this identifies $G$ with
the group scheme in the statement.

Over $U_\Delta$ the cover is finite \'etale. After extension to
$\overline{k}$, $H$ acts simply transitively on each geometric fibre.
Thus the natural morphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deck-torsor}
 G\times_kY_U\longrightarrow Y_U\times_{U_\Delta}Y_U,
       \qquad(g,y)\longmapsto(g\cdot y,y),
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism, which proves the torsor assertion. Finally, the
geometric ramification indices give \eqref{eq:pullback-boundary}.

 \iota(\pi_a(y))=[a_1y_1^2:\cdots:a_Ny_N^2],

$M_c=\operatorname{diag}(c_ia_i)_{i=1}^N$. The discriminant of
the pencil $sQ_b+tQ_c$ is
\[
 \det(sM_b+tM_c)
 =\left(\prod_{i=1}^N a_i\right)
   \prod_{i=1}^N(sb_i+tc_i).
\]
Since $a\in U(k)$, all $a_i$ are nonzero. Moreover,
$\Delta_{ij}\ne0$ for $i\ne j$, so the linear factors
$sb_i+tc_i$ are nonzero and pairwise nonproportional. Thus the
discriminant has $N$ distinct zeros in $\mathbf P^1_{\overline{k}}$.

$\overline{k}$, $Y_a$ is a smooth complete intersection of two quadrics
of dimension $N-3=n$.

\[
 F_a:\mathbf P^{N-1}\longrightarrow\mathbf P^{N-1},\qquad
 [y_1:\cdots:y_N]\longmapsto[a_1y_1^2:\cdots:a_Ny_N^2]
\]

finite morphism $\pi_a$. The generic fibre of $(\pi_a)_{\overline{k}}$
is obtained over $\overline{k}(\mathbf P^n)$ by adjoining square roots of
\[
 f_i=\frac{a_N\ell_i}{a_i\ell_N},\qquad 1\leq i<N.
\]
These squareclasses are independent, since
$\operatorname{ord}_{E_j}(f_i)=\delta_{ij}$ for $1\leq i,j<N$. Thus the
generic fibre of $(\pi_a)_{\overline{k}}$ is integral of degree
$2^{N-1}$.

$n$ and therefore dominates $\mathbf P^n$ under the finite morphism
$\pi_a$. Together with smoothness, this proves that $Y_a$ is
geometrically integral and that $\deg(\pi_a)=2^{N-1}=2^{n+2}$.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyperplane-discriminants}
 \delta_i=d_i\prod_{h\ne i}a_h\in k^\times/k^{\times2},\qquad
 d_i=\prod_{h\ne i}\Delta_{ih},\qquad \prod_{i=1}^5\delta_i=1.

equal to $1$. Rescaling by $\lambda$ replaces $c_i$ by
$\lambda^2c_i$; the isomorphism
$[X:Z_1:Z_2]\mapsto[\lambda X:Z_1:Z_2]$ preserves the projection
and the distinguished point. Thus the local equations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-quadric-model}
 f_1(x)=c_1z_1^2,\qquad f_2(x)=c_2z_2^2,\qquad
 c_i=f_i(a)\in R\setminus\{0\},\qquad P_0=(a,1,1).
\end{equation}
If both $c_i$ are units, take $P_v=P_0$. Otherwise, after
interchanging the indices, we may assume $\operatorname{ord}_v(c_1)>0$ and $c_2\in R^\times$.
Smoothness of the reduction of $C_1$ gives a coordinate $x_r$ with
$\partial\bar f_1/\partial x_r(\bar a)\ne0$. Fixing the other
base coordinates, Hensel's lemma gives $x\equiv a\pmod\varpi$
with $f_1(x)=c_1\varpi^2$. Since
$f_2(x)/c_2\in1+\varpi R$, choose $z_2\in1+\varpi R$ with
$z_2^2=f_2(x)/c_2$ and put $P_v=(x,\varpi,z_2)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:repaired-intersections}
 n_v(\mathscr C_1,\pi_a(P_v))=\operatorname{ord}_v(c_1)+2\geq3,\qquad
 n_v(\mathscr C_2,\pi_a(P_v))=0.
\end{equation}
Both quadratic values are nonzero, so the image belongs to $U(k_v)$.

For $n\geq3$, the Brauer assertion follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:pointed-quadrics-arithmetic}. For $n=2$, let
$\mathcal M$ be the relative smooth locus of
\eqref{eq:local-quadric-model}. The line
\[
 A=\{x=\bar a,\ z_2=1\}\simeq\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf F_v}
\]
lies in $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F_v}$: along $A$, the Jacobian minor in
$x_r,z_2$ equals
$-2\bar c_2\,\partial\bar f_1/\partial x_r(\bar a)\ne0$.
Both $P_0$ and $P_v$ are sections of $\mathcal M$ reducing into
$A$, with $z_1$ respectively equal to $1$ and $0$ on reduction.
For $\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(Y_a)$, the normalized class
$\beta=\alpha-\alpha(P_0)$ is $2$-torsion by
Proposition~\ref{prop:pointed-quadrics-arithmetic} and evaluation
at $P_0$. Restricting $\beta$ to $\mathcal M_{k_v}$ and applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:residue} gives
$\beta(P_v)=\beta(P_0)=0$, which proves the assertion.

Fix $(x_v)_v\in\prod_v\mathcal C_v(\mathbf P^n,D)$ and finitely many approximation
conditions, keeping $S$ fixed. Choose a finite $T\supseteq S$
containing the prescribed approximation places and those above $2$,
such that, for every $v\notin T$, the forms $q_i$ are integral and
define the prescribed closures $\mathscr C_i$, the reductions of $C_1,C_2$
are smooth and meet transversely, and $\mathcal W(\mathcal O_v)\ne\varnothing$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:open}, choose nonempty open sets
$V_v\subset U(k_v)\cap\mathcal C_v(\mathbf P^n,D)$, $v\in T$, compatible with
the prescribed approximation conditions. Strong approximation on
$\mathbf P^n\setminus Z$ gives $a\in U(k)$ belonging to all $V_v$ and
satisfying $a\in\mathcal W(\mathcal O_v)$ for every $v\notin T$.
Construct $Y_a$ and $P_0$, and choose a finite $T'\supseteq T$
containing the places where either global coefficient $c_i$ is not a unit.

For $v\in T'\setminus T$, choose $P_v$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:quadric-repair}. Put $Q_v=P_v$ at these places and
$Q_v=P_0$ elsewhere. The lemma and reciprocity give
\[
 \sum_v\operatorname{inv}_v\alpha(Q_v)=\sum_v\operatorname{inv}_v\alpha(P_0)=0
 \qquad(\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(Y_a)).
\]
Here $\alpha(P_0)\in\operatorname{Br}(k)$ is one fixed global class, and each
local modification preserves its evaluation. In particular, we do
not assume that the surface has trivial Brauer group.
Thus $(Q_v)_v\in Y_a(\mathbf A_k)^{\operatorname{Br}(Y_a)}$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:pointed-quadrics-arithmetic} gives
$P\in Y_a(k)$ sufficiently close to $Q_v$ for every $v\in T'$.
Require $\pi_a(P)\in V_v$ at $T$, and a Campana image at
$T'\setminus T$. By openness, we may also require $Z_1Z_2\ne0$.

coordinates $[X_0:\cdots:X_n:Z_1:Z_2]$ for $P$. The vector $X$

$c_i$ are units, the equations would force both $Z_i$ into

vector. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:good-intersection-parity}
 n_v(\mathscr C_i,x)=\operatorname{ord}_v(q_i(X))

\end{equation}
These are the local-component intersection multiplicities.

and those at $T'\setminus T$ hold by local repair. Thus
$x\in\mathcal C_S(\mathbf P^n,D)$ gives the required approximation.
