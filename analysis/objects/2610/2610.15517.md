\label{thm:BS05}
Let $A$ be an algebra.
Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[{\rm (1)}]
\item 
$A$ is a weakly symmetric algebra of Euclidean type, and has a singular Cartan matrix.
\item 
$A$ is a symmetric algebra of Euclidean type, and has a singular Cartan matrix.
\item 
$A \cong T(B)$ for some representation-infinite tilted algebra $B$ of Euclidean type.
\end{enumerate}

{\mathcal S}:= \{                         
T(p,q), T(2,2,r)^*, T(3,3,3), T(2,4,4), T(2,3,6) \mid\\

<!-- prp-3: body_md -->
\label{prp:fundamental-prop}
For any $\lambda \in \Bbbk^\times$,
$\Lambda_\lambda$ is weakly symmetric algebra of Euclidean type with a singular Cartan matrix.
<!-- end prp-3 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
It is obvious that $\Lambda_\lambda$ is weakly symmetric by looking at the structures of projective indecomposable right (resp.\ left) modules
(see Definition \ref{dfn:La_la}). 

The Cartan matrix of $\Lambda_\lambda$ is given by
$$
\begin{bmatrix} 
2&1&1&2&1\\
1&2&1&1&0\\
1&1&2&1&0\\
2&1&1&2&1\\
1&0&0&1&2
 \end{bmatrix},
$$
and its determinant is 0.

                                    
Recall that a twisted multifold extension $T^n_\phi(A)$ of an algebra $A$ was defined as the (classical) orbit category
$$
T^n_\phi(A):= \hat{A}/{\langle \hat{\phi}\nu_A^n \rangle},
$$
where $n$ is a positive integer, and $\phi$ is an automorphism of $A$,
$\nu_A$ is the Nakayama automorphism of $\hat{A}$, and
$\hat{\phi}$ is an automorphism of $\hat{A}$ induced by $\phi$ canonically
(see \cite{asashiba2002derived} for details).
We use this construction.

Let $B$ be the hereditary algebra of type $\widetilde{A}_{2,3}$
defined by the quiver $\mathsf{Q}'$ obtained from $\mathsf{Q}$ by removing the arrows $\alpha_4$ and $\beta_3$.
For each $\lambda \in \Bbbk^\times$, define an automorphism $\phi_\lambda$ of $B$ by
fixing all vertices of $\mathsf{Q}'$ and by 
$$
\phi_\lambda(\alpha):= \begin{cases}
\lambda\alpha & \text{if $\alpha = \alpha_1$}\\
\alpha &\text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$
for all arrows $\alpha$ in $\mathsf{Q}'$.

Then it is easy to see by the computation of orbit category stated in
\cite{asashiba2011generalization} (by taking its skeleton) that we have
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:La_la-as-tme}
\Lambda_\lambda \cong T^1_{\phi_\lambda}(B) = \hat{B}/{\langle g_\lambda \rangle},
\end{equation}
where we put $g_\lambda:= \widehat\phi_\lambda\nu_B$.
Since $B$ is hereditary of type $\widetilde A_{2,3}$,
it is a tilted algebra of Euclidean type.
Since $\phi_\lambda$ fixes the vertices of $\mathsf{Q}'$, we have $g_\lambda(x^{[i]})=x^{[i+1]}$ for all $x \in \mathsf{Q}'_0$ and  $i \in \mathbb{Z}$.
Thus ${\langle g_\lambda \rangle}$ is an admissible infinite cyclic group of automorphisms of $\hat B$. The isomorphism \eqref{eq:La_la-as-tme}
therefore shows that $\Lambda_\lambda$ is of Euclidean type.

<!-- prp-4: body_md -->
\label{prp:symmetric-iff-la=1}
For any $\lambda \in \Bbbk^\times$, 
$\Lambda_\lambda$ is symmetric if and only if $\lambda = 1$.
<!-- end prp-4 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $\lambda \in \Bbbk^\times$, and $B$ be the hereditary algebra of type $\tilde{A}_{2,3}$ as in the proof of Proposition \ref{prp:fundamental-prop}.
By \cite[Theorem 2.1]{ohnuki2000automorphisms}, we have an isomorphism
$$
\Lambda_\lambda \cong \hat{B}/{\langle \widehat{\phi}_\lambda\nu_B \rangle} \cong B \ltimes (DB)_{\phi_\lambda}.
$$
Assume that $\Lambda_\lambda$ is symmetric.
Then by \cite[Lemma 1.3]{ohnuki2000automorphisms},
$\phi_\lambda$ is an inner automorphism of $B$, that is,
$\phi_\lambda = u^{-1} (\operatorname{-}) u$ for some unit $u \in B$.
Since $\phi_\lambda$ fixes all vertices $i \in \mathsf{Q}'_0 =\{1,\dots,5\}$, we have $u^{-1} e_i u = e_i$. Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:u-commutes-e_i}
e_i u  = u e_i
\end{equation}
for all $i = 1, \dots, 5$.
Since $1_B = e_1 + \cdots + e_5$ and $e_iBe_i = \Bbbk e_i$ for all $i = 1,\dots, 5$, we have 
$$
u = \sum_{i1}^5 u e_i \overset{\eqref{eq:u-commutes-e_i}}{=} \sum_{i1}^5 e_i u e_i = \sum_{i1}^5 c_i e_i
$$
for some $c_1, \dots, c_5 \in \Bbbk$.
Note that since $u$ is a unit of $B$, $0 \ne ue_i = c_i e_i$, and hence $c_i \ne 0$ for all $i=1,\dots, 5$.
Put $p:= \alpha_1 \alpha_2 \alpha_3$ and $q:= \beta_1 \beta_2$.
Then both are paths from 1 to 4.
Therefore,
$\phi_\lambda(p) = \lambda p$ shows that $\lambda p = u^{-1} p u = c_1^{-1} c_4 p$.
Moreover, $\phi_\lambda(q) = q$ shows that $q = u^{-1} q u = c_1^{-1} c_4 q$.
Hence $\lambda = c_1^{-1} c_4 = 1$.

Conversely, if $\lambda = 1$, then $\phi_\lambda = \phi_1 = \operatorname{id}_B$, and
$\Lambda_1 = B \ltimes DB$ is symmetric.

<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:nonprj-ind=mod/soc}
Let $\Lambda$ be a selfinjective algebra, and $M$ an indecomposable right $\Lambda$-module. 
Then $M(\mathrm{soc}\,\Lambda) \ne 0$ if and only if $M$ is projective in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
($\Rightarrow$).
Let $\{e_{1},\dots, e_n\}$ be an orthogonal set of primitive idempotents of $\Lambda$ with $1_{\Lambda}=e_{1}+\cdots +e_{n}$. 
Then since $\Lambda$ is selfinjective, $e_i\Lambda$ is injective and has a simple socle for all $i = 1, \dots, n$, and
we have $\mathrm{soc}\,\Lambda=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n} \mathrm{soc}\,(e_{i}\Lambda)$ as right $\Lambda$-modules. 
By the assumption that $M(\operatorname{soc}\Lambda) \ne 0$, 
there exist $i=1,\ldots,n$ and $m \in M$ such that 
$m \cdot \operatorname{soc} e_i\Lambda \ne 0$. 
Then we can define a homomorphism $f \colon e_{i}\Lambda \to M$ of right $\Lambda$-modules
by $e_{i}a\mapsto me_{i}a$ for all $a \in \Lambda$. 
By construction, $f(\operatorname{soc} e_{i}\Lambda)= m\operatorname{soc} e_{i}\Lambda \ne 0$.
Thus $\operatorname{soc} e_i \Lambda \not\subseteq \operatorname{Ker} f$.
Since $\operatorname{soc} e_i \Lambda$ is a simple module,
$(\operatorname{soc} e_i \Lambda) \cap \operatorname{Ker} f = 0$.
Since $\operatorname{soc} e_i \Lambda$ is an essential submodule of $e_i \Lambda$,
we have $\operatorname{Ker} f = 0$.
Therefore, $f$ is a monomorphism.
Since $ e_i\Lambda$ is injective, it is a direct summand of $M$,
and hence we have $M \cong e_i\Lambda$ because $M$ is indecomposable.

\noindent
($\Leftarrow$) 
If $M$ is projective in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda$, then
there exists a primitive idempotent $e$ 
such that $M \cong e\Lambda$.
Then we have $M(\operatorname{soc}\Lambda) \cong (e\Lambda)(\operatorname{soc}\Lambda) = e(\operatorname{soc}\Lambda) = \operatorname{soc} e\Lambda \ne 0$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- ntn-1: body_md -->
For a finite-dimensional algebra $A$, we denote by
$\operatorname{mod} A$ the category of finitely generated right $A$-modules, and
by $\operatorname{ind} A$ a complete set of isomorphism classes of representatives of indecomposable right $A$-modules.

<!-- prp-7: body_md -->
\label{prp:nonproj-ind=mod/soc}
Let $\Lambda$ be a selfinjective algebra.
Then we have a disjoint union
$$
\operatorname{ind} \Lambda = \operatorname{ind} (\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda) \sqcup {\mathcal P}_{\Lambda},
$$
where ${\mathcal P}_\Lambda$ is a complete set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable projective right modules.
<!-- end prp-7 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The canonical surjective homomorphism $\Lambda \to \Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda$ yields
the fully faithful embedding $\operatorname{mod} (\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda) \to \operatorname{mod} \Lambda$ whose image coincides with $\{M \in \operatorname{mod} \Lambda \mid M(\operatorname{soc}\Lambda) = 0\}$.
This shows the assertion by Lemma \ref{lem:nonprj-ind=mod/soc}.

<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:ass-same}
Let $\Lambda$ be a selfinjective algebra, and
$$
(\ast)\quad 0 \to X \xrightarrow{f} Y \xrightarrow{g} Z \to 0
$$
an exact sequence in $\operatorname{mod}(\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)$
$($or equivalently, an exact sequence in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda$ such that any $W \in \{X, Y, Z\}$ has no nonzero projective direct summands$)$.
Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[{\rm(1)}]
\item 
$(*)$ is an almost split sequence in $\operatorname{mod}(\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)$.
\item
$(\ast)$ is an almost split sequence in $\operatorname{mod}\Lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus, there exists a translation $X \dashleftarrow Z$
in $\Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc} \Lambda}$ if and only if there exists one
in ${}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda}$ arising from an almost split sequence
of the form $(*)$ in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda$ whose middle term has
no nonzero projective direct summands.
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
(1) $\Rightarrow$ (2).
It is enough to prove that $g$ is right almost split in $\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda$. 
Let $h \colon W \to Z$ be a non-retraction with $W \in \operatorname{ind}\Lambda$. 
If $W(\operatorname{soc} \Lambda) = 0$, then 
$W \in \operatorname{mod} (\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)$, and $g \colon Y \to Z$ turns out to be a morphism in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda$.
Then there exists a morphism 
$h' \colon W \to Y$ in $\operatorname{mod} (\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)$
such that $h=g h'$ by assumption.
If $W(\operatorname{soc} \Lambda) \ne 0$, then, by Lemma \ref{lem:nonprj-ind=mod/soc}, 
there exists a primitive idempotent $e\in \Lambda$ such that $W \cong e \Lambda$. 
Since $e\Lambda$ is projective in $\operatorname{mod}\Lambda$, 
there exists a morphism 
$h'\colon W \to Y$ 
such that $h = g h'$ in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda$. 
Therefore, in any case, $g$ is right almost split.

(2) $\Rightarrow$ (1).
This follows from Proposition \ref{prp:nonproj-ind=mod/soc} and the fact 
that the inclusion functor $\operatorname{mod}(\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda) \to \operatorname{mod}\Lambda$ is fully faithful and exact.

<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:prj-inj-La-/soc}
Let $\Lambda$ be a connected selfinjective algebra, which is not semisimple.
Then the functor $\operatorname{prj} \Lambda \to \operatorname{prj} (\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)$ sending
$P$ to $P/P(\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)$ induces a bijection 
$$
(\operatorname{prj} \Lambda)/\cong \ \to\  (\operatorname{prj} (\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda))/\cong.
$$
Dually, the functor $\operatorname{inj} \Lambda \to \operatorname{inj}(\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)$ sending
$I$ to $\operatorname{rad} I$ induces a bijection 
$$
(\operatorname{inj} \Lambda)/\cong \ \to\  (\operatorname{inj} (\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda))/\cong.
$$
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $\{e_i,\dots, e_n\}$ be a basic set of primitive idempotents in $\Lambda$.
Then
$$
|(\operatorname{prj} \Lambda)/\cong| = n = |(\operatorname{inj} \Lambda)/\cong|.
$$
Since $\Lambda$ is not semisimple, we have $\operatorname{soc} \Lambda \le \operatorname{rad} \Lambda$.
Hence we also have
$$
|(\operatorname{prj} (\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda))/\cong| = n = | (\operatorname{inj} (\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda))/\cong|.
$$
Note that
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\operatorname{prj} \Lambda)/\cong &= \{[e_i\Lambda]\mid i=1,\dots,n\}\\
(\operatorname{prj} (\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda))/\cong &=\{[\overline{e_i}(\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)]\mid i=1,\dots,n\}\\
(\operatorname{inj} \Lambda)/\cong &= \{[D(\Lambda e_i)] \mid i=1,\dots, n\},\quad \text{and}\\
(\operatorname{inj}(\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda))/\cong &= \{[D((\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)\overline{e_i})] \mid i=1,\dots, n\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\overline{e_i}:= e_i + \operatorname{soc}\Lambda \in \Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda$, and the bracket denotes the isoclass.
Then the isomorphisms
$e_i\Lambda/(e_i\Lambda)(\operatorname{soc} \Lambda) \cong \overline{e_i}(\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)$ and
$\operatorname{rad} D(e_i\Lambda) \cong D((\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)\overline{e_i})$ for all $i=1,\dots, n$ show the assertion.

<!-- prp-8: body_md -->
\label{prp:stAR-iso-AR(over-socle)-as-val-qv}
Let $\Lambda$ be a selfinjective algebra. 
Then we have 
$$
{}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda} \cong \Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda}
$$
as valued quivers $($not necessarily as translation quivers$)$.
<!-- end prp-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
We construct an isomorphism
$$
F \colon {}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda} \to \Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda}
$$
of valued quivers.
The equality \eqref{eq:stAR=over-soc} gives an identification between
$({}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda})_0$ and $(\Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda})_0$.
Using this we define $F_0 \colon ({}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda})_0 \to (\Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda})_0$
to be the identity.
To define a map $F_1 \colon ({}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda})_1 \to (\Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda})_1$,
we show that the following conditions are equivalent for any $a \ge 1$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item 
There exists an arrow $X \xrightarrow{(a,b)} Y$ in $\Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda}$ with a value
$(a,b)$ for some $b \ge 1$.
\item 
There exists a source map                     
$$
X \to Y^{(a)} \oplus Y'                                          
$$
such that any direct summand of $Y'$               
is not isomorphic to $Y$.               
\item 
When $X$ is non-injective in $\operatorname{mod} (\Lambda/\operatorname{soc} \Lambda)$,
there exists an almost split sequence in $\operatorname{mod}(\Lambda/\operatorname{soc} \Lambda)$ of the form
$$
0 \to X \to Y^{(a)} \oplus Y' \to Z \to 0;
$$
or when 
$X$ is injective in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda/\operatorname{soc} \Lambda$ ,
there exists an isomorphism $f \colon X \to \operatorname{rad} e\Lambda$ for some primitive idempotent $e$ by Lemma \ref{lem:prj-inj-La-/soc}, and $X/\operatorname{soc} X \cong Y^{(a)} \oplus Y'$,
where in each case, any direct summand of $Y'$               
is not isomorphic to $Y$.               
\item 
When $X$ is not isomorphic to $\operatorname{rad} e\Lambda$ for any primitive idempotent $e \in \Lambda$,
                                        
there exists an almost split sequence in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda$ of the form
$$
0 \to X \to Y^{(a)} \oplus Y' \to Z \to 0
$$
in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda$ such that any $W \in \{X, Y, Y', Z\}$ has no nonzero projective direct summands;

or when there exists an isomorphism $f \colon X \to \operatorname{rad} e\Lambda$ for some primitive idempotent $e$, and $X/\operatorname{soc} X \cong Y^{(a)} \oplus Y'$,
where in each case, any direct summand of $Y'$               
is not isomorphic to $Y$.               
\item 
There exists an arrow $X\xrightarrow{(a,b)} Y$ in ${}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda}$ with a value
$(a,b)$ for some $b \ge 1$.
\end{enumerate}

The equivalences between (1), (2) and (3) are trivial.

(3) $\Leftrightarrow$ (4).
Note that $X$ is injective in $\operatorname{mod}(\Lambda/\operatorname{soc} \Lambda)$ if and only if
there exists an isomorphism $f \colon X \to \operatorname{rad} e\Lambda$ for some primitive idempotent $e$.
Then in the first case, this equivalence follows from Lemma \ref{lem:ass-same} 
and in the second case, it is trivial.

The equivalence of (4) and (5) follows by definition.


Dually, the same argument for a sink map shows that
there exists an arrow $X \to Y$ in ${}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda}$ with
a value $(d, b)$ for some $d \ge 1$.

Altogether, we see that there exists an arrow $X \to Y$ with
a value $(a, b)$ in $\Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda}$ if and only if so does in
${}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda}$.

Hence we can define a map $F_1 \colon ({}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda})_1 \to (\Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda})_1$ as the identity map.
It is obvious that $F = (F_0, F_1) \colon {}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda} \to \Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda}$ turns out to be a quiver isomorphism, and it preserves the values on arrows as shown above.

<!-- prp-9: body_md -->
\label{prp:stAR-again}
Let $\Lambda$ be a selfinjective algebra, which is not semisimple. 
Then we have 
$$
{}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda} \cong \Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda}'
$$
as valued translation quivers, where $\Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda}'$ is constructed
from $\Gamma_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda}$ by adding the translations
$$
D((\Lambda/\operatorname{soc} \Lambda)e) \dashleftarrow e(\Lambda/\operatorname{soc} \Lambda)
$$
for all primitive idempotents $e$ in $\Lambda/\operatorname{soc} \Lambda$.
<!-- end prp-9 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
By Proposition \ref{prp:stAR-iso-AR(over-socle)-as-val-qv},
it suffices to show that there exists a translation
$X \dashleftarrow Z$
                 
       
                       
               
in ${}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda}$ if and only if so does in $\Gamma'_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda}$.
By Lemma \ref{lem:ass-same}, we easily see the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item 
There exists a translation
$X \dashleftarrow Z$
                 
       
                       
               
in ${}_{\text{\rm s}}\Gamma_{\Lambda}$.

\item 
There exists a translation
$X \dashleftarrow Z$
                 
       
                       
               
in $\Gamma_{\Lambda}$.

\item 
There exists an almost split sequence of the form
$0\to X \to Y \to Z \to 0$ in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda$.

\item 
One of the following holds
\begin{enumerate}
\item 
There exists an almost split sequence of the form
$0\to X \to Y \to Z \to 0$ in $\operatorname{mod}(\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda)$.
\item
$X \cong \operatorname{rad} P$ and $Z \cong P/\operatorname{soc} P$ in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda$ for some projective indecomposable $\Lambda$-module $P$.
In this case, we have an almost split sequence of the form
$$
0 \to \operatorname{rad} P \to P \oplus (\operatorname{rad} P/\operatorname{soc} P) \to P/\operatorname{soc} P \to 0
$$
in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
\item 
There exists a translation
$X \dashleftarrow Z$
                 
       
                       
               
in $\Gamma'_{\Lambda/\operatorname{soc}\Lambda}$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- thm-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:ext-GS}
Consider a complex of $\Lambda$-$\Lambda$-bimodules
$$
Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}}:= (\cdots\to  Q_3 \xrightarrow{A_3} Q_2 \xrightarrow{A_2}Q_1 \xrightarrow{A_1} Q_0 \xrightarrow{A_0} M\xrightarrow{A_{-1}} 0 \to \cdots)
$$
such that $A_0$ is an epimorphism, $Q_i$ are finitely generated projective $\Lambda$-$\Lambda$-bimodules for all $i\ge 0$, and $M$ is projective in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$.
If $\Lambda/\mathbf{r} \otimes_{\Lambda}Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}}$ is
                                      
   
                                                                                                         
   
                                                                    
                         
acyclic,
then so is $Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}}$, i.e., $Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}}$ is a projective bimodule resolution of $M$.
<!-- end thm-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
We set $T:= \Lambda/\mathbf{r} \otimes_\Lambda \operatorname{-}$ for short.
Assume that $T(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})$ is acyclic.
By induction on $n \ge 0$ we show that $\operatorname{Im} A_n = \operatorname{Ker} A_{n-1}$
and $\operatorname{Ker} A_{n-1}$ is projective in  $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$, which completes
the proof.


\subsection*{Case $n = 0$}
Since $A_0$ is an epimorphism we have $\operatorname{Im} A_0 = M = \operatorname{Ker} A_{-1}$, and $\operatorname{Ker} A_{-1} = M$ is projective in $\operatorname{mod}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$.
                                                                   
    
                                                                      
    
                       
                                                                       
\subsection*{Case $n \ge 1$}
We have an exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ex-seq}
0 \to \operatorname{Ker} A_{n-1}\overset{\tau_{n-1}}{\hookrightarrow} Q_{n-1} \xrightarrow{A_{n-1}} \operatorname{Im} A_{n-1}\to 0.
\end{equation}
By the induction hypothesis $\operatorname{Im} A_{n-1} = \operatorname{Ker} A_{n-2}$
is projective in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$, which shows that the sequence
\eqref{eq:ex-seq} splits in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$.
Hence $\operatorname{Ker} A_{n-1}$ turns out to be projective in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}$
as desired because so is $Q_{n-1}$.
Moreover the sequence \eqref{eq:ex-seq} gives us a split exact sequence
$$
0 \to T(\operatorname{Ker} A_{n-1})\xrightarrow{T(\tau_{n-1})} T(Q_{n-1}) \xrightarrow{T(A_{n-1})} T(\operatorname{Im} A_{n-1})\to 0
$$
in $\operatorname{mod} \Lambda$.
In particular $T(\tau_{n-1})$ is a monomorphism.
Using this sequence we show
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HT}
H_{n-1}(T( Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})) \cong T(H_{n-1}(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})).
\end{equation}
Apply $T$ to the commutative diagram
$$
\vcenter{
\xymatrix{
Q_n && Q_{n-1}&& Q_{n-2}\\
&\operatorname{Im} A_n & \operatorname{Ker} A_{n-1}& \operatorname{Im} A_{n-1} &\operatorname{Ker} A_{n-2}
\ar"1,1";"1,3"^{A_n}
\ar@{->>}"1,1";"2,2"_{\rho_n}
\ar@{^{(}->}"2,2";"2,3"_{\sigma_n}
\ar@{^{(}->}"2,3";"1,3"_{\tau_{n-1}}
\ar"1,3";"1,5"^{A_{n-1}}
\ar@{->>}"1,3";"2,4"^{\rho_{n-1}}
\ar@{=}"2,4";"2,5"_{\sigma_{n-1}}
\ar@{^{(}->}"2,5";"1,5"_{\tau_{n-2}}
}}
$$
to have the commutative diagram
$$
\vcenter{
\xymatrix{
T(Q_n) && T(Q_{n-1})&& T(Q_{n-2})\\
&T(\operatorname{Im} A_n) & T(\operatorname{Ker} A_{n-1})& T(\operatorname{Im} A_{n-1}) &T(\operatorname{Ker} A_{n-2}).
\ar"1,1";"1,3"^{T(A_n)}
\ar@{->>}"1,1";"2,2"_{T(\rho_n)}
\ar"2,2";"2,3"_{T(\sigma_n)}
\ar@{>->}"2,3";"1,3"_{T(\tau_{n-1})}
\ar"1,3";"1,5"^{T(A_{n-1})}
\ar@{->>}"1,3";"2,4"^{T(\rho_{n-1})}
\ar@{=}"2,4";"2,5"_{T(\sigma_{n-1})}
\ar@{>->}"2,5";"1,5"_{T(\tau_{n-2})}
}}
$$
Looking at this diagram we see
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{n-1}(T(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})) &=\operatorname{Ker} T(A_{n-1}) = \operatorname{Ker} T(\rho_{n-1})=T(\tau_{n-1})(T(\operatorname{Ker} A_{n-1})),\ \text{and}\\
B_{n-1}(T(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})) &= T(\tau_{n-1})(\operatorname{Im} T(\sigma_n)).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore we have the following diagram with exact rows
$$
\xymatrix{
T(\operatorname{Im} A_n) &&T(\operatorname{Ker} A_{n-1}) & T(H_{n-1}(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})) &0\\
&\operatorname{Im} T(\sigma_n)\\
&B_{n-1}(T(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})) & Z_{n-1}(T(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})) & H_{n-1}(T(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})) & 0,
\ar"1,1";"1,3"^{T(\sigma_n)}
\ar"1,3";"1,4"^{\text{can}}
\ar"1,4";"1,5"
\ar@{->>}"1,1";"2,2"
\ar@{^{(}->}"2,2";"1,3"
\ar"2,2";"3,2"_{T(\tau_{n-1})}^{\wr}
\ar"1,3";"3,3"^{T(\tau_{n-1})}_{\wr}
\ar@{-->}"1,4";"3,4"
\ar@{^{(}->}"3,2";"3,3"
\ar"3,3";"3,4"^{\text{can}}
\ar"3,4";"3,5"
}
$$
where the vertical maps are isomorphisms because
$T(\tau_{n-1})$ is a monomorphism.
Hence by the universality of the cokernel and by five lemma
$T(\tau_{n-1})$ induces an isomorphism \eqref{eq:HT}.
Since $T(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})$
                                     
                      
is acyclic,
the isomorphism \eqref{eq:HT} implies that
$0 = T(H_{n-1}(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})) \cong H_{n-1}(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})/\mathbf{r} H_{n-1}(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})$.
Here note that $H_{n-1}(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}})$ is a finitely generated left $\Lambda$-module
by assumption.
Then by Nakayama's lemma we have $H_{n-1}(Q_{\raisebox{1pt}{\scalebox{0.4}{$\bullet$}}}) = 0$, i.e.,
$\operatorname{Im} A_{n} = \operatorname{Ker} A_{n-1}$.
