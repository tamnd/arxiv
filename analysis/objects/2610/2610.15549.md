<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:joint_scalar}
The pair \((\widetilde{T}^n,X^n)\) generated by Algorithm~\ref{alg:grm_scalar}
has the same law as the generative pair \((T^n,X^n)\). In particular,
for every \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\),
    \begin{equation}
\label{eq:joint_scalar}
    X_i\mid(\widetilde{T}_i=k)
    \sim
    \mathcal{N}(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2),
    \qquad
    I(X^n;\widetilde{T}^n)
    =
    n\,I(X;T).
    \end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Both constructions use the same source letter \(X^n\sim f_X^{\otimes n}\). It remains to
match the kernels \(P_{\widetilde{T}^n\mid X^n}\) and \(P_{T^n\mid X^n}\); the
joints then factor in the same way.

Since each \(\sigma_k^2>0\), one has \(f_X(x)>0\) for all \(x\), so the
weights in Algorithm~\ref{alg:grm_scalar} are well-defined. Bayes' rule
for the generative pair \((T,X)\) gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pk_bayes}
    p_k(x)
    =
    \frac{
    \pi_k\,\mathcal{N}(x;\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)
    }{
    f_X(x)
    }
    =
    P(T=k\mid X=x).
\end{equation}
The simulation step is exact \eqref{eq:stoch_sim}, so
\(x\mapsto\bigl(p_1(x),\ldots,p_K(x)\bigr)\)
\cite{LiElGamal2018,FlamichGunduzREC}.
Thus \(P_{\widetilde{T}\mid X}=P_{T\mid X}\), and therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:joint_factor}
    P_{\widetilde{T},X}
    =
    P_{\widetilde{T}\mid X}\,P_X
    =
    P_{T\mid X}\,P_X
    =
    P_{T,X}.
\end{equation}
In particular, \(P(\widetilde{T}=k)=\pi_k>0\) and
\(X\mid(\widetilde{T}=k)\sim X\mid(T=k)\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)\).
Mutual information depends on a pair only through its joint law, so
\(I(X;\widetilde{T})=I(X;T)\).

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:grm_scalar}
If \(D<\min_k\sigma_k^2\), then
\begin{equation}
    R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)
    =
    h(X)-\tfrac12\log(2\pi e\,D)
    =
    R(D).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Because \(D<\min_k\sigma_k^2\), the choice \(\theta=D\) satisfies
\eqref{eq:theta_scalar} and lies strictly below every component variance.
Uniqueness of the water level (Appendix~\ref{app:rwf_scalar}) therefore
yields \(D_k^{\star}=D\) for every \(k\). Substituting into \eqref{eq:R_GRM_scalar} and
\eqref{eq:Rcond_closed} gives
\begin{equation}
    R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)
    =
    I(X;T)
    +
    \sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \pi_k\cdot\tfrac12\log(\sigma_k^2/D).
\end{equation}
The differential entropy of \(\mathcal{N}(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)\) is
\(\tfrac12\log(2\pi e\sigma_k^2)\), hence
\begin{align}
    &\sum_{k=1}^{K} \pi_k\cdot\tfrac12\log(\sigma_k^2/D)\nonumber\\
    =&
    \sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \pi_k
    \Bigl(
    \tfrac12\log(2\pi e\sigma_k^2)
    -
    \tfrac12\log(2\pi e\,D)
    \Bigr)
    \nonumber\\
    =&
    h(X\mid T)
    -
    \tfrac12\log(2\pi e\,D).
\end{align}
Combining with \(I(X;T)+h(X\mid T)=h(X)\) yields
\begin{align}
    R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)
    &=
    I(X;T)
    +
    h(X\mid T)
    -
    \tfrac12\log(2\pi e\,D)
    \nonumber\\
    &=
    h(X)
    -
    \tfrac12\log(2\pi e\,D)
    =
    \mathrm{SLB}(D).
\end{align}
Together with \(\mathrm{SLB}(D)\leq R(D)\le R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)\), one has
\(R(D)=\mathrm{SLB}(D)=R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)\).

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{re:gmm_slb_coincidence}
The condition in Theorem~\ref{thm:grm_scalar} coincides
with the condition for the tightness of the mixture SLB.
The coincidence is due to a property of Gaussians, which persists for
multivariate GMMs but fails for general finite
mixtures, where the mixture SLB may be tight while GRM is not.
We further derive this in
Section~\ref{sec:grm_general}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:converse}
We note that the converse of Theorem \ref{thm:grm_scalar} is also true. It follows by contradiction.
Suppose GRM attains \(R(D)\) at some distortion $D$ with
\(\min_k\sigma_k^2<D<D_{\max}\).
In the region, for the mixture \(R(D)>\mathrm{SLB}(D)\), so
every RDF-achieving reproduction is purely discrete
\cite[Theorem 3]{Rose1994}.
Reverse water-filling, however, still leaves at least one component
active.
GRM encodes that component by the scalar Gaussian test channel at a
distortion strictly below its variance, where \(R(D)=\mathrm{SLB}(D)\) for a
Gaussian source, and therefore, yields an absolutely continuous part in the
conditional reconstruction \cite[Theorem 3]{Rose1994}.
The resulting unconditional law of \(\widehat{X}\) then has a density on
\(\mathbb{R}\), contradicting discreteness.
Hence GRM cannot attain \(R(D)\) for \(D>\min_k\sigma_k^2\).
Combined with Theorem~\ref{thm:grm_scalar} and continuity at the
threshold, GRM is Shannon-optimal if and only if
\(D\le\min_k\sigma_k^2\).
However this converse does not extend to multivariate cases naturally, as scalar Gaussian is the unique source distribution that admits continuous reconstruction for the entire range of positive rates \cite[Section IV-C]{Rose1994}. The only exception is where all the components are isotropic multivariate Gaussians, which is equivalent under
additive squared error to \(d\) i.i.d.\ scalar Gaussians of variance
\(\sigma^2\).

\label{lem:ex_slb_construction}
There exist distinct, everywhere positive probability densities
\(f_1,f_2\) on \(\mathbb{R}\), each with mean zero, variance one and finite differential entropy, i.e., $|h(f_k)|<\infty$, such

    f_k(x)
    =
    (\nu*\varphi_D)(x)
    :=
    \int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_D(x-y)\,\nu(dy),
    \qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.
\]

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:slb_not_grm}
Let \(P(T=1)=P(T=2)=1/2\), and let \(X\) conditioned on \(T=k\) have
density \(f_k\) from Lemma~\ref{lem:ex_slb_construction}. The marginal
density of \(X\) is \((f_1+f_2)/2=\varphi\). Thus \(X\) is standard
Gaussian and
\begin{equation}
    R(D)=\mathrm{SLB}(D)=\tfrac12\log(1/D),
    \qquad D\in(0,1].
\end{equation}
Since $h(f_k)$ is finite, $\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)$ is finite and the equality criterion of the SLB applies. For a scalar source under squared error, equality in the SLB at
\(D>0\) holds if and only if the source density can be written as
\(\nu*\varphi_D\) for some probability measure \(\nu\)
\cite{Berger1971,CoverThomas}. Lemma~\ref{lem:ex_slb_construction}
rules out this representation for both components. Therefore,
\[
    R_k(D)>\mathrm{SLB}_k(D),
    \qquad k\in\{1,2\},\quad D\in(0,1).
\]
The mixture SLB is therefore tight throughout \((0,1)\), whereas both
component SLB inequalities are strict throughout that interval.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:comp_slb}
Fix \(D>0\).
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
    \par\hangindent 40\p@ \(R_k(D)=\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)\) for every \(k\);
    \par\hangindent 40\p@ \(R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)=\mathrm{SLB}(D)\).
\end{enumerate}
In either case 
\(R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)=\mathrm{SLB}(D)=R^{ }(D)\).
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
\textit{Sufficiency.}
Assume \(R_k(D)=\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)\) for every \(k\).
The choice \(D_k=D\) is admissible in \eqref{eq:Rcond_sep}, so
\begin{align}
    R_{\mathbf{X}|T}(D)
    &\le
    \sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \pi_k R_k(D)
    =
    \sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \pi_k\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)
    \nonumber\\
    &=
    h(\mathbf{X}|T)
    -
    \frac{d}{2}
    \log\!\bigl(2\pi e\,D/d\bigr).
\end{align}
Adding the label cost and applying definition of mutual information yields
\(R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)\le\mathrm{SLB}(D)\).
The sandwich
\(\mathrm{SLB}(D)\le R^{ }(D)\le R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)\)
forces
\(R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)=\mathrm{SLB}(D)=R^{ }(D)\).

\textit{Necessity.}
Assume \(R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)=\mathrm{SLB}(D)\).
For every admissible allocation \(\{D_k\}\) with
\(\sum_k\pi_k D_k=D\),
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \pi_k R_k(D_k)
    &\ge
    \sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \pi_k\mathrm{SLB}_k(D_k)
    \nonumber\\
    &=
    h(\mathbf{X}|T)
    -
    \frac{d}{2}
    \log(2\pi e) 
    \nonumber\\
    & \qquad \qquad \, \, \, \, \, -
    \frac{d}{2}
    \sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \pi_k\log(D_k/d).
\end{align}
Concavity of \(\log\) gives
\(\sum_k\pi_k\log D_k\le\log D\), with equality if and only if
\(D_k=D\) for every \(k\) since $\pi_k>0$.
Hence
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \pi_k R_k(D_k)
    \ge
    \sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \pi_k\mathrm{SLB}_k(D),
\end{equation}
and therefore
\(R_{X|T}^{ }(D)\ge\sum_k\pi_k\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)\).
But
\(R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)=\mathrm{SLB}(D)\) rearranges as
\(R_{X|T}^{ }(D)=\sum_k\pi_k\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)\).
The infimum defining \(R_{X|T}^{ }(D)\) is thus attained if and only if both equalities hold, which forces
\(D_k\equiv D\) and \(R_k(D)=\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)\) for every \(k\).

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
We note that condition~(1) requires \emph{exact} tightness against the
unclipped Shannon formula \(\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)\).
Here we lift the convention of identifying a rate-distortion function with
\(\max\{0,\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)\}\). Specifically, whenever
\(\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)<0\), one has \(R_k(D)\ge0>\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)\), so~(1)
fails even though the clipped bound is met.
Under finite differential entropy, each map
\(D\mapsto\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)\) is continuous and strictly decreasing from
\(+\infty\) to \(-\infty\).
Hence, whenever \(R_k(D)=0\) for some component $k$, \(\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)<0\) almost surely and the condition 1) fails.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:grm_asymp}
Assume \(h(\mathbf{X})>-\infty\) and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:comp_slb_asymp}
    \lim_{D\downarrow 0}
    \bigl(R_k(D)-\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)\bigr)
    =
    0,
    \qquad
    k=1,\ldots,K.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:grm_asymp}
    \lim_{D\downarrow 0}
    \bigl(R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)-\mathrm{SLB}(D)\bigr)
    =
    \lim_{D\downarrow 0}
    \bigl(R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)-R^{ }(D)\bigr)
    =
    0.
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The identity
\(\mathrm{SLB}(D)=I(\mathbf{X};T)+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k\mathrm{SLB}_k(D)\)
follows from \(h(\mathbf{X})=I(\mathbf{X};T)+h(\mathbf{X}|T)\).

\begin{equation}

    \le
    R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)-\mathrm{SLB}(D)

    \sum_{k=1}^{K}

\end{equation}
The rightmost expression tends to zero as \(D\downarrow0\) by

The remaining claim follows from
\(\mathrm{SLB}(D)\le R^{ }(D)\le R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)\).

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Each nondegenerate Gaussian in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) meets its MSE SLB at total
distortion \(D\) if and only if reverse water-filling is fully active, i.e.,
if and only if \(D/d\) does not exceed the smallest eigenvalue of its
covariance \cite{Berger1971,CoverThomas}.
Under the stated hypothesis,

Theorem~\ref{prop:comp_slb} therefore yields
\(R_{\mathrm{GRM}}(D)=\mathrm{SLB}(D)=R^{ }(D)\).

\label{lem:centroid}
Assume \(\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{X}\|^2<\infty\), and let \(J\) be a
finite-valued random variable jointly distributed with \(\mathbf{X}\).
Among all codebooks \(\{\mathbf{a}_j\}\subset\mathbb{R}^d\),

\(\mathbf{a}_j=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid J=j]\)
for every \(j\) with \(P(J=j)>0\).

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:centroid_mse}
    \mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{a}_J\|^2

    \mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{X}\|^2
    -
    \mathbb{E}\|\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid J]\|^2.
\end{equation}
In particular, the infimum in \eqref{eq:rdf_M} may be taken over
conditional kernels \(P_{J\mid\mathbf{X}}\) with
\(|\mathcal{J}|\le M\) and
\(\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid J]\|^2\le D\).

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:zero_rate_vec}
Let \(\delta:=D_{\max}-D\).
Then
\begin{equation}
    R^{ }(D_{\max}-\delta)
    =
    \frac{\delta}{2\tau^2}+o(\delta),
    \qquad\delta\downarrow0,
\end{equation}
and the same expansion holds for every \(R_M^{ }(D_{\max}-\delta)\) with
\(M\ge2\).
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
    R^{ }(D)
    \ge
    \frac{D_{\max}-D}{2\tau^2}
\end{equation}
for every \(D\in[0,D_{\max}]\).

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The proof has three steps. First, a relative-entropy mean inequality gives a global
lower bound on both the Shannon RDF and its finite-alphabet restriction.
Second, it is shown that a binary test channel attains this bound to first order near
\(D=D_{\max}\). Finally, the monotonicity in \eqref{eq:RM_mono} extends the
binary result to every \(M\ge2\). The variational and truncation arguments
used below are established in Appendix~\ref{app:zero}.

\textit{Lower bound.}
Let \(P_{\widehat{\mathbf{X}}\mid\mathbf{X}}\) be any test channel with
distortion at most \(D\). The claim is immediate if
\(I(\mathbf{X};\widehat{\mathbf{X}})=\infty\); otherwise, let
\(Q_{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}
:=P_{\mathbf{X}\mid\widehat{\mathbf{X}}=\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}\).
The conditional-mean property gives
\[
    D_{\max}-D
    \le
    \mathbb{E}\|
        \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid\widehat{\mathbf{X}}]\|^2.
\]
For \(P_{\widehat{\mathbf{X}}}\)-almost every
\(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}\), applying the relative-entropy mean inequality
\eqref{eq:tau_kl} to \(Q_{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}\) and then integrating
yields
\begin{align}
    D_{\max}-D
    &\le
    2\tau^2
    \int
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}\Vert P_{\mathbf{X}})
    P_{\widehat{\mathbf{X}}}(d\widehat{\mathbf{x}})
    \nonumber\\
    &=
    2\tau^2I(\mathbf{X};\widehat{\mathbf{X}}).
\end{align}
Taking the infimum over all admissible test channels proves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:zero_global_lower}
    R^{ }(D)\ge\frac{D_{\max}-D}{2\tau^2},
    \qquad 0\le D\le D_{\max}.
\end{equation}

\textit{Binary upper bound.}
Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,\tau^2)\). The variational characterization
\eqref{eq:tau_var} and the bounded-likelihood-ratio approximation in
Appendix~\ref{app:zero} provide a measure
\(Q\ll P_{\mathbf{X}}\) such that, with
\[
    \mathbf{m}:=\mathbb{E}_Q[\mathbf{X}],
    \qquad
    K_Q:=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q\Vert P_{\mathbf{X}}),
\]
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bounded_Q_ratio}
    \frac{\|\mathbf{m}\|^2}{2K_Q}
    \ge\tau^2-\varepsilon,
    \qquad
    L:=\frac{dQ}{dP_{\mathbf{X}}}\le B
\end{equation}
for some finite \(B>1\). In particular, \(K_Q>0\) and
\(\mathbf{m}\ne\mathbf{0}\).

For \(0<q\le B^{-1}\), define
\[
    P(J=1\mid\mathbf{X})=qL,
    \qquad
    P(J=0\mid\mathbf{X})=1-qL.
\]
Then \(P(J=1)=q\), \(P_{\mathbf{X}\mid J=1}=Q\), and
\[
    \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid J=1]=\mathbf{m},
    \qquad
    \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid J=0]
    =-\frac{q}{1-q}\mathbf{m}.
\]
Using these conditional means as the two reconstruction codewords gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:binary_deficit}
    D_{\max}-\mathbb{E}\|
        \mathbf{X}-\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid J]\|^2
    =
    \frac{q}{1-q}\|\mathbf{m}\|^2.
\end{equation}
For any sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), choose
\[
    q_\delta:=\frac{\delta}{\|\mathbf{m}\|^2+\delta}.
\]
Then \(q_\delta\le B^{-1}\), and \eqref{eq:binary_deficit} shows that the
distortion is exactly \(D_{\max}-\delta\). The rare-binary expansion established
in Appendix~\ref{app:zero} gives
\begin{align}
    I(\mathbf{X};J)
    &=
    q_\delta K_Q+O(q_\delta^2)
    \nonumber\\
    &=
    \frac{K_Q}{\|\mathbf{m}\|^2}\delta+O(\delta^2)
    \le
    \frac{\delta}{2(\tau^2-\varepsilon)}+O(\delta^2),
\end{align}
where the last inequality follows from
\eqref{eq:bounded_Q_ratio}. Consequently,
\[
    \limsup_{\delta\downarrow0}
    \frac{R_2^{ }(D_{\max}-\delta)}{\delta}
    \le
    \frac{1}{2(\tau^2-\varepsilon)}.
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and combining this bound with the
lower bound \eqref{eq:zero_global_lower} yields
\[
    R_2^{ }(D_{\max}-\delta)
    =
    \frac{\delta}{2\tau^2}+o(\delta).
\]
Finally, for every \(M\ge2\), \eqref{eq:RM_mono} gives
\[
    R^{ }(D_{\max}-\delta)
    \le
    R_M^{ }(D_{\max}-\delta)
    \le
    R_2^{ }(D_{\max}-\delta).
\]
Applying \eqref{eq:zero_global_lower} to the left-hand side completes the
sandwich and proves the asserted expansion for \(R^{ }\) and every
\(R_M^{ }\), \(M\ge2\).

1)
Set \(m:=\lfloor M/K\rfloor\). The hypothesis \(M/K\to\infty\) forces
\(m\to\infty\).
It is enough to exhibit one \(M\)-point codebook whose MSE tends to zero.
Allocate \(m\) codewords to each of the \(K\) components (using at most
\(M\) points) and take the union of the \(K\) codebooks.

Fix a Gaussian \(\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})\) with eigenvalues at
most \(\lambda_{\max}\).
Rotate to principal axes and form a product uniform scalar quantizer with
\(\ell:=\max\{\lfloor m^{1/d}\rfloor,1\}\) levels per coordinate on
\([-A,A]\), where \(A:=\sqrt{4\lambda_{\max}\log\ell}\), sending overflow
to the nearest endpoint.
Let \(\varphi\) and \(\Phi\) denote the standard normal density and cdf.
The Mills ratio \cite{Mills1926} \(R(x)=(1-\Phi(x))/\varphi(x)\) satisfies \(R(x)\le 1/x\)
for \(x>0\). Thus if
\(Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\) and \(\alpha=A/\sigma\), then
\(\mathbb{E}[(Z-A)_+^2]\le\sigma^2\varphi(\alpha)/\alpha\).
The choice of \(A\) gives \(\alpha\ge 2\sqrt{\log\ell}\), hence the
two-sided overflow second moment is
\(O\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(\log\ell)\,\ell^{-2}\bigr)\) uniformly for
\(\sigma^2\in(0,\lambda_{\max}]\). The interior granular error is at most
\((A/\ell)^2=O\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(\log\ell)\,\ell^{-2}\bigr)\).
Summing \(d\) coordinates, the vector MSE is
\(O\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(1+\log m)\,m^{-2/d}\bigr)\), with a constant
depending only on \(d\).
The union codebook therefore satisfies
\[
D_M^{\star }(\mathbf{X}^{(K)})
=
O\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(1+\log(M/K))(M/K)^{-2/d}\bigr)
\to0.
\]

2)
Set \(r:=\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}}\).
On the ball \(B_k\) of radius \(r\) about \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\), the component
density is bounded below by a positive constant depending only on
\(\lambda_{\max}\) and \(d\).
With \(\pi_k=1/K\), the mixture density is therefore bounded below by
\(c/K\) on each \(B_k\), for some \(c>0\) independent of \(K\).
The separation \(\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_k-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k'}\|\ge 4r\) makes the
enlarged balls of radius \(2r\) pairwise disjoint, so each codeword lies
in at most one such neighborhood.

Let \(m_k\) be the number of codewords in the \(2r\)-neighborhood of
\(\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\), so \(\sum_k m_k\le M\).
If some \(m_k=0\), that ball contributes a \(K\)-independent positive
amount and the lower bound only increases.
Repeating the volume argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:floor} on each \(B_k\)
with \(m_k\ge1\) therefore yields
\[
D_M^{\star }(\mathbf{X}^{(K)})
\ge
\frac{c}{K}
\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\kappa_d v^{1+2/d}\, m_k^{-2/d},
\]
where \(v\) is the volume of a ball of radius \(r\).
The right-hand side, subject to \(\sum_k m_k\le M\), is minimized when
\(m_k=M/K\), and that value scales with \(\Omega\bigl((K/M)^{2/d}\bigr)\).

3)
If \(M/K\) is bounded, then \(K/M\) is bounded away from zero, so the
right-hand side of (ii) is bounded away from zero.

Let \(\varphi\) be the standard normal density, and define
\begin{equation}
    \rho(t)
    :=
    \begin{cases}
    \exp\!\bigl(-(1-t^2)^{-1}\bigr), & |t|<1,\\
    0, & |t|\ge1,
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
which is a nonnegative smooth function supported on \([-1,1]\).
Fix \(w\in(0,1/4)\), set \(\rho_w(x):=\rho(x/w)\), and define
\begin{align}
    \widetilde{\psi}(x)
    &:=
    -4\bigl(\rho_w(x-1)+\rho_w(x+1)\bigr)
    \nonumber\\ &\qquad+
    \bigl(\rho_w(x-2)+\rho_w(x+2)\bigr)
    +
    6\,\rho_w(x).
\end{align}
This function is even, smooth, and supported on \([-2-w,2+w]\). The five
translated functions in its definition have disjoint supports because
\(w<1/4\). Write
\[
    I_0:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_w(x)\,dx,
    \qquad
    I_2:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^2\rho_w(x)\,dx.
\]
Since \(\rho_w\) is even, \(\int x\rho_w(x)\,dx=0\). Moreover,
\(\int(x-a)^2\rho_w(x-a)\,dx=I_2+a^2 I_0\) yields
\begin{align}
    \int\widetilde{\psi}
    &=
    \bigl(6-4-4+1+1\bigr)I_0
    =
    0,
    \nonumber\\
    \int x\widetilde{\psi}
    &=
    0,
    \nonumber\\
    \int x^2\widetilde{\psi}
    &=
    \bigl(6-4-4+1+1\bigr)I_2\\
    & \qquad+
    \bigl(-4\cdot1^2-4\cdot1^2+2^2+2^2\bigr)I_0\\
    &=
    0.
\end{align}
where the first-moment identity also follows from the evenness of
\(\widetilde{\psi}\).

The function \(\widetilde{\psi}\) is bounded, and \(\varphi\) is
continuous and strictly positive on its compact support. We may therefore
choose \(\varepsilon>0\) sufficiently small that
\(\psi:=\varepsilon\widetilde{\psi}\) satisfies
\(|\psi|\le\tfrac12\varphi\) pointwise.
Set \(f_1:=\varphi+\psi\) and \(f_2:=\varphi-\psi\).
Then \(f_k\ge\tfrac12\varphi>0\) and \(\int f_k=1\). The moment
identities give
\[
    \int x f_k(x)\,dx=0,
    \qquad
    \int x^2 f_k(x)\,dx=1,
    \qquad k\in\{1,2\}.
\]
Moreover, \(f_1\neq f_2\) because \(\psi\not\equiv0\), and
\((f_1+f_2)/2=\varphi\). Since \(f_k=\varphi\) outside a compact set,
both densities have finite differential entropy.

It remains to show that neither density has the Gaussian-convolution
representation stated in the lemma. For every \(D>0\) and every
probability measure \(\nu\), the convolution \(\nu*\varphi_D\) is real
analytic. To see this, consider its extension
\[
    g(z):=
    \int_{\mathbb{R}}
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi D}}
    \exp\!\left(-\frac{(z-y)^2}{2D}\right)\nu(dy)
\]
to \(z\in\mathbb{C}\). If \(z=a+ib\), the magnitude of the integrand is
bounded by
\[
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi D}}\exp\!\left(\frac{b^2}{2D}\right).
\]
This bound is uniform in \(y\) and locally uniform in \(z\), so
differentiation under the integral is valid and \(g\) is analytic.
Consequently, if \(f_k=\nu*\varphi_D\), then \(f_k\) and
\(f_k-\varphi=\pm\psi\) would be real analytic. The function \(\psi\),
however, is zero outside a compact interval and is not identically zero,
which is impossible for a real-analytic function on \(\mathbb{R}\).
Thus neither \(f_1\) nor \(f_2\) has the required representation.

Let \(J\) be a random variable taking values in a finite set
\(\mathcal{J}\), jointly distributed with \(\mathbf{X}\).
Mutual information \(I(\mathbf{X};J)\) is a functional of the joint law
\(P_{\mathbf{X},J}\) only, and does not depend on the codebook
\(\{\mathbf{a}_j\}\).
Thus, for any fixed kernel \(P_{J\mid\mathbf{X}}\), it is enough
to minimize the distortion over the codebook.

Since \(\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{X}\|^2<\infty\), the random vector
\(\mathbf{X}\) lies in \(L^2\), hence
\(\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid J]\) exists as an \(L^2\) random vector.
Write \(\mathbf{m}_J:=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid J]\) and
\(\mathbf{m}_j:=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid J=j]\) whenever
\(P(J=j)>0\).
For every codebook,
\begin{align}
    \bigl\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{a}_J\bigr\|^2
    &=
    \bigl\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{m}_J\bigr\|^2
    +
    \bigl\|\mathbf{m}_J-\mathbf{a}_J\bigr\|^2
    \nonumber\\
    &\qquad
    +
    2\bigl(\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{m}_J\bigr)^{\mathsf{T}}
    \bigl(\mathbf{m}_J-\mathbf{a}_J\bigr).
\end{align}
Taking the conditional expectation given \(J\) kills the cross term,
because \(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{m}_J\mid J\bigr]=\mathbf{0}\)
almost surely.
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:centroid_pythagoras}
    \mathbb{E}\bigl\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{a}_J\bigr\|^2
    =
    \mathbb{E}\bigl\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{m}_J\bigr\|^2
    +
    \mathbb{E}\bigl\|\mathbf{m}_J-\mathbf{a}_J\bigr\|^2.
\end{equation}
The second summand is nonnegative, and vanishes if and only if
\(\mathbf{a}_J=\mathbf{m}_J\) almost surely, i.e., if and only if
\(\mathbf{a}_j=\mathbf{m}_j\) for every \(j\) with \(P(J=j)>0\).
On the event \(\{J=j\}\) with \(P(J=j)=0\) the value of \(\mathbf{a}_j\)
does not appear in \eqref{eq:centroid_pythagoras}; such labels may be
deleted without increasing cardinality.
When \(\mathbf{a}_J=\mathbf{m}_J\), the second summand vanishes, and
expanding the first together with
\(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{m}_J\bigr]
=\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{m}_J\|^2\) yields \eqref{eq:centroid_mse}.

Consequently, replacing an arbitrary codebook by the centroids
\(\mathbf{a}_j=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid J=j]\) leaves \(I(\mathbf{X};J)\)
unchanged and does not increase distortion.
Every SSQ test channel of size at most \(M\) is thus dominated by one with the same
conditional law and centroid reconstruction, and the infimum
\eqref{eq:rdf_M} may be restricted to kernels \(P_{J\mid\mathbf{X}}\)
with \(|\mathcal{J}|\le M\) and
\(\mathbb{E}\bigl\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}\mid J]\bigr\|^2\le D\).

The constraint set in \eqref{eq:rdf_M} enlarges with \(M\), and is contained

The limit \eqref{eq:RM_limit} is the finite-output-alphabet approximation

Fix \(D\in(0,D_{\max})\) and \(\varepsilon>0\). Continuity of \(R^{ }\) on

Clip the reproduction to a cube of side \(A\) large enough that the clipping
distortion is at most \(\eta/2\), then quantize the cube by a cubic lattice
of mesh \(\delta\) small enough that the additional MSE is at most

Data processing does not increase mutual information, and replacing each
lattice point by the conditional centroid cannot increase distortion
(Lemma~\ref{lem:centroid}).
Hence \(R_M^{ }(D)\le R^{ }(D)+\varepsilon\).
Monotonicity \eqref{eq:RM_mono} yields \eqref{eq:RM_limit}.

For any \(P_{J|\mathbf{X}}\) and any codebook,
\begin{align}
    &\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{a}_J\|^2\nonumber\\
    =&
    \mathbb{E}
    \Bigl[
    \sum_{j=1}^{M}
    P(J=j|\mathbf{X})\,\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{a}_j\|^2
    \Bigr]
    \ge
    \mathbb{E}
    \Bigl[
    \min_{1\le j\le M}
    \|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{a}_j\|^2
    \Bigr],
\end{align}
so no (soft or hard) \(M\)-ary scheme beats \(D_M^{\star }\).

For positivity, a nondegenerate GMM density is continuous and bounded below
by some \(c>0\) on a closed ball \(B\) of radius \(r>0\) and volume
\(v>0\).
For an arbitrary codebook, the Voronoi cells partition \(B\) into at most
\(M\) measurable pieces of volumes \(v_j\) with \(\sum_j v_j=v\).
On a cell of volume \(v_j\),
\(\int\min_a\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}\|^2\,d\mathbf{x}\ge\kappa_d v_j^{1+2/d}\)
for a dimensional constant \(\kappa_d>0\) (the minimum is the second moment
about the centroid, and is homogeneous of degree \(1+2/d\)).
Convexity of \(t\mapsto t^{1+2/d}\) yields
\(\sum_j v_j^{1+2/d}\ge M\cdot(v/M)^{1+2/d}=v^{1+2/d}M^{-2/d}\), whence
\(D_M^{\star }\ge c\kappa_d v^{1+2/d}M^{-2/d}>0\).
The scalar case \(d=1\) recovers the elementary bound
\(D_M^\star\ge c\ell^3/(12M^2)\) on an interval of length \(\ell\).

For attainment, let \(\{\mathbf{a}_j^\star\}\) nearly minimize
\eqref{eq:floor_expr} and set
\(J^\star=\arg\min_j\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{a}_j^\star\|^2\).
Then \(I(\mathbf{X};J^\star)=H(J^\star)\le\log M\) and the distortion can be
made arbitrarily close to the floor, so
\(R_M^{ }(D_M^{\star })\le\log M\).
For larger rate the feasible set still cannot beat \(D_M^{\star }\),
hence saturation.
Items 1--3 follow.
For item 4, a nondegenerate GMM density is bounded, strictly positive on a
nonempty open set, and has finite moments of every order, so
\(0<\int f_{\mathbf{X}}^{d/(d+2)}<\infty\).
Zador's theorem \cite{Zador1982} therefore gives
\[
\lim_{M\to\infty}
M^{2/d}\,D_M^{\star }
=
G_d
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}
f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})^{d/(d+2)}\,d\mathbf{x}
\in(0,\infty),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:zador}.
In dimension one, \(G_1=1/12\) \cite{PanterDite1951}.
