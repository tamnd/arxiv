<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:TV-main}
For every fixed $p\in(0,1)$ and $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$,
\[
 t_{\rm mix}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)=\left(\frac{p^2}{2(1-p)\pi^2}+o(1)\right)n^2\log n.
\]
More explicitly, for $0<\delta<1$ the distance at $\lfloor(1-\delta)t_n^\star\rfloor$ tends to one, whereas for every $\delta>0$ the distance at $\lceil(1+\delta)t_n^\star\rceil$ tends to zero. At $p=1/2$, the location is $n^2\log n/(4\pi^2)$.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:static}
For every $n\ge2$ and $f:\mathfrak S_n\to\mathbb R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:static-main}
 \lVert f-\mathbb E_{U_n}f\rVert_{L^1(U_n)}
 \le C\sqrt n\exp\!\left(C\sqrt{\log n\log\log(n+2)}\right)\mathcal B_n(f).
\end{equation}
The optimal constant has order $n^{1/2+o(1)}$ in the sense that it is at least $c\sqrt n$ and at most $n^{1/2+\eta}$ times a constant depending on every fixed $\eta>0$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:entropy-main}
For every fixed $p\in(0,1)$,
\[
 \frac1{2\kappa}\le\liminf_n\frac{\tau_n}{n^2\log n}
 \le\limsup_n\frac{\tau_n}{n^2\log n}\le\frac1\kappa.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \lim_{s\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}
 H_n(\tau_n-\lfloor sn^2\rfloor)=\infty,
 \qquad
 \lim_{s\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 H_n(\tau_n+\lceil sn^2\rceil)=0.
\]
For each fixed $s$, the earlier time is nonnegative for all sufficiently large $n$. These limits give an $O_p(n^2)$ entropy window about $\tau_n$. No sharp first-order location for $\tau_n$ is asserted.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:coarea}
If $\lVert \1_E-\pi(E)\rVert_1\le C B(\1_E)$ for every set $E$, then $\lVert f-\mathbb E_\pi f\rVert_1\le C B(f)$ for every real $f$. The best constant equals the supremum of $2\pi(E)(1-\pi(E))/B(\1_E)$ over nontrivial sets with nonzero boundary.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For $a\le\min f$, $b\ge\max f$,
$f-\mathbb E f=\int_a^b(\1_{\{f>u\}}-\pi(f>u))\,du$.
The triangle inequality bounds its $L^1$ norm by the integral of the centered-indicator norms. For every pair $x,y$,
$|f(x)-f(y)|=\int_a^b|\1_{\{f(x)>u\}}-\1_{\{f(y)>u\}}|\,du$.
Finite summation and integration commute, proving the upper bound. Indicators give the reverse comparison of optimal constants.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:products}
On a product of finitely many independent probability spaces, centered $L^1$ inequalities with constants $C_j$ tensorize with constant $\max_j C_j$, when their directional boundaries are added. Also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:var-tensor}
 \operatorname{Var} f\le\sum_j\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}_j f,
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Var}_j$ varies only the $j$th variable, holding the others fixed. For a function $h$ of independent Bernoulli($p$) bits,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bernoulli-var}
 \operatorname{Var} h\le\sum_j p(1-p)\mathbb E|h(c^{j,1})-h(c^{j,0})|^2.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For two independent variables $X,Y$, insert $\mathbb E_X f$ between $f$ and $\mathbb E f$ in the $L^1$ norm. Apply the $X$ inequality to the first difference and the $Y$ inequality to the second; Jensen bounds each $Y$ difference of $\mathbb E_X f$ by the expectation of the corresponding difference of $f$. Induction gives the assertion for any number of factors without multiplying constants.
For variance, $\operatorname{Var} f=\mathbb E_Y\operatorname{Var}_X f+\operatorname{Var}_Y\mathbb E_X f$, and the last term is at most $\mathbb E_X\operatorname{Var}_Y f$ by Jensen applied to the centered function in $Y$. Induct again. A single Bernoulli conditional variance is $p(1-p)$ times the squared two-value difference, giving \eqref{eq:bernoulli-var}.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:external-functional}
On a nontrivial multislice of length $N$, with smallest positive class $k$, put
\[
 \mathcal E_{\rm RT}(f)=\frac12\sum_{a<b}\mathbb E(f(\omega^{ab})-f(\omega))^2.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:external-poincare}
 \operatorname{Var} f\le \frac1N\mathcal E_{\rm RT}(f),
\end{equation}
and for a universal $C_{\rm LS}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:external-LSI}
 \operatorname{Ent}(f^2)\le\frac{C_{\rm LS}\log(N/k)}{N}
 \sum_{a<b}\mathbb E(f(\omega^{ab})-f(\omega))^2.
\end{equation}
For $r=N$, $k=1$, this is the uniform-permutation case. For a binary slice with both class sizes at least $N/3$, the logarithm in \eqref{eq:external-LSI} is bounded by $\log3$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For the generator $\mathcal Lf=\sum_{a<b}(f(\omega^{ab})-f(\omega))$, each unordered transposition has rate one and its Dirichlet form is $\mathcal E_{\rm RT}$. On $\mathfrak S_N$, its spectral gap is $N$ by the complete-graph case of Caputo--Liggett--Richthammer \cite{CaputoLiggettRichthammer2010}. Functions on a multislice lift to functions on $\mathfrak S_N$ by identifying labels of the same color, preserving means, variances, and the displayed form, so \eqref{eq:external-poincare} follows. Salez's main multislice theorem \cite{Salez2021} gives a log-Sobolev constant at most $(4/\log2)\log(N/k)$ for form $(2N)^{-1}\sum_{a<b}\mathbb E(\Delta_{ab}f)^2$. Multiplying all rates by $N$ divides the functional-inequality constant by $N$. This proves \eqref{eq:external-LSI}, allowing a universal numerical factor in $C_{\rm LS}$. These published results are inputs, not new assertions proved here.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:routing-basic}
On any multislice of length $N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:RT-route}
 B_{\rm RT}(f)\le2\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}i(N-i)\mathbb E|\Delta_{i,i+1}f|
 \le\frac{N^2}{2}B_{\rm path}(f).
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{align}
 \lVert f-\mathbb E f\rVert_1&\le C N B_{\rm path}(f),\label{eq:crude-path}\\
 \alpha\log(1/\alpha)&\le
 C N\log(N/k)B_{\rm path}(\1_E),\quad \alpha=\mathbb P(E),\label{eq:smallset-path}\\
 \alpha\log(1/\alpha)&\le
 \frac{C\log(N/k)}N B_{\rm RT}(\1_E).\label{eq:smallset-RT}
\end{align}
For a consecutive split into intervals of lengths $m,N-m$, averaging within the two intervals gives $\widetilde f$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:middle-averaging}
 \mathbb E|\widetilde f(\omega^{m,m+1})-\widetilde f(\omega)|
 \le2 B_{\rm path}(f).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For $a<b$, perform swaps with indices $a,a+1,\ldots,b-1,b-2,\ldots,a$; their composition exchanges exactly $a,b$. Telescoping the difference of $f$ along this path and using invariance of the uniform law under every path prefix charges each edge in $[a,b)$ at most twice. There are $i(N-i)$ pairs straddling $i$, proving \eqref{eq:RT-route}.
For an indicator, $\mathcal E_{\rm RT}(\1_E)=B_{\rm RT}(\1_E)/2$, so \eqref{eq:external-poincare} gives $2\alpha(1-\alpha)\le B_{\rm RT}(\1_E)/N$. Coarea proves \eqref{eq:crude-path}. Equations \eqref{eq:smallset-path}--\eqref{eq:smallset-RT} are \eqref{eq:external-LSI} applied to an indicator, followed by routing when needed.
For \eqref{eq:middle-averaging}, Jensen and conjugation by the within-interval averaging permutations give the average of $\mathbb E|\Delta_{ab}f|$ over $a\le m<b$. Its routing coefficient at edge $i$ is at most
$2\#\{(a,b):a\le m<b,\ a\le i<b\}/[m(N-m)]\le2$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:concentration}
If $X$ is the number of marked slots in a uniform sample of $s$ slots from $N$, of which $A$ are marked, and $\mu=sA/N$, then
\[
 \mathbb E e^{tX}\le(1-A/N+(A/N)e^t)^s\quad(t\in\mathbb R).
\]
In particular, for $0<\delta<1$,
\[
 \mathbb P(X\le(1-\delta)\mu)\le e^{-\delta^2\mu/2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(X\ge(1+\delta)\mu)\le e^{-\delta^2\mu/3}.
\]
For any fixed $b>1$, the upper tail at $b\mu$ is at most $e^{-c_b\mu}$. A uniform word of length $N$ with $k$ occurrences of a specified color avoids that color in a fixed interval of length $d$ with probability at most $e^{-kd/N}$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Set $w_i=e^t$ on marked slots and $w_i=1$ otherwise. The moment generating function is $e_s(w)/\binom Ns$, where $e_s$ is the elementary symmetric polynomial. Among positive vectors with fixed sum, $e_s$ is maximized at the constant vector: replacing two entries $a,b$ by their average preserves their sum and increases their product, while
$e_s=e_s(\widehat a,\widehat b)+(a+b)e_{s-1}(\widehat a,\widehat b)+ab e_{s-2}(\widehat a,\widehat b)$.
Repeated averaging, or compactness and this inequality at a maximum, proves the claim, including $s=0,1$. Thus the generating function is bounded by the binomial one. Exponential Markov inequality, optimization in $t$, and the elementary estimates for $(1\pm\delta)\log(1\pm\delta)$ give the displayed tails. This is also the classical sampling-without-replacement comparison discussed in \cite{BardenetMaillard2015}.
The last probability is $\binom{N-k}{d}/\binom Nd$, interpreted as zero if $d>N-k$. Each factor in its product representation is at most $1-k/N$, proving the bound.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:sqrt-boundary}
Let $H$ be the product of symmetric groups on disjoint blocks whose sizes are between $m$ and $M$. Let $Q_H$ average over its action on a uniform finite orbit space, and let $R_{E,H}(x)$ be the number of within-block transpositions $t$ with $\1_E(tx)\ne\1_E(x)$. If all nontrivial block sizes are at least $m$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sqrt-local}
 \lVert \1_E-Q_H\1_E\rVert_1\le\frac{C}{\sqrt m}\mathbb E\sqrt{R_{E,H}}.
\end{equation}
In particular, for all transpositions on $\mathfrak S_n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sqrt-boundary}
 2U_n(E)(1-U_n(E))\le\frac{C}{\sqrt n}\mathbb E_{U_n}\sqrt{R_E}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal L=\sum_t(T_t-I)$ sum the allowed transpositions, where $T_tf(x)=f(tx)$ (the right-action convention works identically), and let $S_u=e^{u\mathcal L}$. Define
\[
 \Gamma(f,h)(x)=\frac12\sum_t(f(tx)-f(x))(h(tx)-h(x)),\qquad\Gamma(f)=\Gamma(f,f).
\]
Reversibility gives $-\mathbb E f\mathcal Lh=\mathbb E\Gamma(f,h)$. The sum of transpositions is central in the group algebra of $H$, so $T_tS_u=S_uT_t$. Jensen yields $\Gamma(S_uh)\le S_u\Gamma(h)$. Differentiation proves
\[
 S_u(h^2)-(S_uh)^2=2\int_0^uS_v\Gamma(S_{u-v}h)\,dv.
\]
Each integrand is at least $\Gamma(S_uh)$ by the preceding commutation inequality. Therefore $\Gamma(S_uh)\le1/(2u)$ when $|h|\le1$. By $L^1$ duality, reversibility, and Cauchy--Schwarz for the finite sum defining $\Gamma$,
\[
 \lVert f-S_uf\rVert_1\le\sup_{|h|\le1}\int_0^u\mathbb E\sqrt{\Gamma(f)}\,
 \lVert \sqrt{\Gamma(S_vh)}\rVert_\infty\,dv
 \le\sqrt{2u}\,\mathbb E\sqrt{\Gamma(f)}.
\]
The last bound is independent of the test function $h$.
On each $H$-orbit the generator has gap at least $m$: lift functions to the product of uniform symmetric groups, apply \eqref{eq:external-poincare} on each block, and tensorize variance. For an indicator of conditional mass $\alpha$, positivity preservation implies
\[
 \lVert f-S_uf\rVert_{1,\mathrm{orbit}}
 =2\{\operatorname{Var} f-\operatorname{Cov}(f,S_uf)\}
 \ge(1-e^{-mu})\,2\alpha(1-\alpha).
\]
The covariance inequality follows by diagonalizing the symmetric generator. Average over orbits, take $u=1/m$, and use $\Gamma(\1_E)=R_{E,H}/2$ to prove \eqref{eq:sqrt-local}. A single block gives \eqref{eq:sqrt-boundary}.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:physical}
Let $J(i)=n+1-i$. For a fixed cut vector, reversing the order of packets while preserving each packet's internal order is the position permutation $R_cJ$. The physical and packet-reversal chains have identical total-variation and relative-entropy distances from uniform at every integer time.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Reading the one-line permutation $R_c$ backwards reverses packet order and reverses the internal reversal already made; this is exactly $R_cJ$. Reflection of cuts gives $JR_cJ=R_{c^{\rm ref}}$, with $c^{\rm ref}_i=c_{n-i}$ and the same cut law. At the kernel level, right reversal, denoted $\mathsf J$, therefore commutes with $P_n$. The physical kernel is $P_n\mathsf J$, so its $t$th power is $P_n^t\mathsf J^t$. A deterministic bijection preserves uniform measure, total variation, and relative entropy, proving the assertion. The identity is a kernel identity; individual packet reversals need not commute with $J$.

\label{prop:onecard}
The $f_k$ form a basis and

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:pair-local-bounds}
Every nearest-neighbor pair edge, including one incident to an adjacent pair, satisfies
\[
 Q_n(x,y)\ge qp^4,\qquad Q_n(x,x)\ge p^4.
\]
Writing $k_p=2\min(p,q)p^2>0$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stir-dom}
 E_{\rm nn}(g)+\sum_i g(i,i+1)^2
 \le C_p\langle g,(I-T_n)g\rangle.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
For a nearest-neighbor pair move, the moving tagged card and its destination form a two-position interval containing no other tagged card. Make that interval a packet of length two, and make the other tagged card a singleton. These requirements prescribe one absent cut and at most four present cuts. They are compatible even if the initial or final tagged positions are adjacent; shared requirements are the same present cut. Missing exterior boundaries only increase the event probability. Thus the move has probability at least $qp^4$. Making both tagged cards singletons prescribes at most four present cuts and proves the holding bound.
Symmetry gives $\langle g,(I-Q_n)g\rangle\ge qp^4E_{\rm nn}(g)$. By \eqref{eq:T-def},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:killed-energy}
 \mathcal E_n(g):=\langle g,(I-T_n)g\rangle
 =\frac12\sum_{x,y}Q_n(x,y)(g(x)-g(y))^2
 +\sum_i r_i g(i,i+1)^2.
\end{equation}
Since $r_i\ge k_p$, \eqref{eq:stir-dom} follows. No nonadjacent endpoint square is attributed to the killing term.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:killed-gap}
For fixed $p\in(0,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:killed-gap}
 n^2(1-\rho_n)\longrightarrow D\pi^2=\kappa.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
We give both variational bounds, specifying the interpolation and all limiting operations.

\paragraph{Interior jump law and uniform moments.}
In infinite volume a tagged displacement is $Z=G_R-G_L$, so direct convolution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jump-law}
 k(u):=\mathbb P(Z=u)=\frac{p}{2-p}q^{|u|},\quad
 \sum_uuk(u)=0,\quad\sum_uu^2k(u)=2D.
\end{equation}
Finite-deck tagged displacements are bounded in absolute value by $G_L+G_R$, so they have uniformly exponentially decaying tails. Sorting the two destinations minimizes their sum of squared displacements from the ordered starting positions: if two destinations are reversed, switching their assignments lowers the squared cost by a nonnegative product of their separations. Thus the squared displacement of the ordered pair is at most the sum of squared tagged displacements. It follows that the ordered-pair displacement has bounded moments of every fixed order and a uniform exponential tail.

Fix $0<\delta<1/3$. For pairs whose endpoints and mutual separation are all at least $\delta n$, stop each packet exploration after $\lfloor\delta n/4\rfloor$ gaps. The four inspected sets are disjoint. The stopped explorations agree with independent infinite-volume explorations unless at least one survives the stopping length, an event of probability at most $C_pq^{\delta n/4}$. Hence for each fixed finite set of jumps $(u,v)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-local}
 Q_n((i,j),(i+u,j+v))=k(u)k(v)+O_p(q^{\delta n/4})
\end{equation}
uniformly on these pairs. The ordered and tagged descriptions agree on the successful stopped event.

\paragraph{Rayleigh upper bound.}
Use $h_n(i,j)=f_1(i)-f_1(j)$. The cosine identities give
\[
 \sum_{i<j}h_n(i,j)^2=n\sum_if_1(i)^2-\Big(\sum_if_1(i)\Big)^2=n^2/2.
\]
On $\mathcal T=\{(x,y):0<x<y<1\}$ put $h(x,y)=\cos(\pi x)-\cos(\pi y)$. Symmetry on the square gives
\[
 \int_\mathcal T h^2=1/2,\qquad\int_\mathcal T|\nabla h|^2=\pi^2/2.
\]
For fixed $M$, retain $|u|,|v|\le M$ in the interior. Taylor expansion of $h$ about the cell centers, \eqref{eq:pair-local}, and Riemann summation give the corresponding continuum quadratic form. Uniform second-moment tails bound the omitted jump energy by a quantity tending to zero as $M\to\infty$, uniformly in $n$. A boundary strip of width $\delta n$ contains $O(\delta n^2+n)$ states; the $C/n$-Lipschitz test and the uniform pair second moment bound its energy by $C_p(\delta+n^{-1})$. Let first $n\to\infty$, then $M\to\infty$, and then $\delta\downarrow0$. The kernel energy converges to $D\int_\mathcal T|\nabla h|^2$. Also $|h_n(i,i+1)|\le\pi/n$, so its killing energy is $O_p(n^{-1})$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-limsup}
 \limsup_n n^2\beta_n\le D\pi^2.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Square extension and preservation of norm.}
Take Rayleigh minimizers $g_n$ normalized by $n^{-2}\sum_{i<j}g_n(i,j)^2=1$. Along any subsequence their energies are bounded by \eqref{eq:pair-limsup}. Define a square array
\[
 a_{ij}=\begin{cases}g_n(i,j),&i<j,\\0,&i=j,\\-g_n(j,i),&i>j.\end{cases}
\]
Let $E_\square$ be the sum of squared horizontal and vertical differences of this $n$ by $n$ array, each edge once. Edge counting gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-extension}
 E_\square(a)=2E_{\rm nn}(g_n)+4\sum_i g_n(i,i+1)^2.
\end{equation}
It is bounded by \eqref{eq:stir-dom}. Place $a_{ij}$ at $((i-1/2)/n,(j-1/2)/n)$, copy nearest values to the outer boundary, and interpolate affinely on rectangles divided by their southwest--northeast diagonal. This triangulation is invariant under exchanging coordinates; call the antisymmetric interpolant $\mathcal I_n$. Let $V_n$ equal $a_{ij}$ on $[(i-1)/n,i/n)\times[(j-1)/n,j/n)$.
Elementary affine interpolation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interpolation}
 \int|\nabla \mathcal I_n|^2\le CE_\square(a),\quad
 \lVert \mathcal I_n-V_n\rVert_2^2\le Cn^{-2}E_\square(a),\quad
 \lVert V_n\rVert_2^2=2.
\end{equation}
To verify the bounds, subtract a corner value on each triangle. The affine variation is bounded by its vertex differences, and its gradient is bounded by those differences divided by the mesh. Multiply by triangle area and sum. Each side difference is charged a bounded number of times; a diagonal difference is bounded by twice the two squared orthogonal side differences. Copied boundary values add no new differences, and boundary half-rectangles have comparable aspect ratio. This proves both inequalities with a universal constant.

Here $H^1([0,1]^2)$ denotes square-integrable functions with square-integrable weak first derivatives: a weak derivative $\partial_i W$ satisfies $\int W\partial_i\varphi=-\int(\partial_i W)\varphi$ for every smooth test function $\varphi$ compactly supported in the open square. Strong $L^2$ convergence means convergence in its norm; weak $L^2$ convergence means convergence of inner products against every fixed $L^2$ function. The sequence $\mathcal I_n$ is bounded in this space by \eqref{eq:interpolation}. For completeness, its compactness in $L^2$ can be seen using the orthonormal square cosine basis $e_{ab}(x,y)=c_ac_b\cos(a\pi x)\cos(b\pi y)$, where $c_0=1$ and $c_j=\sqrt2$ for $j>0$. For any $W\in H^1$ put $\widehat W_{ab}=\int_{[0,1]^2}We_{ab}$. Parseval and the weak-derivative identity give
\[
 \lVert W\rVert_2^2=\sum_{a,b\ge0}|\widehat W_{ab}|^2,\qquad
 \lVert \nabla W\rVert_2^2=\pi^2\sum_{a,b\ge0}(a^2+b^2)|\widehat W_{ab}|^2.
\]
These identities follow first for smooth functions by even reflection to a periodic square and Fourier expansion, and then for $H^1$ functions by reflection and mollification. The squared mass above frequency $K$ is at most $C/K^2$. Extract convergent subsequences of finitely many low coefficients and use this tail bound. Thus a subsequence converges strongly in $L^2$ to an antisymmetric $U$ with norm squared two. Bounded gradients have a weakly convergent subsequence in the Hilbert space $L^2$; testing against smooth functions identifies them as the weak derivatives of $U$. Equation \eqref{eq:interpolation} gives the same strong limit for $V_n$. Restricting $U$ to $\mathcal T$ gives a function $g$ with $\int_\mathcal T g^2=1$.

\paragraph{Finite-displacement lower bounds.}
Define
\[
 \mathcal T_\delta=\{(x,y):x>\delta,\ y<1-\delta,\ y-x>\delta\}.
\]
For a fixed vector $w=(u,v)\in\mathbb Z^2$, the scaled difference
$n[V_n(\cdot+w/n)-V_n]$ is bounded in $L^2$ on any such interior: telescoping along at most $|u|+|v|$ grid edges and Cauchy--Schwarz bound its squared norm by $C_wE_\square(a)$. For a smooth test $\varphi$ compactly supported in the interior,
\[
 \int n[V_n(x+w/n)-V_n(x)]\varphi(x)\,dx
 =\int V_n(x)n[\varphi(x-w/n)-\varphi(x)]\,dx.
\]
Strong $L^2$ convergence therefore identifies the weak limit as $w\cdot\nabla g$. Retain only jumps with $|u|,|v|\le M$ and starts whose cell centers lie in $\mathcal T_{\delta/2}$. For large $n$ these include the cells needed for the integrals on $\mathcal T_\delta$. Equation \eqref{eq:pair-local} applies uniformly. Its exponentially small coefficient error multiplies a bounded finite-displacement energy, so vanishes. Weak lower semicontinuity in $L^2$, applied to the finite list of differences, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-jump-liminf}
 \liminf_n\mathcal E_n(g_n)\ge
 D_M\int_{\mathcal T_\delta}|\nabla g|^2,\quad
 D_M=\frac12\left(\sum_{|u|\le M}u^2k(u)\right)
                   \left(\sum_{|v|\le M}k(v)\right).
\end{equation}
Indeed symmetry cancels the mixed derivative term, and the two pure derivative coefficients agree. Killing and all omitted jump terms are nonnegative. Now let $M\to\infty$; \eqref{eq:jump-law} gives $D_M\to D$. Then let $\delta\downarrow0$, exhausting $\mathcal T$, to obtain $\liminf_n\mathcal E_n(g_n)\ge D\int_\mathcal T|\nabla g|^2$.

\paragraph{The continuum constant.}
Antisymmetry gives $\int_{[0,1]^2}U=0$. In the same cosine expansion the constant coefficient vanishes, so
\[
 \int_{[0,1]^2}|\nabla U|^2\ge\pi^2\int_{[0,1]^2}U^2.
\]
Both integrals are twice their triangular counterparts. Hence $\int_\mathcal T|\nabla g|^2\ge\pi^2$. Since the normalization makes $\mathcal E_n(g_n)=n^2\beta_n$, this proves the lower bound matching \eqref{eq:pair-limsup}. Every subsequence attaining the original liminf admits this extraction, so the asserted limit holds. The diagonal Dirichlet condition is encoded by the antisymmetric extension; no unproved convergence of boundary traces is required.

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:mean-gap}
If $Z$ has a finite integer log-concave law, then across every threshold $t$ with positive mass on both sides,
\[
 \mathbb E[Z\mid Z>t]-\mathbb E[Z\mid Z\le t]\ge\sqrt{\operatorname{Var} Z}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
First let $W\ge0$ be integer log-concave and write $S_j=\mathbb P(W\ge j)$. On its positive support,
\[
 \frac{\mathbb P(W=j)}{S_j}
 =\left(1+\sum_{h\ge1}\prod_{\ell=0}^{h-1}
 \frac{p_{j+\ell+1}}{p_{j+\ell}}\right)^{-1}
\]
is increasing, because every successive product of mass ratios decreases when the starting index increases. Products stop at the upper end of the support, and terms beyond it are zero. Thus $S_{j+1}/S_j=1-p_j/S_j$ decreases; initial zeros in the mass function give survival ratios equal to one and cause no problem. Since $S_0=1$, $S_{i+j}\le S_iS_j$. If $m=\mathbb E W=\sum_{j\ge1}S_j$, then
\[
 \frac12\mathbb E[W(W-1)]=\sum_{i,j\ge1}S_{i+j}\le m^2,
 \qquad\operatorname{Var} W\le m(m+1).
\]
Let $\alpha=\mathbb P(Z\le t)$, $a=t-\mathbb E[Z\mid Z\le t]$, and $b=\mathbb E[Z\mid Z>t]-t-1$. Both are nonnegative. Reflection and translation of the two conditional laws preserve log-concavity, so their variances are at most $a(a+1)$ and $b(b+1)$. With $\Delta=a+b+1$, total variance is at most
\[
 \alpha a(a+1)+(1-\alpha)b(b+1)+\alpha(1-\alpha)\Delta^2.
\]
For fixed $a+b=\Delta-1$ this is convex in $a$ and therefore maximized at an endpoint. At $b=0$ it is $\alpha(2-\alpha)\Delta^2-\alpha\Delta\le\Delta^2$; the other endpoint follows by exchanging $\alpha$ and $1-\alpha$. This proves the result.

The ratio $\pi_{x+1}/\pi_x=(A-x)(s-x)/[(x+1)(B-s+x+1)]$ decreases, so the law is log-concave, and it also proves $\pi_xu_x=\pi_{x+1}d_{x+1}$. The mean is $\mu=As/(A+B)$, and $u_x-d_x=-(A+B)(x-\mu)$. Summing this stationary drift over $x\le t$ cancels interior flows and gives

where $\alpha_t=\mathbb P(X\le t)$ and $m_\pm$ are the two conditional means. For independent copies $X,Y$, Jensen and telescoping give

<!-- lemma-15: body_md -->
\label{lem:hypergeom}
For universal constants $C,C_0$, if $K\ge C_0\log(r+1)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hypergeom}
 \lVert F-\mathbb E_\pi F\rVert_1\le CK^{-3/2}\mathcal E_{\rm ex}(F).
\end{equation}
The case $r=1$ is a one-point space and is understood separately.
<!-- end lemma-15 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
We first prove a conditional-variance inequality for pair resampling, then convert it to an all-sets boundary estimate.

\paragraph{Pair heat-bath gap.}
For every unordered pair $\{i,j\}$, at rate one replace $(X_i,X_j)$ by its conditional law given all other coordinates. Write $Q_{ij}F=\mathbb E[F\mid X_\ell,\ell\notin\{i,j\}]$ for this conditional-averaging operator. The generator is $\mathcal L_{\rm hb}=\sum_{i<j}(Q_{ij}-I)$. It is reversible, and its Dirichlet form is
$\sum_{i<j}\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(F\mid X_\ell,\ell\notin\{i,j\})$.
Use unit-transfer distance $d(x,y)=\frac12\sum_j|x_j-y_j|$, the shortest number of valid unit transfers connecting the states. Such a path exists by transferring units from coordinates where $x$ exceeds $y$ to those where it is smaller; capacities remain respected.

For neighboring states with discrepancy $e_i-e_j$, update the same coordinate pair. Updating $\{i,j\}$ has the same conditional total in the two copies and couples identically, eliminating the discrepancy. If exactly one discrepancy coordinate is updated, the two conditional totals differ by one. In a common uniform random ordering of the available $K_a+K_b$ slots, select the first $s$ versus the first $s+1$ slots. The conditional samples have the right laws and differ in one selected slot, so the resulting states remain at unit-transfer distance. Other updates preserve the discrepancy. Thus distance stays at most one and its survival probability is at most $e^{-t}$, since at every instant its current coordinate pair rings at rate one. Gluing neighboring couplings along a shortest path gives contraction by $e^{-t}$ for arbitrary starts.

For a nonconstant generator eigenfunction $F$ with eigenvalue $-\gamma$, its finite Lipschitz seminorm $\max_{x\ne y}|F(x)-F(y)|/d(x,y)$ is positive. The coupling implies $e^{-\gamma t}\operatorname{Lip}(F)\le e^{-t}\operatorname{Lip}(F)$, hence $\gamma\ge1$. Irreducibility follows because conditional pair resampling permits every valid unit transfer with positive probability. Diagonalizing the generator, which is self-adjoint in $L^2(\pi)$ by reversibility, therefore proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HB-poincare}
 \operatorname{Var}_\pi F\le\sum_{i<j}\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(F\mid X_\ell,\ell\notin\{i,j\}).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Conditional scalar bounds.}
For a pair $i,j$, put $A=K_i$, $B=K_j$, and $s=X_i+X_j$. Conditional on all other coordinates, $X_i$ has the scalar law above. The marginal $s$ is hypergeometric with sample size $A+B$ from $N$ slots of which $m$ are selected. Since $m/N\in[1/3,2/3]$, \cref{lem:concentration} applied also to unselected slots shows that
\[
 \mathbb P\{s\notin[(A+B)/6,5(A+B)/6]\}\le Ce^{-c(A+B)}\le Ce^{-cK}.
\]
For an indicator $F=\1_E$, apply \eqref{eq:HB-poincare}, multiply by two, and use \cref{lem:scalar-exchange} on good conditionings. On the others, $2\operatorname{Var}(F\mid\cdot)\le1/2$. Summation over pairs gives, with $\alpha=\pi(E)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyper-error}
 2\alpha(1-\alpha)\le CK^{-3/2}\mathcal E_{\rm ex}(\1_E)+Cr^2e^{-cK}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{All rare sets.}
Complement $E$ so $\alpha\le1/2$. Denote the last error by $\eta_0$. If $\alpha\ge2\eta_0$, it is at most half of $2\alpha(1-\alpha)$ and can be absorbed. Otherwise
$\log(1/\alpha)\ge cK-2\log r-O(1)\ge c'K$
when $C_0$ is sufficiently large; its size also handles the fixed constants for $r\ge2$. Lift $\1_E$ to the binary multislice of selected and unselected slots. Its smallest class is at least $N/3$. Its complete-transposition boundary is exactly $\mathcal E_{\rm ex}(\1_E)$, since a swap of a selected slot of type $i$ with an unselected slot of type $j$ has $X_i(K_j-X_j)$ realizations; swaps with no count change contribute zero. Equation \eqref{eq:smallset-RT} yields
\[
 2\alpha(1-\alpha)\le2\alpha
 \le\frac C{NK}\mathcal E_{\rm ex}(\1_E)
 \le CK^{-3/2}\mathcal E_{\rm ex}(\1_E).
\]
In the last inequality $N\ge K\ge1$. Both cases prove the indicator estimate, and \cref{lem:coarea} proves \eqref{eq:hypergeom} for every $F$.

The last inequality follows by differentiating $\sqrt{x}\log(1/x)$ on $(0,1]$. Thus one universal $K_\star$ handles these sets as well. \begin{equation}\label{eq:stable-average}
 B_L(Qf)\le4B_L(f),\qquad B_R(Qf)\le4B_R(f).

\label{lem:cell-routing}

 a,a+1,\ldots,b-1,b-2,\ldots,a.
\end{equation}

Choose consecutive blocks of sizes comparable to an integer $b$, and let $Q$ average over label and position permutations within those blocks. There are $R\asymp n/b$ blocks. The erasure estimate in \cref{lem:block-average} gives
\[
 \lVert f-Qf\rVert_1\le Cb\mathcal B_n(f),\qquad \mathcal B_n(Qf)\le4\mathcal B_n(f).
\]
The averaged function is table-measurable. Its smallest cell mean satisfies $\lambda_*\asymp b^2/n$. Provided $b^2/n\ge C_0\log n$, \cref{lem:coarse-table} gives
\[
 \lVert Qf-\mathbb E f\rVert_1\le CR^2\sqrt n\,\mathcal B_n(Qf)
 \le C\frac{n^{5/2}}{b^2}\mathcal B_n(f).
\]
Therefore
\[
 C_n^\star\le C\left(b+\frac{n^{5/2}}{b^2}\right).
\]
Take $b\asymp n^{5/6}$, using a partition into block sizes differing by at most one. The two terms are then of order $n^{5/6}$, and the density hypothesis holds for large $n$. Increase $C$ for the remaining finite set of deck sizes.

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:two-thirds}
For $b^2/n\ge K\log n$ and $1\le b\le n$, with $K$ a sufficiently large universal constant,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiscale-bound}
 C_n^\star\le C\left(b+\frac n{\sqrt b}+\frac{n^2\log n}{b^2}\right).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
 C_n^\star\le Cn^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\qquad(n\ge2).
\]

We give the local estimate, sum it, and then remove its additive exceptional-event error.

\paragraph{A merging step.}
Suppose $F\in[0,1]$ depends only on a table whose current label and position blocks have sizes comparable to $\ell$. Merge neighboring label blocks, so the new label blocks have sizes comparable to $2\ell$, and let $Q_H$ perform the additional within-merged-block label averaging. The same proof will apply to position merging.

Set $\lambda_\ell=c_0\ell^2/n$, with a sufficiently small universal $c_0$, and let $G_\ell$ be the event that all current table entries are at least $\lambda_\ell$. Every cell has hypergeometric mean at least $2\lambda_\ell$. Thus \cref{lem:concentration} and a union bound over at most $n^2$ cells give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:merging-bad}
 \eta_\ell:=U_n(G_\ell^c)\le n^2e^{-c\ell^2/n}.
\end{equation}

For a level set $E_u=\{F>u\}$, let $R_{E_u,H}$ count crossing transpositions within merged label blocks. On $G_\ell$, any crossing table switch has at least $\lambda_\ell^2$ realizations by exchanging one label from each of two indicated cells. All these exchanges lie in the same merged label block. Hence $R_{E_u,H}=0$ or $R_{E_u,H}\ge\lambda_\ell^2$, so
\[
 \sqrt{R_{E_u,H}}\1_{G_\ell}\le\lambda_\ell^{-1}R_{E_u,H}.
\]
The total number of within-merged-block transpositions is at most $Cn\ell$. Applying the local square-root inequality \eqref{eq:sqrt-local}, whose gap parameter is of order $\ell$, gives
\[
 \lVert \1_{E_u}-Q_H\1_{E_u}\rVert_1
 \le\frac C{\lambda_\ell\sqrt\ell}
 B_H^{\rm RT}(\1_{E_u})+C\sqrt n\,\eta_\ell.
\]
Here $B_H^{\rm RT}$ is the sum of the $L^1$ differences over the permitted complete transpositions. Integrating over $0\le u\le1$ and using Jensen for $F-Q_HF$ yields
\[
 \lVert F-Q_HF\rVert_1
 \le\frac C{\lambda_\ell\sqrt\ell}B_H^{\rm RT}(F)
       +C\sqrt n\,\eta_\ell.
\]
Every permitted exchange has endpoints in a block of length $O(\ell)$. The path construction of \cref{lem:routing-basic}, restricted to each such block, has total congestion at most $C\ell^2$ at an adjacent edge. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-merge-cost}
 \lVert F-Q_HF\rVert_1\le C\frac n{\sqrt\ell}B_L(F)+C\sqrt n\,\eta_\ell.
\end{equation}
The same estimate with $B_R$ holds for position merging.

\paragraph{Nested averaging.}
Start with consecutive blocks of sizes between constant multiples of $b$, and average an indicator $f=\1_E$ within both label and position blocks. This initial erasure costs at most $Cb\mathcal B_n(f)$ by \cref{lem:block-average}. Then alternate label and position merging through scales $\ell_j\asymp2^jb$ until both partitions have one block. One construction is to start with a nearly balanced binary bisection tree of the interval $[n]$ and reverse its splits; at each level all lengths differ by a bounded factor. The intermediate label and position scales can differ by at most a factor two. This suffices for every constant in \eqref{eq:merging-bad}--\eqref{eq:local-merge-cost}.

A current function is always the \emph{direct} average of $f$ over the current label and position groups. Indeed, averaging over a coarser subgroup after a nested finer subgroup equals averaging over the coarser subgroup alone. The two sides commute. Thus \cref{lem:block-average} bounds each current boundary by $4\mathcal B_n(f)$; there is no factor $4^j$ after $j$ levels. Summing the geometric costs gives
\[
 \sum_j\frac n{\sqrt{\ell_j}}\le C\frac n{\sqrt b}.
\]
The final average is the constant $\mathbb E f$. There are $O(\log n)$ merging operations and all $\ell_j\ge cb$, so the total additive error is bounded by
\[
 E_n(b)=C n^{5/2}\log n\,e^{-cb^2/n}.
\]
Consequently, for every set $E$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:merging-indicator}
 2\alpha(1-\alpha)\le C\left(b+\frac n{\sqrt b}\right)\mathcal B_n(\1_E)+E_n(b),
 \qquad\alpha=U_n(E).
\end{equation}
If $b$ is comparable to $n$, the same bound follows directly from the one-sided crude inequality, so possible terminal degenerate merges need not be used.

\paragraph{Rare sets.}
Complement $E$ to arrange $\alpha\le1/2$. For $\alpha\ge2E_n(b)$, the additive error in \eqref{eq:merging-indicator} is at most half the left side and is absorbed. If $\alpha<2E_n(b)$, then
\[
 \log(1/\alpha)\ge cb^2/n-\tfrac52\log n-\log\log n-O(1)
 \ge c'b^2/n
\]
under the assumed condition with $K$ sufficiently large. Applying \eqref{eq:smallset-path} to the full permutation multislice, with $N=n$ and $k=1$, gives
\[
 2\alpha(1-\alpha)\le2\alpha
 \le C\frac{n^2\log n}{b^2}\mathcal B_n(\1_E).
\]
Together the two cases imply \eqref{eq:multiscale-bound} for indicators, and coarea gives arbitrary functions. Taking $b\asymp n^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}$ makes the first and third terms of that order and the middle term smaller. The hypothesis holds for large $n$; enlarge the final constant for the finite remainder.

<!-- proposition-9: body_md -->
\label{prop:static-lower}
The constants in \cref{thm:static} satisfy
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{C_n^\star}{\sqrt n}
 \ge \sup_{c>0}\frac{1-e^{-c}}{2\sqrt c}>0.
\]
The supremum is approximately $0.319086$, attained at the positive solution of $e^c=1+2c$.
<!-- end proposition-9 -->
<!-- proof-32: body_md -->
For $1\le m\le n/2$, let $E_m$ be the event that none of the labels $1,\ldots,m$ is in positions $1,\ldots,m$. Sampling without replacement gives
\[
 \alpha_{n,m}:=U_n(E_m)=\frac{\binom{n-m}{m}}{\binom nm}.
\]
Only the label transposition $s_m$ and the position transposition $s_m$ can change this event. Conditional on $E_m$, the probability that label $m+1$ lies in the first $m$ positions is $m/(n-m)$; this is the conditional exit probability for the label move. Similarly the probability that position $m+1$ contains a label at most $m$ is $m/(n-m)$, the exit probability for the position move. Since each transposition preserves $U_n$, its total expected indicator difference is twice its exit flow. Hence
\[
 \mathcal B_n(\1_{E_m})=\frac{4m}{n-m}\alpha_{n,m},\qquad
 C_n^\star\ge\frac{(n-m)(1-\alpha_{n,m})}{2m}.
\]
Choose $m=\lfloor\sqrt{cn}\rfloor$ for fixed $c>0$. The product formula for $\alpha_{n,m}$ gives
\[
 \log\alpha_{n,m}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\log\left(1-\frac m{n-j}\right)
 =-\frac{m^2}{n}+O\left(\frac{m^3}{n^2}\right)\longrightarrow-c.
\]
Division by $\sqrt n$ proves the displayed lower bound. Differentiating $(1-e^{-c})/(2\sqrt c)$ gives the equation $e^c=1+2c$. Convexity of the exponential, its derivative at zero, and its growth at infinity show that it has exactly one positive solution; the function tends to zero at both ends, so that solution is the maximum. The numerical approximation is not needed for the theorem.
