<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:rate-vanishes}
Let $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Z}$ be finite alphabets, let $Q\in\Delta(\mathcal{X})$ be a fixed base distribution, and let $P_Z$ be a fixed distribution on $\mathcal{Z}$. For $\varepsilon\ge 0$, define the feasible set
\[
\mathcal{F}(\varepsilon)
:=
\left\{
\bigl(P_{X\mid Z=z}\bigr)_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}
:
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{X\mid Z=z},Q)\le \varepsilon
\ \text{for all } z\in\mathcal{Z}
\right\}.
\]
Define the constrained capacity
$C(\varepsilon)
:=
\sup_{\bigl(P_{X\mid Z=z}\bigr)\in\mathcal{F}(\varepsilon)}
I(Z;X),$
where the mutual information is computed under the joint law
$P_{Z,X}(z,x)=P_Z(z)P_{X\mid Z=z}(x).$
Then $\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0} C(\varepsilon)=0.$
In particular, any achievable embedding rate under the uniform per-key TV constraint must approach $0$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We first show $C(0)=0$. If
$\bigl(P_{X\mid Z=z}\bigr)_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\in\mathcal{F}(0),$
then for every $z\in\mathcal{Z}$,
$d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{X\mid Z=z},Q)=0.$
Since total variation distance vanishes if and only if the two distributions are equal~\citep{keener2010theoretical}, it follows that
$P_{X\mid Z=z}=Q,$ for all $ z\in\mathcal{Z}.$
Hence the conditional law of $X$ does not depend on $Z$, so $X$ and $Z$ are independent. Therefore
$I(Z;X)=0.$
Taking the supremum over all feasible channels shows that $C(0)=0.$

We now prove that $C(\varepsilon)\to 0$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$. Let $(\varepsilon_n)_{n\ge 1}$ be any sequence with $\varepsilon_n\downarrow 0$. For each $n$, choose a feasible channel family
$\bigl(P^{(n)}_{X\mid Z=z}\bigr)_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\in\mathcal{F}(\varepsilon_n)$
such that
$I_n := I_{P^{(n)}}(Z;X)\ge C(\varepsilon_n)-\frac1n.$
Because $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Z}$ are finite, the product of simplices
$\prod_{z\in\mathcal{Z}} \Delta(\mathcal{X})$
is compact~\citep{Munkers84}. Hence, there exists~\citep{rudin1964principles} a subsequence, which we still index by $n$, such that for every $z\in\mathcal{Z}$,
$P^{(n)}_{X\mid Z=z}\to P^\star_{X\mid Z=z}$
for some limiting channel family $\bigl(P^\star_{X\mid Z=z}\bigr)_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}$.

Since each $P^{(n)}$ is feasible,
$d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(P^{(n)}_{X\mid Z=z},Q\right)\le \varepsilon_n
$ for all $z.$
Letting $n\to\infty$ gives
$d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(P^\star_{X\mid Z=z},Q\right)=0
$ for all $ z,$
and therefore
$P^\star_{X\mid Z=z}=Q $ for all $z\in\mathcal{Z}.$
So under the limiting channel, $X$ is independent of $Z$, and thus
$I_{P^\star}(Z;X)=0.$

Because mutual information is continuous in the joint distribution on finite alphabets~\citep{polyanskiy2025information, CoverT2006}, we have
$I_n = I_{P^{(n)}}(Z;X)\to I_{P^\star}(Z;X)=0.$
But
$C(\varepsilon_n)\le I_n+\frac1n,$
so taking limits yields
$\limsup_{n\to\infty} C(\varepsilon_n)\le 0.$
Since mutual information is always nonnegative,
$C(\varepsilon_n)\ge 0,$
hence
$\lim_{n\to\infty} C(\varepsilon_n)=0.$

Because this holds for every sequence $\varepsilon_n\downarrow 0$, it follows that
$\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0} C(\varepsilon)=0.$
This proves the claim.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main-correct-converse}
Assume the conditional-memoryless model. Let a sequence of length-$n$ watermarking schemes satisfy the uniform per-key $\varepsilon$-TV constraint, and let the decoder error probability
\(
P_e^{(n)}:=\Pr[\widehat M\neq M]
\)
tend to zero as $n\to\infty$. If
\(
R_n:=\frac{k}{n}
\)
denotes the embedded rate in bits per token, then for every $n$,
\(
R_n
\le
\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n \widetilde C_t(\varepsilon)
+
\frac{1+nR_n P_e^{(n)}}{n},
\)
 where $\widetilde C_t(\varepsilon)$ is the universal constrained channel capacity given in the Appendix as Definition~\ref{def:universal-Ct}. Consequently, if $(R_n)_n$ is bounded and $P_e^{(n)}\to 0$, then
\(
\limsup_{n\to\infty} R_n
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n \widetilde C_t(\varepsilon).
\)

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:correct-converse}
Assume the conditional-memoryless model. Let a sequence of length-$n$ watermarking schemes satisfy the uniform per-key $\varepsilon$-TV constraint, and let the decoder error probability
\[
P_e^{(n)}:=\Pr[\widehat M\neq M]
\]
tend to zero as $n\to\infty$. If
\[
R_n:=\frac{k}{n}
\]
denotes the embedded rate in bits per token, then for every $n$,
\[
R_n
\le
\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n \widetilde C_t(\varepsilon)
+
\frac{1+nR_n P_e^{(n)}}{n}.
\]
Consequently, if $(R_n)_n$ is bounded and $P_e^{(n)}\to 0$, then
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty} R_n
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n \widetilde C_t(\varepsilon).
\]

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since $M$ is uniform on $\mathcal M$ with $|\mathcal M|=2^k$,
\[
H(M)=k=nR_n.
\]
By Fano's inequality~\citep{CoverT2006},
\[
H(M\mid X_{1:n},V_{1:n})
\le
h_2(P_e^{(n)}) + P_e^{(n)}\log(|\mathcal M|-1)
\le
1+nR_nP_e^{(n)}.
\]
Therefore,
\[
nR_n
=
H(M)
=
I(M;X_{1:n},V_{1:n}) + H(M\mid X_{1:n},V_{1:n}),
\]
so
\[
nR_n
\le
I(M;X_{1:n},V_{1:n}) + 1+nR_nP_e^{(n)}.
\]
Because $M$ and $V_{1:n}$ are independent,
\[
I(M;X_{1:n},V_{1:n})
=
I(M;X_{1:n}\mid V_{1:n}).
\]
Hence
\[
nR_n
\le
I(M;X_{1:n}\mid V_{1:n}) + 1+nR_nP_e^{(n)}.
\]
Applying the chain rule gives
\[
I(M;X_{1:n}\mid V_{1:n})
=
\sum_{t=1}^n I(M;X_t\mid X_{1:t-1},V_{1:n}).
\]

Fix $t$. Let
\[
U_t := (X_{1:t-1},V_{1:n}).
\]
Since $Z_t$ is a deterministic function of $(M,V_t)$, and by the conditional-memoryless assumption
\[
P_{X_t\mid M,V_{1:n},X_{1:t-1}}
=
P_{X_t\mid Z_t},
\]
we have the conditional Markov chain
\[
M \longrightarrow Z_t \longrightarrow X_t
\qquad \text{given } U_t.
\]
Therefore, by data processing~\citep{CoverT2006},
\[
I(M;X_t\mid U_t)\le I(Z_t;X_t\mid U_t).
\]
Thus
\[
I(M;X_{1:n}\mid V_{1:n})
\le
\sum_{t=1}^n I(Z_t;X_t\mid U_t).
\]

Now expand the conditional mutual information:
\[
I(Z_t;X_t\mid U_t)
=
\sum_{u} P_{U_t}(u)\, I(Z_t;X_t\mid U_t=u).
\]
Fix any $u$ with $P_{U_t}(u)>0$. Conditioned on $U_t=u$, the channel from $Z_t$ to $X_t$ is still the same single-letter channel family
\[
(P_{X_t\mid Z_t=z})_{z\in\mathcal Z},
\]
while the input law of $Z_t$ becomes the posterior distribution
\[
P_{Z_t\mid U_t=u}.
\]
Because the scheme satisfies the uniform per-key $\varepsilon$-TV constraint, this channel family belongs to $\mathcal F_t(\varepsilon)$. Hence, by Definition~\ref{def:universal-Ct},
\[
I(Z_t;X_t\mid U_t=u)\le \widetilde C_t(\varepsilon).
\]
Since this holds for every $u$,
\[
I(Z_t;X_t\mid U_t)\le \widetilde C_t(\varepsilon).
\]
Therefore,
\[
I(M;X_{1:n}\mid V_{1:n})
\le
\sum_{t=1}^n \widetilde C_t(\varepsilon).
\]

Substituting back into the Fano bound yields
\[
nR_n
\le
\sum_{t=1}^n \widetilde C_t(\varepsilon) + 1+nR_nP_e^{(n)}.
\]
Dividing by $n$ gives
\[
R_n
\le
\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n \widetilde C_t(\varepsilon)
+
\frac{1+nR_nP_e^{(n)}}{n}.
\]
This proves the finite-$n$ bound.

If $(R_n)_n$ is bounded and $P_e^{(n)}\to 0$, then the additive term on the right tends to $0$, so
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty} R_n
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n \widetilde C_t(\varepsilon).
\]

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:tv-achievability}
Assume now that:

\begin{enumerate}
\item $Q_t\equiv Q$ does not depend on $t$;
\item the feasible set is the same at every time, i.e.
\[
\mathcal{F}_t(\varepsilon)\equiv \mathcal{F}(\varepsilon);
\]
\item the encoder uses a fixed channel family
\[
\bigl(P_{X\mid Z=z}\bigr)_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\in \mathcal{F}(\varepsilon)
\]
at every time;
\item the induced state process $Z_1,\dots,Z_n$ is i.i.d. with marginal $P_Z$.
\end{enumerate}

Then any rate
\[
R < I(Z;X)
\]
is achievable with vanishing block error probability. Consequently, any rate
\[
R < C(\varepsilon)
:=
\sup_{\bigl(P_{X\mid Z=z}\bigr)_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\in \mathcal{F}(\varepsilon)}
I(Z;X)
\]
is achievable.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Under the stated assumptions, the induced channel from $Z^n=(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)$ to $X^n=(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ is a discrete memoryless channel with transition law
\[
P_{X^n\mid Z^n}(x^n\mid z^n)=\prod_{t=1}^n P_{X\mid Z}(x_t\mid z_t).
\]
Fix any channel family in $\mathcal{F}(\varepsilon)$ and let the input distribution of $Z$ be the induced marginal $P_Z$. Let $R<I(Z;X)$.

Next, construct a random codebook~\citep{bok:MW} by drawing
\[
z^n(1),\dots,z^n(2^{nR})
\]
independently, each with i.i.d. components distributed according to $P_Z$. To transmit message $m$, send the corresponding codeword $z^n(m)$ through the channel, producing $x^n$ according to
\[
\prod_{t=1}^n P_{X\mid Z}(x_t\mid z_t(m)).
\]
At the decoder, use jointly typical decoding: declare $\widehat m$ to be the unique index such that $(z^n(\widehat m),x^n)$ is jointly typical.

By the standard random-coding theorem for discrete memoryless channels~\citep{bok:MW}, the average probability of error over the random code ensemble tends to zero as $n\to\infty$ whenever
\[
R<I(Z;X).
\]
Hence there exists at least one deterministic codebook with block error probability tending to zero at every rate below $I(Z;X)$.

Since the chosen channel family belongs to $\mathcal{F}(\varepsilon)$, the resulting scheme satisfies the per-key TV constraint. Finally, because the argument holds for every feasible channel family, taking the supremum over all
\[
\bigl(P_{X\mid Z=z}\bigr)_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\in\mathcal{F}(\varepsilon)
\]
shows that every rate
\[
R<C(\varepsilon)
\]
is achievable.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:sequence-tv}
Fix a message $m\in\mathcal{M}$ and a realized side-information sequence $v_{1:n}\in\mathbb{F}_q^n$. Let
\[
P^{(m,v_{1:n})}_{X_{1:n}}
:=
P_{X_{1:n}\mid M=m,V_{1:n}=v_{1:n}}
\]
denote the watermarked sequence law, and let
\[
Q^{(n)}_{X_{1:n}}
:=
\prod_{t=1}^n Q_t
\]
denote the reference product law formed from the base next-token distributions.
Assume that, conditioned on $(M,V_{1:n})=(m,v_{1:n})$, the watermarked sequence law factors as
\[
P^{(m,v_{1:n})}_{X_{1:n}}(x_{1:n})
=
\prod_{t=1}^n P_{X_t\mid M=m,V_t=v_t}(x_t),
\]
and that for every $t\in[1:n]$,
\[
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(P_{X_t\mid M=m,V_t=v_t},\,Q_t\right)\le \varepsilon_t.
\]
Then
\[
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(P^{(m,v_{1:n})}_{X_{1:n}},\,Q^{(n)}_{X_{1:n}}\right)
\le
1-\prod_{t=1}^n (1-\varepsilon_t).
\]
In particular, if $\varepsilon_t\le \varepsilon$ for all $t$, then
\[
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(P^{(m,v_{1:n})}_{X_{1:n}},\,Q^{(n)}_{X_{1:n}}\right)
\le
1-(1-\varepsilon)^n
\le
n\varepsilon.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For each $t$, write
\[
P_t := P_{X_t\mid M=m,V_t=v_t},
\qquad
Q_t := Q_t.
\]
By assumption,
\[
P^{(m,v_{1:n})}_{X_{1:n}} = \bigotimes_{t=1}^n P_t,
\qquad
Q^{(n)}_{X_{1:n}} = \bigotimes_{t=1}^n Q_t.
\]

For each $t$, by the coupling characterization of total variation distance, there exists a coupling
\[
\pi_t \in \Pi(P_t,Q_t)
\]
of random variables $(Y_t,Z_t)$ such that
\[
\Pr[Y_t\neq Z_t] = d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_t,Q_t)\le \varepsilon_t.
\]
Take these couplings independently across $t$, producing a joint coupling of
\[
Y_{1:n}=(Y_1,\dots,Y_n)\sim \bigotimes_{t=1}^n P_t
\]
and
\[
Z_{1:n}=(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)\sim \bigotimes_{t=1}^n Q_t.
\]
Under this product coupling,
\[
\Pr[Y_{1:n}=Z_{1:n}]
=
\prod_{t=1}^n \Pr[Y_t=Z_t]
=
\prod_{t=1}^n \bigl(1-\Pr[Y_t\neq Z_t]\bigr)
\ge
\prod_{t=1}^n (1-\varepsilon_t).
\]
Therefore,
\[
\Pr[Y_{1:n}\neq Z_{1:n}]
=
1-\Pr[Y_{1:n}=Z_{1:n}]
\le
1-\prod_{t=1}^n (1-\varepsilon_t).
\]
Applying again the coupling characterization of total variation distance gives
\[
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\bigotimes_{t=1}^n P_t,\ \bigotimes_{t=1}^n Q_t\right)
\le
\Pr[Y_{1:n}\neq Z_{1:n}]
\le
1-\prod_{t=1}^n (1-\varepsilon_t).
\]
This proves the first claim.

For the second claim, if $\varepsilon_t\le \varepsilon$ for all $t$, then
\[
1-\prod_{t=1}^n (1-\varepsilon_t)
\le
1-(1-\varepsilon)^n.
\]
Finally, Bernoulli's inequality implies
\[
(1-\varepsilon)^n \ge 1-n\varepsilon
\qquad \text{for } \varepsilon\in[0,1],
\]
and hence
\[
1-(1-\varepsilon)^n \le n\varepsilon.
\]
This proves the uniform bound.

Consider the binary alphabet $\mathcal X=\{0,1\}$ and let
$Q_X(0)=Q_X(1)=\tfrac12$.
Let the secret key $S$ be uniform on $\{0, 1\}$:
$\Pr[S=0]=\Pr[S=1]=\tfrac12.$
For every message $m$, define
$P_{X\mid S=0,M=m}(0)=1,P_{X\mid S=0,M=m}(1)=0,$

=

=

Q_X(0),$

=

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:average-key-detectable}
Let \(\mathcal X=\mathcal V=\{1,\ldots,N\}\), and let
\(Q(x)=\frac1N, x\in\mathcal X.\)
Let the secret key \(V\sim \mathrm{Unif}(\mathcal V)\). For each realized key value \(v\in\mathcal V\),
define the keyed output distribution
\(P_{X|V=v}=\delta_v.\)
Then the following holds:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The scheme is perfectly distortion-free on average over the key:
    \(\mathbb E_V[P_{X|V}] = Q.\)

    \item The scheme is far from per-key distortion-free. For every \(v\in\mathcal V\),
    \(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{X|V=v},Q) = d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_v,Q) =
    1-\frac1N. \)

    \item If the detector observes both \(X\) and the realized key \(V\), then the null law
    \( H_0:\quad V\sim \mathrm{Unif}(\mathcal V),\quad X\sim Q,\quad X\perp V \)
    and the watermarked law
    \(H_1:\quad V\sim \mathrm{Unif}(\mathcal V),\quad X=V\)
    have total variation distance
    \(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{X,V}^{H_1},P_{X,V}^{H_0})
    =
    1-\frac1N.\)
    In particular, the test
    \(\Phi(x,v)=\mathbf 1\{x=v\}\)
    satisfies
    \[
    \mathbb P_{H_1}[\Phi(X,V)=1]=1,
    \quad
    \mathbb P_{H_0}[\Phi(X,V)=1]=\frac1N,
    \]
    and hence has distinguishing advantage \(1-1/N\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, perfect average-key distortion-freeness does not prevent strong detectability once the
realized key is used by the detector. Moreover, for every \(\varepsilon<1\), choosing \(N\) large enough
so that
\(1-\frac1N>\varepsilon\)
gives an average-key perfectly distortion-free scheme whose keyed detection advantage exceeds the
per-key TV budget \(\varepsilon\).

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
First, for each \(x\in\mathcal X\),
\(\mathbb E_V[P_{X|V}(x)]
=
\frac1N\sum_{v=1}^N \delta_v(x)
=
\frac1N
=
Q(x).\)
Therefore
\(\mathbb E_V[P_{X|V}]=Q,\)
so the scheme is perfectly distortion-free on average over the key.

Next, fix \(v\in\mathcal V\). Since \(P_{X|V=v}=\delta_v\) and \(Q\) is uniform,
\(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_v,Q)
=
\frac12\sum_{x=1}^N |\delta_v(x)-Q(x)|.
\)
The term \(x=v\) contributes
\(
\left|1-\frac1N\right|=1-\frac1N,
\)
while the remaining \(N-1\) terms contribute
\(
\sum_{x\neq v}\left|0-\frac1N\right|
=
\frac{N-1}{N}
=
1-\frac1N.
\)
Thus
\(
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_v,Q)
=
\frac12\left(1-\frac1N+1-\frac1N\right)
=
1-\frac1N.
\)

It remains to compute the joint distinguishability: Under \(H_1\),
\[
P_{X,V}^{H_1}(x,v)
=
\begin{cases}
\frac1N, & x=v,\\
0, & x\neq v.
\end{cases}
\]
Under \(H_0\), \(X\) and \(V\) are independent uniforms, so
\(
P_{X,V}^{H_0}(x,v)=\frac1{N^2}
\quad \forall x,v.
\)
Therefore
\[
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{X,V}^{H_1},P_{X,V}^{H_0})
=
\frac12
\sum_{v=1}^N
\sum_{x=1}^N
\left|
P_{X,V}^{H_1}(x,v)-P_{X,V}^{H_0}(x,v)
\right|.
\]
For the \(N\) diagonal pairs \(x=v\), the absolute difference is
\(
\left|\frac1N-\frac1{N^2}\right|
=
\frac{N-1}{N^2}.
\)
For the \(N^2-N\) off-diagonal pairs \(x\neq v\), the absolute difference is
\(
\left|0-\frac1{N^2}\right|
=
\frac1{N^2}.
\)
Hence
\(
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{X,V}^{H_1},P_{X,V}^{H_0})
=
\frac12
\left[
N\cdot \frac{N-1}{N^2}
+
(N^2-N)\cdot \frac1{N^2}
\right].
\)
Simplifying,
\[
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{X,V}^{H_1},P_{X,V}^{H_0})
=
\frac12
\left[
\frac{N-1}{N}
+
\frac{N-1}{N}
\right]
=
1-\frac1N.
\]
Finally, for the test
\(
\Phi(x,v)=\mathbf 1\{x=v\},
\)
we have under \(H_1\) that \(X=V\) always, so
\(
\mathbb P_{H_1}[\Phi(X,V)=1]=1.
\)
Under \(H_0\), \(X\) and \(V\) are independent uniform random variables on \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\), so
\(
\mathbb P_{H_0}[\Phi(X,V)=1]
=
\sum_{v=1}^N \mathbb P[V=v]\mathbb P[X=v]
=
N\cdot \frac1N\cdot \frac1N
=
\frac1N.
\)
Thus the test has distinguishing advantage
\(
1-\frac1N.
\)
This proves the proposition.
