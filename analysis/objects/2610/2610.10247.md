semigroup on a nonatomic $\sigma$-finite measure space $(X,\mathcal F,\mu)$.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:stein-maximal}
Let $1<p<\infty$, and let $(T_t)_{t\geq0}$ be a strongly continuous
positive symmetric sub-Markovian semigroup on a $\sigma$-finite measure space
$(X,\mathcal F,\mu)$.  Then, 
for every $f\in L^p(\mu)$,
\[
 \|T^*f\|_{L^p(\mu)}\leq p'\|f\|_{L^p(\mu)}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \left\|\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t>0}|T_tf|\right\|_{L^p(\mu)}
 \leq p'\|f\|_{L^p(\mu)}.
\]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Assume that $P$ is a positive contraction on $L^1$ and $L^{\infty}$ which is self-adjoint on $L^2$ and satisfies the sub-Markov inequality $P \mathbf 1 \leq \mathbf 1.$ Then, \cite{Starr1966} implies that
\[
    \left\|\sup_{n\geq0}|P^{2n} f|\right\|_{L^p}
    \leq p' \|f\|_{L^p};
\]
see \cite[Estimate~A, p.~489; see also p.~490]{Bradley1991} for the exact constant $p'.$
Applying this estimate to $P=T_{h/2},$ $h>0,$ and using the semigroup property $P^{2n}=T_{nh},$ we obtain
\[
    \left\|\sup_{n\geq0}|T_{nh}f|\right\|_{L^p}
    \leq p'\|f\|_{L^p}.
\]
Now, taking $h=2^{-k}$, letting $k\to\infty,$ and using the monotone convergence theorem, we obtain the maximal inequality for the supremum restricted to positive dyadic numbers. Finally, approximating arbitrary $t>0$ by dyadic numbers and
using the strong $L^p$-continuity of $(T_t)_{t\geq0}$ yields
\[
    \left\|\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t>0}|T_tf|\right\|_{L^p}
    \leq p'\|f\|_{L^p},
\]
as desired.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:finite-dyadic}
For every $1<p<\infty$ and every integer $N\geq0$,
\[
 D_{p,N}\leq D_{p,N+1}\leq p'.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}D_{p,N}=p'.
\]
In particular, for every $\tau>0$ there are an integer $N\geq1$ and a
nonzero $F:\Omega_N\to[0,\infty)$ such that
\[
 \left\|\max_{0\leq n\leq N}E_nF\right\|_{L^p(\Omega_N)}
 \geq(p'-\tau)\|F\|_{L^p(\Omega_N)}.
\]
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Identify $\Omega_N$ with the atoms of the dyadic $\sigma$-algebra of depth $N$
on $[0,1)$.  Under this identification, $E_n$ is conditional expectation
onto the dyadic $\sigma$-algebra of depth $n$.  Doob's inequality gives
$D_{p,N}\leq p'$.  If a function on $\Omega_N$ is regarded as a function
on $\Omega_{N+1}$ independent of the last coordinate, all its conditional
expectations are unchanged, and the new terminal expectation equals the
function itself, which was already the $n=N$ term.  Hence
$D_{p,N}\leq D_{p,N+1}$.

It remains to identify the limit. For $x\in[0,1)$, let
$\mathcal DF(x)$ be the supremum of
\[
 \left|\frac1{|I|}\int_I F(y)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} y\right|
\]
over all dyadic intervals $I\subset[0,1)$ containing $x$.
Its sharp $L^p$ norm is $p'$; see
\cite[(1.3) and the paragraph following it, p.~311]{Melas2005}.  If $F$ is
a dyadic step function of depth $N$, then all conditional expectations
beyond depth $N$ equal $F$, and therefore
\[
 \|\mathcal DF\|_p\leq
 \left(\sup_{M\geq1}D_{p,M}\right)\|F\|_p.
\]
Finite-depth dyadic step functions are dense among the nonnegative
functions in $L^p([0,1))$.  Moreover,
\[
 |\mathcal DF-\mathcal DG|\leq\mathcal D(F-G)
\]
for nonnegative $F,G$, so Doob's inequality allows passage to the limit.
It follows that $\|\mathcal D\|_{L^p\to L^p}\leq\sup_M D_{p,M}$.
Sharpness and the already proved upper bound give
$p'\leq\sup_M D_{p,M}\leq p'$.  Hence monotonicity yields
$D_{p,N}\uparrow p'$.  Given $\tau>0$, choose $N$ so that
$D_{p,N}>p'-\tau$; the final statement then follows from
\eqref{eq:finite-dyadic-definition}.

<!-- head-1: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:stein-maximal}, only the lower bound remains. Without loss of generality, $0<\mu(E)<\infty$.
Indeed, by $\sigma$-finiteness there is a measurable
$\widetilde E\subseteq E$ with $0<\mu(\widetilde E)<\infty$.
The map $f\mapsto T_{t_0}(\mathbf 1_{\widetilde E}f)$ is compact,
being the composition of the compact map
$f\mapsto T_{t_0}(\mathbf 1_Ef)$ with the bounded map
$f\mapsto\mathbf 1_{\widetilde E}f$.

Replacing $\mu$ by $\mu/\mu(E)$, we may assume $\mu(E)=1$.
Fix $1<p<\infty$, an integer $N\ge1$, and $\varepsilon>0$.
Recall the definitions of $\Omega_N$, $E_n$, and $D_{p,N}$ from
Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. In what follows, we shall construct an isometric lattice embedding $J: L^p(\Omega_N) \to L^p(X)$ and numbers $s_0> \ldots>s_N>0$ such that 
\[
  \|T_{s_n}JF-JE_nF\|_{L^p(\mu)} \le 2^N\varepsilon \|F\|_{L^p(\Omega_N)}
\]
holds for all $F \in L^p(\Omega_N)$, $0 \le n \le N$. Since $(E,\mu|_E)$ is a nonatomic probability space, we can choose independent
Rademacher functions $(r_m)_{m\ge1}$ on it and extend them by $1$
outside $E$.
By \eqref{eq:Lp-strong-continuity}, there is $0<s_0<t_0$ such that
\[
 \|T_{s_0}\mathbf 1_E-\mathbf 1_E\|_p<\varepsilon.
\]

We choose $k_1<\cdots<k_N$ and $s_0>\cdots>s_N>0$ inductively.
Put $k_0=0$ and, for the indices already chosen, write
\[
 w_S=\mathbf 1_E\prod_{j\in S}r_{k_j},
 \qquad w_\varnothing=\mathbf 1_E.
\]
Suppose that $k_1,\ldots,k_{j-1}$ and $s_0,\ldots,s_{j-1}$ are fixed.
Fix $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,j-1\}$ and set $v_m=w_Sr_m$
for $m>k_{j-1}$.
Since
\[
 \langle v_m,v_\ell\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}
 =\int_Er_mr_\ell\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu=\mathbf 1_{\{m=\ell\}},
\]
by Bessel's inequality, we have $v_m\rightharpoonup0$ weakly in $L^2(\mu)$. Moreover,
$v_m=\mathbf 1_Ev_m$, so the compactness assumption  gives
\[
 \|T_{t_0}v_m\|_2\longrightarrow0.
\]
On $L^2(\mu)$, write $T_t=e^{-tA}$, where $A$ is nonnegative and
self-adjoint, and let $\mathcal P$ be the spectral measure corresponding to $A$. The spectral theorem and H\"older's inequality give,
for each fixed $0<s<t_0$,
\[
 \|T_sv_m\|_2=\bigg(\int_0^{\infty}e^{-sa}\mathcal \, d\mathcal{P}_{v_m,v_m}(a)\bigg)^{1/2}
 \le\|T_{t_0}v_m\|_2^{s/t_0}\|v_m\|_2^{1-s/t_0}
 =\|T_{t_0}v_m\|_2^{s/t_0}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Contractivity gives $\|T_sv_m\|_1\le1$ and $\|T_sv_m\|_\infty\le1$.
Interpolating with the $L^2$ estimate, we obtain
\[
 \|T_sv_m\|_p
 \le\|T_sv_m\|_2^{\min\{2/p,\,2/p'\}}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]

The preceding limits hold for each of the finitely many pairs $(i,S)$
with $0\le i<j$ and $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,j-1\}$. Choose $k_j>k_{j-1}$
so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-compactness-annihilation}
 \|T_{s_i}w_{S\cup\{j\}}\|_p<\varepsilon
\end{equation}
 holds simultaneously for all such pairs. Having fixed $k_j$, use
\eqref{eq:Lp-strong-continuity} to choose $0<s_j<s_{j-1}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-compactness-preservation}
 \|T_{s_j}w_S-w_S\|_p<\varepsilon
 \qquad\bigl(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,j\}\bigr).
\end{equation}

 Once the induction is complete, define
\[
 JF=\mathbf 1_EF(r_{k_1},\ldots,r_{k_N}),
 \qquad F:\Omega_N\to\mathbb C.
\]
Independence gives
\[
 \|JF\|_{L^p(\mu)}^p
 =2^{-N}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_N}|F(\omega)|^p
 =\|F\|_{L^p(\Omega_N)}^p.
\]
Also $J|F|=|JF|$, so $J$ is an isometric lattice embedding.
 
 For $S\subseteq \lbrace 1,...,N\rbrace$, we define the Walsh characters on $\Omega_N$ as
$$W_S(\omega)=\prod_{j\in S}\omega_j~,$$
and observe that the conditional expectation acts diagonally on them, namely
\begin{equation}
 E_nW_S=
 \begin{cases}
  W_S,&S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\\
  0,&S\nsubseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:Walsh-conditional}
\end{equation}
                                                                            
We have $JW_S=w_S$. If $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$, apply
\eqref{eq:local-compactness-preservation} with $j=n$; the initial choice
of $s_0$ covers $n=0$. Otherwise, let $j=\max S>n$ and apply
\eqref{eq:local-compactness-annihilation} to $S\setminus\{j\}$ at time
$s_n$. Together with \eqref{eq:Walsh-conditional}, these estimates give
\[
 \|T_{s_n}JW_S-JE_nW_S\|_p\le\varepsilon
 \qquad(0\le n\le N).
\]

Expand $F=\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}}c_SW_S$. Since
$|c_S|\le\|F\|_{L^1(\Omega_N)}\le\|F\|_{L^p(\Omega_N)}$,
\begin{align*}
 \left\|\max_{0\le n\le N}|T_{s_n}JF-JE_nF|\right\|_p
 &\le\sum_{n=0}^N\sum_S|c_S|
       \|T_{s_n}JW_S-JE_nW_S\|_p\\
 &\le(N+1)2^N\varepsilon\|F\|_{L^p(\Omega_N)}.
\end{align*}
For $F\ge0$, the positivity of and preservation of finite maxima of $J$ yields
\begin{align*}
 \|T^*JF\|_p
 &\ge\left\|\max_{0\le n\le N}T_{s_n}JF\right\|_p\\
 &\ge\left\|\max_{0\le n\le N}E_nF\right\|_{L^p(\Omega_N)}
       -(N+1)2^N\varepsilon\|F\|_{L^p(\Omega_N)}.
\end{align*}
Divide by $\|JF\|_p=\|F\|_{L^p(\Omega_N)}$ for nonzero $F\ge0$.
Taking the supremum in \eqref{eq:finite-dyadic-definition} and then
letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ with $N$ fixed gives
\[
 D_{p,N}\le\|T^*\|_{p\to p}\le p'.
\]
Let $N\to\infty$ and apply Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-dyadic}.
This proves the theorem.

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor: semigroup examples}
Let $d\geq 1$, $1<p<\infty$.
Let $(T_t)_{t\ge0}$ be one of the following strongly continuous positive symmetric sub-Markovian semigroups: 
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item The heat semigroup on $L^p(\mathbb{R}^d,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x)$,  $T_t=\mathcal{H}_t^d=e^{t \Delta}$. 
    \item The periodic heat semigroup on $L^p(\mathbb{T}^d,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} m)$ with $\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} m$ the normalized Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{T}^d$. 
    \item The Dunkl-semigroup $T_t=e^{t\Delta_{\kappa}}$ associated to a general root system and a non-negative multiplicity function $\kappa$; see \cite{DziubanskiHejna} or \cite{Rosler}. 
       \item The Schr\"odinger semigroup on $L^p(\mathbb{R}^d,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x)$, $T_t= e^{-tH}$ with $H=-\Delta + V,$ where $V\ge0$ and $V\in L^1_{{\rm loc}}$; see \cite[Section 2]{KucharskiWrobel}.

\item The heat semigroup $T_t=e^{t\Delta_M}$ on
$L^p(M,\mu)$, where $M$ is a complete connected
$d$-dimensional Riemannian manifold without boundary,
$\mu$ is its Riemannian volume measure, and $\Delta_M$
is the nonpositive self-adjoint Laplace--Beltrami operator;
see \cite[Sections~2.2 and~3.1--3.2]{Grigoryan2006}.
       
       \item The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup on $L^p(\mathbb{R}^d,\gamma_d)$, $T_t=e^{tL}$ with $L=\Delta - x\cdot\nabla$, where $\gamma_d$ is the standard Gaussian measure; see \cite[Section~2.2, (2.27), Theorem 2.5]{Urbina2019} (after a change of Gaussian normalization).

<!-- head-2: body_md -->
Note first that all the described semigroups are sub-Markovian. This is either explicitly stated in the provided references or can be easily deduced from them. 

The common idea in the proofs of items (a)-(e) is to show that $T_t(\mathbf 1_E)$ is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator (hence compact) for a suitable set $E$ and any $t>0.$ This will be deduced from the fact that the kernel of $f\mapsto T(\mathbf 1_E f)$ belongs to the $L^2$ space over the product domain.

We start with the proofs of items (a)--(c). Taking the multiplicity
function $\kappa=0$, we see that (a) is a subcase of (c).
Furthermore, once (a) is established, (b) follows from a maximal
transference principle of Kenig and Tomas
\cite[Theorem~1]{KenigTomas}. We note that, while the preservation
of the constants is not explicitly stated in
\cite[Theorem~1]{KenigTomas}, it can be read from the proof;
see also the first paragraph in \cite[p.~167]{AuscherCarro}. Thus it remains to establish c), for which we shall use the notation in \cite{DziubanskiHejna}.  Denote by $h_t(x,y)$ the Dunkl heat kernel, let $w$ be the Dunkl measure and take $E$ to be a Euclidean ball in $\mathbb{R}^d$. Then, using symmetry, the semigroup property of $h_t$,
and the estimates \cite[eq.~(1.13), eq.~(2.1)]{DziubanskiHejna},
we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\int_E \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} h_t(x,y)^2\,\mathrm dw(x)\,\mathrm dw(y)=\int_{E}h_{2t}(y,y)\,\mathrm dw(y) <\infty,\qquad t>0.
\end{equation*}
This implies that $T_t(\mathbf 1_E)$ is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator; hence, it is compact. An application of Theorem \ref{thm:local-compactness} completes the proof for items a)-c).

Now, item d) follows from a). Indeed, using the Feynman--Kac formula \cite{Sznitman}
(or the Trotter product formula), we see that, for any $f\ge0$,
\[
e^{-tH}f(x)\le \mathcal H_t^d f(x), \qquad x-a.e.\
\]
Thus, repeating the argument used to prove (c) with $\kappa=0$
and $E$ a Euclidean ball, we conclude that the operator
$f\mapsto e^{-tH}(\mathbf 1_E f)$ is Hilbert--Schmidt.
In particular, it is compact, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-compactness} applies.

For (e), the required semigroup properties and smoothness of
its heat kernel $h_t$ follow from
\cite[Theorems~3.1 and~3.3, and (3.11)--(3.12)]{Grigoryan2006}.
For a nonempty relatively compact open set $E\subset M$,
symmetry and the semigroup identity give, for every $t>0$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int_E\int_M h_t(x,y)^2\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(y)
 =\int_E h_{2t}(y,y)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(y)
 \leq\mu(E)\sup_{y\in\overline E}h_{2t}(y,y)<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $f\mapsto T_t(\mathbf 1_Ef)$ is Hilbert--Schmidt, hence
compact. Note that Riemannian volume is nonatomic and $\sigma$-finite,
so Theorem~\ref{thm:local-compactness} applies.

Each of the semigroups in items (f)--(h) is associated to an orthogonal expansion. In particular, their negative generators have discrete spectrum on $L^2$, their eigenvalues can be ordered in an increasing sequence $\lambda_n\to \infty,$ and the corresponding eigenspaces are finite dimensional. Therefore $T_t$ itself is compact for any $t>0$ and Theorem \ref{thm:local-compactness} applies with $E$ being the full space.


 
\qed

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By Bochner subordination, $(S_t)_{t\geq0}$ is a strongly continuous
positive symmetric sub-Markovian semigroup; see \cite[Theorem~5.2, Proposition~13.1, and Remark~13.4]{SSV2012}.

\[
 f\longmapsto
 S_t\mathbf1_{[0,R]}(A)(\mathbf1_Ef)
 =\bigl(S_te^{t_0A}\mathbf1_{[0,R]}(A)\bigr)
   T_{t_0}(\mathbf1_Ef)

 \bigl\|S_t(\mathbf1_Ef)

 \leq e^{-t\Phi(R)}\|f\|_2,
 \qquad f\in L^2(\mu).
\]
Since $\Phi(R)\to\infty$, these compact operators converge in operator
norm to $f\mapsto S_t(\mathbf1_Ef),$ which is therefore also compact. Theorem~\ref{thm:local-compactness} gives the conclusion.

<!-- end corollary-6 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By Theorem \ref{thm:stein-maximal}, only the lower bound remains to be proven.
Since \(J\) is a positive \(L^p\)-isometry, it is a lattice embedding. Note that, for every $f\in L^p(Y)$ with $f\ge0$ and all
$t_1,\ldots,t_N>0$, by positivity and preservation of finite maxima of $J$, we have
\[
J\Big( \sup_{1 \le i \le N} T_{t_i} f \Big)= \sup_{1 \le i \le N} JT_{t_i} f= \sup_{1 \le i \le N} S_{t_i} Jf. 
\]
Therefore, since $J$ is an $L^p$-isometry, we get
\[
\Big\| \sup_{1 \le i \le N} T_{t_i} f \Big\|_{L^p(Y)} \le \|S^*(Jf) \|_{L^p(X)} \le  \|S^* \|_{L^p(X) \to L^p(X)} \|f \|_{L^p(Y)}. 
\]
Since $N$ and $t_1,\dots,t_N$ are arbitrary, this yields
\[
\|T^*f\|_{L^p(Y)} \le \|S^*\|_{L^p(X) \to L^(X)} \|f \|_{L^p(Y)}.
\]
Using positivity and the arbitrariness of $f\ge0$, we obtain
\[
p'=\|T^*\|_{L^p(Y) \to L^p(Y)} \le \|S^* \|_{L^p(X) \to L^p(X)},
\]
which finishes the proof.

We suppress the dependence of $f_j$ on $L$. For every
$\Lambda$-periodic $h\in L^1(Q)$, a change of variables gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-cell-identity}
 \int_{L^{-1}(Q+\lambda)}h(Lx)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x
 =L^{-d}\int_Qh(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x,
 \qquad \lambda\in\Lambda.
\end{equation}
Since $L$ is a multiple of $k$, both $E$ and $Q\setminus E$ are
unions of these cells. Induction therefore gives
\begin{align*}
 \int_Qf_{j+1}(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x
 &=\delta b+a(1-\delta)\int_Qf_j(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x=1,\\
 \int_Qf_{j+1}(x)^p\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x
 &=\delta b^p+a^p(1-\delta)\int_Qf_j(x)^p\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x\\
 &=\delta b^p+\int_Qf_j(x)^p\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-moments}
 \int_Qf_j(x)^p\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x=1+j\delta b^p,
 \qquad 0\le f_j\le a^n,
 \qquad 0\le j\le n.
\end{equation}

For $x\in E$, the choice of $E$ and the identity
$\delta(a-b)=a-1$ give
\[
 \frac{1}{|B_r|}\int_{x+B_r}f_1(y)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} y
 =a-\frac{\delta(a-b)}{|B_r|}
 =a-\frac{a-1}{|B_r|}=:m.
\]
Since $|B_r|>2$, we have $m>(a+1)/2$. Strict convexity of
$t\mapsto t^{-1/p}$ gives
\[
 p'b
 =\frac1\delta\int_{1-\delta}^{1}t^{-1/p}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} t
 <\frac{a+1}{2}<m.
\]

To pass from periodic functions to compactly supported functions,
we first restrict the averaging radii. For nonnegative
$h\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^d)$, write
\[
 \mathscr B_{*,r}^dh(x)
 =\sup_{0<t\le r}\frac{1}{|B_t|}
       \int_{x+B_t}h(y)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} y.
\]
Then $\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_j$ is $\Lambda$-periodic and
$0\le\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_j\le a^n$. In what follows, $C_n$ may
depend on $n$ and on the fixed parameters, but not on $L$, $x$, or
$j\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$. Its value may change from line to line.

On each cell $L^{-1}(Q+\lambda)$, the function $f_{j+1}-f_1$
equals either zero or $a(f_j(L\,\cdot)-1)$, and hence has integral
zero. Thus, in its integral over $x+B_r$, only cells whose closures
meet $\partial(x+B_r)$ contribute. Their union is contained in
\[
 \left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^d:\bigl||y-x|-r\bigr|
       \le\frac{\operatorname{diam}(Q)}L\right\},
\]
whose measure is at most $C_n/L$, uniformly in $x$. Since
$|f_{j+1}-f_1|\le2a^n$, it follows that
\[
 \sup_{x\in E}
 \left|\frac{1}{|B_r|}\int_{x+B_r}f_{j+1}(y)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} y-m\right|
 \le\frac{C_n}{L}.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \int_E(\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_{j+1})^p\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x
 \ge\delta m^p-\frac{C_n}{L}.
\]
On the other hand, if $\operatorname{dist}(x,E+\Lambda)>r/L$, then, for
$0<t\le r$, we have (see Figure~\ref{fig:lattice-construction})
\[
 \frac{1}{|B_{t/L}|}\int_{x+B_{t/L}}f_{j+1}(y)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} y
 =\frac{a}{|B_t|}\int_{Lx+B_t}f_j(y)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} y.
\]
Taking the supremum in $t$ gives
\[
 \mathscr B_{*,r}^df_{j+1}(x)
 \ge a(\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_j)(Lx).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \left|\left\{x\in Q\setminus E:
       \operatorname{dist}(x,E+\Lambda)\le r/L\right\}\right|
 \le\frac{C_n}{L},
\]
because this set lies within distance $r/L$ of finitely many faces
of translates of $E$. We estimate the contribution of this set using
$\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_j\le a^n$. Applying
\eqref{eq:appendix-cell-identity} to
$(\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_j)^p$ and using $a^p(1-\delta)=1$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 \int_Q(\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_{j+1})^p\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x
 &\ge\delta m^p
      +a^p\int_{Q\setminus E}(\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_j)(Lx)^p\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x
      -\frac{C_n}{L}\\
 &=\delta m^p+\int_Q(\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_j)^p\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x
      -\frac{C_n}{L}.
\end{align*}
Summing over $j=0,\ldots,n-1$ and using
$\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_0=1$ gives
\[
 \int_Q(\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_n)^p\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x
 \ge1+n\delta m^p-\frac{C_n}{L}.
\]
Since $m>p'b$,
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1+n\delta m^p}{1+n\delta b^p}
 =\left(\frac mb\right)^p>(p')^p.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:appendix-moments}, this allows us to choose
first $n$ and then $L$ large enough so that
\[
 \|\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_n\|_{L^p(Q)}
 >p'\|f_n\|_{L^p(Q)}.
\]
Fix these choices of $n$ and $L$.

It remains to obtain a smooth compactly supported test function.
For a positive integer $N$, put $u=f_n\mathbf 1_{NQ}$, where
$NQ=\{Nx:x\in Q\}$. Since $NQ$ is the disjoint union of $N^d$
translates of $Q$,
\[
 \|u\|_p^p=N^d\int_Qf_n(x)^p\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x.
\]
If $x\in NQ$ and $\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial(NQ))>r$, then
$\mathscr B_{*,r}^du(x)=\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_n(x)$.
The remaining part of $NQ$ has measure at most $C_nN^{d-1}$.
Periodicity and $\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_n\le a^n$ therefore give
\[
 \|\mathscr B_{*,r}^du\|_p^p
 \ge N^d\int_Q(\mathscr B_{*,r}^df_n)^p\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x
       -C_nN^{d-1}.
\]
The strict inequality on $Q$ now yields, for all sufficiently
large $N$,
\[
 \|\mathscr B_{*,r}^du\|_p>p'\|u\|_p.
\]
Fix such an $N$. By approximating $u$ arbitrarily well in
$L^p(\mathbb R^d)$ by smooth compactly supported functions and
using the boundedness of the maximal operator, we obtain the
same strict inequality for some smooth compactly supported function.
