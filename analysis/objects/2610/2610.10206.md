<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:unknotting-number-link}
    Let $L$ be a link in $S^3$ with $\ell \geq 1$ components.
    The \emph{unknotting number} $\ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L}$ is the minimum number of crossing changes required to turn $L$ into the $\ell$-component unlink.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:band-path}
    A \emph{band path} on a diagram of $L$ consists of
    \begin{itemize}
        \item an edge of the diagram between two neighbouring crossings of $L$;
        \item a sequence of crossing-increasing Reidemeister~II moves we want to perform on that edge, possibly interleaved with Reidemeister~I moves. This is the ``path'' along which we slide the chosen edge to reach a target edge of the diagram.
    \end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rmk:u-orientation-independent}
    The unknotting number is defined purely in terms of crossing changes, and a crossing change simply swaps the overstrand and the understrand at a crossing, independently of any orientation.
    Therefore, $\ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L}$ is well defined for \emph{unoriented} links.
    This is in sharp contrast with many of the invariants we will use to \emph{bound} $\ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L}$ in Appendix~\ref{sec:bounds}, which do require a choice of orientation.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:slice-genus}
    A \emph{slice surface} for a knot $K$ is a compact, connected, oriented surface $F$ smoothly embedded in $B^4$ such that $\partial F = K$.
    The \emph{slice genus} $\ensuremath{{g}_4\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{K}$ of $K$ is the minimal genus of a slice surface for $K$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:weak-strong-genus}
    Let $L = \cup_{i=1}^{\ell} K_i$ be a link.
    A \emph{weak slice surface} for $L$ is a compact, connected, oriented surface $F \subset B^4$ such that $\partial F = L$.
    The \emph{weak slice genus} $\ensuremath{{g}_4\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L}$ is the minimal genus of a weak slice surface for $L$.

    A \emph{strong slice surface} for $L$ is a compact, oriented surface $F \subset B^4$ with $\ell$ disjoint components $F_1, \dots, F_\ell$ such that $\partial F_i = K_i$ for $i \in \{1,\dots,\ell\}$.
    The \emph{strong slice genus} $\ensuremath{{g}_4^{\ast}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L}$ is the minimal \emph{total} genus of a strong slice surface for $L$; i.e.,
    \[
    \ensuremath{{g}_4^{\ast}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L} = \min \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^{\ell} g(F_i) : F = \bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\ell} F_i \subset B^4 \text{ is a strong slice surface for } L \right\}.
    \]
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:ribbon-genus}
    A \emph{ribbon surface} for $K$ is a slice surface $F \subset B^4$ for $K$ with no local maxima with respect to the radial function of $B^4$.
    The \emph{ribbon genus} $\ensuremath{{g}_r\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{K}$ of $K$ is the minimal genus of a ribbon surface for $K$.
    \smallskip
    A knot with $\ensuremath{{g}_r\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{K} = 0$ is called a \emph{ribbon knot}; equivalently, it bounds a \emph{ribbon disc} in $B^4$.
<!-- end definition-5 -->

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rmk:env-limitations}
    The shape is a hard upper bound on diagram size. 
    A diagram exceeding $c_{\max}$ crossings or $\ell_{\max}$ components during an episode cannot be represented (illegal state), and the episode is lost rather than truncated.
    Since band moves may increase the number of crossings, some bands are inadmissible.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:answer-charge-cap}
    Recall that $i_{\text{cap}} = 8$ is the highest invariant value in our datasets.
    We chose $W$ so that one unit of answer costs more than all the actions of a won episode together, and $R_{\text{win}} = W (i_{\text{cap}} + 1)$ so that it exceeds the largest charge, $W i_{\text{cap}}$, by $W$ as well.

    In the unknotting, strong ribbon, and strong slice frameworks, the answer is charged once, when the episode is won.
    In the strong frameworks, a band can reach a state with no legal move, and the episode then ends on an error.
    When we charged the genus band by band in these frameworks, ending an episode early on an error was cheaper than playing on and paying for the genus built, and the agents learned to end their episodes on an error.
    In the unknotting framework, charging every crossing change taught the agent to avoid crossing changes, although they are its only \texttt{end} actions.
    Without the cap, the first wins found in training used many crossing changes and were worth less than a truncated episode, and the episodes of the agent went back to the time limit.
    Since the charge is at most $W i_{\text{cap}}$, every won episode is better rewarded than every episode that is not won, whatever its answer.
    The better reward of a lower answer is guaranteed below the cap. 
    Answers from $i_{\text{cap}}$ and above receive the same charge and are compared only through the length of the path.
    If the initial diagram has at least two crossings, a truncated episode loses less than $R_{\text{win}}$ through its actions and the crossings it adds, so an error is worse rewarded than any other outcome.

    In the ribbon and slice frameworks, the genus is charged as the surface is built, with no cap.
    Here a lower genus is always better rewarded. 
    However, the reward of a won episode is guaranteed to be higher than that of every episode that is not won only up to genus $i_{\text{cap}}$.
    A truncated episode has already paid for the genus it built, so an error can be cheaper than a long truncated episode.
    Since ribbon and slice always have a legal move, they reach an error only when the diagram outgrows the shape.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:g4-lower-bound-unknotting}
For any link $L$, we have $\ensuremath{{g}_4\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L} \leq \ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rmk:orientation-dependence-g4}
The weak slice genus depends on the choice of orientation of $L$, since the bounding surface $F$ must be oriented compatibly with $L$, while the strong slice genus is independent of the orientation.
Since $\ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L}$ is also orientation-independent (Remark~\ref{rmk:u-orientation-independent}), the strongest lower bounds from the slice genus are
\[
\ensuremath{{g}_4^{\ast}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L} \geq \max_{\vec{o}} \ensuremath{{g}_4\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L_{\vec{o}}}, \qquad \ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L} \geq \max_{\vec{o}} \ensuremath{{g}_4\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L_{\vec{o}}},
\]
where $\vec{o} \in \{ +1, -1 \}^{\ell}$ is a choice of orientation.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:lower-bound-unknotting-link}
    Let $L = K_1 \cup \dots \cup K_{\ell}$ be a link.
    Then, we have
    \[
        \ensuremath{\left\lvert \mathrm{lk} \right\rvert\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else\!(#1)\fi}}{L} + \sum_{i=1}^{\ell} \ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{K_i} \leq \ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L},
    \]
    where $\ensuremath{\left\lvert \mathrm{lk} \right\rvert\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else\!(#1)\fi}}{L}$ denotes the absolute linking number of $L$, which is the sum of the absolute values of the linking numbers between the components of $L$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
The inequality of Proposition \ref{prop:lower-bound-unknotting-link} is an equality whenever $L$ is \emph{simply-linked}.
See Theorem \ref{thm:unknotting-simply-linked-links} for details.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:lower-bound-strong-genus-link-components}

\label{thm:diagramUpperBoundLinks}

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:trivial-upper-bound}
For any link $L$,
\[
\ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L} \leq \left\lfloor \frac{\mathrm{c}(L)}{2} \right\rfloor.
\]
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
It is a known fact that any diagram $D$ of $L$ with $c$ crossings can be turned into a diagram of the unlink by changing some of its crossings.
Let $\mathcal{C}(D)$ be the set of crossings of $D$ and $A \subset \mathcal{C}(D)$ be the subset of crossings that yields a diagram $U$ of the unlink if changed.
Let now $S$ be the diagram we obtain from $D$ if we change all the crossings in $B = \mathcal{C}(D) \smallsetminus A$. This is the mirror of $U$, which is again the unlink.
Therefore, we get the unlink with at most $\min \{ \lvert A \rvert, \lvert B \rvert \} \leq \frac{c(D)}{2}$ crossing changes.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rmk:disconnected-mt}
Tristram's Theorem~2$\cdot$27 holds for $\omega = -1$ and for $\omega = \exp(\nicefrac{2q \pi i}{2q+1})$ with $2q+1$ an odd prime \cite[Definitions~2$\cdot$3, 2$\cdot$14]{tristram1969cobordism} and is stated for an arbitrary, possibly disconnected, spanning surface $F \subset B^{4}$. If $F$ has $m$ connected components, it reads
\[
  \lvert \sigma_L(\omega) \rvert + \lvert (\eta_L(\omega)+ 1) - m \rvert \leq 2g(F) + \ell - m.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:murasugi-tristram} is the case $m = 1$, which Kauffman extended to all roots of unity of prime power order.
In the strong slice case, the surface $F = F_1 \sqcup \dots \sqcup F_{\ell}$ is a disjoint union of $\ell$ surfaces, one per component of $L$, so $m = \ell$ and the inequality becomes a bound on the strong slice genus:
\[
    \frac{\lvert \sigma_L(\omega) \rvert + \lvert (\eta_L(\omega)+ 1) - \ell \rvert}{2} \leq \ensuremath{{g}_4^{\ast}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L}.
\]
In particular, if $L$ is strongly slice, then $\sigma_L(\omega) = 0$ and $\eta_L(\omega) = \ell - 1$.
See \cite[Theorem~3]{conway2019signature} for a version valid for locally flat surfaces and for all $\omega$ outside the set of \emph{Knotennullstellen}, and \cite[Theorem~2.1]{florensgilmer2003links} for the connected case.

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor:unlinking-signed-slice-torus}
Let $\nu$ be a slice-torus link invariant, and let $L$ be a link.
If $L$ admits an unknotting sequence consisting of $u_+$ changes of a positive crossing into a negative one and $u_-$ changes of a negative crossing into a positive one, then
\[
    \nu(L) \leq u_{+} \qquad\text{and}\qquad -\nu(L) \leq u_{-} .
\]
Combining the two inequalities, we get $\left\lvert \nu(L) \right\rvert \leq \ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L}$.
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Consider an unknotting sequence from $L$ with $u_{+}$ positive crossing changes, $u_{-}$ negative ones, and $u_{+} + u_{-} = N = \ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L}$.
    We can describe the sequence from $L$ to the unlink $U_{\ell}$ as
    \begin{center}
        \begin{tikzcd}
        L = L^{(0)} \arrow[r, "u_1"] & L^{(1)} \arrow[r, "u_2"] & \cdots \arrow[r, "u_N"] & L^{(N)} = U_{\ell}
        \end{tikzcd}    
    \end{center}
    Let $\delta_j \coloneqq \nu(L^{(j)}) - \nu(L^{(j-1)})$.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item If $u_j$ turns a positive crossing into a negative one (i.e., is one of the $u_{+}$), then in the notation of Proposition~\ref{prop:crossing-change-slice-torus}, we have $L^{(j-1)} = L_{+}$ and $L^{(j)} = L_{-}$, so $0 \leq \nu(L^{(j-1)}) - \nu(L^{(j)}) \leq 1$, which gives $-1 \leq \delta_j \leq 0$.
        \item If $u_j$ turns a negative crossing into a positive one (i.e., is one of the $u_{-}$), then with $L^{(j-1)} = L_{-}$ and $L^{(j)} = L_{+}$, we obtain $0 \leq \nu(L^{(j)}) - \nu(L^{(j-1)}) \leq 1$; that is, $0 \leq \delta_j \leq 1$.
    \end{itemize}
    In particular, by summing all $N$ inequalities,
    \[
        -u_{+} \leq \sum_{j=1}^{N} \delta_j \leq u_{-}.
    \]
    Recall that $\nu(U_{\ell}) = 0$, so 
    \begin{align*}
        -\nu(L) & = \nu(U_{\ell}) - \nu(L) = \nu(L^{(N)}) - \nu(L^{(0)}) \\
        & = \nu(L^{(N)}) - \nu(L^{(N-1)}) + \nu(L^{(N-1)}) - \, \ldots \,  -\nu(L^{(1)})  + \nu(L^{(1)}) - \nu(L^{(0)}) \\
        & = \sum_{j=1}^{N} \delta_j.
    \end{align*}
    Hence,
    \[
        -u_{+} \leq -\nu(L) \leq u_{-},
    \]
    which yields the two stated inequalities.
    Finally, since $u_{+} \geq \max\{\nu(L), 0\}$ and $u_{-} \geq \max\{-\nu(L), 0\}$, we get $\ensuremath{{u}\IfNoValueF{#1}{\if\relax\detokenize{#1}\relax\else(#1)\fi}}{L} = u_+ + u_- \geq \left\lvert \nu(L) \right\rvert$.
