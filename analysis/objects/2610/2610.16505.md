\label{main}

\label{OtoSO}
For each lattice $\Lambda\subset V$ there is a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism: \[\mathcal{M}(O(V), O(\widehat{\Lambda}),W_{2k}\otimes \theta_d) \cong M(SO(V),SO(\widehat{\Lambda}), W_{2k}\otimes \theta_d)\]

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The restriction map from $O(\widehat{V})$ to $SO(\widehat{V})$ induces a Hecke-equivariant injective homomorphism \[\mathcal{M}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Lambda}),W) \longrightarrow M(SO(V),SO(\widehat{\Lambda}),W)\] for any representation $W$ of $O(V)$. The image consists of functions \[\varphi\in M(SO(V),SO(\widehat{\Lambda}),W)\] such that $\varphi(g) = \varphi((-I)\cdot g) = (-I)\cdot \varphi(g)$ for every $g\in SO(\widehat{V})$. When $-I$ acts trivially on $W$ this condition is automatically satisfied and so we get a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism. All of the representations constructed above have this property, establishing the result.

\label{thm: 6.12 in HTV}
Suppose that $\Lambda\subset V$ is an even lattice in a positive definite ternary quadratic space $(V,Q)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Suppose that the associated quaternion algebra $B = \textup{Cl}^0(V)$ has discriminant $D$ and that the order $\mathcal{O} = \textup{Cl}^0(\Lambda)$ is residually unramified everywhere of discriminant $N = \textup{discrd}(\mathcal{O}) = \frac{1}{2}\textup{disc}(\Lambda)$. Let $N^+$ and $N^-$ be the products of the prime powers $p^e||N$ that satisfy $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}) = 1$ and $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}) = -1$ respectively. 

Then for each $k \geq 0$ and each square-free $d\mid N$ we have a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism (for all Hecke operators $T_p$ with $p \nmid N$):
$$\mathcal{S}(O(V), O(\widehat{\Lambda}), W_{2k}\otimes \theta_d) \cong 
\bigoplus_{M|N^-} S_{2k+2}^{M-\textup{new}}(\Gamma_0(MN^{+}), \varepsilon_D^{(MN^{+})}\varepsilon_d^{(MN^{+})})$$
where the sum runs over $M|N^-$ with $v_p(M) \equiv v_p(N) \bmod 2$ for all primes $p \,|\, N^-$.

\label{samespace}
Suppose $\Lambda,\Pi\subset V$ are lattices such that $O(\widehat{\Lambda}) = O(\widehat{\Pi})$. Then for each representation $W$ of $O(V)$ there is an equality of Hecke modules: \[\mathcal{M}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Lambda}),W) = \mathcal{M}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Pi}),W).\]
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $O(\widehat{\Lambda}) = O(\widehat{\Pi})$ is follows that $\mathcal{M}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Lambda}),W) = \mathcal{M}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Pi}),W)$ as spaces. We also have that: \[O(\widehat{\Lambda})\,g\, O(\widehat{\Lambda}) = O(\widehat{\Pi})\,g\, O(\widehat{\Pi})\] for any $g\in O(\widehat{V})$. This implies that we get the same Hecke representatives and hence the same Hecke operators on both sides.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{psip}
Let $\Lambda_p$ be a ternary $\mathbb{Z}_p$-lattice in a quadratic space $(V_p,Q)$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ such that $\Lambda_p\sim\langle a,p,pb\rangle$ for some $a,b\in\{1,r\}$. Then $\Pi_p = \psi_{(p)}(\Lambda_p)$ is an index $p$ sublattice such that $O(\Lambda_p) = O(\Pi_p)$.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
It is clear that $\Pi_p$ is an index $p$ sublattice of $\Lambda_p$ since $\text{det}(\psi_{(p)}) = p$. Note that since $\Lambda_p$ is an elementary lattice (i.e.\ $\Lambda_p^*/\Lambda_p$ is an elementary $p$-group), Theorem $1.1(1)$ of Allcock \cite{allcock2025} tells us that $O(\Lambda_p)$ is maximal in $O(V_p)$ among the orthogonal groups of all $p$-adic lattices. Hence, if we can show that $O(\Lambda_p)\subseteq O(\Pi_p)$ then we will be done.

By standard arguments, there are exactly $p^2+p+1$ sublattices of $\Lambda_p$ of index $p$ up to change of basis. If $\Lambda_p$ has basis $\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}$ with respect to the above Gram matrix then these sublattices have bases \[\{pv_1,v_2,v_3\} \qquad \{v_1+\alpha v_2, pv_2, v_3\} \qquad \{v_1+\alpha v_3, v_2 + \beta v_3, pv_3\},\] with $\alpha,\beta\in \{0,1,...,p-1\}$, and corresponding Gram matrices: \[\begin{pmatrix}
p^2a & 0 & 0\\ 0 & p & 0\\ 0 & 0 & pb
\end{pmatrix}\qquad \begin{pmatrix}
a+p\alpha^2 & p^2\alpha & 0\\ p^2\alpha & p^3 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & pb
\end{pmatrix} \qquad \begin{pmatrix}
a+pb\alpha^2 & pb\alpha\beta & p^2b
\alpha\\ pb\alpha\beta & p(1+b\beta^2) & p^2b\beta\\ p^2b\alpha & p^2b\beta & p^3b
\end{pmatrix}.\] Note that the first Gram matrix corresponds to the lattice $\Pi_p$, and is visibly the only one having norm ideal $p\mathbb{Z}_p$. Now if $\gamma\in O(\Lambda_p)$ then $\gamma\Lambda_p = \Lambda_p$ and $\gamma\Pi_p$ is a sublattice of $\Lambda_p$ of index $p$. However, isometries preserve norm ideals and so we must have that $\gamma\Pi_p = \Pi_p$, i.e.\ $\gamma\in O(\Pi_p)$. This gives $O(\Lambda_p)\subseteq O(\Pi_p)$, as required.

\label{ram}
Let $\Lambda_p$ be a ternary lattice in a quadratic space $(V_p,Q)$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, let $B_p = \mathrm{Cl}^0(V_p)$ be the associated quaternion algebra and $\mathcal{O}_p = \mathrm{Cl}^0(\Lambda_p)$ be the associated local order.

\vspace{0.1in}
\begin{itemize}
\item{If $p$ is odd and $\Lambda_p \sim \langle 1,1,a \rangle$ for some $a\in\{1,r\}$, then $B_p\cong M_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is unramified and $\mathcal{O}_p\cong M_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ is maximal of discriminant $1$ (hence unramified).}
\vspace{0.1in}
\newpage
\item{If $p$ is odd and $\Lambda_p \sim \langle 1,a,pb \rangle$ for some $a,b\in\{1,r\}$, then $B_p$ is ramified if and only if $S(V_p) = -1$ and $p\equiv 1 \bmod 4$ or $S(V_p) = 1$ and $p \equiv 3 \bmod 4$. In the ramified case, the order $\mathcal{O}_p$ is the unique maximal order of discriminant $p$ (unramified with $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}) = -1$), otherwise $\mathcal{O}_p\cong\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}_p & \mathbb{Z}_p\\ p\mathbb{Z}_p & \mathbb{Z}_p\end{pmatrix}$ is an Eichler order of discriminant $p$ (unramified with  $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}) = 1$).}
\vspace{0.1in}
\item{If $p$ is odd and $\Lambda_p \sim \langle 1,a,p^2b\rangle$ for some $a,b\in\{1,r\}$, then $B_p$ is unramified. We have that $\mathcal{O}_p\cong\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}_p & \mathbb{Z}_p\\ p^2\mathbb{Z}_p & \mathbb{Z}_p\end{pmatrix}$ is an Eichler order of discriminant $p^2$ when $p\equiv 1 \bmod 4$ and $a = 1$ or $p\equiv 3\bmod 4$ and $a= r$ (hence unramified with $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}) = 1$) and is a non-Eichler order of discriminant $p^2$ otherwise (unramified with $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}) = -1$).}
\vspace{0.1in}
\item{If $p=2$ and $\Lambda_p\sim\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix},2a\right\rangle$ for some $a\in\{1,3,5,7\}$, then $B_p\cong M_2(\mathbb{Q}_2)$ is unramified and $\mathcal{O}_p\cong M_2(\mathbb{Z}_2)$ is maximal (unramified with $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}) = 1$).}
\end{itemize}
<!-- end lem-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
If $p$ is odd and $\Lambda_p\sim \langle 1,1,a\rangle$ then by the Clifford correspondence we would have $\mathrm{discrd}(\mathcal{O}_p) = 1$, so that $B_p \cong M_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is unramified and $\mathcal{O}_p\cong M_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ is maximal (since for $B_p$ ramified the unique maximal order has reduced discriminant $p$). This order has Eichler invariant $1$ by the classification of orders in local quaternion algebras.

If $p$ is odd and $\Lambda_p\sim \langle 1,a,pb\rangle$ then $B_p = \left(\frac{-a/4,-a(pab)/4}{\mathbb{Q}_p}\right)\cong \left(\frac{-a,-pb}{\mathbb{Q_p}}\right)$. Note that $(-a,-pb)_p = (-1,-1)_p(-1,pb)_p(a,-1)_p(a,pb)_p = (-1,pb)_pS(V_p)$. Since $B_p$ is ramified if and only if $(-a,-pb)_p = -1$, we either require $S(V_p) = -1$ and $(-1,pb) = 1$ (i.e.\ $p\equiv 1 \bmod 4$) or $S(V_p) = 1$ and $(-1,pb)_p = -1$ (i.e.\ $p\equiv 3 \bmod 4$). Note that by the Clifford correspondence we have $\mathrm{discrd}(\mathcal{O}_p) = p$, and by the classification of orders in local quaternion algebras, we must have that $\mathcal{O}_p$ is maximal with Eichler invariant $-1$ when $B_p$ is ramified and Eichler with Eichler invariant $1$ when $B_p$ is unramified.

If $p$ is odd and $\Lambda_p\sim\langle 1,a,p^2b\rangle$ then $B_p = \left(\frac{-a/4,-a(p^2ab)/4}{\mathbb{Q}_p}\right)$. Note that \[(-a/4,-p^2a^2b/4)_p = (-a,-b)_p = 1\] and so $B_p\cong M_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is unramified. Note that under the Clifford correspondence we must have $\mathrm{discrd}(\mathcal{O}_p) = p^2$. By the classification of orders in local quaternion algebras, there are three possibilities up to isomorphism, two unramified (with Eichler invariants $\pm 1$) and one ramified (with $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}_p) = 0$). Exercise $10$ of Chapter $24$ of Voight \cite{voight2021quaternion} tells us that the Eichler invariant in this case is given by the Legendre symbol $\left(\frac{-a}{p}\right)$, which leads to the explicit conditions given in the result.

If $p=2$ and $\Lambda_p \sim \left\langle\begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix},2a\right\rangle$ then by the Clifford correspondence we have $\mathrm{discrd}(\mathcal{O}_p) = 1$ and so $B_p\sim M_2(\mathbb{Q}_2)$ is unramified and $\mathcal{O}_p \cong M_2(\mathbb{Z}_2)$ is maximal (again since for $B_p$ ramified the unique maximal order has reduced discriminant $p$). This order has $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}_p) = 1$ by the classification of orders in local quaternion algebras.

<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{2p2}
Let $\Lambda$ be as above. Then for each $k\geq 0$ and $d \,|\, p$ there is a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism (for Hecke operators away from $p$): \[\mathcal{S}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Lambda}),W_{2k}\otimes\theta_d) \cong S_{2k+2}^{\textup{new}}(\Gamma_0(p),\varepsilon_p^{(p)}\varepsilon_d^{(p)})\]
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Since $\Lambda_p \sim \langle a,p,pb\rangle$ for some $a,b\in\{1,r\}$ we can apply $\hat{\psi}_{(p)}$ to $\Lambda$ to get a new even lattice $\Pi = V \cap \hat{\psi}_{(p)}(\Lambda)$ with $\mathrm{disc}(\Pi) = 2p^4$ and satisfying $O(\widehat{\Lambda}) = O(\widehat{\Pi})$ (since $O(\Lambda_p) = O(\Pi_p)$ by Proposition \ref{psip} and $\Lambda_q = \Pi_q$ for all $q \neq p$).

Scaling the quadratic map allows us to view $\Pi$ as an even lattice in $(V,Q^{(\frac{1}{p})})$ with $\mathrm{disc}(\Pi) = 2p$ and local lattices:

\[\Pi_t \sim\begin{cases} \langle p^{-1}, p^{-1}, 2p^{-1}\rangle \sim \langle 1,1,2p\rangle & \text{ if } t \nmid 2p\\ \left\langle \begin{pmatrix}0 & p^{-1}\\ p^{-1} & 0\end{pmatrix}, -2p^{-1}\right\rangle \sim  \left\langle\begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}, -2p\right\rangle & \text{ if } t = 2\\ \langle 1,r,pr\rangle & \text{ if } t = p\equiv 1 \bmod 8\\ \langle 1,1,pr\rangle & \text{ if } t = p\equiv 3 \bmod 8\\ \langle 1,r,p\rangle & \text{ if } t=p\equiv 5 \bmod 8\\ \langle 1,1,p\rangle & \text{ if } t=p\equiv 7 \bmod 8\end{cases}\]

By Lemma \ref{samespace} and Lemma \ref{scaling} there is an equality of Hecke modules: \[\mathcal{S}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Lambda}), W) = \mathcal{S}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Pi}), W)\] for any representation $W$ of $O(V)$.

By Lemma \ref{ram} the quadratic space $(V,Q^{(\frac{1}{p})})$ gives rise to a quaternion algebra $B = \mathrm{Cl}^0(V)$ of discriminant $p$, and the order $\mathcal{O} = \mathrm{Cl}^0(\Pi)$ is maximal with $\mathrm{discrd}(\mathcal{O}) = p$, hence residually unramified everywhere with $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}) = -1$ (also by Lemma \ref{ram}). The result now follows from the results of Hein-Tornaria-Voight referenced earlier (see Theorem \ref{thm: 6.12 in HTV}).

Note that $S(V_{\infty}) = 1$ since $(V,Q)$ is positive definite.
