<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:vanishingCH}
Let \(\Lambda\) be a Legendrian sphere in
\((\mathbb S^{2n+1},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})\), where \(n\ge 1\). If
\[
\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)\ne (-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+1},
\]
then \((\mathbb S^{2n+1}_{+1}(\Lambda),\xi_{+1}(\Lambda))\) is algebraically overtwisted.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:nofilling1}
If \(\Lambda\subset(\mathbb S^{2n+1},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})\) is a Legendrian sphere with
\[
\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)\ne (-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+1},
\]
then
\((\mathbb S^{2n+1}_{+1}(\Lambda),\xi_{+1}(\Lambda))\) admits no weak fillings.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The results are only relevant when $n$ is odd, in which case it is easily checked that $H^{2}(\mathbb S^{2n+1}_{+1}(\Lambda), \mathbb R)=0$. Hence any weak filling of $(\mathbb S^{2n+1}_{+1}(\Lambda),\xi_{+1}(\Lambda))$ may be deformed to a strong filling \cite[Proposition 6]{MNW12}. According to \cite[Corollary 7]{MNW12} (which generalizes  the $\dim = 3$ case of \cite[Corollary 6]{NW:WeakFillings}), the vanishing of $CH(\mathbb S^{2n+1}_{+1}(\Lambda),\xi_{+1}(\Lambda), \mathbb C)$ (provided by \Cref{thm:vanishingCH}) obstructs the existence of strong symplectic fillings.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:3D}
Let \(\Lambda\) be a Legendrian knot in \((\mathbb S^{3},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})\). Suppose that
\[
\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)\neq -1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)=2g_s(\Lambda)-1,
\]
where \(g_s(\Lambda)\) denotes the smooth slice genus of the underlying
topological knot type. Then, for every positive integer \(k\), the contact
manifold
\[
(\mathbb S^{3}_{+1/k}(\Lambda),\xi_{+1/k}(\Lambda))
\]
is algebraically overtwisted and tight. In particular, if \(\Lambda\) bounds an
exact, oriented Lagrangian surface that is not a disk, then
\((\mathbb S^{3}_{+1/k}(\Lambda),\xi_{+1/k}(\Lambda))\) is algebraically overtwisted
and tight for every positive integer \(k\).
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- question-1: body_md -->
For \(n\geq 2\), does contact $(+1)$-surgery along a Lagrangian-fillable Legendrian sphere
in \((\mathbb S^{2n+1},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})\) always produce a tight contact manifold?
<!-- end question-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:vanishingCH1}
Let \((\mathbb S^{2n+1},\Lambda,\xi_{\mathrm{std}})\), \(n\ge 2\), be an asymptotically
dynamically convex pair in the sense of \cite[Definition 5.2]{Lazarev:flexiblefillings}.
Suppose that \(\Lambda\) admits an exact spin Lagrangian filling
\(L\subset(\mathbb D^{2n+2},\omega_{\mathrm{std}})\) such that the Maslov class of \(L\)
vanishes and
\[
H^*(L;\mathbb C)\ne \mathbb C.
\]
Then, for any choice of auxiliary data, the contact DGA of
\((\mathbb S^{2n+1}_{+1}(\Lambda),\xi_{+1}(\Lambda))\) admits no
\(\mathbb Z\)-graded augmentation over \(\mathbb C\).
<!-- end proposition-1 -->

\(L\) that is not diffeomorphic to a ball, then
\((\mathbb S^{2n+1}_{+1}(\Lambda),\xi_{+1}(\Lambda))\) is algebraically overtwisted.
<!-- end conjecture-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:contact}
Let \(\Lambda\subset(\mathbb S^{2n+1},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})\) be a Legendrian sphere. Then
there exists a contact form \(\alpha\) on \(\mathbb S^{2n+1}\) for which the following conditions are satisfied:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The shortest Reeb orbit has period \(4\pi\) and Conley--Zehnder index
\(n+2\).
\item For any exact filling \((W,\lambda)\) of \((\mathbb S^{2n+1},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})\) with \(\lambda|_{\partial W}=\alpha\), the positive symplectic cohomology
\(SH^{*,<4\pi+\delta}_+(W;\mathbb Q)\), for \(0<\delta\ll 1\), has rank two, and the composition
\[
SH^{*,<4\pi+\delta}_+(W;\mathbb Q)
\longrightarrow H^{*+1}(W;\mathbb Q)
\longrightarrow H^0(W;\mathbb Q)
\]
is surjective.
\item Up to Legendrian isotopy, all Reeb chords of \(\Lambda\) have action
either less than \(1\) or greater than \(9\pi-1>4\pi\), and the signed count
of the Reeb chords with action less than \(1\) equals \(\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)\).
\end{enumerate}

\item a sequence of contact forms

\label{def:ADC_pair}

\[

\]

\begin{enumerate}
\item a sequence of contact forms
\(\alpha_1\ge \alpha_2\ge \cdots\) and
\item an increasing sequence \(D_1<D_2<\cdots\) with \(\lim_i D_i=\infty\)
\end{enumerate}
such that all Reeb chords $c$ of \(\Lambda\) with respect to \(\alpha_i\) having action \( \leq D_i\) and representing the trivial homotopy class in \(\pi_1(Y,\Lambda)\) are nondegenerate and have positive SFT degree. That is, 
\[
|c|_{\operatorname{SFT}}=\mu_{\operatorname{CZ}}-1>0.
\]
The triple \((Y,\Lambda,\xi)\) is called an \emph{ADC pair} if both the Reeb orbits and the Reeb chords satisfy the corresponding positivity conditions for the chosen sequences of contact forms and period/action thresholds.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:Euler}
Let \(L\) be an oriented relatively spin exact Lagrangian filling of
\(\Lambda\subset(\mathbb S^{2n+1},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})\) in a Liouville filling \(W\) of
\((\mathbb S^{2n+1},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})\). We suppose that $\Lambda$ and the contact form $\alpha$ on $\mathbb S^{2n+1}$ used to set up our Floer cohomologies are as in \Cref{prop:contact}. If \(1\le a<9\pi-1\), then
\[
\chi\bigl(HW^{*,<a}_+(L)\bigr)
=
(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda).
\]
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The Euler characteristic of \(HW^{*,<a}_+(L)\) is equal to the Euler characteristic of the cochain complex \(C_+(L,H_a)\). This complex is generated by non-constant Reeb chords of action less than \(a\). According to \Cref{prop:contact}, these are precisely the Reeb chords of action less than \(1\). Such Reeb chords are in one-to-one correspondence with self-intersections of the Lagrangian projection of \(\Lambda\).

By \cite[Proposition 3.3]{EES},
\[
\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)
=
(-1)^{\frac{(n-2)(n-1)}{2}}
\sum_c(-1)^{|c|_{\operatorname{SFT}}}.
\]
Since \(\dim L=n+1\), the grading relation above gives
\[
|c|_{\operatorname{SFT}}=n-|\overline{c}|-1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\chi\bigl(HW^{*,<a}_+(L)\bigr)
=
\sum_c(-1)^{|\overline{c}|}
=
(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda).
\]
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
The construction of the operations \(\smile\), \(\mu_2\), and \(m\) requires the use of \(C^2\)-small Hamiltonians on the domain \(W\). Thus, for these product-type structures, one cannot simply use a mixed Hamiltonian-Floer cascade model with \(H=0\) on \(W\) to handle the Morse--Bott degeneracy of constant orbits. The issue is that \(H=0\) is not Morse--Bott nondegenerate along \(\partial W\). Since stable manifolds of critical points are contained in the interior of \(W\), the Fredholm setup breaks down when the only outputs from the zero part are critical points, unless all inputs and outputs are critical points; see \cite[\S 2.1]{Zhou:ADC}. However, when one input is a critical point and the other is a non-constant orbit or chord, the unstable manifolds of critical points may reach the boundary, creating analytic difficulties in setting up the moduli spaces.\footnote{It is nevertheless possible to build an \(H^*(W)\)-module structure on \(SH^*(W)\) or \(HW^*(L)\) using a cascade model by treating the input from \(H^*(W)\) as a constraint at a marked point.} This is not merely a technical issue: some related constructions should not carry a unital ring structure; see \Cref{rmk:ring}.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:nofilling}
Under the assumptions of \Cref{thm:vanishingCH}, \((\mathbb S^{2n+1}_{+1}(\Lambda), \xi_{+1}(\Lambda))\) does not admit a Liouville filling.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We argue by contradiction. Suppose that \((\mathbb S^{2n+1}_{+1}(\Lambda), \xi_{+1}(\Lambda))\) admits a Liouville
filling \(V\). Then \(V\cup W_{\Lambda}\) is a Liouville filling of \((\mathbb S^{2n+1},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})\). Let
\[
L\subset  V\cup W_\Lambda, \quad \partial L = \Lambda
\]
be the core Lagrangian disk of the surgery cobordism.

We assume that the contact form $\alpha$ on $\mathbb S^{2n+1}$ is as described in \Cref{prop:contact}. Then there are no Reeb chords of \(\Lambda\) with action in \([1,4\pi+\delta)\), for \(0<\delta\ll 1\). Hence
\[
HW^{*,<4\pi+\delta}(L)=HW^{*,<1}(L).
\]
While the filtered wrapped Floer cohomology product $\mu_2$ a priori maps \(HW^{*,<1}(L)\otimes HW^{*,<1}(L)\) to \(HW^{*,<2}(L)\), the action gap for Reeb chords  identifies \(HW^{*,<2}(L)\) with \(HW^{*,<1}(L)\). Thus the pair-of-pants product endows \(HW^{*,<1}(L)\) with the structure of a unital ring.

By \cite[Proposition 3.2]{product}, the map
\[
H^0(V\cup W_\Lambda)\longrightarrow
SH^{*,<4\pi+\delta}(V\cup W_\Lambda)
\]
is zero. Using the closed-open map of Equation  \eqref{eqn:CO}, the image of the unit \(1\in H^0(L)\) in \(HW^{*,<4\pi+\delta}(L)=HW^{*,<1}(L)\) is therefore zero. Since this image is the unit of the ring \(HW^{*,<1}(L)\), we conclude that
\[
HW^{*,<1}(L)=0.
\]
The tautological exact sequence of Equation \eqref{eqn:exact_L} then gives
\[
\chi\bigl(HW^{*,<1}_+(L)\bigr)=-\chi(H^{\ast}(L)) = -\chi(L) =-1,
\]
as \(L\) is a disk. On the other hand, by \Cref{prop:Euler},
\[
\chi\bigl(HW^{*,<1}_+(L)\bigr)
=
(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda).
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+1},
\]
contradicting the hypothesis.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rmk:finite}
Pardon's construction \cite{Pardon:Contact} produces a contact DGA generated by
all good Reeb orbits. In contrast, \cite{Bao-Honda} constructs filtered versions
\(\operatorname{CC}^{L_i}(Y,\alpha_i,\Theta_i)\), generated by Reeb orbits of action at most
\(L_i > 0\), for a sequence of contact forms \(\alpha_i\) that are only \(C^0\)-close
to one another. The cobordism maps
\[
\operatorname{CC}^{L_i}(Y,\alpha_i,\Theta_i)\to \operatorname{CC}^{L_j}(Y,\alpha_j,\Theta_j),
\qquad i<j,
\]
do not form a direct system of DGA maps on the chain level, but they do form a
direct system on homology. The contact homology is then defined as the direct
limit of these homologies. It follows that if the total contact homology vanishes, then the filtered contact homology vanishes for all sufficiently large thresholds. Similarly, the augmented filtered symplectic/wrapped Floer cohomology used in \S \ref{s4} and \ref{s5} requires an augmentation to the filtered contact algebra with a sufficiently high threshold.  Hence for our purposes, working with such filtered
versions is sufficient if one uses the virtual setup of \cite{Bao-Honda}. In
what follows, we suppress this technical distinction.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{Lemma:ConstructibleIntersection}
Let $D_{1} \supset D_{2} \supset \cdots$ be a sequence of non-empty subsets of $\mathbb C^{N}$ which are constructible in the Zariski topology. Then $\cap_{j} D_{j} \neq \emptyset$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Write $\overline{D}_{j}$ for the Zariski closure of $D_{j}$, for any $j$. Since $\mathbb C[x_{1}, \dots, x_{N}]$ is Noetherian and the $\overline{D}_{j}$ are closed and nested, there is some $j_{0}$ such that $\overline{D}_{j} = \overline{D}_{j_{0}}$ for $j \geq j_{0}$. Each $D_{j}$ contains a set of the form $\overline{D}_{j} \setminus Y_{j}$ where $Y_{j} \subset \overline{D}_{j}$ is closed and nowhere dense. Hence for $j \geq j_{0}$, the $Y_{j}$ are closed subsets of $\overline{D}_{j_{0}}$ with dense complements in the Zariski topology, and therefore in the standard topology, cf. \cite[Theorem 2.33]{Mumford}. As $\overline{D}_{j_{0}}$ is a closed subset of $\mathbb C^{N}$, the Baire category theorem (applied to the complements of the $Y_{j}$ using the standard topology) implies that $\overline{D}_{j_{0}} \setminus \cup_{j \geq j_{0}} Y_{j} \subset \cap_{j} D_{j}$ is non-empty.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{Lemma:aug}
Let $\mathfrak{A} = (SV, \partial)$ be a finitely filtered, semi-free CDGA with $\mathbb C$ coefficients and $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ grading. Then $\mathfrak{A}$ admits an augmentation if and only if $H\mathfrak{A} \neq 0$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Clearly the existence of an augmentation implies the non-vanishing of homology. We first work out a finitely generated version of the desired result using the assumed filtration $V^{0} \subset V^{1} \subset \cdots \subset V$.

For each $i$, write $\mathfrak{A}^{i} = (SV^{i},\partial)$. Let $V^{i}_{ev}$ and $V^{i}_{odd}$ be the subspaces of $V^{i}$ of even and odd grading, respectively. We have an associated augmentation variety
\begin{equation*}
Z^{i} \subset \mathbb C^{N_{i}}, \quad N_{i} = \dim V^{i}_{ev}
\end{equation*}
with coordinate variables indexed by generators $v_{ev, j} \in V^{i}_{ev}$, defined as follows. Let
\begin{equation*}
P: \mathfrak{A}^{i} \rightarrow \mathbb C[V^{i}_{ev}]
\end{equation*}
be the algebra map which sends each generator $v_{odd} \in V^{i}_{odd}$ to zero, and preserves generators in $V^i_{ev}$. Then let
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{I}^{i} \subset \mathbb C[V^{i}_{ev}]
\end{equation*}
be the ideal generated by the $P(\partial v_{odd})$, and $Z^{i}$ the variety associated to $\mathcal{I}^{i}$. The points
\begin{equation*}
\epsilon = (\epsilon_{1}, \cdots, \epsilon_{N_{i}}) \in Z^{i}
\end{equation*}
exactly correspond to the augmentations of $\mathfrak{A}^{i}$ by $\epsilon v_{ev, j} = \epsilon_{j}$. The following conditions are then equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $1 \in \mathcal{I}^{i}$.
\item $Z^{i} = \emptyset$.
\item $\mathfrak{A}^{i}$ does not admit an augmentation.
\end{enumerate}
The first equivalence follows from the weak Nullstellensatz and the second follows from the definition of an augmentation of $\mathfrak{A}^{i}$.

Suppose that $\mathfrak{A}^{i}$ does not admit an augmentation, so that $1 \in \mathcal{I}^{i}$. It follows that there are some $p_{j} \in \mathbb C[V^{i}_{ev}]$ such that
\begin{equation*}
1 = \sum p_{j}P(\partial v_{odd, j})
\end{equation*}
where the sum is taken over the $v_{odd, j} \in V^{i}_{odd}$. Since the $p_{j}$ are polynomials in the additive generators $v_{ev}$ of $V^{i}_{ev}$, each non-zero summand of $\partial p_{j}$ contains at least one generator $v_{odd} \in V^{i}_{odd}$ as a factor. Hence using the definition of $P$ and the Leibniz rule we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\partial(p_{j}v_{odd, j}) = (\partial p_{j})v_{odd, j} + p_{j}\partial v_{odd, j} \implies 1 = P(\partial x), \quad x = \sum p_{j} v_{odd,j}.
\end{equation*}

By the definition of $P$, we can write
\begin{equation*}
\partial x = 1 + z, \quad P(z) = 0
\end{equation*}
with $z$ being an even-degree element of $\mathfrak{A}^{i}$. Each summand of $z$ must have at least two $V^{i}_{odd}$ factors by degree considerations. Since squares of odd-degree generators vanish in $\mathfrak{A}$, there is some $m > 0$ for which $z^{m} = 0$. Moreover, for all $j > 0$
\begin{equation*}
\partial z^{j} =  j z^{j-1}\partial z = jz^{j-1}\partial^{2}x = 0.
\end{equation*}
It follows that
\begin{equation*}
\partial \sum_{j=1}^{m} (-1)^{j+1}z^{j-1} x = (1 + z) - z(1 + z) + \cdots + (-1)^{m-1}z^{m-1}(1 + z) = 1.
\end{equation*}
So we've established that if $\mathfrak{A}^{i}$ does not admit an augmentation for some $i > 0$ then the unit is exact in $\mathfrak{A}^{i}$ and hence $H\mathfrak{A}^{i} = 0$.

Now suppose that $H\mathfrak{A} \neq 0$. For each $i$, $H\mathfrak{A}^{i} \neq 0$ and hence $Z^{i} \neq \emptyset$ by our already-established results. Restrictions of augmentations define morphisms
\begin{equation*}
\pi_{i}: Z^{i+1} \to Z^{i},
\end{equation*}
given by projecting $V^{i+1}_{ev}$ to the subspace $V^{i}_{ev} \subset V^{i+1}_{ev}$. Augmentations of $\mathfrak{A}$ are exactly the sequences $(z_{i})_{i \geq 0}$ with $z_{i} \in Z^{i}$ and $\pi_{i}(z_{i+1}) = z_{i}$ for all $i \in \mathbb Z_{\geq 0}$.

Now we establish that
\begin{equation*}
Z^{\infty, i} = \cap_{I \geq i} Z^{I, i}
\end{equation*}
is non-empty for all $i$, where
\begin{equation*}
Z^{I, i} = \pi^{I,i}Z^{I} \subset \mathbb C^{N_{i}}, \quad \pi^{I, i} = \pi_{i}\cdots \pi_{I-1}.
\end{equation*}
By Chevalley's theorem (see \cite[Exercise II.3.19]{Hartshorne}), the $Z^{I,i}$ are constructible in the Zariski topology. They are each non-empty by assumption and nested, $Z^{I+1, i} \subset Z^{I,i}$.

Applying \Cref{Lemma:ConstructibleIntersection} to the $Z^{I,i}$, each $Z^{\infty,i}$ is non-empty. Next we show that
\begin{equation*}
\pi_{i}Z^{\infty, i+1} = Z^{\infty, i}.
\end{equation*}
The inclusion $\subset$ is clear. For the reverse inclusion, let $x \in Z^{\infty, i}$. For each $I > i$, the set
\begin{equation*}
F^x_{I} = \pi_{i}^{-1}(x) \cap Z^{I, i+1}
\end{equation*}
is constructible and non-empty, since $x \in Z^{I,i} = \pi_{i}Z^{I,i+1}$. The $F^x_{I}$ are also nested. By \Cref{Lemma:ConstructibleIntersection}, there is some $y \in \cap_{K} F^x_{K} \subset Z^{\infty, i+1}$, and $\pi_{i}(y) = x$.

Finally, choose any $z_{0} \in Z^{\infty, 0}$ and inductively choose $z_{i+1} \in Z^{\infty, i+1}$ with $\pi_{i}(z_{i+1}) = z_{i}$. The resulting sequence $(z_{i})$ defines an augmentation of $\mathfrak{A}$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:delta^2}
The differential satisfies \(\delta^2=0\).
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We give the standard boundary-of-one-dimensional-moduli-spaces argument,
suppressing orientation signs and the details of the virtual perturbation
scheme; see \Cref{rmk:ana} below. It suffices to show
\[
\delta_+^2=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\delta_{+,0}\circ\delta_+ + \delta_0\circ\delta_{+,0}=0.
\]

For \(\delta_+^2=0\), consider a compactified moduli space
\(\mathcal M_{x,y,\Gamma}\) of virtual dimension \(1\). Its codimension-one boundary
has two types of contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Floer breaking:
\[
\mathcal M_{x,z,\Gamma_1}\times \mathcal M_{z,y,\Gamma_2},
\qquad
\Gamma=\Gamma_1\sqcup \Gamma_2.
\]
\item SFT breaking at a negative puncture:
\[
\bigl(\mathcal M_{x,y,\Gamma_1\sqcup\{\gamma\}}\times
\mathcal M_{\gamma,\Gamma_2}\bigr)/(\mathbb Z/\kappa_\gamma),
\qquad
\Gamma=\Gamma_1\sqcup \Gamma_2.
\]
This comes from the previously mentioned ``bubbling'' of holomorphic planes in $\widehat{Y}_-$, as well as the collision of negative punctures.
\end{enumerate}
A priori, one might also have a boundary stratum in which an interior negative
puncture approaches the boundary, producing one of more disk bubbles
\[
\nu:(\mathbb D\setminus\{0\},\partial\mathbb D)\to(\widehat W,\widehat L)
\]
with an interior negative puncture asymptotic to a Reeb orbit \(\gamma\) on
\(Y_-\). Such bubbles do not occur. Indeed, write
\[
W_+=W\cup (1,\infty)\times Y_+,\qquad
W_-=(0,1)\times Y_-,
\]
and let \(\lambda_-=\lambda|_{Y_-}\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:diskbubble}
\int_{\nu^{-1}(W_+)}\nu^*\mathrm d\lambda
+
\int_{\nu^{-1}(W_-)}\nu^*\mathrm d\lambda_-
\end{equation}
is non-negative because \(\nu\) is \(J\)-holomorphic and \(J\) is compatible with
the relevant symplectic forms/contact forms. On the other hand, by Stokes'
theorem and the exactness of \(\widehat L\), the same quantity equals
\[
-\int_\gamma \lambda_-<0,
\]
a contradiction.

After weighting all negative punctures by the augmentation \(\epsilon\), the
SFT-breaking contributions cancel precisely by the augmentation equation for
\(\epsilon\), while the Floer-breaking contributions give \(\delta_+^2=0\). The
identity
\(\delta_{+,0}\circ\delta_+ + \delta_0\circ\delta_{+,0}=0\) is proved in the
same way, using the compactified one-dimensional moduli spaces
\(\mathcal M_{x,p,\Gamma}\).

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
\label{rmk:ana}
We record several comments on the analytic foundations and invariance properties
of \(HW^*(L,\epsilon)\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item The open strata of \(\mathcal M_{x,y,\Gamma}\) and \(\mathcal M_{x,p,\Gamma}\),
consisting of smooth curves, can be arranged to be cut out transversely for
a generic almost complex structure, using the Hamiltonian perturbation and
somewhere-injectivity. However, the contact homology moduli spaces
\(\mathcal M_{\gamma,\Gamma}\) on \(Y_-\) are not generally transversely cut out.
Therefore, one must use a virtual perturbation theory, such as Pardon's
virtual fundamental cycles \cite{Pardon:Contact} or the semi-global
Kuranishi charts of Bao--Honda \cite{Bao-Honda}, both for the contact
homology moduli spaces and for the augmented wrapped Floer moduli spaces.

\item Fix a contact form \(\alpha_-=\lambda|_{Y_-}\), an
\(\alpha_-\)-compatible almost complex structure \(J\) on the negative end,
and auxiliary data \(\theta\in\Theta_I(Y_-,\alpha_-,J)\). After extending
these data, one obtains \(HW^*(L,\epsilon)\). A priori, this cohomology
depends on the chosen extension data. Standard continuation maps and
homotopies of continuation maps, as in \cite[\S 10]{Pardon}, show that it
is independent of the extension data once \(\alpha_-\), \(J\), \(\theta\),
and the augmentation \(\epsilon\) are fixed. Removing dependence on
\(\alpha_-\), \(J\), and \(\theta\) requires a chain-level homotopy theory
for the contact homology DGA; this will be developed in \cite{DG}. For the
purposes of this paper, it is enough to work with \(HW^*(L,\epsilon)\)
relative to the fixed data defining \(\epsilon\).

\item Although the chain-level well-definedness and functoriality for contact homology are not established yet, the set of homotopy classes of augmentations is indeed a contact invariant by the work of Chaidez \cite{chaidez2024contact}. Hence the set of linearized contact homologies is a contact invariant. Consequently, the set of augmented wrapped Floer cohomologies associated to a Lagrangian $L$ (with augmentations varying) is an invariant following the arguments of \cite{chaidez2024contact}.

\item For computations, the virtual perturbation theory should satisfy the following compatibility property: whenever the compactified moduli space is transversely cut out, the virtual count agrees with the geometric signed count. In Pardon's framework for contact homology, this is \cite[Proposition 4.33]{Pardon:Contact}.

\item The same comments apply to all augmented constructions below.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:product}
For \(a,b>0\), the product induces
\[
\mu_2:
HW^{*,<a}(L,\epsilon)\otimes HW^{*,<b}(L,\epsilon)
\to
HW^{*,<a+b}(L,\epsilon).
\]
For \(a,b\ll 1\) sufficiently small, this product agrees with the cup product structure on \(H^*(L)\). The image of $1 \in H^{0}(L)$ under the inclusion $H^{\ast}(L) \to HW^{*,<a}(L,\epsilon)$ is a two-sided unit, by the compatibility of $\mu_2$ with continuation maps.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The proof follows the construction of TQFT operations in \cite{Ritter1}, except that curves are now allowed to have interior negative punctures asymptotic to Reeb orbits on \(Y_-\). These punctures are algebraically weighted by the augmentation \(\epsilon\). As in the proof of \Cref{prop:delta^2}, interior negative punctures cannot escape to the boundary, by the exactness/energy argument above. The codimension-one SFT-breaking contributions cancel by the augmentation equation, and the remaining boundary strata give the \(A_\infty\)-type relation showing that \(\mu_2\) is a chain map.

For the last claim, consider the energy of a curve \(u\) contributing to \eqref{eqn:mu_2_aug}:
\[
\int_{u^{-1}(W_+)}
\frac{1}{2}|\mathrm d u-X_H\otimes\beta|^2
+
\int_{u^{-1}(W_-)}u^*\mathrm d\lambda_-,
\]
where \(W_+=W\cup(1,\infty)\times Y_+\), \(W_-=(0,1)\times Y_-\), and \(\lambda_-=\lambda|_{Y_-}\). This quantity is non-negative. By Stokes' theorem and the condition \(\mathrm d\beta\le 0\), it is bounded above by
\[
\mathcal A_{(a+b)H}(z)-\mathcal A_{aH}(x)-\mathcal A_{bH}(y)
-\sum_{\gamma_i\in\Gamma}\int_{\gamma_i}\lambda_-.
\]
If \(a,b\) are sufficiently small, then all relevant Hamiltonian chords are constant chords and their actions are smaller than the minimal period of a Reeb orbit on \(Y_-\). Therefore no negative punctures can occur. The operation then reduces to the usual product for exact Lagrangian fillings, which is the cup product on \(H^*(L)\).

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
\label{rmk:ring}
The group \(SH^*(W,\epsilon)\) is not a ring. It should be viewed as one part
of the symplectic cohomology of a cobordism in the sense of
Cieliebak--Oancea \cite{CieOan}.\footnote{Cieliebak--Oancea use symplectic
homology conventions. With our grading convention, their symplectic homology is
identified with \(SH^{N-*}\), without dualizing.} The missing piece is dual to
the positive symplectic cohomology of the negative boundary. The full
cobordism theory has a ring structure and generalizes Rabinowitz Floer
cohomology, which corresponds to the trivial cobordism.

<!-- proposition-9: body_md -->
\label{prop:sphere}
Let \(W\) be a Liouville cobordism from a contact manifold \(Y_-\) to
\((\mathbb S^{2n+1},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})\). Let \(\epsilon\) be an augmentation of the contact homology algebra of \(Y_-\), defined by fixing geometric and perturbative data as in \cite{Bao-Honda, Pardon:Contact}. Assume that the Liouville form on \(W\), restricted to the positive boundary \(\mathbb S^{2n+1}\) of the cobordism, is the contact form $\alpha$ of \Cref{prop:contact}. Then the map
\[
\delta_{\partial_+}:
SH^{*,<4\pi+\delta}_+(W,\epsilon)
\longrightarrow H^{*+1}(\mathbb S^{2n+1})
\]
has image containing \(1\in H^0(\mathbb S^{2n+1})\).
<!-- end proposition-9 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The proof follows the strategies of \cite[Theorem 1.1]{product} and \cite[Proposition 2.11]{Zhou:RP2n-1}, with the negative ends of holomorphic curves algebraically capped off by the augmentation \(\epsilon\).

More concretely, consider the generator \(\check{\gamma}_{\min}\) associated to the shortest Reeb orbit \(\gamma_{\min}\) of the contact form $\alpha$, following the notation of \cite{product}. The holomorphic curves whose counts define the map \(\delta_{\partial_+}\) are in the moduli spaces \(\mathcal M^{\partial_+}_{\check{\gamma}_{\min},p,\Gamma}\), where \(p\) represents the unit in \(H^0(\mathbb S^{2n+1})\). The relevant curves for the differential of \(\check{\gamma}_{\min}\) lie in the moduli spaces \(\mathcal M_{\check{\gamma}_{\min},y,\Gamma}\).

We shall apply a neck-stretching along the positive boundary of $W$, i.e. $(\mathbb S^{2n+1},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})$. By the same action and index estimates used in \cite[\S 4.2.2]{BGMZ}, holomorphic curves appearing in the SFT buildings associated to such a degeneration cannot be negatively asymptotic to Reeb orbits in $(\mathbb S^{2n+1},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})$. As usual, we exclude trivial cylinders from such buildings. In particular, \(\mathcal M_{\check{\gamma}_{\min},y,\Gamma}\) and \(\mathcal M^{\partial_+}_{\check{\gamma}_{\min},p,\Gamma}\) are empty whenever $\Gamma \ne \emptyset$. Elements of these $\Gamma =\emptyset$ moduli spaces can be identified with the corresponding curves in the Liouville filling obtained by rounding the corners of
\[
\mathbb B^2(3)\times \mathbb B^{2n-2}(3)\times \mathbb B^2(2),
\]
described in the construction of $\alpha$. Using this filling, the
map $\delta_{\partial_{+}}$ hits \(1\in H^0(\mathbb S^{2n+1})\) by \cite[Proposition 3.2 and proof of Theorem 1.1]{product}. Hence the same is true for \(SH^{*,<4\pi+\delta}_+(W,\epsilon)\).

\chi\bigl(HW^{*,<1}_+(L,\epsilon)\bigr)=-\chi(L)=-1.

For \(k=1\), algebraic overtwistedness of $(\mathbb S^{3}_{+1/k}(\Lambda),\xi_{+1/k}(\Lambda))$ follows immediately from \Cref{thm:vanishingCH}, since in dimension \(3\) the contact homology can only be non-zero when $\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)=-1$.

\[

\]
the contact Ozsv\'ath--Szab\'o invariant of \((\mathbb S^3_{+1/k}(\Lambda),\xi_{+1/k}(\Lambda))\) is non-zero for every positive integer \(k\), and the non-vanishing of this invariant implies tightness.

Finally, suppose that \(\Lambda\) bounds an exact, orientable Lagrangian surface \(L\) that
is not a disk. By Chantraine's formula \cite{ChantraineConcordance},
\[
\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)=-\chi(L)=2g(L)-1.
\]
Since \(L\) is a smooth slice surface, \(g_s(\Lambda)\le g(L)\). On the other
hand, the slice--Bennequin inequality gives
\[
\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)\le 2g_s(\Lambda)-1.
\]

\[
\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)=2g_s(\Lambda)-1.
\]
Since \(L\) is not a disk, \(g(L)>0\), so \(\operatorname{tb}(\Lambda)\ne -1\). Therefore the first part of the corollary applies.

<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{prop:ind}
Let \((Y_+,\Lambda,\xi_+)\) be an ADC pair such that
\[
\pi_1(Y_+)=1,
\qquad
\pi_1(Y_+,\Lambda)=1.
\]
Let \(W\) be a Liouville cobordism from \(Y_-\) to \(Y_+\) with \(c_1(W)=0\). Let
\(L\subset W\) be an exact spin Lagrangian filling of \(\Lambda\) with vanishing
Maslov class. Finally, let \(\epsilon\) be a \(\mathbb Z\)-graded augmentation of the
contact homology algebra of \(Y_-\), with respect to a global trivialization of
\(\det_{\mathbb C}TW\). Then \(HW^*_+(L,\epsilon)\), as a \(\mathbb Z\)-graded vector space,
is independent of the choices of \(W\), \(L\), and \(\epsilon\).
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
We may apply Lazarev's neck-stretching argument adapted to the augmented setting. Choose the sequence of contact forms and action thresholds from the ADC condition in \Cref{def:ADC_pair}, meaning that the SFT degrees of orbits and chords (up to action thresholds tending to infinity) are positive. Since \(W\) contains the negative symplectization end of \(Y_+\), these contact forms can be realized on contact hypersurfaces in \(W\), given as graphs in the collar end of the cobordism. 

Consider the moduli spaces \(\mathcal M_{x,y,\Gamma}\) of Equation \eqref{eqn:wrapped_aug} that define the differential on \(HW^*_+(L,\epsilon)\). After stretching the neck along an ADC hypersurface, any curve entering the lower cobordism must break along closed Reeb orbits or Reeb chords of \((Y_+,\Lambda)\). The assumptions \(\pi_1(Y_+)=1\) and \(\pi_1(Y_+,\Lambda)=1\) ensure that the relevant orbits and chords lie in the homotopy classes to which the ADC positivity condition applies. Since the augmentation is \(\mathbb Z\)-graded, only degree-zero negative punctures at \(Y_-\) can contribute after weighting by \(\epsilon\). The ADC index positivity then rules out lower-level contributions to the rigid moduli spaces that define the differential and continuation maps.

Consequently, in the direct-limit complex computing \(HW^*_+(L,\epsilon)\), the differentials and continuation maps are computed entirely in the symplectization of \(Y_+\). Thus the resulting \(\mathbb Z\)-graded vector space depends only on the ADC pair \((Y_+,\Lambda,\xi_+)\), and not on the cobordism, the Lagrangian filling, or the augmentation.
