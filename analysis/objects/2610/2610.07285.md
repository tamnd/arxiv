<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:median-edges}
For $0<q<1$, define $F^{-1}(q)=\inf\{p\in[0,1]:F(p)\ge q\}$. The lower and upper median edges are $a_F^-=F^{-1}(1/2)$ and $a_F^+=\lim_{q\downarrow1/2,\ q>1/2}F^{-1}(q)$, respectively. The median gap has width $\Delta_F=a_F^+-a_F^-$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:atomic-family}
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    
                                                           
{For $\mu\in(0,1/2]$ and $\Delta\in[0,1]$, let $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{at}}_{\mu,\Delta}$ be the class of distributions $F$ with finite support in $[0,1]$, median gap $\Delta_F=\Delta$, and quantiles satisfying $F^{-1}(1/2-q)=a_F^-$ and $F^{-1}(1/2+q)=a_F^+$ for every $0<q<\mu$.}
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:atomic}
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                
{Consider prices taking values $0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8$. If each price has probability $1/4$, then $a^-=0.4$, $a^+=0.6$, and $\Delta_F=0.2$. If instead these prices have probabilities $0.25,0.20,0.30,0.25$, respectively, the median lies inside the atom at $0.6$: now $a^-=a^+=0.6$ and $\Delta_F=0$.} Both distributions satisfy Definition~\ref{def:atomic-family} with $\mu=0.05$.
                                                                                                
{Figure~\ref{fig:example-atomic} shows that the same support can yield either a positive median gap or no median gap, depending on the probability masses.}
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:continuous-family}
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      
{For $\beta\ge0$, $\varepsilon_0\in(0,1/4]$, and $\Delta\in[0,1)$, let $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{ac}}_{\beta,\Delta,\varepsilon_0}$ be the class of absolutely continuous distributions $F$ on $[0,1]$ with bounded density and median gap $\Delta_F=\Delta$, satisfying the following conditions. There exist constants $c_-,c_+>0$ and remainder functions $r_-,r_+$ such that, for every $0<q<\varepsilon_0$,}
                
                                                                                   
                                                   
                            
              
{
\begin{align*}
 a_F^--F^{-1}(1/2-q)&=c_-q^{1/(\beta+1)}(1+r_-(q)), \\
 F^{-1}(1/2+q)-a_F^+&=c_+q^{1/(\beta+1)}(1+r_+(q)).
\end{align*}
}
The remainder functions satisfy $|r_\pm(q)|\le1/2$ and $r_\pm(q)\to0$ as $q\downarrow0$.
                                                                                                                                                                                        
{When $\beta=0$, they additionally satisfy $|r_\pm(q)|\le Lq^\eta$ for some $L\ge0$ and $\eta>0$. The density bound, constants, and remainder functions may depend on $F$.}
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- example-2: body_md -->
\label{ex:no-gap}
With $\Delta=0$, let $F_{\beta,0}$ have density $f_{\beta,0}(p)=(\beta+1)2^\beta|p-1/2|^\beta$ for $0\le p\le1$. These give uniform, V-shaped, and quadratic examples at $\beta=0,1,2$ respectively. Their quantiles satisfy $F_{\beta,0}^{-1}(1/2\pm q)=1/2\pm(2q)^{1/(\beta+1)}/2$ for $0<q<1/2$.
Thus they satisfy Definition~\ref{def:continuous-family} with zero remainder. Figure~\ref{fig:example-no-gap} displays the densities and their quantile functions. The shaded band isolates the local median window.
<!-- end example-2 -->
<!-- example-3: body_md -->
\label{ex:positive-gap}
For $\Delta\in(0,1)$, put $a^\pm=(1\pm\Delta)/2$ and let $F_{\beta,\Delta}$ have density
\begin{align}
 f_{\beta,\Delta}(p)=\frac{(\beta+1)2^\beta}{(1-\Delta)^{\beta+1}}
 \begin{cases}(a^--p)^\beta,&0\le p<a^-,\\
     0,&a^-<p<a^+, \\
 (p-a^+)^\beta,&a^+<p\le1.\\
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:canonical-gap-density}
\end{align}
These examples belong to Definition~\ref{def:continuous-family}, with $r_\pm=0$, $c_-=c_+=(1-\Delta)2^{-\beta/(\beta+1)}$, and $M=(\beta+1)/(1-\Delta)$. For $\beta>0$ the density vanishes at the gap edges. Equation~\eqref{eq:canonical-gap-density} also supplies the explicit instances for our lower bound discussed in Theorem \ref{lb:thm-positive-gap}. The quantiles satisfy $F_{\beta,\Delta}^{-1}(1/2\pm q)=a^\pm\pm(1-\Delta)(2q)^{1/(\beta+1)}/2$ for $0<q<1/2$.
Figure~\ref{fig:example-positive-gap} shows the resulting jump at the median for $\Delta=0.2$.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:main-decomposition}
Fix $T,B$, an initial and terminal inventory $y_0$, and a feasible
online policy $\pi$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=$(\alph*)$]
\item For every realized price sequence and policy trajectory,
\begin{align*}
 V_1^{\mathsf{hs}}(B,T,y_0;\mathbf p)
 -\sum_{t=1}^T(-p_tx_t^\pi)
 =\sum_{t=1}^T
 \partial R_t(y_{t-1}^\pi,x_t^\pi;\mathbf p_{\ge t}).
\end{align*}
\item Consequently,
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Reg}(\pi;B,T,y_0)
 =\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\left[
 \mathbb E\left[
 \partial R_t(y_{t-1}^\pi,x_t^\pi;\mathbf p_{\ge t})
 \mid\mathcal F_{t-1}
 \right]\right].
\end{align*}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Substitute the inventory update $y_t^\pi=y_{t-1}^\pi+x_t^\pi$
into the definition of compensation and sum over time. Successive
continuation values cancel, and the terminal value
$V_{T+1}^{\mathsf{hs}}(y_0;\varnothing)$ is zero, giving part (a).
Taking expectations and conditioning each summand on the past gives
part (b). The inner expectation fixes the current inventory and
averages over the current price, actual future, and fresh policy
randomization.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lb:lem-accounting}
Let $\pi$ be a feasible cyclic policy and $\pi^{\mathsf{hs}}$ a
hindsight-optimal feasible policy. Put
$M_t:=\max_{0\leq s\leq t}(-S_s)^+$ and define the correction cost
\begin{align}
    C_T(\pi)
    :=\sum_{t:\xi_t=+1}W_t\ell_t
      +\sum_{t:\xi_t=-1}(\Delta+Z_t)u_t.
    \label{lb:eq-correction-cost}
\end{align}
The following statements hold pathwise.
\begin{enumerate}[label=$(\alph*)$]
\item The inventory satisfies
\begin{align}
    y_t=S_t+U_t-L_t.
    \label{lb:eq-state-ledger}
\end{align}
\item The upper-side correction count satisfies $U_T\geq M_T$.
\item The marginal compensations satisfy
\begin{align}
    \sum_{t=1}^T
       \partial R_t(y_{t-1}^\pi,x_t^\pi;\mathbf p_{\geq t})
    =R_T(\pi^{\mathsf{hs}};0)-R_T(\pi;0)
    =C_T(\pi)-C_T(\pi^{\mathsf{hs}}).
    \label{lb:eq-two-accountings}
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
At a lower-side price, $x_t^\pi=\xi_t-\ell_t$, while at an
upper-side price, $x_t^\pi=\xi_t+u_t$.  Hence
\begin{align*}
    y_t
    &\stackrel{(a)}{=}\sum_{s=1}^t x_s^\pi
     \stackrel{(b)}{=}S_t+U_t-L_t,
\end{align*}
where $(a)$ uses $y_0=0$, and $(b)$ uses
\eqref{lb:eq-corrections}.  Since $y_t\geq0$ and $L_t\geq0$,
$U_t\geq-S_t$.  The process $U$ is nondecreasing, so maximizing over
$t$ proves the mandatory-reflection claim.

Let $R_T^\circ:=\sum_{t=1}^T-p_t\xi_t$ be the reward of the
unconstrained actions of the static median-sign rule.  Direct substitution gives
\begin{align*}
    R_T(\pi;0)-R_T^\circ
    &\stackrel{(c)}{=}
      a^-L_T-\sum_{\xi_t=+1}W_t\ell_t
      -a^+U_T-\sum_{\xi_t=-1}Z_tu_t\\
    &\stackrel{(d)}{=}
      a^-S_T-\Delta U_T
      -\sum_{\xi_t=+1}W_t\ell_t
      -\sum_{\xi_t=-1}Z_tu_t\\
    &\stackrel{(e)}{=}a^-S_T-C_T(\pi).
\end{align*}
Here $(c)$ uses $p_t=a^--W_t$ or $p_t=a^++Z_t$ according to the
side, $(d)$ uses $L_T-U_T=S_T$, which follows from
$y_T=0$ and \eqref{lb:eq-state-ledger}, and $(e)$ uses
$a^+-a^-=\Delta$ and \eqref{lb:eq-correction-cost}.  The terms
$R_T^\circ+a^-S_T$ are common to all cyclic feasible policies.
Applying the identity to $\pi$ and $\pi^{\mathsf{hs}}$ proves the
second equality in \eqref{lb:eq-two-accountings}.

For the first equality, sum \eqref{lb:eq-partial-regret} along the
trajectory of $\pi$.  Consecutive hindsight values cancel, the initial
term is $V_1^{\mathsf{hs}}(0;\mathbf p)=R_T(\pi^{\mathsf{hs}};0)$, and
the terminal term is $V_{T+1}^{\mathsf{hs}}(0;\varnothing)=0$ because
$y_T=0$.  This proves the pathwise telescope.

\label{lb:lem-order-statistic}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lb:lem-deadline-sum}
Fix $\kappa\geq1$, put $a:=1/\kappa\in(0,1]$, and define
\begin{align*}
    \Phi_\kappa(T)
    :=\log T\mathbbm{1}\{\kappa=1\}
      +T^{(\kappa-1)/(2\kappa)}\mathbbm{1}\{\kappa>1\}.
\end{align*}
There exists a constant $C_\kappa<\infty$, depending only on $\kappa$,
such that $\mathbb E[\sum_{y:\sigma_y\leq T}(y/(\sigma_y+1))^a]
\leq C_\kappa\Phi_\kappa(T)$ for every $T\geq2$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The probability that the walk first reaches $-y$ at time $s$ satisfies
\begin{align}
    \mathbb P(\sigma_y=s)
    =\frac{y}{s}\mathbb P(S_s=-y);
    \label{lb:eq-first-passage-identity}
\end{align}
both sides are zero when one of $s,y$ is even and the other is odd.
For completeness,
consider the walk during its first $s-1$ steps. Among paths ending at
$-y+1$, exclude those that have already reached $-y$. Reversing every
step after the first visit to $-y$ gives a one-to-one correspondence
between these excluded paths and all paths ending at $-y-1$.
With the convention that a
binomial coefficient is zero when its lower index is not an integer in its
usual range, appending the final down-step therefore gives
\begin{align*}
    2^s\mathbb P(\sigma_y=s)
    &\stackrel{(a)}{=}\binom{s-1}{(s-y)/2}
      -\binom{s-1}{(s-y-2)/2}\\
    &\stackrel{(b)}{=}\frac{y}{s}\binom{s}{(s-y)/2}
     =\frac{y}{s}\,2^s\mathbb P(S_s=-y),
\end{align*}
where $(a)$ counts the paths that have not yet reached $-y$ and appends
their final down-step, and $(b)$ uses
$\binom{s-1}{j}-\binom{s-1}{j-1}=(s-2j)\binom sj/s$
at $j=(s-y)/2$. This proves \eqref{lb:eq-first-passage-identity} directly.
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb E\left[
       \sum_{y:\sigma_y\leq T}
       \left(\frac{y}{\sigma_y+1}\right)^a
    \right]
    &\stackrel{(a)}{=}
      \sum_{s=1}^T\sum_{y=1}^s
      \left(\frac{y}{s+1}\right)^a
      \frac{y}{s}\mathbb P(S_s=-y)\\
    &\stackrel{(b)}{\leq}
      \sum_{s=1}^T s^{-a-1}\mathbb E|S_s|^{a+1}\\
    &\stackrel{(c)}{\leq}
      \sum_{s=1}^T s^{-(a+1)/2}\\
    &\stackrel{(d)}{\leq}C_\kappa\Phi_\kappa(T).
\end{align*}
Here $(a)$ uses \eqref{lb:eq-first-passage-identity}; $(b)$ uses
$(s+1)^{-a}s^{-1}\leq s^{-a-1}$ and groups the terms as a
one-sided moment of $S_s$; $(c)$ uses Lyapunov's inequality with
$a+1\leq2$ and $\mathbb E S_s^2=s$; and $(d)$ uses
$\sum_{s=1}^Ts^{-1}\leq1+\log T
\leq(1+1/\log2)\log T$ for $T\geq2$ when $a=1$, while for $a<1$ it uses
$1+\int_1^T x^{-(a+1)/2}\,dx\leq C_aT^{(1-a)/2}$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lb:lem-upper-deadlines}
There exists a constant $C_F<\infty$, depending only on
$F$, such that for every $T\geq2$ there is a hindsight selection
of upper-side arrivals with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=$(\alph*)$]
\item Exactly $M_T$ arrivals are selected, with at least $M_t$ selected
arrivals by every time $t$.
\item If each selected arrival is changed from a sale to a hold, the
expected total edge premium is at most $C_F\Phi_\kappa(T)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Fix the sign path.  At time $\sigma_y\leq T$, the number of upper-side
arrivals is
\begin{align*}
    N_y^+
    :=\#\{s\leq\sigma_y:\xi_s=-1\}
    =\frac{\sigma_y+y}{2}\geq y.
\end{align*}
At each deadline $\sigma_y$, select the cheapest upper-side arrival that
has occurred by that deadline and has not already been selected.  The
deadlines are strictly increasing in $y$, so this selects one new arrival
at each of the $M_T$ attained levels.  Moreover, $M_t\geq y$ if and only
if $\sigma_y\leq t$, which proves the deadline property.

Conditional on the sign path, upper-side premiums are independent with
the law of $\psi_+(U)$. At most $y-1$ of the
$N_y^+$ available arrivals were removed before deadline $y$; therefore the
newly selected premium is no larger than the $y$th order statistic among
those $N_y^+$ premiums.  Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-order-statistic} and the formula
$N_y^+=(\sigma_y+y)/2$ give the conditional bound
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb E[\text{$y$th selected premium}\mid(\xi_s)_{s\leq T}]
    &\stackrel{(a)}{\leq}
      A_+\left(\frac{y}{N_y^++1}\right)^{1/\kappa}\\
    &\stackrel{(b)}{\leq} C_F
      \left(\frac{y}{\sigma_y+1}\right)^{1/\kappa}.
\end{align*}
Here $(a)$ uses Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-order-statistic} after at most $y-1$
removals, and $(b)$ uses $N_y^++1\geq(\sigma_y+1)/2$ and
$A_+2^{1/\kappa}\leq2A_+$, so the constant depends only on $F$.
Summing over attained deadlines and then applying
Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-deadline-sum} proves the premium bound.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lb:lem-reverse-repair}
Put $H_T:=S_T+M_T$. There exists a constant
$C_F<\infty$, depending only on $F$, such that
for every $T\geq2$ there is a hindsight selection of exactly $H_T$ lower-side
arrivals with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=$(\alph*)$]
\item The expected total edge premium is at most
$C_F\Phi_\kappa(T)$.
\item Together with the upper selection of
Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-upper-deadlines}, the selected holds produce a path
that is nonnegative at every time and ends at zero.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Define the reversed walk $\widehat S_r:=S_{T-r}-S_T$ for
$0\leq r\leq T$.
Its increments are $-\xi_{T-r+1}$, so it is again a simple symmetric
walk.  An upper-side arrival for the reversed walk is precisely a
lower-side arrival in forward time, and
\begin{align*}
    \max_{0\leq r\leq T}(-\widehat S_r)^+
    &\stackrel{(a)}{=}\max_{0\leq s\leq T}(S_T-S_s)^+
    \stackrel{(b)}{=}S_T+M_T=H_T.
\end{align*}
Here $(a)$ substitutes the reversed walk and reindexes $s=T-r$, and
$(b)$ uses $\min_{s\leq T}S_s=-M_T\leq S_T$.
Apply the greedy construction in Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-upper-deadlines} to
$\widehat S$.  It selects exactly $H_T$ forward lower-side arrivals and,
by Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-order-statistic} applied to the lower-side law
$\psi_-(U)$ and the same deadline-sum calculation, has expected premium
at most $C_F\Phi_\kappa(T)$.

Let $L_t^\sigma$ be the number of these selected lower arrivals up to
forward time $t$.  The reversed deadline property at reverse time
$T-t$ is, on writing
$\mathcal R_t^{\mathrm{rev}}:=\max_{s\in[t,T]}(S_T-S_s)^+$,
$L_T^\sigma-L_t^\sigma\geq\mathcal R_t^{\mathrm{rev}}$.
Since $L_T^\sigma=H_T$, it follows that
\begin{align}
    L_t^\sigma\leq S_T+M_T-\mathcal R_t^{\mathrm{rev}}.
    \label{lb:eq-lower-count-prebound}
\end{align}
We now verify the forward-time inequality
\begin{align}
    L_t^\sigma\leq S_t+M_t.
    \label{lb:eq-lower-forward-bound}
\end{align}
If $M_t=M_T$, then
$\mathcal R_t^{\mathrm{rev}}\geq(S_T-S_t)^+$, and hence
$\mathcal R_t^{\mathrm{rev}}\geq S_T+M_T-S_t-M_t$.  If $M_t<M_T$, a time
$s^\star\in[t,T]$ satisfies $S_{s^\star}=-M_T$, so
$\mathcal R_t^{\mathrm{rev}}\geq S_T+M_T$.  Since $S_t+M_t\geq0$, the latter
bound also gives
$\mathcal R_t^{\mathrm{rev}}\geq S_T+M_T-S_t-M_t$.  Substitution into
\eqref{lb:eq-lower-count-prebound} proves
\eqref{lb:eq-lower-forward-bound} in both cases.

Let $U_t^\sigma$ be the count from the upper selection.  Its deadline
property gives $U_t^\sigma\geq M_t$.  Therefore
\begin{align*}
    y_t^\sigma
    &=S_t+U_t^\sigma-L_t^\sigma
      \stackrel{(a)}{\geq} S_t+M_t-L_t^\sigma
      \stackrel{(b)}{\geq}0,
\end{align*}
where $(a)$ uses the upper deadline property $U_t^\sigma\geq M_t$, and
$(b)$ is \eqref{lb:eq-lower-forward-bound}. At time
step $T$, $U_T^\sigma=M_T$ and
$L_T^\sigma=S_T+M_T$, so $y_T^\sigma=0$.

\label{lb:prop-comparator}

depending only on their displayed parameters, such that for every $T\geq2$
there is a capacity-feasible hindsight policy $\sigma$ satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=$(\alph*)$]
\item Its expected correction cost obeys
$\mathbb E[C_T(\sigma)]
    \leq
    \Delta\mathbb E[M_T]
    +C_{F,\rho}\Phi_\kappa(T)
    +C_{\rho}.$

\item $C_T(\pi^{\mathsf{hs}})\leq C_T(\sigma)$ pathwise.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
First use the upper and lower selected holds from
Lemmas~\ref{lb:lem-upper-deadlines} and
\ref{lb:lem-reverse-repair}.  They define actions in
$\{-1,0,1\}$: a selected arrival is held, and every unselected arrival
uses the action prescribed by the static median-sign rule.  The path is nonnegative and cyclic by
Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-reverse-repair}.  It remains to enforce the capacity.

Define $\mathcal G_T:=\{\max_{0\leq t\leq T}|S_t|\leq\rho T/4\}$.
The maximal Hoeffding inequality for a Rademacher walk gives
$\mathbb P(\mathcal G_T^c)
    \leq2\exp\left(-\frac{\rho^2T}{32}\right).$
On $\mathcal G_T$, $U_t^\sigma\leq U_T^\sigma=M_T$ and
$L_t^\sigma\geq0$, whence
\begin{align*}
    y_t^\sigma
    &=S_t+U_t^\sigma-L_t^\sigma
      \stackrel{(a)}{\leq} |S_t|+M_T
      \leq2\max_{s\leq T}|S_s| \stackrel{(b)}{\leq}\frac{\rho T}{2}
      \leq B.
\end{align*}
Here $(a)$ uses $U_t^\sigma\leq M_T$ and $L_t^\sigma\geq0$, and
$(b)$ uses the defining inequality of $\mathcal G_T$.
On $\mathcal G_T^c$, replace the selected path by the all-hold path,
which stays at zero and is feasible.  This event is known to the hindsight
comparator because it depends on the complete sign path.

On $\mathcal G_T$, the upper holds pay the fixed amount $\Delta M_T$.
The two expected edge-premium bounds are each
$C_F\Phi_\kappa(T)$.  On $\mathcal G_T^c$, every all-hold
correction costs at most one, so $C_T(\sigma)\leq T$.  Multiplying by the preceding Hoeffding bound on
$\mathbb P(\mathcal G_T^c)$ and enlarging the constant proves
the expected-cost bound. Finally, the common-term revenue identity
in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-accounting} shows that maximizing
hindsight reward over feasible policies is equivalent to minimizing
$C_T$.  Since $\sigma$ is feasible, the pathwise inequality for
$\pi^{\mathsf{hs}}$ follows.

\label{lb:lem-boundary-estimates}
There exist universal constants $c,C>0$ such that for every $T\geq1$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=$(\alph*)$]
\item For every $L\geq0$,
$\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb P(\overline Y_{t-1}\leq L)\leq C(L+1)\sqrt T$.
\item $\mathbb E[M_T]\geq c\sqrt T$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The reflection principle gives, for every integer $j\geq0$,
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb P(\overline Y_t=j)
    =\mathbb P(S_t=j)+\mathbb P(S_t=-j-1).
\end{align*}
The binomial coefficient bound
$\sup_{m\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb P(S_t=m)\leq C/\sqrt{t+1}$ therefore yields
$\mathbb P(\overline Y_t\leq L)\leq C(L+1)/\sqrt{t+1}$.
Summing this estimate for $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ and using
$\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(t+1)^{-1/2}\leq2\sqrt T$ proves
the boundary-occupation claim.

For the second claim, $M_T\geq(-S_T)^+$ and symmetry gives
$\mathbb E[(-S_T)^+]=\mathbb E|S_T|/2$. We bound this absolute moment using
$\mathbb E S_T^2=T$ and
$\mathbb E S_T^4=3T^2-2T\leq3T^2$.  Paley--Zygmund applied to $S_T^2$ gives
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb P(S_T^2\geq T/2)
    &\stackrel{(a)}{\geq}\frac{(1-1/2)^2(\mathbb E S_T^2)^2}{\mathbb E S_T^4}
    \stackrel{(b)}{\geq}\frac1{12}.
\end{align*}
Here $(a)$ applies Paley--Zygmund to the nonnegative variable $S_T^2$
at half its mean, and $(b)$ substitutes the second- and fourth-moment bounds.
Thus $\mathbb E|S_T|\geq\sqrt{T/2}/12$, proving
the reflection-size claim.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lb:lem-survival}
Let $X_s=x+S_s$ be a simple symmetric random walk started from an integer
$x\geq1$, and let $\tau_0:=\inf\{s\geq0:X_s=0\}$.  There is a universal
$c>0$ such that, for every $s\geq0$,
$\mathbb P_x(\tau_0>s)\geq c\bigl(1\wedge(x/\sqrt{s+1})\bigr)$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Reflection of paths at their first hit of zero gives
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb P_x(\tau_0>s)
    =\mathbb P(1-x\leq S_s\leq x),
\end{align*}
with the lattice parity inherited from $S_s$.  If
$1\leq x\leq\sqrt{s+1}/4$, the interval $[1-x,x]$ contains at least $c_0x$ points of
the admissible parity, all lying within distance $\sqrt{s+1}$ of zero.
The elementary central-binomial lower bound is
$\mathbb P(S_s=j)\geq c_1/\sqrt{s+1}$ for every such admissible $j$.
Summing gives $c x/\sqrt{s+1}$.  If
$x>\sqrt{s+1}/4$, monotonicity of the survival probability in the
starting height and the preceding estimate at an integer height of order
$\sqrt{s+1}$ give a positive constant.  Adjusting $c$ handles the
finitely many cases in which $\sqrt{s+1}/4<1$.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lb:lem-last-return}
For the fixed integer $2\leq L\leq\sqrt T$ and $z\in(0,z_0]$, there
exists a universal constant $c>0$ such that
$\mathbb E[A_T]\geq c(L/\sqrt T)\mathbb E[U_{\mathrm{out}}]$.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Let $g_T:=\max\{0\leq t\leq T:\overline Y_t=0\}$ be the last return
of the reflected free walk to zero. Since $M_t$ is
constant after $g_T$, $M_T=M_{g_T}$.  Feasibility and
\eqref{lb:eq-state-ledger} imply $U_{g_T}\geq M_{g_T}$.  Therefore,
pathwise,
\begin{align}
    A_T
    &=U_T-M_T
      \stackrel{(a)}{=}(U_{g_T}-M_{g_T})+\sum_{t>g_T}u_t
      \notag\\
    &\stackrel{(b)}{\geq}\sum_{t=1}^Tu_t\mathbf 1\{t>g_T\}.
    \label{lb:eq-after-last-return}
\end{align}
Here $(a)$ uses $M_T=M_{g_T}$ and splits the correction sum at $g_T$,
and $(b)$ uses $U_{g_T}\geq M_{g_T}$.

For each $t$, put
$I_t:=\mathbf 1\{\xi_t=-1,Z_t\leq z,\overline Y_{t-1}>L\}$.
The product $u_tI_t$ is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable.  On $I_t=1$,
$\overline Y_{t-1}$ is an integer at least $L+1$ and the current sign is
$-1$, so $\overline Y_t\geq L$.  Conditional on $\mathcal F_t$, future
signs are fresh Rademacher variables.  Until its next hit of zero, the
reflected walk starting from $\overline Y_t$ is an ordinary simple walk.
Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-survival}, $L\leq\sqrt T$, and
$T-t+1\leq T+1$ therefore give
\begin{align}
    \mathbb P(t>g_T\mid\mathcal F_t)
    \geq c\left(1\wedge
             \frac{L}{\sqrt{T-t+1}}\right)
    \geq c'\frac{L}{\sqrt T}
    \qquad\text{on }\{I_t=1\}.
    \label{lb:eq-conditional-last-return}
\end{align}
Using \eqref{lb:eq-after-last-return}, the tower property, and
\eqref{lb:eq-conditional-last-return},
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb E[A_T]
    &\stackrel{(a)}{\geq}
      \sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E[u_tI_t\mathbf 1\{t>g_T\}]\\
    &\stackrel{(b)}{=}
      \sum_{t=1}^T
      \mathbb E\!\left[u_tI_t
        \mathbb P(t>g_T\mid\mathcal F_t)\right]\\
    &\stackrel{(c)}{\geq}
      c'\frac{L}{\sqrt T}\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E[u_tI_t]
     =c'\frac{L}{\sqrt T}\mathbb E[U_{\mathrm{out}}].
\end{align*}
Here $(a)$ restricts the nonnegative sum in
\eqref{lb:eq-after-last-return}, $(b)$ uses
$\mathcal F_t$-measurability and the tower property, and $(c)$ uses
\eqref{lb:eq-conditional-last-return}.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
The common-term identity in Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-accounting}, optimality of
$\pi^{\mathsf{hs}}$, and Proposition~\ref{lb:prop-comparator} imply
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Reg}(\pi;B,T,0)
    &=\mathbb E[C_T(\pi)-C_T(\pi^{\mathsf{hs}})] \notag\\
    &\stackrel{(a)}{\geq}
      \Delta\mathbb E[A_T]+z\mathbb E[U_{\mathrm{exp}}]
      -C_{F,\rho}\Phi_\kappa(T)-C_\rho.
    \label{lb:eq-reduced-cost}
\end{align}
In step $(a)$, the online upper corrections contribute at least
$\Delta U_T+zU_{\mathrm{exp}}$, the online lower premium is
nonnegative, the comparator contributes at most
$\Delta\mathbb E M_T+C\Phi_\kappa(T)+C$, and
$A_T=U_T-M_T$.

The partition \eqref{lb:eq-upper-decomposition} and
$U_T=M_T+A_T$ give
\begin{align}
    U_{\mathrm{exp}}
    =M_T+A_T-U_{\mathrm{in}}-U_{\mathrm{out}}.
    \label{lb:eq-expensive-identity}
\end{align}
Substituting \eqref{lb:eq-expensive-identity} into
\eqref{lb:eq-reduced-cost} and dropping the nonnegative term
$z\mathbb E[A_T]$ yields
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Reg}(\pi;B,T,0)
    &\geq
      \Delta\mathbb E[A_T]
      +z\mathbb E[M_T]
      -z\mathbb E[U_{\mathrm{in}}]
      -z\mathbb E[U_{\mathrm{out}}]
      -C\Phi_\kappa(T)-C \notag\\
    &\stackrel{(b)}{\geq}
      \left(c\Delta\frac{L}{\sqrt T}-z\right)
      \mathbb E[U_{\mathrm{out}}]
      +z\mathbb E[M_T]-z\mathbb E[U_{\mathrm{in}}]
      -C\Phi_\kappa(T)-C,
    \label{lb:eq-before-balance}
\end{align}
where $(b)$ uses Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-last-return}.

Choose
\begin{align*}
    z:=\frac{c\Delta}{2}\frac{L}{\sqrt T}.
\end{align*}
For the value of $L$ below, $z\leq z_0$ for all sufficiently large $T$.
The coefficient of $\mathbb E[U_{\mathrm{out}}]$ in
\eqref{lb:eq-before-balance} is nonnegative.  Lemmas
\ref{lb:lem-boundary-estimates} and \ref{lb:lem-scarcity}, followed by
this choice of $z$, therefore give
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{Reg}(\pi;B,T,0)
    &\stackrel{(c)}{\geq}
      c_1L
      -Cz^{\kappa+1}(L+1)\sqrt T
      -C\Phi_\kappa(T)-C \notag\\
    &\stackrel{(d)}{\geq}
      c_1L
      -c_2\frac{L^{\kappa+2}}{T^{\kappa/2}}
      -C\Phi_\kappa(T)-C.
    \label{lb:eq-final-balance}
\end{align}
Here $(c)$ uses $\mathbb E M_T\geq c\sqrt T$ and
Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-scarcity}; $(d)$ uses $L\geq2$ and
$z\asymp_{\Delta}L/\sqrt T$.

Let $\gamma:=\kappa/(2(\kappa+1))$ and
$L:=\lfloor aT^\gamma\rfloor$, where $a\in(0,1)$ is fixed below.
For sufficiently large $T$,
$2\leq L\leq\sqrt T$, $z\leq z_0$, and
$L\geq(a/2)T^\gamma$. Moreover,
$L^{\kappa+2}/T^{\kappa/2}\leq a^{\kappa+2}T^\gamma$ because
$\gamma(\kappa+2)-\kappa/2=\gamma$.  Choose $a$ sufficiently small
that $c_2a^{\kappa+2}\leq c_1a/4$.  The first two terms in
\eqref{lb:eq-final-balance} are then at least
$(c_1a/4)T^\gamma$ for all sufficiently large $T$.

It remains to compare the hindsight premium.  If $\kappa=1$, then
$\Phi_\kappa(T)=\log T=o(T^{1/4})$. If $\kappa>1$, then
$\gamma-(\kappa-1)/(2\kappa)=1/(2\kappa(\kappa+1))>0$,
so again $\Phi_\kappa(T)=o(T^\gamma)$.  Enlarging the threshold
$T_0(F,\rho)$ absorbs these lower-order terms and all preceding
endpoint restrictions.  Since
$\gamma=(\beta+1)/(2\beta+4)$, this proves
the bound in Theorem~\ref{lb:thm-positive-gap}.

The bound combines the exact accounting in Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-accounting}
with the feasible hindsight comparison in Proposition~\ref{lb:prop-comparator}.
Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-scarcity} limits cheap corrections near the floor,
while Lemma~\ref{lb:lem-last-return} bounds the cost of making them farther
away. Every constant is independent of $T$ and of the online policy;
it may depend on the fixed distribution $F$ and on $\rho$. In particular,
the positive gap and the two local edge constants are held fixed as
$T$ varies.

\label{ce:bridge}
Let $D_t=y_t^{\textup{\textsf{CE}}}-y_0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=$(\alph*)$]
\item For every $1\le t\le T$, $\mathbb E D_{t-1}^2\le\tau_t$, and $D_T=0$.

Write $\widetilde x_t$ for the tentative action. Conditional on
$\mathcal F_{t-1}$, its mean is $-D_{t-1}/\tau_t$ and its square is
one, outside a probability-zero price-tie event. The centered inventory
after projection is the clipping of $D_{t-1}+\widetilde x_t$ to
$[(-y_0)\vee(-(\tau_t-1)),(B-y_0)\wedge(\tau_t-1)]$.
This interval contains zero, so clipping cannot increase the absolute
value. For $\tau_t\ge2$,
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E[D_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 &\stackrel{(a)}\le
 D_{t-1}^2+2D_{t-1}\mathbb E[\widetilde x_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]+1
 \\
 &\stackrel{(b)}=
 (1-2/\tau_t)D_{t-1}^2+1.
\end{align*}

$\mathbb E D_0^2=0\le T$, taking expectations and using the tower property

 D_t
 &\stackrel{(a)}{\le}\sum_{s=\ell+1}^t\widetilde x_s \stackrel{(b)}{=}Z_t-Z_\ell-\sum_{s=\ell+1}^t D_{s-1}/\tau_s

$\mathbb P(\max_t|Z_t|\ge z/2)\le2e^{-z^2/(32T)}$;

For $m$ purchases and $m$ sales, an exchange argument assigns purchases to the $m$ smallest prices and sales to the $m$ largest. Increasing $m$ by one until $\lfloor T/2\rfloor$ adds a nonnegative price spread. Thus the balanced rank prescription in the introduction is optimal. Break any price ties by independent continuous random numbers; this selects an optimal prescription without changing profit.

Conditional on the ordered price--tie-breaker pairs, their temporal ordering is a uniform permutation. The action labels are therefore a uniform permutation of a population with equal numbers of $+1$ and $-1$, and at most one zero. Let $S_j$ be its partial sum. For fixed $j<T$, expose the first $s\le j$ labels. The Doob martingale is
$M_s=\mathbb E[S_j\mid\text{first }s\text{ labels}]=(T-j)S_s/(T-s)$.
Given the first $s-1$ labels, the range of $M_s$ has length at most two, because $(T-j)/(T-s)\le1$ and the next label lies in $[-1,1]$. A centered random variable with range length two has conditional moment-generating function at most $e^{\theta^2/2}$: this follows by twice differentiating its log moment-generating function, whose second derivative is a variance bounded by one. Iteration gives $\mathbb E e^{\theta S_j}\le e^{j\theta^2/2}$. Markov's inequality with $\theta=h/j$ gives $\mathbb P(|S_j|\ge h)\le2e^{-h^2/(2j)}$. Also $S_T=0$. A union bound gives
$\mathbb P(\max_{j\le T}|S_j|\ge\eta T)\le2T e^{-\eta^2T/2}$.
Off this event the rank prescription is storage-feasible, so the static and storage optima agree. The difference is always nonnegative and at most $T$, since all-hold is feasible and each reward is at most one. This proves Proposition~\ref{prop:static-interior}. The concentration argument applies to the hindsight ordering; the adaptive online inventory is treated separately in Appendix~\ref{app:interior-transfer}.

conditional law of $\mathbf U$ independent of the past. We first show
\begin{align*}
 U_1\le W\le1,\qquad \lambda\le\log2<3/4.
\end{align*}
For the first inequality, take an optimal future plan from zero. If its
first action is to hold, a plan starting from one sells instead; if its
first action is to buy, a plan starting from one holds instead. In both
cases the two plans have the same inventory after this action, so the
new plan can follow the original continuation and remains feasible.

Lemma~\ref{lem:main-marginal-range}.

 \stackrel{(c)}{=}\log2.

where (a) is the tail integral formula for $0\le W\le1$,

$\mathbb P(E_a^c\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})\le(1-a)/a$, and (c) evaluates the integral.

$\partial R_t(0,x_t^\pi;p_t,\mathbf U)

 &\quad\stackrel{(a)}{=}
   \mathbb E[(W-p_t)^+\mid\mathcal F_{t-1},p_t]
       -x_t^\pi(\lambda-p_t)\\
 &\quad\stackrel{(b)}{\ge}
   \mathbb E[(W-p_t)^+\mid\mathcal F_{t-1},p_t]
       -(\lambda-p_t)^+.
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}

 &\stackrel{(a)}{\ge}\int_{3/4}^1\int_p^1(u-p)\,du\,dp
 \stackrel{(b)}{=}\frac1{384}.
\end{align*}
Here (a) uses the preceding conditional bound and the independent

<!-- lemma-15: body_md -->
\label{lem:stars-concavity}\label{lem:stars-queue}
Fix a future price vector in $[0,1]^\tau$ and a capacity $B$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=$(\alph*)$]
\item $V_\tau^B$ is discretely concave, its real marginals form a
decreasing list, and each marginal is one of the future prices.
Thus the marginals lie in $[0,1]$, and $\mathbb E V_\tau^B$ is also concave.
\item Prepending price $p$ updates every new real marginal by
\begin{align}
 \widetilde{\Delta V}(y)=\Delta V_\tau^B(y+1)\vee\bigl(p\wedge\Delta V_\tau^B(y-1)\bigr).
 \label{eq:stars-minmax}
\end{align}
For this formula only, use ordering conventions $\Delta V_\tau^B(y)=+\infty$
for $y\le0$ and $\Delta V_\tau^B(y)=-\infty$ beyond the old domain. They are
not random values whose expectations are taken.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-15 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
Induct on the number of future prices. The zero-price domain is a
singleton; after one price the first marginal, if present, is $p$.
Suppose the old list decreases. At an inventory where the hold action
is feasible, Bellman's maximization improves the hold reward by
$(p-\Delta V(y))^++(\Delta V(y+1)-p)^+$, omitting missing actions; at most one term
is positive. Here and in the next display, suppress the old horizon
and write $\Delta V_y=\Delta V_\tau^B(y)$. Subtract the new values at
adjacent old states:
\begin{align*}
 \widetilde{\Delta V}(y)
 &\stackrel{(a)}{=}\Delta V_y+(p-\Delta V_y)^++(\Delta V_{y+1}-p)^+
      -(p-\Delta V_{y-1})^+-(\Delta V_y-p)^+\\*
 &\stackrel{(b)}{=}p+(\Delta V_{y+1}-p)^+-(p-\Delta V_{y-1})^+\\*
 &\stackrel{(c)}{=}\Delta V_{y+1}\vee\bigl(p\wedge\Delta V_{y-1}\bigr).
\end{align*}
where (a) subtracts the two Bellman improvements over holding,
(b) uses $a+(p-a)^+-(a-p)^+=p$, and (c) uses
$\Delta V_{y+1}\le \Delta V_{y-1}$. At the floor the update is $p\vee\Delta V_2$;
a newly feasible top state $y=\tau+1$ must sell and gives
$p\wedge\Delta V_\tau^B(\tau)$.
At the physical cap the unavailable purchase is omitted. The first
marginal from the singleton case is $p$. These checks establish
\eqref{eq:stars-minmax} at every boundary without subtracting infinite values.

Both inputs $\Delta V(y+1),\Delta V(y-1)$ decrease in $y$, so the updated list also
decreases. Each new entry is an old entry or $p$. This closes the
induction, proving support-valued marginals and discrete concavity.
Expectation preserves the concavity inequalities, and its first
differences are $\lambda_\tau^B(y)$ by linearity.

<!-- lemma-17: body_md -->
\label{lem:stars-fair}
Fix integers $\tau,y\ge1$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=$(\alph*)$]
\item With absolute comparison constants $c,C>0$,
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb P_{1/2}(\sigma_y>\tau)&=\mathbb P(-y\le S_\tau<y)
 \asymp \frac{y}{\sqrt{\tau+1}}\wedge1.
\end{align*}
\item For every fixed sign probability and $x\ge0$,
\begin{align}
 \mathbb P\left(\max_{j\le \tau}(S_j-\mathbb E S_j)\ge x\right)
 &\le e^{-x^2/(2\tau)}.\label{eq:stars-hoeffding}
\end{align}
The same inequality holds for the negative centered walk.
\item If $a>1/2$ and $y\le(2a-1)\tau$, then
\begin{align}
 \mathbb P_a(\sigma_y>\tau)
 \le e^{-((2a-1)\tau-y)^2/(2\tau)}.\label{eq:stars-failure}
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-17 -->
<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
Reflection after the first hit of $y$ gives
$\mathbb P(\sigma_y\le \tau)=\mathbb P(S_\tau\ge y)+\mathbb P(S_\tau>y)$; symmetry proves
the claimed fair-survival identity. A central binomial mass is of order
$(\tau+1)^{-1/2}$. One direct verification at $\tau=2m$ is
$4^{-m}\binom{2m}{m}=\prod_{j=1}^m(1-1/(2j))$: take logarithms,
use $-x-x^2\le\log(1-x)\le-x$ for $x\le1/2$, and sum. At odd
times multiply the previous central mass by $(2m+1)/(2m+2)$.
There are $y$ parity points in $[-y,y)$ unless the support cuts them
off, giving the upper bound. For $y\le\sqrt \tau/4$, all their masses
are comparable to the central mass: their ratios are products of
$(m-i+1)/(m+i)$ at even times, or $(m-i+1)/(m+i+1)$ at odd times,
with at most $\sqrt \tau/4$ factors. Their log ratios are bounded below
by $-C\sum_{i\le\sqrt \tau/4}i/\tau\ge-C'$. This gives the lower bound
in this range; monotonicity in $y$ extends it to the remaining range.
Finitely many small $\tau$ are absorbed in the constants.

For a sign $X$, the second derivative of its log moment-generating
function is a tilted variance, at most one. Integrating twice yields
$\mathbb E e^{\lambda(X-\mathbb E X)}\le e^{\lambda^2/2}$. Stop the resulting
nonnegative exponential supermartingale at the first centered crossing
of $x$ or epoch $\tau$. Its value at a crossing is at least
$e^{\lambda x-\tau\lambda^2/2}$, so the crossing probability is at most
$e^{-\lambda x+\tau\lambda^2/2}$. Taking $\lambda=x/\tau$ proves
\eqref{eq:stars-hoeffding}. Negating the signs gives the lower tail;
$\{\sigma_y>\tau\}\subset\{S_\tau<y\}$ then gives \eqref{eq:stars-failure}.

\begin{align}
 b_{y+1}G_y-(1-b_y)G_{y-1}=f(y)-\bar f,\qquad 0\le y\le J.
 \label{eq:stars-sc-accounting-equation}
\end{align}
Omit the downward term at zero and the upward term at $J$; the
latter equation uses $\sum_{i=0}^J\pi_i(f(i)-\bar f)=0$.
Thus the weighted partial sums in the construction give precisely
the expected-increment identity needed to account for $f(y)$.

At a reference state $y=y_{t-1}<J$, we have
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E[\Phi(y_t)-\Phi(y_{t-1})\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 &\stackrel{(a)}{=}\gamma_{\tau_t-1}^B(y+1)G_y-
                           (1-\gamma_{\tau_t-1}^B(y))G_{y-1}\\
 &\stackrel{(b)}{\ge}b_{y+1}G_y-(1-b_y)G_{y-1}
 \stackrel{(c)}{=}f(y)-\bar f,
\end{align*}
where $(a)$ conditions on the independent current price, $(b)$ uses
the assumed lower bounds on the two thresholds and $G_j\ge0$, and $(c)$ is
\eqref{eq:stars-sc-accounting-equation}. At zero the downward
terms in this chain are omitted.
At $y=J$ the purchase term is zero, so no bound on edge $J+1$
is needed. Above $J$, $\Phi$ is flat and $f(y)\le f(J)\le\bar f$,
so the same inequality holds, even at a constrained boundary.
Sum it and use the tower property. The potential telescopes on
the reference trajectory; $0\le\Phi\le\Phi(J)$ proves the claimed accounting bound.

Apply \eqref{eq:stars-at-hinges} to an existing marginal

\end{align*}

 &\stackrel{(a)}{\le}\mathbb P(W\le a^-)
 \stackrel{(b)}{\le}\mathbb P_{1/2}(\sigma_{\ell}>\tau)+\varepsilon_T
 \stackrel{(c)}{\le}C\ell/\sqrt \tau+\varepsilon_T.
\end{align*}
where (a) uses the absence of support in $(a^-,a^+)$ and the

or below $a^-$ remain.

<!-- proposition-11: body_md -->
\label{prop:stars-continuous-sampling}
Fix an $M$-Lipschitz distribution $F$. Let $K_t\ge1$ be chosen
from the past before observing $p_t$, and use $K_t$ shared fresh i.i.d.\
futures independent of the current price and actual future. Then
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Reg}_T(\pi^K)
 \le\operatorname{Reg}_T(\pi^\circ)+M\mathbb E\sum_{t=1}^{T-1} K_t^{-1/2}.
\end{align*}
In particular $K_t\ge(T-t+1)^2$ adds at most $M\log T$,
preserving all the continuous-class rates.

\emph{First bound the value of one additional unit under the reference policy.}
Let $J_\tau(y)$ be the expected reward of the fixed reference rule
$\pi^\circ$ starting at $y$ with $\tau$ decision epochs left,
still ending at zero.
The continuation value $J_\tau$ is defined by following this fixed
online policy.
Run this policy from adjacent feasible inventories on the same prices.
Decreasing thresholds imply that the higher process's action is no
larger than the lower one's. A crossing would require a purchase at
$z$ and a sale at $z+1$, hence a price both below and above the same
threshold $\lambda_{\tau-1}^B(z+1)$ when $\tau$ decisions remain.
Holding at ties prevents this too. Walls
remove outward actions; if the upper state is on the terminal
boundary, it must sell and the lower state cannot buy. The distance
therefore stays one or becomes zero. Until coalescence the actions
agree; at coalescence the upper action is one less. Both end at zero,
so coalescence occurs, and their reward difference is exactly the
price at that epoch. Consequently
\begin{align}
 0\le J_\tau(y+1)-J_\tau(y)\le1,\qquad
 |\mathcal Q_\tau(y,p,x)-\mathcal Q_\tau(y,p,z)|\le|x-z|,
 \label{eq:stars-online-lipschitz}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal Q_\tau(y,p,x)=-px+J_{\tau-1}(y+x)$. The second
inequality follows by summing the first between feasible residual
inventories; the reward difference lies between $-p(x-z)$ and
$(1-p)(x-z)$ when $x>z$. We use $\mathcal Q$ to evaluate a chosen
action followed by the fixed reference rule.

\emph{Next compare the actions at the sampled policy's current inventory.}
Condition on its pre-price history, including $y$ and $K=K_t$, and
write $\tau=\tau_t$ for the number of remaining decisions.
Let $X$ be the reference action at that state and $\widehat X$ the
\textsf{STARS} action. Shared scenarios keep the two empirical thresholds
ordered. Each is an average of $K$ variables in $[0,1]$, so its
variance is at most $1/(4K)$ and its mean absolute error at most
$1/(2\sqrt K)$ by Cauchy--Schwarz. In the next display,
$\lambda_b=\lambda_{\tau-1}^B(y+1)$ and
$\lambda_s=\lambda_{\tau-1}^B(y)$ are the existing population thresholds,
and their hats denote the corresponding shared-scenario sample means.
Independence of the current price
and the $M$-Lipschitz CDF give
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E[|X-\widehat X|\mid\text{past}]
 &\stackrel{(a)}{\le}\mathbb E[|F(\lambda_b)-F(\widehat \lambda_b)|
                  +|F(\lambda_s)-F(\widehat \lambda_s)|\mid\text{past}]\notag\\
 &\stackrel{(b)}{\le}M\mathbb E[|\lambda_b-\widehat\lambda_b|
                    +|\lambda_s-\widehat\lambda_s|\mid\text{past}]
 \stackrel{(c)}{\le}M/\sqrt K.\label{eq:stars-sample-actions}
\end{align}
where (a) integrates the buy and sell disagreement intervals using
independence of the current price and scenarios, (b) applies the
$M$-Lipschitz property of $F$, and (c) uses the two mean absolute
error bounds $1/(2\sqrt K)$ proved above.
Missing channels have zero error, forced actions agree, and ties are
null under the continuous price law.

\emph{Finally sum the reward differences along the sampled path.}
Using the fixed-policy equation $J_\tau(y)=\mathbb E_p\mathcal Q_\tau(y,p,X)$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 &\left|J_\tau(y)-
 \mathbb E[-p_t\widehat X+J_{\tau-1}(y+\widehat X)\mid\text{past}]\right|\\
 &\qquad\stackrel{(a)}{\le}
 \mathbb E[|\mathcal Q_\tau(y,p_t,X)-\mathcal Q_\tau(y,p_t,\widehat X)|
       \mid\text{past}]\\
 &\qquad\stackrel{(b)}{\le}\mathbb E[|X-\widehat X|\mid\text{past}]
 \stackrel{(c)}{\le}M K_t^{-1/2}.
\end{align*}
where (a) uses the fixed-policy equation and
$|\mathbb E Z|\le\mathbb E|Z|$, (b) applies \eqref{eq:stars-online-lipschitz},
and (c) applies \eqref{eq:stars-sample-actions} at the empirical state.
At the last epoch, the terminal target forces the same action for
both policies, so the terminal summand is zero.
Take expectations and sum along the empirical path. The $J$ terms
telescope to $J_T(0)$ since $J_0(0)=0$. This bounds the difference
of expected online rewards; subtracting from the common hindsight
benchmark proves the proposition. The asserted schedule uses
$\sum_{\tau=2}^T1/\tau\le\log T$.

\label{lem:sampled-compensation}
Let $F$ be any law on $[0,1]$. Conditional on the pre-price history,
fix a feasible inventory $y$, a continuation length $\tau=\tau_t-1=T-t$, and an integer
$K=K_t\ge1$ chosen from that history. The $K$ shared scenario futures
are i.i.d., independent of the current price and actual future.
Let $\widehat{\partial\bar R}_{\tau,K}^B(y)$ be the expected marginal
compensation of the resulting \textup{\textsf{STARS}} action, averaging over all three
sources of randomness. Let $\partial\bar R_\tau^B(y)$ be the local
compensation of the population-threshold reference rule, as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:stars-compensation}. There is an absolute
constant $C<\infty$ such that
$\widehat{\partial\bar R}_{\tau,K}^B(y)
\le2\partial\bar R_\tau^B(y)+C/K$.

\emph{First separate the sampling error from the reference compensation.}
Condition also on the current price $p$. For an existing buy channel,
put $Z=W-p$, where $W=\Delta V_\tau^B(y+1)$ is the actual future
marginal. Its $K$ scenario copies are $Z_1,\ldots,Z_K$; write
$\bar Z=K^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^K Z_j$,
$A=\mathbb E Z^+$, $D=\mathbb E(-Z)^+$, and $m=\mathbb E Z=A-D$.
These expectations are conditional on the fixed history and price.
Holding at ties makes the buy indicator $\mathbf 1\{\bar Z>0\}$.
The actual future is independent of the scenarios, so its expected
compensation in this channel is
\begin{align*}
 L_K
 &\stackrel{(a)}{=}
   \mathbb E[Z^+-Z\mathbf 1\{\bar Z>0\}]
 \stackrel{(b)}{=} A-m\mathbb P(\bar Z>0).
\end{align*}
Here (a) subtracts the chosen channel's gain from hindsight's positive
gain, and (b) uses independence of the actual future and scenarios.
If $m>0$, this equals $D+m\mathbb P(\bar Z\le0)$; if $m<0$, it equals
$A+(-m)\mathbb P(\bar Z>0)$; if $m=0$, it equals $A=D$.
The reference channel cost is $A\wedge D$. Thus the additional
compensation is the absolute mean advantage multiplied by the
probability that the scenarios give it the wrong sign.
In particular, a population tie adds no expected compensation,
regardless of the sampled action.

\emph{Next bound the cost of a wrong-sign estimate.}
Suppose first that $m>0$, and put $v=\operatorname{Var}(Z)$.
Since $Z\in[-p,1-p]\subset[-1,1]$, we have
$|Z-m|\le1$ and
$v\le\mathbb E Z^2\le\mathbb E|Z|=m+2D$.
If $m\le D$, the extra term above is at most $D$.
If $m>D$, then $v\le3m$, and the one-sided Bernstein inequality gives
\begin{align*}
 m\mathbb P(\bar Z\le0)
 &\stackrel{(a)}{\le}
 m\exp\!\left(-\frac{Km^2}{2(v+m/3)}\right)
 \stackrel{(b)}{\le}m e^{-3Km/20}
 \stackrel{(c)}{\le}\frac{20}{3eK}.
\end{align*}
Step (a) applies Bernstein to the independent centered variables
$Z_j-m$, whose absolute values are at most one and whose variances
equal $v$. Step (b) substitutes $v\le3m$. Step (c) maximizes
$u e^{-3Ku/20}$ over $u\ge0$.
For $v=0$ the wrong-sign probability is zero, so the same bound holds.
If $m<0$, apply this argument to $-Z$: its negative-part expectation
is $A$, and its nonpositive sample-mean event contains
$\{\bar Z>0\}$. Thus in all cases
$L_K\le2(A\wedge D)+20/(3eK)$.

For a sell channel take $Z=p-\Delta V_\tau^B(y)$ instead. Selling again
occurs exactly when its sample mean is positive, and the same proof
applies. Shared scenarios preserve the ordering of the two marginal
thresholds, so the mutually exclusive channel indicators describe a
feasible maximizing action.
Integrating the fixed-price inequalities over $F$, their reference
costs sum to $\partial\bar R_\tau^B(y)$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:stars-compensation}. Summing the at most two
existing channels gives the lemma, for example with $C=40/(3e)$.
Unavailable channels are omitted. At a compulsory-action state both
policies and hindsight take that action, so compensation is zero;
this includes the terminal epoch $\tau=0$.

Fix the finite atomic law $F$ with positive median gap and
$\rho\in(0,1)$ as above. There are constants $A,\tau_0,c,C>0$,
depending only on $F$ and $\rho$, such that, with

\begin{enumerate}[label=$(\alph*)$]
\item If $y\ge J_{\tau}$, then

\item If $y\le H_{\tau}$, then
$\mathbb P(\widehat\lambda_{\tau}^B(y)\le a^-)\le\delta_{\tau}$.
\item If $\mu^+<1/2$, the logarithmic buffer $h_{\bar\tau}$ in

\emph{Above the buffer, sampling rarely prevents a sale.}
For $W=\Delta V_{\tau}^B(y)$, the gap and fair-walk representation give
\begin{align*}
 a^+-\mathbb E W
 &\ge\Delta\mathbb P(W\le a^-)-\mathbb E(W-a^+)^+ \ge c\bigl((y/\sqrt \tau)\wedge1\bigr)-r^y.
\end{align*}
The second line uses Lemma~\ref{lem:stars-fair}; capping the queue
only increases its failure probability. If $\mu^+=1/2$, the upward
hinge is zero and the $r^y$ term is omitted. Choose $A$ so that,
at $y=J_{\tau}$, the right side is at least $c\log \tau/\sqrt \tau$ for
large $\tau$. Monotonicity in $y$ gives the same lower bound for larger
indices. Hoeffding for $K$ independent variables in $[0,1]$ then
bounds the first probability by $\exp(-c \tau\log^2 \tau)$.

\emph{Below $H_{\tau}$, the scenarios detect the value of buying.}
The fair walk has the moderate-deviation bound
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb P_{1/2}(\sigma_y\le \tau)
 \ge c\,\tau^{-c_*/2}/\sqrt{\log \tau},\qquad y\le H_{\tau}.
\end{align*}
We prove this estimate directly, keeping track of its polynomial
exponent because it determines the required sample size.
For even $\tau=2m$ and parity-admissible $z=2\ell$, the ratio of the
binomial mass at $z$ to its central mass is
$\prod_{i=1}^\ell(m-i+1)/(m+i)$. Using
$\log(1-u)\ge-u-u^2$ for $0\le u\le1/2$ and
$\log(1+u)\le u$ gives a log ratio at least
$-\ell^2/m-C\ell^3/m^2$.
For odd $\tau=2m+1$ and $z=2\ell+1$, the corresponding product is
$\prod_{i=1}^\ell(m-i+1)/(m+i+1)$, with log ratio at least
$-\ell(\ell+1)/m-C(\ell+1)^3/m^2$.
Thus in either parity, uniformly for $z=O(\sqrt{\tau\log \tau})$,
the mass at $z$ is at least
$c \tau^{-1/2}\exp(-z^2/(2\tau)-o(1))$; the central mass is bounded
below by $c/\sqrt \tau$ by the binomial estimate in
Lemma~\ref{lem:stars-fair}.
There are at least $c\sqrt{\tau/\log \tau}$ admissible positions between
$H_{\tau}$ and $H_{\tau}+\lfloor\sqrt{\tau/\log \tau}\rfloor+2$.
For each, $z^2/(2\tau)\le(c_*/2)\log \tau+C$. Summing their masses
and using $\{S_{\tau}\ge y\}\subset\{\sigma_y\le \tau\}$ proves the bound.

For $y\le H_{\tau}$, the capacity comparison and the positive drift of
the count immediately below $a^-$ now give
$q:=\mathbb P(W>a^-)\ge c \tau^{-7/4}/\sqrt{\log \tau}$ and
$d:=\mathbb P(W<a^-)\le C e^{-c\tau}$.
The capacity error $CT^2e^{-c_\rho T}$ is absorbed into
$Ce^{-c'\tau}$ uniformly in $T\ge \tau$.
Put $X=W-a^-$. Its positive values are at least $\Delta$, so
$m:=\mathbb E X\ge\Delta q-d\ge\Delta q/2$ beyond a fixed cutoff.
Also $\operatorname{Var}(X)\le\mathbb E X^2\le q+d\le2q$ and $m\le q$.
Bernstein, with centered increments bounded by one, yields
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb P(\widehat\lambda_{\tau}^B(y)\le a^-)
 &\le\exp\!\left(-\frac{Km^2}{2(\operatorname{Var}(X)+m/3)}\right)
 \le e^{-cKq}
 \le\exp\!\left(-\frac{c \tau^{1/4}}{\sqrt{\log \tau}}\right).
\end{align*}
This is at most $Ce^{-c'\tau^{1/5}}$ after changing constants. Since
the preceding estimate decreases with $K$, the bound holds for every
$K\ge(\tau+1)^2$.

\emph{Below the logarithmic buffer, the upper median atom induces a

The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:stars-at-thresholds} gives
$\lambda_{\tau}^B(y)-a^+\ge c_1r^y-C_2y/\sqrt \tau-C_3\varepsilon_T$
for $y=O(\log \tau)$. Choose the fixed constant in $h_{\bar\tau}$ large enough
that at $y=h_{\bar\tau}+1$ this is at least $c\log \bar\tau/\sqrt{\bar\tau}$.
Concavity extends the margin to smaller indices. Hoeffding and
$K\ge \bar\tau^2$ give the last claim.
