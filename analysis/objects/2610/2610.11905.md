<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{IntroTheorem1}
There exists $\varepsilon = \varepsilon(g,d) > 0$ with the following property: for any polarized abelian variety $(A,L)$ over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ of dimension $g$ with $L$ symmetric, and any irreducible closed subvariety $V \subseteq A$ that generates $A$ of degree $\deg_L V \leq d$, with $\operatorname{Stab}(V)$ finite, we have 
$$\widehat{h}_{A,L}(V) \geq \varepsilon \max \{1, h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A)\}.$$
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{IntroTheorem2}
There exists positive constants $\varepsilon = \varepsilon(g,d) > 0, M = M(g,d) > 0$ such that the following holds: let $K$ be a global function field and $(A,L)$ a polarized abelian variety with $L$ symmetric. For any integral subvariety $V \subseteq A_{\overline{K}}$ satisfying $\deg_L(V) \leq d$, such that $V$ generates $A$ and $\operatorname{Stab}(V)$ is finite, we have 
$$\widehat{h}_{A,L}(V) \geq \varepsilon h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(B),$$
where $B$ is an abelian variety with an isogeny $\pi: A \to B$ of degree at most $M$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{IntroTheorem3}
In the setting of Theorem \ref{IntroTheorem2}, assume that $V = C$ is a curve. Then there exists positive constants $\varepsilon = \varepsilon(g, \deg_L(C)) > 0, M = M(g,\deg_L(C)) > 0, N = N(g,\deg_L(C)) > 0$ such that 
$$\# \left\{ x \in C(\overline{K}) \mid \widehat{h}_{A,L}(x) < \varepsilon h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(B) \right\} \leq N,$$
where $B$ is an abelian variety with an isogeny $\pi: A \to B$ of degree at most $M$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{IntroTheorem4}
Let $K$ be an algebraically closed, countable GVF and $A/K$ an abelian variety. Let $L$ be a symmetric ample line bundle on $A$ and $V$ a subvariety of $A$. Then
$$\widehat{h}_{A,L}(V) = 0 \text{ if and only if } V \text{ is special.}$$

<!-- defn-1: body_md -->
An \textbf{adelic curve} is the data $(K, (\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \nu), \phi)$ of a field $K,$ a measure space $(\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \nu),$ and a map $\phi: \Omega \rightarrow M_K,$ where $M_K$ is the set of all absolute value functions on $K,$ satisfying the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mathcal{A}$ is an $\sigma$-algebra and $\nu$ is a measure on $(\Omega, \mathcal{A}),$
    \item For any $a \in K^{\times},$ the function $\omega \mapsto \log \phi(\omega)(a)$ is integrable with respect to $\nu$ (and thus is also $\mathcal{A}$-measurable).
\end{enumerate}
We say that this adelic curve is \textbf{proper} if for all $a \in K^{\times},$ $\int\limits_{\Omega} \log \phi(\omega)(a) d\nu = 0.$
<!-- end defn-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
Let $(K, (\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \nu), \phi)$ be an adelic curve. Let $\Omega_{\infty}$ be the set of $\omega \in \Omega$ for which $\phi(\omega)$ is archimedean, and let $\Omega_0$ be the set of $\omega \in \Omega$ for which $\phi(\omega)$ is trivial. Then, $\Omega_{\infty}$ is $\nu$-measurable, and if $K$ is countable, then $\Omega_0$ is as well.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- defn-2: body_md -->
An \textbf{adelic line bundle} is a one-dimensional vector space $V$ along with metrics $\|\cdot\|_{\omega}$ on $V \otimes_K K_{\omega}$ for each $\omega \in \Omega,$ such that the family of these metrics are dominated (\cite[Definition 4.1.2]{CM20}) and measurable (\cite[\S 4.1.3]{CM20}).
<!-- end defn-2 -->
<!-- defn-3: body_md -->
Let $X$ be a projective scheme over $\operatorname{Spec} K$. An \textbf{adelic line bundle} on $X$ is an invertible $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_X$-module $L$ equipped with a metric family $\varphi$ which is dominated (\cite[Definition 6.1.9]{CM20}) and measurable (\cite[Definition 6.1.27]{CM20}).
<!-- end defn-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Note that already over a number field $K$, the Chen--Moriwaki line bundles are more general than the ones introduced by Zhang \cite{Zha95b} as we only assume a dominated condition on the metrics, instead of triviality at all but finitely many places. As such, we will distinguish these two notions by calling the Chen--Moriwaki version CM adelic line bundles. Later in the paper we describe how these two notions of adelic line bundles are related.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- defn-4: body_md -->
Let $V$ be an adelic line bundle on $S.$ Its Arakelov degree is defined to be
\begin{align}
\label{eqn:arakelovdegree}
    \int_{\Omega} -\log \|s\|_{\omega} d\nu. 
\end{align}
for any nonzero $s \in V.$
<!-- end defn-4 -->
<!-- defn-5: body_md -->
Let $\omega \in \Omega$ and $D$ a Cartier divisor on $X.$ Let $X^{\operatorname{an}}_{\omega}$ be the Berkovich analytification of $X$ with respect to the valued field $(K, \phi(\omega)).$ A \textbf{Green function} for $D$ is a continuous function $g$ on some Zariski-dense open $U$ of $X$ such that for any nonempty Zariski open of $X$ where $D$ is cut out by the function $f,$ $g + \log f$ extends to a continuous function on $U^{\operatorname{an}}.$
<!-- end defn-5 -->
<!-- defn-6: body_md -->
An \textbf{adelic Cartier divisor} is the data of a Cartier divisor $D$ on $X$ and a family of compatible Green functions $\{g_{\omega}\}_{\omega \in \Omega}$ such that the associated metric family given by $e^{-g_{\omega}(\bullet)}$ is dominated and measureable (in the same sense as for adelic line bundles).
<!-- end defn-6 -->
<!-- defn-7: body_md -->
We denote by $\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(X^{\operatorname{an}})$ to be the group (under tensor product) of isomorphism classes of pairs $(L, \ensuremath{\left\| \cdot \right\|})$ of a line bundle $L$ on $X$ and a continuous metric $\ensuremath{\left\| \cdot \right\|}$ of $L$ on $X^{\operatorname{an}},$ where isomorphisms are given by isomorphisms of line bundles which preserve the metric. We call such pairs \textbf{metrized line bundles.}
<!-- end defn-7 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\cite{yuan2021adelic} also defines the category of metrized line bundles. However, we will only need to use the group, so we will not concern ourselves with this definition.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- defn-8: body_md -->
We say that a metrized line bundle $L$ on $X$ is a \textbf{model adelic line bundle} if there exists a pair $(\mathcal{X}, \overline{\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}}),$ where $\mathcal{X}$ is a projective model of $X$ over $k$ and $\overline{\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}}$ is
\begin{enumerate}
    \item a line bundle on $\mathcal{X}$ extending $L^{\otimes e}$ for some positive integer $e,$ if $k$ is a field, or
    \item a hermitian line bundle $(\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}, \ensuremath{\left\| \cdot \right\|})$ on $\mathcal{X}$ if $k = \mathbb{Z},$ where $\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}$ is a line bundle on $\mathcal{X}$ extending $L^{\otimes e}$ for some positive integer $e$ and $\ensuremath{\left\| \cdot \right\|}$ are continuous metrics of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{C})$ in $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C}),$ such that these metrics are invariant under complex conjugation.  
\end{enumerate}
The subcategory of $\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(X^{\operatorname{an}})$ consisting of model adelic line bundles is denoted $\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(X/k)_{\mathrm{mod}}.$
<!-- end defn-8 -->
<!-- defn-9: body_md -->
An \textbf{adelic line bundle} is an object of $\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(X^{\operatorname{an}})$ which is isomorphic to the limit of a sequence in $\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(X/k)_{\mathrm{mod}}$. We denote the category of adelic line bundles as $\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(X/k)$.
<!-- end defn-9 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Yuan--Zhang later provide a different definition of adelic line bundles, namely as equivalence classes of adelic divisors (see \cite[\S 2.4]{yuan2021adelic}). However, this construction is equivalent to the definition we use, which is taken from \cite[\S 1]{yuan2021adelic} (see also \cite[\S 3.4]{yuan2021adelic}).
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
Let $X \to Y$ be a projective flat morphism of quasiprojective varieties over $k$ of relative dimension $n$, where $k$ is either $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}$ or a field. Suppose $Y$ is normal. 
The Deligne pairing on model adelic line bundles induces a symmetric and multilinear functor \[\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(X)^{n+1}_{\mathrm{int}} \rightarrow \widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(Y)_{\mathrm{int}}\] 
compatible with base changes $Y' \to Y$ where for $Y'$ any quasiprojective normal variety over $k$ such that $X \times_Y Y' $ is integral.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
Every countable GVF arises from a proper adelic curve.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- defn-10: body_md -->
Let $I$ be a set. Then, a \textbf{filter} is a family of subsets $\omega$ satisfying the following three properties:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\emptyset \not \in \omega,$
    \item If $A \subset B \subset I$ and $A \in \omega,$ then $B \in \omega,$
    \item If $A, B \in \omega,$ then $A \cap B \in \omega$ too.
\end{enumerate}
An \textbf{ultrafilter} is a filter which is maximal; that is, for all $A \subset I,$ either $A \in \omega$ or $I - A \in \omega.$
<!-- end defn-10 -->
<!-- defn-11: body_md -->
\label{defn: ultrafilter}
Suppose that we have a set $I,$ an ultrafilter $\omega,$ and, for each $i \in I,$ a GVF $K_i.$ Then, we define the \textbf{ultraproduct} of the $K_i$ with respect to $\omega$ as the quotient of \[\left\{(x_i)\in \prod\limits_{i \in I} K_i \mid h_{K_i}(x_i) \text{ bounded}\right\}\] by the equivalence relation that $(x_i) \sim (y_i)$ if $x_i = y_i$ on a set $S \in \omega.$
<!-- end defn-11 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{GVFConstruction1}
Let $\omega$ be a non-principal ultrafilter on a set $I$ and let $X$ be a finite type quasi-projective variety over a field $k$ and fix an embedding $X \xhookrightarrow{} \mathbb{P}_{k}^N$. For each $i \in I$, let $K_i$ be a GVF field mapping to $X$ by $x_i: \operatorname{Spec} K_i \to X.$ After viewing $x_i$ as a point in $\mathbb{P}^N$, assume that $h(x_i)$ is uniformly bounded. Let $K_{\omega}$ be the ultraproduct of the $K_i$ with respect to $\omega.$ Then there exists a map
$$x_{\omega}: \operatorname{Spec} K_{\omega} \to X$$
induced by the $x_i$'s.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
First, suppose that $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is a finite type affine scheme over $\mathbb{Z}$ with an embedding $\operatorname{Spec} A \rightarrow \mathbb{A}^N_{\mathbb{Z}},$ and we have points $\operatorname{Spec} K_i \rightarrow \operatorname{Spec} A$ where the $K_i$ are GVFs indexed by a set $I,$ such that the coordinates of the points in $\mathbb{A}^N_{\mathbb{Z}}$ have bounded height. That is, there is a constant $C$ such that for all $i, j,$ the image of the $j$th coordinate $T_j$ under the morphism $\mathbb{Z}[T_1, T_2, \ldots, T_N] \rightarrow K_i$ has height at most $C.$ We can then form the ring map \[\mathbb{Z}[T_1, T_2, \ldots, T_N] \rightarrow \prod\limits_{i \in I} K_i,\] whose image lies in the set of sequences whose heights are bounded. We may then further quotient to get a morphism $\mathbb{Z}[T_1, T_2, \ldots, T_N] \rightarrow K_{\omega}.$ Finally, since the image of the ideal cutting out $A$ vanishes in each $K_i,$ it vanishes in $K_{\omega},$ so this induces a morphism $A \rightarrow K_{\omega},$ and thus a point $\operatorname{Spec} K_{\omega} \rightarrow \operatorname{Spec} A.$
\par 
In our situation, we may cover $\mathbb{P}^N$ with $N+1$ distinct affine opens $\operatorname{Spec} A_j \simeq \mathbb{A}^N_{\mathbb{Z}}.$ Then there exists $j$ such that $I_j = \{i \mid x_i \in \operatorname{Spec} A_j\}$ lies in $\omega.$ We may then pass to this subsequence and deal only with points landing in $\operatorname{Spec} A_j,$ owing to the existence of the map $\prod\limits_{i \in I_j} K_i \rightarrow K_{\omega}$ given by extending the tuple $(f_i)_{i \in I_j}$ with zeroes for all $i \in I - I_j.$ This reduces us to the case of the previous paragraph, giving us the desired result.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem: GVF structure given by map from adelic line bundles}
There is a natural bijection between the set of GVF structures on $K$, which we assume are trivial on $k$ if $k$ is a field, and the set of group homomorphisms \[\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(K/k) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\] such that the composition \[\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(K/k) \rightarrow \widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(K/k) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\] sends (Cauchy sequences of) effective divisors to nonnegative numbers.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
To prove this lemma, we first show that there is a natural way to map from $\text{LDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(K)$ to $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(K/k) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}.$ We then show that every positive functional extends uniquely to a group homomorphism $\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(K/k) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ satisfying the condition, and that every such functional on $\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(K/k)$ restricts to a positive functional on $\text{LDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(K/k).$
\par To give the map $\text{LDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(K) \rightarrow \widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(K/k),$ we do casework. Suppose first that $k$ is a field. Then, by \cite[Corollary 8.4]{GVFfoundations}, the quotient of $\text{LDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(K)$ by the lattice ideal generated by $\text{LDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)$ is equal to \[\varinjlim (\operatorname{Div}(X/k) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q})\] ranging over all projective varieties $X$ equipped with isomorphisms $k(X) \simeq K.$ On the other hand, recall from \cite[\S 2.4.3]{yuan2021adelic} that \[\widehat{\operatorname{Div}(K/k)} = \varinjlim \widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(U/k),\] ranging over all quasiprojective varieties $U$ with isomorphisms $k(U) \simeq K,$ where $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(U/k)$ was the completion of $\varinjlim \operatorname{Div}(X, U)$ over projective models $X$ of $U;$ see \cite[\S 2.2.3]{yuan2021adelic} for the definition of $\operatorname{Div}(X, U).$ The morphisms \[\operatorname{Div}(X/k) = \widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(X/k) \rightarrow \widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(K/k) \rightarrow \widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(K/k) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}\] for each projective variety $X/k$ with $k(X) \simeq K$ then gives us our desired map $\text{LDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(K) \rightarrow \widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(K/k) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}.$
\par In the case where $k = \mathbb{Z},$ we instead consider the set $\text{ADiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(K)$ from \cite{sza23}, which we may do by \cite[Theorem 8.1]{GVFfoundations}. From the definition, this is given as the direct limit of arithmetic divisors $\text{ADiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{X}),$ which is just $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{X})$ as defined in \cite[\S 2.2]{yuan2021adelic}. We can now map $\text{ADiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(K)$ into $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(K/k) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q},$ since $\mathcal{X}$ is a quasiprojective variety with function field $K.$ Notice that in both of these cases that our map sends principal lattice divisors to principal divisors and positive ones to effective divisors.
\par We now turn to showing that positive functionals on $\text{LDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(K)$ extend to positive functionals on $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(K/k) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}.$ Note in the arithmetic case that, by \cite[Proposition 3.1.16]{sza23}, positive functionals extend uniquely to positive functionals from $\text{ADiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(K)$ (where positivity for $\text{ADiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(K)$ refers to the effective cone). In particular, in either case, we have positive functionals $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X/k) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ for projective $X/k$ which are compatible with the morphisms induced by functoriality.
\par This means that for every quasi-projective variety $\mathcal{U}$ with function field $K,$ we get a positive functional $l:\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(\mathcal{U})_{\text{mod}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ (that is, one preserving the order given in \cite[\S 2.4.1]{yuan2021adelic}) using the projection maps $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{U}) \rightarrow \widehat{\operatorname{Div}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{X})$ and then taking colimits. Furthermore, these are also compatible with the morphisms arising from functoriality, since these projection maps also commute with the pullback morphisms arising from these diagrams:
\[\begin{tikzcd}
	{\mathcal{U}'} && {\mathcal{X}'} \\
	\\
	{\mathcal{U}} && {\mathcal{X}}
	\arrow[hook, from=1-1, to=1-3]
	\arrow[from=1-1, to=3-1]
	\arrow[from=1-3, to=3-3]
	\arrow[hook, from=3-1, to=3-3]
\end{tikzcd}\]
We now show that this functional uniquely extends to a morphism $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(\mathcal{U}) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}.$ To do this, fix a boundary divisor $\overline{\mathcal{E}_0} \in \widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(\mathcal{X}_0, \mathcal{U}).$ Then, recall that $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(\mathcal{U})$ is defined by completing with respect to the boundary norm with respect to $\overline{\mathcal{E}_0}.$ 
\par However, for every Cauchy sequence of divisors $\mathcal{D}_i,$ note that there is a sequence of positive rational numbers $\varepsilon_i$ converging to zero, such that \[-\epsilon_i \overline{\mathcal{E}_0} \leq \mathcal{D}_i - \mathcal{D}_{i'} \leq \epsilon_i \overline{\mathcal{E}_0}\] for all $i' \geq i \geq 1.$ Applying $l,$ since $l$ is positive, we get that \[-\epsilon_i l(\overline{\mathcal{E}_0}) \leq l(\mathcal{D}_i) - l(\mathcal{D}_{i'}) \leq \epsilon_i l(\overline{\mathcal{E}_0}).\] But this means that the sequence of $l(\mathcal{D}_i)$ is Cauchy in $\mathbb{R},$ so it therefore converges; we define $l$ of this sequence to be that limit. The same argument shows that this is well-defined, and sends Cauchy sequences of effective divisors to nonnegative numbers. 
\par This condition also uniquely pins down $l.$ To check this, notice that the positivity condition applied to the Cauchy sequences $\pm (\mathcal{D}_i - \mathcal{D}_j) + \epsilon_j \overline{\mathcal{E}_0}$ (for fixed $j$) implies that, if we denote by $\mathcal{D}$ the Cauchy sequence in $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(\mathcal{U}),$ \[\pm(l(\mathcal{D}) - l(\mathcal{D}_j)) + \epsilon_j l(\overline{\mathcal{E}_0}) \geq 0,\] or that \[\epsilon_j l(\overline{\mathcal{E}_0}) \geq |l(\mathcal{D}) - l(\mathcal{D}_j)|.\] This implies that $l(\mathcal{D})$ has to be the limit of the $l(\mathcal{D}_j).$
\par The uniqueness and the compatibility of the functionals on $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(\mathcal{U}/k)_{\text{mod}}$ imply the functionals $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(\mathcal{U}/k) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ are compatible, so we get a morphism $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(K/k) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ (and thus one from $\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(K/k) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$). Finally, this is trivial on principal divisors by assumption, so this actually induces our desired morphism $\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(K/k) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ (and using \cite[Proposition 2.5.1]{yuan2021adelic}).
\par For the other direction, given our morphism $\widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(K/k) \rightarrow \mathbb{R},$ we get a functional on lattice divisors by \[\text{LDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(K) \rightarrow \varinjlim (\widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(X/k) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}) \rightarrow \widehat{\operatorname{Div}}(K/k) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} \rightarrow \widehat{\operatorname{Pic}}(K/k) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}.\] This sends principal divisors to zero, and the positivity condition implies, in particular, that positive lattice divisors are sent to nonnegative numbers, which finishes the proof of the lemma.

\label{YuanZhangChenMoriwai1}
The data of the metrics $\{| \cdot |_{\omega}\}_{\omega \in \Omega}$ gives $L$ the structure of a Chen--Moriwaki adelic line bundle on $X/K$.

First by \cite[Remark 3.9]{DHS24}, we know that if $\overline{L}$ is a lattice adelic line bundle then its analytification is a CM adelic line bundle; see \cite[Definition 3.8]{DHS24} for the definition of a lattice adelic line bundle. Then $\overline{N} = \pi^* \overline{O(D)}$ is an adelic line bundle and has a Green function $g$, with logarithmic poles on $\pi^*D$, satisfying $g(x) = h(\pi(x))$, where $h$ is the Green function for $\overline{O(D)}.$

As $\overline{O(D)}$ is a lattice adelic line bundle, it an integrable CM adelic line bundle on $K$ itself by \cite[Lemma 3.7 and Remark 3.9]{DHS24}. We hence obtain that the metrics of $\overline{\mathcal{L}}$ is dominated, since the metrics for $\overline{\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}_n}$ are. Measurability follows from the fact that pointwise limit of measurable family of metrics are still measurable by \cite[Proposition 6.1.29]{CM20}, and hence $\overline{\mathcal{L}}$ induces a CM adelic line bundle as desired.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{EqualityMeasures1}
Let $K$ be a countable GVF and $X$ a projective variety over $K$. View $K$ as an adelic curve and let $\overline{L},\overline{M}$ be two nef semipositive adelic line bundles which are geometrically semiample and big. Assume that there exists $ a > 0$ such that $a \overline{L} - \overline{M}$ is nef, and that
$$h_{\overline{L}}(X) = h_{\overline{M}}(X) = 0.$$
Then there exists a countable GVF extension $K'/K$, such that after viewing $K' = (K', (\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)$ as an adelic curve, we have 
$$\frac{c_1(\overline{L})_{v}^{\dim X}}{\deg_L(X)} = \frac{c_1(\overline{M})_{v}^{\dim X}}{\deg_M(X)}$$
for all $v \in \Omega$ outside a set of measure zero.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By Proposition \ref{SmallPointsExistence1}, we find a countable extension $K'/K$, algebraically closed, such that $X(K')$ has a generic sequence $(x_n)$ of small points for $\overline{L}$. Since $a \overline{L} - \overline{M}$ is nef, we know that $a h_{\overline{L}} \geq h_{\overline{M}}.$ Hence the same sequence is also small for $\overline{M}$. 
\par 
Viewing $x_n$ as a sequence of closed subschemes of $X/K'$, the statement of \cite[Theorem F, \S 1.2.4]{chen2024positivity} tells us that these points equidistribute to a global family of Borel measures given by the Monge-Amp\`ere measures (see \cite[Definition 7.5.3]{chen2024positivity}). However, this convergence of measures is over the global adelic space $X_{K'}^{\operatorname{an}}$ which is the disjoint union of $X_{v}^{\operatorname{an}}$ for $v \in \Omega$. To obtain the equidistribution on an individual fiber $X_{\omega}^{\operatorname{an}}$, we apply \cite[Proposition 7.7.1]{chen2024positivity}, which tells us that, for all places $v$ on our adelic curve $K'$ outside of a set of measure zero, the small height points $x_n$ equidistribute to the Monge-Amp\`ere measure $\frac{c_1(\overline{L})_v^{\dim X}}{\deg_L(X)}$, where we normalize it so that it is a probability measure. As $(x_n)$ is a generic sequence of small points for $\overline{M}$ too, we obtain the equality
$$\frac{c_1(\overline{L})_v^{\dim X}}{\deg_L(X)} = \frac{c_1(\overline{M})_v^{\dim X}}{\deg_M(X)}$$
for $v \in \Omega$ outside a set of measure zero, as desired.

The only part of the proof of this lemma which doesn't immediately generalize is the claim, which is part of \cite[Proposition 2.11]{yamaki2013geometric}, that $X/G_X$ being special holds if and only if $X$ is. Furthermore, to prove that claim along the lines of Yamaki's proof, it suffices to check the following if $\phi: A \rightarrow B$ is a homomorphism of abelian varieties, then the map sends points of canonical height zero on $A$ to points of canonical height zero on $B,$ and furthermore that this restricted map is surjective. For the first fact, this follows since for any ample symmetric line bundles $L, M$ on $A, B,$ respectively, there is some integer $n > 0$ such that $L^{\otimes n} \otimes \phi^*M^{-1}$ is ample, meaning that $n \hat{h}_L(x) \geq \hat{h}_M(\phi(x)) \geq 0$ for all $x \in A;$ in particular, if $x$ is canonical height zero, $\phi(x)$ is too.

\label{SmallBasis1}
Let $(K, (\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \nu),\phi)$ be a non-archimedean adelic curve and let $A/K$ be an abelian variety. Assume that $A$ has almost everywhere good reduction and let $L$ be a symmetric ample line bundle and endow $L$ with its canonical adelic metric $\overline{L}$. Then if $H^0(A,L)$ has a basis $\{s_1,\ldots,s_N\}$ with 
\begin{equation} \label{eq: SmallAverage1}
\int_{\Omega} \log \sup |s_i|_v d \nu(v) \leq 0
\end{equation}
for all $i$, we must have $h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A) = 0$.

<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
By Proposition \ref{GoodReductionKeyFormula1}, viewing $\det(\pi_* \overline{L})$ as a Chen-Moriwaki adelic line bundle \cite[\S 4.1.1.7]{CM20}, we have
$$24 \widehat{\deg} \det(\pi_*L) = (-12 d) \widehat{\deg}(e^*\overline{\omega_A}),$$ since we have an isometry of adelic line bundles over almost every place.
As $\widehat{\deg}(e^*\overline{ \omega_A}) = h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A)$ (see for example \cite[Section 5.5]{yuan2021adelic}), we have
$$\widehat{\deg} \det(\pi_*L) = -\frac{d}{2} h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A).$$
Now using our basis of sections $s_1,\ldots,s_N$, we obtain a section $s_1\wedge \cdots \wedge s_N$ of $\det(\pi_*L)$ which satisfies 
$$|s_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge s_N|_v \leq \prod_{i=1}^{N} \sup |s_i|_v$$
for all places $v$. Hence 
$$\int_{\Omega} \log |s_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge s_N|_v^{-1} d \nu(v) \geq -\int_{\Omega} \left(\sum_{i=1}^{N} \log \sup |s_i|_v \right) d \nu(v) \geq 0.$$
We therefore conclude that 
$$-\frac{d}{2} h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A) \geq 0 \implies h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A) = 0$$
as desired (since the Faltings height is nonnegative).

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{GVFDivisor3}
Assume that $A/K$ has almost everywhere good reduction. Then Theorem \ref{GVFDivisor1} is true.

We may assume that $K$ is algebraically closed. As in the proof of Theorem \ref{GVFDivisor2}, we may assume that $\operatorname{Stab}(D)$ is finite. Then $D$ is an ample divisor. Now by replacing $D$ with $D + [-1]^*D$, we may assume that $D$ is symmetric, at the cost of losing the integrality of $D$. $$\widehat{h}_{A,L}(D) = \widehat{h}_{A,L}(A) + \int_{\Omega} \left(\int_{A^{\operatorname{an}}_v} \log |s(x)|_v d \mu_{A,v} \right) d \nu(v) = \int_{\Omega} \log \sup_{x \in A^{\operatorname{an}}_v} |s(x)|_v d\nu(v),$$

$$\int_{\Omega} \log \sup |s(x)|_v d \nu(v) = 0$$
and hence $s$ has small average. We now aim to construct many small sections of the adelic line bundle $\overline{L}^2$. This step appears in Yamaki's proof (\cite{Yam17}). \begin{proposition} \label{DivisorSupport1}
There exists $q_0,\ldots,q_g \in A[\ell^{\infty}]$ such that $D_{q_0} \cap \cdots \cap D_{q_g} = \emptyset$. 
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Observe that given $x \in A(K)$, the set of $q \in A(K)$ such that $x \in D_q$ forms a proper subvariety. We now construct inductively $q_0,\ldots,q_g$ such that for $0 \leq j \leq g,$ the irreducible components $\cap_{i=0}^{j} D_{q_i}$ all have codimension $j.$ Given $D_{q_0},\ldots,D_{q_j}$, we choose points $x_1,\ldots,x_N$ from each of the irreducible components of $\cap_{i=0}^{j} D_{q_i}$. Since $A[\ell^{\infty}]$ is Zariski dense in $A$, we may find $q_{j+1} \in A[\ell^{\infty}]$ such that $D_{q_{j+1}}$ does not contain any of the $x_i$'s. Then $D_{q_{g+1}}$ intersects any irreducible component of $\cap_{i=0}^{j} D_{q_i}$ properly, and hence the irreducible components of $\cap_{i=0}^{j+1} D_{q_i}$ has codimension $j+1$ as desired, finishing the induction. 
\end{proof}

Proposition \ref{DivisorSupport1} gives us sections $s_0,\ldots,s_g$ of $\overline{L}^2$ which all have small average, such that they form a basepoint free set, and thus we obtain a morphism $f: A \rightarrow \mathbb{P}^g_K$ given by the sections $s_0, s_1, \ldots, s_g.$ We now want to produce enough sections to obtain a closed embedding, not just basepoint freeness. 

\begin{proposition} \label{DivisorSupport2}
There exists finitely many torsion points $x_1,\ldots,x_m \in  A[\ell^{\infty}](K)$ such that $\prod_{i=1}^{m} f_{x_i} : A \to (\mathbb{P}^g_{K})^m$ factors into $A \to B \xhookrightarrow{} (\mathbb{P}^g_{K})^m$, where $B$ is an abelian variety over $K$, $\pi: A \to B$ is an isogeny and $f_x$ is given by $f \circ t_x: A \rightarrow \mathbb{P}^g.$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We have to be careful in this proposition as $K$ may be of positive characteristic and hence an injection on the level of points need not imply a closed embedding due to inseparability. 
\par 
For each $x \in A(K),$ let $\Gamma_x = (f_x,f_x)^{-1}(\Delta) \subseteq A \times A,$ where $\Delta$ is the diagonal of $\mathbb{P}^g \times \mathbb{P}^g$. Then $\Gamma_x$ is a closed subscheme of $A^2$. We will consider the intersection $\bigcap_{x \in A[\ell^{\infty}](K)} \Gamma_x$ which is a closed subscheme. We define 
$$H = \{ a \in A \mid f \circ T_a = f \}.$$
This is a closed subscheme of $A$, where given an affine scheme $S$ we have 
$$H(T) = \{ a \in A(S) \mid f_S \circ T_a = f_S\}.$$
Note that $H$ must factor through $T_x^{-1}(f^{-1}(f(x)))$ for all $x \in A(K),$ and hence is a finite group scheme, since the fiber of $f$ over some point is finite (for instance by using the construction in Proposition \ref{DivisorSupport2}). We define $B = A/H$ so that we have an isogeny $\pi: A \to B$. 

\begin{lemma} \label{DivisorSupport3}
View $A \times_B A$ as a closed subscheme of $A \times A$. We have $R = A \times_B A$. 
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $S$ be an affine $K$-scheme. Recall that $R = \cap_{x \in A[\ell^{\infty}](K)} \Gamma_x$, where 
$$\Gamma_x(S) = \{(a,b) \in A(S)^2 \mid f_S(a+x_S) = f_S(b+x_S)\}.$$
By definition it is clear that $(A \times_B A)(S) \subseteq \Gamma_x(S)$ for any $x$ and so we have $A \times_B A \subseteq R$. 
\par 
For the reverse inclusion, let $(a,b) \in R(S)$. Set $h = b-a \in A(S)$. Then by definition, we have 
$$(f_S \circ T_h)(x_S+a) = f_S(x_S+a) \text{ for all } x \in A[\ell^{\infty}](K)$$
where $x_S$ denotes the base change to $S$. We claim that $\{x_S + a \}$ as $x$ ranges over $A[\ell^{\infty}](K)$ forms a scheme-theoretic dense set. If this were true, then $f_S \circ h = f_S$ and hence we get the reverse inclusion $R \subseteq A \times_B A$, giving us equality $R = A \times_B A$ as desired. 
\par It now suffices to prove that $\{x_T\}$ is scheme-theoretically dense in $A_T$. But $A[\ell^{\infty}](K)$ is Zariski dense in $A(K)$ and hence is scheme-theoretically dense in $A$ as $A$ is reduced. Then scheme-theoretic density in $A_T$ follows from \cite[Lemma IX.4.4]{SGA3}. 
\end{proof}

Using Lemma \ref{DivisorSupport3}, by Noetherianness we may find finitely many $x_1,\ldots,x_m \in A[\ell^{\infty}](K)$ such that $A \times_B A = \cap_{i=1}^{m} \Gamma_{x_i}$. If we let $f_i$ denote the morphism $f_{x_i}$, then $\prod_{i=1}^{m} f_i: A \to (\mathbb{P}^g)^{m}$ factors through $A \to B \to (\mathbb{P}^g)^m$. We claim that $B \to (\mathbb{P}^g)^{m}$ has to be a closed immersion. By \cite[\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04XV}{Tag 04XV}]{stacks-project}, as $B$ is projective, it suffices to show that it is a monomorphism. This can be checked by using test schemes $T$, in which case it is straightforward using the fact that $A \times_B A = \cap_{i=1}^{m} \Gamma_{x_i}$, finishing the proof of our Proposition.
\end{proof}

Using the Segre embedding, we may embed $(\mathbb{P}^g)^m$ into $\mathbb{P}^{(g+1)^m-1}$. Let $M$ be the pullback of $O(1)$ to $B$, so that $\pi^* M =\otimes_{i=1}^{m} t_x^* L^2$. Let $M' = M \otimes [-1]^*M$, so that $M'$ is symmetric. Then 
$$\pi^* M' \simeq \bigotimes_{i=1}^{m} \left(t_x^* L^2 \otimes t_{-x}^* L^2 \right) \simeq \bigotimes_{i=1}^{m} L^4 \simeq L^{4m},$$ 
where the second isomorphism is by the theorem of the square. Endowing $M'$ with a canonical adelic metric $\overline{M}$, we obtain that $\pi^* \overline{M}' = \overline{L}^{4m}$. 
\par 
Now using the embedding $B \xhookrightarrow{} (\mathbb{P}^{(g+1)^m-1})$, we obtain a set of sections $\{s_1,\ldots,s_N\}$ for $M$, such that under pullback to $A$ by $\prod_{i=1}^{m} f_i$, they have small average for the adelic line bundle $\otimes_{i=1}^{m} t_x^* \overline{L}^2$. Applying $[-1]^*$ gives us  a corresponding set of sections $\{s'_1,\ldots,s'_N\}$ for ${-1}^*M$ whose pullback to $A$ have small average for $\otimes_{i=1}^{m} t^*_{-x} \overline{L}^2$. 
\par Now observe that products of small average sections still have small average. Hence $\{s_i \otimes s'_j\}$ give us a set of small average sections for $\overline{M}'$. Furthermore, they induce a closed immersion of $B$ to projective space, say $\mathbb{P}^{N}$. Then by ampleness, we know that for some large enough $n$, we have a surjection $H^0(\mathbb{P}^N,O(n)) \to H^0(B,(M')^n)$. On the other hand, taking products of $\{s_i \otimes s'_j\}$ gives us a basis of $H^0(\mathbb{P}^N,O(n))$ with small average, and hence $(\overline{M}')^n$ is a symmetric adelic line bundle on $B$ with a basis of small average sections. Applying Proposition \ref{SmallBasis1} gives us that $h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(B) = 0$. This implies that $B$ is defined over the height zero subfield, contradicting the fact that $A$ is traceless as desired.

<!-- proposition-16: body_md -->
\label{HeightEquidistribution2}
Let $X$ be a projective variety over $K$ and $\overline{L}, \overline{M}$ two nef line bundles such that $L$ and $M$ are both geometrically semiample and big. Assume there exists some $a > 0$ such that $a \overline{L} - \overline{M}$ is nef. Then $h_{\overline{L}}(X) = 0$ implies that for any geometrically integral effective divisor $D$ of $X$, we have 
$$\frac{ h_{\overline{L}}(D)}{\deg_L(X)} = \frac{h_{\overline{M}}(D)}{\deg_M(X)}.$$
where $h_{\overline{L}}, h_{\overline{M}}$ are the un-normalized heights (that is, $h_{\overline{L}}(D) = \widehat{\deg}(\overline{L}^d|_D)$).
<!-- end proposition-16 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Let $d = \dim X$. We first check that all mixed intersections between $\overline{L}$ and $\overline{M}$ vanish on $X.$ Indeed since $a \overline{L} - \overline{M}$ is nef, it follows from $\overline{L}^{d+1} = 0$ that $(\overline{L} + \overline{M})^{d +1} = 0$ too. Expanding out, as each term is non-negative by nefness, it follows that each mixed intersection must be zero. 
\par 
We now prove that for any integrable adelic line bundle $\overline{N}$, we have 
\begin{equation} \label{eq: DivisorDegree1}
\frac{\overline{L}^d \cdot \overline{N}}{\deg_L(X)} = \frac{\overline{M}^d \cdot \overline{N}}{\deg_M(X)}.
\end{equation}
Let $\overline{L}_t = (1-t) \overline{L} + t \overline{M}$ and let 
$$F(t) = \frac{\overline{L}_t^d \cdot \overline{N}}{L_t^d}.$$ 
Let $\overline{\Delta} = \overline{M} - \overline{L}$ so that $\Delta = M - L$. We set 
$$c_t = \frac{L_t^{d-1} \cdot \Delta}{L_t^d}$$
so that if $\overline{D}_t = \overline{\Delta} - c_t \overline{L}_t$, by definition we have $D_t \cdot L_t^{d-1} = 0$. Now given our $\overline{N}$, we may assume that $L_t^{d-1} \cdot N = 0$ by adding a multiple of $\overline{L}$ to $\overline{N}$, which won't change the quantity $\overline{L}^d \cdot \overline{N}$ and $\overline{M}^d \cdot \overline{N}$.
\par 
Consider the quadratic form induced on the space of integrable adelic line bundles $\overline{N}$, which satisfies $N \cdot L_t^{d-1}= 0$, by defining
$$\langle \overline{N}_1, \overline{N}_2 \rangle_t := \overline{L}_t^{d-1} \cdot \overline{N}_1 \cdot \overline{N}_2.$$
Since $L_t$ is big and $\overline{L}_t$ is nef and semi-positive, by Proposition \ref{HodgeIndex1} it follows that $\langle \bullet , \bullet \rangle_t$ is negative semi-definite. Observe that $\langle \overline{D}_t, \overline{D}_t \rangle_t = 0,$ since $\overline{D}_t$ is a linear combination of $\overline{L}, \overline{M},$ and all of the arithmetic intersections of these adelic line bundles are zero. Therefore, by negative semi-definiteness, it follows that $\langle \overline{D}_t, \overline{N} \rangle_t = 0$. Expanding out, this gives us $$\overline{L}_t^{d-1} \cdot \overline{\Delta} \cdot \overline{N} = c_t \overline{L}_t^d \cdot \overline{N}.$$
We now set $A(t) = \overline{L}_t^{d} \cdot \overline{N}$ and $v(t) = L_t^{d}$. We may compute that 
$$A'(t) = d \overline{L}_t^{d-1} \overline{\Delta} \cdot \overline{N} = dc_t \overline{L}_t^d \cdot \overline{N} = d c_t A(t).$$
Similarly we have $v'(t) = d c_t v(t)$. Hence $A'(t)v(t) = A(t)v'(t)$ for all $t,$ and so 
$$\frac{d}{dt} \left(\frac{A(t)}{v(t)} \right) = 0.$$
Evaluating at $t =0$ and $1$ gives us (\ref{eq: DivisorDegree1}) as desired.
\par 
We now replace $X$ with a blowup, so that $D$ is a Cartier divisor. Then $L$ and $M$ are still geometrically semiample and big. Let $N = O(D)$ and give $\overline{N}$ an adelic metric so that it is integrable. We thus have 
$$
\frac{\overline{L}^d \cdot \overline{N}}{\deg_L(X)} = \frac{\overline{M}^d \cdot \overline{N}}{\deg_M(X)}.$$
On the other hand, if $s$ is the canonical section of $O(D)$, we have the formula 
\begin{equation} \label{eq: DivisorHeight1}
h_L(D) = \widehat{\deg}(\overline{L}^d \vert_D) = \overline{L}^d \cdot \overline{N} + \int_{\Omega_K} \left(\int_{X_v^{\operatorname{an}}} \log |s|_v d c_1(\overline{L})^d_v \right)d \nu(v).
\end{equation}
and similarly for $M$. Here, we let $c_1(\overline{L})^d_v$ be the un-normalized measure, i.e. it has total mass $\deg_L(X)$. We now apply Proposition \ref{EqualityMeasures1}, which holds for geometrically semiample and big line bundles. We hence obtain that outside a set of measure zero, we have $\frac{c_1(\overline{L})_v^d}{\deg_L(X)} = \frac{c_1(\overline{M})_v^d}{\deg_{M}(X)}$. Combining with (\ref{eq: DivisorHeight1}) gives us $\frac{h_L(D)}{\deg_L(X)} = \frac{h_M(D)}{\deg_M(X)}$ as desired.

We proceed by induction on $\dim D$. First, observe that the image of $D$ to any $V$ must be either a point or the entire $V$. Indeed otherwise the image of $D$ is a positive dimensional subvariety of $V$ with canonical height zero, just as in the proof of Proposition \ref{Induction1}. By minimality of $V$, the image of $D$ must be a coset, say $x+B$. By Proposition \ref{Induction2}, we know that $\dim A \geq 2 \dim V$. This implies that 
$$\dim(A/B) = \dim A - \dim B \geq 2 \dim V - \dim B > 2 \dim V - \dim V = \dim V.$$
In particular, we cannot have $q(V) = A/B$ which contradicts Proposition \ref{Induction1}. 
\par 
Now let's assume that $\pi: D \to V$ is surjective where $\pi$ is the first projection. We let $\nu: V' \to V$ be the normalization of $V$ inside $K(V)$ and endow $K(V)$ with the polarized GVF structure $(K(V),  \nu^*\overline{L},\ldots,\nu^*\overline{L})$. As all prime divisors of $V$ have canonical height $> 0$ by assumption of minimality, it follows by the proof of Lemma \ref{HeightZeroSubfield1} that $A_{K(V)}$ remains traceless over the height zero subfield. 
\par 
Now as in the proof of Proposition \ref{Induction2}, it follows that $D_{K(V)}$ has height zero and lies inside $V_{K(V)}^{r-1}$. But $V_{K(V)}$ and $A_{K(V)}$ is still a minimal counterexample in terms of $(\dim V, \dim A)$ whereas $\dim D_{K(V)} = \dim D - \dim V$. Hence either $\dim D - \dim V = 0,$ or we can apply induction. Either way, we find that 
$$\gcd(\dim D - \dim V ,\dim V) > 1 \implies \gcd(\dim D,\dim V) > 1$$
as desired.

\label{GVFBogomolov2}
Let $K$ be a countable non-archimedean GVF and let $k$ be the height zero subfield. Assume both $K$ and $k$ are algebraically closed. Then if $V \subseteq A$ has canonical height zero, $V$ must be of the form $x + B + \operatorname{Tr}(W_K)$ where $x$ is a point of height zero, $B$ is an abelian subvariety, and $W \subseteq B$ a subvariety defined over $k_0$.

<!-- proposition-23: body_md -->
\label{IsogenyBound1}
There exists an isogeny $\pi: A \to B$, with $\ker(\pi) \subseteq A[p^{2g}]$, so that $D_B$ is minimal.
<!-- end proposition-23 -->
<!-- proof-32: body_md -->
Let $D_A = A[p^{\infty}]$. By \cite[Theorem 1.5]{LNV13}, we know that $q_{D_A} \leq \lfloor \nu(c) \rfloor$, where $\nu: [0,c+d] \to \mathbb{R}$ is the Newton polygon for $D_A$, with $d$ denoting the dimension and $c$ the codimension, where $c+d$ adds up to the height of $D_A$ which is $2g$. As noted by the discussion after \cite[Theorem 1.2]{LNV13}, we have 
$$\nu(c) \leq \frac{cd}{c+d} \leq \frac{(c+d)^2}{(c+d)} = 2g.$$
Hence we can find $\pi: D_A \to D_0$, with $D_0$ minimal and $\ker(\pi) \subseteq D_A[p^{2g}]$. Let $H = \ker(\pi)$. Then taking $B = A/\ker(\pi)$ gives us an isogenous abelian variety with $D_B \simeq D_0$ which is minimal as desired.

<!-- proposition-24: body_md -->
\label{MinimalTrace1}
Let $A/K$ be an abelian variety with $D_A$ minimal, and assume there exists $B/k$ so that there is an isogeny $\pi: B_K \to A$. Then $A$ itself is defined over $k$.
<!-- end proposition-24 -->
<!-- proof-33: body_md -->
Let $H = \ker(\pi)$, which is a finite flat group subscheme over $K$. We want to prove that $H$ descends to $H'$ over $k$, in which then $A \simeq (B/H')_K$ and hence $A$ is constant. We split $H$ to its $p$-part and $p$-primary part, giving us a decomposition $H = H_p \times H_0$, where $H_p$ is a finite flat $p$-group scheme and $H_0$ is finite and etale. Then $H_0$ automatically descends to $k$ and by quotienting out $H_0$, we may assume that $\pi$ is a $p$-primary isogeny.
\par 
We first note that any minimal $p$-divisible group $D$ over $K$, must have already been defined over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$. Indeed, choose a $p$-divisible group $D'$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ with the same Newton polygon as $D$. After base changing to $k$, the group $D'_K$ lies in the same isogeny class as $D$. But both $D'_K$ and $D$ are minimal \cite[Corollary 4.9]{LNV13} and hence must be isomorphic to each other.     
\par 
We now choose $D'_A$ over $k$ so that $D'_{A,K} \simeq D_A$. Let $D_B$ be the $p$-divisible group of $B$ over $k$. The isogeny $\pi: B_K \to A$ gives us an isogeny $\pi_D: D'_{B,K} \to D'_{A,K}$. By \cite[Lemma 2.8]{LNV13}, 
$$\operatorname{Hom}(D_B,D'_A) \simeq \operatorname{Hom}(D_{B,K}, D'_{A,K}),$$
the isogeny $\pi$ descends to $\pi'_D: D_B \to D'_A$ and hence $\ker(\pi_D)$ also descends to $\ker(\pi'_D)$ which is defined over $k$. But $\ker(\pi) = \ker(\pi_D)$ and so $\ker(\pi)$ descends over $k$ as desired.

<!-- theorem-12: body_md -->
\label{NewGapPrinciple1}
Let $K = k(B)$ be a function field for a curve of characteristic $p$. Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $K$ and $L$ a symmetric ample line bundle. Let $V \subseteq A_{\overline{K}}$ be a subvariety such that $\operatorname{Stab}(V)$ is finite and $V-V$ generates $A$. Then there exists $c = c(\dim A, \deg_L(V)) > 0$ and $M = M(\dim A, \deg_L(V))$, such that 
$$\widehat{h}_{A,L}(V) \geq c h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A_0)$$
where $A_0$ is an abelian variety with an isogeny $\pi: A \to A_0$ of degree at most $M$.
<!-- end theorem-12 -->
<!-- proof-35: body_md -->
Let $\deg_L(V) = d$. Then as $V-V$ generates $A$, it follows from \cite[Lemma 2.5]{GGK21} that $\deg_L(A)$ is bounded in terms of $d$. Applying \cite[Lemma 2.2(iv)]{GGK21} allows us to assume that $(A,L)$ is a principal polarization by passing to an isogeneous abelian variety (whose degree is bounded by the lemma).
\par Now, fix some $n \geq 3$ not divisible by $p$. Consider the Hilbert scheme of the universal abelian variety $\mathfrak{A}/\mathcal{A}_{g,n}.$ Since $\deg_L(V)$ is bounded, we may restrict to a finite type scheme $\mathbb{H}$ for which all such $(A,V)$'s appear as a point on $\mathbb{H}/\mathcal{A}_{g,n}$. We further pass to a Zariski open $\mathbb{H}^{\circ}$ that parametrizes geometrically integral subvarieties $V$, with $\operatorname{Stab}(V)$ finite and $V-V$ generating $A$, and we give $\mathbb{H}^{\circ}$ the reduced scheme structure. 
\par Now let $T \subseteq \mathbb{H}^{\circ}$ be an irreducible component. Then we have a principally polarized abelian scheme $\mathcal{A}$ over $T$ and a flat family of geometrically integral subvarieties $\mathcal{V} \subseteq \mathcal{A}$. We may find a symmetric line bundle $\mathcal{L}$ on $\mathcal{A}$ inducing twice the polarization and then endow $\mathcal{L}$ with a canonical adelic metric $\overline{\mathcal{L}}$. Since $\mathcal{V}$ is integral, we can form the Deligne pairing $$\overline{M} = \langle \overline{\mathcal{L}},\ldots, \overline{\mathcal{L}} \rangle_{\mathcal{V}/\mathbb{H}^{\circ}}.$$ Then for any GVF field $K$, a GVF-valued point $s \in \mathbb{H}^{\circ}(K)$ corresponding to the pair $(A, V)$ satisfies $$h_{\overline{M}}(s) = a \widehat{h}_{A,L}(V)$$ for some fixed constant $a > 0$ depending only on $\deg_L(V).$ 
\par Now assume for the sake of contradiction that our theorem is false. By Proposition \ref{IsogenyBound1}, we may assume $A$ has minimal $p$-divisible group by taking an isogeny of degree dividing $p^{2g}$ from the original $A$ to an abelian variety $A'$ with minimal $p$-divisible group and replacing $V$ with its image in $A'$. 
\par Hence we may find a sequence of abelian varieties $A_n/K_n$, with minimal $p$-divisible group, and subvarieties $V_n$ with $(A_n,V_n)$ lying in $T$, such that 
 $$\widehat{h}_{A_n,L}(V_n) < \frac{1}{n} h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A_n).$$
 Since there are only finitely many Newton polygons, we may assume that $A_m \in \mathcal{A}_{g,n}^{\nu,\min}(K_n)$ for the same $\nu$. Now cover $\mathcal{A}_{g,n}$ with finitely many affine opens $U_i$. We may assume all the $A_m$'s lie in the same open, say $U_1$. Using $\mathcal{L}^4$, we may embed $\mathfrak{A}_{U_i} \xhookrightarrow{} \mathbb{P}^N$. Now by \cite[Theorem 1.1]{MY25}, combining with Zhang's fundamental inequality, there exists some $C = C(\dim A, \deg_L(V))> 0$ such that 
 \begin{equation} \label{eq: Embedding1}
 \widehat{h}(\iota(V_n)) \leq C h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A_n)
 \end{equation}
 where $\widehat{h}(\iota(V))$ denotes the Weil height of $\iota(V)$ inside $\mathbb{P}^N$. 
 \par 
 Fix an ample line bundle $\overline{M}$ on $T$. We degenerate using $\varepsilon_n = h_{\overline{M}}(x_n   )$, where we may assume that $h_{\overline{M}}(x_n) > 0$ where $x_n = [(A_n,V_n)]$, as otherwise $h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A_n) = 0$. Applying Proposition \ref{GVFConstruction1} to the sequence $([A_n,V_n],K_n[\varepsilon_n])$, we obtain an ultrafilter limit $x_{\omega} = [A_{\omega},V_{\omega}]$ over $K_{\omega}$ that lies in $T(K_{\omega})$. Then by definition, we have $h_{\overline{M}}(x_{\omega}) = 1 > 0$.  
 \par 
 We first claim that $h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A_{\omega}) > 0$. Indeed let's assume that $h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A_{\omega}) = 0$. Then by (\ref{eq: Embedding1}), we also have $\widehat{h}(\iota(V_{\omega})) = 0$. Let $k_{\omega}$ be the height zero subfield of $K_{\omega}$. Then $\widehat{h}(\iota(V_{\omega})) = 0$ and $h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A_{\omega}) = 0$ implies that both $A_{\omega}$ and $V_{\omega}$ are defined over $\overline{k_{\omega}}$. Hence $x_{\omega} \in T(\overline{k_{\omega}})$, but this contradicts $h_{\overline{M}}(x_{\omega}) > 0$. Hence $h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A_{\omega}) > 0$.
 \par 
 Now as each $A_n$ lie in $\mathcal{A}_{g,3}^{\nu,\min}$, it follows that $A_{\omega}$ also does. Now we have $\widehat{h}_{A_{\omega},L}(V_{\omega}) = 0$. Using Theorem \ref{GVFBogomolov2}, it follows that 
$$V_{\omega} = a + B_{\omega} + \operatorname{Tr}(W_{K_{\omega}})$$
where $B_{\omega}$ is an abelian subvariety of $A_{\omega}$, and $W$ is a subvariety of $\operatorname{Tr}_{\overline{K_{\omega}}/\overline{k_{\omega}}}(A_{\omega})$ where $k_{\omega}$ is the height zero subfield of $K_{\omega}$.  As $\operatorname{Stab}(V_{\omega})$ is finite, $B$ must be zero. Then as $V-V$ generates $A$, it follows that the image of $W_{K_{\omega}} - W_{K_{\omega}}$ generates $A$ and hence we must have $\operatorname{Tr}_{\overline{K_{\omega}}/\overline{k_{\omega}}}(A_{\omega})$ being full dimensional.
\par 
We now apply Proposition \ref{MinimalTrace1}. As $A_{\omega}$ has minimal $p$-divisible subgroup, it follows that $A_{\omega}$ is defined over $\overline{k_{\omega}}$. Then $h_{\operatorname{Fal}}(A_{\omega}) = 0$, which is a contradiction. This finishes our proof, where we obtain uniform constants that may depend on the characteristic of $k$.
\par 
Finally for sufficiently large positive characteristic, as already noted by Hultberg \cite[Theorem 4]{Hul26}, the Bogomolov conjecture for GVFs in characteristic zero gives a uniform height gap for all sufficiently large positive characteristic. This removes the dependence on the characteristic $p$.
