<!-- thma-1: body_md -->
Let $M$ be a closed, connected, oriented $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-manifold of dimension $m=2n$ or $2n-1$, with $n\ge2$ and $m\ge4$, such that $\pi_1(M)$ is finite or infinite cyclic and (T) holds. Then the following diagram of exact sequences commutes, except that the two maps $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)\to\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)$ agree only up to inversion:
\vspace{.5cm}
$$
\begin{matrix}
\xymatrix@!C@C-35pt{ \Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(M) \ar[dr] \ar@/^2pc/[rr] &&
\widetilde \mathcal H (M) \ar[dr] \ar@/^2pc/[rr] &&
\operatorname{Aut}_{\bullet}(P)\ar[dr]^{\beta} \\
&\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P) \ar[dr] \ar[ur] &&
\operatorname{Aut}_{\bullet}(M)  \ar[dr]^{\alpha}\ar[ur] &&\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(P)\\
\pi_1(\mathcal E_{\bullet} (P)) \ar[ur] \ar@/_2pc/[rr] && \widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P, M)
\ar[ur]^\gamma \ar@/_2pc/[rr]&& \widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(M)\ar[ur] }
\end{matrix}
$$
\vspace{1cm}

\noindent In particular there is an exact sequence of pointed sets
\[
  \pi_1(\mathcal E_{\bullet} (P))\to\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P,M)\xrightarrow{\ \gamma\ }\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(P),
\]
and every element of the image of $\pi$ preserves the class $c_*[M]\in H_m(P;\mathbb{Z})$.
<!-- end thma-1 -->
<!-- thmb-1: body_md -->
For $M=S^1\times S^{m-1}$ with $m\ge4$ we have $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2$. Including orientation-reversing equivalences, the group of all based self-equivalences is $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3$.
<!-- end thmb-1 -->
<!-- thmc-1: body_md -->
For $M=S^3\times S^3$ there is a split short exact sequence
\[
  0\to(\mathbb{Z}/12)^2\xrightarrow{\ \gamma\ }\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(S^3\times S^3)\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }SL_2(\mathbb{Z})\to0,
\]
in which $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ acts on $(\mathbb{Z}/12)^2$ through its standard action modulo $12$. Hence $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(S^3\times S^3)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/12)^2\rtimes SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$. This group is not a direct product, and its abelianization is $\mathbb{Z}/12$. The relative spin bordism group $\widehat\Omega^{spin}_7(P,M)$ has order $144$ and $\gamma$ is a bijection onto the kernel.
<!-- end thmc-1 -->
<!-- thmd-1: body_md -->
$\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(S^3\times S^4)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/24)\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ is a group of order $192$, and $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(S^4\times S^4)\cong D_4\times\mathbb{Z}/2$ is a group of order $16$, where $D_4$ is the dihedral group of order $8$. In both cases the kernel of $\pi$ is the group of pinch-fold twists, which is computed from the Whitehead products in the homotopy groups of the factors.
<!-- end thmd-1 -->
<!-- thmf-1: body_md -->
For $M=S^p\times S^p$ with $p\ge2$, the kernel of $\pi$ is $Q_p\oplus Q_p$ with $Q_p=\pi_{2p}(S^p)/[\pi_{p+1}(S^p),\iota_p]$, and the image of $\pi$ is the group of isometries $A$ of the intersection form with $ab[\iota_p,\iota_p]=cd[\iota_p,\iota_p]=0$. In particular $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(S^5\times S^5)$ is the theta group $\Gamma_\theta\subset SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$, and $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(S^7\times S^7)$ is an extension of $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ by $(\mathbb{Z}/120)^2$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:no-F}
The $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-structure $\nu$ need not factor through $c$. All bordism groups below are taken with the fixed structure $\nu$ on $M$, and the reference maps to $P$ do not depend on $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-structures. <!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:surgery}
After a sequence of surgeries on embedded spheres in the interior of $V$, the triple $(V,\Phi)$ is replaced by a $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-bordant triple $(V',\Phi')$ rel $\partial V$ with $V'$ connected, $\Phi'|_{\partial V}=\Phi|_{\partial V}$, and such that $\Phi'_*\colon\pi_j(V')\to\pi_j(P)$ is an isomorphism for $j\le\ell_d$. In that case the pair $(V',C)$ is $\ell_d$-connected, so $H_j(V',C;G)=0$ for $j\le\ell_d$ and every abelian group $G$ with trivial action.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $\Phi|_C$ is an isomorphism on $\pi_j$ for $j\le n$ and $\ell_d\le n$, the map $\Phi_*$ is onto on $\pi_j$ for $j\le\ell_d$. We kill $\ker(\Phi_*\colon\pi_j(V)\to\pi_j(P))$ for $j=1,2,\dots,\ell_d$ in turn. For $j=1$ the kernel is normally generated by finitely many elements, because $\pi_1(V)$ is finitely presented and $\pi_1(P)=\pi_1(M)$ is finitely presented. For $j\ge2$ the previous steps make the universal cover of $V$ $(j-1)$-connected, so $\pi_j(V)$ is a finitely generated module over the Noetherian ring $\mathbb{Z}\pi_1(M)$ (here we use that $\pi_1(M)$ is finite or infinite cyclic). Indeed, $P$ is obtained from $M$ by attaching cells of dimension at least $n+2$, so the lift $\widetilde\Phi\colon\widetilde V\to\widetilde P$ to universal covers has $\pi_{j+1}(\widetilde P,\widetilde V)\cong H_{j+1}(\widetilde P,\widetilde V)$ because the pair is $j$-connected, and this is finitely generated because $H_{j+1}(\widetilde P,\widetilde M)=0$ for $j+1\le n+1$ while $V$ and $M$ are finite complexes up to homotopy. The exact sequence $\pi_{j+1}(\widetilde P,\widetilde V)\to\pi_j(V)\to\pi_j(P)$ then shows that the kernel of $\Phi_*$ on $\pi_j$ is a quotient of a finitely generated module, hence is finitely generated. Represent each generator by an embedded sphere $S^j\subset\mathrm{int}\,V$; this is possible because $2j<d$. Since $2j+2\le d$, the dual handle of the surgery has index $d-j\ge j+2$, so surgery on $S^j$ does not create elements of $\pi_i$ for $i\le j$. Since $\pi_j(BO\langle n+1 \rangle)=0$ for $j\le n$, the $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-structure restricted to $S^j$ is null-homotopic, and the choice of a null-homotopy gives a stable framing of the normal bundle of $S^j$ in $V$. In the stable range $d-j>j$ it is an honest framing, for which the $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-structure extends over the trace of the surgery. Since $\Phi$ is null-homotopic on $S^j$, it extends over the trace as well. Surgery on the sphere replaces $\pi_j(V)$ by its quotient by the submodule generated by the class of the sphere and does not change $\pi_i$ for $i<j$. Since the sphere lies in the kernel of $\Phi_*$, the map $\Phi$ induces on the quotient the map to $\pi_j(P)$ induced by $\Phi_*$, which is still onto. As the spheres kill the whole kernel, it is injective as well. After the last step $\Phi'_*$ is an isomorphism on $\pi_j$ for $j\le\ell_d$. The surgered manifold is connected because we only perform surgeries on spheres of dimension at least $1$ in a manifold of dimension at least $5$.

Finally $\pi_j(C)\to\pi_j(V')$ is an isomorphism for $j\le\ell_d$, because both $\pi_j(C)$ and $\pi_j(V')$ map isomorphically to $\pi_j(P)$ and the composite is the map induced by $\Phi|_C$. Hence $\pi_j(V',C)=0$ for $j\le\ell_d$, and by the relative Hurewicz theorem and the universal coefficient theorem $H_j(V',C;G)=0$ for $j\le\ell_d$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:lifting}
Assume (T). Let $(V,\Phi)$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:surgery} with $d\in\{m+1,m+2\}$, with $\Phi_*$ an isomorphism on $\pi_j$ for $j\le\ell_d$, and let $\Phi_A\colon A\to M$ be a map with $c\circ\Phi_A\simeq\Phi|_A$. Then there is a lift of $\Phi$ extending $\Phi_A$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Lifts in the sense of the definition are what obstruction theory for the fibration replacing $c$ produces. Since $X$ is $n$-connected, the obstructions to lifting rel $A$ lie in $H^{i+1}(V,A;\pi_i(X))$ for $i\ge n+1$. The coefficient systems are pulled back from $P$ and are constant by (T), and the fibre $X$ is simply connected, so ordinary obstruction theory applies. By Poincar\'e--Lefschetz duality $H^{i+1}(V,A;G)\cong H_{d-i-1}(V,C;G)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:surgery} these groups vanish when $d-i-1\le\ell_d$. The largest degree occurring is $d-n-2$, which is $m-n-1\le\ell_{m+1}$ for $d=m+1$ and $m-n\le\ell_{m+2}$ for $d=m+2$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:PD_upgrade}
Let $M$ be a closed oriented manifold whose fundamental group $\pi$ is finite or infinite cyclic, and let $f\colon M\to M$ be a map of degree one that induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$. Then $f$ is a homotopy equivalence.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\widetilde f\colon\widetilde M\to\widetilde M$ be a lift to the universal cover; it is equivariant with respect to the automorphism $f_*$ of $\pi$. Since $f$ has degree one and induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$, the usual cap product argument with $\mathbb{Z}\pi$-coefficients shows that $\widetilde f_*\colon H_j(\widetilde M)\to H_j(\widetilde M)$ is a split surjection for every $j$ (see \cite[Chapter~2]{wall_surgery}). Explicitly, Poincar\'e duality for the closed manifold $M$ with $\mathbb{Z}\pi$-coefficients gives isomorphisms $H_j(\widetilde M)\cong H^{m-j}(M;\mathbb{Z}\pi)$, and the Umkehr map $f^!\colon H_j(\widetilde M)\to H_j(\widetilde M)$ obtained from $f^*$ on $H^{m-j}(M;\mathbb{Z}\pi)$ satisfies $\widetilde f_*\circ f^!=\mathrm{id}$, because $f_*[M]=[M]$ and $f_*$ is an isomorphism on $\pi_1$. The automorphism group of $\pi$ is finite, so some power $f^k$ induces the identity on $\pi$, and $(\widetilde f_*)^k=(\widetilde f^{\,k})_*$ is a surjective $\mathbb{Z}\pi$-linear endomorphism of $H_j(\widetilde M)$. The ring $\mathbb{Z}\pi$ is Noetherian and $\widetilde M$ has a finite free $\mathbb{Z}\pi$-chain complex, so $H_j(\widetilde M)$ is a Noetherian module, and a surjective endomorphism of a Noetherian module is injective. Hence $\widetilde f_*$ is injective, and therefore bijective. Thus $\widetilde f$ is a homology isomorphism of simply connected spaces, hence a homotopy equivalence, and so is $f$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{bordism exact}
The sequence
\[
\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(M)\to\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)\to\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P,M)\xrightarrow{\ \partial\ }\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(M)\to\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(P)
\]
is exact.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The sequence is the long exact sequence of the pair $(P,M)$ in the homology theory $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_*$, with $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(M)$ and $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P,M)$ replaced by the subgroups $\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(M)$ and $\partial^{-1}\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(M)$. Exactness at $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)$ is unchanged. At $\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P,M)$ the kernel of $\partial$ is the image of $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)$, which lies in the subgroup. At $\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(M)$ the kernel of the map to $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(P)$ is $\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(M)\cap\operatorname{im}\partial$, which is $\partial$ of $\partial^{-1}\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(M)$ by definition.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:normal_form}
Every class in $\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P,M)$ has a representative in standard form. If two standard form representatives $(W,F)$ and $(W',F')$ with boundaries $-M\sqcup N$ and $-M\sqcup N'$ define the same class, then there is a $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-manifold $T$ of dimension $m+2$ with
\[
\partial T=W\cup W'\cup(M\times I)\cup Y_N ,
\]
where $M\times I$ joins $\partial_1W$ to $\partial_1W'$, $Y_N$ is a $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-bordism between $N$ and $N'$, and $T$ carries a map to $P$ extending $F$, $F'$, $c\circ\mathrm{pr}_1$ on $M\times I$, and a map $G_N\colon Y_N\to M$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $[W_0,F_0]\in\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P,M)$, so $[\partial W_0,F_0|_{\partial W_0}]$ has degree $0$ over $M$. Form $W_1=W_0\sqcup(M\times I)$ with the map $F_0\sqcup c\circ\mathrm{pr}_1$. The class of $(M\times I,c\circ\mathrm{pr}_1)$ in $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P,M)$ is zero: it is the part $M\times I\times\{0\}$ of the boundary of $M\times I\times I$, and the rest of that boundary maps to $M$ under $c\circ\mathrm{pr}_1$. So $W_1$ represents the same class. Put $\partial_1W_1=-M\times\{0\}$ and $N_1=\partial W_0\sqcup M\times\{1\}$, with the map $F_0|_{\partial W_0}\sqcup\mathrm{id}$; it has degree $0+1=1$. Finally join all components of $W_1$ by boundary connected sums along arcs ending in $N_1$ (and by interior connected sums with closed components). This does not change the relative bordism class, and it keeps $\partial_1W_1$ and the degree. The result is a standard form representative.

For the second assertion, a relative bordism between the two representatives is a $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-manifold $T$ with $\partial T=W\cup Y\cup W'$, where $Y$ is a bordism over $M$ between $\partial W=-M\sqcup N$ and $\partial W'=-M\sqcup N'$, and the map to $P$ extends the given maps on $W$, $W'$ and maps $Y$ into $M$. In $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(M)$ we have $[-M,\mathrm{id}]+[N,g]=[-M,\mathrm{id}]+[N',g']$, and cancelling in this group gives $[N,g]=[N',g']$, so there is a bordism $Y_N$ over $M$ between $N$ and $N'$. Let $Y'=(M\times I)\sqcup Y_N$. The closed manifold $\Sigma=Y\cup(-Y')$ over $M$ satisfies $[W\cup Y'\cup W']=[W\cup Y\cup W']-[\Sigma]=-[\Sigma]$ in $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)$, because $T$ is a null-bordism of $W\cup Y\cup W'$. Replacing $Y_N$ by $Y_N\sqcup\Sigma$, which is again a bordism over $M$ between $N$ and $N'$, makes $[W\cup Y'\cup W']=0$, and a null-bordism of this closed manifold is the required $T$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
$\widetilde{\mathcal H}(M)$ is the set of equivalence classes of pairs $(W,F)$, where $W$ is a compact $(m+1)$-dimensional $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-manifold with $\partial W=\partial_1W\sqcup\partial_2W$, $\partial_1W=-M$, $\partial_2W=M$ (with the given $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-structure on both ends), and $F\colon W\to M$ is a map with $F|_{\partial_1W}=\mathrm{id}_M$ and $F|_{\partial_2W}=f$ a based homotopy equivalence. Two pairs $(W,F)$, $(W',F')$ are equivalent if there is a based homotopy $h$ from $f=F|_{\partial_2W}$ to $f'=F'|_{\partial_2W'}$ such that the closed manifold
\[
-W'\cup_{\partial_1}W\cup_{\partial_2}(M\times I)
\]
with the map $F'\cup F\cup h$ is zero in $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(M)$. The base point is the class of $(M\times I,\mathrm{pr}_1)$.

$\widetilde{\mathcal H}(P)$ is defined in the same way with $F\colon W\to P$, $F|_{\partial_1W}=c$ and $F|_{\partial_2W}=f$ a based map $M\to P$ that is an $(n+1)$-equivalence, that is, $f=\phi\circ c$ for a unique $\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(P)$ (so $f\simeq\phi\circ c$ is an iso on $\pi_i$, $i\le n$); the homotopy $h$ is a homotopy in $P$, and the closed manifold has to vanish in $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{exactA}
There is an exact sequence of pointed sets
\[
\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(M)\to\widetilde{\mathcal H}(M)\to\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)\xrightarrow{\ \alpha\ }\widehat\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(M).
\]
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The first map sends $[N,g]$ to the class of $(M\times I\sqcup N,\ \mathrm{pr}_1\sqcup g)$, and the second sends $(W,F)$ to $[F|_{\partial_2W}]$. It is well defined, because equivalent pairs have homotopic restrictions. The composite of the first two maps is trivial, since the restriction is the identity.

If $(W,F)\mapsto[\mathrm{id}_M]$, then $f\simeq\mathrm{id}_M$ by a based homotopy $h$. Then $Z:=W\cup(M\times I)$, with the ends of $M\times I$ glued to $\partial_2W$ and $\partial_1W$, and the map $F\cup h$, is a closed $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-manifold over $M$, and $(W,F)$ is equivalent to the image of $[Z,F\cup h]$, because $-(M\times I\sqcup Z)\cup W\cup(M\times I)$ is bordant over $M$ to $Z-Z=0$. This is exactness at $\widetilde{\mathcal H}(M)$.

Finally $\alpha(f)=0$ means that $(M,\nu,f)$ and $(M,\nu,\mathrm{id})$ are bordant over $M$. A bordism is a pair $(W,F)$ with $\partial W=-M\sqcup M$, $F|_{\partial_1W}=\mathrm{id}$ and $F|_{\partial_2W}=f$, that is an element of $\widetilde{\mathcal H}(M)$ mapping to $[f]$. Conversely such an element gives a bordism. This is exactness at $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{HeM=HeP}
Assume (T). The map $c_\sharp\colon\widetilde{\mathcal H}(M)\to\widetilde{\mathcal H}(P)$, $(W,F)\mapsto(W,c\circ F)$, is a bijection.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
\emph{Well defined.} If $f=F|_{\partial_2W}$ is a self-equivalence of $M$, then $c\circ f=\phi_f\circ c$ is an $(n+1)$-equivalence. A homotopy $h$ in $M$ gives the homotopy $c\circ h$, and $c$ maps the null-bordism in $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(M)$ to one in $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)$.

\emph{Surjective.} Let $(W,F)\in\widetilde{\mathcal H}(P)$ with $F|_{\partial_2W}=\phi\circ c$. We may assume $W$ connected (join components by interior connected sums, which does not change the class). Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:surgery} with $A=\partial_1W$, $C=\partial_2W$ and $d=m+1$; note that $\Phi|_C=\phi\circ c$ is an isomorphism on $\pi_j$ for $j\le n$. After surgery in the interior, which changes $(W,F)$ within its class in $\widetilde{\mathcal H}(P)$ (the trace of a surgery rel boundary is a $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-cobordism rel boundary, so the closed manifold in the equivalence relation is null-bordant), we may assume that $F_*$ is an isomorphism on $\pi_j$ for $j\le\ell_{m+1}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:lifting}, applied to the lift $\mathrm{id}_M$ on $A=\partial_1W$, gives a lift $\widehat F\colon W\to M$ of $F$ with $\widehat F|_{\partial_1W}=\mathrm{id}_M$. Put $\widehat f=\widehat F|_{\partial_2W}$. Then $(W,\widehat F)$ is a $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-bordism over $M$ between $(M,\mathrm{id})$ and $(M,\widehat f)$, so $\widehat f_*[M]=[M]$, so $\widehat f$ has degree one. Also $c\circ\widehat f\simeq\phi\circ c$ is an isomorphism on $\pi_j$ for $j\le n$, so $\widehat f$ is an isomorphism on $\pi_1$, because $n\ge2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:PD_upgrade}, $\widehat f$ is a homotopy equivalence, so $(W,\widehat F)\in\widetilde{\mathcal H}(M)$ and $c_\sharp(W,\widehat F)=(W,c\widehat F)$ is equivalent to $(W,F)$ by the homotopy $c\widehat F\simeq F$.

\emph{Injective.} Let $(W,F),(W',F')\in\widetilde{\mathcal H}(M)$ with $c_\sharp$-images equivalent. Then there is a based homotopy $h_P\colon cf\simeq cf'$ in $P$ such that $Z_P:=-W'\cup W\cup(M\times I)$ with the map $cF'\cup cF\cup h_P$ bounds a $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-manifold $(V,\Phi)$ over $P$. Replacing disjoint unions by connected sums gives equivalent pairs, so we may assume $W$ and $W'$ are connected; then $Z_P$ is connected, and we may take $V$ connected. Put $A:=W\cup W'$ (the union of the two copies glued along $\partial_1=M$, so $A$ is connected) and $C:=M\times I$, so $\partial V=A\cup C$ with $A\cap C=M\times\{0,1\}$. The map $\Phi|_A$ has the lift $F\cup F'$ to $M$. The map $\Phi|_C$ is the homotopy $h_P$, which is an isomorphism on $\pi_j$ for $j\le n$ because its ends are. By Lemma~\ref{lem:surgery} we may assume $\Phi_*$ is an isomorphism for $j\le\ell_{m+2}$ ($\dim V=m+2$). By Lemma~\ref{lem:lifting} there is a lift $\widehat\Phi\colon V\to M$ extending $F\cup F'$ on $A$. Its restriction $h:=\widehat\Phi|_C\colon M\times I\to M$ is a based homotopy from $f$ to $f'$, and $(V,\widehat\Phi)$ is a null-bordism over $M$ of the closed manifold $-W'\cup W\cup(M\times I)$ with the map $F'\cup F\cup h$. So $(W,F)\sim(W',F')$ in $\widetilde{\mathcal H}(M)$.

For $m=2n+1$ and $d=m+2$ the existence group $H_{n+1}(V,C;\pi_{n+1}(X))$ is a middle-dimensional obstruction group that surgery cannot kill, and for $m\ge2n+2$ the existence groups $H_{d-i-1}(V,C;\pi_i(X))$ with $i=n+1$ lie above $\ell_d$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{exactB}
There is an exact sequence of pointed sets
\[
\pi_1(\mathcal E_\bullet(P))\to\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)\to\widetilde{\mathcal H}(M)\to\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(P)\xrightarrow{\ \beta\ }\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_m(P).
\]
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
We use $\widetilde{\mathcal H}(M)\cong\widetilde{\mathcal H}(P)$ (Lemma~\ref{HeM=HeP}). The map $\widetilde{\mathcal H}(P)\to\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(P)$ sends $(W,F)$ to $\phi$, where $F|_{\partial_2W}=\phi\circ c$. It is well defined, since a homotopy $f\simeq f'$ gives $\phi=\phi'$. The map $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)\to\widetilde{\mathcal H}(P)$ sends $[N,g]$ to $(M\times I\sqcup N,\ c\circ\mathrm{pr}_1\sqcup g)$. A loop $\lambda$ in $\mathcal E_\bullet(P)=\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(P,P)_{\simeq}$ based at $\mathrm{id}$ gives $\widehat\lambda\colon P\times S^1\to P$, and $\pi_1(\mathcal E_\bullet(P))\to\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)$ sends $\lambda$ to $[M\times S^1,\ \widehat\lambda\circ(c\times\mathrm{id})]$, where $M\times S^1$ carries the product of $\nu$ with the bounding $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-structure on $S^1$.

\emph{Exactness at $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(P)$.} Suppose $(W,F)\mapsto\phi$. Then $W$ is a bordism over $P$ from $(M,c)$ to $(M,\phi c)$, so $\beta(\phi)=0$. Conversely, if $\beta(\phi)=0$, a bordism over $P$ from $(M,c)$ to $(M,\phi c)$ is a pair in $\widetilde{\mathcal H}(P)$ with image $\phi$.

\emph{Exactness at $\widetilde{\mathcal H}(P)$.} The composite $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)\to\widetilde{\mathcal H}(P)\to\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(P)$ is trivial. If $(W,F)\mapsto\mathrm{id}_P$, then $f=F|_{\partial_2W}\simeq c$ by a based homotopy $h$, and $Z=W\cup(M\times I)$, with the ends of $M\times I$ glued to $\partial_2W$ and $\partial_1W$, and the map $F\cup h$, is a closed manifold over $P$. As in the proof of Lemma~\ref{exactA}, $(W,F)$ is equivalent to the image of $[Z,F\cup h]$.

\emph{Exactness at $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)$.} A class $[N,g]$ maps to the base point iff there is a homotopy $h$ from $c$ to $c$, that is, a loop $\lambda$ in $\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(M,P)$ based at $c$, such that $(M\times I\sqcup N)\cup(M\times I)$ with the map $c\circ\mathrm{pr}_1\sqcup g\cup h$ vanishes in $\Omega^{BO\langle n+1 \rangle}_{m+1}(P)$. This closed manifold is $N\sqcup M\times S^1$ with the maps $g$ and the loop, so the condition says $[N,g]=-[M\times S^1,h]$. Since $c^*\colon\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(P,P)\to\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(M,P)$ is a weak equivalence ($P$ is an $n$-type and $c$ is $(n+1)$-connected), every loop $\lambda$ in $\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(M,P)$ at $c$ is of the form $c^*$ of a loop in $\mathcal E_\bullet(P)$ at $\mathrm{id}$, and $-[M\times S^1,h]=[M\times S^1,\bar h]$ for the reversed loop. Hence the kernel is the image of $\pi_1(\mathcal E_\bullet(P))$.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{gamma_well_defined}
The class $\gamma(\xi)$ is a well-defined element of $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)$, independent of the representative, the surgeries and the lift. Moreover $\pi(\gamma(\xi))=1$.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Put $f=r|_{\partial_1W}$. Since $c\circ f\simeq F|_{\partial_1W}=c$, the map $f$ induces isomorphisms on $\pi_i$ for $i\le n$, in particular on $\pi_1$, and $\pi(f)=1$ once we know $f$ is an equivalence. The triple $(W,r)$ is a $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-bordism over $M$ between $(N,g)$ and $(M,f)$, since $\partial W=-M\sqcup N$. So $f_*[M]=g_*[N]=[M]$, and $f$ has degree one. By Lemma~\ref{lem:PD_upgrade}, $f$ is a homotopy equivalence.

For independence, let $(W,F,r)$ and $(W',F',r')$ be two such choices for the same class. Lemma~\ref{lem:normal_form} gives a $BO\langle n+1 \rangle$-manifold $T$ over $P$ with $\partial T=W\cup W'\cup(M\times I)\cup Y_N$, with $W$, $W'$ and $Y_N$ carrying the lifts $r$, $r'$ and $G_N$; these agree on $N$ and $N'$ by construction. \label{invcom}

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:coaction}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Let $G$ be a topological group, $\varphi\colon X\to G$ and $\psi\colon S^m\to G$ based maps. Then $\nabla\circ(\varphi\vee\psi)\circ\mathrm{pinch}\simeq\varphi\cdot(\psi\circ q)$ (pointwise product in $[X,G]_\bullet$). Consequently $\varphi\circ t_{\tilde\kappa}\simeq\varphi\cdot q^*(\varphi\circ\tilde\kappa)$.
\item[(b)] If $q\circ\tilde\kappa\simeq*$, then $q\circ t_{\tilde\kappa}\simeq q$. Let $K\subset X$ be the complement of the open top cell. If $\tilde\kappa$ and $\tilde\kappa'$ are represented by maps into $K$, then $t_{\tilde\kappa}$ and $t_{\tilde\kappa'}$ restrict to the inclusion on $K$ and $t_{\tilde\kappa}\circ t_{\tilde\kappa'}\simeq t_{\tilde\kappa+\tilde\kappa'}\simeq t_{\tilde\kappa'}\circ t_{\tilde\kappa}$.
\item[(c)] If $h\colon X\to X$ is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, then $h\circ t_{\tilde\kappa}\circ h^{-1}\simeq t_{h_*\tilde\kappa}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
(a) Let $j\colon G\vee G\to G\times G$ be the inclusion and $\mu$ the multiplication, so $\mu\circ j=\nabla$. The map $j\circ(\varphi\vee\psi)\circ\mathrm{pinch}$ has components $(\varphi\circ\mathrm{pr}_X\circ\mathrm{pinch},\ \psi\circ q)\simeq(\varphi,\psi\circ q)$, because $\mathrm{pr}_X\circ\mathrm{pinch}\simeq\mathrm{id}_X$. Apply $\mu$. Taking $\psi=\varphi\circ\tilde\kappa$ gives the second statement.

(b) The first claim holds since $q\circ t_{\tilde\kappa}=\nabla(q\vee q\tilde\kappa)\circ\mathrm{pinch}\simeq q\circ\mathrm{pr}_X\circ\mathrm{pinch}\simeq q$. For the second, $t_{\tilde\kappa}\circ\tilde\kappa'\simeq\tilde\kappa'$, so $t_{\tilde\kappa}\circ t_{\tilde\kappa'}\simeq\nabla(t_{\tilde\kappa}\vee\tilde\kappa')\circ\mathrm{pinch}$. This is the map that collapses the boundaries of two disjoint discs $D_1,D_2$ in the top cell and folds in $\tilde\kappa'$ on $D_1$ and $\tilde\kappa$ on $D_2$. For $m\ge2$ an isotopy of the open top cell exchanges $D_1$ and $D_2$, so the composite is symmetric in $\tilde\kappa,\tilde\kappa'$. If $D\supset D_1\cup D_2$ is a disc, collapsing the boundaries of $D_1$ and $D_2$ inside $D$ is the comultiplication $S^m\to S^m\vee S^m$ applied to the sphere obtained by collapsing $\partial D$. So the two-disc map is the single-disc map for $\nabla(\tilde\kappa\vee\tilde\kappa')\circ(\text{comultiplication})=\tilde\kappa+\tilde\kappa'$.

(c) Since $h$ is a diffeomorphism, $\mathrm{pinch}_D\circ h^{-1}=(h^{-1}\vee\mathrm{id})\circ\mathrm{pinch}_{h(D)}$, so $h\,t_{\tilde\kappa}\,h^{-1}=\nabla(\mathrm{id}\vee h\tilde\kappa)\circ\mathrm{pinch}_{h(D)}$. An orientation-preserving embedded disc in a connected manifold is isotopic to any other, so this is homotopic to $t_{h_*\tilde\kappa}$.

\label{lem:orbit}
Let $p,q\ge2$, $M=S^p\times S^q$, $Y$ a space, and $g\in\pi_q(Y)$. Let $\varphi_0=g\circ\mathrm{pr}_2\colon M\to Y$. Then the fibre of the restriction map $[M,Y]_\bullet\to[S^p\vee S^q,Y]_\bullet$ over the class of $\varphi_0|_{S^p\vee S^q}=(*,g)$ is the orbit of $\varphi_0$ under $\pi_{p+q}(Y)$, and the stabilizer of $\varphi_0$ is the subgroup
\[
[\pi_{p+1}(Y),g]:=\{[a,g]\mid a\in\pi_{p+1}(Y)\}\subset\pi_{p+q}(Y).
\]
Hence the fibre is in bijection with $\pi_{p+q}(Y)/[\pi_{p+1}(Y),g]$. The same holds with the factors interchanged: for $g\in\pi_p(Y)$ and $\varphi_0=g\circ\mathrm{pr}_1$ the stabilizer is $[\pi_{q+1}(Y),g]$.
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Write $K=S^p\vee S^q$, so $M=K\cup_we^{p+q}$ with $w=[\iota_p,\iota_q]$, and consider the cofibre sequence $S^{p+q-1}\xrightarrow{w}K\to M\xrightarrow{q}S^{p+q}$. The fibration sequence $\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(S^{p+q},Y)\to\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(M,Y)\to\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(K,Y)\xrightarrow{w^*}\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(S^{p+q-1},Y)$ shows that the fibre of the restriction map over $[(*,g)]$ is the quotient of $\pi_{p+q}(Y)$ by the action of $\pi_1(\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(K,Y),(*,g))$, and that $\pi_{p+q}(Y)$ acts on the fibre through the coaction, by the definition of the action, since $w^*(*,g)=[*,g]=0$.

It remains to identify the stabilizer. Maps $\varphi\colon M\to Y$ with $\varphi|_{K}=(*,g)$ strictly are adjoint to based maps $S^p\to F_g$, where $F_g=\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(S^q,Y)_g$ is the component of $g$; under the translation $F_g\simeq\Omega^qY$ they are elements of $\pi_p(\Omega^qY)=\pi_{p+q}(Y)$, and the element $b$ corresponds to $\varphi_0+b\circ q$. The group $\pi_1(\mathrm{Map}_\bullet(K,Y),(*,g))=\pi_{p+1}(Y)\times\pi_1(F_g,g)$ acts on $\pi_p(F_g,g)$. The factor $\pi_1(F_g,g)=\pi_{q+1}(Y)$ acts trivially, because $F_g\simeq\Omega^qY$ is a loop space and $\pi_1$ acts trivially on the homotopy groups of an $H$-group. The factor $\pi_{p+1}(Y)$ acts by translation by the boundary map of the evaluation fibration $F_g\to\mathrm{Map}(S^q,Y)_g\to Y$, which by G.~W.~Whitehead \cite{whitehead46} is $a\mapsto\pm[a,g]$. Hence the stabilizer is $[\pi_{p+1}(Y),g]$. The statement with the factors interchanged follows by symmetry.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{splitting}

\label{thm:bordism_S3S3}
Let $M = S^3 \times S^3$ and $P = K(\mathbb{Z}^2, 3)$. Then
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\widehat{\Omega}^{\mathrm{spin}}_6(M) = 0$, and
\item[(ii)] $\widehat{\Omega}^{\mathrm{spin}}_7(P,M)$ is a finite abelian group of order at most $144=2^4\cdot 3^2$.
\end{itemize}

<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
We use the spin bordism groups of a point in low degrees \cite{abp}, namely $\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_q\cong\mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{Z}/2,\ \mathbb{Z}/2,\ 0,\ \mathbb{Z},\ 0,\ 0,\ 0$ for $q=0,\dots,7$, and the two lowest differentials of the AHSS. By \cite{teichner}, for a space $X$ the differential $d_2\colon E^2_{p,0}=H_p(X;\mathbb{Z})\to E^2_{p-2,1}=H_{p-2}(X;\mathbb{Z}/2)$ is the composite of reduction mod $2$ with the dual of $\mathrm{Sq}^2$, and $d_2\colon E^2_{p,1}\to E^2_{p-2,2}$ is the dual of $\mathrm{Sq}^2$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Part (i).} The stable splitting $\Sigma^\infty M_+\simeq S^0\vee S^3\vee S^3\vee S^6$ gives $\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_k(M)\cong\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_k\oplus(\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_{k-3})^2\oplus\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_{k-6}$. For $k=6$ this is $0\oplus0\oplus\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}$, and the degree map is an isomorphism onto $\mathbb{Z}$. Hence $\widehat{\Omega}^{\mathrm{spin}}_6(M)=\ker(\deg)=0$. For $k=7$ the splitting gives $\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_7(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}^2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2$, where $\mathbb{Z}^2=\Omega_4^{\mathrm{spin}}\otimes H_3(M)$ is the bottom filtration piece of the AHSS.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Part (ii).} By Lemma~\ref{bordism exact} and Part~(i), $\partial=0$ and $\widehat{\Omega}^{\mathrm{spin}}_7(P,M)\cong\operatorname{coker}\bigl[c_*\colon\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_7(M)\to\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_7(P)\bigr]$. We use the AHSS for $P$ in total degree $7$. Let $F_p\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_7(P)$ denote the image of $\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_7(P^{(p)})\to\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_7(P)$, where $P^{(p)}$ is the $p$-skeleton, so that $F_p\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_7(P)/F_{p-1}\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_7(P)\cong E^\infty_{p,7-p}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:homology_K(ZZ,3)} and universal coefficients, $H_6(P;\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3$, $H_5(P;\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$, $H_4(P;\mathbb{Z}/2)=0$ and $H_7(P;\mathbb{Z}/2)=0$. The terms of total degree $7$ are
\[
E^2_{7,0}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/3)^2,\quad E^2_{6,1}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3,\quad E^2_{5,2}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2,\quad E^2_{4,3}=0,\quad E^2_{3,4}\cong\mathbb{Z}^2,
\]
and $E^2_{p,q}=0$ for $p\le2$ in this total degree.

The differential $d_2\colon E^2_{8,0}\to E^2_{6,1}$ has rank one. Indeed, reduction mod $2$ is an isomorphism $H_8(P;\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4\to H_8(P;\mathbb{Z}/2)$, because $H_7(P;\mathbb{Z})$ has no $2$-torsion, and $d_2$ is then dual to $\mathrm{Sq}^2\colon H^6(P;\mathbb{Z}/2)\to H^8(P;\mathbb{Z}/2)$. <!-- lemma-16: body_md -->
\label{lem:AutN}
Let $M=S^3\times S^3$, let $N:=[M,S^3]_\bullet$, a group under pointwise multiplication in $S^3$, and let $x,y\in N$ be the two coordinate projections.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The element $c:=[x,y]$ is central in $N$ and has order $12$, $N/\langle c\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^2$ is generated by the images of $x$ and $y$, and
\[
  N=\langle x,y,c\mid c\ \text{central},\ c^{12}=1,\ [x,y]=c\rangle .
\]
\item[(b)] The assignment $f\mapsto f^*$, $f^*(\varphi)=\varphi\circ f$ for a based map $\varphi\colon M\to S^3$ representing an element of $N$, is an anti-isomorphism from $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)$ onto the group $\operatorname{Aut}^+(N)$ of automorphisms of $N$ that induce a matrix of determinant $1$ on $N/\langle c\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^2$. In particular $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)\cong\operatorname{Aut}^+(N)$ as groups. The matrix induced by $f^*$ on $N/\langle c\rangle=H^3(M;\mathbb{Z})$ is the transpose of $\pi(f)$ in the dual basis.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
(a) Write $M=(S^3\vee S^3)\cup_w e^6$ with $w=[\iota_1,\iota_2]$, and let $q\colon M\to S^6$ be the pinch map. The Puppe sequence gives an exact sequence of groups
\[
  \pi_4(S^3)^2\xrightarrow{(\Sigma w)^*}\pi_6(S^3)\xrightarrow{q^*}N\xrightarrow{i^*}\pi_3(S^3)^2=\mathbb{Z}^2 .
\]
Since the suspension of a Whitehead product is null, $\Sigma w\simeq0$, so $q^*$ is injective and its image is a copy of $\pi_6(S^3)\cong\mathbb{Z}/12$. The map $i^*$ is onto because $i^*(x),i^*(y)$ form a basis.

The commutator of $g,h\in N$ is the composite of the diagonal $M\to M\times M$, the map $g\times h$, and the commutator map $S^3\times S^3\to S^3$. The commutator map is trivial on $S^3\vee S^3$, so $[g,h]$ factors as
\[
  M\xrightarrow{\ \bar\Delta\ }M\wedge M\xrightarrow{\ g\wedge h\ }S^3\wedge S^3\xrightarrow{\ \langle\iota_3,\iota_3\rangle\ }S^3,
\]
where $\bar\Delta$ is the reduced diagonal and $\langle\iota_3,\iota_3\rangle\in\pi_6(S^3)$ is the Samelson product. Since $\dim M=6$, the map $\bar\Delta$ may be deformed into the $6$-skeleton $(S^3\vee S^3)\wedge(S^3\vee S^3)$ of $M\wedge M$. If $h=q^*\alpha$, then $h$ is trivial on $S^3\vee S^3$, so $g\wedge h$ is trivial on this skeleton and $[g,h]=1$. Hence $q^*\pi_6(S^3)$ is central. For $g=x$ and $h=y$ the composite $(x\wedge y)\circ\bar\Delta$ is the quotient map $M\to S^6$, so $[x,y]=q^*\langle\iota_3,\iota_3\rangle$. By Bott \cite{bott}, the Samelson product $\langle\iota_3,\iota_3\rangle$ generates $\pi_6(S^3)\cong\mathbb{Z}/12$. Thus $c$ generates $\ker i^*$, which proves the first two assertions. The presentation follows, since the group defined by it is a central extension of $\mathbb{Z}^2$ by $\mathbb{Z}/12$ with the same generators and the same commutator.

(b) Because $S^3$ is a group, precomposition $f^*\colon N\to N$ is a homomorphism, and $(f\circ g)^*=g^*\circ f^*$. Then $f^*$ and $\psi$ are homomorphisms that agree on the generators $x,y$, so $f^*=\psi$. The map $f$ induces an isomorphism on $H^3$ and, having degree $\det=1$, on $H^6$, so it is a homology isomorphism of simply connected spaces and hence a homotopy equivalence.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:S3xS3_split}
There is a split short exact sequence
\[
  0 \longrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/12 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/12 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(S^3 \times S^3) \xrightarrow{\pi} SL_2(\mathbb{Z}) \longrightarrow 0 .
\]
Identify the kernel with $V=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^2,\mathbb{Z}/12)$. Then $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ acts on $V$ by $A\cdot\lambda=\lambda\circ A^{-1}$, the dual of the standard action on $(\mathbb{Z}/12)^2$. Here $A$ is the matrix induced on $N/\langle c\rangle$ by an automorphism of $N$; since $f\mapsto f^*$ reverses composition, the action of $\pi(f)$ in $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)$ differs from this one by the inverse transpose, which is an inner automorphism of $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$, so the semidirect product is the same. Hence
\[
  \operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(S^3 \times S^3) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/12)^2\rtimes SL_2(\mathbb{Z}).
\]
This group is not a direct product $(\mathbb{Z}/12)^2\times SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$: its abelianization is $\mathbb{Z}/12$, while that of the direct product is $(\mathbb{Z}/12)^3$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:AutN} we may work in $\operatorname{Aut}^+(N)$. Taking the induced matrix on $N/\langle c\rangle=\mathbb{Z}^2$ gives a homomorphism $\operatorname{Aut}^+(N)\to SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$. It is onto: for $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c'&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $ad-bc'=1$, the assignment $x\mapsto x^ay^{c'}$, $y\mapsto x^by^d$, $c\mapsto c$ respects the defining relations, because $N$ has class $2$ and so $[x^ay^{c'},x^by^d]=c^{ad-bc'}=c$, and it induces $A$ on $N/\langle c\rangle$ and the identity on $\langle c\rangle$, hence is bijective.

An automorphism in the kernel has the form $\tau_\lambda(g)=g\,c^{\lambda(\bar g)}$ for $\lambda\in V$, where $\bar g$ is the image of $g$ in $\mathbb{Z}^2$ (these are the pinch-fold twists considered below). This identifies the kernel with $V\cong(\mathbb{Z}/12)^2$. The kernel is thus a copy of $\pi_6(S^3)^2$, where $\pi_6(S^3)\cong\mathbb{Z}/12$ by Toda \cite{toda}; it is the kernel in Sieradski's exact sequence \cite[Example~3]{sieradski}. That the commutator $c=[x,y]$ has order $12$ is Bott's computation of the Samelson product \cite{bott}, used in Lemma~\ref{lem:AutN}. If $\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}^+(N)$ induces $A$, then $\varphi(c)=c$, and a direct computation gives $\varphi\tau_\lambda\varphi^{-1}=\tau_{\lambda\circ A^{-1}}$. This is the stated action.

For the splitting we use the presentation $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})=\langle S,U\mid S^4=1,\ S^2=U^3\rangle$ with
\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad U=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
(the amalgam $\mathbb{Z}/4*_{\mathbb{Z}/2}\mathbb{Z}/6$, with $S^2=U^3=-I$). Define $\tilde S,\tilde U\in\operatorname{Aut}^+(N)$ by
\[
\tilde S\colon x\mapsto y,\ y\mapsto x^{-1}c,\ c\mapsto c;\qquad \tilde U\colon x\mapsto y,\ y\mapsto x^{-1}y\,c,\ c\mapsto c .
\]
They induce $S$ and $U$ on $N/\langle c\rangle$ (we write the matrix of an automorphism with the images of $x$ and $y$ as columns). In the class-$2$ group $N$ the commutator is bilinear and $c$ is central, so one computes directly:
\begin{align*}
\tilde S^2(x)&=x^{-1}c,&\tilde S^2(y)&=y^{-1}c,&\tilde S^4(x)&=(x^{-1}c)^{-1}c=x,&\tilde S^4(y)&=y,\\
\tilde U^2(x)&=x^{-1}yc,&\tilde U^2(y)&=x^{-1}c,&\tilde U^3(x)&=x^{-1}c,&\tilde U^3(y)&=y^{-1}c.
\end{align*}
Hence $\tilde S^4=1$ and $\tilde U^3=\tilde S^2$. (For the lifts without the factor $c$ the relation $\tilde U^3=\tilde S^2$ fails, so the central correction matters.) By the presentation, $S\mapsto\tilde S$, $U\mapsto\tilde U$ defines a homomorphism $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{Aut}^+(N)$ that is a section of the projection, so the sequence splits.

The action is not trivial: $S$ sends $\tau_\lambda$ to $\tau_{\lambda\circ S^{-1}}$, which differs from $\tau_\lambda$ for $\lambda=(1,0)$. For $G=V\rtimes SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ we have $G^{ab}=V_{SL_2(\mathbb{Z})}\times SL_2(\mathbb{Z})^{ab}$, where $V_{SL_2(\mathbb{Z})}$ denotes the coinvariants. The map $\lambda\mapsto\lambda\circ U^{-1}-\lambda$ on $V$ has determinant $\det(U^{-1}-I)=1$ and is therefore invertible on $(\mathbb{Z}/12)^2$, so the coinvariants vanish. Since $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})^{ab}\cong\mathbb{Z}/12$, we get $G^{ab}\cong\mathbb{Z}/12$.

Conversely every $t_{\tilde\kappa}$ with $\tilde\kappa\in\pi_7(M)=\pi_7(S^3)\oplus\pi_7(S^4)$ lies in $\ker\pi$: it is the identity on $S^3\vee S^4$ and has degree one, because the Hurewicz image of $\tilde\kappa$ in $H_7(M)$ vanishes.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:coaction}(b) (take $K=S^3\vee S^4$) the map $\Psi\colon\pi_7(M)\to\ker\pi$, $\tilde\kappa\mapsto t_{\tilde\kappa}$, is a surjective homomorphism. Its kernel consists of the classes $\tilde\kappa=(b_1,b_2)$ with $b_1\cdot\mathrm{pr}_1\simeq\mathrm{pr}_1$ and $b_2\cdot\mathrm{pr}_2\simeq\mathrm{pr}_2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit} the stabilizer of $\mathrm{pr}_2$ is $[\pi_4(S^4),\iota_4]=D$, and the stabilizer of $\mathrm{pr}_1$ is $[\pi_5(S^3),\iota_3]=0$, since Whitehead products vanish in the $H$-space $S^3$. Hence $\ker\Psi=0\oplus D$ and $\ker\pi\cong\pi_7(S^3)\oplus Q\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/24$. This proves (a).

\emph{The image.} The maps $s$ and $\sigma$ are orientation-preserving based diffeomorphisms; $s$ has degree $(-1)(-1)=1$ because both $x\mapsto\bar x$ on $S^3$ and $r$ on $S^4$ have degree $-1$, and $\sigma$ has the inverse $(x,y)\mapsto(x\eta_3(y)^{-1},y)$. They realize $(e,a)=(-1,0)$ and $(1,1)$. The assignment $f\mapsto(e,a)$ is a homomorphism $\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)\to\{\pm1\}\times\mathbb{Z}/2$: if $g$ has invariants $(e',a')$, then $g\circ j_2=j_1(a'\eta_3)+j_2(e'\iota_4)$, so $(f\circ g)_1j_2=f_1j_1(a'\eta_3)+f_1j_2(e'\iota_4)=a'\eta_3+e'a\eta_3$, which is $(a+a')\eta_3$ in $\pi_4(S^3)=\mathbb{Z}/2$, and the signs multiply. Hence $G=\operatorname{im}\pi$ is the subgroup of $\{\pm1\}\times\mathbb{Z}/2$ generated by the invariants of $s$ and $\sigma$, which are $(-1,0)$ and $(1,1)$, and all four pairs occur, so $G\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$. This proves (b), and $|\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)|=48\cdot4=192$.

\emph{The action and the splitting.} Write elements of $K:=\ker\pi$ as $(a,b)$, with $a\in\pi_7(S^3)=\mathbb{Z}/2$ and $b\in Q$, meaning $t_{\tilde\kappa}$ for $\tilde\kappa=j_1a+j_2b$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:coaction}(c), for a diffeomorphism $h$ of degree one, $h\,t_{\tilde\kappa}\,h^{-1}=t_{h_*\tilde\kappa}$. For $s$: $s\circ j_1a=j_1(\bar{\ }\circ a)$ and $\bar{\ }\circ a=-a=a$, since inversion in $S^3$ acts on homotopy groups by $-1$ and $\pi_7(S^3)=\mathbb{Z}/2$; and $s\circ j_2b=j_2(r\circ b)$ with $r\circ b=(-\iota_4)\circ b=-b+[\iota_4,\iota_4]\circ H(b)\equiv-b$ modulo $D$. So $s_*(a,b)=(a,-b)$. For $\sigma$: $\sigma\circ j_1a=j_1a$ and $\sigma\circ j_2b=j_1(\eta_3\circ b)+j_2b$, because $\sigma(*,y)=(\eta_3(y),y)$. So $\sigma_*(a,b)=(a+\varepsilon(b),b)$.

Now $s^2=\mathrm{id}$, and $\sigma^2(x,y)=(x\eta_3(y)^2,y)\simeq\mathrm{id}$, since $\eta_3\cdot\eta_3=2\eta_3=0$ in $\pi_4(S^3)$. The commutator $c_0=s\sigma s^{-1}\sigma^{-1}$ lies in $\ker\pi$, because $G$ is abelian, and $\mathrm{pr}_2\circ c_0=\mathrm{pr}_2$ strictly (the second coordinate is reflected twice and shifted trivially), so the $Q$-component of $c_0$ is zero by Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit}. Thus $c_0=(a_0,0)$. Put $k=\mathrm{id}$ if $a_0=0$ and $k=(0,\nu_4)\in K$ if $a_0\ne0$, and $s'=sk$. Then $s'^2=s\,k\,s\,k=(s_*k)\,k=(0,-\nu_4)(0,\nu_4)=1$ or $s'^2=1$ trivially. Moreover
\[
[s',\sigma]=s\,(k\sigma k^{-1}\sigma^{-1})\,s^{-1}\cdot c_0=s_*\bigl((0,\nu_4)-\sigma_*(0,\nu_4)\bigr)+c_0=(\varepsilon(\nu_4),0)+(a_0,0)=0,
\]
using $\varepsilon(\nu_4)\ne0$ and $|\mathbb{Z}/2|=2$. So $s'$ and $\sigma$ are commuting involutions, and $\langle s',\sigma\rangle\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ maps isomorphically onto $G$. This is a splitting, and the action of $G$ on $K$ is the one stated. This proves (c).

<!-- theorem-9: body_md -->
\label{thm:SpSp}
Let $p\ge2$ and $M=S^p\times S^p$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The kernel of $\pi\colon\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)\to GL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is the group of pinch-fold twists, and
\[
\ker\pi\cong Q_p\oplus Q_p,\qquad Q_p:=\pi_{2p}(S^p)\big/[\pi_{p+1}(S^p),\iota_p].
\]
\item[(b)] The image of $\pi$ consists of the isometries $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ of $s_M$ with $ab\,[\iota_p,\iota_p]=0=cd\,[\iota_p,\iota_p]$. Explicitly, it is
\begin{itemize}
\item $\{\pm I,\pm\sigma_0\}$ for $p$ even,
\item $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ for $p=3,7$,
\item the theta group $\Gamma_\theta=\{A\in SL_2(\mathbb{Z})\mid A\equiv I\text{ or }\sigma_0\bmod 2\}$ for $p$ odd and $p\ne1,3,7$.
\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-9 -->
<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
\emph{(a)} The argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:S4S4}(b) applies verbatim, with $S^4$ replaced by $S^p$ and $\pi_8$ by $\pi_{2p}$. Let $f\in\ker\pi$ and $f=(f_1,f_2)$. Since $f$ induces the identity on $\pi_p(M)=\mathbb{Z}^2$ and a map out of $S^p\vee S^p$ is determined by its values on the two spheres, $f$ restricts to the inclusion on $S^p\vee S^p$, so $f_i$ and $\mathrm{pr}_i$ agree there. By Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit} with $Y=S^p$ and $g=\iota_p$ we have $f_i=b_i\cdot\mathrm{pr}_i$ for some $b_i\in\pi_{2p}(S^p)$, and the stabilizer of $\mathrm{pr}_i$ is $[\pi_{p+1}(S^p),\iota_p]$. Put $\tilde\kappa=j_1b_1+j_2b_2\in\pi_{2p}(M)$, where $j_i$ are the inclusions of the factors. As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:S3S4}, $f=t_{\tilde\kappa}$. Conversely, a twist $t_{\tilde\kappa}$ with $\tilde\kappa\in\pi_{2p}(M)$ restricts to the inclusion on $S^p\vee S^p$, has degree one because the Hurewicz image of $\tilde\kappa$ vanishes ($2p>p$), and is therefore a homotopy equivalence by Section~\ref{subsec:twists}. It lies in $\ker\pi$. The map $\Psi\colon\pi_{2p}(M)\to\ker\pi$, $\tilde\kappa\mapsto t_{\tilde\kappa}$, is a homomorphism by Lemma~\ref{lem:coaction}(b), because every class in $\pi_{2p}(M)=\pi_{2p}(S^p)^2$ is represented by a map into $S^p\vee S^p$. It is surjective, and $\Psi(\tilde\kappa)=\mathrm{id}$ if and only if both components $b_i$ lie in the stabilizer. Hence $\ker\Psi=[\pi_{p+1}(S^p),\iota_p]^{\oplus2}$ and $\ker\pi\cong Q_p\oplus Q_p$.

\emph{(b)} Let $f\in\operatorname{Aut}_\bullet(M)$. As $f$ has degree one, $\pi(f)=A$ is an isometry of $s_M$. We may take $f$ cellular. Write $K=S^p\vee S^p$ and $M=K\cup_we^{2p}$. Then $f|_K=g_A$, where $g_A$ sends $\iota_1\mapsto a\iota_1+c\iota_2$ and $\iota_2\mapsto b\iota_1+d\iota_2$. The pair $(M,K)$ is $(2p-1)$-connected and $K$ is $(p-1)$-connected, so by the Blakers--Massey theorem $\pi_{2p}(M,K)\to\pi_{2p}(M/K)=\mathbb{Z}$ is an isomorphism, as $2p\le 3p-2$. Hence the kernel of $\pi_{2p-1}(K)\to\pi_{2p-1}(M)$ is $\mathbb{Z}w$. Since $f$ is cellular, $g_A\circ w\in\mathbb{Z}w$, say $g_A\circ w=kw$, and $k$ is the degree of $f$, so $k=1$. By bilinearity of the Whitehead product on $\pi_p\times\pi_p$,
\[
g_A\circ w=[a\iota_1+c\iota_2,\,b\iota_1+d\iota_2]=ab\,[\iota_1,\iota_1]+ad\,[\iota_1,\iota_2]+cb\,[\iota_2,\iota_1]+cd\,[\iota_2,\iota_2].
\]
Now $[\iota_2,\iota_1]=(-1)^{p}[\iota_1,\iota_2]$, and $\pi_{2p-1}(K)=\pi_{2p-1}(S^p)\oplus\pi_{2p-1}(S^p)\oplus\mathbb{Z}\,w$ with $[\iota_i,\iota_i]$ the Whitehead square in the $i$-th summand. Comparing components, $g_A\circ w=w$ holds if and only if $ab[\iota_p,\iota_p]=0$, $cd[\iota_p,\iota_p]=0$ and $ad+(-1)^pbc=1$. The last condition says that $f$ has degree one. Conversely, if these three conditions hold then $g_A$ extends to a map $f_A\colon M\to M$, which acts by $A$ on $H^p$ and by $ad\pm bc=1$ on $H^{2p}$, because $x_i^2=0$ in $H^*(M)$. So $f_A$ is a homology isomorphism of simply connected spaces, hence a based homotopy equivalence of degree one with $\pi(f_A)=A$. This proves the description of the image as a set of matrices.

For $p$ even $[\iota_p,\iota_p]$ has infinite order, so the conditions say $ab=cd=0$ and $ad+bc=1$. If $a=0$ then $bc=1$ and $d=0$, and if $b=0$ then $ad=1$ and $c=0$. This gives $\{\pm I,\pm\sigma_0\}$, and these are isometries of $s_M$. For $p=3,7$ the Whitehead square vanishes and the image is all of $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$. For the remaining odd $p$ the Whitehead square is non-zero of order $2$, and the conditions say that $ab$ and $cd$ are even. An integer matrix of determinant $1$ whose rows $(a,b)$ and $(c,d)$ both have even product is, modulo $2$, one of $I$ and $\sigma_0$. This is the theta group $\Gamma_\theta$.
