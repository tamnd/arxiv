<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{MainThm}
	Let $\Sigma$ be a (necessarily) non-orientable compact minimal surface bounded by $C$ of Morse index two.  The following is true:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item If $\Sigma$ is embedded, then $|e(\Sigma)|\leq g(\Sigma)+1$ while if $\Sigma$ is immersed and  embedded near $C$ and $g(\Sigma)=1$, then $|e(\Sigma)|=2$;
			\item If $g(\Sigma)=1$, then $1\leq \mathrm{null}_D(\Sigma)\leq 3$ and, moreover, $\mathrm{null}_D(\Sigma)=1$ when and only when $\Sigma$ is, up to symmetry, equal to $\Sigma_{LB}$;
		\item If $g(\Sigma)=1$, i.e., $\Sigma$ is a M\"{o}bius band, and $|\kappa|\geq 1$, then $\Sigma$ is, up to symmetry, equal to $\Sigma_{LB}$;
						\item If $\Sigma$ is embedded, then $g(\Sigma)\neq 2$, i.e., there are no embedded minimal punctured Klein bottles of index two.
	\end{enumerate}
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The first claim of Item (1) is an immediate consequence of Proposition \ref{NodalCountWProp}.  To see the second claim observe that by Proposition \ref{NodalCountWProp}, when $g(\Sigma)=1$, $|e(\Sigma)|\leq 2$.  As $\Sigma$ is embedded near $C$, $e(\Sigma)$ is even.   By Proposition \ref{NoWindZeroProp}, $e(\Sigma)\neq 0$ concluding the proof.
	
Item (2) follows from Propositions \ref{JacobiNodalProp} and \ref{NullProp}.   	Item (3) is shown in Proposition \ref{NoSlowSpeedCharProp}.   Finally, Item (4) is shown in Theorem \ref{NoKBInd2Thm}.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{IndLowBndProp} If $\Sigma$ is not a hemisphere, then
	$$\mathrm{ind}_D(\Sigma)\geq 2.$$
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $\mathrm{ind}_D(\Sigma)\leq 1$, then there must be a non-trivial linear relation
	$$
	a \mathbf{e}_1^\perp+b\mathbf{e}_2^\perp=0, a^2+b^2 =1.
	$$
	Taking the cone over $\Sigma$ gives a minimal cone, $\mathcal{C}$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$ bounded by the plane $x_3=x_4=0$. This cone, $\mathcal{C}$ contains the line spanned by $a \mathbf{e}_1+b\mathbf{e}_2$, i.e., the cone splits off a line.  One has $\Sigma=\mathcal{C}\cap \mathbb{S}^3$ is smooth only when $\mathcal{C}$ is a half-plane in which case $\Sigma$ is a hemisphere.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
Let $\Gamma$ be a, possibly immersed, oriented minimal surface bounded by $C$.  The following is true:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item \label{HS} If $\Gamma$ is embedded, then $\Gamma$ is a hemisphere;
		\item \label{Almgren} If $\Gamma$ has (oriented) genus zero, i.e., is topologically a disk, then $\Gamma$ is a hemisphere;
		\item \label{IndOrient} If $\Gamma$ is not a hemisphere, then $\mathrm{ind}_D(\Gamma)\geq 3$.
	\end{enumerate}
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Item \eqref{HS} was shown by Hardt-Simon \cite{Hardt2011}.  For Item \eqref{Almgren} we note that we may Schwarz reflect $\Gamma$ to obtain a minimal immersion of $\mathbb{S}^2$ into $\mathbb{S}^3$.  By \cite{almgrenInteriorRegularityTheorems1966} (cf. \cite{calabiMinimalImmersionsSurfaces1967}) this is an equatorial sphere and so $\Gamma$ is a hemisphere.  Finally, for \eqref{IndOrient} we note that as $\Gamma$ is oriented, there is a well-defined choice of unit normal on $\Gamma$ and so the functions $n_1$ and $n_2$ are well defined eigenfunctions.  Unless $\Gamma$ is a hemisphere they are linearly independent.  Hence, if $\Gamma$ is not a hemisphere, the multiplicity of the $-2$ eigenvalue of $-L_\Gamma$ is at least two and so there must be a eigenvalue $\mu<-2$ of $-L_{\Gamma}$ with corresponding eigenfunction that does not change sign.  That is, when $\Gamma$ is not a hemisphere, $\mathrm{ind}_D(\Gamma)\geq 3$.

\label{OrientedProp} For $f_\theta=\cos \theta x_3+\sin \theta x_4$, consider the open subset $H_\theta=\left\{f_\theta>0\right\}\cap \Sigma$ of $\Sigma$.  The sets $H_\theta$ are non-empty and
when $\Sigma$ is embedded, they are orientable.
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Without loss of generality we may consider $\theta=0$ so $f_\theta=x_3$.  If $H_0=\left\{x_3>0\right\}\cap \Sigma$ were empty, then by the strict maximum principle $\Sigma\subset \left\{x_3=0\right\}$ which is only possible if $\Sigma$ were a hemisphere which is excluded.  
	
	Now suppose $\Sigma$ is embedded.  Fix a closed curve $\sigma\subset H_0$.  Choose $\epsilon>0$ small so that $\sigma \subset B_{\pi-\epsilon}^{\mathbb{S}}(\mathbf{e}_3)$ and so  $\partial B_{\pi-\epsilon}^{\mathbb{S}}(\mathbf{e}_3)$ meets $\Sigma$ transversally.  Let $\hat{\Sigma}$ be the component of $\Sigma \cap  B_{\pi-\epsilon}^{\mathbb{S}}(\mathbf{e}_3)$ containing $\sigma$ --  this is an embedded submanifold with boundary.  As  $\partial B_{\pi-\epsilon}^{\mathbb{S}}(\mathbf{e}_3)$ is topologically $\mathbb{S}^2$ and $\partial \hat{\Sigma}\subset \partial B_{\pi-\epsilon}^{\mathbb{S}}(\mathbf{e}_3)$ is a 1-cycle, there is a subset $S\subset \partial B_{\pi-\epsilon}^{\mathbb{S}}(\mathbf{e}_3)$ so $\partial S=   \partial\hat{\Sigma}$.  By rounding out the corners of $S\cup \hat{\Sigma}$ one obtains an embedded closed submanifold of $\mathbb{S}^3$ that contains $\sigma$. 
 and which we continue to denote by $\hat{\Sigma}$.	It follows from Alexander's theorem that $\hat{\Sigma}$ is two-sided and so there is a well defined choice of unit normal to $\hat{\Sigma}$.  Hence, there is a consistent choice of normal along $\sigma$ and so $H_0$ is two-sided and orientable.

<!-- prop-4: body_md -->
\label{x3nodalcountprop}
	Let $f_\theta=\cos \theta x_3+\sin \theta x_4$ and suppose $\Sigma$ has Morse index two.
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item When $\Sigma$ is embedded, $f_\theta$ has exactly two nodal domains.  
		\item When $\Sigma$ is immersed,    $f_\theta$ has between two and $g(\Sigma)+2$ nodal domains.  If the number is strictly larger than two, then at least one domain is non-orientable.
	\end{enumerate}
<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By Proposition \ref{OrientedProp}, the sets $\left\{f_\theta>0\right\}$ and $\left\{f_{\theta}<0\right\}=\left\{f_{\theta+\pi}>0\right\}$ must both be non-empty so there are at least two nodal domains.  If $\Sigma$ is embedded, then by Proposition \ref{OrientedProp} each component of both sets is orientable.  When $\Sigma$ is immersed, then at most $g(\Sigma)$ components of the union of the two sets may be non-orientable with the rest orientable.
	
	Consider the function $f'=f_\theta\circ \pi$.  This is an even Dirichlet eigenfunction of $\Delta_{\Sigma'}$ with eigenvalue $-2$.  If $U'$ is a nodal domain of $f'$, i.e., a component of $\Sigma'\setminus \left\{f'=0\right\}$, then so is $\zeta (U')$ and $U=\pi(U')$ is a nodal domain of $f_\theta$.  In particular, if $U$ is orientable, then $U'\neq \zeta(U')$, while if $U$ is non-orientable, then $U'=\zeta(U')$.
	
	When $\Sigma$ is embedded each nodal domain of $f_{\theta}$ is orientable and so if there are more than two of them, then $f'$ has at least six nodal domains and none are fixed by $\zeta$.  The same is true when $\Sigma$ is immersed and all the nodal domains are orientable.  Otherwise,  there exists at least one non-orientable domain of $f_{\theta}$ and at most $g(\Sigma)$ of them.  In particular,  if the number of nodal domains is more than $g(\Sigma)+2$, then there are at least three such domains and so $f'$ has at least six nodal domains not fixed by $\zeta$.
		In all cases we may choose three of the domains $\Omega_1, \Omega_2, \Omega_3$ of $f'$ so $\Omega_{-i}=\zeta(\Omega_i)$ form a distinct set.  That is, there are six distinct nodal domains $\Omega_{\pm i}$, $1\leq i \leq 3$ of $f'$ which are involuted by $\zeta$.
	
	For $1\leq i \leq 3$, consider the linearly independent odd functions 
	$$f'_i=\left\{\begin{array}{cc} f' & \Omega_i \\ -f' & \Omega_{-i} \\ 0 & \mbox{otherwise} \end{array} \right.
	$$
	We note that as $\Sigma$ is not totally geodesic, $|A_\Sigma|^2$ is non-zero away from isolated points.
	Hence, integrating by parts  implies
	$$
	\int_{{\Sigma}'}  |\nabla_{\Sigma'} f'_i|^2-|A_{\Sigma'}|^2 (f_i')^2 -2 (f_i')^2< \int_{{\Sigma}'}  |\nabla_{\Sigma'} f'_i|^2-2 (f_i')^2 =0
	$$
	and,	for $i\neq j$,
	$$
	\int_{\Sigma'} \nabla_\Sigma' f'_i \nabla_\Sigma' f'_j -|A_{\Sigma'}|^2 f_i' f_j'- 2 f_i' f_j' =0.
	$$

	
	Thus, by the variational characterization of eigenvalues, there are three odd eigenfunctions of $L_{\Sigma'}$,  $i=1,2,3$  with eigenvalues $\lambda_i<0$.  That is $\dim \mathcal{U}'_-\geq 3$ which contradicts the index hypothesis

<!-- thm-4: body_md -->
If $\Sigma$ is any embedded non-orientable minimal surface with boundary $C$, then $\Sigma \setminus \left\{f_\theta=0\right\}$ has exactly two components.
<!-- end thm-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By Proposition \ref{OrientedProp}, there are at least two components of $\Sigma \setminus \left\{f_\theta=0\right\}$ and all of them are orientable.  As there are no closed minimal surfaces in $\mathbb{S}^3\setminus \left\{f_\theta=0\right\}$, all the components have boundary that lie on the sphere $\left\{f_\theta=0\right\}$.   Hence, as $\Sigma^+=\left\{f_\theta>0\right\}\cap \Sigma$ is orientable we may fix an orientation on it so that the induced orientation on $\partial \Sigma_+$ is consistent with that induced from the sphere $\left\{f_\theta=0\right\}$.  As such, $\Sigma_+  $ splits $\mathbb{S}^3 \cap \left\{f_\theta>0\right\}$ into two regions $\Omega_\pm$ with $\partial \Omega_\pm \subset \left\{f_\theta=0\right\}\cup \Sigma_+$ labeled so $\Omega_+$ induces the fixed orientation $\Sigma_+$ while $\Omega_-$ induces the opposite one.

Pick a component, $\Gamma$, of $\Sigma^+$.  We note that $\Gamma$ is unstable which can be seen by making a choice of unit normal (which is well defined since $\Gamma$ is orientable) and observing that the second variation is negative when one plugs in $f_\theta$.   As $\Gamma$ is oriented, we may think of it as an integral current and so $\gamma=\partial \Gamma$ is supported in $\bar{\Omega}_\pm$. By the direct method there is a least area integral current $\Upsilon$ in $\bar{\Omega}_+$ with $\partial \Upsilon=\gamma$. By Solomon-White's maximum principle \cite{SolWhite} and the fact that there are no closed minimal surfaces in $\left\{f_\theta>0\right\}$, either $\mathrm{spt}(\Upsilon)\subset \partial \Omega_+$ or $\ensuremath{\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}} (\Upsilon)\setminus \ensuremath{\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}} (\gamma)\subset \Omega_+$,  In the latter case, $\ensuremath{\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}} (\Upsilon)$ is a smooth stable minimal surface in $\Omega_+$ by standard regularity theory, but this is not possible for the same reason $\Gamma$ is unstable.  For the same reason, $\ensuremath{\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}} (\Upsilon)$ is disjoint from $\Sigma_+$ and so we must have $\ensuremath{\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}} (\Upsilon)\subset \left\{f_\theta=0\right\}$.  The same is true in $\Omega_-$.  It follows that $\Gamma$ is the only component of $\Sigma^+$ as any of the other components would act as a barrier.  Arguing in the same way for $f_{\theta+\pi}$ gives the result.

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{NodalCountWProp} Suppose $\Sigma$ is a non-orientable minimal surface bounded by $C$ of index two. 
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item If $\Sigma$ is embedded, then $|e(\Sigma)|\leq g(\Sigma)+1$;
		\item If $\Sigma$ is immersed, then $|e(\Sigma)|\leq 2g(\Sigma)$.
     \end{enumerate}  
     In particular, an immersed minimal M\"obius band of index two has $|e(\Sigma)|\leq 2$.
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Consider the hemispheres $H_\pm$ given by $x_3=0$ and $\pm x_4\geq 0$.  The number of times the outward conormal $\nu$ points into $H_+$ is bounded from below by $|e(\Sigma)|$ and the same is true of $H_-$.  It follows that there are at least  $2|e(\Sigma)|$ points where $\nabla_\Sigma x_3=0$ on $C$.  Consider the graph associated to the nodal set of $x_3$ so we have $|v_\partial|\geq 2|e(\Sigma)|$.  Hence, it follows from \eqref{EulerIneqSigEqn} and \eqref{EdgeEst} that
$$
|f|-|e(\Sigma)|\geq |f|-|e|+|v|\geq 1-g(\Sigma).
$$

When $\Sigma$ is embedded and index two, Proposition \ref{x3nodalcountprop} implies $|f|=2$ and so
$$
g(\Sigma)+1\geq |e(\Sigma)|.
$$
When $\Sigma$ is an immersed and each face is a disk the same bound holds and we may use that $g(\Sigma)\geq 1$ implies $2g(\Sigma)\geq g(\Sigma)+1$ to obtain the second claim in this case.  Finally, when $\Sigma$ is immersed and the number of faces is more than three, then at least one is not a disk and Proposition \ref{x3nodalcountprop} implies $|f|\leq g(\Sigma)+2$ while the strictness of the Euler inequality implies
$$
2g(\Sigma)\geq |e(\Sigma)|.
$$

<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{OddEigenFuncNDLem}
	Suppose $g(\Sigma)=1$ and  $u\in C^\infty({\Sigma'})$ is a non-trivial odd eigenfunction of $L_{\Sigma'}$ with Dirichlet boundary conditions.  The number of nodal domains of $u$ is an even integer.   Moreover, if each nodal domain is topologically a disk, then the number is either two or at least six.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let $Z'=\left\{u=0\right\}$ be the nodal set of $u$ which is a finite graph.  As $u\circ \zeta=-u$,  $\zeta(Z')=Z'$ so $Z=\pi (Z')\subset \Sigma$ is a well defined graph as well.  We note that if $U'$ is a component of ${\Sigma'}\setminus Z'$ on which $u>0$ one has $u<0$ on $\zeta(U')$.  This means there are an even number of components of ${\Sigma'}\setminus Z'$, i.e., an even number of nodal domains.  Another property is that if $p\in \partial U' \setminus \partial {\Sigma'}\subset Z'$, then any neighborhood of $p$ meets a nodal domain other than $U'$.
	
	Now suppose all the nodal domains are topological disks. We treat two cases.  The first is that one of the choices of $U'$ has the property that $\pi|_{\bar{U}'}$ is not a homeomorphism onto its image.  As $\pi$ is a closed continuous map, this means there are distinct points $p_\pm\in \bar{U}_+'$ with $\pi(p_+)=\pi(p_-)$.  Connecting $p_-$ to $p_+$ by some path ${\Gamma}$ in $\bar{U}'$ one has that $\gamma=\pi\circ\Gamma$ is a closed curve in $\Sigma$ homotopic to the core of the M\"obius band.  It follows that $\Sigma \setminus \pi(\bar{U}')$ and hence also $\Sigma \setminus(\pi(\bar{U}'\cup Z'))$ consists of planar domains.  Moreover,  this set is either empty, an annulus or has at least two connected components.  The number of components of ${\Sigma'}\setminus ( U'\cup \zeta(U')\cup Z')$ is at least twice the number of components of $\Sigma \setminus \pi(\bar{U}'\cup Z')$ and so is at least four unless $\Sigma \setminus \pi(\bar{U}'\cup Z')$ is empty or an annulus.  However, disks can't cover annuli so either the set is empty or has at least four components.  That is, $u$ has either two nodal domains or at least six.
	
	In the second case we may assume that  $\pi|_{\bar{U}'}$ is a homeomorphism onto its image for each nodal domain $U'$.  Suppose for the sake of contradiction there are four components of ${\Sigma'}\setminus Z'$. This implies there are two components $U_\pm$ of $\Sigma\setminus Z$.  Now let $U_\pm'$ denote the two nodal domains of $u$ on which $u>0$ labeled so that $\pi(U_\pm')=U_\pm$.  By construction, $U_+'$ cannot share an edge with $U_-'$.   Moreover,  $U_+'$ cannot share an edge with $\zeta (U_+')$ as if $p'$ was a point on such an edge, then $\zeta(p')$ would also have this property.  That is $p',\zeta(p')\in \bar{U}_+'$ contradicting the hypothesis that $\pi$ was injective on $\bar{U}_+'$.  Hence, $U_+'$ may share an edge only with $\zeta(U_-')$ and vice versa.  Likewise, $U_-'$ may share an edge only with $\zeta(U_+')$.
	
	Now let $v$ be a vertex of $Z'$, i.e., a point where $u$ has a critical point.  We claim any sufficiently small neighborhood of $v$ meets only $V_+'=U_+'\cup \zeta(U_-')$ or $V_-'=U_-'\cup \zeta (U_+')$ but not both.  Indeed, if this is not true then one can find a point $p'$ near $v$ on an edge of $Z'$ that meets both $V_+'$ and $V_-'$ but this is not possible by the previous paragraph.   It follows that $\bar{V}_+'$ is disjoint from $\bar{V}_-'$, but both are non-empty sets and so this is only possible if $\Sigma'$ is disconnected which is the desired contradiction.

<!-- prop-6: body_md -->
\label{JacobiNodalProp}
	If $g(\Sigma)=1$ and $\Sigma$ has Morse index two, then $\mathrm{null}_D(\Sigma)\leq 3$.
<!-- end prop-6 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let $C_+'$ be one of the components of $\partial {\Sigma'}$ which can be identified with the great circle $C$.
	
	If $\mathrm{null}_D(\Sigma)=\dim \mathcal{D}'_->3$ is strictly greater than three, then, by the Calderon unique continuation theorem, there is a non-zero element $u\in \mathcal{D}'_-$ so that on $C_+'$, 
	$$
	\int_{C_+'} \partial_\nu u ds=\int_{C_+'} \partial_\nu u \cos s ds = \int_{C_+'} \partial_\nu u \sin s  ds=0
	$$
	where here $s$ is the arclength parameter of $C_+'$.
	That is the outward normal derivative of $u$ is orthogonal to the constant function and the two simplest trigonometric polynomials.
	
	It follows by the Sturm-Hurwitz theorem that $\partial_\nu u$ has at least four zeros on $C_+$.  As $\zeta$ maps $C_+'$ to $C_-'$, the other component of $\partial \Sigma$, and $u$ is odd the same is true on $C_-'$.  Hence, $\nabla_{\Sigma'} u$ vanishes on at least eight points on  $\partial {\Sigma'}$.
	
	Let us denote by $v_\partial'$ the critical points of $u$ on $\partial {\Sigma'}$ so $|v_\partial'|\geq 8$.
	It follows from \eqref{EdgeEst} and the Euler inequality \eqref{EulerIneqSigPrimeEqn} that the number of nodal domains, $|f'|$ of $u$ satisfies
	$$
	|f'|-4\geq	|f'|-\frac{1}{2}|v_\partial'|\geq |f'|-|e'|+|v'|\geq \chi({\Sigma'})=0.
	$$
	Moreover, this inequality is strict unless each face, i.e., each component of ${\Sigma'}\setminus Z'$ is a disk.  That is, either
	$
	|f'|>4$
	or $|f'|=4$ and each face is a disk.  However, the latter is ruled out by Lemma \ref{OddEigenFuncNDLem}.  Hence, as $u$ is odd, the number of faces is even and so we conclude that $|f'|\geq 6$.   Arguing as in the proof of Proposition \ref{NodalCountWProp} implies there are at least three odd Dirichlet eigenfunctions of $L_{\Sigma'}$ with negative eigenvalue which contradicts the index assumption.

<!-- prop-7: body_md -->
\label{NoWindZeroProp}
	If $g(\Sigma)=1$, then $e(\Sigma)\neq 0$.
<!-- end prop-7 -->
<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
In general, if $\Sigma$ is embedded, and not necessarily minimal, one has $e(\Sigma)\neq 0$.  However,  an appropriate modification of Boy's surface with a small disk removed shows there are immersed M\"{o}bius bands with winding zero and so minimality is essential.
<!-- end rem-1 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Consider the doubled surface of $\Sigma$, obtained by Schwarz reflection across $C$,  which we denote by $\hat{\Sigma}$.  This is a minimal immersion of a Klein bottle into $\mathbb{S}^3$.  Taking the double cover gives $\hat{\Sigma}'$ which is a minimal immersion of a torus.  It is a standard result, see for instant, \cite{almgrenInteriorRegularityTheorems1966}, that the second fundamental form is the real part of a holomorphic quadratic differential. Since, $\hat{\Sigma}'$ is not a sphere we have that $|A_{\Sigma}|$ does not identically vanish.  As $\hat{\Sigma}'$ has genus one, it follows that $|A_\Sigma|$ is nowhere vanishing.  In particular, $\kappa\neq 0$ from which the claim follows.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{ComputationLem}
	On $C'=\partial \Sigma'$ one has (up to sign)
	$$
	\nabla_{\Sigma'} R_{12}= S_{\Sigma'}(\mathbf{T})=A_{\Sigma'}(\mathbf{T},\nu ) \nu=\kappa\nu \mbox{ and }	\nabla_{\Sigma'} R_{34}= \nu.
	$$
	Likewise,
	$$
	\nabla^2 R_{12}(X,Y)=-(\nabla_{\mathbf{T}} A_{\Sigma'})(X,Y)=(\mathbf{T}\cdot \kappa)((\mathbf{T}\cdot X) (\mathbf{v} \cdot Y))+(\mathbf{T}\cdot Y) (\mathbf{v} \cdot X))
	$$
	while
	$$
	\nabla^2 R_{34} (X,Y)= 0.
	$$
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Write $\partial \Sigma'=C'=C'_+\cup C'_-$.   We compute that, up to relabelling, on $C'_+=\partial \Sigma'$
	$$
	\nabla_\Sigma x_1= x_2 \mathbf{T}, \nabla_\Sigma x_2 =-x_1 \mathbf{T}.
	$$
and
$$
	\nabla_\Sigma x_3= \cos \theta(s) \nu= n_4 \nu, \nabla_{\Sigma} x_4=\sin \theta(s) \nu =-n_3 \nu.
	$$
As such
$$
\nabla_{\Sigma'} R_{12}= (x_2 n_2 +x_1 n_1)\mathbf{T}-S_{\Sigma'}( x_1 \mathbf{e}_2^\top - x_2 \mathbf{e}_1^\top)= S_{\Sigma'}(\mathbf{T})= \kappa \nu.
$$ 
Similarly, 
$$
\nabla_{\Sigma'} R_{34} =-S_{\Sigma'}(x_3 \mathbf{e}_4^\top - x_4 \mathbf{e}_3^\top) +(n_4^2 + n_3^2) \nu= \nu.
$$

We have on $C'$
$$
\nabla^2_{\Sigma'} R_{12}(\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{T})= \mathbf{T}\cdot (\mathbf{T}\cdot R_{12}))- \nabla^{\Sigma'}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathbf{T} \cdot R_{12}=0.
$$
As $L_{\Sigma'} R_{12}=0$, and $R_{12}=0$ on $C'$, it follows that
$$
\nabla^2_{\Sigma'} R_{12}(\nu, \nu)=0.
$$
Using the previous computations we have
$$
\nabla^2_{\Sigma'} R_{12}(\mathbf{T}, \nu)= \mathbf{T}\cdot \partial_\nu R_{12}- \nabla^{\Sigma'}_{\mathbf{T}} \nu \cdot R_{12}=  \mathbf{T}\cdot \kappa
$$
Where we used that $C$ was a geodesic so $\nabla^{\Sigma'}_{\mathbf{T}} \nu=0$.
This gives the formula for $\nabla^2_{\Sigma'} R_{12}$.

For the same reason
$$

	\begin{enumerate}
		\item $\kappa$ has critical points only on the set of endpoints of the $M_i$;
		\item $\kappa$ has a critical point off of the set of endpoints of the $M_i$.
	\end{enumerate}

$$
|f|-2N \geq 2-2g(\Sigma)
$$
and the inequality is strict unless all the domains are disks.   When $g(\Sigma)=1$, the index hypothesis and Lemma \ref{OddEigenFuncNDLem} imply either $|f|= 2$ or $|f|\leq 4$ and the inequality is strict.  That is,

For $g(\Sigma)\geq 2$, the index hypothesis implies $|f|\leq 4$ so 
$$
2g(\Sigma)\geq 2N-2
$$
which gives the claimed bound.
	
	In the second case, on the oriented double cover we see that $\kappa$ has at least $2N$ critical points on $C'_+$. 	$$
	|f| -4N\geq 2- 2g(\Sigma)
	$$

		\end{align*}
	\end{enumerate}

	b R_{12}+c R_{34}=0

	$$
	b + c e^{2\omega}=0.
	$$
	This implies $\omega$ is constant along $x=0$.  Conversely, if $\omega=c_0$ is constant along $x=0$, then setting $b=-1$ and $c=e^{-2c_0}$ yields
	$$
	\partial_\nu (b R_{12}+c R_{34})=0,
	$$
	along $x=0$.
	By Calderon unique continuation we then have $b R_{12}+c R_{34}=0$ so $R_{12}$ and $R_{34}$ are linearly dependent.

	$$
	v_1=b R_{12}+cR_{34}
	$$

	$$
	\mathbf{F}_k(x,y)= (\cos y\cos x, \sin y \cos x, \cos 2k y \sin x, \sin 2k y \sin x).
	$$
	In the coordinates $x$ and $y$ we may express the stability operator of $\bar{\tau}_{1,2k}'$ as
	$$
	L_{\bar{\tau}_{1,2k}'} f= \partial_x^2 f+ a_k(x) \partial_x f + b_k(x) \partial_y^2 f + V_k(x) f
	$$
	where
	$$
	a_k(x)=\frac{\left(4k ^2-1\right) \sin (2 x)}{4k ^2+1-\left(4k ^2-1\right) \cos (2 x)}
	$$
	$$
	b_k(x)=\frac{2}{4 k^2+1-\left(4 k^2-1\right) \cos (2 x)}
	$$
	and
	$$
	V_k(x)=2 + \frac{32k^2}{\left(4k^2+1-\left(4k^2-1\right) \cos (2 x)\right)^2}.
	$$
	In particular, we expect to find a basis of odd eigenfunctions of the eigenspace of $-L_{\bar{\tau}_{1,2k}'}$ associated to $\lambda$ of the form
	$$
	f(x,y)=f_n(x) \cos ny \mbox{ or }  f(x,y)=g_n(x) \sin n y
	$$
	where $f_n$ (and $g_n$) is even in the sense that $f_n(\pi-x)=f_n(x)$ when $n$ is odd and odd in the sense that $f_n(\pi-x)=-f_n(x)$ when $n$ is even.  Moreover, $f_n$ (and  $g_n$) satisfy
	$$
	\partial_x^2 u+ a_k(x) \partial_x u -n^2  b_k(x)  u+ V_k(x) u+\lambda u=0.
	$$
	One may verify that this is a Sturm-Liouville eigenvalue problem associated to the energy
	$$
	E_n^k[u]= \int_{0}^\pi \left( |u'|^2 +(n^2 b_k(x)- V_k(x)) u^2 \right) w_k(x)dx
	$$
	with Dirichlet boundary conditions  where here
	$$
	w_k(x)=\sqrt{4k^2+1-\left(4 k^2-1\right) \cos (2 x)}.
	$$

	$$R_{12}=  \frac{\sqrt{2} k\sin (2 x)}{\sqrt{4k^2+1-\left(4k^2-1\right) \cos (2 x)}}=f_0(x)
	$$

	We note that $f_0$ has exactly two nodal domains, $(0, \frac{\pi}{2})$ and $(\frac{\pi}{2}, \pi)$. It readily follows from Sturm-Liouville theory and the fact that $b_k(x)\geq 0$, that any function $u$ that is odd and any $n\geq 0$ even 
	$$
	E_n^k[u]\geq E_0^k[f_0] = 0
	$$

	For $n$ odd, we note that if $f_n$ is a non-trivial even eigenfunction associated to $E_n$ that changes sign, then it cannot have a zero at $x=\pi/2$.  This is because evenness implies $f_n'(\frac{\pi}{2})=0$.  Moreover, if $f_n$ has any zero in $(0,\frac{\pi}{2})$ then the associated odd eigenfunction $f_n(x) \cos n y$ to $L_{\bar{\tau}_{1,2k}'}$ has a nodal domain contained inside $[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]\times [0, 2\pi]$ and so by domain monotonicity cannot contribute to the index or nullity.
