<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:modulus}
A function \(\vartheta:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)\) is a modulus of continuity
if it is nondecreasing, \(\vartheta(0)=0\), and
\(\lim_{t\downarrow0}\vartheta(t)=0\).  It is a Dini modulus if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dini}
 [\vartheta]_{\mathrm{Dini}}
 :=
 \int_0^1\vartheta(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}<\infty.
\end{equation}
For a continuously differentiable map \(F\), put
\begin{equation*}
 \vartheta_F(t)
 :=
 \sup\bigl\{\|DF(x)-DF(y)\|:|x-y|\leq t\bigr\},
 \qquad 0\leq t\leq1.
\end{equation*}
We write \(F\in C^{1,\mathrm{Dini}}\) if \(\vartheta_F\) is a Dini
modulus.  If \(F\) is defined on an interval, the supremum is taken
over that interval.  If its domain is a parameter circle, we use the
cyclic distance.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:regular-curve}
A compact curve \(\Gamma\subset\mathbb R^n\) is a regular
\(C^{1,\mathrm{Dini}}\) curve if
$
 \Gamma=\Phi([a,b]),
$
where \(\Phi\) is parametrized by arc length,
\(\Phi\in C^{1,\mathrm{Dini}}([a,b];\mathbb R^n)\), and \(\Phi\) is one-to-one,
apart from the possible equality \(\Phi(a)=\Phi(b)\) when the curve is
closed.  Thus
$
 |\Phi'(t)|=1,
 \ t\in[a,b].
$
In the closed case, we also require
\(\Phi'(a)=\Phi'(b)\), and we regard \(\Phi\) as a regular
\(C^{1,\mathrm{Dini}}\) embedding of
\(\mathbb R/(b-a)\mathbb Z\).  The derivative modulus is then computed using
the cyclic distance on this parameter circle.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let \(0\leq\alpha<n\).  Let \(\sigma\) and \(\omega\) be locally finite
positive Borel measures on \(\mathbb R^n\), possibly with common point masses.
Suppose that at least one of the measures is supported on a regular
\(C^{1,\mathrm{Dini}}\) curve.  Then \(\|\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}_\sigma f\|_{L^2(\omega)} \leq \mathfrak N_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}} \|f\|_{L^2(\sigma)}\) if and only if the following conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the two one-tailed \(\mathcal A_2^\alpha\) conditions with holes;
\item the two punctured \(A_2^\alpha\) conditions;
\item the testing conditions for \(\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}\) and its adjoint
over all rotated cubes.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm-comparison}
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}
 \approx
 \sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}^{\mathrm{dual}}.
\end{equation}
The constants depend on \(n\), \(\alpha\), and the fixed finite graph
cover of the curve.  More precisely, they may depend on the number of
graph pieces, their collar and separation constants, the biLipschitz
constants of the graph maps, and the Dini integrals of their derivative
moduli.  They may also depend on the fixed cutoff, the fixed goodness
exponent, and the resulting choices of \(A_0\), \(\gamma\), and \(r\).
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:dyadic-Dini}
Let $\vartheta$ be a nondecreasing modulus.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-Dini}
 \sum_{k=0}^\infty \vartheta(2^{-k})
 \approx
 \vartheta(1)+\int_0^1\vartheta(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}.
\end{equation}
For each fixed $c\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dilated-Dini}
 \sum_{k=0}^\infty
 \vartheta\bigl(\min\{1,c2^{-k}\}\bigr)
 \leq C_c\left(\vartheta(1)+[\vartheta]_{\mathrm{Dini}}\right).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For $k\geq0$ and $2^{-k-1}<t\leq2^{-k}$, monotonicity gives \(\vartheta(2^{-k-1}) \leq \vartheta(t) \leq \vartheta(2^{-k})\). Integration with respect to $dt/t$ and summation in $k$ give
\eqref{eq:dyadic-Dini}.  To obtain \eqref{eq:dilated-Dini}, choose an
integer $m_c$ such that $c\leq2^{m_c}$.  The terms with $k\leq m_c$ are
bounded by $(m_c+1)\vartheta(1)$, and the remaining sum is bounded by a
shift of the sum in \eqref{eq:dyadic-Dini}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:Taylor-Dini}
Let $F\in C^1(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb R^n)$.  If $|x-y|\leq1$, then \(|F(y)-F(x)-DF(x)(y-x)| \leq |x-y|\,\vartheta_F(|x-y|)\).
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By the fundamental theorem of calculus,
\[
F(y)-F(x)-DF(x)(y-x)
 =\int_0^1\bigl(DF(x+t(y-x))-DF(x)\bigr)(y-x)\,dt.
\]
The norm of the integrand is at most
$\vartheta_F(|x-y|)|x-y|$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:graph-modulus}
Let \(\Gamma:[c,d]\to\mathbb R^n\) be parametrized by arc length.  Suppose,
after a rotation, that
\[
 \Gamma_1'(s)\geq c_0>0,
 \qquad c\leq s\leq d.
\]
Write \(u=\Gamma_1(s)\), and let \(\Gamma(s)=(u,g(u))\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:graph-modulus}
 \vartheta_g(r)
 \leq C_{c_0}\vartheta_\Gamma(C_{c_0}r),
 \qquad
 0<r\leq C_{c_0}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
In particular, \(g\in C^{1,\mathrm{Dini}}\) whenever
\(\Gamma\in C^{1,\mathrm{Dini}}\).
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since \(u'(s)=\Gamma_1'(s)\geq c_0\), the inverse function
\(s=s(u)\) satisfies \(|s(u)-s(v)|\leq c_0^{-1}|u-v|\). Moreover, \(g'(u) =\frac{\Gamma_\perp'(s(u))}{\Gamma_1'(s(u))}\). The denominators are at least \(c_0\), and
\(|\Gamma'(s)|=1\).  Hence \(|g'(u)-g'(v)| \leq C_{c_0} |\Gamma'(s(u))-\Gamma'(s(v))| \leq C_{c_0} \vartheta_\Gamma(c_0^{-1}|u-v|)\). Choose \(C_{c_0}\geq\max\{1,c_0^{-1}\}\) large enough that the
preceding estimate has the form in \eqref{eq:graph-modulus}.  If
\(0<r\leq C_{c_0}^{-1}\), then \(C_{c_0}r\leq1\), so the argument of
\(\vartheta_\Gamma\) lies in its defining interval \([0,1]\).
This proves \eqref{eq:graph-modulus}.  For
\(C_{c_0}^{-1}\leq r\leq1\), we have
\(\vartheta_g(r)\leq2\|g'\|_\infty\).  A change of variables on the
remaining part of the Dini integral proves the last assertion.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:graph-extension}
Let \(I^*\) be a bounded open interval, and let
\(\widehat I\Subset I^*\).  Assume that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-collar}
 \operatorname{dist}(\widehat I,\mathbb R\setminus I^*)
 \geq \kappa |I^*|
\end{equation}
for some \(\kappa>0\).  Let
\(g:I^*\to\mathbb R^{n-1}\) be \(C^{1,\mathrm{Dini}}\).  Suppose that
\(t_0\in I^*\), \(g(t_0)=0\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-smallness}
 \|g'\|_{L^\infty(I^*)}\leq\varepsilon_0.
\end{equation}
There is a compactly supported function
$\widetilde g\in C^{1,\mathrm{Dini}}(\mathbb R;\mathbb R^{n-1})$ such that
$\widetilde g=g$ on $\widehat I$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extension-slope}
 \|\widetilde g'\|_\infty\leq C_\kappa\varepsilon_0.
\end{equation}
If $\vartheta_g$ is the modulus of $g'$, then the derivative of
$\widetilde g$ has a modulus satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extension-modulus}
 \vartheta_{\widetilde g}(t)
 \leq C_\kappa\vartheta_g(t)
 +C_\kappa\varepsilon_0\min\{1,t/|I^*|\},\qquad 0<t\leq1.
\end{equation}
In particular, $\vartheta_{\widetilde g}$ is a Dini modulus.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By \(g(t_0)=0\) and \eqref{eq:local-smallness}, \(\|g\|_{L^\infty(I^*)} \leq \varepsilon_0|I^*|\). Condition \eqref{eq:fixed-collar} gives
\(\rho\in C_c^\infty(I^*)\) such that \(\rho=1\) on
\(\widehat I\) and \(\|\rho'\|_\infty\leq C_\kappa|I^*|^{-1}, \quad \|\rho''\|_\infty\leq C_\kappa|I^*|^{-2}\). Extend $\rho g$ by zero outside $I^*$ and put
$\widetilde g=\rho g$.  Then \(\widetilde g'=\rho g'+\rho'g\). The preceding bounds for \(g\), \(g'\), and \(\rho'\) give

constants.

We also need a fixed upper cutoff.  Put \(\rho=\min\{R,r_0\}\).  If
\(\varepsilon<\rho\), then \(s_{\varepsilon,R}(x,y)\chi(|x-y|) -s_{\varepsilon,\rho}(x,y)\) is supported in \(\{r_0\leq|x-y|\leq2r_0\}\).  If
\(\varepsilon\geq\rho\), then \(\rho=r_0\), and
\(s_{\varepsilon,R}\chi\) is supported in
\(\{\rho/2<|x-y|<2\rho\}\).  Another application of
\cref{lem:annular-A2} gives the required bound for the source-radial
cutoff.  The difference between that cutoff and
\(q_{\varepsilon,R}\) is controlled by
\eqref{eq:target-source-truncation}.  More explicitly, if \(T_a\)
denotes the operator with kernel
\(a(x,y)\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}(x,y)\), then, uniformly in
\(0<\varepsilon<R\),
\begin{align*}
 \|T_{q_{\varepsilon,R}\chi}\|
 &\leq \|T_{s_{\varepsilon,R}\chi}\|
     +C\sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}
 \leq \mathfrak N_{\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}}^s
     +C\sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}
 \leq \mathfrak N_{\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}}^q
     +C\sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}.
\end{align*}
The first and last inequalities use
\eqref{eq:target-source-truncation}.  The middle inequality uses the
two cases above and \cref{lem:annular-A2}.  Consequently,
\begin{align}
 &\sup_{0<\varepsilon<R}
 \left\|\int
q_{\varepsilon,R}(x,y)\chi(|x-y|)
 \mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}(x,y)f(y)\,
d\widetilde\sigma(y)\right\|_{L^2(\widetilde\omega)}
 \notag\\
 &\qquad\leq C\left(
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}}^q
 +\sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha(\sigma,\omega)}\right)
 \|f\|_{L^2(\widetilde\sigma)}.
 \label{eq:fixed-upper-pullback}
\end{align}
This estimate also holds for the adjoint.  Repeating the preceding three
inequalities with each operator norm replaced by its normalized testing
expression proves the forward and dual testing estimates.

Multiplying \eqref{eq:near-decomposition} by
\(q_{\varepsilon,R}\), we obtain
\begin{align*}
q_{\varepsilon,R}\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}
 &=D\Psi(x)q_{\varepsilon,R}\chi\mathbf K_\Psi^{\alpha,n}
 +q_{\varepsilon,R}(1-\chi)\mathbf K_\Psi^{\alpha,n}+q_{\varepsilon,R}
 (\mathbf E_{x,\mathrm{near}}+\mathbf E_{\mathrm{far}}).
\end{align*}
The matrix \(D\Psi(x)\) depends only on the left variable.  Thus
we apply the quasicube form of \cref{lem:cutoff-stability} with
\(\Omega=\Psi^{-1}\).  Equation \eqref{eq:A2-pullback} bounds its
combined quasicube constant by
\(C\mathfrak A_2^\alpha(\sigma,\omega)\).  The lemma and
\eqref{eq:uniform-error-operator} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-direction-change}
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}}^q
 \leq C\left(
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf K_\Psi^{\alpha,n}}^s
 +(1+\Lambda_{\psi,r_0})
   \sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}\right).
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:smooth-tangent-comparison}, the source-radial norm on the
right is bounded by the tangent-line norm plus
\(C\sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}\).  Repeating the preceding inequalities
with each operator norm replaced by its normalized testing expression
proves forward testing.  Expansion at \(y\) proves dual testing because
\(D\Psi(y)\) then depends only on the left variable for the adjoint.
The error in both estimates is
\(C(1+\Lambda_{\psi,r_0})\sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}\).  In both
testing estimates, the left-hand side uses the \(q\)-cutoffs, while the
first term on the right-hand side uses the \(s\)-cutoffs.  Equation
\eqref{eq:smooth-tangent-comparison} gives the testing
constants.

For the reverse inequality, we use the following identity for a graph
shear: \(Du(x)^2=0\). Consequently, \((I-\chi(x,y)Du(x))(I+\chi(x,y)Du(x))=I\). Equation \eqref{eq:near-decomposition} is \(\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}} =(I+\chi Du(x))\mathbf K_\Psi^{\alpha,n} +\mathbf E_{x,\mathrm{near}}+\mathbf E_{\mathrm{far}}\). It follows that
\begin{align}
 \mathbf K_\Psi^{\alpha,n}
 &=\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}-\chi Du(x)\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}
 -(I-\chi Du(x))
 (\mathbf E_{x,\mathrm{near}}+\mathbf E_{\mathrm{far}}).
 \label{eq:reverse-kernel-identity}
\end{align}
Multiply \eqref{eq:reverse-kernel-identity} by
\(q_{\varepsilon,R}\).  The first term is one of the truncations used in
\(\mathfrak N_{\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}}^q\).  For the second term,
\[
q_{\varepsilon,R}(x,y)\chi(|x-y|)
Du(x)\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}(x,y)
 =Du(x)\bigl[q_{\varepsilon,R}(x,y)\chi(|x-y|)
 \mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}(x,y)\bigr].
\]
Equations \eqref{eq:target-source-truncation} and
\eqref{eq:fixed-upper-pullback} therefore give
\[
 \|T_{q_{\varepsilon,R}\chi Du\,\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}}\|
 \leq C\|Du\|_\infty\left(
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}}^q
 +\sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}\right).
\]
For the last term, multiplication by \(\chi\) only decreases the
positive annular majorants used in
\eqref{eq:uniform-error-operator}.  Hence
\[
 \|T_{q_{\varepsilon,R}(I-\chi Du)
 (\mathbf E_{x,\mathrm{near}}+\mathbf E_{\mathrm{far}})}\|
 \leq C(1+\|Du\|_\infty)
 \Lambda_{\psi,r_0}\sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}.
\]
The norms in the last two displays are from
\(L^2(\widetilde\sigma)\) to \(L^2(\widetilde\omega)\).
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reverse-q-change}
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf K_\Psi^{\alpha,n}}^q
 \leq C\left(
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}}^q
 +(1+\Lambda_{\psi,r_0})
   \sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}\right).
\end{equation}
The same estimates, with the same factor
\(1+\Lambda_{\psi,r_0}\), hold for the normalized \(q\)-testing
constants.
Expansion at \(y\) gives the reverse dual \(q\)-testing estimate.

These estimates use the target-radial cutoffs
\(q_{\varepsilon,R}\).  Since \(|\mathbf K_\Psi^{\alpha,n}(x,y)| \leq C|x-y|^{\alpha-n}\), the support comparison
\eqref{eq:target-source-cutoff-support} and
\cref{lem:annular-A2} also give
\begin{align*}
 &\sup_{0<\varepsilon<R}
 \left\|\int
 (q_{\varepsilon,R}-s_{\varepsilon,R})(x,y)
 \mathbf K_\Psi^{\alpha,n}(x,y)f(y)\,
d\widetilde\sigma(y)\right\|_{L^2(\widetilde\omega)}\leq C_L\sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}
 \|f\|_{L^2(\widetilde\sigma)}.
\end{align*}
The same estimate holds for the adjoint and for both normalized testing
constants.  Combining it with \eqref{eq:reverse-q-change} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reverse-source-change}
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf K_\Psi^{\alpha,n}}^s
 \leq C\left(
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}}^q
 +(1+\Lambda_{\psi,r_0})
   \sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}\right),
\end{equation}
and the analogous forward and dual \(s\)-testing estimates, with the
same factor \(1+\Lambda_{\psi,r_0}\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:smooth-tangent-comparison} converts the left-hand sides to the
tangent-line constants.

Finally, under \(X=\Psi(x)\), \(Y=\Psi(y)\), the target-radial kernel \(\tau_{\varepsilon,R}(|X-Y|)\mathbf K^{\alpha,n}(X,Y)\) pulls back to \(q_{\varepsilon,R}(x,y)\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}(x,y)\). The measures become \(\widetilde\sigma\) and
\(\widetilde\omega\), the source function becomes \(f\circ\Psi\), and
an original testing set \(Q\) becomes \(\Psi^{-1}Q\).  In particular, \(\|f\circ\Psi\|_{L^2(\widetilde\sigma)} =\|f\|_{L^2(\sigma)}\). Therefore
\begin{align*}
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf K^{\alpha,n}}^s(\sigma,\omega)
 &=\mathfrak N_{\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}}}^q
   (\widetilde\sigma,\widetilde\omega),\\
 \mathfrak T_{\mathbf K^{\alpha,n},\mathcal F}^s(\sigma,\omega)
 &=\mathfrak T_{\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}},\Psi^{-1}\mathcal F}^q
   (\widetilde\sigma,\widetilde\omega),\\
 \mathfrak T_{\mathbf K^{\alpha,n},\mathcal F}^{s,\mathrm{dual}}
   (\sigma,\omega)
 &=\mathfrak T_{\mathbf K^{\mathrm{pb}},\Psi^{-1}\mathcal F}^{q,\mathrm{dual}}
   (\widetilde\sigma,\widetilde\omega).
\end{align*}
Apply \eqref{eq:smooth-tangent-comparison} to the fractional Riesz
kernel in the original coordinates and to the conformal kernel in the
pullback coordinates.  Together with
\eqref{eq:one-direction-change} and
\eqref{eq:reverse-source-change}, these identities prove the norm and
testing comparisons in the proposition.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:variance}
Let $\mu$ be a positive Borel measure and let $J$ be a dyadic cube or
quasicube.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance}
 \|P_J^\mu\mathbf x\|_{L^2(\mu)}^2
 =\int_J|x-m_J^\mu|^2\,d\mu(x)
 =\frac1{2\mu(J)}
   \iint_{J\times J}|x-y|^2\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y),
\end{equation}
with the usual value $0$ when $\mu(J)=0$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->

over all dyadic subquasicubes \(Q\subseteq J\), including \(J\) itself.
Hence \(P_J^\mu\mathbf x=\mathbf 1_J(\mathbf x-m_J^\mu)\). The second equality in \eqref{eq:variance} follows by expanding
$|x-y|^2$ and using
$m_J^\mu=\mu(J)^{-1}\int_Jx\,d\mu(x)$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:energy-invariance}
Let \(F,\Omega:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n\) be globally biLipschitz.  Put \(\widetilde\Omega=F^{-1}\circ\Omega,\quad \widetilde\sigma=F^*\sigma,\quad \widetilde\omega=F^*\omega\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy-equivalence}
 \mathcal E_{\alpha,\widetilde\Omega}^{\mathrm{strong}}
   (\widetilde\sigma,\widetilde\omega)
 \approx
 \mathcal E_{\alpha,\Omega}^{\mathrm{strong}}
   (\sigma,\omega),
\end{equation}
and the same comparison holds for the dual strong energy constants.  The
constants depend only on $n$, $\alpha$, and the biLipschitz constants of
$F$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
For every ordinary dyadic grid \(\mathcal D\), \(F^{-1}(\Omega\mathcal D)=\widetilde\Omega\mathcal D\). We compare the two lines of
\eqref{eq:strong-energy-definition}.  Deep embedding is preserved because \(\operatorname{qdist}_{\widetilde\Omega} (F^{-1}E,F^{-1}G) =\operatorname{qdist}_{\Omega}(E,G)\). The families
$M_{r,\varepsilon\text{-deep}}(I)$ and
$M_{r,\varepsilon\text{-deep}}^\ell(I)$ therefore correspond under \(F\).
Alternate cubes, the auxiliary cubes in the refined energy condition, and
dyadic subpartitions also correspond under \(F\), by their definitions.
More precisely,
\begin{align*}
F^{-1}(A\Omega\mathcal D)
 &=A\widetilde\Omega\mathcal D,\\
 \{F^{-1}J:J\in M_{r,\varepsilon\text{-deep}}(I)\}
 &=M_{r,\varepsilon\text{-deep}}(F^{-1}I),\\
 \{F^{-1}J:J\in
M_{r,\varepsilon\text{-deep},\Omega\mathcal D}^{\ell}(I)\}
 &=M_{r,\varepsilon\text{-deep},
 \widetilde\Omega\mathcal D}^{\ell}(F^{-1}I).
\end{align*}
Hence the indices in both lines of
\eqref{eq:strong-energy-definition} correspond under $F$.

Let $J\subset I$ correspond to
$\widetilde J\subset\widetilde I$.  For a quasicube \(Q\), put
\(a_Q=|Q|^{1/n}\).  Recall that
\begin{align*}
P^\alpha(Q,\nu)
 &=\int_{\mathbb R^n}
 \frac{a_Q}{(a_Q+|x-c_Q|)^{n+1-\alpha}}\,d\nu(x),\\
 \mathcal P^\alpha(Q,\nu)
 &=\int_{\mathbb R^n}
 \left(\frac{a_Q}{(a_Q+|x-c_Q|)^2}\right)^{n-\alpha}
d\nu(x).
\end{align*}
The strong energy condition uses \(P^\alpha\).

Let \(L\) be a common biLipschitz constant for \(F\) and \(F^{-1}\).
Then \(c_{\widetilde J}=F^{-1}(c_J),\quad L^{-1}|J|^{1/n} \leq|\widetilde J|^{1/n} \leq L|J|^{1/n}\), and \(L^{-1}|u-c_J| \leq|F^{-1}(u)-F^{-1}(c_J)| \leq L|u-c_J|\). Using \eqref{eq:pullback-measure}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
P^\alpha(\widetilde J,
        \mathbf 1_{\widetilde I}\widetilde\sigma)
 &=
 \int_I
 \frac{|\widetilde J|^{1/n}}
 {\left(|\widetilde J|^{1/n}
 +|F^{-1}(u)-F^{-1}(c_J)|\right)^{n+1-\alpha}}
 \,d\sigma(u)\\
 &\approx
 \int_I
 \frac{|J|^{1/n}}
 {\left(|J|^{1/n}+|u-c_J|\right)^{n+1-\alpha}}
 \,d\sigma(u)\\
 &=P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_I\sigma).
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Poisson-comparison}
 \frac{P^\alpha(\widetilde J,\mathbf 1_{\widetilde I}\widetilde\sigma)}
      {|\widetilde J|^{1/n}}
 \approx
 \frac{P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_I\sigma)}{|J|^{1/n}}.
\end{equation}
Replacing \(P^\alpha\) by \(\mathcal P^\alpha\) in the preceding calculation
gives \(\mathcal P^\alpha(\widetilde J, \mathbf 1_{\widetilde I}\widetilde\sigma) \approx \mathcal P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_I\sigma)\).
The masses of corresponding sets are equal: \(\widetilde\sigma(\widetilde I)=\sigma(I), \quad \widetilde\omega(\widetilde J)=\omega(J)\). By
\cref{lem:variance},
\begin{align}
 \|P_{\widetilde J}^{\widetilde\omega}\mathbf x\|_{L^2(\widetilde\omega)}^2
 &=\frac1{2\omega(J)}
   \iint_{J\times J}
   |F^{-1}(u)-F^{-1}(v)|^2
   \,d\omega(u)d\omega(v)
 \notag\\
 &\approx
 \frac1{2\omega(J)}
   \iint_{J\times J}|u-v|^2\,d\omega(u)d\omega(v)
 \notag\\
 &=\|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_{L^2(\omega)}^2.
 \label{eq:variance-comparison}
\end{align}
Equations \eqref{eq:Poisson-comparison} and
\eqref{eq:variance-comparison}, followed by summation over the corresponding
deep cubes, compare the first line of
\eqref{eq:strong-energy-definition}.  Apply the same two comparisons to
each \(I\in A\Omega\mathcal D\) and each \(\ell\geq0\).  This compares
the second line.  Taking the suprema over the grids, subpartitions, alternate tops, and
\(\ell\geq0\) proves one inequality in \eqref{eq:energy-equivalence}.  For the opposite inequality, apply the comparison just
proved with \(F\) replaced by \(F^{-1}\), \(\Omega\) replaced by
\(F^{-1}\circ\Omega\), and the pulled-back pair in place of
\((\sigma,\omega)\).
Interchanging the measures gives the dual statement.

\label{lem:end-side-geometry}
There are \(A_0=A_0(n,\Omega)\) and
\(\gamma_0=\gamma_0(n,\Omega,A_0)\) such that, for

 |u|\approx\ell(J)+|y-c_J|,\qquad
 |y'|\leq\frac{CA_0}{\gamma}|u|,\qquad
 |x^1-z^1|\leq\frac C\gamma|u|.
\end{equation}

 \label{eq:side-geometry}\\
 \mathcal H^1(J^*\cap L_0)&\geq c\gamma\ell(J),\qquad
J^*\cap L_0\subset

 \label{eq:side-shadow}
\end{align}

\label{lem:forward-hole}
If \(\mathcal J\) is admissible, then
\begin{align}
 &\sum_{J\in\mathcal J}
 \left(
 \frac{P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{I\setminus\gamma J}\sigma)}
 {\ell(J)}\right)^2
 \|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_{L^2(\omega)}^2
 \leq C\beta
 \left[
 \bigl(\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}}\bigr)^2
 +\mathcal A_2^\alpha
 \right]\sigma(I).
 \label{eq:forward-hole}
\end{align}

<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Terms with \(\omega(J)=0\) vanish.  Since \(\omega\) is supported on
\(L_0\), the variance of \(\mathbf x\) on \(J\) is the variance of the
first coordinate.  Put
\[
a_J=\frac{P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\mathrm E(J^*)}\sigma)}
 {\ell(J)},\qquad
b_J=\frac{P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma)}
 {\ell(J)}.
\]
Since \(I\setminus J^*=\mathrm E(J^*)\dot\cup\mathrm S(J^*)\),
linearity of \(P^\alpha\) and \((a_J+b_J)^2\leq2a_J^2+2b_J^2\)
reduce the proof to the two corresponding energy sums.
By \cref{lem:end-reversal} and the variance identity,
\[
a_J^2\|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2
 \leq C\int_J
 \left|R_{\Psi,1}^{\alpha,n}
  (\mathbf 1_{\mathrm E(J^*)}\sigma)
 -\mathbb E_J^\omega R_{\Psi,1}^{\alpha,n}
 (\mathbf 1_{\mathrm E(J^*)}\sigma)\right|^2d\omega.
\]
First take a finite subfamily
\(\mathcal J_0\subset\mathcal J\).  Terms for which
\(I\setminus J^*=\varnothing\) have zero Poisson factor and may be
discarded.  If no term remains, there is nothing to prove.  Otherwise
put
\[
d_0=\min_{J\in\mathcal J_0}\operatorname{dist}(J,I\setminus J^*),
 \qquad
D_0=\max_{J\in\mathcal J_0}
 \sup_{\substack{x\in J\\y\in I\setminus J^*}}|x-y|.
\]
Since \(J^*=\gamma J\), quasicube geometry gives \(d_0\geq c_\Omega(\gamma-1) \min_{J\in\mathcal J_0}\ell(J)>0, \quad D_0\leq\operatorname{diam} I\). Choose one tangent-line truncation with \(0<\delta<d_0,\quad R>D_0\). Then \(t_{\delta,R}=1\) on
\(J\times(I\setminus J^*)\) for every \(J\in\mathcal J_0\).
The half-open convention gives the partition \(I=J^*\dot\cup\mathrm E(J^*)\dot\cup\mathrm S(J^*)\). Consequently, for \(x\in J\),
\begin{align*}
 \mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}
   (\mathbf 1_{\mathrm E(J^*)}\sigma)(x)
 &=
 \mathbf R_{\Psi,\delta,R}^{\alpha,n,\mathrm{tl}}
   (\mathbf 1_I\sigma)(x)
 -\mathbf R_{\Psi,\delta,R}^{\alpha,n,\mathrm{tl}}
   (\mathbf 1_{J^*}\sigma)(x)
 -\mathbf R_{\Psi,\delta,R}^{\alpha,n,\mathrm{tl}}
   (\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma)(x).
\end{align*}
In the next three testing estimates,
each transform denotes this fixed tangent-line truncation.
After subtracting the \(\omega\)-average on \(J\), the triangle
inequality gives three oscillation terms.  Testing on \(I\) gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{J\in\mathcal J_0}\int_J
 |R_{\Psi,1}^{\alpha,n}(\mathbf 1_I\sigma)|^2\,d\omega
 &\leq\beta\int_I
 |R_{\Psi,1}^{\alpha,n}(\mathbf 1_I\sigma)|^2\,d\omega
 \leq\beta\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}}^2\sigma(I).
\end{align*}
Testing on each \(J^*\), followed by
\eqref{eq:admissible-family}, gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{J\in\mathcal J_0}\int_J
 |R_{\Psi,1}^{\alpha,n}(\mathbf 1_{J^*}\sigma)|^2\,d\omega
 &\leq\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}}^2
       \sum_{J\in\mathcal J_0}\sigma(J^*)
 \leq\beta\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}}^2\sigma(I).
\end{align*}
For the side term, apply \cref{lem:C1-energy} to a vector test
function whose only nonzero component is the first one.  By duality,
\[
 \int_J\left|R_{\Psi,1}^{\alpha,n}
 (\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma)
 -\mathbb E_J^\omega R_{\Psi,1}^{\alpha,n}
 (\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma)\right|^2d\omega
 \leq Cb_J^2\|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2.
\]
All bounds are uniform in the truncation.  Letting the finite
subfamilies \(\mathcal J_0\) increase to \(\mathcal J\), monotone
convergence applies to the nonnegative energy sums.  It remains to
estimate
\[
B=\sum_{J\in\mathcal J}
 \left(
 \frac{P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma)}
 {\ell(J)}\right)^2
 \|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2.
\]
Write
\[
 \mathcal P^\alpha(J,\nu)
 \approx_\Omega\int_{\mathbb R^n}
 \left(\frac{\ell(J)}
 {(\ell(J)+|y-c_J|)^2}\right)^{n-\alpha}d\nu(y).
\]
Suppose first that \(n-1\leq\alpha<n\).  Since
\(\mathrm S(J^*)\subset J^c\), \(P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma) \leq C\mathcal P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma)\). Using
\(\|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2\leq C\ell(J)^2\omega(J)\) and the
one-tailed condition gives
\begin{align*}
 &\left(
 \frac{P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma)}
 {\ell(J)}\right)^2
 \|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2\leq C\mathcal A_2^\alpha
 \int_{\mathrm S(J^*)}
 \left(\frac{\ell(J)}
 {\ell(J)+|y-c_J|}\right)^{n+1-\alpha}d\sigma(y).
\end{align*}
If \(0\leq\alpha<n-1\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{align*}
P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma)^2
 &\leq C_\Omega
 \mathcal P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma)
 \int_{\mathrm S(J^*)}
 \frac{\ell(J)^{\alpha+2-n}}
 {(\ell(J)+|y-c_J|)^2}\,d\sigma(y).
\end{align*}
Using
\(\|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2\leq C\ell(J)^2\omega(J)\) and \(\omega(J)\mathcal P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma) \leq C\mathcal A_2^\alpha\ell(J)^{n-\alpha}\), we obtain
\begin{align*}
 &\left(
 \frac{P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\mathrm S(J^*)}\sigma)}
 {\ell(J)}\right)^2
 \|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2\leq C\mathcal A_2^\alpha
 \int_{\mathrm S(J^*)}
 \left(\frac{\ell(J)}
 {\ell(J)+|y-c_J|}\right)^2d\sigma(y).
\end{align*}
Thus, with \(q_\alpha=\min\{2,n+1-\alpha\}>1\),
\begin{equation*}
B\leq C\mathcal A_2^\alpha
 \int_I\sum_{\substack{J\in\mathcal J\\y\in\mathrm S(J^*)}}
 \left(\frac{\ell(J)}
 {\ell(J)+|y-c_J|}\right)^{q_\alpha}d\sigma(y).
\end{equation*}
By \cref{lem:end-side-geometry}, if \(d(y)=\operatorname{dist}(y,L_0)\) and
\(y\in\mathrm S(J^*)\), then \eqref{eq:side-geometry} and
\eqref{eq:side-shadow} hold.
Hence, by bounded overlap,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{J\in\mathcal J\\y\in\mathrm S(J^*)}}\ell(J)
 \leq\frac C\gamma
 \int_{|t-y^1|\leq C\gamma d(y)}
 \sum_{J\in\mathcal J}\mathbf 1_{J^*}(t,0)\,dt
 \leq C\beta d(y).
\]
Moreover, \eqref{eq:side-geometry} gives
\(\ell(J)\leq Cd(y)\) and
\(|y-c_J|\geq c\,d(y)\).  Since \(1<q_\alpha\leq2\),
the number \(t_J=\ell(J)/d(y)\) satisfies \(t_J\leq C\), and hence
\(t_J^{q_\alpha}\leq C^{q_\alpha-1}t_J\).  Therefore
\begin{align*}
 &\sum_{\substack{J\in\mathcal J\\y\in\mathrm S(J^*)}}
 \left(\frac{\ell(J)}
 {\ell(J)+|y-c_J|}\right)^{q_\alpha}\leq
C\sum_{\substack{J\in\mathcal J\\y\in\mathrm S(J^*)}}
 \left(\frac{\ell(J)}{d(y)}\right)^{q_\alpha}
 \leq
 \frac C{d(y)}
 \sum_{\substack{J\in\mathcal J\\y\in\mathrm S(J^*)}}\ell(J)
 \leq C\beta.
\end{align*}
Thus
\(B\leq C\beta\mathcal A_2^\alpha\sigma(I)\), which proves
\eqref{eq:forward-hole}.

\label{lem:fill-hole}
For every admissible family,
\begin{align}
 &\sum_{J\in\mathcal J}
 \left(
 \frac{P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\gamma J}\sigma)}
 {\ell(J)}\right)^2
 \|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2
 \leq
C\beta A_2^{\alpha,*,\mathrm{punct}}\sigma(I).
 \label{eq:fill-hole}
\end{align}
After interchanging the measures, the dual estimate is
\begin{align*}
 &\sum_{J\in\mathcal J}
 \left(
 \frac{P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{\gamma J}\omega)}
 {\ell(J)}\right)^2
 \|P_J^\sigma\mathbf x\|_2^2
 \leq C\beta A_2^{\alpha,\mathrm{punct}}\omega(I).
\end{align*}
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Let \(\mathfrak P\) be the set of common point masses, and write
\[
 \omega(Q,\mathfrak P)
 =\omega(Q)-\sup_{p\in Q\cap\mathfrak P}\omega(\{p\}),
\]
where the supremum of the empty set is \(0\).  If
\(J^*\cap\mathfrak P\neq\varnothing\), choose a largest common
\(\omega\)-atom \(p_J\).  Such an atom exists because
\(\omega(J^*)<\infty\): if the positive supremum of the common atomic
masses were not attained, infinitely many of them would exceed half of
that supremum.
If \(p_J\in J\), the minimizing property of variance gives
\[
 \|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2
 \leq\int_J|x-p_J|^2\,d\omega(x)
 \leq C\ell(J)^2\omega(J^*,\mathfrak P).
\]
If \(p_J\notin J\), then
the half-open sets \(J\) and \(\{p_J\}\) are disjoint.  Since \(p_J\)
is a largest common \(\omega\)-atom in \(J^*\), \(\omega(J) \leq \omega\bigl(J^*\setminus\{p_J\}\bigr) =\omega(J^*,\mathfrak P)\). The minimizing property of the barycenter, followed by the quasicube
diameter bound, now gives
\begin{align*}
 \|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2
 &\leq\int_J|x-c_J|^2\,d\omega(x)\leq C\ell(J)^2\omega(J)
 \leq C\ell(J)^2\omega(J^*,\mathfrak P).
\end{align*}
When \(J^*\cap\mathfrak P\) is empty,
\(\omega(J^*,\mathfrak P)=\omega(J^*)\).  Centering at \(c_J\) gives
\[
 \|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2
 \leq C\ell(J)^2\omega(J)
 \leq C\ell(J)^2\omega(J^*)
 =C\ell(J)^2\omega(J^*,\mathfrak P).
\]
Hence \(\|P_J^\omega\mathbf x\|_2^2 \leq C\ell(J)^2\omega(J^*,\mathfrak P)\). Also, \(P^\alpha(J,\mathbf 1_{J^*}\sigma) \leq C\frac{\sigma(\gamma J)}{\ell(J)^{n-\alpha}}\). Thus the left side of \eqref{eq:fill-hole} is at most
\[
C\sum_{J\in\mathcal J}
 \frac{\sigma(J^*)^2\omega(J^*,\mathfrak P)}
 {\ell(J)^{2(n-\alpha)}}.
\]
Since the puncture is in the \(\omega\)-factor, we use the starred
punctured condition.  Since
\(\ell(J^*)=\gamma\ell(J)\), it gives
\[
 \frac{\sigma(J^*)\omega(J^*,\mathfrak P)}
 {\ell(J)^{2(n-\alpha)}}
 \leq C_\gamma A_2^{\alpha,*,\mathrm{punct}}.
\]
Bounded overlap now gives
\[
C_\gamma A_2^{\alpha,*,\mathrm{punct}}
 \sum_{J\in\mathcal J}\sigma(J^*)
 \leq C_\gamma\beta
A_2^{\alpha,*,\mathrm{punct}}\sigma(I).
\]
Interchanging \(\sigma\) and \(\omega\) punctures the
\(\sigma\)-factor.  This gives the unstarred constant in the dual
estimate stated above.

\label{lem:line-geometry}
There are constants \(c_\Omega,C_\Omega>0\) with the following
properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(3J\cap L_0=\varnothing\), \(y\in J\), and \(x\in L_0\), then \(|y'|\geq c_\Omega\ell(J),\quad |y-x|\approx_\Omega\ell(J)+|x-c_J|\). \item Suppose \(3J\cap L_0\neq\varnothing\).  If \(\gamma\) is large,
\(x\in L_0\setminus\gamma J\), \(y,z\in J\), and
\(w_t=z+t(y-z)\), then
\begin{align*}
 |w_t'|&\leq C_\Omega\ell(J),\\
 |w_t^1-x^1|&\geq c_\Omega\gamma\ell(J),\\
 |w_t'|&\leq \frac{C_\Omega}{\gamma}|w_t^1-x^1|,\\
 |w_t^1-x^1|&\approx_\Omega\ell(J)+|x-c_J|.
\end{align*}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-15 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Write \(J=\Omega Q\).  The biLipschitz bounds imply \(\operatorname{diam} J\leq C_\Omega\ell(J),\quad \operatorname{dist}(J,(\gamma J)^c)\geq c_\Omega(\gamma-1)\ell(J)\), where the second inequality remains valid for half-open quasicubes after
decreasing \(c_\Omega\).  If \(3J\cap L_0=\varnothing\), then
\(L_0\subset(3J)^c\).  The preceding distance bound with \(\gamma=3\)
therefore gives \(\operatorname{dist}(J,L_0)\geq c_\Omega\ell(J)\).  This proves the
first assertion.

For the second, choose \(\zeta\in3J\cap L_0\).  Since
\(w_t\in\operatorname{conv}(J)\), we have
\(|w_t-c_J|\leq C_\Omega\ell(J)\), and hence
\(|w_t-\zeta|\leq C_\Omega\ell(J)\).  This gives the first bound.
Since \(x\notin\gamma J\),
\(|x-c_J|\geq c_\Omega\gamma\ell(J)\).  Therefore, after increasing
\(\gamma\), \(|x-w_t|\geq |x-c_J|-|w_t-c_J| \geq c_\Omega\gamma\ell(J)\). Subtracting the transverse bound gives the second and third bounds.
The estimates for \(|x-c_J|\) and \(|w_t-c_J|\) give the last
comparison.

\label{lem:backward-reversal}
Assume that \(3J\cap L_0\neq\varnothing\), and let \(\nu\) be a finite
positive Borel measure supported on \(L_0\setminus\gamma J\).  Under
\eqref{eq:graph-map}, for the above choice of \(\gamma\) and every
\(y,z\in J\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:backward-reversal}
 |y^1-z^1|\frac{P^\alpha(J,\nu)}{\ell(J)}
 \leq C|\mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}\nu(y)
       -\mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}\nu(z)|.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-16 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Put \(p=n+1-\alpha\), \(q=p-1=n-\alpha\), and write \(h=y-z=(h_1,h'),\quad w_t=z+th\). For \(x=(x^1,0)\in L_0\setminus\gamma J\), set \(a=w_t^1-x^1,\quad u=w_t',\quad b=u+\psi(w_t^1)-\psi(x^1),\quad r=(a^2+|b|^2)^{1/2}\). BiLipschitz quasicube geometry gives \(|u|\leq\eta|a|,\quad |b|\leq\delta|a|,\quad r\approx|a|\approx\ell(J)+|x-c_J|\), where \(\eta=\frac{C_\Omega}{\gamma}, \quad \delta=\eta+\|D\psi\|_\infty\). The bound in \eqref{eq:graph-map}, followed by the choice of \(\gamma\)
above, ensures that
\begin{align}
 \delta^2+p\delta(1+\|D\psi\|_\infty)&\leq q/2,
 \label{eq:backward-choice-one}\\
p\eta\{1+\delta(1+\|D\psi\|_\infty)\}&\leq1/2.\notag
\end{align}

Suppose first that \(|h'|\leq|h_1|\).  If \(h_1=0\), then \(y=z\),
so the desired estimate is trivial.  We may therefore assume
\(h_1\neq0\).  For \(\phi_x(t)=a r^{-p}\), direct differentiation gives
\begin{align*}
 \phi_x'(t)
 &=\frac{h_1}{r^{p+2}}N_1(t,x),\\
N_1(t,x)
 &=-qa^2+|b|^2
 -pa\,b\cdot\frac{h'}{h_1}
 -pa\,b\cdot D\psi(w_t^1).
\end{align*}
By \eqref{eq:backward-choice-one}, \(N_1(t,x)\leq-\frac q2a^2\). The sign is fixed for every \(x,t\).  Integration first in \(t\), then
in \(x\), yields
\[
 \left|
 (R_{\Psi,1}^{\alpha,n}\nu)(y)
 -(R_{\Psi,1}^{\alpha,n}\nu)(z)
 \right|
 \geq c|h_1|\int\!\!\int_0^1\frac{a^2}{r^{p+2}}\,dt\,d\nu(x)
 \geq c|h_1|\frac{P^\alpha(J,\nu)}{\ell(J)}.
\]

Suppose next that \(|h'|>|h_1|\).  Differentiate the transverse vector:
\begin{align}
 \frac d{dt}(u r^{-p})
 &=h'r^{-p}+\mathbf e_x(t),\notag\\
 \mathbf e_x(t)
 &=-p\,u r^{-p-2}
 \left\{ah_1+b\cdot h'
 +b\cdot D\psi(w_t^1)h_1\right\},\notag\\
 |\mathbf e_x(t)|
 &\leq p\eta\{1+\delta(1+\|D\psi\|_\infty)\}
 |h'|r^{-p}
 \leq\frac12|h'|r^{-p}.
 \label{eq:backward-case-two}
\end{align}
The reverse triangle inequality gives
\begin{align*}
 \left|\int_{L_0}\int_0^1
 \bigl(h'r^{-p}+\mathbf e_x(t)\bigr)\,dt\,d\nu(x)\right|
 &\geq |h'|\int_{L_0}\int_0^1r^{-p}\,dt\,d\nu(x)
 -\int_{L_0}\int_0^1|\mathbf e_x(t)|\,dt\,d\nu(x)\\
 &\geq\frac{|h'|}{2}
 \int_{L_0}\int_0^1r^{-p}\,dt\,d\nu(x).
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\[
 \left|
 (\mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}\nu(y))'
 -(\mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}\nu(z))'
 \right|
 \geq c|h'|\frac{P^\alpha(J,\nu)}{\ell(J)}
 \geq c|h_1|\frac{P^\alpha(J,\nu)}{\ell(J)}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:backward-reversal}.  The calculation uses
\(|\psi(s)-\psi(t)|\leq\|D\psi\|_\infty|s-t|\) and the pointwise
bound for \(D\psi\), but no modulus of \(D\psi\).

 \mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}\nu_J(x)

\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-cone-proof}

 \qquad x\in P\cap L_0,\quad y\in E_p,
\end{equation}
where \(c_0>0\) depends only on \(\Omega\).  Define measures on
\([0,\infty)\) by
\begin{align*}
 \lambda(B)
 &=\widetilde\omega
   \bigl(\{x\in P\cap L_0:|x-p|\in B\}\bigr),\\
 \tau(B)
 &=\widetilde\sigma
   \bigl(\{y\in E_p:|y-p|\in B\}\bigr).
\end{align*}
The half-open sets \(P\) and \(E_p\) are disjoint.  Thus \(p\) belongs
to at most one of them.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:no-common-radial-origin}
 \lambda(\{0\})\tau(\{0\})=0.
\end{equation}
Thus the hypothesis \eqref{eq:Hardy-no-common-zero} of
\cref{lem:Hardy} holds.
For \(r=|y-p|\), the kernel estimate and
\eqref{eq:endpoint-cone-proof} give
\begin{align}
 \left|\mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}
   (\mathbf 1_P\widetilde\omega)(y)\right|
 &\leq C\int_{[0,\infty)}(r+t)^{-s}\,d\lambda(t)
 \leq C\left[
r^{-s}\lambda([0,r])
 +\int_{[r,\infty)}t^{-s}\,d\lambda(t)
 \right].
 \label{eq:hardy-kernel-split}
\end{align}
If \(\tau(\{0\})>0\), then
\eqref{eq:no-common-radial-origin} gives
\(\lambda(\{0\})=0\).  At \(r=0\), we therefore read the first term as
zero and the second as \(\int_{(0,\infty)}t^{-s}\,d\lambda(t)\).

It remains to bound \(B_1\) and \(B_2\) in \cref{lem:Hardy}.  Recall that
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal P^\alpha(H,\nu)
 =\int_{\mathbb R^n}
 \left(
  \frac{|H|^{1/n}}
  {\bigl(|H|^{1/n}+|z-c_H|\bigr)^2}
 \right)^s d\nu(z).
\end{equation*}
Thus
\begin{align}
 \mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^\alpha
 &=\sup_{H\in\Omega\mathcal Q^n}
 \frac{\widetilde\omega(H)}{|H|^{s/n}}
 \mathcal P^\alpha
   (H,\mathbf 1_{H^c}\widetilde\sigma),
 \label{eq:one-tail-forward-local}\\
 \mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*}
 &=\sup_{H\in\Omega\mathcal Q^n}
 \frac{\widetilde\sigma(H)}{|H|^{s/n}}
 \mathcal P^\alpha
   (H,\mathbf 1_{H^c}\widetilde\omega).
 \label{eq:one-tail-dual-local}
\end{align}
The estimates below use the reproducing Poisson integral
\(\mathcal P^\alpha\).

Both \(P\cap L_0\) and \(E_p\) are contained in a ball
\(B(p,C_1\ell(P))\).  Hence the expressions defining the Hardy
constants vanish when \(\rho>C_1\ell(P)\).  We may therefore fix
\(0<\rho\leq C_1\ell(P)\).

We first estimate
\[
B_1(\rho)
 :=\lambda([0,\rho])
   \int_{[\rho,\infty)}r^{-2s}\,d\tau(r).
\]
Let \(S_{\rho,p}\subset L_0\) be the half-open interval of length
\(2\rho\) which starts at \(p\) when \(p\in P\), and which ends at
\(p\) when \(p\notin P\).  Then \(\{x\in P\cap L_0:|x-p|\leq\rho\}\subset S_{\rho,p}\). Let \(H_\rho=Q_{S_{\rho,p}}\) be the quasicube supplied by
\cref{lem:axis-sections}.  The construction in that lemma gives \(\ell(H_\rho)\approx_\Omega\rho, \quad H_\rho\subset B(p,C_2\rho), \quad |c_{H_\rho}-p|\leq C_2\rho\). Choose \(C_0>4C_2\).  If \(y\in E_p\) and
\(|y-p|\geq C_0\rho\), then \(y\in H_\rho^c\),
\(|y-c_{H_\rho}|\approx|y-p|\), and
\begin{equation*}
 |y-p|^{-2s}
 \leq C\rho^{-s}
 \left(
  \frac{|H_\rho|^{1/n}}
  {\bigl(|H_\rho|^{1/n}+|y-c_{H_\rho}|\bigr)^2}
 \right)^s.
\end{equation*}
The factor \(\lambda([0,\rho])\) is bounded by
\(\widetilde\omega(H_\rho)\), while the part of
\(\widetilde\sigma\) in the integral is supported in \(H_\rho^c\).
Therefore
\begin{align}
 &\lambda([0,\rho])
 \int_{[C_0\rho,\infty)}r^{-2s}\,d\tau(r)\leq
C\frac{\widetilde\omega(H_\rho)}{|H_\rho|^{s/n}}
 \mathcal P^\alpha
   (H_\rho,\mathbf 1_{H_\rho^c}\widetilde\sigma)
 \leq C\mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^\alpha.
 \label{eq:B1-far}
\end{align}

It remains to treat \([\rho,C_0\rho)\).  Put
\begin{align*}
X_\rho^0&=\{x\in P\cap L_0:|x-p|\leq\rho\},\qquad
Y_\rho^1=\{y\in E_p:\rho\leq|y-p|<C_0\rho\}.
\end{align*}
By \eqref{eq:endpoint-cone-proof},
\(\operatorname{dist}(X_\rho^0,Y_\rho^1)\geq c_0\rho\).  Choose
\(0<\delta<c_0/(10\sqrt n\operatorname{Lip}(\Omega))\).  Partition the
preimage space into half-open cubes of side \(\delta\rho\).  Let
\(\mathcal U_\rho\) be the images under \(\Omega\) of the cubes which
meet \(X_\rho^0\), and let \(\mathcal V_\rho\) be those which meet
\(Y_\rho^1\).  Each family has at most \(N=N(n,\Omega,C_0)\) members.
No member of one family is a member of the other.  Thus every
\(U\in\mathcal U_\rho\) and \(V\in\mathcal V_\rho\) is a pair of
disjoint quasicubes, and \(\ell(U)=\ell(V)=\delta\rho, \quad |z-c_U|\leq C\rho\quad(z\in V)\). Consequently, \(\mathcal P^\alpha(U,\mathbf 1_{U^c}\widetilde\sigma) \geq c\frac{|U|^{s/n}}{\rho^{2s}} \widetilde\sigma(V)\). By \eqref{eq:one-tail-forward-local}, \(\rho^{-2s}\widetilde\omega(U)\widetilde\sigma(V) \leq C\mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^\alpha\). Summing over at most \(N^2\) pairs gives
\begin{align}
 &\lambda([0,\rho])
 \int_{[\rho,C_0\rho)}r^{-2s}\,d\tau(r)\leq
 \rho^{-2s}
 \widetilde\omega(X_\rho^0)
 \widetilde\sigma(Y_\rho^1)
 \leq C\mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^\alpha.
 \label{eq:B1-middle}
\end{align}
Equations \eqref{eq:B1-far} and \eqref{eq:B1-middle} show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B1-bound}
B_1\leq C\mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^\alpha.
\end{equation}

We next estimate
\[
B_2(\rho)
 :=\tau([0,\rho])
   \int_{[\rho,\infty)}t^{-2s}\,d\lambda(t).
\]
Partition the preimage space into half-open cubes of side \(\rho\), and
let \(\mathcal H_\rho\) be the images under \(\Omega\) of the cubes
which meet \(Y_\rho^0=\{y\in E_p:|y-p|\leq\rho\}\). There are at most \(N_0=N_0(n,\Omega)\) such quasicubes.  Each
\(H\in\mathcal H_\rho\) satisfies \(\ell(H)=\rho,\quad H\subset B(p,C_3\rho),\quad |c_H-p|\leq C_3\rho\). Choose \(C_4>4C_3\).  Then the source
set \(\{x\in P\cap L_0:|x-p|\geq C_4\rho\}\) lies in \(H^c\) for every
\(H\in\mathcal H_\rho\), and \(|x-p|^{-2s} \leq C\rho^{-s} \left( \frac{|H|^{1/n}} {\bigl(|H|^{1/n}+|x-c_H|\bigr)^2} \right)^s\). The set \(Y_\rho^0\) is covered by \(\mathcal H_\rho\), while
\(\{x\in P\cap L_0:|x-p|\geq C_4\rho\}\subset H^c\) for every
\(H\in\mathcal H_\rho\).  Hence
\begin{align}
 &\tau([0,\rho])
 \int_{[C_4\rho,\infty)}t^{-2s}\,d\lambda(t)\leq
C\sum_{H\in\mathcal H_\rho}
 \frac{\widetilde\sigma(H)}{|H|^{s/n}}
 \mathcal P^\alpha
   (H,\mathbf 1_{H^c}\widetilde\omega)
 \leq C\mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*}.
 \label{eq:B2-far}
\end{align}

For the intermediate part, put \(X_\rho^1 =\{x\in P\cap L_0:\rho\leq|x-p|<C_4\rho\}\). The sets \(X_\rho^1\) and \(Y_\rho^0\) are separated by
\(c_0\rho\).  Repeat the half-open grid construction above, with
quasicubes of side \(\delta\rho\).  The two resulting families have at
most \(N_1=N_1(n,\Omega,C_4)\) members.  If \(U\) meets \(X_\rho^1\) and
\(V\) meets \(Y_\rho^0\), then \(U\cap V=\varnothing\) and \(\mathcal P^\alpha(V,\mathbf 1_{V^c}\widetilde\omega) \geq c\frac{|V|^{s/n}}{\rho^{2s}} \widetilde\omega(U)\). The dual one-tailed condition gives \(\rho^{-2s}\widetilde\sigma(V)\widetilde\omega(U) \leq C\mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*}\). After summing over the finitely many pairs,
\begin{align*}
 &\tau([0,\rho])
 \int_{[\rho,C_4\rho)}t^{-2s}\,d\lambda(t)\leq
 \rho^{-2s}
 \widetilde\sigma(Y_\rho^0)
 \widetilde\omega(X_\rho^1)
 \leq C\mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*}.
\end{align*}
Together with \eqref{eq:B2-far}, this proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B2-bound}
B_2\leq C\mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*}.
\end{equation}
The constant in \eqref{eq:B1-bound} and \eqref{eq:B2-bound} depends only
on \(n\), \(\alpha\), the biLipschitz constants of \(\Omega\), and the
constants in \eqref{eq:coordinate-monotonicity}.  It is independent of
\(P\), \(p\), \(\rho\), and the truncation parameters.

It remains to check atoms on the half-open boundaries.  By
\eqref{eq:no-common-radial-origin}, no common atom occurs on both sides
of the singular Hardy kernel.  The quasicubes used above are also
half-open.  An atom on a boundary belongs to one member of the
partition, and the complementary measure in
\eqref{eq:one-tail-forward-local} or
\eqref{eq:one-tail-dual-local} lies in the corresponding hole.  Thus
\eqref{eq:B1-bound} and \eqref{eq:B2-bound} include endpoint atoms.

Applying \cref{lem:Hardy} to \eqref{eq:hardy-kernel-split} yields
\[
 \int_{E_p}\left|
 \mathbf R_\Psi^{\alpha,n}
   (\mathbf 1_P\widetilde\omega)
 \right|^2d\widetilde\sigma
 \leq C\left(
 \mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^\alpha
 +\mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*}
 \right)\widetilde\omega(P).
\]
Apply this estimate to the two endpoint unions.  Add the unrestricted
testing estimate on \(P\) and the estimates for the finitely many
separated members of the partition of \(3P\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:local-tripled}.

For every fixed globally biLipschitz quasicube map \(\Omega\) and every
Borel set \(E\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A2-restriction}

 \leq C_\Omega\mathfrak A_{2,\Omega}^\alpha(\sigma,\omega).
\end{equation}

Put \(\omega_E=\mathbf 1_E\omega\), let \(\mathfrak P\) be the common atoms
of \((\sigma,\omega)\), and let
\(\mathfrak P_E=\mathfrak P\cap E\).  The two one-tailed constants are
monotone:
\[
 \mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^\alpha(\sigma,\omega_E)
 \leq\mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^\alpha(\sigma,\omega),
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*}(\sigma,\omega_E)
 \leq\mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*}(\sigma,\omega).
\]
It remains to consider the two punctured products
\[
 \frac{\sigma(Q,\mathfrak P_E)\omega_E(Q)}
 {|Q|^{2(1-\alpha/n)}}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \frac{\sigma(Q)\omega_E(Q,\mathfrak P_E)}
 {|Q|^{2(1-\alpha/n)}}.
\]
Fix an \(\Omega\)-quasicube \(Q\).  If
\(Q\cap\mathfrak P=\varnothing\), both products decrease under the
restriction.  We therefore assume that
\(Q\cap\mathfrak P\neq\varnothing\).

We first record an elementary fact.  If \(\mu(Q)<\infty\), the
\(\mu\)-masses of any nonempty collection of atoms in \(Q\) have a
maximum.  Indeed, if the supremum \(M>0\) were not attained, infinitely
many distinct atoms would have mass greater than \(M/2\), contradicting
\(\mu(Q)<\infty\).

Choose \(p\in Q\cap\mathfrak P\) such that \(\sigma(\{p\}) =\max_{q\in Q\cap\mathfrak P}\sigma(\{q\})\). If \(p\in E\), then \(p\) is also a largest common
\(\sigma\)-atom for the restricted pair.  Hence
\(\sigma(Q,\mathfrak P_E)=\sigma(Q,\mathfrak P)\), and the first
punctured product is bounded by
\(A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,\mathrm{punct}}(\sigma,\omega)\).

Suppose that \(p\notin E\).  Then \(\sigma(Q,\mathfrak P_E) \leq\sigma(Q,\mathfrak P)+\sigma(\{p\})\). After division by \(|Q|^{2(1-\alpha/n)}\), the term
\(\sigma(Q,\mathfrak P)\omega_E(Q)\) is bounded by
\(A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,\mathrm{punct}}(\sigma,\omega)\).
Choose \(r\in Q\cap\mathfrak P\) such that \(\omega(\{r\}) =\max_{q\in Q\cap\mathfrak P}\omega(\{q\})\). We write
\[
 \sigma(\{p\})\omega_E(Q)
 =
 \sigma(\{p\})
 \bigl[\omega_E(Q)-\mathbf 1_E(r)\omega(\{r\})\bigr]
 +\mathbf 1_E(r)\sigma(\{p\})\omega(\{r\}).
\]
The expression in brackets is at most
\(\omega(Q)-\omega(\{r\})=\omega(Q,\mathfrak P)\).  Since
\(\sigma(\{p\})\leq\sigma(Q)\), division by
\(|Q|^{2(1-\alpha/n)}\) shows that the first term is bounded by the
original starred punctured characteristic
\(A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*,\mathrm{punct}}(\sigma,\omega)\).

If \(r=p\), the last term vanishes because \(p\notin E\).  It remains
to consider \(r\in E\) and \(r\neq p\).  Put \(a=\Omega^{-1}(p),\quad b=\Omega^{-1}(r),\quad d=|a-b|_\infty\), and let
\[
C=\prod_{k=1}^n[a_k-d/4,a_k+d/4),
 \qquad H=\Omega C.
\]
Then \(p\in H\), \(r\notin H\), and \(\ell(H)=d/2,\quad |H|^{1/n}\approx_\Omega d,\quad |p-r|\approx_\Omega d\). Therefore the starred one-tailed condition with a hole gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*}(\sigma,\omega)
 &\geq
 \frac{\sigma(H)}{|H|^{1-\alpha/n}}
 \mathcal P^\alpha(H,\mathbf 1_{H^c}\omega)\gtrsim_\Omega
 \frac{\sigma(\{p\})\omega(\{r\})}
 {d^{2(n-\alpha)}}.
\end{align*}
Since \(p,r\in Q\), their preimages belong to the ordinary cube
\(\Omega^{-1}Q\).  Thus \(d\leq\ell(Q)\), while
\(|Q|^{1/n}\approx_\Omega\ell(Q)\).  It follows that
\[
 \frac{\sigma(\{p\})\omega(\{r\})}
 {|Q|^{2(1-\alpha/n)}}
 \lesssim_\Omega
 \mathcal A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*}(\sigma,\omega).
\]
This proves the required estimate for the unstarred punctured
characteristic of the restricted pair.

The starred punctured characteristic is termwise monotone.  Indeed,
let \(r\) be a largest common \(\omega\)-atom of the full pair in
\(Q\).  If \(r\in E\), it remains a largest common \(\omega\)-atom
after restriction, and \(\omega_E(Q,\mathfrak P_E) =\omega_E(Q)-\omega(\{r\}) \leq\omega(Q)-\omega(\{r\}) =\omega(Q,\mathfrak P)\). If \(r\notin E\), then \(\omega_E(Q,\mathfrak P_E) \leq\omega_E(Q) \leq\omega(Q)-\omega(\{r\}) =\omega(Q,\mathfrak P)\). Multiplication by
\(\sigma(Q)/|Q|^{2(1-\alpha/n)}\) gives
\[
A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*,\mathrm{punct}}(\sigma,\omega_E)
 \leq
A_{2,\Omega}^{\alpha,*,\mathrm{punct}}(\sigma,\omega).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:A2-restriction}.

diagonal rotation fixed for this chart in the proof of

Euclidean vector norm and vector testing are unchanged, while the
combined Muckenhoupt constants are comparable with dimensional
constants.  A cube \(R_iQ\) in the rigid graph coordinates
corresponds to the rotated cube \(\mathcal U_i^{-1}(R_iQ)\) in the
original coordinates; the translation in \(\mathcal U_i\) only changes
the center.  Therefore the hypothesis over all rotated cubes gives
unrestricted testing for the relabeled pair over
\(R_i\mathcal Q^n\).  Put

 \qquad

 \qquad

\((\widetilde\sigma_i,\widetilde\omega^{(i)})\) over this family.  The
cutoff comparison is uniform in the inner and outer
truncation radii.  The combined Muckenhoupt comparison is

 \leq C\mathfrak A_2^\alpha(\sigma,\omega).

 \mathfrak T_i+\mathfrak T_i^{\mathrm{dual}}

 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}^{\mathrm{dual}}
 \right).
\end{equation}

\noindent\emph{Step 4: apply the $T1$ theorem to the smooth kernel.}
Fix \(i\) and put \(\Omega=R_i\).  For the pair
\((\sigma,\omega_i)\), the preceding steps give

 +\mathcal A_{2,R_i}^{\alpha,*}
 +A_{2,R_i}^{\alpha,\mathrm{punct}}
 +A_{2,R_i}^{\alpha,*,\mathrm{punct}}<\infty,\\
 &\mathcal E_{\alpha,R_i}^{\mathrm{strong}}
 +\mathcal E_{\alpha,R_i}^{\mathrm{strong,dual}}<\infty.
\end{align*}
The energy constants are uniform over every dyadic quasigrid
\(R_i\mathcal D\).  They include the subpartition, alternate-top, and
refined families in \cite[Definition~5]{EnergyCommon}.

For every \(Q\in R_i\mathcal Q^n\) and \(1\leq j\leq n\), vector
testing gives
\begin{align*}
 \int_Q|R_{j,\sigma}^{\alpha,n}(\mathbf 1_Q)|^2\,d\omega_i
 &\leq\mathfrak T_i^2\sigma(Q),\\
 \int_Q|R_{j,\omega_i}^{\alpha,n,*}(\mathbf 1_Q)|^2\,d\sigma
 &\leq(\mathfrak T_i^{\mathrm{dual}})^2\omega_i(Q).
\end{align*}
Thus both scalar quasicube testing conditions hold, and
\eqref{eq:restricted-original-testing} gives their required bounds.
The constants in these two displays use the tangent-line truncations.
Denote the corresponding scalar smooth testing constants by
\(\mathfrak T_{i,j}^{s}\) and
\(\mathfrak T_{i,j}^{s,\mathrm{dual}}\).  By
\eqref{eq:smooth-tangent-comparison}, they satisfy
\[
 \mathfrak T_{i,j}^{s}
 +\mathfrak T_{i,j}^{s,\mathrm{dual}}
 \leq C\left(
 \mathfrak T_i+\mathfrak T_i^{\mathrm{dual}}
 +\sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}\right).
\]
Here we apply \cref{lem:tangent-smooth-comparison} to
\(K_j^{\alpha,n}\), with a constant independent of \(j\).

The scalar Riesz kernel in the current rigid coordinates is \(K_j^{\alpha,n}(x,y) =c_{\alpha,n}\frac{y_j-x_j}{|y-x|^{n+1-\alpha}}\). Direct differentiation gives
\begin{align*}
 |K_j^{\alpha,n}(x,y)|
 &\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha-n},\\
 |\nabla_xK_j^{\alpha,n}(x,y)|
 +|\nabla_yK_j^{\alpha,n}(x,y)|
 &\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha-n-1}.
\end{align*}
If \(|x-x'|\leq|x-y|/2\), the mean value theorem applied to the second
derivatives gives
\[
 |\nabla_xK_j^{\alpha,n}(x,y)-\nabla_xK_j^{\alpha,n}(x',y)|
 \leq C|x-x'|\,|x-y|^{\alpha-n-2}.
\]
The analogous estimate holds in the \(y\)-variable and for the adjoint
kernel.  Thus this is a standard \(\alpha\)-fractional
Calder\'on--Zygmund kernel of smoothness exponent \(\delta=1\).  Its
constants depend only on \(n\) and \(\alpha\).  Therefore
\cite[Theorem~2]{GoodLambda}, with
\(T^\alpha=R_j^{\alpha,n}\) and \(\Omega=R_i\), applies to
\((\sigma,\omega_i)\).  Its hypotheses are the four Muckenhoupt
conditions, the two quasitesting conditions, and the two strong
quasienergy conditions verified above.  It gives
\[
 \mathfrak N_{R_j^{\alpha,n}}^{s}(\sigma,\omega_i)
 \leq C\left(
 \sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}^{\mathrm{dual}}\right).
\]
By \eqref{eq:smooth-tangent-comparison}, the tangent-line norm satisfies
\[
 \mathfrak N_{R_j^{\alpha,n}}(\sigma,\omega_i)
 \leq C\left(
 \sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}^{\mathrm{dual}}\right).
\]
Since
\[
 \|\mathbf R_\sigma^{\alpha,n}f\|_{L^2(\omega_i;\ell^2)}^2
 =\sum_{j=1}^n
 \|R_{j,\sigma}^{\alpha,n}f\|_{L^2(\omega_i)}^2,
\]
we obtain
\begin{equation*}
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}(\sigma,\omega_i)
 \leq C\left(
 \sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}^{\mathrm{dual}}
 \right).
\end{equation*}
Returning to the original coordinates gives the same estimate for
\((\sigma,\omega_i)\).  By \eqref{eq:measure-decomposition},
\begin{align*}
 \|\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}_\sigma f\|_{L^2(\omega)}^2
 &=\sum_{i=1}^N
   \|\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}_\sigma f\|_{L^2(\omega_i)}^2
 \leq C_N\left(
 \sqrt{\mathfrak A_2^\alpha}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}^{\mathrm{dual}}
 \right)^2
 \|f\|_{L^2(\sigma)}^2.
\end{align*}
This proves sufficiency.

We finish with necessity.  Fix any tangent-line truncation
\(\mathbf R_{\varepsilon,R}^{\alpha,n,\mathrm{tl}}\) used in the definition of the
operator norm.  Its kernel is zero on the diagonal.  Hence, for every
rotated cube \(Q\),
\[
 \int_Q
 \left|\mathbf R_{\varepsilon,R}^{\alpha,n,\mathrm{tl}}(\mathbf 1_Q\sigma)\right|^2
d\omega
 \leq
 \mathfrak N_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}^2\sigma(Q).
\]
For each \(1\leq j\leq n\), the \(j\)-th truncated scalar component
has operator norm at most \(\mathfrak N_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}\), and
Hilbert space duality gives the same bound for its adjoint.  Summing the
squared \(L^2(\sigma)\) norms of the \(n\) adjoint components gives
\[
 \int_Q
 \left|
 \bigl(\mathbf R_{\varepsilon,R}^{\alpha,n,\mathrm{tl}}\bigr)^*
 (\mathbf 1_Q\omega)\right|^2d\sigma
 \leq
n\mathfrak N_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}^2\omega(Q).
\]
These estimates are uniform in \(\varepsilon,R\).  Thus
\[
 \mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}
 +\mathfrak T_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}^{\mathrm{dual}}
 \leq(1+\sqrt n)\mathfrak N_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}.
\]

The necessity of the two one-tailed conditions with holes and the two
punctured conditions is proved in
\cite[Theorem~5(2)]{CurvePaper}; see also
\cite[Sections~2.2 and 2.2.1]{CurvePaper}.  That proof has no regularity
assumption on the support of either measure.  It applies to the
tangent-line truncations used here.  Each separated-set comparison in the
cited proof is first made on a bounded piece.  Choose
\(\varepsilon\) smaller than the separation and \(R\) larger than the
diameter of that piece.  The tangent-line multiplier is then one on the
relevant product.  The estimates are independent of \(\varepsilon\) and
\(R\).  Exhausting an unbounded tail by bounded pieces and using monotone
convergence for the positive Poisson expressions gives the full
one-tailed conditions.

For completeness, the fractional Riesz vector has the strong
ellipticity used for the one-tailed conditions.  Cover the unit sphere by
finitely many cones.  For each cone, one can choose a unit vector \(e\)
such that \(\left| e\cdot\mathbf K^{\alpha,n}(x,y) \right| \geq c_{n,\alpha}|x-y|^{\alpha-n}\) whenever \(y-x\) belongs to that cone, and
\(e\cdot\mathbf K^{\alpha,n}(x,y)\) does not change sign there.  The
punctured argument removes a largest common
point mass in each cube.  Since every truncated kernel is zero on the
diagonal, a common atom paired with itself makes no contribution.  Thus
the common-point-mass proof in
\cite[Section~2.2.1]{CurvePaper} gives \(\mathfrak A_2^\alpha \leq C\mathfrak N_{\mathbf R^{\alpha,n}}^2\).
The testing estimates are uniform over all rotations.  If
\(\rho(x)=Ux+a\), where \(U\) is orthogonal, then \(\mathbf K^{\alpha,n}(\rho x,\rho y) =U\mathbf K^{\alpha,n}(x,y)\). The Euclidean norm of the vector kernel is unchanged.  Equivalently,
one may apply \cite[Theorem~5(2)]{CurvePaper} with each rigid motion
as the quasicube map; all its biLipschitz constants are one.  The
estimates are therefore uniform over all rotated cubes.  Together with
the testing estimates above, this proves necessity and the opposite
inequality in \eqref{eq:norm-comparison}.

We first prove sufficiency.  Partition the target tube into finitely many
smaller graph boxes subordinate to enlarged graph neighbourhoods of the
kind constructed in \cref{lem:nested-arcs}.  The part of the curve that
can approach one of these boxes lies in its enlarged graph neighbourhood;
all other pieces of the curve are separated from the box.  After a rigid
motion, the curve in one such neighbourhood has the form \(y=(t,\psi(t)),\quad t\in J\), where \(\|\psi'\|_\infty\) is small.  In a smaller neighbourhood write \(x=(s,\psi(s)+z),\quad z\in\mathbb R^{n-1}\). The Jacobian of this change of variables is one.  The nested-arc
separation in \cref{lem:nested-arcs} and the small slope give \(d_\Gamma(x)\approx|z|, \quad |x-y|\approx\bigl(|s-t|^2+|z|^2\bigr)^{1/2}\). The arc-length density
\((1+|\psi'(t)|^2)^{1/2}\) is bounded above and below.

Extend \(f\) by zero in the tangential variable.  For fixed
\(z\neq0\), the kernel size estimate and Young's inequality give
\[
 \|\mathbf R^{1,n}_{ds_\Gamma}f(\cdot,z)\|_{L_s^2}
 \leq C\|k_{|z|}\|_{L^1}\|f\|_{L^2(ds_\Gamma)},
 \qquad
k_\rho(t):=\mathbf 1_{[-C,C]}(t)(t^2+\rho^2)^{(1-n)/2}.
\]
For \(0<\rho<\rho_0\),
\[
 \|k_\rho\|_{L^1}
 \leq
 \begin{cases}
C\rho^{2-n},&n\geq3,\\
C\log(e/\rho),&n=2.
 \end{cases}
\]
When \(n\geq3\), polar coordinates in \(\mathbb R^{n-1}\) give
\[
 \int_{|z|<\rho_0}|z|^\beta\|k_{|z|}\|_{L^1}^2\,dz
 \leq C\int_0^{\rho_0}\rho^{\beta+2-n}\,d\rho.
\]
This is finite exactly when \(\beta>n-3\).  When \(n=2\), the
corresponding integral is
\[
C\int_0^{\rho_0}\rho^\beta\log^2(e/\rho)\,d\rho,
\]
which is finite exactly when \(\beta>-1=n-3\).

Near an endpoint, the region \(s\geq0\) is covered by the preceding
estimate.  On the remaining region \(s<0\), put
\(s_-=-s\) and \(\delta=(s_-^2+|z|^2)^{1/2}\).  Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \left|
 \int_0^{|J|}
 \frac{f(t)}{((s-t)^2+|z|^2)^{(n-1)/2}}\,dt
 \right|^2
 \leq C\delta^{3-2n}\|f\|_2^2.
\]
Integration in the \(n\) variables \((s_-,z)\) again gives
\[
C\int_0^{\rho_0}\delta^{\beta+2-n}\,d\delta.
\]
Contributions from nonlocal pieces of the curve have bounded kernels.
They map \(L^2(ds_\Gamma)\) to \(L^2(\omega_\beta)\) because
\(\omega_\beta(U_{\rho_0})<\infty\).  The Green remainder has the same
property.  Summing the finitely many local estimates proves sufficiency.
The argument also bounds all tangent-line truncations uniformly, since
their multipliers are bounded by a fixed constant.

For \(n=2\), the assumed local finiteness condition is already

\(\gamma'(0)\).

\(z_s:=z-(z\cdot\tau_s)\tau_s\).  Then \(|z_s|\approx|z|\).  After translating
the tangent variable by \(z\cdot\tau_s\), the normal part of the tangent

\[
 -z_s\int_{-r+O(|z|)}^{r+O(|z|)}

\]
Its absolute value is comparable to \(|z|^{2-n}\); changes at the two
finite endpoints are bounded.

C\int_0^r
 \frac{t\vartheta_\gamma(t)}{(t^2+\rho^2)^{n/2}}\,dt,
 \qquad \rho:=|z|.
\]
A dyadic decomposition at \(t=\rho\) bounds this by
\[
C\rho^{2-n}
 \left(
  \vartheta_\gamma(\rho)
  +\sum_{\substack{k\geq0\\2^k\rho<r}}
   2^{-k(n-2)}\vartheta_\gamma(2^{k+1}\rho)
 \right).
\]

than the fixed tangent-line lower bound.  The part of the source outside the smaller arc and the Green remainder
are continuous across that arc.  The singular term tends to infinity,

 \geq c\int_0^{\rho_1}

 =c\int_0^{\rho_1}\rho^{\beta+2-n}\,d\rho.
\]
The last integral diverges when \(\beta\leq n-3\), with logarithmic
divergence at equality.  This proves necessity.
