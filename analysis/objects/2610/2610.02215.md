<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The search begins with the empty word and tries each possible next letter.
If an extension of a word avoiding additive squares creates one, the new
occurrence must end at the appended letter. With prefix sums $S_i$, the last
two blocks of length $k$ have equal sum exactly when
\begin{equation}
 S_n-2S_{n-k}+S_{n-2k}=0.\label{M-eq:suffix}
\end{equation}
Reject an extension if this holds for some $1\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$;
otherwise continue recursively. Induction on length shows that this procedure
visits every word avoiding additive squares, and no others.

For each alphabet, the completed search has a surviving word at the
listed length $g$ and no survivor at length $g+1$. The witnesses in
Supplement S1 prove the lower bounds; the empty final layers prove
the upper bounds, since every longer avoiding word would have an
avoiding prefix in that layer. Supplement S5 specifies the complete
runs and their subtree partitions.

The value 62 and its two mutually reversed maximizers were reported in
\cite{M-P26}; the search here reobtains them. For the values 86 and 88,
separately written implementations also exhaust all 3372 subtrees rooted at
avoiding prefixes of length eight. Their totals agree with the single-process
search. The search invariant and the completion of every subtree, rather than
agreement of totals alone, establish exhaustiveness.


Every $c=6,7$
search was completed by a graphics-processor breadth-first implementation
and at least one separately written CPU depth-first program using
\eqref{M-eq:suffix}. Supplement S5 reports visit counts and distinguishes
single-process counts from parallel totals. Supplement S1 prints all thirteen
maximum-length witnesses. This proves every entry for the stated
normalized classes through diameter seven.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{M-lem:domain}\label{M-lem:sign}
The zero set of a nonzero equation \eqref{M-eq:line} meets $D$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
 (\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3,\ \delta_2+\delta_3,\ \delta_3)
 \label{M-eq:gaps}
\end{equation}
has both a positive and a negative entry.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For positive consecutive gaps $p,q,r$, the four letters are
$0,p,p+q,p+q+r$. The block-sum difference is the dot product of
\eqref{M-eq:gaps} with $(p,q,r)$. A nonzero linear form vanishes at a strictly
positive vector exactly when its coefficients have both signs: balance the
positive and negative contributions, and assign arbitrary positive values to
coordinates with zero coefficient. Dividing by $p$ gives a point of $D$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{M-prop:cover}
The 77 words $W_1,\ldots,W_{77}$ of length 139 in Supplement S2 satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \bigcap_{i=1}^{77}B(W_i)=(H\cap D)\cup E.\label{M-eq:cover}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For every listed word, compute all block-count differences, reject a zero
vector, and form the line set by Lemma~\ref{M-lem:domain}. The check verifies
that every word fails on $H$ and at all fourteen points of $E$.
For the converse, work in $D\setminus(H\cup E)$ and represent the current
intersection as whole lines together with isolated points. Start with the
first word's line union. When intersecting with the next word's line union,
retain common lines, retain existing points on at least one new line, and
add intersections of old and new distinct lines. Keep only points in the
working domain, and remove points already represented by retained lines.

For $a+bx+cy=0$ and $d+ex+fy=0$, a unique intersection exists when $bf-ce\ne0$,
and is
\begin{equation}
 x=\frac{cd-af}{bf-ce},\qquad y=\frac{ae-bd}{bf-ce}.\label{M-eq:intersection}
\end{equation}
Distinct parallel lines contribute nothing; coincident lines are retained.
These rules preserve the exact represented set at each step and require only
integer and rational arithmetic.

The supplied independently implemented checker performs all
$77\cdot4830=371{,}910$ comparisons and the stated set operations.
After the tenth word no whole residual line remains, and 125 isolated points
remain. The remaining 67 words remove all of them. Supplement S2 prints the
