<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:head}
For $0\le t\le1/5$, a zero of $H_t$ with $|\operatorname{Re} z|\le X$ satisfies
$|\operatorname{Im} z|<1/5$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Initially all zeros in this finite region are real. The classical strip
bound gives $|\operatorname{Im} z|\le1$ throughout the time interval. If the
conclusion fails, consider the closed set of zero-time pairs with
$0\le x\le X$, $1/5\le y\le1$, and $0\le t\le1/5$.
It is compact and disjoint from $t=0$, so its least time $t_1>0$
is attained. No zero can enter across $x=X$ at height at least $1/5$,
by \eqref{eq:wall}; the same holds at $x=-X$ by symmetry.
Continuity of zeros therefore forces a first contact at height
$h=1/5$. We do not need continuity of the unrestricted maximum over
a window whose vertical sides other zeros can cross.
At a first contact in $0\le x\le X$, the right
boundary is excluded by \eqref{eq:wall}. The imaginary axis is zero-free
because $H_t(iy)$ is an integral of a positive integrand.
A multiple contact is excluded by backward Hermite splitting as in
\cite[Proposition 3.1(ii)]{P}: a zero of multiplicity $m\ge2$ at
$z_1=x_1+ih$ has, at time $t_1-v$, a neighbouring zero
$z_1+i\sqrt{2v}\lambda_m+O(v)$, where $\lambda_m>0$ is the largest
root of the probabilists' Hermite polynomial. Its imaginary part is
larger than $h$ for small $v>0$, contradicting first contact. Because
the contact is away from the vertical sides, this earlier zero remains
inside the finite region.

For a simple contact zero $x+ih$, its own conjugate contributes $-1/h$
to its vertical velocity. Every other conjugate pair contributes $-2K_h$,
where
\[
 K_h(d,v)=\frac{h-v}{d^2+(h-v)^2}
          +\frac{h+v}{d^2+(h+v)^2}
 =\frac{2h(d^2+h^2-v^2)}
        {[d^2+(h-v)^2][d^2+(h+v)^2]}.
\]
Pairs with $v\le h$ have nonnegative contribution to $K_h$.
A pair with $v>h$ lies outside $[-X-1,X+1]$ by first contact and
\eqref{eq:wall}, so $|d|\ge1$ and $v\le1$ imply $K_h>0$.
Real zeros carry half-pair weight. Thus $h'\le-1/h<0$, contradicting
first contact from below. Compactness of the finite rectangle and
continuity of zeros justify attainment of a first contact.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:approx}
If $x\ge6\cdot10^{10}$, $0\le t\le1/5$, and $3\le y\le6$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx}
 \left|\frac{H_t(z)}{B_t(z)}-
       P_N(s_B,t)-\gamma P_N(s_A,t)\right|<10^{-6}.
\end{equation}
On the subband $4\le y\le6$, one also has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disk}
 \left|\frac{H_t(z)}{B_t(z)}-1\right|<\frac7{20}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We use the exact heat-convolution decomposition in
\cite[equations (39)--(41)]{P} and its Proposition 6.2 for the
Riemann--Siegel coefficients at arbitrary real part. For the summands
we recover the bound of its Proposition 6.1 using a vertical Stirling
estimate. We do not apply its Theorem 1.3 or Proposition 6.3 at these
larger heights.

\emph{Source formulas and their domains.}
Here we restate the inputs with their original equation numbers.
For $g^*(s)=\overline{g(\overline s)}$, equation (39) of~\cite{P} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomposition}
 H_t(z)=\sum_{n\le N}r_{t,n}(v)+\sum_{n\le N}r_{t,n}^*(w)
             +R_{t,N}(v)+R_{t,N}^*(w).
\end{equation}
It holds for $t>0$, $\operatorname{Re} z\ne0$, and every integer $N\ge0$.
Writing
\[
 \mathcal M(s)=\frac{s(s-1)}{16}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),\qquad
 I_N(s)=\int_{N\swarrow N+1}
       \frac{u^{-s}e^{i\pi u^2}}{e^{\pi iu}-e^{-\pi iu}}\,du,
\]
equations (37)--(38) give $r_{0,n}(s)=\mathcal M(s)n^{-s}$ and
$R_{0,N}(s)=\mathcal M(s)I_N(s)$. The contour is directed southwest
across $(N,N+1)$. Equations (40)--(41) say that, for either of these
functions $g_0$ and its Gaussian evolution $g_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shifted-heat}
 g_t(s)=e^{-t\eta^2/4}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-\sqrt t V\eta}
  g_0(s+\sqrt t V+t\eta/2)\frac{e^{-V^2}}{\sqrt\pi}\,dV,
\end{equation}
provided $\operatorname{Im} s$ and $\operatorname{Im}(s+t\eta/2)$ have the same nonzero sign.

For real $u$, $T'>0$, $a=\sqrt{T'/(2\pi)}$, $N=\lfloor a\rfloor$,
and $\rho=1-2(a-N)$, Proposition 6.2 gives, for every integer $K\ge1$,
\begin{gather}
 I_N(u+iT')=\omega_N a^{-u}
     \left(\sum_{k=0}^K\frac{C_k(\rho,u)}{a^k}
                           +RS_K(u+iT')\right),\label{eq:RS-source}\\
 \omega_N=(-1)^{N-1}
   \exp\left[-i\left(\frac{T'}2\log\frac{T'}{2\pi}
                         -\frac{T'}2-\frac\pi8\right)\right].\notag
\end{gather}
Its equations (53) and (58) give
\[
 C_0(\rho,u)=C_0(\rho)=
 \frac{e^{\pi i(\rho^2/2+3/8)}-i\sqrt2\cos(\pi\rho/2)}
      {2\cos(\pi\rho)},\qquad |C_0(\rho)|\le\tfrac12,
\]
with removable values at $\rho=\pm1/2$. Its equations (54)--(57) are
\begin{align}
 |C_k(\rho,u)|&\le\frac{\sqrt2}{2\pi}
            \frac{9^u\Gamma(k/2)}{2^k} &&(u>0,\ k\ge1),\label{eq:C-positive}\\
 |C_k(\rho,u)|&\le P_0\,2^{-u}\Gamma(k/2)\kappa_0^k
                  &&(u\le0,\ k\ge1),\label{eq:C-negative}\\
 |RS_K(u+iT')|&\le\tfrac17\,2^{3u/2}\Gamma((K+1)/2)(1.1/a)^{K+1}
                  &&(u\ge0),\label{eq:RS-positive}\\
 |RS_K(u+iT')|&\le\tfrac12(9/10)^{\lceil-u\rceil}
               \Gamma((K+1)/2)(1.1/a)^{K+1}
                  &&(u<0,\ K+u\ge2),\label{eq:RS-negative}
\end{align}
where $P_0=\sqrt2/(4\pi^2)$ and
$\kappa_0=((3-2\log2)\pi)^{-1/2}$. There is no upper bound on $K$
and no lower bound on $u$; the negative-part remainder requires
exactly the displayed condition $K+u\ge2$.

\emph{A uniform vertical Stirling estimate.}
Euler--Maclaurin gives, on the slit plane,
\begin{align*}
 \theta(z)&=\log\Gamma(z)-(z-\tfrac12)\operatorname{Log} z+z-\tfrac12\log(2\pi)\\
 &=\frac1{12z}-\frac1{360z^3}
   -\frac14\int_0^\infty\frac{B_4(\{u\})}{(z+u)^4}\,du.
\end{align*}
Here the branch of $\log\Gamma$ is continued from the positive real
axis. The integral is holomorphic off the nonpositive real axis, so
analytic continuation extends the identity from $\operatorname{Re} z>0$.
Since $|B_4|\le1/30$ and
$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(u^2+v_0^2)^{-2}\,du=\pi/(2v_0^3)$,
for $v_0=|\operatorname{Im} z|>1$ we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertical-stirling}
 |\theta(z)|\le\frac1{12v_0}
       +\frac{1/360+\pi/240}{v_0^3}
       <\frac1{12(v_0-1)}.
\end{equation}
This estimate is uniform in the real part, including its unbounded
negative tail.

Here are details of the real-part extension. They also control the
unbounded negative tail in the Gaussian integral.
Write $T=x/2$, $T'=T+\pi t/8$, $a=\sqrt{T'/(2\pi)}$, and $A=T-3$.
For $-3\le\sigma\le4$, the remainder has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}

 \qquad |\omega_N|=1,

where $|C_0|\le1/2$. We claim the deliberately loose bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps}
 |\varepsilon_\sigma|\le
 \frac{0.3\,9^\sigma+0.04\,2^{-\sigma}}{a-1}+10^{-8}<\frac1{25}.
\end{equation}

On the shifted contour in \eqref{eq:shifted-heat}, put
$U=\sigma+\sqrt t V$, where $V$ has density $e^{-V^2}/\sqrt\pi$.

\eqref{eq:vertical-stirling} and Taylor expansion of $\log M_0$

 \frac{U^2+6|U|+2/3}{4A}\le\frac{U^2+1}{A}.
\]

$0<h^2\le A_2$, $0\le v\le h$, and $(d,v)\ne(0,h)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-derivative}
 K_h(d,v)\ge h\left(\frac\lambda p K_p(d,v)
                              -\mu\partial_pK_p(d,v)\right).
\end{equation}

Set $D=d^2$, $r=v^2$. Direct differentiation gives
\[
 K_p=\frac{2pw}{\Delta},\qquad
 \partial_pK_p=\frac{(2w+4B)\Delta-8Bw^2}{\Delta^2}.
\]
Thus the bracket in \eqref{eq:kernel-derivative} is

 \frac{K_h}{h}=\frac{2v_0}{v_0^2+4Dr}
 \ge\frac2{v_0+4r}=\frac2{D+A+3r}

$\Phi\ge0$ gives $2/(D+A_2+3r)\ge\mathcal N/\Delta^2$.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:two-density}
If $\varepsilon_0<1/2$, then for $x\ge X$ and $t^-\le t\le t^+$,
\begin{align}
 S(x,p,t)&\ge C^*:=5.5716-10^{-6}-c_1\psi
                         -\frac{\varepsilon_1}{1-\varepsilon_0},
                         \label{eq:new-C}\\
 \partial_pS(x,p,t)&\le G^*:=c_2\psi_2
       +\frac{\varepsilon_2}{1-\varepsilon_0}
       +\left(\frac{\varepsilon_1}{1-\varepsilon_0}\right)^2
       +2\cdot10^{-10}.\label{eq:new-G}
\end{align}

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
All primes below denote complex differentiation in $z$, with $t$ fixed.
Write $H_t=B_tF$. Since $S=-\operatorname{Im}(H_t'/H_t)$,
$\partial_yS=-\operatorname{Re}(H_t'/H_t)'$.

\emph{Normalizer.} Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \alpha''(s)=s^{-3}+2(s-1)^{-3}-\tfrac12s^{-2},\qquad
 |\alpha''|\le2/x^2+24/x^3<3/x^2.
\]
With $\beta=\alpha+(t/2)\alpha\alpha'$, one has
$B_t'/B_t=-i\beta(w)/2$ and $(B_t'/B_t)'=-\beta'(w)/4$.
The bounds already proved for $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ yield
\[
 |\beta'|\le|\alpha'|+\tfrac t2(|\alpha'|^2+|\alpha||\alpha''|)
 <10^{-10},\qquad |s_B''|\le t|\alpha''|/8<10^{-20}.
\]
More precisely, $|s_B'|\le1/2+t/[4(x-6)]<1/2+10^{-12}$.
Consequently $|s_B'|\le c_1$ and $|s_B'|^2\le c_2$; the latter
inequality is obtained from this sharper bound, not by squaring $c_1$.

\emph{Fixed cutoff.} At $z_0=x+ip$ put $N_0=N(z_0)$ and, on
$|z-z_0|\le r_0$, define
\[
 P=P_{N_0}(s_B,t),\quad R=\gamma P_{N_0}(s_A,t),\quad E=F-P-R,
 \qquad T=F-\zeta(s_B),\quad \varepsilon=T/\zeta(s_B).
\]
The disk lies in $3\le\operatorname{Im} z\le3.1$ and $\operatorname{Re} z>6\cdot10^{10}$.
The cutoff changes by at most one. The same argument as in
\eqref{eq:frozen-error}, with extra summands bounded by
$3/60000^2$, gives $|E|<2\cdot10^{-6}$ on the disk and
$|E(z_0)|<10^{-6}$. Cauchy's estimates therefore give
$|E'(z_0)|\le\eta_1$ and $|E''(z_0)|\le\eta_2$.

At the centre, $\operatorname{Re} s_B\ge u$. The finite perturbation
$P-\zeta(s_B)$ has terms $(h_n-1)n^{-s_B}$ for $n\le N_0$
and $-n^{-s_B}$ for $n>N_0$.
Because $N_0\ge N_*>N_1$, the perturbation tail $N_1<n\le N_0$
and the zeta tail $n>N_0$ are disjoint. Each is bounded by
$n^{-u_8}$, so their union is covered by the single integral tail
in \eqref{eq:new-delta}; there is no omitted zeta tail.
Termwise differentiation then gives
\begin{align*}
 |P-\zeta(s_B)|&\le\delta_0,\\
 |(P-\zeta(s_B))'|&\le c_1\delta_1,\\
 |(P-\zeta(s_B))''|&\le c_2\delta_2+10^{-20}\delta_1.
\end{align*}
Here the integral test applies since $(\log y)^j y^{-u_8}$ decreases
for $y\ge N_1$, $j\le2$.

For $R$, equation~\eqref{eq:reflected-heat} and $e>0$ give
\[
 \sum_{n\le N_0}|\gamma h_nn^{-s_A}|
 \le1.01N_0^{-p}\left(\frac{N_0^{e+1}}{e+1}+N_0^e\right).
\]
A reflected summand $\tau_n$ has $\tau_n'=\lambda_n\tau_n$,
$\tau_n''=(\lambda_n^2+\lambda_n')\tau_n$, where
$\lambda_n=\gamma'/\gamma-s_A'\log n$.
The normalizer bounds give $|\lambda_n|\le L+2$ and
$|\lambda_n'|<10^{-9}$: use
$\gamma'/\gamma=(i/2)(\beta(v)+\beta(w))$ and the bounds for
$\beta'$ and $s_A''$.
Since $L+2<2\log N_0+3$, the preceding sum multiplied by
$(2\log N_0+3)^j$, $j\le2$, decreases with $N_0\ge N_*$.
Indeed, each power of $N_0$ has exponent at most $1-u_8\le-1.025$,
whereas its logarithmic factor contributes at most
$4/(2\log N_0+3)<0.16$ to its logarithmic derivative.
Thus $|R^{(j)}|\le\rho_j$, for $j=0,1,2$.
It follows that $|T|\le e_0$, $|T'|\le e_1$, and
\[
 |T''|\le c_2\delta_2+\rho_2+\eta_2+10^{-20}\delta_1.
\]

\emph{Division by zeta.} Put $\varrho=\zeta'/\zeta$ and
$\iota=1/\zeta(s_B)$. The absolutely convergent Euler product gives
$|\varrho(s_B)|\le\psi$, $|\varrho'(s_B)|\le\psi_2$, and
$|\iota|\le Z$. Differentiation yields
\[
 |\iota'|\le Zc_1\psi,\qquad
 |\iota''|\le Z\{10^{-20}\psi+c_2(\psi_2+\psi^2)\}.
\]
The small $s_B''$ term in this second inequality must be retained.
In the estimate for $(T\iota)''$, the two such contributions total
at most $10^{-20}Z(\delta_1+\psi e_0)$.
Since $Ze_0<1/2$, we have $\delta_0<1/2$ and $e_0<1/2$.
Moreover $\psi\le J(2)<1$ and $\delta_1<7$: the finite part is
at most $(\log N_1)\delta_0<6$ and its integral tail is below
$10^{-3}$. These elementary inequalities are included in
Section~\ref{sec:kernel-checks}. Therefore the contribution is
less than $8\cdot10^{-20}Z$, covered by the allowance $10^{-9}Z$
in $e_2$. Leibniz's rule now proves
$|\varepsilon^{(j)}|\le\varepsilon_j$, $j=0,1,2$.

Finally $F=\zeta(s_B)(1+\varepsilon)$, so
\begin{align*}
 F'/F&=s_B'\varrho(s_B)+\varepsilon'/(1+\varepsilon),\\
 (F'/F)'&=s_B''\varrho(s_B)+(s_B')^2\varrho'(s_B)
       +\varepsilon''/(1+\varepsilon)
       -(\varepsilon'/(1+\varepsilon))^2.
\end{align*}
Combine these identities with
$-\operatorname{Im}(B_t'/B_t)\ge L/4-10^{-6}$ and
$|(B_t'/B_t)'|<10^{-10}$ to obtain \eqref{eq:new-C}--\eqref{eq:new-G}.
In particular, the last $2\cdot10^{-10}$ covers both the normalizer
and $s_B''\varrho$ terms. The time endpoints in the proposition
were inserted termwise in the heat bounds and the positive finite
sums; no monotonicity of $H_t'/H_t$ itself is asserted.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:derivative-step}
Assume $M(0)\le q_0<B=p^2$ and choose
$0=t_0<\cdots<t_n\le1/5$, $d_i=t_{i+1}-t_i$, and
$q_0>q_1>\cdots>q_n\ge b=1/25$.
For $0\le i<n$, suppose $C_i,G_i,\lambda_i,\mu_i\ge0$ satisfy
\[
 S(x,p,t)\ge C_i,\qquad \partial_pS(x,p,t)\le G_i
 \quad(x\ge X,\ t_i\le t\le t_{i+1}),
\]
and the polynomial \eqref{eq:kernel-poly}, with
$(A_2,\lambda,\mu)=(q_i,\lambda_i,\mu_i)$, is nonnegative for
$D\ge0$, $0\le r\le q_i$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:new-k}
 k_i=4\left\{\frac{\lambda_i C_i}{p}-\mu_iG_i
       -\frac{2\lambda_i}{B-q_i}
       -\frac{2\mu_i(B+q_i)}{(B-q_i)^2}\right\}>0.
\end{equation}
If $e^{k_id_i}(2+k_iq_{i+1})\ge2+k_iq_i$ for every $i$, then
$M(t_n)\le q_n$ and $\Lambda\le t_n+q_n/2$.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
At a highest simple zero $x+ih$, with $b<q=h^2\le q_i$, symmetry
and Lemma~\ref{lem:head} permit $x>X$. Its velocity is
$h'=-1/h-2\sum'K_h(d_j,v_j)$, where the sum omits its own
conjugate pair and real zeros have half-pair weight.
Every $v_j\le h$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel-derivative} applies.
The full sums at height $p$ are $S$ and $\partial_pS$. They converge
absolutely and locally uniformly in $p$ above the zero strip, as do
the differentiated sums: paired summands are $O(d_j^{-2})$ there.
The removed pair contributes
\[
 K_p(0,h)=\frac{2p}{B-q},\qquad
 \partial_pK_p(0,h)=-\frac{2(B+q)}{(B-q)^2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \sum\nolimits'K_h\ge h\left\{
 \frac{\lambda_i}{p}\left(C_i-\frac{2p}{B-q}\right)
 -\mu_i\left(G_i+\frac{2(B+q)}{(B-q)^2}\right)\right\}
 \ge hk_i/4.
\]
The last bracket decreases with $q<B$. Thus $q'\le-2-k_iq$.
Induction, the strict barriers, compact attainment at positive time,
and backward vertical Hermite splitting are exactly as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:step}; at contact the classical strip theorem gives
$q\le q_i$, which is the polynomial certificate's range. If the
barrier reaches $b$ early, the strip theorem keeps $M\le b$ for
all later times. Otherwise the stated exponential gate gives
$M(t_{i+1})\le q_{i+1}$. The final strip theorem proves the conclusion.
