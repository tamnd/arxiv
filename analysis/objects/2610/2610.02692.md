<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:N0-basis}
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:el-def}
    e_l^{(d)}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{d,l}}}\sum_{|\alpha|=l}z^{\alpha}, l\ge0
    \end{equation}
    is an orthonormal basis of $N_0$, where \(C_{d,l}=\binom{l+d-1}{d-1}\), $\alpha=(\alpha_1,\cdots,\alpha_d)\in\mathbb{N}^{d}$, $|\alpha|=\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_d=l$ and $z^{\alpha}=z_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots z_d^{\alpha_d}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Firstly, we show $e_l^{(d)}\perp M_0$. For each generator $g_{i}=z_i-z_{i+1}$ and each monomial $z^\alpha$, we have
   \[\langle g_{i} z^\alpha, e_l^{(d)}\rangle
   = \langle z^{\alpha+e_i}-z^{\alpha+e_{i+1}}, e_l^{(d)}\rangle = 0,\]
   since the two terms cancel by symmetry of the symmetrized sum defining $e_l^{(d)}$, where $e_{i}=\overbrace{(0,\cdots,0,1,0\cdots,0)}^{i-th}$. Hence $e_l^{(d)}\perp M_0$.

   Secondly, if $f=\sum_\alpha a_\alpha z^\alpha\in N_0$. Then 
   \[\langle f,(z_i-z_{i+1})z^\beta\rangle=0\text{ for all }i,\beta\] 
   implies 
   \[a_{\beta+e_i}=a_{\beta+e_{i+1}}.\] 
   Thus all coefficients with the same total degree $l$ are equal, i.e., $a_\alpha=a_\beta$ if $|\alpha|=|\beta|$, we assume it is $c_l$ if $|\beta|=l$. Hence
   \[f=\sum_{l\ge0} c_l \sum_{|\alpha|=l}z^\alpha= \sum_{l\ge0} c_l\sqrt{C_{d,l}}\, e_l^{(d)},\]
   so $\{e_l^{(d)}\}$ spans $N_0$. Therefore it is an orthonormal basis.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:basis}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Esl-def}
    E_{s,l}= \left(\prod_{i=1}^d \theta_{s_i}^{(i)}(z_i)\right)
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{d,l}}}  \sum_{i_1+\cdots+i_d=l}
    \eta_1(z_1)^{i_1}\cdots\eta_d(z_d)^{i_d},\qquad0\le s_i<d_i,\ l\ge0,
\end{equation}
is an orthonormal basis of $N_{\eta}$, where $C_{d,l}$ as in the lemma.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By Wold decomposition, for each $1\le i\le d$ we have
    \[H^2(z_i)=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\eta_i(z_i)^m K_i .\]
    For every multi-index $s=(s_1,\dots,s_d)$ with $0\le s_i<d_i$, set
    \[H_{s_i}^2(z_i)=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{\theta_{s_i}^{(i)}(z_i)\eta_i(z_i)^m:\ m\ge 0\right\}.\]
    Then
    \[H^2(\mathbb D^d)=\bigoplus_s\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^d H_{s_i}^2(z_i)\right).\]
    Fix such an index $s$. Define for $m_i\ge 0$
    \[\begin{array}{llll}
    	U_s:&H^2(\mathbb D^d)&\longrightarrow&\bigotimes_{i=1}^d H_{s_i}^2(z_i)\\
    	&z_1^{m_1}\cdots z_d^{m_d}&\mapsto&\prod_{i=1}^d\theta_{s_i}^{(i)}(z_i)\eta_i(z_i)^{m_i}.\
    \end{array}\]  
    Since $\{z_1^{m_1}\cdots z_d^{m_d}\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $H^2(\mathbb D^d)$ and
    \[\left\{\prod_{i=1}^d\theta_{s_i}^{(i)}(z_i)\eta_i(z_i)^{m_i}:\ m_i\ge 0\right\}\]
    is an orthonormal basis of $\bigotimes_{i=1}^d H_{s_i}^2(z_i)$, the map $U_s$ can be extent to an unitary operator.

    We now check the image of $M_0$ under $U_s$. For $1\le i\le d-1$, $U_s$ intertwines multiplication by $z_i-z_{i+1}$ on $H^2(\mathbb D^d)$ with multiplication by $\eta_i(z_i)-\eta_{i+1}(z_{i+1})$ on the target space, up to the fixed factor $\prod_{j=1}^d\theta_{s_j}^{(j)}(z_j)$. Concretely, for any monomial $z_1^{m_1}\cdots z_d^{m_d}$,
    \[U_s\bigl((z_i-z_{i+1})z_1^{m_1}\cdots z_d^{m_d}\bigr)=(\eta_i(z_i)-\eta_{i+1}(z_{i+1}))\prod_{j=1}^d\theta_{s_j}^{(j)}(z_j)\eta_j(z_j)^{m_j}.\]
    This relation extends linearly to all polynomials: for any polynomial $p$,
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:intertwine-poly}
       U_s\bigl((z_i-z_{i+1})p(z)\bigr)
       =(\eta_i(z_i)-\eta_{i+1}(z_{i+1}))\,U_s(p).
    \end{equation}
    Since polynomials are dense in $H^2(\mathbb{D}^d)$ and $U_s$ is unitary (hence bounded), the relation~\eqref{eq:intertwine-poly} holds for all $f\in H^2(\mathbb{D}^d)$ with $(z_i-z_{i+1})f\in H^2(\mathbb{D}^d)$. Consequently, $U_s$ maps the closed ideal generated by $\{z_i-z_{i+1}\}$ onto the restriction to $\bigotimes_i H_{s_i}^2(z_i)$ of the closed ideal generated by $\{\eta_i-\eta_{i+1}\}$, i.e.,
    \[U_s(M_0)=M_\eta\cap\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^d H_{s_i}^2(z_i)\right).\]
    Since $U_s$ is unitary, it preserves orthogonal complements, so
    \[U_s(N_0)=\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^d H_{s_i}^2(z_i)\right)\ominus M_\eta.\]
    Applying $U_s$ to the orthonormal basis $\{e_l^{(d)}\}_{l\ge 0}$ of $N_0$ gives
    \[U_s(e_l^{(d)})=\left(\prod_{i=1}^d\theta_{s_i}^{(i)}(z_i)\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{d,l}}}\sum_{i_1+\cdots+i_d=l}\eta_1(z_1)^{i_1}\cdots\eta_d(z_d)^{i_d}.\]
    Since $U_s$ is unitary, these vectors form an orthonormal basis of
    $\bigl(\bigotimes_{i=1}^d H_{s_i}^2(z_i)\bigr)\ominus M_\eta$.

    Since the slices are mutually orthogonal, we may take the orthogonal direct sum over all admissible multi-indices $s$, we conclude that $\{E_{s,l}\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $N_\eta$.
    When $d=2$, we have $C_{2,l}=l+1$, and the basis reduces to
    \[\theta_{s_1}^{(1)}(z_1)\theta_{s_2}^{(2)}(z_2)\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{l+1}}\sum_{i_1+i_2=l}\eta_1(z_1)^{i_1}\eta_2(z_2)^{i_2}.\]

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:Bd}
   $B_d$ is a weighted shift with respect to $\{e_l^{(d)}\}_{l\ge0}$, i.e.,
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:Bd-action}
      B_d\,e_l^{(d)}=\sqrt{\frac{l+1}{l+d}}\;e_{l+1}^{(d)},
   \end{equation}
   and its defect operators are diagonal
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:Bd-defect}
      (I-B_dB_d^*)\,e_l^{(d)}=\frac{d-1}{l+d-1}\,e_l^{(d)},\qquad (I-B_d^*B_d)\,e_l^{(d)}=\frac{d-1}{l+d}\,e_l^{(d)}.
   \end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The coefficient $\langle M_{z_i}e_l^{(d)},e_{l+1}^{(d)}\rangle$ is independent of $i$ by symmetry. A direct computation gives
    \[\langle z_i e_l^{(d)},\,e_{l+1}^{(d)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{d,l}\,C_{d,l+1}}}\cdot C_{d,l}=\sqrt{\frac{C_{d,l}}{C_{d,l+1}}}=\sqrt{\frac{l+1}{l+d}},\]
    using $C_{d,l+1}=\frac{l+d}{l+1}C_{d,l}$. Since $z_i e_l^{(d)}$ is homogeneous of degree $l+1$, its projection of this vector onto $N_0$ is a constant multiple of $e_{l+1}^{(d)}$, giving \eqref{eq:Bd-action}.

    The adjoint acts by
    \[B_d^* e_l^{(d)}=\sqrt{\frac{l}{l+d-1}}\;e_{l-1}^{(d)}\enspace(l\ge1),\qquad B_d^* e_0^{(d)}=0.\]
    Hence
    \[B_d^*B_d e_l^{(d)}=\frac{l+1}{l+d}\,e_l^{(d)}\enspace(l\ge0),\qquad B_dB_d^* e_l^{(d)}=\frac{l}{l+d-1}\,e_l^{(d)}\enspace(l\ge1),\qquad B_dB_d^* e_0^{(d)}=0.\]
    The formula for $B_dB_d^*$ also holds at $l=0$. Therefore
    \[(I-B_dB_d^*)e_l^{(d)}=\Bigl(1-\frac{l}{l+d-1}\Bigr)e_l^{(d)}=\frac{d-1}{l+d-1}\,e_l^{(d)},\]
    \[(I-B_d^*B_d)e_l^{(d)}=\Bigl(1-\frac{l+1}{l+d}\Bigr)e_l^{(d)}=\frac{d-1}{l+d}\,e_l^{(d)}.\]

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:Seta}
	For every $1\le i\le d$,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:Seta-action}
		S_{\eta_i} E_{s,l}=\sqrt{\frac{C_{d,l}}{C_{d,l+1}}}\, E_{s,l+1}=\sqrt{\frac{l+1}{l+d}}\, E_{s,l+1}.
	\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Multiplication by $\eta_i(z_i)$ sends $\theta^{(i)}_{s_i}\eta_i^m$ to $\theta^{(i)}_{s_i}\eta_i^{m+1}$, hence it maps each summand $H^2_{s_i}(z_i)=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\theta^{(i)}_{s_i}\eta_i^m:m\ge0\}$ into itself. Consequently $\eta_i E_{s,l}$ lies in the slice $\mathcal H_s=\bigotimes_{j=1}^d H^2_{s_j}(z_j)$, and
	\[\eta_i E_{s,l}=\left(\prod_{j=1}^d\theta^{(j)}_{s_j}(z_j)\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{d,l}}}\sum_{k_1+\cdots+k_d=l}\eta_1(z_1)^{k_1}\cdots\eta_i(z_i)^{k_i+1}\cdots\eta_d(z_d)^{k_d}.\]
	The slices $\{\mathcal H_s\}_s$ are mutually orthogonal, and
	\[N_\eta=\bigoplus_s(\mathcal H_s\ominus M_\eta),\qquad\mathcal H_s\cap N_\eta=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{E_{s,a}:a\ge0\}.\]
	Thus $P_{N_\eta}(\eta_i E_{s,l})$ belongs to $\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{E_{s,a}:a\ge0\}$, so
	\[S_{\eta_i}E_{s,l}=\sum_{a\ge0}\langle \eta_i E_{s,l},\,E_{s,a}\rangle\,E_{s,a}.\]
	
	Since in $\mathcal H_s$, the family $\{\prod_j\theta^{(j)}_{s_j}\eta_j^{m_j}\}$ is orthonormal, each $E_{s,a}$ is a linear combination of $\prod_j\theta^{(j)}_{s_j}\eta_j^{m_j}$ with $\sum_j m_j=a$, hence is homogeneous of total $\eta$-degree $a$. Since $\eta_i E_{s,l}$ has $\eta$-degree $l+1$, so
	\[\langle \eta_i E_{s,l},\,E_{s,a}\rangle=0\qquad(a\neq l+1),\]
	and therefore
	\[S_{\eta_i}E_{s,l}=\langle \eta_i E_{s,l},\,E_{s,l+1}\rangle\,E_{s,l+1}.\]
	
	It remains to find the coefficient. Write
	\[E_{s,l+1}=\left(\prod_{j=1}^d\theta^{(j)}_{s_j}(z_j)\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{d,l+1}}}\sum_{p_1+\cdots+p_d=l+1}\eta_1(z_1)^{p_1}\cdots\eta_d(z_d)^{p_d}.\]
	Since $\prod_j\theta^{(j)}_{s_j}$ has norm $1$ and $\{\eta_1^{a_1}\cdots\eta_d^{a_d}\}$ is orthonormal,
	\[\langle \eta_i E_{s,l},\,E_{s,l+1}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{d,l}\,C_{d,l+1}}}\sum_{\substack{k_1+\cdots+k_d=l\\ p_1+\cdots+p_d=l+1}}\bigl\langle\eta_i^{k_i+1}\prod_{j\neq i}\eta_j^{k_j},\;\eta_1^{p_1}\cdots\eta_d^{p_d}\bigr\rangle.\]
	The inner product is $1$ if \[\begin{cases}
		p_j = k_j + 1 & j=i\\
		p_j = k_j & j\neq i
	\end{cases},\] and $0$ otherwise. Thus each of the $C_{d,l}$ multi-indices $(k_1,\dots,k_d)$ summing to $l$ contributes $1$, yielding
	\[\langle \eta_i E_{s,l},\,E_{s,l+1}\rangle=\frac{C_{d,l}}{\sqrt{C_{d,l}\,C_{d,l+1}}}=\sqrt{\frac{C_{d,l}}{C_{d,l+1}}}=\sqrt{\frac{l+1}{l+d}}.\]
	So
	\[S_{\eta_i}E_{s,l}=\sqrt{\frac{C_{d,l}}{C_{d,l+1}}}\,E_{s,l+1}=\sqrt{\frac{l+1}{l+d}}\,E_{s,l+1}.\] 
	The expression is independent of $i$, so all $S_{\eta_i}$ coincide.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:Sz-action}

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:commutator}
   For each $s'$,
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:comm-model}
       U_{s'}\,\bigl[S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_i}\bigr]\big|_{N_{s'}}\,U_{s'}^*=T_iT_i^*\otimes(I-B_dB_d^*)-T_i^*T_i\otimes(I-B_d^*B_d).
   \end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Set $S=A_i\otimes I+T_i\otimes B_d$. On $K_i$ we have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:cross-vanish}
       A_i^*T_i=0,\qquad T_iA_i^*=0,
    \end{equation}
    and consequently $T_i^*A_i=0$ and $A_iT_i^*=0$. Indeed, $\operatorname{ran}T_i=\mathbb C\cdot 1$ and the backward shift formula on a model space gives $A_i^*1=0$, so $A_i^*T_i=0$; likewise $T_iA_i^*f=\langle A_i^*f,\bar z_i\eta_i\rangle 1=\langle f,A_i\bar z_i\eta_i\rangle 1$ and $A_i(\bar z_i\eta_i)=P_{K_i}(\eta_i)=0$ because $\eta_i\in\eta_i H^2\perp K_i$.

    Expanding $S^*S$ and $SS^*$,
    \[\begin{aligned}
       S^*S &=(A_i^*\otimes I+T_i^*\otimes B_d^*)(A_i\otimes I+T_i\otimes B_d)\\
       &=A_i^*A_i\otimes I+A_i^*T_i\otimes B_d+T_i^*A_i\otimes B_d^*+T_i^*T_i\otimes B_d^*B_d,\\[4pt]
       SS^* &=(A_i\otimes I+T_i\otimes B_d)(A_i^*\otimes I+T_i^*\otimes B_d^*)\\
       &=A_iA_i^*\otimes I+A_iT_i^*\otimes B_d^*+T_iA_i^*\otimes B_d+T_iT_i^*\otimes B_dB_d^*.
    \end{aligned}\]
    By \eqref{eq:cross-vanish} the cross terms vanish, so
    \[S^*S=A_i^*A_i\otimes I+T_i^*T_i\otimes B_d^*B_d,\qquad SS^*=A_iA_i^*\otimes I+T_iT_i^*\otimes B_dB_d^* .\]

    Subtracting and using $A_i^*A_i-A_iA_i^*=T_iT_i^*-T_i^*T_i$ from \eqref{eq:defect-model} gives
    \[[S^*,S]=T_iT_i^*\otimes(I-B_dB_d^*)-T_i^*T_i\otimes(I-B_d^*B_d),\]

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
    Let $N_\eta=H^2(\mathbb D^d)\ominus M_\eta$ with $d\ge2$, where $\eta_1,\dots,\eta_d$ are nonconstant one-variable inner functions with $\eta_j(0)=0$, and fix $1\le i\le d$. If $D_i=\prod_{j\ne i}d_j<\infty$, then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
       \left\lVert [S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_i}] \right\rVert_{\operatorname{H.S}}^2=D_i(d-1)^2\left[\frac{\pi^2}{3}-2\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{k^2}\right]+D_i-2D_i(d-1)\,|\eta_i'(0)|^2 .
    \end{equation}
    If $D_i=\infty$, then $[S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_i}]$ is not Hilbert-Schmidt.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
For a fix layer $s'$ and write $T=T_i$, $B=B_d$, $\theta=\theta^{(i)}_{s_i}$. Since $Tf=\langle f,\bar z_i\eta_i\rangle\,1$ and $T^*f=f(0)\,\bar z_i\eta_i$ with $\|\bar z_i\eta_i\|=1$,
\[
TT^*\theta=\theta(0)\cdot 1,
\qquad
T^*T\theta
=\langle z_i\theta,\,\eta_i\rangle\,\bar z_i\eta_i .
\]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:commutator} and Lemma~\ref{lem:Bd},
\[
U_{s'}[S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_i}]U_{s'}^*
(\theta\otimes e_l^{(d)})
=\frac{d-1}{l+d-1}\,\theta(0)\,(1\otimes e_l^{(d)})
-\frac{d-1}{l+d}\,\langle z_i\theta,\eta_i\rangle\,
(\bar z_i\eta_i\otimes e_l^{(d)}).
\]
The vectors $1\otimes e_l^{(d)}$ and $\bar z_i\eta_i\otimes e_l^{(d)}$ lie in $K_i\otimes\operatorname{span}\{e_l^{(d)}\}$ and are generally not orthogonal. Their inner product is
\[
\langle 1\otimes e_l^{(d)},\ \bar z_i\eta_i\otimes e_l^{(d)}\rangle
=\langle 1,\bar z_i\eta_i\rangle_{K_i}\,
\|e_l^{(d)}\|^2
=\overline{\eta_i'(0)},
\]
since $\eta_i(0)=0$ implies $\langle 1,\bar z_i\eta_i\rangle=(\bar z_i\eta_i)(0)=\overline{\eta_i'(0)}$. Expanding the squared norm,
\begin{align*}
\bigl\lVert[S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_i}]E_{s,l}\bigr\rVert^2
={}&\frac{(d-1)^2}{(l+d-1)^2}\,|\theta(0)|^2
+\frac{(d-1)^2}{(l+d)^2}\,\bigl|\langle z_i\theta,\eta_i\rangle\bigr|^2\\
&-\frac{2(d-1)^2}{(l+d-1)(l+d)}\,
\operatorname{Re}\Bigl(\theta(0)\,
\overline{\langle z_i\theta,\eta_i\rangle}\;\overline{\eta_i'(0)}\Bigr).
\end{align*}
Summing over $s_i=0,\dots,d_i-1$ and using that $\{\theta^{(i)}_{s_i}\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $K_i$ containing unit vectors $1,\bar z_i\eta_i$, Parseval's identity yields
\[
\sum_{s_i}|\theta^{(i)}_{s_i}(0)|^2
=\sum_{s_i}|\langle\theta^{(i)}_{s_i},1\rangle|^2=1,
\qquad
\sum_{s_i}|\langle z_i\theta^{(i)}_{s_i},\eta_i\rangle|^2
=\sum_{s_i}|\langle\theta^{(i)}_{s_i},\bar z_i\eta_i\rangle|^2=1,
\]
and for the cross term, we have
\[
\sum_{s_i}\theta^{(i)}_{s_i}(0)\,
\overline{\langle z_i\theta^{(i)}_{s_i},\eta_i\rangle}
=\sum_{s_i}
\langle\bar z_i\eta_i,\ \theta^{(i)}_{s_i}\rangle
\langle\theta^{(i)}_{s_i},\ 1\rangle
=\langle\bar z_i\eta_i,\,1\rangle
=\eta_i'(0).
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:si-sum}
\sum_{s_i=0}^{d_i-1}
\bigl\lVert[S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_i}]E_{s,l}\bigr\rVert^2
=\frac{(d-1)^2}{(l+d-1)^2}
+\frac{(d-1)^2}{(l+d)^2}
-\frac{2(d-1)^2\,|\eta_i'(0)|^2}{(l+d-1)(l+d)} .
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is independent of~$s'$.

Now sum over $l\ge0$, we have 
\[
\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(l+d-1)^2}
=\frac{\pi^2}{6}-\sum_{k=1}^{d-2}\frac{1}{k^2},
\qquad
\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(l+d)^2}
=\frac{\pi^2}{6}-\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{k^2},
\]
\[
\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(l+d-1)(l+d)}
=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\frac{1}{l+d-1}-\frac{1}{l+d}\Bigr)
=\frac{1}{d-1}.
\]
Summing \eqref{eq:si-sum} over $l$ gives, for each layer $s'$,
\begin{align*}
\left\lVert \bigl[(S_{z_i}|_{N_{s'}})^*,\,S_{z_i}|_{N_{s'}}\bigr] \right\rVert_{\operatorname{H.S}}^2
&=(d-1)^2\Bigl[\frac{\pi^2}{3}
-\sum_{k=1}^{d-2}\frac{1}{k^2}-\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{k^2}\Bigr]
-\frac{2(d-1)^2|\eta_i'(0)|^2}{d-1}\\
&=(d-1)^2\Bigl[\frac{\pi^2}{3}-2\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{k^2}\Bigr]
+1-2(d-1)\,|\eta_i'(0)|^2 ,
\end{align*}
by $\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d-2}k^{-2}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d-1}k^{-2}-(d-1)^{-2}$.

By the layer decomposition \eqref{eq:layer-split}, the Hilbert--Schmidt norm of $[S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_i}]$ is this common layer contribution multiplied by the number $D_i=\prod_{j\neq i}d_j$ of layers. If $D_i<\infty$, this yields \eqref{eq:main}. If $D_i=\infty$,  Write $a=l+d-1$, $b=l+d$, the summand in \eqref{eq:si-sum} is
\[
(d-1)^2\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac1a-\frac1b\Bigr)^2
+\frac{2(1-|\eta_i'(0)|^2)}{ab}\Bigr]>0,
\]
since $a\neq b$ and $|\eta_i'(0)|\le1$ by the Schwarz lemma. Hence the sum in \eqref{eq:layer-split} diverges, and $[S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_i}]$ is not Hilbert--Schmidt.
<!-- end proof-8 -->

\[
\left\lVert [S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_i}] \right\rVert_{\operatorname{H.S}}^2

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:example}
To anchor the abstract formula in a concrete case, take $d=3$ and let
\[
\eta_1(z)=z^2,\quad \eta_2(z)=z^3,\quad \eta_3(z)=z.
\]
All three are finite Blaschke products vanishing at $0$. Their model space dimensions are $d_1=2$, $d_2=3$, $d_3=1$. For $i=1$, we have
\[
D_1=d_2d_3=3,\qquad |\eta_1'(0)|=0\;\;(\text{since }\eta_1(z)=z^2).
\]
With $d=3$, compute $\sum_{k=1}^{2}k^{-2}=1+\frac14=\frac54$. Then formula~\eqref{eq:main} gives
\[
\left\lVert [S_{z_1}^*,S_{z_1}] \right\rVert_{\operatorname{H.S}}^2
=3\cdot 2^2\left[\frac{\pi^2}{3}-\frac52\right]+3-2\cdot3\cdot2\cdot0
=12\left(\frac{\pi^2}{3}-\frac52\right)+3
=4\pi^2-30+3=4\pi^2-27.
\]
For $i=3$, we have $D_3=d_1d_2=6$, $|\eta_3'(0)|=1$ (since $\eta_3(z)=z$), giving
\[
\left\lVert [S_{z_3}^*,S_{z_3}] \right\rVert_{\operatorname{H.S}}^2
=6\cdot4\left[\frac{\pi^2}{3}-\frac52\right]+6-2\cdot6\cdot2\cdot1
=24\left(\frac{\pi^2}{3}-\frac52\right)-18
=8\pi^2-60-18=8\pi^2-78.
\]
Note that the commutator for $i=3$ ($\eta_3(z)=z$) is smaller than for $i=1$ ($\eta_1(z)=z^2$), illustrating the phenomenon that $|\eta_i'(0)|$ closer to $1$ reduces the commutator norm. The coefficient of $|\eta_i'(0)|^2$ in \eqref{eq:main} is negative. Hence the commutator norm decreases when $|\eta_i'(0)|$ increases.

\label{prop:cross-comm}
For each $s''$,
\[
V_{s''}\,\bigl[S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_j}\bigr]\big|_{N_{s''}}\,V_{s''}^*

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:cross}
Let $N_\eta$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and fix $i\neq j$. If $D_{ij}=\prod_{k\neq i,j}d_k<\infty$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross-main}
\begin{aligned}
\left\lVert [S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_j}] \right\rVert_{\operatorname{H.S}}^2
=D_{ij}(d-1)^2\Bigl[\frac{\pi^2}{3}
-\sum_{k=1}^{d-2}\frac{1}{k^2}
-\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{k^2}\Bigr]
-2D_{ij}(d-1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
If $D_{ij}=\infty$, then $[S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_j}]$ is not Hilbert--Schmidt.

This reflects the fact that the rank-one operators $T_i^*$ and $T_j$ enter as a pure tensor product whose Hilbert-Schmidt norm carries no $\eta_i$-dependence.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:schatten}
Assume $D_i<\infty$. Then for every $1\le i\le d$, the self-commutator $[S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_i}]$ belongs to the Schatten--von Neumann class $\mathcal{S}^p$ for all $p>1$. The cross commutator $[S_{z_i}^*,S_{z_j}]$ ($i\neq j$) when $D_{ij}<\infty$ belongs to the Schatten-von Neumann class $\mathcal{S}^p$ for all $p>\frac{1}{2}$.

From Proposition 3.3, under the layer decomposition we have
\[
U_{s'} [S_{z_i}^*, S_{z_i}] U_{s'}^*
=
T_i T_i^* \otimes (I - B_d B_d^*) - T_i^* T_i \otimes (I - B_d^* B_d).
\]

Lemma 2.4 gives
\[
(I - B_d B_d^*) e_l^{(d)}
=
\frac{d-1}{l+d-1} e_l^{(d)},
\qquad
(I - B_d^* B_d) e_l^{(d)}
=
\frac{d-1}{l+d} e_l^{(d)}.
\]

Thus, on the \(l\)-th fiber
\[
\mathcal F_l = \operatorname{span}\{\theta_{s_i}^{(i)} \otimes e_l^{(d)} : s_i\},
\]
the restriction is
\[
R_l
:=
[S_{z_i}^*, S_{z_i}] \big|_{\mathcal F_l}
=
\frac{d-1}{l+d-1} T_i T_i^* \otimes I
-
\frac{d-1}{l+d} T_i^* T_i \otimes I.
\]

Since both \(T_i T_i^*\) and \(T_i^* T_i\) are rank-one operators on \(K_i\), their linear combination \(R_l\) has rank at most two. Let \(\sigma_{l,1} \ge \sigma_{l,2} \ge 0\) be its nonzero singular values. From the Hilbert-Schmidt norm computation,
\[
\|R_l\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=
\frac{(d-1)^2}{(l+d-1)^2}
+
\frac{(d-1)^2}{(l+d)^2}
-
\frac{2(d-1)^2|\eta_i'(0)|^2}{(l+d-1)(l+d)}
= O(l^{-2}).
\]
Hence
\[
\sigma_{l,1}^2 + \sigma_{l,2}^2 = O(l^{-2}),
\]
and therefore
\[
\sigma_{l,1} = O(l^{-1}), \qquad \sigma_{l,2} = O(l^{-1}).
\]

Let \((\sigma_k)_{k\ge1}\) be the global sequence of singular values. The number of layers is \(D_i = \prod_{j\neq i} d_j\), and each fiber \(\mathcal F_l\) contributes at most two singular values. Consequently, for \(l=0,1,\dots,L\), there are \(D_i(L+1)\) fibers, contributing at most \(2D_i(L+1)\) singular values. Hence for \(K \approx 2D_iL\),
\[
\sigma_K \approx \frac{C}{L} \approx \frac{C'}{K}.
\]
Thus the global singular values satisfy
\[
\sigma_k = O(k^{-1}).
\]

It follows that there exists a constant \(C>0\) such that for all \(k\ge1\),
\[
\sigma_k \le C k^{-1}.
\]
For any \(p > 1\),
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \sigma_k^p
\le
C^p \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k^{-p}
< \infty.
\]
Therefore
\[
[S_{z_i}^*, S_{z_i}] \in \mathcal S^p \quad (p > 1).
\]

For \(p = 1\), from \(\sigma_{l,1} \ge c/(l+1)\) for some \(c>0\) (which follows from the explicit formula for \(\|R_l\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\)), we obtain
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \sigma_k
\ge
\sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \sigma_{l,1}
\ge
\sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \frac{c}{l+1}
= \infty.
\]
Thus
\[
[S_{z_i}^*, S_{z_i}] \notin \mathcal S^1.
\]

From Proposition 4.1, under the bi-layer decomposition we have
\[
V_{s''} [S_{z_i}^*, S_{z_j}] V_{s''}^*
=
T_i^* \otimes T_j \otimes (B_d^* B_d - B_d B_d^*).
\]

Lemma 2.4 gives
\[
(B_d^* B_d - B_d B_d^*) e_l^{(d)}
=
\frac{d-1}{(l+d)(l+d-1)} e_l^{(d)}.
\]

Thus, on the \(l\)-th fiber
\[
\mathcal F_l = \operatorname{span}\{\theta_{s_i}^{(i)} \otimes \theta_{s_j}^{(j)} \otimes e_l^{(d)} : s_i, s_j\},
\]
the restriction is
\[
\widetilde R_l
:=
[S_{z_i}^*, S_{z_j}] \big|_{\mathcal F_l}
=
\frac{d-1}{(l+d)(l+d-1)} \, T_i^* \otimes T_j \otimes I.
\]

Since both \(T_i^*\) and \(T_j\) are rank-one operators, their tensor product \(T_i^* \otimes T_j\) is also rank-one, hence \(\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde R_l)=1\). Let \(\sigma_l\) be its unique nonzero singular value. Because
\[
\|T_i^* \otimes T_j\|_{\mathrm{HS}} = \|T_i^*\|_{\mathrm{HS}} \|T_j\|_{\mathrm{HS}} = 1,
\]
we obtain
\[
\sigma_l = \frac{d-1}{(l+d)(l+d-1)}.
\]
Consequently, as \(l\to\infty\),
\[
\sigma_l = O(l^{-2}).
\]

Let \((\sigma_k)_{k\ge1}\) be the global sequence of singular values. The number of bi-layers is \(D_{ij} = \prod_{k\neq i,j} d_k\), and each fiber \(\mathcal F_l\) contributes exactly one singular value \(\sigma_l\). Therefore, for \(l=0,1,\dots,L\), there are \(D_{ij}(L+1)\) fibers, contributing \(D_{ij}(L+1)\) singular values. Hence for \(K\approx D_{ij}L\),
\[
\sigma_K \approx \frac{C}{L^2} \approx \frac{C'}{K^2}.
\]
Thus the global singular values satisfy
\[
\sigma_k = O(k^{-2}).
\]

It follows that there exists a constant \(C>0\) such that for all \(k\ge1\),
\[
\sigma_k \le C k^{-2}.
\]
Therefore, for any \(p>\frac{1}{2}\),
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \sigma_k^p
\le
C^p \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k^{-2p}
< \infty.
\]
Hence
\[
[S_{z_i}^*, S_{z_j}] \in \mathcal S^p \quad (p>\frac{1}{2}).
\]

In particular, taking \(p=1\) gives \([S_{z_i}^*, S_{z_j}]\in \mathcal S^1\), i.e., the cross-commutator is trace class.
