<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:intro-main}
	Let $\mathcal M$ be an essentially small Krull--Schmidt $n$-abelian category.
	\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
		\item Let $X$ be a topological space and let $\sigma$ be an
		isomorphism-invariant assignment of closed subsets of $X$
		to objects of $\mathcal M$. Then $(X,\sigma)$ is an $n$-support datum
		if and only if $\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$ is wide for every subset
		$\omega\subseteq X$.
		
		\item Every wide subcategory of $\mathcal M$ is the intersection of
		the prime wide subcategories containing it, with the empty
		intersection understood as $\mathcal M$.
		
		\item For every classifying $n$-support datum $(X,\sigma)$
		on $\mathcal M$, the map
		\[
		\phi\colon X\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathbf{Prime}_n(\mathcal M)}(\mathcal M),
		\qquad
		\phi(x)=\{M\in\mathcal M\mid x\notin\sigma(M)\}
		\]
		is a homeomorphism satisfying
		$\phi^{-1}(\operatorname{Supp}(M))=\sigma(M)$ for every $M\in\mathcal M$.
	\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\cite[Definition~2.3]{HJ21}\label{def:wide}
		Let $\mathcal M$ be an $n$-abelian category. A full additive subcategory
		$\mathcal W\subseteq\mathcal M$ is \emph{wide} if it is closed under finite direct sums
		and direct summands and satisfies the following conditions.
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(W\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
			\item Every morphism in $\mathcal W$ admits an $n$-kernel and an $n$-cokernel
			in $\mathcal M$ all of whose terms belong to $\mathcal W$. \label{W1}
			\item Every $n$-extension in $\mathcal M$ with end terms in $\mathcal W$ is equivalent
			to an $n$-extension whose middle terms all belong to $\mathcal W$. \label{W2}
		\end{enumerate}
		We write $\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)$ for the poset of wide subcategories ordered by inclusion.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\cite[Definition~2.5]{HJ21}\label{def:minimal}
		Let $f:M\to N$ be a morphism in $\mathcal M$.
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
			\item An $n$-kernel $K_n\to\cdots\to K_1\to M$ of $f$ is
			\emph{minimal} if the maps $K_i\to K_{i-1}$ belong to
			$\operatorname{rad}_{\mathcal M}$ for $2\leq i\leq n$.
			\item An $n$-cokernel $N\to C_1\to\cdots\to C_n$ of $f$ is
			\emph{minimal} if the maps $C_i\to C_{i+1}$ belong to
			$\operatorname{rad}_{\mathcal M}$ for $1\leq i<n$.
			\item An $n$-extension $X\to E_n\to\cdots\to E_1\to Y$ is
			\emph{minimal} if its maps $E_i\to E_{i-1}$ belong to
			$\operatorname{rad}_{\mathcal M}$ for $2\leq i\leq n$.
		\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\cite[Proposition~2.4 and Definition~2.5]{HJ21}\label{lem:additive-minimal-representatives}
		In a Krull--Schmidt \(n\)-abelian category, the following properties hold:
		\begin{enumerate}
			\item Every morphism has a minimal \(n\)-kernel and a minimal
			\(n\)-cokernel.
			
			\item Every \(n\)-extension class has a minimal representative.
			
			\item Each minimal representative is a direct summand, as a complex,
			of every corresponding representative and is unique up to isomorphism.
			Moreover, the comparison morphisms and isomorphisms may be chosen to
			be the identity on the prescribed endpoints.
		\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\cite[Corollary~2.6]{HJ21}\label{lem:minimal-characterization}
		Let $\mathcal M$ be a Krull--Schmidt $n$-abelian category and let $\mathcal W\subseteq\mathcal M$ be a subcategory closed under finite direct sums and direct summands.  Then $\mathcal W$ is wide if and only if:
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
			\item for every morphism $f:M\to N$ in $\mathcal W$, all terms of a (hence any) minimal $n$-kernel and minimal $n$-cokernel of $f$ belong to $\mathcal W$;
			\item for every $X,Y\in\mathcal W$, all middle terms of every minimal $n$-extension of $Y$ by $X$ belong to $\mathcal W$.
		\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:intersection}
		Let $\mathcal M$ be a Krull--Schmidt $n$-abelian category and let
		$\{\mathcal W_\lambda\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ be a family of wide subcategories.
		Then $\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal W_\lambda$ is wide. The empty intersection is understood to be $\mathcal M$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Set $\mathcal W=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal W_\lambda$. Then $\mathcal W$ is full and
		closed under finite direct sums and direct summands. Let $f:M\to N$ be
		a morphism in $\mathcal W$, and choose a minimal $n$-kernel and a minimal $n$-cokernel of
		$f$ in $\mathcal M$. Since $M,N\in\mathcal W_\lambda$ for all $\lambda\in\Lambda$,
		Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-characterization} shows that every term of these
		minimal complexes lies in each $\mathcal W_\lambda$, and hence in $\mathcal W$.
		
		Let $X,Y\in\mathcal W$, and let an arbitrary minimal $n$-extension of $Y$ by $X$ be given.
		Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-characterization} once again, we see
		that all middle terms of this extension lie in each $\mathcal W_\lambda$, and
		therefore in $\mathcal W$. Hence $\mathcal W$ satisfies the criterion of
		Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-characterization}, and so $\mathcal W$ is wide.
		
		If $\Lambda=\varnothing$, then $\mathcal W=\mathcal M$, which is wide.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Intersection closure is not immediate from the definition when \(n\ge2\),
		since the higher kernels, cokernels, and extension representatives need not
		be unique up to isomorphism. Minimal representatives provide a common
		representative in every member of the family and hence establish this closure.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:complete-lattice}
		Let $\mathcal M$ be an essentially small Krull--Schmidt $n$-abelian category.
		Then $\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)$ is a complete lattice. For every family
		$\{\mathcal W_\lambda\}$ of wide subcategories,
		\[
		\bigwedge_\lambda\mathcal W_\lambda
		=
		\bigcap_\lambda\mathcal W_\lambda,
		\qquad
		\bigvee_\lambda\mathcal W_\lambda
		=
		\operatorname{wide}_{\mathcal M}\!\left(\bigcup_\lambda\mathcal W_\lambda\right),
		\]
		where $\operatorname{wide}_{\mathcal M}(\mathcal S)$ denotes the least wide
		subcategory of $\mathcal M$ containing $\mathcal S$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $\mathcal M$ is essentially small, $\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)$ is a set.
		Proposition~\ref{prop:intersection} shows that arbitrary meets exist.
		For a class $\mathcal S$ of objects of $\mathcal M$, the collection of wide
		subcategories containing $\mathcal S$ is nonempty, as it contains
		$\mathcal M$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:intersection}, their intersection is
		wide and is the least wide subcategory containing $\mathcal S$.
		Taking $\mathcal S=\bigcup_\lambda\mathcal W_\lambda$ gives the formula for
		joins.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:directed-unions}
		The union of a nonempty family of wide subcategories that is
		directed under inclusion is wide.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal W=\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal W_i$ be such a union, viewed as a full
		subcategory. It contains zero and is closed under isomorphisms and
		direct summands. Any two objects lie in one $\mathcal W_i$, so their
		direct sum also lies in $\mathcal W$. If $f:X\to Y$ is a morphism in
		$\mathcal W$, choose $i$ with $X,Y\in\mathcal W_i$. An $n$-kernel and an
		$n$-cokernel supplied by $\mathcal W_i$ have all their terms in $\mathcal W$.
		Likewise, an $n$-extension with endpoints in $\mathcal W$ has both
		endpoints in some $\mathcal W_i$, where it has an equivalent representative
		with all middle terms in $\mathcal W_i$. Thus $\mathcal W$ is wide.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:topology}
		The subsets $\mathbf{Z}(\mathcal S)$ are the closed sets of a topology on $\mathscr W$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For any family $\{\mathcal S_\lambda\}$ of classes of objects of $\mathcal M$, we have
		\[
		\bigcap_\lambda \mathbf{Z}(\mathcal S_\lambda)
		=
		\mathbf{Z}\!\left(\bigcup_\lambda\mathcal S_\lambda\right).
		\]
		For the union of two closed sets, let $\mathcal P\in\mathscr W$. Since
		$\mathcal P$ is wide, it is closed under finite direct sums and direct
		summands. Hence
		$\mathcal P\cap(\mathcal S_1\oplus\mathcal S_2)\neq\varnothing$
		if and only if $\mathcal P\cap\mathcal S_1\neq\varnothing$ and
		$\mathcal P\cap\mathcal S_2\neq\varnothing$.
		Indeed, if $M_1\oplus M_2\in\mathcal P$, then closure under direct
		summands gives $M_1,M_2\in\mathcal P$, while the converse follows from
		closure under finite direct sums.
		Taking complements in $\mathscr W$ yields
		$\mathbf{Z}(\mathcal S_1)\cup \mathbf{Z}(\mathcal S_2)
		=
		\mathbf{Z}(\mathcal S_1\oplus\mathcal S_2)$.
		Finally,
		$\mathbf{Z}(\varnothing)=\mathscr W$, while
		$\mathbf{Z}(\operatorname{obj}\mathcal M)=\varnothing$, since every wide subcategory
		contains the zero object.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
The resulting topological space is denoted by
		$\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathscr W}(\mathcal M)$ and is called the \emph{spectrum of $\mathcal M$
			with respect to $\mathscr W$}. For an object $M\in\mathcal M$, define its
		\emph{support} by
		$\operatorname{Supp}(M)=\mathbf{Z}(\{M\})=\{\mathcal P\in\mathscr W\mid M\notin\mathcal P\}$,
		and set
		$\mathbf{U}(M)=\{\mathcal P\in\mathscr W\mid M\in\mathcal P\}$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:point-closure}
		For $\mathcal P\in\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathscr W}(\mathcal M)$,
		\[
		\overline{\{\mathcal P\}}
		=\{\mathcal Q\in\mathscr W\mid \mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal P\}.
		\]
		In particular, $\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathscr W}(\mathcal M)$ is a $T_0$-space.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal S_{\mathcal P}=\operatorname{obj}\mathcal M\setminus\operatorname{obj}\mathcal P$. By definition,
		$\mathbf{Z}(\mathcal S_{\mathcal P})=\{\mathcal Q\in\mathscr W\mid \mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal P\}$,
		which is a closed subset containing $\mathcal P$.
		Now let $\mathbf{Z}(\mathcal S)$ be a closed subset containing $\mathcal P$. Then
		$\mathcal P\cap\mathcal S=\varnothing$, so $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal S_{\mathcal P}$.
		Hence $\mathbf{Z}(\mathcal S_{\mathcal P})\subseteq \mathbf{Z}(\mathcal S)$. Therefore
		$\mathbf{Z}(\mathcal S_{\mathcal P})$ is the smallest closed subset containing $\mathcal P$,
		and thus equals $\overline{\{\mathcal P\}}$.
		Finally, if two points have the same closure, then the above description
		implies that the corresponding wide subcategories contain each other.
		Hence they are equal, and $\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathscr W}(\mathcal M)$ is a $T_0$-space.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:canonical-inverse}
		For every subset $\omega\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathscr W}(\mathcal M)$,
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-intersection}
			\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}(\omega)
			=\bigcap_{\mathcal P\notin\omega}\mathcal P.
		\end{equation}
		In particular, $\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}(\omega)$ is wide.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For $M\in\mathcal M$, we have
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
			M\in\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}(\omega)
			&\Longleftrightarrow \operatorname{Supp}(M)\subseteq\omega\\
			&\Longleftrightarrow \mathcal P\notin\operatorname{Supp}(M)\text{ for all }\mathcal P\notin\omega\\
			&\Longleftrightarrow M\in\mathcal P\text{ for all }\mathcal P\notin\omega,
			\qquad\text{by }\operatorname{Supp}(M)=\{\mathcal P\mid M\notin\mathcal P\}.
		\end{aligned}
		\]
		This proves \eqref{eq:inverse-intersection}. Since every $\mathcal P$ is a
		wide subcategory, Proposition~\ref{prop:intersection} implies that
		$\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}(\omega)$ is wide. If $\omega=\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathscr W}(\mathcal M)$,
		the intersection on the right is empty and hence equals $\mathcal M$, which is
		wide.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:subcat-spcl}
		For every subcategory $\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal M$, the subset $\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal X)$ is
		specialization-closed.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal P\in\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal X)$ and let
		$\mathcal Q\in\overline{\{\mathcal P\}}$. By
		Proposition~\ref{prop:point-closure}, we have $\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal P$.
		Since $\mathcal P\in\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal X)$, there exists some $M\in\mathcal X$ with
		$M\notin\mathcal P$. Hence $M\notin\mathcal Q$, and therefore
		$\mathcal Q\in\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal X)$.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:n-support-datum}
		An \emph{$n$-support datum} on $\mathcal M$ is a pair $(X,\sigma)$, where
		\[
		\sigma:\operatorname{obj}\mathcal M\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}_{cl}(X)
		\]
		is constant on isomorphism classes and satisfies the following conditions.
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(NSD\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
			\item $\sigma(0)=\varnothing$. \label{NSD1}
			\item $\sigma(M\oplus N)=\sigma(M)\cup\sigma(N)$.\label{NSD2}
			\item If $K_n\to\cdots\to K_1\to M$
			is a minimal $n$-kernel of a morphism $f:M\to N$, then
			\[
			\sigma(K_i)\subseteq\sigma(M)\cup\sigma(N),
			\quad 1\leq i\leq n.
			\]\label{NSD3}
			\item If $N\to C_1\to\cdots\to C_n$ is a minimal $n$-cokernel of $f:M\to N$, then
			\[
			\sigma(C_i)\subseteq\sigma(M)\cup\sigma(N),
			\quad 1\leq i\leq n.
			\]\label{NSD4}
			\item If $X_0\to E_n\to\cdots\to E_1\to Y_0$ is a minimal $n$-extension of $Y_0$ by $X_0$, then
			\[
			\sigma(E_i)\subseteq\sigma(X_0)\cup\sigma(Y_0),
			\quad 1\leq i\leq n.
			\]\label{NSD5}
		\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
For $n\geq2$, the minimality assumptions in
		Definition~\ref{def:n-support-datum} cannot be omitted. Indeed, for any
		object $C$, the sequence
		$0\longrightarrow C\xrightarrow{1_C}C$
		is a $2$-cokernel of the zero morphism $0\to0$, see
		\cite[Remark~2.3]{J16}. Taking $\mathscr W=\{\mathbf0\}$ and
		$C\neq0$ would then give the false inclusion
		$\operatorname{Supp}(C)\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(0)$. For larger $n$, the same example can be padded with zero terms.
		Thus conditions imposed on all representatives would generally fail;
		minimal representatives remove this obstruction.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:support-preimages-wide}
		If $(X,\sigma)$ is an $n$-support datum, then
		$\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$ is wide for every subset $\omega\subseteq X$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Fix $\omega\subseteq X$. By \ref{NSD1} and \ref{NSD2}, the subcategory
		$\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$ contains the zero object and is closed under
		finite direct sums. It is closed under isomorphisms by assumption.
		If $M\oplus N\in\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$, then
		\[
		\sigma(M)\cup\sigma(N)=\sigma(M\oplus N)\subseteq\omega,
		\]
		so both summands belong to $\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$.
		
		For a morphism $f:M\to N$ in this subcategory, the union
		$\sigma(M)\cup\sigma(N)$ is contained in $\omega$.
		Conditions \ref{NSD3} and \ref{NSD4} therefore put every term of
		its minimal $n$-kernel and minimal $n$-cokernel in
		$\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$. If the endpoints of a minimal $n$-extension
		belong to this subcategory, condition \ref{NSD5} puts all its middle
		terms there as well. Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-characterization} now
		shows that $\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$ is wide.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:support-characterization}
		Let \(X\) be a topological space and let
		$\sigma:\operatorname{obj}\mathcal M\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}_{cl}(X)$
		be an assignment that is constant on isomorphism classes.
		Then the following conditions are equivalent:
		\begin{enumerate}
			\item \((X,\sigma)\) is an \(n\)-support datum.
			
			\item For every subset \(\omega\subseteq X\), the full subcategory
			\[
			\sigma^{-1}(\omega)
			=
			\{M\in\mathcal M\mid \sigma(M)\subseteq\omega\}
			\]
			is wide.
		\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The forward implication is Lemma~\ref{lem:support-preimages-wide}.
		For the converse, assume that $\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$ is wide for
		every $\omega\subseteq X$.
		
		Taking $\omega=\varnothing$, we obtain
		$0\in\sigma^{-1}(\varnothing)$, and hence
		$\sigma(0)=\varnothing$. This proves \ref{NSD1}.
		
		For $M,N\in\mathcal M$, take $\omega=\sigma(M)\cup\sigma(N)$.
		The objects $M$ and $N$ belong to the wide subcategory
		$\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$, so their direct sum belongs to it. Thus
		\[
		\sigma(M\oplus N)\subseteq\sigma(M)\cup\sigma(N).
		\]
		Taking instead $\omega=\sigma(M\oplus N)$ and using closure under
		direct summands gives the reverse inclusion. This proves \ref{NSD2}.
		
		Let $f:M\to N$ be a morphism and again put
		$\omega=\sigma(M)\cup\sigma(N)$. The morphism lies in the full
		wide subcategory $\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$. By
		Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-characterization}, every term of its minimal
		$n$-kernel and minimal $n$-cokernel belongs to
		$\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$. These are exactly the inclusions in
		\ref{NSD3} and \ref{NSD4}.
		
		Finally, for endpoints $X_0,Y_0\in\mathcal M$, put
		$\omega=\sigma(X_0)\cup\sigma(Y_0)$. Applying
		Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-characterization} to the wide subcategory
		$\sigma^{-1}(\omega)$ puts every middle term of every minimal
		$n$-extension of $Y_0$ by $X_0$ in this subcategory. This is
		\ref{NSD5}, completing the converse.
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:canonical-support}
		For every family $\mathscr W\subseteq\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)$, the assignment
		\[
		\operatorname{Supp}:\operatorname{obj}\mathcal M\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}_{cl}(\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathscr W}(\mathcal M))
		\]
		defines an $n$-support datum.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The assignment is constant on isomorphism classes because each member
		of $\mathscr W$ is closed under isomorphisms. Each $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$ is
		closed because $\operatorname{Supp}(M)=\mathbf Z(\{M\})$. By
		Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical-inverse}, $\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}(\omega)$ is wide for
		every subset $\omega$ of the spectrum. The conclusion follows from
		Theorem~\ref{prop:support-characterization}.
<!-- end proof-10 -->

For $n=1$, the equivalence in
		Theorem~\ref{prop:support-characterization} holds in any

<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:radical}
		For $\mathcal X\in\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)$, its $\mathscr W$-\emph{radical} is
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:radical}
			\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}
			=\bigcap_{\mathcal P\in\mathscr W,~~\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal P}\mathcal P.
		\end{equation}
		The empty intersection is $\mathcal M$. We call $\mathcal X$
		$\mathscr W$-\emph{radical} if $\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}=\mathcal X$, and
		write $\mathbf{Rad}_{\mathscr W}(\mathcal M)$ for the poset of such subcategories.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:two-radicals}
		The notation $\operatorname{rad}_{\mathcal M}(U,V)$ denotes a group of morphisms in the
		categorical Jacobson radical. By contrast,
		$\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}$ is a subcategory obtained by intersecting
		members of the chosen family $\mathscr W$. The former is used to
		define minimal complexes; the latter is a closure operation on
		$\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)$ and depends on $\mathscr W$.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:radical-containment}
		For every wide subcategory $\mathcal X$, we have
		$\mathcal X\subseteq\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}$. Moreover, for every
		$\mathcal P\in\mathscr W$,
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:radical-containment}
			\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal P
			\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
			\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}\subseteq\mathcal P.
		\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Every factor in \eqref{eq:radical} contains $\mathcal X$, so their
		intersection contains $\mathcal X$. This also holds for the empty intersection.
		If $\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal P\in\mathscr W$, then $\mathcal P$ is one of those
		factors, giving $\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}\subseteq\mathcal P$.
		The reverse implication follows from
		$\mathcal X\subseteq\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}$.
<!-- end proof-11 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:radical-closure}
		The operation $\sqrt[\mathscr W]{-}$ is a closure operator on
		$\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)$: it is extensive, monotone, and idempotent.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
The extensivity follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:radical-containment}.
		If $\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal Y$, every member of $\mathscr W$
		containing $\mathcal Y$ contains $\mathcal X$. Intersecting these families
		gives $\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}\subseteq\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal Y}$.
		Finally, by \eqref{eq:radical-containment}, the same members of
		\(\mathscr W\) contain \(\mathcal X\) and
		\(\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}\). Their defining intersections are therefore equal,
		which proves idempotence.
<!-- end proof-12 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:radical-intersections}
		The $\mathscr W$-radical wide subcategories are exactly the
		intersections of subfamilies of $\mathscr W$, including the empty
		intersection $\mathcal M$.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
If $\mathcal X$ is radical, equation~\eqref{eq:radical} gives the required
		expression. Conversely, suppose that
		$\mathcal X=\bigcap_{\mathcal P\in\mathscr V}\mathcal P$ for a subfamily
		$\mathscr V\subseteq\mathscr W$. Every member of $\mathscr V$
		contains $\mathcal X$, so the intersection defining its radical is contained
		in $\mathcal X$. Extensivity gives
		$\mathcal X\subseteq\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}\subseteq\mathcal X$.
<!-- end proof-13 -->
<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:radical-support}
		For every wide subcategory $\mathcal X$,
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:radical-support-composite}
			\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}(\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal X))=\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X},
			\qquad
			\operatorname{Supp}(\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X})=\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal X).
		\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
By \eqref{eq:inverse-intersection} and
		\eqref{eq:subcategory-support},
		\[
		\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}(\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal X))
		=\bigcap_{\mathcal P\notin\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal X)}\mathcal P
		=\bigcap_{\mathcal P\in\mathscr W,~~\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal P}\mathcal P
		=\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}.
		\]
		For each $\mathcal P\in\mathscr W$, equation~\eqref{eq:radical-containment}
		gives $\mathcal X\nsubseteq\mathcal P$ if and only if
		$\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal X}\nsubseteq\mathcal P$. Formula
		\eqref{eq:subcategory-support} therefore gives equality of the two
		supports.
<!-- end proof-14 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:rad-param}
		The assignments \(\operatorname{Supp}\) and \(\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}\) induce mutually inverse
		order isomorphisms
		\[
		\mathbf{Rad}_{\mathscr W}(\mathcal M)
		\overset{\operatorname{Supp}}{\underset{\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}}{\rightleftarrows}}
		\mathbf{Param}_{\mathscr W}(\mathcal M).
		\]
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
For a radical $\mathcal X$, \eqref{eq:radical-support-composite} gives
		$\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}(\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal X))=\mathcal X$. For
		$\omega=\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal Y)$, Proposition~\ref{prop:radical-closure}
		and Lemma~\ref{lem:radical-support} show that
		$\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}(\omega)=\sqrt[\mathscr W]{\mathcal Y}$ is radical
		and has support $\omega$. Both assignments preserve inclusion
		by their definitions.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:galois}
		For a wide subcategory $\mathcal X$ and any subset $\omega$ of the spectrum,
		\[
		\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal X)\subseteq\omega
		\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
		\mathcal X\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}(\omega).
		\]
		Thus $\operatorname{Supp}\dashv\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}$ is an adjunction of posets, with
		closure operator $\operatorname{Supp}^{-1}\circ\operatorname{Supp}=\sqrt[\mathscr W]{-}$.
		Theorem~\ref{thm:rad-param} describes its fixed points.

<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{def:prime}
		A proper wide subcategory $\mathcal P\subsetneq\mathcal M$ is \emph{prime} if 
		\[
		\{\mathcal X\in\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)\mid \mathcal P\subsetneq\mathcal X\}
		\]
		has a least element.  We write $\mathbf{Prime}_n(\mathcal M)$ for the set of prime wide subcategories.
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
We follow the least-element convention in
		\cite[Remark~2.9]{M23}, since a unique minimal element
		need not be a least element in an infinite poset.
		This notion of primeness need not coincide with
		meet-primality in the lattice.
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:prime-dagger}
		A proper wide subcategory $\mathcal P$ is prime if and only if $\mathcal P\subsetneq\mathcal P^{\dagger}$.
		In this case $\mathcal P^{\dagger}$ is the least wide subcategory properly containing $\mathcal P$.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
If $\mathcal P^+$ is the least wide subcategory properly containing $\mathcal P$, then $\mathcal P^+$ is contained in every wide subcategory properly containing $\mathcal P$ and also belongs to this family, hence $\mathcal P^{\dagger}=\mathcal P^+$.  Conversely, if $\mathcal P\subsetneq\mathcal P^{\dagger}$, then by construction $\mathcal P^{\dagger}$ is contained in every wide subcategory properly containing $\mathcal P$.
<!-- end proof-16 -->
<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:prime-cmi}
		A wide subcategory $\mathcal P$ is prime if and only if it is completely meet-irreducible in $\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Suppose $\mathcal P$ is prime, and let $\mathcal P^+$ be the least wide subcategory properly containing $\mathcal P$.  If
		$\mathcal P=\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathcal X_i$
		and no $\mathcal X_i$ equals $\mathcal P$, then every $\mathcal X_i$ properly contains $\mathcal P$, so $\mathcal P^+\subseteq\mathcal X_i$ for all $i$.  Hence $\mathcal P^+\subseteq\bigcap_i\mathcal X_i=\mathcal P$, a contradiction.
		
		Conversely, suppose $\mathcal P$ is completely meet-irreducible.  It is not the top element.  If $\mathcal P^{\dagger}=\mathcal P$, then $\mathcal P$ is the meet of the family of wide subcategories properly containing it.  Complete meet-irreducibility would force one member of that family to equal $\mathcal P$, a contradiction.  Thus $\mathcal P\subsetneq\mathcal P^{\dagger}$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-dagger} applies.
<!-- end proof-17 -->
<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:prime-separation}
		Let $\mathcal X$ be a wide subcategory of $\mathcal M$ and let $M\notin\mathcal X$.
		There is a prime wide subcategory $\mathcal P$ with
		$\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal P$ and $M\notin\mathcal P$. Consequently,
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-intersection}
			\mathcal X=\bigcap_{\substack{\mathcal P\in\mathbf{Prime}_n(\mathcal M)\\\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal P}}\mathcal P,
			\qquad
			\mathbf{Rad}_{\mathbf{Prime}_n(\mathcal M)}(\mathcal M)=\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M).
		\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
The collection
		\[
		\mathsf S=\{\mathcal W\in\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)\mid
		\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal W,\ M\notin\mathcal W\}
		\]
		is a set because $\mathcal M$ is essentially small, and it contains $\mathcal X$.
		Every nonempty chain in $\mathsf S$ has its union as an upper
		bound in $\mathsf S$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:directed-unions}.
		The empty chain has the upper bound $\mathcal X$. By Zorn's lemma, there exists a maximal element \(\mathcal P\) of
		\(\mathsf S\).
		Every wide subcategory strictly containing $\mathcal P$ must contain $M$.
		Hence
		\[
		\mathcal P^+=\operatorname{wide}_{\mathcal M}(\operatorname{obj}\mathcal P\cup\{M\})
		\]
		is the least wide subcategory strictly containing $\mathcal P$.
		Thus $\mathcal P$ is prime and has the required separation property.
		Separating each object outside $\mathcal X$ proves the first equality in
		\eqref{eq:prime-intersection}. For $\mathcal X=\mathcal M$, this equality uses the
		empty-intersection convention. The second equality in
		\eqref{eq:prime-intersection} then follows from Definition~\ref{def:radical}.
<!-- end proof-18 -->
<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:spcl1-points}
		If $X$ is $T_0$, the map
		\[
		X\longrightarrow\mathbf{Spcl}_1(X),
		\qquad
		x\longmapsto V_x,
		\]
		is a bijection. The least specialization-closed subset properly containing $V_x$ is $V_x\cup\{x\}$.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
First $V_x$ is specialization-closed.  Indeed, if $y\in V_x$ and $z\in\overline{\{y\}}$, then $x\in\overline{\{z\}}$ would imply $x\in\overline{\{y\}}$ by transitivity of specialization, a contradiction.
		
		We next show that $V_x\cup\{x\}$ is specialization-closed.  If $z\in\overline{\{x\}}$ and $z\notin V_x$, then $x\in\overline{\{z\}}$.  Since $X$ is $T_0$, mutual specialization forces $z=x$.  Now let $T$ be specialization-closed with $V_x\subsetneq T$.  Choose $y\in T\setminus V_x$.  Then $x\in\overline{\{y\}}\subseteq T$, hence $V_x\cup\{x\}\subseteq T$.  Therefore $V_x\in\mathbf{Spcl}_1(X)$.
		
		Conversely, let $V\in\mathbf{Spcl}_1(X)$ and let $T$ be the least specialization-closed subset properly containing $V$.  Choose $x\in T\setminus V$.  If $y\in V$, then $x\notin\overline{\{y\}}$, since otherwise specialization-closedness of $V$ would imply $x\in V$.  Hence $V\subseteq V_x$.
		
		Suppose that some $y\in V_x\setminus V$.  Then
		$V\cup\overline{\{y\}}$
		is a specialization-closed subset properly containing $V$.  Since $T$ is the least such subset, $T\subseteq V\cup\overline{\{y\}}$.
		Since $x\in T\setminus V$, it follows that $x\in\overline{\{y\}}$, contradicting $y\in V_x$.  Hence $V=V_x$.
		
		Finally, if $V_x=V_y$, then $x\notin V_y$ and $y\notin V_x$, so $y\in\overline{\{x\}}$ and $x\in\overline{\{y\}}$.  The $T_0$ property gives $x=y$.
<!-- end proof-19 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def:classifying}
		An $n$-support datum $(X,\sigma)$ on $\mathcal M$ is \emph{classifying} if:
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(CNSD\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
			\item $X$ is noetherian and sober; 
			\item the assignments
			\[
			\sigma(\mathcal W)=\bigcup_{M\in\mathcal W}\sigma(M),
			\qquad
			\sigma^{-1}(V)=\{M\in\mathcal M\mid\sigma(M)\subseteq V\}
			\]
			induce mutually inverse lattice isomorphisms
			\begin{equation}\label{eq:classifying-lattice}
				\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)
				\overset{\sigma}{\underset{\sigma^{-1}}{\rightleftarrows}}
				\mathbf{Spcl}(X).
			\end{equation}
		\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:reconstruction}
		Let $(X,\sigma)$ be a classifying $n$-support datum on $\mathcal M$.
		Define
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi}
			\phi:X\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathbf{Prime}_n(\mathcal M)}(\mathcal M),
			\qquad \phi(x)=\sigma^{-1}(V_x).
		\end{equation}
		Then $\phi$ is a homeomorphism. Moreover,
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-object}
			\phi(x)=\{M\in\mathcal M\mid x\notin\sigma(M)\}.
		\end{equation}
		For every $M\in\mathcal M$, this homeomorphism satisfies
		$\phi^{-1}(\operatorname{Supp}(M))=\sigma(M)$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
We first identify the points. Sobriety implies that $X$ is $T_0$,
		so Lemma~\ref{lem:spcl1-points} gives a bijection
		$x\mapsto V_x$ from $X$ to $\mathbf{Spcl}_1(X)$.
		Since the lattice isomorphism preserves arbitrary meets, it preserves completely meet-irreducible elements. Thus it
		identifies $\mathbf{Spcl}_1(X)$ with the completely meet-irreducible
		elements of $\mathbf{Wide}_n(\mathcal M)$, which are the prime wide subcategories
		by Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-cmi}. This proves that $\phi$ is
		well defined and bijective.
		
		For each $M\in\mathcal M$, closedness of $\sigma(M)$ gives
		\[
		\sigma(M)\subseteq V_x
		\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\notin\sigma(M).
		\]
		Indeed, $x\notin V_x$, which proves the forward implication.
		For the reverse implication, if $x\notin\sigma(M)$ and
		$y\in\sigma(M)$, then
		$\overline{\{y\}}\subseteq\sigma(M)$. Hence
		$x\notin\overline{\{y\}}$, so $y\in V_x$.
		This establishes \eqref{eq:phi-object} and therefore
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-preimage-support}
			\phi^{-1}(\operatorname{Supp}(M))=\sigma(M).
		\end{equation}
		Every closed subset of the prime spectrum is an intersection of
		object supports, by \eqref{eq:closed-intersection-support}.
		Its inverse image under $\phi$ is consequently an intersection
		of closed subsets of $X$. Thus $\phi$ is continuous.
		
		It remains to prove that $\phi$ is closed. We show first that
		every closed subset of $X$ is the support of one object.
		Let $C\subseteq X$ be nonempty and closed.
		Noetherianity means that descending chains of closed subsets
		stabilize. It follows that $C$ is a finite union of nonempty
		irreducible closed subsets: otherwise choose a minimal closed
		subset $F$ that has no such decomposition. The set $F$ cannot
		be irreducible, so it is the union of two proper closed subsets
		of $F$, which are also closed in $X$. Each has a finite
		irreducible decomposition by minimality, giving one for $F$, a
		contradiction. Write the resulting decomposition as
		$C=C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_r$.
		Since $X$ is sober, each $C_i$ has a unique generic point
		$x_i$, that is, $C_i=\overline{\{x_i\}}$. Hence
		$C=\overline{\{x_1\}}\cup\cdots\cup\overline{\{x_r\}}$.
		
		
		For each $i$, the point closure $\overline{\{x_i\}}$ is
		specialization-closed. The two assignments in
		\eqref{eq:classifying-lattice} are inverse, so
		\[
		\overline{\{x_i\}}
		=\bigcup_{M\in\sigma^{-1}(\overline{\{x_i\}})}\sigma(M).
		\]
		As $x_i$ belongs to this union, there is an object $M_i$ with
		$x_i\in\sigma(M_i)\subseteq\overline{\{x_i\}}$.
		
		The set $\sigma(M_i)$ is closed and contains $x_i$, so it also
		contains $\overline{\{x_i\}}$. Therefore
		$\sigma(M_i)=\overline{\{x_i\}}$.
		The direct-sum axiom yields
		$C=\sigma(M_1\oplus\cdots\oplus M_r)$. For $C=\varnothing$, the same assertion holds with the zero
		object, by $\sigma(0)=\varnothing$.
		
		Thus, for every closed $C\subseteq X$, there is an object
		$M_C$ with $C=\sigma(M_C)$. Equation
		\eqref{eq:phi-preimage-support} and surjectivity of $\phi$ give 
		$\phi(C)
		=\phi\bigl(\phi^{-1}(\operatorname{Supp}(M_C))\bigr)
		=\operatorname{Supp}(M_C)$, 
		which is closed in the prime spectrum. Hence $\phi$ is a closed
		continuous bijection, and therefore a homeomorphism.
<!-- end proof-20 -->
<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
\label{rem:weaker-topology}
		The conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:reconstruction} remains valid if the

		by the following weaker conditions: \(X\) is \(T_0\), and every nonempty

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:example-cluster}
		The subcategory $\mathcal M$ is $2$-cluster tilting in $\operatorname{mod}\Lambda$.
		Consequently, it is $2$-abelian.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
The category $\operatorname{mod}\Lambda$ is Hom-finite and $\mathcal M$ is the additive closure of
		finitely many objects. Taking evaluation maps associated with
		bases of their Hom-spaces gives right and left $\mathcal M$-approximations.
		Thus $\mathcal M$ is functorially finite. It contains all projectives
		and all injectives, so it is also generating and cogenerating.
		
		Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(-,Y)$ to the projective resolution
		\eqref{eq:main-2exact} gives the cochain complex
		\[
		\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(B,Y)\xrightarrow{v^*}\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(C,Y)
		\xrightarrow{u^*}\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(D,Y)
		\]
		in degrees $0,1,2$. By \eqref{eq:hom-table}, these complexes
		are as follows:
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:ext-complexes}
			\begin{array}{c|c}
				Y&\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(B,Y)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(C,Y)
				\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(D,Y)\\\hline
				A&\mathbf{k}\longrightarrow0\longrightarrow0\\
				B&\mathbf{k}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{k}\longrightarrow0\\
				C&0\longrightarrow\mathbf{k}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{k}\\
				D&0\longrightarrow0\longrightarrow\mathbf{k}
			\end{array}
		\end{equation}
		The nonzero differentials are isomorphisms: precomposition sends
		$1_B$ to $v$ in the row $Y=B$, and $1_C$ to $u$ in the row
		$Y=C$. Thus
		\[
		\operatorname{Ext}^1_\Lambda(A,Y)=\ker u^*/\operatorname{im}v^*=0
		\qquad(Y=A,B,C,D).
		\]
		For $Y=A,D$, the degree-one term is zero; for $Y=B$, the
		degree-zero differential is surjective; for $Y=C$, the
		degree-one differential is injective. The other twelve groups
		$\operatorname{Ext}^1_\Lambda(X,Y)$, with $X\in\{B,C,D\}$ and
		$Y\in\{A,B,C,D\}$, vanish because $B,C,D$ are projective.
		Additivity in both variables therefore gives $\operatorname{Ext}^1_\Lambda(\mathcal M,\mathcal M)=0$.
		
		
		It remains to exclude $E$ from both Ext-orthogonal subcategories.
		Maps of representations give
		\[
		\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(B,E)=0,
		\qquad \operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(C,E)=\mathbf{k},
		\qquad \operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(D,E)=0.
		\]
		Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(-,E)$ to \eqref{eq:main-2exact}
		therefore gives
		$\operatorname{Ext}^1_\Lambda(A,E)\cong\mathbf{k}$.
		The projective resolution
		\[
		0\longrightarrow D\xrightarrow{u}C\longrightarrow E
		\longrightarrow0
		\]
		and $\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(C,D)=0$ give
		\[
		\operatorname{Ext}^1_\Lambda(E,D)
		\cong\operatorname{coker}\bigl(
		\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(C,D)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(D,D)\bigr)
		\cong\mathbf{k}.
		\]
		By \eqref{eq:representation-decomposition}, every module
		$Z\notin\mathcal M$ has $E$ as a direct summand. Additivity of
		$\operatorname{Ext}^1$ then gives both $\operatorname{Ext}^1_\Lambda(A,Z)\neq0$ and
		$\operatorname{Ext}^1_\Lambda(Z,D)\neq0$. Since $A,D\in\mathcal M$, neither
		Ext-orthogonal subcategory contains such a $Z$. Together with
		$\operatorname{Ext}^1_\Lambda(\mathcal M,\mathcal M)=0$, this proves the two required
		equalities.
		Since $\mathcal M$ is a $2$-cluster tilting subcategory of the abelian
		category $\operatorname{mod}\Lambda$, it is $2$-abelian by
		\cite[Theorem~3.16]{J16}.

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:example-wide-classification}
		The wide subcategories of $\mathcal M$ are exactly
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:8wide}
			\mathbf0,
			\quad
			\operatorname{add} A,\ \operatorname{add} B,\ \operatorname{add} C,\ \operatorname{add} D,
			\quad
			\operatorname{add}(A\oplus C),\ \operatorname{add}(B\oplus D),
			\quad
			\mathcal M.
		\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal W$ be wide. By Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-characterization},
		the minimal $2$-kernel of $w:B\to A$ in
		\eqref{eq:minimal-table} gives
		\[
		A,B\in\mathcal W\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C,D\in\mathcal W.
		\]
		The minimal $2$-kernel and $2$-cokernel of $v:C\to B$ give
		\[
		B,C\in\mathcal W\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A,D\in\mathcal W,
		\]
		and the minimal $2$-cokernel of $u:D\to C$ gives
		\[
		C,D\in\mathcal W\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A,B\in\mathcal W.
		\]
		Finally, if $A,D\in\mathcal W$, the minimal $2$-extension
		\eqref{eq:main-2exact} gives $B,C\in\mathcal W$ by
		Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-characterization}. Thus any wide
		subcategory containing one of the pairs
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:example-forcing-pairs}
			\{A,B\},\quad \{B,C\},\quad \{C,D\},\quad \{A,D\}
		\end{equation}
		is all of $\mathcal M$.
		
		To verify the remaining candidates, first consider either
		$\mathcal W=\operatorname{add}(A\oplus C)$ or $\mathcal W=\operatorname{add}(B\oplus D)$.
		Write $(S,T)=(A,C)$ in the first case and $(S,T)=(B,D)$
		in the second. In both cases, \eqref{eq:hom-table} gives
		\[
		\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(S,T)=\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(T,S)=0,
		\qquad
		\operatorname{End}_\Lambda(S)=\operatorname{End}_\Lambda(T)=\mathbf{k}.
		\]
		Every morphism
		\[
		f:S^{\oplus a}\oplus T^{\oplus b}
		\longrightarrow S^{\oplus c}\oplus T^{\oplus d}
		\]
		is therefore a block diagonal matrix with one $c\times a$
		matrix and one $d\times b$ matrix over $\mathbf{k}$. Independent
		changes of basis in these two blocks put each matrix into its
		rank normal form. Consequently, there are decompositions of
		its source and target as $I\oplus X_0$ and $I\oplus Y_0$,
		with $I,X_0,Y_0\in\mathcal W$, for which
		\[
		f=\begin{pmatrix}1_I&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}:
		I\oplus X_0\longrightarrow I\oplus Y_0.
		\]
		For this form of $f$, a $2$-kernel and a $2$-cokernel in $\mathcal M$
		are, respectively,
		\[
		0\longrightarrow X_0\xrightarrow{\binom{0}{1}}
		I\oplus X_0,
		\qquad
		I\oplus Y_0\xrightarrow{(0\;1)}Y_0\longrightarrow0.
		\]
		Indeed, applying $\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(Z,-)$ or
		$\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(-,Z)$ for any $Z\in\mathcal M$ gives the required
		exact sequences, since the displayed inclusion and projection
		split and $f$ is the identity on $I$. Transporting these
		complexes along the chosen source and target isomorphisms
		proves condition~\ref{W1} for every morphism in each of the
		two subcategories.
		
		For $X,Y\in\operatorname{add}(A\oplus C)$, every summand of
		$\operatorname{Ext}^2_\Lambda(Y,X)$ obtained by additivity in both variables
		is one of
		\[
		\operatorname{Ext}^2_\Lambda(A,A),\quad \operatorname{Ext}^2_\Lambda(A,C),\quad
		\operatorname{Ext}^2_\Lambda(C,A),\quad \operatorname{Ext}^2_\Lambda(C,C),
		\]
		and all four vanish by \eqref{eq:ext2-example}.
		For $X,Y\in\operatorname{add}(B\oplus D)$, the module $Y$ is projective,
		so $\operatorname{Ext}^2_\Lambda(Y,X)=0$ as well.
		By \cite[Proposition~2.9(4)]{HJ21}, equivalence classes of
		$2$-extensions of $Y$ by $X$ in $\mathcal M$ are identified with
		$\operatorname{Ext}^2_\Lambda(Y,X)$. Thus every such extension with endpoints
		in either of the two subcategories is equivalent to
		\[
		0\longrightarrow X\xrightarrow{1_X}X\xrightarrow{0}Y
		\xrightarrow{1_Y}Y\longrightarrow0.
		\]
		The middle terms lie in the same subcategory as the endpoints,
		which proves condition~\ref{W2}. Hence both
		$\operatorname{add}(A\oplus C)$ and $\operatorname{add}(B\oplus D)$ are wide.
		
		For $\operatorname{add} Z$ with $Z\in\{A,B,C,D\}$, the same verification
		uses a single matrix block and the equality
		$\operatorname{Ext}^2_\Lambda(Z,Z)=0$. The zero subcategory satisfies the
		two conditions using zero complexes, and $\mathcal M$ itself is wide.
		Finally, closure under direct summands and
		\eqref{eq:representation-decomposition} show that every wide
		subcategory of $\mathcal M$ is determined by the subset of
		$\{A,B,C,D\}$ it contains. Every proper subset with at least
		three elements contains one of the pairs in
		\eqref{eq:example-forcing-pairs}. Among the two-element subsets,
		only $\{A,C\}$ and $\{B,D\}$ avoid these pairs. This exhausts
		all sixteen subsets and proves \eqref{eq:8wide}.

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor:no-classifying-example}
		The $2$-abelian category $\mathcal M$ in \eqref{eq:example-M} admits no classifying $2$-support datum in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:classifying}.
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
If $(Y,\sigma)$ were classifying, Theorem~\ref{thm:reconstruction} would give a homeomorphism
		\[
		Y\cong\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathbf{Prime}_2(\mathcal M)}(\mathcal M).
		\]
		Hence $|\mathbf{Spcl}(Y)|=25$.  But the classifying property would also give a lattice isomorphism
		\[
		\mathbf{Wide}_2(\mathcal M)\cong\mathbf{Spcl}(Y),
		\]
		contradicting $|\mathbf{Wide}_2(\mathcal M)|=8$.

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-separation} shows that prime-radicality
		holds for every wide subcategory under our general hypotheses.
		The obstruction in this example is the failure to realize all
		specialization-closed subsets as supports of wide subcategories. It is also visible directly
		in the lattice: if $\mathcal A=\operatorname{add} A$, $\mathcal B=\operatorname{add} B$,
		and $\mathcal C=\operatorname{add} C$, then
		\[
		\mathcal A\wedge(\mathcal B\vee\mathcal C)=\mathcal A,
		\qquad
		(\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal B)\vee(\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal C)=\mathbf0.
		\]
		Thus $\mathbf{Wide}_2(\mathcal M)$ is not distributive, whereas the lattice of
		specialization-closed subsets of any space is distributive.
