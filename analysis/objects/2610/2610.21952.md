<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
For \(N\in\mathbb{N}\), define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clouds}
\mathcal{C}_N
:=
\left\{
E(g_n):1\le n\le N
\right\},
\qquad
\mathcal{P}_N
:=
\left\{
E_1(g_n):1\le n\le N
\right\}.
\end{equation}
These sets are obtained by sampling \(E(t)\) and \(E_1(t)\), respectively,
at the Gram points \(g_n\) of the Hardy \(Z\)-function. We refer to
\(\mathcal{C}_N\) and \(\mathcal{P}_N\) as the full and prime \(N\)-th Gram
clouds, respectively.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The complete numerical database, together with the data sets, source
code, computational scripts, animations, and supplementary material underlying
the numerical experiments in this paper, is available in the accompanying
Zenodo repository~\cite{JerbyZenodoGramPoints}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
We will refer to the pair \( t_-(a),t_+(a) \) associated with an isolated Gram point \( g_n \) as a \emph{confined} pair of zeros if, throughout the deformation \( 0\leq a\leq1 \), these
two branches remain the only zeros of \( Z(t; \gamma(a) ) \), counted with multiplicity,
in the vertical Gram strip
\[
       g_{n-1} < Re(t) \leq g_{n+1}.
\]
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The confinement condition is stronger than requiring \( g_n \) to be an
isolated bad Gram point. Recall that a \emph{Gram block} of length \( N \)
is a sequence
\[
g_m,g_{m+1},\ldots,g_{m+N},
\]
whose endpoints are good and whose intermediate Gram points are bad
\cite{Edwards,RosserYoheSchoenfeld}. Thus an isolated bad Gram point
\( g_n \) is the unique interior point of a Gram block of length two,
\(
(g_{n-1},g_{n+1}]
\). Rosser's rule asserts that a Gram block of length \( N \) contains exactly
\( N \) zeros of \( Z(t) \) on the real axis. Hence a length-two block
normally contains two zeros. However, Rosser's rule is not universal, and
Trudgian proved that it fails a positive proportion of the time
\cite{Trudgian}. Thus isolated badness does not, in general, guarantee a
two-zero local configuration. It should be noted, however, that among the first \(250{,}000\) Gram points
in the data set considered, no violations of Rosser's rule occur.
Consequently, every isolated bad Gram point encountered in this range is
associated with a confined pair.

It should be noted that in our previous work~\cite{JerbyDiscriminant},
the viscosity bound
\(
        \mu(g_n)>4
\)
was investigated at substantially larger indices, including a systematic
computation of all bad Gram points up to the range
\( n\leq 2.5 \times10^{7}\).
The numerical evidence revealed a sharp distinction between isolated and
non-isolated bad Gram points. The bound was seen to persist for the isolated 
configurations, whereas bad Gram points belonging to longer Gram blocks
could have viscosity far below \(4\).  In particular, the low-viscosity
``corrupt'' points observed there were non-isolated. An extreme example is the non-isolated
\(g_{9807962}\), for which
\(\mu(g_{9807962})=0.0750883\).
Finally, we note that the data suggest more than the lower bound
\(\mu(g_n)>4\) for isolated bad Gram points associated with a confined pair
of zeros.  They also suggest the asymptotic relation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:liminf_scaled_mu}
\liminf_{\substack{n\to\infty\\
g_n\ \mathrm{isolated\ bad,\ confined}}}
\frac{2\pi}{\log(g_n/2\pi)}\,\mu(g_n)
=
2.
\end{equation}
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:layer_decomposition}
For every Gram point \(g_n\), the AFE sum admits the finite decomposition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:E_layer_decomposition}
        E(g_n)
        =
        1+\sum_{j\ge 1}E_j(g_n).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the full cloud \(E(g_n)\) is obtained by adding the
multiplicative-depth layers, together with the contribution of the term
\(k=1\).
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By definition,
\[
        E(g_n)
        =
        \sum_{k\le N(g_n)}
        \frac{e^{ig_n\log k}}{\sqrt{k}} .
\]
The term \(k=1\) contributes \(1\).  Every integer \(k\ge 2\) has a unique
prime factorization, and hence a well-defined value of \(\Omega(k)\).  The
sets
\[
        \{k\le N(g_n):\Omega(k)=j\},
        \qquad j\ge 1,
\]
therefore form a disjoint partition of the integers \(2\le k\le N(g_n)\).
Splitting the sum according to this partition gives
\[
        E(g_n)
        =
        1+
        \sum_{j\ge 1}
        \sum_{\substack{k\le N(g_n)\\ \Omega(k)=j}}
        \frac{e^{ig_n\log k}}{\sqrt{k}}
        =
        1+\sum_{j\ge1}E_j(g_n),
\]
as claimed.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
It should be noted that in the classical random-phase heuristic model, the
prime sum
\[
        \sum_{p\le X}\frac{e^{it\log p}}{\sqrt p}
\]
is typically considered to behave like a weighted planar random walk. In this work \(t\) is sampled only at the Gram points \(g_n\), which already shifts from the classical heuristic setting. Rather, it gives a natural ``null model'' for the prime cloud
\(\mathcal{P}_N\). 

From this point of view, the striking feature of the isolated bad Gram
points is that they do not behave like generic samples from this random
phase model. This suggests that the condition of being an isolated bad Gram point imposes
a nontrivial constraint on the prime phases. The reason this is plausible is the prime--zero duality expressed by the
explicit formula. In particular, prime sums and zero configurations are not independent
objects, but two sides of the same oscillatory structure.  Thus a special
local zero configuration near \(g_n\), such as the confined pair associated
with an isolated bad Gram point, may leave a visible signature in the prime
projection \(E_1(g_n)\).  In this interpretation, the prime lobes are not a
generic random-walk feature. Rather, they are the geometric trace of conditioning the
prime phases on a special local zero configuration.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
It is instructive to compare this with the simplest random-phase model.
Suppose that, for every prime \(p\), the phase \(\Theta_p\) were genuinely
uniform on \([0,2\pi)\), independently of all other prime phases. Then
\[
        \mathbb E(\cos\Theta_p)
        =
        \mathbb E(\sin\Theta_p)
        =
        0,
\]
and
\[
        \mathbb E(\cos^2\Theta_p)
        =
        \mathbb E(\sin^2\Theta_p)
        =
        \frac12,
        \qquad
        \mathbb E(\cos\Theta_p\sin\Theta_p)=0.
\]
Hence the covariance contributed by the \(p\)-th prime would be
\[
        \frac{1}{2p}I_2.
\]
Adding the prime contributions gives
\[
        \Sigma_N
        \approx
        \frac12
        \left(
        \sum_{p\leq N}\frac1p
        \right)
        I_2.
\]
The covariance would therefore be rotationally symmetric, and the local
contours would be circles.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
For \(t\in\mathbb R\), define the partial AFE sums
\[
        S_j(t)
        :=
        \sum_{k=1}^{j}
        \frac{e^{it\log k}}{\sqrt{k}},
        \qquad
        1\le j\le N(t).
\]
Thus \(S_{N(t)}(t)=E(t)\).  The curve
\(
        \{S_j(t):1\le j\le N(t)\}
\)
will be called the \emph{partial-sum trunk} at \(t\). We use these trunks only as a diagnostic.  For an isolated bad Gram point
\(g_n\), we compare the three trunks at
\(
        g_{n-1}, g_n, g_{n+1}
\).
Since
\(
        g_{n+1}-g_n
        \sim
        \frac{\pi}{\theta'(g_n)} \) and
       \(
        \theta'(g_n)
        \sim
        \frac12\log\!\left(\frac{g_n}{2\pi}\right)
\),
one Gram step changes the \(k\)-th phase by
\(
        (g_{n+1}-g_n)\log k
        \sim
        \pi\frac{\log k}{\log N}
\).
Thus low-index terms rotate very little from one Gram point to the next,
whereas terms near \(k=N\) rotate by almost \(\pi\).  This simple phase shear
is useful for visualizing how cancellation at the central bad point may be
converted into reinforcement at one of the neighboring good Gram points. Figure~\ref{fig:generic_trunks} shows representative isolated bad Gram points
away from the lowest-viscosity regime.  
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75 \textwidth]{generic-trunks.png}
    \caption{Trunks for five representative non-extremal isolated
    bad Gram points \(n=60677,169832,34454,124634,87302\).  For each triple,
    the trajectories \(S_j(g_{n-1})\), \(S_j(g_n)\), and \(S_j(g_{n+1})\) are
    shown in green, red, and blue, respectively.  Filled circles mark the
    terminal values \(S_N(g_m)\).}
    \label{fig:generic_trunks}
\end{figure}

No uniform three-trunk pattern is
visible in these generic examples.
A more coherent pattern appears among some of the isolated bad Gram points
with very small viscosity.  Figure~\ref{fig:low_visc_trunks} shows the five
smallest-viscosity examples in the present data set.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{low-visc-trunks.png}
    \caption{Trunks for the five lowest-viscosity isolated bad Gram
    points \(n=8569,45924,64256,39324,96237\).  The colors are as in
    Figure~\ref{fig:generic_trunks}.}
    \label{fig:low_visc_trunks}
\end{figure}

In these examples, one neighboring trunk terminates near a comparatively
small endpoint, while the opposite neighboring trunk reaches a much larger
endpoint.  Writing
\[
        A_{\mathrm{act}}
        :=
        \max\{|Z(g_{n-1})|,|Z(g_{n+1})|\},
\]
the observed coherent configurations suggest the rough slope estimate
\[
        |Z'(g_n)|
        \asymp
        \frac{A_{\mathrm{act}}}{g_{n+1}-g_n}
        \sim
        \frac{\theta'(g_n)}{\pi}A_{\mathrm{act}}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
        \mu(g_n)
        =
        \left|\frac{Z'(g_n)}{Z(g_n)}\right|
        \asymp
        \frac{\theta'(g_n)}{\pi}
        \frac{A_{\mathrm{act}}}{|Z(g_n)|}.
\]
This gives an intuitive explanation for why very small viscosity is difficult
to achieve and how the bound arises in such coherent configurations. The same phase organization that
produces cancellation at the central bad point tends, after one Gram step, to
produce a compensating neighboring excursion, and this excursion is measured
on the derivative scale by division by the Gram spacing.

We emphasize that this is only a qualitative mechanism.  We do not expect
every extremal point to arise from such a coherent three-trunk
