<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rk_alpha0}
The case $\alpha=0$ is closely related to a result of Caffarelli \cite{Caffarelli_1993}, stating that a convex subsolution to the equation $\det\mathsf{D}^2u=1$ on a domain in $\mathbb{R}^n$ cannot vanish on an affine subspace of dimension $n/2$ or higher (see also \cite{CaffarelliYuan2022,Mooney_2015,MooneyRakshit2023}). Indeed, the above answer implies this result by the comparison principle, whereas the shorter proof of the result by Mooney \cite{Mooney_2015} can be adapted to give one direction of the answer for $\alpha=0$ (the other, easier direction is provided by a Pogorelov-type barrier; see below). Nevertheless, even in this case, our proof of the former direction is different from Mooney's, thus giving yet another proof of Caffarelli's result.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rk_motivation}
For a solution $u$ to a Monge-Amp\`ere equation on a convex domain, ``having finite slope'' (at a boundary point) is essentially a local Lipschitz property and follows on many occasions from the vast existing work on boundary/global estimates for Monge-Amp\`ere equations 
(see e.g.\ \cite{Le_2, Le_3, Le_4, Le-Savin, Mohammed, Savin-Zhang}), whereas ``having infinite slope'' is a less studied aspect. Our motivation for studying the latter comes from affine differential geometry, where ``having infinite slope everywhere on $\partial\Omega$'' (equivalent to $\lim_{x\to\partial\Omega}\|\mathsf{D} u(x)\|\to+\infty$) corresponds to the \emph{Euclidean completeness} of the Legendre dual hypersurface in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$. For example, \emph{hyperbolic affine spheres} and their generalization, convex hypersurfaces of \emph{constant affine Gauss-Kronecker curvature}, are given respectively by convex functions $w,u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^\infty(\Omega)$ satisfying 
$$
\det \mathsf{D}^2 w=(-w)^{-n-2}\  \text{ in $\Omega$},\quad	w|_{\partial\Omega}=0,
$$
$$
\det \mathsf{D}^2 u=(-w)^{-n-2}\  \text{ in $\Omega$, with $w$ as above};
$$
the infiniteness of slopes has been established for $w$ in \cite{Cheng-Yau}, for $u$ in \cite{Li-Simon-Chen} under smoothness assumptions, and generalized in \cite{Li-Simon-Zhao,  Nie-Seppi_1, Nie-Seppi_3,Nie-Seppi_2, Wu-Zhao, Xiong-Yang}.

<!-- thmx-1: body_md -->
\label{thm_A}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a bounded convex domain, $F\subset\partial\Omega$ be a $d$-dimensional flat boundary piece, and $L:={\mathrm{Aff}}(F)$ be the affine hull of $F$. 
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item\label{item_thmA1}
Let $\alpha\leq 2d-n$, and let $u\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ be a convex function vanishing on $F$ and satisfying any of the conditions
\begin{itemize}
	\vspace{3pt}
	\item $\det\mathsf{D}^2u\geq M\,{\mathrm{dist}}(\,\cdot\,,L)^\alpha\ $ in $\Omega$,
	\vspace{2pt}
	\item $\det\mathsf{D}^2u\geq M\,{\mathrm{dist}}(\,\cdot\,,\partial\Omega)^\alpha\ $ in $\Omega$,
	\vspace{2pt}	
	\item $u<0$ and $\det\mathsf{D}^2u\geq M\,(-u)^\alpha\ $ in $\Omega$,
	\vspace{3pt}	
\end{itemize}
   
                                                                                                                                                 
   
where $M>0$. Then $u$ has infinite slope at $F$.
\item\label{item_thmA2}
Let $\alpha\leq 2d-2n$. Then there does not exist any convex function $u\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ satisfying 
$$
\det \mathsf{D}^2u\geq M\,{\mathrm{dist}}(\,\cdot\,,L)^\alpha\ \text{ in }\Omega
$$
with $M>0$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end thmx-1 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Although part \ref{item_thmA2} is stated only for the first equation in \eqref{eqn_introequations}, the same conclusion for the second equation follows  because ${\mathrm{dist}}(\,\cdot\,,\partial\Omega)\leq {\mathrm{dist}}(\,\cdot\,,L)$ and $\alpha\leq 2d-2n<0$. In contrast, in part \ref{item_thmA1}, $\alpha$ does not have a definite sign, hence there is no direct implication between the statements for different equations. Also note that the third equation does not suffer from existence issues: it is known to admit a convex solution whenever $\alpha\neq n$, while the case $\alpha=n$ amounts to the Monge-Amp\`ere eigenvalue problem  (see e.g.\ \cite{Gu, Le_2} for an overview). Moreover, the solution is known to be unique for $\alpha<n$, while a recent work \cite{Zhou} shows the same for $\alpha>n$.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rk_critical}
In the easier case $\alpha<2d-n$,  Theorem \ref{thm_A} \ref{item_thmA1} can be proved alternatively via barriers, which give the following more refined estimate for the first two (resp.\ the last) equation in \eqref{eqn_introequations}:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_introholder}
-u \gtrsim {\mathrm{dist}}(\,\cdot\,,L)^\frac{\alpha+2n-2d}{n}\quad\Big(\text{resp. }-u \gtrsim {\mathrm{dist}}(\,\cdot\,,L)^\frac{2n-2d}{n-\alpha}\Big)\ \ \text{ in }\Gamma \text{ near }F,
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ is any cone with apex on $F$ and contained in $\Omega$ near the apex. When $\alpha=2d-n$, so that the exponents in \eqref{eqn_introholder} are $1$, these estimates still hold but fail to be sharp and do not imply the infiniteness of slopes. We expect a sharp estimate with a logarithmic factor to do so, as already established for $d=n-1$ by Le \cite{Le_4} and Gu \cite{Gu} (see \S \ref{subsec_related} below).

<!-- thmx-2: body_md -->
\label{thm_B}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a bounded convex domain contained in a convex cone $\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ such that the ridge $L$ of $\Gamma$ has dimension $d$ and contains some $x_0\in\partial\Omega$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
	\item\label{item_thmB1}
Let  $\alpha> 2d-n$, and let $u\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ be a convex function vanishing on $\partial\Omega$ and satisfying either of the conditions
\begin{itemize}
	\vspace{3pt}
	\item $\det\mathsf{D}^2u\leq M\,{\mathrm{dist}}(\,\cdot\,,L)^\alpha\ $ in $\Omega$, 
	\vspace{2pt}	
	\item $u<0$ and $\det\mathsf{D}^2u\leq M\,(-u)^\alpha\ $ in $\Omega$,
	\vspace{3pt}	
\end{itemize}
where $M>0$. Then $u$ has finite slope at $x_0$. 
                                                                                           
   
                                                                        
   
	\item\label{item_thmB2}
	 Let $\alpha>2d-2n$, and let $g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}$ be a nonnegative measurable function satisfying
$$
	 g(x)\leq M\,{\mathrm{dist}}(x,L)^\alpha\ \ \text{ for all }x\in\Omega
$$
	 with $M>0$. Then there exists a unique convex solution $u\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ to the Dirichlet problem 
	\begin{equation}\label{eqn_thmB2}
	\det\mathsf{D}^2u=g\  \text{ in }\Omega,\quad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0.
	\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end thmx-2 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
The classical existence theorem states that a sufficient condition for \eqref{eqn_thmB2} to admit a convex solution is $\int_\Omega g(x)\mathsf{d} x<+\infty$. A new existence result of Le \cite[Theorem 1.1]{Le_5} implies that the condition can be weakened to 
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_Leexistence}
\int_\Omega g(x)\,{\mathrm{dist}}(x,\partial\Omega)\mathsf{d} x<+\infty.
\end{equation}
Theorem \ref{thm_B} \ref{item_thmB2} is not covered by this result. Indeed, if $g(x)={\mathrm{dist}}(x,L)^\alpha$ and $\Omega$ is the interior of $\Gamma\cap B_r(x_0)$, where $B_r(x_0)$ is a ball centered at $x_0$, then \eqref{eqn_Leexistence} holds if and only if $\alpha>d-n-1$, which is stronger than our threshold $\alpha>2d-2n$ unless $d=n-1$.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- corx-1: body_md -->
\label{cor_C}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a bounded convex domain contained in a convex cone $\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ such that the ridge $L$ of $\Gamma$ has dimension $d$ and contains a flat boundary piece $F\subset\partial\Omega$ as a subset with nonempty interior. 
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
	\item There exists a convex function $u\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ satisfying
	$$
	\det \mathsf{D}^2u\asymp {\mathrm{dist}}(\,\cdot\,,L)^\alpha\ \text{ in }\Omega, \quad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0
	$$
	if and only if $\alpha>2d-2n$. Such a function $u$ has finite slope at $F$ if and only if $\alpha>2d-n$.
	\item\label{item_corC2}
	Let $u\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ be a nonzero convex function satisfying
$$
\det \mathsf{D}^2u\asymp (-u)^\alpha\ \text{ in }\Omega, \quad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0
$$
for some $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}$. Then $u$ has finite slope at $F$ if and only if $\alpha>2d-n$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemma_AlexandrovMP}
For any convex function $u\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ such that $u|_{\partial\Omega}=0$, we have
$$
		|u(x)|^n\leq C_n\,{\mathrm{diam}}(\Omega)^{n-1} {\mathrm{dist}}(x,\partial\Omega)\mathcal{M}u(\Omega),
$$
where the constant $C_n>0$ only depends on the dimension $n$, and ${\mathrm{diam}}(\Omega)$ denotes the diameter of $\Omega$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lemma_comparison}
If two convex functions $u,v\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ satisfy
$$
u|_{\partial\Omega}\geq v|_{\partial\Omega},\qquad \det\mathsf{D}^2u\leq \det\mathsf{D}^2v \ \text{ in }\Omega,
$$
then we have $u\geq v$ in $\Omega$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma_existence}
Let $\mu$ be a Borel measure on $\Omega$ with total mass $\mu(\Omega)<+\infty$ and $\varphi:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ be a function which can be extended to a continuous convex function on $\overline{\Omega}$ (cf.\ Remark \ref{rk_extension} below). Then there exists a convex function $u\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ such that
$$
\det\mathsf{D}^2u=\mu\ \text{ in }\Omega,\quad u|_{\partial\Omega}=\varphi.
$$

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lemma_stability}
Let $u_k:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$, $k=1,2,\cdots$ be a sequence of convex functions converging to a convex function $u_\infty:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ uniformly on compact subsets of $\Omega$. Then the Monge-Amp\`ere measure $\mathcal{M}u_k$ converges weakly to $\mathcal{M}u_\infty$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lemma_uniform}
Let $u_k:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$, $k=1,2,\cdots$ be a sequence of convex functions pointwise converging to a function $u_\infty:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$. Then $u_\infty$ is convex and the convergence $u_k\to u_\infty$ is uniform on compact subsets of $\Omega$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop_slopes}
Given a convex function $u\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ and a boundary point $x_0\in\partial\Omega$, the following conditions are equivalent to each other:
\begin{itemize}
	\item The limit \eqref{eqn_slope} is finite for some $x_1\in\Omega$.

	\item The limit \eqref{eqn_slope} is finite for all $x_1\in\Omega$.
	\item $u$ admits a subgradient at $x_0$, i.e., $\partial u(x_0)\neq\varnothing$.
	\item There exists a sequence $(x_k)_{k=1,2,\cdots}$ of points in $\Omega$ tending to $x_0$ such that $u$ is differentiable\footnote{A convex function $u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ is differentiable at $x\in\Omega$ if and only if $\partial u(x)$ consists of a single point, namely the gradient $\mathsf{D} u(x)$. The set of points where $u$ is not differentiable has measure zero. See \cite[\S 25]{Rockafellar}.} at $x_k$ and $|\mathsf{D} u(x_k)|$ does not tend to $+\infty$.
\end{itemize}

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rk_extension}
As in much of the literature on Monge-Amp\`ere equations, we assume $u\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ in the above discussions for simplicity, but this assumption is not essential. In fact, every convex function $u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ can be uniquely  extended to a lower semicontinuous convex function $u:\overline{\Omega}\to(-\infty,+\infty]$ by setting its boundary values to be
$$
u|_{\partial\Omega}:\partial\Omega\to (-\infty,+\infty],\qquad u(x_0):=\liminf_{x\in\Omega,\,x\to x_0}u(x).
$$
This extension belongs to $C(\overline{\Omega})$ if and only if $u|_{\partial\Omega}\in C(\partial\Omega)$ (which requires $u|_{\partial\Omega}<+\infty$ in particular); see e.g.~\cite[\S 4]{Nie-Seppi_1}. Most of the discussions above actually only require $u|_{\partial\Omega}<+\infty$, and do not need $u|_{\partial\Omega}$ to be continuous. 
For example, in Lemma \ref{lemma_existence}, we may only assume that $\varphi:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ is the boundary value of some convex function $\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ in the above sense (although the resulting solution $u$ might not be in $C(\overline{\Omega})$).
This assumption is equivalent to the condition that $\varphi$ is lower semicontinuous and restricts to a convex function on every line segment contained in $\partial\Omega$ (see \cite[Lemma 4.6]{Nie-Seppi_1}).
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lemma_Lecomparison}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a bounded convex domain and $u,v\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ be convex functions such that 
$$
0\geq u\geq v \ \text{ on } \partial\Omega,\quad\det\mathsf{D}^2u\leq M(-u)^\alpha\ \text{ and }\ \det\mathsf{D}^2v\geq M(-v)^\alpha \ \text{ in }\Omega
$$
for $\alpha<n$ and $M>0$. Moreover, assume $v<0$ in $\Omega$. Then we have $u\geq v$ in $\Omega$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
\textbf{Case $\alpha<0$.} The usual comparison principle (Lemma \ref{lemma_comparison}) has a classical generalization to sub- and supersolutions of the equation $\det\mathsf{D}^2u=F(x,u)$ with right-hand side $F(x,u)$  non-decreasing in $u$ (see e.g.\ \cite[Prop.\ 2]{Cheng-Yau}, \cite[Lemma 6.4]{Nie-Seppi_2}), from which the required statement for $\alpha\leq 0$ follows. For completeness, we now give a simplified proof for the case $\alpha<0$, where $(-u)^\alpha$ is strictly increasing in $u$.

Suppose by contradiction that $u<v$ somewhere in $\Omega$. We may pick $\epsilon>0$ such that
$$
E:=\{x\in\overline{\Omega}\mid u(x)< v(x)-\epsilon\}
$$
is nonempty. Since $u|_{\partial\Omega}\geq v|_{\partial\Omega}$, we have $\overline{E}\subset\Omega$ and $u=v-\epsilon$ on $\partial E$. Then, on the one hand, since $u=v-\epsilon$ on $\partial E$ and $u\leq v-\epsilon$ in $E$, the mass of the Monge-Amp\`ere measure of $u$ on $E$ is no less than that of $v-\epsilon$ (see e.g.\ \cite[Lemma 1.4.1]{Gutierrez}):
$$
\mathcal{M}u(E)\geq \mathcal{M}(v-\epsilon)(E)=\mathcal{M}v(E);
$$ 
on the other hand, by the hypothesis on $\det\mathsf{D}^2u$ and $\det \mathsf{D}^2v$, we have
$$
\mathcal{M}u(E)\leq M\int_{E}(-u(x))^\alpha\mathsf{d} x< M\int_E(-v(x))^\alpha\mathsf{d} x\leq \mathcal{M}u(E)
$$
(the ``$<$'' uses the conditions $\alpha<0$, $v<0$ in $\Omega$, and $\overline{E}\subset\Omega$, the last two ensuring that the integrals are finite), a contradiction.

\textbf{Case $\alpha\geq0$.} Let us first give a proof under the extra assumption $v|_{\partial\Omega}<0$. Suppose by contradiction that $u<v$ somewhere in $\Omega$. By assumptions (note that $u$ and $v$ are nonpositive), the ratio $u/v$ is continuous on $\overline{\Omega}$, and we have $u/v\leq 1$ on $\partial\Omega$ and $u/v>1$ somewhere in $\Omega$. Therefore, $u/v$ attains its maximum
$$
\Lambda:=\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}>1
$$
at some $x_0\in\Omega$. The function $w:=\Lambda v$ has the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item\label{item_leproof1}
$u\geq w$ in $\Omega$, with equality attained at $x_0$;
\item\label{item_leproof2} 
$u>w$ on $\partial\Omega$;
\item\label{item_leproof3} 
$\det \mathsf{D}^2w\geq M(-w)^\alpha$.
\end{enumerate}
They follow respectively from the definition of $w$, the inequalities $u\geq v$ and $v>w$ on $\partial\Omega$ (the latter uses $v|_{\partial\Omega}<0$), and a calculation similar to \eqref{eqn_scaledownv}.
By property \ref{item_leproof2}, we may pick $\epsilon>0$ such that 
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_leproof1}
u\geq w+\epsilon\ \text{ on }\partial\Omega.
\end{equation}
Since $(-u)^\alpha$ is non-increasing in $u$, it follows from properties \ref{item_leproof1} and \ref{item_leproof3} that
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_leproof2}
\det \mathsf{D}^2u\leq M(-u)^\alpha\leq M(-w)^\alpha\leq \det \mathsf{D}^2w=\det \mathsf{D}^2(w+\epsilon)
\end{equation}
By the usual comparison principle (Lemma \ref{lemma_comparison}), \eqref{eqn_leproof1} and \eqref{eqn_leproof2} imply that $u\geq w+\epsilon$ in $\Omega$, contradicting property \ref{item_leproof1}. This completes the proof when $v|_{\partial\Omega}<0$. 

The general case can be reduced to the $v|_{\partial\Omega}<0$ case by dilating $v$ as follows. Again, suppose by contradiction that $u<v$ somewhere in $\Omega$. By translation, we may assume $0\in\Omega$ and $u(0)<v(0)$. Dilating the domain of $v$ radially by a factor of $\lambda>1$ and restricting the dilated function to $\Omega$, we obtain the function
$$
v_\lambda:\overline{\Omega}\to \mathbb{R},\quad v_\lambda(x):=v(\lambda^{-1}x).
$$
Since $v(0)<0$, we have, by convexity,
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_leproof2.1}
v_\lambda(x)=v\big((1-\lambda^{-1})0+\lambda^{-1}x\big)\leq (1-\lambda^{-1})v(0)+\lambda^{-1}v(x)<\lambda^{-1}v(x)\ \text{ for all } x\in\overline{\Omega}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $v_\lambda|_{\partial\Omega}<0$.
Meanwhile, the condition $\det\mathsf{D}^2v\geq M(-v)^\alpha$ implies 
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_leproof3}
\det \mathsf{D}^2v_\lambda(x)=\lambda^{-2n}(\det \mathsf{D}^2v)(\lambda^{-1}x)\geq \lambda^{-2n}M(-v_\lambda(x))^\alpha\ \text{ for all } x\in\Omega.
\end{equation}
Replacing $v$ by $v_\lambda$, we may repeat the above derivation of contradiction for $v|_{\partial\Omega}<0$ as long as we pick $\lambda>1$ small enough such that properties \ref{item_leproof1}, \ref{item_leproof2} and \ref{item_leproof3} hold. More specifically, by first letting $\lambda$ be smaller than
$$
\frac{u(0)}{v_\lambda(0)}=\frac{u(0)}{v(0)}>1
$$
and noting that $u/v_\lambda<\lambda$ on $\partial\Omega$ by \eqref{eqn_leproof2.1}, we may assume that $u/v_\lambda$ attains its maximum $\Lambda>1$ at some $x_0\in\Omega$ and set $w:=\Lambda v_\lambda$ as before. We then adjust $\lambda$ and check those properties (with $v$ replaced by $v_\lambda$):
\begin{itemize}
\item By \eqref{eqn_leproof2.1} and the definition of $\Lambda$ and $w$, properties \ref{item_leproof1} and \ref{item_leproof2} hold.
       
                                                                           
   
                                                                   
   
\item 
By \eqref{eqn_leproof3}, we have
$$
\det \mathsf{D}^2w= \Lambda^n\det\mathsf{D}^2 v_\lambda\geq \Lambda^n\lambda^{-2n}M(-v_\lambda)^\alpha=M\lambda^{-2n}\Lambda^{n-\alpha}(-w)^\alpha.
$$
Since $\alpha<n$ and $\Lambda>1$, by decreasing $\lambda$ (which increases $\Lambda$) if necessary, we may arrange that $\lambda^{-2n}\Lambda^{n-\alpha}\geq1$, so that property \ref{item_leproof3} holds.
\end{itemize}
Now that the three properties are established, the rest of the argument is the same as that for $v|_{\partial\Omega}<0$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop_d0}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a bounded convex domain, $u\in C(\overline{\Omega})$ be a convex function, and $\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a proper convex cone. Assume that 
\begin{itemize}
\item The boundary $\partial\Omega$ contains the origin $0\in\mathbb{R}^n$.
\item $\Gamma\cap B_\epsilon \setminus\{0\}\subset \Omega$ for some $\epsilon>0$.
\item
$u(0)=0$.
\item
$u$ has finite slope at $0$.
\end{itemize}
Consider the family $(u_\lambda)_{\lambda\geq1}$ of convex functions defined by
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_propd0}
u_\lambda:\Gamma\cap B_{\lambda\epsilon}\to\mathbb{R},\quad u_\lambda(x):=\lambda u(\lambda^{-1}x).
\end{equation}
Then there exists a $1$-homogeneous continuous convex function $u_\infty$ on $\Gamma$ such that $u_\lambda$ converges to $u_\infty$ uniformly on compact subsets of $\Gamma$ as $\lambda\to+\infty$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
\label{rk_domain}
	The domain of $u_\lambda$ expands as $\lambda$ increases, and eventually contains any compact subset of $\Gamma$. The statement ``$u_\lambda$ converges uniformly on compact subsets of $\Gamma$'' should be understood as ``for any compact set $K\subset\Gamma$, there exists $\lambda_0\geq1$ such that $(u_\lambda)_{\lambda\geq\lambda_0}$ is defined on $K$ and converges uniformly on $K$''.
<!-- end remark-8 -->
<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
\label{rk_homogeneity}
By a ``$1$-homogeneous convex function on $\Gamma$'', we mean a convex function $f:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}$ such that $f(tx)=tf(x)$ for all $t\geq0$. Given a hyperplane $H\subset\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{0\}$ such that $H\cap\Gamma$ is nonempty and compact, such a function $f$ is determined by its restriction to $H\cap\Gamma$, which can be any convex function $H\cap\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}$. Requiring $f$ to be continuous is the same as requiring $f|_{H\cap \Gamma}$ to be continuous up to the boundary.  A basic fact which plays a crucial role below is that for any $1$-homogeneous convex function $f:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}$, the Monge-Amp\`ere measure $\mathcal{M}f$ (defined in the interior $\Gamma^\circ$ of $\Gamma$) vanishes. A proof goes as follows. Given a point $x\in\Gamma^\circ$ and a subgradient $a\in\partial f(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^n$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{equation_homogeneousMA}
f(x')\geq f(x)+a\cdot (x'-x)\ \ \text{ for all }x'\in\Gamma.
\end{equation}
By taking $x'=tx$ with $0<t<1$ and $t>1$ respectively, we infer that
\begin{equation}\label{equation_homogeneousMA2}
f(x)=a\cdot x.
\end{equation}
Inserting \eqref{equation_homogeneousMA2} back into \eqref{equation_homogeneousMA}, we get
$$
f(x')\geq a\cdot x'\ \ \text{ for all }x'\in\Gamma,
$$
which means that $a$ is also a subgradient of $f$ at the origin $0$, i.e., $a$ is contained in
$$
\partial f(0)=\big\{b\in\mathbb{R}^n\,\big|\,f(y)\geq b\cdot y \text{ for all }y\in\Gamma\big\}=\bigcap_{y\in\Gamma}\big\{b\in\mathbb{R}^n\,\big|\,f(y)\geq b\cdot y\big\}.
$$
In the last expression, we wrote the convex set $\partial f(0)$ as an intersection of affine half-spaces. By \eqref{equation_homogeneousMA2}, $a$ lies on the boundary of one of these half-spaces, hence also lies on the boundary of $\partial f(0)$. Since the boundary of a convex set in $\mathbb{R}^n$ has zero Lebesgue measure, we conclude that $\mathcal{M}f$ is the zero measure, as required.

\end{align}

Suppose by contradiction that $u$ has finite slope at $0$. Then we may pick a proper convex cone $\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ such that the assumptions of Proposition \ref{prop_d0} hold. The proposition and the stability of Monge-Amp\`ere measure (Lemma \ref{lemma_stability}) yield the following weak convergence of measures\footnote{This convergence should be understood in a sense similar to Remark \ref{rk_domain}, i.e., for any Borel set $B\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ such that $\overline{B}$ is compact and contained in $\Gamma^\circ$, the domain of definition of $\mathcal{M}u_\lambda$ contains $B$ when $\lambda$ is sufficiently large, and $\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\mathcal{M}u_\lambda(B)=0$.}

\end{equation}

$$

Strictly speaking, the equalities make sense only when $u$ is $C^2$; otherwise, a suitable reinterpretation via measures is required. We leave this reinterpretation to the reader.
}, in the definition \eqref{eqn_propd0} of $u_\lambda$.

\end{equation}
for any $x\in\Gamma$ and sufficiently large $\lambda\geq1$, as required.

Under condition \eqref{eqn_infiniteproof2}, estimate \eqref{eqn_proofpropd0.5} is still valid and yields the desired conclusion.
Indeed, this estimate only requires \eqref{eqn_infiniteproof1} to hold for all $x$ in a region of the form $\Gamma\cap B_\epsilon\setminus\{0\}\subset\Omega$ rather than on the whole $\Omega$, whereas Lemma \ref{lemma_twodistances} implies that the restrictions of \eqref{eqn_infiniteproof1} and \eqref{eqn_infiniteproof2} to such regions are equivalent to each other (more precisely, there exist $M,\epsilon>0$ such that \eqref{eqn_infiniteproof1} holds on $\Gamma\cap B_\epsilon\setminus\{0\}\subset\Omega$ if and only if the same is true for \eqref{eqn_infiniteproof2}, with possibly different $M$ and $\epsilon$).


Finally, under condition \eqref{eqn_infiniteproof3}, the situation is similar: we note that 
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_proofpropd02}
-C_1|x|\leq u(x)\leq -C_2|x|\ \ \text{ for all }x\in\Gamma\cap B_\epsilon\setminus\{0\}\subset\Omega
\end{equation}
for constants $C_1> C_2>0$, where the first ``$\leq$'' follows from the contradiction assumption 	``$u$ has finite slope at $0$'', which means there exists a subgradient $a\in\partial u(0)$, and hence $u(x)\geq a\cdot x\geq -|a|\,|x|$;

Therefore, we can use \eqref{eqn_proofpropd0.5} again to complete the proof of the claim.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lemma_LA}
	Let $A$ (resp.\ $C$) be a non-degenerate symmetric matrix of dimension $d_A$ (resp.\ $d_C$), $B$ be a $d_A\times d_C$ matrix, and let  $p,q\in\mathbb{R}$, $v\in\mathbb{R}^{d_A}$. Then we have the following formulas for determinants and criteria for positive definiteness:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_det1}
	\det\begin{pmatrix}
		A&B\\
		B^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}&C
	\end{pmatrix}
	=\det(A)\det\left(C-B^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}} A^{-1}B\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_pd1}
\begin{pmatrix}
	A&B\\
	B^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}&C
\end{pmatrix}>0
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ 
A>0\,\text{ and }\, C-B^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}} A^{-1}B>0,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_det2}
                                                                              
		\det(p A+q vv^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}})=p^{d_A-1}(p+q\,v^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}A^{-1}v)\det(A).
\end{equation}
Moreover, when $p,A>0$, 
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_pd2}
	p A+q vv^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}>0 
	\ \Longleftrightarrow\
                           
                   
      
\det(p A+q vv^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}})>0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Equation \eqref{eqn_det1} follows from the congruences
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_congruence}
\begin{pmatrix}
	A&B\\
	B^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}&C
\end{pmatrix}
\sim
\begin{pmatrix}
	A&0\\
	0&C-B^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}A^{-1}B
\end{pmatrix}
\sim
\begin{pmatrix}
	A-BC^{-1}B^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}&0\\
	0&C
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
which also yield
$$
\det(A)\det\left(C-B^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}} A^{-1}B\right)=\det(C)\det\left(A-B C^{-1}B^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}\right).
$$
Equation \eqref{eqn_det2} is trivial if $p=0$ or $q=0$, and otherwise follows from the above equality by letting $C=-q^{-1}$, $B=v$ and rescaling $A$. The equivalence \eqref{eqn_pd1} follows from \eqref{eqn_congruence}. Finally, to show the equivalence \eqref{eqn_pd2}, we may assume $p=1$ and $A=I$ by multiplying $pA+qvv^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}$ both on the left and right by $(pA)^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ and replacing $v$ by $(pA)^{-\frac{1}{2}}v$. If $v=0$, the equivalence is trivial; otherwise, for $I+q vv^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}$ to be positive definite, it is sufficient that $x^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}\big(I+qvv^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}\big)x>0$ for $x=v$ and all $x\in v^\perp\setminus\{0\}$. But we have
$$
x^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}\big(I+qvv^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}\big)x=
\begin{cases}
	|v|^2+q|v|^4=|v|^2\det(I+qvv^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}})&\text{ if }x=v\\
	px^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}Ax>0 & \text{ if }x\in v^\perp\setminus\{0\}
\end{cases}
$$
by \eqref{eqn_det2}. The required equivalence follows.

\label{lemma_barrier}
	Given $\alpha>2d-n$ and $M,R>0$, there exists $\Lambda>0$ such that the function

		f:\mathbb{R}^n=&\ \mathbb{R}^d\times\mathbb{R}^{n-d}\to \mathbb{R},\\[-0.2cm]
		&f(y,z):=(|y|^2+\Lambda)|z|^\beta,\quad \beta:=\frac{\alpha+2n-2d}{n}>1
	\end{align*}
	satisfies 
	\begin{equation}\label{eqn_lemmabarrier1}
	\mathsf{D}^2 f(y,z)>0\ \text{ and }\ \det\mathsf{D}^2 f(y,z)\geq M|z|^\alpha \quad \text{if }  |y|<R\text{ and }z\neq 0.

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
We will omit the variables $(y,z)$ in expressions like $f(y,z)$ and $\mathsf{D}^2f(y,z)$. The Hessian $\mathsf{D}^2f$ can be written in the block form
	\begin{equation}\label{eqn_fhessianblocks}
		\mathsf{D}^2 f=\begin{pmatrix}
			\partial^2_{yy}f&\partial^2_{yz}f\\[1pt]
			\partial^2_{zy}f&\partial^2_{zz}f
		\end{pmatrix},
	\end{equation}
where $\partial_{yy}^2f$ denotes the matrix $\big(\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial y_i\partial y_j}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq d}$, and similarly for the other blocks. Computing the second derivatives, we obtain 
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_proofbarrier0}
	\begin{split}
	&\partial^2_{yy}f=2|z|^\beta I_d,\qquad \partial^2_{zz}f=\beta(|y|^2+\Lambda)|z|^{\beta-2}\left(I_{n-d}+\frac{\beta-2}{|z|^2}zz^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}\right),\\
	&\partial_{zy}^2f=2\beta|z|^{\beta-2} zy^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}},\qquad \partial_{yz}^2f=2\beta|z|^{\beta-2} y z^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}},
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $I_m$ denotes the $m\times m$ identity matrix. To apply Lemma \ref{lemma_LA}, note that $\partial^2_{yy}f>0$ for $z\neq0$, whereas
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_proofbarrier1}
	\begin{split}
	\partial^2_{zz}f-\partial^2_{zy}f(\partial_{yy}^2f)^{-1}\partial^2_{yz}f&=\beta|z|^{\beta-2}(|y|^2+\Lambda)\left(I_{n-d}+\frac{\beta-2}{|z|^2}zz^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}\right)-\frac{(2\beta|z|^{\beta-2})^2}{2|z|^\beta}|y|^2zz^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}\\
	&=\beta|z|^{\beta-2}\left[(|y|^2+\Lambda)I_{n-d}+\frac{(\beta-2)\Lambda-(\beta+2)|y|^2}{|z|^2}zz^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}\right]
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
has the form $pA+qvv^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}$ with $p,A>0$. Therefore, by \eqref{eqn_pd1} and \eqref{eqn_pd2}, the first inequality in \eqref{eqn_lemmabarrier1} follows from the second, hence it suffices to establish the latter. This amounts to a straightforward calculation: applying \eqref{eqn_det1} first, then \eqref{eqn_proofbarrier0} and \eqref{eqn_proofbarrier1}, and finally \eqref{eqn_det2}, we obtain
	\begin{align*}
		\det \mathsf{D}^2 f&=\det \partial^2_{yy}f\, \det\left(\partial^2_{zz}f-\partial^2_{zy}f(\partial_{yy}^2f)^{-1}\partial^2_{yz}f\right)\\
		&=(2|z|^\beta)^d (\beta|z|^{\beta-2})^{n-d}\det\left[(|y|^2+\Lambda)I_{n-d}+\frac{(\beta-2)\Lambda-(\beta+2)|y|^2}{|z|^2}zz^{\raisebox{\depth}{$\mskip-1mu\scriptstyle\intercal$}}\right]\\
		&=(2|z|^\beta)^d (\beta|z|^{\beta-2})^{n-d}(|y|^2+\Lambda)^{n-d-1}\left(|y|^2+\Lambda+(\beta-2)\Lambda-(\beta+2)|y|^2\right)\\
                                                                                                        
&=2^d\beta^{n-d}\left(\frac{\Lambda-|y|^2}{\Lambda+|y|^2}\beta-1\right)|z|^{n\beta-2(n-d)}(|y|^2+\Lambda)^{n-d}.
	\end{align*}
	Since $n\beta-2(n-d)=\alpha$ and $\beta>1$, we conclude that $\det \mathsf{D}^2 f\geq M|z|^\alpha$ for $|y|<R$ and $z\neq0$ if $\Lambda=\Lambda(n,d,\beta,M,R)$ is sufficiently large, as required.

Let $v$ be the function given by Lemma \ref{lemma_barrier3}, where we take the same $M$ as in \eqref{eqn_proofexistence0.1} and take $R$ large enough such that $\Omega\subset\Gamma\cap B_R$. $$

$$
\det\mathsf{D}^2u_k\leq \det\mathsf{D}^2u_{k+1}\leq \det\mathsf{D}^2v\ \ \text{ in }\Omega,
$$
whereas $u_k|_{\partial\Omega}=u_{k+1}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\geq v|_{\partial\Omega}$, so the comparison principle (Lemma \ref{lemma_comparison}) gives
$$
u_k\geq u_{k+1}\geq v \ \text{ in }\Omega.
$$
Therefore, the pointwise limit function
$$
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R},\quad u(x):=\lim_{k\to\infty}u_k(x)
$$
is well defined. By Lemma \ref{lemma_uniform}, $u$ is also convex and the convergence $u_k\to u$ is uniform on compact subsets of $\Omega$. Then it follows from the stability of Monge-Amp\`ere measures (Lemma \ref{lemma_stability}) that 
$$
\det\mathsf{D}^2u=\lim_{k\to\infty}\det\mathsf{D}^2u_k=g.

To show that $u$ provides a solution to \eqref{eqn_proofexistence0}, it only remains to check that
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_proofexistence1}
\liminf_{x\in\Omega,\ x\to x_0}u(x)=0
\end{equation}
for all $x_0\in\partial\Omega$, which is a necessary and sufficient condition for $u$ to be extendable to a continuous convex function on $\overline{\Omega}$ with $u|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ (see Remark \ref{rk_extension}). For $x_0\in F=\partial\Omega\cap L$, \eqref{eqn_proofexistence1} holds because

$$
At $x_0\in \partial\Omega\setminus L$, although $v$ might not vanish, we can still deduce \eqref{eqn_proofexistence1} from the Alexandrov maximum principle. We now present a unified proof of \eqref{eqn_proofexistence1} for all $x_0\in\partial\Omega$.

Given $\epsilon>0$, consider the level sets
$$

$$
Note that $u_k\geq v$ implies $E_{k,\epsilon}\subset E_\epsilon$.
The key to the proof is the fact that the mass of $E_{k,\epsilon}$ under the Monge-Amp\`ere measure of $u_k$ has an upper bound independent of $k$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn_proofexistence2}
\mathcal{M}u_k(E_{k,\epsilon})\leq \mathcal{M}u_k(E_{\epsilon})\leq\mathcal{M}v(E_\epsilon)<+\infty,
\end{equation}
where the first ``$\leq$'' follows from $E_{k,\epsilon}\subset E_\epsilon$, the second from $\det\mathsf{D}^2 u_k\leq \det \mathsf{D}^2v$, and the last ``$<$'' holds  because $E_\epsilon$ is bounded away from the singular locus $L$ of $\det\mathsf{D}^2v$.

Since $u_k|_{\partial\Omega}=0$, $E_{k,\epsilon}$ is a compact convex set and we have $u_k|_{\partial E_{k,\epsilon}}=-\epsilon$. Therefore, we can apply the Alexandrov maximum principle (Lemma \ref{lemma_AlexandrovMP}) to the function $u_k+\epsilon$ on $E_{k,\epsilon}$. In view of \eqref{eqn_proofexistence2}, this gives
$$
u_k(x)\geq-\epsilon-C\, {\mathrm{dist}}(x,\partial E_{k,\epsilon})^\frac{1}{n}\ \ \text{ for all }x\in E_{k,\epsilon},
$$
where the constant $C=C(n,{\mathrm{diam}}(\Omega),\mathcal{M}v(E_\epsilon))>0$  depends on $\epsilon$ but not on $k$. Since ${\mathrm{dist}}(x,\partial E_{k,\epsilon})< {\mathrm{dist}}(x,\partial\Omega)$ for $x\in E_{k,\epsilon}$ and $u_k(x)>-\epsilon$ for $x\in \Omega\setminus E_{k,\epsilon}$, it follows that
$$
u_k(x)\geq -\epsilon-C\,{\mathrm{dist}}(x,\partial\Omega)^\frac{1}{n}\ \ \text{ for all }x\in\Omega.
$$
The same holds for $u(x)$ by letting $k\to\infty$. Therefore, for any $x_0\in\partial\Omega$, we have
$$
0\geq \liminf_{x\in\Omega,\,x\to x_0}u(x)\geq -\epsilon -C\liminf_{x\in\Omega,\,x\to x_0}{\mathrm{dist}}(x,\partial\Omega)^\frac{1}{n}=-\epsilon.
$$
Since $\epsilon>0$ is arbitrary, this implies \eqref{eqn_proofexistence1} and completes the proof.
