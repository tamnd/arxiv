<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
Let $\Lambda$ be an even lattice of discriminant $2p^2$ in a positive definite, ternary quadratic space $(V,Q)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then for each $k\geq 0$ and $d\in\{1,p\}$ there exists a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism (for the algebra generated by the Hecke operators $T_q$ with $q\neq p$ prime): \[\mathcal{S}(O(V), O(\widehat{\Lambda}),W_{2k}\otimes \theta_d) \cong S_{2k+2}^{\textup{new}}\left(\Gamma_0(p),\varepsilon^{(p)}_p\varepsilon^{(p)}_d\right).\]
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{OtoSO}
For each lattice $\Lambda\subset V$ there is a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism: \[\mathcal{M}(O(V), O(\widehat{\Lambda}),W_{2k}\otimes \theta_d) \cong M(SO(V),SO(\widehat{\Lambda}), W_{2k}\otimes \theta_d)\]
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The restriction map from $O(\widehat{V})$ to $SO(\widehat{V})$ induces a Hecke-equivariant injective homomorphism \[\mathcal{M}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Lambda}),W) \longrightarrow M(SO(V),SO(\widehat{\Lambda}),W)\] for any representation $W$ of $O(V)$. The image consists of functions \[\varphi\in M(SO(V),SO(\widehat{\Lambda}),W)\] such that $\varphi(g) = \varphi((-I)\cdot g) = (-I)\cdot \varphi(g)$ for every $g\in SO(\widehat{V})$. When $-I$ acts trivially on $W$ this condition is automatically satisfied and so we get a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism. All of the representations constructed above have this property, establishing the result.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm: 6.12 in HTV}
Suppose that $\Lambda\subset V$ is an even lattice in a positive definite ternary quadratic space $(V,Q)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Suppose that the associated quaternion algebra $B = \textup{Cl}^0(V)$ has discriminant $D$ and that the order $\mathcal{O} = \textup{Cl}^0(\Lambda)$ is residually unramified everywhere of discriminant $N = \textup{discrd}(\mathcal{O}) = \frac{1}{2}\textup{disc}(\Lambda)$. Let $N^+$ and $N^-$ be the products of the prime powers $p^e||N$ that satisfy $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}) = 1$ and $\varepsilon_p(\mathcal{O}) = -1$ respectively. 

Then for each $k \geq 0$ and each square-free $d\mid N$ we have a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism (for all Hecke operators $T_p$ with $p \nmid N$):
$$\mathcal{S}(O(V), O(\widehat{\Lambda}), W_{2k}\otimes \theta_d) \cong 
\bigoplus_{M|N^-} S_{2k+2}^{M\textup{-new}}(\Gamma_0(MN^{+}), \varepsilon_D^{(MN^{+})}\varepsilon_d^{(MN^{+})})$$
where the sum runs over $M|N^-$ with $v_p(M) \equiv v_p(N) \bmod 2$ for all primes $p \,|\, N^-$.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{samespace}
Suppose $\Lambda,\Pi\subset V$ are lattices such that $O(\widehat{\Lambda}) = O(\widehat{\Pi})$. Then for each representation $W$ of $O(V)$ there is an equality of Hecke modules: \[\mathcal{M}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Lambda}),W) = \mathcal{M}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Pi}),W).\]
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $O(\widehat{\Lambda}) = O(\widehat{\Pi})$ is follows that $\mathcal{M}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Lambda}),W) = \mathcal{M}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Pi}),W)$ as spaces. We also have that: \[O(\widehat{\Lambda})\,g\, O(\widehat{\Lambda}) = O(\widehat{\Pi})\,g\, O(\widehat{\Pi})\] for any $g\in O(\widehat{V})$. This implies that we get the same Hecke representatives and hence the same Hecke operators on both sides.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
\label{scaling}
Let $\Lambda\subset V$ be a lattice in a quadratic space $(V,Q)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. The Hecke module $\mathcal{M}(O(V),O(\widehat{\Lambda}),W)$ is invariant under non-zero scaling of the underlying quadratic map $Q$.
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
This is also clear. For any $\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}$ the scaled quadratic space $(V,Q^{(\alpha)})$ has the same orthogonal group as $(V,Q)$. The same is true for $(\widehat{V},Q^{(\alpha)})$ and $(\widehat{V},Q)$. Consequently, the subgroup $O(\widehat{\Lambda})$ is invariant under scaling of the underlying quadratic map, which by Lemma \ref{samespace} implies an equality of spaces of orthogonal modular forms and Hecke operators.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{p-mod}
Let $p$ be an odd prime. Suppose that $(V_p,Q)$ is a ternary quadratic space over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ containing a $p$-adic lattice $\Lambda_p\subset V_p$ with Gram matrix $\langle a,p,pb\rangle$ for some $a,b\in\{1,r\}$. Then the $p$-adic sublattice $\Pi_p\subset V_p$ of index $p$ with Gram matrix $\langle p^2a,p,pb\rangle$ satisfies $O(\Lambda_p) = O(\Pi_p)$.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Note that since $\Lambda_p$ is an elementary lattice (i.e.\ $\Lambda_p^*/\Lambda_p$ is an elementary $p$-group), Theorem $1.1(1)$ of Allcock \cite{allcock2025} tells us that $O(\Lambda_p)$ is maximal in $O(V_p)$ among the orthogonal groups of all $p$-adic lattices. Hence, if we can show that $O(\Lambda_p)\subseteq O(\Pi_p)$ then we will be done.

By standard arguments, there are exactly $p^2+p+1$ sublattices of $\Lambda_p$ of index $p$ up to change of basis. If $\Lambda_p$ has basis $\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}$ with respect to the above Gram matrix then these sublattices have bases \[\{pv_1,v_2,v_3\} \qquad \{v_1+\alpha v_2, pv_2, v_3\} \qquad \{v_1+\alpha v_3, v_2 + \beta v_3, pv_3\},\] with $\alpha,\beta\in \{0,1,...,p-1\}$, and corresponding Gram matrices: \[\begin{pmatrix}
p^2a & 0 & 0\\ 0 & p & 0\\ 0 & 0 & pb
\end{pmatrix}\qquad \begin{pmatrix}
a+p\alpha^2 & p^2\alpha & 0\\ p^2\alpha & p^3 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & pb
\end{pmatrix} \qquad \begin{pmatrix}
a+pb\alpha^2 & pb\alpha\beta & p^2b
\alpha\\ pb\alpha\beta & p(1+b\beta^2) & p^2b\beta\\ p^2b\alpha & p^2b\beta & p^3b
\end{pmatrix}.\] Note that the first Gram matrix corresponds to the lattice $\Pi_p$, and is visibly the only one having norm ideal $p\mathbb{Z}_p$. Now if $\gamma\in O(\Lambda_p)$ then $\gamma\Lambda_p = \Lambda_p$ and $\gamma\Pi_p$ is a sublattice of $\Lambda_p$ of index $p$. However, isometries preserve norm ideals and so we must have that $\gamma\Pi_p = \Pi_p$, i.e.\ $\gamma\in O(\Pi_p)$. This gives $O(\Lambda_p)\subseteq O(\Pi_p)$, as required.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{2-unimod}
Let $a,b,c\in\{\pm 1, \pm 5\}$ and let $\Delta = ab+ac+bc$. Suppose that $(V_2,Q)$ is a ternary quadratic space over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ containing a $2$-adic lattice $\Lambda_2\subset V_2$ with Gram matrix $\langle a,b,c\rangle$. Then the $2$-adic sublattice $\Pi_2\subset V_2$ of index $2$ with Gram matrix: \[\begin{pmatrix}
a+c & c & 2c\\ c & b+c & 2c\\ 2c & 2c & 4c    
\end{pmatrix}\sim\begin{cases}\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 1\\ 1 & 2
\end{pmatrix}, 12abc\right\rangle & \text{ if } \Delta \equiv 3 \bmod 8\\ \left\langle\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 1\\ 1 & 0
\end{pmatrix},-4abc\right\rangle & \text{ if } \Delta \equiv 7 \bmod 8\end{cases}\] satisfies $O(\Lambda_2) = O(\Pi_2)$.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Theorem $1.1(2)$ of Allcock \cite{allcock2025} shows that $O(\Lambda_2)$ is maximal in $O(V_2)$ among the orthogonal groups of all $2$-adic lattices. Hence it again suffices to show that $O(\Lambda_2) \subseteq O(\Pi_2)$.

Fix a basis $\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}$ for $\Lambda_2$ realising the Gram matrix above. The $2^2+2+1 = 7$ sublattices of $\Lambda_2$ of index $2$ then have bases: \[\{2v_1,v_2,v_3\} \qquad \{v_1+\alpha v_2,2v_2,v_3\}\qquad \{v_1+\alpha v_3, v_2 + \beta v_3, 2v_3\}\] with $\alpha,\beta\in\{0,1\}$ and corresponding Gram matrices:

\[\begin{pmatrix}
4a & 0 & 0\\ 0 & b & 0\\ 0 & 0 & c
\end{pmatrix}\qquad \begin{pmatrix}
a+b\alpha^2 & 2b\alpha & 0\\ 2b\alpha & 4b & 0\\ 0 & 0 & c
\end{pmatrix} \qquad \begin{pmatrix}
a+c\alpha^2 & c\alpha\beta & 2c
\alpha\\ c\alpha\beta & b+c\beta^2 & 2c\beta\\ 2c\alpha & 2c\beta & 4c
\end{pmatrix}.\]

Note that $\Pi_2$ is the sublattice with Gram matrix corresponding to the choice $\alpha=\beta = 1$. It is also the only sublattice having norm ideal $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (the rest give norm ideal $\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_2$). A similar argument to before shows that $O(\Lambda_2)\subseteq O(\Pi_2)$, as required.

It remains to show the equivalence of Gram matrices.  First we notice that: \[\begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 1 & 0\\ -\frac{2bc}{\Delta} & -\frac{2ac}{\Delta} & 1\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}
a+c & c & 2c
\\ c & b+c & 2c\\ 2c & 2c & 4c
\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 & -\frac{2bc}{\Delta}\\ 0 & 1 & -\frac{2ac}{\Delta}\\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}a+c & c & 0\\ c & b+c & 0\\ 0 & 0 & \frac{4abc}{\Delta}\end{pmatrix}\] (noting that $\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}_2^{\times}$ since $a,b,c\in\{1,3,5,7\}$). The rest then follows by realising that the top left $2\times 2$ block is unimodular of type II with determinant $\Delta$, so must be equivalent to $\begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ when $\Delta \equiv 7 \bmod 8$ or $\begin{pmatrix}2 & 1\\ 1 & 2\end{pmatrix}$ when $\Delta \equiv 3 \bmod 8$ (Theorem $8.9$ of \cite{gerstein2008basic}).
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- prop-3: body_md -->

\begin{enumerate}[label = (\roman*)]

By the recipe outlined in Subsection \ref{Ternary Lattices and Quaternion orders} we find that the even Clifford algebra of $(V,Q^{(\frac{2}{p})})$ is a definite quaternion algebra $B = \text{Cl}^0(V)$ of discriminant $p$. The result then follows from the results of Hein-Tornaria-Voight (see Theorem \ref{thm: 6.12 in HTV}).
