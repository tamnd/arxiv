<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $A$ be a separable unital AT algebra of real rank zero.  Then
the complexity rank of $A$ is at most one.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
Every irrational rotation algebra $A_\theta$ has complexity rank one.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A unital $C^*$-algebra has \emph{real rank zero} if its
self-adjoint elements with finite spectrum are norm dense in its self-adjoint part.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Let $A$ be a $C^*$-algebra. Denote by $A_{sa}$ its self-adjoint part.
For $a,b\in A$ and $\varepsilon>0$, write $a\approx_\varepsilon b$
if $\|a-b\|<\varepsilon$. For a subset $S\subseteq A$, write
$a\in_\varepsilon S$ if $a\approx_\varepsilon s$ for some $s\in S$.
We use $[a,b]$ to denote $ab-ba$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a class of $C^*$-algebras. A $C^*$-algebra $A$ is locally in $\mathcal{C}$ if, for any finite subset $X$ of $A$ and any $\varepsilon>0$, there is a $C^*$-subalgebra $C$ of $A$ that belongs to $\mathcal{C}$ and satisfies $x \in_\varepsilon C$ for all $x \in X$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:complexity}
Let $A$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra, and let $\mathcal{C}$ be a class of unital $C^*$-algebras. Then $A$ decomposes over $\mathcal{C}$ if, for every finite subset $X$ of the unit ball of $A$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there exist $C^*$-subalgebras $C, D$, and $E$ of $A$ that are in the class $\mathcal{C}$ and contain $1_A$, and a positive contraction $h \in E$ such that

(i) $\|[h, x]\|<\varepsilon$ for all $x \in X$;

(ii) $h x \in_\varepsilon C,\left(1_A-h\right) x \in_\varepsilon D$, and $h\left(1_A-h\right) x \in_\varepsilon E$ for all $x \in X$;

(iii) $e\in_\varepsilon C$ and $e\in_\varepsilon D$ for every $e$ in the unit ball of $E$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
Let $\alpha$ be an ordinal number.

(i) If $\alpha=0$, let $\mathcal{D}_0$ be the class of unital $C^*$-algebras that are locally finite-dimensional.

(ii) If $\alpha>0$, let $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$ be the class of unital $C^*$-algebras that decompose over $C^*$-algebras in $\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha} \mathcal{D}_\beta$.

A unital $C^*$-algebra has \emph{finite complexity} if it is in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$ for some $\alpha$, in which case its \emph{complexity rank} is the smallest possible $\alpha$. We say a unital $C^*$-algebra has complexity rank zero if and only if it is locally finite-dimensional.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The class of $C^*$-algebras $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$ is closed under taking finite direct sums, quotients and inductive limits with unital connecting maps. Any unital $C^*$-algebra that is locally in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$
is in $\mathcal{D}_\alpha$; see  \cite[Lemmas 2.5--2.7]{JW}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:criterion}
A unital $C^*$-algebra $A$ has complexity rank at most one if and only if the
following condition holds.  For every finite subset $X$ of the unit ball of
$A$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there are finite-dimensional unital
$C^*$-subalgebras $C,D,E\subseteq A$ and a positive contraction $h\in E$
such that $E\subseteq C\cap D$ and, for every $x\in X$,
$$
 \|[h,x]\|<\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 hx\in_\varepsilon C,
 \qquad
 (1-h)x\in_\varepsilon D,
 \qquad
 h(1-h)x\in_\varepsilon E.
$$
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{def:AT-RR0}
A \emph{circle block} is an algebra $M_s(C(\mathbb T))$.  A \emph{circle algebra}
is a finite direct sum of circle blocks and finite-dimensional full matrix
algebras.  A separable unital $C^*$-algebra $A$ is an \emph{AT algebra} if $A$ can be written as an inductive limit of circle algebras
$$
 A=\varinjlim(A_n,\phi_{n,m}),
$$
where every connecting homomorphism $\phi_{n,m}$ is
unital.
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def:partial-map}
Let
$$
 B=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}B_i,
 \qquad
 C=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{q}C_j,
$$
where $B_i$ and $C_j$ are unital $C^*$-algebras, and let $\phi:B\to C$ be a homomorphism.  If $\iota_i:B_i\to B$ is the
canonical inclusion and $\pi_j:C\to C_j$ is the canonical projection, then
the $(i,j)$-\emph{partial homomorphism} of $\phi$ is
$$
 \phi^{i,j}=\pi_j\circ\phi\circ\iota_i:B_i\longrightarrow C_j.
$$
                                                                              
                                 
Set $p_{ij}=\phi^{i,j}(1_{B_i})$.  Thus
$\phi^{i,j}$ is unital as a map into $p_{ij}C_jp_{ij}$.  For each fixed
$j$, the projections $p_{1j},\ldots,p_{lj}$ are mutually orthogonal; if
$\phi$ is unital, then their sum is $1_{C_j}$.

<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{def:spectrum-spv}
Equip $\mathbb T$ with the chordal metric $d_{\mathbb T}(z,w)=|z-w|$. Let $P\in M_{k_1}(C(\mathbb T))$ be a projection of
constant rank $k\geq1$, and let
$$
 \psi:C(\mathbb T)\longrightarrow PM_{k_1}(C(\mathbb T))P
$$
be a unital homomorphism. Put $P_z=P(z)$. For each $z\in\mathbb T$,
there are points $x_1(z),\ldots,x_k(z)\in\mathbb T$ and a unitary
$U_z\in M_{k_1}(\mathbb C)$ such that
$$
\psi(f)(z)=P_zU_z
\left(\begin{array}{cccc}
                   f(x_1(z)) &  &  &   \\
                    & \ddots &  &    \\
                    &  &  f(x_k(z))&  \\
                    &  &  & \begin{array}{ccc}
                              0 &  &  \\
                               & \ddots &  \\
                               &  & 0
                            \end{array}
                    \\
                 \end{array}
                 \right)
U_z^*P_z.
$$

Write
$$
\operatorname{SP}\psi_z
 =\{x_1(z),\ldots,x_k(z)\}\,\,\text{(counting multiplicity)}.$$
  We call $\operatorname{SP}\psi_z$ the \emph{spectrum} of $\psi$
at the point $z$.
The \emph{spectrum of the homomorphism} is
$$
 \operatorname{SP}(\psi)
 =\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb T}\operatorname{SP}\psi_z\subseteq\mathbb T,
$$
where multiplicities are omitted in the union.

Following \cite[Definition~1.4.2]{EG}, the
\emph{spectral variation} of $\psi$ is
$$
 \operatorname{SPV}(\psi)
 =
 \sup_{z,z'\in \mathbb T}
 \min_{\sigma\in S_k}
 \max_{1\leq i\leq k}
 d_{\mathbb T}\bigl(x_i(z),x_{\sigma(i)}(z')\bigr),
$$
where $S_k$ denotes the permutation group on $\{1,\ldots,k\}$, and
$\{x_i(z)\}_{i=1}^k$ and $\{x_i(z')\}_{i=1}^k$ are the spectra of
$\psi$ at $z$ and $z'$, respectively.

We write $d_k$ for the matching metric on unordered multisets
of $k$ points in $\mathbb T$, counted with multiplicity:
$$
 d_k\bigl(\{x_1,\ldots,x_k\},\{y_1,\ldots,y_k\}\bigr)
 :=
 \min_{\sigma\in S_k}\max_{1\leq i\leq k}
 |x_i-y_{\sigma(i)}|.
$$
This distance is independent of the enumerations of the multisets. The map
$z\mapsto\operatorname{SP}\psi_z$ is continuous for this metric.
The points in a fiber are unordered, and the minimizing permutation
may depend on the pair of fibers. In particular, the definition does
not require a continuous labeling of the individual eigenvalues
around the circle. This distinction is useful for the cyclic models
in Section~3, whose eigenvalues can be permuted after one circuit.

For a unital homomorphism
$$
 \psi:M_l(C(\mathbb T))\longrightarrow PM_{k_1}(C(\mathbb T))P,
$$
let
$$
 \psi_1:C(\mathbb T)\longrightarrow
 \psi(e_{11}\otimes1_{\mathbb T})M_{k_1}(C(\mathbb T))\psi(e_{11}\otimes1_{\mathbb T}),
 \qquad
 \psi_1(f)=\psi(e_{11}\otimes f).
$$
As in \cite[Section~1.4.3]{EG}, define
$$
 \operatorname{SP}\psi_z
 :=\operatorname{SP}(\psi_1)_z,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{SP}(\psi):=\operatorname{SP}(\psi_1),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{SPV}(\psi)
 :=\operatorname{SPV}(\psi_1).
$$
Thus $\operatorname{SP}\psi_z$ contains $\operatorname{rank}(\psi_1(1))$ points, counted with
multiplicity.

We call $\#(\operatorname{SP}\psi_z)$ the \emph{multiplicity} of $\psi$. Then $$
\#(\operatorname{SP}\psi_z)=
\#(\operatorname{SP}(\psi_1)_z)=\operatorname{rank}(\psi_1(1)).$$

If $B$ and $C$ are circle algebras and $\phi:B\to C$ is a homomorphism,
then $\operatorname{SPV}(\phi)$ is the maximum of
$\operatorname{SPV}(\phi^{i,j})$ over all nonzero circle-to-circle partial
homomorphisms.  A partial homomorphism involving a finite-dimensional block
is assigned spectral variation zero; if there is no nonzero circle-to-circle partial
homomorphism, the maximum is defined to be zero.
<!-- end definition-8 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:small-variation}
Let $A$ be a unital AT algebra with presentation
$$
 A=\varinjlim(A_n,\phi_{n,m}).
$$
Then $A$ has real rank zero if and only if, for every $n$ and
$\delta>0$, there is $m>n$ such that
$$
 \operatorname{SPV}(\phi_{n,m})<\delta.
$$
In this case, $\operatorname{SPV}(\phi_{n,r})\leq\operatorname{SPV}(\phi_{n,m}) <\delta$ for every $r\geq m$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
\label{def:graded-invariant}
For a unital $C^*$-algebra $B$, put $K_*(B)=K_0(B)\oplus K_1(B)$ and
$$
 K_*(B)^+
 =\left\{\bigl([p],[u+(1_r-p)]\bigr):
 \begin{array}{l}
 p\in M_r(B)\text{ is a projection},\ r\geq1,\\
 u\in U(pM_r(B)p)
 \end{array}\right\}.
$$
The \emph{scaled ordered $K_*$-group} of $B$ is
$$
 \bigl(K_*(B),K_*(B)^+,([1_B],0)\bigr),
$$
with its $\mathbb Z/2$-grading.  For a circle block, in the
standard $K$-theory coordinates,
$$
 K_*(M_s(C(\mathbb T)))^+
 =\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:a>0\}\cup\{(0,0)\},
 \qquad ([1],0)=(s,0).
$$
For $M_s$, the invariant is $(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z_{\geq0},s)$, concentrated in
degree zero.  Finite direct sums have the coordinatewise graded order.
<!-- end definition-9 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:AT-classification}
Two separable unital AT algebras of real rank zero are isomorphic if and
only if their scaled ordered $K_*$-groups are isomorphic, with the grading
and order unit preserved.

Let $p\in M_t(C(\mathbb T))$ be a projection of constant rank $sN$, and let
$$
 \psi:M_s(C(\mathbb T))\longrightarrow pM_t(C(\mathbb T))p
$$
be a unital homomorphism.  Let
$\zeta\in C(\mathbb T)$ be the coordinate function with $\zeta(z)=z$.  The degree of
$\psi$ is
$$
 d_{\psi}
 =\operatorname{wind}\det\!\left(
 \psi(\operatorname{diag}(\zeta,1,\ldots,1))+(1_{M_t(C(\mathbb T))}-p)
 \right).
$$
Equivalently, the induced $K_1$-map is
$$
K_1(\psi)\,:\,  K_1(M_s(C(\mathbb T)))\cong\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\times d_{\psi}}K_1(M_t(C(\mathbb T)))\cong\mathbb{Z}.

For the zero homomorphism, we set $N=d_{\psi}=0$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:spectral-cover}
Let
$$
 \psi:M_s(C(\mathbb T))\longrightarrow M_{sN}(C(\mathbb T))
$$
be a unital homomorphism with nonzero degree.  Let $0<\delta<1$ and
suppose that $\operatorname{SPV}(\psi)<\delta$.  Then
$$
 N\geq\frac{\pi}{2\arcsin(\delta/2)}.
$$
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For each $z\in\mathbb T$, write
$$
 \operatorname{SP}\psi_z=\{x_1(z),\ldots,x_N(z)\}\,\,\text{(counting multiplicity)}.
$$
Let $U\in M_{sN}(C(\mathbb T))$ be defined by
$$
 U=\psi\left(\left(
 \begin{array}{ccc}
   \zeta&&\\
   &\ddots&\\
   &&\zeta
 \end{array}
 \right)\right).
$$
Since $\zeta(z)=z$, the eigenvalues of $U(z)$ are the points
$x_1(z),\ldots,x_N(z)$, each repeated $s$ times.

We prove that $\operatorname{SP}(\psi)=\mathbb T$.  First,
$$
 \operatorname{SP}(\psi)=\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb T}\operatorname{SP}\psi_z
          =\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb T}\sigma(U(z))=\sigma(U)\subseteq\mathbb T.
$$
Indeed, $U-\lambda1_{sN}$ is invertible if and only if every
$U(z)-\lambda1_{sN}$ is invertible: the pointwise inverses form a
continuous matrix-valued function.  Hence $\operatorname{SP}(\psi)$ is compact.

Suppose that $\lambda\in\mathbb T\setminus\sigma(U)$.  A continuous argument
on $\mathbb T\setminus\{\lambda\}$ and functional calculus give a self-adjoint
element $h\in M_{sN}(C(\mathbb T))$ such that $U=e^{ih}$.  The path
$t\mapsto e^{ith}$ joins $1_{sN}$ to $U$, so
$$
 [U]=0\quad\text{in }K_1(M_{sN}(C(\mathbb T)))\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
On the other hand,
$$
 [1_s\otimes\zeta]
 =s[\operatorname{diag}(\zeta,1,\ldots,1)]
 \quad\text{in }K_1(M_s(C(\mathbb T))),
$$
and therefore
$$
 [U]=K_1(\psi)([1_s\otimes\zeta])=s d_\psi\neq0.
$$
This is a contradiction.

Now fix a base point $z_0\in\mathbb T$.  Let $\lambda\in\mathbb T$.  There are
$z\in\mathbb T$ and $r\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$ such that $x_r(z)=\lambda$.
Since $\operatorname{SPV}(\psi)<\delta$, there is a permutation $\sigma\in S_N$ with
$$
 |x_k(z)-x_{\sigma(k)}(z_0)|<\delta
 \qquad(1\leq k\leq N).
$$
Taking $k=r$ gives
$$
 |\lambda-x_{\sigma(r)}(z_0)|<\delta.
$$
Thus $\{x_1(z_0),\ldots,x_N(z_0)\}$ is a chordal $\delta$-net in $\mathbb T$.

If $\mu=e^{i\beta}\in\mathbb T$, then
$$
 |e^{i\alpha}-\mu|<\delta
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \left|\sin\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\right|<\frac{\delta}{2}.
$$
Each chordal ball of radius $\delta$ intersects $\mathbb T$ in an arc of
angular length $4\arcsin(\delta/2)$.  The $N$ arcs cover $\mathbb T$, so
$$
 4N\arcsin(\delta/2)\geq2\pi.
$$

<!-- definition-11: body_md -->
\label{def:cyclic-map}
Let $N,s\geq1$ and $d\in\mathbb Z$.  Define the \emph{standard cyclic
homomorphism} $\Psi_{N,d}^{(s)}$ by
\begin{align*}
 \Psi_{N,d}^{(s)}:M_s\otimes C(\mathbb T)&\longrightarrow M_s\otimes M_N(C(\mathbb T)),\\
 a\otimes1_{\mathbb T}&\longmapsto a\otimes1_N,\\
 1_s\otimes\zeta&\longmapsto1_s\otimes S_{N,d}.
\end{align*}

 In particular, if
$P=\sum_{|\ell|\leq L}a_\ell\otimes\zeta^\ell$ with each $a_\ell\in M_s$, then
$$
 \Psi_{N,d}^{(s)}(P)
 =\sum_{|\ell|\leq L}a_\ell\otimes S_{N,d}^{\ell}.
$$
<!-- end definition-11 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:cyclic-K}
The map $\Psi_{N,d}^{(s)}$ induces multiplication by $N$ on $K_0$ and
multiplication by $d$ on $K_1$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The generator $[e_{11}\otimes1]$ of the source $K_0$-group is sent to
$[e_{11}\otimes1_N]$. This projection has rank $N$, so its class is
$N$ times the rank-one generator of the target $K_0$-group.
For $K_1$, apply the map to the standard generator
$u=\operatorname{diag}(\zeta,1,\ldots,1)$. Its image is
$$
 \Psi_{N,d}^{(s)}(u)
 =e_{11}\otimes S_{N,d}+(1_s-e_{11})\otimes1_N.
$$
Under the determinant description of
$K_1(M_{sN}(C(\mathbb T)))\cong\mathbb Z$, its class is the winding number of
$\det S_{N,d}(z)=(-1)^{N-1}z^d$, which is $d$.

Thus, the two integers have distinct roles: $N$ counts multiplicity,
whereas $d$ measures winding.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:straightening}
Let
$$
 \alpha:M_k(C(\mathbb T))\longrightarrow pM_t(C(\mathbb T))p
$$
be a unital homomorphism, and suppose that $\operatorname{rank}(p)=kR$. Then there is
a $C(\mathbb T)$-linear identification
$$
 pM_t(C(\mathbb T))p\cong M_k\otimes M_R(C(\mathbb T))
$$
under which
$$
 \alpha(a\otimes1_{\mathbb T})=a\otimes1_R,
 \qquad
 \alpha(1_k\otimes\zeta)=1_k\otimes V
$$
for every $a\in M_k$ and for some unitary $V\in M_R(C(\mathbb T))$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Put $f_{ab}=\alpha(e_{ab}\otimes1)$ and $q=f_{11}$.
The projections $f_{aa}$ are mutually equivalent, are orthogonal,
and sum to $p$. Consequently $q$ has constant rank $R$.
Choose a continuous orthonormal frame for its range, written as a
rectangular matrix $v\in M_{t,R}(C(\mathbb T))$ with
$v^*v=1_R$ and $vv^*=q$.

For completeness, such a frame can be constructed by pulling $q$
back to the interval $[0,1]$. A continuous frame exists on the
interval. Its endpoint frames differ by a unitary $u\in U(R)$.
Multiplying the frame by a path from $1_R$ to $u^*$ makes the
endpoint frames agree, so the corrected frame descends to $\mathbb T$.
Here we use that $U(R)$ is path connected.

For $1\leq a\leq k$, set $v_a=f_{a1}v$ and form the rectangular
matrix $W=(v_1\ \cdots\ v_k)$. The matrix-unit relations give
$$
 v_a^*v_b=\delta_{ab}1_R,\qquad
 W^*W=1_{kR},\qquad WW^*=p.
$$
Hence $b\mapsto W^*bW$ is a $C(\mathbb T)$-linear isomorphism from
$pM_t(C(\mathbb T))p$ onto $M_k\otimes M_R(C(\mathbb T))$, and
$$
 W^*f_{ab}W=e_{ab}\otimes1_R.
$$
The unitary $\alpha(1_k\otimes\zeta)$ commutes with every $f_{ab}$.
After this identification it belongs to the commutant of
$M_k\otimes1_R$, which is $1_k\otimes M_R(C(\mathbb T))$.
It therefore has the required form $1_k\otimes V$, with $V$ unitary.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:full-model}
Let $\phi:M_s(C(\mathbb T))\to M_{sN}(C(\mathbb T))$ be a unital homomorphism of
degree $d$. Then
$$
 KK(\phi)=KK(\Psi_{N,d}^{(s)}),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{SPV}(\Psi_{N,d}^{(s)})\leq2\sin\frac{\pi}{2N}.
$$
If $d=0$, the cyclic map has finite-dimensional range and spectral
variation zero.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Both maps induce multiplication by $N$ on $K_0$ and by $d$ on $K_1$,
by the definition of degree and Proposition~\ref{prop:cyclic-K}.
Since
$$
 K_0(M_s(C(\mathbb T)))\cong\mathbb Z,
 \qquad K_1(M_s(C(\mathbb T)))\cong\mathbb Z,
$$
the $\operatorname{Ext}^1$ terms in the universal coefficient theorem
vanish. Thus the $KK$-class is determined by the induced $K_0$- and
$K_1$-maps \cite{RS}, so $KK(\phi)=KK(\Psi_{N,d}^{(s)})$.

If $d=0$, then $S_{N,0}$ is a constant matrix and
$$
 \Psi_{N,0}^{(s)}(M_s(C(\mathbb T)))
 =M_s\otimes C^*(S_{N,0})\subseteq M_{sN}(\mathbb C).
$$
The range is finite-dimensional and the fiber spectra are constant.
Hence $\operatorname{SPV}(\Psi_{N,0}^{(s)})=0$.

Suppose that $d\neq0$. For $z\in\mathbb T$,
$$
 \det(\lambda1_N-S_{N,d}(z))=\lambda^N-z^d.
$$
There are $N$ distinct roots of $\lambda^N=z^d$, and these are exactly
the eigenvalues of $S_{N,d}(z)$, each with multiplicity one.
Thus Definition~\ref{def:spectrum-spv} gives
$$
 \operatorname{SP}(\Psi_{N,d}^{(s)})_z=\{\lambda\in\mathbb T:\lambda^N=z^d\},
$$
with each root counted once, since the spectrum is defined using the
$e_{11}$-corner.

Given $z,w\in\mathbb T$, choose $t\in[-\pi,\pi]$ such that
$$
 z^d=e^{it}w^d.
$$
Multiplication by $e^{it/N}$ matches the spectrum at $w$ with the
spectrum at $z$. Therefore,
$$
 d_N\bigl(\operatorname{SP}(\Psi_{N,d}^{(s)})_z,\operatorname{SP}(\Psi_{N,d}^{(s)})_w\bigr)
 \leq |e^{it/N}-1|
 =2\left|\sin\frac{t}{2N}\right|
 \leq2\sin\frac{\pi}{2N}.
$$
Taking the supremum over $z,w\in\mathbb T$ proves the estimate.

$\{x_{i,l}:l\geq1\}$ in its unit ball. Set
$$
 X_k=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}
       \{\psi_{i,k}(x_{i,l}):1\leq l\leq k\}\subseteq B_k.
$$
For each $1\leq i\leq k$, apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral-composition} to the map
$\psi_{i,k}:B_i\to B_k$ with $\varepsilon=2^{-k}$.
This gives a number $0<\rho_{i,k}<2^{-k}$ such that, for every
circle algebra $C$ and every unital homomorphism $\eta:B_k\to C$,
$$
 \operatorname{SPV}(\eta)<\rho_{i,k}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{SPV}(\eta\circ\psi_{i,k})<2^{-k}.
$$
Set
$$
 \rho_k=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\rho_{i,k}.
$$
Since the minimum is taken over finitely many positive numbers,
$0<\rho_k<2^{-k}$. Consequently the same tolerance works for
all the maps already chosen:
$$
 \operatorname{SPV}(\eta)<\rho_k
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{SPV}(\eta\circ\psi_{i,k})<2^{-k}
 \quad(1\leq i\leq k).
$$
Here $\rho_k$ depends only on the maps already chosen and the
required bound $2^{-k}$, so it can be fixed before choosing the
next stage and its connecting map.

Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:local-model} to $X_k$ with tolerances
$2^{-k}$ and $\rho_k$, and let $\delta_k>0$ be the resulting constant.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:small-variation}, there is $n_{k+1}>n_k$ such that
$$
 \operatorname{SPV}(\phi_{n_k,n_{k+1}})<\delta_k.
$$
Choose $\psi_{k,k+1}$ to be a model map for $\phi_{n_k,n_{k+1}}$.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:local-model},
$$
 KK(\psi_{k,k+1})=KK(\phi_{n_k,n_{k+1}}),
 \qquad \operatorname{SPV}(\psi_{k,k+1})<\rho_k.
$$
There are finite-dimensional unital subalgebras
$C_k,D_k,E_k\subseteq B_{k+1}$ and a positive contraction $h_k\in E_k$
with $E_k\subseteq C_k\cap D_k$ satisfying the four estimates of
Proposition~\ref{prop:local-model} on $X_k$ with tolerance $2^{-k}$.
This completes the induction. Set
$$
 B=\varinjlim(B_k,\psi_{k,l}).
$$
For each fixed $i$, the choice of $\rho_k$ gives
$$
 \operatorname{SPV}(\psi_{i,k+1})<2^{-k}\qquad(k\geq i).
$$
Hence $B$ has real rank zero by Theorem~\ref{thm:small-variation}.

We next identify the two limits. For each $k$, define unital
homomorphisms
$$
 \alpha_k=\mathrm{id}:A_{n_k}\longrightarrow B_k,
 \qquad
 \beta_k=\phi_{n_k,n_{k+1}}:B_k\longrightarrow A_{n_{k+1}},
$$
using $B_k=A_{n_k}$. Then
\begin{align*}
 KK(\beta_k\circ\alpha_k)&=KK(\phi_{n_k,n_{k+1}}),\\
 KK(\alpha_{k+1}\circ\beta_k)
 &=KK(\phi_{n_k,n_{k+1}})=KK(\psi_{k,k+1}).
\end{align*}
In particular, the following diagram commutes in $KK$:
$$
 \begin{CD}
 A_{n_k} @>{KK(\phi_{n_k,n_{k+1}})}>> A_{n_{k+1}}\\
 @V{KK(\alpha_k)}VV @VV{KK(\alpha_{k+1})}V\\
 B_k @>{KK(\psi_{k,k+1})}>> B_{k+1}.
 \end{CD}
$$
Thus $(A_{n_k},\phi_{n_k,n_l})$ and $(B_k,\psi_{k,l})$ are
$KK$-shape equivalent. Both limits have real rank zero, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:KK-shape} gives $A\cong B$.

It remains to verify the three-corner estimates in $B$.
Let $X$ be a finite subset of the unit ball of $B$, and fix
$\varepsilon>0$. Write $\iota_i:B_i\to B$ for the canonical maps.
Choose a stage $i$ and indices $l_x$ such that
$$
 y_x:=\iota_i(x_{i,l_x}),\qquad
 \|x-y_x\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\quad(x\in X).
$$
Here is the unit-ball approximation in detail, including the case
of noninjective connecting maps. Given a small $\tau>0$, approximate
each $x$ by $\iota_j(a_x)$ within $\tau$ at a common stage $j$.
Then $\|\iota_j(a_x)\|<1+\tau$. Since
$$
 \|\iota_j(a_x)\|=\lim_{r\to\infty}\|\psi_{j,r}(a_x)\|,
$$
there is a common later stage $r$ at which all these representatives
have norm less than $1+2\tau$. Put
$b_x=\psi_{j,r}(a_x)/(1+2\tau)$, so $\|b_x\|\leq1$ and
$\|x-\iota_r(b_x)\|<3\tau$.
Choose $x_{r,l_x}$ within $\tau$ of $b_x$.
Taking $\tau<\varepsilon/16$ gives the required approximation with
$i=r$. Thus, no injectivity assumption is used.

Choose $k\geq\max\{i,l_x:x\in X\}$ with $2^{-k}<\varepsilon/2$.
Then $\psi_{i,k}(x_{i,l_x})\in X_k$. Apply $\iota_{k+1}$ to
$C_k,D_k,E_k,h_k$, and denote their images by $C,D,E,h$.
The algebras remain finite-dimensional and unital, and
$$
 h\in E,\qquad E\subseteq C\cap D.
$$
Contractivity gives the four estimates in Proposition \ref{prop:criterion} for
$y_x$ with tolerance $2^{-k}$. Consequently,
\begin{align*}
 \|[h,x]\|&<2^{-k}+2\|x-y_x\|<\varepsilon,\\
 hx&\in_{\,2^{-k}+\varepsilon/2}C,\\
 (1-h)x&\in_{\,2^{-k}+\varepsilon/2}D,\\
 h(1-h)x&\in_{\,2^{-k}+\varepsilon/2}E.
\end{align*}
By
Proposition~\ref{prop:criterion}, $B$ has complexity rank at most one. Since $A$ is isomorphic to $B$, the same is true for $A$.
