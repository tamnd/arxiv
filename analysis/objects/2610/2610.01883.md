<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Let $(\phi_0, \mathscr{S}_0, Q_0, F_0)$ be a solution to the water wave problem~\eqref{steady Euler system} that is incompressible in that $\mu = 0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\rm(\alph*)]
	\item \textup{(Periodic waves)} Suppose that for some $k > 0$,  $\phi_0$ and $\mathscr{D}_0$ are $2\pi/k$-periodic in the $x$-direction. If the wave is nondegenerate as a solution of the incompressible problem, then there exists a real-analytic curve 
	\[
		{\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{per} = \left\{ (\phi^\mu,\mathscr{S}^\mu, Q^\mu, \mu) : |\mu| \ll 1  \right \}
	\]
	of solutions to the compressible problem~\eqref{steady Euler system} that are also $2\pi/k$-periodic in $x$ with fixed Froude number $F_0$ and such that $(\phi^0,\mathscr{S}^0,Q^0) = (\phi_0, \mathscr{S}_0, Q_0)$.
	\item \textup{(Solitary waves)}
	If instead $(\phi_0, \mathscr{S}_0,Q_0, F_0)$ is an incompressible solitary wave that is nondegenerate, then there exists a corresponding real-analytic curve
	\[
		{\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{sol} = \left\{ (\phi^\mu, \mathscr{S}^\mu, \mu) : |\mu| \ll 1 \right\}
	\]
	of solutions to the compressible problem~\eqref{steady Euler system} with fixed Froude number $F_0$ and Bernoulli constant $Q_0 = 1/2$ that are also solitary waves and such that $(\phi^0,\mathscr{S}^0) = (\phi_0, \mathscr{S}_0)$.

\end{enumerate}

\label{intro JRE theorem}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
See Theorems~\ref{periodic JRE theorem} and \ref{solitary JRE theorem} for rigorous versions of this result. The precise definition of nondegenerate is given in~\eqref{definition non-degenerate periodic} for periodic waves and~\eqref{definition non-degenerate solitary} for solitary waves. It holds generically in the sense that for the many families of incompressible waves constructed via analytic global bifurcation theory, all but a discrete set of solutions are nondegenerate. For example, the famous curve of Stokes waves begins at a laminar flow and terminates at an ``extreme wave'' with a $120^\circ$ corner at its crest. Applying a corollary of Theorem~\ref{intro JRE theorem} along this family furnishes lightly compressible but large-amplitude periodic waves that are likewise arbitrarily close to having the same corner singularity.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{intro JRE remark}
Formally, one expects to be able to write $\phi^\mu$ as the incompressible relative potential $\phi_0$ with an $O(\mu)$ correction, in an appropriate norm, to account for the compressible effects. More generally, this suggests a power series expansion
\[
	\phi^\mu = \phi_0 + \sum_{m \geq 1} \mu^m \phi_m ,
\]
where each term $\phi_m$ is found by solving the hierarchy of equations that results from inserting this ansatz into the system~\eqref{steady Euler system} and doing matched asymptotics. This very natural approach was introduced by Janzen~\cite{janzen1913beitrag} and Rayleigh~\cite{rayleigh1916flow}, and is used extensively in the applied literature. It is somewhat more complicated for free boundary problems as one must also make corrections to the domain. Nonetheless, our construction gives a \emph{rigorous} justification for the Janzen--Rayleigh expansion: the validity of the power series representation is an immediate consequence of the analytic implicit function theorem. The equations defining the first-order corrections to the potential and domain are given in~\eqref{periodic JRE u} and \eqref{periodic JRE w}, respectively, but this can in principle be carried out to arbitrary order in $\mu$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
While we only consider gravity waves, a straightforward adaptation of the argument applies to capillary-gravity waves. Similarly, one can construct lightly compressible three-dimensional waves of various types. The conformal mapping reformulation adopted in Section~\ref{local bifurcation section} will not work for this, but it is possible to instead use a naïve flattening transform as we do in Section~\ref{front section}. It would also be interesting to consider the case of compressible water waves with vorticity. Recently, Xie and Xin~\cite{xie2010global,xie2010existence} were able to construct large families of subsonic rotational flows through a rigid nozzle (absent gravity). We believe a version of their approach could be used in the free boundary setting as well by incorporating our techniques.

\label{intro global periodic theorem}
Let ${\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{per}$ be the real-analytic curve of lightly compressible periodic waves furnished by Theorem~\ref{intro JRE theorem}. There exists a curve ${\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$ of compressible periodic water waves admitting the $C^0$ parameterization
\[
	{\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per} = \left\{ (\phi(s),\mathscr{S}(s),Q(s),\mu(s) ) : s \in [0,\infty) \right\} 
\]
with ${\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{per} \subset {\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$. In the limit $s \to \infty$, one of the following alternatives must occur:
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\rm(A\arabic*)]
			\item \label{intro periodic unboundedness alternative} \textup{(Unboundedness)} Conformal degeneracy~\eqref{intro conformal degeneracy} or velocity degeneracy occurs~\eqref{intro velocity degeneracy}: 
			\[
				 N_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathscr{D}(s)) + N_{\mathrm{v}}(\phi(s), \mu(s)) \to \infty,
			\]
			where $\mathscr{D}(s)$ is the fluid domain determined by $\mathscr{S}(s)$.
			\item \label{intro transsonic limit alternative} \textup{(Sonic point)} At a point in $\overline{\mathscr{D}(s)}$, the flow approaches transsonic:
			\begin{equation}
			\label{intro transsonic limit}
		\lim_{s \to \infty} \inf_{\mathscr{D}(s)} \left(c(s)^2 - |\nabla \phi(s)|^2 \right) = 0,
			\end{equation}
			where $c(s)$ is the speed of sound at the parameter value $s$.
			\item \label{intro stagnation alternative} \textup{(Surface stagnation)} At a point on the free boundary $\mathscr{S}(s)$, the flow approaches stagnation:
			\[
				\lim_{s \to \infty} \inf_{\mathscr{S}(s)} |\nabla \phi(s)| = 0.
			\]
			\item \label{intro periodic loop alternative} \textup{(Closed loop)} There exists $T > 0$ such that 
			\[
				\left(\phi(s+T),\, \mathscr{S}(s+T),\, Q(s+T),\, \mu(s+T) \right)= \left(\phi(s),\, \mathscr{S}(s), \, Q(s),\, \mu(s) \right)
			\]
			for all $ s \in [0,\infty)$. If this occurs, then necessarily ${\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$ contains two distinct incompressible solutions.
		\end{enumerate}

There exists $\alpha^2 > 0$, a $C^{2+\beta}$  fluid domain $\mathscr{D}$, and relative velocity potential $\phi \in C^{2+\beta}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})$ solving the (non-dimensionalized) steady isentropic Euler equations~\eqref{steady Euler system} and for which the velocity field and domain are $2\pi/k$-periodic in $x$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $f$ be the unique holomorphic mapping on $\mathcal{D}$ with imaginary part $w$ and such that $f - \operatorname{id}$ is $2\pi$-periodic in $\xi$. By the definition of $\mathcal{O}$ in \eqref{definition periodic O delta}, we see that $f$ is injective on $\partial\mathcal{D}$. If~\ref{f is conformal case} holds, then $f$ is conformal on $\overline{\mathcal{D}}$; we claim the same is true in the case~\ref{connected component case} by a continuity argument. 

Indeed, suppose that there is a continuous curve $\{ (u(s), w(s), Q(s), \lambda(s), \mu(s)) : s \in [0,1] \} \subset \mathcal{O}$ with $w(0) =0$ and $w(s) = w$. Denote by $f(s)$ the corresponding holomorphic mapping obtained from $w(s)$. Let $\mathcal{D}^0 \colonequals (-\pi,\pi) \times (-kh,0)$ be a single period of the conformal domain and consider the contour $\mathcal{C}$ corresponding to its boundary given positive orientation. For each $s \in [0,1]$, we can slightly deform the lateral boundaries of $\mathcal{C}$ so that they avoid any zeros of $\partial_\zeta f(s)$, and each side is a $2\pi$ translation of the other in the $\xi$ direction; call the resulting contour $\mathcal{C}(s)$.  By the definition of $\mathcal{O}$, we know that $\partial_\zeta f(s)$ is nonvanishing on the upper boundary $\Gamma_1^0$ of $\mathcal{D}^0$. Since $w(s) + \eta + kh$ is harmonic in $\mathcal{D}$, vanishes on $\Gamma_0$, and is bounded below by $\delta > 0$ on $\Gamma_1$ by the definition of $\mathcal{O}$, the maximum principle gives $w(s)+\eta+kh > 0$ in $\mathcal{D}$. The Hopf lemma then implies $\partial_\eta (w(s) + \eta) > 0$ on $\Gamma_0$, and hence $|\partial_\zeta f(s)| > 0$ on the lower boundary $\Gamma_0^0$ of $\mathcal{D}^0$, meaning $\mathcal{C}(s)$ contains no zeros of $\partial_\zeta f(s)$. 
On the other hand, $\partial_\zeta f(s)$ is bounded, so clearly it has no poles. By periodicity and the Cauchy argument principle, we therefore have that
\[
	\frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{\Gamma_0^0} \frac{\partial_\zeta^2 f(s)}{\partial_\zeta f(s)} \, d\zeta - \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_1^0} \frac{\partial_\zeta^2 f(s)}{\partial_\zeta f(s)} \, d\zeta = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}(s)} \frac{\partial_\zeta^2 f(s)}{\partial_\zeta f(s)} \, d\zeta = \# \{ \zeta \in \mathcal{D}^0 : \partial_\zeta f(s)(\zeta) = 0 \}. 
\]
The quantity on the far right-hand side is the winding number. As it is  integer-valued, by continuity it is independent of $s$. Evaluating the far left-hand side at $f(0) = \operatorname{id}$, we infer that the winding number vanishes for all $s \in [0,1]$, meaning that $\partial_\zeta f(s)$ is nonvanishing for all such $s$.  

Therefore, in both cases~\ref{f is conformal case} and \ref{connected component case}, the mapping $f$ is conformal in $\mathcal{D}$.  As we have shown $f$ is injective on $\partial\mathcal{D}$, the Picard--Darboux theorem now implies that $f$ is globally injective on $\overline{\mathcal{D}}$. We may then define the physical domain $\mathscr{D}$ and its boundary components by~\eqref{correspondence conformal and physical domains}, and set $\phi \colonequals (u + \lambda \xi) \circ f^{-1}$.

In the isothermal case $\gamma=1$, we have that $\alpha^2 = 1/\mu$ and $B = Q$, since $H(1) = 0$. For the polytropic case $\gamma >1$, the value of $\mu$ instead determines $B$, and to find $\alpha$ we use the relation
\[
	Q = B - H(1) = \frac{1}{\gamma-1} \left( \frac{1}{\mu} - \alpha^2 \right),
\]
which gives
\[
	\alpha^2 = \frac{1}{\mu} - (\gamma-1) Q.
\]
Note that this formula holds also in the isothermal case. 

We can now recover the density by inverting the enthalpy in Bernoulli's law:
\[
	\rho =  H^{-1}\left(B-\frac{1}{2} |\nabla \phi|^2 - \frac{1}{F^2} y\right) = \left\{
		\begin{aligned}
			\left( \frac{\gamma-1}{\alpha^2} (B -\frac{1}{2} |\nabla \phi|^2 - \frac{1}{F^2} y ) \right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}} & \qquad \textrm{if } \gamma > 1 \\
			\exp{\left( \frac{1}{\alpha^2}(Q-\frac{1}{2} |\nabla \phi|^2 - \frac{1}{F^2} y ) \right)} & \qquad \textrm{if } \gamma = 1.
		\end{aligned}
		\right.
\]
The argument of $H^{-1}$ on the right-hand side above is positive by the definition of $\mathcal{O}$, and so this gives a positive density. The dynamic condition is therefore satisfied on $\mathscr{S}$, as $\rho =  1$ there thanks to the Bernoulli condition $\mathscr G(u,w,Q,\lambda;\mu) = 0$. To see that this satisfies the continuity of mass equation, we rewrite $\mathscr F_1(u,w,Q,\lambda;\mu) = 0$ in the physical variables. If $\gamma = 1$, this yields
\[
	\Delta \phi = \mu \nabla \phi \cdot \nabla \left( \frac{1}{2} |\nabla \phi|^2 + \frac{1}{F^2} y \right) = -\frac{1}{\alpha^2} \nabla \phi \cdot \nabla H(\rho) = -\frac{1}{\rho} \nabla \phi \cdot \nabla \rho.
\]
The polytropic case is similar:
\begin{align*}
	\Delta \phi & = \frac{1}{(\gamma-1)B} \left( \nabla \phi \cdot \nabla + (\gamma-1) \Delta \phi \right) \left( B - H(\rho) \right) \\
		& =  \left(1-\frac{H(\rho)}{B} \right) \Delta \phi -\frac{1}{(\gamma-1) B}  \nabla \phi \cdot \frac{\nabla P(\rho)}{\rho}.
\end{align*}
Rearranging slightly and recalling the definition of $P$ in~\eqref{definition of P}, this becomes
\begin{align*}
	0 &  = \frac{\alpha^2}{(\gamma-1)B} \rho^{\gamma-1} \Delta \phi + \frac{\alpha^2}{(\gamma-1) B} \rho^{\gamma-2} \nabla \phi \cdot \nabla \rho,
\end{align*}
which is clearly equivalent to the conservation of mass. Finally, the conservation of momentum follows immediately from taking the gradient of Bernoulli's law and applying conservation of mass.

We only sketch the argument in the interest of keeping the presentation self-contained. First, observe that for any $\lambda$, we have the trivial solution 
\[
	(u, w, Q, \lambda,\mu) =  (0, 0, \tfrac{\lambda^2}{2}, \lambda, 0),
\]
which physically corresponds to an incompressible water wave for which the interface is completely flat and the velocity field is purely constant and horizontal with speed $\lambda$. Linearizing $(\mathscr F,\mathscr G)$ at this point yields the mapping
\begin{equation}
\label{linearized F periodic}
	D_{(u,w,Q)} \mathscr F(0, 0, \tfrac{\lambda^2}{2},\lambda;0) 
		\begin{pmatrix} 
			\dot u \\
			\dot w \\
			\dot Q
		\end{pmatrix}
			=
		\begin{pmatrix}
			\Delta \dot u \\
			\Delta \dot w
		\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\label{linearized G periodic}
		D_{(u,w,Q)} \mathscr G(0,0,\tfrac{\lambda^2}{2},\lambda;0) 
		\begin{pmatrix} 
			\dot u \\
			\dot w \\
			\dot Q
		\end{pmatrix}
			=  \left( -\lambda^2 \partial_\eta \dot w + \lambda \partial_\xi \dot u + \frac{1}{F^2} \dot w - \dot Q \right)\Big|_{\Gamma_1}.
\end{equation}
Let 
\[
\begin{aligned}
	& \mathscr{H}(w, R; \lambda) 
	\\
	& \qquad \colonequals \left(\mathscr F_2(0,w,R+\tfrac{\lambda^2}{2},\lambda; 0),~ \mathscr G(0,w,R+\tfrac{\lambda^2}{2},\lambda; 0) \right) \colon \tilde{\mathcal{O}} \subset \mathscr X_{2\mathrm{per}} \times \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathscr Y_{2\mathrm{per}} \times \mathscr Y_{3\mathrm{per}},
\end{aligned}
\]
for the neighborhood
\[
	\tilde{\mathcal{O}} \colonequals \{ (w,R,\lambda) \in \mathscr X_{2\mathrm{per}} \times \mathbb{R}^2 : (0,w,R+\tfrac{\lambda^2}{2},\lambda,0) \in \mathcal{O} \}.
\]
Each element $(w,R,\lambda)$ of the zero-set of $\mathscr{H}$ represents an incompressible periodic wave with $Q = R+\lambda^2/2$. Clearly, $\mathscr{H}$ is real analytic, and 
\[
	\mathscr{H}(0,0,\lambda) = 0 \quad \textrm{for all } \lambda \in \mathbb{R}, \qquad 
	D_{(w,R)} \mathscr{H}(0,0,\lambda) \begin{pmatrix} \dot w \\ \dot R \end{pmatrix}
		 = 
		 \begin{pmatrix} 
		 	\Delta \dot w \\
			\left( -\lambda^2 \partial_\eta \dot w + \frac{1}{F^2} \dot w -\dot R\right)|_{\Gamma_1}
		\end{pmatrix}.
\] 
Elliptic regularity implies that this is a Fredholm operator. To compute its index, we expand $\dot w$ as a Fourier cosine series. A routine calculation then shows that the dimension of the kernel is given exactly by the number of positive integer solutions $n$ to the so-called \emph{dispersion relation}:
\[ 
	0  = \mathfrak{d}(n,\lambda) \colonequals -\lambda^2 + \frac{1}{F^2} \frac{\tanh{(nkh)}}{n}. 
\]
For each $n \geq 1$, there exists a unique positive root $\lambda_n^*$ of $\mathfrak{d}(n,\,\cdot\,)$. We compute that
\[
	\operatorname{ker}{D_{(w,R)} \mathscr{H}(0,0,\lambda_n^*)} = \operatorname{span}{\{\dot w_n\}} \oplus \{ 0\}, \qquad \dot w_n(\xi,\eta) \colonequals \cos{(n\xi)} \sinh{\left( n(\eta+kh) \right)}.  
\]
Exploiting again the Fourier multiplier expression for $D_w \mathscr{H}(0,0,\lambda_n^*)$, it is readily confirmed that the range is characterized by 
\[
	\operatorname{rng}{D_{(w,R)} \mathscr{H}(0,0,\lambda_n^*)} = \left\{ (Y_2, Y_3) \in \mathscr Y_{2\mathrm{per}} \times \mathscr Y_{3\mathrm{per}} : \int_{\mathcal{D}^0} Y_2 \dot w_n \, d\xi \, d\eta + \frac{1}{(\lambda_n^*)^2} \int_{\Gamma_{1}^0} Y_3 \dot w_n \, d\xi = 0 \right\},
\]
where $\mathcal{D}^0$ and $\Gamma_{1}^0$ are a single period of the conformal domain and the preimage of the free boundary, respectively. Thus, the range is codimension $1$ and so the linearized operator is Fredholm index $0$. The above characterization of the range also allows us to infer that
\[
	D_{(w,R)} D_\lambda \mathscr{H}(0,0,\lambda_n^*) \dot w_n = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -2 \lambda_n^* \partial_\eta \dot w_n|_{\Gamma_1} \end{pmatrix} \not\in \operatorname{rng}{D_{(w,R)} \mathscr{H}(0,0,\lambda_n^*)}.
\]
Applying the (analytic) Crandall--Rabinowitz theorem, we conclude that there exists a real-analytic curve $\tilde{{\mathscr K}}_n^{\mathrm{loc}}  \subset \mathscr{H}^{-1}(0)$ so that locally the zero-set of $\mathscr{H}$ is the union of $\tilde{\mathscr K}_{n}^{\mathrm{loc}} $ and the trivial solution curve $\{ (0,0,\lambda) : \lambda \in \mathbb{R} \}$.  

We focus on the case $n=1$. Undoing the change of variables, the above argument gives a real-analytic curve 
\[
	{\mathscr K}_{k,h}^{\mathrm{loc}}  \subset (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(0,\,\cdot\,, \,\cdot\,, \,\cdot\,;\, 0)^{-1}(0)
\]
of nontrivial solutions bifurcating from the trivial solution $(0,0,(\lambda_1^*)^2/2, \lambda_1^*,0)$. Perhaps shrinking the curve slightly, the linearized operator is invertible at each point except the point of bifurcation. Then, applying Theorem~\ref{homoclinic global ift} furnishes the global curve ${\mathscr K}_{k,h}$. Using Schauder estimates and the fact that the domain is compact, we can prove that $(\mathscr F,\mathscr G)$ is relatively locally proper on the zero-set and it is semi-Fredholm; this is essentially done in the more general compressible setting in Lemmas~\ref{local properness lemma} and \ref{periodic Fredholmness lemma}. Thus, the loss of compactness~\ref{K loss of compactness alternative} and loss of Fredholmness~\ref{K loss of fredholmness alternative} alternatives can be eliminated. The closed loop alternative~\ref{K loop} is ruled out through the analysis of nodal properties, leaving only the possibility of blowup~\ref{K blowup alternative}.

\label{periodic JRE theorem}
Suppose that $(0,w_0,Q_0)$ is a solution to the periodic incompressible wave problem~\eqref{conformal governing equations periodic} that is nondegenerate \eqref{definition non-degenerate periodic} with wave number $k > 0$, conformal depth $h > 0$, and Froude number $F$. There exists a curve ${\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{per}$ of solutions to the compressible periodic wave problem \eqref{steady Euler system} with the real-analytic parameterization
\[
	{\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{per} = \left\{ (u^{\mu} , w^{\mu} , Q^\mu, \lambda_0,\mu) \in \mathscr X_\mathrm{per} \times \mathbb{R}^3 : ~ |\mu| < \mu_0 \right\} \subset \mathcal{O} \cap (\mathscr F, \mathscr G)^{-1}(0),
\] 
for some $\mu_0 > 0$. The solutions along ${\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{per}$ admit the infinite power series representation 
\begin{equation}
\label{JRE}
	u^\mu = \sum_{m \geq 1} \mu^m u_m, \qquad w^\mu = w_0 + \sum_{m \geq 1} \mu^m w_m, \qquad Q^\mu = Q_0 + \sum_{m \geq 1} \mu^m Q_m
\end{equation}
where the leading-order terms $(u_1, w_1,Q_1)$ are the unique solutions in $\mathscr X_\mathrm{per} \times \mathbb{R}$ to the homogeneous Poisson problem
\begin{equation}
    \label{periodic JRE u}
    \left\{
    \begin{aligned}
        \Delta u_1 & = \lambda_0 \partial_\xi \left( \frac{1}{2} \frac{\lambda_0^2}{|\nabla w_0+\mathbf{e}_2|^2} + \frac{1}{F^2}w_0 \right) & \qquad& \text{in } \mathcal{D} \\
        \partial_\eta u_1 &=0 &\qquad & \text{on } \partial\mathcal{D}
      \end{aligned} \right.
\end{equation}
and homogeneous Laplace equation with Robin condition 
\begin{equation}
\label{periodic JRE w}
      \left\{
      \begin{aligned}
        \Delta w_1 & =0 &\qquad & \text{in }\mathcal{D}\\
        -\frac{\lambda_0^2( \nabla w_0+\mathbf{e}_2) \cdot\nabla w_1 }{|\nabla w_0+\mathbf{e}_2|^4}+\frac{1}{F^2} w_1 & = -\frac{\lambda_0 \partial_\xi u_1}{|\nabla w_0 + \mathbf{e}_2|^2} + Q_1 & \qquad & \text{on } \Gamma_1 \\
        w_1 & = 0 & \qquad & \textrm{on } \Gamma_0.
    \end{aligned}
    \right.
\end{equation}

It follows from the general formula~\eqref{linsystem} for the linearized operator (evaluated at $u = 0$, $\mu = 0$) and the assumption that $(0,w_0,Q_0,\lambda_0,0)$  is nondegenerate~\eqref{definition non-degenerate periodic} that
\begin{align*}
	\mathscr{L}_0 & \colonequals D_{(u,w,Q)} (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(0,w_0,Q_0,\lambda_0,0) \\
	& =  
	\begin{pmatrix}
		\Delta_{{\mathrm{o}} } & 0 & 0\\
		0 & \Delta_{\mathrm{e}}   & 0 \\
		\frac{\lambda_0}{|\nabla w_0+\mathbf{e}_2|^2} \partial_\xi|_{\Gamma_1} & \left( -\frac{\lambda_0^2}{|\nabla w_0+\frac{1}{kh}\mathbf{e}_2 |^4} (\nabla w_0+\frac{1}{kh}\mathbf{e}_2) \cdot \nabla + \frac{1}{F^2} \right)|_{\Gamma_1} & -1 
	\end{pmatrix},
\end{align*}
where 
\begin{align*}
	\Delta_{\mathrm{o}}   \colonequals \Delta\Big|_{\mathscr X_{1\mathrm{per}}}^{\mathscr Y_{1\mathrm{per}}} \qquad \textrm{and} \qquad 
	\Delta_{{\mathrm{e}} } \colonequals \Delta\Big|_{\mathscr X_{2\mathrm{per}}}^{\mathscr Y_{2\mathrm{per}}}
\end{align*}
 are the Laplacian with the appropriate domain and codomain restrictions. In particular, $\Delta_{\mathrm{o}} $ is invertible, the lower right block operator is $D_{(w,Q)}(\mathscr F_2,\mathscr G)$, which is invertible by the nondegeneracy assumption~\eqref{definition non-degenerate periodic}, and the lower left entry is clearly a bounded operator. Therefore
\[
	 D_{(u,w,Q)} (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(0,w_0,Q_0,\lambda_0,0) \colon \mathscr X_\mathrm{per} \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathscr Y_\mathrm{per} \quad \textrm{is an isomorphism.}
\]
The existence of the curve ${\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{per}$ follows simply from the real analytic implicit function theorem, which also justifies the power series expansion. To compute the first-order terms, we differentiate the equation in $\mu$ to see that
\[
\mathscr{L}_0	\begin{pmatrix}
		u_1 \\  w_1 \\ Q_1
	\end{pmatrix}
		= 
	- 
		\begin{pmatrix} 
			D_\mu\mathscr F(0,w_0,Q_0,\lambda_0,0) \\
			D_\mu\mathscr G(0,w_0,Q_0,\lambda_0,0) 
		\end{pmatrix}

			\begin{pmatrix}
				\lambda_0 \partial_\xi \left( \frac{1}{2} \frac{\lambda_0^2}{|\nabla w_0+\frac{1}{kh}\mathbf{e}_2|^2} + \frac{1}{F^2}w_0 \right) \\
				0 \\ 0
			\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This leads directly to the PDEs in~\eqref{periodic JRE u} and \eqref{periodic JRE w}.

For a survey of this literature, see \cite[Section 5]{haziot2022traveling}. We mention that our formulation is inspired by the recent work of Haziot and Wheeler~\cite{haziot2023large} on (incompressible) solitary waves with constant vorticity. Consider the trivial solution $(0,0,F,0)$. The linearized operator there is generally \emph{not} Fredholm in this case due to the unboundedness of the domain. \label{periodic global bifurcation theorem}
Fix a wave number $k > 0$, conformal depth $h > 0$, Froude number $F > 0$, and a corresponding nondegenerate solution $(0,w_0,Q_0)$ to the periodic incompressible water wave problem.  Let ${\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{per}$ be the real-analytic curve of lightly compressible periodic waves furnished by Theorem~\ref{periodic JRE theorem}. There exists a curve ${\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$ of compressible periodic water waves admitting the $C^0$ parameterization
\[
	{\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per} = \left\{ (u(s), w(s), Q(s),\lambda_0, \mu(s) ) : s \in [0,\infty) \right\} \subset (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)^{-1} (0) 
\]
and satisfying the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\rm(\alph*)]
	\item \label{periodic extension part} ${\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{per} \subset {\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$ and $(u(0), w(0), Q(0), \lambda_0, \mu(0)) = (0,w_0,Q_0,\lambda_0, 0)$.
	\item \label{periodic physical part} Every solution on ${\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$ is physical in that the corresponding mapping $f(s)$ is conformal and injective on $\overline{\mathcal{D}}$.
	\item \label{periodic alternatives part} In the limit $s \to \infty$, one of the following alternatives must occur:
		\begin{enumerate}[label=\rm(A\arabic*)]
			\item \label{periodic unboundedness alternative} \textup{(Unboundedness)} The quantity
			\[
				 \|u(s) \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\mathcal{D})} + \| w(s) \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\mathcal{D})} + |\mu(s)| \to \infty. 
			\]
			\item \label{boundary degeneracy alternative} \textup{(Boundary degeneracy)} Either
			\[
				\operatorname{dist}{(\mathscr{S}(s), \mathscr{B})} \to 0 \qquad \textrm{or} \qquad \lim_{s\to\infty} \sup_{\substack{\zeta, \zeta^\prime \in \Gamma_1 \\ \zeta \neq \zeta^\prime}} \frac{|\zeta-\zeta^\prime|}{|f(\zeta) - f(\zeta^\prime)|} = \infty,
			\]
			where $f(s)$ is the conformal mapping derived from $w(s)$ and $\mathscr{S}(s)$ is the corresponding free boundary in the physical domain.

			\item \label{transsonic limit alternative} \textup{(Transsonic flow)} At a point in $\mathcal{D}$, the flow approaches transsonic:
			\begin{equation}
			\label{transsonic limit}
		\lim_{s \to \infty} \inf_{\mathcal{D}} \left(c(s)^2 - \frac{|\nabla u(s) + \lambda_0 \mathbf{e}_1|^2}{|\nabla w(s) + \frac{1}{kh}\mathbf{e}_2|^2}  \right) = 0.
			\end{equation}
			\item \label{stagnation alternative} \textup{(Stagnation)} A relative stagnation point forms on the free surface:
			\[
				\lim_{s \to \infty} \inf_{\Gamma_1} |\partial_\xi u(s) + \lambda_0| = 0.
			\]

			\item \label{periodic loop alternative} \textup{(Closed loop)} There exists $T > 0$ such that 
			\[
				\left(u(s+T),\, w(s+T),\, Q(s+T),\, \mu(s+T) \right)= \left(u(s),\, w(s), \, Q(s),\, \mu(s) \right)
			\]
			for all $ s \in [0,\infty)$. If this occurs, then necessarily ${\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$ contains two distinct incompressible solutions.
		\end{enumerate}
	\item At each parameter value $s$, ${\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$ admits a local reparameterization that is real analytic. 
\end{enumerate}

\label{subsonic ADN lemma}
Suppose that $(u,w,Q,\lambda,\mu) \in (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)^{-1}(0)$
with $\mu > 0$ is subsonic in that
\[
    \inf_{\mathcal{D}} \left(c^2
    - \frac{|\nabla u+\lambda \mathbf{e}_1|^2}
    {|\nabla w + \mathbf{e}_2|^2} \right) > 0.
\]
Then, the corresponding linearized operator
$D_{(u,w)} (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(u,w,Q,\lambda; \mu)$ is elliptic in
the ADN sense.

\label{ADN ellipticity condition physical}
    \frac{1}{\mu} > \frac{\gamma+1}{2} |\nabla \phi|^2
    + \frac{\gamma-1}{F^2} y
    = |\nabla \phi|^2
    + (\gamma-1) \left( \frac{1}{2} |\nabla \phi|^2
    + \frac{1}{F^2} y \right).
\end{equation}
First consider the case $\gamma = 1$, so that in light of

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{lopat prop}
Assume the interior ellipticity
condition~\eqref{interior ellipticity condition}. Then the
following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\rm(\alph*)]
\item \label{lopat surface part}
At each point of $\Gamma_1$ and for each $\tau \neq 0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{lopat determinant}
	\sum_{i<j} |M_{ij}(\tau)| \, \geq \, |M_{14}(\tau)|
	= \frac{|\tau|\,\sigma^2}{4\kappa \mathcal{E} \,|\mathbf{q}|^3},
\end{equation}
and every minor $M_{ij}$ vanishes wherever $\sigma = 0$. In
particular, the Lopatinskii condition holds uniformly on
$\Gamma_1$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
\label{definition sigma0}
	\sigma_0 \colonequals
	\inf_{\Gamma_1} \frac{\sigma^2}{|\mathbf{q}|^3} > 0.
\end{equation}
\item \label{lopat bed part}
On $\Gamma_0$, where the boundary rows are
$(\partial_\eta \dot u, \dot w)$ with symbol rows $(\nu,0)$ and
$(0,1)$, the minor $M_{12}$ of the corresponding Lopatinskii
matrix equals $1/(4i\kappa^2 \mathcal{E} \tau) \neq 0$, so the
Lopatinskii condition holds there with no further assumptions.
\end{enumerate}

$\kappa = 1$ regardless of $\sigma$. First assume that $\sigma \neq 0$. For $\mu > 0$ the poles

R_1
&\colonequals

\mathcal{B}(A^0)^{-1} =
\frac{1}{2 i \kappa}

\dfrac{\sigma}{\mathcal{E} |\mathbf{q}|^2}

-\dfrac{\kappa \sigma^2
\bigl(\kappa w_\xi + i(w_\eta+1)\bigr)}
{|\mathbf{q}|^4}
\\[10pt]

&
-\dfrac{i\sigma
\bigl(\kappa w_\xi + i(w_\eta+1)\bigr)}
{|\mathbf{q}|^2}
\end{pmatrix},
\\[12pt]
R_2
&\colonequals
\operatorname*{Res}_{\nu = i\tau}
\mathcal{B}(A^0)^{-1} =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 0 \\[4pt]
0 &
\dfrac{\sigma\bigl(w_\xi+i(w_\eta+1)\bigr)}
{2|\mathbf{q}|^2}
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\]

R_1 + i\tau R_2 )$. A direct check shows $R_1$ has rank
one --- its first row equals $-(i\kappa\sigma/|\mathbf{q}|^2)$
times its second, so $\det R_1 = 0$ --- and the first entry of
$R_2\mathbf{e}_2$ vanishes. Consequently, in the minor
\[
	M_{14} = \det \begin{pmatrix} R_1\mathbf{e}_1 & \tfrac{i\tau}{\kappa}R_1\mathbf{e}_2 + i\tau
R_2\mathbf{e}_2 \end{pmatrix},
\]
 the $R_1$-contribution cancels identically
and only the cross term survives:
\[
	M_{14} = i\tau \det \begin{pmatrix} R_1\mathbf{e}_1
	& R_2\mathbf{e}_2 \end{pmatrix} 
	= \frac{\tau\,\sigma^2\big(w_\xi+i(w_\eta+1)\big)}
	{4\kappa \mathcal{E}\,|\mathbf{q}|^4}.
\]
Since $|w_\xi + i(w_\eta+1)| = |\mathbf{q}|$, this
gives~\eqref{lopat determinant} for $\mu>0$; all
$\kappa$-dependence beyond the prefactor cancels. At the
incompressible endpoint $\mu=0$ (still $\sigma\neq0$) the poles
merge at $\nu=i\tau$, the off-diagonal entry of $A^0$ vanishes,
and a direct evaluation at the single pole returns the same
formula with $\kappa=1$, so~\eqref{lopat determinant} extends
continuously across $\mu=0$.

Next, suppose instead that $\sigma = 0$. Every entry of the first row of
$\mathcal{B}$ carries a factor of $\sigma$, so the first row of
$\Lambda(\tau)$ vanishes identically and every $2\times2$ minor
is zero, consistent with $|M_{14}|\to0$ as $\sigma\to0$.
Combining the two cases proves part~\ref{lopat surface part}.

For part~\ref{lopat bed part}, the first column of the
$\Gamma_0$ Lopatinskii matrix is
\[
	\begin{pmatrix} \displaystyle \operatorname*{Res}_{\nu=i\tau/\kappa} \frac{\nu}{A_{11}^0} \\ 
\displaystyle 0 \end{pmatrix}  = \begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle \frac{1}{2\kappa^2 \mathcal{E}} \\ 0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A similar residue calculation shows that the second entry of the second column is
$1/(2i\tau)$. Since the lower-left entry vanishes, we therefore find that
\[
	M_{12} 	= \frac{1}{4i\kappa^2 \mathcal{E}\tau} \neq 0. \qedhere
\]

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{kappa degeneracy remark}
The symbol degeneracy $\kappa=1$ and the breakdown of the
Lopatinskii condition are distinct phenomena. By~\eqref{mu c2
identity}, $\kappa=1$ occurs either at the incompressible
endpoint $\mu=0$, where it is harmless, or, when $\mu\neq0$,
precisely at a stagnation point $\sigma=0$. Consequently, for a
compressible solution ($\mu>0$) the no-stagnation condition
$\sigma_0>0$ is equivalent to $\kappa>1$ holding uniformly on
$\Gamma_1$, and the resulting lower bound is continuous across
$\mu=0$, so the ellipticity and Lopatinskii constants remain
uniform along the entire bifurcation curve.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{schauder lemma}
Fix $\delta > 0$ and let
$(u,w,Q,\lambda,\mu) \in \mathcal{O}_\delta$. Then, for all
$(\dot u, \dot w, \dot Q) \in \mathscr X_\mathrm{per} \times \mathbb{R}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{schauder2}
\begin{aligned}
    \| \dot u \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\mathcal{D})}
    + \| \dot w \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\mathcal{D})}
    & \leq C \bigg(
    \Big\| D_{(u,w)} \mathscr F(u,w,Q,\lambda;\mu)
    \big(\dot u, \dot w\big)
    \Big\|_{\mathscr Y_{1\mathrm{per}} \times \mathscr Y_{2\mathrm{per}}} \\
    & \qquad
    + \Big\| D_{(u,w)} \mathscr G(u,w,Q,\lambda;\mu)
    \big(\dot u, \dot w \big)
    \Big\|_{\mathscr Y_{3\mathrm{per}}} \\
    & \qquad
    + |\dot Q|
    + \| \dot u \|_{C^0(\mathcal{D})}
    + \| \dot w \|_{C^0(\mathcal{D})} \bigg),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the constant $C > 0$ depends only on $\delta$,
$\beta$, $\gamma$, $F$, $kh$, and upper bounds for
$|\lambda|$, $|\mu|$, $|Q|$, and
$\| (u,w) \|_{\mathscr X_\mathrm{per}}$.

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
We first verify that the hypotheses of the linear elliptic
theory hold with constants controlled by the stated
quantities. By the definition~\eqref{definition periodic
O delta} of $\mathcal{O}_\delta$, the ellipticity function
satisfies $\inf_\mathcal{D} \mathcal{E} \geq |\mu|\delta$ when
$\mu \neq 0$; on the other hand, the elementary bound
$|\mathcal{E} - 1| \leq |\mu| C_1$ holds for all $\mu$, where
$C_1$ is controlled by $\gamma$, $F$, $kh$, and
$\|(u,w)\|_{\mathscr X_\mathrm{per}}$. If $|\mu| \leq 1/(2C_1)$ the
latter gives $\mathcal{E} \geq 1/2$, while otherwise the
former gives $\mathcal{E} \geq \delta/(2C_1)$; in either case
\[
    \inf_\mathcal{D} \mathcal{E} \geq \mathcal{E}_0
    \colonequals \min\Big\{ \tfrac12,\
    \tfrac{\delta}{2 C_1} \Big\} > 0.
\]
Likewise, the fourth condition in the definition of $\mathcal{O}_\delta$ gives
$\sigma_0 \geq \delta$. Since moreover
$\mathcal{E} \leq 1 + |\mu| C_1$ by the same elementary bound
and $\kappa^2 = 1 + \mu(\partial_\xi u + \lambda)^2/
(\mathcal{E} |\mathbf{q}|^2)$ is bounded above by the stated
quantities and $\mathcal{E}_0$, we conclude
from~\eqref{gen:elliptic},
\eqref{proper ellipticity roots}, and
Proposition~\ref{lopat prop} that the boundary value
problem~\eqref{genlinsystem} is uniformly elliptic in the
Douglis--Nirenberg sense, properly elliptic, and satisfies
the Lopatinskii condition uniformly on $\partial\mathcal{D}$,
with all constants controlled as claimed. The classical
Schauder theory for elliptic systems satisfying the
complementing condition
\cite[Theorem 9.2]{agmon1964estimates} therefore yields, for
every solution $(\dot u, \dot w) \in
C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\overline\mathcal{D})$
of~\eqref{genlinsystem} with data $h_1, h_2 \in
C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta}(\overline{\mathcal{D}})$ and $f, g \in
C_\mathrm{per}^{1+\beta}(\Gamma_1)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{adn schauder}
\begin{aligned}
    \| \dot u \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\mathcal{D})}
    + \| \dot w \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\mathcal{D})}
    & \leq C \Big(
    \| h_1 \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta}(\mathcal{D})}
    + \| h_2 \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta}(\mathcal{D})}
    + \| f \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{1+\beta}(\Gamma_1)}
    + \| g \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{1+\beta}(\Gamma_1)} \\
    & \qquad \qquad
    + \| \dot u \|_{C^0(\mathcal{D})}
    + \| \dot w \|_{C^0(\mathcal{D})} \Big).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

It remains to match~\eqref{linsystem} with the data
in~\eqref{genlinsystem}. Given
$(\dot u, \dot w, \dot Q) \in \mathscr X_\mathrm{per} \times
\mathbb{R}$, set
\[
\begin{aligned}
    h_1 & \colonequals D_{(u,w,Q)}\mathscr F_1(u,w,Q,\lambda; \mu)
    \begin{pmatrix} \dot u \\ \dot w \\ \dot Q\end{pmatrix},
    \\
    h_2 & \colonequals \Delta \dot w,
    \\
    f &\colonequals \dot{\mathfrak{B}}
    = D_{(u,w,Q)}\mathscr G(u,w,Q,\lambda; \mu)
    \begin{pmatrix} \dot u \\ \dot w \\ \dot Q \end{pmatrix} {}+ \dot Q,
    \end{aligned}
\]
where we have used~\eqref{Lcbernoulli}. The remaining side
conditions are homogeneous: the kinematic datum is
$g = \partial_\eta \dot u = 0$ on $\Gamma_1$, and
$\partial_\eta \dot u = 0$, $\dot w = 0$ on $\Gamma_0$,
all built into the space $\mathscr X_\mathrm{per}$. Under the
identifications
$\mathscr Y_{1\mathrm{per}}\times\mathscr Y_{2\mathrm{per}}
= C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta} \times C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta}$ and
$\mathscr Y_{3\mathrm{per}} = C_\mathrm{per}^{1+\beta}(\Gamma_1)$,
the data lie in exactly the spaces required
by~\eqref{adn schauder}, and
\begin{align*}
    \| h_1 \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta}}
    + \| h_2 \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta}}
    + \| f \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{1+\beta}(\Gamma_1)}
    & \leq
    \big\| D_{(u,w,Q)}\mathscr F
    (\dot u, \dot w, \dot Q)\big\|_{\mathscr Y_{1\mathrm{per}}
    \times\mathscr Y_{2\mathrm{per}}} \\
    & \quad
    + \big\| D_{(u,w,Q)}\mathscr G
    (\dot u, \dot w, \dot Q)\big\|_{\mathscr Y_{3\mathrm{per}}}
    + |\dot Q|,
\end{align*}
the term $|\dot Q|$ accounting for the splitting
$f = D_{(u,w,Q)}\mathscr G(\dot u,\dot w,\dot Q) + \dot Q$.
Applying~\eqref{adn schauder} with $g = 0$ and the data
bounded as above gives~\eqref{schauder2}.

\label{local properness lemma}
For each $\delta > 0$, the zero-set of $(\mathscr F,\mathscr G)$ is locally proper: for any closed and bounded set $K \subset \mathcal{O}_\delta$, we have that 
\[
	(\mathscr F,\mathscr G)^{-1}(0) \cap K \subset\subset \mathscr X_\mathrm{per} \times \mathbb{R}^3.
\]
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Fix $\delta > 0$. Let $\{ (u_j, w_j, Q_j, \lambda_j, \mu_j)\} \subset \mathscr X_\mathrm{per} \times \mathbb{R}^3$ be a bounded sequence in $(\mathscr F,\mathscr G)^{-1}(0) \cap \mathcal{O}_\delta$; we must show that $\{ (u_j, w_j, Q_j, \lambda_j, \mu_j)\}$ has a subsequence converging in $\mathscr X_\mathrm{per} \times \mathbb{R}^3$. Note that each term in the sequence is a solution of the PDE system
\[
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
	\Delta u_j & = \mu_j \left(  (\gamma-1) \Delta u_j + (\nabla u_j  + \lambda_j \mathbf{e}_1) \cdot \nabla  \right)  \left( \frac{1}{2} \frac{|\nabla u_j + \lambda_j \mathbf{e}_1|^2}{|\nabla w_j+\mathbf{e}_2|^2} + \frac{1}{F^2} (w_j+\eta) \right)   \\
	\Delta w_j & = 0
\end{aligned}
\right.
\qquad \textrm{in } \mathcal{D}
\]
together with the boundary conditions 
\[
\left\{ \begin{aligned}
	\partial_\eta u_j & = 0 & \qquad & \textrm{on } \partial \mathcal{D} \\
	\frac{1}{2} \frac{| \nabla u_j+\lambda_j \mathbf{e}_1|^2}{|\nabla w_j + \mathbf{e}_2|^2} + \frac{1}{F^2} w_j -Q_j  &= 0 & \qquad & \textrm{on } \Gamma_1 \\
	w_j & = 0 & \qquad & \textrm{on } \Gamma_0.
\end{aligned} \right.
 \]
 Of course, it suffices to show that $\{(u_j,w_j, Q_j,\lambda_j,\mu_j)\}$ has a convergent subsequence in $C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\overline{\mathcal{D}}) \times \mathbb{R}^3$. By boundedness we know that, passing to subsequences, $(Q_j,\lambda_j,\mu_j) \to (Q, \lambda, \mu)$ for some $Q, \lambda, \mu \in \mathbb{R}$. Moreover, by compactness of the embedding, we can pass to a further subsequence to conclude that there exists $u, w \in C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\overline{\mathcal{D}})$ such that $u_j \to u$ and $w_j \to w$ in $C_\mathrm{per}^2(\overline{\mathcal{D}})$. We upgrade this to convergence in the full $C^{2+\beta}_\mathrm{per}$ norm using the following standard argument.
  
Because of translation invariance in the $\xi$-direction, $(\partial_\xi u_j, \partial_\xi w_j)$ satisfies the ``quasilinearized'' problem
\[
	D_{(u,w)} (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(u_j,w_j,Q_j,\lambda_j,\mu_j) \begin{pmatrix} \partial_\xi u_j \\ \partial_\xi w_j \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
This is a system with interior coefficients uniformly bounded in $C^{\beta}_\mathrm{per}(\overline{\mathcal{D}})$ and boundary coefficients uniformly bounded in $C^{1+\beta}_\mathrm{per}(\Gamma_1)$. Thanks to the definition of $\mathcal{O}_\delta$, it is moreover elliptic in the ADN sense with a constant depending only on $\delta$. Applying the Schauder estimates of Lemma~\ref{schauder lemma} (whose proof does not use the parity or mean-zero restrictions built into $\mathscr X_\mathrm{per}$, so that it applies to $(\partial_\xi u_j, \partial_\xi w_j)$ even though these have the opposite parity), we therefore have that $\{ (\partial_\xi u_j, \partial_\xi w_j) \}$ is uniformly bounded in $C^{2+\beta}_\mathrm{per}(\overline{\mathcal{D}})$. 
This gives convergence of all of the derivatives of $w_j$ of order up to two in $C^{\beta}_\mathrm{per}(\overline{\mathcal{D}})$ except perhaps $\partial_\eta^2 w_{j}$. But, since each $w_j$ is harmonic, so too is the limit $w$, and we have
\[
	\partial_\eta^2 w_j = -  \partial_\xi^2 w_j \longrightarrow_{C^\beta_\mathrm{per}} - \partial_\xi^2 w = \partial_\eta^2 w.
\]

 
By the same reasoning, we have that all derivatives of $u_j$ up to order two except perhaps $\partial_\eta^2 u_j$ are convergent in $C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta}(\overline{\mathcal{D}})$. Differentiating the equation $\mathscr F_1(u_j,w_j,Q_j,\lambda_j,\mu_j) = 0$ in $\eta$ shows that $\partial_\eta u_j$ solves
\[
	D_u \mathscr F_1(u_j, w_j, Q_j,\lambda_j, \mu_j)\, \partial_\eta u_j = g_j,
\]
where $\{g_j\}$ is uniformly bounded in $C^\beta_\mathrm{per}(\overline\mathcal{D})$, and $D_u\mathscr F_1$ is a uniformly elliptic PDE operator with coefficients bounded uniformly in $C^{\beta}_\mathrm{per}(\overline{\mathcal{D}})$. As $\partial_\eta u_j$ satisfies homogeneous Dirichlet conditions on $\partial\mathcal{D}$, standard (scalar) Schauder estimates imply that it is uniformly bounded in $C^{2+\beta}_\mathrm{per}(\overline{\mathcal{D}})$, which completes the argument.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{periodic Fredholmness lemma}
For any $(u,w,Q,\lambda,\mu) \in (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)^{-1}(0) \cap \mathcal{O}$, the linearized mapping $D_{(u,w,Q)}(\mathscr F,\mathscr G)$ is semi-Fredholm $\mathscr X_\mathrm{per} \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathscr Y_\mathrm{per}$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
First note that it suffices to establish the semi-Fredholmness of $D_{(u,w)} (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)$ as mapping $\mathscr X_\mathrm{per} \to \mathscr Y_\mathrm{per}$, since this differs from the linearized operator by a finite-rank perturbation. Let $(u_*,w_*,Q_*,\lambda_*,\mu_*) \in \mathcal{O}_\delta$ be given, and consider the corresponding linearized operator 
\[
	\mathscr{L} = ( \mathscr{L}_1, \mathscr{L}_2, \mathscr{L}_3 ) \colonequals D_{(u,w)}(\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(u_*,w_*,Q_*, \lambda_*, \mu_*) \colon \mathscr X_{\mathrm{per}} \to \mathscr Y_\mathrm{per}.
\]
As a consequence of the Schauder estimates from Lemma~\ref{schauder lemma}, which hold in view of the definition of $\mathcal{O}_\delta$, we have the bound
\begin{align*}
	\| \dot u \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\mathcal{D})} + \| \dot w \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\mathcal{D})}  & \leq C \Big( \| \dot u \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta}(\mathcal{D})} + \| \dot w \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta}(\mathcal{D})} + \left\| \mathscr{L}_1 \begin{pmatrix} \dot u \\ \dot w \end{pmatrix} \right\|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta}(\mathcal{D})} \\ 
	& \qquad   + \left\| \mathscr{L}_2 \begin{pmatrix} \dot u \\ \dot w \end{pmatrix} \right\|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{\beta}(\mathcal{D})}  +  \left\| \mathscr{L}_3  \begin{pmatrix} \dot u \\ \dot w \end{pmatrix} \right\|_{C^{1+\beta}(\Gamma_1)}\Big),
\end{align*}
where the constant $C > 0$ depends only on lower bounds on $\delta$ and upper bounds on $|Q_*|$, $|\lambda_*|$, $\mu_*$, and the $C^{2+\beta}$ norms of $u_*$ and $w_*$. Since the spaces represented by the norms on the left-hand side of this inequality are compactly embedded in the spaces represented by the (interior) norms on the right-hand side, a standard argument gives that $\mathscr{L}$ is semi-Fredholm.

Applying Theorem~\ref{homoclinic global ift} at the initial incompressible solution $(0,w_0, Q_0, \lambda_0, 0)$ furnishes the continuous  curve ${\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$. Local uniqueness implies that ${\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{per} \subset {\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$, which proves the statement in part~\ref{periodic extension part}. Since each point on ${\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$ is necessarily in the connected component of $\mathcal{O}$ containing $(0,w_0, Q_0,\lambda_0, 0)$, the physicality of the solutions asserted in part~\ref{periodic physical part} is a consequence of Lemma~\ref{periodic equivalence lemma}.

Next consider the limiting behavior as $s \to \infty$ along ${\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$. Thanks to Lemmas~\ref{local properness lemma} and \ref{periodic Fredholmness lemma}, the loss of compactness alternative~\ref{K loss of compactness alternative} and loss of Fredholmness alternative~\ref{K loss of fredholmness alternative} can be excluded, respectively. This leaves only  blowup~\ref{K blowup alternative} and the closed loop alternative~\ref{K loop}. 

Assume first that the latter of these occurs. Then it must be that $\mu(T) = \mu(0) = 0$ for some $T > 0$. At the initial solution $(0,w_0,Q_0,\lambda_0,0)$, we have by local uniqueness that every solution lies on ${\mathscr C}_{\mathrm{loc}} ^\mathrm{per}$, and hence $\mu(s) < 0$ for $0 < T-s \ll 1$. Therefore, there must exist some $s_* \in (0,T)$ with $\mu(s_*) = 0$. Thus, the alternative~\ref{periodic loop alternative} occurs.

Suppose instead that we have blowup, which here means specifically that
\begin{align*}
	N(s) & \colonequals \|u(s) \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\mathcal{D})} + \| w(s) \|_{C_\mathrm{per}^{2+\beta}(\mathcal{D})} + |Q(s)| +|\mu(s)| \\
		& \qquad + \frac{1}{\operatorname{dist}{((u(s), w(s), Q(s), \lambda_0, \mu(s)), \partial\mathcal{O})}} \\
		& \longrightarrow \infty.
\end{align*} 
Recalling the definition of $\mathcal{O}$ in~\eqref{definition periodic O delta}, in order for ${\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$ to come arbitrarily close to $\partial \mathcal{O}$, thanks to Lemma~\ref{subsonic ADN lemma}, we must have that either~\ref{boundary degeneracy alternative}, \ref{transsonic limit alternative}, or \ref{stagnation alternative} occurs. On the other hand, if the distance between ${\mathscr C}_\mathrm{per}$ and $\partial\mathcal{O}$ is uniformly positive and $(u(s),w(s))$ is uniformly bounded in $\mathscr X_\mathrm{per}$, then taking the mean of the dynamic condition~\eqref{cbernoulli periodic} over a period reveals that
\[
	Q(s) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\pi}^\pi \frac{1}{2} \frac{|\nabla u(s)(\xi,0) + \lambda_0 \mathbf{e}_1|^2}{|\nabla w(s)(\xi,0) + \mathbf{e}_2|^2} \, d\xi,
\]
hence $Q(s)$ must likewise be uniformly bounded. This allows us to pare down the unboundedness statement to~\ref{periodic unboundedness alternative}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
For any classical solution to the steady isentropic Euler equations~\eqref{two-phase isentropic Euler system}, the mass flux in the lower layer $\mathfrak{m}_-$, mass flux in the upper layer $\mathfrak{m}_+$, and flow force $\mathfrak{s}$ defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{conserved quantities}
\begin{aligned}
	\mathfrak{m}_-(x) & \colonequals \int_{\mathscr{D}_-^x } \rho_- \partial_x \phi_- \, dy,  \qquad 
	\mathfrak{m}_+(x)  \colonequals  \int_{\mathscr{D}_+^x} \rho_+ \partial_x \phi_+ \, dy, \\
	\mathfrak{s}(x) & \colonequals \int_{\mathscr{D}_-^x} \left( P_-(\rho_-) + \rho_- (\partial_x \phi_-)^2 \right) \, dy  + \int_{\mathscr{D}_+^x} \left( P_+(\rho_+) + \rho_+ (\partial_x \phi_+)^2 \right) \, dy
\end{aligned} 
\end{equation}
are independent of $x$, where $\mathscr{D}_\pm^x \colonequals \mathscr{D}_\pm \cap ( \{ x \} \times (0,1)) $ is the $x$-section of the corresponding fluid layer.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
The $x$-independence of $\mathfrak{m}_\pm$ is an immediate consequence of the conservation of mass in~\eqref{two-phase steady Euler equation} and kinematic boundary condition~\eqref{two-phase kinematic condition}: for any $x_1 < x_2$, we have
\[
	\mathfrak{m}_-(x_2) - \mathfrak{m}_-(x_1) = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} \int_0^{h(x)} \nabla \cdot \left( \rho_- \nabla \phi_- \right) \, dy \, dx = 0.
\]
The proof for $\mathfrak{m}_+$ is similar. Likewise, the conservation of the flow force $\mathfrak{s}$ results from writing the horizontal component of the momentum equations in conservative form
\[
	\partial_x P_\pm + \partial_x \left( \rho_\pm (\partial_x \phi_\pm)^2 \right) + \partial_y \left( \rho_\pm (\partial_x \phi_\pm) (\partial_y \phi_\pm) \right) = 0 \qquad \textrm{in } \mathscr{D}_\pm
\]
and integrating, using the kinematic condition and the continuity of the pressure to eliminate the boundary terms on $\mathscr{S}$.

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
Recall that we took the upstream limit of trace of $\rho_-$ on $\mathscr{S}$ to be the characteristic density. Thus, in our nondimensionalized variables, we should have $\rho_-(x,h(x)) \to 1$ as $x \to -\infty$. Since the far-field density limits are all of the form~\eqref{far-field density ansatz}, we find that
\[
	 B_- = \frac{1}{2} U^2 + \frac{d}{F^2}, \qquad (\rho_0+\delta)\mu B_+ =  (\rho_0+\delta)\mu \left( \frac{1}{2} U^2 + \frac{d}{F^2}\right) +  \log{(\rho_0+\delta)}.
\]
In particular, this implies that $\rho_\pm \to R_\pm$ as $x \to -\infty$, where 
\begin{equation}
\label{upstream density}
	R_-(y) = e^{-\mu \frac{y-d}{F^2}}, \qquad R_+(y) = (\rho_0+\delta) e^{-(\rho_0+\delta) \mu \frac{y-d}{F^2}},
\end{equation}
and in the downstream limit, $\rho_\pm \to R_\pm^\dagger$ as $x \to +\infty$, with
\begin{equation}
\label{downstream density}
	R_-^\dagger(y) = e^{-\mu\left( \frac{1}{2} ((U_-^\dagger)^2 - U^2) + \frac{y-d}{F^2}  \right)}, \qquad R_+^\dagger(y) = (\rho_0+\delta) e^{-(\rho_0+\delta) \mu \left( \frac{1}{2} ((U_+^\dagger)^2 - U^2) + \frac{y-d}{F^2} \right)}.
\end{equation}

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{conjugate flow corollary}
If $(\phi,h)$ is a classical solution to the steady isentropic Euler equations~\eqref{two-phase isentropic Euler system}, then the limiting depths $d$ and $d^\dagger$ and velocities $U$, $U_+^\dagger$, and $U_-^\dagger$ must satisfy the following system of equations. 

\begin{enumerate}[label=\rm(\alph*)]
\item \textup{(Conservation of energy)} The dynamic condition on $\mathscr{S}$ implies that
\begin{equation}
\label{conservation of energy}
\begin{aligned}
	\frac{\rho_0-1+\delta}{2} U^2 + \frac{\rho_0-1+\delta}{F^2} d & = Q = 
	\frac{\rho_0+\delta}{2} (U_+^\dagger)^2 - \frac{1}{2} (U_-^\dagger)^2 + \frac{\rho_0-1+\delta}{F^2} d^\dagger. 
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\item \textup{(Conservation of mass flux)}  The conservation of the mass flux in the upper and lower layers implies that
\begin{equation}
\label{conservation of mass flux} 
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
	 Ue^{-\frac{\mu}{2} U^2 } \frac{ 1 - e^{- \frac{\mu d}{F^2}} }{1- e^{- \frac{\mu d^\dagger}{F^2} }} & =
	U_-^\dagger e^{-\frac{\mu}{2} (U_-^\dagger)^2 } \\
	 U e^{-(\rho_0+\delta)\frac{\mu}{2}U^2} \frac{  e^{\frac{(\rho_0+\delta) \mu (1-d)}{F^2}} - 1}{e^{\frac{(\rho_0+\delta)\mu (1-d^\dagger)}{F^2} } -1 } & =
	U_+^\dagger e^{-(\rho_0+\delta) \frac{\mu}{2}(U_+^\dagger)^2  }. 
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{equation}
\item \textup{(Conservation of flow force)} Denoting by $R_\pm$ the (trivial) density for the upstream flow in $\mathscr{D}_\pm$ and $R_\pm^\dagger$ the trivial density for the downstream flow, then conservation of the flow force implies 
\begin{equation}
\label{conservation of flow force}
\begin{aligned}
	 & \left(1 + \mu U^2 \right) \frac{R_-(d) - R_-(0)}{\mu} + \left(1 + (\rho_0+\delta) \mu U^2 \right) \frac{R_+(1) - R_+(d)}{(\rho_0+\delta)^2 \mu}  \\
		 & \qquad\qquad = \left(1 + \mu (U_-^\dagger)^2 \right) \frac{R_-^\dagger(d^\dagger) - R_-^\dagger(0)}{\mu}     + \left(1 + (\rho_0+\delta) \mu (U_+^\dagger)^2 \right) \frac{R_+^\dagger(1) -  R_+^\dagger(d^\dagger)}{(\rho_0+\delta)^2 \mu}.
		\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
The conservation of energy~\eqref{conservation of energy} simply results from evaluating the Bernoulli condition~\eqref{steady two-phase dynamic} with the hypothetical upstream and downstream flows. Likewise, the first equation in~\eqref{conservation of mass flux} is just found by rearranging the equality
\[
	U \left( R_-(d) - R_-(0) \right) = \lim_{x\to -\infty} \mathfrak{m}_-(x) = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \mathfrak{m}_-(x) = U_-^\dagger \left( R_-^\dagger(d^\dagger) - R_-^\dagger(0) \right).
\]
The second conservation of mass equation in~\eqref{conservation of mass flux} follows from the same argument but in the upper layer.


Equating the upstream and downstream limits of the force results in the identity 
\begin{align*}
	\mathfrak{s} & = \int_0^d \left( P_-(R_-) + R_- U^2 \,  \right) dy + \int_d^1 \left( P_+(R_+) + R_+ U^2 \,  \right) dy \\
		& = \int_0^{d^\dagger} \left( P_-(R_-^\dagger) + R_-^\dagger (U_-^\dagger)^2 \,  \right) dy + \int_{d^\dagger}^1 \left( P_+(R_+^\dagger) + R_+^\dagger (U_+^\dagger)^2 \,  \right) dy.
\end{align*}
Using the explicit forms of the pressure and of the upstream~\eqref{upstream density} and downstream densities~\eqref{downstream density}, these integrals can be evaluated to yield
\begin{align*}
	 & \left(\frac{1}{\mu_-} + U^2 \right) \frac{1}{\mu_-} (R_-(d) - R_-(0)) + \left(\frac{1}{\mu_+} + U^2 \right) \frac{1}{\mu_+} ( R_+(1) - R_+(d) ) \\
		\qquad & = \left(\frac{1}{\mu_-} + (U_-^\dagger)^2 \right) \frac{1}{\mu_-}   (R_-^\dagger(d^\dagger) - R_-^\dagger(0) )   + \left(\frac{1}{\mu_+} + (U_+^\dagger)^2 \right) \frac{1}{\mu_+} (R_+^\dagger(1) -  R_+^\dagger(d^\dagger) ).
\end{align*}
Multiplying by $\mu$, we arrive at the identity~\eqref{conservation of flow force}.

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{large incompressible fronts theorem}
  Fix a $\beta \in (0,1)$ and density ratio $\rho_0 \in (0,1)$.  There exist a global curve ${\mathscr K}_\mathrm{f}$ of solutions to the incompressible internal wave problem \eqref{stream function problem} that admits the global $C^0$ parameterization
  \[
   	{\mathscr K}_\mathrm{f} = \left\{ \left(\psi(s), h(s), d(s) \right) : s \in \mathbb{R} \right\}
\]
with $(\psi(0), h(0), d(0)) = (0,0,1/(1+\sqrt{\rho_0}))$. 
  At each parameter value $s$, the Froude number and downstream depth and velocities are given by
  \begin{equation}
  \label{incompressible conjugate flow relations}
  	F^2  = \frac{1-\sqrt{\rho_0}}{1+\sqrt{\rho_0}}, \qquad d^\dagger = \frac{1}{1+\sqrt{\rho_0}}, \qquad U_\pm^\dagger = U_\pm^\dagger(s) = U \frac{d_\pm(s)}{d_\pm^\dagger},
\end{equation}
where $d_-(s) \colonequals d(s)$, $d_+(s) \colonequals 1-d(s)$, $d_-^\dagger \colonequals d^\dagger$, and $d_+^\dagger \colonequals 1 - d^\dagger$,
and
  \[ 
    \psi(s) \in C_\mathrm{b}^{2+\beta}(\overline{\mathscr{D}_+(s)}) \cap C_\mathrm{b}^{2+\beta}(\overline{\mathscr{D}_-(s)}) \cap C_\mathrm{b}^0(\overline{\mathscr{D}(s)}), \qquad h(s) \in C_\mathrm{b}^{2+\beta}(\mathbb{R}), 
  \]
  where $\mathscr{D}(s)$ denotes the fluid domain corresponding to $h(s)$ and $d(s)$.  Moreover, $h(s)$ is locally real analytic, and $\psi_\pm(s)$ admits an analytic extension to $\overline{\mathscr{D}_\pm(s)}$.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\rm(\alph*)]
  \item \label{global bore monotone part} \textup{(Strict monotonicity)} Each solution on ${\mathscr K}_\mathrm{f}$ is a strictly monotone front:
    \begin{equation}
      \begin{aligned}
        (\operatorname{sgn}{s}) \partial_x h(s) &< 0 & \qquad &  \textrm{on } \mathbb{R}, \\
        (\operatorname{sgn}{s}) \partial_x \psi_\pm(s) & < 0 & \qquad & \textrm{in } \overline{\mathscr{D}_\pm(s)} \setminus \mathscr{B}_\pm , \\
        \partial_y \psi_\pm(s) & < 0 & \qquad & \textrm{in } \overline{\mathscr{D}_\pm(s)}.
      \end{aligned} \label{monotonicity Euler variables}
    \end{equation}
  \item \textup{(Limits)} \label{front limits part} In the limit along the elevation front portion of ${\mathscr K}_\mathrm{f}$, overturning occurs in that 
    \begin{equation}
      \limsup_{s \to -\infty} \| \partial_x h(s) \|_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R})} = \infty. \label{overturning} 
    \end{equation}
	 In the limit along depression front portion of ${\mathscr K}_\mathrm{f}$, either \emph{overturning} occurs or the interface comes arbitrarily close to the upper wall: 
  \begin{equation}
    \limsup_{s \to \infty} \| \partial_x h(s) \|_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R})} = \infty \quad \textrm{or} \quad \lim_{s \to \infty} d(s) = 1. \label{depression limit alternatives} 
  \end{equation}
  	\item \label{front discrete set part} There exist a discrete set $S \subset \mathbb{R}$ such that $(\psi(s), h(s), d(s))$ is nondegenerate for $s \not\in S$. 
  \end{enumerate}

\label{good unknowns lemma}
Suppose that 
\[
	(u,w) \in (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(\,\cdot\,; 0,0)^{-1}(0), \qquad (\tilde u, w) \in (\tilde\mathscr F,\tilde\mathscr G)(\,\cdot\,;d)^{-1}(0)

are incompressible fronts in the relative potential and relative stream function formulation, respectively.

		\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_\pm(\tilde u,w) \begin{pmatrix} \dot{\tilde{v}} \\ \dot w \end{pmatrix} \colonequals \begin{pmatrix} \dot{\tilde v} + \frac{ (1\mp \eta) (\partial_\eta \tilde u)}{\partial_\eta f_{2\pm}}  \dot w \\   \dot w\end{pmatrix}.

	\item 
	\label{equivalence of linearizations part} For all $(\dot v,\dot w) \in \mathscr X_\mathrm{f}$ with $\Delta^w \dot v = 0$ in $\mathcal{D}$, we have the identity
	 \[
 		D_{(\tilde u,w)} ( \tilde{\mathscr F},\tilde{\mathscr G})(\mathcal{C}^w u,w;d)  \tilde{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{C}^w u,w) \begin{pmatrix} \mathcal{C}^w \dot v \\ \dot w \end{pmatrix}  = D_{(u,w)}( {\mathscr F},{\mathscr G})(u,w;0,0)  \mathcal{T}(u,w) \begin{pmatrix} \dot v \\ \dot w \end{pmatrix}. 
 	\]

		D_{(u,w)} \mathscr F(u,w;0,0) \mathcal{T}(u,w)\begin{pmatrix} \dot v \\ \dot w \end{pmatrix} & = \Delta^w \dot v \\
		D_{(\tilde u,w)} \tilde\mathscr F(\tilde u,w;d) \tilde{\mathcal{T}}(\tilde u,w)\begin{pmatrix} \dot{\tilde v} \\ \dot w \end{pmatrix} & = \Delta^w \dot{\tilde v}

\label{nondegeneracy front lemma}
Suppose now that $(\tilde u, w) \in \tilde{\mathcal{O}}_\mathrm{f}$ is a nondegenerate solution of the stream function problem in that

From Lemma~\ref{correspondence lemma} and Lemma~\ref{good unknowns lemma}, it follows that $(\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(u,w; 0,0) = 0$ and the kernel of $D_{(u,w)} (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(u,w;0,0)$ is trivial. It remains only to show that $D_{(u,w)} (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(u,w;0,0)$ is surjective. With that in mind, let $Y = (Y_1, Y_2, Y_3,Y_4) \in \mathscr Y_\mathrm{f}$ be given; we must prove that there exists $(\dot v, \dot w) \in \mathscr X_\mathrm{f}$ so that

	D_{(u,w)} (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(u,w;0,0) \mathcal{T}(u,w) 
		\begin{pmatrix} 
			\dot v \\ 
			\dot w 
		\end{pmatrix} 
		= Y.

First, we wish to solve the inhomogeneous problem in the interior with simplified boundary conditions. We claim that there exists $\dot v_1 \in \dot C_\mathrm{f}^{2+\beta}(\overline{\mathcal{D}_+}) \cap  \dot C_\mathrm{f}^{2+\beta}(\overline{\mathcal{D}_-})$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{inhomogeneous Poisson equation}
	\left\{ \begin{aligned}
		\Delta^w \dot v_1 & = Y_1 & \qquad & \textrm{in } \mathcal{D}_+ \cup \mathcal{D}_- \\
		\partial_\eta \dot v_1 & = 0 &\qquad& \textrm{on } \Gamma_0^+ \cup \Gamma_0^-.
	\end{aligned} \right.
\end{equation}
To see this, consider first the problem in the physical variables. Since $w \in C^{2+\beta}_\mathrm{f}(\mathbb{R})$, we can extend $Y_{1\pm} \circ f^{-1}$ to a function $Z^\pm \in C_\mathrm{b}^{\beta}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})$ vanishing in a neighborhood of $\mathscr{B}_\mp$ and in the upstream limit $x \to -\infty$. The Laplacian in $\mathscr{D}$ can be inverted explicitly using an integral kernel. Indeed, setting 
\[
	V^\pm(x,y)  \colonequals \int_{-\infty}^\infty \int_{0}^1  {G}^\pm(x,y; \, x^\prime, y^\prime) Z^\pm(x^\prime, y^\prime) \, d y^\prime \, dx^\prime,
\]
for the functions ${G}^\pm$ given by
\begin{align*}
	{G}^+(x,y; x^\prime, y^\prime) & \colonequals \frac{1}{4\pi} \log{\left( \frac{\cosh{(\frac{\pi}{2} (x-x^\prime))} - \cos{(\frac{\pi}{2} (y+y^\prime))}}{\cosh{( \frac{\pi}{2} (x-x^\prime))} - \cos{(\frac{\pi}{2} (y-y^\prime))}}\right)} \\
	{G}^-(x,y; x^\prime, y^\prime) & \colonequals \frac{1}{4\pi} \log{\left( \frac{\cosh{(\frac{\pi}{2} (x-x^\prime))} - \cos{(\frac{\pi}{2} (y+y^\prime))}}{\cosh{(\frac{\pi}{2} (x-x^\prime))} - \cos{(\frac{\pi}{2} (y-y^\prime))}}\right)},
\end{align*}
it is easy to confirm that $V^\pm \in C_\mathrm{b}^{2+\beta}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})$,  and
\[
	\left\{ \begin{aligned}
		\Delta {V}^\pm &  = Z^\pm &\qquad& \textrm{in } \mathscr{D} \\
		 V^\pm & = 0 &\qquad& \textrm{on } \mathscr{B}_\mp \\
		 \partial_y V^\pm & = 0 & \qquad & \textrm{on } \mathscr{B}_\pm.
	\end{aligned} \right.
\]
The exponential decay of the kernels $G^\pm$ in the $x$- and $x^\prime$-directions, moreover, ensures that
\[
	\lim_{x \to - \infty} \partial^\gamma V^\pm(x, \,\cdot\,) = 0\quad \textrm{for all } |\gamma| \leq 2, \quad \textrm{and } \lim_{x \to +\infty} \partial^\gamma V^\pm(x,\,\cdot\,) \textrm{ exists for all } |\gamma| \leq 2.
\]

Now, taking $v^\pm \colonequals V^\pm \circ f |_{\mathcal{D}_\pm}$, we find that $\Delta^w v^\pm = Y_1$ in $\mathcal{D}_\pm$ , and $\partial_\eta v^\pm = 0$ on $\Gamma_0^\pm$. 
Finally, let $\dot v_1$  be the function whose restriction to $\mathcal{D}_\pm$ is $v^\pm - v^\pm(0,\pm1)$; this choice furnishes a solution to the Poisson problem~\eqref{inhomogeneous Poisson equation} lying in the space $\dot C_\mathrm{f}^{2+\beta}(\overline{\mathcal{D}_+}) \cap  \dot C_\mathrm{f}^{2+\beta}(\overline{\mathcal{D}_-})$. 

On the other hand, Lemma~\ref{good unknowns lemma}~\ref{equivalence of linearizations part} and the nondegeneracy of $(\tilde u,w)$ also ensures the existence of a unique $(\dot v_2, \dot w_2) \in \mathscr X_\mathrm{f}$ such that 
\[
	 D_{( u, w)} ( \mathscr F,  \mathscr G)( u, w;0,0) {\mathcal{T}}(u, w) \begin{pmatrix} \dot{v}_2 \\ \dot w_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ Y_2 \\ Y_3 \\ Y_4 \end{pmatrix} 
	 - 
	 \begin{pmatrix} 
	 	0 \\
		D_{(u, w)} \mathscr G(u,w;0,0) {\mathcal{T}}( u, w) \begin{pmatrix} \dot v_1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} 
	\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Simply taking $\dot v = \dot v_1 + \dot v_2$ and $\dot w = \dot w_2$ completes the proof of the claim and therefore the lemma.

<!-- theorem-9: body_md -->
\label{front theorem}
Suppose that for a fixed $\rho_0$ and $d$, $(u_0,w_0) \in \mathcal{O}_\mathrm{f}$ is a solution to the incompressible internal front problem~\eqref{flattened equations} which is nondegenerate in that $(\mathcal{C}^w u_0, w_0)$ is a nondegenerate solution of the corresponding stream function problem~\eqref{definition nondegenerate stream function}. There exists a two-dimensional real-analytic manifold $\mathscr{M}_\mathrm{f}^{\mathrm{loc}} $ of solutions to the compressible internal front problem \eqref{steady two-phase system} with the global parameterization
\[
	\mathscr{M}_\mathrm{f}^{\mathrm{loc}}  = \left\{ (u^{\mu,\delta}, w^{\mu,\delta}; \mu,\delta) \in \mathscr X_\mathrm{f} \times \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} : |\mu| < \mu_0,~|\delta|< \delta_0 \right\} \subset \mathcal{O}_\mathrm{f} \cap (\mathscr F, \mathscr G)^{-1}(0),
\] 
for some $\mu_0 > 0$ and $\delta_0 > 0$. The solutions along $\mathscr{M}_\mathrm{f}^{\mathrm{loc}} $ admit the infinite power series representation 
\begin{equation}
\label{JRE front}
\begin{aligned}
	 u^{\mu,\delta} & = u_0 + \sum_{m+n \geq 1} \mu^m \delta^n u_{m,n}, \qquad w^{\mu,\delta} = w_0 + \sum_{m+n \geq 1} \mu^m \delta^n w_{m,n} \\
	 (U_+^\dagger)^{\mu,\delta} & = U \frac{1-d}{1-d_0^\dagger} + \mu \frac{\rho_0 U (1-d)(d-d_0^\dagger)\left( F^2 U^2 (d+d_0^\dagger-2) - (1-d_0^\dagger)^2 \right)}{2 F^2 (1-d_0^\dagger)^3}  + O(\mu^2 + \delta) \\
	 (U_-^\dagger)^{\mu,\delta} & = U\frac{d}{d_0^\dagger} + \mu \frac{Ud \left( F^2 U^2 d^2 -F^2 U^2 (d_0^\dagger)^2 - d (d_0^\dagger)^2 + (d_0^\dagger)^3 \right)}{2F^2 (d_0^\dagger)^3} +O(\mu^2+\delta),
	 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $F^2 = \frac{1-\sqrt{\rho_0}}{1+\sqrt{\rho_0}}$ and $d_0^\dagger = 1/(1+\sqrt{\rho_0})$ are the Froude number and downstream depth for the incompressible case, and the leading-order terms $(u_{1,0}, w_{1,0})$ are the unique solutions in $\mathscr X_\mathrm{f}$ of the linear problem 
\[
	D_{(u,w)}(\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(u_0,w_0;0,0) \begin{pmatrix} u_{1,0} \\ w_{1,0} \end{pmatrix} = -D_\mu (\mathscr F,\mathscr G)(u_0,w_0;0,0).
\]

<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
The existence of the manifold $\mathscr{M}_\mathrm{f}^{\mathrm{loc}} $ follows from Lemma~\ref{nondegeneracy front lemma} and the (analytic) implicit function theorem. To derive the specific Janzen--Rayleigh asymptotics claimed in~\eqref{JRE front}, we do an expansion of the conjugate flow equations in Corollary~\ref{conjugate flow corollary} at the starting incompressible flow. 

Set $\delta = 0$, and let $\dot U_\pm^\dagger \colonequals \partial_{\mu}|_{\mu = 0} (U_\pm^\dagger)^{\mu,0}$. Consider first the conservation of mass flux in the lower layer in~\eqref{conservation of mass flux}, which here implies
\[
		 U e^{-\frac{\mu}{2} U^2  } \frac{ 1 - e^{- \frac{\mu d}{F^2}} }{1- e^{- \frac{\mu d^\dagger}{F^2} }}  =
	U_-^\dagger e^{-\frac{\mu}{2} (U_-^\dagger)^2 } \qquad \textrm{for } d^\dagger = (d^\dagger)^{\mu,0}, \quad U_-^\dagger = (U_-^\dagger)^{\mu,0},\quad 0 \leq \mu \ll 1.
\]
Note that we already know that $(U_-^\dagger)^{0,0} = U d/d_0^\dagger$ by the conjugate flow equations in the incompressible case; see Remark~\ref{conjugate flow remark}. Then, differentiating both sides of the above identity in $\mu$ and setting $\mu = 0$ (and using that $\partial_\mu (d^\dagger)^{\mu,0}|_{\mu=0} = 0$), we eventually arrive at 
\[
	U \frac{d}{d_0^\dagger} \left( \frac{d_0^\dagger -d}{2F^2} - \frac{U^2}{2} \right) = \dot U_-^\dagger - \frac{1}{2} \left(U \frac{d}{d_0^\dagger} \right)^3. 
\]
Rearranging this and simplifying leads to the $O(\mu)$ term for the expansion of $U_-^\dagger$ in~\eqref{JRE front}. The same type of manipulation applied to the conservation of mass flux in the upper layer gives the corresponding formula for $\dot U_+^\dagger$.

<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
In principle, one can also determine asymptotic expansions (to any order) for the downstream depth $(d^\dagger)^{\mu,\delta}$ of the corresponding fronts along $\mathscr{M}_\mathrm{f}^{\mathrm{loc}} $. However, because $d_0^\dagger$ is a double root of conservation of flow force equation when $\mu = 0$, it turns out that computing even the $O(\mu)$ term in the expansion of $(d^\dagger)^{\mu,\delta}$ requires expanding the compressible conjugate flow system to at least second order. The resulting equations are complicated enough as to be uninstructive, so we do not provide them here. In the same way, one can also expand all of the solutions in $\delta$.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{front corollary}
Let $\{\mathscr{A}_j^\mathrm{f}\}$  be the (at most countable) distinguished arcs of the global family of incompressible fronts ${\mathscr K}_\mathrm{f}$ given in Theorem~\ref{large incompressible fronts theorem}. There exists a corresponding at most countable ordered list $\{ \mathscr{M}_j^\mathrm{f}\}$ of three-dimensional real-analytic manifolds of compressible fronts. Each $\mathscr{M}_j^\mathrm{f}$ has the structure of a (real-analytic) fiber bundle over the base $\mathscr{A}_j^\mathrm{f}$.
