<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:nonhomoge IC}
The first interface condition of \eqref{eq:model} can be written in the more general form
$[\![\bm{u}]\!]+K\{\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u})\bm{n}\}_\omega=\tilde\bm{g}_D$ with the weighted average
$\{\bm{v}\}_\omega=\omega_1\bm{v}_1+\omega_2\bm{v}_2$, $\omega_1+\omega_2=1$. Since the traction jump
is prescribed, $\{\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u})\bm{n}\}_\omega=\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u}_1)\bm{n}-\omega_2\bm{g}_N$, so that all these
formulations coincide up to a modification of the right data with $\bm{g}_D=\tilde\bm{g}_D+\omega_2K\bm{g}_N$, and we work with
$\omega=(1,0)$ throughout. The choice \eqref{eq:softside} of the softer side is the natural
one in the highly contrasted case $\mu_1\ll\mu_2$, in the spirit of
\cite{BCDE2021,ErnStephansenZunino2009}.

<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:cut}
There is $\zeta\in(0,1)$ such that, for any $T\in\mathcal{T}_h^\Gamma$ and all $i=1,2$, there is
$\check\bm{x}_{T^i}\in T^i$ such that $B(\check\bm{x}_{T^i},\zeta h_T)\subset T^i$.
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- assumption-2: body_md -->
\label{ass:resolve}
There is $\gamma\in(0,1)$ such that, for any $T\in\mathcal{T}_h^\Gamma$, there is a point
$\check\bm{x}_T\in\mathbb{R}^d$ such that, setting $T^\dagger:=B(\check\bm{x}_T,\gamma^{-1}h_T)$,
the following holds: (i) $T\subset T^\dagger$; (ii) for any $\bm{s}\in T^\Gamma$,
$d(\check\bm{x}_T,T_{\bm{s}}\Gamma)\ge\gamma h_T$, where $T_{\bm{s}}\Gamma$ is the tangent plane
to $\Gamma$ at $\bm{s}$; (iii) for any $F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial T}$, there is
$\bm{x}_F\in T^\dagger$ such that $d(\bm{x}_F,F)\ge\gamma h_T$.
<!-- end assumption-2 -->
<!-- assumption-3: body_md -->
\label{ass:grading}
For any $T\in\mathcal{T}_h$, let the neighbouring layers $\triangle_j(T)\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ be defined
by induction as $\triangle_0(T):=T$ and
$\triangle_{j+1}(T):=\bigcup\{\overline{T'}\mid T'\in\mathcal{T}_h,\ \overline{T'}\cap
\overline{\triangle_j(T)}\ne\emptyset\}$. There is $n_0\in\mathbb{N}$ such that, for all
$T\in\mathcal{T}_h$, the ball $T^\dagger$ of \Cref{ass:resolve} satisfies
$T^\dagger\subset\triangle_{n_0}(T)$.
<!-- end assumption-3 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:wp}
For any $T\in\mathcal{T}_h$ and any $\hat{\bm{v}}_T\in\hat{\bm{V}}_T$, the linear systems \eqref{eq:E1} and \eqref{eq:E2}
have a unique solution.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Both are square linear systems, so it suffices to prove injectivity. For \eqref{eq:E2} this
is clear. For \eqref{eq:E1}, let $\bm {\mathcal {E}}:=\bm {\mathcal {E}}_{T^1}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)$ solve \eqref{eq:E1} with zero
right-hand side and take $\bm{\tau}=\mathbb{C}_1\bm {\mathcal {E}}=\bm{G}_{T^1}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)$, which is an admissible test
function. This gives
\[
(\bm {\mathcal {E}},\mathbb{C}_1\bm {\mathcal {E}})_{T^1}
+\big\langle(\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK)K\,\mathbb{C}_1\bm {\mathcal {E}}\bm{n},\mathbb{C}_1\bm {\mathcal {E}}\bm{n}\big\rangle_{T^\Gamma}=0 .
\]
Since $\mathbb{C}_1$ is symmetric positive definite and, by \eqref{eq:ImSK} below,
$(\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK)K=\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}S_hK$ is symmetric positive semi-definite, both
terms are nonnegative, whence $\bm {\mathcal {E}}=\bm{0}$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:regimes}
The definition \eqref{eq:Sh} is designed so that one single formulation covers the whole
range of compliancies. When $\alpha=\beta=0$, i.e.\ for a perfectly bonded interface, one has
$K= \mathsf{0}$, hence $\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK=\bm{\mathbb{I}}$ and $S_h=\delta h_T^{-1}\bm{\mathbb{I}}$: the interface terms in
\eqref{eq:E1} then enforce weakly the continuity of the displacement, and $s_T^\Gamma$ is a
Nitsche-type penalty of size $\delta/h_T$. In the opposite regime
$\alpha,\beta\gg h_T/\delta$, one has $S_h\simeqK^{-1}$, so that $s_T^\Gamma$ represents
the physical interface stiffness, and $\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK\simeq \mathsf{0}$, so that the interface
contributions to \eqref{eq:E1} and \eqref{eq:aT} disappear; in the limit
$\alpha,\beta\to+\infty$ the discrete problem decouples into two independent problems with a
traction-free boundary condition on $\Gamma$, which is the correct behaviour of
\eqref{eq:model} in that limit. Both limits, and the degenerate cases $\alpha=0$ or
$\beta=0$, are uniformly accessible; this is confirmed numerically in \Cref{ssec:robust}.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:Sh}
Let $S_h=(h_T\delta^{-1}\bm{\mathbb{I}}+K)^{-1}$ and let $|\cdot|$ denote the matrix norm induced by
the Euclidean norm on $\mathbb{R}^d$. Then $S_h$, $K$ and $S_hK$ are symmetric and
commute, $S_hK$ is positive semi-definite, and
\begin{align}
\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK&=\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}S_h,\label{eq:ImSK}\\
|S_h|&\le\tfrac{\delta}{h_T},\label{eq:Shbound}\\
|\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK|\le1,&\qquad |S_hK|\le1,\label{eq:unitbound}\\
|(\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK)K|&\le\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}.\label{eq:ImSKK}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $K$ is symmetric positive semi-definite, it is diagonalisable in an orthonormal basis
with nonnegative eigenvalues $\{k_j\}_{j=1}^d$ (for the isotropic compliancy considered here,
$k_1=\beta$ in the normal direction and $k_j=\alpha$, $j\ge2$, in the tangential ones). In
that basis, $S_h$ is diagonal with entries $(h_T/\delta+k_j)^{-1}$, whence the symmetry,
the commutation and \eqref{eq:Shbound}. Multiplying $S_h(h_T\delta^{-1}\bm{\mathbb{I}}+K)=\bm{\mathbb{I}}$ by
$S_h^{-1}$ on the left gives \eqref{eq:ImSK}. The eigenvalues of $S_hK$ are
$k_j/(h_T/\delta+k_j)\in[0,1)$, which gives \eqref{eq:unitbound}, and those of
$(\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK)K=\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}S_hK$ are
$\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}k_j/(h_T/\delta+k_j)\in[0,h_T/\delta)$, which gives \eqref{eq:ImSKK}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:disc}
Let \Cref{ass:cut} be fulfilled and let $l\in\mathbb{N}$. There is $c_{\rm disc}>0$,
depending on $\rho,\zeta$ and $l$, such that for any $T\in\mathcal{T}_h$, all $i=1,2$ and any
$v_{T^i}\in\mathbb{P}^{l}(T^i)$, the following inequalities hold true:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{(discrete trace inequality)}
$\|v_{T^i}\|_{(\partial T)^i\cup T^\Gamma}\le c_{\rm disc}h_T^{-1/2}\|v_{T^i}\|_{T^i}$;
\item \emph{(discrete inverse inequality)}
$\|\nabla v_{T^i}\|_{T^i}\le c_{\rm disc}h_T^{-1}\|v_{T^i}\|_{T^i}$;
\item \emph{(discrete Poincar\'e inequality)} if $(v_{T^i},1)_{B(\check\bm{x}_{T^i},h_T)}=0$,
then $\|v_{T^i}\|_{T^i}\le c_{\rm disc}h_T\|\nabla v_{T^i}\|_{T^i}$;
\item \emph{(discrete Korn inequality)} for $\bm{v}_{T^i}\in{\bm{P}}^{l}(T^i)$,
$\|\nabla\bm{v}_{T^i}\|_{T^i}\le c_{\rm disc}\|\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\|_{T^i}$ whenever
$(\bm{v}_{T^i},\bm{r})_{B(\check\bm{x}_{T^i},h_T)}=0$ for all $\bm{r}\in RM$.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:mtr}
Let \Cref{ass:resolve} be fulfilled. There is $c_{\rm mtr}>0$, depending on $\rho$ and
$\gamma$, such that for any $T\in\mathcal{T}_h$, any $v\in H^1(T^\dagger)$ and all $i=1,2$,
\[
\|v\|_{(\partial T)^i\cup T^\Gamma}\le c_{\rm mtr}\Big(h_T^{-1/2}\|v\|_{T^\dagger}
+\|v\|_{T^\dagger}^{1/2}\|\nabla v\|_{T^\dagger}^{1/2}\Big).
\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:norm}
The $\mu$-weighted face contribution involves the full difference
$\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i}$, whereas the $\lambda$-weighted one involves the
stabilisation quantity ${\bf S}(\hat{\bm{v}}_{T^i})=\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i}\bm{v}_{T^i}$
only. This distinction, which is the one made in the fitted case \cite{HuangBurman2024}, is
what makes the constants in \Cref{lem:locstab} and in \Cref{thm:stab} independent of the
Lam\'e coefficients; see also \Cref{rem:locking}. Since $\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i}$ is an
$L^2$-orthogonal projection and $\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}$ is a polynomial of degree $k$ on each
subface, one has $\|S(\hat{\bm{v}}_{T^i})\|_{(\partial T)^i}\le
\|\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i}\|_{(\partial T)^i}$, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stabnorm}
s_T^{\partial T}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T,\hat{\bm{v}}_T)\le|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{v}}_T|\!|\!|_{a_T}^2 .
\end{equation}
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:proj}
Let $k\ge1$. For any $T\in\mathcal{T}_h$, all $i=1,2$ and any $\bm{v}_{T^i}\in{\bm{P}}^{k+1}(T^i)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proj}
h_T^{-1}\big\|\bm{v}_{T^i}-\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i}\bm{v}_{T^i}\big\|_{(\partial T)^i}^2
\lesssim\|\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\|_{T^i}^2 .
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\bm{\Pi}_{RM}$ denote the $L^2(T^i)$-orthogonal projection onto $RM$ and set
$\bm\psi:=\bm{v}_{T^i}-\bm{\Pi}_{RM}(\bm{v}_{T^i})$. Since $RM\subset{\bm{P}}^{1}(T^i)$ and $k\ge1$, the
trace on any subface $F^i$ of a rigid-body motion belongs to ${\bm{P}}^{k}(F^i)$, so that
$\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i}\bm{\Pi}_{RM}\bm{v}_{T^i}=\bm{\Pi}_{RM}\bm{v}_{T^i}$ and the left-hand
side of \eqref{eq:proj} is unchanged if $\bm{v}_{T^i}$ is replaced by $\bm\psi$. Using that
$\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i}$ is an $L^2$-orthogonal projection, then the discrete trace, the
discrete Poincar\'e and the discrete Korn inequalities of \Cref{lem:disc}, which are licit
because $\bm{\psi}$ is orthogonal to $RM$ (hence to the constants) in $L^2(T^i)$, we obtain
\[
h_T^{-1}\|(\bm{I}-\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i})\bm\psi\|_{(\partial T)^i}^2
\le h_T^{-1}\|\bm\psi\|_{(\partial T)^i}^2
\lesssim h_T^{-2}\|\bm\psi\|_{T^i}^2\lesssim\|\nabla\bm\psi\|_{T^i}^2
\lesssim\|\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm\psi)\|_{T^i}^2=\|\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\|_{T^i}^2,
\]
where the last equality holds because every element of $RM$ has a vanishing symmetric
gradient.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:locstab}
There are $0<\alpha_\flat\le\alpha_\sharp<+\infty$ such that, for any $T\in\mathcal{T}_h$ and any
$\hat{\bm{v}}_T\in\hat{\bm{V}}_T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:locstab}
\alpha_\flat|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{v}}_T|\!|\!|_{a_T}^2\le\mathcal{N}_T(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)\le\alpha_\sharp|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{v}}_T|\!|\!|_{a_T}^2,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:NT}
\mathcal{N}_T(\hat{\bm{v}}_T):=\sum_{i=1,2}\Big(2\mu_i\|\bm {\mathcal {E}}_{T^i}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)\|_{T^i}^2
+\lambda_i\|D_{T^i}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)\|_{T^i}^2
+\tfrac{2\mu_i+d\lambda_i}{h_T}\|{\bf S}(\hat{\bm{v}}_{T^i})\|_{(\partial T)^i}^2\Big)
+s_T^\Gamma(\bm{v}_T,\bm{v}_T).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
\emph{Lower bound.} We first bound $\sum_{i=1,2}\|\mathbb{C}_i^{1/2}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\|_{T^i}^2$. Testing
\eqref{eq:E1}--\eqref{eq:E2} with $\bm{\tau}=\mathbb{C}_i\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})$ and moving the interface
terms to the right-hand side, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1,2}\|\mathbb{C}_i^{1/2}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\|_{T^i}^2
&=\sum_{i=1,2}\Big[\big(\bm {\mathcal {E}}_{T^i}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T),\mathbb{C}_i\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\big)_{T^i}
-\big\langle\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i},\mathbb{C}_i\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\bm{n}_T
\big\rangle_{(\partial T)^i}\Big]\\
&\quad+\big\langle(\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK)K\bm{G}_{T^1}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)\bm{n},
\mathbb{C}_1\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^1})\bm{n}\big\rangle_{T^\Gamma}\\
&\quad+\big\langle(\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK)[\![\bm{v}_T]\!]_\Gamma,
\mathbb{C}_1\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^1})\bm{n}\big\rangle_{T^\Gamma}.
\end{align*}
Write $X:=\big(\sum_{i=1,2}\|\mathbb{C}_i^{1/2}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\|_{T^i}^2\big)^{1/2}$. The first term is
bounded by $\|\mathbb{C}_i^{1/2}\bm {\mathcal {E}}_{T^i}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)\|_{T^i}X$. In the second one,
$\mathbb{C}_i\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\bm{n}_T$ has its components in ${\bm{P}}^{k}(F^i)$ on every subface, so that
$\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i}$ may be replaced by ${\bf S}(\hat{\bm{v}}_{T^i})$; using
\eqref{eq:Csquare} and the discrete trace inequality, it is bounded by
\[
c_{\rm disc}\Big(\tfrac{2\mu_i+d\lambda_i}{h_T}\Big)^{1/2}
\|{\bf S}(\hat{\bm{v}}_{T^i})\|_{(\partial T)^i}\,X .
\]
For the third term, we use \eqref{eq:ImSKK}, \eqref{eq:Csquare} and the discrete trace
inequality, together with $\delta=2\mu_1+d\lambda_1$, which give the bound
\[
\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}(2\mu_1+d\lambda_1)c_{\rm disc}^2h_T^{-1}
\|\mathbb{C}_1^{1/2}\bm {\mathcal {E}}_{T^1}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)\|_{T^1}X
=c_{\rm disc}^2\|\mathbb{C}_1^{1/2}\bm {\mathcal {E}}_{T^1}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)\|_{T^1}X .
\]
For the fourth one, we use the factorisation
$\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK=\big(\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}\big)^{1/2}
\big(\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}S_h\big)^{1/2}S_h^{1/2}$ of \Cref{lem:Sh} together with
$\big|\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}S_h\big|\le1$, which yields
\[
\Big(\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}\Big)^{1/2}\|S_h^{1/2}[\![\bm{v}_T]\!]_\Gamma\|_{T^\Gamma}
\|\mathbb{C}_1\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^1})\bm{n}\|_{T^\Gamma}\le c_{\rm disc}
\|S_h^{1/2}[\![\bm{v}_T]\!]_\Gamma\|_{T^\Gamma}X .
\]
Collecting the four bounds and dividing by $X$ shows that $X^2$ is bounded, up to a
constant, by $\mathcal{N}_T(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)$, which controls the first two contributions to \eqref{eq:tnorm}.
It remains to bound the face contributions. The $\lambda$-weighted ones are directly part of
$\mathcal{N}_T(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)$. For the $\mu$-weighted ones, we write
$\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i}={\bf S}(\hat{\bm{v}}_{T^i})+(\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i}\bm{v}_{T^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i})$
and invoke \Cref{lem:proj}, which gives
\[
\frac{2\mu_i}{h_T}\|\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i}\|_{(\partial T)^i}^2
\lesssim\frac{2\mu_i}{h_T}\|{\bf S}(\hat{\bm{v}}_{T^i})\|_{(\partial T)^i}^2
+2\mu_i\|\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\|_{T^i}^2 ,
\]
and both right-hand sides have already been bounded. Note that no $\lambda$ appears in this
last step, which is the reason for the definition \eqref{eq:tnorm}.

\emph{Upper bound.} By \eqref{eq:stabnorm}, the third contribution to
$\mathcal{N}_T(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)$ is bounded by $|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{v}}_T|\!|\!|_{a_T}^2$, and so is $s_T^\Gamma$. For the first
two, we test \eqref{eq:E1}--\eqref{eq:E2} with $\bm{\tau}=\mathbb{C}_i\bm {\mathcal {E}}_{T^i}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)$ and argue exactly as
above, replacing $\mathbb{C}_i\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})$ by $\mathbb{C}_i\bm {\mathcal {E}}_{T^i}(\hat{\bm{v}}_T)$ and using
$\sum_{i=1,2}\|\mathbb{C}_i^{1/2}\bm {\mathcal {E}}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\|_{T^i}^2\le a_T(\hat{\bm{v}}_T,\hat{\bm{v}}_T) $.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:stab}
The discrete bilinear form $a_h$ defined in \eqref{eq:ah} is symmetric, and there are
$0<\alpha_\flat\le\alpha_\sharp<+\infty$, independent of $h$, of the Lam\'e coefficients and
of $K$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coer}
\alpha_\flat|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{v}}_h|\!|\!|_h^2\le a_h(\hat{\bm{v}}_h,\hat{\bm{v}}_h)\le\alpha_\sharp|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{v}}_h|\!|\!|_h^2,
\qquad\forall\,\hat{\bm{v}}_h\in\hat{\bm{V}}_h .
\end{equation}
Moreover, $|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|_h$ is a norm on $\hat{\bm{V}}_{h0}$, so that the space semi-discrete problem

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:consistency}
Assume that $\bm{u}(t)|_{\Omega_i}\in\bm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega_i)$ for some $s>\tfrac12$, $i=1,2$.
Then, for any $\hat{\bm{v}}_h\in\hat{\bm{V}}_{h0}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consist}
\big|\Xi_h(\bm{u}(t);\hat{\bm{v}}_h)\big|\lesssim|\bm{u}(t)|_{\sharp h}\,|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{v}}_h|\!|\!|_h .
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Using the representation \eqref{eq:aTcomp} of $a_T$, the definition \eqref{eq:lT} of $\ell_T$
and the definition \eqref{eq:lift} of the lifting, and recalling that
$\bm{g}_D=[\![\bm{u}]\!]+K\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u}_1)\bm{n}$ and $\bm{g}_N=[\![\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u})\bm{n}]\!]$ on $\Gamma$, we may
write $\Xi_h(\bm{u};\hat{\bm{v}}_h)=\Psi+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_h}\Phi_T$, where $\Phi_T$ collects the terms
produced by the lifting, i.e. $\Phi_T$ is obtained from \eqref{eq:aTcomp} upon replacing
$\bm {\mathcal {E}}_{T^1}(\hat{\bm{I}}_T\bm{u})$ by $\bm{\mathcal{L}}_{T^1}(\bm{g}_D)$. Integrating by parts the volume term
$(\nabla\!\cdot\!\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u}),\bm{v}_{T^i})_{T^i}$ coming from the momentum equation, using the
definitions \eqref{eq:E1}--\eqref{eq:E2}, the single-valuedness of $\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u})\bm{n}$ across the
mesh interfaces not lying on $\Gamma$ and the two interface conditions, all the terms
involving $\bm{g}_D$ and $\bm{g}_N$ cancel and one is left with
\begin{multline}\label{eq:Xisplit}
\Xi_h(\bm{u};\hat{\bm{v}}_h)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_h}\bigg\{\sum_{i=1,2}\Big[
\big(\mathbb{C}_i\bm\eta_{T^i}(\bm{u}),\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\big)_{T^i}
+\big\langle\mathbb{C}_i\bm\eta_{T^i}(\bm{u})\bm{n}_T,
\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i}\big\rangle_{(\partial T)^i}\Big]\\
-\big\langle(\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK)\mathbb{C}_1\bm\eta_{T^1}(\bm{u})\bm{n},[\![\bm{v}_T]\!]_\Gamma
\big\rangle_{T^\Gamma}
+\big\langleS_h\big([\![\bm{I}_T\bm{u}]\!]_\Gamma-[\![\bm{u}]\!]\big),[\![\bm{v}_T]\!]_\Gamma
\big\rangle_{T^\Gamma}
+s_T^{\partial T}\big(\hat{\bm{I}}_T\bm{u},\hat{\bm{v}}_T\big)\bigg\}.
\end{multline}
We bound the five contributions in turn, writing $\Lambda_i:=2\mu_i+d\lambda_i$ for brevity.
The first one is bounded by
$\|\mathbb{C}_i^{1/2}\bm\eta_{T^i}(\bm{u})\|_{T^i}\|\mathbb{C}_i^{1/2}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{T^i})\|_{T^i}$. In the second
one, we insert $\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i}\big(\mathbb{C}_i\bm\eta_{T^i}(\bm{u})\bm{n}_T\big)$ and split
\begin{multline*}
\big\langle\mathbb{C}_i\bm\eta_{T^i}(\bm{u})\bm{n}_T,\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i}\big\rangle_{(\partial T)^i}
=\big\langle\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i}\big(\mathbb{C}_i\bm\eta_{T^i}(\bm{u})\bm{n}_T\big),
{\bf S}(\hat{\bm{v}}_{T^i})\big\rangle_{(\partial T)^i}\\
+\big\langle(\bm{I}-\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i})\big(\mathbb{C}_i\bm\eta_{T^i}(\bm{u})\bm{n}_T\big),
\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i}\big\rangle_{(\partial T)^i}.
\end{multline*}
Since $\mathbb{C}_i\bm\eta_{T^i}(\bm{u})\bm{n}_T=\mathbb{C}_i\big(\bm {\mathcal {E}}_{T^i}(\hat{\bm{I}}_T\bm{u})
+\bm{\mathcal{L}}_{T^i}(\bm{g}_D)\big)\bm{n}_T-\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u}_i)\bm{n}_T$ and the first contribution has its components
in ${\bm{P}}^{k}(F^i)$ on every subface, the second term above equals
$-\big\langle(\bm{I}-\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i})(\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u}_i)\bm{n}_T),
\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i}\big\rangle_{(\partial T)^i}$, which is bounded by the third
contribution to \eqref{eq:sharp} times
$(2\mu_i/h_T)^{1/2}\|\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i}\|_{(\partial T)^i}\le|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{v}}_T|\!|\!|_{a_T}$.
The first term above is bounded, using \eqref{eq:Csquare}, by
\begin{multline}\label{eq:cons2}
\Big(\tfrac{h_T}{\Lambda_i}\Big)^{1/2}\|\mathbb{C}_i\bm\eta_{T^i}(\bm{u})\bm{n}_T\|_{(\partial T)^i}
\cdot\Big(\tfrac{\Lambda_i}{h_T}\Big)^{1/2}\|{\bf S}(\hat{\bm{v}}_{T^i})\|_{(\partial T)^i}\\
\le h_T^{1/2}\|\mathbb{C}_i^{1/2}\bm\eta_{T^i}(\bm{u})\|_{(\partial T)^i}
\cdot\Big(\tfrac{\Lambda_i}{h_T}\Big)^{1/2}\|{\bf S}(\hat{\bm{v}}_{T^i})\|_{(\partial T)^i},
\end{multline}
and the last factor in \eqref{eq:cons2} is bounded by $|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{v}}_T|\!|\!|_{a_T}$, because
$\tfrac{2\mu_i}{h_T}\|{\bf S}(\hat{\bm{v}}_{T^i})\|^2\le\tfrac{2\mu_i}{h_T}
\|\bm{v}_{(\partial T)^i}-\bm{v}_{T^i}\|^2$ by \eqref{eq:stabnorm}, while the $\lambda$-weighted
part is a term of \eqref{eq:tnorm}. For the third contribution we use the factorisation
$\bm{\mathbb{I}}-S_hK=\big(\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}\big)^{1/2}
\big(\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}S_h\big)^{1/2}S_h^{1/2}$ of \Cref{lem:Sh}, which gives, using
again \eqref{eq:Csquare} and $\delta=\Lambda_1$,
\[
\Big(\tfrac{h_T}{\delta}\Big)^{1/2}\|\mathbb{C}_1\bm\eta_{T^1}(\bm{u})\bm{n}\|_{T^\Gamma}
\|S_h^{1/2}[\![\bm{v}_T]\!]_\Gamma\|_{T^\Gamma}\le
h_T^{1/2}\|\mathbb{C}_1^{1/2}\bm\eta_{T^1}(\bm{u})\|_{T^\Gamma}
\|S_h^{1/2}[\![\bm{v}_T]\!]_\Gamma\|_{T^\Gamma}.
\]
The fourth contribution is bounded by
$\|S_h^{1/2}([\![\bm{I}_T\bm{u}]\!]_\Gamma-[\![\bm{u}]\!])\|_{T^\Gamma}
\|S_h^{1/2}[\![\bm{v}_T]\!]_\Gamma\|_{T^\Gamma}$. Finally, since
${\bf S}(\hat{\bm{I}}_T\bm{u})=\bm{\Pi}_{(\partial T)^i}(\bm{u}_i-\bm{I}_{T^i}\bm{u})$, the Cauchy--Schwarz
inequality for $s_T^{\partial T}$ together with \eqref{eq:stabnorm} bounds the last
contribution by the fourth term of \eqref{eq:sharp} times $|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{v}}_T|\!|\!|_{a_T}$. Summing over
$T\in\mathcal{T}_h$ and using the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives \eqref{eq:consist}.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:locking}
The factor $\Phi_k(\bm{v})= \sum_{i=1,2}\mu_i^{1/2} (1+\kappa_i)|\bm{v}|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega_i)}$
in \eqref{eq:sharpapprox} degenerates as $\kappa_i=\lambda_i/\mu_i\to+\infty$. A sharper
analysis, splitting $\mathbb{C}_i$ into its $\mu$- and $\lambda$-parts as in
\cite[Thm.~5.2 and Lem.~5.1]{HuangBurman2024}, replaces
$(2\mu_i+d\lambda_i)|\bm{v}|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega_i)}$ by
$\mu_i|\bm{v}|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega_i)}+\lambda_i|\nabla\!\cdot\!\bm{v}|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_i)}$ in the
first three terms of \eqref{eq:sharp}, and the resulting estimate is then uniform in
$\lambda$ whenever $\lambda|\nabla\!\cdot\!\bm{u}|$ stays bounded, i.e.\ the method would be
locking free. This is however \emph{not} the case for the fourth term of \eqref{eq:sharp},
which carries the intrinsic factor $(2\mu_i+d\lambda_i)^{1/2}h^{k+1}$. In the
fitted case \cite{HuangBurman2024}, this obstruction is removed by weighting the stabilisation
by the anisotropic matrix $h_T^{-1}(2\mu_i\bm{\mathbb{I}}+d\lambda_i\bm{n}_T\otimes\bm{n}_T)$ and by measuring
the interpolation error with an $H(\operatorname{div})$-conforming (BDM) interpolate, whose normal trace
error vanishes on every face. Such an interpolate is not available on the cut subcells, whose
boundary comprises the curved piece $T^\Gamma$, and we therefore do not claim locking-free
behaviour here. The numerical experiments of \Cref{ssec:incomp} show a growth of the errors
proportional to $\lambda^{1/2}$ with the stabilisation \eqref{eq:stab}, and confirm that the
anisotropic weight restores the uniformity of the $L^2$-error in practice.

\label{prop:energy}
Let $\bm{u}$ solve \eqref{eq:weak} and let $\hat{\bm{u}}_h$ solve \eqref{eq:semi} with the initial
conditions \eqref{eq:ic}. Assume $\bm{u}|_{\Omega_i}\in C^2(\overline J;\bm{H}^{1+\nu}(\Omega_i))$
with $\nu\in(\tfrac12,k+1]$, $i=1,2$. Then, for all $t\in J$,
\begin{multline}\label{eq:energyest}
\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\big(\partial_{t}\bm{u}_\mathcal{T}-\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}(\partial_{t}\bm{u})\big)
\big\|_{C^0(0,t;\bm{L}^2(\Omega))}+\big\|\hat{\bm{u}}_h-\hat{\bm{I}}_h\bm{u}\big\|_{C^0(0,t;\,h)}\\
\lesssim|\bm{u}|_{C^0(0,t;\sharp h)}+|\partial_{t}\bm{u}|_{L^1(0,t;\sharp h)},
\end{multline}
where
\begin{eqnarray*}
|\bm{u}|_{C^0(0,t;\sharp h)}:=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\Big( \|\bm{u}|_{\natural h}
+\Big\|\sqrt{\frac{\varrho}{\mu_1}}\Big( \partial_{tt}\bm{u}(s) -\bm{I}_{\cal T}(\partial_{tt}\bm{u}(s)) \Big)\Big\|_{\Omega^{\Gamma}}\Big),\\
|\partial_{t}\bm{u}|_{L^1(0,t;\ \natural h)}:=\int_0^t\Big( |\partial_{t}\bm{u}|_{\natural h}
+\Big\|\sqrt{\frac{\varrho}{\mu_1}}\Big( \partial_{ttt}\bm{u}(s) -\bm{I}_{\cal T}(\partial_{ttt}\bm{u}(s)) \Big)\Big\|_{\Omega^{\Gamma}} \Big)ds.
\end{eqnarray*}
Consequently, if $\bm{u}|_{\Omega_i}\in C^3(\overline J;\bm{H}^{k+2}(\Omega_i))$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energyrate}
\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\big(\partial_{t}\bm{u}_\mathcal{T}-\partial_{t}\bm{u}\big)
\big\|_{C^0(0,t;\bm{L}^2(\Omega))}+\big\|\hat{\bm{u}}_h-\bm{u}\big\|_{C^0(0,t;\,h)}
\lesssim h^{k+1} \Phi(t,\bm{u}),
\end{equation}
where $\Phi(t,\bm{u})=|\bm{u}|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;\,\bm{H}^{k+2}(\Omega_1\cup\Omega_2))}
+|\partial_{t}\bm{u}|_{L^1(0,t;\,\bm{H}^{k+2}(\Omega_1\cup\Omega_2))}
+|\partial_{tt}\bm{u}|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;\,\bm{H}^{k+1}(\Omega_1\cup\Omega_2))}
+|\partial_{ttt}\bm{u}|_{L^1(0,t;\,\bm{H}^{k+1}(\Omega_1\cup\Omega_2))}$.

\emph{Step 1: error equation.} Subtracting \eqref{eq:Xi} from \eqref{eq:semi} we obtain, for
all $\hat{\bm{w}}_h\in\hat{\bm{V}}_{h0}$ and all $t\in J$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:erreq}
\big(\varrho\,\partial_{tt}\bm{e}_\mathcal{T}(t),\bm{w}_\mathcal{T}\big)_\Omega
+a_h\big(\hat{\bm{e}}_h(t),\hat{\bm{w}}_h\big)=\Theta_h(t;\hat{\bm{w}}_h),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Theta}
\Theta_h(t;\hat{\bm{w}}_h):=\big(\varrho\big(\partial_{tt}\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}-\partial_{tt}\bm{u}\big),
\bm{w}_\mathcal{T}\big)_{\Omega^{\Gamma}}+\Xi_h(\bm{u}(t);\hat{\bm{w}}_h),
\end{equation}
where we used that $\partial_{tt}\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}=\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}(\partial_{tt}\bm{u})$ and that
$(\varrho(\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}(\partial_{tt}\bm{u})-\partial_{tt}\bm{u}),\bm{w}_\mathcal{T})_{T^i}=0$ for every uncut cell,
because $\bm{I}_{T^i}$ coincides there with the $L^2$-projection onto ${\bm{P}}^{k+1}(T)$. Hence,
by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, \Cref{lem:poincare} and \Cref{lem:consistency},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Thetabound}
\|\Theta_h(t;\cdot)\|_{(\hat{\bm{V}}_{h0})'}\lesssim
\Big\|\sqrt{\frac{\varrho}{\mu_1}}\big(\partial_{tt}\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}-\partial_{tt}\bm{u}\big)\Big\|_{\Omega^\Gamma}
+|\bm{u}(t)|_{\sharp h}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 2: stability argument.} Testing \eqref{eq:erreq} with
$\hat{\bm{w}}_h=\partial_{t}\hat{\bm{e}}_h(t)$, using the symmetry of $a_h$ and integrating by parts in time on the
right-hand side gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\Big\{\tfrac12\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\partial_{t}\bm{e}_\mathcal{T}(t)\big\|_\Omega^2
+\tfrac12a_h(\hat{\bm{e}}_h(t),\hat{\bm{e}}_h(t))\Big\}
=\frac{d}{dt}\Theta_h\big(t;\hat{\bm{e}}_h(t)\big)-\dot\Theta_h\big(t;\hat{\bm{e}}_h(t)\big),
\]
with $\dot\Theta_h(t;\cdot):=(\varrho(\partial_{ttt}\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}-\partial_{ttt}\bm{u}),
\cdot)_{\Omega^\Gamma}+\Xi_h(\partial_{t}\bm{u}(t);\cdot)$, which satisfies the analogue of
\eqref{eq:Thetabound} with $\partial_{t}\bm{u}$ in place of $\bm{u}$. Integrating in time from $0$ to $t$,
using the coercivity \eqref{eq:coer} and reasoning exactly as in the proof of
\cite[Thm.~1]{BDES2021} — that is, bounding the right-hand side by Young's and H\"older's
inequalities and using that the left-hand side evaluated at any $t'\in(0,t)$ is bounded by
the right-hand side — we obtain
\[
\tfrac12\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\partial_{t}\bm{e}_\mathcal{T}\big\|_{C^0(0,t;\bm{L}^2(\Omega))}^2
+\tfrac18\alpha_\flat\|\hat{\bm{e}}_h\|_{C^0(0,t;\,h)}^2
\lesssim\|\Theta_h\|_{C^0(0,t;(\hat{\bm{V}}_{h0})')}^2+\|\dot\Theta_h\|_{L^1(0,t;(\hat{\bm{V}}_{h0})')}^2
+|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{e}}_h(0)|\!|\!|_h^2 .
\]
with $\|\Theta_h\|_{C^0(0,t;\ (\hat{\bm{V}}_{h0})')}:=\sup_{s\in[0,t]} \|\Theta_h(s;\cdot)\|_{(\hat{\bm{V}}_{h0})'}$
and $\|\dot{\Theta}_h\|_{L^1(0,t;\ (\hat{\bm{V}}_{h0})')}:=\int_0^t \|\dot{\Theta}_h(s;\cdot)\|_{(\hat{\bm{V}}_{h0})'}ds$. Still reasoning as in this proof (where one uses $\bm{e}_{\cal T}(0)=\bm{0}$) shows that $|\!|\!|\hat{\bm{e}}_h(0)|\!|\!|_h\lesssim |\!|\!|\bm{u}_0|\!|\!|_h$. Combining this bound with the estimate \eqref{eq:Thetabound} proves \eqref{eq:energyest}. 

\emph{Step 3: convergence rates.} The estimate \eqref{eq:energyrate} follows from \eqref{eq:energyest} and the triangle inequality, after invoking the approximation properties in \Cref{lem:consistency} and those of the gradient reconstruction from \Cref{lem:sharpapprox}.

\label{thm:ritzL2}
Let $k\ge1$ and assume $\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{k+2}(\Omega_1\cup\Omega_2)$ and \eqref{eq:reg}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ritzL2}
\big\|\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}-\bm{R}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}\big\|_\Omega
\lesssim h^{k+2}\sum_{i=1,2}\mu_i\,(1+\kappa_i)^2
\,|\bm{u}|_{\bm{H}^{k+2}(\Omega_i)},
\end{equation}
where $\bm{R}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}$ denotes the cell component of $\hat{\bm{R}}_h\bm{u}$.

Set $\bm{\hat\epsilon}_h:=\hat{\bm{I}}_h\bm{u}-\hat{\bm{R}}_h\bm{u}$ and take
$\bm{\vartheta}:=\bm\epsilon_\mathcal{T}$ in \eqref{eq:dual}, with solution $\bm{\zeta}$. Integrating by
parts cellwise, using that $\bm{\sigma}(\bm{\zeta})\bm{n}$ is single valued across the mesh interfaces not

vanish on $\partial\Omega$, and inserting the reconstructed dual stress
$\bm{G}_{T^i}(\hat{\bm{I}}_T\bm{\zeta})$ exactly as in \cite[Prop.~5.2]{HuangBurman2024}, one obtains
\[
\|\bm\epsilon_\mathcal{T}\|_\Omega^2
=\Xi_h^{\rm ell}\big(\bm{\zeta};\bm{\hat\epsilon}_h\big)
+a_h\big(\bm{\hat\epsilon}_h,\hat{\bm{I}}_h\bm{\zeta}\big),
\]
where in the first term the roles of the primal and the dual solutions are interchanged. The
first term is bounded by $|\bm{\zeta}|_{\sharp h}|\!|\!|\bm{\hat\epsilon}_h|\!|\!|_h$ thanks to
\Cref{lem:consistency}, and $|\bm{\zeta}|_{\sharp h}\lesssim h\,\Phi_0(\bm{\zeta})$ by
\Cref{lem:sharpapprox} applied with $k=0$, i.e.\ using only the $H^2$-regularity
\eqref{eq:reg}. The second term is treated as in \cite[Steps~4--6~of~Proposition~5.2]{HuangBurman2024}: 
\begin{align*}
	a_h&(\bm{\hat\epsilon}_h,\hat{\bm{I}}_h(\bm{\zeta}))
	=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_h}\Big[ (\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u}),\bm{I}_T(\bm{\zeta})-\bm{\zeta})_T
	-a_T( \bm{u}-\hat{\bm{I}}_T \bm{u}, \bm{\zeta}-\hat{\bm{I}}_T \bm{\zeta})\\
	&+\big\langle S_h [\![\bm{u}-\bm{I}_T\bm{u}]\!], K \big(\bm{\sigma}(\bm{\zeta}_1) -\bm{G}_{T^1}(\hat{\bm{I}}_T\bm{\zeta})\big)\bm{n} \big\rangle_{T^\Gamma}

	&+\big\langle K \big(\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u}_1) -\bm{G}_{T^1}(\hat{\bm{I}}_T\bm{n})\big)\bm{n}, S_h[\![\bm{\zeta} -\bm{I}_T\bm{\zeta}]\!] \big\rangle_{T^\Gamma}
	+\big\langle [\![ \big(\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u}) -\bm{G}_T(\hat{\bm{I}}_T\bm{u})\big)\bm{n} ]\!], \bm{\zeta}_2 -\bm{I}_{T^2}\bm{\zeta}\big\rangle_{T^\Gamma} \\

\eqref{eq:ritz} with the test function $\hat{\bm{I}}_h\bm{\zeta}$, subtracting
$\big(\bm{I}_T(\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u})),\bm{I}_T(\bm{\zeta})-\bm{\zeta}\big)_T$, and expanding the interface stabilisation by means of the

by $\|\bm\epsilon_\mathcal{T}\|_\Omega$ gives \eqref{eq:ritzL2}.

\label{prop:L2}
Let $\bm{u}$ solve \eqref{eq:model} and let $\hat{\bm{u}}_h$ solve \eqref{eq:semi} with the initial
condition \eqref{eq:ic}. Assume $k\ge1$, that \eqref{eq:reg} holds and that
$\bm{u}\in C^1(\overline J;\bm{H}^{k+2}(\Omega_1\cup\Omega_2))$. Then, for all $t\in J$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L2est}
\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\big(\bm{u}_\mathcal{T}-\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}\big)
\big\|_{C^0(0,t;\bm{L}^2(\Omega))}\lesssim
h^{k+2}\,
\Psi_k(t,\bm{u}).
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 1: error equation.} We use a different error decomposition than in
\Cref{prop:energy}: we set $\hat{\bm{d}}_h(t):=\hat{\bm{u}}_h(t)-\hat{\bm{R}}_h\bm{u}(t)$ for $t\in J$,
where $\hat{\bm{R}}_h$ is the elliptic projection \eqref{eq:ritz}. From \eqref{eq:semi} and
\eqref{eq:ritz} we infer that, for all $\hat{\bm{w}}_h\in\hat{\bm{V}}_{h0}$,
\[
\big(\varrho\,\partial_{tt}\bm{d}_\mathcal{T}(t),\bm{w}_\mathcal{T}\big)_\Omega
+a_h\big(\hat{\bm{d}}_h(t),\hat{\bm{w}}_h\big)
=\big(\varrho\,\partial_{tt}\bm\varpi(t),\bm{w}_\mathcal{T}\big)_\Omega
+\big(\varrho\big(\partial_{tt}\bm{u}-\partial_{tt}\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}\big),\bm{w}_\mathcal{T}\big)_{\Omega^\Gamma},
\]
with $\bm\varpi(t):=\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}(t)-\bm{R}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}(t)$, where we used
$-\nabla\!\cdot\!\bm{\sigma}(\bm{u})=\bm{f}-\varrho\partial_{tt}\bm{u}$ and the fact that $\bm{I}_{T^i}$ coincides
with the $L^2$-projection on the uncut cells.

\emph{Step 2: stability argument.} Let $\chi\in J$ and set
$\hat{\bm{z}}_h(t):=\int_t^{\chi}\hat{\bm{d}}_h(s)\,ds$, so that
$\partial_{t}\hat{\bm{z}}_h=-\hat{\bm{d}}_h$ and $\hat{\bm{z}}_h(\chi)=\bm{0}$. Testing the above
identity with $\hat{\bm{w}}_h=\hat{\bm{z}}_h(t)$, integrating by parts in time and using the symmetry
of $a_h$, we obtain, exactly as in \cite[Thm.~2]{BDES2021},
\begin{multline*}
\tfrac14\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\bm{d}_\mathcal{T}\big\|_{C^0(0,t;\bm{L}^2(\Omega))}^2
\le\tfrac12\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\bm\varpi(0)\big\|_\Omega^2
+\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\partial_{t}\bm\varpi\big\|_{L^1(0,t;\bm{L}^2(\Omega))}^2\\
+\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\partial_{t}\big(\bm{u}-\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}\big)
\big\|_{L^1(0,t;\bm{L}^2(\Omega^\Gamma))}^2,
\end{multline*}
where we also used that $\bm\varpi(0)-\bm{d}_\mathcal{T}(0)=\bm{0}$ and
$a_h(\hat{\bm{z}}_h(0),\hat{\bm{z}}_h(0))\ge0$.

\emph{Step 3: conclusion.} Since
$\bm{u}_\mathcal{T}-\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u}=\bm{d}_\mathcal{T}-\bm\varpi$, the triangle
inequality gives
\begin{multline*}
\big\|\varrho^{1/2}(\bm{u}_\mathcal{T}-\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u})\big\|_{C^0(0,t;\bm{L}^2(\Omega))}
\lesssim\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\bm\varpi\big\|_{C^0(0,t;\bm{L}^2(\Omega))}
+\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\partial_{t}\bm\varpi\big\|_{L^1(0,t;\bm{L}^2(\Omega))}\\
+\big\|\varrho^{1/2}\partial_{t}(\bm{u}-\bm{I}_\mathcal{T}\bm{u})\big\|_{L^1(0,t;\bm{L}^2(\Omega^\Gamma))}.
\end{multline*}
The first two terms are bounded by \Cref{thm:ritzL2} applied to $\bm{u}(t)$ and to $\partial_{t}\bm{u}(t)$
(note that $\hat{\bm{R}}_h$ commutes with $\partial_{t}$, since the data of \eqref{eq:ritz} depend
linearly on $\bm{u}$), and the last one is of order $h^{k+2}$ by \Cref{lem:approx}, which gives
\eqref{eq:L2est} with $\Psi_k$ defined in \eqref{eq:Psi}.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:optimal}
The rates $h^{k+1}$ in \Cref{prop:energy} and $h^{k+2}$ in \Cref{prop:L2} are optimal for the
polynomial degrees used, namely $k$ on the faces and $k+1$ in the cells. The constants do not
depend on the compliancy tensor $K$, nor on how $\Gamma$ cuts the mesh, and they depend on
the material parameters only through the explicit factors indicated in \eqref{eq:sharpapprox} and
\eqref{eq:ritzL2}. All the estimates degenerate as $\kappa_i\to+\infty$, see
\Cref{rem:locking}.

\label{rem:rkenergy}
The SDIRK schemes \eqref{eq:tableaux} are $A$-stable but, contrary to the Newmark scheme with
$\beta=\tfrac14$, $\gamma=\tfrac12$, they are dissipative: the discrete energy
$\hat E^n$ of \eqref{eq:discenergy} (with $\varsigma:=0$) decays in the absence of external
forcing. The dissipation is concentrated on the poorly resolved high-frequency components,
which is precisely the mechanism that makes these schemes more accurate than the
conservative one at a given $\Delta t$; the energy loss is quantified numerically in
\Cref{ssec:case2}.
