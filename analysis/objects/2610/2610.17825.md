\label{def-control}
The MF-SDE system \eqref{fmf} is said to be terminally controllable on $[0,T]$ if, for any $x_T\in L^2_{{\cal F}_T}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^n)$, there exists a pair $(x_0,u(\cdot))\in \mathbb{R}^n\times L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^m)$ such that the corresponding state satisfies $x(T;x_0, u(\cdot))=x_T\;\hbox{\rm a.s.}$
If \eqref{fmf} is terminally controllable on $[0,T]$ for every $T\in(0,\infty)$, then it is simply said to be terminally controllable.

The MF-SDE system \eqref{fmf} is said to be exactly controllable on $[0,T]$ if, for any initial state $x_0\in\mathbb{R}^n$ and any target $x_T\in L^2_{{\cal F}_T}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^n)$,  there exists a control $u(\cdot)\in L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^m)$ such that the corresponding state satisfies $x(T;x_0, u(\cdot))=x_T \;\hbox{\rm a.s.}$
If \eqref{fmf} is exactly controllable on $[0,T]$ for every $T\in(0,\infty)$, then it is simply said to be exactly controllable.

\label{def-minimal-norm}
For any $T>0$, $x_0\in\mathbb{R}^n$, and $x_T\in L^2_{\mathcal{F}_T}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^n)$, 
                               
                 
               
            
                                   
          
                                                       
        
    
                                                               
                                                                     
     
             
                                      
                                       
     
a control $u^*(\cdot)\in{\cal U}(T,x_0,x_T)$ is called a minimal norm control of ${\rm\bf (NP)}^T_{x_0,x_T}$ if
${\cal J}_F\big(u^*(\cdot)\big)
=\inf_{u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}(T,x_0,x_T)}{\cal J}_F\big(u(\cdot)\big)$,
where ${\cal U}(T,x_0,x_T)$ and ${\cal J}_F(\cdot)$ are defined in \eqref{ad-control} and \eqref{cost-F}, respectively.
The corresponding state $x^*(\cdot)\in L^2_\mathbb{F}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^n))$ is called an optimal state, and $\big(x^*(\cdot),u^*(\cdot)\big)$ is called an optimal pair.

\label{wang922l1}
Let $K\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ be of full row rank and set $r\triangleq m-n$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{wang922e2}
K^\dagger= K^\top(KK^\top)^{-1},
\qquad
\Pi_K= I_m-K^\dagger K.
\end{equation}
Then $K^\dagger K$ is the orthogonal projection of $\mathbb{R}^m$ onto $\operatorname{Ran}(K^\top)$, while $\Pi_K$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{Ker} K$.
Moreover, there exists a matrix $U_K\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{wang922e3}
KU_K=0,
\qquad
U_K^\top U_K=I_r,
\qquad
U_KU_K^\top=\Pi_K.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $K$ has full row rank, $KK^\top$ is positive definite and hence invertible. Set $P_K= K^\dagger K=K^\top(KK^\top)^{-1}K$.
Then $P_K^\top=P_K$, and $P_K^2
                                            
=P_K$.
Hence $P_K$ is an orthogonal projection; see, e.g., \cite[Lecture 6]{Trefethen-Bau97}. 

Clearly, $\operatorname{Ran}(P_K)\subseteq\operatorname{Ran}(K^\top)$. 
Conversely, for any $y\in\operatorname{Ran}(K^\top)$, there exists $z\in\mathbb{R}^n$ such that $y=K^\top z$.
Hence, $
P_Ky
=K^\top(KK^\top)^{-1}KK^\top z
=K^\top z
=y$.
Therefore,
\[
\operatorname{Ran}(P_K)=\operatorname{Ran}(K^\top),
\]
and $P_K=K^\dagger K$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{Ran}(K^\top)$.
On the other hand, since $\operatorname{Ran}(K^\top)=(\operatorname{Ker} K)^\perp$, it follows that
$\Pi_K=I_m-P_K$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{Ker} K$.


Since $\operatorname{Rank} K=n$, it follows that $\dim(\operatorname{Ker} K)=m-n=r$.
Let $\{u_1,\cdots,u_r\}$ be an orthonormal basis of $\operatorname{Ker} K$, and set
\[U_K=(u_1,\cdots,u_r)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}.\]
                  
                                       
     
Then
\[
KU_K=0,
\qquad
U_K^\top U_K=I_r,
\qquad
\operatorname{Ran}(U_K)=\operatorname{Ker} K.
\]
Consequently,
   
                                
       
                
                           
              
   
       
$U_KU_K^\top$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{Ran}(U_K)=\operatorname{Ker} K$. By the uniqueness of the orthogonal projection onto a given subspace,
\[
U_KU_K^\top=\Pi_K.
\]
This proves the assertion \eqref{wang922e3} and completes the proof.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{Wang921l1}
Suppose that the MF-SDE system \eqref{fmf} is terminally controllable on $[0,T]$.
Then there exists a constant ${\cal C}>0$ such that, for every $x_T\in L^2_{\mathcal F_T}(\Omega;\mathbb R^n)$ satisfying
$\|x_T\|_{L^2_{\mathcal F_T}(\Omega;\mathbb R^n)}\leq 1$,
there is a pair
$(x_0,u(\cdot))\in \mathbb R^n\times L^2_{\mathbb F}(0,T;\mathbb R^m)$
such that $x(T;x_0,u(\cdot))=x_T$, and
\begin{align}\label{Wang921e1}
\|x_0\|^2+\mathbb{E}\int_0^T\|u(t)\|^2\,\mathrm d t \leq {\cal C}.
\end{align}
Moreover, the corresponding state satisfies
\begin{align}\label{Wang921e2}
\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big\|x\big(t;x_0,u(\cdot)\big)\big\|^2\Big]\leq {\cal C}.
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Set $X\triangleq\mathbb R^n\times L^2_{\mathbb F}(0,T;\mathbb R^m)$,
$Y\triangleq L^2_{\mathcal F_T}(\Omega;\mathbb R^n)$,
and equip $X$ with the norm
$\|(x_0,u(\cdot))\|_X^2\triangleq \|x_0\|^2+\|u(\cdot)\|^2_{L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^m)}$.
Since \eqref{fmf} is terminally controllable on $[0,T]$, ${\cal T}$ is surjective. Hence, by the open mapping theorem, ${\cal T}(B_X(0,1))$ is an open neighborhood of $0$ in $Y$.
Therefore, there exists a constant $r>0$ such that
$B_Y(0,r)\subseteq{\cal T}(B_X(0,1))$.
Let $x_T\in Y$ satisfy $\|x_T\|_{Y}\leq1$.
Then
\[
\big\|r\frac {x_T} 2 \big\|_{Y}\leq\frac r2<r.
\]
Consequently, there exists $(z_0,v(\cdot))\in B_X(0,1)$ such that
${\cal T}(z_0,v(\cdot))=r\frac {x_T} 2$.
By the linearity of ${\cal T}$, setting $(x_0,u(\cdot))\triangleq\frac2r(z_0,v(\cdot))$
gives
${\cal T}(x_0,u(\cdot))=x(T;x_0,u(\cdot))=x_T$.
Moreover,
\[
\|x_0\|^2+\|u(\cdot)\|^2_{L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^m)}
=\frac4{r^2}\|(z_0,v(\cdot))\|_X^2
\leq\frac4{r^2}.
\]
Thus the assertion \eqref{Wang921e1} follows by taking ${\cal C}=4r^{-2}$.

Finally, by the stability estimate for the MF-SDE system \eqref{fmf}, there exists a constant ${\cal C}>0$, depending only on the coefficients of \eqref{fmf} and $T$, such that
\[
\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big\|x\big(t;x_0,u(\cdot)\big)\big\|^2\Big]
\leq
{\cal C}\Big[
\|x_0\|^2+\mathbb{E}\int_0^T\|u(t)\|^2\,\mathrm d t
\Big].
\]
Combining this estimate with \eqref{Wang921e1} yields \eqref{Wang921e2}. This completes the proof.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{rank-condition}
For any $T>0$, the MF-SDE system \eqref{fmf} is terminally controllable on $[0,T]$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{rank-conditions}
\operatorname{Rank}(D)=\operatorname{Rank}(D+\bar D)=n.
\end{equation}
Consequently, \eqref{fmf} is terminally controllable if and only if \eqref{rank-conditions} holds.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The sufficiency follows from \cite[Proposition 5]{Goreac14}. It therefore remains to prove the necessity. 
We establish the two rank conditions separately by contradiction.

\noindent
\textbf{Step 1: Full row rank of $D$.}
Suppose, to the contrary, that $\operatorname{Rank} D<n$. Then we can find a unit vector $q\in\mathbb{R}^n$ such that 
\begin{equation}\label{wang921e5}
q^\top D=0.
\end{equation} 

For any $T\in (0,\infty)$ and any $\varepsilon\in(0,T)$, set 
\begin{equation}\label{wang921e3}
\theta_\varepsilon=\frac{W(T-\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{T-\varepsilon}}
\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad
\xi_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1/2}q\, \theta_\varepsilon\big[W(T)-W(T-\varepsilon)\big].
\end{equation}
Since $\theta_\varepsilon$ is $\mathcal{F}_{T-\varepsilon}$-measurable and $W(T)-W(T-\varepsilon)$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_{T-\varepsilon}$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{wang921e4}
\mathbb{E}\big[\theta_\varepsilon\big]=0,\qquad
\mathbb{E}\big[\|\xi_\varepsilon\|^2\big]=1,\qquad
\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_\varepsilon\,\big|\,\mathcal{F}_{T-\varepsilon}\big]=0.
\end{equation}
Since \eqref{fmf} is terminally controllable on $[0,T]$, Lemma \ref{Wang921l1}, applied to the target $\xi_\varepsilon\in L^2_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^n)$, ensures the existence of $(x_{0,\varepsilon}, u_\varepsilon(\cdot))\in \mathbb{R}^n\times L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^m)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{wang921e8}
x_\varepsilon(T)=\xi_\varepsilon, \qquad
\|x_{0,\varepsilon}\|^2+\mathbb{E}\int_0^T\|u_\varepsilon(t)\|^2\,\mathrm d t\leq {\cal C},\qquad
\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|x_\varepsilon(t)\|^2\Big]\leq {\cal C},
\end{equation}
where $x_\varepsilon(\cdot)=x\big(\cdot;x_{0,\varepsilon},u_\varepsilon(\cdot)\big)$, and ${\cal C}>0$ is independent of $\varepsilon$.

On the other hand, by \eqref{wang921e5} and the decomposition $u_\varepsilon(\cdot)=\widetilde u_\varepsilon(\cdot)+\bar u_\varepsilon(\cdot)$, we have $q^\top Du_\varepsilon(\cdot)+q^\top \bar D \bar u_\varepsilon(\cdot)=q^\top D\widetilde u_\varepsilon(\cdot)+q^\top \widehat D \bar u_\varepsilon(\cdot)=q^\top \widehat D \bar u_\varepsilon(\cdot)$.
Define
\begin{align*}
&f_\varepsilon(\cdot)=q^\top\big[Ax_\varepsilon(\cdot)+\bar A\bar x_\varepsilon(\cdot)
 +B u_\varepsilon(\cdot)+\bar B\bar u_\varepsilon(\cdot)\big],\\
\mbox{and}\quad&
g_\varepsilon(\cdot)=q^\top\big[Cx_\varepsilon(\cdot)+\bar C\bar x_\varepsilon(\cdot)\big].
\end{align*}
Then, integrating \eqref{fmf} over $[T-\varepsilon,T]$ and using $x_\varepsilon(T)=\xi_\varepsilon$, we obtain
\begin{align}\label{wang921e6}
q^\top \xi_\varepsilon=q^\top x_\varepsilon(T-\varepsilon)+\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T f_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d t+\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T \big[g_\varepsilon(t)+q^\top \widehat D\bar u_\varepsilon(t)\big]\,\mathrm d W(t).
\end{align}
Taking conditional expectation on both sides with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{T-\varepsilon}$ and using \eqref{wang921e4} gives
\begin{align}\label{wang921e7}
0=q^\top x_\varepsilon(T-\varepsilon)+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T f_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d t \,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{T-\varepsilon}\Big].
\end{align}
Subtracting \eqref{wang921e7} from \eqref{wang921e6} yields
\begin{align}
&q^\top \xi_\varepsilon-\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T q^\top \widehat D\bar u_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d W(t)\notag\\
&=\Big\{\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T f_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d t-\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T f_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d t \,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{T-\varepsilon}\Big] \Big\}+\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T g_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d W(t)\label{wang921e10}\\
&=: I_1(\varepsilon)+I_2(\varepsilon).\notag
\end{align}

We next estimate $I_1(\varepsilon)$ and $I_2(\varepsilon)$ on the right-hand side of \eqref{wang921e10}. For $I_1(\varepsilon)$, the $L^2$-contraction property of conditional expectation, H\"older's inequality, and \eqref{wang921e8} imply that
\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}\big[\|I_1(\varepsilon)\|^2\big]
&\leq \varepsilon\mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T\|f_\varepsilon(t)\|^2\,\mathrm d t\notag\\
&\leq {\cal C}\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T\big[\|x_\varepsilon(t)\|^2+\|u_\varepsilon(t)\|^2\big]\,\mathrm d t\label{wang921e9}\\
&\leq {\cal C}\varepsilon.\notag
\end{align}
For $I_2(\varepsilon)$, the It\^o isometry and \eqref{wang921e8} lead to
\begin{align}\label{wang921e12}
\mathbb{E}\big[\|I_2(\varepsilon)\|^2\big]
\leq {\cal C}\mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|x_\varepsilon(s)\|^2\,\mathrm d t
\leq {\cal C}\varepsilon.
\end{align} 

It remains to obtain a lower bound for the left-hand side of
\eqref{wang921e10}.
Since $\theta_\varepsilon$ is $\mathcal{F}_{T-\varepsilon}$-measurable, the conditional It\^o isometry gives
\begin{align*}
&\mathbb{E}\Big[q^\top \xi_\varepsilon \int_{T-\varepsilon}^T q^\top \widehat D\bar u_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d W(t)\Big]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\Big[\varepsilon^{-1/2} \theta_\varepsilon \mathbb{E}\Big( \int_{T-\varepsilon}^T 1 \,\mathrm d W(t) \int_{T-\varepsilon}^T q^\top \widehat D\bar u_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d W(t)\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{T-\varepsilon}\Big)\Big]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\Big[\varepsilon^{-1/2} \theta_\varepsilon  \int_{T-\varepsilon}^T  q^\top \widehat D\bar u_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d t\Big].
                                                                          
    
\end{align*}
Since $\bar u_\varepsilon(\cdot)$ is deterministic, it follows that
\[
\mathbb{E}\Big[q^\top \xi_\varepsilon \int_{T-\varepsilon}^T q^\top \widehat D\bar u_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d W(t)\Big]
=\varepsilon^{-1/2}\mathbb{E}\big[\theta_\varepsilon\big] \int_{T-\varepsilon}^T  q^\top \widehat D\bar u_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d t
=0,
\]
where the last equality follows from \eqref{wang921e4}.
Thus,
\begin{align}\label{wang921e13}
\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\|q^\top \xi_\varepsilon\!-\! \int_{T-\varepsilon}^T \!q^\top \widehat D\bar u_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d W(t)\Big\|^2\Big]
=\mathbb{E}\big[\|\xi_\varepsilon\|^2\big]+\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T\!\big\| q^\top \widehat D\bar u_\varepsilon(t) \big\|^2\,\mathrm d t
\geq 1.
\end{align}

Finally, combining \eqref{wang921e10}, \eqref{wang921e9}, \eqref{wang921e12}, and \eqref{wang921e13}, we conclude that
\begin{equation*}\begin{aligned}
1\leq 2\mathbb{E}\big[\|I_1(\varepsilon)\|^2+\|I_2(\varepsilon)\|^2\big]\leq {\cal C}\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}\end{equation*}
Since $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary, letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ yields a contradiction. Therefore, $\operatorname{Rank}(D)=n$.

\noindent
\textbf{Step 2: Full row rank of $\widehat D=D+\bar D$.}
Suppose that $\operatorname{Rank}(\widehat D)<n$, and choose a unit vector $q_0 \in\mathbb{R}^n$ satisfying $q_0^\top \widehat D=0$. 
For any $\varepsilon\in(0,T)$, set the target $\eta_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1/2}q_0\big[W(T)-W(T-\varepsilon)\big]$. It follows that $\mathbb{E}\big[\|\eta_\varepsilon\|^2\big]=1$ and
$\mathbb{E}\big[\eta_\varepsilon\,\big|\,\mathcal{F}_{T-\varepsilon}\big]=0$. 
Terminal controllability of \eqref{fmf} on $[0,T]$, together with Lemma \ref{Wang921l1}, provides $(z_{0,\varepsilon}, v_\varepsilon(\cdot))\in \mathbb{R}^n\times L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^m)$ satisfying 
$X_\varepsilon(T)=\eta_\varepsilon$, 
$\|z_{0,\varepsilon}\|^2+\mathbb{E}\int_0^T\|v_\varepsilon(t)\|^2\,\mathrm d t\leq {\cal C}$, and
$\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_\varepsilon(t)\|^2\big]\leq {\cal C}$,
where $X_\varepsilon(\cdot)=x\big(\cdot;z_{0,\varepsilon},v_\varepsilon(\cdot)\big)$.

Since
$v_\varepsilon(\cdot)=\widetilde v_\varepsilon(\cdot)+\bar v_\varepsilon(\cdot)$,
$\bar v_\varepsilon(\cdot)=\mathbb{E}[v_\varepsilon(\cdot)]$, and $q_0^\top\widehat D=0$, we have
$q_0^\top\big[Dv_\varepsilon(\cdot)+\bar D\bar v_\varepsilon(\cdot)\big]
=q_0^\top D\widetilde v_\varepsilon(\cdot)+q_0^\top\widehat D\bar v_\varepsilon(\cdot)
=q_0^\top D\widetilde v_\varepsilon(\cdot)$.
Proceeding as in Step 1 to derive \eqref{wang921e10}, we have
\begin{align*}              
&q_0^\top \eta_\varepsilon-\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T q_0^\top D\widetilde v_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d W(t)\\
&\qquad=\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T F_\varepsilon (t)\,\mathrm d t-\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T F_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d t \,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{T-\varepsilon}\Big]+\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T G_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d W(t),
\end{align*}
where $F_\varepsilon(\cdot)=q_0^\top\big[AX_\varepsilon(\cdot)+\bar A\bar X_\varepsilon(\cdot)
 +B v_\varepsilon(\cdot)+\bar B\bar v_\varepsilon(\cdot)\big]$, and
$G_\varepsilon(\cdot)=q_0^\top\big[CX_\varepsilon(\cdot)+\bar C\bar X_\varepsilon(\cdot)\big]$.
As in \eqref{wang921e9} and \eqref{wang921e12}, we have
\begin{equation*}\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\|\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T F_\varepsilon (t)\,\mathrm d t-\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T F_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d t \,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{T-\varepsilon}\Big]+\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T G_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d W(t)\Big\|^2\Big]
\leq {\cal C}\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}\end{equation*}
On the other hand,
\begin{align*}
&\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\|q_0^\top \eta_\varepsilon-\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T q_0^\top D\widetilde v_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm d W(t)\Big\|^2\Big]\\
&=1+\mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T\big\| q_0^\top D\widetilde v_\varepsilon(t)\big\|^2\,\mathrm d t
-2 \mathbb{E}\int_{T-\varepsilon}^T\varepsilon^{-1/2}q_0^\top D \mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde v_\varepsilon(t)\big]\,\mathrm d t\\
&\geq 1.
\end{align*}
Here we apply $\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde v_\varepsilon(t)\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[v_\varepsilon(t)-\mathbb{E}\big[v_\varepsilon(t)]\big]=0$.
Combining the above estimates yields $1\leq {\cal C}\varepsilon$ for any $\varepsilon\in (0,T)$, which is impossible as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Hence, $\operatorname{Rank}(D+\bar D)=n$.
This completes the proof.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{wang922p1}
Suppose that $\operatorname{Rank}(D)=\operatorname{Rank}(D+\bar D)=n$. Then the exact controllability of the MF-SDE system \eqref{fmf} is equivalent to that of the MF-BSDE system \eqref{fmf-2}.
Furthermore, suppose that \eqref{fmf} is exactly controllable on $[0,T]$. Then, for every $x_0\in\mathbb{R}^n$ and $x_T\in L^2_{\mathcal{F}_T}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^n)$, Problems {\rm\bf (NP)$_{x_0,x_T}^T$} and {\rm\bf (COP)$_{x_0,x_T}^T$} admit unique optimal controls $u^*(\cdot)$ and $v^*(\cdot)$, respectively, and
\begin{equation}\label{wang922e8}
u^*(\cdot)= f\big(x^*(\cdot),z^*(\cdot),v^*(\cdot)\big),
\end{equation}
where $\big(x^*(\cdot),z^*(\cdot)\big)$ is the solution to the MF-BSDE \eqref{fmf-2} with terminal condition $x^*(T)=x_T$ and control $v^*(\cdot)$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We first prove the equivalence of exact controllability. Let $u(\cdot)$ be any control for \eqref{fmf}, with $u(\cdot)\in L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^m)$, and let $x(\cdot)$ be the corresponding state. Define $z(\cdot)$ and $v(\cdot)$ by \eqref{wang922e9}. Then \eqref{wang922e1} and \eqref{wang922e5} imply that $\big(x(\cdot),z(\cdot)\big)$ satisfies \eqref{fmf-2}. Conversely, for any $v(\cdot)\in L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^r)$ and terminal state $x_T$, let $\big(x(\cdot),z(\cdot)\big)$ be the solution of \eqref{fmf-2}, and define $u(\cdot)$ by \eqref{wang922e1}. A direct substitution shows that $x(\cdot)$ satisfies \eqref{fmf}. Hence, for every $T>0$, the two systems have the same reachable initial-terminal pairs, and their exact controllability on $[0,T]$ is equivalent. This argument is analogous to that in \cite[Theorem 3.6]{Yu21}.

Now suppose that \eqref{fmf} is exactly controllable on $[0,T]$. Define the bounded linear terminal map ${\cal T}_0:L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^m)\longrightarrow L^2_{\mathcal{F}_T}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^n)$ by ${\cal T}_0u(\cdot)= x(T;0,u(\cdot))$.
For fixed $x_0\in\mathbb{R}^n$, the linearity of \eqref{fmf} gives $x(T;x_0,u(\cdot))=x(T;x_0,0)+{\cal T}_0u(\cdot)$.
Hence,
\[
{\cal U}(T,x_0,x_T)
={\cal T}_0^{-1}\big(\{x_T-x(T;x_0,0)\}\big),
\]
which is closed in $L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^m)$. Moreover, it is an affine subset, and the exact controllability of \eqref{fmf} guarantees that it is nonempty. Therefore, ${\cal U}(T,x_0,x_T)$ is a nonempty closed affine subset of the Hilbert space $L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^m)$.
                                                                                                       
Hence, by the projection theorem in Hilbert spaces, there exists a unique $u^*(\cdot)\in{\cal U}(T,x_0,x_T)$ such that ${\cal J}_F\big(u^*(\cdot)\big)=\inf_{u(\cdot)\in {\cal U}(T,x_0,x_T)}{\cal J}_F\big(u(\cdot)\big)$. Therefore, Problem {\bf (NP)$_{x_0,x_T}^T$} admits a unique optimal control $u^*(\cdot)$.
                                                                                                                                                                                             

By the first assertion, \eqref{fmf-2} is also exactly controllable on $[0,T]$, and therefore ${\cal V}(T,x_0,x_T)$ is a nonempty closed affine subset of $L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^r)$. Moreover, the matrices
\begin{align}\label{wang923e1}
\Theta\triangleq
\begin{pmatrix}
Q&S^\top\\
S&R
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\widehat\Theta\triangleq
\begin{pmatrix}
\widehat Q&\widehat S^\top\\
\widehat S&\widehat R
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align}
are positive semidefinite. Indeed,
$\Theta=\big(C\,-I_n\big)^\top(DD^\top)^{-1}\big(C\,-I_n\big)\geq0$,
and similarly $\widehat\Theta\geq0$. Hence, by \eqref{cost-functional},
\begin{align*}
{\cal J}_B\big(v(\cdot)\big)
\geq \mathbb{E}\int_0^T\|\widetilde v(t)\|^2\,\mathrm d t +\int_0^T\|\bar v(t)\|^2\,\mathrm d t
=\|v(\cdot)\|^2_{L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^r)}.
\end{align*}
Therefore, ${\cal J}_B(\cdot)$ is coercive. Since the solution $\big(x(\cdot),z(\cdot)\big)$ of \eqref{fmf-2} depends affinely and continuously on $v(\cdot)$, the functional ${\cal J}_B(\cdot)$ is continuous and strictly convex on $L^2_\mathbb{F}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^r)$. In particular, it is weakly lower semicontinuous. Therefore, by the direct method of the calculus of variations, Problem {\bf (COP)$_{x_0,x_T}^T$} admits a unique optimal control $v^*(\cdot)$.

It remains to establish \eqref{wang922e8}. For any $u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}(T,x_0,x_T)$, define $z(\cdot)$ and $v(\cdot)$ by \eqref{wang922e9}. Then, by \eqref{wang922e1}, the corresponding $(x(\cdot),z(\cdot))$ satisfies \eqref{fmf-2}, $v(\cdot)\in{\cal V}(T,x_0,x_T)$, and $u(\cdot)=f\big(x(\cdot),z(\cdot),v(\cdot)\big)$.
Conversely, for any control $v(\cdot)\in{\cal V}(T,x_0,x_T)$, if $(x(\cdot),z(\cdot))$ is the corresponding solution of \eqref{fmf-2}, then \eqref{wang922e1} defines a control $u(\cdot)=f\big(x(\cdot),z(\cdot),v(\cdot)\big)\in{\cal U}(T,x_0,x_T)$.
Moreover, by \eqref{cost-functional},
\[
{\cal J}_F\big(u(\cdot)\big)={\cal J}_B\big(v(\cdot)\big).
\]
Thus, Problems {\bf (NP)$_{x_0,x_T}^T$} and {\bf (COP)$_{x_0,x_T}^T$} are equivalent under the transformations \eqref{wang922e9} and \eqref{wang922e1}. 
                
Let $v^*(\cdot)$ be the optimal control of {\bf (COP)$_{x_0,x_T}^T$}, and let $\big(x^*(\cdot),z^*(\cdot)\big)$ be the corresponding solution of \eqref{fmf-2}. Set $\widehat u(\cdot)\triangleq f\big(x^*(\cdot),z^*(\cdot),v^*(\cdot)\big)$.
Then $\widehat u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}(T,x_0,x_T)$. For any $u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}(T,x_0,x_T)$, let $v(\cdot)\in{\cal V}(T,x_0,x_T)$ be the control defined by \eqref{wang922e9}. By \eqref{cost-functional} and the optimality of $v^*(\cdot)$,
\[
{\cal J}_F\big(\widehat u(\cdot)\big)
={\cal J}_B\big(v^*(\cdot)\big)
\leq{\cal J}_B\big(v(\cdot)\big)
={\cal J}_F\big(u(\cdot)\big).
\]
Hence $\widehat u(\cdot)$ is an optimal control of {\bf (NP)$_{x_0,x_T}^T$}. By the uniqueness of the latter,
\begin{equation*}\begin{aligned}
u^*(\cdot)=f\big(x^*(\cdot),z^*(\cdot),v^*(\cdot)\big),
\end{aligned}\end{equation*}
which proves \eqref{wang922e8}.
This completes the proof.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{control=NP}
The MF-SDE system \eqref{fmf} is exactly controllable on $[0,T]$ if and only if, for every $x_0\in\mathbb{R}^n$ and $x_T\in L^2_{\mathcal{F}_T}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^n)$, Problem {\rm\bf (NP)$_{x_0,x_T}^T$} admits a unique minimal norm control.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Suppose first that \eqref{fmf} is exactly controllable on $[0,T]$. =x_0,
\qquad
x^*_\lambda(T)=x_T.
\]
Since $x_0$ and $x_T$ are arbitrary, the MF-BSDE system \eqref{fmf-2} is exactly controllable on $[0,T]$. Proposition \ref{wang922p1} then implies that the MF-SDE system \eqref{fmf} is exactly controllable on $[0,T]$. This completes the proof.

\label{wang929r1}
Proposition \ref{wang929p1} provides a deterministic Riccati-based criterion for the exact controllability of the constant-coefficient MF-SDE system \eqref{fmf}. In contrast with the Gramian characterization in Lemma \ref{wang924l1}, which requires solving the matrix-valued stochastic equation \eqref{sde-Phi} and evaluating the expectation in \eqref{wang924e3}, the condition
$P(0)>0$
can be checked by solving the two deterministic Riccati equations \eqref{Riccati-Sigma}--\eqref{Riccati-P} backward in time. Moreover, these Riccati equations are already required in the construction of the minimal norm control in Corollary \ref{wang924c1}. Thus, the controllability test introduces essentially no additional computational procedure beyond that used to construct the optimal control.

This characterization is also useful for numerical verification of exact controllability. Let $P_h(0)$ be a numerical approximation of $P(0)$, and suppose that an error bound
\[
\|P_h(0)-P(0)\|_2\leq\varepsilon_h
\]
is available. Here $\|\cdot\|_2$ denotes the matrix spectral norm induced by the Euclidean norm on $\mathbb{R}^n$. Since $P(0)$ is symmetric, Weyl's eigenvalue perturbation inequality (see, e.g., \cite[Theorem 4.3.1]{Horn-Johnson90}) gives
\[
\lambda_{\min}\big(P(0)\big)
\geq
\lambda_{\min}\Big(\frac{P_h(0)+P_h(0)^\top}{2}\Big)-\varepsilon_h.
\]
Consequently, under the rank condition $\operatorname{Rank}(D)=\operatorname{Rank}(D+\bar D)=n$, the computable condition
\[
\lambda_{\min}\Big(\frac{P_h(0)+P_h(0)^\top}{2}\Big)>\varepsilon_h
\]
guarantees the exact controllability of \eqref{fmf}.

\end{align*}

\label{explicit-example}
Let $T=1$, $n=1$, $m=2$, $x_0=0$, $x_T=1$, and set
\begin{align*}
A=-\frac12,\,
\bar A=\frac12,\,
B=(1,-1),\,
\bar B=(-1,2),\,C=1,\,
\bar C=-1,\,
D=(0,1),\,
\bar D=(0,0).
\end{align*}
Then $\operatorname{Rank} D=\operatorname{Rank}\widehat D=1$, and we may choose $U=\widehat U=(1,\,0)^\top$.

For these coefficients, the solutions of the Riccati equations \eqref{Riccati-Sigma} and \eqref{Riccati-P} are $\Sigma(t)=1-t$ and $P(t)=(1-t)-\ln(2-t)$ with $t\in [0,1]$, respectively. In particular,
\[
P(0)=1-\ln2>0.
\]
Hence, by Proposition \ref{wang929p1}, the MF-SDE system \eqref{fmf} is exactly controllable on $[0,1]$.
Then the solution of the MF-BSDE \eqref{bsde-varphi} is $\big(\varphi(t),\beta(t)\big)=(1,0)$ for $t\in[0,1]$.
Therefore, by \eqref{wang924e12},
\[
\lambda
=P(0)^{-1}\big(\varphi(0)-x_0\big)
=\frac{1}{1-\ln2}.
\]
For this choice of $\lambda$, the MF-SDE \eqref{sde-y} becomes
\begin{equation*}\begin{aligned}             
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{d} y^*_\lambda(t)
=-\frac12\widetilde y^*_\lambda(t)\,\mathrm d t
+\Big[\widetilde y^*_\lambda(t)-\frac{\bar y^*_\lambda(t)}{2-t}\Big]\,\mathrm d W(t),
\qquad t\in[0,1],\\
&y^*_\lambda(0)=\lambda.
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{aligned}\end{equation*}
Taking expectations gives $\bar y^*_\lambda(t)=\lambda$. Consequently, the centered process $\widetilde y^*_\lambda(\cdot)$ satisfies a linear SDE, whose explicit solution is
\begin{align*}               
\widetilde y^*_\lambda(t)
=\lambda e^{W(t)-t}
\Big[\int_0^t\frac{e^{-W(s)+s}}{2-s}\,\mathrm d s
-\int_0^t\frac{e^{-W(s)+s}}{2-s}\,\mathrm d W(s)
\Big].
\end{align*}
                                                              
                    
        
     
                   
                      
                                           
                    
            
     
        
     
                         
                    
        
               
                                          
                                         
       
     

Using \eqref{optimality}, \eqref{feedback-z}, and \eqref{NP-control}, we obtain the unique minimal norm control
\begin{align*}                   
u^*(t)
=\Big(\widetilde y^*_\lambda(t),\, \frac{\lambda(1-t)}{2-t}\Big)^\top,\qquad t\in[0,1].
\end{align*}
Moreover, It\^o's formula applied to $|\widetilde y^*_\lambda(t)|^2$ gives
$\mathbb{E}\big[|\widetilde y^*_\lambda(t)|^2\big]
=\lambda^2\big(\frac{1}{2-t}-\frac12\big)>0$,
for $t\in(0,1]$.
Thus, although both $x_0=0$ and $x_T=1$ are deterministic, the first component of the minimal norm control is genuinely stochastic.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{wang929r2}
The above example also illustrates the need for developing numerical methods for NP problems of MF-SDE systems. Even when both the initial state and the terminal target are deterministic, the minimal norm control may contain a nontrivial stochastic component and generally does not admit a simple closed-form representation. On the other hand, the constructive approach developed in this paper expresses the minimal norm control in terms of Riccati equations, an MF-BSDE, and a decoupled MF-SDE, thereby providing a feasible framework for its numerical computation. The design and convergence analysis of the corresponding numerical algorithms will be investigated in our future work.
