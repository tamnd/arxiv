<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:G}
Unless stated otherwise, \(G:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)\) satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(G\arabic*),leftmargin=2.5em]
\item \(G\) is finite and convex;
\item \(G(0)=0\), and \(0\) is the unique minimizer of \(G\);
\item \(G\) is coercive: \(G(u)\to\infty\) as \(|u|\to\infty\).
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:center-existence}
Suppose
\(\int G((1+\eta)x)\,\nu(dx)<\infty\) for some \(\eta>0\).
If \(\nu\ne0\), then \(\mathcal M_G(\nu)\) is a nonempty compact interval.
If \(G\) is strictly convex, it is a singleton.  Moreover
\[
 \mathcal M_G(\nu)\subseteq
 \bigl[\inf\operatorname{supp}(\nu),\,\sup\operatorname{supp}(\nu)\bigr],
\]
with the usual interpretation when the support is unbounded.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:flat-bottom}
The unique-minimum clause in (G2) is substantive.  For example, if
\(G(u)=\operatorname{dist}(u,[-1,1])^2\) and \(\nu=\delta_0\), then
\(\mathcal M_G(\nu)=[-1,1]\), which is not contained in
\(\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}\nu)=\{0\}\).  More generally, when
\(\operatorname*{arg\,min} G=[\ell,r]\), the existence and continuity results remain valid,
but the support-hull bound expands to
\[
 [\inf\operatorname{supp}(\nu)-r,\ \sup\operatorname{supp}(\nu)-\ell].
\]
All named losses in \Cref{tab:centers}, with parameters in their stated
open ranges, have the unique minimizer \(0\).
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:profiles}
The closed-left and open-left raw profiles are
\begin{align}
 H_G^\le(\mu,c)
  &:=\mathcal V_G(\mu_c^{\le,-})+\mathcal V_G(\mu_c^{\le,+}),\label{eq:Hle}\\
 H_G^<(\mu,c)
  &:=\mathcal V_G(\mu_c^{<,-})+\mathcal V_G(\mu_c^{<,+}).\label{eq:Hlt}
\end{align}
The boundary-relaxed profile is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hrel}
 \underline H_G(\mu,c)
 :=\min\{H_G^\le(\mu,c),H_G^<(\mu,c)\}.
\end{equation}
At \(c=\pm\infty\), all three profiles are defined to equal
\(\mathcal V_G(\mu)\).
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:conditional-centers}
For a fixed \(c\), every minimizer of
\[
 \inf_{a,b\in\mathbb R}\int
 \left[
   G(x-a)\1_{\{x\le c\}}+G(x-b)\1_{\{x>c\}}
 \right]\mu(dx)
\]
satisfies
\[
 a\in\mathcal M_G(\mu_c^{\le,-}),\qquad
 b\in\mathcal M_G(\mu_c^{\le,+})
\]
whenever the corresponding measure is nonzero.  The analogous statement
holds for the open-left convention.  Under strict convexity, both active
levels are unique.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For fixed \(c\), the objective is the sum
\[
 \int_{(-\infty,c]}G(x-a)\,\mu(dx)
 +\int_{(c,\infty)}G(x-b)\,\mu(dx).
\]
The variables separate, and each active variable must minimize its own
summand.  Strict convexity invokes
\Cref{prop:center-existence}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:halfline}
Fix \(a<b\).  The map
\[
 x\longmapsto G(x-a)-G(x-b)
\]
is nondecreasing.  Consequently, among all Borel sets \(A\), a minimizer of
\[
 \int\!\left[G(x-a)\1_A(x)+G(x-b)\1_{A^c}(x)\right]\mu(dx)
\]
can be chosen as a lower half-line.  If the two losses tie on an interval,
either complete assignment of the tie interval is optimal, so an open-left
or closed-left threshold representative exists.  The same conclusion
holds for \(a>b\) after interchanging labels.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Put \(d=b-a>0\) and \(u=x-b\).  The displayed difference is
\(G(u+d)-G(u)\).  Increments of a convex function over an interval of fixed
length are nondecreasing in the base point.  The objective equals
\[
 \int G(x-b)\,\mu(dx)
 +\int_A\!\bigl[G(x-a)-G(x-b)\bigr]\,\mu(dx).
\]
It is minimized pointwise by including the negative-difference set and
excluding the positive-difference set.  The strict sublevel set of a
nondecreasing function is a lower half-line, and its zero set is an
interval.  Assigning the entire zero interval to either side gives one of
the two stated boundary conventions.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:two-state}
Let
\[
 Q_G(\mu):=\inf_{\substack{a,b\in\mathbb R\\A\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R)}}
 \int\!\left[G(x-a)\1_A(x)+G(x-b)\1_{A^c}(x)\right]\,\mu(dx).
\]
Then
\[
 Q_G(\mu)
 =\inf_{c\in\overline{\mathbb R}}H_G^\le(\mu,c)
 =\min_{c\in\overline{\mathbb R}}\underline H_G(\mu,c).
\]
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
If \(a=b\), every assignment has the same integrand and any threshold is a
valid representative.
For \(a\ne b\), \Cref{prop:halfline} shows that, for every fixed pair, a
threshold assignment is at least as good as any Borel assignment.
Conversely, threshold assignments are Borel assignments.  Minimizing the
active levels at each threshold gives the relaxed profile.  The equality
with the raw closed-left infimum follows from
\(H_G^<(\mu,c)=H_G^\le(\mu,c-)\); see
\Cref{prop:profile-regularity}.  The same proposition gives lower
semicontinuity and attainment of the relaxed minimum.  Thus the relaxation
preserves the optimal value, although it can add a limiting action at an
atom.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:omega}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutset}
 \mathcal C_G(\mu):=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{c\in\overline{\mathbb R}}\underline H_G(\mu,c).
\end{equation}
For an action \(\theta=(a,b,c,\sigma)\), with
\(\sigma\in\{\le,<\}\), write
\[
 q_\theta(x)=
 \begin{cases}
  a\1_{\{x\le c\}}+b\1_{\{x>c\}},&\sigma=\le,\\
  a\1_{\{x<c\}}+b\1_{\{x\ge c\}},&\sigma=<,
 \end{cases}
\]
using the extended-order convention when \(c=\pm\infty\).
The action correspondence \(\mathcal A_G(\mu)\) consists of all such
\(\theta\) for which \(c\in\mathcal C_G(\mu)\), the convention \(\sigma\) attains
\eqref{eq:Hrel}, and each active level belongs to the corresponding center
set.  If one cell is empty, its inactive level is set equal to the active
level.  Finally,
\[
 \mathcal O_G(\mu):=\{q_\theta:\theta\in\mathcal A_G(\mu)\}
\]
is the associated encoding correspondence.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:center-order}
If probability laws \(\mu\preceq_{\mathrm{st}}\nu\) satisfy the standing
integrability assumptions, then
\[
 m_G^-(\mu)\le m_G^-(\nu),\qquad
 m_G^+(\mu)\le m_G^+(\nu).
\]
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Couple \(X\sim\mu,Y\sim\nu\) so that \(X\le Y\) almost surely.  If
\(m_1>m_2\), convexity makes
\[
 z\longmapsto G(z-m_1)-G(z-m_2)
\]
nonincreasing.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:center-crossing}
 \mathbb E[G(X-m_1)-G(X-m_2)]
 \ge \mathbb E[G(Y-m_1)-G(Y-m_2)].
\end{equation}
If \(x=m_G^-(\mu)>y=m_G^-(\nu)\), optimality makes the left side of
\eqref{eq:center-crossing}, with \((m_1,m_2)=(x,y)\), nonpositive and the
right side nonnegative.  Both must be zero.  Then \(y\) is also a
\(\mu\)-center, contradicting the minimality of \(x\).  The proof for the
maximal centers is identical: equality would make \(x\) a \(\nu\)-center
larger than \(m_G^+(\nu)\).
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:conditional-order}
Whenever both regimes at \(c\) have positive mass,
\[
 m_G^+(\mu_c^{\le,-})\le c\le m_G^-(\mu_c^{\le,+}).
\]
Both endpoints of the lower and upper conditional center sets are
nondecreasing functions of \(c\).  If
\(\mathcal M_G(\mu)=\{m_G(\mu)\}\), then any conditional-center selections satisfy
\[
 a(c)\to m_G(\mu)\quad(c\to+\infty),\qquad
 b(c)\to m_G(\mu)\quad(c\to-\infty).
\]
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:equivariance}
Let \(T_t\mu\) be the law of \(X+t\).  Then
\[
 \underline H_G(T_t\mu,c+t)=\underline H_G(\mu,c),\qquad
 \mathcal C_G(T_t\mu)=\mathcal C_G(\mu)+t,
\]
and an optimal cut-level triple \((a,b,c)\) becomes
\((a+t,b+t,c+t)\).

If \(G(su)=s^\gamma G(u)\) for every \(s>0\), and \(S_s\mu\) is the law of
\(sX\), then
\[
 \underline H_G(S_s\mu,sc)=s^\gamma\underline H_G(\mu,c),\qquad
 \mathcal C_G(S_s\mu)=s\mathcal C_G(\mu),
\]
with levels \((sa,sb)\).
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The substitutions \(m'=m-t\) and \(m=su\), respectively, give
\[
 \mathcal V_G(T_t\nu)=\mathcal V_G(\nu),\quad
 \mathcal M_G(T_t\nu)=\mathcal M_G(\nu)+t,
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal V_G(S_s\nu)=s^\gamma\mathcal V_G(\nu),\quad
 \mathcal M_G(S_s\nu)=s\mathcal M_G(\nu).
\]
Translation and positive scaling carry every half-line restriction to its
corresponding restriction at the transformed cutoff.  Applying the
identities to both profile terms proves the claims.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:retained-axioms}
The two-regime correspondence has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item It is law invariant.
\item It is cash equivariant.  If \(t\in\mathbb R\), then
  \[
   \mathcal A_G(\mathcal L(X+t))=\mathcal A_G(\mathcal L(X))+t,
  \]
  where \(\theta+t=(a+t,b+t,c+t,\sigma)\), with the natural convention at
  \(c=\pm\infty\).  Corresponding encodings satisfy
  \(\widehat{X+t}_{\,\theta+t}=\widehat X_\theta+t\) almost surely.
\item If \(G(su)=s^\gamma G(u)\) for \(s>0\), then, for every \(s>0\),
  the action correspondence is positive-scale equivariant:
  \[
   \mathcal A_G(\mathcal L(sX))=s\mathcal A_G(\mathcal L(X)),\qquad
   \widehat{sX}_{\,s\theta}=s\widehat X_\theta ,
  \]
  where \(s\theta=(sa,sb,sc,\sigma)\).
\item Every \(\theta\in\mathcal A_G(\mathcal L(X))\) has \(a\le b\), and its
  deterministic encoding is nondecreasing.  Moreover
  \[
   \operatorname*{ess\,inf}X
   \le \widehat X_\theta
   \le \operatorname*{ess\,sup}X
   \quad\text{almost surely},
  \]
  with the usual extended-real interpretation.
\item If \(X\) has at most two support points, then every optimal encoding
  is exact on the loss: \(\widehat X_\theta=X\) almost surely.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Law invariance follows because the profile, center sets, and optimal actions
are defined from \(\mathcal L(X)\).  The translation and scaling statements are
\Cref{prop:equivariance}, including the corresponding transformation of the
boundary convention.  For two nonempty regimes,
\Cref{cor:conditional-order} gives \(a\le c\le b\); an empty-regime output
is constant by definition.  The support-hull part of
\Cref{prop:center-existence} places each active level in the closed hull of
its regime support, proving the range bound and internal monotonicity.
Finally, a law with at most two support points admits a two-level encoding
of cost zero.  Since \(0\) is the unique minimizer of \(G\), every
zero-cost optimum agrees with \(X\) almost surely.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:ray-additivity}
Suppose \(G\) is positively homogeneous as in
\Cref{prop:retained-axioms}.  Choose the encodings compatibly with
translation and positive scaling.  Here \(\widehat Z\) denotes the chosen
optimal encoding of a loss \(Z\).  For \(\lambda>0\) and \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \widehat{\lambda X+t}=\lambda\widehat X+t.
\]
The same identity holds at \(\lambda=0\) at the encoding level, because a
constant loss is reproduced exactly.
In particular, when \(Y=\lambda X+t\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ray-additivity}
 \widehat{X+Y}=\widehat X+\widehat Y .
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For \(\lambda>0\), the first identity is
\Cref{prop:retained-axioms}(ii)--(iii).  Apply it to
\(Y=\lambda X+t\) and to \(X+Y=(1+\lambda)X+t\) to obtain
\eqref{eq:ray-additivity}.  If \(\lambda=0\), then \(Y=t\) has the constant
encoding \(t\), and translation equivariance gives the same conclusion.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:fixed-partition}
Let \(A\) be an exogenously fixed event with
\(\mathbb P(A),\mathbb P(A^c)>0\), and summarize a loss by one \(G\)-center
on \(A\) and one on \(A^c\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item If \(X\le Y\) almost surely, then the minimal and maximal conditional
center selections for \(X\) do not exceed their counterparts for \(Y\), in
either regime.
\item If \(G(u)=u^2\), then the two conditional means are additive:
\[
 \bigl(\mathbb E[X+Y\mid A],\mathbb E[X+Y\mid A^c]\bigr)
 =
 \bigl(\mathbb E[X\mid A],\mathbb E[X\mid A^c]\bigr)
 +
 \bigl(\mathbb E[Y\mid A],\mathbb E[Y\mid A^c]\bigr).
\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Conditioning the inequality \(X\le Y\) on either common event gives
first-order stochastic order of the two conditional laws, so (i) follows
from \Cref{prop:center-order}.  Part (ii) is linearity of conditional
expectation.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:mean-shadow}
Let \(G(u)=u^2\) and \(X\in L^2\).  Every optimal regime encoding preserves
the unconditional mean:
\[
 \mathbb E\widehat X_\theta=\mathbb E X,\qquad
 \theta\in\mathcal A_G(\mathcal L(X)).
\]
Consequently the probability-weighted barycenter of the two reported levels
is the monotone, cash-additive, and additive scalar risk functional
\(\mathbb E X\), independently of the selected optimal cutoff.
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
On every nonempty regime, the quadratic center is the corresponding
conditional mean.  Applying the law of total expectation to the two regime
events gives \(\mathbb E\widehat X_\theta=\mathbb E X\).  If one regime is empty, the encoding
is the unconditional mean and the same identity holds.
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Suppose a single-valued one-regime center \(m_G\) is subadditive on every
conditional probability space, and the same event \(A\) is optimal for
\(X\), \(Y\), and \(X+Y\).  Applying subadditivity under
\(\mathbb P(\,\cdot\mid A)\) and
\(\mathbb P(\,\cdot\mid A^c)\) gives
\[
 a_{X+Y}\le a_X+a_Y,\qquad b_{X+Y}\le b_X+b_Y.
\]
For quadratic loss these are equalities by
\Cref{prop:fixed-partition}; upper expectiles provide a subadditive
one-regime example for \(\tau\in[1/2,1)\), their coherent parameter range
\citep{BelliniKlarMuellerRosazza2014}.  This result is deliberately
regime-aligned: endogenous repartitioning invalidates its premise.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- proposition-9: body_md -->
\label{prop:monotonicity-fails}
Let \(G(u)=u^2\) and let the three states be equiprobable.  Set
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
       &1&2&3\\ \hline
 X     &0&1&3\\
 Y     &0&2&3
 \end{array}
 \qquad\text{so that }X\le Y.
\]
The unique optimal support partition of \(X\) is
\(\{0,1\}\mid\{3\}\), with levels \((1/2,3)\), whereas the unique optimal
support partition of \(Y\) is
\(\{0\}\mid\{2,3\}\), with levels \((0,5/2)\).  Hence both optimized
levels decrease under the deterioration \(X\le Y\), and the encoded losses
are not ordered.
<!-- end proposition-9 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
For \(X\), the costs of the two nontrivial threshold partitions are
\[
 \frac{(0-\tfrac12)^2+(1-\tfrac12)^2}{3}=\frac16,
 \qquad
 \frac{(1-2)^2+(3-2)^2}{3}=\frac23.
\]
Thus \(\{0,1\}\mid\{3\}\) is the unique optimal partition.  For \(Y\),
the same two costs occur in the opposite order:
\[
 \frac{(2-\tfrac52)^2+(3-\tfrac52)^2}{3}=\frac16,
 \qquad
 \frac{(0-1)^2+(2-1)^2}{3}=\frac23.
\]
The corresponding reported values are
\[
 \widehat X=(1/2,1/2,3),\qquad
 \widehat Y=(0,5/2,5/2).
\]
The desired inequality fails in both the first and third states.  The full
closed cutoff gaps do not affect any value on the support.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{prop:subadd-fails}
Let \(G(u)=u^2\) on three equiprobable states and set
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
       &1&2&3\\ \hline
 X     &0&0&2\\
 Y     &0&2&3\\
 X+Y   &0&2&5 .
 \end{array}
\]
The variables \(X\) and \(Y\) are comonotone.  Their unique optimal support
partitions induce the level pairs
\[
 (0,2),\qquad (0,5/2),\qquad (1,5)
\]
for \(X,Y,X+Y\), respectively.  Consequently
\[
 5>2+\frac52,
\]
and the scenario-wise subadditivity inequality fails.
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
The two-point loss \(X\) is reproduced exactly, so its optimized cost is
zero.  The calculation for \(Y\) is the second half of the proof of
\Cref{prop:monotonicity-fails}: its optimal partition is
\(\{0\}\mid\{2,3\}\), with levels \((0,5/2)\) and cost \(1/6\).
For \(X+Y\), splitting \(\{0,2\}\mid\{5\}\) gives levels \((1,5)\) and
cost
\[
 \frac{(0-1)^2+(2-1)^2}{3}=\frac23,
\]
whereas splitting \(\{0\}\mid\{2,5\}\) gives cost
\[
 \frac{(2-\tfrac72)^2+(5-\tfrac72)^2}{3}=\frac32.
\]
Hence the stated partition is unique.  At the third state the combined
report is \(5\), while the sum of the separate reports is \(2+5/2\);
at the first state the combined lower report \(1\) also exceeds \(0+0\).
<!-- end proof-11 -->
<!-- proposition-11: body_md -->
\label{prop:D-envelope}
For \(p\ge1\) and any jointly defined \(X,Y\in L^p\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D-envelope}
 D_{2,p}(X+Y)
 \le
 \min\!\left\{
  D_{2,p}(X)+D_{1,p}(Y),\
  D_{1,p}(X)+D_{2,p}(Y)
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
In general neither \(D_{2,p}\) nor \(Q_G\) is subadditive with its own
two-level value on the right-hand side.
<!-- end proposition-11 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
For any two-point codebook \(A=\{z_1,z_2\}\) and \(m\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(X+Y,A+m)\le \operatorname{dist}(X,A)+|Y-m|
\]
pointwise.  Minkowski's inequality and then minimization over \(A\) and
\(m\) give the first bound inside the minimum; interchanging \(X,Y\) gives
the second.  In \Cref{prop:subadd-fails},
\[
 D_{2,2}(X)=0,\qquad
 D_{2,2}(Y)=\sqrt{\frac16},\qquad
 D_{2,2}(X+Y)=\sqrt{\frac23},
\]
which disproves two-level subadditivity.  The corresponding squared
distortions are \(0,1/6,2/3\), so \(Q_G\) fails it as well.
<!-- end proof-12 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
For quadratic loss, the one-regime center is the mean and is monotone and
additive.  Upper expectiles generated by asymmetric quadratic loss satisfy
coherence properties for \(\tau\in[1/2,1)\), whereas quantiles
need not be subadditive
\citep{BelliniKlarMuellerRosazza2014,Artzner1999,RockafellarUryasev2002}.
The counterexamples
above use the mean inside every block.  They show that favorable axioms of
the one-regime center do not automatically pass through endogenous

 \le\operatorname*{ess\,sup}X+\operatorname*{ess\,sup}Y
\]

not subadditivity of the two-regime summary.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:psi-characterization}
Condition \eqref{eq:psi-characterization} is equivalent to weak convergence
plus uniform \(\psi\)-integrability,
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\sup_n
 \int_{\{\psi>M\}}\psi\,d\nu_n=0.
\]
It is also equivalent to convergence of integrals for every continuous
\(f\) satisfying \(|f|\le C\psi\).  These equivalences follow by truncating
at compact sublevel sets of the continuous coercive function \(\psi\).
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Weak convergence controls \(\psi\wedge M\).  Together with convergence of
the \(\psi\)-moments, this makes the tails uniformly small; for example,
\[
 y\1_{\{y>2M\}}\le2(y-M)_+,\qquad
 \int(\psi-M)_+\,d\nu_n
 =\int\psi\,d\nu_n-\int(\psi\wedge M)\,d\nu_n.
\]
The finitely many initial indices are handled separately.  Conversely,
uniform tail control and weak convergence give moment convergence.
Truncating a continuous \(f\) with \(|f|\le C\psi\) on the compact sets
\(\{\psi\le M\}\) proves the test-function characterization.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:wasserstein}
For probability laws and \(G(u)=|u|^p\), \(p\ge1\), \(\psi\)-weak
convergence is exactly \(p\)-Wasserstein convergence: weak convergence plus
convergence of the \(p\)th absolute moments \citep{Villani2009}.  The same holds for losses
whose two tails are comparable to \(|u|^p\).  In particular, check,
absolute, and Huber losses induce the \(1\)-Wasserstein topology, whereas
quadratic and asymmetric quadratic losses induce the \(2\)-Wasserstein
topology.

<!-- proposition-12: body_md -->
\label{prop:profile-regularity}
For fixed \(\mu\), \(c\mapsto H_G^\le(\mu,c)\) is c\`adl\`ag on
\(\mathbb R\), with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:left-limit}

\end{equation}

 =\min\{H_G^\le(\mu,c-),H_G^\le(\mu,c)\}

\(\mathcal C_G(\mu)\) is a nonempty compact subset of \(\overline{\mathbb R}\).

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:regime-conv}
Assume \(\mu_n\xrightarrow{\psi}\mu\), and the limiting relaxed profile has a unique
finite minimizer \(c^\star\) satisfying
\[
 \mu(\{c^\star\})=0,\qquad
 0<\mu((-\infty,c^\star))<1.
\]
Assume also that the two limiting restrictions have unique \(G\)-centers
\(a^\star,b^\star\).  Let \(c_n\in\mathcal C_G(\mu_n)\), and choose an action
\(\theta_n=(a_n,b_n,c_n,\sigma_n)\in\mathcal A_G(\mu_n)\).  Then
\[
 c_n\to c^\star,\qquad a_n\to a^\star,\qquad b_n\to b^\star.
\]
For the canonical right-continuous graph representatives
\(r_n=r_{a_n,b_n,c_n}\) and
\(r=r_{a^\star,b^\star,c^\star}\):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \(r_n(x)\to r(x)\) at every \(x\ne c^\star\);
\item \(r_n\to r\) in \(L^p_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R)\) for every finite
  \(p\ge1\);
\item \(r_n\to r\) in the local Skorokhod \(J_1\) topology.
\end{enumerate}
If the cost-realizing convention assigns an atom at \(c_n\) to the lower
cell, its exact optimal encoding need not equal \(r_n\) at that one point.
Nevertheless \(\mu_n(\{c_n\})\to0\), so the discrepancy vanishes in
\(\mu_n\)-probability and in expected loss.  Strict convexity of \(G\)
implies the required uniqueness of the limiting centers.

<!-- proposition-13: body_md -->
\label{prop:step-equivalence}
Let \(a<b\), let \(a_n\le b_n\), and let \(r_n,r\) have the form
\eqref{eq:cadlag-regime}.  Suppose the parameter sequences are bounded.
On every compact interval containing \(c\) in its interior, the following
are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \((a_n,b_n,c_n)\to(a,b,c)\);
\item \(r_n(x)\to r(x)\) at every continuity point of \(r\);
\item \(r_n\to r\) in the \(J_1\) topology.
\end{enumerate}
Each condition implies Lebesgue \(L^p\) convergence for every finite \(p\).
If
\(a=b\), the cutoff is not identifiable and only convergence of the
constant function is meaningful.
<!-- end proposition-13 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
(a) implies (b) directly.  For (a) implies (c), on
\([L,U]\) choose an increasing piecewise-linear homeomorphism fixing
\(L,U\) and satisfying \(\lambda_n(c)=c_n\).  Then
\(\|\lambda_n-\mathrm{id}\|_\infty\to0\), and
\[
 \sup_{x\in[L,U]}|r_n(\lambda_n(x))-r(x)|
 \le\max\{|a_n-a|,|b_n-b|\}\to0.
\]

Assume (b).  For every \(\varepsilon>0\) small enough that
\(c-\varepsilon,c+\varepsilon\in(L,U)\), the two limiting values at these
points are the distinct numbers \(a,b\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\),
the evaluations therefore lie on different plateaus, so
\[
 c-\varepsilon<c_n\le c+\varepsilon.
\]
Hence \(c_n\to c\).  Evaluating at any fixed points on the two sides then
gives \(a_n\to a\) and \(b_n\to b\), so (b) implies (a).  General \(J_1\)
convergence implies convergence at every
continuity point of the limit, hence (c) implies (b).  Finally, boundedness
and pointwise convergence away from one point give \(L^p\) convergence by
dominated convergence.

<!-- proposition-14: body_md -->
\label{prop:finite-exact}
For \(\mu\) in \eqref{eq:finite-law},
\[
 \min_{c\in\overline{\mathbb R}}\underline H_G(\mu,c)
 =\min_{0\le j\le m-1}C_j.
\]
If \(m=1\), the unique optimal encoding is the constant \(x_1\), and every
cutoff in \(\overline{\mathbb R}\) is optimal.  If \(m\ge2\) and \(j\ge1\) minimizes
\(C_j\), every cutoff in the closed gap
\([x_j,x_{j+1}]\) realizes that split under one of the two boundary
conventions.  Conversely, every nonconstant optimal threshold corresponds
to a minimizing split index.  Thus complete enumeration returns a global,
not merely local, optimum.  For \(m\ge2\), the one-regime cost \(C_0\) is
strictly larger than at least one nontrivial split cost.
<!-- end proposition-14 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
For \(m=1\), every threshold has one active cell, its center is \(x_1\), and
the cost is zero.  Now let \(m\ge2\).  Every threshold partition of the
ordered distinct support is a prefix and its complementary suffix, except
for the empty/full one-regime endpoints.
For a fixed prefix, \Cref{prop:conditional-centers} makes the two block
optimizations independent, giving exactly \eqref{eq:Cj-general}.  A cutoff
strictly inside \((x_j,x_{j+1})\) realizes split \(j\) under either
convention.  At \(x_j\) or \(x_{j+1}\), the boundary relaxation chooses the
convention that still realizes split \(j\), hence the full closed gap.
No other partition is induced by a threshold.
Finally, let \(m_0\) be a center of the full law.  At least one extreme
support value, say \(x_e\in\{x_1,x_m\}\), differs from \(m_0\).  Isolate
that extreme in a singleton cell and evaluate the other cell at \(m_0\).
The resulting cost is at most
\[
 C_0-p_eG(x_e-m_0)<C_0,
\]
where the strict inequality uses the unique minimum of \(G\) at zero.

<!-- algorithm-1: body_md -->
\label{alg:master}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \emph{Input.} Supply a finite support, convex loss \(G\), block-cost
error bound \(\varepsilon_{\rm block}\), and selection tolerance
\(\varepsilon_{\rm sel}\).  Use the uniform candidate-cost bound
\(\varepsilon_C:=2\varepsilon_{\rm block}\), since a nontrivial candidate
adds two block costs (the one-block candidate satisfies the same bound).
\item Sort values, reject invalid weights, and collapse exact duplicates.
If one support value remains, return its constant encoding and the full
cutoff set \(\overline{\mathbb R}\), then stop.
\item Compute a center and cost estimate \(\widehat C_0\) for the full
block.
\item For \(j=1,\ldots,m-1\), compute lower and upper block centers and
costs, and set \(\widehat C_j\) equal to their sum.
\item Set
\[
\widehat C_{\min}=\min_{0\le j\le m-1}\widehat C_j,\qquad
\widehat J=
\{j:\widehat C_j\le\widehat C_{\min}+\varepsilon_{\rm sel}\}.
\]
\item For every \(j\in\widehat J\setminus\{0\}\), report the candidate
interval \([x_j,x_{j+1}]\) and a display cutoff computed by an
overflow-safe midpoint routine, both regime levels, and the severe-regime
probability \(1-\sum_{i\le j}p_i\).
\item \emph{Output.} Report \(\widehat C_{\min}\), \(\widehat J\), the
candidate intervals, levels, severe-regime probabilities, and the bound from
\Cref{prop:numerical-certificate}.  Call \(\widehat J\) complete only when
its stated tolerance condition holds; exact arithmetic is the special case
\(\varepsilon_{\rm block}=\varepsilon_{\rm sel}=0\).
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-15: body_md -->
\label{prop:numerical-certificate}
Let the exact candidates be \(C_0,\ldots,C_{m-1}\), and suppose
\[
 |\widehat C_j-C_j|\le\varepsilon_C
 \qquad(0\le j\le m-1).
\]
If an index \(\widehat j\) is selected with
\[
 \widehat C_{\widehat j}
 \le\min_j\widehat C_j+\varepsilon_{\rm sel},
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-certificate}
 C_{\widehat j}
 \le\min_jC_j+2\varepsilon_C+\varepsilon_{\rm sel}.
\end{equation}
If each of the two block costs has error at most
\(\varepsilon_{\rm block}\), one may take
\(\varepsilon_C=2\varepsilon_{\rm block}\).
Moreover, if \(\varepsilon_{\rm sel}\ge2\varepsilon_C\), then every exact
minimizer is retained:
\[
 \operatorname*{arg\,min}_j C_j\subseteq\widehat J.
\]
Without this inequality, an exact minimizer can be omitted from
\(\widehat J\).
<!-- end proposition-15 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Let \(j^\star\) minimize the exact costs.  Then
\[
 C_{\widehat j}
 \le\widehat C_{\widehat j}+\varepsilon_C
 \le\widehat C_{j^\star}
   +\varepsilon_{\rm sel}+\varepsilon_C
 \le C_{j^\star}
   +\varepsilon_{\rm sel}+2\varepsilon_C.
\]
For completeness, let \(\widehat k\) minimize \(\widehat C_j\).  If
\(j^\star\) is any exact minimizer, then
\[
 \widehat C_{j^\star}-\widehat C_{\widehat k}
 \le (C_{j^\star}+\varepsilon_C)
      -(C_{\widehat k}-\varepsilon_C)
 \le2\varepsilon_C.
\]
Hence \(j^\star\in\widehat J\) under the stated condition.  Equality can
occur in both error bounds, so no smaller universal tolerance guarantees
inclusion.

<!-- proposition-16: body_md -->
\label{prop:four-rule}
For \eqref{eq:four-law} and \(G(u)=u^2\), let
\[
 J(u,v):=\{j\in\{1,2,3\}:y_j<y_{j+1}\},
\qquad
 J^\star(u,v):=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{j\in J(u,v)}C_j.
\]
For every \(j^\star\in J^\star(u,v)\), a displayed cutoff and the two
levels are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:four-cut}
 c^\star=\frac{y_{j^\star}+y_{j^\star+1}}2,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:four-levels}
 a^\star=\frac1{j^\star}\sum_{i=1}^{j^\star}y_i,\qquad
 b^\star=\frac1{4-j^\star}\sum_{i=j^\star+1}^4y_i.
\end{equation}
The full optimal cutoff set is
\[
 \bigcup_{j\in J^\star(u,v)}[y_j,y_{j+1}].
\]
If support values coincide, indices with \(y_j=y_{j+1}\) are
inadmissible; equivalently, collapse the duplicate atom and add its
probabilities before running the weighted algorithm.

<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Every threshold-admissible lower regime is a prefix of the ordered
multiset.  A split through equal adjacent values would divide one atom and
is therefore inadmissible.  For \(k\) equally weighted observations, the
identity
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^k(z_i-\bar z)^2
 =\frac1k\sum_{1\le i<\ell\le k}(z_i-z_\ell)^2
\]
and the outer weight \(1/4\) give
\eqref{eq:C1-four}--\eqref{eq:C3-four}.  The conditional means give
\eqref{eq:four-levels}.  \Cref{prop:finite-exact} proves global optimality
and shows that a minimizing split contributes its full closed support gap.
The midpoint is only a deterministic display representative.  The fixed
values \(0\) and \(3\) make the law nondegenerate, so the one-regime
candidate is never optimal: isolating an extreme support atom at which a
one-regime center has nonzero residual strictly lowers its cost.  Thus the
three displayed split costs exhaust all optima.
