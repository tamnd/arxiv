<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For every sufficiently large odd integer $n$, there exist a regular vertex-transitive tournament $D_n$
on $n$ vertices and a balanced antidirected caterpillar $T_n$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
|V(T_n)|=n-1,\qquad
\Delta(T_n)\le(1+o(1))\frac{n}{\log_2 n},\qquad T_n\nsubseteq D_n.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-semi}
\delta^0(D_n)=\bar\delta^{0}(D_n)=\frac{n-1}{2}
=\frac{|E(T_n)|}{2}+\frac12.
\end{equation}
The tree $T_n$ may be chosen as a function of $n$ alone.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:ec}
For every sufficiently large odd integer $n$, there exists a regular vertex-transitive tournament on
$n$ vertices that is $s_n$-e.c., where

\[
M=\frac{n-1}{2},\qquad
s_n=\left\lfloor\log_2 M-4\log_2\log M-C\right\rfloor
\]
for a sufficiently large fixed absolute constant $C>0$. In particular,

\[
s_n=\log_2 n-O(\log\log n).
\]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:leaf}
Let $D$ be an $s$-e.c. tournament on $n$ vertices, and let $T$ be an antidirected tree on $n-1$
vertices. Suppose that $U\subseteq V(T)$ is nonempty and that every vertex outside $U$ is a leaf
whose neighbour belongs to $U$. If $|U|+1\le s$, then $T\nsubseteq D$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Suppose that $\varphi$ embeds $T$ in $D$, and let $w$ be the unique vertex outside
$\varphi(V(T))$. Write $U^+$ and $U^-$ for the sources and sinks of $T$ that belong to $U$,
and put $X=\varphi(U^+)$ and $Y=\varphi(U^-)$. Since $|X|+|Y|+1\le s$, existential closure
applied to $X\cup\{w\}$ and $Y$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:incompatible}
\begin{gathered}
z\notin X\cup Y\cup\{w\},\\
zx\in E(D)\quad(x\in X\cup\{w\}),\qquad
 yz\in E(D)\quad(y\in Y).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Including $w$ among the prescribed vertices ensures that $z$ lies in the copy. Thus
$z=\varphi(v)$ for some $v\in V(T)\setminus U$, and $v$ is a leaf with a neighbour $u\in U$.
If $u\in U^+$, the arc $uv\in E(T)$ requires $\varphi(u)z\in E(D)$, contrary to
$z\varphi(u)\in E(D)$. If $u\in U^-$, the arc $vu\in E(T)$ requires
$z\varphi(u)\in E(D)$, contrary to $\varphi(u)z\in E(D)$. Either case
gives a contradiction.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
{Let $s_n$ be the integer sequence fixed in Theorem~\ref{thm:ec}; recall that
$s_n=\log_2 n-O(\log\log n)$.} For each
sufficiently large odd $n$, choose an $s_n$-e.c. regular vertex-transitive tournament $D_n$
supplied by that theorem. To apply Lemma~\ref{lem:leaf}, we need a spine with at most $s_n-1$
vertices. We take it as long as possible subject to having equally many sources and sinks, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
m=\frac{n-1}{2},\qquad r=\left\lfloor\frac{s_n-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
For large $n$ we have $2\le r\le m$, $2r+1\le s_n$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-asymptotic}
r=\frac12\log_2 n-O(\log\log n).
\end{equation}
Start with the path on the ordered vertices $a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2,\ldots,a_r,b_r$, with arc set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spine}
\{a_i b_i:1\le i\le r\}\cup\{a_{i+1}b_i:1\le i<r\}.
\end{equation}
Attach $m-r$ new leaves to the vertices $a_1,\ldots,a_r$, with arcs directed away from the
spine, and another $m-r$ new leaves to $b_1,\ldots,b_r$, with arcs directed towards the spine.
In each group distribute the leaves as evenly as possible: if $m-r=qr+\ell$, where
$0\le\ell<r$, give the first $\ell$ vertices $q+1$ leaves each and every other vertex $q$ leaves.
Call the resulting tree $T_n$. Its underlying graph is a caterpillar, and every vertex is a source or
a sink. The source class consists of the $r$ vertices $a_i$ and the $m-r$ leaves attached to the
$b_i$'s; the sink class consists of the other $m$ vertices. Thus $T_n$ is balanced, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tree-order}
|V(T_n)|=2m=n-1,\qquad |E(T_n)|=2m-1=n-2.
\end{equation}
Each spine vertex has at most two neighbours on the spine and at most
$\lceil(m-r)/r\rceil$ attached leaves. All other vertices have degree one, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tree-degree}
\Delta(T_n)\le2+\left\lceil\frac{m-r}{r}\right\rceil
=1+\left\lceil\frac mr\right\rceil
=(1+o(1))\frac n{\log_2 n},
\end{equation}
where the last equality follows from $m\sim n/2$ and~\eqref{eq:r-asymptotic}.

Now take $U=\{a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_r,b_r\}$. Every vertex outside $U$ is a leaf adjacent to $U$,
and $|U|+1=2r+1\le s_n$. Lemma~\ref{lem:leaf} therefore gives $T_n\nsubseteq D_n$. Since
$D_n$ is regular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:final-semi}
\delta^0(D_n)=\bar\delta^{0}(D_n)=m=\frac{|E(T_n)|}{2}+\frac12.
\end{equation}
The attachment rule determines $T_n$ from $n$ and the fixed sequence $s_n$, independently of the
choice of $D_n$. This proves the theorem.
