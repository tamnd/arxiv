\label{mainthm:A}
Let $\phi\colon X\to Y$ be a Q-chart with $X$ elastic.
For every $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant vertical connection $K$, horizontal connection $H$ and Riemannian metric $g$ on $X$ there exist a unique vertical connection $K_Y$, a unique horizontal connection $H_Y$ and a unique Riemannian metric $g_Y$ on $Y$ such that
\[
  K_Y\circ T^2\phi=T\phi\circ K,\qquad
  H_Y\circ T_2\phi=T^2\phi\circ H,\qquad
  g_Y\circ T_2\phi=g .
\]
Conversely, every vertical connection, horizontal connection and Riemannian metric on $Y$ arises in this way from a unique $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant one on $X$.
Moreover $K$ is torsion-free, effective or flat if and only if $K_Y$ is; $(K,H)$ is a full connection if and only if $(K_Y,H_Y)$ is; $K$ is a Levi-Civita connection of $g$ if and only if $K_Y$ is one of $g_Y$; each $T_x\phi$ is a linear isometry; and a path $\gamma$ in $X$ is a geodesic of $K$ if and only if $\phi\circ\gamma$ is a geodesic of $K_Y$.

<!-- mainthm-2: body_md -->
\label{mainthm:B}
Let a diffeologically discrete group $\Lambda$ act principally on an elastic space $X$, and let $\phi\colon X\to Y=X/\Lambda$ be the quotient map.
Then $\phi$ is a Q-chart, and $\Psi(\phi)$-invariance means $\Lambda$-invariance.
Let $(K,H)$ be a $\Lambda$-invariant full connection and let $g$ be a $\Lambda$-invariant Riemannian metric.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item Every geodesic of $K_Y$ lifts to a geodesic of $K$, uniquely once the initial point is fixed; the geodesics of $Y$ correspond bijectively to the $\Lambda$-orbits of geodesics of $X$.
\item If $X\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$, then $Y\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$.
\item If $X\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$ and the geodesic spray of $(K,H)$ is complete, then so is that of $(K_Y,H_Y)$; the geodesic flows are intertwined by $T\phi$, and every $w\in TY$ is the initial velocity of exactly one geodesic of $Y$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- mainthm-3: body_md -->
\label{mainthm:C}
Let $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item $C^\infty(\mathbb{T}_\alpha,\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}$, $\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{T}_\alpha)=\mathbb{R}\,\bar\partial$ and $\Omega^1(\mathbb{T}_\alpha)=\mathbb{R}\,\vartheta$.
\item The Riemannian metrics on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ are exactly $\rho\,\vartheta^2$ with $\rho\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ a constant.
\item The vertical connections on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ form a one-parameter family; all of them are torsion-free, effective and flat.
\item For every constant $\rho>0$, the metric $\rho\,\vartheta^2$ has a unique Levi-Civita connection; it is independent of $\rho$, namely the connection $K_0$ induced by the trivial connection on $\mathbb{R}$.
  The geodesics of $K_0$ are the paths $t\mapsto[x+tv]$ $(x,v\in\mathbb{R})$, and its geodesic flow is complete.
\item The pseudo-distance induced by $\rho\,\vartheta^2$ vanishes identically; nevertheless, for $[x]\neq[y]$ no path from $[x]$ to $[y]$ realizes the infimum defining it, that is, every such path has positive length.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- defn-1: body_md -->
\label{def:over}
Let $p\colon E\to M$ and $p'\colon E'\to M$ be maps.
A map $f\colon E\to E'$ is a \emph{map over $M$} if $p'\circ f=p$, that is, if $f$ is a morphism in the slice category $\mathsf{Diff}/M$.
In particular, for $F,F'\in\mathcal{F}$ a map $f\colon FM\to F'M$ is over $M$ (with respect to the projections $\pi^F_M$, $\pi^{F'}_M$) if $\pi^{F'}_M\circ f=\pi^F_M$, i.e.\ if $(f,\operatorname{id}_M)$ is a bundle morphism in the sense of \cite[Def.~A.2]{Shio}.

<!-- defn-2: body_md -->
\label{def:vconn}
A \emph{vertical connection} on $M$ is a map $K\colon T^2M\to TM$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item $K\circ\lambda_M=\operatorname{id}_{TM}$;
\item $\pi_M\circ K=\pi_M\circ\pi_{TM}$;
\item $\lambda_M\circ K=T(K)\circ\lambda_{TM}$;
\item $\lambda_M\circ K=T(K)\circ\tau_{TM}\circ T(\lambda_M)$;
\item $K\circ\kappa_{TM}=\kappa_M\circ(\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times K)$;
\item $K\circ T_{(2)}\kappa_M=\kappa_M\circ(\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times K)$.
\end{enumerate}
It is \emph{torsion-free} if $K\circ\tau_M=K$, and \emph{effective} if
\[
  \Theta^K_M:=\bigl\langle\pi_{TM},\,T(\pi_M),\,K\bigr\rangle\colon
  T^2M\longrightarrow T_3M
\]
is a diffeomorphism.
The \emph{curvature morphism} of $K$ is $C_K:=K\circ T(K)\circ\tau_{TM}-K\circ T(K)\colon T^3M\to TM$, the difference being taken in the fibres of $\pi_M$ \cite[Def.~5.1]{Shio}; $K$ is \emph{flat} if $C_K=0_M\circ\pi^{T^3}_M$.
<!-- end defn-2 -->
<!-- defn-3: body_md -->
\label{def:hconn}
A \emph{horizontal connection} on $M$ is a map $H\colon T_2M\to T^2M$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item $T(\pi_M)\circ H=\operatorname{pr}_1$;
\item $\pi_{TM}\circ H=\operatorname{pr}_2$;
\item $\lambda_{TM}\circ H=T(H)\circ(\lambda_M\times 0_{TM})$;
\item $\tau_{TM}\circ T(\lambda_M)\circ H=T(H)\circ(0_{TM}\times\lambda_M)$;
\item $H\circ\kappa^{(1)}=\kappa_{TM}\circ(\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times H)$;
\item $H\circ\kappa^{(2)}=T_{(2)}\kappa_M\circ(\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times H)$,
\end{enumerate}
where in (c), (d) the pairs take values in $T^2M\times_{T(\pi_M)}T^2M\cong T(T_2M)$ via \eqref{eq:chi}.
A pair $(K,H)$ is a \emph{full connection} if
\[
  \text{(F1)}\ \ K\circ H=0_M\circ\pi_M\circ\operatorname{pr}_1,\qquad
  \text{(F2)}\ \ \mu_M\circ\langle K,\pi_{TM}\rangle+H\circ U_M=\operatorname{id}_{T^2M},
\]
where $U_M:=\langle T(\pi_M),\pi_{TM}\rangle\colon T^2M\to T_2M$,
\[
  \mu_M:=T(+_M)\circ\bigl((\lambda_M\circ\operatorname{pr}_1)\times(0_{TM}\circ\operatorname{pr}_2)\bigr)
  \colon T_2M\to T^2M ,
\]
and the sum in (F2) is taken in the bundle $\pi_{TM}\colon T^2M\to TM$.
<!-- end defn-3 -->
<!-- defn-4: body_md -->
\label{def:metric}
A \emph{Riemannian metric} on $M$ is a map $g\colon T_2M\to\mathbb{R}$ whose restriction to $T_xM\times T_xM$ is a positive definite symmetric bilinear form for every $x\in M$.
A vertical connection $K$ is a \emph{Levi-Civita connection} of $g$ if it is torsion-free and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LC}
  d(g)=+_\mathbb{R}\circ\bigl\langle g\circ(K\times_\pi\pi_{TM}),\;
  g\circ(\pi_{TM}\times_\pi K)\bigr\rangle ,
\end{equation}
as maps $T(T_2M)\to\mathbb{R}$, where $d(g):=\operatorname{pr}_2\circ T(g)$, $T(T_2M)$ is identified with $T^2M\times_{T(\pi_M)}T^2M$ by \eqref{eq:chi}, and $(K\times_\pi\pi_{TM})(A,B)=(K(A),\pi_{TM}(B))$, $(\pi_{TM}\times_\pi K)(A,B)=(\pi_{TM}(A),K(B))$.

<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:geod-spray}
Let $(K,H)$ be a full connection on $M$ with geodesic spray $S$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item A path $\gamma$ is a geodesic of $K$ if and only if $\dot\gamma$ is an integral curve of $S$.
\item If $c\colon\mathbb{R}\to TM$ is an integral curve of $S$, then $\gamma:=\pi_M\circ c$ is a geodesic and $\dot\gamma=c$.
\item If $M\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$, a geodesic is determined by its initial velocity.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
(i) Let $W:=T(\dot\gamma)\circ\partial\colon\mathbb{R}\to T^2M$.
Then $\pi_{TM}W=\dot\gamma$ and $T(\pi_M)W=T(\pi_M\dot\gamma)\partial=\dot\gamma$, so $U_MW=(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)$ and $HU_MW=S\dot\gamma$.
If $\gamma$ is a geodesic, then $KW=0_M\gamma$, and since $\lambda_M\circ0_M=0_{TM}\circ0_M$ and $T(+_M)\circ 0_{T_2M}=0_{TM}\circ+_M$ we get $\mu_M\langle K,\pi_{TM}\rangle W=0_{TM}\dot\gamma$, the zero of the fibre of $\pi_{TM}$ over $\dot\gamma$; hence (F2) gives $W=S\dot\gamma$.
Conversely, if $W=S\dot\gamma$ then $KW=KH\langle\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma\rangle=0_M\gamma$ by (F1).
(ii) Since $T(\pi_M)\circ S=\operatorname{id}_{TM}$ by (a) of Definition~\ref{def:hconn}, $\dot\gamma=T(\pi_M)T(c)\partial=T(\pi_M)Sc=c$; now apply (i).
(iii) If $\gamma,\gamma'$ are geodesics with $\dot\gamma(0)=\dot\gamma'(0)$, then $\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma'$ are integral curves of $S$ with the same initial value by (i), so they coincide because $M\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$ (case $n=1$, $Z$ a point), and $\gamma=\pi_M\dot\gamma=\pi_M\dot\gamma'=\gamma'$.

<!-- defn-5: body_md -->
\label{def:qchart}
A map $\phi\colon X\to Y$ of diffeological spaces is a \emph{Q-chart} if
\begin{enumerate}[label=(Q\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
\item $\phi$ is a subduction;
\item the diagonal is D-open in $X\times_\phi X=\{(x,x')\mid\phi(x)=\phi(x')\}$;
\item the second (equivalently, the first) projection $X\times_\phi X\to X$ is a local diffeomorphism.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- defn-6: body_md -->
\label{def:transition}
A \emph{transition} of $X$ is a diffeomorphism $h\colon A\to B$ between D-open subsets $A=\operatorname{dom} h$ and $B$ of $X$.
We set
\[
  \Psi(\phi):=\{h \text{ transition of }X\mid \phi\circ h=\phi\text{ on }\operatorname{dom} h\}.
\]

<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:miy-germ}
Let $\phi$ be a Q-chart and $(x,x')\in X\times_\phi X$.
There is $h\in\Psi(\phi)$ with $h(x')=x$, and the germ of such an $h$ at $x'$ is unique.

<!-- lem-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:miy-toolkit}
Let $\phi\colon X\to Y$ be a Q-chart and $F\in\mathcal{F}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item $F\phi$ is a Q-chart; in particular it is a subduction, hence surjective.
\item For every $x\in X$ the restriction $F_x\phi\colon F_xX\to F_{\phi(x)}Y$ is a bijection.
\item If $F\phi(\xi')=F\phi(\xi)$, there is $h\in\Psi(\phi)$ with $Fh(\xi')=\xi$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\xi=(w_1,\dots,w_k)$, $\xi'=(w_1',\dots,w_k')$ with $T_k\phi(\xi)=T_k\phi(\xi')$, and let $x,x'$ be their base points.
By the case $F=T$ there are $h_i\in\Psi(\phi)$ with $Th_i(w_i')=w_i$, whence $h_i(x')=x$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:miy-germ} all $h_i$ have the same germ at $x'$, so they agree on a D-open neighbourhood $U$ of $x'$, and by locality of $T$ the maps $Th_i$ agree on $TU\supseteq T_{x'}X$.
Hence $h:=h_1$ satisfies $T_kh(\xi')=\xi$.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:etale}
Let $\phi\colon X\to Y$ be a Q-chart with $X$ elastic, and let $F\in\mathcal{F}$.
Then $Y$ is elastic by \cite[Thm.~3.24]{Miy}, and
\[
  \Pi^F_\phi:=\bigl(\pi^F_X,\,F\phi\bigr)\colon FX\longrightarrow
  X\times_Y FY=\{(x,\eta)\mid\phi(x)=\pi^F_Y(\eta)\}
\]
is a diffeomorphism.
In other words, the square formed by $F\phi$, $\pi^F_X$, $\pi^F_Y$ and $\phi$ is a pullback in $\mathsf{Diff}$.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The map $\Pi^F_\phi$ is well defined by naturality of the projections and bijective by Lemma~\ref{lem:miy-toolkit}(ii).
It remains to show that its inverse is smooth, and since smoothness is a local property of plots it suffices to lift plots locally.

\emph{The case $F=T$.} Let $(Q,R)\colon U\to X\times_Y TY$ be a plot and $u_0\in U$.
Since $T\phi$ is a subduction, after shrinking $U$ there is a plot $R'\colon U\to TX$ with $T\phi\circ R'=R$.
Then $\phi(\pi_XR'(u))=\pi_YR(u)= \phi(Q(u))$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:miy-germ} there is $h\in\Psi(\phi)$ with $h(\pi_XR'(u_0))=Q(u_0)$.
After shrinking $U$ further, $R'(U)\subseteq T(\operatorname{dom} h)$ and $\tilde R:=Th\circ R'$ is a plot of $TX$ with $T\phi\circ\tilde R=T(\phi h)\circ R'=R$.
The plot $u\mapsto\bigl(Q(u),\,\pi_X\tilde R(u)\bigr)=\bigl(Q(u),h(\pi_XR'(u))\bigr)$ of $X\times_\phi X$ sends $u_0$ into the diagonal, which is D-open by (Q2); hence $\pi_X\tilde R=Q$ near $u_0$.
Thus $(Q,R)=\Pi^T_\phi\circ\tilde R$ near $u_0$.

\emph{The case $F=T^n$.} By induction, using that $T^{n-1}\phi$ is a Q-chart (Lemma~\ref{lem:miy-toolkit}(i)) and the case $F=T$ applied to it,
\[
  T^nX\cong T^{n-1}X\times_{T^{n-1}Y}T^nY
  \cong\bigl(X\times_Y T^{n-1}Y\bigr)\times_{T^{n-1}Y}T^nY
  \cong X\times_Y T^nY ,
\]
and the composite of these diffeomorphisms is $\Pi^{T^n}_\phi$.

\emph{The case $F=T_k$.} The inverse is $(x,(w_1,\dots,w_k))\mapsto\bigl((\Pi^T_\phi)^{-1}(x,w_1),\dots, (\Pi^T_\phi)^{-1}(x,w_k)\bigr)$, which is smooth by the case $F=T$.

<!-- fact-1: body_md -->
\label{fact:subduction-coeq}
Subductions in $\mathsf{Diff}$ are effective epimorphisms: a subduction $q\colon A\to B$ is the coequalizer of its kernel pair $A\times_BA\rightrightarrows A$.
Consequently, if $f\colon A\to Z$ is constant on the fibres of a subduction $q$, there is a unique smooth map $\bar f\colon B\to Z$ with $\bar f\circ q=f$; indeed, $\bar f$ exists as a map of sets, and $\bar f\circ P$ is smooth for every plot $P$ of $B$ because $P$ lifts locally along $q$.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:descent}
Let $\phi\colon X\to Y$ be a Q-chart with $X$ elastic (hence $Y$ elastic), and let $F,F'\in\mathcal{F}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item A map $f\colon FX\to Z$ is of the form $f=f_Y\circ F\phi$ for some map $f_Y\colon FY\to Z$ if and only if $f$ is $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant; in this case $f_Y$ is unique.
\item Let $f\colon FX\to F'X$ be a map over $X$.
  There exists a map $f_Y\colon FY\to F'Y$ over $Y$ with
  \[
    F'\phi\circ f=f_Y\circ F\phi
  \]
if and only if $f$ is $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant; in this case $f_Y$ is unique.
Conversely, every map $f_Y\colon FY\to F'Y$ over $Y$ arises in this way from a unique $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant map $f$ over $X$, namely
  \[
    f=(\Pi^{F'}_\phi)^{-1}\circ\bigl\langle\pi^F_X,\;f_Y\circ F\phi\bigr\rangle .
  \]
We write $f\leftrightarrow f_Y$ for this correspondence.
\item The correspondence of \textup{(ii)} is compatible with composition and identities: if $f\leftrightarrow f_Y$ and $f'\leftrightarrow f'_Y$ for composable $f\colon FX\to F'X$, $f'\colon F'X\to F''X$, then $f'\circ f\leftrightarrow f'_Y\circ f_Y$, and $\operatorname{id}_{FX}\leftrightarrow\operatorname{id}_{FY}$.
  In particular, $f$ is a diffeomorphism if and only if $f_Y$ is.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
(i) If $f=f_Y\circ F\phi$, then $f\circ Fh=f_Y\circ F(\phi h)=f$, so $f$ is invariant.
Conversely, let $f$ be invariant.
If $F\phi(\xi)=F\phi(\xi')$, then by Lemma~\ref{lem:miy-toolkit}(iii) there is $h\in\Psi(\phi)$ with $Fh(\xi')=\xi$, hence $f(\xi)=f(Fh(\xi'))=f(\xi')$.
Since $F\phi$ is a subduction, Fact~\ref{fact:subduction-coeq} gives a unique $f_Y$ with $f_Y\circ F\phi=f$.
Uniqueness in both directions follows from surjectivity of $F\phi$.

(ii) If $f_Y$ over $Y$ with $F'\phi\circ f=f_Y\circ F\phi$ exists, then for $h\in\Psi(\phi)$ the maps $f\circ Fh$ and $F'h\circ f$ lie over $h\circ\pi^F_X$ and have the same image $f_Y\circ F\phi$ under $F'\phi$, so they coincide by Corollary~\ref{cor:detect}; thus $f$ is invariant.
Conversely, let $f$ be invariant and over $X$.
Then $F'\phi\circ f$ is invariant in the sense of (i), because $F'\phi\circ f\circ Fh=F'\phi\circ F'h\circ f= F'(\phi h)\circ f=F'\phi\circ f$, so by (i) there is a unique $f_Y$ with $f_Y\circ F\phi=F'\phi\circ f$; it is a map over $Y$ since $\pi^{F'}_Y f_Y F\phi= \phi\,\pi^{F'}_Xf=\phi\,\pi^F_X=\pi^F_Y F\phi$ and $F\phi$ is surjective.
Finally, given $f_Y$ over $Y$, the map
\[
  f:=(\Pi^{F'}_\phi)^{-1}\circ\bigl\langle\pi^F_X,\;f_Y\circ F\phi\bigr\rangle
  \colon FX\to F'X
\]
is well defined and smooth by Proposition~\ref{prop:etale}, is a map over $X$, and satisfies $F'\phi\circ f=f_Y\circ F\phi$.
It is invariant: for $h\in\Psi(\phi)$ both $f\circ Fh$ and $F'h\circ f$ lie over $h\circ\pi^F_X$ and have the same image $f_Y\circ F\phi$ under $F'\phi$, so they coincide by Corollary~\ref{cor:detect}.
The two constructions are mutually inverse by surjectivity of $F\phi$ and by Corollary~\ref{cor:detect}.

(iii) The composite $f'\circ f$ is again an invariant map over $X$, and $F''\phi\circ f'\circ f=f'_Y\circ F'\phi\circ f=f'_Y\circ f_Y\circ F\phi$; clearly $\operatorname{id}_{FX}\leftrightarrow\operatorname{id}_{FY}$.
If $f$ is a diffeomorphism, then $f^{-1}$ is a map over $X$ (hence maps $F'(\operatorname{dom} h)$ onto $F(\operatorname{dom} h)$) and is invariant, since $f\circ Fh=F'h\circ f$ gives $f^{-1}\circ F'h=Fh\circ f^{-1}$; if $f^{-1}\leftrightarrow g_Y$, then $g_Y\circ f_Y$ and $f_Y\circ g_Y$ correspond to identities, so $g_Y=f_Y^{-1}$ by injectivity of the correspondence.
The converse is the same argument applied to the lift of $f_Y^{-1}$.

\label{thm:transport}
For a $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant structure on $X$ we write the subscript $Y$ for the corresponding structure on $Y$ given by \textup{(1)--(3)} below.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \textup{(Vertical connections)} For every $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant vertical connection $K$ on $X$ there is a unique vertical connection $K_Y$ on $Y$ such that
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:K-rel}
    K_Y\circ T^2\phi=T\phi\circ K ,
  \end{equation}
and every vertical connection on $Y$ arises in this way from a unique $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant $K$.
Moreover
  \[
    \Theta^{K_Y}_Y\circ T^2\phi=T_3\phi\circ\Theta^K_X,\qquad
    C_{K_Y}\circ T^3\phi=T\phi\circ C_K ,
  \]
and $K$ is torsion-free \textup{(}resp.\ effective, flat\textup{)} if and only if $K_Y$ is.
\item \textup{(Horizontal and full connections)} For every $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant horizontal connection $H$ on $X$ there is a unique horizontal connection $H_Y$ on $Y$ such that
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:H-rel}
    H_Y\circ T_2\phi=T^2\phi\circ H ,
  \end{equation}
and every horizontal connection on $Y$ arises in this way from a unique $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant $H$.
For corresponding $K\leftrightarrow K_Y$ and $H\leftrightarrow H_Y$, the pair $(K,H)$ is a full connection if and only if $(K_Y,H_Y)$ is.
If $K$ is $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant and effective, its partner $H$ is $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant and $H_Y$ is the partner of $K_Y$.
\item \textup{(Metrics)} For every $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant Riemannian metric $g$ on $X$ there is a unique Riemannian metric $g_Y$ on $Y$ such that $g=g_Y\circ T_2\phi$, and every Riemannian metric on $Y$ arises in this way from a unique $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant $g$.
  Every $T_x\phi\colon(T_xX,g)\to (T_{\phi(x)}Y,g_Y)$ is a linear isometry.
\item \textup{(Levi-Civita connections)} For corresponding $K\leftrightarrow K_Y$ and $g\leftrightarrow g_Y$, $K$ is a Levi-Civita connection of $g$ if and only if $K_Y$ is a Levi-Civita connection of $g_Y$.
\item \textup{(Geodesics)} For corresponding $K\leftrightarrow K_Y$, a path $\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X$ is a geodesic of $K$ if and only if $\phi\circ\gamma$ is a geodesic of $K_Y$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- lem-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:transfer}
Let $D$ be as above and $F'\in\mathcal{F}$.
Suppose that, for $M=X,Y$, maps $a_M,b_M\colon DM\to F'M$ satisfy
\[
  F'\phi\circ a_X=a_Y\circ D\phi,\qquad F'\phi\circ b_X=b_Y\circ D\phi,
  \qquad \pi^{F'}_X\circ a_X=\pi^{F'}_X\circ b_X .
\]
Then $a_X=b_X$ if and only if $a_Y=b_Y$.
The analogous statement holds for maps $a_M,b_M\colon DM\to\mathbb{R}$ with $a_X=a_Y\circ D\phi$ and $b_X=b_Y\circ D\phi$.
<!-- end lem-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
If $a_Y=b_Y$, then $F'\phi\circ a_X=F'\phi\circ b_X$, and $a_X=b_X$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:detect}.
If $a_X=b_X$, then $a_Y\circ D\phi=b_Y\circ D\phi$, and $a_Y=b_Y$ since $D\phi$ is surjective.
In the real-valued case, $a_Y=b_Y$ clearly implies $a_X=b_X$, and the converse follows from the surjectivity of $D\phi$.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Axiom (b) says precisely that $K\colon T^2M\to TM$ is a map over $M$.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:descent}(ii) with $F=T^2$, $F'=T$, the relation \eqref{eq:K-rel} is a bijection between $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant maps $K\colon T^2X\to TX$ over $X$ and maps $K_Y\colon T^2Y\to TY$ over $Y$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}, it remains only to verify that the two sides of axioms (a), (c)--(f) satisfy the required intertwining relations.

\label{rem:transport-scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:transport} identifies the Riemannian structures on $Y$ considered in \cite{Shio} with the corresponding $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant structures on $X$.
It is not a global lifting statement for paths or for solutions of differential equations; in particular, it does not imply that every geodesic of $Y$ lifts to a geodesic of $X$.

<!-- defn-8: body_md -->
\label{def:principal-action}
The action is \emph{principal} if it is free and the \emph{division map}
\[
  \operatorname{div}\colon X\times_\phi X\to\Lambda,\qquad \operatorname{div}(\eta x,x)=\eta,
\]
is smooth.
<!-- end defn-8 -->
<!-- lem-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:principal-qchart}
Let the action be principal.
For $\eta\in\Lambda$ put $\Gamma_\eta:=\{(\eta x,x)\mid x\in X\}=\operatorname{div}^{-1}(\eta)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item Each $\Gamma_\eta$ is D-open, $X\times_\phi X=\coprod_{\eta\in\Lambda}\Gamma_\eta$, and $\phi$ is a Q-chart.
\item For every $h\in\Psi(\phi)$ and $x\in\operatorname{dom} h$ there are $\eta\in\Lambda$ and a D-open $U\ni x$ with $h|_U=\eta|_U$.
\item Let $X$ be elastic and $F,F'\in\mathcal{F}$.
  A map $f\colon FX\to F'X$ over $X$ is $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant if and only if $f\circ F\eta=F'\eta\circ f$ for all $\eta\in\Lambda$; a map $f\colon FX\to Z$ is $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant if and only if $f\circ F\eta=f$ for all $\eta\in\Lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-6 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
(i) If $P$ is a plot of $X\times_\phi X$, then $\operatorname{div}\circ P$ is a plot of $\Lambda$, hence locally constant, so $P^{-1}(\Gamma_\eta)$ is open; thus $\Gamma_\eta$ is D-open.
The decomposition is disjoint by freeness.
The diagonal is $\Gamma_e$, which gives (Q2).
The projection $\operatorname{pr}_2$ restricts to a bijection $\Gamma_\eta\to X$ with smooth inverse $x\mapsto(\eta x,x)$, which gives (Q3), and (Q1) holds because quotient maps are subductions.
(ii) The map $u\mapsto\operatorname{div}(h(u),u)$ is smooth on $\operatorname{dom} h$, hence locally constant as in (i), and $h(u)=\operatorname{div}(h(u),u)\,u$.
(iii) Each $\eta\in\Lambda$ is a diffeomorphism of $X$ with $\phi\eta=\phi$, i.e.\ an element of $\Psi(\phi)$ with domain $X$; this gives one direction.
Conversely, by (ii) $\operatorname{dom} h$ is covered by D-open sets $U$ on which $h=\eta_U$ for some $\eta_U\in\Lambda$, and by locality $Fh=F\eta_U$ on $FU$.

<!-- ex-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:principal}
(a) Let $G$ be a diffeological group and $N\subseteq G$ a subgroup whose subset diffeology is discrete, acting by $\eta\cdot a:=a\eta^{-1}$.
The action is free, and $\operatorname{div}(b,a)=b^{-1}a$ is smooth, because it is the restriction of a smooth map $G\times G\to G$ with values in $N$.
Hence the action is principal and $G\to G/N$ is a Q-chart; this is the principal fibration of \cite[art.~8.15]{PIZ13} and recovers \cite[Prop.~3.17]{Miy}.
Normality of $N$ is not needed.

(b) Let a countable group $\Lambda$ act freely on $X$.
By \cite[Prop.~3.16]{Miy}, $\phi$ is a Q-chart.
Since $\Gamma_\eta$ is the image of the diagonal under the diffeomorphism $(x,x')\mapsto(\eta x,x')$ of $X\times_\phi X$, it is D-open by (Q2).
Hence $\operatorname{div}$ is locally constant, and therefore smooth as a map to the diffeologically discrete group $\Lambda$; the action is principal.

<!-- cor-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:discrete-quotient}
Let $G$ be an elastic group \textup{(}a diffeological group whose underlying space is elastic\textup{)} and $N\trianglelefteq G$ a normal subgroup whose subset diffeology is discrete, and let $\phi\colon G\to G/N$.
The germs of elements of $\Psi(\phi)$ are precisely the germs of the left translations $L_\eta$, $\eta\in N$.
Consequently every left-invariant vertical connection, horizontal connection and Riemannian metric on $G$ is $\Psi(\phi)$-invariant and hence descends uniquely to $G/N$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}.

<!-- lem-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:global-lift}
Let the action be principal.
For every path $\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to Y$ and every $x\in\phi^{-1}(\gamma(0))$ there is a unique path $\tilde\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X$ with $\phi\circ\tilde\gamma=\gamma$ and $\tilde\gamma(0)=x$.
Any two lifts of $\gamma$ differ by the action of a unique $\eta\in\Lambda$.
<!-- end lem-7 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Let $\tilde\gamma_1,\tilde\gamma_2\colon J\to X$ be lifts of $\gamma|_J$ over an open interval $J$.
Then $t\mapsto\operatorname{div}(\tilde\gamma_2(t),\tilde\gamma_1(t))$ is a plot of $\Lambda$ on the connected set $J$, hence constant, say $\eta$, and $\tilde\gamma_2=\eta\tilde\gamma_1$ on $J$; if the lifts agree at one point, then $\eta=e$ by freeness.
This proves uniqueness and the last assertion.

For existence, let $I$ be the union of all open intervals $J\ni0$ over which $\gamma$ has a lift with value $x$ at $0$.
By uniqueness these local lifts agree on overlaps, so they glue to a lift $\tilde\gamma\colon I\to X$; moreover $I\neq\emptyset$: since $\phi$ is a subduction, $\gamma$ has a lift $\sigma$ on some open interval around $0$, and $\eta\sigma$ with $\eta:=\operatorname{div}(x,\sigma(0))$ has value $x$ at $0$.
Suppose $b:=\sup I<\infty$.
There is a lift $\sigma$ of $\gamma$ over an open interval $J\ni b$, and $J\cap I$ is a nonempty open interval.
By the first part, $\tilde\gamma=\eta\sigma$ on $J\cap I$ for some $\eta$, so $\tilde\gamma$ and $\eta\sigma$ glue to a lift over $I\cup J\supsetneq I$, a contradiction.
The same argument rules out a finite lower endpoint, so $I=\mathbb{R}$.

<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:elast-curve-quotient}
If $X\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$, then $Y\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Fix $n\ge0$, a vector field $V$ on $T^nY$, a map $s\colon Z\to T^nY$, and two solutions $\Phi,\Phi'\colon\mathbb{R}\times Z\to T^nY$ of $(T^nY,V,s)$.
For $z\in Z$ the slices $\Phi_z:=\Phi(-,z)$ and $\Phi'_z$ are integral curves of $V$ with initial value $s(z)$: indeed $T(\Phi_z)\circ\partial=T(\Phi)\circ(\partial\times0_Z)\circ \iota_z=V\circ\Phi_z$, where $\iota_z(t)=(t,z)$ and $T(\iota_z)\circ\partial=(\partial\times0_Z)\circ\iota_z$ by naturality of the zero section (cf.\ \cite[Rem.~6.20]{Shio}).
Choose $\xi\in(T^n\phi)^{-1}(s(z))$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:lift-vf}, the lifts of $\Phi_z$ and $\Phi'_z$ through $\xi$ are integral curves of $\tilde V$ with the same initial value, so they coincide because $X\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$.
Applying $T^n\phi$ gives $\Phi_z=\Phi'_z$.
As $z$ was arbitrary, $\Phi=\Phi'$.

<!-- prop-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:flow-descends}
Let $X\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$, let $(K,H)$ be a $\Lambda$-invariant full connection whose geodesic spray $S$ is complete, and let $\Phi_X$ be its geodesic flow \textup{(}Convention~\ref{conv:flow}\textup{)}.
Then there exists a unique smooth map $\Phi_Y\colon\mathbb{R}\times TY\to TY$ such that
\[
  \Phi_Y\circ(\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times T\phi)=T\phi\circ\Phi_X .
\]
Moreover, $\Phi_Y$ is a geodesic flow of $(K_Y,H_Y)$; in particular, the geodesic spray $S_Y$ of $(K_Y,H_Y)$ is complete.
<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Since $\langle\operatorname{id},\operatorname{id}\rangle\circ T\phi=T_2\phi\circ\langle\operatorname{id},\operatorname{id}\rangle$, \eqref{eq:H-rel} gives $S_Y\circ T\phi=T^2\phi\circ S$, and $S$ is $\Lambda$-invariant: $S\circ T\eta=T^2\eta\circ S$.
For $\eta\in\Lambda$ both $\Phi_X\circ(\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times T\eta)$ and $T\eta\circ\Phi_X$ solve the parametrized system $(TX,S,T\eta)$ with parameter object $TX$; for the first this uses $T(T\eta)\circ0_{TX}=0_{TX}\circ T\eta$, for the second the invariance of $S$.
As $X\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$ they coincide.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:lift-vf}(i) the fibres of $\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times T\phi$ are the sets $\{t\}\times(\Lambda\text{-orbit})$, so $T\phi\circ\Phi_X$ is constant on them, and since $\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times T\phi$ is a subduction, Fact~\ref{fact:subduction-coeq} yields a unique $\Phi_Y$ as stated.
It solves $(TY,S_Y,\operatorname{id}_{TY})$: first $\Phi_Y(0,T\phi(v))=T\phi(\Phi_X(0,v))=T\phi(v)$; second, using $T(\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times T\phi)\circ(\partial\times0_{TX})=(\partial\times0_{TY})\circ (\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times T\phi)$,
\[
  T(\Phi_Y)\,(\partial\times0_{TY})\,(\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times T\phi)
  =T(T\phi\,\Phi_X)\,(\partial\times0_{TX})
  =T^2\phi\,S\,\Phi_X=S_Y\,T\phi\,\Phi_X
  =S_Y\,\Phi_Y\,(\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times T\phi),
\]
and $\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{R}\times T\phi$ is surjective.

<!-- cor-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:uniqueness}
Let $X\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$, and let $(K,H)$ be a $\Lambda$-invariant full connection with complete geodesic spray.
Then $Y\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$, the geodesic spray of $(K_Y,H_Y)$ is complete, and every $w\in TY$ is the initial velocity of exactly one geodesic of $K_Y$, namely $\phi\circ\gamma$, where $\gamma$ is the geodesic of $K$ with initial velocity $(T_x\phi)^{-1}(w)$ for any $x\in\phi^{-1}(\pi_Y(w))$.
<!-- end cor-3 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
The first two assertions are Theorem~\ref{thm:elast-curve-quotient} and Proposition~\ref{prop:flow-descends}.
Let $v:=(T_x\phi)^{-1}(w)$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:geod-spray}(ii), $\gamma:=\pi_X\circ\Phi_X(-,v)$ is a geodesic of $K$ with $\dot\gamma(0)=v$, so $\phi\circ\gamma$ is a geodesic of $K_Y$ with initial velocity $T\phi(v)=w$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}(5).
Uniqueness is Lemma~\ref{lem:geod-spray}(iii) applied to $Y\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$; in particular the geodesic $\phi\circ\gamma$ does not depend on the choice of $x$.

<!-- rem-6: body_md -->
\label{rem:variational}
Under the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:uniqueness}, if moreover $K$ is a Levi-Civita connection of a $\Lambda$-invariant metric $g$, then $Y\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$ carries the full connection $(K_Y,H_Y)$ whose vertical part is the Levi-Civita connection of $g_Y$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}(4)) and whose geodesic spray is complete.
Thus all standing hypotheses of the variational theory of \cite[\S\S6.4--6.5]{Shio} are satisfied on $Y$; in particular, on $Y$ the critical paths of the energy functional are precisely the geodesics \cite[Props.~6.30, 6.33]{Shio}, and length-minimizing regular paths are geodesics after reparametrization \cite[Prop.~6.37]{Shio}.

<!-- lem-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:irr-basic}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item $\Lambda_\alpha$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$, and its subset diffeology is discrete.
\item The action of $\Lambda_\alpha$ on $\mathbb{R}$ is principal, $q$ is a Q-chart, and a structure on $\mathbb{R}$ is $\Psi(q)$-invariant if and only if it is invariant under all translations $L_\eta$, $\eta\in\Lambda_\alpha$.
\item $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ is an elastic group and $\mathbb{T}_\alpha\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$.
\item The D-topology of $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ is indiscrete.
\item For $n\ge0$ and $k\ge1$ there are diffeomorphisms $T^n\mathbb{T}_\alpha\cong\mathbb{T}_\alpha\times\mathbb{R}^{2^n-1}$ and $T_k\mathbb{T}_\alpha\cong\mathbb{T}_\alpha\times\mathbb{R}^k$ under which $T^nq$ and $T_kq$ become $q\times\operatorname{id}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-9 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
(i) $\Lambda_\alpha$ is not cyclic because $\alpha$ is irrational, and every non-cyclic subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$ is dense.
A plot $P\colon U\to\Lambda_\alpha$ is, by definition of the subset diffeology, smooth as a map $U\to\mathbb{R}$.
On each connected component of $U$ its image is connected and countable, hence a singleton.
Since the components of $U$ are open, $P$ is locally constant.
(ii) The division map $(x',x)\mapsto x'-x$ is smooth, so this is Example~\ref{ex:principal}(a) together with Lemma~\ref{lem:principal-qchart}(iii).
(iii) $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ is elastic by \cite[Thm.~3.24]{Miy} (see also \cite[Cor.~3.25]{Miy}); $\mathbb{R}\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$ \cite[Rem.~6.20]{Shio}, so $\mathbb{T}_\alpha\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:elast-curve-quotient}.
(iv) Let $V\subseteq\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ be D-open and nonempty.
Then $q^{-1}(V)$ is a nonempty open $\Lambda_\alpha$-invariant subset of $\mathbb{R}$; it contains an open interval $I$.
For $x\in\mathbb{R}$ the open set $x-I$ contains some $\eta\in\Lambda_\alpha$ by density, so $x\in I+\eta\subseteq q^{-1}(V)$.
Hence $V=\mathbb{T}_\alpha$.
(v) We have $T^n\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2^n-1}$ with $T^nL_\eta=L_\eta\times\operatorname{id}$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:lift-vf}(i), $T^nq$ is a subduction whose fibres are the orbits of $\Lambda_\alpha$ acting on the first factor.
The map $q\times\operatorname{id}$ is also a subduction (a product of subductions) with the same fibres, so the induced bijection $T^n\mathbb{T}_\alpha\to\mathbb{T}_\alpha\times\mathbb{R}^{2^n-1}$ is a diffeomorphism by Fact~\ref{fact:subduction-coeq}.
The case of $T_k$ is identical.

<!-- lem-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:euclid-vconn}
Let $U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ be open.
Every vertical connection on $U$ is of the form
\[
  K_\Gamma(u,v_0,v_1,v_{01})=\bigl(u,\;v_{01}+\Gamma(u)(v_0,v_1)\bigr)
\]
for a unique smooth family $\Gamma$ of bilinear maps $\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^n$, and every such $K_\Gamma$ is a vertical connection \cite[Ex.~3.5]{Shio}.
The connection $K_\Gamma$ is torsion-free if and only if every $\Gamma(u)$ is symmetric.
It is always effective; its partner is $H_\Gamma\bigl((u,v_0),(u,v_1)\bigr)=\bigl(u,v_1,v_0,-\Gamma(u)(v_1,v_0)\bigr)$ and its geodesic spray is $S_\Gamma(u,v)=\bigl(u,v,v,-\Gamma(u)(v,v)\bigr)$.
<!-- end lem-11 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
By axiom (b), $K(u,v_0,v_1,v_{01})=(u,k(u,v_0,v_1,v_{01}))$ with $k$ smooth.
By axioms (e), (f) and Remark~\ref{rem:coordinates}, for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$,
\[
  k(u,v_0,tv_1,tv_{01})=t\,k(u,v_0,v_1,v_{01}),\qquad
  k(u,tv_0,v_1,tv_{01})=t\,k(u,v_0,v_1,v_{01}).
\]
A smooth function $f$ with $f(tw)=tf(w)$ for all $t$ is linear, since $f(w)=\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}f(tw)=Df(0)w$.
Hence $k$ is linear in $(v_1,v_{01})$ for fixed $(u,v_0)$, and linear in $(v_0,v_{01})$ for fixed $(u,v_1)$.
Thus
\[
  k(u,v_0,v_1,v_{01})=k(u,v_0,v_1,0)+k(u,v_0,0,v_{01}),
\]
where the first term is bilinear in $(v_0,v_1)$, say $\Gamma(u)(v_0,v_1)$, and the second is linear in $(v_0,v_{01})$ and vanishes for $v_{01}=0$, hence equals $B(u)v_{01}$.
Axiom (a) gives $K(u,0,0,v)=(u,B(u)v)=(u,v)$, so $B(u)=\operatorname{id}$.
The statement on torsion follows from $\tau(u,v_0,v_1,v_{01})=(u,v_1,v_0,v_{01})$.
The map
\[
  \Theta(u,v_0,v_1,v_{01})=\bigl((u,v_0),(u,v_1),(u,v_{01}+\Gamma(u)(v_0,v_1))\bigr)
\]
has the smooth inverse obtained by subtracting $\Gamma(u)(v_0,v_1)$.
By axioms (a), (b) of Definition~\ref{def:hconn}, the partner has the form $H((u,v_0),(u,v_1))=(u,v_1,v_0,w)$, and (F1) forces $w=-\Gamma(u)(v_1,v_0)$.
Finally $S_\Gamma(v)=H_\Gamma(v,v)$.

<!-- lem-12: body_md -->
\label{lem:line}
Let $\rho>0$ and $c$ be constants.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item $K_c$ is flat.
\item $K_c$ is a Levi-Civita connection of $\rho\,dt^2$ if and only if $c=0$.
\item The geodesic spray $S_c$ is complete if and only if $c=0$.
  The geodesic flow of $(K_0,H_0)$ is $\Phi(t,(x,v))=(x+tv,v)$, and the geodesics of $K_0$ are the affine maps $t\mapsto x+tv$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-12 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
(i) Write points of $T^3\mathbb{R}$ as $(x,v_0,v_1,v_{01};v_2,v_{02},v_{12},v_{012})$, the last four coordinates being the derivative in the new direction $2$ as in \cite[Ex.~5.2]{Shio}.
Then
\[
  T(K_c)=\bigl(x,\;v_{01}+cv_0v_1,\;v_2,\;v_{012}+c(v_{02}v_1+v_0v_{12})\bigr),
\]
\[
  K_c\,T(K_c)=\bigl(x,\;v_{012}+c\,v_{02}v_1+c\,v_0v_{12}+c\,v_{01}v_2+c^2v_0v_1v_2\bigr).
\]
The flip $\tau_{T\mathbb{R}}$ exchanges the directions $1$ and $2$, i.e.\ $v_1\leftrightarrow v_2$ and $v_{01}\leftrightarrow v_{02}$, fixing $v_0$, $v_{12}$, $v_{012}$ \cite[Ex.~5.2]{Shio}.
The expression above is invariant under this exchange, so $C_{K_c}=0$.

(ii) Let $g=\rho\,dt^2$.
We have $T(\partial)(t,w)=(t,1,w,0)$, so $\nabla^{K_c}_\partial\partial=K_c(t,1,1,0)=c\,\partial$.
If $K_c$ is a Levi-Civita connection, \cite[Prop.~4.12]{Shio} gives $0=\partial\bigl(g(\partial,\partial)\bigr)=2g(\nabla^{K_c}_\partial\partial,\partial) =2c\rho$, so $c=0$.
Conversely, $K_0$ is torsion-free.
Write points of $T(T_2\mathbb{R})$ as $(x,a,b;\dot x,\dot a,\dot b)$; under \eqref{eq:chi} they correspond to the pairs $A=(x,a,\dot x,\dot a)$, $B=(x,b,\dot x,\dot b)$ in $T^2\mathbb{R}$.
Since $g(x,a,b)=\rho ab$, we get $d(g)=\rho(\dot ab+a\dot b)$, while $g(K_0A,\pi_{T\mathbb{R}}B)+g(\pi_{T\mathbb{R}}A,K_0B)=\rho\dot a\,b+\rho a\,\dot b$.
Hence \eqref{eq:LC} holds.

(iii) By Lemma~\ref{lem:euclid-vconn}, $S_c(x,v)=(x,v,v,-cv^2)$, so the integral curves of $S_c$ solve $\dot x=v$, $\dot v=-cv^2$.
For $c\neq0$ and $v(0)\neq0$ the solution $v(t)=v(0)/(1+cv(0)t)$ does not extend to all of $\mathbb{R}$, so no geodesic flow exists.
For $c=0$ the integral curves are $t\mapsto(x+tv,v)$.
The last assertion follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:geod-spray}.

<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:irr}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item $C^\infty(\mathbb{T}_\alpha,\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}$.
  The vector fields on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ are precisely $a\,\bar\partial$, $a\in\mathbb{R}$, and $\Omega^1(\mathbb{T}_\alpha)=\mathbb{R}\,\vartheta$; the form $\vartheta$ is closed but not exact.
\item The Riemannian metrics on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ are exactly $g_\rho=\rho\,\vartheta^2$, $g_\rho([x],v_0,v_1)=\rho\,v_0v_1$, with $\rho>0$ a constant; every $T_xq\colon(T_x\mathbb{R},\rho\,dt^2)\to(T_{[x]}\mathbb{T}_\alpha,g_\rho)$ is a linear isometry.
  In particular the set of Riemannian metrics on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ is parametrized by $\rho\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$.
\item The vertical connections on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ are exactly
  \[
    K_c([x],v_0,v_1,v_{01})=([x],\,v_{01}+c\,v_0v_1),\qquad c\in\mathbb{R} .
  \]
All of them are torsion-free, effective and flat; the partner of $K_c$ is
  \[
        H_c\bigl(([x],v_0),([x],v_1)\bigr)=([x],v_1,v_0,-c\,v_0v_1).
  \]
\item For every $\rho>0$, $K_0$ is the unique Levi-Civita connection of $g_\rho$.
  The geodesic spray of $(K_0,H_0)$ is complete, with geodesic flow $\Phi(t,([x],v))=([x+tv],v)$, and the geodesics of $K_0$ are exactly the paths $t\mapsto[x+tv]$.
  For $c\neq0$ the geodesic spray of $(K_c,H_c)$ is not complete.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}, Proposition~\ref{prop:descent} and Lemma~\ref{lem:irr-basic}(ii), each kind of structure on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ corresponds bijectively to the $\Lambda_\alpha$-invariant structures of the same kind on $\mathbb{R}$, compatibly with all properties listed there.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:dense-const}, every $\Lambda_\alpha$-invariant continuous coefficient on $\mathbb{R}$ is constant; this is used in each step.

(i) A smooth function on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ is $f_Y$ for a $\Lambda_\alpha$-invariant $f\in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$, hence constant.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:descent}(ii) with $F=T^0$, $F'=T$ (cf.\ Lemma~\ref{lem:lift-vf}(ii) with $n=0$), vector fields on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ correspond to $\Lambda_\alpha$-invariant vector fields on $\mathbb{R}$.
Every vector field on $\mathbb{R}$ is $a(t)\,\partial$, and invariance gives $a(t+\eta)=a(t)$ for $\eta\in\Lambda_\alpha$; hence $a$ is constant and the vector field on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ is $a\,\bar\partial$.
By \cite[Lem.~2.15]{Shio}, $\Omega^1(M)$ is the set of smooth maps $TM\to\mathbb{R}$ that are linear on the fibres.
Such maps on $T\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ correspond to $\Lambda_\alpha$-invariant maps on $T\mathbb{R}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:descent}(i)), and fibrewise linearity is preserved because each $T_xq$ is a linear bijection.
They lift to the $\Lambda_\alpha$-invariant forms $b\,dt$ on $\mathbb{R}$, so $b$ is constant and they are the forms $b\,\vartheta$.
The form $\vartheta$ is closed, since $q^*d\vartheta=d(dt)=0$ and $q^*$ is injective on forms ($q$ being a subduction); it is not exact because the only smooth functions are constants.

(ii) By Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}(3), metrics on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ correspond to $\Lambda_\alpha$-invariant metrics $\rho(t)\,dt^2$ on $\mathbb{R}$, and $\rho$ is constant.

(iii) By Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}(1) and Lemma~\ref{lem:euclid-vconn}, vertical connections on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ correspond to the connections $K_c$ on $\mathbb{R}$ with $c\in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ and $c(t+\eta)=c(t)$ for $\eta\in\Lambda_\alpha$, i.e.\ to constant $c$; the formula follows from $K_c\circ T^2q=Tq\circ K_c^{\mathbb{R}}$ and $T^nq=q\times\operatorname{id}$.
Torsion-freeness, effectivity and flatness transfer from $\mathbb{R}$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}(1) and Lemmas~\ref{lem:euclid-vconn}, \ref{lem:line}(i), and the partner is the descent of $H_c$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}(2).

(iv) By Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}(4), the Levi-Civita connections of $g_\rho$ correspond to the $\Lambda_\alpha$-invariant Levi-Civita connections of $\rho\,dt^2$ on $\mathbb{R}$, which by (iii) and Lemma~\ref{lem:line}(ii) reduce to $K_0$.
Since $\mathbb{R}\in\mathsf{Elast}_{\mathrm{curve}}$ and the spray of $(K_0,H_0)$ on $\mathbb{R}$ is complete, Proposition~\ref{prop:flow-descends} and Corollary~\ref{cor:uniqueness} give the flow and the geodesics.
Suppose that the spray $S$ of $(K_c,H_c)$ on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ is complete for some $c\neq0$, and let $v\neq0$.
Then $t\mapsto\Phi(t,([0],v))$ is an integral curve of $S$ defined on $\mathbb{R}$.
The spray $S^{\mathbb{R}}_c$ on $\mathbb{R}$ satisfies $T^2q\circ S_c^{\mathbb{R}}=S\circ Tq$ (proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:flow-descends}), so it is the lift $\tilde S$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:lift-vf}(ii) for $n=1$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:lift-vf}(iii) we obtain an integral curve of $S_c^{\mathbb{R}}$ through $(0,v)$ defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$, contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:line}(iii).

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:d-zero}
For every $\rho>0$, the pseudo-distance $d_{g_\rho}$ on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ vanishes identically, and for $[x]\neq[y]$ no path from $[x]$ to $[y]$ realizes the infimum.
The same holds if the infimum is taken over regular paths only.
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
Let $\gamma\in\operatorname{Path}(\mathbb{T}_\alpha;[x],[y])$ and let $\tilde\gamma$ be its lift with $\tilde\gamma(0)=x$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:global-lift}); then $\tilde\gamma(1)=y+\eta$ for some $\eta\in\Lambda_\alpha$.
Since each $T_xq$ is an isometry, $L(\gamma)=\sqrt\rho\int_0^1|\tilde\gamma'(t)|\,dt\ge\sqrt\rho\, |y+\eta-x|$, with equality for the affine path from $x$ to $y+\eta$.
Every $\eta\in\Lambda_\alpha$ arises in this way, so
\[
  d_{g_\rho}([x],[y])=\inf_{\eta\in\Lambda_\alpha}\sqrt\rho\,|y+\eta-x|=0
\]
by density.
The affine paths with $y+\eta\neq x$ are regular, and the infimum of $|y+\eta-x|$ over $\eta\in\Lambda_\alpha\setminus\{x-y\}$ is still $0$ because $\Lambda_\alpha\setminus\{x-y\}$ is dense; this gives the last assertion.
(For $[x]=[y]$ and $y=x$ this means that there are regular loops at $[x]$ of arbitrarily small length.)
If $L(\gamma)=0$, then $\tilde\gamma'=0$ on $[0,1]$, so $[y]=q(\tilde\gamma(1))=q(\tilde\gamma(0))=[x]$.

<!-- rem-8: body_md -->
\label{rem:KSS-distance}
The pseudo-distance $d_g$ is the one of \cite[(3.6)]{KSS}; on elastic spaces the weak Riemannian metrics of \cite{KSS} are the maps $T_2M\to\mathbb{R}$ that are bilinear, symmetric and positive on the fibres \cite[Rem.~4.4]{Shio}.
By Remark~\ref{rem:KSS-definite}, $g_\rho$ is definite in the sense of \cite[Def.~3.7]{KSS} with respect to the generating family $\{q\}$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:d-zero} is consistent with the results of \cite{KSS}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \cite[Thm.~3.27]{KSS} asserts that $d_g$ is a distance if $g$ is definite with respect to a generating family which \emph{separates points} in the sense of \cite[Def.~3.25]{KSS}.
  By Proposition~\ref{prop:d-zero}, no generating family of the diffeology of $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ with respect to which $g_\rho$ is definite separates points.
\item \cite[Thm.~3.21]{KSS} asserts that on a connected diffeological space the D-topology is finer than the topology $\mathcal O_d$ defined by $d_g$.
  The space $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ is connected, being the image of $\mathbb{R}$.
  Since the D-topology of $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ is indiscrete (Lemma~\ref{lem:irr-basic}(iv)), every open $d_{g_\rho}$-ball is all of $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$; this gives a second proof that $d_{g_\rho}\equiv0$.
\end{enumerate}
In \cite[Ex.~4.3]{KSS} a definite metric on the line with a doubled half-line has pseudo-distance zero between a single pair of distinct points; on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ it vanishes between all pairs.

<!-- rem-9: body_md -->
\label{rem:pi1}
The irrational torus is a standard example showing that a diffeology carries substantially more information than its D-topology.
The D-topology of $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$ is indiscrete (Lemma~\ref{lem:irr-basic}(iv)), as is the quotient topology of $\mathbb{R}/\Lambda_\alpha$; nevertheless its diffeological homotopy theory is nontrivial, since $\pi_1(\mathbb{T}_\alpha)\cong\Lambda_\alpha\cong\mathbb{Z}^2$ \cite[art.~8.38]{PIZ13}.
As shown in this section, it also carries a nontrivial Riemannian geometry: the metrics $\rho\,dt^2$ on $\mathbb{R}$ descend to the metrics $g_\rho=\rho\,\vartheta^2$ on $\mathbb{T}_\alpha$, each of which has a unique Levi-Civita connection, namely $K_0$, with complete geodesic flow, although the induced pseudo-distance vanishes identically.
Thus the diffeology retains both homotopical and differential-geometric information that is invisible to the D-topology.
