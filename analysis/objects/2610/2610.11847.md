<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:fields_increment}
Let $R\subseteq \mathbb{F}_3^n$ and $A\subseteq \mathbb{F}_3^n$ with $|A|\geq 3^n\beta$. Suppose that there are no solutions to the equation $x+y=2r$ where $x,y\in A$ and $r\in R$. Then there exists a subspace $V$ of codimension $1$ such that for some translate $t$ we have $$|(t+V)\cap R|/|V|\geq \Bigl(1+\frac{\beta}{2}\Bigr)|R|/3^n.$$
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let us define Fourier Transform of $f:\mathbb{F}_3^n\rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ as 
$$\hat{f}(\gamma):=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_x f(x)\overline{\gamma(x)},$$
where $\gamma$ is an element of the dual group of $\mathbb{F}_3^n$.\\
From our assumption we have
$$\sum_x 1_{2\cdot R}(x)1_A*1_A(x) = 0,$$
which by Plancherel's Theorem can be written as
$$\sum_{\gamma}\widehat{1_{2\cdot R}}(\gamma)\overline{\widehat{1_A}(\gamma)^2}=0.$$
We separate the trivial term $\gamma=1$ to get
$$\frac{1}{3^{3n}}|2\cdot R||A|^2 = 0 -\sum_{\gamma\neq 1}\widehat{1_{2\cdot R}}(\gamma)\overline{\widehat{1_A}(\gamma)^2}.$$
Write $M:=\mathop{\mathrm{max}}_{\gamma\neq 1}|\widehat{1_{2\cdot R}}(\gamma)|$, then we can estimate
$$\frac{1}{3^{3n}}|2\cdot R||A|^2\leq M\sum_{\gamma\neq 1}|\widehat{1_A}(\gamma)|^2 \leq \frac{M}{3^{n}}|A|.$$
Rearranging for $M$ and estimating $|A|\geq 3^n\beta$ we get
$$M\geq \frac{|2\cdot R|\beta}{3^n}.$$
Let $\gamma_{0} \neq 1$ be the character achieving the maximum $M$ and define
$$V = \{x\in \mathbb{F}_3^n : \gamma_0(x)=1\}.$$ Since $\gamma_{0}$ is non-trivial, $V$ is a subspace of codimension 1. Let $\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ be densities of $2\cdot R$ within the three cosets of $V$. On these cosets $\gamma_0$ takes values $1,\omega,\omega^2$ respectively, where $\omega = \exp(2\pi i /3)$. We can then write
$$\frac{1}{3} \left| \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 \bar{\omega} + \alpha_2 \bar{\omega}^2 \right| = |\widehat{1_{2\cdot R}}(\gamma_{0})|$$
Since $1+\bar{\omega}+\bar{\omega}^2=0$ we can write
$$|(\alpha_0-\alpha)+(\alpha_1-\alpha)\bar{\omega}+(\alpha_2-\alpha)\bar{\omega}^2|= 3M.$$
Thus for some $i\in\{0,1,2\}$ we have
$$\alpha_i-\alpha \geq \frac{|2\cdot R|\beta/2}{3^n},$$
which is the desired density increment of $2\cdot R$ on a translate of $V$. Notice that a factor of $1/2$ has to be present because we are only interested in density increments and not decrements. If $2r\in t+V$ for some $r\in R$ then $r\in t/2+V$ so we automatically get the same increment for $R$ on a translate of the same subspace.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:freiman_ruzsa}
Let $A\subseteq \mathbb{F}_3^n$ and $|A+A|\leq K|A|.$ Then $A$ is contained in an affine subspace of size at most $3^{C_{FR}K}|A|$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:f3_subspace}
Let $k,n$ be positive integers and let $A_1\cup A_2\cup\cdots\cup A_k\cup R=\mathbb{F}_3^n$ be a partitioning. Assume that if $x,y\in A_i$ for some $A_i$, then $(x+y)/2\notin R$ and that $|R|\geq 3^n/10.$ Then $R$ contains an affine subspace of codimension at most $O(k^2).$
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We will iteratively apply Lemma~\ref{lem:fields_increment} $z$ times ($z$ to be chosen later in the proof). In the first iteration we let $V_1=\mathbb{F}_3^n$ and $R_1=R$. $A^1$ will be the largest colour present in $V_1$ (we use superscript not to confuse with the colours $A_i$ from the partition). As a result of the lemma for $V_i,R_i,A^i$ we get $t_{i+1}+V_{i+1}$ on which $R_i$ has increased density. Because the equation $x+y=2r$ is translation invariant the same rule holds, that is if $A^{i+1}$ is the largest colour in $t_{i+1}+V_{i+1}$ and $x,y\in t_{i+1}+A^{i+1}$ we have $(x+y)/2\notin t_{i+1}+R_{i+1}$. Thus further iteration is possible. 

    Let $\alpha_i=|R_i|/|V_i|$ so that $\alpha_1\geq 1/10$, then Lemma~\ref{lem:fields_increment} guarantees
    $$\alpha_{i+1}\geq \Bigl(1+\frac{1-\alpha_i}{2k}\Bigr)\alpha_i.$$
    Clearly there exists an absolute constant $C$, such that $1-\alpha_i$ halves every $Ck$ steps, therefore
    $$1-\alpha_{z}\leq 2^{-\lfloor z/Ck \rfloor}.$$
    Choose $z = 4\lceil C_{FR}\rceil \lceil C\rceil k^2=O(k^2)$ so that $1-\alpha_z < 3^{-2C_{FR}k}.$
    
    We now remove all colours one by one from $V_z$ with $k$ applications of Theorem~\ref{thm:freiman_ruzsa}. Choose the largest colour in $V_z$, call it $A$. Because of our assumption $(A+A)\cap(2\cdot R)=\emptyset$ and so $|A+A|\leq |V_z|-|R|\leq k|A|$. That means $A$ is contained in an affine subspace of size at most $3^{C_{FR}k}|A|$. Because $3^{C_{FR}k}|A|<|V_z|$ this is a proper affine subspace. Its two parallel affine subspaces contain no points from $A$. On one of them density of the remaining colours is at most $\frac{3}{2}2^{-s}$. We repeat this argument $k$ times to obtain an affine subspace of codimension at most $z+k=O(k^2)$ contained entirely inside $R_z$.

    We always have a proper affine subspace containing any colour as
    $$3^{C_{FR}k}\Bigl(\frac{3}{2}\Bigr)^k2^{-s} < 1.$$
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:bohr_set_increment}
Let $N\geq 1$ be an odd integer and let $A,R\subseteq B$, where $B\subseteq \mathbb{Z}_N$ is a regular Bohr set of rank $d$ and width $\rho$. Let $|R|=\alpha|B|$ and $|A|= \beta |B|$. Suppose that there are no solutions to the equation $x+y=2r$ where $x,y\in A$ and $r\in R$.
Then there exists a Bohr set $B'$ of rank $d+1$ and width at least $\Omega(\rho\beta/2^{d+1})$ such that we have
$$|(t+B')\cap R|/|B'|\geq \Bigl(1+\frac{\beta}{2^{d+4}}\Bigr)\alpha.$$
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We begin by choosing a possibly large subset $A'\subseteq A$ with the property $A'+A'\subseteq 2\cdot B$. It is not hard to see that by choosing only elements with the same parity after multiplication by all characters generating $B$ we get $|A'|\geq |A|/2^d$.
Consider the function $f(x)=1_{2\cdot R}(x)-\alpha 1_{2\cdot B}(x)$. Define its fourier coefficients as 
$$\hat{f}(\gamma):=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_x f(x)\overline{\gamma(x)},$$
where $\gamma$ is an element of the dual group of $\mathbb{Z}_N$. Notice that $\hat{f}(\gamma_0)=0$, where $\gamma_0=1$ is the trivial character. Then by Parseval's Theorem we have
$$\sum_{x}f(x)1_{A'}*1_{A'}(x) = N^2\sum_{\gamma}\hat{f}(\gamma)\overline{\hat{1_{A'}}(\gamma)}^2.$$
as we use unnormalised convolution. By using the assumption we have
$$\alpha|A'|^2=\alpha\sum_{x\in {2\cdot B}}1_{A'}*1_{A'}(x)= \Bigl|\sum_x f(x)1_{A'}*1_{A'}(x)\Bigr|\leq N^2\sum_{\gamma}|\hat{f}(\gamma)||\hat{1_{A'}}(\gamma)|^2.$$
Notice that if we were considering $A$ instead of $A'$, then the first equality would not be true. In this sense the loss of the $2^d$ factor is necessary. As a consequence we get
$$\frac{\beta |A'||R|}{2^dN^2} \leq \frac{\alpha|A'|^2}{N^2} = \sum_{\gamma}|\hat{f}(\gamma)||\hat{1_{A'}}(\gamma)|^2 \leq |\hat{f}(\gamma_{\max})|\sum_{\gamma}|\hat{1_{A'}}(\gamma)|^2 = |\hat{f}(\gamma_{\max})|\frac{|A'|}{N}$$
and thus $|\hat{f}(\gamma_{\max})|\geq \frac{\beta}{2^d}\cdot\frac{|R|}{N}$. 

To obtain a density increment, choose $B'$ to be generated by the same characters as $B$ with the addition of $\gamma_{\max}$. Let the width be $\rho'\gg \rho\beta/2^{d+1}$ so that $B'+B\subseteq B_{1+\rho'/\rho}$ and $|B_{1+\rho'/\rho}|\leq (1+\beta/2^{d+4})|B|$ because of regularity of $B$. By considering $\sum_t 1_B*1_{B'}(t)f(t)$ in a standard way we find a density increment on a translate of $B'$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:progressions}
Let $N\geq 1$ be an integer and let $A,R\subseteq [N]$, with $|A|\geq \beta N\geq N^{4/5}$. Suppose that there are no solutions to the equation $x+y=2r$ where $x,y\in A$ and $r\in R$.
Then there exists an arithmetic progression $P$ with length at least $N^{1/4}$ such that
$$|P\cap R|/|P|\geq \Bigl(1+\beta/32\Bigr)\frac{|R|}{N}.$$
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Consider $A,R$ as subsets of $\mathbb{Z}_{2N+1}$. Clearly there are no solutions to the equation $x+y=2r$ with $x,y\in A$ and $r\in R$ because there is no wrap around modulo $2N+1$. Because $[N]$ can be viewed as a translate of rank 1 Bohr set (with width $1/4$) by Lemma~\ref{lem:bohr_set_increment} there exists a Bohr set $B'$ with rank 2 and width $\Omega(\beta)$ such that for some translate $t$ of $B'$ we have  
$$|(t+B')\cap R|/|B'|\geq \Bigl(1+\beta/32\Bigr)\frac{|R|}{N}.$$ 
By Lemma~\ref{lem:find_progression} we can find $P$ contained in a dilate of $B'$ of length at least $N^{1/4}$ and with simple averaging argument we can ensure the same or larger density of $R$ on this progression.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:termination}
Let $k,N$ be positive integers with $k<N$ and let
$$A_1\cup A_2\cup\cdots\cup A_k\cup R=[N]$$ 
be a partitioning. Assume that if $x,y\in A_i$ for some $A_i$, then $(x+y)/2\notin R$ and that $|R|\geq (1-(10k)^{-6})N.$ Then $R$ contains an arithmetic progression of length at least $N^{\exp(-O(k\log\log (2k)))}$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
Let $A\subseteq [N]$ with $|A+A|\leq K|A|.$ There exists a Generalised Arithmetic Progression with dimension at most $O(\log^4 K)$ and size at least $\exp(-O(\log^4 K))|A|$, such that
$$\mathcal{P}\subseteq 2A-2A.$$
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
(Of Lemma~\ref{lem:termination})
We will show how to find a progression of length $N^{1/O(\log^4k)}$ which avoids one of the colours and on which density of $R$ is at least $1-(k-1)^{-6}$. Then the lemma is proved by induction on $k$ as with $k=0$ the statement is vacuously true.

Let $I_0=[N]$ and iterate up to $1000k$ times the following:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Let $\rho_i = 1-|R\cap I_i|/|I_i|$ be the compliment (of $R$) density within $I_i$.
    \item {If $i=1000k$ terminate with $I=I_i$ (CASE 1). In that case $$\rho=\rho_i<(1-1/200k)^{1000k}\rho_0\leq \rho_0/4.$$}
    \item Split our interval $I_i=\{1,2,3,\dots, |I_i|\}$ into even numbers and odd numbers.
    \item If on both parts compliment density is at least $(1-1/200k)\rho_i$ terminate with $I=I_i$ (CASE 2).
    \item Otherwise continue iteration with $I_{i+1}$ being the parity class with complement density at most $(1-1/200k)\rho_i$.
\end{itemize}

Let $M=|I|$ and choose the largest colour within $I$, call it $A$. Again divide $I$ into two parity classes and choose one where $A$ has at least $\lfloor \rho M/2k\rfloor$ elements, call it $J$ and the other one $J'$. Let $B=A\cap J$ and $\rho_J=1-|J\cap R|/|J|.$

Crucially because $(B+B)\cap (2\cdot R)=\emptyset$ we have
$$|B+B|\leq M-|I\cap (2\cdot R)|\leq k|A|\leq 2k|B|.$$
Thus $B$ is a set of small doubling. If $k|B|<\sqrt{M}$, then $R$ trivially contains an arithmetic progression of length at least $\sqrt{M}$ by the pigeonhole principle, so let us assume the opposite. By the Bogolyubov-Ruzsa's Lemma we find a Generalised Arithmetic Progression $\mathcal{P}$ with dimension at most $O(\log^4 k)$ and size at least $\exp(-O(\log^4k))|B|$ such that
$$\mathcal{P}\subseteq 2B-2B.$$
Choose $P$ to be the longest dimension of $\mathcal{P}$ so that 
$$|P|\geq |\mathcal{P}|^{1/O(\log^4 k)}\geq M^{1/O(\log^4 k)}.$$
Define $P'$ to be the first $\lfloor|P|/10^7k\rfloor$ elements of $P$.

By containment and the Plünnecke's inequality we have
$$|B-P'|\leq |2B-3B|\leq (2k)^5|B|.$$
And so there are at most $(2k)^5 \rho M$ translates of $P'$ which contain any element of $B$ and thus $A$ because elements of $A\setminus B$ trivially have the other parity.

Thus the number of translates of $P'$ which are contained wholly in $I\setminus A$ is at least
$$|J|-2M/10^6k-(2k)^5\rho M\geq |J|(1-1/200k).$$
Averaging their complement densities we obtain one translate $t+P\subseteq I\setminus A$ such that its complement density is
$$1-|(t+P')\cap R|/|P'| \leq \frac{\rho_J}{1-1/200k}.$$

We now finish the proof regardless of whether we are in CASE~1 or CASE~2. In both cases we choose $t+P'$ which has size at least $(N/2^{1000k})^{1/O(\log^4 k)}=N^{1/O(\log^4 k)}$.

If we are in CASE~1, then $\rho_J\leq 2\rho\leq \rho_0/2$ and so
$$\frac{\rho_J}{1-1/200k}\leq \rho_0\leq (10k)^{-6}$$
as desired.

If we are in CASE~2, then $\rho_J\leq (1+1/100k)\rho$ and so
$$\frac{\rho_J}{1-1/200k}\leq (1+1/25k)\rho_0\leq (1+1/25k)(10k)^{-6}\leq 10^{-6}(k-1)^{-6}$$
as desired.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:progression_in_integers}
Let $k,N$ be positive integers with $k<N$ and let
$$A_1\cup A_2\cup\cdots\cup A_k\cup R=[N]$$ 
be a partitioning. Assume that if $x,y\in A_i$ for some $A_i$, then $(x+y)/2\notin R$ and that $|R|\geq N/10.$ Then $R$ contains an arithmetic progression of length at least $N^{\exp(-O(k\log(2k)))}$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We apply Corollary~\ref{cor:progressions} $s$ times (to be decided later in the proof). Let $N_0=N$ and denote the length of the progression in the first application as $N_1$, second $N_2$ etc. Let $\alpha_0=\alpha$ and denote the density of $R$ in the first application as $\alpha_1$, second $\alpha_2$ etc. Denote the subsequent progressions as $P_0=[N], P_1,\cdots, P_s$. Let $A^{i}$ be the largest intersection of any colour with $P_i$. Because there at $k$ colours we have $|A_i|\geq \frac{1-\alpha_i}{k}N_i$.
    
    From the conclusion of Corollary~\ref{cor:progressions} we know that for any $i=0,1,\cdots,s-1$ we have
    $$N_{i+1}\geq N_i^{1/4}$$
    and
    $$\alpha_{i+1}\geq \Bigl(1+\frac{1-\alpha_i}{2k}\Bigr)\alpha_i.$$
    Observe that
    $$\frac{1-\alpha_{i+1}}{1-\alpha_i} \leq 1-\frac{\alpha_i}{2k}\leq 1-\frac{1}{20k}$$
    and so we have $1-\alpha_{i+{20k}}\leq (1-\alpha_i)/2$. In other words, the proportion of the complement of $R$ in $P_i$ halves every $20k$ steps.

    In order to apply Lemma~\ref{lem:termination} the complement proportion must be at most $(10k)^{-6}$ as so $s=\lceil120k\log_2k\rceil=O(k\log(2k))$ suffices. Finally for the interval $N_s$ we apply Lemma~\ref{lem:termination} to obtain an arithmetic progression of length $N^{\exp(-O(k\log(2k)))}$ inside $R$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:small_energy}
Let $N\geq 1$ be an integer and let $A,R\subseteq \mathbb{Z}_N$. Let $|R|=\alpha N$ and $|A|=\beta N$ with $\alpha\geq 1/10$  and $\alpha+\beta\leq 1$. Assume that $E(A)\leq  |A|^3/M$ and that if $x,y\in A$ then $x+y\notin R$.
Then there exists an arithmetic progression $P$ of length at least $$N^{\Omega((\beta/(1-\alpha))^2\log(2/(1-\alpha))^{-1})}$$ such that we have
$$|P\cap R|/|P|\geq \Bigl(1+\Omega(\beta M)\Bigr)\frac{|R|}{N}.$$
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Consider the balanced function 
$$f(x)=1_R(x)-\alpha$$
and define its fourier coefficients as 
$$\hat{f}(\gamma):=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_x f(x)\overline{\gamma(x)},$$
where $\gamma$ is an element of the dual group of $\mathbb{Z}_{N}$. Then by Parseval's Theorem we have
$$\sum_{x}f(x)1_A*1_A(x) = N^2\sum_{\gamma}\hat{f}(\gamma)\overline{\hat{1_A}(\gamma)}^2.$$

By using the assumption we have
$$\alpha\beta^2 N^2=\alpha |A|^2 \leq \Bigl|\sum_x (1_{R}(x)-\alpha)1_A*1_A(x)\Bigr|\leq N^2\sum_{\gamma}|\hat{f}(\gamma)||\hat{1_A}(\gamma)|^2.$$
Let $\eta = \frac{\alpha\beta}{2}$ and define the large spectrum of $\hat{f}$ as
$$S := \{\gamma : |\hat{f}(\gamma)|\geq \eta\}.$$
Let us split the sum to the sum of elements in $S$ and other elements.
$$\alpha\beta^2\leq \sum_{\gamma\notin S}|\hat{f}(\gamma)||\hat{1_A}(\gamma)|^2 + \sum_{\gamma\in S}|\hat{f}(\gamma)||\hat{1_A}(\gamma)|^2\leq \eta\frac{|A|}{N} + \sum_{\gamma\in S}|\hat{f}(\gamma)||\hat{1_A}(\gamma)|^2$$
and so
$$\frac{\alpha\beta^2}{2} \leq \alpha\beta^2 - \frac{\eta\beta}{2}\leq \sum_{\gamma\in S}|\hat{f}(\gamma)||\hat{1_A}(\gamma)|^2.$$
Since $\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}}1_A*1_A(x)^2=E(A)\leq |A|^3/M$ we get by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
$$\frac{\alpha^2\beta^4}{4} \leq \Bigl(\sum_{\gamma\in S}|\hat{f}(\gamma)|^2\Bigr)\cdot\frac{8|A|^3}{MN^3}$$
and so
$$\frac{\alpha^2\beta M}{32} \leq \sum_{\gamma\in S}|\hat{f}(\gamma)|^2.$$

We now routinely obtain a density increment based on the large $L_2$ norm we just showed.


Define a regular Bohr set $B'$ of rank $|S|$ and width between $1/100$ and  $1/200$ generated by the characters from $S$. Then because $|\hat{\mu}_{B'}(\gamma)|=|\hat{1_{B'}}(\gamma)| /|B'|$ is at least $9/10N$ on the characters $\gamma\in S$ we have
$$\sum_x |f * \mu_{B'}(x)|^2 = N\sum_{\gamma} |\hat{f}(\gamma)|^2 |\hat{\mu}_B(\gamma)|^2 \geq \frac{N}{2}\sum_{\gamma \in S} |\hat{f}(\gamma)|^2\geq \frac{\alpha^2 \beta MN}{64}.$$
We now use this fact to extract a density increment on some $x$, that is large $f * \mu_{B'}(x)$. Notice that we are interested only in positive deviations from the mean so we cannot simply take average value, instead we use the fact that the mean of $f*\mu_{B'}(x)$ is 0 to write
$$\frac{\alpha^2 \beta M}{64}\leq \mathbb{E}_x (f * \mu_{B'}(x))^2 + \alpha\cdot \mathbb{E}_x \Bigl(f * \mu_{B'}(x)\Bigr) = \mathbb{E}_x (f * \mu_{B'}(x))(f * \mu_{B'}(x)+\alpha).$$
Now the second factor is always positive and so
$$\frac{\alpha^2\beta M}{64}\leq \max_x \Bigl(f * \mu_{B'}(x)\Bigr) \cdot \alpha.$$
Therefore there exists an $x$ for which
$$\frac{\alpha \beta M}{64} \leq \max_x \Bigl(1_R * \mu_{B'}(x)-\alpha\Bigr).$$
Therefore there exists a translate $x$ with 
$$\frac{|(x+B')\cap R|}{|B'|}\geq \alpha\Bigr(1+\frac{\beta M}{64}\Bigl).$$

We now show $B'$ actually contains a bohr set with a smaller set of generators. Let $C=\mathbb{Z}_n\setminus R$ and apply Chang's lemma to the set
$$\Lambda = \Bigl\{\gamma : |\hat{1_C}(\gamma)|\geq \frac{\eta}{1-\alpha}|C|/N\Bigr\}.$$
Observe that $S$ and $\Lambda$ are identical with the possible exception of the trivial character because $\hat{1_C}(\gamma)=-\hat{1_R}(\gamma)$ for non-trivial $\gamma$. Thus there exists a set of characters $\Gamma$ with
$$|\Gamma|\ll \Bigl(\frac{1-\alpha}{\eta}\Bigr)^2\log(N/|C|)\ll \Bigl(\frac{1-\alpha}{\eta}\Bigr)^2\log(2/(1-\alpha))$$
such that $S\subseteq\mathrm{spec}(\Gamma)$. As a consequence there exists a regular Bohr set $$B=\mathrm{Bohr}(\Gamma,\rho)\subseteq \mathrm{Bohr}(S,1/200) \subseteq B',$$
where $\rho = \Omega(|\Gamma|^{-2})$ such that $|B'_{1+\rho}|\leq 1.01|B'|$. By considering all translates of $B$ that intersect $B'$ we find one where the density increment is at least $1+\Omega(\beta M)$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:find_progression} we extract an arithmetic progression of length at least $N^{\Omega(1/|\Gamma|)}$ from $B$ with the increment on $R$ at least $1+\Omega(\beta M)$.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
{(Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers)}\label{thm:bsg}
    Let $A\subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ and $E(A)\geq |A|^3/K$. Then there exists a set $A'\subseteq A$ such that $|A'|\gg |A|/K$ and 
    $$|A'+A'|\ll K^{10}|A'|.$$
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:large_energy}
Let $N,k\geq 1$ be integers and let $R\subseteq \mathbb{Z}_N$ be a set with $|R|=\alpha N$ where $\alpha\geq 1/10$.  Let $A_1,A_2,\cdots,A_k\subseteq \mathbb{Z}_N$ be disjoint sets with $|A_i|\geq \beta N$. Assume that $E(A_i)\geq  |A_i|^3/M$ and that if $x,y\in A_i$ for some $A_i$, then $x+y\notin R$.

Then there exists an arithmetic progression $P$ of length at least $N^{1/\Omega(k\log^4M)}$ such that we have
$$|P\cap R|/|P|\geq \Bigl(1+\frac{\beta k}{\exp(C\log^4 M)}\Bigr)\frac{|R|}{N}.$$
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:bsg} to the sets $A_1,A_2,\cdots,A_k$ to find subsets $A_1',A_2',\cdots, A_k'$. To these subsets apply Theorem~\ref{thm:freiman} in order to obtain regular bohr sets $B_1,B_2,\cdots, B_k$ with the following properties.
$$\mathrm{rank}(B_i)\ll \log^4 M$$
$$\exp(\log^4 M)|A_i\cap B_i|\geq |A_i|.$$
Let $B'$ be a regular bohr set generated by the union of generators of $B_1,B_2,\cdots B_k$ and with width $\rho \approx \frac{1}{(k\log^4
M)^2}$  such that for every $B_i$ we have $|B_i^{1+\rho}|\leq 1.01|B_i|$.

Let us now use Lemma~\ref{lem:find_progression} to find an arithmetic progression $P$ of length at least $N^{1/\Omega(k\log^4M)}$ contained in $B'_{\epsilon}$ with $\epsilon\approx \rho^2$. Notice that $B'_{1-\epsilon} + P\subseteq B'$.

Define $T_i$ to be the set of all translates of $P$ such that the colour $A_i$ for $i\leq k$ intersects it in at least $|P|/8\exp(\log^4M)$ elements. To lower bound $|T_i|$ we write
$$|T_i||P| + (|B_i|-|T_i|)\frac{|P|}{8\exp(\log^4M)}\geq \frac{1}{2}\sum_{t\in B_i} |A_i\cap(t+P)|\geq \frac{1}{4}|A_i'||P|\geq \frac{|B_i||P|}{4\exp(\log^4M)}$$
and so we have $|T_i|\gg \frac{|B_i|}{\exp(\log^4M)}$. Let $T=\bigcup_i T_i$, then any $t\in T$ can belong to at most $\exp(\log^4 M)$ sets $T_i$ because total density of such colours can be at most $1$ within $t+P$. Therefore we have
$$|T|\geq \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k|T_i|}{\exp(\log^4M)}\geq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^k|B_i|}{\exp(\log^4M)^2}\geq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^k|A_i|}{\exp(\log^4M)^3}\geq \frac{\beta kN}{\exp(4\log^4M)}.$$

We now consider two options. Let us first suppose that for some $t\in T$ we have 
$$|R\cap (t+P)|\geq \alpha \Bigl(1-\frac{1/1000}{\exp(\log^4 M)}\Bigr)|P|.$$

Then we use Corollary~\ref{cor:progressions} with $\beta'=\frac{1}{8\exp(\log^4M)}$ to show a large, $1+\beta'/2$, density increment of $R$ on $t+P'$, where $|P'|\geq |P|^{1/4}$.

Otherwise we calculate average size of $|R\cap(t+P)|/|P|$ where $t\notin T$, call it $r$. Notice that
$$(N-|T|)r + |T|\Bigl(1-\frac{1/1000}{\exp(\log^4 M)}\Bigr)\alpha \geq \alpha N$$
and so
$$(N-|T|)r \geq \alpha(N-|T|) + \alpha\frac{|T|/1000}{\exp(\log^4 M)}.$$
Upon dividing both sides by $N-|T|$ we have a density increment on some $x\notin T$ of
$$1+\frac{|T|/1000}{(N-|T|)\exp(\log^4 M)}\geq 1 + \frac{\beta k}{1000\exp(5\log^4 M)}.$$

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:general_increment}

Let $N,k\geq 1$ be integers and let $R\subseteq [N]$ be a set with $|R|=\alpha N$, where $\alpha\geq 1/10$.  Let $A_1,A_2,\cdots,A_k\subseteq [N]$ be disjoint subsets of $[N]$ with size at least $(1-\alpha)N/4k$. Assume that if $x,y\in A_i$ for some $A_i$, then $(x+y)/2\notin R$.

Then there exists an arithmetic progression $P$ of length at least $N^{ck^{-2}\log(2/(1-\alpha)))^{-1}}$ such that we have
$$|P\cap R|/|P|\geq \Bigl(1+\frac{1-\alpha}{k}\exp(c\log^{1/4} k)\Bigr)\frac{|R|}{N}.$$
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We begin by moving the problem to $\mathbb{Z}_N$ without losing generality. Of course we have to do it in a way that does not introduce spurious solutions so let us proceed carefully. We start by splitting each $A_i$ into even and odd elements.
$$A_i=A_i^{odd}\cup A_i^{even}.$$
One of the sets $A_i^{odd}$, $A_i^{even}$ has at least $|A_i|/2$ elements. Then choose our new $A_i$ to be 
$$\{(a-1)/2 : a\in A_i^{odd}\},$$
or
$$\{a/2 : a\in A_i^{even}\},$$
whichever is larger. Let $I_1$ be the indices $i$ for which the first alternative was chosen and $I_2$ for which the second. Choose $I_1$ if $\sum_{i\in I_1}|A_i| > \sum_{i\in I_2}|A_i|$ and $I_2$ otherwise. Remove all remaining indices. If we have chosen $I_2$, let us keep $R$ as it is, if we have chosen $I_1$ let us subtract 1 from every element of $R$. This way we have sets $A_i$ for $i\in I_1$ or $i\in I_2$ such that all their elements are smaller than $N/2$ and so no wrap-around occurs. Thus for all such $A_i$ we have
$$\sum_{x\in \mathbb{Z}_N}1_R(x)1_{A_i}*1_{A_i}(x)=0.$$
We combine Lemma~\ref{lem:small_energy} and Lemma~\ref{lem:large_energy} with $M=\exp(C'\log^{1/4}k)$. 
Now if for any $A_i$ we have $$E(A_i)\leq |A_i|^3/M$$ we are in position to use Lemma~\ref{lem:small_energy}. If that is not the case we use Lemma~\ref{lem:large_energy}.
In one case our density increment is $1+\Omega((1-\alpha) M/k)$ and in the other case $1+\frac{1-\alpha}{\exp(C\log^{4} M)}$. Since for our choice of $M$ these two are optimised to be larger than $1+(1-\alpha)\exp(c\log^{1/4} k)/k$ we have finished the proof.

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{thm:longer_progression_in_integers}
Let $k,N$ be positive integers with $k<N$ and let
$$A_1\cup A_2\cup\cdots\cup A_k\cup R=[N]$$ 
be a partitioning. Assume that if $x,y\in A_i$ for some $A_i$, then $(x+y)/2\notin R$ and that $|R|\geq N/10.$ Then $R$ contains an arithmetic progression of length at least $$N^{\exp(-O(k\log\log(2k))}$$
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The reasoning is similar to the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:progression_in_integers} but we get stronger density increment on a shorter progression, thus we need to calculate how these two changes balance out. We iterate Lemma~\ref{lem:general_increment} $s$ times ($s$ to be decided later). Let $N_i$ be the length of arithmetic progression on which $R$ is dense after $i$ steps, so that $N_0=N$. Similarly as before let $P_i$ be the consecutive progressions and let $\alpha_i$ be the density of $R$ on $P_i$. Let $\beta_i=(1-\alpha_i)/4k$. Since this is $1/4$ of average colour density on $P_i$ all colours below this density can cover at most the fraction of $1/4$ of $P_i$. It is not necessarily the case that there are $k/4$ colours of density at least $\beta_i$ but we can assume so without loss of generality for the following reason. Choose the largest colour in $P_i$ and from any $\beta_i N_i$ elements of it create a brand new colour, which obviously satisfies the assumption. After at most $k$ steps we removed at most $\beta_i k = (1-\alpha)/4$ elements. Thus there must exist a colour with at least $\beta_i P_i$ elements and we can apply Lemma~\ref{lem:general_increment} with the same $k$. Similarly as before
$$\alpha_{i+1}\geq \Bigl(1+\frac{(1-\alpha_i)\exp(c\log^{1/4}k)}{k}\Bigr)\alpha_i$$
and so
$$\frac{1-\alpha_{i+1}}{1-\alpha_i} \leq 1-\frac{\alpha_i \exp(c\log^{1/4}k)}{k}\leq 1-\frac{\exp(c\log^{1/4}k)}{10k}.$$
Thus after at most $20k/\exp(c\log^{1/4}k)$ steps the size of the complement of $R$ halves, that is for any $i$ we have 
\begin{equation}
\frac{1-\alpha_{i+20k/\exp(c\log^{1/4}k)}}{1-\alpha_i} \leq \frac{1}{2}. \tag{*}
\end{equation}

We can apply Lemma~\ref{lem:termination} once $1-\alpha_i\leq (10k)^{-6}$ and so we can assume the opposite for all $i\leq s$. Moreover, by (*) we see that taking $s=O(k/\exp(c\log^{1/4}k))$ suffices (the $\log(2k)$ term is overpowered by the $\exp(c\log^{1/4}k)$ term). By the conclusion of Lemma~\ref{lem:general_increment} we have
$N_{i+1}\geq N_i^{ck^{-2}\log(2/(1-\alpha_i)))^{-1}}\geq N_i^{ck^{-2}\log (2k)}$.

Thus we apply Lemma~\ref{lem:termination} to the interval of length
$$N_s\geq N_i^{(ck^{-2}\log (2k))^s} \geq N^{\exp(-O(k/\exp(c\log^{1/4}k)))}$$
thus getting the desired length of a progression in $R$ owed to the worse progression length coming from Lemma~\ref{lem:termination}.
