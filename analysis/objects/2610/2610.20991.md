<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
A normal complex surface singularity whose minimal good resolution graph has at least two nodes does not admit a rational homology disk smoothing.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{corollary:ClassificationOfGraphs}
The minimal good resolution graph of a normal surface singularity admitting a \(\mathbb{Q}\)HD smoothing\xspace is either the linear graph of a cyclic quotient singularity of type \(\frac{1}{p^2}(1,pq-1)\), with \(p>q>0\) relatively prime, or one of the graphs in Figures~\ref{figure:fig1} and \ref{figure:fig2}.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:PreciseMain}
The normal complex surface singularities admitting a \(\mathbb{Q}\)HD smoothing\xspace are precisely the following:

\begin{itemize}
\item the cyclic quotient singularities of type \(\frac{1}{p^2}(1,pq-1)\), with \(p>q>0\) relatively prime;

\item the weighted homogeneous singularities corresponding to the taut graphs of Figure~\ref{figure:fig1}; and

\item the weighted homogeneous singularities with resolution graphs of Figure~\ref{figure:fig2} whose normalized cross ratio is \(\lambda=e^{\pm\pi i/3}\) for \((3,3,3;4)\), \(\lambda=2\) for \((2,4,4;3)\), and \(\lambda=9\) for \((2,3,6;2)\).
\end{itemize}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:qhd-properties}
Every rational homology disk smoothing of a normal surface singularity is \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Gorenstein and occurs over a one-dimensional smoothing component.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:rational-conjecture}
Let \((V,E) \to (X,0)\) be the minimal good resolution of a rational surface singularity which is not a rational double point. Then
 
\[
 h^1(V,\mathcal{S}) \le h^1(V,\mathcal{L}),
\]
 
with equality if and only if \((X,0)\) is quasi-homogeneous.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark--1: body_md -->
Park--Shin~\cite{ParkShin2026} prove that the Wahl defect \(\Delta(X,0) := h^1(V,\mathcal{O}_V)-h^1(V,\mathcal{S})+h^1(V,\mathcal{L})\) of a non-Gorenstein \(\mathbb{Q}\)-Gorenstein normal surface singularity is nonnegative, with equality precisely in the weighted homogeneous case. As an application, they show that every normal surface singularity admitting a rational homology disk smoothing is weighted homogeneous. Together with the known classification in the weighted homogeneous case, this also gives Corollary~\ref{cor:PreciseMain}.
<!-- end remark--1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:increment}
At a step \(D \mapsto D+G\) of \eqref{eq:sequence},
 
\[
 \Phi(D+G)-\Phi(D)=c_{\mathcal{S}}(D,G)-c_{\mathcal{O}}(D,G).
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Restriction of logarithmic vector fields to \(G\) gives a natural exact sequence
 
\[
 0 \to \mathcal{O}_{G} \to \mathcal{S}|_{G} \to \Theta_{G}(-\log\Sigma_G) \to 0,
\]
 
where \(\Sigma_G\) is the reduced divisor of intersection points \(G \cap(E-G)\). By adjunction,
 
\[
 \Theta_{G}(-\log\Sigma_G) \simeq \mathcal{O}_{G}(-K_G-\Sigma_G) \simeq \mathcal{L}|_{G}.
\]
 
Tensoring with \(\mathcal{O}_V(-D)|_G\) gives
 
\[
 0 \to \mathcal{O}_V(-D)|_{G} \to \mathcal{S}(-D)|_{G} \to \mathcal{L}(-D)|_{G} \to 0.
\]
 
At a computation step, 
 
\[\deg(\mathcal{L}(-D)|_G)=c_1(\mathcal{L})G-DG<0,\]
 
so \(H^0(G,\mathcal{L}(-D)|_G)=0\) and
 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cL-zero}
 c_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)=0.
\end{equation}
 
The cohomology sequence of the tensored exact sequence therefore gives
 
\[
 H^0(G,\mathcal{O}_V(-D)|_{G}) \simeq H^0(G,\mathcal{S}(-D)|_{G}).
\]
 
Moreover, since \(G\) is a curve, its second coherent cohomology vanishes, so the same cohomology sequence gives
 
\[
h^1(G,\mathcal{S}(-D)|_G)
=h^1(G,\mathcal{O}_V(-D)|_G)+h^1(G,\mathcal{L}(-D)|_G).
\]


Since \(f\) is proper with fibers of dimension at most one, \(R^qf_*\mathcal{G}=0\) for \(q\ge2\) and every coherent \(\mathcal{G}\)~\cite{GrauertRemmert1984}. As \(X\) is Stein, Leray and Cartan's Theorem~B give
 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coherent-H2-zero}
 H^2(V,\mathcal{G})=0.
\end{equation}

For each \(\mathcal{P} \in \{\mathcal{O}_V,\mathcal{S},\mathcal{L}\}\), consider the restriction exact sequence
 
\[
 0 \to \mathcal{P}(-D-G)
 \to \mathcal{P}(-D)
 \to \mathcal{P}(-D)|_{G}
 \to 0.
\]
 
The associated cohomology sequence and \eqref{eq:coherent-H2-zero} give
 
\[
 h^1(V,\mathcal{P}(-D-G)) -h^1(V,\mathcal{P}(-D))
 = c_{\mathcal{P}}(D,G) -h^1(G,\mathcal{P}(-D)|_{G}).
\]
 
Subtracting the formulas for \(\mathcal{L}\) and \(\mathcal{O}_V\) from the formula for \(\mathcal{S}\), and using the cohomology identity above and \eqref{eq:cL-zero}, gives the assertion.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:terminal-positivity}
Every coefficient of the exceptional rational cycle \([\mathcal{L}]-c_1(\mathcal{L})\) is strictly positive.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We use the intersection pairing to regard \(c_1(\mathcal{L})\) as an exceptional rational cycle. By the definition of \([\mathcal{L}]\), we have
 
\[
 ([\mathcal{L}]-c_1(\mathcal{L}))E_i \le 0
\]
 
for every \(i\).

This cycle is nonzero. Indeed, if \([\mathcal{L}]=c_1(\mathcal{L})\), then \(\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_V(-K_V-E)\) gives
 
\[
 Z_K=E+[\mathcal{L}],
\]
 
where \(Z_K\) is the numerical anticanonical cycle. This cycle would be integral, so the singularity would be numerically Gorenstein and rational. Its minimal good resolution graph would then be an \(ADE\) graph by N\'emethi~\cite[Example~7.1.5(d)]{Nemethi2022}. Such a graph has at most one node, contrary to our assumption.

Negative definiteness and connectedness now imply that all coefficients are positive~\cite[Corollary~2.1.19(a)]{Nemethi2022}.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:logarithmic-connection}
Let \(\mathcal{P}\) be a line bundle on \(V\), and write its first Chern class as an exceptional rational cycle
 
\[
 c_1(\mathcal{P})=\sum_i b_iE_i.
\]
 
Then \(\mathcal{P}\) admits an integrable logarithmic connection with residue \(-b_i\) along \(E_i\).
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Choose a positive integer \(n\) such that \(nc_1(\mathcal{P})\) is an integral exceptional divisor. For a rational surface singularity, the Chern class map
 
\[
 c_1 \colon \operatorname{Pic}(V) \to H^2(V,\mathbb{Z})
\]
 
is an isomorphism by N\'emethi~\cite[Proposition~7.1.10]{Nemethi2022}. The equality of Chern classes therefore gives
 
\[
 \mathcal{P}^{\otimes n} \simeq \mathcal{O}_V(nc_1(\mathcal{P})).
\]
 
The trivial connection on \(\mathcal{O}_V\) induces an integrable logarithmic connection on \(\mathcal{O}_V(nc_1(\mathcal{P}))\), with residue \(-nb_i\) along \(E_i\); see Esnault--Viehweg~\cite[Lemma~2.7]{EsnaultViehweg1992}.

Let \(\nabla^{(n)}\) denote the induced connection on
\(\mathcal{P}^{\otimes n}\).  Choose an open cover \(\{U_\alpha\}\) of
\(V\) such that \(\mathcal{P}\) has a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
section \(s_\alpha\) on each \(U_\alpha\). We can therefore write
 
\[
 \nabla^{(n)}(s_\alpha^{\otimes n})
 =s_\alpha^{\otimes n} \otimes A_\alpha,
\]
 
where \(A_\alpha\) is a logarithmic one-form on \(U_\alpha\). On
overlaps, \(s_\beta=g_{\alpha\beta}s_\alpha\), so
\[
A_\beta=A_\alpha+n\,dg_{\alpha\beta}/g_{\alpha\beta}.
\]
Thus the forms \(A_\alpha/n\) define a logarithmic connection on \(\mathcal{P}\). It is integrable because \(dA_\alpha=0\), and its residue along \(E_i\) is \(-b_i\).

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:local-system-vanishing}
For every rank-one complex local system \(\mathbb{V}\) on \(V-E\), we have
 
\[
 R\Gamma(V,j_!\mathbb{V})=0.
\]
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We describe the neighborhoods used to establish this vanishing
result. Choose \(X\) as the intersection of a local analytic model of
the singularity with a sufficiently small open ball of radius
\(\varepsilon\). For \(0<\delta<\varepsilon\), let \(X_\delta \subset
X\) be its intersection with the concentric open ball of radius
\(\delta\), and put
 
\[
 W_\delta=f^{-1}(X_\delta).
\]
 
The local conical structure of an isolated complex singularity identifies these punctured neighborhoods with products of the same link and radial intervals. In particular, for \(\varepsilon\) sufficiently small, the inclusions
 
\[
 X_\delta-\{0\} \hookrightarrow X-\{0\}
\]
 
are homotopy equivalences. Since \(f\) is an isomorphism away from the exceptional divisor, the corresponding inclusions
 
\[
 W_\delta-E \hookrightarrow V-E
\]
 
are also homotopy equivalences, and induce isomorphisms on cohomology with coefficients in the local system \(\mathbb{V}\).


The neighborhoods \(W_\delta\) are cofinal among neighborhoods of \(E\): if \(W\supset E\) is open, properness makes \(f(V\setminus W)\) closed and disjoint from \(0\), so \(X_\delta\cap f(V\setminus W)=\emptyset\) for all sufficiently small \(\delta\), and hence \(W_\delta\subset W\).

Now put \(K=Rj_*\mathbb{V}\), and let \(i \colon E \hookrightarrow V\). For every sufficiently small \(\delta\), the natural identifications
 
\[
 R\Gamma(V,K) \simeq R\Gamma(V-E,\mathbb{V}),
 \quad
 R\Gamma(W_\delta,K) \simeq R\Gamma(W_\delta-E,\mathbb{V})
\]
 
show that the restriction morphism
 
\[
 R\Gamma(V,K) \to R\Gamma(W_\delta,K)
\]
 
is a quasi-isomorphism. Since \(E\) is compact, cohomology on \(E\) with coefficients in \(i^{-1}K\) is obtained as the direct limit of cohomology over its neighborhoods; see Kashiwara--Schapira~\cite[Proposition~2.5.1 and Remark~2.6.9]{KashiwaraSchapira1990}. Using the cofinal system \(W_\delta\), we conclude that the natural restriction morphism
 
\[
 R\Gamma(V,K) \to R\Gamma(E,i^{-1}K)
\]
 
is a quasi-isomorphism.

Since \(j^{-1}K \simeq \mathbb{V}\), the localization triangle is
 
   
                                                     
   
 
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.5em, column sep=2em]
	j_!\mathbb{V} \arrow[rr] & & K \arrow[dl] \\
	& i_*i^{-1}K \arrow[ul, "+1" description] &
\end{tikzcd}
\]
 
Applying \(R\Gamma(V,-)\), the map between the second and third terms becomes the quasi-isomorphism just established. The first term must therefore vanish, which proves the lemma.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:positive-residue-comparison}
Let \(\mathcal{P}\) be a line bundle on \(V\) with an integrable logarithmic connection whose residues along all components of \(E\) are positive. If \(H^1(V,\mathcal{P})=0\), then the logarithmic de Rham differential induces an isomorphism
 
\[
 H^1(V,\Omega_V^1(\log E) \otimes \mathcal{P})
 \to H^1(V,\Omega_V^2(\log E) \otimes \mathcal{P}).
\]
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Write \(\nabla\) for the connection on \(\mathcal{P}\). Its logarithmic de Rham complex is
 
\[
 \operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{P},\nabla)
 = [\mathcal{P} \to \Omega_V^1(\log E) \otimes \mathcal{P}
 \to \Omega_V^2(\log E) \otimes \mathcal{P}],
\]
 
placed in degrees \(0,1,2\). Its differentials are induced by \(\nabla\) and are \(\mathbb{C}\)-linear.

Let \(j \colon V-E \hookrightarrow V\) be the inclusion, and let
\(\mathbb{V}\) be the local system of horizontal sections of
\(\nabla\) on \(V-E\).
Deligne's logarithmic de Rham comparison theorem, together with
Poincar\'e--Verdier duality, identifies the logarithmic de Rham
complex with the extension by zero of its horizontal local system
when no residue is a nonpositive integer; see
Deligne~\cite[II, Proposition~3.13]{Deligne1970} and the
extension-by-zero formulation in
Esnault--Viehweg~\cite[\S2.11]{EsnaultViehweg1992}.
Since all residues of \(\nabla\) are positive, the natural morphism
\[
j_!\mathbb{V} \longrightarrow
\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{P},\nabla)
\]
is a quasi-isomorphism.
 
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-system-vanishing} therefore gives
 
\[
 \mathbb{H}^k(V,\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{P},\nabla))=0
\]
 
for every \(k\).

The vanishing used in Equation~\eqref{eq:coherent-H2-zero} gives
 
\[
 H^q(V,\mathcal{G})=0 \quad(q \ge 2)
\]
 
for every coherent sheaf \(\mathcal{G}\) on \(V\). Consider the hypercohomology spectral sequence
 
\[
 E_1^{p,q}=H^q(V,\Omega_V^p(\log E) \otimes \mathcal{P})
 \Longrightarrow
 \mathbb{H}^{p+q}(V,\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{P},\nabla)).
\]
 
Only the columns \(p=0,1,2\) and the rows \(q=0,1\) can be
nonzero. Since \(H^1(V,\mathcal{P})=0\), the terms
\(E_2^{1,1}\) and \(E_2^{2,1}\) are the kernel and cokernel
of the map in the statement. No higher differential enters or
leaves either term: incoming differentials have source in a
row \(q\ge2\), and outgoing differentials have target in a
column \(p\ge3\). Thus \(E_2^{1,1}=E_\infty^{1,1}=0\) and
\(E_2^{2,1}=E_\infty^{2,1}=0\), by the vanishing of
hypercohomology.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:nonzero}
Let \(0 \le j<N\). If \(\rho_{G_j}(D_j) \ne 0\), then
 
\[
 c_{\mathcal{S}}(D_j,G_j) \ge c_{\mathcal{O}}(D_j,G_j).
\]
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
As in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:increment}, \(\deg(\mathcal{L}(-D_j)|_{G_j})<0\), and the restriction sequence identifies
\[
H^0(G_j,\mathcal{S}(-D_j)|_{G_j})
\simeq H^0(G_j,\mathcal{O}_V(-D_j)|_{G_j}).
\]

The logarithmic de Rham complex of \((\mathcal{L}(-D_j),\nabla_j)\) has the form
 
\[
 \mathcal{L}(-D_j)
 \xrightarrow{\nabla_j}
 \mathcal{S}(-D_j)
 \xrightarrow{\nabla_j^1}
 \mathcal{O}_V(-D_j).
\]
 
The second differential preserves sections vanishing along \(G_j\), and hence induces a map on the restrictions to \(G_j\). Let \(r_{\mathcal{S},j}\) and \(r_{\mathcal{O},j}\) denote the restriction maps on global sections. We obtain a commutative diagram
 
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
 H^0(V,\mathcal{S}(-D_j))
 \arrow[r,"r_{\mathcal{S},j}"]
 \arrow[d,"\nabla_j^1"']
 &
 H^0(G_j,\mathcal{S}(-D_j)|_{G_j})
 \arrow[d,"\overline{\nabla}_j^1"]
 \\
 H^0(V,\mathcal{O}_V(-D_j))
 \arrow[r,"r_{\mathcal{O},j}"']
 &
 H^0(G_j,\mathcal{O}_V(-D_j)|_{G_j}).
\end{tikzcd}
\]

On the subbundle
 
\[
 \mathcal{O}_V(-D_j)|_{G_j} \subset \mathcal{S}(-D_j)|_{G_j},
\]
 
the induced differential is, up to sign, multiplication by the residue \(\rho_{G_j}(D_j)\); see Esnault--Viehweg~\cite[Lemma~2.6(b)]{EsnaultViehweg1992}. Under the preceding identification of section spaces, \(\overline{\nabla}_j^1\) is multiplication by the nonzero scalar \(\pm\rho_{G_j}(D_j)\), hence is an isomorphism.

Since the right-hand vertical map is an isomorphism, the
diagram induces a surjection
\(\operatorname{coker} r_{\mathcal{S},j}\twoheadrightarrow
\operatorname{coker} r_{\mathcal{O},j}\), which gives the inequality.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:zero-vanishing}
For the divisor \(D^+\) defined above, we have
 
\[
 H^1(V,\mathcal{O}_V(-D^+))=0.
\]
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let \(Z_K=\sum z_iE_i\) denote the numerical anticanonical cycle as before, with \(z_i>0\); see Némethi~\cite[Corollary~2.1.19]{Nemethi2022}. Put
 
\[
 Z_0 :=\lfloor Z_K\rfloor,\quad
 P_0 :=\sum_{\substack{\text{\(E_i\) is not a leaf}\\0<z_i<1}}E_i.
\]
 
The relation \(c_1(\mathcal{L}(-E'))=Z_K-E-E'\) gives
 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dplus}
 D^+=Z_0+P_0.
\end{equation}
 
This follows coefficientwise from \(b_i=z_i-1\) on leaves
and \(b_i=z_i-2\) on the other components.

Write \(Z_K=Z_0+F_0\), with all coefficients of \(F_0\) in \([0,1)\). Apply Fujino's analytic relative vanishing theorem for surfaces (\cite[Theorem~11.3(2)]{Fujino2021}) to the smooth klt pair \((V,F_0)\), the line bundle \(\mathcal{O}_V(-Z_0)\), the auxiliary divisor \(D=0\), and the resolution map \(f:V \to X\). The numerical condition holds because
 
\[
 (-Z_0)E_i+(D-(K_V+F_0))E_i =-(K_V+Z_K)E_i=0
\]
 
for every exceptional component. Thus
 
\[
 R^1f_*\mathcal{O}_V(-Z_0)=0,\quad
 H^1(V,\mathcal{O}_V(-Z_0))=0.
\]

It remains to add \(P_0\). Set
 
\[
 \Sigma=\bigcup_{z_i \ge 1}E_i=\operatorname{Supp} Z_0.
\]
 
The anticanonical equations are
 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:concavity}
 \sum_{j \sim i}z_j=2-d_i(1-z_i).
\end{equation}
 
For \(z_i<1\), they imply
 
\[
 \sum_{j \sim i}z_j \le 2z_i<2.
\]
 
Along a path, this inequality expresses discrete concavity. Hence \(\Sigma\) is connected (possibly empty): a path between two distinct components would have endpoint values at least one and internal values below one, contrary to concavity.

Assume first that \(\Sigma \ne \emptyset\). Every connected component of the subgraph determined by \(\operatorname{Supp} P_0\) has a unique edge to \(\Sigma\). Uniqueness follows because the graph is a tree. To see existence when \(\Sigma\) contains a component that is not a leaf, join a vertex in \(\operatorname{Supp} P_0\) to a vertex of \(\Sigma\) that is not a leaf. The corresponding path in the dual graph lies in the subgraph determined by \(E'\). The first vertex of this path outside the starting component has a positive coefficient, is not a leaf, and does not lie in \(\operatorname{Supp} P_0\); hence it lies in \(\Sigma\). If \(\Sigma\) contains only leaves, its connectedness forces it to consist of a single leaf. Then every vertex that is not a leaf has coefficient in \((0,1)\), so \(P_0=E'\), which meets that leaf.

Let \(q\) be a vertex in \(\operatorname{Supp} P_0\) adjacent to \(\Sigma\). Since \(z_q<1\), the inequality following from \eqref{eq:concavity} shows that the coefficient of the adjacent vertex in \(\Sigma\) is less than two. It is at least one, so its coefficient in \(Z_0\) is exactly one. Every other boundary neighbor of that connected component of \(\operatorname{Supp} P_0\) is a leaf with coefficient below one and hence has coefficient zero in \(Z_0\).

In each connected component of \(\operatorname{Supp} P_0\), first add the vertex adjacent to \(\Sigma\), and then add the remaining vertices in order away from \(\Sigma\). For the first vertex, the only nonzero adjacent contribution is the coefficient of that adjacent component; for every later vertex it is the coefficient of the previously added predecessor. Immediately before \(E_i\) is added, the current cycle \(Z\) therefore satisfies \(ZE_i=1\). Hence the quotient in
 
\[
 0 \to \mathcal{O}_V(-Z-E_i) \to \mathcal{O}_V(-Z) \to \mathcal{O}_{E_i}(-Z) \to 0
\]
 
is \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1)\); its \(H^0\) and \(H^1\) vanish, so \(H^1\) is unchanged. If \(\Sigma\) is empty, then \(Z_0=0\) and \(P_0=E'\), and the result follows from \eqref{eq:initial-O}. Equation~\eqref{eq:Dplus} proves the lemma.

Near infinity, the corresponding local generators \(v_i\) and \(\widetilde\eta_i\) satisfy
 
\[
 v_i=z_i^{d_i}u_i\pmod{u_i^2},\quad

\]
 
After restriction to \(E_i\), these transition formulas give

 u_i\partial_{u_i}=v_i\partial_{v_i},\quad

\]

The image of \(\eta_iP_i(z_i)\partial_{z_i}\) generates the tangential quotient globally: near infinity it is represented by

and \(w_i^{t_i}P_i(w_i^{-1})\) has value \(1\) at \(w_i=0\). Hence every section has the stated affine form, with \(\tau_i\in\mathbb{C}\).



The section is consequently represented near infinity by
 
\[
 \widetilde\eta_i\left(\{w_i^{t_i-2}B_i(w_i^{-1})+d_i\tau_iw_i^{t_i-1}P_i(w_i^{-1})\}v_i\partial_{v_i}-\tau_iw_i^{t_i}P_i(w_i^{-1})\partial_{w_i}\right).
\]
 
The tangential coefficient is regular. Since \(P_i\) is monic of degree \(t_i\), regularity of the normal coefficient implies that \(B_i\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(t_i-1\). The coefficient of \(w_i^{-1}\) must also vanish, which gives \([z_i^{t_i-1}]B_i=-d_i\tau_i\).

For every connected component \(R_\alpha\) of \(\mathcal{R}\), the natural map
 
\[
 H^0(R_\alpha,Q_{\mathcal{S}}|_{R_\alpha})
 \to \bigoplus_{i \in I_\alpha}H^0(E_i,\mathcal{L}(-D^-)|_{E_i})
\]
 
is zero.
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Fix a global section on \(R_\alpha\). Its tangential part already vanishes on every component with \(\beta_i>0\), by Lemma~\ref{lem:zero-component-sections}. For a component with \(\beta_i=0\), use the coordinates, local generator, and coefficients \(B_i,\tau_i\) from that lemma. Notice that \(\beta_i=0\) implies that every neighbor of \(E_i\) belongs to \(R_\alpha\), by \eqref{eq:beta-boundary}.

Consider an intersection point \(p_{ij}=E_i \cap E_j\) with \(\beta_i=\beta_j=0\). Choose normal-crossing coordinates \((x,y)\) with \(E_i=(x=0)\) and \(E_j=(y=0)\). In the logarithmic tangent fiber at this point, the normal and tangential Euler directions on \(E_i\) are \(x\partial_x\) and \(y\partial_y\), respectively; on \(E_j\) their roles are reversed. Multiplying a branch parameter by a unit does not change these directions in the fiber. Write
 
\[
 \eta_j(p_{ij})=\gamma_{ij}\eta_i(p_{ij}),\quad
 \gamma_{ij} \in \mathbb{C}^*,\quad
 \gamma_{ji}=\gamma_{ij}^{-1}.
\]
 
The two restrictions of our section then have values
 
\begin{align*}
 &\eta_i(p_{ij})(B_i(a_{ij})x\partial_x+\tau_iP_i'(a_{ij})y\partial_y),\\
 &\gamma_{ij}\eta_i(p_{ij})(\tau_jP_j'(a_{ji})x\partial_x+B_j(a_{ji})y\partial_y).
\end{align*}
 
Equality of these values gives
 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matching}
 \begin{aligned}
 B_i(a_{ij})&=\gamma_{ij}P_j'(a_{ji})\tau_j,\\
 P_i'(a_{ij})\tau_i&=\gamma_{ij}B_j(a_{ji}).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
 
If instead \(\beta_i=0\) and \(\beta_j>0\), the tangential part on \(E_j\) is zero. Comparing its value with the normal direction on \(E_i\) therefore gives \(B_i(a_{ij})=0\).

For an edge with \(\beta_i=\beta_j=0\), put
 
\[
 \lambda_{ij}=-\gamma_{ij}\frac{P_j'(a_{ji})}{P_i'(a_{ij})}.
\]
 
The intersection points on each component are distinct, so these scalars are nonzero, and \(\gamma_{ji}=\gamma_{ij}^{-1}\) gives \(\lambda_{ij}\lambda_{ji}=1\). Since \(\deg B_i \le t_i-1\), Lagrange interpolation gives
 
\[
 [z_i^{t_i-1}]B_i=\sum_{j \sim i}\frac{B_i(a_{ij})}{P_i'(a_{ij})}.
\]
 
Combining this identity with Lemma~\ref{lem:zero-component-sections}, \eqref{eq:matching}, and the vanishing of \(B_i(a_{ij})\) when \(\beta_j>0\), we obtain
 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau-system}
 -d_i\tau_i+
 \sum_{\substack{j \sim i\\\beta_j=0}}\lambda_{ij}\tau_j=0,\quad
 \lambda_{ij}\lambda_{ji}=1.
\end{equation}

The subgraph on the vertices with \(\beta_i=0\) is a forest. In each connected component, choose a root and nonzero scalars \(c_i\) recursively by
 
\[
 c_j=c_i/\lambda_{ij}
\]
 
whenever \(i\) is the parent of \(j\). Substitute \(\tau_i=c_i\sigma_i\) into \eqref{eq:tau-system} and divide the \(i\)-th equation by \(c_i\). The resulting system has coefficient matrix \((E_iE_j)_{\beta_i=\beta_j=0}\), a negative definite principal submatrix of the intersection matrix. Hence every \(\sigma_i\), and therefore every \(\tau_i\), is zero.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{lem:conormal-injection}
Let \(R\) be a reduced divisor supported on \(E\). The action on the conormal bundle of \(R\) induces a canonical injection
 
\[
 \ker(\mathcal{S}|_R \to \Theta_R) \hookrightarrow \mathcal{O}_R.
\]
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
A logarithmic vector field preserves the ideal \(\mathcal{I}_R\), and hence induces a derivation on \(\mathcal{O}_R\) and a differential operator on \(\mathcal{I}_R/\mathcal{I}_R^2\). Both depend only on its restriction as a section of \(\mathcal{S}|_R\). If the induced derivation on \(\mathcal{O}_R\) is zero, the action on \(\mathcal{I}_R/\mathcal{I}_R^2\) is \(\mathcal{O}_R\)-linear. The conormal sheaf is invertible because \(R\) is a Cartier divisor on the smooth surface \(V\). Its endomorphism sheaf is canonically \(\mathcal{O}_R\), so this action defines the asserted morphism. We check its injectivity locally.

At a smooth point of \(R\) that does not lie on \(E-R\), choose coordinates with \(R=(x=0)\) and write
 
\[
 \theta=ax\partial_x+b\partial_y.
\]
 
The induced derivation on \(\mathcal{O}_R\) is zero exactly when \(b \in (x)\). The conormal action is multiplication by \(a\bmod(x)\). If this function is zero, both coefficients of \(\theta|_R\) are zero.

At an intersection point of two components of \(R\), take \(R=(xy=0)\) and write
 
\[
 \theta=ax\partial_x+by\partial_y.
\]
 
In the ring \(\mathbb{C}\{x,y\}/(xy)\), zero induced derivation means \(a \in (y)\) and \(b \in (x)\). The conormal action is multiplication by
 
\[
 \frac{\theta(xy)}{xy}=a+b\pmod{(xy)}.
\]
 
If this element is zero, then \(a=-b\) belongs to \((x) \cap (y)=0\) in this ring. Thus both coefficients vanish, and \(\theta|_R=0\).

Finally, at an intersection point of \(R\) with \(E-R\), choose coordinates with \(R=(x=0)\) and the other component given by \((y=0)\). Write
 
\[
 \theta=ax\partial_x+by\partial_y.
\]
 
The induced derivation on \(\mathcal{O}_R\) is zero exactly when \(b \in (x)\), and the conormal action is multiplication by \(a\bmod(x)\). Its vanishing again gives \(\theta|_R=0\).

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:lattice-estimate}
Let \((X,0)\) be a rational surface singularity whose minimal good resolution graph \(\Gamma\) has at least two nodes and is of type \(\mathcal{A}\), \(\mathcal{B}\), or \(\mathcal{C}\). If the chosen construction of \(\Gamma\) contains \(m\) operations of type (B-1), then
 
\[
 h^1(V,\mathcal{L}) \le m.
\]
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Let \(\Gamma_0\) be the initial graph of the chosen construction. Denote its central vertex by \(E_0\) and its three leaves by \(E_1,E_2,E_3\). We use the same symbols for the corresponding basis elements of \(\Lambda_{\Gamma_0}\). Their intersection numbers are
 
\[
 E_0^2=-1,\quad
 E_i^2=-d_i,\quad
 E_0E_i=1,\quad
 E_iE_j=0\quad (1 \le i \ne j \le 3),
\]
 
where the leaves are ordered so that
 
\[
 (d_1,d_2,d_3)=
 \begin{cases}
 (3,3,3),&\text{for type }\mathcal{A},\\
 (4,4,2),&\text{for type }\mathcal{B},\\
 (6,3,2),&\text{for type }\mathcal{C}.
 \end{cases}
\]

The intersection form of \(\Gamma_0\) is negative semidefinite,
since \(\sum_{i=1}^3d_i^{-1}=1\).
Write \(\xi_0=x_0E_0+\sum_{i=1}^3x_iE_i\). The following choices satisfy \(\xi_0E_v=k_{\Gamma_0}(E_v)\) on the vertex basis:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 \text{Type}
 &(x_0;x_1,x_2,x_3)
 &(\xi_0E_0,\xi_0E_1,\xi_0E_2,\xi_0E_3)
 &\xi_0^2\\ \hline
 \mathcal{A}
 &(-1;0,0,0)
 &(1,-1,-1,-1)
 &-1\\
 \mathcal{B}
 &(0;0,0,1)
 &(1,0,0,-2)
 &-2\\
 \mathcal{C}
 &(0;-\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3},1)
 &(1,2,-1,-2)
 &-3
\end{array}
\]
 
Completing the square gives
 
\[
 \mathfrak{q}_{\Gamma_0}(D)=\frac{D^2+\xi_0D}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(D+\frac{\xi_0}{2}\right)^2-\frac{\xi_0^2}{8}.
\]
 
The first term on the right is nonpositive because the intersection form is negative semidefinite. The values of \(\xi_0^2\) in the table therefore imply
 
\[
 \mathfrak{q}_{\Gamma_0}(D)
 \le -\frac{\xi_0^2}{8}
 \le \frac{3}{8}
 <1.
\]

Since \(k_{\Gamma_0}\) is characteristic, \(\mathfrak{q}_{\Gamma_0}(D)\) is integral for \(D\in\Lambda_{\Gamma_0}\). Thus the bound \(\mathfrak{q}_{\Gamma_0}(D)<1\) gives \(\mathfrak{q}_{\Gamma_0}(D)\le0\).

We next examine how \(\mathfrak{q}\) changes under the blow-up operations. For either operation, let \(T\) be the graph before the blow-up and \(T_+\) the graph after it, and let \(\pi\colon T_+ \to T\) denote the corresponding blow-down. Write \(R\) for the basis element associated with the new exceptional vertex, and let \(C_\pi\) be the set of vertices corresponding to components through the blow-up center. Define the total-transform homomorphism \(\pi^*\colon\Lambda_T \to \Lambda_{T_+}\) by
 
\[
 \pi^*E_v=
 \begin{cases}
 E_v+R,&v \in C_\pi,\\
 E_v,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
 
Every integral cycle $D_+$ on \(T_+\) has a unique expression
 
\[
 D_+=\pi^*D+uR,\quad
 D \in \Lambda_T,\quad u \in \mathbb{Z}.
\]
 
For an operation of type (B-1),
 
\[
 \mathfrak{q}_{T_+}(D_+)-\mathfrak{q}_T(D)
 =-\frac{u(u+3)}{2}
 \le 1.
\]
 
For an operation of type (B-2),
 
\[
 \mathfrak{q}_{T_+}(D_+)-\mathfrak{q}_T(D)
 =-\frac{u(u+1)}{2}
 \le 0.
\]

Let \(T\) now denote the graph obtained after all the blow-up operations and immediately before the modification (M). Starting from the bound \(\mathfrak{q}_{\Gamma_0} \le 0\) and applying the preceding inequalities successively, we obtain
 
\[
 \mathfrak{q}_T(D) \le m
 \quad\text{for every }D \in \Lambda_T.
\]

The modification (M) preserves the vertices and edges and changes only the weight of the unique vertex of weight \(-1\). We therefore identify \(\Lambda_T\) and \(\Lambda_\Gamma\) as free abelian groups using their common vertex basis. Let \(a\) be the coefficient of this vertex in an integral cycle \(D\). Its weight changes from \(-1\) to \(-k\), where \(k=4\), \(3\), or \(2\) for types \(\mathcal{A}\), \(\mathcal{B}\), or \(\mathcal{C}\), respectively.  Hence
 
\[
 \mathfrak{q}_\Gamma(D) = \mathfrak{q}_T(D)-\frac{k-1}{2}a(a-1) \le \mathfrak{q}_T(D) \le m,
\]
 
where the first inequality follows from \(a(a-1) \ge 0\) for every integer \(a\).
Since \([\mathcal{L}]\ge E'\ge0\), \eqref{eq:cohomology-formula} gives
 
\[
 h^1(V,\mathcal{L})
 =\mathfrak{q}_\Gamma([\mathcal{L}])
 \le m. \qedhere
\]

\label{thm:main-technical}
Let \((X,0)\) be a rational surface singularity whose minimal good resolution graph \(\Gamma\) is of type \(\mathcal{A}\), \(\mathcal{B}\), or \(\mathcal{C}\) and has at least two nodes. Then
 
\begin{equation*}                          
 h^1(V,\mathcal{S})+\sum_{i=0}^{n}(d_i-3) \le 0.
\end{equation*}

	Proposition~\ref{prop:cohomological-comparison}, and
	\eqref{eq:twisted-L-comparison} give
	\[
		h^1(V,\mathcal{S})
		=r-4+h^1(V,\mathcal{S}(-E'))
		\le r-4+h^1(V,\mathcal{L}(-E'))

		h^1(V,\mathcal{S})+\sum_{v\in\Gamma}(d_v-3)

	\]
