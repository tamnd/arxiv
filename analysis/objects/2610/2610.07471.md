<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm.Complex}
Let $1,\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ be a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis for a cubic field $K\subset\mathbb{R}$ which has a complex embedding. Then there are constants $c,\kappa>0$, depending only on $\bm{\alpha}$, such that
\[
G(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\tau})\ \ge\ c\,(\log|\bm{\tau}|)^{\kappa}
\]
for infinitely many $\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{N}^2$. In particular $\sup_{\tau_1,\tau_2\ge 1}G(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\tau})=\infty$.

\label{lem.Dict}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem.Tails}
Let $\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^2$ be such that $\overline{Az_{\bm{\alpha}}}=X$. Then for every $T>0$ the set $\{\hat g_{\bm{\tau}} z_{\bm{\alpha}}:\tau_1,\tau_2>T\}$ is dense in $X$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The matrix $(u_{\bm{\alpha}}^{\,t})^{-t}=u_{\bm{\alpha}}^{-1}$ has second and third columns $\bm{e}_1$ and $\bm{e}_2$, so for every $\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^2$ we have that
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.DualVecs}
\tau_1\bm{e}_1,\ \tau_2\bm{e}_2\in\hat g_{\bm{\tau}}^{-1}u_{\bm{\alpha}}^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^3=(\hat g_{\bm{\tau}} z_{\bm{\alpha}})^* .
\end{equation}
Fix $T>0$ and let
\[
E_T=\bigl\{\mathcal{L}\in X:s\bm{e}_i\in\mathcal{L}^*\text{ for some }i\in\{1,2\}\text{ and some }0<s\le T\bigr\}.
\]
By \eqref{eqn.DualVecs}, $\hat g_{\bm{\tau}} z_{\bm{\alpha}}\in E_T$ whenever $\min\{\tau_1,\tau_2\}\le T$. We claim that $E_T$ is closed and has empty interior. Granting this, let $\mathcal{U}\subseteq X$ be non-empty and open. Then $\mathcal{U}\setminus E_T$ is non-empty and open, so by hypothesis it contains a point $\hat g_{\bm{\tau}} z_{\bm{\alpha}}$, and this point has $\tau_1,\tau_2>T$.

To see that $E_T$ is closed, let $\mathcal{L}_n\in E_T$ converge to $\mathcal{L}\in X$, with $s_n\bm{e}_i\in\mathcal{L}_n^*$ and $0<s_n\le T$. Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that $i$ is the same for every $n$ and that $s_n\rightarrow s\in[0,T]$. Choose representatives $M_n\rightarrow M$ in $\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})$ of $\mathcal{L}_n$ and $\mathcal{L}$, and write $s_n\bm{e}_i=M_n^{-t}\bm{v}_n$ with $\bm{v}_n\in\mathbb{Z}^3\setminus\{\bm 0\}$. The vectors $\bm{v}_n=s_nM_n^{\,t}\bm{e}_i$ are bounded, so after passing to a further subsequence $\bm{v}_n=\bm{v}$ is constant, and then $s_n\bm{e}_i=M_n^{-t}\bm{v}\rightarrow M^{-t}\bm{v}$. Since $\bm{v}\not=\bm 0$ we have that $M^{-t}\bm{v}\not=\bm 0$, so $0<s\le T$ and $s\bm{e}_i=M^{-t}\bm{v}\in\mathcal{L}^*$. Hence $\mathcal{L}\in E_T$.

To see that $E_T$ has empty interior, let $\mathcal{L}\in X$ and let $k\in\mathrm{SO}(3)$. Then $(k\mathcal{L})^*=k\mathcal{L}^*$, so $k\mathcal{L}\in E_T$ only if $k\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}\bm{e}_1\cup\mathbb{R}\bm{e}_2$ for some $\bm{v}\in\mathcal{L}^*\setminus\{\bm 0\}$. For each such $\bm{v}$ the set of $k\in\mathrm{SO}(3)$ with $k\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}\bm{e}_1\cup\mathbb{R}\bm{e}_2$ is a union of four cosets of $\mathrm{Stab}(\bm{v})\cong \mathrm{SO}(2)$, and so it has Haar measure zero. Since $\mathcal{L}^*$ is countable, the set of $k$ with $k\mathcal{L}\in E_T$ has measure zero, and in particular there are $k$ arbitrarily close to the identity with $k\mathcal{L}\not\in E_T$. Since $k\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}$ as $k\rightarrow e$, this shows that $\mathcal{L}$ is not an interior point of $E_T$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem.Unit}
There is $u\in K$ with $0<u<1$ and $u\Lambda=\Lambda$. For any such $u$ we have that $|\sigma(u)|=u^{-1/2}$, and the number
\[
\vartheta=\frac{\arg\sigma(u)}{2\pi}
\]
is irrational. Consequently, for every $k_0\in\mathbb{N}$, the set $\{k\vartheta\bmod 1:k\ge k_0\}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $Z_\Lambda=\{x\in K:x\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda\}$. This is a subring of $K$ which contains $\mathbb{Z}$ and is contained in $\Lambda$ (because $1\in\Lambda$), so it is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-module. Since $\mathcal O_K\Lambda$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-module which spans $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, there is an integer $c\ge 1$ with $c\,\mathcal O_K\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda$, and therefore $c\,\mathcal O_K\subseteq Z_\Lambda$. Thus $Z_\Lambda$ is an order in $K$, and by Dirichlet's unit theorem its unit group has rank $1$. Let $u_0\in Z_\Lambda^\times$ have infinite order. Since $u_0$ is real and is not $\pm 1$, we have that $|u_0|\not=1$, and one of the four numbers $\pm u_0^{\pm 1}$ lies in $(0,1)$; call it $u$. Then $u,u^{-1}\in Z_\Lambda$, so $u\Lambda=\Lambda$.

Now let $u$ be any such element. Then $u\in Z_\Lambda^\times$, so $\operatorname{N}(u)=\pm 1$, and since $\operatorname{N}(u)=u|\sigma(u)|^2>0$ we have that $|\sigma(u)|^2=1/u$. If $\vartheta$ were rational, say $\vartheta=p/q$ with $q\in\mathbb{N}$, then $\sigma(u^{2q})=|\sigma(u)|^{2q}e^{4\pi i p}$ would be real, so $u^{2q}\in\mathbb{Q}$. However, this would imply that $u=\pm 1$, a contradiction.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem.Mult}
There are a constant $c_1>0$ and a sequence of elements $\gamma^{(1)},\gamma^{(2)},\ldots$ of $\Lambda$, say $\gamma^{(r)}=x_r+y_r\alpha_1+z_r\alpha_2$ with $x_r,y_r,z_r\in\mathbb{Z}$, such that, writing $Q_r=|\sigma(\gamma^{(r)})|$,
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=4pt,parsep=4pt]
\item[(i)] $|\gamma^{(r)}|\le 1/2$ and $\gcd(y_r,z_r)=1$;
\item[(ii)] $\operatorname{Re}\sigma(\gamma^{(r)})<0<\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\gamma^{(r)})$;
\item[(iii)] $Q_r\rightarrow\infty$ as $r\rightarrow\infty$, and $Q_r\,\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\gamma^{(r)})\le c_1$ for every $r$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For $(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^2$ put
\[
\mathcal I(y,z)=y\,\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_1)+z\,\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_2),\]
and
\[\mathcal T(y,z)=\tfrac12\bigl(y(\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha_1)-3\alpha_1)+z(\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha_2)-3\alpha_2)\bigr).
\]
If $\gamma=x+y\alpha_1+z\alpha_2$ with $x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}$ then \[\mathcal I(y,z)=\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\gamma).\] Furthermore, since
\[\operatorname{Re}\sigma(\gamma)=\frac12(\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma)-\gamma)\quad \text{and}\quad \operatorname{Tr}(\gamma)=3x+y\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha_1)+z\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha_2),\]we also have that
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.ReSigma}
\mathcal T(y,z)=\operatorname{Re}\sigma(\gamma)-\gamma.
\end{equation}

The linear forms $\mathcal I$ and $\mathcal T$ are linearly independent. To see this, for real $y,z$ put $\nu=y\alpha_1+z\alpha_2\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\zeta=y\sigma(\alpha_1)+z\sigma(\alpha_2)\in\mathbb{C}$. Then $\mathcal T(y,z)=\operatorname{Re}\zeta-\nu$, so if $(y,z)$ annihilated both forms then the $\mathbb{R}$-linear map $\mathbb{R}^3\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}$ given by $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x+y\alpha_1+z\alpha_2,\ x+y\sigma(\alpha_1)+z\sigma(\alpha_2))$ would send $(0,y,z)$ and $(\nu,0,0)$ to the same point $(\nu,\nu)$. This map is an isomorphism, since its matrix is obtained from the matrix $(\sigma_i(\alpha_j))$ of embeddings, hence $y=z=0$.

Next, $\mathcal I(y,z)\not=0$ for $(y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$, since otherwise $\sigma(y\alpha_1+z\alpha_2)$ would be real, so that $y\alpha_1+z\alpha_2\in\mathbb{Q}$, contradicting the linear independence of $1,\alpha_1,\alpha_2$. In particular $\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_2)\not=0$, and
\[
w=-\frac{\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_1)}{\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_2)}\qquad\text{satisfies}\qquad \mathcal I(y,z)=\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_2)\,(z-wy),
\]
with $w$ irrational. By linear independence $\mathcal T(1,w)\not=0$, and we write $\mathcal T(1,w)=-\delta c_0$ with $\delta\in\{\pm 1\}$ and $c_0>0$.

Let $p_r/q_r$ be the convergents of the continued fraction expansion of $w$, so that $q_r\rightarrow\infty$, $\gcd(p_r,q_r)=1$, $0<|q_rw-p_r|<1/q_r$, and $q_rw-p_r$ alternates in sign. For the infinitely many $r$ for which the sign of $p_r-q_rw$ equals $\delta$ times the sign of $\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_2)$, put $(y,z)=\delta(q_r,p_r)$. Then $\gcd(y,z)=1$, $y=\delta q_r$, and
\[
\mathcal I(y,z)=\delta\,\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_2)\,(p_r-q_rw)\in\Bigl(0,\frac{|\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_2)|}{q_r}\Bigr).
\]
Since $(y,z)=y(1,w)+(0,z-wy)$ we have that
\[
\mathcal T(y,z)=y\,\mathcal T(1,w)+(z-wy)\,\mathcal T(0,1)=-c_0q_r+O(1/q_r).
\]
Choose $x\in\mathbb{Z}$ to be a nearest integer to $-(y\alpha_1+z\alpha_2)$, and let $\gamma=x+y\alpha_1+z\alpha_2$, so that $|\gamma|\le 1/2$. By \eqref{eqn.ReSigma}, $\operatorname{Re}\sigma(\gamma)\le\frac12-c_0q_r+O(1/q_r)$, which is negative for $r$ large, and together with $\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\gamma)=\mathcal I(y,z)>0$ this gives (ii). Finally $Q=|\sigma(\gamma)|\ge|\operatorname{Re}\sigma(\gamma)|\rightarrow\infty$, while
\[
Q\le|\operatorname{Re}\sigma(\gamma)|+|\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\gamma)|\le c_0q_r+1
\]
for $r$ large, so that $Q\,\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\gamma)\le(c_0q_r+1)|\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_2)|/q_r\le 2c_0|\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_2)|$ once $q_r\ge 1/c_0$. Discarding finitely many $r$ and relabelling gives the sequence in the statement, with $c_1=2c_0|\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\alpha_2)|$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop.Ladder}
Let $\beta=n+\bm{m}\cdot\bm{\alpha}$ and $\beta'=n'+\bm{m}'\cdot\bm{\alpha}$ be elements of $\Lambda$, and suppose that
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=4pt,parsep=4pt]
\item[(i)] $\beta>0$ and $m_1,m_2<0$;
\item[(ii)] $m_1',m_2'>0$ and, writing $M=m_1'$, $N=m_2'$, $C_1=M/|m_1|$ and $C_2=N/|m_2|$, we have that $C_1,C_2\ge 3$;
\item[(iii)] $|\beta'|<\beta$;
\item[(iv)] $4MN\beta<1$;
\item[(v)] the three $2\times 2$ minors of the matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}n&m_1&m_2\\ n'&m_1'&m_2'\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ have greatest common divisor $1$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $G(\bm{\alpha},(M,N))\ge\min\{\lfloor C_1\rfloor,\lfloor C_2\rfloor\}$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Write $a=1/C_1=|m_1|/M$ and $b=1/C_2=|m_2|/N$, so that $a,b\le 1/3$, and write $\lambda_0=MN\beta$ and $\lambda_0'=MN\beta'$, so that $|\lambda_0'|<\lambda_0$ by (iii), and $\lambda_0<1/4$ by (iv). Put $J=\min\{\lfloor C_1\rfloor,\lfloor C_2\rfloor\}\ge 3$ and $Y=J\lambda_0+\lambda_0'$. Then $Y>0$, and since $J\le 1/\max\{a,b\}$ we have that $Y\le\lambda_0(J+1)\le\lambda_0\bigl(1/\max\{a,b\}+1\bigr)$.

\noindent\emph{Lattice points below the top of the ladder:} We first show that every $(\tilde n,\bm{k})\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^2$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.Forcing}
0<\tilde n+\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}\le\frac{Y}{MN},\qquad |k_1|<M,\qquad |k_2|<N,
\end{equation}
is an integer combination of $(n,m_1,m_2)$ and $(n',m_1',m_2')$. Let $\tilde\beta=\tilde n+\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}$ and let $D$ be the determinant of the integer matrix with rows $(n,m_1,m_2)$, $(n',m_1',m_2')$ and $(\tilde n,k_1,k_2)$. Adding $\alpha_1$ times the second column and $\alpha_2$ times the third column to the first replaces the first column by $(\beta,\beta',\tilde\beta)^t$ without changing $D$, and expanding along the last row then gives
\[
D=\tilde\beta\,(m_1m_2'-m_2m_1')-k_1(\beta m_2'-m_2\beta')+k_2(\beta m_1'-m_1\beta').
\]
Here
\[m_1m_2'-m_2m_1'=|m_2|M-|m_1|N=MN(b-a),\]
while
\[|\beta m_2'-m_2\beta'|\le\beta N+|m_2||\beta'|\quad \text{and} \quad|\beta m_1'-m_1\beta'|\le\beta M+|m_1||\beta'|.\]
Using \eqref{eqn.Forcing} and $|\lambda_0'|<\lambda_0$ we have that
\[
|D|< Y|b-a|+2\lambda_0+(a+b)|\lambda_0'|\le Y|b-a|+\lambda_0(2+a+b).
\]
Now $|b-a|\le\max\{a,b\}\le 1/3$, so by our bound for $Y$ and by (iv),
\[
|D|<\lambda_0\bigl(1+\max\{a,b\}+2+a+b\bigr)\le 4\lambda_0<1.
\]
Since $D$ is an integer, $D=0$, and $(\tilde n,\bm{k})$ lies in the $\mathbb{Q}$-span of the first two rows, which is two dimensional because by (v) the minors are not all zero. By the theory of the Smith normal form, the greatest common divisor of the $2\times 2$ minors of an integer $2\times 3$ matrix of rank two is the product of the elementary divisors of the subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}^3$ generated by its rows, so (v) says precisely that this subgroup is the intersection of its $\mathbb{Q}$-span with $\mathbb{Z}^3$. This proves the claim.

We also note for later use that
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.YSmall}
\frac{Y}{MN}=J\beta+\beta'<(C_1+1)\beta\le 2C_1\beta=\frac{2M\beta}{|m_1|}\le 2M\beta=\frac{2\lambda_0}{N}<1 .
\end{equation}

\noindent\emph{The rungs:} Fix $j_0$ with $1\le j_0\le J$ and put $\bm{k}_0=(0,(j_0-1)|m_2|)$. Then $\bm{k}_0\in W_{(M,N)}$, because $(j_0-1)|m_2|\le(C_2-1)|m_2|=N-|m_2|<N$. We will show that $s(\bm{k}_0)=j_0\beta+\beta'$. Since the $J$ numbers $j_0\beta+\beta'$ are distinct, this will prove the proposition.

For $j\ge 1$ the vector $\bm{k}=j\bm{m}+\bm{m}'$ has $k_1=M-j|m_1|$ and $k_2=N-j|m_2|$, so $\bm{k}\in W_{(M,N)}-\bm{k}_0$ if and only if $0\le M-j|m_1|<M$ and $0\le N-(j-j_0+1)|m_2|<N$, which is to say that
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.RungRange}
1\le j\le C_1\qquad\text{and}\qquad j_0\le j\le C_2+j_0-1 .
\end{equation}
Both conditions hold for $j=j_0$, and for this $j$ we have that $\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}\equiv j_0\beta+\beta'\bmod 1$, with $0<\beta+\beta'\le j_0\beta+\beta'\le Y/MN<1$ by (iii) and \eqref{eqn.YSmall}. Hence $\langle\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}\rangle=j_0\beta+\beta'$, and $s(\bm{k}_0)\le j_0\beta+\beta'$.

On the other hand, suppose that $\bm{k}\in W_{(M,N)}-\bm{k}_0$ satisfies $\langle\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}\rangle\le j_0\beta+\beta'$, and let $\tilde n\in\mathbb{Z}$ be such that $\tilde n+\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}=\langle\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}\rangle$. Then $0\le k_1<M$ and $|k_2|<N$. By our choice of $\tilde n$ and the inequality $j_0\beta+\beta'\le Y/MN$ from the previous paragraph, we have that \[0<\tilde n+\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}\le Y/MN.\]
By the first part of the proof, there are $a_1,a_2\in\mathbb{Z}$ with $(\tilde n,\bm{k})=a_1(n,m_1,m_2)+a_2(n',m_1',m_2')$, and therefore
\[
k_1=a_2M-a_1|m_1|,\qquad \langle\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}\rangle=a_1\beta+a_2\beta' .
\]
The condition $0\le k_1<M$ says that $a_1/C_1\le a_2<a_1/C_1+1$, so that $a_2=\lceil a_1/C_1\rceil$. If $a_1\le 0$ then $a_1/C_1\le a_2\le 0$, so $|a_2|\le|a_1|/C_1$ and
\[
a_1\beta+a_2\beta'\le-|a_1|\beta+|a_1|\beta/C_1\le 0,
\]
which contradicts $\langle\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}\rangle>0$. Therefore $a_1\ge 1$ and $a_2\ge 1$. If $a_2\ge 2$ then $a_1>(a_2-1)C_1\ge(a_2-1)J$, so that $a_1\ge(a_2-1)J+1\ge J+a_2-1$, and
\[
a_1\beta+a_2\beta'-\frac{Y}{MN}=(a_1-J)\beta+(a_2-1)\beta'\ge(a_2-1)(\beta+\beta')>0,
\]
which contradicts $\langle\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}\rangle\le Y/MN$. Therefore $a_2=1$ and $\bm{k}=a_1\bm{m}+\bm{m}'$ with $a_1\ge 1$, and \eqref{eqn.RungRange} gives $a_1\ge j_0$, so that $\langle\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}\rangle=a_1\beta+\beta'\ge j_0\beta+\beta'$. This shows that $s(\bm{k}_0)=j_0\beta+\beta'$, and completes the proof.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop.Window}
There are constants $Q_1,c_2,c_3,c_4>0$ with the following property. Let $\gamma$ be an element of the sequence of Lemma \ref{lem.Mult} with $Q\ge Q_1$, and let $k\ge c_2\log Q$ be an integer with $2\pi k\vartheta\in\mathcal{A}_\gamma$ modulo $2\pi$. \min\{C_1,C_2\}\ge\frac{Q}{c_3}\qquad\text{and}\qquad \max\{M,N\}\le c_4\,Q\,u^{-k/2}.
\]
In particular, with $\bm{\tau}=(M,N)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.WindowOut}
G(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\tau})\ge\frac{Q}{c_3}-1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\log|\bm{\tau}|\le\frac{k}{2}\log(1/u)+\log(c_4Q).
\end{equation}

By Lemma \ref{lem.Mult}(iii) and \eqref{eqn.MuBd}, both $v_0$ and $|\mu|$ tend to $0$ as $Q\rightarrow\infty$ along the sequence, so we may choose $Q_1$ so large that $Q^2\ge 2\delta_0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.SmallAngles}
2v_0+|\mu|\le\eta_0/2
\end{equation}

F_1'&:=-2P_1Q\cos(\varphi+\mu+\chi_1)=2P_1Q\sin v,\\

\begin{equation}\label{eqn.MainTermSizes}
|F_1|\ge\frac{2P_1\delta_0}{Q^2},\qquad F_1'\ge\frac{2P_1\delta_0}{Q},\qquad |F_2|\ge P_2\eta_0,\qquad F_2'\ge P_2Q\eta_0 .

\[
C_1=\frac{m_1'}{|m_1|}\ge\frac{9}{11}\cdot\frac{F_1'}{|F_1|}=\frac{9}{11}\cdot\frac{Q\sin v}{\sin s},\qquad
C_2=\frac{m_2'}{|m_2|}\ge\frac{9}{11}\cdot\frac{F_2'}{|F_2|}\ge\frac{9}{11}\cdot\frac{Q\eta_0/2}{3\eta_0/2}=\frac{3Q}{11}.
\]
Since $\sin s=\sin(v+|\mu|)\le\sin v+|\mu|$ and $\sin v\ge\sin v_0$,
\[
\frac{\sin v}{\sin s}\ge\frac{1}{1+|\mu|/\sin v}\ge\frac{1}{1+|\mu|/\sin v_0},
\]
and by \eqref{eqn.MuBd} and Lemma \ref{lem.Mult}(iii), $|\mu|/\sin v_0\le\frac{\pi c_1}{2Q^2}\cdot\frac{Q^2}{\delta_0}=64\pi P_1P_2c_1$. Therefore $\min\{C_1,C_2\}\ge Q/c_3$ with $c_3=\max\{\frac{11}{9}(1+64\pi P_1P_2c_1),\frac{11}{3}\}$, and (ii) holds provided that $Q_1\ge 3c_3$. For (iv), we have that
\begin{align*}
MN\beta=(t^{1/2}m_1')(t^{1/2}m_2')&\le\Bigl(\frac{11}{10}\Bigr)^2F_1'F_2'
\le\frac{121}{100}\cdot4P_1P_2Q^2\cdot2\sin v_0\cdot\frac{3\eta_0}{2}\\
&=\frac{121}{100}\cdot 12P_1P_2\eta_0\delta_0\le\frac{121\cdot 12}{100\cdot 128}<\frac14 .
\end{align*}

\noindent\emph{The size of $\bm{\tau}$:} Finally,
\begin{align*}
N=m_2'&\le\frac{11}{10}F_2'\,t^{-1/2}\le\frac{11}{10}\cdot 2P_2Q\cdot\frac{3\eta_0}{2}\,u^{-k/2}\le 4P_2\eta_0\,Q\,u^{-k/2},\\
M=m_1'&\le\frac{11}{10}F_1'\,t^{-1/2}\le\frac{11}{10}\cdot 2P_1Q\cdot\frac{2\delta_0}{Q^2}\,u^{-k/2}\le 5P_1\delta_0\,u^{-k/2},
\end{align*}
so we may take $c_4=\max\{4P_2\eta_0,5P_1\delta_0\}$. The inequalities \eqref{eqn.WindowOut} now follow from Proposition \ref{prop.Ladder}.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem.Hitting}
Let $\vartheta$ be irrational and let $\Phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow(0,1]$ be non-increasing with $\|q\vartheta\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\ge\Phi(q)$ for every $q\in\mathbb{N}$. Let $I\subseteq\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ be an arc of length $\ell\in(0,1)$, let $k_0\ge 0$ be an integer, and put $R=\lceil 1/\ell\rceil$. Then there is an integer $k$ with
\[
k_0<k\le k_0+R\bigl(\Phi(R)^{-1}+1\bigr)\qquad\text{and}\qquad k\vartheta\bmod 1\in I .
\]
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Replacing $I$ by $I-k_0\vartheta$, we may assume that $k_0=0$. By Dirichlet's theorem there is an integer $q$ with $1\le q\le R$ and $\|q\vartheta\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}<1/R\le\ell$. Put $\delta=\|q\vartheta\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}>0$, and let $\varepsilon=q\vartheta-p$, where $p$ is the nearest integer to $q\vartheta$, so that $|\varepsilon|=\delta$ and $jq\vartheta\equiv j\varepsilon\bmod 1$ for every $j$. Suppose, with little loss of generality, that $\varepsilon>0$. Write $I=[a,a+\ell)\bmod 1$ with $a\in[0,1)$, and let $j\ge 1$ be the least integer with $j\varepsilon>a$. Then $(j-1)\varepsilon\le a$, so $a<j\varepsilon\le a+\delta<a+\ell$, and $j\varepsilon\bmod 1\in I$. Moreover $j\le a/\varepsilon+1<1/\delta+1$, so $k=jq$ satisfies $k\le R(\Phi(R)^{-1}+1)$, because $\delta\ge\Phi(q)\ge\Phi(R)$.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- theorem--2: body_md -->
Let $\xi$ be an algebraic number with $|\xi|=1$ which is not a root of unity. Then there are effectively computable constants $c_\xi>0$ and $C_\xi\ge 1$, depending only on $\xi$, such that $|\xi^q-1|\ge c_\xi\,q^{-C_\xi}$ for every $q\in\mathbb{N}$.
<!-- end theorem--2 -->
<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem.Baker}
There are effectively computable constants $c_5>0$ and $C\ge 1$ such that
\[
\|q\vartheta\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\ge c_5\,q^{-C}\qquad\text{for every }q\in\mathbb{N} .
\]
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Put $\xi=\sigma(u)/\overline{\sigma(u)}$. This is an algebraic number, being a quotient of two conjugates of $u$, it has $|\xi|=1$, and $\xi=e^{2i\arg\sigma(u)}=e^{4\pi i\vartheta}$. It is not a root of unity, since $\xi^n=1$ would force $2n\vartheta\in\mathbb{Z}$, contradicting Lemma \ref{lem.Unit}. By the quoted theorem,
\[
c_\xi q^{-C_\xi}\le|\xi^q-1|=2|\sin(2\pi q\vartheta)|\le 2\pi\|2q\vartheta\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\le 4\pi\|q\vartheta\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}},
\]
which gives the lemma with $c_5=c_\xi/4\pi$ and $C=C_\xi$.
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- head-3: body_md -->
Let $\gamma$ be an element of the sequence in Lemma \ref{lem.Mult} with $Q\ge Q_1$, let $\mathcal{A}_\gamma$ be the arc \eqref{eqn.ArcDef}, and let $I\subseteq\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ be the image of $\mathcal{A}_\gamma$ under division by $2\pi$, an arc of length
\[
\ell=\frac{v_0}{2\pi}\ge\frac{\sin v_0}{2\pi}=\frac{\delta_0}{2\pi Q^2}.
\]
Apply Lemma \ref{lem.Hitting} with $k_0=\lceil c_2\log Q\rceil$, with $\Phi(q)=c_5q^{-C}$ from Lemma \ref{lem.Baker} (decreasing $c_5$ if necessary so that $\Phi\le 1$), and with $R=\lceil 1/\ell\rceil\le 2\pi Q^2/\delta_0+1$. This produces an integer $k\ge c_2\log Q$ with $2\pi k\vartheta\in\mathcal{A}_\gamma$ modulo $2\pi$ and
\[
k\le c_2\log Q+1+R\bigl(c_5^{-1}R^{C}+1\bigr)\le c_6\,Q^{2(C+1)},
\]
for a constant $c_6$ depending only on $\bm{\alpha}$ and $u$. By Proposition \ref{prop.Window} there is $\bm{\tau}=(M,N)\in\mathbb{N}^2$ with
\[
G(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\tau})\ge\frac{Q}{c_3}-1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\log|\bm{\tau}|\le\frac{k}{2}\log(1/u)+\log(c_4Q)\le c_7\,Q^{2(C+1)}.
\]
The second inequality gives $Q\ge(\log|\bm{\tau}|/c_7)^{1/(2(C+1))}$, and substituting this into the first gives
\[
G(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\tau})\ge c\,(\log|\bm{\tau}|)^{\kappa},\qquad\kappa=\frac{1}{2(C+1)},
\]
for a suitable $c>0$, provided that $Q$ is sufficiently large. Letting $\gamma$ run through the sequence of Lemma \ref{lem.Mult}, we have $Q\rightarrow\infty$, so $G(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\tau})\rightarrow\infty$. This completes the proof of Theorem \ref{thm.Complex}.

We close with a remark. Baker's theorem is used only for the rate, and it is possible to circumvent it, at the cost of a more slowly growing lower bound for the gaps function. Both $\sigma(u)$ and $\overline{\sigma(u)}$ are algebraic units, so the number $\xi$ in the proof of Lemma \ref{lem.Baker} is a unit in the normal closure of $K$, a field of degree at most $6$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. For $q\in\mathbb{N}$ the number $\xi^q-1$ is a non-zero algebraic integer whose conjugates are bounded in absolute value by $H^q+1$, where $H>1$ is the largest absolute value of a conjugate of $\xi$. Taking norms gives $|\xi^q-1|\ge(H^q+1)^{-5}$, and hence $\|q\vartheta\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\gg H^{-5q}$ in place of Lemma \ref{lem.Baker}. The argument above then gives $G(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\tau})\gg(\log\log|\bm{\tau}|)^{1/2}$ for infinitely many $\bm{\tau}$.
