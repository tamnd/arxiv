<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:corridor}
The corridor is $K=S_X\cupS_Y\cupS_Z$ with
\begin{align*}
S_X &:= (-\infty,0]\times[0,1]\times[0,1] && \text{(entry arm)},\\
S_Y &:= [0,1]\times[0,5]\times[0,1] && \text{(middle arm)},\\
S_Z &:= [0,1]\times[5,6]\times[0,\infty) && \text{(exit arm)},
\end{align*}
the instantiation, with middle straight $M=4$, of the corridor family
$K_M$ of \cite{OurUB}; the turn from $-\hat{x}$ to $+\hat{y}$ occupies the
first corner cube $[0,1]^3$, the turn from $+\hat{y}$ to $+\hat{z}$ the second
corner cube $[0,1]\times[5,6]\times[0,1]$.  A \emph{body} is a compact
connected set $B\subset\mathbb{R}^3$ with $\operatorname{vol}(B)>0$; a \emph{motion} is a
continuous $g:[0,1]\to SE(3)$; and $(B,g)$ \emph{traverses} $K$ if
$g_t(B)\subseteqK$ for all $t$, $g_0(B)\subseteqS_X$, and
$g_1(B)\subseteqS_Z$.  Then
$V^{*}(K):=\sup\{\operatorname{vol}(B):(B,g)\text{ traverses }K\}$.
This matches \cite{OurUB} (weak traversal); the motions certified below in
fact end past the second corner cube, so they traverse in the strong sense
as well, and lower bounds proved for either reading agree.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:axisconv}
The intersection of $K$ with every line parallel to a coordinate axis is a
(possibly empty or unbounded) interval.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix the two frozen coordinates and read off the case.  Lines parallel to
$\hat{x}$: for $(y,z)\in[0,1]^2$ the trace is $(-\infty,0]\cup[0,1]=
(-\infty,1]$; for $y\in(1,5]$, $z\in[0,1]$ it is $[0,1]$; for $y\in[5,6]$,
$z\ge0$ it is $[0,1]$; otherwise empty.  Lines parallel to $\hat{y}$: for
$x<0$, $z\in[0,1]$ it is $[0,1]$; for $x\in[0,1]$, $z\in[0,1]$ it is
$[0,5]\cup[5,6]=[0,6]$; for $x\in[0,1]$, $z>1$ it is $[5,6]$.  Lines
parallel to $\hat{z}$: for $x<0$, $y\in[0,1]$ it is $[0,1]$; for $x\in[0,1]$,
$y\in[0,5)$ it is $[0,1]$; for $x\in[0,1]$, $y\in[5,6]$ it is
$[0,1]\cup[0,\infty)=[0,\infty)$.  All are intervals.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:corners}
An axis-aligned box with all $8$ corners in $K$ is contained in $K$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let the box be $[x_0,x_1]\times[y_0,y_1]\times[z_0,z_1]$.  If $x_0<0$: the
corners at $x=x_0$ lie in $S_X$ (the only piece reaching $x<0$), forcing
$y_1\le1$, $z_1\le1$; a corner at $x=x_1$ then lies in $K$ with
$y,z\in[0,1]$, forcing $x_1\le1$; hence the box lies in
$(-\infty,1]\times[0,1]\times[0,1]\subseteqS_X\cupS_Y$.  If $x_0\ge0$: all
corners lie in $S_Y\cupS_Z$, so $x_1\le1$.  If moreover $z_1\le1$, every
point of the box has $x,z\in[0,1]$ and $y\in[0,6]$, hence lies in $S_Y$
(if $y\le5$) or $S_Z$ (if $y\ge5$).  If instead $z_1>1$, the corners at
$z=z_1$ force $y_0,y_1\in[5,6]$, and the box lies in
$[0,1]\times[5,6]\times[0,\infty)=S_Z$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:points}
Every lattice point that the pipeline reports as surviving has its entire
continuous trajectory, under the staircase motion of
\S\ref{sec:semantics}(3), inside $K$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
A translation substep moves the point along a segment parallel to a
coordinate axis; by Lemma~\ref{lem:axisconv} the segment lies in $K$ iff
its endpoints do, which is what the pipeline checks.  A rotation substep
moves the point along a circular arc contained in its axis-aligned bounding
box; the pipeline verifies the box's $8$ corners, so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:corners} the box --- hence the arc --- lies in $K$.
Concatenating substeps covers the whole motion.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:caveat}
The pipeline's points-to-cubes step (a $4\times4\times4$-sampled cube
counted as inside the corridor) is asserted, not proven, in the source
notebook --- near $K$'s reflex edges a rotated cube admits
$\Theta(h)$-deep excursions between in-corridor sample points.
Point-survival soundness is Proposition~\ref{prop:points}.  This affects
the \emph{absolute} rigor of all published pipeline numbers equally (the
original $1.81$ of \cite{GGTW25} included), so comparative claims --- the
entire ladder of Table~\ref{tab:ladder} --- are untouched; and nothing in
the upper bounds of \cite{OurUB} rests on the rule.  The step is
upgradeable: proving it (the tolerances may absorb the excursions) or
quantifying an $\varepsilon$-correction would upgrade every lower bound's
rigor at once.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Run unmodified at tolerances $(0.005,\,0.05^\circ)$, the certification
pipeline of \cite{GGTW25} assigns to the five explicit pose paths of this
note the certified volumes of Table~\ref{tab:ladder}; in particular, to the
path of Section~\ref{sec:polish} the values $1.8355162$ at $h=0.002$ and
$1.8558404$ at $h=0.001$.  Each certified value $C$ asserts: there is a
compact union $B$ of exactly $C/h^3$ grid cubes with $\operatorname{vol}(B)=C$, every
lattice sample point of which remains inside $K$ throughout an explicit
continuous rigid motion carrying $B$ from the entry arm to the exit arm
(unconditionally, by Proposition~\ref{prop:points}); that $B$ itself
remains inside $K$ is the pipeline's counting rule
(Remark~\ref{rem:caveat}).  Granting that rule (with the connectedness
evidence recorded in \S\ref{sec:soundness}),
\[
V^{*}(K_4)\;\ge\;1.8558404 .
\]

\label{rem:hazard}
One genuine hazard of the pipeline deserves disclosure.  Its swept-arc
routine normalizes the rotation axis by the rotation angle, so a substep
with \emph{exactly zero} rotation produces $0/0=\mathrm{NaN}$ bounding
boxes, and the certificate silently collapses to $0.000000$; any synthetic
path containing exact pure-translation steps triggers this.  The backbone
path of Section~\ref{sec:construction} contains three such steps (its
entry, middle-straight and exit slides); its certificates carry a
disclosed angular dither on those three steps, of at most $0.0434^\circ$
and recorded pose-by-pose in the run logs.  The dither angles lie below the
$0.05^\circ$ smoothing tolerance, and in an independent re-implementation
of the motion semantics the dithered and undithered paths carve
\emph{identical} voxel sets, so the disclosed certificates are unaffected.
The three refined paths (runs I--III) contain no zero-rotation steps ---
their smallest rotation steps are $0.0031^\circ$, $0.0019^\circ$ and
$0.0013^\circ$ respectively, two orders above single-precision
degeneracy --- and need no dither; every
certification run records an explicit scan for degenerate rotation
substeps.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:fp32}
The search evaluator runs in single precision on a GPU, and one of its
volumes is reproducible only as a function of the \emph{evaluator
instance}, not of the path alone: the same path scores $1.88134775$ versus
$1.88134813$ at two internal memory-chunking settings --- exactly $3$
boundary voxels out of $\sim15\times10^6$ --- because the voxel-transform
product $[M,3]\times[3,P]$ rounds differently at different chunk widths
$P$.  The volume \emph{is} bit-reproducible at fixed
(grid, element budget, chunk cap), independently of process identity:
across a mid-run process restart, and across independent re-measurement
days later on a fresh instance with matched settings, values reproduced to
$0.0$.  We therefore adopt the rule that any two paths are compared only on
one evaluator instance, and every success gate below was \emph{self-anchored}:
the quantity to beat was re-measured on the instance that judged the
challenger.  (The pipeline's own certificates are unaffected; this remark
concerns the search-side evaluator.)

For a path $P$ and a \emph{basis grid} $h$, let $S_P(h)$ be the
pose-sampled carve, at grid $h$, of the pipeline's own motion semantics:
the path smoothed at the certification tolerances $(0.005,\,0.05^\circ)$
and decomposed into the axis staircase ($\approx29{,}000$ substep poses),
evaluated locally on CPU in $\approx1$--$2$ hours.  $S_P(h)$ sees the
certified motion but not the swept-arc erosion or the counting window, so
it overestimates; the missing deduction is measured on an \emph{anchor}
path $A$ with a known certificate $C_{g}(A)$ and transferred:
\[
\mathrm{pred}_g^{(h)}(P)\;=\;S_P(h)\;-\;\bigl[S_A(h)-C_{g}(A)\bigr].
\]
In its first, single-basis form ($h=10^{-2}$, anchored on the published
$1.81$ of the control path) the predictor forecast the backbone's first
certificate as $1.825\pm0.010$; the pipeline returned $1.8279$.
