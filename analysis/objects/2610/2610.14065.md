<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:integrability}
The geodesic flow is analytically Liouville integrable on $S$ if there
are globally defined, single-valued real-analytic functions
\[
 F_1=E,F_2,\ldots,F_n:W\longrightarrow\mathbb R
\]
on one open neighborhood $W$ of the entire $S$, such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(A\arabic*)]
\item $\{F_i,F_j\}=0$ on $W$ for all $i,j$;
\item the ambient differentials $dF_1,\ldots,dF_n$ are linearly
independent on a dense subset of $S$.
\end{enumerate}
Independence is tested in $T_z^*(T^*M)$, rather than after restricting
the covectors to $T_zS$, on which $dE$ vanishes.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:entropy}
Let $(M,g)$ satisfy Definition~\ref{def:integrability}. Then the
topological entropy of its unit-speed geodesic flow is zero:
\[
 h_{\mathrm{top}}(g)=0.
\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:count}
Under Definition~\ref{def:integrability}, there are a constant $C<\infty$
and a Borel set $\mathcal G\subset M\times M$ of full product Riemannian measure
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:countbound}
 N_{q,y}(T)\leq C(1+T)^n
 \qquad ((q,y)\in\mathcal G,\ T\geq1).
\end{equation}
Both $C$ and $\mathcal G$ are independent of $T$, and $C$ is independent of
the endpoints.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:elliptic}
Under Definition~\ref{def:integrability}, if $M$ is simply connected,
then, for every integer $N\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:betabound}
 \beta_M(N):=\sum_{j=0}^N\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_j(\Omega M;\mathbb Q)
 \leq C'(1+N)^n.
\end{equation}
Here $\Omega M$ is the based-loop space. Consequently $M$ is rationally
elliptic, meaning that $\sum_{j\geq2}\dim_{\mathbb Q}(\pi_j(M)\otimes\mathbb Q)<\infty$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:flow}
Let $V$ be an analytic vector field on a compact analytic manifold $P$
without boundary, with flow $\psi_t$. For each fixed $R>0$, the map
\[
 [-R,R]\times P\longrightarrow P,\qquad (t,z)\longmapsto\psi_t(z)
\]
is globally subanalytic after an analytic Euclidean embedding of $P$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Compactness makes $V$ complete. Analytic dependence for ordinary
differential equations makes the flow map analytic on $\mathbb R\times P$.
Its restriction to $[-R,R]\times P$ has a compact subanalytic graph,
which proves the assertion.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:uniform}
Let $\mathcal P\subset\mathbb R^d$ and
$\mathcal Z\subset\mathcal P\times\mathbb R^a$ be globally subanalytic.
For $p\in\mathcal P$, write
$\mathcal Z_p=\{x:(p,x)\in\mathcal Z\}$.
There is one integer $K<\infty$ such that every $\mathcal Z_p$ has
at most $K$ connected components. In particular, every finite
$\mathcal Z_p$ has at most $K$ points.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Apply Hardt's theorem to the parameter projection
$\mathcal Z\to\mathcal P$. Over each of finitely many parameter
pieces all fibers are homeomorphic to one model. Each model has
finitely many connected components by the finite decomposition
property. Take the maximum of these finitely many numbers; empty
fibers contribute zero. The components of a finite set are its points.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:integration}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Let $X$ be globally subanalytic and let
$a:X\times\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be globally subanalytic. If
$a(x,\cdot)$ is absolutely Lebesgue integrable for every $x\in X$,
then
\[
 I(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}a(x,r)\,\mathrm d r
\]
is constructible on $X$.
\item Let $D\subset\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R^a$ be globally subanalytic,
with variables $(p,x)$. Suppose $\mathcal Z\subset D$ is described
by finitely many conditions $C(p,x)=0$ or $C(p,x)>0$, where each
$C$ is constructible on $D$, with finite unions, intersections, or
complements relative to $D$ allowed. There is one integer $K<\infty$ such that
every solution set $\mathcal Z_p$ has at most $K$ connected
components. Every finite solution set therefore has at most $K$ points.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Part (i) is the required special case of Cluckers--Miller's integration
theorem \cite[Theorem 1.3]{CM}. It also follows from the volume theorem
of Comte--Lion--Rolin \cite{CLR}: the positive and negative parts
$a^\pm$ give globally subanalytic subgraph families
\[
 \{(r,t):0<t<a^\pm(x,r)\}.
\]
Their finite areas are constructible functions of $x$. Their difference
is the stated integral by Tonelli's theorem.

Part (ii) uses the o-minimality theorem for restricted real-analytic
functions and exponentiation \cite[Corollary 5.13]{DMM}, together
with cylindrical cell decomposition \cite{vdD}. Here restricted analytic
functions are analytic functions used on compact boxes to which they
extend analytically; logarithms are included as inverses of the
exponential. The consequence needed here is that the combined solution
set has a finite cylindrical cell decomposition with the parameter coordinates
first. At fixed $p$, each cell contributes either nothing or one
connected cell. The total number of cells therefore bounds the number
of connected components of every solution set. If the solution set
is finite, each nonempty cell fiber is a point. Every function in
\eqref{eq:constructible} belongs to the analytic and logarithmic
setting of that theorem, which verifies its applicability.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:families}
There are finitely many globally subanalytic homeomorphisms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:families}
 \tau_\ell:B_\ell\times\mathbb T^n\longrightarrow U_\ell,
 \qquad f(\tau_\ell(b,\theta))=b,\qquad 1\leq\ell\leq L,
\end{equation}
where each $B_\ell\subset\mathbb R^{n-1}$ is a cell of dimension $n-1$
consisting of regular values with nonempty fibers. The sets $U_\ell$
are disjoint open subsets of $S$, made up of whole connected fiber
components, and invariant under every $X_i$. Their complement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma}
 \Sigma=S\setminus\bigcup_{\ell=1}^L U_\ell
 \quad\text{is closed and satisfies}\quad \dim\Sigma\leq2n-2.
\end{equation}
The constants later obtained on these products apply on all of
$B_\ell$, without restricting to compact subsets.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
\emph{Control the fibers over critical values.}
Let $D=\operatorname{Crit}(f)$ be the critical locus and $\Delta=f(D)$ the set of
critical values. The rank condition is analytic, and the regular locus
is dense, so $D$ is a proper analytic set of dimension at most $2n-2$.
Both $D$ and $\Delta$ are compact and globally subanalytic. By Sard's
theorem $\Delta$ has measure zero. Its subanalytic stratification
therefore has no $(n-1)$-dimensional piece, so $\dim\Delta\leq n-2$.
On $S\setminus D$, the map $f$ is a submersion with $n$-dimensional
fibers. The fiber dimension estimate \eqref{eq:dimension} gives
\[
 \dim\bigl(f^{-1}(\Delta)\setminus D\bigr)
 \leq n+\dim\Delta\leq2n-2.
\]
Together with the bound for $D$, this yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:badfiberdim}
 \dim f^{-1}(\Delta)\leq2n-2.
\end{equation}
This step removes entire fibers over critical values, not only their
critical points; a regular point can lie over a critical value.

\emph{Identify each regular connected component as a torus.}
For a regular value $b$, a connected component $L_b$ of $f^{-1}(b)$
is a compact $n$-manifold. The fields $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ are tangent
to it and form a basis at every point. Their commuting complete flows
define an $\mathbb R^n$-action. Every orbit is open, so connectedness makes
the action transitive. The stabilizer $\Lambda_b$ of a point is closed
and discrete, the latter because the fields are independent. The orbit map induces a
bijective local analytic diffeomorphism $\mathbb R^n/\Lambda_b\to L_b$,
hence an analytic diffeomorphism. Its quotient is therefore compact,
which forces $\Lambda_b$ to be a lattice of rank $n$. This identifies
$L_b$ analytically with an $n$-torus.

\emph{Use Hardt's theorem to choose subanalytic product families.}
Apply \eqref{eq:hardt} to $f$ over $f(S)\setminus\Delta$, refining
its finite partition into cells. On one cell $B$, write its product
homeomorphism as $H:B\times K\to f^{-1}(B)$ and set
$H_b(x)=H(b,x)$. Fix $b_0\in B$. For each connected component
$K_0$ of $K$, the manifold $L_0=H_{b_0}(K_0)$ is a regular torus.
Choose a basis of its period lattice and the resulting analytic
identification $j_0:\mathbb T^n\to L_0$. Its graph is compact and globally
subanalytic. Then
\[
 \tau(b,\theta)=H_b\bigl(H_{b_0}^{-1}(j_0(\theta))\bigr)
\]
is a globally subanalytic homeomorphism from $B\times\mathbb T^n$ onto
the corresponding family of connected components. There are finitely
many components and cells, so this gives finitely many products.
The analytic identification is chosen on one fixed fiber only; Hardt's
homeomorphism transports it topologically to the others.

\emph{Retain full-dimensional cells and estimate what was omitted.}
Keep the cells of dimension $n-1$. Each omitted regular-value cell
has dimension at most $n-2$, so its inverse image has dimension at
most $n+(n-2)=2n-2$. Along with \eqref{eq:badfiberdim}, this proves
the dimension estimate in \eqref{eq:sigma}.
A full-dimensional cell is open in $\mathbb R^{n-1}$. Each component of
the compact reference fiber is open and closed in that fiber because
there are only finitely many components. The product homeomorphism
therefore makes each retained $U_\ell$ open in $f^{-1}(B_\ell)$,
which is itself open in $S$.
Thus $\Sigma$ is closed. Every $X_i$ preserves
$f$, and its continuous orbit stays in the same connected component
of a fiber. Hence all the sets $U_\ell$ and $\Sigma$ are invariant.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:goodpairs}
There is a Borel full-measure set $\mathcal G\subset M\times M$, independent
of any length bound, such that for every $(q,y)\in\mathcal G$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item all geodesics from $q$ to $y$ are nonconjugate at their endpoints;
\item $N_{q,y}(T)<\infty$ for every finite $T$;
\item every connecting geodesic, after unit-speed normalization,
starts in one of the $U_\ell$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For each integer $m\geq1$, consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exceptionalmap}
 \Psi_m:\Sigma\times[0,m]\longrightarrow M\times M,
 \qquad \Psi_m(z,s)=(\pi z,\pi\varphi_s(z)).
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:flow} makes this map globally subanalytic for this
fixed $m$. Its domain has dimension at most $2n-1$, so its image
has measure zero in $M\times M$. Since $\Sigma$ is compact, the
image is compact. Consequently
\[
 \mathcal B=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\Psi_m(\Sigma\times[0,m])
\]
is Borel and null. Outside $\mathcal B$, no finite positive-time trajectory
from $\Sigma$ connects the two endpoints. This is the first use of
the fixed-time flow lemma; a countable union suffices here.

Next consider the global exponential endpoint map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Exp}
 \operatorname{Exp}:TM\longrightarrow M\times M,\qquad
 \operatorname{Exp}(q,\xi)=(q,\exp_q\xi).
\end{equation}
In local bundle coordinates its derivative has block form
\[
 d\operatorname{Exp}_{(q,\xi)}=
 \begin{pmatrix}I&0\\ *&D_\xi\exp_q\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus $(q,\xi)$ is regular precisely when the vertical derivative
$D_\xi\exp_q$ is invertible. For $\xi\neq0$, this is equivalent
to the two endpoints being nonconjugate along the corresponding
geodesic \cite[Theorem 18.1]{Milnor}; it does not exclude conjugate
points in the interior of the segment. At $\xi=0$ the derivative
is the identity.
The critical values form a null set $\mathcal C$ by Sard's theorem. They
are Borel: the critical locus is closed, and its intersections with
integer-radius compact disk bundles have compact images whose union
is $\mathcal C$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:goodset}
 \mathcal G=(M\times M)\setminus
 \bigl(\mathcal B\cup\mathcal C\cup\{(q,q):q\in M\}\bigr).
\end{equation}
This is the required Borel set of full measure.

For $(q,y)\in\mathcal G$, every point of $\operatorname{Exp}^{-1}(q,y)$ is regular.
The inverse function theorem makes this fiber discrete, since the
source and target of $\operatorname{Exp}$ have the same dimension. It is also
closed. Its intersection with a compact velocity disk is compact and
discrete, hence finite. Removing the diagonal excludes the zero
velocity; indeed every connecting length is at least $d_g(q,y)>0$.
The exclusion of $\mathcal B$ places every normalized initial covector in
one of the retained families.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:AC}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item A continuous globally subanalytic path
$\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb R^a$ is absolutely continuous and has finite length.
\item Let $p:\widetilde L\to L$ be a smooth covering that is a local
diffeomorphism. If $\gamma:[0,1]\to L$ is absolutely continuous,
every continuous lift $\widetilde\gamma$ is absolutely continuous and
\[
 dp_{\widetilde\gamma(r)}\widetilde\gamma'(r)=\gamma'(r)
 \quad\text{for almost every }r.
\]
Absolute continuity on manifolds is understood in smooth local coordinates.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For (i), the one-variable decomposition theorem for globally
subanalytic functions gives a finite partition on whose open intervals
every coordinate of $\gamma$ is $C^1$ and monotone. Let $h$ be one coordinate on a
partition interval $(a,b)$, with its continuous endpoint values.
The fundamental theorem of calculus on compact subintervals, followed
by passage to the endpoints, gives
\[
 \int_a^b|h'(r)|\,\mathrm d r=|h(b)-h(a)|<\infty,\qquad
 h(x)-h(a)=\int_a^x h'(r)\,\mathrm d r.
\]
The fixed sign of $h'$ justifies these limits. Thus $h$ is absolutely
continuous on $[a,b]$. Finite gluing proves absolute continuity of
all coordinates on $[0,1]$. The estimate
$|\gamma'|\leq\sum_j|\gamma_j'|$ also gives finite length.

For (ii), cover the compact lifted path by inverse-branch neighborhoods
for $p$. In local coordinates, choose smaller balls with closures in
larger balls on which each inverse branch is smooth. Its derivative
is bounded on a smaller closed ball, so the inverse branch is
Lipschitz there. Continuity and compactness give a finite subdivision
of the parameter interval such that each path piece lies in one of
these neighborhoods. On each piece the lift is a smooth Lipschitz
inverse branch composed with $\gamma$, and is therefore absolutely
continuous. The ordinary chain rule holds almost everywhere on each
piece. Finite gluing proves the claim. All constants are chosen for
this individual lifted path.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:Adefinable}
Every integral in \eqref{eq:Adefinition} converges absolutely.
Each component of $A=(A_1,\ldots,A_n):B\times Q\to\mathbb R^n$ is a
constructible function of the combined variables $(b,u)$, in the
sense of \eqref{eq:constructible}.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
\emph{Absolute integrability for every parameter.}
Fix $(b,u)$. The path $\gamma_{b,u}$ is continuous and globally
subanalytic, so Lemma~\ref{lem:AC}(i) makes it absolutely continuous
with finite length. Its derivative is tangent to $L_b$ almost
everywhere, as follows by differentiating the vanishing normal
coordinates in a submanifold chart. Since $L_b$ is compact and
$\alpha_i$ is smooth there,
some finite $C_{i,b}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L1}
 \int_0^1\left|\alpha_i|_{\gamma_{b,u}(r)}
                       (\gamma_{b,u}'(r))\right|\,\mathrm d r
 \leq C_{i,b}\int_0^1|\gamma_{b,u}'(r)|\,\mathrm d r<\infty.
\end{equation}
No common bound in $b$ or $u$ is required by
Theorem~\ref{thm:integration}(i).

\emph{A single globally subanalytic integrand.}
At points with $0<r<1$ where the partial derivative in $r$ exists, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integrand}
 \mathfrak a_i(b,u,r)=\sum_{j=1}^n
  (\Gamma(\gamma_{b,u}(r))^{-1})_{ij}
  \left\langle X_j(\gamma_{b,u}(r)),\partial_r\gamma_{b,u}(r)\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
Set $\mathfrak a_i=0$ at every other point, including outside $(0,1)$.
The closure property for partial derivatives from
Section~\ref{sec:ominimal} applies to this jointly globally subanalytic

\label{lem:developing}
For each $b\in B$, there is a homeomorphism
$h_b:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$, with $h_b(0)=0$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hA}
 h_b(u)=A(b,u)\qquad(u\in Q).
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pdefinition}
 P(b)=[\,A(b,e_1)\ \cdots\ A(b,e_n)\,]
\end{equation}

lattice, and

 h_b(u+k)=h_b(u)+P(b)k
 \qquad(u\in\mathbb R^n,\ k\in\mathbb Z^n).
\end{equation}

Write $\psi_i^t$ for the flow of $X_i$. Starting at $z_b$, the
commuting action gives an analytic covering
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Theta}
 \Theta_b:\mathbb R^n\longrightarrow L_b,\qquad
 \Theta_b(a)=\psi_1^{a_1}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_n^{a_n}(z_b).
\end{equation}
Its stabilizer $\Lambda_b=\Theta_b^{-1}(z_b)$ is the full lattice
from Proposition~\ref{prop:families}; its deck transformations are
translations $a\mapsto a+\lambda$, $\lambda\in\Lambda_b$.
The map $u\mapsto\tau(b,\rho(u))$ is another universal cover of
$L_b$, now with deck transformations $u\mapsto u+k$ for $k\in\mathbb Z^n$.
Uniqueness of universal covers gives a homeomorphism $h_b$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covers}
 \Theta_b\circ h_b=\tau_b\circ\rho,
 \qquad h_b(0)=0,\qquad \tau_b(\theta)=\tau(b,\theta).
\end{equation}
This construction is for a fixed $b$. The map $h_b$ on the unbounded
cover is used only in this covering-space argument. The functions
used later in the counting theorem are its values on the cube $Q$,
identified with the constructible functions $A(b,u)$.

Commutativity of the flows implies
$d\Theta_b|_a(e_j)=X_j(\Theta_b(a))$. The duality of the coframe
therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullback}
 \Theta_b^*\alpha_i=da_i,
\end{equation}
where $a_i$ denotes the $i$th flow-time coordinate of $a\in\mathbb R^n$.
For $u\in Q$, the path $r\mapsto h_b(ru)$ is the lift of
$\gamma_{b,u}$ starting at zero. Lemma~\ref{lem:AC}(ii) makes this
lift absolutely continuous. By \eqref{eq:pullback}, the almost-everywhere
chain rule yields
\[
 \frac{d}{dr}\bigl(h_b(ru)\bigr)_i
     =\alpha_i|_{\gamma_{b,u}(r)}(\gamma_{b,u}'(r)).
\]
Integrating proves \eqref{eq:hA}. This calculation also explains why
the coframe integral records flow time despite the limited regularity
of the angular trivialization.

For $k\in\mathbb Z^n$, equation~\eqref{eq:covers} gives
$h_b(u+k)-h_b(u)\in\Lambda_b$. This difference is continuous in
$u$ and the lattice is discrete, so it is a constant $\lambda_b(k)$.
Conjugation by $h_b$ identifies the two deck groups; conjugation by
$h_b^{-1}$ is its inverse. Consequently
$\lambda_b:\mathbb Z^n\to\Lambda_b$ is an isomorphism onto the entire
stabilizer. Setting $u=0$ and $k=e_j$ gives
\[
 \lambda_b(e_j)=h_b(e_j)=A(b,e_j).
\]
Thus the columns of $P(b)$ form a basis of the full lattice, rather
than merely a sublattice. In particular $P(b)$ is invertible, and
additivity proves \eqref{eq:equivariance}. Its entries are constructible
because they are obtained by evaluating $A$ at fixed vertices of $Q$. Finally,
$X_1=X_E$, so geodesic motion in \eqref{eq:Theta} adds $s e_1$ to
the flow-time vector.

\label{lem:circle}
Let $D$ be globally subanalytic and let
$c:D\times[0,1]\to S^1$ be globally subanalytic. Assume that
$r\mapsto c(p,r)$ is continuous for every $p\in D$. There is
$C_*<\infty$ such that every continuous lift $x:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ with
$c(p,r)=e^{2\pi i x(r)}$ satisfies
\[
 \sup_{r,r'\in[0,1]}|x(r)-x(r')|\leq C_*.
\]
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Choose the point $1\in S^1$ as a cut and define
\[
 W_p=\{r\in[0,1]:c(p,r)=1\}.
\]
The total set $\{(p,r):r\in W_p\}$ is globally subanalytic, being
the inverse image of the fixed circle point $1$ under $c$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform} therefore gives one integer $K$
bounding the number of connected components of every $W_p$.
For a lift $x$, we have $W_p=x^{-1}(\mathbb Z)$. On each connected
component of $W_p$, continuity forces $x$ to be constant at a single
integer.

to attain more than $K$ distinct integers on the interval between
$r$ and $r'$. Each such integer needs a different component of
$W_p$, a contradiction. Thus $C_*=K+2$ suffices.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:winding}
There is a constant $C_0$ such that every endpoint relation
\eqref{eq:endpoint}, in any retained chart, with $s\geq0$ and
$u,v\in Q$, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:winding}
 \|k\|_\infty\leq C_0(1+s),
 \qquad \|k\|_\infty=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|k_j|.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Fix a chart $U$. Define its $j$th angular projection by
\[
 \vartheta_j:U\longrightarrow S^1,\qquad
 \vartheta_j=\operatorname{pr}_j\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb T^n}\circ\tau^{-1}.
\]
Here $\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb T^n}$ discards the parameter $b$ from
$B\times\mathbb T^n$, and $\operatorname{pr}_j$ selects its $j$th circle factor.
For an initial covector $z\in U$, a duration $\sigma\in[0,1]$,
and a normalized path variable $r\in[0,1]$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circlefamily}
 c_j((z,\sigma),r)=\vartheta_j(\varphi_{\sigma r}(z)).
\end{equation}
These are well-defined because $U$ is invariant. Lemma~\ref{lem:flow}
with $R=1$ makes the short-time flow globally subanalytic.
The inverse $\tau^{-1}$ is globally subanalytic as well, so
\eqref{eq:circlefamily} has that property in all variables on
$D\times[0,1]$, where $D=U\times[0,1]$. For each parameter the path
is continuous. Lemma~\ref{lem:circle}, applied for $j=1,\ldots,n$,
gives a single $D_0$ bounding the displacement in every lifted angular
coordinate during any segment of duration at most one.

Now let $s>0$ and choose one continuous lift of the angular orbit
on $[0,s]$. Divide this interval into at most $\lceil s\rceil$ pieces
of duration at most one. Every new initial point remains in $U$, so
the same bound applies on each piece. By the triangle inequality,
\[
 \|\widetilde\theta(s)-\widetilde\theta(0)\|_\infty
 \leq D_0\lceil s\rceil.
\]
For the lift \eqref{eq:liftedorbit}, the endpoints are $u$ and $v+k$.
Since $\|u-v\|_\infty\leq1$, it follows that
\[
 \|k\|_\infty\leq D_0\lceil s\rceil+1
 \leq(D_0+1)(1+s).
\]
For $s=0$, the lift is constant and $u=v+k$, so
$\|k\|_\infty\leq1$. Taking the maximum of $D_0+1$ over the
finitely many charts proves \eqref{eq:winding}.

Corollary~\ref{cor:endpoint}, every solution of \eqref{eq:Rfamily}

 (b,u,v,s)\longmapsto\xi=s\,Z_\ell(b,u)^\sharp_g\in T_qM.
\end{equation}

For each fixed $T>0$, the extended nonnegative function

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:normalization}
Let $(M^n,g)$ be closed, connected, and real analytic, with $n\geq2$.
Each of the following hypotheses supplies integrals satisfying
Definition~\ref{def:integrability} for the same metric $g$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item There are single-valued real-analytic functions
$I_2,\ldots,I_n:S\to\mathbb R$, constant along the unit-speed geodesic
flow and with linearly independent differentials on a dense subset
of $S$. Their radial extensions
\[
 J_i(q,p)=I_i(q,p/|p|_g),\qquad p\neq0,
\]
satisfy $\{J_i,J_j\}|_S=0$.
\item On an open neighborhood $W$ of an entire surface
$E^{-1}(e_*)$, $e_*>0$, there are single-valued real-analytic
functions $F_1=E,F_2,\ldots,F_n$ that Poisson commute on $W$
and have independent ambient differentials on a dense subset of $W$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
\emph{Integrals given on the unit energy surface.}
In (i), each $J_i$ is analytic on $T^*M\setminus0_M$, where
$0_M$ denotes the zero section, and is homogeneous of degree zero
in the fibers. For $c>0$, put $\delta_c(q,p)=(q,cp)$. The identity
$\delta_c^*\omega=c\omega$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dilationbracket}
 \{F\circ\delta_c,G\circ\delta_c\}
       =c\{F,G\}\circ\delta_c.
\end{equation}
Since $J_i\circ\delta_c=J_i$ and $E\circ\delta_c=c^2E$,
\[
 \{J_i,J_j\}\circ\delta_c=c^{-1}\{J_i,J_j\},\qquad
 \{E,J_i\}\circ\delta_c=c\{E,J_i\}.
\]
The first bracket vanishes on $S$ by assumption. The second vanishes
there because $J_i|_S=I_i$ is a first integral. Every nonzero covector
is a positive dilation of a point of $S$, so both brackets vanish on
all of $T^*M\setminus0_M$. On $S$, independence of the $dI_i$
is equivalent to ambient independence of $dE,dJ_2,\ldots,dJ_n$,
because $dE\neq0$ and $\ker dE=TS$. Thus $E,J_2,\ldots,J_n$
satisfy Definition~\ref{def:integrability}.

Conversely, start with that definition and set $I_i=F_i|_S$.
Let $r(q,p)=(q,p/|p|_g)$ be the radial retraction onto $S$, and
let $\mathcal V=p\partial_p$ be the fiber radial vector field.
On $S$, where $dE(\mathcal V)=1$, differentiating the retraction gives
\[
 dr_z(w)=w-dE_z(w)\mathcal V_z,\qquad
 dJ_i=dF_i-dF_i(\mathcal V)dE.
\]
Pairing these covectors with the Poisson tensor shows
$\{J_i,J_j\}|_S=0$, because every resulting term contains either
$\{F_i,F_j\}$ or a bracket with $E$.
This verifies the equivalence with the unit-energy formulation (i).

\emph{Independence assumed in an ambient neighborhood.}
In (ii), compactness of $E^{-1}(e_*)$ gives numbers
$0<a<e_*<b$ such that $\{a<E<b\}\subset W$. Choose a point
$z_0$ of independence in this annulus and set $e_0=E(z_0)$.
The sphere bundle $S_{e_0}=E^{-1}(e_0)$ is connected: its base is
connected and its fiber is $S^{n-1}$. The ambient analytic section
\[
 \left.(dF_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dF_n)\right|_{S_{e_0}}
 \quad\text{of}\quad
 \left.\bigwedge\nolimits^n T^*(T^*M)\right|_{S_{e_0}}
\]
is nonzero at $z_0$. The identity principle for analytic sections on
a connected analytic manifold makes its nonvanishing locus dense
in $S_{e_0}$. Restriction here means restriction of an ambient
bundle section; pulling it back as a differential form on $S_{e_0}$
would give zero because $dE$ annihilates its tangent space.

Set $c=\sqrt{2e_0}$. Then $\delta_c(S)=S_{e_0}$, and on the open
neighborhood $\delta_c^{-1}(W)$ of $S$ the functions
\[
 \widetilde F_1=c^{-2}F_1\circ\delta_c=E,\qquad
 \widetilde F_i=F_i\circ\delta_c\quad(2\leq i\leq n)
\]
commute by \eqref{eq:dilationbracket}. Invertibility of $d\delta_c$
transfers dense ambient independence to $S$. These functions satisfy
Definition~\ref{def:integrability}; the metric and its unit-speed
geodesic flow have not been rescaled.

Choose one $(q,y)\in\mathcal G$. Lemma~\ref{lem:goodpairs} verifies
nonconjugacy for every connecting geodesic, as required in
\eqref{eq:gromov}. For an integer $N\geq1$, put
\[
 T_N=\max\left\{1,T_0,\frac{N+b+1}{a}\right\}.
\]
Then $\lfloor aT_N-b\rfloor\geq N$ and

Simple connectivity makes $\Omega M$ connected, so
$\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_0(\Omega M;\mathbb Q)=1$. Equations~\eqref{eq:gromov}
and \eqref{eq:countbound} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:betaproof}
 \beta_M(N)\leq1+N_{q,y}(T_N)
       \leq1+C(1+T_N)^n\leq C'(1+N)^n.
\end{equation}
Increasing $C'$ includes $N=0$.

To deduce rational ellipticity, note that a closed smooth manifold
has the homotopy type of a finite CW complex. Thus our simply connected
$M$ has finite rational type and finite rational
Lusternik--Schnirelmann category, as required by the homotopy growth
theorem. If its rational homotopy were infinite dimensional,
\cite[Theorem 2.1(ii)]{FHT82} would give $c>1$ and degrees
$q_i\to\infty$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyperbolic}
 \dim_{\mathbb Q}(\pi_{q_i}(M)\otimes\mathbb Q)\geq c^{q_i}.
\end{equation}
The Milnor--Moore identification, recalled in
\cite[p.~2521]{FHT17}, is
\[
 H_*(\Omega M;\mathbb Q)\cong U\bigl(\pi_*(\Omega M)\otimes\mathbb Q\bigr),
\]
where $U$ is the universal enveloping algebra of the graded homotopy
Lie algebra. The graded Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt theorem makes its
canonical map into $U$ injective. Hence, for $j\geq2$,
\[
 \dim_{\mathbb Q}(\pi_j(M)\otimes\mathbb Q)
 \leq\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_{j-1}(\Omega M;\mathbb Q)\leq\beta_M(j-1).
\]
This contradicts \eqref{eq:hyperbolic} and the polynomial bound
\eqref{eq:betaproof}. Thus the rational homotopy of $M$ is finite
dimensional.
