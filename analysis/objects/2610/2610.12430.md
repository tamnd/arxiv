<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:restricted-viscosity}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ be open, $\gamma\ge0$, and $\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^n$ be closed. A function $u\in C(\Omega)$ is a viscosity solution of
\begin{equation} \label{main-eqn}
 F(D^2u,Du,x)=f(x) \quad \text{in} \quad \Omega \quad \text{when} \quad Du \not\in (\overline{B}_\gamma \cup \Sigma)
\end{equation}
with some given $f\in C(\Omega)$ if the following conditions hold. If $\phi\in C^2(\Omega)$ touches $u$ from below at $x_0\in\Omega$ with $|D\phi(x_0)|>\gamma$, $D\phi(x_0)\notin\Sigma$, then
\[
 F(D^2\phi(x_0),D\phi(x_0),x_0)\le f(x_0).
\]
If $\phi$ touches $u$ from above at $x_0$ and the same gradient condition holds, then
\[
 F(D^2\phi(x_0),D\phi(x_0),x_0)\ge f(x_0).
\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:extremal-reduction} Let $F$ satisfy \eqref{F-structure}, $u \in C(\Omega)$ be a viscosity solution of \eqref{main-eqn} with $f \in C(\Omega) \cap L^\infty(\Omega)$. Assume that 
\[
 C_F=\|f\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}+\|F(0,0,\cdot)\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)} < \infty.
\]
Then, in the viscosity sense,
\[
 \mathcal M^-_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\le C_F, \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal M^+_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\ge-C_F \quad \text{in} \quad \Omega.
\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The proof is standard, see \cite{CaffarelliCabre} for example. However, as it is not long and for completeness, we provide the details. Let $\phi \in C^2$ touch $u$ from below at $x_0$ and assume $p=D\phi(x_0) \not \in (\overline{B}_\gamma \cup \Sigma)$. With $X=D^2\phi(x_0)$, Definition~\ref{def:restricted-viscosity} and \eqref{F-structure} give
\[
 \mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(X)-\mu_0|p|\le F(X,p,x_0)-F(0,0,x_0)\le f(x_0)-F(0,0,x_0)\le C_F.
\]
This proves the lower extremal inequality. If $\phi$ touches from above at an active gradient, then
\[
 \mathcal P^+_{\lambda,\Lambda}(X)+\mu_0|p|\ge F(X,p,x_0)-F(0,0,x_0)\ge f(x_0)-F(0,0,x_0)\ge-C_F,
\]
which proves the upper inequality.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A set \(\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^n\) is \emph{purely one-unrectifiable} if
\[
 \mathcal H^1(\Sigma\cap g(I))=0
\]
for every bounded interval \(I\subset\mathbb R\) and every Lipschitz map \(g:I\to\mathbb R^n\).
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:stability-sigma}
The following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(\mathcal H^1(\Sigma)=0\), then \(\Sigma\) is purely one-unrectifiable.
\item A countable union of purely one-unrectifiable sets is purely one-unrectifiable.
\item If \(\Sigma\) is purely one-unrectifiable, then \(a\Sigma+b\) is purely one-unrectifiable for every \(a\ne0\) and \(b\in\mathbb R^n\). In particular, \(-\Sigma\) is purely one-unrectifiable.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The first statement is immediate. For the second, if \(\Sigma=\bigcup_j\Sigma_j\) and \(g\) is Lipschitz, then
\[
 \mathcal H^1(\Sigma\cap g(I))\le\sum_j\mathcal H^1(\Sigma_j\cap g(I))=0.
\]
For the third, if \(S(x)=ax+b\), then \(S^{-1}\circ g\) is Lipschitz and
\[
 \mathcal H^1\bigl((a\Sigma+b)\cap g(I)\bigr)=|a|\mathcal H^1\bigl(\Sigma\cap S^{-1}(g(I))\bigr)=0.
\]
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{remark-oct-7}
It follows from Lemma \ref{lem:stability-sigma}-\textup{(i)} that if \(\dim_{\mathcal H}\Sigma<1\), then \(\mathcal H^1(\Sigma)=0\), hence \(\Sigma\) is purely one-unrectifiable. On the other hand, note also that the class of purely one-unrectifiable sets is strictly larger. Indeed, in \(\mathbb R^2\), let  \(\mathcal K\) be the four-corner Cantor set defined by
\begin{equation} \label{four-corner-Cantor}
 \mathcal K=C_{1/4}\times C_{1/4} \quad \text{where} \quad C_{1/4}=\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j4^{-j}:a_j\in\{0,3\}\Big\}.
\end{equation}
As indicated in \cite[Example 15.2 and the discussion following
Definition 15.3, pp.~203-204]{Mattila}, \(\mathcal H^1(\mathcal K) \in (0, \infty)\), and \(\mathcal K\) is purely one-unrectifiable. Thus pure one-unrectifiability is not a small Hausdorff-dimension assumption.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:contact-gradient}
Let \(E\subset\mathbb R^n\) be measurable,  \(\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^n\) be closed and purely one-unrectifiable, and let \(G:E\to\mathbb R^n\) be Lipschitz. Suppose that \(G\) is differentiable relative to \(E\) at almost every point of
\[
 E_\Sigma=\{x\in E:G(x)\in\Sigma\}
\]
and the derivative \(A_x\) of $G$ satisfies \(\operatorname{rank}A_x\ge1\) almost everywhere \(x \in E_\Sigma\). Then \(|E_\Sigma|=0\).
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We assume by contradiction that \(|E_\Sigma|>0\). From the continuity of the Lebesgue measure, and replacing \(E_\Sigma\) by its intersection with a sufficiently large ball, we may assume that \(E_\Sigma\)  is bounded and still has positive measure. Using Kirszbraun's theorem (see \cite{Kirszbraun, Valentine}), we can extend the function \(G\) Lipschitzly to \(\mathbb R^n\), and denote the resulting map by \(\widetilde G\). By Rademacher's theorem (see \cite{Rademacher} and \cite[Theorem 3.2, p. 103]{Evans}), \(\widetilde G\) is differentiable almost everywhere. At almost every Lebesgue density point \(x\) of \(E_\Sigma\) where the relative derivative \(A_x\) and \(D\widetilde G(x)\) both exist, one has
\[
 D\widetilde G(x)=A_x.
\]
To prove this claim, let us fix \(e\in\mathbb S^{n-1}\) and we prove that \(D\widetilde G(x)e=A_xe\). Since \(E_\Sigma\) has density one at \(x\), we first claim that
\begin{equation} \label{claim-oct-5}
 \operatorname{dist}(x+he,E_\Sigma)=o(|h|)\qquad\text{as }h\to0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, if \eqref{claim-oct-5} fails, there are $\delta>0$ and $h_k\to0$ when $k \rightarrow \infty$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(x+h_ke,E_\Sigma)\geq\delta|h_k|, \quad \forall \ k \in \mathbb{N}.
\]
Hence
\[
 B_{\delta|h_k|}(x+h_ke)
 \subset B_{(1+\delta)|h_k|}(x)\setminus E_\Sigma,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \frac{|B_{(1+\delta)|h_k|}(x)\setminus E_\Sigma|}
      {|B_{(1+\delta)|h_k|}|}
 \geq \left(\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}\right)^n.
\]
This contradicts the density-one property of $E_\Sigma$ at $x$.

                                                                                                                                                                                                                                

\smallskip
Now, due to \eqref{claim-oct-5}, we can choose $y_h\in E_\Sigma$ such that
\[
 |y_h-(x+he)|=o(|h|).
\]
As $\widetilde G=G$ on $E_\Sigma$ and $\widetilde G$ is Lipschitz, it follows that
\[
 \widetilde G(x+he)-\widetilde G(x)
 =G(y_h)-G(x)+O(|x+he-y_h|)
 =G(y_h)-G(x)+o(|h|).
\]
Also, the differentiable relative expansion of $G$ at $x$ gives
\[
 G(y_h)-G(x)=A_x(y_h-x)+o(|y_h-x|).
\]
As $y_h-x=he+o(|h|)$, we obtain
\[
 \widetilde G(x+he)-\widetilde G(x)
 =hA_xe+o(|h|).
\]
Hence \(D\widetilde G(x)e=A_xe\), and therefore \(D\widetilde G(x)=A_x\).

\smallskip
Since $\operatorname{rank}D\widetilde G(x)\geq1$ for almost every
$x\in E_\Sigma$, at least one column of $D\widetilde G(x)$ is nonzero.
Consequently, up to a null set,
\[
 E_\Sigma
 =\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}
 \{x\in E_\Sigma:|D\widetilde G(x)e_i|\geq k^{-1}\}.
\]
As $|E_\Sigma|>0$, countable subadditivity yields indices
$i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and $k\geq1$ for which
\[
 \mathcal{F}=\{x\in E_\Sigma:|D\widetilde G(x)e_i|\geq k^{-1}\}
\]
has positive measure. Now, for each $x \in \mathbb R^n$, write $x=z+se_i$, where $z\in e_i^\perp$ and $s\in\mathbb R$. Since
this change of coordinates has Jacobian one, it follows from Tonelli's theorem that
\[
 0<|F|
 =\int_{e_i^\perp}\int_{\mathbb R}
    \mathbf 1_F(z+se_i)\,ds\,dz
 =\int_{e_i^\perp}|F_z|\,dz,
\]
where $dz$ denotes Lebesgue measure on $e_i^\perp$ and $F_z=\{s\in\mathbb R:z+se_i\in \mathcal{F}\}$. For $n=1$, this is the counting measure on $e_i^\perp=\{0\}$. Hence $|F_z|>0$ for some $z\in e_i^\perp$. We then let \(g(s)=\widetilde G(z+se_i)\). Since \(g\) is Lipschitz and, for almost every \(s\in F_z\),
\begin{equation} \label{g-deriv-oct-5}
 |g'(s)|=|D\widetilde G(z+se_i)e_i|\ge k^{-1}.
\end{equation}
On the other hand, \(g(F_z)\subset\Sigma\cap g(I)\) for a bounded interval \(I\) containing \(F_z\). Since \(\Sigma\) is purely one-unrectifiable, \(\mathcal H^1(g(F_z))=0\). From this, and the one-dimensional area formula \eqref{one-dim-area}, we see that \(g'=0\) almost everywhere on \(F_z\), and we obtain a contradiction to \eqref{g-deriv-oct-5}. The proof is then complete.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:semiconcave-properties}
Let \(u\) be semi-concave in an open set \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n\).
Then \(u\) is locally Lipschitz and has a second-order expansion almost
everywhere in \(\Omega\). Suppose that, at \(x\in\Omega\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semiconcave-expansion}
 u(x+h)=u(x)+p\cdot h+\frac12h^TAh+o(|h|^2),
 \qquad A\in\mathbb S^n.
\end{equation}
Then \(p=Du(x)\), and for every sequence \(x_j\to x\) and every choice
\(p_j\in D^+u(x_j)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supergradient-expansion}
 p_j=p+A(x_j-x)+o(|x_j-x|).
\end{equation}
In addition, if \(\phi\in C^1\) touches \(u\) from below at \(x\), then \(u\) is
differentiable at \(x\) and \(Du(x)=D\phi(x)\). Moreover, suppose that \eqref{eq:semiconcave-expansion} holds at \(x\),
that \(u\) sloves 
\[
 \mathcal M^-_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\le c\quad\text{in }\Omega,
\]
in the viscosity sense, and \(|p|>\gamma\), \(p\notin\Sigma\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semiconcave-classical-inequality}
 \mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(A)-\mu_0|p|\le c.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The assertion that $u$ is locally Lipschitz follows from \cite[Theorem 2.17, p. 33]{CannarsaSinestrari}. On the other hand, on each ball compactly contained in \(\Omega\), there is \(K\ge0\)
such that \(K|z|^2/2-u(z)\) is convex in this ball. Then, the almost-everywhere expansion follow from the Aleksandrov's theorem for convex functions; see
\cite[Theorem~2.3.1(i), p. 42]{CannarsaSinestrari} and \cite[Theorem 6.9, p. 273]{Evans}.

\smallskip
Fix \(x\) satisfying \eqref{eq:semiconcave-expansion}, and choose such a
constant \(K\) such that the function $\psi(z)= K|z|^2/2-u(z)$ is convex on a small ball centered at \(x\). Let us define
\[
 P(z)=u(x)+p\cdot(z-x)+\frac12(z-x)^TA(z-x),
 \qquad R(z)=u(z)-P(z).
\]
Thus \(R(z)=o(|z-x|^2)\). It suffices to consider indices $j$ for which
\(r_j=|x_j-x|>0\), since \(D^+u(x)=\{p\}\). Let \(h_j=x_j-x\), \(0<\delta<1\), \(|e|=1\), and \(t_j=\delta r_j\). For all
such sufficiently large \(j\), it follows from the convexity of $\psi$ that
\[
 u(x_j\pm t_je)
 \le u(x_j)\pm t_jp_j\cdot e+\frac K2t_j^2;
\]
see \cite[Proposition~3.3.1, pp. 56-57]{CannarsaSinestrari}, for example. Hence
\[
 \frac{u(x_j+t_je)-u(x_j)}{t_j}-\frac K2t_j
 \le p_j\cdot e
 \le
 \frac{u(x_j)-u(x_j-t_je)}{t_j}+\frac K2t_j.
\]
Since $u = P +R$, and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{P(x_j+t_je)-P(x_j)}{t_j}
   &=p\cdot e+(Ah_j)\cdot e+\frac{t_j}{2}e^TAe,\\
 \frac{P(x_j)-P(x_j-t_je)}{t_j}
   &=p\cdot e+(Ah_j)\cdot e-\frac{t_j}{2}e^TAe,
 \end{aligned}
\]
we obtain
\begin{align*}
 \left|(p_j-p-Ah_j)\cdot e\right|
 & \le \frac{K+\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{2}\delta r_j
      +\frac{2}{\delta r_j}
       \sup_{z\in B_{2r_j}(x)}|R(z)|\\
       & \leq \frac{K+\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{2}\delta r_j
      +\frac{2 o(r_j^2)}{\delta r_j}.
\end{align*}
Taking the supremum over
\(|e|=1\), dividing by \(r_j\), and letting \(j\to\infty\), we obtain
\[
 \limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{|p_j-p-Ah_j|}{r_j}
 \le \frac{K+\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{2}\delta.
\]
Since \(\delta\) is arbitrary, \eqref{eq:supergradient-expansion}
follows.

\smallskip
Next, suppose that \(\phi\in C^1\) touches \(u\) from below at \(x\).
The convexity of the function\(\psi(z) =K|z|^2/2-u(z)\) near \(x\) gives
\(D^+u(x)\ne\varnothing\). Choose \(q\in D^+u(x)\). For all
sufficiently small \(h\),
\begin{equation} \label{phi-ext-oct-5}
 \phi(x+h)-\phi(x)
 \le u(x+h)-u(x)
 \le q\cdot h+\frac K2|h|^2.
\end{equation}
Applying these inequalities with \(h=te\) and \(h=-te\), divide by \(t\),
and let \(t\downarrow0\), we obtain \(q\cdot e=D\phi(x)\cdot e\) for every
unit vector \(e\). Hence, \(q=D\phi(x)\). Now, we substitute $q=D\phi(x)$ into \eqref{phi-ext-oct-5} and use the first-order extension of $\phi$ at $x$ to infer that
\[
 u(x+h)=u(x)+D\phi(x)\cdot h+o(|h|).
\]
This proves that $u$ is differentiable at $x$ and $Du(x) = D\phi(x)$.

\smallskip
Finally, assume the hypotheses of the last assertion in the lemma. For
\(\eta>0\),  we define
\[
 \psi_\eta(z)=P(z)-\eta|z-x|^2, \quad x \in \Omega.
\]
Since
\[
 u(z)-\psi_\eta(z)=R(z)+\eta|z-x|^2\ge0
\]
for \(z\) sufficiently close to \(x\), the function \(\psi_\eta\)
touches \(u\) from below at \(x\). Also,
\[
 D\psi_\eta(x)=p,\qquad D^2\psi_\eta(x)=A-2\eta I.
\]
The gradient \(p\) satisfies \(|p|>\gamma\) and \(p\notin\Sigma\). Therefore, 
\[
 \mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(A-2\eta I)-\mu_0|p|\le c.
\]
Then, by letting \(\eta\downarrow0\), we obtain
\eqref{eq:semiconcave-classical-inequality}. The proof is complete.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:measure} For every given \(0< \lambda <\Lambda\) and \(\mu_0 \geq 0\), there are constants \(\gamma_0>0\), \(\delta_0\in(0,1)\), and \(M_0>1\) such that the following holds. Let \(\Sigma\) be a closed purely one-unrectifiable set, \(0\le\gamma\le\gamma_0\), and \(u:B_1\to\mathbb R\) be lower semi-continuous satisfying
\begin{equation} \label{M-eqn-Oct-3}
 \mathcal M^-_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\le1 \quad \text{in} \quad B_1,
\end{equation}
in the viscosity sense. Assume that 
\begin{equation} \label{hyp-oct-3}
 u\ge0, \quad \text{and} \qquad |\{u>M_0\}\cap B_1|>(1-\delta)|B_1|
\end{equation}
for some $\delta \in (0, \delta_0]$. Then \(u>1\) in \(B_{1/4}\).
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We set
\[
M_0=2+5\sqrt2, \quad \gamma_0 = \sqrt{5}, \quad \text{and} \quad \delta_0 = \delta_0(n, \lambda, \Lambda, \mu_0) \in (0,1)
\]
where the number $\delta_0(n, \lambda, \Lambda, \mu_0)$ will be determined. We split the proof into two steps. In the first step, we prove the result with an additional assumption that $u$ is a semi-concave function. Then, in the second step, we remove such an additional assumption and prove the lemma.

\medskip
\noindent 
{\bf Step 1}: We prove Lemma~\ref{lem:measure} with an additional assumption that \(u\) is semi-concave. Assume that \eqref{hyp-oct-3} holds with some $\delta \in (0, \bar{\delta}_0]$ for some universal constant $\bar{\delta}_0 \in (0,1)$ depending only on $n, \lambda, \Lambda$ and $\mu_0$  that will be determined, and assume also that \(u\) is semi-concave. We prove the assertion of the lemma by a contradiction argument. Let
\[
\mathcal U=\{u>M_0\}\cap B_{1/4}, \quad \text{and} \quad \phi(z)=-10|z|^{1/2}, \quad z \in \mathbb R^n.
\]
By the inner regularity of Lebesgue measure, we can choose a compact set \(U\subset\mathcal U\) such that \(|\mathcal U\setminus U|<\delta|B_1|\). 

\smallskip
We assume by contradiction that there is some \(x_0\in B_{1/4}\) such that \(u(x_0)\le1\).  For every \(x\in U\), we minimize 
\[
 u(y)-\phi(y-x)=u(y)+10|y-x|^{1/2}
\]
over \(y\in B_1\). Note that as \(u\ge0\) and $x \in U \subset B_{1/4}$, every sequence \(z_j\in B_1\) with \(|z_j|\to1\) satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{j\to\infty}\bigl(u(z_j)+10|z_j-x|^{1/2}\bigr)\ge5\sqrt3>1+5\sqrt2\ge u(x_0)+10|x_0-x|^{1/2}.
\]
Thus a minimizing sequence stays in a compact subset of \(B_1\), and the lower semi-continuity of $u$ gives an existence of a minimizer \(y\in B_1\). In fact, choose \(\sigma_0\in(0,1/4)\) so small that
\[
10(3/4-\sigma_0)^{1/2}>1+5\sqrt2.
\]
If \(|y|\ge1-\sigma_0\), then as \(u(y)\ge0\) and \(|y-x|\ge3/4-\sigma_0\), we have
\[u(y)+10|y-x|^{1/2}> u(x_0)+10|x_0-x|^{1/2}\]
contradicting to the minimizing. Thus every minimizer belongs to \(B_{1-\sigma_0}\). Moreover, if $y$ is a minimizer, we have
\[
 u(y)\le1+5\sqrt2=M_0-1 < u(x),
\]
and therefore \(y\ne x\). Moreover, the function \(z\mapsto u(y)+\phi(z-x)-\phi(y-x)\) touches \(u\)
from below at \(y\); since \(y\ne x\), Lemma~\ref{lem:semiconcave-properties}
gives \(Du(y)=D\phi(y-x)\). In summary, for every $x \in U$ at the
corresponding contact point $y$ satisfies
\begin{equation} \label{conclusion-oct-2}
\left\{
\begin{array}{ll}
& y \in B_{1-\sigma_0}, \quad y\ne x, \quad u(y) \leq M_0-1, \quad Du(y)=D\phi(y-x),\\
& u(z)\ge u(y)+\phi(z-x)-\phi(y-x) \quad \text{for all } \ z \in B_1.
\end{array} \right.
\end{equation}
Let \(T\) be the set of all such contact points.  We note that $T$ is closed. Indeed, let $y_j\in T$ with $y_j\to y$. Choose $x_j\in U$ such that
$y_j$ minimizes $u(z)+10|z-x_j|^{1/2}$ over $z\in B_1$. Since $U$
is compact, after passing to a subsequence we have $x_j\to x\in U$.
For every $z\in B_1$,
\[
 u(y_j)+10|y_j-x_j|^{1/2}
 \leq u(z)+10|z-x_j|^{1/2}.
\]
By lower semicontinuity of $u$,
\[
 u(y)+10|y-x|^{1/2}
 \leq u(z)+10|z-x|^{1/2}.
\]
Thus $y\in T$, and $T$ is closed.

\smallskip
Now, since \(U\) is compact, \(T \subset \overline B_{1-\sigma_0}\) and it is closed, hence \(T\) is compact. Moreover, due to \eqref{conclusion-oct-2}
\[
 T\subset\{u\le M_0-1\}.
\]
Note also that  $ D\phi(z)=-5|z|^{-3/2}z$ and $D\phi$ is one-to-one on \(\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}\). Hence it follows from \eqref{conclusion-oct-2} that every \(y\in T\) determines the unique corresponding \(x\in U\). We write \(m(y)=x\). Thus \(m(T)=U\) and
\[
 Du(y)=D\phi(y-m(y)).
\]

\smallskip
Next, we show that \(Du\) is Lipschitz on \(T\). Since \(U\subset B_{1/4}\), \(T\subset B_{1-\sigma_0}\), \(u(x)>M_0\) on \(U\), and \(u(y)\le M_0-1\) on \(T\), we have
\[
u(m(y)) - u(y) \geq 1, \quad \forall  y \in T.
\] 
Also, due to Lemma~\ref{lem:semiconcave-properties}, $u$ is Lipschitz on \(\overline{B}_{1-\sigma_0}\). Hence, there is $\eta>0$, which depends on $u$, such that
\begin{equation} \label{y-my}
 |y-m(y)|\ge\eta>0,\qquad y\in T.
\end{equation}  
                                                                    
   
            
                
                                      
                             
                              
   
               
                                      
                                                          
                                          
               
Let $y_1,y_2\in T$ with $y_1 \ne y_2$,  and let us denote $r=2|y_1-y_2|>0$. We consider the case that
\begin{equation} \label{case-1-oct-8}
 r<\min\{\sigma_0/2,\eta/2\}.
\end{equation}
In this case, we note that $B_{r}(y_j) \subset B_{1-\sigma_0/2}$ for $j=1,2$. Also, since
$y_j$ minimizes $u(\xi)-\phi(\xi-m(y_j))$ over $\xi \in B_1$,
\begin{equation} \label{minizer-oct-8}
 u(z)\geq u(y_j)
       +\phi(z-m(y_j))-\phi(y_j-m(y_j)), \quad \forall \ z \in B_{1}.
\end{equation}
Also, note that for any $z \in B_r(y_j)$, we have
\begin{equation} \label{w-est-oct-8}
 |(y_j-m(y_j))+t(z-y_j)|\geq |y_j-m(y_j)|-|(z-y_j)|\geq\eta-r>\eta/2, \quad \forall \ t \in [0, 1].
\end{equation}
Moreover, for $\xi\ne0$, we also note that
\begin{equation} \label{D2-phi-oct-8}
 D^2\phi(\xi)
 =-5|\xi|^{-3/2}I
  +\frac{15}{2}|\xi|^{-7/2}\xi\otimes\xi,
 \quad \text{and hence}\quad
 \|D^2\phi(\xi)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq5|\xi|^{-3/2}.
\end{equation}
Then, by using Taylor's formula, \eqref{w-est-oct-8},  and the fact that $D\phi(y_j-m(y_j))=Du(y_j)$, we see that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \phi(w+h))-\phi(w)
 &=Du(y_j)\cdot h
   +\int_0^1(1-t)h^TD^2\phi(w+th)h\,dt\\
 &\geq Du(y_j)\cdot h
   -\frac52(2/\eta)^{3/2}|h|^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $w=y_j-m(y_j)$ and $h=z-y_j$, for $z \in B_{r}(y_j)$ and $j =1,2$. Since $|h|<r$, it follows this last estimate and \eqref{minizer-oct-8} that
\begin{equation} \label{u-z-below-oct-3}
 u(z)\geq u(y_j)+Du(y_j)\cdot(z-y_j)
          -\frac52(2/\eta)^{3/2}r^2, \quad \forall \ z \in B_r(y_j),\quad j=1,2.
\end{equation}
On the other hand, since $u$ is semi-concave in $B_1$, there is $K\geq0$ such that
$x\mapsto u(x)-\frac K2|x|^2$ is concave on
$B_{1-\sigma_0/2}$. Then, using 
the concavity of the map $x\mapsto u(x)-\frac K2|x|^2$ and $Du(y_2)=D\phi(y_2-m(y_2))$, we infer that
\[
u(z)-\frac K2|z|^2 \leq u(y_2)-\frac K2|y_2|^2
    +(Du(y_2)-Ky_2)\cdot(z-y_2), \quad z \in B_{1-\sigma_0/2}.
\]
Rearranging the terms and using 
$|z|^2-|y_2|^2-2y_2\cdot(z-y_2)=|z-y_2|^2$, we obtain
\[
 u(z)\leq u(y_2)+Du(y_2)\cdot(z-y_2)
       +\frac K2|z-y_2|^2, \quad z \in B_{1-\sigma_0/2}.
\]
Finally, as $|z-y_2|\leq |z-y_1|+|y_1-y_2|
          <r+\frac r2=\frac{3r}{2}$, it follows that
\[
 u(z)\leq u(y_2)+Du(y_2)\cdot(z-y_2)
          +\frac{9K}{8}r^2, \quad   z \in B_{1-\sigma_0/2}.
\]
On the other hand, observe also that \eqref{u-z-below-oct-3} particular implies that
\[
u(y_1)\geq u(y_2)+Du(y_2)\cdot(y_1-y_2)
          -\frac52(2/\eta)^{3/2}r^2.
\]
By combining the last two estimates, we obtain
\[
u(z) \leq u(y_1) + Du(y_2) \cdot (z-y_1) +Cr^2, \quad \forall \  z \in B_{1-\sigma_0/2}.
\]
From this, and by using \eqref{u-z-below-oct-3} with $j =1$ and the fact that $\overline{B}_{r}(y_1) \cup \overline{B}_r(y_2) \subset B_{1-\sigma_0/2}$, we obtain
\[
 (Du(y_1)-Du(y_2))\cdot(z-y_1)\le Cr^2, \quad \forall \ z \in \overline{B}_r(y_1).
\]
Taking \(z=y_1+re\) and \(z=y_1-re\), with \(e\in\mathbb S^{n-1}\), we obtain
\[
 |Du(y_1)-Du(y_2)|\le Cr
\]
when \eqref{case-1-oct-8} holds. On the other hand, if \eqref{case-1-oct-8} fails, i.e. \(r\ge\min\{\sigma_0/2,\eta/2\}\), it follows from \eqref{y-my} and the definition of $\phi$ that
\begin{align*}
|Du(y_1)-Du(y_2)| & = |D\phi(y_1 - m(y_1)) - D\phi(y_2 - m(y_2))| \leq 2 \sup_{y \in T}|D\phi (y -m(y))| \\
& \leq 2 \sup_{B_{5/4}\setminus B_{\eta}}|D\phi| \leq C |y_1 - y_2|.
\end{align*}
Hence, from both cases, we have proved that \(Du\) is Lipschitz on \(T\). 

\smallskip Next, note from \eqref{conclusion-oct-2} that for each $y\in T$,  $y \ne m(y)$ and 
\[
 Du(y)=D\phi(y-m(y))
 =-5|y-m(y)|^{-3/2}(y-m(y)).
\]
This, \eqref{y-my}, and $|y-m(y)|\le5/4$ imply that $2\sqrt5\le |Du(y)|\le5\eta^{-1/2}$ for all $y\in T$. Hence, we infer that the map $m$ is defined by
\[
 m(y)=y+ \frac{25Du(y)}{|Du(y)|^3}, \quad y \in T,
 \]
is also Lipschitz in $T$.

\smallskip
Next, from Lemma \ref{lem:semiconcave-properties} and \eqref{y-my}, for almost every \(y\in T\), the function \(u\) is twice differentiable and
\[
 D^2u(y)\ge D^2\phi(y-m(y)).
\]
Let us denote \(r_y=|y-m(y)|\). Then $r_y \in (\eta, 5/4]$ and \(D^2\phi(y-m(y))\) has the radial eigenvalue \((5/2)r_y^{-3/2}>0\) with eigenvector $(y -m(y))/r_y$ and the tangential eigenvalue \(-5r_y^{-3/2}\). Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{rank}D^2u(y)\ge1
\]
for almost every \(y\in T\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:semiconcave-properties},
\(Du|_T\) is differentiable relative to \(T\) with derivative
\(D^2u(y)\) at almost every such point. Lemma~\ref{lem:contact-gradient} yields
\[
 |\{y\in T:Du(y)\in\Sigma\}|=0.
\]
As \(r_y\le5/4\), and we have
\[
 |Du(y)| = |D\phi(y-m(y))|=5r_y^{-1/2}\ge2\sqrt5 > \gamma_0.
\]
Thus, for almost every \(y\in T\), Lemma~\ref{lem:semiconcave-properties}
gives a second-order expansion of \(u\), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:contact-gradient} gives \(Du(y)\notin\Sigma\).
At such a point, we also have \(|Du(y)|\ge2\sqrt5>\gamma\). From this and since \(u\) satisfies
\eqref{M-eqn-Oct-3}, we apply Lemma~\ref{lem:semiconcave-properties}to infer that
\[
 \mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2u(y))-\mu_0|Du(y)|\le1
\]
or equivalently, 
\begin{equation} \label{u-test-PDE}
 \lambda\operatorname{tr}(D^2u(y))^+-\Lambda\operatorname{tr}(D^2u(y))^--\mu_0|Du(y)|\le1.
\end{equation}
In addition, since \(D^2u (y)\ge D^2\phi(y-m(y))\), we have
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(D^2u(y))^-\le\operatorname{tr}\bigl((D^2\phi(y-m(y)))^-\bigr) = 5(n-1)r_y^{-3/2}\le C r_y^{-3/2}.
\]
Using this, $|Du(y)|=5r_y^{-1/2}$ and $r_y \in (\eta, 5/4]$, we infer from \eqref{u-test-PDE} that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lambda\operatorname{tr}\bigl((D^2u(y))^+\bigr)
 &\leq1+\Lambda\operatorname{tr}\bigl((D^2u(y))^-\bigr)
          +\mu_0|Du(y)|\\
 &\leq1+
 \left(5\Lambda(n-1)+\frac{25}{4}\mu_0\right)r_y^{-3/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore,
\begin{equation} \label{Du-est-Oc-3}
 |D^2u(y)|
 \leq\operatorname{tr}\bigl((D^2u(y))^+\bigr)
     +\operatorname{tr}\bigl((D^2u(y))^-\bigr)
 \leq C\left(1+r_y^{-3/2}\right)
\end{equation}
with some $C = C(n, \lambda, \Lambda, \mu_0)>0$.

\smallskip
Now, by Kirszbraun's theorem (see \cite{Kirszbraun, Valentine}), we can extend $m:T\to\mathbb R^n$ to a Lipschitz map
$\widetilde m:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$. Due to Rademacher's theorem (see \cite{Rademacher} and \cite[Theorem 3.2, p. 103]{Evans}), $\widetilde m$ is differentiable at almost every where in $\mathbb R^n$. Let $y$ be a density point of $T$ at
which $\widetilde m$ is differentiable and the relative expansion of
$Du|_T$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:semiconcave-properties} holds. For $z\in T$ with
$z$ sufficiently close to $y$,
\begin{equation} \label{Du-expansion-oct-8}
 Du(z)-Du(y)=D^2u(y)(z-y)+o(|z-y|).
\end{equation}
Since $\widetilde m=m$ on $T$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 z-m(z)-(y-m(y))
 &=(z-y)-\bigl(\widetilde m(z)-\widetilde m(y)\bigr)\\
 &=(I-D\widetilde m(y))(z-y)+o(|z-y|).
\end{aligned}
\]
Using $Du(z)=D\phi(z-m(z))$ on $T$ and expanding $D\phi$ at
$y-m(y)$, we obtain
\[
 Du(z)-Du(y)
 =D^2\phi(y-m(y))(I-D\widetilde m(y))(z-y)
  +o(|z-y|).
\]
By Comparing this expansion and the expansion \eqref{Du-expansion-oct-8}, we infer that
\[
 \bigl[D^2u(y)
   -D^2\phi(y-m(y))(I-D\widetilde m(y))\bigr](z-y)
 =o(|z-y|),
 \qquad z\in T,\quad z\to y.
\]
For each $e\in\mathbb S^{n-1}$, as in \eqref{claim-oct-5}, it follows from the density-one property of $T$ at
$y$ that there is $z_h\in T$ with $z_h-y=he+o(h)$ as $h\downarrow0$.
Substituting $z=z_h$, dividing by $h$, and letting $h\downarrow0$, we get
\[
 D^2u(y)=D^2\phi(y-m(y))(I-D\widetilde m(y)).
\]
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          
   
                                   
   
From this identity, \eqref{Du-est-Oc-3}, and the fact that  \(\|(D^2\phi(y-m(y)))^{-1}\|\le Cr_y^{3/2}\) due to the explicit formulation of $D^2\phi$ in \eqref{D2-phi-oct-8}, we infer that
\[
 |D\widetilde m(y)|\le1+C r_y^{3/2}|D^2u(y)|\le C_0
\]
for almost every \(y\in T\), where $C_0 = C_0 (n, \lambda, \Lambda, \mu_0)>0$. Since $\widetilde m(T)=m(T)=U$, every $x\in U$ has at least one
preimage in $T$, it follows from the area formula \cite[Theorem 3.8, p.119]{Evans} that
\[
 |U|
 =\int_U 1\,dx
 \leq\int_{\mathbb R^n}
   \#\{y\in T:\widetilde m(y)=x\}\,dx
 =\int_T|\det D\widetilde m(y)|\,dy \leq C_0^n|T|.
\]
This and as $|U|\ge|B_{1/4}|-2\delta|B_1|$, it follows that
\[
|B_{1/4}|-2\delta|B_1| \leq C_0^n|T|.
\]
On the other hand, we have \(T\subset\{u\leq M_0-1\}\cap B_1
\subset\{u\leq M_0\}\cap B_1
\). Then, it follows from the measure assumption in \eqref{hyp-oct-3} that  \(|T|\le\delta|B_1|\), and therefore
\[
|B_{1/4}|-2\delta|B_1| \leq  C_0 ^n\delta|B_1|.
\]
Note that $C_0 = C_0(n, \lambda, \Lambda, \mu_0)>0$. Then, we obtain a contradiction for  $\delta \in (0, \bar{\delta}_0]$ when choosing \(\bar{\delta}_0 = \delta_0(n, \lambda, \Lambda, \mu_0) \in (0,1)\) sufficiently small.\\

\noindent
\smallskip
{\bf Step 2}: We remove the  semi-concavity assumption on \(u\) used in {\bf Step 1} by using inf-convolution. Let $M_0>0, \mu_0\ge0$ and $\bar{\delta}_0 = \bar{\delta}_0(n, \lambda, \Lambda, \mu_0) \in (0,1)$ be the numbers defined in {\bf Step 1}. We choose 
\[ \delta_0 = \bar{\delta}_0/4.\]  
Let \(u\) be lower semi-continuous and we assume that \eqref{hyp-oct-3} holds with some $\delta \in (0, \delta_0]$. Let us denote \(v=\min\{u,2M_0\}\). We note that \(v\) is also satisfies  \eqref{M-eqn-Oct-3} in the viscosity sense. Let \(v_\varepsilon\) be the inf-convolution defined in \eqref{inf-convol} in Section~\ref{semi-concave}. Moreover, observe that

\]
and the set $\{v_\varepsilon>M_0\}$ increases as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Hence $|\{v_\varepsilon>M_0\}|$ converges to \(|\{u>M_0\}|\) as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

\end{equation}
Note that the set $a\Sigma$ is closed and purely one-unrectifiable as proved in Lemma \ref{lem:stability-sigma}.  As $\eta>0$ is sufficiently small,  we have
$a^n>1/2$. Since $v_\varepsilon\uparrow v=\min\{u,2M_0\}$ in $B_a$
and $\{v\leq M_0\}=\{u\leq M_0\}$, it follows from the continuity of Lebesgue measure and  the measure hypothesis \eqref{hyp-oct-3}  that

 |\{v_\varepsilon\leq M_0\}\cap B_a|

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:strict-comparison}
Let $\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^n$ be closed and purely one-unrectifiable. Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ be non-empty, open and bounded, and $u\in C(\overline\Omega)$ satisfy
\[
 \mathcal M^-_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\le1\quad\text{in }\Omega
\]
in the viscosity sense. Suppose $\psi\in C^2(\overline\Omega)$ such that $u\ge\psi$ on $\partial\Omega$ and that there are $\eta,c_0>0$ such that, for every $x\in\Omega$ and $|q|\le\eta$,
\[
 |D\psi(x)+q|>\gamma,
 \quad \text{and} \quad
 \mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2\psi(x))-\mu_0|D\psi(x)+q|\ge1+c_0.
\]
Assume also that $D^2\psi(x)$ has a positive eigenvalue for every $x\in\Omega$. Then $u\ge\psi$ in $\Omega$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Suppose by contradiction that $\min_{\overline\Omega}(u-\psi)=-a<0$ with some $a>0$. Since $u-\psi\ge0$ on $\partial\Omega$, we can choose a compact set $K\Subset\Omega$ such that
\[
 \min_K(u-\psi)=-a,
 \qquad
 u-\psi>-\frac a4\quad\text{on }\overline\Omega\setminus K.
\]
Let $u_\varepsilon$ be the inf-convolution of $u$ over $\overline\Omega$ defined in \eqref{inf-convol}. It follows that $u_\epsilon$ converges uniformly to $u$ on $\overline\Omega$. Also, for a sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, and let $K_\epsilon$ be a neighborhood of $K$, $K \Subset K_\epsilon \Subset \Omega$, such that $u_\varepsilon$ satisfies the same viscosity inequality as $u$ in $K_\epsilon$, and $u_\varepsilon$ is semi-concave in $K_\epsilon$. Let us fix \(\varepsilon>0\) sufficiently small such that these properties hold,  and 
\[
 \min_Kw<-\frac{3a}{4},
 \quad \text{and} \quad
 w>-\frac a3\quad\text{on }\overline\Omega\setminus K
\]
where $w=u_\varepsilon-\psi$. Decrease $\eta$, if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that  $2\eta\operatorname{diam}\Omega<a/6$. For each $q\in\overline B_\eta$, let $y_q$ be any minimizer of $w(x)-q\cdot x$ over $\overline\Omega$. Comparing its values at a point where $w<-3a/4$ and at any point of $\overline\Omega\setminus K$, we infer that every such minimizer $y_q$ belongs to $K$. Let us define
\[
 T=\displaystyle{\underset{q\in\overline B_\eta}\cup}\big \{y\in\overline\Omega:  w(y)-q\cdot y=\min_{x \in \overline\Omega}(w(x)-q\cdot x)\big\} \subset K.
\]
Because contact relation is closed in $K\times\overline B_\eta$, $T$ is compact. Corresponding to some $q \in \overline{B}_\eta$, at $y_q \in T$, the function $x\mapsto\psi(x)+q\cdot(x-y_q)+u_\varepsilon(y_q)-\psi(y_q)$ touches $u_\varepsilon$ from below. Then, applying Lemma~\ref{lem:semiconcave-properties}, we have
\[
 Du_\varepsilon(y_q)=D\psi(y_q)+q \quad \text{or} \quad Du_\varepsilon(y_q)- D\psi(y_q) =q.
\]
Thus, $q$ is uniquely determined, and the map 
\[
Q : T \mapsto \overline{B}_\eta \subset R^n: \quad Q =Du_\varepsilon-D\psi
\]
is well-defined and $Q(T) = \overline{B}_\eta$. We claim that the map $Q$ is Lipschitz on $T$. Indeed, let $y_j\in T$ with  $j=1,2$, we infer from the minimum at $y_1$ and the minimum at $y_2$ that
\begin{equation} \label{minimize-oct-4}
 \begin{aligned}
 w(z)&\ge w(y_1)+Q(y_1) \cdot(z-y_1), \quad z \in \Omega,\\
 w(y_1)&\ge w(y_2)+Q(y_2) \cdot(y_1-y_2).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Let $h = \operatorname{dist}(K_\epsilon,\partial\Omega)/2)>0$. Then, choose a semi-concavity constant $\kappa_0>0$ for $w$ on $\overline{K}_\epsilon$. At $y_2$, we have
\[
 w(z)\le w(y_2)+ Q(y_2) \cdot(z-y_2)+\frac{\kappa_0}{2}|z-y_2|^2, \quad \forall \ z\in B_h(y_2).
\]
Combining this with \eqref{minimize-oct-4}, we infer that
\begin{equation} \label{Q-y12-oct-8}
 (Q(y_1) -Q(y_2))\cdot(z-y_1)\le\frac{\kappa_0}{2}|z-y_2|^2, \quad \forall \ z\in B_h(y_2).
\end{equation}
Let us denote $d=|y_1-y_2|$. If $2d<h$, we use $z=y_1\pm de$ with $|e|=1$ in \eqref{Q-y12-oct-8} to infer that
\[
 \pm(Q(y_1)-Q(y_2))\cdot e\le2\kappa_0 d \quad \text{and therefore} \quad
 \quad |Q(y_1)-Q(y_2)|\le2 \kappa_0|y_1-y_2|.
\]
On the other hand, if $2d\ge h$, then $|Q(y_1)-Q(y_2)|\le2\eta\le(4\eta/h)d$. Thus $Q$ is Lipschitz on $T$ with the Lipschitz constant
\[
L = \max\{2\kappa_0, 4\eta/h\}.
\]
Since $Q(T)=\overline B_\eta$ and $Q$ Lipschitz, it follows that
\[
|B_\eta|= |Q(T)| \leq L^n |T|.
\]
Therefore, $|T|>0$.

\smallskip
As $u_\varepsilon$ is semi-concave in $K_\epsilon$ and $T \subset K \subset K_\epsilon$, it follows from Alexandrov's theorem that at almost every $y\in T$, the second-order expansion of $u_\varepsilon$ exists. Moreover, as $x \mapsto u_\varepsilon(x)-\psi(x)-q\cdot x$ attains the  minimum at $y$, we have
\[
 D^2u_\varepsilon(y)\ge D^2\psi(y).
\]
Hence $D^2u_\varepsilon(y)$ has a positive eigenvalue and $\operatorname{rank}D^2u_\varepsilon(y)\ge1$. Since $Du_\varepsilon|_T=Q+D\psi$ is Lipschitz, its relative derivative at almost every such $y$ is $D^2u_\varepsilon(y)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:semiconcave-properties}. Then, we infer from Lemma~\ref{lem:contact-gradient} that
\[
 |\{y\in T:Du_\varepsilon(y)\in\Sigma\}|=0.
\]
From this and as $|T|>0$, we can choose $y \in T$ where $u_\epsilon$ admits the second-order expansion  and $Du_\varepsilon(y)\notin\Sigma$. From assumption on $\psi$, $|Du_\varepsilon(y)|=|D\psi(y)+q|>\gamma$. It then follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:semiconcave-properties}, the assumption in the lemma, and the fact that $D^2u_\varepsilon(y)\ge D^2\psi(y)$ that
\[
 1\ge\mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2u_\varepsilon(y))-\mu_0|Du_\varepsilon(y)|
 \ge\mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2\psi(y))-\mu_0|D\psi(y)+q|
 \ge1+c_0.
\]
Hence, we have a contradiction as $c_0>0$, and the proof is complete.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:doubling} For every  $0 <\lambda <\Lambda$, $\mu_0 \geq 0$,  and $\gamma_0>0$ ,  there is $M_1>1$ such that the following assertion holds. Assume that $\Sigma$ is a closed purely one-unrectifiable set,  $0\le\gamma\le\gamma_0$, and $u\in C(B_2)$ is non-negative and satisfies
\[
\mathcal M^-_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\le1\quad\text{in }B_2,
\]
in the viscosity sense,  and $u>M_1$ in $B_{1/4}$. Then
\begin{equation} \label{doubling-est}
u>1\quad\text{in }B_{3/2}.
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let  us fixed $p>0$ such that $\lambda(p+1)-\Lambda(n-1)-2\mu_0>0$. Then, let $b(x)=|x|^{-p}$, and it follows from the direct calculation that
\[
 \mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2b)-\mu_0|Db|
 =pr^{-p-2}\bigl(\lambda(p+1)-\Lambda(n-1)-\mu_0r\bigr), \quad r = |x| <2.
\]
Now, with some $M_1>0$ to be determined shortly, let
\[
 B(x)=\frac{M_1}{2\cdot4^p}\bigl(|x|^{-p}-(7/4)^{-p}\bigr),
 \qquad \frac14\le|x|\le\frac74.
\]
We have $B=0$ on $\partial B_{7/4}$ and $B<M_1/2$ on $\partial B_{1/4}$. For some fixed $\gamma_0>0$, by choosing $M_1$ sufficiently large, we obtain, for $1/4<|x|<7/4$ and $|q|\le1$,
\[
 B\ge1\quad\text{if }|x|\le3/2,
 \qquad |DB|>\gamma_0+1,
 \qquad \mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2B)-\mu_0|DB+q|\ge2.
\]
Indeed, the last inequality follows from
\[
 \mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2B)-\mu_0|DB+q|
 \ge \frac{M_1p}{2\cdot4^p}r^{-p-2}
       \bigl(\lambda(p+1)-\Lambda(n-1)-\mu_0r\bigr)-\mu_0.
\]
Note also that the radial eigenvalue of $D^2B$ is positive.

\smallskip
Now, we apply Lemma~\ref{lem:strict-comparison} in $B_{7/4}\setminus\overline B_{1/4}$. Note that in this domain, on the outer boundary $\partial B_{7/4}$, we have$u\ge0=B$, and on the inner boundary $\partial B_{1/4}$, we have $u\ge M_1>B$ due to the assumption in the lemma and the continuity of $u$. Thus $u\ge B$ in the annulus $B_{7/4}\setminus\overline B_{1/4}$, which is almost the assertion in the lemma except that we may have the equality. To derive the assertion with the strict inequality, we note that for with $\theta>0$ sufficiently small, we also have $u \geq (1+\theta)B$ on $\partial (B_{7/4}\setminus\overline B_{1/4})$. Moreover,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
& \mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2((1+\theta)B))-\mu_0|D((1+\theta)B)+q|\\
 &=(1+\theta)\Big[\mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2B)-\mu_0\big|DB+\frac q{1+\theta}\big|\Big]\\
 &\ge2(1+\theta),\qquad |q|\le1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, $|D((1+\theta)B)+q|>\gamma$.  Then, applying Lemma~\ref{lem:strict-comparison} again, we also infer that $u\ge(1+\theta)B>1$ in $B_{3/2}\setminus\overline B_{1/4}$. From this and the assumption that $u>M_1>1$ in $\overline{B}_{1/4}$, we see that $u>1$ on $B_{3/2}$. Hence, the estimate \eqref{doubling-est} is proved, and the proof is complete.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:Leps} For every $0< \lambda <\Lambda$ and $\mu_0 \geq 0$, there are $\gamma_0>0$, $\varepsilon>0$, and $C>0$ such that the following holds. Suppose that $\Sigma$ is closed and purely one-unrectifiable, $0\le\gamma\le\gamma_0$, and $u\in C(B_2)$ is non-negative and satisfies, 
\[
\mathcal M^-_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\le1\quad\text{in }B_2
\]
in the viscosity sense, and $\displaystyle{\inf_{B_1}u\le1}$. Then
\begin{equation} \label{L-espilon-Oct5}
 |\{u>t\}\cap B_1|\le Ct^{-\varepsilon},\qquad t>0.
\end{equation}

Let $M, \delta_0$ be the constants defined in Corollary~\ref{cor:measure-point}. Let $\delta = \delta_0$, and 
\[
 A_k=\{u>M^k\}\cap B_1,\qquad k\ge1.
\]
Note that if $|A_1|>(1-\delta)|B_1|$, then it folllows from Corollary~\ref{cor:measure-point} that $u>1$ in $B_{3/2}$. From this and as $u \in C(\overline B_1)$, we see that that $\underset{B_1}\inf  u>1$, and we obtain a contradiction. Hence
\begin{equation} \label{A-1-oct-5}
 |A_1|\le(1-\delta)|B_1|.
\end{equation}
\smallskip
Next, we claim that
\begin{equation} \label{A-k-oct-6}
 |A_{k+1}|\le(1-5^{-n}\delta)|A_k|, \quad \forall \ k \geq 1.
\end{equation}
To prove the claim, we apply Lemma~\ref{lem:ink} with $E=A_{k+1}$ and $F=A_k$. We then need to verify the assumptions in Lemma~\ref{lem:ink}. Since $E \subset A_1$,  the first assumption in  Lemma~\ref{lem:ink}, $|E| \leq (1-\delta)|B_1|$, holds due to \eqref{A-1-oct-5}. Now, suppose that $B_r(x)\subset B_1$ satisfies

Since $B_{2r}(x)\subset B_2$ and $M^k\ge1$, we apply Corollary~\ref{cor:scaled-measure} to $u$ in $B_{2r}(x)$, with $\kappa=M^k$ to conclude that $u>M^k$ in $B_r(x)$, and therefore $B_r(x)\subset A_k$, and this verifies second assumption in Lemma~\ref{lem:ink}. Thus, \eqref{A-k-oct-6} follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:ink}.

\smallskip

 |A_k|\le |B_1|\vartheta^k=|B_1|M^{-\varepsilon k},\qquad k\ge1.

In other words, we have proved that

which infers \eqref{L-espilon-Oct5} and the proof is complete.

Because the argument requires some technical room, we begin by establishing a Harnack inequality for the equation that is posed in $B_2$. Let $c\ge0$, and suppose that $u\in C(B_2)$ is non-negative and satisfies, in the viscosity sense,
\begin{equation} \label{eqn-oct-5}
 \mathcal M^-_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\le c,\qquad \mathcal M^+_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\ge-c \quad \text{in} \quad B_2,
\end{equation}
where $\Sigma$ is closed and purely one-unrectifiable. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{harnack-local-B2}
 \sup_{B_{1/2}}u\le C\left(\inf_{B_{1/2}}u+c+\gamma\right),
\end{equation}
where $C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\mu_0)$.
For a small $\zeta>0$, let us denote
\[
 A=\max\left\{c+\inf_{B_{1/2}}u,\frac\gamma{\gamma_0}\right\}+\zeta,
\]
where $\gamma_0$ is chosen to work in Corollary~\ref{cor:measure-point} and Theorem~\ref{thm:Leps}. By using the scaling $u \mapsto u/A$ and replace $c$ by $c/A\le1$, the threshold $\gamma$ by $\gamma/A\le\gamma_0$, and the set $\Sigma$ by $A^{-1}\Sigma$, it suffices to prove the bound $\underset{B_{1/2}} \sup u$ by a universal constant under the assumption that $\underset{B_{1/2}} \inf  u \leq 1$.

\smallskip
Let \(\beta>0\) to be determined, and 
\begin{equation} \label{t-def-oct-5}
 t=\max_{\overline B_1}u(x)(1-|x|)^\beta,
\end{equation}
where the product is extended by zero on \(\partial B_1\).  Note that as \(u\) is continuous in \(B_2\), the maximum in the definition above is attained. Then, we define
\[
 h_t(x)=t(1-|x|)^{-\beta},\qquad x\in B_1.
\]
It is clear from the definition that \(h_t\ge u\) in \(B_1\). Also, note that if \(t\le1\), the bound $\underset{B_{1/2}} \sup u$ is immediate. Hence, we only need to consider the case that \(t>1\). In this case, the maximum in \eqref{t-def-oct-5} is attained at some \(x_0\in B_1\), and we denote
\[
 H_0 = u(x_0)=h_t(x_0) =t(2r)^{-\beta}\ge1\quad \text{for} \quad r=\frac{1-|x_0|}{2} \in (0, 1/2].
\]
Since $\underset{B_1}\inf u\le1$ and $\gamma\le\gamma_0$, it follows from  Theorem~\ref{thm:Leps} that
\begin{equation} \label{measure-1-oct-5}
 |\{u>H_0/2\}\cap B_r(x_0)|
 \le 2^\varepsilon C_0 H_0^{-\varepsilon}
 =2^\varepsilon C_0 t^{-\varepsilon}(2r)^{\beta\varepsilon},
\end{equation}
where $C_0>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$ depend only on $n,\lambda,\Lambda,\mu_0$.

\smallskip
Next, let $M,\delta_0$ be as in Corollary~\ref{cor:measure-point}, $\delta = \delta_0$, and fix
\[
 \beta=\max\left\{1,\frac n\varepsilon\right\}.
\]
Then, we choose $\mu\in(0,1/2)$ sufficiently small, depending only on $M$ and $\beta$,
such that
\[
 M\bigl((1-\mu)^{-\beta}-1\bigr)\le\frac14.
\]
Since $\beta\ge1$ and $(1-\mu)^{-\beta}-1\ge\beta\mu\ge\mu$, we obtain
\[
 \frac{\mu^2}{(1-\mu)^{-\beta}-1}\le\mu\le1,
 \quad \text{and} \quad
 \frac{\mu\gamma}{(1-\mu)^{-\beta}-1}
 \le\gamma\le\gamma_0.
\]
On the other hand,  recall that
\[
 u(x)\le h_t(x)=t(1-|x|)^{-\beta},\qquad
 2r=1-|x_0|, \quad \text{and} \quad H_0=t(2r)^{-\beta}.
\]
Then, for every $x\in B_{2\mu r}(x_0)$, we have
\[
 1-|x|
 \ge1-|x_0|-|x-x_0|
 >2r-2\mu r
 =2r(1-\mu).
\]
Therefore,
\[
 u(x)
 \le t(1-|x|)^{-\beta}
 \le t\bigl(2r(1-\mu)\bigr)^{-\beta}
 =(1-\mu)^{-\beta}H_0,
 \qquad x\in B_{2\mu r}(x_0).
\]
Now, let us define $A_\mu = (1-\mu)^{-\beta} -1$, and
\[
 v(x)=\frac{(1-\mu)^{-\beta} H_0-u(x_0+\mu r x)}{A_\mu H_0},\qquad x\in B_2.
\]
Then \(v\ge0\), \(v(0)=1\), and the corresponding transformed exceptional set for $v$ is \(-\frac{\mu r}{A_\mu H_0}\Sigma\), hence purely one-unrectifiable. Moreover, at active gradients,
\begin{align*}
& \mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2v)-\mu_0|Dv|\le \mathcal P^-_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2v)-\mu r\mu_0|Dv| \\
 & =-\frac{(\mu r)^2}{A_\mu H_0}\bigl(\mathcal P^+_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^2u)+\mu_0|Du|\bigr)
 \le\frac{(\mu r)^2}{A_\mu H_0}\le1.
\end{align*}
The corresponding transformed threshold for $v$ is
\[
 \frac{\mu r\gamma}{A_\mu H_0}\le\frac{\mu\gamma}{A_\mu}\le\gamma_0.
\]
Since \(v(0)=1\), it follows from the contrapositive of Corollary~\ref{cor:measure-point} that
\begin{equation} \label{v-density-oct-6}
 |\{v\le M\}\cap B_1|\ge\delta|B_1|.
\end{equation}
For every $x \in B_1$ such that \(v(x) \le M\), we have
\[
 u(x_0+\mu r x)
 \ge H_0((1-\mu)^{-\beta} -MA_\mu)
 =H_0\bigl(1-(M-1)A_\mu\bigr)
 \ge\frac{3H_0}{4}>\frac{H_0}{2}.
\]
Hence, we infer from \eqref{v-density-oct-6} that
\begin{equation} \label{measure-2-oct-5}
 |\{u>H_0/2\}\cap B_{\mu r}(x_0)|\ge\delta|B_1|(\mu r)^n.
\end{equation}
Since $2r\le1$ and $\beta\varepsilon\ge n$, it follows from the two measure bounds \eqref{measure-1-oct-5} and \eqref{measure-2-oct-5} that
\[
 \delta|B_1|\mu^n r^n
 \le 2^\varepsilon C_0 t^{-\varepsilon}(2r)^{\beta\varepsilon}
 \le 2^{n+\varepsilon}C_0t^{-\varepsilon}r^n.
\]
Hence
\[
 t\le\left(\frac{2^{n+\varepsilon}C_0}{\delta|B_1|\mu^n}\right)^{1/\varepsilon}.
\]
Since $u\le h_t$ in $B_1$, we have 
\[ \sup_{B_{1/2}}u\le2^\beta\max\{1,t\}\le C,\]
where $C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\mu_0)$. Note that when undoing the normalization, and letting $\zeta\downarrow0$, we obtain
\[
 \sup_{B_{1/2}}u
 \le C\max\left\{c+\inf_{B_{1/2}}u,\frac\gamma{\gamma_0}\right\}
 \le C\left(c+\inf_{B_{1/2}}u+\gamma\right),
\]
for $C = C(n, \lambda, \Lambda, \mu_0)>0$. This proves \eqref{harnack-local-B2}.

\smallskip
We now assume that $u$ satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:harnack-intro} in $B_1$. Let us denote
\[
 C_F=\|f\|_{L^\infty(B_1)}+\|F(0,0,\cdot)\|_{L^\infty(B_1)}.
\]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:extremal-reduction}, in the viscosity sense, $u$ satisfies
\[
 \mathcal M^-_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\le C_F,\qquad
 \mathcal M^+_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\ge-C_F\quad\text{in }B_1.
\]
Fix $z\in B_{1/2}$ and define $U_z(y)=u(z+y/4)$ for $y\in B_2$. Since $B_{1/2}(z)\subset B_1$, this function is well defined. Its corresponding threshold is $\gamma/4$, its exceptional set is $\Sigma/4$, and its drift coefficient is $\mu_0/4$. Its extremal inequalities have right-hand sides $C_F/16$ and $-C_F/16$. Increasing the drift coefficient to $\mu_0$ preserves both inequalities. Thus the estimate \eqref{harnack-local-B2} implies that
\begin{equation}\label{harnack-small-ball}
 \sup_{B_{1/8}(z)}u
 \le C_*\left(\inf_{B_{1/8}(z)}u+C_F+\gamma\right),
\end{equation}
where $C_*=C_*(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\mu_0)\ge1$ is independent of $z$.

\smallskip
Now, let $x,y\in B_{1/2}$ and 
\[
 z_i=y+\frac{i}{8}(x-y),\qquad i=0,\ldots,8.
\]
Then $z_i\in B_{1/2}$ and $|z_{i+1}-z_i|=|x-y|/8<1/8$. Applying \eqref{harnack-small-ball} with center $z_i$ gives
\[
 u(z_{i+1})\le C_*\bigl(u(z_i)+C_F+\gamma\bigr),
 \qquad i=0,\ldots,7.
\]
Iterating these eight inequalities, we obtain
\[
 u(x)\le C_*^8u(y)
       +(C_F+\gamma)\sum_{i=1}^{8}C_*^i
 \le C\bigl(u(y)+C_F+\gamma\bigr).
\]
Taking the supremum over $x\in B_{1/2}$ and the infimum over $y\in B_{1/2}$, we obtain the desired Harnack inequality in the theorem.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:extremal-reduction}, it follows that, in the viscosity sense, $u$ satisfies
\begin{equation} \label{Pucci-Oct-5}
 \mathcal M^-_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\le C_F, \quad \text{and}\quad \mathcal M^+_{\gamma,\Sigma,\mu_0}(D^2u,Du)\ge-C_F \quad \text{in} \quad B_1
\end{equation}
where
\[
C_F= \|f\|_{L^\infty(B_1)}+\|F(0,0,\cdot)\|_{L^\infty(B_1)}.
\]
We first derive a scale-invariant oscillation estimate. Fix $x_0\in B_{1/2}$ and $0<R\le1/2$, and write
\[
 M_R=\sup_{B_R(x_0)}u,\qquad m_R=\inf_{B_R(x_0)}u,\qquad \omega(R)=M_R-m_R.
\]
Put $r=R/2$ and we define in $B_{2r}(x_0)=B_R(x_0)$ the following two functions
\[
 v=u-m_R+r\gamma,\qquad w=M_R-u+r\gamma.
\]
The function $v$ is a viscosity solution for the same equation as $u$. On the other hand, $w$ is a viscosity solution of
\[
F^*(D^2w, Dw,x) = f^*(x) \quad \text{in} \quad B_R(x_0) \quad \text{when} \quad Dw \not \in (\bar{B}_\gamma \cup (-\Sigma))
\]
where
\[
 F^*(X,p,x)=-F(-X,-p,x), \quad \text{and} \quad f^*(x)=-f(x), \quad (X, p, x) \in \mathbb{S}^n \times \mathbb R^n \times B_1.
\]
The operator $F^*$ satisfies \eqref{F-cond} as $F$, and $-\Sigma$ is purely one-unrectifiable.

\smallskip
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:harnack-intro} to $v$ and $w$ in $B_r(x_0)$, after the scaling $x \mapsto x_0+ry$ with $y\in B_1$. The resulting Harnack estimates hold in $B_{r/2}(x_0)=B_{R/4}(x_0)$. We note that for $v$, the scaled operator is
\[
 F_r(X,p,y)=r^2F(r^{-2}X,r^{-1}p,x_0+ry),
\]
so its ellipticity constants are $\lambda,\Lambda$, its gradient coefficient is $r\mu_0\le\mu_0$, its corresponding threshold is $r\gamma$, and its corresponding right-hand-side bound in \eqref{Pucci-Oct-5} is $r^2C_F$. The same statements hold for the reflected operator associated with $w$. Since
\[
 \inf_{B_{r/2}(x_0)}v\ge r\gamma \quad \text{and} \quad \inf_{B_{r/2}(x_0)}w\ge r\gamma,
\]
the additive term $r\gamma$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:harnack-intro} is at most either infimum. Hence the two Harnack inequalities hold with a constant $C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\mu_0)>1$ such that
\[
 M_{R/4}-m_R+r\gamma\le C\bigl(m_{R/4}-m_R+r\gamma+r^2C_F\bigr),
\]
and
\[
 M_R-m_{R/4}+r\gamma\le C\bigl(M_R-M_{R/4}+r\gamma+r^2C_F\bigr).
\]
By adding these two inequalities, we obtain
\begin{equation*} 
 \omega(R)+\omega(R/4)+2r\gamma\le C\bigl(\omega(R)-\omega(R/4)+2r\gamma+2r^2C_F\bigr).
\end{equation*}
Therefore,
\begin{equation} \label{osc-R-oct-5}
 \omega(R/4)\le\theta\omega(R)+C_0(R\gamma+R^2C_F), \quad \text{with} \quad \theta=\frac{C-1}{C+1}<1,
\end{equation}
where $C_0$ depends only on $n,\lambda,\Lambda,\mu_0$.

\smallskip
Choose $\alpha\in(0,1)$ so small that $\theta<4^{-\alpha}$. Iterating the inequality \eqref{osc-R-oct-5} on the radii $R_j=4^{-j}R_0$, we infer that
\begin{equation} \label{omega-R-j-oct-5}
 \omega(R_j)\le\theta^j\omega(R_0)+C_0\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\theta^{j-1-i}\bigl(R_i\gamma+R_i^2C_F\bigr).
\end{equation}
Because $R_i=4^{-i}R_0$ and $4^\alpha\theta<1$, we have
\[
 \omega(R_j)\le C4^{-\alpha j}\bigl(\omega(R_0)+R_0\gamma+R_0^2C_F\bigr).
\]
Now, recall that $K=\|u\|_{L^\infty(B_1)}+C_F$. Note that $K=0$, then $u=0$ and the result is immediate. Hence, it remains to consider $K>0$. Let
\[
 R_0 =
\left\{
\begin{array}{ll} 
\frac14\min\left\{1,\frac K\gamma\right\} & \quad \text{if} \quad \gamma>0, \\
\frac14 & \quad \text{if} \quad \gamma =0.
\end{array} \right.
\]
Then $B_{R_0}(x_0)\subset B_1$ and
\[
 \omega(R_0)+R_0\gamma+R_0^2C_F\le CK.
\]
From this and \eqref{omega-R-j-oct-5},  it follows that
\[
 \omega(R_j)
 \le C4^{-\alpha j}
       \bigl(\omega(R_0)+R_0\gamma+R_0^2C_F\bigr)
 \le CK\left(\frac{R_j}{R_0}\right)^\alpha.
\]
Consequently, for $0<s\le R_0$, we choose $j$ so that $R_{j+1}<s\le R_j$ and obtain
\begin{equation} \label{omega-s-oct-5}
 \operatorname{osc}_{B_s(x_0)}u\le CK\left(\frac{s}{R_0}\right)^\alpha.
\end{equation}

\smallskip
Now, let $x,y\in B_{1/2}$ and $d=|x-y|$. If $d\le R_0/4$, we apply \eqref{omega-s-oct-5} for the ball $B_{4d}(x)$. Since $y\in B_{4d}(x)$, we infer that
\[
 |u(x)-u(y)|\le \operatorname{osc}_{B_{4d}(x)}u\le CK R_0^{-\alpha}d^\alpha.
\]
On the other hand, if $d>R_0/4$, we also have
\[
 |u(x)-u(y)|\le2\|u\|_{L^\infty(B_1)}\le CK R_0^{-\alpha}d^\alpha.
\]
Hence,
\[
|u(x)-u(y)|\le CK R_0^{-\alpha}d^\alpha, \quad \forall \ x, y \in B_{1/2},
\]
where $C= C(n, \lambda, \Lambda, \mu_0)>0$. Finally, from the choice of $R_0$, we have $R_0^{-\alpha}\le C(1+(\gamma/K)^\alpha)$, and therefore
\[
 [u]_{C^\alpha(B_{1/2})}
 \le CKR_0^{-\alpha}
 \le C\left(K+K^{1-\alpha}\gamma^\alpha\right).
\]
The proof is then complete.

Let \(\Sigma=\mathbb R e_1\). For every smooth function depending only on \(x_1\), the gradient lies in \(\Sigma\), hence they are viscosity solutions to considered class of fully nonlinear equation and both extremal inequalities. In particular, for each $k \in \mathbb{N}$, let 
\[
 u_k(x)=\sin(kx_1), \quad x = (x_1, x') \in \mathbb R \times \mathbb R^{n-1}, \quad |x| <1.
\]
We have
\[
Du_k(x)=k\cos(kx_1)e_1 \in \Sigma,
\qquad
\|Du_k\|_{L^\infty(B_1)}=k.
\]
Also, for \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), since \(|u_k|\le1\), for all \(x,y\in B_{1/2}\),
\[
|u_k(x)-u_k(y)|
\le \min\{k|x-y|,2\}
\le 2^{1-\alpha}k^\alpha|x-y|^\alpha.
\]
Consequently,
\[
[u_k]_{C^\alpha(B_{1/2})}
\le 2^{1-\alpha}k^\alpha.
\]
On the other hand, for \(k>\pi\), we choose
\[
x_k=\frac{\pi}{2k}e_1,
\quad \text{and} \quad
y_k=-\frac{\pi}{2k}e_1.
\]
Both points belong to \(B_{1/2}\), and moreover
\[
u_k(x_k)=1,
\quad
u_k(y_k)=-1,
\quad \text{and} \quad
|x_k-y_k|=\frac{\pi}{k},
\]
and then
\[
[u_k]_{C^\alpha(B_{1/2})}
\ge
\frac{|u_k(x_k)-u_k(y_k)|}{|x_k-y_k|^\alpha}
=
\frac{2}{\pi^\alpha}k^\alpha.
\]
Thus, for \(k>\pi\),
\[
\frac{2}{\pi^\alpha}k^\alpha
\le [u_k]_{C^\alpha(B_{1/2})}
\le 2^{1-\alpha}k^\alpha.
\]
In particular, the H\"older seminorm grows at order \(k^\alpha\),
and hence tends to infinity as \(k\to\infty\), despite
\(\|u_k\|_{L^\infty(B_1)}\le1\). As such, the H\"{o}lder estimate in Theorem \ref{thm:holder-intro} does not hold.

\]

\]
