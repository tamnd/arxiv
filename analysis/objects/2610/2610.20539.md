Define the local block-partition domain as
\begin{equation}
Z_{k,r}^{(q)}
=
S_k^{(q)}
\times
X_{k,r}.
\label{eq:local_block_partition_domain}

\begin{equation}

e_{k,r}(z)

\label{eq:residual_definition}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}

=
m_{k,r}(z)
-
B_{k,r}h_{k,r}^{(q)},
\qquad
z\in Z_{k,r}^{(q)},
\label{eq:lb_formulation_two}
\end{equation}

B_{k,r}

\rho_{k,r}B_{k,r}^{\mathrm{raw}}

\omega_{k,r}>0,
\label{eq:residual_safety_factor}
\end{equation}
$\rho_{k,r}\geq 1$ is a residual-slope safety factor,
$\omega_{k,r}>0$ is an additive residual safety margin, and

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:convergence}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{assump:exhaustive_branching},
\ref{assump:exhaustive_node_refinement}, and
\ref{assump:lb_consistency} hold. Suppose further that every accepted
partitionwise corrected model satisfies the deterministic validity condition
of Proposition~\ref{prop:formulation_two_deterministic}. Then the
residual-corrected two-level branch-and-bound algorithm is globally
convergent. In particular, if the algorithm terminates with
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{UB}^{\star}
-
\mathrm{LB}^{\star}
\leq
\epsilon,
\label{eq:global_termination}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{LB}^{\star}$ denotes the global lower bound over the active
nodes, then the incumbent solution $x^\star$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
f(x^\star)-f^\star
\leq
\epsilon,
\label{eq:epsilon_global_optimality}
\end{equation}
where $f^\star$ denotes the global optimal objective value.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop:formulation_two_deterministic}, every accepted
partitionwise corrected model is a valid lower bound on its corresponding
local domain. Proposition~\ref{prop:reduced_lb_validity} therefore implies
that every node lower bound is valid. Since the incumbent is obtained from a
feasible sampled point,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{LB}^{\star}
\leq
f^\star
\leq
\mathrm{UB}^{\star}
=
f(x^\star).
\label{eq:global_bound_relation}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the pruning condition
$\mathrm{LB}^{(q)}\geq\mathrm{UB}^{\star}$ cannot eliminate a node containing
a point that strictly improves the current incumbent.

Consider an infinite execution of the algorithm. If a node containing a
global minimizer is pruned, validity of its lower bound implies that the
current incumbent already attains the global optimal value. Otherwise, at
least one branch containing a global minimizer remains active. By
Assumption~\ref{assump:exhaustive_node_refinement}, any active regions that
continue to determine the global lower bound are eventually refined to
arbitrarily large tree levels. Assumption~\ref{assump:exhaustive_branching}
and Proposition~\ref{prop:local_domain_refinement} then imply that the
corresponding coupling and private-variable regions shrink to zero diameter.
By Assumption~\ref{assump:lb_consistency}, the associated lower bounds become
asymptotically tight with respect to the objective over these shrinking
regions. Since the node lower bounds remain valid, it follows that
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{LB}^{\star}\rightarrow f^\star.
\end{equation}

Along a nested sequence of refined regions containing a global minimizer, the
sampling construction provides feasible sampled points within the
corresponding regions. Since the diameters of these regions converge to zero,
a sequence of such feasible sampled points converges to a global minimizer.
By continuity of $f$, their objective values converge to $f^\star$. Since the
incumbent is the best feasible objective value obtained by the algorithm,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{UB}^{\star}\rightarrow f^\star.
\end{equation}
Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{UB}^{\star}
-
\mathrm{LB}^{\star}
\rightarrow0,
\end{equation}
which establishes global convergence. Finally, if the algorithm terminates according to
\eqref{eq:global_termination}, then
\begin{equation}
f(x^\star)-f^\star
=
\mathrm{UB}^{\star}-f^\star
\leq
\mathrm{UB}^{\star}-\mathrm{LB}^{\star}
\leq
\epsilon.
\label{eq:epsilon_optimality_proof}
\end{equation}
Therefore, $x^\star$ is an $\epsilon$-global optimizer.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Fix $z^i\in\mathcal Z^{\mathrm{fit}}$ and
$j\in\mathcal N(i)$. Since the residual gradient is
$H$-Lipschitz, the $C^{1,1}$ Taylor bound gives
\begin{equation}
\left|

\right|

\label{eq:app_residual_taylor}
\end{equation}

\eqref{eq:neighbor_direction} and \eqref{eq:raw_residual_slope} yields
\begin{equation}

\nabla e(z^i)^\top u_{ij}
\right|
\le
B^{\mathrm{raw}}
+
\frac{H}{2}\|z^j-z^i\|
\le
B^{\mathrm{raw}}
+
\frac{H}{2}d_{\mathcal N}.
\label{eq:app_directional_derivative_bound}
\end{equation}

If $\nabla e(z^i)\neq0$, let
\[
v_i
=
\frac{\nabla e(z^i)}
     {\|\nabla e(z^i)\|}.
\]
By the definition of $\alpha_i$, there exists
$j\in\mathcal N(i)$ such that
\[
|v_i^\top u_{ij}|
\ge
\alpha_i.
\]
Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\alpha_i\|\nabla e(z^i)\|
\le
B^{\mathrm{raw}}
+
\frac{H}{2}d_{\mathcal N}.
\end{equation}
Since $\kappa_i=1/\alpha_i$ and $\kappa_i\le\kappa$,
\begin{equation}
\|\nabla e(z^i)\|
\le
\kappa
\left(
B^{\mathrm{raw}}
+
\frac{H}{2}d_{\mathcal N}
\right),
\end{equation}
which also holds trivially when $\nabla e(z^i)=0$. This proves
\eqref{eq:sample_gradient_bound}.

Now fix any $z\in Z$. By the definition of the fill distance, there exists
a fitting point $z^i\in\mathcal Z^{\mathrm{fit}}$ such that
\[
\|z-z^i\|\le h.
\]
Applying the $C^{1,1}$ Taylor bound at $z^i$ gives
\begin{equation}
e(z)
\ge
e(z^i)
+
\nabla e(z^i)^\top(z-z^i)
-
\frac{H}{2}\|z-z^i\|^2.
\label{eq:app_residual_lower_taylor}
\end{equation}
Since $e(z^i)\ge0$ by
\eqref{eq:nonnegative_fitting_residual},
\begin{align}
e(z)
&\ge
-
\|\nabla e(z^i)\|\,\|z-z^i\|
-
\frac{H}{2}\|z-z^i\|^2
\\
&\ge
-
\kappa
\left(
B^{\mathrm{raw}}
+
\frac{H}{2}d_{\mathcal N}
\right)h
-
\frac{H}{2}h^2.
\end{align}
This proves \eqref{eq:global_residual_bound}.
