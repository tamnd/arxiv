<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:known}
Compare degree \(m=n+1\) in Theorem~4 of~\cite{MMMRP2026}
and association order zero in the elimination of
\cite[Theorem~6.3, p.~84; Theorem~6.4, p.~87]{F1998}.
Suppose the polynomial
coordinate is cyclic of rank four in their structure system,
and both scalar constructions have nonzero fourth-order leading
coefficients. Their monic normalizations in powers of \(T\)
are identical over \(\mathbb C(y)\).
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The determinant of \(\Phi_n\) is a nonzero constant, by the
three-term recurrence. Taking determinants in
\eqref{eq:new-system} therefore gives the coefficient identity
\[
 \det(aI+sM_n)=\det(aI+sC)=:\delta.
\]
Regularity excludes rational \(S\), whose Hankel rank would be
finite. In the rearranged identity
\[
 TS=\frac{(a+sC_1)S+sD}{-sBS+a-sC_2}
\]
the denominator cannot vanish, since \(B\not\equiv0\).
If \(\delta=0\), the fractional linear map is constant over
\(\mathbb C(y)\), making \(TS\), hence \(S\), rational.
Thus \(\delta\not\equiv0\).

For a two-by-two matrix \(X\),
\(\operatorname{adj}(aI+sX)=aI+s\operatorname{adj}X\).
Adjugation of \eqref{eq:new-system} thus gives
\begin{equation}
 a\Delta\Phi_n=U_n(T\Phi_n)+\Phi_n V,\quad
 U_n=-\operatorname{adj}M_n,\quad V=\operatorname{adj}C.
 \label{eq:old-system}
\end{equation}
The thesis's sign \(-S\)
\cite[Definition~4.1, p.~61, Eq.~(4.1)]{F1998} and \(\gamma_0=1\)
give the same \(V\) in its Riccati equation (6.1), p.~77.
Its Theorem~6.2, p.~82, identifies the left matrix with \(U_n\),
since \(T\Phi_n\) is invertible. Hence \eqref{eq:old-system}
is its (6.29)--(6.32), with dictionary
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 (G_0,E_0,F_0,H_0)&=(-B,C_1,C_2,-D),\\[7pt]
 (E_{n+1},F_{n+1},H_{n+1})&=(L_n,-\ell,-\Theta_n),\quad
 H_n/\gamma_n=-p.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:dictionary}
\end{equation}
The same four relations are published in
\cite[Lemmas~2--3, p.~453, Eqs.~(25)--(28)]{FHR2001}.
The identity \(F_{n+1}=-\ell\) also agrees with its recurrence
(6.5), p.~78. Since \(\delta\ne0\), adjugation is reversible:
the two systems define the same linear shift map for
\emph{every formal matrix state}, not only for \(\Phi_n\).

At degree \(m=n+1\), the old elimination uses the lower-left
coordinate at index \(m\), the new one the upper-left at
\(m-1\). The recurrence gauge is
\[
 \Phi_m=W_m\Phi_{m-1},\quad
 W_m=\begin{pmatrix}y-\beta_m&-\gamma_m\\[7pt]1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since \(\det W_m=\gamma_m\ne0\), compatibility (45)
in~\cite{MMMRP2026},
\(a\Delta W_m=M_mW_m-(TW_m)M_{m-1}\),
and the product rule show that \(X\mapsto W_mX\) gauges every
formal state from index \(m-1\) to \(m\). Its lower-left
coordinate is the old upper-left one, so both eliminations give
universal scalar relations for the same observable. For 1998 we
execute (6.51)--(6.53), p.~87, rather than copying the expanded
list.\footnote{The list below (6.52), p.~87, prints
\(b_2(Tb_2)(Ta_1)\) in the \(T^3\) coefficient instead of
\(b_2(Tb_2)(T^2a_1)\). The publication gives the shift instructions
\cite[Eqs.~(43)--(44), p.~455]{FHR2001} without that list.
The pairs with coefficients (6.40), p.~84, follow from (6.43)--(6.46),
p.~85, by multiplication and substitution, without dividing by
\(K_3\) or \(K_7\); hence they remain valid for every formal state
when an intermediate divisor vanishes.}

Vectorize the common system as \(Tz=H(y)z\), with polynomial
coordinate \(f=(1,0,0,0)z\). Set
\begin{equation}
 h_0=(1,0,0,0),\quad h_{j+1}=(Th_j)H,\quad
 \mathcal C=\begin{pmatrix}h_0\\[7pt]h_1\\[7pt]h_2\\[7pt]h_3\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:cyclic}
\end{equation}
Since \(T^jf=h_jz\) and \(\det\mathcal C\not\equiv0\), the
first four observations determine every state, giving the unique
monic universal relation
\[
 L_*=T^4+\sum_{j=0}^3b_jT^j,\quad
 (b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3)=-h_4\mathcal C^{-1}.
\]
Two distinct such relations would differ by one of order at most three,
contradicting independence of \(h_0,\ldots,h_3\). For their
nonzero leading coefficients \(a_4,b_4\) in powers of \(T\),
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1}{a_4}\mathcal O_{26}=\frac{1}{b_4}\mathcal O_{98}=L_*.
 \label{eq:equivalence}
\end{equation}
Coefficients multiply from the left. This identifies entire
operators, not all equations annihilating one polynomial.
Triangular conversion between \(T\) and \(\Delta\), with
nonzero leading factors, preserves the equivalence.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:degrees}
For a normalized regular input satisfying the Riccati identity
\eqref{eq:riccati}, with \(a\not\equiv0\) and \(B\) allowed to
vanish, put
\[
 u=a-sC_2,\quad v=C_1+C_2,\quad
 t=\max\{\deg u-2,\deg B-2,\deg v-1\}.
\]
For every \(n\geq0\), the polynomial structure coefficients are
uniquely determined by the fixed input and satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \deg\Theta_n&\leq t,\\[7pt]
 \deg(L_n-C_1)&\leq
 \max\{\deg(a+sC_1)-1,\deg B-1,\deg\Theta_n-1\},\\[7pt]
 \deg(L_n+C_2)&\leq
 \max\{\deg u-1,\deg D+1,\deg\Theta_n-1\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:degree-bounds}
\end{equation}
If \(t\geq0\), write \([y^j]f\) for the coefficient of \(y^j\)
in \(f\), put \([j]_q=(q^j-1)/(q-1)\),
\(\alpha=[y^{t+2}]u\), \(b=[y^{t+2}]B\),
\(e=[y^{t+1}]v\). Then
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 [y^t]\Theta_n
 &=-\gamma_{n+1}q^{-n-2}
 \big\{\alpha[2n+3]_q+b(1+q^{2n+2})+eq^{2n+3}\big\},\\[7pt]
 [y^{t+1}](L_n+C_2)
 &=\alpha[n+1]_q+q^n(b+qe).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:top-coefficients}
\end{equation}
The corresponding degrees are \(t\) and \(t+1\) exactly
when these coefficients are nonzero; otherwise they are at most
\(t-1\) and \(t\), respectively. Each top coefficient can vanish
at at most one index, unless it vanishes for every \(n\).
Uniform cancellation of the top coefficient is equivalent to
\(b=\alpha/(q-1)\), \(e=-(1+q^{-1})b\) for \(\Theta_n\),
and to \(\alpha=0\), \(b+qe=0\) for \(L_n+C_2\).

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Write \(P=P_{n+1}\), \(R=R_n\), and
\(\kappa=RP_n-PR_{n-1}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_j\ne0\),
with \(\kappa=1\) at \(n=0\).
The old structure identities
\cite[Theorem~6.2, p.~82, Eqs.~(6.29)--(6.32)]{F1998}
give the coefficient dictionary \eqref{eq:dictionary} by
adjugation, which requires no division by the transfer
determinant for the actual polynomial matrix. The constant
Casoratian \(\kappa\) makes the pair \((L_n,\Theta_n)\) unique.
Initial recurrences (6.4), (6.6), p.~78, give the same
identification at \(n=0\).
Set \(\eta=S-R/P\). The tail and remainder identities
\cite[Lemmas~6.1--6.2, p.~81, Eqs.~(6.21)--(6.22)]{F1998}, together with the
Casoratian (2.24), p.~23, give
\[
 \eta=\kappa\gamma_{n+1}y^{-2n-3}+O(y^{-2n-4}),\quad
 \Delta\eta=O(y^{-2n-4}).
\]
The old coefficient residual (6.27), p.~82, becomes
\begin{equation}
 \kappa\Theta_n=P(TP)
 \big[u\Delta\eta-v\eta
 -B\{S(T\eta)+\eta(TS)-\eta(T\eta)\}\big].
 \label{eq:degree-residual}
\end{equation}
This uses only \(T\eta=\eta+s\Delta\eta\) to collect the
linear terms. Since \(S=O(y^{-1})\) and
\(\deg P(TP)=2n+2\), its three groups have orders at most
\(\deg u-2\), \(\deg v-1\), and \(\deg B-2\).
The left side is polynomial, proving the first bound.
The other two follow by comparing degrees in the old
structures, rewritten as
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (a+sC_1)\Delta P&=(L_n-C_1)P+\Theta_nP_n-B(TR),\\[7pt]
 u\Delta R&=(L_n+C_2)R+\Theta_nR_{n-1}+D(TP).
 \end{aligned}
\]
At \(n=0\), \(R=1\) and \(R_{-1}=0\), so the same bounds
hold.
The reduced input \(u\Delta S=B S(TS)+vS+D\) gives
\(\deg D\leq t\), hence \(\deg(L_n+C_2)\leq t+1\).
Leading terms in the residual and the first structure give
\eqref{eq:top-coefficients}; after removing nonzero prefactors,
these are affine in \(q^{2n+3}\) and \(q^n\), respectively.
Since \(0<q<1\), the cancellation criteria follow.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:zero-normalization}
Fix \(n\geq1\), and put \(r=q^n\), \(d=q-1\).
The family \eqref{eq:family} is positive and regular, and the
data \eqref{eq:example-input} satisfy the hypotheses of
Theorem~4 of~\cite{MMMRP2026}, with no common input factor.
Nevertheless, all five prescribed coefficients vanish:
\(\mathcal O_0=0\). The polynomial coordinate of the structure
system is cyclic of rank four, and its unique monic universal
fourth-order relation is given by \(L\) in \eqref{eq:old-L}.

For every real \(\varepsilon\), the perturbation
\(J_\varepsilon=J+\varepsilon e_0e_0^*\) has a positive regular
spectral measure and Riccati data \eqref{eq:perturbation}.
Every coefficient of its prescribed operator
\(\mathcal O_\varepsilon\) is divisible by \(\varepsilon^2\),
and the leading coefficient has exactly that order of vanishing.
Division before specialization gives
\[
 \left.\frac{\mathcal O_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2}
 \right|_{\varepsilon=0}
 =q^{36}r^5d^{12}y^{20}(q^4r^2-1)L\ne0.
\]
This is the same scalar equation, up to a nonzero rational factor,
as the 1998 shift-and-eliminate procedure executed with the
correct shifts, and its published 2001 counterpart.
For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\ne0\), cyclic rank is
four and both scalar constructions have nonzero fourth-order
leading coefficients. Thus Proposition~\ref{prop:known}'s domain
is nonempty.
