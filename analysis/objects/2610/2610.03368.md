<!-- customthm-1: body_md -->
{\ref{toricboundreg}}
    Over an algebraically closed field, a toric ideal $P_A$ with multiplicity $e$ satisfies $$\operatorname{reg}(P_A) \leq e^{e}(e-1).$$
<!-- end customthm-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The previous inequality is false if $k$ is not algebraically closed (\cite{caviglia2026quadraticlinearstrandsprime}). Let $R = \mathbb{R}[x_j, ix_j : 1 \leq j \leq h]$ with $i^2 = 1$ the imaginary unit. Let $S = \mathbb{R}[y_j, z_j : 1 \leq j \leq h]$ and let $P$ be the kernel of the map $S \to R$, $y_j \mapsto x_j$, $z_j \mapsto ix_j$. Then $P$ is a height $h$ homogeneous prime and the multiplicity of $S/P$ is $2$. Thus, the height can be arbitrarily larger than the multiplicity.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
If we assume that $I = P$ is prime and $k$ is algebraically closed, then \eqref{pdreg} holds for $n = 1, 2$ for trivial reasons. If $n = 1$, then $P$ is either $(0)$ or a linear form. If $n = 2$, then $P$ is (0), principal, or the homogeneous maximal ideal.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
A $c$-nomial ideal need not contain a regular sequence of polynomials with at most $c$ terms, nor the converse. For  
    example, if $\mathfrak{m} \subseteq k[x,y,z]$ is the homogeneous maximal ideal, then the ideal $(y^2-xz) + \mathfrak{m}^3$ contains the regular sequence $x^3, y^3, z^3$, but is not a monomial ideal under any change of variables.
    
    Conversely, toric ideals need not contain a maximal regular sequence consisting of 2-nomials. To see this, first notice that any 2-nomial in a toric ideal $P_A$ can be written as a scalar times a binomial $u - v$ for $u,v$ monomials. Because $P_A$ contains no monomials, if $a$ is a monomial and $a(u - v)$ is a 2-nomial appearing in a regular sequence, we can replace it with $u - v$. That is, any regular sequence of $2$-nomials can be replaced by one of the form $u_1 - v_1, \dots, u_l - v_l$ for $u_i, v_i$ coprime monomials. However, toric ideals of high enough codimension do not have maximal regular sequences of this form (\cite{Cattanicompleteintersections}). For example, the toric ideal of $$A = \begin{bmatrix}
        1 & 1 & \dots & 1\\
        1 & 2 & \dots & 14\\
        1 & 2^2 & \dots & 14^2\\
        \vdots& &\ddots  &\vdots\\
        1 & 2^{10} & \dots & 14^{10}
    \end{bmatrix}$$
    is height 3, but contains no binomial regular sequence of length 3, and hence no $2$-nomial regular sequence of length 3.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{controlledregseq}
    Let $S$ be a polynomial ring over an algebraically closed field $k$, and let $P \subseteq S$ a homogeneous prime ideal of multiplicity $e \geq 2$. Fix a minimal homogeneous generating set of $P$, and assume that the generators of degree at least 2 each have at most $c$ terms. Let $L$ be the sequence of linear forms, if any, appearing in that generating set. Then there exist variables $x_{i_1}, \dots, x_{i_b}$ with $b \leq c\frac{e(e^{e}-1)}{e-1}$ and a homogeneous maximal regular sequence $L, f_{1}, \dots, f_{h} \in P$ with each $f_i$ a degree $e$ form in $k[x_{i_1}, \dots, x_{i_b}]$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $l = |L|$. Let $\overline{P}$ be the image of $P$ in $S/L$ and let $h = \operatorname{ht}(\overline{P})$. As $\overline{P}$ is a nondegenerate prime of the same multiplicity as $P$, $h \leq e-1$ and there is a maximal regular sequence of degree $e$ in $\overline{P}$, and hence in $P$ as well. We construct degree $e$ forms $f_1, \dots, f_h$ such that $L, f_{1}, \dots, f_h$ is a maximal regular sequence recursively.  Because $e \ge 2$, we have $h \geq 1$. Fix $B$ the set of degree $e$ forms obtained by multiplying the minimal generators of degree at least 2 by monomials which are not in $(L)$. If $h = 1$, then let $f_1$ be any element of $B$.
    
    Now, suppose that we have constructed $L, f_{1}, \dots, f_{m}$ with $m < h$. Let $P_1, \dots, P_s$ be the associated primes of $(L, f_{1}, \dots, f_{m})$. Because this is a homogeneous regular sequence, $P_1, \dots, P_s$ all have height $l + m$ (in particular, they are all minimal). The multiplicity of a regular sequence is given by the product of the degrees, which in this case is $e^m$, and each minimal prime contributes to the multiplicity according to the additivity formula (see \cite[Ch. V \S 2]{Serre_2000}). Thus, $s \leq e^m$. Let $V$ be the vector space of degree $e$ forms of $P$, and $W_i$ the space of degree $e$ forms of $P_i$. A basis for $V$ can be obtained from the set of generators of $P$ times monomials of the appropriate degree. Because $P$ has a regular sequence of length $h + l > m + l$ in degree $e$, we know that $V \not\subseteq W_i$. By the avoidance lemma \eqref{avoidancelemma}, there is a \begin{equation}
        f_{m+1} = \sum a_ib_i \in \left(P \setminus \bigcup_{i=1}^s P_s\right), \label{regseqrep}
    \end{equation} where $a_i \in k$ and each $b_i$ a basis element of $V$ avoiding $P_i$. Because each $P_i$ contains $L$, the set of monomials times $L$ is contained in $P_i$, so that each $b_i$ is not in $(L)$ i.e. it is in $B$. 

    We now consider the number of variables used in $f_1, \dots, f_h$, recalling that $h \leq e-1$. Each $f_{m}$ is a sum of at most $e^{m-1}$ polynomials of degree $e$ with support of size at most $c$. Thus, at most $ce^m$ variables occur in $f_{m}$. As such, the elements $f_{1}, \dots, f_{h}$ use at most $$ce + ce^2 + \dots + ce^{h} = c\frac{e(e^{h+1}-1)}{e-1} \leq c\frac{e(e^{e}-1)}{e-1}$$ variables.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
Suppose in Lemma \ref{controlledregseq} that $L = \emptyset$. Examine the new variables added by $f_{m+1}$ as in \eqref{regseqrep}. For each minimal prime $P_i$, we obtain a $c$-nomial $b_i \notin P_i$. If there is a term of some $b_j$ not divisible by any variable appearing in $f_1, \dots, f_m$, then $b_j$ will avoid all of the minimal primes, so we can take $f_{m+1} = b_j$. Otherwise, each term of each $b_i$ contains a variable already appearing in $f_1, \dots, f_m$. In this case $f_{m+1}$ is a sum of at most $e^{m}$ polynomials of degree $e$ with at most $c$ elements in the support, and each summand adds at most $c(e-1)$ variables. Thus, in the worst case at most $ce^m(e-1)$ new variables occur in $f_{m+1}$. As such, the elements $f_{1}, \dots, f_{h}$ use at most \begin{equation}\label{nondegenbound}
        ce + ce(e-1) + \dots + ce^{h-1}(e-1) = ce^{h} \leq ce^{e-1}
    \end{equation} variables.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- cor-1: body_md -->
\label{pdbound}
    In the notation of Lemma \ref{controlledregseq}, if $P$ is nondegenerate, then the projective dimension of $P$ is at most  $ce^{e-1} - 1$.
<!-- end cor-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Because $P$ is a minimal prime of $(f_{1}, \dots, f_h)$, we have $P \subseteq k[x_{i_1}, \dots, x_{i_b}]$. By  \eqref{nondegenbound}, $b \leq ce^{e-1}$. The result follows from Hilbert's Syzygy Theorem and the Auslander Buchbaum formula, noting that $P$ has nonzero depth.

<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
Over an algebrically closed field, a homogeneosu $c$-nomial prime ideal $P$ with multiplicity $e$ satisfies \[\operatorname{reg}(P) \leq (e-1)^{2^{b-2}+1}\] where $b = c\frac{e(e^{e}-1)}{e-1}$ if $P$ is degenerate, or $b = ce^{e-1}$ if $P$ is nondegenerate.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $L, f_1, \dots, f_h$ be the maximal regular sequence in $P$ given in Lemma \ref{controlledregseq}. If an $l \in L$ uses a variable not appearing in the elements $f_1, \dots, f_h$, say $x_i$, then quotienting by $l$ amounts to substituting out the variable $x_i$, and so does not change the way the elements $f_1, \dots, f_h$ are written, nor the multiplicity or regularity of $P$. Therefore, we may assume that every form in $L$ uses only variables appearing in $f_1, \dots, f_h$ i.e. only the variables $x_{i_1}, \dots, x_{i_b}$. Under this assumption, $P \subseteq k[x_{i_1}, \dots, x_{i_b}]$. We now quotient by the remaining elements in $L$, and assume that $P \neq (0)$ is a nondegenerate prime in a polynomial ring $S$ in at most $b$ variables over $k$. Let $f$ be a degree $h(e-1)$ polynomial such that $P = (f_1, \dots, f_h) : f$, as in \cite{chardin_2000}. The short exact sequence \[0 \to S/P(-h(e-1)) \xrightarrow{\cdot f} S/(f_1, \dots, f_h) \to S/(f_1, \dots, f_h, f) \to 0\] yields that the regularity of $S/P(-h(e-1))$ is bounded above by the max of the regularity of $S/(f_1, \dots, f_h)$, which is $h(e-1)$, and the regularity of $S/(f_1, \dots, f_h, f)$ plus 1. By \eqref{pdreg} this at most $(h(e-1))^{2^{b-2}}.$ Thus, $h \leq (e-1)$ implies \[\operatorname{reg}(P) \leq  (h(e-1))^{2^{b-2}} - h(e-1) + 1 \leq (e-1)^{2^{b-2}+1}.\]
