<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\cite[Theorem 1.1]{Brauer}
Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}$ given by an integral Weierstrass equation. Let $P\in E(\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}})$ have infinite order with $P \in E(\ensuremath{\mathbb{R}})^{0}$, where $E(\ensuremath{\mathbb{R}})^{0}$ denotes the connected component of the identity of $E(\ensuremath{\mathbb{R}})$. Then there exists $\omega=\omega(E, P)>0$ such that 
\[
\#\{n \in \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}\colon |n|\leq B, y(nP) \text{ is a sum of two squares}\} \ll_{E, P} \frac{B}{\left(\log B\right)^{\omega}}. 
\]

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{Main ideal norm result}
Let $E/K$ be an elliptic curve over a number field $K$ given by an integral Weierstrass equation, and suppose that $E$ has complex multiplication by an order $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ in the imaginary quadratic field $F\subseteq K$. Let $L/K$ be a finite abelian extension with conductor $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{f}}=\mathfrak{f}_{L/K}$.

Let $P\in E(K)$ be a non-torsion point and $D_{\alpha}$ be the elliptic denominator sequence associated to $P$, to be defined in \Cref{denominator sequence def}. If there exists $\alpha_0\in\mathcal{O}$ with $\alpha_0 \in (\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}$, where $\pi \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ is defined in \Cref{Lambda estimate}, such that $(D_{\alpha_0}, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{f}})=1$ and the image of $D_{\alpha_0}$ under the Artin map $\operatorname{Art}_{L/K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{f}}} \colon I_{K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{f}}} \longrightarrow \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ is nontrivial, then there exists $\omega=\omega(E,P)>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
\#\left\{
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}\colon |\alpha|\leq B,\;
y(\alpha P)\text{ is an ideal norm from }L
\right\}
\ll_{E,P,L}
\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\omega-\varepsilon}},
\]
where the implicit constant has dependence on $P$ of the form $\exp\left(
O_{E,L}\left(\widehat h(P)/\log\widehat h(P)\right)\right)$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->

Similarly, the result by Bhakta et al.\ \cite[Theorem 1.7]{Brauer} includes a technical condition, namely $P \in E(\mathbb{R})^{0}$, which is necessary for a similar reason: the image of the denominator ideal under the Dirichlet character (Kronecker symbol) is nontrivial. For comparison and a more detailed explanation, we refer the reader to the proofs of \cite[Theorem 1.6, Proposition 3.9]{Brauer} and compare it with \Cref{Lambda estimate}.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{Streng module}
For any $n\geq 0$, the group $E_{n}(K_\nu)$ is a $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}[\omega]$--submodule of $E(K_\nu)$. Moreover, for $n \geq 1$, we have an isomorphism of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--modules
\[
E_{n}(K_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}})/E_{n+1}(K_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}})\cong l, 
\]
where $l=\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_{K_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}}/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ is the residue field of $K_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For $n=0$, see \cite[Theorem 2.13]{Edison1}; for $n\geq 1$, see \cite[Corollary 2.10]{Streng}.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{valuation}
Let $P \in E_1(K)$ and $\alpha \in \operatorname{End}(E)$, then $\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}\left([\alpha]P\right) \geq \nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(\alpha)$, with equality if $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \nmid \alpha\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
From \cite[Corollary 2.10]{Streng}, we know that there is an isomorphism of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--modules $E_i(K)/E_{i+1}(K) \cong \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$, so $E_i(K)/E_{i+1}(K)$ is a residue field of size $\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})$. If $\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(\alpha\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K)=0$, then $[\alpha]$ acts as an automorphism in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$. Otherwise, $[\alpha]$ acts as multiplication by $0$ in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$. This proves the lemma.
<!-- end proof-2 -->

One should note that $N_{L/K}(I_L^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}})$ is a subgroup of the Takagi group $T_{L/K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$, therefore the use of group characters to perform the elliptic sieve will result an upper bound that is not sharp. However, we note that this is not the main source of error.

<!-- defn-2: body_md -->
\label{denominator sequence def}
Let $P=(x, y)$ be a non-torsion point in $E(K)$. For $\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\operatorname{End}(E)$, we define the sequence of ideals $D_{\alpha}$ in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$, indexed by elements of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, by 
\begin{equation}
x\left([\alpha]P\right)\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_{K}=A_{\alpha}D_{\alpha}^{-2},
\end{equation}
where $A_{\alpha}$ and $B_{\alpha}$ are coprime $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_{K}$--ideals. If $K$ has class number $1$, we instead define the sequence of elements $B_{\alpha}$, indexed by elements of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, where $D_{\alpha}=\left(B_{\alpha}\right)$. We will call $\left(D_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}}$ and $\left(B_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}}$ elliptic denominator sequences (of ideals and elements respectively).

Let $M$ be an element in the ideal
\[
\mathfrak{M}(P)=\operatorname{lcm}\{\operatorname{ann}(P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}) \colon \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \text{ prime }\},
\]
where $\operatorname{ann}(P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})$ is the annihilator of $P$ in the $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--module $E(K_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}})/E_{0}(K_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}})$. Let $\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$ be elements of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\operatorname{End}(E)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ is a principal ideal domain and two of $\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$ are multiples of $M$, the generator of $\mathfrak{M}(P)=(M)$, then the sequence of elements $\left(B_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}}$ can be chosen to satisfy
\begin{equation}
B_{\alpha+\beta}B_{\alpha-\beta}B_{\gamma}^2=B_{\alpha+\gamma}B_{\alpha-\gamma}B_{\beta}^2-B_{\beta+\gamma}B_{\beta-\gamma}B_{\alpha}^2.
\end{equation}
\item If $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ is not a principal ideal domain and each of $\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$ is either a multiple of $M$, or coprime to $M$, then there is a sequence of ideals $\{\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}}$ in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$ with 
\begin{align}
D_{\alpha}&=\psi_{\alpha}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}^{-1}, \\
\psi_{\alpha+\beta}\psi_{\alpha-\beta}\psi_{\gamma}^2 &= \psi_{\alpha+\gamma}\psi_{\alpha-\gamma}\psi_{\beta}^2 - \psi_{\beta+\gamma}\psi_{\beta-\gamma}\psi_{\alpha}^2, \\
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+\beta}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha-\beta}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}^2 &= \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+\gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha-\gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}^2 = \mathfrak{g}_{\beta+\gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta-\gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}^2.
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}

Let $P=(x, y)$ be a non-torsion point in $E(K)$. Let $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{a}}$ be an ideal in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$. An elliptic denominator sequence indexed by ideals $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{a}}$ of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, associated to $P$, is given by

D_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{a}}}=\sum_{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathfrak{a}}}D_{\alpha}.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{divisibility}
Let $\alpha, \beta$ be elements in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, $\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}$ be ideals in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ and $\alpha \in \mathfrak{a}, \beta \in \mathfrak{b}$.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Weak divisibility: for all $\alpha, \beta \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, if $\alpha \mid \beta$, then $D_{\alpha} \mid D_{\beta}$.
\item Strong divisibility: $D_{\mathfrak{a}+ \mathfrak{b}}=D_{\mathfrak{a}}+ D_\mathfrak{b}$. 
\end{enumerate}

See \cite[Lemmata 3.2, 3.3]{Streng}.

By weak divisibility, we have $D_{\alpha}=D_{\alpha\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}}$ for all $\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$. It is also worth emphasising that when we state the strong divisibility property, one must refer to an ideal--indexed sequence.

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{symmetry law}
Let $M$ be as in \Cref{CM EDS}. Let $\alpha, r \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ with $r$ being a multiple of $M$. Let $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ be a prime in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$ and $k \in \ensuremath{\mathbb{N}}$. Suppose that $K$ is a class number $1$ number field and $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k$ divides $B_{r}/\operatorname{gcd}(B_r, B_M)$, then for all $x+y\tau \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau]$,
\[
B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}\equiv \begin{cases}
			 U^{\frac{x(x-1)}{2}}V^{\frac{y(y-1)}{2}}
\left(\frac{B_{\alpha+r}}{B_\alpha}\right)^{x}
\left(\frac{B_{\alpha+r\tau}}{B_\alpha}\right)^{y}
\left(\frac{B_{\alpha}B_{\alpha+(1+\tau)r}}
{B_{\alpha+r}B_{\alpha+r\tau}}\right)^{xy}
B_{\alpha} \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k, & \text{if $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k \nmid B_\alpha$}\\
            0 \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}, & \text{if $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k \mid B_{\alpha}$},
		 \end{cases}
\]
where
\[
U=\frac{B_{M+r}B_{M-r}}{B_M^2},
\qquad
V=\frac{B_{M+r\tau}B_{M-r\tau}}{B_M^2}
\]
and all the quotients in the first case are $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$--adic units.
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Note that for any $x+y\tau \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, we have $((\alpha)+(x+y\tau)r)+(r)=(\alpha)+(r)$, so strong divisibility (\Cref{divisibility}) immediately implies that 
\begin{equation}\label{gcdd}
\gcd(B_{\alpha}, B_{r})=\gcd(B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}, B_{r})
\end{equation}
for every $x+y\tau \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$. 

Therefore, if $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k$ divides $B_{\alpha}$, then we are done, so assume not. 

It is obvious that $r \vert r\tau$, so for $a \equiv b \bmod r\tau$, we have $a \equiv b \bmod B_{r\tau}$ and hence $a\equiv b \bmod B_r$.

Using the recurrence relation and if we replace $m$ by $M$ and $n$ by $\alpha+(x+y\tau)r$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{RR1}
B_{M+r}B_{M-r}B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}^2 \equiv B_{\alpha+(x+1+y\tau)r}B_{\alpha+(x-1+y\tau)r}B_{M}^2 \bmod B_{r}^2.
\end{equation}
Similarly, replacing $r$ by $r\tau$, $m$ by $M$ and $\alpha$ by $\alpha+(x+y\tau)$, we also obtain
\begin{equation}\label{RR2}
B_{M+r\tau}B_{M-r\tau}B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}^2 \equiv B_{\alpha+(x+(1+y)\tau)r}B_{\alpha+(x+(y-1)\tau)r}B_{M}^2 \bmod B_{r}^2.
\end{equation}
Let 
\begin{equation}\label{a_l}
U=\frac{B_{M+r}B_{M-r}}{B_M^2}, V=\frac{B_{M+r\tau}B_{M-r\tau}}{B_M^2}, a_{x+y\tau}=\frac{B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}}{\gcd(B_{\alpha}, B_{r})}.
\end{equation}

By weak divisibility (\Cref{divisibility}), the assumption of $r$ being a multiple of $M$ implies that $U$ and $V$ are both elements in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$. Recall from (\ref{gcdd}) that $\gcd(B_{\alpha}, B_{r})=\gcd(B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}, B_{r})$, so $a_{x+y\tau}$ is an element in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$ coprime with $B_r/\gcd(B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}, B_{r})$ and this allows us to divide (\ref{RR1}) by $B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}^2B_{M}^2$, we get 
\[
U \equiv \frac{a_{x+1+y\tau}a_{x-1+y\tau}}{a_{x+y\tau}^2} \bmod \frac{B_r^2}{\gcd(B_{\alpha}B_{M}, B_r)^2},
\]
where every $a_{x+y\tau}$ is coprime to the modulus. If we now fix the value of $y$ and induct on $x$, then we obtain 
\[
a_{x+y\tau}\equiv U^{\frac{x(x-1)}{2}}a_{1+y\tau}^xa_{y\tau}^{1-x} \bmod \frac{B_r^2}{\gcd(B_{\alpha}B_{M}, B_r)^2}.
\]

We now do the same for (\ref{RR2}): divide both sides by $B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}^2B_{M}^2$ to obtain
\[
V \equiv \frac{a_{x+(1+y)\tau}a_{x+(y-1)\tau}}{a_{x+y\tau}^2} \bmod \frac{B_r^2}{\gcd(B_{\alpha}B_{M}, B_r)^2},
\]
then we fix the value of $x$ and it follows from induction on $y$ that 
\begin{align*}
a_{x+y\tau}
&\equiv V^{\frac{y(y-1)}{2}} a_{x+\tau}^{y} a_{x}^{1-y} \\
&\equiv V^{\frac{y(y-1)}{2}}
\left(
U^{\frac{x(x-1)}{2}} a_{1+\tau}^{x} a_{\tau}^{1-x}
\right)^{y}
\left(
U^{\frac{x(x-1)}{2}} a_{1}^{x} a_{0}^{1-x}
\right)^{1-y} \\
&\equiv
U^{\frac{x(x-1)}{2}}
V^{\frac{y(y-1)}{2}}
\left(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{0}}\right)^{x}
\left(\frac{a_{\tau}}{a_{0}}\right)^{y}
\left(\frac{a_{1+\tau} a_{0}}{a_{1} a_{\tau}}\right)^{xy}
a_{0}
\bmod \frac{B_r^2}{\gcd(B_{\alpha} B_{M}, B_r)^2}.
\end{align*}
Multiplying both sides by $\operatorname{gcd}(B_\alpha, B_r)$, we obtain 
\begin{align*}
a_{x+y\tau}\operatorname{gcd}(B_{\alpha}, B_r)
&\equiv 
\operatorname{gcd}(B_{\alpha}, B_r)C^{\frac{x(x-1)}{2}}
D^{\frac{y(y-1)}{2}} \\
& \quad \cdot
\left(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{0}}\right)^{x}
\left(\frac{a_{\tau}}{a_{0}}\right)^{y}
\left(\frac{a_{1+\tau} a_{0}}{a_{1} a_{\tau}}\right)^{xy}
a_{0}
\bmod \frac{\operatorname{gcd}(B_{\alpha}, B_r)B_r^2}{\gcd(B_{\alpha} B_{M}, B_r)^2}.
\end{align*}

By assumption, $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k$ divides $B_{r}/\operatorname{gcd}(B_r, B_M)$. Since $B_{r}/\operatorname{gcd}(B_r, B_M)$ divides the modulus clearly, so as $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k$. Now apply the definition of $a_{x+y\tau}$ from (\ref{a_l}), then the first case in the proposition is proved. Furthermore, recall that $a_{x+y\tau}$ is coprime with $B_r/\gcd(B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}, B_{r})$ and therefore coprime to the modulus as well. Therefore, $U$, $V$, $B_{\alpha +r}B_{\alpha}^{-1}=a_1/a_{0}^{-1}$, $B_{\alpha + r\tau}B_{\alpha}^{-1}=a_{\tau}a_0^{-1}$ and $B_{1+\tau}B_{\alpha}\left(B_{1}B_{\tau}\right)^{-1}a_{1+\tau}a_{0}a_{1}^{-1} a_{\tau}^{-1}$ are all $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$--adic units.

<!-- prop-4: body_md -->
\label{PID periodicity}
Suppose $K$ is a class number $1$ number field. Let $M$ be as in \Cref{CM EDS}, let $k \in \ensuremath{\mathbb{N}}$ and let $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ be a prime ideal in $O_K$. Let 
\[
r(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)=M \operatorname{ord} \left([M]P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^{k+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}([M]P)}\right),
\]
and 
\[
\pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)=\phi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)r(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)=(\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})-1)\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^{k-1})r(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k),
\] 
where $\phi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{a}})=\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{a}})^{\times}=\prod_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k \mid\!\mid \ensuremath{\mathfrak{a}}}(\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})-1)\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^{k-1})$ is the generalisation of the Euler totient function. If $x+y\tau \in \pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, or equivalently $\pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)$ divides both $x$ and $y$, then we have 
\[
B_{\alpha + (x+y\tau)r(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)}\equiv B_{\alpha} \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k.
\]
In other words, the sequence $B_{\alpha} \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k$ is periodic with period dividing $\pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)$.
<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Write $r=r(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)$ throughout the proof. Suppose $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k$ does not divide $B_\alpha$. By assumption, we have 
\[
[r]P \equiv O \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^{k+\nu_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}(MP)}
\]
and therefore $k+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(MP)\leq \nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(rP)$. For all prime ideals $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$, we have 
\begin{align*}
\nu_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}(B_r)
&= \nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(rP) \\
&\geq k + \nu_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}(MP) \\
&= k+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(B_M).
\end{align*}
Therefore,
\[
\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k \mid \frac{B_r}{\operatorname{gcd}(B_M,B_r)} 
\quad \text{and} \quad 
M \mid r.
\]

Recall from \Cref{symmetry law} that if $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k \nmid B_{\alpha}$, then for every 
$x+y\tau \in \operatorname{End}(E)$,
\[
B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}
\equiv 
U^{\frac{x(x-1)}{2}}
V^{\frac{y(y-1)}{2}}
\left(\frac{B_{\alpha+r}}{B_\alpha}\right)^{x}
\left(\frac{B_{\alpha+r\tau}}{B_\alpha}\right)^{y}
\left(\frac{B_{\alpha}B_{\alpha+(1+\tau)r}}{B_{\alpha+r}B_{\alpha+r\tau}}\right)^{xy}
B_{\alpha}
\bmod{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k},
\]
where 
\[
U=\frac{B_{M+r}B_{M-r}}{B_M^2}, 
\qquad 
V=\frac{B_{M+r\tau}B_{M-r\tau}}{B_M^2}.
\]

Note that \Cref{symmetry law} also showed that $C$ and $D$ are $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$--adic units. 
In particular, by strong divisibility,
\[
\operatorname{gcd}(B_{\alpha}, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)
= \operatorname{gcd}(B_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)
= \operatorname{gcd}(B_{\alpha}, B_r, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k).
\]
Now
\[
\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)=\phi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)=(\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})-1)\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^{k-1}),
\]
so for any $u \in (\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)^{\times}$, if $\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)\mid x$ and $\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)\mid y$, then
\[
u^x \equiv 
u^y \equiv 
u^{\frac{x(x-1)}{2}} \equiv 
u^{\frac{y(y-1)}{2}} \equiv 
u^{xy} \equiv 1 
\bmod{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k}.
\]

Thus by definition of $\pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)$, if $\pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)$ divides $(x+y\tau)r$, then 
\[
B_{\alpha + (x+y\tau)r}\equiv B_{\alpha} \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k.
\]
\qedhere

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{ray class}
Let $A, B, C$ be elements in a number field $K$ that satisfy $A=B+C$. Let $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$ be an integral ideal in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$ and consider the group $I_K^\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}} / T_{L/K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$. Suppose $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}$ is a fractional ideal in $K$ such that $B\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}$ is an ideal contained in $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$ and at least one of $A\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}$ and $C\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}$ is relatively prime to $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$ , then $A\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}} \sim C\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We were given $A=B+C$, so without loss of generality assume $C\neq 0$ and divide $C$ on both sides to get 
\[
\frac{A}{C}=\frac{B+C}{C}=\frac{B}{C}+1.
\]
Since $B\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}} \subseteq \ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$, equivalently $\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(B\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}})\geq \nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}})>0$ for every prime $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \mid \ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$. We also assumed that at least one of $A\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}$ and $C\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}$ is relatively prime to $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$; without loss of generality take $\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(C\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}})=0$, so we may deduce that for every $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}\mid\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$,
\[
\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(BC^{-1}\right)=\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(B\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}})-\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(C\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}) \geq \nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}) \implies BC^{-1} \equiv 0 \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}. 
\]
Note that $(A\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}})(C\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}})^{-1}=(AC^{-1})$ as ideals, therefore it follows that $AC^{-1}=A\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}(C\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}})^{-1}=(\alpha)$ with $\alpha \equiv 1 \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$. Equivalently, $AC^{-1} \in P_{K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$, hence $A\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}$ and $C\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}$ represent the same ray class in $I_K^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}/P_K^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$. Since we have assumed that $\mathfrak{f}_{L/K}\mid\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$, we have $P_K^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}\subseteq T_{L/K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$ and therefore $A\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}} \sim C\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}$.

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{Ray class symmetry}
Let $K$ be a number field of class number greater than $1$. Let $M$ be as in \Cref{CM EDS}. Let $\alpha, r \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ with $r$ being a multiple of $M$. Let $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ be a prime in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$ and $k \in \ensuremath{\mathbb{N}}$; denote $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}} = \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k$ and consider the group $I_{K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}/T_{L/K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$. Suppose $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k$ is a factor of the integral ideal $D_{r}\left(D_r+D_M\right)^{-1}$ and $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} + D_{\alpha}=\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$, then for all $x+y\tau \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, 
\begin{equation}\label{ray class congruence}
\begin{split}
D_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r} &\sim 
U^{\frac{x(x-1)}{2}}
V^{\frac{y(y-1)}{2}}
\left(D_{\alpha+r}D_{\alpha}^{-1}\right)^{x} \\
&\quad \cdot \left(D_{\alpha+r\tau}D_{\alpha}^{-1}\right)^{y}
\left(D_{\alpha+(1+r)\tau}D_{\alpha}
\left(D_{\alpha+r}D_{\alpha+r\tau}\right)^{-1}\right)^{xy} \\
&\quad \cdot D_{\alpha}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For number fields $K$ with class number greater than $1$, \Cref{CM EDS} tells us that there is a sequence of fractional ideals $\{\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}}$ in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$ with 
\begin{align}
D_{\alpha}&=\psi_{\alpha}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}^{-1}, \\
\psi_{\alpha+\beta}\psi_{\alpha-\beta}\psi_{\gamma}^2 &= \psi_{\alpha+\gamma}\psi_{\alpha-\gamma}\psi_{\beta}^2 - \psi_{\beta+\gamma}\psi_{\beta-\gamma}\psi_{\alpha}^2, \\
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+\beta}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha-\beta}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}^2 &= \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+\gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha-\gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}^2 = \mathfrak{g}_{\beta+\gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta-\gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}^2.
\end{align}

As in the case of $\operatorname{Cl}(K)=1$, we use the recurrence relation and replace the indices $\beta$, $\gamma$ by $M$ and $\alpha + (x+y\tau)r$ to obtain 
\begin{equation}\label{NON_PID_RR}
\psi_{M+r}\psi_{M-r}\psi_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r}^2 = \psi_{\alpha+(x+1+y\omega)r}\psi_{n+(x-1+y\omega)r}\psi_{M}^2 + \psi_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r+M}\psi_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r-M}\psi_r^2.
\end{equation}

Next, as elements in $K$, divide both sides by $\psi_M^2\psi_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r}^2$ to obtain 
\begin{align*}
\psi_{M+r}\psi_{M-r}\psi_{M}^{-2} 
&= \psi_{\alpha+(x+1+y\omega)r}\psi_{\alpha+(x-1+y\omega)r}\psi_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r}^{-2} \\
& + \psi_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r+M}\psi_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r-M}\frac{\psi_r^2}{\psi_M^2}\psi_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r}^{-2}.
\end{align*}

By \Cref{CM EDS}, we also have a sequence of fractional ideals that satisfy 
\begin{align*}
\mathfrak{g}_{M+r}\mathfrak{g}_{M-r}\mathfrak{g}_{M}^{-2} 
&= \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r+M}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r-M}\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}_{r}^2\left(\mathfrak{g}_{M}\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r}\right)^{-2} \\
&=\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha+(x+1+y\omega)r}\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}_{n+(x-1+y\omega)r}\ensuremath{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r}^{-2}.
\end{align*}

Now we wish to apply \Cref{ray class}, then we can multiply $\left(\mathfrak{g}_{M+r}\mathfrak{g}_{M-r}\right)^{-1}\mathfrak{g}_{M}^{2}$ on both sides of (\ref{NON_PID_RR}) and yield the following congruence:
\begin{equation}\label{DA}
D_{M+r}D_{M-r}D_{M}^{-2}\sim D_{\alpha+(x+1+y\omega)r}D_{\alpha+(x-1+y\omega)r}D_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r}^{-2}.
\end{equation}

To do so, we need to show that $D_{M+r}D_{M-r}D_{M}^{-2}$ and $D_{\alpha+(x+1+y\omega)r}D_{\alpha+(x-1+y\omega)r}D_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r}^{-2}$ are ideals coprime to $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$. Indeed, by strong divisibility of $\left(D_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}}$ and the fact that $D_{\alpha}=D_{\alpha\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}}$ for all $\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, we know that for all $\gamma \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, 
\[
D_{\alpha+\gamma r}+D_{r}=D_{(\alpha+\gamma r)+(r)}=D_{(\alpha)+(r)}=D_{\alpha}+D_{r}.
\]
We also assumed that $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ is not a prime ideal factor of $D_{\alpha}$. Therefore, $D_{\alpha+\gamma r}$ is an integral ideal coprime to $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ for all $\gamma \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ and hence (\ref{DA}) follows from \Cref{ray class} and (\ref{NON_PID_RR}). 

Repeating the same argument, we also obtain 
\[
D_{M+r\tau}D_{M-r\tau}D_{M}^{-2} \sim D_{\alpha+(x+(y+1)\tau)r}D_{\alpha+(x+(y-1)\tau)r}D_{\alpha+(x+y\tau)r}^{-2}. 
\]

Similar to the case of $Cl(K)=1$, we set 
\begin{equation}
U=\frac{D_{M+r}D_{M-r}}{D_M^2}, V=\frac{D_{M+r\omega}D_{M-r\omega}}{D_M^2}, a_{x+y\omega}=\frac{D_{\alpha+(x+y\omega)r}}{D_{\alpha}+D_{r}}.
\end{equation}
Apply the induction argument, we obtain 
\[
a_{x+y\omega} \sim
U^{\frac{x(x-1)}{2}}
V^{\frac{y(y-1)}{2}}
\left(a_1a_0^{-1}\right)^{x}
\left(a_{\omega}a_{0}^{-1}\right)^{y}
\left(a_{1+\omega}a_{0}\left(a_{1}a_{\omega}\right)^{-1}\right)^{xy}
a_{0}.
\]

Since $D_{\alpha}+D_{r}$ is coprime to the modulus $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}=\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k$, $[D_{\alpha}+D_{r}]$ is a well--defined ray class and we can multiply it on both sides to conclude the proof.

<!-- cor-1: body_md -->
\label{Non PID periodicity}
Suppose $K$ is a number field of class number greater than $1$. Let $M$ be as in \Cref{CM EDS}, let $k \in \ensuremath{\mathbb{N}}$ and let $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ be a prime ideal in $O_K$. Let 
\[
r(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)=M \operatorname{ord} \left([M]P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^{k+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}([M]P)}\right).
\]
Assume $D_{\alpha}$ is coprime to the prime ideal $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ and consider the group $I_{K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}/T_{L/K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$, where $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$ is the ideal $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k$. For every $\beta \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, if $\beta \equiv \alpha \bmod \pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)$, where 
\[
\pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)=r(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)h^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}(K), \text{ $h^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}(K)$ is the ray class number,}
\] 
then the denominator ideals satisfy $D_{\beta}\sim D_{\alpha}$.
<!-- end cor-1 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
In \Cref{CM EDS}, we stated that $M$is an element in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, while $h^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}(K)$ is clearly a natural number. Hence, $\pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k)$ is an element in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ for any $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$--prime $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$.
<!-- end remark-4 -->

We first need to show that $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^k$ does divide the integral ideal $D_r(D_r+D_M)^{-1}$, whose proof is the exact same as the one in \Cref{PID periodicity}. <!-- prop-6: body_md -->

\end{multline}
where $\operatorname{ord}(\chi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}))$ denotes the multiplicative order of the root of unity $\chi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})$.

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Assume $\chi(D_{\alpha})\not\in \{0, 1\}$, so $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$ is coprime with $D_{\alpha}$ and $D_{\alpha} \not\in T_{L/K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$, hence not an ideal norm in $L$. By unique factorisation of ideals in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$, we have 
\[
\chi(D_{\alpha})=\prod_{i=1}^n\chi_{\operatorname{Art}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})^{e_i} \neq 1.
\]

If we further have $\operatorname{ord}(\chi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_i))$ dividing $\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_i}(D_{\ell})$, then $\chi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_i)^{e_i}=1$. But since we assumed $\chi(D_{\alpha})\neq 1$, there must exist at least a prime ideal $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}$ with  $\operatorname{ord}(\chi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_i)) \nmid \nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_i}(D_{\alpha})$ and $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_i \mid D_{\alpha}$. 

We claim that it is sufficient to consider the set of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--principal prime ideals
\[
\Lambda=\{(\ell) \text{ prime in }\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}} \text{ and }\operatorname{Nm}(\ell)\leq X \colon \ell \equiv \alpha \bmod \pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}, \alpha \in (\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}\}.
\]
If $\ell \in \Lambda$, then by \Cref{Non PID periodicity} we have $D_{\ell} \sim D_{\alpha}$, so $\chi(D_{\ell})=\chi(D_{\alpha})\neq 0, 1$. From \cite[Proposition 7.20]{Cox}, there is a bijection between $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$--ideal prime to $f$ and $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--ideal prime to $f$. There are only $O(1)$ $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--prime ideals that are not coprime to $f$, so we may estimate the desired quantity from above with the prime ideal theorem (see \cite[Chapter 7.2, Corollary 1]{Narkiewicz}) and together with the equidistribution result, we obtain the asymptotic

\]
In the case of $\operatorname{Cl}(K)=1$, the exact same argument applies to $B_{\alpha}$, except we consider $B_{\alpha} \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$ instead.

As in the original proof, we will give a lower bound to $\#\Lambda$ by estimating the number of prime elements in $\Lambda$ only (note that $\operatorname{ann}(P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})$ does not need to be a prime ideal). To do so, we consider the multiples $\left[\ell\right]P$ where $\ell$ runs over all principal prime ideals in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$. If $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a prime ideal in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$ such that $\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\left[\ell\right]P\right) > \nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P) \geq 0$, then we have either $\ell=\operatorname{ann}(P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})$ or $P \equiv O \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ (equivalently, $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \in T$).

The former is obvious; if $P \equiv O \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$, then we claim that $\ell\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K = \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$, or equivalently $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}} = \ell$. Indeed, if $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \in T$, then we must have $P \in E_{\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)}(K)$, $P \not\in E_{\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)+1}(K)$ and $[\ell]P \in E_{\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)+1}(K)$. In other words, $\ell$ is an element in $\operatorname{ann}(P)$ in the $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--module $E_{\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)}(L)/E_{\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)+1}(L)$. Let $W=E_{\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)}(K)/E_{\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)+1}(K)$. From \cite[Corollary 2.10]{Streng}, we know that we have an $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--module isomorphism $W \cong \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$. This implies that $W$ is a simple $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--module, therefore $\operatorname{ann}(P)$ is a proper maximal ideal in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ (by assumption, $\operatorname{ann}(P)$ cannot be the entire endomorphism ring $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$). 

Overall, we now have a containment of prime ideals $\ell \subseteq \operatorname{ann}(P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}) \subsetneq \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$. Since there are only $\#S_2=O(1)$ primes that are not coprime to $f$, the conductor of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, we may assume that both $\operatorname{ann}(P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})$ and $\ell$ are coprime to $f$. It follows that both $\operatorname{ann}(P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})$ and $\ell$ are maximal ideals of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$. Therefore, as $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--ideals, we must have $\operatorname{ann}(P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})=\ell$, which gives the isomorphism 
\begin{equation}\label{isomorphism module}
\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\ell \cong \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\operatorname{ann}(P) = M \cong \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}.
\end{equation}

Since $f\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}} + \ell = \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, $\ell\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F$ is an $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F$-ideal prime to $f$ of the same norm and hence we also have the isomorphism 
\[
\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F/\ell\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F \cong \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}},
\]
which tells us that $\left[\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \colon \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F/\ell\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F\right]=1$. Let $p$ be the rational prime below $\ell\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F$ and $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$, then we have two possible cases: if $p$ is inert or ramifies in $F$, then $p=\ell\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F$ or $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}=\left(\ell\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F\right)^2$; both implies that $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}=\ell\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F$ and hence $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}} = \ell$. If $p$ splits completely in $F$, then $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}} = \ell \text{ or } \bar{\ell}$. But if $\ell$ is a prime in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, then so as $\bar{\ell}$ and therefore the argument also applies to $\bar{\ell}$, with the corresponding $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$--prime being $\bar{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}$. So without loss of generality, we showed that if $P \equiv O \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$, then $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}} =\ell$. This means counting the size of $T$ is equivalent to counting the number of principal prime ideals $\ell$ in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ that fail to be the annilator of $P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ in the $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--module $E_{0}(K)/E_{1}(K)$. 

From the proof of \Cref{Lambda estimate}, we know there exists a set of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$-prime $\ell$ with $\operatorname{Nm}(\ell) \leq X$ whose cardinality can be bounded from below by the asymptotic
\[
\frac{1}{\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}}\cdot\frac{1}{h(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})}\frac{X}{\log X}
\]
such that $ \operatorname{ord}\left(\chi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})\right) \nmid \nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(D_{\ell})$ and $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \nmid \ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$ for some $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$--prime $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$. For these $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--primes $\ell$, we necessarily have $\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(D_{\ell})>0$. If $\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}([\ell]P)=\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)$, then $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \in T$ and $\ell = \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$. Overall, by excluding $\#S_1$, $\#S_2$ and $\#T$ of the $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$--primes $\ell$, these primes are now principal primes that lie in $\Lambda$ with $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \not\in S_1$, hence give the result.

By definition, the set can be bounded from above by
\begin{align*}
N_{1}(B)
&\ll \sum_{\log B \leq \operatorname{Nm}(\ell_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}) \leq B} \#\{\operatorname{Nm}(\alpha) \leq B \colon \alpha \equiv 0 \bmod (\ell_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\} \\

\end{align*}
where the bound follows from the fact that we have specified one residue class in $\left(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K/\ell_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K\right)^{\times}$. Note that here we have interpreted $\ell_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}$ as an element in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$ rather than in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$. 

Denote $\tilde{E}$ to be $E/K$ reduction modulo $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$. Since $E/K$ has complex multiplication by $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$, there is an isomorphism 
\[
\operatorname{End}_{\ensuremath{\mathbb{C}}}(E) \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{End}{\overline{\ensuremath{\mathbb{F}}_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}}(\tilde{E}),
\]
where $\overline{F_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}$ denotes the algebraic closure of the residue field $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$, and there exists a prime $\eta \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ such that $\eta \xmapsto{\sim} \operatorname{Frob}_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \in \operatorname{End}{\overline{F_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}}(\tilde{E})$.

By \cite[Theorem V.1.1]{Silverman1},
\begin{align*}
\#\tilde{E}(\ensuremath{\mathbb{F}}_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}})
&=\operatorname{deg}\left(\mathbbm{1}-\operatorname{Frob}_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}\right) \\
&=\operatorname{deg}(1-\eta) \\
&=\operatorname{Nm}(1-\eta).
\end{align*}

i.e. $[1-\eta]P \equiv O \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$. Since $\ell_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} = \operatorname{ann}(P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})$, we must have $1-\eta \in \ell_p\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_K$, therefore $\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(1-\eta) \geq \operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ell_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})$. Using Hasse's bound, we obtain 
\[
\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ell_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})\leq \operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(1-\eta)=\#\tilde{E}(\overline{\ensuremath{\mathbb{F}}_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}})\leq \operatorname{Nm}_{K/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})+1+2\sqrt{\operatorname{Nm}_{K/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})} \ll \operatorname{Nm}_{K/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}),
\]
which then iimplies $\frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{K/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})} \ll \frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ell_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})}$. So overall, this yields
\begin{align*}
N_{1}(B)
&\ll \sum_{\substack{\log B \leq \operatorname{Nm}(\ell) \leq B \\ \ell \in \Lambda}} \frac{B}{\operatorname{Nm}_{K/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ell_\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})\operatorname{Nm}_{K/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})} \\

&\ll B\sum_{n \geq \log B} \frac{1}{n^{[K\colon F]+1}} \\
&\ll \frac{1}{[K\colon F]}\frac{B}{\left(\log B\right)^{[K\colon F]}},
\end{align*}
where the final estimate follows from extending the sum over all elements with norm at least $\log B$ and the use of integral bound.

An upper bound for $N_{0}(B)$ is

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
The proof proceeds in the exact same way as the original proof in \cite[Lemma 4.5]{Brauer}. Since we have assumed that $\ell$ does not divide the conductor $f=[\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_{F}\colon \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}]$, we always have $\ell\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_{F}=\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}$ for some prime ideal $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}$ in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F$ of the same norm (see \cite[Proposition 7.20]{Cox}). Therefore, we can apply Selberg sieve for number fields to the primes in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}_F$. Using the version in \cite[Theorem 14, Lemmata 15, 16]{Debaene}, this gives us 
\begin{align*}
N_{0}(B) 
& \leq \frac{2\pi B}{\sqrt{\Delta_{F}}}\prod_{\substack{\log B \leq \operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq \sqrt{B} \\ \ell \in \Lambda, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ell}} \left(1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}})}\right) + O\left(B(\log B)^6\right) \\
&\ll B \prod_{\substack{\log B \leq \operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq \sqrt{B} \\ \ell \in \Lambda, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ell}}
\left(1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}})}\right).
\end{align*}

By Mertens' theorem for number fields (see \cite[Theorem 1 (C1)]{Garcia}), we have
\[
\prod_{\substack{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq \log B \\ \ell \in \Lambda, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ell}}
\left(1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}})}\right)^{-1}
\ll 
\prod_{\substack{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq \log B}}
\left(1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}})}\right)^{-1}
\ll \log|\Delta_{F}| \log\log B.
\]

Hence, it remains to bound
\[
\prod_{\substack{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq \sqrt{B} \\ \ell \in \Lambda, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ell}}
\left(1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}})}\right)
\]
from above. To do so, notice that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log \prod_{\substack{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq \sqrt{B} \\ \ell \in \Lambda, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ell}}
\left(1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}})}\right)^{-1}
&= -\sum_{\substack{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ell) \leq \sqrt{B} \\ \ell \in \Lambda, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ell}}
\log\left(1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ell)}\right) \\
&\geq \sum_{\substack{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq \sqrt{B} \\ \ell \in \Lambda, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ell}}
\frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}})} 
\end{aligned}
\]

Provided that $\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}})=p$, or equivalently $p$ splits in $F$, then the sum will diverge and giving us a non-trivial bound. By partial summation, this is 
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq \sqrt{B} \\ \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}} = \ell,\ \ell \in \Lambda}}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{B}}+\int_{1}^{\sqrt{B}} \sum_{\substack{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq u \\ \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}} = \ell,\ \ell \in \Lambda}}
\frac{1}{u^2} du.
\]

By Chebotarev Density Theorem, the elements of $\Lambda$ that correspond to rational primes that split completely in $F$ has density $1/2$, so \Cref{Lambda size} shows that 
\[
\sum_{\substack{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq \sqrt{B} \\ \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}} = \ell,\ \ell \in \Lambda}}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{B}}=\frac{1}{\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}}\cdot\frac{1}{h(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})}\frac{2}{\log B}-\frac{\#S_1+\#S_2+\#T}{\sqrt{B}}, \text{ and }
\]
\begin{align*}
\int_{1}^{\sqrt{B}} \sum_{\substack{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq u \\ \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}} = \ell,\ \ell \in \Lambda}}
\frac{1}{u^2} du
&=\frac{1}{\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}}\cdot\frac{1}{h(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})}\int_{1}^{\sqrt{B}} \frac{1}{u\log u} du - (\#S_1+\#S_2+\#T)\int_{1}^{\sqrt{B}} \frac{1}{u^2}du \\
&=\frac{1}{\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}}\cdot\frac{1}{h(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})}\log\log(\sqrt{B})+(\#S_1+\#S_2+\#T)\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{B}}\right) \\
&=\frac{1}{\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}}\cdot\frac{1}{h(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})}\left(\log\log(B)+O(1)\right)-(\#S_1+\#S_2+\#T)\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{B}}\right).
\end{align*}

Overall, we have 
\[
\log \prod_{\substack{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq \sqrt{B} \\ \ell \in \Lambda, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ell}}
\left(1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}})}\right)^{-1}
\geq \frac{1}{\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}}\cdot\frac{1}{h(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})}\left(\log\log(B)+O(1)\right)-(\#S_1+\#S_2+\#T).
\]

Exponentiating and take reciprocals give 
\[
 \prod_{\substack{\log B \leq \operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}) \leq B \\ \ell \in \Lambda, \ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}} \cap \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ell}}
\left(1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{Nm}_{F/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}})}\right)
\ll \frac{B\log|\Delta_{F}| \log\log B}{\left(\log B\right)^{\left(1/\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}\right)\left(1/h(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})\right)}}\exp(\#S_1+\#S_2+\#T).
\]
and hence the result.

If $y([\alpha]P) \in K$ is an ideal norm in $L$, then $y([\alpha]P) \in T_{L/K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$, where the conductor of the extension $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{f}}_{L/K}$ divides the modulus $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$. Let $\chi$ be a group character as described in (\ref{character}). As stated in the hypotheses of the theorem, we assume the existence of $\alpha_0 \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$ with $\alpha_0 \in (\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}$ such that $\chi(D_{\alpha_{0}})\not\in\{0, 1\}$. Hence, we may apply \Cref{Lambda estimate} the previous lemmata in this section, giving us the estimate
\begin{align*}
&\#\{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}\colon |\alpha|\leq B, y(\alpha P) \text{ is an ideal norm from } L\} \\
&\ll N_0(B)+N_1(B)\\
&\ll \frac{1}{[K\colon F]}\frac{B}{\left(\log B\right)^{[K\colon F]}}+\frac{B\log|\Delta_{F}| \log\log B}{\left(\log B\right)^{\left(1/\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}\right)\left(1/h(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})\right)}}\exp(\#S_1+\#S_2+\#T).
\end{align*}

Note that $\# S_1 = O_L(1)$, $\# S_2 = O_E(1)$, and $\log|\Delta_F| = O_E(1)$ (since the choice of curve determines the endomorphism ring, and hence the imaginary quadratic field $F$). In addition, it is obvious that
\[
\frac{1}{\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}}\frac{1}{h(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})} \leq 1 \leq [K\colon F],
\]
therefore the first term gets dominated by the second term. Our estimate hence becomes
\[
\#\{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}\colon |\alpha|\leq B, y(\alpha P) \text{ is a norm from } L\} 
\ll_{E, L}\frac{B \log\log B}{\left(\log B\right)^{\left(1/\#(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\pi\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})^{\times}\right)\left(1/h(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})\right)}}\exp(\#T).
\]

To get the final bound in the theorem, which depends explicitly on $P$, we proceed to show that
\begin{equation}\label{size of T}
\#T\ll_{E} \frac{\hat{h}(P)}{\log\hat{h}(P)},
\end{equation}
whose proof is almost identical to that of \cite[Lemma 4.6]{Brauer}, except that now we use the ideal version for the estimates.
If $\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)>0$, then $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a factor of $D_1$. Therefore, we have 
\[
\#T=\omega\left(\prod_{\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)>0}\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}\right)=\omega(D_{1}),
\]
where $\omega(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{a}})$ denotes the number of distinct prime ideal factors of the ideal $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{a}}$. 

By definition, we have $\operatorname{Nm}(D_{1})\leq h(x(P))$, where $h(\cdot)$ is the naive height function. Theorem 1.1 of \cite{Silverman5} implies that for all $P \in E(K)$, 
\[
\left|\hat{h}(P)-\frac{1}{2}h(x(P))\right|<2h(E),
\]
therefore $\log\operatorname{Nm}(D_{1})\leq\hat{h}(P)+O_{E}(1)$. $\omega(D_{1})$ can be bounded by 
\[
\omega(D_{1}) \ll \frac{\log\operatorname{Nm}(D_{1})}{\log\log\operatorname{Nm}(D_{1})},
\]
and since $\hat{h}(P)\gg_{E} 1$ by \cite[Theorem VIII.9.10(a)]{Silverman1}, this completes the proof of (\ref{size of T}). 

Finally, recall that $\pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}})$ is an upper bound for the period of the sequence $(D_{\alpha})_{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}}$ (resp.\ $(B_{\alpha})_{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}}$) in the group $I_{K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}/T_{L/K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$ (resp.\ modulo $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$). Let $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_1^{k_1}\ldots\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_n^{k_n}$ be the unique ideal factorisation of $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$, then from \Cref{PID periodicity} and \Cref{Non PID periodicity} we have 
\[
\pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}})=\begin{cases}
			\phi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}})\prod_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}} \mid \ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}M \operatorname{ord} \left([M]P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^{k+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}([M]P)}\right), & \text{if $\operatorname{Cl}(K)=1$}\\
            \bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(h^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_i^{k_i}}(K)\right)M \operatorname{ord} \left([M]P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}_i^{k_i+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}([M]P)}\right), & \text{otherwise},
		 \end{cases}
\]
where $M$ is an element in the ideal $\mathfrak{M}(P)$ as described in \Cref{CM EDS}. Note that we have the bound
\[
 \operatorname{ord} \left([M]P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^{k+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}([M]P)}\right) \leq |E_{k+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}([M]P)+1}(K_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}})/E_{k+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}([M]P)}(K_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}})|=\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}),
\]
and by definition the size of $M$ can be bounded by $\prod_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}|E(K_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}/E_{0}(K_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}})|$, uniformly with respect to $P$. Therefore, in both cases we can bound the size of $\pi$ by $O_{E, L}(1)$. 

It is also obvious that $h(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}})=O_{E}(1)$. Hence, our final estimate is 
\[
\#\{\alpha \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}\colon |\alpha|\leq B, y(\alpha P) \text{ is an ideal norm from } L\} 
\ll_{E, P, L}\frac{B}{\left(\log B\right)^{\omega-\varepsilon}}
\]
for any $\varepsilon >0$, where $\omega=\omega(E, L)>0$, and the implicit constant is $\exp\left(O_{E, L}\left(\hat{h}(P)/\log\hat{h}(P)\right)\right)$, completing the proof.

In this example, we consider the elliptic curve 
\[
E/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)\colon y^2 = x^3 - 2x,
\]
This curve has complex multiplication by $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}[i]$ and the Mordell--Weil group $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}^2 \oplus \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/2\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}$, with $E(\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i))_{\text{tors}}=\{O, (0, 0)\}$ and generators $P_1=(-1,1)$ and $P_2=(i-1,-2)$ (see the corresponding \href{https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/2.0.4.1/4096.1/CMd/1}{LMFDB entry} for more details). The EDS associated to $P_1$, starting at $B_0$, is listed in the second column of \Cref{Table EDS}.

We now apply the main theorem to $L/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)$, where $L$ is the ray class field of $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)$ with modulus $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}=(1+i)^4=\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^4=(4)$, and therefore $T_{L/K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}=P_K^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$. A SageMath computation yields $\#\operatorname{Cl}_{\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}=[L\colon \ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)]=2$. Since $L$ is a quadratic extension of $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)$ with $1+i$ being the only ramified prime, LMFDB allows us to identify $L$ as $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_8)$, the $8$--th cyclotomic field (see \href{https://www.lmfdb.org/NumberField/?degree=4&ram_quantifier=exactly&ram_primes=2&ur_primes=3&subfield=x\%5E2\%2B1&field_is=ab&cm_field=yes}{LMFDB search result}). 

Furthermore, as $\#\operatorname{Cl}_{\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}=2$, we deduce that
\[
\operatorname{Cl}_{\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}=\{[1], [1+2i]\}\cong \left(\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}/\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}\right)^{\times}/\{\pm1, \pm i\}.
\]
Next, to find a character $\chi\colon \operatorname{Cl}_{\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}\rightarrow\ensuremath{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}$ and an $\alpha\in\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}]$ satisfying $(\alpha, \pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}))=1$ and $\chi(B_{\alpha})\notin\{0,1\}$, we consider the character table of $\operatorname{Cl}_{\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\operatorname{Cl}_{\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}} 	& [1] 		& [1+2i] \\ \hline
\chi_0 		& 1 		& 1 \\
\chi_1 		& 1 		& -1
\end{array}
\]
Clearly, only the non--trivial character $\chi_1$ could possibly satisfy the hypothesis of our main theorem. With reference to \Cref{Table EDS} again, we see that $\chi_{1}(B_{2+i})=\chi_1([2-i])=-1$. Note that $P_1$ has everywhere good reduction, so we may take $M=1$ and $k=4$ in \Cref{PID periodicity}, this gives us an upper bound for the period of the EDS: 
\[
\pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}})=\phi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^4)r(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^4)=\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^3)(\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})-1)M \operatorname{ord} \left([M]P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^{2+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}([M]P)}\right)=8(4)=2^5,
\]
where $\operatorname{ord} \left([M]P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^{2+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}([M]P)}\right)=4$ as observed from \Cref{Table EDS}. Therefore, $\alpha=2+i$ is a valid choice. Since all hypotheses are satisfied, \Cref{Main ideal norm result} shows that 
\[
\#\left\{
\alpha\in \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}[i] \colon |\alpha|\leq B,\;
y(\alpha P_1)\text{ is an ideal norm from } L
\right\}
\ll_{\varepsilon}
\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\frac{1}{2048}-\varepsilon}}.
\]

As previously mentioned, in this example we consider the elliptic curve 
\[
E/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)\colon y^2 = x^3 - 2x,

where $\theta=(1+i)\sqrt{-\frac{5}{2}}$ has minimal polynomial $\theta^4+25$ (one may also verify that $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)=\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i,\sqrt{-10})$). Since $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)\subset\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)$, $P_1 \in E(\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta))$ and we may choose $P_1=(-1, 1)$ and $P_2=(i-1,-2)$ as the generators of the free part, as well as considering the EDS generated by $P_1$ again as in \Cref{example Q(i)}. The EDS associated to $P_1$, starting at $D_0$, is listed in \Cref{Table EDS}.

SageMath computations show that $\#\operatorname{Cl}_{\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}=[L\colon \ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)]=4$, which by \Cref{Non PID periodicity}, also bounds the period of the ray classes corresponding to $D_{\alpha}$. To find a suitable ray class group character, we need to compute the ray class group explicitly. To do so, note that the primes $2\pm i \in \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}[i]$ ramify in $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)$:

(5)=(2+i)(2-i)=\left(5, \sqrt{10}+2i-1\right)^2\left(5, 2(\sqrt{-10}+i)+1\right)^2=\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}_1^2\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}_2^2.

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
\operatorname{Cl}_{\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}	& [1] 	& [\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}_1] 	& [\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}_1] ^2 	& [\ensuremath{\mathfrak{q}}_1] ^3 \\ \hline
\chi_0 				& 1 		& 1 		& 1 			& 1 \\
\chi_1 				& 1 		& i 		& -1 			& -i \\
\chi_2 				& 1 		& -1 		& 1 			& -1 \\
\chi_3 				& 1 		& -i 		& -1 			& i
\end{array}

\#\left\{
\alpha\in \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}[i] \colon |\alpha|\leq B,\;

\right\}
\ll_{\varepsilon}
\frac{B}{(\log B)^{\frac{1}{8}-\varepsilon}}.

\label{sieve example 3}
In this example, we consider the elliptic curve 
\[
E/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-2})\colon y^2=x^3+x^2-3x+1.
\]
This curve has complex multiplication by $\ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}=\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{-2}]$ and and the Mordell--Weil group $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}^2 \oplus \ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}/2\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}$. The discriminant is $\Delta=2^9=-(\sqrt{-2})^{18}$, so the only prime of bad reduction is $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}=\sqrt{-2}$ (see the corresponding \href{https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/2.0.8.1/1024.1/CMa/1}{LMFDB entry} for more details). We now consider the EDS associated to the point $P=(0, -1)$ with 
\[
[\sqrt{-2}]P=\left(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{4\sqrt{-2}}\right), \text{ where } [\sqrt{-2}](x, y)=\left( \frac{-x^2 - 1}{2(x - 1)}, \frac{\sqrt{-2}y(x^2 - 2x - 1)}{4(x - 1)^2} \right), 
\]
 where we have listed some of the values of the EDS in \Cref{Table EDS sqrt-2} (see \cite[Chapter II, Proposition 2.3.1]{Silverman2} for the derivation of the formula for $[\sqrt{-2}]$) . Note that the points $P$ and $[\sqrt{-2}]P$ are $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Z}}$--linearly independent, so they may be taken as generators of the free part of the Mordell-Weil group. 

We would like to apply the main theorem to this choice of $E$ and $P$ with the extension $L/\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-2})$, where $L$ is the ray class field of $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-2})$ for the modulus $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}=\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^2$. A SageMath computation yields $\#\operatorname{Cl}_{\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-2})}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}=[L\colon \ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(i)]=2$. Since $L$ is a quadratic extension of $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-2})$ with $\sqrt{-2}$ being the only ramified prime, LMFDB allows us to identify $L$ as $\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_8)$, the $8$--th cyclotomic field (see \href{https://www.lmfdb.org/NumberField/?degree=4&ram_quantifier=exactly&ram_primes=2&subfield=x\%5E2\%2B2&field_is=ab}{LMFDB search result}). 

Since $\operatorname{Cl}_{\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-2})}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}} \cong C_2$, so it has the same character table as in \Cref{example Q(i)}. However, we claim that the hypothesis of the main theorem is not satisfied here. To see this, note that $P$ and $[\sqrt{-2}]P$ are both nonsingular modulo $\mathfrak{p}$, so we set $M=1$ and $k=2$ in \Cref{PID periodicity}. Refer to \Cref{Table EDS sqrt-2}, we see that $[2]P = \left(\frac{5}{4}, -\frac{7}{8}\right)$, so we obtain 
\[
\pi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}})=\phi(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^2)r(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^2)=\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})(\operatorname{Nm}(\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}})-1)M \operatorname{ord} \left([M]P \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}^{2+\nu_{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{p}}}([M]P)}\right)=4.
\] 
This means that the EDS associated to $P$, $B_{\alpha}$, is periodic modulo $\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}$ with period bounded from above by $4$ : for any $\alpha, a+b\sqrt{-2} \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{O}}$,
\[
B_{\alpha + (a + b\sqrt{-2})} \equiv B_{\alpha} \bmod \ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}} \text{ whenever } 4 \mid a, b, 
\]

Since our method is unable to determine if an element in $T_{L/K}^{\ensuremath{\mathfrak{m}}}$ is an ideal norm from $L=\ensuremath{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_8)$ or not, our conclusion does not hold.
