<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A solution $X(t) \equiv 0$ of~\eqref{eq:sde_eucl} is said to be \emph{stable in probability} for $t \geq 0$ if for any $s \geq 0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$,
		\[
		\lim_{x \to 0} \mathbb{P}\left\{ \sup_{t > s} |X^{s,x}(t)| > \varepsilon \right\} = 0.
		\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
The solution $X(t) \equiv 0$ of~\eqref{eq:sde_eucl} is said to be (asymptotically) stable in the large if it is stable in probability and, in addition, for all $s$ and $x$,
		\[
		\mathbb{P}\left\{ \lim_{t \to \infty} X^{s,x}(t) = 0 \right\} = 1.
		\]
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- problem-1: body_md -->
Find $u_i(x)$ and $a_i(x)$ such that 
		a given point $x_{eq} \in \Gamma$ is asymptotically stable in 
		the large, for the process \eqref{eq:sde_eucl}.
<!-- end problem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
There exist smooth functions $a_i, u_i \in C^\infty(\overline{\Gamma})$ 
		such that the equilibrium point $x_{eq}$ of the process 
		\eqref{eq:sde_eucl} is asymptotically stable in probability in 
		the large. Moreover, a choice of the controls is 
		\begin{align}
			&a_i(x) = |x-x_{\rm eq}|^2, \quad u_i(x) = -\alpha (x_i-x_{\rm eq,i}), 
			\qquad i = 1,\ldots,d.
		\end{align}
		for a suitable $\alpha>0$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Without loss of generality take $x_{eq} = 0$. Define $
		V(x) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^d x_i^2$.
		The parameter $\alpha > 1$ in the control laws is to be determined. The infinitesimal generator 
		of \eqref{eq:sde_eucl} is locally
		\begin{align*}
			LV(x)&=\sum_{i=1}^N u_i(x)\,\partial_{x_i}V
			+\frac12\sum_{i=1}^N a_i^2\,\partial_{x_i}^2V\\
			&\quad+\frac12\sum_{i=1}^N a_i(\partial_{x_i}a_i)(\partial_{x_i}V).
		\end{align*}
		Since $A_i = \partial_{x_i}$ and $a_i = |x|^2$ for $i = 1,\ldots,d$, 
		each term computes as:
		For $i=1,\ldots,d$,
		\[
		\partial_{x_i}V=x_i,\qquad
		\partial_{x_i}a_i=2x_i,\qquad
		\partial_{x_i}^2V=1.
		\]
		Substituting gives
		\begin{align*}
			LV
			&= \sum_{i=1}^d (-\alpha x_i)(x_i)
			+ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^d 
			\left[|x|^4 \cdot 1 + |x|^2(2x_i)(x_i)\right] \\
			&= -\alpha\sum_{i=1}^d x_i^2 + \frac{d}{2}|x|^4 + |x|^2\sum_{i=1}^d x_i^2 \\
			&= -\alpha |x|^2 + \frac{d+2}{2}|x|^4 \\
			&= -2\alpha V + 2(d+2)V^2.
		\end{align*}
		Therefore, if $U$ is chosen sufficiently small so that
		\[
		V(x) \le \frac{\alpha}{2(d+2)}
		\qquad \text{for all } x \in U,
		\]
		then on $U\setminus\{x_{eq}\}$ we have
		\[
		LV \le -\alpha V < 0.
		\]
		By~\cite[Theorem~5.3]{khasminskii2011stochastic}, this implies that $x_{eq}$
		is stable in probability. Since $V$ has an infinitesimal upper limit,
		stability in probability is in fact uniform
		(\cite[Remark~5.3]{khasminskii2011stochastic}).
		
		Next, we prove recurrence. Let $B_r = B_r(x_{eq})$ be a closed ball with $\overline{B_r} \subset U$, 
		and set $D_r = \Gamma \setminus B_r$. The second order part of $L$ is 
		\[
		\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^d a_i^2(x)\,(\xi^i)^2
		= \frac{1}{2}|x|^4 \sum_{i=1}^d (\xi^i)^2.
		\]
		By construction, $a_i(x) = |x|^2 > 0$ on all of $D_r$ for $i = 1,\ldots,d$. 
		Since $\overline{D_r}$ is compact and each $a_i$ is continuous, $
		\inf_{x \in D_r} a_i(x) \geq \lambda > 0$
		for some constant $\lambda > 0$. Therefore,
		\[
		\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^d a_i^2(x)\,(\xi^i)^2
		\geq \frac{\lambda^2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^d (\xi^i)^2
		= \frac{\lambda^2}{2}\,|\xi|^2,
		\]
		so $L$ is uniformly elliptic on $D_r$. Consider the mixed Dirichlet--Neumann boundary value problem
		\[
		\begin{cases}
			Lu_r(x) = -1,              & x \in D_r, \\[0.4em]
			u_r(x) = 0,                & x \in \partial B_r, \\[0.4em]
			\dfrac{\partial u_r}{\partial n}(x) = 0, & x \in \partial\Gamma.
		\end{cases}
		\]
		
		Then from classical regularity theory  for mixed elliptic boundary value problems, there exists a unique weak solution \(u_r\) to this problem. See \cite[Section 9.6]{lions2012non}. Moreover, since the Dirichlet and Neumann parts of the boundary are separated by a non-zero distance, standard boundary regularity theory applies locally near each boundary point after flattening the boundary. In particular, combining the Euclidean half-space regularity results for the pure Dirichlet and pure Neumann problems with a  standard localization argument, one concludes that \(u_r\in C^\infty(\overline{D_r})\). See \cite{gilbarg1983trudinger,agranovich2015sobolev}.
		
		From \cite[Theorem 3.1.3]{pilipenko2014introduction}, $u_r(x) = \mathbb{E}_x[\tau_r]$ 
		is the expected first hitting time of $B_r$. Combining uniform stability in probability with the positive recurrence of
		$B_r$ established above, \cite[Theorem~5.7]{khasminskii2011stochastic} yields
		that $x_{eq}$ is asymptotically stable in probability in the large. We note that Khasminskii's results are stated for diffusions on $\mathbb{R}^\ell$
		without boundary, whereas our process is a reflected diffusion on $\Gamma$.
		However, the proof of \cite[Theorem~5.7]{khasminskii2011stochastic} relies only on the strong Markov property.
		For reflected diffusion~\eqref{eq:sde_eucl}, it is the unique solution of the
		submartingale problem, which yields the strong Markov property
		(see~\cite{pilipenko2014introduction}).
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
Let $\Gamma \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ be a bounded connected open set with smooth boundary, and let $V \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$ satisfy
		$ \nabla^2 V(x) \geq \mu I $
		for some $\mu>0$ and all $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$. Assume that \(V\) has a unique minimizer
		\(x_\ast \in \Gamma\). Then the minimizer $x_\ast$ is asymptotically stable
		in probability in the large for the reflected diffusion \eqref{eq:sde_eucl} with the choice of controls,
		\[
		a_i(x)=\sigma|\nabla V(x)|^2,\qquad
		u_i(x)=-\alpha\partial_{x_i}V(x),
		\]
		for $i=1,\ldots,d$, where $\alpha>0$ and $\sigma>0$ are constants.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
An equilibrium $x_{eq} \in M$ of~\eqref{eq:sdeman} is said to be \emph{stable in probability} for $t \geq 0$ if for any $s \geq 0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$,
		\[
		\lim_{x \to x_{eq}} \mathbb{P}\left\{ \sup_{t > s} d_g\bigl(X^{s,x}(t), x_{eq}\bigr) > \varepsilon \right\} = 0.
		\]

An equilibrium $x_{eq} \in M$ of~\eqref{eq:sdeman} is said to be \emph{(asymptotically) stable in the large} if it is stable in probability and, in addition, for all $s \geq 0$ and $x \in M$,
		\[
		\mathbb{P}\left\{ \lim_{t \to \infty} d_g\bigl(X^{s,x}(t), x_{eq}\bigr) = 0 \right\} = 1.
		\]

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
There exists a smooth function $V \in C^\infty(M)$ such that
		\[
		V(x) \ge 0, \qquad V^{-1}(0)=\{x_{eq}\},
		\]
		and the Hessian of $V$ is positive definite at $x_{\rm eq}$.
		By the Morse lemma \cite[Lemma 2.2]{milnor1963morse}, there exist local coordinates
		$(x_1,\ldots,x_d)$ centered at $x_{eq}$, defined on a neighborhood $U$ of
		$x_{eq}$, such that $
		V(x)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^d x_j^2$
		on $U$. Write
		\[
		A_i=\sum_{j=1}^d c_i^j(x)\,\partial_{x_j},
		\qquad i=1,\ldots,m.
		\]
		Choose
		\[
		a_i(x)=2V(x), \qquad
		u_i(x)=-\alpha\,A_iV(x),
		\qquad i=1,\dots,m,
		\]
		for all $x\in M$.
		
		Here $\alpha>0$ is to be
		determined. Since $dV(x_{eq})=0$ and $V(x_{eq})=0$, we have
		\[
		u_i(x_{eq})=0, \qquad a_i(x_{eq})=0, \qquad i=1,\ldots,m,
		\]
		so $x_{eq}$ is an equilibrium of \eqref{eq:sdeman}.
		
		On $U$ we have
		\[
		A_i a_i= 2A_iV,
		\qquad i=1,\ldots,m.
		\]
		Substituting into
		\[
		LV = \sum_{i=1}^{m} u_i\,A_iV
		+ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}
		\left[a_i^2\,A_i^2V + a_i(A_i a_i)(A_iV)\right]
		\]
		gives
		\begin{align*}
			LV&=\sum_{i=1}^m(-\alpha A_iV)(A_iV)
			+\frac12\sum_{i=1}^m(2V(x))^2 A_i^2V\\
			&\quad+\frac12\sum_{i=1}^m2V(x)(2A_iV)(A_iV)\\
			&=-\alpha\sum_{i=1}^m(A_iV)^2+2V(x)\sum_{i=1}^m(A_iV)^2\\
			&\quad+\frac12(2V(x))^2\sum_{i=1}^m A_i^2V.
		\end{align*}
		
		Let $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_d)^\top$ and
		\begin{align*}
			c_i(x)&:=\bigl(c_i^1(x),\ldots,c_i^d(x)\bigr)^\top\in\mathbb R^d,\\
			G(x)&:=\sum_{i=1}^m c_i(x)c_i(x)^\top.
		\end{align*}
		Since
		\[
		A_iV = \sum_{j=1}^d c_i^j(x)\,x_j = c_i(x)\cdot x,
		\]
		we obtain
		\[
		\sum_{i=1}^m (A_iV)^2 = x^\top G(x)x.
		\]
		By assumption (ii), the vectors $\{A_1(x),\ldots,A_m(x)\}$ span $T_xM$ for
		every $x\in U$, hence $G(x)$ is positive definite for every $x\in U$.
		After shrinking $U$ if necessary, there exist constants $\lambda,\Lambda>0$
		such that
		\[
		\lambda |x|^2 \le x^\top G(x)x \le \Lambda |x|^2
		\qquad \text{for all } x\in U.
		\]
		Since $V(x)=\frac12|x|^2$ on $U$, this implies
		\[
		2\lambda V(x) \le \sum_{i=1}^m (A_iV)^2 \le 2\Lambda V(x)
		\qquad \text{for all } x\in U.
		\]
		Moreover, since $\sum_{i=1}^m A_i^2V$ is smooth on $\overline{U}$, there
		exists a constant $C>0$ such that $
		\left|\sum_{i=1}^m A_i^2V\right| \le C
		$ on  $U.$
		Therefore,
		\begin{align*}
			LV
			&\le -2\alpha\lambda V + 4V(x)\Lambda V + 2V(x)^2 C \\
			&= -2\alpha\lambda V + C_1 V^2,
		\end{align*}
		where $C_1:=4\Lambda+2C$.
		
		Therefore, if $U$ is chosen sufficiently small so that
		\[
		V(x)\le \frac{\alpha\lambda}{C_1}
		\qquad \text{for all } x\in U,
		\]
		then on $U\setminus\{x_{eq}\}$ we have
		\[
		LV \le -\alpha\lambda V < 0.
		\]
		By~\cite[Theorem~5.3]{khasminskii2011stochastic}, this implies that
		$x_{eq}$ is stable in probability. Since $V$ has an infinitesimal upper
		limit, stability in probability is in fact uniform
		(\cite[Remark~5.3]{khasminskii2011stochastic}).
		
		\medskip
		Next we show positive recurrence. Let $W$ be a neighborhood of $x_{eq}$ with
		$W \subset U$, and let $
		D = M \setminus W.$
		The second-order part of $L$ has principal symbol
		\[
		\sigma_x(\xi,\xi)
		=
		\frac12 \sum_{i=1}^m a_i(x)^2 \bigl(g(A_i(x),\xi)\bigr)^2,
		\qquad \xi \in T_xM.
		\]
		
		Since $V^{-1}(0)=\{x_{eq}\}$ and $x_{eq}\notin D$, the function $V$ is strictly
		positive on the compact set $D$. Therefore there exists $\eta>0$ such that
		\[
		V(x)\ge \eta
		\qquad \text{for all } x\in D.
		\]
		If we choose $a_i(x)=2V(x)$ for all $x\in M$, then
		\[
		a_i(x)\ge 2\eta
		\qquad \text{for all } x\in D,\ i=1,\dots,m.
		\]
		
		Consider the function $\sum_{i=1}^m \bigl(g(A_i(x),\xi)\bigr)^2$.
		This is strictly positive away from the equilibrium because
		\[
		\mathrm{span}\{A_1(x),\dots,A_m(x)\}=T_xM
		\qquad \text{for all }x\in D.
		\]
		Hence there exists $\nu>0$ such that
		\[
		\sum_{i=1}^m \bigl(g(A_i(x),\xi)\bigr)^2 \ge \nu |\xi|^2
		\qquad \text{for all }x\in D,\ \xi\in T_xM.
		\]
		Therefore,
		\[
		\sigma_x(\xi,\xi)
		\ge
		\frac{(2\eta)^2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^m \bigl(g(A_i(x),\xi)\bigr)^2
		\ge
		2\eta^2 \nu |\xi|^2.
		\]
		Thus $L$ is uniformly elliptic on all of $D$. By classical elliptic theory, there
		exists a unique bounded solution $u\in C^\infty(\overline{D})$ to the
		Dirichlet boundary value problem
		\[
		\begin{cases}
			Lu(x) = -1, & x\in D, \\[0.4em]
			u(x) = 0,   & x\in \partial W.
		\end{cases}
		\]
		Existence and uniqueness of a weak solution follow by the same
		arguments as in the Euclidean case; see
		\cite[Theorems~1.3 and~1.13]{grigoryan_elliptic_notes}.
		The argument
		extends to the compact Riemannian domain $D$ with smooth boundary in a straightforward manner.	Together with the Sobolev and compact embedding results on manifolds
		\cite[Section~2.10]{aubin2012nonlinear}, these yield solvability.
		Since the data are smooth, standard
		interior and Dirichlet boundary regularity in local charts imply
		$u\in C^\infty(\overline D)$; see~\cite{agranovich2015sobolev}.
		The maximum principle further implies $u\ge0$. Applying Dynkin's formula
		\cite[Theorem~7.4.1]{oksendal2003stochastic} formally to $\tau_W$ gives
		$u(x)=\mathbb{E}_x[\tau_W]\le \|u\|_\infty<\infty$. However, Dynkin's
		formula assumes $\mathbb{E}_x[\tau_W]$ is finite. The argument can be made
		rigorous by applying Dynkin's formula to the bounded stopping time
		$\tau_W\wedge t$ and passing to the limit $t\to\infty$ via monotone
		convergence. Particularly, define the first hitting time of \(W\) by $
		\tau_W:=\inf\{t\ge 0: X_t\in W\},$
		and for \(t>0\) set $
		\sigma_t:=\tau_W\wedge t.$
		Then \(\sigma_t\) is a bounded stopping time, so Dynkin's formula applies:
		\[
		\mathbb E_x[u(X_{\sigma_t})]
		=
		u(x)+\mathbb E_x\int_0^{\sigma_t} Lu(X_s)\,ds.
		\]
		Since \(Lu=-1\) on \(D\), this becomes
		$
		\mathbb E_x[u(X_{\sigma_t})]
		=
		u(x)-\mathbb E_x[\sigma_t],
		$
		or equivalently,$
		\mathbb E_x[\sigma_t]
		=
		u(x)-\mathbb E_x[u(X_{\sigma_t})].
		$
		Because \(u\ge 0\) on \(D\), it follows that $
		\mathbb E_x[\sigma_t]\le u(x).$
		On the other hand, \(\sigma_t=\tau_W\wedge t\) is increasing in \(t\) and
		\[
		\sigma_t\uparrow \tau_W
		\qquad \text{as } t\to\infty.
		\]
		Therefore, by the monotone convergence theorem,
		\[
		\mathbb E_x[\tau_W]
		=
		\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb E_x[\tau_W\wedge t]
		\le u(x)<\infty.
		\]
		Hence the hitting time \(\tau_W\) has finite expectation, so \(W\) is positive recurrent.
		
		Combining uniform stability in probability with the positive recurrence of
		$W$ established above, \cite[Theorem~5.7]{khasminskii2011stochastic}
		yields that $x_{eq}$ is asymptotically stable in probability in the large.
