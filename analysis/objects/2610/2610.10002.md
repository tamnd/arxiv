<!-- schemedefinition-1: body_md -->
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple graph and let $D\subseteq\mathbb F_2^V$ be nonempty.
A scheme consists of a finite probability space $(R,\mu)$ with full support
($\mu(r)>0$ for every $r\in R$), whose distribution is independent of the
input $x\in D$, and share maps in arbitrary finite alphabets,
\[
 W_e:D\times R\to\mathcal W_e,\qquad e\in E.
\]
Vertex $v$ sees $\mathsf{View}_v(x,r)=(W_e(x,r):e\ni v)$.
Correctness requires
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf{View}_v(x,r)=\mathsf{View}_v(y,s)\ \Longrightarrow\ x_v=y_v
 \label{eq:correctness}
\end{equation}
for all $x,y\in D$ and $r,s\in R$.
For a source draw $\mathsf R\sim\mu$, perfect privacy requires
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf{View}_v(x,\mathsf R)\stackrel{d}{=}\mathsf{View}_v(y,\mathsf R)
 \quad\text{whenever }x_v=y_v,
 \label{eq:privacy}
\end{equation}
where $\stackrel{d}{=}$ denotes equality in distribution.
<!-- end schemedefinition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:zero}
A deterministic scheme exists if and only if every active vertex is incident to
a pair-relation edge.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:local-switching}
For every vertex $v$ there is a \emph{switch bit}
$\alpha_v:D\to\mathbb F_2$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf{View}_v(x,r)=A_v^{x_v}(r\oplus\alpha_v(x)).
  \label{eq:local-switching}
\end{equation}
If both local bits occur, the reference-view sets
$\{A_v^0(0),A_v^0(1)\}$ and $\{A_v^1(0),A_v^1(1)\}$ are disjoint.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
With the uniform source, privacy makes the two views at $x$ the same
unordered pair, with repetitions allowed, as at its reference input.
Set $\alpha_v(x)=0$ when their ordering agrees with the reference and
$\alpha_v(x)=1$ when it is reversed.  If the two reference views coincide,
choose $\alpha_v(x)=0$.  A view shared by different local bits would
contradict correctness.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:onebit}
For every finite graph $G$ and every nonempty binary domain $D$,
$m_G(D)\le2$ if and only if there is a signed subgraph $(H,q)$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\par\hangindent 40\p@ $H$ contains no pair-relation edge;
\par\hangindent 40\p@ $(H,q)$ is $D$-flat;
\par\hangindent 40\p@ every active vertex is either incident in $G$ to a pair-relation edge or
is switched in $(H,q)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix $x^0\in D$.  For $e=uv\in F$, set
\begin{equation}
 g_e(x)=q_u(e)(x_u\oplus x_u^0)\oplus q_v(e)(x_v\oplus x_v^0).
 \label{eq:g-edge}
\end{equation}
Flatness gives
\[
 \bigoplus_{e\in F}z_eg_e(x)=\operatorname{turn}_q(z)\cdot(x\oplus x^0)=0
 \qquad(z\in Z_1(H;\mathbb F_2)).
\]
Choose a root in each connected component of $H$.  Define $h_v(x)$ as the
XOR of the $g_e(x)$ along a path from the root to $v$, with value zero at
the root.  The XOR of two such paths belongs to the cycle space, so the
vanishing cycle sums make $h_v(x)$ independent of the chosen path.
In particular, for $e=uv\in F$,
\begin{equation}
 h_u(x)\oplus h_v(x)=g_e(x).
 \label{eq:potential}
\end{equation}
For uniform $R\in\mathbb F_2$, the share on $e=uv\in F$ can therefore be written
at either endpoint as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 W_e(x,R)&=R\oplus h_u(x)\oplus q_u(e)(x_u\oplus x_u^0)\\
         &=R\oplus h_v(x)\oplus q_v(e)(x_v\oplus x_v^0).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:selected-share}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the XOR of the two displayed expressions is
$h_u(x)\oplus h_v(x)\oplus g_e(x)=0$ by \eqref{eq:g-edge}--\eqref{eq:potential}.
Pair-relation edges carry one endpoint bit and all remaining edges carry
constants.  At $v$, write $U_v=R\oplus h_v(x)$; the selected shares are
$(U_v\oplus q_v(e)(x_v\oplus x_v^0):e\ni v)$.
Since $U_v$ is uniform, this tuple's distribution depends only on $x_v$;
pair-relation shares are also determined by $x_v$, proving privacy of the
complete view.  Opposite incidence signs reveal $x_v\oplus x_v^0$, and a
pair-relation edge reveals $x_v$ deterministically.  Every active vertex
therefore reconstructs, while an inactive vertex already has a fixed bit.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
A deterministic scheme yields the empty selected subgraph by
Proposition~\ref{prop:zero}.  Otherwise uniformize the two-state source and
apply Lemma~\ref{lem:local-switching}.  Keep the regular edges introduced
above; Appendix~\ref{app:one-bit-proofs} derives their compatibility equation
\eqref{eq:edge-compatibility} from endpoint privacy.
Correctness forces every active vertex not covered by a pair relation to see
both incidence signs, hence to be switched.  XORing
\eqref{eq:edge-compatibility} over any $z\in Z_1(H;\mathbb F_2)$ cancels every
switch bit and gives
$\operatorname{turn}_q(z)\cdot x=\bigoplus_{e\in F:z_e=1}c_e$ throughout $D$.
Thus the signed subgraph $(H,q)$ is $D$-flat.
The full necessity argument is in Appendix~\ref{app:one-bit-proofs}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:k4-onebit}
For every nonempty $D\subseteq\mathbb F_2^4$,
\begin{equation}
 m_{K_4}(D)\le2
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Rel}(D)\cap E_{\rm even}\ne\{0\}.
 \label{eq:k4-onebit}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, some pair parity or the total parity is constant on $D$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Suppose first that a pair parity is constant.  Relabel so that $12$ is a
pair-relation edge, and sign the path $1-3-4-2$ oppositely at $3$ and $4$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:k4-certificates}(a)).  Delete every path edge that is itself a
pair-relation edge.  The remaining forest contains no such edge and is
$D$-flat automatically: its cycle space is $\{0\}$.
Vertices $1,2$ are pair-covered; each of $3,4$ either is pair-covered
by a deleted incident edge or retains both opposite signs and is switched.
Theorem~\ref{thm:onebit} therefore gives a scheme with at most two states.

If the total parity is constant and there is no pair-relation edge, sign a
Hamiltonian cycle oppositely at every vertex (Fig.~\ref{fig:k4-certificates}(b)).
Its cycle space has one nonzero element, whose turn vector is $1111\in\operatorname{Rel}(D)$.
Thus the cycle is $D$-flat and switches every vertex, so the same theorem
applies.  The case with a pair-relation edge was already handled above.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:C5-scheme}
Every binary domain contained in $C_5$ has a three-state scheme.  With
$R\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb Z_3)$, set, in $\mathbb Z_3$,
\begin{align}
 W_{12}&=R,            &W_{13}&=0,          &W_{14}&=R-x_1,\nonumber\\
 W_{23}&=R-x_2,        &W_{24}&=0,          &W_{34}&=R-x_2-x_3.
 \label{eq:C5-shares}
\end{align}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Parties $1$ and $2$ see $(R,0,R-x_1)$ and $(R,R-x_2,0)$ and recover their
bits by subtraction.  With $U=R-x_2$, party $3$ sees $(0,U,U-x_3)$; with
$U=R-x_1$ and $x_1-x_2-x_3\equiv x_4\pmod3$, party $4$ sees
$(U,0,U+x_4)$.  Thus each party reconstructs, while uniformity of $U$ makes
its complete-view distribution depend only on its local bit.  Restricting the
input domain preserves the scheme.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:k4-upper}
For every nonempty $D\subseteq\mathbb F_2^4$, one has $m_{K_4}(D)\le4$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $R,S$ be independent uniform bits.  On the six edges of $K_4$, set
\begin{align}
 W_{12}&=R,& W_{13}&=R\oplus x_1,&
 W_{34}&=R\oplus x_1\oplus x_3,\nonumber\\
 W_{14}&=S,& W_{24}&=S\oplus x_4,&
 W_{23}&=S\oplus x_4\oplus x_2.
 \label{eq:k4-four-state}
\end{align}
Correctness follows from
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x_1&=W_{12}\oplus W_{13},& x_2&=W_{23}\oplus W_{24},\\
 x_3&=W_{13}\oplus W_{34},& x_4&=W_{14}\oplus W_{24}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Up to a fixed ordering of its incident edges, each vertex $v$ sees
$(U_v,U_v\oplus x_v,Z_v)$.  For vertices $1,2,3,4$, respectively, take
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (U_1,Z_1)&=(R,S),\\
 (U_2,Z_2)&=(S\oplus x_4,R),\\
 (U_3,Z_3)&=(R\oplus x_1,S\oplus x_4\oplus x_2),\\
 (U_4,Z_4)&=(S,R\oplus x_1\oplus x_3).
\end{aligned}
\]
For every fixed input these are pairs of independent uniform bits, so the
complete view distribution depends only on $x_v$.  The source $(R,S)$ has
four states, and the same formulas work on every subdomain $D$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:full-k4}
For every nonempty $D\subseteq\mathbb F_2^4$,
\begin{equation}
 m_{K_4}(D)=
 \begin{cases}
 1, & \text{if every active vertex is pair-covered},\\
 2, & \text{if not, and }\operatorname{Rel}(D)\cap E_{\rm even}\ne\{0\},\\
 3, & \text{if }D\sim_{\Gamma_4}T_6\text{ or }D\sim_{\Gamma_4}C_5,\\
 4, & \text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:full-classification}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{K_4}(D)\in\{0,1,\log_2 3,2\},
  \label{eq:spectrum}
\end{equation}
and the value $\log_2 3$ occurs exactly in the orbits of $T_6$ and $C_5$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Proposition~\ref{prop:zero} gives the first case, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:k4-onebit} gives the second after excluding the first.
Suppose now that $m_{K_4}(D)>2$ but $m_{K_4}(D)\le3$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:odd-tetra}, $D$ contains a four-point minimal obstruction
$T$.  Heredity and the values in Table~\ref{tab:orbit-summary}
(proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit-values}) force $T\in\mathcal O_6$.
Apply a symmetry to normalize $T=T_6$.

For every $p\notin T_6\cup\{1100\}$, the extension check in
Table~\ref{tab:extension} in Appendix~\ref{app:k4-signed} exhibits an
$\mathcal O_1$--$\mathcal O_5$ obstruction inside $T_6\cup\{p\}$.  Its minimum support
is four, so heredity forbids $p\in D$.  Therefore
$T_6\subseteq D\subseteq C_5$, giving exactly $D=T_6$ or $D=C_5$.
Conversely, the construction in Theorem~\ref{thm:C5-scheme} and the
relation spaces in \eqref{eq:T6C5-rel} give minimum support three for
both sets.  Thus precisely these two orbits have minimum support three.
Every remaining domain needs at least four states, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:k4-upper} supplies a four-state scheme for every $K_4$
domain.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:heredity}
If $D'\subseteq D$ is nonempty, then $m_G(D')\le m_G(D)$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Restrict every share map to $D'\times R$.  Both
\eqref{eq:correctness} and \eqref{eq:privacy} are restrictions of conditions
already valid on $D$.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
In a deterministic scheme, privacy makes every edge share $W_{uv}$ a function
of $x_v$ at endpoint $v$ and a function of $x_u$ at endpoint $u$.  If $v$ is
active, correctness forces some incident share to be nonconstant in $x_v$.
Because the secrets are binary, that share determines $x_v$ from $x_u$, and
$x_u\oplus x_v$ is constant on $D$.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:odd-tetra}
A nonempty domain $D\subseteq\mathbb F_2^4$ satisfies $m_{K_4}(D)>2$ if and only if it contains
an odd affine tetrahedron.  Consequently, the inclusion-minimal one-bit-negative
domains are exactly the odd affine tetrahedra, and every minimal obstruction has
four points.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Theorem~\ref{thm:k4-onebit} says that negativity is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 L(D)^\perp\cap E_{\rm even}=\{0\}.
 \label{eq:negative-even}
\end{equation}
Fix $x^0\in D$.  The evaluation map
$a\mapsto(a\cdot(x\oplus x^0))_{x\in D\setminus\{x^0\}}$ is therefore injective on
$E_{\rm even}$.  Three of its coordinates retain rank three; call the
corresponding points $x^1,x^2,x^3$.  Their differences span a
three-dimensional space $U$, since otherwise
$\dim(E_{\rm even}\cap U^\perp)\ge1$.  The unique nonzero normal to $U$ is thus
odd, so $\{x^0,x^1,x^2,x^3\}$ is an odd affine tetrahedron.

Conversely, such a tetrahedron has a one-dimensional relation space generated
by an odd vector, hence no nonzero even relation and is negative by
Theorem~\ref{thm:k4-onebit}.  Heredity handles every domain containing it.
Every proper subset has $\dim L\le2$, so
$\dim(L^\perp\cap E_{\rm even})\ge1$ and is one-bit-positive, proving
minimality.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:weight-collapse}
For every graph and domain, a scheme with three positive source states whose
weight vector is neither $\mathsf U$ nor $\mathsf Q$ implies a scheme for the same instance
with source support at most two.  Consequently, if $m_G(D)>2$ but
$m_G(D)\le3$, every minimum-support realization has weights $\mathsf U$ or $\mathsf Q$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Assume the weight vector is neither $\mathsf U$ nor $\mathsf Q$.
If the weights are distinct and none is $1/2$, different nonempty subsets
of $R$ have different masses.  Thus, at inputs with the same local bit,
privacy forces the blocks producing each view value to contain exactly the
same source states.  Fixing any one source state therefore gives identical
views in every privacy comparison, hence a deterministic private scheme;
correctness survives restriction of the source support.

If exactly two weights agree, then outside $\mathsf U,\mathsf Q$ equal-mass blocks either
both contain the uniquely weighted state or both omit it.  For inputs with
the same local bit, the view value produced by that state is therefore the
same.  Fix that state to obtain a deterministic scheme as above.

In the remaining case, write $R=\{r_\star,\ell_1,\ell_2\}$, where $r_\star$
has weight $1/2$ and $\ell_1,\ell_2$ have distinct positive weights.
The only distinct nonempty source blocks of equal mass are
$\{r_\star\}$ and $\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}$.
Fix a vertex and two inputs with the same local bit.
Outside the partition $\{\{r_\star\},\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}\}$, privacy matches
the view values state by state: blocks with unique masses determine their
source subsets, and these also determine any remaining block.
In particular, for the partition into three singleton blocks, the two
light-state blocks determine the remaining heavy-state block.
In the exceptional partition, privacy may only preserve or exchange the
two mass-$1/2$ view values.
Restrict the source to $R'=\{r_\star,\ell_1\}$ and give both states weight
$1/2$.  In every privacy comparison the two views are unchanged or exchanged,
so their uniform distribution is unchanged, even if the values coincide.
Privacy is therefore preserved, and correctness survives restriction of the
source support.

\label{thm:three-normal}
A three-state scheme with weights $p$ exists if and only if the preceding data
can be chosen so that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 M_{v,x}(a)=M_{v,y}(a)
 \quad\text{if }x_v=y_v,\ a\in \mathcal A_{v,x_v},\\
 \sigma_v(x,r)=\Sigma_v\bigl(\kappa_{v,x}(r)\bigr)
 \quad\text{for }(x,r)\in\mathcal X,
 \end{gathered}
\]
where each map
$\Sigma_v:\bigsqcup_{b\in S_v}\mathcal A_{v,b}\to
\prod_{e\ni v}(\mathcal X/{\equiv_e})$ is injective.  In that case canonical
edge shares are $W_e(x,r)=[(x,r)]_{\equiv_e}$.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Given a scheme, take $\mathcal A_{v,b}$ to be the support of the view distribution at
local bit $b$ and set $\kappa_{v,x}(r)=\mathsf{View}_v(x,r)$.  Privacy gives
the equality of local masses, while correctness makes the two local-bit label
sets disjoint.  Equal $\kappa_v$-labels generate $\equiv_e$ on every incident
edge, so $\sigma_v$ factors through the local label.  The original edge share
is constant on every $\equiv_e$-class; hence equality of two resulting
signatures would make the corresponding original views equal, and the factor
$\Sigma_v$ is injective.

Conversely, use the stated canonical shares.  Their incident view at $v$ is
$\Sigma_v(\kappa_{v,x}(r))$; injectivity makes it equivalent to the local
label.  The mass equality therefore gives privacy, and disjoint label sets for
the two bits give correctness.

\label{lem:o1-uniform-flip}
For every active flip $i$ and unchanged vertex $u\ne i$, privacy gives a
permutation $\pi_u\in S_3$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf{View}_u(e_i,\pi_u(r))=\mathsf{View}_u(0,r)\qquad(r\in R).
 \label{eq:o1-view-perm}
\end{equation}

\[
 W_{uv}(0,\pi_v^{-1}\pi_u(r))=W_{uv}(0,r)\qquad(r\in R).
\]
Thus $\pi_v^{-1}\pi_u$ preserves each block of $P_{uv}$ individually, and
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{e\ni i}d_e\ge3,\qquad
 \sum_{uv:\,u,v\ne i}d_{uv}\le2.
 \label{eq:o1-local-block-counts}
\end{equation}
More precisely, let the three unchanged vertices be $u_1,u_2,u_3$.
If
\[
 \pi_{u_1}=\pi_{u_2},\qquad
 \pi_{u_3}=\pi_{u_1}\circ\gamma
\]
for a three-cycle $\gamma$ of the source states, then $P_{iu_3}$ is the
fine partition, equivalently $d_{iu_3}=2$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Because the source is uniform, equality of the view distributions at $0$ and
$e_i$ means equality of the corresponding multisets of three view values.
A permutation $\pi_u\in S_3$ therefore matches their occurrences, giving
\eqref{eq:o1-view-perm}.  Comparing the two endpoint expressions for
$W_{uv}(e_i,\pi_u(r))$ gives the displayed blockwise invariance.
To examine correctness at the changed vertex $i$, write its base-input and
changed-input views as
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F_i(s)&=(W_{iu}(0,s):u\ne i),\\
 G_i(t)&=(W_{iu}(0,\pi_u^{-1}(t)):u\ne i).
\end{aligned}
\]
The privacy matching at each unchanged $u$ gives
$W_{iu}(e_i,t)=W_{iu}(0,\pi_u^{-1}(t))$, so $G_i(t)$ is exactly the
view at $i$ after the flip, expressed using base-input shares.
Thus $F_i(R)$ and $G_i(R)$ are the supports before and after the flip.
They must be disjoint because the local secret at $i$ changed.

If the sum of the incident $d_e$ values were at most two, either at most one coordinate would
be nonconstant, when all other coordinates are the same constants and its
marginal support makes $F_i(R)$ and $G_i(R)$ meet, or there would be two
coordinates with two-block partitions.  For distinct two-block partitions,
the base and changed joint supports have sizes at least three and two in a
four-point product and must meet.  For equal partitions, either the two
transported singleton states coincide, giving the same diagonal support, or
the third state lies in both doubleton blocks.  This proves the first bound.

Up to common left multiplication, conjugation, and permutation of the
unchanged vertices, the permutation triple is
\[
 (1,1,1),\ (1,1,\tau),\ (1,1,\gamma),\
 (1,\tau_1,\tau_2),\ (1,\gamma,\gamma^{-1}),
\]
where $\tau_1\ne\tau_2$ are transpositions and $\gamma$ is a three-cycle.

relative permutations around the opposite triangle are, respectively,

partition to be constant; under a transposition it forces the two exchanged
states into the same block, so the corresponding $d_e$ is at most one.  The sums of $d_e$ over the opposite triangles

\label{lem:o1-heavy-light}
For every active flip $i$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\par\hangindent 40\p@ at least two edges incident to $i$ are nonconstant at the base input;
\par\hangindent 40\p@ at least two edges in the triangle opposite $i$ are constant at the base
input.
\end{enumerate}

Inspecting the five partitions of $\{r_\star,\ell_1,\ell_2\}$ shows that a privacy matching
at an unchanged vertex is either induced by a source permutation fixing $r_\star$
(and possibly swapping the light states), or its full base-view partition is
$\{\{r_\star\},\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}\}$ and the matching exchanges the two mass-$1/2$
blocks; call this exchange a \emph{root swap}.  Indeed, fine matchings fix $r_\star$, a
two-block partition isolating one light state can only be transported to the
one isolating the other, and only the displayed partition has another
equal-mass block exchange.

Let $T$ be the set of unchanged vertices using root swaps.  If $T=\varnothing$,
all permutation-induced matchings fix $r_\star$, so the changed star at source state $r_\star$ equals
the base star at $r_\star$, violating correctness.  If all three unchanged vertices
use root swaps, each base full view has partition
$\{\{r_\star\},\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}\}$; every
incident edge is therefore constant or heavy-versus-light.  Swapping all three
roots exchanges the base heavy and light star tuples, leaving their support
unchanged.  Hence $1\le|T|\le2$.

Consider an opposite edge crossing the nontrivial cut $T\mid T^c$.  At the
root-swap endpoint, a nonconstant edge would exchange its heavy and light
labels; at the permutation-matched endpoint, source state $r_\star$ is fixed and its heavy label
is preserved.  These requirements are incompatible, so every crossing edge is
constant.  A nontrivial cut of a triangle has two crossing edges, proving (ii).

If at most one edge incident to $i$ were nonconstant, every base-constant
coordinate would remain the same point mass after the flip, and the sole
nonconstant coordinate would have the same marginal support before and after.
The two star supports would intersect, proving (i).
