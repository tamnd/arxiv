<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:commute} The two operations, $\mathtt{diag}()$ and $\overline R()$, are inverse bijections between the family of simple non-bipartite quadrangulations of the projective plane  and the family of 2-connected strongly involutive self-dual maps. They satisfy the following properties.  
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[a)] If $G$ is a 2-connected strongly involutive self-dual map, then $G=\mathtt{diag}(\overline R(G))$ as planar maps.
    \item[b)] If $Q$ is a non-bipartite quadrangulation of the projective plane, then $Q=\overline R(\mathtt{diag}(Q))$ as planar maps.
    \item[c)] If $Q$ is a non-bipartite quadrangulation of the projective plane with cover map $\widetilde Q$, then $\widetilde Q= R(\mathtt{diag}(Q))$ as projective planar maps.

\end{enumerate}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:diameterispolyhedral}
    If $X$ is an extremal point set consisting of $n\ge 4$ distinct points, then there is a unique simple non-bipartite quadrangulation $\mathcal{Q}(X)=(V,E)$ of the projective plane such that there is a bijective vector labeling $\mathbf{u}:V\rightarrow X$ with the following properties. 
    \begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
    \item $ij\in E$ if and only if $\mathbf{u}_i\mathbf{u}_j$ is a diameter of $X$.
    \item $(\mathcal{Q}(X),\mathbf{u})$ is neighborhood-coned.
   \item The cone rotation system of $(\mathcal{Q}(X),\mathbf{u})$ matches one of the rotation systems of $\mathcal{Q}(X)$.
    \end{enumerate}
Furthermore, this unique quadrangulation is $\overline{R}(\mathtt{skel}\mathcal{B}(X))$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
First we argue for uniqueness. If there is such a quadrangulation $\mathcal{Q}(X)$, then its graph is fixed by $a)$. Its two rotation systems are fixed by $b)$ and $c)$. As the graph and the rotation system determine the NBQP map $\mathcal{Q}(X)$ is determined by the properties $a),b),c)$.
    
    Next, we show that $\overline{R}(\mathtt{skel}\mathcal{B}(X))$ works. By Corollary  \ref{cor:canonicalissisd} we know that $\mathtt{skel}\mathcal{B}(X)$ is strongly involutive self-dual and by Theorem \ref{thm:2connect} it is 2-connected. Hence, by Theorem \ref{thm:commute} $\mathcal{Q}(X):=\overline R(\mathtt{skel}\mathcal{B}(X))$ is an NBQP. Let $\mathbf{u}:V(\mathcal{Q}(X))\rightarrow X$ be the natural vector labeling. Consider now how the radial graph construction appears geometrically. We know that a given vertex $v$ is incident to a face $F_w$ if and only if $vw$ is a diameter pair. Hence $R(\mathtt{skel}\mathcal{B}(X))$, the radial graph of the skeleton, is the bipartite graph on vertex classes $X$ and $\{F_x|x\in X\}$ with edge set $\{(v,F_w) | v,w\in X \text{ and }|v-w|=1 \}$. By Theorem \ref{thm:canonicalselfduality} we know that in the canonical self-duality $v$ is sent to $F_v$. Therefore, the reduced radial graph $\mathcal{Q}(X)$ is indeed the diameter graph of $X$. By Lemma \ref{lem:precise} $(a)$, it is also neighborhood-coned.

    Finally, recall the rotation definition for an NBQP (Section \ref{sec:rotation}). We lift the quadrangulation to the sphere and consider the rotation in one of the color classes. We have also seen that the lifted quadrangulation map $\widetilde Q$ is the same as the radial map of the diagonal graph of the NBQP; this is exactly Theorem \ref{thm:commute} $(c)$.  Hence, in this case, a possible rotation system for $\mathcal{Q}(X)$ is given by the rotation of the vertices of $R(\mathtt{skel}\mathcal{B}(X))$ that correspond to the vertex class $X$. That is, for part $c)$ we have to show that faces of $\mathcal{B}(X)$ incident to a given vertex $v$ correspond to the vertices of $X$ that are diameter far from $v$ and the faces appear in the right order. This follows immediately from Lemma \ref{lem:precise} $(b)-(c)$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:stressfree}
    Suppose $Q=(V,E)$ is a NBQP map with a rotation system $\rho$ and $\mathbf{u}:V\rightarrow \mathbb{R}^3$ is a vector labeling of $Q$ with the following properties:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
        \item $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ is neighborhood-coned.
        \item The cone rotation system of $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ matches the rotation system $\rho$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Then $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ is stress-free.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We adopt the steps of the famous proof of Cauchy's stress-freeness theorem. Suppose $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ carries a nonzero stress $S$. Color the edges of $Q$ red where $S_{ij}>0$ and blue if $S_{ij}<0$ and also apply this coloring to the bars of the framework. If we delete all edges where $S_{ij}=0$, then we end up with a 2-colored graph. From Lemma \ref{lemma:quiet} we see that there must be a quiet vertex in this graph, that is, a vertex $i$ where the red (and blue) edges are consecutive around $i$. As  $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ is neighborhood-coned and the cone rotation system matches the rotation system $\rho$, this means that there is a plane $H$ that goes through $\mathbf{u}_i$ and separates the red and blue edges emanating from $\mathbf{u}_i$, see Figure \ref{fig:stressfreeproof}. The idea is to show that $i$ cannot be in equilibrium, as all edges are pushing or pulling it into the same half-space bounded by $H$. Let $\mathbf{n}$ be the normal vector of $H$ pointing to the half-space containing the red edges. For each red edge $ij$ we have $\mathbf{n}^{\top}(\mathbf{u}_j-\mathbf{u}_i)>0$ and hence $S_{ij}\mathbf{n}^{\top}(\mathbf{u}_j-\mathbf{u}_i)>0$. On the other hand, for each blue edge $\mathbf{n}^{\top}(\mathbf{u}_j-\mathbf{u}_i)<0$, which also implies $S_{ij}\mathbf{n}^{\top}(\mathbf{u}_j-\mathbf{u}_i)>0$. For the uncolored edges incident to $i$, we simply have $S_{ij}\mathbf{n}^{\top}(\mathbf{u}_j-\mathbf{u}_i)=0$. Therefore, 
    $\sum\limits_{j\in N(i)}S_{ij}\mathbf{n}^{\top}(\mathbf{u}_j-\mathbf{u}_i)>0$  and hence $\mathbf{n}^{\top}( \sum\limits_{j\in N(i)}S_{ij}(\mathbf{u}_j-\mathbf{u}_i))>0$.     As $S$ is a stress, we have $\sum\limits_{j\in N(i)}S_{ij}(\mathbf{u}_j-\mathbf{u}_i)=0$, a contradiction.

As $a_1a_2$ is a contractible diagonal, $a_1a_2$ is not an edge and $\mathbf{u}_{a_1}=\mathbf{u}_{a_2}$ is not a problem in the sense that $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ is still a framework.

Then we show that there is an infinitesimal motion that pulls $a_1$ and $a_2$ apart in the right relative direction. Then we turn the infinitesimal motion into a real flexing. Finally we show that a small initial part of the flexing satisfies the required properties $a),b)$ and $c)$.

    \begin{lemma}\label{lem:splitstressfree}
        $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ is stress-free.
    \end{lemma}
    \begin{proof}
     Suppose that $S:E \rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ is a stress on $(Q,\mathbf{u})$. Let $S'$ be the modified version of $S$ where we set $S'_{ab_1}=S_{a_1b_1}+S_{a_2b_1}$ and $S'_{ab_2}=S_{a_1b_2}+S_{a_2b_2}$. Clearly if $S$ is a stress for $(Q,\mathbf{u})$, then $S'$ is a stress for $(Q/a_1a_2,\mathbf{u}')$. On the other hand $(Q/a_1a_2,\mathbf{u}')$ is stress-free by Lemma \ref{lem:stressfree}, hence $S'$ must be the zero-stress, i.e. $S_{a_1b_1}+S_{a_2b_1}=0$, $S_{a_1b_2}+S_{a_2b_2}=0$ and $S$ is zero on the other edges. By the definition of a stress, this implies that $S_{a_1b_1}(\mathbf{u}_{b_1}-\mathbf{u}_{a_1})+S_{a_1b_2}(\mathbf{u}_{b_2}-\mathbf{u}_{a_1})=0$. As $\mathbf{u}_{b_1}-\mathbf{u}_{a_1}$ and  $\mathbf{u}_{b_2}-\mathbf{u}_{a_1}$ are not parallel $S$ is also zero on $a_1b_1$ and $a_1b_2$ and similarly on $a_2b_1,a_2b_2$. Hence $S$ is the zero-stress. Therefore, $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ is stress-free. 
    
    \end{proof}
    We note that the previous lemma is essentially the same as Whiteley's argument in \cite{whiteley1990vertex} where he studies a similar vertex splitting procedure for generic frameworks. Our framework is very much non-generic, hence we cannot directly apply those results. 
     
     \begin{lemma}\label{lem:infinitesimalexists}
         There is a non-rigid infinitesimal motion $\mathbf{v}$ of $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ such that $\mathbf{v}_{a_1}-\mathbf{v}_{a_2}\neq \mathbf{0}$. Furthermore, we can pick $\mathbf{v}$ so that $\mathbf{n}^\top (\mathbf{v}_{a_1}-\mathbf{v}_{a_2})<0$.
     \end{lemma}
    \begin{proof}
        If $\mathbf{v}_{a_1}=\mathbf{v}_{a_2}$ then $\mathbf{v}$ is also an infinitesimal motion of $(Q/a_1a_2,\mathbf{u}')$ by setting $\mathbf{v}_a=\mathbf{v}_{a_1}$. Hence it is enough to show that $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ has more non-rigid infinitesimal motions than $(Q/a_1a_2,\mathbf{u}')$, that is we need that $\dim(\mathbf{Inf}_{(Q,\mathbf{u})})>\dim(\mathbf{Inf}_{(Q/a_1a_2,\mathbf{u}')})$. This is intuitively clear: splitting the vertex should introduce 3 degrees of freedom, doubling the two edges takes away only 2 of those. As the frameworks are stress-free we can calculate these dimensions. Since $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ and $(Q/a_1a_2,\mathbf{u}')$ are stress-free, we have $\dim(\mathbf{Str}_{(Q,\mathbf{u})})=0$ and $\dim(\mathbf{Str}_{(Q/a_1a_2,\mathbf{u}')})=0$. Plugging this into \eqref{eq:dimension} we obtain

     
    \begin{equation*}
        \dim(\mathbf{Inf}_{(Q,\mathbf{u})})=3n-(2n-2)+\dim(\mathbf{Str}_{(Q,\mathbf{u})})=n+2,
    \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*}
        \dim(\mathbf{Inf}_{(Q/a_1a_2,\mathbf{u}')})=3(n-1)-(2n-4)+\dim(\mathbf{Str}_{(Q/a_1a_2,\mathbf{u}')})=n+1.
    \end{equation*}
    
  
     Hence, we have found an infinitesimal motion $\mathbf{v}$ of $(Q,\mathbf{u})$ such that $\mathbf{v}_{a_1}-\mathbf{v}_{a_2}\neq \mathbf{0}$. We need to turn this into a proper flexing, but there is a last adjustment to make. It is important that we pull $a_1$ and $a_2$ apart in the right direction.
    
    Recall that $\mathbf{n}=(\mathbf{u}_{a_1}-\mathbf{u}_{b_1})\times(\mathbf{u}_{a_1}-\mathbf{u}_{b_2})$, that is, it points to the halfspace that contains $N_1$. Since $\mathbf{v}$ is an infinitesimal motion we have $(\mathbf{v}_{b_1}-\mathbf{v}_{a_1})^\top(\mathbf{u}_{b_1}-\mathbf{u}_{a_1})=0$ and $(\mathbf{v}_{b_1}-\mathbf{v}_{a_2})^\top(\mathbf{u}_{b_1}-\mathbf{u}_{a_2})=0$. Using that $\mathbf{u}_{a_1}=\mathbf{u}_{a_2}$ the second can be rewritten as $(\mathbf{v}_{b_1}-\mathbf{v}_{a_2})^\top(\mathbf{u}_{b_1}-\mathbf{u}_{a_1})=0$. Hence 

        $$(\mathbf{v}_{b_1}-\mathbf{v}_{a_1})^\top(\mathbf{u}_{b_1}-\mathbf{u}_{a_1})-(\mathbf{v}_{b_1}-\mathbf{v}_{a_2})^\top(\mathbf{u}_{b_1}-\mathbf{u}_{a_1})=0$$

        This simplifies to $(\mathbf{v}_{a_2}-\mathbf{v}_{a_1})^\top(\mathbf{u}_{b_1}-\mathbf{u}_{a_1})=0$.       \end{proof}

\label{thm:thrpairtyemb}

<!-- theorem-12: body_md -->
A graph on $n\ge4$ vertices is a diameter graph in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with $2n-2$ edges if and only if it is the graph of a simple non-bipartite quadrangulation of the projective plane.
<!-- end theorem-12 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Suppose $X$ is extremal and $|X|\ge 4$. By Theorem \ref{thm:2connect} and Corollary \ref{cor:canonicalissisd} we know that $\mathtt{skel}(\mathcal{B}(X))$ is 2-connected and strongly involutive self-dual. Let $G=\mathtt{skel}(\mathcal{B}(X))$. Hence, the diameter graph $\Gamma(X)=\mathtt{gr}(\mathcal{Q}(X))=\mathtt{gr}(\overline{R}(G))$ is an NBQP graph. The other direction is given by Theorem \ref{thm:main_nbqp_to_diam}.
<!-- end proof-11 -->
<!-- theorem-13: body_md -->
A graph on at least 2 vertices is a diameter graph in $\mathbb{R}^3$ if and only if it is a subgraph of an NBQP graph and it has at least one edge.
<!-- end theorem-13 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $Q$ be an NBQP map, and let $Q'$ be a subgraph of it with at least one edge. By Theorem \ref{thm:main_nbqp_to_diam} there is an extremal set $X$ so that $\mathcal{Q}(X)=Q$. By Lemma \ref{lem:stressfree} this diameter realization of $Q$ is stress-free. Since the framework is stress-free, every sufficiently small perturbation of its edge lengths admits a realization arbitrarily close to the original framework.  This follows from applying the Implicit Function Theorem to the squared-edge-length map, see 10.5 and 15.2.2 in \cite{lovasz2019graphs}. (Essentially, the stress-freeness condition expresses that the hypersurfaces corresponding to the edge length constraints meet transversally at the solution $X$.) 

    Therefore we can prescribe edge lengths 1 on $E(Q')$ and edge lengths strictly smaller than 1 on $E(Q)\setminus E(Q')$. This has a realization $X'\subset \mathbb{R}^3$ close to $X$.  If the perturbation is close enough to $X$, then the pairs of points that were strictly closer to each other than 1 are still strictly closer than 1. As $Q'$ has at least one edge, the diameter cannot decrease. Furthermore we can simply delete all vertices in $V(Q)\setminus V(Q')$. Hence, the diameter graph of $X'$ is exactly $Q'$. 

    In the other direction, we could argue the following way. Suppose $X$ is a set of points in $\mathbb{R}^3$. We could extend the ideas in Lemma \ref{lem:diameterispolyhedral} to show that the diameter graph $\Gamma(X)$ has a drawing in the projective plane which extends to an NBQP. Again the vertex rotations would correspond to the neighborhood rotations. This would require us to carefully analyze the skeleton of $\mathcal{B}(X)$. We choose an easier path: we can simply chain together the previous theorems. Any diameter graph in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is a generalized thrackleable graph (Theorem \ref{thm:diamisgent}). Any generalized thrackleable graph has a parity embedding in the projective plane (Theorem \ref{thm:thrpairtyemb}). Any parity embedding in the projective plane is a subgraph of an NBQP graph (Lemma \ref{lem:eqmaxcases}).  Hence, any diameter graph is a subgraph of an NBQP graph, and it must obviously have at least one edge since we have at least 2 vertices.

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:3conequiv}
    Let $G$ be a 2-connected SISD map. Then the following are equivalent:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $G$ is 3-connected.
        \item $\overline R(G)$ has no separating 4-cycles.
        \item For every proper subset $S\subset V(\overline R(G))$ that contains at least 4 vertices, we have $|E(\overline R(G)[S])|\le 2|S|-3$.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- lemma-14: body_md -->
\label{lem:selfdualsimple}
    Any 3-connected self-dual graph is simple.
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Note that  $X$ is strongly critical if and only if for any proper subset $S\subset X$ with at least 4 vertices we have at most $2|S|-3$ diameters spanned by $S$.

Lemma \ref{lem:3conequiv} and Lemma \ref{lem:selfdualsimple} tell us that the skeleton $G$ is simple and 3-connected, if and only if $\overline R(G)$ has no proper subset $S$ with at least 4 vertices and spanning more than  $2|S|-3$ edges. As $\overline R(G)=\mathcal{Q}(X)$ by Lemma \ref{lem:diameterispolyhedral}, this means that the skeleton is simple and 3-connected if and only if $X$ is strongly critical.
