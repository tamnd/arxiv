\label{thm:mobius}
Suppose that $x^{0.58+\varepsilon }\leqslant H\leqslant x$. Then, uniformly for all $\alpha \in \mathbb{R} $, one has
\[
\Bigl| \sum_{x<n \leqslant x+H}\mu(n) e(n\alpha) \Bigr| \ll H(\log x)^{-1/3+\varepsilon }.
\]

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:mangoldt}
Suppose that $x^{0.58+\varepsilon }\leqslant H\leqslant x$ and that $A\geqslant 1$ is a large constant. Then one has
\[
\Bigl| \sum_{x<n \leqslant x+H} \Lambda (n) e(n\alpha) \Bigr| \leqslant H(\log x)^{-A}
\] and 
\begin{equation}\label{ec1.1}
\Bigl| \sum_{x<n \leqslant x+H} \mu (n) e(n\alpha) \Bigr| \leqslant H(\log x)^{-A},
\end{equation}
unless there exists an integer $1\leqslant q\ll (\log x)^{O_A(1)}$ such that
\[
\left\| q\alpha\right\| \ll \frac{x(\log x)^{O_A(1)}}{H^2}.
\]

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:second-moment}
Suppose that $q\geqslant 1$, $T'\geqslant T^{1/3} $ and $N\leqslant (T+T')^4$ with $T\geqslant 0$.  Then,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\chi \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)}}\int_{T}^{T+T'}\Bigl| \sum_{N<n \leqslant 2N}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{1/2+it}} \Bigr|^2 \,\mathrm{d} t\ll qT'(\log q(T'+T))^3+\frac{N}{1+T}. \]
Likewise,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\chi \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)}}\int_{T}^{T+T'}\Bigl| \sum_{N<n \leqslant 2N}\frac{\chi(n)\,\log n}{n^{1/2+it}} \Bigr|^2 \,\mathrm{d} t\ll qT'(\log q(T'+T))^{5} +\frac{N}{1+T}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
This follows from a truncated version of \cite[Lemma 2]{Zhan}, which concerns the second moment of Dirichlet $L$-functions in short intervals. We briefly discuss the argument below, which essentially follows \cite[Corollary 2.14]{MRTI}.	

We first focus on the first estimate.  Set $\mathcal D{\bf1}_I(s,\chi) =\sum_{n\in I}\chi(n)n^{-s}$ for a  finite interval $I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}$. The Fundamental theorem of calculus  gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:2.1}
\mathcal D {\bf1}_{(N,2N]}\Big(\frac12+it, \chi\Big) =\frac{1}{(2N)^{1/2}} \mathcal D {\bf1}_{(N,2N]}(it,\chi) + \int_N^{2N} \mathcal D {\bf1}_{(N,x]} (it,\chi) \frac{\,\mathrm{d} x}{2x^{3/2}}. 
\end{align}
By the triangle inequality, it therefore suffices to prove that
\begin{align}\label{eq:second-estimate}
\sum_{\chi \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)} \int_{T}^{T+T'}\bigl|  \mathcal D{\bf1}_{[1,N]} (it,\chi) \bigr|^2 \,\mathrm{d} t \ll qT'N(\log q(T'+T))^{3} + \frac{N^2}{(1+T)}.
\end{align} 

Employing the truncated Perron's formula (cf. \cite[Lemma 2.4]{MRTI}) with $U=(q(T'+T))^5$ one has
\[
\bigl|  \mathcal D{\bf1}_{[1,N]} (it,\chi) \bigr| \ll \biggl|  \int_{-U}^{U} L\bigl( 1+\frac{1}{\log N} +i(t+u),\chi \bigr) \frac{N^{1+\frac{1}{\log N} + iu}}{1+\frac{1}{\log N} +iu} \,\mathrm{d} u \biggr| + \frac{N(\log 2U)}{U}+1.
\]
We shall shift the contour to the line with real part $1/2$. To such an end, it is useful recalling the convexity bound  \cite[Theorem 3]{Ramachandra-conve}, which asserts that
 \[
 L(\sigma +it,\chi) \ll (q(1+|t|))^{\varepsilon+\max\left\{0, \frac{1-\sigma}{2}\right\}}
 \]
  uniformly for $1/2\leqslant \sigma \leqslant 2$ and $|t|\geqslant 1$, where   $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary but fixed. Observing that the only possible pole arises from the principal character $\chi_0$, and its contribution is $O(\frac{N}{|t|})$, we obtain that
\begin{align}\label{eq:2.2}
\bigl|  \mathcal D{\bf1}_{[1,N]} (it,\chi) \bigr| \ll  &\biggl|  \int_{-U}^{U} L\bigl( \frac12+i(t+u),\chi \bigr) \frac{N^{\frac12 + iu}}{\frac12 +iu} \,\mathrm{d} u \biggr| +\log (2N) +\frac{N(qU)^{\varepsilon}}{U}\nonumber
\\
&+\frac{(qU)^{1/4}N^{1/2}}{U}(qU)^{\varepsilon}+{\bf1}_{\chi=\chi_0} \frac{N}{1+t}.
\end{align}

It is worth noting that the assumption $N\ll U^{4/5}$ entails that the terms $N(qU)^{\varepsilon}U^{-1}$ and $(qU)^{1/4}N^{1/2}(qU)^{\varepsilon}U^{-1}$ are $O(1)$. Applying Holder's inequality with respect to the variable $u$ and then splitting the range of integration for the $u$-integral into $|u|\leqslant T'+T$ and the complementary set, and making a dyadic dissection, we obtain
\begin{align}\label{hhh}
&\sum_{\chi \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)} \int_{T}^{T+T'}\bigl|  \mathcal D{\bf1}_{[1,N]} (it,\chi) \bigr|^2 \,\mathrm{d} t\nonumber\\
\ll& N(\log2 U) \sum_{\substack{\chi \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\\ }} \int_{T}^{T+T'}\int_{-U}^{U} \frac{\bigl|  L\bigl( \frac12+i(t+u),\chi \bigr) \bigr|^2}{1+|u|} \,\mathrm{d} u \,\mathrm{d} t+q T'\log (2U)+\frac{N^2}{(1+T)}\nonumber\\
\ll& N (\log2U)^2 \sup_{|u|\leqslant (T'+T)}  \sum_{\substack{\chi \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\\ }} \int_{T}^{T+T'}  \bigl| L(\frac12+i(t+u),\chi) \bigr|^2 \,\mathrm{d} t+q T'\log (2U)+\frac{N^2}{(1+T)}\\
&+ 	N (\log2U)^2 \sup_{(T'+T)\leqslant V\leqslant U} \int_{T}^{T+T'}   \sum_{\substack{\chi \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\\ }} \int_{V}^{2V} \frac{\bigl| L(\frac12+i(t+u),\chi) \bigr|^2}{1+|u|} \,\mathrm{d} u \,\mathrm{d} t.\nonumber
\end{align}
By the second-moment estimate for Dirichlet $L$-functions over long intervals (cf. \cite[Theorem 10.1]{montgomery}), the last term is $O(qNT' (\log qU)^3)$, as desired. For the first integral when $T'\leqslant T$ we apply \cite[Lemma 2]{Zhan}, while otherwise we use \cite[Theorem 10.1]{montgomery}. In either case, we again infer that it is $O(qNT' (\log q(T+T'))^3)$, whence the estimate \eqref{eq:second-estimate} follows accordingly. 



As for the second estimate, employing summation by parts we deduce that
\[
\Bigl| \sum_{N<n \leqslant 2N}\frac{\chi(n)\,\log n}{n^{s}} \Bigr| \ll (\log N) \Bigl|  \sum_{N<n \leqslant 2N}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^s} \Bigr| +\frac1N \sum_{N<N'\leqslant 2N} \Bigl|  \sum_{N<n \leqslant N'}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^s} \Bigr|.
\]
Therefore, we may remove the logarithmic weight at a cost of $O(\log2N)$, and hence $O((\log 2N)^2)$ in the second moment. Since $\log2N\ll\log2q(T+T')$, this proves the second estimate and completes the proof.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:fourth-moment}
	Let $q\in\mathbb{N}$ with $\log q\ll \log T$ and $T\geqslant 0$, and $\varepsilon>0$ be sufficiently small. Suppose that $ T'\gg T^{\varepsilon }$ and $N \leqslant (T+T')^4$. Then one has
\[
\sum_{\chi \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)}\int_{T}^{T+T'}\Bigl| \sum_{N<n \leqslant 2N}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{1/2+it}} \Bigr|^{4}\,\mathrm{d} t\ll T^{\varepsilon}(qT'+q^{71/96}T^{2/3}) + \frac{N^2}{(1+T^{3})}.
\]
If in addition $T'=T$, the same bound holds with $(\log x)^{O(1)}$ replacing the $T^{\varepsilon}$ factor. The same estimates hold with $\chi(n)$ replaced by
$\chi(n)\log n$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
As in Lemma~\ref{lem:second-moment}, the second term in the above line arises from the contribution of the principal character. We may evaluate this separately. Indeed, from~\eqref{eq:2.2}, its contribution to the mean value of $|\mathcal D {\bf1}_{(N,2N]}(it, \chi)|^4$ can be bounded by
\[
\sum_{\chi \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)} \int_{T}^{T+T'}\Bigl( {\bf1}_{\chi=\chi_0} \frac{N}{(1+t)} \Bigr)^4 \,\mathrm{d} t\ll \frac{N^4 }{1+T^{3}}.
\]
This is acceptable, since~\eqref{eq:2.1} gives a weight of size $\Omega(N^2)$ to the fourth moment of $|\mathcal D {\bf1}_{(N,2N]}(\frac12+it, \chi)|$. Therefore, in what follows, we may ignore the contribution of the principal character.

An argument similar to that in the proof of Lemma \ref{lem:second-moment} together with \cite[Theorem 3]{Jutila} yields the desired estimate with the term $q^{71/96}T^{2/3}$ replaced by $qT^{2/3}$ whenever $T^{1/2+\varepsilon }\leqslant T' \leqslant T^{2/3}$. To obtain the sharper dependence on $q$ and extend the validity range of $T'$, we use a more precise fourth-moment bound for Dirichlet $L$-functions in short intervals.

We first notice that \cite[Theorem 4]{JM} yields
\[
\sum_{\chi \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)} \int_{T}^{T+T'} \bigl|  L(\frac{1}{2}+it,\chi)  \bigr|^4 \,\mathrm{d} t \ll T^\varepsilon (qT' + q^{\frac{2(1+\eta)}{3}} T^{2/3}),
\]
whenever $T^\varepsilon\ll T'\ll T^{2/3}$ and $\log q\ll \log T$, where $\eta\geqslant 0$ is an admissible exponent  towards the Ramanujan--Petersson conjecture. The Kim--Sarnak bound in \cite[Appendix 2]{Kim} allows one to take $\eta=7/64$, giving $2(1+\eta)/3 \leqslant 71/96$. Hence, when $T^\varepsilon \ll T'\ll T^{2/3}$, the truncated argument in the proof of Lemma \ref{lem:second-moment} combined with the fourth moment (cf.~\cite[Lemma 10.9]{Har}) in place of \cite[Theorem 10.1]{montgomery}, and  the above fourth moment bound in place of \cite[Lemma 2]{Zhan} then gives the claimed bound.

 Now  assume that $T^{2/3}\leqslant T'\leqslant T$. Partition $[T,T+T']$ into intervals of length $\Theta(T^{2/3})$. Applying the estimate
 \[
\sum_{\chi \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)}\int_{U}^{U+\Theta(T^{2/3})}\Bigl| \sum_{N<n \leqslant 2N}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{1/2+it}} \Bigr|^{4}\,\mathrm{d} t\ll qT^{2/3+\varepsilon}
\]
uniformly over the left endpoints $T\leqslant U\leqslant T+T'$ of these intervals, and summing over the intervals yields the desired bound.

Finally, the case $T'\geqslant T$ is handled by the argument used in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:second-moment} but employing instead the fourth-moment estimate for Dirichlet $L$-functions over long intervals (cf.~\cite[Lemma 10.9]{Har}) to bound the analogue of the first integral in (\ref{hhh}). This completes the proof.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:large-values}
Let $(a_{n})$ be a sequence of complex numbers with $\lvert a_{n}\rvert\leqslant 1$. Suppose that $W$ is a finite set of pairs $(t,\chi)$, where $t\in [T, T+U]$ and $\chi$ is a primitive character modulo $q$, such that whenever $(t,\chi)\neq (t',\chi')$ then either $\chi\neq \chi'$ or $\lvert t-t'\rvert\geqslant 1$. Assume that \begin{equation}\label{yy5}
\Big\lvert \sum_{N<n \leqslant 2N} a_{n}\chi(n) n^{it}\Big\rvert\geqslant V\end{equation} for all $(t,\chi)\in W$. Then,
\begin{equation}\label{rr4}
\lvert W\rvert\ll (\log 2qU)^{2} \big(N^{2}V^{-2}+qU\min(NV^{-2},N^{4}V^{-6})\big).\end{equation}
Moreover, suppose that $q\leqslant N^{6/5}$. Then, if $ (qU)^{2/3}\leqslant N\leqslant (qU)^{5/6}$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{pp9}
\lvert W\rvert\ll (\log 2qU)^{2}N^{2}V^{-2}+ (qU)^{\varepsilon}qU N^{12/5}V^{-4},\end{equation} and if $N>(qU)^{5/6}$ then 
\begin{equation}\label{pp91}
\lvert W\rvert\ll (\log 2qU)^{2}N^{2}V^{-2}+(qU)^{\varepsilon}(qU)^{1/2} N^{3}V^{-4}.\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We consider the set
$$
W_{U}=\bigl\{ (t-T,\chi):\ \ (t,\chi)\in  W  \bigr\},
$$ 
and define the sequence of complex numbers $b_{n}=a_{n}n^{iT}$. By the above assumptions, $W_{U}$ is a finite set of pairs $(t,\chi)$, where $t\in [0,U]$ and $\chi$ is a primitive character modulo $q$, with the property that whenever $(t,\chi)\neq (t',\chi')$ then either $\chi\neq \chi'$ or $\lvert t-t'\rvert\geqslant 1$. Moreover, $\lvert b_{n}\rvert=\lvert a_{n}\rvert\leqslant 1$ and whenever $(t,\chi)\in W_{U}$ then 
$$\Big\lvert \sum_{N<n \leqslant 2N} b_{n}\chi(n) n^{it}\Big\rvert=\Big\lvert \sum_{N<n \leqslant 2N} a_{n}\chi(n) n^{i(t+T)}\Big\rvert\geqslant V.$$
 The estimate (\ref{rr4}) now follows from \cite[Lemma 10.2]{Har}.


In order to obtain (\ref{pp9}) and \eqref{pp91} we observe that a direct consequence of \cite[Theorem 1.1]{CGY} entails whenever $ N\geqslant (qU)^{2/3}$ that
\begin{equation}\label{2.8}\lvert W\rvert\ll (qU)^{\varepsilon}(N^{2}V^{-2}+(qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4}+q^{4/3}UN^{2}V^{-4}+qU N^{12/5}V^{-4}).\end{equation}
We first prove a stronger, loss-separated version of this bound, namely
\begin{align}\label{w-loss-sep}
	|W| \ll (\log 2qU)^{2} N^2V^{-2} +(qU)^\varepsilon \big((qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4}+q^{4/3}UN^{2}V^{-4}+qU N^{12/5}V^{-4}\big).
\end{align}
We split the analysis into two cases according to whether $N^2V^{-2} \leqslant (qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4}$ or not. If this inequality holds, the second term dominates the first one in (\ref{2.8}). Therefore, one has
\[
\lvert W\rvert\ll (qU)^{\varepsilon}((qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4}+q^{4/3}UN^{2}V^{-4}+qU N^{12/5}V^{-4}).
\]
This gives the desired bound \eqref{w-loss-sep}. If instead, $N^2V^{-2} \geqslant (qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4}$, we have from \eqref{rr4} that
\[
\lvert W\rvert\ll (\log 2qU)^2 (N^2V^{-2} + qU N^{4} V^{-6}).
\]
We then note that under the assumption $N^2V^{-2} \geqslant (qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4}$ it transpires that
\[
qU N^{4} V^{-6} =\frac{\big( (qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4} \big)^2}{N^2V^{-2}} \leqslant (qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4}.
\]
It then follows that
\[
\lvert W\rvert\ll (\log 2qU)^2 (N^2V^{-2} + (qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4})\ll (\log 2qU)^2 N^2V^{-2} + (qU)^\varepsilon (qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4}.
\]
This also gives the estimate \eqref{w-loss-sep}.

We now return to the proof of \eqref{pp9} and \eqref{pp91}.  If $q\leqslant N^{6/5}$  it is apparent that the third term in \eqref{w-loss-sep} is always smaller than the fourth one, namely
$$q^{4/3}UN^{2}V^{-4}\leqslant qU N^{12/5}V^{-4},$$
 and therefore,
 \[
|W| \ll (\log 2qU)^{2} N^2V^{-2} +(qU)^\varepsilon \big((qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4}+qU N^{12/5}V^{-4}\big). 
 \]
 
Assume first that $N\leqslant (qU)^{5/6}$. Then one further has that
$$ (qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4}\leqslant qU N^{12/5}V^{-4}.$$ Combining the above two estimates thereby delivers the conclusion (\ref{pp9}). If instead $q\leqslant N^{6/5}$ but $N>(qU)^{5/6}$ then $$ q^{4/3}UN^{2}V^{-4}\leqslant qU N^{12/5}V^{-4}\leqslant (qU)^{1/2}N^{3}V^{-4},$$ whence (\ref{pp91}) holds as well.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:major-arc}
Suppose that $H\geqslant x^{17/30+\varepsilon}$.  Then one has
$$
\sup_{\alpha\in \mathfrak M}\Bigl| \sum_{x\leqslant n\leqslant x+H}\mu(n)e(\alpha n) \Bigr| \ll He^{-(\log x)^{1/8}}.$$
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Write $\alpha =\frac aq+\beta$, where $ a\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $q\in\mathbb{N}$ with $(a,q)=1$ such that $0\leqslant a\leqslant q\leqslant (\log x)^{3A}$ and $\lvert \beta\rvert\leqslant \frac{(\log x)^{5A}}{H}$. Splitting the  sums according to  $q_0=\gcd(q,n)$,  and employing orthogonality of characters it follows that 
\begin{align*}
\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}&\mu(n)e(\alpha n)=\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\mu(n)e(\beta n)e(an/q)
\\
&=\sum_{q_{0}q_{1}=q}\sum_{\substack{ r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q_{1})\\ (r,q_{1})=1}}\sum_{\substack{\frac{x}{q_{0}}<n\leqslant \frac{x}{q_{0}}+\frac{H}{q_{0}}\\ n\equiv r\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q_{1})}}\mu(q_{0}n)e(\beta q_{0}n)e(an/q_{1})
\\
&=\sum_{q_0q_{1}=q} \sum_{\substack{r \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q_{1})\\ (r,q_{1})=1}} e\Big(\frac{ar}{q_{1}}\Big) \phi(q_{1})^{-1} \sum_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q_{1})} \overline{\chi(r)}  \sum_{\frac{x}{q_{0}}<n\leqslant \frac{x}{q_{0}}+\frac{H}{q_{0}}} \mu(q_{0}n)e(\beta q_{0}n) \chi(n).
\end{align*}
Therefore, writing\begin{equation}\label{dd3}
C(a,\chi)=\sum_{\substack{r \,(\mathrm{mod}\,q_{1})\\ (r,q_{1})=1}} e\Big(\frac{ar}{q_{1}}\Big)  \overline{\chi(r)},\end{equation} 
it follows that
$$\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}\mu(n)e(\alpha n)=\sum_{q_0q_{1}=q}\phi(q_{1})^{-1}\sum_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q_{1})}C(a,\chi)\sum_{\substack{\frac{x}{q_{0}}<n\leqslant \frac{x}{q_{0}}+\frac{H}{q_{0}}}} \mu(q_{0}n)e(\beta q_{0}n) \chi(n).$$

We shall next focus on the inner sum. To such an end it is worth considering the $L$-function
$$ L_{q_{0}}(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(q_{0}n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}.$$ By employing multiplicativity and the fact that any $n\in\mathbb{N}$ with $(n,q_{0})>1$ satisfies $\mu(q_{0}n)=0$ it transpires that
\begin{align*}L_{q_{0}}(s,\chi)=\mu(q_0)\sum_{\substack{n=1\\ (n,q_{0})=1}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}&=\mu(q_{0})\prod_{p}\Big(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\Big).\prod_{p|q_{0}}\Big(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1}
\\
&=\frac{\mu(q_{0})}{L(s,\chi)}\prod_{p|q_{0}}\Big(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1}.

Combining the preceding two estimates, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq1}
|\mathcal{S}| \ll M^{2-2\sigma_1} (\log x)^{O(1)}
+\frac{qx^{1+\varepsilon }}{H}M^{\min\{1-2\sigma_1,\,12/5-4\sigma_1,\,4-6\sigma_1\}},
\end{equation}
provided that $M\leqslant (qx/H)^{5/6}$. On the other hand, if $M\geqslant (qx/H)^{5/6}$ it transpires from \eqref{pp91} that
\begin{align}\label{m-large-gm}
|\mathcal{S}| \ll M^{2-2\sigma_1} (\log x)^{O(1)} + x^\varepsilon \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{1/2} M^{3-4\sigma_1}.
\end{align}
 We now define some auxiliary functions $f_M$ and $h_M$ as follows. If
$M\leqslant (qx/H)^{5/6}$, set 
\begin{align}\label{f-q-large}
f_M(\sigma)=
\begin{cases}
1-2\sigma, & 1/2\leqslant \sigma\leqslant 7/10,\\
12/5-4\sigma, & 7/10\leqslant \sigma\leqslant 8/10,\\
4-6\sigma, & 8/10\leqslant \sigma\leqslant 1,
\end{cases} \qquad \text{and} \qquad  h_M(\sigma)=1.
\end{align}
If instead $M\geqslant (qx/H)^{5/6}$, we set
\[
f_M(\sigma)=
\begin{cases}
1-2\sigma, & \text{if $qx/H\leqslant M^{4-4\sigma}$},\\
3-4\sigma, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
h_M(\sigma)=
\begin{cases}
1, &  \text{if $qx/H\leqslant M^{4-4\sigma}$},\\
1/2, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
For simplicity, we write $f$ and $h$ in place of $f_M$ and $h_M$ when no confusion can arise. Therefore, applying (\ref{eq1}) if $M\leqslant (qx/H)^{5/6}$, and \eqref{m-large-gm} and \eqref{rr4} when $M\geqslant (qx/H)^{5/6}$ one always has
\begin{align}\label{bound-m-small-beta}
|\mathcal{S}|
\ll M^{2-2\sigma_1} (\log x)^{O(1)}
+x^\varepsilon \left(\frac{qx}{H}\right)^{h(\sigma_1)}M^{f(\sigma_1)}.
\end{align}
Similarly, we have
 \begin{equation}\label{bound-n}
\lvert \mathcal{S}\rvert\ll N^{2-2\sigma_{2}} (\log x)^{O(1)}+ x^\varepsilon \Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{h_{N}(\sigma_{2})}N^{f_N(\sigma_{2})}.\end{equation}
For the $R$-polynomial, we notice first that for any positive integer $g$ one has from Montgomery--Hal\'asz--Huxley's large values estimates that
\begin{align}\label{r-large}
|\mathcal S| \ll x^\varepsilon (R^{2g(1-\sigma_3)} +\frac{qx}{H} R^{g(4-6\sigma_3)}).
\end{align}
Moreover, if one further has
$$\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{2/3}\leqslant R^{g}\leqslant \Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{5/6}$$ then one may apply Theorem \ref{thm:large-values} to deduce that
\begin{equation}\label{bound-r-2}
 \lvert \mathcal{S}\rvert\ll R^{2g(1-\sigma_{3})} (\log x)^{O(1)}+\frac{qx^{1+\varepsilon }}{H}R^{gf(\sigma_{3})},\end{equation}
where $f(\sigma_3)$ is as in \eqref{f-q-large}. 
 
In what follows, we write $V=\sum_{q_0q_1=q }d_{6k+3}(q_{0})\lvert \mathcal{S}\rvert M^{\sigma_{1}}N^{\sigma_{2}}R^{\sigma_{3}}$. By (\ref{small-beta}) it suffices to prove that, for all $1/2\leqslant \sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3\leqslant 1$, then
\[
  V=\sum_{q_0q_1=q }d_{6k+3}(q_{0})\lvert \mathcal{S}\rvert M^{\sigma_{1}}N^{\sigma_{2}}R^{\sigma_{3}}\ll q^{1/2 } x (\log x)^{-A}.
\] 
We shall divide the analysis into four cases according to whether the diagonal terms dominate in \eqref{bound-m-small-beta} and in  \eqref{bound-n}.

\subsection*{Case 1.1 } If
$M^{2-2\sigma_1}(\log x)^{O(1)} \geqslant x^\varepsilon \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_1)} M^{f(\sigma_1)}\text{ and }N^{2-2\sigma_2}(\log x)^{O(1)} \geqslant x^\varepsilon \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_2)} N^{f(\sigma_2)}.$
 We first assume that $q\geqslant (\log x)^{3A}$ and note from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, \eqref{bound-m-small-beta} and \eqref{bound-n} that
\begin{align*}
 V& \ll (\log x)^{O(1)}  \sum_{q_0q_1=q }d_{6k+3}(q_{0}) \big(M^{2-2\sigma_1} N^{2-2\sigma_2}\big)^{1/2} M^{\sigma_1} N^{\sigma_2} R
\\
&\ll (\log x)^{O(1)}x \sum_{q_0q_1=q } \frac{ d_{6k+3}(q_{0})}{q_0} \ll q^{1/2}x(\log x)^{-A}.
\end{align*}
If instead $q\leqslant (\log x)^{3A}$, observing that $a_r$ may be expressed as the Dirichlet convolution of $\mu, \log \cdot$ and $1_{[N_i,2N_i]}$ and deducing that $|t|>(\log x)^{4A}$ by employing a similar argument to that in (\ref{jhjh}), we have from Lemma \ref{lem:upper-bd-diri} that 
\begin{align}\label{pointwise-r}
\Big| \sum_{r\sim R} \frac{a_r \chi(r)}{r^{1/2+it}}\Big| \ll R^{1/2} (\log x)^{-3A/2},
\end{align}
and therefore,
\begin{align*}
 V&\ll (\log x)^{O(1)} \sum_{q_0q_1=q }d_{6k+3}(q_{0})(M^{2-2\sigma_{1}}N^{2-2\sigma_{2}})^{1/2}M^{\sigma_{1}}N^{\sigma_{2}}R(\log x)^{-3A/2}
\\
&\ll (\log x)^{O(1)-3A/2}x \sum_{q_0q_1=q } \frac{ d_{6k+3}(q_{0})}{q_0} \ll q^{1/2}x(\log x)^{-A}.	
\end{align*}

\subsection*{Case 1.2 } If
$M^{2-2\sigma_1}(\log x)^{O(1)} \leqslant x^\varepsilon \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_1)} M^{f(\sigma_1)}\text{ and }N^{2-2\sigma_2}(\log x)^{O(1)} \leqslant x^\varepsilon \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_2)} N^{f(\sigma_2)}.$ 
As suggested by the definitions of $f(\sigma)$ and $h(\sigma)$, we must further decompose the estimates according to the size of $q$.

\subsection*{Case 1.2 (i)}
Suppose that $M,N\leqslant (qx/H)^{5/6}$. Then \eqref{bound-m-small-beta} yields
\[
|\mathcal S|\ll
\frac{qx^{1+\varepsilon }}{H}M^{f_M(\sigma_1)},
\]
where $f_M(\sigma_1)$ is as in
\eqref{f-q-large}; the analogous estimate holding with $N$ and
$\sigma_2$ in lieu of $M$ and $\sigma_1$. Hence, given any real number $1/4\leqslant a\leqslant 1/2$, one has
\begin{align}\label{a-sigma}
|\mathcal S|^a M^{\sigma_1} \ll x^\varepsilon (qx/H)^a M^{af(\sigma_1)+\sigma_1}\ll x^\varepsilon (qx/H)^a M^{\frac{7-4a}{10}}.
\end{align}
Here, the second bound follows by  noticing  from the definition of $f(\sigma)$ in \eqref{f-q-large} that for every $1/4\leqslant a< 1/2$, the inequality 
\[
af(\sigma) +\sigma \leqslant af(7/10) +7/10 =\frac{7-4a}{10}
\]
holds, where we essentially used the fact that the maximum of the left side of the above line is attained when $\sigma=7/10$.  

We first consider the range  $\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{2/3}\leqslant R^{2}\leqslant \Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{5/6}$ so that \eqref{bound-r-2} is applicable. We may take $c_{1}=\frac{1}{4}$, $a_{1}=b_{1}=3/8$, and $c_{2}=1/8$, $a_{2}=b_{2}=7/16$. Then, using the  bound \eqref{a-sigma} for both the $M$-polynomial and $N$-polynomial,  in conjunction with \eqref{bound-r-2} for the choice $g=2$ to the $R$-polynomial gives
\begin{align*}
x^{-\varepsilon}V\ll&
\left(\frac{qx}{H}M^{f(\sigma_1)}\right)^{a_1}
\left(\frac{qx}{H}N^{f(\sigma_2)}\right)^{b_1}
\left(R^{4(1-\sigma_3)}\right)^{c_1}
M^{\sigma_1}N^{\sigma_2}R^{\sigma_3}\\
&+
\left(\frac{qx}{H}M^{f(\sigma_1)}\right)^{a_2}
\left(\frac{qx}{H}N^{f(\sigma_2)}\right)^{b_2}
\left(\frac{qx}{H}R^{24/5-8\sigma_3}\right)^{c_2}
M^{\sigma_1}N^{\sigma_2}R^{\sigma_3}\nonumber\\
=&
\left(\frac{qx}{H}\right)^{a_1+b_1}
M^{a_1f(\sigma_1)+\sigma_1}N^{b_1f(\sigma_2)+\sigma_2}R + \frac{qx}{H} M^{a_2f(\sigma_1)+\sigma_1}N^{b_2f(\sigma_2)+\sigma_2}R^{3/5}.\nonumber
\end{align*}
Consequently, it follows from the second estimate in \eqref{a-sigma} that
\begin{align*}
V \ll& x^\varepsilon \Big(\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{a_{1}+b_{1}}(MN)^{\frac{7-4a_{1}}{10}}R+\frac{qx}{H}(MN)^{\frac{7-4a_{2}}{10}}R^{3/5}\Big)\\
 \ll& x^\varepsilon \Big(\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{3/4}(MN)^{11/20}R
+\frac{qx}{H}(MN)^{21/40}R^{3/5} \Big)\\
\ll& q^{1/2} x^{1+\varepsilon } \Big( \frac{q^{1/4}x^{3/10}R^{9/20}}{H^{3/4}}+q^{1/2}x^{21/40}R^{3/40}H^{-1}\Big), 
\end{align*}
where in the last inequality we have used the fact that $MNR\ll x$.

Using the assumptions $q\ll x^{2/25+\varepsilon }$, $R\ll x^{1/5}$ (from \eqref{uu}), and $H\geqslant x^{29/50+\varepsilon }$, and rescaling $\varepsilon$ if necessary, we obtain
\[
V \ll q^{1/2} x^{1+\varepsilon } \big( x^{\frac1{50}+\frac3{10}+\frac9{100}-\frac{87}{200}+\varepsilon } +x^{\frac1{25}+\frac{21}{40}+\frac3{200}-\frac{29}{50}-\varepsilon/2 }\big)\ll q^{1/2}x^{1-\varepsilon/2 },
\]
as desired.

We next assume that  either $R\leqslant \Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{1/3}$ or  $R\geqslant (qx/H)^{5/12}$. Let $g\geqslant 1$ be an integer to be specified later. We now take $c_{1}=(2g)^{-1}$, $a_{1}=b_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(1-c_{1})$, and $c_{2}=(6g)^{-1}$, $a_{2}=b_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1-c_{2})$ and apply the estimates in \eqref{bound-m-small-beta} and \eqref{r-large},  to obtain 
\begin{align}\label{bb89}
x^{-\varepsilon}V \ll& \Big(\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)M^{f(\sigma_{1})}\Big)^{a_{1}} \Big(\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)N^{f(\sigma_{2})}\Big)^{b_{1}}(R^{g(2-2\sigma_{3})})^{c_{1}}M^{\sigma_{1}}N^{\sigma_{2}}R^{\sigma_{3}}\nonumber
\\
&+\Big(\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)M^{f(\sigma_{1})}\Big)^{a_{2}} \Big(\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)N^{f(\sigma_{2})}\Big)^{b_{2}}\Big(\frac{qx}{H}R^{g(4-6\sigma_{3})}\Big)^{c_{2}}M^{\sigma_{1}}N^{\sigma_{2}}R^{\sigma_{3}}\nonumber
\\
=&\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{a_{1}+b_{1}} M^{a_1f(\sigma_{1})+\sigma_1}N^{b_1f(\sigma_2)+\sigma_{2}}R+\frac{qx}{H}M^{a_{2}f(\sigma_{1})+\sigma_1} N^{f(\sigma_{2})b_{2}+\sigma_2}R^{2/3}.
\end{align}
Consequently, it follows from \eqref{a-sigma} and the fact that $MNR\ll x$ that
\begin{align*}
V &\ll \Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{2a_{1}}(MN)^{\frac{7-4a_{1}}{10}}R+\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)(MN)^{\frac{7-4a_{2}}{10}}R^{2/3}
\\
&\ll \Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{2g}}(MN)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{10g}}R+\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)(MN)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{30g}}R^{2/3}
\\
&\ll q^{1/2}x^{1+\varepsilon } \big(\frac{q^{1/2-1/2g} x^{1/2-2/5g} R^{1/2-1/10g}}{ H^{1-1/2g}} +\frac{q^{1/2} x^{1/2+1/30g } R^{1/6-1/30g}}{H}\big),
\end{align*}
whence taking $g=3$ yields
\begin{align}\label{ttj}
V&\ll x^{1+\varepsilon }  q^{1/2}\Big(\frac{q^{1/3}x^{11/30}R^{7/15}}{H^{5/6}}+q^{1/2}x^{23/45}R^{7/45}H^{-1}\Big).
\end{align}

We shall then assume first that $R^3<qx/H$ and insert the above bound into the previous estimate to obtain 
$$V\ll  q^{1/2}x^{1+\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{q^{22/45}x^{47/90}}{H^{89/90}}+q^{149/270}x^{76/135}H^{-142/135}\Big).$$ Consequently, by the assumption $q\leqslant x^{2/25+\varepsilon}$, it transpires that 
$$V\ll  q^{1/2}x^{1+\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{x^{421/750+\varepsilon}}{H^{89/90}}+x^{683/1125}H^{-142/135}\Big).$$
The preceding bound is acceptable whenever $H\geqslant \max( x^{1263/2225+\varepsilon},x^{2049/3550+\varepsilon}),$ as desired. Note that both of the above exponents are $<29/50$.

We then assume instead that $q\leqslant R^{12/5}Hx^{-1}$. It follows from (\ref{ttj}) and the restriction $R\ll x^{1/5}$ at the statement of the proposition that
\[V\ll q^{1/2}x^{1+\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{x^{1/30}R^{19/15}}{H^{1/2}}+\frac{x^{1/90}R^{61/45}}{H^{1/2}}\Big)
\ll q^{1/2}x^{1+\varepsilon}(x^{43/150}H^{-1/2}+x^{127/450}H^{-1/2}),
\]
which delivers the desired result once $H\geqslant x^{43/75+\varepsilon}$ and $H\geqslant x^{127/225+\varepsilon}$.

\subsection*{Case 1.2 (ii)}
We suppose next that $N \leqslant (qx/H)^{5/6}\leqslant M$. Under the latter assumption, equation \eqref{bound-m-small-beta} shows that for any real number $1/4\leqslant a\leqslant 1/2$ one has
\[
|\mathcal S|^a M^{\sigma_1} \ll 
\begin{cases}
\big(qx^{1+\varepsilon }/H\big)^a M^{a(1-2\sigma_1)+\sigma_1} \qquad &\text{ if } \frac{qx}{H} \leqslant M^{4-4\sigma_1};	\\
\big(qx^{1+\varepsilon }/H\big)^{a/2} M^{a(3-4\sigma_1) +\sigma_1} &\text{ otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
We thus conclude that
\begin{align}\label{a-sigma-m-large}
|\mathcal S|^a M^{\sigma_1} \ll x^\varepsilon  M^{1-a} (qx/H)^{3a/2-1/4}
\end{align}
uniformly for $1/2\leqslant \sigma_1 \leqslant 1$ and $1/4\leqslant a\leqslant 1/2$, where we essentially used the fact that the maximum is attained when $qx/H \asymp M^{4-4\sigma_1}$. Since $N\leqslant(qx/H)^{5/6}$, inequality~\eqref{a-sigma} also holds with $M$ and $\sigma_1$ replaced by $N$ and $\sigma_2$, respectively.

We still assume first that $(qx/H)^{2/3} \leqslant R^2\leqslant (qx/H)^{5/6}$. A similar procedure as the one in Case 1.2 (i) would ultimately lead to a worse exponent for $H$, and hence one is forced to deploy a finer argument. Indeed, we then note that whenever $f(\sigma)$ is defined as in \eqref{f-q-large}, one has for any real $c\in [1/8,1/4]$ the inequality
\[
2cf(\sigma) +\sigma \leqslant 2cf(7/10) +7/10 =\frac{7-8c}{10}.
\]
Therefore, we conclude from the bound \eqref{bound-r-2} corresponding to the $R$-polynomial, the estimate \eqref{a-sigma-m-large}
for the $M$-polynomial, the estimate \eqref{a-sigma} for the $N$-polynomial, and the choice of parameters $(a_1,b_1,c_1)=(1/2,1/4,1/4)$ whenever the first term in \eqref{bound-r-2} dominates, and $(a_2,b_2,c_2)=(1/2,3/8,1/8)$ whenever the second one dominates, that
\begin{align*}
x^{-\varepsilon }V \ll & \Big( \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_1)} M^{f(\sigma_1)}\Big)^{a_1}	\Big(\frac{qx}{H} N^{f(\sigma_2)}\Big)^{b_1}	 \Big( R^{4-4\sigma_3}\Big)^{c_1}	M^{\sigma_1}N^{\sigma_2} R^{\sigma_3}\\
&+ \Big( \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_1)} M^{f(\sigma_1)} \Big)^{a_2}	\Big( \frac{qx}{H} N^{f(\sigma_2)}\Big)^{b_2}	 \Big( \frac{qx}{H} R^{2f(\sigma_3)}\Big)^{c_2}	M^{\sigma_1}N^{\sigma_2} R^{\sigma_3}\\
\ll& \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{\frac{3a_1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+b_1} M^{1-a_1} N^{\frac{7-4b_1}{10}} R+\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{\frac{3a_2}{2} -\frac{1}{4}+b_2+c_2}  M^{1-a_2} N^{\frac{7-4b_2}{10}} R^{\frac{7-8c_2}{10}}
\\
\ll& \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{3/4} M^{1/2} N^{3/5} R+\Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)M^{1/2} N^{11/20} R^{3/5}.
\end{align*}

Since $MNR \ll x$, we now bound the first term employing the assumptions $q\leqslant x^{2/25+\varepsilon }, R\ll x^{1/5}$ and $N \leqslant (qx/H)^{5/6}$. This then yields
\begin{align}\label{abc2}
V \ll & q^{1/2}x^{1+\varepsilon } \Big( \big(\frac{qx}{H^3}\big)^{1/4} N^{1/10} R^{1/2} +q^{1/2}H^{-1}x^{1/2}N^{1/20}R^{1/10}\Big)\nonumber\\
\ll& 	 q^{1/2}x^{1+\varepsilon } \Big( x^{23/50} H^{-5/6} +x^{\frac{27}{50}}H^{-1}N^{1/20}R^{1/10} \Big).
\end{align}
The first term is acceptable provided that $H\gg x^{0.58+\varepsilon }$. For the second one, one gets the desired bound whenever $N^{1/2}R\ll X^{2/5}$ and $H\gg x^{0.58+\varepsilon}$. This completes the analysis of the present case, since the latter condition follows from \eqref{bb13}.

We now assume that either $R \leqslant \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{1/3}$ or $R\geqslant \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{5/12}$. In this case, we apply the estimate \eqref{r-large} to the $R$-polynomial. Arguing as in \eqref{bb89} but using \eqref{a-sigma-m-large} to estimate the $M$-polynomial and taking $c_{1}=(2g)^{-1}$, $a_{1}=b_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(1-c_{1})$, and $c_{2}=(6g)^{-1}$, $a_{2}=b_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1-c_{2})$, this gives
\[
V \ll x^\varepsilon \Big( \Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{\frac{3a_1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+b_1} M^{1-a_1} N^{\frac{7-4b_1}{10}} R+ \Big(\frac{qx}{H}\Big)^{\frac{3a_2}{2} -\frac{1}{4}+b_2+c_2}  M^{1-a_2} N^{\frac{7-4b_2}{10}} R^{2/3} \Big).
\]
Consequently,
\[
V\ll x^\varepsilon\left(\big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{1-\frac{5}{8g}}
M^{\frac12+\frac{1}{4g}}
N^{\frac12+\frac{1}{10g}}R+
\big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{1-\frac{1}{24g}}
M^{\frac12+\frac{1}{12g}}
N^{\frac12+\frac{1}{30g}}R^{2/3}\right).
\]
Taking $g=3$ and recalling $MNR \ll x$, we then have
\begin{align}\label{f22}
V &\ll x^\varepsilon\left(\big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{19/24}M^{7/12}N^{8/15}R + \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{71/72}M^{19/36}N^{23/45}R^{2/3}\right)\nonumber\\
&\ll q^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{1+\varepsilon}\left(
q^{\frac{7}{24}}x^{\frac{13}{40}}
M^{\frac{1}{20}}R^{\frac{7}{15}}H^{-\frac{19}{24}}+
q^{\frac{35}{72}}x^{\frac{179}{360}}
M^{\frac{1}{60}}R^{\frac{7}{45}}H^{-\frac{71}{72}}
\right).	
\end{align}



If $q\leqslant R^{12/5}H x^{-1}$, we have from the condition $M\ll H^{10} x^{-16/3-\varepsilon }$ in \eqref{uu} and $R\ll x^{1/5}$ that
\begin{align*}
V&\ll q^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{1+\varepsilon/2}\bigl( 
R^{\frac{7}{6}}x^{\frac{1}{30}}M^{\frac{1}{20}}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
+R^{\frac{119}{90}}x^{\frac{1}{90}}M^{\frac{1}{60}}H^{-\frac{1}{2}} \bigr)
\\
&\ll q^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{1+\varepsilon/2}(x^{4/15}M^{1/20}H^{-1/2}+x^{62/225}M^{1/60}H^{-1/2})\ll q^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{1+\varepsilon/2}\bigl( 
x^{-\varepsilon}+x^{\frac{14}{75}}H^{-\frac{1}{3}} \bigr),
\end{align*}
which is acceptable provided that $H\gg x^{14/25+\varepsilon }$.

It only remains to handle the case when $R^3<qx/H$. Inserting the preceding condition into (\ref{f22}) and employing the restriction $q\ll x^{2/25+\varepsilon},$ we notice that
\begin{align*}
V\ll& q^{1/2}x^{1+\varepsilon }\Big(  q^{161/360}x^{173/360}M^{1/20}H^{-341/360}
+q^{581/1080}x^{593/1080}M^{1/60}H^{-1121/1080}\Big)\\
\ll & q^{1/2}x^{1+\varepsilon }\Big( x^{1549/3000} M^{1/20} H^{-341/360} +x^{5329/9000} M^{1/60} H^{-1121/1080}\Big)
\\
\ll& q^{1/2}x^{1+\varepsilon }\Big( x^{749/3000}  H^{-161/360} +x^{4529/9000} H^{-941/1080}\Big)\ll q^{1/2}x^{1-\varepsilon },
\end{align*}
where in the last inequalities we used the provisos $M\ll H^{10} x^{-16/3-\varepsilon }$ and $H\gg x^{0.58 }$.


As we assumed that $N\leqslant M$, the case $M < (qx/H)^{5/6} \leqslant N$ would not occur, whence it only remains to examine the instance in which $ (qx/H)^{5/6}\leqslant \min(M, N)$.

\subsection*{Case 1.2 (iii)}
Suppose  that $ (qx/H)^{5/6}\leqslant M, N$. In such a situation, we merely apply Montgomery--Hal\'asz--Huxley's bound to the $R$-polynomial (equation \eqref{r-large}). We shall instead employ the estimate \eqref{a-sigma-m-large} for both the $N$ and the $M$-polynomial, and deduce by making the choices $c_{1}=(2g)^{-1}$, $a_{1}=b_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(1-c_{1})$, and $c_{2}=(6g)^{-1}$, $a_{2}=b_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1-c_{2})$ that
\begin{align*}
x^{-\varepsilon }V \ll & \Big( \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_1)} M^{f(\sigma_1)}\Big)^{a_1}	\Big( \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_2)}  N^{f(\sigma_2)}\Big)^{a_1}	 \Big( R^{2g(1-\sigma_3)}\Big)^{c_1}	M^{\sigma_1}N^{\sigma_2} R^{\sigma_3}\\
&+ \Big( \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_1)} M^{f(\sigma_1)} \Big)^{a_2}	\Big(  \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_2)}  N^{f(\sigma_2)}\Big)^{a_2}	 \Big( \frac{qx}{H} R^{g(4-6\sigma_3)}\Big)^{c_2}	M^{\sigma_1}N^{\sigma_2} R^{\sigma_3}\\
\ll & \big( \frac{qx}{H}\big)^{3a_1-1/2}(MN)^{1-a_1} R + \big( \frac{qx}{H}\big)^{3a_2-1/2+c_2}(MN)^{1-a_2}R^{2/3}.
\end{align*}
We next note that $q\leqslant N^{6/5}H/x\ll (MN)^{3/5}H/x$ since $N\leqslant M$ by assumption. Therefore, the preceding bound combined with the condition $MN\ll x/R$, the proviso $x^{4/25 } \ll R\ll x^{1/5}$ and the choice $g=3$ yields
\begin{align*}
V&\ll q^{1/2}x^\varepsilon  \Big( q^{1/4} (x/H)^{3/4} (MN)^{7/12} R+ q^{17/36}(x/H)^{35/36}(MN)^{19/36} R^{2/3}\Big)\\
&\ll  q^{1/2}x^{1+\varepsilon}\Big(x^{-1/2}(NM)^{11/15}RH^{-1/2}+ x^{-1/2}H^{-1/2}(NM)^{73/90}R^{2/3}\Big)
\\
&\ll  q^{1/2} x^{1+\varepsilon } \Big(x^{7/30}R^{4/15} H^{-1/2} + x^{14/45}R^{-13/90} H^{-1/2}\Big)
\\
&\ll q^{1/2} x^{1+\varepsilon } \Big(x^{43/150} H^{-1/2} + x^{36/125} H^{-1/2}\Big)\ll  q^{1/2} x^{1-\varepsilon }
\end{align*}
provided that $H\geqslant x^{0.58 }$. This gives the desired bound.

\subsection*{Case 1.3 } If
$(\log x)^{O(1)} M^{2-2\sigma_1}\geqslant x^\varepsilon \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_1)} M^{f(\sigma_1)} \text{ and }N^{2-2\sigma_2}(\log x)^{O(1)} \leqslant x^\varepsilon \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_2)} N^{f(\sigma_2)}.$
Under these circumstances, we notice that the first term in  \eqref{bound-m-small-beta} dominates. Therefore, we have
\[
|\mathcal S| \ll (\log x)^{O(1)} M^{2-2\sigma_1};
\]
while for the $N$-polynomial, the classical bound \eqref{rr4} suffices, namely
\[
|\mathcal S|\ll\frac{qx^{1+\varepsilon}}{H}N^{g(\sigma_2)},
\]
where $g(\sigma_2) = \min\left\{1-2\sigma_2,4-6\sigma_2\right\}$. It then follows from \cite[(7.2.17)]{Har} that for any real $1/6\leqslant b\leqslant 1/2$ we have
\[
bg(\sigma) +\sigma \leqslant bg(3/4) +3/4=\frac{3-2b}{4}.
\]
In an analogous manner as above, we employ \eqref{r-large} for the $R$-polynomial. Therefore, as in the proof of case 3 (ii) of \cite[Lemma 7.3]{Har}, we apply H\"older's inequality for the choices $a_{1}=\frac{1}{2}$, $b_{1}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2g}$, $c_{1}=\frac{1}{2g}$ and $a_{2}=\frac{1}{2}$, $b_{2}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{6g}$, $c_{2}=\frac{1}{6g}$. This gives
\begin{align*}
V \ll & x^\varepsilon \Big( MR (qx/H)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2g}} N^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4g}} + MR^{2/3} (qx/H)^{\frac{1}{2}} N^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{12g}}\Big)\\
\ll & q^{1/2} x^{1+\varepsilon } \Big( (x/H)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2g}} N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4g}} + x^{1/6}H^{-\frac{1}{2}} M^{1/3} N^{-\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{12g}} \Big),	
\end{align*}
where we employed the proviso $MNR\ll x$ in the second line and used the trivial bound $q^{-1/(2g)}\leqslant1$ in the first one. Now taking $g=3$ delivers
\[
V\ll q^{1/2} x^{1+\varepsilon } \Big( (x/H)^{1/3}N^{-5/12} + x^{1/6}M^{1/3} N^{-5/36}H^{-1/2} \Big).
\]
The first term is acceptable since, by \eqref{uu}, we have $N\gg (x/H)^{4/5+\varepsilon }$. For the second term, since $M\ll x/(NR)$, it transpires that
\[
M^{1/3}N^{-5/36}\ll x^{1/3} N^{-17/36} R^{-1/3}\ll x^{-22/225 }H^{17/45}\ll H^{1/2}x^{-1/6 -2\varepsilon },
\]
where in the penultimate inequality we have used that $N\gg (x/H)^{4/5+\varepsilon }$ and $R\gg x^{4/25 }$ from \eqref{uu}, the last inequality in the above line holding provided that $H\gg x^{31/55+10\varepsilon}$. Therefore, we are done in this case.


\subsection*{Case 1.4 }If $(\log x)^{O(1)}M^{2-2\sigma_1} \leqslant x^\varepsilon \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_1)} M^{f(\sigma_1)} \text{ and }(\log x)^{O(1)}N^{2-2\sigma_2} \geqslant x^\varepsilon \big(\frac{qx}{H}\big)^{h(\sigma_2)} N^{f(\sigma_2)}.$
 This case is symmetrical to Case 1.3, whence no further details are thereby required.

This completes the estimation of \eqref{small-beta} for all $1/2\leqslant \sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3\leqslant 1$. We now turn to the complementary range $\beta \geqslant x(\log x)^{A/3}/H^2$.

\subsection*{Case 2. $x(\log x)^{A/3}/H^2\leqslant \beta$} In this context one has $F(\beta)=2\beta H$, and the goal becomes to prove \eqref{large-beta} in this range. We first note that the Diophantine approximation conditions in \eqref{sss} entail that $q\beta\leqslant x^{-2/25-\varepsilon}$. Consequently, whenever $H\ll x^{32/55}$, one has
\[
(q\beta H)^{2/3} \leqslant H^{2/3} x^{-4/75 } \ll (x/H)^{4/5} \ll M.
\]
Thus, the lower-bound hypotheses on $M$ and $N$ required to apply \eqref{pp9} are satisfied. Arguing as in the preceding range, we apply Theorem \ref{thm:large-values} to the Dirichlet polynomials running over $m$, $n$ and $r$, the only change being that the assumption underlying \eqref{pp91} regarding the sizes of $N$ and $M$ is now $N,M>(q\beta H)^{5/6}$. More precisely, one gets analogous estimates to those in \eqref{bound-m-small-beta}-\eqref{bound-r-2}, namely
\begin{align} \label{bound-m-large-beta}
|\mathcal S| \ll (\log x)^{O(1)}M^{2-2\sigma_1}  + x^\varepsilon (q\beta H)^{h(\sigma_1)}M^{f(\sigma_1)},	
\end{align}
where if $M\leqslant (q\beta H)^{5/6}$ then   $f(\sigma_1)$ and $h(\sigma_1)$ are defined as in \eqref{f-q-large}; and otherwise one has
\[
f(\sigma_1)=
\begin{cases}
1-2\sigma_1, & q\beta H\leqslant M^{4-4\sigma_1},\\
3-4\sigma_1, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
h(\sigma_1)=
\begin{cases}
1, & q\beta H\leqslant M^{4-4\sigma_1},\\
1/2, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The same argument applied to the $N$-polynomial gives
\begin{align} \label{bound-n-large-beta}
|\mathcal S| \ll N^{2-2\sigma_2} (\log x)^{O(1)} + x^\varepsilon (q\beta H)^{h(\sigma_2)}N^{f(\sigma_2)}.	
\end{align}
For the $R$-polynomial, the Montgomery--Hal\'asz--Huxley's large values estimate delivers,  for every positive integer $g$, the bound
\begin{align}\label{r-large-beta-large}
|\mathcal S| \ll x^\varepsilon (R^{2g(1-\sigma_3)} +q\beta H R^{g(4-6\sigma_3)}).
\end{align}
Moreover, if 
$$(q\beta H)^{2/3}\leqslant R^{g}\leqslant (q\beta H)^{5/6}$$ then one may apply Theorem \ref{thm:large-values} to deduce that
\begin{equation}\label{bound-r-2-beta-large}
 \lvert \mathcal{S}\rvert\ll  (\log x)^{O(1)}R^{2g(1-\sigma_{3})}+x^\varepsilon q\beta HR^{gf(\sigma_{3})},\end{equation}
where $f(\sigma_3)$ is the piecewise-linear function defined in \eqref{f-q-large}. 
 
As before, set $V=\sum_{q_0q_1=q } d_{6k+3}(q_{0})\lvert \mathcal{S}\rvert M^{\sigma_{1}}N^{\sigma_{2}}R^{\sigma_{3}}$. To prove \eqref{large-beta}, it is enough to show that, uniformly for $1/2\leqslant \sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3\leqslant 1$, one has
\[
  V=\sum_{q_0q_1=q } d_{6k+3}(q_{0})\lvert \mathcal{S}\rvert M^{\sigma_{1}}N^{\sigma_{2}}R^{\sigma_{3}}\ll (q\beta x)^{1/2} H (\log x)^{-A}.
\] 
In an analogous manner, we divide the proof into four cases, according to whether the diagonal term dominates in each of \eqref{bound-m-large-beta} and \eqref{bound-n-large-beta}.

\subsection*{Case 2.1} If both the bound $(\log x)^{O(1)}M^{2-2\sigma_1}  \geqslant x^\varepsilon (q\beta H)^{h(\sigma_1)}M^{f(\sigma_1)}$ and its counterpart $(\log x)^{O(1)} N^{2-2\sigma_2}  \geqslant  x^\varepsilon (q\beta H)^{h(\sigma_2)}N^{f(\sigma_2)}.$
Suppose first that $q\geqslant(\log x)^{3A}$. Then, combining \eqref{bound-m-large-beta} and \eqref{bound-n-large-beta} with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\begin{align*}
 V& \ll (\log x)^{O(1)}  \sum_{q_0q_1=q }d_{6k+3}(q_{0}) \big(M^{2-2\sigma_1} N^{2-2\sigma_2}\big)^{1/2} M^{\sigma_1} N^{\sigma_2} R
\\
&\ll (\log x)^{O(1)}x \sum_{q_0q_1=q } \frac{ d_{6k+3}(q_{0})}{q_0} \ll q^{1/2}x(\log x)^{-A}\ll \frac{(q\beta x)^{\frac12}H }{ (\log x)^A},
\end{align*}
where in the last step we used the assumption $\beta \geqslant x(\log x)^{A/3}/H^2$. This proves the desired estimate in this case.

It remains to consider  $1\leqslant q\leqslant (\log x)^{3A}$.  In this range, the pointwise estimate \eqref{pointwise-r} provides an arbitrarily large logarithmic saving for the $R$-polynomial. Therefore, arguing as in Case 1.1 we obtain
\[
V\ll q^{1/2} x(\log x)^{-3A/2+O(1)} \ll (q\beta x)^{1/2}H (\log x)^{-A},
\]
where we used the condition $\beta \geqslant x(\log x)^{A/3}/H^2$.


\subsection*{Case 2.2} If both the bound $M^{2-2\sigma_1} (\log x)^{O(1)} \leqslant x^\varepsilon (q\beta H)^{h(\sigma_1)}M^{f(\sigma_1)}$ and its counterpart $N^{2-2\sigma_2} (\log x)^{O(1)} \leqslant  x^\varepsilon (q\beta H)^{h(\sigma_2)}N^{f(\sigma_2)}$ hold.  
\subsection*{Case 2.2 (i)} Suppose that $M,N \leqslant (q\beta H)^{5/6}$. In this case the functions $f(\sigma)$ and $h(\sigma)$ in estimates \eqref{bound-m-large-beta} and \eqref{bound-n-large-beta} are as in \eqref{f-q-large}. Therefore, in a similar manner as in \eqref{a-sigma}, one has for any real number  $1/4\leqslant a\leqslant 1/2$ the bound
\begin{align}\label{a-sigma-large-beta}
|\mathcal S|^a M^{\sigma_{1}} \ll x^\varepsilon (q\beta H)^a M^{af(\sigma_1)+\sigma_1}\ll x^\varepsilon (q\beta H)^{a}M^{\frac{7-4a}{10}}
\end{align}
for arbitrary $1/2\leqslant \sigma_1,\sigma_2\leqslant 1$. An analogous estimate holds as well for $N$.

As in the proof of Case 1.2 (i) we first assume that $ (q\beta H)^{2/3}\leqslant R^{2}\leqslant (q\beta H)^{5/6}$. It then follows from \eqref{bound-r-2-beta-large} that
\[
|\mathcal S| \ll x^\varepsilon \big( R^{4-4\sigma_3} + q\beta H R^{24/5-8\sigma_3}\big).
\]
Similarly as therein, we take $a_1=b_1=3/8,c_1=1/4$, and $a_2=b_2=7/16$, $c_2=1/8$ and deduce from the above estimates and  $MNR\ll x$ that
\begin{align*}
V \ll & x^{\varepsilon } \Big( (q\beta H)^{2a_1} M^{a_1f(\sigma_1)+\sigma_1}N^{a_1 f(\sigma_2)+\sigma_2}R + q\beta H M^{a_2f(\sigma_1)+\sigma_1}N^{a_2 f(\sigma_2)+\sigma_2} R^{3/5}\Big)\\
\ll & x^{\varepsilon } \big( (q\beta H)^{3/4} (MN)^{11/20} R+ q\beta H (MN)^{21/40} R^{3/5}\big)\\
\ll &  x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H\big( (q\beta)^{1/4} x^{1/20} R^{9/20} H^{-1/4} +(q\beta)^{1/2} x^{1/40} R^{3/40}\big).	
\end{align*}
We now use the assumptions $q\beta \ll x^{-2/25-\varepsilon }$ and $R\ll x^{1/5}$ in conjunction with the condition $H\gg x^{0.58+\varepsilon}$ to obtain
\[
V \ll x^{\varepsilon/2} (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big(x^{3/25-\varepsilon } H^{-1/4} +x^{-\varepsilon }\big) \ll  x^{-\varepsilon/2} (q\beta x)^{1/2} H,
\]
as desired.

We then consider the case in which either $R\leqslant (q\beta H)^{1/3}$ or $R\geqslant (q\beta H)^{5/12}$. Taking $c_1=1/6$, $a_1=5/12$ and $c_2=1/18$, $a_2=17/36$, we have by \eqref{bound-r-2-beta-large} for the choice $g=3$ and $MNR\ll x$  that
\begin{align}\label{ec135}
V &\ll x^\varepsilon \Big( (q\beta H)^{2a_1} M^{a_1f(\sigma_1)+\sigma_1}N^{a_1 f(\sigma_2)+\sigma_2} R +q\beta H M^{a_2f(\sigma_1)+\sigma_1}N^{a_2 f(\sigma_2)+\sigma_2} R^{2/3}\Big)\nonumber\\
&\ll x^\varepsilon \Big((q\beta H)^{2a_{1}}(MN)^{\frac{7-4a_{1}}{10}}R+(q\beta H)(MN)^{\frac{7-4a_{2}}{10}}R^{2/3}\Big)\nonumber
\\
&\ll  x^\varepsilon  \big((q\beta H)^{5/6} (MN)^{8/15} R+ q\beta H (MN)^{23/45} R^{2/3}\big)\nonumber\\
&\ll x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big( (q\beta)^{1/3} x^{1/30} R^{7/15} H^{-1/6} +(q\beta)^{1/2} x^{1/90} R^{7/45}\big).
\end{align}
Suppose first that $R^3\leqslant q\beta H$. One then deduces from this assumption and $q\beta \ll x^{-2/25-\varepsilon }$ that
\begin{align*}
V \ll &x^{\varepsilon/2} (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big((q\beta)^{22/45}x^{1/30}H^{-1/90}+(q\beta)^{149/270}x^{1/90}H^{7/135}\big)\\
&\ll x^{\varepsilon/2} (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big( x^{-\varepsilon  }+ x^{-223/6750-\varepsilon  }H^{7/135}\big) \ll x^{-\varepsilon/2 } (q\beta x)^{1/2} H 
\end{align*}
provided that $H\ll x^{223/350}$, which holds in view of the restriction $H\ll x^{32/55}$.

On the other hand, suppose that $R \geqslant (q\beta H)^{5/12}$. Then $q\beta \leqslant R^{12/5}/H$, whence inserting this inequality and the assumption $R\ll x^{1/5}$ in (\ref{ec135}) we find that
\begin{align*}
V&\ll x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H (x^{1/30}R^{19/15}H^{-1/2}+x^{1/90}R^{61/45}H^{-1/2})
\\
&\ll x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H (x^{43/150} H^{-1/2} + x^{127/450}H^{-1/2} ).
\end{align*}
The preceding bound is acceptable whenever $H\gg x^{43/75+3\varepsilon }$, as desired. 

 

\subsection*{Case 2.2 (ii)} Suppose that $N \leqslant (q\beta H)^{5/6} \leqslant M$. Since $M\geqslant (q\beta H)^{5/6}$, one may apply \eqref{bound-m-large-beta} to the $M$-polynomial. Arguing as in \eqref{a-sigma-m-large}, we thus obtain
\begin{align}\label{sigma-m-ll}
	|\mathcal S|^a M^{\sigma_1} \ll x^\varepsilon M^{1-a} (q\beta H)^{\frac{3a}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}
\end{align}
 uniformly for  $1/2\leqslant \sigma_1\leqslant 1$ and $1/4\leqslant a\leqslant 1/2$.
 
 When $(q\beta H)^{2/3} \leqslant R^2 \leqslant (q\beta H)^{5/6}$, we make the choices $b_1=c_1=1/4, a_1=1/2$, and $a_2=1/2, b_2=3/8$ and $c_2=1/8$. Employing \eqref{sigma-m-ll} for the $M$-polynomial, the analogue of \eqref{a-sigma-large-beta} for the $N$-polynomial, and \eqref{bound-r-2-beta-large} with $g=2$ for the $R$-polynomial, and using the inequality after \eqref{a-sigma-m-large} and $MNR\ll x$, we obtain 
 \begin{align*}
V\ll& x^\varepsilon \big((q\beta H)^{\frac{3a_1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+b_1} M^{1-a_1} N^{\frac{7-4b_1}{10}} R+(q\beta H)^{\frac{3a_2}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+b_2+c_2} M^{1-a_2} N^{\frac{7-4b_2}{10}} R^{\frac{7-8c_2}{10}}\big)\\
\ll& x^\varepsilon \big((q\beta H)^{3/4}M^{1/2} N^{3/5} R + q\beta H M^{1/2}N^{11/20} R^{3/5}\big)\\
\ll & x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big((q\beta)^{1/4} H^{-1/4}N^{1/10} R^{1/2} +(q\beta)^{1/2} N^{1/20} R^{1/10}\big). 
\end{align*}
We then bound the first term using the assumptions $N \ll (q\beta H)^{5/6}$, $q\beta \ll x^{-2/25-\varepsilon}$ and  $R\ll x^{1/5}$; and the second one employing $q\beta \ll x^{-2/25-\varepsilon }$. This leads to 
\begin{align*}
V&\ll x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big((q\beta)^{1/3} H^{-1/6}x^{1/10} +(q\beta)^{1/2} N^{1/20} R^{1/10}\big)
\\
&\ll x^\varepsilon   (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big(x^{11/150-\varepsilon}H^{-1/6}+x^{-1/25-\varepsilon}N^{1/20}R^{1/10}\big),
\end{align*}
the first term being acceptable if $H\gg x^{1/2}$, and the second one being of the required size whenever $N^{1/2}R\ll x^{2/5}$, which was assumed in \eqref{bb13}.


If instead either $R\leqslant (q\beta H)^{1/3}$ or $R\geqslant (q\beta H)^{5/12}$, arguing as in Case 2.2 (i), we shall apply \eqref{bound-r-2-beta-large} with $g=3$ to the $R$-polynomial. Hence, the choices $(a_1,b_1,c_1)=(5/12,5/12,1/6)$ and $(a_2,b_2,c_2)=(17/36,17/36,1/18)$ combined with the condition $MNR\ll x$, deliver
\begin{align}\label{ec72}
V \ll & x^\varepsilon \big( (q\beta H)^{\frac{3a_1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+b_1} M^{1-a_1} N^{\frac{7-4b_1}{10}} R+(q\beta H)^{ \frac{3a_2}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+b_2+c_2} M^{1-a_2} N^{\frac{7-4b_2}{10}} R^{2/3} \big)\nonumber\\
\ll& x^\varepsilon \big((q\beta H)^{19/24} M^{7/12}N^{8/15}R+(q\beta H)^{71/72}M^{19/36}N^{23/45}R^{2/3}\big)\nonumber\\
\ll& x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{\frac12} H \big( (q\beta)^{\frac{7}{24}} H^{-\frac{5}{24}} x^{\frac{1}{30}} M^{\frac{1}{20}} R^{\frac{7}{15}}
+(q\beta)^{\frac{35}{72}} H^{-\frac{1}{72}} x^{\frac{1}{90}} M^{\frac{1}{60}} R^{\frac{7}{45}} \big).	
\end{align}

We assume $q\beta H\leqslant R^{12/5}$ first. It then transpires by the above equation and the assumption $R\ll x^{1/5}$ that
\begin{align*}
V \ll& x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big( x^{1/30} M^{1/20} R^{7/6} H^{-1/2} + x^{1/90} M^{1/60} R^{119/90} H^{-1/2}\big)\\
\ll&  x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big( x^{4/15} M^{1/20} H^{-1/2} + x^{62/225} M^{1/60} H^{-1/2}\big)\\
\ll & x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big( x^{-2\varepsilon} + x^{14/75}  H^{-1/3}\big)\ll x^{-\varepsilon} (q\beta x)^{1/2} H,
\end{align*}
the last inequalities following provided that $M\ll H^{10} x^{-16/3-40\varepsilon}$, which is assumed in \eqref{uu}, and $H\gg x^{0.58+\varepsilon }$.

If instead $R\leqslant (q\beta H)^{1/3}$, we insert the condition $q\beta \ll x^{-2/25-\varepsilon }$ into (\ref{ec72}) to derive that
\begin{align*}
V&\ll  x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big(  (q\beta )^{161/360} H^{-19/360} x^{1/30} M^{1/20}  +(q\beta)^{581/1080} H^{41/1080} x^{1/90} M^{1/60} \big)
\\
&\ll  x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big( x^{-11/4500}M^{\frac{1}{20}}H^{-19/360} +x^{-\frac{431}{13500}}M^{1/60}H^{41/1080} \big).
\end{align*}
This is acceptable when $M\ll H^{10}x^{-16/3-\varepsilon }$ and $x^{0.58+\varepsilon }\leqslant H \leqslant x^{0.59}$, since then 
\begin{align*}
V&\ll  x^\varepsilon (q\beta x)^{1/2} H \big( x^{-1211/4500}H^{161/360} +x^{-\frac{1631}{13500}}H^{221/1080} \big)\ll x^{-\varepsilon} (q\beta x)^{1/2} H,
\end{align*}
as desired.
\subsection*{Case 2.2 (iii)} Suppose that $M,N \geqslant (q\beta H)^{5/6}$. We shall apply inequality \eqref{sigma-m-ll} to both the M-polynomial and the $N$-polynomial. Arguing as in Case 1.2 (iii), we also apply \eqref{r-large-beta-large} with $g=3$ to the $R$-polynomial. It then follows by making the choices $(a_1,b_1,c_1)=(5/12,5/12,1/6)$ and $(a_2,b_2,c_2)=(17/36,17/36,1/18)$ that

We first claim that it suffices to prove the estimate for the range $x^{0.58+\varepsilon }\leqslant H\leqslant x^{32/55 }$. Indeed, when $H\geqslant x^{32/55}$, we may decompose $(x,x+H]$ into intervals of length $\Theta(x^{0.58+\varepsilon })$ and apply the preceding claim to each of the former intervals. Moreover, Proposition \ref{prop:major-arc} provides quasipolynomial cancellation for the exponential sum on the major arcs. We may therefore restrict our attention to the minor arcs, where it suffices to prove that
\[
\sup_{\alpha \in \mathfrak m} \Big|\sum_{x<n \leqslant x+H} \mu(n) e(n\alpha)\Big| \ll H(\log x)^{-1/3+\varepsilon }.
\]
In light of Lemma \ref{lem:reduce-to-mn}, it further suffices to show that
\begin{align}\label{mn-bound-a}
\sup_{\alpha \in \mathfrak m} \Big|\sum_{x<n \leqslant x+H} \mu_{\boldsymbol N}(n) e(n\alpha)\Big| \ll H(\log x)^{-A/100 }
\end{align} where $\mu_{\boldsymbol N}$ is defined in \eqref{mobius-n}.                                                                                                                                           

                                                                                                                                                                                                                      


Let $k=20$ in \eqref{mobius-n} and denote $N_i= x^{\gamma_i}$ for $1\leqslant i\leqslant 39$, $R=x^{\gamma_{40}}$ and $P_1=x^{\gamma_{41}}$.  Whenever $(N_i,2N_i]$ is the supporting range of a M\"obius factor, the construction in \eqref{mobius-n} gives $\gamma_i\leqslant1/20$. Thus,

\gamma_i\geqslant 0,\qquad	\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant 41} \gamma_i=1, \qquad \text{and } \gamma_i\leqslant 1/20 \quad\text{ when } 20\leqslant i\leqslant 41.
\end{align}
 Set $H=x^\theta$. Lemmas \ref{lem:type-ii}--\ref{lem:type-iii} show that \eqref{mn-bound-a}  holds uniformly for $x^{0.58+\varepsilon }\leqslant H \leqslant x^{32/55}$ whenever at least one of the following four conditions is satisfied:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item[Case 1] (Type I sum). There exists some $\gamma_i$ such that $\gamma_i>1-\theta$.
	\item[Case 2] (Type I$_2$ sum). There exist $\gamma_i\neq \gamma_j$ such that $\gamma_i+\gamma_j \geqslant 4(1-\theta)/3$.
	\item[Case 3] (Type II sum). There exists a  partition $\left\{1,\dots,41\right\}=I\sqcup J$ such that 
    \[
	1-\theta <\sum_{i\in I} \gamma_i,\sum_{j\in J} \gamma_j<\theta.
	\]
	\item[Case 4] (Type III sum). There exists a  partition $\left\{1,\dots,41\right\}=I\sqcup J \sqcup K$ such that
	\begin{align}\label{con1}
	 4(1-\theta)/5< \sum_{j\in J} \gamma_j \leqslant \sum_{i\in I} \gamma_i &< 10\theta -16/3;\\ \label{con2}
	 4/25 \leqslant \sum_{k\in K}\gamma_k &\leqslant 1/5;\\
	\text{and}\quad \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in J}\gamma_j+\sum_{k\in K} \gamma_k&\leqslant 2/5. \label{con3}
			\end{align}
	
\end{enumerate}
It therefore remains to prove that every sequence  $\left\{\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_{41}\right\}$ satisfying \eqref{cond-gamma} falls into one of these four cases.

We may assume that Cases 1, 2, and 3 do not occur. We shall prove that Case 4 must then hold.

For any partition of the index set $\left\{\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_{41}\right\}$ into two disjoint subsets $\mathcal I$ and $\mathcal J$, write  $\gamma_I$ and $\gamma_J$ as the summation over the elements in $\mathcal I$ and $\mathcal J$ respectively, and label the two subsets so that $\gamma_I\leqslant\gamma_J$. Since Case 3 does not occur, we have
 \[
 \gamma_I = \min\left\{\gamma_I,\gamma_J\right\} <1-\theta.
 \]
Choose a partition $(\mathcal I,\mathcal J)$ for which the discrepancy $\gamma_J-\gamma_I$ is minimal, and set  $d=\gamma_J-\gamma_I$. Clearly,
\begin{align}\label{d-lower-bd}
d= 1-2\gamma_I >2\theta -1.
\end{align}	

Since neither Case 1 nor Case 2 occur and by our assumption $\gamma_J>\theta >4(1-\theta)/3$ provided that $\theta>4/7$, we thereby have $|\mathcal J|\geqslant3$. We claim that, in fact, $|\mathcal J|=3$. Let $\gamma'$ be the smallest element in $\mathcal J$. Indeed, if we transfer $\gamma'$ from $\mathcal J$ to $\mathcal I$, the minimality of the discrepancy gives
\[
|(\gamma_J-\gamma') -(\gamma_I +\gamma')| =|d-2\gamma'|\geqslant d.
\]
Since $\gamma'>0$, this forces
\begin{align}\label{gamma'-lower}
\gamma'\geqslant d.	
\end{align} 
Meanwhile, since $(\mathcal I,\mathcal J)$ is the partition so that the discrepancy $\gamma_J-\gamma_I$ is minimal and $\gamma_I<1-\theta$, we must have 
\begin{equation}\label{dfd}
\gamma_J-\gamma'\leqslant \gamma_I <1-\theta.\end{equation}
Now if  $|\mathcal J|\geqslant 4$ we then have from the above inequality, the assumption on $\gamma'$, and \eqref{d-lower-bd}-\eqref{gamma'-lower} that 
\[
6\theta -3 <3\gamma' <\gamma_J-\gamma'<1-\theta.
\]
This leads to $\theta\leqslant 4/7$, which contradicts the proviso $\theta>0.58$. Thus, $|\mathcal J|=3$.

 Moreover, we also have from \eqref{d-lower-bd} and \eqref{gamma'-lower} that
\[
1-\gamma_I =\gamma_J \geqslant 3\gamma' \geqslant 3(1-2\gamma_I).
\]
This gives 
\begin{align}\label{gamma-i}
	\gamma_I \geqslant 2/5 \qquad \text{and } \qquad \gamma'\leqslant 1/5.
\end{align}

Let $K=\{\gamma'\}$, and write  $I$ for the index of elements in $\mathcal I$ and $J$ for the index of elements in $\mathcal J/\left\{\gamma'\right\}$. It remains to verify that $(I,J,K)$ satisfies the conditions of Case 4. First,
\[
\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_i =\gamma_I <1-\theta < 10\theta -16/3,
\]
where the last inequality holds provided that $\theta >19/33$. Next,  it follows directly from (\ref{dfd}) that
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i\in J}\gamma_j=\gamma_J-\gamma' \leqslant \gamma_I=\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_i.	
\end{align*}
Furthermore, using both (\ref{gamma-i}) and the bound $\gamma_{J}\geqslant \theta$ we also have 
\[
\sum_{j\in J}\gamma_j = \gamma_J-\gamma'\geqslant \theta -1/5 >\frac{4(1-\theta)}{5}
\]
provided that $\theta>5/9$. The above three inequalities give (\ref{con1}). Moreover, it follows from \eqref{d-lower-bd} and \eqref{gamma'-lower} that
\[
\gamma' \geqslant 1-2\gamma_I \geqslant 1-2(1-\theta) =2\theta -1> 4/25
\]
whenever $\theta>0.58$. Combining this with the upper bound given in \eqref{gamma-i} delivers the condition (\ref{con2}), it only remaining to verify the third one~\eqref{con3}. Using \eqref{gamma-i}, we have
\[
\frac{\sum_{j\in J}\gamma_j}{2}+\gamma' = \frac{1-\gamma_I+\gamma' }{2} \leqslant \frac{1-2/5+1/5}{2} =2/5.
\]
Thus, all the conditions of Case 4 are satisfied, thereby completing the proof.

By the above lemma, we may write $N(\sigma,T,U,q)=R_{I}(\sigma,T,U,q)+R_{II}(\sigma,T,U,q)$, where $R_{I}(\sigma,T,U,q)$ denotes the number of Type I zeros, that is, zeros satisfying (\ref{hhg}), and $R_{II}(\sigma,T,U,q)$, that denotes the number of Type II zeros, that is, zeros satisfying (\ref{po}).

Followig the proof of Lemma 24 in \cite{May-Pratt}, we deduce that
\begin{align*}R_{II}(\sigma,T,U,q)&\ll \frac{(\log T)^{O(1)}}{Y^{4\sigma-2}}\sum_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)}\int_{T-2U}^{T+2U}\Big\lvert M\Big(\frac{1}{2}+it\Big)L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+it,\chi\Big)\Big\rvert^{4} dt
\\
&\ll U^{\frac{25}{21}(1-2\sigma)+9\delta/2}(U+T^{\frac{25}{42}+\varepsilon})\ll U^{\frac{50}{21}(1-\sigma)+5\delta},
\end{align*} where in the second step we employed the assumption (\ref{eq-moment}) and in the last one we used the proviso $T\leqslant U^{2+\varepsilon}$.

In order to bound the type I zeros, we introduce $$\widetilde{b_{n}}=\Big(\frac{N}{n}\Big)^{\sigma} a(n)\chi(n)\exp\Big(-\frac{n}{Y}\Big),\ \ \ \ \widetilde{D}_{N}(t)=\sum_{n\in [N,2N]} \widetilde{b_{n}}n^{it}.$$
By the classical zero-detecting methodology, it suffices to show (see the proof of Theorem 1.2 in \cite{GM}) that if $U^{\delta}\leqslant N <Y(\log T)^{2}$ and $W_{\chi}$ is a $1$-separated set in $[T-U, T+U]$ such that $\lvert \widetilde{D}_{N} (t)\rvert\gg N^{\sigma}$ then one has $\lvert W_{\chi}\rvert\leqslant U^{50(1-\sigma)/21+\varepsilon}$. We begin by showing first that whenever $U^{5/7}\ll N\ll U^{25/21+\varepsilon}$ then \begin{equation}\label{11.8}\lvert W_{\chi}\rvert\leqslant U^{50(1-\sigma)/21+\varepsilon}.\end{equation}

Indeed, if $\sigma\leqslant 7/10$, by (\ref{rr44}) it follows that 
$$\lvert W_{\chi}\rvert\ll U^{\varepsilon}\big(N^{2-2\sigma}+UN^{1-2\sigma}\big).$$ We then claim that the right side of the above equation is $O(U^{50(1-\sigma)/21+\varepsilon}).$ This may be seen by observing that when $U^{5/7}\ll N\ll U^{25/21+\varepsilon}$ and $\sigma=7/10$ then
$$N^{2-2\sigma}+UN^{1-2\sigma}=N^{3/5}+UN^{-2/5}\ll U^{5/7+\varepsilon}=U^{50(1-\sigma)/21+\varepsilon}.$$ If instead $\sigma<7/10$ then $N^{2-2\sigma}\ll U^{50(1-\sigma)/21+\varepsilon}$, whence it suffices to show that 
\begin{equation}\label{khl}N\Big(\frac{U^{50/21}}{N^{2}}\Big)^{\sigma}\ll U^{29/21+\varepsilon}.\end{equation} We may deduce that the above holds since it is satisfied when $\sigma=7/10$ and since $N^{2}\ll U^{50/21+2\varepsilon}$.

We shall now focus on the instance $\sigma>7/10$, and suppose first that $U^{5/7}\ll N\ll U^{5/6}$. Note that under such circumstances, the third term in the right side of (\ref{RR}) dominates over the second one. Therefore, an application of such a bound yields
$$\lvert W_{\chi}\rvert\ll U^{\varepsilon}\big(N^{2-2\sigma}+UN^{12/5-4\sigma}\big).$$ As above, the term $N^{2-2\sigma}$ is $O(U^{50(1-\sigma)/21+3\varepsilon}).$ Therefore, it is enough showing that

$$\lvert W_{\chi}\rvert\ll U^{\varepsilon}\big(N^{2-2\sigma}+\min(UN^{1-2\sigma},UN^{4-6\sigma})\big).$$
