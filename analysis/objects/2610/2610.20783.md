<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:no-2-cycle}
The scalar heterogeneous HK model has no nonconstant orbit of period two.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Suppose two distinct states $p,q$ satisfy $q=F(p)$ and $p=F(q)$, where
$F$ denotes the HK update. Choose $i$ with
$|q_i-p_i|=\Delta:=\max_j|q_j-p_j|>0$. Reflecting the line if necessary,
assume $q_i=p_i+\Delta$. Since $q_i$ averages opinions within $\rho_i$
of $p_i$, we have $\Delta\le\rho_i$.

Let $I=N_i(p)\cap N_i(q)$, $D=N_i(p)\setminus I$, and
$E=N_i(q)\setminus I$. A departing source cannot leave through the
right boundary, because
$q_j\le p_j+\Delta\le q_i+\rho_i$ for $j\in D$.
It therefore leaves through the left boundary, giving
$p_j\le q_j+\Delta<q_i-\rho_i+\Delta\le q_i$.
Similarly, a new source cannot enter from the left: if
$p_j<p_i-\rho_i$, then $q_j\le p_j+\Delta<q_i-\rho_i$.
Hence every $j\in E$ satisfies
$q_j\ge p_j-\Delta>p_i+\rho_i-\Delta\ge p_i$.

Removing the departing sources from the average for $q_i$ can only
increase it; removing the entering sources from the average for $p_i$
can only decrease it. Consequently,
\[
 \sum_{j\in I}p_j\ge |I|q_i,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j\in I}q_j\le |I|p_i.
\]
But $q_j\ge p_j-\Delta$ for all $j$, with strict inequality at $j=i$.
Since self inclusion gives $i\in I$,
\[
 \sum_{j\in I}q_j
 >\sum_{j\in I}p_j-|I|\Delta
 \ge |I|(q_i-\Delta)=|I|p_i,
\]
a contradiction.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:cycle}
Let $n=6$. For the initial condition $x(0)=(0,z^*,2/5,3/5,1-z^*,1)^T\in \mathbb{R}^n$ with observation radii profile $\rho=(0.001,0.25,0.59,0.59,0.25,0.001)$ (see Table~\ref{tab:config} and Fig.~\ref{fig:config}), the resulting heterogeneous HK dynamics {\color{black}is periodic with fundamental period ten}. As a result, the heterogeneous HK model does not always converge.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For the given initial condition, we have two anchor agents $i=1$ and $i=6$ at $0$ and $1$, respectively (which we show to be fixed throughout the evolution). Call agents $2$ and $5${\color{black}, initially located at $z^*$ and $1-z^*$,} the green agents, and the agents at $2/5$ and $1-2/5=3/5$ the blue agents. 

The symmetry of this initial condition around $1/2$ and the symmetry of the the observation radii implies symmetrical evolution of the dynamics around $1/2$ throughout. Assuming the endpoints remain fixed, write
$x(t)=\bigl(0,g(t),b(t),1-b(t),1-g(t),1\bigr)^\top$. We will prove simultaneously that this representation holds for all
time.

Consider the working intervals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:region}
 g\in I_g:=[\frac1{250},\frac7{50}],\qquad
 b\in I_b:=[\frac14,\frac25].
\end{equation}
Here $0<g<b<1-b<1-g<1$. Each endpoint observes only itself, since its
nearest neighbor is at distance $g\ge1/250>1/1000$. The left blue
agent always observes $0,g,b,1-b$, excludes $1$, and observes $1-g$ precisely
when $b+g\ge41/100$. The left green agent always observes $0,g$ and observes
$b$ precisely when $b-g\le1/4$. It excludes the other three agents:
\[
 1-b-g\ge\frac{46}{100}>\frac14,\qquad
 1-2g\ge\frac{72}{100}>\frac14,\qquad
 1-g>\frac14.
\]
The right agents have the corresponding reflected neighborhoods. For a generic $b\in I_b,g\in I_g$  define the {\color{black}binary indicator} variables
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gates}
 v=\mathbf1_{\{b+g\ge41/100\}},\qquad
 u=\mathbf1_{\{b-g\le1/4\}}.
\end{equation}
Note that $u,v$ depend on $b,g$. With this, $b(t)$ and $g(t)$ can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
 \begin{aligned}
 b(t+1)&=\begin{cases}
       2/5,&v(t)=1,\\
       (1+g(t))/4,&v(t)=0,
      \end{cases}
 &g(t+1)&=\begin{cases}
       (b(t)+g(t))/3,&u(t)=1,\\
       g(t)/2,&u(t)=0,
      \end{cases}
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $u(t),v(t)$ are the corresponding indicator variables to $b(t),g(t)$. 
For example, when $v(t)=1$ the {\color{black}opinions of the} five agents observed by the left blue agent
{\color{black}sum} to $0+g(t)+b(t)+(1-b(t))+(1-g(t))=2$, which explains the reset to $2/5$.

If the initial condition $z^*$ {\color{black}were} instead set to any $z\in I:=\left[\frac{139}{1000},\frac7{50}\right]$,
starting from $(b,g)=(2/5,z)$, successive applications of
\eqref{eq:F} give Table~\ref{tab:itinerary} due to the fact that 
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 b(t)+g(t)&>41/100 &&(t=0,1,2,3,9),\\
 b(t)+g(t)&<41/100 &&(t=4,5,6,7,8),\\
 b(t)-g(t)&>1/4   &&(t=0,1,2,3,4),\\
b(t)-g(t)&<1/4   &&(t=5,6,7,8,9).
 \end{aligned}
\]
These strict inequalities verify the switches throughout $I$.

Noticing that $z^*$, the {\color{black}fixed-point} solution of $z=2161/15552+z/1024$, belongs to the interior of $I${\color{black}, which leads} to the corresponding {\color{black}period-$10$} orbit outlined in that table, concludes the proof.
