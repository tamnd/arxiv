<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{intro_thm}
Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety and let $\mathcal F$ be a smooth co-rank one foliation on $X$.
Suppose that $K_{\mathcal F}$ is big or that $K_{\mathcal F}$ is not pseudo-effective.

Then we may run a $K_{\mathcal F}$-MMP $\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow X'$ such that, writing $\mathcal F' := \phi_*\mathcal F$, the following hold: 
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $K_{\mathcal F}$ is big, then $\mathcal F'$ is a minimal model, i.e. $K_{\mathcal F'}$ is nef; or
\item if $K_{\mathcal F}$ is not pseudo-effective, then $\mathcal F'$ admits a Mori fibre space, i.e. a contraction  
$\eta\colon X'\to Z$ onto a normal projective variety $Z$ such that $\operatorname{dim} Z<\operatorname{dim} X$, 
$\rho(X'/Z)=1$ and $-K_{\mathcal F'}$ is $\eta$-ample.  
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
Let $X$ be a normal projective variety, let $\mathcal F$ be a
co-rank one foliation on $X$ which admits local holomorphic first integrals, and
let $\Delta\geq0$ be a $\mathbb Q$-divisor such that
$K_{\mathcal F}+\Delta$ is $\mathbb Q$-Cartier.

Then we may write
\[
\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)
=
\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)_{K_{\mathcal F}+\Delta\geq0}
+Z_{-\infty}
+\sum_i\mathbb R_{\geq0}[\xi_i],
\]
where each $\xi_i$ is a rational curve tangent to $\mathcal F$
satisfying
\[
0<-(K_{\mathcal F}+\Delta)\cdot\xi_i\leq2\operatorname{dim} X,
\]
and $Z_{-\infty}$ is the closed cone generated by the classes
of curves contained in a non-log canonical centre of
$(\mathcal F,\Delta)$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Let $X$ be a normal variety and let 
$\mathcal F$ be a foliation on $X$. 
\begin{itemize}
\item If $\operatorname{Sing}(X, \mathcal F) = \emptyset$, then $\mathcal F$ is quasi-smooth.
\item Let $X$ be a normal variety, let $Y$ be a smooth variety and consider the foliation $\mathcal F$ on $X \times Y$ induced by the projection $X \times Y \to Y$.  Then $\mathcal F$ is quasi-smooth.
\end{itemize}

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem_tang_zero}
Note that if $X$ is a variety with rational singularities, $\mathcal F$ is 
a quasi-smooth foliation and $j\colon V \to X$ is a morphism from a variety with rational singularities such that $j(V)$ 
is a subvariety of $X$ which is tangent to $\mathcal F$, then the composition of the natural morphisms

is identically zero. Here $dj$ is the morphism guaranteed to exist by \cite[Theorem 1.10]{KS21}.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def_kregular}
Let
\(
(X,x)\subseteq (\mathbb C^N,x)
\)
be an irreducible analytic germ, and let \(0\leq k\leq\operatorname{dim} X\).
We say that \(X\) is {\bf \(k\)-regular at \(x\)} if there exists an
open neighbourhood \(U\subseteq\mathbb C^N\) of \(x\) such that the natural restriction map
\[
H^0(U,\Omega_U^k)
\longrightarrow
H^0\bigl(X_U^\circ,\Omega_{X_U^\circ}^k\bigr)
\]
is surjective, where
\(
X_U\coloneqq X\cap U\)
and
\(
X_U^\circ\coloneqq X_U\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(X_U).
\)
<!-- end definition-1 -->

\label{r_kregular}
Using the same notations as in Definition \ref{def_kregular}, note that \(0\)-regularity means that every
holomorphic function on \(X_U^\circ\) extends to a holomorphic function
on \(U\). In particular, a normal analytic germ is \(0\)-regular.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop_canonical_comparison}
Let \(X\) be a normal analytic variety, let \(Y\) be a smooth analytic
curve, and let
\(
f\colon X\longrightarrow Y
\)
be a dominant holomorphic morphism inducing a co-rank one foliation
\(\mathcal F\) on \(X\). 

Then, 
\[
K_{\mathcal F}
\sim
K_X-f^*K_Y-R(f).
\]
Moreover, let \(\Sigma_Y\subset Y\) be a reduced divisor such that
\(
\operatorname{Supp} R(f)\subseteq f^{-1}(\Sigma_Y)
\),
and set
\(
\Sigma_X:=\bigl(f^{-1}(\Sigma_Y)\bigr)_{\mathrm{red}}
\).

Then
\(
R(f)=f^*\Sigma_Y-\Sigma_X
\),
and therefore
\[
K_{\mathcal F}
\sim
K_X+\Sigma_X-f^*(K_Y+\Sigma_Y).
\]
In particular, these conclusions hold if
\(
f^{-1}(Y\setminus\Sigma_Y)\longrightarrow Y\setminus\Sigma_Y
\)
is smooth.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since the statement is determined in codimension one, we may compute
at the generic point of a vertical  prime divisor \(P\subset X\). If \(t\) is a
local parameter at \(f(P)\), then
\[
f^*t=u x^{m_P}
\]
at the generic point of \(P\), where \(u\) is a unit, \(x\) is a
local parameter for \(P\) and $m_P\ge 1$. Thus, the image of
\(
f^*\Omega_Y^1\longrightarrow\Omega_X^{[1]}
\)
vanishes to order \(m_P-1\) along \(P\). Taking reflexive determinants
gives
\[
K_{\mathcal F}
\sim
K_X-f^*K_Y-R(f).
\]

Now suppose that
\(
\operatorname{Supp} R(f)\subseteq f^{-1}(\Sigma_Y)
\).
If \(P\) lies over a point of \(\Sigma_Y\), then its coefficient in
\(f^*\Sigma_Y-\Sigma_X\) is \(m_P-1\). If \(P\) does not lie over
\(\Sigma_Y\), then \(m_P=1\). Thus,
\[
R(f)=f^*\Sigma_Y-\Sigma_X,
\]
as claimed. 

Finally, smoothness over \(Y\setminus\Sigma_Y\) implies that
\(m_P=1\) for every vertical prime divisor \(P\) lying over
\(Y\setminus\Sigma_Y\), and hence our claim follows.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{l_lc_comparison}
Let \(X\) be a normal analytic variety, let \(Y\) be a smooth analytic
curve, and let
\(
f\colon X\to Y
\)
be a dominant holomorphic morphism inducing a co-rank one foliation
\(\mathcal F\) on \(X\). Let \(B\geq0\) be an \(\mathbb R\)-divisor,
let \(y\in Y\), and set
\(
X_y:=\bigl(f^{-1}(y)\bigr)_{\mathrm{red}}.
\)
Let \(x\in X_y\). Assume that  \((X,X_y+B)\) is log canonical at \(x\). 

Then
\((\mathcal F,B)\) is log canonical at \(x\).
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We work in a neighbourhood of \(x\). In particular, after shrinking
this neighbourhood, every component of \(R(f)\) which meets it is
contained in \(X_y\). By Proposition~\ref{prop_canonical_comparison},
\[
K_{\mathcal F}+B
\sim_{\mathbb R}
K_X+X_y+B-f^*(K_Y+\{y\}).
\]
In particular, \(K_{\mathcal F}+B\) is \(\mathbb R\)-Cartier near \(x\).

 Let
\(
\mu\colon X'\to X
\)
be a birational morphism 
and let \(E\) be a prime divisor on \(X'\) such that \(\mu(E)\) contains \(x\).
Set \(
f':=f\circ\mu
\),
let \(\mathcal F'\) be the foliation induced on \(X'\), and set
\(
X'_y:=\bigl((f')^{-1}(y)\bigr)_{\mathrm{red}}
\).
Proposition~\ref{prop_canonical_comparison} easily implies that 
\[
a(E,\mathcal F,B)
=
a(E,X,X_y+B)+1-\epsilon(E).
\]
Since \((X,X_y+B)\) is log canonical at \(x\), it follows that 
\[
a(E,\mathcal F,B)
\geq
-1+1-\epsilon(E)
=
-\epsilon(E).
\]
Thus \((\mathcal F,B)\) is log canonical at \(x\).

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop_lc_fibre_adjunction}

\]

\(
\mathcal F'
\) be the foliation induced on \(X'\)

\(
\Gamma:=p_*\widetilde\Delta'
\). Then \(
K_{\widetilde X'}\sim_{\mathbb Q}p^*K_{X'}
\),
and
\(
D'\sim_{\mathbb Q}p^*(K_{\mathcal F'}+\Gamma).
\)
Consequently, \(X'\) is klt and \((\mathcal F',\Gamma)\) is log
canonical. Moreover,
\(
\Gamma=\pi_*^{-1}\Delta'+E_{\mathrm{non\text{-}inv}},
\)
where \(E\) is the reduced \(\pi\)-exceptional divisor. This proves
Properties~{\rm (1)} and {\rm (2)}.

For every rational \(0<t<\tau\), the divisor
\(
K_{\mathcal F'}+\Gamma+tK_{X'}
\)
is \(\pi\)-ample, and, in particular, \(K_{\mathcal F'}+\Gamma\) is
\(\pi\)-nef. This proves Properties~{\rm (4)} and {\rm (5)}.

Since \(\psi\) consists of finitely many
\((K_{\overline{\mathcal F}}+\overline\Delta)\)-negative steps, we
may choose a rational number
\(
0<t_0<\tau
\)
such that every step of \(\psi\) remains
\(
(K_{\overline{\mathcal F}}+\overline\Delta+tK_{\overline X})
\)-

\((K_{\widetilde{\mathcal F}}+\widetilde\Delta)\)-trivial and
\(K_{\widetilde X}\)-negative, and hence are negative with respect to
\(

for every \(t>0\). On the other hand, \(p\) is crepant with respect
to both \(K_{\widetilde X'}\) and
\(K_{\widetilde{\mathcal F}'}+\widetilde\Delta'\), and is therefore
trivial with respect to their sum for every \(t>0\).

\((

\]

Property (6) is a direct consequence of (4) and the Negativity Lemma.

we have  obtained an open
neighbourhood \(U_x\) of \(x\), a projective birational morphism
\(

\textup{(1)--(6)} hold over \(U_x\) for every \(0<t<t_x\).

\(

Choose a rational number
\[

The uniqueness proved above gives a unique isomorphism

X'_{x_j}|_{V_{x_i}\cap V_{x_j}}

over \(V_{x_i}\cap V_{x_j}\).  Thus, the local models glue
to a normal analytic variety \(X'\) and a proper birational morphism
\(
\pi\colon X'\longrightarrow V,

\(V:=V_{x_1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{x_r}.
\)

It remains to verify that \(\pi\) is projective.  Fix a rational number
\(0<t<t_0\) and set
\[
M_{t}:=K_{\mathcal F'}+\Gamma+tK_{X'}.
\]
For each $i$, let \(\pi_{x_i}\colon X'_{x_i}\longrightarrow U_{x_i}\)

corresponding $\mathbb Q$-Cartier divisor on $X'_{x_i}$.

and $\pi_{x_i}$ is proper, the set
\(
\pi_{x_i}^{-1}\bigl(\overline{V_{x_i}}\bigr)

$q_iM_{t,i}$ is Cartier on a neighbourhood of this set.

$\mathcal O_{X'}(qM_t)$ is $\pi$-ample.

Properties \textup{(1), (2), (4)} and \textup{(6)} are local on the
base.  Properties \textup{(3)} and \textup{(5)} hold on every
\(V_{x_i}\) for all \(0<t<t_0\), by the choice of \(t_0\).
They therefore hold over \(V\).  Replacing \(X\) by \(V\), the theorem follows.

\label{t_cone}

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{p_bott_vanishing}
Let $X$ be a variety with rational singularities, let $\mathcal F$ be a quasi-smooth co-rank one foliation on $X$, 
and suppose that $\operatorname{Sing}(X, \mathcal F)$ has codimension at least three in $X$.
Let $C$ be a  curve tangent to $\mathcal F$.  

Then $c_1(N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F})\cdot C = 0$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $U = X \setminus \operatorname{Sing}(X, \mathcal F)$.  Recall that $c_1(N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}|_U)$ lies in the image of 
\[
H^1(U, N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}|_U) \to H^1(U, \Omega^1_U),
\]
(cf. \cite[Lemma 2.2]{DPPT26}).
Since $X$ has rational singularities and $N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}$ is locally free, it is also $S_k$ for all $k \ge 0$, hence Lemma \ref{lem_coho} implies that 
the natural morphism
\begin{align}
\label{iso1}
H^1(X, N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}) \to H^1(U, N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}|_U)
\end{align}
is an isomorphism and
as $\Omega^{[1]}_X$ is $S_2$, Lemma \ref{lem_coho} implies that the natural morphism 
\begin{align}\label{inj}
H^1(X, \Omega^{[1]}_X) \to H^1(U, \Omega^1_U)
\end{align}
is injective.
From the isomorphism \eqref{iso1} and the injection \eqref{inj} we see that $c_1(N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F})$ lies in the image of the map $s\colon H^1(X, N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}) \to H^1(X, \Omega^{[1]}_X)$.

Let $\tilde C \to C$ be the normalisation and let $j\colon \tilde C \to X$ be the composed map.  Since $X$ has rational singularities we have a natural morphism $j^*\Omega^{[1]}_X \to \Omega^1_{\tilde C}$, \cite[Theorem 1.10]{KS21}  which induces a map
$r\colon H^1(X, \Omega^{[1]}_X) \to H^1(\tilde C, \Omega_{\tilde C}^1)$ and for any line bundle $L$ on $X$ we have that $c_1(j^*L)$ lies in the image of $r$.

Thus, $c_1(j^*N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F})$ is in the image of the morphism $r\circ s\colon H^1(X, N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}) \to H^1(\tilde C, \Omega_{\tilde C}^1)$.
Since $C$ is tangent to $\mathcal F$, by Remark \ref{rem_tang_zero}
 the morphism $j^*N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F} \to \Omega^1_{\tilde C}$ is the zero morphism, hence $c_1(j^*N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}) = 0$, as required.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{t_contraction}
Let $X$ be a $\mathbb Q$-factorial projective variety with rational singularities and let $\mathcal F$ be a quasi-smooth co-rank one foliation on $X$, 
and suppose that $\operatorname{Sing}(X, \mathcal F)$ has codimension at least three in $X$.

and $(X, B)$ is log canonical. Let $R$ be a $(K_{\mathcal F}+B)$-negative extremal ray.

Then there exists a contraction $c_R\colon X \rightarrow Y$ such that for all curves $\Sigma$ on $X$, we have that 
$c_R(\Sigma)$ is a point if and only if $[\Sigma] \in R$.
Moreover, if $c_R$ is a small contraction, then the $(K_{\mathcal F}+B)$-flip exists.

By Theorem \ref{t_cone} we have $R = \mathbb R_+[\xi]$ where $\xi$

Proposition \ref{p_bott_vanishing} 
guarantees that

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{t_malgrange}
Let $(X,x)$ be a germ of a normal variety and let $\mathcal F$ be a co-rank one foliation on $X$.
Suppose that 
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\operatorname{dim} \operatorname{Sing} X \leq \operatorname{dim} X -3$;
\item $\operatorname{dim} \operatorname{Sing} \mathcal F \leq \operatorname{dim} X - 3$;
\item $N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}$ is a line bundle; and 
\item $X$ has rational singularities.
\end{enumerate}



Then $\mathcal F$ admits a holomorphic first integral.  Moreover, there exist germs of analytic functions $f, g \in \mathcal O_{X, x}$ with $g(x) \neq 0$, such that $N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}$
is generated by $gdf$.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
This is essentially a rephrasing of \cite[Main Theorem]{CLN08}, with some minor modifications 
which we now explain.

Let \(n\coloneqq\operatorname{dim} X\). If \(n<3\), assumptions (1) and (2) imply that
\(X\) and \(\mathcal F\) are nonsingular near \(x\), and the claim
follows from Frobenius theorem. We may therefore assume
that \(n\geq3\).

Let $X^\circ = X \setminus \operatorname{Sing} X$.
We recall that {\it ibid} implies the conclusion of the Theorem under the following four hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $H^1(X^\circ, \mathcal O_{X^\circ}) = 0$;
\item[(b)] $X$ is $k$-regular for $k = 0, 1$ (cf. Definition \ref{def_kregular}); 
\item[(c)] $\mathcal F$ is defined by a holomorphic germ of a 1-form $\omega$ such that on $X^\circ$ 
$\operatorname{dim} \operatorname{Sing} \omega \leq \operatorname{dim} X -3$;
\item[(d)] $\operatorname{dim} \operatorname{Sing} X \leq \operatorname{dim} X - 3$.
\end{enumerate}


Hypothesis (d) of  \cite[Main Theorem]{CLN08} is our Hypothesis (1) above. 
Hypothesis (c) is an easy consequence of Hypotheses (2) and (3).
Hypothesis (a) follows from Hypotheses (1) and (4) above and the fact 
that rational singularities are Cohen-Macaulay.


It remains to consider Hypothesis (b).  We observe that $0$-regularity of $X$ is 
implied by the fact that $X$ is normal (cf. Remark \ref{r_kregular}).  The role of $1$-regularity in the proof of \cite[Main Theorem]{CLN08}
is only needed to guarantee that if $\mu\colon \tilde X\to X$ is a resolution of singularities of $X$ and $\eta$
is a $1$-form on $X^\circ$, then $\mu^*\eta$ extends to a holomorphic $1$-form on $\tilde X$ and that $\mu^*\eta$ 
vanishes when restricted to any component of $\mu^{-1}(x)$.

We now verify that this property holds in our setting.
Let $E$ denote the normalisation of $\mu^{-1}(x)$ and consider the diagram 
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
E \arrow[r, "i"] \arrow[d, "m"]& \tilde X  \arrow[d, "\mu"]\\
\{x\} \arrow[r, "j"] & X.
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

If $\eta$ is a $1$-form on $X^\circ$, then $\eta$ naturally gives a reflexive $1$-form on all of $X$.  
By \cite[Theorem 1.10]{KS21} we have a morphism $d_{refl}\mu\colon \mu^*\Omega^{[1]}_X \to \Omega^1_{\tilde X}$ and the 
image of $\eta$ under this morphism gives our required extension of $\mu^*\eta$ to a holomorphic $1$-form on all of $\tilde X$ (which we will continue to denote by $\mu^*\eta$).
To verify that $\mu^*\eta$ vanishes on each component of $E$ it suffices to show that $d_{refl} i\circ d_{refl}\mu$ is the zero morphism.  We observe that
by \cite[Theorem 1.10]{KS21} we have that $d_{refl} i\circ d_{refl}\mu = d_{refl}m \circ d_{refl}j$ and since $\{x\}$ is a point, $d_{refl}j$ is the zero morphism and we can conclude.
 

With these alterations, the proof of \cite[Main Theorem]{CLN08} goes through to prove our claim.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem_quasi_smooth_preserved}
Let $X$ be a normal projective variety and let $\mathcal F$ be a co-rank one foliation on $X$.  Suppose that 
\begin{itemize}
\item $X$ is $\mathbb Q$-factorial; 
\item $X$ has terminal singularities; 
\item $\mathcal F$ is quasi-smooth; and
\item $\mathcal F$ has canonical singularities.
\end{itemize}  
Let $f\colon X \dashrightarrow X'$ be a step of a $K_{\mathcal F}$-MMP and let $\mathcal F'$ be the foliation induced on $X'$. 

Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X'$ is $\mathbb Q$-factorial;
\item $X'$ has terminal singularities;
\item $\mathcal F'$ is quasi-smooth; and 
\item $\mathcal F'$ has canonical singularities. 
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Item (4) is an immediate consequence of the negativity lemma.  By the proof of Theorem \ref{t_contraction} we know that $f$ is also a step in a $K_{X}$-MMP, hence Items (1) and (2) likewise follow, cf. \cite{KM98}.  It remains to show that (3) holds.
We now show that the conditions of Theorem \ref{t_malgrange} are satisfied.

Since $X'$ has terminal singularities it follows that $\operatorname{dim} \operatorname{Sing} X' \leq \operatorname{dim} X' - 3$.
We next show that $\operatorname{dim} \operatorname{Sing} \mathcal F' \leq \operatorname{dim} X' - 3$. 
Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there exists a component $Z \subset \operatorname{Sing} \mathcal F'$ such that $Z$ is of codimension two in $X'$.
Since $\mathcal F$ is quasi-smooth this is only possible if $Z \subset \operatorname{Exc} f^{-1}$.  Since $\mathcal F$ has canonical singularities, the negativity lemma implies that $\mathcal F'$ has terminal singularities at the generic point of $Z$.  However, an easy calculation shows that if $Z \subset \operatorname{Sing} \mathcal F'$, then the blow-up of $X'$ along $Z$ extracts a divisor of discrepancy zero, a contradiction.

By Proposition \ref{p_bott_vanishing} we know that $N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}$ is $f$-trivial, and so the basepoint free theorem implies that $f_*N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}$ is a line bundle.  Note that $f_*N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F}$ is equal to $N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F'}$ away from a subset of codimension at least two, hence the two sheaves are equal, and so $N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F'}$ is locally free.

Finally, $X'$ has rational singularities because $X'$ has terminal singularities.

We now apply Theorem \ref{t_malgrange} to see that at every point $x \in X'$, the foliation  $\mathcal F'$ admits a local first integral and $N^{[*]}_{\mathcal F'}$ is generated by a $1$-form $df$.  
To see that $\mathcal F'$ is quasi-smooth it then suffices to note that $\operatorname{dim} \operatorname{Sing} (X', \mathcal F') \leq \operatorname{dim} X' - 3 = \operatorname{rk} \mathcal F' - 2$.

By Theorem \ref{t_contraction}, all the required divisorial
contractions and flips exist, and are also steps of a $K_X$-MMP.
By Lemma \ref{lem_quasi_smooth_preserved}, the hypotheses on $X$
and $\mathcal F$ are preserved by these steps.

We first suppose that $K_{\mathcal F}$ is big.
Choose an effective $\mathbb Q$-divisor
$D\sim_{\mathbb Q}K_{\mathcal F}$ and a sufficiently small rational

Then $\epsilon D$ is big.

Run a $K_{\mathcal F}$-MMP, and denote its successive models by
$(X_i,\mathcal F_i)$ and the strict transform of $D$ by $D_i$.
Then $D_i\sim_{\mathbb Q}K_{\mathcal F_i}$. Proposition \ref{p_bott_vanishing} 
implies that 
if $R_i$ is the ray contracted at the $i$-th step, then 
$R_i$ is 
\(
(K_{X_i}+\epsilon D_i)\)-negative.

$(K_X+\epsilon D)$-MMP.
Thus $(X_i,\epsilon D_i)$ remains klt, and
\cite[Theorem 1.1]{Hashizume26} implies termination.

$K_{\mathcal F}+A$ is nef, and run a $K_{\mathcal F}$-MMP with
scaling of $A$.

strict transform of $A$ on $X_i$.

\[
\tau:=\inf\{t\geq0\mid
K_{\mathcal F}+tA\text{ is pseudo-effective}\}>0.
\]

pullback of the nef divisor

divisor. Hence $K_{\mathcal F}+\lambda_iA$ is pseudo-effective,
which proves the claim.

Choose a rational number $0<\epsilon<\tau$ sufficiently small so 
that $(X,\epsilon A)$ is klt.
For every ray $R_i$ selected by the scaling program, we have that $R_i$ is 
\(
(K_{X_i}+\epsilon A_i)\)-negative. Thus, each step of this MMP is also  a step of the ordinary
$(K_X+\epsilon A)$-MMP.
Since its initial boundary $\epsilon A$ is big,
\cite[Theorem 1.1]{Hashizume26} again implies termination.
