In this algebraic argument, row and column permutations and diagonal rescalings by nonzero elements of \(F\) preserve the entry-product condition for \ensuremath{C} and its inverse, and preserve nonvanishing of all 3-minors. Normalize the first of the four selected rows to all ones. On column pair j, write the next selected row as \ensuremath{(r_{j},r_{j})}. Invertibility gives, for distinct rows \ensuremath{h,l},

\begin{equation}
\sum_m\frac{C_{hm}}{C_{\ell m}}=0\qquad(h\ne\ell),
\end{equation}

because \(T_{m\ell}=1/(6C_{\ell m})\). Applied in both directions to the first two selected rows, this says \(\sum_jr_j=0\) and \(\sum_jr_j^{-1}=0\). The three nonzero numbers \ensuremath{r_{j}} are consequently the roots of \(X^3-r_0r_1r_2\). Since characteristic is not three, they are distinct. Scale and order so \(r_j=1,\omega,\omega^2\), with \ensuremath{\omega} a primitive cube root of unity.

Write the other two selected rows on pair \(j\) as \((a_j,b_j)\) and \((s_ja_j,s_jb_j)\). Applying the same two row-ratio identities shows that, after scaling the fourth row, \(s_j=r_j\) or \(s_j=r_j^{-1}\). Indeed every permutation of the three cube roots is a scalar multiple of either of those two indexed triples.

Put \(A_j=a_j+b_j\). Orthogonality in the row-ratio sense between the first two rows and the next two gives

\begin{equation}
\sum_jA_j=0,\qquad\sum_j\frac{A_j}{r_j}=0,\qquad\sum_js_jA_j=0,\qquad\sum_j\frac{s_j}{r_j}A_j=0.
\end{equation}

For either choice of \ensuremath{s}, these include all three Fourier equations on three distinct cube roots, whose coefficient matrix is invertible. Therefore \(A_j=0\) and \(b_j=-a_j\).

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}p_j^+&=hr_j^2+\ell a_js_j^2,\\ p_j^-&=hr_j^2-\ell a_js_j^2.\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

for scalars \(h,\ell\). This follows by applying the same invertible three-point Fourier matrix separately to \(p_j^++p_j^-\) and \((p_j^+-p_j^-)/a_j\). \sum_j\frac{hr_j^2}{D_j}=0,\qquad\sum_j\frac{r_jhr_j^2}{D_j}=0.
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
a_j^2s_j=\kappa r_j
