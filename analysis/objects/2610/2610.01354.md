<!-- introtheorem-1: body_md -->
\label{t:intro1}
    Let $A$ be an abeloid variety over ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^+)}$.
    After a finite separable affinoid field extension,
    there exists an isomorphism (as sheaves on the analytic site)
    \begin{equation*}
        A\cong E/M,
    \end{equation*}
    where
    \[ 0 \longrightarrow T \longrightarrow E \longrightarrow B \longrightarrow 0\]
    is an extension of an abeloid variety $B$ with \textit{generically good reduction}
    by a split algebraic torus $T\cong \mathbb{G}_{m,{\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^+)}}^r$, and $\underline{\mathbb{Z}^r}\cong M\subseteq{} E$ is a full lattice. 
   
    The resulting uniformization cross is uniquely determined by $A$ and functorial in $A$. Its ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^\circ)}$-fiber is the classical uniformization cross of $A^\circ$, and $E\to A$ is the universal analytic covering space of $A$.
<!-- end introtheorem-1 -->
<!-- introtheorem-2: body_md -->
\label{t:intro2}
Let $V$ be a Henselian valuation ring with fraction field $F$, and suppose that the residue fields of $\operatorname{Spec}(V)$ at non-generic points are algebraically closed. Let $E_F\to \operatorname{Spec}(F)$ be a semi-abelian variety. After replacing $F$ by a finite separable field extension and $V$ by the unique valuation ring dominating it, $E_F$ extends to a semi-abelian scheme over $\operatorname{Spec}(V)$.
Once it does, $E_F$ admits a (lft-)Néron model over $\operatorname{Spec}(V)$ whose fiberwise identity component is a semi-abelian scheme.

<!-- introtheorem-3: body_md -->
\label{t:intro3}
    Let $A$ be an abeloid variety over ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^+)}$.
    After a finite separable affinoid field extension, $A$ 
    admits a marked Néron model $\mathcal{E}$ that fits into a short exact sequence
                                                                                                                                                                                                     
        \[\begin{tikzcd}
        	0 & \mathcal{T} & {\mathcal{E}} & \mathcal{B} & 0
        	\arrow[from=1-1, to=1-2]
        	\arrow[from=1-2, to=1-3]
        	\arrow[from=1-3, to=1-4]
	        \arrow[from=1-4, to=1-5]

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{e:affinoid_field}
Let us consider examples of higher rank valuation rings and affinoid fields.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.5em]
\item Let $x\in X$ be a point of an analytic adic space. The completed residue field $(\kappa(x),\kappa(x)^+)$ at $x$ is an affinoid field, and the height of $\kappa(x)^+$ is the codimension of $x$ plus $1$. If $Y\to X$ is a morphism of adic spaces, then
\begin{equation*}
    Y_x:=Y\times_X \operatorname{Spa}(\kappa(x),\kappa(x)^+)
\end{equation*}
defines an adic space over $ \operatorname{Spa}(\kappa(x),\kappa(x)^+)$ under mild conditions (e.g., when $Y\to X$ is locally of weakly topologically finite type). 

\item More explicitly, consider the adic closed unit disc
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{C}_p}:=\operatorname{Spa}(\mathbb{C}_p\langle \hspace{0.15mm}t\hspace{0.15mm}\rangle, \mathbb{C}_p^\circ\langle \hspace{0.15mm}t\hspace{0.15mm}\rangle).
\end{equation*}
The Gauss point $g\in \mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{C}_p}$ is given by the valuation $|\sum_n a_n t^n|_{g} := \max_n |a_n|_p$.  Let $x\in \mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{C}_p}$ be a height two point generalizing to $g$, i.e., a type $5$ point of $g$ (see, e.g., \cite[Lecture 11]{Conrad2015PerfectoidSeminar}). Then $x$ is given by a rank $2$ valuation on $K := \operatorname{Frac}(\mathbb{C}_p \langle \hspace{0.15mm}t\hspace{0.15mm} \rangle)^\wedge$, where we complete with respect to the Gauss norm. If $K^+$ denotes the associated valuation ring, then $(K, K^+)$ is an affinoid field whose adic spectrum has two points.

\item  Consider $K:=\mathbb{Q}_p((t))$ with the $t$-adic topology. We equip the residue field $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with the $p$-adic 
valuation.
This induces the rank $2$ valuation 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:valuation_example}\tag{$*$}
        K^\times \ni f=\sum_{j\geq j_0, \,f_{j_0}\neq 0} f_j t^j\longmapsto (\lvert f\rvert_t, \lvert f_{j_0}\rvert_p)\in \Gamma_{{\lvert \,\cdot\,\rvert_t}}\times \Gamma_{\lvert \,\cdot\,\rvert_p},
    \end{equation} 
     where the value group carries the lexicographic order. Its valuation ring is
     \begin{equation*}
         K^+=\mathbb{Z}_p + t\mathbb{Q}_p[[t]].
     \end{equation*}
     Moreover, the residue field is $k=\mathbb{Q}_p$, and $K^+/{K^{\circ\circ}}=\mathbb{Z}_p$. This gives another example of ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^+)}$ of rank 2. The valuation (\ref{eq:valuation_example}) is the closed point of ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^+)}$, and the generic point is given by the $t$-adic valuation. More generally, this construction can be carried out for every
     valued field
     in place of $\mathbb{Q}_p$.
    \end{enumerate}

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{t:classical_uniformization_analytic}
   Let $A$ be an abeloid variety over ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^\circ)}$. After a finite separable field extension, there exists an isomorphism $A\cong E/M$, where $0 \to T\to E\to B \to 0$ is an extension of an abeloid variety $B$ with good reduction by a split algebraic torus $T\cong (\mathbb{G}_{m,K}^{\mathrm{an}})^r$, and $\underline{\mathbb{Z}^r}\cong M\subseteq{} E$ is a closed subgroup.
   
   In other words, we have a diagram of short exact sequences of adic group spaces over ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^\circ)}$ which we call the \textit{uniformization cross}\footnote{In fond remembrance of Robert Coleman, who used this terminology in conversations with the second author.} of $A\cong E/M$:
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 
\[\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=0.9em,row sep=0.7em]
	&& 0 && \\
	&& M \\
	0 & T & E & B & 0. \\
	&& A \\
	&& 0
	\arrow[from=1-3, to=2-3]
	\arrow[from=2-3, to=3-3]
	\arrow[from=3-1, to=3-2]
	\arrow[from=3-2, to=3-3]
	\arrow[from=3-3, to=3-4]
	\arrow[from=3-3, to=4-3]
	\arrow[from=3-4, to=3-5]
	\arrow[from=4-3, to=5-3]
\end{tikzcd}\]
The horizontal extension is called the Raynaud extension of $A$.

Moreover, the Raynaud uniformization $E\to A$ is the universal (analytic) covering space of $A$ in the sense that $H^1(E_{\mathrm{an}}, \mathbb{Z})=0$ and that $E\to A$ is final among pointed morphisms $S\to A$ where $S$ is a connected adic space with $H^1(S_{\mathrm{an}}, \mathbb{Z})=0$. Every homomorphism between abeloid varieties that admit a uniformization cross uniquely lifts to their uniformization cross.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{l:valuative criterion}
    Let $f\colon X\to S$ be a quasi-separated and locally ${}^+\!$weakly topologically finite type morphism of
    adic spaces. Then $f$ is partially proper if and only if for every affinoid space $\operatorname{Spa}(R, R^+)\to S$, the natural map
    \[\mathrm{Mor}_S(\operatorname{Spa}(R, R^+),X)\longrightarrow \mathrm{Mor}_S(\operatorname{Spa}(R, R^\circ),X)\]
    is bijective.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{p:valuative_strong_criterion}
    Let $S\to {\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^+)}$ be an
    adic space and suppose that $X\to S$ is partially proper (separated, respectively). For every 
    adic space $T\to S$, the canonical and natural maps
    \begin{equation*}
        \mathrm{Mor}_S(T,X)\longrightarrow \mathrm{Mor}_{S^\circ}(T^\circ,X^\circ)\longrightarrow \mathrm{Mor}_S(T^\circ,X)
    \end{equation*}
    are bijective (injective, respectively).
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
It follows from the universal property of fiber products that the second map is bijective. Let us now consider the first map. By gluing, we may assume that $T$ is affinoid. The statement for the partially proper case now follows from \cref{eq:affinoid_circ,l:valuative criterion}.
    
    In the separated case, suppose that $f,g\colon T\to X$ are such that $f^\circ=g^\circ$. The equalizer $\mathrm{Eq}(f,g)\to T$ is a closed immersion, hence in particular partially proper. The partially proper case of this proposition implies that $(-)^\circ$ is fully faithful on the category of partially proper $T$-spaces. Since $(\mathrm{Eq}(f,g))^\circ\to T^\circ$ is an isomorphism, it follows that $\mathrm{Eq}(f,g)\to T$ is an isomorphism as well.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{l:circ_embedding}
    Let $X\to {\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^+)}$ be a morphism of
    adic spaces. Then $j\colon X^\circ \to X$ is a quasi-compact topological embedding, every element of $X$ is a specialization of an element of $X^\circ$, and $\mathcal{O}_X \to j_*\mathcal{O}_{X^\circ}$ is an isomorphism. Moreover,
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
    \item $j_*\colon \mathrm{Coh}(X^\circ)\to \mathrm{Coh}(X)$ is an equivalence with inverse $j^{-1}$.
    \item The square
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 
\[\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=scriptsize]
	{\mathrm{CohAlg}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}} & {\mathrm{CohAlg}(X^\circ)^{\mathrm{op}}} \\
	{\mathrm{Adic}_{X}^\mathrm{finite}} & {\mathrm{Adic}_{X^\circ}^\mathrm{finite}}
	\arrow["{j^{-1}}", from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow["{\underline{\operatorname{Spa}}_{X}}"', from=1-1, to=2-1]
	\arrow["{\underline{\operatorname{Spa}}_{X^\circ}}"', from=1-2, to=2-2]
	\arrow["{(-)^{\circ}}", from=2-1, to=2-2]
\end{tikzcd}\]
    commutes up to natural isomorphism. All four functors are equivalences.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The morphism $j$ is quasi-compact by base change. It is a topological embedding as it is the inclusion of the ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^\circ)}$-fiber. Since every morphism of analytic adic spaces maps rank one points to rank one points, $X^\circ$ contains all rank one points of $X$ (cf.\ \cite[Lem.\ 1.1.10] {Huber1996EtaleCohomology}). Therefore, every element of $X$ is a specialization of an element of $X^\circ$.
    
    (1) follows as in the proof of \cite[Lem.\ 3.1]{Mann2023NormalIrreducibleAdicSpaces}. Indeed, the isomorphism $\mathcal{O}_X \to j_*\mathcal{O}_{X^\circ}$ is immediate from \cref{eq:affinoid_circ}, since it suffices to consider sections of the sheaves on affinoid opens. Recall that the category of coherent sheaves on an affinoid adic space $\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^+)$ is equivalent to the category of finite $A$-modules, see \cite[Satz.\ 3.3.18]{Huber1993Bewertungsspektrum}. Hence the isomorphism $\mathcal{O}_X \cong j_*\mathcal{O}_{X^\circ}$ together with the fact that the preimage under $j$ of an affinoid open is affinoid implies that $j_*\colon \mathrm{Coh}(X^\circ)\to \mathrm{Coh}(X)$ is an equivalence. (2) is a consequence of (1), see \cite[Satz.\ 3.12.12]{Huber1993Bewertungsspektrum} for the vertical arrows.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{l:dense_cocont}
    Let $j\colon X\to X'$ be a quasi-compact injective morphism of
    adic spaces such that every point of $X'$ is a specialization of a point of $X$. Suppose that $X=\bigcup U_i$ is an open cover and set $U_i^\mathrm{sat}:=\mathrm{int}((X'\setminus X) \cup U_i)$. Then $X'=\bigcup U_i^\mathrm{sat}$ and $U_i^\mathrm{sat}\cap X = U_i$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Note that $j$ is a homeomorphism onto its image since it is an injective quasi-compact map between analytic adic spaces, see \cite[Lem.\ 1.3.15]{Huber1996EtaleCohomology}. One verifies that $U_i^\mathrm{sat}= X'\setminus\overline{X\setminus U_i}$ and $U_i^\mathrm{sat}\cap X = U_i$.
    
    Now, suppose toward a contradiction that there exists a point $x' \in X' \setminus \bigcup U_i^\mathrm{sat} = \bigcap \overline{X \setminus U_i}$. Let $V\subseteq X'$ be an affinoid neighborhood of $x'$. Then $\operatorname{Spa} (\kappa(x'), \kappa(x')^+)\xrightarrow{\varphi}V\to X'$ factors through $V$, as it is a homeomorphism onto the set of generalizations of $x'$. Therefore $\varphi^{-1}(V\cap X)$ is quasi-compact as $X\to X'$ is quasi-compact.
    It is also nonempty and thus contains a closed point $x$, which is a minimal generalization of $x'$ in $X$.
    
    Let $l$ be such that $x \in U_l$. As $j$ is spectral and quasi-compact, $j(X \setminus U_l) \subseteq X'$ is pro-constructible (cf.\ \cite[(1.1.13)]{Huber1996EtaleCohomology}). 
     Hence the closure of $X \setminus U_l$ in $X'$ consists of all specializations of points in $X \setminus U_l$ (see \IfNoValueTF{9}{\cite[Tag~\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0}{\texttt{0}}]{StacksProject}}
	{\IfNoValueTF{0}{\cite[Tags~\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0}{\texttt{0}}, \href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/9}{\texttt{9}}]{StacksProject}}
	{\cite[Tags~\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0}{\texttt{0}}, \href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/9}{\texttt{9}}, \href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0}{\texttt{0}}]{StacksProject}}}3or the spectral case; the general case follows from this). Therefore there exists $y \in X \setminus U_l$ such that $y \succ x'$. As generalizations are totally ordered and $x \in U_l$, it must be that $x \succ y$, contradicting the minimality of $x$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{r:cohomology_comparison}
    Let $j\colon X\subseteq X'$ be as in \cref{l:dense_cocont}. Then it follows from \cref{l:dense_cocont} that $j_*\colon \mathrm{Sh}(X_{\mathrm{an}})\to \mathrm{Sh}(X'_{\mathrm{an}})$ preserves epimorphisms.
    In particular, one obtains $H^i(X_{\mathrm{an}}, \mathcal{F})=H^i(X'_{\mathrm{an}}, j_*\mathcal{F})$ for $i\geq 1$ and abelian sheaves $\mathcal{F}$. When $\mathcal{F}$ is a sheaf of (non-commutative) groups, then one similarly verifies the equality for $i=1$.

        H^i(X^\circ_{\mathrm{an}}, G^\circ)=H^i(X_{\mathrm{an}}, G).
    \end{equation*}

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{l:circ_reflect_pp}
	Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a partially proper morphism of adic spaces over ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^+)}$. Then $f$ is finite (a closed immersion, proper, surjective, an epimorphism of sheaves on the analytic site, respectively) if and only if $f^\circ$ has the respective property.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The functor $(-)^\circ$ preserves these properties since it is a base change.  The converse statement for \textit{finite} and \textit{closed immersion} is a consequence of 
{\cref{p:valuative_strong_criterion,l:circ_embedding}}. For \textit{proper} we may assume that $Y$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated. Then \cite[Lem.\ 1.3.13]{Huber1996EtaleCohomology} says that since $f$ is partially proper, the closure $\overline{X^\circ}=X$ is quasi-compact. Hence $f$ is quasi-compact and therefore proper. \textit{Surjectivity} follows readily, as a partially proper map is specializing.
For the last property, suppose that $f^\circ$ is an epimorphism of sheaves on the analytic site, i.e., $f^\circ$ admits sections locally on $Y^\circ$. A cover of $Y^\circ$ can be extended to a cover of $Y$ in the sense of \cref{l:dense_cocont}. Then, \cref{p:valuative_strong_criterion} shows that the sections over ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^\circ)}$ lift to sections over ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^+)}$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{l:circ_reflects_tft_smooth}
    Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a morphism that is locally of ${}^+\!$weakly topologically finite type between
    adic spaces over ${\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^+)}$.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $f$ is locally of topologically finite type (flat, étale, smooth, respectively) if and only if $f^\circ$ has the respective property.
        \item If $X$ is separated, then $f$ is an open immersion if and only if $f^\circ$ is.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The morphism $f^\circ$ inherits these properties by base change. We show that $(-)^\circ$ reflects them as well.
    
    (1) For {\textit{flatness}}, assume that $\mathcal{O}_{Y,f(z)}\to \mathcal{O}_{X,z}$ is flat for all rank one points $z\in X$. Let $x\in X$ be arbitrary and let $x'$ be its rank one generalization. Then $f(x')$ is the rank one generalization of $f(x)$ and we obtain a commutative square
                                                                                                                                                                                                         
\[\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=small]
	{\mathcal{O}_{Y,\, f(x)}} & {\hphantom{.}\mathcal{O}_{X,\, x}\hphantom{.}} \\
	{\mathcal{O}_{Y, \,f(x')}} & {\hphantom{.}\mathcal{O}_{X,\, x'}.}
	\arrow[from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow[from=1-1, to=2-1]
	\arrow[from=1-2, to=2-2]
	\arrow[from=2-1, to=2-2]
\end{tikzcd}\]
    By \cite[Lem.\ 1.1.10(iii)]{Huber1996EtaleCohomology} the vertical
    maps are local and flat (and hence faithfully flat). The lower map is local and flat by assumption. The flatness of the upper map now follows from \IfNoValueTF{3}{\cite[Tag~\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0}{\texttt{0}}]{StacksProject}}
	{\IfNoValueTF{9}{\cite[Tags~\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0}{\texttt{0}}, \href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/3}{\texttt{3}}]{StacksProject}}

We now treat the case where $f^\circ$ is \textit{locally of topologically finite type}.
    By \cref{eq:affinoid_circ}, the claim boils down  to showing that if $f\colon X=\operatorname{Spa}(B, B^+)\to \operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)=Y$ is such that there exists a quotient map 
    \begin{equation*}
        (A, A^+[K^\circ])\langle X_1,\dots, X_n\rangle\to (B, B^+[K^\circ]),\ X_i\mapsto b_i\in B^+[K^\circ]
    \end{equation*}
    and a finite subset $E = \{r_1,\dots, r_m\} \subseteq B^+$ with $B^+ = \overline{A^+[B^{\circ\circ} \cup E]}^{\raisebox{-0.3ex}{\hspace{0.2mm}}}$, then $f$ is of topologically finite type.
    Since $K^+$ is a valuation ring, $K^\circ$ is the filtered union of subrings $K^+[e^{-1}]$ ranging over $e\in K^+\cap (K^\circ)^\times$. Hence $B^+[K^\circ]$ is the filtered union of such $B^+[e^{-1}]$. Therefore, we may choose $e_0\in K^+\cap (K^\circ)^\times$ such that $e_0 b_i \in B^+$ for all $i = 1,\dots, n$. The morphism above can then be factored at the level of topological rings as follows:
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             
\[\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.15em,row sep=tiny]
	{A\langle X_1,\dots, X_n\rangle} && {B.} \\
	& {A\langle X_1',\dots, X_n'\rangle}
	\arrow["{X_i\, \mapsto\,  b_i}", from=1-1, to=1-3]
	\arrow["{X_i\,\mapsto\, e_0^{-1} X_i'}"', from=1-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow["{X_i'\,\mapsto\, e_0 b_i}"', from=2-2, to=1-3]
\end{tikzcd}\]
    The left diagonal map is an isomorphism since $e_0\in (K^\circ)^\times$, so the right map is necessarily a quotient morphism of Huber rings. This implies that the induced map
    \begin{equation*}
        (A, A^+)\langle X_1,\dots, X_n, Y_1,\dots, Y_m\rangle\to (B, B^+),\ X_i \mapsto e_0 b_i,\ Y_j\mapsto r_j,
    \end{equation*}
    is a quotient map.
	
    With this, the \textit{étale} case is a consequence of \cite[Prop.\ 1.7.5]{Huber1996EtaleCohomology}, which implies that the property can be verified on rank 1 points if the morphism is locally of topologically finite type.
    
    For \textit{smoothness}, assume that $X^\circ \to Y^\circ$ is smooth. We may assume that $Y$ is affinoid. By \cite[Cor.\ 1.6.10]{Huber1996EtaleCohomology} there exists an open cover $X^\circ=\bigcup U_i$ and étale morphisms $g_i$ such that the following diagrams commute
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         
\[\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=small]
	{U_i} \\
	& {\mathbb{B}_{Y^\circ}^{n_i}} & {\mathbb{A}_{Y^\circ}^{n_i}.} \\
	{Y^\circ}
	\arrow["{g_i}"', from=1-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow[from=1-1, to=2-3]
	\arrow[from=1-1, to=3-1]
	\arrow["{\mathrm{can}}"{description}, from=2-2, to=2-3]
	\arrow["{\mathrm{can}}"', from=2-2, to=3-1]

\label{t:uniformization_analytic}

   After a finite separable affinoid field extension,
   there exists an isomorphism $A\cong E/M$, where $0 \to T\to E\to B \to 0$ is an extension of an abeloid variety $B$ with generically good reduction by a split algebraic torus $T\cong \mathbb{G}_{m,{\operatorname{Spa}(K, K^+)}}^r$, and $\underline{\mathbb{Z}^r}\cong M\subseteq{} E$ is a full lattice.

\[\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=0.9em,row sep=0.7em]
	&& 0 && \\
	&& M \\
	0 & T & E & B & 0. \\
	&& A \\
	&& 0
	\arrow[from=1-3, to=2-3]
	\arrow[from=2-3, to=3-3]
	\arrow[from=3-1, to=3-2]

	\arrow[from=3-4, to=3-5]

\end{tikzcd}\]

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Every {homomorphism}

For the first statement we may assume that $s$ is the closed point by considering the base change to $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec}(V),s})$. Since every connected component of $G_s$ is hit by a section of $\operatorname{Spec}(V)$, 
    $\pi_0(G_s)\cong G(V)/G^0(V)=E_F(F)/G^0(V)$.

\label{l:smooth_int_closed}

<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
(1): Since $\ensuremath{\mathrm{sp}}_{\mathcal{X}}$ is glued from open subspaces, it suffices to show that for every smooth connected affine admissible formal scheme $\operatorname{Spf}(A)\to {\operatorname{Spf}(K^+)}$, $A$ is integrally closed in $A[\varpi^{-1}]$, i.e., in $A([\varpi^{-1}])^\circ$.  Note that by \cite[Prop.\ 3.4.1]{Luetkebohmert2016RigidGeometryCurves} we have $A([\varpi^{-1}])^\circ=A\otimes_{K^+}K^\circ$, as the special fiber is smooth and hence reduced. Moreover, we have 
    \begin{equation*}
        K^{\circ\circ}(A\otimes_{K^+}K^\circ)=K^{\circ\circ}(K^\circ A)= K^{\circ\circ}A.
    \end{equation*}
    Therefore it suffices to show  that $A/K^{\circ\circ}A$ is integrally closed in $(A\otimes_{K^+}K^\circ)/K^{\circ\circ}(A\otimes_{K^+}K^\circ)$. Geometrically, this is the map
    \begin{equation*}
        \operatorname{Spf}(A)\times_{{\operatorname{Spf}(K^+)}} \operatorname{Spec}(k) \to \operatorname{Spf}(A)\times_{{\operatorname{Spf}(K^+)}} \operatorname{Spec}(K^+/{K^{\circ\circ}}),
    \end{equation*}
    i.e., the inclusion of the generic fiber of the reduction. Since the reduction is smooth, hence normal and flat, this shows the claim. Indeed, normality of $\operatorname{Spec}(A/K^{\circ\circ}A)$ implies that $A/K^{\circ\circ}A$ is integrally closed in its total ring of fractions $Q$. Flatness implies that $A\otimes_{K^+}k$ embeds into  $Q$. 

    (2): Since $\mathrm{sp}_\mathcal{X}\colon {\mathcal{X}\vphantom{X}_\eta^{\mathrm{ad}}}\to\mathcal{X}$ is continuous and surjective, the connectedness of $\mathcal{X}\vphantom{X}_\eta^{\mathrm{ad}}$ implies that of $\mathcal{X}$. Suppose that $\mathcal{X}$ is connected, and let ${e\in \Gamma(\mathcal{X}\vphantom{X}_\eta^{\mathrm{ad}}, \mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}\vphantom{X}_\eta^{\mathrm{ad}}})}$ be idempotent, i.e., a root of $T^2-T=0$. By (1), ${\Gamma(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})= \Gamma(\mathcal{X}\vphantom{X}_\eta^{\mathrm{ad}}, \mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}\vphantom{X}_\eta^{\mathrm{ad}}}^+)}$  is integrally closed in $\smash{\Gamma(\mathcal{X}\vphantom{X}_\eta^{\mathrm{ad}}, \mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}\vphantom{X}_\eta^{\mathrm{ad}}})}$. Hence $\smash{e\in \Gamma(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})}$ and therefore $e=0,1$ since $\smash{\mathcal{X}}$ is connected. This shows the first equivalence. For the second, note that by \cref{l:dense_cocont} $\mathcal{X}\vphantom{X}_\eta^{\mathrm{ad}}$ is connected if and only if $(\mathcal{X}\vphantom{X}_\eta^{\mathrm{ad}})^\circ$ is connected. Hence the statement follows from the previously shown equivalence in the special case $K^+=K^\circ$.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{r:marked_model_analytic}
    Observe the following implications in the above setting:
    \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=3em]
    	\item A (strong) marked Néron model is unique if it exists, and the universal property of $\mathcal{G}'$ equips it with a group structure induced by $G$.         \item The open subgroup of $G$ determined by its marked (strong marked, respectively) Néron model is the maximal open neighborhood of $e_G$ admitting a smooth formal fiberwise connected (smooth connected, respectively) model over ${\operatorname{Spf}(K^+)}$.
        \item If a strong marked N\'eron model $\mathcal{G}'$ of $G$ exists, then a marked N\'eron model exists and is given by the fiberwise identity component of $\mathcal{G}'$, i.e., the union of the fiberwise topological connected components of the unit sections. It defines an open quasi-compact subgroup, as this can be checked on the reduction where it is known by the theory of group schemes.
    \end{enumerate}
