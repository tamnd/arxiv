<!-- twr-1: body_md -->
\label{thmproj}
For every integer $k \geq 1$ there exists a constant $c_k>1$ such that for every $n \geq k+2$ there exists an $n$-dimensional real normed space $X$, which is $c_k$-shady in codimension $k$.
<!-- end twr-1 -->
<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lemfix}
Let $k\geq 1$ be an integer and let $X$ be an $n$-dimensional real normed space which is $c$-shady in codimension $k$ for some $c>1$. Then there exists $\delta>0$ such that for any linear operator $T: X \to X$ with $\dim \operatorname{Fix}(T) \geq n-k$ and $\|T-I\| \geq 1$, we  have $\|T\| \geq 1 + \delta$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Assume for contradiction that the conclusion is false. Then we can choose a sequence of linear operators $T_m: X \to X$ such that $n-k \leq \dim \operatorname{Fix}(T_m) \leq n-1$, $\|T_m - I\| \geq 1$ and $\|T_m\| \leq 1 + \frac{1}{m}$ for every $m \geq 1$. As $\operatorname{Fix}(T_m)$ is a non-zero subspace, we also have $\|T_m\| \geq 1$ for all $m$. Since $X$ is finite-dimensional, the set of linear operators $T: X \to X$ satisfying $\|T\| \leq 2$ is compact. Passing to a subsequence, we may therefore assume that $(T_m)$ converges in the operator norm to linear operator $T: X \to X$. As $\|T_m\|\to 1$, we have then $\|T\|=1$, and moreover $\|T-I\| \geq 1$. Since $\dim \operatorname{Fix}(T_m) \geq n-k$ we can choose $n-k$ unit vectors $u_1^{(m)}, \ldots u_{n-k}^{(m)} \in \operatorname{Fix}(T_m)$, which are pairwise orthogonal with respect to a fixed inner product on $X$. Passing to a further subsequence if necessary, we may assume that $u_j^{(m)} \to u_j$ for $j=1, \ldots, n-k$, where $u_1, \ldots, u_{n-k}$ are still linearly independent. Since $T_m(u_j^{(m)})=u_j^{(m)}$, we obtain
$$T(u_j) = \lim_{m \to \infty} T_m(u_j^{(m)}) = \lim_{m \to \infty} u_j^{(m)} = u_j,$$
so that $u_j \in \operatorname{Fix}(T)$ for $j=1, \ldots, n-k$. It follows that $\dim \operatorname{Fix}(T) \geq n-k$. Since $\|T\|=1$, we have $\|T^j\|\leq 1$ for every $j\geq 0$. Hence the sequence $(S_N)_{N=1}^{\infty}$ of Ces\`aro means 
$$S_N=\frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} T^k$$ 
satisfies $\|S_N\|\leq 1$ for every $N$. As $X$ is finite-dimensional, the mean ergodic theorem yields the convergence of $S_N$ in the operator norm to a linear projection $P:X \to \operatorname{Fix}(T)$. Moreover,  $\|P\|\leq 1$ and since every non-zero projection has norm at least $1$, we get $\|P\|=1$. If $\dim\operatorname{Fix}(T)<n$, then $P$ is a norm-one projection onto a subspace of codimension at most $k$, contradicting the fact that $X$ is $c$-shady in codimension $k$. Consequently, we must have $\dim \operatorname{Fix}(T)=n$ and thus $T=I$. This contradicts the inequality $\|T-I\| \geq 1$ and concludes the proof.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lemshady}
Let $k\geq 1$ be an integer and let $X$ be an $n$-dimensional real normed space, where $n \geq k+2$. Let $V_1, \ldots, V_m$ be finite-dimensional real normed spaces such that  every $i=1, \ldots, m$ we have $\dim V_i \geq k+2$ and the space $V_i$ is $c_i$-shady in codimension $k$ for some $c_i>1$. Assume that for every non-zero $x \in X$ there exists a $1$-complemented subspace $V \subseteq X$ containing $x$ and isometric to $V_i$ for some $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$. Then $X$ is $c$-shady in codimension $k$ for some constant $c>1$ depending only on the spaces $V_1,\ldots,V_m$.
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $P:X \to Y$ be a linear projection with $n-k \leq \dim Y \leq n-1$ and let $x \in \ker P$ be non-zero. Choose a subspace $V$ such that $x \in V$ and $V$ is isometric to $V_i$ for some $i \in \{1, \ldots, m\}$. Let $Q: X \to V$ be a projection of norm $1$. Let $U: V_i \to V$ be an isometry and define $T: V_i \to V_i$ as $T = U^{-1}QPU$. Then 
$$\|T\| \leq \|U^{-1}\| \cdot \|Q\| \cdot \|P \| \cdot \|U\| = \|P\|.$$
Moreover, 
$$(T-I)(U^{-1}(x))=U^{-1}QP(x) - U^{-1}(x) = -U^{-1}(x)$$
and hence $\|T-I\| \geq 1$. We now claim that $U^{-1}(Y \cap V) \subseteq \operatorname{Fix}(T)$. Indeed, let us take $v \in U^{-1}(Y \cap V)$. Then $U(v) \in Y \cap V$, so that $P(U(v)) = U(v)$ and $Q(U(v))=U(v)$. Therefore
$$T(v) = U^{-1}QPU(v)=U^{-1}Q(U(v))=U^{-1}(U(v))=v.$$
Hence, we conclude that 
$$\dim \operatorname{Fix}(T) \geq \dim Y \cap V \geq \dim Y + \dim V - \dim X \geq n-k + \dim V - n = \dim V - k.$$
Lemma~\ref{lemfix} applied to the space $V_i$ now yields
$$ \|T\| \geq 1+\delta_i,$$ 
where $\delta_i>0$ depends only on $V_i$. Consequently, 
$$\|P\|\geq \|T\|\geq 1+\delta_i.$$
It follows that $X$ is $c$-shady in codimension $k$ for $c=1+\min\{ \delta_1, \ldots, \delta_m\}>1$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
Let $V_1, \ldots, V_m$ be finite-dimensional real normed spaces and let
$$X = V_1 \oplus_{\infty} \ldots \oplus_{\infty} V_m.$$
Then for every non-zero $x \in X$ there exists a $1$-complemented subspace $V \subseteq X$ such that $x \in V$ and $V$ is isometric to $V_i$ for some $i \in \{1, \ldots, m\}$.
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For a fixed non-zero vector $x=(x_1, \ldots, x_m) \in X$ choose an index $i \in \{1, \ldots, m\}$ such that $\|x\| = \|x_i\|_{V_i}$. Let $f \in V_i^*$ be a linear functional satisfying $\|f\|=1$ and $f(x_i)=\|x_i\|_{V_i}$. For $j \in \{1, \ldots, m\}$, $j \neq i$ we define a mapping $U_j: V_i \to V_j$ as 
$$U_j(v) = \frac{f(v)}{\|x_i\|_{V_i}}x_j \qquad \text { for } v \in V_i.$$ Moreover, let also $U_i:V_i \to V_i$ be the identity on $V_i$. We further define a linear operator $T: V_i \to X$ as
$$T(v) = (U_1(v), \ldots, U_m(v)),$$
for $v \in V_i$. Since $\|x_j\|_{V_j} \leq \|x_i\|_{V_i}$ for any $j$, we have $\|T\| \leq 1$. The opposite inequality follows from the fact that $\|T(v)\| \geq \|U_i(v)\|=\|v\|$ for any $v \in V_i$. Thus, $T$ is an isometry and moreover $T(x_i)=x$. Hence, $V:=T(V_i) \subseteq X$ is a subspace isometric to $V_i$ and containing $x$. It remains to show that $V$ is $1$-complemented in $X$. Clearly, the canonical projection $Q: X \to V_i$ is of norm $1$. Moreover, for $v \in V_i$ we have $QT(v)=U_i(v)=v$. In particular, if we now define $P=TQ: X \to V$, then $P$ is a projection onto $V$, since $P(T(v))=TQT(v)=T(v)$ for $v \in V_i$. Furthermore, we have $\|P\| \leq \|T\| \cdot \|Q\| = 1$, so $\|P\|=1$ and the conclusion follows.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For every $i \in \{k+2, k+3\}$, there exists a space $V_i$ which is $c_i$-shady in codimension $k$ for some $c_i > 1$. Since the numbers $k+2$ and $k+3$ are relatively prime, the Frobenius coin theorem implies that every integer $n \geq (k+1)(k+2)$ can be written in the form $a(k+2) + b(k+3)$ for some integers $a, b \geq 0$. For such $n$, let us take 
$$X= \underbrace{V_{k+2}\oplus_\infty\cdots\oplus_\infty V_{k+2}}_{a\text{ times}} \oplus_\infty \underbrace{V_{k+3}\oplus_\infty\cdots\oplus_\infty V_{k+3}}_{b\text{ times}}.$$ 
By the previous lemma and Lemma~\ref{lemshady}, the space $X$ is $c$-shady in codimension $k$ for some constant $c>1$ depending only on $V_{k+2}$ and $V_{k+3}$. On the other hand, for every integer $ k+2\leq n \leq (k+1)(k+2)-1$ there exists an $n$-dimensional real normed space which is $c(n)$-shady in codimension $k$ for some $c(n)>1$. Thus, for every $n \geq k+2$ there exists a $C$-shady space in codimension $k$, where 
$$C = \min \{c, c(k+2), \ldots, c((k+1)(k+2)-1)\}>1.$$
<!-- end proof-4 -->
