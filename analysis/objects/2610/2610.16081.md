<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For every $\alpha>0$, there exists $P_\alpha<\infty$ such that, for every $P\in{\mathbb R}^3$ with $|P|\ge P_\alpha$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main_goal}
 E(P,\alpha)=E(0,\alpha)+1.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\nonumber
 \operatorname{Ker}\bigl(H(P,\alpha)-E(0,\alpha)-1\bigr)=\{0\}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $H(P,\alpha)$ has no ground state at sufficiently large total momentum, and the interval $\mathcal I_\alpha$ is bounded.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Our proof is essentially dimension-independent, and only requires integrability of $|k|^{-2}$ around $0$. We are confident that an extension of our approach to $d >3$ is possible in case one considers the coupling function (up to a constant chosen so that the Fourier transform of its square evaluates to $\alpha/|x|$)
    $$g(k) = \sqrt{\alpha }|k|^{-(d-1)/2};$$
    see also \cite{PeetersWuDevreese1986,Moller}, where this generalization of the three-dimensional Fr\"ohlich model was introduced.
    We are also convinced that the critical threshold $P_\alpha$ is non-increasing in the dimension, which can be seen using path integral methods. Since the inputs \ref{I:hvz}-\ref{I:gross} are not all available in general dimensions, we postpone an extension to higher dimensions to future work.

In ~\cite{LampartMitrouskasMysliwy}, the operator $(P-P_{\mathrm f})^2$ is used, whereas \eqref{eq:intro-H} and \cite{DybalskiSpohn,Polzer2023} use $\frac12(P-P_{\mathrm f})^2$.  One can map one convention to the other by a scaling argument.

\label{lemma:pull}

 =-g(k)\bigl(H(q-k)+1-E(q)\bigr)^{-1}\psi_q

for almost every $k$ and every $|q|\le2r$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:riesz-local}
For all sufficiently large $\Lambda$ and every $|q|\le2r$, the cutoff Hamiltonian $H_\Lambda(q)$ has a simple ground-state eigenvalue $E_\Lambda(q)$ separated from the rest of the spectrum by at least $\gamma/2>0$.  Its rank-one projection $p_{\Lambda,q}$ can be chosen so that
\begin{equation}\nonumber
 \sup_{|q|\le2r}{\left\|p_{\Lambda,q}-p_q\right\|}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Moreover, writing $\psi_{\Lambda,q}=\frac{p_{\Lambda,q}\psi_q}{{\left\|p_{\Lambda,q}\psi_q\right\|}}$, one has
\begin{equation}\nonumber
 \delta_\Lambda = \sup_{|q|\le2r}{\left\|\psi_{\Lambda,q}-\psi_q\right\|}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The maps $q\mapsto p_{\Lambda,q}$ and $q\mapsto\psi_{\Lambda,q}$ are norm-continuous on $\overline{B_{2r}}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Recall that $\gamma$ was chosen as the uniform spectral gap of $H(q)$ for $|q| \le 2 r$. Fix $q \in B_{2r}(0)$. Let  $\Gamma_q \subset \rho(H(q))$ be the spherical contour with radius $\gamma3/4$ centred at $E(q)$. By definition of the resolvent we then have the simple bound
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:physical_countour}
        \sup_{q \in B_{2r}} \sup_{z \in \Gamma_q} \Vert (H(q)  - z)^{-1} \Vert \le \frac 4 \gamma.
    \end{equation}
    By Proposition \ref{prop:uniform_resolvent_conv} we have for $z$ not in the spectrum of any $H(q)$ that
    $$\sup_{q \in B_{2r}} \Vert (H_\Lambda (q) - z)^{-1} - (H(q) - z)^{-1} \Vert \to 0.$$
    This  yields via the resolvent identity the uniform convergence on the compact set $\Gamma_q$. Indeed, choose $b>C_0 +1$, where $-C_0$ is the uniform lower bound for all $H_\Lambda (P)$ with $\Lambda$ sufficiently large and $P \in {\mathbb{R}}^3$ (cf. Proposition \ref{prop:form-comparison}). Then all $(H_\Lambda(P)+b)^{-1}$ are bounded operators with norm $\le 1$. Abbreviating $R_\Lambda(z,q) = (H_\Lambda(q)-z)^{-1}$ with $R(z,q) = (H(q)-z)^{-1}$ and $D_\Lambda(z,q) = (H_\Lambda (q)-z) (H_\Lambda(q)+b)^{-1}$  as well as $D(z,q) =(H(q)-z) (H(q)+b)^{-1}$, we have (for now)  the formal identity
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:invert_calc}
        R_\Lambda(z,q) - R(z,q)= D_\Lambda (z,q) ^{-1}\big (R_\Lambda(-b,q) - R(-b,q)\big)D(z,q)^{-1}.
    \end{equation}
    This is verified by calculating  $D_\Lambda(z,q) (R_\Lambda(z,q) - R(z,q))D(z,q) = R_\Lambda(-b,q)-R(-b,q)$ and then multiplying the inverses on both sides. We have that the resolvent at $-b$ converges uniformly as $\Lambda \to \infty$ in $|q|\le 2r$, so it remains to show that $D_\Lambda(z,q)$ is invertible with bounded inverse for $\Lambda$ large enough and $(z,q) \in \Gamma_q \times B_{2r}$. This is seen by writing 
    $$D_\Lambda(z,q) = D(z,q)\big[ I-(z+b)D(z,q)^{-1}\big(R_\Lambda(-b,q)- R(-b,q)\big)\big].$$
    The fact that $D(z,q)$ is invertible is seen by definition. Moreover, by the identity $D(z,q) = 1-(z+b)(H(q)+b)^{-1}$, we also find that $D(z,q)$ has uniformly bounded operator norm. 
    For the inverse we find the formula
    $$D(z,q)^{-1}=1+(z+b)(H(q)-z)^{-1}.$$
    by the contour bound \eqref{eq:physical_countour} found earlier, the above is uniformly bounded in our parameter range. Because $R_\Lambda(-b,q)- R(-b,q)$ converges uniformly in $q$ over compacts, we can bound 
    $$\sup\limits_{|q|\le 2r}\sup\limits_{z \in \Gamma_q}\Vert(z+b)D(z,q)^{-1}\big(R_\Lambda(-b,q)- R(-b,q)\big)\Vert \le \frac 1 2$$
    for $\Lambda$ large enough. The Neumann series then shows that $D_\Lambda (z,q)$ is invertible with uniformly bounded norm in the considered parameter range. This verifies \eqref{eq:invert_calc} 
    and so
    $$\sup_{q \in B_{2r}} \sup_{z \in \Gamma_q} \Vert (H_\Lambda (q) - z)^{-1} - (H(q) - z)^{-1} \Vert \to 0.$$
    The conclusion is that the Riesz projections are converging uniformly, meaning
    $$\sup_{q \in B_{2r}}\Vert p_{\Lambda,q} - p_q \Vert \to 0,$$
    which yields the last two assertions.

\label{lem:AK}
The operator $A_K:\mathcal F\supset\mathcal D(A_K)\to L^2(K;\mathcal F)$ is closed and
\begin{equation}\nonumber
 {\left\|A_K\xi\right\|}_{L^2(K;\mathcal F)}^2=\Vert\mathcal N_K ^{1/2}\xi\Vert^2.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
As proven in Appendix \ref{app:basic_froehlich}, it holds that
$$a(k) H_\Lambda (q) = ( H_\Lambda (q-k) +1) a(k)+g_\Lambda (k).$$
on the operator core and for the respective ground states.
By \ref{I:gross} we have $H_\Lambda (q-k) \ge E_\Lambda (0)$. Because $E_\Lambda (q)$ converges uniformly over all $q$,
$$H_\Lambda (q-k)+1 - E_\Lambda(q) \ge E_\Lambda (0) +1 - E_\Lambda(q) \ge \frac 1 3$$
for $\Lambda$ sufficiently large because $|q|\le2r$. In particular, the operator on the left-hand side is invertible. The proof then follows via Lemma \ref{lem:finite_cutoff_commutator_gs} by plugging in the ground state and solving for $a(k) \psi_{\Lambda,q}$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:shifted}
Let $K_q\subset \overline{B_{2r}}$, $K_k \subset {\mathbb{R}}^3$ be compact. Then
\[
 \sup_{q\in K_q,\,k\in K_k}{\left\|(H_\Lambda(q-k)+1-E_\Lambda(q))^{-1}-(H(q-k)+1-E(q))^{-1}\right\|}\longrightarrow0.
\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
First, note that  $H_\Lambda(q-k)+1-E_\Lambda(q)$ has spectrum bounded below by $1/3$ by Lemma \ref{lem:cutoff-pull}. The same bound holds for $H(q-k)+1-E(q)$. Therefore, both inverses exist and are well-defined via the functional calculus. 
    The claim follows from the resolvent identity, \eqref{eq:uniform-energy-convergence}, Proposition \ref{prop:uniform_resolvent_conv} and the uniform boundedness of the inverses.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Thanks to Lemma \ref{lem:cutoff-pull} the pull-through formula is already verified for the model with cutoff. The convergence of $a(k) \psi_{\Lambda,q} \to a(k) \psi_q$ follows from Proposition \ref{lem:riesz-local} and Proposition \ref{lem:AK}. We show that the left and right sides both converge as functions in $L^2(K;{\mathcal{F}})$, for arbitrary $K \subseteq {\mathbb{R}}^3$ compact. Indeed, we first pass to the limit on the right hand side, and then use closeness to obtain the desired convergence. Because
$$\bigl(H_\Lambda(q-k)+1-E_\Lambda(q)\bigr)^{-1} \to \bigl(H(q-k)+1-E(q)\bigr)^{-1}$$
 uniformly in operator norm, 
$$\Vert \bigl(H_\Lambda(q-k)+1-E_\Lambda(q)\bigr)^{-1} \psi_{\Lambda,q} - \bigl(H(q-k)+1-E(q)\bigr)^{-1} \psi_q \Vert \to 0.$$
Multiplying this by $g_\Lambda$, which is just $g$ on $K$ and integrating on $K$ shows
$$g_\Lambda(k)\bigl(H_\Lambda(q-k)+1-E_\Lambda(q)\bigr)^{-1} \psi_{\Lambda,q} \to g(k)\bigl(H(q-k)+1-E(q)\bigr)^{-1} \psi_q $$
in $L^2(K;{\mathcal{F}})$.
This implies the pull-through formula \eqref{eq:ptr} without cutoff thanks to the convergence of ground states and closeness of $A_K$ for almost every $k \in {\mathbb{R}}^3$.
\eqref{eq:ptrbound} is then a direct consequence of the pull-through formula by noting that the inverse operator on the right-hand side is uniformly bounded (by choice of $|q|\le 2r$). Finally, the convergence 
$$\sup_{|q|\le2r}{\left\|A_K(\psi_{\Lambda,q}-\psi_q)\right\|}_{L^2(K;\mathcal F)}\longrightarrow0$$
also follows from applying the pull-through formula to $A_K (\psi_{\Lambda,q}- \psi_q)$, which yields
\begin{equation}
    \nonumber
    \begin{split}
        &\sup\limits_{|q|\le 2r} \Vert A_K (\psi_{\Lambda,q}- \psi_q)\Vert_{L^2 (K; {\mathcal{F}})} ^2 = \sup\limits_{|q|\le 2r} \int_K {\mathrm {d}} k \, \big\Vert a(k) \psi_{\Lambda,q}- a(k) \psi_q \big\Vert^2   \\
        &\le \sup\limits_{|q|\le 2r} \int_K {\mathrm {d}} k \, g(k)^2 \big\Vert \bigl(H_\Lambda(q-k)+1-E_\Lambda(q)\bigr)^{-1} \psi_{\Lambda,q} - \bigl(H(q-k)+1-E(q)\bigr)^{-1} \psi_q \big\Vert^2. 
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side tends to $0$ thanks to Lemma \ref{lem:shifted} and the fact that 
$\int_K {\mathrm {d}} k \, g(k)^2 < \infty$
in three dimensions. This verifies \eqref{eq:graphconv} and thus finishes the proof.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:measurable}
Let $B$ be a standard Borel space and let $B \ni q\mapsto\eta_q\in\mathcal F$ be strongly measurable.  The Fock components may be represented by jointly measurable functions
\[
 (q,k_1,\ldots,k_n)\longmapsto\eta_q^{(n)}(k_1,\ldots,k_n).
\]
If, in addition, a locally square-integrable annihilation field is given as a strongly measurable map $(q,k)\mapsto F(q,k)\in\mathcal F$, then the component representatives may be chosen so that $F(q,k)=a(k)\eta_q$ almost everywhere on every bounded momentum set.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Fix $n \in \mathbb N$. Since ${\mathcal{F}}^{(n)}$ is separable, choose a dense, countable set $\{\nu_j:j \in \mathbb N\}$ and fix a measurable representative for each of them. Abbreviate a momentum vector by $\mathbf{x}=(x_1,...,x_n) \in \mathbb R ^{3n}$. Let now
    $$j_m (q) = \min \left\{ j : \Vert \nu_j - \eta_q ^{(n)} \Vert < 2^{-m} \right\}, \qquad s_m (q,\mathbf x) =\nu_{j_m(q)}(\mathbf x).$$
    Note that $s_m$ is measurable in the first variable by the assumption that $q \to \eta_q$ is strongly measurable. Clearly $s_m(q,\cdot)$ is also measurable. Joint measurability follows, as $j_m \in \mathbb N$. For fixed $q$,
    $$\sum\limits_{m=1}^\infty \Vert s_{m+1}(q,\cdot) - s_m (q,\cdot) \Vert < \infty,$$
    meaning that $s_m (q,\cdot) \to \eta^{(n)}_q$ in $L^2({\mathbb R} ^{3n})$. By the triangle inequality and monotone convergence we find
    $$\sum\limits_{m=1}^\infty | s_{m+1}(q,x) - s_m (q,x) | < \infty$$
    for almost every $x$.
    Therefore, $v(q,x) =\lim\limits_{m\to \infty }s_m(q,x)$ exists pointwise for almost every $x$ (we set it to $0$ on a possible zero set). Note that almost everywhere $v(q,\cdot) = \eta_q ^{(n)}$; this is best seen by Fatou's Lemma, because
    $$\int {\mathrm {d}} x \, |v(q,x) - \eta^{(n)}_q (x)|^2 \le \liminf_{m \to \infty} \int {\mathrm {d}} x \, |s_m(q,x)  - \eta^{(n)}_q (x)|^2 = 0.$$
    Finally, $v$ is jointly measurable as limit of jointly measurable functions, as desired. The statement about annihilation fields follows directly from what we have just shown and Fubini's Theorem.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:creation}
If $D_\eta(f)+X_\eta(f)<\infty$, then \eqref{eq:component} defines a vector of $\mathcal F$ and
\begin{align}\label{eq:creationnorm}
 {\left\|C^\eta f\right\|}^2
 &=\int_{B_r} \,\mathrm d q \, |f(q)|^2{\left\|\eta_q\right\|}^2
 +\iint_{B_r\times B_r}\,\mathrm d q\,\,\mathrm d q' \, \overline{f(q)}f(q')
 \left\langle a(P-q')\eta_q,a(P-q)\eta_{q'}\right\rangle.
\end{align}
Moreover, in case the right-hand side is finite, we also have the difference bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:creationdiff}
 {\left\|(C^\eta-C^{\widetilde\eta})f\right\|}^2
 \le\left(
 \sup_{q\in B_r}{\left\|\eta_q-\widetilde\eta_q\right\|}^2
 +\sup_{q\in B_r}\int_{B_r}\,\mathrm d q'\,{\left\|a(P-q')(\eta_q-\widetilde\eta_q)\right\|}^2
 \right){\left\|f\right\|}^2.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Fix $f $ measurable with $D_\eta(f)+X_\eta(f)<\infty$. We start on the $n$-th Fock sector. Abbreviate $\mathbf k = (k_1,...,k_n) \in {\mathbb{R}}^{3n}$ and calculate
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:channel_inner_product_first_calculation}
        \begin{split}
            &\langle (C^\eta f)^{(n)}, (C^\eta f)^{(n)}\rangle \\
            &= \frac 1 n \int{\mathrm {d}} \mathbf{k}\, \sum\limits_{i,j=1}^n 1_{B_r(P)}(k_i)1_{B_r(P)}(k_j)\overline{f(P-k_i)\eta_{P-k_i}^{(n-1)}(\mathbf{k}\backslash k_i})f(P-k_j) \eta_{P-k_j}^{(n-1)}(\mathbf{k}\backslash k_j).
        \end{split}
    \end{equation}
    Here, $\mathbf{k}\backslash k_i = (k_1,\dots,\hat{k}_i,\dots,k_n)$, where a hat indicates an omitted index.
    Let us now separate two cases. If $i = j$, the terms become
    $$\int_{B_r} {\mathrm {d}} q \, |f(q)|^2 \big\Vert \eta_{q}^{(n-1)} \big\Vert ^2.$$
    Because there are $n$ of them, the prefactor $1/n$ in \eqref{eq:channel_inner_product_first_calculation} cancels, giving the first term in \eqref{eq:creationnorm}. The cross-terms evaluate after a change of variables $u_j = P-k_j$ to 
    $$(n-1)
    \iint_{B_r \times B_r} {\mathrm {d}} q {\mathrm {d}} q' \overline{f(q)} f(q') \langle \eta^{(n-1)}_{q}(P-q',\cdot), \eta^{(n-1)}_{q'} (P-q,\cdot) \rangle.$$ 
    But $\sqrt{n-1}\eta_q ^{(n-1)} (P-q',\cdot) = a(P-q')\eta_q ^{(n-1)}$.
    Summing over $n$ and exchanging summation and integration by dominated convergence (recall that we asked for $D_\eta (f) + X_\eta (f) < \infty$) yields \eqref{eq:creationnorm}. Next, note that $C^{\kappa}- C^{\theta} = C^{\kappa-\theta}$ for two families $\kappa,\theta$. We can therefore extract \eqref{eq:creationdiff} from \eqref{eq:creationnorm} by considering $\eta = \kappa - \theta$ and bounding both terms on the right side.
    For the first term, we bound
    $$\int_{B_r} \,\mathrm d q \, |f(q)|^2{\left\|\eta_q\right\|}^2 \le \sup_{q \in B_r} \Vert \eta_q\Vert^2 \Vert f \Vert^2.$$
    For the second term, use $xy \le \frac 12  (x^2+y^2)$ to bound
    \begin{equation}
        \nonumber
        \begin{split}
            \left| \overline{f(q)}f(q')
 \left\langle a(P-q')\eta_q,a(P-q)\eta_{q'}\right\rangle \right| &\le \big|\overline{f(q)}\big| \, \Vert a(P-q')\eta_{q} \Vert \, \big|f(q')\big| \, \Vert a(P-q)\eta_{q'} \Vert \\
 &\le \frac 1 2 \left( |f(q)|^2 \Vert a(P-q')\eta_{q} \Vert^2 + |f(q')|^2 \Vert a(P-q)\eta_{q'} \Vert^2  \right).
        \end{split}
    \end{equation}
Integrating this over $B_r \times B_r$ yields the bound
$$X_\eta (f)  \le \frac 1 2 \iint_{B_r \times B_r}{\mathrm {d}} q {\mathrm {d}} q' \,  |f(q)|^2 \Vert a(P-q')\eta_{q} \Vert^2 + |f(q')|^2 \Vert a(P-q)\eta_{q'} \Vert^2.$$
Noting that
$$\int_{B_r}{\mathrm {d}} q \, |f(q)|^2  \int_{B_r }{\mathrm {d}} q' \, \Vert a(P-q')\eta_{q} \Vert^2 \le \int_{B_r} {\mathrm {d}} q \, |f(q)|^2  \sup\limits_{q'' \in B_r}\int_{B_r }{\mathrm {d}} q' \,   \Vert a(P-q')\eta_{q''} \Vert^2$$
finishes the proof.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:cutoff-channel}
For every $f\in L^2(B_r)$,
\begin{equation}\nonumber
 \bigl(H_\Lambda(P)-E_\Lambda(0)-1\bigr)J_{\Lambda,P}f
 =J_{\Lambda,P}M_{h_\Lambda}f+S_{\Lambda,P}f
\end{equation}
with $h_\Lambda(q)=E_\Lambda(q)-E_\Lambda(0)$. In particular, $J_{\Lambda,P}f\in\mathcal D(H_\Lambda(P))$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let $\mathfrak C$ be the operator core from Lemma \ref{lem:finite_cutoff_core}. Let $\Xi \in \mathfrak C$. Moreover, take $f \in L^2(B_r)$. Then, by the component-wise definition of $J_{\Lambda,P}$ and bosonic symmetry,
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:simple_J_action}
        \langle \Xi, J_{\Lambda,P} f \rangle = \int_{B_r} {\mathrm {d}} q \, f(q) \langle a(P-q) \Xi, \psi_{\Lambda,q} \rangle.
    \end{equation}
    \eqref{eq:simple_J_action} is directly extended to $\Xi \in \mathcal D({\mathcal{N}}^{1/2})$ because $\mathfrak C$ is a core for ${\mathcal{N}}^{1/2}$ as well.
    Using now Lemma \ref{lem:finite_cutoff_commutator} , we find
    $$a(P-q)H_{\Lambda}(P) \xi = \left[ (H_\Lambda(q) +1)a(P-q) + g_\Lambda (P-q) \right] \xi.$$
    Inserting this into \eqref{eq:simple_J_action} with the choice $\Xi = H_\Lambda (P) \xi$ for $\xi \in \mathfrak C$ together with the self-adjointness of $H_{\Lambda}(q)$ and Lemma \ref{lem:finite_cutoff_commutator_gs} yields
    \begin{equation}
        \nonumber
        \begin{split}
            \langle H_\Lambda (P)\xi, J_{\Lambda,P} f \rangle &= \int_{B_r} {\mathrm {d}} q \, f(q) \langle a(P-q) \xi , (E_{\Lambda}(q)+1) \psi_{\Lambda,q} \rangle + \int_{B_r} {\mathrm {d}} q \, f(q) g_\Lambda(P-q)\langle \xi, \psi_{\Lambda,q}    \rangle \\
            &= \langle \xi, J_{\Lambda,P} M_{E_\Lambda(\cdot)+1} f \rangle + \langle \xi, S_{\Lambda,P}f \rangle. 
        \end{split}
    \end{equation}
    The second equality follows by the definition of $S_{\Lambda,P}f$ and once again applying \eqref{eq:simple_J_action}. Subtracting $E_\Lambda(0)+1$ from the Hamiltonian in the last display yields
    $$\langle [H_\Lambda (P) - E_\Lambda (0)-1] \xi , J_{\Lambda,P}f \rangle = \langle \xi , J_{\Lambda,P} M_{h_\Lambda}f + S_{\Lambda,P}f \rangle. $$

Next, \eqref{eq:creationdiff} implies together with Proposition \ref{lem:riesz-local} and \eqref{eq:graphconv} that $J_{\Lambda,P} \to J_P$ in operator norm. Now, because $ \sup\limits_{|q|\le 2r} |E_\Lambda (q) -E(q)| \to 0$ thanks to Proposition \ref{prop:form-comparison}, we have $\Vert h_\Lambda - h \Vert _\infty \to 0$.     $$
    \bigl(H_\Lambda(P)-E_\Lambda(0)-1\bigr)J_{\Lambda,P}f \to 
    \bigl(H(P)-E(0)-1\bigr)J_{P}f.
    $$

    But also
    $$\Vert (H_\Lambda(P) - E_{*}-i)^{-1} - (H(P) - E_{*}-i)^{-1}\Vert \to 0$$

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:projection}
There are constants $C<\infty$ and $P_0<\infty$ such that, for every
$P\in{\mathbb{R}}^3$ with $|P|\ge P_0$, one has $\frac12\le G_P\le\frac32$.
In particular, $G_P$ is invertible and ${\mathscr{C}}_P$ is closed.
Put $F_P=G_P^{-1/2}$ and $U_P=J_PF_P$.
Then $U_P:L^2(B_r)\to{\mathcal{F}}$ is an isometry onto ${\mathscr{C}}_P$, and the
orthogonal projections onto ${\mathscr{C}}_P$ and ${\mathscr{C}}_P^\perp$ are given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection-formula}
 \Pi_P=U_PU_P^*=J_PG_P^{-1}J_P^*,
 \qquad Q_P=1-\Pi_P.
\end{equation}
Moreover, ${\left\|F_P-1\right\|}+{\left\|\Pi_P-J_PJ_P^*\right\|}\le C\beta_P^2$ and,
for every $f\in L^2(B_r)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection-energy}
 (1-C\beta_P^2)\left\langle f,M_hf\right\rangle
 \le\left\langle F_Pf,M_hF_Pf\right\rangle
 \le(1+C\beta_P^2)\left\langle f,M_hf\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
Finally, $\Pi_P{\mathcal{F}}\subset\mathcal D(H(P))$, and both $\Pi_P$ and $Q_P$ leave the form domain $\mathcal Q$ invariant.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:projection-energy-error}
There is a constant $C<\infty$ such that, for all sufficiently large
$|P|$ and every $f\in L^2(B_r)$,
\begin{equation}
    {\left\|M_h^{1/2}R_Pf\right\|}
 +{\left\|M_h^{1/2}(F_P-1)f\right\|}
 \le C\beta_P^2{\left\|M_h^{1/2}f\right\|},\label{eq:projection-energy-norm}\\
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
     \big|\left\langle f,R_PM_hf\right\rangle\big|
 \le C\beta_P^2\left\langle f,M_hf\right\rangle.\nonumber
\end{equation}
The constant is independent of $P$ and $f$.

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Recall that $R_P$ is defined to be the operator with integral kernel
    $$k(q,q') = \left\langle a(P-q')\psi_q,a(P-q)\psi_{q'}\right\rangle.$$
    Therefore, for any $f \in L^2(B_r)$,
    $$|R_Pf(q)| = \left| \int_{B_r} {\mathrm {d}} x \, k(q,x) f(x) \right| \le 4 b_P(q) \int_{B_r}{\mathrm {d}} x \, b_P(x) |f(x)|.$$
    We now bound $\Vert M_h ^{1/2} R_Pf\Vert $. Writing out the inner product explicitly, we find
    \begin{equation}
        \nonumber
        \begin{split}
            \Vert M_h ^{1/2} R_Pf\Vert^2 = \int_{B_r} {\mathrm {d}} q \, h(q) |R_P f(q)|^2 &\le 16 \int {\mathrm {d}} q \,  h(q) b_P (q)^2  \left( \int {\mathrm {d}} q' \,  b_P (q') |f(q')| \right)^2 \\
            &\le 16\Vert M_h ^{1/2} f \Vert^2 \beta_P ^2  \int {\mathrm {d}} q \,  h(q) b_P (q)^2 .
        \end{split}
    \end{equation}
    Because $0<h(q) \le H_* = \Vert h \Vert_{L^\infty (B_r)}\le 1/2$ almost everywhere,
    $\int {\mathrm {d}} q \,  h(q) b_P (q)^2 \le H_* ^2 \beta_P ^2,$
    which implies with the previous bound and taking square roots on both sides that
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:first_term}
        \Vert M_h ^{1/2} R_Pf\Vert \le H_* \beta_P ^2 4 \Vert M_h ^{1/2} f \Vert.
    \end{equation}
    Next,
    $$\left\langle f,R_PM_hf\right\rangle = \langle M_h ^{1/2}R_P f,  M_h ^{1/2}f \rangle.$$
    The second claimed inequality therefore follows straight from \eqref{eq:first_term} and Cauchy-Schwarz. We continue with bounding the second term in \eqref{eq:projection-energy-norm}. Choose $P$ large enough so that $H_* \beta_P ^2 4 < \frac 1 8$. Then $F_P = (1+R_P)^{-1/2}$ is a well-defined, bounded operator. In particular,  $\Vert R_P \Vert < 1/8$ due to Lemma \ref{lem:projection-kernel}. This implies that the series expansion
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:F_P_series_expansion}
        F_P - 1 = \sum\limits_{n=1}^\infty c_n R_P ^n
    \end{equation}
    converges in operator norm; in the above $c_n = (-1)^n \binom{2n}{n}/4^n$ are the power series coefficients of $(1+z)^{-1/2}$. Iteratively applying \eqref{eq:first_term} with $R_P ^n f$ instead of just $f$ for any $n \in \mathbb N$ yields the bound
    $$\Vert M_h ^{1/2} R_P ^n f \Vert \le (4H_* \beta_P^2)^n \Vert M_h ^{1/2}f \Vert.$$
    Because $M_h ^{1/2}$ is a bounded operator, we may multiply it on both sides of \eqref{eq:F_P_series_expansion} and apply it to each summand. The resulting norm bound yields
    $$\Vert M_h ^{1/2}(F_P - 1)f \Vert \le \sum\limits_{n=1}^\infty |c_n| \, \Vert M_h^{1/2} R_P^nf\Vert \le \Vert M_h ^{1/2} f \Vert \sum\limits_{n=1}^\infty |c_n| (4H_* \beta_P ^2)^n =\Vert M_h ^{1/2} f \Vert ( (1- 4H_* \beta_P ^2)^{-1/2}-1).$$
    Using $(1-x)^{-1/2} - 1 \le 2x$ for $0 < x \le 1/2$, the final bound
    $$\Vert M_h ^{1/2}(F_P - 1)f \Vert \le 8H_* \beta_P ^2\Vert M_h ^{1/2} f \Vert$$
    follows, and the proof is complete.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Choose again $|P|$ large enough so that $4H_* \beta_P ^2 < 1/8$. Then, because
$$1-\Vert R_P \Vert  \le  G_P  \le 1+ \Vert R_P \Vert,$$
we find the claimed bound $1/2 \le G_P \le 3/2$. In particular, $F_P = G_P ^{-1/2}$ is well-defined, as before. This directly yields

$$\Pi_P ^2 = U_P U_P^* U_P U_P ^* = U_P U_P ^* = \Pi_P.$$
The fact that $\Pi_P$ is self-adjoint and bounded was already verified. Thus, $\Pi_P$ is the orthogonal projection onto ${\mathscr{C}}_P$. \label{prop:channel-estimates}
There are constants $C<\infty$ and $P_0<\infty$ such that, for every
$P\in{\mathbb{R}}^3$ with $|P|\ge P_0$ and every $f\in L^2(B_r)$,

     A_P[U_Pf]\ge(1-C\beta_P^2)\left\langle f,M_hf\right\rangle
\end{equation}

    \label{eq:channel-cross-bound}

\end{equation}

 -2C\beta_P{\left\|y\right\|}\left\langle f,M_hf\right\rangle^{1/2}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The comparison in \eqref{eq:projection-energy} is used to express the preceding estimates in terms of $\langle f,M_hf\rangle$. The proof below first gives these estimates with $\langle F_Pf,M_hF_Pf\rangle$ instead. This is already sufficient for the argument in Section \ref{sec:finale}. Indeed, the lower bound on ${\mathscr{C}}_P$ and the cross-term estimate only need to involve the same nonnegative quantity so that the cross-term error can be absorbed. We pass back to $f$ for aesthetic reasons and because it does not come with too much additional work.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
We will see that the key input in the preceding estimates is the finiteness of $\beta_P^2$. Indeed, it allows us to use the weighted Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
\[
    \int_{B_r}\mathrm dq \, |f(q)|b_P(q)
    \le \beta_P\langle f,M_hf\rangle^{1/2},
\]
thereby controlling the error terms relative to the low-energy action, which may be arbitrarily small. Since $h(q)$ is comparable to $|q|^2$ near zero and $b_P(q)\to b_P(0)>0$ as $q\to0$, the integrand has a singularity of order $|q|^{-2}$. This singularity is locally integrable precisely when $d>2$. Thus, in dimensions one and two, $\beta_P$ would be infinite and the present estimate would fail. In dimension three, we moreover have $\beta_P=O(|P|^{-1})$, which makes the resulting errors small enough to absorb for sufficiently large $|P|$.

\label{lem:channel-S-estimates}
For every $|P|>r$ and every $f\in L^2(B_r)$,

 {\left\|S_Pf\right\|}\le\beta_P\left\langle f,M_hf\right\rangle^{1/2}, \qquad \big|\left\langle f,J_P^*S_Pf\right\rangle\big|

There is $\Lambda_0<\infty$ such that, for every $\Lambda\ge\Lambda_0$, 
\begin{itemize}
    \item $e_\Lambda(0)=E_\Lambda(0),$
    \item $e_\Lambda(q)\ge e_\Lambda(0)$ for $q\in{\mathbb{R}}^3$,

 \ge\min\left\{\frac{|P|^2}{8},\frac{|P|}{4\Lambda}\right\}

\end{equation}

 H_\Lambda(P)\ge c_0K(P)-C_0
\end{equation}
for every $P$ and every sufficiently large $\Lambda$. The same estimate holds for $H(P)$. Furthermore, there are numbers $\varepsilon_\Lambda\to0$, with $0\le\varepsilon_\Lambda\le C_\alpha \Lambda^{-1/2}$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formcompare}
 |(H(P)-H_\Lambda(P))[\xi]|
 \le\varepsilon_\Lambda\bigl(H_\Lambda(P)[\xi]+b{\left\|\xi\right\|}^2\bigr)
\end{equation}
for every $\xi\in\mathcal Q$, uniformly in $P$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-sided}
 (1-\varepsilon_\Lambda)(H_\Lambda(P)+b)
 \le H(P)+b
 \le(1+\varepsilon_\Lambda)(H_\Lambda(P)+b).
\end{equation}
In particular, the cutoff fiber Hamiltonians converge in norm-resolvent sense uniformly in the fiber parameter $P$ to the physical fiber Hamiltonian and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-energy-convergence}
 \sup_{P\in{\mathbb R}^3}|E_\Lambda(P)-E(P)|\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
where the convergence rate is $O_\alpha(\Lambda^{-1/2})$.
<!-- end proposition-9 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
The form construction and the lower bound \eqref{eq:coercive} can be derived by applying \cite[Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3]{GW} in each fiber and using \eqref{eq:LY}. Taking $S\to\infty$ in \eqref{eq:LY} yields
    $$|(H_\Lambda(P) - H(P))[\xi]| \le \frac{C_\alpha}{\Lambda^{1/2}}\Vert (K(P)+1)^{1/2}\xi \Vert^2$$
    for any $\xi \in \mathcal Q$. By \eqref{eq:coercive}, $K(P)+1 \le C_1 (H_\Lambda(P)+b)$ uniformly in $P$ and large $\Lambda$, which shows \eqref{eq:formcompare}. To derive \eqref{eq:two-sided}, use $\eqref{eq:formcompare}$, which says that
    $$-\epsilon_\Lambda(H_\Lambda(P)+b) \le H(P)+b - (H_\Lambda(P)+b) \le \epsilon_\Lambda (H_\Lambda(P)+b).$$
    Re-arranging then yields \eqref{eq:two-sided}. Finally, taking infima in \eqref{eq:two-sided} gives the spectral inequalities
    $$(1-\epsilon_\Lambda)(E_\Lambda(P)+b) \le E(P)+b \le (1+\epsilon_\Lambda)(E_\Lambda(P)+b).$$
    Subtracting $E_\Lambda(P)+b$ in the last display yields $|E_\Lambda(P)-E(P)|\ \le \epsilon_\Lambda (E_\Lambda(P)+b)$. But the first inequality in the last display yields
    $$(1-\epsilon_\Lambda)(E_\Lambda(P)+b) \le E(P)+b \le E(0)+b+1.$$
    Thus $|E_\Lambda(P)-E(P)|\ \le \epsilon_\Lambda (E(0)+b+1)/(1-\epsilon_\Lambda),$ showing the claim.

<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{prop:uniform_resolvent_conv}
    Let $K \subset {\mathbb{R}}^3$ be compact. Then, for any $z \in \bigcap_{ P \in K} \rho(H(P))$ we have
    $$ \sup\limits_{P \in K} \Vert ( H_\Lambda(P) - z)^{-1} - (H(P) - z)^{-1} \Vert \to 0.$$
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
First choose $b$ as found in  Proposition \ref{prop:form-comparison}. The case of general $z \in \bigcap_{ P \in K} \rho(H(P))$ follows by the resolvent identity once it is verified that $\sup_{P \in K} \Vert (H(P) - z)^{-1}\Vert < \infty$. In order to see this, recall that the family $\{H(P): P \in K\}$ is norm resolvent continuous, which yields the uniform bound. Next, \eqref{eq:two-sided} implies
    $$ \frac{1}{(1+\epsilon_\Lambda)}  ( H_\Lambda(P)+b )^{-1} \le ( H(P)+b )^{-1} \le \frac{1}{(1-\epsilon_\Lambda)} ( H_\Lambda(P)+b )^{-1}.$$
    Since the spectrum of $H(P) + b$ is uniformly bounded from below by some $a >0$ for all $P \in K$, we conclude that
    $$ -\epsilon_\Lambda I \le ( H(P)+b )^{-1} - ( H_\Lambda(P)+b )^{-1} \le \epsilon_\Lambda I.$$
    Because the error is independent of $P$, uniform convergence follows, which was exactly the claim.

<!-- lemma-16: body_md -->
\label{lem:finite_cutoff_core}
For every $P\in\mathbb R^3$ and finite $\Lambda>0$, $H_\Lambda(P)$ is self-adjoint on $\mathcal D(K(P))$. The graph norms of $H_\Lambda(P)$ and $K(P)$ are equivalent, and $\mathfrak C$ is an operator core for both operators.
<!-- end lemma-16 -->
<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
As $g_\Lambda$ is an $L^2$ function, $\Phi(g_\Lambda)$ is infinitesimally operator bounded by $K(P)$. The self-adjointness and core assertions then follow by Kato-Rellich, as for example shown in \cite{Moller}; the core used there is contained in $\mathfrak C \subseteq \mathcal D(K(P))$. The same operator-bound gives equivalences of graph norms.

<!-- lemma-17: body_md -->
\label{lem:finite_cutoff_commutator}
For every $\Xi\in\mathfrak C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite_cutoff_commutator}
 a(k)H_\Lambda(P)\Xi
       =\bigl(H_\Lambda(P-k)+1\bigr)a(k)\Xi+g_\Lambda(k)\Xi
\end{equation}
as an identity in $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^3_k;\mathcal F)$, and hence for almost every $k$. In particular, $a(k)\Xi\in\mathfrak C$ for every $k$ when the smooth component representatives are used, and $H_\Lambda(P)\Xi\in\mathcal D(\mathcal N^{1/2})$.
<!-- end lemma-17 -->
<!-- proof-28: body_md -->
The component formula for $a(k)$ and standard commutation relations give for $\xi \in \mathfrak C$
    $$a(k)K(P) \xi = (K(P-k) +1)a(k) \xi, \qquad a(k) \Phi(g_\Lambda) \xi = \Phi(g_\Lambda) a(k) \xi + g_\Lambda (k) \xi.$$
    Adding both equations up yields the formula \ref{eq:finite_cutoff_commutator}. Smoothness and compact support yield $a(k) \xi \in \mathfrak C$. Moreover, $H_\Lambda(P)\xi$ has only finitely many non-zero components, so the vector is contained in $\mathcal D({\mathcal{N}}^{1/2})$. Finite particle number, compact support and $g_\Lambda \in L^2$ show that the claimed identity holds even in $L^2({\mathbb{R}}^3;{\mathcal{F}})$.
