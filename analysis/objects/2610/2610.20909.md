We start the argument with an important consequence of \eqref{Sv}. We fix $\zeta \in S$ and let $S_{N(\zeta)} V_\zeta^\perp \subset T_\zeta S$ be the image of of $V_\zeta^\perp$ under $S_{N(\zeta)}$; $S_{N(\zeta)} V_\zeta^\perp$ is itself a subspace. We will make use of two projectors: $\pi_{V_\zeta^\perp}: T_\zeta S \rightarrow V_\zeta^\perp$, the orthogonal projector onto $V_\zeta^\perp$, and  $\pi_\zeta: T_\zeta S \rightarrow S_{N(\zeta)} V_\zeta^\perp$, the orthogonal projection onto $S_{N(\zeta)} V_\zeta^\perp$.
 
 
We claim that there exists $c_1 > 0$ such that the following holds true:
\begin{equation} \label{wecon}
| \pi_{\zeta} e | \geq c_1 |e|
\end{equation} 
for any $e \in V_\zeta^\perp$. We argue by contradiction: assume \eqref{wecon} is false. This implies the existence of a unit vector $e \in V_\zeta^\perp$
such that $|e \cdot S_{N(\zeta)} v| < c_1 |S_{N(\zeta)} v| \leq c_1 \nu_0 |v|, \forall v \in V_\zeta^\perp$.  Using the symmetry of $S_{N(\zeta)}$ we obtain $| S_{N(\zeta)} e \cdot  v| <  c_1 \nu_0 |v|, \forall v \in V_\zeta^\perp$, which implies $|\pi_{V_\zeta^\perp} S_{N(\zeta)} e| < c_1 \nu_0$. Let $w = S_{N(\zeta)} e- \pi_{V_\zeta^\perp} S_{N(\zeta)} e$. This vector has the following properties:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $w \in V_\zeta$;
    
    \item Its size is bounded below by
    \[
    |w|^2= | S_{N(\zeta)} e|^2- |\pi_{V_\zeta^\perp} S_{N(\zeta)} e|^2 > \nu_0^2 - c_1^2 \nu_0^2,
    \]
    and above by
    \[
    |w|^2= | S_{N(\zeta)} e|^2- |\pi_{V_\zeta^\perp} S_{N(\zeta)} e|^2 \leq  | S_{N(\zeta)} e|^2 \leq \nu_0^2;
    \]

    \item It also satisfies
    \[
    | S_{N(\zeta)} e \wedge w|= |\pi_{V_\zeta^\perp} S_{N(\zeta)} e| \cdot |w| \leq c_1 \nu_0^2.  
    \]
\end{itemize}

Thus we obtain a contradiction with \eqref{Sv} provided that $c_1 \nu_0^2 <  \nu_0 ( \nu_0^2 - c_1^2 \nu_0^2)^\frac12$; obviously this is achievable if $c_1 >0$ is chosen small enough in \eqref{wecon}.

We make two simplifying assumptions: there exists $\zeta_0 \in S$ such that $N(\zeta_0) \in \mathcal H$ and that $\mathcal H^\perp \subset V_{\zeta_0}^\perp$. \end{equation}
If this were not the case, then, since $e_i \in \mathcal H^\perp$, $|\pi_0 e_i \cdot e_i| \ll 1$ and this would be in contradiction with \eqref{wecon}.

The first observation is that \eqref{aux30} follows from the more general estimate
\begin{equation} \label{aux30a}
\left( \sum_{x_0} \prod_{i=1}^k \| f_{i,Q_{R^{\frac12+\delta_0}}(x_0)} \|_{L^2(U_i)}^{\frac2{k-1}} \right)^{\frac{k-1}2} \lesssim \prod_{i=1}^k \| f_{i} \|_{L^2(U_i)},
\end{equation}
where $x_0$ is allowed to run over a larger set restricted only by $Q_{R^{\frac12+\delta_0}}(x_0) \subset Q_R$; of course we keep the condition that the set of parallelepipeds have the finite intersection property. The strategy here is standard, inspired by the work in \cite{BeCaTa}. We bound $\| f_{i,Q_{R^{\frac12+\delta_0}}(x_0)} \|_{L^2(U_i)}$ in terms of norms of wave packets and invoke the end-point multilinear Kakeya to obtain our result. The end-point multilinear Kakeya result we need comes from the work of Carbery and Valdimarsson \cite{CaVa}, which in turn is based on the work of \cite{Gu-main}, and this is because the work in \cite{CaVa} states the end-point estimate at all levels of multilinearity. 

There is only a small, insignificant technicality that we need to address. The above strategy works well if the wave packets are built on the scale of the uncertainty principle but this is not the case for the families of wave packets $\mathcal{T}_i$ which we used so far. This is why we modify the wave packet structure in each family $\mathcal{T}_i$ by simply replacing the spatial localization from $R^\frac{1+\delta}2$ to $C R^\frac12$ for some large $C$ (here we work on the family of wave packet adapted to a parallelepiped $Q_R$) - this is the more classical way to define wave packets; we call this new family $\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_i$. 

Below we use the $\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_i$ adapted to the $Q_{R^{1+2\delta_0}}$, which is the parallelepiped containing $Q_R$; the reason we do so is because the basic unit upon which we perform summation is of size $R^{\frac12+\delta_0}$. Let $c_{i,T}= \|f_{i,T}\|^2_{L^2}$. It is easy to see that 
\begin{equation} \label{aux30b}
\| f_{i,Q_{R^{\frac12+\delta_0}}(x_0)} \|_{L^2(U_i)} \lesssim \left( \sum_{T \in \tilde{\mathcal T}_i} 
\langle R^{-\frac12-\delta_0} d(T, Q_{R^{\frac12+\delta_0}}(x_0)) \rangle^{-N} c_{i,T} \right)^\frac12,
\end{equation}
 For now, let us ignore the tubes in each class which do not intersect $Q_{R^{\frac12+\delta_0}}(x_0)$, as they come with additional decay.  For the tubes which intersect $Q_{R^{\frac12+\delta_0}}(x_0)$, we have that
\[
\begin{split}
\prod_{i=1}^k \| f_{i,Q_{R^{\frac12+\delta_0}}(x_0)} \|^{\frac2{k-1}}_{L^2(U_i)}  \lesssim R^{-n(\frac12+\delta_0)} \int_{Q_{R^{\frac12+\delta_0}}(x_0)}
\left( \prod_{i=1}^k \sum_{T_i \in \tilde{\mathcal T}_i} c_{i,T_{i}} \chi_{T_i}(x) \right)^\frac1{k-1} dx 
\end{split}
\]
and as a consequence
\[
\begin{split}
\sum_{x_0} \prod_{i=1}^k \| f_{i,Q_{R^{\frac12+\delta_0}}(x_0)} \|^{\frac2{k-1}}_{L^2(U_i)} & \lesssim R^{-n(\frac12+\delta_0)} \int_{Q_{R}}
\left( \prod_{i=1}^k \sum_{T_i \in \tilde{\mathcal T}_i} c_{i,T_{i}} \chi_{T_i}(x) \right)^\frac1{k-1} dx \\
& \lesssim ( \prod_{i=1}^k \sum_{T_i \in \tilde{\mathcal T}_i} c_{i,T_{i}} )^{\frac1{k-1}},
\end{split}
\]
where in passing to the last inequality we have used \cite[Theorem 1]{CaVa} in a rescaled version: the tubes in \cite{CaVa} have diameter $1$, while here they have diameter $R^{\frac12+\delta_0}$. Since $\|f_i\|_{L^2} \approx (\sum_{T_i \in \tilde{\mathcal T}_i} c_{i,T_{i}} )^\frac12$, \eqref{aux30a} follows. 

For the tubes which do not intersect $Q_{R^{\frac12+\delta_0}}(x_0)$, we can use a similar argument combined with the rapid decay from \eqref{aux30b} to complete the proof of \eqref{aux30a}.

We first remark that is suffices to establish the (apparently) stronger estimate
\begin{equation} \label{aux111b}
\left( \sum_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \| \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|_{L^\frac{2}{n-2}(P \cap Q_R)}^{\frac2{n-1}} \right)^{\frac{n-1}2}  
\lesssim  R^{\frac{n-1}2}  C(\bm{\nu}) \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \| f_{i} \|_{L^2(U_i)}.
\end{equation}
We start with the generic estimate
\[
\| \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|_{L^\frac{2}{n-2}(P)} \lesssim  C(\bm{\nu}) \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \| f_{i} \|_{L^2(U_i)}.
\]
It is standard that this estimate localizes, in that it suffices to consider the relevant masses within the parallelepiped $P$; precisely we have
\begin{equation} \label{aux111a}
\| \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|_{L^\frac{2}{n-2}(P)} \lesssim  C(\bm{\nu}) \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \left(\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}_i} \langle z_i \rangle^{-N} \| \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|_{L^\infty_{x_i} L^2_{\bar x_i}(P+ \nu_i^{-1} z_i)} \right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}_i= \{ z \in \mathbb{Z}^n: z_i=0 \}$. For fixed $i$, we have the estimate
\[
\sum_{P \in \mathcal{P}: c_i(P)=constant} \left(\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}_i} \langle z_i \rangle^{-N} \| \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|_{L^\infty_{x_i} L^2_{\bar x_i}(P+ \nu_i^{-1} z_i)} \right)^2 \lesssim \| \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|_{L^\infty_{x_i} L^2_{\bar x_i}}^2 \lesssim \|f_{i}\|_{L^2},
\]
where we recall that $c(P)$ is the center of the parallelepiped $P$. The above is a consequence of the fact that the mass of $\mathcal E_i f_{i}$ within parallelepipeds $P$ with $c_i(P)=constant$ has good orthogonality properties, which in turn is a consequence of the dispersive properties of the operator $\mathcal E_i$. From this last estimate we obtain
\[
\begin{split}
& \sum_{P \in \mathcal{P}: P \subset Q_R} \left(\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}_i} \langle z_i \rangle^{-N} \| \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|_{L^\infty_{x_i} L^2_{\bar x_i}(P+ \nu_i^{-1} z_i)} \right)^2 \\
\lesssim & R^\frac12 \sum_{P \in \mathcal{P}: c_i(P)=constant} \left(\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}_i} \langle z_i \rangle^{-N} \| \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|_{L^\infty_{x_i} L^2_{\bar x_i}(P+ \nu_i^{-1} z_i)} \right)^2 \\
\lesssim &  R^\frac12 \|f_{i}\|_{L^2}. 
\end{split}
\]
From this and \eqref{aux111a} we obtain \eqref{aux111b}.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lsc}
Let $\mathcal{Q}\subseteq \mathcal{P}$ be a collection of parallelepipeds in $\mathcal{P}$ and let $N\geq 1$. Then there exists $O(N\#\mathcal{Q}^{\frac{1}{N}})$ many sparse collections $\mathcal{O}_l\subseteq \mathcal{P}_{R_l}$ which cover $\mathcal{Q}$ such that $1\lesssim R_l\lesssim \#\mathcal{Q}^{C^N}$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We prove a weak-type estimate using the same strategy from \cite{Be5}. First, we normalize $\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}}{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}*}}{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}}{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}*}*}{f_j}_{L^2(d\sigma_j)}=1$ for all $j$ and for any $\lambda>0$ we define:
\begin{align}
    E(\lambda)\vcentcolon= \{P\in\mathcal{P}\mid \|\prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal E_if_i\|_{L^{\frac{2}{k-1}}(P)}\geq\lambda\}
\end{align}
By the generic $k$-linear restriction estimate we have $\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}}{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}*}}{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}}{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}*}*}{\prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal E_if_i}_{L^{\frac{2}{k-1}}(P)}\lesssim C(\bm{\nu})$ and therefore we may assume that $\lambda\lesssim C(\bm{\nu})$.

We apply Lemma \ref{lsc} to $E(\lambda)$ to obtain sparse collections $\mathcal{O}_l=\{Q_j: Q_j \in \mathcal{P}_{R_l}\}_{j=1}^N$. We fix one such sparse collection 
$\mathcal{O}_l$ and seek to provide an estimate on it. For each $Q$ in this collection collection, we invoke the refined estimate 
\begin{equation} \label{aux111aa}
\| \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|_{L^\frac{2}{n-2}(Q)} \lesssim  C(\bm{\nu}) R_l^\epsilon \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \left(\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}_i} \langle z_i \rangle^{-\bar N} \| \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|_{L^\infty_{x_i} L^2_{\bar x_i}(Q+ \nu_i^{-1} R_l z_i)} \right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}_i= \{ z \in \mathbb{Z}^n: z_i=0 \}$; this is \eqref{aux111a} adapted to the larger parallelepiped $Q$. We also recall the convention that $x=(x_i,\bar x_i)$ where $x_i$ is the coordinate of $x$ in the $e_i$ direction, while $\bar x_i$ collects all the other coordinates. 

Next, we claim that, assuming $\bar N$ is large enough, for each $i$ the following holds true
\begin{equation} \label{ssp}
    \sum_{j=1}^N \sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}_i} \langle z_i \rangle^{-\bar N} \| \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|^2_{L^\infty_{x_i} L^2_{\bar x_i}(Q_j+ \nu_i^{-1} R_l z_i)}  \lesssim \|f_i\|^2_{L^2}.
\end{equation}
We postpone the proof for now and continue with our argument. Using \eqref{aux111aa}, Hölder's inequality, and  \eqref{ssp}, for any $p\geq\frac{2}{k}$ we have
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{j=1}^N \sum_{ j=1}^N \|\prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal E_if_i\|_{L^\frac{2}{k-1}( Q_j)}^p &\lesssim R_l^{\epsilon p} C(\bm{\nu})^p \sum_{j=1}^N \prod_{i=1}^k \left(\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}_i} \langle z_i \rangle^{-2 \bar N} \| \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|_{L^\infty_{x_i} L^2_{\bar x_i}( Q_j+ \nu_i^{-1} R_l z_i)} \right)^p\\
    &\leq R_l^{\epsilon p} C(\bm{\nu})^p \prod_{i=1}^k \left( \sum_{ j=1}^N \sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}_i} \langle z_i \rangle^{- \bar N} \| \mathcal E_i f_{i} \|^2_{L^\infty_{x_i} L^2_{\bar x_i}(Q_j+ \nu_i^{-1} R_l z_i)} \right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
    &\lesssim R_l^{\epsilon p} C(\bm{\nu})^p \prod_{i=1}^k \@ifstar{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}}{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}*}}{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}}{\@ifstar{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}}{\@ifstar{\norm}{\norm*}*}*}*}{f_i}_{L^2(d\sigma_j)}^p = R_l^{\epsilon p} C(\bm{\nu})^p.
\end{align*}
In other words, for sparse families the loss depends only on the side length of a single parallelepiped instead of the (much larger) side length of the parallelepiped containing the union.

We then combine these facts together to estimate $\#E(\lambda)$ as follows:
\begin{align*}
    \#E(\lambda) &\lesssim \lambda^{-p} \sum_{P\in E(\lambda)} \|\prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal E_if_i\|_{L^{\frac{2}{k-1}}(P)}^p
    \lesssim \lambda^{-p} \sum_l \sum_{\substack{P\in\mathcal{P}\\P\cap\cup_{\mathcal{O}_l}\neq\emptyset}} \|\prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal E_if_i\|_{L^{\frac{2}{k-1}}(P)}^p\\
    & \lesssim \lambda^{-p} \sum_l R_l^{\epsilon p} C(\bm{\nu})^p
     \lesssim \lambda^{-p} C(\bm{\nu})^p N \cdot \#E(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{N}} (\#E(\lambda)^{C^N})^{\epsilon p} \\
    & = \lambda^{-p} C(\bm{\nu})^p N \cdot \#E(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{N}+\epsilon p C^N},
\end{align*}
where we used the notation $\cup_{\mathcal{O}_l} = \cup_{Q \in  \mathcal{O}_l} Q$. Let $\gamma=\frac{1}{N}+\epsilon p C^N$ so that, after rearranging this inequality, we have
\begin{align*}
    \#E(\lambda)\lesssim C(\bm{\nu})^{\frac{p}{1-\gamma}} N^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}} \lambda^{-\frac{p}{1-\gamma}}.
\end{align*}
If $q>\frac{p}{1-\gamma}$ then we estimate
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\|\prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal E_if_i\|_{L^{\frac{2}{k-1}}(P)}^q \lesssim \sum_{\lambda\lesssim C(\bm{\nu})} \lambda^q \#E(\lambda)\lesssim C(\bm{\nu})^{\frac{p}{1-\gamma}} N^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}} \sum_{\lambda\lesssim C(\bm{\nu})} \lambda^{q-\frac{p}{1-\gamma}}\lesssim C(\bm{\nu})^q N^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}.
\end{align*}

By picking $N$ large enough and then $\epsilon$ small enough, we can run this argument for any $q>p$ which concludes the proof; in particular, tracking the parameters carefully shows that this can be done as long as $q\geq p+(p+n+1)\frac{C}{\log(\frac{1}{\epsilon})}$, see \cite{Be5} for details.
