<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:aap}Let $Q\subset N\subset M$ be inclusions of tracial
von Neumann algebras, with $M$ having separable predual. The following
conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every finite subset $F\subset N$, every $z\in M$, and every
$\eta,\epsilon>0$, there are a trace-preserving automorphism $\alpha$
of $M$ fixing $Q$ pointwise and $y\in N$ such that 
\[
\max_{a\in F}\|\alpha(a)-a\|_{2}<\eta,\qquad\|z-\alpha(y)\|_{2}<\epsilon.
\]
\item For every finite subset $F\subset N$ and every $\epsilon>0$, there
is a surjective trace-preserving isomorphism $\Phi:N\to M$ fixing
$Q$ pointwise such that 
\[
\max_{a\in F}\|\Phi(a)-a\|_{2}<\epsilon.
\]
\item There is a normal trace-preserving endomorphism $\rho:M\to M$ with
$\rho(M)=N$ which is a pointwise $L^{2}$ limit of a sequence of
trace-preserving automorphisms of $M$ fixing $Q$ pointwise.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, fix a unital von Neumann subalgebra $E\subset N$. Conditions
(1) and (2) remain equivalent if, for every additional tolerance $\kappa>0$,
the map $\alpha$, respectively $\Phi$, is required to satisfy
\[
\alpha(e)=UeU^{*},\quad\text{respectively }\Phi(e)=UeU^{*}\quad(e\in E),\qquad\|U-1\|<\kappa,
\]
for some unitary $U\in M$. The bound on $U-1$ is in operator norm.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Write $G=\operatorname{Aut}_{\tau,Q}(M)$ for the group of trace-preserving
automorphisms fixing $Q$ pointwise. We prove (1)$\Rightarrow$(2),
including the additional assertion about $E$; taking $E=\mathbb{C}1$
gives the original assertion. We use the Baire category argument of
\cite[Proposition 1]{ShlyakhtenkoFuchsian}, retaining both the restriction
of an automorphism and its implementing unitary.

Choose $L^{2}$-dense sequences $(a_{j})_{j\geq1}$ in $N$ and $(z_{j})_{j\geq1}$
in $M$. On the space of normal trace-preserving embeddings $N\to M$
fixing $Q$ pointwise, put 
\[
d(\phi,\psi)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}\min\{1,\|\phi(a_{j})-\psi(a_{j})\|_{2}\}.
\]
This metric induces pointwise $L^{2}$ convergence. The space is complete.
Indeed, if $(\phi_{n})$ is Cauchy, then for each $a\in N$ the sequence
$(\phi_{n}(a))$ is $L^{2}$-Cauchy and satisfies $\|\phi_{n}(a)\|=\|a\|$.
A uniformly operator-norm bounded $L^{2}$-Cauchy sequence has its
limit in $M$. These limits define a unital linear map on $N$, preserve
products and adjoints by continuity on norm-bounded sets, and preserve
the trace. Faithfulness of the trace makes it injective. It is normal:
if $b_{i}\uparrow b$ in $N_{+}$ and $c=\sup_{i}\phi(b_{i})$, then
$c\leq\phi(b)$ and normality of the traces gives $\tau(c)=\tau(b)=\tau(\phi(b))$,
hence $c=\phi(b)$. The condition of fixing $Q$ is closed.

Let $\mathcal{X}$ be the closure of
\[
\{(\alpha|_{N},U):\alpha\in G,\ U\in\mathcal{U}(M),\ \alpha|_{E}=\operatorname{Ad}U|_{E}\}
\]
in the product of this embedding space and the unitary group with
its operator-norm metric. This is a complete metric space containing
$(\iota,1)$, where $\iota:N\hookrightarrow M$ is inclusion. Every
$(\phi,U)\in\mathcal{X}$ still satisfies $\phi|_{E}=\operatorname{Ad}U|_{E}$.
For $j,k\geq1$, set
\[
\mathcal{O}_{j,k}=\{(\phi,U)\in\mathcal{X}:\text{ there is }y\in N\text{ with }\|z_{j}-\phi(y)\|_{2}<1/k\}.
\]
Each $\mathcal{O}_{j,k}$ is open, being a union of open conditions
on the value at a fixed $y$.

To see density, it suffices to approximate a pair $(\alpha_{0}|_{N},U_{0})$
from the set whose closure defines $\mathcal{X}$. A neighborhood
controls the first coordinate on a finite set $F\subset N$ and the
second coordinate in norm. Apply the strengthened condition (1) to
$F$ and $\alpha_{0}^{-1}(z_{j})$, obtaining $\beta\in G$, $y\in N$,
and an implementing unitary $V$ with both the displacement on $F$
and $\|V-1\|$ arbitrarily small, and with approximation error less
than $1/k$. Then $\alpha_{0}\beta$ is implemented on $E$ by $\alpha_{0}(V)U_{0}$,
and
\[
\begin{aligned}\|\alpha_{0}\beta(a)-\alpha_{0}(a)\|_{2} & =\|\beta(a)-a\|_{2}\quad(a\in F),\\
\|\alpha_{0}(V)U_{0}-U_{0}\| & =\|V-1\|,\\
\|z_{j}-\alpha_{0}\beta(y)\|_{2} & <1/k.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the resulting pair belongs to the given neighborhood and to $\mathcal{O}_{j,k}$.

By the Baire category theorem, $\bigcap_{j,k}\mathcal{O}_{j,k}$ is
dense in $\mathcal{X}$. If $(\phi,U)$ belongs to this intersection,
each $z_{j}$ belongs to the $L^{2}$ closure of $\phi(N)$. The image
$\phi(N)$ is a von Neumann subalgebra of $M$. Its trace-preserving
conditional expectation therefore fixes every $z_{j}$. By $L^{2}$
density, this expectation is the identity, so $\phi(N)=M$. Choosing
$(\phi,U)$ sufficiently close to $(\iota,1)$ on the prescribed finite
set and in the unitary coordinate proves (2), with the additional
bound on $\|U-1\|$.

For the original conditions, assume (2). By separability, choose surjective
trace-preserving isomorphisms $\Phi_{j}:N\to M$, fixing $Q$, which
converge pointwise in $L^{2}$ to the inclusion. Fix one surjective
trace-preserving isomorphism $\Phi:N\to M$ fixing $Q$. With $\iota:N\hookrightarrow M$
denoting inclusion, the maps 
\[
\gamma_{j}=\Phi_{j}\Phi^{-1}\in G
\]
converge pointwise in $L^{2}$ to $\rho=\iota\Phi^{-1}$, whose range
is $N$. This proves (3). Conversely, if $\gamma_{j}\to\rho$ as in
(3), then 
\[
\Phi_{j}=\gamma_{j}\rho^{-1}:N\to M
\]
are surjective trace-preserving isomorphisms fixing $Q$ and converging
to the inclusion. Thus (3)$\Rightarrow$(2).

It remains to prove (2)$\Rightarrow$(1), again including the additional
assertion. Given $F,z,\eta,\epsilon,\kappa$, choose a surjective
isomorphism $\Phi:N\to M$ fixing $Q$, moving each $a\in F$ by less
than $\eta/2$, and implemented on $E$ by a unitary $U$ with $\|U-1\|<\kappa/2$.
Put $y=\Phi^{-1}(z)\in N$. Choose a second surjective isomorphism
$\Psi:N\to M$ fixing $Q$, with
\[
\max_{a\in F}\|\Psi(a)-a\|_{2}<\eta/2,\qquad\|\Psi(y)-y\|_{2}<\epsilon,
\]
and implemented on $E$ by a unitary $V$ with $\|V-1\|<\kappa/2$.
The automorphism $\alpha=\Phi\Psi^{-1}$ belongs to $G$, and
\[
\|\alpha(a)-a\|_{2}\leq\|a-\Psi(a)\|_{2}+\|\Phi(a)-a\|_{2}<\eta\quad(a\in F),
\]
while
\[
\|\alpha(y)-z\|_{2}=\|\Psi^{-1}(y)-y\|_{2}=\|y-\Psi(y)\|_{2}<\epsilon.
\]
For $e\in E$, the identity $\alpha(VeV^{*})=UeU^{*}$ shows that
$\alpha|_{E}$ is implemented by $\alpha(V^{*})U$, and
\[
\|\alpha(V^{*})U-1\|\leq\|V-1\|+\|U-1\|<\kappa.
\]
This proves (1).

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:caap}Let $A\subset B\subset C$ be unital inclusions
of separable $C^{*}$-algebras, and let $\tau$ be a trace on $C$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every finite subset $F\subset B$, every $z\in C$, and every
$\eta,\epsilon>0$, there are a trace-preserving automorphism $\alpha$
of $C$ fixing $A$ pointwise and $y\in B$ such that 
\[
\max_{b\in F}\|\alpha(b)-b\|<\eta,\qquad\|z-\alpha(y)\|<\epsilon.
\]
\item For every finite subset $F\subset B$ and every $\epsilon>0$, there
is a surjective trace-preserving isomorphism $\Phi:B\to C$ fixing
$A$ pointwise such that 
\[
\max_{b\in F}\|\Phi(b)-b\|<\epsilon.
\]
\item There is a trace-preserving endomorphism $\rho:C\to C$ with $\rho(C)=B$
which is a point-norm limit of a sequence of trace-preserving automorphisms
of $C$ fixing $A$ pointwise.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, for a specified unital $C^{*}$-subalgebra $E\subset B$,
conditions (1) and (2) remain equivalent with the additional requirement
that, for every $\kappa>0$, the map be implemented on $E$ by a unitary
$U\in C$ with $\|U-1\|<\kappa$.

<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:closures}(1) Suppose that $A\subset B\subset C$ are separable
unital $C^{*}$-algebras, that $C$ carries a faithful tracial state
$\tau$, and that $B\subset C$ has CAAP relative to $A$, with trace-preserving
witnessing maps. Then, in the GNS representation of $\tau$, the inclusion
$W^{*}(B)\subset W^{*}(C)$ has AAP relative to $W^{*}(A)$. If CAAP
holds with small unitary implementation on $E\subset B$, then AAP
holds with small unitary implementation on $W^{*}(E)$: extend the
surjective trace-preserving isomorphisms to the generated von Neumann
algebras, retaining the same implementing unitaries.

(2) Having AAP or CAAP relative to a subalgebra implies the same property
relative to any smaller unital subalgebra.

<!-- rem-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:implementation}If $N=W^{*}(Q,E)$, then fixing $Q$ and
conjugating $E$ by unitaries converging in norm to $1$ already imply
pointwise $L^{2}$ convergence to the inclusion. The corresponding
statement holds in norm when $B=C^{*}(A,E)$. Thus, in this situation,
the finite-set approximation condition follows from the requirement
of small unitary implementation.

For CAAP, small unitary implementation on $E$ is equivalent to small
unitary implementation on $TET^{*}$ for any $T\in\mathcal{U}(B)$,
with the fixed subalgebra $A$ unchanged. Indeed, if $\Phi|_{E}=\operatorname{Ad}U|_{E}$,
then $\Phi$ is implemented on $TET^{*}$ by $W=\Phi(T)UT^{*}$, and
\[
\|W-1\|\leq\|\Phi(T)-T\|+\|U-1\|.
\]
We can choose $F$ to contain $T$, thus bounding $\Vert\Phi(T)-T\Vert$.
Replacing $T$ by $T^{*}$ proves the converse.

take on at most $p-1$ distinct values.

$N_{m}^{-r/2}=N_{m}^{-p}$, we see that for large enough $m$,

then implies that the limit law of $(H_{m},H_{m}^{*})$ (and thus
of $(Z_{m},Z_{m}^{*})$) is the circular law.

\sum t_{k}(M^{k-1}-1)+\sum s_{k}(M^{k}-1)=0
\]
(because the total degree of $a$ must be zero). Subtracting these
we get
\[
(M-1)\sum s_{k}M^{k-1}=0.
\]
Since each $|s_{k}|\leq2p$, this forces $s_{k}=0$ once $M$ is large
enough. It follows that also $\sum t_{k}(M^{k-1}-1)=0$, which, because
$|t_{k}|\leq2p$, also forces $t_{k}=0$ for large enough $M$.

For condition (2), choose
\[
\begin{gathered}3\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{p}\leq m,\qquad j_{i+1}-j_{i}\geq2\quad(1\leq i<p),\\
v_{i}\in\{g_{j_{i}},h_{j_{i}}\}\qquad(1\leq i\leq p).
\end{gathered}
\]
Only $O_{p}(m^{p-1})$ selections fail these conditions. Write
\[
\begin{gathered}s_{2j-1}=a^{r_{j}},\qquad s_{2j}=b^{r_{j}},\qquad n_{0}=0,\\
v_{i}=s_{1}\cdots s_{n_{i}},\qquad n_{i}\in\{2j_{i}-2,2j_{i}-1\}.
\end{gathered}

With $v_{0}=e$, put
\[

\]
where $\ell$ counts the nonzero powers in the reduced alternating

cancellation of adjacent powers in a product of the $d_{i}^{\pm1}$
can occur only between $d_{i}$ and $d_{i}^{-1}$. Thus, for $\varepsilon_{\nu}\in\{-1,1\}$

\[

\]

\[

\]
Hence $d_{i}$ are free inside $\mathbb{F}_{2}$, and so are the $v_{i}=d_{1}\cdots d_{i}$.

Consider now the signed sum
\[
\widetilde{R}_{m}=\sum_{j=2}^{m}\lambda(g_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda(h_{j})=R_{m}-1.
\]
Lemma \ref{lem:sparse-clt} now shows that $(2m-1)^{-1/2}\widetilde{R}_{m}$
(and thus $(2m-1)^{-1/2}R_{m}$) converges in $*$-distribution to
the circular law. Consequently the spectral distributions $\nu_{m}$
of 
\[

\]
converge weakly to the free Poisson law
\[
d\nu(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{4-s}{s}}\,\mathbf{1}_{(0,4)}(s)\,ds
\]
(see \cite[Example 15.15 and Proposition 12.13]{NicaSpeicher}). In
particular, $\nu(\{0\})=0$.

Now let 
\[
\widetilde{x}_{m}=R_{m}^{*}(1+R_{m}R_{m}^{*})^{-1}\delta_{e}.
\]
Since 
\[

\]
the spectral theorem gives 
\[
\|\widetilde{x}_{m}\|_{2}^{2}+\|R_{m}\widetilde{x}_{m}-\delta_{e}\|_{2}^{2}=\tau\bigl((1+R_{m}R_{m}^{*})^{-1}\bigr).
\]
Thus, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
\begin{aligned}\tau\bigl((1+R_{m}R_{m}^{*})^{-1}\bigr) & =\int\frac{1}{1+2ms}\,d\nu_{m}(s)\\
 & \leq\nu_{m}([0,\varepsilon])+\frac{1}{1+2m\varepsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\]

and $(1+2m\varepsilon)^{-1}<\eta^{2}/8$. The preceding identity and

so each of the two norms is less than $\eta/2$.

\begin{align*}
\mathcal{A}*\mathcal{Q} & =\mathcal{A}_{1}*\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{A}_{2}=W^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{1},u,v,Q)*_{W^{*}(v)}\mathcal{A}_{2}\\
 & =(\mathcal{A}_{1}*_{W^{*}(u)}W^{*}(u,v,Q))*_{W^{*}(v)}\mathcal{A}_{2}\\
 & =(U\mathcal{A}_{1}U^{*}*_{W^{*}(UuU^{*})}W^{*}(u,v,Q))*_{W^{*}(v)}\mathcal{A}_{2}
\end{align*}
because $U\in W^{*}(u,v,Q)$. Since $C^{*}(UuU^{*},v)=C^{*}(u,v,Q)$
also $W^{*}(UuU^{*},v)=W^{*}(u,v,Q)$; moreover $UuU^{*}$ and $v$
are free, so $W^{*}(u,v,Q)=W^{*}(UuU^{*})*W^{*}(v)$. Thus
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{A}*\mathcal{Q} & =(U\mathcal{A}_{1}U^{*}*_{W^{*}(UuU^{*})}W^{*}(u,v,Q))*_{W^{*}(v)}\mathcal{A}_{2}\\
 & =(U\mathcal{A}_{1}U^{*}*_{W^{*}(UuU^{*})}[W^{*}(UuU^{*})*W^{*}(v)])*_{W^{*}(v)}\mathcal{A}_{2}\\
 & =(U\mathcal{A}_{1}U^{*}*W^{*}(v))*_{W^{*}(v)}\mathcal{A}_{2}=W^{*}(U\mathcal{A}_{1}U^{*})*\mathcal{A}_{2}.
\end{align*}

It follows that $U\mathcal{A}_{1}U^{*}$ and $\mathcal{A}_{2}$ are
free in $\mathcal{A}*\mathcal{Q}$. But then $UA_{1}U^{*}$ and $A_{2}$
are free in $A_{1}*_{\textrm{red}}A_{2}*_{\textrm{red}}Q$. It follows
that the maps
\begin{align*}
x & \mapsto UxU^{*},\qquad x\in A_{1},\\
y & \mapsto y,\qquad y\in A_{2}
\end{align*}
extend to a trace-preserving $C^{*}$-monomorphism $\Psi_{\epsilon}$
of $A_{1}*_{\textrm{red}}A_{2}$ into $A_{1}*_{\textrm{red}}A_{2}*_{\textrm{red}}Q$.
Since $\{u,v\}\cup Q\subset C^{*}(UuU^{*},v)$, it follows that the
image of this monomorphism contains both $Q$ and $U$, and thus $A_{1}$
and $A_{2}$, and so is surjective; thus it gives an isomorphism of
$A_{1}*_{\textrm{red}}A_{2}*_{\textrm{red}}Q$ with $A_{1}*_{\textrm{red}}A_{2}$.

Taking $\epsilon\to0$, we see that $\Psi_{\epsilon}$ converges to
the inclusion $A_{1}*_{\textrm{red}}A_{2}\subset A_{1}*_{\textrm{red}}A_{2}*_{\textrm{red}}Q$.

\mathcal{Z}*_{\textrm{red}}\mathcal{Z} & \cong\mathcal{Z}*_{\textrm{red}}\mathcal{Z}*_{\textrm{red}}D\\
 & \cong\mathcal{Z}*_{\textrm{red}}D*_{\textrm{red}}\mathcal{Z}*_{\textrm{red}}D\\

\end{align*}
This answers \cite[Question 4.5]{HirshbergPhillips}.

<!-- prop-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:elementary}If $N\subset M$ has AAP, then the inclusion
is elementary: $N\preceq M$.
<!-- end prop-7 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
By Proposition \ref{prop:aap}, there are surjective trace-preserving
isomorphisms $\Phi_{j}:N\to M$ converging pointwise in $L^{2}$ to
inclusion. Fix a formula $\varphi$ and a tuple $a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})$
from $N$. Isomorphisms preserve formula values: they preserve the
algebraic operations and trace, and surjectivity identifies the domains
of every infimum and supremum. Hence 
\[
\varphi^{N}(a)=\varphi^{M}(\Phi_{j}(a)).
\]
Since $\|\Phi_{j}(a_{i})\|=\|a_{i}\|$ and $\|\Phi_{j}(a_{i})-a_{i}\|_{2}\to0$,
uniform continuity gives 
\[
\varphi^{N}(a)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\varphi^{M}(\Phi_{j}(a))=\varphi^{M}(a).
\]
