<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:intro-main}
Let $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ be a premutation triple in an extriangulated category $(\mathcal C,\mathbb E,\mathfrak s)$, and put
$$
  \mathcal I=\mathcal S\cap\mathcal Z=\mathcal Z\cap\mathcal V.
$$
Then the following statements are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The canonical pretriangulated category
$$
  (\mathcal Z/[\mathcal I],\Sigma,\Omega,\nabla,\Delta)
$$
constructed in \cite[Theorem~3.37]{Iitsuka} is a triangulated category.
\vspace{2mm}

\item The premutation triple satisfies Iitsuka's condition $(\mathrm{MT4})'$ of \cite[Condition~4.6]{Iitsuka}.
\vspace{2mm}

\item The premutation triple satisfies $(\mathrm{MT4})$ of \cite[Remark~4.9(1)]{Iitsuka}.
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{2mm}

If $\underline{\mathcal X}$ denotes the image of a subcategory $\mathcal X$ in $\mathcal C/[\mathcal I]$, then the equivalent condition in {\rm (3)} is
$$
 (\mathcal Z/[\mathcal I],\Sigma,\Omega,\nabla,\Delta)
 \text{ is triangulated}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \underline{\mathcal Z\langle1\rangle}^{-}=\underline{\mathcal Z\langle-1\rangle}^{+}.
$$
We recall the relative shifts $\langle1\rangle$, $\langle-1\rangle$ and the functors $(\,\cdot\,)^{-}$, $(\,\cdot\,)^{+}$ in {\rm Section~\ref{sec:prelim}}.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:relative-structures}
Let $\mathcal I$ be a subcategory of $\mathcal C$. Following Herschend, Liu and Nakaoka \cite[Definition~3.18 and Proposition~3.19]{HerschendLiuNakaoka}, as recalled by Iitsuka \cite[Example~2.17]{Iitsuka}, we define closed subfunctors
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}(C,A)
  &=\{\delta\in\mathbb E(C,A)\mid
      \delta\circ -:\mathcal C(\mathcal I,C)\to\mathbb E(\mathcal I,A)\text{ is zero}\},\\
\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}(C,A)
  &=\{\delta\in\mathbb E(C,A)\mid
      -\circ\delta:\mathcal C(A,\mathcal I)\to\mathbb E(C,\mathcal I)\text{ is zero}\}.
\end{align*}
For $\delta\in\mathbb E(C,A)$, $a:A\to A'$ and $c:C'\to C$, we write
$$
  a\delta=a_{*}\delta\in\mathbb E(C,A'),
  \qquad
  \delta c=c^{*}\delta\in\mathbb E(C',A).
$$
Thus a product such as $\lambda_Zfh^U$ means that one first pulls back $\lambda_Z$ along $f$ and then along $h^U$. This is the convention of \cite[Remark~2.2(4)--(5)]{Iitsuka}.
These subfunctors determine relative extriangulated structures
$$
  (\mathcal C,\mathbb E_{\mathcal I},\mathfrak s_{\mathcal I})
 \quad\text{and}\quad
  (\mathcal C,\mathbb E^{\mathcal I},\mathfrak s^{\mathcal I}).
$$
Following Iitsuka \cite[Example~2.17(3)]{Iitsuka}, we also write
$$
  \mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}=\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}\cap\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}
$$
and denote the induced intersection relative structure by
$$
  (\mathcal C,\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I},\mathfrak s^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}).
$$
Here a closed subfunctor means an additive subfunctor for which the restricted realization is again an extriangulated structure, see Herschend, Liu and Nakaoka \cite[Proposition~3.16]{HerschendLiuNakaoka} and Iitsuka \cite[Proposition~2.16]{Iitsuka}. In particular, all three relative structures satisfy $(\mathrm{ET4})$ and $(\mathrm{ET4})^{\mathrm{op}}$.

<!-- axiom-1: body_md -->
\label{axiom:et4}
Suppose that
$$
 A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{f'}D\overset{\delta}{\dashrightarrow}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 B\xrightarrow{g}C\xrightarrow{g'}F\overset{\delta'}{\dashrightarrow}
$$
are $\mathfrak s$-triangles. We require an object $E$ and a commutative diagram
$$
\begin{CD}
A @>{f}>> B @>{f'}>> D\\
@V{1_A}VV @V{g}VV @V{d}VV\\
A @>{h}>> C @>{h'}>> E\\
@. @V{g'}VV @V{e}VV\\
@. F @= F
\end{CD}
$$
such that
$$
 A\xrightarrow{h}C\xrightarrow{h'}E\overset{\delta''}{\dashrightarrow}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 D\xrightarrow{d}E\xrightarrow{e}F\overset{f'_{*}\delta'}{\dashrightarrow}
$$
are $\mathfrak s$-triangles. The extension identities are
$$
 d^{*}\delta''=\delta,
 \qquad
 f_{*}\delta''=e^{*}\delta'.
$$

\label{axiom:et4op}
If
$$
 D\xrightarrow{f'}A\xrightarrow{f}B\overset{\delta}{\dashrightarrow}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 F\xrightarrow{g'}B\xrightarrow{g}C\overset{\delta'}{\dashrightarrow}
$$
are $\mathfrak s$-triangles, then we require an object $E$ and a commutative diagram
$$
\begin{CD}
D @>{d}>> E @>{e}>> F\\
@V{1_D}VV @V{h'}VV @V{g'}VV\\
D @>{f'}>> A @>{f}>> B\\
@. @V{h}VV @V{g}VV\\
@. C @= C
\end{CD}
$$
for which
$$
 E\xrightarrow{h'}A\xrightarrow{h}C\overset{\delta''}{\dashrightarrow}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 D\xrightarrow{d}E\xrightarrow{e}F\overset{(g')^{*}\delta}{\dashrightarrow}
$$
are $\mathfrak s$-triangles. The corresponding identities are
$$
 \delta'=e_{*}\delta'',
 \qquad
 d_{*}\delta=g^{*}\delta''.
$$

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:approximations}
A morphism $I_X\to X$ with $I_X\in\mathcal I$ is a right $\mathcal I$-approximation if every map from an object of $\mathcal I$ to $X$ factors through it. Left approximations are defined dually. A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is $\mathcal I$-monic if every morphism $X\to I$ with $I\in\mathcal I$ factors through $f$, and $g:Y\to Z$ is $\mathcal I$-epic if every morphism $I\to Z$ with $I\in\mathcal I$ factors through $g$. Iitsuka \cite[Lemma~2.20]{Iitsuka} proves that, for an $\mathfrak s$-triangle $X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z\overset{\delta}{\dashrightarrow}$, one has $\delta\in\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}(Z,X)$ exactly when $f$ is $\mathcal I$-monic, and $\delta\in\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}(Z,X)$ exactly when $g$ is $\mathcal I$-epic. Following Iitsuka \cite[Definition~2.21]{Iitsuka}, we assume that a subcategory used in the notion of strong functorial finiteness is closed under direct summands. We say that $\mathcal I$ is \emph{strongly contravariantly finite} in a subcategory $\mathcal X$ if every $X\in\mathcal X$ admits a right $\mathcal I$-approximation which is a deflation. We define strong covariant finiteness dually, and \emph{strong functorial finiteness} means both.

\label{def:ideal-quotient}
For a subcategory $\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal C$, we write $[\mathcal X](A,B)$ for the subgroup of morphisms $A\to B$ factoring through an object of $\mathcal X$. We denote the additive quotient by $\mathcal C/[\mathcal X]$. If $\mathcal X$ is a subcategory of $\mathcal C$, we write $\underline{\mathcal X}$ for its full image in $\mathcal C/[\mathcal I]$. Thus $\underline{\mathcal Z}=\mathcal Z/[\mathcal I]$. We use underlines whenever a statement concerns objects, subcategories or functors in the quotient. This convention is important for Conditions $(\mathrm{MT4})'$ and $(\mathrm{MT4})$ below.

\cite[Corollary~2.31]{Iitsuka}\label{lem:stable-lift}
Assume that $\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal Z$ is strongly functorially finite. If a morphism $f:X\to Y$ in $\mathcal Z$ becomes an isomorphism in $\mathcal C/[\mathcal I]$, then there exist $I,J\in\mathcal I$ and an isomorphism in $\mathcal C$
$$
  \begin{pmatrix} f & j\\ i & k\end{pmatrix}:
  X\oplus J\xrightarrow{\sim}Y\oplus I.
$$

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:premutation}
A triple $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ of subcategories of $\mathcal C$ is a \emph{premutation triple} if the following conditions hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(MT1)]
$$
  \mathcal S\cap\mathcal Z=\mathcal Z\cap\mathcal V=:\mathcal I,
$$
and $\mathcal I$ is strongly functorially finite in $\mathcal Z$.

\item[(MT2)] The relative orthogonality conditions
$$
  \mathbb E^{\mathcal I}(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z)=0,
  \hspace{2mm}
  \mathbb E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z\langle-1\rangle)=0,
$$
and
$$
  \mathbb E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)=0,
 \hspace{2mm}
  \mathbb E^{\mathcal I}(\mathcal Z\langle1\rangle,\mathcal V)=0
$$
hold.

\item[(MT3)] Put
$$
  \widetilde{\mathcal U}=\operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal S),
\hspace{2mm}
  \widetilde{\mathcal T}=\operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal V,\mathcal Z).
$$
Then
$$
  \operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Z)\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal U},
\hspace{2mm}
  \operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Z)\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal T},
$$
$\mathcal S$ and $\mathcal Z$ are extension closed in $(\mathcal C,\mathbb E^{\mathcal I},\mathfrak s^{\mathcal I})$, and $\mathcal Z$ and $\mathcal V$ are extension closed in $(\mathcal C,\mathbb E_{\mathcal I},\mathfrak s_{\mathcal I})$.
\end{enumerate}

\label{thm:iitsuka-pretri}
Let $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ be a premutation triple and put $\underline{\mathcal Z}=\mathcal Z/[\mathcal I]$. By \cite[Theorem~3.37]{Iitsuka},
$$
  (\underline{\mathcal Z},\Sigma,\Omega,\nabla,\Delta)
$$
is a pretriangulated category. Here $\nabla$ and $\Delta$ are the classes of standard right and left triangles defined in \cite[Definition~3.26 and Definition~3.35]{Iitsuka}, respectively. Moreover, by \cite[Lemma~3.16]{Iitsuka}, $(\Sigma,\Omega)$ is an adjoint pair, with $\Sigma$ left adjoint to $\Omega$. Equivalently, there are natural bijections
$$
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\underline{\mathcal Z}}(\Sigma X,Y)
  \cong
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\underline{\mathcal Z}}(X,\Omega Y)
$$
for all $X,Y\in\underline{\mathcal Z}$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:roundtrip}
Let $Z\in\mathcal Z$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Put $W=\Omega Z$ and $U=W\langle1\rangle$. For any representative $\widetilde\beta_Z$ of $\beta_Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-identity}
  \lambda_Z\,\widetilde\beta_Z\,h^U
  =-h_{Z\langle-1\rangle}\,\lambda^W.
\end{equation}
\item Put $W=\Sigma Z$ and $T=W\langle-1\rangle$. For any representative $\widetilde\alpha_Z$ of $\alpha_Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-identity}
  h_T\,\widetilde\alpha_Z\,\lambda^Z
  =-\lambda_W\,h^{Z\langle1\rangle}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Here compositions involving an extension are read in the standard extriangulated sense. In particular, $\lambda^W$ in {\rm part~(1)} is the cosyzygy extension of $W$, whereas $\lambda_W$ in
{\rm part~(2)} is the syzygy extension of $W$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By \cite[Remark~3.18]{Iitsuka}, the adjunction correspondence is described by an explicit diagram, with the morphisms in $\underline{\mathcal Z}$ determined only modulo maps factoring through $\mathcal I$.
More explicitly, suppose that
$\underline{f}:X\to\Omega Y$ corresponds under the adjunction to
$\underline{f'}:\Sigma X\to Y$. Then ambient representatives $f$ and $f'$ can be
chosen so that
$$
  h_{Y\langle-1\rangle}f\lambda^X
  =-\lambda_Yf'h^{X\langle1\rangle}.
$$
Take $\underline{f}=1_{\Omega Z}$. We may choose $f=1_{\Omega Z}$ as its ambient
representative. The corresponding quotient morphism is
$\underline{f'}=\beta_Z$. Let $b_Z:\Sigma\Omega Z\to Z$ be the ambient
representative $f'$ supplied by the correspondence. Substituting
$X=\Omega Z$ and $Y=Z$ gives the formula below.
$$
  \lambda_Z b_Z h^U=-h_{Z\langle-1\rangle}\lambda^W.
$$
If $\widetilde\beta_Z$ is any other representative, then $\widetilde\beta_Z-b_Z$ factors through an object of $\mathcal I$. Since the fixed syzygy extension $\lambda_Z$ belongs to $\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}$, pullback along a morphism factoring through $\mathcal I$ is zero. Hence
$$
  \lambda_Z(\widetilde\beta_Z-b_Z)h^U=0,
$$
which proves \eqref{eq:beta-identity}.

For the second formula, take $1_{\Sigma Z}$ in the same adjunction correspondence. Its image under the inverse correspondence is $\alpha_Z$.
In the preceding formula, take $X=Z$, $Y=\Sigma Z$,
$\underline{f}=\alpha_Z$ and $\underline{f'}=1_{\Sigma Z}$. We may choose
$f'=1_{\Sigma Z}$ as an ambient representative. Let $a_Z$ be the ambient
representative $f$ supplied by the correspondence. This gives the formula
below.
$$
  h_Ta_Z\lambda^Z=-\lambda_Wh^{Z\langle1\rangle}.
$$
Any other representative differs from $a_Z$ by a morphism factoring through $\mathcal I$. The cosyzygy extension $\lambda^Z$ lies in $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}$, so pushforward along such a morphism is zero. This gives \eqref{eq:alpha-identity}.

  \sigma U\langle-1\rangle\longrightarrow U^{-}\longrightarrow U
  \overset{\chi_U}{\dashrightarrow}.
\end{equation*}
Its extension is
$$
  \chi_U=\lambda_{\sigma U}h^U.
$$
Dually, for $T\in\widetilde{\mathcal T}$ there is an object $T^{+}$ in an $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}$-triangle
\begin{equation*}
  T\longrightarrow T^{+}\longrightarrow\omega T\langle1\rangle
  \overset{\chi^T}{\dashrightarrow}.
\end{equation*}
Its extension is
$$
  \chi^T=h_T\lambda^{\omega T}.
$$

 (\,\cdot\,)^{-}:\underline{\widetilde{\mathcal U}}\longrightarrow\underline{\widetilde{\mathcal U}^{-}},
 \quad
 (\,\cdot\,)^{+}:\underline{\widetilde{\mathcal T}}\longrightarrow\underline{\widetilde{\mathcal T}^{+}}.
$$
Fix the defining triangles once and for all. If $\mathcal X\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal U}$, let $\mathcal X^{-}$ be the full subcategory of $\mathcal C$ formed by the objects isomorphic in $\mathcal C$ to a chosen object $X^{-}$ with $X\in\mathcal X$. The notation $\underline{\mathcal X}^{-}$ denotes the full subcategory of $\underline{\widetilde{\mathcal U}^{-}}$ formed by the images of the chosen objects, and $\underline{\mathcal Y}^{+}$ is defined dually. <!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Set
$$
  T=Z\langle-1\rangle,
  \quad W=\Omega Z,
  \quad U=W\langle1\rangle.
$$
By the chosen relative triangles and $(\mathrm{MT3})$, we have
$$
 T\in\operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Z)\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal T},
 \quad
 U\in\operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Z)\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal U}.
$$
Choose a representative
$$
  \widetilde\beta_Z:\Sigma\Omega Z\longrightarrow Z
$$
of $\beta_Z$. Since $\sigma U=\Sigma W=\Sigma\Omega Z$, the reflection triangle \eqref{eq:sigma-reflection} takes the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-reflection}
  U\xrightarrow{h^U}\Sigma\Omega Z\longrightarrow S^U\dashrightarrow,
  \qquad S^U\in\mathcal S.
\end{equation}

Since $\mathcal Z$ is full, the isomorphism $\beta_Z$ in $\underline{\mathcal Z}$ is also an isomorphism between the same objects in $\mathcal C/[\mathcal I]$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-lift}, applied to $\widetilde\beta_Z$, there are $I_0,I_1\in\mathcal I$ and an isomorphism in $\mathcal C$
\begin{equation*}
  B=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \widetilde\beta_Z & a\\ b & c
  \end{pmatrix}\colon
  \Sigma\Omega Z\oplus I_0\xrightarrow{\sim} Z\oplus I_1.
\end{equation*}
Put
$$
  Y=Z\oplus I_1,
  \quad S'=S^U\oplus I_0.
$$
Taking the direct sum of \eqref{eq:beta-reflection} with a split triangle and transporting it along $B$, we obtain an $\mathfrak s^{\mathcal I}$-triangle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-first-input}
  U\xrightarrow{q}Y\xrightarrow{r}S'\dashrightarrow,
  ~~\mbox{where}~ S'\in\mathcal S,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-def}
  q=B\binom{h^U}{0}.
\end{equation}
In particular, the $Z$-component of $q$ is $\widetilde\beta_Zh^U$.

On the other hand, the fixed syzygy triangle of $Z$ is
$$
  T\longrightarrow I_Z\xrightarrow{p_Z}Z\overset{\lambda_Z}{\dashrightarrow}.
$$
For objects of $\mathcal Z$, this triangle belongs to the intersection relative structure by the dual of \cite[Lemma~2.32]{Iitsuka}, using the orthogonality in (MT2). After taking the direct sum with the split triangle on $I_1$, we get an $\mathfrak s^{\mathcal I}$-triangle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-second-input}
  T\xrightarrow{i}I'\xrightarrow{p}Y
  \overset{\widetilde\lambda_Z}{\dashrightarrow},
  ~~~\mbox{where}~
  I'=I_Z\oplus I_1\in\mathcal I,
\end{equation}
where
$$
  \widetilde\lambda_Z=(\lambda_Z,0).
$$

We may therefore apply $(\mathrm{ET4})^{\mathrm{op}}$ in the relative extriangulated category $(\mathcal C,\mathbb E^{\mathcal I},\mathfrak s^{\mathcal I})$ to \eqref{eq:beta-first-input} and \eqref{eq:beta-second-input}. It gives an object $E_Z$ and $\mathfrak s^{\mathcal I}$-triangles
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-output-one}
  T\xrightarrow{x}E_Z\xrightarrow{y}U
  \overset{\chi_Z}{\dashrightarrow},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-output-two}
  E_Z\xrightarrow{c}I'\xrightarrow{rp}S'\dashrightarrow.
\end{equation}
We display the four conflations in this application of $(\mathrm{ET4})^{\mathrm{op}}$ as follows.
$$
\begin{CD}
T @>{x}>> E_Z @>{y}>> U\\
@V{1_T}VV @V{c}VV @V{q}VV\\
T @>{i}>> I' @>{p}>> Y\\
@. @V{rp}VV @V{r}VV\\
@. S' @= S'
\end{CD}
$$
This is the standard $(\mathrm{ET4})^{\mathrm{op}}$ diagram of Nakaoka--Palu \cite[Remark~2.22]{NakaokaPalu}. The top row is \eqref{eq:beta-output-one}. The middle row is \eqref{eq:beta-second-input}, the right column is \eqref{eq:beta-first-input}, and the middle column is \eqref{eq:beta-output-two}.

The extension compatibility in $(\mathrm{ET4})^{\mathrm{op}}$ says that the extension in \eqref{eq:beta-output-one} is the pullback of $\widetilde\lambda_Z$ along $q$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
  \chi_Z=q^{*}\widetilde\lambda_Z
  =(\widetilde\beta_Zh^U)^{*}\lambda_Z
  =\lambda_Z\,\widetilde\beta_Z\,h^U.
\end{equation*}
The second equality follows from the definition of $q$ in \eqref{eq:q-def} and the fact that the $I_1$-component of $\widetilde\lambda_Z$ is zero.

Now apply Lemma~\ref{lem:roundtrip}(i). Since $T=Z\langle-1\rangle$ and $W=\Omega Z$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chi-beta-identify}
  \chi_Z=-h_T\lambda^W.
\end{equation}
By the construction of $(\,\cdot\,)^{+}$ in \cite[Notation~4.4]{Iitsuka}, the extension $h_T\lambda^W$ is precisely the extension realized by the defining triangle
\begin{equation*}
  T\longrightarrow T^{+}\longrightarrow W\langle1\rangle=U\dashrightarrow.
\end{equation*}
Moreover, this defining extension lies in the intersection
$$
  \mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}(U,T)
  =\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}(U,T)\cap\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}(U,T).
$$
Equation \eqref{eq:chi-beta-identify} therefore shows that $\chi_Z$ lies in the intersection relative structure, although it was first obtained in $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}$. Let
$$
  T\xrightarrow{s^T}T^+\longrightarrow U\overset{h_T\lambda^W}{\dashrightarrow}
$$
be the triangle defining $T^+$. Since $\mathfrak s$ is additive,
$$
 \mathfrak s(-h_T\lambda^W)
 =\big[T\xrightarrow{-s^T}T^+\longrightarrow U\big].
$$
Thus \eqref{eq:beta-output-one} and this triangle are equivalent realizations of the same extension. In particular, their middle terms are isomorphic. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E-is-T-plus}
  E_Z\cong T^{+}=Z\langle-1\rangle^{+}.
\end{equation}

Finally, \eqref{eq:beta-output-two} has middle term $I'\in\mathcal I$ and third term $S'\in\mathcal S$. By the definition \eqref{eq:ut-minus-tt-plus},
$$
  E_Z\in\operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal I,\mathcal S)=\widetilde{\mathcal U}^{-}.
$$
Together with \eqref{eq:E-is-T-plus}, this gives
$$
  Z\langle-1\rangle^{+}\in\widetilde{\mathcal U}^{-},
$$
as required.
For an arbitrary object $T'\in\mathcal Z\langle-1\rangle$, its image in the
quotient is isomorphic to the image of a chosen object
$Z\langle-1\rangle$. Applying the functor $(\,\cdot\,)^{+}$ gives
$\underline{(T')^{+}}\cong\underline{Z\langle-1\rangle^{+}}$. The object on the right is
the image of an object of $\widetilde{\mathcal U}^{-}$. This proves the asserted inclusion of
essential images in $\mathcal C/[\mathcal I]$.

\label{thm:main}
Let $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ be a premutation triple and let
$$
  (\underline{\mathcal Z},\Sigma,\Omega,\nabla,\Delta),
  \quad \underline{\mathcal Z}=\mathcal Z/[\mathcal I],
$$
be the canonical pretriangulated category constructed in \cite[Theorem~3.37]{Iitsuka}. Then the following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(\underline{\mathcal Z},\Sigma,\Omega,\nabla,\Delta)$ is triangulated.
\item $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ satisfies $(\mathrm{MT4})'$.
\item $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ satisfies $(\mathrm{MT4})$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Iitsuka proves $(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)$ in \cite[Remark~4.9(1)]{Iitsuka} and $(2)\Rightarrow(1)$ in \cite[Theorem~4.8]{Iitsuka}. We prove $(1)\Rightarrow(2)$.

Assume that the canonical pretriangulated structure is triangulated. Then its suspension functor $\Sigma$ is an autoequivalence. By Theorem~\ref{thm:iitsuka-pretri}, $\Sigma$ is left adjoint to $\Omega$. Standard adjunction theory shows that a right adjoint of an equivalence is a quasi-inverse, see Mac~Lane \cite[Chapter~IV, Sections~1 and~4]{MacLane}. Hence $(\Sigma,\Omega)$ is an adjoint equivalence. In particular, every component
$$
  \alpha_Z\colon Z\longrightarrow\Omega\Sigma Z,
  \quad
  \beta_Z\colon \Sigma\Omega Z\longrightarrow Z
$$
is an isomorphism in $\underline{\mathcal Z}$.


Lemmas~\ref{lem:counit-half} and~\ref{lem:unit-half} directly give the
following inclusions in $\mathcal C/[\mathcal I]$.
$$
 \underline{\mathcal Z\langle1\rangle}^{-}\subseteq\underline{\widetilde{\mathcal T}}^{+},
 \quad
 \underline{\mathcal Z\langle-1\rangle}^{+}\subseteq\underline{\widetilde{\mathcal U}}^{-}.
$$
We have obtained the two clauses of $(\mathrm{MT4})'$, and hence $(1)\Rightarrow(2)$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:one-sided}
We can also apply the two lemmas separately, without assuming that the adjunction is an equivalence. Namely,
$$
  \beta_Z\text{ invertible for all }Z
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \underline{\mathcal Z\langle-1\rangle}^{+}\subseteq\underline{\widetilde{\mathcal U}}^{-},
$$
whereas
$$
  \alpha_Z\text{ invertible for all }Z
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \underline{\mathcal Z\langle1\rangle}^{-}\subseteq\underline{\widetilde{\mathcal T}}^{+}.
$$
Thus the two clauses of $(\mathrm{MT4})'$ are necessary for the unit and the
counit of the mutation adjunction, respectively, to be invertible.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:functorial-characterization}
Let $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ be a premutation triple. The following conditions are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ satisfies $(\mathrm{MT4})$.
\item The canonical pretriangulated structure on $\mathcal Z/[\mathcal I]$ is triangulated.
\item The mutation functor $\Sigma$ is an autoequivalence.
\item The mutation functor $\Omega$ is an autoequivalence.
\item The adjoint pair $(\Sigma,\Omega)$ is an adjoint equivalence.
\item Both the unit $\alpha$ and the counit $\beta$ are natural isomorphisms.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, we have $(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)$. From the definition of a triangulated category, we obtain $(2)\Rightarrow(3)$ and $(2)\Rightarrow(4)$. If (3) holds, the right adjoint $\Omega$ of the equivalence $\Sigma$ is a quasi-inverse, so (5) holds; the implication $(4)\Rightarrow(5)$ is dual. Standard properties of adjunctions give $(5)\Leftrightarrow(6)$, see Mac~Lane \cite[Chapter~IV, Sections~1 and~4]{MacLane}. Finally, under (5), the functors $\Sigma$ and $\Omega$ are mutually quasi-inverse, so Iitsuka \cite[Corollary~3.38]{Iitsuka} gives (2).
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:full-faithfulness}
Let $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ be a premutation triple.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\Sigma$ is fully faithful, then the following inclusion holds in $\mathcal C/[\mathcal I]$.
$$
  \underline{\mathcal Z\langle1\rangle}^{-}\subseteq\underline{\widetilde{\mathcal T}}^{+}.
$$

\item If $\Omega$ is fully faithful, then the following inclusion holds in $\mathcal C/[\mathcal I]$.
$$
  \underline{\mathcal Z\langle-1\rangle}^{+}\subseteq\underline{\widetilde{\mathcal U}}^{-}.
$$
\item If both $\Sigma$ and $\Omega$ are fully faithful, then $(\mathrm{MT4})$ holds.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For the adjunction between $\Sigma$ and $\Omega$, standard adjunction theory identifies full faithfulness of $\Sigma$ with invertibility of the unit,  see Mac~Lane \cite[Chapter~IV, Section~3]{MacLane}. We obtain (1) from Lemma~\ref{lem:unit-half}. Dually, full faithfulness of $\Omega$ is equivalent to invertibility of the counit, so Lemma~\ref{lem:counit-half} gives (2). If both functors are fully faithful, both the unit and the counit are invertible. Hence the adjunction is an equivalence, and Corollary~\ref{cor:functorial-characterization} gives (3).

<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{def:concentric-tcp}
Following Liu and Nakaoka \cite[Definitions~2.1 and~2.3]{LiuNakaoka}, a pair $(\mathcal U,\mathcal V)$ is a cotorsion pair if
$$
  \mathbb E(\mathcal U,\mathcal V)=0,\quad
  \mathcal C=\operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}(\mathcal V,\mathcal U)
  =\operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}(\mathcal V,\mathcal U).
$$
A pair of cotorsion pairs $((\mathcal S,\mathcal T),(\mathcal U,\mathcal V))$ is a \emph{twin cotorsion pair} if $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal U$, and it is \emph{concentric} if
$$
  \mathcal S\cap\mathcal T=\mathcal U\cap\mathcal V.
$$
Iitsuka recalls the same definitions in \cite[Definitions~A.2 and~A.4]{Iitsuka}, see also Nakaoka \cite[Definition~3.3]{Nakaoka} and Zheng--Wei \cite[Section~2]{ZhengWei}.
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def:tcp-inclusions}
Nakaoka's construction uses the following two subcategories:
$$
  \mathcal N^{\mathrm i}=\operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}(\mathcal V,\mathcal S),
  \quad
  \mathcal N^{\mathrm f}=\operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}(\mathcal V,\mathcal S).
$$
Thus, $\mathcal N^{\mathrm i}$ consists of the objects which occur as third
terms of triangles whose first term lies in $\mathcal V$ and whose middle term lies
in $\mathcal S$; $\mathcal N^{\mathrm f}$ consists of the objects which occur as
first terms of triangles whose middle term lies in $\mathcal V$ and whose third term
lies in $\mathcal S$. We will use the following two quotient inclusions from Iitsuka
\cite[Condition~A.27]{Iitsuka}:
$$
  \underline{\mathcal Z\langle1\rangle}^{-}\subseteq\underline{\mathcal N^{\mathrm i}},
  \quad
  \underline{\mathcal Z\langle-1\rangle}^{+}\subseteq\underline{\mathcal N^{\mathrm f}}.
$$
Iitsuka records these inclusions immediately after observing that Nakaoka's
proof works under precisely these two weaker hypotheses, see \cite[the
paragraph preceding Condition~A.27]{Iitsuka}.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:tcp-inclusions-triangulated}
Let $((\mathcal S,\mathcal T),(\mathcal U,\mathcal V))$ be a concentric twin cotorsion pair in a triangulated category. If the two inclusions in Definition~\ref{def:tcp-inclusions} hold, then the canonical pretriangulated category
$$
 (\underline{\mathcal Z},\Sigma,\Omega,\nabla,\Delta)
$$
is triangulated.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Iitsuka observes in the paragraph preceding
\cite[Condition~A.27]{Iitsuka} that the proof of
\cite[Theorem~A.26(1)]{Iitsuka} remains valid under the two weaker inclusions
in Definition~\ref{def:tcp-inclusions}. These inclusions are precisely
Conditions $(\mathrm{Nak1})$ and $(\mathrm{Nak2})$ of
\cite[Condition~A.27]{Iitsuka}. The argument is based on
\cite[Remark~5.8]{Nakaoka}, which gives natural isomorphisms
$$
  \Sigma\Omega\cong\mathrm{Id}_{\underline{\mathcal Z}},
  \qquad
  \Omega\Sigma\cong\mathrm{Id}_{\underline{\mathcal Z}}.
$$
Hence $\Sigma$ and $\Omega$ are mutually quasi-inverse. By
\cite[Example~3.15(4) and Theorem~A.22]{Iitsuka}, the mutation functors and
the right and left triangles in this construction agree with the canonical
structure associated with the premutation triple
$(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$. Therefore the canonical pretriangulated category is
triangulated.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:tcp-application}
Let $((\mathcal S,\mathcal T),(\mathcal U,\mathcal V))$ be a concentric twin cotorsion pair in a triangulated category and let $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ be its associated premutation triple. The following conditions are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The following two inclusions hold:
$$
  \underline{\mathcal Z\langle1\rangle}^{-}\subseteq\underline{\mathcal N^{\mathrm i}},
  \quad
  \underline{\mathcal Z\langle-1\rangle}^{+}\subseteq\underline{\mathcal N^{\mathrm f}}.
$$
\item The associated premutation triple satisfies $(\mathrm{MT4}^{-})$.
\item The associated premutation triple satisfies $(\mathrm{MT4})$.
\item The canonical pretriangulated structure on $\mathcal Z/[\mathcal I]$ is triangulated.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Iitsuka proves $(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)$ in \cite[Remark~4.9(3) and Condition~A.27]{Iitsuka} and records $(3)\Rightarrow(2)$ in \cite[Remark~4.9(3)]{Iitsuka}.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:tcp-inclusions-triangulated}, $(1)$ implies (4). By Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, (4) implies (3). Thus all the conditions are equivalent.

<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{def:reducible}
A triple satisfying $(\mathrm{MT1})$ and $(\mathrm{MT2})$ satisfies $(\mathrm{RT3})$ if
$$
  \operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Z)
  \subseteq
  \operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal S),
  \quad
  \operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Z)
  \subseteq
  \operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal V,\mathcal Z),
$$
and if $\mathcal S$, $\mathcal Z$ and $\mathcal V$ are extension closed in $(\mathcal C,\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I},\mathfrak s^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I})$. It satisfies $(\mathrm{RT4})$ if $\mathcal I$ is strongly contravariantly finite in $\mathcal S$, strongly covariantly finite in $\mathcal V$, and
$$
  \mathcal S\langle-1\rangle=\mathcal V\langle1\rangle.
$$
A \emph{prereducible triple} is a triple satisfying $(\mathrm{MT1})$, $(\mathrm{MT2})$ and $(\mathrm{RT3})$. A \emph{reducible triple} is a prereducible triple that also satisfies $(\mathrm{RT4})$.
<!-- end definition-8 -->
<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:reducible-application}
Let $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ be a reducible triple. The following conditions are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ is a premutation triple.
\item $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ is a mutation triple.
\item The subcategories $\mathcal S$ and $\mathcal Z$ are closed under extensions in
$$
  (\mathcal C,\mathbb E^{\mathcal I},\mathfrak s^{\mathcal I}),
$$
and the subcategories $\mathcal Z$ and $\mathcal V$ are closed under extensions in
$$
  (\mathcal C,\mathbb E_{\mathcal I},\mathfrak s_{\mathcal I}).
$$
\end{enumerate}
In particular, for a reducible triple, the only possible obstruction to being a mutation triple is the extension closure requirement in $(\mathrm{MT3})$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
We first prove $(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)$. Iitsuka \cite[Corollary~5.4]{Iitsuka} defines the canonical pretriangulated quintuple for a prereducible triple by the same $\Sigma,\Omega,\nabla,\Delta$ as in the premutation construction, and Iitsuka \cite[Corollary~5.9]{Iitsuka} proves that this quintuple is triangulated for a reducible triple. Therefore, if a reducible triple is also a premutation triple, we may apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main} to the same canonical structure and obtain $(\mathrm{MT4})$. Hence the triple is a mutation triple. The converse follows from the definition.


Here the two canonical structures do agree. Under $(\mathrm{MT2})$, the
approximation triangles which define $\langle1\rangle$ and
$\langle-1\rangle$ may be chosen in the intersection relative structure.
Under $(\mathrm{RT3})$, the reflection and coreflection triangles needed for
the standard right and left triangles may also be chosen there. The
independence of these choices in \cite[Corollary~5.4]{Iitsuka} identifies the
resulting functors and triangle classes with those of the premutation
construction.


It remains to compare (1) and (3). A reducible triple already satisfies $(\mathrm{MT1})$, $(\mathrm{MT2})$ and $(\mathrm{RT3})$. The first clause of $(\mathrm{RT3})$ gives
$$
 \operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Z)
 \subseteq
 \operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal S)
 \subseteq
 \operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal S).
$$
Thus the right cone--cocone clause of $(\mathrm{MT3})$ is automatic. The second clause of $(\mathrm{RT3})$ gives dually
$$
 \operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Z)
 \subseteq
 \operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal V,\mathcal Z)
 \subseteq
 \operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal V,\mathcal Z).
$$
Hence the left cone--cocone clause of $(\mathrm{MT3})$ is automatic as well. Consequently, under the defining assumptions of a reducible triple, condition $(\mathrm{MT3})$ is equivalent to its extension closure clause. This clause is exactly (3). Therefore (1) and (3) are equivalent.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:reducible-counterexample}
There exists a reducible triple which is not a premutation triple. Consequently, a reducible triple need not be a mutation triple.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $Q$ be the linearly oriented quiver
$$
  1\longrightarrow2\longrightarrow3\longrightarrow4
$$
and let $\mathcal C=\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}} kQ$, with its standard exact structure. For $1\leq a\leq b\leq4$, write $M_{ab}$ for the interval module supported on the vertices $a,a+1,\ldots,b$, with identity maps on the arrows inside its support.

We shall use two elementary calculations for interval modules. They are included to make the verification of the relative conditions transparent. One has
$$
 \dim_k\operatorname{Hom}(M_{ab},M_{cd})=
 \begin{cases}
  1 & \text{if }c\leq a\leq d\leq b,\\
  0 & \text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
$$
and
$$
 \dim_k\operatorname{Ext}^1(M_{ab},M_{cd})=
 \begin{cases}
  1 & \text{if }a<c\leq b+1\leq d,\\
  0 & \text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
$$
The first formula follows directly from the commutativity relations for a morphism of interval representations. For the second, if $b<4$, use the projective resolution
$$
  0\longrightarrow M_{b+1,4}\longrightarrow M_{a4}
  \longrightarrow M_{ab}\longrightarrow0.
$$
If $b=4$, the module $M_{a4}$ is projective.

For later reference, the groups $\operatorname{Ext}^1(S,I)$ with $S$ an indecomposable object of $\mathcal S$ and $I$ an indecomposable object of $\mathcal I$ are displayed in the following table. Each entry is its $k$-dimension.
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 \dim_k\operatorname{Ext}^1(S,I)
   & I=M_{11} & I=M_{23} & I=M_{34}\\ \hline
 S=M_{11} &0&1&0\\
 S=M_{22} &0&0&1\\
 S=M_{23} &0&0&1\\
 S=M_{34} &0&0&0
\end{array}
$$

Set
$$
 \mathcal I=\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(M_{11}\oplus M_{23}\oplus M_{34}),
 \quad
 \mathcal Z=\mathcal I,
$$
$$
 \mathcal S=\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(\mathcal I\oplus M_{22}),
 \quad
 \mathcal V=\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(\mathcal I\oplus M_{44}).
$$
We verify that $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ is reducible.

Condition $(\mathrm{MT1})$ is immediate. Indeed,
$$
  \mathcal S\cap\mathcal Z=\mathcal Z\cap\mathcal V=\mathcal I,
$$
and $\mathcal I$ is strongly functorially finite in $\mathcal Z=\mathcal I$.

We next check $(\mathrm{MT2})$. Since $\mathcal Z=\mathcal I$, the identity morphism of an object of $\mathcal I$ shows that
$$
  \mathbb E^{\mathcal I}(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z)=0,
  \quad
  \mathbb E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)=0.
$$
The same observation gives
$$
 \operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal I,\mathcal Z)=\mathcal I,
 \quad
 \operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal I)=\mathcal I.
$$
It remains to verify
$$
  \mathbb E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal S,\mathcal I)=0,
  \quad
  \mathbb E^{\mathcal I}(\mathcal I,\mathcal V)=0.
$$
By additivity, it is enough to check indecomposable pairs. For the first equality, the only pairs of indecomposable objects $S\in\mathcal S$ and $I\in\mathcal I$ with $\operatorname{Ext}^1(S,I)\neq0$ give, up to isomorphism, the following non-split exact sequences
$$
  0\longrightarrow M_{23}\longrightarrow M_{13}
  \longrightarrow M_{11}\longrightarrow0,
$$
$$
  0\longrightarrow M_{34}\longrightarrow M_{24}
  \longrightarrow M_{22}\longrightarrow0,
$$
and
$$
  0\longrightarrow M_{34}\longrightarrow M_{24}\oplus M_{33}
  \longrightarrow M_{23}\longrightarrow0.
$$
None of the three deflations is $\mathcal I$-epic. In the first case the identity of $M_{11}$ does not lift because $\operatorname{Hom}(M_{11},M_{13})=0$. In the second case the nonzero map $M_{23}\to M_{22}$ does not lift because $\operatorname{Hom}(M_{23},M_{24})=0$. In the third case the identity of $M_{23}$ does not lift because
$$
  \operatorname{Hom}(M_{23},M_{24})=\operatorname{Hom}(M_{23},M_{33})=0.
$$
Each nonzero group in the table is one-dimensional over $k$. Moreover,
$\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}(S,I)$ is a $k$-linear subspace of
$\operatorname{Ext}^1(S,I)$. In every relevant case, the displayed non-split
exact sequence represents a nonzero generator. The preceding calculation
shows that this generator does not belong to $\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}(S,I)$. Hence
$\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal S,\mathcal I)=0$.


For the second equality, extensions whose second argument belongs to $\mathcal I$ cannot be nonzero elements of $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}$. Hence only the extra object $M_{44}\in\mathcal V$ needs to be considered. The only relevant non-split exact sequence is
$$
  0\longrightarrow M_{44}\longrightarrow M_{24}
  \longrightarrow M_{23}\longrightarrow0.
$$
The nonzero map $M_{44}\to M_{34}$ does not extend across $M_{24}$ because
$$
  \operatorname{Hom}(M_{24},M_{34})=0.
$$
Therefore the inflation is not $\mathcal I$-monic, and $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}(\mathcal I,\mathcal V)=0$. This proves $(\mathrm{MT2})$.

We turn to $(\mathrm{RT3})$. Since $\mathcal Z=\mathcal I$, every $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}$-conflation whose first two terms belong to $\mathcal Z$ splits. Indeed, its first term belongs to $\mathcal I$, so the defining $\mathcal I$-monic condition forces the identity of that term to factor through the inflation. Consequently,
$$
  \operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Z)=\mathcal I.
$$
Dually,
$$
  \operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Z)=\mathcal I.
$$
The required two inclusions in $(\mathrm{RT3})$ now follow from split conflations.

It remains to check extension closure in the intersection relative structure. Let $S_1,S_2$ be indecomposable objects of $\mathcal S$. If $S_1\in\mathcal I$, then $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}(S_2,S_1)=0$, and if $S_2\in\mathcal I$, then $\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}(S_2,S_1)=0$. The only remaining pair is $(S_2,S_1)=(M_{22},M_{22})$, and
$$
 \operatorname{Ext}^1(M_{22},M_{22})=0.
$$
Hence $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal S,\mathcal S)=0$. By biadditivity, this equality holds for arbitrary finite direct sums and direct summands of the displayed indecomposables. Therefore every intersection-relative conflation with end terms in $\mathcal S$ splits, so $\mathcal S$ is extension closed. The same argument, using $\operatorname{Ext}^1(M_{44},M_{44})=0$, gives $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal V,\mathcal V)=0$. It is immediate that $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal Z,\mathcal Z)=0$. Thus the extension closure clause of $(\mathrm{RT3})$ holds.

Finally, consider $(\mathrm{RT4})$. The canonical epimorphism
$$
  M_{23}\longrightarrow M_{22}
$$
is a right $\mathcal I$-approximation. Its kernel is $M_{33}$. Indeed,
$$
  \operatorname{Hom}(M_{11},M_{22})=\operatorname{Hom}(M_{34},M_{22})=0
$$
and $\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}(M_{23},M_{22})=1$. Thus $\mathcal I$ is strongly contravariantly finite in $\mathcal S$. Dually, the canonical monomorphism
$$
  M_{44}\longrightarrow M_{34}
$$
is a left $\mathcal I$-approximation with cokernel $M_{33}$, and $\mathcal I$ is strongly covariantly finite in $\mathcal V$.

These approximation properties also determine the two relative shift subcategories. Let $f:I_0\to M_{22}$ be an $\mathcal I$-epic deflation with $I_0\in\mathcal I$. The relevant morphism spaces are
$$
 \operatorname{Hom}(M_{11},M_{22})=\operatorname{Hom}(M_{34},M_{22})=0,
 \qquad
 \dim_k\operatorname{Hom}(M_{23},M_{22})=1.
$$
Thus only a summand isomorphic to $M_{23}$ can map nontrivially to $M_{22}$. Since $f$ is $\mathcal I$-epic, at least one such component is nonzero. After an automorphism of the $M_{23}$-isotypic summand of $I_0$, the morphism $f$ is the canonical epimorphism $M_{23}\to M_{22}$ on one summand and zero on a complementary object of $\mathcal I$. Therefore
$$
 \ker f\cong M_{33}\oplus I'
 \quad\text{for some }I'\in\mathcal I.
$$
We briefly justify the passage to arbitrary objects of $\mathcal S$. If the target of an $\mathcal I$-epic deflation has a summand in $\mathcal I$, the identity of that summand lifts and the corresponding part splits off. It remains to consider a target $M_{22}^{\oplus n}$. After discarding source summands which map trivially, the deflation is represented by a matrix
$$
 M_{23}^{\oplus m}\longrightarrow M_{22}^{\oplus n}.
$$
The $\mathcal I$-epic property says that this matrix has rank $n$. Row and column operations reduce it to the direct sum of $n$ copies of the canonical epimorphism $M_{23}\to M_{22}$ and a zero map on a complementary object of $\mathcal I$. Its kernel therefore belongs to $\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(\mathcal I\oplus M_{33})$. Conversely, $M_{33}$ is obtained from the displayed approximation and each object of $\mathcal I$ is obtained from a split conflation. Hence
$$
  \mathcal S\langle-1\rangle=\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(\mathcal I\oplus M_{33}).
$$
The dual matrix argument applies to arbitrary objects of $\mathcal V$. Here
$$
 \dim_k\operatorname{Hom}(M_{44},M_{34})=1.
$$
It follows that
$$
  \mathcal V\langle1\rangle=\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(\mathcal I\oplus M_{33}).
$$
Thus $(\mathrm{RT4})$ holds, and $(\mathcal S,\mathcal Z,\mathcal V)$ is a reducible triple.

It remains to show that it is not premutation. Consider the non-split exact sequence
$$
  0\longrightarrow M_{22}\longrightarrow M_{12}
  \longrightarrow M_{11}\longrightarrow0.
$$
Since
$$
  \operatorname{Hom}(M_{22},\mathcal I)=0,
$$
its extension class belongs to $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}(M_{11},M_{22})$. Both end terms lie in $\mathcal S$, whereas $M_{12}\notin\mathcal S$. Therefore $\mathcal S$ is not extension closed in $(\mathcal C,\mathbb E^{\mathcal I},\mathfrak s^{\mathcal I})$. Condition $(\mathrm{MT3})$ fails, so the reducible triple is not a premutation triple and hence is not a mutation triple.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:reducible-question}
Iitsuka \cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.9]{Iitsuka} asks whether every reducible triple is a mutation triple. In Proposition~\ref{prop:reducible-counterexample}, we give a negative answer. In our example, the cone and cocone conditions in $(\mathrm{MT3})$ hold. The intersection relative extension closure required by $(\mathrm{RT3})$ also holds. However, extension closure in $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}$ fails. Thus the extension closure condition in Theorem~\ref{thm:reducible-application} is a genuine obstruction.

In Proposition~\ref{prop:reducible-counterexample}, we have $\mathcal Z=\mathcal I$, so the quotient $\underline{\mathcal Z}$ is zero. Iitsuka's question \cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.9]{Iitsuka} does not exclude this case. The next example shows that the same failure can occur when the quotient is nonzero.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:nonzero-reducible-counterexample}
There exists a reducible triple which is not a premutation triple and whose associated quotient is nonzero.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Let $\Gamma$ be the disjoint union of the linearly oriented quiver
$1\to2\to3\to4$ and the one-loop quiver with vertex $5$ and loop
$\varepsilon$. Put $A=k\Gamma/(\varepsilon^2)$. We work in $\mathcal C=\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}} A$ with
its standard exact structure. On the first component, we retain the interval modules $M_{ab}$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:reducible-counterexample}. On the second component, let
$L$ be the simple module and let $P$ be the indecomposable projective module.
The module $P$ has length two and occurs in the non-split
exact sequence
$$
  0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow0.
$$
The algebra supported at vertex $5$ is $R=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$. If
$t:R\to k$ sends $a+b\varepsilon$ to $b$, then
$$
 R\longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_k(R,k),
 \quad x\longmapsto \bigl(y\longmapsto t(yx)\bigr),
$$
is an isomorphism of $R$-bimodules. Thus $R$ is symmetric, and $P$ is
injective as well as projective.

Every module supported at vertex $5$ belongs to $\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(P\oplus L)$.

We now set
$$
 \mathcal I=\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(M_{11}\oplus M_{23}\oplus M_{34}\oplus P),
$$
$$
 \mathcal Z=\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(\mathcal I\oplus L),\quad
 \mathcal S=\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(\mathcal I\oplus M_{22}),\quad
 \mathcal V=\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(\mathcal I\oplus M_{44}).
$$
We verify that this is the required example. There are no morphisms or
extensions between modules supported on different connected components of
$\Gamma$. Consequently, all the following calculations separate according to
the support of the modules.

On the first component, we use the calculations in
Proposition~\ref{prop:reducible-counterexample} without change. On the
second component, the deflation $P\to L$ is a right $\mathcal I$-approximation, and
the inflation $L\to P$ is a left $\mathcal I$-approximation. The kernel of the first
map and the cokernel of the second are both $L$. Hence $\mathcal I$ is strongly
functorially finite in $\mathcal Z$, and
$$
  \mathcal Z\langle-1\rangle=\mathcal Z=\mathcal Z\langle1\rangle.
$$
Since $P$ is projective-injective, the four relative extension groups in
$(\mathrm{MT2})$ vanish on the second component. Combining this with the
verification in Proposition~\ref{prop:reducible-counterexample} proves
$(\mathrm{MT1})$ and $(\mathrm{MT2})$.

We next check $(\mathrm{RT3})$. The modules supported at vertex $5$ form the
extension closed subcategory $\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(P\oplus L)$. Moreover, projectivity and
injectivity of $P$ imply that every exact sequence on this component belongs to
both relative structures. Split exact sequences with an end term $P$ therefore
give
$$
 \operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(P\oplus L),\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(P\oplus L))
 =\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(P\oplus L)
 =\operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(P\oplus L),\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}} P),
$$
and, dually,
$$
 \operatorname{CoCone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E_{\mathcal I}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(P\oplus L),\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(P\oplus L))
 =\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(P\oplus L)
 =\operatorname{Cone\hspace{0.5pt}}_{\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}_{\mathcal I}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}} P,\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(P\oplus L)).
$$
Together with the first-component calculation in
Proposition~\ref{prop:reducible-counterexample}, these equalities prove the two
cone--cocone inclusions and the extension closure clause of $(\mathrm{RT3})$.

We still use the approximation maps $M_{23}\to M_{22}$ and $M_{44}\to M_{34}$
from Proposition~\ref{prop:reducible-counterexample}; they remain $\mathcal I$-approximations,
and their kernel and cokernel are both $M_{33}$. All the other indecomposable
objects of $\mathcal S$ and $\mathcal V$ belong to $\mathcal I$. Hence
$$
  \mathcal S\langle-1\rangle
  =\operatorname{\mathsf{add}\hspace{-0.8pt}}(\mathcal I\oplus M_{33})
  =\mathcal V\langle1\rangle.
$$
This proves $(\mathrm{RT4})$, so the triple is reducible.

The non-split exact sequence
$$
  0\longrightarrow M_{22}\longrightarrow M_{12}
  \longrightarrow M_{11}\longrightarrow0
$$
still belongs to $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}$. Indeed,
$\operatorname{Hom}(M_{22},M_{11}\oplus M_{23}\oplus M_{34})=0$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:reducible-counterexample}, and $\operatorname{Hom}(M_{22},P)=0$ because
the two modules have disjoint supports. Since $M_{12}\notin\mathcal S$, the
subcategory $\mathcal S$ is not extension closed in $\mathbb E^{\mathcal I}$. Thus the triple is
not a premutation triple.

Finally, $L$ is nonzero in $\mathcal Z/[\mathcal I]$. Maps between different components
vanish, and every composite
$$
  L\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow L
$$
is zero: the first map has image in the socle of $P$, whereas the second map
factors through the top of $P$. Thus $1_L$ does not factor through any object
of $\mathcal I$, and $\mathcal Z/[\mathcal I]\neq0$.
