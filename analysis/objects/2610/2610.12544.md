<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:tree}
    Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a rooted tree structure on the vertex set $[L]$, namely a connected, acyclic graph with a designated root $o\in [L]$.  
    For each $i\in [L]\setminus \{o\}$, denote $\operatorname{pa}(i)$ as its unique parent, which represents the edge $(i,\operatorname{pa}(i))$. 
    Then the tree structure $\mathcal{T}$ can be specified as
    \begin{align}
        \mathcal{T} := \big\{ \{o\}, \big(i,\operatorname{pa}(i)\big)_{i\in [L] \setminus \{o\}}\big\}. \label{eq:tree structure.}
    \end{align}
    In particular, repeatedly following the parent map from any nonroot vertex reaches $o$ after finite steps.
    We further denote $\mathfrak{T}$ as the set of all trees on $[L]$ and suppress its dependence on $L$.
                                                                                                                                                               

    For any discrete random vector $Z = (Z_1,\cdots,Z_L)$, we say that $Z$ follows a tree distribution if there exists a dependence tree $\mathcal{T} \in \mathfrak{T}$ such that the law of $Z$ admits the following factorization
    \begin{align}
        p_Z(z_1,\cdots,z_L) = p_o(z_o) \prod_{i\neq o} p_i(z_i \mid z_{\operatorname{pa}(i)}). \label{eq:follow dependence tree}
    \end{align}
    Here the root $o$ and parent map $\operatorname{pa}(i)$ correspond to tree structure $\mathcal{T}$. 
    We further say that $p_Z$ factorizes according to $\mathcal{T}$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:tree-mixtures}
                                                                                                                                                                                           
Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a rooted tree on the index set $[L]$, with root $o \in [L]$ and parent map $\operatorname{pa}(\cdot)$ for nonroot vertices as in Definition \ref{def:tree}.
A $J$-state \emph{hidden Markov tree} on $\mathcal{T}$ is a distribution $q$ on $\mathbb V^L$ of the form
\begin{align}
    q(x) = \sum_{z\in[J]^L} q(x,z) \qquad \text{with}\qquad  q(x,z) := \nu(z_o) \prod_{i\neq o} A_i\big(z_i, z_{\operatorname{pa}(i)}\big) \prod_{i=1}^L B_i(x_i, z_i). \label{eq:hidden-tree-law}
\end{align}
Here, $\nu$ is the root-state distribution on $[J]$. For hidden state $b\in[J]$, the transition kernel $A_i(\cdot,b)$ is a distribution on $[J]$ for $i\neq o$, and the emission kernel $B_i(\cdot,b)$ is a distribution on $\mathbb V$ for $i\in[L]$.

 
The class $\mathcal{M}_{J,R}$ consists of all mixtures of $R$ such models:
\[
    q(x)=\sum_{r=1}^R \pi_r q_r(x)
    \qquad \text{with} \qquad \pi_r\geq0,\quad \sum_{r=1}^R\pi_r=1,
\]
where each $q_r$ is a $J$-state hidden Markov tree with its own topology $\mathcal{T}_r$, root distribution, transition kernels, and emission kernels.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
For fixed component topologies, a distribution in the class $\mathcal{M}_{J,R}$ has $O\big(RLJ(J+V)\big)$ parameters.
This representation complexity can be much smaller than the support size: even a single component with strictly positive emission kernel has full support on $\mathbb V^L$.
Consequently, guarantees that scale with the support size may incur a dependence on $V^L$, even when the distribution admits a compact representation with small $J$ and $R$.
 
Mixtures enrich this representation by allowing components to differ in tree topology, transitions, and emissions \citep{meila2000mixtures}, while increasing the parameter count only linearly in $R$.
To illustrate this, suppose that all components share a common tree topology.
The mixture can then be represented as a single HMT with $RJ$ states with block diagonal transition matrices.
This structure requires only $O(LRJ^2)$ transition parameters, reduced from $O(LR^2J^2)$ for a generic $RJ$-state HMT with dense transitions.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:projection error}
    Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a class of probability distributions on $\mathbb V^L$. 
                                                     
    For any distribution $p$ on $\mathbb V^L$, define the KL approximation error w.r.t.~$\mathcal{F}$ by 
    \begin{align}\label{eq:projection error}
        \mathsf{Proj}_{\mathcal{F}}(p) := \inf_{q\in \mathcal{F}} D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p\|q\big). 
    \end{align}
    In particular, we write $\mathsf{Proj}_{J,R} := \mathsf{Proj}_{\mathcal{M}_{J,R}}$ for the MHMT class $\mathcal{M}_{J,R}$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:tree-mixture-estimation}

There exists a transformer class $\Theta$ of the form \eqref{eq:transformer predictor}, with an architecture depending only on $(n,L,V,J,R)$ (specified in Proposition \ref{prop:appendix-parallel-mixture} with $\delta=\varepsilon=1/n$), such that for any distribution $p^\star$ on $\mathbb V^L$, the ERM $\widehat{\theta}$ over $\Theta$ computed from $n$ i.i.d.~samples drawn from $p^\star$ satisfies
\begin{align}
    \mathbb E[\mathcal{L}(\widehat{\theta})] \leq C_{1} \bigg(\frac{s_{\mathsf{max}}}{L} \mathsf{Proj}_{J,R}(p^\star) + \frac{K_{\rm par}}{n}\, \mathsf{poly}\log(n,L,V,J,R) \bigg).  \label{eq:estimation error}
\end{align}
                                                                                                                                            
Here, $C_{1}>0$ is a universal constant, the expectation is over training samples, and 
\begin{align}\label{eq:Kpar}
K_{\rm par} := R[LJ(J+V)+J^3].
\end{align}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Theorem \ref{thm:tree-mixture-estimation} applies to arbitrary tree structures.
    When latent dependencies are organized into only a few hierarchical levels, component trees of bounded height provide a natural model.
    For such structure, Appendix \ref{app:shallow tree} gives a complementary construction for approximation and the resulting estimation and sampling guarantees. 
    Its architectural budgets replace the $J^3$ complexity term with $J^2$, with depth proportional to the tree height.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:end-to-end}
Assume the conditions and adopt the notation of Theorem \ref{thm:tree-mixture-estimation}.
                                                                                
Let $p_{Y\mid S}$ denote the output distribution of the $T$-step MDM sampler using the ERM-based mask predictor $p_{\widehat{\theta}}$ and the realized unmasking sets $\mathcal{S}=(S^1,\ldots,S^T)$ with $|S^t| = s_t$.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                  
                      
                                                                                                                               
Then for any $1\leq T \leq L$, one has 
\begin{align}
    & \mathbb E_{\{X^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^n,S} \big[D_{\mathrm{KL}} \big( p^\star \,\|\, p_{Y\mid S}\big)\big] \notag \\
    &\quad \leq C_{2}\bigg( \varepsilon_{\mathsf{parall}}(p^\star;s) + s_{\mathsf{max}} \mathsf{Proj}_{J,R}(p^\star) + \frac{L K_{\rm par}}{n}\, \mathsf{poly}\log (n,L,V,J,R)\bigg), \label{eq:sampling error}
\end{align}
where $C_{2}>0$ is a universal constant, and the expectation is over the training samples and random unmasking sets $S$.

\label{rem:parallel}

        \le \frac{6\log(eL)}{T}\,\mathsf{DTC}(p^\star). \label{eq:TC and DTC control for parallelization error}
    \end{align}

We approximate the latent variable by a finite grid. Let $s=\lfloor R^{1/k}\rfloor\geq1$, and partition $[0,1]^k$ into $s^k\leq R$ cubes $\{C_j\}_{j=1}^{s^k}$ of side length $1/s$, assigning their boundaries so that the cells are disjoint. Denote by $z_j$ the midpoint of $C_j$, and define
\[
    w_j:=\mu(C_j),\qquad
    q_R(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{s^k}w_j\prod_{i=1}^L p_i(x_i\mid z_j).
\]
Since each component is a product distribution, $q_R\in\mathcal M_{1,R}$. For $z\in[0,1]^k$, let $j(z)$ be the index of its cell. Consider the joint distributions
\[
    P(\mathrm{d} z,x):=\mu(\mathrm{d} z)\prod_{i=1}^L p_i(x_i\mid z),
    \qquad
    Q(\mathrm{d} z,x):=\mu(\mathrm{d} z)\prod_{i=1}^L p_i(x_i\mid z_{j(z)}).
\]
Their marginals on $x$ are $p^\star$ and $q_R$, respectively:
\begin{align*}
    \int_{[0,1]^k} Q(\mathrm{d} z,x) = \int_{[0,1]^k} \prod_{i=1}^L p_i(x_i\mid z_{j(z)})\,\mu(\mathrm{d} z) = \sum_{j=1}^{s^k} \int_{C_j} \prod_{i=1}^L p_i(x_i\mid z_{j})\,\mu(\mathrm{d} z) = q_R(x).
\end{align*}
By the data-processing inequality and the chain rule for KL divergence,
\begin{align*}
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^\star\, \| \, q_R)
    &\leq D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\, \|\, Q)\\
    &=\int_{[0,1]^k}\sum_{i=1}^L
      D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p_i(\cdot\mid z)\,\|\,p_i(\cdot\mid z_{j(z)})\big)\,\mu(\mathrm{d} z)\\
    &\leq LA^2\int_{[0,1]^k}\|z-z_{j(z)}\|_2^{2\alpha}\,\mu(\mathrm{d} z)\\
    &\leq LA^2\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{k}}{2s}\bigg)^{2\alpha}
     \leq k^\alpha LA^2R^{-2\alpha/k}.
\end{align*}
Here the last two inequalities control the maximum distance to the cell midpoint and $s\geq R^{1/k}/2$. Together with $\mathcal M_{1,R}\subseteq\mathcal M_{J,R}$, this proves \eqref{eq:cont latent repre proj error} with $C_{k,\alpha}=k^\alpha$.

It remains to verify the sufficient condition \eqref{eq:continuous latent repre sufficient for KL}. Let $F(a):=\log\sum_{v=1}^V e^{a_v}$. For $u=\operatorname{softmax}(a)$, we have
\[
    \nabla F(a)=u,\qquad
    \nabla^2F(a)=\operatorname{diag}(u)-uu^\top,
    \qquad \|\nabla^2F(a)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq1.
\]
For any $a,b\in\mathbb R^V$,
\begin{align*}
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\mathrm{softmax}(a)\,\|\,\operatorname{softmax}(b)\big)
    &= \sum_{v=1}^V \big(\mathrm{softmax}(a)_v\log\mathrm{softmax}(a)_v - \mathrm{softmax}(a)_v\log\mathrm{softmax}(b)_v\big)\\
    &=F(b)-F(a)-\nabla F(a)^\top(b-a)\\
    &\leq\frac12\|b-a\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
Taking $a=g_i(z)$ and $b=g_i(z')$, and applying \eqref{eq:continuous latent repre sufficient for KL} gives
\[
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p_i(\cdot\mid z)\,\|\,p_i(\cdot\mid z')\big)
    \leq\frac12\|g_i(z)-g_i(z')\|_2^2
    \leq A^2\|z-z'\|_2^{2\alpha}.
\]
This implies \eqref{eq:continuour latent repre KL condition} and completes the proof.

\label{lem:renyi-compression}
Let $p^\star$ be a distribution on $\mathbb V^L$ and $\alpha\in(0,1)$. For any integers $J\geq1$ and $R\geq2$, it holds that
\begin{align}
    \mathsf{Proj}_{J,R}(p^\star)\leq\mathsf{Proj}_{1,R}(p^\star)
    \leq L\log V\min\bigg\{1,
    \bigg(\frac{e^{H_\alpha(p^\star)}}{R}\bigg)^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}\bigg\}.
    \label{eq:renyi-error-bound}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- head-2: body_md -->
We maintain the $R-1$ largest atoms and approximate the remaining mass by a uniform distribution. Enumerate the points of $\mathbb V^L$ as $x_{(1)},\ldots,x_{(V^L)}$, with probabilities $p_{(j)}:=p^\star(x_{(j)})$ in decreasing order. Set $p_{(j)}=0$ for $j>V^L$, and let
\[
    S_\alpha:=\sum_{j\geq1}p_{(j)}^\alpha
    =e^{(1-\alpha)H_\alpha(p^\star)},\quad
    \tau_{R-1}:=\sum_{j\geq R}p_{(j)}.
\]
If $\tau_{R-1}=0$, then $p^\star$ is a mixture of at most $R-1$ point masses. Each point mass is a product distribution, so $\mathsf{Proj}_{1,R}(p^\star)=0$.

For $\tau_{R-1}>0$, let $u$ be the uniform distribution on $\mathbb V^L$, and define
\[
    q_R:=\sum_{j=1}^{R-1}p_{(j)}\delta_{x_{(j)}}+\tau_{R-1}u,
    \qquad
    v:=\frac{1}{\tau_{R-1}}\sum_{j=R}^{V^L}p_{(j)}\delta_{x_{(j)}}.
\]
Since Dirac measures and the uniform distribution are product distributions, $q_R\in\mathcal M_{1,R}$.
Furthermore, $p^\star=\sum_{j=1}^{R-1}p_{(j)}\delta_{x_{(j)}}+\tau_{R-1}v$. 
By the convexity of KL divergence,
\begin{align*}
    \mathsf{Proj}_{1,R}(p^\star)
    &\leq D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^\star\|q_R)
     \leq\tau_{R-1}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(v\|u)\\
    &=\tau_{R-1}\big(L\log V-H(v)\big)
     \leq\tau_{R-1}L\log V.
\end{align*}
To bound the remaining mass $\tau_{R-1}$, notice that
\[
    \tau_{R-1}
    =\sum_{j\geq R}p_{(j)}^\alpha p_{(j)}^{1-\alpha}
    \leq p_{(R)}^{1-\alpha}S_\alpha,
    \quad \text{and }
    R\, p_{(R)}^\alpha\leq\sum_{j=1}^R p_{(j)}^\alpha\leq S_\alpha.
\]
Therefore,
\[
    \tau_{R-1}\leq
    \bigg(\frac{S_\alpha}{R}\bigg)^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}S_\alpha
    =\bigg(\frac{e^{H_\alpha(p^\star)}}{R}\bigg)^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}.
\]
Combining this bound with $\tau_{R-1}\leq1$ and $\mathcal M_{1,R}\subseteq\mathcal M_{J,R}$ proves \eqref{eq:renyi-error-bound}.

<!-- head-3: body_md -->
We first control the change in the logits under a perturbation of the parameters, and then cover the sparse parameter space. Write $\|Z\|_{2,\infty}:=\max_i\|Z_{i,\cdot}\|_2$, and let $d_\Theta(\theta,\theta')$ be the largest Frobenius distance between corresponding parameter matrices, including $E,P$, and $W_{\mathrm{out}}$. Set
\[
    \overline B:=B\vee1,\qquad
    \overline R:=R_{\mathrm{embed}}\vee1,\qquad
    a:=eNM\overline B\,\overline R.
\]

For $\|Z\|_{2,\infty}\leq r$, the bounded attention activation gives
\begin{align*}
    \|\mathrm{head}_\theta(Z)_{i,\cdot}\|_2
    &\leq\frac1L\sum_{j=1}^L
      \big|\sigma_{\mathrm{attn}}
        (\langle Z_{i,\cdot}Q,Z_{j,\cdot}K\rangle)\big|
      \|Z_{j,\cdot}V\|_2
    \leq Br.
\end{align*}
Hence the attention layer maps the row radius $r$ to at most $(1+MB)r$. The feed-forward layer multiplies this radius by at most $1+B^2$, since ReLU is one-Lipschitz and vanishes at zero. Denoting the hidden states by $H_\theta^{(\ell)}(x)$, with $H_\theta^{(0)}(x)=\mathsf{Embed}_{E,P}(x)$, we obtain
\[
    \|H_\theta^{(\ell)}(x)\|_{2,\infty}
    \leq R_{\mathrm{embed}}\big[(1+MB)(1+B^2)\big]^\ell
    \leq a^{cN},\qquad 0\leq\ell\leq N,
\]
uniformly over $x$ and $\theta\in\Theta$.

Next, let $\Delta:=d_\Theta(\theta,\theta')$. The trainable embeddings satisfy
\[
    \|H_\theta^{(0)}(x)-H_{\theta'}^{(0)}(x)\|_{2,\infty}
    \leq\|E-E'\|_{\mathrm F}+\|P-P'\|_{\mathrm F}
    \leq2\Delta.
\]
For one attention head, the sigmoid activation is $1/4$-Lipschitz. For inputs $Z,Z'$ with row norms at most $r$, we have
\begin{align*}
    \|\mathrm{head}_\theta(Z)
      -\mathrm{head}_\theta(Z')\|_{2,\infty}
    &\leq\big(B+\tfrac12 B^3r^2\big)
           \|Z-Z'\|_{2,\infty},\\
    \|\mathrm{head}_\theta(Z)
      -\mathrm{head}_{\theta'}(Z)\|_{2,\infty}
    &\leq\big(r+\tfrac12 B^2r^3\big)\Delta.
\end{align*}
For the feed-forward layer, the corresponding bounds are
\begin{align*}
    \|\mathsf{FFN}_\theta(Z)
      -\mathsf{FFN}_\theta(Z')\|_{2,\infty}
    &\leq(1+B^2)\|Z-Z'\|_{2,\infty},\\
    \|\mathsf{FFN}_\theta(Z)
      -\mathsf{FFN}_{\theta'}(Z)\|_{2,\infty}
    &\leq2Br\Delta.
\end{align*}
Combining these bounds with the hidden-state bound gives
\[
    \|H_\theta^{(\ell)}(x)-H_{\theta'}^{(\ell)}(x)\|_{2,\infty}
    \leq a^{cN}\big(
       \|H_\theta^{(\ell-1)}(x)-H_{\theta'}^{(\ell-1)}(x)\|_{2,\infty}
       +\Delta\big).
\]
Starting from the embedding perturbation bound and iterating over $N$ blocks, we obtain
\[
    \|H_\theta^{(N)}(x)-H_{\theta'}^{(N)}(x)\|_{2,\infty}
    \leq a^{cN^2}\Delta,
\]
after increasing the universal constant $c$.

For the output embedding, let $\|\cdot\|_{\infty,\infty}$ denote the largest absolute matrix entry. Then
\begin{align*}
    &\|H_\theta^{(N)}(x)W_{\mathrm{out}}
       -H_{\theta'}^{(N)}(x)W'_{\mathrm{out}}\|_{\infty,\infty}\\
    &\qquad\leq
       B\|H_\theta^{(N)}(x)-H_{\theta'}^{(N)}(x)\|_{2,\infty}
       +\|H_{\theta'}^{(N)}(x)\|_{2,\infty}
        \|W_{\mathrm{out}}-W'_{\mathrm{out}}\|_{\mathrm F}\\
    &\qquad\leq a^{cN^2}\Delta.
\end{align*}
Since clipping is one-Lipschitz, taking the supremum over masked sequences yields
\[
    d_\infty(F_\theta,F_{\theta'})
    \leq\Lambda d_\Theta(\theta,\theta'),
    \qquad \Lambda:=a^{cN^2}.
\]

It remains to cover the parameters. The input and positional embeddings have $(V+1)D+LD$ entries, each transformer block has $3MD^2+2DD_{\mathrm{ff}}$ entries, and the output embedding has $DV$ entries. This gives the stated value of $P_{\mathrm{tr}}$.

For a fixed support $\mathcal I\subseteq[P_{\mathrm{tr}}]$ of size $S$, denote
\[
    \Theta_{\mathcal I}
    :=\{\theta\in\Theta:\mathsf{supp}(\theta)\subseteq\mathcal I\}.
\]
Ignoring the embedded-input constraint temporarily, the parameters on this support lie in a product of Frobenius balls of radius $B$, with total dimension $S$. Covering each ball at radius $\zeta/2$ gives a product cover of size at most $(1+4B/\zeta)^S$ in the maximum Frobenius distance. For each ball intersecting $\Theta_{\mathcal I}$, choose one parameter from the intersection as its new center. The radius is then at most $\zeta$, and every center satisfies all constraints, including the embedded-input bound. Consequently,
\[
    \log\mathcal N(\zeta,\Theta_{\mathcal I},d_\Theta)
    \leq S\log\bigg(1+\frac{4B}{\zeta}\bigg).
\]
Every parameter with at most $S$ nonzero entries belongs to one of these support classes. Taking the union over supports gives
\begin{align*}
    \log\mathcal N(\zeta,\Theta,d_\Theta)
    &\leq\log\binom{P_{\mathrm{tr}}}{S}
       +S\log\bigg(1+\frac{4B}{\zeta}\bigg)\\
    &\leq S\log\frac{eP_{\mathrm{tr}}}{S}
       +S\log\bigg(1+\frac{4B}{\zeta}\bigg).
\end{align*}
Take $\zeta=\eta/\Lambda$ and $A_{\mathrm{TF}}:=e(1+4B\Lambda)$. Transferring the parameter cover to the logits proves \eqref{eq:sparse-cover}, and
\[
    \log A_{\mathrm{TF}}
    \leq CN^2\log\big(eNM(B\vee1)(R_{\mathrm{embed}}\vee1)\big).
\]
Since $A_{\mathrm{TF}}\geq e$ and $0<\eta\leq1$, the covering bound is at most $C(\Theta)\log(e/\eta)$. The logits already lie in $[-G,0]$, so both parts of Assumption~\ref{ass:class} hold.

Finally, softmax is one-Lipschitz in the maximum norm. Applying it rowwise therefore transfers the same cover to the predictor class. This completes the proof.

\label{lem:regularization}
Let $0<\delta<1$, $\rho_\delta:=\delta/V$, and $G_\delta:=\log(V/\delta)$. The following properties hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item If $q=\sum_{r=1}^R\pi_rq_r\in\mathcal M_{J,R}$, then
    \begin{align}
        q^\delta=\sum_{r=1}^R\pi_rq_r^\delta\in\mathcal M_{J,R},
        \quad
        B_{i,r}^\delta(v,z)
        =(1-\delta)B_{i,r}(v,z)+\frac{\delta}{V}.
        \label{eq:refresh-mixture}
    \end{align}
    Here $q_r^\delta=P_\delta q_r$ is obtained by replacing the emission tables $B_{i,r}$ with $B_{i,r}^\delta$ as in \eqref{eq:hidden-tree-law}. 
    The root distributions, transitions, component trees, and mixture weights are unchanged.

    \item For any distribution $q$ on $\mathbb V^L$, any $y\in\mathbb V^L$, and any masked sequence $c\in\overline{\mathbb V}^L$,
    it holds that:
    \begin{align}
        q^\delta(y) \geq\rho_\delta^L,\quad  q^\delta(y)\geq(1-\delta)^Lq(y),\quad q_i^\delta(v\mid c)\geq\rho_\delta, \quad \forall i\notin W(c),\text{ and } v\in\mathbb V.
        \label{eq:refresh-positive}
    \end{align}
    Here $W(c):=\{j\in[L]:c_j\ne\mathtt M\}$ and
    $q_i^\delta(v\mid c):=\mathbb P_{X\sim q^\delta}[X_i=v\mid X_{W(c)}=c_{W(c)}]$.
    Hence, $-G_{\delta}\le \log q_i^{\delta}(v\mid c) \le 0$. 

    \item For any distributions $p,q$ on $\mathbb V^L$,
    \begin{align}
        D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q^\delta)
        \leqD_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)+L\log\frac1{1-\delta}.

    \end{align}
\end{enumerate}

For part (i), fix a representation $q=\sum_{r=1}^R\pi_rq_r$ as in Section~\ref{sec:problem setup}. Notice that
\[
    \sum_{a\in\mathbb V}B_{i,r}(a,z)Q_\delta(a,v)
    =(1-\delta)B_{i,r}(v,z)+\frac\delta V
    =B_{i,r}^\delta(v,z).
\]
Substituting the hidden-tree factorization into \eqref{eq:refresh} gives
\begin{align}
    (P_\delta q_r)(y)
    &=\sum_{z\in[J]^L}\nu_r(z_{o_r})
      \prod_{i\ne o_r}A_{i,r}(z_i,z_{\operatorname{pa}_r(i)})
      \prod_{i=1}^L\bigg[\sum_{a\in\mathbb V}
          B_{i,r}(a,z_i)Q_\delta(a,y_i)\bigg]\notag\\
    &=\sum_{z\in[J]^L}\nu_r(z_{o_r})
      \prod_{i\ne o_r}A_{i,r}(z_i,z_{\operatorname{pa}_r(i)})
      \prod_{i=1}^L B_{i,r}^\delta(y_i,z_i)
      =q_r^\delta(y).
    \label{eq:proof-refresh-factorization}
\end{align}
Thus, regularization changes only the emission tables. By linearity, $q^\delta=\sum_{r=1}^R\pi_rq_r^\delta\in\mathcal M_{J,R}$. Since \eqref{eq:refresh} is defined using the observed law $q$, the resulting law does not depend on the chosen hidden-tree representation.

For part (ii), the bounds $Q_\delta(a,b)\geq\rho_\delta$ and $Q_\delta(a,a)\geq1-\delta$ imply
\begin{align}
    q^\delta(y)
    &\geq\sum_{x\in\mathbb V^L}q(x)\rho_\delta^L
     =\rho_\delta^L,
    \label{eq:proof-refresh-full-support}\\
    q^\delta(y)
    &\geq q(y)\prod_{i=1}^LQ_\delta(y_i,y_i)
     \geq(1-\delta)^Lq(y).
    \label{eq:proof-refresh-domination}
\end{align}
Fix a masked sequence $c$ and write $W=W(c)$. Summing over the coordinates outside $W$ gives
\[
    \mathbb P_{X\sim q^\delta}[X_W=c_W]
    =\sum_{x\in\mathbb V^L}q(x)\prod_{j\in W}Q_\delta(x_j,c_j)
    \geq\rho_\delta^{|W|}>0.
\]
Hence, for $i\notin W$ and $v\in\mathbb V$,
\[
    q_i^\delta(v\mid c)
    =\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb V^L}q(x)
        Q_\delta(x_i,v)\prod_{j\in W}Q_\delta(x_j,c_j)}
       {\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb V^L}q(x)
        \prod_{j\in W}Q_\delta(x_j,c_j)}
    \geq\rho_\delta.
\]
                                                                            

For part (iii), the result is immediate if $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)=+\infty$. Otherwise, applying \eqref{eq:proof-refresh-domination} yields
\begin{align*}
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q^\delta)-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)
    &=\sum_{y:p(y)>0}p(y)\log\frac{q(y)}{q^\delta(y)}\\
    &\leq L\log\frac1{1-\delta}.
\end{align*}
This proves the lemma. 
In particular, regularization also preserves any restriction on the component tree topologies, including a prescribed height bound.

Fix $q$ and write $r=q^\delta$. Let $r_{\mathrm{cond}}$ denote its conditional predictor on masked positions. 
We use the same training experiment as in Section~\ref{sec:prelim}: $\mathbb P[\tau=t]=s_t/L$, $M^\tau$ is uniform among subsets of size $m_\tau:=\sum_{u=1}^\tau s_u$, and $I$ is uniform in $M^\tau$.

As in Section \ref{sec:prelim}, $X_{W^\tau}$ denotes the sequence obtained by replacing all tokens outside $W^\tau$ with $\mathtt M$.
Define
                                                                                                                                  
\[
    \mathcal L(r_{\mathrm{cond}})
    :=\mathbb E\big[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p_I^\star(\cdot\mid X_{W^\tau})
         \,\|\,r_I(\cdot\mid X_{W^\tau})\big)\big].
\]
                                             
By Lemma~\ref{lem:kl}, adding and subtracting the log conditional probability under $r$ gives
\begin{align}
    \mathcal L(\theta)
    &=\mathbb E\bigg[\log\frac{p_I^\star(X_I\mid X_{W^\tau})}
                                 {p_{\theta,I}(X_I\mid X_{W^\tau})}\bigg]\notag\\
    &=\mathcal L(r_{\mathrm{cond}})
      +\mathbb E\bigg[\log\frac{r_I(X_I\mid X_{W^\tau})}
                              {p_{\theta,I}(X_I\mid X_{W^\tau})}\bigg].
    \label{eq:proof-exact-mixture-network-split}
\end{align}
For any $f,g\in\mathbb R^V$, the log-sum-exp function is one-Lipschitz in the maximum norm. Hence,
\begin{align}
    |\log\mathrm{softmax}_v(f)-\log\mathrm{softmax}_v(g)|
    &\leq |f_v-g_v|
       +\bigg|\log\sum_{a=1}^V e^{f_a}
              -\log\sum_{a=1}^V e^{g_a}\bigg|\notag\\
    &\leq2\|f-g\|_\infty.
    \label{eq:proof-logsoftmax-lipschitz}
\end{align}
Since $r_i(\cdot\mid x)=\mathrm{softmax}(F_{i,\cdot}^{\delta,q}(x))$ for $i\notin W(x)$, applying this bound in \eqref{eq:proof-exact-mixture-network-split} and taking the infimum over $\theta\in\Theta$ yields
\[
    \varepsilon_{\mathsf{approx}}(\Theta)
    \leq\mathcal L(r_{\mathrm{cond}})
       +2d_{\infty,\mathrm{mask}}(F^{\delta,q},\mathcal F(\Theta)).
\]

It remains to control $\mathcal L(r_{\mathrm{cond}})$ by $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^\star\|r)$. We use a subset-averaging argument, analogous to the entropy calculation in \citet[proof of Theorem~3.3]{chen2025optimal}. For $W\subseteq[L]$, denote by $p_W^\star$ and $r_W$ the corresponding marginals. Let
\[
    \overline d_j
    :=\binom Lj^{-1}\sum_{\substack{W\subseteq[L]\\|W|=j}}
       D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_W^\star\|r_W),\quad \forall \, 0\leq j\leq L,
\]
and write $\Delta_j:=\overline d_{j+1}-\overline d_j$ for $0\leq j<L$. By the KL chain rule, for $i\notin W$,
\begin{align*}
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{W\cup\{i\}}^\star\|r_{W\cup\{i\}})
    -D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_W^\star\|r_W)
    =\mathbb E_{X_W\sim p_W^\star}
      \big[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p_i^\star(\cdot\mid X_W)
           \,\|\,r_i(\cdot\mid X_W)\big)\big].
\end{align*}
If $W$ is a uniform $j$-subset and $I$ is uniform in $[L]\setminus W$, then $W\cup\{I\}$ is a uniform $(j+1)$-subset. Averaging the preceding identity gives
\begin{align}
    \mathbb E_{W,I,X_W}\big[
        D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p_I^\star(\cdot\mid X_W)
           \,\|\,r_I(\cdot\mid X_W)\big)\big]
    =\Delta_j\geq0.
    \label{eq:proof-kl-level-increment}
\end{align}
All these divergences are finite because $r$ has full support. Since $\overline d_0=0$ and $\overline d_L=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^\star\|r)$,
\begin{align}
    \sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\Delta_j=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^\star\|r).
    \label{eq:proof-kl-increments-telescope}
\end{align}
At training time $t$, the context set $W^t$ is uniform of size $L-m_t$, where $m_t=\sum_{u=1}^t s_u$. 
Therefore,
\begin{align}
    \mathcal L(r_{\mathrm{cond}})
    =\frac1L\sum_{t=1}^T s_t\Delta_{L-m_t}
    \leq\frac{s_{\max}}{L}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\Delta_j
     =\frac{s_{\max}}{L}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^\star\|r).
    \label{eq:proof-general-schedule-kl}
\end{align}
Here we use $\Delta_j\geq0$ and the fact that the indices $L-m_t$ are distinct, since $s_t\geq1$. Combining this with the network approximation bound proves \eqref{eq:dist-net}.

Under the uniform approximation condition, Lemma~\ref{lem:regularization}(iii) further gives, for every $q\in\mathcal M_{J,R}$,
\begin{align*}
    \varepsilon_{\mathsf{approx}}(\Theta)
    &\leq\frac{s_{\max}}{L}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^\star\|q^\delta)+2\epsilon\\
    &\leq\frac{s_{\max}}{L}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^\star\|q)
       +s_{\max}\log\frac1{1-\delta}+2\epsilon.
\end{align*}
Taking the infimum over $q\in\mathcal M_{J,R}$ proves \eqref{eq:approx-oracle}. The same argument applies when this infimum is restricted to $\mathcal Q$.

 \label{eq:message-identities-joint}

Write the ReLU network as
\[
z_0=x,\quad
z_\ell=\operatorname{ReLU}(A_\ell z_{\ell-1}+b_\ell)
\quad (1\le\ell<d), \quad z_d = A_d z_{d-1}+b_d. 
\]
and pad all layer vectors to dimension $W$.
Set all attention updates to zero, and reserve two groups
of $W$ auxiliary coordinates together with a constant coordinate.

Suppose the current row is $h=(z^\top,v^\top,1)$, where
$z$ contains the current NN input and $v$ contains the old
values in the destination coordinates. To implement
$z\mapsto\operatorname{ReLU}(Az+b)$, choose
\[
W_{\rm TF}=
\begin{pmatrix}
A^\top&0&0\\
0&I_W&-I_W\\
b^\top&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U_{\rm TF}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&I_W&0\\
0&-I_W&0\\
0&I_W&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then the residual FFN satisfies
\begin{align*}
h+\operatorname{ReLU}(hW_{\rm TF})U_{\rm TF}
&=\big(z^\top,\,
[v+\operatorname{ReLU}(Az+b)-\operatorname{ReLU}(v)+\operatorname{ReLU}(-v)]^\top,\,1\big)\\
&=\big(z^\top,\operatorname{ReLU}(Az+b)^\top,1\big).
\end{align*}
Thus one block writes the next hidden layer exactly.
Alternating the source and destination groups implements
successive layers:
\[
(z_0,0)\longmapsto(z_0,z_1)
\longmapsto(z_2,z_1)\longmapsto(z_2,z_3)
\longmapsto\cdots.
\]

For the final affine layer, take $r=Az+b$ and use four
groups of hidden activations to produce
\[
\Delta v
=\operatorname{ReLU}(r)-\operatorname{ReLU}(-r)-\operatorname{ReLU}(v)+\operatorname{ReLU}(-v)
=r-v.
\]
The residual update therefore gives $v+\Delta v=Az+b$.

To count parameters, let
$s_\ell=\|A_\ell\|_0+\|b_\ell\|_0$.
For each hidden layer, the displayed matrices have
\[
\|W_{\rm{TF},\ell}\|_0=s_\ell+2W,\quad
\|U_{\rm{TF},\ell}\|_0=3W,
\]
while the final affine layer uses at most $2s_d+6W$
nonzero coefficients. Including the initial input copy
and final coordinate selection yields
\[
S_{\rm TF}\le C\big(S+(d+1)W\big).
\]
The construction uses $O(d+1)$ blocks, auxiliary
dimension $O(W)$, and FFN width at most $4W$.
Moreover, for each hidden-layer block,
\[
\|W_{\rm{TF},\ell}\|_F^2
=\|A_\ell\|_F^2+\|b_\ell\|_2^2+2W
\le (W^2+W)B^2+2W,
\quad
\|U_{\rm{TF},\ell}\|_F^2=3W.
\]
The final block satisfies the same bounds up to constants,
so every FFN matrix has Frobenius norm at most
$C(W+1)(B+1)$.
For parallel networks, use disjoint auxiliary and FFN
coordinates; sparsities, auxiliary dimensions, and
squared Frobenius norms then add across branches.

We first write the parallelization error in terms of average mutual information. For $j\in[L]$, define
\[
    Z_j:=\mathbb E_{\substack{|W|=j-1\\i\notin W}}\big[I(X_i;X_W)\big],
    \qquad d_r:=Z_{r+1}-Z_r,\quad r\in[L-1],
\]
where $I(\cdot;\cdot)$ denotes the mutual information, $(W,i)$ is uniform among pairs of the indicated sizes and $X \sim p^\star$. 
For a fixed $i$, coupling nested uniform subsets of $[L]\setminus\{i\}$ gives $I(X_i;X_W)\leq I(X_i;X_{W'})$ whenever $W\subseteq W'$. Averaging over $i$ therefore gives $0=Z_1\leq\cdots\leq Z_L$, so $d_r\geq0$.

Let $H_j$ be the average of $H(X_W)$ over uniform subsets $W\subseteq[L]$ of size $j$, with $H_0=0$. Then by the connection between entropy and mutual information,
\begin{align}
    Z_j = \mathbb E_{\substack{|W|=j-1\\i\notin W}}\big[H(X_i)+ H(X_W) - H(X_{W\cup\{i\}}) \big]  = H_1 + H_{j-1} - H_j. \notag
\end{align}
Moreover, 
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{j=1}^L Z_j=LH_1-H_L=\mathsf{TC}(p^\star), \quad LZ_L=\sum_{i=1}^L I(X_i;X_{[L]\setminus\{i\}})=\mathsf{TC}(p^\star)+\mathsf{DTC}(p^\star). 
\end{align*}
Therefore, 
\begin{align}
    \mathsf{TC}(p^\star)=\sum_{r=1}^{L-1}(L-r)d_r,
    \qquad
    \mathsf{DTC}(p^\star)=\sum_{r=1}^{L-1}r\,d_r.
    \label{eq:tc-dtc-increments}
\end{align}

\begin{align}

\end{align}

\end{align}
