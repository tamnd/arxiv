<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm: Wang--Zhu}
Let $4 \le n \le 7$. Suppose $X^n$ is a connected orientable noncompact $n$-manifold that admits a complete metric of uniformly positive scalar curvature. Then $X$ admits an exhaustion by compact smooth codimension-zero submanifolds $K_1 \subset K_2 \subset \dots \subset X$ and a complete metric $g$ of uniformly positive scalar curvature such that near each hypersurface $\Sigma_i \coloneq  \partial K_i$, the metric is of product form $g = g_{\Sigma_i} + dt^2$.

\label{thm: dim 4}
Let $M$ be a closed, connected, oriented, smooth $4$-manifold with $b_2^+(M)>1$, and suppose the Seiberg--Witten invariant $\operatorname{sw}_M(\mathfrak s)\neq 0$ for some $Spin^{c}$ structure $\mathfrak s$. Then $\operatorname{Int} X$ doesn't admit a complete metric of uniformly positive scalar curvature.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor: K3}
Let $M$ be a closed smooth oriented 4-manifold which is homotopy equivalent to a K3 surface. Then $M \setminus \{p\}$ does not admit a complete metric of uniformly positive scalar curvature.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
Take any smooth homotopy K3 manifold $K$ and for $r \ge 1$ consider its $r$-fold blowup $M = K\#_r\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2}$. Then $M \setminus \{p\}$ does not admit a complete metric of uniformly positive scalar curvature.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->

Note that such obstructions on punctured manifolds don't extend to connected sums in general, even when the summands are closed. Indeed, let $M$ be an oriented K3 manifold with the standard smooth structure, and we write $M^{\mathrm{op}}$ for $M$ with the opposite orientation. Then $M \# M^{\mathrm{op}} \cong \#_{22} (S^2 \times S^2)$ (see e.g. \cite[p. 558]{Reutter2023Semisimple}), and hence admits a metric of positive scalar curvature.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem: torsion}
Let $W^* = W \cup_Y (Y\times [0,\infty))$ be a cylindrical $4$-manifold with a Riemannian metric $g_{W^*}$ that restricts to the product metric $g_Y + dt^2$ on the cylindrical end. Let $\mathfrak s_{W^*}$ be a $Spin^c$ structure on $W^*$ and let $L \coloneq \det(\mathfrak s_{W^*})$ be its determinant line bundle. If $A$ is a unitary connection on $L$ whose curvature $F_A$ is square-integrable on $W^*$, then, for every $t\ge 0$, $c_1 (L|_{Y \times \{t\}})\in H^2(Y;\mathbb Z)$ is a torsion class.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $L_t=L|_{Y \times \{t\}}$ for each $t\ge 0$. All $L_t$ are topologically isomorphic, so $c:=c_1(L_t) \in H^2(Y;\mathbb Z)$ is independent of $t$. Write $\beta_t:=F_A|_{Y\times\{t\}}$, then $F_A=\beta_t+dt\wedge \alpha_t$ where
$\alpha_t\in i\Omega^1(Y)$.
The 2-form $\beta_t$ is closed and $\left[\frac{i}{2\pi}\beta_t\right]=c_{\mathbb R}\in H^2(Y;\mathbb R)$. If $c$ is not torsion, then $c_\mathbb{R} \neq 0$, so we can find a nonzero harmonic representative $h$ of $c_\mathbb{R}$ by Hodge theory. Then $\|\beta_t\|_{L^2(Y)}^2
\ge \|2\pi \,h\|_{L^2(Y)}^2 =:C>0$. Then integration yields 
$$\int_{Y\times[0,\infty)}|F_A|^2 dV = \int_0^\infty\int_Y
\bigl(|\beta_t|^2+|\alpha_t|^2\bigr)\,dV_Y\,dt
\ge
\int_0^\infty\|\beta_t\|_{L^2(Y)}^2\,dt
\ge
\int_0^\infty C\,dt
=\infty,$$
contradicting $F_A\in L^2$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
Let $W$ be a compact connected oriented $4$-manifold with $b_2^+(W)>0$, carrying a metric of positive scalar curvature that is a product near its boundary. Let $W^*$ be its cylindrical completion. For every fixed $Spin^c$ structure, there exists a smooth, compactly supported real 2-form $\eta$ such that no finite-energy $(\mathfrak s_{W^*}, \eta)$-monopoles exist on $W^*$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By our assumption there exists $R_0 > 0$ such that $R \ge R_0$ on $W^*$. Let $\eta \in \Omega^2_c(W^*)$ be an arbitrary compactly supported real 2-form such that $\|\eta\|_{L^\infty} \le \frac{R_0}{8}$. First we show that uniformly positive scalar curvature excludes irreducible solutions through the Weitzenb\"ock formula. 

Let $(\Phi, A)$ be a finite-energy $(\mathfrak s_{W^*}, \eta)$-monopole on $W^*$. It follows from \eqref{eq: energy}, $R \ge R_0>0 $ and $\eta \in \Omega^2_c(W^*)$ that $E_\eta(\Phi, A) < \infty$ implies $\int_{W^*}|\Phi|^2< \infty$ and $\int_{W^*} |F_A|^2 < \infty$.

Let $\chi \colon \mathbb{R} \to [0,1]$ be a smooth cutoff function such that $\chi(t) \equiv 1$ for $t \le 0$, and $\chi(t) \equiv 0$ for $t \ge 1$.

For each $L \ge 0$, let $\chi_L \colon W^* \to [0,1]$ be the cutoff function such that $\chi_L (t, y) = \chi(t-L)$ on $[L, L+1] \times Y$, $\chi_L \equiv 1$ on $W\cup(Y\times[0,L])$, and $\chi_L \equiv 0$ on $Y\times[L+1,\infty)$. Thus there exists $C >0$ independent of $L$ such that $|d\chi_L| \le C$.

By the Weitzenb\"ock formula \eqref{eq: Weitzenbock}, the Seiberg--Witten equations \eqref{eq: SW}, and \eqref{eq: q},
\begin{align*}
0 & = \slashed{D}_A^{\,*}\slashed{D}_A\Phi \\
& =(\nabla^A)^*\nabla^A\Phi
+\frac{R_g}{4}\Phi
+\frac12\mathbf{c}(F_A^+)\Phi \\
& =(\nabla^A)^*\nabla^A\Phi
+\frac{R_g}{4}\Phi
+\frac12( \frac12q(\Phi) - \mathbf{c}(i \eta^+))\Phi \\
& =(\nabla^A)^*\nabla^A\Phi
+\frac{R_g}{4}\Phi
+ \frac18 |\Phi|^2\Phi 
- \frac12\mathbf{c}(i\eta^+)\Phi.
\end{align*}

Taking the real part of the $L^2$ inner product with $\chi_L^2 \Phi$ gives

\begin{align*}
0   & =   \Re \int_{W^*}   \langle \big((\nabla^A)^*\nabla^A\Phi
+\frac{R_g}{4}\Phi
+ \frac18 |\Phi|^2\Phi 
- \frac12\mathbf{c}(i\eta^+)\Phi \big), \,  \chi_L^2 \Phi \rangle dV_g\\
& =  \Re \int_{W^*}  \chi_L^2|\nabla^A\Phi|^2 + 2  \chi_L \langle \nabla^A \Phi, d \chi_L \otimes \Phi\rangle 
+\frac{R_g}{4}\chi_L^2|\Phi|^2 + \frac{1}{8} \chi_L^2 |\Phi|^4 
 \\
  & \quad - \frac12 \chi_L^2\langle \mathbf{c}(i\eta^+)\Phi, \Phi \rangle \\
  & \ge  \int_{W^*} - |d\chi_L|^2|\Phi|^2
+\frac{R_0}{4}\chi_L^2|\Phi|^2 + \frac{1}{8} \chi_L^2 |\Phi|^4 - \frac{R_0}{8} \chi_L^2 |\Phi|^2 \\
& \ge   - C\int_{[L, L+1] \times Y}|\Phi|^2
+\frac{R_0}{8} \int_{W^*}\chi_L^2|\Phi|^2   \\
 & \xrightarrow{L\to \infty} \frac{R_0}{8} \int_{W^*}|\Phi|^2.
\end{align*}
This shows for a solution $(\Phi, A)$, we must have $\Phi \equiv 0$.


Thus all solutions to \eqref{eq: SW} are reducible. Then \cite[Proposition 4.4.1]{Nicolaescu2000SeibergWitten} and \cite[Corollary 27.2.3]{KronheimerMrowka2007Monopoles}  further show that using $b_2^+(W) > 0$, we can choose a sufficiently small perturbation $\eta$ such that no solutions exist. Here for completeness we briefly sketch the argument. Since all solutions to \eqref{eq: SW} are reducible, we must have $F_A^+ = - i\eta^+$. By cylindrical Hodge theory, since $b_2^+(W) > 0$, there exists a nonzero square-integrable closed self-dual real 2-form $\kappa$ on $W^*$. By Lemma \ref{lem: torsion}, $F_A \in L^2$ implies that $c_1(\det \mathfrak s_{W^*})$ restricts to a torsion class on every cross-section of the end cylinder. Then by \cite[Lemma 27.2.1]{KronheimerMrowka2007Monopoles} we must have 
$$\int_{W^*} - i\eta^+ \wedge \kappa = \int_{W^*} F_A^+ \wedge \kappa = -2\pi i \langle c_1(\det \mathfrak s_{W^*}), [\kappa] \rangle.
$$
Here the pairing on the right-hand side is computed using a compactly supported closed representative of the real Chern class, and is independent of $\eta$.
We choose $L$ so that $\int_{W^*}\chi_L|\kappa|^2>0$.
Taking $\eta=\varepsilon\chi_L\kappa$, there is a unique $\varepsilon$ for the the above identity holds. We thus choose $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small so that $\|\eta\|_{L^\infty} \le \frac{R_0}{8}$, and $\varepsilon$ avoids that unique value. Then no solutions to the perturbed Seiberg--Witten equations \eqref{eq: SW} exist with this choice of $\eta$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop: neck}
Let $M$ be a closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold with $b_2^+(M)>1$, and suppose $\operatorname{sw}_M(\mathfrak s)\neq 0$ for some $Spin^{c}$ structure $\mathfrak s$. Suppose $W \subset M$ is a compact codimension-zero submanifold with smooth boundary $Y$, and $W$ carries a metric of positive scalar curvature $g$ that is a product $g_Y + dt^2$ near its boundary. Let $(W^*, g^*)$ be its cylindrical completion and denote the product extension of $\mathfrak s|_W$ to $W^*$ by $\mathfrak s_{W^*}$. Then for any compactly supported real 2-form $\eta \in \Omega^2_c(W^*)$, there exists a smooth finite energy $(\mathfrak s_{W^*}, \eta)$-monopole on $W^*$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Write $W^*= W \cup_Y (Y \times [0,\infty))$. Enlarging the compact core $W$ by a finite cylindrical
segment and retaining the notation $W$, we may assume $\eta \in  \Omega^2_c(W)$.  


Let $V\coloneq  \overline{M \setminus W}$. Let $h$ be any metric on $V$ such that $h=dt^2+g_Y$ on a boundary collar.

For each $T \in \mathbb{N}$, write $W_T = W \cup_{Y \times \{0\}} (Y \times [0,T])$. Identify $M$ with $W_T \cup_{Y} V$. Let $g_T$ be the metric on $M$ such that 
$$g_T =\begin{cases}
g &\text{on $W$}\\
g_Y + dt^2 &\text{on $Y \times [0,T]$}\\
h &\text{on $V$.}\\
\end{cases}
$$
Then $g_T$ has positive scalar curvature on $W_T$. Notice that the $Spin^c$ structure $\mathfrak s$ of $M$ remains the same under these metrics. 

Since $\operatorname{sw}_M(\mathfrak s)\neq 0$, on each $(M, g_T)$, the Seiberg--Witten equations admit a solution for generic perturbations. Approximating $\eta$ by generic perturbations and applying Seiberg--Witten compactness on $(M,g_T)$ shows that $\operatorname{sw}_M(\mathfrak s)\neq 0$ guarantees the existence of a $(\mathfrak s,\eta)$-monopole $(\Phi_T,A_T)$.

By Lemma \ref{lem: energy}, 
$$E_{\eta}(\Phi_T, A_T) = 4\int_M|\eta^+|^2\,dV_{g_T}
-4\pi^2\int_M c_{\mathfrak s}^2,$$
so $E_{\eta}(\Phi_T, A_T)$ is uniformly bounded on $M$. 

Using Lemma \ref{lem: key estimate} to bound $\Phi_T$ in terms of $R$ and $\eta$, we see that restricting to $W_T$, we have
\begin{align*}
E_{\eta}(\Phi_T|_{W_T}, A_T|_{W_T}) & \le   E_{\eta}(\Phi_T, A_T) + \int_V \frac{\max(0, - R_h)}{4}|\Phi_T|^2 
dV_h \le C
\end{align*}
for some uniform constant $C$ depending on $(V,h)$ and $\eta$ but independent of $T$.

Then using Lemma \ref{lem: identities} and the elementary norm inequality $|a+b|^2\le2|a|^2+2|b|^2$, this in turn gives uniform upper bounds on $\|F_{A_T}\|_{L^2(W_T)}$, $\|\Phi_{T}\|_{L^4(W_T)}$ and $\|\nabla^{A_T} \Phi_{T}\|_{L^2(W_T)}$.

Since $E_{\eta}(\Phi_T, A_T)$, $\|F_{A_T}\|_{L^2(W_T)}$, $\|\Phi_T\|_{L^4(W_T)}$ and $\|\nabla^{A_T} \Phi_T\|_{L^2(W_T)}$ are uniformly bounded, we can apply the local compactness theory (see \cite[Theorem 5.2.1]{KronheimerMrowka2007Monopoles} and also \cite[Section 4.4.2]{Nicolaescu2000SeibergWitten}) to conclude that there is a sequence of smooth gauge transformations $u_T: W_T \to S^1$ such that after passing to a subsequence, the transformed configurations $u_T(\Phi_T, A_T)$ converge weakly to a configuration $(\Phi_\infty, A_\infty)$ on $W^*$ that solves \eqref{eq: SW}, and the convergence is strong in $C^\infty$ on every compact subset of $W^*$.

From the weak convergence we conclude that $E_\eta(\Phi_\infty, A_\infty)$ is bounded. Thus $(\Phi_\infty, A_\infty)$ is a desired $(\mathfrak s_{W^*}, \eta)$-monopole on $W^*$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem: b_2^+}
Let $X$ be a compact connected oriented smooth $4$-manifold with boundary and suppose $W \subset X$ is a smooth connected codimension-zero submanifold such that $X\setminus W$ is contained in a collar neighborhood of $\partial X$. Then $b_2^+(W) = b_2^+(X)$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By removing part of the collar, we can get $X' \subset X$ diffeomorphic to $X$ with $X' \xhookrightarrow{\iota_1} W \xhookrightarrow{\iota_2} X$.


By Poincar\'e duality, $H_2(X';\mathbb{R}) \cong H^2_c(\operatorname{Int} X'; \mathbb{R})$.
We consider the intersection form extended to $\mathbb{R}$ coefficients $Q_{X'} \colon H^2_c(\operatorname{Int} X';\mathbb{R}) \times H^2_c(\operatorname{Int} X';\mathbb{R}) \to \mathbb{R}$ given by $Q_{X'}(a,b) = \int_{X'} a \wedge b$. Let $(\iota_1)_!$ denote extension by zero.
We compute
$$
Q_{X'}(a,b) = \int_{X'} a \wedge b  = \int_{W} (\iota_1)_!(a) \wedge (\iota_1)_!(b) = Q_W((\iota_1)_!(a), (\iota_1)_!(b)).
$$
Hence the image of any positive definite subspace of $H^2_c(\operatorname{Int} X';\mathbb{R})$ remains positive in $H^2_c(\operatorname{Int} W;\mathbb{R})$. Thus $b_2^+ (X') \le b_2^+(W)$.

Similarly we can show $b_2^+ (W) \le b_2^+(X)$. Since $X'$ and $X$ are diffeomorphic, $b_2^+ (X') = b_2^+ (X)$. We thus have $b_2^+(W) = b_2^+ (X)$ as desired.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem: puncture}
Let $M$ be a closed connected oriented smooth $4$-manifold. Let $B_1, \dots, B_k$ be pairwise disjoint open balls in $M$. Let $U=\cup_{i=1}^k B_i$ and let $X = M \setminus B$. Then $b_2^+(M) = b_2^+(X)$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Since $H^1(U;\mathbb Z)=H^2(U;\mathbb Z)=0$, the long exact sequence of the pair $(M,U)$ gives an isomorphism
$H^2(M,U;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\cong} H^2(M;\mathbb Z)$.

By excision,
$H^2(M,U;\mathbb Z)\cong H_c^2(M\setminus B;\mathbb Z)
=H_c^2(X;\mathbb Z)$. Hence
$H_c^2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H^2(M;\mathbb Z)$.

This isomorphism identifies the integral intersection forms. Thus their positive indices agree, so $b_2^+(X)=b_2^+(M)$.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Suppose to the contrary that $\operatorname{Int} X$ admits a complete metric of uniformly positive scalar curvature. Then by Theorem \ref{thm: Wang--Zhu}, $\operatorname{Int} X$ admits an exhaustion by compact smooth codimension-zero submanifolds $K_1 \subset K_2 \subset \dots \subset \operatorname{Int} X$ and a complete metric $g$ of uniformly positive scalar curvature such that near each hypersurface $\Sigma_i \coloneq  \partial K_i$, the metric is of product form $g = dt^2 + g_{\Sigma_i}$.

For large enough $i$, $\operatorname{Int} X \setminus K_i$ lies in a cylinder diffeomorphic to $\partial X \times (0, \infty)$. Fix such an $i$. Then $\operatorname{Int} X \setminus K_i$ lies in a collar neighborhood of $\partial X$, so Lemma \ref{lem: b_2^+} gives $b_2^+(K_i) = b_2^+(X) >0$. Then by Proposition \ref{prop: obstruction}, $K_i$ cannot carry a metric of positive scalar curvature that is a product near the boundary, a contradiction.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm: psc bordism}
Let $M$ be a closed connected $n$-manifold, $n\ge 5$, with fundamental group $\pi$, and let
\[
u\colon M\to B\pi
\]
be the classifying map of the universal covering $\widetilde M\to M$. That is, $u$ induces an isomorphism on the fundamental group.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $M$ is spin, then $M$ admits a metric of positive scalar curvature if and only if
\[
[M,u]\in \Omega_n^{Spin,+}(B\pi).
\]

\item If $M$ is oriented and $\widetilde M$ is nonspin, then $M$ admits a metric of positive scalar curvature if and only if
\[
[M,u]\in \Omega_n^{SO,+}(B\pi).
\]
\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A \emph{(stable) tangential structure} is a map $\theta: B \to BO.$
Let $V\to X$ be a vector bundle with stable classifying map $f \colon X \to BO$. A $\theta$-structure on $V$ is a lift $\ell \colon X \to B$ of $f$ through $\theta$, i.e. $f=\theta\circ\ell$. 
A $\theta$-structure on a manifold $M$ is a $\theta$-structure on the tangent bundle $TM$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Let $\theta\colon B\to BO$ be a tangential structure.
A $\theta$-cobordism between closed $n$-manifolds
$M_0$ and $M_1$ equipped with $\theta$-structures is a compact
$(n+1)$-manifold $W$ with boundary $\partial W=M_0^{\mathrm{op}}\sqcup M_1$,
together with a $\theta$-structure on its tangent bundle
whose induced boundary structures agree with the prescribed
structures on $M_0$ and $M_1$.
Here the boundary structures are induced using
$TW|_{\partial W}\cong T(\partial W)\oplus\mathbb R$.

If $M_0$ and $M_1$ have identified boundaries $Y$, a
$\theta$-cobordism \emph{relative to the boundary} is a cobordism
with corners whose boundary faces are $M_0$, $M_1$, and
$Y\times[0,1]$, with the usual identifications along their
boundaries, and whose $\theta$-structure on the vertical face
$Y\times[0,1]$ is the product of the common boundary structure.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
Let $Z$ be a topological space.
Let $B = BSO \times Z$, and let $\theta \colon B \to BO$ be the composition of the projection and the natural map $BSO \times Z \to BSO \to BO$. Then two closed oriented manifolds equipped with maps to $Z$ are $\theta$-cobordant if and only if they represent the same element of the bordism group $\Omega_n^{SO}(Z)$.


Similarly, if we let $B' = BSpin \times Z$, and let $\theta' \colon B' \to BO$ be the composition of the projection and the natural map $BSpin \times Z \to BSpin \to BO$, then two closed spin manifolds  equipped with maps to $Z$  are $\theta'$-cobordant if and only if they represent the same element of the bordism group $\Omega_n^{Spin}(Z)$.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $M$ be a manifold. A tangential structure $\theta \colon B \to BO$ is called the \emph{(stable) tangential $2$-type} of $M$ if the tangent bundle $TM$ admits a $2$-connected $\theta$-structure $\ell_M \colon M \to B$ and $\theta$ is $2$-coconnected.

Here, $2$-connected means that $\ell_M$ induces isomorphisms on $\pi_0$ and $\pi_1$ and a surjection on $\pi_2$, while $2$-coconnected means that $\theta$ induces an injection on $\pi_2$ and isomorphisms on $\pi_k$ for all $k\ge 3$.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def: extension}
Let $\Sigma\subset M$ be a hypersurface, and let $\theta\colon B \to BO$ be the tangential $2$-type of $\Sigma$. We say that the tangential $2$-type of $\Sigma$ \emph{extends to $M$} if there exist $\theta$-structures $\ell_\Sigma \colon \Sigma \to B$, 
$\ell_M \colon M \to B$
such that 
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\ell_\Sigma$ is $2$-connected,
\item $\ell_\Sigma$ is homotopic to $\ell_M\circ\iota$, where $\iota: \Sigma\hookrightarrow M$ is the inclusion.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- example-3: body_md -->
\label{ex: product tangential structure}
Let $\theta\colon B\to BO$ be the tangential $2$-type of a closed manifold $\Sigma$, and let $\ell_\Sigma\colon \Sigma\to B$ be a $2$-connected $\theta$-structure. Then the tangential $2$-type of $\Sigma$ extends to the cylinder $C=\Sigma\times[0,1]$ by setting
$\ell_C=\ell_\Sigma\circ \operatorname{proj}_\Sigma \colon C \to B.$   

Consequently, if $W\subset \Sigma\times[0,1]$ is any codimension-zero submanifold that contains a slice $\Sigma\times \{t\}$, then the restriction of $\ell_C$ to $W$ gives a $\theta$-structure on $W$ that extends the tangential 2-type of $\Sigma$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem: psc product}
Let $n \ge 6$. Let $Y$ be a closed connected oriented manifold of dimension $n-1$ and $C = Y \times [0, 1]$. Let $\Sigma$ be a closed connected oriented hypersurface separating $Y \times \{0\}$ and $Y \times \{1\}$ in $C$. Suppose $C$ admits a metric $g$ of positive scalar curvature such that $g$ is a product $g_\Sigma + dt^2$ in a tubular neighborhood of $\Sigma$ avoiding the boundary. Then $C$ admits a metric $\widetilde{g}$ of positive scalar curvature such that $\widetilde{g} = g$ near $Y \times \{0\}$, and $\widetilde{g}$ equals a product $\widetilde{g}_{Y \times \{1\}} + dt^2$ in a collar neighborhood of $Y \times \{1\}$.

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $n \ge 6$. Let $W$ be the compact submanifold of $C$ bounded by $Y \times \{0\}$ and $\Sigma$. $W$ can be viewed as a cobordism $W \colon Y \rightsquigarrow \Sigma$. We restrict the projection $C \to Y$ to $W$ and obtain a retraction $r \colon W \to Y$. Let $\theta \colon B \to BO$ be the tangential 2-type of $Y$. Then $\theta$ extends to $W$ by Example \ref{ex: product tangential structure}. Choose the $2$-connected $\theta$-structure
$\ell_Y\colon Y\to B$ defining the tangential $2$-type of $Y$, and write $f_Y=\theta\circ\ell_Y$.
Set $\ell_W=\ell_Y\circ r$ and $f_W=f_Y\circ r$. Let $\nu(Y)$ denote a stable normal bundle of $Y$, with a chosen stable trivialization of $TY\oplus\nu(Y)$. Since $W\subset Y\times[0,1]$, we have $TW\cong r^*TY\oplus\mathbb R$, so pulling back the chosen
trivialization to $W$ gives a stable trivialization of $TW\oplus r^*\nu(Y)
\cong r^*(TY\oplus\nu(Y))\oplus\mathbb R$.
We use the $\theta$-structure induced by this identification.

We first proceed as in \cite[Proposition 6.4]{Rade2023MuBubbles} and form a $\theta$-cobordism from $W$ to some $W_2$ with a metric of positive scalar curvature that is a product near the boundary. Note that the tangential data are already implicitly preserved through the surgery traces in \cite[Proposition 6.4]{Rade2023MuBubbles}; here we just make this explicit. This is done via the following two steps. Since $\ell_Y=\ell_W\circ\iota\colon Y\to B$ is $2$-connected, \((\ell_Y)_*\) is an isomorphism on $\pi_1$ and a surjection on $\pi_2$, and $(\ell_W)_*$ is surjective on both $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$. 

For each homotopy $H\colon V\times[0,1]\to Y$ used below, the map $f_Y\circ H(\,\cdot\,,0)$ classifies the stable tangent bundle of $V$. Hence $f_Y\circ H$ classifies the stable tangent bundle of $V\times[0,1]$, with strict lift $\ell_Y\circ H$.
Thus in the steps below, whenever we homotope a retraction, we include the corresponding cylinder before the surgery trace. All such homotopies are fixed near the boundary, so these cylinders are $\theta$-cobordisms relative to the boundary. 


\emph{Step 1:} We kill $K_1 \coloneq \ker\bigl(r_*\colon\pi_1(W)\to\pi_1(Y)\bigr) = \ker\bigl((\ell_W)_*\colon\pi_1(W)\to\pi_1(B)\bigr)$. This kernel is finitely generated as a normal subgroup of $\pi_1(W)$, since $\pi_1(W)$ is finitely generated and $\pi_1(Y)$ is finitely presented. Thus we can choose a finite number of normal generators.

Let $\alpha$ be one of the chosen normal generators of $K_1$. Since $1 < n/2$ we can represent $\alpha$ by an embedding $\alpha \colon S^1 \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Int} W$. Because $r\circ\alpha$ is null-homotopic in $Y$, we can choose an extension 
\[
F_\alpha\colon D^2\to Y,
\qquad
F_\alpha|_{S^1}=r\circ\alpha.
\]

Since $W$ is oriented the normal bundle of $\alpha$ is trivial. Using
the chosen null-homotopy $F_\alpha$, we can thus choose a framing of $\nu(\alpha, W)$ that is compatible with the stable trivialization of $r^*\nu(Y)\oplus TW$, and choose a corresponding tubular neighborhood $S^1\times D^{n-1}$. We homotope $r$, fixing the central loop and a neighborhood of $\partial W$, so that it is constant in the normal-disk direction on this neighborhood. We transport the stable trivialization through this homotopy and continue to set $\ell_W=\ell_Y\circ r$. 

The surgery trace $T_\alpha\colon W\rightsquigarrow W_\alpha$ is obtained by attaching the $2$-handle $D^2\times D^{n-1}$ to $W\times[0,1]$.
Extend $r$ to a map $r_{T_\alpha}\colon T_\alpha\to Y$ by $r_{T_\alpha} = r \circ \operatorname{proj}_W$ on $W \times [0,1]$, and $r_{T_\alpha}(z,v)=F_\alpha(z)$ on the handle. Set $\ell_{T_\alpha}=\ell_Y\circ r_{T_\alpha}$ and $f_{T_\alpha}=f_Y\circ r_{T_\alpha}$. By the choice of framing, the stable trivialization extends to $r_{T_\alpha}^*\nu(Y)\oplus TT_\alpha$. By our construction, $f_{T_\alpha}$ is a stable tangent classifying map extending $f_W$, and $\theta\circ\ell_{T_\alpha}=f_{T_\alpha}$. Thus $T_\alpha$ is a $\theta$-cobordism.

Repeating this construction for all the normal generators gives a $\theta$-cobordism relative to the boundary $T_1\colon W\rightsquigarrow W_1$ and a retraction $r_{T_1}\colon T_1\to Y$. Let $r_1 \coloneq r_{T_1}|_{W_1} \colon W_1 \to Y$. Set $\ell_{W_1}=\ell_Y\circ r_1$ and $f_{W_1}=f_Y\circ r_1$. Then $\ell_{W_1}$ is a
$\theta$-structure on $W_1$, with
$\theta\circ\ell_{W_1}=f_{W_1}$. The extended stable trivialization on $T_1$ restricts to a stable trivialization of $r_1^*\nu(Y)\oplus TW_1$. The map $r_1$ induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$ and, being a retraction, is surjective on $\pi_2$. Thus $r_1$ is $2$-connected, and the inclusion $Y\hookrightarrow W_1$ is $1$-connected.


\emph{Step 2:} We further need to kill $K_2 \coloneq \ker \bigl((r_1)_* \colon \pi_2(W_1) \to \pi_2 (Y)\bigr)$. Since $r_1$ is a retraction, the long exact sequence of the pair $(W_1,Y)$ identifies $K_2 \cong \pi_2(W_1,Y).$ Since $(W_1,Y)$ is a finite CW pair and $r_1$ induces an isomorphism on $\pi_0$ and $\pi_1$, $\pi_2(W_1, Y)$ is finitely generated as a $\mathbb Z[\pi_1(Y)]$-module \cite{Wall1965Finiteness}. Since $2 < n/2$ we can represent each of those module generators $\beta$ by an embedding $\beta \colon S^2 \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Int} W_1$. Since $r_1 \circ \beta$ is contractible, there is a map $G_\beta \colon D^3 \to Y$ such that $G_\beta|_{S^2}=r_1\circ \beta$.

The stable trivialization of $r_1^*\nu(Y) \oplus TW_1$ induces a stable trivialization of $\beta^*r_1^*\nu(Y) \oplus \beta^* TW_1$. Since $r_1\circ\beta$ extends over $D^3$, the bundle $\beta^*r_1^*\nu(Y)$ is trivial. Thus $\beta^* TW_1 \cong \nu(S^2,W_1) \oplus TS^2$ is stably trivial.
Since $TS^2$ is stably trivial, it follows that the normal bundle of $\beta$ in $W_1$, $\nu(S^2,W_1)$, is stably trivial as well. Its rank is $n-2 \geq 4$, so it is trivial, and the compatible stable normal framing can be realized by an actual framing. Choose this framing and a tubular neighborhood $S^2\times D^{n-2}$ of $\beta$.

As in Step 1, we homotope $r_1$, fixing the central sphere and a neighborhood of $\partial W_1$, in the attaching neighborhood $S^2\times D^{n-2}$ to be constant in the normal-disk direction. We transport the stable trivialization through this homotopy. Extend $r_1$ over the $3$-handle $D^3\times D^{n-2}$ by $(z,v)\mapsto G_\beta(z)$. By the choice of framing, the stable trivialization extends over the trace. Composing the extended map with
$\ell_Y$ and $f_Y$ therefore gives a strict
$\theta$-structure and its stable tangent classifying map, respectively.

Hence we can kill $K_2$ in finitely many surgery steps. We end up with a $\theta$-cobordism relative to the boundary $T_2\colon W_1 \rightsquigarrow W_2$, such that $W_2$ is obtained from $W_1$ by 2-surgeries in the interior, and a retraction $r_2 \colon W_2 \to Y$ that induces isomorphisms on $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$. Being a retraction, it is surjective on $\pi_3$. Thus $r_2$ is $3$-connected, and the inclusion $Y\hookrightarrow W_2$ is $2$-connected.

\vspace{1em}

Together $T_1$ and $T_2$ give a $\theta$-cobordism relative to the boundary $T \colon W \rightsquigarrow W_2$.


Now we take $W_2$ with the opposite direction, $W_2^{\mathrm{op}}$, and regard $W_2^{\mathrm{op}}$ as a cobordism from $\Sigma$ to $Y$. Since the inclusion $Y \hookrightarrow W_2^{\mathrm{op}}$ is 2-connected, and $\Sigma$ admits a metric of positive scalar curvature $g_\Sigma$, by Gajer's theorem on the trace of surgery \cite{Gajer1987Riemannian} (see also \cite[Remark 2.12 (iii)]{Frenck2026Doubling}), $W_2^{\mathrm{op}}$ admits a positive scalar curvature metric $g_{W_2^{\mathrm{op}}}$ which extends $g_\Sigma$ on $\Sigma$ and is of product type near both boundaries. 


We let $C_2 \coloneq W \cup_{\Sigma} W_2^{\mathrm{op}}$. Then the metric $g$ on $W$ and the metric $g_{W_2^{\mathrm{op}}}$ on $W_2^{\mathrm{op}}$ glue together smoothly to get a positive scalar curvature metric $g_{C_2}$ on $C_2$, such that $g_{C_2} = g$ on $W$ and $g_{C_2}$ is a product near the boundary $Y_+$ coming from $W_2^{\mathrm{op}}$.


Then by gluing together the trivial cobordism over $W$ and the $\theta$-cobordism relative to the boundary $T^{\mathrm{op}} \colon W^{\mathrm{op}} \rightsquigarrow W_2^{\mathrm{op}}$, we get a $\theta$-cobordism relative to the boundary from $W \cup_{\Sigma} W^{\mathrm{op}}$ to $C_2$.

By \cite[Proposition 2.13]{Frenck2026Doubling}, since $C_2$ admits a positive scalar curvature metric $g_{C_2}$, the cylinder $Y \times [0,1]$ admits a positive scalar curvature metric $\widetilde{g}$ such that $g_{C_2}$ and $\widetilde{g}$ agree in a neighborhood of the boundary. This shows $\widetilde{g} = g$ near $Y \times \{0\}$ and $\widetilde{g}$ is a product near $Y \times \{1\}$.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop: surgery}
Let $n \ge 3$. Let $M^n$ be a closed smooth manifold admitting a positive scalar curvature metric. Suppose $Q\subset M$ is a compact codimension-zero submanifold admitting a handle decomposition with all handles of index at most $n-3$. Then $M\setminus\operatorname{Int}Q$ admits a positive scalar curvature metric that is a product near its boundary.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Equip the $0$-handles of $Q$ with standard positive scalar curvature metrics that are products near their boundaries. Since all remaining handles have index at most $n-3$, Gajer's theorem on the trace of surgery \cite{Gajer1987Riemannian} extends these metrics to a positive scalar curvature metric on $Q$ that is a product near its boundary.


Take $$W=\bigl(M\times[0,1]\bigr)\setminus
\operatorname{Int}\bigl(Q\times[1/2,1]\bigr),$$ 
rounding corners compatibly with the boundary collars. Then $W$ gives a relative cobordism from $M\sqcup Q^{\mathrm{op}}$ to $M\setminus\operatorname{Int} Q$. Relative to the incoming boundary $M\sqcup Q^{\mathrm{op}}$,
the cobordism has a $(k+1)$-handle for each
$k$-handle of $Q$.

We can view $W$ as the trace of interior surgeries from $M\sqcup Q^{\mathrm{op}}$ to $M\setminus\operatorname{Int} Q$. Each $k$-surgery corresponds to removing one $(k+1)$-handle of $Q \times [0,1/2]$. Since $Q$ only consists of handles of index at most $n-3$, all the surgeries are of codimension $\ge 3$. Also note they are in the interior and away from the boundary. Then we can apply the surgery theorem of Gromov--Lawson \cite{GromovLawson1980Classification} and get a metric of positive scalar curvature on $M\setminus\operatorname{Int} Q$. The metric near the boundary is unchanged and is thus still a product near the boundary.

\label{lem: boundary}
Let $(X, \partial X)$ be a compact connected oriented smooth $5$-manifold with non-empty boundary $\partial X$. Assume each component of $\partial X$ is simply connected. Suppose $\operatorname{Int} X$ admits a complete metric of uniformly positive scalar curvature. Let $\Sigma$ be a component of $\partial X$. Then
$$\Sigma \cong_{\mathrm{homeo}} \begin{cases}
\#_k(S^2\times S^2)  & \text{for some $k \ge 0$ if $\Sigma$ is spin,} \\
\#_{p}\mathbb{CP}^2\#_{q}\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^2 & \text{for some $p, q \ge 0$, $p+q >0$ if $\Sigma$ is nonspin.} \\
\end{cases}
$$
When $k = 0$, $\#_0(S^2\times S^2)$ denotes $S^4$.

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Suppose that $\operatorname{Int} X$ admits a complete metric of uniformly positive scalar curvature. Let $\Sigma$ be a component of $\partial X$. Then by Theorem \ref{thm: Wang--Zhu}, we can get a separating hypersurface with a metric of positive scalar curvature in a cylinder diffeomorphic to $\Sigma \times [0,1]$. By \cite[Proposition 31]{KumarSen2025ExoticStructures}, there exists at least one smooth structure on $\Sigma$ that admits a positive scalar curvature metric.

If $\Sigma$ is nonspin, then $\Sigma$ has an odd intersection form $Q_\Sigma$. Let $p = b_2^+(\Sigma)$ and $q = b_2^-(\Sigma)$. If $Q_\Sigma$ is definite (i.e. one of $p,q$ is $0$), then since $\Sigma$ is smooth, Donaldson's diagonalization theorem \cite{Donaldson1983GaugeTheory} implies that $Q_\Sigma = p \langle 1 \rangle \oplus q \langle -1 \rangle$. If $Q_\Sigma$ is indefinite (i.e., $p,q >0$), the algebraic classification of indefinite odd unimodular forms also gives $Q_\Sigma = p \langle 1 \rangle \oplus q \langle -1 \rangle$. Consequently, by Freedman's theorem \cite{Freedman1982Topology}, $\Sigma$ is homeomorphic to $\#_{p}\mathbb{CP}^2\#_{q}\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^2$. 

If $\Sigma$ is spin, then by \cite[Observation 30]{KumarSen2025ExoticStructures}, $\hat{A}(\Sigma) = 0$. In dimension 4 $\hat{A}(\Sigma) = - \sigma(\Sigma)/8$. Thus $\Sigma$ is a closed simply connected spin 4-manifold with zero signature, and by Freedman's theorem \cite{Freedman1982Topology}, $\Sigma$ is homeomorphic to $\#_{k} (S^2 \times S^2)$ for some $k \ge 0$.

Since each $Y_j'$ and each $Q_j$ is simply connected, by van Kampen's theorem, $\pi_1(N) = \pi_1 (X) =: \pi$.

Take a classifying map $u_0 \colon N \to B\pi$. Since each $C_j \cup_{Y_j'} Q_j$ is simply connected, $u_0 |_{C_j \cup_{Y_j'} Q_j}$ is null-homotopic. By the homotopy extension property, we can fix a point $b \in B\pi$ and homotope $u_0$ to another classifying map $u \colon N \to B\pi$ such that for each $j$, $u|_{C_j \cup_{Y_j'} Q_j}$ is the constant map to $b$. We denote by $b$ the constant map to $b$.
Then we can extend $u|_X$ to a map $\hat{u} \colon DX \to B \pi$ by setting $\hat{u} = u$ on $X$ and extending by the constant map $\hat{u} \equiv b$ on $X^{\mathrm{op}}$.

Then we get the following bordism equivalence in $\Omega_5^{Spin}(B\pi)$:
$$[N, u] + [N^{\mathrm{op}}, b] = [DX, \hat{u}] + \sum_{j=1}^m [DU_j, b].$$

Since $\Omega_5^{Spin}(*) = 0$ \cite{Milnor1963Spin}, we have $[N^{\mathrm{op}}, b] = 0 = [DU_j, b]$, so 
$$[N, u] = [DX, \hat{u}] \in \Omega_5^{Spin}(B\pi).$$
Since $DX$ admits a metric of positive scalar curvature, Theorem \ref{thm: psc bordism} (1) implies that $N$ admits a metric of positive scalar curvature. 

Since each $Q_j$ consists of only $0$ and $2$-handles, by Proposition \ref{prop: surgery}, $X' = N \setminus \sqcup_j \operatorname{Int} Q_j$ admits a metric of positive scalar curvature that is a product near the boundary as desired.
\vspace{1em}


\emph{Case 2:} Assume $X$ is totally nonspin. Let $I$ denote the set of indices $j$ where $Y_j'$'s are spin, and let $J$ denote the set of indices $j$ where $Y_j'$'s are nonspin.

For each $j \in I$, we still cap off $Y_j'$ by $Q_j$. Define $U_j \coloneq  C_j \cup Q_j$.


For the nonspin components, $\sqcup_{j \in J} Y_j'$ has signature zero, so $\sqcup_{j \in J} (Y_j')^{\mathrm{op}}$ bounds a compact 5-manifold $\hat{Q}$ as constructed in Lemma \ref{lem: cap}. If $J = \emptyset$ then we just take $\hat{Q} =\emptyset$. When $J\neq\emptyset$, $\hat{Q}$ is connected, simply connected, and admits a metric of positive scalar curvature that is product near the boundary. Define $\hat U\coloneq  \cup_{j \in J} C_j \cup_{Y_j'} \hat{Q}$.

We consider the closed manifold
$$N\coloneq  X \cup_{Y_j, j \in I} U_j \cup_{Y_j, j \in J} \hat{U} = X' \cup_{Y_j', j \in I} Q_j \cup_{Y_j', j \in J} \hat{Q}.$$
See Figure \ref{fig: totally nonspin} for a visualization. Because $X$ is totally nonspin, $N$ is also totally nonspin by our construction.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width= 0.9\linewidth]{totally_nonspin.png}
    \caption{The manifold $N$ in the totally nonspin case. In the case drawn, $|I| = 0$ and $|J|= 2$.}
    \label{fig: totally nonspin}
\end{figure}

Denote $\pi\coloneq  \pi_1 (X) = \pi_1(X')$.
By van Kampen's theorem, since each $Q_j$ and each $Y_j$ for $j \in I$ are connected and simply connected, attaching them to $X'$ does not change the fundamental group. 

We first assume $J \neq \emptyset$. $\hat{Q}$ is connected and simply connected, but it is attached to $X'$ over $|J|$ simply connected components, so by van Kampen's theorem for fundamental groupoids,
$$\pi_1(N) = \pi_1(X') * F_{|J|-1} = \pi * F_{|J|-1} \coloneq \Gamma,$$
where $F_{|J|-1}$ is the free group on $|J|-1$ elements. 



Notice that 
$$B\Gamma\simeq B\pi\vee\bigvee^{|J|-1}S^1.$$

Let $b$ denote the wedge point where all the wedge sums take place. Let $Bi \colon B\pi \to B\Gamma$ be the inclusion map, and $Br: B\Gamma \to B\pi$ be the map where all $S^1$'s are collapsed to the wedge point.  $Br \circ Bi =\operatorname{id}$. For simplicity of notation, we identify $\Omega_5^{SO}(B\pi)$
with its image in $\Omega_5^{SO}(B\Gamma)$ under $(Bi)_*$. Similarly we identify $\Omega_5^{SO}(*)$ with its image in $\Omega_5^{SO}(B\pi)$ and $\Omega_5^{SO}(B\Gamma)$ by mapping $*$ to $b$.

We can compute 
\begin{align*}
\Omega_5^{SO}(B\Gamma) & \cong\Omega^{SO}_5 (*) \oplus \widetilde \Omega_5^{SO}(B\Gamma) & \text{ by \eqref{eq: reduced bordism}}\\
& \cong \Omega^{SO}_5 (*) \oplus \widetilde \Omega_5^{SO}(B\pi) \oplus (\bigoplus^{|J|-1} \widetilde \Omega_5^{SO}(S^1))  & \text{ by \eqref{eq: additivity}}\\
& \cong  \Omega_5^{SO}(B\pi) \oplus (\bigoplus^{|J|-1} \widetilde \Omega_5^{SO}(S^1))  & \text{ by \eqref{eq: reduced bordism}.}
\end{align*}
Recall from \eqref{eq: bordism S^1} that $\widetilde\Omega_5^{SO}(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $[\mathbb{CP}^2 \times S^1, \operatorname{pr}_{S^1}]$ generates the group $\widetilde\Omega^{SO}_5(S^1)$. 

$(Br)_* \colon \Omega_5^{SO}(B\Gamma) \to \Omega_5^{SO}(B\pi)$ is projection onto the first summand of the above decomposition. Then the kernel of $(Br)_*$ is generated by
\begin{align}\label{eq: P_i}
P_i=
[\mathbb{CP}^2\times S^1,\,
\ell_i\circ\operatorname{pr}_{S^1}],    
\end{align}
where \(\ell_i:S^1\to B\Gamma\) represents the \(i\)-th free generator. The standard product metric of $\mathbb{CP}^2\times S^1$ has positive scalar curvature. 

Take a classifying map $u_0\colon X \to B\pi$. Similar to the spin case, since the boundary components are simply connected, we can take $u_0$ to equal the constant $b$ on the boundary collars. Extend it constantly to get a map 
$u: N \to B\pi$. 

On the other hand, we can also build a classifying map $c\colon N \to B\Gamma$ from $u_0$. To get $c$ from $u_0$, we still extend by constant $b$ to all the $Q_j$'s for $j \in I$. To extend to $\hat Q$, fix one $j \in J$ as the center of the star, by Lemma \ref{lem: star}, we can construct a continuous map from $\hat U$ to the star graph on $|J|$ vertices. Gluing all the vertices together yields a continuous map from $\hat{U}$ to $\bigvee_{|J|-1}S^1$. Gluing this map with the map $u_0: X \to B\pi$ yields the map $c: N \to B\Gamma$. Under the identification $\pi_1(N)\cong\pi*F_{|J|-1}$, the induced map $c_*$ sends the subgroup $\pi_1(X)\cong\pi$ isomorphically onto the $\pi$-factor and the additional free generators to the corresponding circle generators in $B\Gamma$, so $c$ is a classifying map. By our construction, $[N, u] = (Br)_* [N, c] \in \Omega_5^{SO}(B\pi)$. Our goal is to show $[N,c] \in \Omega_5^{SO,+}(B\Gamma)$.


We also extend $u_0$ to a map $\hat{u} \colon DX \to B \pi$ by letting $\hat{u} = u$ on $X$ and extend by the constant map $\hat{u} \equiv b$ on $X^{\mathrm{op}}$.

We get the bordism equivalence in $\Omega_5^{SO}(B\pi)$:
$$[N, u] + [N^{\mathrm{op}}, b] = [DX, \hat{u}] + \sum_{j \in I} [DU_j, b] + [D\hat{U}, b].$$

Now since $D\hat{U}$ bounds $\hat{U} \times [0,1]$ after rounding corners and the constant map $b$ extends over $\hat{U} \times [0,1]$, we have $[D\hat{U}, b] = 0$. Similarly $[DU_j, b] = 0$.
Thus
$$[N, u] - [DX, \hat{u}] = [N, b].$$


Since $b$ is the constant map, $[N, b] \in \Omega_5^{SO}(*)$. As shown by \cite[Theorem 3]{GromovLawson1980Classification}, every class in $\Omega_5^{SO}(*)$ has a simply connected representative carrying a metric of positive scalar curvature. Let $(M, b)$ be such a PSC representative in the class of $[N, b]$. 

Thus
$$[N, u] = [DX, \hat{u}] + [M, b] \in \Omega_5^{SO}(B\pi).$$

To show $[N,c] \in \Omega_5^{SO}(B\Gamma)$ has a PSC representative, we consider the class $[N,c] - [N, u] \in \Omega_5^{SO}(B\Gamma)$. Since  $[N, u] = (Br)_* [N, c] \in \Omega_5^{SO}(B\pi)$, we find $[N,c] - [N, u] \in \ker((Br)_* \colon \Omega_5^{SO}(B\Gamma) \to \Omega_5^{SO}(B\pi))$.

Since $\ker(Br)_*$ is generated by the $P_i$'s as defined in \eqref{eq: P_i}, we can find $a_i \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that 
$$[N,c] - [N, u] = \sum_{i=1}^{|J|-1}a_i P_i \in \Omega_5^{SO}(B\Gamma).$$

Therefore we have in $\Omega_5^{SO}(B\Gamma)$ that
$$[N,c] = [N, u] + \sum_{i=1}^{|J|-1}a_i P_i = [DX, \hat u] + [M,b] + \sum_{i=1}^{|J|-1}a_i P_i.$$

By our choice, each representative on the right-hand side has a metric of positive scalar curvature, so the class $[N,c]$ has a PSC representative. Since $N$ is totally nonspin, by Theorem \ref{thm: psc bordism} (2), $N$ admits a metric of positive scalar curvature.

The above argument works when $J \neq \emptyset$. If $J=\emptyset$, then we simply have $\pi_1(N) = \pi_1(X') = \pi$. The same argument as above applies with $\hat Q$ omitted, $\Gamma=\pi$, and $c=u$, and we get the bordism equivalence $[N,c] = [DX, \hat u] + [M,b]$ in $\Omega_5^{SO}(B\Gamma)$, which again shows $N$ admits a metric of positive scalar curvature by Theorem \ref{thm: psc bordism} (2).


Lastly, we show that $X'$ admits the desired metric of positive scalar curvature that is a product near the boundary. Let $Q\coloneq  \cup_{j \in I}  Q_j \cup \hat{Q}$. Then $N = X' \cup Q$. So far, we have shown that $N$ has a metric of  positive scalar curvature, and $Q$ admits a metric of positive scalar curvature that is a product near the boundary.

There is an oriented bordism from $N\sqcup Q^{\mathrm{op}}$ to $X'$ relative to their identified boundary. Explicitly, take
\[
W= N \times [0,1]\setminus \operatorname{Int}\bigl(Q\times[1/2,1]\bigr)
\]
rounding corners compatibly with the boundary collars.
Its incoming boundary is $(N\times\{0\})\sqcup(Q^{\mathrm{op}}\times\{\frac12\})$, its outgoing boundary is $X'\times\{1\}$, and its vertical
boundary is $\partial X'\times[\frac12,1]$.

The map $u\circ\operatorname{pr}_N\colon W\to B\pi$ restricts to $u$ on $N$, to the constant map $b$ on $Q^{\mathrm{op}}$ and the vertical boundary, and to $u|_{X'}$ on $X'$. By construction, $u|_{X'}$ classifies the universal cover of $X'$. Thus $W$ is a bordism over $B\pi$ relative to the boundary.

Since $X'$ is totally nonspin and has dimension $5$, by the version of the surgery theorem for manifolds with boundary in \cite[Lemma 2.6]{Frenck2023Nonspin} (cf. \cite[Theorem 1.5]{EbertFrenck2021Surgery} and \cite[Remark 2.12(i)]{Frenck2026Doubling}), the manifold $X'$ admits a metric of positive scalar curvature which restricts to the same metric near the boundary as $N\sqcup Q^{\mathrm{op}}$. Thus $X'$ admits a metric of positive scalar curvature that is a product near the boundary as desired.
