\label{Def:GelfandTripple}
Let
$\mathscr V _1,\mathscr V _2$ be
topological vector spaces
and $\mathcal H$ be a Hilbert space such that
$$
\mathscr V _1\hookrightarrow \mathcal H \hookrightarrow
\mathscr V _2.
$$
Then
$(\mathscr V _1,\mathcal H ,\mathscr V _2)$ is called a 
\emph{Gelfand triple}, if the following is
true:
\begin{itemize}
\item there is a hypo-continuous map
$(u,v)\mapsto (u,v)_{\mathcal H}$ from
$\mathscr V _2\times \mathscr V _1$
to $\mathbf C$ such that
$u\mapsto (u,v)_{\mathcal H}$ is linear
for every $v\in \mathscr V _1$ and
$v\mapsto (u,v)_{\mathcal H}$ is conjugate
linear for every $u\in \mathscr V _2$;

\vspace{0.1cm}

\item $\mathscr V _1$ is dense in $\mathcal H$;

\vspace{0.1cm}

\item if $u,v\in \mathcal V _1$, then
$(u,v)_{\mathcal H}$ is the scalar product
of $u$ and $v$ with respect to $\mathcal H$.
\end{itemize}

\label{Def:SolidBF}
Let $\mathscr B$ be a
\emph{QBF space} on
$\mathbf R^{d}$.
Then $\mathscr B$ is
called \emph{solid} if the
following conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item
for any
$f,g\in \mathcal M (\mathbf R^{d})$
which satisfy
$g\in \mathscr B$ and $|f|
\le |g|$, then $f\in \mathscr B$ and
  
\begin{equation}\label{Eq:Solid}
\Vert f\Vert _{\mathscr B}\le C\Vert g\Vert _{\mathscr B},
\end{equation}
  
for some constant $C>0$, independent of $f$ and $g$ above;

\vspace{0.1cm}

\item
for any $x\in \mathbf R^{d}$,
there is a constant $c>0$ and a function
$f\in \mathscr B$ such that $|f|>c$ on a non-null convex
set in $\mathbf R^{d}$ containing $x$.
\end{enumerate}
The space space $\mathscr B$ is called \emph{strict solid}
if it is solid and \eqref{Eq:Solid} holds for $C=1$.

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal B \hookrightarrow \ell^\infty (\mathbf N)$ be a topological vector space.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathscr I _\mathcal B$ is a vector space.

\vspace{0.1cm}

\item If in addition $\mathcal B$ is a quasi-Banach space,
then $\mathscr I _\mathcal B$ is a quasi-Banach space of the same
order as $\mathcal B$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The assertion (1) follows from the fact that bounded sets remain bounded under continuous operations.

\par

Suppose that $\mathcal B$ is a quasi-Banach space of order $r>0$, and
let $T_1,T_2\in \mathscr I _\mathcal B$, $\{ f_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\in \operatorname{ON}$,
and
$\{ g_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\in \operatorname{ON}$. Then
  
\begin{align*}
\Vert  \{ ((T_1+T_2)f_j,g_j)\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\Vert _{\mathcal B}^r
&\le
\Vert  \{ (T_1f_j,g_j)\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\Vert _{\mathcal B}^r
+
\Vert  \{ (T_2f_j,g_j)\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\Vert _{\mathcal B}^r
\\[1ex]
&\le
\Vert T_1\Vert _{\mathscr I _\mathcal B}^r + \Vert T_2\Vert _{\mathscr I _\mathcal B}^r.
\end{align*}
  
By taking the supremum over all possible 
$\{ f_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\in \operatorname{ON}$
and
$\{ g_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\in \operatorname{ON}$ of the left-hand side, one obtains
$$
\Vert T_1+T_2\Vert _{\mathscr I _\mathcal B}^r
\le
\Vert T_1\Vert _{\mathscr I _\mathcal B}^r + \Vert T_2\Vert _{\mathscr I _\mathcal B}^r,
$$
which shows that $\mathscr I _\mathcal B$ is a quasi-normed space
of the same order as $\mathcal B$.

\par

We need to prove that $\mathscr I _\mathcal B$ is complete.
Suppose that $\{T_k \} _{k\in \mathbf N}$ is a Cauchy sequence
in $\mathscr I _\mathcal B$. Since $\mathcal B \hookrightarrow \ell ^\infty (\mathbf N)$,
it follows that $\mathscr I _\mathcal B \hookrightarrow \mathscr I _\infty$,
which in turn implies that $\{T_k \} _{k\in \mathbf N}$ is a Cauchy sequence
in $\mathscr I _\infty$, also. Since $\mathscr I _\infty$ is complete, there is a unique
$T\in \mathscr I _\infty$ such that
  
\begin{equation}
\label{Eq:OpNormLimit}
\lim _{k\to \infty} \Vert T_k-T\Vert _{\mathscr I _\infty}=0.
\end{equation}
  

\par

Now let $\{ f_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\operatorname{ON}$ and $\{ g_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\in \operatorname{ON}$
be fixed, and let
  
\begin{alignat*}{3}
\kappa &= \{\kappa (j)\} _{j\in \mathbf N} &
\quad &\text{and} & \quad
\kappa _k&= \{\kappa _k(j)\} _{j\in \mathbf N},
\intertext{where}
\kappa (j) &= (Tf_j,g_j)_{L^2} &
\quad &\text{and} & \quad
\kappa _{k}(j) &= (T_kf_j,g_j)_{L^2}.
\end{alignat*}
  
Then \eqref{Eq:OpNormLimit} shows that
  
\begin{equation}
\label{Eq:kappaLimit}
\lim _{k\to \infty} \kappa _k= \kappa ,
                                    
                                                                 
\end{equation}
  
with convergence in $\ell ^\infty (\mathbf N)$.
Since $T_k\in \mathscr I _\mathcal B$ and since $\mathcal B$
is a quasi-Banach space,
one has 
  
\begin{equation}
\label{Eq:AlternativeLimit}
\lim _{k\to \infty}
\Vert \kappa _k-\tilde \kappa \Vert _{\mathcal B}=0,
\end{equation}
  
for some $\tilde \kappa \in \mathcal B$, which depends on the choice of
$\{ f_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N},\{ g_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\in \operatorname{ON}$. Moreover,
since $\{T_k\}_{k \in \mathbb N}$ is a Cauchy sequence in $\mathscr I_\mathcal B$,
it follows that \eqref{Eq:AlternativeLimit} holds uniformly with
respect to $\{ f_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N}$ and $\{ g_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\in \operatorname{ON}$.

\par

A combination of \eqref{Eq:kappaLimit}, \eqref{Eq:AlternativeLimit},
and the fact that $\mathcal B \hookrightarrow \ell ^\infty (\mathbf N)$,
gives $\tilde \kappa = \kappa$, and since
\eqref{Eq:AlternativeLimit} holds uniformly with
respect to $\{ f_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N}$ and
$\{ g_j\} _{j\in \mathbf N}\in \operatorname{ON}$, one obtains
$$
\lim _{k\to \infty} \Vert T_k-T\Vert _{\mathscr I _\mathcal B}=0.
$$
This gives the result.

<!-- rem-9: body_md -->
In general, a solid QBF space $\mathscr B$ on
$\mathbf R^{2d}$ is not phase rotational invariant.
For example, suppose that $\mathscr B =L^{p,q}(\mathbf R^{2d})$ for some
$p,q\in (0,\infty ]$. Then $\mathscr B _\rho \neq \mathscr B$ when
$p\neq q$ and $\rho \notin 2\mathbf Z^{d}$.

\par

On the other hand, if $p=q$, then $\mathscr B _\rho = \mathscr B$
for every $\rho \in \mathbf R^{d}$. Consequently, $L^{p,p}(\mathbf R^{2d})=L^p(\mathbf R^{2d})$
is phase rotational invariant.

\label{Thm:FracFourOnModSpaces}
Let $\mathscr B$ be a solid QBF space on $\mathbf R^{2d}$,
$\mathscr B _\rho$ be as in
Definition \ref{Def:FrFTScales}, and let
$\omega ,\omega _\rho \in \mathscr P _A(\mathbf R^{2d})$ be such that

$\mathscr F _\rho$ on $\mathcal H _{\flat _1}'(\mathbf R^{d})$ restricts to a
homeomorphism from $M(\omega ,\mathscr B )$ to
$M(\omega _\rho ,\mathscr B _\rho)$,
and
  
\begin{equation}
\label{Eq:FrFTModNorm}
\Vert \mathscr F _\rho f\Vert _{M(\omega _\rho ,\mathscr B _\rho )}
\asymp
\Vert f\Vert _{M(\omega ,\mathscr B )},
\qquad f\in \mathcal H _{\flat _1}'(\mathbf R^{d}),\ \rho \in \mathbf R^{d} .
\end{equation}
  
If, in addition, $\mathscr B$ is strict solid, then

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
It suffices to prove \eqref{Eq:FrFTModNorm}.
Let $\phi _0$ be as in \eqref{Eq:GaussWindow},
and let $f\in \mathcal H _{\flat _1}'(\mathbf R^{d})$.
By (1.35) in \cite{ToBhMa} and \eqref{Eq:FrFTScalesProp},
we obtain
  
\begin{equation}
\label{Eq:FrFTonSTFT}
|V_{\phi _0}(\mathscr F _\rho f)| = |V_{\phi _0} f(A_\rho \, \cdot \,  )| .
\end{equation}
  
This gives
$$
\Vert \mathscr F _\rho f\Vert _{M(\omega _\rho ,\mathscr B _\rho )}
=
\Vert V_\phi (\mathscr F _\rho f)\cdot \omega _\rho\Vert _{\mathscr B _\rho}
\asymp
\Vert V_\phi f(A_\rho \, \cdot \,  )\cdot \omega (A_\rho \, \cdot \,  )\Vert _{\mathscr B _\rho}
=
\Vert V_\phi f\cdot \omega\Vert _{\mathscr B},
$$
which gives \eqref{Eq:FrFTModNorm}. 
If, in addition, $\mathscr B$ is strict solid, then
the previous computations hold with equality at each step. This
implies that \eqref{Eq:FrFTModNorm} holds with equality, and the
result follows.
