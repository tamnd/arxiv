{\rm (see \cite{GG03}) Let $P$ be a dcpo. Then $P$ is meet continuous if and only if for each $U\in\sigma(P)$ and $x\in U$, $\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em}(U\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} x)\in\sigma(P)$.}

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
{\rm  Let $P$ be a meet-continuous dcpo. If $\Sigma P$ is locally compact and coherent, then $\Sigma P$ is sober.}

We  proof the Theorem 3.2 by establishing the following four claims.



$\mathbf{Claim\ 1:}$ Let $K\subseteq P$ be a compact upper set.
If $$\cdot\cdot\cdot\leq x_{n+1}\leq x_{n}\leq\cdot\cdot\cdot\leq x_3\leq x_2\leq x_1$$
is a decreasing sequence in $K$, then $\bigwedge\limits_{1\leq n}x_{n}\in K$.

Let $L=\bigcap\limits_{1\leq n}\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} x_n$. Thus $L$ is exactly the set of all lower bounds of the sequence.

For each $n\geq1$, $K\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} x_n$ is a nonempty closed subset
in the compact subspace $K$.
By the finite intersection property of $K$, we obtain
$$K\cap L=\bigcap_{1\leq n}(K\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} x_n)\neq\emptyset.$$
In particular, $L$ is nonempty. We next show that $L$ is directed.

Let $a,b\in L$. By coherence of $\Sigma P$, $Q=\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} a\cap\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} b$
is compact.
For every $1\leq n$, $a,b\leq x_n$ and then $x_n\in Q\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} x_n$.
Thus the sets $Q\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} x_n$ are nonempty closed
subsets of the compact space $Q$, and they form a decreasing
sequence. Similarly,
it follows that
$$
Q\cap L
=
\bigcap_{1\leq n}(Q\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} x_n)
\neq\emptyset.
$$
Choose $c\in Q\cap L$. Then $a,b\leq c$ and $c\in L$. This proves that $L$ is directed.

Since $P$ is a dcpo, the element
$s=\bigvee L$ exists. Furthermore, we can easily deduce that $s=\bigwedge\limits_{1\leq n}x_n\in L$.

Take a $k\in K\cap L$. Then $k\leq s$ and hence $s\in K$ because $K$ is an upper set. This proves Claim 1.

$\mathbf{Claim \ 2:}$ Let $A\subseteq P$ be an irreducible Scott closed set
and  $\{U_n\}_{1\leq n}$ a sequence of Scott open sets
satisfying for each $1\leq n$, $A\cap U_n\neq\emptyset$.
Then $$A\cap(\bigcap\limits_{1\leq n}U_n)\neq\emptyset.$$


For each $1\leq m$, choose $a_{m}\in A\cap U_m$. As $\Sigma P$ is locally compact, there exist a
 Scott open set $V_m$ and a compact upper set $K_m$ such that $$
a_{m}\in A\cap V_m\ \text{and}\
V_m\subseteq K_m\subseteq U_m.
$$

In the following, we construct a triangular array
$c_{j,n}(1\leq j\leq n)$ with the following properties
$$c_{j,n}\in A\cap V_j,\ c_{j,n}\leq c_{j+1,n}\ \text{and}\ c_{j,n+1}\leq c_{j,n}.$$

Firstly, choose any $c_{1,1}\in A\cap V_1$. Let $$W_{1}=\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em}(V_{1}\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} c_{1,1}).$$ By Lemma 3.1, $W_{1}\in\sigma(P)$. In addition, $c_{11}\in A\cap W_{1}$ and $A\cap V_{2}\neq\emptyset$. The irreducibility of $A$ gives $A\cap W_{1}\cap V_{2}\neq\emptyset$. Choose $c_{2,2}\in A\cap W_{1}\cap V_{2}$. Since $A$ is lower and $c_{1,2}\leq c_{1,1}$, we have $c_{1,2}\in A$ and hence $c_{1,2}\in A\cap V_{1}$. So we have
$$c_{j,2}\in A\cap V_j,j=1,2\ \text{ and }\ c_{1,2}\leq c_{2,2},c_{1,2}\leq c_{1,1}.$$


Suppose that the $n$-th ($2\leq n$) row has been constructed such that
$$c_{1,n}\leq c_{2,n}\leq\cdots\leq c_{n,n}$$ $$\ c_{j,n}\in A\cap V_{j} (1\leq j\leq n)$$ and $$ c_{j,k}\leq c_{j,k-1}(1\leq j\leq k-1,2\leq k\leq n).$$
Let $W_0=P$ and define
$$
W_j
=\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em}\bigl(
V_j\cap W_{j-1}\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} c_{j,n}
\bigr),\ 1\leq j\leq n.
$$
As $W_{1}$ is open, by introduction and using the Lemma 3.1, each $W_{j}$ is Scott-open.

We assert that for each $1\leq j\leq n$, $c_{j,n}\in W_j$.


For $j=1$, this follows directly from $c_{1,n}\in V_1$
and $W_0=P$.
For $j>1$, suppose that $c_{j-1,n}\in W_{j-1}$.
Since $W_{j-1}$ is an upper set and
$c_{j-1,n}\leq c_{j,n}$, we obtain
$c_{j,n}\in W_{j-1}$.
Together with $c_{j,n}\in V_j$, this yields
$$
c_{j,n}\in
V_j\cap W_{j-1}\cap\downarrow c_{j,n}
\subseteq W_j.
$$
The assertion follows by induction.

In particular, $A\cap W_n\neq\emptyset$.
Since also $A\cap V_{n+1}\neq\emptyset$, irreducibility of
$A$ allows us to choose
$$
c_{n+1,n+1}\in A\cap V_{n+1}\cap W_n.

For every $1\leq i\leq n$,
suppose that $c_{i+1,n+1}\in W_i$ has already been chosen.

\in

\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} c_{i,n}
\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} c_{i+1,n+1}.
$$
This choice ensures that
$$
c_{i,n+1}\leq c_{i,n}
\text{ and }
c_{i,n+1}\leq c_{i+1,n+1}.
$$
Moreover, $c_{i,n}\in A$ and $A$ is a lower set, so
$c_{i,n+1}\in A$.
The membership $c_{i,n+1}\in W_{i-1}$ makes the next step
of the reverse construction possible. So we obtain the elements $$c_{1,n+1},c_{2,n+1},\cdots, c_{n+1,n+1}$$ satisfying all requirements. This completes the induction and constructs the required
triangular array.

contained in $V_j\subseteq K_j$.
By Claim 1, the infimum
$d_j=\inf\limits_{j\leq n}c_{j,n}$ belongs to $K_j$.
Since $d_j\leq c_{j,j}\in A$ and $A$ is a lower set, we have $d_j\in A\cap K_j$.

For every $j+1\leq n$, we have $d_j\leq c_{j,n}\leq c_{j+1,n}$.
Hence $d_j$ is a lower bound of the set $\{c_{j+1,n}\mid j+1\leq n\}$.
By the definition of $d_{j+1}$,we get $d_j\leq d_{j+1}$.
Thus $\{d_j\mid 1\leq j\}$ is a chain. As $P$ is a dcpo, $$d=\sup_{1\leq j}d_j$$ exists.

$\mathbf{Claim\ 3:}$ Let $A\subseteq P$ be an irreducible Scott closed set,
and $\{E_n\}_{1\leq n}$ a sequence of Scott open sets such that for each $1\leq n$, $A\cap E_{n}\neq\emptyset$.
There exists
$$
b\in A\cap\bigcap_{1\leq n}E_n
$$
such that $\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} b$ is a $G_\delta$ set in $\Sigma P$.

Set $F_0=P$. Choose any $b_{1}\in A\cap E_{1}$. By the locally compactness, there exist a Scott open set $F_{1}$ and a compact upper set $Q_{1}$ such that $$b_{1}\in A\cap F_{1}\ \text{ and }F_{1}\subseteq Q_{1}\subseteq E_{1}\cap F_{0}.$$

Suppose $F_{n}\in\sigma(P)$ and $Q_{n}\in K(P)$ have been chosen such that
$$A\cap F_n\neq\emptyset\ \text{ and }F_n\subseteq Q_n\subseteq E_n\cap F_{n-1}.$$

As $A\cap E_{n+1}\neq\emptyset$ and $A\cap F_{n}\neq\emptyset$, together with the irreducibility of $A$, we have $A\cap E_{n+1}\cap F_{n}\neq\emptyset$. Take any $b_{n+1}\in A\cap E_{n+1}\cap F_{n}$. Using the locally compactness for the case $b_{n+1}\in E_{n+1}\cap F_{n}\in\sigma(P)$, there are $F_{n+1}\in\sigma(P)$ and $Q_{n+1}\in K(P)$ satisfying $$A\cap F_{n+1}\neq\emptyset\ \text{ and }F_{n+1}\subseteq Q_{n+1}\subseteq E_{n+1}\cap F_{n}.$$

By introduction, this produces the required all $F_n$ and $Q_n$ ($1\leq n$).

The construction implies for each $n$,
$$Q_{n+1}\subseteq F_n\subseteq Q_n.$$
In particular, the sequence $\{Q_n\}_{1\leq n}$ is decreasing.

Let $\mathscr C=\{C\in \Gamma P\mid C\subseteq A\ \text{ and for each}\ 1\leq n, C\cap Q_{n}\neq\emptyset\}$.

The family $\mathscr C$ is nonempty because $A\in\mathscr C$.

Let $\{C_i\mid i\in I\}$ be a nonempty chain in $\mathscr C$
under inclusion and put $\widetilde{C}=\bigcap\limits_{i\in I}C_i$.
The set $\widetilde{C}$ is Scott closed and contained in $A$.
Fix $1\leq n$, each $Q_n\cap C_i$
is a nonempty closed subset of the compact space $Q_n$.
As all $C_i$ form a chain, every finite subfamily of $\{Q_n\cap C_i\mid i\in I\}$ has
nonempty intersection. It follows form $Q_{n}\in K(P)$ that
$$
Q_n\cap \widetilde{C}
=
\bigcap_{i\in I}(Q_n\cap C_i)
\neq\emptyset.
$$
In other words, $\widetilde{C}$ belongs to $\mathscr C$. By Zorn's lemma,  $\mathscr C$ contains a minimal element $B$ under the inclusion order.

Next, we prove that $B$ is irreducible.

$B$ is nonempty because it meets the set $Q_1$. Let $O$ be a Scott open set meeting $B$. Then $B\setminus O$ is a proper Scott closed subset of $B$.
The minimality of $B$ implies that $B\setminus O\not\in\mathscr C$.
Clearly, $B\setminus O$ is closed and contained in $A$. As a consequence, there exists a $Q_{n}$ such that
$$
(B\setminus O)\cap Q_n=\emptyset.
$$
Equivalently, $B\cap Q_n\subseteq O$. Now suppose $O$ and $H$ are Scott open sets meeting $B$.
Choose $Q_{r}$ and $Q_{s}$ such that
$$
B\cap Q_r\subseteq O,
\ \text{ and }
B\cap Q_s\subseteq H.
$$
Take $N=\max\{r,s\}$. As the sequence $\{Q_n\}_{1\leq n}$ is decreasing, we obtain
$$
\emptyset\neq B\cap Q_N\subseteq O\cap H.
$$
Thus $O\cap H\cap B\neq\emptyset$, proving that $B$ is irreducible.


For every $n\geq1$, we have $Q_{n+1}\subseteq F_n\ \text{ and }\ B\cap Q_{n+1}\neq\emptyset$.
Hence every $F_n$ meets $B$.
By Claim 2, we can choose 
$$
b\in B\cap\bigcap_{n\geq1}F_n.

Since $B$ is a lower set containing $b$, we have
$\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} b\subseteq B$.
The set $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} b$ is Scott closed and meets every $Q_n$. Thus $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} b\in\mathscr C$.
Using the minimality of $B$ again, we have $B=\downarrow b$.

$$O_n=\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em}(F_n\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} b).$$
By Lemma 3.1, each $O_n$ is Scott open.
It follows from $b\in F_n$ that $b\in O_n$.
As $O_n$ is an upper set, it yields that
$\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} b\subseteq\bigcap_{n\geq1}O_n$.

Conversely, let $z\in\bigcap\limits_{1\leq n}O_n$.
Then every $O_n$ provides an element $u_n$
such that
$$u_n\in F_n,\ u_n\leq b\ \text{ and }\ u_n\leq z.$$
Consequently, the Scott closed set
$C_{z}=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} b\cap\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} z$
meets every $Q_n$ because
$$u_n\in C_{z}\cap F_n\subseteq C_{z}\cap Q_n.$$
Moreover, $C_{z}\subseteq\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} b=B\subseteq A.$
Hence $C_{z}\in\mathscr C$ and the minimality of $B$ implies
$C_{z}=B=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} b$.
In particular, $b\in\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} z$ or equivalently $b\leq z$.
We conclude that $\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} b=\bigcap_{n\geq1}O_n.$
Thus $\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} b$ is a Scott $G_\delta$ set. The proof of Claim 3 is complete.

$\mathbf{Claim\ 4:}$ $\Sigma P$ is sober.

Let $A\subseteq P$ be an arbitrary irreducible
Scott-closed set and define
$$
D_A=
\{b\in A\mid
  \mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} b\textnormal{ is a }G_\delta
  \textnormal{ set in}\ \Sigma P\}.
$$
We shall show that $D_A$ is a nonempty directed set and further Scott dense
in $A$.

By Claim 3 and take each $E_n=P$,
this gives an element of $D_A$, proving that $D_A\neq\emptyset$.

Next, let $G$ be any Scott open set meeting $A$.
Using  Claim 3 and taking each $E_n=G$, this gives a point $g\in A\cap G$ such that $\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} g$ is
a $G_\delta$ set.
So we have $g\in D_A\cap G$ or alternatively $D_A\cap G\neq\emptyset$.
This proves $\overline{(D_{A})}_{\Sigma P}=A$.

Let $p,q\in D_A$. Then there are Scott open sets
$G_n$ and $H_n$ such that
$$
\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} p=\bigcap_{1\leq n}G_n,\ \mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} q=\bigcap_{1\leq n}H_n.
$$
Clearly, every $G_n$ and $H_n$ meet $A$.
We apply Claim 3 to the interleaved sequence
$$
G_1,H_1,G_2,H_2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,G_n,H_n\cdot\cdot\cdot
$$
and hence there exists a $l\in A$ such that $\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} l$ is a
Scott $G_\delta$ set and
$$
l\in
\left(\bigcap_{1\leq n}G_n\right)
\cap
\left(\bigcap_{1\leq n}H_n\right)
=
\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} p\cap\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} q.
$$
Equivalently, $l\in D_A$ and $ p, q\leq l$. Thus, $D_A$ is directed.

Since $P$ is a dcpo, the supremum
$a^{\ast}=\sup D_A$ exists.
The set $A$ is Scott closed and contains $D_A$. So $A$ contains $a^{\ast}$ and further
$\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} a^{\ast}\subseteq A$ as $A$ is lower.
Besides, $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} a^{\ast}$ is a Scott closed set containing
$D_A$. Hence, $$A=\overline{(D_{A})}_{\Sigma P}\subseteq\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} a^{\ast}.$$ We have proved $A=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} a^{\ast}=\overline{\{a^{\ast}\}}_{\Sigma P}$. Therefore $\Sigma P$ is sober.

Set $$\mathscr{E}=\{\sigma(P)\setminus\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} V_{F}\mid F\text{ is a finite subset of }\mathbb{N}\}.$$

Let $F$ be an irreducible closed subset in $\Sigma P$.

$\mathbf{Claim\ 1:}$ $\sigma(F)=\{U\cap F\mid U\in\sigma(P)\}$.

It is not difficult to check that each $U\cap F$ is Scott open in the subdcpo $F$. Suppose $\widetilde{U}$ is open in $\Sigma F$. Set $\widehat{U}=P\setminus(F\setminus\widetilde{U})$. It follows from $\widetilde{U}\in\sigma(F)$ and $F\in\Gamma P$ that $\widehat{U}\in\sigma(P)$. Besides, $$\widehat{U}\cap F=(P\setminus(F\setminus\widetilde{U}))\cap F=((P\setminus F)\cup\widetilde{U})\cap F=\widetilde{U}.$$ Hence, Claim 1 is proved.

Since $\Sigma P$ is locally compact and second countable, using Claim 1, $\Sigma F$ is also locally compact and second countable.

From now on, all orders, open sets and closures are taken in this
inherited dcpo $F$.

We enumerate a countable base of $\Sigma F$ as $$\mathscr B_0=\{B_n\mid n\in\mathbb N, B_{n} \mbox{ is a nonempty open set in } \Sigma F\}$$ and put
$U_n=\bigcap\limits_{i=0}\limits^{n}B_i$. As $F$ is irreducible, each $U_{n}$ is still nonempty. In addition, for each nonempty $O\in\sigma(F)$, there exist a $j\in\mathbb{N}$ such that $U_{j}\subseteq O$.

In the following, assume that $\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}U_{n}=\emptyset$.

As $\Sigma F$ is locally compact, we can
choose compact saturated subsets $K_{n}\in K(F)$ such that
$$
\varnothing\neq\operatorname{int}(K_n)
\subseteq K_n\subseteq U_n.
$$

By Lemma 4.3, For every nonempty compact subset $K_{n}$,
$K_{n}=\uparrow\operatorname{Min}K_{n}$.


Put $$S=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb N}\operatorname{Min}K_j.$$ As each nonempty subset $\operatorname{Min}K_n$ is contained in $U_{n}$. We assert that $cl(S)_{\Sigma F}=F$.

For all countable ordinals $\lambda$, define
$$S_0=S,\ S_{\alpha+1}=S_\alpha\cap
  \bigcap_{n\in\mathbb N}\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em}(S_\alpha\cap U_n),$$ and if $0\leq\lambda<\omega_{1}$ is a limit ordinal,
$$S_\lambda=\bigcap_{\alpha<\lambda}S_\alpha.$$

This induction gives that $S_\alpha\subseteq S_\beta$ if $\beta\leq\alpha<\omega_1$.


$\mathbf{Claim\ 2:}$ If $s\in S_\alpha,r\in S$ and $ r\leq s$, then $r\in S_\alpha$.

The statement holds when $\alpha=0$.

Suppose this result holds for every countable ordinal $\alpha$. Let $s\in S_{\alpha+1}$ and $r\leq s$ for some $r\in S$. Then $s\in S_{\alpha}$ and for each $n\in\mathbb{N}$, there exists $s_{n}\in S_{\alpha}\cap U_{n}$ satisfying $s\leq s_{n}$. So $r\leq s_{n}$ and further $r\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb N}\downarrow(S_\alpha\cap U_n)$.
 By assumption, $r\in S_{\alpha}$. Thus, $r\in S_{\alpha+1}$.

Suppose $\alpha< \omega_{1}$ is a limit ordinal and the statement is valid for all $\beta<\lambda$. The construction of $S_{\alpha}=\bigcap\limits_{\beta<\alpha}S_\beta$ shows that $S_{\alpha}$ is a lower set in $S$.

Therefore, Claim 2 is proved.

$\mathbf{Claim\ 3:}$ $\forall\ \alpha<\omega_1$, $cl(S_\alpha)_{\Sigma F}=F$.

We proceed by transfinite induction on $\alpha$.
The case $\alpha=0$ is immediate by the fact $cl(S)_{\Sigma F}=F$.

Suppose first that $\alpha<\omega_{1}$ is not a limit ordinal and $cl(S_\alpha)_{\Sigma F}=F$.
Set $$A_n=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em}(S_\alpha\cap U_n).$$ As $F$ is irreducible in $\Sigma F$, we deduce that $cl(A_n)_{\Sigma F}=F$. By Lemma 4.2, $cl(\bigcap\limits_n A_n)_{\Sigma F}=F$. Let $\widetilde{O}\in\sigma(F)$ be a nonempty open set. We can fix a $U_{j}\subseteq \widetilde{O}$. Choose a $$z\in \operatorname{int}K_j\cap\bigcap\limits_n A_n.$$ Then there exist a $m\in{\rm Min}(K_{j})$ satisfying $m\leq z$. At the same time, for each $n$, we can fix a $t_{n}\in S_{\alpha}\cap U_{n}$ such that $z\leq t_{n}$.
By Claim 2, $m\in S_{\alpha}$ and further $m\in\bigcap\limits_{n}A_{n}$. So we obtain $$m\in S_{\alpha+1}\cap{\rm Min}(K_{j})\subseteq S_{\alpha+1}\cap K_{j}\subseteq S_{\alpha+1}\cap U_{j}\subseteq S_{\alpha+1}\cap\widetilde{O}.$$ Now, we assert that $cl(S_{\alpha+1})_{\Sigma F}=F$. This proves the successor step.






Finally, suppose that
$\alpha$ is a nonzero limit ordinal. Suppose that $cl(S_\beta)_{\Sigma F}=F$ for every
$\beta<\alpha$. Choose an increasing cofinal
sequence $\{\beta_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ such that
$$\beta_n<\alpha \text{ and } \sup\limits_n\beta_n=\alpha.$$ Then we get $S_\alpha=\bigcap\limits_n S_{\beta_n}$. By assumption and Lemma 4.2,

$$
cl(\bigcap_n\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} S_{\beta_n})_{\Sigma F}
=
\bigcap_n cl(\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} S_{\beta_n})_{\Sigma F}
=F.
$$
For any nonempty open set $\widehat{O}$, choose
$$

\text{ and }z_{1}\in\operatorname{int}K_i \cap\bigcap_n\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} S_{\beta_n}.$$  Select a $m_{1}\in\operatorname{Min}K_i$ such that $m_{1}\leq z_{1}$.

For every index $n$, there is
$r_n\in S_{\beta_n}$ satisfying $z_{1}\leq r_n$. By Claim 2 again, $m_{1}\in\bigcap\limits_{n} S_{\beta_n}$. It follows from $$\operatorname{Min}K_i\subseteq K_{i}\subseteq U_{i}\subseteq\widehat{O}$$ that $m_{1}\in S_{\alpha}\cap \widehat{O}$. Consequently, $cl(S_{\alpha})_{\Sigma F}=F$. We finish the proof of Claim 3.

Furthermore, by Claim 3 and the irreducibility of $F$, it follows that for each $n$ and $\alpha$,
$$cl(S_\alpha\cap U_n)_{\Sigma F}=F.$$

By Lemma 4.6, $\Lambda(\sigma(P))$ is a compact metrizable space. Define a mapping $c:\Gamma(P)\longrightarrow\sigma(P)$ by $c(A)=P\setminus A$. The topology $\tau$ on $\Gamma(P)$ is given by

Then $M=(\Gamma(P),\tau)$ is also a compact metrizable space because $c$ is a homeomorphism between $M$ and $\Lambda(\sigma(P))$.



Define the order embedding $\eta:F\longrightarrow M$ by $\eta(x)=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} x$. For each open set $U$, let
$$
\Diamond U=\{A\in\Gamma(P)\mid A\cap U\neq\varnothing\}.

Then we have $$c(\Diamond U)=\{B\in\sigma(P)\mid (P\setminus B)\cap U\neq\emptyset\}=\sigma(P)\setminus\mathord{\uparrow}\hspace{0.05em} U\in\lambda(\sigma(P)).$$ In other words, $\Diamond U\in\tau$. In addition, $\eta^{-1}(\Diamond U)=U$ as $U$ is upper in $P$.

It follows from the compactness of $K$ that $$\Phi(K)

$c(M\setminus Q_K)=\Phi(K)$. Consequently, $Q_K$ is closed in $M$. Hence $Q_K$ is compact.

$\mathbf{Claim\ 4:}$ $\operatorname{Min}Q_K=\eta(\operatorname{Min}K)$.

Let $A\in\operatorname{Min}Q_K$. Choose a $k\in A\cap K$. Then $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} k\in Q_{K}$ and $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} k\subseteq A$. The minimality of $A$ implies $A=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} k=\eta(k)$. Suppose there is a $k_{1}\in K$ such that $k_{1}\leq k$. Then $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} k_{1}\in Q_{K}$ and $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} k_{1}\subseteq\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} k$. Using $A\in\operatorname{Min}Q_K$ again, $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} k_{1}=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} k$. Equivalently, $k\in\operatorname{Min}K$.

Conversely, choose any $k\in\operatorname{Min}K$. Suppose $B\in Q_K$ and $B\subseteq\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} k$. Then we can fix a $b\in B\cap K$ such that $b\leq k$. It follows from $k\in\operatorname{Min}K$ that $b=k$. As $B$ is lower in $P$, $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} b=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} k\subseteq B$. As a result, $B=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} k$, proving that $\eta(k)\in\operatorname{Min}Q_K$.

Fix a metric $d:M\times M\longrightarrow[0,\infty)$. For every positive integer $r$, define

\left\{

As $Q_{K}$ is compact in $M$, each $H_r(K)$ is compact in $M^{2}$.

$\mathbf{Claim\ 5:}$ $\operatorname{Min}Q_K$ is a $G_{\delta}$ subset in $M$.

=

It follows from $\widehat{A}\not\in\operatorname{Min}Q_K=\eta(\operatorname{Min}K)$ that $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em}\hat{k}$ is a proper subset of $\widehat{A}$. This means that $\frac{1}{\hat{r}}\leq d(\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em}\hat{k},\widehat{A})$ for some positive integer $\hat{r}$. Consequently, $\widehat{A}\in\pi_2(H_{\hat{r}}(K))$.

Conversely, let $\widetilde{A}\in\pi_2(H_{\tilde{r}}(K))$ ($1\leq\tilde{r}$). Fix a $\widetilde{B}\in Q_{K}$, $\widetilde{B}\subseteq \widetilde{A}$ and $\frac{1}{\tilde{r}}\leq d(\widetilde{B},\widetilde{A})$. So we have $\widetilde{B}\neq\widetilde{A}$. Hence $\widetilde{A}$ is not a minimal element in $Q_{K}$.

Now, we have proved $Q_K\setminus\operatorname{Min}Q_K=\bigcup\limits_{r\geq1}\pi_2(H_r(K))$. As a consequence, $$\operatorname{Min}Q_K=Q_K\setminus\bigcup\limits_{r\geq1}\pi_2(H_r(K))=Q_K\cap(\bigcap\limits_{1\leq r}(P\setminus\pi_2(H_r(K)))).$$
By Lemma 4.9, the closed set $Q_{K}$ is a $G_{\delta}$ set. Hence, $\operatorname{Min}Q_K$ is also a $G_{\delta}$ set in $M$. The Claim 5 is proved.


For each $n,j\in\mathbb{N}$, set
$$E_{n,j}=\operatorname{Min}Q_{K_j}\cap\Diamond U_n.$$

By Lemma 4.9, Lemma 4.10 and Claim 5, each subspace $(E_{n,j},\tau|_{E_{n,j}})$ is Polish. Consider the topological sum
$$
Z=\coprod_{(n,j)\in\mathbb N^2}E_{n,j}.$$
By Lemma 4.11, $Z$ is polish. Note that $A\in E_{n,j}$ if and only if $A=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} a$ for some $a\in\operatorname{Min}K_j\cap U_n$. For convenience, we can write each element of $Z$ as
$$(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s),\text{ for some } s\in\operatorname{Min}K_j\cap U_n.$$ Define a mapping
$\pi:Z\longrightarrow M$ by $\pi(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)=\eta(s)$. Clearly, the map $\pi$ is continuous by the definition of the topological sum.

Define a relation $\prec$ on $Z$ by
$$
(n+1,k,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t)\prec(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s\subseteq\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t.$$

$\mathbf{Claim\ 6:}$ There exists a $\gamma<\omega_{1}$ such that
$\forall(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)\in Z,\ \rho_{\prec}(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)<\gamma$.



We assert that the relation $\prec$ is a analytic well-founded relation.

Suppose $(x_{m},y_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\prec$ is a sequence converging to $(x,y)$. For convenience, let $$x_{m}=(n_{m}+1,k_{m},\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t_{m})\text{ and }y_{m}=(n_{m},j_{m},\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s_{m})$$ with $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s_{m}\subseteq\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t_{m}$. As different parts $E_{n,j}$ are disjoint and clopen, we can fix $x\in E_{p,q}$,$y\in E_{l,r}$ and $N\in\mathbb{N}$ such that for each $N\leq m$, $(x_{m},y_{m})\in E_{p,q}\times E_{l,r}$. Then we obtain
$$p=n_{m}+1,q=k_{m},l=n_{m},r=j_{m}(N\leq m).$$
Now $\{(p,q,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t_{m})\}_{N\leq m}$ and $\{(l,r,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s_{m})\}_{N\leq m}$ are two sequences converging to $x$ and $y$ in $E_{p,q}$ and $E_{l,r}$ respectively. For convenience, we fix $x=(p,q,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t)$ and $y=(l,r,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)$. As a consequence, $\{\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t_{m}\}_{N\leq m}$ and $\{\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s_{m}\}_{N\leq m}$ converge to $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t$ and $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s$ in $M$ respectively. Then the sequence $\{(\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s_{m},\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t_{m})\}_{N\leq m}$ contained in $\mathscr{H}$ converges to $(\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t)$, where $\mathscr{H}=\{(A,B)\in M^{2}\mid A\subseteq B\}$. By Lemma 4.8, $$\mathscr{H}=(c\times c)^{-1}(\{(U,V)\in\sigma(F)\times\sigma(F)\mid V\subseteq U\})$$ is closed in $M^{2}$. Then we have $(\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t)\in\mathscr{H}$ since $M^{2}$ is metrizable. In other words, $\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s\subseteq\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t$ and hence $x\prec y$. This proves that $\prec\subseteq Z^{2}$ is sequentially closed and further closed. Note that every closed set is a analytic set. So we deduce that $\prec$ is analytic.


Assume that the relation $\prec$ fails to be well-founded. Then there is a decreasing chain $$(n_{0},j_{0},\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s_{0})\succ(n_{1},j_{1},\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s_{1})\succ\cdot\cdot\cdot\succ(n_{i},j_{i},\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s_{i})\succ(n_{i+1},j_{i+1},\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s_{i+1})\succ\cdot\cdot\cdot.$$ By the definition of $\prec$, we have
$$
s_0\leq s_1\leq\cdots\leq s_{i}\leq s_{i+1}\leq\cdots
\text{ and }
s_i\in U_{n_0+i}.
$$
As $F$ is Scott closed in $P$, $s=\sup\limits_i\limits^{P} s_i\in F$. For each $n$, choose a $i$ such that $n\leq n_{0}+i$. Then we have
$$s_i\in U_{n_0+i}\subseteq U_n$$ and further $s\in U_n$ because $U_{n}$ is upper. This shows that $s\in\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}U_{n}$, a contradiction. Hence $\prec$ is well-founded.

We determine the measure $\mathbf{S}$ on $Z$ by taking $\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{B}(\tau_{Z})$, where $\tau_{Z}$ is the topology on $Z$. Now $(Z,\mathbf{S})$ is a measure space and $\prec$ is analytic on $Z\times Z$ and well-founded. Using Lemma 4.12, Claim 6 is valid.


$\mathbf{Claim\ 7:}$ $\forall\alpha<\omega_1,\forall(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)\in Z$, if $ s\in S_\alpha$, then $\rho_{\prec}(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)\geq\alpha$.


The initial case $\alpha=0$ is immediate.

Suppose the condition holds when $0<\alpha<\omega_{1}$ is not a limit ordinal. $\forall(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)\in Z$ with $s\in S_{\alpha+1}$,
$$\exists t\in S_\alpha\cap U_{n+1},\ s\leq t.
$$
Since $S_{\alpha}\subseteq S_{0}=\bigcup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm Min}K_{j}$, we can choose $k$ such that $t\in\operatorname{Min}K_k$.
Then $(n+1,k,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t)\prec(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)$ and hence $\rho_{\prec}(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)\geq\rho_{\prec}(n+1,k,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} t)+1\geq\alpha+1.$ This shows the successor step.


Suppose $\alpha$ is not a nonzero limit ordinal and the statement hold for all $\beta<\alpha$. Choose any $(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)\in Z$ and $s\in S_{\alpha}$. Then $s\in\bigcap\limits_{\beta<\alpha}S_{\beta}$ and by assumption
$\rho_{\prec}(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)\geq\beta$. Hence we obtain $$\alpha=\bigvee\limits_{\beta<\alpha}\beta\leq\rho_{\prec}(n,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s).$$

Therefore, Claim 7 holds for all countable ordinals.


By Claim 3, we take $\gamma$ the ordinal number presented in Claim 6 and get $cl(S_\gamma)_{\Sigma F}=F$. As $U_0\neq\varnothing$, there exists $s\in S_\gamma\cap U_0$. Choose a label $j$ such that $s\in\operatorname{Min}K_j$. This label $j$ must exist as $S_\gamma\subseteq S_{0}$. By Claim 7 and Claim 6, $\gamma\leq\rho(0,j,\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em} s)<\gamma$, a contradiction.

Therefore, the assumption $\bigcap\limits_{n\in \mathbb{N}}=\emptyset$ is impossible.

Now we have $\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}} U_{n}\neq\emptyset$ and choose any  $p\in\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}} U_{n}$. Then $p$ belongs to each nonempty open set in $\Sigma F$. We deduce that $p$ is the largest element in $F$. Consequently, $$F=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em}_{F} p=\mathord{\downarrow}\hspace{0.05em}_{P}p=cl(\{p\})_{\Sigma P}.$$ Hence $\Sigma P$ is sober.
