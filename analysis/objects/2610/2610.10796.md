<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
There is a noncircular oval \(\Gamma_0\) such that \(\mathcal T^n\Gamma_0\) is an
oval for every integer \(n\geq0\).
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:inverse}
Let \(F\) be a regular convex closed \(C^2\) curve with curvature
\(0\leq K\leq K_0<1\). There is a unique periodic solution of
\[
  \delta_s=K-\sin\delta,\qquad 0\leq\delta\leq\arcsin K_0.
\]
It is positive and defines a regular convex closed rear
\(\mathcal B F\) with curvature
\[
  0<k\leq\frac{K_0}{\sqrt{1-K_0^2}}.
\]
The map \(\mathcal B\) preserves central symmetry and its center, is continuous in
geometric \(C^2\), and carries smooth families of fronts to smooth families of
rears. Every \(C^r\) front with \(r\geq2\) has a \(C^{r+1}\) rear.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The period map advances a solution by one circuit of length \(L\). Since the
steering vector field points into the interval \(I=[0,\arcsin K_0]\) at both
endpoints, this map sends \(I\) into itself. By the mean value theorem, the
difference \(w\) of two solutions in \(I\) satisfies
\[
  w_s=-a(s)w,\qquad a(s)\geq\sqrt{1-K_0^2}>0,
\]
so the period map is a contraction on \(I\) and therefore has a unique fixed
point. Every periodic solution on the larger branch \(0<\delta<\pi/2\) also
lies in \(I\), because at a maximum of \(\delta\) the differential equation
gives \(\sin\delta\leq K_0\).

Since \(\delta_s+\delta=K+\delta-\sin\delta\geq K\), an integrating factor
over a period gives
\[
  (1-e^{-L})\delta(s)
  \geq\int_{s-L}^{s}e^{t-s}K(t)\,dt>0.
\]
The last inequality is strict because \(K\geq0\) and
\(\int_0^L K(s)\,ds=2\pi\).

Choose a tangent-angle lift \(\Theta\) for \(F\), put
\(\Psi=\Theta-\delta\), and define
\[
  R(s)=F(s)-\tau(\Psi(s)).
\]
Then \(R_s=\cos\delta\,\tau(\Psi)\), so \(R\) is regular and closed, and
\(\mathcal T R=F\). Since \(0<\delta\leq\arcsin K_0\), the curvature
\(k=\tan\delta\) of \(R\) satisfies \(0<k\leq K_0/\sqrt{1-K_0^2}\). Because
\(\delta\) is periodic, the tangent of \(R\) turns through \(2\pi\), so \(R\)
is embedded and bounds a strictly convex body. By uniqueness, if \(F\) is
centrally symmetric, then \(\delta\) has period \(L/2\) and \(\tau(\Psi)\)
reverses after \(L/2\), so \(R\) is centrally symmetric with the same center.

In normalized front arclength, the period map for fronts near \(F\) is a
uniform contraction that varies continuously with \(K\). Thus its fixed point
varies continuously with \(K\), and the periodic solution \(\delta\) depends
continuously in \(C^1\) on the front in \(C^2\). Since \(x_s=\cos\delta>0\),
the rear and its arclength parametrization also depend continuously on the
front in \(C^2\). The same argument gives smooth dependence on parameters.

For regularity, a \(C^r\) front has a \(C^{r-1}\) tangent angle \(\Theta\).
The equation \(\Psi_s=\sin(\Theta-\Psi)\) gives a \(C^r\) solution \(\Psi\),
and \(x_s=\cos(\Theta-\Psi)>0\) gives a \(C^r\) change of arclength. Thus
the rear tangent is \(C^r\) in rear arclength and the rear curve is
\(C^{r+1}\).

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:translator-equation}
Let \(f\in C^\infty(0,\pi)\) satisfy \(f\geq m>1\). If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:translator-integral}
  \int_\theta^{\theta+d(\theta)}f(t)\,dt=\sin\theta,
  \qquad d(\theta)=\arctan\frac{\sin\theta}{f(\theta)},
\end{equation}
then \(g=\theta+d\) is an increasing diffeomorphism of \((0,\pi)\), and the
curve \(C\) defined by \eqref{eq:XZ} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:translator-vector}
  C(\theta)+\tau(\theta)=C(g(\theta))+(V,0)
\end{equation}
for a constant \(V\).
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Differentiating \eqref{eq:translator-integral} gives
\[
  f(g)\,g'=f+\cos\theta>0.
\]
Since \(0<d\leq\sin\theta/m<\pi-\theta\), the endpoint limits of \(g\)
are \(0\) and \(\pi\), so \(g\) is an increasing diffeomorphism of
\((0,\pi)\). The integral identity gives the vertical component of
\eqref{eq:translator-vector}.

For the horizontal component, the cotangent addition formula with
\(\cot d=f/\sin\theta\) gives \((f+\cos\theta)\cot g=f\cot\theta-\sin\theta\).
Since \((X\circ g)'=f(g)g'\cot g=(f+\cos\theta)\cot g\), we obtain
\[
  \frac d{d\theta}\bigl(X(\theta)+\cos\theta-X(g(\theta))\bigr)
  =f\cot\theta-\sin\theta-(f+\cos\theta)\cot g=0.
  \qedhere
\]
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:Pmonotone}
For every bounded measurable function \(f:(0,\pi)\to\mathbf{R}\) with \(f\geq m>1\),
the function \(\mathcal P f\) is positive and continuous on \((0,\pi)\).
Moreover, \(\mathcal P\) is order preserving, and \(f=\mathcal P f\) if and
only if \(f\) satisfies \eqref{eq:translator-integral}.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The primitive \(A_f\) and its inverse are continuous, and
\(0<D_f\leq\sin\theta/m<\pi/2\), so \(\mathcal P f\) is positive and
continuous. If \(f\leq h\), then the integral of \(h\) reaches \(\sin\theta\)
no later than that of \(f\), so \(D_h\leq D_f\) and
\(\mathcal P f\leq\mathcal P h\). Finally, \(f=\mathcal P f\) is
equivalent to \eqref{eq:translator-integral} by the definition of \(D_f\).
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:barriers}
For all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
  f_\varepsilon^-\leq\mathcal P f_\varepsilon^-\leq\mathcal P f_\varepsilon^+
  \leq f_\varepsilon^+\qquad(0<\theta<\pi).
\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For \(d=\arctan(\sin\theta/f(\theta))\), put
\[
  \mathcal R(f)=\int_\theta^{\theta+d}f(t)\,dt-\sin\theta.
\]
The integral increases with its upper limit, and cotangent decreases. Hence
\[
  \mathcal R(f)\geq0\iff D_f\leq d\iff\mathcal P f\geq f,
\]
and the reversed inequalities are equivalent as well.

Each barrier has the form \(f(\theta)=A+B\cos\theta\). Exact integration
gives
\[
  \mathcal R(f)=f(\theta)\,d-\sin\theta
  +B\sin\theta\,(\cos d-1)+B\cos\theta\,(\sin d-d).
\]
Since \(f(\theta)=\sin\theta\,\cot d\),
\[
  \frac{2\mathcal R(f)}{d^2\sin\theta}
  =\frac{2(d\cot d-1)}{d^2}
   +B\frac{2(\cos d-1)}{d^2}
   +\frac{B\cos\theta}{f(\theta)}
        \frac{2(\sin d-d)}{d^2\tan d}.
\]
In the variable \(d\), the three quotients extend to even analytic functions at
\(d=0\) with values \(-2/3\), \(-1\), and \(-1/3\) and remainders \(O(d^2)\).
For \(f=f_\varepsilon^\pm\) with \(b=\pm1\), we have \(B=-2/3+\varepsilon b\),
\(f(\theta)^{-1}=\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)\), and \(0<d\leq 2\varepsilon\sin\theta\), so
Taylor's theorem at \(d=0\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \frac{2\mathcal R(f)}{d^2\sin\theta}
  &=-\frac23-B-\frac{B\cos\theta}{3f(\theta)}+O(\varepsilon^2)\\
  &=\varepsilon\left(\frac29\cos\theta-b\right)+O(\varepsilon^2),
\end{aligned}
\]
uniformly for \(0<\theta<\pi\). Thus the leading coefficient is at least
\(7/9\) for \(f_\varepsilon^-\) (\(b=-1\)), which gives
\(f_\varepsilon^-\leq\mathcal P f_\varepsilon^-\), and at most \(-7/9\) for \(f_\varepsilon^+\)
(\(b=1\)), which gives \(\mathcal P f_\varepsilon^+\leq f_\varepsilon^+\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:Pmonotone} gives \(\mathcal P f_\varepsilon^-\leq\mathcal P f_\varepsilon^+\).
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:hairpin}
For every sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), equation
\eqref{eq:translator-integral} has a solution \(f\in C^\infty(0,\pi)\)
with \(f_\varepsilon^-\leq f\leq f_\varepsilon^+\). The corresponding curve \(C\) is a
hairpin in a strip of finite width and satisfies
\[
  \mathcal T C=C+(V,0)
\]
for some \(V>0\).
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Starting with \(f_0=f_\varepsilon^-\), define \(f_{n+1}=\mathcal P f_n\).
Lemmas~\ref{lem:Pmonotone} and \ref{lem:barriers} give
\[
  f_\varepsilon^-\leq f_0\leq f_1\leq\cdots\leq f_\varepsilon^+.
\]
Let \(f=\lim_n f_n\) on \((0,\pi)\). The functions \(f_n\) share a lower bound
\(m>1\), and monotone convergence gives
\(\int_0^\pi(f-f_n)(t)\,dt\to0\). Since \(f_n\leq f\), we have
\(D_{f_n}\geq D_f\), and comparing the two defining integrals yields
\[
  0\leq D_{f_n}(\theta)-D_f(\theta)
  \leq\frac1m\int_0^\pi(f-f_n)(t)\,dt\longrightarrow0.
\]
Passing to the limit in \(f_{n+1}=\mathcal P f_n\) gives
\(f=\mathcal P f\), so \(f\) satisfies \eqref{eq:translator-integral}.

Since the fixed-point identity makes \(f\) continuous, \(A_f\) and its
inverse are \(C^1\), and hence so are \(D_f\) and \(f\). Iterating this
argument gives \(f\in C^\infty(0,\pi)\).

Define \(C\) by \eqref{eq:XZ}. Its radius of curvature is
\(f/\sin\theta>0\), and \(Z'>0\) makes it embedded. Since \(m\leq f\leq M\)
for some \(M<\infty\), at both ends the curve has infinite arclength,
\(X\) tends to \(-\infty\), and \(Z\) has a finite limit. Thus the ends are
asymptotic to horizontal lines. Lemma~\ref{lem:translator-equation} gives
\(\mathcal T C=C+(V,0)\), and evaluating \eqref{eq:translator-vector} at
\(\theta=\pi/2\) yields
\[
  V=-\int_{\pi/2}^{g(\pi/2)}f(t)\cot t\,dt>0.
  \qedhere
\]
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:pulse}
There is a constant \(a>0\) such that, for every integer \(j\geq0\), there are
constants \(C_j,D_j>0\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pulse-bounds}
  \lvert y^{(j)}(s)\rvert\leq C_j e^{-a\lvert s\rvert},\qquad
  \lvert y^{(j)}(s)\rvert\leq D_j\,y(s).
\end{equation}
Moreover, \(K_*(s)\geq b_0\,y(s)\) for a constant \(b_0>0\), and
\[
  \int_\mathbf{R} y(s)\,ds=\pi,\qquad
  \Delta=\int_\mathbf{R}(1-c(s))\,ds>0.
\]
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let \(u\) be rear arclength and \(\theta(u)\) the rear tangent angle, and set
\(r(u)=\tan(\theta(u)/2)\). Then
\[
  (\log r)_u=\frac{\theta_u}{\sin\theta}=\frac1{f(\theta)},
  \qquad K_*(u)=\frac{\sin\theta(u)}{f(\theta(u))}.
\]
Since \(m\leq f\leq M\), both \(\sin\theta\) and \(K_*\) have two-sided
exponential bounds at the ends. By \eqref{eq:hairpin-curvatures},
\(0<1-x'=1-c\leq y^2/c=K_*(x)^2x'\). Substituting \(u=x(s)\) gives
\[
  0<\Delta=\int_\mathbf{R}(1-c(s))\,ds\leq\int_\mathbf{R} K_*(u)^2\,du<\infty,
\]
so \(s-x(s)\) is bounded on \(\mathbf{R}\). Because \((\log r)_u=1/f\) is bounded,
shifting the argument by \(s-x(s)\) changes \(r\),
\(\sin\theta=2r/(1+r^2)\), and \(K_*=\sin\theta/f\) by bounded factors. Thus
\[
  K_*(s)\geq b_0\,K_*(x(s))=b_0\frac{y(s)}{c(s)}\geq b_0\,y(s)
\]
and \(K_*(s)=O(y(s))\), together with the exponential bound for \(y(s)\) in
\eqref{eq:pulse-bounds}.

We prove \(\lvert y^{(j)}(s)\rvert\leq D_j\,y(s)\) by induction on \(j\geq0\).
Take \(D_0=1\), and suppose that the bound holds through order \(j\).
Since \(0<y<1/20\) and \(c=\sqrt{1-y^2}\), through order \(j\) the
derivatives of \(c\) are bounded and those of \(y/c\) are \(O(y)\).
Applying \((c^{-1}\partial_s)^i\) to \(K_*(x(s))=y(s)/c(s)\) yields
\(K_*^{(i)}(x(s))=O(y(s))=O(K_*(x(s)))\) for \(0\leq i\leq j\).
Since \(x(\mathbf{R})=\mathbf{R}\), we have \(K_*^{(i)}=O(K_*)=O(y)\) for
\(0\leq i\leq j\). Taking the \(j\)th derivative of \(y'=c\,(K_*-y)\)
then gives \(\lvert y^{(j+1)}\rvert\leq D_{j+1}\,y\), completing the induction and
proving \eqref{eq:pulse-bounds}. Finally, the change of variables
\(y(s)\,ds=K_*(u)\,du\) gives \(\int_\mathbf{R} y(s)\,ds=\pi\), since the rear
tangent turns through \(\pi\).

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:periodization}
There is a constant \(\beta>0\) such that, for every integer \(r\geq0\), there
is a constant \(C_r>0\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:periodization}
  \lVert Y_H-y\rVert_{C^r(I_H)}
  \leq C_re^{-\beta H}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large \(H\).
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For \(s\in I_H\) and \(m\ne0\), we have \(\lvert s-mH\rvert\geq(\lvert m\rvert-1/2)H\), so
the exponential bounds in Lemma~\ref{lem:pulse} justify termwise
differentiation and summation and give \eqref{eq:periodization}.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:front-error}
For all sufficiently large \(H\),
\[
  \lvert K_H(s)-\overline K_H(s)\rvert\leq Ce^{-\beta H}Y_H(s).
\]
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Put \(y_m(s)=y(s-mH)\) and \(G(z)=(1-z^2)^{-1/2}\). The formulas for \(K_H\)
and \(K_*\) give
\[
  K_H-\overline K_H
  =\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}} y_m'\bigl(G(Y_H)-G(y_m)\bigr).
\]
For large \(H\), all arguments of \(G\) lie in a fixed interval
\([0,b]\) with \(b<1\), on which \(G\) is Lipschitz. Since
\(\lvert y_m'\rvert\leq D_1 y_m\) by \eqref{eq:pulse-bounds}, the sum is bounded in
absolute value by \(C\sum_{m\ne n}y_m\,y_n\). For \(n=m+\ell\) with
\(\ell\ne0\), at least one of \(\lvert s-mH\rvert\) and \(\lvert s-nH\rvert\) is at least
\(\lvert \ell\rvert H/2\), so \eqref{eq:pulse-bounds} gives
\[
  y_m\,y_n\leq Ce^{-a\lvert \ell\rvert H/2}(y_m+y_n).
\]
Summing over \(m\) and then over \(\ell\ne0\) completes the proof.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:pairs}
For all sufficiently large \(H\), the functions \(k_H\) and \(K_H\) are the
curvatures of smooth centrally symmetric ovals \(R_H\) and \(F_H\) with
\(\mathcal T R_H=F_H\) and \(R_H=\mathcal B F_H\). Their perimeters are \(2P(H)\)
and \(2H\), respectively, where
\[
  P(H)=\int_0^H c_H(s)\,ds=H-\Delta+O(e^{-\beta H}).
\]
The function \(P\) is continuous, and the curvatures of both curves are at
most \(1/10\).
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Choose \(\Theta_H\) with \(\Theta_H'=K_H\), and define \(F_H\) by
\(F_H'=\tau(\Theta_H)\). Since
\(\delta_H\) is \(H\)-periodic and \(\int_0^H Y_H(s)\,ds=\int_\mathbf{R} y(s)\,ds=\pi\),
\[
  \int_0^H K_H\,ds=\int_0^H(\delta_H'+Y_H)\,ds=\pi.
\]
The tangent reverses after each interval of length \(H\), so \(F_H\)
closes after two periods and is centrally symmetric. Set
\[
  \Psi_H=\Theta_H-\delta_H,\qquad R_H=F_H-\tau(\Psi_H).
\]
Then \(\Psi_H'=Y_H\) and \(R_H'=c_H\,\tau(\Psi_H)\), so \(R_H\) is the
corresponding rear, also closed and centrally symmetric, with half-perimeter
\(P(H)\) and positive curvature \(k_H\). Since \(\tau(\Psi_H)\) also reverses
after each interval of length \(H\), the curves \(R_H\) and \(F_H\) have the
same center of symmetry.

Lemmas~\ref{lem:pulse} and \ref{lem:front-error} give
\[
  K_H\geq\overline K_H-Ce^{-\beta H}Y_H
       \geq(b_0-Ce^{-\beta H})Y_H>0
\]
for large \(H\). Both closed curves have positive curvature and turning
number \(1\), and hence are ovals. On \(I_H\), estimate
\eqref{eq:periodization} gives
\[
  K_H=K_*+O(e^{-\beta H}),\qquad
  \frac{Y_H}{c_H}=\frac yc+O(e^{-\beta H}).
\]
Since the front and rear hairpin curvatures \(K_*\) and \(y/c\) are less than
\(1/20\), periodicity bounds the curvatures of \(R_H\) and \(F_H\) by \(1/10\)
for all sufficiently large \(H\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse} gives
\(R_H=\mathcal B F_H\).

By \eqref{eq:periodization}, \(c_H=c+O(e^{-\beta H})\) on \(I_H\), whereas
\(1-c=O(y^2)\) decays exponentially outside \(I_H\). Since \(c_H\) is
\(H\)-periodic, we have
\[
  H-P(H)=\int_{I_H}(1-c_H(s))\,ds
        =\int_\mathbf{R}(1-c(s))\,ds+O(e^{-\beta H})
        =\Delta+O(e^{-\beta H}),
\]
where decreasing \(\beta\) absorbs polynomial factors in \(H\). The same
estimates show that \(P\) is continuous.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:width}
There is a constant \(C_W>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(H\),
the oval \(F_H\) has width at most \(C_W\) in some direction.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Rotate \(F_H\) so that its tangent angle \(\Theta_H\) increases from \(0\) to
\(\pi\) on \(I_H\). Its vertical width is
\[
  W_H=\int_{-H/2}^{H/2}\sin\Theta_H(s)\,ds.
\]
On \(I_H\), Lemmas~\ref{lem:pulse} and \ref{lem:front-error} give
\(K_H(s)\leq CY_H(s)\leq C'e^{-a\lvert s\rvert}\), so
\[
  \sin\Theta_H(s)\leq\min\bigl\{\Theta_H(s),\,\pi-\Theta_H(s)\bigr\}
  =\min\left\{\int_{-H/2}^s K_H(t)\,dt,\,\int_s^{H/2} K_H(t)\,dt\right\}
  \leq\frac{C'}a e^{-a\lvert s\rvert}.
\]
Integrating over \(I_H\) gives \(W_H\leq 2C'/a^2\).

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:matching}
There are constants \(C,\beta>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large
\(H\),
\[
  \int_{\mathbf{R}/P(H)\mathbf{Z}}\lvert k_H(u)-K_{P(H)}(u)\rvert\,du
  \leq Ce^{-\beta H}.
\]
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Put \(P=P(H)\) and \(J_H=x_H(I_H)\). The interval \(J_H\) has length \(P\),
so its translates by \(P\mathbf{Z}\) tile the line.

On \(I_H\), Lemma~\ref{lem:periodization} and the positive lower bounds for
\(c_H\) and \(c\) give
\[
  \lVert c_H-c\rVert_{L^\infty(I_H)}
  +\lVert Y_H/c_H-y/c\rVert_{L^\infty(I_H)}
  \leq Ce^{-\beta H}.
\]
Integrating \(x_H'-x'=c_H-c\) with \(x_H(0)=x(0)\) yields
\(\lVert x_H-x\rVert_{L^\infty(I_H)}\leq CHe^{-\beta H}\). Since
\(k_H(x_H)=Y_H/c_H\), \(K_*(x)=y/c\), and \(K_*'\) is bounded,
\[
  \lVert k_H-K_*\rVert_{L^\infty(J_H)}
  =\lVert k_H(x_H)-K_*(x_H)\rVert_{L^\infty(I_H)}
  \leq CHe^{-\beta H}.
\]
Because \(P\leq H\) and \(K_*>0\), unfolding the tail of \(\overline K_P\) gives
\[
  \lVert k_H-\overline K_P\rVert_{L^1(J_H)}
  \leq P\lVert k_H-K_*\rVert_{L^\infty(J_H)}
       +\int_{\mathbf{R}\setminus J_H}K_*(u)\,du.
\]
Since \(x(s)-s\) and \(x_H(s)-x(s)\) are bounded on \(I_H\), the endpoints
of \(J_H\) are \(\pm H/2+O(1)\), so the tail integral is exponentially
small. Decreasing \(\beta\) to absorb polynomial factors in \(H\) gives
\[
  \lVert k_H-\overline K_P\rVert_{L^1(\mathbf{R}/P\mathbf{Z})}\leq Ce^{-\beta H}.
\]
For the front, Lemma~\ref{lem:front-error} gives
\[
  \lvert K_P-\overline K_P\rvert\leq Ce^{-\beta P}Y_P.
\]
Integrating over a period and using \(\int_0^P Y_P(s)\,ds=\pi\) yields
\[
  \lVert K_P-\overline K_P\rVert_{L^1(\mathbf{R}/P\mathbf{Z})}
  \leq Ce^{-\beta P}\leq C'e^{-\beta H}.
\]
The triangle inequality completes the proof.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:interpolation}
Let \(G_0\) and \(G_1\) be centered ovals with the same
half-perimeter \(L\) and curvature at most \(K_0\). Choose arclength origins
at which the tangent directions of \(G_0\) and \(G_1\) agree. Then there is a
smooth path \(\Gamma\) of centered ovals from \(G_0\) to \(G_1\) with
curvature at most \(K_0\) such that
\[
  W(\Gamma)+S_0(\Gamma)+S_1(\Gamma)
  \leq C(1+L)^2\lVert k_{G_1}-k_{G_0}\rVert_{L^1(0,L)}.
\]
The constant \(C\) depends only on \(K_0\).

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Set \(k_t=(1-t)k_{G_0}+tk_{G_1}\), and let
\[
  \theta_t(s)=\theta_0+\int_0^s k_t(u)\,du,
\]
where \(\theta_0\) is the common initial tangent angle. Define
\[
  X_t(s)=\int_0^s\tau(\theta_t(u))\,du
      -\frac12\int_0^L\tau(\theta_t(u))\,du
  \qquad(0\leq s\leq L),
\]
and extend by \(X_t(s+L)=-X_t(s)\). Each curvature function is
\(L\)-periodic and has integral \(\pi\), so this formula defines a smooth
centered oval of curvature \(k_t\), parametrized by arclength.

Put \(\varepsilon=\lVert k_{G_1}-k_{G_0}\rVert_{L^1(0,L)}\). Then
\(\lVert \dot\theta_t\rVert_\infty\leq\varepsilon\) and
\(\lVert \dot X_t\rVert_\infty\leq(3/2)L\varepsilon\). For
\(\dot X_t=\xi\tau+\eta\nu\), we have \(\lvert \xi\rvert,\lvert \eta\rvert\leq\lvert \dot X_t\rvert\),
and the identity \(\eta_s=\dot\theta_t-k_t\xi\) gives
\[
  \lVert \eta\rVert_\infty\leq CL\varepsilon,\qquad
  \lVert \eta_s\rVert_\infty\leq C(1+L)\varepsilon.
\]
Since the full perimeter is \(2L\), integrating over \(s\) and \(t\) completes
the proof.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:jacobi}
Fix \(0<\widehat K<1\). Let \(\Gamma\) be a smooth path of smooth
regular convex closed fronts with curvature at most \(\widehat K\).
Their images under \(\mathcal B\) form a smooth path, and
\begin{align}
  W(\mathcal B\Gamma)&\leq W(\Gamma),\label{eq:W}\\
  S_0(\mathcal B\Gamma)&\leq C_0W(\Gamma),\label{eq:S0}\\
  S_1(\mathcal B\Gamma)&\leq C_1\bigl(W(\Gamma)+S_0(\Gamma)\bigr),\label{eq:S1}\\
  S_2(\mathcal B\Gamma)&\leq C_2\bigl(W(\Gamma)+S_0(\Gamma)+S_1(\Gamma)\bigr).
    \label{eq:S2}
\end{align}
The constants depend only on \(\widehat K\).
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Smooth dependence follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse}. At a fixed time,
let \(x\) be rear arclength, write \(\dot R=\xi\tau+\eta_R\nu\), and
denote the front normal velocity by \(\eta_F\). Since \(F=R+\tau\) and
\(\dot\tau=(\eta_{R,x}+k\xi)\nu\), we have
\[
  \dot F=\xi\tau+(\eta_R+\eta_{R,x}+k\xi)\nu,\qquad
  \nu_F=\cos\delta\,\nu-\sin\delta\,\tau.
\]
Since \(k=\tan\delta\), taking the inner product of \(\dot F\) with \(\nu_F\)
cancels the tangential terms and gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobi}
  (1+\partial_x)\eta_R=\sec\delta\,(\eta_F\circ s),
\end{equation}
where \(s=s(x)\) is the corresponding front arclength. Let \(\ell\) be
the rear perimeter. On \(\mathbf{R}/\ell\mathbf{Z}\), the inverse of
\(1+\partial_x\) is
\[
  (\mathcal R_\ell f)(x)=\frac1{1-e^{-\ell}}
      \int_{x-\ell}^x e^{-(x-t)}f(t)\,dt.
\]
Its kernel is positive and has integral \(1\). Since \(dx=\cos\delta\,ds\),
we have
\[
  \int\lvert \sec\delta\,(\eta_F\circ s)\rvert\,dx
  =\int\lvert \eta_F\rvert\,ds,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:W}.

Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse} bounds \(\sec\delta\) and the rear curvature in
terms of \(\widehat K\), so the total turning of the rear tangent gives
\[
  \ell\geq\frac{2\pi\sqrt{1-\widehat K^2}}{\widehat K}>0.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal R_\ell\) maps \(L^1\) to \(L^\infty\) with a uniform
bound, proving \eqref{eq:S0}. Equation~\eqref{eq:jacobi} then gives
\eqref{eq:S1}, and differentiating \eqref{eq:jacobi} once yields
\[
  \eta_{R,xx}=(\sec\delta)_x\,(\eta_F\circ s)
       +\sec^2\delta\,(\eta_{F,s}\circ s)-\eta_{R,x}.
\]
The identity \(\delta_x=\sec\delta\,(K-\sin\delta)\) bounds the coefficients
using only \(\widehat K\) and proves \eqref{eq:S2}. No bound on the
perimeter of the front or on derivatives of its curvature is used.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:complete}
Let \(G_j\) be regular closed curves joined by piecewise smooth paths
\(\Gamma_j\). Suppose that all intermediate curvatures are bounded in absolute
value by a common constant, and that
\[
  \sum_j\bigl(S_0(\Gamma_j)+S_1(\Gamma_j)+S_2(\Gamma_j)\bigr)<\infty.
\]
Then \(G_j\) converges in geometric \(C^2\) to a regular closed \(C^2\)
curve.
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
After choosing parametrizations that agree at the junctions, concatenate the
paths. On each segment, remove the tangential velocity by reparametrization.
With \(g=\lvert X_u\rvert\), the change of parameter solves
\[
  \partial_t\varphi=-\frac{\xi\circ\varphi}{g\circ\varphi}.
\]
Initializing each segment with the endpoint parametrization of the previous
segment leaves \(S_0\), \(S_1\), and \(S_2\) unchanged and gives
\(\partial_tX=\eta\nu\) in a single transported \(1\)-periodic parameter \(u\).
The inward-normal variation formulas are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-flow}
  \partial_t\tau=\eta_s\nu,\qquad
  \partial_t k=\eta_{ss}+k^2\eta,\qquad
  \partial_t\log g=-k\eta.
\end{equation}
Summability makes \(X\), \(\tau\), \(k\), and \(\log g\) uniformly Cauchy.
The limiting speed is positive because \(\log g\) has a finite uniform
limit.

Let \(X_j\), \(g_j\), \(\tau_j\), \(\nu_j\), and \(k_j\) be the
resulting quantities on \(G_j\). Switch to normalized arclength on \([0,1]\)
by setting
\[
  \ell_j=\int_0^1 g_j(u)\,du,\qquad
  h_j(u)=\ell_j^{-1}\int_0^u g_j(v)\,dv,\qquad
  Y_j=X_j\circ h_j^{-1}.
\]
The maps \(h_j\) converge in \(C^1\) to a map with positive derivative.
Consequently,
\[
  Y_j'=\ell_j\tau_j\circ h_j^{-1},\qquad
  Y_j''=\ell_j^2(k_j\nu_j)\circ h_j^{-1}
\]
converge uniformly, as does \(Y_j\). Their limit is a regular closed
\(C^2\) parametrization.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:shadow}
Let \(Q_n\) be centrally symmetric ovals centered at the origin, and put
\(A_n=\mathcal B Q_{n+1}\). Suppose that \(Q_n\) and \(A_n\) have the same
half-perimeter \(L_n\), and that all these curves have curvature at most
a common constant \(K_0<1\). For each pair, choose arclength origins at which
the tangent directions of \(Q_n\) and \(A_n\) agree, and define
\[
  e_n=(1+L_n)^2\lVert k_{Q_n}-k_{A_n}\rVert_{L^1(0,L_n)},\qquad
  r_n=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}e_m.
\]
There are constants \(\eta_*,C>0\), depending only on \(K_0\), with
the following property. If \(r_0\leq\eta_*\), then all backward iterates
\[
  Z_n^{(N)}=\mathcal B^{N-n}Q_N\qquad(N\geq n)
\]
are defined. For each fixed \(n\), the curves \(Z_n^{(N)}\) converge in
geometric \(C^2\) as \(N\to\infty\) to a smooth oval \(X_n\). These limits
satisfy
\[
  X_n=\mathcal B X_{n+1},\qquad \mathcal T X_n=X_{n+1},
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shadow-distance}
  d_{\mathrm H}(X_n,Q_n)\leq Cr_n,
\end{equation}
where \(d_{\mathrm H}\) denotes Hausdorff distance.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
\emph{Interpolation paths.}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:interpolation}, there are smooth paths \(\Lambda_n\) of
centered ovals from \(Q_n\) to \(A_n\) with curvature at most \(K_0\) and
\[
  W(\Lambda_n)+S_0(\Lambda_n)+S_1(\Lambda_n)\leq d_n,
  \qquad d_n=C_{\mathrm{int}}e_n.
\]
Put \(a_n=\sum_{m=n}^\infty d_m=C_{\mathrm{int}}r_n\). All constants below
depend only on \(K_0\).

Set \(\widehat K=(1+K_0)/2\), and take the constants
\(C_0\), \(C_1\), and \(C_2\) from Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi} for this curvature bound. Put
\[
  M_0=\max\{1,C_0\},\qquad
  M_1=\max\{1,C_1(1+M_0)\},\qquad
  C_*=C_2(1+M_0+M_1)+\widehat K^2\,C_0,
\]
and choose \(\eta_*>0\) so small that
\(C_*\,C_{\mathrm{int}}\,\eta_*<\widehat K-K_0\).

\emph{Invariant family.}
Let \(\mathcal D_n\) consist of the curves joined to \(Q_n\) by a
piecewise smooth path of centered ovals with curvature
at most \(\widehat K\) and
\[
  W\leq a_n,\qquad S_0\leq M_0a_n,\qquad S_1\leq M_1a_n.
\]
We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariance}
  \mathcal B(\mathcal D_{n+1})\subseteq\mathcal D_n.
\end{equation}
Let \(G\in\mathcal D_{n+1}\), and let \(\Gamma\) be a path from
\(Q_{n+1}\) to \(G\) satisfying the definition of \(\mathcal D_{n+1}\).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse}, the path \(\mathcal B\Gamma\) is defined and starts
at \(A_n\). Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  W(\mathcal B\Gamma)&\leq a_{n+1},&\qquad
  S_0(\mathcal B\Gamma)&\leq C_0a_{n+1},\\
  S_1(\mathcal B\Gamma)&\leq C_1(1+M_0)a_{n+1},&\qquad
  S_2(\mathcal B\Gamma)+\widehat K^2\,S_0(\mathcal B\Gamma)&\leq C_*a_{n+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The path \(\mathcal B\Gamma\) starts at \(A_n\) with curvature at most
\(K_0\). As long as its curvature remains at most \(\widehat K\),
equation \eqref{eq:normal-flow} in a parametrization with zero tangential
velocity gives
\[
  \lVert k(t)-k(0)\rVert_\infty
  \leq S_2(\mathcal B\Gamma)+\widehat K^2\,S_0(\mathcal B\Gamma)
  \leq C_*a_{n+1}<\widehat K-K_0,
\]
so the curvature stays below \(\widehat K\) throughout \(\mathcal B\Gamma\).
Since \(a_n=d_n+a_{n+1}\), concatenating \(\Lambda_n\) with \(\mathcal B\Gamma\)
gives a path that satisfies the bounds defining \(\mathcal D_n\), proving
\eqref{eq:invariance}. Hence every \(Z_n^{(N)}\) is defined and belongs to
\(\mathcal D_n\).

\emph{Convergence.}
Every curve on \(\Lambda_N\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_N\), because the path
up to that curve satisfies the defining bounds with \(d_N\leq a_N\).
Repeated use of \eqref{eq:invariance} therefore shows that
\[
  \Gamma_{n,N}=\mathcal B^{N-n}\Lambda_N
\]
exists, stays in \(\mathcal D_n\), and joins \(Z_n^{(N)}\) to
\(Z_n^{(N+1)}\). Iterating Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi} gives
\[
  W(\Gamma_{n,N})\leq d_N,\qquad
  S_0(\Gamma_{n,N})\leq M_0d_N,\qquad
  S_1(\Gamma_{n,N})\leq M_1d_N.
\]
For \(N\geq n+1\), we have \(\Gamma_{n,N}=\mathcal B\Gamma_{n+1,N}\), so
\eqref{eq:S2} gives
\[
  S_2(\Gamma_{n,N})\leq C_2(1+M_0+M_1)d_N.
\]
For fixed \(n\), Lemma~\ref{lem:complete} therefore applies to the sequence
with \(N\geq n+1\). Let \(X_n\) be its geometric \(C^2\) limit.

The limit \(X_n\) has curvature \(0\leq k\leq\widehat K<1\), and
its unit tangent has turning number \(1\) because the unit tangent maps
converge uniformly. Relative to the tangent at each point of \(X_n\), the
tangent angle increases from \(0\) to \(2\pi\) around the curve, so projection
onto the inward normal first increases from \(0\) and then decreases back to
\(0\). Every tangent line is therefore supporting, and \(X_n\) bounds a convex
body.

Since \(\mathcal B\) is continuous in geometric \(C^2\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse}, the identities \(Z_n^{(N)}=\mathcal B Z_{n+1}^{(N)}\)
pass to the limit to give
\[
  X_n=\mathcal B X_{n+1},\qquad \mathcal T X_n=X_{n+1}.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse} again, each \(X_n\) has positive curvature and
gains one derivative at each step \(X_n=\mathcal B X_{n+1}\), so iterating
\(X_n=\mathcal B^rX_{n+r}\) shows that every \(X_n\) is smooth.

\emph{Distance from the comparison curves.}
Every \(Z_n^{(N)}\) can be joined to \(Q_n\) by a path in \(\mathcal D_n\).
In a parametrization with zero tangential velocity, each point moves a
distance at most \(S_0\leq M_0a_n\), so
\(d_{\mathrm H}(Z_n^{(N)},Q_n)\leq M_0a_n\). Passing to the limit and
using \(a_n=C_{\mathrm{int}}r_n\) proves \eqref{eq:shadow-distance}.

Use the fixed translating hairpin from Theorem~\ref{thm:hairpin}. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:pairs}, the half-perimeter function \(P\) is
continuous and satisfies
\[
  \frac\Delta2\leq H-P(H)\leq\frac{3\Delta}2
\]
for all sufficiently large \(H\). By the intermediate value theorem, each
sufficiently large \(H_0\) determines a sequence \((H_n)_{n\geq0}\) with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hrec}
  P(H_{n+1})=H_n,\qquad H_n\geq H_0+\frac{\Delta}{2}n.
\end{equation}

Translate each pair \((R_{H_n},F_{H_n})\) so that its center of symmetry
is the origin, and set \(Q_0=F_{H_0}\). Inductively rotate the pair
\((R_{H_{n+1}},F_{H_{n+1}})\) so that the rear tangent and the tangent of
\(Q_n\) agree at \(u=0\) in the arclength coordinates used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:matching}. Define
\[
  Q_{n+1}=F_{H_{n+1}},\qquad A_n=\mathcal B Q_{n+1}=R_{H_{n+1}}.
\]
Because \(\mathcal T\) commutes with translations and rotations, these choices
preserve the relations \(\mathcal T R_{H_n}=F_{H_n}\) and all curvature
estimates. By \eqref{eq:Hrec},
\(Q_n\) and \(A_n\) have the same half-perimeter \(H_n\), and their chosen
tangent directions agree.

Theorem~\ref{thm:matching} gives
\[
  e_n\leq C(1+H_n)^2e^{-\beta H_{n+1}}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:Hrec}, decreasing \(\beta\) to absorb the polynomial factor
yields, for some \(\beta'>0\),
\[
  r_0\leq C\sum_{n=0}^\infty e^{-\beta'(H_0+n\Delta/2)}
      \leq C'e^{-\beta'H_0}\longrightarrow0
      \qquad(H_0\to\infty).
\]
All curves \(Q_n\) and \(A_n\) have curvature at most
\(K_0=1/10\). For sufficiently large \(H_0\),
Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow} gives smooth ovals \(X_n\) with
\(\mathcal T X_n=X_{n+1}\). Hausdorff distance \(d\) changes the width in each
direction by at most \(2d\), so Lemma~\ref{lem:width} and
\eqref{eq:shadow-distance} show that each \(X_n\) has width at most
\(C_W+2Cr_n\leq C_W+2Cr_0\) in some direction. Since this bound is
independent of \(n\), whereas the \(n\)th iterate of a circle of
radius \(r\) is a circle of radius \(\sqrt{r^2+n}\) and has width
\(2\sqrt{r^2+n}\) in every direction, \(\Gamma_0=X_0\) is not a circle.
