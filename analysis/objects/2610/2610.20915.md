<!-- maintheorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main-intro}
\textit{Let $(G,\theta)$ be a finitely generated 1-smooth oriented pro-$p$ group with torsion-free orientation. Suppose that $G$ is a pro-$p$ $\mathrm{PD}^3$ group. Then}
\[K_\theta(G)=1 \qquad\textit{or}\qquad K_\theta(G)\textit{ is infinitely generated}.\]
\textit{Equivalently, if $K_\theta(G)$ is finitely generated, then} $K_\theta(G)=1$.
<!-- end maintheorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
An oriented pro-$p$ group $(G,\theta)$ is \textit{Kummerian} if, for every $n\geq1$, the natural reduction map
 \[\mathrm H^1\!\left(G,\mathbb Z_p(\theta)/p^n\right)\longrightarrow \mathrm H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)\]
is surjective.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
An oriented pro-$p$ group $(G,\theta)$ is \textit{1-smooth} if every closed subgroup $H\leq G$, equipped with the restricted orientation $\theta|_H$, is Kummerian.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
A torsion-free oriented pro-$p$ group $(G,\theta)$ is \textit{$\theta$-abelian} if
\[K_\theta(G)=1\]
and $\ker(\theta)$ is a free abelian pro-$p$ group.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:canonical}
Let $(G,\theta)$ be a finitely generated Kummerian oriented pro-$p$ group with torsion-free orientation. Then: 
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\ker(\theta)/K_\theta(G)$ is a free abelian pro-$p$ group;
\item \[ I_\theta(G)=K_\theta(G);\]
\item with $\bar G=G/K_\theta(G)$ and the induced orientation $\bar\theta$, the pair $(\bar G,\bar\theta)$ is $\bar\theta$-abelian and is the maximal $\theta$-abelian quotient of $(G,\theta)$; 
\item $K_\theta(G)\subseteq\Phi(G)$.
	\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The equivalence between Kummerianity, freeness of $\ker(\theta)/K_\theta(G)$, and $\bar\theta$-abelianity of the quotient is \cite[Theorem~2.2]{QuadrelliHHA}. In the torsion-free Kummerian setting, the equality $I_\theta(G)=K_\theta(G)$ and the maximality of the resulting $\theta$-abelian quotient are given by \cite[Proposition~2.6 and Corollary~2.9]{QuadrelliWeigelBP}. The final inclusion is part of \eqref{eq:Kbasic}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The distinction between $K_\theta(G)$ and its isolator is important in general. Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical} is the reason that no such distinction appears in the remainder of this paper: 1-smoothness implies Kummerianity, so the canonical maximal $\theta$-abelian quotient is genuinely $G/K_\theta(G)$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:H1}
Let $(G,\theta)$ be as in Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical}, and put
\[K=K_\theta(G), \qquad\bar G=G/K.  \]
Then the quotient map induces an isomorphism
\[ G/\Phi(G) \simeq \bar G/ \Phi(\bar G). \]
Equivalently, inflation is an isomorphism
\[\inf: \mathrm H^1(\bar G,\mathbb F_p) \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} \mathrm H^1(G,\mathbb F_p).\]
In particular,
\[d(\bar G)=d(G).\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $K\subseteq\Phi(G)$, one has \[ \Phi(\bar G)=\Phi(G)/K. \]Hence
\[\bar G/\Phi(\bar G) \simeq G /\Phi(G). \]
Taking continuous homomorphisms to $\mathbb F_p$ gives the degree-one inflation isomorphism.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:quotient-uniform}
Let $(G,\theta)$ be finitely generated and Kummerian with torsion-free orientation. Then
\[ \bar G=G/K_\theta(G)\]
is a finitely generated uniform pro-$p$ group. In particular, it is $p$-adic analytic and 
\[\dim(\bar G)=d(\bar G)=d(G).\]
Moreover, \begin{equation} \mathrm H^\bullet(\bar G,\mathbb F_p)   \simeq \bigwedge\nolimits^\bullet\mathrm H^1(\bar G,\mathbb F_p)
\label{eq:barGcohomology}\end{equation} as graded $\mathbb F_p$-algebras.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical}, $(\bar G,\bar\theta)$ is $\bar\theta$-abelian. Proposition~4.2 of \cite{QuadrelliHHA} identifies torsion-free $\theta$-abelian pro-$p$ groups with locally uniform pro-$p$ groups. Since $\bar G$ is finitely generated, the subgroup $\bar G\leq\bar G$ is itself finitely generated; applying the definition of local uniformity to this subgroup shows explicitly that $\bar G$ is uniform. A uniform pro-$p$ group is $p$-adic analytic, and its analytic dimension equals its minimal number of generators. Lemma~\ref{lem:H1} gives $d(\bar G)=d(G)$. Finally, \eqref{eq:barGcohomology} is Lazard's cohomology theorem for uniform pro-$p$ groups \cite{Lazard}; see also \cite[(4.1)]{QuadrelliHHA}.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
A pro-$p$ group $G$ is a Poincar\'e duality pro-$p$ group of dimension $n$, abbreviated $\mathrm{PD}^n$, if it has finite cohomological dimension $n$, satisfies the standard finiteness condition, and its continuous cohomology satisfies Poincar\'e duality in dimension $n$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:PDdimensions}
If $G$ is a pro-$p$ $\mathrm{PD}^3$ group, then
\[\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\mathrm H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\mathrm H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)=d(G).\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Poincar\'e duality gives a perfect pairing between degree one and degree two mod-$p$ cohomology. The mod-$p$ dualizing character is trivial for a pro-$p$ group, since its image in $\mathbb F_p^\times$ has both $p$-power order and order dividing $p-1$ (and $\mathbb F_2^\times$ is trivial). Hence
\[\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\mathrm H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)= \dim_{\mathbb F_p}\mathrm H^1(G,\mathbb F_p).\]
Finally, $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\mathrm H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)=d(G)$ for every finitely generated pro-$p$ group.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:GJPPZ}
Let $G$ be a finitely generated pro-$p$ $\mathrm{PD}^3$ group and let $1\neq N\trianglelefteq G$ be a finitely generated normal subgroup of infinite index. Then one of the following alternatives holds: \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $N\simeq\mathbb Z_p$ and $G/N$ is virtually Demu\v{s}kin;
\item $N$ is Demu\v{s}kin and $G/N$ is virtually $\mathbb Z_p$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
This is exactly \cite[Corollary~1.5]{GrunewaldEtAl}. The same pro-$p$ alternative is recalled in the later subgroup analysis of Castellano and Zalesskii \cite{CastellanoZalesskii}.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:cyclic-extension}
Let
\[ 1\longrightarrow C \longrightarrow E \longrightarrow Q \longrightarrow 1 \]
be an exact sequence of finitely generated pro-$p$ groups with $C\simeq\mathbb Z_p$ and $Q$ $p$-adic analytic. Then $E$ has finite rank and is therefore $p$-adic analytic.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $H\leq E$ be any closed subgroup. There is an exact sequence 
\[ 1\longrightarrow H\cap C \longrightarrow H \longrightarrow HC/C \longrightarrow  1.\]
The subgroup $HC/C$ is closed in $Q$: it is the continuous image of the compact group $H$ in the Hausdorff group $Q$. Since $C\simeq\mathbb Z_p$, we have
\[ d(H\cap C)\leq1. \]
Since $Q$ is $p$-adic analytic, it has finite rank. If $r=\operatorname{rk}(Q)$, then
\[d(HC/C)\leq r.\]Lifting generators of $HC/C$ to $H$ and adjoining at most one generator of $H\cap C$ yields
\[ d(H)\leq r+1.\]
This bound holds for every closed subgroup $H\leq E$, so $E$ has finite rank. By the finite-rank characterization of finitely generated $p$-adic analytic pro-$p$ groups \cite[Theorem~5.1]{QuadrelliHHA}, $E$ is $p$-adic analytic.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $(G,\theta)$ be a finitely generated 1-smooth oriented pro-$p$ group with torsion-free orientation. Suppose that $G$ is a pro-$p$ $\mathrm{PD}^3$ group. Then 
\[K_\theta(G)=1 \qquad\text{or}\qquad K_\theta(G)\text{ is infinitely generated}. \]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Set
\[K=K_\theta(G), \qquad \bar G=G/K. \]
Since $(G,\theta)$ is 1-smooth, it is Kummerian. Therefore Propositions~\ref{prop:canonical} and~\ref{prop:quotient-uniform} apply: $\bar G$ is a uniform, hence torsion-free $p$-adic analytic, pro-$p$ group and 
\begin{equation}
d(\bar G)=d(G).\label{eq:dmain}
\end{equation}
		
Assume for contradiction that $K$ is nontrivial and finitely generated. We first note that $K$ has infinite index in $G$. Indeed, $K\subseteq\Phi(G)$, so \eqref{eq:dmain} implies that $\bar G$ is nontrivial. Since $\bar G$ is uniform, it is torsion-free; hence a nontrivial $\bar G$ is infinite. Thus
\[[G:K]=\infty.\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:GJPPZ} now leaves two possibilities.
		
\smallskip

\noindent\textit{Case 1: $K$ is Demu\v{s}kin and $\bar G$ is virtually $\mathbb Z_p$.} Because $\bar G$ is $p$-adic analytic and has an open subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p$, its analytic dimension is one. Since $\bar G$ is uniform, 
\[d(\bar G)=\dim(\bar G)=1.\]
Equation \eqref{eq:dmain} gives $d(G)=1$. Hence $G$ is procyclic. The group $G$ has finite cohomological dimension, so it has no nontrivial $p$-torsion; therefore \[G\simeq\mathbb Z_p. \]This contradicts $\operatorname{cd}(G)=3$.
		
\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Case 2: $K\simeq\mathbb Z_p$ and $\bar G$ is virtually Demu\v{s}kin.}
The quotient $\bar G$ is already $p$-adic analytic by Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient-uniform}. Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclic-extension} applied to 
\[ 1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow\bar G \longrightarrow 1\]
shows that $G$ itself is $p$-adic analytic.
		
Now apply Quadrelli's analytic 1-smoothness theorem \cite[Theorem~5.7]{QuadrelliHHA}: a finitely generated $p$-adic analytic 1-smooth pro-$p$ group with torsion-free orientation is $\theta$-abelian. Therefore \[K_\theta(G)=1,\]contradicting the assumption that $K$ is nontrivial.
		
Both alternatives lead to contradictions. Hence a finitely generated $K_\theta(G)$ must be trivial.

Its force comes from the fact that the canonical Kummerian quotient simultaneously has three properties: its kernel is normal and lies in $\Phi(G)$, its quotient is analytic, and degree-one cohomology is preserved. These are exactly the features that make the normal-subgroup theorem effective.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:canonical-transgression}
Let $(G,\theta)$ satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~4.1, and put 
\[K=K_\theta(G),\qquad Q=G/K,\;\; V=H^1(G,\mathbb F_p),\;\; d=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}V.\]
Choose an identification
\[\operatorname{tr}:H^3(G,\mathbb F_p)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb F_p\]
and define the alternating trilinear form
\[T_G:V\times V\times V\longrightarrow\mathbb F_p,\;\; T_G(x,y,z)=\operatorname{tr}(x\smile y\smile z).\]
Let
\[\mu_G:\bigwedge\nolimits^2 V\longrightarrow V^*\]
be the linear map determined by
\[\mu_G(x\wedge y)(z)=T_G(x,y,z).\] Then there is an isomorphism of $\mathbb F_p$-vector spaces 
\[H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)^Q\simeq\ker(\mu_G).\]
Consequently,
\[\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)^Q= \binom d2-\operatorname{rank}(\mu_G), \]
and in particular
\[\max\left\{0,\binom d2-d\right\} \leq \dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)^Q\leq \binom d2. \]
If $K\ne1$, then
\[H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)^Q\ne0.\]
Moreover, in this case the following sharper conclusions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $d=2$, then
\[\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)^Q=1.\]
\item If $d=3$, then $T_G=0$, and
\[\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)^Q=3.\]
\item If $d=4$, then
\[\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)^Q \in\{3,6\}.\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
By Lemma~2.6, inflation induces an isomorphism
\[H^1(Q,\mathbb F_p)\xrightarrow{\sim} H^1(G,\mathbb F_p).\]
The Hochschild--Serre five-term exact sequence for
\[ 1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow G \longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow 1 \]
therefore identifies the transgression with an injection
\[H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)^Q \hookrightarrow H^2(Q,\mathbb F_p)\]
whose image is precisely the kernel of degree-two inflation. Hence
\[H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)^Q \simeq \ker\left( H^2(Q,\mathbb F_p)\xrightarrow{\inf}H^2(G,\mathbb F_p) \right).\]
	
By Proposition~2.7, $Q$ is uniform and $d(Q)=d$. Lazard's theorem gives
\[H^\bullet(Q,\mathbb F_p) \simeq \bigwedge\nolimits^\bullet H^1(Q,\mathbb F_p). \]Using the degree-one inflation isomorphism, we obtain an identification 
\[ H^2(Q,\mathbb F_p) \simeq\bigwedge\nolimits^2 V.\]
Under this identification, degree-two inflation is the cup-product map
\[ c_G:\bigwedge\nolimits^2V \longrightarrow H^2(G,\mathbb F_p),\;\;x\wedge y\longmapsto x\smile y. \]
In particular, squares of degree-one classes vanish in $H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)$, also when $p=2$. Thus $T_G$ is alternating.
	
Since $G$ is a pro-$p$ Poincar\'e duality group of dimension three, cup product induces a perfect pairing
\[H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)\times H^2(G,\mathbb F_p) \longrightarrow H^3(G,\mathbb F_p). \]
Composing with $\operatorname{tr}$ gives an isomorphism
\[ \operatorname{PD}_G: H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)\xrightarrow{\sim}V^*,\]
defined by
\[ \operatorname{PD}_G(\alpha)(z) =\operatorname{tr}(\alpha\smile z).\]
Consequently,
\[\operatorname{PD}_G\circ c_G=\mu_G.\]
Since $\operatorname{PD}_G$ is an isomorphism,
\[\ker(c_G)=\ker(\mu_G), \] which proves the asserted identification. The dimension formula follows immediately. As $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}V^*=d$, one has $\operatorname{rank}(\mu_G)\leq d$, giving the stated bounds.
	
Now suppose that $K\ne1$. Then $H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)\ne0$. Choose a nonzero class $\varphi\in H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)$. The conjugation action of the pro-$p$ group $Q$ on continuous homomorphisms $K\to\mathbb F_p$ is continuous when the latter space is given the discrete topology. Indeed, compactness of $K$ ensures that the stabilizer of each continuous homomorphism is open. Thus the $Q$-orbit of $\varphi$ is finite.
	
The vector space spanned by this orbit is a nonzero finite-dimensional $Q$-submodule on which the action factors through a finite $p$-group. Every nonzero representation of a finite $p$-group over $\mathbb F_p$ has a nonzero fixed vector. Therefore
\[H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)^Q\ne0.\]
	
It remains to examine small values of $d$. If $d=2$, then $\bigwedge^3V^*=0$, so $T_G=0$ and $\mu_G=0$. Hence the invariant subspace has dimension one.
	
If $d=3$, the space $\bigwedge^3V^*$ is one-dimensional. Every nonzero alternating trilinear form on a three-dimensional vector space induces an isomorphism
\[\bigwedge\nolimits^2V\xrightarrow{\sim}V^*.\]
Since $\ker(\mu_G)\ne0$, the form $T_G$ must vanish. Consequently $\mu_G=0$, and the invariant subspace has dimension three.
	
Finally, suppose $d=4$. Every nonzero alternating trilinear form on a four-dimensional vector space can be written, after choosing a suitable basis, as
\[T_G=e_1^*\wedge e_2^*\wedge e_3^*.\]
The associated map $\mu_G$ then has rank three. Thus its rank is either zero or three, according as $T_G$ vanishes or not. It follows that
\[\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\ker(\mu_G) =6-\operatorname{rank}(\mu_G) \in\{6,3\}.\]

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:canonical-kernel-action}
Let $(G,\theta)$ satisfy the hypotheses	of Theorem~4.1 and suppose that $K=K_\theta(G)\ne1$. Put $Q=G/K$ and $d=d(G)$. Then
\[\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)=\infty,\]
whereas
\[1\leq \dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)^Q \leq\binom d2.\]
More precisely, its dimension is $\binom d2-\operatorname{rank}(\mu_G)$, with $\mu_G$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-transgression}.
	
The conjugation action of $Q$ on $K/\Phi(K)$ is nontrivial. Furthermore, if $2\leq d\leq4$, then the cohomology algebra $H^\bullet(G,\mathbb F_p)$ is not generated by its degree-one part. In particular, $G$ is not a Bloch--Kato pro-$p$ group.

\[\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^1(K,\mathbb F_p)=d(K)=\infty.\]The bounds and the dimension formula follow from Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-transgression}.

Under Poincar\'{e} duality, the rank of this map equals $\operatorname{rank}(\mu_G)$. For $d=2$ and $d=3$, the preceding proposition gives $\mu_G=0$. For $d=4$, its rank is at most three. In every case $2\leq d\leq4$, the cup-product map is therefore not surjective, since

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:classification}
Let $(G,\theta)$ satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and assume that $K_\theta(G)$ is finitely generated. Then $G$ is uniform of dimension three and $\theta$-abelian. More precisely: \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item if $\theta$ is trivial, then
\[G\simeq\mathbb Z_p^3;\]
\item if $\theta$ is nontrivial, then
\[G\simeq\mathbb Z_p^2\rtimes_\theta\mathbb Z_p,\]
where the final factor acts on $\mathbb Z_p^2$ by scalar exponentiation through $\theta$. 
\end{enumerate}
Moreover,
\[\mathrm H^\bullet(G,\mathbb F_p)\simeq \bigwedge\nolimits^\bullet\mathrm H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)\]
as graded $\mathbb F_p$-algebras.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, finite generation of $K_\theta(G)$ implies $K_\theta(G)=1$. Since $(G,\theta)$ is Kummerian, \cite[Theorem~2.2]{QuadrelliHHA} implies that it is $\theta$-abelian. By \cite[Proposition~4.2]{QuadrelliHHA}, the underlying group is locally uniform; finite generation therefore makes $G$ uniform.
		
A uniform pro-$p$ group has cohomological dimension equal to its analytic dimension. Since $G$ is $\mathrm{PD}^3$, 
\[\dim(G)=3.\]If $\theta$ is trivial, then $\theta$-abelianity says simply that $G$ is free abelian pro-$p$, and dimension three gives $G\simeq\mathbb Z_p^3$.

Suppose now that $\theta$ is nontrivial. The image $\operatorname{im}(\theta)$ is a nontrivial closed torsion-free pro-$p$ subgroup of $1+p\mathbb Z_p$ (or of $1+4\mathbb Z_2$ when $p=2$), hence isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p$. We have an exact sequence 
\[1\longrightarrow\ker(\theta) \longrightarrow G \longrightarrow\operatorname{im}(\theta)
\longrightarrow 1.\]
The kernel is free abelian pro-$p$, and $\operatorname{im}(\theta)\simeq\mathbb Z_p$ is a
free pro-$p$ group of rank one, hence projective. The extension therefore splits. Since $\dim(G)=3$, the kernel has rank two. The $\theta$-abelian relations show that the action of the final $\mathbb Z_p$ factor on $\mathbb Z_p^2$ is scalar exponentiation through $\theta$. 

Finally, the cohomology statement follows from Lazard's theorem for uniform pro-$p$ groups.

<!-- example-3: body_md -->
\label{ex:Demu\v{s}kin-extension}
Let $p=3$, and let $F=\mathbb Q_3(\zeta_3)$, where $\zeta_3$ is a primitive third root of unity. Write $D=G_F(3)$ for the maximal pro-$3$ Galois group of $F$, and let $\chi:D\longrightarrow 1+3\mathbb Z_3$ be its cyclotomic orientation.
	
By the classical description of maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of local fields, $D$ is a Demu\v{s}kin pro-$3$ group with
\[d(D)=[F:\mathbb Q_3]+2=4,\qquad \operatorname{cd}(D)=2.\]
The cyclotomic orientation $\chi$ coincides with the canonical Demu\v{s}kin orientation, and $(D,\chi)$ is 1-smooth.
	
Consider the cyclotomic semidirect product $G=\mathbb Z_3\rtimes_\chi D$, where $D$ acts on $\mathbb Z_3$ by multiplication through $\chi$. Let $\pi:G\longrightarrow D$ be the natural projection and equip $G$ with the orientation $\theta=\chi\circ\pi$.
	
The valuation-theoretic description of maximal pro-$3$ Galois groups of Laurent series fields gives an isomorphism of oriented pro-$3$ groups
\[(G,\theta)\simeq \bigl(G_{F((T))}(3),\chi_{F((T))}\bigr).\]
Indeed, since the residue field of $F((T))$ has characteristic zero, its pro-$3$ inertia group is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_3(1)$. The coefficient-field embedding provides a splitting of the corresponding Galois sequence, and conjugation on inertia is given by the cyclotomic character. In particular, $(G,\theta)$ is 1-smooth by the Kummer-theoretic lifting property of maximal pro-$3$ Galois groups. The construction also belongs to the elementary-type family described by Quadrelli--Weigel \cite[Section~5.3 and Proposition~5.13(f)]{QuadrelliWeigelBP}.
	
The group $G$ fits into a split exact sequence
\[1\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_3\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow 1.\]
Since $\mathbb Z_3$ is a pro-$3$ $\mathrm{PD}^1$ group and $D$ is a pro-$3$ $\mathrm{PD}^2$ group, the extension theorem for profinite Poincar\'e duality groups \cite[Theorem~3.7.4]{NSW} implies that $G$ is a pro-$3$ $\mathrm{PD}^3$ group.
	
The canonical kernel of this extension can be identified explicitly. By Quadrelli--Weigel \cite[Proposition~5.9]{QuadrelliWeigelBP},
\[K_\theta(G)=K_\chi(D),\]where the subgroup on the right is identified with its image in the canonical copy of $D$ in $G$. This kernel is nontrivial. Indeed, if $K_\chi(D)=1$, then Kummerianity would imply that $(D,\chi)$ is $\chi$-abelian. Consequently $D$ would be uniform, and hence
\[\operatorname{cd}(D)=\dim(D)=d(D)=4,\]	contrary to $\operatorname{cd}(D)=2$.
	
Quadrelli--Weigel \cite[Theorem~5.1]{QuadrelliWeigelBP} prove that the canonical kernel of a Demu\v{s}kin group with its canonical torsion-free orientation is free pro-$p$. Therefore $K_\theta(G)$ is free pro-$3$. Since it is nontrivial, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} shows that it cannot be finitely generated. Thus $K_\theta(G)$ is a free pro-3 group of infinite rank. Moreover, $G$ is not $3$-adic analytic: otherwise the analytic 1-smoothness theorem \cite[Theorem~5.7]{QuadrelliHHA} would force $G$ to be $\theta$-abelian, contradicting $K_\theta(G)\neq1$.
	
The mod-$3$ cohomology of $G$ can also be calculated explicitly. Since $\chi$ is trivial modulo $3$, the map
\[G=\mathbb Z_3\rtimes_\chi D\longrightarrow\mathbb F_3,\qquad (a,g)\longmapsto a \bmod 3\]
is a continuous homomorphism. It determines a class $x\in\mathrm H^1(G,\mathbb F_3)$ whose restriction generates $\mathrm H^1(\mathbb Z_3,\mathbb F_3)$. The Hochschild--Serre spectral sequence for the split extension has only two potentially nonzero rows, corresponding to $\mathrm H^0(\mathbb Z_3,\mathbb F_3)$ and $\mathrm H^1(\mathbb Z_3,\mathbb F_3)$. The class $x$ lifts the generator of the second row, while inflation identifies the first row with $\mathrm H^\bullet(D,\mathbb F_3)$. Multiplication by $x$ identifies the two rows, so the spectral sequence collapses multiplicatively. Since $3$ is odd, graded commutativity gives $x^2=0$, and consequently
\[\mathrm H^\bullet(G,\mathbb F_3)\simeq\mathrm H^\bullet(D,\mathbb F_3)\otimes_{\mathbb F_3}\bigwedge(x)\] as graded $\mathbb F_3$-algebras.
	
In particular, \[d(G)=d(D)+1=5.\]
Put $W=\mathrm H^1(D,\mathbb F_3)$. Then
\[\mathrm H^1(G,\mathbb F_3)=W\oplus\mathbb F_3x.\]Since $D$ is Demu\v{s}kin, cup product induces a nondegenerate alternating pairing
\[W\times W\longrightarrow\mathrm H^2(D,\mathbb F_3)\simeq\mathbb F_3.\]
Under the preceding cohomological decomposition, we have
\[\mathrm H^2(G,\mathbb F_3)\simeq\mathrm H^2(D,\mathbb F_3)\oplus\bigl(W\smile x\bigr).\]
Products of two elements of $W$ span the first summand, while products of $x$ with elements of $W$ span the second. Hence the cup-product map
\[\bigwedge\nolimits^2\mathrm H^1(G,\mathbb F_3)\longrightarrow\mathrm H^2(G,\mathbb F_3)\] is surjective. By Poincar\'e duality, the map $\mu_G$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-transgression} is therefore surjective as well, and
\[\operatorname{rank}(\mu_G)=\dim_{\mathbb F_3}\mathrm H^2(G,\mathbb F_3)=5.\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-transgression} now yields
\[\dim_{\mathbb F_3}\mathrm H^1(K_\theta(G),\mathbb F_3)^{G/K_\theta(G)}=\binom52-5=5.\] Thus $G$ is a nonanalytic 1-smooth pro-$3$ $\mathrm{PD}^3$ group with a nontrivial free canonical kernel of infinite rank. Its first kernel cohomology is infinite-dimensional, whereas its invariant subspace has dimension five, determined explicitly by the triple cup-product form. Moreover, its mod-$3$ cohomology is generated in degree one with quadratic relations, and $G$ is Bloch--Kato as the maximal pro-$3$ Galois group of a field containing $\mu_3$. The example therefore realizes the infinite-kernel alternative of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} while satisfying the cohomological restrictions of Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-transgression}.
