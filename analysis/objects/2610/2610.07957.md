<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{defWHfact}\citep[Ch.1, Sec.1]{CG81}
Let $A(t)$  be a continuous  and non-singular $p\times p$ matrix
function on the contour ${\mit\varGamma} $.

{A standard right Wiener--Hopf factorisation} of $A(t)$ is  its
representation in the form
$$
A(t)=A_-(t)D(t)A_+(t), \ t\in {\mit\varGamma}.
$$
Here $A_{\pm}(t)$ are continuous and non-singular matrix functions on
${\mit\varGamma} $ that admit analytic continuation into  $D_{\pm}$ and their
continuations $A_{\pm}(z)$ are continuous and non-singular in $\overline D_{\pm}$;
$D(t)={\rm diag}\,[t^{\rho_1},\ldots,t^{\rho_p}]$, where integers
 $\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_p$ are called {the right partial indices}
of $A(t)$.

If $\rho_1=\cdots=\rho_p=0$, then the factorisation is called {canonical}.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{genformGK}
Let $A(t)\in G{\mathfrak A}^{p\times p}({\mit\varGamma})$ and
\begin{equation}\label{factWH} 
A(t)=A_-(t)D(t)A_+(t)
\end{equation}
be a standard Wiener--Hopf factorisation of the matrix function $A(t)$ with partial indices $\rho_1 \leq \ldots \leq \rho_p$. 
Then the representation
\begin{equation}\label{generalFact}
A(t)=G_-(t)D(t)G_+(t),
\end{equation}
where $G_-(t)= A_-(t)Q_-(t)$, $G_+(t)= Q_+(t)A_+(t)$, is also a Wiener--Hopf factorisation of $A(t )$ for any $Q_-(t)\in \mathcal Q_-(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_p)$. Moreover, any factorisation of $A(t)$ can be obtained from the original factorisation in a similar way with an appropriate choice of $Q_-(t)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{Birkfacto} 
\emph{A right Birkhoff factorisation} of a matrix function 
$A(t)\in G{\mathfrak A}^{p\times p}({\mit\varGamma})$ is its representation in the following form:
\begin{equation}\label{Birkfact}
A(t)=D_b(t)B_-(t)B_+(t), \ \ t\in {\mit\varGamma},
\end{equation}
where $B_\pm(t) \in G{\mathfrak A}_\pm^{p\times p}({\mit\varGamma})$ and 
$D_b(t) ={\rm diag}\left[t^{\beta_1},\ldots,t^{\beta_p}\right]$. The integers
$\beta_1, \ldots, \beta_p$ are called {the right Birkhoff indices} of $A(t)$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{defLU}
Let $A\in {\mathbb C}^{p\times p}$ be a non-singular $p\times p$ matrix. 

{LU factorisation} of $A$ is its representation in the following form
\begin{equation}\label{LU}
A=L\cdot U,
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the lower triangular matrix, and $U$ is the upper triangular matrix.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
LU and UL factorisations can be considered as the simplest examples of the canonical Wiener--Hopf factorisations in an abstract decomposing algebra.

Let ${\mathfrak A}={\mathbb C}^{p\times p}$ be the algebra of all complex $p\times p$
matrices. The subalgebras ${\mathfrak A}_+={\mathbb C}^{p\times p}_+$,
${\mathfrak A}_-={\mathbb C}^{p\times p}_-$, 
${\mathfrak A}_-^0={\mathbb C}^{0,p\times p}_-$
are defined as the algebras of all lower triangular matrices,  all the upper triangular matrices, and all the upper triangular matrices with the zero diagonal, respectively.
Then ${\mathfrak A}$ is the decomposing algebra: ${\mathfrak A}={\mathfrak A}_-^0\oplus{\mathfrak A}_+$.

If $A\in {\mathfrak A}$, then 
$$
A=LU=A_+A_-, \qquad A=\widetilde{U}\widetilde{L}=\widetilde{A}_-\widetilde{A}_+
$$
can be considered as the canonical left/right Wiener--Hopf factorisation in 
the decomposing algebra ${\mathfrak A}$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{defblockLU}
Let $A\in {\mathbb C}^{p\times p}$ be a non-singular $p\times p$ matrix. The {block LU factorisation} of $A\in {\mathcal B}(k_1,\ldots,k_s)$ is its representation in the following form
\begin{equation}\label{blockLU}
A=L\cdot U,
\end{equation}
where $L, U \in {\mathcal B}(k_1,\ldots,k_s)$, and $L$ is the lower block triangular matrix,  $U$ is the block upper triangular matrix.
For the partition $\{1,\ldots,1\}$ we get the ordinary LU factorisation.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
During a normalising process of an original factorisation $A(t)=A_-(t)D(t)A_+(t)$
we will use the block PLU factorisation of the matrix $A_-(\infty)$. The matrix is endowed with the block structure generated by the  partial indices. If $A(t)$ has the distinct partial indices then the partition is $\{1,\ldots,1\}$ and we get the PLU factorisation of matrix $A_-(\infty)$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{Nor}
The transition from the original factorisation~(\ref{factWH}) to the 
factorisation~(\ref{generalFact}) by using any matrix function 
$Q_-(t)\in \mathcal Q_-(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_p) $ will be called {the  normalisation of the factorisation (\ref{factWH}) at infinity}. The matrix function $Q_-(t)$ is called the {normalisation matrix}.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{canonPnorm}
Let $P$ be any  permutation matrix of order $p$.
The Wiener--Hopf factorisation of the matrix function $A(t)$:
\begin{equation}\label{rcanfact}
A(t)=C_-(t)D(t)C_+(t)
\end{equation}
is called {P-normalised} if the following conditions are fulfilled:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The matrix function $B_-(t)=PD^{-1}(t)P^{-1} C_-(t)D(t)$ belongs to the algebra 
$\mathfrak{A}_{-}^{p\times p}({\mit\varGamma})$, 
\item $B_-(\infty)=P$.
\end{enumerate}

We will consider P-normalisation  as {canonical normalisation} of the factorisation.
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{Bir}
Let $A(t)=A_{-}(t)D(t)A_{+}(t)$ be a known factorisation of a matrix function $A(t)$. For the matrix $A_-(\infty)$, fix a permutation $P$ such that $A_-(\infty)$ admits a block PLU factorisation.                                                                                                                                                    

Let the matrix function $A(t)\in G{\mathfrak A}^{p\times p}({\mit\varGamma})$ admit a $P$-normalised Wiener--Hopf factorisation:
$$
A(t)=C_-(t)D(t)C_+(t).
$$
Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item This Wiener--Hopf factorisation generates a Birkhoff factorisation by the formula
\begin{equation}\label{Bir_form}
A(t)=PD(t)P^{-1}B_-(t)B_+(t),
\end{equation}
where $B_-(t)=PD^{-1}(t)P^{-1} C_-(t)D(t)$, $B_+(t)=C_+(t)$.

\item The given $P$-normalised Wiener--Hopf factorisation is unique.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
\, By the first condition of Definition~ \ref{canonPnorm}, we have $B_-(t)\in GW_{-}^{p\times p}(\mathbb{T})$. Then relation \eqref{Bir_form} is verified directly.


Let us prove the uniqueness of the $P$-normalised Wiener--Hopf factorisation.
Assume that $A(t)=\widetilde C_-(t)D(t)\widetilde C_+(t)$ is another $P$-normalised factorisation of $A(t)$ and
$$A(t)=PD(t)P^{-1} \widetilde B_-(t)\widetilde B_+(t),$$ is the corresponding Birkhoff factorisation.
Then $\widetilde B_-^{-1}(t)B_-(t)= \widetilde B_+(t)B_+^{-1}(t)$, and, therefore, by Liouville's theorem, this matrix function is a constant invertible matrix. Hence, $\widetilde B_-^{-1}(t)B_-(t)=\widetilde B_-^{-1}(\infty)B_-(\infty)=I_p$, due to the second condition of Definition \ref{canonPnorm}. Thus, $\widetilde B_-(t)=B_-(t)$ and $\widetilde C_-(t)=C_-(t)$, $\widetilde C_+(t)=C_+(t)$ .
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The condition $B_-(\infty)=P$, which ensures the uniqueness of the $P$-normalised factorisation, can be replaced by $B_-(\infty)= A_0$, where $A_0$ is any invertible matrix. The condition $B_-(\infty)=P$ allows us to obtain a simplest form of the factors $C_-(t)$, $B_-(t)$ in $P$-normalised factorisations.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{sness}
Let a matrix function $A(t)$ admit 
the $P$-normalised factorisation for some permutation matrix $P$. Then, for any Wiener--Hopf factorisation
$$A(t)=A_-(t)D(t)A_+(t),$$
the matrix $A_-(\infty)$ admits a block PLU factorisation. Here block structure of
$A_-(\infty)$ is generated by the right partial indices of $A(t)$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
\, Suppose that the matrix function $A(t)$ admits a $P$-normalised factorisation
$$
A(t)=C_-(t)D(t)C_+(t),
$$
where $P$ is some permutation matrix. First we prove that for this factorisation $C_-(\infty)$ admits a block PLU factorisation, where the block structure is generated by the right partial indices of $A(t)$. 

Let $\varkappa_1<\ldots<\varkappa_s$ be the distinct partial indices of $A(t)$ and 
$k_1,\ldots, k_s$ their multiplicities. Thus
$$
D(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
t^{\varkappa_1}I_{k_1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&t^{\varkappa_2}I_{k_2}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&t^{\varkappa_s}I_{k_s}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

By the first condition of Definition~\ref{canonPnorm},
the following inclusion holds:
\begin{equation}\label{condi1}
PD^{-1}(t)\bigl(P^{-1} C_-(t)\bigr)D(t)\in \mathfrak{A}_{-}^{p\times p}({\mit\varGamma}).
\end{equation}
Denote
$$
L_-(t)=P^{-1} C_-(t)\in \mathfrak{A}_{-}^{p\times p}({\mit\varGamma}).
$$
Define $\widetilde{L}_-(t):= D^{-1}(t)L_-(t)D(t)$. Then $\widetilde{L}_-(t) \in \mathfrak{A}_{-}^{p\times p}({\mit\varGamma})$ by virtue (\ref{condi1}) and 
$${L}_-(t)= D(t)\widetilde{L}_-(t)D(t).
$$


Partition $\widetilde {L}_-(t)$ into blocks:                                        
$$
\widetilde {L}_-(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
L_{11}^-(t)&L_{12}^-(t)&\cdots&L_{1s}^-(t)\\
L_{21}^-(t)&L_{22}^-(t)&\cdots&L_{2s}^-(t)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
L_{s1}^-(t)&L_{s2}^-(t)&\cdots&L_{ss}^-(t)
\end{pmatrix},
$$
where the block $L_{ij}^-(t)\in {\mathfrak A}_-^{k_i \times k_j}$.


Then we have
$$
L_-(t)=D(t){\widetilde L}_-(t)D^{-1}(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
L_{11}^-(t)&t^{-\varkappa_{21}}L_{12}^-(t)&\cdots&t^{-\varkappa_{s1}}L_{1s}^-(t)\\
t^{\varkappa_{21}}L_{21}^-(t)&L_{22}^-(t)&\cdots&t^{-\varkappa_{s2}}L_{2s}^-(t)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
t^{\varkappa_{s1}}L_{s1}^-(t)&t^{\varkappa_{s2}}L_{s2}^-(t)&\cdots&L_{ss}^-(t)
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Here $\varkappa_{ji}=\varkappa_j-\varkappa_i>0$ for $j>i$.


The condition $L_-(t)\in \mathfrak{A}_{-}^{p\times p}({\mit\varGamma})$
implies that $t^{\varkappa_{ij}}L_{ij}^-(t):={\widetilde L}_{ij}^-(t) \in {\mathfrak A}_-^{k_i\times k_j}$ for $i>j$.
Consequently, 
$$
L_0:=L_-(\infty)=\begin{pmatrix}
L_{11}^-(\infty)&0&\cdots&0\\
{\widetilde L}_{21}^-(\infty)&{L}_{22}^-(\infty)&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
{\widetilde L}_{s1}^-(\infty)&{\widetilde L}_{s2}^-(\infty)&\cdots&L_{ss}^-(\infty)
\end{pmatrix},
$$
is block lower triangular matrix. Hence,
$$
C_-(\infty)=PL_0,
$$
that is, for the $P$-normalised factorisation $$
A(t)=C_-(t)D(t)C_+(t),
$$
the matrix $C_-(\infty)$ admits a block PLU factorisation.

Further, by Theorem~\ref{genformGK},
for any Wiener--Hopf factorisation
$$
A(t)=A_-(t)D(t)A_+(t)
$$
the following representation holds:
$$
A_-(t)=C_-(t)Q_-(t),
$$
where $Q_-(t)$ has the form~(\ref{gQminus}). In particular,
$$
A_-(\infty)=C_-(\infty)Q_-(\infty).
$$
Denote $U_0:=Q_-(\infty)$. Then $U_0$ is an upper
block triangular matrix.


Thus,
$$
A_-(\infty)=P\bigl(P^{-1}C_-(\infty)\bigr)Q_-(\infty)
=PL_0U_0,
$$
that is, the matrix $A_-(\infty)$ admits a block PLU factorisation.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{basethm}  
Let $A(t)\in G\mathfrak A^{p\times p}$.  $A(t)$ admits a $P$-normalised right Wiener--Hopf factorisation if and only if for some factorisation
$A(t) = A_-(t) D(t) A_+(t)$
the matrix $A_-(\infty)$ admits the block PLU factorisation for the given permutation 
$P$.

If this condition is fulfilled then the P-normalised
factorisation 
\begin{equation}\label{canonnormfact}
A(t) = C_-(t) D(t)C_+(t) 
\end{equation}
has the form
\begin{equation}\label{canonnormAm}
C_-(t) = P
\begin{pmatrix}
I_{k_1} + t^{-1}c_{11} 
& t^{-\varkappa_{21}-1} c_{12}  
& \cdots 
& t^{-\varkappa_{s-1,1}-1} c_{1,s-1}
& t^{-\varkappa_{s1}-1} c_{1s} \\
c_{21} 
& I_{k_2} + t^{-1}c_{22}  
& \cdots 
& t^{-\varkappa_{s-1,2}-1} c_{2,s-1}
& t^{-\varkappa_{s2}-1} c_{2s} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots\\
c_{s-1,1} 
&c _{s-1,2} 
& \cdots 
& I_{k_{s-1}} + t^{-1}c_{s-1,s-1}\quad
& t^{-\varkappa_{s,s-1}-1} c_{s-1, s} 
\\
c_{s1} 
& c_{s2} 
& \cdots 
&c _{s,s-1}
& I_{k_s} + t^{-1}c_{ss}
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
$C_+(t)= Q_+(t)A_+(t)$.
Here $c_{ij}(t) \in {\mathfrak A}_{-}^{k_i \times k_j}$. We will call this form of the factor $C_-(t)$ canonical.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
\, The necessity of the  condition of the theorem is proved in 
Theorem~\ref{sness}.

Now we prove the sufficiency. First, we prove the existence of $P$-normalised factorisation for $P = I$. This means that the following conditions are satisfied:


\begin{center} 
\emph{all upper left sub-matrices of $A_-(\infty)$ of sizes}
            
$
k_1\times k_1, (k_1+k_2)\times (k_1+k_2),\ldots, (k_1+k_2+\cdots + k_s)\times (k_1+k_2+\cdots +k_s)  
$
                
\emph{are non-singular.}
\end{center}
 
We prove the sufficiency by induction on the number $k$ of upper left blocks of $A_-^{(k)}(t)$ in canonical form in the factorisation $A(t) = A_-^{(k)}(t) D(t) A_+^{(k)}(t)$. 

For the case $P = I$ the matrix $A_-(\infty):= A_0$ admits the block LU factorisation:
$
A_0 = L \cdot U,
$
where $L$ is a lower block triangular matrix with $I_{k_1}, \dots, I_{k_s}$ as diagonal elements and $U$ is an upper block triangular matrix. This block LU factorisation is unique.


\noindent\textbf{1st step of the normalising process.}
Let us take $Q_1 = U^{-1}$ as a constant normalising matrix.
Obviously, $Q_1 \in Q_-(\varrho_1, \dots, \varrho_p)$, that is, $Q_1$ is a normalising matrix. Then
\begin{equation}\label{1step}
A(t) = A_-^{(1)}(t) D(t) A_+^{(1)}(t), \quad t\in {\mit\varGamma},
\end{equation}
where $A_-^{(1)}(t) = A_-(t) Q_1$, $A_+^{(1)}(t) = D^{-1}(t)Q_1^{-1}D(t)A_+(t)$,
is a new factorisation of $A(t)$. This factorisation is normalised by the condition 
$
A_-^{(1)}(\infty) = A_-(\infty) U^{-1} = L,
$
that is
$$
A_-^{(1)}(t) = \left(
\begin{array}{c|ccc} 
I_{k_1} + t^{-1} A_{11}^{(1)} & t^{-1} A_{12}^{(1)} & \dots & t^{-1} A_{1s}^{(1)} \\
\hline
A_{21}^{(1)} & I_{k_2} + t^{-1} A_{22}^{(1)} & \dots & t^{-1} A_{2s}^{(1)} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
A_{s1}^{(1)} & A_{s2}^{(1)} & \dots & I_{k_s} + t^{-1} A_{ss}^{(1)}
\end{array}
\right), \quad A_{ij}^{(1)}(t) \in {\mathfrak A}_{-}^{k_i \times k_j}.
$$


This means that after the first step we obtain the factorisation for which the upper
left block of  size $k_1\times k_1$ 
has the canonical form. For certain matrix functions the process can be finished after this
step.


\noindent\textbf{2nd step of the normalising process.} Now we normalise the 
factorisation (\ref{1step}) so that the upper left block of size 
$(k_1+k_2)\times (k_1+k_2)$ has the canonical form.
Select the upper left block of size $(k_1+k_2)\times (k_1+k_2)$ in the matrix function
$A_-^{(1)}(t)$:
$$
G_{11}^{(1)}(t)=\begin{pmatrix} 
I_{k_1} + t^{-1} A_{11}^{(1)} & t^{-1} A_{12}^{(1)}\\
A_{21}^{(1)} & I_{k_2} + t^{-1} A_{22}^{(1)} \\
\end{pmatrix}
$$
and represent $A_-^{(1)}(t)$ in the block form
\begin{equation}\label{blockA1}
A_-^{(1)}(t)=\left(\begin{array}{c|c}
G_{11}^{(1)}&G_{12}^{(1)}\\
\hline
G_{21}^{(1)}&G_{22}^{(1)}
\end{array}
\right).
\end{equation}

Put 
$
Q_2(t)= \left(\begin{array}{c|c}
Q_{11}^{(2)}&0\\
\hline
0&I_{k_3+\cdots +k_s}
\end{array}
\right),
$
where
$
Q_{11}^{(2)}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
I_{k_1}&t^{-1}q_{12}^{(2)}\\
0&I_{k_2}
\end{pmatrix}
$
and $q_{12}^{(2)}(t)$ is $k_1\times k_2$ matrix polynomial in $t^{-1}$ of degree at most
$\varkappa_{21}-1$. The coefficients of $q_{12}^{(2)}(t)$ should be determined.
$Q_2(t)$ has the same block structure as $A_-^{(1)}(t)$ in (\ref{blockA1}).
Obviously, $Q_2(t)$ belongs to the group $Q_-(\varrho_1, \dots, \varrho_p)$.

Let us set $A_-^{(2)}(t) = A_-^{(1)}(t) Q_2(t)$, $A_+^{(2)}(t) = D^{-1}(t)Q_2^{-1}(t)D(t)A_+^{(1)}(t)$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{2step}
A(t) = A_-^{(2)}(t) D(t) A_+^{(2)}(t), \quad t\in {\mit\varGamma},
\end{equation}
is the factorisation of $A(t)$. Here
$
A_-^{(2)}=\left(\begin{array}{c|c}
G_{11}^{(1)}Q_{11}^{(2)}&G_{12}^{(1)}\\
\hline
G_{21}^{(1)}Q_{11}^{(2)}&G_{22}^{(1)}
\end{array}
\right).
$
It is not difficult to see that
$$
G_{21}^{(1)}Q_{11}^{(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}
A_{31}^{(2)}&A_{32}^{(2)}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
A_{s1}^{(2)}&A_{s2}^{(2)}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Let us find the block
$$
G_{11}^{(1)}Q_{11}^{(2)}=\begin{pmatrix} 
I_{k_1} + t^{-1} A_{11}^{(1)} \quad
&t^{-1}\left[(I_{k_1} + 
t^{-1} A_{11}^{(1)})q_{12}^{(2)}+A_{12}^{(1)}\right]\\
A_{21}^{(1)} & I_{k_2} + t^{-1} A_{22}^{(1)} \\
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The matrix function $I_{k_1} + t^{-1} A_{11}^{(1)}(t)$ is invertible in a neighborhood of infinity and we have
$$
t^{-1}\left[(I_{k_1} + 
t^{-1} A_{11}^{(1)})q_{12}^{(2)}+A_{12}^{(1)}\right]=t^{-1}(I_{k_1} + 
t^{-1} A_{11}^{(1)})\left[q_{12}^{(2)}+
(I_{k_1} + t^{-1} A_{11}^{(1)})^{-1}A_{12}^{(1)}\right].
$$
The matrix function $\left(I_{k_1} + t^{-1} A_{11}^{(1)}(t)\right)^{-1}A_{12}^{(1)}(t)$ is analytic
in a neighborhood of infinity. Expand it into the Laurent series and put 
$q_{12}^{(2)}(t)=-P_{\varkappa_{21}-1}(t)$. Here $P_{\varkappa_{21}-1}(t)$
is the corresponding Laurent polynomial for this series. Then matrix function
$
t^{-1}(I_{k_1} + t^{-1} A_{11}^{(1)})\left[q_{12}^{(2)}+(I_{k_1} + t^{-1} A_{11}^{(1)})^{-1}A_{12}^{(1)}\right]
$ 
can be represented as
$t^{-\varkappa_{21}-1}A_{12}^{(2)}(t)$, 
where $A_{12}^{(2)}(t)\in {\mathfrak A}_-^{k_1\times k_2}$.

Thus, we get 
$$
A_-^{(2)}(t) = \left(
\begin{array}{cc|ccc} 
I_{k_1} + t^{-1} A_{11}^{(2)} & t^{-\varkappa_{21}-1} A_{12}^{(2)} 
& t^{-1} A_{13}^{(2)}& \dots & t^{-1} A_{1s}^{(2)} \\
A_{21}^{(2)} & I_{k_2} + t^{-1} A_{22}^{(2)} &t^{-1}A_{23}^{(2)}&  \dots & t^{-1} A_{2s}^{(2)} \\
\hline
A_{31}^{(2)} & A_{32}^{(2)} & I_{k_3} + t^{-1} A_{33}^{(2)} & \dots & t^{-1} A_{2s}^{(2)} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots&\ddots & \vdots \\
A_{s1}^{(2)} & A_{s2}^{(2)} & A_{s3}^{(2)} &\dots & I_{k_s} + t^{-1} A_{ss}^{(2)}
\end{array}
\right),
$$
where all $A_{ij}^{(2)}(t) \in {\mathfrak A}_{-}^{k_i \times k_j}$.

Therefore, using the matrix $Q_-^{(2)}(t)=Q_2(t)Q_1\in Q_-(\varrho_1, \dots, \varrho_p)$ as a normalising matrix, we normalise the factorisation (\ref{factWH}) so that the upper left block of size 
$(k_1+k_2)\times (k_1+k_2)$ of $A_-^{(2)}(t)$ has the canonical form.
For certain matrix functions, the process terminates after this
step.



\noindent\textbf{(m+1)-th step of a normalising process.}
Suppose there exists a normalising matrix 
$Q_-^{(m)}(t) \in {\mathcal Q}_{-}(\varrho_1, \dots, \varrho_p)$, for some $m \ge 2$, that reduces the factorisation (\ref{factWH}) to the factorisation

\begin{equation}\label{mfact}
A(t) = A_-^{(m)}(t) D(t) A_+^{(m)}(t), \quad t\in {\mit\varGamma},
\end{equation}
where $A_-^{(m)}(t)$ has the upper left block of size
$(k_1 + \dots + k_m) \times (k_1 + \dots + k_m)$ in the canonical form.

Let us select two blocks in $A_-^{(m)}(t)$: the $(k_1 + \dots + k_m) \times (k_1 + \dots + k_m)$ canonical block, and the next diagonal block that we must reduce to the canonical form.
For brevity, we denote the diagonal elements by 
$D_{jj}^{(m)}(t):=I_{k_j}+t^{-1}A_{jj}^{(m)}(t)$.
Now we have
\begin{multline*}
   
A_-^{(m)}(t)=\\ 
   
   
\left(
\begin{array}{cccc|c|ccc}
D_{11}^{(m)}
& t^{-\varkappa_{21}-1}A_{12}^{(m)}
& \cdots
& t^{-\varkappa_{m 1}-1}A_{1m}^{(m)}
& t^{-1}A_{1,m+1}^{(m)}
& t^{-1}A_{1,m+2}^{(m)}
& \cdots
& t^{-1}A_{1,s}^{(m)}
\\
A_{21}^{(m)}
& D_{22}^{(m)}
& \cdots
& t^{-\varkappa_{m 2}-1}A_{2m}^{(m)}
& t^{-1}A_{2,m+1}^{(m)}
& t^{-1}A_{2,m+2}^{(m)}
& \cdots
& t^{-1}A_{2,s}^{(m)}
\\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots  & \vdots&&\vdots
\\
A_{m 1}^{(m)}
& A_{m 2}^{(m)}
& \cdots
& D_{mm}^{(m)}
& t^{-1}A_{m,m+1}^{(m)}
& t^{-1}A_{m,m+2}^{(m)}
& \cdots
& t^{-1}A_{m, s}^{(m)}
\\
\hline
A_{m+1,1}^{(m)}
& A_{m+1,2}^{(m)}
& \cdots
& A_{m+1,m}^{(m)}
& D_{m+1,m+1}^{(m)}
& t^{-1}A_{m+1,m+2}^{(m)}
& \cdots
& t^{-1}A_{m+1,s}^{(m)}
\\
\hline
A_{m+2,1}^{(m)}
& A_{m+2,2}^{(m)}
& \cdots
& A_{m+2,m}^{(m)}
&A_{m+2,m+1}^{(m)}
& D_{m+2,m+2}^{(m)}
&\cdots
& t^{-1}A_{m+2,s}^{(m)}
\\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots &\vdots &\ddots & \vdots
\\
A_{s, 1}^{(m)}
& A_{s, 2}^{(m)}
& \cdots
& A_{s,m}^{(m)}
& A_{s,m+1}^{(m)}
& A_{s,m+2}^{(m)}
& \cdots
& D_{ss}^{(m)}
\end{array}
\right)
\end{multline*}
   

Thus, we obtain the following block form of $A_-^{(m)}(t)$:
\begin{equation}\label{mFactWH}
A_-^{(m)}(t)=\left(\begin{array}{c|c|c}
G_{11}^{(m)}&G_{12}^{(m)}&G_{13}^{(m)}\\
\hline
G_{21}^{(m)}&G_{22}^{(m)}&G_{23}^{(m)}\\
\hline
G_{31}^{(m)}&G_{32}^{(m)}&G_{33}^{(m)}
\end{array}
\right).
\end{equation}
Now we  form a normalising matrix $Q_{m+1}(t)\in {\mathcal Q}_{-}(\varrho_1, \dots, \varrho_p)$ with the same block structure as $A_-^{(m)}(t)$ in (\ref{mFactWH}):
$$
Q_{m+1}(t) =
\left(\begin{array}{c|c|c}
I_{k_1+\ldots+k_m}&t^{-1}q_{m+1}&0\\
\hline
0&I_{k_{m+1}}
&0\\
\hline
0&0&I_{k_{m+2} + \dots + k_s}
\end{array}
\right), {\mbox\ {\rm where}\ \ }
q_{m+1}(t) =
\begin{pmatrix}
q_{1,m+1}^{(m+1)} \\
\vdots \\
q_{m,m+1}^{(m+1)} \\
\end{pmatrix},
$$
and $q_{ij}^{(m+1)}(t)$ is $k_i\times k_j$ matrix polynomial in $t^{-1}$ of degree at most
$\varkappa_{ji}-1$. 


Let us normalise the factorisation (\ref{mfact}) by $Q_{m+1}(t) \in Q_{-}(\varrho_1, \dots, \varrho_p)$:
\begin{equation}\label{mplus1fact}
A(t) = A_-^{(m+1)}(t) D(t) A_+^{(m+1)}(t), \quad t\in {\mit\varGamma}.
\end{equation}
Here $A_-^{(m+1)}(t)=A_-^{(m)}(t)Q_{m+1}(t), \quad 
A_+^{(m+1)}(t) = D^{-1}(t)Q_{m+1}^{-1}(t)D(t)A_+^{(m)}(t).
$

Then for $A_-^{(m+1)}(t)=A_-^{(m)}(t)Q_{m+1}(t)$ we have:
$$
A_-^{(m+1)}(t)=
\left(
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
G_{11}^{(m)}&t^{-1}G_{11}^{(m)}q_{m+1}+G_{12}^{(m)}&G_{13}^{(m)}\\
\hline
G_{21}^{(m)}&t^{-1}G_{21}^{(m)}q_{m+1}+G_{22}^{(m)}&G_{23}^{(m)}\\
\hline
G_ {31}^{(m)}&t^{-1}G_{31}^{(m)}q_{m+1}+G_{32}^{(m)}&G_{33}^{(m)}\\
\end{array}
\right),
$$
where the block
                  
$$
t^{-1}G_{21}^{(m)}q_{m+1}+G_{22}^{(m)}=
t^{-1}\left(
A_{m+1,1}^{(m)}, \cdots, A_{m+1,m}^{(m)}\right)
\begin{pmatrix}
q_{1,m+1}^{(m+1)} \\
\vdots \\
q_{m,m+1}^{(m+1)} \\
\end{pmatrix}
+D_{m+1,m+1}^{(m)}$$ has the  form $D_{m+1,m+1}^{(m+1)}$,
                 
and
$
t^{-1}G_{31}^{(m)}q_{m+1}+G_{32}^{(m)}=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_{m+2,m+1}^{(m+1)}\\
\vdots\\
A_{s,m+1}^{(m+1)}
\end{pmatrix}.
$

It  remains to find the block $t^{-1}G_{11}^{(m)}(t)q_{m+1}(t)+G_{12}^{(m)}(t)$ in $A_-^{(m+1)}$.

Let us write the block $G_{12}^{(m)}(t)$ in the form:
$G_{12}^{(m)}(t)=t^{-1}A_{m+1}(t)$, where
$A_{m+1}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
A_{1,m+1}^{(m)}\\
\vdots\\
A_{m,m+1}^{(m)}\\
\end{pmatrix}.
$
Now 
$$
t^{-1}G_{11}^{(m)}(t)q_{m+1}(t)+G_{12}^{(m)}(t)= t^{-1}\left[G_{11}^{(m)}(t)q_{m+1}(t)+
A_{m+1}(t)\right].
$$
Since the matrix $G_{11}^{(m)}(\infty)=
\begin{pmatrix}
I_{k_1}&0&\dots&0\\
*&I_{k_2}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
*&*&\dots&I_{k_m}
\end{pmatrix}
$ is invertible, the matrix function $G_{11}^{(m)}(t)$ is invertible in a neighborhood of infinity
and
$$t^{-1}G_{11}^{(m)}(t)q_{m+1}(t)+G_{12}^{(m)}(t)= t^{-1}G_{11}^{(m)}(t)
\left[q_{m+1}(t)+(G_{11}^{(m)}(t))^{-1}A_{m+1}(t)\right].$$
Here the block column $(G_{11}^{(m)}(t))^{-1}A_{m+1}(t)$ is analytic at infinity.
We expand all the block elements of this column into Laurent series in a neighborhood of infinity and represent $(G_{11}^{(m)}(t))^{-1}A_{m+1}(t)$ in the following form:
$$
(G_{11}^{(m)}(t))^{-1}A_{m+1}(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
P_{\varkappa_{m+1,1}-1}(t)+t^{-\varkappa_{m+1,1}}O_{1,m+1}(t)\\
\vdots\\
P_{\varkappa_{m+1,m}-1}(t)+t^{-\varkappa_{m+1,m}}O_{m,m+1}(t)\\
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Here $P_{\varkappa_{m+1,j}-1}(t)$ is the corresponding Laurent polynomial
of the series and $O_{j,m+1}(t)\in {\mathfrak A}^{k_j\times k_{m+1}}$.
Now we set $q_{j,m+1}^{(m+1)}(t)= -P_{\varkappa_{m+1,j}-1}(t)$ and arrive 
at the following equality:
$$
t^{-1}G_{11}^{(m)}(t)q_{m+1}(t)+G_{12}^{(m)}(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
t^{-\varkappa_{m+1,1}-1}A_{1,m+1}^{m+1}(t)\\
\vdots\\
t^{-\varkappa_{m+1,m}-1}A_{m,m+1}^{m+1}(t)
\end{pmatrix}:=t^{-1}G_{11}^{(m)}(t)
\begin{pmatrix}
t^{-\varkappa_{m+1,1}}O_{1,m+1}(t)\\
\vdots\\
t^{-\varkappa_{m+1,m}}O_{m,m+1}(t)\\
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Therefore, the normalising matrix $Q_-^{(m)}(t)Q_{m+1}(t)\in Q_-(\varrho_1, \dots, \varrho_p)$ reduces the factorisation (\ref{factWH}) to the factorisation (\ref{mplus1fact}) in which the upper left block of size 
$(k_1+\cdots +k_{m+1})\times (k_1+\cdots +k_{m+1})$ of $A_-^{(m+1)}(t)$ has the canonical form. For the case $P=I_p$, by induction we get the factorisation (\ref{canonnormfact}) with the factor $A_-(t)$ in the canonical form (\ref{canonnormAm}).
It remains to  verify that this factorisation is an I-normalised factorisation.

Direct calculation shows that 
\begin{multline*}
B_-(t)=D^{-1}(t)C_-(t)D = \\
\begin{pmatrix}
I_{k_1} + t^{-1}c_{11} 
& t^{-1} c_{12}  
& \cdots 
& t^{-1} c_{1,s-1}
& t^{-1} c_{1s} \\
t^{-\varkappa_{21}}c_{21} 
& I_{k_2} + t^{-1}c_{22}  
& \cdots 
& t^{-1} c_{2,s-1}
& t^{-1} c_{2s} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots\\
t^{-\varkappa_{s-1,1}}c_{s-1,1} 
&\ t^{-\varkappa_{s-1,2}}c _{s-1,2} 
& \cdots 
& I_{k_{s-1}} + t^{-1}c_{s-1,s-1}\quad
& t^{-1} c_{s-1, s} 
\\
t^{-\varkappa_{s,1}}c_{s1} 
& t^{-\varkappa_{s,2}}c_{s2} 
& \cdots 
&t^{-\varkappa_{s,s-1,1}}c _{s,s-1}
& I_{k_s} + t^{-1}c_{ss}
\end{pmatrix},
\end{multline*}
and conditions (1), (2) of Definition \ref{canonPnorm} are fulfilled. 
                                                                                                                                                             
Hence the factorisation with the factor $C_-(t)$ in the canonical form is indeed an 
I-normalised factorisation.


Now we consider the general case. Suppose that for  $A(t)$ there exists a factorisation $$A(t)=A_-(t)D(t)A_+(t),$$ 
where $A_-(\infty)$ admits a block PLU factorisation with some permutation $P$:
$A_-(\infty)=PLU$.
Note that in this case any factorisation of $A(t)$ has this property.

Let us define the matrix function $F(t)=P^{-1}A(t)$. Then 
$$F(t)=(P^{-1}A_-(t))D(t)A_+(t)$$ 
is the right Wiener--Hopf factorisation of $F(t)$, and for $F_-(t)=P^ {-1}A_-(t)$ the condition $F_-(\infty)=LU$ is satisfied.
By the part of the theorem already proved, $F(t)$ admits the $I$-normalised Wiener--Hopf factorisation $F(t)=K_-(t)D(t)F_+(t)$. Then $A(t)=(PK_-(t))D(t)F_+(t)$ is the $P$-normalised factorisation of $A(t)$. The theorem is completely proved.
