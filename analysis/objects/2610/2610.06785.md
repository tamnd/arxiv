<!-- conjecture-1: body_md -->
\label{conj:trefethen}
Let $G \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ be a Gaussian matrix and $\Pi(\cdot)$ be the partial pivoting map. For any $\alpha, M >0$,
\[\mathbb{P} \left[\mathrm{growth}(\Pi(G)) > n^{\frac12 + \alpha}\right] < n^{-M} \]
for all $n$ sufficiently large.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:orthog}
Let $A \in \mathop{\rm GL}_n(\mathbb{R})$ and $Q \sim \mathrm{Haar}(\mathrm{O}(n))$. Then
\[\mathbb{P}\left[ \mathrm{growth}(QA) >  \frac{14 \, n^{\frac32} \sqrt{\log(e n)}}{\delta}\right] < \delta \qquad \text{for all} \quad \delta \in (0,1).\]

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:gauss_nopivot}
Let $G \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ be a Gaussian matrix. Then $n^{-\frac32}\mathrm{growth}(G) \overset{d}{\rightarrow} X$, where $X \sim \mathrm{InvExp}\big(\frac2{3\sqrt{\pi}}\big)$, i.e., $\Pr[X \le x] = e^{-\frac{2}{3 \sqrt{\pi} x}}$ for $x>0$.

\label{thm:smooth_pp}
For every $\alpha >0$, there exist constants $C_\alpha, n_\alpha$ such that, for all $n \ge n_\alpha$, every matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ with $\|A\|_2 \le 1$ and every $\epsilon \in (0,1)$ satisfy

where $G \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is a Gaussian matrix and $\Pi(\cdot)$ is the partial pivoting map.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lm:orthog}
Let $Q \sim \mathrm{Haar}(\mathrm{O}(n))$, $S,T\subset[n]$ be fixed subsets of size $m$, and $\bm x \in \mathbb{S}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\bm y_1, \bm y_2, \ldots, \bm y_k \in \mathbb{S}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R})$ be fixed for some $k \le m$. Then
\[\mathbb{P} \left[ \max_{j \in [k]} |\bm y_j^T (Q_{S,T})^{-1} \bm x | >t \right] \le \frac{6
}{t} \sqrt{\frac{(n-m+1)\log(ek)}{m}} \qquad \text{for all} \quad t >0.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Without loss of generality, we may take $B = Q_{[m],[m]}$ and $\bm x = \bm e_1$. First we identify the distribution of $(Q_{[m],[m]})^{-1} \bm{e}_1$. Note that $\bm u := Q_{:,[m]}(Q_{[m],[m]})^{-1} \bm{e}_1$ satisfies $\bm u_1 = 1$ and is in the intersection of the column space of $Q_{:,[m]}$ and $\mathrm{span}\{\bm e_1, \bm e_{m+1}, \ldots, \bm e_n\}$, which is almost surely one-dimensional. A unit vector in this intersection, after multiplication by a random $\pm 1$ sign, is distributed uniformly on $\mathbb{S}^{n-m}(\mathbb{R})$, the unit sphere in this $(n-m+1)$ dimensional subspace, and so 
\[\|(Q_{[m],[m]})^{-1} \bm{e}_1\|_2 = \|\bm u\|_2 \overset{d}{=}|\bm \eta_1|^{-1},\]
where $\bm \eta \sim \mathrm{Unif}(\mathbb{S}^{n-m}(\mathbb{R}))$. By the orthogonal invariance of $Q_{[m],[m]}$, the direction of $(Q_{[m],[m]})^{-1} \bm{e}_1$ is uniform and independent of its length. Therefore, $(Q_{[m],[m]})^{-1} \bm{e}_1 \overset{d}{=} |\bm \eta_1|^{-1} \bm \xi $, where $\bm \xi \sim \mathrm{Unif}(\mathbb{S}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}))$ is independent of $\bm \eta$. By \cite[Lemma 2.2]{dasgupta2003elementary},
\[ \mathbb{P}\left[|\bm \eta_1 |<a \right] \le 2 \sqrt{n-m+1} \; a\qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathbb{E}\left[ \max_{j \in [k]} |\bm y_j^T \bm \xi|  \right] \le 3 \sqrt{\frac{\log(ek)}m},\]
where the second estimate follows from a union bound and integration of their upper tail estimate. By independence,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{P} \left[ \max_{j \in [k]} |\bm y_j^T (Q_{[m],[m]})^{-1} \bm x | >t \right] &= \mathbb{E} \left[ \mathbb{P} \left[ |\bm \eta_1| < \frac{1}{t} \max_{j \in [k]} |\bm y_j^T \bm \xi| \, \bigg| \max_{j \in [k]} |\bm y_j^T \bm \xi| \right] \right] \\
&\le \frac{2 \sqrt{n-m+1}}{t} \mathbb{E}\left[ \max_{j \in [k]} |\bm y_j^T \bm \xi|  \right] \\
&\le \frac{6}{t} \sqrt{\frac{(n-m+1) \log(ek)}m}.
\end{align*}
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Without loss of generality, by $Q \sim \mathrm{Haar}(\mathrm{O}(n))$ and the QR factorization, it suffices to consider upper triangular $A$. Then, by the block inversion formula,
\[(QA)/(QA)_{[k],[k]} = (Q/Q_{[k],[k]})A_{[k]^c,[k]^c} = (Q_{[k]^c,[k]^c})^{-T}A_{[k]^c,[k]^c},\]
where $[k]^c= [n]\setminus[k]$. Let $\alpha = \max_j \|A \bm e_j\|_2 \le \sqrt{n} \|QA\|_{\max}$. Then every column of $\alpha^{-1} A$ has two-norm at most one. Applying Lemma \ref{lm:orthog} to each row of $\alpha^{-1} U$ and summing the failure probabilities, we obtain
\[\mathbb{P}\left[ \frac{\|U\|_{\max}}{\|QA\|_{\max}} >t \right] \le  \frac{6\sqrt{n}}{t} \sum_{m = 1}^n \sqrt{\frac{(n-m+1) \log (em)}{m}} \le \frac{6 n \sqrt{\log(en)}}{t} \sum_{m=1}^n \frac1{\sqrt{m}} \le \frac{12 n^{3/2} \sqrt{\log(en)}}{t} .\]
Next, we consider $L$. Note that, because $A$ is upper triangular, $QA$ and $Q$ have the same lower triangular factor \cite[proof of Lemma 5.1]{shah2026entry}. The subdiagonal entries of the $i^{th}$ column of $L$ have law $(g_{i+1},\ldots, g_{n})/g_{i}$ for $g_i,\ldots,g_n$ i.i.d. $N(0,1)$, and therefore standard Gaussian estimates give, for $t \ge 1$,
\begin{equation}\label{gauss_L_law}
\mathbb{P}\left[\|L\|_{\max} >t\right] \le \sum_{i = 1}^{n-1} \mathbb{P}\left[\max_{j>i}|L_{ji}| >t \right] \le \frac{n-1}{t}\sqrt{\frac2 {\pi}}\, \mathbb{E} \left[ \max_{j \in [n]} |g_j|\right] \le \frac{2(n-1) \sqrt{\log (en)}}{\sqrt{\pi} t}.
\end{equation}
Combining the two probabilistic bounds for $L$ and $U$ completes the proof.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lm:row_norms}
Let $G$ be an $n \times n$ Gaussian matrix with LU factorization $G = LU$, and
\[X_k = \| (-G_{k+1,[k]}G_{[k],[k]}^{-1},1)\|_2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad Y_k = \max_{j \ge k+1} \left| \frac{(-G_{k+1,[k]}G_{[k],[k]}^{-1},1)G_{[k+1],j}}{X_k} \right|\]
for $k \in [n-1]$. Then
\[\frac{1}{X_k^2} \sim \mathrm{Beta}\left(\frac12,\frac k2\right), \qquad Y_k \overset{d}{=} \max_{j \in [n-k]} |g_j|, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \max_{j\ge k+1} |U_{k+1,j}| = X_k Y_k\]
for all $k \in [n-1]$, where $g_1,\ldots,g_{n-1}$ are i.i.d. $N(0,1)$. Furthermore, $X_1,\ldots,X_{n-1}$ are mutually independent, and $X_k$ is independent of $Y_k$ for $k \in [n-1]$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\bm x_k^T = (-G_{k+1,[k]}G_{[k],[k]}^{-1},1)$ for $k \in [n-1]$. We have
\[U_{k+1,j} = (G/G_{[k],[k]})_{1,j-k} =\bm x_k^T G_{[k+1],j} \qquad \text{for} \quad k+1 \le j,\]
from which $\max_{j \ge k+1} |U_{k+1,j}| = X_k Y_k$ for $k \in [n-1]$ follows immediately. Conditional on the first $k$ columns, $\bm x_k^T/X_k$ is fixed, the remaining columns are Gaussian, and so their inner products with $\bm x_k^T/X_k$ are independent $N(0,1)$. This gives the distribution of $Y_k$ and its independence from $X_k$.

Next, consider $X_k$. The vector $\bm{x}_k/X_k$ spans $\mathrm{null}(G_{[k+1],[k]}^T)$, and is therefore, after a random signing, uniformly distributed on the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^{k}(\mathbb{R})$. $X_k^{-2}$ is the squared magnitude of its last entry, and so $X_k^{-2}$ is Beta distributed with parameters $\alpha = \frac{1}{2}$ and $\beta = \frac{k}{2}$. Finally, the independence of $X_k$ to $X_{k+1},\ldots,X_{n-1}$ follows from considering the application of a Haar orthogonal matrix to the first $k+1$ rows of $G$. This operation preserves the Gaussian distribution of the matrix, fully randomizes $X_k$, but leaves the values of $X_{k+1},\ldots,X_{n-1}$ unchanged.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lm:beta}
Let $X_1,\ldots, X_{n-1}$ be independent positive random variables satisfying $X_k^{-2} \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\frac12,\frac k2)$ for $k \in [n-1]$, 
\[c_k = \frac{2 \Gamma(\frac{k+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi} \Gamma(\frac k2)} \quad \text{for} \quad k \in [n-1], \qquad \text{and} \qquad \alpha_n =  \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} c_k.\]
Then $\alpha_n = \displaystyle{\frac{2 \sqrt{2}}{3 \sqrt{\pi}} n^{\frac32} }+ O(n^{\frac12})$ and, for fixed $x>0$,
\[\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{k \in [n-1]} X_k \le \alpha_n x \right] \rightarrow e^{-\frac{1}{x}}.\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Using the estimate $\Gamma(x+c)/\Gamma(x) = x^c + O_c(x^{c-1})$, we have
\[\alpha_n = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}} \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{ \Gamma(\frac{k+1}{2})}{ \Gamma(\frac k2)} =  \sqrt{\frac2\pi} \, \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} k^{\frac12} + O(n^{\frac12}) = \frac{2 \sqrt{2}}{3 \sqrt{\pi}} n^{\frac32} + O(n^{\frac12}).\]
For $n$ sufficiently large, $1/(\alpha_n x) < 1/2$ and $|(1-u^2)^{\frac{k}{2}-1}-1|\le C k u^2$ for all $k \in [n-1]$ and $0 \le u \le 1/(\alpha_n x)$, for some constant $C$. Therefore,
\[p_k:=\mathbb{P}\left[X_k \ge \alpha_n x\right] = c_k  \int_{0}^{\frac1{\alpha_nx}} (1-u^2)^{\frac k2 -1} \, d u = \frac{c_k}{\alpha_n x} \left(1 + O_x\left(n^{-2}\right)\right).\]
Independence and the estimate $\log(1-p) = -p+O(p^2)$ imply our desired result
\[\log \mathbb{P}\left[\max_{k \in [n-1]} X_k \le \alpha_n x \right] = - \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} p_k + O\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} p_k^2 \right) = - \frac1x + O_{x}\left(n^{-1}\right).\]
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Lemma \ref{lm:beta} gives control of $X_k$. In order to estimate $\|U\|_{\max}$, we require concentration of $Y_k$. Let $g_1,g_2,\ldots$ be i.i.d. $N(0,1)$. We have
\[\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\max_{j \in [m]} |g_j|}{\sqrt{2 \log m}} \right] \rightarrow 1 \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathbb{E}\left[\left|\max_{j \in [m]} |g_j| - \mathbb{E} \left[ \max_{j \in [m]} |g_j| \right] \right| \right] \le \sqrt{2\pi}, \]
by \cite[Exercise 2.11c]{wainwright2019high} and \cite[Proposition 10.3]{halko2011finding}, respectively. The quantity $\mathbb{E} \max_{j \in [m]} |g_j|$ is monotonic in $m$, and so, for every fixed $\epsilon \in (0,1)$,
\[\sup_{\epsilon n \le m \le n} \mathbb{E} \left[ \left| \frac{\max_{j \in [m]} |g_j|}{\sqrt{2 \log n}} -1\right|\right]\rightarrow 0  \qquad \text{and}  \qquad \sup_{m \in [n]} \mathbb{E} \left[ \left| \frac{\max_{j \in [m]} |g_j|}{\sqrt{2 \log n}} -1\right|\right]\le C.\]
We have $X_k^{-2} \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\frac12,\frac k2)$, and so $\mathbb{P}[X_k > t] \le \frac{\pi c_k}{2t}$ for all $t>0$. Then, by conditioning on each $Y_k$ and using the independence of $X_k$ and $Y_k$,
\[\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{k \in [n-1]} X_k \left|\frac{Y_k}{\sqrt{2 
\log n}} - 1 \right| > \delta \alpha_n \right]  \le \frac{\pi}{2\delta \alpha_n} \sum_{k =1}^{n-1} c_k \, \mathbb{E} \left[\left|\frac{Y_k}{\sqrt{2 
\log n}} - 1 \right| \right] \rightarrow 0\]
for all $\delta >0$. Note that the first row of $U$ is the first row of $G$, and can be safely ignored when considering the scale $\alpha_n \sqrt{2 \log n}$. Therefore, by Lemma \ref{lm:beta},
\[\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{ \|U\|_{\max}}{\alpha_n \sqrt{2 \log n}} \le  x \right] \rightarrow e^{-\frac{1}{x}}.\]
Now, consider $L$. The subdiagonal entries of the $i^{th}$ column of $L$ have the same law as in the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:orthog}, and so \eqref{gauss_L_law} holds here as well and $n^{-3/2}\|L\|_{\max} \rightarrow 0$ in probability. 

Finally, we have $\|G\|_{\max} /(2 \sqrt{\log n} ) \rightarrow 1$ in probability, and so, by Slutsky's theorem,
\[\frac{\|U\|_{\max}}{n^{3/2} \|G\|_{\max}} =  \frac{ \alpha_n}{\sqrt{2} n^{3/2}}\frac{\|U\|_{\max}}{\alpha_n \sqrt{2 \log n}} \frac{2 \sqrt{\log n}}{\|G\|_{\max}} \overset{d}{\longrightarrow}  X.\]

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lm:gauss_bounds}
Let $G \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ be Gaussian and $M \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$, $\Delta \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ be fixed, with $\|\Delta\|_2 \le K$. Then
\begin{align}
\mathbb{P}\left[\|(G+\Delta)M\|_F^2 > 2 (\sqrt{m}+K)^2\|M\|_F^2 + 4 x \|M\|_2^2\right] &\le e^{-x} \qquad \text{and} \\
\mathbb{P}\left[\sigma_k((G+\Delta)M) > \|M\|_F + (\sqrt{m} +K + \sqrt{2 x/k}) \|M\|_2\right] &\le e^{-x}
\end{align}
for all $x >0$ and $k \le \min(m,p)$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $f_k(A):= \sqrt{\sum_{i =1}^k \sigma_i(A)^2}$. Both $G \mapsto \|(G+\Delta)M\|_F$ and $G \mapsto f_k((G+\Delta)M)$ are $\|M\|_2$-Lipschitz, and so, by \cite[Proposition 10.1]{halko2011finding} and the triangle inequality,
\[\mathbb{E}\,\|(G+\Delta)M\|_F \le \left( \sqrt{m} +K \right)\|M\|_F \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathbb{E}\, f_k((G+\Delta)M)\le \sqrt{k} \left( \|M\|_F +  \left( \sqrt{m} +K \right)\|M\|_2\right).\]
The desired result follows from Gaussian concentration \cite[Proposition 10.3]{halko2011finding} with deviation $\sqrt{2x} \|M\|_2$ and the bounds $(a+b)^2 \le 2(a^2+b^2)$ and $\sigma_k(A) \le f_k(A)/\sqrt{k}$.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lm:wide_gauss}
Let $G \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ be Gaussian and $\Delta \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ be fixed, with $m \le r$ and $n \ge 3 r$. Then
\[\mathbb{P}\left[ \|(G +\Delta)^\dagger \|_2^2 > \frac{2 e^{\frac{2x}{r}+2}}{r}  \right] \le e^{-x}\]
for all $x \ge 1$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 2r}$ have orthonormal columns in $\mathrm{null}(\Delta)$. Then $(G+\Delta)Q \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times 2r}$ is Gaussian and $(G+\Delta)(G+\Delta)^T \succeq (G+\Delta)QQ^T(G+\Delta)^T$, so $\|(G +\Delta)^\dagger \|_2 \le \|((G +\Delta)Q)^\dagger \|_2 = \|(GQ)^\dagger\|_2$. Next, add $r-m$ Gaussian rows to $GQ$, which can only decrease the smallest singular value. When $r>1$, apply \cite[Proposition A.3]{halko2011finding} with $t = e\sqrt{2/r}\,e^{x/r}$ to the resulting $r \times 2r$ matrix. When $r=1$, this is a standard result about $\chi_2^2$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lm:gauss_sing_val}
Let $G \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ be Gaussian and $\Delta \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ be fixed. Then
\[\mathbb{P}\left[ \sigma_{n-j}(G+\Delta) \le \frac{c j \theta}{\sqrt n} \right] \le n^{j+1} \theta^{\frac14 j^2}\]
for all $4 \le j < n$ and $\theta \in (0,1]$, for some absolute constant $c \in (0,1]$.

This follows immediately from \cite[(7) of Theorem 1.7]{nguyen2018random} by taking $\gamma = 1/2$, $k = j+1$, $\epsilon = c j \theta/(j+1)$, using $\sup_{a}\mathbb{P}[|X-a|\le \epsilon]\le \sqrt{2/\pi} \; \epsilon$ for $X \sim N(0,1)$, and choosing $c$ sufficiently small.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lm:gauss_convex}
Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^n$ be distributed as $N(\bm v,I_n)$ conditioned on lying in a convex set $\mathcal{K} \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ of positive Gaussian measure with $\mathcal{K}=-\mathcal{K}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\left\| \mathbb{E} X  \right\|_2 \le \| \bm v \|_2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathbb{E} \left[ e^{\bm u^T (X - \mathbb{E} X)}\right] \le e^{\frac12 \|\bm u\|_2^2} \qquad \text{for all} \quad \bm u \in \mathbb{R}^n.
\end{equation}
For fixed $M \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$ and $x >0$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{P}\left[\|X^T M\|_2 > \sqrt{ 2 \|M\|_F^2 + 4x \|M\|_2^2} + \| \bm v\|_2\|M\|_2 \right] \le e^{-x}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let $\varphi_n(\bm x) = (2 \pi)^{-n/2}e^{-\|\bm x\|_2^2/2}$ be the density of a standard Gaussian vector and
\[f(\bm y) = \log \int_{\mathcal{K}} e^{\bm y^T \bm x} \varphi_n(\bm x) \, d \bm x.\]
Then $X_{\bm y}$, distributed as $N(\bm y,I_n)$ conditioned on lying in $\mathcal{K}$, has density $\bm{1}_{\mathcal{K}}(\bm x) e^{\bm y^T \bm x - f(\bm y)} \varphi_n(\bm x)$. In addition, $\nabla f(\bm y) = \mathbb{E} X_{\bm y}$ and $\nabla^2 f(\bm y) = \mathrm{Cov}(X_{\bm y})$. Therefore $\nabla^2 f(\bm y) \succeq 0$. Next, we aim to upper bound this quantity. By \cite[Theorem 6]{prekopa1973logarithmic},
\[\log \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \bm{1}_{\mathcal{K}}(\bm x) \varphi_n(\bm x - \bm y) \, d \bm x = f(\bm y) - \frac12 \|\bm y\|_2^2\]
is concave, and so $\nabla^2 f(\bm y) \preceq I_n$. By the symmetry of $\mathcal{K}$, $\nabla f( 0) = 0$. We have
\[\mathbb{E} X_{\bm y} = \nabla f(\bm y) - \nabla f(0) = \int_0^1 \nabla^2 f(t \bm y) \bm y \, d t,\]
and, by $0 \preceq \nabla^2 f(t \bm y) \preceq I_n$,
\[\|\mathbb{E} X_{\bm y}\|_2 \le \int_0^1 \|\nabla^2 f(t \bm y) \bm y\|_2 \, dt \le \|\bm y\|_2.\]
Similarly,
\begin{align*}
\log \mathbb{E} \left[ e^{\bm u^T (X_{\bm y} - \mathbb{E} X_{\bm y})}\right] &= f(\bm y + \bm u) - f(\bm y) - \bm u^T \mathbb{E} X_{\bm y} \\ &= \int_0^1 (1-t) \, \bm u^T \nabla^2 f(\bm y +t \bm u) \bm u \, d t \\
&\le \int_0^1 (1-t) \|\bm u\|_2^2 \, dt \\
&= \frac12 \|\bm u\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
By \cite[Theorem 2.1]{hsu2012tail} applied to $X_{\bm y} - \mathbb{E} X_{\bm y}$ with $A = (M M^T)^{1/2}$ and $\mathrm{trace}((MM^T)^2) \le \|M\|_2^2 \|M\|_F^2$,
\[\|(X_{\bm y} - \mathbb{E} X_{\bm y})^T M\|_2^2 \le 2 \|M\|_F^2 + 4x \|M\|_2^2\]
with probability at least $1- e^{-x}$, which implies our final desired bound, by the triangle inequality.
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lm:gauss_extension}
Let $k<n$ and the fixed matrix $M \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ satisfy $\sigma_{n-k}(M) \ge \mu$ and $\Sigma_k(M) \le 2 k \mu^{-2}$ for some $0<\mu \le \sqrt{k}$. Let $G \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 4k}$ be Gaussian, $\Delta \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 4k}$ be fixed with $\|\Delta\|_2 \le K$, and $B = [M \;(G + \Delta)]$. Then, for any $x \ge 1$,
\begin{align}
\|B^\dagger\|_2^2 &\le C \mu^{-2} (1+K^2/k)e^{\frac{Cx}{k}} \qquad \text{and}\\
\|B^\dagger\|_F^2 &\le C k \mu^{-2} (1+K^2/k)e^{\frac{Cx}{k}}
\end{align}
with probability at least $1 - 3 e^{-x}$, for some absolute constant $C$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Without loss of generality, let $M$ be diagonal with $M_{ii} = \sigma_{n-i+1}(M)$. Let $\hat M = M_{[n]\setminus[k],[n]\setminus[k]}$, partition $G + \Delta = \begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \end{bmatrix}$, where $X \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times 4k}$ and $Y \in \mathbb{R}^{(n-k) \times 4k}$, and let $Q = X^T(XX^T)^{-1/2} \in \mathbb{R}^{4k \times k}$. Now consider the matrix
\[\hat B = \begin{bmatrix} XQ & 0 \\ Y Q & \hat M \end{bmatrix} \qquad \text{with} \qquad \hat B^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} (XQ)^{-1} & 0 \\ - \hat M^{-1}  Y Q (XQ)^{-1} & \hat M^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.\]
We have
\[B B^T - \hat B \hat B^T = \begin{bmatrix} M_{[k],[k]}M_{[k],[k]}^T & 0 \\  0 & 0  \end{bmatrix} + (G+\Delta)(I-Q Q^T)(G + \Delta)^T \succeq 0,\]
and so we may instead work with $\hat B$ and bound the blocks of $\hat B^{-1}$. By Lemma \ref{lm:gauss_bounds} applied to $(YQ)^T \hat M^{-1}$, conditional on $X$, and Lemma \ref{lm:wide_gauss} applied to $X$,
\begin{align*}
\|\hat M^{-1}YQ\|_F^2 &\le 2(\sqrt{k}+K)^2 \|\hat M^{-1}\|_F^2 + 4x \|\hat M^{-1}\|_2^2 \le C \mu^{-2} (k^2 + kK^2 + x), \\
\|\hat M^{-1}YQ\|_2^2 &\le \left(\|\hat M^{-1}\|_F +(\sqrt{k}+K+\sqrt{2x})\|\hat M^{-1}\|_2\right)^2 \le C \mu^{-2}(k+K^2 + x), \quad \text{and} \\
     \|(XQ)^{-1}\|_2^2 &= \|X^\dagger\|_2^2 \le \frac{2e^2}{k} e^{2x/k}
\end{align*} 
with probability at least $1-3 e^{-x}$. Combining this with the bounds
\begin{align*}
\|\hat B^{-1}\|_F^2 &\le (k +  \|\hat M^{-1}YQ\|_F^2 )\|(XQ)^{-1}\|_2^2+ 2 k \mu^{-2} \quad \text{and} \\
\|\hat B^{-1}\|_2^2 &\le 3\big[(1+\|\hat M^{-1}YQ\|_2^2)\|(XQ)^{-1}\|_2^2+\mu^{-2}\big],
\end{align*}
and the inequality $1 + x/k + K^2/k \le (1+K^2/k)e^{x/k}$ completes the proof.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lm:adding_rows}
Let $M \in  \mathbb{R}^{n \times (n+k)}$ have rank $n$, the columns of $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{(n+k)\times k}$ be an orthonormal basis for $\mathrm{null}(M)$, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times (n+k)}$, and $j<k/2$. Then
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{n+k-2j}\left(\begin{bmatrix} M \\ B \end{bmatrix}\right) \ge \left(\|M^\dagger\|_2 + \frac{1 + \sigma_{j+1}(BM^\dagger)}{\sigma_{k-j}(BQ)} \right)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Let $W \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times (k-2j)}$ have orthonormal columns orthogonal to the bottom $j$ left singular directions of $BQ$ and top $j$ left singular directions of $BM^\dagger$, so that 
\[\sigma_{k-2j}(W^T BQ)\ge \sigma_{k-j}(BQ) \qquad  \text{and} \qquad  \|W^TBM^\dagger\|_2 \le \sigma_{j+1}(BM^{\dagger}).\]
We have
\[\sigma_{n+k-2j}\left(\begin{bmatrix} M \\ B \end{bmatrix}\right) \ge \sigma_{n+k-2j}\left(\begin{bmatrix} M \\ W^T B \end{bmatrix}\right) = \sigma_{\min}\left(\begin{bmatrix} M \\ W^T B \end{bmatrix}\right),\]
and so it suffices to consider the system $M \bm x = \bm a $, $W^T B \bm x = \bm b$ and control the size of the solution $\bm x = M^\dagger \bm a + Q (W^TBQ)^\dagger (\bm b - (W^TBM^\dagger) \bm a)$. We have
\begin{align*}
\|\bm x\|_2 &\le \|M^\dagger\|_2 \| \bm a \|_2 + \frac{\|\bm b\|_2 + \|W^TBM^\dagger\|_2 \|\bm a\|_2}{\sigma_{k-2j}(W^T BQ)} \\
&\le \left(\|M^\dagger\|_2 + \frac{1+\|W^TBM^\dagger\|_2}{\sigma_{k-2j}(W^T BQ)} \right) \sqrt{\|\bm a\|_2^2 + \|\bm b\|_2^2}\\
&\le \left(\|M^\dagger\|_2 + \frac{1+\sigma_{j+1}(BM^{\dagger})}{\sigma_{k-j}(BQ)} \right) \sqrt{\|\bm a\|_2^2 + \|\bm b\|_2^2}.
\end{align*}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:truncated} Let $p >0$ and $r< n/8$, and suppose that, for some deterministic $\tau,\eta >0$,
\[\mathbb{P}\left[ \Sigma_r(T_t)\le \tau \text{ for all } t<n\right] \ge 1 - \eta.\]
Then, for all sufficiently large $n$, depending only on $p$,
\[\mathbb{P}\left[ \mathrm{growth}(\Pi(G)) > \sqrt{1 + \tau} \, e^{ C_p \left(r + \frac{\lambda}r\right)}\right] \le \eta + n^{-p}.\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lm:control}
For every $t<n$ and $r\ge 1$, there is a $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable decomposition
\[E_t = X_t + Y_t Q_t^T,\]
where $Q_t \in \mathbb{R}^{ n \times \min(t,r)}$ satisfies $Q_t^T Q_t = I$. If $t \le r$, every row of $X_t$ has norm one. If $t>r$ and the $\mathcal{H}_t$-measurable condition $\Sigma_r(T_t) \le \tau$ holds, then
\[\mathbb{P}\left[  \max_{i} \|(X_t)_{i,[n]}\|_2^2 > 1 + (2+4x)\tau \, \Big| \, \mathcal{H}_t \right] \le n e^{-x} \qquad \text{for all} \quad x >0.\]
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
When $t \le r$, we may simply take $X_t = [0 \; I]P_t$ and put the rest in the rank $r$ part. When $t > r$, consider a singular value decomposition
\[T_t^{-1} = Z_t + H V^T,\]
where $V^TV = I_r$ and $Z_t$ contains the $t-r$ smallest inverse singular values. Now, let
\[ X_t = [ - G_{\pi^c_t,[t]}Z_t \; I ]P_t, \qquad Y_t = - G_{\pi^c_t,[t]} H, \qquad \text{and} \qquad Q_t = P_t^T \begin{bmatrix} V \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}.  \]
We have $\|Z_t\|_2^2 \le \|Z_t\|_F^2 = \Sigma_r(T_t) \le \tau$, and each row of $X_t$ has squared two-norm $1 + \|G_{i,[t]} Z_t\|_2^2$. Note that, conditional on $\mathcal{H}_t$, the unpivoted rows at step $t$ are distributed as a standard Gaussian conditioned to lie in $\mathcal{K}_t$. Applying Lemma \ref{lm:gauss_convex} with zero mean and taking a union bound provides our desired result.
<!-- end proof-12 -->
<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lm:fix}
Let $n - t> 4r$, condition on $\mathcal{F}_t$, and let $E_t = X_t + Y_t Q_t^T$ be as in Lemma \ref{lm:control}. If every row of $X_t$ has two-norm at most $\zeta$, then, for every $q \ge 1$,
\[\max_i \|(E_{t+4r})_{i,[n]}\|_2 \le \zeta \, e^{C_q\left(r+\frac{\lambda}{r}\right)},\]
except with conditional probability at most $C n^{-q}$, where $C$ and $C_q$ do not depend on $\|Y_t\|_2$.
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Let $G_\star = G_{[n],[t+4r]\setminus[t]}$ contain the next $4r$ columns of the matrix, ordered according to $E_t$, and consider the result of $4r$ further steps of elimination applied to
\[E_t G_\star = X_t G_\star + Y_t (Q_t^T G_\star) \in \mathbb{R}^{(n-t)\times 4r}.\]
Note that, conditional on $\mathcal{F}_t$, $Q_t^T G_\star \in \mathbb{R}^{\min(t,r) \times 4r}$ is Gaussian. Let $P$ permute the rows indexed by $\pi_t^c$ so that the next $4r$ pivot rows come first, in pivot order, and the remaining rows follow, in the order of $\pi_{t+4r}^c$. Let the matrix $J \in \mathbb{R}^{(n-t-4r)\times(n-t)}$ encode the row operations performed during these additional $4r$ elimination steps, with its remaining rows returned to the order of $\pi^c_{t+4r}$. In particular, 
\[J = [- (PE_t G_\star)_{[n-t]\setminus[4r],[4r]} ((PE_t G_\star)_{[4r],[4r]})^{-1} \;\, I_{n-t-4r}]P.\]
Notably, $J E_t = E_{t+4r}$ and $J E_t G_\star = 0$, the result of elimination. Therefore,
\[0 =J E_t G_\star = J X_t G_\star + J Y_t (Q_t^T G_\star) = (J X_t G_\star (Q_t^T G_\star)^\dagger + J Y_t) (Q_t^T G_\star),\]
and so 
\[E_{t+4r} = J X_t  +J Y_t Q_t^T = J(X_t -  (X_t G_\star) (Q_t^T G_\star)^\dagger Q_t^T).  \]
Note that we have removed the dependence on $Y_t$ and its size, replacing it by the controlled portion $(X_t G_\star)$ and a Gaussian pseudoinverse $(Q_t^T G_\star)^\dagger$. Because $J$ encodes $4r$ steps of partial pivoting, it is the product of $4r$ matrices, each of whose rows are of the form $\bm e_i - \ell_i \bm e_j$ for $|\ell_i| \le 1$, and so $\|J_{i,[n-t]}\|_1 \le 2^{4r}$ for all rows. Therefore, by the triangle inequality,
\begin{align*}
 \|(E_{t+4r})_{i,[n]}\|_2 &\le \sum_{j=1}^{n-t}|J_{ij}|\|(X_t -  X_t G_\star (Q_t^T G_\star)^\dagger Q_t^T)_{j,[n]}\|_2 \\
&\le 2^{4r}\left( \zeta + \max_{i} \|(X_t G_\star)_{i,[4r]}\|_2 \|(Q_t^T G_\star)^\dagger\|_2 \right)
\end{align*}
for all $i$. Each row of $X_t G_\star$ has independent Gaussian entries with variance at most $\zeta^2$. By Lemmas \ref{lm:gauss_bounds} and \ref{lm:wide_gauss}, with $x = (q+1) \lambda$, we have
\[ \max_{i} \|(X_t G_\star)_{i,[4r]}\|_2^2 \le C_q \zeta^2(4r + \lambda) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \|(Q_t^T G_\star)^\dagger\|_2^2 \le \frac{C_q}{r} e^{C_q \lambda/r}\]
with conditional probability at least $1 - C n^{-q}$. Combining these two bounds proves our desired result.
<!-- end proof-13 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Let $q = p + 4$ and apply Lemma \ref{lm:control} at every starting stage $t < n - 4r$ with $x = q \lambda$. Then
\[\max_i \|(X_t)_{i,[n]}\|_2^2 \le C_q (1 + \lambda)(1 + \tau)\]
for all such $t$, with probability at least $1 - \eta - n^{2-q}$, where $\eta$ accounts for the truncated inverse bound failing, and $n^{1-q} \times n$ accounts for each of the desired bounds failing. Now, apply Lemma \ref{lm:fix} at each of these starting stages and take a union bound. For $u \le 4r$, the deterministic bound $\|(E_{u})_{i,[n]}\|_2 \le 2^u$ suffices. We have
\[\max_{u<n} \max_i \|(E_{u})_{i,[n]}\|_2  \le \sqrt{1 + \tau} \, e^{C_q\left(r + \frac{\lambda}{r}\right)}\]
with probability at least $1 - \eta - C n^{2-q}$, after absorbing the $\sqrt{1+\lambda}$ into the exponential.
At a fixed stage $t$, the row bound is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable and each Schur complement entry divided by its elimination row norm is a standard Gaussian. Applying the standard bound $\mathbb{P}[|X|>h] \le 2 e^{-\frac12 h^2}$ for $X \sim N(0,1)$ for $h = \sqrt{2 q \lambda}$ and union bounding over all the Schur complement entries, which contain all the entries of $U$, we get that
\[ \|U\|_{\max} \le \sqrt{ 1 + \tau } \, e^{C_p\left(r + \frac{\lambda}{r}\right)}\]
with probability at least $1-\eta - C n^{3-q}$. Note that throughout this argument, we take union bounds over stage-wise conditional estimates, and do not use estimates conditioned on success at all stages. 

Finally, by partial pivoting, $\|L\|_{\max} =1$, and $\|G\|_{\max} \ge 1$ with all but an exponentially small probability, completing the proof.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{lm:extension}
Let $m + 4k \le n$, $k<m$, $d\ge 16$, and $k \ge 100d$. For every $\beta \ge 1$, there is a constant $C_\beta$ such that, for every $0<\mu < \sqrt{k}$,
\[\mathbb{P}\left[\sigma_{m-k}(T_m)\ge \mu, \quad  \Sigma_k(T_m) \le 2 k \mu^{-2}, \quad \text{and} \quad 
\sigma_{m+4k-d}(T_{m+4k}) \le \frac{\mu d}{k} e^{-C_\beta \left(1 + \frac{k \lambda}{d^2}\right)} \right] \le n^{-\beta}.\]
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Let $s = 4k$ and suppose the $\mathcal{F}_m$-measurable event 
\[\sigma_{m-k}(T_m)\ge \mu \qquad \text{and} \qquad  \Sigma_k(T_m) \le 2 k \mu^{-2}\]
holds. Conditional on $\mathcal{F}_m$, the pivots $\pi_m$ are fixed and $G_{\pi_m,[m+s]}$ is the fixed block $T_m$ and $s$ additional Gaussian columns. By Lemma \ref{lm:gauss_extension} with $x = (\beta+1) \lambda$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extend_estimates}
    \|(G_{\pi_m,[m+s]})^\dagger\|_2^2 \le \mu^{-2} e^{C_\beta\left(1+ \frac{\lambda}{k}\right)} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \|(G_{\pi_m,[m+s]})^\dagger\|_F^2 \le k \mu^{-2} e^{C_\beta\left(1+ \frac{\lambda}{k}\right)}
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-3n^{-(\beta+1)}$. Note that this concerns the extension of the rows specifically chosen by $\pi_m$, and so this cost is only paid once.

Now, to treat the next $s$ pivot rows, we fix a set $S$, $|S|=s$, and consider the result of $m$ steps of elimination with partial pivoting without the rows indexed by $S$. Let $\pi^{S}_{m}$ be the first $m$ pivots in that situation. Conditional on all the rows outside of $S$, $G_{\pi^S_m,[m+s]}$ is fixed and $G_{S,[m+s]}$ is Gaussian. In particular, whether \eqref{eq:extend_estimates} holds for $G_{\pi^S_m,[m+s]}$ is already determined. We do not condition on $S$ being the next pivot set. Now, on the event that  \eqref{eq:extend_estimates} does hold for $G_{\pi^S_m,[m+s]}$, let the columns of $Q_S$ be an orthonormal basis for $\mathrm{null}(G_{\pi^S_m,[m+s]})$. Using Lemma \ref{lm:gauss_bounds} with $x = (s+\beta+1)\lambda$ and \eqref{eq:extend_estimates},
\[\sigma_{\lfloor d/2 \rfloor +1}(G_{S,[m+s]} (G_{\pi^S_m,[m+s]})^\dagger) \le \sqrt{k} \mu^{-1} e^{C_\beta\left(1+ \frac{\lambda}{d}\right)}\]
with probability at least $1-n^{-s-\beta-1}$. By Lemma \ref{lm:gauss_sing_val} with $\theta = e^{-C_\beta\left(1+\frac{k\lambda}{d^2}\right)}$, $j = \lfloor d/2 \rfloor$ and $C_\beta$ a large enough constant so that $s^{j+1} \theta^{\frac14 j^2} \le n^{-s-\beta-1}$,
\[\sigma_{s-\lfloor d/2 \rfloor}(G_{S,[m+s]}Q_S)\ge \frac{d}{\sqrt{k}}e^{-C_\beta\left(1+\frac{k\lambda}{d^2}\right)}\]
with probability at least $1-n^{-s-\beta-1}$.

Because $2 \lfloor d/2 \rfloor \le d$, $\mu \le \sqrt{k}$, and $k \ge 100 d$, by Lemma \ref{lm:adding_rows},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:singval_rowextend}
\sigma_{m+s-d}\left( \begin{bmatrix} G_{\pi^S_m,[m+s]} \\ G_{S,[m+s]} \end{bmatrix} \right) \ge \frac{\mu d}{k} e^{-C_\beta\left(1+\frac{k\lambda}{d^2}\right)}
\end{equation}
for a sufficiently large constant $C_\beta$, where we have absorbed the factor $e^{C_\beta \frac{\lambda}{d}}$ in the numerator because $\lambda/d \le k \lambda/d^2$. There are at most $n^s$ choices for $S$. Therefore, the probability that some candidate satisfies \eqref{eq:extend_estimates} but fails \eqref{eq:singval_rowextend} is at most $2 n^{-\beta-1}$. Applying this to the actual next $s$ pivot rows $S$, for which $\pi^S_m = \pi_m$, completes the proof.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:truncated_bound}
For every $\gamma >0$, there exist constants $C_\gamma,n_\gamma$ such that, for $n \ge n_\gamma$, 
\[\max_{k<n} \Sigma_r(T_k) \le \frac{C_\gamma n}{r} e^{C_\gamma \left(\frac{\lambda}{r} +\log \log n \right)}.\]
with probability at least $1-n^{-\gamma}$, simultaneously for all $r \ge \lceil \sqrt{\lambda} \rceil$.

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
For an integer $d \ge \lceil \sqrt{\lambda} \rceil$, consider the recursion $k(d) = \max(100\, d,\lceil d^2/\lambda \rceil)$, with $d_0 = d$ and $d_{i+1} = k(d_i)$, and let
\[ h(d) = \sum_{i|d_i<n}\left(1 + \frac{\lambda}{d_i}\right) \qquad \text{and} \qquad g(d) = \frac{d}{\sqrt{n}} e^{-D_\gamma h(d)}.\]
We use a factor of $100$ so that Lemma \ref{lm:extension} applies, and use the quadratic term so that the sequence grows quickly once $d >\lambda$. The quantity $h(d)$ tracks the total exponential loss accumulated over each stage from Lemma \ref{lm:extension}, and $g(d)$ provides the iterated thresholds for singular values coming from Lemma \ref{lm:extension}. Note that $h(d)$ is non-increasing and $g(d)/d$ is non-decreasing in $d$. We aim to prove that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sing_val_bounds}
\sigma_{t-d}(T_t) \ge g(d) \qquad \text{for all} \quad \lceil \sqrt{\lambda} \rceil \le d <t \le n,
\end{equation}
which implies our desired result. In particular, if \eqref{eq:sing_val_bounds} holds, then, for every $t > r \ge \lceil \sqrt{\lambda} \rceil$,
\[\Sigma_r(T_t)\le \sum_{d=r}^{t-1}\frac{1}{g(d)^2} \le \frac{r^2}{g(r)^2} \sum_{d=r}^{\infty} \frac{1}{d^2} \le \frac{2 r}{g(r)^2} = \frac{2n}{r}e^{2 D_\gamma h(r)}.\]
In addition,
\[h(r) \le C\left(\frac{\lambda}{r}+ \log \log n\right),\]
as $\sum_{i} \lambda/d_i \le \lambda/r \sum_{i \ge 0} 100^{-i} \le 2 \lambda/r$, and $h(d)$ involves at most $O(\log \log n)$ steps, $O(\log \log n)$ for $d_i$ to exceed $\lambda$ and another $O(\log \log n)$ steps for $d_i$ to exceed $n$. 

What remains is to prove \eqref{eq:sing_val_bounds} holds with probability $1- n^{-\gamma}$. We do so by induction on $t$. Let $\beta = \gamma + 2$, $D_\gamma$ be sufficiently large, and $n_\gamma$ be large enough that $\sqrt{\lambda} \ge 16$.

First, suppose that $k(d) \ge t/8$. There are $O(n^t)$ choices of $t$ rows, and so we may bound $\sigma_{t-d}(T_t)$ by union bounding over $G_{S,[t]}$ for all $|S|=t$. In particular, by Lemma \ref{lm:gauss_sing_val} applied $O(n^t)$ times with $\theta = c^{-1} e^{-C_\gamma \left(1 + \frac{\lambda}{d} \right)}$, the probability that 
\[\sigma_{t-d}(T_t)<g(d) = \frac{d}{\sqrt{n}} e^{-D_\gamma h(d)} \le   \frac{cd}{\sqrt{t}} \left(c^{-1} e^{-C_\gamma \left(1 + \frac{\lambda}{d}\right)} \right)\]
is at most
\[\exp\left((t+d+1) \lambda - \frac{d^2}{4}\left[C_\gamma \left(1+\frac{\lambda}{d}\right) - \log \frac{1}{c} \right] \right).\]
By assumption, $t \lambda \le 8 k(d)  \lambda \le C(d \lambda + d^2)$, so this probability is at most $n^{-\beta}$ for $C_\gamma$ sufficiently large.

Now, consider the case where $k(d)<t/8$, and let $u = t - 4k(d)$, so that $u>t/2>k(d)$. If our desired result \eqref{eq:sing_val_bounds} holds at stage $u$, then
\[\sigma_{u-k}(T_u) \ge g(k) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Sigma_k(T_u) \le \sum_{\ell = k}^{u-1} g(\ell)^{-2} \le 2 k g(k)^{-2}. \]
Note that $g(k) \le k/\sqrt{n} \le \sqrt{k}$, so we may apply Lemma \ref{lm:extension} with $\mu = g(k)$, providing our desired inductive step with all but probability $n^{-\beta}$. Taking a union bound over the $<n^2$ pairs $(t,d)$, the total failure probability is at most $n^2 \times n^{-\beta} = n^{-\gamma}$, completing the proof.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:smooth_general}
For every $\alpha >0$, there exist constants $C_\alpha,n_\alpha$ such that, for all $n \ge n_\alpha$, every $\Delta \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, $\epsilon >0$, and every $\lceil \sqrt{\lambda} \rceil \le r < n/8$,
\[\mathbb{P}\left[ \mathrm{growth} (\Pi(\Delta +\epsilon G)) > C_\alpha \left(1 + \frac{\|\Delta\|_2}{\epsilon} \right)^3 \sqrt{\frac{n}{r}} \, e^{C_\alpha \left(r + \frac{\lambda}{r}\right)} f_{\frac{ \|\Delta\|_2}{\epsilon}}(r) \right] < n^{-\alpha}.\]
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Without loss of generality, let $\epsilon =1$. We define $B = \Delta + G$, $\pi_t$ to be the first $t$ pivot indices of $B$, $T_t = B_{\pi_t,[t]}$, and $K = \|\Delta\|_2$. $\mathcal{H}_t$ records $\pi_t$ and $T_t$, and $\mathcal{F}_t$ records $B_{[n],[t]}$. The elimination matrices are given by
\[E_t = [ - B_{\pi_t^c,[t]}T_t^{-1} \;  I]P_t\]
and we use the decomposition $E_t = X_t + Y_t Q_t^T$ from Lemma \ref{lm:control}, where $T_t^{-1} = Z_t + H V^T$, with
\[X_t = [-B_{\pi_t^c,[t]}Z_t \; I]P_t, \qquad Y_t = -B_{\pi_t^c,[t]}H, \qquad \text{and} \qquad Q_t = P_t^T \begin{bmatrix} V \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}.\]

First, we convert the truncated inverse bound into a bound on growth (i.e., modify Proposition \ref{prop:truncated}). Suppose $\Sigma_r(T_t)\le \tau$ for every $t<n$ with probability at least $1-\eta$. We aim to bound growth under this hypothesis. Conditional on $\mathcal{H}_t$, each non-pivoted row in the first $t$ coordinates is $N(\Delta_{i,[t]},I)$ restricted to same origin-symmetric convex set $\mathcal{K}_t$ as before. For $t>r$, on the $\mathcal{H}_t$ measurable event that  $\Sigma_r(T_t)\le \tau$, Lemma \ref{lm:gauss_convex} and a union bound implies that
\[
\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{i \in \pi_t^c} \|B_{i,[t]}Z_t\|_2
> \left(\sqrt{2+4q \lambda}+K\right)\sqrt{\tau} \, \Big| \, \mathcal{H}_t\right]<n^{1-q},\]
and so, for $C_q$ large enough and $R=C_q(1+K^2+\lambda)(1+\tau)$,
\[\mathbb{P}\left[\max_i
\|(X_t)_{i,[n]}\|_2^2>R \, \Big| \, \mathcal{H}_t \right] <n^{1-q}\]
on that same event. Again, for $t \le r$, the rows of $X_t$ have two-norm one, so the bound always holds.  Now, let 
\[B_\star = B_{[n],[t+4r]\setminus[t]}, \qquad G_\star = G_{[n],[t+4r]\setminus[t]}, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Delta_\star = \Delta_{[n],[t+4r]\setminus[t]},\]
and consider $4r$ additional steps of Gaussian elimination. Each row of $X_t B_\star$ has independent Gaussian entries, with  variance at most $R$ and mean row two-norm at most $K \sqrt{R}$. Also, conditional on $\mathcal{F}_t$,
\[Q_t^T B_\star = Q_t^T \Delta_\star + Q_t^T G_\star,\]
where $Q_t^TG_\star$ is Gaussian. By Lemmas \ref{lm:gauss_bounds} and \ref{lm:wide_gauss}, conditioned on the event $\max_i \|(X_t)_{i,[n]}\|_2^2 \le R$,
\[ \max_{i} \|(X_t B_\star)_{i,[4r]}\|_2^2  \le C_q R(K^2+ r + \lambda) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \|(Q_t^T B_\star)^\dagger\|_2^2 \le C r^{-1} e^{C_q\frac{\lambda}{r}},\]
except with conditional probability at most $C n^{-q}$. Again, let $J$ record the next $4r$ steps of elimination, with the non-pivot rows returned to increasing order. As in Lemma \ref{lm:fix},
\[E_{t+4r} = J(X_t - X_t B_\star (Q_t^T B_\star)^\dagger Q_t^T) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \max_{i} \|J_{i,[n-t]}\|_1 \le 2^{4r}.\]
Taking a union bound, together with the deterministic bound for $u \le 4r$, we have
\[\max_{u<n} \max_i \|(E_u)_{i,[n]}\|_2 \le (1+K)^2 \sqrt{1+\tau} \, e^{C_q \left(r + \frac{\lambda}{r}\right)}\]
with probability at least $1 - \eta - C n^{2 - q}$. For a row $\bm e^T$ of $E_u$ and a column $j>u$, conditional on $\mathcal{F}_u$,
\[\bm e^T B_{[n],j} \sim N(\bm e^T \Delta_{[n],j},\|\bm e\|_2^2).\]
Using the bound $|\bm e^T \Delta_{[n],j}| \le K \|\bm e \|_2$ and applying a Gaussian tail bound over the $O(n^3)$ Schur complement entries loses an additional factor $K + \sqrt{2 q \lambda}$. The same probabilistic argument lower bounding the max norm of the matrix also holds for $\|\Delta + G \|_{\max}$, and still $\|L\|_{\max} = 1$. All together, we obtain
\[\mathrm{growth}(\Pi(\Delta + G)) \le (1+K)^3 \sqrt{1 + \tau} \, e^{C_q \left(r+ \frac{\lambda}{r}\right)}\]
with probability $1-\eta - Cn^{3-q}$ at least. This completes the first half of the proof. 

What remains is to produce a modified truncated inverse bound (i.e., modify Proposition \ref{prop:truncated_bound}). First, we adapt Lemma \ref{lm:extension}. Restricting to the $\mathcal{F}_m$-measurable event $\sigma_{m-k}(T_m) \ge \mu$ and $\Sigma_{k}(T_m) \le 2 k \mu^{-2}$. Lemma \ref{lm:gauss_extension} multiplies both bounds in \eqref{eq:extend_estimates} by $v_k$. As in Lemma \ref{lm:extension}, we fix a set $S$ of size $s=4k$ and condition on all the other rows. Again, $\pi_m^S$ is the first $m$ pivots after deleting $S$, $j = \lfloor d/2\rfloor$, and here the columns of $Q_S$ form an orthonormal basis for $\mathrm{null}(B_{\pi_m^S,[m+s]})$. On the event that $B_{\pi_m^S,[m+s]}$ does satisfy the above two bounds, Lemma \ref{lm:gauss_bounds} with $x = (s+\beta + 1)\lambda$ implies that
\[\sigma_{j +1}(B_{S,[m+s]} (B_{\pi^S_m,[m+s]})^\dagger) \le \sqrt{k} \mu^{-1} v_k e^{C_\beta \left(1+ \frac{\lambda}{d}\right)},\]
except with conditional probability at most $n^{-s-\beta-1}$. The small singular value estimate for $B_{S,[m+s]}Q_S$ is unchanged, as Lemma \ref{lm:gauss_sing_val} is unchanged by a non-zero mean. Together, the new output threshold for Lemma \ref{lm:extension} is
\[\frac{\mu d}{kv_k} e^{-C_\beta \left(1+\frac{k \lambda}{d^2}\right)}.\]
The quantity $f_K(r)$ tracks the accumulation of the additional term $v_k$ in the denominator. Take $\beta = \gamma +2$ and choose $D_\gamma$ sufficiently large. Because $f_{K}(d)$ is non-increasing in $d$, $g_{K}(d) /d$ is non-decreasing still. The same argument holds for the product recurrence $k(d)<n$, now with
\[\frac{g_{K}(d)}{g_{K}(k)} = \frac{d}{k v_k} e^{-D_\gamma \left(1+\frac{\lambda}{d}\right)},\]
which leads to the worse bound
\[\max_{t<n} \Sigma_r(T_t) \le \frac{2n}{r} e^{2 D_\gamma h(r)}f_K(r)^2 \le  \frac{C_\gamma n}{r} e^{C_\gamma \left(\frac{\lambda}{r}+\log \log n\right)} f_{K}(r)^2\]
with probability at least $1-n^{-\gamma}$.

The result follows by combining both arguments, with $\gamma = \alpha + 1$, $\eta = n^{- \gamma}$, and $q = \alpha + 4$.
