<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
We call the stratum $\mathring S_u$ the \emph{(open) double Bruhat cell} for the symmetric space $G/K$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Let $\widetilde G=G\times G$. We consider the involution
\[
\widetilde\theta:G\times G\to G\times G,
\qquad
(g,h)\longmapsto(\theta(h),\theta(g)).
\]
Denote the fixed-point group by $\widetilde K=\widetilde G^{\widetilde\theta}$. Consider the symmetric space $\widetilde G/\widetilde K$. It follows from Evens--Lu \cite{EL06} that the intersections of diagonal $G$-orbits with $(B^+,B^-)$-orbits are precisely the $T$-leaves of the natural Poisson structure on $\widetilde G/\widetilde K$ as a Poisson homogeneous space; see also Lu \cite{Lu14}. The map
\[
G\times G\to G,
\qquad
(g,h)\longmapsto g\theta(h)^{-1}
\]
induces an isomorphism $\widetilde G/\widetilde K\cong G$. Under this isomorphism, diagonal $G$-orbits in $\widetilde G/\widetilde K$ are identified with the twisted conjugacy classes, and $(B^+,B^-)$-orbits are identified with $(B^+,\theta(B^-))$ double cosets in $G$. Under this isomorphism, $S$ is precisely the diagonal $G$-orbit of $\widetilde K$. This leads to the stratum $\mathring S_u$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:prelim-strata-intersection}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathring S_u\neq\emptyset$ if and only if $u\leq w^\bullet$ in the Bruhat order.
\item $S_u=\bigsqcup_{u\leq v\leq w^\bullet}\mathring S_v$.
\item For $u\leq w^\bullet$, $\mathring S_u\subset S$ is a smooth, connected, affine subvariety of dimension $\ell(w^\bullet)-\ell(u)+r$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We prove Part~(1). Let $\theta'=\operatorname{Int}(\dot w^\bullet)\theta$. Then $\theta'$ fixes $B^+$ and $B^-$. Let $C$ be the $\theta'$-twisted conjugacy class of $(\dot w^\bullet)^{-1}$. Then $\iota(G/K)=C\cdot\dot w^\bullet$. Since $B^+K/K\subset G/K$ is dense, $B^+(\dot w^\bullet)^{-1}B^+\cap C$ is dense in $C$. By \cite{CLT10}*{Section 2.4}, the intersection 
\[
C\cap B^+\dot uB^-=(\iota(G/K)\cap B^+\dot uB^-\dot w^\bullet)(\dot w^\bullet)^{-1}
\]
is non-empty if and only if $u\leq w^\bullet=(w^\bullet)^{-1}$.

We prove Part~(2). We follow the notation in the preceding remark. By the Bruhat decomposition, we have
\[
\overline{B^+\dot uB^-\dot w^\bullet}
=
\bigsqcup_{u\leq v}B^+\dot vB^-\dot w^\bullet.
\]
We can equivalently consider the closure relations in $\widetilde G/\widetilde K$, since $S$ is closed in $G$. Then we can apply Richardson's theorem. In particular,
\[
S_u
=
S\cap \iota^{-1}(\overline{B^+\dot uB^-\dot w^\bullet})
=
\bigsqcup_{u\leq v\leq w^\bullet}\mathring S_v.
\]
The finiteness condition in Richardson's theorem is not needed here, since $S$ is closed; see \cite{Ri92}*{Theorem 2.1}. The assertions about smoothness, connectedness, and dimension in Part~(3) follow from \cite{Ri92}*{Corollary 1.5}. Finally, $B^+\dot uB^-\dot w^\bullet$ is affine, and $\iota(\mathring S_u)$ is closed in this double Bruhat cell. Hence $\mathring S_u$ is affine.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:enveloping-order}
Let $\lambda,\nu\in\breve X_{\mathrm{sc}}$. Then
\[
[\lambda]=[\nu]
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\lambda-\nu\in\breve X_{\mathrm{ad}}.
\]
If $\nu\preceq\lambda$, these conditions are equivalent to
$\lambda-\nu\in Q_\theta^+$, and then $\nu\leq\lambda$.
Consequently,
\[
E_\lambda=\bigl(k[S_{\mathrm{sc}}]_{\leq\lambda}\bigr)_{[\lambda]},
\]
and $E_\lambda$ has a good filtration with factors $V(\nu)^*$ for
$\nu\in\breve X_{\mathrm{sc}}^+$ such that
$\lambda-\nu\in Q_\theta^+$, each occurring once.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The first equivalence follows from \eqref{eq:enveloping-D}.
Suppose $\nu\preceq\lambda$ and $[\lambda]=[\nu]$. Then
$\lambda-\nu\in\breve X_{\mathrm{ad}}\subset Q$, so
$\nu\leq\lambda$. By \cite{BS25b}*{Lemma 2.3},
$\lambda-\nu$ lies in the nonnegative rational cone generated by
the $\overline\alpha_i$. Since these form a $\mathbb Z$-basis of
$\breve X_{\mathrm{ad}}$, its coefficients are nonnegative integers.
Thus $\lambda-\nu\in Q_\theta^+$. The converse follows from
$Q_\theta^+\subset\breve X_{\mathrm{ad}}$.

The good-filtration factor $V(\nu)^*$ of
$k[S_{\mathrm{sc}}]$ is indecomposable, and the $D$-action commutes
with $G$. It therefore has a single $D$-character, namely $[\nu]$,
as its $B^-$-eigenline is represented by $\chi_\nu$.
Exactness of character spaces and the preceding
equivalence therefore retain precisely the factors with
$\lambda-\nu\in Q_\theta^+$. These are also the factors of
$\bigl(k[S_{\mathrm{sc}}]_{\leq\lambda}\bigr)_{[\lambda]}$,
proving the asserted equality.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:Ttorsor}
The quotient morphism
\[
\pi:\widehat E^0\longrightarrow\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace
\]
is a Zariski-locally trivial $\breve T_{\mathrm{sc}}$-torsor.
More precisely, every $x\in\widehat E^0$ has a $\breve T_{\mathrm{sc}}$-stable affine
neighbourhood of the form
\[
\pi^{-1}(W)\cong W\times \breve T_{\mathrm{sc}}
\]
over an affine open subset $W\subset\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Recall that $q_\eta=\chi_\eta e^\eta\in k[\widehat E]$ and that
$D(gq_\eta)$, for $g\in G(k)$, cover $\widehat E^0$.
For $\lambda\in\breve X_{\mathrm{sc}}^+$, put
$q_\lambda=\chi_\lambda e^\lambda$.
We first prove that the scheme-theoretic stabilizers are trivial.
For any $\nu\in\breve X_{\mathrm{sc}}$, choose $n$ sufficiently
large that $n\eta\pm\nu\in\breve X_{\mathrm{sc}}^+$. On
$D(q_\eta)$, the functions
\[
q_\nu=\frac{q_{n\eta+\nu}}{q_\eta^n},\qquad
q_{-\nu}=\frac{q_{n\eta-\nu}}{q_\eta^n}
\]
are regular and homogeneous of degrees $\nu$ and $-\nu$.
Multiplicativity of the eigenfunctions gives
\[
q_{n\eta+\nu}q_{n\eta-\nu}=q_{2n\eta}=q_\eta^{2n},
\]
so $q_\nu q_{-\nu}=1$. Thus both functions are units on
$D(q_\eta)$.

Let $x$ be a geometric point of $D(q_\eta)$, and let
$N=\operatorname{Stab}_{\breve T_{\mathrm{sc}}}(x)$ be its
scheme-theoretic stabilizer. The homogeneity identity

If $Y\subset X$ and $\ensuremath{\mathbf {Y}}\xspace\subset\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace$ are closed $G$-orbits, then $\pi|_Y:Y\to\ensuremath{\mathbf {Y}}\xspace$ is an isomorphism. Moreover, if
\[
\widetilde\omega_X=\omega_X\otimes\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_X(\partial X),
\qquad
\widetilde\omega_{\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace}=\omega_{\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace}\otimes\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_{\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace}(\partial\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace),
\]
then
\[
\widetilde\omega_X\simeq\pi^*\widetilde\omega_{\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
The extension of $\pi$ follows from \cite{Kno91}*{Theorem 4.1}, the assertion on closed orbits from \cite{Tan18}*{Lemma 1.2}, and the identity of logarithmic canonical sheaves from \cite{Tan18}*{Lemma 1.4}.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Assume $\operatorname{char}k=p>2$, and let $X$ be an equivariant embedding of $S$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
\item If $X$ is smooth, then $X$ admits a Frobenius splitting relative to
\[
(p-1)\left(\partial X+\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}}X_{s_i}\right)
\]
that compatibly splits every boundary divisor and every stratum closure $X_u$.
\item Every equivariant embedding $X$ admits a Frobenius splitting compatible with every boundary divisor and every stratum closure $X_u$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
We prove the two statements together. We follow the outline of \cite{Tan18}*{Proposition 2.1(i)}, using the distinguished sections of Section~\ref{sec:wonderful-splitting}. By \cite{Tan18}*{Theorem 2.1(i)}, there is a $G$-equivariant rational resolution
\[
q:\widetilde X\longrightarrow X
\]
such that $q$ is projective and $\widetilde X$ is a nonsingular quasi-projective toroidal embedding. Since $X$ is normal and $q$ is proper and birational, $q_*\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_{\widetilde X}=\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_X$. By \cite{BK05}*{Lemma 1.1.8}, a splitting of $\widetilde X$ descends to $X$, together with compatibility with the images of compatibly split subvarieties. It therefore suffices to construct the compatible splitting on $\widetilde X$; the relative assertion for smooth $X$ will follow at the end. Thus we may assume for the construction that $X$ is nonsingular, toroidal, and quasi-projective.

           
By \cite{Tim07}*{Section 29.2} or \cite{Kno91}, $X$ admits a nonsingular toroidal equivariant completion. A splitting of the completion restricts to $X$, as does compatibility with the relevant closed subschemes. We may therefore further assume that $X$ is complete. 

Lemma \ref{lem:embeddings-toroidal-map} gives a $G$-equivariant morphism $\pi:X\longrightarrow\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace$.
The sections of Section~\ref{subsec:wonderful-distinguished-sections}
come from the restricting of
\[
\varphi_i\in E_{\overline{\omega_i}}
\cong H^0(\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace,\ensuremath{\mathcal {L}}\xspace_{-\overline{\omega_i}}).
\]
By Corollary~\ref{cor:si}, their zero schemes are
$Z(\varphi_i)=\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace_{s_{\kappa(i)}}$.
Put $s=\prod_{i\in\mathbf{I}}\varphi_i$. Multiplication gives
$s\in E_{\overline\rho}$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:wc} identifies
\[
\ensuremath{\mathcal {L}}\xspace_{-\overline\rho}\cong\widetilde\omega_{\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace}^{-1}.
\]
If $\ensuremath{\mathbf {Y}}\xspace\subset\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace$ is the closed orbit, put $\overline s=s|_{\ensuremath{\mathbf {Y}}\xspace}$.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:dv} and the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:WCsplit}, $\overline s^{p-1}$ defines the
closed-orbit splitting after a nonzero scalar normalization.
           
By Lemma \ref{lem:embeddings-toroidal-map}, the pullback
\[
\widetilde s=\pi^*s
\]
is a section of $\widetilde\omega_X^{-1}$. Let $s_{\partial X}$ be the canonical section of $\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_X(\partial X)$ and set
\[
\sigma_X=s_{\partial X}\widetilde s\in H^0(X,\omega_X^{-1}).
\]
For every closed $G$-orbit $Y\subset X$, the restriction $\pi|_Y$ identifies $Y$ with $\ensuremath{\mathbf {Y}}\xspace$ and identifies $\widetilde s|_Y$ with $\overline s$. Hence $(\widetilde s|_Y)^{p-1}$ splits $Y$ after normalization by the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:WCsplit}.

           
The local expansion at a point of a closed $G$-orbit now shows that
\[
\sigma_X^{p-1}\in H^0(X,\omega_X^{1-p})
\]
defines a Frobenius splitting of $X$ after a nonzero scalar normalization. This is precisely the local argument in \cite{Tan18}*{Proposition 2.1(i)}; see also \cite{BK05}*{Theorem 1.3.8 and Exercise 1.3.E.4}. The factor $s_{\partial X}$ implies that every boundary divisor is compatibly split.

           
The explicit factorization of $s$ determines further compatibly split subschemes. Indeed, we have 
\[
\widetilde s=\prod_{i\in\mathbf{I}}\pi^*\varphi_i,
\]
and $Z(\sigma_X)$ is reduced and compatibly split by
\cite{BK05}*{Proposition~1.3.11}. On the open orbit, the support
of $Z(\pi^*\varphi_i)$ is $S_{s_{\kappa(i)}}$; this follows
from Corollary~\ref{cor:si} and the compatibility of the stratum
definitions with the adjoint quotient. Since $\kappa$ permutes
$\mathbf{I}$, every nonempty $X_{s_i}$ is an irreducible component of
$Z(\sigma_X)$ and is compatibly split.

We obtain compatibility with $X_u$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:prelim-strata-intersection}, standard combinatorics on Bruhat orders, and \cite{BK05}*{Proposition~1.2.1}.

                            
                                                       
                                                                  
                                                                   
                                                                
                                                                   
                                                                 
                                                          
                                                          

                                                                    
                                                                 
                                                                  
                                                                     

Finally, when $X$ is smooth, the compatible splitting is relative to
\[
(p-1)\left(\partial X+\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}}X_{s_i}\right)
\]
by \cite{BK05}*{Theorem 1.4.10}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:AmpleDiv}
Assume that $\operatorname{char}k\neq2$. Let $X$ be a smooth projective
toroidal embedding of the symmetric space $S$, with boundary divisors
$D_1,\dots,D_n$. Then there exist positive integers $a_1,\dots,a_n$ such
that $\sum_{i=1}^n a_iD_i$ is an ample effective Cartier divisor.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Let $\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace$ be the wonderful compactification of $S_{\mathrm{ad}}$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:embeddings-toroidal-map}, the canonical map
$S\to S_{\mathrm{ad}}$ extends to a $G$-equivariant morphism
\[
\pi:X\longrightarrow\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace.
\]
The local structure results used below apply to symmetric spaces in
characteristic different from $2$; see \cite{DCS99}*{Proposition~3.8 and
Theorem~3.9} and \cite{Tan18}*{Section~3, pp.~502--503}.

Choose the standard affine chart $\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace_0=\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace^-\subseteq\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace$ of
Section~\ref{subsec:wonderful-closed-orbit}. The compatible
local structure theorems give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ample-boundary-chart}
\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace_0\simeq U\times\ensuremath{\mathbb {A}}\xspace^{r_{\mathrm{ad}}},
\qquad
X_0:=\pi^{-1}(\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace_0)\simeq U\times Z,
\end{equation}
where $U=U_P^-$, $r_{\mathrm{ad}}$ is the rank of
$S_{\mathrm{ad}}$, and $Z$ is a toric variety with dense torus
$\breve T$ whose fan is the fan of $X$. The boundary divisors of $X$
correspond bijectively to the torus-invariant prime divisors
$Z_1,\dots,Z_n$ of $Z$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ample-boundary-components}
D_i\cap X_0=U\times Z_i.
\end{equation}
For the equality of fans and the boundary correspondence, see
\cite{Tan18}*{Lemma~1.2(i)}; the inverse-image description in
\eqref{eq:ample-boundary-chart} is used in
\cite{Tan18}*{proof of Theorem~2.1(i), p.~501}.
The $G$-translates of $\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace_0$ cover $\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace$.

Since $Z$ is closed in the open subvariety $X_0$ of the projective
variety $X$, it is quasiprojective. A quasiprojective toric variety
has an effective ample Cartier divisor supported on its toric boundary:
this follows from the invariant-divisor description of its Picard group
and the character decomposition of global sections in
\cite{CLS11}*{Theorem~4.2.1 and Proposition~4.3.3}, applied to a very
ample line bundle. These descriptions are valid over any algebraically
closed field; the same consequence is used in
\cite{Tan18}*{proof of Theorem~2.1(i), p.~501}.
Choose such a divisor
\[
E_Z=\sum_{i=1}^n b_iZ_i,\qquad b_i\in\ensuremath{\mathbb {Z}}\xspace_{\geq0},
\]
and set $E=\sum_i b_iD_i$. Since $X$ is smooth, $E$ is Cartier.
By \eqref{eq:ample-boundary-components},
\[
\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_X(E)|_{X_0}\simeq\operatorname{pr}_Z^*\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_Z(E_Z).
\]
This sheaf is ample, since $\operatorname{pr}_Z:U\times Z\to Z$ is
affine and pullback by an affine morphism preserves ampleness
\cite{Stacks}*{Tag~0892(2)}. Since $E$ is $G$-stable and
$\pi^{-1}(g\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace_0)=gX_0$, its restriction to $\pi^{-1}(g\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace_0)$ is
ample for every $g\in G(k)$. Relative ampleness is local on the base
\cite{Stacks}*{Tag~01VJ}; hence $E$ (on $X$) is $\pi$-ample.

We next choose an ample effective Cartier divisor $H$ on $\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace$ whose
support is $\partial\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace$. Use the projective realization
\[
\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace=\overline{G_{\mathrm{ad}}\cdot[h]}
\subseteq\ensuremath{\mathbb {P}}\xspace\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_k({}^{\theta_{\mathrm{ad}}}V,V)\bigr)
\]
of \cite{DCS99}*{construction preceding Lemma~3.4 and Theorem~3.9}.
The open orbit consists of invertible maps, while every boundary point
represents a singular map; this is stated in
\cite{DCS99}*{proof of Theorem~3.9(ii), p.~287}.
Here ${}^{\theta_{\mathrm{ad}}}V$ is the twist of $V$ by
$\theta_{\mathrm{ad}}$, and $h$ is the canonical linear isomorphism.
Thus the determinant restricts to a nonzero section
$\delta\in H^0(\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace,\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace(d))$, where $d=\dim V$, and
\[
H=\operatorname{div}_{\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace}(\delta),
\qquad
\operatorname{Supp}(H)=\partial\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace,
\qquad
\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace(H)\simeq\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_\ensuremath{\mathbf {X}}\xspace(d).
\]
In particular, $H$ is an ample effective Cartier divisor.

The pullback $\pi^*H$ is an effective Cartier divisor, since its defining
section is nonzero on the dense open orbit $S$. Its support is contained
in $\partial X$, because $\pi(S)\subseteq S_{\mathrm{ad}}$.
Since $E$ is $\pi$-ample and $H$ is ample, there exists an integer
$N\geq0$ such that
\[
A=E+N\pi^*H
\]
is ample by \cite{Stacks}*{Tag~0892(1)}. Hence
$A=\sum_i c_iD_i$ with $c_i\in\ensuremath{\mathbb {Z}}\xspace_{\geq0}$.

It remains to make every coefficient of $A$ positive. Put $\Delta=\sum_iD_i$,
which is Cartier because $X$ is smooth. For $m\gg0$, the sheaf
$\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_X(\Delta+(m-1)A)$ is globally generated
\cite{Stacks}*{Tag~01Q3(7)}. Tensoring with the ample sheaf
$\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_X(A)$ shows that $\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_X(\Delta+mA)$ is ample
\cite{Stacks}*{Tag~0890}. Therefore
\[
D=\Delta+mA=\sum_{i=1}^n(1+mc_i)D_i
\]
is an ample effective Cartier divisor with all coefficients strictly
positive, as required.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:global-f-regular-strata}
Assume that $\operatorname{char}k=p>2$. Let $X$ be a projective
equivariant embedding of $S$. Then $X_u$ is globally $F$-regular for
every $u\leq w^\bullet$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Suppose first that $X$ is smooth and toroidal. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:AmpleDiv}, there is an ample effective Cartier
divisor
\[
D=\sum_{i=1}^n a_iD_i,\qquad a_i>0,
\]
with $\operatorname{Supp}(D)=\partial X$. Let $\varphi$ be the
splitting constructed in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. It splits $X$
relative to $(p-1)\partial X$ and compatibly splits every $X_u$.
Iterating this relative splitting gives a stable splitting along
$(p^e-1)\partial X$. Choose $e$ such that $a_i\leq p^e-1$ for all
$i$, and restrict along
\[
\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_X(D)\hookrightarrow\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_X((p^e-1)\partial X).
\]
We obtain a stable splitting along $D$ whose induced Frobenius
splitting still compatibly splits every $X_u$; see
\cite{BT06}*{Lemma~3.1(4)--(5)} and
\cite{BK05}*{proof of Lemma~1.4.11}.

Since $X_u$ meets $S$, it is not contained in $\operatorname{Supp}(D)$.
Consequently, $D|_{X_u}$ is an ample effective Cartier divisor, and
the stable splitting descends to a stable splitting of $X_u$ along
$D|_{X_u}$ by \cite{BK05}*{Proposition~1.4.3}, applied to the
iterated Frobenius. Moreover,
\[
X_u\setminus\operatorname{Supp}(D|_{X_u})=X_u\cap S=S_u,
\]
which is strongly $F$-regular by 
Proposition~\ref{prop:DBFregular}. Theorem~\ref{thm:gloFre}(2)
therefore implies that $X_u$ is globally $F$-regular.

For an arbitrary projective embedding $X$, choose a projective
$G$-equivariant resolution
\[
q:\widetilde X\longrightarrow X
\]
with $\widetilde X$ smooth and toroidal, as in
\cite{Tan18}*{Theorem~2.1(i)}. Then $\widetilde X$ is projective and
$q_*\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_{\widetilde X}=\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_X$, since $X$ is normal. Let
$\widetilde X_u$ be the closure of $S_u$ in $\widetilde X$, and write
$q_u:\widetilde X_u\to X_u$ for the restriction of $q$. The preceding
argument gives a stable splitting of $\widetilde X$ along an ample
effective Cartier divisor supported on $\partial\widetilde X$,
compatible with $\widetilde X_u$. Since this divisor does not contain
$\widetilde X_u$, \cite{BT06}*{Lemma~3.2} gives
\[
(q_u)_*\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_{\widetilde X_u}=\ensuremath{\mathcal {O}}\xspace_{X_u}.
\]
The variety $\widetilde X_u$ is globally $F$-regular by the smooth
toroidal case. Hence $X_u$ is globally $F$-regular by
\cite{BT06}*{Corollary~3.2}.
