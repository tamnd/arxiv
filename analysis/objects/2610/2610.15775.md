<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The choice of the unit vector $\vec\nu$, rather than the contravariant vector $J\vec a^3$ itself, avoids geometric source terms in the acoustic subsystem.
Since $\vec\nu$ has unit length, $\big(\partial_{\xi^3}\vec\nu\big)\cdot\vec\nu = 0$, and the pressure contribution to the last term vanishes identically, i.e., $\vec\nu\cdot\partial_{\xi^3}\big(p\,J\vec a^3\big) = \partial_{\xi^3}(n\,p)$, for any mesh geometry.
The remaining geometric term, $-\big(\partial_{\xi^3}\vec\nu\big)\cdot\varrho\vec v\,(J\vec a^3\cdot\vec v)$, is advective and is neglected below together with the bulk advection.
Contributions from the horizontal derivatives, including the curvature terms on the sphere and the cross terms of non-orthogonal meshes, belong to the horizontal operator $\mathbf{R}_H$.
In contrast, projecting onto $J\vec a^3$ instead of $\vec\nu$ would introduce the additional pressure term $-\tfrac12\,p\,\partial_{\xi^3}\|{J\vec a^3}\|^2$ in the vertical subsystem.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:other_formulations}
As noted in Section~\ref{sec:equations}, the pressure depends only on the potential-temperature density $\varrho\theta$ in the formulation \eqref{eq:EulerPotentialTemperature0}.
Consequently, the density does not appear in the approximate flux Jacobian \eqref{eq:fluxJac_app} at all, and it enters the vertical subsystem only through the pointwise gravity term.
This is not the case for other formulations of the compressible Euler equations: if the thermodynamic equation is written, e.g., for the total energy density, the pressure depends on all prognostic variables, and the corresponding flux Jacobian is considerably denser.
In Section~\ref{sec:nestedschur}, we exploit this sparsity to eliminate density and potential-temperature density analytically through local Schur complements.

The methods presented here can nonetheless be applied to dynamical cores based on other formulations.
Since Rosenbrock-W methods admit any approximation of the Jacobian, the vertical subsystems can always be formulated in terms of $(\varrho, \varrho\tilde w, \varrho\theta)$.
To this end, the state at which the Jacobian is assembled is converted to the potential-temperature formulation, and, in every stage, the right-hand side $\mathbf{B}_i$ and the stage vector $\mathbf{K}_i$ are mapped between both sets of variables with the linearization of this change of variables.
These transformations act node by node and can be fused into the projections \eqref{eq:restriction_lift}, so their cost is small compared with the column solves.

Using the potential-temperature formulation in both the explicit and the implicit parts is nonetheless beneficial for three reasons: (i) no transformations are required; (ii) since the approximate Jacobian is derived from the same conservative discretization, the implicit stages conserve $\varrho\theta$ to machine precision, whereas the node-wise transformations generally break the discrete conservation of the thermodynamic variable of the dynamical core in the implicit stages (a proof of Rosenbrock-W's conservation of linear invariants is provided in Appendix~\ref{app:linear_invariants}); and (iii) structure-preserving discretizations are available for this formulation \cite{artiano2025structure}.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:lmars}
The blocks $\underline{\mathbf{\mathcal A}}_{12}$, $\underline{\mathbf{\mathcal A}}_{22}$, and $\underline{\mathbf{\mathcal A}}_{33}$ contain the linearized LMARS dissipation of the numerical fluxes \eqref{eq:vertical_numflux} \cite{chen2013control}, which only acts at the two edge nodes of each element.
In particular, the dissipation of the density flux is proportional to the jump of $\tilde p$, which depends on $\varrho\theta$; this is the origin of the coupling between $\varrho$ and $\varrho\theta$ in $\underline{\mathbf{\mathcal A}}_{12}$, which, for an interior element, is zero everywhere except at its two corner entries $(1,1)$ and $(M,M)$.
At the two physical (bottom and top) boundaries, the reflecting-wall flux has no pressure jump, so the corresponding corner entries of $\underline{\mathbf{\mathcal A}}_{12}$ and $\underline{\mathbf{\mathcal A}}_{22}$ vanish, whereas the corner entry of $\underline{\mathbf{\mathcal A}}_{33}$ remains.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:topboundary}
Both condensation strategies condense the upper edge of the top element (node $M$ of element $N_z$) exactly like every interior interface, even though that node lies on the physical top boundary and therefore never couples to a neighbor above.
It would be possible to leave those DOFs in $\underline{\mathbf{\mathcal A}}$ instead, at the cost of a larger, non-uniform block for the last element alone.
We use the uniform version for both strategies, since it keeps every elemental block the same size and shape, which simplifies the implementation at the cost of a fixed, small number of extra condensed DOFs per column ($3$ for one-sided, $2$ for two-sided) that does not grow with $N_z$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:rosenbrock_invariants}
Let $\mathbf{W}$ define a linear invariant of $\dot{\mathbf{U}} = \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U})$, and let the approximate Jacobian satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobian_invariant}
	\mathbf{W}^T \underline{\mathbf{J}} = \underline{\mathbf{0}} .
\end{equation}
Then the Rosenbrock-W method \eqref{eq:rosenbrockW}--\eqref{eq:Rosenbrock_update} preserves the linear invariant, i.e., $\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{U}^{n+1} = \mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{U}^n$, provided that the stage equations are solved exactly.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We show by induction over the stages that $\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{K}_i = 0$ for all $i$.
Multiplying the stage equation \eqref{eq:rosenbrockW} from the left by $\mathbf{W}^T$ and using \eqref{eq:jacobian_invariant} gives
\begin{equation*}
	\frac{1}{\hat\gamma\Delta t}\,\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{K}_i
	= \mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{R}\Big(\mathbf{U}^n + \textstyle\sum_{j<i} \tilde{a}_{ij}\mathbf{K}_j\Big)
	+ \frac{1}{\Delta t}\sum_{j<i} c_{ij}\,\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{K}_j.
\end{equation*}
The first term on the right-hand side vanishes by \eqref{eq:linear_invariant}, irrespective of the argument of $\mathbf{R}$.
For $i = 1$, the sum is empty, so $\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{K}_1 = 0$.
If $\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{K}_j = 0$ for all $j < i$, the sum vanishes, and hence $\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{K}_i = 0$.
Finally, \eqref{eq:Rosenbrock_update} yields $\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{U}^{n+1} = \mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{U}^n + \sum_i m_i\,\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{K}_i = \mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{U}^n$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:vertical_invariants}
Consider one vertical column with reflecting walls at the bottom and top, at which the numerical fluxes of $\varrho$ and $\varrho\theta$ vanish.
Let $\mathbf{W}_\varrho$ be the vector with entries $J^{(e)}_k\omega_k$ at the density degrees of freedom of the column, ordered as in \eqref{eq:orderingJac}, and zeros elsewhere, and define $\mathbf{W}_{\varrho\theta}$ analogously for the potential-temperature density.
Then
\begin{equation}
	\mathbf{W}_\varrho^T\underline{\mathbf{J}}^V = \underline{\mathbf{0}}, \qquad \mathbf{W}_{\varrho\theta}^T\underline{\mathbf{J}}^V = \underline{\mathbf{0}} .
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $\underline{\mathbf{J}}^V = \partial\mathbf{R}^V/\partial\mathbf{U}^V$ with the frozen coefficients of Section~\ref{sec:jacobian_hevi}, it suffices to show that $\mathbf{W}_\varrho^T\mathbf{R}^V(\mathbf{U}^V) = 0$ and $\mathbf{W}_{\varrho\theta}^T\mathbf{R}^V(\mathbf{U}^V) = 0$ for all $\mathbf{U}^V$.
We consider the density; the argument for the potential-temperature density is identical.
Denote by $\tilde{\mathbf{f}}^{\varrho}_m = n^{(e)}_m(\varrho\tilde w)^{(e)}_m$ the nodal flux of the density equation.
By \eqref{eq:vertical_residual},
\begin{equation*}
	\mathbf{W}_\varrho^T\mathbf{R}^V
	= \sum_{e}\sum_{k=0}^{N} J^{(e)}_k\omega_k\, R^{\varrho,(e)}_k
	= -\sum_{e}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{N}\sum_{m=0}^{N} \hat D_{km}\,\tilde{\mathbf{f}}^{\varrho}_m + \hat{{f}} ^{\varrho}_{e+\frac12} - \hat{{f}} ^{\varrho}_{e-\frac12}\right].
\end{equation*}
The volume term vanishes, because the columns of $\hat{\underline{\mathbf{D}}} = \underline{\mathbf{Q}} - \underline{\mathbf{B}}$ sum to zero: since the differentiation matrix is exact for constants, $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{1} = \underline{\mathbf{M}}\underline{\mathbf{D}}\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}$, and the SBP property $\underline{\mathbf{Q}} + \underline{\mathbf{Q}}^T = \underline{\mathbf{B}}$ yields $\mathbf{1}^T\underline{\mathbf{Q}} = (\underline{\mathbf{Q}}^T\mathbf{1})^T = (\underline{\mathbf{B}}\mathbf{1} - \underline{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{1})^T = \mathbf{1}^T\underline{\mathbf{B}}$, i.e., $\mathbf{1}^T\hat{\underline{\mathbf{D}}} = \mathbf{0}^T$.
The surface terms telescope, since the surface numerical flux and the contravariant metric terms at each interior interface are single-valued, leaving only the fluxes at the bottom and top walls, which vanish by assumption.
Hence $\mathbf{W}_\varrho^T\mathbf{R}^V = 0$ for all $\mathbf{U}^V$, and differentiation yields $\mathbf{W}_\varrho^T\underline{\mathbf{J}}^V = \underline{\mathbf{0}}$.
For the potential-temperature density, the same argument applies with the nodal flux $n^{(e)}_m\theta^{(e)}_{f,m}(\varrho\tilde w)^{(e)}_m$; the numerical flux $\hat{{f}} ^{\varrho\theta}$ is single-valued because its dissipative term uses the interface average $\left\{\hspace*{-3pt}\left\{\theta\right\}\hspace*{-3pt}\right\}$ rather than the element-local values of $\theta_f$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
Note that the conservation of linear invariants here is guaranteed by the particular choice of the discrete metric terms of the Jacobian approximation, which is derived from the DGSEM semidiscretization.
    In general, a consistent and locally conservative discretization of the Jacobian does not guarantee the conservation of linear invariants.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:hevi_invariants}
Assume that the split-form DGSEM discretization conserves the total mass and the total potential-temperature density, i.e., that the corresponding vectors $\mathbf{W}$ define linear invariants of $\dot{\mathbf{U}} = \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U})$.
Then the HEVI Rosenbrock-W scheme with the approximate Jacobian $\underline{\mathbf{J}} = \underline{\mathbf{L}}\,\underline{\mathbf{J}}^V\underline{\mathbf{P}}$ of Section~\ref{sec:jacobian_hevi} preserves both quantities, provided that the column systems \eqref{eq:vertical_stage} are solved exactly.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\mathbf{W}$ define the total mass, and let $\mathbf{W}_V$ collect the corresponding weights of all columns in the ordering of $\underline{\mathbf{J}}^V$, i.e., the vectors $\mathbf{W}_\varrho$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:vertical_invariants}, scaled by the horizontal quadrature weights of each column.
The lift $\underline{\mathbf{L}}$ of \eqref{eq:restriction_lift} maps the density component of the vertical subsystem to the density component of the full state without modification, and $\mathbf{W}$ vanishes at all momentum degrees of freedom.
Hence $\mathbf{W}^T\underline{\mathbf{L}} = \mathbf{W}_V^T$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:vertical_invariants}, applied column by column, yields
\begin{equation*}
	\mathbf{W}^T\underline{\mathbf{J}} = \mathbf{W}^T\underline{\mathbf{L}}\,\underline{\mathbf{J}}^V\underline{\mathbf{P}} = \mathbf{W}_V^T\underline{\mathbf{J}}^V\underline{\mathbf{P}} = \underline{\mathbf{0}} .
\end{equation*}
Lemma~\ref{lem:rosenbrock_invariants} then gives the result.
The same argument applies to the total potential-temperature density.
