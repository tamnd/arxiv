\label{thm:intro-numerical}
The Heinloth weight is constant on each $P_\alpha$, with value
$\delta_\alpha$, and for every rank $n\geq1$ one has
\begin{align}\label{eq:intro-weak-closure}
 \overline{S_\alpha^\star}\subseteq S_\alpha^\star\cup
 \bigcup_{\delta_\beta>\delta_\alpha}S_\beta^\star,
 \qquad\star\in\{\mathrm{Hod},\mathrm{Dol},\mathrm{dR}\}.
\end{align}
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:intro-fullchains}
For $n\geq2$, the union of stable full chain strata is closed in the
full semistable moduli space and consists of stable points. For their
labels $s,t$ and $\star\in\{\mathrm{Hod},\mathrm{Dol},\mathrm{dR}\}$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:intro-fullchains}
 \overline{S_s^\star}\subseteq\bigcup_{t\geq s}S_t^\star,
 \qquad
 \dim S_s^\star-\dim S_t^\star
 =(t_1-s_1)+(t_{n-1}-s_{n-1}),
\end{align}
where $t\geq s$ means coordinatewise comparison.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:intro-counterexamples}
For every $g\geq2$ and $n\geq4$, there are distinct connected
components $P_s,P_t$ of the full fixed locus, both consisting
entirely of stable full chains, such that
\begin{align}\label{eq:intro-counterexamples}
 \varnothing\ne\overline{S_s^\star}\cap S_t^\star
 \subsetneq S_t^\star,
 \qquad\star\in\{\mathrm{Hod},\mathrm{Dol},\mathrm{dR}\}.
\end{align}
Consequently, Simpson's nestedness conjecture fails for the Dolbeault
and de~Rham moduli spaces in every rank $n\geq4$, and the same closure
condition fails in the Hodge moduli space.

<!-- defn-1: body_md -->
\label{def:heinloth-weight}
For a system of Hodge bundles $E=\bigoplus_pE^p$ of degree zero,
with $\theta:E^p\to E^{p-1}\otimes K_X$, its \emph{Heinloth weight} is
$\delta(E)=\sum_p p\deg E^p$, using the normalization of
\cite[Examples~1.4--1.5]{Hei16} given in
\eqref{eq:heinloth-weight} below.

<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:determinant-weight}
The line $\mathscr L$ descends equivariantly to $Y$.  At a semistable
system of Hodge bundles $E=\bigoplus_pE^p$, with
$\theta:E^p\to E^{p-1}\otimes K_X$, the weight of the descended fiber is
$\delta(E)=\sum_p p\deg E^p$.  In particular, this integer depends only
on the connected component of the full fixed locus containing the
coarse point of $E$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The closed points of a fiber of the good moduli morphism correspond to
polystable objects, as in Simpson's construction
\cite{Sim94a,Sim94b}.  Suppose such an object is
$\bigoplus_iE_i\otimes W_i$, where the $E_i$ are pairwise nonisomorphic
stable objects.  Each $E_i$ has degree zero, while the automorphism group
of the direct sum is $\prod_i\mathrm{GL}(W_i)$.  The character of this group on
$\mathscr L$ is
$\prod_i\det(W_i)^{\chi(E_i)+h\mathrm{rk}(E_i)}=1$, because Riemann--Roch gives
$\chi(E_i)=\deg E_i-h\mathrm{rk}(E_i)$.
The descent criterion \cite[Theorem~10.3]{Alp13} therefore applies to
the whole semistable stack.  The scaling linearization descends as
well, since scaling commutes with the framing group and full faithfulness
of descent carries both its isomorphism and its cocycle identity to
the coarse space.

For the graded object in the statement, multiplication by $t^p$ on
$E^p$ defines an isomorphism $(E,t\theta)\to(E,\theta)$.
Composing the natural scaling transport with this isomorphism computes
the action on the descended fiber, whose weight is
$\sum_p p\chi(E^p)+h\sum_p p\mathrm{rk}(E^p)
=\sum_p p\deg E^p$.
Since the equivariant line is pulled back from the coarse space,
this calculation also applies to a graded object which is semistable
but not polystable, and neither the representative nor its grading
changes the weight. The character of an equivariant
line is locally constant on the fixed locus, which proves the last
assertion.

<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:maximal-weight}
If $(V,D^\lambda)$ has Simpson type $\alpha$, then
$W(F)\leq\delta_\alpha$ for every Griffiths transverse filtration
$F^\bullet V$, with equality if and only if $F^\bullet V$ is a Simpson filtration.
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Normalize the indices so that $F^0V=V$, which gives
$W(F)=\sum_{p\geq1}\deg F^pV$.
If $F^pV=F^{p+1}V$ is a repeated proper step, then transversality
implies that it is preserved by $D^\lambda$, so its degree
is nonpositive by semistability.
Let $P_V$ be the ordinary Harder--Narasimhan polygon of $V$.
After omitting repeated contributions from the sum for $W(F)$, each
proper rank occurs at most once, so
$W(F)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}P_V(k)$, where we also use
$P_V(k)\geq0$ because $\deg V=0$.
This is a bound for every transverse filtration of the fixed object,
without any restriction on the span of its indices.

If $E=\mathrm{Gr}_F(V,D^\lambda)$ is unstable, its maximal
destabilizing Higgs subbundle $G$ is preserved by the Hodge action,
so $G=\bigoplus_pG^p$ is graded.
Following \cite[Section~3]{Sim10}, define $F'^pV$ as the inverse image
of $G^{p-1}\subseteq F^{p-1}V/F^pV$ under the quotient map
$F^{p-1}V\to F^{p-1}V/F^pV$.
The fact that $G$ is a Higgs subbundle proves transversality of $F'$,
and there is an exact sequence of systems of Hodge bundles
\begin{align}\label{eq:modification-sequence}
 0\longrightarrow E/G\longrightarrow
 \mathrm{Gr}_{F'}(V,D^\lambda)
 \longrightarrow G^{[1]}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad (G^{[1]})^p=G^{p-1}.
\end{align}
Here $G^{[1]}$ denotes the shift of the Hodge grading.
This construction uses the Leibniz rule and transversality, so it is
valid for every $\lambda$, including zero.
Taking the weighted sum of degrees in
\eqref{eq:modification-sequence} gives
$W(F')-W(F)=\deg G>0$.
Thus every modification of an unstable associated graded increases
$W$ by a positive integer, while the preceding bound remains fixed, which proves termination
from every initial filtration.

For a Simpson filtration, the Rees bundle
$\sum_p t^{-p}F^pV$ carries the operator $tD^\lambda$ and
specializes to its semistable graded Higgs bundle.
Its nonzero fibers represent the scaling orbit of the original object,
so the special coarse point is its unique scaling limit
\cite{Sim10}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:determinant-weight} then identifies the value of $W$
attained by a Simpson filtration with $\delta_\alpha$, also when that limit is strictly
semistable, which proves the inequality and the equality criterion
because every preceding modification increases $W$ strictly.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:numerical-range}
For a semistable object of rank $n$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:numerical-range}
 0\leq\delta\leq \frac{h\,n(n^2-1)}6.
\end{align}
The lower equality holds precisely when the underlying vector bundle
is semistable.  The upper equality holds precisely when the object
admits an oper filtration.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\mu_1\geq\cdots\geq\mu_n$ be the ordinary HN slopes of the
underlying bundle, repeated according to rank, so that their sum is
zero.  At a break between two different slopes, a gap greater than
$2h$ would give an HN prefix $F$ with
$\mathrm{Hom}(F,(V/F)\otimes K_X)=0$.
Its second fundamental form would vanish, so $F$ would be invariant.
Every proper HN prefix at such a break has positive degree, which
contradicts semistability of the object.
Thus $\mu_i-\mu_{i+1}\leq2h$ for all $i$, and
\[
 P_V(k)=\frac1n\sum_{i\leq k<j}(\mu_i-\mu_j)
 \leq\frac{2h}{n}\sum_{i\leq k<j}(j-i)
 =h\,k(n-k).
\]
The bound for $W$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:maximal-weight}, applied to a
Simpson filtration, proves the upper inequality after summation.
The lower bound follows from the trivial filtration, and $\delta=0$
if $V$ is semistable because $P_V=0$. Conversely, if $V$ is unstable,
a positive degree HN subbundle $F\subset V$ gives the Griffiths
transverse filtration $V\supset F\supset0$ of weight $\deg F>0$,
so $\delta>0$.

If the upper equality holds, then
$P_V(k)=h\,k(n-k)$ for every $1\leq k<n$.
The strict concavity of these values forces every HN factor to have
rank one, with degrees $(n-1)h,(n-3)h,\ldots,-(n-1)h$.
Let $F_k$ denote the resulting full HN flag.
For $k<n-1$, the slope inequalities give
$\mathrm{Hom}(F_k,(V/F_{k+1})\otimes K_X)=0$, so this flag is
Griffiths transverse.
Every adjacent graded map is nonzero, since otherwise the positive
degree prefix $F_k$ would be invariant.
Its source and target are line bundles of the same degree, so the map
is an isomorphism.
Conversely, an oper filtration has these graded degrees and hence
$W=h\,n(n^2-1)/6$, which forces equality by maximality and the upper
bound already proved.  For rank one the same assertions hold with
the trivial flag.

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:specialization-deficit}
With the above notation, the change of the Heinloth weight is
\begin{align}\label{eq:specialization-deficit}
 \delta_\beta-\delta_\alpha
 =\sum_p\ell_p+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\deg G_j.
\end{align}
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $R$ be the base ring, with generic point $\eta$ and special point
$s$, and let $\mathscr V$ be the underlying vector bundle of the family
on $X\times\mathrm{Spec}(R)$. A Simpson filtration over the geometric generic point is defined
over a finite extension of $\kappa(\eta)$, since it is a point of a
flag parameter space of finite type.
After replacing $R$ by the corresponding valuation ring, the
filtration is defined over $\eta$, and properness of the relative
flag Quot scheme with its prescribed ranks and degrees then extends
it with every quotient $\mathscr V/F^p\mathscr V$ flat over $R$.
The kernels are flat over $R$, and flatness of the quotients ensures
that their fibers inject into the fibers of $\mathscr V$.
They are consequently torsion free on the smooth curve and hence
locally free.  Flatness and local freeness on the fibers then imply
that the kernels are vector bundles on $X\times\mathrm{Spec}(R)$.

The map induced by the operator from $F^p\mathscr V$ to
$(\mathscr V/F^{p-1}\mathscr V)\otimes K_X$ is linear over the structure
sheaf, because the symbol term belongs to $F^{p-1}\mathscr V$.
It vanishes on the generic fiber and its target is flat over $R$,
so it vanishes everywhere. The extended flag therefore remains
Griffiths transverse, even though its special quotients need not
be vector bundles on $X$.

Let $\widehat F^p_s$ be the saturation of $F^p_s$ in $\mathscr V_s$.
Saturation preserves nesting, and locally, if $fv\in F^p_s$ for a
nonzero function $f$, then $v\in\widehat F^{p-1}_s$, and the identity
$D^\lambda(fv)=fD^\lambda(v)+\lambda v\otimes df$ shows that
$fD^\lambda(v)$ belongs to $\widehat F^{p-1}_s\otimes K_X$.
The quotient by this saturated step is torsion free, so $f$ can be
cancelled, which proves transversality of $\widehat F_s$.
Flatness preserves the degree of every kernel, and therefore
$W(\widehat F_s)=\delta_\alpha+\sum_p\ell_p$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:maximal-weight} ensures that the subsequent modification
process terminates, and
\eqref{eq:modification-sequence} shows that its successive increases
are exactly $\deg G_1,\ldots,\deg G_N$.
The process ends at a Simpson filtration of weight $\delta_\beta$,
which proves
\eqref{eq:specialization-deficit}.

<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:weak-closure}
For every rank $n\geq1$ and every connected component $P_\alpha$
of the full fixed locus, one has
\begin{align}\label{eq:weak-closure}
 \overline{S_\alpha^\star}
 \subseteq S_\alpha^\star\cup
 \bigcup_{\delta_\beta>\delta_\alpha}S_\beta^\star,
 \qquad
 \star\in\{\mathrm{Hod},\mathrm{Dol},\mathrm{dR}\},
\end{align}
where each closure is taken in the indicated coarse moduli space.
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For an actual family as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:specialization-deficit}, the formula first gives
$\delta_\beta\geq\delta_\alpha$.
If equality holds, then every saturation length is zero and
there is no destabilizing modification, so the special graded Higgs
bundle is already semistable.
The quotients of the extended flag are locally free on both fibers
and hence on the total family, which gives a family of semistable
systems of Hodge bundles.  Its grading identifies it with each of its Higgs
scalings, so its coarse image lies in the fixed locus.
Since the spectrum of the valuation ring is connected, its generic
and special images belong to the same connected component of the full fixed locus.
Consequently equality forces $\beta=\alpha$.

We next explain why actual families suffice for every coarse boundary
point.  Use a framed GIT parameter scheme $Q\to Y$ with its universal
family \cite{Sim94a,Sim94b}, and let $U$ be the inverse image of
$S_\alpha^{\mathrm{Hod}}$.
The closure $Z=\overline U$ is invariant and closed.
Its image under the good quotient is closed and contains
$S_\alpha^{\mathrm{Hod}}$, while
$Z$ is contained in the inverse image of
$\overline{S_\alpha^{\mathrm{Hod}}}$.
Hence its image is exactly that closure.
For a closed point $y$ of the latter, $Z$ contains the closed orbit
over $y$, because every invariant closed subset contains the closed
orbit in each quotient fiber which it meets.
Choose a point of that orbit.  If $y$ is not already in the source
stratum, curve selection in $Z$ gives a curve through the chosen point
whose generic point belongs to $U$.
Normalization and localization give a discrete valuation ring, and
the universal family on $Q$ gives an actual semistable family with
special coarse point $y$.
The preceding argument now proves the Hodge inclusion.

For either specified fiber, the closure of its source piece is
contained in the intersection of that fiber with the ambient Hodge
closure.  Applying the Hodge inclusion and the common indexing by fixed components
 proves the Dolbeault and de Rham assertions.

<!-- cor-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:degree-filtration}
For $a\in\mathbb Z$, the subsets $Z_a^\star=\{\delta\geq a\}$ form a finite closed
filtration of each moduli space.  In each level
$Z_a^\star\setminus Z_{a+1}^\star$, the strata with
$\delta_\alpha=a$ form a finite decomposition into subsets that are
both open and closed.
<!-- end cor-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-closure}, each $Z_a^\star$ is a finite
union of strata whose closures remain in that union, so it is closed.
In $Z_a^\star\setminus Z_{a+1}^\star$, each stratum is closed by
the same theorem, and its complement is a finite union of closed
strata, which proves openness.

<!-- prop-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:fullchain-labels}
The stable full chain components of the full fixed locus are indexed
by
\begin{align}\label{eq:fullchain-label-set}
 \mathscr P_{n,h}
 =\{s\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^{n-1}:Cs\leq2h\mathbf1\}.
\end{align}
For $s\in\mathscr P_{n,h}$, the corresponding component is
\begin{align}\label{eq:fullchain-component}
 P_s\cong\mathrm{Pic}^{s_1}(X)
       \times\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathrm{Sym}^{d_i(s)}(X).
\end{align}
These components are smooth, projective and irreducible, and their
labels satisfy $1\leq s_k\leq hk(n-k)$.
<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
All arrows of a stable full chain are nonzero, since a zero arrow
would split it into two nonzero Higgs subbundles, which cannot both
have negative degree. Over the function field, the Higgs operator
is therefore one regular nilpotent Jordan block, whose invariant
subspaces are exactly its suffixes. Every invariant subsheaf of a
given rank lies in the corresponding suffix bundle and has degree
no greater than that bundle, so stability is equivalent to negativity
of every proper suffix degree. Since the total degree is zero, this
is equivalent to $s_k>0$ for $1\leq k<n$. The arrow divisors
are effective, which gives the remaining inequalities in
\eqref{eq:fullchain-label-set}.

Conversely, choose $s$ satisfying these inequalities, a line bundle
$L_1\in\mathrm{Pic}^{s_1}(X)$ and effective divisors $D_i$ of
degrees $d_i(s)$. Define the subsequent lines by
$L_{i+1}=L_i\otimes K_X^{-1}\otimes\mathcal O_X(D_i)$ and take the arrows induced by
the canonical sections of these divisors. Formula
\eqref{eq:fullchain-degrees} recovers the prescribed prefix degrees and
total degree, so the resulting chain is stable. The top line and
arrow divisors determine it up to isomorphism, because successive
scalar changes of the graded lines identify any two choices of
nonzero arrows with the same divisors. This gives the
parametrization \eqref{eq:fullchain-component}, including in
families, as in \cite{HH22}.

We verify that these are components of the full fixed locus.
At a stable fixed point, the grading is unique up to a common
translation. After normalizing this translation, an equivariant
universal family exists \'etale locally along the stable fixed
locus, and its weight subbundles have locally constant ranks and
degrees. Thus the displayed parameter space has open image in the
stable fixed locus, and hence in the full fixed locus. Its image
is also closed, because the parameter space is projective and the
coarse moduli space is separated. Since the parameter space is
connected and irreducible, its image is a connected component of
the full fixed locus, entirely contained in the stable locus.

Finally, since the entries of $C^{-1}$ are nonnegative and the
vector $u_k=hk(n-k)$ satisfies $Cu=2h\mathbf1$, applying $C^{-1}$
to the inequality in \eqref{eq:fullchain-label-set} gives $s_k\leq u_k$,
which proves both the bound and the finiteness of the label set.

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:fullchain-boundary}
For $\star\in\{\mathrm{Hod},\mathrm{Dol},\mathrm{dR}\}$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:fullchain-closure}
 \overline{S_s^\star}\subseteq
 \bigcup_{t\in\mathscr P_{n,h},\ t\geq s}S_t^\star.
\end{align}
At every boundary point of type $t$, there is such a family with
\begin{align}\label{eq:fullchain-defect}
 t-s=\ell\geq0,\qquad
 \delta_t-\delta_s=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\ell_k,\qquad
 d(s)-d(t)=C\ell.
\end{align}
The union $Y_{\mathrm{fc}}^\star=\bigcup_sS_s^\star$ is closed in
the full moduli space and consists entirely of stable points.
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let $y\in\overline{S_s^\star}$. The framed GIT argument in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-closure} gives an actual trait with
generic point in $S_s^\star$ and special coarse point $y$, using
the corresponding parameter fiber in the Dolbeault and de~Rham cases.
Let $V_s$ be its underlying special bundle. After a finite base
extension, the Quot and saturation construction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:specialization-deficit} gives a saturated
transverse flag $E_1\subset\cdots\subset E_n=V_s$ with
$\deg E_k=s_k+\ell_k>0$ for $1\leq k\leq n-1$.

If an adjacent graded arrow were zero, the corresponding prefix
$E_k$ would be preserved by $D^\lambda$, which contradicts
semistability because $\deg E_k>0$. Thus every graded arrow is
nonzero, and the positivity of the prefix degrees shows that the graded
full chain is stable. The special object itself is then stable,
because any proper nonzero invariant subbundle of nonnegative degree would,
through its intersection filtration, give an invariant graded
subsheaf of the same degree, contradicting stability of the graded
chain.

The saturated flag is consequently a Simpson filtration, whose Rees
family identifies the graded chain with the scaling limit of the
special object. The prefix degrees of this chain therefore give the target label
$t_k=s_k+\ell_k$, and summing them and using
\eqref{eq:fullchain-degrees} gives \eqref{eq:fullchain-defect}.
This proves \eqref{eq:fullchain-closure} without any restriction
to the stable locus in the ambient space.

Every $S_s^\star$ consists of stable points by

many labels, and the closure of each of their strata remains in
their union, so that union is closed.

\label{lem:stable-component-attractor}
Let $P_\alpha$ be an irreducible stable fixed component. Its
strata in all three spaces are irreducible and consist of stable
points, with
\begin{align}\label{eq:stable-attractor-dimension}
 \left\{\begin{aligned}
  &\dim S_\alpha^{\mathrm{Dol}}=\dim S_\alpha^{\mathrm{dR}}
   =n^2h+1+\dim P_\alpha,\\
  &\dim S_\alpha^{\mathrm{Hod}}
   =\dim S_\alpha^{\mathrm{Dol}}+1.
 \end{aligned}\right.
\end{align}
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The inverse image of the stable open under an extended scaling
orbit contains zero, and hence contains a nonzero point, so
invariance of stability shows that the entire attractor is stable.
At a stable fixed Higgs bundle the infinitesimal automorphisms of trace zero
vanish, and Serre duality then gives the vanishing of the
corresponding obstructions. The scalar factor is the smooth Hodge
space of rank one, so the Hodge moduli space is smooth over the
parameter near the stable fixed locus. The smooth locus is open
and invariant under scaling, which extends this conclusion to the
attractor. Bia{\l}ynicki-Birula theory therefore makes the
Hodge and Dolbeault limit maps locally trivial fibrations with
affine space fibers \cite{BB73,BB76,JS19}, which proves their
irreducibility.

In the Dolbeault tangent space, the symplectic form has scaling
weight one, so its integral weights are paired as $w$ and $1-w$.
The positive weight dimension is consequently $n^2h+1$, which
gives the Dolbeault formula, as in \cite[Proposition~2.10]{HH22}. At every point of $P_\alpha$, the
polarized complex variation of Hodge structure supplies a Rees
curve with parameter $\lambda=t$ \cite[Section~5.2]{Sim10}.
Thus $d\lambda$ is nonzero there and gives one additional tangent
direction of positive weight in the Hodge space, which proves
its dimension formula. The same curve shows that the nonzero
parameter part of the Hodge stratum is nonempty, and this open
subset is isomorphic by scaling to
$\mathbb G_m\times S_\alpha^{\mathrm{dR}}$. Its irreducibility
and dimension give the remaining assertion.

\label{rem:fullchain-polygon}
The following example compares the maximum of $W$ with the
coordinatewise order of filtration polygons. For $g=2$ and $n=5$, choose a full chain with line
degrees $(2,1,-1,0,-2)$ and arrow degrees $(1,0,3,0)$.
Its prefix degrees are $(2,3,2,2)$, so it is stable, and its
Simpson filtration with weights $(4,3,2,1,0)$ gives $W=9$.
Removing the step of rank three gives another transverse
filtration, with weights $(3,2,1,1,0)$ and $W=7$.
Its associated graded Higgs bundle is the direct sum of the stable
chain of slope $2/3$ formed by the first three lines and the stable
chain of slope $-1$ formed by the last two lines.
The former is therefore the maximal destabilizing subobject,
and one Simpson modification raises its three weights by one
and recovers the Simpson filtration.

The polygonal line of the shorter filtration joins $(2,3)$ to
$(4,2)$, and hence has height $5/2$ at rank three, whereas the
Simpson flag has height $2$ there. Thus $W$ increases to its
maximum while that height decreases. These are filtration
polygons, which need not be concave, and the calculation
explains why the maximum property of $W$ cannot be replaced
by the usual HN polygon argument.

\label{lem:hecke-curve}
There is a morphism equivariant under $\mathbb G_m$
$f:\mathbb P^1\to\mathcal M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,n)^s$ such that
$f(0)=[(E,\theta)]$, $f(\infty)=[(E_\infty,\theta_\infty)]$ and
$f(\mathbb A^1)\subset S_s^{\mathrm{Dol}}$, where
$c\in\mathbb G_m$ acts on the affine coordinate $\tau$ by
$\tau\mapsto c\tau$.
<!-- end lem-4 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Consider the subsheaf
$E'=L_1\oplus L_2\oplus\bigoplus_{i=3}^n(L_i\otimes\mathcal O_X(-a))$ of $E$.
It is preserved by $\theta$, since the second arrow vanishes to
order two at $a$, and we denote the induced Higgs field by
$\theta'$. Choose a local parameter $z$ at $a$ and local
generators $l_i$ of $L_i$, so that a local frame of $E'$ is given
by $v_1=l_1$, $v_2=l_2$ and $v_i=zl_i$ for $i\geq3$. The
second arrow of $\theta'$ vanishes at $a$, while the span of
$v_4,\ldots,v_n$ is preserved by its value at $a$. Consequently,
the subspaces
\[
 V_\tau=\langle v_3+\tau v_2,v_4,\ldots,v_n\rangle
 \subset E'_a,
 \qquad
 V_\infty=\langle v_2,v_4,\ldots,v_n\rangle
\]
are invariant under $\theta'_a$ and form a subbundle $\mathcal V$
of $E'_a\otimes\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}$ of rank $n-2$.

Let $p_X:X\times\mathbb P^1\to X$ be the projection, let
$i:\{a\}\times\mathbb P^1\hookrightarrow X\times\mathbb P^1$
be the inclusion, and let
$\mathcal Q=(E'_a\otimes\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1})/\mathcal V$.
Then
\[
 \mathcal E=
 \ker\bigl(p_X^*E'\longrightarrow i_*\mathcal Q\bigr)
 \otimes p_X^*\mathcal O_X(a)
\]
is a vector bundle on $X\times\mathbb P^1$. Indeed, on the
finite parameter chart the kernel has local frame
$zv_1,zv_2,v_3+\tau v_2,v_4,\ldots,v_n$, and on the chart with
coordinate $\sigma=\tau^{-1}$ it has local frame
$zv_1,zv_3,v_2+\sigma v_3,v_4,\ldots,v_n$. The invariance of
$\mathcal V$ gives an induced Higgs field on this kernel and hence
on $\mathcal E$. Each fiber has degree zero, because
$\deg E'=-(n-2)$, the quotient has length two and tensoring by
$\mathcal O_X(a)$ increases the degree by $n$.

At $\tau=0$, tensoring the first local frame by $z^{-1}$ recovers
the original lines $L_1,\ldots,L_n$ and their arrows. At
$\sigma=0$, the second local frame gives $L_1,L_2\otimes\mathcal O_X(a),L_3\otimes\mathcal O_X(-a)$
and $L_4,\ldots,L_n$, which multiplies the first and third
arrows by $z$ and divides the second arrow by $z^2$. These are
precisely the two asserted endpoints.

For $c\in\mathbb G_m$, multiplication by $c^{n-i}$ on the
$i$th summand of $E'$ identifies $(E',c\theta')$ with
$(E',\theta')$ and sends $V_\tau$ to $V_{c\tau}$. It therefore
identifies the scaled Higgs bundle at $\tau$ with the Higgs bundle
at $c\tau$, which proves equivariance of the family. Both endpoints
are stable, and openness of stability gives a stable fiber with
$\tau\ne0,\infty$. Equivariance then gives stability at every
such parameter, since these parameters form a single
orbit under $\mathbb G_m$. Thus the family defines the asserted
morphism to the stable coarse moduli space. Finally, for each
finite $\tau$, its scaling limit is the point at zero, which
shows that $f(\mathbb A^1)\subset S_s^{\mathrm{Dol}}$.

\label{lem:derham-smoothing}
Let $(V,D)$ be a flat bundle of rank $n\geq4$ with a Simpson full flag whose
associated graded is stable. Suppose that its first three arrow
degrees are $(1,0,d)$, where $d\geq1$, and that the first and third
arrows have a common simple zero. If the full chain type obtained
by replacing these degrees by $(0,2,d-1)$ is stable, then
$(V,D)$ is the special fiber of a family of flat bundles over
$\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb C[[t]]$ whose generic Simpson type is
the modified one.
<!-- end lem-5 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Let the given flag be
$0=E_0\subset E_1\subset\cdots\subset E_n=V$, with
$L_i=E_i/E_{i-1}$, and denote the common zero by $a$.
The stability of its graded Higgs bundle implies that $(V,D)$
is stable: the intersection filtration of an invariant subbundle
would otherwise give a graded invariant subsheaf of nonnegative
degree. We fix the determinant flat bundle and a compatible
framing at a point distinct from $a$.

Retain the saturated partial flag
$L_1\subset E_3\subset\cdots\subset V$, with its ranks and degrees,
and impose
$D(L_1)\subset E_3\otimes K_X$ and
$D(E_i)\subset E_{i+1}\otimes K_X$ for $3\leq i<n$.
Let $E_{23}=E_3/L_1$. The induced second fundamental maps give
$\eta:L_1\otimes K_X^{-1}\to E_{23}$ and
$\rho:E_{23}\to L_4\otimes K_X$, which are $\mathcal O_X$-linear because the symbols of the
connection vanish in the respective quotients.
Exterior multiplication by $\eta$ gives the map
$\eta^\wedge:E_{23}\to
\det(E_{23})\otimes L_1^{-1}\otimes K_X$.
In a frame in which $\eta=(x,y)^{\mathsf T}$, its row is $(-y,x)$.
For $L_\sigma=L_1\otimes L_4\otimes\det(E_{23})^{-1}$,
we consider partial flags together with a section
$\sigma\in H^0(X,L_\sigma)$ satisfying
\begin{align}\label{eq:derham-factorization}
 \rho=\sigma\eta^\wedge.
\end{align}
In particular, $\rho\eta=0$.

The specified point belongs to this problem. Indeed, the first
arrow identifies $L_1\otimes K_X^{-1}$ with
$L_2\otimes\mathcal O_X(-a)$, and $\eta$ factors through the
inclusion
$\phi:L_2\otimes\mathcal O_X(-a)\to L_2$.
The third arrow
$\gamma:L_3\to L_4\otimes K_X$ vanishes at $a$, so division by
this Cartier divisor gives a unique global section $\sigma$
with $\gamma=\sigma\phi$, under the natural identifications of line bundles.
If $D_3$ is the divisor of $\gamma$, then the divisor of
$\sigma$ is $D_3-a$, so $\deg L_\sigma=d-1$ and
$\sigma$ is nonzero at $a$.

The fixed determinant fiber of Simpson's stable framed de~Rham
representation scheme \cite[Theorem~6.13]{Sim94b}, relative flag
Quot schemes \cite[Tag~09TQ]{Stacks}, and the relative scheme of
sections of $L_\sigma$ give a parameter scheme $Q_\sigma$
locally of finite type with a universal object. The equation
\eqref{eq:derham-factorization} is algebraic, using principal parts
to describe the operator. We work near the specified point
$q\in Q_\sigma$ and require $\eta$ to be nonzero at a fixed point
away from $a$, so it is injective on every fiber.
We will prove that $Q_\sigma$ is smooth at $q$ and has a tangent
direction which removes the zero of $\eta$.

Also consider the strict problem retaining
the original full flag and imposing the same factorization.
Let $\mathcal P\subset\mathcal P'$ be the trace zero
endomorphisms preserving the full and partial flags. Let
$\mathcal N\otimes K_X\subset\mathcal N'\otimes K_X$ be the
trace zero permissible connection differences for these flags.
Define $\mathcal N_\sigma$ and $\mathcal N'_\sigma$ to be the
sheaves of pairs $(\Psi,\dot\sigma)$ in
$(\mathcal N\otimes K_X)\oplus L_\sigma$ and
$(\mathcal N'\otimes K_X)\oplus L_\sigma$, respectively,
satisfying the linearization of
\eqref{eq:derham-factorization}. They are vector bundles, since
in adapted frames the outgoing entries of $\rho$ are defined
by the other permissible operator entries and $\sigma$.
The partial problem has two outgoing entries and the strict
problem has one, and all these entries are off diagonal, so
fixing the trace does not affect this description.

The deformation complexes are the two term complexes
\begin{align}\label{eq:derham-deformation-complexes}
 \mathcal F_{\mathrm{str}}^\bullet:
   \mathcal P\longrightarrow\mathcal N_\sigma,
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_{\mathrm{par}}^\bullet:
   \mathcal P'\longrightarrow\mathcal N'_\sigma.
\end{align}
Their differential is the infinitesimal change of frame: its
connection component is $[D,-]$, including the derivative,
and its section component is the induced action on
$L_\sigma$. More explicitly, with the convention
$D\mapsto g^{-1}Dg$, an endomorphism $f$ acts on the section
by $-f_\sigma\sigma$, where
$f_\sigma=f_{L_1}+f_{L_4}-\mathrm{tr}(f_{E_{23}})$.
The differential is $\mathbb C$-linear.

The second hypercohomology groups of
\eqref{eq:derham-deformation-complexes} are complete obstruction
spaces. To see this directly, use an affine adapted
trivializing cover and a small Artin extension with kernel $I$.
Locally lift the free operator entries and the section, then
define the outgoing entries by
\eqref{eq:derham-factorization}, and adapted transition matrices
also lift with fixed determinant. The triple overlap errors
and the operator and section compatibility errors form a
total degree two \v{C}ech cocycle of the relevant two term
complex, tensored with $I$. Corrections change it by a
coboundary, and vanishing of its class is equivalent to gluing
the lifts. There is no additional local equation error, since
\eqref{eq:derham-factorization} defines the outgoing entries
as a graph. The same calculation gives the tangent spaces, and
framings add smooth directions. Relative integrability adds
no equation on a curve.

First,
$\mathbb H^2(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{str}}^\bullet)=0$.
For $F^pV=E_{n-p}$, extended by $V$ and zero, consider
\[
 \mathcal C_j^\bullet:
 F^j\mathcal E\!nd_0(V)\xrightarrow{[D,-]}
 F^{j-1}\mathcal E\!nd_0(V)\otimes K_X,
 \qquad 0\leq j\leq n,
\]
where $F^j\mathcal E\!nd_0(V)$ consists of endomorphisms
sending $F^pV$ into $F^{p+j}V$. Then $\mathcal C_0^\bullet$
is the usual strict complex, and $\mathcal C_n^\bullet$ has
only the degree one term $F^{n-1}\mathcal E\!nd_0(V)\otimes K_X$.
For $j\geq1$, the inclusion $\Psi\mapsto(\Psi,0)$ in degree
one identifies these complexes with the positive filtration of
$\mathcal F_{\mathrm{str}}^\bullet$, since $\sigma$ has weight
zero and positive changes do not alter the adjacent arrows.
Their successive quotients are weight summands of the trace zero
deformation complex of the stable graded Higgs bundle, whose
second hypercohomology vanishes by stability and Serre duality.
Thus $\mathbb H^2(\mathcal C_1^\bullet)=0$.
The weight zero quotient can be checked explicitly. Let $\theta_i$
be the original graded arrows, viewed as sections of
$L'_i=\mathcal H\!om(L_i,L_{i+1})\otimes K_X$.
Eliminating the third arrow variation using
$\gamma=\sigma\phi$ gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:derham-graded-factorization}
 \mathcal F_{\mathrm{str}}^\bullet/\mathcal C_1^\bullet:
  \mathcal O_X^{\oplus(n-1)}\longrightarrow
  \left(\bigoplus_{i\ne3}L'_i\right)\oplus L_\sigma.
\end{align}
For a trace zero diagonal tuple $(c_1,\ldots,c_n)$, let
$\chi_i=c_i-c_{i+1}$. The components of the differential are
$\chi_i\theta_i$ for $i\ne3$ and $(\chi_3-\chi_1)\sigma$.
Replacing the third adjacent character by $\chi_3-\chi_1$ is
an invertible row operation, so these characters identify the
trace zero diagonal space with $\mathbb C^{n-1}$.
The corresponding sections $\theta_i$ and $\sigma$ are all nonzero. Multiplication by a nonzero section
of a line bundle surjects on $H^1$, since its cokernel is
torsion. Thus \eqref{eq:derham-graded-factorization} has vanishing second
hypercohomology, which proves the assertion for the strict problem.

The quotient of the two deformation complexes is
\begin{align}\label{eq:derham-factorization-normal}
 \mathcal F_{\mathrm{par}}^\bullet/\mathcal F_{\mathrm{str}}^\bullet:
  \mathcal H\!om(L_2,L_3)
    \xrightarrow{-\phi}
  \mathcal H\!om(L_2\otimes\mathcal O_X(-a),L_3)
\end{align}
in degrees zero and one. Indeed, the extra gauge entry is
$f:L_2\to L_3$. If $w$ and $u$ are the additional incoming
and outgoing operator entries, the linearized factorization
gives $u=-\sigma w$, leaving only $w$ free. The gauge action
on $w$ is $-f\phi$. Its derivative occurs in the already
permitted strict middle arrow entry and hence vanishes in
this quotient. Restriction and projection along the full
flag make this description independent of a splitting.
Since $\phi$ has the single simple zero $a$,
\eqref{eq:derham-factorization-normal} has only the
cohomology sheaf $\mathbb C_a$ in degree one. The long exact
sequence therefore gives
$\mathbb H^2(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{par}}^\bullet)=0$ and a
surjection $\mathbb H^1(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{par}}^\bullet)
\to H^0(X,\mathbb C_a)\cong\mathbb C$, which measures $w(a)$.

Consequently $Q_\sigma$ is smooth at $q$. Choose a tangent vector
with $w(a)\ne0$ and a formal arc in this smooth scheme with
that tangent. A local homomorphism
$\mathcal O_{Q_\sigma,q}\to\mathbb C[[t]]$, obtained from smooth
coordinates, pulls the universal object back to an actual
family over $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb C[[t]]$.
Choose adapted frames on the special fiber with
$\eta=(z,0)^{\mathsf T}$, where $z$ is a local parameter at $a$,
and extend them over the completed disc of the family. Then
$\eta=(x,y)^{\mathsf T}$ with $x=z+t a_1(z,t)$ and
$y=t a_2(z,t)$ for $a_1,a_2\in\mathbb C[[z,t]]$, where
$a_2(0,0)\ne0$ by the choice of $w(a)$, and the factorization
gives $\rho=g(-y,x)$ with $g$ representing $\sigma$.

The inverse image of $\eta(L_1\otimes K_X^{-1})$ in $E_3$
gives a locally free middle step $G$, with
$G/L_1\cong L_1\otimes K_X^{-1}$, and
\eqref{eq:derham-factorization} ensures
$D(G)\subset E_3\otimes K_X$.
Since $a_2$ is a unit, the first Fitting ideal of the cokernel
of $\eta$ on the completed disc is $(x,y)=(z,t)$, so this
cokernel is locally free after inverting $t$.
This also proves generic saturation globally: the nonfree locus
is closed on the proper curve over the trait, its special fiber
is supported only at $a$, and faithful flatness of completion
detects any horizontal component there. Thus $G$ is a saturated
generic middle step. The generic second and third graded lines
have degrees $\deg L_2-1$ and $\deg L_3+1$. The first arrow is the
isomorphism $L_1\to(L_1\otimes K_X^{-1})\otimes K_X$,
and the third arrow is the nonzero section $\sigma$.

For the middle arrow, let the operator on local lifts of
$E_{23}$ have middle block $d+M\otimes dz$, with
$M=(m_{ij})$ having entries in $\mathbb C[[z,t]]$.
Its coefficient is
\begin{align}\label{eq:derham-middle-arrow}
 c=(-y,x)\left(
       \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\binom{x}{y}
       +M\binom{x}{y}\right).
\end{align}
At $z=0$, both $x$ and $y$ are $O(t)$, while
$\partial_zx=1+O(t)$ and $\partial_zy=O(t)$, so
\eqref{eq:derham-middle-arrow} gives
$c(0,t)=-t a_2(0,0)+O(t^2)\ne0$ over $\mathbb C((t))$.
Changing the lifts adds multiples of $\eta$, annihilated by
$(-y,x)$, so the calculation is intrinsic. The remaining
arrows are nonzero by specialization.
The generic arrow degrees are therefore $(0,2,d-1)$ at the
first three positions, with all later degrees unchanged.
By hypothesis this full chain is stable, so its transverse flag
is a Simpson filtration, whose Rees family identifies the graded
chain with the scaling limit.

<!-- thm-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:counterexamples}
For every $g\geq2$ and $n\geq4$, the labels $s,t$ defined above
satisfy
\[
 \varnothing\ne\overline{S_s^\star}\cap S_t^\star
 \subsetneq S_t^\star,
 \qquad \star\in\{\mathrm{Hod},\mathrm{Dol},\mathrm{dR}\}.
\]
Consequently, Simpson's nestedness conjecture fails for the Dolbeault
and de~Rham moduli spaces in every rank $n\geq4$, and the same closure
condition fails in the Hodge moduli space.
<!-- end thm-4 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Choose $a\in X$ and a source full chain of label $s$ whose second
arrow divisor is $2a$. When $n\geq5$, choose the remaining divisor
of degree two away from $a$, which is possible on every $X$ under
consideration. Lemma~\ref{lem:hecke-curve} gives a curve whose
finite parameters belong to $S_s^{\mathrm{Dol}}$ and whose point
at infinity has label $t$, since the change in the first three
arrow degrees is $(1,-2,1)$. Hence
$\overline{S_s^{\mathrm{Dol}}}\cap S_t^{\mathrm{Dol}}$ is
nonempty. The same curve lies in the fiber $\lambda=0$ of the
Hodge moduli space, with its finite parameters in
$S_s^{\mathrm{Hod}}$ and its point at infinity in
$S_t^{\mathrm{Hod}}$, which proves nonemptiness there as well.

For the de~Rham fiber, choose a target full chain whose first
arrow divisor is $a$ and whose third arrow has a simple zero at
$a$, with every remaining zero away from $a$. This is possible
for all the displayed target labels, including $n=5$, when the
third divisor is $a+\Delta$ with $\deg\Delta=2$ and
$a\notin\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta)$. The variation of Hodge
structure and its Rees family used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-component-attractor} give a flat bundle
whose Simpson graded is this chain. Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:derham-smoothing} produces an actual trait in
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,n)$ with this target as special
type and source type $s$ on the generic fiber, since the first
three arrow degrees change from $(1,0,d)$ to $(0,2,d-1)$.
Thus $\overline{S_s^{\mathrm{dR}}}\cap S_t^{\mathrm{dR}}$
is also nonempty.

In all three spaces the two strata are irreducible and have the same
dimension by \eqref{eq:stable-attractor-dimension}. Their
labels differ only at the internal step of rank two, so the dimension
gap in \eqref{eq:fullchain-dimension-gap} is zero and
Corollary~\ref{cor:internal-defect} shows that each intersection is
a proper subset of the target stratum. All closures are taken in
the full coarse spaces, and both labels index connected components
of the full fixed locus, which proves the theorem and hence disproves
Simpson's nestedness conjecture.
