A \emph{positive cuboid configuration} on $D_N$ is a triple of rational points $Q_i=(t_i,\eta_i)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compatibility}
 0<t_i<1\quad(i=1,2,3),
 \qquad t_1t_2+t_1t_3+t_2t_3=1.
\end{equation}
No condition on the signs of the ordinates is imposed.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For every positive cuboid configuration on $D_N$, the subgroup
\[
 H=\langle Q_1,Q_2,Q_3\rangle\subseteq D_N(\mathbb{Q})
\]
has rank at least two. In particular, $\operatorname{rank} E_N(\mathbb{Q})\ge2$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:cuboid-map}
Suppose $a,b,c,d,e,f,g\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cuboid}
 \begin{gathered}
 d^2=a^2+b^2,\qquad e^2=a^2+c^2,\qquad f^2=b^2+c^2,\\
 g^2=a^2+b^2+c^2.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Set $N=\operatorname{sf}(abcg/2)$ and choose $s\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ with $abcg/2=Ns^2$. Then the points
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cuboid-points}
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_1&=\left(\frac{ag}{de+bc},\frac{2s}{de+bc}\right),\\
 Q_2&=\left(\frac{bg}{df+ac},\frac{2s}{df+ac}\right),\\
 Q_3&=\left(\frac{cg}{ef+ab},\frac{2s}{ef+ab}\right)
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
form a positive cuboid configuration on $D_N$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Put $h=de+bc$. The identity
\[
 d^2e^2-b^2c^2=a^2g^2
\]
gives $h^2-a^2g^2=2bch>0$. Hence $0<ag/h<1$, and
\[
 \frac{ag}{h}-\left(\frac{ag}{h}\right)^3
 =\frac{2abcg}{h^2}
 =N\left(\frac{2s}{h}\right)^2.
\]
The other two points are treated cyclically.

To check the last equation in \eqref{eq:compatibility}, consider the triangle with side lengths $af$, $be$, and $cd$. These are the face diagonals of the box with edges $ab$, $ac$, and $bc$, and they satisfy the strict triangle inequalities. For example,
\[
 (be)^2+(cd)^2-(af)^2=2b^2c^2>0,
\]
and cyclically, so this triangle is acute. If $\alpha_1$ is the angle opposite $af$, the cosine rule gives
\[
 \cos\alpha_1=\frac{bc}{de},
 \qquad \sin\alpha_1=\frac{ag}{de}.
\]
Thus $t_1=\tan(\alpha_1/2)$, and cyclically $t_i=\tan(\alpha_i/2)$. Since $\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3=\pi$, the cosine addition formula for the three half-angles yields
\[
 0=\prod_{i=1}^3\cos(\alpha_i/2)
       \bigl(1-t_1t_2-t_1t_3-t_2t_3\bigr).
\]
All three cosine factors are positive. This proves the required identity. The same triangle has area $abcg/2$, which also explains the normalization of $N$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:cuboid-rank}
Every positive rational perfect cuboid determines a squarefree integer
\[
 N=\operatorname{sf}(abcg/2)
\]
for which $\operatorname{rank} E_N(\mathbb{Q})\ge2$. More precisely, the three points in \eqref{eq:cuboid-points} generate a subgroup of rank at least two.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:rank-zero}
Rank zero is excluded before any of the rank-one argument is used. Indeed, every point in a positive configuration has $0<t<1$ and is therefore non-torsion by \eqref{eq:torsion}. A rank-zero congruent-number curve has no such point.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:actual-swap}
Under the replacement $G\mapsto G+T$, the entries satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual-swap}
 \begin{aligned}
 (A_k(G+T),B_k(G+T))
 &=\begin{cases}
 (B_k(G),A_k(G)),&k\text{ odd},\\
 (A_k(G),B_k(G)),&k\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $[2]T=O$,
\[
 [k](G+T)=[k]G+[k]T
 =\begin{cases}[k]G+T,&k\text{ odd},\\ {}[k]G,&k\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Substitution in \eqref{eq:sequences} proves the claim, first for the points and then for their abscissas.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:positivity}
Let $Q_1,Q_2,Q_3$ be a positive cuboid configuration on $D_N$. For every choice of signs $\sigma_i\in\{1,-1\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forbidden-sum}
 \sigma_1Q_1+\sigma_2Q_2+\sigma_3Q_3\ne S.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Negating a point changes only its ordinate. Thus it suffices to rule out $Q_1+Q_2+Q_3=S$ for arbitrary choices of the ordinates.

Suppose that this equality holds. Since $S=-S$, the divisor
\[
 (S)+(Q_1)+(Q_2)+(Q_3)-4(O)
\]
is principal. There is therefore a rational function with this divisor. The pole orders of $t$ and $\eta$ at $O$ are two and three, respectively, and the Riemann--Roch space is
\[
 L(4O)=\langle 1,t,\eta,t^2\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}.
\]
The function vanishes at $S$, so its constant term is zero. Its $\eta$ coefficient cannot vanish: a polynomial in $t$ which vanishes at $S$ has even vanishing order there, whereas the prescribed multiplicity of $S$ is one. Its $t^2$ coefficient cannot vanish either, since its pole has exact order four. After scaling, the function is consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parabola}
 \eta-\alpha t^2-\beta t,
 \qquad \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q},\quad\alpha\ne0.
\end{equation}

Substitution of $\eta=\alpha t^2+\beta t$ in the curve equation, followed by removal of the root $t=0$ corresponding to $S$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic-positivity}
 N\alpha^2t^3+(2N\alpha\beta+1)t^2+N\beta^2t-1=0.
\end{equation}
Its roots, counted with multiplicity, are $t_1,t_2,t_3$. Write $s_1,s_2,s_3$ for their elementary symmetric functions. Vieta's formulas give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vieta}
 s_1=-\frac{2N\alpha\beta+1}{N\alpha^2},
 \qquad s_2=\frac{\beta^2}{\alpha^2},
 \qquad s_3=\frac1{N\alpha^2}.
\end{equation}
By compatibility, $s_2=1$, so $\beta=\alpha$ or $\beta=-\alpha$.

If $\beta=\alpha$, then $s_1=-2-1/(N\alpha^2)<0$, contrary to $t_i>0$. If $\beta=-\alpha$, then $s_1=2-1/(N\alpha^2)$, and
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^3(1-t_i)=1-s_1+s_2-s_3=0.
\]
This contradicts $t_i<1$. The divisor argument includes repeated points through their multiplicities, so no distinctness assumption on the original triple is needed.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:oddB}
A compatible triple selected from these sequences cannot have an odd number of entries from $B$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Write its abscissas as $n_i(z)/d_i(z)$, using the expressions in \eqref{eq:AB-table}. Clearing denominators in \eqref{eq:compatibility} gives $Z(z)=0$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cleared}
 Z=n_1n_2d_3+n_1n_3d_2+n_2n_3d_1-d_1d_2d_3
 \in\mathbb{Z}[z].
\end{equation}

Suppose first that exactly one entry, say the third, is from $B$. For each of the two $A$ entries, one of the numerator and denominator has degree one greater than the other and leading coefficient $1$ or $-1$. Both $B$ factors have the same degree and leading coefficient $1$ or $-1$. In the four summands of \eqref{eq:cleared}, each of the four possible choices of numerator or denominator for the first two positions occurs exactly once. Thus exactly one summand uses both larger-degree $A$ factors. It has strictly largest degree and leading coefficient $1$ or $-1$. Consequently $Z$ has leading coefficient $1$ or $-1$.

If all three entries are from $B$, multiply each numerator-denominator pair by a sign so that its numerator is monic. Let $\delta_i\in\{1,-1\}$ be the denominator leading coefficients. All four summands in \eqref{eq:cleared} have the same degree, with combined leading coefficient
\[
 \delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3-\delta_1\delta_2\delta_3\in\{2,-2\}.
\]
Also $n_i\equiv d_i\pmod2$ as polynomials. Reducing \eqref{eq:cleared} modulo two therefore gives $Z\equiv0$. It follows that $Z/2\in\mathbb{Z}[z]$ has leading coefficient $1$ or $-1$.

In both cases, the rational-root theorem forces the rational number $z$ to be an integer. This contradicts \eqref{eq:base}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:residual}
If a positive cuboid configuration generates a subgroup of rank one, then, after the allowed changes of signs, base point, and labels, it has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_1&=[a]G,\\
 Q_2&=[b]G+T,\\
 Q_3&=[c]G+S+T,
 \end{aligned}
 \qquad a,b\text{ positive odd},\quad c\text{ positive even},
\end{equation}
where $G$ is non-torsion and $0<t(G)<1$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $b_0$ be the number of entries from $B$ and $o$ the number of odd indices. Lemma~\ref{lem:oddB} makes $b_0$ even. If $o$ were odd, changing $G$ to $G+T$ would make $b_0$ odd, again contradicting that lemma. Thus $o$ is also even.

Suppose $b_0=0$. If all three indices are even, the unique term of largest degree in \eqref{eq:cleared} is $-d_1d_2d_3$, with leading coefficient $-1$. If precisely two indices are odd, the unique term of largest degree is the one containing those two odd numerators and the even denominator; it has leading coefficient $1$. The rational-root theorem excludes both possibilities. Therefore $b_0=2$.

If all indices are even, replace the base point by
\[
 G'=[2]G+S.
\]
It is non-torsion and has abscissa in $(0,1)$. Its sequences satisfy the point identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:halving-indices}
 \mathcal A_k(G')=\mathcal A_{2k}(G),
 \qquad \mathcal B_k(G')=\mathcal B_{2k}(G).
\end{equation}
Indeed, $[k]G'=[2k]G+[k]S$, and $k+\epsilon(k)$ is odd. Thus all three indices can be divided by two while preserving their sequence labels. Repeating this operation finitely many times yields at least one odd index. Reapplying the preceding exclusions then shows that there are exactly two odd indices and two $B$ entries.

If both odd indices were $B$ entries, the replacement $G\mapsto G+T$ would produce three $A$ entries, which have already been excluded. The odd entries must therefore be one $A$ and one $B$, while the even entry is $B$. This is \eqref{eq:residual}.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:Ingram}
Let $N>0$ be squarefree and let $G\in E_N(\mathbb{Q})$ be a non-torsion point with $X(G)<0$. For every $m\ge3$, the denominator $D_m$ has a primitive prime divisor $p$ at which $E_N$ has good reduction. In particular, $p\nmid2N$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
