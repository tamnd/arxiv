\@gobble{thm:singer}
Let $q$ be a prime power and let $\alpha$ be a primitive element of
$\mathbb{F}_{q^{d+1}}$. There exists a labelling of points and hyperplanes of
$\operatorname{PG}(d,q)$ for which the point-hyperplane incidence matrix is

\@gobble{thm:quadric-int}
Let $Q\subset\operatorname{PG}(d,q)$ be a non-degenerate quadric with

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\@gobble{thm:bdh}
Given $H\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(n-k)\times 2n}$ with symplectic deficit
$S=H_{z}H_{x}^{\top}-H_{x}H_{z}^{\top}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(n-k)\times(n-k)}$,

<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\@gobble{rem:rank-S-even}
The factor of $\tfrac{1}{2}$ in $c=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(S)$ is
well defined because $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(S)$ is always even. Recall that
$S$ is \emph{skew-symmetric} by construction, i.e.\
$S^{\top}=-S$. A standard result in linear algebra states that
over any field $F$ of characteristic $\ne 2$, every skew-symmetric
matrix admits a symplectic block-diagonalisation
$S=P^{\top}\,J_{r}\,P$, where $P$ is invertible and $J_{r}$ is the
direct sum of $r$ copies of the $2\times 2$ block
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & 1\\-1 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
together with zeros; consequently $\operatorname{rank}(S)=2r$ is automatically
even~\cite[Ch.~5]{ArtinGeometricAlgebra1957}. Physically, this
reflects the fact that each ebit contributes a pair of
canonically-conjugate Bell-pair generators to the parity-check
matrix, so the symplectic deficit absorbed by entanglement
necessarily comes in pairs. In characteristic $2$ the result fails;
we work throughout in characteristic $p>2$ (since the relevant
qudit dimensions are $q=p^{t}$ with $p$ odd, or $q=2^{t}$ where
the cancellation lemma forces $S=0$ trivially).
<!-- end remark-1 -->

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\@gobble{lem:cancellation}
Let $q=p^{t}$. With $H=(A\mid M_{Q}A)$ as in
Section~\ref{sec:Hcandidate} and $M_{Q}$ a circulant of equal row
sums, the symplectic deficit \eqref{eq:S-defn} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:S-decomposition}
   S \;=\; (k-\lambda)\,(M_{Q}-M_{Q}^{\top})
        \;+\; \lambda\,(M_{Q}J - JM_{Q}^{\top})
        \pmod p.
\end{equation}
The second term vanishes because we have $M_{Q}J=JM_{Q}^{\top}=\sigma\,J$
where $\sigma=\sum_{i}(\boldsymbol\tau_{Q})_{i}$. The coefficient
$(k-\lambda)=q^{d-1}\equiv 0\pmod p$ whenever $d\ge 2$ and
$p\mid q$. Hence $S\equiv 0\pmod p$, so the EAQECC parameter obeys
$c(d,q)=0$ over $\mathbb{F}_{q}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\@gobble{thm:main-existence}
For every prime power $q$ and integer $d\ge 2$, the construction of
Section~\ref{sec:construction} yields a valid $\mathbb{F}_{q}$-ary stabilizer
code with parameters
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:main-params}
   \mathcal Q(q,d) \;=\; [[\,\theta_{d}(q),\;k(q,d),\;d_{\min}(q,d)\,]]_{q},
\end{equation}
where the logical dimension is given by:
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:k-formula}
   k(q,d) \;=\; \theta_{d}(q) - \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H),\qquad
   H=(A\mid M_{Q}A).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We verify the three claims in turn.

\emph{(i) Symplectic isotropy.}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:cancellation}, the parity-check matrix
$H=(A\mid M_{Q}A)$ satisfies $H_{z}H_{x}^{\top}-H_{x}H_{z}^{\top}\equiv
0\pmod p$, so $\mathcal S=\mathrm{rowspan}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H)$ is symplectic
self-orthogonal. Consequently, the stabilizer group obtained by
exponentiating $\mathcal S$ in the Pauli representation is
\emph{Abelian} (Section~\ref{sec:prelim}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:symp-iso}),
which is precisely the prerequisite for $\mathcal S$ to define a
valid stabilizer code~\cite{Gottesman1997}.

\emph{(ii) Stabilizer dimension.} Since the rows of $H$ are linearly
independent over $\mathbb{F}_{q}$ only up to $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H)$, we have
$\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal S=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H)$.

\emph{(iii) Logical dimension.}
The centralizer
$\mathcal C(\mathcal S)\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}$ is the symplectic
complement of $\mathcal S$. Because the symplectic form
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{sym}}$ is non-degenerate on
$\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}$, the symplectic-complement formula
gives $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal C=2n-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal S=
2n-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H)$. Logical operators are the equivalence classes
in the symplectic quotient $\mathcal C/\mathcal S$, whose
$\mathbb{F}_{q}$-dimension equals
$\dim\mathcal C-\dim\mathcal S=2n-2\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H)$. Since each
logical qudit contributes a canonically-conjugate pair of generators
(one logical $\bar Z$ and one logical $\bar X$), the number of
logical qudits is
\[
   k(q,d)\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\dim\mathcal C-\dim\mathcal S\bigr)
   \;=\;n-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H),
\]
which is~\eqref{eq:k-formula}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\@gobble{lem:hamada}
Let $p$ be a prime and let $A$ be the Singer-circulant point-hyperplane
incidence matrix of $\operatorname{PG}(d,p)$ over $\mathbb{F}_{p}$. Then we have
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:hamada-closed}
   \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(A) \;=\; \binom{p+d-1}{d} + 1.
\end{equation}
Moreover, for the $V_{1}$ stabilizer matrix $H=(A\mid M_{Q}A)$,
$\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(H)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(A)$, since the column span of $A$ over
$\mathbb{F}_{p}$ is closed under left-multiplication by any circulant
(circulants commute and the column span is an
$\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]/\langle X^{n}-1\rangle$-submodule).

rank-equality $\operatorname{rank}(H)=\operatorname{rank}(A)$ follows because circulant

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\@gobble{lem:two-torsion}
Let $\mathcal Q(q,d)$ be the $V_{1}$ stabilizer code on $G=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$
with $n=\theta_{d}(q)=(q^{d+1}-1)/(q-1)$. Assume that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item $n$ is even (equivalently $G$ has a non-trivial $2$-torsion
element $g_{2}=g^{n/2}$);
\item the cyclic cross-correlation
$(\boldsymbol\tau_{H}\star\boldsymbol\tau_{Q}^{(-1)})_{i}=|E\cap(i-Q)|$
is constant modulo $p$, equivalently $M_{Q}\boldsymbol\tau_{H}\equiv c\,\mathbf 1\pmod p$
for some $c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}$.
\end{enumerate}
Then the Pauli operator
$P_{2}:=Z_{0}\cdot Z_{n/2}^{q-1}$
commutes with every stabilizer generator, i.e.,
$P_{2}\in\mathcal C(\mathcal S)$. Provided $P_{2}\notin\mathcal S$,
$d_{\min}(\mathcal Q(q,d))\le 2$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $a=e_{0}+(q-1)\,e_{n/2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}$ and $b=\mathbf 0$. The
symplectic-commutativity check associated with stabilizer row $r$ is now
$a\cdot H_{x}[r]^{\top}-b\cdot H_{z}[r]^{\top}=a\cdot (M_{Q}A)[r,:]$.
The circulant identity $M_{Q}A=\mathrm{circ}(M_{Q}\boldsymbol\tau_{H})$
(since both $A$ and $M_{Q}$ are circulants and $\mathrm{col}_{j}(A)
=\mathrm{shift}_{j}(\boldsymbol\tau_{H})$) gives
\begin{align*}
   (M_{Q}A)[r,0]   &= (M_{Q}\boldsymbol\tau_{H})[r], \\
   (M_{Q}A)[r,n/2] &= \mathrm{shift}_{n/2}(M_{Q}\boldsymbol\tau_{H})[r].
\end{align*}
The corresponding check becomes
\[
   (M_{Q}\boldsymbol\tau_{H})[r] + (q-1)\,\mathrm{shift}_{n/2}(M_{Q}\boldsymbol\tau_{H})[r]
   \;\equiv\; 0 \pmod p.
\]
Under hypothesis (ii), $M_{Q}\boldsymbol\tau_{H}\equiv c\mathbf 1$, so
both terms equal $c$ and the sum is $c(1+(q-1))=qc\equiv 0\pmod p$.
Hence $P_{2}\in\mathcal C(\mathcal S)$.

\@gobble{lem:n-parity}
For $q$ odd, $n=\theta_{d}(q)=(q^{d+1}-1)/(q-1)$ is even iff $d$ is
odd. For $q$ even, $n$ is always odd.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\@gobble{thm:cross-corr}
Let $K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d+1}}$ with $q$ odd and $d\ge 3$. Let $E$ and $Q\subset G=K^{*}/F^{*}$
denote the trace-zero hyperplane and formal trace-of-square quadric
indicators, respectively. Then the cyclic cross-correlation
\[
   \nu[k] \;:=\; |E\cap(k-Q)|
\]
satisfies $\nu[k]\equiv 1\pmod q$ for every $k\in G$.
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
\textbf{Step 1: Lift cosets to $K^{*}$.} Define
\[
   M(k) \;:=\; \bigl|\{\gamma\in K^{*}:\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^{k}\gamma)=0\,\wedge\,\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^{2})=0\}\bigr|.
\]
Each coset of $F^{*}$ in $K^{*}$ has $q-1$ elements. For each ordered
pair of cosets $([\beta],[\gamma])\in E\times Q$ with
$[\beta][\gamma]^{-1}=\alpha^{k}F^{*}$, the number of element pairs
$(\beta,\gamma)\in K^{*}\times K^{*}$ realising this coset pair is
$(q-1)^{2}$. On the other hand, upon fixing $\beta=\alpha^{k}\gamma\,u$
with $u\in F^{*}$, the condition $\operatorname{Tr}(\beta)=0$ becomes
$u\,\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^{k}\gamma)=0$; with $u\ne 0$ this forces
$\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^{k}\gamma)=0$. Then $u$ is unconstrained over
$F^{*}$. Counting the element pairs
$(\beta,\gamma)\in K^{*}\times K^{*}$ jointly satisfying
$\operatorname{Tr}(\beta)=0$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^{2})=0$ in two ways --- once
coset-by-coset (giving $(q-1)^{2}\,\nu[k]$) and once via the lifted
counter $M(k)$ together with the unconstrained $u\in F^{*}$
(giving $(q-1)\,M(k)$) --- the two enumerations agree, yielding
$(q-1)\,M(k)=(q-1)^{2}\,\nu[k]$, hence
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:Mk-vs-nu}
   M(k) \;=\; (q-1)\,\nu[k].
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 2: Geometric interpretation.}
Upon adding the trivial solution $\gamma=0$, we have
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:Nk-Mk}
   N(k) \;:=\; M(k)+1 \;=\; (q-1)\nu[k]+1 \;=\; \bigl|H_{k}\cap Q^{*}\bigr|,
\end{equation}
where $H_{k}=\{\delta\in K:\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^{k}\delta)=0\}$ is an $F$-hyperplane
of $K$ (since $\operatorname{Tr}$ is $F$-linear), and
$Q^{*}=\{\delta\in K:\operatorname{Tr}(\delta^{2})=0\}$ is the trace-of-square
quadric in $K\cong F^{d+1}$.

\textbf{Step 3: $|H_{k}\cap Q^{*}|\equiv 0\pmod q$.}
$Q^{*}$ is the zero set of the quadratic form
$Q^{*}(\delta)=\operatorname{Tr}(\delta^{2})$ on the $F$-vector space
$K\cong F^{d+1}$, non-degenerate for $q$ odd
\cite{LidlNiederreiter1997}. Restricting our discourse, to $H_{k}\cong F^{d}$ gives
a quadratic form $Q^{*}|_{H_{k}}$ of rank $r\in\{d-2,\,d-1,\,d\}$.
Then, by \cite[Theorems 6.26--6.27]{LidlNiederreiter1997}, for
\emph{any} quadratic form on $F^{m}$ with $m\ge 3$, we have:
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:LN-quadcount}
   \bigl|\{x\in F^{m}: Q(x)=0\}\bigr| \;\equiv\; 0\pmod q.
\end{equation}
For non-degenerate $Q$ the count is $q^{m-1}$ ($m$ odd) or
$q^{m-1}\pm(q-1)q^{m/2-1}$ ($m$ even, hyperbolic/anisotropic), each
divisible by $q$ for $m\ge 3$. For degenerate $Q$ of rank $r<m$, the
count factors as $q^{m-r}\cdot|\{Q=0\text{ in }F^{r}\}|$, with the
factor $q^{m-r}$ supplying the divisibility (whenever $m-r\ge 1$).
Applied to $Q^{*}|_{H_{k}}$ with $m=d\ge 3$, we obtain $N(k)\equiv 0\pmod q$.

\textbf{Step 4: Conclude.}
Combining \eqref{eq:Nk-Mk} with $N(k)\equiv 0\pmod q$:
$(q-1)\nu[k]+1\equiv 0\pmod q$, i.e.\ $(q-1)\nu[k]\equiv -1\pmod q$.
Since $q-1\equiv -1\pmod q$ and $(-1)^{-1}=-1$, we get
$\nu[k]\equiv 1\pmod q$.
<!-- end proof-5 -->

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\@gobble{thm:two-value}
Let $q$ be odd and $d\ge 3$ odd. The cross-correlation
$\nu[k]=|E\cap(k-Q)|$ takes exactly two distinct values
$\{V_{1}, V_{2}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}$, and these values differ by
\[
   V_{2}-V_{1} \;=\; q^{(d-1)/2}.
\]
Equivalently, $N(k)=|H_{k}\cap Q^{*}|$ takes two values whose
difference is $q^{(d-1)/2}(q-1)$.
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The proof refines Step~3 of Theorem~\ref{thm:cross-corr} using the
classical zero-count formulas for quadratic forms over finite fields
\cite[Theorems 6.26--6.27]{LidlNiederreiter1997}.

\textbf{Step 1: Rank classification of $Q^{*}|_{H_{k}}$.}
$Q^{*}(\delta)=\operatorname{Tr}(\delta^{2})$ is a non-degenerate quadratic form
on $K\cong F^{m}$ with $m=d+1$ (even since $d$ odd). Its
associated bilinear form is
$B(\delta,\delta')=\operatorname{Tr}(2\delta\delta')$, and the radical is
$\{0\}$ since $q$ is odd. For any hyperplane
$H_{k}=\{\delta:\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^{k}\delta)=0\}$, the form
$Q^{*}|_{H_{k}}$ on $H_{k}\cong F^{d}$ has a rank of either:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item $r=d$ (the generic case, when $H_{k}$ is \emph{not} the
$B$-orthogonal hyperplane to any element);
\item $r=d-1$ (the special case, when $H_{k}=\alpha^{-2k}\cdot K^{B}_{\delta_{0}}$
for a specific $\delta_{0}\in K$ corresponding to the $B$-orthogonal
of the polar of $H_{k}$).
\end{itemize}
A drop from $r=d-1$ to $r=d-2$ would require $H_{k}$ to be both
self-polar and totally singular, which is impossible for
non-degenerate $Q^{*}$ in characteristic $\ne 2$. So only the two
ranks above occur.

\textbf{Step 2: Zero counts at rank $d$.}
For $r=d$ on $F^{d}$ with $d$ odd, the count
$|\{Q=0\}|=q^{d-1}$ \emph{independent of the form's discriminant}
\cite[Theorem 6.27]{LidlNiederreiter1997}. Hence we have:
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:N-generic}
   N_{\mathrm{gen}} \;:=\; q^{d-1}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 3: Zero counts at rank $d-1$.}
For $r=d-1$ on $F^{d}$, the form has 1-dim radical, and the
restriction to a non-radical complement gives a non-degenerate
rank-$(d-1)$ form on $F^{d-1}$. Since $d-1$ is \emph{even}, this
non-degenerate form is hyperbolic ($Q^{+}$) or anisotropic ($Q^{-}$)
\cite[Theorem 6.26]{LidlNiederreiter1997}. The total count factors as follows:
\begin{align*}
   N_{\mathrm{spec}} &\;=\; q\cdot \bigl|\{Q^{\pm}=0\}\text{ on }F^{d-1}\bigr| \\
                     &\;=\; q\cdot\bigl(q^{d-2}\pm(q-1)q^{(d-1)/2-1}\bigr) \\
                     &\;=\; q^{d-1}\pm(q-1)q^{(d-1)/2}.
\end{align*}

\textbf{Step 4: Determine the sign for $Q^{*}=\operatorname{Tr}(\cdot^{2})$.}
The Witt index~\cite{Cassels1978QuadraticForms,Scharlau1985}
of $Q^{*}$ on $K=F^{m}$ ($m=d+1$ even) equals
$m/2$ when the discriminant is a square in $F^{*}/(F^{*})^{2}$, else
$m/2-1$. For $\operatorname{Tr}(\delta^{2})$, the discriminant is
$(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}\det(\operatorname{Tr}(e_{i}e_{j}))$ for any $F$-basis
$\{e_{i}\}$ of $K$. By a standard calculation
\cite[\S 7]{LidlNiederreiter1997}, the discriminant is the
square of the field discriminant, hence a square; so $Q^{*}$ is
\emph{hyperbolic}.

When $H_{k}$ has $Q^{*}|_{H_{k}}$ of rank $d-1$, the form induced on
$F^{d-1}$ inherits its Witt structure from $Q^{*}$: it is
\emph{anisotropic} (Witt index drops by $1$ on a singular hyperplane).
Hence the sign in Step~3 is negative, yielding:
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:N-special}
   N_{\mathrm{spec}} \;=\; q^{d-1} - (q-1)\,q^{(d-1)/2}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 5: Conclude.}
By Steps 2--4, $N(k)\in\{N_{\mathrm{gen}}, N_{\mathrm{spec}}\}$ with
\[
   N_{\mathrm{gen}}-N_{\mathrm{spec}} \;=\; (q-1)\,q^{(d-1)/2}.
\]
Translating to $\nu$ via $\nu[k]=(N(k)-1)/(q-1)$ from
\eqref{eq:Nk-Mk}:
\[
   V_{\mathrm{gen}} \;=\; \frac{q^{d-1}-1}{q-1} =\! \theta_{d-2}(q),
   \:\:
   V_{\mathrm{spec}} \;=\; \theta_{d-2}(q) - q^{(d-1)/2}.
\]
The difference is $V_{\mathrm{gen}}-V_{\mathrm{spec}}=q^{(d-1)/2}$,
proving the claim.

<!-- theorem-10: body_md -->
\@gobble{thm:clifford-reduction-d2}
Throughout this T heorem $\mathbf x,\mathbf z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}$ are
column vectors and the operators $X^{\mathbf x},Z^{\mathbf z}$ live
in the $n$-qudit generalised Pauli group $\mathcal G_{n}$ defined in
Section~\ref{sec:prelim}: they act on the computational basis as
$X^{\mathbf x}\lvert \mathbf y \rangle=\lvert \mathbf y+\mathbf x \rangle$ and
$Z^{\mathbf z}\lvert \mathbf y \rangle=\omega^{\mathbf z\cdot\mathbf y}\lvert \mathbf y \rangle$
with $\omega=e^{2\pi i/q}$ and component-wise addition modulo $q$.
Let $q$ be an odd prime with $\mathrm{ord}_{n}(q)=3$, where
$n=q^{2}+q+1$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item There exists a circulant symmetric matrix
$M=\mathrm{circ}(\mathbf m)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:M-eqn}
   A\,M \;=\; M_{Q}\,A \qquad \text{and}\qquad M^{\top}=M.
\end{equation}
\item The block matrix
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:S-M}
   S_{M} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} I_{n} & M \\ 0 & I_{n} \end{pmatrix}
   \;\in\;\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n\times 2n}
\end{equation}
is a symplectic transformation, and the corresponding Clifford gate is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:U-M}
   U_{M} \;=\; H^{\otimes n}\,\cdot\, \mathrm{CZ}^{(M)}
              \,\cdot\, H^{\otimes n},
\end{equation}
where $H^{\otimes n}$ is the qudit Hadamard on each of the $n$
qudits and
$\mathrm{CZ}^{(M)}=\prod_{1\le i\le j\le n}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}^{M_{ij}}$
is the diagonal phase circuit, satisfying:
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:U-M-action}
   U_{M}^{\dagger}\,Z^{\mathbf z}\,U_{M}
   \;=\; Z^{\mathbf z}\,X^{\mathbf z M},\qquad
   U_{M}^{\dagger}\,X^{\mathbf x}\,U_{M}
   \;=\; X^{\mathbf x}.
\end{equation}
\item Define the trivial $Z$-only CSS code $\mathcal Q_{0}(q,2)$ with
parity-check matrix $H_{0}=(A\mid 0)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times 2n}$. Then we have
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:Clifford-eq}
   U_{M}\,\mathcal Q_{0}(q,2)\;=\;\mathcal Q(q,2),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the two stabilizer codes are Clifford-equivalent, with the
2021 quadric pattern $\boldsymbol\tau_{Q}$ playing exactly the role
of the $X$-block coupling in the centralizer of
$\mathcal Q(q,2)$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
\emph{(i) Existence of $M$.}
We seek $\mathbf m\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}$ such that $\boldsymbol\tau_{H}\!*\!
\mathbf m=\boldsymbol\tau_{Q}\!*\!\boldsymbol\tau_{H}$ in
$R=\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]/\langle X^{n}-1\rangle$ (where $*$ denotes circulant
multiplication), with the additional palindromic constraint
$\mathbf m_{i}=\mathbf m_{-i\bmod n}$ ($M=M^{\top}$).

Pass to the CRT decomposition $R\cong\bigoplus_{f\in\mathcal F}\mathbb{F}_{q^{\deg f}}$. For each factor $f$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item if $\tau_{H,f}\ne 0$, the equation forces $m_{f}=\tau_{Q,f}$;
\item if $\tau_{H,f}=0$, $m_{f}$ is free.
\end{itemize}
The palindromic constraint of $\mathbf m(X)\equiv\mathbf m(X^{-1})$
permutes the CRT factors by $f\leftrightarrow f^{*}$ (reciprocal
involution) and requires that, $m_{f^{*}}=$ image of $m_{f}$ under the
reciprocal map. By Theorem~\ref{thm:tau-zero-count}, in every
non-trivial reciprocal pair $\{f,f^{*}\}$ \emph{exactly one}
component has $\tau_{H,\cdot}=0$. Hence in every pair, one of the
two $m$-values is forced and the other is free, so the palindromic
identification can always be made: set the free $m_{f}$ equal to the
reciprocal image of the forced $m_{f^{*}}=\tau_{Q,f^{*}}$. The
trivial CRT component $f=X-1$ is self-reciprocal and pinned by the
forced equation $m_{X-1}=\tau_{Q,X-1}$. This shows that a
palindromic $\mathbf m$ exists.

\emph{(ii) Symplecticity.} For $S_{M}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} I &
M\\0 & I\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ over $\mathbb{F}_{q}$, the symplectic
of condition $S_{M}^{\top}\Omega\,S_{M}=\Omega$ associated with
$\Omega=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & I\\-I & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
reduces to $M^{\top}-M=0$, which holds by part (i). The Clifford
realisation
$U_{M}=H^{\otimes n}\mathrm{CZ}^{(M)}H^{\otimes n}$ is verified by
tracking conjugation: a Hadamard sweep maps $Z\leftrightarrow X$,
the CZ-cluster $\mathrm{CZ}^{(M)}$ maps $X^{\mathbf x}\to
X^{\mathbf x}Z^{\mathbf x M}$ (and leaves $Z^{\mathbf z}$
invariant), and the second Hadamard sweep returns the result to
the original $Z$/$X$ partition, yielding
\eqref{eq:U-M-action}.

\emph{(iii) Stabilizer reduction.} The stabilizer of
$\mathcal Q_{0}(q,2)$ is generated by the rows
$\bigl(\mathbf a_{i},\mathbf 0\bigr)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}$, $i=1,\dots,n$, of
$H_{0}=(A\mid 0)$. By \eqref{eq:U-M-action}, conjugation by $U_{M}$
maps each $Z^{\mathbf a_{i}}\to Z^{\mathbf a_{i}}X^{\mathbf a_{i}M}=
Z^{\mathbf a_{i}}X^{(M_{Q}A)_{i}}$, where the last equality uses
$AM=M_{Q}A$. Hence the image stabilizer is generated by the rows
$\bigl(\mathbf a_{i},(M_{Q}A)_{i}\bigr)$, which is precisely the row
span of $H=(A\mid M_{Q}A)$.

qudit-Clifford implementability: every architecture that supports
qudit CZ gates supports the required layer.

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:paired-rigidity}, it suffices to show that no Pauli
operator of weight $w\le 7$ lies in the centralizer of
$\mathcal Q(7,2)$ but outside its row span. We carry out an
exhaustive Singer-Frobenius-orbit-canonical paired-column search at
each weight $w\in\{1,\dots,7\}$.

The combined Singer-cycle and Frobenius group
$G=\langle\sigma,\phi\rangle$ where $\sigma:i\mapsto i+1\bmod n$ and
$\phi:i\mapsto 7\,i\bmod n$ has order
$|G|=n\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{n}(7)=57\cdot 3=171$, and acts on Pauli
support sets by the symplectic action that preserves the centralizer.
Each canonical orbit only has to be tested once.

Furthermore, a canonical (lex-min) representative $S$ of any orbit
satisfies $0\in S$: if not, the cyclic shift by $-\min(S)$ produces a
strictly smaller representative, contradicting canonicality. Hence
the search reduces to subsets of the form $S=\{0\}\cup S'$ with
$S'\subseteq\{1,\dots,56\}$ of size $w-1$. For $w=7$, this yields
$\binom{56}{6}=32{,}468{,}436$ subsets.

For each canonical $S$, we form the support-restricted matrix
$M_{S}=M_{\mathrm{cent}}[:,\,S\cup(S+n)]\in\mathbb{F}_{7}^{(n-k)\times 2w}$
and compute its right kernel $K_{S}=\ker(M_{S})\subset\mathbb{F}_{7}^{2w}$. A
weight-$w$ centralizer element with Pauli support exactly $S$ exists
iff $K_{S}$ contains a vector $\mathbf v$ for which: (i) the symplectic
support
$\{i\in S:v_{i}\ne 0\text{ or }v_{i+w}\ne 0\}$
equals $S$; and (ii) $\mathbf v$ projected to full length is not in
the row span of $H$. Condition (i) is implied by $K_{S}\ne 0$ at the
canonical-orbit level: if $K_{S}=0$, no centralizer element has a

\@gobble{thm:tau-zero-count}

<!-- remark-28: body_md -->
\@gobble{rem:minus-one-hyp-fail}
The hypothesis $-1\notin\langle p\rangle$ in
$(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$ is the exact algebraic condition that pairs
each non-trivial Frobenius orbit $\{f\}$ with a distinct
\emph{reciprocal} orbit $\{f^{*}\}\ne\{f\}$, so that the palindromic
CRT identification used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:clifford-reduction-d2} succeeds. When the hypothesis
fails --- for instance, when $\mathrm{ord}_{n}(p)$ is even and
$-1\in\langle p\rangle$ --- some Frobenius orbits become
self-reciprocal ($f=f^{*}$), the CRT reciprocal-pair balance
breaks, and the symmetric-circulant $M$ required by the Clifford
bridge no longer exists in general. In that regime the code
$\mathcal Q(p,2)$ still exists as an $\mathbb{F}_{p}$-ary stabilizer code
(the Cancellation Lemma still applies) but it is not
Clifford-equivalent to the trivial $Z$-only Singer CSS code via the
Hadamard--CZ factorisation of Theorem~\ref{thm:clifford-reduction-d2};
its structural analysis and parameters warrant a separate treatment
that is left for future work.

<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Identify $\boldsymbol\tau_{H}$ with the polynomial
$E(X)=\sum_{i\in E}X^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]/\langle X^{n}-1\rangle$, where
$E\subset\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is the trace-zero hyperplane (a Singer-translate of
the line at infinity in $\operatorname{PG}(2,p)$). Because $E$ is a planar
$(n,p+1,1)$-difference set, we have:
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:planar-EE}
\begin{aligned}
   E(X)\,E(X^{-1}) &\;\equiv\; (p+1) + (J(X)-1)\\
   &\;\equiv\; J(X) \pmod{p,\;X^{n}-1},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $J(X):=1+X+\cdots+X^{n-1}=(X^{n}-1)/(X-1)$, is the
``all-ones'' polynomial.

\emph{Step 1 (At-least-one vanishing per pair).}
$J$ is supported entirely on the trivial CRT factor $f_{0}=X-1$: at
every $f\ne f_{0}$ it reduces to $0$. Hence
\eqref{eq:planar-EE} gives $E_{f}\cdot E_{f^{*}}=0$ in
$\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]/f\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{\deg f}}$ for every non-trivial pair
$(f,f^{*})$, where $f^{*}$ is the reciprocal polynomial. The latter
is a field, so it has no zero divisors, forcing
$E_{f}=0$ \emph{or} $E_{f^{*}}=0$.

\emph{Step 2 (Hamada-Smith degree-sum
identity~\cite{Hamada1973,Smith1969}).}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:hamada} (Hamada-Smith for $\operatorname{PG}(2,p)$),
$\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(A)=\binom{p+1}{2}+1=\tfrac{p(p+1)}{2}+1$.
Since $A=\mathrm{circ}(\boldsymbol\tau_{H})$, its $\mathbb{F}_{p}$-rank equals
the sum of degrees of those CRT factors at which
$\boldsymbol\tau_{H}$ does \emph{not} vanish:
$\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(A)=\sum_{f:E_{f}\ne 0}\deg f$. Therefore we have"
\begin{equation}
\@gobble{eq:zero-deg-sum}
   \sum_{f:E_{f}=0}\deg f \;=\; n-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(A)
   \;=\; \frac{p(p+1)}{2} \;=\; \frac{n-1}{2}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 3 (Pairing forces equality).}
The hypothesis $-1\notin\langle p\rangle$ implies that no non-trivial
Frobenius orbit is fixed by $i\mapsto -i$; hence 
$f\leftrightarrow f^{*}$ acts on $\mathcal F\setminus\{f_{0}\}$ as a
free product of $2$-cycles. Within each pair we have, $\deg f=\deg f^{*}$, so
the partition into reciprocal pairs gives
$\sum_{\text{nontriv}}\deg f \;=\; 2\!\sum_{\text{one-per-pair}}\deg f$;
in particular, every selector that picks one element of each pair has a
total degree of $\tfrac{n-1}{2}$. By Step~1 the zero-set is
\emph{some} selector of at-least-one-per-pair, so we have
$\sum_{f:E_{f}=0}\deg f\ge\tfrac{n-1}{2}$, with equality iff the
zero-set picks \emph{exactly} one from each pair.
Equation~\eqref{eq:zero-deg-sum} achieves equality, hence the
zero-set is a `one-from-each-pair' selector and
\[
   \#\{f:E_{f}=0\} \;=\; \#\{\text{non-trivial pairs}\}
   \;=\; \frac{|\mathcal F|-1}{2}.
\]

<!-- remark-30: body_md -->
\@gobble{rem:bch-route-revised}
Equation~\eqref{eq:tau-zero-count-thm} shows that the original
BCH-count conjecture is \emph{false}: there are far more factors
where $\boldsymbol\tau_{H}$ vanishes than $p-1$. The proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:tau-zero-count} gives a sharp algebraic explanation
of the $(|\mathcal F|-1)/2$ pattern: it is forced jointly by the
planar-difference-set identity $E(X)E(X^{-1})\equiv J(X)$ (which
gives at-least-one-zero per reciprocal pair) and the Hamada-Smith
rank, which pins down the total degree of zero factors at
$(n-1)/2$ and hence enforces ``exactly one zero per pair.''

A correct BCH-style proof of Conjecture~\ref{conj:paired-rigidity}
must therefore exploit the joint structure of \emph{both}
$\boldsymbol\tau_{H}$ and $\mathbf u=M_{Q}\boldsymbol\tau_{H}$, plus
the centralizer constraint of $a_{f}u_{f}=b_{f}\tau_{f}$ in each CRT
component (Lemma~\ref{lem:crt-cent}). Combining
Theorem~\ref{thm:tau-zero-count} with the analogous identity for
$\mathbf u$ would isolate the four cells of Lemma~\ref{lem:crt-cent}
into precise frequencies and it is a promising next step. We leave
this finer count as future work.
