<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $a,b,c\in k^\times$, let $m\in k\setminus\{0,4abc\}$, and let
$X_m$ be the surface in \eqref{eq:projective}.
For every hyperplane section $H$ of $X_m$ and every integer
$n\ge2$, Campana strong approximation with the Brauer--Manin
obstruction holds for $\bigl(X_m,(1-1/n)H\bigr)$. More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 \overline{(X_m\setminus H)(k)}^{\,\mathrm C}
 =\mathcal A_{\mathrm C}(X_m,\Delta_H)^{\operatorname{Br}(X_m)}.
\end{equation}
The closure is taken in the Campana topology, and the right-hand
side is the intersection with $X_m(\mathbf A_k)^{\operatorname{Br}(X_m)}$.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- cor-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:boundary}
For any integers $n_x,n_y,n_z\ge2$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbifold}
 \Delta_{\boldsymbol n}
 =\left(1-\frac1{n_x}\right)D_x+
 \left(1-\frac1{n_y}\right)D_y+
 \left(1-\frac1{n_z}\right)D_z.
\end{equation}
Campana strong approximation with the Brauer--Manin obstruction holds
for $(X_m,\Delta_{\boldsymbol n})$. Equivalently,
\[
 \overline{U_m(k)}^{\,\mathrm C}
 =\mathcal A_{\mathrm C}(X_m,\Delta_{\boldsymbol n})^{\operatorname{Br}(X_m)}.
\]

<!-- rmk-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:topology}
The Campana topology in \eqref{eq:density} uses the local Campana
spaces of \cite{NS} as reference sets and allows boundary-valued local
points to be approximated by strict points. It differs from
\cite[Definition~2.15]{MNS}, whose strict part is restricted with
respect to local integral points on the complement and whose topology
separates the strict and boundary strata; see also
\cite[Remark~1.3]{MU}. Accordingly, all strong approximation statements
in this paper are understood with respect to the topology specified
here.
Since $X_m$ is a smooth cubic surface, it is geometrically rational;
hence $\operatorname{Br}(X_m)=\operatorname{Br}_1(X_m)$ and $\operatorname{Br}(X_m)/\operatorname{Br}_0(X_m)$ is finite
\cite[Corollaries~6.9.11 and Proposition~9.2.6]{Poonen}.
Already for $a=b=c=1$, the group $\operatorname{Br}(U_m)$ may also contain
transcendental classes \cite[Section~4]{CTWX}.
<!-- end rmk-1 -->
<!-- defn-1: body_md -->
\label{def:strong}
We say that $(X,\Delta)$ satisfies \emph{Campana strong
approximation} if $X^\circ(k)$ is dense in
$\mathcal A_{\mathrm C}(X,\Delta)$. We say that it satisfies
\emph{Campana strong approximation with the Brauer--Manin obstruction}
if
\[
 \overline{X^\circ(k)}^{\,\mathrm C}
 =\mathcal A_{\mathrm C}(X,\Delta)^{\operatorname{Br}}.
\]
It satisfies \emph{Campana strong approximation away from $T$} if
$X^\circ(k)$ is dense in
$\mathcal A_{\mathrm C}^{T}(X,\Delta)$. The corresponding property with
the Brauer--Manin obstruction requires density in
$\operatorname{pr}^{T}\bigl(\mathcal A_{\mathrm C}(X,\Delta)^{\operatorname{Br}}\bigr)$.
Thus, away from $T$, the obstruction is defined by projecting the full
Brauer--Manin set.

\label{def:weak}
Following \cite[Definition~2.7(ii)]{NS}, we say that the fixed
$\mathcal O_S$-model $(\mathscr X,\mathscr D)$ satisfies \emph{Campana weak approximation}
if $\mathcal C_S(X,\Delta)$ is dense in $\mathcal A_S(X,\Delta)$ equipped with

 \overline{\mathcal C_S(X,\Delta)}
 =\mathcal A_S(X,\Delta)\cap X(\mathbf A_k)^{\operatorname{Br}(X)},
\]
where the closure is taken in $\mathcal A_S(X,\Delta)$.

<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:open}
The space $\mathcal C_v(X,\Delta)$ is clopen in $X(k_v)$; when
$v\notin S$, it is also compact. Its intersection with any dense
Zariski-open subset of $X$ is dense in $\mathcal C_v(X,\Delta)$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For $v\notin S$, the integral locus $\mathscr X(\mathcal O_v)$ is compact and open
in $X(k_v)$.
                                                       
                                                                        
                                                                        
           
Reduction of sections modulo a fixed power of
$\mathfrak m_v$ is locally constant. For a finite weight, failure of
the Campana condition means that an intersection multiplicity lies in
$\{1,\ldots,n_i-1\}$; for an infinite weight, the condition requires
avoidance of the boundary modulo $\mathfrak m_v$. Thus the local
Campana set is clopen in the compact open integral locus and hence is
compact and open in $X(k_v)$. For $v\in S$, the set is $X(k_v)$.
A proper closed subset of a smooth, geometrically integral variety
over a local field contains no nonempty analytic open subset
\cite[Remark~3.5.76]{Poonen}. This proves the density assertion.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:equivalence}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Campana strong approximation for $(X,\Delta)$ is equivalent to
Campana weak approximation for every finite
$S\supseteq\Omega_k^\infty$ and every $\mathcal O_S$-model of $(X,\Delta)$,
in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:weak}. The same equivalence holds
after imposing the Brauer--Manin obstruction throughout.
\item For a finite $T\subset\Omega_k$, Campana strong approximation
away from $T$ is equivalent to the density of
                      
$\mathcal C_S(X,\Delta)$ in
$\mathcal A_S^{T}(X,\Delta)=\prod_{v\notin T}\mathcal C_v(X,\Delta)$
for every finite $S\supseteq\Omega_k^\infty\cup T$ and every
$\mathcal O_S$-model. With the Brauer--Manin obstruction, density is required
in
$\operatorname{pr}^{T}\bigl(\mathcal A_S(X,\Delta)
\cap X(\mathbf A_k)^{\operatorname{Br}(X)}\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For a fixed model, the open subsets $\mathcal A_R(X,\Delta)$
exhaust $\mathcal A_{\mathrm C}(X,\Delta)$ and satisfy
\[
 X^\circ(k)\cap\mathcal A_R(X,\Delta)=\mathcal C_R(X,\Delta).
\]
Therefore, density in every such open subset is equivalent to density in
their union. The same argument applies after intersecting with the full
Brauer--Manin set. For any other model, the fixed-$S$ Campana space is
again an open subset equipped with the product topology, because its
reference sets differ at only finitely many places. This proves (1).

Part (2) follows from the same argument applied to the open blocks
$\mathcal A_S^{T}$ with $S\supseteq T$ and, in the obstruction case,
to their intersections with the projection of the full Brauer--Manin
set.

<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:monotonicity}
Let $T\subset\Omega_k$ be finite, possibly empty.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Campana strong approximation away from $T$ for $(X,\Delta_1)$
implies Campana strong approximation away from $T$ for
$(X,\Delta_2)$.
\item Assume that $\operatorname{Br}(X)/\operatorname{Br}_0(X)$ is finite.
Campana strong approximation with the Brauer--Manin obstruction
away from $T$ for $(X,\Delta_1)$ implies the corresponding property
for $(X,\Delta_2)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The inclusion
$\mathcal A_{\mathrm C}(X,\Delta_1)\hookrightarrow
\mathcal A_{\mathrm C}(X,\Delta_2)$ is continuous and dense. Indeed, in
a basic neighbourhood, keep the components at a sufficiently large
finite set of places and replace the remaining components by integral
points avoiding $\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta_1)$; such points exist outside a finite
set of places \cite[Theorem~7.7.2]{Poonen}. They satisfy the Campana
conditions even for infinite weights. The same construction applies to
the spaces away from $T$.

For~(2), spread out finitely many representatives of
$\operatorname{Br}(X)/\operatorname{Br}_0(X)$ and enlarge that finite set so that the original and
replacement adelic components are integral outside it and all relevant
evaluations vanish there \cite[proof of Proposition~8.2.1]{Poonen}.
Thus the replacements preserve orthogonality to $\operatorname{Br}(X)$, which proves
density for the Brauer--Manin subsets and for their projections away
from $T$. Both assertions now follow from the assumed density and the
inclusion
$(X\setminus\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta_1))(k)\subseteq
(X\setminus\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta_2))(k)$.

\label{lem:localfibres}
Let $H$ be a reduced effective divisor containing no fibre of $f$,
and assume that its restriction to the generic fibre is reduced of
degree at most two. Outside a fixed finite set of places, every smooth
$k$-fibre $C$ with a $k$-point $R$ has local points whose sections avoid
$H$, provided either that $R$ avoids the reduction of $Z_f$ or that $H$
avoids $Z_f$.
<!-- end lem-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $\Sigma_f\subset\mathbf P^1_k$ be the finite reduced subscheme of base
points over which $f$ has singular fibres. Choose a finite set
$T_0\supset S$ such that $f$ extends to a flat conic bundle
$\widetilde f:\mathscr X_{\mathcal O_{T_0}}\to\mathbf P^1_{\mathcal O_{T_0}}$, the model
$\mathscr X_{\mathcal O_{T_0}}$ is smooth over $\mathcal O_{T_0}$, and the closures of
$\Sigma_f$ and $Z_f$ are finite \'etale over $\mathcal O_{T_0}$. We further
choose $T_0$ so that, for every $v\notin T_0$, the singular geometric
fibres of the reduction of $\widetilde f$ are pairs of distinct lines
whose nodes are precisely the reductions of $Z_f$. Enlarge $T_0$ so
that no fibre is contained in the closure of $H$. There is a uniform
bound on the number of intersection points with $H$ on any fibre
component not contained in $H$; we also exclude residue fields that are
too small for the point choices below. If $H\cap Z_f=\varnothing$,
exclude in addition the places where their closures meet. The resulting
set $T_0$ depends on $f$, $H$, and the models, but not on $C$.

The special fibre of the closure of $C$ is either smooth or a pair of
distinct lines. In the smooth case, it has a rational point outside $H$;
in the split case, a component not contained in $H$ has a smooth
rational point outside $H$. These points lift to the chosen fibre by
Hensel's lemma, applied where $f$ is smooth.

A non-split singular conic over the residue field has only one rational
point, namely its node. Hence the reduction of $R$ must be that node.
This is impossible when $R$ avoids $Z_f$. If instead $H$ avoids $Z_f$,
then the section defined by $R$ itself avoids $H$. This proves the
assertion in all cases.

\label{lem:twoavoid}

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:cubic}
Let $X\subset\mathbf P^3_k$ be a smooth cubic surface with a $k$-line
$\ell$, and let $f$ be the conic bundle given by planes through
$\ell$. Let $H$ be a hyperplane section not containing $\ell$.
If $H\cap Z_f=\varnothing$, then, for every integer $n\ge2$, Campana
strong approximation with the Brauer--Manin obstruction holds for
$\bigl(X,(1-1/n)H\bigr)$. The same conclusion
holds without the assumption $H\cap Z_f=\varnothing$, provided there is
a second bundle $g$ satisfying Lemma~\ref{lem:twoavoid}.
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let $L$ be the hyperplane class. Each $f$-fibre has class
$L-\ell$, so $H\cdot(L-\ell)=2$. The plane spanned by such a fibre
contains $\ell$; hence no $f$-fibre is contained in $H$. Every
hyperplane section is reduced: over $\overline{k}$, a nonreduced section would
be $2M+M'$ or $3M$ for lines on $X$, contradicting $L\cdot M=1$, since
$M^2=-1$ and distinct lines intersect at most once. Thus the restriction
of $H$ to a general fibre is reduced of degree two. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:equivalence}, it suffices to prove the fixed-$S$
statement for an arbitrary model. Choose a finite set $T\supset S$
containing the prescribed places and the exceptional places of
Lemma~\ref{lem:localfibres}.

Contract $\ell$ to obtain a degree-four del Pezzo surface with a
$k$-point, as in \cite[Proposition~2.4]{LM}. The theorem of Salberger
and Skorobogatov \cite[Theorem~0.1]{SS}, together with birational
invariance \cite[p.~1990]{HS}, gives ordinary weak approximation with
the Brauer--Manin obstruction on $X$. Given an adelic Campana
point orthogonal to $\operatorname{Br}(X)$,
choose neighbourhoods at the places in $T$ that are contained,
for $v\in T\setminus S$, in the local Campana sets. Ordinary
approximation gives a rational point $R$ in these neighbourhoods whose
fibre $C=f^{-1}(f(R))$ is smooth and meets $H$ in a reduced divisor of
degree two.

If $H\cap Z_f=\varnothing$, Lemma~\ref{lem:localfibres} supplies
local points on $C$ whose sections avoid $H$ at every place outside $T$.
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer} to approximate $R$ at the places in
$T$ gives a strict Campana point for the original set $S$ satisfying all
the prescribed approximations. If a second bundle satisfies
Lemma~\ref{lem:twoavoid}, apply Theorem~\ref{thm:two} instead.

<!-- prop-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:twolines}
	Let $X\subset\mathbf P^3_k$ be a smooth cubic surface containing two distinct
	$k$-lines $\ell_1,\ell_2$. If a hyperplane section $H$ does not
	contain $\ell_1$, then, for every integer $n\ge2$, Campana strong
	approximation with the Brauer--Manin obstruction holds for
	$\bigl(X,(1-1/n)H\bigr)$.
	If the lines intersect, this holds for every hyperplane other than
	the plane containing them both, after interchanging the lines if necessary.
	If the lines are disjoint, Campana strong approximation holds for
	\emph{every} hyperplane section $H$, without the Brauer--Manin obstruction.
<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $L$ be the hyperplane class, and let $f,g$ be the conic bundles
	associated with $\ell_1,\ell_2$.
	Suppose first that the lines are disjoint. Then
	\[
	(L-\ell_1)\cdot\ell_2=(L-\ell_2)\cdot\ell_1=1,
	\]
	so $\ell_2$ and $\ell_1$ are sections of $f$ and $g$, respectively.
	A section $s$ of $g$ meets every fibre in a smooth point, since
	$dg\circ ds=\mathrm{id}$. Every singular $g$-fibre therefore has a
	smooth point over its residue field, and its two components are defined
	over that field. Thus the fibre hypothesis of
	Lemma~\ref{lem:twoavoid} holds for $A=g(Z_f)$, and
	Proposition~\ref{prop:cubic} yields the assertion.
	The section of $f$ shows that its generic conic is rational, so
	$k(X)\simeq k(t)(u)$ and $X$ is $k$-rational. Consequently,
	$\operatorname{Br}(X)=\operatorname{Br}_0(X)$ by birational invariance
	\cite[Corollary~6.8.7]{Poonen}. Since no plane contains
	two disjoint lines, interchanging the lines if necessary ensures that
	$\ell_1\not\subset H$ for any given hyperplane section $H$.
	
	Now suppose that the lines intersect.
	Let $f,g$ be the conic bundles associated with $\ell_1,\ell_2$.
	Write $\ell_3$ for the third line in their common plane; it is a
	$k$-line because the residual divisor has degree one and is defined
	over $k$. Let $a_0\in\mathbf P^1(k)$ satisfy
	$g^{-1}(a_0)=\ell_1+\ell_3$, and set $A=g(Z_f)$. We now verify the
	fibre hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:twoavoid}.
	
	Work over $\overline k$ and take $q\in Z_f$. If its $f$-fibre is
	$\ell_2+\ell_3$, then $q\in\ell_3$, so $g(q)=a_0$. Otherwise, write
	that fibre as $L+M$. Both lines are disjoint from $\ell_2$, since
	$\ell_2$ belongs to a different $f$-fibre. The plane containing $L$ and
	$M$ is the tangent plane $T_qX$, and its section on $X$ is
	$\ell_1+L+M$.
	
	If the $g$-fibre through $q$ is singular, choose a component $N$
	passing through $q$. Every line on $X$ passing through $q$ lies in
	$T_qX$, so $N$ is one of $\ell_1,L,M$. Every component of a $g$-fibre
	meets $\ell_2$, because its plane contains $\ell_2$. Thus $N=\ell_1$,
	and the $g$-fibre is $\ell_1+\ell_3$.
	
	Consequently, every fibre of $g$ above $A$ is either smooth over
	its residue field or the split fibre over $a_0$. This verifies the
	hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:twoavoid}, including when the three lines
	are concurrent. The second part of Proposition~\ref{prop:cubic} proves
	the assertion. For a hyperplane other than the common plane, interchange
	$\ell_1,\ell_2$ if necessary.

 T_1=T_0\cup\{v\notin T_0:\operatorname{ord}_v(t)<0\}.

 (ax^2+by^2+c)u-y-mx^2u^3=0.

\label{lem:initialpoles}
Suppose that $a,b,c\in\mathcal O_v^\times$, $m\in\mathcal O_v$, and
$R\in U_m(k_v)\cap\mathscr V(\mathcal O_v)$. Then either $R$ has affine integral
coordinates, or its pole-order triple is one of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initialpoles}
 (0,r,r),\qquad(r,0,r),\qquad(r,r,0),\qquad r\ge1.
\end{equation}
Here the pole order of a coordinate $\lambda$ is
$\max\{0,-\operatorname{ord}_v(\lambda)\}$, with the pole order of $0$ defined to be zero.
After one application of $\Phi$, the corresponding triples are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstcycle}
 (2r,3r,5r),\qquad(r,2r,3r),\qquad(r,r,2r).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-7 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Choose primitive coordinates $[\xi:\eta:\zeta:\omega]$ for $R$,
with $\omega\ne0$. If $\omega$ is a unit, the affine coordinates are
integral. Otherwise, the reduction of $R$ lies on exactly one side of the boundary triangle,
since the vertices are excluded. If this side is $D_x$, then
$\eta,\zeta$ are units, and
\[
 \xi\eta\zeta=\omega(a\xi^2+b\eta^2+c\zeta^2-m\omega^2)
\]
implies $\operatorname{ord}_v(\xi)\ge\operatorname{ord}_v(\omega)$. Thus the pole orders are
$(0,r,r)$ for $r=\operatorname{ord}_v(\omega)\ge1$. The other two sides give the
remaining cases.

Write $R=(x,y,z)$ and
\[
 x'=yz/a-x,\qquad y'=x'z/b-y,\qquad z'=x'y'/c-z,
\]
so that $\Phi(R)=(x',y',z')$. For $(0,r,r)$, each successive product
has strictly larger pole order than the coordinate being subtracted,
giving $(2r,3r,5r)$.

For $(r,0,r)$, the identity $axx'=by^2+cz^2-m$ has a unique term
of least valuation, namely $cz^2$, with valuation $-2r$. Thus
$\operatorname{ord}_v(x')=-r$. The products $x'z$ and $x'y'$ then have pole orders
$2r$ and $3r$, respectively, giving $(r,2r,3r)$.

For $(r,r,0)$, only $by^2$ has valuation $-2r$ in the same identity,
so $\operatorname{ord}_v(x')=-r$. At the point $(x',y,z)$, the only term of valuation
$-2r$ in the identity
\[
 byy'=ax'^2+cz^2-m
\]
is $ax'^2$, so $\operatorname{ord}_v(y')=-r$. Since $z$ is integral, $z'=x'y'/c-z$ has
pole order $2r$. This gives $(r,r,2r)$ and proves
\eqref{eq:firstcycle} in every residue characteristic.

<!-- lem-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:growth}
Suppose that $a,b,c\in\mathcal O_v^\times$, $m\in\mathcal O_v$, and $R\in U_m(k_v)$
has pole orders $(A,B,A+B)$ with $0<A\le B$. Then $\Phi(R)$ has pole orders
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 (A+2B,\,2A+3B,\,3A+5B).
\end{equation}
For a point with pole orders $(A,B,A+B)$, the boundary
multiplicities in the natural model are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orders}
 (n_v(\mathscr D_x,R),n_v(\mathscr D_y,R),n_v(\mathscr D_z,R))=(B,A,0).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-8 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Write $R=(x,y,z)$. In the three successive mutations, the products
have pole orders $A+2B$, $2A+3B$, and $3A+5B$, respectively. Each
exceeds the pole order of the coordinate being subtracted, proving
\eqref{eq:recurrence}. The resulting first two entries remain in
increasing order, the last entry is their sum, and $A+2B\ge2A$.

Multiplying $[x:y:z:1]$ by $\pi_v^{A+B}$ gives primitive coordinates
with valuations $(B,A,0,A+B)$. Formula~\eqref{eq:min} therefore gives
\eqref{eq:orders}.

<!-- prop-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:amplification}
Put $M=\max\{n_x,n_y,n_z\}$ and choose $N\ge1$ such that
$2^{N-1}\ge M$. Let $\varphi=\Phi^N$. At every finite place where
$a,b,c$ are units and $m$ is integral, one has
\[
 \varphi\bigl(U_m(k_v)\cap\mathscr V(\mathcal O_v)\bigr)
 \subset\mathcal C_v(X_m,\Delta_{\boldsymbol n})
\]
for the natural model and the weights $\boldsymbol n$.
<!-- end prop-7 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Let $R\in U_m(k_v)\cap\mathscr V(\mathcal O_v)$. If its affine coordinates are
integral, they remain integral under the Vieta involutions, so all
boundary multiplicities of $\varphi(R)$ are zero. Otherwise,
Lemma~\ref{lem:initialpoles} gives pole orders $(A,B,A+B)$ after the
first cycle, with $0<A\le B$ and $A\ge r\ge1$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:growth} preserves this form and at least doubles the
smaller entry at each further cycle. After $N$ cycles, that entry is at
least $2^{N-1}r\ge M$. By \eqref{eq:orders}, the two nonzero boundary
multiplicities are therefore at least $M$, proving the Campana
conditions.

<!-- rmk-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:movingfibre}
Vieta iteration is performed on $U_m$ and need not preserve the
conic $C_t$. For example, the local point $p_v$ chosen in
Lemma~\ref{lem:vertices} above a pole of $t$ has pole orders $(r,0,r)$,
where $r=\operatorname{ord}_v(t^{-1})$. Its first two cycles have pole orders
$(r,2r,3r)$ and $(5r,8r,13r)$. The final point lies on a different fibre
of $f$. Thus Proposition~\ref{prop:amplification} does not contradict
the local obstruction on the original conic described in
Remark~\ref{rem:smallpole}.

<!-- rmk-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:continuity}
The approximation argument using Vieta involutions works by taking
the inverse image of an approximating point. After fixing $\varphi$, we
choose $A$ close to the prescribed local points and set
$R_0=\varphi^{-1}(A)$. We then choose $R$ close to $R_0$. Continuity
makes $\varphi(R)$ close to $\varphi(R_0)=A$. We do not claim that
applying $\varphi$ keeps a point close to its original location.

<!-- rmk-6: body_md -->
\label{rem:finiteweights}
The generalised Markoff approximation theorems above concern finite weights.
Vieta iteration forces positive orders to grow, and the rational-conic
machinery also concerns finite weights. These arguments therefore do not
establish approximation for infinite weights. The failures of integral
approximation recalled in the introduction show why this restriction is
necessary.
