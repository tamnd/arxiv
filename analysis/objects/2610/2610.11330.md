\label{ass:class}
The integers $d,k\ge1$, the bounds $\Gamma_{\mathrm{coef}}>0$ and $0<\lambda\le\Lambda$,
and the horizon $T>0$ are known to the algorithm. Every component of
$b$ and $\sigma$ belongs to $C_b^k([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^d)$, every time--space
derivative of total order at most $k$, including order zero, has absolute

\[
 \lambdaI_d\preceq a(t,z)\preceq\LambdaI_d,
 \qquad (t,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^d.
\]
Derivatives at the time endpoints have continuous one-sided extensions.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{fw:upper}
Algorithm~\ref{fw:algorithm}, denoted by $A_F$, is query-only,
terminates almost surely and samples \eqref{eq:forwardtarget} exactly.
There is a constant $C>0$, depending only on the fixed coefficient

\begin{equation}\label{cx:forward-upper}
 \sup_{(b,\sigma)\in\mathcal F_T}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{W}_{A_F}\le C(t_m+m).
\end{equation}
The constant is uniform in the initial state, horizon and requested grid.

\label{bridge:general}
Algorithm~\ref{bridge:general-algorithm}, denoted by $A_B$, is

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{cx:admissible}
An admissible algorithm receives the class bounds, horizon, endpoint
data and requested grid, uses only the coefficient queries and
primitives specified in Section~\ref{sec:model}, and terminates almost
surely with an exact sample for every allowed choice of coefficients
and inputs in its target family. Its expected work may be infinite.
For fixed endpoints, define
\begin{align}
 \mathfrak R_F^x(T,m)
 &=\inf_A\sup_{\substack{(b,\sigma)\in\mathcal F_T\\
                         \mathbf t\in\mathcal G_F(T,m)}}
       \mathbb{E}_{b,\sigma}\mathcal{W}_A(T,x,\mathbf t),\nonumber\\
 \mathfrak R_B^{x,y}(T,m)
 &=\inf_A\sup_{\substack{(b,\sigma)\in\mathcal F_T\\
                         \mathbf t\in\mathcal G_B(T,m)}}
       \mathbb{E}_{b,\sigma}\mathcal{W}_A(T,x,y,\mathbf t).
 \label{cx:risks}
\end{align}
The infima are over admissible query-only algorithms for the respective
families. A single algorithm within each supremum must handle all
coefficients and grids in that supremum; it accesses the unknown
coefficients only through point queries.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{main:rates}\label{cx:rates}
The algorithms $A_F$ and $A_B$ of Theorems~\ref{fw:upper}
and~\ref{bridge:general} are admissible. There are constants $c,C>0$,
depending only on the fixed coefficient class and primitive costs,
such that the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(i)] For every $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$, $T>0$ and $m\ge1$,
\begin{equation*}
 \begin{aligned}
 c(T+m)&\le\mathfrak R_F^x(T,m)\le\sup_{\substack{(b,\sigma)\in\mathcal F_T\\
                      \mathbf t\in\mathcal G_F(T,m)}}
       \mathbb{E}_{b,\sigma}\mathcal{W}_{A_F}(T,x,\mathbf t)
 \le C(T+m).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Thus $A_F$ is minimax optimal jointly in $T$ and $m$.

\item[\rm(ii)] For every fixed $T>0$ and $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^d$, there is
a finite constant $C_{T,|y-x|}>0$, depending only on $T$, $|y-x|$,
the coefficient class and primitive costs, such that for all $m\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{cx:bridge-rate}
 \begin{aligned}
 cm&\le\mathfrak R_B^{x,y}(T,m)\le\sup_{\substack{(b,\sigma)\in\mathcal F_T\\\mathbf t\in\mathcal G_B(T,m)}}
       \mathbb{E}_{b,\sigma}\mathcal{W}_{A_B}(T,x,y,\mathbf t)
 \le C_{T,|y-x|}m.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $A_B$ is minimax optimal in $m$ for fixed $T,x,y$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
For bridges, the minimax optimality established here concerns the
number of requested observations $m$, with the horizon and endpoints
fixed. The construction also improves the dependence on the horizon
relative to the natural terminal-rejection method of
Section~\ref{br5:terminal-rejection}. For equal endpoints, that
method has worst-case expected work at least $c_1e^{c_2T}$, as
witnessed by an admissible constant-coefficient diffusion, whereas
our sampler has the uniform bound $O\{(1+T)^Q\log(3+T)+m\}.$
Thus the horizon dependence is polynomial up to a logarithmic
factor. The sharp minimax dependence on the bridge horizon remains
open and is a direction of future research.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{supp:fw:retry}
Conditional on a fixed history $\mathcal H$, let
$(C_k,S_k,Y_k)_{k\ge1}$ be independent identically distributed trial records,
where $C_k\ge0$ is the work of trial $k$, $S_k\in\{0,1\}$ indicates success (or not), and $Y_k$ is the proposed output. Suppose
$\mathbb{P}(S_k=1\mid\mathcal H)=q>0$ and
$\mathbb{E}(C_k\mid\mathcal H)\le c$. Then, for
$\nu=\inf\{k:S_k=1\}$, we have $\nu<\infty$ almost surely and $\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\nu} C_k\,\middle|\,\mathcal H\right]\le c/q.$
The successful trial's output has the one-trial conditional law given
success.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Conditional on $\mathcal H$, $\nu$ is geometric with parameter $q$.
The event $\{\nu\ge k\}$ depends only on the first $k-1$ trials and is
conditionally independent of $C_k$. Then by the conditional Tonelli theorem, we have
\[
 \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k\ge1}\mathbf{1}\{\nu\ge k\}C_k
       \,\middle|\,\mathcal H\right]
 =\sum_{k\ge1}(1-q)^{k-1}\mathbb{E}(C_k\mid\mathcal H)\le c/q.
\]
If $Y_k$ is the output in trial $k$, then for a measurable set $A$,
\[
 \mathbb{P}(Y_\nu\in A\mid\mathcal H)
 =\sum_{k\ge1}(1-q)^{k-1}
       \mathbb{P}(Y_k\in A,S_k=1\mid\mathcal H)
 =\mathbb{P}(Y_1\in A\mid S_1=1,\mathcal H),
\]which finishes the proof.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Lemma~\ref{core:shorttransition} gives the exact conditional law and a
uniform conditional expected-work bound for every short step in
Algorithm~\ref{fw:algorithm}. Successive conditioning and
Chapman--Kolmogorov identity, integrating out auxiliary states, therefore give
\eqref{eq:forwardtarget}. The routines use only coefficient values.
Their constants are calibrated on unit windows from the class bounds;
this has fixed cost and requires no coefficient queries outside $[0,T]$,
including when $T<1$.

The integers $n_i$ can be computed by counting loops in
$O(N_{\mathrm{short}})$ work. Forming the intermediate times,
managing states and writing outputs costs
$O(N_{\mathrm{short}}+m)$. Summing the conditional work bounds and
using \eqref{fw:counts} gives
\[
 \mathbb{E}\mathcal{W}_{A_F}\le C_0(1+N_{\mathrm{short}}+m)
 \le C(t_m+m),
\]
since $h_{\mathrm{env}}$ is a class constant and $m\ge1$.

\label{core:parametrix}
\label{supp:core:parametrix}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:class}, for every fixed
$T_{\mathrm{win}}>0$ there are computable constants $C_g,C_H,C_p^*>0$
such that, for $0<h=t-s\le\min\{T_{\mathrm{win}},T\}$,
\eqref{core:parametrix-series} converges absolutely to $p(e)$ and
\[
 g(e)\le C_gG_{\kappa,h}(w),\qquad
 |H(e)|\le C_Hh^{-1/2}G_{\kappa,h}(w),\qquad
 0\le R(e)\le C_p^*,\qquad \kappa=2\Lambda.
\]
The constants depend only on $d,\Gamma_{\mathrm{coef}},\lambda,\Lambda,T_{\mathrm{win}}$.
As a result, the
Chapman--Kolmogorov identity holds pointwise.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Fix $T_{\mathrm{win}}>0$ and restrict attention to $h\le\min\{T_{\mathrm{win}},T\}$.
Throughout the proof, we suppress the dependence of $C_H$ and $C_p^*$
on $T_{\mathrm{win}}$ in the notation.

The product rule gives $|\partial_\xi a_{ij}|\le2d\Gamma_{\mathrm{coef}}^2$ for every
time or spatial coordinate $\xi$. Hence
\[
 |b(s,u)|\le B_b:=\sqrt d\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{coef}},\qquad
 \|a(s,u)-a(t,v)\|_F\le L_a(h+|w|),
\] where $L_a:=2d^{5/2}\Gamma_{\mathrm{coef}}^2.$
With $r=|w|/\sqrt h$ and $\kappa=2\Lambda$, ellipticity yields $\frac{g(e)}{G_{\kappa,h}(w)}\le C_g e^{-r^2/(4\Lambda)} \leq C_g,$ with $C_g:=\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{d/2}.$
Using $|A^{-1}w/h|\le r/(\lambda\sqrt h)$ in
\eqref{core:defect}, we obtain $|H(e)|\le h^{-1/2}P_{T_{\mathrm{win}}}(r)g(e),$ where
\[
 P_{T_{\mathrm{win}}}(r):=\frac{B_b}{\lambda}r+
 \frac{L_a}{2}(r+\sqrt{T_{\mathrm{win}}})
 \left(\frac{r^2}{\lambda^2}+\frac{\sqrt d}{\lambda}\right)
 =\sum_{j=0}^3\alpha_jr^j.
\]
Since $\sup_{r\ge0}r^je^{-r^2/(4\Lambda)}=(2j\Lambda/e)^{j/2}$
for $j\ge1$, the computable choice
\[
C_H:=C_g\left[\alpha_0+
\sum_{j=1}^3\alpha_j(2j\Lambda/e)^{j/2}\right]
\]
gives $g(e)\le C_gG_{\kappa,h}(w)$ and $|H(e)|\le C_Hh^{-1/2}G_{\kappa,h}(w).$

For a bounded Lipschitz function $f$ and time pair $q < t$, we denote $F_{t,f}(q,z)=\int g(q,z;t,v)f(v)\,\,\mathrm{d} v.$
Then we have
\begin{equation*}
 \sup_z|F_{t,f}(t-h,z)-f(z)|
 \le\sqrt h\left(C\|f\|_\infty+
 \sqrt{d\Lambda}\operatorname{Lip}(f)\right),
\end{equation*}
where $C$ depends only on $d,\lambda,\Lambda,L_a$.

For $f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$ and $s<\beta<t$, the initial-variable
derivatives of $F_{t,f}$ are bounded on $[s,\beta]\times\mathbb{R}^d$.
It\^o's formula and \eqref{core:defect} therefore imply
\[
 \mathbb{E} F_{t,f}(\beta,X_\beta)-F_{t,f}(s,u)
 =\int_s^\beta\int P_{s,u;q}(\,\mathrm{d} z)
       \int H(q,z;t,v)f(v)\,\,\mathrm{d} v\,\,\mathrm{d} q.
\]
The terminal approximation and continuity of $X$ identify the limit
of the left side as $\beta\uparrow t$. The absolute integral on the
right is at most $2C_H\sqrt h\,\|f\|_\infty$. Therefore, by Fubini's theorem and dominated convergence theorem, 
\begin{equation}\label{supp:ana:measure-duhamel}
 P_{s,u;t}(\,\mathrm{d} v)=\left[g(s,u;t,v)+
 \int_s^t\int H(q,z;t,v)P_{s,u;q}(\,\mathrm{d} z)\,\,\mathrm{d} q\right]\,\mathrm{d} v.
\end{equation}

For ordered times $s<q_1<\cdots<q_n<t$, set $q_0=s$, $q_{n+1}=t$
and $\Delta_i=q_{i+1}-q_i$.
Gaussian convolution in the definition of $K_n$, followed by the
Dirichlet integral, gives
\begin{align}
 \mathsf Z_n(h)
&=\int_{\substack{\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_n>0\\\Delta_1+\cdots+\Delta_n<h}}\prod_{i=1}^n\Delta_i^{-1/2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\Delta_1\cdots\,\mathrm{d}\Delta_n
   =\frac{\pi^{n/2}h^{n/2}}{\Gamma(1+n/2)}
 \label{supp:ana:term-bound}
\end{align} and $|K_n(e)|\le C_gC_H^n\mathsf Z_n(h)G_{\kappa,h}(w).$
In particular, all convolution integrals are absolutely convergent.
Iterating \eqref{supp:ana:measure-duhamel} gives
\[
 P_{s,u;t}(\,\mathrm{d} v)=
 \left[\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}K_n(s,u;t,v)+\mathcal R_m(s,u;t,v)\right]\,\mathrm{d} v,
 \qquad
 \|\mathcal R_m(s,u;t,\cdot)\|_1\le C_H^m\mathsf Z_m(h).
\]
Since $\frac{C_H^{n+2}\mathsf Z_{n+2}(h)}{C_H^n\mathsf Z_n(h)}
 =\frac{\pi C_H^2h}{1+n/2}\longrightarrow0,$
the remainder tends to zero and the series converges absolutely
and in $L^1(\,\mathrm{d} v)$.
Also, each $K_n$ is jointly continuous for $s<t$.
Then by the bound \eqref{supp:ana:term-bound} the parametrix series is locally uniformly convergent on $s<t$.
Its sum is therefore continuous and, by the vanishing remainder,
agrees with the continuous transition density at every endpoint: $p(e)=g(e)+\sum_{n\ge1}K_n(e).$
Hence,
$p=g+p\otimes H$ pointwise.

Finally, let $u_h=C_H\sqrt{\pi h}$. Splitting the series into even
and odd terms and using
$\Gamma(j+3/2)\ge(\sqrt\pi/2)j!$ gives $\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{u_h^n}{\Gamma(1+n/2)}
 \le(1+2u_h/\sqrt\pi)e^{u_h^2}.$
Consequently, with $u_*:=C_H\sqrt{\pi T_{\mathrm{win}}},$
$0\le R(e)\le C_p^*:=C_g(1+2u_*/\sqrt\pi)e^{u_*^2}.$
All constants depend only on $d,\Gamma_{\mathrm{coef}},\lambda,\Lambda,T_{\mathrm{win}}$.
Taking $T_{\mathrm{win}}=1$ gives the constants used in the main text.
The Markov property implies the Chapman--Kolmogorov identity almost
everywhere in the final endpoint. Continuity and the Gaussian bound
make its integral continuous there, so the identity holds pointwise.

For every $j\ge1$ and $0<h\le1$, a query-only construction generates

We first describe the algorithm, and then show that is satisfies \eqref{core:Tnproperties} with the work guarantee. Draw independent $U_0\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)$ and
$\chi_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, $1\le i\le j$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{core:randomgaps}
 Q_0=-\log U_0,\qquad Q_i=\chi_i^2/2\ (1\le i\le j),
 \qquad \Delta_i=\frac{hQ_i}{\sum_{l=0}^jQ_l}.
\end{equation}
On the null event that any gap is zero, return zero before forming
denominators. Otherwise the normalized gaps have the Dirichlet law
with parameters $(1,1/2,\ldots,1/2)$; their density on the gap simplex
is $\mathsf Z_j(h)^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^j\Delta_i^{-1/2}$.
Let $\tau_0=s$, $\tau_{i+1}=\tau_i+\Delta_i$, $z_0=u$ and
$z_{j+1}=v$. Conditional on the gaps, generate the intermediate
states of a Gaussian bridge with covariance parameter $\kappaI_d$.
Its joint density is
\[
 \frac{\prod_{i=0}^jG_{\kappa,\Delta_i}(z_{i+1}-z_i)}
 {G_{\kappa,h}(v-u)}.
\]
For an explicit implementation, draw $z_i$, $1\le i\le j$,
successively with conditional mean and covariance
\[
 z_{i-1}+\frac{\tau_i-\tau_{i-1}}{t-\tau_{i-1}}(v-z_{i-1}),
 \qquad
 \frac{(\tau_i-\tau_{i-1})(t-\tau_i)}{t-\tau_{i-1}}\kappaI_d.
\]
Writing $G_i=G_{\kappa,\Delta_i}(z_{i+1}-z_i)$, define
\begin{equation}\label{core:random-correction}
 \Upsilon_j=\mathsf Z_j(h)
 \frac{g(s,u;\tau_1,z_1)}{G_0}
 \prod_{i=1}^j
 \frac{\sqrt{\Delta_i}H(\tau_i,z_i;\tau_{i+1},z_{i+1})}{G_i}.
\end{equation}
Cancellation against the gap and bridge densities proves the
expectation in \eqref{core:Tnproperties}. The bounds of
Lemma~\ref{core:parametrix} applied to each factor give its pathwise
bound. Absolute integrability follows from the same bounds.

All gaps, bridge states and factors require $O(j+1)$ scalar work in
fixed dimension. The normalizers are evaluated without the Gamma
function using the quantity
\[
 \mathsf Z_0(h)=1,\qquad \mathsf Z_1(h)=2\sqrt h,
 \qquad \mathsf Z_{j+2}(h)=\frac{2\pi h}{j+2}\mathsf Z_j(h).
\]Therefore, the proof is finished.

The choices of $b_{\mathrm{env}}$ and $h_{\mathrm{env}}$ imply

\[

 \mathbb{E} N_l\le2^{-l},\qquad
 \mathbb{E}\sum_{l\ge0}N_l\le2.

 Z_0
 \le (1+C_2/N)^N\int_{E_0}F
 \le e^{C_2}p_{T_0}.
\]
Therefore $M_{\mathrm{env}}\le Cp_{T_0}$, uniformly in $n$ and
the coefficients within the class.
Each complete trial succeeds with probability
$p_{T_0}/M_{\mathrm{env}}\ge C^{-1}$.
Conditional on success, its density is
\[
 \frac{F/M_{\mathrm{env}}}{p_{T_0}/M_{\mathrm{env}}}
 =\frac{F}{p_{T_0}},
\]
the exact coarse-bridge density.

For completeness, the work of one trial includes the augmented
graph draw as well as the selected component's proposal and tests.
Let $E_{\mathrm{aug}}$ be the number of edges in the augmented graph.
The original edge coins have uniformly bounded expected work,
and the auxiliary edge coins return one deterministically.
Lemma~\ref{br5:dag} therefore bounds the expected work of the graph
draw, including its initial estimates and internal retries, by
$O\{E_{\mathrm{aug}}N^3\log(4E_{\mathrm{aug}}N)\}$.
The within-cell proposal and acceptance tests cost $O(N^2)$ in expectation,
uniformly over the selected original graph path.
The bad-edge and exit trial bounds proved above consequently give
the following uniform bound for one complete trial:
\[
 c_{\mathrm{trial}}
 =C\left\{
 E_{\mathrm{aug}}N^3\log(4E_{\mathrm{aug}}N)
 +N^2+n+NS_{\mathrm{cell}}
 \right\}.
\]

After a failed trial, the algorithm restarts the augmented graph
draw with fresh initial estimates and fresh component randomness.
Thus complete trials are independent and identically distributed
for fixed coefficients and calibrated graph.
Lemma~\ref{supp:fw:retry} bounds the expected work through the first
success by
\[
 c_{\mathrm{trial}}\frac{M_{\mathrm{env}}}{p_{T_0}}
 \le Cc_{\mathrm{trial}}.
\]
The one-time graph construction cost is accounted for separately
in Section~\ref{br5:bridge-sampler-proof}.

\label{br5:insertion}
Starting from an exact coarse bridge, the procedure of
Section~\ref{br5:insert-section} inserts all requested times with
the exact joint bridge law and terminates almost surely. Its expected
additional work is $O(m+N)$, uniformly in $n$, the coefficient class

Fix an insertion time and condition on the retained states.
Write $p_i=p(e_i)$, and first suppose that
$|v-u|^2/h'\le D_0n$.
On $S_0$, the covariance of the first frozen kernel differs from
$A=a(r,v)$ by at most
\[
 \|a(t,z)-A\|_F
 \le L_a\{h'+\sqrt{D_1nh'}\}
 \le\frac{L_a\{h_0+\sqrt{D_1h_0}\}}{n}
 \le\frac1{2n}.
\]
The second frozen covariance is $A$.
Both are therefore bounded above by $A_+=A+I_d/n$.
Ellipticity and $A_+\preceq(\Lambda+1)I_d$ give
\[
 g(e_1)\le C_g'G_{A_+,\tau_1}(z-u),
 \qquad
 g(e_2)\le C_g'G_{A_+,\tau_2}(v-z).
\]
Moreover, $\tau_i\le h\le h_0n^{-3}$, and the displacement
conditions defining $S_0$ make both sharp-coin calls admissible.
Thus the two sharp coins and the two uniform comparisons have
product mean $p_1p_2/\mathcal E_0(z)$.

On $S_1$,
\[
 \frac{K_{\mathrm b}G_{c,\tau_1}(z-u)}
      {\delta_1G_{2c,\tau_1}(z-u)}
 =
 \exp\left\{\frac{D_1n-|z-u|^2/\tau_1}{4c}\right\}
 \le1.
\]
Combining this comparison with the two transition coins gives
mean $p_1p_2/\mathcal E_1(z)$.
The calculation on $S_2$ is identical for the second subinterval.
Since the sets $S_j$ partition $\mathbb{R}^d$, choosing component $j$
with probability $M_j/(M_0+M_1+M_2)$ and then applying its tests
gives the one-trial accepted density
\[
 \frac{p(s,u;t,z)p(t,z;r,v)}{M_0+M_1+M_2},
\]
which has mass $p(s,u;r,v)/(M_0+M_1+M_2)$, and conditioning on
acceptance gives the required conditional bridge density.

We next bound the expected work of these mixture trials.
Covariance comparison gives
\[
 \frac{G_{A_+,h'}(v-u)}{g(s,u;r,v)}
 \le
 \exp\left\{\frac{|v-u|^2}{2\lambda^2nh'}\right\}
 \le e^{D_0/(2\lambda^2)},
\]
so $M_0\le Cg(s,u;r,v)$.
The Gaussian covariances in $M_1$ and $M_2$ lie between
$ch'I_d$ and $2ch'I_d$. Hence
\[
 M_1+M_2
 \le C\exp\left\{
       \left[-\frac{D_1}{4c}+\frac{D_0}{2\lambda}\right]n
       \right\}
       g(s,u;r,v)
 \le Cg(s,u;r,v).
\]
Here \eqref{br5:insertion-constants} makes the exponent negative.
The existing edge also satisfies the conditions of
Lemma~\ref{br5:local-inputs}, which gives
$p(s,u;r,v)\ge\tfrac12g(s,u;r,v)$.
Thus $M_0+M_1+M_2\le Cp(s,u;r,v)$, so each mixture trial has
a uniformly positive success probability.

The Gaussian draws, matrix operations, uniform comparisons and
coin calls have uniformly bounded expected work in fixed dimension.
Throughout retries, the existing states and insertion time remain
fixed, while the mixture index, candidate state and acceptance
randomness are regenerated independently.
Lemma~\ref{supp:fw:retry} therefore gives uniformly bounded
conditional expected sampling work for moderate endpoints.

For $|v-u|^2/h'>D_0n$, the Gaussian bridge proposal has density
\[
 \frac{G_{c,\tau_1}(z-u)G_{c,\tau_2}(v-z)}{G_{c,h'}(v-u)}.
\]
The two transition coins give accepted density
$p_1p_2/(K_{\mathrm b}^2G_{c,h'}(v-u))$.
This again yields the required conditional bridge law.
Each trial has uniformly bounded conditional expected work, so
Lemma~\ref{supp:fw:retry} bounds the conditional expected sampling
work by
\[
 C\frac{G_{c,h'}(v-u)}{p(s,u;r,v)}.
\]
The fixed preliminary operations can be absorbed into this bound,
since $p(s,u;r,v)\le K_{\mathrm b}G_{c,h'}(v-u)$.
For every fixed pair of endpoints, the acceptance probability is
positive, so this insertion also terminates almost surely.

It remains to average the last cost on the large-displacement
event. Each successful insertion preserves the joint bridge law,
by the conditional-density calculations above.
The times $s,r$ are deterministic functions of the requested
grid and coarse grid; they do not depend on the sampled states.
Writing $T_0=2n$ and $p_{T_0}=p(0,0;T_0,0)$, their bridge marginal
therefore gives the expected large-displacement contribution
\begin{equation}\label{br5:insertion-cancel}
 \frac{C}{p_{T_0}}\iint
 p(0,0;s,u)G_{c,h'}(v-u)
 \mathbf{1}_{\{|v-u|^2/h'>D_0n\}}
 p(r,v;T_0,0)\,\,\mathrm{d} u\,\,\mathrm{d} v.
\end{equation}
Here the factor $p(s,u;r,v)$ in the endpoint marginal cancels
the denominator of the conditional work bound.
At $s=0$, the first transition measure is a point mass at zero;
at $r=T_0$, the final endpoint is fixed at zero.

If $r<T_0$, then $r$ is a right coarse endpoint and
$T_0-r\ge h$.
By \eqref{br5:global-density}, the last density in
\eqref{br5:insertion-cancel} is at most $Ch^{-d/2}$, while
$p_{T_0}^{-1}\le Ce^{C_*n}$.
Also,
\[
 \int G_{c,h'}(w)
 \mathbf{1}_{\{|w|^2/h'>D_0n\}}\,\,\mathrm{d} w
 \le Ce^{-D_0n/(4c)}.
\]
Integrating the first transition probability therefore bounds
\eqref{br5:insertion-cancel} by
\[
 Ch^{-d/2}
 \exp\{[C_*-D_0/(4c)]n\}.
\]
This is uniformly bounded because $h\asymp n^{-3}$ and
$D_0/(4c)=C_*+1$.

If $r=T_0$, then $v=0$ and $s\ge T_0-h\ge1$.
The first density has a fixed supremum bound by
\eqref{br5:global-density}.
Integrating over $u$ and using the same Gaussian tail estimate
gives the preceding bound without the factor $h^{-d/2}$.
Thus the large-displacement contribution is uniformly bounded
in this case as well.

Combining the moderate and larger-displacement estimates shows
that each insertion, once its neighboring states are identified,
has uniformly bounded unconditional expected sampling work.
Linearity of expectation gives $O(m)$ for all such sampling work.
Merging the ordered grids, identifying neighboring states, copying
coincident states and writing the outputs requires $O(N+m)$
additional work.

Theorems~\ref{fw:upper} and~\ref{bridge:general} establish admissibility
of $A_F$ and $A_B$. Since writing each requested state has positive
cost, every admissible algorithm in either family satisfies
$\mathcal{W}_A\ge c_{\mathrm{out}}m$ for a fixed $c_{\mathrm{out}}>0$.
We first establish the additional lower bound in $t_m$ for unconditional diffusion sampling.

Fix $T$, $x$, the grid and an admissible unconditional exact diffusion algorithm $A$,
and let $\tau=t_m$. Nonemptiness of $\mathcal F_T$ supplies a matrix
$\Sigma_0$ satisfying
\[
 |(\Sigma_0)_{ij}|\le\Gamma_{\mathrm{coef}},\qquad
 \lambdaI_d\preceq\Sigma_0\Sigma_0^\top\preceq\LambdaI_d:
\]
take the value of an admissible diffusion coefficient at any one point.
The constant coefficient $\sigma(t,z)=\Sigma_0$ satisfies the same
bounds and has all positive-order derivatives zero. Set $v=(\Sigma_0\Sigma_0^\top)_{11}\le\Lambda$.

Suppose first that $\tau\ge1$ and let $N=\lfloor\tau\rfloor$.
Choose a fixed $\phi\in C_c^\infty((0,1))$ with positive integral
$\bar\phi$ and $\|\phi^{(r)}\|_\infty\le1$ for $0\le r\le k$,
extended by zero outside $(0,1)$. Such a function is obtained by
scaling any nonzero nonnegative smooth bump. Put
$\delta=\Gamma_{\mathrm{coef}}/2$ and $\mu=\delta\bar\phi>0$. For
$\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^N$, define
\begin{equation}\label{fw:sign-family}
 b_\varepsilon(t,z)=\delta e_1
       \sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\varepsilon_j\phi(t-j),\qquad
 \sigma_\varepsilon(t,z)=\Sigma_0.
\end{equation}
Here $e_1$ is the first coordinate vector. The temporal supports are
disjoint, all spatial derivatives vanish, and the drift is zero after
$N$. Thus every pair in this finite family belongs to $\mathcal F_T$.

Give the signs an independent fair prior, independently of the
algorithmic randomness. In the next calculations, expectations include
both sources of randomness. Let $S=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\varepsilon_j$,
and let $Y$ be the first coordinate of the algorithm's last output
minus $x_1$. Exactness for each member of the family gives
\begin{equation}\label{fw:sign-moments}
 Y\mid\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu S,v\tau),\qquad
 \mathbb{E}[YS]=\mu N,\qquad \mathbb{E} Y^2=\mu^2N+v\tau.
\end{equation}

Represent the algorithmic randomness by an independent sequence of
the allowed uniform and Gaussian draws, called the random tape.
Fixing this sequence and the query replies determines the execution.
Only the draws actually used are charged. Since the sign family is
finite and $A$ terminates almost surely on each member, there is a
common probability-one set of tapes on which all these executions
terminate.

For the convenience of the proof, reveal $\varepsilon_j$ whenever the algorithm
first makes a coefficient query at a time in $[j,j+1)$,
$0\le j<N$. This includes queries whose actual replies do not depend
on that sign; the additional information does not change the algorithm.
Let $N_R$ be the number of signs revealed, $S_R$ their sum, and
$\mathcal H$ the sigma-field generated by the complete query record,
these revelations and the full random tape. Each revealed sign
requires a distinct coefficient query, and $Y$ is
$\mathcal H$-measurable. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{fw:read-identities}
 \mathbb{E}[S\mid\mathcal H]=S_R,\qquad
 \mathbb{E} S_R^2=\mathbb{E} N_R.
\end{equation}
With the tape fixed, changing any unrevealed signs leaves every query
reply and hence the entire execution unchanged. Conditional on the
record and tape, these signs therefore remain independent and fair,
which proves the first identity. For the second, order the first
revelations and pad this sequence with zeros to length $N$.
Given the tape and the preceding revelations, the next previously unqueried
time interval, or termination without another revelation, is determined.
The sign in any such new interval is still independent and fair. The
padded increments consequently have conditional mean zero and are
orthogonal. Their sum is $S_R$ and their sum of squares is $N_R$,
proving the second identity. Spatially adaptive queries provide no
additional information because the coefficients in
\eqref{fw:sign-family} are independent of space.

Using \eqref{fw:sign-moments}, \eqref{fw:read-identities} and
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, we obtain
\[
 \mu^2N^2=(\mathbb{E}[YS_R])^2
 \le (\mu^2N+v\tau)\mathbb{E} N_R.
\]
Since $\tau/2\le N\le\tau$ and $v\le\Lambda$, then
\begin{equation*}
 \mathbb{E} N_R\ge\frac{\mu^2N^2}{\mu^2N+v\tau}
 \ge\frac{\mu^2}{4(\mu^2+\Lambda)}\,\tau.
\end{equation*}
Each revelation requires a coefficient query with positive cost,
separate from the output writes. The average expected work over the
finite sign family is therefore at least
$c_1\tau+c_{\mathrm{out}}m$, so some deterministic member has at
least this expected work. The constants depend only on the class and
primitive costs. This argument does not require finite expected work
of $A$: its output moments in \eqref{fw:sign-moments} are finite by
exactness, and $N_R\le N$.

For $0<\tau<1$, output cost alone suffices because $\tau+m\le2m$.
Combining the two cases gives, for every admissible $A$ and every requested grid,
\begin{equation}\label{fw:fixed-grid-lower}
 \sup_{(b,\sigma)\in\mathcal F_T}
 \mathbb{E}_{b,\sigma}\mathcal{W}_A(T,x,\mathbf t)\ge c(t_m+m).
\end{equation}
Together with Theorem~\ref{fw:upper}, this also proves
fixed-grid optimality. For the grid supremum, choose
$t_i=iT/m$, so $t_m=T$. The lower bound holds for every admissible
algorithm on this grid, while Theorem~\ref{fw:upper} gives
$\mathbb{E}\mathcal{W}_{A_F}\le C(T+m)$ uniformly over all allowed grids.
Taking the infimum over algorithms proves part~(i).

For bridges, fix $T,x,y$ and write $H=H_{T,x,y}$. The output cost
gives $\mathfrak R_B^{x,y}(T,m)\ge c_{\mathrm{out}}m$.
Theorem~\ref{bridge:general} gives
\[
 \sup_{\substack{(b,\sigma)\in\mathcal F_T\\
                 \mathbf t\in\mathcal G_B(T,m)}}
 \mathbb{E}_{b,\sigma}\mathcal{W}_{A_B}(T,x,y,\mathbf t)
 \le C_B\{H^Q\log(2+H)+m\}.
\]
Since $m\ge1$, the right side is at most
$C_{T,|y-x|}m$, where one may take $C_{T,|y-x|}=C_B\{H^Q\log(2+H)+1\},$
which proves part~(ii).
