\label{prop:pde-potential}
Let $u,v>0$ belong to $C^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^n)\cap\mathcal L_s(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and satisfy
\[
 (-\Delta)^s u=v^p,\qquad (-\Delta)^s v=u^q
 \qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^n.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pde-to-potential}
 u=K_s*v^p,\qquad v=K_s*u^q
 \qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^n.
\end{equation}
Conversely, every finite positive solution of \eqref{eq:pde-to-potential} solves the fractional system in the distributional sense; after the local regularity established below, the equations hold in the usual pointwise sense whenever the pointwise definition of the fractional Laplacian is used.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The argument is standard, but we include it for completeness. Let $G_R(x,y)$ denote the Green kernel of $(-\Delta)^s$ in $B_R$ with zero exterior data, and set
\[
 U_R(x)=\int_{B_R}G_R(x,y)v(y)^p\,\,\mathrm{d} y,
 \qquad x\in B_R,
\]
extending $U_R$ by zero outside $B_R$.  Then
\[
 (-\Delta)^sU_R=v^p\quad\text{in }B_R,
 \qquad U_R=0\quad\text{on }B_R^c.
\]
Thus $u-U_R$ is $s$-harmonic in $B_R$ and agrees with the positive function $u$ on $B_R^c$. The maximum principle therefore gives
\[
 0\le U_R(x)\le u(x).
\]
As $R\to\infty$, domain monotonicity of the Green kernels and the explicit ball Green function (see, e.g., \cite{ChenSong1998,RosOton2016}) give
\[
 G_R(x,y)\uparrow K_s(x-y)
 \qquad (x\ne y).
\]
Monotone convergence now yields
\[
 I_{2s}(v^p)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}K_s(x-y)v(y)^p\,\,\mathrm{d} y\le u(x),
\]
so this potential is finite.  Set
\[
 h_u=u-I_{2s}(v^p)\ge0.
\]
Since $K_s$ is the fundamental solution of $(-\Delta)^s$,
\[
 (-\Delta)^s h_u=0
\]
in all of $\mathbb{R}^n$ in the distributional sense.  Also $0\le h_u\le u$, hence $h_u\in\mathcal L_s(\mathbb{R}^n)$.  The Liouville theorem for nonnegative entire $s$-harmonic functions implies that $h_u$ is constant; see, for example, \cite{ChenLiOu2006,JarohsWeth2016,ZhuoChenCuiYuan2014}.  Thus
\[
 u=c_u+I_{2s}(v^p),\qquad c_u\ge0.
\]
The same argument gives
\[
 v=c_v+I_{2s}(u^q),\qquad c_v\ge0.
\]
In fact, both constants vanish. If $c_u>0$, then $u\ge c_u$, and hence
\[
 v(x)\ge I_{2s}(u^q)(x)
 \ge c\,c_u^q\int_{|y|>R}|x-y|^{2s-n}\,\,\mathrm{d} y=\infty,
\]
because the radial integral at infinity is a positive multiple of
\[
 \int_R^\infty r^{2s-1}\,\,\mathrm{d} r,
\]
which diverges for every $s>0$. This contradicts the finiteness of $v$. Thus $c_u=0$; the same argument gives $c_v=0$, and \eqref{eq:pde-to-potential} follows.

Conversely, applying $(-\Delta)^s$ distributionally to $K_s*F$ gives $F$, since $(-\Delta)^sK_s=\delta_0$.  Applying this with $F=v^p$ and $F=u^q$ proves the reverse statement.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $n\ge2$, $0<s<1$, and $p,q>0$. There do not exist finite continuous functions $u,v:\mathbb{R}^n\to(0,\infty)$ satisfying \eqref{eq:system} pointwise if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subcritical}
 \frac1{p+1}+\frac1{q+1}>\frac{n-2s}{n}.
\end{equation}

 \qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^n

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:pq}
Every positive finite continuous solution of \eqref{eq:system} satisfies $pq>1$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For $R>0$, set
\[
 m_u(R)=\inf_{B_R}u,\qquad m_v(R)=\inf_{B_R}v.
\]
Both quantities are positive. If $x,y\in B_R$, then $|x-y|\le2R$ and hence $K_s(x-y)\ge cR^{2s-n}$. It follows that
\[
 m_u(R)\ge cR^{2s} m_v(R)^p,
 \qquad
 m_v(R)\ge cR^{2s} m_u(R)^q.
\]
Combining the inequalities gives
\[
 m_u(R)^{1-pq}\ge cR^{2s(1+p)}.
\]
If $pq<1$, the left-hand side is at most $u(0)^{1-pq}$, while the right-hand side tends to infinity. If $pq=1$, the left-hand side is one and the same contradiction follows.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:source}
There exists $C$ such that every solution satisfies, for all $R>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-bounds}
 \int_{B_R}f\le CR^{n-2s-\beta},
 \qquad
 \int_{B_R}g\le CR^{n-2s-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L1-local}
 \int_{B_4}(f+g+u+v)\le C,
\end{equation}
and, for $x\in B_2$ and $0<\ell\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 \int_{|x-y|\ge\ell}K_s(x-y)(f(y)+g(y))\,\,\mathrm{d} y
 \le C\ell^{2s-n}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let
\[
 M_f=\int_{B_1}f,\qquad M_g=\int_{B_1}g.
\]
For $x\in B_1$, the kernel has a uniform positive lower bound on $B_1\times B_1$, hence
\[
 u(x)\ge cM_g,\qquad v(x)\ge cM_f.
\]
It follows that
\[
 M_f\ge cM_g^q,\qquad M_g\ge cM_f^p,
\]
and therefore $M_f\ge cM_f^{pq}$. By \cref{lem:pq}, $M_f+M_g\le C$. Applying this unit-ball estimate to the scaled pair \eqref{eq:scaling} and changing variables yields \eqref{eq:source-bounds}.

For $x\in B_4$, decompose the contribution to the potential from $\mathbb{R}^n\setminus B_8$ into annuli $2^j\le|y|<2^{j+1}$, $j\ge3$. By \eqref{eq:source-bounds}, the $g$-contribution is bounded by
\[
 C\sum_{j\ge3}2^{j(2s-n)}\int_{B_{2^{j+1}}}g
 \le C\sum_{j\ge3}2^{-j\alpha}<\infty,
\]
and similarly for $f$. On the near region, Tonelli's theorem and
\[
 \sup_{y\in B_8}\int_{B_4}K_s(x-y)\,\,\mathrm{d} x<\infty
\]
combine with the source-mass bounds to give \eqref{eq:L1-local}.

For the tail estimate, observe that $K_s(x-y)\le C\ell^{2s-n}$ whenever $|x-y|\ge\ell$. The part over $B_8$ is therefore bounded by $C\ell^{2s-n}$, while the annular estimate above bounds the contribution from $\mathbb{R}^n\setminus B_8$ by $C$. Since $\ell^{2s-n}\ge1$ for $0<\ell\le1$, this proves \eqref{eq:tail}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:regularity}
Let $(u,v)$ be a finite continuous positive solution of \eqref{eq:system}. Then $u,v,f,g$ are $C^1$ on every compact subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$. More precisely, if $2s\le1$, then after finitely many local bootstrap steps one may arrange
\[
 f,g\in C^{0,\theta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^n)
 \quad\text{for some }\theta>1-2s.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Fix $K\Subset K_1$ and choose $\chi\in C_c^\infty(K_1)$ equal to one near $K$. Decompose
\[
 u=K_s*(\chi g)+K_s*((1-\chi)g).
\]
The second term is smooth on $K$: derivatives of the kernel are uniformly dominated there by a fixed multiple of $K_s(x_0-y)$ for some $x_0\in K$, which is integrable against $g$ by \eqref{eq:potential}. For the compactly supported near part, the standard local fractional Riesz-potential estimate gives a gain of any H\"older order strictly smaller than $2s$ (with the usual Zygmund interpretation at integer thresholds). Since $u,v$ are strictly positive on $K_1$, the maps $t\mapsto t^p$ and $t\mapsto t^q$ are smooth on the ranges of $u,v$ there. Thus each regularity gain for $u,v$ is inherited by $f,g$ and can be iterated. A finite number of iterations gives $C^1$ regularity, and, when $2s\le1$, further iteration gives a H\"older exponent $\theta>1-2s$ for $f,g$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:virial-general}
Let $X\in C_c^1(\mathbb{R}^n;\mathbb{R}^n)$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PhiX}
 \Phi_X(x,y)=\frac{(X(x)-X(y))\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^2}.
\end{equation}
Then $\Phi_X\in L^1(\pi)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virial-general}
 -\int fX\cdot\nabla u
 -\int gX\cdot\nabla v
 =(n-2s)\iint\Phi_X(x,y)\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Because $X$ is Lipschitz,
\[
 |\Phi_X(x,y)|\le \|\nabla X\|_\infty
\]
when $|x-y|\le1$. Hence the diagonal singularity in $\Phi_X\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi$ is no worse than $|x-y|^{2s-n}$, which is locally integrable for every $s>0$. Away from the diagonal, at least one endpoint lies in a fixed compact set whenever $\Phi_X\ne0$, and the marginal identities \eqref{eq:marginals} give absolute integrability.

For $\varepsilon>0$, define
\[
 G_\varepsilon^g(x)=\int_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}\nabla K_s(x-y)g(y)\,\,\mathrm{d} y,
\]
and similarly $G_\varepsilon^f$. If $2s>1$, the gradient kernel is locally integrable and $G_\varepsilon^g\to\nabla u$ locally uniformly. If $0<2s\le1$, use \cref{lem:regularity} with $g\in C^{0,\theta}$ and $\theta>1-2s$. Since $\nabla K_s$ is odd,
\[
 \int_{\varepsilon<|z|<r}\nabla K_s(z)\,\,\mathrm{d} z=0,
\]
so, uniformly on a fixed compact set,
\begin{align*}
 |G_{\varepsilon'}^g(x)-G_\varepsilon^g(x)|
 &\le C\int_{\varepsilon'}^\varepsilon r^{2s-n-1}r^\theta r^{n-1}\,\,\mathrm{d} r\\
 &\le C\varepsilon^{2s+\theta-1}.
\end{align*}
Thus $G_\varepsilon^g\to\nabla u$ locally uniformly; the same holds for $f,v$.

Set
\[
 I_\varepsilon=-\int fX\cdot G_\varepsilon^g-\int gX\cdot G_\varepsilon^f.
\]
For fixed $\varepsilon$, Fubini is legitimate. Using
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grad-kernel}
 \nabla K_s(z)=-(n-2s)\frac{z}{|z|^2}K_s(z)
\end{equation}
and exchanging variables in the second term gives
\[
 I_\varepsilon=(n-2s)
 \iint_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}\Phi_X(x,y)\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y).
\]
The left side converges to the left side of \eqref{eq:virial-general} by the local uniform convergence of the truncated gradients. The right side converges by the absolute integrability already established.

one has

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:localization}
For every $\varepsilon>0$, there is $C_\varepsilon$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localization}
 |E_f-E_g|
 +\iint|\Phi(x,y)-\eta(x)+w(x)D(x,y)|\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)
 \le \varepsilon E+C_\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Fix $0<\delta<1/4$ and write
\[
 \ell(z)=\delta d(z)^m
\]
when $d(z)>0$. Call $(x,y)$ close if $|x-y|\le\ell(z)$ for at least one endpoint $z\in\{x,y\}$ with $d(z)>0$, and far otherwise. Since $d$ is one-Lipschitz, $d$ is comparable along the segment $[x,y]$ for a close pair.

The derivative matrix is
\[
 \nabla X(x)=\eta(x)\mathrm{Id}-w(x)\omega_x\otimes\omega_x.
\]
Taylor's formula and \eqref{eq:cutoff-estimates} give, for a close pair,
\begin{align}
 |\Phi-\eta(x)+w(x)D(x,y)|
 &\le C\delta d(z)^{N-2+m}\le C\delta d(z)^N,\label{eq:close1}\\
 |\eta(x)-\eta(y)|
 &\le C\delta d(z)^{N-1+m}\le C\delta d(z)^N.\label{eq:close2}
\end{align}
Since $d(z)^N\lesssim\eta(x)+\eta(y)$ on close pairs, the marginal identities give a contribution at most $C\delta E$.

For a far pair, every endpoint $z\in B_2$ satisfies $|x-y|>\ell(z)$. Using $0\le D\le1$, $|X(z)|\lesssim d(z)^N$, and \eqref{eq:cutoff-estimates},
\[
 |\Phi-\eta(x)+w(x)D|+|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|
 \le C\sum_{z\in\{x,y\}:d(z)>0}
 \left(d(z)^{N-1}+\frac{d(z)^N}{\ell(z)}\right).
\]
For the terms associated with $x$, \eqref{eq:tail} bounds the integral by
\[
 C\int_{B_2}f(x)
 \left(d(x)^{N-1}+\frac{d(x)^N}{\ell(x)}\right)
 \ell(x)^{2s-n}\,\,\mathrm{d} x.
\]
The two exponents of $d$ are
\begin{align*}
 N-1-m(n-2s)&=2n+1+2s(n+3)>0,\\
 N-m(n+1-2s)&=n-1+2s(n+3)>0.
\end{align*}
Thus the far contribution is bounded by a constant $C_{\delta}$. The $y$-terms are identical.

The marginal identities also give
\[
 E_f-E_g=\iint(\eta(x)-\eta(y))\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y).
\]
Combining the close- and far-pair estimates, the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:localization} is bounded by $C\delta E+C_\delta$. Choosing $\delta$ so that $C\delta\le\varepsilon$ proves the lemma.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:interval}
Let $I,J\subset\mathbb{R}$ be nonempty open intervals, possibly unbounded. Let $c_I,c_J\ge0$, with $I$ bounded whenever $c_I>0$ and $J$ bounded whenever $c_J>0$. Then
\begin{align}
 &\int_{I\times J}(c_I+c_J)\mathcal K\,\,\mathrm{d} s\,\,\mathrm{d} s'\nonumber\\
 &\qquad\le
 \int_{I\times J}\Bigl[
 c_I\mathcal K_I+c_J\mathcal K_J
 +\bigl((n-2s)\min(c_I,c_J)\mathcal D+|c_I-c_J|\bigr)\mathcal K
 \Bigr]\,\,\mathrm{d} s\,\,\mathrm{d} s'.\label{eq:interval-ineq}
\end{align}
All terms are nonnegative extended integrals; an endpoint term with zero coefficient is defined to be zero without evaluating its endpoint average.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
If at most one coefficient is positive, then $|c_I-c_J|=c_I+c_J$ and the result is pointwise. Suppose both are positive, so $I,J$ are bounded. Write $m_I,m_J$ for the midpoints and $d_0=m_I-m_J$. Integration by parts in the first variable gives
\[
 \int_I(\mathcal K-\mathcal K_I)\,\,\mathrm{d} s
 =-\int_I(s-m_I)\partial_s\mathcal K\,\,\mathrm{d} s.
\]
Since
\[
 \partial_s\mathcal K
 =-(n-2s)\frac{h}{\rho^2+h^2}\mathcal K,
 \qquad
 \partial_{s'}\mathcal K
 =(n-2s)\frac{h}{\rho^2+h^2}\mathcal K,
\]
adding the two integration-by-parts identities yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:midpoint-identity}
 \int_{I\times J}(2\mathcal K-\mathcal K_I-\mathcal K_J)
 =(n-2s)\int_{I\times J}
 \frac{h^2-d_0h}{\rho^2+h^2}\mathcal K.
\end{equation}
The pushforward of $\,\mathrm{d} s\,\,\mathrm{d} s'$ under $h=s-s'$ has density
\[
 \varrho(h)=|I\cap(J+h)|=F(|h-d_0|),
\]
where $F$ is nonincreasing. If $d_0\ge0$ and $h>0$, then $|h-d_0|\le|h+d_0|$, hence $\varrho(h)\ge\varrho(-h)$; the inequality reverses for $d_0\le0$. Pairing positive and negative $h$ against the odd function
\[
 h\longmapsto \frac{h}{\rho^2+h^2}\mathcal K(h)
\]
gives
\[
 d_0\int_{I\times J}\frac{h}{\rho^2+h^2}\mathcal K\ge0.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:midpoint-identity} is at most
\[
 (n-2s)\int_{I\times J}\mathcal D\mathcal K.
\]
Multiply by $c=\min(c_I,c_J)$. Each remaining individual endpoint defect is bounded by $\int_{I\times J}\mathcal K$ because its endpoint average is nonnegative. The leftover coefficients sum to $|c_I-c_J|$, which gives \eqref{eq:interval-ineq}.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:angular-errors}
One has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:he-mismatch}
 \mathbb{E}_e\iint|h_e(x)-h_e(y)|\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)
 \le C_\kappa\delta E+C_{\kappa,\delta},
\end{equation}
and, with
\[
 D_e(x,y)=\frac{((x-y)\cdot e)^2}{|x-y|^2},
\]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direction-error}
 \mathbb{E}_e\iint h_e(x)D_e(x,y)\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)
 \le
 \iint w(x)D(x,y)\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)+C\kappa E.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
On a close pair, integrate \eqref{eq:he-derivative} along the segment to obtain
\[
 |h_e(x)-h_e(y)|
 \le C_\kappa\delta d(z)^{N-n-1+m}
 =C_\kappa\delta d(z)^{N+2}
 \le C_\kappa\delta d(z)^N.
\]
The interaction marginals give a contribution $C_\kappa\delta E$. On far pairs, \eqref{eq:he-derivative} and \eqref{eq:tail} give, for the $x$-endpoint contribution,
\[
 C_\kappa\int_{B_2}f(x)d(x)^{N-n}\ell(x)^{2s-n}\,\,\mathrm{d} x,
\]
whose $d$-exponent is
\[
 N-n-m(n-2s)=n+2+2s(n+3)>0.
\]
Thus the far contribution is bounded by $C_{\kappa,\delta}$, proving \eqref{eq:he-mismatch}.

On the support of $h_e(x)$,
\[
 |D_e(x,y)-D(x,y)|\le2|e-\omega_x|\le2\kappa d(x).
\]
Average, use \eqref{eq:he-normalization} and $dw\le C\eta$, and apply the marginal identity to obtain \eqref{eq:direction-error}.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:layer-loss}
For every $P\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:layer-loss}
 \mathbb{E}_e(L_f(e)+L_g(e))
 \le
 \bigl(C_\kappa\delta+C_{\kappa,\delta}P^{-1}\bigr)E+CP.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
It is enough to treat the $f,u$ term. Fix $x$ with $1<|x|<2$, write $d=d(x)$ and $\ell=\delta d^m$. If a component $I$ has $|I|\le\ell$, then
\[
 \inf_I d\ge d-\ell\ge(1-\delta)d,
 \qquad
 |I|\le\delta d\le2\delta\inf_I d,
\]
so it is retained. The derivative estimate \eqref{eq:he-derivative} gives an entire weight loss at most $C_\kappa\delta d^N$.

If $|I|>\ell$, at least one of its two portions from $x$ contains a segment of length $\ell/2$. Let $F$ be the directions whose forward segment of that length remains in $\{f>t\}$. Polar integration centered at $x$ gives
\[
 \int_{B_{\ell/2}(x)}f
 \ge c\,t\ell^n\mathbb{P}_e(F).
\]
The long-interval event is contained in $F\cup(-F)$, hence, using \eqref{eq:L1-local},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:long-prob}
 \mathbb{P}_e\{|I|>\ell\}\le\frac{C}{t\ell^n}.
\end{equation}
For high levels $t\ge Pd^{-k}$, the expected long-interval loss is bounded by the supremum of $h_e(x)$ times \eqref{eq:long-prob}, hence by
\[
 C_\kappa d^{N-n}\,C\delta^{-n}P^{-1}d^{k-mn}
 =C_{\kappa,\delta}P^{-1}d^N,
\]
because $k=n(m+1)$. Integrating the short- and high-long losses in $t$ and then against $u(x)\,\,\mathrm{d} x$ gives
\[
 \bigl(C_\kappa\delta+C_{\kappa,\delta}P^{-1}\bigr)E_f.
\]

For the remaining levels, use \eqref{eq:he-normalization}:
\[
 \int_0^{\min\{f(x),Pd^{-k}\}}
 \mathbb{E}_e(h_e(x)-\chi_f(e,x,t))\,\,\mathrm{d} t
 \le Pd^{-k}w(x)
 \le CPd^{N-1-k}\le CP,
\]
since $N-1-k=n+1$. Integrating against $u$ and using \eqref{eq:L1-local} gives $CP$. The proof for $g,v$ is identical.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The integral system \eqref{eq:system} is the exact Riesz-potential form of
\[
 (-\Delta)^s u=v^p,\qquad (-\Delta)^s v=u^q.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:pde-potential} shows that the two formulations agree in the whole-space class used in this paper. More generally, the interval-pressure argument applies to any PDE solution class for which the same potential representation is available without a nonzero $s$-harmonic remainder.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
For a weighted layer $|x|^B u(x)^q>t$, the endpoint relation is $u=t^{1/q}|x|^{-B/q}$, so the endpoint value changes along the component and creates an additional short-interval error. In the present unweighted problem, every retained endpoint satisfies the exact identity $u_I=t^{1/q}$. This removes the endpoint-weight error from the pressure recovery step \eqref{eq:recover-pressure}. For the local weighted problem, see \cite{FazlyGhoussoub2014,HuangZou2026,OpenAI2026,Phan2012}.
