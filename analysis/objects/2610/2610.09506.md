            \item $\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }((\mathbf{T} f)(X)) =k\mathbb{E}_{\{u,v\}\sim\pi_{1}^{\sigma}}(f(\{u,v\}))$,
            \item $\operatorname{Var}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }((\mathbf{T} f)(X)) =\operatorname{Var}_{\eta\sim\pi_{k}^{\sigma}}(\sum_{\{u,v\}\in \eta}f(\{u,v\}))$,

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
First we prove claim \emph{(i)}. Let $\eta'\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}^{(\sigma)}(k)$ be fixed. Since $\eta'$ is a completion of $\sigma$, each $v\in V$ has exactly $\mathrm{res}_{\sigma}(v)$ incident edges in the graph $\eta'$. Assign these edges bijectively to the labeled stubs $(v,1),\dotsc,(v,\mathrm{res}_{\sigma}(v))$. There are $\mathrm{res}_{\sigma}(v)!$ such assignments at $v$, and the assignments at different vertices can be chosen independently. After choosing these assignments, each edge $\{u,v\}\in \eta'$ has exactly one stub at $u$ and one stub at $v$. By pairing these stubs we obtain a valid pairing which projects to $\eta'$.
 
    Conversely, suppose $X\in\mathcal{Q}$ is a valid pairing which projects to $\eta'$. Then each edge $\{u,v\}\in \eta'$ is the projection of exactly one pair $\{\vec{u}, \vec{v}\}$ in $X$. This identifies the stub assigned to each endpoint of this edge, and therefore recovers each assignment from the preceding step as $X$ varies over all valid pairings projecting to $\eta'$.
    
    Therefore this construction defines a bijection from the set of valid pairings projecting to $\eta'$ to the set of assignments of edges to stubs; in turn,
        \begin{align}\label{eq:preimages}
            |\{ X\in\mathcal{Q} \,:\, \Phi(X) = \eta'\}| &= \prod_{v\in V} \mathrm{res}_{\sigma}(v)!.
        \end{align}    
    Since the right-hand side of~\cref{eq:preimages} does not depend on $\eta'$, claim~\emph{(i)} follows.

    For claim~\emph{(ii)}, it follows from statement~\emph{(i)} that the random variable $(\mathbf{T} f)(X)$ where $X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}$ has the same law as the random variable $\sum_{\{u,v\}\in\eta}f(\{u,v\})$ where $\eta\sim\pi_{k}^{\sigma}$. Additionally, it can be checked that
        \begin{align}\label{eq:expectation-sum-f}
            \mathbb{E}_{\eta\sim\pi_{k}^{\sigma}} \Bigl( \sum_{\{u,v\}\in \eta} f(\{u,v\}) \Bigr) &= \sum_{\{u,v\}\in \mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}^{(\sigma)}(1)} \mathbb{P}_{\eta\sim \pi^{\sigma}_{k}}(\{u,v\}\in \eta) f(\{u,v\})\notag\\
            &= k \sum_{\{u,v\}\in \mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}^{(\sigma)}(1)} \pi_{1}^{\sigma}(\{u,v\}) f(\{u,v\})\notag\\
            &= k \mathbb{E}_{\{u,v\}\sim\pi_{1}^{\sigma}}(f(\{u,v\})).
        \end{align}
    Thus claim~\emph{(ii)} follows, as does claim~\emph{(iii)} for similar reasons.

    Finally, we prove claim \emph{(iv)}. By definition, $p_{\alpha}=\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(\alpha\in X)$. Thus, after some rearranging, we have
        \begin{align*}
            \sum_{\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}}p_{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\alpha)^{2} &= \sum_{\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}} \Bigl(\sum_{\substack{X\in\mathcal{Q}\\\alpha\in X}}{\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}} (X)\Bigr) \widehat{f}(\alpha)^{2}\\
            &= \sum_{X\in\mathcal{Q}}{\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}} (X) \sum_{\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in X}\widehat{f}(\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\})^{2}\\
            &= \mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}} ((\mathbf{T}(f^{2}))(X))\\
            &= \mathbb{E}_{\eta\sim\pi_{k}^{\sigma}} \Bigl(\sum_{\{u,v\}\in \eta} f(\{u,v\})^{2}\Bigr)\\
            &= k \|f\|_{\pi_{1}^{\sigma}}^{2},
        \end{align*}
    where the final two equalities use the projection law and statement~\emph{(ii)}, applied to $f^{2}$.

    For the upper bound, let $A=\{X\in\mathcal{Q}:\alpha\in X\}$ and $B=\mathcal{Q}$, and take disjoint copies of these sets as the two vertex classes. For $X\in A$ and $Y\in B$, draw an edge $\{X,Y\}$ if there exists a stub $\vec{z}\in\mathcal{S}\setminus \alpha$ such that $\theta_{\vec{u},\vec{z}} X=Y$.

Therefore, for each $X\in A$, there are at least $2k-2-4\Delta^{2}$ edges incident to $X$ in the bipartite graph.

    Now let $Y\in B$ be fixed. If $\alpha\in Y$, then the degree of $Y$ is zero. If $\alpha\notin Y$, the stub $\vec{v}$ has a partner stub $\vec{z}\in\mathcal{S}$, i.e., such that $\{\vec{v}, \vec{z}\}\in Y$. The only possible neighbor of $Y$ is $X=\theta_{\vec{u},\vec{z}}Y$, and this is a neighbor precisely when $X$ is valid. Combining the two cases, the degree of $Y$ is at most one. 

    By counting the edges spanning both sides and using the degree bounds obtained above, it follows that
        \begin{align*}
            |A|(2k-2-4\Delta^{2}) &= p_{\alpha}|\mathcal{Q}|(2k-2-4\Delta^{2}) \le |B|=|\mathcal{Q}|,
        \end{align*}
    which, up to rearranging, is the upper bound in~\cref{eq:pa-bound}.

    For the lower bound, let $A'$ denote the set of all pairings $X\in\mathcal{Q}$ such that $\{v,u\}\notin\Phi(X)$, and let $B'$ denote the set of all pairings $Y\in\mathcal{Q}$ such that $\alpha\in Y$. Let $X\in A'$, and let $\{\vec{z},\vec{v}\},\{\vec{w},\vec{u}\}\in X$ denote the (necessarily distinct) pairs containing the stubs $\vec{v},\vec{u}$ in $X$. For each choice of a distinct third pair $\beta=\{\vec{u'},\vec{v'}\}\in X$ together with an orientation $(\vec{u'},\vec{v'})$, draw an edge $\{X,Y\}$ from $X$ to the pairing
        \begin{align*}
            Y &= (X\setminus\{\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\},\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\},\beta\})\cup\{\alpha,\{\vec{z},\vec{u'}\},\{\vec{w},\vec{v'}\}\},
        \end{align*}
    provided $Y\in\mathcal{Q}$ as written.

    For each $X\in A'$, \emph{a priori} there are $2(k-2)$ oriented choices for the pair $\beta$. By the same neighborhood count as in the preceding step, at least $2(k-2)-4\Delta^{2}$ choices satisfy the condition that $u'$ avoids the closed neighborhood of $z$ in $\sigma\cup \Phi(X)$ and $v'$ avoids the closed neighborhood of $w$ in $\sigma\cup \Phi(X)$. Each of these choices produces a valid pairing $Y$ via the surgery above: indeed, the two edges $\{z,u'\}$ and $\{w,v'\}$ are non-loops and absent from $\sigma\cup \Phi(X)$. Moreover, neither equals $\{v,u\}$ since $z,w\notin\{v,u\}$ by validity of $X$ and the absence of $\{v,u\}$ from $\Phi(X)$. Furthermore they cannot equal each other either: equality would require either $z=w$ and $u'=v'$, making $\beta$ project to a loop, or $z=v'$ and $u'=w$, contradicting the neighborhood conditions because $\{u',v'\}\in \Phi(X)$. Thus each of these choices gives a valid pairing $Y$ containing $\alpha$. For fixed $X$, the partners of $\vec{z}$ and $\vec{w}$ in $Y$ recover the ordered pair $(\vec{u'},\vec{v'})$, so distinct oriented choices of $\beta$ produce distinct neighbors. Hence the degree of each vertex $X\in A'$ is at least $2(k-2)-4\Delta^{2}$.

    For the opposite side, let $Y\in B'$ be fixed. Specifying the ordered stubs $\vec{z},\vec{w}$ determines their partners $\vec{u'},\vec{v'}$ in $Y$, and hence recovers both the original pairing $X$ and the orientation of $\beta$. There are at most $(2k)^{2}$ choices of these ordered stubs. Some choices may not give a valid inverse, but including them in the upper bound is unproblematic.
    
    By counting edges once again, it follows that
        \begin{align*}
            |A'|(2(k-2)-4\Delta^{2}) &\le |B'|4k^{2}=|\mathcal{Q}|p_{\alpha}4k^{2}.
        \end{align*}
    There are at most $\mathrm{res}_{\sigma}(v)\mathrm{res}_{\sigma}(u)\le\Delta^{2}$ pairs of stubs projecting to $\{u,v\}$, so the union bound and the upper bound in~\cref{eq:pa-bound} give 
        \begin{align}\label{eq:prob-vu-notin-fq}
            \mathbb{P}_{X\sim \pi_{\mathcal{Q}} } (\{v,u\}\notin \Phi(X)) &\ge 1-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2k-2-4\Delta^{2}} \ge 1-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{k},
        \end{align}
    and hence it follows that
        \begin{align*}
            |\mathcal{Q}| (2(k-2)-4\Delta^{2})(1-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{k}) &\le |\mathcal{Q}|p_{\alpha}4k^{2}.
        \end{align*}
    Rearranging and simplifying, we have
        \begin{align*}
            p_{\alpha} &\ge \frac{(2(k-2)-4\Delta^{2})}{4k^{2}} (1-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{k}) \ge \frac{1}{2k}(1-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{k})(1-\frac{4\Delta^{2}}{k}) \ge\frac{1-5\Delta^{2}/k}{2k}.
        \end{align*}
    Here, we used $4+4\Delta^{2}\le8\Delta^{2}$ and $(1-x)(1-y)\ge1-x-y$ for nonnegative $x,y$. This proves the lower bound in~\cref{eq:pa-bound} and completes the proof.

We first prove three separate estimates and then combine them at the end. First, we compare $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathbf{T} f, \mathbf{T} f)$ with $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{good}}(\mathbf{T} f, \mathbf{T} f)$. We claim that
        \begin{align}\label{eq:good-vs-all-energy}
            \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{good}}(\mathbf{T} f,\mathbf{T} f) &\le (1+\frac{20\Delta^{2}}{k})\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathbf{T} f,\mathbf{T} f).
        \end{align}
    To this end, fix $\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}$. By~\cref{lem:increment-lift-f}, it follows that
        \begin{align*}
            &\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}}(((\mathbf{T} f)(\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X)-(\mathbf{T} f)(X))^{2})\\
            &\quad=\sum_{(\vec{z},\vec{w})}\sum_{\substack{X\in\mathcal{Q}\\\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\},\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\}\in X}}\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(X)((\mathbf{T} f)(\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X)-(\mathbf{T} f)(X))^{2}\\
            &\quad=\sum_{(\vec{z},\vec{w})}\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}}(\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\},\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\}\in X)\times\Bigl(\widehat{f}(\{\vec{v},\vec{w}\})\\
            &\qquad+\widehat{f}(\{\vec{u},\vec{z}\})-\widehat{f}(\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\})-\widehat{f}(\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\})\Bigr)^{2}.
        \end{align*}
    In the last two expressions the outer sum is over ordered pairs of distinct stubs in $\mathcal{S}\setminus\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}$. The first equality groups pairings according to the partners of $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{u}$; pairings containing $\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}$ contribute zero. The same expression holds if we interchange $\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} $ with $\pi_{\mathcal{P}}$. Thus,
        \begin{align}\label{eq:increment-lift-f-bound-a}
            &\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}}(((\mathbf{T} f)(\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X)-(\mathbf{T} f)(X))^{2})\notag\\
            &\quad\le \Bigl(\max_{(\vec{z},\vec{w})}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}}(\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\},\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\}\in X)}{\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}(\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\},\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\}\in X)}\Bigr)\notag\\
            &\qquad\times\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}(((\mathbf{T} f)(\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X)-(\mathbf{T} f)(X))^{2}).
        \end{align}
    The claimed comparison~\cref{eq:good-vs-all-energy} therefore reduces to a uniform bound on the ratios appearing above.

    For any two disjoint pairs $\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\},\{\vec{z},\vec{w}\}\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}$ satisfying $\{u,v\},\{z,w\}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}^{(\sigma)}(1)$, we obtain from~\cref{lem:single-pair-marginals} and the proof of the upper bound appearing therein (applied with the first pair held fixed) that
        \begin{align}\label{eq:mu-sigma-two-pairs}
            &\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in X,\ \{\vec{z},\vec{w}\}\in X)\notag\\
            &\quad= \mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in X) \mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(\{\vec{z},\vec{w}\}\in X\mid\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in X)\notag\\
            &\quad\le \frac{1}{(2k-2-4\Delta^{2})(2k-4-4\Delta^{2})}.
        \end{align}
    This bound also holds if either pair does not appear in any valid pairing, since the joint inclusion probability is then zero.

    Separately, for the unrestricted law, fixing one pair leaves $2k-2$ stubs to pair, so
        \begin{align}\label{eq:nu-sigma-single-pair}
            \mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }(\{\vec{v}, \vec{u}\}\in X)&=\frac{(2k-3)!!}{(2k-1)!!}=\frac{1}{2k-1},
        \end{align}
    since the number of ways to partition a set of $2k$ objects (in this case stubs) into pairs is $(2k-1)!!$. Similarly, for a second pair $\{\vec{z},\vec{w}\}\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}$ disjoint from the first, one has
        \begin{align}\label{eq:nu-sigma-two-pairs}
            \mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }( \{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in X,\ \{\vec{z},\vec{w}\}\in X) &= \frac{(2(k-2)-1)!!}{(2k-1)!!} = \frac{1}{(2k-1)(2k-3)}.
        \end{align}
    Computing the ratio of~\cref{eq:mu-sigma-two-pairs} and~\cref{eq:nu-sigma-two-pairs}, we have
        \begin{align}\label{eq:nu-sigma-mu-sigma-ratio}
            \frac{(2k-1)(2k-3)}{(2k-2-4\Delta^{2})(2k-4-4\Delta^{2})} &= \Bigl(1+\frac{1+4\Delta^{2}}{2k-2-4\Delta^{2}}\Bigr) \Bigl(1+\frac{1+4\Delta^{2}}{2k-4-4\Delta^{2}}\Bigr)\notag\\
            &\le (1+\frac{5\Delta^{2}}{k})^{2}\notag\\
            &= 1+\frac{10\Delta^{2}}{k}+\frac{25\Delta^{4}}{k^{2}} \le 1+\frac{20\Delta^{2}}{k},
        \end{align}
    since $\Delta\ge1$ and $k \ge 10\Delta^{2}$. Substituting this uniform ratio bound into~\cref{eq:increment-lift-f-bound-a} and summing over all $\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}$ proves~\cref{eq:good-vs-all-energy}.

    The second estimate relates $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathbf{T} f,\mathbf{T} f)$ to $\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2}$. We claim that
        \begin{align}\label{eq:third-ingredient}
            \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathbf{T} f,\mathbf{T} f) &\le ( 1+\frac{1}{k} )\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2}.
        \end{align}
    To prove the claim we require an estimate on the variance of $\mathbf{T} f$ over $\pi_{\mathcal{P}}$. A slightly more generic estimate works here. Suppose $g\in\mathbb{R}^{\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}}$. We claim that 
        \begin{align}\label{eq:variance-bound-g}
            \operatorname{Var}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} } \Bigl(\sum_{\alpha\in X} g(\alpha) \Bigr) &\le \frac{2k-2}{(2k-1)(2k-3)} \|g\|_{2}^{2}.
        \end{align}
    It suffices to prove~\cref{eq:variance-bound-g} in the case $\sum_{\alpha}g(\alpha)=0$. Observe first that
        \begin{align}\label{eq:disjoint-pairs-identity}
            \sum_{\substack{(\alpha,\beta)\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}\times\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}\\\alpha\cap\beta=\varnothing}} g(\alpha)g(\beta) &= \Bigl(\sum_{\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}} g(\alpha)\Bigr)^{2}  + \|g\|_{2}^{2} - \sum_{\vec{v} \in\mathcal{S}}\Bigl( \sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2} \\ \vec{v}\in\alpha}} g(\alpha) \Bigr)^{2},
        \end{align}
    where the first sum is over all ordered pairs $(\alpha,\beta)$ of disjoint pairs of stubs. To see this, expand the rightmost term:
        \begin{align*}
            \sum_{\vec{v} \in\mathcal{S}}\Bigl(\sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2} \\ \vec{v}\in\alpha}} g(\alpha) \Bigr)^{2} &= \sum_{\vec{v} \in\mathcal{S}} \sum_{\substack{(\alpha,\beta)\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}\times\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}\\\vec{v}\in\alpha\cap\beta}} g(\alpha)g(\beta).
        \end{align*}  
    The coefficient of $g(\alpha)g(\beta)$ in this expansion is $|\alpha\cap\beta|$, which equals $1$ when the pairs share exactly one stub, $2$ when they coincide, and $0$ when they are disjoint. Therefore~\cref{eq:disjoint-pairs-identity} follows upon inspection. Since we assume that $\sum_{\alpha} g(\alpha) = 0$, the first term on the right-hand side vanishes; the sum of squares is nonnegative, so it follows that
        \begin{align}\label{eq:disjoint-pairs-inequality}
            \sum_{\substack{(\alpha,\beta)\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}\times\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}\\\alpha\cap\beta=\varnothing}} g(\alpha)g(\beta) &\le \|g\|_{2}^{2}.
        \end{align}
    By~\cref{eq:nu-sigma-single-pair}, we have
        \begin{align*}
            \mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }\Bigl(\sum_{\alpha\in X}g(\alpha)\Bigr) &= \frac{1}{2k-1}\sum_{\alpha} g(\alpha)=0.
        \end{align*}
    Expanding the square, we obtain
        \begin{align*}
            \operatorname{Var}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}\Bigl(\sum_{\alpha\in X}g(\alpha)\Bigr) &= \mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}\Bigl(\Bigl(\sum_{\alpha\in X}g(\alpha)\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)\\
            &= \sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}}\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}(\alpha,\beta\in X)\,g(\alpha)g(\beta)\\
            &= \frac{\|g\|_{2}^{2}}{2k-1}+\frac{1}{(2k-1)(2k-3)}\sum_{\alpha\cap\beta=\varnothing}g(\alpha)g(\beta).
        \end{align*}
    The last equality separates the diagonal terms $\alpha=\beta$ and uses the fact that distinct pairs sharing a stub cannot both belong to $X$. Applying~\cref{eq:disjoint-pairs-inequality} now gives
        \begin{align*}
            \operatorname{Var}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}\Bigl(\sum_{\alpha\in X}g(\alpha)\Bigr) &\le \Bigl(\frac{1}{2k-1}+\frac{1}{(2k-1)(2k-3)}\Bigr)\|g\|_{2}^{2}\\
            &= \frac{2k-2}{(2k-1)(2k-3)}\|g\|_{2}^{2}.
        \end{align*}
    Thus~\cref{eq:variance-bound-g} holds for all $g$, and hence if $g=\widehat{f}$, we have
        \begin{align}\label{eq:variance-bound-lift-f}
            \operatorname{Var}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }((\mathbf{T} f)(X)) &\le \frac{2k-2}{(2k-1)(2k-3)}\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2}.
        \end{align}

    Next we transfer~\cref{eq:variance-bound-lift-f} to an energy bound by computing the unrestricted quadratic form for $\mathbf{T} f$. By~\cref{eq:nu-sigma-single-pair} and the definition of $\mathbf{T} f$, it follows that
        \begin{align*}
            \mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }((\mathbf{T} f)(Y)) &= \sum_{\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}} \widehat{f}(\alpha)\mathbb{P}_{Y\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }(\alpha\in Y) = \frac{1}{2k-1}\sum_{\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}}\widehat{f}(\alpha).
        \end{align*}
    Therefore, using the preceding and~\cref{lem:gen-lift}, we have
        \begin{align}\label{eq:gen-lift-f-variance}
            (\mathbf{L}\mathbf{T} f)(X) &=(2k-1)(\mathbf{T} f)(X)-\sum_{\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\notag\\
            &=(2k-1)(\mathbf{T} f)(X) -(2k-1)\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }((\mathbf{T} f)(Y))\notag\\
            &=(2k-1)((\mathbf{T} f)(X)-\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }((\mathbf{T} f)(Y))),
        \end{align}
    where $X\in\mathcal{P}$ is fixed. Consequently, by applying~\cref{eq:variance-bound-lift-f} and~\cref{eq:gen-lift-f-variance}, we have that
        \begin{align*}
            \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathbf{T} f,\mathbf{T} f) &= \langle\mathbf{T} f,\mathbf{L}\mathbf{T} f\rangle_{\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }\\
            &= (2k-1)( \mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }((\mathbf{T} f)(X)^{2}) -(\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }((\mathbf{T} f)(X)))^{2})\\
            &= (2k-1)\operatorname{Var}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{P}} }((\mathbf{T} f)(X))\\
            &\le \frac{2k-2}{2k-3}\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2}\\
            &= ( 1+\frac{1}{2k-3} )\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2}\\
            &\le ( 1+\frac{1}{k} )\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2}.
        \end{align*}
    The last inequality uses $k\ge3$, which follows from our assumption $k\ge 10\Delta^{2}$. This proves~\cref{eq:third-ingredient}.

    Finally, we bound $\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2}$. By~\cref{lem:uniform-projection-lift}\emph{(iv)} and the lower bound in~\cref{lem:single-pair-marginals}, which applies to each pair $\alpha$ with $\widehat{f}(\alpha)\ne0$, it follows that
        \begin{align*}
            \|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2} &\le \frac{2k}{1-5\Delta^{2}/k} \sum_{\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}} p_{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\alpha)^{2} = \frac{2k^{2}}{1-5\Delta^{2}/k}\|f\|_{\pi_{1}^{\sigma}}^{2}.
        \end{align*}
    Since $k\ge10\Delta^{2}$, we have $0\le5\Delta^{2}/k\le1/2$. Using $(1-x)^{-1}\le1+2x$ for $0\le x\le1/2$, we have
        \begin{align}\label{eq:hat-f-norm-bound}
            \|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2} &\le 2k^{2}(1+\frac{10\Delta^{2}}{k})\|f\|_{\pi_{1}^{\sigma}}^{2}\notag\\
            &= (2k+20\Delta^{2})k\|f\|_{\pi_{1}^{\sigma}}^{2}.
        \end{align}

    It remains to put all three ingredients together. Combining~\cref{eq:good-vs-all-energy,eq:third-ingredient}, we obtain
        \begin{align*}
            \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{good}}(\mathbf{T} f,\mathbf{T} f) &\le (1+\frac{20\Delta^{2}}{k})\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathbf{T} f,\mathbf{T} f)\\
            &\le (1+\frac{20\Delta^{2}}{k})(1+\frac{1}{k})\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2}\\
            &= (1+\frac{1}{k}+\frac{20\Delta^{2}}{k}+\frac{20\Delta^{2}}{k^{2}})\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2}\\
            &\le (1+\frac{50\Delta^{2}}{k})\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{2}.
        \end{align*}
    Now we use~\cref{eq:hat-f-norm-bound} to get
        \begin{align*}
            \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{good}}(\mathbf{T} f,\mathbf{T} f) &\le (1+\frac{50\Delta^{2}}{k}) (2k+20\Delta^{2})k\|f\|_{\pi_{1}^{\sigma}}^{2}\\
            &= (2k+120\Delta^{2}+\frac{1000\Delta^{4}}{k}) k\|f\|_{\pi_{1}^{\sigma}}^{2}\\
            &\le (2k+220\Delta^{2})k\|f\|_{\pi_{1}^{\sigma}}^{2}.
        \end{align*}
    The last inequality uses $k\ge10\Delta^{2}$. This proves~\cref{eq:lemma-bound-1}.

The operator $\mathbf{L}_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{P}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{Q}}$ is the part of the Laplacian sum over neighbors outside the valid set. In particular we have that
        \begin{align}\label{eq:gen-bad-norm-1}
            \|\mathbf{L}_{2}\mathbf{T} f\|_{\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }^{2} &= \mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }( ((\mathbf{L}_{2} \mathbf{T} f)(X))^{2})\notag\\
            &= \frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }\Bigl( \Bigl(\sum_{\substack{\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}\\ \theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X\notin\mathcal{Q}}} (\mathbf{T} f (X)-\mathbf{T} f (\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X)) \Bigr)^{2}\Bigr).
        \end{align}
    It follows from~\cref{lem:increment-lift-f} that
        \begin{align*}
            (\mathbf{T} f)(X)-(\mathbf{T} f)(\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X) &= \sum_{\alpha\in X\setminus \theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X}\widehat{f}(\alpha)-\sum_{\alpha\in \theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X\setminus X}\widehat{f}(\alpha).
        \end{align*}
    The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality then gives
        \begin{align}\label{eq:lift-f-difference-squared}
            ((\mathbf{T} f)(\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X)-(\mathbf{T} f)(X))^{2} &\le 4\sum_{\alpha\in X\setminus \theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X}\widehat{f}(\alpha)^{2} + 4\sum_{\alpha\in \theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X\setminus X}\widehat{f}(\alpha)^{2}.
        \end{align}
    To use this in~\cref{eq:gen-bad-norm-1}, we first claim that for each $X\in\mathcal{Q}$, at most $8k\Delta^{2}$ transpositions produce an invalid pairing. Fix $X\in\mathcal{Q}$ and a stub $\vec{v}$, and let $\vec{z}$ denote its partner in $X$. We count the choices of a second stub $\vec{u}\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\{\vec{v}\}$ for which $\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X\notin\mathcal{Q}$.

    If $\vec{u}=\vec{z}$, then the transposition interchanges two partners and leaves $X$ unchanged. Otherwise, let $\vec{w}$ denote the partner of $\vec{u}$ in $X$. These four stubs are distinct, although their underlying vertices need not be. The transposition $\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}$ replaces $\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\}$ and $\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\}$ with $\{\vec{u},\vec{z}\}$ and $\{\vec{v},\vec{w}\}$, leaving all other pairs unchanged.

    This transposition can be invalid only if $u$ belongs to the closed neighborhood of $z$ or $w$ belongs to the closed neighborhood of $v$ in $\sigma\cup \Phi(X)$. Each closed neighborhood contains at most $\Delta+1$ vertices, and hence at most $\Delta(\Delta+1)\le2\Delta^{2}$ stubs. For the second condition, there are at most $2\Delta^{2}$ choices of $\vec{w}$, each of which uniquely determines its partner $\vec{u}$ in $X$. Consequently, for each $\vec{v}$, at most $4\Delta^{2}$ choices of $\vec{u}$ give an invalid transposition. Summing over the $2k$ choices of $\vec{v}$ gives at most $(2k)(4\Delta^{2})=8k\Delta^{2}$ invalid transpositions in total.

    Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to~\cref{eq:gen-bad-norm-1} and using this bound, we obtain
        \begin{align*}
            \|\mathbf{L}_{2}\mathbf{T} f\|_{\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }^{2} &\le 2 k\Delta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }\Bigl( \sum_{\substack{\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}\\ \theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X\notin\mathcal{Q}}} ((\mathbf{T} f)(\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X)-(\mathbf{T} f)(X))^{2} \Bigr).
        \end{align*}
    Then we use~\cref{eq:lift-f-difference-squared} to obtain
        \begin{align}\label{eq:gen-bad-lift-norm-bound-intermediate}
            \|\mathbf{L}_{2}\mathbf{T} f\|_{\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }^{2} &\le 8k\Delta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }\Bigl( \sum_{\substack{\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}\\ \theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X\notin\mathcal{Q}}} \Bigl(\sum_{\alpha\in X\setminus\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X}\widehat{f}(\alpha)^{2}+\sum_{\alpha\in\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X\setminus X}\widehat{f}(\alpha)^{2}\Bigr)\Bigr).
        \end{align}
    For each $\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}$ and $X\in\mathcal{Q}$, let $N_{\alpha}^{-}(X)$ and $N_{\alpha}^{+}(X)$ count the unordered pairs of stubs whose transpositions produce an invalid pairing and, respectively, remove or introduce $\alpha$. Explicitly,
        \begin{align*}
            N_{\alpha}^{-}(X) &= \Bigl|\Bigl\{\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}:\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X\notin\mathcal{Q},\ \alpha\in X\setminus\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X\Bigr\}\Bigr|,\\
            N_{\alpha}^{+}(X) &= \Bigl|\Bigl\{\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}:\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X\notin\mathcal{Q},\ \alpha\in\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}X\setminus X\Bigr\}\Bigr|.
        \end{align*}
    Putting this into~\cref{eq:gen-bad-lift-norm-bound-intermediate}, we have the upper bound
        \begin{align}\label{eq:gen-bad-lift-norm-bound}
            \|\mathbf{L}_{2}\mathbf{T} f\|_{\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }^{2} &\le 8k\Delta^{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}} \widehat{f}(\alpha)^{2}\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(N_{\alpha}^{-}+N_{\alpha}^{+}).
        \end{align}
    We now handle the removal and introduction terms separately.

    For the removal term, if $\alpha\notin X$, then $N_{\alpha}^{-}=0$. If $\alpha\in X$, the preceding count gives $N_{\alpha}^{-}\le8\Delta^{2}$. Recalling that $p_{\alpha}=\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(\alpha\in X)$, we obtain
        \begin{align*}
            \mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(N_{\alpha}^{-}) &\le8\Delta^{2}\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(\alpha\in X) =8\Delta^{2}p_{\alpha}.
        \end{align*}
    For the introduction term in~\cref{eq:gen-bad-lift-norm-bound}, we will show that
        \begin{align}\label{eq:bad-introduction-count}
            \mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(N_{\alpha}^{+})&\le16\Delta^{2}p_{\alpha}
        \end{align}
    whenever $\widehat{f}(\alpha)\ne0$. Begin by fixing $\alpha=\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}$ with $\widehat{f}(\alpha)\ne0$, so $v\ne u$ and $\{v,u\}\notin\sigma$. If $\alpha\in X$, then $N_{\alpha}^{+}=0$. Otherwise, denote the partner of $\vec{v}$ in $X$ by $\vec{z}$ and the partner of $\vec{u}$ by $\vec{w}$. Exactly two transpositions introduce $\alpha$: $\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{w}}$ or $\theta_{\vec{u},\vec{z}}$. Both of these transpositions replace the original pairs by $\alpha$ and $\{\vec{z},\vec{w}\}$. Thus $N_{\alpha}^{+}$ equals two if this replacement is invalid, and zero otherwise. Splitting according to whether the graph edge $\{v,u\}$ is already present gives
        \begin{align}\label{eq:bad-introduction-split}
            \mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(N_{\alpha}^{+}) &=2\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(N_{\alpha}^{+}=2,\ \{v,u\}\in \Phi(X))\notag\\
            &\qquad+2\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(N_{\alpha}^{+}=2,\ \{v,u\}\notin \Phi(X)).
        \end{align}
    We will bound each probability by $4\Delta^{2}p_{\alpha}$.

    For the first probability appearing on the right-hand side of~\cref{eq:bad-introduction-split}, there are at most $\mathrm{res}_{\sigma}(v)\mathrm{res}_{\sigma}(u)\le \Delta^{2}$ stub pairs projecting to $\{v,u\}$. It can be checked that as long as $k\ge 10\Delta^{2}$, by~\cref{lem:single-pair-marginals}, it holds
        \begin{align*}
            \frac{1}{4k}&\le p_{\alpha} \le \frac{1}{k}.
        \end{align*}
    We use these simplified bounds solely for convenience. By the union bound, it holds
        \begin{align*}
            \mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(N_{\alpha}^{+}=2,\ \{v,u\}\in \Phi(X)) &\le\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(\{v,u\}\in \Phi(X)) \le\frac{\Delta^{2}}{k}\le4\Delta^{2}p_{\alpha},
        \end{align*}
    as desired.

    For the second probability appearing on the right-hand side of~\cref{eq:bad-introduction-split}, we compare the number of pairings with $N_{\alpha}^{+}=2$ and $\{v,u\}\notin \Phi(X)$ with the number of valid pairings containing $\alpha$, whose total count is $p_{\alpha}|\mathcal{Q}|$. As in the proof of~\cref{lem:single-pair-marginals}, we construct a bipartite graph and compare degrees on its two sides. Define the vertex sets
        \begin{align*}
            A &= \{X\in\mathcal{Q}:N_{\alpha}^{+}(X)=2,\ \{v,u\}\notin\Phi(X)\},\\
            B &= \{Y\in\mathcal{Q}:\alpha\in Y\}.
        \end{align*}
    Note that $|B|=p_{\alpha}|\mathcal{Q}|$.

    Fix $X\in A$, and as before denote the partner of $\vec{v}$ in $X$ by $\vec{z}$ and the partner of $\vec{u}$ by $\vec{w}$. For each third pair $\beta=\{\vec{u'},\vec{v'}\}\in X$, distinct from these two pairs, together with an orientation $(\vec{u'},\vec{v'})$, draw an edge from $X$ to the pairing
        \begin{align*}
            Y &= (X\setminus\{\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\},\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\},\beta\})\cup\{\alpha,\{\vec{z},\vec{u'}\},\{\vec{w},\vec{v'}\}\},
        \end{align*}
    provided $Y\in\mathcal{Q}$. As in the proof of~\cref{lem:single-pair-marginals}, $Y$ is valid if $u'$ avoids the closed neighborhood of $z$ in $\sigma\cup\Phi(X)$ and $v'$ avoids the closed neighborhood of $w$ in $\sigma\cup\Phi(X)$. Thus each $X$ under consideration admits at least
        \begin{align}\label{eq:q-choices}
            2(k-2)-4\Delta^{2}&\ge k
        \end{align}
    choices producing a valid pairing $Y$ containing $\alpha$, provided, e.g., that $k\ge 10 \Delta^{2}$. For fixed $X$, the partners of $\vec{z},\vec{w}$ in $Y$ recover the orientation $(\vec{u'},\vec{v'})$, so distinct choices produce distinct neighbors. Hence each vertex in $A$ has degree at least $k$.

    Now fix $Y\in B$. Specifying the ordered stubs $\vec{z},\vec{w}$ determines their partners $\vec{u'},\vec{v'}$ in $Y$. Reversing the three-pair exchange above then determines both $X$ and the orientation of $\beta$. We bound the number of choices for the ordered tuple $(\vec{z},\vec{w})$ which give rise to $X\in A$. Since $N_{\alpha}^{+}(X)=2$, both $\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{w}}$ and $\theta_{\vec{u},\vec{z}}$ are invalid relative to $X$. These transpositions have the same effect: they remove $\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\}$ and $\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\}$ and introduce $\alpha$ and $\{\vec{z},\vec{w}\}$. The new edge $\{v,u\}$ is permitted since $\widehat{f}(\alpha)\ne0$, and absent from $\Phi(X)$ by assumption. Thus invalidity must come from $\{\vec{z},\vec{w}\}$: either $z=w$ or $\{z,w\}\in\sigma\cup\Phi(X)$.

    We claim that either $z=w$ or else $\{z,w\}\in\sigma\cup\Phi(Y)$ also holds. If $z=w$ or $\{z,w\}\in\sigma$, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, $\{z,w\}\in\Phi(X)$, and this edge can be removed in forming $Y$ only if
        \begin{align*}
            \{z,w\} &\in \{\{u,w\},\{z,v\},\{u',v'\}\}.
        \end{align*}
    We check all three cases and rule out each one by exhibiting a contradiction. If $\{z,w\}=\{u,w\}$, then $z=u$, so the original pair $\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\}\in X$ projects to $\{v,u\}$, contrary to the assumption $\{v,u\}\notin\Phi(X)$. If $\{z,w\}=\{z,v\}$, then $w=v$, so the original pair $\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\}\in X$ projects to $\{v,u\}$, again a contradiction. Finally, if $\{z,w\}=\{u',v'\}$, then either $u'=z$ and $v'=w$, in which case the new pairs $\{\vec{z},\vec{u'}\}$ and $\{\vec{w},\vec{v'}\}$ project to loops, or $u'=w$ and $v'=z$, in which case they project to the same edge. Both possibilities contradict the validity of $Y$. Hence none of the three removed edges is $\{z,w\}$, and consequently $z=w$ or $\{z,w\}\in\sigma\cup \Phi(Y)$.

    Therefore, for each valid $Y$ containing $\alpha$, there are at most $2k-2$ choices for $\vec{z}$, and, for each choice, the closed neighborhood of $z$ in $\sigma\cup\Phi(Y)$ contains at most $\Delta(\Delta+1)\le2\Delta^{2}$ possible stubs $\vec{w}$, so there are at most
        \begin{align*}
            (2k)(2\Delta^{2})&=4k\Delta^{2}
        \end{align*}
    ordered choices of $(\vec{z},\vec{w})$. Consequently, each vertex in $B$ has degree at most $4k\Delta^{2}$.

    We now combine the degree bounds from both sides. Counting edges gives
        \begin{align*}
            |A|k &\le |B|4k\Delta^{2}=p_{\alpha}|\mathcal{Q}|4k\Delta^{2}.
        \end{align*}
    Therefore,
        \begin{align*}
            k|\mathcal{Q}|\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(N_{\alpha}^{+}=2,\ \{v,u\}\notin \Phi(X)) &\le4k\Delta^{2}p_{\alpha}|\mathcal{Q}|.
        \end{align*}
    Dividing by $k|\mathcal{Q}|$ gives
        \begin{align*}
            \mathbb{P}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(N_{\alpha}^{+}=2,\ \{v,u\}\notin \Phi(X)) &\le4\Delta^{2}p_{\alpha},
        \end{align*}
    as desired. Substituting this and the identical bound for the first term into~\cref{eq:bad-introduction-split}, we obtain
        \begin{align*}
            \mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }(N_{\alpha}^{+}) &\le2(4\Delta^{2}p_{\alpha})+2(4\Delta^{2}p_{\alpha})=16\Delta^{2}p_{\alpha},
        \end{align*}
    proving~\cref{eq:bad-introduction-count}. Combining the bounds on $\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}}(N_{\alpha}^{-})$ and $\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}}(N_{\alpha}^{+})$ for the pairs with $\widehat{f}(\alpha)\ne0$, we obtain from~\cref{eq:gen-bad-lift-norm-bound} that
        \begin{align*}
            \|\mathbf{L}_{2}\mathbf{T} f\|_{\pi_{\mathcal{Q}} }^{2} &\le 8 k \Delta^{2} (8\Delta^{2}+16\Delta^{2}) \sum_{\alpha\in\binom{\mathcal{S}}{2}}p_{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\alpha)^{2} = 192 k^{2} \Delta^{4}\|f\|_{\pi_{1}^{\sigma}}^{2},
        \end{align*}
    where the equality holds by~\cref{lem:uniform-projection-lift}\emph{(iv)}. This proves~\cref{eq:bound-2-lemma}.

The bound in~\cref{eq:covariance-bound} is unchanged by adding constants to $f$, since in particular for any $\eta\in \mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}^{(\sigma)}(k)$ and any constant $c$, it holds

        \end{align*}
    This proves~\cref{eq:tf-norm-bound}, which in turn proves~\cref{eq:covariance-bound}. The lemma is proved.

\label{lem:dirichlet-energy-decomposition}
    Let $\mathcal{X}$ be a pure $m$-dimensional simplicial complex, and let $1\le j\le \ell\le k\le m$ be fixed integers. Let $\sigma\in\mathcal{X}(m-k)$. Then the following facts hold.
        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
            \item For each $\eta\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k)$ and each $\rho\subseteq\eta$ with $|\rho|=k-\ell$, we have
                \begin{align}\label{eq:fact-1}
                    \frac{\pi_{k}^{\sigma}(\eta)}{\binom{k}{k-\ell}} &= \pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}(\rho)\pi_{\ell}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\eta\setminus\rho).
                \end{align}
        \end{enumerate}
    Furthermore, let $g\in\ell^{2}(\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k),\pi_{k}^{\sigma})$ be fixed. For each $\rho\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k-\ell)$, define $g^{(\rho)}(\eta')=g(\rho\cup\eta')$ for $\eta'\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}(\ell)$. Then
        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
            \setcounter{enumi}{1}

            \item $\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{j}^{(\sigma)}}(g,g)=\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{j}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}}(g^{(\rho)},g^{(\rho)}))$.
        \end{enumerate}

To prove claim~\emph{(i)}, fix $\eta\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k)$ and $\rho\subseteq\eta$ with $|\rho|=k-\ell$. There are exactly $|\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}(\ell)|$ facets of $\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}$ containing $\rho$. Since $\pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}$ is obtained by choosing a facet uniformly and then choosing one of its $(k-\ell)$-faces uniformly, we have
        \begin{align*}
            \pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}(\rho) &= \frac{|\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}(\ell)|}{\binom{k}{k-\ell}|\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k)|}.
        \end{align*}
    The measures on the facets of both links are uniform. Consequently,
        \begin{align*}
            \pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}(\rho)\pi_{\ell}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\eta\setminus\rho) &= \frac{|\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}(\ell)|}{\binom{k}{k-\ell}|\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k)|}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}(\ell)|} = \frac{\pi_{k}^{\sigma}(\eta)}{\binom{k}{k-\ell}}.
        \end{align*}
    This proves claim~\emph{(i)}.

    We next prove claim~\emph{(ii)}. By definition, the Dirichlet energy is given by
        \begin{align*}
            \mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{\ell}^{(\sigma)}}(g,g) &= \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\eta,\tau\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k)}\pi_{k}^{\sigma}(\eta)\mathbf{P}_{\ell}^{(\sigma)}(\eta,\tau)(g(\eta)-g(\tau))^{2}.
        \end{align*}
    To expand $\mathbf{P}_{\ell}^{(\sigma)}(\eta,\tau)$, note that $\mathbf{P}_{\ell}^{(\sigma)}$ sums over retained faces $\rho\subseteq\eta\cap\tau$ of size $k-\ell$. Each such face is chosen with probability $1/\binom{k}{k-\ell}$, after which the completion $\tau\setminus\rho$ is chosen with probability $\pi_{\ell}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\tau\setminus\rho)$. Expanding the kernel and grouping by $\rho$, we obtain
        \begin{align*}
            &\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{\ell}^{(\sigma)}}(g,g)\\
            &\quad= \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k-\ell)}\sum_{\substack{\eta,\tau\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k)\\\rho\subseteq\eta\cap\tau}}\frac{\pi_{k}^{\sigma}(\eta)}{\binom{k}{k-\ell}}\pi_{\ell}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\tau\setminus\rho)(g(\eta)-g(\tau))^{2}\\
            &\quad= \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k-\ell)}\pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}(\rho)\sum_{\substack{\eta,\tau\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k)\\\rho\subseteq\eta\cap\tau}}\pi_{\ell}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\eta\setminus\rho)\pi_{\ell}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\tau\setminus\rho)(g^{(\rho)}(\eta\setminus\rho)-g^{(\rho)}(\tau\setminus\rho))^{2},
        \end{align*}
    where the second equality uses claim~\emph{(i)} and the definition of $g^{(\rho)}$. By the change of variables $\eta'=\eta\setminus\rho$ and $\eta''=\tau\setminus\rho$, we have
        \begin{align*}
            &\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{\ell}^{(\sigma)}}(g,g)\\
            &\quad= \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k-\ell)}\Bigl(\pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}(\rho)\sum_{\eta',\eta''\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}(\ell)}\Bigl(\pi_{\ell}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\eta')\pi_{\ell}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\eta'')\\
            &\qquad\qquad\times(g^{(\rho)}(\eta')-g^{(\rho)}(\eta''))^{2}\Bigr)\Bigr)\\
            &\quad= \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k-\ell)}\Bigl(\pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}(\rho)\Bigl(\sum_{\eta'\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}(\ell)}\pi_{\ell}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\eta')g^{(\rho)}(\eta')^{2}\\
            &\qquad\qquad-\Bigl(\sum_{\eta'\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}(\ell)}\pi_{\ell}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\eta')g^{(\rho)}(\eta')\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)\Bigr)\\
            &\quad= \mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}}(\operatorname{Var}_{\eta'\sim\pi_{\ell}^{\sigma\cup\rho}}(g^{(\rho)}(\eta'))).
        \end{align*}
    Finally, we prove claim~\emph{(iii)}. Fix $\tau\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k-j)$ and $\rho\subseteq\tau$ with $|\rho|=k-\ell$. By the definition of the induced measures and the same facet count used in claim~\emph{(i)}, we have
        \begin{align*}
            \pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}(\rho)\pi_{\ell-j}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\tau\setminus\rho) &= \frac{\pi_{k-j}^{\sigma}(\tau)}{\binom{k-j}{k-\ell}}.
        \end{align*}
    For the energy average, we have
        \begin{align*}
            &\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{j}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}}(g^{(\rho)},g^{(\rho)}))\\
            &\quad= \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k-\ell)}\pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}(\rho)\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{j}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}}(g^{(\rho)},g^{(\rho)})\\
            &\quad= \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k-\ell)}\pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}(\rho)\sum_{\tau'\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}(\ell-j)}\pi_{\ell-j}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\tau')\operatorname{Var}_{\eta'\sim\pi_{j}^{\sigma\cup\rho\cup\tau'}}(g^{(\rho)}(\tau'\cup\eta')).
        \end{align*}
    The second equality applies claim~\emph{(ii)} within each link $\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}$, with deletion size $j$. By the change of variables $\tau=\rho\cup\tau'$ and the definition of $g^{(\rho)}$, we obtain
        \begin{align*}
            &\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{j}^{(\sigma\cup\rho)}}(g^{(\rho)},g^{(\rho)}))\\
            &\quad= \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k-j)}\sum_{\substack{\rho\subseteq\tau\\|\rho|=k-\ell}}\pi_{k-\ell}^{\sigma}(\rho)\pi_{\ell-j}^{\sigma\cup\rho}(\tau\setminus\rho)\operatorname{Var}_{\eta'\sim\pi_{j}^{\sigma\cup\tau}}(g(\tau\cup\eta'))\\
            &\quad= \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k-j)}\frac{\pi_{k-j}^{\sigma}(\tau)}{\binom{k-j}{k-\ell}}\Bigl(\sum_{\substack{\rho\subseteq\tau\\|\rho|=k-\ell}}1\Bigr)\operatorname{Var}_{\eta'\sim\pi_{j}^{\sigma\cup\tau}}(g(\tau\cup\eta'))\\
            &\quad= \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{X}^{(\sigma)}(k-j)}\pi_{k-j}^{\sigma}(\tau)\operatorname{Var}_{\eta'\sim\pi_{j}^{\sigma\cup\tau}}(g(\tau\cup\eta'))\\
            &\quad= \mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{j}^{(\sigma)}}(g,g).
        \end{align*}
    The second equality uses the identity proved above, and the third evaluates the inner sum as $\binom{k-j}{k-\ell}$. The last equality is claim~\emph{(ii)} with $\ell$ replaced by $j$. This proves claim~\emph{(iii)} and completes the proof.

We first claim that
        \begin{align}\label{eq:finite-local-variance}
            \operatorname{Var}_{\eta\sim\pi_{k}^{\sigma}}(g(\eta)) &\le C_{k}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{2}^{(\sigma)}}(g,g),
        \end{align}
    for some $C_{k}\ge 1$ depending only on $k$. We will then use~\cref{lem:dirichlet-energy-decomposition}.

    We first give a crude bound on $|\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}^{(\sigma)}(k)|$. Each $\eta\in \mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}^{(\sigma)}(k)$ consists of exactly $k$ edges such that $\sigma\cup\eta$ is a valid realization of $\mathbf{d}$. Any such completion can be obtained by pairing $2k$ labeled half-edges, with $\mathrm{res}_{\sigma}(v)$ half-edges assigned to each vertex $v\in V$. There are exactly $(2k-1)!!$ partitions of $2k$ objects, in this case half-edges, into pairs, so $|\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}^{(\sigma)}(k)|\le(2k-1)!!$.

    Next we claim that the graph of the walk $\mathbf{P}_{2}^{(\sigma)}$ is connected. Suppose otherwise, and choose facets $\eta,\eta'\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}^{(\sigma)}(k)$ in different connected components maximizing $|\eta\cap\eta'|$. Choose an edge $\{v,w\}\in\eta'\setminus\eta$. Since the two completions have the same degrees, by removing their common edges we have that
        \begin{align*}
            \mathrm{deg}_{\eta\setminus\eta'}(z') &= \mathrm{deg}_{\eta'\setminus\eta}(z')
        \end{align*}
    for each $z'\in V$. The chosen edge $\{v,w\}\in\eta'\setminus\eta$ makes the right-hand side positive at both $v$ and $w$. The left-hand side is therefore positive at these vertices as well, so we can choose edges
        \begin{align*}
            \{u,v\},\{w,z\} &\in\eta\setminus\eta'.
        \end{align*}
    Now $\{u,v\}$ makes the left-hand side positive at $u$. Applying the same equality at $u$ gives an edge
        \begin{align*}
            \{u,u'\} &\in\eta'\setminus\eta.
        \end{align*}
    Simplicity gives $u\ne v$ and $z\ne w$. Moreover, $u=w$ or $z=v$ would make one of $\{u,v\},\{w,z\}$ equal to $\{v,w\}$, contradicting their membership in $\eta\setminus\eta'$. Thus $u,z\notin\{v,w\}$, which also implies $\{u,u'\}\ne\{v,w\}$. The equality $u=z$ is allowed. Thus we have a walk $(u',u,v,w,z)$ on at most five vertices.             \mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{2}}(g,g) &=\sum_{\substack{\eta\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}(m)\\\{u,v\}\in\eta}}\pi_{m}(\eta)\sum_{\substack{\tau\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}(m)\\\{u,v\}\notin\tau}}\mathbf{P}_{2}(\eta,\tau) \le p\frac{2}{m}\frac{2}{3}=\frac{4p}{3m}.

        \end{align*}
    This proves the remaining bound.

If $K=0$, then $\mathbf{Q}^{r}(x,\cdot)=\pi$ for every $x$, and the conclusion follows, so we assume $K>0$. Fix $x\in\mathcal{Y}$ and write $\mu(y)=\mathbf{Q}^{r}(x,y)/\pi(y)$. Set $A=\{y\in\mathcal{Y}:\mu(y)< e^{1+8K}\}$. The function $u\mapsto u\log u - u + 1$ is nonnegative on $u\ge 0$ and satisfies $u\log u-u+1\ge 8Ku$ for each $u\ge e^{1+8K}$. Thus
        \begin{align*}
            8K\sum_{y\notin A}\pi(y)\mu(y) &\le\sum_{y\notin A}\pi(y)(\mu(y)\log \mu(y)-\mu(y)+1)\\
            &\le\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\pi(y)(\mu(y)\log \mu(y)-\mu(y)+1)\\
            &=D(\mathbf{Q}^{r}(x,\cdot)\,\Vert\,\pi)\le K.
        \end{align*}
    Consequently $a=\sum_{y\in A}\pi(y)\mu(y)\ge7/8$. Define $\nu(y)=\mu(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{y\in A\}}/a$. Then $\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi}(\nu(y))=1$, and
        \begin{align*}
            \frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\pi(y)|\mu(y)-\nu(y)| &=\frac{1}{2}(a(\frac{1}{a}-1)+1-a)=1-a\le\frac{1}{8}.
        \end{align*}
    Additionally, we have
        \begin{align*}
            \operatorname{Var}_{y\sim\pi}(\nu(y)) &\le\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\pi(y)\nu(y)^{2}\le \frac{e^{1+8K}}{a} \sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\pi(y)\nu(y)\le\frac{8}{7}e^{1+8K}\le e^{2+8K}.
        \end{align*}
    For each integer $s\ge1$, the triangle inequality and stationarity of $\pi$ give
        \begin{align}\label{eq:triangle-inequality-h-h0}
            \sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\pi(y)|\mathbf{Q}^{s}(\mu-\nu)(y)| &\le\sum_{y,z\in\mathcal{Y}}\pi(y)\mathbf{Q}^{s}(y,z)|\mu(z)-\nu(z)|\notag\\
            &=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Y}}\pi(z)|\mu(z)-\nu(z)|\le\frac{1}{4}.
        \end{align}
    By reversibility,
        \begin{align*}
            \pi(y)(\mathbf{Q}^{s}\mu)(y) &=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Y}}\pi(z)\mathbf{Q}^{s}(z,y)\mu(z)=\mathbf{Q}^{r+s}(x,y),
        \end{align*}
    and therefore
        \begin{align*}
            \|\mathbf{Q}^{r+s}(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}} &=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}} \pi(y) |(\mathbf{Q}^{s} \mu)(y) - 1|\\
            &\le \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi}(|(\mathbf{Q}^{s}\mu)(y) - (\mathbf{Q}^{s}\nu)(y)|) + \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi}(|(\mathbf{Q}^{s}\nu)(y) - 1|)\\
            &\le \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\pi(y) |(\mathbf{Q}^{s}\nu)(y) - 1|^{2} \Bigr)^{1/2},
        \end{align*}
    where the last inequality follows from~\cref{eq:triangle-inequality-h-h0} and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality. By reversibility and positive semidefiniteness, $\mathbf{Q}$ has an $\ell^{2}(\mathcal{Y},\pi)$-orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions with eigenvalues in $[0,1]$. Since $\nu-\mathbf{1}$ is orthogonal to the first such eigenfunction with eigenvalue $\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{Q}) = 1$, it follows that
        \begin{align*}
            \sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\pi(y) |(\mathbf{Q}^{s}\nu)(y) - 1|^{2} & \le \lambda_{2}(\mathbf{Q})^{2s}\|\nu-\mathbf{1}\|_{\pi}^{2} = \lambda_{2}(\mathbf{Q})^{2s}\operatorname{Var}_{y\sim\pi}(\nu(y)).
        \end{align*}
    Combining the preceding bounds, we obtain
        \begin{align*}
            \|\mathbf{Q}^{r+s}(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}} &\le \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{Q})^{s}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{y\sim\pi}(\nu(y))}\\
            &\le\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{2}\exp(1+4K-s(1-\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{Q}))).
        \end{align*}
    Taking $s=\lfloor4(K+1)/(1-\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{Q}))\rfloor$, we have $s(1-\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{Q}))\ge4(K+1)-(1-\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{Q}))\ge4K+3$, since $0<1-\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{Q})\le1$. The last expression is therefore at most $1/8+e^{-2}/2<1/4$, uniformly in $x$. Since $s\le4(K+1)/(1-\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{Q}))$, this proves the lemma.

We use the pairing construction from~\cref{sec:deep-link-expansion} with $\sigma=\varnothing$ and $k=m$. As in that setting, write $\pi_{\mathcal{P}}$ and $\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}$ for the uniform probability measures on $\mathcal{P}$ and $\mathcal{Q}$, respectively. Fix $X\in\mathcal{Q}$.
    
    Define the kernel $\widehat{\mathbf{P}}$ on $\mathcal{Q}$ as follows. From $X\in\mathcal{Q}$, choose $W\in\binom{X}{2}$ uniformly and replace its two pairs by a uniformly chosen pairing of their four stubs for which the resulting pairing belongs to $\mathcal{Q}$. For each such $W$, let $\mathcal{M}(W)$ be the set of all three pairings of these four stubs, and write
        \begin{align*}
            g_{X}(W) &=|\{W'\in\mathcal{M}(W):(X\setminus W)\cup W'\in\mathcal{Q}\}|.
        \end{align*}
    Since $X$ was valid to begin with, $1\le g_{X}(W)\le3$. The two selected stub pairs correspond bijectively to two selected graph edges. If those edges are vertex-disjoint, valid stub completions correspond bijectively to graph completions, each with probability $1/g_{X}(W)$. If the selected pairs are $\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\},\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\}$ with $v=u$, the matching $\{\{\vec{u},\vec{z}\},\{\vec{v},\vec{w}\}\}$ gives the same graph as the original matching, while the third matching contains the pair $\{\vec{v},\vec{u}\}$, which projects to a loop. Thus $g_{X}(W)=2$, and the probability of restoring the graph is $1/2+1/2=1$. Averaging over the selected pairs gives, for every $\tau'\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}(m)$,
        \begin{align*}
            &\sum_{\substack{Y\in\mathcal{Q}\\\Phi(Y)=\tau'}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}(X,Y)\\
            &\quad=\frac{1}{\binom{m}{2}}\sum_{W\in\binom{X}{2}}\frac{|\{W'\in\mathcal{M}(W):(X\setminus W)\cup W'\in\mathcal{Q},\ \Phi((X\setminus W)\cup W')=\tau'\}|}{g_{X}(W)}\\
            &\quad=\mathbf{P}_{2}(\Phi(X),\tau').
        \end{align*}
    The same projection identity holds for powers of the kernels, by induction. Let $s\ge 1$ be fixed. Then 
        \begin{align}\label{eq:pairing-projection-recursion}
            \sum_{\substack{Y\in\mathcal{Q}\\\Phi(Y)=\tau'}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,s+1}(X,Y) &=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{Q}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,s}(X,Z)\mathbf{P}_{2}(\Phi(Z),\tau')\notag\\
            &=\sum_{\tau''\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}(m)}\mathbf{P}_{2}(\tau'',\tau') \sum_{\substack{Z\in\mathcal{Q} \\ \Phi(Z)=\tau''}}  \widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,s}(X,Z)\notag\\
            &=\sum_{\tau''\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}(m)}\mathbf{P}_{2}^{s}(\Phi(X),\tau'')\mathbf{P}_{2}(\tau'',\tau')\notag\\
            &=\mathbf{P}_{2}^{s+1}(\Phi(X),\tau'),
        \end{align}
    where in the second equality we group the terms according to $\tau''=\Phi(Z)$.
    
    Now let $r\ge 1$ be an integer. Using~\cref{lem:uniform-projection-lift}\emph{(i)} and the identity~\cref{eq:pairing-projection-recursion}, the log-sum inequality gives
        \begin{align*}
            &D(\mathbf{P}_{2}^{r}(\Phi(X),\cdot)\,\Vert\,\pi_{m})\\
            &\quad=\sum_{\tau'\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}(m)}\mathbf{P}_{2}^{r}(\Phi(X),\tau')\log\frac{\mathbf{P}_{2}^{r}(\Phi(X),\tau')}{\pi_{m}(\tau')}\\
            &\quad=\sum_{\tau'\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}(m)}\Bigl(\sum_{\substack{Y\in\mathcal{Q}\\\Phi(Y)=\tau'}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,Y)\Bigr)\log\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{Y\in\mathcal{Q}\\\Phi(Y)=\tau'}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,Y)}{\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{Y\in\mathcal{Q}\\\Phi(Y)=\tau'}}{\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}}(Y)}\\
            &\quad\le\sum_{\tau'\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{d}}(m)}\sum_{\substack{Y\in\mathcal{Q}\\\Phi(Y)=\tau'}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,Y)\log\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,Y)}{\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(Y)}\\
            &\quad=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Q}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,Y)\log\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,Y)}{\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}(Y)}\\
            &\quad=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Q}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,Y)\log\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,Y)}{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(Y)}+\log\frac{|\mathcal{Q}|}{|\mathcal{P}|}\\
            &\quad\le D(\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,\cdot)\,\Vert\,\pi_{\mathcal{P}}).
        \end{align*}
    Next, define the kernel $\mathbf{K}$ on $\mathcal{P}$ by uniformly choosing two distinct pairs and resampling the pairing of their four stubs. Each of the three possible pairings is chosen with probability $1/3$, and all other pairs remain unchanged. Extend $\widehat{\mathbf{P}}$ to a Markov kernel on $\mathcal{P}$ by setting $\widehat{\mathbf{P}}(Z,\cdot)=\mathbf{K}(Z,\cdot)$ for $Z\notin\mathcal{Q}$. This leaves its laws started in $\mathcal{Q}$ unchanged. Every transition with positive probability under $\widehat{\mathbf{P}}$ also has positive probability under $\mathbf{K}$, and the same holds for their powers. Hence~\cref{lem:weak-triangle-inequality} gives
        \begin{align}\label{eq:relative-entropy-weak-triangle}
            D(\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,\cdot)\,\Vert\,\pi_{\mathcal{P}}) &\le2D(\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,\cdot)\,\Vert\,\mathbf{K}^{r}(X,\cdot))\notag\\
            &\qquad+\log\Bigl(1+\Bigl\|\frac{\mathbf{K}^{r}(X,\cdot)}{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}-\mathbf{1}\Bigr\|_{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}\Bigr).
        \end{align}
    We bound the two terms on the right-hand side separately.

    For the first term in~\cref{eq:relative-entropy-weak-triangle}, recall from the proof of~\cref{lem:invalid-transpositions}, with $\sigma=\varnothing$ and $k=m$, that the number of invalid transpositions from $X$ is at most $8m\Delta^{2}$. Each nonidentity replacement of two pairs is obtained by exactly two transpositions: for example, replacing $\{\vec{v},\vec{z}\},\{\vec{u},\vec{w}\}$ by $\{\vec{u},\vec{z}\},\{\vec{v},\vec{w}\}$ is obtained by $\theta_{\vec{v},\vec{u}}$ or $\theta_{\vec{z},\vec{w}}$. Each such replacement has probability $1/(3\binom{m}{2})$ under $\mathbf{K}$, so
        \begin{align}\label{eq:invalid-transitions-bound}
            \sum_{Y\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{Q}}\mathbf{K}(X,Y) &\le\frac{8m\Delta^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{3\binom{m}{2}}=\frac{8\Delta^{2}}{3(m-1)}\le\frac{8\Delta^{2}}{m}.
        \end{align}
    Conditional on selecting $W\in\binom{X}{2}$ for replacement, each of the three pairings of its four stubs is chosen with probability $1/3$ under $\mathbf{K}$, and each valid completion is selected with probability $1/g_{X}(W)$ under $\widehat{\mathbf{P}}$. Applying the log-sum inequality when grouping choices $(W,W')$ that give the same pairing yields
        \begin{align*}
            &D(\widehat{\mathbf{P}}(X,\cdot)\,\Vert\,\mathbf{K}(X,\cdot))\\
            &\quad=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Q}}\widehat{\mathbf{P}}(X,Y)\log\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{P}}(X,Y)}{\mathbf{K}(X,Y)}\\
            &\quad=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Q}} \Bigl(\sum_{\substack{(W,W')\\(X\setminus W)\cup W'=Y}}\frac{1}{\binom{m}{2}\,g_{X}(W)}\Bigr) \log\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{(W,W')\\(X\setminus W)\cup W'=Y}}\frac{1}{\binom{m}{2}\,g_{X}(W)}}{\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{(W,W')\\(X\setminus W)\cup W'=Y}}\frac{1}{3\binom{m}{2}}}\\
            &\quad\le \frac{1}{\binom{m}{2}}\sum_{W\in\binom{X}{2}}\sum_{\substack{W'\in\mathcal{M}(W)\\(X\setminus W)\cup W'\in\mathcal{Q}}} \frac{1}{g_{X}(W)}\log\frac{3}{g_{X}(W)}\\
            &\quad=\frac{1}{\binom{m}{2}}\sum_{W\in\binom{X}{2}}\log\frac{3}{g_{X}(W)}.
        \end{align*}
    Using $\log(3/u)\le3-u$ for $1\le u\le3$, followed by the bound in~\cref{eq:invalid-transitions-bound}, we obtain
        \begin{align}\label{eq:heatbath-local-kl}
            D(\widehat{\mathbf{P}}(X,\cdot)\,\Vert\,\mathbf{K}(X,\cdot)) &\le\frac{1}{\binom{m}{2}}\sum_{W\in\binom{X}{2}}(3-g_{X}(W)) =3\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{Q}}\mathbf{K}(X,Y)\le\frac{24\Delta^{2}}{m}.
        \end{align}
    By~\cref{lem:entropy-chain-rule}, for every integer $s\ge1$,
        \begin{align*}
            D(\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,s}(X,\cdot)\,\Vert\,\mathbf{K}^{s}(X,\cdot)) &\le D(\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,s-1}(X,\cdot)\,\Vert\,\mathbf{K}^{s-1}(X,\cdot))+\frac{24\Delta^{2}}{m},
        \end{align*}
    where we use~\cref{eq:heatbath-local-kl}. Applying this inequality repeatedly gives
        \begin{align}\label{eq:pairing-path-kl}
            D(\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{\,r}(X,\cdot)\,\Vert\,\mathbf{K}^{r}(X,\cdot)) &\le r\frac{24\Delta^{2}}{m},
        \end{align}
    since both initial distributions assign mass one to $X$.
    
    For the second term in~\cref{eq:relative-entropy-weak-triangle}, fix a labelling of the set $\mathcal{S}$ of all $2m$ stubs, and let $\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}$ denote the set of orderings of $\mathcal{S}$. Define the map $\Psi:\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\to\mathcal{P}$ according to
        \begin{align*}
            (\vec{v}_{1},\dotsc,\vec{v}_{2m}) &\mapsto\{\{\vec{v}_{1},\vec{v}_{2}\},\dotsc,\{\vec{v}_{2m-1},\vec{v}_{2m}\}\}
        \end{align*}
    for each $(\vec{v}_{1},\dotsc,\vec{v}_{2m})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}$. $\Psi$ identifies each ordering of the stubs with a pairing of the stubs by forgetting the order of the pairs and the order of the stubs within each pair. Each fiber $\Psi^{-1}(Z)$ of this map contains $(m!)2^{m}$ ordered presentations of $Z$, independent of $Z\in\mathcal{P}$. Set $p=\frac{2m-1}{3(m-1)}\in[2/3,1]$. Then $\frac{2p}{\binom{2m}{2}}=\frac{1}{3\binom{m}{2}}$, which is the probability assigned by $\mathbf{K}$ to each nontrivial transition. Define the kernel $\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}$ on $\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}$ by staying at the current ordering with probability $1-p$, and otherwise exchanging two uniformly chosen distinct entries.

    Fix an ordering $\widetilde{X}=(\vec{v}_{1},\dotsc,\vec{v}_{2m})$ with $\Psi(\widetilde{X})=X$, and let $t\ge3m\log(2m)$. For each ordering $\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}$, let $\varphi_{\widetilde{Y}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{2m}$ be the unique permutation satisfying
        \begin{align*}
            \widetilde{Y} &=(\vec{v}_{\varphi_{\widetilde{Y}}(1)},\dotsc,\vec{v}_{\varphi_{\widetilde{Y}}(2m)}).
        \end{align*}
    This gives a bijection between $\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}$ and $\mathfrak{S}_{2m}$ under which the initial ordering $\widetilde{X}$ corresponds to the identity permutation $\iota$.

    Let $\mathbf{Q}$ denote the random transposition kernel from~\cref{thm:random-transposition-l2}, with $N=2m$. Under the identification $\widetilde{Y}\mapsto\varphi_{\widetilde{Y}}$, exchanging entries $i$ and $j$ of an ordering corresponds to replacing $\varphi_{\widetilde{Y}}$ by $\varphi_{\widetilde{Y}}\circ(i\,j)$. Choosing the two entries uniformly therefore gives one step of $\mathbf{Q}$. Thus, after identifying the rows and columns by this bijection, $\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}=(1-p)\mathbf{I}+p\mathbf{Q}$. Since $\varphi_{\widetilde{X}}=\iota$, it follows that for each $\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}$,
        \begin{align*}
            e^{t(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}-\mathbf{I})}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}) &=e^{t((1-p)\mathbf{I}+p\mathbf{Q}-\mathbf{I})}(\iota,\varphi_{\widetilde{Y}}) =e^{pt(\mathbf{Q}-\mathbf{I})}(\iota,\varphi_{\widetilde{Y}}) =\mu_{pt}(\varphi_{\widetilde{Y}}),
        \end{align*}
    where in the last equality we recall the definition of $\mu_{t}$ in~\cref{thm:random-transposition-l2}. Since $p\ge2/3$ and $t\ge3m\log(2m)$, we have $pt\ge2m\log(2m)$. Thus, by~\cref{thm:random-transposition-l2},
        \begin{align*}
            \Bigl\|\frac{e^{t(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}-\mathbf{I})}(\widetilde{X},\cdot)}{\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}}-\mathbf{1}\Bigr\|_{\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}}^{2} &\le1.
        \end{align*}
    
    We now apply this estimate to the walk $\mathbf{K}$ on $\mathcal{P}$. For every $\widetilde{Z}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}$ and $Y\in\mathcal{P}$, we claim that
        \begin{align}\label{eq:pairing-projection}
            \sum_{\substack{\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\\\Psi(\widetilde{Y})=Y}}\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}(\widetilde{Z},\widetilde{Y}) &=\mathbf{K}(\Psi(\widetilde{Z}),Y).
        \end{align}
    Fix $\widetilde{Z}$ and write $Z=\Psi(\widetilde{Z})$. Suppose first that $Y\ne Z$ is obtained by replacing two pairs of $Z$. The two exchanges producing this replacement give two distinct orderings representing $Y$, each reached with probability $p/\binom{2m}{2}$. Thus, using $p=(2m-1)/(3(m-1))$, we have
        \begin{align*}
            \sum_{\substack{\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\\\Psi(\widetilde{Y})=Y}}\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}(\widetilde{Z},\widetilde{Y}) &=\frac{p}{\binom{2m}{2}}+\frac{p}{\binom{2m}{2}} =\frac{1}{3\binom{m}{2}}=\mathbf{K}(Z,Y).
        \end{align*}
    For $Y=Z$, the sum includes the original ordering, reached with probability $1-p$, and the $m$ orderings obtained by exchanging the two stubs within one pair, each reached with probability $p/\binom{2m}{2}$. Therefore
        \begin{align*}
            \sum_{\substack{\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\\\Psi(\widetilde{Y})=Z}}\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}(\widetilde{Z},\widetilde{Y}) &=1-p+\frac{pm}{\binom{2m}{2}} =\frac{1}{3}=\mathbf{K}(Z,Z).
        \end{align*}
    If $Y\ne Z$ cannot be obtained by replacing two pairs, both sides of the claimed identity are zero. For $X$ and its ordering $\widetilde{X}$ fixed earlier, the same induction as in~\cref{eq:pairing-projection-recursion} gives, for every integer $s\ge0$,
        \begin{align*}
            \mathbf{K}^{s}(X,Y) &=\sum_{\substack{\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\\\Psi(\widetilde{Y})=Y}}\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}^{\,s}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}).
        \end{align*}
    Therefore it follows from~\cref{eq:pairing-projection} that
        \begin{align*}
            e^{t(\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{I})}(X,Y) &=e^{-t}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s}}{s!}\mathbf{K}^{s}(X,Y) =e^{-t}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s}}{s!}\sum_{\substack{\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\\\Psi(\widetilde{Y})=Y}}\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}^{\,s}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}) =\sum_{\substack{\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\\\Psi(\widetilde{Y})=Y}}e^{t(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}-\mathbf{I})}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}).
        \end{align*}
    Since each pairing has $2^{m} m!$ ordered presentations, we have
        \begin{align*}
            |\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}| &=(2m)!=2^{m} m!\,|\mathcal{P}|,
        \end{align*}
    and, for each $\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}$ with $\Psi(\widetilde{Y})=Y$, we have
        \begin{align*}
            \frac{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(Y)}{2^{m} m!}&=\frac{1}{(2m)!}=\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}(\widetilde{Y}).
        \end{align*}
    Dividing the preceding transition identity by $\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(Y)$ therefore gives
        \begin{align*}
            \frac{e^{t(\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{I})}(X,Y)}{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(Y)}-1 &=\frac{1}{2^{m} m!}\sum_{\substack{\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\\\Psi(\widetilde{Y})=Y}}\Bigl(\frac{e^{t(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}-\mathbf{I})}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})}{\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}(\widetilde{Y})}-1\Bigr).
        \end{align*}
    Applying Jensen's inequality to the preceding average, we obtain
        \begin{align*}
            \Bigl\|\frac{e^{t(\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{I})}(X,\cdot)}{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}-\mathbf{1}\Bigr\|_{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}^{2} &\le\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(Y)}{2^{m} m!}\sum_{\substack{\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\\\Psi(\widetilde{Y})=Y}}\Bigl(\frac{e^{t(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}-\mathbf{I})}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})}{\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}(\widetilde{Y})}-1\Bigr)^{2}\\
            &=\sum_{\widetilde{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}}{\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}}(\widetilde{Y})\Bigl(\frac{e^{t(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}-\mathbf{I})}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})}{\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}(\widetilde{Y})}-1\Bigr)^{2}\\
            &=\Bigl\|\frac{e^{t(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}-\mathbf{I})}(\widetilde{X},\cdot)}{\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}}-\mathbf{1}\Bigr\|_{\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}}^{2}\le1,
        \end{align*}
    using the choice $t\ge3m\log(2m)$. Here, the first equality uses $\pi_{\mathcal{P}}(Y)/(2^{m} m!)=\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}(\widetilde{Y})$ and the fact that each ordering projects to exactly one pairing.

    It remains to pass from continuous time back to discrete time. The kernel $\mathbf{K}$ is symmetric, since each nonidentity transition has probability $1/(3\binom{m}{2})$. For $f\in\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P},\pi_{\mathcal{P}})$, let $W$ range over partial pairings with $m-2$ pairs. Each $W$ has three completions, and each pairing contains $\binom{m}{2}$ such $W$. Therefore
        \begin{align*}
            \langle f,\mathbf{K}f\rangle_{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}} &=\frac{1}{3|\mathcal{P}|\binom{m}{2}}\sum_{W}\sum_{\substack{Y,Z\in\mathcal{P}\\W\subseteq Y\cap Z}}f(Y)f(Z) = \frac{1}{3|\mathcal{P}|\binom{m}{2}}\sum_{W}\Bigl(\sum_{\substack{Y\in\mathcal{P}\\W\subseteq Y}}f(Y)\Bigr)^{2}\ge0.
        \end{align*}
    Thus $\mathbf{K}$ has eigenvalues in $[0,1]$. Let $\phi_{1},\dotsc,\phi_{|\mathcal{P}|}$ be an orthonormal basis of right eigenfunctions in $\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P},\pi_{\mathcal{P}})$, with $\phi_{1}=\mathbf{1}$ and corresponding eigenvalues $\lambda_{j}(\mathbf{K})$. For every integer $r\ge3m\log(2m)$, using $x^{2r}\le e^{-2r(1-x)}$ for $0\le x\le 1$ and evaluating the preceding heat-kernel bound at $t=r$, we have
        \begin{align*}
            \Bigl\|\frac{\mathbf{K}^{r}(X,\cdot)}{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}-\mathbf{1}\Bigr\|_{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}^{2} &=\sum_{j=2}^{|\mathcal{P}|}\lambda_{j}(\mathbf{K})^{2r}\phi_{j}(X)^{2}\\
            &\le\sum_{j=2}^{|\mathcal{P}|}e^{-2r(1-\lambda_{j}(\mathbf{K}))}\phi_{j}(X)^{2}\\
            &=\Bigl\|\frac{e^{r(\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{I})}(X,\cdot)}{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}-\mathbf{1}\Bigr\|_{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}\le1.
        \end{align*}
    Thus the second term in~\cref{eq:relative-entropy-weak-triangle} satisfies
        \begin{align*}
            \log\Bigl(1+\Bigl\|\frac{\mathbf{K}^{r}(X,\cdot)}{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}-\mathbf{1}\Bigr\|_{\pi_{\mathcal{P}}}^{2}\Bigr) &\le\log2\le1.
        \end{align*}
    Combining this with~\cref{eq:relative-entropy-weak-triangle,eq:pairing-path-kl} and the entropy projection estimate at the start of the proof gives
        \begin{align*}
            D(\mathbf{P}_{2}^{r}(\Phi(X),\cdot)\,\Vert\,\pi_{m}) &\le2r\frac{24\Delta^{2}}{m}+1.
        \end{align*}
    The preceding upper bound increases with $r$, so we first bound it on a finite interval and then use entropy monotonicity to cover all later times. For every integer $3m\log(2m)\le r\le4m\log(2m)$, we have
        \begin{align*}
            2r\frac{24\Delta^{2}}{m}+1 &\le192\Delta^{2}\log(2m)+1\le193\Delta^{2}\log(2m).
        \end{align*}
    This interval contains $r_{0}=\lceil3m\log(2m)\rceil$, since $m\ge2$. Finally, stationarity of $\pi_{m}$ and~\cref{lem:entropy-chain-rule}, applied with both kernels equal to $\mathbf{P}_{2}$, give
        \begin{align*}
            D(\mathbf{P}_{2}^{s+1}(\tau,\cdot)\,\Vert\,\pi_{m}) &\le D(\mathbf{P}_{2}^{s}(\tau,\cdot)\,\Vert\,\pi_{m})
        \end{align*}
    for every realization $\tau$ and integer $s\ge0$. Thus the bound at $r_{0}$ holds at every later integer time. Since $X\in\mathcal{Q}$ was arbitrary and every realization has a preimage under $\Phi$, this proves~\cref{prop:two-edge-entropy-bound}.
