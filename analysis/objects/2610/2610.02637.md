<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:jet-ident}
Assume $f,h\in C^{k+1}$ and that $\Phi$ is an embedding. 
Then there is a continuous (and smooth when $\mathcal{A}$ is a smooth invariant manifold) function $G_k:\Phi(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{R}$ with $x^{(k)}(t)=G_k(\mathbf{y}_t)$ for all $t$, namely $G_k=(\mathcal{L}_f^{\,k}h)\circ\Phi^{-1}$, where $\mathcal{L}_f$ is the Lie derivative along $f$. 
No smallness condition on $\tau$ enters: the delay step is constrained only by the genericity of the embedding.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:jet-chart}
For generic $(h,f)$ and $p\geq 2d$, the derivative map
$$\Theta(s)=\big(h(s),\,(\mathcal{L}_f h)(s),\,\dots,\,(\mathcal{L}_f^{\,p}h)(s)\big)$$
is itself an embedding of $\mathcal{A}$ and is the classical differential embedding, and the observability map of the system~\citep{takens1981, packard1980, Whalen2015Observability}. 
The full lift $G=(G_0,\dots,G_p)=\Theta\circ\Phi^{-1}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-ident} is therefore the transition map between two generic charts of the same attractor: the delay chart, which is what a scalar sensor measures, and the jet chart, in which a system that is $n$th-order in the observable takes the graph form $x^{(n)}=F(x,\dots,x^{(n-1)})$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:nth-order}. 
The lifting layer thus constructs a new embedding of the data, and the map it must represent is a diffeomorphism between embedded copies of $\mathcal{A}$---a generic composition, with no reason to be additively separable in the delay coordinates.

\label{prop:jet-stability}
Assume, in addition to the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-ident}, that $\mathcal{A}$ is a compact smooth invariant manifold with the ambient-induced metric and that
$\Theta=(h,\mathcal{L}_f h,\dots,\mathcal{L}_f^{\,p}h)$ is an embedding of $\mathcal{A}$. Write $F=\Theta\circ\Phi^{-1}$ and $\mathbf{z}=(z_0,\dots,z_p)$ for the jet coordinates. 
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item  $F:\Phi(\mathcal{A})\to\Theta(\mathcal{A})$ is a diffeomorphism;
its differential between attractor tangent spaces is
$DF_{\Phi(s)}=D\Theta_s\circ(D\Phi_s)^{-1}$, and $F$ conjugates the two
coordinate flows,
$F\circ\big(\Phi\,\varphi^{t}\,\Phi^{-1}\big)
 =\big(\Theta\,\varphi^{t}\,\Theta^{-1}\big)\circ F$.
\item In the jet chart the dynamics take the companion form
$\dot z_0=z_1,\;\dots,\;\dot z_{p-1}=z_p,\;\dot z_p=G(\mathbf{z})$ with the
single closure $G=(\mathcal{L}_f^{\,p+1}h)\circ\Theta^{-1}$---the graph form
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:nth-order} that the equation layer reads: in this chart the
entire vector field reduces to one unknown function.
\item  Let
$\alpha_\Delta=\inf_{s\in\mathcal{A}}
 \sigma_{\min}\!\big(D\Phi_s|_{T_s\mathcal{A}}\big)$ and
$M=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{A}}\|D\Theta_s\|$. Then $\alpha_\Delta>0$ and
$\sup_{\mathbf{y}\in\Phi(\mathcal{A})}
 \big\|DF_{\mathbf{y}}|_{T_{\mathbf{y}}\Phi(\mathcal{A})}\big\|
 \le M/\alpha_\Delta$.
\item If a learned map $\widehat F$ satisfies
$\|\widehat F-F\circ\pi\|_\infty\le\varepsilon$ on a neighborhood of
$\Phi(\mathcal{A})$ of radius below its reach, with $\pi$ the nearest-point
projection, then for a measured delay vector
$\tilde{\mathbf{y}}=\Phi(s)+\eta$ with $\|\eta\|$ below that radius,
\begin{equation}
  \|\widehat F(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})-\Theta(s)\|
  \;\le\;
  \varepsilon+c\,\frac{M}{\alpha_\Delta}\,\|\eta\|,
  \label{eq:jet-stability}
\end{equation}
with $c$ a curvature constant of the image ($c=1$ for perturbations tangent
to $\Phi(\mathcal{A})$).
\end{enumerate}

\label{rem:harmonic}
For $\ddot x=-\omega^2 x$ observed through $x$, two delays suffice: from
$x(t-\Delta)=x(t)\cos\omega\Delta-(\dot x(t)/\omega)\sin\omega\Delta$,
\begin{equation}
  \dot x(t)
  \;=\;
  \omega\,\frac{x(t)\cos\omega\Delta-x(t-\Delta)}{\sin\omega\Delta},
  \label{eq:harmonic-exact}
\end{equation}
exactly, at every $\Delta$ with $\sin\omega\Delta\neq0$. This is not
a finite difference: its weights are flow-adapted, its error does not grow
with $\Delta$, and it fails precisely at the resonances
$\omega\Delta=k\pi$, where the delay pair ceases to identify the state and
$\alpha_\Delta\to0$. Undersampling harms reconstruction through
observability, not truncation. (Here $m=2$ sits below the generic Takens
bound; the theorem's conditions are sufficient, not necessary.)

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:rep-classes}
Let $G_k$ be as in Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-ident}. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item By the Kolmogorov superposition theorem \citep{Kolmogorov1957, Arnold2009_Kolmogorov}, every continuous function on a compact $K\subset\mathbb{R}^m$ can be written $\sum_{q=1}^{2m+1}\Phi_q\big(\sum_{p=1}^{m}\phi_{qp}(y_p)\big)$---the two-layer architecture with inner width $2m+1$. 
The classical inner functions are only continuous; smooth spline parameterizations of both layers are instead dense in $C(K)$~\citep{lai2021kolmogorov}, so the inner width is a capacity hyperparameter rather than the theorem's $2m+1$ (the probes below use width $12$ for $m=7$). 
Hence every $G_k$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-ident} lies in the closure of the compositional (two-layer) lift class. 
\item The one-layer class is the additive family $\{\sum_p \phi_p(y_p)\}$: every $C^{2}$ member has identically vanishing mixed partials $\partial^2 F/\partial y_p\,\partial y_q$ ($p\neq q$). The composition $G_k=(\mathcal{L}_f^{\,k}h)\circ\Phi^{-1}$ lies in this class only when all its mixed partials vanish identically on $\Phi(\mathcal{A})$, which for nonlinear $f$ fails generically (it can hold in special cases: a scalar $\dot{x}=f(x)$ with $h=x$ gives $G_1(\mathbf{y})=f(y_1)$, and for linear $f$  Remark~\ref{rem:harmonic} gives an exactly linear $G_k$); Lemma~\ref{lem:mixed} below computes the mixed partial in closed form for $\ddot{x}=g(x)$. 
The additive class is therefore a proper subclass; how far a given $G_k$ lies from it is a per-system quantity, measured exactly in Table~\ref{tab:undersampled-jet} (Remark~\ref{rem:certificates}).
\item For the compositional (two-layer) form $F_k(\mathbf{y})=\sum_q\Psi_{kq}\big(\sum_i\phi_{qi}(y_i)\big)$, the Jacobian factorizes symbolically,
\begin{equation}
  DF(\mathbf{y})=A(\mathbf{y})\,B(\mathbf{y}),\qquad
  A_{kq}=\Psi'_{kq}\big(\textstyle\sum_i\phi_{qi}(y_i)\big),\qquad
  B_{qi}=\phi'_{qi}(y_i),
  \label{eq:kan-jacobian}
\end{equation}
so the differential of the learned multivariate chart change is expressed entirely through derivatives of its univariate edges. With $U_{\mathbf{y}}$ an orthonormal basis of the tangent space $T_{\mathbf{y}}\Phi(\mathcal{A})$---estimable from data alone by local principal components---local invertibility of the learned map on the attractor is certified by $\sigma_{\min}\big(A(\mathbf{y})B(\mathbf{y})U_{\mathbf{y}}\big)>0$, and its noise amplification by the tangent condition number $\kappa_F(\mathbf{y})=\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$: both are computable from the trained network with no ground truth.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:mixed}
Let $\ddot{x}=g(x)$ with $g\in C^{5}(\mathbb R)$, write
$\varphi^{s}(x,v)=\bigl(X(s;x,v),\,V(s;x,v)\bigr)$ for its phase-space
flow, and let $K\subset\mathbb R^{2}$ be a compact set of states
$(x,v)$ with nonempty interior. For $\Delta>0$ define the two-sample
delay map
$$
  H_\Delta(x,v)=\bigl(x,\;X(-\Delta;x,v)\bigr).
$$
Then there is $\Delta_0>0$ such that for every $\Delta\in(0,\Delta_0]$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $H_\Delta$ is a $C^{2}$ diffeomorphism from a neighborhood of $K$
      onto its image, so the window-to-velocity map
      $G_{1,\Delta}=\pi_v\circ H_\Delta^{-1}$ is well defined and $C^{2}$
      on the open set $H_\Delta(\operatorname{int}K)$;
\item uniformly for $\mathbf y=(y_1,y_2)\in H_\Delta(K)$,
      \begin{equation}
        \frac{\partial^{2} G_{1,\Delta}}{\partial y_1\,\partial y_2}(\mathbf y)
        =\frac{\Delta}{12}\,g''(y_1)+O(\Delta^{2});
        \label{eq:mixed}
      \end{equation}
\item if $U\subset\operatorname{int}K$ is open and $g''$ has no zero on
      the $x$-projection of $U$, then, after shrinking $\Delta_0$,
      $G_{1,\Delta}$ is not additively separable on $H_\Delta(U)$: no
      functions $\phi_1,\phi_2$ satisfy
      $G_{1,\Delta}(\mathbf y)=\phi_1(y_1)+\phi_2(y_2)$ there;
\item if $g$ is affine, then $G_{1,\Delta}$ is exactly affine, hence
      additively separable, on $H_\Delta(K)$;
\item $G_{1,\Delta}$ admits an additive approximation on $H_\Delta(K)$
      with sup-norm error $O(\Delta^{2})$, namely
      $\mathbf y\mapsto (y_1-y_2)/\Delta+\tfrac{\Delta}{2}g(y_1)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
\emph{Step 1: expansion of the delay map.}
Along a solution, $\ddot x=g$, $\dddot x=g'\dot x$,
$x^{(4)}=g''\dot x^{2}+g'g$, and
$x^{(5)}=g'''\dot x^{3}+3g''g\,\dot x+(g')^{2}\dot x$. Taylor's theorem
in $s$ with integral remainder gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
X(-\Delta;x,v)
  &= x-\Delta\,Q(x,v,\Delta),\\
Q
  &= v-\frac{\Delta}{2}g(x)+\frac{\Delta^{2}}{6}g'(x)\,v \\
  &\quad-\frac{\Delta^{3}}{24}
    \bigl(g''(x)v^{2}+g'(x)g(x)\bigr)
    +\Delta^{4}S(x,v,\Delta)
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Q}
\end{equation}
where
$S(x,v,\Delta)=\int_0^1\frac{(1-\theta)^{4}}{4!}\,
 P\bigl(\varphi^{-\theta\Delta}(x,v)\bigr)\,d\theta$
with $P(x,v)=g'''(x)v^{3}+3g''(x)g(x)v+g'(x)^{2}v$.
Since $g\in C^{5}$, $P\in C^{2}(\mathbb R^{2})$ and the flow is $C^{5}$,
so $S$ and its first two derivatives in $(x,v)$ are bounded uniformly
on $K'\times[0,\Delta_0]$ for any compact neighborhood $K'$ of $K$.

\emph{Step 2: $H_\Delta$ is a diffeomorphism onto its image.}
From \eqref{eq:Q},
$\partial_v Q=1+\tfrac{\Delta^{2}}{6}g'(x)-\tfrac{\Delta^{3}}{12}g''(x)v
 +\Delta^{4}\partial_v S=1+O(\Delta^{2})$
uniformly on $K'$, so for $\Delta_0$ small $\partial_v Q\ge\tfrac12$ on
$K'\times(0,\Delta_0]$. Hence for each fixed $x$ the map
$v\mapsto X(-\Delta;x,v)=x-\Delta Q$ is strictly decreasing on the
$v$-slice of $K'$, which makes $H_\Delta$ injective on $K'$, and
$\det DH_\Delta=-\Delta\,\partial_v Q\neq0$. Thus $H_\Delta$ is a $C^{2}$
diffeomorphism of $K'$ onto its image, and $H_\Delta(\operatorname{int}K)$
is open. This proves (i).

\emph{Step 3: inversion.}
For $\mathbf y\in H_\Delta(K')$ put $q=(y_1-y_2)/\Delta$; then
$q=Q(y_1,v,\Delta)$ with $v=G_{1,\Delta}(\mathbf y)$. Because
$\partial_v Q\ge\tfrac12$, the implicit function theorem (with $\Delta$
as a parameter) yields $v=\mathcal V(y_1,q,\Delta)$, $C^{2}$ in
$(y_1,q)$ with derivatives bounded uniformly in $\Delta\in(0,\Delta_0]$
on the compact set $\mathcal K=\{(y_1,q)\}$ induced by $K'$ (note
$q=v+O(\Delta)$, so $\mathcal K$ is bounded). Let
$$
  v_3(y_1,q,\Delta)
  =q+\frac{\Delta}{2}g(y_1)-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{6}g'(y_1)\,q
   +\frac{\Delta^{3}}{24}g''(y_1)\,q^{2}
   -\frac{\Delta^{3}}{24}g'(y_1)g(y_1)
$$
be the third fixed-point iterate of $v=q+\tfrac{\Delta}{2}g
-\tfrac{\Delta^{2}}{6}g'v+\tfrac{\Delta^{3}}{24}(g''v^{2}+g'g)
-\Delta^{4}S(y_1,v,\Delta)$, and set $w=\mathcal V-v_3$. Substituting
$\mathcal V=v_3+w$ into this equation and collecting terms gives
$$
  \Bigl(1+\frac{\Delta^{2}}{6}g'(y_1)
        -\frac{\Delta^{3}}{24}g''(y_1)(\mathcal V+v_3)\Bigr)\,w
  =\Delta^{4}\,T(y_1,q,\Delta),
$$
where $T$ collects $S(y_1,\mathcal V,\Delta)$ together with the
$O(\Delta^{4})$ terms produced by the truncation of $v_3$; $T$ is a
composition of $C^{2}$ functions with uniform bounds, so $T$ and its
first two $(y_1,q)$-derivatives are uniformly bounded. Dividing by the
bracket, which is $1+O(\Delta^{2})$, yields
\begin{equation}
  G_{1,\Delta}(\mathbf y)
  =q+\frac{\Delta}{2}g(y_1)-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{6}g'(y_1)\,q
   +\frac{\Delta^{3}}{24}g''(y_1)\,q^{2}
   -\frac{\Delta^{3}}{24}g'(y_1)g(y_1)
   +\Delta^{4}R(y_1,q,\Delta),
  \label{eq:inverse-expansion}
\end{equation}
with $R\in C^{2}$ in $(y_1,q)$ and $R$, $\partial R$, $\partial^{2}R$
uniformly bounded on $\mathcal K\times(0,\Delta_0]$. Rewriting in
$(y_1,y_2)$,
\begin{align}
  G_{1,\Delta}(\mathbf y)
  ={}&\frac{y_1-y_2}{\Delta}+\frac{\Delta}{2}g(y_1)
      -\frac{\Delta}{6}g'(y_1)(y_1-y_2)
      +\frac{\Delta}{24}g''(y_1)(y_1-y_2)^{2}
      \notag\\
     &-\frac{\Delta^{3}}{24}g'(y_1)g(y_1)
      +\Delta^{4}R\!\Bigl(y_1,\frac{y_1-y_2}{\Delta},\Delta\Bigr).
  \label{eq:y-expansion}
\end{align}

\emph{Step 4: the mixed partial.}
The first, second, and fifth terms of \eqref{eq:y-expansion} are
additive and contribute nothing to $\partial^{2}_{y_1y_2}$. The third
and fourth contribute
$$
  \frac{\Delta}{6}g''(y_1)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  -\frac{\Delta}{12}g''(y_1)-\frac{\Delta}{12}g'''(y_1)(y_1-y_2),
$$
respectively. Since $K$ has bounded velocity,
$y_1-y_2=\Delta q=O(\Delta)$ uniformly on $H_\Delta(K)$, so the last
piece is $O(\Delta^{2})$. For the remainder, the chain rule gives
$$
  \partial^{2}_{y_1y_2}\Bigl[\Delta^{4}R\Bigl(y_1,\tfrac{y_1-y_2}{\Delta},\Delta\Bigr)\Bigr]
  =-\Delta^{3}\,\partial_{qy_1}R-\Delta^{2}\,\partial_{qq}R
  =O(\Delta^{2}),
$$
using the uniform bounds on $\partial^{2}R$. Summing,
$\partial^{2}_{y_1y_2}G_{1,\Delta}
 =\tfrac{\Delta}{6}g''-\tfrac{\Delta}{12}g''+O(\Delta^{2})
 =\tfrac{\Delta}{12}g''(y_1)+O(\Delta^{2})$ uniformly on $H_\Delta(K)$,
which is (ii).

\emph{Step 5: consequences.}
(iii) If $|g''|\ge c>0$ on the $x$-projection of an open
$U\subset\operatorname{int}K$, then by (ii) there is $\Delta_0'$ such
that $|\partial^{2}_{y_1y_2}G_{1,\Delta}|\ge c\Delta/24>0$ on the open
set $H_\Delta(U)$ for $\Delta\le\Delta_0'$. Any $C^{2}$ function of the
form $\phi_1(y_1)+\phi_2(y_2)$ has identically vanishing mixed partial,
and if $G_{1,\Delta}$ agreed with such a sum on an open set the sum
would be $C^{2}$ there; hence no additive representation exists on
$H_\Delta(U)$.
(iv) If $g(x)=ax+b$, the vector field is affine, so $\varphi^{s}$,
$H_\Delta$, and $H_\Delta^{-1}$ (on the image, where it exists by (i))
are affine, and $G_{1,\Delta}=\pi_v\circ H_\Delta^{-1}$ is affine.
(v) In \eqref{eq:inverse-expansion} the first two terms are additive in
$(y_1,y_2)$, while $q$, $g$ and its derivatives, and $R$ are uniformly
bounded on $\mathcal K$, so the remaining terms are uniformly
$O(\Delta^{2})$ on $H_\Delta(K)$.

The requirement $\operatorname{int}K\neq\emptyset$ in (iii) is not technical. 
On a curve $\{y_2=h(y_1)\}$ every function is trivially additive ($\phi_1=G\circ(\mathrm{id},h)$, $\phi_2=0$), so on the delay image of a single closed orbit of the conservative oscillator $\ddot x=g(x)$ the additive class always suffices, whatever $g$ is.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:stencil}
If $x\in C^{p+1}$ with $|x^{(p+1)}|\leq M$ on the window span, degree-$p$ polynomial interpolation on the window yields finite-difference weights $c^{(k)}_i$ with $\big|x^{(k)}(t)-\sum_i c^{(k)}_i\,x(t-i\tau)\big| \leq C_{p,m}\,M\, \tau^{\,p+1-k}$. 
Hence the distance from $G_k$ to the linear class---and a fortiori to the additive and compositional classes---is $O(\tau^{\,p+1-k})$ as $\tau\to 0$: at dense sampling all three classes are near-optimal, and nothing is gained from nonlinearity or depth.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:undersampled}
A time series sampled at interval $\tau$ is undersampled for order $k$ when the truncation bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:stencil} is vacuous over any usable window---$\tau$ a non-negligible fraction of the dynamics' characteristic time---while the delay map remains an embedding with conditioning $\alpha_\Delta$ bounded away from zero (Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-stability}). 
In this regime $G_k$ exists and is stably invertible on the attractor, but only as a genuine composition: it is far from the linear and additive classes, and only the compositional class is guaranteed to contain it (Proposition~\ref{prop:rep-classes}). 
Reconstruction fails not when $\tau$ is large per se, but when $\alpha_\Delta\to0$ or the delay map loses injectivity (Remark~\ref{rem:harmonic}).
The \emph{aliasing wall} is the sampling interval at which the second condition fails: $\alpha_\Delta\to0$ or $\Phi$ ceases to be injective (Remark~\ref{rem:harmonic}).

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:certificates}
A general lower bound on the additive distance is delicate---$\Phi(\mathcal{A})$
is a $d$-dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^m$, so mixed-partial arguments
do not apply off the image---but it is measurable per system: with fixed spline
knots the additive model is linear in its parameters, its squared loss is
convex, and the fitted additive error in Table~\ref{tab:undersampled-jet} is
therefore (up to knot resolution and optimization tolerance) the class
distance rather than a training artifact. The linear rows are exact class
optima by construction (ridge regression). Three conventions and caveats
attach to the stability statements. Norms and singular values are taken with
per-order-normalized outputs and the ambient-induced metric on
$\mathcal{A}$; with heterogeneous derivative scales the conditioning claims
hold after this normalization, which the experiments apply throughout.
The perturbation bound~\eqref{eq:jet-stability} concerns the learned map on
a tubular neighborhood: ambient noise moves the delay vector off
$\Phi(\mathcal{A})$, so the off-manifold extension of $\widehat F$---shaped
in practice by training on noisy inputs, as in the field experiment
below---is part of the estimator, not an afterthought. Finally, the
propositions assume a smooth autonomous flow on a compact invariant
manifold: for fractal attractors the smooth-manifold hypotheses should be
replaced by the box-dimension embedding theory
of~\citet{sauer1991embedology}, under which $\Phi^{-1}$ is guaranteed
continuous rather than smooth; non-smooth dynamics (the Bouc--Wen memory
law) and forced systems (extend the state by the forcing phase) require the
correspondingly extended hypotheses.

<!-- head-1: body_md -->
Let $\varphi^{t}$ be the flow of $\dot s=f(s)$ on a manifold $M$ with
$f\in C^{p}$, let $\mathcal{A}\subset M$ be a compact invariant set of
box-counting dimension $d$, and let $h\in C^{p}(M,\mathbb{R})$ be the
observable, $x(t)=h(s(t))$. With the delay map $\Phi$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-ident} and the differential map
$\Theta(s)=\big(h(s),\,(\mathcal{L}_f h)(s),\,\dots,\,(\mathcal{L}_f^{\,p}h)(s)\big)$
of Corollary~\ref{cor:jet-chart}, we have
$x^{(k)}(t)=(\mathcal{L}_f^{\,k}h)(s(t))$, since
$\tfrac{d^k}{d\sigma^k}h(\varphi^{\sigma}s)\big|_{\sigma=0}
 =(\mathcal{L}_f^{\,k}h)(s)$. Write $\mathbf{y}_t=\Phi(s(t))$. We use two
hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(H1)] $\Phi$ is injective on $\mathcal{A}$. This holds for generic
    (prevalent) $(h,\tau)$ when $m\ge 2d+1$ for a smooth $\mathcal{A}$
    \citep{takens1981}, and when $m>2d$ for a fractal $\mathcal{A}$
    \citep{sauer1991embedology}.
  \item[(H2)] $\Theta$ is injective on $\mathcal{A}$ (generic for
    $p\ge 2d$, Corollary~\ref{cor:jet-chart}). Used only in part~(ii).
\end{itemize}
Unlike Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-ident}, part~(i) below requires only (H1),
not that $\Phi$ be an embedding, and so covers fractal attractors without
modification (cf.\ Remark~\ref{rem:certificates}).

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:ka-delay-jet}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] \emph{Existence.} Under (H1) there is a unique continuous map
    $G=(G_0,\dots,G_p):\Phi(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{R}^{p+1}$ with
    \begin{equation}
      G\circ\Phi=\Theta,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
      x^{(k)}(t)=G_k(\mathbf{y}_t)\quad\text{for all } t,\ k=0,\dots,p.
      \label{eq:ka-existence}
    \end{equation}
  \item[(ii)] \emph{Chart transition.} Under (H1) and (H2),
    $G:\Phi(\mathcal{A})\to\Theta(\mathcal{A})$ is a homeomorphism that
    conjugates the flow in delay coordinates to the flow in jet
    coordinates,
    \begin{equation}
      G\circ\bigl(\Phi\,\varphi^{t}\,\Phi^{-1}\bigr)
      =\bigl(\Theta\,\varphi^{t}\,\Theta^{-1}\bigr)\circ G .
      \label{eq:ka-conjugacy}
    \end{equation}
    If $\mathcal{A}$ is a smooth manifold and $\Phi,\Theta$ are
    embeddings, $G$ is a diffeomorphism, as in
    Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-stability}(i).
  \item[(iii)] \emph{Exact two-layer representation with a shared inner
    layer.} There exist a cube $K=[a,b]^m\supset\Phi(\mathcal{A})$ and
    continuous functions $\psi_{q,i}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}$ and
    $\Psi_{k,q}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ such that for all
    $\mathbf{y}\in\Phi(\mathcal{A})$,
    \begin{equation}
      G_k(\mathbf{y})=\sum_{q=0}^{2m}\Psi_{k,q}\!\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}
        \psi_{q,i}(y_i)\Bigr),\qquad k=0,\dots,p .
      \label{eq:ka-exact}
    \end{equation}
    The inner functions $\psi_{q,i}$ do not depend on $k$; only the outer
    layer depends on the derivative order. The whole jet map is therefore
    a single two-layer KAN of shape $m\to 2m+1\to p+1$.
  \item[(iv)] \emph{Approximation by spline KANs.} For every
    $\varepsilon>0$ there is a two-layer KAN with spline edges and a
    shared inner layer,
    $\hat G_k(\mathbf{y})
     =\sum_{q}\hat\Psi_{k,q}\bigl(\sum_i\hat\psi_{q,i}(y_i)\bigr)$,
    such that
    \begin{equation}
      \sup_{\mathbf{y}\in\Phi(\mathcal{A})}\ \max_{0\le k\le p}\
      \bigl|\hat G_k(\mathbf{y})-G_k(\mathbf{y})\bigr|<\varepsilon .
      \label{eq:ka-approx}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We prove each part in turn.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item  By (H1), $\Phi:\mathcal{A}\to\Phi(\mathcal{A})$ is a continuous
bijection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space, hence a
homeomorphism, and $\Phi^{-1}:\Phi(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{A}$ is
continuous. Set $G:=\Theta\circ\Phi^{-1}$. Each component
$G_k=(\mathcal{L}_f^{\,k}h)\circ\Phi^{-1}$ is continuous because
$\mathcal{L}_f^{\,k}h$ is continuous for $f,h\in C^{p}$ and $k\le p$, and
$$
  x^{(k)}(t)=(\mathcal{L}_f^{\,k}h)(s(t))
  =(\mathcal{L}_f^{\,k}h)\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf{y}_t)\bigr)
  =G_k(\mathbf{y}_t).
$$
Any $G'$ with $G'\circ\Phi=\Theta$ equals $\Theta\circ\Phi^{-1}$ on
$\Phi(\mathcal{A})$, which gives uniqueness. No condition on $\tau$ enters
beyond (H1).

\item Under (H2), $\Theta$ is likewise a homeomorphism onto
$\Theta(\mathcal{A})$, so $G=\Theta\circ\Phi^{-1}$ is a homeomorphism with
inverse $\Phi\circ\Theta^{-1}$. The conjugacy follows by substitution:
$$
  G\,\Phi\,\varphi^{t}\,\Phi^{-1}
  =\Theta\,\varphi^{t}\,\Phi^{-1}
  =\bigl(\Theta\,\varphi^{t}\,\Theta^{-1}\bigr)\bigl(\Theta\,\Phi^{-1}\bigr)
  =\bigl(\Theta\,\varphi^{t}\,\Theta^{-1}\bigr)\,G .
$$
In the smooth case, compositions and inverses of embeddings onto their
images are diffeomorphisms.

\item \textit{Step 1: extension to a cube.} $\Phi(\mathcal{A})$ is
compact, so it lies in some cube $K=[a,b]^m$ and is closed in $K$. By the
Tietze extension theorem each $G_k$ extends to a continuous
$\tilde G_k:K\to\mathbb{R}$ agreeing with $G_k$ on $\Phi(\mathcal{A})$.
This step is needed because the Kolmogorov--Arnold theorem applies to
continuous functions on a cube, whereas $G_k$ is defined only on the
(typically lower-dimensional, possibly fractal) set $\Phi(\mathcal{A})$.
The extension is not unique, but every extension agrees with $G_k$
wherever data can occur.

\textit{Step 2: Kolmogorov's theorem.} After rescaling $K$ to $[0,1]^m$,
the Kolmogorov superposition theorem
\citep{Kolmogorov1957, Arnold2009_Kolmogorov}, in its form with universal
inner functions, provides continuous $\psi_{q,i}$ ($q=0,\dots,2m$,
$i=1,\dots,m$), depending only on $m$, such that every continuous $g$ on
$[0,1]^m$ admits
$g(\mathbf{y})=\sum_{q=0}^{2m}\chi_q\bigl(\sum_i\psi_{q,i}(y_i)\bigr)$
with continuous outer functions $\chi_q$ depending on $g$. Applying this to
each $\tilde G_k$ with the same $\psi_{q,i}$ yields outer functions
$\Psi_{k,q}$; restricting to $\Phi(\mathcal{A})$ gives
\eqref{eq:ka-exact}.

\item Let $n_h=2m+1$ and $s_q(\mathbf{y})=\sum_i\psi_{q,i}(y_i)$,
whose range over $K$ is a compact interval $I_q$; let $I_q^{1}$ denote
$I_q$ enlarged by $1$ on each side.

\textit{Tolerance for the inner layer.} Each $\Psi_{k,q}$ is uniformly
continuous on $I_q^{1}$, so there is $\delta\in(0,1)$ such that for all
$k,q$,
$$
  |u-v|<\delta\ \Longrightarrow\
  |\Psi_{k,q}(u)-\Psi_{k,q}(v)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2n_h}.
$$

\textit{Inner layer.} Piecewise-linear interpolants, and hence splines of degree $d$ are dense in $C[a,b]$. 
Choose splines $\hat\psi_{q,i}$ with $\|\hat\psi_{q,i}-\psi_{q,i}\|_\infty<\delta/m$. 
Then $\hat s_q=\sum_i\hat\psi_{q,i}$ satisfies $|\hat s_q-s_q|<\delta$, so $\hat s_q(\mathbf{y})\in I_q^{1}$.

\textit{Outer layer.} Choose splines $\hat\Psi_{k,q}$ on $I_q^{1}$ with
$\|\hat\Psi_{k,q}-\Psi_{k,q}\|_{\infty,I_q^{1}}<\varepsilon/(2n_h)$.

\textit{Combination.} For $\mathbf{y}\in\Phi(\mathcal{A})$,
$$
  \bigl|\hat\Psi_{k,q}(\hat s_q)-\Psi_{k,q}(s_q)\bigr|
  \le\underbrace{\bigl|\hat\Psi_{k,q}(\hat s_q)-\Psi_{k,q}(\hat s_q)\bigr|}_{<\,\varepsilon/2n_h}
  +\underbrace{\bigl|\Psi_{k,q}(\hat s_q)-\Psi_{k,q}(s_q)\bigr|}_{<\,\varepsilon/2n_h}.
$$
Summing over the $n_h$ hidden units gives $|\hat G_k-G_k|<\varepsilon$. The
inner splines were chosen independently of $k$, so the inner layer remains
shared.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:ka-residual}
Suppose the system is $n$th-order in the observable, $n\le p$, so that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:nth-order} holds, $x^{(n)}=F\bigl(x,x',\dots,x^{(n-1)}\bigr)$,
with a continuous closure $F$ (namely
$F=(\mathcal{L}_f^{\,n}h)\circ\Theta_{n-1}^{-1}$ when the $(n-1)$-jet map
$\Theta_{n-1}$ is injective on $\mathcal{A}$; this is the closure of the
companion form in Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-stability}(ii)). If $F$ is
$L$-Lipschitz and the lift satisfies $\|\hat G-G\|_\infty<\varepsilon$ on
$\Phi(\mathcal{A})$, then along every trajectory
\begin{equation}
  \Bigl|\hat G_n(\mathbf{y}_t)
    -F\bigl(\hat G_0(\mathbf{y}_t),\dots,\hat G_{n-1}(\mathbf{y}_t)\bigr)\Bigr|
  \le\bigl(1+L\sqrt{n}\bigr)\,\varepsilon .
  \label{eq:ka-residual}
\end{equation}

Insert $G_n(\mathbf{y}_t)=F\bigl(G_0(\mathbf{y}_t),\dots,G_{n-1}(\mathbf{y}_t)\bigr)$
and apply the triangle inequality. 
The first term is bounded by $\varepsilon$; the second by $L\,\bigl\|(\hat G_k-G_k)_{k<n}\bigr\|_2\le L\sqrt{n}\,\varepsilon$.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:ka-interpretation}
Proposition~\ref{prop:ka-delay-jet} supplies the logical chain behind the
lifting layer.
\emph{Existence} (i) makes the lift well-posed at any $\tau$ for which
(H1) holds; where (H1) fails, no architecture can succeed, which is the
content of the aliasing wall of Definition~\ref{def:undersampled}
(Table~\ref{tab:undersampled-jet} at $\Delta t=0.8$) and of the Bouc--Wen
memory variable seen through $(x,\dot x)$ (Note~\ref{sec:supp-boucwen}).
\emph{Chart transition} (ii) shows that the flow read in jet coordinates
is conjugate to the observed flow, and in those coordinates the vector
field reduces to the single closure of
Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-stability}(ii); this is what licenses reading
the equation layer's edges as the governing law.
\emph{Representation} (iii) shows that depth two is sufficient for every
system satisfying (H1), whatever the nonlinearity or sampling rate, and
that one inner layer can serve all $p+1$ derivative orders, which is the
jet operator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:jetop} in its compositional (two-layer)
form. 
                                                                   
                                                                                               
Lemma~\ref{lem:mixed} supplies the complementary lower bound by showing
that a single additive layer can be insufficient. Thus, two layers are
sufficient in general and become necessary within the nonseparable regime
identified by the lemma.
Corollary~\ref{cor:ka-residual} connects lift
accuracy to the residual of the discovered law.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:ka-scope}
The inner functions of the Kolmogorov--Arnold theorem are continuous in general, but far from smooth and often ``Cantor-like'' \citep{girosi1989representation}.
Part~(iii) is therefore an existence statement about architecture;
part~(iv) is what a trained spline KAN realizes, and it inherits no convergence rate from the theorem. 
Rates must come from the smoothness of $G$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-stability}) and from spline approximation theory \citep{lai2021kolmogorov}. 
In particular, the width used in the experiments ($12$ for $m=7$) is a capacity choice, not the theorem's $2m+1$ with its pathological inner functions, as noted in Proposition~\ref{prop:rep-classes}(i).  
Corollary~\ref{cor:ka-residual} assumes a Lipschitz closure and exact targets; measurement noise enters separately through Proposition~\ref{prop:jet-stability}(iv).

composition closes the gap, as Eq.~\eqref{eq:comp-map} predicts. Extending
the sweep locates the lower boundary: the compositional advantage persists
through $\Delta t=0.6$ ($x'$: $0.61$ versus $0.21$ additive and $0.00$
linear), and by $\Delta t=0.8$---below one sample per orbit turn---every
class fails on $x'$ (best $R^2=0.21$): the aliasing wall of
Definition~\ref{def:undersampled}, where no representation can recover
information the samples no longer carry.
