This is a more restrictive definition of a convex toric domain than is given in \cite{Gard}, where $\Omega$ is just required to be convex, and not necessarily defined by a function.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:two-polydisks-ball}
Let $a\in [1, 2.869].$ Suppose that there is a symplectic embedding \newline $P(a,1)\coprod P(a,1)\hookrightarrow B(c).$ Then $$c\geq 3+\left(\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{4}-\sqrt{\frac{3(1+\sqrt{5})}{8}}\right)(a-1)\approx 3+(0.207)(a-1).$$
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
When $a=1,$ this bound is sharp, as two copies of $P(1,1)$ do in fact symplectically embed into $B(c)$ for any $c>3,$ as illustrated below.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
In the proof of this theorem, we give a lower bound for $c$ that is valid for all $a\geq 1$. For large values of $a$, the bound is weaker than the volume constraint \newline $\text{vol}(B(c))\geq 2 \text{vol}(P(a,1))$. The interval $[1,2.869]$ is (a numerical approximation of) where our bound is stronger than the volume constraint. Other numerical constants throughout arise similarly.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:two-polydisks-polydisk}
Let $a\in [1,2.348].$ If $P(a,1)\coprod P(a,1)$ symplectically embeds into $P(c,c)$, then $$c\geq \begin{cases} 2 & 1\leq a\leq 2\\ 2+\left(\frac{23-\sqrt{401}}{24}\right)(a-1) & 2\leq a\leq 2.348.\end{cases}$$
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
For $1\leq a\leq 2,$ this bound is sharp by a similar moment map picture as for the previous result (and follows trivially from the case $a=1$).

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:reflection-isotopy}
Let $r:{\mathbb C}^2\to{\mathbb C}^2$ be the reflection $r(z,w)=(w,z).$ Suppose that $X_{\Omega^-}$ and $X_{\Omega^+}$ are convex toric domains such that $X_{\Omega^-}\cup \left(r\left(X_{\Omega^-}\right)\right)\subseteq X_{\Omega^+}$. Let $\iota:X_{\Omega^-}\hookrightarrow X_{\Omega^+}$ be the inclusion map. Then $\iota$ and $r\circ\iota,$ considered as symplectic embeddings of $X_{\Omega^-}$ into $X_{\Omega^+}$, are isotopic by a path of symplectic embeddings if and only if there exists a positive real number $R$ such that $X_{\Omega^-}\subseteq B(R)\subseteq X_{\Omega^+}.$

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:convex-integral-path}
A \textit{convex integral path} is a simple and piecewise linear curve $\Lambda$ in the first quadrant ${\mathbb R}_{\geq 0}^2$ from a point $(0,y(\Lambda))$ to a point $(x(\Lambda),0)$ such that 
\begin{itemize}
\item The above endpoints and all vertices of $\Lambda$ lie in ${\mathbb Z}_{\geq 0}^2.$
\item The plane region bounded by the coordinate axes and $\Lambda$ is convex.
\item Each edge of $\Lambda$ (segment between two consecutive lattice points) is either vertical or has nonpositive slope. 
\end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:convex-generator}
A \textit{convex generator} is a convex integral path where each edge is labeled either $``e"$ or $``h"$, and no two edges that have the same slope may both be labeled $``h"$, and no vertical or horizontal edges may be labeled $``h".$ Two convex generators are considered equivalent when they have the same underlying convex integral path, and the labelings are the same up to permutations among the edges with a fixed slope. Convex generators will only be considered modulo this equivalence.

By associating to each factor $e_{ab}$ or $h_{ab}$ an edge vector $(a,-b)$ of the same labeling, we may identify a convex generator with a commutative formal product of terms $e_{ab}$ and $h_{ab}$, where no factor $h_{ab}$ may be repeated, and no factor $h_{10}$ or $h_{01}$ may appear. Note that in either case, $a$ and $b$ are coprime by our definition of an edge of a convex integral path. Given two convex generators with no $h_{ab}$ factor in common, we define their product to be the convex generator obtained considering them as products of $e_{ab}$ and $h_{ab}$ terms and formally multiplying these products.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:convex-action}
If $\Lambda=e_{ab}$ or $h_{ab}$ and $\Omega\subset{\mathbb R}_{\geq 0}^2$ defines a convex toric domain $X_{\Omega}$ as in Section 1, define the \textit{action} $\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}(\Lambda)$ as the value of $c$ for which the line $bx+ay=c$ is externally tangent to $\Omega.$ For general $\Lambda$, the action $\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}(\Lambda)$ is the sum of the actions of each $e_{ab}$ and $h_{ab}$ appearing in $\Lambda$ (with multiplicity).
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
For any $\Omega$ defining a convex toric domain $X_{\Omega}$, we have $\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}(e_{10})=y(\Omega)$ and $\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}(e_{01})=x(\Omega),$ where $(x(\Omega),0)$ and $(0,y(\Omega))$ are respectively where the defining curve for $\Omega$ meets the $x$- and $y$-axes.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:convex-index} The \textit{index} of a convex generator $\Lambda$ is defined as 
$$I(\Lambda)=2(L(\Lambda)-1)-h(\Lambda),$$
where $L(\Lambda)$ is the number of lattice points (including on the boundary) in the plane region bounded by the coordinate axes and the path $\Lambda$, and $h(\Lambda)$ is the number of edges of $\Lambda$ that are labeled $``h".$
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:convex-minimal}
A convex generator $\Lambda$ is \textit{minimal} for $\Omega$ if all edges of $\Lambda$ are labeled $``e"$ and if $\Lambda'\neq\Lambda$ is a convex generator with all edges labeled $``e"$ and $I(\Lambda')=I(\Lambda)$, then $\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}(\Lambda')>\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}(\Lambda).$
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{def:concave-integral-path}A \textit{concave integral path} is a path $\Lambda$ as in Definition \ref{def:convex-integral-path}, except that:
\begin{itemize}
\item No edges of $\Lambda$ are horizontal or vertical.
\item The compact region bounded by the coordinate axes and $\Lambda$ has convex complement (in ${\mathbb R}_{\geq 0}^2)$ instead of being convex itself.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def:concave-generator} A concave generator is a concave integral path where each edge is labeled either $``e"$ or $``h"$ with the same restrictions and notion of equivalence as in Definition \ref{def:convex-generator} (except that the condition that no vertical or horizontal edges may be labeled $``h"$ is now vacuous).
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{def:concave-action} When $\Omega$ defines a concave toric domain and $\Lambda$ is a concave generator, the action $\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}(\Lambda)$ is defined the same as for the convex case, except that ``externally tangent'' is replaced by ``internally tangent''. We may identify a concave generator with a commutative formal product of $h_{ab}$ and $e_{ab}$ terms and define products of concave generators as in the convex case.
<!-- end definition-8 -->
<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
\label{def:concave-index} When $\Lambda$ is a concave generator, its index is defined as 

$$I(\Lambda)=2L^{\text{int}}(\Lambda)+h(\Lambda),$$

where $L^{\text{int}}(\Lambda)$ is the number of lattice points in the plane region bounded by $\Lambda$ and the coordinate axes that do not lie on $\Lambda$, and $h(\Lambda)$ has the same meaning as in the convex case.
<!-- end definition-9 -->
<!-- definition-10: body_md -->
\label{def:concave-maximal} We say that a concave generator $\Lambda$ is \textit{maximal} for $\Omega$ when it uniquely maximizes action for $\Omega$ among all concave generators with the same index and all edges labeled $``e"$.
<!-- end definition-10 -->
<!-- definition-11: body_md -->
\label{def:multiplicity} For either a concave or a convex generator $\Lambda$, we define its \textit{multiplicity} $m(\Lambda)$ to be its number of edges. Equivalently, when we identify $\Lambda$ with a formal product of $e_{ab}$ and $h_{ab}$ terms, $m(\Lambda)$ is the sum of the exponents of each of these factors that appear.
<!-- end definition-11 -->
<!-- definition-12: body_md -->
\label{def:are}
When $\Lambda$ is a concave or convex integral path or generator, define $\text{Area}(\Lambda)$ to be the area of the compact region in ${\mathbb R}_{\geq 0}^2$ bounded by $\Lambda$ and the coordinate axes.
<!-- end definition-12 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:main-convex}
Let $X_{\Omega_i}$ for $i=1,\ldots, m$ and $X_{\Omega'}$ be convex toric domains. Suppose that there is a symplectic embedding $\varphi:\coprod_{i=1}^m X_{\Omega_i}\hookrightarrow X_{\Omega'}.$ Let $\Lambda'$ be a convex generator which is minimal for $X_{\Omega'}.$ Let $I_1,\ldots,I_m$ be nonnegative even integers such that $I_1+\ldots+I_m=I(\Lambda').$ Then there exist convex generators $\Lambda^1,\ldots,\Lambda^m$  and factorizations $\Lambda'=\Lambda'_1\ldots\Lambda_n'$ and $\Lambda^i=\Lambda_1^i\ldots\Lambda_n^i$ for each $i$ such that the following conditions hold:

\begin{itemize}
\item{(a)} For each $i\in\{1,\ldots, m\},$ we have $I(\Lambda^i)=I_i.$
\item{(b)} For each $j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$, we have $\sum_{i=1}^m I(\Lambda_j^i)=I(\Lambda'_j).$
\item{(c)} For each subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$, we have $\sum_{i=1}^m I(\prod_{j\in S}\Lambda_j^i)=I(\prod_{j\in S}\Lambda_j').$
\item{(d)} For each $j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$, we have $\sum_{i=1}^m \mathcal{A}_{\Omega_i}(\Lambda_j^i)\leq \mathcal{A}_{\Omega'}(\Lambda_j').$
\item{(e)} For all $j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$, we have $\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda_j^i)+y(\Lambda_j^i)-\frac{1}{2}h(\Lambda_j^i)\geq x(\Lambda_j')+y(\Lambda_j')+m(\Lambda_j')-1.$ We will refer to this inequality as the ``genus bound''.
\item{(f)} Given $k,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$ if $\Lambda_k'\neq\Lambda_j'$ or if $\Lambda_k^i\neq\Lambda_j^i$ for any $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\},$ then $\Lambda_k^i$ and $\Lambda_j^i$ have no elliptic factors $e_{ab}$ in common for any $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}.$
\item{(g)} For all $j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and all $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$, the following inequality holds whenever $I(\Lambda'_j)>2$: $$\text{min}(x(\Lambda'_j),y(\Lambda'_j))\geq\text{min}(x(\Lambda^i_j),y(\Lambda^i_j)).$$
\end{itemize}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
The condition ``$\Lambda'$ is minimal for $X_{\Omega'}$'' can be weakened to ``all edges of $\Lambda'$ are labeled $`e'$''. We only use the condition that $\Lambda'$ is minimal to guarantee that it represents a nonzero ECH class. That this is true when all edges are labeled $``e"$ follows from Conjecture A.3 in \cite{Beyond}, which is proved as Corollary 4.15 in \cite{Choi}.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:repeated-column}
Suppose that a column $C=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda'\\ \Lambda^1 \\ \vdots \\ \Lambda^m\end{pmatrix}$ occurs (at least) twice in the matrix of convex generators described in the previous section with $I(\Lambda')>0$. Then one of the $\Lambda^i$ equals $e_{ab}$ for some nonnegative and coprime $a,b$ and the remaining generators $\Lambda^k$ for $k\neq i$ are empty. We also have the relations:
\begin{itemize}
\item $a+b=x(\Lambda')+y(\Lambda')+m(\Lambda')-1$ and
\item $ab=2\text{Area}(\Lambda').$
\end{itemize}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
A similar restriction for repeated convex generators appears in Proposition 3.4 in \cite{CN} to obstruct embeddings of a (single) polydisk $P(a,1)$ into a ball $B(c).$
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For any convex generator $\Lambda$, an application of Pick's Theorem yields

$$L(\Lambda^k)=\text{Area}(\Lambda)k^2+\frac{1}{2}(x(\Lambda)+y(\Lambda)+m(\Lambda))k+1.$$

\begin{itemize}
\item Example 1: $\Lambda=e_{10}e_{01}$ has area $1$ and $x(\Lambda)+y(\Lambda)+m(\Lambda)=4,$ and this formula correctly predicts $L((e_{01}e_{10})^k)=k^2+2k+1=(k+1)^2.$
\item Example 2: $\Lambda=e_{11}$ has area $1/2$ and $x(\Lambda)+y(\Lambda)+m(\Lambda)=3,$ so this formula predicts $L(e_{11}^k)=\frac{1}{2}k^2+\frac{3}{2}k+1,$ which correctly matches a count of $1+2+\ldots+(k+1).$ 
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}
A polynomial counting the number of lattice points in dilates of a lattice polytope is known as an Ehrhart polynomial. When the polytope is only assumed to have rational vertices, the counting function is called an Ehrhart quasipolynomial instead. See \cite{Ehrhart} for examples of these being applied to obstruct symplectic embeddings of an ellipsoid into another ellipsoid.
\end{remark}

Since the column $C$ is repeated, none of the convex generators considered may contain any edges labeled $``h"$. Merging two columns equal to $C$ gives the following equality for $k=1$ and $k=2$:

\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^m I((\Lambda^i)^k)=&2\left(\sum_{i=1}^m\text{Area}(\Lambda^i)\right)k^2+\left(\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda^i)+y(\Lambda^i)+m(\Lambda^i)\right)k\\
=&2\text{Area}(\Lambda')k^2+(x(\Lambda')+y(\Lambda')+m(\Lambda'))k\\
=& I\left((\Lambda')^k\right).
\end{align*}

Since both middle expressions are quadratic in $k$ with constant terms of zero, being equal for $k=1$ and $k=2$ guarantees that they are equal for all $k.$ In particular, their linear coefficients are equal:

$$\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda^i)+y(\Lambda^i)+m(\Lambda^i)=x(\Lambda')+y(\Lambda')+m(\Lambda').$$

We combine with the genus bound $$\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda^i)+y(\Lambda^i)\geq x(\Lambda')+y(\Lambda')+m(\Lambda')-1$$ to find

$$\sum_{i=1}^m m(\Lambda^i)\leq 1.$$

It follows that one of the $\Lambda^i$ has $m(\Lambda^i)=1$ and the remaining $\Lambda^k$ for $k\neq i$ are empty. So $\Lambda^i=e_{ab}$ for some coprime and nonnegative integers $a$ and $b$, where the relations in the statement of the lemma come from equating the linear and quadratic coefficients above.
<!-- end proof-1 -->

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:index-shift}
Let $\Lambda$ be a convex generator. Let $c_{ab}(\Lambda)$ be the value for which the line \newline$bx+ay=c_{ab}(\Lambda)$ is externally tangent to $\Lambda$. Then $$I(e_{ab}\Lambda)=I(e_{ab})+I(\Lambda)+2c_{ab}(\Lambda).$$
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
As in the proof of the previous lemma, an application of Pick's Theorem yields $$I(\Lambda)=2\text{Area}(\Lambda)+x(\Lambda)+y(\Lambda)+m(\Lambda)-h(\Lambda).$$ All terms except the area term are additive, so $$I(e_{ab}\Lambda)=I(e_{ab})+I(\Lambda)+2\left(\text{Area}(e_{ab}\Lambda)-\text{Area}(\Lambda)-\text{Area}(e_{ab})\right).$$

That $c_{ab}(\Lambda)$ is the difference between $\text{Area}(e_{ab}\Lambda)$ and $\text{Area}(e_{ab})+\text{Area}(\Lambda)$ may be seen by subdividing the plane region bounded by the axes and $e_{ab}\Lambda$ into pieces.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We apply Theorem \ref{thm:main-convex} to $\Lambda'=e_{11}^{d'}$, which may be seen to be minimal for $B(c),$ and choose the index split as $I(\Lambda^1)=I(\Lambda^2)=\frac{d'(d'+3)}{2}$, with $d'$ being chosen such that $d'(d'+3)$ is a multiple of $4$.

Let $e_{11}^{d'}=e_{11}^{d_1}\ldots e_{11}^{d_n}$ be a factorization given by Theorem \ref{thm:main-convex}. First, we check that as $d'\to\infty$, there must be some column with $d_i$ arbitrarily large.

Suppose for contradiction that $d_i\leq K$ always. There are only finitely many possible columns, so some must be repeated arbitrarily many times as $d'\to\infty.$ By Lemma \ref{lem:repeated-column}, there must be a repeated column either of the form $\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^d\\ \emptyset \\ e_{ab}\end{pmatrix}$ or of the form $\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^d \\ e_{ab} \\ \emptyset\end{pmatrix}.$ Also, by the chosen index split $I(\Lambda^1)=I(\Lambda^2)=\frac{d'(d'+3)}{2},$ there must be repeated columns of both forms. However, when we merge two columns, one of each form, the index of the negative generators is additive on merging, but $I(e_{11}^{d_1+d_2})>I(e_{11}^{d_1})+I(e_{11}^{d_2})$. 

We now divide our problem into two cases. In either case, we may assume that $x(\Lambda^i)$ and $y(\Lambda^i)$ are bounded above by a fixed constant multiple of $d$, as otherwise the action inequality $$dc\geq\sum_{i=1}^2 x(\Lambda^i)+ay(\Lambda^i)$$ gives a stronger bound for $c$ than the statement of our theorem.

Case 1: There exists a repeated column $C_1$ of the form $\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^r\\ \emptyset \\ e_{mn}\end{pmatrix}$ or of the form $\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^r \\ e_{mn} \\ \emptyset\end{pmatrix}.$ Without loss of generality, take the second case. Note that not both of these cases can occur by index considerations as above.

Let $C_2=\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^d\\ \Lambda^1 \\ \Lambda^2 \end{pmatrix}$ be a column where $d$ may be taken to be arbitrarily large. Since every repeated column is empty in the component of $\Lambda^2$, there are such columns where $I(\Lambda^2)\geq I(\Lambda^1).$ In particular, $I(\Lambda^2)\geq\frac{d^2+3d}{2}.$

We immediately have the following constraints from Theorem \ref{thm:main-convex}.

The index constraint (a) yields
$$d(d+3)=I(\Lambda^1)+I(\Lambda^2)\leq 2(x(\Lambda^1)+1)(y(\Lambda^1)+1)+2(x(\Lambda^2)+1)(y(\Lambda^2)+1).$$

By the assumption that $x(\Lambda^i)$ and $y(\Lambda^i)$ are $O(d)$, the righthand side is $$2\sum_{i=1}^2 x(\Lambda^i)y(\Lambda^i)+O(d),$$

Also, the genus bound gives

$$\sum_{i=1}^2x(\Lambda^i)+y(\Lambda^i)\geq 3d-1.$$

By merging $C_1$ and $C_2$ and applying the index constraint to the merged column, we find that 
\begin{align*}
2c_{mn}(\Lambda^1)&=I(e_{mn}\Lambda^1)-I(e_{mn})-I(\Lambda^1)\\ &=I(e_{11}^{d+r})-I(e_{11}^r)-I(e_{11}^d)\\ &=2dr
\end{align*} 
by Lemma \ref{lem:index-shift}, and so the line $nx+my=dr$ is externally tangent to $\Lambda^1.$ In particular, the point $(x(\Lambda^1),y(\Lambda^1))$ lies on or above this line by convexity, giving an additional constraint $\frac{n}{r}x(\Lambda_1)+\frac{m}{r}y(\Lambda_1)\geq d.$

Hence, we may bound 
\begin{align*}
d&\leq \frac{n}{r}x(\Lambda^1)+\frac{m}{r}y(\Lambda^1)\\&\leq\max\left(\frac{n}{r},\frac{m}{r}\right)(x(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^1))\\&< \left(\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)(x(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^1))
\end{align*}

using the relations $mn=r^2$ and $m+n=3r-1$ from Lemma \ref{lem:repeated-column}, and solving for $m/r$ and $n/r$ explicitly.

Finally, we use the action inequality $$dc\geq (x(\Lambda^1)+x(\Lambda^2))+a(y(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^2)).$$

Putting $X_i=x(\Lambda^i)/d$ and similarly for $Y_i$, we keep only the leading terms in $d$ and take $d\to\infty$ to find

\begin{align*}c&\geq\text{min}\left\{X_1+aY_1+X_2+aY_2\bigg\vert \sum_{i=1}^2 X_i+Y_i\geq 3, \sum_{i=1}^2 X_iY_i\geq \frac{1}{2}, X_1+Y_1\geq\frac{2}{3+\sqrt{5}},X_2Y_2\geq\frac{1}{4}\right\}\\&=\begin{cases} 3+\left(\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{4}-\sqrt{\frac{3(1+\sqrt{5})}{8}}\right)(a-1) & 1\leq a\leq a^* \\\sqrt{2a}+\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2} & a\geq a^*,\end{cases}\end{align*}
where $a^*=\frac{(5+3\sqrt{5})+\sqrt{66+30\sqrt{5}}}{2}\approx 11.62.$
\begin{remark}
Note that for large $a$, this bound is weaker than the volume constraint $c\geq 2\sqrt{a}.$ This is to be expected because for large values of $a,$ the volume bound may be recovered by merging all columns, using an even index split, and ignoring the genus bound. For large $a$, the index bound is stronger than the genus bound, and in order to use the genus bound as above, we could not guarantee an equal index split in the column considered. 

For $1\leq a\leq 2.869,$ this bound is sharper than the volume bound.
\end{remark}

Case 2: There do not exist any repeated columns. In this case, for any fixed $\varepsilon>0$, there are only finitely many columns of the form $\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^d\\ \Lambda^1 \\ \Lambda^2\end{pmatrix}$ with $d\leq1/\varepsilon.$ Let $N=N(\varepsilon)$ be an upper bound for the number of such columns.

We merge all columns with $d>1/\varepsilon$ in the above notation, resulting in a column of the form $C_1=\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^r\\ \Lambda^1 \\ \Lambda^2\end{pmatrix}$ with $d'-r<N/\varepsilon$ since at most $N$ columns are excluded from the merge, each with exponent of $e_{11}$ smaller than $1/\varepsilon$ in the first entry. Also, let $C_2=\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^s\\ \tilde{\Lambda}^1 \\ \tilde{\Lambda}^2\end{pmatrix}$ be the merge of the remaining columns, so $s<N/\varepsilon.$ Let $p,q>0$  so that $e_{pq}$ contains the $\tilde{\Lambda_i}$ as subsets. Here $p,q$ are chosen to be independent of $d'$, but may depend on $\varepsilon.$ Note that $c_{pq}(\Lambda^i)$ is bounded by a constant (depending on $\varepsilon$) multiple of $d'$ by the assumption on $x(\Lambda^i)$ and $y(\Lambda^i).$

We may estimate 

\begin{align*}
\frac{d'^2+3d'}{2}=I(\Lambda^i\tilde{\Lambda^i})\leq I(e_{pq}\Lambda^i)=I(e_{pq})+I(\Lambda^i)+2c_{pq}(\Lambda^i)
\end{align*}

to conclude that the difference $\frac{d'^2}{2}-I(\Lambda^i)$ is bounded above by a constant multiple of $d'.$

So, in this case, after taking the limit $d'\to\infty$, we no longer have the constraint \newline $X_1+Y_1\geq\frac{2}{3+\sqrt{5}}$, but we obtain a new constraint that $X_iY_i\geq\frac{1}{4}$ for $i=1,2.$

Also, the action inequality for $C_1$ reads

\begin{align*}
rc&\geq x(\Lambda^1)+ay(\Lambda^1)+x(\Lambda^2)+ay(\Lambda^2)
\\ c&\geq\left(\frac{1}{d'+N/\varepsilon}\right)\left(x(\Lambda^1)+ay(\Lambda^1)+x(\Lambda^2)+ay(\Lambda^2)\right).
\end{align*}

Dividing through all constraints by leading powers of $d'$ and taking $d'$ to be sufficiently large compared to $\varepsilon$, we obtain

\end{align*}

This bound for $c$ is always larger than the bound we found in case $1,$ so the result follows.

Case 1: No column is repeated as $d\to\infty$.

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and merge all columns of the form $$\begin{pmatrix}e_{10}^ae_{01}^b\\ \tilde{\Lambda^1}\\ \tilde{\Lambda^2}\end{pmatrix}$$ for which $\frac{1}{a+b}<\varepsilon.$ Let $\{C_k\}_{k\in S}$ be this set of columns. By assumption, there are only finitely many possible columns which violate this inequality, say $N=N(\varepsilon).$

Call the resulting merged column $$\begin{pmatrix}e_{10}^Ae_{01}^B\\ \Lambda^1 \\ \Lambda^2\end{pmatrix}.$$

There are at most $N$ columns that are excluded from this merge, all of the form $\begin{pmatrix}e_{10}^xe_{01}^y\\ \star \\ \star\end{pmatrix}$ with $x+y\leq 1/\varepsilon.$ Since the sum of the exponents of $e_{10}$ and $e_{01}$ in the first entry of all columns are both $d$, it follows that $d-A$ and $d-B$ are both bounded above by $N/\varepsilon.$ Also, for $k\in S$, if $C_k=\begin{pmatrix}e_{10}^{a_k}e_{01}^{b_k}\\ \Lambda^1_k \\ \Lambda^2_k\end{pmatrix},$ then

$$\frac{|S|}{\varepsilon}\leq\sum_{k\in S}(a_k+b_k)\leq\sum_{i=1}^n (a_i+b_i)=2d,$$

so we may bound $|S|\leq 2d\varepsilon.$

The action inequality then reads

\begin{align*}
(x(\Lambda^1)+x(\Lambda^2))+a(y(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^2))&\leq c(A+B)\\ &\leq 2dc,
\end{align*} which we rearrange to obtain the constraint
$$c\geq\frac{1}{2d} \left(x(\Lambda^1)+x(\Lambda^2)+a(y(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^2))\right).$$

The genus bound applied to the merged column reads:
\begin{align*}
x(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^1)+x(\Lambda^2)+y(\Lambda^2)&\geq 2(A+B)-|S|\\ &\geq 4d-4N/\varepsilon-2d\varepsilon
\end{align*}

The index equality reads:

\begin{align*}
2(AB+A+B)&=I(\Lambda^1)+I(\Lambda^2)\leq 2(1+o(1))\sum_{i=1}^2 x(\Lambda^i)y(\Lambda^i)\\
\sum_{i=1}^2 x(\Lambda^i)y(\Lambda^i)&\geq\frac{1}{1+o(1)}(AB+A+B)\\ &\geq \frac{d^2+O(d)}{1+o(1)}
\end{align*}

Since $d$ may be taken to be arbitrarily large and $\varepsilon$ may be taken to be arbitrarily small, independently of $d$, we may take limits to find that

$$c\geq \min \left\{\frac{1}{2}\left(x_1+x_2+a(y_1+y_2)\right)\bigg\vert\ x_1y_1+x_2y_2\geq 1 \text{ and } x_1+y_1+x_2+y_2\geq 4\right\}.$$

Solving this optimization problem, we obtain:

\begin{align*}c\geq \begin{cases} 2+\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)(a-1) & 1\leq a\leq 7+4\sqrt{3}\\\sqrt{a} & a\geq 7+4\sqrt{3}.\end{cases}
\end{align*}

For any such column, we have the relations $rs=2RS$ and $r+s=2(R+S)-1$ from Lemma \ref{lem:repeated-column}.

Now, let $\begin{pmatrix}e_{10}^Ae_{01}^B\\ \tilde{\Lambda^1}\\ \emptyset\end{pmatrix}$ be the merge of all columns with an empty generator, and let $C=\begin{pmatrix}e_{10}^{d-A}e_{01}^{d-B}\\ \Lambda^1\\ \Lambda^2\end{pmatrix}$ be the merge of the other columns. 

The action inequality applied to $C$ reads $$c(2d-A-B)\geq \sum_{i=1}^2 x(\Lambda^i)+ay(\Lambda^i).$$

By the assumed index split we have $I(\Lambda^2)=d^2$ to first order, and hence by similar estimates to the previous cases:

$$x(\Lambda^2)y(\Lambda^2)\geq \frac{1}{2}d^2+O(d).$$

The genus bound applied to $C$ gives

$$\sum_{i=1}^2 x(\Lambda^i)+y(\Lambda^i)\geq 2(2d-A-B)-k,$$

where $k$ is the number of columns merged to produce $C.$

Also, applying the index equality to the column $C$ produces.

$$x(\Lambda^1)y(\Lambda^1)+x(\Lambda^2)y(\Lambda^2)\geq (d-A)(d-B)+O(d).$$

We also have a constraint that if a column $\begin{pmatrix}e_{10}^Re_{01}^S\\ e_{rs} \\ \emptyset\end{pmatrix}$ occurs, then considering the index of the column obtained by merging it with $C$ gives

$$2c_{rs}(\Lambda_1)=I(e_{10}^{d-A+R}e_{01}^{d-B+S})-I(e_{10}^Re_{01}^S)-I(e_{10}^{d-A}e_{01}^{d-B})$$

from Lemma \ref{lem:index-shift}.

By the assumption that $R$ and $S$ stay bounded, we find $$c_{rs}(\Lambda_1)=R(d-B)+S(d-A)+O(1).$$ As in the analogous case in Theorem 1.2, this means that the point $(x(\Lambda^1),y(\Lambda^1))$ lies on or above the line $$sx+ry=R(d-B)+S(d-A)+O(1).$$ This gives a new constraint

\begin{align*}
\text{max}(r,s)(x(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^1))&\geq sx(\Lambda^1)+ry(\Lambda^1)\\ &\geq S(d-A)+R(d-B)+O(1)\\ &\geq\text{max}(S,R)\cdot\text{min}(d-A,d-B)+O(1).
\end{align*}

The relation $r+s=2(R+S)-1$ implies that $\frac{\text{max}(S,R)}{\text{max}(s,r)}\geq\frac{1}{4},$ so

$$x(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^1)\geq \frac{1}{4}\text{min}(d-A,d-B)+O(1).$$

By merging the first two columns with an empty generator and considering the index, we find that $$I(e_{10}^{R+P}e_{01}^{S+Q})=I(e_{10}^Re_{01}^S)+I(e_{01}^Re_{01}^Q),$$ which is only possible when either $R=P=0$ or $S=Q=0.$ In either case, the equalities of Lemma 3.3 guarantee that the nonzero exponent is $1.$ Now, if either $(r,s)$ or $(p,q)$ equals $(0,1)$, then the action inequality applied to either column reads $c\geq a,$ which is a stronger bound than our statement when $a\geq 2$.


The action inequality applied to $C$ then reads

\begin{align*}
c(A+B)&\geq (x(\Lambda^1)+x(\Lambda^2))+a(y(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^2))\\&=\left(x(\Lambda^1)+x(\Lambda^2)+y(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^2)\right)
\end{align*}

The righthand side is at least $2(A+B)-1$ by the genus bound, so by taking $A+B\to\infty$ we obtain $c\geq 2$, which is our claimed bound when $1\leq a\leq 2.$

Hence, if either $(r,s)$ or $(p,q)$ is $(0,1),$ then $c\geq\text{min}(a,2)$

So, in this sub-case, we are just left with the possibility $(r,s)=(p,q)=(1,0).$ Since the roles of $e_{10}$ and $e_{01}$ are symmetric for the positive boundary $P(c,c)$, we may assume without loss of generality that $(R,S)=(P,Q)=(1,0)$ and so the two repeated columns are $\begin{pmatrix}e_{10}\\ \emptyset \\ e_{10}\end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix}e_{10}\\ e_{10}\\ \emptyset\end{pmatrix}.$

                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     

Since both repeated columns have $e_{10}$ in the first component, we may assume that $B>A$ in the definition of the column $C.$

By merging the repeated columns with $C$ and comparing index, we find that $y(\Lambda^1)=y(\Lambda^2)=B.$

The action inequality applied to $C$ then reads

\begin{align*}

&=\left(x(\Lambda^1)+x(\Lambda^2)+y(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^2)\right)+2B(a-1)
\end{align*}

Combining with the genus bound $$x(\Lambda^1)+x(\Lambda^2)+y(\Lambda^1)+y(\Lambda^2)\geq 2(A+B)-1$$ and taking $A+B\to\infty$ gives the bound $c\geq 2+(a-1)=1+a$ (since $B>A$ by assumption).

So, altogether in the case that there are repeated columns of both forms, we find $c\geq\text{min}(2,a,1+a)=\text{min}(2,a),$ which agrees with our claimed bound for $c$ when $1\leq a\leq 2$ and is larger than it for $a>2.$

Taking the minimum of the possible lower bounds for $c$ as a function of $a$ in all above cases gives the statement of Theorem \ref{thm:two-polydisks-polydisk}.

Let $X_{\Omega_i}$ for $i=1,\ldots,m$ and $X_{\Omega'}$ be concave toric domains. Suppose that there is a symplectic embedding $\varphi:\coprod_{i=1}^m X_{\Omega_i}\hookrightarrow X_{\Omega'}$. \item{(a)} We have $I(\Lambda')=\sum_{i=1}^m I(\Lambda^i).$
\item(b) For each subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$, we have $\sum_{i=1}^m I(\prod_{j\in S}\Lambda_j^i)=I(\prod_{j\in S}\Lambda_j').$

$$c_{11}(\Lambda_j')\geq c_{11}(\Lambda_j^i),$$

\end{itemize}

The inequality in part $(e)$ may be given as in \cite{Beyond} as a result of facts about the topological complexity $J_0$ of a curve. We instead give a direct proof from the relative adjunction formula, with the same types of arguments as we will see in the appendix. In the following, $n(C_j)$ is the number of ends (positive or negative) of the curve $C_j,$ and $n^\pm(C_j)$ are the number of positive and negative ends. Also, $n_e^\pm$ and $n_h^\pm$ are respectively the number of elliptic and hyperbolic orbits at positive/negative ends of $C_j$.  

We recall the relative adjunction formula (see \cite{LectureNotes} Section 3.3 in the case of a symplectization, though it remains unchanged for the cobordism case)

$$c_\tau(C)=\chi(C)+Q_\tau(C)+w_\tau(C)-2\delta(C),$$

where $C\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)$ is a somewhere injective curve and $\delta(C)$ is a weighted count of singularities of $C.$ For generic $J$, connected curves $C$ of ECH index zero in a cobordism have $\delta(C)=0$ by the index inequality $\text{ind}(C)\leq I(C)-2\delta(C)$ (see 3.3 in \cite{LectureNotes} for the statement in symplectizations and Section 3.9 for the discussion of the cobordism case). As $J$ is generic, the Fredholm index on the lefthand side must be non-negative, which forces $\delta(C)=0$ too.  We apply this to $C=C_j$ in our factorization and use the formulae in Section 4 to evaluate the terms

\begin{itemize}
\item $c_\tau(C_j)=x(\Lambda'_j)+y(\Lambda'_j)-\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda_j^i)+y(\Lambda_j^i)$
\item $Q_\tau(C_j)=2\text{Area}(\Lambda'_j)-2\sum_{i=1}^m \text{Area}(\Lambda_j^i)$ 
\end{itemize}

and hence obtain

$$
x(\Lambda_j')+y(\Lambda_j')-\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda_j^i)+y(\Lambda_j^i)=2\text{Area}(\Lambda_j')-2\sum_{i=1}^m\text{Area}(\Lambda_j^i)+w_\tau(C_j)+\chi(C_j).
$$

To simplify this, we use the identity $$2\text{Area}(\Lambda)=I(\Lambda)-(x(\Lambda)+y(\Lambda)+m(\Lambda)-h(\Lambda)),$$ which follows from Pick's Theorem. Note that $h(\Lambda_j')=0$ by assumption.

\begin{align*}
2\left(x(\Lambda_j')+y(\Lambda_j')\right)-2\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda_j^i)+y(\Lambda_j^i)&=\sum_{i=1}^m \left(m(\Lambda_j^i)-h(\Lambda_j^i)\right)-m(\Lambda_j')\\&+w_\tau(C_j)+2-2g(C_j)-n(C_j)
\end{align*}

We use the trivial bound $g(C_j)\geq 0$ and rearrange to obtain

\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda_j^i)+y(\Lambda_j^i)-\frac{1}{2}h(\Lambda_j^i)&\geq x(\Lambda_j')+y(\Lambda_j')-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^m m(\Lambda_j^i)+\frac{1}{2}m(\Lambda_j')-1\\ &+\frac{1}{2}\left(n-w_\tau(C)\right).
\end{align*}

We evaluate the last term as follows. Since $0=\text{ind}(C_j)=I(C_j)=0$, the equality condition for the index inequality in \cite{LectureNotes} tells us each positive end of $C_j$ is asymptotic to a simple Reeb orbit and no two ends of $C_j$ are asymptotic to the same orbit. We may write $w_\tau(C_j)=w_\tau^+(C_j)-w_\tau^-(C_j)$ where $w_\tau^\pm(C_j)$ is the contribution to $w_\tau(C_j)$ from the positive/negative orbits.  From the positive partition conditions, we have $w_\tau^+(C_j)=CZ^{I,+}(C_j)-CZ^{\text{ind},+}(C_j),$ where the superscript $+$ denotes the contribution to the Conley-Zehnder term from only the positive ends. In our trivialization, each end at an elliptic orbit contributes $1$ to the $CZ^{\text{ind},+}$ term and contributes its multiplicity to $CZ^{I,+}$, which is also $1.$ So, altogether, $w_\tau^+(C_j)=0$

                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              

By the above negative partition conditions, $w_\tau^-(C_j)= CZ^I(C_j)-CZ^{\text{ind}}(C_j)$ from the writhe bound. In our trivialization, each elliptic orbit $e_{ab}$ has a Conley-Zehnder index of $1$ and each hyperbolic orbit $h_{ab}$ has Conley-Zehnder index of $0$. Since each hyperbolic orbit has multiplicity $1$, we get a total contribution of $\sum_{i=1}^m h(\Lambda_j^i)-\sum_{i=1}^m m(\Lambda_j^i)$ to $CZ^I(C_j)$ from the negative orbits. We similarly see that the contribution to $CZ^\text{ind}(C)$ from the negative orbits may be computed as 

\begin{align*}n_e^-(C_j)&=n^-(C_j)-n_h^-(C_j)\\ &=n^-(C_j)-\sum_{i=1}^m h(\Lambda_j^i).
\end{align*}Substituting this data into the above yields

\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda_j^i)+y(\Lambda_j^i)-\frac{1}{2}h(\Lambda_j^i)&\geq x(\Lambda_j')+y(\Lambda_j')+\frac{1}{2}m(\Lambda_j')-1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^m m(\Lambda_j^i)+\frac{1}{2}n(C_j)\\ &-\frac{1}{2}\left(-\sum_{i=1}^m m(\Lambda_j^i)+\sum_{i=1}^m h(\Lambda_j^i)+n^-(C)-\sum_{i=1}^m h(\Lambda_j^i)\right),
\end{align*}
which we may rearrange to achieve the genus bound: 
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda_j^i)+y(\Lambda_j^i)-\frac{1}{2}h(\Lambda_j^i)&\geq x(\Lambda_j')+y(\Lambda_j')+\frac{1}{2}m(\Lambda_j')-1+\frac{1}{2}n^+(C_j)\\
&= x(\Lambda_j')+y(\Lambda_j')+m(\Lambda_j')-1.
\end{align*}

Finally, condition (g) comes from intersection positivity. It is proved in \cite{Quant} that $I(C_1+C_2)\geq I(C_1)+I(C_2)+2C_1\cdot C_2,$ where $C_1\cdot C_2$ is the the algebraic count of intersections of $C_1$ with $C_2.$ In particular, $I(C_1+C_2)\geq I(C_1)+I(C_2)$ when $C_1$ and $C_2$ have no components in common.

Let $C_1$ be the curve $C_j$ in the factorization described above. Let $C_2$ be a $J$-holomorphic curve guaranteed by proof of the main theorem with positive end given by $e_{10}$ with index split $I_j=2$ (and hence $I_k=0$ for $k\neq j$). Both curves are necessarily connected and the index assumption $I(\Lambda_j')>2$ guarantees that they are not equal. The only two cases for the negative ends of $C_2$ are $e_{10}$ and $e_{01}.$ 

If the negative end of $C_2$ is given by $e_{10},$ then the above index inequality becomes $$I(\Lambda'_j e_{10})-I(\Lambda^i_j e_{10})\geq I(\Lambda'_j)-I(\Lambda^i_j).$$ Counting lattice points shows that $I(\Lambda e_{10})=I(\Lambda)+2y(\Lambda)+2$ for any convex generator $\Lambda,$ and substituting this gives $y(\Lambda'_j)\geq y(\Lambda^i_j).$ If instead, the negative end of $C_2$ is given by $e_{01}$, then we get an inequality $y(\Lambda'_j)\geq x(\Lambda^i_j),$ so in either case $y(\Lambda'_j)\geq\text{min}(x(\Lambda_j^i),y(\Lambda_j^i)).$ Repeating the argument with a curve $C_2$ with a positive end of $e_{01}$ completes the argument.

Given a concave generator $\Lambda$, applying Pick's Theorem to the index formula for a concave generator yields $2\text{Area}(\Lambda)=I(\Lambda)-x(\Lambda)-y(\Lambda)+m(\Lambda)-h(\Lambda).$ From considering the relative adjunction formula applied to the $j^{\text{th}}$ column of the matrix as in the convex case, we see

\begin{align*}
x(\Lambda_j')+y(\Lambda_j')-\sum_{i=1}^m \left(x(\Lambda_j^i)+y(\Lambda_j^i)\right)&=2\text{Area}(\Lambda_j')-2\sum_{i=1}^m\text{Area}(\Lambda_j^i)+2-2g-n+w_\tau(C_j).
\end{align*}

Applying Pick's formula in the same manner as in the convex case and rearranging yields:

\begin{align*}
2\left(x(\Lambda_j')+y(\Lambda_j')\right)-2\sum_{i=1}^m \left(x(\Lambda_j^i)+y(\Lambda_j^i)\right)&=\left(m(\Lambda_j')-\sum_{i=1}^m m(\Lambda_j^i)\right)-h(\Lambda_j')+2-2g-n+w_\tau(C_j).
\end{align*}

Finally, we use $g(C_j)\geq 0$ to obtain

\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda_j^i)+y(\Lambda_j^i)&\geq x(\Lambda_j')+y(\Lambda_j')-1+\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^m m(\Lambda_j^i)-m(\Lambda_j') \right)\\ &+\frac{1}{2}h(\Lambda_j')+\frac{1}{2}(n-w_\tau(C_j)).
\end{align*}

As in the convex case, since $\text{ind}(C_j)=I(C_j),$ we find that $w_\tau(C_j)=CZ^I(C_j)-CZ^{\text{ind}}(C_j)$ from the equality condition for the index inequality. Compared to the convex case, the partition conditions are reversed (see Section 3.9 in \cite{LectureNotes}). So, each positive simple elliptic orbit (at which $C_j$ has an end at a cover of) contributes $-1$ to $CZ^{\text{ind}}(C_j)$ and the negative of its multiplicity to $CZ^I(C_j)$. Each positive hyperbolic end contributes $0$ to both, so, altogether, we have $$w_\tau^+(C_j)=n_e^+(C_j)-m_e(\Lambda_j')=n^+(C_j)-m(\Lambda_j'),$$ where $m_e$ denotes the total multiplicity of all elliptic factors. The second equality holds because each positive hyperbolic orbit contributes $1$ to both terms on the righthand side. Each negative end (at an elliptic orbit by assumption) contributes $+1$ to both $CZ^{I}$ and to $CZ^{\text{ind}}$ since these ends are simple. So, altogether, $w_\tau(C_j)=w_\tau^+(C_j)=n^+(C_j)-m(\Lambda_j')$ with the same notation as in the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main-convex}.

Hence, we find 
\begin{align*}
\frac{1}{2}(n(C)-w_\tau(C_j))&=\frac{1}{2}\left(n^+(C_j)+n^-(C_j)-n^+(C_j)+m(\Lambda'_j)\right)\\ &=\frac{1}{2}n^{-}(C_j)+\frac{1}{2}m(\Lambda_j')\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^m m(\Lambda_j^i)+\frac{1}{2}m(\Lambda_j'),
\end{align*}
and the genus bound follows.

We recall from \cite{Concave} that the relative intersection number $Q_\tau$ may be defined on a pair $(C,C')$ by 

$$Q_\tau(C,\tilde{C})=C\cdot \tilde{C}-\ell_\tau(C,\tilde{C}).$$

Since this is bilinear in the relative homology classes $[C],[\tilde{C}]$, it may be evaluated in terms of $Q_\tau(C)$ and $Q_\tau(\tilde{C})$ separately by a polarization identity:

\begin{align*}Q_\tau(C,\tilde{C})&=\frac{1}{2}\left(Q_\tau(C\cup \tilde{C})-Q_\tau(C)-Q_\tau(\tilde{C})\right)\\
&=\left(\text{Area}(\Lambda'\tilde{\Lambda}')-\sum_{i=1}^m \text{Area}(\Lambda^i\tilde{\Lambda}^i)\right)\\ &\phantom{{}={}}-\left(\text{Area}(\Lambda')-\sum_{i=1}^m \text{Area}(\Lambda^i)\right)-\left(\text{Area}(\tilde{\Lambda}')-\sum_{i=1}^m \text{Area}(\tilde{\Lambda}^i)\right).
\end{align*}

By the bounds for linking numbers in \cite{LectureNotes}, each term $\ell_\tau(\zeta^+,\tilde{\zeta}^+)$ is $\leq 0,$ and $\ell_\tau(\zeta^-,\tilde{\zeta}^-)$ is greater than or equal to the common minimum winding number of $\zeta^-,\tilde{\zeta}^-$ around their associated orbit $\gamma^-.$ 

For a fixed simple elliptic Reeb orbit $\gamma^-$ in $\partial X_{\Omega_j}$, let $q_1,\ldots,q_r$ and $\tilde{q}_1,\ldots,\tilde{q}_s$ denote the multiplicities of the ends of $C$ and $\tilde{C}$, respectively, at $\gamma^-$. In particular, $q:=\sum_{i=1}^r q_i$ equals the exponent of $\gamma$ in the factorization of $\Lambda^j,$ and likewise $\tilde{q}:=\sum_{i=1}^s \tilde{q}_i$ for $\tilde{\Lambda}^j$.

Then, the linking number of $C$ and $\tilde{C}$ at $\gamma^-$ is greater than or equal to $\sum_{i,j}\text{min}(q_i,\tilde{q}_j).$ It is straightforward to verify 

$$\sum_{i,j}\text{min}(q_i,\tilde{q}_j)\geq \text{min}(q,\tilde{q}),$$

and the result follows by summing over all Reeb orbits, and using the nonnegativity of the signed intersection number $C\cdot\tilde{C}.$

We use the above proposition to verify that there cannot exist a $J$-holomorphic current $ \mathcal{C}$ in the completed cobordism that is described by the column $\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^5\\e_{10}^2e_{11}\\e_{10}^3e_{01}\\e_{10}^3e_{01}\end{pmatrix},$ even though such a current is not ruled out by Theorem \ref{thm:main-convex}.

Let $\mathcal{C}'$ be a current with positive ends given by $e_{11}^3$ and index split \newline$I(\Lambda^1)=I(\Lambda^2)=I(\Lambda^3)=6$, as in the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main-convex}. It must be connected, as a direct search reveals that there does not exist a factorization $$\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}\\ \Lambda_1^1\\ \Lambda_1^2\\ \Lambda^3_1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^2\\ \Lambda_2^1\\ \Lambda^2_2\\ \Lambda^3_2\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^3\\ \Lambda^1\\ \Lambda^2\\ \Lambda^3\end{pmatrix}$$ or $$\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}\\ \Lambda^1_1\\ \Lambda_1^2\\ \Lambda_1^3\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}\\ \Lambda_2^1\\ \Lambda_2^2\\ \Lambda_2^3\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}\\ \Lambda_3^1\\ \Lambda_3^2\\ \Lambda_3^3\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^3\\ \Lambda^1\\ \Lambda^2\\ \Lambda^3\end{pmatrix},$$ where each column satisfies the index constraint and the righthand side of either equation has the prescribed index split.

Note that $\mathcal{C}$ cannot have $\mathcal{C}'$ as a component because the generator $e_{10}^2e_{11}$ in the first negative component does not have any index $6$ factor. Hence Proposition A.1 applies.

If, for example, the curve $\mathcal{C}'$ is given by the column $\begin{pmatrix}e_{11}^3\\ e_{10}^3\\e_{10}^3\\ e_{10}^3\end{pmatrix}$ then the inequality of Proposition A.1 becomes

\begin{equation}\label{eq:example-bound}\sum_i \text{min}(3,m_{10}(\Lambda^i))+3\sum_i y(\Lambda^i)\leq 15\end{equation}

since $\text{Area}(e_{10}^3\Lambda)=\text{Area}(\Lambda)+3y(\Lambda)$ for any convex generator $\Lambda$.
This rules out the existence of a current $\mathcal{C}$ as the lefthand side of (\ref{eq:example-bound}) is $(2+3+3)+3(1+1+1)=17>15.$ 

The other possibilities for the ends of $\mathcal{C}'$ yield a similar contradiction.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:uniqueness-index-zero}
Let $C\in \mathcal{M}^J(\alpha,\beta)$ satisfy the following conditions. 
\begin{itemize}
\item All components of $C$ have genus zero.
\item $C$ is somewhere-injective.
\item The ECH index of $C$ is zero.
\item All orbits in $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are elliptic.
\end{itemize}

Then, $C$ is the unique index zero and somewhere-injective element of $\mathcal{M}^J(\alpha,\beta).$

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:area-lemma}
Let $C=\sum_{j=1}^k C_j\in \mathcal{M}^J(\alpha,\beta)$ be somewhere-injective, with all orbits in $\alpha$ and $\beta$ elliptic. Let $\Lambda^+$ and $\Lambda^1,\ldots,\Lambda^m$ be the convex generators corresponding to $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Then,

\begin{align*}
&\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda^i)+y(\Lambda^i)\\=&x(\Lambda^+)+y(\Lambda^+) + \frac{1}{2}\left(m(\Lambda^+)-\sum_{i=1}^m m(\Lambda^i)\right)-k \\ &+ \sum_{j=1}^k g(C_j)+\frac{1}{2}\left(n(C)-w_\tau(C)-I(C)\right)+\delta(C),
\end{align*}
where $n(C)$ is the number of ends of $C$ (or equivalently, the number of punctures of the domain Riemann surface).
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
This is an application of the relative adjunction formula $$c_\tau(C)=\chi(C)+Q_\tau(C)+w_\tau(C)-2\delta(C).$$ These terms may be evaluated as in our proof of the genus bound:
\begin{itemize}
\item $c_\tau(C)=x(\Lambda^+)+y(\Lambda^+)-\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda^i)+y(\Lambda^i)$
\item $Q_\tau(C)=2\text{Area}(\Lambda^+)-2\sum_{i=1}^m \text{Area}(\Lambda^i).$ 
\item $\chi(C)=2-2\sum_{j=1}^k g(C_j)-n(C).$ 
\end{itemize}


Also, whenever $\Lambda$ is a convex generator with all edges labeled ``$e$'', Pick's formula gives 

\begin{align*}
2\text{Area}(\Lambda)&=2L(\Lambda)-2-(x(\Lambda)+y(\Lambda)+m(\Lambda))\\ &=I(\Lambda)-x(\Lambda)-y(\Lambda)-m(\Lambda).
\end{align*}

Substituting into the relative adjunction formula, and using the condition $$I(C)=I(\Lambda^+)-\sum_{i=1}^m I(\Lambda^i)$$ gives the desired result.
<!-- end proof-14 -->
<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
Suppose that $C\in \mathcal{M}^J(\alpha,\beta)$ has index zero and no multiply covered components. Then, for every simple orbit $\alpha_i$ appearing in $\alpha,$ each end of $C$ at a cover of $\alpha_i$ has multiplicity $1.$ Also, no two ends of $C$ are asymptotic to covers of the same simple negative Reeb orbit $\beta_i$ appearing in $\beta$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
This follows from the equality condition for the ``partition conditions'' described in Section 3.9 of \cite{LectureNotes}.
<!-- end proof-15 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Suppose for contradiction that $C'$ is another current in the same moduli space $\mathcal{M}^J(\alpha,\beta)$. If $C$ and $C'$ have any components in common, we delete those from both curves and replace $C$ and $C'$ with the remaining sub-currents.

Let $k$ be the number of components of $C$.

The assumptions on $C$ imply that $I(C)=\delta(C)=g(C_j)=0$ in the statement of Lemma \ref{lem:area-lemma} and so we obtain:

\begin{align*}
&\left(\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda^i)+y(\Lambda^i)+\frac{1}{2}m(\Lambda^i)\right)\\
=&\left(x(\Lambda^+)+y(\Lambda^+)+\frac{1}{2}m(\Lambda^+)\right)-k+\frac{1}{2}\left(n(C)-w_\tau(C)\right).
\end{align*}

Since $C'$ has ends at the same Reeb orbits with multiplicity as $C$, the lefthand side is equal to $Q_\tau(C,C)=Q_\tau(C),$ which we compute as

$$2\text{Area}(\Lambda^+)-2\sum_{i=1}^m \text{Area}(\Lambda^i)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^m x(\Lambda^i)+y(\Lambda^i)+m(\Lambda^i)\right)-x(\Lambda^+)-y(\Lambda^+)-m(\Lambda^+).$$

Put $m^+=m(\Lambda^+)$ and $m^-=\sum_{i=1}^m m(\Lambda^i).$ Then combining with the previous identity yields

\begin{align*}
C\cdot C'&=Q_\tau(C,C')+\ell_\tau(C,C')\\
&=-k+\ell_\tau(C,C')+\frac{1}{2}\left(n(C)-m^++m^--w_\tau(C)\right) \tag{*}
\end{align*}

where the first equality is from the definition of $Q_\tau$ in \cite{Quant}. We recall the definition 

$$\ell_\tau(C,C')=\sum_{\gamma^+}\sum_{\zeta^+,\tilde{\zeta}^+}\ell_\tau(\zeta^+,\tilde{\zeta}^+)-\sum_{\gamma^-}\sum_{\zeta^-,\tilde{\zeta}^-}\ell_\tau(\zeta^-,\tilde{\zeta}^-),$$

where $\gamma^{\pm},\tilde{\gamma}^{\pm}$ run over the set of positive/negative simple Reeb orbits at which $C,C'$ have ends at covers of, and $\zeta^{\pm},\tilde{\zeta}^{\pm}$ are the (possibly empty) braids around $\gamma^{\pm}$ corresponding to the ends of $C$ and $C'.$


The righthand side of (*) is $-k$ plus a sum of contributions from each simple Reeb orbit in $\alpha$ and $\beta$, separately. We check that each Reeb orbit contributes nonpositively to the sum. Let $\gamma$ be a simple Reeb orbit appearing in $\alpha$ with multiplicity $q$. The linking bound for positive orbits in \cite{LectureNotes} shows that it contributes nonpositively to the $\ell_\tau$ term in the sum. Its contribution to the rest of the righthand side is $$\frac{1}{2}\left(n(\gamma)-m(\gamma)-(m(\gamma)-n(\gamma))\right)=0$$ by the partition data in the previous lemma. 

If instead $\gamma$ is a simple orbit appearing in $\beta$, with multiplicity $q$, let $q=q_1+\ldots+q_r$ be the partition associated to the ends of $C'$ at $\gamma$. Then the linking bound tells us that the contribution to $\ell_\tau$ is at most $$-\sum_{i=1}^r\text{min}(q,q_i)=-q,$$ since $C$ has a single end at $\gamma.$ The contribution to the rest of the righthand side from $\gamma$ is $$\frac{1}{2}(1+q-(1-q))=q,$$ so altogether, $\gamma$ contributes nonpositively in this case too. Hence we find $C\cdot C'\leq -1$, which contradicts intersection positivity.
