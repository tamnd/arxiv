<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
Let $(\mathfrak a,\{-,-\})$ be a Lie affgebra and let $P:\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a$ be an affine map such that $P(\{x,y\}_P)=\{P(x),P(y)\}$ for all $x,y\in\mathfrak a$. Then $\{-,-\}_P$ is a Lie affgebra bracket, and $P$ is a homomorphism from the descendant Lie affgebra to the original one. Its linear part is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight $-1$ on every tangent Lie algebra.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
Ordered affine Lie factorizations of a Lie affgebra $\mathfrak a$ are in bijection with pairs $(P,e)$ consisting of an idempotent affine Rota-Baxter operator and an idempotent fixed point at which it is split. The factors are $\operatorname{Im}P$ and $P^{-1}(e)$, and $P$ is the projection onto the first factor along the second. The complementary map $Q_e(x)=\langle x,P(x),e\rangle$ is an idempotent affine Rota-Baxter operator corresponding to the reversed factorization.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
The affine Rota-Baxter operators on the canonical Lie affgebra associated with $\mathfrak g$ are exactly the maps
$$
P(x_++x_-)=x_++p,
\quad x_+\in\mathfrak g_+,\quad x_-\in\mathfrak g_-,
$$
where $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_+\oplus\mathfrak g_-$ is a vector space decomposition into Lie subalgebras and $p\in\mathfrak g_-$ satisfies $[p,\mathfrak g_+]\subseteq\mathfrak g_+$. Every such operator is idempotent and split at $p$.
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
All vector and affine spaces are over the field $\mathbf{k}$ fixed above, and $\mathbf{k}^{\times}=\mathbf{k}\setminus\{0\}$. For a vector space $V$, we write $\operatorname{End}(V)=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbf{k}}(V)$ for its space of linear endomorphisms and $\operatorname{id}$ for the identity map. Products of maps denote composition, with $ST=S\circ T$. For linear endomorphisms, $[S,T]=ST-TS$. For a Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, we write $\operatorname{ad}_a(x)=[a,x]$ for $a,x\in\mathfrak g$.
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- defi-1: body_md -->
\label{d0}
\textup{\cite{ABR2025,OS1966}} An \textbf{affine space} over $\mathbf{k}$ is a nonempty set $\mathfrak a$ equipped with ternary operations
$$
\langle-,-,-\rangle:\mathfrak a^3\to\mathfrak a,
\quad
(-,-,-):\mathbf{k}\times\mathfrak a\times\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a
$$
satisfying the following conditions for all $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{k}$ and $x,y,z\in\mathfrak a$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*]
\item $(\mathfrak a,\langle-,-,-\rangle)$ is an abelian heap.
\item The maps $(\alpha,x,-):\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a$ and $(-,x,y):\mathbf{k}\to\mathfrak a$ are homomorphisms of heaps, where the heap operation on $\mathbf{k}$ is $\langle\alpha,\beta,\gamma\rangle=\alpha-\beta+\gamma$ for $\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbf{k}$.
\item For each $x$, the map $(-,x,-)$ is an action of the multiplicative monoid of $\mathbf{k}$, namely
$$
(1,x,y)=y,\quad (\alpha,x,(\beta,x,y))=(\alpha\beta,x,y).
$$
\item $(0,x,y)=x$.
\item $(\alpha,x,y)=\langle(\alpha,z,y),(\alpha,z,x),x\rangle$.
\end{enumerate}
For a fixed $x\in\mathfrak a$, we call $(-,x,-)$ the \textbf{affine action} with base $x$.

\textup{\cite{ABR2025}} A \textbf{Lie affgebra} is an affine space $\mathfrak a$ equipped with a biaffine map $\{-,-\}:\mathfrak a\times\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a$ such that, for all $x,y,z\in\mathfrak a$,
$$
\langle\{x,y\},\{x,x\},\{y,x\}\rangle
=
\{y,y\},
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\big\langle
&\{x,\{y,z\}\},
\{x,\{x,x\}\},
\{y,\{z,x\}\},
\{y,\{y,y\}\},
\{z,\{x,y\}\}
\big\rangle
=
\{z,\{z,z\}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
An affine map preserving the bracket is called a \textbf{homomorphism} of Lie affgebras. A bijective homomorphism is an \textbf{isomorphism}, and an isomorphism from a Lie affgebra to itself is an \textbf{automorphism}. A point $e\in\mathfrak a$ is \textbf{idempotent} if $\{e,e\}=e$.

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{p2}
\textup{\cite{G2012}} Let $\mathfrak g$ be a Lie algebra and let $R\in\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak g)$ satisfy $R^2=R$. Then $R$ is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight $-1$ if and only if $\operatorname{Im}R$ and $\ker R$ are Lie subalgebras of $\mathfrak g$.
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $R^2=R$, its image equals its fixed subspace:
$$
\operatorname{Im}R=\operatorname{Fix}R
:=\{x\in\mathfrak g\mid R(x)=x\}.
$$
Indeed, $R(Rx)=Rx$, and every fixed vector $x$ equals $Rx$. For each $x\in\mathfrak g$, the decomposition $x=Rx+(x-Rx)$ has $Rx\in\operatorname{Im}R$ and $x-Rx\in\ker R$. If $z$ belongs to both subspaces, then $z=Rz=0$. Consequently,
$$
\mathfrak g=\operatorname{Im}R\oplus\ker R.
$$

Suppose that $R$ satisfies the Rota-Baxter identity of weight $-1$. For $x,y\in\operatorname{Im}R$, that identity gives $[x,y]=R([x,y])$, so the image is a Lie subalgebra. For $x,y\in\ker R$, it gives $R([x,y])=0$, so the kernel is a Lie subalgebra.

Conversely, assume that both summands are Lie subalgebras. Write $x=x_++x_-$ and $y=y_++y_-$ according to the displayed direct sum. Then
$$
R\big([Rx,y]+[x,Ry]-[x,y]\big) =R\big([x_+,y_+]-[x_-,y_-]\big) =[x_+,y_+]=[Rx,Ry],
$$
because $R$ is the identity on its image and zero on its kernel. This proves the converse.

<!-- prop-7: body_md -->
\label{p3}
Let $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a(\mathfrak g;\kappa,\lambda,s)$ be a Lie affgebra and let $P:\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a$ be an affine map. Set $p=P(0)\in\mathfrak g$ and $R(x)=P(x)-P(0)$ for $x\in\mathfrak g$. Then $R\in\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak g)$ is the linear part of $P$ and $P(x)=R(x)+p$. The descendant operation is given by
\begin{equation}\label{e10}
\{x,y\}_P
=
[x,y]_{R,-1}
+\kappa R(x)
+(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(y-x)
+s+\kappa(p).
\end{equation}
Its bilinear part is $[-,-]_{R,-1}$ as defined in Eq.~(\ref{e7}). This formula holds for every linear map $R$.
<!-- end prop-7 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Using Eq.~(\ref{e2}) and $P(x)=R(x)+p$, we have
\begin{align*}
\{P(x),y\}
&=[R(x),y]+[p,y]+\kappa R(x)+\kappa(p)+\lambda(y)-\lambda R(x)-\lambda(p)+s,\\
\{x,y\}
&=[x,y]+\kappa(x)+\lambda(y)-\lambda(x)+s,\\
\{x,P(y)\}
&=[x,R(y)]+[x,p]+\kappa(x)+\lambda R(y)+\lambda(p)-\lambda(x)+s.
\end{align*}
In the chosen coordinates, the heap operation in Eq.~(\ref{e8}) is $a-b+c$. The terms involving $\kappa$, $\lambda$ and $s$ therefore combine as follows:
\begin{align*}
&\kappa(R(x)+p)-\kappa(x)+\kappa(x)=\kappa R(x)+\kappa(p),\\
&\lambda(y-R(x)-p)-\lambda(y-x)+\lambda(R(y)+p-x)=\lambda R(y-x),\\
&s-s+s=s.
\end{align*}
Moreover, $[p,y]+[x,p]=[p,y-x]=\operatorname{ad}_p(y-x)$. Hence Eq.~(\ref{e7}) gives
\begin{align*}
\{x,y\}_P
&=\{P(x),y\}-\{x,y\}+\{x,P(y)\}\\
&=[R(x),y]+[x,R(y)]-[x,y]+[p,y]+[x,p]\\
&\quad+\kappa R(x)+\kappa(p)+\lambda R(y-x)+s\\
&=[x,y]_{R,-1}+\kappa R(x)+(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(y-x)+s+\kappa(p),
\end{align*}
which is Eq.~(\ref{e10}).

<!-- thm-4: body_md -->
\label{t1}
Let $\mathfrak a$ be a Lie affgebra and let $P:\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a$ be an affine map. If $P$ satisfies Eq.~(\ref{e9}), then $\{-,-\}_P$ is a Lie affgebra bracket and $P:(\mathfrak a,\{-,-\}_P)\to(\mathfrak a,\{-,-\})$ is a Lie affgebra homomorphism. Moreover, the linear part of $P$ is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight $-1$ on every tangent Lie algebra.
<!-- end thm-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Choose an origin and write $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a(\mathfrak g;\kappa,\lambda,s)$. Set $p=P(0)$ and $R(x)=P(x)-P(0)$, so that $R$ is linear and $P(x)=R(x)+p$. We first derive the consequences of Eq.~(\ref{e9}). By Proposition~\ref{p3}, we have
\begin{align*}
P(\{x,y\}_P)
&=R([x,y]_{R,-1})+R\kappa R(x)
+R(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(y-x)\\
&\quad+R(s+\kappa(p))+p
\quad\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e10}))},\\
\{P(x),P(y)\}
&=[R(x)+p,R(y)+p]+\kappa(R(x)+p)
+\lambda(R(y)-R(x))+s\\
&=[R(x),R(y)]+\kappa R(x)
+(\lambda+\operatorname{ad}_p)R(y-x)+s+\kappa(p).
\end{align*}
Eq.~(\ref{e9}) equates these two expressions. Evaluating at $x=y=0$ gives the constant identity below. After subtracting the constant terms, setting $y=x$ gives the identity involving $\kappa$, and setting $x=0$ gives the identity involving $\lambda$. Removing these linear terms leaves the bilinear identity. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{e11}
\begin{gathered}
R([x,y]_{R,-1})=[R(x),R(y)],\quad R\kappa R=\kappa R,\\
R(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)=(\lambda+\operatorname{ad}_p)R,\\
R(s+\kappa(p))=s-p+\kappa(p).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first equality is the Rota-Baxter identity of weight $-1$. Consequently, $[-,-]_{R,-1}$ is a Lie bracket and $R:(\mathfrak g,[-,-]_{R,-1})\to(\mathfrak g,[-,-])$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

We next verify the structural identity for $\kappa R$ and $\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p$. By bilinearity, we have
\begin{align*}
[x-R(x),y-R(y)]
&=[x,y]-[x,R(y)]-[R(x),y]+[R(x),R(y)]\\
&=[R(x),R(y)]-\bigl([R(x),y]+[x,R(y)]-[x,y]\bigr)\\
&=[R(x),R(y)]-[x,y]_{R,-1}
\quad\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e7}))}.
\end{align*}
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{e12}
[x,y]_{R,-1}=[R(x),R(y)]-[x-R(x),y-R(y)].
\end{equation}
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{e13}
\begin{aligned}
(\operatorname{id}-R)([x,y]_{R,-1})
&=[x,y]_{R,-1}-[R(x),R(y)]
&&\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e11}))}\\
&=-[x-R(x),y-R(y)]
&&\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e12}))}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The linear identities in Eq.~(\ref{e11}) also give
\begin{equation}\label{e14}
\begin{aligned}
(\operatorname{id}-R)\kappa R
&=\kappa R-R\kappa R=0,\\
(\operatorname{id}-R)(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)
&=\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p-(\lambda+\operatorname{ad}_p)R\\
&=\operatorname{ad}_p(\operatorname{id}-R).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Using Eqs.~(\ref{e11})-(\ref{e14}) and $\operatorname{ad}_p(x)=[p,x]$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
&\quad (\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)([x,y]_{R,-1})
-[(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(x),y]_{R,-1}
+[x,\kappa R(y)]_{R,-1}
-[x,(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(y)]_{R,-1}\\
&=\lambda([R(x),R(y)])-[\lambda R(x),R(y)]
+[R(x),\kappa R(y)]-[R(x),\lambda R(y)]\\
&\quad+[p,[R(x),R(y)]]-[[p,R(x)],R(y)]-[R(x),[p,R(y)]]\\
&\quad-[p,[x-R(x),y-R(y)]]
+[[p,x-R(x)],y-R(y)]\\
&\quad+[x-R(x),[p,y-R(y)]]\\
&=0\quad\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e1}) and the Jacobi identity)}.
\end{align*}
In the last step, Eq.~(\ref{e1}) makes the first line on the right zero, while the next line and the final two lines vanish separately by the Jacobi identity. Therefore
\begin{align*}
(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)([x,y]_{R,-1})
=[(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(x),y]_{R,-1}
-[x,\kappa R(y)]_{R,-1}
+[x,(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(y)]_{R,-1}.
\end{align*}
This is the condition in Proposition~\ref{tstr} for the new bracket and endomorphisms.

We now verify the two affine identities directly. Eq.~(\ref{e10}) is biaffine and gives $\{x,x\}_P=\kappa R(x)+s+\kappa(p)$. Thus
\begin{align*}
\{x,y\}_P+\{y,x\}_P
&=[x,y]_{R,-1}+[y,x]_{R,-1}+\kappa R(x+y)
+(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(y-x+x-y)+2s+2\kappa(p)\\
&=\kappa R(x)+\kappa R(y)+2s+2\kappa(p)\\
&=\{x,x\}_P+\{y,y\}_P,
\end{align*}
which is affine antisymmetry. Setting $y=x$ in the structural identity above yields
\begin{align*}
0&=[(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(x),x]_{R,-1}
-[x,\kappa R(x)]_{R,-1}
+[x,(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(x)]_{R,-1}\\
&=-[x,\kappa R(x)]_{R,-1}
\quad\text{(by skew symmetry)}.
\end{align*}

For the affine Jacobi identity, Eq.~(\ref{e10}) gives
\begin{align*}
\{y,z\}_P-\{x,x\}_P
&=[y,z]_{R,-1}+\kappa R(y-x)
+(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(z-y).
\end{align*}
The constant $s+\kappa(p)$ cancels in this difference. Applying Eq.~(\ref{e10}) again, we have
\begin{align*}
\{x,\{y,z\}_P\}_P-\{x,\{x,x\}_P\}_P
&=[x,\{y,z\}_P-\{x,x\}_P]_{R,-1}
+(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(\{y,z\}_P-\{x,x\}_P)\\
&=[x,[y,z]_{R,-1}]_{R,-1}
+[x,\kappa R(y-x)]_{R,-1}
+[x,(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(z-y)]_{R,-1}\\
&\quad+(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)([y,z]_{R,-1})
+(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)\kappa R(y-x)
+(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)^2(z-y).
\end{align*}
Let $\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ denote summation over $(x,y,z)$, $(y,z,x)$ and $(z,x,y)$. The last two terms cancel in this sum because
$$
(y-x)+(z-y)+(x-z)=0,\quad (z-y)+(x-z)+(y-x)=0.
$$
The nested bracket terms sum to zero by the Jacobi identity for $[-,-]_{R,-1}$. Hence
\begin{align*}
&\quad \sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\bigl(\{x,\{y,z\}_P\}_P-\{x,\{x,x\}_P\}_P\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\Bigl(
[x,\kappa R(y-x)]_{R,-1}
+[x,(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(z-y)]_{R,-1}
+(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)([y,z]_{R,-1})\Bigr)\\
&=\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\Bigl(
(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)([y,z]_{R,-1})
-[(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(y),z]_{R,-1}\\
&\quad+[y,\kappa R(z)]_{R,-1}
-[y,(\lambda R+\operatorname{ad}_p)(z)]_{R,-1}\Bigr)
-\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[x,\kappa R(x)]_{R,-1}\\
&=0\quad\text{(by the structural identity above)}.
\end{align*}
The second equality uses skew symmetry and cyclic reindexing. The last equality uses the structural identity at $y,z$ and $[x,\kappa R(x)]_{R,-1}=0$. This proves the affine Jacobi identity and therefore shows that $\{-,-\}_P$ is a Lie affgebra bracket.

Finally, Eq.~(\ref{e9}) states exactly that $P$ preserves this bracket. The chosen origin was arbitrary, and the first equality in Eq.~(\ref{e11}) proves the assertion about the linear part of $P$ at every origin.

\label{d3}
An affine map $P:\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a$ on a Lie affgebra $(\mathfrak a,\{-,-\})$ is a \textbf{strong Rota-Baxter operator of weight $\theta$} if $\{-,-\}_{P,\theta}$ is a Lie affgebra bracket and, for all $x,y\in\mathfrak a$,
\begin{equation}\label{e25}
P(\{x,y\}_{P,\theta})=\{P(x),P(y)\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end defi-8 -->
<!-- thm-6: body_md -->
\label{t3}
Let $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a(\mathfrak g;\kappa,\lambda,s)$ be a Lie affgebra and let $P:\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a$ be an affine map. Let $R\in\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak g)$ be its linear part and put $p=P(0)\in\mathfrak g$, so that $P(x)=R(x)+p$. Then $P$ is a strong Rota-Baxter operator of weight $\theta$ if and only if
\begin{enumerate}[label={\rm(\roman*)}]
\item $P$ satisfies the affine identity in Eq.~(\ref{e25}).
\item $\mathcal J_P=0$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end thm-6 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Suppose first that $P$ is strong. Eq.~(\ref{e25}) holds by definition, and Eq.~(\ref{e21}) expresses the descendant bracket with coefficients $([-,-]_{R,\theta},\kappa_P,\lambda_P,s_P)$. Proposition~\ref{tstr} therefore gives
$$
\lambda_P([x,y]_{R,\theta})
=[\lambda_P(x),y]_{R,\theta}
-[x,\kappa_P(y)]_{R,\theta}
+[x,\lambda_P(y)]_{R,\theta},
$$
which is $\mathcal J_P(x,y)=0$ by Eq.~(\ref{e26}).

Conversely, assume (i) and (ii). Expanding the two sides of Eq.~(\ref{e25}), we have
\begin{align*}
P(\{x,y\}_{P,\theta})
&=R([x,y]_{R,\theta})+R\kappa_P(x)+R\lambda_P(y-x)+R(s_P)+p
\quad\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e21}))},\\
\{P(x),P(y)\}
&=[R(x)+p,R(y)+p]+\kappa(R(x)+p)+\lambda R(y-x)+s\\
&=[R(x),R(y)]+\kappa R(x)
+(\lambda+\operatorname{ad}_p)R(y-x)+s+\kappa(p).
\end{align*}
The constant terms agree by setting $x=y=0$. Subtracting them and setting $y=x$ identifies the $\kappa$ terms; setting $x=0$ identifies the $\lambda$ terms. The remaining equality is the bilinear identity. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{e27}
R([x,y]_{R,\theta})=[R(x),R(y)], \quad R\kappa_P=\kappa R, \quad R\lambda_P=(\operatorname{ad}_p+\lambda)R, \quad R(s_P)=s-p+\kappa(p).
\end{equation}
The first identity makes $R$ an ordinary Rota-Baxter operator of weight $\theta$, so $[-,-]_{R,\theta}$ is a Lie bracket. By Eq.~(\ref{e26}), condition (ii) is exactly the structural identity displayed above. Proposition~\ref{tstr}, applied to this Lie bracket, $\kappa_P,\lambda_P$ and $s_P$, now proves that Eq.~(\ref{e21}) is a Lie affgebra bracket. Condition (i) makes $P$ a homomorphism from this descendant to the original Lie affgebra, so $P$ is strong.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- prop-9: body_md -->
\label{p5}
With the notation of Theorem~\ref{t3}, assume that $P$ satisfies Eq.~(\ref{e25}). Then
\begin{equation}\label{e28}
R\mathcal J_P(x,y)=0
\end{equation}
for all $x,y\in\mathfrak g$. Equivalently, $\operatorname{Im}\mathcal J_P\subseteq\ker R$.
<!-- end prop-9 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
By Eq.~(\ref{e27}), $R$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism from $(\mathfrak g,[-,-]_{R,\theta})$ to $(\mathfrak g,[-,-])$, with $R\kappa_P=\kappa R$ and $R\lambda_P=(\lambda+\operatorname{ad}_p)R$. Applying $R$ to Eq.~(\ref{e26}), we obtain
\begin{align*}
R\mathcal J_P(x,y)
&=(\lambda+\operatorname{ad}_p)[R(x),R(y)]-[(\lambda+\operatorname{ad}_p)R(x),R(y)]\\
&\quad+[R(x),\kappa R(y)]-[R(x),(\lambda+\operatorname{ad}_p)R(y)]\\
&=[p,[R(x),R(y)]]-[[p,R(x)],R(y)]-[R(x),[p,R(y)]]
\quad\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e1}))}\\
&=0\quad\text{(by the Jacobi identity)}.
\end{align*}
<!-- end proof-11 -->
<!-- cor-3: body_md -->
\label{c3}
With the notation of Theorem~\ref{t3}, suppose that the linear part $R$ of $P$ is injective. Then $P$ is a strong Rota-Baxter operator of weight $\theta$ if and only if it satisfies Eq.~(\ref{e25}).
<!-- end cor-3 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Only sufficiency requires proof. Proposition~\ref{p5} gives $R\mathcal J_P=0$, and injectivity implies $\mathcal J_P=0$. Theorem~\ref{t3} applies.

<!-- cor-4: body_md -->
\label{c4}
Let $\theta\in\mathbf{k}$, let $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a(\mathfrak g;\kappa,\lambda,s)$ be a Lie affgebra, and let $P:\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a$ be an affine map. Let $R\in\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak g)$ be the linear part of $P$, put $p=P(0)$, and let $\kappa_P,\lambda_P$ be given by Eq.~(\ref{e22}). Thus $P(x)=R(x)+p$. Then $P$ is a strong Rota-Baxter operator of weight $\theta$ on $\mathfrak a$ if and only if the following identities hold for all $x,y\in\mathfrak g$:
\begin{equation}\label{e29}
[R(x),R(y)]=R([x,y]_{R,\theta}),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{e30}
\lambda_P([x,y]_{R,\theta})
=[\lambda_P(x),y]_{R,\theta}-[x,\kappa_P(y)]_{R,\theta}+[x,\lambda_P(y)]_{R,\theta},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{e31}
R\kappa R=\kappa R,\quad
R\lambda_P=(\operatorname{ad}_p+\lambda)R,\quad
R(s+\kappa(p))=s-p+\kappa(p).
\end{equation}
<!-- end cor-4 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{t3}, $P$ is a strong Rota-Baxter operator of weight $\theta$ if and only if Eq.~(\ref{e25}) holds for all $x,y\in\mathfrak g$ and $\mathcal J_P=0$. The coefficient comparison in Eq.~(\ref{e27}), together with Eq.~(\ref{e22}), shows that Eq.~(\ref{e25}) is equivalent to Eqs.~(\ref{e29}) and~(\ref{e31}). Eq.~(\ref{e26}) identifies $\mathcal J_P=0$ with Eq.~(\ref{e30}).

<!-- cor-5: body_md -->
\label{c5}
Let $\mathfrak a$ be a Lie affgebra and let $P:\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a$ be an affine map. Then $P$ is an affine Rota-Baxter operator in the sense of Definition~\ref{d1} if and only if it is a strong Rota-Baxter operator of weight $-1$.
<!-- end cor-5 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
By Eq.~(\ref{e20}), the two descendant operations agree at weight $-1$. If $P$ is an affine Rota-Baxter operator, Theorem~\ref{t1} shows that the descendant is a Lie affgebra bracket, so $P$ is strong of weight $-1$. The converse follows directly from the definitions.
<!-- end proof-14 -->
<!-- ex-1: body_md -->
\label{x1}
Let $\mathfrak g$ have basis $e_1,e_2$ with $[e_1,e_2]=e_2$. Take $\kappa=\operatorname{id}$, $s=0$, and define $\lambda(e_1)=e_1$ and $\lambda(e_2)=0$. Then Eq.~(\ref{e1}) holds, so this choice defines a Lie affgebra. Let $\theta=1$ and let $P$ be the constant map with value $0$. Both sides of Eq.~(\ref{e25}) equal zero. However, $\kappa_P=0$ and $\lambda_P=2\lambda-\operatorname{id}$. Hence $\lambda_P(e_1)=e_1$, $\lambda_P(e_2)=-e_2$ and, since $[-,-]_{R,1}=[-,-]$, we obtain $\mathcal J_P(e_1,e_2)=-e_2\neq0$.
Thus the affine identity alone does not imply that the corrected descendant is a Lie affgebra bracket at weight $1$.

<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{l2}
Under the canonical translation identification of the tangent spaces, we have
$$
\kappa_a=\kappa,\quad R_a=R, \quad \lambda_a=\lambda+\operatorname{ad}_a,\quad s_a=s-(\operatorname{id}-\kappa)a,\quad p_a=p-(\operatorname{id}-R)a.
$$
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
In the coordinates with new origin $a$, a point represented by $x\in\mathfrak g$ is the old point $a+x$. The new coordinate of the bracket value is therefore $\{a+x,a+y\}-a$. Expanding the standard form gives
\begin{align*}
\{a+x,a+y\}-a
&=[a+x,a+y]+\kappa(a+x)+\lambda(y-x)+s-a\\
&=[x,y]+[a,y-x]+\kappa(x)+\lambda(y-x)+\kappa(a)+s-a\\
&=[x,y]+\kappa(x)+(\lambda+\operatorname{ad}_a)(y-x)
+s-(\operatorname{id}-\kappa)a.
\end{align*}
This proves the formulas for $\kappa_a,\lambda_a$ and $s_a$. Likewise, $P(a+x)-a=R(x)+R(a)+p-a=R(x)+p-(\operatorname{id}-R)a$, which proves the formulas for $R_a$ and $p_a$.

<!-- defi-9: body_md -->
\label{d4}
Let $P:\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a$ be an affine map on a Lie affgebra $\mathfrak a$. A point $e\in\mathfrak a$ is an \textbf{idempotent fixed point of $P$} if $\{e,e\}=e$ and $P(e)=e$.
<!-- end defi-9 -->
<!-- thm-7: body_md -->
\label{t4}
The class
\begin{equation}\label{e37}
\Omega(\mathfrak a,P)
=
[(s,p)]
\in
\operatorname{coker}F_{\kappa,R}
=
(\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g)/\operatorname{Im}F_{\kappa,R}
\end{equation}
is independent of the chosen origin, under the canonical translation identifications of the tangent spaces. Moreover, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label={\rm(\roman*)}]
\item $\Omega(\mathfrak a,P)=0$.
\item $P$ has an idempotent fixed point.
\item there exists $e\in\mathfrak g$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{e38}
(\operatorname{id}-\kappa)e=s,
\quad
(\operatorname{id}-R)e=p.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
If these conditions hold and $e_0$ is one idempotent fixed point, then the set of all idempotent fixed points is
\begin{equation}\label{e39}
e_0+
\big(
\ker(\operatorname{id}-\kappa)
\cap
\ker(\operatorname{id}-R)
\big).
\end{equation}
In particular, it is an affine space with translation space $\ker(\operatorname{id}-\kappa)\cap\ker(\operatorname{id}-R)$.
<!-- end thm-7 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Under a change of origin by $a$, Lemma~\ref{l2} identifies $\kappa_a$ with $\kappa$ and $R_a$ with $R$, so the linear map $F_{\kappa,R}$ is unchanged under the stated identifications. The constant terms satisfy
\begin{align*}
(s_a,p_a)
&=\bigl(s-(\operatorname{id}-\kappa)a,\ p-(\operatorname{id}-R)a\bigr)
\quad\text{(by Lemma~\ref{l2})}\\
&=(s,p)-F_{\kappa,R}(a)
\quad\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e36}))}.
\end{align*}
Consequently, $[(s_a,p_a)]=[(s,p)]$ in $\operatorname{coker}F_{\kappa,R}$. This proves independence of the origin.

Suppose that (i) holds. By the definition of the quotient in Eq.~(\ref{e37}), the equality $[(s,p)]=0$ means that $(s,p)$ belongs to $\operatorname{Im}F_{\kappa,R}$. Thus there is $e\in\mathfrak g$ with $F_{\kappa,R}(e)=(s,p)$, which is exactly (iii). These equations give
$$
\{e,e\}=[e,e]+\kappa(e)+\lambda(e-e)+s =\kappa(e)+s=e, \quad P(e)=R(e)+p=e.
$$
Hence $e$ is an idempotent fixed point of $P$ and (ii) holds.

Conversely, if (ii) holds, let $e$ be an idempotent fixed point. The equations $\{e,e\}=e$ and $P(e)=e$ give $(\operatorname{id}-\kappa)e=s$ and $(\operatorname{id}-R)e=p$, respectively. Thus $F_{\kappa,R}(e)=(s,p)$, proving (iii), and $(s,p)\in\operatorname{Im}F_{\kappa,R}$, proving (i).

Finally, if $e_0$ is one solution, linearity gives
$$
F_{\kappa,R}(e)=(s,p) \Longleftrightarrow F_{\kappa,R}(e-e_0)=0 \Longleftrightarrow e-e_0\in \ker(\operatorname{id}-\kappa)\cap\ker(\operatorname{id}-R).
$$
This proves Eq.~(\ref{e39}) and identifies the translation space of the set of idempotent fixed points.

<!-- prop-10: body_md -->
\label{p6}
Let $\mathfrak a$ and $\mathfrak a'$ be Lie affgebras with affine maps $P:\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a$ and $P':\mathfrak a'\to\mathfrak a'$, respectively, and let $\varphi:\mathfrak a\to\mathfrak a'$ be a Lie affgebra isomorphism satisfying $\varphi P=P'\varphi$. Choose origins and use the notation of Proposition~\ref{thom}. Write $P(x)=R(x)+p$, $P'(x)=R'(x)+p'$ and $\varphi(x)=\psi(x)+q$. Then $\psi$ induces an isomorphism
$$
\overline{\psi\oplus\psi}:
\operatorname{coker}F_{\kappa,R}
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{coker}F_{\kappa',R'}
$$
which sends $\Omega(\mathfrak a,P)$ to $\Omega(\mathfrak a',P')$.
<!-- end prop-10 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
The homomorphism criterion in Eq.~(\ref{e3}) and the relation $\varphi P=P'\varphi$ give
$$
\psi\kappa=\kappa'\psi,\quad \psi R=R'\psi, \quad \psi s=s'-(\operatorname{id}-\kappa')q,\quad \psi p=p'-(\operatorname{id}-R')q.
$$
It follows that $(\psi\oplus\psi)F_{\kappa,R}(x)=F_{\kappa',R'}(\psi x)$, so $\psi\oplus\psi$ descends to the stated map on cokernels. Moreover, $(\psi\oplus\psi)(s,p)=(s',p')-F_{\kappa',R'}(q)$, which proves that the obstruction classes correspond.
<!-- end proof-17 -->

If nonempty, the set of idempotent points is an affine space with translation space $\ker(\operatorname{id}-\kappa)$.

<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{l2}, changing the origin replaces $s$ by $s-(\operatorname{id}-\kappa)a$, which leaves its cokernel class unchanged. The remaining assertions follow by solving $(\operatorname{id}-\kappa)e=s$.

<!-- prop-12: body_md -->
\label{p8}
Let $P$ be a strong Rota-Baxter operator of arbitrary weight $\theta$ on a Lie affgebra $\mathfrak a$. Then $\operatorname{Im}P$ is a Lie subaffgebra of the original Lie affgebra $(\mathfrak a,\{-,-\})$. In particular, this holds for every affine Rota-Baxter operator by Corollary~\ref{c5}.
<!-- end prop-12 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
The image of an affine map is an affine subspace. Let $a=P(x)$ and $b=P(y)$ be two points of $\operatorname{Im}P$. By the defining homomorphism identity, $\{a,b\}=\{P(x),P(y)\}=P(\{x,y\}_{P,\theta})$. Hence $\{a,b\}\in\operatorname{Im}P$.
<!-- end proof-21 -->
<!-- defi-10: body_md -->
The \textbf{affine kernel of $P$ at $e$} is $\operatorname{Ker}_eP=P^{-1}(e)$.
The \textbf{complementary affine map at $e$} is $Q_e(x)=\langle x,P(x),e\rangle$.
<!-- end defi-10 -->
<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
\label{l3}
Let $P$ be affine and idempotent, and let $e\in\operatorname{Im}P$. Then
\begin{enumerate}[label={\rm(\roman*)}]
\item $Q_e$ is affine and idempotent.
\item $\operatorname{Im}Q_e=\operatorname{Ker}_eP$.
\item $P Q_e=Q_eP=e$, where $e$ on the right denotes the constant map with value $e$.
\item for every $x\in\mathfrak a$,
\begin{equation}\label{e42}
x=\langle P(x),e,Q_e(x)\rangle.
\end{equation}
\item the representation in Eq.~(\ref{e42}) is unique with $P(x)\in\operatorname{Im}P$ and $Q_e(x)\in\operatorname{Ker}_eP$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
The map $Q_e$ is affine because it is the affine combination $x-P(x)+e$. Since $P$ preserves heap operations,
$$
P(Q_e(x)) =P(\langle x,P(x),e\rangle) =\langle P(x),P^2(x),P(e)\rangle =\langle P(x),P(x),e\rangle=e.
$$
Hence $\operatorname{Im}Q_e\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}_eP$. If $y\in\operatorname{Ker}_eP$, then $Q_e(y)=\langle y,e,e\rangle=y$, so equality holds and $Q_e^2=Q_e$. Moreover, $Q_e(P(x))=\langle P(x),P^2(x),e\rangle=e$.
This proves (i)-(iii).

The heap identities give $\langle P(x),e,Q_e(x)\rangle=\langle P(x),e,\langle x,P(x),e\rangle\rangle=x$, which proves (iv).

For uniqueness, suppose $x=\langle a,e,b\rangle$, where $a\in\operatorname{Im}P$ and $b\in\operatorname{Ker}_eP$. Then $P(x)=\langle P(a),P(e),P(b)\rangle=\langle a,e,e\rangle=a$, and hence $Q_e(x)=\langle x,P(x),e\rangle=b$.

<!-- prop-13: body_md -->
\label{p9}
Let $P$ be an idempotent strong Rota-Baxter operator of weight $\theta$ on a Lie affgebra $\mathfrak a$, and let $e\in\operatorname{Im}P$ be an idempotent point. Then $\operatorname{Ker}_eP$ is a Lie subaffgebra of the descendant Lie affgebra $(\mathfrak a,\{-,-\}_{P,\theta})$. In particular, for an idempotent affine Rota-Baxter operator the affine kernel is a Lie subaffgebra of $(\mathfrak a,\{-,-\}_P)$.
<!-- end prop-13 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
The singleton $\{e\}$ is a Lie subaffgebra of the original Lie affgebra because $\{e,e\}=e$. Since $P:(\mathfrak a,\{-,-\}_{P,\theta})\to(\mathfrak a,\{-,-\})$ is a Lie affgebra homomorphism, $\operatorname{Ker}_eP=P^{-1}(\{e\})$ is an affine subspace closed under the descendant bracket.

<!-- ex-3: body_md -->
\label{x3}
Let $\mathfrak g$ be the abelian Lie algebra of dimension two with basis $u,v$. Define $\kappa(u)=0$, $\kappa(v)=u$, $\lambda=0$ and $s=0$.
Since $\mathfrak g$ is abelian, Eq.~(\ref{e1}) holds automatically, and $\{x,y\}=\kappa(x)$ defines a Lie affgebra.

Let $R(u)=u$ and $R(v)=0$.
Then $R^2=R$. Since the tangent Lie algebra is abelian, $R$ is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight $-1$. Moreover, $\kappa R=0$ gives Eq.~(\ref{e16}), while $\lambda=0$ and $s=p=0$ give Eqs.~(\ref{e17}) and~(\ref{e18}). Hence $R$, regarded as an affine map, is an idempotent affine Rota-Baxter operator.

Its image is $\mathbf{k}u$, which is a Lie subaffgebra. However, $\operatorname{Ker}_0R=\mathbf{k}v$ is not a Lie subaffgebra of the original Lie affgebra, because $\{v,v\}=\kappa(v)=u\notin\mathbf{k}v$.
Thus the affine Rota-Baxter identity and idempotency alone do not imply affine factorization into two Lie subaffgebras.

<!-- thm-8: body_md -->
\label{t5}
With the notation above, the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label={\rm(\roman*)}]
\item $\operatorname{Ker}_eP$ is a Lie subaffgebra of the original Lie affgebra $\mathfrak a$.
\item
$$
\kappa_e(\mathfrak g_-)
\subseteq
\mathfrak g_-,
\quad
\lambda_e(\mathfrak g_-)
\subseteq
\mathfrak g_-.
$$
\item
$$
R\kappa_e=\kappa_eR,
\quad
R\lambda_e=\lambda_eR.
$$
\end{enumerate}
Whenever these conditions hold, $\operatorname{Im}P$ and $\operatorname{Ker}_eP$ are both Lie subaffgebras of the original Lie affgebra.
<!-- end thm-8 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
With $e$ as origin, we have $\operatorname{Im}P=\mathfrak g_+$ and $\operatorname{Ker}_eP=\mathfrak g_-$. Both are Lie subalgebras of $(\mathfrak g,[-,-])$ by Proposition~\ref{p2}.

Suppose (i) holds. For $x\in\mathfrak g_-$, closure under the affine bracket gives
$$
\kappa_e(x)=\{x,x\}\in\mathfrak g_-,
\quad \lambda_e(x)=\{0,x\}\in\mathfrak g_-
\quad\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e2}) with $s=0$)}.
$$
This proves (ii). Conversely, if (ii) holds and $x,y\in\mathfrak g_-$, then
$$
\{x,y\}=[x,y]+\kappa_e(x)+\lambda_e(y-x)\in\mathfrak g_-,
$$
because all three terms lie in $\mathfrak g_-$. Thus (i) and (ii) are equivalent.

By Proposition~\ref{p8}, $\mathfrak g_+$ is a Lie subaffgebra. Applying the same diagonal and zero-argument evaluations to $\mathfrak g_+$ shows that $\kappa_e$ and $\lambda_e$ preserve it. Therefore $R\kappa_eR=\kappa_eR$ and $R\lambda_eR=\lambda_eR$. Under (ii), the maps also preserve $\ker R$, so
$$
R\kappa_e(\operatorname{id}-R)=0,\quad R\lambda_e(\operatorname{id}-R)=0.
$$
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
R\kappa_e
&=R\kappa_eR+R\kappa_e(\operatorname{id}-R)=\kappa_eR,\\
R\lambda_e
&=R\lambda_eR+R\lambda_e(\operatorname{id}-R)=\lambda_eR.
\end{align*}
This proves (iii). If (iii) holds and $x\in\ker R$, then
$$
R\kappa_e(x)=\kappa_eR(x)=0,\quad
R\lambda_e(x)=\lambda_eR(x)=0,
$$
so both endomorphisms preserve $\mathfrak g_-$, proving (ii). The final assertion follows from Proposition~\ref{p8} and (i).

<!-- thm-9: body_md -->
\label{t6}
Let $(\mathfrak a,\{-,-\})$ be a Lie affgebra. There is a bijection between the following sets:
\begin{enumerate}[label={\rm(\alph*)}]
\item ordered affine Lie factorizations $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a_+\oplus_e\mathfrak a_-$.
\item pairs $(P,e)$ in which $P$ is an idempotent affine Rota-Baxter operator, $e\in\operatorname{Im}P$ is idempotent, and $P$ is split at $e$.
\end{enumerate}
Under this correspondence, $\mathfrak a_+=\operatorname{Im}P$ and $\mathfrak a_-=\operatorname{Ker}_eP$, while $P$ is the affine projection onto $\mathfrak a_+$ along $\mathfrak a_-$. Moreover, $Q_e(x)=\langle x,P(x),e\rangle$ is also an idempotent affine Rota-Baxter operator, split at $e$, and corresponds to the reversed factorization $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a_-\oplus_e\mathfrak a_+$.
<!-- end thm-9 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
Start with a pair $(P,e)$ in (b). Its image is a Lie subaffgebra by Proposition~\ref{p8}, and its fibre over $e$ is a Lie subaffgebra because $P$ is split at $e$. If $a$ belongs to both factors, then $P(a)=a$ and $P(a)=e$, so $a=e$. Lemma~\ref{l3} gives the unique decomposition
$$
x=\langle P(x),e,Q_e(x)\rangle,
\quad P(x)\in\operatorname{Im}P,\quad Q_e(x)\in\operatorname{Ker}_eP.
$$
Hence these factors give an ordered affine Lie factorization.

Conversely, let $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a_+\oplus_e\mathfrak a_-$ be an ordered affine Lie factorization. Since both factors contain $e$ and are closed under the bracket,
$$
\{e,e\}\in\mathfrak a_+\cap\mathfrak a_-=\{e\}.
$$
Choose $e$ as origin and write $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a(\mathfrak g;\kappa,\lambda,0)$, where $\mathfrak g=T_e\mathfrak a$. The two factors become vector subspaces $\mathfrak g_+,\mathfrak g_-$ with $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_+\oplus\mathfrak g_-$. By Corollary~\ref{c1}, both are Lie subalgebras and are preserved by $\kappa$ and $\lambda$.

Let $R$ be the projection onto $\mathfrak g_+$ along $\mathfrak g_-$. For $x=x_++x_-$ and $y=y_++y_-$, with $x_\pm,y_\pm\in\mathfrak g_\pm$, we have
\begin{align*}
R([R(x),y]+[x,R(y)]-[x,y])
=R([x_+,y_+]-[x_-,y_-])
=[x_+,y_+]
=[R(x),R(y)].
\end{align*}
Here $R$ fixes $\mathfrak g_+$ and vanishes on $\mathfrak g_-$, and both summands are Lie subalgebras. Preservation of the summands also gives
$$
R\kappa(x_++x_-)=\kappa(x_+)=\kappa R(x_++x_-),
\quad R\lambda(x_++x_-)=\lambda(x_+)=\lambda R(x_++x_-).
$$
Thus $R^2=R$, $R\kappa R=\kappa R$ and $R\lambda R=\lambda R$.

Regard $R$ as the affine projection $P$ fixing $e$. Its affine Rota-Baxter identity can now be checked directly:
\begin{align*}
P(\{x,y\}_P)
&=R([x,y]_{R,-1})+R\kappa R(x)+R\lambda R(y-x)
\quad\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e10}))}\\
&=[R(x),R(y)]+\kappa R(x)+\lambda(R(y)-R(x))\\
&=\{P(x),P(y)\}
\quad\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e2}) with $s=p=0$)}.
\end{align*}
Moreover, $P^2=P$, $P(e)=e$, $\operatorname{Im}P=\mathfrak a_+$ and $\operatorname{Ker}_eP=\mathfrak a_-$. In particular, $P$ is split at $e$, so $(P,e)$ belongs to (b).

The projection associated with the factorization sends the unique expression $\langle a,e,b\rangle$ to $a$. Starting with $(P,e)$, the decomposition from Lemma~\ref{l3} therefore recovers $P$ itself. Starting with a factorization, the image and fibre of its projection recover its two factors. This proves that the constructions are inverse.

Finally, in the coordinates above,
$$
Q_e(x_++x_-)=x_++x_--R(x_++x_-)=x_-.
$$
It is the projection for the reversed factorization. Applying the construction just proved to that ordered factorization shows that $Q_e$ is an idempotent affine Rota-Baxter operator split at $e$.

\end{equation}

With $e$ as origin, put $p_e=P(e)$ and let $R$ be the linear part of $P$, so that $P(x)=R(x)+p_e$. The tangent Rota-Baxter assumption means that $R$ satisfies Eq.~(\ref{e6}) at weight $\theta$. Since $P^+_{\theta,e}(x)=P(x)+\theta x$ in these coordinates, Eq.~(\ref{e2}) gives
\begin{align*}
&\{P^+_{\theta,e}(x),P^+_{\theta,e}(y)\}-\{P(x),P(y)\}\\
=&\ \theta[P(x),y]+\theta[x,P(y)]+\theta^2[x,y]
+\theta\kappa_e(x)+\theta\lambda_e(y-x)\\
=&\ \theta\bigl([R(x),y]+[x,R(y)]+\theta[x,y]
+[p_e,y]+[x,p_e]+\kappa_e(x)+\lambda_e(y-x)\bigr).
\end{align*}
Using Eq.~(\ref{e7}) and $[p_e,y]+[x,p_e]=\operatorname{ad}_{p_e}(y-x)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{e53}
\{P^+_{\theta,e}(x),P^+_{\theta,e}(y)\}-\{P(x),P(y)\}
=\theta\bigl([x,y]_{R,\theta}+\kappa_e(x)
+(\lambda_e+\operatorname{ad}_{p_e})(y-x)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For every $z$, the difference between the two components of $H_e(z)$ is $\theta z$. Thus $H_e$ is injective, and Eq.~(\ref{e53}) forces the preimage of the product bracket, if it exists, to be
\begin{equation}\label{e54}
\{x,y\}^{\diamond}_{P,e}
=[x,y]_{R,\theta}+\kappa_e(x)+(\lambda_e+\operatorname{ad}_{p_e})(y-x).
\end{equation}
Because Eq.~(\ref{e53}) already gives the required difference of components, both components agree with those of $H_e(\{x,y\}^{\diamond}_{P,e})$ precisely when the second components agree. Hence graph closure is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{e55}
P\big(\{x,y\}^{\diamond}_{P,e}\big)=\{P(x),P(y)\}.
\end{equation}

We now compute the difference in Eq.~(\ref{e55}):
\begin{align*}
P\big(\{x,y\}^{\diamond}_{P,e}\big)-\{P(x),P(y)\}
&=R([x,y]_{R,\theta})-[R(x),R(y)]
+(R\kappa_e-\kappa_eR)(x)\\
&\quad+\bigl(R(\lambda_e+\operatorname{ad}_{p_e})
-(\lambda_e+\operatorname{ad}_{p_e})R\bigr)(y-x)
+p_e-s_e-\kappa_e(p_e)\\
&=(R\kappa_e-\kappa_eR)(x)
+\bigl(R(\lambda_e+\operatorname{ad}_{p_e})
-(\lambda_e+\operatorname{ad}_{p_e})R\bigr)(y-x)\\
&\quad+p_e-s_e-\kappa_e(p_e)
\quad\text{(by Eq.~(\ref{e6}))}.
\end{align*}
If this is zero for all $x,y$, setting $x=y=0$ gives $s_e+\kappa_e(p_e)=p_e$. After removing the constant term, setting $y=x$ gives $R\kappa_e=\kappa_eR$, and setting $x=0$ gives the remaining commutation relation. These are exactly Eq.~(\ref{e52}). Conversely, those three identities make the displayed difference zero, proving graph closure. Finally,
$$
\{P(e),P(e)\}=\{p_e,p_e\}=\kappa_e(p_e)+s_e,
$$
so the last condition is equivalent to idempotency of $P(e)$.

Since $\psi$ is surjective, this is equivalent to $\operatorname{ad}_q=0$, or $q\in Z(\mathfrak g)$. The first and third equations in Eq.~(\ref{e3}) hold automatically. Conversely, every Lie algebra automorphism $\psi$ and central vector $q$ satisfy the homomorphism criterion and define a bijective affine map. Comparing the linear and constant parts of $\varphi P=P'\varphi$ gives $\psi R=R'\psi$ and $\psi(p)+q=R'(q)+p'$, which is equivalent to Eq.~(\ref{e68}).

\label{t10}
Let $\mathfrak g$ be abelian of finite dimension $n\geq0$, with basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$. The equivalence classes of affine Rota-Baxter operators on $\mathfrak a_{\mathrm{can}}(\mathfrak g)$ are indexed by $r=0,\ldots,n$. A representative of the class indexed by $r$ is
\begin{equation}\label{e69}
P_r\left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i e_i\right)
=
\sum_{i=1}^r x_i e_i.
\end{equation}
Here $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in\mathbf{k}$.
For $n=0$, the empty sum denotes the unique point and there is one class. There are two classes in dimension one and three classes in the abelian case of dimension two.

Theorem~\ref{t9} gives $P=R+p$, where $R^2=R$ and $p\in\ker R$. Conversely, these conditions suffice because every subspace of an abelian Lie algebra is a Lie subalgebra and Eq.~(\ref{e65}) holds. In Proposition~\ref{p14}, take $\psi=\operatorname{id}$ and $q=-p$. Since $Z(\mathfrak g)=\mathfrak g$, this is an allowed automorphism. By Eq.~(\ref{e68}), the conjugate operator has the same linear part $R$ and constant term
$$
p+(\operatorname{id}-R)(-p)=R(p)=0.
$$

Choose a basis of $\operatorname{Im}R$ followed by a basis of $\ker R$. The map $R$ fixes the basis vectors in its image and sends those in its kernel to zero. Thus, with $r=\operatorname{rank}R$, it is conjugate to the projection in Eq.~(\ref{e69}). Every displayed representative is a projection and therefore an affine Rota-Baxter operator. Finally, Eq.~(\ref{e68}) shows that any equivalence conjugates the linear parts, so it preserves their ranks. Thus the representatives exist, exhaust all operators and are pairwise inequivalent.

<!-- thm-14: body_md -->
\label{t11}
Let $\mathfrak r_2$ have basis $h,e$ and bracket $[h,e]=e$. Every affine Rota-Baxter operator on $\mathfrak a_{\mathrm{can}}(\mathfrak r_2)$ is equivalent to exactly one normal form in the following table:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{normal form} & P(uh+ve) & \text{parameter} \\ \hline
C_0 & 0 & {} \\
C_e & e & {} \\
C_\alpha & \alpha h & \alpha\in\mathbf{k}^\times \\
I & uh+ve & {} \\
A_t & ve+th & t\in\mathbf{k} \\
B & uh & {} \\
D & (u-v)h & {}
\end{array}
$$
Here $u,v\in\mathbf{k}$, and the first column names the affine maps specified by the second column. Different rows are inequivalent. For $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{k}^\times$, we have $C_\alpha\sim C_\beta$ if and only if $\alpha=\beta$. For $t,t'\in\mathbf{k}$, we have $A_t\sim A_{t'}$ if and only if $t=t'$.
<!-- end thm-14 -->
<!-- proof-39: body_md -->
The derived ideal is $[\mathfrak r_2,\mathfrak r_2]=\mathbf{k}e$. An automorphism must therefore send $e$ to $de$ with $d\neq0$. Writing its value at $h$ as $ah+ce$ and preserving $[h,e]=e$ gives $ad=d$, hence $a=1$. Conversely, all these maps preserve the bracket. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{e70}
\operatorname{Aut}_{\rm Lie}(\mathfrak r_2)
=\{\psi_{c,d}:\psi_{c,d}(h)=h+ce,\
\psi_{c,d}(e)=de\mid c\in\mathbf{k},\ d\in\mathbf{k}^{\times}\}.
\end{equation}
If $z=ah+be$ is central, then $[z,e]=ae=0$ and $[z,h]=-be=0$, so $z=0$. By Proposition~\ref{p14}, equivalence is therefore given by
$$
R'=\psi_{c,d}R\psi_{c,d}^{-1},\quad p'=\psi_{c,d}(p).
$$

Write $P=R+p$ as in Theorem~\ref{t9}. Since $R$ is a projection, its rank is $0$, $1$ or $2$. At rank zero, $P$ is the constant map with value $p=\alpha h+\beta e$. Using Eq.~(\ref{e70}), we have
$$
\psi_{c,d}(\alpha h+\beta e)
=\alpha(h+ce)+\beta de
=\alpha h+(\alpha c+\beta d)e.
$$ If $p=0$, the result is $C_0$. If $\alpha=0$ and $\beta\neq0$, the choice $c=0$, $d=\beta^{-1}$ gives $C_e$. If $\alpha\neq0$, the choice $d=1$, $c=-\beta/\alpha$ gives $C_\alpha$. The coefficient $\alpha$ is invariant under every automorphism. These classes are therefore distinct, including distinct nonzero values of $\alpha$. At rank two, $R=\operatorname{id}$ and $p\in\ker R=0$, giving $I$.

At rank one, $(\mathfrak g_+,\mathfrak g_-)=(\operatorname{Im}R,\ker R)$ is an ordered pair of distinct lines. Every line is a Lie subalgebra. Besides $\mathbf{k}e$, every line is $\mathbf{k}(h+ae)$ for some $a\in\mathbf{k}$, and $\psi_{c,d}\bigl(\mathbf{k}(h+ae)\bigr)=\mathbf{k}(h+(c+da)e)$. If one line is $\mathbf{k}e$, the other is sent to $\mathbf{k}h$ by choosing $d=1$ and $c=-a$. If both lines are $\mathbf{k}(h+ae),\mathbf{k}(h+be)$ with $a\neq b$, take $d=(b-a)^{-1}$ and $c=-a(b-a)^{-1}$. The ordered pair is then sent to $(\mathbf{k}h,\mathbf{k}(h+e))$. Consequently there are precisely three orbits of ordered pairs:
$$
(\mathbf{k}e,\mathbf{k}h),\quad(\mathbf{k}h,\mathbf{k}e),\quad(\mathbf{k}h,\mathbf{k}(h+e)).
$$
They are distinct because every automorphism preserves $\mathbf{k}e$ and the order of the image and kernel.

For $(\mathbf{k}e,\mathbf{k}h)$, the allowable translation vectors are $p=th$ with arbitrary $t\in\mathbf{k}$, since $[th,e]=te$. This gives $A_t$. An automorphism preserving this ordered pair has $c=0$ and arbitrary $d\neq0$, so it fixes $h$ and cannot change $t$.

For $(\mathbf{k}h,\mathbf{k}e)$, write $p=te$. Then $[p,h]=-te$ belongs to $\mathbf{k}h$ only if $t=0$. The projection is $R(uh+ve)=uh$, giving $B$. For $(\mathbf{k}h,\mathbf{k}(h+e))$, write $p=t(h+e)$. Again $[p,h]=-te\in \mathbf{k}h$ forces $t=0$. The decomposition
$$
uh+ve=(u-v)h+v(h+e)
$$
gives the projection $R(uh+ve)=(u-v)h$, hence $D$.

Conversely, the constant maps in the table are affine Rota-Baxter operators by Proposition~\ref{p7}, since every point of the canonical Lie affgebra is idempotent. The identity map also satisfies Eq.~(\ref{e9}). For the three rows of rank one, the displayed complementary lines are Lie subalgebras, $p$ lies in the kernel, and the calculations above verify Eq.~(\ref{e65}). Theorem~\ref{t9} therefore proves existence for every listed parameter. Rank separates the cases of ranks zero, one and two. Within rank one, the three orbits of ordered pairs are distinct and the stabilizer calculation separates the parameters of $A_t$. This proves exhaustiveness and pairwise inequivalence of the whole table.
