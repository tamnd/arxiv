<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For a general threefold $X_+$ in \eqref{eq:Xplusintro} the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item There is a Calabi--Yau threefold $X_-$ obtained from $X_+$ by
flopping 16 disjoint $(-1,-1)$-curves.
The threefold $X_-$ admits a genus-one fibration over $\mathbb P^2$ of index exactly 4.
In natural integral divisor bases $(H_{+},F_{+})$ on $X_+$ and $(H_{-},F_{-})$ on $X_-$, with $H_{-}=H_{+}$ and $F_{-}=2H_{+}-F_{+}$, the cubic data are
\[
H_{+}^3=7,\quad H_{+}^2F_{+}=6,\quad F_{+}^2=0,
\]
\[
H_{-}^3=7,\quad H_{-}^2F_{-}=8,\quad H_{-}F_{-}^2=4,\quad F_{-}^3=0.
\]
(Propositions~\ref{prop:flop}, \ref{prop:topdata} and~\ref{prop:index4}.)
\item For $(z_1,z_2)$ in a dense Zariski-open subset of
$(\mathbb C^*)^2$, the Laurent model \eqref{eq:laurent} admits a smooth projective crepant model $Y=Y_{z_1,z_2}$ with
\[
e(Y)=88,\qquad (h^{1,1}(Y),h^{2,1}(Y))=(46,2).
\]
For very general parameters the Mordell--Weil group of its elliptic presentation is $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$.
(Propositions~\ref{prop:MW}, \ref{prop:rho46} and~\ref{prop:euler88}.)
\item The fundamental period is annihilated by the Picard--Fuchs system
\eqref{eq:L2}--\eqref{eq:L3}, which has generic rank 6 and two maximally
unipotent boundary points.
Their indicial algebras are isomorphic to $\mathcal R_{-}\otimes\mathbb C$ and $\mathcal R_{+}\otimes\mathbb C$, respectively, where
\[
\mathcal R_{-}=\mathbb Q[H_{-},F_{-}]/(4H_{-}^2-8H_{-}F_{-}+9F_{-}^2,F_{-}^3)
\]
and
\[
\mathcal R_{+}=\mathbb Q[H_{+},F_{+}]/(F_{+}^2,H_{+}^2(6H_{+}-7F_{+}))
\]
are presentations of the rational even cohomology rings of $X_-$ and $X_+$, with divisor classes placed in degree 1.
(Propositions~\ref{prop:PF}, \ref{prop:minuschart}, \ref{prop:pluschart}, Corollary~\ref{cor:Aindminus}.)
\item The system admits a nondegenerate rational horizontal alternating
form.
Its 4 Yukawa functions are the rational functions in
\eqref{eq:Yukawas}.  The corresponding mirror maps and formal prepotential give genus-zero
predictions.
Their first vertical genus-zero predictions agree with independent intersection-theoretic calculations of $176$ vertical lines and $100$ vertical conics on $X_-$; here ``vertical'' means contained in a fiber of the genus-one fibration $X_-\to\mathbb P^2$.
(Propositions~\ref{prop:yukawa}, \ref{prop:firstrow}, \ref{prop:176lines} and~\ref{prop:100conics}.)
\end{enumerate}

<!-- head-1: body_md -->
We work over $\mathbb C$.
A Calabi--Yau threefold $X$ is a smooth projective threefold with $K_X\sim0$ and $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=H^2(X,\mathcal O_X)=0$.
``General'' means on a dense Zariski-open set and ``very general'' means outside a countable union of proper closed subsets.
We use \emph{chamber} both for the two GIT chambers of the auxiliary toric problem and for the corresponding A-model birational models $X_\pm$, and
\emph{chart} for the coordinates near the two B-model boundary points.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:sectionsM}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $h^i(M,\mathcal O_M)=1$ for $i=0$ and $h^i(M,\mathcal O_M)=0$ for $i>0$.
\item Although $H^0(A,\mathcal O_A(-1,3))=0$, the restriction has sections:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gcicycohomology}
H^0(M,\mathcal O_M(-1,3))
 \simeq H^1(A,\mathcal O_A(-4,1))
 \simeq H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-4))\otimes H^0(\mathbb P^4,\mathcal O(1)),
\end{equation}
a space of dimension $3\cdot5=15$.
\item If the zero locus $X_+$ of a section $s\in H^0(M,\omega_M^{-1})$ is
smooth of dimension 3, then $X_+$ is a Calabi--Yau threefold.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
(1) In the sequence $0\to\mathcal O_A(-3,-2)\to\mathcal O_A\to\mathcal O_M\to0$ the first term is acyclic by the K\"unneth formula, since $H^*(\mathbb P^4,\mathcal O(-2))=0$.
(2) Twist the same sequence by $\mathcal O_A(-1,3)$:
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_A(-4,1)
 \xrightarrow{\times P}\mathcal O_A(-1,3)
 \longrightarrow\mathcal O_M(-1,3)\longrightarrow0 .
\]
By the K\"unneth formula, the groups $H^0$ and $H^1$ of $\mathcal O_A(-1,3)$ vanish because $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)$ is acyclic.
The connecting map therefore gives
\eqref{eq:gcicycohomology}.

(3) Adjunction gives $K_{X_+}=(K_M+c_1(\omega_M^{-1}))|_{X_+}=0$.
The sequence $0\to\omega_M\to\mathcal O_M\to\mathcal O_{X_+}\to0$, part (1), and Serre duality $h^i(\omega_M)=h^{4-i}(\mathcal O_M)$ give $h^0(\mathcal O_{X_+})=1$, so $X_+$ is connected, and $h^1(\mathcal O_{X_+})=h^2(\mathcal O_{X_+})=0$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:ybasis}
For general $P$ the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $y_1,y_2,y_3$ form a basis of
$H^0(M,\mathcal O_M(-1,2))\simeq H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-4))$.
\item Multiplication induces an isomorphism
\[
H^0(M,\mathcal O_M(-1,2))\otimes H^0(\mathbb P^4,\mathcal O(1))
\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }H^0(M,\mathcal O_M(-1,3)).
\]
Consequently every generalized section has the form
\[
s=\ell_1y_1+\ell_2y_2+\ell_3y_3,\qquad \ell_j\in H^0(\mathbb P^4,\mathcal O(1)).
\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:sectionsM}(2), applied to the sequence
\[
0\to\mathcal O_A(-4,0)\to\mathcal O_A(-1,2)\to\mathcal O_M(-1,2)\to0,
\]
gives an isomorphism
\[
\delta:H^0(M,\mathcal O_M(-1,2))\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }H^1(A,\mathcal O_A(-4,0))
\simeq H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-4)),
\]
onto a space of dimension 3.
If $\sum_{j=1}^3c_jy_j=0$, restriction to $M\cap\{v=0\}=\{f_0=0\}\subset\mathbb P^4$ gives $\sum_{j=1}^3c_jf_j\in\mathbb C f_0$, which for general quadrics forces $c=0$.
This proves (1).
The connecting maps $\delta$ for $\mathcal O_A(-1,2)$ and $\mathcal O_A(-1,3)$ commute with multiplication by $\ell\in H^0(\mathbb P^4,\mathcal O(1))$.
On the targets this multiplication is, by K\"unneth, the isomorphism
\[
\bigl(H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-4))\otimes H^0(\mathbb P^4,\mathcal O)\bigr)\otimes H^0(\mathbb P^4,\mathcal O(1))
\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-4))\otimes H^0(\mathbb P^4,\mathcal O(1)).
\]
This proves (2); the dimension count $3\cdot5=15$ agrees with
\eqref{eq:gcicycohomology}.

\label{prop:minuschamber}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $\vartheta\in\mathfrak C_-$.  Every $\vartheta$-semistable point
of $Z$ has $x\ne0$ and $y\ne0$, $\mathbb G$ acts freely on $Z^{ss}$, and $Z^{ss}/\mathbb G\simeq X_-$.

\item $\mathcal E$ is globally generated of rank 3 and

(1) If $x=0$ on $Z$, then $\mathsf A(y)(u,v)^t=0$ forces $(u,v)=0$, so $(u,v,x)=0$, which is unstable.
On $\{x\ne0,\ y\ne0\}$ an element of $\mathbb G$ fixing a point has $\lambda_2=1$ (from $x$) and $\lambda_1^{-1}\lambda_2^2=1$ (from $y$), so the action is free.
The quotient of this open set is the total space of $(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-1)\boxtimes\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^4}(2))^{\oplus2}$ over $\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^4$, with fiber coordinates $(u,v)$, because $\deg u=\deg v=(1,0)=-q_Y+2q_X$.
In it the 4 equations $\mathsf A(y)(u,v)^t=\mathbf f(x)$ define the graph of the unique solution $(u,v)$ over $Z(\bar{\mathbf f})$, since $\mathsf A$ is an injective bundle map; the last equation gives $Z(\sigma_-)$.

(2) Tensor \eqref{eq:Qbundle} by $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^4}(2)$; since $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-1)$ is acyclic, $H^0(\mathcal Q\boxtimes\mathcal O(2))= H^0(\mathcal O^{\oplus4}\boxtimes\mathcal O(2))$.
The statement for $\mathcal O(1,1)$ is K\"unneth.

(3) $\mathcal Q$ is a quotient of a trivial bundle, hence globally generated, and $\det\mathcal Q=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(2)$ by \eqref{eq:Qbundle}.
By (2) and Bertini's theorem for globally generated bundles, the zero locus of a general section, i.e.\ of general data, is smooth of codimension 3, and adjunction gives $K_{X_-}\sim0$.
For the cohomology, the Koszul complex of $\sigma_-$ has terms
\begin{gather*}
\mathcal E^*=(\mathcal Q^*\boxtimes\mathcal O(-2))\oplus\mathcal O(-1,-1),\qquad
\Lambda^3\mathcal E^*=\mathcal O(-3,-5),\\
\Lambda^2\mathcal E^*=\mathcal O(-2,-4)\oplus
(\mathcal Q^*(-1)\boxtimes\mathcal O(-3)).
\end{gather*}
The terms $\mathcal E^*$ and $\Lambda^2\mathcal E^*$ are acyclic by K\"unneth, since $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^4}(-2)$, $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-1)$, $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^4}(-4)$ and $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^4}(-3)$ are acyclic, and $\Lambda^3\mathcal E^*$ is the canonical bundle.
Hence $h^i(\mathcal O_{X_-})=1,0,0,1$ for $i=0,1,2,3$; in particular $X_-$ is connected.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:flop}
For general data, $f_0,\ldots,f_3$ meet in 16 reduced points $x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(16)}\in\mathbb P^4$ at which $\ell=(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)$ does not vanish.
The base locus of the generalized sections on $M$ is the disjoint union of the curves $C_i=\mathbb P^1\times\{x^{(i)}\}$, and $N_{C_i/X_+}\simeq\mathcal O(-1)^{\oplus2}$.
The birational map $X_+\dashrightarrow X_-$ induced by \eqref{eq:auxsystem} is an isomorphism away from $\bigcup C_i$ and the lines
\[
C_i'=\{\ell_1(x^{(i)})y_1+\ell_2(x^{(i)})y_2+\ell_3(x^{(i)})y_3=0\}\times\{x^{(i)}\}
\subset\mathbb P^2_y\times\mathbb P^4_x,
\]
and it is the simultaneous flop of the 16 disjoint $(-1,-1)$-curves $C_i$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The 4 general quadrics $f_j$ meet in $2^4=16$ reduced points, and general linear forms $\ell_j$ do not vanish simultaneously at them.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:ybasis} the generalized sections are the $\ell\cdot y$, and at a point of $M$ with $y_j\ne0$ one can choose $\ell_j$ nonzero there and the other $\ell_k$ zero; hence the base locus is $\{y=0\}$, which is $\bigcup C_i$ by
\eqref{eq:yzero}.

\emph{Normal bundles.}  We have $N_{C_i/A}=T_{x^{(i)}}\mathbb P^4\otimes\mathcal O_{C_i}
=\mathcal O^{\oplus4}$, and $dP|_{C_i}=\sum_{j=0}^3u^{3-j}v^j\,df_j(x^{(i)})$, where the 4 linear forms $df_j(x^{(i)})$ on $T_{x^{(i)}}\mathbb P^4$ are independent because the 16 points are reduced. Hence $N_{C_i/M}$ is the kernel of $\mathcal O^{\oplus4}\to\mathcal O(3)$, $(\xi_j)\mapsto\sum_{j=0}^3u^{3-j}v^j\xi_j$, which is $\mathcal O(-1)^{\oplus3}$ by \eqref{eq:syzygyP1}. The section $y$ vanishes along $C_i$, and its derivative $dy:N_{C_i/M}\to\mathcal O_M(-1,2)^{\oplus3}|_{C_i}=
\mathcal O(-1)^{\oplus3}$ satisfies $D(u,v)\circ dy=d\mathbf f$, which is the
inclusion of $N_{C_i/M}$ into $\mathcal O^{\oplus4}$; hence $dy$ is injective, and an isomorphism since both bundles have degree $-3$.
The derivative of $s=\ell\cdot y$ along $C_i$ is therefore the constant map $\mathcal O(-1)^{\oplus3}\to\mathcal O(-1)$ given by $\ell(x^{(i)})\ne0$.
It is surjective, so $X_+$ is smooth along $C_i$ and $N_{C_i/X_+}$ is its kernel $\mathcal O(-1)^{\oplus2}$.

\emph{Where the chambers agree.}  By Propositions~\ref{prop:pluschamber}
and~\ref{prop:minuschamber}, $X_\pm$ are the quotients of the semistable parts of $Z$ for the two chambers.
A point of $Z$ with $x\ne0$, $y\ne0$ and $(u,v)\ne0$ is semistable in both.
The points of $X_+$ with $y=0$ form $\bigcup C_i$; the points of $X_-$ with $(u,v)=0$ are those with $\mathbf f(x)=0$, i.e.\ $x=x^{(i)}$, together with $\ell(x^{(i)})\cdot y=0$, which form $\bigcup C_i'$.
Hence $X_+\setminus\bigcup C_i\simeq X_-\setminus\bigcup C_i'$, and both complements have codimension 2.

\emph{The flop.}  The classes $H_{+}$ and $H_{-}$ are base-point free, pulled
back from $\mathbb P^4$, and big because $X_\pm\to\mathbb P^4$ are generically finite, as we now check.
The curves on $X_+$ contracted by $X_+\to\mathbb P^4$ lie in the fibers $\{[u:v]\mid P(u,v,x)=0\}\cap\{s=0\}$, which are finite unless $\mathbf f(x)=0$, in which case the fiber is $C_i$.
Likewise the fiber of $X_-\to\mathbb P^4$ over $x$ is finite unless $\mathbf f(x)=0$, in which case it is $C_i'$.
Let $X_\pm\to\overline X$ be the Stein factorizations; they are the contractions of $\bigcup C_i$ and $\bigcup C_i'$ to the same normal variety $\overline X$, which has 16 ordinary double points because $N_{C_i/X_+}=\mathcal O(-1)^{\oplus2}$.
The class $F_{+}$ satisfies $F_{+}\cdot C_i=1$ and is ample relative to $X_+\to\overline X$, whereas its transform $2H_{-}-F_{-}$ on $X_-$ satisfies $(2H_{-}-F_{-})\cdot C_i'=-1$, because $H_{-}\cdot C_i'=0$ and $F_{-}\cdot C_i'=1$.
By the uniqueness of the flop \cite[\S6.1]{KM}, $X_-\to\overline X$ is the flop of $X_+\to\overline X$, i.e.\ the simultaneous Atiyah flop of the 16 disjoint curves $C_i$.

\label{prop:topdata}
\begin{enumerate}
\item On $X_+$,
\[
H_{+}^3=7,\qquad H_{+}^2F_{+}=6,\qquad F_{+}^2=0,\qquad
c_2(X_+)=4H_{+}^2+3H_{+}F_{+},

c_2(X_+)\cdotH_{+}=46,\qquad c_2(X_+)\cdotF_{+}=24,\qquad e(X_+)=-88 .

H_{-}^3=7,\quad H_{-}^2F_{-}=8,\quad H_{-}F_{-}^2=4,\quad F_{-}^3=0, \qquad c_2(X_-)\cdotH_{-}=46,\quad c_2(X_-)\cdotF_{-}=36,

\item For every $D\in\operatorname{Pic}(X_\pm)$ and any flopping curve
$C_i$,
\[
D^3\big|_{X_-}=D^3\big|_{X_+}-16\,(D\cdot C_i)^3,\qquad c_2(X_-)\cdot D=c_2(X_+)\cdot D+32\,(D\cdot C_i).

(1) Since $X_+$ is the zero locus of a section of $\mathcal O_M(-1,3)$ on the hypersurface $M$, integration over $X_+$ is integration over $A$ against $[X_+]=(3F+2H)(3H-F)$, and
\[
c(T_{X_+})=\frac{(1+F)^2(1+H)^5}{(1+3F+2H)(1+3H-F)}\Big|_{X_+}.
\]
With $F^2=0$ and $\int_AFH^4=1$ one finds $H_{+}^3=9-2=7$, $H_{+}^2F_{+}=6$, $c_2(X_+)=4H_{+}^2+3H_{+}F_{+}$, and hence $c_2\cdotH_{+}=4\cdot7+3\cdot6=46$, $c_2\cdotF_{+}=4\cdot6=24$; the degree-3 part of $c(T_{X_+})$ gives $e(X_+)=-88$.
Since $h^{1,1}(X_+)=2$ \cite{AAGGL,GG}, this gives $h^{2,1}(X_+)=46$.

(2) On $\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^4$ one has $c(\mathcal Q)=(1-F_{-})^{-2} =1+2F_{-}+3F_{-}^2$, so
\[
[X_-]=c_2(\mathcal Q\boxtimes\mathcal O(2))\,(F_{-}+H_{-}) =(3F_{-}^2+4F_{-}H_{-}+4H_{-}^2)(F_{-}+H_{-}) =7F_{-}^2H_{-}+8F_{-}H_{-}^2+4H_{-}^3,
\]
and the normal bundle sequence gives $c_2(X_-)=2H_{-}^2+5H_{-}F_{-}-2F_{-}^2$.
Integration gives the displayed numbers.
The Euler numbers agree because a flop replaces smooth rational curves by smooth rational curves.

(3) Write $D=aH_{-}+bF_{-}=(a+2b)H_{+}-bF_{+}$, so that $D\cdot C_i=-b$.
By (1),
\[
D^3\big|_{X_+}=7(a+2b)^3-18(a+2b)^2b=7a^3+24a^2b+12ab^2-16b^3,

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:index4}
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(X_+)/\operatorname{tors}\simeq\mathbb ZH_{+}\oplus\mathbb ZF_{+}, \qquad
\operatorname{Pic}(X_-)/\operatorname{tors}\simeq\mathbb ZH_{-}\oplus\mathbb ZF_{-}.
\]

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Since $h^{1,1}(X_+)=2$ and $h^{2,0}(X_+)=0$, the Picard rank is 2, so a class in $\operatorname{Pic}(X_+)/\operatorname{tors}$ can be written $D=rH_{+}+sF_{+}$ with $r,s\in\mathbb Q$.

Moreover $DH_{+}^2=7r+6s$ and $DH_{+}F_{+}=6r$ are integers, so $r=(7r+6s)-6r-6s\in\mathbb Z$.

\[
(mH_{-}+nF_{-})\cdot F_{-}^2=4m .
\]
The divisor $H_{-}$ gives a multisection of degree 4.
Conversely, the closure of a closed point of the generic fiber is a prime divisor on the smooth threefold $X_-$, hence Cartier, and its degree over the base is divisible by 4 by the preceding calculation.
Thus the index is exactly $4$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:HVelimination}

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:doublecover}
For $(z_1,z_2)\in U$, the hypersurface \eqref{eq:laurent} is birational to a normal projective double cover $\pi:Y_0\to\mathcal P=(\mathbb P^1)^3_{a,b,d}$, branched along a reduced divisor of degree $(4,4,4)$, with trivial dualizing sheaf and

Put
\[
\mathsf L:=1-z_2ab,\qquad
\kappa:=z_1(1+a)^2(1+b)^2(1+d)^2 .
\]
Multiplying \eqref{eq:laurent} by $a^2b^2cd$ gives $\widetilde G:=a^2b^2d\mathsf L\,c-\kappa(1+c)^2=0$, i.e.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadraticinc}
\kappa c^2+(2\kappa-a^2b^2d\mathsf L)c+\kappa=0 .
\end{equation}
Thus $(a,b,c,d)\mapsto(a,b,d)$ is generically of degree 2.
The discriminant of \eqref{eq:quadraticinc} is
\[
\Delta_c=(2\kappa-a^2b^2d\mathsf L)^2-4\kappa^2
=a^2b^2d\mathsf L\,\Psi,\qquad
\Psi:=a^2b^2d\mathsf L-4\kappa .
\]
Since $a,b$ are units on the torus, $(ab)^2$ is a square, and on the hypersurface the function
\[
\xi:=\frac{2\kappa c+2\kappa-a^2b^2d\mathsf L}{ab}
\]
satisfies, by \eqref{eq:quadraticinc},
\begin{equation}
\xi^2=d\mathsf L\Psi.\label{eq:affinedouble}
\end{equation}
Conversely, where $\kappa\ne0$, $c$ is recovered from $\xi$, so
\eqref{eq:affinedouble} has the same function field.

To compactify, write $a=a_1/a_0$, $b=b_1/b_0$, $d=d_1/d_0$ and put
\begin{align*}
\mathsf L_h&=a_0b_0-z_2a_1b_1,\\
\Psi_h&=a_1^2b_1^2\mathsf L_h\,d_0d_1
 -4z_1a_0b_0(a_0+a_1)^2(b_0+b_1)^2(d_0+d_1)^2 .
\end{align*}
The branch section $\Delta_{\mathrm{br}}=d_0d_1\mathsf L_h\Psi_h$ has degree $(4,4,4)$, and the double cover $Y_0$ is the hypersurface $\{\eta^2=\Delta_{\mathrm{br}}\}$ in the total space of $\mathcal O_{\mathcal P}(2,2,2)$, with $\pi_*\mathcal O_{Y_0}=\mathcal O_{\mathcal P}\oplus
\mathcal O_{\mathcal P}(-2,-2,-2)$.

\emph{Reducedness.}  For $z_2\ne0$ the form $\mathsf L_h$ is irreducible.
As a binary quadratic form in $(d_0,d_1)$, $\Psi_h$ has coefficients $-4T$, $S-8T$, $-4T$ with
\[
S=a_1^2b_1^2\mathsf L_h,\qquad T=z_1a_0b_0(a_0+a_1)^2(b_0+b_1)^2,
\]
which have no common factor, and discriminant $S(S-16T)$, whose square class $\mathsf L_h(S-16T)$ is not a square.
Hence $\Psi_h$ is irreducible, and $\Delta_{\mathrm{br}}$ is a product of 4 distinct irreducible factors, hence reduced.

\emph{Normality and the canonical class.}  A double cover
$\{\eta^2=\Delta_{\mathrm{br}}\}$ with reduced branch divisor is a hypersurface that is regular in codimension 1, hence normal.
The double-cover formula gives $\omega_{Y_0}=\pi^*(\omega_{\mathcal P}\otimes\mathcal O_{\mathcal P}(2,2,2)) =\mathcal O_{Y_0}$, and the cohomology follows from $\pi_*\mathcal O_{Y_0}=\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O(-2,-2,-2)$ and K\"unneth.

\emph{The residue.}  Since $1/(abcd\,G)=ab/\widetilde G$, we have
$\Omega=\operatorname{Res}_c\bigl(ab\,\mathrm{d}a\wedge \mathrm{d}b\wedge \mathrm{d}c\wedge \mathrm{d}d/\widetilde G\bigr) =ab\,\mathrm{d}a\wedge \mathrm{d}b\wedge \mathrm{d}d/(\partial\widetilde G/\partial c)$, and $\partial\widetilde G/\partial c=a^2b^2d\mathsf L-2\kappa(1+c)=-ab\,\xi$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The branch divisor is not an arrangement of smooth transverse components: $\Psi_h$ has special singularities and tangencies, so the existence of a crepant resolution cannot be read off from its degree.

Rather than controlling these centers directly (compare \cite{IL}), we pass to the Weierstrass model, where the resolution can be carried out explicitly.

<!-- proposition-14: body_md -->
\label{prop:fundperiod}
On $\mathcal O_-$,
\begin{align}
\Phi_-(z_1,z_2)
&=\sum_{n\ge0}\sum_{m=0}^{2n}\binom{n+m}{m}\binom{2n}{m}^2

&=\sum_{n\geq0}\sum_{m=0}^{2n}
 \frac{(2n)!^4\,(n+m)!}{(n!)^5\,(m!)^3\,(2n-m)!^2}\,z_1^nz_2^m.\label{eq:fundperiod}
\end{align}
