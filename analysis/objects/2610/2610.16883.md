We use $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{R}_+$, and $\mathbb{R}_{++}$ to denote the sets of real,
nonnegative real, and strictly positive real numbers.
The space of real symmetric $p\times p$ matrices and its cones of
positive semidefinite and positive definite matrices are denoted by
$\mathbb{S}^p$, $\mathbb{S}^p_+$, and $\mathbb{S}^p_{++}$, respectively.
Unless otherwise specified, $\|\cdot\|$ denotes the Euclidean norm,
and $\langle x,y\rangle=x^\top y$ is the inner product of vectors of
the same dimension.
For matrices $A$ and $B$ of the same dimensions, the Frobenius inner
product and norm are defined by $\langle A,B\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(A^\top B)$
and $\|A\|_{\mathrm{F}}=\sqrt{\langle A,A\rangle}$.
We write $I_p$, or simply $I$ when the dimension is clear, for the $p\times p$
identity matrix, and $\mathrm{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_p)$ for the diagonal matrix
with diagonal entries $a_1,\ldots,a_p$.
The smallest and largest eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix $A$ are
written as $\lambda_{\min}(A)$ and $\lambda_{\max}(A)$.
For $A\in\mathbb{S}^p_+$, $A^{1/2}$ denotes its unique symmetric positive
semidefinite square root; when $A\in\mathbb{S}^p_{++}$, we write

The unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^p$ is denoted by
$\mathbb{U}^{p-1}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^p:\|u\|=1\}$, and

A random vector $\xi$ is said to be \textit{elliptically distributed} (or has \textit{elliptical distribution}), denoted by $\xi \sim \operatorname{EC}(\mu_0, \Sigma_0, P_0)$, if it admits the stochastic representation
\begin{align}
\label{eq:EC-stochastic-rep}
    \xi \overset{d}{=} \mu_0 + r\Sigma_0^{1/2} u,
\end{align}
where $\mu_0 \in \mathbb{R}^p$ is the location vector, $\Sigma_0\in {\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}$ is the shape matrix, $r$ is a random variable supported on $\mathbb{R}_+$, $P_0$ is the distribution of $r$, $u\sim \mathrm{Unif}(\mathbb{U}^{p-1})$ is a random vector distributed uniformly on the unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^p$, and $r$ and $u$ are independent. 
A random vector is elliptically distributed with parameters $\mu_0$, $\Sigma_0$ and $P_0$ 
if and only if its characteristic function can be written as
\[
    \varphi(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{it^\top\xi}] = \exp(it^\top \mu_0) \psi(t^\top \Sigma_0 t),
\]
where $\psi:[0,+\infty)\to \mathbb{C}$ is the characteristic generator that is uniquely determined by $P_0$ (up to scale). 
Moreover, the density function of an elliptically distributed random vector $f_{\xi}$, if exists, 
        
has the form
\[
    f_{\xi}(x) = c\cdot g\left((x-\mu_0)^\top \Sigma_0^{-1} (x-\mu_0)\right),
\]
                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                                         
   
where $c$ is a normalization scaler, and $g: \mathbb{R}_+\to \mathbb{R}_+$ is called a density generator, which is also uniquely determined by $P_0$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rmk:ellip}
    The tail behavior and moment properties of an elliptically distributed random vector $\xi$ can be very flexible and are determined by the distribution of $r$. For example, if $\mathbb{E}[r]$ exists, then the mean of $\xi$ is given by $
        \mathbb{E}[\xi] = \mu_0 + \mathbb{E}[r]\Sigma_0^{1/2}\mathbb{E}[u]=\mu_0,$
    since $r$ and $u$ are independent and $\mathbb{E}[u]=0$. More generally, the $k$-th moment of $\xi$ exists if and only if 
    the $k$-th moment of $r$ exists, since 
    \[
        \mathbb{E}[\|\xi-\mu_0\|^k] = \mathbb{E}[r^k\|\Sigma_0^{1/2}u\|^k] = \mathbb{E}[r^k]\cdot\mathbb{E}[\|\Sigma_0^{1/2}u\|^k],
    \]
    and $u$ is a bounded random vector. Nevertheless, even when the first or second moment of $\xi$ does not exist, the location vector $\mu_0$ and the shape matrix $\Sigma_0$ remain well defined through the stochastic representation~\eqref{eq:EC-stochastic-rep}.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rmk:scale}
In the stochastic representation~\eqref{eq:EC-stochastic-rep}, the scale of the shape matrix $\Sigma_0$ is not identifiable without a normalization on the radial distribution $P_0$. Indeed, for any constant $c>0$,
\[
    \xi \overset{d}{=} \mu_0 + r\Sigma_0^{1/2}u
    \;\overset{d}{=}\;
    \mu_0 + (c^{-1/2}r)\,(c\Sigma_0)^{1/2}u,
\]
so that the same elliptical distribution can be generated by simultaneously rescaling $r$ and $\Sigma_0$. Consequently, only the shape of $\Sigma_0$ is identifiable, while its scale depends on the choice of the distribution of $r$. To ensure identifiability, one usually imposes a scale normalization. In this paper, we fix the scale of $\Sigma_0$ by requiring $
    \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_0)=p,$
so that $\Sigma_0$ is uniquely defined.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rmk:tighter-lb}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:cutting-alg} differs from the classical Kelley
cutting-plane method~\citep{ref:kelley1960cutting} in the way the lower bound of the objective function is successively constructed. In the classical cutting-plane method, the lower bound of the objective function $\hat{{v}}_N(x)$ is given by a piecewise-linear function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kelley-lb}
    \hat v_N(x) \ge \max_{j=0,1,\ldots,k}
    \big\{\hat v_N(x_j) + \langle g_j^N, x - x_j\rangle\big\},
    \qquad g_j^N \in \partial \hat v_N(x_j).
\end{equation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:cutting-alg} instead builds a scenario-wise piecewise-linear
lower bound of $F(\cdot,\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scenario-lb}
    F(x,\mu_i,\Sigma_i) \ge \hat F_i(x) \triangleq \max_{j=0,1,\ldots,k}
    \big\{F_{i,j} + \langle g_{i,j}, x - x_j\rangle\big\},
    \qquad i = 1,\ldots,N.
\end{equation}
By the monotonicity of $\rho$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:convex}), a global lower bound on $\hat{{v}}_N(x)$ is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:our-lb}
    \hat v_N(x) \ge \rho\big(\{\hat F_i(x)\}_{i=1}^N\big).
\end{equation}
We claim that in practice, \eqref{eq:our-lb} is tighter than \eqref{eq:kelley-lb}. To see this, define $
    h_j(x) \triangleq \rho\big(\{F_{i,j} + \langle g_{i,j}, x - x_j\rangle\}_{i=1}^N\big)$,
which is convex as the composition of 
the monotone convex functional 
$\rho$ with affine mappings.
By the monotonicity of $\rho$, 
$
    h_j(x) \leq \hat{v}_N(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}
$
and $h_j(x_j) = \hat{v}_N(x_j)$,
which implies that $\partial h_j(x_j) \subseteq \partial \hat v_N(x_j)$. We assume that the Kelley subgradient in~\eqref{eq:kelley-lb} is taken to be $g_j^N \in \partial h_j(x_j)$, which is the natural choice in practice, since it is precisely the subgradient produced by the chain rule from the scenario data $(F_{i,j}, g_{i,j})$ used in Algorithm \ref{alg:cutting-alg}. Then, for every $x \in \mathcal{X}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lb-comparison}
    \rho\big(\{\hat F_i(x)\}_{i=1}^N\big)
    \;\ge\; \max_{j=0,1,\ldots,k} h_j(x)
    \;\ge\; \max_{j=0,1,\ldots,k}
    \big\{\hat v_N(x_j) + \langle g_j^N, x - x_j\rangle\big\},
\end{equation}
where the first inequality is by the monotonicity of $\rho$
and the fact that $\max_{j=0,1,\ldots,k}\,\bigl\{F_{i,j}+\langle g_{i,j},\,x-x_j\rangle\bigr\} \geq F_{i,j}+\langle g_{i,j},\,x-x_j\rangle$, and the second inequality is by the first order condition of convexity, i.e., $
    h_j(x)\geq h_j(x_j)+\langle g_j^N,x-x_j\rangle = \hat v_N(x_j)+\langle g_j^N,x-x_j\rangle.$
The inequality \eqref{eq:lb-comparison} is typically strict. Indeed, the first inequality in \eqref{eq:lb-comparison} holds with equality at $x$ only if there is a common index $j^*$ such that $\hat F_i(x) = F_{i,j^*} + \langle g_{i,j^*}, x - x_{j^*}\rangle$
simultaneously for all scenarios $i$ contributing to the value of $\rho$, and the second holds with equality only if $h_{j^*}$ is affine. After the first few iterations, however, different scenarios are typically maximized by different cuts, so that no common index $j^*$ exists and
\eqref{eq:our-lb} is strictly tighter than \eqref{eq:kelley-lb}.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
The master problem \eqref{eq:master} in Algorithm \ref{alg:cutting-alg} 
can be reformulated as linear programming problem (if $\mathcal{X}$ is a polyhedron) or conic programming problem (if $\mathcal{X}$ is conic-representable) for many commonly used risk measure. 
In these cases, the master problem is solvable by standard
off-the-shelf solvers. For instance:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item Expectation: $\mathbb{E}[\{\hat{F}_i\}_{i=1}^N]=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i \hat{F}_i$. \eqref{eq:master} becomes a linear programming problem.
    \item Conditional Value-at-Risk: $\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(\{\hat{F}_i\}_{i=1}^N)= \min_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\{s + \tfrac{1}{(1-\alpha)N}
        \sum_i [\hat F_i-s]_+\}$. \eqref{eq:master} becomes a linear programming problem with an extra scalar variable $s$.
    \item Entropic risk: $\rho(\{\hat{F}_i\}_{i=1}^N)=\tfrac{1}{\eta}\log\bigl(\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i e^{\eta \hat F_i}\bigr)$. \eqref{eq:master} can be formulated as an
        exponential cone programming problem.
\end{itemize}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemma:sup-P-E}
    Let $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^p$ be an elliptically distributed random vector defined as in \eqref{eq:EC-stochastic-rep} and admit a bounded density function $f_\xi$, i.e., $\|f_{\xi}\|_{\infty}\triangleq\sup_{y\in \mathbb{R}^p} f_\xi(y) < \infty$. Then for any compact set $X\subset \mathbb{R}^p$, it holds that
    \begin{enumerate}[label = (\roman*)]
        \item $\lim\limits_{\delta \to 0} \sup_{x\in X} {\mathbb{P}}(\|\xi-x\|\leq \delta)=0$,
        \item for $p \ge 2$, $\sup_{x\in X} \mathbb{E}[1/\|\xi-x\|] < \infty$. 
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Part (i). Let $\mathbb{B}(x,\delta)\triangleq \{y\in\mathbb{R}^p: \|y-x\|\leq \delta\}$. Then for any $x \in X$ and $\delta > 0$, it holds that
    \[
        {\mathbb{P}}(\|\xi - x\| \le \delta)
        = \int_{\mathbb{B}(x,\delta)} f_\xi(y)\,\mathrm{d} y
        \le \|f_\xi\|_\infty \, |\mathbb{B}(x,\delta)|,
    \]
    where $|\mathbb{B}(x,\delta)|$ denotes the volume of $\mathbb{B}(x,\delta)$. Since $|\mathbb{B}(x,\delta)|\to 0$ as $\delta\to 0$, we conclude that ${\mathbb{P}}(\|\xi - x\| \le \delta)$ also tends to $0$ as $\delta\to 0$.

    Part (ii). Let $x \in X$ be arbitrary but fixed. We first note that
    \[
        \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{\|\xi - x\|}\right]
        = \int_{\mathbb{R}^p} \frac{f_\xi(y)}{\|y-x\|}\,\mathrm{d} y
        = \int_{\|y-x\|\le 1} \frac{f_\xi(y)}{\|y-x\|}\,\mathrm{d} y
        + \int_{\|y-x\|>1} \frac{f_\xi(y)}{\|y-x\|}\,\mathrm{d} y.
    \]
    For the second term,
    \[
        \int_{\|y-x\|>1} \frac{f_\xi(y)}{\|y-x\|}\,\mathrm{d} y
        \le \int_{\|y-x\|>1} f_\xi(y)\,\mathrm{d} y \le 1.
    \]
    For the first term, since $f_\xi$ is uniformly bounded over $\mathbb{R}^p$, it follows that
    \[
        \int_{\|y-x\|\le 1} \frac{f_\xi(y)}{\|y-x\|}\,\mathrm{d} y
        \le \|f_\xi\|_\infty \int_{\|y-x\|\le 1} \frac{\mathrm{d} y}{\|y-x\|} = \|f_\xi\|_{\infty} \int_{\|z\|\leq 1} \frac{\mathrm{d} z}{\|z\|}.
    \]
    It remains to show that $\int_{\|z\|\leq 1} \frac{1}{\|z\|} \mathrm{d} z$ is bounded. By polar coordinates, let $z=r\alpha$, where $r\in[0,1]$ and $\alpha\in \mathbb{U}^{p-1}$. Then
    \[
        \int_{\|z\|\le 1} \frac{\mathrm{d} z}{\|z\|} = |\mathbb{U}^{p-1}| \int_0^1 r^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d} r,
    \]
    where $|\mathbb{U}^{p-1}| $ denotes the volume of the unit sphere. Note that $\int_0^1 r^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d} r$ is finite if and only if $p\geq 2$, which shows that $\mathbb{E}[1/\|\xi-x\|]$ is finite when $p\geq 2$. Since the upper bound is independent of $x$, we conclude that $\sup_{x\in X} \mathbb{E}[1/\|\xi-x\|]$ is also finite.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma:g-z}
    Let $g(x)\triangleq x/\|x\|$ for $0\neq x\in \mathbb{R}^p$. Then for any $x,y\in \mathbb{R}^p$ satisfying $tx+(1-t)y\neq 0,\forall t\in [0,1]$, it holds that 
    \[
        \|g(x)-g(y)\|\leq \sup_{t\in[0,1]} \frac{1}{\|tx+(1-t)y\|} \|x-y\|.
    \]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The result is a direct application of the mean value inequality for vector-valued functions. First note that the Jacobian matrix of $g$ is given by
    \[
        Dg(z) = \frac{1}{\|z\|} \left(I-\frac{zz^\top}{\|z\|^2}\right),
    \]
    and it holds that 
    \[
        \|Dg(z)\|_2 = \frac{1}{\|z\|}
    \]
    since $I-\frac{zz^\top}{\|z\|^2}$ is an orthogonal projection matrix and $\|I-\frac{zz^\top}{\|z\|^2}\|=1$. Then by the mean value inequality for vector-valued functions, we have
    \begin{align*}
        \|g(x)-g(y)\|\;\leq\; &\sup_{t\in[0,1]} \|Dg(tx+(1-t)y)\|_2 \|x-y\|\\
        \;=\; &\sup_{t\in[0,1]} \frac{1}{\|tx+(1-t)y\|} \|x-y\|,
    \end{align*}
    which completes the proof.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lemma:phi1-ULLN}
    Let $X\subset \mathbb{R}^p$ be a compact set. Then it holds that
    \begin{align}
        \sup_{x\in X}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(x,\xi_i)-\mathbb{E}[\varphi_1(x,\xi)]\right\|\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}  0. \label{eq:ULLN}
    \end{align}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $\mathbb{B}(x,r)\triangleq \{y\in \mathbb{R}^p: \|y-x\|\leq r\}$. Let $x_1,\ldots,x_N$ be the centers of a $\delta$-net of $X$, i.e., $X\subset \bigcup_{j=1}^N \mathbb{B}(x_j,\delta)$. Then for any $x\in X$ and $x_j$ satisfying $\|x-x_j\|\leq \delta$, it holds that
    \begin{align*}
        &\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(x, \xi_i) - \mathbb{E}[\varphi_1(x,\xi)]\right\|\\ \;\leq\; &\left\|\mathbb{E}[\varphi_1(x,\xi)] - \mathbb{E}[\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)]\right\| + \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(x, \xi_i) - \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(x_j, \xi_i)\right\| + \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(x_j, \xi_i) - \mathbb{E}[\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)]\right\|\\
        \;\leq\; &\mathbb{E}[\|\varphi_1(x,\xi)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)\|] + \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left\|\varphi_1(x,\xi_i)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi_i)\right\| + \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(x_j, \xi_i) - \mathbb{E}[\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)]\right\|.
    \end{align*}
    Hence, we have 
    \begin{align}
        &\sup_{x\in X}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(x,\xi_i)-\mathbb{E}[\varphi_1(x,\xi)]\right\|\nonumber\\
        \;\leq\; & \max_{1\leq j\leq N} \sup_{x:\|x-x_j\|\leq \delta}\mathbb{E}[\|\varphi_1(x,\xi)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)\|] + \max_{1\leq j\leq N} \sup_{x:\|x-x_j\|\leq \delta}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left\|\varphi_1(x,\xi_i)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi_i)\right\|\nonumber\\ &+ \max_{1\leq j\leq N} \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(x_j, \xi_i) - \mathbb{E}[\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)]\right\|. \label{ineq:ULLN-cover}
    \end{align}
    Now let $n\to\infty$ and we analyze the right hand side of \eqref{ineq:ULLN-cover} term by term. For the second term, we first note that for fixed $n$, 
    \[
        \max_{1\leq j\leq N} \sup_{x:\|x-x_j\|\leq \delta}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left\|\varphi_1(x,\xi_i)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi_i)\right\| \leq \max_{1\leq j\leq N} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \sup_{x:\|x-x_j\|\leq \delta}\left\|\varphi_1(x,\xi_i)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi_i)\right\|.
    \]
    Since $\|\varphi_1(x,\xi_i)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi_i)\|$ is uniformly bounded by $2$, by the strong law of large numbers, it follows that
    \[
        \limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{1\leq j\leq N} \sup_{x:\|x-x_j\|\leq \delta}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left\|\varphi_1(x,\xi_i)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi_i)\right\|\leq \max_{1\leq j\leq N} E\left[\sup_{x:\|x-x_j\|\leq \delta}\|\varphi_1(x,\xi)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)\|\right]
    \]
    almost surely.
    Similarly, for the third term, by the strong law of large numbers, it follows that 
    \[
        \max_{1\leq j\leq N} \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(x_j, \xi_i) - \mathbb{E}[\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)]\right\| \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0.
    \]
    For the first term, it is independent of $n$ and we note that
    \[
        \sup_{x:\|x-x_j\|\leq \delta}\mathbb{E}[\|\varphi_1(x,\xi)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)\|] \leq E\left[\sup_{x:\|x-x_j\|\leq \delta}\|\varphi_1(x,\xi)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)\|\right].
    \]
    Combining the above upper bound for the three terms, we have
    \begin{align}
        \limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in X}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(x,\xi_i)-\mathbb{E}[\varphi_1(x,\xi)]\right\| \leq 2\max_{1\leq j\leq N} \mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{x:\|x-x_j\|\leq \delta}\|\varphi_1(x,\xi)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)\|\right], \label{ineq:ULLN-limsup}
    \end{align}
    almost surely. Finally, it suffices to show that the right hand side of \eqref{ineq:ULLN-limsup} converges to $0$ as $\delta\to 0$. To see this, we note that by Lemma \ref{lemma:phi-1-ub}, it holds that
    \begin{align*}
        \mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{x:\|x-x_j\|\leq \delta}\|\varphi_1(x,\xi)-\varphi_1(x_j,\xi)\|\right] \;\leq\; &2 {\mathbb{P}}(\|\xi-x_j\|\leq 2\delta) + \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{2\delta}{\|\xi-x_j\|}\mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi-x_j\|>2\delta\}}\right]\\
        \;\leq\; & 2 {\mathbb{P}}(\|\xi-x_j\|\leq 2\delta) + \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{2\delta}{\|\xi-x_j\|}\right].
    \end{align*}

\label{lemma:h-ub}
    Let $X\subset \mathbb{R}^p$ and $Y\subset \{\Sigma\in {\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}: \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma) = p\}$ be compact sets. Let $\xi$ be arbitrary but fixed. Define 
    \[
        h(\mu,\Sigma,\xi) \triangleq \frac{(\xi-\mu)(\xi-\mu)^\top}{(\xi-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu)}.
    \]
    Then for any $\delta>0$ and $(\mu,\Sigma), (\mu',\Sigma')\in X\times Y$ such that $\|\mu-\mu'\| + \|\Sigma-\Sigma'\| \leq \delta$, it holds that 
    \begin{align}
        \|h(\mu,\Sigma,\xi) - h(\mu',\Sigma',\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq 2p\mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi-\mu'\|\leq 2\delta\}} + C\delta + C\frac{\delta}{\|\xi-\mu'\|}\mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi-\mu'\|>2\delta\}}, \label{ineq:oscillation-bd-mu-Sigma}
    \end{align}
    where $C$ is a constant only depending on choice of set $X$ and $Y$.

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We first consider some constants that only depend on the choice of $X$ and $Y$ or the whole space. 
    Since $Y$ is a compact subset of set of positive definite matrices, we have
    \[
        \underline{\lambda} \triangleq \inf_{\Sigma\in Y} \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma) > 0. 
    \]
    Moreover, since $\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p$ for all $\Sigma\in Y$, we have 
    \[
        \sup_{\Sigma\in Y}\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma) \leq p. 
    \]
    Now we note that 
    \[
        \|h(\mu,\Sigma,\xi) - h(\mu',\Sigma',\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq \|h(\mu,\Sigma,\xi)-h(\mu',\Sigma,\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}} + \|h(\mu',\Sigma,\xi)-h(\mu',\Sigma',\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}}. 
    \]
    In the following, we show that 
    \begin{align}
        \label{ineq:oscillation-bd-mu-Sigma-1}
        \|h(\mu,\Sigma,\xi)-h(\mu',\Sigma,\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq 2p\mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi-\mu'\|\leq 2\delta\}} + C_1 \frac{\delta}{\|\xi-\mu'\|} \mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi-\mu'\|\geq 2\delta\}}
    \end{align}
    and 
    \begin{align}
        \label{ineq:oscillation-bd-mu-Sigma-2}
        \|h(\mu',\Sigma,\xi)-h(\mu',\Sigma',\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq C_2\delta,
    \end{align}
    where $C_1$ and $C_2$ are constants only depending on $\underline{\lambda}$ and $p$. Then combining the above three inequalities, we show that \eqref{ineq:oscillation-bd-mu-Sigma} holds.

    Now we show that \eqref{ineq:oscillation-bd-mu-Sigma-1} holds. 
    First note that a crude bound gives that 
    \[
        \|h(\mu,\Sigma,\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}} = \frac{\|\xi-\mu\|_2^2}{(\xi-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu)} \leq \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma) \leq p,
    \]
    where the equality is by the fact that $\|xx^\top\|_{\text{F}}=\|x\|_2^2$, and the first inequality is by $\|x\|_2^2 \leq \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma) x^\top\Sigma^{-1}x$. Then it holds that
    \[
        \|h(\mu,\Sigma,\xi)-h(\mu',\Sigma,\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq 2p.
    \]
    Then to show \eqref{ineq:oscillation-bd-mu-Sigma-1}, it suffices to show that if $\|\xi-\mu'\|\geq 2\delta$, then
    \[
        \|h(\mu,\Sigma,\xi)-h(\mu',\Sigma,\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq  C_1 \frac{\delta}{\|\xi-\mu'\|}.
    \]
    Now assume that $\|\xi-\mu'\|\geq 2\delta$ and let $v\triangleq \Sigma^{-1/2}(\xi-\mu)$ and $u\triangleq \Sigma^{-1/2}(\xi-\mu')$. Then we have
    \begin{align}
        \|h(\mu,\Sigma,\xi) - h(\mu',\Sigma,\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}}  \;=\; &\left\|\Sigma^{1/2} \frac{vv^\top}{\|v\|_2^2}\Sigma^{1/2}-\Sigma^{1/2} \frac{uu^\top}{\|u\|_2^2}\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\nonumber\\
        \;\leq\; &\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)\left\| \frac{vv^\top}{\|v\|_2^2}- \frac{uu^\top}{\|u\|_2^2}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\nonumber\\
        \;=\; &\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma) \left\|\overline{v}(\overline{v}-\overline{u})^\top+(\overline{v}-\overline{u})\overline{u}^\top\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
        \quad (\overline{v}\triangleq v/\|v\|,\overline{u}\triangleq u/\|u\|)
        \nonumber\\
        \;\leq\; &p \left(\|\overline{v}\|\|\overline{v}-\overline{u}\| + \|\overline{u}\|\|\overline{v}-\overline{u}\|\right)\nonumber\\
        \;=\; &2p\left\|\frac{v}{\|v\|}-\frac{u}{\|u\|}\right\|.\label{ineq:2p-v-u}
    \end{align}
    Since $\|\mu-\mu'\|\leq \delta$ and $\|\xi-\mu'\|\geq 2\delta$, we note that $\xi$ does not fall on the line segment that connects $\mu$ and $\mu'$, and hence, $tv+(1-t)u\neq 0$ for all $t\in [0,1]$. Then by Lemma \ref{lemma:g-z}, it holds that 
    \begin{align*}
        (\text{RHS of}~\eqref{ineq:2p-v-u}) \;\leq\; &2p\sup_{t\in [0,1]} \frac{1}{\|tu+(1-t)v\|}\|u-v\|\\
        \;\leq\; &2p\sup_{t\in [0,1]} \frac{1}{\lambda_{\min} (\Sigma^{-1/2})\|t(\xi-\mu)+(1-t)(\xi-\mu')\|} \lambda_{\max} (\Sigma^{-1/2})\|\mu-\mu'\|\\
       \;=\; &2p \frac{\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma^{-1/2})}{\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma^{-1/2})} \sup_{t\in [0,1]} \frac{1}{\|\xi-z_t\|} \|\mu-\mu'\| \quad  (\text{Let}~z_t\triangleq t\mu+(1-t)\mu')\\
        \;\leq\; &4p\sqrt{\frac{p}{\underline{\lambda}}}\frac{\|\mu-\mu'\|}{\|\xi-\mu'\|}\\
        \;\leq\; &4p\sqrt{\frac{p}{\underline{\lambda}}}\frac{\delta}{\|\xi-\mu'\|},
    \end{align*}
    where the third inequality is by the inequality
        \[
        \|\xi-z_t\| \geq \|\xi-\mu'\| -\|z_t-\mu'\| \geq \|\xi-\mu'\| - \delta \geq \frac{1}{2}\|\xi-\,\mu'\|.
    \]
    The above completes the proof of \eqref{ineq:oscillation-bd-mu-Sigma-1} with $C_1 = 4p\sqrt{\frac{p}{\underline{\lambda}}}$.

    Now we show that \eqref{ineq:oscillation-bd-mu-Sigma-2} holds. We have
    \begin{align*}
        \|h(\mu',\Sigma,\xi)-h(\mu',\Sigma',\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}} \;=\; &\left\|\frac{(\xi-\mu')(\xi-\mu')^\top}{(\xi-\mu')^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu')}-\frac{(\xi-\mu')(\xi-\mu')^\top}{(\xi-\mu')^\top\Sigma'^{-1}(\xi-\mu')}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\\
        \;\leq\; &\|\xi-\mu'\|_2^2 \left|\frac{1}{(\xi-\mu')^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu')}-\frac{1}{(\xi-\mu')^\top\Sigma'^{-1}(\xi-\mu')}\right|\\
        \;=\; &\|\xi-\mu'\|_2^2\frac{\left|(\xi-\mu')^\top\Sigma'^{-1}(\xi-\mu')-(\xi-\mu')^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu')\right|}{(\xi-\mu')^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu')(\xi-\mu')^\top\Sigma'^{-1}(\xi-\mu')}\\
        \;\leq\; &\frac{\left|(\xi-\mu')^\top(\Sigma'^{-1}-\Sigma^{-1})(\xi-\mu')\right|}{\|\xi-\mu'\|_2^2/p^2}\\
        \;\leq\; &p^2\|\Sigma'^{-1}-\Sigma^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{F}},
    \end{align*}
    where the second inequality is by $(\xi-\mu')^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu') \geq \|\xi-\mu'\|_2^2\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma^{-1})=\|\xi-\mu'\|_2^2/\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)\geq \|\xi-\mu'\|_2^2/p$, and the third inequality is by $\left|(\xi-\mu')^\top(\Sigma'^{-1}-\Sigma^{-1})(\xi-\mu')\right|\leq \|\xi-\mu'\|_2^2 \|\Sigma'^{-1}-\Sigma^{-1}\|_{\text{F}}$. Finally, we note that 
    \[
        \|\Sigma'^{-1}-\Sigma^{-1}\|_{\text{F}} = \|\Sigma'^{-1}(\Sigma-\Sigma')\Sigma^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma^{-1}) \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma'^{-1}) \|\Sigma-\Sigma'\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq \delta/\underline{\lambda}^2.
    \]
    Hence, we conclude that
    \[
        \|h(\mu',\Sigma,\xi)-h(\mu',\Sigma',\xi)\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq p^2 \delta/\underline{\lambda}^2
    \]
    and completes the proof of \eqref{ineq:oscillation-bd-mu-Sigma-2} with $C_2=p^2/\underline{\lambda}^2$.

Define
    \[
        H_k \triangleq \operatorname{aff}(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_k),
        \qquad k=1,\ldots,p+1,
    \]
    where $\operatorname{aff}(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_k)$ is the affine hull of $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_k$.
    For each $k=1,\ldots,p$, the affine subspace $H_k$
    has dimension at most $k-1\leq p-1$ and hence has

    $(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_k)$, we have

        {\mathbb{P}}\bigl(
            \xi_{k+1}\in H_k

        \bigr)
        =0
        \quad\text{almost surely}.
    \]

    \[

        \qquad k=1,\ldots,p.

    $\xi_{k+1}\notin H_k$ simultaneously for all
    $k=1,\ldots,p$.

        \inf_{\substack{\|\mu\|\geq M,(\mu,\Sigma)\in\Theta}} \|\Phi_n(\mu,\Sigma)\| = &\inf_{\substack{\|\mu\|\geq M, (\mu,\Sigma)\in\Theta}} \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(\mu,\xi_i)\right\| + \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_2(\mu,\Sigma,\xi_i)\right\|_{\mathrm{F}} \\

    \end{align*}
    then it is enough to show that
    \[
        \liminf_{n\to\infty} \inf_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^p, \|\mu\|\geq M} \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(\mu,\xi_i)\right\| > 0,~\text{almost surely.}
    \]
                                               
                                        
Since $r$ is a single-valued random variable, its distribution is tight and hence the distribution of $\xi$ is also tight.
     Let $R>0$ be a sufficiently large constant such that 
    $
        P(\|\xi\| > R) < 1/8.
$
                                            
    We note that
    \begin{align}
        \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(\mu,\xi_i)\right\| \;=\; & \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left(\varphi_1(\mu,\xi_i)\mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| \le R\}} + \varphi_1(\mu,\xi_i)\mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| > R\}}\right)\right\| \label{ineq:b2-1}             
                                                                                                                                                                                                          
    \end{align}
    It remains to find a lower bound for the right hand side of \eqref{ineq:b2-1} as $n\to\infty$. Let $u\triangleq \mu/\|\mu\|$. By Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, for any $x\in \mathbb{R}^p$, 
 $   
        -u^\top x \leq |u^\top x| \leq \|u\|\|x\| = \|x\|. 
    $
    Then
    \begin{align}\label{ineq:b2-2}
        \text{RHS of}~\eqref{ineq:b2-1}\geq \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left(-u^\top\frac{\xi_i-\mu}{\|\xi_i-\mu\|}\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| \le R\}} + \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left(-u^\top\frac{\xi_i-\mu}{\|\xi_i-\mu\|}\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| > R\}} .
    \end{align}
  If $\|\xi_i\| \le R$, then 
    \[
        -u^\top\frac{\xi_i-\mu}{\|\xi_i-\mu\|} = \frac{-u^\top\xi_i+\|\mu\|}{\|\xi_i-\mu\|} \geq \frac{\|\mu\|-R}{\|\mu\|+R},
    \]
    where the inequality is by $u^\top\xi_i \leq \|\xi_i\| \leq R$ and $\|\xi_i-\mu\| \leq \|\mu\|+\|\xi_i\|\leq \|\mu\|+R$. Now let $M$ be sufficiently large such that $M>2R$, then for $\|\mu\| \geq M > 2R$, 
    \begin{align*}
         \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left(-u^\top\frac{\xi_i-\mu}{\|\xi_i-\mu\|}\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| \le R\}} \geq \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| \le R\}}.
    \end{align*}
    For the second term in the right hand side of \eqref{ineq:b2-2}, we use the trivial bound
    \[
     \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left(-u^\top\frac{\xi_i-\mu}{\|\xi_i-\mu\|}\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| > R\}} \geq -\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| > R\}},
    \]
    where the inequality is by the fact that, for any unit vectors $x$ and $y$, $x^\top y \leq 1$.
    Combining the above two lower bounds, we note that for any $\mu$ satisfying $\|\mu\| \geq M > 2R$,
    \begin{align*}
        \text{RHS of \eqref{ineq:b2-1}} \;\geq\; \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| \le R\}} -\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| > R\}}
        \;=\;  \frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\cdot \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| > R\}}.
    \end{align*}
    In particular, by the choice of $R$ and strong law of large numbers, it holds that $\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| > R\}} < 1/8 $, and thus, by setting $M > \max\{\|\mu_0\|+1, 2R\}$, we derive that
    \begin{align}
        \liminf_{n\to\infty} \inf_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^p, \|\mu\|\geq M} \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(\mu,\xi_i)\right\| \geq \liminf_{n\to\infty} \left\{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\cdot \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathds{1}_{\{\|\xi_i\| > R\}}\right\} > 0, \label{ineq:mu>M}
    \end{align}
    almost surely. 


    \underline{ Proof of \eqref{ineq:b3}}. 
                  
    Recall that by the definition, 
    \begin{align}
        \label{eq:b3-Phi}
        \|\Phi_n(\mu,\Sigma)\| = \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_1(\mu,\xi_i)\right\| + \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_2(\mu,\Sigma,\xi_i)\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}.
    \end{align}
    In \eqref{ineq:mu>M}, we have shown that the first term of \eqref{eq:b3-Phi} is strictly positive as $\|\mu\|\geq M$. 
    Then to show \eqref{ineq:b3}, it suffices to show that, for the second term of \eqref{eq:b3-Phi}, there exists $c>0$ such that
    \begin{align}\label{ineq:b3-1}
        \liminf_{n\to\infty} \inf_{\substack{\|\mu\|\leq M, \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\leq c,\\\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\Sigma\in {\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}}}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_2(\mu,\Sigma,\xi_i)\right\|_{\mathrm{F}} > 0
    \end{align}
    almost surely. We note that 
    \begin{align*}
        \left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \varphi_2(\mu,\Sigma,\xi_i)\right\|_{\mathrm{F}} \;=\; &\left\|\frac{\Sigma}{p}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}{(\xi_i-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\\
        \geq\; &\left\|\Sigma/p\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}-\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}{(\xi_i-\mu)^\top\Sigma_{}^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\\
        \geq\;&\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}-\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}{(\xi_i-\mu)^\top\Sigma_{}^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}},
    \end{align*}
    where the second inequality holds for $\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma) = p$. In the following, to show \eqref{ineq:b3-1}, we are to show that there exists a constant $c>0$ such that 
    \begin{align}\label{ineq:b3-2}
        \limsup_{n\to\infty} \sup_{\substack{\|\mu\|\leq M, \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\leq c,\\\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\Sigma\in {\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}}}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}{(\xi_i-\mu)^\top\Sigma_{}^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq \frac{1}{2p}~\text{almost surely},
    \end{align}
    since $1/\sqrt{p}-1/(2p)>0$.


To show \eqref{ineq:b3-2}, let $c>0$ be a constant to be specified shortly. We first introduce an auxiliary matrix by 
\[
    \Sigma^{(c)}\triangleq\frac{\Sigma+cI_p}{1+c}
\]
for each $\Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}$ with $\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p$,
and show that for every $\mu\in\mathbb{R}^p$ and every
$\Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}$ with $\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p$,
\begin{align}\label{ineq:b3-regularized-comparison}
    \left\|
        \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}
             {(\xi_i-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
    \leq(1+c)
    \left\|
        \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}
             {(\xi_i-\mu)^\top(\Sigma^{(c)})^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}.
\end{align}
To see this, note that
$(\Sigma+cI_p)^{-1}\preceq\Sigma^{-1}$ and
$(\Sigma^{(c)})^{-1}=(1+c)(\Sigma+cI_p)^{-1}$.
Therefore, for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^p$,
\begin{align*}
    0\preceq
    \frac{(x-\mu)(x-\mu)^\top}
         {(x-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu)}
    \preceq
    \frac{(x-\mu)(x-\mu)^\top}
         {(x-\mu)^\top(\Sigma+cI_p)^{-1}(x-\mu)}
    =(1+c)
    \frac{(x-\mu)(x-\mu)^\top}
         {(x-\mu)^\top(\Sigma^{(c)})^{-1}(x-\mu)}.
\end{align*}
Then we obtain
\begin{align*}
    0\preceq
    \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
    \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}
         {(\xi_i-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}
    \preceq
    (1+c)\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
    \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}
         {(\xi_i-\mu)^\top(\Sigma^{(c)})^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)},
\end{align*}
and hence \eqref{ineq:b3-regularized-comparison} holds. 

Then by \eqref{ineq:b3-regularized-comparison} and the triangle inequality, it holds that for every $\|\mu\|\leq M$ and every $\Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}$
with $\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p$ and $\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\leq c$,
\begin{align}
&\left\|
        \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}
             {(\xi_i-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq (1+c)
    \left\|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}
             {(\xi_i-\mu)^\top(\Sigma^{(c)})^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\nonumber\\
    \leq \;&(1+c)\left\{\left\|
        \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}
             {(\xi_i-\mu)^\top(\Sigma^{(c)})^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}
        -\mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu)(\xi-\mu)^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu)^\top(\Sigma^{(c)})^{-1}(\xi-\mu)}
        \right]
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\right.\nonumber\\&\quad+\left.
    \left\|
        \mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu)(\xi-\mu)^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu)^\top(\Sigma^{(c)})^{-1}(\xi-\mu)}
        \right]
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\right\}. \label{ineq:b3-3}
\end{align}
Hence, to show \eqref{ineq:b3-2}, it suffices to bound the right hand side of \eqref{ineq:b3-3} term by term. 

Now we deal with the second term in \eqref{ineq:b3-3}. To do this, we first claim that
\begin{align}\label{eq:b3-population-zero}
    \lim_{t\downarrow0}
    \sup_{\substack{\|\mu\|\leq M,\ \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\leq t,\\
                    \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\ \Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}}}
    \left\|
        \mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu)(\xi-\mu)^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu)}
        \right]
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}=0.
\end{align}
To ease notation, let
\[
    S(t) \triangleq \sup_{\substack{\|\mu\|\leq M,\ \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\leq t,\\
                    \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\ \Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}}}
    \left\|
        \mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu)(\xi-\mu)^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu)}
        \right]
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}.
\]
Then to show \eqref{eq:b3-population-zero}, for each integer $\ell\geq1$, choose a
pair $(\mu_\ell,\Sigma_\ell)$ satisfying
$\|\mu_\ell\|\leq M$, $\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_\ell)=p$,
$\Sigma_\ell\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}$, and $\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_\ell)\leq1/\ell$,
such that
\begin{align}\label{ineq:b3-4}
     S(1/\ell) \leq \left\|
        \mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu_\ell)(\xi-\mu_\ell)^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu_\ell)^\top\Sigma_\ell^{-1}(\xi-\mu_\ell)}
        \right]
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}} + \frac{1}{\ell}.
\end{align}
Since $\{(\mu,\Sigma):\|\mu\|\leq M,\
       \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\ \Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{+}}\}$
is compact, there exists a subsequence of $(\mu_\ell,\Sigma_\ell)$, indexed by $\ell_k$, such that
\[
    \mu_{\ell_k}\to\mu',\qquad
    \Sigma_{\ell_k}\to\Sigma'\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{+}},\qquad
    \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma')=p,\qquad
    \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma')=0.
\]
Let $v$ be a unit eigenvector of $\Sigma'$ corresponding to
the eigenvalue zero. For any fixed $x\in\mathbb{R}^p$ satisfying
$v^\top(x-\mu')\neq0$, the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality implies that
\[
    (x-\mu_{\ell_k})^\top\Sigma_{\ell_k}^{-1}(x-\mu_{\ell_k})
    \geq
    \frac{[v^\top(x-\mu_{\ell_k})]^2}
         {v^\top\Sigma_{\ell_k}v}
    \longrightarrow+\infty,
\]
where the convergence is by $[v^\top(x-\mu_{\ell_k})]^2\to [v^\top(x-\mu')]^2>0$ and $v^\top\Sigma_{\ell_k}v\downarrow v^\top\Sigma'v=0$.
Consequently,
\[
    \frac{(x-\mu_{\ell_k})(x-\mu_{\ell_k})^\top}
         {(x-\mu_{\ell_k})^\top\Sigma_{\ell_k}^{-1}
          (x-\mu_{\ell_k})}
    \longrightarrow0.
\]
Since by assumption, $\xi$ admits a density,
${\mathbb{P}}(v^\top(\xi-\mu')=0)=0$. Moreover, the upper bound established
in the proof of condition (a) gives that
\[
    \left\|
        \frac{(x-\mu_{\ell_k})(x-\mu_{\ell_k})^\top}
             {(x-\mu_{\ell_k})^\top\Sigma_{\ell_k}^{-1}
              (x-\mu_{\ell_k})}
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
    \leq\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_{\ell_k})\leq p.
\]
Thus, by the dominated convergence theorem,
\begin{align*}
    \left\|
        \mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu_{\ell_k})(\xi-\mu_{\ell_k})^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu_{\ell_k})^\top\Sigma_{\ell_k}^{-1}
                  (\xi-\mu_{\ell_k})}
        \right]
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
   \leq
    \mathbb{E}\left[
        \left\|
            \frac{(\xi-\mu_{\ell_k})(\xi-\mu_{\ell_k})^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu_{\ell_k})^\top\Sigma_{\ell_k}^{-1}
                  (\xi-\mu_{\ell_k})}
        \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
    \right]
    \longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}
Then by \eqref{ineq:b3-4}, $S(1/\ell_k)\to 0$ as $k\to\infty$. Moreover, since $S(t)$ is nonincreasing as $t$ decreases, this proves \eqref{eq:b3-population-zero}. Then by \eqref{eq:b3-population-zero}, there exists a deterministic
constant $c\in(0,1)$ such that
\begin{align}\label{ineq:b3-population-small}
    \sup_{\substack{\|\mu\|\leq M,\ \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\leq2c,\\
                    \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\ \Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}}}
    \left\|
        \mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu)(\xi-\mu)^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu)}
        \right]
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
    <\frac1{4p}.
\end{align}
Fix such a constant $c$ for the rest of the proof.
For each $\Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}$ with $\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p$, recall the definition of $\Sigma^{(c)}$:
\[
    \Sigma^{(c)}\triangleq\frac{\Sigma+cI_p}{1+c}.
\]
Then if $\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\leq c$, it holds that
\[
    \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma^{(c)})
    \leq\frac{2c}{1+c}\leq2c.
\]
Consequently, \eqref{ineq:b3-population-small} implies that
\begin{align}\label{ineq:b3-sigma-c-expectation}
    &\sup_{\substack{\|\mu\|\leq M,\ \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\leq c,\\
                     \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\ \Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}}}
    \left\|
        \mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu)(\xi-\mu)^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu)^\top(\Sigma^{(c)})^{-1}(\xi-\mu)}
        \right]
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}} \leq
    \sup_{\substack{\|\mu\|\leq M,\ \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\leq2c,\\
                    \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\ \Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}}}
    \left\|
        \mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu)(\xi-\mu)^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu)}
        \right]
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
    <\frac1{4p}.
\end{align}

Now we consider the first term in \eqref{ineq:b3-3}. We note that for $\Sigma\in {\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}$, it holds that 
\[
    \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma^{(c)})
    =\frac{\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)+c}{1+c}
    \geq\frac{c}{1+c}>0,
\]
or in other words, $\Sigma^{(c)} \succeq \frac{c}{1+c}I_p$.
The discussion of the first term in \eqref{ineq:b3-3} is an application of Lemma~\ref{lemma:h-ULLN} to the compact sets
\[
    X\triangleq \{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^p:\|\mu\|\leq M\}
    \quad\text{and}\quad Y\triangleq
    \left\{\Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}:
        \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\ \Sigma\succeq\frac{c}{1+c}I_p\right\}.
\]
Note that for $(\mu,\Sigma)\in X\times Y$,
\begin{align*}
    \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\varphi_2(\mu,\Sigma,\xi_i)
     -\mathbb{E}[\varphi_2(\mu,\Sigma,\xi)]
    =
    -\left\{
        \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}
             {(\xi_i-\mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}
        -\mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu)(\xi-\mu)^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu)}
        \right]
    \right\}.
\end{align*}
Thus, Lemma~\ref{lemma:h-ULLN} implies that
\begin{align}\label{ineq:b3-regularized-uniform}
&\sup_{\substack{\|\mu\|\leq M,\\
                     \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\ \Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}}} \left\|
        \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}
             {(\xi_i-\mu)^\top(\Sigma^{(c)})^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}
        -\mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu)(\xi-\mu)^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu)^\top(\Sigma^{(c)})^{-1}(\xi-\mu)}
        \right]
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\nonumber\\
    \leq &\sup_{\substack{\|\mu\|\leq M,\ \Sigma\succeq cI_p/(1+c),\\
                     \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\ \Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}}}
    \left\|
        \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}
             {(\xi_i-\mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}
        -\mathbb{E}\left[
            \frac{(\xi-\mu)(\xi-\mu)^\top}
                 {(\xi-\mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1}(\xi-\mu)}
        \right]
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
    \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}0.
\end{align}

Then combining \eqref{ineq:b3-3} and the upper bounds \eqref{ineq:b3-sigma-c-expectation}, 
\eqref{ineq:b3-regularized-uniform}, we conclude that
\begin{align*}
    &\limsup_{n\to\infty}
    \sup_{\substack{\|\mu\|\leq M,\ \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\leq c,\\
                    \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)=p,\ \Sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^p_{++}}}}
    \left\|
        \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        \frac{(\xi_i-\mu)(\xi_i-\mu)^\top}
             {(\xi_i-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\xi_i-\mu)}
    \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\frac{1+c}{4p}<\frac1{2p}
    \qquad\text{almost surely},
\end{align*}
where the last inequality follows from $c<1$.
This completes the proof of \eqref{ineq:b3}.

Let
\[
    \mathscr{P}_q(\mathbb{R})\triangleq \left\{\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}):\int_{\mathbb{R}} |z|^q\mu(\mathrm{d} z)<\infty\right\}
\]
be the set of all probability measures on $\mathbb{R}$ with finite $q$-th order moment.
Since $\rho$ is law-invariant, it induces a functional
$\mathcal R:\mathscr{P}_q(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}$ by
\[
    \mathcal R(\mu)\triangleq \rho(X),
\]
where $X\in L^q$ is any random variable whose law is $\mu$, and the domain of $\rho$ is given by
\[
    \operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{R}) = \left\{\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}):X\in L^q, X\sim \mu, \rho(X) < \infty\right\}.
\]
Let the $q$-Wasserstein distance be defined as
\[
    W_q(\mu,\nu) \triangleq \inf_{\gamma\in \Gamma(\mu,\nu)} \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times \mathbb{R}}|x-y|^q \gamma(\mathrm{d} x,\mathrm{d} y)\right)^{1/q} = \inf_{X\sim\mu,Y\sim\nu}
    \left(\mathbb E |X-Y|^q\right)^{1/q} ,\quad \mu,\nu\in \mathscr{P}_q(\mathbb{R}).
\]
In the following, we first show that functional $\mathcal{R}:\mathscr{P}_q(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}$ is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the $q$-Wasserstein distance on its domain. Let $\mu,\nu\in \operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{R})$, and let $X\sim \mu,Y\sim\nu$. Then, by the $L^q$-Lipschitz continuity of $\rho$,
\[
    |\mathcal R(\mu)-\mathcal R(\nu)|
    =
    |\rho(X)-\rho(Y)|
    \le
    \kappa_1 \|X-Y\|_{L^q}
    =
    \kappa_1
    \left(\mathbb E |X-Y|^q\right)^{1/q}.
\]
Since the above inequality holds for every possible $X$ and $Y$, by taking infimum over $X\sim \mu,Y\sim \nu$, it holds that
\[
    |\mathcal R(\mu)-\mathcal R(\nu)|
    \le
    \kappa_1
    \inf_{X\sim\mu,Y\sim\nu}
    \left(\mathbb E |X-Y|^q\right)^{1/q} = \kappa_1 W_q(\mu,\nu).
\]
Given $x\in \mathcal{X}$, let $P_x$ be the law of $g(x,\xi)$ induced by the distribution of $\xi$, and $\widehat{P}_{N,x}$ be the uniform distribution over $\{g(x,\xi_i)\}_{i=1}^N$. Then for every $x\in\mathcal X$,
\[
    |\widehat{v}_N(x)-{v}(x)|
    =
    |\mathcal R(\widehat P_{N,x})-\mathcal R(P_x)|
    \le
    \kappa_1 W_q(\widehat P_{N,x},P_x).
\]
Taking the supremum over $x\in\mathcal X$ on both sides, we obtain
\[
    \sup_{x\in\mathcal X}
    |\widehat{v}_N(x)-{v}(x)|
    \le
    \kappa_1
    \sup_{x\in\mathcal X}
    W_q(\widehat P_{N,x},P_x).
\]
Then to show the result, it remains to show that
\[
    \sup_{x\in\mathcal X}
    W_q(\widehat P_{N,x},P_x)
    \to 0
    \qquad \text{a.s.}
\]
 as $ N\to\infty$.
To show this, let $\varepsilon >0$ be a constant. Since $\mathcal X$ is compact, there exists a finite $\varepsilon$-net $\{x_1,\dots,x_M\}\subset \mathcal X$ such that for every
$x\in\mathcal X$, there is some $x_j$ satisfying
$\|x-x_j\|\le \varepsilon$. For such $x_j$, the triangle inequality implies that
\begin{align}
    \label{ineq:Wq-3}
    W_q(\widehat P_{N,x},P_x)
    \le
    W_q(\widehat P_{N,x},\widehat P_{N,x_j})
    +
    W_q(\widehat P_{N,x_j},P_{x_j})
    +
    W_q(P_{x_j},P_x).
\end{align}
Now we analyze the right hand side of \eqref{ineq:Wq-3} term by term. For the third term, since $g(x,\xi) \sim P_x$ and $g(x_j,\xi) \sim P_{x_j}$, by definition of $q$-Wasserstein distance, we have
\begin{align}\label{ineq:Wq-term3}
     W_q(P_x,P_{x_j}) \le\; &\left(
        \mathbb{E} [|g(x,\xi)-g(x_j,\xi)|^q] \right)^{1/q}
    \le \left(\mathbb{E} [\kappa_2(\xi)^q\|x-x_j\|^q] \right)^{1/q} = 
    \|x-x_j\|\|\kappa_2(\xi)\|_{L^q}\nonumber\\
    \leq \;&\varepsilon\|\kappa_2(\xi)\|_{L^q},
\end{align}
where the second inequality is by the Lipschitz continuity of $g$.
Likewise, for the first term, construct the coupling
\[
    \widehat\gamma_N \triangleq \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \delta_{(g(x,\xi_i),g(x_j,\xi_i))} \in \Gamma(\widehat{P}_{N,x}, \widehat{P}_{N,x_j}).
\]
Then we have
\begin{align}\label{ineq:Wq-term1}
    W_q(\widehat P_{N,x},\widehat P_{N,x_j})
    \le\; &\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times \mathbb{R}} |s-t|^q \widehat\gamma_N(\mathrm{d} s,\mathrm{d} t)\right)^{1/q} = 
    \left(
        \frac1N
        \sum_{i=1}^N
        |g(x,\xi_i)-g(x_j,\xi_i)|^q
    \right)^{1/q}\nonumber\\
    \le\; &\|x-x_j\|
    \left(\frac1N \sum_{i=1}^N \kappa_2(\xi_i)^q
    \right)^{1/q} \le\; \varepsilon \left(\frac1N \sum_{i=1}^N \kappa_2(\xi_i)^q \right)^{1/q}.
\end{align}
Combining \eqref{ineq:Wq-3}, \eqref{ineq:Wq-term3}, and \eqref{ineq:Wq-term1} and taking supremum on both sides of \eqref{ineq:Wq-3}, we obtain
\begin{align}\label{ineq:sup-Wq}
    \sup_{x\in\mathcal X}
    W_q(\widehat P_{N,x},P_x)
    \le\;&
    \max_{1\le j\le M}
    W_q(\widehat P_{N,x_j},P_{x_j}) +\varepsilon
    \left[
        \left(
            \frac1N
            \sum_{i=1}^N
            \kappa_2(\xi_i)^q
        \right)^{1/q}
        +
        \|\kappa_2(\xi)\|_{L^q}
    \right].
\end{align}
For each fixed $x_j$, since $g(x_j,\xi)\in L^q$, the empirical measure
$\widehat P_{N,x_j}$ converges to $P_{x_j}$ in $W_q$ almost surely. Hence, since the net is finite,
\[
    \max_{1\le j\le M}
    W_q(\widehat P_{N,x_j},P_{x_j})
    \to 0
    \qquad \text{a.s. as}\; N\to\infty.
\]
Moreover, by the strong law of large numbers,
\[
    \left(
        \frac1N
        \sum_{i=1}^N
        \kappa_2(\xi_i)^q
    \right)^{1/q}
    \to
    \|\kappa_2\|_{L^q}
    \qquad \text{a.s.}
\]
Consequently, \eqref{ineq:sup-Wq} implies that
\[
    \limsup_{N\to\infty}
    \sup_{x\in\mathcal X}
    W_q(\widehat P_{N,x},P_x)
    \le
    2\varepsilon \|\kappa_2\|_{L^q}
    \qquad \text{a.s.}
\]
Now let $\varepsilon\to 0$, we obtain
\[
    \limsup_{N\to\infty}
    \sup_{x\in\mathcal X}
    W_q(\widehat P_{N,x},P_x)= 0
    \qquad \text{a.s.}
\]
This completes the proof.

The proof adapts the convergence analysis of Kelley's method
(see, e.g., \citealp{ref:kelley1960cutting}) to the master problem \eqref{eq:master}. We first prove two preliminary facts: a Lipschitz estimate for $\rho$, and a uniform bound on the cut slopes $g_{i,j}$.

For $a = (a_1,\ldots,a_N) \in \mathbb{R}^N$, write $\rho(\{a_i\}_{i=1}^N)$ for the
risk of the uniform distribution over $\{a_1,\ldots,a_N\}$ as in
\eqref{eq:saa}. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho-lip}
    \left|\rho\big(\{a_i\}_{i=1}^N\big) - \rho\big(\{b_i\}_{i=1}^N\big)\right|
    \;\le\; \max_{1\le i\le N} |a_i - b_i|,
    \qquad \forall\, a, b \in \mathbb{R}^N.
\end{equation}
To see this, let $c \triangleq \max_{1\le i\le N}|a_i - b_i|$. Then by the monotonicity and translation invariance of $\rho$,
\[
    \rho(\{a_i\}_{i=1}^N) \;\le\; \rho(\{b_i\}_{i=1}^N + c) \;=\; \rho(\{b_i\}_{i=1}^N) + c.
\]
Exchanging the roles of $a$ and $b$, we have \eqref{eq:rho-lip}.

Next we bound the cut slopes. Fix $i \in \{1,\ldots,N\}$. The function $F(\cdot,\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$ is convex and finite on $\mathbb{R}^d$. Hence $\partial_x F(x,\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$ is
nonempty for every $x \in \mathcal{X}$, and is bounded uniformly over the
compact set $\mathcal{X}$
\citep[Theorem~23.4]{rockafellar1997convex}. Define
\begin{align}
    \label{def:G}
    G \;\triangleq\; \max_{1\le i\le N}\; \sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\;
    \sup\big\{\|g\| : g \in \partial_x F(x,\mu_i,\Sigma_i)\big\} \;<\; \infty.
\end{align}
Then $\|g_{i,j}\| \le G$ for all $i$ and $j$. Moreover, for any
$x, y \in \mathcal{X}$ and any $g \in \partial_x F(x,\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$, the
subgradient inequality gives
$F(y,\mu_i,\Sigma_i) \ge F(x,\mu_i,\Sigma_i) + \langle g, y - x\rangle
\ge F(x,\mu_i,\Sigma_i) - G\|y - x\|$. Exchanging $x$ and $y$ shows that
$F(\cdot,\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$ is $G$-Lipschitz continuous on $\mathcal{X}$.
Combining this with \eqref{eq:rho-lip}, $\hat{v}_N$ is $G$-Lipschitz continuous on $\mathcal{X}$.


\underline{Step 1: proof of part (i).} For $k \ge 0$, 
let 
\[
    \hat{F}_i^k(x) \triangleq \max_{j=0,1,\ldots,k}
    \big\{F_{i,j} + \langle g_{i,j}, x - x_j\rangle\big\}\quad\text{for}~i=1,\ldots,N,
\]
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-fn}
    \underline{v}_k(x) \;\triangleq\;
    \rho\Big(\left\{\hat{F}_i^k(x)\right\}_{i=1}^N\Big).
\end{equation}
Recall that $\mathrm{LB}_{k+1}$ is the optimal value of \eqref{eq:master}.
We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:master-equiv}
    \mathrm{LB}_{k+1} \;=\; \underline{v}_k(x_{k+1})
    \;=\; \min_{x\in\mathcal{X}} \underline{v}_k(x).
\end{equation}
To see this, let $x \in \mathcal{X}$ be fixed. By construction, a vector $\hat{F} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ is feasible for \eqref{eq:master} at this $x$ if and only if $\hat{F}_i \ge \hat{F}_i^k(x)$ for $i=1,\ldots,N$. Moreover, the objective of \eqref{eq:master} is nondecreasing in $\hat{F}$ by the monotonicity of $\rho$. Then by fixing $x$, the partial minimization of \eqref{eq:master} over $\hat{F}$ has value $\underline{v}_k(x)$, and hence \eqref{eq:master-equiv} follows. The minimum on the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:master-equiv} is attained since $\underline{v}_k$ is continuous in $x$, and $\mathcal{X}$ is compact.

Since $g_{i,j} \in \partial_x F(x_j,\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$, the subgradient inequality gives that 
\[
    F(x,\mu_i,\Sigma_i) \ge F_{i,j} + \langle g_{i,j}, x - x_j\rangle
\]
for all $x \in \mathcal{X}$ and all $i, j$. Hence
$F(x,\mu_i,\Sigma_i) \ge \hat{F}_i^k(x)$ for $i=1,\ldots,N$, and the monotonicity of $\rho$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-under}
    \hat{v}_N(x) \;\ge\; \underline{v}_k(x), \qquad \forall\, x\in\mathcal{X}.
\end{equation}
Minimizing both sides of \eqref{eq:model-under} over $\mathcal{X}$ and using \eqref{eq:master-equiv}, we have $\mathrm{LB}_{k+1} \le \hat{v}_N^*$. Also, $\mathrm{UB}_{k+1} = \min_{j=0,1,\ldots,k}\hat{v}_N(x_j) \ge \hat{v}_N^*$. This
shows the sandwich inequality in part~(i). The sequence $\{\mathrm{UB}_k\}$ is nonincreasing because it is a running minimum. The sequence $\{\mathrm{LB}_k\}$ is nondecreasing because
$\underline{v}_{k+1} \ge \underline{v}_k$ pointwise, and hence $\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}} \underline{v}_{k+1}(x) \geq \min_{x\in\mathcal{X}} \underline{v}_{k}(x) $.

\underline{Step 2: the gap estimate.} We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-estimate}
    \mathrm{UB}_{k+1} - \mathrm{LB}_{k+1} \;\le\; G\,\|x_{k+1} - x_j\|,
    \qquad \forall\, j = 0,1,\ldots,k.
\end{equation}
Let $j \in \{0,\ldots,k\}$ be fixed. For every $i$,
\[
    \hat{F}_i^k(x_{k+1})
    \;\ge\; F_{i,j} + \langle g_{i,j}, x_{k+1} - x_j\rangle
    \;\ge\; F(x_j,\mu_i,\Sigma_i) - G\|x_{k+1} - x_j\|,
\]
where the first inequality is by the definition of $\hat{F}_i^k$, and the second inequality is by
$F_{i,j} = F(x_j,\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$ and the inequality $\left|\langle g_{i,j}, x_{k+1} - x_j\rangle\right| \leq G\|x_{k+1}-x_j\|$. The monotonicity and translation invariance of $\rho$ then give
\begin{align}\label{ineq:LB-v}
    \mathrm{LB}_{k+1} \;=\; \underline{v}_k(x_{k+1})
    \;\ge\; \rho\Big(\big\{F(x_j,\mu_i,\Sigma_i)\big\}_{i=1}^N\Big)
        - G\|x_{k+1} - x_j\|
    \;=\; \hat{v}_N(x_j) - G\|x_{k+1} - x_j\|.
\end{align}
On the other hand, by the definition of $\mathrm{UB}_{k+1}$ we have $\mathrm{UB}_{k+1} \leq \hat{v}_N(x_j)$, and \eqref{eq:gap-estimate} follows.

\underline{Step 3: proof of parts (ii) and (iii).} Let
$\Delta_k \triangleq \mathrm{UB}_k - \mathrm{LB}_k$. By part~(i), $\Delta_k \ge 0$
and $\{\Delta_k\}$ is nonincreasing. Since $\mathcal{X}$ is compact, the
sequence $\{x_k\}$ admits a convergent subsequence $\{x_{k_l}\}$. A
convergent sequence is a Cauchy sequence, so
$\|x_{k_{l+1}} - x_{k_l}\| \to 0$ as $l \to \infty$. Applying
\eqref{eq:gap-estimate}, we have $\Delta_{k_{l+1}} \le G\|x_{k_{l+1}} - x_{k_l}\| \to 0$. Hence
$\Delta_k \to 0$. Combining $\Delta_k \to 0$ with (i), we have $\mathrm{UB}_k \downarrow \hat{v}_N^*$ and $\mathrm{LB}_k \uparrow \hat{v}_N^*$.

Now let $\bar{x}$ be an accumulation point of $\{x_k\}$, and let
$\{x_{m_l}\}$ be a subsequence with $x_{m_l} \to \bar{x}$. By \eqref{ineq:LB-v} and the fact that $\hat{v}_N(x_{m_l}) \ge \mathrm{UB}_{m_{l+1}}$, we obtain
\[
    \hat{v}_N^* \;\le\; \hat{v}_N(x_{m_l})
    \;\le\; \mathrm{LB}_{m_{l+1}} + G\|x_{m_{l+1}} - x_{m_l}\|
    \;\to\; \hat{v}_N^*,
\]
where the convergence uses $\mathrm{LB}_k \uparrow \hat{v}_N^*$ and
$\|x_{m_{l+1}} - x_{m_l}\| \to 0$. Hence
$\hat{v}_N(x_{m_l}) \to \hat{v}_N^*$. By the continuity of $\hat{v}_N$ on
$\mathcal{X}$, $\hat{v}_N(\bar{x}) = \hat{v}_N^*$. This proves part~(ii).

Finally, let $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $\Delta_k \to 0$, there exists a finite
$K$ with $\Delta_K \le \varepsilon$, and the algorithm terminates at
iteration $K$. The returned point satisfies
$\hat{v}_N(x^*) = \mathrm{UB}_K \le \mathrm{LB}_K + \varepsilon
\le \hat{v}_N^* + \varepsilon$, where the last inequality is by part~(i).
This proves part~(iii).
