\label{lem:target-density-pmp}
	The space $\mathcal C_K$ is uniformly dense in $C(\mathcal M)$.  Moreover $A_0$ satisfies the positive maximum principle and $A_0\bm{1}=0$.

We first prove density.  Let $f\in C(\mathcal M)$ and $\eta>0$.  Choose a smooth
	$\phi$ on $\Gamma$ with
	\(
	\left\lVert \phi-f|_\Gamma\right\rVert_\infty<\eta.
	\)
	Fix a smooth collar cutoff $\chi$ such that \(\chi(r)=1\) for \(0\le r \le r_0/4\), and \(\chi(r)=0\) for \(r \ge r_0/2\). Extending $\phi$
	to every page by $\chi(r)\phi(y)$ and defining \(g_i(y,r):=f_i(y,r)-\chi(r)\phi(y)\) we obtain \(\sup_i \|g_i(y,0)\|_{L^\infty(\Gamma)}\le \eta\). 
	For \(\delta >0\), select a smooth cutoff \(\theta_\delta\) such that \(\theta_\delta(r)=0\) for \(0\le r \le \delta\), and \(\theta_\delta=1\) for \(r \ge 2\delta\). Defining \(g_i^\delta(y,r):=\theta_\delta(r)g_i(y,r)\), we have
	\[\|g_i^\delta-g_i\|_\infty\le \sup_{\substack{y\in\Gamma\\0\le r\le 2\delta}}|g_i(y,r)|\le \sup_{\substack{1\le i\le k, y\in\Gamma\\0\le r\le 2\delta}}|g_i(y,r)-g_i(y,0)|+\eta.\]
	Let us denote the supremum in the right hand side by \(\omega(\delta)\). Because the \(g_i\)'s are continuous up to the boundary, \(\Gamma\) is compact and there are finitely many pages we have \(\omega(\delta)\to 0\) as \(\delta \to 0\). Therefore \(\|g_i^\delta-g_i\|_\infty \le o_\delta(1)+\eta\). 
	
	Now, we can choose smooth approximations \(q_i \in C^\infty(M_i)\) supported outside of the collar \(\{(y,r):0\le r \le \delta/2\}\) such that \(\|q_i-g_i^\delta\|_\infty >\varepsilon\) for all \(\varepsilon >0\). Moreover, by the triangle inequality and the finite number of pages
	\(\|u-f\|_{C(\mathcal M)}\le \varepsilon+o_\delta(1)+\eta\) which proves the density of \(\mathcal G\).

	\Lambda_\varepsilon
	:=
	\left\lVert q_\varepsilon\right\rVert_\infty
	\max_{1\le i\le k}(-Q_{ii})<\infty.
	\)
	Let $N^\varepsilon$ be a rate-$\Lambda_\varepsilon$ Poisson process, independent of all Brownian drivers.  Starting from page $i$, run Brownian motion on
	$(\widetilde M_i,\widetilde g_i^\varepsilon)$ until the first proposal time of $N^\varepsilon$.  If the current point is outside the switching collar, keep the same page.  If the current point is $(i,y,s)$ with $|s|\le\varepsilon$, choose $j\ne i$ with probability
	\(\lambda_{ij}^\varepsilon(y,s)/\Lambda_\varepsilon
	\)
	and keep page $i$ with the remaining probability.  After an accepted page change, restart Brownian motion on the new double from the point with the same collar coordinates $(y,s)$.  Iterate this construction at the successive proposal times.
	
	Each doubled page is compact, hence its Brownian motion is conservative.  The proposal process has finitely many points on bounded intervals almost surely, so the recursive construction is defined for all times and page jumps cannot accumulate.
	
	The process is Feller.  To make this explicit, let $S_t^\varepsilon=S_{1,t}^\varepsilon\oplus \cdots S_{m,t}^\varepsilon$, where \(S_{i,t}^\varepsilon C(\widetilde M_i) \to C(\widetilde M_i)\) denotes the Feller semigroup of the Brownian motion on page \(\widetilde{M}_i\). 	\(
	(B_\varepsilon f)(i,y,s)
	:=
	\sum_{j\ne i}\lambda_{ij}^\varepsilon(y,s)
	\bigl(f(j,\phi_{ij}(y,s))-f(i,y,s)\bigr),
	\)
	in the switching collar, where \(\phi_{ij}:(y,s)\mapsto (y,s)\); outside that collar the summand is defined to be zero.  Because $q_\varepsilon$ is continuous, vanishes at the edge of its support, \(f\) is continuous and the maps \(\phi_{ij}\) are smooth, $B_\varepsilon$ maps $C(\widetilde E)$ to itself and
	\(
	\left\lVert B_\varepsilon\right\rVert\le 2\Lambda_\varepsilon.
	\)

	\[
	T_t^\varepsilon
	=
	S_t^\varepsilon
	+
	\sum_{m\ge1}
	\int_{0<s_1<\cdots<s_m<t}
	S_{s_1}^\varepsilon B_\varepsilon
	S_{s_2-s_1}^\varepsilon B_\varepsilon\cdots
	B_\varepsilon S_{t-s_m}^\varepsilon
	\,ds_1\cdots ds_m .

The limiting interface law is independent of the second fundamental
	forms of the pages. Indeed, the first normal variation of the Fermi
	volume density enters the microscopic generator through
	\(
	\kappa_i^\varepsilon(y,r)\)
	whose boundary value is the mean-curvature coefficient up to the
	chosen normal convention. The corresponding corrector contribution
	\(\kappa_i^\varepsilon\partial_r v_i^\varepsilon\) vanishes in the limit by the
	estimate established in Proposition~\ref{prop:generator-convergence}.
	Thus curvature remains only through the bulk operators
	\(\Delta_{g_i}\) and produces no additional leading-order interface
	term.
