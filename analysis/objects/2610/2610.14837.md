<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:mainnorm}
Let $K\ge1$, $0<p<\infty$, $\alpha>-1$, and $0<c<1$. Every
$K$-quasiregular mapping $f:B\to\mathbb R^n$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainnorm}
 \|f\|_{p,\alpha}^p
 \asymp |f(0)|^p+
 \int_B\bigl(\delta(x)D_{\mathrm{av},c}f(x)\bigr)^p\,dV_\alpha(x).
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
 \|f\|_{p,\alpha}^p
 \asymp |f(0)|^p+
 \int_B\left(\int_{B(x,c\delta(x))}J_f(y)\,\mathrm d y\right)^{p/n}
 dV_\alpha(x).
\]
The constants depend only on $n,K,p,\alpha,c$. In particular, $f\in\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$ if and only if either of
the above integrals is finite.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:maindomination}
Let $K\ge1$, $0<p<\infty$, and $\alpha>-1$. Every relatively dense
measurable set is dominating for $\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$. There is a dimensional
constant $K_*(n)$, with $K_*(2)=1$, such that the converse holds if
$K\geK_*(n)$. The converse also holds whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialrange}
 pK^{1/(n-1)}>n+\alpha.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:mainconformal}
Suppose $n\ge3$, $\alpha>-1$, and $0<p<\infty$. A measurable set
$E\subset B$ is dominating for $\mathcal A^p_{1,\alpha}$ precisely under the
following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $p<Q$, then $V_\alpha(E)>0$;
\item if $p=Q$, then, for one fixed $r_0\in(0,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logdensity}
 \liminf_{d\downarrow0}\ \inf_{\omega\in\partial B}
 \frac{1}{\log(r_0/d)}
 \int_d^{r_0}\Theta_E(\omega,r)\,\frac{dr}{r}>0;
\end{equation}
\item if $p>Q$, then $E$ is relatively dense.
\end{enumerate}
Condition~\eqref{eq:logdensity} is independent of $r_0$. The closed unit
ball of $\mathcal A^p_{1,\alpha}$ is compact in its ambient $L^p_\alpha$ metric
if and only if $p<Q$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:local}
Let $f$ be $K$-quasiregular on a domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$. For each $s>0$ and every ball $B(a,2r)\Subset\Omega$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localsup}
 \sup_{B(a,r)}|f|
 \le C\left(\fint_{B(a,2r)}|f|^s\,\mathrm d x\right)^{1/s}.
\end{equation}
There are $\vartheta=\vartheta(n,K)>0$ and $C=C(n,K,s)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localholder}
 |f(x)-f(y)|
 \le C\left(\frac{|x-y|}{r}\right)^\vartheta
 \left(\fint_{B(a,2r)}|f|^s\,\mathrm d z\right)^{1/s},
 \qquad x,y\in B(a,r/2).
\end{equation}
The same statements hold after subtracting a constant vector from $f$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The coordinate functions of a quasiregular mapping solve $\mathcal A$-harmonic equations of type $n$, with structure constants controlled by $n$ and $K$. The supremum estimate follows from the scalar estimate discussed after Lemma~2.1 in~\cite{AG25}; see also~\cite[Theorem~3.34 and Chapter~14]{HKM}. Applying that scalar estimate to each coordinate and using equivalence of the Euclidean and coordinate norms in $\mathbb R^n$, we obtain \eqref{eq:localsup}.

For $x,y\in B(a,r/2)$, the local H\"older estimate bounds $|f(x)-f(y)|$ by $C(|x-y|/r)^\vartheta$ times the supremum of $|f|$ on an intermediate ball. An application of \eqref{eq:localsup} on a slightly larger ball then yields \eqref{eq:localholder}. This is the usual local H\"older theorem for quasiregular mappings; see~\cite[Chapter~6]{HKM} and~\cite[Section~3]{FN}. Finally, subtracting a constant leaves both sides of the distortion inequality unchanged.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:caccioppoli}
If $f$ is $K$-quasiregular on $\Omega$, $b\in\mathbb R^n$, and $0\le\eta\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:caccioppoli}
 \int_\Omega\eta^n|Df|^n\,\mathrm d x
 \le (nK)^n\int_\Omega|f-b|^n|\nabla\eta|^n\,\mathrm d x.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $C_{1j}$ denote the entries in the first row of $\operatorname{cof} Df$. By the cofactor identity,
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^n\partial_j\bigl((f_1-b_1)C_{1j}\bigr)=J_f
\]
in distributions. For a smooth mapping, this follows by differentiating the determinant and using $\sum_j\partial_jC_{1j}=0$. For the present mapping, mollification on a neighborhood of $\operatorname{supp}\eta$ proves the identity: the derivatives converge in $L^n$, the cofactors in $L^{n/(n-1)}$, and the Jacobians in $L^1$. Since $f$ is continuous, its mollifications also converge uniformly on that neighborhood. We may therefore pass to the limit in the product with the cofactors.

Set $I=\int\eta^n|Df|^n$. Testing the identity with $\eta^n$ and using \eqref{eq:distortion}, we obtain
\[
 I\le K\int\eta^nJ_f
 =-nK\int\eta^{n-1}(f_1-b_1)
            \sum_j C_{1j}\partial_j\eta.
\]
The Euclidean norm of a row of $\operatorname{cof} Df$ is at most $|Df|^{n-1}$. Hence H\"older's inequality yields
\[
 I\le nK I^{(n-1)/n}
       \left(\int|f-b|^n|\nabla\eta|^n\right)^{1/n}.
\]
If $I=0$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, we divide by $I^{(n-1)/n}$ and take the $n$th power to obtain \eqref{eq:caccioppoli}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:cap}
For $\omega\in\partial B$ and $0<R\le1$,
\[
 V_\alpha(B\cap B(\omega,R))\asymp R^Q.
\]
Moreover, for every fixed $L\ge1$ there is $C_L$ such that, if $a\in B$, $t=\delta(a)$, and $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thincap}
 V_\alpha\bigl(\{x\in B:|x-a|<Lt,\ \delta(x)<\varepsilon t\}\bigr)
 \le C_L\varepsilon^{\alpha+1}t^Q.
\end{equation}
The constants depend only on the displayed parameters and on $n,\alpha$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Consider first $R<1/4$. If $x=(1-u)\zeta\in B(\omega,R)$, where $\zeta\in\partial B$, then $0<u<R$ and $|\zeta-\omega|<2R$. The area of this spherical cap is at most $CR^{n-1}$. Using polar coordinates, we obtain the upper estimate
\[
 V_\alpha(B\cap B(\omega,R))
 \le CR^{n-1}\int_0^R u^\alpha\,\mathrm d u
 =\frac{C}{\alpha+1}R^Q.
\]
For the lower estimate, the ball centered at $(1-R/2)\omega$ with radius $R/8$ lies in the cap, and $\delta(x)\asymp R$ on that ball. Its weighted volume is comparable to $R^Q$. The range $1/4\le R\le1$ follows from this fixed lower bound and $V_\alpha(B)<\infty$.

To prove \eqref{eq:thincap}, first suppose $Lt<1/4$. Then $|a|>3/4$. On the indicated set, $|x|>1/2$, and the possible directions $x/|x|$ form a spherical cap of radius at most $C Lt$. Its area is at most $C(Lt)^{n-1}$. The claimed bound now follows by integrating through the depths $0<u<\varepsilon t$. If $Lt\ge1/4$, we use the full boundary collar instead. Its weighted volume is at most $C(\varepsilon t)^{\alpha+1}$, and $t^{-(n-1)}\le(4L)^{n-1}$ absorbs the missing factor. This proves \eqref{eq:thincap} in both cases.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:hyperbolic}
For each fixed $R<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighttransport}
 \delta(\phi_a(u))\asymp\delta(a),\qquad
 \delta(\phi_a(u))^\alpha J_{\phi_a}(u)\asymp\delta(a)^Q,
 \qquad |u|\le R,
\end{equation}
uniformly in $a\in B$. In particular, for fixed $r\in(0,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyperbolicvolume}
 V_\alpha(\Delta_r(a))\asymp\delta(a)^Q,
 \qquad \operatorname{diam}\Delta_r(a)\le C_r\delta(a).
\end{equation}
If $0<r<R<1$, there is a sequence $(a_j)$ such that the balls $\Delta_r(a_j)$ cover $B$ and the balls $\Delta_R(a_j)$ have bounded overlap.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For $|u|\le R$, the denominator in \eqref{eq:automorphismdepth} and \eqref{eq:automorphismjac} lies between $(1-R)^2$ and $(1+R)^2$. Since $1-|a|^2\asymp\delta(a)$ and $1-|\phi_a(u)|^2\asymp\delta(\phi_a(u))$, it follows from these identities that \eqref{eq:weighttransport} holds. Integrating, we obtain the volume assertion. The diameter assertion also follows from the derivative bound in \eqref{eq:automorphismjac}; alternatively, the Euclidean center and radius of $\Delta_r(a)$ are
\[
 \frac{(1-r^2)a}{1-r^2|a|^2},\qquad
 \frac{r(1-|a|^2)}{1-r^2|a|^2}.
\]

For the covering, use the hyperbolic metric $d(a,x)=2\operatorname{arctanh}\rho(a,x)$. Choose a maximal $h$-separated sequence, with $0<h<2\operatorname{arctanh} r$. Maximality gives a covering by the $d$-balls of radius $h$, hence by $\Delta_r(a_j)$. The $d$-balls of radius $h/2$ centered at $a_j$ are disjoint. The volume element
\[
 d\tau(x)=\frac{\,\mathrm d x}{(1-|x|^2)^n}
\]
is invariant under the ball automorphisms, by \eqref{eq:automorphismdepth} and \eqref{eq:automorphismjac}. Thus every $d$-ball of a fixed finite radius has the same positive finite $\tau$-volume. If a point belongs to several $\Delta_R(a_j)$, the disjoint balls of radius $h/2$ associated with their centers all lie in the ball about that point of radius $2\operatorname{arctanh} R+h/2$. Comparing invariant volumes bounds their number.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:evaluation}
For $f\in\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$ and $a\in B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localevaluation}
 |f(a)|^p\le C\delta(a)^{-Q}
 \int_{B(a,\delta(a)/2)}|f(x)|^p\,dV_\alpha(x).
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:evaluationgrowth}
 |f(a)|\le C\delta(a)^{-Q/p}\|f\|_{p,\alpha}.
\end{equation}
For each fixed $f\in\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:littleoh}
 \delta(a)^{Q/p}|f(a)|\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{as }|a|\to1.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
On $B(a,\delta(a)/2)$ one has $\delta(x)\asymp\delta(a)$. Apply \eqref{eq:localsup} with outer radius $\delta(a)/2$ and exponent $p$. On replacing ordinary volume by $dV_\alpha$, we obtain \eqref{eq:localevaluation}. Enlarging the integral to $B$, we then deduce \eqref{eq:evaluationgrowth}.

The ball in \eqref{eq:localevaluation} lies in $\{x:\delta(x)<3\delta(a)/2\}$. The integral of $|f|^p$ over this boundary collar tends to zero as $\delta(a)\to0$, by integrability. This proves \eqref{eq:littleoh} for all approaches to the boundary.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:complete}
The metric space $(\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha},d_p)$ is complete. If $f_j,f\in\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$ and $d_p(f_j,f)\to0$, then $f_j\to f$ locally uniformly on $B$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We first prove the convergence assertion directly. In fact, only continuity of the proposed limit is needed. Let $E\Subset B$, and choose a compact neighborhood $E'\Subset B$ of $E$. For sufficiently small fixed $r>0$, every $B(a,r)$ with $a\in E$ is contained in $E'$. Define
\[
 \omega_f(r)=\sup\{|f(x)-f(y)|:x,y\in E',\ |x-y|\le r\}.
\]
The difference $f_j-f$ need not be quasiregular. Translation by the fixed vector $f(a)$ does preserve quasiregularity, so we use $f_j-f(a)$ instead. Applying \eqref{eq:localsup} to it and then using \eqref{eq:elementarypower}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 |f_j(a)-f(a)|^p
 &\le Cr^{-n}\int_{B(a,r)}|f_j(x)-f(a)|^p\,\mathrm d x\\
 &\le Cr^{-n}\|f_j-f\|_{p,\alpha}^p+C\omega_f(r)^p.
\end{align*}
The weight is bounded above and below on $E'$, and the constants are independent of $a$ and $j$. First let $j\to\infty$, and then let $r\downarrow0$. Uniform continuity on $E'$ shows that the convergence is uniform on $E$.

Now let $(f_j)$ be a Cauchy sequence in \eqref{eq:metric}. Since the ambient weighted $L^p$ space is complete, the sequence has a limit $g\in L^p_\alpha(B,\mathbb R^n)$. In particular, the sizes $\|f_j\|_{p,\alpha}$ are bounded. By Proposition~\ref{prop:evaluation} and Lemma~\ref{lem:local}, the sequence is locally uniformly bounded and equicontinuous. A diagonal Arzel\`a--Ascoli argument then allows us to pass to a subsequence that converges locally uniformly to a continuous mapping $h$. The quasiregular compactness theorem recalled in Section~\ref{sec:local} shows that $h$ satisfies the original distortion bound, with constants allowed.

The same subsequence converges to $g$ in $L^p_\alpha$. On each compact ball its uniform limit and its $L^p$ limit agree almost everywhere. Exhausting $B$, we obtain $h=g$ almost everywhere. Hence $h\in\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$, and the whole sequence converges to $h$ in the metric. This proves completeness.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:maximal}
For every fixed $c\in(0,1)$ and every $K$-quasiregular mapping $f$,
\[
 \int_B(\mathcal M_cf)^p\,dV_\alpha
 \asymp\int_B|f|^p\,dV_\alpha.
\]
The constants depend only on $n,K,p,\alpha,c$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The lower estimate follows from $\mathcal M_cf\ge|f|$. Choose $c<c'<1$. Cover $B(x,c\delta(x))$ by a bounded number of balls whose doubled balls lie in $B(x,c'\delta(x))$. Their radii are fixed multiples of $\delta(x)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:local} and comparability of the weight imply
\[
 (\mathcal M_cf(x))^p
 \le C\delta(x)^{-Q}
       \int_{B(x,c'\delta(x))}|f(y)|^p\,dV_\alpha(y).
\]
Integrating in $x$ and applying Tonelli's theorem, we see that it remains to prove
\[
 \int_{\{x:|x-y|<c'\delta(x)\}}\delta(x)^{-n}\,\mathrm d x\le C.
\]
Indeed, since $\delta$ is Lipschitz, we have on this set
\[
 (1-c')\delta(x)<\delta(y)<(1+c')\delta(x).
\]
The set is therefore contained in a Euclidean ball about $y$ of radius $C\delta(y)$, and the integrand is at most $C\delta(y)^{-n}$. This proves the bound. Tonelli's theorem also shows that the argument is valid with infinite integrals.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Fix $c\in(0,1)$ and put
\[
 G(x)=\delta(x)D_{\mathrm{av},c}f(x).
\]
All constants in this proof may depend on $c$, in addition to
$n,K,p,\alpha$. We first show that the Bergman integral controls the
right-hand side of \eqref{eq:mainnorm}. Choose $c'=(1+c)/2$ and a cutoff
which equals one on $B(x,c\delta(x))$, vanishes outside
$B(x,c'\delta(x))$, and has gradient bounded by $C_c/\delta(x)$.
Applying the Caccioppoli inequality with the constant vector equal to zero, we obtain
\[
 G(x)\le C\mathcal M_{c'}f(x).
\]
It now follows from the local maximal estimate and bounded evaluation at zero that
\[
 |f(0)|^p+\int_B G(x)^p\,dV_\alpha(x)
 \le C\|f\|_{p,\alpha}^p.
\]
This assertion is automatic if the right-hand side is infinite.

For the reverse inequality, suppose that $G\in L^p_\alpha(B)$.
The local supremum estimate first converts energy into oscillation.
Indeed, let $b_x$ be the average of $f$ on $B(x,c\delta(x))$.
Translation by $b_x$ preserves quasiregularity. Applying the local estimate
with exponent $n$, followed by the Poincar\'e inequality on this ball,
we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oscillationenergy}
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{osc}_{B(x,c\delta(x)/2)}f
 &\le C\left(\fint_{B(x,c\delta(x))}|f-b_x|^n\,\mathrm d y\right)^{1/n}\\
 &\le Cc\delta(x)
       \left(\fint_{B(x,c\delta(x))}|Df|^n\,\mathrm d y\right)^{1/n}
 \le CG(x).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Poincar\'e's inequality is applied componentwise. Replacing the Euclidean
matrix norm by the operator norm changes only a dimensional constant.

We first use \eqref{eq:oscillationenergy} to control the fixed interior
ball. Set $h=c/16$, and let $u,v\in B(0,1/2)$ satisfy $|u-v|\le h$.
For every $z\in B(u,h)$, we have $z\in B(0,3/4)$ and hence
$\delta(z)>1/4$. Therefore
\[
 |u-z|<h,\qquad
 |v-z|<2h=\frac c8<\frac{c\delta(z)}2.
\]
Both points lie in the oscillation ball centered at $z$. Thus, by
\eqref{eq:oscillationenergy},
\[
 |f(u)-f(v)|^p\le CG(z)^p
 \qquad(z\in B(u,h)).
\]
The weight is bounded above and below by positive constants on
$B(0,3/4)$, and consequently
$V_\alpha(B(u,h))\asymp h^n$, uniformly in $u$.
Integrating the preceding inequality over $B(u,h)$ and taking
$p$th roots yields
\[
 |f(u)-f(v)|\le C\|G\|_{p,\alpha},
\]
where the dependence on $h$ is absorbed into the allowed dependence
on $c$.

Now divide the segment from zero to any $x\in B(0,1/2)$ into
pieces of length at most $h$. The number of pieces is bounded
in terms of $c$, and all subdivision points remain in $B(0,1/2)$.
Adding the successive differences, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interiorcontrol}
 \sup_{B(0,1/2)}|f|
 \le |f(0)|+C\|G\|_{p,\alpha}.
\end{equation}
This last step uses only the ordinary triangle inequality in
$\mathbb R^n$, so the argument also applies when $p<1$.

To reach a point near the boundary, we use a radial chain whose step
length fits the oscillation balls in \eqref{eq:oscillationenergy}.
Set $\lambda=1+c/4$. For $x=r\omega$, where $1/2<r<1$ and
$\omega\in\partial B$, let
\[
 x_j=\bigl(1-\lambda^j(1-r)\bigr)\omega,
\]
and stop at the first index $J$ for which
$\lambda^J(1-r)\ge1/2$. Then $x_j\in B\setminus\overline{B(0,1/2)}$
for $j<J$, whereas
\[
 1-\lambda/2<|x_J|\le1/2.
\]
In particular, the last point lies in the interior ball and the chain
does not pass through the origin. Moreover,
\[
 |x_{j+1}-x_j|=(\lambda-1)\delta(x_j)
             =c\delta(x_j)/4<c\delta(x_j)/2.
\]
Applying \eqref{eq:oscillationenergy} with center $x_j$ and adding the
successive differences gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialchain}
 |f(x)|\le \sup_{B(0,1/2)}|f|
                +C\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}G(x_j).
\end{equation}
If the last point lies on the sphere of radius $1/2$, the same bound
follows by continuity.

For $j\ge0$, define $H_j(x)=G(x_j)$ on
$\{x:\lambda^j\delta(x)<1/2\}$, and put $H_j(x)=0$ elsewhere.
The points $x_j$ used in this definition all have modulus greater than
$1/2$. Under the radial change of variables $y=x_j$, the angular
variable is unchanged and
\[
 \delta(x)=\lambda^{-j}\delta(y),\qquad
 d|x|=\lambda^{-j}d|y|,
 \qquad \frac{|x|^{n-1}}{|y|^{n-1}}\le2^{n-1}.
\]
Consequently, integration in polar coordinates leads to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chainintegral}
 \int_B H_j(x)^p\,dV_\alpha(x)
 \le 2^{n-1}\lambda^{-j(\alpha+1)}
           \int_B G(y)^p\,dV_\alpha(y).
\end{equation}
The decay comes from the radial variable and the weight; its exponent
is $\alpha+1$.

If $0<p\le1$, it follows from subadditivity, \eqref{eq:chainintegral}, and Tonelli's theorem that
\[
 \int_B\left(\sum_{j\ge0}H_j\right)^p\,dV_\alpha
 \le 2^{n-1}\sum_{j\ge0}\lambda^{-j(\alpha+1)}
                     \|G\|_{p,\alpha}^p.
\]
If $p>1$, we apply Minkowski's inequality to a finite sum and obtain
\[
 \left\|\sum_{j=0}^mH_j\right\|_{p,\alpha}
 \le 2^{(n-1)/p}\sum_{j=0}^m
          \lambda^{-j(\alpha+1)/p}\|G\|_{p,\alpha}.
\]
Monotone convergence allows $m$ to tend to infinity. Both geometric
series converge because $\alpha>-1$. These estimates, together with
\eqref{eq:radialchain}, \eqref{eq:interiorcontrol}, and the finiteness of
$V_\alpha(B)$, imply
\[
 \|f\|_{p,\alpha}^p
 \le C\left(|f(0)|^p+\int_BG^p\,dV_\alpha\right).
\]
This proves the equivalence, including its assertion about finiteness.

Finally, quasiregularity and the singular-value inequality give
$J_f\le|Df|^n\le KJ_f$ almost everywhere. Since
$|B(x,c\delta(x))|=|B(0,1)|c^n\delta(x)^n$, one has
\[
 G(x)=\frac1{c|B(0,1)|^{1/n}}
       \left(\int_{B(x,c\delta(x))}|Df(y)|^n\,\mathrm d y\right)^{1/n}.
\]
The asserted integrated-Jacobian formula follows on replacing $|Df|^n$ by $J_f$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:poles}
There is a constant $K_*(n)\ge1$, with $K_*(2)=1$, such that the following holds. For every $\sigma>0$, $\omega\in\partial B$, and $t\ge0$, there is a $K_*(n)$-quasiregular mapping $F_{\sigma,\omega,t}:B\to\mathbb R^n$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polemodulus}
 |F_{\sigma,\omega,t}(x)|=|x-(1+t)\omega|^{-\sigma}.
\end{equation}
The distortion bound is independent of $\sigma,\omega,t$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
In dimension two, the nonvanishing holomorphic function $z\mapsto z-(1+t)\omega$ has a holomorphic logarithm in the disk. Thus
\[
 F_{\sigma,\omega,t}(z)
 =\exp\bigl(-\sigma\log(z-(1+t)\omega)\bigr)
\]
has the required modulus, and $K_*(2)=1$.

For $n\ge3$, let $Z$ and $\Lambda$ be the maps in Lemma~\ref{lem:zorich}, proved in Appendix~\ref{app:zorich}. Put $y=(1+t)\omega$ and choose $R_\omega\in SO(n)$ with $R_\omega(-\omega)=e_n$. Since
\[
 \bigl(R_\omega(x-y)\bigr)_n=1+t-\langle x,\omega\rangle>0
 \qquad(x\in B),
\]
the inverse branch $\Lambda$ is defined at every $R_\omega(x-y)$, including when $t=0$. Set
\[
 A_\sigma=\sigma\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,\ldots,1,-1)
\]
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poleconstruction}
 F_{\sigma,\omega,t}(x)
 =Z\bigl(A_\sigma\Lambda(R_\omega(x-y))\bigr).
\end{equation}
The map $A_\sigma$ is an orientation-preserving similarity: it reverses two coordinate signs and multiplies all coordinates by the same factor. Thus it has distortion one, independently of $\sigma$. By the chain rule, the outer distortion in \eqref{eq:poleconstruction} is at most the product of those of $Z$ and $\Lambda$. The inner maps are locally bi-Lipschitz, so the exceptional sets in the chain rule have null preimages. By Lemma~\ref{lem:zorich}, we may take
\[
 K_*(n)=\max\{3\pi/2,\,n-1+2\sqrt{n-1}\}^{4(n-1)}.
\]
Finally, the last coordinate of $A_\sigma\Lambda(R_\omega(x-y))$ is $-\sigma\log|x-y|$. Formula~\eqref{eq:zorichmodulus} now gives \eqref{eq:polemodulus}.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:poleintegral}
If $s>Q$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poleintegral}
 \int_B|x-(1+t)\omega|^{-s}\,dV_\alpha(x)
 \asymp t^{Q-s},\qquad 0<t\le1,
\end{equation}
uniformly in $\omega\in\partial B$. For every $s>0$, a pole on the boundary satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundarypoleintegral}
 \int_B|x-\omega|^{-s}\,dV_\alpha(x)<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s<Q.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Put $y=(1+t)\omega$. Since $|x-y|\ge t$ for $x\in B$, decompose the ball into shells where
\[
 2^jt\le|x-y|<2^{j+1}t,\qquad j\ge0.
\]
The part of a ball $B(y,R)$ in $B$, for $R\ge t$, lies in $B(\omega,2R)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:cap}, its weighted volume is at most $CR^Q$; for $R\ge1$, the same bound follows from $V_\alpha(B)<\infty$. Thus
\[
 \int_B|x-y|^{-s}\,dV_\alpha
 \le C\sum_{j\ge0}(2^jt)^{Q-s}
 \le Ct^{Q-s}.
\]
For the lower bound, use $B((1-t)\omega,t/8)$. On this ball $\delta(x)\asymp t$ and $|x-y|\asymp t$, so its contribution is at least $ct^{Q-s}$.

For a boundary pole, the same dyadic estimate at radii tending to zero proves finiteness when $s<Q$. To prove divergence when $s\ge Q$, put $t_j=4^{-j}$ and use the pairwise disjoint balls
\[
 B((1-t_j)\omega,t_j/8).
\]
Their contributions are bounded below by $ct_j^{Q-s}$. These terms do not form a summable sequence when $s\ge Q$, which proves \eqref{eq:boundarypoleintegral}.

\label{lem:concentration}
The normalized mappings just defined satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniformconcentration}
 \lim_{r\uparrow1}\sup_{a\in B}
       \int_{B\setminus\Delta_r(a)}|h_a|^p\,dV_\alpha=0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Set $s=\sigma p>Q$ and $t=\delta(a)$. Let $L>4$. If $|x-a|>Lt$, then
\[
 |x-(1+t)\omega|\ge|x-a|-2t>\tfrac12Lt.
\]
The shell calculation in Lemma~\ref{lem:poleintegral}, together with \eqref{eq:peakprofile}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peakfar}
 \int_{\{|x-a|>Lt\}}|h_a|^p\,dV_\alpha
 \le C L^{Q-s}.
\end{equation}
On the region $|x-a|\le Lt$, $\delta(x)<\varepsilon t$, we apply the first estimate in \eqref{eq:peakbounds} and \eqref{eq:thincap} to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peakthin}
 \int_{\{|x-a|\le Lt,\ \delta(x)<\varepsilon t\}}|h_a|^p\,dV_\alpha
 \le C_L\varepsilon^{\alpha+1}.
\end{equation}
The remaining points satisfy $|x-a|\le Lt$ and $\delta(x)\ge\varepsilon t$. Since $1-|a|^2\ge t$ and $1-|x|^2\ge\varepsilon t$, it follows from the definition of $\rho$ that
\[
 \rho(a,x)^2\le\frac{L^2}{L^2+\varepsilon}.
\]
Choose a fixed $r<1$ larger than the square root of the last expression. The remaining region lies in $\Delta_r(a)$, uniformly in $a$. First choose $L$ so large that \eqref{eq:peakfar} is small, and then choose $\varepsilon$ so small that \eqref{eq:peakthin} is small. This proves \eqref{eq:uniformconcentration}.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:localsmallness}
Fix $0<r<R<1$, $K\ge1$, $0<p<\infty$, and $D,m,A,s>0$. Let $\nu$ be a positive Borel measure on $B(0,r)$, extended by zero to $\mathbb R^n$, such that
\[
 \nu(B(0,r))\ge m,\qquad
 \nu(B(z,t))\le At^s
 \quad(z\in\mathbb R^n,\ 0<t\le1).
\]
If $g$ is $K$-quasiregular on $B(0,R)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localdoubling}
 \int_{B(0,R)}|g|^p\,\mathrm d x
 \le D\int_{B(0,r)}|g|^p\,\mathrm d x<\infty,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:measurelocalsmallness}
 \int_{B(0,r)}|g|^p\,\mathrm d x
 \le C\int_{B(0,r)}|g|^p\,\mathrm d\nu,
\end{equation}
where $C$ depends only on the fixed parameters.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
We may suppose that the integral over the smaller ball is nonzero and normalize it to one. If no uniform constant exists, there are mappings $g_j$ and measures $\nu_j$ satisfying the hypotheses such that
\[
 \int_{B(0,r)}|g_j|^p\,\mathrm d x=1,\qquad
 \int_{B(0,R)}|g_j|^p\,\mathrm d x\le D,\qquad
 \int_{B(0,r)}|g_j|^p\,\mathrm d\nu_j\longrightarrow0.
\]
By the local estimate and quasiregular compactness, we may pass to a diagonal subsequence converging locally uniformly on $B(0,R)$ to a quasiregular mapping or a constant $g$. Since the convergence is uniform on $\overline{B(0,r)}$, we have $\int_{B(0,r)}|g|^p\,\mathrm d x=1$. Thus $g$ is not identically zero.

The zeros of $g$ in $\overline{B(0,r)}$ are finite. Indeed, a nonzero constant has none, whereas a nonconstant quasiregular mapping has discrete zeros in the larger ball. Denote these zeros by $z_1,\ldots,z_N$. If $N>0$, choose $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ so that $NA\varepsilon^s<m/2$. The union of the balls $B(z_k,\varepsilon)$ then has $\nu_j$-measure less than $m/2$ for every $j$. On its compact complement in $\overline{B(0,r)}$, the function $|g|$ has a positive minimum. By local uniform convergence, $|g_j|$ is also bounded away from zero there, while the complement has $\nu_j$-measure at least $m/2$. This contradicts the last limit. If $N=0$, the same argument uses the whole closed ball.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:singularreverse}
Let $\mu$ be a positive Borel measure on $B$. Suppose that there are $r\in(0,1)$ and $m,M,s>0$ such that, for every $a\in B$, $z\in\mathbb R^n$, and $0<t\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diffusemeasure}
 \mu(\Delta_r(a))\ge m\delta(a)^Q,
 \qquad
 \mu\bigl(\Delta_r(a)\cap B(z,t\delta(a))\bigr)
 \le Mt^s\delta(a)^Q.
\end{equation}
Then, for every fixed $K\ge1$ and $0<p<\infty$,
\[
 C^{-1}\|f\|_{p,\alpha}^p
 \le\int_B|f|^p\,\mathrm d\mu
 \le C\|f\|_{p,\alpha}^p,
 \qquad f\in\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}.
\]
The constant depends only on $n,\alpha,K,p,r,m,M,s$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
First, $\Delta_r(a)$ is covered by a bounded number of Euclidean balls of radius $\delta(a)$, since its diameter is at most $C_r\delta(a)$. Applying the second condition in \eqref{eq:diffusemeasure} with $t=1$, we obtain
\[
 \mu(\Delta_r(a))\le C_rM\delta(a)^Q.
\]
Pull back the restricted measure to $B(0,r)$ and normalize it by setting
\[
 \nu_a(F)=\delta(a)^{-Q}\mu(\phi_a(F)),
 \qquad F\subset B(0,r)\text{ Borel}.
\]
Its mass is at least $m$. The automorphism $\phi_a$ is Lipschitz on $\overline{B(0,r)}$ with constant $L_r\delta(a)$, by \eqref{eq:automorphismjac} and conformality. Thus the image of $B(z,t)\cap B(0,r)$, when nonempty, lies in a ball of radius $2L_rt\delta(a)$. When $2L_rt\le1$, the second condition in \eqref{eq:diffusemeasure} applies to that ball. Otherwise, the preceding total-mass bound applies. Consequently
\[
 \nu_a(B(z,t))\le At^s\qquad(0<t\le1),
\]
with $A$ independent of $a$.

Choose $r<R<1$ and a covering
\[
 B_j=\Delta_r(a_j),\qquad B_j^*=\Delta_R(a_j)
\]
whose enlarged balls have overlap at most $N$, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:hyperbolic}. Fix $f\in\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$, and write
\[
 A_j=\int_{B_j}|f|^p\,dV_\alpha,\qquad
 A_j^*=\int_{B_j^*}|f|^p\,dV_\alpha.
\]
Call $j$ good if $A_j^*\le2NA_j$. Since $f\in\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$, it follows from bounded overlap that $\sum_jA_j^*<\infty$. The remaining balls account for at most half the integral:
\[
 \sum_{j\text{ not good}}A_j
 \le\frac1{2N}\sum_jA_j^*
 \le\frac12\|f\|_{p,\alpha}^p.
\]
Since the smaller balls cover $B$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:goodmass}
 \sum_{j\text{ good}}A_j\ge\frac12\|f\|_{p,\alpha}^p.
\end{equation}
For a good index, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:localsmallness} to $g=f\circ\phi_{a_j}$ and $\nu=\nu_{a_j}$. By the weighted change of variables \eqref{eq:weighttransport}, we have
\[
 A_j\asymp\delta(a_j)^Q\int_{B(0,r)}|g|^p\,\mathrm d u,\qquad
 A_j^*\asymp\delta(a_j)^Q\int_{B(0,R)}|g|^p\,\mathrm d u.
\]
Hence \eqref{eq:localdoubling} holds with $D$ independent of $j$, and \eqref{eq:measurelocalsmallness} becomes
\[
 A_j\le C\int_{B_j}|f|^p\,\mathrm d\mu.
\]
Summing over good indices and using \eqref{eq:goodmass} together with bounded overlap, we obtain the reverse inequality.

For the forward inequality, we combine \eqref{eq:hyperbolicsup} with the upper mass bound to obtain
\[
 \int_{B_j}|f|^p\,\mathrm d\mu
 \le\mu(B_j)\sup_{B_j}|f|^p
 \le CA_j^*.
\]
Summation proves the result. In particular, applying this estimate to a nonzero constant shows that $\mu(B)<\infty$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:sampling}
There is an integer $m=m(n,K,p,\alpha)$ with the following property. Subdivide each $W\in\mathcal W$ into $m^n$ congruent cubes $E$, and choose $a_E\in E$. The atomic measure of finite mass
\[
 \mu_m=\sum_E V_\alpha(E)\,\delta_{a_E}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \|f\|_{p,\alpha}^p
 \asymp\int_B|f|^p\,\mathrm d\mu_m
 =\sum_E V_\alpha(E)|f(a_E)|^p

\label{prop:nonlinear}

\[

\]
Its restriction to $B$ is therefore quasiconformal onto its image.

\]
The second estimate follows by changing variables on the support $B(0,\varepsilon)$, where the weight is uniformly comparable to one. The ratio tends to infinity as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

\label{lem:conformal-pole-size}

growth. At interior points, evaluation is right-continuous in the
distortion bound: for fixed $p>0$, $\alpha>-1$, and $K_0\ge1$,

integer $m$, the tail has distortion at most $K_0+1/m$. By the limit theorem,
the same bound holds for $f$. Letting $m\to\infty$, we see that $f$
has distortion at most $K_0$. We may now pass to the limit at $a$ to obtain
\[
 \limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathcal E_{K_j,p,\alpha}(a)
 \le\mathcal E_{K_0,p,\alpha}(a).

The reverse inequality follows from

local H\"older modulus, as do their evaluation suprema.
A finite covering argument then shows that the convergence is uniform on each compact set.

compact subset as $K\downarrow1$. The boundary orders in
\eqref{eq:conformalevaluation} and Corollary~\ref{cor:criticaldistortion}
show that the convergence is not uniform on $B$.

Let $K\ge1$, $0<p\le q<\infty$, $\alpha>-1$, and let $\mu$ be a positive locally finite Borel measure on $B$. For a fixed $r\in(0,1)$, set
\[
 \Gamma_r(a)=\frac{\mu(\Delta_r(a))}{\delta(a)^{Qq/p}}.
\]
The condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:carleson}
 \sup_{a\in B}\Gamma_r(a)<\infty
\end{equation}
implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embedding}
 \int_B|f|^q\,d\mu\le C\|f\|_{p,\alpha}^q
 \qquad(f\in\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}),
\end{equation}
and the inclusion is continuous in the ambient metrics. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vanishing}
 \Gamma_r(a)\longrightarrow0\qquad(|a|\to1),
\end{equation}
the inclusion is compact. Both converses hold if $K\geK_*(n)$ or if \eqref{eq:radialrange} holds. The two geometric conditions are independent of the fixed radius $r$.
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
First observe that local finiteness bounds $\Gamma_r$ on compact subsets: if $\delta(a)\ge t>0$, the balls $\Delta_r(a)$ lie in a common compact subset of $B$, and the denominator is bounded below. Thus \eqref{eq:vanishing} implies \eqref{eq:carleson}. To compare two fixed radii $r_1<r_2$, cover $\overline{B(0,r_2)}$ by finitely many hyperbolic balls of radius $r_1$ and apply $\phi_a$. Their new centers have boundary distances comparable to $\delta(a)$. This proves radius independence of boundedness and vanishing; the opposite implications follow by inclusion.

Choose a covering $\{\Delta_r(a_j)\}$ for which the enlargements $\Delta_R(a_j)$ have bounded overlap, where $r<R<1$, and put
\[
 A_j(f)=\int_{\Delta_R(a_j)}|f|^p\,dV_\alpha.
\]
Such a covering exists by Lemma~\ref{lem:hyperbolic}. For any set $J$ of indices, it follows from \eqref{eq:hyperbolicsup} and $q/p\ge1$ that
\begin{align*}
 \int_{\bigcup_{j\in J}\Delta_r(a_j)}|f|^q\,d\mu
 &\le C\sum_{j\in J}\Gamma_r(a_j)A_j(f)^{q/p}\\
 &\le C\left(\sup_{j\in J}\Gamma_r(a_j)\right)
       \left(\int_{\bigcup_{j\in J}\Delta_R(a_j)}
                          |f|^p\,dV_\alpha\right)^{q/p}.
\end{align*}
Bounded overlap is used in the second line; infinite sums follow by monotone convergence. Taking all indices, we obtain \eqref{eq:embedding}. By testing a nonzero constant, we also find that $\mu(B)<\infty$. If $\Delta_r(a_j)$ meets $\{\delta<s\}$, then $\delta(a_j)<C_0s$ and $\Delta_R(a_j)\subset\{\delta<C_0s\}$ for a fixed $C_0$. We may therefore apply the same estimate to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localizedembedding}
 \int_{\{\delta<s\}}|f|^q\,d\mu
 \le C\left(\sup_{\delta(a)<C_0s}\Gamma_r(a)\right)
       \left(\int_{\{\delta<C_0s\}}|f|^p\,dV_\alpha\right)^{q/p}.
\end{equation}

If $f_j\to f$ in the metric $d_p$, then $f_j\to f$ locally uniformly by Proposition~\ref{thm:complete}. Their $L^p_\alpha$ boundary tails are uniformly small: apply \eqref{eq:elementarypower} to $f_j=(f_j-f)+f$ for large $j$, and then handle the finitely many other terms. By formula~\eqref{eq:localizedembedding}, their $L^q(\mu)$ tails are uniformly small as well. On each compact subset, it follows from uniform convergence and local finiteness of $\mu$ that the sequence converges in $L^q(\mu)$. Applying \eqref{eq:elementarypower} to the difference on the remaining collar completes the proof of continuity.

Under \eqref{eq:vanishing}, we infer from formula~\eqref{eq:localizedembedding} that the $L^q(\mu)$ tails are uniformly small on every bounded Bergman family. Any bounded sequence has a locally uniformly convergent subsequence, whose limit lies in $\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$ by Fatou's lemma. Together with the uniform tail estimate, compact convergence implies convergence in $L^q(\mu)$, as required.

For the converses, take the normalized mappings $h_a$ from Section~\ref{sec:concentration}; these mappings are also available by Proposition~\ref{prop:radialrange} under \eqref{eq:radialrange}. Their norms equal one. By \eqref{eq:peakbounds}, we have
\[
 \int_B|h_a|^q\,d\mu
 \ge c_r\delta(a)^{-Qq/p}\mu(\Delta_r(a)).
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:embedding} implies \eqref{eq:carleson}. A compact inclusion has a bounded image of the unit ball, so \eqref{eq:embedding} also follows by positive scaling. If \eqref{eq:vanishing} failed, there would be $a_j\to\partial B$ with $\Gamma_r(a_j)\ge\varepsilon>0$. A subsequence of $h_{a_j}$ would converge in $L^q(\mu)$, and a further subsequence would converge $\mu$-almost everywhere. But \eqref{eq:peaklocalzero} gives pointwise convergence to zero throughout $B$. The $L^q(\mu)$ limit would therefore be zero, contrary to the displayed lower bound. This proves necessity of \eqref{eq:vanishing}.

<!-- proposition-11: body_md -->
\label{thm:compactcriterion}
A bounded family $\mathcal F\subset\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$ is relatively compact in $d_p$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniformtails}
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}
       \int_{\{\delta<t\}}|f|^p\,dV_\alpha=0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-11 -->
<!-- proof-29: body_md -->
Under \eqref{eq:uniformtails}, every sequence in $\mathcal F$ has a subsequence $(f_j)$ converging locally uniformly to a $K$-quasiregular mapping $f$. It follows from Fatou's lemma that $f$ belongs to the Bergman class, and the same tail bound holds for $f$. On $\{\delta\ge t\}$ the integral of $|f_j-f|^p$ tends to zero by uniform convergence. On $\{\delta<t\}$ it is bounded, by \eqref{eq:elementarypower}, by $c_p$ times the two tails. First choose $t$ small and then let $j\to\infty$. The subsequence converges in $d_p$.

Conversely, by relative compactness, for each $\varepsilon>0$ we can choose finitely many mappings $g_1,\ldots,g_N$ such that every $f\in\mathcal F$ satisfies $\|f-g_i\|_{p,\alpha}^p<\varepsilon$ for some $i$. Choose $t$ so small that all the tails of the $g_i$ are smaller than $\varepsilon$. Then
\[
 \int_{\{\delta<t\}}|f|^p\,dV_\alpha
 \le c_p\left(\|f-g_i\|_{p,\alpha}^p+
              \int_{\{\delta<t\}}|g_i|^p\,dV_\alpha\right)
 <2c_p\varepsilon,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:uniformtails}.

<!-- proposition-13: body_md -->
\label{thm:dilation}
If $f\in\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$ and $f_r(x)=f(rx)$, then
\[
 \|f_r-f\|_{p,\alpha}\longrightarrow0\qquad(r\uparrow1).
\]
Hence bounded $K$-quasiregular mappings extending to a neighborhood of $\overline B$ are dense in $\mathcal A^p_{K,\alpha}$.
<!-- end proposition-13 -->
<!-- proof-32: body_md -->
Put $\tau=1-r<1/16$. If $\delta(x)\ge2\tau$, then $|rx-x|\le\delta(x)/2$, so $|f_r(x)|\le\mathcal M_{1/2}f(x)$. By the maximal estimate and \eqref{eq:elementarypower}, there is an integrable majorant for
\[
 \boldsymbol 1_{\{\delta\ge2\tau\}}|f_r-f|^p.
\]
The integral over this region therefore tends to zero by dominated convergence.

On the remaining collar, $\tau\le\delta(rx)<3\tau$. Using the local estimate on $B(rx,\tau/4)$, where the weight is comparable to $\tau^\alpha$, we obtain
\[
 |f(rx)|^p\le C\tau^{-Q}
        \int_{B(rx,\tau/4)}|f(y)|^p\,dV_\alpha(y).
\]
For fixed $y$, the set of $x$ with $\delta(x)<2\tau$ and $|rx-y|<\tau/4$ has diameter at most $\tau/(2r)<\tau$. When nonempty, it lies in a cap of radius $C\tau$ centered at a nearest boundary point to one of its members. By Lemma~\ref{lem:cap}, its weighted volume is at most $C\tau^Q$. Moreover, each such $y$ satisfies $\delta(y)<4\tau$. Applying Tonelli's theorem, we now obtain
\[
 \int_{\{\delta<2\tau\}}|f_r|^p\,dV_\alpha
 \le C\int_{\{\delta<4\tau\}}|f|^p\,dV_\alpha\longrightarrow0.
\]
The corresponding tail of $f$ also tends to zero. By \eqref{eq:elementarypower}, convergence therefore holds on the collar as well. Finally, $f_r$ has the same distortion bound, is defined on $B(0,1/r)$, and is bounded on $\overline B$.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:zorich}
For $n\ge3$, there are a locally Lipschitz quasiregular mapping
\[
 Z:\mathbb R^{n-1}\times\mathbb R\longrightarrow\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}
\]
and a quasiconformal homeomorphism
\[
 \Lambda:H_+\longrightarrow(-1,1)^{n-1}\times\mathbb R,
 \qquad H_+=\{v\in\mathbb R^n:v_n>0\},
\]
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zorichmodulus}
 |Z(u,s)|=e^s,\qquad Z\circ\Lambda=\mathrm{id}_{H_+},
 \qquad \Lambda_n(v)=\log|v|.
\end{equation}
Their outer distortions are at most $L_n^{2(n-1)}$, where
\[
 L_n=\max\{3\pi/2,\,n-1+2\sqrt{n-1}\}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-33: body_md -->
Put $d=n-1$ and $C=[-1,1]^d$. We construct a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism $h$ from $C$ onto the closed upper hemisphere of the unit sphere. The radial map
\[
 P(u)=\frac{\|u\|_\infty}{|u|}u\quad(u\ne0),\qquad P(0)=0,
\]
maps $C$ onto the closed unit ball of $\mathbb R^d$. Its inverse is
\[
 P^{-1}(v)=\frac{|v|}{\|v\|_\infty}v\quad(v\ne0).
\]
Both are Lipschitz. For example, using the triangle inequality and
$\bigl|\|u\|_\infty-\|v\|_\infty\bigr|\le|u-v|$, we obtain a Lipschitz constant $3$ for $P$. The same calculation applied to the inverse, with $\|u\|_\infty\ge|u|/\sqrt d$, leads to the bound $d+2\sqrt d$ for $P^{-1}$.

Map the unit ball of $\mathbb R^d$ onto the upper hemisphere by
\[
 H(v)=\left(\frac{\sin(\pi|v|/2)}{|v|}v,\ \cos(\pi|v|/2)\right),
\]
with its continuous value at zero. To check the bi-Lipschitz property, write $v=r\theta$ and $w=s\zeta$. Using the identity
\begin{align*}
 |H(v)-H(w)|^2
 &=4\sin^2\frac{\pi(r-s)}4
   +\sin\frac{\pi r}2\sin\frac{\pi s}2|\theta-\zeta|^2
\end{align*}
together with the inequalities $r\le\sin(\pi r/2)\le\pi r/2$ for $0\le r\le1$, we obtain
\[
 |v-w|\le|H(v)-H(w)|\le\frac\pi2|v-w|.
\]
The cases $r=0$ or $s=0$ follow by continuity. Thus $h=H\circ P$ is bi-Lipschitz. Its boundary is mapped to the equator. We may therefore take
\[
 L_n=\max\{3\pi/2,\,n-1+2\sqrt{n-1}\}
\]
as a common bi-Lipschitz constant for $h$ and $h^{-1}$. We choose the orientation of $h$ so that the map below is orientation preserving; precomposition with one coordinate reflection, if needed, does not change $L_n$.

On the beam $C\times\mathbb R$, define
\[
 Z(u,s)=e^sh(u).
\]
On its interior this is a homeomorphism onto $H_+$. At almost every $u$, the vectors in the range of $Dh(u)$ are orthogonal to $h(u)$, because $|h(u)|=1$. Consequently,
\[
 |DZ(u,s)(v,\tau)|^2
 =e^{2s}\bigl(|Dh(u)v|^2+\tau^2\bigr).
\]
Every singular value of $DZ$ lies between $L_n^{-1}e^s$ and $L_ne^s$. In particular, its outer distortion and the outer distortion of its inverse are at most $L_n^{2(n-1)}$.

We extend $Z$ to all beams by reflection. For $m=(m_1,\ldots,m_d)\in\mathbb Z^d$, let
\[
 C_m=2m+C,\qquad
 q_m(u)_j=(-1)^{m_j}(u_j-2m_j),\qquad
 S=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,-1).
\]
For $u\in C_m$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reflectionextension}
 Z(u,s)=e^sS^{m_1+\cdots+m_d}h(q_m(u)).
\end{equation}
The definitions agree on common faces. Indeed, the folded coordinates agree there, their images under $h$ lie on the equator, and $S$ fixes the equator pointwise. The formula therefore defines a continuous mapping. On each compact subset only finitely many beams occur. Their Lipschitz restrictions can therefore be glued, so the extension is locally Lipschitz across the faces as well.

Each domain reflection changes orientation once, and the accompanying target reflection changes it once more. Thus the Jacobian in \eqref{eq:reflectionextension} is positive almost everywhere. Orthogonal reflections preserve singular values, so the same distortion bound holds on every beam. The union of the beam faces has volume zero. Hence the extension is quasiregular and still satisfies $|Z(u,s)|=e^s$.

Finally, on $H_+$ the inverse of the original branch is
\[
 \Lambda(v)=\left(h^{-1}\left(\frac v{|v|}\right),\log|v|\right).
\]
It is locally bi-Lipschitz and quasiconformal, with outer distortion at most $L_n^{2(n-1)}$. Formula \eqref{eq:zorichmodulus} follows immediately.
