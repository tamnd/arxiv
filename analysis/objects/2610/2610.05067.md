<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:1}
    Let $H_n=\frac 11 +\frac 12+\cdots +\frac 1n$ be the $n$-th harmonic
    number and let $H^m_n=H_m-H_n$.
    Let \[\Lambda_n(a)=\sup\{i\cdot  M_i(a), n/2< i\leq n\}\] and
    \[\lambda_n(a)=\inf\{i\cdot m_i(a), n/2< i\leq n\}.\]
    Then $\Lambda_{2n\!-\!1}(a)\geq 1/H^{2n\!-\!1}_{n-1}$ and $\lambda_{2n}(a)\leq 1/2H^{2n}_{n}$. 
    The inequalities~(\ref{eq:1}) and~(\ref{eq:2}) follow from standard properties of
    the harmonic numbers. The sequence $H^{2n\!-\!1}_{n-1}$ is descending and $H^{2n}_{n}$ is ascending, with limit ${\log 2}$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\ell_1\leq \ell_2\leq \cdots\leq\ell_n$ be the lengths of the sticks after
    breaking $n$ times, sorted in ascending order. They add up to one, and $M_n(a)$ is equal
    to $\ell_n$. 
    The maximal stick length only decreases if we break the longest stick.
    It follows that 
    \begin{equation}\label{ineq:M_n}
        M_{n+k}(a)\geq \ell_{n-k},
    \end{equation} 
    for $k=0,\ldots,n-1$.
    Since the $\ell_i$ add up
    to one, we have 
    \begin{equation}\label{cut}
    M_n(a)+M_{n+1}(a)+\cdots+M_{2n\!-\!1}(a)\geq 1.
    \end{equation}
    By definition 
    \begin{equation}\label{ineq:Lambda}\Lambda_{2n\!-\!1}(a)\geq (n+k)\cdot M_{n+k}(a)\end{equation}
    for all $k=0,\ldots,n-1$ which implies
    that
    \begin{equation}\label{Lambdabound}
    \Lambda_{2n\!-\!1}(a)\cdot H^{2n\!-\!1}_{n-1}=\sum_{k={0}}^{n-1}\frac {\Lambda_{2n\!-\!1}(a)}{n+k}\geq 1.
    \end{equation}
    This gives the required lower bound on $\Lambda_{2n\!-\!1}(a)$.
    
    To bound $\lambda_{2n}(a)$ we work in the opposite direction, gluing broken sticks
    back together again.
    Let $\ell_1\leq \ell_2\leq \cdots\leq\ell_{2n}$ be the lengths of the sticks after
    breaking $2n$ times, sorted in ascending order. In particular, $m_{2n}(a)$
    is equal to $\ell_1$. We now work our way back, each step gluing two
    sticks. We have that 
    \begin{equation}\label{ineq:m_n}
    m_{2n-k}\leq \ell_{2k+1}        
    \end{equation}
    for $k=0,\ldots,n\!-\!1$
    because at least one of the $2k+1$
    shortest sticks remains unglued. We have
    \begin{equation}\label{glue}
    m_{2n}(a)+m_{2n\!-\!1}(a)+\cdots+m_{n+1}(a)\leq \ell_1+\ell_3+\cdots+\ell_{2n\!-\!1}\leq \frac 12.      
    \end{equation}
    By definition $\lambda_{2n}(a)/k\leq m_k(a)$ for all $n<k\leq 2n$ which implies
    that
    \begin{equation}\label{lambdabound2}
    \lambda_{2n}(a)\cdot H^{2n}_{n}\leq \frac 12.
    \end{equation}
        This gives the required upper bound on $\lambda_{2n}(a)$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A stick-breaking procedure is \emph{greedy} if, at every step,
it breaks a longest stick into two pieces. Each new piece must 
be at most as long as every
remaining stick, but at least as long as half the length of the
longest remaining stick.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A sequence \( a_1, \ldots, a_{2n\!-\!1} \) of odd length is called a \emph{\( (2n\!-\!1) \)-BET sequence} if it satisfies
\[
\Lambda_{2n\!-\!1}(a) = \frac{1}{H_{n-1}^{2n\!-\!1}}\;\text{ and }\;\rho_{k}(a)<2\text{ for all }k\leq 2n\!-\!1.
\]
Similarly, a sequence \( a_1, \ldots, a_{2n} \) of even length is called a \emph{\( 2n \)-BET sequence} if it satisfies
\[
\lambda_{2n}(a) = \frac{1}{2 H_{n}^{2n}}\;\text{ and }\;\rho_{k}(a)<2\text{ for all }k\leq 2n.
\]
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Every $(2n\!-\!1)$-BET sequence has the same stick lengths
after $n$ breaks, namely the lengths in~\eqref{nsticks}.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Suppose that $a_1,\ldots,a_{2n\!-\!1}$ is a $(2n\!-\!1)$-BET sequence.
Then inequalities~\eqref{ineq:M_n} and~\eqref{ineq:Lambda}
in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:1} must both be equalities.
Using the notation of that proof, Writing $\Lambda=\Lambda_{2n\!-\!1}$, we obtain
\[
\ell_{n-k}=M_{n+k}(a)
=\frac{\Lambda}{n+k},
\qquad k=0,\ldots,n-1.
\]
Thus, after the first $n$ breaks, the stick lengths are precisely
those in~\eqref{nsticks}.

To complete the construction, we show that these lengths can
indeed be attained while respecting
$kM_k(a)\leq\Lambda$ at every earlier step.
Starting with the $n$ sticks in~\eqref{nsticks}, we work backwards,
repeatedly gluing the two shortest sticks until only the original
stick remains.
The ratio between the longest and shortest sticks remains
strictly less than $2$. Indeed, the glued stick is longer than
every remaining stick, but shorter than twice the shortest
remaining stick.

 Suppose that the current $k$
stick lengths, in decreasing order, are bounded term by term by
\[
\frac{\Lambda}{k},\;
\frac{\Lambda}{k+1},\;
\ldots,\;
\frac{\Lambda}{2k-1}.
\]
The sum of the two shortest lengths is at most
\[
\frac{\Lambda}{2k-2}+\frac{\Lambda}{2k-1}
<\frac{\Lambda}{k-1}.
\]
Since the glued stick becomes the longest, the resulting
$k-1$ lengths are bounded term by term by
\[
\frac{\Lambda}{k-1},\;
\frac{\Lambda}{k},\;
\ldots,\;
\frac{\Lambda}{2k-3}.
\]
Induction therefore gives $kM_k(a)\leq\Lambda$ for
$k=1,\ldots,n$. Reversing the gluing procedure gives the
required sequence of breaks.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
There is only one way to produce a $2n$-BET sequence and it is greedy.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Suppose that $a_1,\ldots,a_{2n}$ is a $2n$-BET sequence.
    This implies that inequality~(\ref{glue}) in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:1}
    is an equality:
    \[\ell_1+\ell_3+\cdots+\ell_{2n\!-\!1}=\frac 12.\]
    This implies that 
    \[
    \ell_2+\ell_4+\cdots+\ell_{2n}=\frac 12
    \]
    as well.
    In particular, $\ell_{2j-1}=\ell_{2j}$ and $m_{2n-k}=\ell_{2k+1}$
    for $k=0,\ldots,n-1$. The inequality in (\ref{lambdabound2}) must
    be an equality and \[j\cdot m_{j}=\frac{1}{2H_n^{2n}}\] for $j=n+1,\ldots,2n$.
    It follows that the $2n$-tuple of stick-lengths after
    $2n$ breaks must be as in (\ref{2nsticks}). If we work
    back from $2n$ to $n$ breaks by gluing sticks, this can only be done 
    by gluing the two smallest stick to maintain the ratio~$<2$.

   We show that the only way to work back to the first break
is by greedy stick gluing, proving uniqueness of the
$2n$-BET sequence.
Write $\lambda=\lambda_{2n}$ and let
$(\ell_1^m,\ldots,\ell_m^m)$ be the stick lengths after
$m$ breaks, in decreasing order.
Each gluing step must preserve the bound
$m\ell_m^m\geq\lambda$.
We have already proved that
\[
(\ell_1^n,\ldots,\ell_n^n)
=
\left(
\frac{2\lambda}{n+1},
\frac{2\lambda}{n+2},
\ldots,
\frac{2\lambda}{2n}
\right).
\]
    We argue by induction backwards from $n$ to $1$ that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds}
\frac{2\lambda}{m+j-1}>\ell_j^m
\geq\frac{2\lambda}{m+j},
\qquad j=1,\ldots,m.
\end{equation}
These bounds hold at $m=n$. Suppose that they hold for some
$m\geq2$. To return to $m-1$ breaks, we must glue two sticks.
Both of the two shortest sticks have length strictly less than
$\lambda/(m-1)$, since
\[
\ell_m^m\leq\ell_{m-1}^m
<\frac{2\lambda}{2m-2}
=\frac{\lambda}{m-1}.
\]
Leaving either one unglued would therefore violate
$(m-1)\ell_{m-1}^{m-1}\geq\lambda$.
Thus, we must glue the two shortest sticks.
Their combined length satisfies
\[
\frac{2\lambda}{m-1}
>
\ell_{m-1}^m+\ell_m^m
>
\frac{2\lambda}{m}
>
\ell_1^m.
\]
Here the first two inequalities follow from
\[
\frac{2\lambda}{2m-2}+\frac{2\lambda}{2m-1}
>
\ell_{m-1}^m+\ell_m^m
\geq
\frac{2\lambda}{2m-1}+\frac{2\lambda}{2m}.
\]
Hence, the glued stick becomes the longest, and
\[
\ell_1^{m-1}=\ell_{m-1}^m+\ell_m^m,
\qquad
\ell_j^{m-1}=\ell_{j-1}^m
\quad (j=2,\ldots,m-1).
\]
The bounds~\eqref{eq:bounds} follow directly for $j>1$,
and the displayed inequalities establish them for $j=1$.
This completes the induction.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
Suppose that a sequence $(a)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all3}
\rho_n(a)<2,\qquad
nM_n(a)<\frac{1}{\log 2},\qquad
nm_n(a)>\frac{1}{2\log 2}
\end{equation}
for all $n$.
Let $\ell_n$ be the length of the longest stick after $n$ breaks.
Then
\[
\frac{1}{n\log 2}>\ell_n>
\frac{1}{(n+1)\log 2}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
At each step, the longest stick must be broken.
Indeed, if a stick of length $L$ is broken while a stick of
length $M\geq L$ remains, one of the new pieces has length
at most $L/2$. The resulting ratio is therefore at least
$2M/L\geq2$, contrary to~\eqref{eq:all3}.

Since $\ell_n=M_n(a)$, the upper bound follows directly
from~\eqref{eq:all3}.
The next break splits this longest stick into two pieces,
each of length at least $m_{n+1}(a)$. Hence
\[
\ell_n\geq2m_{n+1}(a)>
\frac{1}{(n+1)\log 2},
\]
which proves the lower bound.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
The BET sequence is the unique eventually greedy stick-breaking sequence that satisfies the bounds
    in~(\ref{ineq:all3}).
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Since the sequence is eventually greedy, there exists $N$
such that stick $n$ has children $2n$ and $2n+1$ whenever
$n\geq N$. Its descendants after $k$ generations are therefore
the sticks indexed by $2^kn,\ldots,2^k(n+1)-1$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{grandchild}
\ell_n=\sum_{j=0}^{2^k-1}\ell_{2^kn+j}.
\end{equation}
By the previous lemma,
\[
\frac{1}{\log 2}
\sum_{j=0}^{2^k-1}\frac{1}{2^kn+j}
>
\ell_n
>
\frac{1}{\log 2}
\sum_{j=1}^{2^k}\frac{1}{2^kn+j}.
\]
As $k\to\infty$, both sums converge to
\[
\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_n^{n+1}\frac{dx}{x}
=\log_2\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right).
\]
Thus, for every $n\geq N$,
\[
\ell_n=\log_2\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right).
\]
In particular, after $n$ breaks the stick lengths are
\[
\log_2\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right),\;
\ldots,\;
\log_2\left(\frac{2n}{2n-1}\right).
\]

We now reconstruct the earlier stages by gluing sticks.
Since $\log(1+x)<x$ for $x>0$, the second shortest stick
satisfies
\[
(n-1)\log_2\left(\frac{2n-1}{2n-2}\right)
<\frac{1}{2\log 2}.
\]
Thus, leaving either of the two shortest sticks unglued
would violate the lower bound on $(n-1)m_{n-1}(a)$.
Both must therefore be glued. Their combined length is
\[
\log_2\left(\frac{2n-1}{2n-2}\right)
+\log_2\left(\frac{2n}{2n-1}\right)
=\log_2\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right).
\]
The resulting lengths are precisely
\[
\log_2\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right),\;
\log_2\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right),\;
\ldots,\;
\log_2\left(\frac{2n-2}{2n-3}\right),
\]
the BET lengths at the preceding stage.
Repeating this argument determines every earlier stage
uniquely.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
Suppose that a unit square is successively partitioned into
rectangular tiles by dividing one tile into four at each step.
Let $M_N$ denote the largest tile area when there are $N$ tiles.
Then
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty} NM_N
\geq \frac{3}{\log4}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Fix a stage with $N$ tiles, which we call the original tiles,
and consider the next $N$ cuts, indexed by $j=0,\ldots,N-1$.
Just before cut $j$, there are $N+3j$ tiles. 

We match the next $N$ cuts to the $N$ tiles. 
Match each original tile that is cut during this period to
the cut that first divides it. Match the original tiles that
remain uncut to the remaining cuts, in any order.
This gives a one-to-one matching between the $N$ original
tiles and the $N$ cuts.

Let $T_j$ be the original tile matched to cut $j$.
This tile is still intact just before that cut: either the
cut divides it for the first time, or it remains uncut
throughout the period. Hence
\[
\operatorname{area}(T_j)\leq M_{N+3j}.
\]
Since the original tiles have total area one, we obtain
\[
1=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\operatorname{area}(T_j)
\leq\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}M_{N+3j}.
\]
Writing
\[
C_N=\max_{0\leq j<N}(N+3j)M_{N+3j},
\]
we therefore have
\[
1\leq C_N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N+3j}.
\]
Since $t\mapsto(1+3t)^{-1}$ is decreasing, the sum is
an upper Riemann sum, and
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N+3j}
=
\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{1+3j/N}
\longrightarrow
\int_0^1\frac{dt}{1+3t}
=\frac{\log4}{3}.
\]
Thus $\liminf_{N\to\infty}C_N\geq3/\log4$,
which implies the claimed bound.
