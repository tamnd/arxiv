<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:unique-oss}
Let $0<\theta<\pi$, and equip $E_\theta$ with the operator space
structure inherited from $C(K_\theta)$. Every contractive linear map
$u:E_\theta\to\mathcal B(H)$ is completely contractive, for every
complex Hilbert space $H$. Consequently, $E_\theta$ has a unique
operator space structure.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- defn-1: body_md -->
\label{def:abstract-operator-space}
An abstract operator space is a normed complex linear space $X$
together with norms $\|\cdot\|_n$ on $M_n(X)$ for all $n\ge1$,
satisfying the following conditions:
\begin{itemize}
\item The norm $\|\cdot\|_1$ on $M_1(X)=X$ agrees with the given
norm of $X$.
\item For all $A\in M_n(X)$ and $B\in M_m(X)$,
\[
\|A\oplus B\|_{n+m}=\max\{\|A\|_n,\|B\|_m\},
\]
where $A\oplus B$ is the block diagonal array with diagonal
blocks $A$ and $B$.
\item For all $A\in M_{n,m}$, $B\in M_{m,n}$ and $C\in M_m(X)$,
\[
\|ACB\|_n
\le
\|A\|_{\ell_2^m\to\ell_2^n}
\|C\|_m
\|B\|_{\ell_2^n\to\ell_2^m}.
\]
\end{itemize}
<!-- end defn-1 -->
<!-- defn-2: body_md -->
\label{def:concrete-operator-space}
A concrete operator space is a triple $(X,i,H)$, where
$X$ is a normed complex linear space, $H$ is a complex
Hilbert space, and $i:X\to\mathcal B(H)$ is a linear isometry.
<!-- end defn-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:ruan}
Let $X$ be an abstract operator space with matrix norms
$\{\|\cdot\|_n:n\ge1\}$. There are a complex Hilbert space
$H$ and a linear isometry $i:X\to\mathcal B(H)$
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ruan-representation}
\|(x_{ij})\|_n
=\bigl\|(\mathrm{id}_{M_n}\otimes i)((x_{ij}))\bigr\|
_{H^n\to H^n}
\end{equation}
for all $n\ge1$ and all $(x_{ij})\in M_n(X)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:ruan}, we may identify an abstract operator
space with a concrete operator space. We shall use the term
operator space for either realization.
<!-- end rem-1 -->
<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:operator Riesz type}
Let $H$ be a real Hilbert space and let
$F\in\mathbb R[x]\otimes\mathcal{B}(H)_{\mathrm{h}}$. Then
$F(t)\geq 0$ for all $t\ge 0$ if and only if
\[
  F(t)=R_0+t\,R_1,
\]
where $R_0,R_1$ are sums of Hermitian squares of polynomials
from $\mathbb R[x]\otimes\mathcal{B}(H)$, that is for some $(U_i)_{i=1}^n,(V_j)_{j=1}^m\subseteq \mathbb{R}[x]\otimes\mathcal{B}(H),$ $R_0=\sum_{i=1}^nU_i^*U_i$ and $R_1=\sum_{j=1}^mV_j^*V_j.$
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:positive-block}
Let $H_1,H_2$ be real Hilbert spaces or complex Hilbert spaces. Let $P\in\mathcal B(H_1)$ and $Q\in\mathcal B(H_2)$ be positive,
and let $X\in\mathcal B(H_2,H_1)$. Then
\[
\begin{pmatrix}P&X\\X^*&Q\end{pmatrix}\ge0
\]
if and only if there is a contraction $K\in\mathcal B(H_2,H_1)$ such that
\[
X=P^{1/2}KQ^{1/2}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:completion}
Let $H$ be a real or complex Hilbert space, let $P,Q\in\mathcal B(H)$ be positive,
and let $S=S^*\in\mathcal B(H)$. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pencil}
P+tS+t^2Q\ge0\qquad\text{for all}\ t\ge0.
\end{equation}
\item[(ii)] There is a contraction $K\in\mathcal B(H)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coupling}
P^{1/2}KQ^{1/2}+Q^{1/2}K^*P^{1/2}\le S.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently, there is an operator $X\in\mathcal B(H)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
\begin{pmatrix}P&X\\X^*&Q\end{pmatrix}\ge0,
\qquad X+X^*\le S.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We first solve the real case.




By Proposition \ref{prop:operator Riesz type} there are finitely many operator polynomials
\(U_i,V_j\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratidecomp}
P+tS+t^2Q
=\sum_i U_i(t)^*U_i(t)+t\sum_jV_j(t)^*V_j(t), \ \text{for all}\ t\geq 0.
\end{equation}
Note that $\sum U_i^*U_i$ has even degree and $t\mapsto t\sum V_j(t)^*V_j(t)$ has odd degree. It follows that
\[
U_i(t)=A_i+tB_i,\qquad V_j(t)=C_j.
\]
for some $A_i,B_i,C_j\in \mathcal{B}(H).$
Comparing coefficients in \eqref{eq:quadratidecomp} yields
\[
P=\sum_i A_i^*A_i,\qquad Q=\sum_i B_i^*B_i,\qquad\text{and}\ S=X+X^*+Z,
\]
where $X=\sum_iA_i^*B_i$ and $Z=\sum_jC_j^*C_j\ge0.$
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-gram}
\begin{pmatrix}P&X\\X^*&Q\end{pmatrix}
=\sum_i
\begin{pmatrix}A_i^*\\B_i^*\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}A_i&B_i\end{pmatrix}\ge0,
\qquad X+X^*\le S.
\end{equation}




By Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-block} we obtain \(X=P^{1/2}K_0Q^{1/2}\) for a real-linear contraction \(K_0\). This shows $(i)$ implies $(ii).$




\medskip
We now deal with the complex case.
Now let \(H\) be complex, and regard it as the real Hilbert space
\(H_{\mathbb R}\) with inner product
\(\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathbb R}=\operatorname{Re}\langle x,y\rangle\) as in Appendix~\ref{app:realification}. Identifying $P,Q$ and $S$ as bounded linear operators on $H_{\mathbb R},$ they are again positive and self-adjoint respectively. Moreover, the operators $P,Q$ and $S$ therefore satisfy the same assumptions on
\(H_{\mathbb R}\), with the same square roots \(P^{1/2},Q^{1/2}\).
These identifications follow from Proposition~\ref{prop:realification} and the square-root identity immediately following it.
The real result supplies a real-linear
contraction \(K_0\) with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-coupling}
P^{1/2}K_0Q^{1/2}+Q^{1/2}K_0^*P^{1/2}\le S.
\end{equation}
In above $K_0^*$ is adjoint as an operator on the real-Hilbert space \(H_{\mathbb R}\).
 Set
\[
K=\frac12(K_0+J^*K_0J)
=\frac12(K_0-JK_0J).
\] where $J$ is as in Appendix~\ref{app:realification}. By Proposition~\ref{prop:realification}, \(K\) is complex-linear and a contraction. Since \(P,Q,S,P^{1/2},Q^{1/2}\)
commute with \(J\), conjugating \eqref{eq:real-coupling} by \(J\),
adding the resulting inequality to \eqref{eq:real-coupling}, and dividing by two gives
\[
P^{1/2}KQ^{1/2}+Q^{1/2}K^*P^{1/2}\le S.
\]
Since \(K\) is now complex-linear, this is precisely \eqref{eq:coupling}
on \(H\). This proves $(i)$ implies $(ii)$ in the complex case. Now we verify the equivalence of \eqref{eq:coupling} and \eqref{eq:block}, using Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-block}.
For any contraction \(K\), putting \(X=P^{1/2}KQ^{1/2}\) gives
\[
\begin{pmatrix}P&X\\X^*&Q\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}P^{1/2}&0\\0&Q^{1/2}\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}I&K\\K^*&I\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}P^{1/2}&0\\0&Q^{1/2}\end{pmatrix}\ge0.
\]
If \eqref{eq:coupling} holds, then \(X+X^*\le S\).
Conversely, if \eqref{eq:block} holds, Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-block} gives a contraction $K$ such that $X=P^{1/2}KQ^{1/2}$, and $X+X^*\le S$ is precisely \eqref{eq:coupling}. Thus \eqref{eq:coupling} and \eqref{eq:block} are equivalent.




To prove $(ii)\Rightarrow(i)$, we can therefore use \eqref{eq:block}.
Testing the positive block on \((h,th)\) and adding
\(t\langle(S-X-X^*)h,h\rangle\ge0\) proves \eqref{eq:pencil}
for every \(t\ge0\).

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:gram}
Suppose $A,B\in\mathcal B(H)$ and $c\in[-1,1]$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:test}
\left\lVertA-tB\right\rVert^2\le 1+t^2-2ct\qquad\text{for all }t\ge0.
\end{equation}
There is a contraction $T\in\mathcal B(H)$ with $\operatorname{Re} T\ge cI$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
\begin{pmatrix}I&T\\T^*&I\end{pmatrix}
\ge
\begin{pmatrix}A^*A&A^*B\\B^*A&B^*B\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Taking $t=0$ and letting $t\to\infty$ in \eqref{eq:test} shows that $A,B$
are contractions. Set
\[
D_A=(I-A^*A)^{1/2},\qquad D_B=(I-B^*B)^{1/2}.
\]
Using $(A-tB)^*(A-tB)\leq \|A-tB\|^2$ we get from \eqref{eq:test}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arc-pencil}
D_A^2+t(A^*B+B^*A-2cI)+t^2D_B^2\ge0\qquad\text{for all }t\ge0.
\end{equation}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:completion} with
$P=D_A^2$, $Q=D_B^2$, and $S=A^*B+B^*A-2cI$. It gives a contraction $K$
satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arc-coupling}
D_AKD_B+D_BK^*D_A\le A^*B+B^*A-2cI.
\end{equation}
Put $T=A^*B-D_AKD_B$. Then $\text{Re}T=\frac{T+T^*}{2}\ge cI$, and
\begin{align*}
&\begin{pmatrix}I&T\\T^*&I\end{pmatrix}
-\begin{pmatrix}A^*A&A^*B\\B^*A&B^*B\end{pmatrix}\\
&\qquad=
\begin{pmatrix}D_A&0\\0&D_B\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}I&-K\\-K^*&I\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}D_A&0\\0&D_B\end{pmatrix}\ge 0,
\end{align*}
as $\begin{psmallmatrix}I&-K\\-K^*&I\end{psmallmatrix}\ge0$ since $K$ is a contraction. Moreover, the block operator on the right of \eqref{eq:gram} is positive implying $T$ is a contraction.

\label{thm:arc}
Let $0<\theta<\pi$, let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space, and let
$u:E_\theta\to\mathcal B(H)$ be linear. Write $A=u(1)$ and $B=u(z)$.
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $u$ is contractive.
\item $\left\lVertA-tB\right\rVert^2\le1+t^2-2(\cos\theta)t$ for every $t\ge0$.
\item There are a Hilbert space $L$, an isometry $V:H\to L$,
a contraction $R:L\to H$, and a unitary $U\in\mathcal B(L)$
with $\sigma(U)\subset K_\theta$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor}
u(a+bz)=R(aI_{L}+bU)V\qquad\text{for all }a,b\in\mathbb C.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

Put $c=\cos\theta$. Since the minimum of $\operatorname{Re}\zeta$ on $K_\theta$ is $c$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-test}
\left\lVert1-tz\right\rVert_{C(K_\theta)}^2
=\max_{\zeta\in K_\theta}(1+t^2-2t\operatorname{Re}\zeta)
=1+t^2-2ct\qquad\text{for all}\ t\ge0.
\end{equation}
Thus $(1)\Rightarrow(2)$.




Assume $(2)$. Corollary~\ref{cor:gram} gives a contraction $T$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:gram} and $\operatorname{Re} T\ge cI$. By Choi--Li
\cite[Theorem 2.1, p.~437]{CL}, there are an isometry $V:H\to H\oplus H$ and
a unitary $U\in\mathcal B(H\oplus H)$ such that

\]
 Since $U$ is unitary,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrum}
\sigma(U)\subset\{\zeta\in\mathbb T:\operatorname{Re}\zeta\ge c\}=K_\theta.
\end{equation}
Define the block operators
\[
W=[\,V\ \ UV\,]:H\oplus H\to H\oplus H,\qquad
F=[\,A\ \ B\,]:H\oplus H\to H.

Then we have

F^*F=\begin{pmatrix}A^*A&A^*B\\B^*A&B^*B\end{pmatrix},

\end{align}
In above we use functional calculus of normal operators. This shows $u$ is a contraction.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space and let
$u:E_\theta\to\mathcal B(H)$ be contractive.
The assertion is immediate if $H=\{0\}$, so assume $H\ne\{0\}$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:arc}, there are a complex Hilbert space $L$,
an isometry $V:H\to L$, a contraction $R:L\to H$, and a unitary
$U\in\mathcal B(L)$ with $\sigma(U)\subseteq K_\theta$ such that
\[
u(a+bz)=R(aI_L+bU)V
\qquad\text{for all }a,b\in\mathbb C.
\]
Fix $n\ge1$. Every element of $M_n(E_\theta)$ has the form $C+Dz$,
where $C=(c_{ij}),D=(d_{ij})\in M_n$ and
$C+Dz=(c_{ij}1+d_{ij}z)_{i,j=1}^n$.
Applying $u$ to each entry gives
\[
u^{(n)}(C+Dz)
=(I_n\otimes R)(C\otimes I_L+D\otimes U)(I_n\otimes V).
\]
Since $R$ and $V$ are contractions,
\[
\|u^{(n)}(C+Dz)\|
\le\|C\otimes I_L+D\otimes U\|.
\]
We now justify the estimate for the operator on the right.
For every $\varepsilon>0$, the spectral theorem gives finitely many
mutually orthogonal spectral projections $P_1,\ldots,P_m$ with
$\sum_{j=1}^mP_j=I_L$, and points
$\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_m\in\sigma(U)$, such that
\[
U_\varepsilon=\sum_{j=1}^m\zeta_jP_j,
\qquad \|U-U_\varepsilon\|\le\varepsilon.
\]
The operator
\[
C\otimes I_L+D\otimes U_\varepsilon
=\sum_{j=1}^m(C+\zeta_jD)\otimes P_j
\]
satisfies the inequality
\[
\|C\otimes I_L+D\otimes U_\varepsilon\|
\le\max_{1\le j\le m}\|C+\zeta_jD\|
\le\sup_{\zeta\in K_\theta}\|C+\zeta D\|.
\]

Letting $\varepsilon\to0$ we obtain
\[
\|u^{(n)}(C+Dz)\|
\le\sup_{\zeta\in K_\theta}\|C+\zeta D\|
=\|C+Dz\|_{M_n(E_\theta)}.
\]
Thus $u^{(n)}$ is contractive for every $n\ge1$, so $u$ is
completely contractive. The operator space structure inherited from
$C(K_\theta)$ is minimal. Since every contractive map from this
space into $\mathcal B(H)$ is completely contractive,
\eqref{eq:max-matrix-norm} shows that its maximal matrix norms are
at most its minimal matrix norms. This completes the proof.

\[
u=Tx=(u_1,u_2),\qquad v=Ty=(v_1,v_2).

\[
\sum_{j=1}^2 |u_j+v_j|
=\|u+v\|_1

onto $\ell_1^2(\mathbb R)$.

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:pairwise-nonisometric}
Let $0<\theta,\varphi<\pi$. Then $E_\theta$ and $E_\varphi$
are complex-linearly isometrically isomorphic if and only if
$\theta=\varphi$.
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Suppose that $T:E_\theta\to E_\varphi$ is a complex-linear
isometric isomorphism. Write
\[
f=T1=a+bz,\qquad g=Tz=c+dz.
\]
These functions, and hence their coefficients, are fixed
throughout the argument. Since $T$ is an isometry,
\[
\|f\|_{C(K_\varphi)}=\|g\|_{C(K_\varphi)}=1.
\]




For every $\zeta\in K_\theta$,
\[
\|f+\overline{\zeta}g\|_{C(K_\varphi)}
=\|1+\overline{\zeta}z\|_{C(K_\theta)}=2.
\]
By compactness, there is $\eta\in K_\varphi$ such that
\[
2=|f(\eta)+\overline{\zeta}g(\eta)|
\le |f(\eta)|+|g(\eta)|\le2.
\]
Since each of $|f(\eta)|$ and $|g(\eta)|$ is at most one,
equality implies
\[
|f(\eta)|=|g(\eta)|=1.
\]
Equality in the triangle inequality also gives
\[
g(\eta)=\zeta f(\eta).
\]




For these fixed functions, put
\[
S=\{\eta\in K_\varphi:|f(\eta)|=|g(\eta)|=1\}.
\]
The preceding argument shows that
\[
K_\theta\subseteq
\left\{\frac{g(\eta)}{f(\eta)}:\eta\in S\right\}.
\]
The quotients are well defined because $|f(\eta)|=1$ on $S$.
If $S$ were finite, the set on the right would be finite,
which is impossible since $K_\theta$ is infinite.
Thus $S$ is infinite.




By definition of $S$, every $\eta\in S$ satisfies
$|f(\eta)|=|g(\eta)|=1$. Since $z(\eta)=\eta$, we have
\[
f(\eta)=a+b\eta,\qquad g(\eta)=c+d\eta.
\]
Consequently,
\[
|a+b\eta|=|c+d\eta|=1
\qquad\text{for all }\eta\in S.
\]




For $\eta\in\mathbb T$,
\[
|a+b\eta|^2
=|a|^2+|b|^2
+2\operatorname{Re}(a\overline{b}\,\overline{\eta}).
\]
If $a\overline{b}\ne0$, the equation $|a+b\eta|=1$ has at most two solutions. Since this
equation holds on the infinite set $S$, we obtain
$a\overline{b}=0$. Similarly, $c\overline{d}=0$.




It follows that
\[
a=0\ \text{or}\ b=0,
\qquad
c=0\ \text{or}\ d=0.
\]
Thus each of $f,g$ is either a constant or a multiple of $z$.
Neither function is zero, since both have norm one.
Moreover, $f,g$ are linearly independent because $T$
is injective and $1,z$ are linearly independent.
They therefore cannot both be constants or both be
multiples of $z$.




If $b=0$, then $f=a$ is a nonzero constant, and independence
forces $g=dz$, so $c=0$. The norm-one condition gives
$|a|=|d|=1$.
If $a=0$, then $f=bz$, and independence forces $g=c$
to be a nonzero constant, so $d=0$. In this case
$|b|=|c|=1$. Hence the map
\[
h(\eta):=\frac{g(\eta)}{f(\eta)},\qquad\text{for all }\eta\in\mathbb T,
\]
has one of the forms
\[
h(\eta)=\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\eta
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
h(\eta)=\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\overline{\eta},
\] for some $\mu,\lambda\in\mathbb T$. Now for $\zeta,\eta\in\mathbb T$, we have
\[
|1+\overline{\zeta}\eta|=2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \eta=\zeta.
\]
Similarly, since $|f(\eta)|=|g(\eta)|=1$,
\[
|f(\eta)+\overline{\zeta}g(\eta)|=2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h(\eta)=\zeta.
\]
Using compactness and the isometry of $T$, we therefore obtain,
for every $\zeta\in\mathbb T$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\zeta\in K_\theta
&\Longleftrightarrow
\|1+\overline{\zeta}z\|_{C(K_\theta)}=2\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
\|f+\overline{\zeta}g\|_{C(K_\varphi)}=2\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
\zeta\in h(K_\varphi).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $K_\theta=h(K_\varphi)$. Since $h$ is a rotation
or a rotation followed by conjugation, it preserves arc
length. Therefore $2\theta=2\varphi$, so $\theta=\varphi$.
