\label{intro:gy:weight function} A \textit{weight function} $\phi$  satisfies the following assumptions.  $\phi$ is absolutely continuous on $(0,\infty)$ and
\begin{equation} \label{cond_int_derivative}
    \int_0^1 |\phi'(t)| t^{n-1} \, \mathrm{d} t<\infty.
\end{equation}

\label{thm:continuity_energy} Let $n\geq 2$ be an integer, $\phi$ be a weight function according to Definition \ref{intro:gy:weight function} and

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lm:rep_formula} Assume $\phi$ is a weight function according to Definition \ref{intro:gy:weight function}.
Let $\Omega$ be a convex body in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Then for every $R>0$ such that $\Omega\subset B_R$ the following formula holds
\begin{equation} \label{eq:layer_cake_formula}
    E_\phi(\partial \Omega) = \phi(R) E_0(\partial \Omega) - \int_0^R \phi'(t) E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t) \, \mathrm{d} t.
\end{equation}
If moreover $\phi' \in L^1([0,1])$, the following formula holds
\begin{align} \label{eq:formula_increasing}
   E_{\phi}(\partial\Omega)&=\phi(0) \, E_0(\partial \Omega) + \int_0^\infty \phi'(t) E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t^c) \, \mathrm{d} t.
                                            
                                                                                                                                                                              
\end{align}
In particular \eqref{eq:formula_increasing} holds true if $\phi$ is monotone and $\phi(0)=\lim_{t\searrow 0}\phi(t) \in \mathbb{R}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By means of the fundamental theorem of calculus, we express $\phi(|x|)$ for a.e. $x \in \partial \Omega$ as 
    \[
    \phi(|x|) = \phi(R) - \int_{|x|}^R \phi'(t) \, \mathrm{d} t = \phi(R) - \int_0^R \phi'(t) \mathds{1}_{(|x|,\infty)}(t) \, \mathrm{d} t\quad\text{ if }|x|<R.
    \]
    Let us assume for now that $\Omega$ is a nondegenerate convex body. Plugging the expression above in $E_\phi(\partial \Omega)$ yields
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:intermediate}
    \begin{aligned}
        E_\phi(\partial \Omega) &= \phi(R)E_0(\partial \Omega) - \int_{\partial \Omega}  \int_0^R \phi'(t) \mathds{1}_{(|x|,\infty)}(t)\, \mathrm{d} t \, \mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    To apply Fubini-Tonelli theorem we check the integrability condition. By monotonicity of the perimeter under inclusion for convex bodies (see also \eqref{eq:monoton incl with multipl}) we know that $E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t) \le |\partial B_t|=\beta_{n-1}t^{n-1}$ for all $t>0$. Thus,
    \begin{equation} \label{necessity_cond}
    \begin{aligned}
        \int_0^R |\phi'(t)| \left( \int_{\partial \Omega} \mathds{1}_{(|x|,\infty)}(t)\, \mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x) \right) \, \mathrm{d} t &= \int_0^R |\phi'(t)| E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t) \, \mathrm{d} t \\ & \le \beta_{n-1} \int_0^R |\phi'(t)| t^{n-1} \, \mathrm{d} t < \infty.
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    Notice that the last term on the right-hand side above is finite by \eqref{cond_int_derivative}. Therefore, exchanging the order of integration, and recalling the notation \eqref{eq:notation_relative_energy}, we obtain
    \begin{equation*}
        E_\phi(\partial \Omega) = \phi(R) E_0(\partial \Omega) - \int_0^R \phi'(t) E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t) \, \mathrm{d} t.
    \end{equation*}
                                                  
                   
                                                                                                           
                      
    
    If $\Omega$ is degenerate, a factor $2$ has to be added in front of both the second term on the right-hand side in \eqref{eq:intermediate} and  in the left-hand side of \eqref{necessity_cond}, and the conclusion \eqref{eq:layer_cake_formula} remains unchanged.
    

    To prove the remaining formulas, since $R>0$ is such that $\Omega \subset B_R$,  for all $t \ge R$, we have $E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t) = E_0(\partial \Omega)$ and $E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t^c) = 0$.

  Using the identity $E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t) = E_0(\partial \Omega) - E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t^c)$ 
    in \eqref{eq:layer_cake_formula} and since $\phi' \in L^1([0,1])$, we get
    \begin{align*}
        E_\phi(\partial \Omega) &= \phi(R) E_0(\partial \Omega) - \int_0^R \phi'(t) \left( E_0(\partial \Omega) - E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t^c) \right) \, \mathrm{d} t \\
        &= \phi(R) E_0(\partial \Omega) - E_0(\partial \Omega) \int_0^R \phi'(t) \, \mathrm{d} t + \int_0^R \phi'(t) E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t^c) \, \mathrm{d} t.
    \end{align*}
   By the fundamental theorem of calculus $\int_0^R \phi'(t) \, \mathrm{d} t = \phi(R) - \phi(0)$.  Substituting this back, the first two terms simplify to $\phi(0)E_0(\partial\Omega)$. Furthermore, since $E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t^c) = 0$ for all $t \ge R$, we can extend the integration domain of the last integral to $[0, \infty)$, yielding \eqref{eq:formula_increasing}.

<!-- customproof-1: body_md -->
{Lemma \ref{lm:continuity_perimeter}}
	
		We claim that for every $\sigma \in \mathbb{S}^{n-1}$, we have
		\begin{equation} \label{eq:claim_peri}
			|\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i)| \to |\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega)| \quad \text{as } i \to \infty.
		\end{equation}
		Assuming this claim holds true, we can  readily conclude the proof by applying the dominated convergence theorem to the formula \eqref{eq:gy:cauchy surface general}.
		Indeed, since the sequence $(\Omega_i)_{i \geq 1}$ is bounded, there exists $R > 0$ such that $\Omega_i \subset B_R$ for all $i \geq 1$. Consequently, for any direction $\sigma \in \mathbb{S}^{n-1}$, the projection $\pi_\sigma(\Omega_i)$ is contained in the projection of the $n$-dimensional ball $B_R$, which is an $(n-1)$-dimensional ball of radius $R$. This provides the uniform bound
		\[
		|\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i)| \leq |\pi_\sigma(B_R)| = \omega_{n-1} R^{n-1}.
		\]
		Dominated convergence theorem allows us to pass the limit inside the integral, yielding
		\[
		\lim_{i \to \infty} E_0(\partial \Omega_i) = \lim_{i \to \infty} \frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}} \int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}} |\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i)| \, \mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma) = \frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}} \int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}} |\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega)| \, \mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma) = E_0(\partial \Omega).
		\]
		
We now turn to the proof of \eqref{eq:claim_peri}.
In view of the assumed Hausdorff convergence of $\Omega_i$, for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists an $i_0$  such that
		\[
		\Omega_i \subseteq \Omega +  B_\varepsilon \quad \text{and} \quad \Omega \subseteq \Omega_i +  B_\varepsilon \qquad \text{for any }i\geq i_0.
		\]
		Exploiting the linearity of the projection, we apply $\pi_{\sigma}$ to both inclusions
		\[
		\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i) \subseteq \pi_{\sigma}(\Omega) +  \pi_{\sigma}(B_\varepsilon) \quad \text{and} \quad \pi_{\sigma}(\Omega) \subseteq \pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i) + \pi_{\sigma}(B_\varepsilon)
		\]
		Since $\pi_{\sigma}(B_1) = B_1^{n-1}$ (the $(n-1)$-dimensional unit ball in the hyperplane $H_\sigma$), we obtain
		\[
		\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i) \subseteq \pi_{\sigma}(\Omega) +  B_\varepsilon^{n-1} \quad \text{and} \quad \pi_{\sigma}(\Omega) \subseteq \pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i) +  B_\varepsilon^{n-1}.
		\]
		This implies that the Hausdorff distance between the projections is bounded by that of the original sets
		\[
		d_H(\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega), \pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i)) \le d_H(\Omega, \Omega_i) < \varepsilon, \qquad \text{for every }i\geq i_0.
		\]
		Thus $\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i) \to \pi_{\sigma}(\Omega)$ in the Hausdorff distance. Since these projections are convex bodies in $\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$, and the $(n-1)$-dimensional Lebesgue measure is continuous with respect to the Hausdorff metric on the class of convex bodies in $\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$ (see, for instance, \cite[Theorem 1.8.20]{Schneider}), it follows that $|\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i)| \to |\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega)|$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{Lemma:Cauhy formula relative a ball}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a convex body. Then 
$$
  E_0(\partial\Omega;B_t)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t)|\, \mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma)-|\Omega\cap\partial B_t|\quad\text{ for every $t>0$}.
$$
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $\Omega\cap\overline{B_t}$ is empty or consists of a single point, the conclusion is immediate. We may therefore assume otherwise.

We apply \eqref{eq:gy:cauchy surface general} to the convex body $\Omega\cap\overline{B_t}$, obtaining
$$
  E_0(\partial(\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t))=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t)|\, \mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma).
$$
Thus to prove the lemma it is sufficient to show that
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:E0 splitting ae t Bt}
   E_0(\partial(\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t))= E_0(\partial\Omega;B_t)+|\Omega\cap\partial B_t|\quad\text{ for $t>0$.}
\end{equation}
We now distinguish two cases, depending on the degeneracy of $\Omega.$

If $\Omega$ is degenerate, then also $\Omega\cap \overline{B}_t$ is degenerate. Moreover, note that $|\Omega\cap \partial B_t|=0$, since  a sphere intersected with any hyperplane has vanishing $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-measure. Using this fact twice, and that $\partial\Omega=\Omega$, we obtain 
\begin{align*}
  E_0(\partial(\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t))=&2|\Omega\cap \overline{B}_t|=2|\Omega\cap B_t|
  =2|\partial\Omega\cap B_t|
  \\
  =&E_0(\partial\Omega;B_t)=E_0(\partial\Omega;B_t)+|\Omega\cap \partial B_t|.
\end{align*}
This proves \eqref{eq:E0 splitting ae t Bt} in the present case. 

Assume now that $\Omega$ is nondegenerate. Firstly, recall that for any two closed sets $C,D \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ we have
\begin{equation*} \label{eq:boundary_intersection}
    \partial(C \cap D) =( \partial C \cap D) \cup (C \cap \partial D).
\end{equation*}
Using this identity and that $\partial\Omega\cap \partial B_t\subset \Omega\cap \partial B_t$ gives that the boundary of the convex body $\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t$ is given by 
\[
\partial(\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t) = \bigl(\partial\Omega\cap \overline{B}_t\bigr) \cup \bigl(\Omega\cap\partial B_t\bigr)= \bigl(\partial\Omega\cap B_t\bigr) \cup \bigl(\Omega\cap\partial B_t\bigr).  
\] 
Note that the last expression on the right is a disjoint union.

Observe now that $B_t$ intersected with the interior of $\Omega$ cannot be empty, because if it were empty, then a hyperplane would separate $\Omega$ from $B_t$. And in such a case the intersection $\Omega\cap \overline{B}_t$ would be at most one point, which we have excluded already. Hence we have that $E_0(\partial(\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t)) = |\partial(\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t)|$ and, 
consequently,
\[ 
\begin{aligned} E_0\bigl(\partial(\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t)\bigr) &= \left|\partial(\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t)\right|= \left|\partial\Omega\cap B_t\right| + \left|\Omega\cap\partial B_t\right| = E_0(\partial\Omega;B_t) + \left|\Omega\cap\partial B_t\right|,\end{aligned} 
\] 
which proves \eqref{eq:E0 splitting ae t Bt} in the second case.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lm:continuity_relative_perimeter}
		Let $(\Omega_i)_{i \geq 1}$ be a sequence of convex bodies. Assume that $\Omega_i$ converges to $ \Omega$ in the Hausdorff metric for some convex body $\Omega$ as $i \to \infty$ . Then for a.e. $t>0$ it holds
		\begin{equation*}
			\begin{aligned}
				&  \lim_{i \to \infty} E_0(\partial \Omega_i; B_t)=  E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t) .
                     
				                                                                                                                       
			\end{aligned}
		\end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since the formula of Lemma \ref{Lemma:Cauhy formula relative a ball} holds for all $\Omega_i$ and for $\Omega$, by dominated convergence theorem, as in Lemma \ref{lm:continuity_perimeter}, it is sufficient to prove for a given $t>0$, that for every $\sigma\in \mathbb{S}^{n-1}$
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:two limits}
    \lim_{i\to\infty}|\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i\cap \overline{B}_t)=|\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega\cap \overline{B}_t)|\quad \text{ and }\quad \lim_{i\to\infty}|\Omega_i\cap \partial B_t|=|\Omega\cap \partial B_t|
\end{equation}
The second limit  might hold only for a.e. $t>0$, in case that $\Omega$ is nondegenerate.

Let us first prove the first limit in \eqref{eq:two limits}. We shall distinguish two cases depending on whether $\Omega\cap B_t$ is empty or not.     


If $\Omega\cap B_t\neq \emptyset$ then  $\Omega$ and $\overline{B}_t$ cannot be separated by a hyperplane and thus from \cite[Theorem 1.8.10]{Schneider} it follows that $\Omega_i\cap \overline{B}_t$ converges to $\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t$ in the Hausdorff metric. In this case we can proceed exactly as we have proven \eqref{eq:claim_peri} in the proof of Lemma \ref{lm:continuity_perimeter} and obtain the first limit in \eqref{eq:two limits}. 

If $\Omega\cap B_t = \emptyset$, then there exists a separating hyperplane between $\Omega$ and $B_t.$ Without loss of generality $\Omega\subset \mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(-\infty,-t]$ and $\Omega\cap\overline{B}_t$ contains at most one point, namely $(0,\ldots,0,-t)$. In particular $|\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega\cap \overline{B}_t)|=0$. So in this case it is sufficient to prove that 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pi sigma Omegi cap}
  \lim_{i\to\infty} |\pi_{\sigma}(\Omega_i\cap \overline{B}_t)|=0.
\end{equation}
By hypothesis $\Omega_i$ converges to $\Omega$ in the Hausdorff metric, and hence for any given $0<\varepsilon<t$ the sets $\Omega_i$ are all contained in $\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(-\infty,-t+\varepsilon]$ for $i$ big enough. Thus $\Omega_i\cap \overline{B}_t$ is contained in the cylinder $\{(x',x_n)\in\mathbb{R}^n;\, |x'|\leq r_{\varepsilon}\text{ and }x_n\in [-t,-t+\varepsilon]\}$, where $r_{\varepsilon}^2=t^2-(t-\varepsilon)^2.$ Observe that $r_{\varepsilon}$ converges to zero, as $\varepsilon\to 0,$ and hence \eqref{eq:pi sigma Omegi cap} follows. 

We now deal with the second limit in \eqref{eq:two limits}. For this we distinguish whether $\Omega$ is degenerate or not. If $\Omega$ is degenerate, then $|\Omega\cap \partial B_t|=0.$ We may assume, after a rotation, that $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\{a\}$ for some $a\in\mathbb{R}$. By assumption of the Hausdorff convergence, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists an $i_0$ such that
$$
  \Omega_i\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[a-\varepsilon,a+\varepsilon]\quad\forall\, i\geq i_0\,.
$$
Thus we obtain that 
$$
  |\Omega_i\cap \partial B_t|\leq |\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times [a-\varepsilon,a+\varepsilon]\cap \partial B_t|\to 0
  =|\Omega\cap \partial B_t|\quad\text{ for $\varepsilon\to 0$}.
$$
This proves the second limit in \eqref{eq:two limits} in the degenerate case. 

Let us now consider the second limit in \eqref{eq:two limits} when $\Omega$ is not degenerate. In this case we will write the limit in the form
$$
  \lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\partial B_t}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_i}(x)\, \mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\int_{\partial B_t}\mathds{1}_{\Omega}(x)\, \mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)
$$
and will use the dominated convergence theorem to conclude. It is thus sufficient to prove that, for almost every $t>0$
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ae t and ae x in pBt}
    \lim_{i\to\infty}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_i}(x)=\mathds{1}_{\Omega}(x)\quad\text{ for $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-a.e. $x\in \partial B_t$}.
\end{equation}



By \eqref{eq:measure_zero_pBt}, we may choose $t>0$ such that 
$|\partial\Omega\cap\partial B_t|=0$.
                                                                                                                             
For such a $t$ it holds that $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-a.e. $x\in\partial B_t$ belongs to $\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\partial\Omega.$ Therefore we will prove \eqref{eq:ae t and ae x in pBt} precisely for these $x$ distinguishing the two case whether $x$ is in the interior of $\Omega$ or $x\in \Omega^c.$ If $x \in \Omega^c$ then $d:=\mathrm{dist}(x, \Omega)>0$ and for $i$ sufficiently large we have $d_H(\Omega_i,\Omega)<d/2$ which implies $x \notin \Omega_i$ and proves \eqref{eq:ae t and ae x in pBt}.

Consider $x \in \mathring{\Omega}$, where $\mathring{\Omega}$ denotes the interior of $\Omega$ with respect to the standard topology in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
This case will follow easily from \cite[Lemma 1.8.1]{Schneider}, which states that $\Omega_i$ converges to $\Omega$ in the Hausdorff distance if and only if $\partial\Omega_i$ converges to $\partial\Omega$ in the same distance. 
If $x\in\mathring{\Omega}$, then $B_{2\varepsilon}(x)\subset\mathring{\Omega}$ for some $\varepsilon>0.$ Using \cite[Lemma 1.8.1]{Schneider} we obtain that $\partial\Omega_i\subset \partial\Omega+B_{\varepsilon}$ for all $i$ big enough. Hence for every $y\in \partial\Omega_i$ there exists $z\in\partial\Omega$ such that $|y-z|<\varepsilon$. This gives that
$$
  |x-y|\geq |x-z|-|z-y|>2\varepsilon-\varepsilon=\varepsilon.
$$
Since $y\in\partial\Omega_i$ was arbitrary, we obtain that $B_{\varepsilon}(x)$ does not intersect $\partial\Omega_i$ and is contained either in the interior of $\Omega_i$ or in $\Omega_i^c.$ The second case is not possible, contradicting the assumption $d_H(\Omega_i,\Omega)\to 0.$ Hence $x\in \Omega_i$ for all $i$ big enough, which proves \eqref{eq:ae t and ae x in pBt} in the case $x\in\mathring{\Omega}.$

{Theorem \ref{thm:continuity_energy}}
		Since the sequence $(\Omega_i)_{i \geq 1}$ is  bounded, there exists $R>0$ such that $\Omega_i \subset B_R$ for all $i \geq 1$, which implies $\Omega \subset B_R$ as well.
		Using the representation formula \eqref{eq:layer_cake_formula}, we can write the energy of each $\Omega_i$ as
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:E_phi_representation}
			E_\phi(\partial \Omega_i) = \phi(R) E_0(\partial \Omega_i) - \int_0^R \phi'(t) E_0(\partial \Omega_i; B_t) \, \mathrm{d} t.
		\end{equation}
		
		We analyze the convergence of the two terms on the right-hand side individually.
		
		First, by the continuity of the perimeter functional for convex bodies (Lemma \ref{lm:continuity_perimeter}), we immediately have 
		\[
		\lim_{i \to \infty} \phi(R) E_0(\partial \Omega_i) = \phi(R) E_0(\partial \Omega).
		\]
		For the integral term in \eqref{eq:E_phi_representation}, the integrand is given by $h_i(t) := \phi'(t) E_0(\partial \Omega_i; B_t) \mathds{1}_{[0,R]}(t)$.
		By Lemma \ref{lm:continuity_relative_perimeter}, we know that $E_0(\partial \Omega_i; B_t) \to E_0(\partial\Omega; B_t)$ for almost every $t > 0$. Thus, $h_i(t)$ converges pointwise almost everywhere to $\phi'(t) E_0(\partial \Omega; B_t) \mathds{1}_{[0,R]}(t)$.
		
		To apply the dominated convergence theorem, we need to find an integrable bounding function. By the monotonicity of the perimeter for convex sets \eqref{eq:monoton incl with multipl}, the relative perimeter of $\Omega_i$ inside the ball $B_t$ is bounded by the perimeter of the ball itself
		\[
		E_0(\partial\Omega_i; B_t) \leq |\partial B_t| = \beta_{n-1} t^{n-1}.
		\]
		This yields the uniform pointwise bound
		\[
		|h_i(t)| \leq \beta_{n-1} |\phi'(t)| t^{n-1} \mathds{1}_{[0,R]}(t).
		\]
	 The assumption \eqref{cond_int_derivative} on the weight function guarantees exactly that the dominating function $|\phi'(t)| t^{n-1}$ is integrable on $[0, R]$. Therefore, by the dominated convergence theorem, we can pass to the limit inside the integral
		\[
		\lim_{i \to \infty} \int_0^R \phi'(t) E_0(\partial\Omega_i; B_t) \, \mathrm{d} t = \int_0^R \phi'(t) E_0(\partial\Omega; B_t)\, \, \mathrm{d} t.
		\]
Thus, taking the two limits on the right-hand side of 
\eqref{eq:E_phi_representation}, we obtain $\lim_{i \to \infty} E_\phi(\partial \Omega_i) = E_\phi(\partial \Omega)$, which concludes the proof.

{Theorem \ref{thm:monotonicity}}
Using the monotonicity under inclusion for the perimeter of \eqref{eq:monoton incl with multipl} and the assumption $\phi(0)\geq 0,$ we obtain that $\phi(0)E_0(\partial \Omega_1) \le \phi(0)E_0(\partial \Omega_2)$.
Hence, due to formula \eqref{eq:formula_increasing}, it remains to prove that 
$\phi'(t)E_0(\partial \Omega_1; B_t^c) \le \phi'(t)E_0(\partial \Omega_2; B_t^c)$ for all $t\geq 0.$ Since $\phi'\geq 0$ by assumption, it is sufficient to show that
\begin{equation} \label{eq:thesis}
	    E_0(\partial \Omega_1; B_t^c) \le E_0(\partial \Omega_2; B_t^c) \quad \text{for } t \ge 0.
\end{equation} 
To show this we consider
the metric projection map $\pi:\mathbb{R}^n \to \Omega_1$ defined as
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:argmin}
		\pi(y)=\operatorname{argmin}_{x \in \Omega_1} |y-x|.
		\end{equation}
Let $H_a(x)$ be a separating hyperplane for $\Omega_1$ at $x \in \partial \Omega_1$ with outer normal vector $a$: that is $H_a(x)=\{z\in \mathbb{R}^n \, :\, (z-x)\cdot a= 0 \}$ and $\Omega_1 \subset \{z\in \mathbb{R}^n \, :\, (z-x)\cdot a\leq 0 \}$. For any such $a$ the ray $\{ x + t a \, : t \geq 0\}$ must intersect $\partial \Omega_2$ at some point $y \in \partial \Omega_2$ and $\pi(y)=x$. Hence $ \partial \Omega_1\subset \pi(\partial \Omega_2) $.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               

Let now $x \in \partial \Omega_1 \cap B_t^c$ and $y \in \Omega_2$ such that $\pi(y)=x$. We claim that $y \in B_t^c$. To show this we use that $y-x$ must be orthogonal to a separating hyperplane for $\Omega_1$. After a rotation, we may assume that $H_{e_n}=\{ z \in \mathbb{R}^n \, : \, z_n=x_n\}$ is a separating hyperplane for $\Omega_1$ such that $\Omega_1 \subset \{ z \leq x_n\}$.
Because $0 \in \Omega_1$, we necessarily have $0 \leq x_n$. 
    Writing the coordinates as $z=(z',z_n) \in \mathbb{R}^{n-1} \times \mathbb{R}$, we have that $y=(x',y_n)$ for some $y_n \geq x_n\geq 0$ and this gives
\[
|y|^2 = |x'|^2 + y_n^2 \geq |x'|^2 + x_n^2 = |x|^2 \geq t^2, 
\]
which proves $y \in B_t^c$. In particular, we have shown that 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inclusion pi and Btcompl}
 \partial \Omega_1 \cap B_t^c \subset \pi \left( \partial \Omega_2 \cap B_t^c\right).
\end{equation}

We now distinguish three cases depending on the degeneracy of the sets $\Omega_1,\, \Omega_2$. Firstly, if both $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$ are degenerate then \eqref{eq:thesis} follows immediately from the fact that $\partial\Omega_1=\Omega_1\subset\Omega_2=\partial\Omega_2$.

In the second case let us assume that neither $\Omega_1$ nor $\Omega_2$ is degenerate. In that case we use that $\pi$ is a $1$-Lipschitz map (see for instance \cite[Theorem 1.2.1]{Schneider}) and for any set $A$ it holds (see for instance \cite[Theorem 2.8]{EvansGariepy})
\begin{equation} \label{eq:inclusion_1}
    \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\pi(A)) \leq   \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(A).
\end{equation}
Thus, using \eqref{eq:inclusion_1} and \eqref{eq:inclusion pi and Btcompl}, we obtain that 
\[
E_0(\partial \Omega_1;B_t^c)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega_1 \cap B_t^c)\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega_2 \cap B_t^c) = E_0(\partial \Omega_2;B_t^c),
\]
establishing \eqref{eq:thesis}.

The third case is when $\Omega_1$ is degenerate and $\Omega_2$ is nondegenerate. We can assume, after a rotation, that $\Omega_1 \subset \{ z \in \mathbb{R}^n \, : \, z_n=0\}$. As $\Omega_2$ is nondegenerate, there must exist some $y^\star \in \Omega_2$ such that $y_n^\star>0$ or $y_n^\star<0$. Let us assume $y_n^\star>0$. Define
\[
\Omega_2^+ = \Omega_2 \cap \{ z_n \geq 0\}, \quad \Omega_2^- = \Omega_2 \cap \{ z_n \leq 0\}.
\]
If $ x \in \partial \Omega_1 \cap B_t^c$ then for both $a=\pm e_n$ the plane $H_a(x)$ is a separating hyperplane for $\Omega_1$. Thus, arguing as after \eqref{eq:argmin}, for almost every $ x \in \partial \Omega_1 \cap B_t^c$ has at least two preimages under $\pi$, that is, there exist
\[
y^+ \in \partial \Omega_2^+ \cap \{ z_n >0\} \quad \text{ and } \quad y^- \in \partial \Omega_2^- \cap \{ z_n \leq 0\}
\]
such that $\pi(y^+)=x$ and $\pi(y^-)=x$. We have to now distinguish two subcases: namely $y^-$ can be chosen such that
$$
  \left\{ \begin{array}{rl}
   y^- \in \partial \Omega_2^- \cap \{ z_n = 0\} & \text{ if $\Omega_2^-$ is degenerate}
   \\
    y^- \in \partial \Omega_2^- \cap \{ z_n < 0\} & \text{ if $\Omega_2^-$ is not degenerate}.
    \end{array}\right.
$$

Therefore, up to an $\mathcal H^{n-1}$-negligible set, it follows that
\[
\partial \Omega_1 \cap B_t^c \subset \pi\left( \partial \Omega_2^+ \cap \{ z_n >0\} \right),\quad  \partial \Omega_1 \cap B_t^c \subset 
\left\{ \begin{array}{l}
  \pi\left( \partial \Omega_2^- \cap \{ z_n = 0\} \right) \text{ if $\Omega_2^-$ is degenerate}
   \\
  \pi\left( \partial \Omega_2^- \cap \{ z_n < 0\} \right) \text{ if $\Omega_2^-$ is not degenerate}.
    \end{array}\right.
\]
Moreover, we have seen that if $x\in B_t^c,$ then $y^{\pm}\in B_t^c.$ 
Hence, using \eqref{eq:inclusion_1} for the first one of these inclusions, we infer
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Omega2plus}
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega_1 \cap B_t^c) \leq \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega_2^+ \cap \{ z_n>0\} \cap B_t^c) = \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega_2 \cap \{ z_n>0\} \cap B_t^c). 
\end{equation}
Similarly, depending on whether $\Omega_2^-$ is degenerate or not, we fall in either the first or the second case. If $\Omega_2^-$ is not degenerate, then using  \eqref{eq:inclusion_1}  gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Omega2minus}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega_1 \cap B_t^c) \leq \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega_2^- \cap \{ z_n < 0\} \cap B_t^c)=& \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega_2 \cap \{ z_n < 0\} \cap B_t^c)
 \\
 =& \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega_2 \cap \{ z_n \leq  0\} \cap B_t^c).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
In the last identity we have used that $\Omega_2^-$ is convex and nondegenerate.
If $\Omega_2^-$ is degenerate, then $\partial\Omega_1=\Omega_1\subset \Omega_2^-=\partial\Omega_2^-$ and the final conclusion of \eqref{eq:Omega2minus} is valid as well. 
Hence, summing up \eqref{eq:Omega2plus} and \eqref{eq:Omega2minus} we obtain
\[
E_0(\partial \Omega_1;B_t^c)= 2\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega_1 \cap B_t^c) \leq \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega_2 \cap B_t^c)=E_0(\partial \Omega_2;B_t^c),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:thesis}.
The case $\Omega_1$ nondegenerate and $\Omega_2$ degenerate cannot occur due to the assumption $\Omega_1 \subset \Omega_2$, and thus the proof is concluded.

\label{lm:est_on_triangle}
Let $n\geq 2$, let $\phi$ be an increasing weight function, $\phi(0)\geq0$ and $\Delta \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be an $(n-1)$-dimensional simplex of vertices $0,z_1, z_2, \dots, z_{n-1}$ with $|z_1| \geq |z_2| \geq \dots \geq |z_{n-1}|$. Then the following estimate holds

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The simplex $\Delta$ is a degenerate convex body and can be parameterized by the linear map $\varphi(t_1,\dots,t_{n-1})$ as
\[
\varphi:(t_1,\dots,t_{n-1})\in T \mapsto \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} t_i \, z_i \quad \text{with } T := \{(t_1,\dots,t_{n-1})\, : \, t_i \geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} t_i \leq 1  \} \]
The Jacobian of $\varphi$ is given by $|z_1 \wedge z_2 \wedge \dots \wedge z_{n-1}| = (n-1)!E_0(\Delta)/2$, see for instance \cite[Formula 9.12.4.3]{berger2009geometry}, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gy:Ep Delta varphi}
  E_{\phi}(\Delta)=2\int_{\varphi(T)}\phi(|x|) \, \mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=(n-1)!E_0(\Delta)\int_T \phi(|\varphi(t)|) \, \mathrm{d} t_1\ldots \, \mathrm{d} t_{n-1}
\end{equation}

We will estimate the integral from below by restricting the domain of integration to the subregion $C_T \subset T$ defined as follows
\[
C_T := \{(t_1,\dots,t_{n-1})\in T: \frac{3}{4}\leq t_1 \leq 1 \}.
\]
For $ (t_1,\dots, t_{n-1})\in C_T$ we have that $\sum_{i=2}^{n-1} t_i \leq 1-t_1 \leq \frac{1}{4}$,
and in particular
\[
|\varphi(t)|=\left|t_1 z_1 + \sum_{i=2}^{n-1} t_i z_i\right| \geq t_1|z_1|-\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}t_i|z_i| \geq \left(t_1 - \sum_{i=2}^{n-1} t_i \right) |z_1| \geq \frac{1}{2} |z_1|
\]


Thus we can estimate $E_\phi(\Delta)$ in \eqref{eq:gy:Ep Delta varphi} as
\begin{align*}
E_\phi(\Delta)  \ge (n-1)!E_0(\Delta)\int_{C_T} \phi \left(\left| \varphi(t) \right| \right) \, \mathrm{d} t_1 \dots \, \mathrm{d} t_{n-1}
\ge (n-1)!E_0(\Delta) |C_T| \phi \left( \frac{1}{2}|z_1| \right).
\end{align*}
Since $C_T$ is a homothetic copy of $T$ scaled by $1/4$ and shifted, more precisely $C_T=1/4\,T+(3/4,0,\ldots,0)$, we have $|C_T| = 4^{-(n-1)}|T| = (4^{n-1}(n-1)!)^{-1}$. Substituting this yields
\begin{align*}
\frac{E_\phi(\Delta)}{E_0(\Delta)} \ge \frac{1}{4^{n-1}}\phi\left(\frac{1}{2}|z_1|\right),
\end{align*}
which concludes the proof.

{Lemma \ref{lm:bound_on_max_radius_increasing}}


    Let $\Omega$ be a convex body in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and let $y_1 \in \partial\Omega$ be a point at maximum distance from the origin, so that $|y_1|=R$. Let $V_1:= \operatorname*{span} \{y_1\}$. Define $y_2 \in \partial \Omega$ as a point farthest from $V_1$. More precisely, consider
\[
b_2:= \max \{ \mathrm{dist}(V_1,z) \, : \, z \in \partial \Omega \}>0,
\]
and $y_2$ is the point where the maximum is achieved. Set $V_2=\operatorname*{span} \{y_1,y_2\}$. Iteratively define
\[
b_j:=\max \{ \mathrm{dist}(V_{j-1},z) \, : \, z \in \partial \Omega \}, \quad \text{for }j=2,\dots, n-1,\;\text{ and }\; b_1:=R
\]
and the corresponding points $y_j$ where such maximum is achieved.  Notice that $b_j>0$ since  $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial \Omega) >0$.
Let us define for $j=1,\ldots,n-1$, the $j$-dimensional simplex $\Delta_j$ of vertices $0,y_1, \dots, y_j$
by
$$
  \Delta_j:=\left\{ \sum_{i=1}^{j}t_iy_i;\,t_i\geq 0\,, \sum_{i=1}^{j}t_i\leq 1\right\},\,\text{ and }\, \Delta:=\Delta_{n-1}.
$$
It is easy to see by induction on $j$ that $\Delta$ is contained in $\Omega$.

The $(n-1)$-dimensional area $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Delta)$ of the simplex $\Delta$ is given by (see again \cite[Formula 9.12.4.3]{berger2009geometry} for instance)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gy:area of simplex}
  \frac{E_0(\Delta)}{2}=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Delta)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}b_1  \cdots  b_{n-1}.
\end{equation}


Moreover, the vertices of $\Delta$ are $\{0,y_1,\dots,y_{n-1} \}$ thus, by calling $P$ the projection of $\Omega$ onto $V_{n-1}$, we infer that $\Delta \subset P(\Omega)$. 
Since $\Delta\subset P(\Omega)$ and by \eqref{eq:gy:cauchy surface general}, we deduce 
\[ E_0(\Delta)\le E_0(P(\Omega))\le E_0(\partial \Omega). 
\]
 Thus we obtain  that $E_0(\Delta)\leq E_0(P(\Omega)) \leq E_0(\partial \Omega)$, and thus
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:b times R const}
  b_2 \cdot \cdots \cdot b_{n-1}\leq (n-1)!\, \frac{E_0(\partial\Omega)}{2 b_1}
  =(n-1)!\, \frac{E_0(\partial\Omega)}{2 R}.
\end{equation}





By definition of $R$ we have that, up to a rotation, $\Omega \subset [-R,R] \times \mathbb{R}^{n-1}$. Similarly by construction and a simple induction argument, it follows that $\Omega$ is contained, up to a rotation, in the hyperrectangle
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Omega in cylinder}
\Omega\subset C_{R,b}:=[-R,R] \times [-b_2,b_2] \times  [-b_3,b_3] \times \cdots \times  [-b_{n-2},b_{n-2}] \times [-b_{n-1},b_{n-1}]^2.
\end{equation}

                                                                                                           
                  
                                
                                                                                                                                          
                
For future reference we define 
\[
b:= \left( b_2 \cdots b_{n-1}  \right)^{\frac{1}{n-2}},
\]
and  \eqref{eq:b times R const} becomes
\begin{equation} \label{eq:b small with R}
   b^{n-2} \leq (n-1)! \frac{E_0(\partial\Omega)}{2R}.
\end{equation}






Since $\phi$  is increasing and $\phi(0)\geq0$ we can make use of the monotonicity property of Theorem \ref{thm:monotonicity} and  compare the energy of the simplex $\Delta$ with the energy of $\partial \Omega$. This leads, using Lemma \ref{lm:est_on_triangle}, to
\begin{align*}
    E_\phi(\partial \Omega) \geq E_\phi (\Delta) \geq
    \frac{1}{4^{n-1}}\, E_0(\Delta) \, \phi \left( \frac{R}{2}\right) .
\end{align*}
Finally, we show that the measure of the simplex is comparable to the perimeter of $\Omega$. Indeed, since $R\geq b_2 \geq \dots \geq b_{n-2} \geq b_{n-1}$, as a consequence of \eqref{eq:Omega in cylinder} one has that the two biggest faces of $\partial C_{R,b}$ are
$$
   [-R,R] \times [-b_2,b_2] \times \cdots  \times [-b_{n-1},b_{n-1}]\times\{\pm b_{n-1}\}
$$
and each of them have $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-measure $2^{n-1} R \cdot b_2 \cdots b_{n-1}$.
Thus one gets
\begin{align*}
    E_0(\partial \Omega) \leq E_0
    (\partial C_{R,b}) \leq n 2^n R \cdot b_2 \cdots b_{n-1} = 2^{n-1}\, n! \,E_0(\Delta).
\end{align*}
Combining both estimates one obtains that 
\begin{align*}
\frac{ E_\phi(\partial \Omega) }{E_0(\partial \Omega) } \geq \frac{ E_\phi(\Delta) }{2^{n-1} n! E_0(\Delta) }\geq \frac{1}{2^{3n-3}\, n!} \, \phi \left( \frac{R}{2}\right)
=\frac{1}{8^{n-1}\,n!}  \, \phi\left(\frac{R}{2}\right).
\end{align*}

\label{cor:intro:existence1}
Let $n\geq 2$, $\phi$ be an increasing weight function with $\phi(0)\geq 0$ and $\lim_{t\to \infty} \phi(t)=\infty$. Then, for every $\lambda>0$ there exists a minimizer  of 
\begin{equation} \label{min_problem_p_pos}

\end{equation}

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $(\Omega_i)_{i\geq 1}$ be a minimizing sequence of convex bodies with  $E_0( \partial \Omega_i)=\lambda$ for all $i\geq 1$.
We observe that we can assume $0 \in {\Omega_i}$ for all $i \ge 1$ without loss of generality, since translating the $\Omega_i$ towards the origin decreases the Euclidean norm of all its points, thanks to the convexity of $\Omega_i$. This operation decreases the energy $E_\phi$.  


 We claim that the sequence of sets $(\Omega_i)_{i\geq 1}$ is bounded; that is, there exists $R>0$ such that $\Omega_i\subset B_R$ for all $i\geq 1$. 
We argue by contradiction and assume the sequence is unbounded. 
Since we established that $0 \in {\Omega_i}$ for all $i\geq1$, we must have that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gy:diam infty}
\lim_{i\to\infty}\text{diam}(\Omega_i)=\infty.
\end{equation}

If $n=2$, this immediately yields a contradiction, as the diameter of a convex set is universally bounded by its perimeter, which is equal to  $\lambda$ for all $i$. On the other hand, for $n\geq 3$, we will deduce that \eqref{eq:gy:diam infty} cannot hold by Lemma \ref{lm:bound_on_max_radius_increasing}, which is applicable since $0 \in {\Omega_i}$. By defining $R_i := \max_{x \in \partial\Omega_i} |x|$, the unbounded diameter assumption implies $R_i \to \infty$. Thus Lemma \ref{lm:bound_on_max_radius_increasing} together with the assumption $\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi(t)=\infty$ yields that $E_\phi(\partial \Omega_i) \to \infty$. This contradicts the assumption that the sequence is minimizing.
This shows that \eqref{eq:gy:diam infty} cannot hold and that the sequence is bounded. 

From the Blaschke selection theorem, see for instance \cite[Theorem 1.8.7]{Schneider}, we infer that, up to a subsequence, $\Omega_i$ converge in the Hausdorff metric to a limiting compact convex set $\Omega_\infty$. The existence of the minimizer is then readily established by combining the continuity of the perimeter and of the energy $E_\phi$, which are guaranteed thanks to Theorem \ref{thm:continuity_energy}.
