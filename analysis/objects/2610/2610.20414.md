<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:fixed-host}
With the notation above, for every fixed $r\ge3$ there are constants
$A_r,B_r,R_r<\infty$ with the following property.
Let $\Gamma=\Gamma_n$ be a sequence of simple $r$-uniform
hypergraphs and let $D=D_n\to\infty$.  Put
\[
 \delta_n:=\max_{v\in V(\Gamma)}
       \left|\frac{d_v}{D}-1\right|,
 \qquad
 T_D:=\frac{\log(1+(r-1)D)}{r-1}.
\]
Suppose that for fixed $q>2$ and $C,L,K<\infty$ the following
conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \emph{Size and stability:}
\[
 N\le D^K,
 \qquad
 \|e^{-(r-1)t\mathsf T}\|_{L^q(\pi)\to L^q(\pi)}
 \le C(1+t)^L\quad(0\le t\le T_D).
\]
\item \emph{Near-regularity and small stability error:}
$\delta_n\to0$ and
\[
 N^{1/q}\left(\delta_n+\frac{\log D}{D}\right)
 (1+\log D)^{2L+2}=o(1).
\]
\item \emph{Small collision errors:}
\[
 D^{A_r}\chi_{R_r}(\Gamma)+\frac{D^{B_r}}{M}
 =o\!\left(D^{-2/(r-1)}\right).
\]
\end{enumerate}
Then, writing $Q_D(1)=(1+(r-1)D)^{-1/(r-1)}$, we have
\[
 \mathbb E\left[\left(\frac{Z_\Gamma}{NQ_D(1)}-1\right)^2\right]=o(1).
\]
In particular, $Z_\Gamma/(NQ_D(1))\to1$ in probability.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:finite-query}
Every call $\mathsf{Test}(S,t;P)$, with $P=\bot$ or $U_P=t$,
returns true exactly when all resources in $S$ are free before $t$.
In particular, $X_v=\mathsf{Test}(\{v\},1;\bot)$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Induct on the number of  candidate priorities below $t$;
every recursive call has a smaller threshold.  If all resources in
$S$ are free before $t$, a true recursive call at an entry $(u,A)$
with $U_A<t$ would say, by induction, that the other resources of $A$
are free before $U_A$.  Since $u$ is free as well, $A$ would have been
accepted, a contradiction.  Thus the test returns true.

Conversely, if a resource of $S$ is covered before $t$, choose an
accepted candidate $A$ meeting $S$ with $U_A<t$ and $u\in A\cap S$.
The entry $(u,A)$ is in the scanner: an omitted incoming candidate
has priority $t$, not less than $t$.  All resources of
$A\setminus\{u\}$ were free before $U_A$, so induction says that
this entry's recursive call returns true.  The test therefore returns
false.  The argument also covers repeated entries of one candidate
in a merged scanner.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:finite-determination}
For every typed incidence cover, every priority horizon $x\in[0,1]$, and every
fixed finite set of nodes, all decisions concerning those nodes before time
$x$ are determined by a finite random neighbourhood almost surely.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Each candidate occurrence has at most $B$ candidate grandchildren,
for some finite bound $B$.  A prescribed chain of $\ell+1$ candidate
occurrences can be a dependency chain only if their independent
priorities decrease, with probability $1/(\ell+1)!$.  There are at
most $B^\ell$ such chains starting at any fixed occurrence, and
$B^\ell/(\ell+1)!\to0$.  Thus every fixed finite collection of decisions has a finite
dependency neighbourhood almost surely.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:candidate-first}
Within a complete-scanner query, no resource type can be registered
twice before a candidate type has been exposed twice.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Suppose a second occurrence of resource type $u$ is born from an
invoked candidate occurrence of type $A$.  The complete scanner of
the earlier $u$ has already been exposed: every batch of forward
resources has its scanners exposed before any further candidate is
invoked, and distinct forward resources of one candidate have
distinct types.  Let $P$ be the earlier occurrence's incoming
candidate type, or $\bot$ if it is the root.  If $A\ne P$, the
earlier scanner exposed an occurrence of type $A$; if $A=P$, the
incoming candidate of the earlier occurrence was already exposed.
In the first case this earlier occurrence is distinct from the
one now invoked: a candidate entered through type $u$ cannot
produce another forward resource of type $u$.  In the second case
the incoming candidate is likewise a distinct occurrence.  Thus
type $A$ repeats before the second birth of $u$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:direct-coupling}
For each fixed resource $v$, the finite-query answer $X_v$ and the
independent-cover answer $Y_v$ can be coupled so that
\[
 \mathbb P(X_v\ne Y_v)\le\mathbb P(\mathcal C_v).
\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Sample the actual priorities $(U_A:A\in E(\Gamma))$ independently
and uniformly.  Explore $\mathcal T_v$ in the prescribed scanner
order.  When an occurrence of  candidate type $A$ is exposed
for the first time, give it priority $U_A$; for every later exposed
occurrence of type $A$, use a fresh independent uniform priority.
Complete the unused occurrence priorities independently.

This produces the correct independent-cover law.  Immediately
before each exposure, the type to be exposed is determined by the
previously revealed information and the fixed scanner order.  If its
type is new, $U_A$ is still unread and independent of that history;
otherwise the assigned priority is fresh.  Induction over exposures
therefore gives independent uniform priorities to all cover
occurrences, without conditioning on $\mathcal C_v^c$.

On $\mathcal C_v^c$, every exposed cover priority equals the actual
priority of its type.  Lemma~\ref{lem:candidate-first} also makes
all registered resource types distinct.  Consequently the actual
and cover queries encounter the same complete scanners and use the
same priorities.  Their recursive answers agree by induction over
the almost surely finite dependency neighbourhood of
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-determination}.  Hence $X_v=Y_v$ on
$\mathcal C_v^c$, proving the bound.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:cavity-equations}
For every directed incidence $A\to v$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cavity-branch-derivative}
 f'_{A\to v}(x)
 =-
 \prod_{u\in A\setminus\{v\}}q_{u\to A}(x).
\end{equation}
Consequently, defining $H_A(x):=\prod_{u\in A}q_{u\to A}(x)$,
we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cavity-root-derivative}
 q'_v(x)=-\sum_{A\ni v}H_A(x).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $f_{A\to v}^{[h]}$ denote the branch message with $h$ remaining
candidate generations and free boundary resources.  Write
$q_{u\to A}^{[h-1]}$ for its child cavity messages.  The branch
through $A$ consumes $v$ at priority $t$ if and only if the other
resource children are free immediately before $t$.  Their subtrees are
disjoint, so for $h\ge1$,
\[
 f_{A\to v}^{[h]}(x)
 =1-\int_0^x
 \prod_{u\in A\setminus\{v\}}q_{u\to A}^{[h-1]}(t)\,dt.
\]
The child messages have index $h-1$, not $h$.  Almost-sure finite
determination gives pointwise convergence of these probabilities; bounded
convergence therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cavity-integral}
 f_{A\to v}(x)
 =1-\int_0^x
 \prod_{u\in A\setminus\{v\}}q_{u\to A}(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}
The branch messages are continuous by \eqref{eq:cavity-integral}, as are their finite products
$q_{u\to A}$.  We may therefore differentiate \eqref{eq:cavity-integral}, obtaining \eqref{eq:cavity-branch-derivative}.
Differentiating \eqref{eq:cavity-root-survival} for the limiting messages gives
\[
 q'_v
 =
 \sum_{A\ni v}f'_{A\to v}
 \prod_{C\ni v,\,C\ne A}f_{C\to v}
 =-
 \sum_{A\ni v}
 q_{v\to A}
 \prod_{u\in A\setminus\{v\}}q_{u\to A},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:cavity-root-derivative}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:cavity-stability}
Fix $r\ge3$, $q>2$, and $C,L,K<\infty$.
Let $\Gamma=\Gamma_n$ be simple $r$-uniform hypergraphs with
$N\le D^K$, $D\to\infty$ and $\delta=o(1)$.  Suppose
\[
 \left\|e^{-(r-1)t\mathsf T}\right\|_{L^q(\pi)\to L^q(\pi)}
 \le C(1+t)^L\qquad(0\le t\le T_D),
\]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bootstrap-assumption}
 N^{1/q}\left(\delta+\frac{\log D}{D}\right)
 (1+\log D)^{2L+2}=o(1).
\end{equation}
Then, uniformly over $v,A\ni v$ and $0\le x\le1$,
\begin{align}
 q_v(x)&=(1+o(1))Q_D(x),\label{eq:cover-survival-asymptotic}\\
 f_{A\to v}(x)&=1-O_r\!\left(\frac{\log D}{D}\right).
 \label{eq:message-near-one}
\end{align}

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:message-estimate}
There is $h_0=h_0(r)>0$ such that the following holds.  
Suppose that $\max_v|y_v(x)|\le h_0$ on an interval $0\le x\le x_0$.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:message-correction}
 1-f_{A\to v}(x)

\end{equation}

       +C_3N^{1/q}Y(\tau)^2\right].
\end{equation}

\[

\[
 Y(\tau)\le C_2(1+T_D)^{L+1}

\]
The bracket is $1+o(1)$ by \eqref{eq:bootstrap-assumption}.

The residual estimate has only been used under its hypothesis on $[0,t]$,
If either bootstrap bound failed before $T_D$,

$m=v(F)$, $r=e(F)$, and $\rho_F=(r-1)/(m-k)$.  Define

\]

Call a matrix on $\Omega_s$ \emph{intersection-invariant} if its
$(S,T)$ entry depends only on $|S\cap T|$.  Such matrices form a
vector space of dimension at most $s+1$.  Their products are
intersection-invariant, since permuting $[n]$ merely relabels
the summation variable.  They are also symmetric; hence their
products are symmetric, so any two such matrices commute.

Both $\overline{\mathsf R}_s$ and
$\overline{\mathsf D\mathsf U}$ are intersection-invariant,
Markov and positive semidefinite on the uniform slice: each is
a conditional averaging map followed by its adjoint, or a
convex combination of such maps.  Thus they commute, and
$H:=\overline{\mathsf R}_s\,\overline{\mathsf D\mathsf U}$
is symmetric, positive semidefinite and Markov.  Its eigenvalues
therefore lie in $[0,1]$.

Every power of $H$ is intersection-invariant, so $H$ has at most
$s+1$ distinct eigenvalues $\theta_0,\ldots,\theta_{d-1}$.
Newton interpolation of $f(x)=e^{-ux}$ at these nodes gives
\[
 e^{-uH}
 =\sum_{h=0}^{d-1}f[\theta_0,\ldots,\theta_h]
   \prod_{i=0}^{h-1}(H-\theta_i I).
\]
The divided-difference mean-value theorem gives
$|f[\theta_0,\ldots,\theta_h]|\le u^h/h!$ on $[0,1]$.
Since $H$ is Markov, $\|H-\theta_i I\|_{q\to q}\le2$.
Taking norms yields
\[
 \|e^{-uH}\|_{q\to q}
 \le\sum_{h=0}^{d-1}\frac{(2u)^h}{h!}
 \le\sum_{h=0}^s\frac{(2u)^h}{h!}
 \le(1+2u)^s,
\]
which is (C).

$\nu_J$ is pointwise $1+O(n^{-c})$ times uniform on the
$m_J=\binom{n-\ell}{s-\ell}$ states containing $J$.
For a starting state $S_0$, the possible generic endpoints are
\[
 G_{S_0}=\{S_L\supset J:S_0\cap S_L=J\}.
\]
They number $\binom{n-s}{s-\ell}=(1+O_s(n^{-1}))m_J$.

Expand $(\mathsf L_J^{(\ell)})^L(S_0,S_L)$ over
intermediate states and chosen resource edges.  A trajectory is
generic precisely when it realises
\eqref{eq:shadow-chain-definition} injectively.  At the first
nongeneric step, the selected $k$-edge $e\supset S$ contains
some previously exposed vertex $x\notin S$.
Conditioning on the prescribed overlap $S\cap T=J$ does not
change the choice of $e$, since each $e\supset S$ allows
$\binom{k-s}{s-\ell}$ choices of $T$.  The enlarged-root bound
\eqref{eq:JK-enlarged-root-count}, applied to a one-edge template
rooted at $S$ with the extra prescription $x$, gives
\[
 \mathbb P(x\in e\mid S,J)
 \le(1+\log n)^{O_F(1)}
 \bigl(n^{-1}+(n^{k-s}p_\star)^{-1}\bigr)\le n^{-c}.
\]
Indeed, the smallest enlarged-root trajectory is
$\min\{n,n^{k-s}p_\star\}$, and the denominator is
$\Theta_F(n^{k-s}p_\star)$ by the one-edge handoff count.
There are only $O_{k,s,L}(1)$ earlier vertices and steps, so
nongeneric trajectories have total row mass $n^{-c}$.

For fixed $S_0,S_L\in G_{S_0}$, every compatible injection of
the labelled endpoint root has the same number of choices,
independent of $S_0,S_L$; it simply orders the vertices of
$J$, $S_0\setminus J$ and $S_L\setminus J$.  Moreover, every
generic ordered trajectory corresponds to the same number of
labelled embeddings of \eqref{eq:shadow-chain-definition},
independent of its endpoints: the labels order the intermediate
$U_i$ and the private $P_i$.  The rooted chain count above gives
the common trajectory $n^{X_L}p_\star^L$, with relative error
$n^{-c}$.  Hence the number of generic ordered trajectories
from any $S_0$ to any $S_L\in G_{S_0}$ is
$(1+O(n^{-c}))a_{n,\ell,L}$ for one positive number
$a_{n,\ell,L}$ independent of the endpoints.

In the expansion of \eqref{eq:lower-shadow-kernel}, each step
has weight
\[
 \frac{d_e}{\binom{k-s}{s-\ell}\sum_{g\supset S}d_g}
 =\frac{1+O(n^{-c})}
 {\binom{k-s}{s-\ell}\binom{n-s}{k-s}p_\star},
\]
by \eqref{eq:resource-degrees} and
the one-edge handoff count.  The generic contribution to
the $L$-step endpoint transition is therefore
$(1+O(n^{-c}))b_{n,\ell,L}$ on $G_{S_0}$, independent of
$S_0,S_L$, and zero outside $G_{S_0}$.

The same row-sum argument as in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre-mixing} now makes the common generic
transition weight $(1+O(n^{-c}))/m_J$.
The exceptional mass is $O(n^{-c})$, so the rows of
$(\mathsf L_J^{(\ell)})^L$ differ from $\nu_J$ in total variation
by $O(n^{-c})$. This gives an $L^\infty$ bound, so by self-adjointness we deduce
\[
 \|\mathsf L_J^{(\ell)}-\Pi_J^{(s)}\|_{L^2(\nu_J)\to L^2(\nu_J)}
 \le n^{-c/L}.
\]
This proves Lemma~\ref{lem:shadow-fibre}, and hence completes
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:resource-semigroup}.

Fix $R\ge4$.  A simple incidence cycle of length $2\ell\le R$
in $\Gamma_\star$ alternates between distinct candidates and distinct
resources.  Its candidates are therefore distinct copies
$F_1,\ldots,F_\ell$ of $F$, while its resources are distinct
$k$-edges $e_1,\ldots,e_\ell$ with
$e_i\in E(F_i)\cap E(F_{i+1})$ (cyclic indices).  Hence the
union $S=\bigcup_{i=1}^\ell F_i$ is exactly a self-avoiding cyclic
$F$-union in the sense defined above, including when $\ell=2$ or when
there are further overlaps.  It has at most $mR/2$ ordinary vertices.
There are finitely many isomorphism types of such unions, depending
only on $F,R$, and for each embedded union only $O_{F,R}(1)$ choices of
the ordered candidates, shared resources, incidence cycle and a candidate
on that cycle.  Consequently the number of candidates on short cycles satisfies
the explicit finite-type bound
\[
 M\chi_R(\Gamma_\star)
 \le O_{F,R}(1)
 \sum_{\substack{S\text{ a cyclic }F\text{-union type}\\

 \#\{S\hookrightarrow H_\star\}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:JK-handoff}
Fix $F$ and sufficiently small $\eta>0$.  For every sufficiently small
$\delta_{\rm JK}>0$ depending only on $F,\eta$, there is an event
$\mathcal G_\star$ with
\[
 \mathbb P(\mathcal G_\star^c)
 \le\exp\{-(\log n)^{4/3}\}
\]
such that the following hold simultaneously for \emph{all} rooted
$k$-templates of order at most $\eta^{-4}$ satisfying the hypotheses
in the respective parts, and for \emph{all} admissible prescribed root
injections.  In parts (iii) and (iv), $C_{A,I}$ and $C_{A,J}$ denote
fixed template-dependent exponents.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item For every role edge $f\in E(F)$, every resource
$e\in E(H_\star)$, and every bijection $\sigma:f\to e$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rooted-F-count}
 \Phi_{F,\sigma}
 =\left(1+n^{-\Omega_{F,\eta}(1)}\right)n^ap_\star^b.
\end{equation}

\item Let $(A,I)$ be a balanced template with
$|V(A)|\le\eta^{-4}$.  If
\(
 i^\star<i_{A,I}^{\delta_{\rm JK}^{1/2}},
\)
then for all root embeddings $\psi$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:JK-balanced-count}
 \Phi_{A,\psi}
 =\left(1+O(\zeta(i^\star)^{\delta_{\rm JK}})\right)
 \widehat\phi_{A,I}(i^\star).
\end{equation}

\item For every strictly balanced template $(A,I)$ with
$|V(A)|\le\eta^{-4}$ we have
\[
 \Phi_{A,\psi}
 \le(1+\log n)^{C_{A,I}}
 \max\{1,\widehat\phi_{A,I}(i^\star)\}.
\]

\item Let $I\subsetneq J\subseteq V(A)$ and
$|V(A)|\le\eta^{-4}$.  Among subtemplates $(B,I)\subseteq(A,I)$ whose vertex
set contains $J$, choose one minimising $\widehat\phi_{B,I}(i^\star)$.  Then
for all injections $\psi:J\hookrightarrow V(H_\star)$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:JK-enlarged-root-count}
 \Phi_{A,\psi}
 \le(1+\log n)^{C_{A,J}}
 \frac{\widehat\phi_{A,I}(i^\star)}
 {\widehat\phi_{B,I}(i^\star)}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

Let $S$ be a cyclic $F$-union. Lemma~12.5 in \cite{JoosKuhn} gives a
resource edge $e$ with $\rho_{S,e}>\rho=b/a$.  The integer surplus is
therefore
\[
 a(e(S)-1)-b(v(S)-k)\ge1,
 \qquad
 v(S)-e(S)/\rho\le k-1/\rho-1/b.
\]
The exponent $k-1/\rho-1/b$ is positive: strict balance excludes a
matching, so some two role edges meet and $\rho\ge1/(k-1)$, giving
$k-1/\rho-1/b\ge1-1/b>0$.

By \cite[Lemmas~12.2 and~12.3]{JoosKuhn}, either $(S,\varnothing)$
is strictly balanced or it contains a proper unrooted subtemplate $B$
that is strictly balanced with $e(B)/v(B)\ge\rho$, chosen with
maximum density and minimum vertex count.  In the second case every
extension root $J\supseteq V(B)$ has density $\rho_{S,J}\ge\rho$.

In the first case the strictly balanced handoff bound from
Proposition~\ref{prop:JK-handoff}(iii) gives
\[
 \#\{S\hookrightarrow H_\star\}
 \le(1+\log n)^{O_{F,S}(1)}n^{v(S)}p_\star^{e(S)}
 \le(1+\log n)^{O_{F,S}(1)}
     n^{k-1/\rho-1/b+O_{F,S}(\eta)}.
\]
In the second case the same handoff bound counts $B$ in at most
$(1+\log n)^{O_{F,S}(1)}n^{O_{F,S}(\eta)}$ ways.  
By \cite[Lemma~12.6]{JoosKuhn}, using the upper bound
in Proposition~\ref{prop:JK-handoff}(iii), gives at most another
$(1+\log n)^{O_{F,S}(1)}n^{O_{F,S}(\eta)}$ completions:
the density condition $\rho_{S,J}\ge\rho$ is retained when the root
is enlarged during that induction.
