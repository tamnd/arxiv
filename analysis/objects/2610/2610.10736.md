<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:finite-range-core-geometry}
There are constants \(c_J,C_J>0\) such that, for every \(L\geq3\),
\(G_L^J\) and every one-vertex deletion of it are connected and
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{gap}(K_{G_L^J})&\geq c_JL^{-2},
 \label{eq:core-volume-gap}\\
 \max_{x,y\in B_L}R_{\rm eff}^{G_L^J}(x,y)
 &\leq C_J\log(2L).
 \label{eq:core-resistance}
\end{align}
Moreover, for every \(a\in B_L\), the punctured graph
\(G_L^J\setminus\{a\}\) is connected and
\begin{equation}
 \max_{x,y\in B_L\setminus\{a\}}
 R_{\rm eff}^{G_L^J\setminus\{a\}}(x,y)
 \leq C_J\log(2L).
 \label{eq:core-punctured-resistance}
\end{equation}
The same conclusions hold for the decorated graph, with constants also
depending on \(r\).
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let \(K_L^{\rm nn}\) be the unit-conductance nearest-neighbour square
Laplacian.  The backbone in \eqref{eq:backbone-hypothesis} gives the form
inequality
\begin{equation}
 K_{G_L^J}\geq j_{\rm bb}K_L^{\rm nn}.
 \label{eq:core-path-form-comparison}
\end{equation}
The standard square Poincar\'e inequality therefore proves
\eqref{eq:core-volume-gap}.  The graph contains the nearest-neighbour
square with conductance \(j_{\rm bb}\), so Rayleigh monotonicity and the
standard dyadic-shell unit flow give \eqref{eq:core-resistance}.

Fix \(a\in B_L\).  The nearest-neighbour square with \(a\) deleted is
connected for \(L\geq3\).  To retain the resistance bound, take a
nearest-neighbour unit flow between two vertices and remove its edges
incident with \(a\).  The currents entering the at most four neighbours
of \(a\) sum to zero.  Transport the positive currents to the negative
currents along paths of length at most four in the surrounding
\(3\)-by-\(3\) square, intersected with \(B_L\) at a side or corner.
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the energy of this local detour by a fixed multiple
of the energy on the removed incident edges.  The resulting flow avoids
\(a\) and has energy at most \(C\log(2L)\).  Rayleigh monotonicity then
proves \eqref{eq:core-punctured-resistance} for \(G_L^J\).

For the decorated graph let \(P\) be the projection onto the uniform
fibre vector and \(Q=I-P\).  Its one-particle Laplacian is
\begin{equation}
 K_{G_L^J}\otimes P+M_{d_J}\otimes Q,
 \qquad d_J(x)=\sum_{y\in B_L}J(y-x)\geq2j_{\rm bb}.
 \label{eq:decorated-backbone-split}
\end{equation}
Here \(M_d\) denotes multiplication by \(d\). This proves the decorated gap.  For resistance, fan a unit current from
one fibre vertex uniformly through the fibres over a nearest-neighbouring
base vertex, route the resulting base current through the preceding
flows, and reverse the fan at the endpoint.  The two fans have bounded
energy and the routed part has energy \(O_{J,r}(\log L)\).  After deleting
one decorated vertex, use the punctured base flow when \(r=1\).  When
\(r\geq2\), redistribute the current over the remaining \(r-1\) fibres
above that base point.  Concretely, choose one nearest-neighbouring base
vertex and replace every current through the deleted fibre by two-edge
paths through its surviving fibres.  The conductances on these paths are
at least \(j_{\rm bb}/r\).  At most \(r\) paths are used, so
Cauchy--Schwarz increases their energy by only an \(r\)-dependent factor.
This proves the punctured decorated bound.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:resistance-point-trace}
For every \(g:V\to\mathcal K\) and \(x\in V\),
\begin{equation}
 \|g(x)\|_{\mathcal K}^2
 \leq\frac2n\sum_{y\in V}\|g(y)\|_{\mathcal K}^2
      +2\rho(G)\mathcal E_G(g).
 \label{eq:resistance-point-trace}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let \(\overline g=n^{-1}\sum_yg(y)\).  Jensen's inequality gives
\(\|\overline g\|^2\leq n^{-1}\sum_y\|g(y)\|^2\).  The variational
definition of resistance, applied after scalar projection onto an
arbitrary unit vector of \(\mathcal K\), gives
\(\|g(x)-g(y)\|^2\leq R_{\rm eff}^G(x,y)\mathcal E_G(g)\).
Average this inequality over \(y\) and use
\(\|g(x)\|^2\leq2\|\overline g\|^2+
2\|g(x)-\overline g\|^2\).

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:abstract-graph-collision}
For every \(1\leq k\leq n/2\),
\begin{align}
 0\leq N_{G,k}
 \leq{}&Ck^2\left(\frac1{n\lambda}+\rho(G)\right)
 \notag\\[-1mm]
 &\times\left[1+C\Delta\left(
       \frac{k^2}{n\lambda}+k\rho_\circ(G)\right)\right]^2
 H_{G,k}^2.
 \label{eq:abstract-graph-collision}
\end{align}
The constant \(C\) is numerical, and the inequality is understood in the
quadratic-form sense.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For \(i<j\), denote by \(q_{ij}(F)\) the part of the product Dirichlet
form carried by product edges having an endpoint on
\(\{x_i=x_j\}\).  We claim that
\begin{equation}
 q_{ij}(F)\leq C\left(\frac1{n\lambda}+\rho(G)\right)
 \|L_0F\|^2.
 \label{eq:single-diagonal-point-trace}
\end{equation}
Consider first an edge in coordinate \(i\), orient its base edge
\(e=(x,x')\) from the diagonal endpoint, freeze all other variables, and
apply Lemma~\ref{lem:resistance-point-trace} to
\begin{equation}
 g(y):=\sqrt{c_{xx'}}
 \bigl[F(\ldots,x',\ldots,y,\ldots)
       -F(\ldots,x,\ldots,y,\ldots)\bigr].
 \label{eq:normal-point-trace-function}
\end{equation}
After summation, the mean term is at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac Cn\langle F,K_iF\rangle
 \leq\frac C{n\lambda}\|L_0F\|^2,
 \label{eq:normal-point-trace-mean}
\end{equation}
because \(K_i\leq\lambda^{-1}K_i^2\leq
\lambda^{-1}L_0^2\).  The fluctuation term is
\begin{equation}
 C\rho(G)\langle F,K_iK_jF\rangle
 \leq\frac{C\rho(G)}4\|L_0F\|^2.
 \label{eq:normal-point-trace-fluctuation}
\end{equation}
The channel in coordinate \(j\) is identical.  For tangential edges one
must sum the spectator coordinates before estimating.  With
\begin{equation}
 A_{\rm sp}:=\sum_{\ell\ne i,j}K_\ell,
 \label{eq:spectator-operator}
\end{equation}
their total mean term is bounded as in
\eqref{eq:normal-point-trace-mean}, while their fluctuation term is
\begin{equation}
 C\rho(G)\langle F,K_jA_{\rm sp}F\rangle
 \leq\frac{C\rho(G)}4
 \langle F,(K_j+A_{\rm sp})^2F\rangle
 \leq\frac{C\rho(G)}4\|L_0F\|^2.
 \label{eq:tangential-point-trace-fluctuation}
\end{equation}
This aggregation is what prevents an extra factor \(k\).  Equations
\eqref{eq:normal-point-trace-mean}--
\eqref{eq:tangential-point-trace-fluctuation} prove
\eqref{eq:single-diagonal-point-trace}.  Summing over fewer than \(k^2/2\)
pairs gives
\begin{equation}
 q_{\rm bad}(F)\leq a_G\|L_0F\|^2,
 \qquad
 a_G:=Ck^2\left(\frac1{n\lambda}+\rho(G)\right).
 \label{eq:abstract-bad-edge-bound}
\end{equation}

We next prove the first-order estimate, including its normalization.  Let
\(\nu_k\) be the uniform probability measure on the \(k\)-subsets of
\(V\), and use the corresponding normalized inner product.  Since this
only multiplies the counting inner product by a scalar, the resulting
operator inequalities are unchanged.  Put \(N_k=\binom nk\),
\(q=n-k+1\), and, for \(b\in A\), set \(B=A\setminus\{b\}\) and
\begin{equation}
 \overline f_B:=\frac1q\sum_{y\notin B}f(B\cup\{y\}).
 \label{eq:abstract-contact-average}
\end{equation}
Jensen's inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 |f(A)|^2\leq2|\overline f_B|^2
 +\frac2q\sum_{y\notin B}
 |f(A)-f(B\cup\{y\})|^2.
 \label{eq:abstract-contact-jensen}
\end{equation}
For \(d_A(b):=\sum_{a\in A}c_{ab}\), average
\eqref{eq:abstract-contact-jensen} with weight \(d_A(b)\) over
\(A\) and \(b\in A\).  Denote by \(M\) and \(F\) the averaged
contributions of the mean and fluctuation terms in
\eqref{eq:abstract-contact-jensen}, respectively.  This yields
\begin{equation}
 \langle f,R_{G,k}f\rangle_{\nu_k}\leq2M+2F.
 \label{eq:abstract-contact-split}
\end{equation}
The change of variables \(A=B\cup\{b\}\), followed by Jensen once
more, gives
\begin{align}
 M
 &\leq\frac1{N_kq}
 \sum_{|B|=k-1}\left(\sum_{b\notin B}\sum_{a\in B}c_{ab}\right)
 \sum_{y\notin B}|f(B\cup\{y\})|^2\notag\\
 &\leq\frac{\Delta k(k-1)}q\|f\|_{\nu_k}^2
 \leq\frac{2\Delta k^2}{n}\|f\|_{\nu_k}^2.
 \label{eq:abstract-contact-mean}
\end{align}

For the fluctuation term use the occupied neighbour \(a\) of \(b\) as
an anchor.  Conditional on \(a\in A\), the set
\(\zeta=A\setminus\{a\}\) is uniform among the \((k-1)\)-subsets of
\(V\setminus\{a\}\).  Denote this law by \(\nu_{k-1}^{(a)}\), put
\(f_a(\zeta)=f(\zeta\cup\{a\})\), and set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_a(f_a):=
 \langle f_a,H_{G\setminus\{a\},k-1}f_a\rangle_{\nu_{k-1}^{(a)}}.
 \label{eq:abstract-conditioned-energy}
\end{equation}
Directly rearranging the fluctuation sum in
\eqref{eq:abstract-contact-split} gives
\begin{align}
 F={}&\frac{k}{nq}\sum_{a\in V}\sum_{b\ne a}c_{ab}
 \sum_{y\ne a}\mathbb E_{\nu_{k-1}^{(a)}}\!\left[
 \1_{\{b\in\zeta,\ y\notin\zeta\}}
 \left|f_a(\zeta\setminus\{b\}\cup\{y\})-f_a(\zeta)\right|^2
 \right].
 \label{eq:abstract-contact-conditioned}
\end{align}
Chen's weighted moving-particle lemma \cite{Chen2017}
bounds the expectation in \eqref{eq:abstract-contact-conditioned} by
\(R_{\rm eff}^{G\setminus\{a\}}(b,y)\mathcal E_a(f_a)\).  Indeed, swap invariance
identifies the one-orientation indicator expectation in
\eqref{eq:abstract-contact-conditioned} with one half of the full swap
expectation in Chen's normalization, so no extra factor is present.  Hence,
using
\(q\geq n/2\) and \(\sum_bc_{ab}\leq\Delta\),
\begin{equation}
 F\leq C\frac{k\Delta\rho_\circ(G)}n
       \sum_{a\in V}\mathcal E_a(f_a).
 \label{eq:abstract-contact-fluctuation}
\end{equation}
Every allowed exclusion move is present in the conditioned energy for
exactly \(k-1\) occupied anchors.  Since
\(\binom nk/\binom{n-1}{k-1}=n/k\), the normalized forms satisfy the
exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \frac{k}{n}\sum_{a\in V}\mathcal E_a(f_a)
 =(k-1)\langle f,H_{G,k}f\rangle_{\nu_k}.
 \label{eq:anchored-energy-identity}
\end{equation}

\eqref{eq:abstract-contact-mean},
\eqref{eq:abstract-contact-fluctuation}, and
\eqref{eq:anchored-energy-identity} proves
\begin{equation}
 \langle f,R_{G,k}f\rangle_{\nu_k}
 \leq C\Delta\frac{k^2}{n}\|f\|_{\nu_k}^2
      +C\Delta k\rho_\circ(G)
       \langle f,H_{G,k}f\rangle_{\nu_k}.
 \label{eq:abstract-contact-bound}
\end{equation}

The exclusion gap equals \(\lambda\): the lower bound follows from the
interchange-process theorem of Caputo, Liggett and Richthammer
\cite{CaputoLiggettRichthammer2010}, and the reverse bound is obtained by
lifting a one-particle eigenfunction.  Since \(N_{G,k}\leq R_{G,k}\),
\eqref{eq:abstract-contact-bound} therefore implies, off the constants,
\begin{equation}
 N_{G,k}\leq b_GH_{G,k},\qquad
 b_G:=C\Delta\left(\frac{k^2}{n\lambda}
                         +k\rho_\circ(G)\right).
 \label{eq:abstract-first-order-collision}
\end{equation}

Apply \eqref{eq:abstract-bad-edge-bound} to the harmonic extension of
\(f\).  The identities \eqref{eq:extension-energy}--
\eqref{eq:collision-as-bad-energy} are purely graph-theoretic and give
\begin{equation}
 N_{G,k}\leq a_G(H_{G,k}+N_{G,k})^2.
 \label{eq:abstract-preabsorbed-collision}
\end{equation}
Put \(\mathcal K=H_{G,k}^{-1/2}N_{G,k}H_{G,k}^{-1/2}\) off the constants.
Then \(0\leq\mathcal K\leq b_G\), and congruence of
\eqref{eq:abstract-preabsorbed-collision}, followed by congruence with
\((I+\mathcal K)^{-1}\), yields
\(\mathcal K(I+\mathcal K)^{-2}\leq a_GH_{G,k}\).
The scalar inequality
\(x\leq(1+b_G)^2x(1+x)^{-2}\) for \(0\leq x\leq b_G\)
proves \eqref{eq:abstract-graph-collision}.  Both sides vanish on the
constants, so the result holds on the full space.

Let \(p_{L,u}^N\), \(p_{L,u}^D\), and \(p_{\infty,u}\) denote the
censored, killed, and full-lattice heat kernels, respectively.  Choose
\begin{equation}
 D>2\sup_{L,x}\sum_{y\in B_L}J(y-x),
 \qquad P_L:=I-D^{-1}K_{J,L}^N.
 \label{eq:finite-range-uniformization}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, add at \(x\) a self-loop of weight
\(D-\sum_{y\in B_L}J(y-x)\).  Then \(P_L\) is a lazy symmetric Markov
kernel with invariant counting measure.  The coordinate nearest-neighbour
conductances are bounded below by \(j_{\rm bb}>0\), all jumps have length
at most \(R_J\), and hence the graph metric is uniformly comparable with
the lattice metric.  Boundary balls in \(B_L\) have uniform volume
doubling.  The nearest-neighbour Poincar\'e inequality, together with
\(K_{J,L}^N\geq j_{\rm bb}K_L^{\rm nn}\), gives the scale-invariant
Poincar\'e inequality on these balls.  Delmotte's theorem
\cite{Delmotte1999}, applied to \(P_L\) and followed by Poissonization,
therefore gives, for \(1\leq v\leq L^2\),
\begin{equation}
 p_{L,v}^N(x,y)\leq C_J\left[
 v^{-1}e^{-c_J\min\{|x-y|^2/v,|x-y|\}}+L^{-2}\right].
 \label{eq:finite-range-gaussian-bound}
\end{equation}
The same construction on \(\mathbb Z^2\) gives the full-lattice estimate without
the \(L^{-2}\) term, and domination by the full-lattice walk gives the
killed estimate.

We now prove the boundary comparison.  Realize the full-lattice walk by
Poisson clocks for the jumps in \(\mathcal S_J\).  The censored walk uses
the same clocks but rejects a jump leaving \(B_L\).  The two paths agree
until the first such clock ring.  Set
\begin{equation}
 \delta_{J,L}(x):=\max\{0,\operatorname{dist}_\infty
 (x,\mathbb Z^2\setminus B_L)-R_J\}.
 \label{eq:finite-range-boundary-distance}
\end{equation}
Symmetry and finite range imply, for every coordinate direction and
\(0\leq\theta\leq1\),
\begin{equation}
 \sum_zJ(z)(e^{\theta z_\nu}-1)\leq C_J\theta^2.
 \label{eq:finite-range-exponential-moment}
\end{equation}
Optimize Doob's inequality for the exponential supermartingale at
\begin{equation*}
 \theta=\min\{\delta_{J,L}(x)/(2C_Ju),1\}.
\end{equation*}
This gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb P_x(\text{a boundary jump is attempted before }u)\\
 &\qquad\leq C_J\exp\!\left[-c_J\min\left\{
 \frac{\delta_{J,L}(x)^2}{u},\delta_{J,L}(x)\right\}\right].
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:finite-range-exit-probability}
\end{equation}

Let \(X\) be the full-lattice walk and \(Y\) the censored walk in this
common-clock construction, and let \(T\) be the first clock ring that
would leave \(B_L\).  Regard rejected rings of \(Y\) as marks.  The
contributions of paths with \(T>u\) agree, and hence
\begin{align}
 |p_{L,u}^N(x,x)-p_{\infty,u}(0,0)|
 \leq{}&\mathbb P_x(Y_u=x,T\leq u)
       +\mathbb P_x(X_u=x,T\leq u).
 \label{eq:finite-range-coupling-difference}
\end{align}
For either term, write \(p_v\) for the transition kernel of the
corresponding walk.  The contribution with \(T\leq u/2\) is, by the strong
Markov property and \eqref{eq:finite-range-gaussian-bound}, at most
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_x(T\leq u/2)\sup_{v\geq u/2,z}p_v(z,x)
 \leq\frac{C_J}{u}\mathbb P_x(T\leq u/2).
 \label{eq:finite-range-early-boundary-event}
\end{equation}
For \(1\leq u\leq2\), the final inequality follows from \(p_v\leq1\)
after enlarging \(C_J\). Otherwise it follows from
\eqref{eq:finite-range-gaussian-bound}, with \(L^{-2}\leq2/u\).
Reversal of diagonal loops bounds the contribution of an event in
\([u/2,u]\) by the same expression.  For \(Y\), this is reversal of the
marked rejected-clock path measure. For \(X\), it is ordinary
reversibility.  Before \(T\) the two constructions coincide, so
\(\mathbb P_x(T\leq u/2)\) is bounded by
\eqref{eq:finite-range-exit-probability}.  Thus, for
\(\mathrm{bc}\in\{N,D\}\) and \(1\leq u\leq L^2\),
\begin{equation}
 \left|p_{L,u}^{\mathrm{bc}}(x,x)-p_{\infty,u}(0,0)\right|
 \leq\frac{C_J}{u}
 \exp\!\left[-c_J\min\left\{
 \frac{\delta_{J,L}(x)^2}{u},\delta_{J,L}(x)\right\}\right].
 \label{eq:not-feeling-boundary}
\end{equation}
Indeed, for the killed kernel,
\begin{equation}
 p_{\infty,u}(0,0)-p_{L,u}^D(x,x)
 =\mathbb P_x(X_u=x,T\leq u),
 \label{eq:finite-range-killed-difference}
\end{equation}
so the same early/late argument also gives the one-sided sign.  The second
term in the minimum in \eqref{eq:not-feeling-boundary} is the bounded-jump
correction.  There are \(O(L)\) sites in each boundary layer and
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{m\geq0}e^{-c_J\min\{m^2/u,m\}}\leq C_J(\sqrt u+1).
 \label{eq:finite-range-boundary-layer-sum}
\end{equation}
Summing \eqref{eq:not-feeling-boundary} therefore gives
\(C_JL/\sqrt u+C_J\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:finite-range-neumann-heat} and
\eqref{eq:finite-range-dirichlet-heat}.

The backbone hypothesis \eqref{eq:backbone-hypothesis} makes zero
the unique minimum of
\(\varepsilon_J\) on the Brillouin torus.  Near zero,
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_J(p)=p^{\mathrm T}A_Jp+O_J(|p|^4),
 \label{eq:finite-range-symbol-taylor}
\end{equation}
whereas it is bounded below away from zero off a fixed neighbourhood.
Splitting the integral into this neighbourhood and its complement, Taylor
expansion near zero and Gaussian tail bounds prove
\eqref{eq:finite-range-local-clt}.  At \(u=L^2\),
\eqref{eq:finite-range-neumann-heat} bounds the nonconstant Neumann trace,
while domination of the killed kernel by the full-lattice kernel bounds
the Dirichlet trace.  The gap in \eqref{eq:core-volume-gap}, the
Dirichlet Poincar\'e inequality following from
\eqref{eq:finite-range-dirichlet-form}, and the semigroup property then
give \eqref{eq:finite-range-large-time-heat}.

 \gamma(z)(S^2-\mathbf S_x\cdot\mathbf S_{x+z})
 \notag\\
 &+\sum_{x\in B_\ell}
 \sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^2\\x+z\notin B_\ell}}
 \gamma(z)S(S-S_x^3)
 +\widehat h\sum_{x\in B_\ell}(S-S_x^3).
 \label{eq:finite-range-dirichlet-spin-hamiltonian}
\end{align}
Its one-particle operator is the zero-exterior form
\eqref{eq:finite-range-dirichlet-form} with \(J\) replaced by
\(\gamma\).
Write
\[
 f_{\ell,\gamma}^{D}(\widehat\beta,S,\widehat h)
 :=-\frac1{\widehat\beta\ell^2}
 \log\operatorname{Tr} e^{-\widehat\beta H_{\ell,\gamma,\widehat h}^D}.
\]

Let
\(\varepsilon_0(p)=2\sum_{\nu=1}^2(1-\cos p_\nu)\).
Near the origin, Taylor's formula and the smallest and largest
eigenvalues of \(A_\gamma\) compare \(\varepsilon_\gamma\) with
\(\varepsilon_0\).  Away from the origin, compactness and the uniqueness
of the zero of \(\varepsilon_\gamma\) give the same two-sided comparison.
Hence
\begin{equation}
 c_J\varepsilon_0(p)\leq\varepsilon_\gamma(p)
 \leq C_J\varepsilon_0(p),\qquad p\in[-\pi,\pi]^2.
 \label{eq:finite-range-symbol-comparison}
\end{equation}
Fourier transformation of the zero extension turns
\eqref{eq:finite-range-symbol-comparison} into a comparison of
Dirichlet forms.  In particular, the nearest-neighbour Green-function
bound gives, uniformly in \(x\in B_\ell\),
\begin{equation}
 \langle\delta_x,(h_{\gamma,\ell}^D)^{-1}\delta_x\rangle
 \leq C_J\log(2\ell).
 \label{eq:finite-range-local-green-bound}
\end{equation}

Adopt the notation of Lemma~\ref{lem:general-projected-entropy} with
\(A=S h_{\gamma,\ell}^D+\widehat h I\) and inverse temperature
\(\widehat\beta\), so that
\(\widehat\beta K=t\,\mathrm d\Gamma(h_{\gamma,\ell}^D)

to \(H_{\ell,\gamma,\widehat h}^D\).

 \leq q\leq C_J\frac{\ell^2\log^2(2\ell)}{t^2}.
 \label{eq:finite-range-projection-defect}

 \label{eq:finite-range-energy-function}\\

 &=\frac{S}{t}G(t\lambda_j+a).
 \label{eq:finite-range-entropy-function}
\end{align}

 \label{eq:lr-torus-fractional-form}

\(K_{J,L}^N\).

For completeness, represent torus frequencies by
\(k\in\{-L,\ldots,L-1\}^2\) and put \(p_k=\pi k/L\).
Use the orthonormal Fourier convention
\(\widehat F(k)=(2L)^{-1}\sum_{x\in\mathbb T_{2L}^2}
F(x)e^{-i\pi k\cdot x/L}\).
Dyadic summation in the jump variable gives, for every nonzero torus
frequency \(p_k\),
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb T_{2L}^2}
 (1+d_{2L}(0,z))^{-2-\sigma}
 (1-\cos(p_k\cdot z))\geq c|p_k|^\sigma.
 \label{eq:lr-torus-symbol-lower}
\end{equation}
Splitting Parseval's sum into \(|p_k|\leq R\) and \(|p_k|>R\) yields
\begin{equation}
 \|F\|_2^2
 \leq CR^2\|F\|_1^2+CR^{-\sigma}\mathcal E_{\sigma,2L}(F).
 \label{eq:lr-torus-frequency-split}
\end{equation}
Optimization in \(R\) proves
\begin{equation}
 \|F\|_2^{2+\sigma}
 \leq C\mathcal E_{\sigma,2L}(F)\|F\|_1^\sigma
 \quad\text{when }\sum_xF(x)=0.
 \label{eq:lr-torus-nash}
\end{equation}
If the formal optimizer lies below \(\pi/L\), use
\eqref{eq:lr-torus-symbol-lower} at the smallest nonzero frequency.
This gives the same inequality.  If it exceeds a fixed constant, the
unit-scale part of \eqref{eq:lr-torus-symbol-lower}, together with
\(\|F\|_2\leq\|F\|_1\), gives the result directly.  Equations
\eqref{eq:lr-reflection-norms}--
\eqref{eq:lr-torus-nash} prove \eqref{eq:lr-fractional-nash}.  At
\(\sigma=2\), reflect only the nearest-neighbour form and repeat the same
Fourier splitting with its quadratic symbol. This proves
\eqref{eq:lr-nn-nash}.

We next derive the centered heat-kernel and trace bounds.  Let
\(T_u=e^{-uK_{J,L}^N}Q_L\), let \(F(u)=\|T_uf\|_2^2\), and set
\(\theta=\sigma\) for \(0<\sigma<2\) and \(\theta=2\) for
\(\sigma=2\).  Since the censored semigroup is Markovian,
\(\|T_uf\|_1\leq\|Q_Lf\|_1\leq2\|f\|_1\).  The two Nash inequalities
therefore imply
\begin{equation}
 F'(u)=-2\langle T_uf,K_{J,L}^NT_uf\rangle
 \leq-cF(u)^{1+\theta/2}\|f\|_1^{-\theta}.
 \label{eq:lr-nash-differential-inequality}
\end{equation}
Integration and self-adjointness give
\begin{equation}
 \|T_u\|_{1\to2}\leq Cu^{-1/\theta},
 \qquad
 \|T_u\|_{1\to\infty}
 \leq\|T_{u/2}\|_{1\to2}^2\leq Cu^{-2/\theta}.
 \label{eq:lr-centered-ultracontractivity}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{align}
 \sup_{x,y}(e^{-uK_{J,L}^N})(x,y)
 &\leq C(u^{-2/\sigma}+L^{-2}),&&0<\sigma<2,\notag\\
 \sup_{x,y}(e^{-uK_{J,L}^N})(x,y)
 &\leq C(u^{-1}+L^{-2}),&&\sigma=2,
 \label{eq:lr-censored-kernel-bound}
\end{align}
and, because the diagonal of \(T_u\) is nonnegative,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\1^\perp}e^{-uK_{J,L}^N}
 =\sum_{x\in B_L}\langle\delta_x,T_u\delta_x\rangle
 \leq L^2\|T_u\|_{1\to\infty}.
 \label{eq:lr-centered-trace-bound}
\end{equation}
This proves \eqref{eq:lr-stable-neumann-tail} and
\eqref{eq:lr-marginal-neumann-tail} for every \(u\geq1\), uniformly in
\(L\).  For \(0<\sigma<2\), these are the finite-volume analogues of
the stable-like estimates in \cite{BarlowBassKumagai2009}. The marginal
bound follows instead from \eqref{eq:lr-nn-nash}.

Let \(X_u\) be the full-lattice walk and couple it to the censored walk by
using the same jump clocks, suppressing a jump only if it leaves \(B_L\).
Let \(\tau_L\) be the first clock ring at which the proposed jump leaves
\(B_L\).  Until time \(\tau_L\) the two paths agree.  If
\(d_L(x)=\operatorname{dist}_\infty(x,\mathbb Z^2\setminus B_L)\), truncation of
jumps at \(d_L(x)/4\), followed by Doob's inequality for the truncated
martingale, gives
\begin{align}
 \mathbb P_x(\tau_L\leq u)
 &\leq C\min\{1,ud_L(x)^{-\sigma}\},&&0<\sigma<2,
 \label{eq:lr-stable-exit}\\
 \mathbb P_x(\tau_L\leq u)
 &\leq C\min\left\{1,
 \frac{u\log(2+d_L(x))}{d_L(x)^2}\right\},&&\sigma=2.
 \label{eq:lr-marginal-exit}
\end{align}
Here the rate of jumps longer than \(d\) is \(O(d^{-\sigma})\), while the
second moment truncated at \(d\) is \(O(d^{2-\sigma})\), or \(O(\log d)\)
at \(\sigma=2\).

Write \(p_{L,u}^N,p_{L,u}^D\), and \(p_u^\infty\) for the censored,
killed, and full-lattice kernels.  Regard every rejected clock ring of the
censored walk as a mark.  The marked path measures of the full and
censored walks agree until \(\tau_L\), and therefore
\begin{align}
 |p_{L,u}^N(x,x)-p_u^\infty(x,x)|
 \leq{}&\mathbb P_x(Y_u=x,\tau_L\leq u)
       +\mathbb P_x(X_u=x,\tau_L\leq u),
 \label{eq:lr-common-clock-difference}\\
 p_u^\infty(x,x)-p_{L,u}^D(x,x)
 ={}&\mathbb P_x(X_u=x,\tau_L\leq u),
 \label{eq:lr-killed-loop-identity}
\end{align}
where \(Y\) denotes the censored path.  For either probability in
\eqref{eq:lr-common-clock-difference}, the contribution from
\(\tau_L\leq u/2\) is, by the strong Markov property, at most
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_x(\tau_L\leq u/2)
 \sup_{v\geq u/2,z}p_v(z,x),
 \label{eq:lr-early-exit-loop-bound}
\end{equation}
with the kernel of the corresponding walk.  Reversal of diagonal loops
moves a rejected mark or exit in \([u/2,u]\) to \([0,u/2]\) and gives the
same bound for the late contribution.  This is ordinary reversibility for
\(X\), and reversibility of the marked rejected-clock measure for \(Y\).
For \(u\leq cL^\sigma\), the \(L^{-2}\) term in
\eqref{eq:lr-censored-kernel-bound} is bounded by \(Cu^{-2/\sigma}\).
Consequently, in the stable case,
\begin{equation}
 |p_{L,u}^N(x,x)-I_J(u)|
 +|p_{L,u}^D(x,x)-I_J(u)|
 \leq Cu^{-2/\sigma}\mathbb P_x(\tau_L\leq u/2).
 \label{eq:lr-common-clock-diagonal}
\end{equation}
At \(\sigma=2\), equations \eqref{eq:lr-full-heat-bound} and
\eqref{eq:lr-censored-kernel-bound} instead give
\begin{equation}
 |p_{L,u}^N(x,x)-I_J(u)|
 +|p_{L,u}^D(x,x)-I_J(u)|
 \leq \frac Cu\mathbb P_x(\tau_L\leq u/2),
 \qquad u\leq cL^2.
 \label{eq:lr-marginal-common-clock-diagonal}
\end{equation}
Summing
\eqref{eq:lr-stable-exit} over the \(O(L)\) sites in every boundary layer
gives
\[
 \sum_{x\in B_L}\mathbb P_x(\tau_L\leq u)
 \leq CL
 \begin{cases}
  u^{1/\sigma},&\sigma>1,\\
  u\log(2+L/u),&\sigma=1,\\
  uL^{1-\sigma}+u^{1/\sigma},&\sigma<1.
 \end{cases}
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:lr-stable-finite-box-heat}. Subtracting the constant
Neumann mode accounts for the harmless \(1\).

At the marginal exponent use the second estimate in
\eqref{eq:lr-censored-kernel-bound}, rather than a sharp marginal bound.
The boundary-layer sum in \eqref{eq:lr-marginal-exit} is
\(O(LR_2(u))\).  Multiplication by \(C/u\) proves
\eqref{eq:lr-marginal-finite-box-heat}.  This deliberately conservative
estimate is sufficient for the box scale
\eqref{eq:lr-marginal-pressure-scales}, which contains an extra
logarithmic volume factor.

The hypothesis \eqref{eq:power-law-hypothesis} implies, for all sufficiently
large \(|z|\),
\begin{equation}
 c(1+|z|)^{-2-\sigma}\leq J(z)
 \leq C(1+|z|)^{-2-\sigma}.
 \label{eq:lr-two-sided-tail}
\end{equation}
The missing finitely many short displacements are supplied, when needed,
by paths in the positive nearest-neighbour backbone.  Fix a radius
\(R_0\) beyond which the lower bound in \eqref{eq:lr-two-sided-tail}
holds.  If \(v\) has mean zero, then
\[
 \frac12\sum_{\substack{x,y\in B_L\\|x-y|\geq R_0}}
 |v(x)-v(y)|^2
 \geq (L^2-C_{R_0})\|v\|_2^2.
\]
Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(L\),
\begin{align}
 \langle v,K_{J,L}^Nv\rangle
 &\geq cL^{-2-\sigma}\frac12
 \sum_{\substack{x,y\in B_L\\|x-y|\geq R_0}}
 |v(x)-v(y)|^2
 \geq cL^{-\sigma}\|v\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:lr-complete-gap-comparison}
\end{align}
The finitely many smaller boxes are absorbed by decreasing the constant,
using the nearest-neighbour Poincar\'e inequality.
At \(\sigma=2\) we retain the weaker consequence \(cL^{-2}\), which is
sufficient for Proposition~\ref{prop:lr-pressure}.

We next construct the resistance flow and keep track of its divergence.
A flow \(\theta\) is antisymmetric on ordered edges with positive
conductance, and its divergence is
\(\operatorname{div}\theta(u)=\sum_{v:J(v-u)>0}\theta(u,v)\).
Given \(x\in B_L\), choose nested sets
\begin{equation}
 \{x\}=Q_0^x\subset Q_1^x\subset\cdots\subset Q_N^x=B_L,
 \qquad r_j:=2^{j-1}r_0\wedge L\quad(j\geq1),
 \label{eq:lr-nested-flow-sets}
\end{equation}
obtained by intersecting and, near the boundary, translating coordinate
squares, so that \(|Q_j^x|\asymp r_j^2\) for \(j\geq1\).  Let
\(\mu_j^x\) be the uniform probability measure on \(Q_j^x\).  After
increasing the fixed \(r_0\), couple consecutive measures, for
\(1\leq j\leq N-1\), by
\begin{equation}
 \pi_j^x(u,v):=\mu_j^x(u)\mu_{j+1}^x(v),\qquad
 |u-v|\leq Cr_j,\qquad
 \max_{u,v}\pi_j^x(u,v)\leq Cr_j^{-4}.
 \label{eq:lr-scale-coupling}
\end{equation}
Discard the diagonal transports \(u=v\), which have zero divergence.
Orient every remaining mass \(\pi_j^x(u,v)\) from \(u\) to \(v\), and
call the resulting flow \(\theta_j^x\).  If \(|u-v|<R_0\), replace this
transport by a fixed nearest-neighbour path.  This preserves the exact
identity
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{div}\theta_j^x=\mu_j^x-\mu_{j+1}^x.
 \label{eq:lr-scale-divergence}
\end{equation}
Let \(\theta_0^x\) transport \(\delta_x\) to \(\mu_1^x\) along fixed
nearest-neighbour paths.  Because \(r_0\) is fixed, this initial flow has
bounded energy.
For every later scale, \eqref{eq:lr-two-sided-tail} and
\eqref{eq:lr-scale-coupling} give
\begin{align}
 \mathcal E_{\rm flow}(\theta_j^x)
 &:=\frac12\sum_{\substack{u,v\in B_L\\J(v-u)>0}}
 \frac{|\theta_j^x(u,v)|^2}{J(v-u)}\notag\\
 &\leq Cr_j^4(r_j^{-4})^2r_j^{2+\sigma}
       +Cr_j^2(r_j^{-4})^2
 \leq Cr_j^{\sigma-2}.
 \label{eq:lr-flow-scale-energy}
\end{align}
The second term bounds the finitely long nearest-neighbour routings.  For
a fixed graph edge, the currents contributed by all later dyadic scales
decrease geometrically.  Hence, pointwise on edges,
\begin{equation}
 \left(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}|\theta_j^x(e)|\right)^2
 \leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}|\theta_j^x(e)|^2.
 \label{eq:lr-flow-overlap}
\end{equation}
It follows that
\(\theta^x:=\theta_0^x+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\theta_j^x\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{div}\theta^x=\delta_x-|B_L|^{-1}\1,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_{\rm flow}(\theta^x)
 \leq C+C\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}r_j^{\sigma-2}.
 \label{eq:lr-flow-telescoping}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\theta^x-\theta^y\) is a unit flow from \(x\) to \(y\).
Thomson's principle gives a uniform resistance diameter for
\(\sigma<2\) and an \(O(\log(2L))\) diameter for \(\sigma=2\).

The same construction gives the punctured estimate without a mass
defect.  Fix \(o\in B_L\) and \(x\in B_L\setminus\{o\}\), and replace
every set by
\(Q_j^x\setminus\{o\}\), and normalize counting measure on the resulting
set.  The product couplings retain \eqref{eq:lr-scale-coupling}.  Whenever
a fixed short path meets \(o\), detour it inside a \(3\)-by-\(3\) or
\(4\)-by-\(4\) neighbourhood. The number and length of these detours are
uniformly bounded.  The divergences telescope to
\begin{equation}
 \delta_x-|B_L\setminus\{o\}|^{-1}\1_{B_L\setminus\{o\}},
 \label{eq:lr-punctured-flow-divergence}
\end{equation}
so subtracting the flows from \(x\) and \(y\) proves the asserted
punctured resistance bound.

It remains to pass to the spin-fibre decoration.  Let \(P\) project onto
the uniform fibre vector and \(Q=I-P\).  Direct summation over the fibre
labels gives
\begin{equation}
 K_{J,L}^{(r)}=K_{J,L}^N\otimes P+M_{d_{J,L}}\otimes Q,
 \qquad d_{J,L}(x)=\sum_{y\in B_L}J(y-x)\geq c_J.
 \label{eq:lr-decorated-split}
\end{equation}
Thus the acoustic gap is the base gap and the optical gap is uniform.
Lift a base flow by distributing the current on an edge \((x,y)\)
equally among its \(r^2\) fibre edges.  Since each has conductance
\(J(y-x)/r\), the lifted energy is \(r^{-1}\) times the base energy.  A
current from a single fibre vertex is first fanned to the uniform fibre
measure by two-edge paths through a fixed inward nearest-neighbouring base
point. This costs only a constant depending on \(r\) and the backbone
weights.  Reverse the fan at the endpoint.  After deletion of one
decorated vertex, use the punctured base flow if \(r=1\). If \(r\geq2\),
distribute the current over the surviving labels above that base point and
use the same local fan.  This proves both decorated resistance estimates
in \eqref{eq:lr-box-resistance}.

Suppose first that \(0<\sigma<2\), put \(\nu=2/\sigma\), and choose

 g=(\log t)^8,\qquad L^2\asymp t^\nu g,
 \qquad m^\sigma\asymp\frac{t}{A\log t},
 \label{eq:lr-stable-pressure-scales}
\end{equation}

with \(A\) large and fixed, gives
\begin{equation}
 \log\operatorname{Tr} e^{-(t/2)\mathcal H_L^{(r,J)}-b\mathcal N}
 \leq Cg(\log t)^{1+\nu}=: \mathcal R_\sigma(t).
 \label{eq:lr-stable-rough-main}
\end{equation}
Indeed, there are \(O(g(\log t)^\nu)\) cells and the descendant factor in
each cell has logarithm \(O(\log t)\).  Set
\begin{equation}
 e_0:=C\frac{g(\log t)^{1+\nu}}t,
 \qquad
 K:=\lfloor c^{-1}L^\sigma e_0\rfloor
 \leq Cg^{1+\sigma/2}(\log t)^{1+\nu}.
 \label{eq:lr-stable-cutoffs}
\end{equation}
Increasing the constant in \(e_0\), the spectral inequality
\eqref{eq:high-energy-spectral-inequality} and
\eqref{eq:lr-stable-rough-main} make the contribution above \(e_0\)
exponentially small.  Equation \eqref{eq:lr-highest-weight} on the full
box excludes \(k>K\) below that energy.

\begin{equation}
 K^2L^{\sigma-2}=o(1),

 \label{eq:lr-stable-collision-delta}
\end{equation}

thermodynamic pressure.  Since \(f=-\beta^{-1}p\),
\eqref{eq:lr-stable-box-pressure} and
\eqref{eq:lr-marginal-box-pressure} prove
\eqref{eq:lr-stable-pressure-limit} and
\eqref{eq:lr-marginal-pressure-limit}.

 \label{eq:lr-free-trial-operator}
\end{equation}

 \mathcal P H_{\ell,J,h}^D\mathcal P\leq K_{J,\ell,h}^D.
 \label{eq:lr-trial-operator-inequality}
\end{equation}

We record the estimates entering the projected entropy calculation.  In
the stable case, with \(\nu=2/\sigma>1\), domination of the killed heat
kernel by the full kernel and \eqref{eq:lr-full-heat-bound} give
\begin{align}
 \rho_*&:=\sup_x
 \bigl(e^{t h_{J,\ell}^D+b}-1\bigr)^{-1}(x,x)
 \leq Ct^{-\nu},
 \label{eq:lr-stable-local-density}\\
 1-\frac{Z_{\mathcal P}}{Z_0}
 &\leq C\ell^2t^{-2\nu}=:Q_{\ell,t}.
 \label{eq:lr-stable-projection-defect}
\end{align}
The heat-trace eigenvalue count following from
\eqref{eq:lr-full-heat-bound} also yields
\begin{align}
 E_1&:=\operatorname{Tr}\frac{2(Sh_{J,\ell}^D+h)}
 {e^{t h_{J,\ell}^D+b}-1}
 &\leq CS\ell^2t^{-1-\nu},
 \label{eq:lr-stable-energy-trace}\\
 E_2&:=\operatorname{Tr}\frac{Sh_{J,\ell}^D+h}
 {\sinh^2((t h_{J,\ell}^D+b)/2)}
 &\leq CS\ell^2t^{-1-\nu}.
 \label{eq:lr-stable-entropy-trace}
\end{align}
The integral at zero in the second estimate is finite precisely because
\(\nu>1\).  The functions of \(t\lambda+b\) in these traces decrease in
\(b\), so the bounds are uniform in the field.

For \(Q_{\ell,t}\leq1/2\), insert
\eqref{eq:lr-stable-local-density}--
\eqref{eq:lr-stable-entropy-trace} into
\eqref{eq:general-projected-free-energy}, using \(q\leq Q_{\ell,t}\).

\begin{align}
 f_{J,\ell}^D(\beta,S,h)
 \leq{}&-\frac1{\beta\ell^2}\Phi_{J,\ell}^D(t,b)
 +CS\left(t^{-1-2\nu}+\ell^2t^{-1-3\nu}\right).
 \label{eq:lr-stable-projected-bound}
\end{align}

 \label{eq:lr-marginal-projected-bound}
\end{align}

\eqref{eq:lr-marginal-dirichlet-bose}, this proves the required marginal
box estimate.

It remains to pass from a Dirichlet box to the infinite system.  Tile a
large square by \(\ell\)-blocks and put the exact Dirichlet Gibbs state in
each block.  This state is invariant under rotations about the third spin
axis, hence its transverse one-spin expectations vanish.  For sites
\(x,y\) in different blocks, put
\(\rho_x=\langle S-S_x^3\rangle\).  The tensor-product state satisfies the
exact estimate
\begin{align}
 \langle S^2-\mathbf S_x\cdot\mathbf S_y\rangle
 &=S(\rho_x+\rho_y)-\rho_x\rho_y
 \leq S(\rho_x+\rho_y).
 \label{eq:lr-cross-block-bound}
\end{align}
After multiplication by \(J(y-x)\), the right side is precisely the sum
of the two pinned terms assigned to this bond by
\eqref{eq:lr-dirichlet-spin-hamiltonian}.  Thus all cross-block bonds,
including arbitrarily long ones, are controlled with no corridor and no
loss of active density.  Entropy is additive.  Incomplete outer blocks
have vanishing density, and their boundary contribution tends to zero by
\eqref{eq:summable-boundary-density}.  The Gibbs variational principle
therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 f_J(\beta,S,h)\leq f_{J,\ell}^D(\beta,S,h).
 \label{eq:lr-block-variational-passage}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:lr-stable-projected-bound} or
\eqref{eq:lr-marginal-projected-bound} with the corresponding Dirichlet
Bose trace proves \eqref{eq:lr-stable-variational-limit} and
\eqref{eq:lr-marginal-variational-limit}.
