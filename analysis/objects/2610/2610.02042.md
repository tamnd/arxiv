\label{thm:pbw}
Let $L$ be a Lie ring. Then the
canonical additive homomorphism $\iota\colon L\to \operatorname{U}(L)$ is injective, and
the canonical homomorphism of commutative rings
\[
\iota_*\colon \operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{Z}}(L)
\longrightarrow \operatorname{gr}\operatorname{U}(L)

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:pbw-basis}
Let $L$ be a Lie ring, and let $\mathcal X\subseteq L$ be a totally
ordered subset such that
\(
L=\bigoplus_{x\in\mathcal X}\langle x\rangle
\)
as an abelian group. Set $M_0=\mathbb{Z}\cdot 1\subseteq \operatorname{U}(L)$. For
$r\geq 1$, let $M_r\subseteq \operatorname{U}(L)$ be the additive subgroup
\[M_r = \langle x_1\cdots x_r \mid x_i\in\mathcal X, \; x_1\leq x_2\leq\cdots\leq x_r\rangle.\]
Then
\[\operatorname{U}(L)=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}M_r,
\qquad
P_{\leq r}\operatorname{U}(L)=\bigoplus_{s=0}^r M_s,
\quad r\geq 0.
\]
In particular,
\(
\operatorname{gr}_r\operatorname{U}(L)\cong M_r
\) for each \(r \geq 0\).
Moreover, for $r\geq1$,
\[
M_r=\bigoplus_{\substack{x_1,\ldots,x_r\in\mathcal X\\
x_1\leq\cdots\leq x_r}}\langle x_1\cdots x_r\rangle,
\]
and each generator $x_1\cdots x_r$ has additive order
\[
\operatorname{ord}(x_1\cdots x_r)
=\gcd\left(\operatorname{ord}(x_1),\ldots,\operatorname{ord}(x_r)\right).
\]
Here we use the convention that $\operatorname{ord}(x)=0$ when $x$ has
infinite order.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:pbw},
$P_{\leq r}\operatorname{U}(L)/P_{\leq r-1}\operatorname{U}(L)\cong\operatorname{Sym}^r_{\mathbb{Z}}(L)$, whose ordered monomials
generate a direct sum of cyclic groups with the stated orders.
Each finite order of a factor annihilates the corresponding product in
$\operatorname{U}(L)$; B\'ezout's identity therefore shows that their greatest common
divisor also annihilates it when positive.  Thus sending each monomial
class to the same product in $\operatorname{U}(L)$ defines a section of
$P_{\leq r}\operatorname{U}(L)\to P_{\leq r}\operatorname{U}(L)/P_{\leq r-1}\operatorname{U}(L)$ with image $M_r$.
This proves the assertion about $M_r$ and gives
$P_{\leq r}\operatorname{U}(L)=P_{\leq r-1}\operatorname{U}(L)\oplus M_r$.
Induction on $r$ yields the displayed decompositions.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:top-layer}
The Lie ring $L_N$ is metabelian and nilpotent of class $N+3$, and
\[
             \gamma_{N+3}(L_N)=\langle u_3\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/64\mathbb{Z}.

<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Metabelianity is inherited from $F_{\mathrm{met}}$, and relation~\textup{(ii)} gives
$\gamma_{N+4}(L_N)=0$.  By relation~\textup{(iii)},
$\gamma_{N+3}(L_N)$ is generated by $u_1,u_2,u_3$.  Relation~\textup{(iv)}
reduces it to $\langle u_3\rangle$, with $64u_3=0$.
We will show that $u_3$ has order $64$.  Define an additive homomorphism
$\mu\colon F_{\mathrm{met}}/\gamma_{N+4}(F_{\mathrm{met}})\to\mathbb{Z}/256\mathbb{Z}$ by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu([x_2,x_1])&=4,&\mu([x_3,x_1])&=2,&
 \mu([x_3,x_2])&=1,\\
 \mu(u_1)&=64,&\mu(u_2)&=16,&\mu(u_3)&=4,
 \end{aligned}
\]
and by zero on all other Hall basis elements.  We claim that $\mu$
annihilates the relations \textup{(i)--(iv)}, and hence descends to $L_N$.

$\gamma_{N+4}(F_{\mathrm{met}})$ and have zero image under $\mu$.

We write bars for classes modulo $Z(T)$. We will
construct a biadditive map
\[
H\colon T/Z(T)\times T/Z(T)\longrightarrow T
\]
such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:condition_on_H}
H(\bar{x},\bar{y})-H(\bar{y},\bar{x})
=[x,y],\qquad x,y\in T.
\end{equation}

We first show that any such map $H$ determines the required retraction
$r$. Since the additive group of $T$ is finitely generated, there is
a decomposition \(
T=\bigoplus_{i=1}^k\langle x_i\rangle
\) into a direct sum of cyclic subgroups.
Order the chosen generators by $x_1<\cdots<x_k$.
By Corollary~\ref{cor:pbw-basis}, there is a direct sum decomposition
\[
P_{\leq 2}\operatorname{U}(T)
=\mathbb{Z}\cdot 1
\oplus\underbrace{\langle x_i\mid 1\leq i\leq k\rangle}_{=T}
\oplus\langle x_i x_j\mid 1\leq i\leq j\leq k\rangle.
\]
Set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r_def}
r(1)=0,\qquad
r(x_i)=x_i,\qquad
r(x_i x_j)=H(\bar{x}_i,\bar{x}_j),\qquad i\leq j.
\end{equation}
Suppose that elements $x_i$ and $x_j$ of $T$ have orders $d_i$
and $d_j$, respectively. The element $x_i x_j$ has additive order
$d=\gcd(d_i,d_j)$ in $\operatorname{U}(T)$. Choose integers $a,b$ such that
$d=a d_i+b d_j$. By biadditivity,
\begin{align*}
dH(\bar{x}_i,\bar{x}_j)
&=(a d_i+b d_j)H(\bar{x}_i,\bar{x}_j)\\
&=aH(d_i\bar{x}_i,\bar{x}_j)
  +bH(\bar{x}_i,d_j\bar{x}_j)=0.
\end{align*}
Thus
\eqref{eq:r_def} defines an additive homomorphism
\(
r\colon P_{\leq 2}\operatorname{U}(T)\longrightarrow T
\)
whose restriction to $T$ is the identity.

For $i\leq j$, relation \eqref{eq:condition_on_H} gives
\begin{align*}
r(x_jx_i)
&=r(x_ix_j+[x_j,x_i])\\
&=H(\bar{x}_i,\bar{x}_j)+[x_j,x_i]\\
&=H(\bar{x}_i,\bar{x}_j)
  +\left(H(\bar{x}_j,\bar{x}_i)-H(\bar{x}_i,\bar{x}_j)\right)\\
&=H(\bar{x}_j,\bar{x}_i).
\end{align*}
It follows by biadditivity that
\[
r(xy)=H(\bar{x},\bar{y}),\qquad x,y\in T.
\]

$r(zt)=H(\bar z,\bar t)=0$ for every $t\in T$. Therefore
$r$ vanishes on $Z(T)\cdot T$ as required.

of $T/Z(T)$

\begin{equation}\label{eq:H_definition}
H(\bar v_i,\bar v_j)=
\begin{cases}
[v_i,v_j],& i>j,\\
0,& i\leq j.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Since the Lie bracket vanishes whenever one of its arguments is central,
this formula is independent of the chosen lifts. It also respects the

$e_i v_i,e_j v_j\in Z(T)$, and consequently
\begin{align*}

e_j[v_i,v_j]&=[v_i,e_j v_j]=0.
\end{align*}
Thus the formula~\eqref{eq:H_definition} defines a biadditive
map $H$. Condition \eqref{eq:condition_on_H} holds by construction.

Moreover, there exist elements $b_1,\ldots,b_m\in T'$ such that the
subgroup $K\subseteq T$ is generated by
\[
d_i p_i+b_i,\qquad 1\leq i\leq m.
\]
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
This follows directly from Lemma~\ref{lem:properties-of-T}. Since
$T'\cong L'$ and $L$ is metabelian, the Lie ring $T$ is metabelian.
Moreover, $T$ is an additive subgroup of $L\times P$. Since the additive
groups of $L$ and $P$ are finitely generated, the additive group of $T$
is finitely generated. Hence the additive group of $T'$ is finitely
generated, and we may choose a decomposition
\(
T'=\bigoplus_{i=1}^k\langle t_i\rangle
\)
into cyclic groups.

We now describe explicit choices of the elements $p_i\in T$ and
$b_i\in T'$. We use the decomposition \eqref{eq:P_decomposition} and
the realization $T\subseteq L\times P$ from \eqref{eq:T_definition}.
For each $i$, choose a lift $a_i\in L$ of
$\rho(e_i)\in L_{\operatorname{ab}}$, and set
\(p_i=(a_i,e_i)\in T\).
Since $P$ is free on $e_1,\ldots,e_m$, the assignment $e_i\mapsto p_i$
extends to an additive section of the projection $T\twoheadrightarrow P$. It follows
that
\[
T=T'\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^m\mathbb{Z} p_i.
\]
Under the identification $K\cong\ker(\rho)$, the subgroup $K$ is
generated by the elements $(0,d_i e_i)$. Since
$d_i\rho(e_i)=0$ in $L_{\operatorname{ab}}$, we have $d_i a_i\in L'$, and
therefore
\[
(0,d_i e_i)
=d_i p_i+\underbrace{(-d_i a_i,0)}_{=:b_i\in T'},
\qquad 1\leq i\leq m.
\]
This proves the assertion.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:central-multiple-commutator}
Let $1\leq i\leq m$, and let $x\in\mathcal X$ satisfy $x<p_i$. Then
\(d_i[p_i,x]\in Z(T)\).
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Since $d_i p_i+b_i\in K$ and $b_i\in T'$, we have
\[
d_i p_i\in K+T'\subseteq Z(T)+T'.
\]
Moreover,
\(
[Z(T)+T',T']=0
\)
because $T$ is metabelian.

\emph{Case 1.} Suppose that $x=t_j$ for some $1\leq j\leq k$. Then
\[
d_i[p_i,t_j]=[d_i p_i,t_j]\in[Z(T)+T',T']=0.
\]

\emph{Case 2.} Suppose that $x=p_j$ for some $1\leq j<i$. For every
$y\in T$, the Jacobi identity gives
\begin{align*}
d_i[[p_i,p_j],y] =
- \Big[
\underbrace{[p_j,y]}_{\in T'},
\underbrace{d_i p_i}_{\in Z(T)+T'}
\Big]
- \Big[
\underbrace{[y, p_i]}_{\in T'},
\underbrace{d_i p_j}_{\substack{\in Z(T)+T'\\ \text{since }d_j\mid d_i}}
\Big] = 0.
\end{align*}

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:multiplication-by-generators}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $q\geq0$ be an integer. Then
\[
T'\cdot M_q\subseteq M_{q+1}.
\]
\item Let $i,q$ be integers with $1\leq i\leq m$ and $q\geq1$. Then

\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Let
\[
u=x_1\cdots x_q\in M_q,
\qquad
x_\nu\in\mathcal X,
\qquad
x_1\leq\cdots\leq x_q,
\]
be a generator of $M_q$. There exists $s$ with $0\leq s\leq q$ such
that
\[
\{x_1,\ldots,x_s\}\subseteq\{t_1,\ldots,t_k\},
\qquad
\{x_{s+1},\ldots,x_q\}\subseteq\{p_1,\ldots,p_m\}.
\]
Thus
\[
u=t_{j_1}\cdots t_{j_s}p_{i_1}\cdots p_{i_{q-s}}
\]
for some
\[
1\leq j_1\leq\cdots\leq j_s\leq k,
\qquad
1\leq i_1\leq\cdots\leq i_{q-s}\leq m.
\]

To prove (1), it suffices by additivity to consider $t_i u$ with
$1\leq i\leq k$. Since $T$ is metabelian, the elements $t_i$ and $t_j$
commute in $\operatorname{U}(T)$. Hence $t_i u$ can
be placed in nondecreasing order without producing correction terms.
Since every $t_j$ precedes every $p_i$ in $\mathcal X$, the resulting
ordered monomial belongs to $M_{q+1}$.

To prove (2), assume $q\geq 1$ and fix $1\leq i\leq m$. Starting with
\[
d_i p_i u
=d_i p_i t_{j_1}\cdots t_{j_s}p_{i_1}\cdots p_{i_{q-s}},
\]
we move $d_i p_i$ to its position in the nondecreasing order. At each step
we use the rewriting rule
\[
(\dots d_ip_i \cdot x \dots)
\rightsquigarrow (\dots x \cdot d_i p_i \dots) + \underbrace{(\dots d_i [p_i,x]\dots)}_{\text{correction term}},
\qquad x \in \mathcal{X}, x<p_i.
\]
The ordered term obtained after all rewrites belongs to $M_{q+1}$.

It remains to consider correction terms. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:central-multiple-commutator},
$d_i[p_i,x]\in Z(T)$. Since $d_i[p_i,x]\in T'$, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{(correction term)}
&=u_1\cdot d_i[p_i,x]\cdot u_2
=d_i[p_i,x]\cdot u_1u_2\\
&\in (Z(T)\cap T')\cdot M_{q-1}\subseteq M_q,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the equality uses the centrality of $d_i[p_i,x]$ and the
last inclusion follows from the first assertion.
Here $u_1u_2$ is obtained from the ordered monomial $u$ by deleting the
occurrence of $x$ involved in the rewriting step; hence $u_1u_2\in M_{q-1}$.
Therefore
\[
d_i p_i u\in M_{q+1}+(Z(T)\cap T')\cdot M_{q-1}
\subseteq M_{q+1}+M_q,
\]
which proves (2).

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:ordered-products-with-commutators}
Let $q\geq1$ be an integer and let $x_1,\ldots,x_q\in\mathcal X$. The product
$x_1\cdots x_q\in \operatorname{U}(T)$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combination of products
of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ordered-product-with-commutators}
c_1\cdots c_h y_1\cdots y_v\in M_{h+v},
\qquad
y_j\in\mathcal X,
\qquad
y_1\leq\cdots\leq y_v,
\end{equation}
where $h,v\geq 0$, each $c_\nu$ is a left-normed commutator of length
$s_\nu\geq 2$ in elements of $\mathcal X$, and
\[
s_1+\cdots+s_h+v=q.
\]
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
We first show that the product $x_1\cdots x_q$ can be rewritten as a
$\mathbb{Z}$-linear combination of products of the form
\[
f_1\cdots f_r p_{i_1}\cdots p_{i_\ell},
\qquad
i_1\leq\cdots\leq i_\ell,
\]
where each $f_j$ is either a left-normed commutator of length at least
$2$ in elements of $\mathcal X$ or one of the elements
$t_1,\ldots,t_k$. To this end, we repeatedly apply the rewriting rule
\[
(\dots p_i\cdot a\dots)
\rightsquigarrow
(\dots a\cdot p_i\dots) - (\dots[a,p_i]\dots),
\]
where $a$ is either a left-normed commutator of weight at least $2$, an
element $t_\alpha$, or an element $p_j$ with $j<i$. The new commutator
$[a,p_i]$ is again left-normed and has weight one greater than that of
$a$, where a letter is regarded as a commutator of weight $1$.

An inversion is a pair of positions, not necessarily adjacent, whose
left factor is $p_i$ and whose right factor is a commutator of weight at
least $2$, a letter $t_\alpha$, or a letter $p_j$ with $j<i$.
In the first term on the right-hand side, swapping the adjacent inverted
pair decreases the number of inversions by one without changing the
number of factors; the second term has fewer factors.
Thus the lexicographically ordered pair consisting of the number of
factors and the number of inversions decreases at every branch, so the
rewriting process terminates.

Every factor $f_j$ belongs to $T'$. Since $T$ is metabelian, the
elements $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ commute with one another in
$\operatorname{U}(T)$. We may therefore place the commutators of length at
least $2$ first, in an arbitrary order, and then arrange the remaining
letters $t_\alpha$ in nondecreasing order. Since every $t_\alpha$
precedes every $p_i$ in $\mathcal X$, each resulting term has the form
\[
c_1\cdots c_h y_1\cdots y_v,
\qquad
y_1\leq\cdots\leq y_v,
\]
appearing in \eqref{eq:ordered-product-with-commutators}.

The sum of the weights
is preserved by each rewriting rule. Since the original product has total weight $q$, every
resulting term satisfies
\[
s_1+\cdots+s_h+v=q.
\]
Finally, $y_1\cdots y_v\in M_v$ and each $c_\nu\in T'$. Repeated
application of Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplication-by-generators}~(1) gives
\[
c_1\cdots c_h y_1\cdots y_v\in M_{h+v}.
\]
This proves the assertion.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:projection-properties}
The projection $p$ has the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every $r\geq 2$,
\[
p\left(\varpi(T)^r\right)\subseteq W_r(T);
\]
\item one has
\[
p\left(K\cdot \operatorname{U}(T)\right)\subseteq K+Z(T)\cdot T.
\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
(1) The products
\[
x_1\cdots x_q,
\qquad q\geq r,
\qquad x_i\in\mathcal X,
\]
generate $\varpi(T)^r$ additively. It is therefore enough to prove that
\(p(x_1\cdots x_q)\in W_r(T)\).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:ordered-products-with-commutators}, we may express
$x_1\cdots x_q$ as a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combination of terms of the
form \eqref{eq:ordered-product-with-commutators}. The projection $p$
annihilates every such term lying in $M_{h+v}$ with $h+v>2$.
Consequently, it remains to consider the following six possibilities:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l@{\qquad}r}
\eqref{eq:ordered-product-with-commutators}\in M_{h+v}
  & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\text{summand}} \\
\hline
M_{0+0} & 1
  & \text{(impossible since }q\geq 2\text{)} \\
M_{0+1} & y_1
  & \text{(impossible since }q\geq 2\text{)} \\
M_{0+2} & y_1y_2
  & \text{(possible only when }q=2\text{)} \\
M_{1+0} & c_1\in\gamma_{s_1}(T),
  & s_1=q \\
M_{1+1} & c_1y_1\in\gamma_{s_1}(T)\cdot T,
  & s_1+1=q \\
M_{2+0} & c_1c_2\in
  \gamma_{s_1}(T)\cdot\gamma_{s_2}(T),
  & s_1+s_2=q
\end{array}
\]
The table shows that
\[
p(x_1\cdots x_q)
\in\gamma_q(T)
+\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}\gamma_i(T)\cdot\gamma_{q-i}(T)
=W_q(T) \overset{\eqref{eq:W_q_inclusion}}{\subseteq} W_r(T).
\]
This proves (1).

(2) Fix an additive generator
\(
d_i p_i+b_i
\)
of $K$, where $1\leq i\leq m$, and an ordered monomial
\[
x_1\cdots x_q\in M_q,
\qquad q\geq 0,
\qquad x_j\in\mathcal X,
\qquad x_1\leq\cdots\leq x_q.
\]
We first prove that
\[
p\left((d_i p_i+b_i)\cdot x_1 \ldots x_q\right)
\in K+Z(T)\cdot T.
\]
We consider the following cases.

\begin{itemize}
\item If $q=0$, then
\(d_i p_i + b_i \in K \subseteq T \subseteq P_{\le 2}\operatorname{U}(T)\), and hence
\[
p(d_i p_i+b_i)=d_i p_i+b_i\in K.
\]

\item If $q=1$, then $(d_i p_i+b_i) x_1 \in P_{\leq 2}\operatorname{U}(T)$, and hence
\[
p\left((d_i p_i+b_i)x_1\right)
=(d_i p_i+b_i)x_1\in K \cdot T \subseteq Z(T)\cdot T.
\]

\item Suppose that $q=2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplication-by-generators}~(2),
\[
d_i p_i\cdot x_1x_2\in M_3+Z(T)\cdot T.
\]
Moreover, Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplication-by-generators}~(1) gives
\[
b_i \cdot x_1 x_2 \in M_3,
\]
because $b_i\in T'$. Thus
\[
p\left((d_i p_i+b_i) \cdot x_1 x_2\right)\in Z(T)\cdot T.
\]

\item Suppose that $q\geq 3$. Since $b_i\in T'$, the two assertions of
Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplication-by-generators} give
\[
d_i p_i \cdot x_1 \ldots x_q \in M_{q+1}+M_q,
\qquad
b_i \cdot x_1 \ldots x_q \in M_{q+1}.
\]
Both $M_q$ and $M_{q+1}$ are annihilated by $p$, so
\[
p\left((d_i p_i+b_i) \cdot x_1 \ldots x_q\right)=0.
\]
\end{itemize}

The elements $d_i p_i+b_i$, $1\leq i\leq m$, generate $K$
additively, while the ordered monomials in the groups $M_q$, $q\geq 0$,
generate $\operatorname{U}(T)$ additively. The required inclusion
\[
p\left(K\cdot \operatorname{U}(T)\right) = p\left(\sum_{i,q} (d_ip_i + b_i) \cdot M_q\right) \subseteq K+Z(T)\cdot T
\]
now follows by additivity.
