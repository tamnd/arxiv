<!-- defn-1: body_md -->
\label{def:brojaUni} Let $\Delta$ be the set of all joint distributions on $(A, X, Y)$ and $\Delta_P\!=\!\{Q_{AXY}{\in} \Delta$: $Q_{AX}\!=\!P_{AX}$ and $Q_{AY}\!=\!P_{AY}\}$ be the set of joint distributions with same marginals on $(A,X)$ and $(A,Y)$ as the true distribution $P_{AXY}$. Then, $
\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}X|Y}):=\min _{Q \in \Delta_P} \mathrm{I}_{Q}({A;X|Y}). $
Here, $\mathrm{I}_{Q}({A;X|Y})$ is the conditional mutual information under joint distribution $Q_{AXY}$ instead of $P_{AXY}$.
<!-- end defn-1 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:uniqueness_noise}

Consider an erroneous step $S_i' = S_i + N$ where N is independent additive noise, $N \perp (A,S_i,S_{i-1})$. Then, $\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_i|S_{i-1}})\geq \mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S'_i|S_{i-1}}).$
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:total_info}
The total information of $S_{<i}$ and $S_{i}$ about the final answer $A$ can be decomposed into four non-negative components:
\begin{align*} 
\mathrm{I}({A;S_{<i},S_{i}}) = \mathrm{Red}({A{:}S_{<i},S_i}) +\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_{<i}| S_{i}}) + \mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_i| S_{<i}}) + \mathrm{Syn}({A{:}S_{<i},S_{i}}).
                                                                                                                                                                     
                 
                                                                                                                                
\end{align*}
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- defn-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:red}
For some $\eta \geq 1$ and $i > 1$, our proposed measure of repetitiveness in a reasoning step $S_i$ is defined as, \begin{equation} \text{Step-RRI} := \mathrm{RRI}_i =  \mathrm{Red}({A{:}S_i,S_{<i}}) - \eta \cdot \max\{\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_i|S_{<i}}), \mathrm{Syn}({A{:}S_i,S_{<i}})\}.\end{equation}
<!-- end defn-2 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:repititive2}
Let the current step $S_i=(S_A,S_N)$ where $S_A$ is relevant to the answer $A$ and $S_N$ is additional independent text, i.e., $\mathrm{I}({A;S_A})>0$ and $S_N \perp A, S_{<i}$ (noise). Suppose the current step is repetitive with the logic already contained in past steps $S_{<i}$, i.e., the relevant part $S_A \subseteq S_{<i}$. Then, we have: (i) $\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_{i}|S_{<i}})$ and $\mathrm{Syn}({A{:}S_{i},S_{<i}})$ are both zero; (ii) $\text{Step-RRI} = \mathrm{Red}({A{:}S_{i},S_{<i}}) - \eta \cdot \max\{\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_i|S_{<i}}), \mathrm{Syn}({A{:}S_i,S_{<i}})\} >0.$
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- defn-3: body_md -->
\label{def:point-wiseRRI}
For a given question $Q$ with a reasoning trajectory of length $T$, we define the Trajectory-RRI as,
$\text{Trajectory-RRI} := \mathrm{RRI}(Q)
=
\sum_{i=2}^{T}
\mathbb{I}\left[\mathrm{RRI}_i > 0\right],
$
where $\mathbb{I}[\cdot]$ denotes the indicator function.
<!-- end defn-3 -->
<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
Let $B'=f(B)$ where $f(\cdot)$ is a deterministic function. Then,
$$\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}B|C}) \geq \mathrm{Uni}({A{:}B'|C})   .$$
\label{lemma:monotonicity_bob}
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
For all $(A,B,C,C')$, we have:
$$\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}B|C \cup C'}) \leq \mathrm{Uni}({A{:}B|C}).$$
\label{lemma:monotonicity_eve}
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The proof follows from monotonicity under local operations, Lemma~\ref{lemma:monotonicity_bob}, since $S_i' = S_i+N$ is a local operation on $S_i$. 

We provide the detailed proof here for completeness. Since $N \perp (A,S_i,S_{i-1})$, the random variables $(A,S_{i-1})$, $S_i$, and $S_i'$ form a Markov chain, $(A,S_{i-1})- S_i - S_i'$. Let $P'$ be the marginal distribution of $(S_i',A,S_{i-1})$, and $P$ be the marginal distribution of $(S_i,A,S_{i-1})$. Let $Q^*
=\arg\min_{Q \in \Delta_P} I_Q(S_i;A\mid S_{i-1}),$
and let
\[
Q^{*'}(s_i',a,s_{i-1})
=
\sum_{s_i} P'(s_i'\mid s_i)Q^*(s_i,a,s_{i-1}).
\]
Then $Q^{*'} \in \Delta_{P'}$. By definition,
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Uni}({S_i{:}A|S_{i-1}})
&= I_{Q^*}(S_i;A\mid S_{i-1}) \\
&\overset{(a)}\geq I_{Q^{*'}}(S_i';A\mid S_{i-1}) \\
&\geq \min_{Q' \in \Delta_{P'}}
    I_{Q'}(S_i';A\mid S_{i-1}) \\
&= \mathrm{Uni}({S_i'{:}A|S_{i-1}}),
\end{align*}
Here, (a) uses the conditional form of the data processing inequality. Therefore, $\mathrm{Uni}({S_i'{:}A\mid S_{i-1}})\leq\mathrm{Uni}({S_i{:}A\mid S_{i-1}})$, i.e., the unique information contributed by the noisy step $S_i'$ about $A$ cannot exceed that of the original step $S_i$.

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
To begin with, 
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_{i}|S_{<i}}) & \overset{(a)}{=} \mathrm{I}({A;S_{i}|S_{<i}}) -\mathrm{Syn}({A{:}S_{i},S_{<i}}) \\
& \overset{(b)}{\leq} \mathrm{I}({A;S_{i}|S_{<i}}) \\
& \overset{(c)}= \mathrm{I}({A;S_A,S_N|S_{<i}}) \\
& \overset{(d)}= \mathrm{I}({A;S_N|S_{<i}}) + \mathrm{I}({A;S_A|S_{<i},S_N}) \\
& \overset{(e)}= 0 + \mathrm{I}({A;S_A|S_{<i},S_N}) \\
& \overset{(f)}=0.
\end{align*}

Here, step (a) holds from  equation~\ref{eq:pid_3} and step (b) holds from the non-negativity of PID terms, $\mathrm{Syn}({A{:}S_{i},S_{<i}})\geq 0 $. Step (c) holds because $S_i=(S_A,S_N)$. Step (d) follows directly from the chain rule of mutual information. Step (e) holds because $S_N \perp A, S_{<i}$ and step (f) holds because $S_A \subseteq S_{<i}$.

Similarly, $\mathrm{Syn}({A{:}S_{i},S_{<i}}) = \mathrm{I}({A;S_{i}|S_{<i}}) - \mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_{i}|S_{<i}}) \leq \mathrm{I}({A;S_{i}|S_{<i}}) = 0$.

Next, since $\text{Step-RRI} := \mathrm{Red}({A{:}S_i,S_{<i}}) - \eta \cdot \max\{\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_i|S_{<i}}), \mathrm{Syn}({A{:}S_i,S_{<i}})\}$, and $\max\{\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_i|S_{<i}}), \mathrm{Syn}({A{:}S_i,S_{<i}})\} = 0,$ we have,
\begin{align*}
\text{Step-RRI} = \mathrm{Red}({A{:}S_{i},S_{<i}}) & \overset{(a)}{=} \mathrm{I}({A;S_{<i}}) -  \mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_{<i}|S_{i}}) \\
& \overset{(b)}{\geq} \mathrm{I}({A;S_{<i}}) -  \mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_{<i}|S_A}) \\
& \overset{(c)}= \mathrm{Red}({A{:}S_A,S_{<i}}) \\
& \overset{(d)}= \mathrm{I}({A;S_A}) - \mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_A|S_{<i}}) \\
& \overset{(e)}\geq  \mathrm{I}({A;S_A}) - \mathrm{I}({A;S_A|S_{<i}}) \\
& \overset{(f)}= \mathrm{I}({A;S_A}) \\
& \overset{(g)}> 0.
\end{align*}
Here, step (a) holds from equation~\ref{eq:pid_2}, step (b) holds from Lemma~\ref{lemma:monotonicity_eve} (Monotonicity under adversarial side information) for unique information since $S_i=(S_A,S_N)$, step (c) and (d) hold from equation~\ref{eq:pid_2}, step (e) holds from equation~\ref{def:brojaUni}, $\mathrm{Uni}({A{:}S_{<i}|S_A}) \leq \mathrm{I}({A;S_A|S_{<i}})$ and non-negativity of PID terms, step (f) holds because $S_A \subseteq S_{<i}$, and step (g) holds since the mutual information of the relevant part $S_A$ is positive.

To assess whether Step-RRI captures true repetitiveness in a reasoning trajectory, we consider three word problems from the GSM8K dataset. For each problem, we first obtain reasoning trajectories from DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B and QwQ-32B. We then generate 1,000 structure-preserving samples by parameterizing selected numerical values in the problem and updating the corresponding quantities in the reasoning trajectory while preserving its reasoning structure. For Problem~1, `Alisa biked \{x\} miles per hour for 4.5 hours. Stanley biked at \{y\} miles per hour for 2.5 hours. How many miles did Alisa and Stanley bike in total?'', we independently sample $x$ and $y$ uniformly from the integer range $[10,100]$ and update the corresponding calculations in the trajectory. For Problem~2, `'Anthony had {x} pencils. He gave 1/2 of his pencils to Brandon, and 3/5 of the remaining pencils to Charlie. He kept the remaining pencils. How many pencils did Anthony keep?'', and for Problem~3, ``James runs {x} miles a day for 5 days a week. If he runs 10 miles an hour, how many hours does he run a week?'', we similarly sample $x$ uniformly from the integer range $[10,1000]$ and update all dependent quantities in the reasoning trajectory. Then, we apply \textsc{Slider}\xspace to get the RRI measures.

For our analysis on GSM8K~\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}, we first identify the problems from the training set for which both QwQ-32B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B reach the correct final numerical answer. Each model response consists of a thinking trajectory followed by the final response, separated by the \texttt{</think>} tag. We extract the text preceding this tag as the reasoning trajectory and use only this portion throughout the analysis. For GPT-4.1, we use the same 1,000 problems and treat its generated responses as the corresponding reasoning trajectories.
