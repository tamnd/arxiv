<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{crossing_lemma} Let $X$ be a $d$-dimensional simplicial complex with $|X(d)|\geq a|X(d-1)|$, for $a>0$. \\
1) If $d\neq 2$ then 
\[\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}(X)\geq \frac{a-(d+2)}{a^{d+2}}\cdot\frac{|X(d)|^{(d+2)}}{|X(d-1)|^{(d+1)}}.\] 
2) Moreover, still for $d\neq 2$, if $d+2 <  a\leq d+2+\frac{2}{d+1}$, this is best possible (up to lower order terms) as $|X(d-1)|\to \infty$.
3) For any $d\ge 1$, one has
$\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(X) \geq \operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_{\mathbb S^{2d}}(X)\geq \frac{a-(d+2)}{a^{d+2}}\cdot\frac{|X(d)|^{(d+2)}}{|X(d-1)|^{(d+1)}}.$
<!-- end corollary-1 -->

<!-- corollary-5: body_md -->

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Consider a drawing $f:X\to\mathbb R^{2d}$ attaining $\operatorname{cr}(X)$. Let $Y\subset X$ be a simplicial complex which is maximal with the property that $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}(f(Y))=0$ (so that, in particular, $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}(Y)=0$). Since the map $f$ can be assumed to be generic, $Y_{(d-1)}=X_{(d-1)}$. 
By Theorem~\ref{Adi}, $|Y(d)|\leq (d+2)|Y(d-1)|$.  
Now, for each $d$-face $\sigma \in X(d)\setminus Y(d)$, let $Y'$ be the smallest subcomplex of $X$ that contains $Y$ and $\sigma$. By maximality of $Y$, $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}(f(Y'))>0$. Thus, the image of $\sigma$ under $f$ must intersect some other $d$-face of $Y$ in its relative interior. Repeating this for every such $\sigma$, each of these faces contributes at least one crossing, and hence \[\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}(X)=\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}(f(X))\geq |X(d)|-|Y(d)|\geq |X(d)|-(d+2)|X(d-1)|.\]
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{whitney_trick}\cite{freedman1994van} Let $f\colon X \to \mathbb{R}^{2d}$ be a generic PL immersion of a simplicial complex $X$ of dimension $d\geq 3$.
Suppose that either for some $d$-simplex $\sigma$ $f(\sigma)$ is not an embedding, or $f(\sigma)$ intersects $f(\sigma')$ in its interior for two $d$-simplexes $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ having a common vertex in $X$. Then, there is an immersion $g \colon X \to \mathbb R^{2d}$ differing from $f$ only in $\sigma$ (in the second case in $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$) such that the number of self-intersection points of $g(\sigma)$ (or, in the second case, between $g(\sigma)$ and $g(\sigma')$) is one less than the number of self-intersection points of $f(\sigma)$ (or, in the second case, intersections between $f(\sigma)$ and $f(\sigma')$), and the rest of the singularities are unchanged.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- head-5: body_md -->
To prove Lemma~\ref{whitney_trick}, one isotopes the map along a $2$-disk that avoids the rest of the image of $f$; this can be assumed to exist by a general position-dimension counting argument, provided that the dimension of the codomain is at least $5$. This is a version of the so-called \emph{Whitney trick}. The difficulty in $\mathbb R^4$ is similar to why the Poincar\'e conjecture is easier to prove in dimensions $d>4$ than in dimensions $d=3,4$. The main result of~\cite{freedman1994van} implies that the analogue for $d=2$, $2d=4$, is false.
<!-- end head-5 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $X$ be an arbitrary $d$-dimensional simplicial complex and $f\colon X \to \mathbb S^{2d}$ be a map that minimizes crossings; $f$ can be assumed to be a generic map. Moreover, if $d\neq 2$, by Lemma~\ref{whitney_trick}, we can assume that there do not exist any two simplices $\sigma,\sigma'\in X(d)$ which share at least one vertex and such that the relative interior of $f(\sigma)$ intersects the relative interior of $f(\sigma')$. In the case of $d=2$ and facewise affine or spherically geodesic triangles, the previous property is immediately satisfied as soon as the vertices are in general position, which can be assumed for a map that minimizes the number of crossings. In all cases that we treat, it can be assumed that crossings only arise between vertex-disjoint simplices. This is precisely why the assumption that $d\neq 2$ is important.


Corollary~\ref{coro:cr-d+2} and Lemma~\ref{whitney_trick} imply that, for $d\neq 2$, $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(X)\geq |X(d)|-(d+2)|X(d-1)|$.

Let $R_p$ be a random induced subcomplex where each vertex of $X$ is included with probability $p$ independently from the rest. 
Thus,
\[\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(R_p)\geq |R_p(d)|-(d+2)|R_p(d-1)|.\]
Using independence, the above inequality, and linearity of expectation,
\[p^{2d+2}\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(X)\geq \mathbb E[\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(R_p)]\geq \mathbb E\left[|R_p(d)|-(d+2)|R_p(d-1)|\right]=p^{d+1} |X(d)|-(d+2) p^d |X(d-1)|.\]
Hence 
\[\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}(X)\geq \operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(X)\geq  \frac{|X(d)|}{p^{d+1}}-\frac{(d+2)|X(d-1)|}{p^{d+2}}.\]
Let us set $p=\frac{a|X(d-1)|}{|X(d)|}$ (the assumption $|X(d)|\ge a|X(d-1)|$ implies that $p\leq 1$).
Replacing $p$ in the previous formula and simplifying, we obtain the claimed bound.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{amplification}
If a linear inequality of the form
$\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_{\iota}(X)\geq \alpha |X(d)|-\beta|X(d-1)|$ holds for all complexes (where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are positive numbers), then under hypothesis $|X(d)|\geq a|X(d-1)|$, the following crossing number inequality is valid:
\[\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}(X)\geq \operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_{\iota}(X)\geq \frac{\alpha a-\beta}{a^{d+2}}\frac{|X(d)|^{d+2}}{|X(d-1)|^{d+1}}.\]

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
If $X$ is a $d$-complex that embeds in $\mathbb S^{2d}$, then $X *[q]$ is a $(d+1)$-complex that embeds in $\mathbb S^{2d+2}$, moreover $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(X)=0$ implies $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(X*[q])=0$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
View $\mathbb S^{2d+2}$ as the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^{2d+1}\oplus\mathbb R^2$. It contains the equatorial sphere $\mathbb S^{2d}\times\{0\}$ and the orthogonal circle $\{0\}\times\mathbb S^1$. Given $f\colon X\to\mathbb S^{2d}$, place its image in the first sphere and choose distinct points $y_1,\ldots,y_q$ on the orthogonal circle.
Starting from $f$, we construct $F\colon X*[q]\to\mathbb S^{2d+2}$ by mapping each cone $X*\{y_i\}$ to the spherical cone with base $f(X)$ and apex $y_i$: each segment from $y_i$ to $x$ is mapped to the shorter great-circle arc from $y_i$ to $f(x)$. Explicitly, writing $y_i$ for its $\mathbb R^2$ coordinate,
\[F([x,y_i,t])=\bigl(\sin(\pi t/2)f(x),\cos(\pi t/2)y_i\bigr),\qquad 0\leq t\leq1,\]
where $t=0$ is the apex and $t=1$ is the base. The formula is well defined under the join identifications and takes values in $\mathbb S^{2d+2}$. Distinct cones meet only along their common base, and within each cone the formula is injective whenever $f$ is injective. Thus $F$ is an embedding if $f$ is.

Now suppose that $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(X)=0$, and choose $f$ with no independent crossings. Two vertex-disjoint $(d+1)$-faces of $X*[q]$ have the form $\sigma*\{y_i\}$ and $\tau*\{y_j\}$, where $i\neq j$ and $\sigma,\tau\in X(d)$ are vertex-disjoint. Their images can meet only in the common base, where their intersection is $f(\sigma)\cap f(\tau)=\varnothing$. A sufficiently close generic PL approximation of $F$ preserves these finitely many disjointness relations, since the relevant images are compact. Thus, $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(X*[q])=0$.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $X$ be a $2$-complex with $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(X)=0$. Consider $Y=X*[q]$; it has $|Y(3)|=q|X(2)|$, $|Y(2)|=|X(2)|+q|X(1)|$, and $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(Y)=0$. Hence, by Adiprasito's theorem for $d=3$, combined with Lemma~\ref{whitney_trick}, $|Y(3)|\leq 5|Y(2)|$. This yields $q|X(2)|\leq 5(|X(2)|+q|X(1)|)$ or, in other words, $|X(2)|\leq 5 \frac{q}{q-5}|X(1)|$. Since this is true for all $q$, $|X(2)|\leq 5|X(1)|$. The contrapositive is that if $|X(2)|> 5|X(1)|$, then $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_{\iota}(X)>0$. Arguing as in Corollary~\ref{coro:cr-d+2} we derive $\operatorname{\mathrm{cr}}_\iota(X)\geq |X(2)|-5|X(1)|$. Lemma~\ref{amplification} finishes the proof.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:first-sparse-interval}
For every $d\geq1$ and $d+2\leq a\leq d+2+\frac{2}{d+1}$, we have $b_d(a)\leq\frac{a-d-2}{a^{d+2}}$. The construction gives $\mathrm{PL}$ drawings in which every $d$-face is crossed at most once.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $n>2d+3$ be relatively prime to $d+1$, and label the vertices of the cyclic polytope $P=C(n,2d)$ by $0,1,\ldots,n-1$ in their moment-curve order (see~\cite[Chapter 0]{Gunter_polytopes_lectures} for background on cyclic polytopes). Call a $(d+1)$-element vertex set separated if it contains no cyclically consecutive vertices, including the pair $\{n-1,0\}$. By Gale's evenness criterion, every set of at most $d$ vertices is a boundary face, and a $(d+1)$-element set spans a boundary $d$-face exactly when it is not separated. Also, the unique affine dependence among $2d+2$ moment-curve vertices has alternating signs. Thus, two vertex-disjoint $d$-simplices cross exactly when their vertices alternate in cyclic order, in which case their relative interiors meet transversely in one point. Simplices sharing vertices meet only in their common face, since any $2d+1$ vertices are affinely independent.

For $r\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, let $\mathcal A_r$ consist of the separated sets whose labels sum to $r$ modulo $n$. There are $\frac{n}{d+1}\binom{n-d-2}{d}$ separated sets: mark one vertex and count the $d+1$ cyclic gaps, which are at least two and sum to $n$, then divide by $d+1$. Cyclic translation $A\mapsto A+1$ maps $\mathcal A_r$ bijectively to $\mathcal A_{r+d+1}$. Our choice of $n$ therefore gives $|\mathcal A_r|=\frac{1}{d+1}\binom{n-d-2}{d}$ for every $r$.

Let $A$ and $B$ be $(d+1)$-element sets and suppose that they alternate in cyclic order. Choose integer lifts $a_0<b_0<a_1<b_1<\cdots<a_d<b_d<a_0+n$ of their $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$-valued labels. Summing the inequalities $1\leq b_i-a_i\leq a_{i+1}-a_i-1$, where $a_{d+1}=a_0+n$, gives
\[d+1\leq\sum_{i=0}^d(b_i-a_i)\leq n-d-1.\]
The sum is congruent modulo $n$ to the difference of the residue classes of $B$ and $A$. Consequently, among the simplices in $\mathcal A_r\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal A_{r+d+1}$, where $0\leq r\leq n-d-2$, crossings occur only between the first and last classes. Writing $A$ for the set in the first class, equality holds on the left, so $B=A+1$. Conversely, every separated $A$ alternates with $A+1$. Thus the crossing pairs in this block are precisely $\{A,A+1\}$ with $A\in\mathcal A_r$.

Glue two copies of $P$ along their boundary to obtain a PL $2d$-sphere, and draw the whole $d$-skeleton of $\partial P$ on their common boundary. In the first copy, we draw the affine simplices from $\mathcal A_0\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal A_d$, and in the second we draw those from $\mathcal A_{d+2}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal A_{2d+2}$. The proper faces of all these simplices already lie on the common boundary of the two copies of $P$, their relative interiors lie in their assigned open half, and the preceding observation shows that this drawing has no crossings. We may now add any selection of the simplices of $\mathcal A_{d+1}$ in the first copy and of $\mathcal A_{2d+3}$ in the second. Each added simplex crosses exactly one previously drawn simplex, its translate by $-1$, and no two added simplices cross. All these vertex sets are distinct. Removing a point outside the drawing gives the required PL drawing in $\mathbb R^{2d}$.

To obtain asymptotic density $a$, add exactly $\left\lfloor(a-d-2)\binom{n-d-2}{d}\right\rfloor$ of these simplices, which is possible throughout the stated interval, and call the resulting complex $X_n$. Since the nonseparated sets are precisely the boundary $d$-faces, the number of such faces is
\[f_d(\partial P)=\binom{n}{d+1}-\frac{n}{d+1}\binom{n-d-2}{d}=d\binom nd+O_d(n^{d-1}).\]

<!-- theorem-11: body_md -->
\label{thm:semi_algebraic_regularity}
For every $0<\eta<1/2$, there exists an integer $M=M(d,\eta)$ such that every sufficiently large finite point set $V\subset\mathbb R^{2d}$ in general position admits an equitable partition $V=V_1\cup\cdots\cup V_K$, where $\max\{2d+2,\eta^{-1}\}\leq K\leq M$, such that all but at most $\eta\binom K{2d+2}$ choices of $2d+2$ distinct parts have same-type transversals. Here, an equitable partition is one whose part sizes differ by at most one.

\[V=V_1\cup\cdots\cup V_K.
\]

\[(I,J),\qquad I,J\subseteq [K], \quad |I|=|J|=d+1,\quad I\cap J=\varnothing,\]
such that $I\cup J$ is same-type and every \(I\)-transversal \(d\)-simplex crosses every \(J\)-transversal \(d\)-simplex, then
\[\sum_{(I,J)\in\mathcal T}\prod_{i\in I\cup J} |V_i|\geq\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1})-O_d\left(\frac{n^{2d+2}}{K}\right)-O_d(\eta n^{2d+2}).\]

\[|V_i|=(1+o(1))\frac nK.\]
Because $p n^d\to\infty$, with high probability, uniformly over all disjoint $(d+1)$-tuples of parts,
\[F_X(V_{i_0},\dots,V_{i_d})=p\prod_{r=0}^d |V_{i_r}|(1+o(1)).
\]

Fix $(I,J)\in\mathcal T$. Every $d$-face of $X$ using one vertex from each part $V_i$ with $i\in I$ crosses every $d$-face of $X$ using one vertex from each part $V_j$ with $j\in J$. Therefore, this pair $(I,J)$ contributes \[F_X((V_i)_{i\in I})\, F_X((V_j)_{j\in J})=p^2\prod_{i\in I\cup J}|V_i|\,(1+o(1))\]
crossings of $X$. Summing over $(I,J)\in\mathcal T$, we obtain
\[\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(X)=\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(f)\geq p^2\sum_{(I,J)\in\mathcal T}\prod_{i\in I\cup J}|V_i|\,(1+o(1)).\]
Hence \[\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(X)\geq p^2\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1})-O_d\left(\frac{p^2 n^{2d+2}}{K}\right)-O_d(\eta p^2 n^{2d+2})-o(p^2 n^{2d+2}).\] 
Letting $\eta\to0$, and then $K=K(d,\eta)\to\infty$ accordingly, we get
\[\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(X)\geq p^2\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1})-o_{\mathbb P}(p^2 n^{2d+2}).\]

It remains to prove the upper bound. Take an optimal rectilinear drawing of the complete complex $\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1}$, so that the number of crossing pairs of $d$-faces is exactly $\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1}).$
Draw $X$ on the same vertex set by mapping the vertices of $X$ in a fixed manner, independently of the randomness involved in the construction of the complex. Let $\mathcal C$ be the set of crossing pairs of $d$-faces in this drawing of $\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1}$. We have \[|\mathcal C|=\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1}).\] For $c=\{\sigma,\tau\}\in\mathcal C$, let $I_c$ be the indicator that
both $\sigma$ and $\tau$ belong to $X$. The number of crossings in this fixed drawing of $X$ is \[Z:=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C} I_c.\] Hence\[\mathbb E Z=p^2 \operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1}).\]

We claim that \[Z=p^2 \operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1})+o_{\mathbb P}(p^2 n^{2d+2}),\] where $o_{\mathbb P}\bigl(p^2 n^{2d+2}\bigr)$ denotes an error term which goes to $0$ in probability after being divided by $p^2n^{2d+2}$.
Indeed, \[\operatorname{Var}(Z)\leq O_d(p^2 n^{2d+2})+O_d(p^3 n^{3d+3}).\] The first term comes from the variances of the individual indicators. For the covariance term, two indicators can be dependent only if their two crossing pairs share a $d$-face. There are $O_d(n^{d+1})$ choices for the shared $d$-face, and then $O_d(n^{d+1})$ choices for each of the two other $d$-faces, giving $O_d(n^{3d+3})$ dependent pairs, each with covariance at most $O(p^3)$.

Since $p n^d\to\infty$, we have that
\[\frac{p^2 n^{2d+2}}{p^4 n^{4d+4}}=o(1)\quad\text{and}\quad \frac{p^3 n^{3d+3}}{p^4 n^{4d+4}}=o(1).\]
Hence, \[\operatorname{Var}(Z)=o(p^4 n^{4d+4}).\]
By Chebyshev's inequality, \[Z=p^2 \operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R{2d}}(\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1})+o_{\mathbb P}(p^2 n^{2d+2}).\]
Since $\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(X)\leq Z$ for every realization, we get \[\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(X)\leq p^2 \operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1})+o_{\mathbb P}(p^2 n^{2d+2}).\]

Combining the lower and upper bounds gives
\[\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(X)=p^2\operatorname{cr}_{\mathbb R^{2d}}(\Delta_{(d)}^{n-1})+o_{\mathbb P}(p^2 n^{2d+2}).\]

<!-- theorem-13: body_md -->
\label{thm:volume_threshold_strict}
For every $d\geq1$, we have $\inf_{0<\theta<1/2}C_d^{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta)<C_d^{\mathrm{comp}}$, and consequently $B_d<C_d^{\mathrm{comp}}$.
<!-- end theorem-13 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
Write $\varepsilon_\theta=\mathbb P(q>\theta)$ and $r_\theta=\mathbb E[q\mid q>\theta]$. Simplicial cones can have normalized solid angle arbitrarily close to $1/2$, for instance when $d$ of the vertices nearly positively span a hyperplane of their linear span and the last vertex lies slightly off it. By continuity, such configurations have neighborhoods of positive probability, so $\varepsilon_\theta>0$ for $\theta<1/2$. Moreover $\varepsilon_\theta\to0$ as $\theta\uparrow1/2$, because $q<1/2$ almost surely, and $r_\theta\to1/2$, because $\theta<r_\theta<1/2$. Since $P(\theta)=1-\varepsilon_\theta$ and $m(\theta)=\bar q-\varepsilon_\theta r_\theta$, we have
\[
\frac{C_d^{\mathrm{vol}}(\theta)}{C_d^{\mathrm{comp}}}=1+\varepsilon_\theta\left(d+2-\frac{2r_\theta}{\bar q}\right)+O(\varepsilon_\theta^2).
\]
The coefficient tends to $d+2-2^{d+1}<0$, proving the claim.

<!-- lemma-14: body_md -->
\label{lem:gaussian_kernel_quadratic}
Let $G$ be an $M\times(M+1)$ standard Gaussian matrix. Almost surely its kernel is one-dimensional; let $\lambda$ be a unit vector spanning it. Conditional on the kernel line, for every deterministic $x\in\mathbb R^{M+1}$,
\[
\|Gx\|^2\stackrel{d}{=}\|P_{\lambda^\perp}x\|^2\chi^2_{M+1}.
\]
Here $\chi_{M+1}$ denotes the Euclidean norm of a vector of $M+1$ independent standard Gaussian variables, sampled independently of the kernel line, so that $\chi_{M+1}^2$ has the chi-square distribution with $M+1$ degrees of freedom. Moreover, the kernel line is uniformly distributed and independent of $GG^\top$.
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
Write the thin QR decomposition of $G^\top$ as $G^\top=QR$. Here $Q$ is an $(M+1)\times M$ matrix with orthonormal columns, so $Q^\top Q=I_M$, and $R$ is an $M\times M$ upper triangular matrix with positive diagonal entries. The orthonormal frame formed by the columns of $Q$ is uniform and independent of $R$. Conditional on its column space $\lambda^\perp$, it is still a uniform orthonormal frame of that space. In particular, the kernel line, which is the orthogonal complement of the column space of $Q$, is uniformly distributed and independent of $R$, hence of $GG^\top=R^\top R$. Thus $Q^\top x$ is uniform on the sphere in $\mathbb R^M$ of radius $r=\|P_{\lambda^\perp}x\|$, independently of $R$. If $U$ is an independent uniform $M\times M$ orthogonal matrix and $e_1$ is the first standard basis vector of $\mathbb R^M$, then, conditional on the kernel line,
\[
\|Gx\|^2\stackrel d=r^2e_1^\top U^\top RR^\top Ue_1.
\]
The matrices $RR^\top$ and $R^\top R$ have the same eigenvalues, and $R^\top R=GG^\top$ has an orthogonally invariant distribution, independent of the kernel line. Consequently, $U^\top RR^\top U$ has the distribution of $GG^\top$. Finally, $(GG^\top)_{11}$ is the squared norm of a row of $G$, and therefore has distribution $\chi^2_{M+1}$.

<!-- lemma-15: body_md -->
\label{lem:gale_volume_separation}
Put $\theta_d=2^{-(d+1)}e^{d/400}$. For every $d\geq130$,
\[
\mathbb P(q>\theta_d)\leq e^{-d/400},\qquad\mathbb P(Q^*\leq\theta_d)\leq4e^{-d/230}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-15 -->
<!-- proof-28: body_md -->
The first bound is Markov's inequality: $\mathbb P(q>\theta_d)\leq\bar q/\theta_d=e^{-d/400}$.

For the second, let $A,B$ be independent $(2d+1)\times(d+1)$ standard Gaussian matrices. Normalizing their columns gives independent uniform spherical vertices, without changing the positive cones or their normalized solid angles. Let $q(A)$ denote the normalized solid angle of the cone generated by the columns of $A$, that is, the normalized volume of the corresponding spherical simplex, and let $\mathcal E$ be the event that the cones generated by $A$ and by $B$ meet nontrivially. The argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:crofton_random_geometric}, with the first simplex fixed, gives $\mathbb P(\mathcal E\mid A)=2\bar q\,q(A)$ and $\mathbb P(\mathcal E)=2\bar q^2$. Therefore the law of $A$ conditioned on $\mathcal E$ is its size-biased law. Denoting the ordinary law by $\mathbb P$ and this conditioned law by $\mathbb P^*$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:size_bias_likelihood}
\frac{d\mathbb P^*}{d\mathbb P}(A)=\frac{q(A)}{\bar q}=:L(A).
\end{equation}
In particular, under $\mathbb P^*$ the variable $q(A)$ has the law of $Q^*$, so
\[
\mathbb P(Q^*\leq\theta_d)=\mathbb P^*(q(A)\leq\theta_d)=\mathbb P^*(L\leq e^{d/400}).
\]

The $(2d+1)\times(2d+2)$ matrix $G=(A\ B)$ has a one-dimensional kernel almost surely. The event $\mathcal E$ says precisely that its unit kernel vector can be written as $\lambda=(a,-b)$, where $a,b\in\mathbb R^{d+1}$ have all coordinates positive. In Gale-dual terms, crossing is a sign condition on the kernel of $G$. For Gaussian vertices, the kernel line is uniform and independent of $GG^\top$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian_kernel_quadratic}). This independence is what what allows us to access the size-biased law: conditioning on $\mathcal E$ prescribes only the signs of the kernel vector. Therefore, under the joint law of $(A,B)$ conditioned on $\mathcal E$,
\[
(a,b)\stackrel d=\frac{(|Z_1|,\ldots,|Z_{2d+2}|)}{(\sum_{j=1}^{2d+2}Z_j^2)^{1/2}},
\]
where the $Z_j$ are independent standard Gaussians.

Set $u=(1,\ldots,1)/\sqrt{d+1}\in\mathbb R^{d+1}$ and $R(A)=\|Au\|^2/(2d+1)$. Under $\mathbb P$, we have $R(A)\stackrel d=\chi_{2d+1}^2/(2d+1)$. Conditional on the kernel line, Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian_kernel_quadratic}, applied to $x=(u,0)\in\mathbb R^{d+1}\times\mathbb R^{d+1}$, gives
\[
R(A)\stackrel d=(1-\langle a,u\rangle^2)\frac{\chi^2_{2d+2}}{2d+1}.
\]
Here the chi-square variable is independent of the kernel line. The identity therefore remains valid under conditioning on $\mathcal E$, which depends only on that line.

On the event that $\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}|Z_i|\geq0.7(d+1)$ and $\sum_{j=1}^{2d+2}Z_j^2\leq2.2(d+1)$, we have
\[
\langle a,u\rangle^2=\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}|Z_i|)^2}{(d+1)\sum_{j=1}^{2d+2}Z_j^2}\geq\frac{49}{220}>\frac15.
\]
Write $h(x)=x-1-\log x$. For a chi-square variable with $k$ degrees of freedom, the Chernoff bound at $kx$ is $e^{-kh(x)/2}$, for the upper tail when $x>1$ and the lower tail when $0<x<1$. The function $\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}|Z_i|$ is $\sqrt{d+1}$-Lipschitz with mean $(d+1)\sqrt{2/\pi}$. By Gaussian concentration, the first condition fails with probability at most $\exp\bigl(-(d+1)(\sqrt{2/\pi}-0.7)^2/2\bigr)\leq e^{-d/210}$. By the Chernoff bound, the second fails with probability at most $e^{-(d+1)h(11/10)}\leq e^{-d/214}$.

Let $\mathcal T=\{R(A)\leq22/25\}$. Since $h(22/25)>1/140$, the Chernoff bound gives
\[
\mathbb P(\mathcal T)\leq\exp\bigl(-(d+\tfrac12)h(22/25)\bigr)\leq e^{-d/140}.
\]
Under $\mathbb P^*$, the factor $1-\langle a,u\rangle^2$ is at most $4/5$ except on an event of probability at most $e^{-d/210}+e^{-d/214}$. Outside that event, the identity in law above bounds the conditional probability of $R(A)>22/25$ by
\[
\mathbb P\bigl(\chi^2_{2d+2}>\tfrac{11}{10}(2d+1)\bigr)\leq e^{-(d+1)h(x_d)},\qquad x_d=\frac{11}{10}\cdot\frac{2d+1}{2d+2},
\]
which is at most $e^{-d/230}$ for $d\geq130$. Hence
\[
\mathbb P^*(\mathcal T^c)\leq e^{-d/210}+e^{-d/214}+e^{-d/230}.
\]

Finally, the likelihood-ratio identity gives
\[
\begin{split}
\mathbb P^*(L\leq e^{d/400})&\leq\mathbb P^*(\mathcal T^c)+\mathbb P^*(\mathcal T\cap\{L\leq e^{d/400}\})\\
&=\mathbb P^*(\mathcal T^c)+\mathbb E\bigl[L\mathbf1_{\mathcal T\cap\{L\leq e^{d/400}\}}\bigr]\\
&\leq\mathbb P^*(\mathcal T^c)+e^{d/400}\,\mathbb P(\mathcal T)\leq4e^{-d/230},
\end{split}
\]
because $1/140-1/400>1/230$. This is the asserted bound for $Q^*$.

\leq\frac{16e^{-d/115}}{(1-e^{-d/400})^{d+2}}
