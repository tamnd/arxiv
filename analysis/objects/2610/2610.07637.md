\label{asm:loss-regularity}

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
First recall the Poisson product identity, including its short proof.
For disjoint sets \(B_r\) of finite intensity \(\nu(B_r)\), their counts \(N_r\) are independent Poisson variables.
Thus a function equal to \(f_r\) on \(B_r\) and to one elsewhere satisfies
\begin{align}
  \mathbb{E}\prod_j f(X_j)
  &=\prod_r\mathbb{E} f_r^{N_r}
  =\prod_r\sum_{k=0}^\infty
    e^{-\nu(B_r)}\frac{\nu(B_r)^k f_r^k}{k!}
  =\exp\ab\{\int(f-1)\,\mathrm d\nu\ab\}.
  \label{eq:replica-poisson-product-identity}
\end{align}
Approximation by simple functions gives this identity for \(f=e^{-h}\), \(h\geq0\), by monotone convergence, and for other integrands whenever both sides and the approximation are integrable.
Independent marks are included by replacing \(\nu\) with intensity times mark distribution.
The same simple-function calculation gives \(\mathbb{E}\sum_j h(X_j)=\int h\,\mathrm d\nu\) for \(h\geq0\).

For any independent scalar or vector mark \(c\), substitution \(r=s\lVert c\rVert^2\) gives
\begin{align}
  d_\alpha\int_0^\infty
    \mathbb{E}[1\wedge s\lVert c\rVert^2]s^{-1-\alpha/2}\,\mathrm ds
  &=d_\alpha\mathbb{E}\lVert c\rVert^\alpha
    \ab[\int_0^1r^{-\alpha/2}\,\mathrm dr
       +\int_1^\infty r^{-1-\alpha/2}\,\mathrm dr]
  \\
  &=\frac{4d_\alpha}{\alpha(2-\alpha)}
    \mathbb{E}\lVert c\rVert^\alpha.
  \label{eq:replica-marked-summability}
\end{align}
If this quantity is finite, there are almost surely only finitely many terms with \(s_j\lVert c_j\rVert^2>1\), and the sum of the remaining terms has finite expectation.
Consequently, \(\sum_j s_j\lVert c_j\rVert^2<\infty\) almost surely.
Taking \(c_j=\@ifstar\mathbf\boldsymbol v_j\), the partial Gaussian sums, conditional on \(\{s_j,\@ifstar\mathbf\boldsymbol v_j\}\), form an \(L^2\)-bounded martingale and converge almost surely and in conditional \(L^2\).
Their characteristic function is
\begin{align}
  \mathbb{E}\exp\ab(i\sum_j\sqrt{s_j}g_j\@ifstar\mathbf\boldsymbol k^\top\@ifstar\mathbf\boldsymbol v_j)
  &=\mathbb{E}\exp\ab(-\frac12\sum_j s_j(\@ifstar\mathbf\boldsymbol k^\top\@ifstar\mathbf\boldsymbol v_j)^2)
  \\
  &=\exp\ab\{d_\alpha\int_0^\infty
    \mathbb{E}_{\@ifstar\mathbf\boldsymbol v}\ab[e^{-s(\@ifstar\mathbf\boldsymbol k^\top\@ifstar\mathbf\boldsymbol v)^2/2}-1]
    s^{-1-\alpha/2}\,\mathrm ds\ab\}.
  \label{eq:replica-conditional-gaussian-series}
\end{align}
Here the second equality is \eqref{eq:replica-poisson-product-identity}.
Using \(\mathbb{E}_g e^{i\sqrt{s}gz}=e^{-sz^2/2}\) and then \eqref{eq:replica-levy-khintchine} proves \eqref{eq:replica-stable-poisson-transform}.
