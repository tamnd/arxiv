<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:scalar-tests}
For real-valued random variables \(X\) and \(Y\) in \(L^1\), the following conditions are equivalent:\footnote{In condition (iv), equality of means is obtained by letting \(t\to\pm\infty\) after subtracting \(|t|\).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y\);
    \item \(\mathbb{E}[X]=\mathbb{E}[Y]\) and \(\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\ \mathbb{E}[(X-t)_+]\le\mathbb{E}[(Y-t)_+]\);
    \item \(\mathbb{E}[X]=\mathbb{E}[Y]\) and \(\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\ \mathbb{E}[(X-t)_-]\le\mathbb{E}[(Y-t)_-]\);
    \item \(\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\ \mathbb{E}[|X-t|]\le\mathbb{E}[|Y-t|]\).
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
For \(\mathbb{R}^d\)-valued random variables \(X\) and \(Y\) in \(L^1\), we write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-cx}
\begin{aligned}
    X\lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}} Y
    &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \forall \xi\in\mathbb{R}^d,\ \xi'X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}}\xi'Y\\
    &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \forall \xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\ \xi'X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}}\xi'Y.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
By the scalar stop-loss characterisation \eqref{eq:stop-loss},
\eqref{eq:projected-cx} is equivalent to
\[
    \forall (\xi,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R},\
    \mathbb{E}[(\xi'X-t)_+]\le\mathbb{E}[(\xi'Y-t)_+].
\]
Indeed, these inequalities imply \(\xi'X\lesssim_{\mathrm{icx}}\xi'Y\) for both
\(\xi\) and \(-\xi\), hence equality of the projected means.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Under an \(L^2\) assumption, the converse follows by testing the functions \(x\mapsto(\xi'x)^2\). Under the sole assumption \(Z=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k)'\in L^1\), absolute-value tests suffice. Indeed, rotational invariance gives\footnote{If \(A'\xi\ne0\), consider \(O_A \in \mathcal O_k\) whose first column is
the unit vector \(A'\xi/\|A'\xi\|\). Then
\(O_A'A'\xi=\|A'\xi\|e_1\), so
\(\xi'AZ\stackrel{\mathcal D}{=}\xi'AO_AZ
=\|A'\xi\|Z_1\).
If \(A'\xi=0\), both sides of the identity are zero.}
\[
    \forall \xi\in\mathbb{R}^d,\ \mathbb{E}[|\xi'AZ|]
    =\sqrt{\xi'AA'\xi}\,\mathbb{E}[|Z_1|],
\]
and similarly for \(B\). Since \(\mathbb P(Z\ne0)>0\), rotational invariance ensures
\(\mathbb{E}[|Z_1|]>0\).\footnote{If \(\mathbb{E}[|Z_1|]=0\), then \(Z_1=0\)
almost surely. Rotational invariance implies that every coordinate
has the same distribution as \(Z_1\), so \(Z=0\) almost surely,
a contradiction.}
Testing \(AZ\lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}} BZ\) with the absolute-value function and
dividing by \(\mathbb{E}[|Z_1|]\), which is finite since \(Z\in L^1\), therefore yields \(AA'\le BB'\).
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
The above equivalence applies to elliptical distributions obtained
as linear images of a common radial random variable. The requirement
of a common radial distribution is essential -- see Theorem~\ref{thm:radial}.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:gauge-boundary}
Let \(\mathcal C\subset\mathbb{R}^d\) be open and convex, with \(0\in\mathcal C\). Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be \(\mathbb{R}^d\)-valued random variables in \(L^1\) satisfying
\[
    \mathbb P(X\in\partial\mathcal C)=1 \quad\text{and}\quad \mathbb P(Y\in\overline{\mathcal C})=1.
\]
If \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y\), then \(\mathbb P(Y\in\partial\mathcal C)=1\).
In particular, positive mass of \(Y\) in \(\mathcal C\)
rules out \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y\).
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Define the gauge of \(\mathcal C\) by
\[
    j_{\mathcal C}: x\in\mathbb{R}^d
    \longmapsto
    \inf\{\lambda>0\mid x\in\lambda\mathcal C\}.
\]
Since \(\mathcal C\) is convex and contains a ball \(B(0,r)\)
for some \(r>0\), its gauge function is well defined, nonnegative,
convex, and satisfies
\[
    \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^d,\ 
    j_{\mathcal C}(x)\le\frac{\|x\|}{r}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
    \mathcal C=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^d\mid j_{\mathcal C}(x)<1\}
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \overline{\mathcal C}
    =\{x\in\mathbb{R}^d\mid j_{\mathcal C}(x)\le1\},
\]
so \(j_{\mathcal C}=1\) on \(\partial\mathcal C\).
If \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y\), testing with \(j_{\mathcal C}\) gives
\[
    1=\mathbb{E}[j_{\mathcal C}(X)]
    \le\mathbb{E}[j_{\mathcal C}(Y)]\le1.
\]
Since \(0\le j_{\mathcal C}(Y)\le1\) almost surely, this implies
\(j_{\mathcal C}(Y)=1\) almost surely, hence
\(Y\in\partial\mathcal C\) almost surely.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:pinelis}
There exist finitely supported \(\mathbb{R}^2\)-valued random variables
\(X\) and \(Y\) such that
\[
    X\lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}} Y
    \quad\text{but}\quad
    X\not\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y.
\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let \(A,B,C\in\mathbb{R}^2\) be three non-collinear points, and let
\(O\) be their centre of gravity, assumed without loss of generality to be the origin. Consider
the triangle
\[
    T=\operatorname{Conv}(\{A,B,C\}),
\]
and let
\[
    D=\frac{A+B}{2},\quad
    E=\frac{B+C}{2}
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    F=\frac{C+A}{2}
\]
be its three edge midpoints.

Let \(X\) be uniformly distributed on \(\{D,E,F\}\) and let
\(Y\) have distribution

It remains to compare all one-dimensional projections.
Fix \(\xi\in\mathbb{R}^2\) and a convex function \(\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\),
and set
\[
    a=\xi'A,\quad b=\xi'B
    \quad\text{and}\quad c=\xi'C.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi'X)]
    &=\frac13\left(
        \phi\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)
       +\phi\left(\frac{b+c}{2}\right)
       +\phi\left(\frac{c+a}{2}\right)
      \right)\\
    \text{and}\quad
    \mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi'Y)]
    &=\frac16\bigl(\phi(a)+\phi(b)+\phi(c)\bigr)
      +\frac12\phi\left(\frac{a+b+c}{3}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Popoviciu's inequality~\cite{Popoviciu1965} states that,
for all convex functions \(\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\),
\[
    \forall a,b,c\in\mathbb{R},\ 
    \frac{\phi(a)+\phi(b)+\phi(c)}{3}
    +\phi\left(\frac{a+b+c}{3}\right)
    \ge
    \frac23\left(
        \phi\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)
       +\phi\left(\frac{b+c}{2}\right)
       +\phi\left(\frac{c+a}{2}\right)
    \right).
\]
Dividing by \(2\) yields precisely
\[
    \mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi'X)]\le\mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi'Y)].
\]
Since \(\xi\) and \(\phi\) were arbitrary, \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}} Y\).

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:angular-homogeneous}
Let \(\phi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}\) be even, convex, and positively
homogeneous of degree \(1\). For all \(\theta\in[0,2\pi)\), set
\[
    u_\theta=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)',
    \quad
    v_\theta=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)'
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    h(\theta)=\phi(u_\theta),
\]
and extend these definitions periodically to \(\mathbb{R}\).
Then \(h\) is continuous, \(\pi\)-periodic, and satisfies
\[
    \forall r\ge0,\ \forall\theta\in\mathbb{R},\quad
    \phi(ru_\theta)=rh(\theta).
\]
The distribution\footnote{Here the word distribution is in the sense of Schwartz, not in the sense of a probability distribution.} 
\(h+h''\) is a nonnegative, \(\pi\)-periodic measure \(\mu\), whose restriction to \([0,\pi)\) is finite, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:homogeneous-absolute}
    \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^2,\ 
    \phi(x)=\frac12\int_{[0,\pi)}|v_\theta'x|\,\mu(d\theta).
\end{equation}
If \(\phi\) is of class \(C^2\) on \(\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\}\),
then
\[
    \mu(d\theta)=\bigl(h(\theta)+h''(\theta)\bigr)\,d\theta,
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angular-sine}
    \forall\theta\in\mathbb{R},\ 
    h(\theta)=\frac12\int_\theta^{\theta+\pi}
       \sin(t-\theta)\bigl(h(t)+h''(t)\bigr)\,dt.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Homogeneity and evenness of \(\phi\) give the polar representation and
\(\pi\)-periodicity of \(h\). Continuity of \(h\) follows from convexity, hence continuity, of \(\phi\).

Suppose first that \(\phi\) is of class \(C^2\) on \(\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\}\).
Differentiating \(h(\theta)=\phi(u_\theta)\) twice gives
\[
    \forall\theta\in\mathbb{R},\ 
    h''(\theta)
    =v_\theta'\nabla^2\phi(u_\theta)v_\theta
     -u_\theta'\nabla\phi(u_\theta).
\]
By Euler's identity for positively homogeneous functions
of degree \(1\),
\[
    \forall\theta\in\mathbb{R},\ 
    u_\theta'\nabla\phi(u_\theta)=\phi(u_\theta)=h(\theta).
\]
Consequently, convexity of \(\phi\) yields
\[
    \forall\theta\in\mathbb{R},\ 
    h''(\theta)+h(\theta)
    =v_\theta'\nabla^2\phi(u_\theta)v_\theta\ge0.
\]
Integrating twice by parts and using
\(h(\theta+\pi)=h(\theta)\), we obtain
\[
    \int_\theta^{\theta+\pi}\sin(t-\theta)h''(t)\,dt
    =2h(\theta)
     -\int_\theta^{\theta+\pi}\sin(t-\theta)h(t)\,dt.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:angular-sine}. Since
\[
    \forall\theta,t\in\mathbb{R},\ 
    |v_\theta'u_t|=|\sin(t-\theta)|,
\]
periodicity and homogeneity give
\eqref{eq:homogeneous-absolute} in the smooth case.

For the general case, consider a sequence of nonnegative \(C^\infty\) mollifier functions
\((\eta_m)_{m\ge1}\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) satisfying
\[
    \forall m \ge 1, \quad \operatorname{supp}(\eta_m)\subset[-1/m,1/m]
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \int_\mathbb{R}\eta_m(\alpha)\,d\alpha=1.
\]

Writing \(\rho_\alpha\) for the rotation by angle \(\alpha\),
we define
\[
    \phi_m(x)
    =\int_\mathbb{R}\phi(\rho_\alpha(x))\eta_m(\alpha)\,d\alpha
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    h_m(\theta)=\phi_m(u_\theta).
\]
Each \(\phi_m\) is even, convex, and positively homogeneous
of degree \(1\). Moreover, a change of variable gives
\[
    \forall\theta\in\mathbb{R},\ 
    h_m(\theta)
    =\int_\mathbb{R} h(\theta+\alpha)\eta_m(\alpha)\,d\alpha
    =\int_\mathbb{R} h(t)\eta_m(t-\theta)\,dt.
\]
The first expression shows that \(h_m\) is \(\pi\)-periodic.
The second shows that \(h_m\) is \(C^\infty\).
Since \(\phi_m(ru_\theta)=rh_m(\theta)\), it follows that
\(\phi_m\) is smooth on \(\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\}\).

Uniform continuity of \(h\) and the support condition on
\(\eta_m\) give
\[
    \|h_m-h\|_\infty
    \le
    \sup_{\substack{\theta\in\mathbb{R}\\|\alpha|\le1/m}}
    |h(\theta+\alpha)-h(\theta)|
    \underset{m\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.
\]
By homogeneity,
\[
    \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^2,\ 
    |\phi_m(x)-\phi(x)|
    \le\|h_m-h\|_\infty\|x\|.
\]

By the smooth case, the measures
\[
    \mu_m(d\theta)
    =\bigl(h_m(\theta)+h_m''(\theta)\bigr)\,d\theta
\]
are nonnegative and satisfy
\[
    \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^2,\ 
    \phi_m(x)
    =\frac12\int_0^\pi|v_\theta'x|\,\mu_m(d\theta).
\]
Their masses on \([0,\pi]\) satisfy
\[
    \mu_m([0,\pi])
    =\int_0^\pi h_m(\theta)\,d\theta,
\]
and are therefore bounded. Compactness of \([0,\pi]\)
allows us to extract a subsequence, still indexed by \(m\),
converging weakly to a nonnegative finite measure \(\nu\) on
\([0,\pi]\).

Since all integrands of interest are \(\pi\)-periodic,
we combine any masses at the two endpoints by setting
\[
    \mu=\nu\big|_{[0,\pi)}+\nu(\{\pi\})\delta_0.
\]
Thus, for all continuous \(\pi\)-periodic functions \(\psi\),
\[
    \lim_{m\to+\infty}\int_0^\pi\psi(\theta)\,\mu_m(d\theta)
    =\int_{[0,\pi)}\psi(\theta)\,\mu(d\theta).
\]
In particular, for all smooth \(\pi\)-periodic functions \(\psi\),
two integrations by parts, with boundary terms vanishing
by periodicity, give
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \int_{[0,\pi)}\psi(\theta)\,\mu(d\theta)
    &=\lim_{m\to+\infty}
      \int_0^\pi
      \psi(\theta)\bigl(h_m(\theta)+h_m''(\theta)\bigr)
      \,d\theta\\
    &=\lim_{m\to+\infty}
      \int_0^\pi
      h_m(\theta)\bigl(\psi(\theta)+\psi''(\theta)\bigr)
      \,d\theta\\
    &=\int_0^\pi
      h(\theta)\bigl(\psi(\theta)+\psi''(\theta)\bigr)
      \,d\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]
The periodic extension of \(\mu\) is therefore \(h+h''\)
in the distributional sense. Finally, for fixed \(x\),
the function \(\theta\mapsto|v_\theta'x|\) is continuous
and \(\pi\)-periodic. Passing to the limit gives
\[
    \phi(x)
    =\lim_{m\to+\infty}\phi_m(x)
    =\frac12\int_{[0,\pi)}|v_\theta'x|\,\mu(d\theta),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:homogeneous-absolute}.

\label{prop:radial-cx}

\(x\mapsto(\|x\|-t)_+\) is convex. Hence

    \mathbb{E}[(R-t)_+]

    \le\mathbb{E}[(\|Y\|-t)_+]
    =\mathbb{E}[(S-t)_+].

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:projected-radial-threshold}
Define
\[
    K_d:\ (t,r)\in\mathbb{R}_+\times\mathbb{R}_+
    \longmapsto \mathbb{E}[(r|V_d|-t)_+].
\]
The minimum
\[
    p_{\mathrm{proj}}
    =\min_{0\le t<1}
      \frac{(1-q)K_d(t,a)+qK_d(t,b)}{K_d(t,1)}
\]
exists, is attained in \((0,1)\), and satisfies
\[
    0<p_{\mathrm{proj}}<\mathbb{E}[S]<1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
    \forall p\in(0,1),\ 
    RU_d\lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}} SU_d
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    p\le p_{\mathrm{proj}}.
\]
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Writing \(F_d\) for the cumulative distribution function of the random variable \(|V_d|\), integration by parts gives, for \(r>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
    K_d(t,r)
    &=\mathbf 1_{\{t<r\}}\int_{t/r}^1(rv-t)f_d(v)\,dv\\
    &=(r-t)_+-r\int_{\min(t/r,1)}^1F_d(v)\,dv.
\end{aligned}
\]
The latter expression shows continuity for \(r>0\).
Since \(K_d(t,0)=0\) and \(0\le K_d(t,r)\le r\),
\(K_d\) is continuous on \(\mathbb{R}_+\times\mathbb{R}_+\).
Since \(f_d\) is positive on \((0,1)\), the former expression gives
\[
    \forall t\in[0,1),\  K_d(t,1)>0.
\]
We may therefore define
\[
    H_d:\ t\in[0,1)\longmapsto
    \frac{(1-q)K_d(t,a)+qK_d(t,b)}{K_d(t,1)}.
\]
This function is continuous and positive.
As \(t\uparrow1\), its numerator tends to
\(qK_d(1,b)>0\), whereas its denominator tends to zero.
Consequently,
\[
    \lim_{t\uparrow1}H_d(t)=+\infty,
\]
so \(H_d\) attains a positive minimum on \([0,1)\).

Moreover, \(H_d(0)=\mathbb{E}[S]\). Since \(|V_d|>0\) almost surely, dominated convergence gives
\[
    \forall r>0,\ 
    \partial_{t+}K_d(0,r)=\lim_{t\downarrow0}
      \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(r|V_d|-t)_+-r|V_d|}{t}\right]=-1.
\]
Straightforward computation then gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    H_{d,+}'(0)
    &=\frac{\mathbb{E}[S]-1}{\mathbb{E}[|V_d|]}<0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(p_{\mathrm{proj}}<\mathbb{E}[S]\), and every minimiser belongs
to \((0,1)\).

By Proposition~\ref{prop:radial-projections}, one-dimensional
projected convex order is equivalent to
\(R|V_d|\lesssim_{\mathrm{icx}} S|V_d|\). For nonnegative random variables,
it suffices to compare stop-loss transforms at nonnegative
thresholds. Since \(K_d(t,1)\) vanishes for \(t\ge1\)
and is positive for \(t\in[0,1)\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
    RU_d\lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}} SU_d
    &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \forall t\ge0,\ 
    pK_d(t,1)\le(1-q)K_d(t,a)+qK_d(t,b)\\
    &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \forall t\in[0,1),\  p\le H_d(t)\\
    &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    p\le\min_{0\le t<1}H_d(t)=p_{\mathrm{proj}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:radial-strict-gap}
We have

    p_{\mathrm{cx}}<p_{\mathrm{proj}}.

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Since \(a<1<b\), we write
\[
    1=(1-\theta)a+\theta b
    \quad\text{where}\quad
    \theta=\frac{1-a}{b-a}\in(0,1).
\]
For \(t\ge0\), the function \(r\mapsto K_d(t,r)\)
is convex, so
\[
    \forall t\ge0,\ 
    K_d(t,1)\le(1-\theta)K_d(t,a)+\theta K_d(t,b).
\]
Using \(q=p_{\mathrm{cx}}\theta\), we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplier-jensen}
    \forall t\in[0,1),\ 
    H_d(t)-p_{\mathrm{cx}}
    =(1-p_{\mathrm{cx}})\frac{K_d(t,a)}{K_d(t,1)} +p_{\mathrm{cx}}\left(
      \frac{(1-\theta)K_d(t,a)+\theta K_d(t,b)-K_d(t,1)}
           {K_d(t,1)}\right).
\end{equation}
Both terms on the right-hand side are nonnegative.

If \(0\le t<a\), then \(K_d(t,a)>0\).

If \(a\le t<1\), positivity of \(f_d\) on \((0,1)\)
gives
\[
    \forall r>t,\ 
    \partial_r^2K_d(t,r)
    =\frac{t^2}{r^3}f_d(t/r)>0.
\]
Thus \(r\mapsto K_d(t,r)\) is strictly convex on \((t,+\infty)\).
Since \(a\le t<1<b\), equality in
\[
    K_d(t,1)\le(1-\theta)K_d(t,a)+\theta K_d(t,b)
\]
would force this convex function to be affine on \([a,b]\),
contradicting its strict convexity on \((t,b)\).
The second term in \eqref{eq:multiplier-jensen} is therefore
positive when \(a\le t<1\).

Combining the two cases, we obtain
\[
    \forall t\in[0,1),\  H_d(t)>p_{\mathrm{cx}}.
\]
Since the minimum defining \(p_{\mathrm{proj}}\) is attained,
it follows that \(p_{\mathrm{proj}}>p_{\mathrm{cx}}\).

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:radial-angular-factor}
For all \(d>2\),
\[
    |V_d|\stackrel{\mathcal D}{=}B_d|V_2|,
\]
where \(B_d\) is nonnegative, independent of \(V_2\), and satisfies

Consequently, for nonnegative random variables
\(R\) and \(S\) in \(L^1\), independent of \(V_2\) and \(V_d\),
\[
    R|V_2|\lesssim_{\mathrm{icx}} S|V_2|
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    R|V_d|\lesssim_{\mathrm{icx}} S|V_d|.
\]
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Let \(Z=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_d)'\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d)\). The Gaussian
representation of uniform directions gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    V_d^2
    \stackrel{\mathcal D}{=}
    \frac{Z_1^2}{\|Z\|^2}
    &=
    \frac{Z_1^2}{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}
    \frac{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}{\|Z\|^2}\\
    &=
    \frac{Z_1^2}{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}
    \frac{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}
         {Z_1^2+Z_2^2+(Z_3^2+\cdots+Z_d^2)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first factor, \(\frac{Z_1^2}{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}\), depends only on
the direction of \((Z_1,Z_2)\). By rotational invariance, this
direction is independent of the radius \(\sqrt{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}\).
Since \((Z_3,\ldots,Z_d)\) is independent of \((Z_1,Z_2)\),
the direction is in fact independent of the pair
\[
    \bigl(Z_1^2+Z_2^2,\;Z_3^2+\cdots+Z_d^2\bigr).
\]
It is therefore independent of the second factor,
\[
    \frac{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}{Z_1^2+\cdots+Z_d^2}.
\]
The two factors are thus independent. Moreover,
\[
    \frac{Z_1^2}{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}
    \stackrel{\mathcal D}{=}V_2^2
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \frac{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}{\|Z\|^2}
    \sim\operatorname{Beta}\left(1,\frac{d-2}{2}\right),
\]
where the latter distribution follows from the fact that
\(Z_1^2+Z_2^2\) and \(Z_3^2+\cdots+Z_d^2\) are independent Gamma random variables with common scale parameter and respective shape parameters \(1\) and~\(\frac{d-2}{2}\).

Taking square roots gives
\[
    |V_d|\stackrel{\mathcal D}{=}B_d|V_2|,
\]
with \(B_d\) independent of \(V_2\) and
\(B_d^2\sim\operatorname{Beta}\left(1,\frac{d-2}{2}\right)\).

For the final implication, take \(B_d\) independent of
\((R,S,V_2)\). Increasing convex order is preserved under
multiplication by an independent bounded nonnegative random
variable, so
\[
    R|V_2|\lesssim_{\mathrm{icx}} S|V_2|
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    RB_d|V_2|\lesssim_{\mathrm{icx}} SB_d|V_2|.
\]
The distributional identity above then yields
\(R|V_d|\lesssim_{\mathrm{icx}} S|V_d|\).

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:planar-cholesky}
Let \(S_1,S_2\in\mathcal S_2^{++}\) and \(p\in(0,1)\).
Let \(u\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\) minimise on \(\mathbb{S}^{1}\) the function
\[
    f:\ x\in\mathbb{R}^2\longmapsto
    p\sqrt{x'S_1x}+(1-p)\sqrt{x'S_2x}.
\]
For a unit vector \(v\perp u\), set \(O=(u\ v)\in\mathcal O_2\)
and consider the Cholesky decompositions
\[
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\
    O'S_iO=L_iL_i'.
\]
Then
\[
    pL_1+(1-p)L_2=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \alpha&0\\
        0&\beta
    \end{pmatrix},
\]
with \(0<\alpha=f(u)\le\beta\).

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
The function \(f\) is continuous on the compact set
\(\mathbb{S}^{1}\), so it admits a minimiser \(u\).
Fix a unit vector \(v\perp u\).
Since \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) are positive definite, \(f\) is smooth
on \(\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\}\). By homogeneity and Taylor's formula,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    f\left(\frac{u+tv}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}\right)
    &=\frac{f(u+tv)}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}\\
    &=f(u)+t\,v'\nabla f(u)
      +\frac{t^2}{2}\bigl(v'\nabla^2f(u)v-f(u)\bigr)
      +o(t^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since the left-hand side is minimised at \(t=0\), we obtain
the first-order condition
\begin{equation}
    v'\nabla f(u)
    =p\,\frac{v'S_1u}{\sqrt{u'S_1u}}
     +(1-p)\,\frac{v'S_2u}{\sqrt{u'S_2u}}
    =0
    \label{eq:two-dimensional-first-order}
\end{equation}
and the second-order condition
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    v'\nabla^2f(u)v
    &=p\left(
        \frac{v'S_1v}{\sqrt{u'S_1u}}
        -\frac{(v'S_1u)^2}{(u'S_1u)^{3/2}}
      \right)\\
    &\quad +(1-p)\left(
        \frac{v'S_2v}{\sqrt{u'S_2u}}
        -\frac{(v'S_2u)^2}{(u'S_2u)^{3/2}}
      \right)
    \ge f(u).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:two-dimensional-second-order}
\end{equation}

Set \(O=(u\ v)\in\mathcal O_2\).
The Cholesky decompositions \(O'S_iO=L_iL_i'\) are given by
\[
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\quad
    L_i=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \sqrt{u'S_i u}&0\\[1ex]
        \displaystyle\frac{v'S_i u}{\sqrt{u'S_i u}}
        &
        \displaystyle\sqrt{
            v'S_i v-\frac{(v'S_i u)^2}{u'S_i u}
        }
    \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:two-dimensional-first-order}, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-dimensional-average}
    pL_1+(1-p)L_2=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \alpha&0\\
        0&\beta
    \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where
\[
    \alpha=f(u)
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \beta=
    p\sqrt{v'S_1v-\frac{(v'S_1u)^2}{u'S_1u}}
    +(1-p)\sqrt{v'S_2v-\frac{(v'S_2u)^2}{u'S_2u}}.
\]
Positive definiteness gives \(\alpha,\beta>0\).

We now prove that \(\beta\ge\alpha\).

First observe that
\[
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^2,\quad
    \|L_i'x\|
    =\sqrt{x'L_iL_i'x}
    =\sqrt{(Ox)'S_i(Ox)}.
\]
Since \(O\) is orthogonal,
\[
    \min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}
    p\|L_1'x\|+(1-p)\|L_2'x\|
    =
    \min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}f(Ox)
    =
    \min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}f(x)
    =\alpha.
\]
By homogeneity, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-dimensional-global-minimum}
    \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^2,\quad
    p\|L_1'x\|+(1-p)\|L_2'x\|
    \ge\alpha\|x\|.
\end{equation}

We seek a nonzero vector \(x\in\mathbb{R}^2\) for which equality holds
in the triangle inequality
\[
    \left\|\bigl(pL_1'+(1-p)L_2'\bigr)x\right\|
    \le p\|L_1'x\|+(1-p)\|L_2'x\|.
\]
Equality holds whenever \(L_1'x\) and \(L_2'x\) are positively
collinear. This occurs when \(x\) is an eigenvector of
\((L_2')^{-1}L_1'\) associated with a positive eigenvalue. This upper triangular matrix has eigenvalues
\[
    \sqrt{\frac{u'S_1u}{u'S_2u}}
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \sqrt{
        \frac{
            v'S_1v-\dfrac{(v'S_1u)^2}{u'S_1u}
        }{
            v'S_2v-\dfrac{(v'S_2u)^2}{u'S_2u}
        }
    },
\]
both positive.

Suppose first that these eigenvalues are distinct.
Let \(x=(x_1,x_2)'\) be an eigenvector associated with
the second eigenvalue. The vectors \(L_1'x\) and \(L_2'x\)
are positively collinear, so equality holds in the triangle
inequality. Together with
\eqref{eq:two-dimensional-global-minimum}, this gives
\[
    \alpha\|x\|
    \le \left\|\bigl(pL_1'+(1-p)L_2'\bigr)x\right\|.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:two-dimensional-average} and squaring,
we obtain
\[
    \alpha^2(x_1^2+x_2^2)
    \le\alpha^2x_1^2+\beta^2x_2^2.
\]
Since \(e_1\) is an eigenvector associated with the first
eigenvalue and eigenvectors associated with distinct eigenvalues
are linearly independent, we have \(x_2\ne0\).
This implies \(\alpha^2\le\beta^2\), hence
\(\beta\ge\alpha\) since \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are positive.

Suppose now that the eigenvalues coincide. Then
\[
    \frac{v'S_1v}{u'S_1u}
    -\frac{(v'S_1u)^2}{(u'S_1u)^2}
    =
    \frac{v'S_2v}{u'S_2u}
    -\frac{(v'S_2u)^2}{(u'S_2u)^2}.
\]
Factoring out the square root of this common value in the
expression for \(\beta\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \beta
    &=
    \sqrt{
        \frac{v'S_1v}{u'S_1u}
        -\frac{(v'S_1u)^2}{(u'S_1u)^2}
    }\,
    \bigl(p\sqrt{u'S_1u}+(1-p)\sqrt{u'S_2u}\bigr)\\
    &=
    \alpha\sqrt{
        \frac{v'S_1v}{u'S_1u}
        -\frac{(v'S_1u)^2}{(u'S_1u)^2}
    }.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence, for \(i\in\{1,2\}\),
\[
    \frac{v'S_iv}{u'S_iu}
    -\frac{(v'S_iu)^2}{(u'S_iu)^2}
    =\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^2,
\]
and therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{v'S_iv}{\sqrt{u'S_iu}}
    -\frac{(v'S_iu)^2}{(u'S_iu)^{3/2}}
    &=
    \sqrt{u'S_iu}\left(
        \frac{v'S_iv}{u'S_iu}
        -\frac{(v'S_iu)^2}{(u'S_iu)^2}
    \right)\\
    &=\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^2\sqrt{u'S_iu}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(f(u)=\alpha\), the second-order condition
\eqref{eq:two-dimensional-second-order} thus becomes
\[
    \left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^2
    \bigl(p\sqrt{u'S_1u}+(1-p)\sqrt{u'S_2u}\bigr)
    =\frac{\beta^2}{\alpha}
    \ge\alpha.
\]
Thus \(\beta^2\ge\alpha^2\), and positivity of \(\alpha\) and
\(\beta\) again yields \(\beta\ge\alpha\).

Suppose that \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}} Y\). By \eqref{eq:necessary},
\[
    \forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{1},\
    p\sqrt{x'\Sigma_1x}+(1-p)\sqrt{x'\Sigma_2x}\ge1.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:planar-cholesky} provides \(O\in\mathcal O_2\) such that the Cholesky
decompositions
\[
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\
    O'\Sigma_iO=L_iL_i'
\]
satisfy
\[
    pL_1+(1-p)L_2=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \alpha&0\\
        0&\beta
    \end{pmatrix}
    \quad\text{with}\quad 1\le\alpha\le\beta.
\]
Set
\[
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\
    M_i=OL_i,
\]
and write \(M=pM_1+(1-p)M_2\). Then
\[
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\
    M_iM_i'=\Sigma_i,
\]
and

    MM'=O
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \alpha^2&0\\
        0&\beta^2
    \end{pmatrix}
    O'\ge I_2.
\]
For \(Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_2)\),
\eqref{eq:gaussian-covariance} gives \(Z\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} MZ\).
Hence, for every convex function \(\phi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbb{E}[\phi(X)]
    &=\mathbb{E}[\phi(Z)]\\
    &\le\mathbb{E}[\phi(MZ)]\\
    &\le p\mathbb{E}[\phi(M_1Z)]
         +(1-p)\mathbb{E}[\phi(M_2Z)]\\
    &=\mathbb{E}[\phi(Y)].
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y\).

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:lipschitz-tests}
Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be \(\mathbb{R}^d\)-valued random variables in \(L^1\).
Then
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
    X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    &\mathbb{E}[\phi(X)]\le\mathbb{E}[\phi(Y)]\\
    &\text{for all Lipschitz convex functions }\phi:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
The forward implication follows directly from the definition
of convex order. Conversely, suppose that the comparison
holds for every Lipschitz convex function.
Affine tests give \(\mathbb{E}[X]=\mathbb{E}[Y]\).

Let \(\phi:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}\) be convex. Choose an affine supporting
function \(\ell\) for \(\phi\) at \(0\) and set
\(\widetilde\phi=\phi-\ell\), so that
\(\widetilde\phi\ge0\) and \(\widetilde\phi(0)=0\).

For all integers \(m\ge1\), define the inf-convolution
\[
    \widetilde\phi_m(x)
    =\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^d}
      \bigl\{\widetilde\phi(y)+m\|x-y\|\bigr\}.
\]
The function
\((x,y)\mapsto\widetilde\phi(y)+m\|x-y\|\)
is convex, so its partial infimum
\(\widetilde\phi_m\) is convex.
The triangle inequality gives
\[
    \forall x,z\in\mathbb{R}^d,\
    |\widetilde\phi_m(x)-\widetilde\phi_m(z)|
    \le m\|x-z\|,
\]
hence \(\widetilde\phi_m\) is \(m\)-Lipschitz.
Moreover,
\[
    \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^d,\
    0\le\widetilde\phi_m(x)
    \le\min\{\widetilde\phi(x),m\|x\|\},
\]
and \(\widetilde\phi_m(x)\) is nondecreasing in \(m\).

We claim that
\(\widetilde\phi_m(x)\uparrow\widetilde\phi(x)\)
for all \(x\in\mathbb{R}^d\).
Fix \(x\) and choose approximate minimisers \(y_m\) such that
\[
    \widetilde\phi(y_m)+m\|x-y_m\|
    \le\widetilde\phi_m(x)+\frac1m
    \le\widetilde\phi(x)+\frac1m.
\]
Since \(\widetilde\phi\ge0\), this implies \(y_m\underset{m\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow} x\).
Also,
\(\widetilde\phi_m(x)\ge\widetilde\phi(y_m)-1/m\),
so continuity of \(\widetilde\phi\) gives
\[
    \liminf_{m\to+\infty}\widetilde\phi_m(x)
    \ge\lim_{m\to+\infty}\widetilde\phi(y_m)
    =\widetilde\phi(x).
\]
Together with
\(\widetilde\phi_m(x)\le\widetilde\phi(x)\),
this proves the claim.

The assumed comparison applies to each
\(\widetilde\phi_m\), giving
\[
    \forall m\ge1,\
    \mathbb{E}[\widetilde\phi_m(X)]
    \le\mathbb{E}[\widetilde\phi_m(Y)].
\]
Monotone convergence yields
\(\mathbb{E}[\widetilde\phi(X)]\le\mathbb{E}[\widetilde\phi(Y)]\).
Since \(\ell\) is affine and \(\mathbb{E}[X]=\mathbb{E}[Y]\),
adding the equal finite expectations
\(\mathbb{E}[\ell(X)]\) and \(\mathbb{E}[\ell(Y)]\) gives
\(\mathbb{E}[\phi(X)]\le\mathbb{E}[\phi(Y)]\).

Thus \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y\).

and \(p\in(0,1)\). If
\[

    \quad\text{and}\quad
    Y\sim p\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_1)+(1-p)\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_2),

    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad

\]
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Multivariate convex order implies one-dimensional projected
convex order, so only the converse requires proof.
Suppose that \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}} Y\).

First assume that all three matrices are positive definite.
Both orders are preserved under linear transformations, so
\[
    \Sigma^{-1/2}X\lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}}\Sigma^{-1/2}Y.
\]
The random variable on the left is standard Gaussian.
The mixture on the right has positive definite
variance-covariance matrices
\[
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\
    \widetilde\Sigma_i
    =\Sigma^{-1/2}\Sigma_i\Sigma^{-1/2}.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:dimension-two-normalised} gives therefore
\[
    \Sigma^{-1/2}X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}}\Sigma^{-1/2}Y.
\]
Applying \(\Sigma^{1/2}\) yields \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y\).

We now consider the general positive semidefinite case.
By \eqref{eq:necessary},
\[
    \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^2,\
    \sqrt{x'\Sigma x}
    \le p\sqrt{x'\Sigma_1x}
         +(1-p)\sqrt{x'\Sigma_2x}.
\]
For \(\varepsilon>0\), apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:square-root-regularisation} with
\(\eta=\varepsilon\|x\|^2\) to obtain
\[
    \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^2,\
    \sqrt{x'(\Sigma+\varepsilon I_2)x}
    \le p\sqrt{x'(\Sigma_1+\varepsilon I_2)x}
         +(1-p)\sqrt{x'(\Sigma_2+\varepsilon I_2)x}.
\]
Let \(Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_2)\) be independent of \((X,Y)\).
By \eqref{eq:necessary},
\[
    X+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,Z
    \lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}} Y+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,Z.
\]
The positive definite case therefore gives
\[
    X+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,Z
    \lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,Z.
\]
For all Lipschitz convex functions \(\phi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}\)
with Lipschitz constant \(L\), conditional Jensen's
inequality and the preceding comparison yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbb{E}[\phi(X)]
    &\le\mathbb{E}[\phi(X+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,Z)]\\
    &\le\mathbb{E}[\phi(Y+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,Z)]\\
    &\le\mathbb{E}[\phi(Y)]
         +L\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\mathbb{E}[\|Z\|].
\end{aligned}
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:lipschitz-tests} gives \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y\).

<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{prop:matrix-construction}
Let \(d\ge2\), \(S_1,S_2\in\mathcal S_d^{++}\) and \(p\in(0,1)\). There exists \(O\in\mathcal O_d\) such that the Cholesky decompositions
\[
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\quad O'S_iO=L_iL_i'
\]
satisfy
\[
    pL_1+(1-p)L_2=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_1&&0\\&\ddots&\\0&&\eta_d\end{pmatrix} \quad \text{with} \quad 0<\eta_1\le\cdots\le\eta_d,
\]
where
\[
    \eta_1=\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}} p\sqrt{x'S_1x}+(1-p)\sqrt{x'S_2x}.
\]
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
We argue by induction on \(d\). The case \(d=2\) is Lemma~\ref{lem:planar-cholesky}. Let \(d\ge3\) and suppose that the result holds in dimension \(d-1\).

Choose a minimiser \(u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\) of
\[
    f:x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\longmapsto p\sqrt{x'S_1x}+(1-p)\sqrt{x'S_2x},
\]
and set \(\eta_1=f(u)>0\). Such a minimiser exists by continuity and compactness.

Let \(V\in\mathcal M_{d,d-1}\) have columns forming an orthonormal basis of \(\operatorname{span}(u)^\perp\), so that \((u\ V)\in\mathcal O_d\). The first-order condition at \(u\) then reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:construction-first-order}
    p\,\frac{V'S_1u}{\sqrt{u'S_1u}}+(1-p)\,\frac{V'S_2u}{\sqrt{u'S_2u}}=0.
\end{equation}

For each \(i\in\{1,2\}\), the matrix \(S_i\) is positive definite, hence so is \((u\ V)'S_i(u\ V)\). Its Schur complement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:construction-schur}
    \widetilde S_i=V'S_iV-\frac{V'S_iu\,u'S_iV}{u'S_iu}
\end{equation}
therefore belongs to \(\mathcal S_{d-1}^{++}\).

We first show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:construction-minimum-bound}
    \forall y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-2},\quad p\sqrt{y'\widetilde S_1y}+(1-p)\sqrt{y'\widetilde S_2y}\ge\eta_1.
\end{equation}
Fix \(y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-2}\) and set \(v=Vy\). Then \(u\) and \(v\) are orthonormal, and \(u\) minimises \(f\) on the unit circle of \(\operatorname{span}(u,v)\). For each \(i\in\{1,2\}\), the restriction of the quadratic form \(x\mapsto x'S_ix\) to this plane has matrix
\[
    \begin{pmatrix}u'S_iu&u'S_iv\\v'S_iu&v'S_iv\end{pmatrix}
\]
in the basis \((u,v)\). The second diagonal entry in the lower triangular matrix of its Cholesky decomposition is
\[
    \sqrt{v'S_iv-\frac{(v'S_iu)^2}{u'S_iu}}=\sqrt{y'\widetilde S_iy}.
\]
The two-dimensional argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:planar-cholesky}, applied in this basis with the minimiser \(u\), therefore gives
\[
    p\sqrt{y'\widetilde S_1y}+(1-p)\sqrt{y'\widetilde S_2y}\ge p\sqrt{u'S_1u}+(1-p)\sqrt{u'S_2u}=\eta_1.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:construction-minimum-bound}.

Applying the induction hypothesis to \(\widetilde S_1,\widetilde S_2\), we obtain \(\widetilde O\in\mathcal O_{d-1}\) such that the Cholesky decompositions
\[
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\quad \widetilde O'\widetilde S_i\widetilde O=\widetilde L_i\widetilde L_i'
\]
satisfy
\[
    p\widetilde L_1+(1-p)\widetilde L_2=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_2&&0\\&\ddots&\\0&&\eta_d\end{pmatrix} \quad \text{with} \quad 0<\eta_2\le\cdots\le\eta_d,
\]
where
\[
    \eta_2=\min_{y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-2}} p\sqrt{y'\widetilde S_1y}+(1-p)\sqrt{y'\widetilde S_2y}\ge\eta_1.
\]
Now, set \(O=(u\ V\widetilde O)\in\mathcal O_d\) and
\[
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\quad L_i=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{u'S_iu}&0\\[1ex]\dfrac{\widetilde O'V'S_iu}{\sqrt{u'S_iu}}&\widetilde L_i\end{pmatrix}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:construction-schur},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:construction-factorisation}
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\quad L_iL_i'=O'S_iO.
\end{equation}
Since each \(L_i\) is lower triangular with positive diagonal entries, the identities in \eqref{eq:construction-factorisation} are Cholesky decompositions.

Finally, \eqref{eq:construction-first-order} gives
\[
    pL_1+(1-p)L_2=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_1&0\\0&p\widetilde L_1+(1-p)\widetilde L_2\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_1&&0\\&\ddots&\\0&&\eta_d\end{pmatrix},
\]
with \(0<\eta_1\le\cdots\le\eta_d\). This completes the induction.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:two-components-arbitrary-dimension}
Let \(d\ge2\), \(p\in(0,1)\) and
\(\Sigma,\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2\in\mathcal S_d^+\). Let
\[
    X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    Y\sim p\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_1)+(1-p)\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_2).
\]
Then
\[
    X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X\lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}} Y.
\]
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Only the reverse implication needs to be proved.

Suppose that \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{1d\text{-}proj\text{-}cx}} Y\). By \eqref{eq:necessary},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-components-directional}
    \forall \xi\in\mathbb{R}^d,\quad \sqrt{\xi'\Sigma\xi}\le p\sqrt{\xi'\Sigma_1\xi}+(1-p)\sqrt{\xi'\Sigma_2\xi}.
\end{equation}
First assume that \(\Sigma=I_d\) and \(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2\in\mathcal S_d^{++}\). Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix-construction} with \(S_i=\Sigma_i\), for \(i\in\{1,2\}\), and set \(M_i=OL_i\). Then \(M_iM_i'=\Sigma_i\), and \eqref{eq:two-components-directional} gives
\[
    \eta_1=\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}} p\sqrt{x'\Sigma_1x}+(1-p)\sqrt{x'\Sigma_2x}\ge1.
\]
Since \(\eta_d\ge\cdots\ge\eta_1\ge1\), we obtain
\[
    \bigl(pM_1+(1-p)M_2\bigr)\bigl(pM_1+(1-p)M_2\bigr)'=O\begin{pmatrix}\eta_1^2&&0\\&\ddots&\\0&&\eta_d^2\end{pmatrix}O'\ge I_d.
\]
For \(Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d)\), Gaussian comparison and Jensen's inequality therefore yield
\[
    X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}}\bigl(pM_1+(1-p)M_2\bigr)Z\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y,
\]
exactly as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:dimension-two}.

If all three variance-covariance matrices are positive definite, the same change of variables as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:dimension-two} reduces the comparison to the normalised case: apply the preceding argument to \(\Sigma^{-1/2}X\) and \(\Sigma^{-1/2}Y\), then use preservation of convex order under the linear map \(x\mapsto\Sigma^{1/2}x\).

Finally, if the variance-covariance matrices are only positive semidefinite, Lemma~\ref{lem:square-root-regularisation} ensures that \eqref{eq:two-components-directional} remains valid after adding \(\varepsilon I_d\) to each variance-covariance matrix, for every \(\varepsilon>0\). The positive definite case thus gives
\[
    X+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,Z\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,Z,
\]
where \(Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d)\) is independent of \((X,Y)\). As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:dimension-two}, letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) in expectations of Lipschitz convex test functions and applying Lemma~\ref{lem:lipschitz-tests} yields \(X\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y\).

<!-- remark-11: body_md -->
\label{rem:two-component-matrix-condition}
Suppose that the equivalent conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-components-arbitrary-dimension} hold. When all three variance-covariance matrices are positive definite, the preceding proof constructs matrices \(M_1,M_2\in\mathcal M_d\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-component-matrix-condition}
    \forall i\in\{1,2\},\quad M_iM_i'=\Sigma_i \qquad\text{and}\qquad \Sigma\le\bigl(pM_1+(1-p)M_2\bigr)\bigl(pM_1+(1-p)M_2\bigr)'.
\end{equation}
For general positive definite \(\Sigma\), these matrices are obtained by multiplying those constructed for the normalised problem with \(\Sigma=I_d\) on the left by \(\Sigma^{1/2}\). To recover the formulation \eqref{eq:sufficient}, set \(P=(pI_d\ \ (1-p)I_d)\) and
\[
    \Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_1&M_1M_2'\\M_2M_1'&\Sigma_2\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}M_1\\M_2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}M_1'&M_2'\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal S_{2d}^+.
\]
Then \(\Gamma\) is the variance-covariance matrix of \((M_1Z,M_2Z)\), where \(Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d)\), and \eqref{eq:two-component-matrix-condition} gives \(\Sigma\le P\Gamma P'\).

The same matrix representation holds when the variance-covariance matrices are singular. Indeed, in the positive definite case, polar decomposition gives \(M_i=\Sigma_i^{1/2}O_i\), with \(O_i\in\mathcal O_d\), for~\(i\in\{1,2\}\). Multiplying both matrices on the right by \(O_1'\) preserves \eqref{eq:two-component-matrix-condition} and allows us to take \(M_1=\Sigma_1^{1/2}\) and \(M_2=\Sigma_2^{1/2}O\), where \(O=O_2O_1'\in\mathcal O_d\).

For every \(\varepsilon>0\), Lemma~\ref{lem:square-root-regularisation} ensures that the directional inequalities hold for \(\Sigma+\varepsilon I_d\), \(\Sigma_1+\varepsilon I_d\) and \(\Sigma_2+\varepsilon I_d\). Applying the positive definite construction and the preceding observation yields \(O_\varepsilon\in\mathcal O_d\) such that, with
\[
    M_{1,\varepsilon}=(\Sigma_1+\varepsilon I_d)^{1/2}, \qquad M_{2,\varepsilon}=(\Sigma_2+\varepsilon I_d)^{1/2}O_\varepsilon,
\]
we have
\[
    \Sigma+\varepsilon I_d\le\bigl(pM_{1,\varepsilon}+(1-p)M_{2,\varepsilon}\bigr)\bigl(pM_{1,\varepsilon}+(1-p)M_{2,\varepsilon}\bigr)'.
\]
By compactness of \(\mathcal O_d\), there exist a sequence \(\varepsilon_m\downarrow0\) and \(\widehat O\in\mathcal O_d\) such that \(O_{\varepsilon_m}\to\widehat O\). Continuity of the positive semidefinite square root gives
\[
    M_{1,\varepsilon_m}\longrightarrow M_1:=\Sigma_1^{1/2}, \qquad M_{2,\varepsilon_m}\longrightarrow M_2:=\Sigma_2^{1/2}\widehat O.
\]
Since the positive semidefinite cone is closed, passing to the limit in the preceding inequality yields
\[
    \Sigma\le\bigl(pM_1+(1-p)M_2\bigr)\bigl(pM_1+(1-p)M_2\bigr)'.
\]
Moreover, \(M_iM_i'=\Sigma_i\) for all \(i\in\{1,2\}\), so \eqref{eq:two-component-matrix-condition} holds. Defining \(\Gamma\) by the same block formula as before therefore gives a positive semidefinite variance-covariance matrix with diagonal blocks \(\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2\) and \(\Sigma\le P\Gamma P'\). Thus, for two components, the sufficient condition \eqref{eq:sufficient} of Jourdain and Pag\`es is also necessary, including for singular variance-covariance matrices.

<!-- remark-12: body_md -->
The Gaussian assumption is not essential to the argument: the matrix construction depends only on the directional inequalities, while the probabilistic comparison uses properties shared by linear images of a common radial random vector. Therefore, the equivalence between multivariate convex order, one-dimensional projected convex order and the directional inequalities \eqref{eq:two-components-directional} remains valid for
\[
    X\stackrel{\mathcal D}{=}\Sigma^{1/2}Z, \qquad \mathcal{L}(Y)=p\mathcal{L}(\Sigma_1^{1/2}Z)+(1-p)\mathcal{L}(\Sigma_2^{1/2}Z),
\]
where \(Z\) is an \(\mathbb{R}^d\)-valued random variable in \(L^1\) with a radial distribution and \(\mathbb P(Z\ne0)>0\). Here, \(\Sigma,\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2\in\mathcal S_d^+\) are scatter matrices, rather than necessarily variance-covariance matrices.

Indeed, rotational invariance gives
\[
    \forall \xi\in\mathbb{R}^d,\ \forall A\in\mathcal M_d,\quad \mathbb{E}[|\xi'AZ|]=\sqrt{\xi'AA'\xi}\,\mathbb{E}[|Z_1|],
\]
where \(Z_1\) denotes the first coordinate of \(Z\). Since \(0<\mathbb{E}[|Z_1|]<+\infty\), absolute-value tests yield the directional inequalities from projected convex order. Conversely, these inequalities give the matrix representation \eqref{eq:two-component-matrix-condition}, by the construction in Remark~\ref{rem:two-component-matrix-condition}. Comparison of linear images of a common radial random vector and Jensen's inequality then yield
\[
    \Sigma^{1/2}Z\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}}\bigl(pM_1+(1-p)M_2\bigr)Z\lesssim_{\mathrm{cx}} Y,
\]
where the last comparison uses \(M_iZ\stackrel{\mathcal D}{=}\Sigma_i^{1/2}Z\) for all \(i\in\{1,2\}\). This includes centred multivariate Student distributions with a common number \(\nu>1\) of degrees of freedom.
