<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $(\mathsf{X}, \mu)$ and $(\mathsf{Y}, \rho)$ be two measure spaces, where we assume $\mu$ to be a probability measure. Let $\alpha \in (1, \infty)$. Suppose $T: L^\alpha(\mu) \to L^\alpha(\rho)$ is a positive linear operator such that there exists $c \in (0, 1)$ such that for every mean-1, non-negative $f \in L^\alpha(\mu)$, we have $\left\lVertTf\right\rVert_\alpha \leq \left\lVertf\right\rVert_\alpha^c \, .$ Then we say $T$ is an \textit{$L^\alpha$-power contraction (with exponent $c$)}.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{t:expContraction}
Let $(\mathsf{X}, \mu)$ and $(\mathsf{Y}, \rho)$ be two measure spaces, where we assume $\mu$ to be a probability measure. Let $\alpha \in (1, \infty)$. Suppose that $T: L^\alpha(\mu) \to L^\alpha(\rho)$ is an $L^\alpha$-power contraction with exponent $c \in (0, 1)$. Then for every $g \in L^\alpha(\mu)$, we have
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:expContraction}
    \left\lVertTg\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\rho)} \leq \left\lVertg\right\rVert_{L^1(\mu)}^{1-c} \cdot \left\lVertg\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu)}^c \, .
\end{equation}
Moreover, $T$ is a contraction (hence bounded).
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The inequality is trivial if $g \equiv 0$, so we assume otherwise.
    First, consider a non-negative function $f \in L^\alpha(\mu)$ for which $\left\lVertf\right\rVert_1 \neq 0$. Let $h = f / \left\lVertf\right\rVert_1$. Then $\left\lVerth\right\rVert_1 = 1$, so the hypothesis of the theorem implies that $\left\lVertTh\right\rVert_\alpha \leq \left\lVerth\right\rVert_\alpha^c$, or equivalently,
    \[ \frac{\left\lVertTf\right\rVert_\alpha}{\left\lVertf\right\rVert_1}  \leq \left(\frac{\left\lVertf\right\rVert_\alpha}{\left\lVertf\right\rVert_1}\right)^c \, . \]
    Multiply both sides by $\left\lVertf\right\rVert_1$, and we get (\ref{eq:expContraction}) for non-negative functions.

    Let $g \in L^\alpha(\mu)$ be any function. Since $T$ is positive, we have $|Tg| \leq T|g|$. Since $|g|$ is non-negative, we have
    $\left\lVertTg\right\rVert_\alpha \leq \left\lVertT|g|\right\rVert_\alpha \leq \left\lVertg\right\rVert_1^{1-c} \cdot \left\lVertg\right\rVert_\alpha^c .$

    Apply Jensen's inequality, $\left\lVertg\right\rVert_1 \leq \left\lVertg\right\rVert_\alpha \, $, to (\ref{eq:expContraction}) to observe that $T$ is a contraction.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:SDPI}
Choose any probability measure $\nu \ll \mu$ for which $f := \frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mu} \in L^\alpha(\mu)$. It is well known that $K^\star f = \frac{\mathrm{d}\nu K}{\mathrm{d}\mu K}$, $\mu  K$-a.e. (cf. \cite[Lemma 2.2]{EM_contraction}), so we have
$\left\lVertK_\mu^\star f\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu K)} \leq \left\lVertf\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu)}^{\eta_\alpha(\mu, K)} \, .$
Therefore, $K^\star_\mu$ is an $L^\alpha$-power contraction with exponent $\eta_\alpha(\mu, K)$ (assuming that this quantity is in $(0, 1)$). In fact, an alternative characterization of $\eta_\alpha(\mu, K)$ is
$\eta_\alpha(\mu, K)= \inf \bigl\{ c \in [0, 1]: \left\lVertK^\star_\mu f\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu K)} \leq \left\lVertf\right\rVert_{L^1(\mu)}^{1-c}\left\lVertf\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu)}^c \text{for all } f \in L^\alpha(\mu) \bigr\} \, .
$
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{t:HolderRenyi}
    Let $K$ be a Markov kernel on a probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$, and let $\alpha > 1$, and let $c \in (0, 1)$ be fixed. The following statements are equivalent.
    \begin{enumerate}[label = (\alph*)]
        \item The dual operator $K_\mu^\star: L^\alpha(\mu) \to L^\alpha(\mu K)$ is an $L^\alpha$-power contraction with exponent $c$.
        \item For every $f \in L^\alpha(\mu)$ and $g \in L^{\alpha'}(\mu K)$, we have
        \begin{align*} &\mathbb{E}_{\mu \otimes K} [f(X)g(Y)] \\
        &\leq \left\lVertf(X)\right\rVert_{L^1(\mu)}^{1-c} \left\lVertf(X)\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu)}^c \left\lVertg(Y)\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha'}(\mu K)} \, . \end{align*}
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
First we prove that (a) implies (b). By the tower property of conditional expectations and H\"older's inequality, we obtain
    \begin{align*}
        \mathbb{E}_{\mu \otimes K}[f(X)g(Y)] 
        &= \mathbb{E}_{\mu K}[\mathbb{E}_{\mu \otimes K}[f(X)g(Y)|Y]] \\
        &= \mathbb{E}_{\mu K}[K^\star_\mu f(Y) g(Y)] \\
        &\leq \left\lVertK^\star_\mu f(Y)\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu K)} \left\lVert g(Y)\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha'}(\mu K)} \, .
    \end{align*}
    We apply Theorem \ref{t:expContraction} to show that (b) holds.

    Next, we prove that (b) implies (a). Let $f \in L^\alpha(\mu)$ be a non-negative function for which $\mathbb{E}_\mu f = 1$ (which implies that $\left\lVertf\right\rVert_{L^1(\mu)} = 1)$. By the tower property, we have
    \begin{align*}
        \left\lVertK^\star_\mu f(Y)\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu K)}^\alpha
        &= \mathbb{E}_{\mu K}[K_\mu^\star f(Y) \cdot K_\mu^\star f(Y)^{\alpha-1}] \\
        &= \mathbb{E}_{\mu  K}[\mathbb{E}_{\mu \otimes K}[f(X)|Y] \cdot K_\mu^\star f(Y)^{\alpha - 1}] \\
        &= \mathbb{E}_{\mu \otimes  K}[f(X)K_\mu^\star f(Y)^{\alpha-1}] \, .
    \end{align*}
    We may apply our hypothesis provided that $(K_\mu^\star f)^{\alpha - 1} \in L^{\alpha'}(\mu K)$. To verify this, recall that the Markov kernel $K$ is a contraction, and so is $K_\mu^\star$. Thus, we apply Jensen's inequality to observe that
    $\mathbb{E}_{\mu K}[K_\mu^\star f(Y)^{(\alpha-1)\cdot \frac{\alpha}{\alpha - 1}}]= \mathbb{E}_{\mu K}[K_\mu^\star f(Y)^\alpha] \leq \mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f(X)^\alpha] < \infty \, .  $
    Hence, we apply our hypothesis to see that
    \begin{align*} 
    \left\lVertK_\mu^\star f(Y)\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu  K)}^\alpha 
    &\leq \left\lVertf(X)\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu)}^{c} \left\lVertK_\mu^\star f(Y)\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu K)}^{\alpha/\alpha'} \, .  \end{align*}
    By dividing both sides by $\left\lVertK_\mu^\star f(Y)\right\rVert_{L^\alpha(\mu K)}^{\alpha/\alpha'}$ (which is positive), and observing that $1 - \frac{1}{\alpha'} = \frac{1}{\alpha}\,$, we obtain (a).
<!-- end proof-2 -->

\label{rectangleBound}

    \end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Set $f = \1_A$ and $g = \1_B$ in Theorem \ref{t:HolderRenyi}, and choose $c = \eta_\alpha(\mu, K)$ as we discussed in Example \ref{ex:SDPI}.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{T:Nec}
    Let $(\mathsf{X}, \mu)$ be a probability space, and let $(\mathsf{Y}, \nu)$ be a measure space. Suppose that $T: L^\alpha(\mu) \to L^\alpha(\nu)$ is a positive linear operator. If $T$ is $(\alpha, \beta)$-hypercontractive for some $\beta \in (1, \alpha)$,
    then $T$ is an $L^\alpha$-power contraction with exponent
    $c = \frac{\alpha'}{\beta'} =  \frac{\alpha(\beta-1)}{\beta(\alpha-1)} \, .$
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By the $(\alpha, \beta)$-hypercontractivity and the Riesz-Thorin interpolation, we must have $\left\lVertTf\right\rVert_\alpha \leq \left\lVertf\right\rVert_\beta \leq \left\lVertf\right\rVert_1^{1-c}\left\lVertf\right\rVert_\alpha^c \, ,$ where $c = \frac{\alpha(\beta-1)}{\beta(\alpha-1)} \,. $
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
We recall that the hypercontractivity of Markov kernels tensorizes in the following sense: Given $\alpha > 1$ and a Markov kernel $K$, we define
$ s_\alpha(K) := \beta_K(\alpha)/\alpha$.
Now let $\alpha > 1$, and consider several Markov kernels $K_1, \ldots, K_n$ with input law $\mu$. By \cite[Theorem 2]{AG76}, we have
$s_\alpha\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^n K_i\right) = \max_{1 \leq i \leq n} s_\alpha(K_i) \, ,$ so by applying (\ref{SDPI_bound}), we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{SDPI-HC-tensorization}
\eta_\alpha\left(\mu^{\otimes n}, \bigotimes_{i=1}^n K_i \right) \leq \max_{1 \leq i \leq n} h_\alpha(\mu, K_i)\, . \end{equation}
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{BIE_lemma}
In the setting of \S\ref{settings}, let
$
E:=\bigcup_{z\in\{0,1\}^k}A_z\times B_z$,
$p:=P_{X^nY^n}(E)$, and
$q:=(P_{X^n}\otimes P_{Y^n})(E)$.
Then
$
p\ge 2^{-\gamma k}$, $
q\le 2^{-k(1-c)},
$
and for every \(\alpha>1\),
\[
d_\alpha(p\Vert q)
\le k\eta_\alpha(\mu^{\otimes n},K^{\otimes n}).
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The successful-agreement event is contained in \(E\), which gives the lower bound on \(p\). By (\ref{Bz_sum_bound}), we have
\[
q=2^{-k}\sum_z P_{Y^n}(B_z)
\le 2^{-k(1-c)}.
\]
Put
$
p_z:=K^{\otimes n}(B_z\mid A_z)$ and $
q_z:=P_{Y^n}(B_z)$, and we denote $(\mu^{\otimes n})_{A_z}$ to be the conditional probability measure given $A_z$, i.e. $\mathrm{d}(\mu^{\otimes n})_{A_z} := \frac{\1_{A_z}}{\mu^{\otimes n}(A_z)} \mathrm{d} \mu^{\otimes n}$. Then uniformity gives
$
D_\alpha((\mu^{\otimes n})_{A_z}\Vert\mu^{\otimes n})=k.
$
Consequently, the data-processing inequality (\ref{DPI}) as well as the definition of SDPI yields
\begin{align*}
d_\alpha(p_z\Vert q_z)
&\le
D_\alpha\!\left(
(\mu^{\otimes n})_{A_z}K^{\otimes n}
\,\middle\Vert\,(\mu K)^{\otimes n}
\right)\\
&\le k\eta_\alpha(\mu^{\otimes n},K^{\otimes n}).
\end{align*}
Moreover, \(p=2^{-k}\sum_zp_z\) and \(q=2^{-k}\sum_zq_z\). By the joint convexity of
$
(u,v)\longmapsto
u^\alpha v^{1-\alpha}
+(1-u)^\alpha(1-v)^{1-\alpha},
$
we obtain
\begin{align*}
2^{(\alpha-1)d_\alpha(p\Vert q)}
&\le
2^{-k}\sum_z2^{(\alpha-1)d_\alpha(p_z\Vert q_z)}\\
&\le
2^{(\alpha-1)k\eta_\alpha(\mu^{\otimes n},K^{\otimes n})}.
\end{align*}
Taking logarithms proves the claim.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:BI}
    Recall the setting in \S\ref{settings}. Let $h_\alpha(\mu, K)$ be from (\ref{SDPI_bound}), $p_\gamma := 2^{-\gamma k}$, and let $q_\alpha(\gamma) \in (0, p_\gamma]$ such that $
        d_\alpha(p_\gamma || q_\alpha(\gamma)) = kh_\alpha(\mu, K)$.
    Then we have
    \[c \geq C_{\mathrm{BI}}(\gamma) := \sup_{\alpha > 1} \left[ 1 + \frac{1}{k} \log_2 q_\alpha(\gamma) \right]_+ \, . \]
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
There exists a unique $q_\alpha(\gamma)$ that satisfies the hypothesis of the theorem because the map $(0, p_\gamma] \ni q \mapsto d_\alpha(p_\gamma\| q)$ decreases continuously from $+\infty$ to zero. The quantity \(q_\alpha(\gamma)\) is the smallest product-law probability compatible with the constraints \(p\geq p_\gamma\) and \(d_\alpha(p\|q)\leq kh_\alpha\).
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Note that the proof is complete once we observe that $q_\alpha(\gamma) \leq q$, since that would imply
$q_\alpha(\gamma) \leq q \leq 2^{-k(1-c)}$,
and the conclusion of the theorem holds once we take the logarithms of both sides. And note that this is immediate if $q \geq p_\gamma$ since $q_\alpha(\gamma) \leq p_\gamma$. On the other hand, if $q \leq p_\gamma$, then $p \geq p_\gamma \geq q$, and the monotonicity (non-decreasing) of $d_\alpha$ in its first argument gives $d_\alpha(p_\gamma|| q) \leq d_\alpha(p||q).$ And by Lemma \ref{BIE_lemma} and (\ref{SDPI-HC-tensorization}), we have
\begin{align}\label{ultimateBound}
    d_\alpha(p_\gamma || q) \leq d_\alpha(p||q) &\leq k \eta_\alpha(\mu^{\otimes n}, K^{\otimes n})\\ &\leq  kh_\alpha(\mu, K) \,.
\end{align}
Hence, the fact that $d_\alpha$ is decreasing in the second argument on $(0, p_\gamma]$ would guarantee that $q_\alpha(\gamma) \leq q$, so we get the desired result.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:caad}
In the setting of \S\ref{settings}, we have
\begin{equation}\label{cLB1}
c\ge
C_{\mathrm{SDPI}}(\gamma) :=\sup_{\alpha>1}
\left[
1-\eta_\alpha(\mu^{\otimes n},K^{\otimes n})
-\alpha'\gamma
\right]_+.
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let \(p,q\) be as in Lemma \ref{BIE_lemma}. Dropping the complementary term in the binary Rényi divergence gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
k\eta_\alpha(\mu^{\otimes n},K^{\otimes n})
&\ge d_\alpha(p\Vert q)\\
&\ge \alpha'\log_2p-\log_2q\\
&\ge -\alpha'\gamma k+k(1-c).
\end{aligned}
\]
Rearranging, using \(c\ge0\), and taking the supremum over \(\alpha>1\) proves the result.

The lower bound induced by hypercontractivity \cite[Theorem 3.1.1]{Zhao2022} says that

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
By \cite[Corollary 1]{VEG_contraction}, if we let $\alpha \to \infty$ (thus $\alpha' \to 1$) in (\ref{cLB1}), we get
    \begin{equation}\label{cLB1-infty} c \geq 1 - \eta_\infty(\mu^{\otimes n}, K^{\otimes n}) - \gamma \, . \end{equation}
    By contrast, if we let $\alpha \to \infty$ for the hypercontractivity estimate (\ref{cLB2}), note that unless $X$ and $Y$ are independent, we must also let $\beta \to \infty$ by \cite[Theorem 3.1]{KA_2016} (thus $\beta' \to 1$), so it becomes $c \geq -\gamma$, which is  a trivial bound. 
    In the case of (\ref{cLB1-infty}), it is possible to obtain a positive lower bound if $\eta_\infty\left( \mu^{\otimes n}, K^{\otimes n}\right) < 1 - \gamma$. One condition that guarantees that is if $K^{\otimes n}$ is $(\gamma+\epsilon)$-ultra-mixing for some $\epsilon \in (0, 1-\gamma]$, i.e. $\frac{K^{\otimes n}(y|x')}{K^{\otimes n}(y|x)} \geq \gamma + \epsilon$ for all $x, x' \in \mathsf{X}^n$ and $y \in \operatorname{supp}(K^{\otimes n}(\cdot|x))$; this is due to \cite[Theorem 7]{VEG_contraction},  which states that for any Markov chain $M$ and $\delta \in (0, 1)$, $\eta_\infty(M) := \sup_{\mu \in \mathcal{P}(\mathsf{X})} \eta_\infty(\mu, M) \leq 1 - \delta$ if and only if $M$ is $\delta$-ultra-mixing, which is also equivalent to the fact that $M$ satisfies $\ln(1/\delta)$-local differential privacy \cite[Theorem 13]{VEG_contraction}.
