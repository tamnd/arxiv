<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For every graph $G$ and positive real numbers $a,b$, if $ \operatorname{mad}(G)<a+b,$ then there is a partition $V(G)=A\cup B$ such that $\operatorname{mad}(G[A])<a$ and $\operatorname{mad}(G[B])<b.$
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:multipart}
For all positive reals $c_1,\ldots,c_k$ and every graph $G$, if $\operatorname{mad}(G)<\sum_{i=1}^k c_i$, then there is a partition
$V(G) = V_1\cup\ldots\cup V_k$ such that $\operatorname{mad}(G[V_i])<c_i$ for every $i \in [k]$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The result follows by induction on $k$. Apply \cref{thm:main} with $a=c_1$ and $b=\sum_{i=2}^k c_i$, and then apply the induction hypothesis to the second part.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:clustered}
    For every positive real $m$, it holds that $\chi_\star(\mathcal{A}_m)=\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor+1$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:relaxedcoloring}
    For any non-negative integers $d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_k$ and every graph $G$ with 
    \[\operatorname{mad}(G) < \sum_{i=1}^k \frac{2d_i+2}{d_i+2}\]
    there is a partition $V(G) = V_1\cup\ldots\cup V_k$ such that, for every $i \in [k]$, all components of $G[V_i]$ have order at most $d_i+1$. In particular, $G$ is $(d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_k)$-colorable.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:labels}
For every graph $G$ and any real $q>\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{mad}(G)$, there exist positive real numbers $(c_v)_{v\in V(G)}$ such that for each $v\in V(G)$  
\[
    \sum_{u\in N(v)}\frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v}<q.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We may assume that $G$ is connected, since otherwise it is enough to apply the argument below separately to each connected component of $G$ (for a component consisting of a single vertex $v$ we simply set $c_v = 1$), using the fact that $\operatorname{mad}(H) \leq \operatorname{mad}(G)$ for every subgraph $H$ of $G$.

For a graph $G$, define a network $\mathcal F(G)$ as follows. Its nodes consist of a source $s$, a sink $t$, one node $x_{uv}$ for each edge $uv\in E(G)$, and one node for each vertex of $G$. For each edge $uv\in E(G)$, there is an arc $sx_{uv}$ of capacity $1$ and arcs $x_{uv}u$ and $x_{uv}v$ of infinite capacity. Finally, for each $v\in V(G)$, there is an arc $vt$ of capacity $\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{mad}(G)$. See \cref{fig:flow-network} for an example.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{flow.pdf}
    \caption{A graph $G$ and the network $\mathcal F(G)$, where $r= \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{mad}(G)$.}
    \label{fig:flow-network}
\end{figure}

Nadara and Smulewicz \cite{NadaraSmulewicz2022} proved that the network $\mathcal F(G)$ has a maximum $s$--$t$ flow of size $e(G)$.
Note that this also follows directly from the max-flow min-cut theorem: if $X$ is the set of vertices of $G$ on the source side of an $s$--$t$ cut of finite capacity, then every node $x_{uv}$ on the source side satisfies $u, v \in X$, so the capacity of the cut is at least $e(G) - e(G[X]) + \frac12 \operatorname{mad}(G)|X| \geq e(G)$. A maximum flow of size $e(G)$ saturates every arc leaving $s$, so letting $p'_{uv}$ be the flow along the arc $x_{uv}v$, we obtain numbers $p'_{uv}\in[0,1]$ such that $p'_{uv}+p'_{vu}=1$  for each edge $uv\in E(G)$, and 
\[\sum_{u\in N(v)}p'_{uv}\leq \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{mad}(G)\]
for each vertex $v\in V(G)$.

We now perturb the constants $p'_{uv}$ slightly so that none of them is equal to $0$ or $1$. 
Consider the assignment $p''_{uv}=\frac{1}{2}$ for each $uv\in E(G)$, and for $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ let $p_{uv}=(1-\varepsilon)p'_{uv}+\varepsilon p''_{uv}$ for each $uv\in E(G)$. 
Then $p_{uv}\in(0,1)$ and $p_{uv}+p_{vu}=1$ for each $uv\in E(G)$, while for each $v\in V(G)$ 
\[\sum_{u\in N(v)}p_{uv}\leq(1-\varepsilon)\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{mad}(G)+\varepsilon\frac{d(v)}{2}.\]
As the graph is finite
and $q>\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{mad}(G)$, by taking $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small we may assume that for each $v\in V(G)$ 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BTmodel}
\sum_{u\in N(v)}p_{uv}<q.
\end{equation}

Such an assignment can be viewed as a Bradley–Terry model for paired comparisons in which the players are the vertices of $G$, the compared pairs are the edges of $G$, and, for every $uv \in E(G)$, player $u$ beats player $v$ with probability $p_{uv}$. This model was first studied by Zermelo \cite{Zermelo1929} in~1929, and independently by Bradley and Terry \cite{BradleyTerry1952} in 1952. As $p_{uv}\in(0,1)$ for every $uv\in E(G)$ and $G$ is connected, for every partition of $V(G)$ into two non-empty parts some player of each part beats some player of the other part with positive probability. Hence, by the result of Ford \cite{Ford1957}, one can assign a value $c_v>0$ to each $v \in V(G)$ such that 
\[\sum_{u \in N(v)} p_{uv} = \sum_{u \in N(v)} \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v}\]
for every $v \in V(G)$. 
For an explicit proof see, e.g., \cite[Corollary~1]{SchnakenbergPenn2014}. 
By \eqref{eq:BTmodel}, the constants $c_v$ satisfy the conditions of the lemma.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Applying \cref{lem:labels} with $q = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)$, which
satisfies $q > \frac12\operatorname{mad}(G)$ as $\operatorname{mad}(G) < a + b$, we obtain positive real numbers $c_v$, for each $v\in V(G)$, such that 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label-bound}
    \sum_{u\in N(v)}\frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v} < \frac{a+b}{2}
\end{equation}
for every $v\in V(G)$. Let 
\[w(uv) = \frac{c_uc_v}{c_u+c_v}\]
for every $uv \in E(G)$.

Define the \emph{potential} of a partition $V(G) = A \cup B$ by 
\[
\Phi({A,B}) \coloneqq \sum_{uv\in E(G[A])\cup E(G[B])} w(uv) - \frac{a}{2}\sum_{u \in A} c_u - \frac{b}{2}\sum_{v\in B} c_v.
\]

Among all partitions $V(G)=A\cup B$, choose one that minimizes $\Phi({A,B})$.
For any $v \in A$, consider the partition obtained by moving $v$ from $A$ to $B$, namely
$A'\coloneqq A\setminus\{v\}$, $B'\coloneqq B\cup\{v\}.$
By the minimality of \(\Phi(A,B)\), we have
\[
    \Phi(A,B)\leq \Phi(A',B').
\]
Only the terms corresponding to edges incident with \(v\), together with
the term involving \(c_v\), change when \(v\) is moved. Consequently,
\[
    \sum_{u \in N(v) \cap A} w(uv)-\frac{a}{2}c_v
    \leq
    \sum_{u \in N(v) \cap B} w(uv)-\frac{b}{2}c_v.
\]
Dividing by $c_v > 0$ and using the definition of $w(uv)$, we obtain
\[
    \sum_{u \in N(v) \cap A}
        \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v}-\frac{a}{2}
    \leq
    \sum_{u \in N(v) \cap B}
        \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v}-\frac{b}{2}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
    \sum_{u \in N(v) \cap A} \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v}- \sum_{u \in N(v) \cap B} \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v}
    \leq
    \frac{a-b}{2}.
\]
On the other hand, by \eqref{eq:label-bound},
\[
    \sum_{u \in N(v) \cap A} \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v} + \sum_{u \in N(v) \cap B} \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v}
    =
    \sum_{u \in N(v)} \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v}
    < \frac{a+b}{2}.
\]
Adding the preceding two inequalities gives
\[
    2\sum_{u \in N(v) \cap A}
        \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v}
    < a
\]
for every $v \in A$.
The case \(v\in B\) is symmetric, so we also have 
\[
    2\sum_{u \in N(v) \cap B}
        \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v}
    < b
\]
for every $v \in B$.
Using the above inequalities, we show that $\operatorname{mad}(G[A])<a$ and $\operatorname{mad}(G[B])<b$.
For any non-empty set $X \subseteq A$, we have 
\begin{align*}
    2e(G[X])
    &= 2\sum_{uv\in E(G[X])}
        \left(\frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v}+\frac{c_v}{c_u+c_v}\right) \\
    &= 2\sum_{v\in X}
        \sum_{u \in N(v) \cap X}
        \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v} \\
    &\leq \sum_{v\in X}
        2\sum_{u \in N(v) \cap A}
        \frac{c_u}{c_u+c_v} \\
    &< \sum_{v\in X} a
     = a|X|.
\end{align*}
Therefore, $\operatorname{mad}(G[A])<a$. 
Similarly, it follows that for any non-empty set $Y \subseteq B$, $2e(G[Y]) < b|Y|$ and $\operatorname{mad}(G[B])<b$. 
Thus the partition \(V(G)=A \cup B\) has the desired properties.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:componentsize}
    For any positive integer $c$, every graph $H$ with $\operatorname{mad}(H)<\frac{2c}{c+1}$ has all components of order at most $c$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Suppose otherwise. Then some component of $H$ has at least $c+1$ vertices, and hence $H$ contains a tree $T$ on exactly $c+1$ vertices. Then \[\operatorname{mad}(H)\geq\operatorname{mad}(T)=\frac{2c}{c+1},\] a contradiction.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
For every positive real $m$, let $k=\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor+1$ and $c=\left\lceil\frac{2k}{2k-m}\right\rceil$. Every graph $G$ with $\operatorname{mad}(G)\leq m$ has a $k$-coloring with clustering $c$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Note that since $2k>m$, we have $(2k-m)^2>0$ and $4k-m>0$, hence $4k^2 > m(4k-m)$, that is $\frac{4k}{4k-m}\cdot k>m$. Thus, $\operatorname{mad}(G)<\frac{4k}{4k-m}\cdot k$. By \cref{cor:multipart} there exists a partition $V_1\cup\ldots\cup V_k$ such that $\operatorname{mad}(G[V_i])<\frac{4k}{4k-m}$ for every $i\in[k]$.

    Since $\frac{2c}{c+1}$ is an increasing function of $c$ and $c \ge \frac{2k}{2k-m}$, we have 
    \[\frac{2c}{c+1} \ge \frac{\frac{4k}{2k-m}}{\frac{2k}{2k-m}+1} = \frac{4k}{4k-m}.\] 
    
Hence, $\operatorname{mad}(G[V_i])<\frac{2c}{c+1}$ for every $i\in[k]$. By \cref{lem:componentsize}, each component of $G[V_i]$, for every $i\in[k]$, has at most $c$ vertices, as needed.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
