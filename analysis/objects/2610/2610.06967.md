<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:growth}
$W\in C^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb R)\cap C([0,T]\times\mathbb R)$ solves \Cref{eq:hjb}, and there exist constants $C,\lambda>0$ such that, uniformly in $t\in[0,T]$,
\[
|W(z,t)| + |W_z(z,t)| \le C\big(1+e^{\lambda |z|}\big).
\]
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:verification}
Under \Cref{ass:growth}, for every admissible control $u_\cdot$ valued in $[0,u_{\max}]$ and every initial state $z_0=z$,
\[
V(z,0) := \sup_{u_\cdot} \mathbb E\left[\int_0^T e^{-\rho t}\Big(\theta_t f_t - \tfrac{c}{2}u_t^2\Big)dt\right] = e^{-\rho\cdot 0}W(z,0) = W(z,0),
\]
and the Markov feedback control $u^*(z,t)$ defined in \Cref{eq:optimal-policy} is optimal.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Because the admissible drift $b(z,u)=\kappa(\bar z-z)+\gamma u$ is affine in $z$ and Lipschitz, uniformly bounded in $u\in[0,u_{\max}]$ by $|b(z,u)|\le \kappa|\bar z-z| + \gamma u_{\max}$, and the diffusion coefficient $\sigma$ is constant, the controlled SDE \eqref{eq:sde} admits, for every admissible $u_\cdot$, a unique strong solution on $[0,T]$. A standard comparison/Gronwall argument (comparing $z_t$ pathwise to the uncontrolled Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process shifted by the bounded drift term $\gamma u_{\max}$) yields, for every $p>0$,
\[
\mathbb E\Big[\sup_{t\le T} e^{p|z_t|}\Big] < \infty,
\]
uniformly over admissible controls. This moment bound, together with \Cref{ass:growth}, guarantees (i) that $\mathbb E[e^z_t f_t]<\infty$ so the objective in \Cref{eq:objective} is well defined for every admissible control, and (ii) that the local martingale arising from applying It\^o's formula to $e^{-\rho t}W(z_t,t)$ along an admissible path is a true martingale (its quadratic variation has finite expectation by Cauchy--Schwarz and the moment bound). Applying It\^o's formula to $e^{-\rho t}W(z_t,t)$, using that $W$ solves \Cref{eq:hjb} so that the drift term inside the expectation is bounded above by the running reward for every admissible $u$ (with equality at $u=u^*$), and taking expectations, gives $W(z,0) \ge \mathbb E[\int_0^T e^{-\rho t}(\theta_t f_t - \tfrac{c}{2}u_t^2)dt]$ for every admissible $u_\cdot$, with equality when $u_t = u^*(z_t,t)$. This is the standard verification argument for controlled diffusions with unbounded (here, exponential) running reward and a bounded control set; see \citet{flemingsoner2006} (Chapter IV) or \citet{pham2009} (Theorem 3.5.2) for the general statement and the precise integrability conditions used here. This is the same verification logic that underlies the classical solution of Merton's continuous-time portfolio problem \citep{merton1971}, adapted here to a bounded control set and an exponential (rather than power or logarithmic) running reward.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Existence of a classical solution $W$ satisfying \Cref{ass:growth} is a separate question from verification. Because $u^{\mathrm{unc}}=(\gamma/c)W_z$ is clipped to $[0,u_{\max}]$, the nonlinearity in \Cref{eq:hjb} is globally Lipschitz in $W_z$ (the clip function is 1-Lipschitz and bounded), which is precisely what removes the blow-up mechanism discussed above and places \Cref{eq:hjb} within the class of semilinear parabolic PDEs with linear-growth nonlinearity for which classical existence results are available (via a Picard/fixed-point argument on the clipped equation, or via the stochastic representation of \citet{pham2009}, Chapter 4). We do not repeat that construction here and instead verify the growth condition numerically in \Cref{sec:numerics}, where the solution is bounded on the computational domain and stable under grid refinement.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
This exercise uses FIFA ranking points, not AFCON tournament points, as the performance measure $Y_i$, and a single cross-section rather than a panel, so $z_i$ should be read as ``footballing strength relative to GDP and population, as of mid-2026,'' and, per \Cref{sec:circularity}, as a residual that mechanically correlates with raw FIFA points at $\rho=0.81$; and $\gamma,c$, and (per the discussion above) $\kappa,\sigma$, are shared across countries and set at illustrative values rather than estimated individually. It nonetheless directly operationalizes, for the first time in this paper, the mechanism the theoretical model was built to capture, and the sensitivity analysis shows its central ranking result is not an artifact of the un-identified parameters.
