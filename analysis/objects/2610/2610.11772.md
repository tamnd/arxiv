<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{D:distance-cdf}
For $\mathbf{X}_n$ a fixed $n$-point set in $\mathds{R}^d$, the \emph{distance c.d.f.} $F(\cdot\,;\mathbf{X}_n,\nu)$ is the c.d.f.\ of the random variable $d(U,\mathbf{X}_n)$ when $U$ is distributed with $\nu$:
\begin{eqnarray*}              
F(t;\mathbf{X}_n,\nu) = \nu\left\{U\in \bigcup_{i=1}^n {\mathscr B}_d(\mathbf{x}_i,t)\right\}  = \nu\{d(U,\mathbf{X}_n) \leq t\}\,.
\end{eqnarray*}
When $\mathbf{X}_n=\mathbf{R}_n$ is a random $n$-point set generated with some probability measure $\mathsf{P}_n$ on $\mathds{R}^{n\times d}$, we define the \emph{mean distance c.d.f.} $F(\cdot\,; \mathsf{P}_n,\nu)$ of the random variable $d(U,\mathbf{R}_n)$ by $F(t; \mathsf{P}_n,\nu) = \mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{P}_n}\{F(t;\mathbf{R}_n,\nu) \}$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{D:quantisation-error}
The \emph{$L_s$-mean quantisation error} of a general probability measure $\mu$ on $\mathds{R}^d$ with finite $s$-th moment $\mathsf{E}\{\|X\|^s\}$ for a given $n$-point set $\mathbf{X}_n$ is the $L_s(\mu)$-mean (and, when $s\geq1$, the $L_s(\mu)$-norm) of the distance function $d(\cdot,\mathbf{X}_n)$:
\begin{eqnarray*}             
E_{\mu,s}(\mathbf{X}_n) = \|d(\cdot,\mathbf{X}_n)\|_{L_s} = \left[\int_{\mathscr X} d^s(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{X}_n)\,\mu(\mbox{\rm d}\mathbf{x})\right]^{1/s}\,, \quad s> 0\,.
\end{eqnarray*}
The quantity $D_{\mu,s}(\mathbf{X}_n)=E_{\mu,s}^s(\mathbf{X}_n)$ is called the $(\mu,s)$-distortion related to $\mathbf{X}_n$ {\rm \cite{Pages97}}. When $\mathbf{X}_n=\mathbf{R}_n$ is random, we define the expected $(\mu,s)$-distortion as $D_{\mu,s}(\mathsf{P}_n)=\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{P}_n}\{E_{\mu,s}^s(\mathbf{R}_n)\}$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{R:distortion-evaluation}
For any probability measure $\mu$ such that $\mathsf{E}\{\|X\|^{2s}\}<\infty$, the $(\mu,s)$-distortion of an arbitrary $n$-point set $\mathbf{X}_n$ can be approximated by the Monte-Carlo estimator $D_{\mu_N,s}(\mathbf{X}_n)=(1/N)\sum_{i=1}^N d^s(U_i,\mathbf{X}_n)$, where the $U_i$ are i.i.d.\ with distribution $\mu$. This estimator satisfies the classical central limit theorem
\begin{eqnarray*}
\sqrt{N} \left[D_{\mu_N,s}(\mathbf{X}_n)-D_{\mu,s}(\mathbf{X}_n)\right] \stackrel{\rm d}{\rightarrow} {\mathcal N}(0,V(\mathbf{X}_n))\,, \quad N\to\infty\,,
\end{eqnarray*}
where $V(\mathbf{X}_n)=D_{\mu,2s}(\mathbf{X}_n)-D_{\mu,s}^2(\mathbf{X}_n)$. When $\mu$ is supported on ${\mathscr X}$ compact, $\mathsf{CR}(\mathbf{X}_n)<\infty$ and a direct application of Hoeffding's inequality gives:
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mbox{for all } \alpha\in(0,1)\,, \ \mathsf{Prob}\left\{|D_{\mu_N,s}(\mathbf{X}_n)-D_{\mu,s}(\mathbf{X}_n)|>\alpha\, \mathsf{CR}^s(\mathbf{X}_n) \right\} < 2\,\e1^{-2\,N\alpha^2} \,,
\end{eqnarray*}
showing the exponentially fast concentration of $D_{\mu_N,s}(\mathbf{X}_n)$ to its mean $D_{\mu,s}(\mathbf{X}_n)$ as $N$ increases. When $\mu$ has unbounded support, depending on its tail properties, other concentration inequalities can also be derived.
\mbox{}~\hfill{\lower-0.3ex\hbox{$\triangleleft$}}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{L:projections-beta}
Let $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_d) \stackrel{{d}}{=}  R \cdot Z^{(d)}$ be a spherically symmetric random vector in $\mathds{R}^d$. Then, for any $m\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}$, we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
X^{(m)}=(X_1,\ldots,X_m) \stackrel{{d}}{=}  R \cdot \sqrt{\beta_{m/2,(d-m)/2}}\cdot Z^{(m)} \,,
\end{eqnarray*}
where $R=\|X\|$, $\beta_{m/2,(d-m)/2}$ and $Z^{(m)}$ are independent.  Moreover, the joint density of $V^{(m)}=X^{(m)}/R$ is
\begin{eqnarray*}
\varphi_m(v_1,\ldots,v_m)= \frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\pi^{m/2}\,\Gamma((d-m)/2)} \, \left(1-\sum_{i=1}^m v_i^2 \right)^{(d-m)/2-1} \ \mbox{ for } \sum_{i=1}^m v_i^2\leq 1\,.
\end{eqnarray*}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{th:dist}
Assume that $d\geq 2$ and $X\stackrel{d}{\sim}\mathbb{P}$ with $\mathbb{P}$ spherically symmetric. Then, for any fixed $\mathbf{u} \in \mathds{R}^d$ we have
\begin{eqnarray}
 \|X  - \mathbf{u} \|^2 \stackrel{{d}}{=} (\|\mathbf{u}\|-R)^2 +4\,\|\mathbf{u}\|\,R \, \beta_{\delta,\delta}\, , \label{eq:quantile2}
\end{eqnarray}

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{Th:radial-equivalence}
For every $n\geq1$ and $s>0$, the functional $\Phi\mapsto {\mathcal J}_{\mu,s}(\Phi;n)$ is convex. If $\mu$ is supported in ${\mathscr B}_d(r_{\max})$, then the infimum distortion over all spherically symmetric distributions is attained by a probability measure such that $\Phi$ is supported on $[0,r_{\max}]$. A spherically symmetric distribution $\mathbb{P}^*$ such that $\Phi^*$ is supported on this interval is globally optimal among all spherically symmetric distributions if and only if
\begin{eqnarray}
S_{\mu,s}(\rho;\Phi^*,n) \leq \overline{S}_{\mu,s}(\Phi^*,n)
\quad\hbox{for every }\rho\in[0,r_{\max}]\,,
\label{eq:radial-equivalence-condition}
\end{eqnarray}
where
\begin{eqnarray*}
\overline{S}_{\mu,s}(\Phi,n) =
\int_{\rho\geq 0} S_{\mu,s}(\rho;\Phi,n) \,\mbox{\rm d}\Phi(\rho) \,.
\end{eqnarray*}
In \eqref{eq:radial-equivalence-condition}, equality holds for $\Phi^*$-almost every $\rho$ and the computable quantity
\begin{eqnarray}
g_{\mu,s}(\Phi;n)=\max_{0\leq\rho\leq r_{\max}} S_{\mu,s}(\rho;\Phi,n)
-\overline{S}_{\mu,s}(\Phi,n)
\label{eq:radial-gap}
\end{eqnarray}
provides a global optimality certificate in terms of expected-distortion efficiency \eqref{ED-efficiency}:
\begin{eqnarray}
1 \geq {\mathscr E}_{\mu,s}(\Phi;n) \geq 1 - \frac{g_{\mu,s}(\Phi;n)}{{\mathcal J}_{\mu,s}(\Phi;n)} \,.
\label{eq:radial-gap-bound}
\end{eqnarray}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The map $\Phi\mapsto A(\Phi;r,t)$ is linear, while $x\mapsto(1-x)^n$ is convex on $[0,1]$. Integration in \eqref{eq:radial-functional} preserves convexity. If $\rho>r_{\max}$, replacing a design point $\mathbf{x}=\rho \mathbf{z}$ with $\|\mathbf{z}\|=1$ by $\mathbf{x}'=r_{\max}\mathbf{z}$ cannot increase its distance from any $\mathbf{u}\in{\mathscr B}_d(r_{\max})$, since
\begin{eqnarray*}
\|\mathbf{u}-\rho\, \mathbf{z}\|^2-\|\mathbf{u}-r_{\max}\,\mathbf{z}\|^2
=(\rho-r_{\max})[\rho+r_{\max}-2\, \mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{z}]\geq0.
\end{eqnarray*}
Hence every spherically symmetric distribution can be projected onto $[0,r_{\max}]$ without increasing the distortion. The set of probability measures on this compact interval is weakly compact, and the distortion is continuous under weak convergence, a minimiser thus exists.

For two c.d.f.\ $\Phi$ and $\Phi'$, differentiation along
$\Phi_\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)\Phi+\epsilon\Phi'$ gives
\begin{eqnarray}
\frac{\mbox{\rm d}}{\mbox{\rm d}\epsilon} {\mathcal J}_{\mu,s}(\Phi_\epsilon;n)
\bigg|_{\epsilon=0+}
=\overline{S}_{\mu,s}(\Phi,n) -
\int_{\rho\geq 0} S_{\mu,s}(\rho;\Phi,n)\,\mbox{\rm d}\Phi'(\rho) \,.
\label{eq:radial-directional-derivative}
\end{eqnarray}
Convexity implies that $\Phi^*$ is optimal if and only if this derivative is non-negative for every $\Phi'$. Taking $\Phi'$ to be a point mass at $\rho$ shows that the condition \eqref{eq:radial-equivalence-condition} is necessary. Conversely, the linearity of \eqref{eq:radial-directional-derivative} in $\Phi-\Phi'$ implies that when \eqref{eq:radial-equivalence-condition} is satisfied, then $\mbox{\rm d}{\mathcal J}_{\mu,s}[(1-\epsilon)\Phi^*+\epsilon\Phi';n]/\mbox{\rm d}\epsilon
\big|_{\epsilon=0+} \geq 0$ for any $\Phi'$, so that $\Phi^*$ is optimal. The condition \eqref{eq:radial-equivalence-condition} is thus sufficient. Since its left-hand side has $\Phi^*$-average $\overline{S}_{\mu,s}(\Phi^*,n)$, equality must hold $\Phi^*$-almost everywhere.
Finally, the optimality of $\Phi^*$ and convexity imply, for any $\Phi$,
\begin{eqnarray*}
{\mathcal J}_{\mu,s}(\Phi;n) \geq {\mathcal J}_{\mu,s}(\Phi^*;n) \!\!\!\! &\geq& \!\!\!\!{\mathcal J}_{\mu,s}(\Phi;n) + \mbox{\rm d}{\mathcal J}_{\mu,s}[(1-\epsilon)\Phi+\epsilon\Phi^*;n]/\mbox{\rm d}\epsilon
\big|_{\epsilon=0+} \\
\!\!\!\! &\geq& \!\!\!\! {\mathcal J}_{\mu,s}(\Phi;n) + \min_{0\leq\rho\leq r_{\max}} \left[\overline{S}_{\mu,s}(\Phi,n) - S_{\mu,s}(\rho;\Phi,n)
\right]\,,
\end{eqnarray*}
that is, \eqref{eq:radial-gap-bound}.

\label{Th:distortion-concentration}
For any $d\geq2$, $n\geq2$, $s>0$, $a>0$, and $\mu$ such that $B_{\mu,s}(a)<\infty$, we have
\begin{eqnarray}\label{eq:Voronoi-variance1}

\end{eqnarray}
and
\begin{eqnarray}
\mathsf{var}\{Q_{n,a}\} \leq B_{\mu,s}^2(a)\,B(d,n)\,,
\label{eq:Voronoi-variance2}
\end{eqnarray}
where 
\begin{eqnarray*}
B(d,n) = \inf_{\theta\in(0,\pi)}\left\{
v_d(\theta)+N_d(\theta/2)\exp[-n v_d(\theta/2)]
\right\}\,.
\end{eqnarray*}
Therefore,
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathsf{Prob}\{|Q_{n,a}-D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}_a^{[n]})|\geq t\} \leq \frac{B_{\mu,s}^2(a)\,B(d,n)}{t^2}\,,
\end{eqnarray*}
and for every fixed $d$,

\quad\hbox{in $L_2$ and in probability as }n\to\infty.
\end{eqnarray}

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $Q_{n,a}^{(-i)}$ denote the distortion obtained after deleting the point $aZ_i$.  This random variable is independent of $Z_i$ conditionally on the other $n-1$ points.  Deleting $aZ_i$ changes the nearest-neighbour distance only for directions in $V_{i,n}$.  Since the distance between a point of norm $r$ and any point of norm $a$ is at most $r+a$, spherical symmetry and independence of the radial and angular components give
\begin{eqnarray*}
0\leq Q_{n,a}^{(-i)}-Q_{n,a}\leq B_{\mu,s}(a)p_{i,n}.
\end{eqnarray*}
The deletion form of the Efron--Stein inequality, see \cite{BoucheronLB2004}, therefore yields
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathsf{var}\{Q_{n,a}\}\leq
\sum_{i=1}^n\mathsf{E}\{(Q_{n,a}^{(-i)}-Q_{n,a})^2\}
\!\!\! &\leq& \!\!\!B_{\mu,s}^2(a)\, \mathsf{E}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^np_{i,n}^2\right\} \\
&\leq& \!\!\! B_{\mu,s}^2(a)\,\mathsf{E}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq n}p_{i,n}\right\} \,,
\end{eqnarray*}
where the last inequality follows from $\sum_i p_{i,n}=1$. This completes the proof of \eqref{eq:Voronoi-variance1}.


Let
$\omega_n=\max_{z\in{\mathcal S}_{d-1}(1)}\min_i\arccos(z^\top Z_i)$ be the angular covering radius. Every Voronoi cell is contained in a cap of radius $\omega_n$, and hence
$\max_i p_{i,n}\leq v_d(\omega_n)$. Now take a covering of the sphere by $N_d(\theta/2)$ caps of radius $\theta/2$ and centres $c_k$, $k=1,\ldots,N_d(\theta/2)$. Assume that each cap contains at least one $Z_i$. Then, any $z\in{\mathcal S}_{d-1}(1)$ belongs to some cap with centre $c_k$ and radius $\theta/2$ and this cap also contains a $Z_i$, implying that $\omega_n\leq\theta$.
A union bound gives
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathsf{Prob}\{\omega_n>\theta\}
\leq N_d(\theta/2)[1-v_d(\theta/2)]^n
\leq N_d(\theta/2)e^{-nv_d(\theta/2)}\,.
\end{eqnarray*}
It follows that
$\mathsf{E}\{\max_i p_{i,n}\}\leq v_d(\theta) + \mathsf{Prob}\{\omega_n>\theta\}$, proving \eqref{eq:Voronoi-variance2}.

For fixed $d$, the i.i.d.\ directions are almost surely dense on the sphere, so $\omega_n\to0$ and $\max_i p_{i,n}\to0$ almost surely. Dominated convergence in \eqref{eq:Voronoi-variance1} proves \eqref{eq:fixed-d-concentration}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{th:kappa_n}
For fixed $d\geq 3$, the $1/n$ quantile $\kappa_{n,d}$ of  $\beta_{\delta,\delta}$ with $\delta=(d-1)/2$ tends to zero as $n$ tends to infinity. When $d \to \infty$ and $n=n(d)$ grows with $d$, we have the following three cases:\\
\noindent{\textit(i)} if $n^{1/d}\to\infty$, then $\lim_{d\to\infty} \kappa_{n,d} = 0$;\\
\noindent{\textit(ii)} if $n^{1/d}\to\lambda$ with $\lambda>1$, then
\begin{eqnarray} \label{asymptotic-kappa} \lim_{d\to\infty} \kappa_{n,d} = \frac12 \left[1-\sqrt{1-1/\lambda^2}\right]\, ;
\end{eqnarray}
\noindent{\textit(iii)} if $(\log n)/d \to 0$, then $\lim_{d\to\infty} \kappa_{n,d} = \frac12$.

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Since $x^{\delta-1}(1-t)^\delta\leq x^{\delta-1}(1-t)^{\delta-1}\leq x^{\delta-1}(1-x)^{\delta-1}$ for $\delta\geq 1$ and all $t\in[0,1)$ and $x\in[0,t]$, we have
\begin{eqnarray}\label{It-ineq1}
\frac{t^\delta (1-t)^\delta}{\delta B(\delta,\delta)} \leq I_t(\delta,\delta) \,. \end{eqnarray}
The derivation of an exploitable upper bound is more delicate.
Assume that $d \geq 3$ and let
$\delta'=\lfloor \delta \rfloor$, the largest integer smaller than or equal to $\delta$.
As $n\geq 2$, we are only interested in values of $t$ less than $1/2$. Since $\delta'\leq \delta$, we have $I_t(\delta,\delta) \leq I_t(\delta',\delta')$, which
can be calculated explicitly by successive integration by parts. Direct calculations yield the following special case of the well-known relation between the c.d.f.\ of the beta and binomial distributions:
\begin{eqnarray}\label{It1}
I_t(\delta',\delta') &=& \sum_{k=0}^{\delta'-1} \binom{2\delta'-1}{k} t^{2\delta'-1-k}(1-t)^k \,, \\
&=& t^{\delta'}(1-t)^{\delta'-1} \sum_{k=0}^{\delta'-1} \binom{2\delta'-1}{k} \left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right)^{\delta'-1-k} \,.
\end{eqnarray}
Using the property $t/(1-t) \leq 1$ for $t\leq 1/2$, with $\sum_{k=0}^{\delta'-1} \binom{2\delta'-1}{k}=4^{\delta'-1}$, we thus obtain
\begin{eqnarray} \label{upper_bound}
I_t(\delta,\delta) \leq I_t(\delta',\delta') \leq t^{\delta'}[4\,(1-t)]^{\delta'-1} \leq \frac12\,[4\,t(1-t)]^{\delta'-1}\,, \ t\leq 1/2\,.
\end{eqnarray}

Together with \eqref{It-ineq1}, this implies, for $n\geq 2$ and $d\geq 5$,
\begin{eqnarray}\label{bounds-kappa}
\frac12 \left[1-\sqrt{1- (2/n)^{1/(\delta'-1)}}\right]  \leq \kappa_{n,d} \leq \frac12 \left[1-\sqrt{(1-4\,c_d\,n^{-1/\delta})_+}\right] \,,
\end{eqnarray}
where $(x)_+=\max\{x,0\}$ and $c_d=[\delta\,B(\delta,\delta)]^{1/\delta} = 1/4+ (\log d)/(4\,d)+{\mathcal O}(1/d)$, $d\to\infty$ (with, moreover, $(4\,c_d)^\delta<d+2$ for all $d \geq 2$, implying that the right-hand side of \eqref{bounds-kappa} is strictly smaller than  1/2 for $n\geq d+2$).

When $d$ is fixed and $n\to\infty$, or $n^{1/d}\to\infty$ as $d\to\infty$, $n^{-1/d}\to 0$ implying that the right-hand side of \eqref{bounds-kappa} tends to zero. Therefore, $\kappa_{n,d}\to 0$.

In case (\textit{ii}), $n^{-1/d}\to\lambda^{-1}$. Taking limits on both sides of \eqref{bounds-kappa}, and using $\delta/d\to1/2$, $\delta'/d\to1/2$, and $c_d\to1/4$, we get $\lim_{d\to\infty}\kappa_{n,d}=(1/2)[1-\sqrt{1-1/\lambda^2}]$.

When $\log n = o(d)$, (case (\textit{iii})), $n^{-1/d}\to 1$ and the left-hand side of \eqref{bounds-kappa} tends to 1/2.

In each of the three regimes considered, Proposition~\ref{th:kappa_n} gives
$\kappa_{n(d),d}\to\kappa_\infty$. Consider the random variable $Z_d=\zeta(n(d),d)$ in \eqref{d2URn}. We first show that, for every $p>0$, $Z_d \to \kappa_\infty$ in $L^p$ (and thus in particular, $m_{n(d),d}=\mathsf{E}\{Z_d\}\to\kappa_\infty$).

Fix $M>1$ and define
\begin{eqnarray*}
n_d^+=\lceil M n(d)\rceil
\quad\mbox{and}\quad
n_d^-=\lfloor n(d)/M\rfloor\,.
\end{eqnarray*}
Multiplication of $n(d)$ by a fixed positive constant does not change its asymptotic regime. Therefore,
$\kappa_{n_d^+,d}\to\kappa_\infty$ and $\kappa_{n_d^-,d}\to\kappa_\infty$.
For every $\epsilon>0$ and all sufficiently large $d$ we have
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathsf{Prob}\{Z_d\leq\kappa_\infty-\epsilon\}
&\leq&
\mathsf{Prob}\{Z_d\leq\kappa_{n_d^+,d}\}
=1-\left(1-\frac1{n_d^+}\right)^{n(d)}
\leq\frac1M\,,\\
\mathsf{Prob}\{Z_d>\kappa_\infty+\epsilon\}
&\leq&
\mathsf{Prob}\{Z_d>\kappa_{n_d^-,d}\}
=\left(1-\frac1{n_d^-}\right)^{n(d)}
\leq\exp(-M)\,.
\end{eqnarray*}
Letting first $d\to\infty$ and then $M\to\infty$ proves convergence in probability. Since $0\leq Z_d\leq1$, convergence holds in $L^p$ for every $p>0$, which also gives $m_{n(d),d}=\mathsf{E}\{Z_d\}\to\kappa_\infty$.

Now, consider \eqref{d2URn} again. Conditioning on $U_d$ gives the exact representation
\begin{eqnarray}\label{joint-exact-representation}
D_{\mu_d,s}(\mathbb{P}_a^{[n(d)]})
=\mathsf{E}\left\{\left[(R_d-a)^2+4\,aR_dZ_d\right]^{s/2}\right\}\,,
\end{eqnarray}
where $Z_d$ may be taken independent of $R_d$. As $Z_d\to\kappa_\infty$ in probability, if $a_d\to a$ then
\begin{eqnarray*}
(R_d-a_d)^2+4\,a_dR_dZ_d \to (r-a)^2+4\,ar\kappa_\infty
\end{eqnarray*}
in probability. Moreover,
\begin{eqnarray*}
0\leq
\left[(R_d-a_d)^2+4\,a_dR_dZ_d\right]^{s/2}
\leq(R_d+a_d)^s\,.
\end{eqnarray*}
Assumption \eqref{joint-radius-assumptions} therefore gives uniform integrability, and \eqref{joint-exact-D-limit} follows.
Using truncation of $R_d$ and uniform continuity on compact sets, we obtain that the convergence in \eqref{joint-exact-D-limit} is locally uniform in $a$. The minimisers $a_d^*$ form a bounded sequence: indeed,
\begin{eqnarray*}
D_{\mu_d,s}(\mathbb{P}_a^{[n(d)]})
\geq\mathsf{E}\{|R_d-a|^s\}\,,
\end{eqnarray*}
whereas the choice $a=0$ gives $D_{\mu_d,s}(\mathbb{P}_0^{[n(d)]})=\mathsf{E}\{R_d^s\}$, which is uniformly bounded. The standard argmin argument can therefore be applied to the limiting function
\begin{eqnarray*}
L(a)=\left[(r-a)^2+4\,ar\kappa_\infty\right]^{s/2}\,.
\end{eqnarray*}
Since $s>0$, its unique minimiser is $a=r(1-2\kappa_\infty)$, and its minimum is $[4\,r^2\kappa_\infty(1-\kappa_\infty)]^{s/2}$. Substitution of the three values in \eqref{joint-kappa-limits} gives \eqref{joint-exact-a-limits} and \eqref{joint-exact-min-D-limits}.

From the results in Section~\ref{S:n-growing-with-d}*, the four quantities $a^*(d,n,s)$, $\widetilde{a^*}(d,n,s)$,
$M_{\psi,1}\widetilde{a^*}(d,n,s)$ and
$\widehat{a^*}(d,s)$ tend to
$\widehat{a^*_\lambda}=\sqrt{3}/2$ when $d\to\infty$. Our numerical results suggest that, in the considered asymptotic regime, $\sigma^*(d,n,s)$ also converges to the limiting value $\widehat{a^*_\lambda} = \sqrt{3}/2$. However, a rigorous theoretical analysis is still required to confirm this observation. The same is true for the asymptotic behaviour of $b^*(d,n,s)$ in the optimised quantiser $\mathbb{P}_{0,b^*}^{[n]}$ when $\mu$ is uniform in ${\mathscr B}_d(1)$. From (\ref{eq:quantile2}*) in Proposition~\ref{th:dist}*, we have to analyse the behaviour of $d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{R}_n)=\min_{i=1,\ldots,n} \left[(\|\mathbf{u}\|-R_i)^2 +4\,\|\mathbf{u}\|\,R_i \, \zeta_i\right]$ when $n\to\infty$, where the $R_i$ are i.i.d., the $\zeta_i$ are i.i.d.\ with $\zeta_i\stackrel{{d}}{=}\beta_{\delta,\delta}$, and the two families are mutually independent. A norm-concentration property for $R=\|X\|$ as $d\to\infty$ does not by itself imply an extreme-value limit for $d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{R}_n)$. Even the cases where $\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{0,b}$ is uniform on ${\mathscr B}_d(b)$ or where $\mathbb{P}=\mu_\sigma$ is multivariate normal pose significant challenges.
