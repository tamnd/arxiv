\subsubsection{Condition (b)}
In this case, the fact that the orbital contains $N-1$ lines implies that there is a homomorphism $\varphi:\mathscr{S}_N\rightarrow\mathscr{S}_{N-1}$. When $N\geqslant 5$, $\mathscr{S}_N$ has a non-trivial normal subgroup, the alternating group $\mathscr{A}_N$. Since $\varphi$ is not injective, we have $\mathscr{A}_N\subseteq {\rm Ker}~\varphi$ for $N\geqslant 5$. Thus using the basic theorem of homomorphisms, we have
\begin{equation}
|{\rm Im}~\varphi|\leqslant \frac{|\mathscr{S}_N|}{|\mathscr{A}_N|}=2.
\end{equation}
However, in Condition (b) the orbital size argument yields $|{\rm Im}~\varphi|\geqslant N-1$, which is clearly impossible for $N\geqslant 5$. Therefore, orbitals satisfying Condition (b) do not exist when $N\geqslant 5$.

The full proof is thus completed by combining the analysis of Conditions (a) and (b).
