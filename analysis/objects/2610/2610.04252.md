\label{Thm: intro wall closed comparison}
For every $d>0$, the function
\[
  k\longmapsto(-1)^kW_{d,k},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},
\]
extends to a polynomial of degree at most $2d-2$, whose value at $k=0$ is
$dN_d$. In particular, the closed invariants are recovered from finitely many
coefficients of the slab function by
\[
  N_d=-\frac1d\sum_{k=1}^{2d-1}\binom{2d-1}{k}W_{d,k}.
\]
<!-- end maintheorem-1 -->
<!-- maintheorem-2: body_md -->
\label{Thm: intro period comparison} Under Assumption~\ref{Ass: analyticity},
the polynomial in Theorem~\ref{Thm: intro wall closed comparison} has degree
$2d-1$ and for every $d>0$ and $\lambda>0$ sufficiently small, we have
\[
  [t^d\log t]\int_{\check X_t^{>0}(\lambda)}\Omega
  =\sum_{k>0}W_{d,k}\lambda^k
   -\sum_{i=1}^3\sum_{k=1}^d W_{d-k,k{\boldsymbol{e}}_i}\lambda^{-k}.
\]
Thus the positive powers of $\lambda$ give the generating function of the
$W_{d,k}$. The finite sum involves the opposite outgoing contact orders, in
the notation of \S\ref{SS: wall invariants}.

The coefficient of $t^d\log t$ admits a rational continuation in $\lambda$,
with possible finite poles only at $0$ and $-1$. This continuation satisfies
\[
  \lim_{\lambda\to\infty}
    [t^d\log t]\int_{\check X_t^{>0}(\lambda)}\Omega
  =\sum_{k=1}^{2d-1}\binom{2d-1}{k}W_{d,k}
  =-dN_d.
\]

<!-- maintheorem-3: body_md -->
\label{Thm: intro tree formula}
Assume that $f$ agrees with the normalized slab function $1+x+y+z+g(t)$ from the
construction of \cite{affinecomplex}, as in Assumption~\ref{Ass: explicit slab
shape}. Then, for every $d>0$,
\[

    \frac1k\binom{2d-1}{k}
    \sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{T}_{d,k}^{\,i}}
      \frac{(-1)^{|\widehat V_\Gamma|}}{|\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)|}.
\]

\label{Prop: Mumford degeneration}
The total space $\mathbb{P}_\Xi$ is smooth and has trivial canonical bundle. The
composition
\[
  \pi\colon\mathbb{P}_\Xi\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^2\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^1=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[\tau]
\]
is a normal crossings degeneration of non-compact Calabi--Yau threefolds with
general fiber $K_{\mathbb{P}^2}$. Its central fiber has four irreducible components
$P,D_1,D_2,D_3$ corresponding to the four regions bordered by the solid red
lines. Here $P\simeq\mathbb{P}^2\times\mathbb{A}^1$, while each $D_i$ is a toric blow-up of
$\operatorname{Tot}\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-3)\big) \times\mathbb{A}^1$.

Restricting to $\sigma=0$ induces one-parameter toric degenerations with
parameter $\tau$ for the polarized toric varieties defined by the regions
bordered by dashed lines. The component $Y$ corresponding to the bounded dashed
region is a toric degeneration of $\mathbb{P}^2$ whose central fiber is the union of
$\mathbb{P}^2$ and three torically blown-up Hirzebruch surfaces $Y_1,Y_2,Y_3$. Moreover,
$\mathbb{P}_\Xi$ is the total space of $N_{Y/\mathbb{P}_\Xi}\simeq\mathcal{O}_Y(-D_\infty)$
with zero section $Y$, where $D_\infty\subset Y$ is the degeneration of the
three coordinate lines given by the boundary of the dashed triangle.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Smoothness is obvious at the trivalent vertices. At the four-valent vertex of
$\Xi$ projecting to $(2,2)$, the integral generators of the edges form the
lattice basis $(1,0,1,0)$, $(1,1,1,1)$, $(-1,0,0,0)$, $(-1,-1,-1,0)$, and
similarly at the two other four-valent vertices.

For $K_{\mathbb{P}_\Xi}=0$, observe that $\mathbb{P}_\Xi$ has eight toric divisors, four
contained in $\{\sigma=0\}$ and four contained in $\{\tau=0\}$, and $\sigma\tau$
vanishes to order one on each of them. Equivalently, $(0,0,1,1)\in M\oplus\mathbb{Z}^2$
pairs to $1$ with each primitive generator of the normal fan of $\Xi$.

Inverting $\tau$ gives the toric variety with momentum polyhedron $\mathbb{R}$ times
the overgraph of $\psi$. Since that overgraph is the standard momentum
polyhedron of $K_{\mathbb{P}^2}$, the general fiber of $\pi$ is toric $K_{\mathbb{P}^2}$. The
statements about $\pi^{-1}(0)=V(\tau)$ and $V(\sigma)$ follow by restricting the
polyhedral decomposition to the corresponding regions.

Every vertex of $\Xi$ lies on the facet defining $Y$, so every maximal cone of
the normal fan contains its ray. Projection along this ray gives the line-bundle
projection
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: Mumford normal bundle}
  p\colon\mathbb{P}_\Xi\simeq\operatorname{Tot}(N_{Y|\mathbb{P}_\Xi})\longrightarrow Y.
\end{equation}
The monomial $\sigma$ is fiber-linear with zero divisor $Y+p^*D_\infty$. It
therefore induces a section of $N_{Y|\mathbb{P}_\Xi}^\vee$ with zero divisor $D_\infty$,
proving $N_{Y|\mathbb{P}_\Xi}^\vee\simeq\mathcal{O}_Y(D_\infty)$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{Lem: toric small modification} Let $M$ be a lattice and let
$\varphi,\psi_0,\psi_1$ be convex integral piecewise affine functions on $M_\mathbb{R}$
with finitely many domains of linearity. Suppose that $\psi_0,\psi_1$ have the
same sets of slopes. Denote the maximal domains of $\varphi$ by $\sigma_i$ and
those of $\psi_k$ by $\tau_j^k$, labeled so that $\psi_0|_{\tau_j^0}$ and
$\psi_1|_{\tau_j^1}$ have the same slope. Put
\[
  \Xi_k=\big\{(m,a,b)\in M_\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^2\mid
    a\geq\varphi(m),\ b\geq\psi_k(m)\big\},\qquad k=0,1.
\]
The identity on the common torus extends to a morphism
$\mathbb{P}_{\Xi_1}\to\mathbb{P}_{\Xi_0}$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: mixed incidence}
  \sigma_I\cap\tau_J^1\neq\emptyset
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \sigma_I\cap\tau_J^0\neq\emptyset
\end{equation}
for all index sets $I,J$, where subscripts denote intersections and the empty
intersection is $M_\mathbb{R}$. Whenever it exists, this morphism is a proper small
toric modification over $\mathbb{A}^2$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $p_i,q_j$ denote the slopes on $\sigma_i,\tau_j^k$. The facets of $\Xi_k$
have primitive inward normals $(-p_i,1,0)$ and $(-q_j,0,1)$ and project onto
$\sigma_i$ and $\tau_j^k$, respectively. Thus the facets indexed by $I,J$ meet
if and only if $\sigma_I\cap\tau_J^k\neq\emptyset$. A normal cone is generated
by the normals of its incident facets. Since these normals generate rays of both
fans, a source cone lies in a target cone if and only if the corresponding
target facets meet. Hence cone containment is equivalent to \eqref{Eqn: mixed
incidence}. The two fans have the same support, the cone generated by their
common rays, so the resulting morphism is proper. It is small because no rays
are added and commutes with the two coordinate monomials because it is induced
by the identity on the lattice.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{Thm: logarithmic period coefficient}
For $d>0$ and $\lambda>0$ sufficiently small, with ${\boldsymbol{e}}_i$ as in
\eqref{Eqn: e_i}, we have
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: logarithmic period coefficient}
  [t^d\log t]\int_{\check X_t^{>0}(\lambda)}\Omega
  =\sum_{i=1}^3\left(
    \sum_{k>0}\lambda^k W_{d,-k{\boldsymbol{e}}_i}
    -\sum_{k=1}^d\lambda^{-k}W_{d-k,k{\boldsymbol{e}}_i}
  \right).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Convergence of $\log f$ near the origin permits termwise integration. Monomials
$x^a y^b$ in the expansion of $\log f$ in \eqref{Eqn: Canonical slab function}
with $ab(a-b)\neq0$ do not contribute to $t^d\log t$:
\begin{multline*}
  \int_{\Gamma(\lambda)}t^r x^ay^b\,\operatorname{dlog} x\wedge\operatorname{dlog} y
  =t^r\int_{t/\lambda^2}^{\lambda}\int_{t/(\lambda x)}^{\lambda}
    x^{a-1}y^{b-1}\,dy\,dx\\
  =\frac{t^r}{b}\int_{t/\lambda^2}^{\lambda}
    \left(\lambda^bx^{a-1}
      -t^b\lambda^{-b}x^{a-b-1}\right)\,dx
  =\frac{t^r\lambda^{a+b}}{ab}
    +\frac{t^{r+a}\lambda^{b-2a}}{a(a-b)}
    -\frac{t^{r+b}\lambda^{a-2b}}{b(a-b)}.
\end{multline*}
The remaining monomials can be written $t^r x^k$, $t^r y^k$,
$t^r z^k=t^{r+k}(xy)^{-k}$ with $k\neq0$. By cyclic invariance of the domain and
the logarithmic measure it suffices to compute
\begin{align*}
  \int_{\Gamma(\lambda)}t^r x^k\,\operatorname{dlog} x\wedge\operatorname{dlog} y
  &=t^r\left[\frac{x^k}{k}
    \left(\log x+2\log\lambda-\log t-\frac1k\right)
    \right]_{t/\lambda^2}^{\lambda}\\
  &=-\frac{t^r\lambda^k}{k}\log t
    +t^r\lambda^k\left(\frac{3\log\lambda}{k}-\frac1{k^2}\right)
    +\frac{t^{r+k}\lambda^{-2k}}{k^2}.
\end{align*}
At the lower endpoint, $\log x+2\log\lambda-\log t=0$, leaving the last term
in the displayed expression.

For $k>0$, tropicalizations of wall type with contact orders $-k{\boldsymbol{e}}_1$ and
$k{\boldsymbol{e}}_1$, taken with endpoints in cells whose tangent cones at the central
vertex contain ${\boldsymbol{e}}_1$ and $-{\boldsymbol{e}}_1$, respectively, contribute the terms
\[
  \lambda^k W_{d,-k{\boldsymbol{e}}_1}\,t^d\log t,\qquad
  -\lambda^{-k}W_{d,k{\boldsymbol{e}}_1}\,t^{d+k}\log t.
\]
The case $a=b=0$ is excluded from $\log f$ by the definition of canonical wall
functions, while the remaining term in \eqref{Eqn: First integral} is
\[
  -\int_{\Gamma(\lambda)}\log t\,\operatorname{dlog} x\wedge\operatorname{dlog} y
  =-\frac12(\log t)^3+3\log\lambda\,(\log t)^2
    -\frac92(\log\lambda)^2\log t.
\]
Extracting the coefficient of $t^d\log t$ for $d>0$, with the integral length
$|k{\boldsymbol{e}}_i|=k$ and the minus sign in \eqref{Eqn: First integral}, gives
\eqref{Eqn: logarithmic period coefficient}.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Redoing this computation with the non-normalized naive mirror function $1+x+y+z$
leads to a term $\log\lambda\cdot t^d\log t$ from $a=b=0$ for all $d>0$. With
this term, the asymptotic limit in the following \S\ref{Sec: asymptotic limit}
can still be done, but requires a much more complicated reasoning involving a
Mellin-Barnes integral and subsequent reparametrization by the mirror map to
obtain the Gromov--Witten potential.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{Prop: asymptotic period}
Under Assumption~\ref{Ass: wall polynomiality}, for every $d>0$ the coefficient
in \eqref{Eqn: logarithmic period coefficient} extends to a rational function of
$\lambda$, with possible finite poles only at $0$ and $-1$, and
\[
  \lim_{\lambda\to\infty}
  [t^d\log t]\int_{\check X_t^{>0}(\lambda)}\Omega
  =\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}W_d(\lambda)
  =-\sum_{i=1}^3P_{d,i}(0).
\]
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Write $\Delta P(k)=P(k+1)-P(k)$. The Newton expansion and the binomial
generating series give
\[
  P_{d,i}(k)=\sum_{j\geq0}\binom{k}{j}\Delta^jP_{d,i}(0),\qquad
  \sum_{k\geq0}\binom{k}{j}(-\lambda)^k
  =\frac{(-\lambda)^j}{(1+\lambda)^{j+1}}.
\]
The first sum is finite since $P_{d,i}$ is a polynomial. Hence
\[
  \sum_{k>0}\lambda^kW_{d,-k{\boldsymbol{e}}_i}
  =\sum_{j\geq0}\Delta^jP_{d,i}(0)
    \frac{(-\lambda)^j}{(1+\lambda)^{j+1}}-P_{d,i}(0).
\]
The right-hand side gives the asserted rational continuation. Every term in the
last sum tends to zero as $\lambda\to\infty$, as does the finite Laurent
polynomial $\sum_{k=1}^d\lambda^{-k}W_{d-k,k{\boldsymbol{e}}_i}$ in \eqref{Eqn: logarithmic
period coefficient}. The stated limit follows.

\label{Lem: support of maps with at most one puncture}
The image of a genus-zero stable punctured map to $X_0$ with at most one marked
point is contained in $V(s+1,\tau)$. If its total curve class is not a positive
multiple of one of the exceptional classes $e_1,e_2,e_3$, its image is contained
in the projective surface
\[
  \big(P\cap V(s+1)\big)\cup Y_1\cup Y_2\cup Y_3.
\]
For every fixed decorated type ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$ satisfying this additional condition,
$\mathscr{M}(X,{\boldsymbol{\tau}})$ is proper.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $f:C^\circ\to X_0$ be such a map, let $\nu:X\to\overline X$ be the resolution, and
put $\overline f=\nu\circ f$ and $\overline P=\nu(P)$. Stability forces $f$ to be
nonconstant. Every compact curve in $\overline X_0$ lies in $\overline P$ since the bundles
describing $P_\pm$ and the $\overline D_i$ in \S\ref{SS: maximal degeneration} are
negative, so their proper curves lie in their zero sections, all contained in
$\overline P$. By connectedness, $\overline f(C)$ is therefore contained in $\overline P$ or is a
point outside it.

In the latter case, $f(C)$ lies in a nontrivial fiber of $\nu$. Outside $\overline P$
these are the $\mathbb{P}^1$-fibers of the unbounded strips in Figure~\ref{Fig: toric
model}, of classes $e_i$, and lie in $V(s+1,\tau)$. In particular, their total
class is a positive multiple of an $e_i$.

It remains to treat the case $\overline f(C)\subset\overline P\simeq\mathbb{P}^1\times\mathbb{P}^2$. Then
\[
  a:=\deg(\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\overline f)^*\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1)
  =f_*[C]\cdot P_+=f_*[C]\cdot P_-\geq0.
\]
If $a>0$, the degree--contact relation \cite[Cor.\,2.30]{ACGS2} requires a marked
point mapping to each of $P_+$ and $P_-$. Since these divisors are disjoint,
this contradicts the assumption on the markings. Hence $a=0$, and connectedness
makes $\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\overline f$ constant. Denote its value by
$s_0\in\mathbb{P}^1$, including the two end values.

If $s_0\neq-1$, the resolution is an isomorphism over the corresponding
$\mathbb{P}^2$-fiber, so $f$ has image in this fiber and class $d\ell$, with $d>0$. Its
intersection with each $D_i$ is $d$. The degree--contact relation would
therefore require a marked point whose image lies in $P\cap D_1\cap D_2\cap
D_3=\emptyset$. Thus $s_0=-1$ and $f$ has image in $\nu^{-1}\big(\overline 
P\cap V(s+1)\big)$, the stated reducible surface.

For a fixed decorated type satisfying this condition, properness follows from
this projective support by the boundedness and stable reduction arguments of
\cite[Cor.\,3.19]{ACGS2}.

\label{Lem: boundary tree contact}
Let ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}=(\tau,\mathbf{A})$ be the decorated type of a punctured logarithmic map,
with underlying graph $G$ and exactly one leg $L$. Suppose all vertices
and bounded edges map to $B_0$, and let $G_\partial\subseteq G$ be the subgraph
mapping to $\partial B_0$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: boundary tree contact}
  u_\tau=u_L=\sum_{v\in V(G)}u_{\mathbf{A}(v)}
    =\sum_{v\in V(G_\partial)}u_{\mathbf{A}(v)}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Summing generalized balancing \cite[\S2.2]{ACGS2} over all vertices cancels the
contributions of the bounded edges, leaving $u_L=\sum_{v\in V(G)}u_{\mathbf{A}(v)}$.
The curve contact orders at vertices mapping to $\operatorname{Int} B_0$ vanish, so only
vertices of $G_\partial$ contribute.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{Lem: vanishing from splitting}
A decorated wall type or rigid closed type ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$ with nonzero invariant has at
most one boundary attachment.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Suppose there are at least two boundary attachments. Call a maximal connected
subgraph spanned by vertices in $\operatorname{Int} B_0$ an interior subtree. Ordinary
balancing excludes an interior subtree with just one attachment unless it
carries the original leg. Since there is at most one original leg, some interior
subtree has at least two attachments. Split along all boundary attachments.

All contact orders of ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$ are parallel to $B_0$. Since the vertex classes
have zero intersection with $P_\pm$, generalized balancing shows that this
remains true under every realizable generization of a split type occurring in
Wu's formula. After splitting, each tropical piece ${\boldsymbol{\omega}}$ therefore has
constant normal coordinate. Every leg $L$ of a boundary piece ${\boldsymbol{\omega}}$ is
pointing inwards and hence fulfills $u_L(\varphi_P)>0$ for $\varphi_P$ the
piecewise affine function on $B$ defined by $P$. Thus the degree--contact
relation for ${\boldsymbol{\omega}}=(\omega,A')$ shows $P\cdot A'>0$. If the common normal
coordinate were nonzero, then for sufficiently small displacements, the
corresponding stable map has image in $P\cap P_+$ or $P\cap P_-$, hence has
class $A'=d\ell$. Thus $P\cdot A'=-3d\leq0$, contrary to $P\cdot A'>0$. Hence
the boundary pieces remain in $B_0$.

The gluing strata are toric strata of $P$, so Wu's splitting formula applies.
Choose displacement vectors with distinct normal components at two attachments
of the chosen interior subtree. The boundary endpoints have normal coordinate
zero, while the interior piece has a single normal coordinate. The displaced
tropical matching conditions are therefore incompatible, and every term in the
splitting formula vanishes.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{Prop: shape of wall types}
Let ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}=(\tau,\mathbf{A})$ be a decorated wall type with $W_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0$ and
underlying tree $G$. Then the part $G_\partial$ mapping to $\partial B_0$ is a
connected tree with a unique last vertex $v_\partial$. This vertex maps to the
interior of a boundary edge, all other vertices of $G_\partial$ to boundary
vertices. Every edge of $G_\partial$ has contact order in $\bigcup_{i=1}^3\mathbb{Z}
u_{e_i}$.

Starting at $v_\partial$, the remainder is a straight chain of at most two
bounded edges followed by the outgoing leg, all with contact order $u_\tau$. Its
vertices other than $v_\partial$ lie on edges of $B_0$ not contained in
$\partial B_0$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The wall-type dimension conditions exclude nontrivial deformations fixing the
outgoing evaluation. In particular, every bounded edge has nonzero contact
order, since otherwise its length could vary independently. Since $u_\tau\neq0$,
Lemma~\ref{Lem: boundary tree contact} shows that $G_\partial$ is nonempty, and
Lemma~\ref{Lem: vanishing from splitting} shows that it is connected. Ordinary
balancing excludes interior leaves, so the remainder is a straight chain of
contact order $u_\tau$, starting at the last vertex $v_\partial$ of
$G_\partial$.

The outgoing leg sweeps out a two-dimensional set, so its supporting line must
vary. Hence $v_\partial$ maps to the interior of a boundary edge, and the chain
avoids the central vertex. Any other vertex of $G_\partial$ mapping to the
interior of a boundary edge, or any vertex of the chain mapping to the interior
of a triangle, could move while fixing the outgoing evaluation. Thus all other
vertices of $G_\partial$ map to boundary vertices, and the chain is subdivided
only at interior edges. A straight line avoiding the central vertex crosses at
most two such edges. Finally, the primitive tangent vectors of the boundary
edges are the $u_{e_i}$, up to sign.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{Rem: corner trees}
At a vertex $v$ mapping to a boundary vertex, let $E_v$ be the
outgoing edge and $E_1,\ldots,E_s$ the incoming edges. Generalized balancing
reads
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: corner balancing}
  u_{E_v}-\sum_{j=1}^s u_{E_j}=u_{\mathbf{A}(v)}.
\end{equation}
For example, at $(1,0)$ the primitive boundary directions pointing away from the
vertex are $u_{e_2}=(-1,1)$ and $u_{e_1}=(-2,-1)$. Writing $a,b$ for the total
incident weights in these directions gives
\[
  u_{\mathbf{A}(v)}=a\,u_{e_2}+b\,u_{e_1}=(-a-2b,a-b).
\]
For $a=b$ this becomes $3a\,u_{f_1}$. The other two corners are treated by the
same balancing rule with their boundary directions.

Although $G_\partial$ has no edges of contact order zero, such edges may occur
in the actual decorated type ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}'=(\tau',\mathbf{A}')$ of a punctured map marked by
${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$. Let $H$ be a connected subtree of its graph carrying no leg and with
all attaching edges of contact order zero. Summing the degree--contact relation
\cite[Prop.\,2.29]{ACGS2} over its vertices cancels the internal edge
contributions and gives $\sum_{v'\in V(H)}\mathbf{A}'(v')\cdot D=0$ for
$D=P,D_1,D_2,D_3$. By the intersection table in \S2, the total class of $H$
pushes forward to $d\beta$ for some $d\geq0$. If $H$ is marked by a single
vertex, this excludes positive multiples of $\ell$ as total curve class,
although $u_\ell=0$.

For a subtree marked by several vertices of ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$, the individual curve
contact orders need not vanish. For example, the representatives of $\beta$ from
\S2 give
\[
  u_{h_1}=u_{h_2}+u_{e_2}+u_{e_3}=u_{h_3}+u_{e_1}=0.
\]
Thus the contact order constraint allows closed types to be connected to a wall
type at any pair of vertices with the same image through an edge of contact
order zero without changing the outgoing vector.

 \frac{r_c}{|\operatorname{Aut}(c)|}(j_c^{\operatorname{ev}})_*

 \varepsilon^*[p_1]\cap[\mathscr{M}(X,{\boldsymbol{\delta}})]^{{\operatorname{virt}}}

 \sum_{[c:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\to{\boldsymbol{\delta}}]\in\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}},p_1}}

The morphisms forgetting curve-class decorations are strict and \'etale, so the
local descriptions and dimension formulas for undecorated stacks from
\cite{ACGS2} apply also to the decorated stacks. For a realizable type $\eta$
occurring here, \cite[Prop.\,3.30]{ACGS2} says that $\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{X},
\boldsymbol\eta)$ is reduced and pure-dimensional of dimension $-2-\dim\eta$.
Retaining evaluation at the unique puncture adds $\dim X=4$ by
\cite[Def.\,5.14]{ACGS2}. Thus $\mathfrak{M}^{\operatorname{ev}}(\mathcal{X},\boldsymbol\eta)$ is reduced and
pure of dimension $2-\dim\eta$. The obstruction theory over the evaluation stack
has virtual relative dimension $c_1(\Theta_X)\cdot A+4(1-g-|\mathbf S|)=0$ by
\cite[(4.18)]{ACGS2}, since $X$ is logarithmically Calabi--Yau, $g=0$ and
$\mathbf S=\{L_{\mathrm{out}}\}$. Consequently, the evaluation stack and virtual
class for ${\boldsymbol{\delta}}$ have dimension one, and those for the refinements in
$\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}},p_1}$ have dimension zero.

Consider a geometric point of $\mathfrak{M}^{\operatorname{ev}}(\mathcal{X},{\boldsymbol{\delta}})$ with actual
decorated type $\boldsymbol\eta$. Its marking gives a contraction 
$\boldsymbol\eta\to{\boldsymbol{\delta}}$
and hence a localization homomorphism $\chi:Q_\eta\to Q_\delta\simeq \mathbb{N}$. The
marking ideal of \cite[Def.\,3.20]{ACGS2} imposes the target strata and the
nodes prescribed by $\delta$, together with the basic-monoid localization
condition. Since $\delta$ is realizable, \cite[Prop.\,3.24]{ACGS2} identifies
the union of the marking and puncturing ideals with
$I_\delta=\chi^{-1}(Q_\delta\setminus\{0\})$.

The complement $F=\chi^{-1}(0)$ is a facet of $Q_\eta$. By
\cite[Rem.\,3.27]{ACGS2}, a smooth local chart of the ${\boldsymbol{\delta}}$-marked stack has
monoidal factor
\[
 \operatorname{Spec}\bigl(\mathbb{C}[Q_\eta]/(\mathrm u^a\mid a\in I_\delta)\bigr)
 =\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[F].
\]
Here $\mathrm u^a$ denotes the monomial associated to $a$. The passage to the 
evaluation stack is strict and smooth, so the same
description applies there.

We compute the fiber equation at a point over $p_1$. The toric coordinate $z$ on
$P$ restricts to a local parameter on $S_1$ at $p_1$. By the definition of
$q_\eta$ and the computation of the outgoing contact order, the pull-back of
$\overline z$ to the outgoing puncture is $(q_\eta,0)$. The evaluation stack records a
lift of the underlying puncture evaluation to $X$, and consequently
$\varepsilon^*(z|_{S_1})$ is a unit times $\mathrm u^{q_\eta}$ in the above
chart.

Compatibility with the contraction gives $\chi(q_\eta)=q_\delta=0$, hence
$q_\eta\in F$. Moreover, $q_\eta\neq0$ at a point over $p_1$, since otherwise
the fiber equation would be a unit. Since $F$ is integral, multiplication by
$\mathrm u^{q_\eta}$ in $\mathbb{C}[F]$ is injective. The smooth local description
therefore shows that $\varepsilon^{-1}(p_1)$ is an effective Cartier divisor.

The support of $V(\mathrm u^{q_\eta})\subset\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[F]$ is the union of the
toric divisors defined by $(\mathrm u^a\mid a\in F\setminus H)$, where $H$ runs
over the facets of $F$ not containing $q_\eta$. The dual face $\tau=\eta\cap
H^\perp$ gives a factorization $\boldsymbol\eta\to{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\xrightarrow{c}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}$
with $\tau$ realizable, $\dim\tau-\dim\delta=1$ and $q_\tau\neq0$. Thus
$[c]\in\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}},p_1}$. By \cite[Prop.\,3.24]{ACGS2}, the union of the
$\tau$-marking and puncturing ideals induces the ideal $(\mathrm u^a\mid a\in
F\setminus H)$ in $\mathbb{C}[F]$. Hence these toric divisors are the local images of
the marking maps $j_c^{\operatorname{ev}}$, proving that the marking maps cover the irreducible
components of $\varepsilon^{-1}(p_1)$.

At a generic point of such a component, take $\eta=\tau$ and write
$F=Q_\tau\cap\delta^\perp=\mathbb{N} p$ with $p$ primitive. Then $q_\tau=r_cp$ by the
definition of $r_c$. The fiber equation is a unit times $(\mathrm u^p)^{r_c}$,
while the ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$-marked locus is defined by $(\mathrm u^p)$. Thus the component
has multiplicity $r_c$.

The marking map $j_c^{\operatorname{ev}}$ is finite of generic degree $|\operatorname{Aut}(c)|$ onto its
image, since the compatible ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$-markings of a ${\boldsymbol{\delta}}$-marked object of
actual type $\tau$ form an $\operatorname{Aut}(c)$-torsor, as in \cite[Rem.\,3.31 and the
proof of Thm.\,5.25]{ACGS2}. Dividing by this degree and summing the
multiplicities proves the first identity.

For the virtual identity, apply the same divisor computation with curve
decorations forgotten. For a fixed underlying contraction $c_0:\tau\to\delta$,
\cite[Prop.\,5.21]{ACGS2} identifies the pull-back of $\mathfrak{M}^{\operatorname{ev}}(\mathcal{X},\tau)$
along $\mathscr{M}(X,{\boldsymbol{\delta}})\to\mathfrak{M}^{\operatorname{ev}}(\mathcal{X},\delta)$ with the disjoint union of
$\mathscr{M}(X,{\boldsymbol{\tau}})$ over all compatible source decorations. The obstruction
theories agree under this pull-back. Virtual pull-back commutes with proper
push-forward and intersection with Cartier divisor classes, so it gives the sum
over these decorations. The group $\operatorname{Aut}(c_0)$ acts on the decorations, with
stabilizer $\operatorname{Aut}(c)$ for a decorated contraction $c$. Each orbit therefore has
$|\operatorname{Aut}(c_0)|/|\operatorname{Aut}(c)|$ elements. Grouping by these orbits turns the coefficient
$r_c/|\operatorname{Aut}(c_0)|$ into $r_c/|\operatorname{Aut}(c)|$, proving the second identity.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{Prop: wall degree comparison}
The moduli space $\mathscr{M}(X,{\boldsymbol{\delta}})$ is proper. For a general point $q\in S_1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: wall degree comparison}
 \frac1k\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathcal{T}_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}},p_1}}
 k_\tau W_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}
 =
 \frac{m_\delta}{|\operatorname{Aut}({\boldsymbol{\delta}})|}
 \deg\bigl(\varepsilon^*[q]\cap[\mathscr{M}(X,{\boldsymbol{\delta}})]^{{\operatorname{virt}}}\bigr).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Properness of the moduli space has already been proved in Lemma~\ref{Lem: support 
of maps with at most one puncture}.

Consider a term of nonzero degree in the second identity of \eqref{Eqn: wall
incidence cycles}, corresponding to $c:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\to{\boldsymbol{\delta}}$. The moduli space
$\mathscr{M}(X,{\boldsymbol{\tau}})$ is nonempty. The actual type of a map in it refines $\tau$ and
satisfies generalized balancing, which descends under contraction. Thus $\tau$
is balanced in the sense of \cite[Def.\,2.2]{CanScat}.

Let $v_{\mathrm{root}}$ be its root vertex and let $b_0$ be the generator of
$\delta_\mathbb{Z}\simeq\mathbb{N}$. Since $\delta_\mathbb{Z}^{\operatorname{gp}}\subset \tau_\mathbb{Z}^{\operatorname{gp}}$ is saturated,
we can extend $b_0$ to a basis $(b_0,b_1)$ and then $q_\tau(b_1)=r_c$. In the
lattice of ${\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(L_{\mathrm{out}})$, the restriction of $\overline z$ vanishes on
$h_{v_{\mathrm{root}}}(b_0)$ and the full outgoing vector $u_\tau$, and takes
value $r_c$ on $h_{v_{\mathrm{root}}}(b_1)$. Moreover,
$\rho(h_{v_{\mathrm{root}}}(b_0))=m_\delta>0$, whereas $\rho(u_\tau)=0$ and
$u_\tau\neq0$. These three vectors are therefore linearly independent. Hence the
outgoing-leg cone has three-dimensional image, and $\dim\tau=2$ makes $\tau$ a
wall type.

The support restriction of Lemma~\ref{Lem: support of maps with at most one
puncture} now puts its height-one tropicalization in $B_0$. Thus non-planar
refinements have zero degree in the virtual identity and can be ignored. By
Proposition~\ref{Prop: shape of wall types}, the root vertex $v_{\mathrm{root}}$
is either the last boundary vertex or has image on an interior edge. Since the
image of $v_{\mathrm{root}}$ specializes to $(1,0)$, the only possible interior
edge is the one joining the central vertex to $(1,0)$. But $\overline z$ vanishes on
the cone over this edge, contradicting $q_\tau\neq0$. Thus $v_{\mathrm{root}}$
is the last boundary vertex. Of the two boundary edges adjacent to $(1,0)$, only
the edge joining it to $(0,1)$ has positive $\overline z$ on its interior. Hence
${\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathcal{T}_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}},p_1}$.

The outgoing-leg image is now contained in the cone over $\Delta$, where we may
use the coordinates $(\overline v,\overline z,\rho)$ fixed above. Evaluation at the root
vertex sends $b_0$ to $m_\delta(1,0,1)$ and $b_1$ to $(a,r_c,b)$ for some
$a,b\in\mathbb{Z}$, since $q_\tau=h_{v_{\mathrm{root}}}^*\overline z$. Together with the
integral parameter along the outgoing leg, $b_0,b_1$ generate its integral
lattice. Their images $m_\delta(1,0,1)$, $(a,r_c,b)$ and $(-k,0,0)$ generate a
sublattice of $\mathbb{Z}^3$ of index $k\,m_\delta r_c$. Thus $k_\tau=k\,m_\delta r_c$.

It remains to group the contractions by their decorated source types. Fix
${\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathcal{T}_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}},p_1}$ and let ${\boldsymbol{\delta}}'$ be its corner contraction.
Every automorphism of ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$ preserves the corner face $\tau\cap q_\tau^\perp$
and hence induces an automorphism of ${\boldsymbol{\delta}}'$. The isomorphisms
${\boldsymbol{\delta}}'\to{\boldsymbol{\delta}}$ form an $\operatorname{Aut}({\boldsymbol{\delta}})$-torsor on which $\operatorname{Aut}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})$ acts.
Its orbits are the isomorphism classes of contractions $c:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\to{\boldsymbol{\delta}}$ over
the fixed target, and their stabilizers are $\operatorname{Aut}(c)$. Thus the sum of
$1/|\operatorname{Aut}(c)|$ over these contractions is $|\operatorname{Aut}({\boldsymbol{\delta}})|/|\operatorname{Aut}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})|$. The
integer $r_c$ is determined by $q_\tau$ and hence is independent of the chosen
isomorphism ${\boldsymbol{\delta}}'\to{\boldsymbol{\delta}}$.

\sloppy
Taking degrees in \eqref{Eqn: wall incidence cycles}, grouping by decorated
source types, and using $W_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}=\deg[\mathscr{M}(X,{\boldsymbol{\tau}})]^{\operatorname{virt}}/|\operatorname{Aut}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})|$ and
$k_\tau=k\,m_\delta r_c$ proves the formula with $q=p_1$. Finally, rational
equivalence of $p_1$ and $q$ on $S_1\simeq\mathbb{P}^1$ gives
$\varepsilon^*[p_1]=\varepsilon^*[q]$, proving the proposition.

\label{Thm: root virtual gluing}
The stack $\mathscr G_\delta$ is proper, and
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: root virtual splitting identity}
  (\phi_\delta)_*[\mathscr{M}(X,{\boldsymbol{\delta}})]^{\operatorname{virt}}
  =\mu_\delta\cdot\Delta^!\Bigl(\prod_{j=0}^a[\mathscr{M}(X,{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_j)]^{\operatorname{virt}}\Bigr)
  \qquad\text{in }A_n(\mathscr G_\delta)_\mathbb{Q}.
\end{equation}
For $a=0$, set $\mu_\delta=1$ and interpret $\Delta^!$ as the identity.

Glue the branches in order, and write $\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}}_j$ for the type
obtained after attaching the first $j$ branches. At the $j$-th step we obtain 
$\mathscr G_j$ as the fiber product and the finite representable morphism
\[
\phi_j: \mathscr{M}(X,\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_j)\longrightarrow \mathscr G_j.
\]
The tropical gluing homomorphism is
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Phi_j\colon
  \widetilde\delta_{j-1,\mathbb{Z}}^{\operatorname{gp}}\oplus\delta_{j,\mathbb{Z}}^{\operatorname{gp}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
  &\longrightarrow{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(E_j)_\mathbb{Z}^{\operatorname{gp}},\\
  (\xi,\xi_j,t)
  &\longmapsto h_{v_0}(\xi)-h_{v_j}(\xi_j)+tu_{E_j}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The two vertex evaluations have linearly independent images, since $u_{E_j}\neq
0$ implies that the endpoints lie on distinct rays. Thus $\Phi_j$ has finite
cokernel, and the gluing is tropically transverse. Put $\nu_j=|\operatorname{coker}\Phi_j|$.

Let $\Psi_j$ be \eqref{Eqn: root tropical gluing map} restricted to the first
$j$ branches. Since $\operatorname{ker}\Psi_{j-1}= \widetilde\delta_{j-1,\mathbb{Z}}^{\operatorname{gp}}$,
projection onto the first $j-1$ edge summands gives an exact sequence
\[
  0\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}\Phi_j
  \longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}\Psi_j
  \longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}\Psi_{j-1}
  \longrightarrow0.
\]
As $\operatorname{coker}\Psi_0=0$, this proves
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: successive root gluing multiplicity}
  \mu_\delta=\prod_{j=1}^a\nu_j.
\end{equation}

On the auxiliary opens of Lemma~\ref{Lem: single leg flatness at root},
\cite[Thm.\,5.5]{Gross} gives a dominant enhancement morphism of degree $\nu_j$
on the evaluation stacks. By \cite[Thm.\,5.1]{Gross}, this dominant morphism is
finite and representable, hence surjective. The enhancement morphism $\phi_j$ of
Deligne-Mumford stacks is its base change. Compatibility of the obstruction
theories, by \cite[Thm.\,5.19]{ACGS2} and \cite[Rem.\,5.2]{Gross}, gives
\[
  (\phi_j)_*[\mathscr{M}(X,\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}}_j)]^{\operatorname{virt}}

$\mathscr{F}_0(q)\times B\mu_k$, so $r_{k*}r_k^*$ is multiplication by $1/k$. The
projection formula proves the assertion.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{Thm: central signed polynomiality}
There is a polynomial $P_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_0}(s)\in\mathbb{Q}[s]$ of degree at most $2d_0-2$
such that
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: central signed polynomiality}
  P_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_0}(k)=(-1)^kR_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_0(k)}(q)
  \quad(k>0),\qquad
  P_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_0}(0)=d_0R_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_0}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Compose with $a_i\colon Y_i\to S_i$, forget all markings except $p$, and
stabilize to obtain
\[
  \nu\colon\mathscr{F}_0(q)\longrightarrow
    \overline{\mathscr{M}}_{0,1}(S_i,d_0)_q.
\]
The subscript denotes evaluation at $q$. Stabilization is an isomorphism near
$p$. Indeed, if the component carrying $p$ is nonconstant, its image lies in
$H_i$, since $f(p)=y_q$ and the $T$-fixed map avoids $P$. It therefore has
positive ruling degree. A maximal subtree contracted by the original map and
containing $p$ contains none of the markings labeled by $E_j$, and all adjacent
components have positive ruling degree, so it also survives. Thus
$\mathcal{L}_p\simeq\nu^*\mathcal{L}_p^{\mathrm{st}}$ equivariantly, with trivial
$T$-linearization on the right. Taking $c_1$ and noting that the codomain of
$\nu$ has dimension $2d_0-2$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: ruling cotangent bound}
  \psi_p^{2d_0-1}=0\qquad\text{on }\mathscr{F}_0(q).
\end{equation}

Summing over the retained fixed components, define
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: central polynomial coefficients}
  P_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_0}(s)
    =\sum_{\mathscr{F}_0(q)}
      \deg\left(\left(1+\frac{s\psi_p}{\hbar}\right)^{-1}
        \cap\gamma_0(q)\right).
\end{equation}
The sum is finite, and the inverse truncates at degree $2d_0-2$. Each
coefficient has equivariant degree zero, hence lies in $\mathbb{Q}$.
Proposition~\ref{Prop: outgoing contribution operator} and Lemma~\ref{Lem:
negative section vanishing} give the asserted values at positive integers.

For the constant term, the map $\mathrm{ft}\colon\mathscr{M}_0^+\to\mathscr{M}_0$ forgetting
the contact order zero marking $p$ and stabilizing is described in
\cite[Thm.\,5.5(2)--(3)]{associativity} as the saturation of the universal
punctured curve over $\mathscr{M}_0$, with compatible relative obstruction theories.
This saturation is an isomorphism away from the remaining marked sections. Since
the markings labeled by $E_j$ map to the fibers $F_j$, disjoint from $F_q$, the
image of $p$ after stabilization avoids these sections on ${\operatorname{ev}}_p^{-1}(F_q)$,
with ${\operatorname{ev}}_p$ as in \eqref{Eqn: ev_p}. Thus a neighborhood of this locus is
identified with an open substack of the universal curve over $\mathscr{M}_0$. The
usual proof of the divisor axiom therefore gives
\[
  \mathrm{ft}_*\bigl((\varepsilon^+)^*[q]\cap[\mathscr{M}_0^+]_T^{\operatorname{virt}}\bigr)
    =(F_q\cdot A)[\mathscr{M}_0]_T^{\operatorname{virt}}=d_0[\mathscr{M}_0]_T^{\operatorname{virt}}.
\]
Since ${\mathsf E}$ pulls back under $\mathrm{ft}$, localization and the projection
formula yield
\[
  P_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_0}(0) = \sum_{\mathscr{F}_0(q)}\deg\gamma_0(q)
    =\deg\bigl(((\varepsilon^+)^*[q]\cup{\mathsf E})
        \cap[\mathscr{M}_0^+]_T^{\operatorname{virt}}\bigr)
    =d_0\deg\bigl({\mathsf E}\cap[\mathscr{M}_0]_T^{\operatorname{virt}}\bigr)
     =d_0R_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_0}.
\]

\label{Cor: wall versus closed comparison}
For every $d>0$, there are polynomials $P_{d,i}(s)\in\mathbb{Q}[s]$, $i=1,2,3$, of
degree at most $2d-2$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: assembled localization comparison}
  P_{d,i}(k)=(-1)^kW_{d,-k{\boldsymbol{e}}_i}\quad(k>0),\qquad
  \sum_{i=1}^3P_{d,i}(0)=dN_d.
\end{equation}
Thus Assumption~\ref{Ass: wall polynomiality} holds, and the limit in
Proposition~\ref{Prop: asymptotic period} equals $-dN_d$.

In \eqref{Eqn: root numerical reduction}, a map parametrized by
$\varepsilon_0^{-1}(q)\subset\mathscr{M}(X,{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_0)$ with $d(\mathbf{A}(v_0))=0$ has image
in $F_q$. Since $F_q\cap F_j=\varnothing$ for all $j$, this forces $a=0$ and
hence $d=0$. Thus only $d_0>0$ contributes.

Remark~\ref{Rem: exhaustion of punctured central factors} gives a closed pair
$({\boldsymbol{\delta}},v_0)$ for every nonzero punctured contribution, preserving the
branches and tropical parameter cone. Thus ${\boldsymbol{\delta}}$ is rigid, and the
degree--contact relation and the intersection table in \S2 give total class
$d\beta$. Adding the outgoing leg and $kf_i$ reverses this operation, with
gluing data as in \eqref{Eqn: closed punctured gluing data}.

For each $i$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: directional localization polynomials}
  P_{d,i}(s)
    =\sum_{[({\boldsymbol{\delta}},v_0)]}
       \frac{m_\delta\mu_{\delta,v_0}}{|\operatorname{Aut}({\boldsymbol{\delta}},v_0)|}
       P_{{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_0}(s)\prod_{j=1}^a b_j,
\end{equation}

  N_d=-\frac1d\sum_{i=1}^3\sum_{k=1}^{2d-1}

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{Thm: explicit closed formula}
Under Assumption~\ref{Ass: explicit slab shape},
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: closed A and B formula}
  \sum_{d\geq1}dN_dt^d=\frac32A(t)^2+3B(t).
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: closed expanded formula}
  \sum_{d\geq1}dN_dt^d
  =\frac32\log^2(1+g(t))+\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}n\frac{(3n)!}{(n!)^3}
    \left(\sum_{j=n+1}^{3n-1}\frac1j\right)
    \frac{t^n}{(1+g(t))^{3n}}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
For $n\geq1$, put
\[
  c_n(s)=\frac{(-1)^n}{(n!)^2}\prod_{j=n+1}^{3n-1}(s+j).
\]
Expanding $\log f$ and extracting the monomials $t^dx^k$ gives, for $d>0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{Eqn: signed wall extraction}
  P_d(s)=-\frac1s[t^d](1+g(t))^{-s}
    \left(\frac1s+\sum_{n\geq1}c_n(s)
      \frac{t^n}{(1+g(t))^{3n}}\right).
\end{equation}
Initially this identity holds for positive integers $s=k$, using the coefficient
$kW_{d,-k{\boldsymbol{e}}_1}$ in $\log f$ and the sign $(-1)^k$. For each $d$, the right-hand
side is a rational function of $s$, so equality on all positive integers proves
\eqref{Eqn: signed wall extraction} identically.

Since $c_n(0)=-a_n$ and $c_n'(0)=-b_n$, the expression in parentheses is
$s^{-1}-A(t)-sB(t)+O(s^2)$. By \eqref{Eqn: normalization and A},
$(1+g(t))^{-s}=\exp(sA(t))$. Consequently,
\[
  -\frac{e^{sA(t)}}s
    \bigl(s^{-1}-A(t)-sB(t)+O(s^2)\bigr)
  =-s^{-2}+\frac12A(t)^2+B(t)+O(s).
\]
The term $-s^{-2}$ has degree zero in $t$. Taking $s=0$ after extracting $[t^d]$
therefore gives $P_d(0)=[t^d](A(t)^2/2+B(t))$. Now $3P_d(0)=dN_d$ proves
\eqref{Eqn: closed A and B formula}, and \eqref{Eqn: normalization and A} gives
the expanded form.
