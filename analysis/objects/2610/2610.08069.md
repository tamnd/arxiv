<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:kernel}
For every $M\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ and $u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$,
\begin{equation}
 L(M,u)=1-\|\Pi_{\ker M}u\|^2,
 \qquad
 C(M)\ge\dim\ker M,
 \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
and $I_d-\Pi_{\ker M}$ has the same residual for every $u$ at cost exactly $\dim\ker M$ (\cref{app:frontier}).
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:frontier}
Set $k=\min\{d,\lfloor b\rfloor\}$ and let $J\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\kappa/2)$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_d^*(b,\kappa)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&k=0,\\[2pt]
 (d-k)\displaystyle\mathbb E\frac1{d+2J},&1\le k\le d.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:frontier}
\end{equation}
For $k\ge1$, an attaining editor is $M=I_d-\Pi_K$, where $K=\operatorname{span}\{w\}\oplus K_\perp$, $w=\bar D/\|\bar D\|$, and $K_\perp$ is a uniformly sampled $(k-1)$-dimensional subspace of $w^\perp$.
It has constant risk over $u$ and cost $k$ on every fit.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:gap}
As $d\to\infty$ with fixed $\alpha\in(0,1)$, no level-$\alpha$ test has minimum power over directions exceeding $\alpha+o(1)$ when
$\kappa=o(\sqrt d)$; the radial test has power tending to one when
$\kappa/\sqrt d\to\infty$.
If simultaneously $\kappa/d\to0$, every fixed hard budget $b$ has minimax repair risk tending to one.
In that regime, achieving expected residual at most a fixed $\varepsilon<1$ requires deleting at least $(1-\varepsilon)d-o(d)$ directions.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:empirical-erasers}
In the calibration experiment \cref{eq:calibration}, suppose a fitted shared affine editor $h\mapsto\widehat M h+\widehat a$ satisfies
$\widehat M\bar D=0$ and $\dim\ker\widehat M=1$ almost surely.
For every true direction $u$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 L(\widehat M,u)&=1-\frac{(u^\top\bar D)^2}{\|\bar D\|^2},\\
 \mathbb E_u L(\widehat M,u)&=\mathcal R_d^*(1,\kappa)
 =(d-1)\mathbb E_{J\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\kappa/2)}\frac1{d+2J}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:empirical-eraser-risk}
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Almost surely $\bar D\ne0$, so the two conditions give $\ker\widehat M=\operatorname{span}\{\bar D\}$; \cref{eq:kernel} gives the pointwise identity, and the cost-one case of \cref{thm:frontier} gives its expectation.
The common affine offset does not change source separation.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:subspace-excess}
In \cref{eq:calibration}, fix $\kappa>0$ and an integer $1\le k\le d$, and let $K=\ker\widehat M$ satisfy $\dim K=k$ almost surely, where the fitted editor may use the full allowed calibration information and independent fitting randomness.
Write $\overline\mathbb E$ for expectation with $U$ uniform on $\mathbb S^{d-1}$ followed by calibration and fitting at direction $U$, and set $R=\|X\|$, $w=X/R$, and $\rho_K=\|(I_d-\Pi_K)w\|^2$, the fraction of the empirical direction outside $K$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \overline\mathbb E L(\widehat M,U)
 =\mathcal R_d^*(k,\kappa)
   +\overline\mathbb E\!\left[g_\kappa(R)\rho_K\right],\\
 g_\kappa(r)=1-d\,\lambda_\kappa(r)\ge0,
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:subspace-excess}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_\kappa(r)=(1-\beta(\sqrt\kappa r))/(d-1)$ and $\beta(\sqrt\kappa r)=\overline\mathbb E[(w^\top U)^2\mid X]$ is the posterior second moment along $w$ in \cref{eq:posterior}.
The identity also holds for every content law $Q$ allowed by \cref{cor:real-content-frontier}.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:selective}
Let $\tau_p$ satisfy $\overline F_{\chi^2_d(\kappa)}(\tau_p)=p$, and let $J\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\kappa/2)$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_d^{\mathrm{sel}}(p,\kappa)
 =\frac{d-1}{p}\mathbb E_J\frac{\overline F_{\chi^2_{d+2J}}(\tau_p)}{d+2J}.
 \label{eq:selective-frontier}
\end{equation}
It is attained by $w=X/\|X\|$ and $A=\{\|X\|^2\ge\tau_p\}$, which acts with probability exactly $p$ at every direction.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:anisotropy}
Let the contrasts be i.i.d.\ with mean $\theta u$ and covariance $\Gamma$ in coordinates where the within-source covariance is $I_d$, with eigenvalues $\gamma_{(1)}\ge\dots\ge\gamma_{(d)}>0$, and set $\bar\kappa=N\theta^2d/\operatorname{tr}\Gamma$.
\begin{compactitems}
\item[(i)] Gaussian or not, the editor of \cref{thm:frontier} satisfies
$\mathbb E_uL\le\frac{d-k}{d-1}\cdot\frac{d}{d+\bar\kappa}$ for every $u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$ and $1\le k\le d$.
\item[(ii)] If the contrasts are Gaussian, every editor within the hard budget $b$, with $k=\min\{d,\lfloor b\rfloor\}\ge1$, has
$\sup_u\mathbb E_uL\ge\max_{k<m\le d}\mathcal R_m^*(k,N\theta^2/\gamma_{(m)})$,
whether or not $\Gamma$ is known.
\end{compactitems}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor:two-level}
In whitened coordinates, the mean-direction erasers have mean residual for the population shift $\delta$ at most $d/(d+\bar\kappa)$, with
\begin{equation}
 \bar\kappa(P,n)=\frac{P\theta^2d}{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_b+\Sigma_w/n)}\le\frac{P\theta^2d}{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_b}\quad\text{for every }n.
 \label{eq:two-level}
\end{equation}
If a participant costs as much as $c$ pairs, then at a fixed total cost $P(c+n)$ the bound is smallest at $n^\star=\sqrt{c\,\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_w/\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_b}$ pairs per participant.
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- corollary-5: body_md -->
\label{cor:real-content-frontier}
Under \cref{eq:real-content-moments,eq:real-content-pair}, the source-neutral cost $\mathcal C_Q(M,a):=\mathbb E\|(M-I_d)V+a\|^2$ of a shared affine editor $h\mapsto Mh+a$ equals $\|M-I_d\|_F^2+\|a\|^2$, and its normalized covariance-adjusted squared mean separation $\mathcal L_Q(M,u):=(M\delta)^\top(MM^\top)^+(M\delta)/\theta^2$ equals $L(M,u)$ of \cref{eq:loss}.
For every hard budget $b\ge0$, the minimax expected loss over fitted editors $(\widehat M,\widehat a)$ satisfying $\mathcal C_Q(\widehat M,\widehat a)\le b$ on every fit and seed, with the worst case over $u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$, equals $\mathcal R_d^*(b,\kappa)$ with $\kappa=N\theta^2/s^2$.
The orthogonal editor of \cref{thm:frontier}, with zero offset, attains this value without knowledge of $Q$.
<!-- end corollary-5 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The fresh variation has $\mathbb E V=0$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(V)=\operatorname{Cov}(C)+\operatorname{Cov}(E)=I_d$, so expanding the quadratic distortion gives the cost identity; the edited source means differ by $M\delta$ with common covariance $MM^\top$, so \cref{eq:kernel} gives the loss identity; only the moments of $Q$ enter.

Next write each pair as its difference and midpoint,
$D_i=H_i^+-H_i^-=\delta+E_i^+-E_i^-$ and $A_i=(H_i^++H_i^-)/2=C_i+(E_i^++E_i^-)/2$.
The Gaussian noise difference and noise average have zero cross-covariance and hence are independent, and both are independent of $C_i$.
Thus $D_i\sim N(\delta,s^2I_d)$, and the midpoints are independent of the contrasts with a law that does not involve $u$; the same holds jointly across the $N$ pairs, and at fixed $s$ the centered contrast residuals are likewise independent of $\bar D$ with a $u$-free law.
Since the endpoints and $(D_i,A_i)_{i\le N}$ determine each other, all directional information in the full pairs is contained in $X$ of \cref{eq:calibration}; the remaining observations can serve only as fitting randomness, which \cref{thm:frontier} already allows.
Together with the two identities, this is the decision problem \cref{eq:minimax}, which gives the lower bound; an offset leaves the loss unchanged and adds nonnegative cost.
The attaining orthogonal editor depends only on $\bar D/\|\bar D\|$ and independent completion, so it has the same cost and risk here, proving equality.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:source-aware}
In the experiment \cref{eq:calibration}, let a correction subtract from one source an estimate $\hat\delta$ of the shift that may depend on the calibration sample, an independent seed, $\theta$ and $s$.
With $\rho(a)=\mathbb E[w^\top u\mid x]$ under the uniform prior of \cref{eq:posterior},
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_d^*(1,\kappa)\;\le\;
 \inf_{\hat\delta}\sup_{u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}}\mathbb E_u\frac{\|\delta-\hat\delta\|^2}{\theta^2}
 \;=\;1-\mathbb E\,\rho(\sqrt\kappa\|X\|)^2
 \;\le\;\frac{d}{d+\kappa},
 \label{eq:source-aware}
\end{equation}
and the two ends differ by at most $1/(d+\kappa)$.
The upper bound is the risk of $\hat\delta=\frac{\kappa}{d+\kappa}\bar D$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Subtraction leaves the within-source covariance unchanged, so the share of squared separation left is $\|\delta-\hat\delta\|^2/\theta^2=\|u-v\|^2$ with $v=\hat\delta/\theta$.
The loss is convex in $v$ and $X$ is sufficient for $u$ at known $s$, so we may take $v$ to be a function of $X$.
Under the uniform prior, rotations fixing $w$ leave the posterior density invariant, so $\mathbb E[u\mid x]=\rho(a)w$, and the posterior expected loss of any $v$ is at least $1-\rho(a)^2$, with equality at $v=\rho(a)w$.
This rule is equivariant under rotations, so its risk does not depend on $u$ and equals its Bayes risk $1-\mathbb E\rho(\sqrt\kappa\|X\|)^2$; a Bayes rule with constant risk is minimax, which gives the equality.
Jensen's inequality gives $\rho(a)^2\le\beta(a)$.
Since $1-\mathbb E\beta(\sqrt\kappa\|X\|)=\mathcal R_d^*(1,\kappa)$ is the Bayes risk of deleting $w$ in \cref{thm:frontier}, this proves the lower bound.
For the upper bound, $\bar D-\delta\sim N(0,s^2I_d/N)$ gives $\mathbb E\|\delta-c\bar D\|^2/\theta^2=(1-c)^2+c^2d/\kappa$, which equals $d/(d+\kappa)$ at $c=\kappa/(d+\kappa)$.
Finally, \cref{eq:bounds} gives $\mathcal R_d^*(1,\kappa)\ge(d-1)/(d+\kappa)$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- corollary-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:unknown}
For $N\ge2$, fix $\kappa>0$ and parameterize $\delta=s\sqrt{\kappa/N}\,u$.
Even with $s>0$ unknown,
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{\widehat M:C(\widehat M)\le b}
 \sup_{s>0,\,u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}}\mathbb E_{s,u}L(\widehat M,u)
 =\mathcal R_d^*(b,\kappa).
 \label{eq:adaptive}
\end{equation}
The editor in \cref{thm:frontier} attains this risk without knowing the shift strength or the noise scale.
<!-- end corollary-6 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Restrict the parameter space to any fixed $s=s_0$ and reveal $s_0$ to the procedure.
This can only lower the minimax risk, and the known-scale lower bound is \cref{eq:frontier}.
Conversely, the editor of \cref{thm:frontier} depends on the sample mean only through its direction, and its random completion uses no unknown parameter.
At each $s>0$, $\sqrt N\bar D/s\sim N(\sqrt\kappa u,I_d)$, so its risk equals \cref{eq:frontier} at every $(s,u)$.
This proves equality.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:average}
For known scale, fixed $\kappa>0$, and $0<b<d$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_d^{\rm av}(b,\kappa)=
 \begin{cases}
  1-b+b\mathcal R_d^{\rm sel}(b,\kappa),&0<b<1,\\
  \mathcal R_d^*(1,\kappa),&b=1,\\
  q\mathcal R_d^{\rm sel}(q,\kappa),\quad q=(d-b)/(d-1),&1<b<d.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:average-frontier}
\end{equation}
The endpoint risks are one at $b=0$ and zero at $b\ge d$.
For $b<1$, delete the mean direction on the largest-norm tail of probability $b$ and otherwise return the identity.
For $b>1$, delete the entire representation on the smallest-norm tail of probability $(b-1)/(d-1)$, and otherwise delete only the mean direction.
These rules have expected cost exactly $b$ at every $u$.

\label{thm:certificate}
Let $\alpha,\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, $c_\alpha=\chi^2_{d,1-\alpha}$, and act exactly when

At $\delta=0$, its probability of taking action is at most $\alpha$.
For nonzero $\delta$, its exact action probability is
$\overline F_{\chi^2_d(\kappa)}(c_\alpha/\varepsilon)$.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:envelope}
For $\alpha,\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, let
\begin{equation}
 t_{\rm mix}=\max\left\{c_\alpha,\ \max_{j\in\mathcal J}Q_{d+2j}(\alpha/\ell_j)\right\}.
 \label{eq:envelope}
\end{equation}
Act with the mean-direction editor when $\|X\|^2\ge t_{\rm mix}$.
For every nonzero $\delta$, the joint bad-action probability in \cref{eq:joint} is at most $\alpha$; null action is also at most $\alpha$.
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 t_{\rm mix}\le\max\{c_\alpha,\chi^2_{d-1,1-\alpha}/\varepsilon\}
 <c_\alpha/\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:strict-improvement}
\end{equation}
Thus its action probability is strictly larger than the confidence-ball rule's at every finite $\kappa\ge0$.

\label{thm:f-certificate}

<!-- corollary-9: body_md -->
\label{cor:phase}
Suppose $d\to\infty$, $\kappa/d\to c>0$, and $p\ge p_0>0$.
Then $\mathcal R_d^{\rm sel}(p,\kappa)\to1/(1+c)$ uniformly over such $p$.
For fixed $\alpha,\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, the known-scale and $F$ rules, both confidence-ball and finite-mixture versions, have action probabilities tending to one when $c>\varepsilon^{-1}-1$ and to zero when $c<\varepsilon^{-1}-1$.
The $F$ result permits any sequence $N\ge2$, including fixed $N$.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
(i) Let $w=\bar D/\|\bar D\|$, with $(u^\top w)^2$ set to zero when $\bar D=0$.
By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, $(\mathbb E|u^\top\bar D|)^2\le\mathbb E[(u^\top w)^2]\,\mathbb E\|\bar D\|^2$.
Since $\mathbb E|u^\top\bar D|\ge|\mathbb E u^\top\bar D|=\theta$ and $\mathbb E\|\bar D\|^2=\theta^2+\operatorname{tr}\Gamma/N$, we get $\mathbb E(u^\top w)^2\ge\bar\kappa/(d+\bar\kappa)$.
Given $w$, uniform completion in $w^\perp$ multiplies the residual $1-(u^\top w)^2$ by $(d-k)/(d-1)$ in expectation, as in \cref{app:frontier}.
Only the mean and covariance of $\bar D$ were used.

(ii) By \cref{eq:kernel}, it suffices to consider rules that choose a deleted subspace $K$ of dimension at most $k$.
Fix $m>k$ and let $S_m$ be spanned by eigenvectors of the $m$ largest eigenvalues of $\Gamma$; the maximum risk over $\mathbb S^{d-1}$ is at least that over unit $u\in S_m$.
For such $u$, the coordinates of the contrasts outside $S_m$ are Gaussian, independent of those inside, and have a law free of $u$, so they act only as fitting randomness.
For $u\in S_m$ the captured fraction is $u^\top\Pi_Ku=u^\top Au$ with $A=\Pi_{S_m}\Pi_K\Pi_{S_m}$, which satisfies $0\preceq A\preceq\Pi_{S_m}$ and $\operatorname{tr} A\le k$.
Such an $A$ is a convex combination of orthogonal projectors of rank at most $k$ inside $S_m$, each dominated by a rank-$k$ projector inside $S_m$, so a randomized rank-$k$ deletion inside $S_m$ has no larger loss at every $u\in S_m$.
Inside $S_m$ the noise covariance of $\bar D$ dominates $\gamma_{(m)}I_d/N$, so the observation can be simulated from one with noise covariance exactly $\gamma_{(m)}I_d/N$ by adding independent Gaussian noise; the restricted problem is therefore no easier than the isotropic $m$-dimensional problem, whose minimax risk is $\mathcal R_m^*(k,N\theta^2/\gamma_{(m)})$ by \cref{thm:frontier}.
Revealing $\Gamma$ cannot increase the minimax risk, so the bound also holds when $\Gamma$ is unknown.
