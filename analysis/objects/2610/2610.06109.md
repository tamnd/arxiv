By~\Thm{thm:siblings} 
(ii),  we have
$\dim \fg_{2\gamma}-\dim \fg_{\gamma}=\dim\fg'_{2\gamma}$
and  $\gamma\in\Delta^{'im}$.
By~\cite{Kbook}, Chapter V, $\gamma\in\Delta^{'im}$ implies $2\gamma\in\Delta^{'im}$, so $\dim \fg'_{2\gamma}>0$.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that all exponents
$m_{\alpha}$,$m_{\alpha+}$, $m_{\alpha-}$ are non-zero 
for all relevant $\alpha\in X$. 

Denote the left-hand side of the above formula by $P(X,M)$, where $M$ stands for the set of exponents
$$
\{m_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in X_0}
\cup
\{m_{\alpha\pm}\}_{\alpha\in X_1}.
$$

We have to verify that $P(X,M)=1$ if $M$ is non-empty.
Suppose, to the contrary, that  $P(X,M)+1$ and $M$ is non-empty.

 For  each $\nu=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in \Sigma} n_{\gamma}\gamma\in Q^+$
we set $\htt \nu:=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in \Sigma} n_{\gamma}$
and introduce
$$X^{\leq i}:=\{\gamma\in X|\ 
\htt \gamma\leq i\};\ \ \ \ s:=\min_{\alpha\in X}\htt \alpha.$$
For all $i$ the sets $X^{\leq i}$ are finite
(since the sets
$\{\gamma\in Q^+|\ \htt \gamma=j\}$ are finite).

Let $\beta\in X$ be such that $\htt \beta=s$.
If $\beta\in X_0$, then the coefficient 
 of $e^{-\beta}$ in the expansion of $P(X;M)$ is equal to $-m_{\beta}$, so
 $m_{\beta}=0$, a contradiction. This gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Xs}
X^{\leq s}\subset X_1.\end{equation}

If $\beta\in X_1$, then the coefficient of $e^{-\beta}$ in the expansion of $P(X;M)$
is $m_{\beta_+}-m_{\beta-}$, so $m_{\beta_+}=m_{\beta_-}$. Then
$$P(X;M)=P(X';M'),\  \text{ 
where }X'_0=X_0\cup\{2\beta\},\ \ 
X'_1=X_1\setminus \{\beta\}$$ 
and 
$$m'_{2\beta}=m_{\beta+},
\ m'_{\alpha}=m_{\alpha},\ \ m'_{\alpha\pm}=m_{\alpha \pm}\ 
\text{ for all }\alpha\in X'\cap X.$$


Repeating the same procedure several times,
we obtain 
$$P(X,M)=P(Y,N),\ \text{ where
}\  Y_0=X_0\cup\{2\alpha|\ 
\alpha\in X_1\cap X^{\leq 2s}\},\  Y_1= X_1\setminus X^{\leq 2s}.$$
Letting $q:=\min_{\alpha\in Y}\htt \alpha$ we have
 $q\leq 2s$ and $Y^{\leq q}\subset Y_0$, which 
contradicts~(\ref{eq:Xs}).

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We sketch the proof, omitting some technical details.

We denote by $\vac$ the highest weight vector of $M(\lambda)$.
The {\em spine} of $\fg$ is the set $Sp$
of all bases of $\Delta$, obtained 
from $\Sigma$ by chains of  reflexions
consisting of isotropic roots.
By~\cite{GHS}, Prop. 4.3.13, $W$ is a Coxeter group 
generated by $\{s_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\pi}$, where
 $$\pi:=\bigl(\cup_{\Sigma'\in Sp} \Sigma'\bigr)
 \setminus \Delta^{iso};$$
 this set is called the set of principal roots.

 Recall that $\alpha$ is a real root.
By~\cite{GHS}, Cor. 5.2.9, there exists $w\in W$
and $\Sigma'\inSp$ such that
 $w\alpha\in \Sigma'$.
for some $w\in W$. We choose $w$ to 
be of the minimal length and prove the assertions by induction on this length.

Consider the case when $w=1$, that is $\alpha\in\Sigma'$
for some $\Sigma'\inSp$. We view $Sp$ as a subgraph of the skeleton 
with the vertices corresponding to bases and the  edges marked by isotropic reflexions.  Choose a base $\Sigma'\inSp$ containing $\alpha$ and having minimal distance from $\Sigma$,
and let 
$$\Sigma=\Sigma_0\overset{\beta_1}{\to}\Sigma_{1}\overset{\beta_2}{\to}\ldots
\overset{\beta_s}{\to}\Sigma_{s}=\Sigma'$$
be a  shortest path 
from $\Sigma$ to $\Sigma'$.
If $\Sigma=\Sigma'$, then $\alpha=\alpha_i\in\Sigma$, and $v=f_i^m \vac$
is the highest weight vector, so the assertions hold. 

Let $\Sigma'\not=\Sigma$.
Recall that $\beta_i\in\Sigma_{i-1}$, so $\alpha\not\in\{\beta_i\}_{i=1}^s$.
Moreover,
$$\Delta^{+}(\Sigma_j)=\Delta^+\setminus\{\beta_i\}_{i=1}^j\cup\{-\beta_i\}_{i=1}^j.$$
Since $\alpha\in\Delta^+$ and the chosen path is shortest, we have $\alpha\in\Delta^+(\Sigma_i)$ for $i=1,\ldots,s$. Hence 
$\beta_i\not=\pm\alpha$ and
the assumption on $\lambda$ gives $$\langle\lambda+\rho,\beta_i^{\vee}\rangle\not=0\  \text{
for }i=1,\ldots,s.$$ 
Therefore,
$$
M(\lambda)=M_{\Sigma'}(\lambda'),\ \text{ for }\lambda'+\rho'=\lambda+\rho,
$$
where  $M_{\Sigma'}(\lambda')$ denotes the Verma module of highest weight $\lambda'$ with respect to the triangular decomposition determined by $\Sigma'$.
Since $\alpha\in\Sigma'$,
$M_{\Sigma'}(\lambda')$ contains a $\Sigma'$-primitive vector 
$v'$ of weight $\lambda'-m\alpha$, which is unique up to a multiplication by non-zero scalar. Let $N$ be the submodule
generated by $v'$.

If $\alpha$ is anisotropic, then $N=M_{\Sigma'}(\lambda'-m\alpha)$.
One has
$$\langle \lambda'-m\alpha+\rho',\beta_i^{\vee}\rangle=
\langle \lambda-m\alpha+\rho,\beta_i^{\vee}\rangle=
\langle s_{\alpha}(\lambda+\rho),\beta_i^{\vee}\rangle=
\langle \lambda+\rho,s_{\alpha}\beta_i^{\vee}\rangle
\not=0,$$
since for $\beta:=s_{\alpha}\beta_i\in\Delta^{iso}$
one has $s_{\alpha}\beta_i^{\vee}\in \mathbb{C}^*\beta^{\vee}$, 
by~\ref{reflections}, and $\langle \lambda+\rho,\beta^{\vee}\rangle\not=0$ by the assumptions
(since $H_{1,\beta}(\lambda)\not=0$).

If $\alpha$ is isotropic, then
$$(1+e^{-\alpha})\ch N=\ch M_{\Sigma'}(\lambda'-\alpha)=
e^{-\alpha}
\ch M_{\Sigma'}(\lambda')=e^{-\alpha}
\ch M(\lambda)=\ch M(\lambda-\alpha)$$
as required. This completes the proof for $w=1$.


Now consider the case $l(w)>0$. Then $w=w_1s_{\gamma}$
for $\gamma\in\pi$ and $w_1\in W$ such that $l(w_1)<l(w)$.
Choose a base $\Sigma'\inSp$ containing $\gamma$ and having minimal distance from $\Sigma$, and let 
$$\Sigma=\Sigma_0\overset{\beta_1}{\to}\Sigma_{1}\overset{\beta_2}{\to}\ldots
\overset{\beta_s}{\to}\Sigma_{s}=\Sigma'$$
be a  shortest path 
between $\Sigma$ and $\Sigma'$. Since $l(w)>1$, $\alpha\not\in\Sigma''$
for all $\Sigma''\in Sp$. In particular,
$\beta_i\not=\pm\alpha$ for all $i=1,\ldots,s$ and $\gamma\not=\pm\alpha$.
As above, this gives, $M(\lambda)=M_{\Sigma'}(\lambda')$
where $\lambda+\rho=\lambda'+\rho'$. Moreover, the assumptions
on $\lambda$ imply
$\langle \lambda+\rho,\gamma^{\vee}\rangle\not\in\mathbb{Z}\langle \gamma^{\vee},\gamma\rangle$.
The induction step now follows from the Enright functor arguments of~\cite{GSsnow}, Theorem 3.2 and~\cite{GK25}, Section 5.7.
