Throughout this paper, vectors are denoted with bold 
lowercase letters ($\bm{a}$) while matrices are denoted by uppercase letters 
($\mathbf{A}$). Given two matrices $\mathbf{A}$ and 
$\mathbf{B}$, $\mathbf{A}\!:\!\mathbf{B}$ denotes the Frobenius inner product. 
Given two vectors $\bm{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_1}$ and $\bm{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_2}$, we define 
$\bm{u} \oplus \bm{v} := [u_i+v_j]_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_1 \times n_2}$. Given two 
vectors $\bm{a}$ and $\bm{b}$ of same dimension, we let $\bm{a} \odot \bm{b}$ 
denote their componentwise product, and $\bm{a} \oslash \bm{b}$ denote their 
componentwise division (assuming $\bm{b}>0$ in the latter case). 
The convex conjugate of a convex function $\psi: \mathbb{R}^m \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ will be 
denoted by $\psi^*$, where $\psi^*(\bm{x})=
\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}} \langle \bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle - \psi(\bm{y})$ for 
every $\bm{x} \in \mathbb{R}^m$. When considering bipartite graphs of the form 
$(V \cup V',E)$, we will write non-oriented edges as $(i,j)$, where 
$i$ denotes an index in the first part of the graph and $j$ denotes a vertex 
from its second part.
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- notation-1: body_md -->
For any tree subgraph $\mathscr{T}$ of the bipartite graph $(V\cup V',E)$, 
we let $\mathbf{r}[\mathscr{T}]$ denote its root node (which
can of course be arbitrary), $\mathbf{s}[\mathscr{T}] = 
\{i\in V|~i\in \mathscr{T}\}$ denote its source nodes and 
$\mathbf{t}[\mathscr{T}] := \{j\in V'|~j\in \mathscr{T}\}$ denote its target nodes. The 
number of nodes of $\mathscr{T}$ is $\# \mathscr{T} =
\# \mathbf{s}[\mathscr{T}] + \# \mathbf{t}[\mathscr{T}]$. 
Given $v \in \mathscr{T}$, $\mathbf{c}[v]$ denotes the set of children nodes for $v$ 
in $\mathscr{T}$, while $\mathbf{p}[v]$ denotes the parent node of 
$v \neq \mathbf{r}[\mathscr{T}]$. Finally, given two nodes $u$ and $v$ in $\mathscr{T}$, 
we write $u \preccurlyeq v$ (resp. $u \succcurlyeq v$) if $u$ belongs to the 
ancestor nodes (resp. successor nodes) of $v$.
<!-- end notation-1 -->

\label{prop:comp-flow}
    Given a tree $\mathscr{T}$ on the bipartite graph $(V\cup V',\mathcal E)$ and dual 
    potentials $(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})$ on $\mathscr{T}$, 
    the flow $\mathbf{P}$ defined as:
    {\footnotesize
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:flow-from-potentials}
        \begin{multlined}
                                                             
            \forall u\in\mathscr{T}\backslash\mathbf{r}[\mathscr{T}],~
            \mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{p}[u]\to u}
            :=
            \begin{cases}
                \displaystyle 
                \sum_{j\in\mathbf{t}[\mathscr{T}]:j\succcurlyeq u} (\nabla (\psi_2^*)(-\mathbf{g}))_j - 
                \sum_{i\in\mathbf{s}[\mathscr{T}]:i\succcurlyeq u} (\nabla (\psi_1^*)(-\mathbf{f}))_i

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:nbopsuniontrees}
    Algorithm \ref{alg:optimizing-union} converges to an optimal solution 
    $(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})$ in $\mathcal{O}(\#\mathscr{T}^- + \#\mathscr{T}^+)$
    iterations.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Suppose that we reach the $k$th iteration of 
	Algorithm~\ref{alg:optimizing-union}. Two cases must be considered.
	Suppose first that $\delta = \delta_\mathrm{max} \le \min\{\delta_\mathrm{eff}^+, 
	\delta_\mathrm{eff}^-\} $. In that case, there is no edge removal, 
	and the update formulas guarantee $\Delta^{(k+1)}=0$, so that the 
	method stops. Moreover, since the updates preserve first-order 
	and complementarity conditions, the triplet 
	$(\mathbf{P}^{(k+1)},\mathbf{f}^{(k+1)},\mathbf{g}^{(k+1)})$ must be optimal on 
	$\mathscr{T}^+\cup (i_0,j_0)\cup\mathscr{T}^-$, hence the result holds.
	
	Suppose now that $\delta= \min\{\delta_\mathrm{eff}^+, 
	\delta_\mathrm{eff}^-\} < \delta_\mathrm{max}$. Then this step will result in 
	removing one edge from $\mathscr{T}^+$ in case
	$\delta= \delta_\mathrm{eff}^+$ and/or one
	edge from $\mathscr{T}^-$ if
	$\delta= \delta_\mathrm{eff}^-$.
	We obtain a new triplet $(\mathbf{P}^{(k+1)},\mathbf{f}^{(k+1)},\mathbf{g}^{(k+1)})$                                  
	with 
	$\Delta^{(k+1)} < \Delta^{(k)}$. Let $(u,v)$ denote an edge 
	removed during the iteration. If this edge is removed 
	from $\mathscr{T}^-$, then $u \in \mathbf{s}[\mathscr{T}^-]$ as shown in 
	Section~\ref{subsec:nsuot} while $v$ is removed from
	 $\mathbf{t}[\mathscr{T}^-]$. Moreover, $\mathbf{f}_u^{(k+1)}+\mathbf{g}_v^{(k+1)}=\mathbf{C}_{u,v}$ 
	since complementarity conditions are preserved, while any subsequent 
	iteration will only reduce $\mathbf{f}_u$ while keeping $\mathbf{g}_v$ at the same 
	value. Thus, dual feasibility is guaranteed on $(u,v)$ for all 
	subsequent iterations, while
	one easily checks that conditions~\eqref{eq:dualfeasuot} are satisfied in the old part of $\mathscr{T}^-$, attached
	to $v$, which has been pruned after the update and
	will not be touched again in the remaining steps. A similar reasoning applies when $(u,v)$ is 
	an edge removed from $\mathscr{T}^+$.

	Overall, for every iteration of Algorithm~\ref{alg:optimizing-union}, 
	either the method terminates or it removes one edge in the 
	worst case (possibly more edges and vertices) while preserving 
	dual feasibility. Since the number of edges that can be removed 
	is at most $(\# \mathscr{T}^- + \# \mathscr{T}^+)$, it follows that the 
	method cannot perform more than $(\# \mathscr{T}^- + \# \mathscr{T}^+)$ 
	iterations. Moreover, if the maximum of iterations is reached, 
	then the last remaining edge must be $(i_0,j_0)$. The updating 
	formula then guarantees that we fall into the first case above, 
	and optimality follows.

We begin by proving~\eqref{eq:gammapm-fullkl}. Using $z_\pm:=\exp(\tau\gamma_\pm)$, 
	the relation \eqref{eq:gamma-pm-kl-impl} can be rewritten as
    \begin{equation*}
        \delta = (\sigma_\pm+\mathbf{P}_{i_0,j_0})z_\pm-\frac{\sigma_\pm}{z_\pm}-\mathbf{P}_{i_0,j_0}.
    \end{equation*}
    Multiplying by $z$ and rearranging the terms gives the quadratic 
    polynomial:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:gammapm-fullkl:auxquad}
        (\sigma_\pm+\mathbf{P}_{i_0,j_0})z_\pm^2 
        - (\delta+\mathbf{P}_{i_0,j_0})z_\pm 
        - \sigma_\pm 
        = 0.
    \end{equation}
    Since $z_\pm\geq  0$, we can compute $z_{\pm}$ by considering the positive 
    root of~\eqref{eq:gammapm-fullkl:auxquad}, which in turns yields the 
    formulas~\eqref{eq:gammapm-fullkl}.
    
    We now turn to proving~\eqref{eq:deltamax-fullkl}. For convenience, define
    $S_\pm = 4\sigma_\pm\widetilde \sigma_\pm$, 
    $K := 4\widetilde \sigma_+\widetilde \sigma_- \exp(\tau \Delta)$, and
    $X :=\delta_\mathrm{max}+\mathbf{P}_{i_0,j_0}\geq0 $.

    \label{eq:deltamax-fullkl-sqrteq}
        (X+\sqrt{S_++X^2})(X+\sqrt{S_-+X^2}) = K, 
    \end{equation}
	The solution of~\eqref{eq:deltamax-fullkl-sqrteq} is
    \begin{equation*}
        X = \frac{
        K^2-S_+S_-
        }{2\sqrt{K(K^2+K(S_++S_-)+S_+S_-)}}.
    \end{equation*}
    Replacing $K,S_+,S_-$ by their expressions, we have
    \begin{align*}
        K^2-S_+S_- 
        &= 16\widetilde \sigma_+\widetilde \sigma_-
        \left(e^{2\tau\Delta}\widetilde \sigma_+\widetilde \sigma_- 
        - \sigma_+\sigma_-\right),\\
        K(K^2+K(S_++S_-)+S_+S_-) 
        &= 64e^{\tau\Delta}\widetilde{\sigma}_+^2\widetilde{\sigma}_-^2
        \left(1+e^{\tau\Delta}(\sigma_+\widetilde{\sigma}_+
        +\sigma_-\widetilde{\sigma}_-)
        +e^{2\tau\Delta}\widetilde{\sigma}_+\widetilde{\sigma}_-\right),
    \end{align*}
   	hence~\eqref{eq:deltamax-fullkl} holds.

		\bm{\alpha}_j^{2}\exp(\tau(-\mathbf{g}_j-\gamma_-))
		-\sum_{i\in\mathbf{s}[\mathscr{T}]:i\succcurlyeq u}

		= 0.
	\end{equation*}

		+ \exp(-\tau\gamma_-)\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{p}[u]\to u}

   from which we obtain

     \begin{equation}
     \begin{cases}
         \mathbf{P}'\mathbbm{1}_{n} = \bm{\alpha}^{\bm{1}}\exp(-\tau^1\mathbf{f}')
         \\
         \mathbf{P}'\mathbbm{1}_{n} = \bm{\alpha}^{\bm{2}}\exp(-\tau^2\mathbf{g}')
     \end{cases}
     \end{equation}
     where $(\mathbf{f}',\mathbf{g}') := (\mathbf{f}^*+(\mu/\tau^1)\mathbbm{1}_{n_1},\mathbf{g}^*+(\mu/\tau^2)\mathbbm{1}_{n_2})$. By dual-feasibility and complementarity of $(\mathbf{P}^*,\mathbf{f}^*,\mathbf{g}^*)$, we have $\mathbf{f}'\oplus\mathbf{g}'\leq \mathbf{C}+\bm{\zeta} $

    \min_{(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n_1+n_2}

    \min_{

    + \frac{1}{\tau^1}\operatorname{\mathsf{KL}}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\alpha}^{\bm{1}})
    + \frac{1}{\tau^2}\operatorname{\mathsf{KL}}(\bm{\nu},\bm{\alpha}^{\bm{2}})
    \end{equation}
and $\mu(1/\tau^1+1/\tau^2)$ is then the Lagrange multiplier for

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By definition of the variables $(\mathbf{P}^\Delta,\mathbf{f}^\Delta,\mathbf{g}^\Delta)$,

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:energ-delta2}
	Let Assumption~\ref{assum:strong-convex} for problem~\eqref{eq:uot}
    Let $\mathbf{P}$ and $\widetilde\mathbf{P}$ respectively denote 
    the input and output of Algorithm \ref{alg:optimizing-union}, and 
    let $\Delta$ be the initial dual violation upon calling 
    Algorithm~\ref{alg:optimizing-union}. Then,
    \begin{equation*}
	\label{eq:energ-delta2}
        \mathcal{E}(\mathbf{P})-\mathcal{E}(\widetilde\mathbf{P}) \ge \beta\Delta^2,
    \end{equation*}
    where 
    $\beta:=\frac{1}{2\left(\frac{\mu_1}{L_1^2}+\frac{\mu_2}{L_2^2}\right)}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
It suffices to consider the restriction of both transport plans to 
	the tree $\widetilde\mathscr{T}=\mathscr{T}^+\cup(i_0,j_0)\cup\mathscr{T}^-$, as this 
	represents the only edges on which the plans may differ. For simplicity, 
	we overload the notation throughout the proof and use $\mathbf{P}$, 
	$\widetilde\mathbf{P}$, $\mathbf{f}$ and $\mathbf{g}$ to denote the restriction of 
	these variables to $\widetilde\mathscr{T}$. By strong convexity of the 
	relaxation function, we have
	\begin{align*}
    \label{eq:diff-succ-energ}
        \mathcal{E}(\mathbf{P})-\mathcal{E}(\widetilde\mathbf{P})
        &\ge
        \langle \nabla \mathcal{E}(\widetilde\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{P}-\widetilde\mathbf{P}\rangle
        +\frac{\mu_1}{2}\lVert (\mathbf{P} 
        -\widetilde\mathbf{P})\mathbbm{1}_{n_2}\rVert^2
        +\frac{\mu_2}{2}\lVert (\mathbf{P} 
        -\widetilde\mathbf{P})^\top\mathbbm{1}_{n_1}\rVert^2 \\
        &\ge 
        \frac{\mu_1}{2}\lVert (\mathbf{P} 
        -\widetilde\mathbf{P})\mathbbm{1}_{n_2}\rVert^2
        +\frac{\mu_2}{2}\lVert (\mathbf{P} 
        -\widetilde\mathbf{P})^\top\mathbbm{1}_{n_1}\rVert^2,
	\end{align*}
	where the second inequality follows from optimality of $\widetilde\mathbf{P}$ 
	on $\widetilde\mathscr{T}$, which ensures $\langle \nabla \mathcal{E}(\widetilde\mathbf{P}),
	\mathbf{P}-\widetilde\mathbf{P}\rangle \ge 0$ (on $\widetilde\mathscr{T}$).
  
	Using the first-order condition \eqref{eq:dualfeasuot} as well as 
	Lipschitz continuity of $\nabla \psi_1^*$ and $\nabla \psi_2^*$ gives
	\begin{eqnarray}
	\label{eq:auxenerg-delta2} 
        &
        &\frac{\mu_1}{2}\lVert (\mathbf{P} -\widetilde\mathbf{P})\mathbbm{1}_{n_1}\rVert^2
        +\frac{\mu_2}{2}\lVert (\mathbf{P} 
        -\widetilde\mathbf{P})^\top\mathbbm{1}_{n_2}\rVert^2
        \nonumber \\
        &=
        &\frac{\mu_1}{2} 
        \lVert \nabla \psi_1^*(-\mathbf{f})-\nabla \psi_1^*(-\widetilde\mathbf{f})\rVert_2^2
        +\frac{\mu_2}{2} \lVert \nabla \psi_2^*(-\mathbf{g})
        -\nabla \psi_2^*(-\widetilde\mathbf{g})\rVert_2^2 
        \nonumber \\
        &\ge 
        &\frac{\mu_1}{2L_1^2}\lVert  \mathbf{f}-\widetilde\mathbf{f}\rVert_2^2
        +\frac{\mu_2}{2L_2^2} \lVert  \mathbf{g}-\widetilde\mathbf{g}\rVert_2^2 
        \nonumber \\
        &\ge 
        &\frac{\mu_1}{2L_1^2} \left(  \mathbf{f}_{i_0}-\widetilde\mathbf{f}_{i_0}\right)^2
        +\frac{\mu_2}{2L_2^2} \left(  \mathbf{g}_{j_0}-\widetilde\mathbf{g}_{j_0}\right)^2.
    \end{eqnarray}
    Recall that $\mathbf{f}_{i_0}+\mathbf{g}_{j_0}=\mathbf{C}_{{i_0},{j_0}} + \Delta$, while 
    Algorithm~\ref{alg:optimizing-union} computes 
    $(\widetilde\mathbf{f}_{i_0},\widetilde\mathbf{g}_{j_0})$ such that
	\begin{equation*}
    	\begin{cases}
        \widetilde\mathbf{f}_{i_0}+\widetilde\mathbf{g}_{j_0}
        =\mathbf{C}_{{i_0},{j_0}} 
        \quad\text{(final dual complementarity)},\\
        \mathbf{f}_{i_0}-\widetilde\mathbf{f}_{i_0}\geq 0
        ~\text{and}~
        \mathbf{g}_{j_0}-\widetilde\mathbf{g}_{j_0}\geq 0
        \quad \text{(decrease of dual variables)}.
    	\end{cases}
	\end{equation*}
	It follows that the last right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:auxenerg-delta2} can 
	bounded below by the minimum value
	\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:min-i0j0}
    	\min_{\substack{a,b\geq 0\\ a+b=\Delta}} 
    	\frac{\mu_1}{2L_1^2}a^2+\frac{\mu_2}{2L_2^2}b^2 
    	= 
    	\frac{1}{2\left(\frac{L_1^2}{\mu_1}+\frac{L_2^2}{\mu_2}\right)}\Delta^2,
	\end{equation}
	proving the result.

	$\{\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell}}\}$, where $\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell}}$ is the plan 
	obtained from $\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell-1}}$ by considering dual violations 
	less than $\Delta_{\ell-1}$ but larger than $\Delta_{\ell}$. 
    For any $\ell \ge 0$, Proposition~\ref{prop:delta-precision} together 
    with Lemma~\ref{lem:energ-delta2} guarantee
    \begin{equation*}
        \mathcal{E}(\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell}})-\mathcal{E}^* 
        \le 
        \Delta_{\ell}\lVert \mathbf{P}^*\rVert_1.
        \qquad \mbox{and} \qquad
        \mathcal{E}(\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell}})-\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell+1}})
        \ge 
        \beta n_\ell \Delta_{\ell}^2.
    \end{equation*}
    Therefore, letting $n_{\ell}$ the number of iterations performed to go 
    from $\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell}}$ to $\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell+1}}$, we have
    \begin{equation*}
        n_{\ell}
        \le \frac{\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell}})-\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell+1}})}
        {\beta\Delta_\ell^2}
        \le \frac{\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell}})-\mathcal{E}_{*}}{\beta\Delta_{\ell}^2}
        \le \frac{\lVert \mathbf{P}^*\rVert_1}{\beta\Delta_{\ell}} 
        = \frac{\lVert \mathbf{P}^*\rVert_1 2^{\ell}}{\beta\Delta}.
    \end{equation*}
    This reasoning shows that an $\varepsilon$-solution is reached after 
    considering at most ${\ell}_\varepsilon 
    = \lceil \ln(\lVert \mathbf{P}^*\rVert_1 \Delta/\varepsilon)/\ln 2\rceil$ 
    terms of the form $\mathbf{P}_{\Delta_{\ell}}$. It follows that the total 
    number of iterations performed by Algorithm~\ref{alg:adapted-ns} is 
    \begin{equation*}
        \sum_{\ell=0}^{\ell_\varepsilon} n_\ell 
        \le 
        \frac{\lVert \mathbf{P}^*\rVert_1}{\beta\Delta_0}
        \left(2^{\ell_\varepsilon+1}-1\right)
        \le
        \frac{4\lVert \mathbf{P}^*\rVert_1^2}{\beta\varepsilon}.
    \end{equation*}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:bound-l1-luot-l2}
	For any triplet $(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})$ satisfying primal feasibility 
	$\mathbf{P} \ge 0$, complementary condition \eqref{eq:compuot} and 
	first order optimality condition \eqref{eq:dualfeasuot} on a tree subgraph of 
	$(V \cup V',E)$, we have
    \begin{equation*}
        \lVert\mathbf{P}\rVert_1
        \leq
            \lVert \bm{\alpha}^{\bm 1}\rVert_1+\lVert \bm{\alpha}^{\bm 2}\rVert_1.
    \end{equation*}
    In particular, the result holds for every iterate of 
   	Algorithm~\ref{alg:adapted-ns} and any solution of \eqref{eq:rot-l2}.

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The optimality conditions~\eqref{eq:dualfeasuot} give
    \begin{equation*}
        2\lVert\mathbf{P}\rVert_{1,\mathscr{T}}
        =
        \lVert \bm{\alpha}^{\bm 1}\rVert_{1,\mathscr{T}}
        +\lVert \bm{\alpha}^{\bm 2}\rVert_{1,\mathscr{T}}
        -\tau\left(\sum_{i\in\mathscr{T}}\mathbf{f}_i
        +\sum_{j\in\mathscr{T}}\mathbf{g}_j\right),
    \end{equation*}
    where $\|\cdot\|_{1,\mathscr{T}}$ only considers coefficients corresponding 
    to edges of $\mathscr{T}$. Let $\mathscr{T}$ be the tree (or forest) associated with 
    the support of $\mathbf{P}$, and let 
    $\mathcal{A}_\mathscr{T}=\{(i_1,j_1),\dots,(i_k,j_k)\}$ be a 
    maximal matching of $\mathscr{T}$ (i.e. a set of edges that do not share 
    vertices and are maximal in the graph for that property), and 
    $\bar{\mathcal{A}}_\mathscr{T}$ be its complementary subgraph (with 
    complementary edges). We have
    \begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		\tau\left(\sum_{i\in\mathscr{T}}\mathbf{f}_i +\sum_{j\in\mathscr{T}}\mathbf{g}_j\right)
		&= 
		\tau\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{A}_\mathscr{T}}(\mathbf{f}_i+\mathbf{g}_j)  
		+  \tau\sum_{i\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}_\mathscr{T}}\mathbf{f}_i
		+  \tau\sum_{j\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}_\mathscr{T}}\mathbf{g}_i
		\\
		&=
		\tau\sum_{\ell=1}^{k} \mathbf{C}_{i_\ell,j_\ell} 
		+ \sum_{i\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}_\mathscr{T}}(\bm{\alpha}_i^{\bm 1}
		-(\mathbf{P}\mathbbm{1})_i)
		+ \sum_{j\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}_\mathscr{T}}(\bm{\alpha}_j^{\bm 2}
		-(\mathbf{P}^\top\mathbbm{1})_j)
		\\
		&\ge 
		-\left(\sum_{i\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}_\mathscr{T}}(\mathbf{P}\mathbbm{1})_i
		+\sum_{j\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}_\mathscr{T}}(\mathbf{P}^\top\mathbbm{1})_j\right)
	\end{aligned}
	\end{equation*}
	By definition of $\mathcal{A}_\mathscr{T}$, for any pair $(i,j) \in V \cup V'$ 
	in $\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathscr{T}}$, the edge $(i,j)$ either does not exist 
	or belongs to $\mathcal{A}_\mathscr{T}$. Thus,
    \begin{equation}
	\label{eq:last-step-bound-l2}
	\left(\sum_{i\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}_\mathscr{T}}(\mathbf{P}\mathbbm{1})_i
            +\sum_{j\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}_\mathscr{T}}(\mathbf{P}^\top\mathbbm{1})_j\right)
            \leq \lVert \mathbf{P}\rVert_{1,\mathscr{T}}
    \end{equation}
    from which the result follows.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
A result similar to that of Lemma~\ref{lem:bound-l1-luot-l2} holds for 
	$\mathrm{UOT-}\operatorname{\mathsf{KL}}$ problem \eqref{eq:rot-kl} assuming 
	$\tau^1=\tau^2=\tau$. Indeed,
    \begin{equation*}
        \begin{aligned}
            0&\geq -\tau \mathbf{P}\!:\! \mathbf{C}\\ 
            &= -\tau\sum_{i=1}^{n_1} \mathbf{f}_i (\mathbf{P}\mathbbm{1}_{n_2})_i
            -\tau
            \sum_{i=1}^{n_2}
            \mathbf{g}_i (\mathbf{P}^\top\mathbbm{1}_{n_1})_i\\
             &= \sum_{i=1}^{n_1} (\mathbf{P}\mathbbm{1}_{n_2})_i\log\left(
         \frac{(\mathbf{P}\mathbbm{1}_{n_2})_i}{\bm{\alpha}_i^{\bm{1}}}
             \right)
             +
             \sum_{i=1}^{n_2} (\mathbf{P}^\top\mathbbm{1}_{n_1})_i\log\left(
         \frac{(\mathbf{P}^\top\mathbbm{1}_{n_1})_i}{\bm{\alpha}_i^{\bm{2}}}
             \right)\\
             &\geq 
             \lVert\mathbf{P}\rVert_1
         \log\left(\frac{\lVert\mathbf{P}\rVert_1}{\lVert \bm{\alpha}^{\bm{1}}\rVert_1}\right)+
         \lVert\mathbf{P}\rVert_1
         \log\left(\frac{\lVert\mathbf{P}\rVert_1}{\lVert \bm{\alpha}^{\bm{2}}\rVert_1}\right),
        \end{aligned}
    \end{equation*}
    where the second line is obtained by complementary condition, the third one by first order 
    conditions \eqref{eq:dualfeasuot} and the last one applying log-sum inequality. 
    From this last line, we deduce that 
    $\lVert\mathbf{P}\rVert_1\leq \sqrt{\lVert \bm{\alpha}^{\bm{1}}\rVert_1 
    \lVert \bm{\alpha}^{\bm{2}}\rVert_1}$. Note that the same relations have been 
    obtained in previous work~\cite{nguyen2023unbalanced}, but our primal-dual 
    perspective allows for a shorter proof.

\label{lem:termination-cond-l2}

	any $(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})$ satisfying $\mathbf{P}\geq 0$, \eqref{eq:compuot},

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Suppose that $\tau=p/q$ where $p$ and $q$ are positive integers. Without loss 
	of generality, we can assume that $p=1$, since replacing $\mathbf{C}$ by 
	$p \mathbf{C}$ would preserve Assumption~\ref{assum:integral-data}. 
	For each tree of the support, we can build integer dual variables
	$\widetilde\mathbf{f}$ and $\widetilde\mathbf{g}$ that satisfy 
	$\widetilde\mathbf{f}_i+\widetilde\mathbf{g}_j = \mathbf{C}_{i,j}$.
    Thus, for each tree $\mathscr{T}_\ell$ of the support of $\mathbf{P}$, we have 
    $(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})=(\widetilde\mathbf{f}-\gamma_\ell,\widetilde\mathbf{g}+\gamma_\ell)$, 
    where
    \begin{equation*}
        \gamma_\ell
        =
        \frac{1}{\tau}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbf{s}[\mathscr{T}_\ell]}\bm{\alpha}_i^{\bm 1}
        -
        \sum_{j\in\mathbf{t}[\mathscr{T}_\ell]}\bm{\alpha}_j^{\bm 2}\right)
        +
        \frac{1}{\#\mathscr{T}_\ell}\left(
            \sum_{i\in\mathbf{s}[\mathscr{T}_\ell]}\widetilde\mathbf{f}_i
            -
            \sum_{j\in\mathbf{t}[\mathscr{T}_\ell]}\widetilde\mathbf{g}_j
        \right)
        \in\mathbb{Z}+\#\mathscr{T}_\ell^{-1}\mathbb{Z} = \#\mathscr{T}_\ell^{-1}\mathbb{Z}.
    \end{equation*}
    Hence, for any $i\in\mathbf{s}[\mathscr{T}_\ell]$ and $j\in\mathbf{t}[\mathscr{T}_{\ell'}]$, 
    we obtain:
    \begin{equation*}
        \mathbf{C}_{i,j}-(\mathbf{f}\oplus\mathbf{g})_{i,j} 
        = 
        \mathbf{C}_{i,j}-\widetilde\mathbf{f}_i+\gamma_\ell
        -\widetilde\mathbf{g}_j-\gamma_{\ell'}
        \in 
        \frac{1}{\#\mathscr{T}_\ell}\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{\#\mathscr{T}_{\ell'}}\mathbb{Z}.
    \end{equation*}
    Since $\#\mathscr{T}_\ell^{-1}\mathbb{Z}+\#\mathscr{T}_{\ell'}^{-1}\mathbb{Z} =
    \mathrm{lcm}\left(\#\mathscr{T}_{\ell}, \#\mathscr{T}_{\ell'}\right)^{-1}\mathbb{Z}$, 
    where $\mathrm{lcm}$ denotes the least common multiple, and
    and $\mathrm{lcm}\left(\#\mathscr{T}_{\ell}, \#\mathscr{T}_{\ell'}\right)\leq
    \#\mathscr{T}_{\ell}\#\mathscr{T}_{\ell'}\leq n_1n_2$, we find that
    \begin{equation*}
        \min_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}
        \mathbf{C}_{i,j} - (\mathbf{f}\oplus\mathbf{g})_{i,j} \geq-\frac{1}{n_1n_2+1}
        \implies 
        \min_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}} 
        \mathbf{C}_{i,j} - (\mathbf{f}\oplus\mathbf{g})_{i,j} \geq 0,
    \end{equation*}
    hence the desired conclusion.
