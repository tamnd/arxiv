<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
	Let $f_1,\dots,f_m$ and $g_1,\dots,g_s$ be arbitrary convex polynomials on $\mathbb{R}^n$ and
	let $K$ be as in \eqref{eq:K}. Then $\operatorname{Sol^w}(K,f)$ is connected, and path-connected if
	it is nonempty.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lm:lindec}
	Let $p$ be a convex polynomial on $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $d\in\mathbb{R}^n$ with $p^\infty(d)<+\infty$.
	Then $p(x+\sigma d)=p(x)+p^\infty(d)\,\sigma$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$ and $\sigma\in\mathbb{R}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix $x$ and put $q(\sigma):=p(x+\sigma d)$, a convex polynomial in one variable. If
	$\deg q\geq 2$, convexity forces the degree to be even with positive leading
	coefficient, so $q(\sigma)/\sigma\to+\infty$ and $p^\infty(d)=+\infty$, contrary to the
	hypothesis. Hence $q$ is affine, with slope
	$\lim_{\sigma\to\infty}(q(\sigma)-q(0))/\sigma=p^\infty(d)$ by \eqref{eq:rec}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:dicho}
	Let $p$ be a convex polynomial on $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $d\in\mathbb{R}^n$ such that $p$ is bounded from
	below on some ray of direction $d$. Then exactly one of the following holds:
	\textnormal{(a)} $p(x+\sigma d)=p(x)$ for all $x,\sigma$, and we say that $p$ is
	\emph{constant along $d$}; or \textnormal{(b)} $p(x+\sigma d)\to+\infty$ as
	$\sigma\to+\infty$, for every $x$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:constr}
	For every $d\in K_\infty$ and every $j$ there is $\gamma_j\geq 0$, depending on $d$, with
	$g_j(x+\sigma d)=g_j(x)-\gamma_j\sigma$ for all $x,\sigma$.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:FW}
	Let $p,g_1,\dots,g_s$ be convex polynomials and $K$ as in \eqref{eq:K}. If $p$ is bounded from below on $K$, then $p$ attains its minimum there.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rmk:D}
	$D$ is a nonempty convex cone. Indeed, $0\in D$; if $d\in D$ and $\lambda\geq 0$, then
	$\lambda d\in K_\infty$ because $K_\infty$ is a cone, and $u,v$ are constant along
	$\lambda d$; and if $d,d'\in D$, then $d+d'\in K_\infty$ because $K_\infty$ is
	convex, and $u(x+\sigma(d+d'))=u((x+\sigma d')+\sigma d)=u(x+\sigma d')=u(x)$ for all $x,\sigma$, and likewise for $v$. It need not be a subspace, since $K_\infty$ need not contain $-d$: for
	$K=\{x_1\geq 0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$ and $u=v=x_2^2$ one gets $D=\{(d_1,0):d_1\geq 0\}$,
	a ray. For $d\in D\setminus\{0\}$, the threshold function $\tau$ of Section~\ref{subs:deg} appears exactly when $-d\notin K_\infty$: both conditions are equivalent to $\gamma_j>0$ for some $j$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:constr}, that is, to some constraint $g_j$ being strictly decreasing along $d$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lm:segment}
	\textnormal{(i)} $W_0=\operatorname*{argmin}_Ku$ and $W_1=\operatorname*{argmin}_Kv$ are nonempty; $v$ attains its minimum over $W_0$ and $u$ attains its minimum over $W_1$, say at
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:zstar}
		z^*\in\operatorname*{argmin}\{v(z):\ z\in W_0\},\qquad w^*\in\operatorname*{argmin}\{u(w):\ w\in W_1\} .
	\end{equation}
	\textnormal{(ii)} For every $t\in(0,1)$ and every $x_t\in W_t$,
	$v(x_t)\leq v(z^*)$ and $u(x_t)\leq u(w^*)$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
(i) $W_0\neq\emptyset$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:FW}, as $u$ is bounded from below on $K$. By \eqref{eq:Thetasys}, applied to $2\text{-}\mathrm{OP}(K,u,v)$ with $\xi=(1,0)$, $W_0$ is again given by a convex polynomial system, and $v$ is bounded from below on $K\supseteq W_0$; so
	Theorem~\ref{thm:FW} applies once more, now to $v$ on $W_0$, and the minimum in
	\eqref{eq:zstar} is attained. Interchanging $u$ and $v$ handles $W_1$.

	(ii) Fix $t\in(0,1)$ and $x_t\in W_t$. As $z^*\in W_0\subseteq K$ and $x_t$ minimizes
	$p_t$ over $K$, we have $p_t(x_t)\leq p_t(z^*)$, that is
	\[
	(1-t)\bigl[u(x_t)-u(z^*)\bigr]+t\bigl[v(x_t)-v(z^*)\bigr]\leq 0 .
	\]
	Since $u(z^*)=\min_Ku$ and $x_t\in K$, the first bracket is nonnegative, and so is
	$1-t$; hence $t[v(x_t)-v(z^*)]\leq 0$, and dividing by $t>0$ yields
	$v(x_t)\leq v(z^*)$. Interchanging $u$ with $v$, $t$ with $1-t$, and $z^*$ with $w^*$ gives $u(x_t)\leq u(w^*)$. The argument needs $t>0$ and $1-t>0$, which is why (ii) is stated for the open interval only.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rmk:segment}
	The point $z^*$ is a \emph{hierarchical} minimizer: it minimizes $v$ among the
	minimizers of $u$. Such points are the viscosity solutions of Attouch
	\cite[Theorem~4.4]{attouch1996viscosity}, and for $v=\frac12\|\cdot\|^2$ and $K=\mathbb{R}^n$,
	$z^*$ is the minimum-norm element of $W_0$ and the first inequality in Lemma~\ref{lm:segment}(ii) is the classical Tikhonov estimate
	$\|x_t\|\leq\|z^*\|$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lm:Kc}
	Assume $D=\{0\}$. Then $K_c$ is compact for every $c\in\mathbb{R}$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
$K_c$ is closed and convex, being an intersection of $K$ with two sublevel sets of
	continuous convex functions. If $K_c=\emptyset$, it is compact; otherwise, were it
	unbounded, its recession cone would be nontrivial
	\cite[Theorem~8.4]{rockafellar1970convex}, so $x_0+\sigma d\in K_c$ for some
	$x_0\in K_c$, some $d\neq 0$ and all $\sigma\geq 0$. Along that ray $u,v\leq c$ by
	definition of $K_c$, while $u,v$ are bounded from below on $K_c\subseteq K$; so
	Corollary~\ref{cor:dicho} applies to each, and alternative (b) there is excluded by the
	upper bound. Hence both $u$ and $v$ are constant along $d$. As the ray lies in $K$, we
	also have $d\in K_\infty$ \cite[Theorem~8.3]{rockafellar1970convex}, whence
	$d\in D=\{0\}$, contradicting $d\neq0$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:nondeg}
	Assume $D=\{0\}$. Then $W_{[0,1]}$ is connected.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Put $c:=\max\{u(w^*),v(z^*)\}$. By Lemma~\ref{lm:Kc} the set $K_c$ is compact, and it
	is given by the convex polynomial system $g_j\leq 0$ $(j=1,\dots,s)$, $u-c\leq 0$,
	$v-c\leq 0$. By Lemma~\ref{lm:segment}(ii), $v(x_t)\leq v(z^*)\leq c$ and
	$u(x_t)\leq u(w^*)\leq c$ for every $t\in(0,1)$ and every $x_t\in W_t$, so
	$W_{(0,1)}\subseteq K_c$; moreover $z^*,w^*\in K_c$, because $u(z^*)=\min_Ku\leq u(w^*)$ and $v(w^*)=\min_Kv\leq v(z^*)$.
	Consider the \emph{subproblem} $2\text{-}\mathrm{OP}(K_c,u,v)$ and put $\widetilde W_t:=\operatorname*{argmin}_{K_c}p_t$.
	Since $K_c\subseteq K$ contains a point of every $W_t$, namely $z^*$ if $t=0$,
	every $x_t\in W_t$ if $t\in(0,1)$, and $w^*$ if $t=1$, we have $\min_{K_c}p_t=\min_Kp_t$ and $\widetilde W_t=W_t\cap K_c$ for all
	$t\in[0,1]$; in particular $\widetilde W_t=W_t$ for $t\in(0,1)$. As $K_c$ is compact,
	the solution mapping of the scalarization of $2\text{-}\mathrm{OP}(K_c,u,v)$ has bounded values, so
	$\widetilde W_{[0,1]}=\operatorname{Sol^w}(K_c,(u,v))$ is connected by
	\cite[Theorem~6.4]{hieu2025unboundedness}, or already by Warburton's theorem
	\cite{warburton1983quasiconcave}. Finally, $W_{[0,1]}=\widetilde W_{[0,1]}\cup W_0\cup W_1$, because $\widetilde W_t=W_t$ for $t\in(0,1)$ and $\widetilde W_t\subseteq W_t$ for $t\in\{0,1\}$; the sets $W_0$ and $W_1$ are convex, hence connected, and they meet $\widetilde W_{[0,1]}$ at $z^*$ and at $w^*$, respectively; so $W_{[0,1]}$ is connected by (T1), applied twice.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:split}
	Let $d\in D\setminus\{0\}$, put $H:=d^\perp$, and write $x=\pi_Hx+\pi_d(x)\,d$ with $\pi_Hx\in H$ and $\pi_d(x)\in\mathbb{R}$; for $A\subseteq H$ put
	$A\oplus\mathbb{R} d:=\{z+\sigma d:\ z\in A,\ \sigma\in\mathbb{R}\}$. With the $\gamma_j\geq 0$ of
	Corollary~\ref{cor:constr}, put
	\[
	J:=\{j:\gamma_j=0\},\qquad J^c:=\{1,\dots,s\}\setminus J,\qquad
	K_J:=\{x: g_j(x)\leq 0,\ j\in J\},
	\]
	where $K_J:=\mathbb{R}^n$ if $J=\emptyset$, and
	$K':=K_J\cap H$. Then:
	\begin{enumerate}[label=\textnormal{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.1em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt,parsep=0pt]
		\item $K\subseteq K_J$, $K_J$ is invariant under $\mathbb{R} d$, and $K_J=K'\oplus\mathbb{R} d$
		with $K'=\{z\in H: g_j(z)\leq 0,\ j\in J\}\neq\emptyset$, given by a convex
		polynomial system on $H\cong\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$;
		\item if $J^c\neq\emptyset$, then $\tau:=\max_{j\notin J}g_j/\gamma_j$ is continuous
		on $\mathbb{R}^n$ and, for $x\in K_J$ and $\sigma\in\mathbb{R}$,
		$x+\sigma d\in K\iff\sigma\geq\tau(x)$; if $J^c=\emptyset$, then $K=K_J$;
		\item $K_J=K+\mathbb{R} d$ and $K'=\pi_H(K)$;
		\item for every $t\in[0,1]$, $\inf_{K_J}p_t=\inf_Kp_t$ and $W_t=W^J_t\cap K$, where $W^J_t:=\operatorname*{argmin}_{K_J}p_t$;
		\item $u':=u|_H$, $v':=v|_H$ are convex polynomials bounded from below on $K'$, so
		$2\text{-}\mathrm{OP}(K',u',v')$ is a problem of the type considered in this section.
	\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By Corollary~\ref{cor:constr}, $g_j(x+\sigma d)=g_j(x)-\gamma_j\sigma$ with
	$\gamma_j\geq 0$; so the constraints split into those constant along $d$, indexed by
	$J$, and those strictly decreasing along $d$, indexed by $J^c$.

	(i) $K\subseteq K_J$ because $K_J$ retains only part of the constraints. For $j\in J$
	we have $g_j(x+\sigma d)=g_j(x)$ for \emph{all} $\sigma\in\mathbb{R}$, so $K_J$ is invariant under $\mathbb{R} d$. Hence
	$x\in K_J\iff\pi_Hx\in K_J\cap H=K'$, whence $K_J=K'\oplus\mathbb{R} d$. Since $K\subseteq K_J$, projecting a point of $K$ onto $H$ gives a point of $K_J\cap H=K'$, so $K'\neq\emptyset$; the description of $K'$ is just its definition.

	(ii) Let $J^c\neq\emptyset$. As $\gamma_j>0$ for $j\notin J$, $\tau$ is well defined and continuous, being a maximum of finitely many polynomials divided by positive constants. Let $x\in K_J$ and $\sigma\in\mathbb{R}$. For $j\in J$ we have
	$g_j(x+\sigma d)=g_j(x)\leq 0$ because $x\in K_J$, while for $j\notin J$, $g_j(x+\sigma d)=g_j(x)-\gamma_j\sigma\leq 0\iff\sigma\geq g_j(x)/\gamma_j$, because $\gamma_j>0$. Thus $x+\sigma d\in K$ if and only if $\sigma\geq g_j(x)/\gamma_j$ for all $j\notin J$, that is, $\sigma\geq\tau(x)$. If $J^c=\emptyset$, then $K_J=K$ by definition.

	(iii) $K+\mathbb{R} d\subseteq K_J$ by (i). Conversely, for $x\in K_J$ part (ii) provides $\sigma$ with $x+\sigma d\in K$ (any $\sigma$ if $J^c=\emptyset$), so $x\in K+\mathbb{R} d$. Then $\pi_H(K)\subseteq\pi_H(K_J)=K'$ by (i), and for $z\in K'\subseteq K_J$ part (ii) applied to $z$ provides $\sigma$ with $z+\sigma d\in K$, whence $z=\pi_H(z+\sigma d)\in\pi_H(K)$.

	(iv) As $d\in D$, every $p_t$ is constant along $d$. From $K\subseteq K_J$ we get
	$\inf_{K_J}p_t\leq\inf_Kp_t$. Conversely, for $x\in K_J$ part (ii) provides $\sigma$
	with $x+\sigma d\in K$, and then $p_t(x)=p_t(x+\sigma d)\geq\inf_Kp_t$. The two infima
	being equal, a point of $K$ minimizes $p_t$ over $K$ exactly when it minimizes $p_t$
	over $K_J$, i.e., $W_t=W^J_t\cap K$.

	(v) By (iii) and constancy along $d$, $u$ and $v$ are bounded from below on
	$K_J\supseteq K'$ by the same constants as on $K$; restrictions of convex polynomials
	to $H$ are convex polynomials.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rmk:relax}
	By Proposition~\ref{prop:split}(iii), passing from $K$ to $K_J=K+\mathbb{R} d$ is a relaxation, and Proposition~\ref{prop:split}(ii) records where each line $x+\mathbb{R} d$, $x\in K_J$, meets $K$: in the half-line $\{x+\sigma d:\sigma\geq\tau(x)\}$ when $J^c\neq\emptyset$, and in the whole line when $J^c=\emptyset$. Each $W^J_t$ is invariant under $\mathbb{R} d$, since $K_J$ is by
	Proposition~\ref{prop:split}(i) and $p_t$ is constant along $d$; hence so is
	$W^J_{[0,1]}:=\bigcup_tW^J_t$, and therefore
	$W^J_{[0,1]}=(W^J_{[0,1]}\cap H)\oplus\mathbb{R} d$, as in the proof of
	Proposition~\ref{prop:split}(i).

	We identify the slice $W^J_{[0,1]}\cap H$. As $u'=u|_H$ and $v'=v|_H$, the scalarization of
	$2\text{-}\mathrm{OP}(K',u',v')$ at $t$ is $p_t|_H$, with solution set $W'_t:=\operatorname*{argmin}_{K'}p_t$, and
	we claim that
	$
	W^J_t\cap H=W'_t .$
	Indeed, if $x\in W^J_t\cap H$, then $x\in K_J\cap H=K'$ and $p_t(x)=\inf_{K_J}p_t\leq\inf_{K'}p_t$ because $K'\subseteq K_J$; so $x\in W'_t$.
	Conversely, let $z\in W'_t$ and let $x\in K_J$ be arbitrary. Then $\pi_Hx\in K'$ by
	Proposition~\ref{prop:split}(i), and $x-\pi_Hx\in\mathbb{R} d$, so $p_t(x)=p_t(\pi_Hx)$ because
	$p_t$ is constant along $d$, and $p_t(\pi_Hx)\geq p_t(z)$ because $z$ minimizes $p_t$
	over $K'$. So $p_t\geq p_t(z)$ on $K_J$, and $z\in K'=K_J\cap H$; hence
	$z\in W^J_t\cap H$. Taking unions over $t$ gives $W^J_{[0,1]}\cap H=W'_{[0,1]}$, so

		W^J_{[0,1]}=W'_{[0,1]}\oplus\mathbb{R} d .
	\end{equation}
If $J^c=\emptyset$, then $K=K_J$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:split}(ii), so $W_{[0,1]}=W^J_{[0,1]}=W'_{[0,1]}\oplus\mathbb{R} d\cong W'_{[0,1]}\times\mathbb{R}$ by \eqref{eq:cylinder}.
If $J^c\neq\emptyset$, then Proposition~\ref{prop:split}(iv), \eqref{eq:cylinder} and
	Proposition~\ref{prop:split}(ii), applied at $z\in K'\subseteq K_J$, give in turn
	\[
	W_{[0,1]}=W^J_{[0,1]}\cap K=\{z+\sigma d:\ z\in W'_{[0,1]},\ \sigma\geq\tau(z)\}.

	Hence $\Phi(z,\rho):=z+(\tau(z)+\rho)d$ maps $W'_{[0,1]}\times[0,\infty)$ into

	it is continuous since $\tau$ is; and its inverse
	$x\mapsto(\pi_Hx,\pi_d(x)-\tau(\pi_Hx))$ is continuous for the same reason.

\label{prop:bi}
	Let $u,v$ be convex polynomials bounded from below on $K$. Then $W_{[0,1]}$ is

We argue by induction on the dimension $n$ of the ambient space, for all data as in
	Section~\ref{subs:seg}. For $n=0$, $K=\{0\}=W_{[0,1]}$. Let $n\geq 1$ and assume the
	claim in dimension $n-1$. If $D=\{0\}$, apply Proposition~\ref{prop:nondeg}. If
	$D\neq\{0\}$, pick $d\in D\setminus\{0\}$; Proposition~\ref{prop:split}(v) produces a
	problem of the same kind on $H\cong\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$, whose set $W'_{[0,1]}$ is connected by

For $\Lambda=\emptyset$ the set $\operatorname{Sol^w}(K,f)$ is empty by \eqref{eq:scalar}, hence
	connected. Assume $\Lambda\neq\emptyset$ and fix once and for all
	$\hat\xi\in\Lambda$. Let $\xi\in\Lambda$ be arbitrary. By \eqref{eq:Lconvex},
	$[\hat\xi,\xi]\subseteq\Lambda$. Put $u:=f_{\hat\xi}$ and $v:=f_{\xi}$, convex
	polynomials bounded from below on $K$ because both weights lie in $\Lambda$; since
	$\eta\mapsto f_\eta$ is affine,
	\[
	(1-t)u+tv=f_{\xi^t},\qquad \xi^t:=(1-t)\hat\xi+t\xi\in\Lambda\quad(t\in[0,1]),
	\]
	so the bicriteria problem $2\text{-}\mathrm{OP}(K,u,v)$ has $W_t=\Theta(\xi^t)$ and
	$W_{[0,1]}=\Theta([\hat\xi,\xi])$. Hence Proposition~\ref{prop:bi}, applied to
	$(u,v)$, shows that $\Theta([\hat\xi,\xi])$ is connected. Moreover, since
	$\hat\xi,\xi\in[\hat\xi,\xi]\subseteq\Lambda$, we have by \eqref{eq:scalar}
	\[
	\Theta(\hat\xi)\cup\Theta(\xi)\subseteq\Theta([\hat\xi,\xi])\subseteq\Theta(\Lambda)
	=\operatorname{Sol^w}(K,f) .
	\]
	Taking the union over $\xi\in\Lambda$ and using the first inclusion above, we obtain \begin{equation}\label{eq:corewing}
		\operatorname{Sol^w}(K,f)=\bigcup_{\xi\in\Lambda}\Theta([\hat\xi,\xi]),\quad
		\Theta(\hat\xi)\subseteq\Theta([\hat\xi,\xi])\ (\xi\in\Lambda),
	\end{equation} Thus $\operatorname{Sol^w}(K,f)$ is a union of connected sets all containing $\Theta(\hat\xi)$, which is nonempty because $\hat\xi\in\Lambda$; hence $\operatorname{Sol^w}(K,f)$ is connected by (T1).

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rmk:core}
	Call $\Theta(\hat\xi)$ the \emph{core} and the sets $\Theta([\hat\xi,\xi])$,
	$\xi\in\Lambda$, the \emph{wings} issued from it. The core is convex and nonempty, each
	wing is connected, and by \eqref{eq:corewing} every wing contains the core and the
	solution set is the union of the wings. In particular, any two points of $\operatorname{Sol^w}(K,f)$ lie in two wings, whose union is connected by (T1): two wings suffice to join any two points, whatever $m$ is.

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The set $\operatorname{Sol^w}(K,f)$ is semi-algebraic: it is defined by the first-order formula
	\[
	x^*\in K\ \wedge\ \neg\exists x\Bigl(x\in K\wedge\bigwedge\nolimits_{i=1}^m
	f_i(x)<f_i(x^*)\Bigr)
	\]
	in the polynomial data, so the Tarski--Seidenberg theorem
	\cite[Chapter~2]{bochnak2013real} applies; this device is used systematically in
	\cite{hieu2020application}. A semi-algebraic set has finitely many connected
	components, each of them semi-algebraic and path-connected
	\cite[Theorem~2.4.5 and Proposition~2.5.13]{bochnak2013real}. Being connected and nonempty, $\operatorname{Sol^w}(K,f)$ is a single such component and is therefore path-connected.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rmk:pareto}
	Theorem~\ref{thm:main} concerns \emph{weak} solutions. For the Pareto solution set $\operatorname{Sol}(K,f)$, the set of $x^*\in K$ for which no $x\in K$ satisfies $f(x)-f(x^*)\in-\mathbb{R}^m_+\setminus\{0\}$, only the inclusions $\bigcup_{\xi\in\Lambda\cap\operatorname{ri}\Delta}\Theta(\xi)\subseteq\operatorname{Sol}(K,f)\subseteq\operatorname{Sol^w}(K,f)$ are available, with $\operatorname{ri}\Delta$ the relative interior of $\Delta$ \cite[Chapter~3]{ehrgott2005multicriteria}; so the method does not reach $\operatorname{Sol}(K,f)$.
	The obstruction is not peculiar to this method: already for strictly quasi-concave data
	the Pareto solution set may be disconnected, with all its components unbounded
	\cite{hoa2007unbounded}.
