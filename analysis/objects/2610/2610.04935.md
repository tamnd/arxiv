<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For every $N\geq3$ there exists a constant $\rho_N\in(0,\infty)$ such that
every nontrivial radial solution $(u,\lambda)$ of \eqref{eq:problem}
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:massbound}
 \int_{B_1}|u|^2\,dx\leq\rho_N.
\end{equation}
Consequently, if $\rho>\rho_N$, problem \eqref{eq:problem} admits no radial
solution.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The regularity assumption in the definition of radial solutions can be weakened.
Indeed, the arguments of this paper only use the one dimensional radial profile
$U$, where $u(x)=U(|x|)$.  It is enough to assume
\[
 U\in C^2((0,1))\cap C^1([0,1]),
 \qquad U'(0)=0,\qquad U(1)=0,
\]
with $U$ satisfying the radial equation on $(0,1)$.  Under these assumptions,
the shooting formulation, the radial Pohozaev identity, and the uniqueness
properties of the corresponding ordinary differential equation remain valid.
We use the stronger classical regularity stated above only for convenience.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:explicit}
The constant in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is uniform in the shooting parameter
and in the nodal number, but it is not asserted to be sharp.  In particular,
the theorem gives a radial nonexistence result for every prescribed mass
strictly larger than $\rho_N$.  We do not record a numerical value here,
since this would require tracking the constants in the coercivity estimate
in Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient} quantitatively.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:spectrum}
Let $\mathcal R_{\rm rad}$ be the set of all masses attained by nontrivial
radial solutions of \eqref{eq:problem}.  There exists a finite number
$\rho_{\rm rad}^*>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrum}
 \mathcal R_{\rm rad}=(0,\rho_{\rm rad}^*)
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 \mathcal R_{\rm rad}=(0,\rho_{\rm rad}^*].
\end{equation}
In particular, every $0<\rho<\rho_{\rm rad}^*$ is attained by at least one
radial normalized solution, while no $\rho>\rho_{\rm rad}^*$ is attained by
a radial solution.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:superradial}
Let $p>\frac{N+2}{N-2}$ and $\mathcal R_{\rm rad}(p)$ denote the set of
the $L^2$ masses of all nontrivial classical radial Dirichlet solutions $(u,\lambda)$,
with $\lambda\in\mathbb R$, solving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:superproblem-intro}
 -\Delta u+\lambda u=|u|^{p-1}u\quad\hbox{in }B_1,\qquad
 u|_{\partial B_1}=0.
\end{equation}
Then there exists $0<\rho_{\rm rad}^*(p)<\infty$ such that
\[
 \mathcal R_{\rm rad}(p)=(0,\rho_{\rm rad}^*(p))
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 \mathcal R_{\rm rad}(p)=(0,\rho_{\rm rad}^*(p)].
\]
In particular, every mass below $\rho_{\rm rad}^*(p)$ is attained by a
radial normalized solution, while no radial normalized solution exists
above it.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:open}
The critical mechanism is specific to the Sobolev exponent: the Fowler
change removes every power of $e^t$ from the nonlinearity, while the
frequency contribution becomes the monotone coefficient $\eta=\gamma e^{2t}$.
The identity \eqref{eq:Hdeta} then turns the desired mass quotient into an
ODE estimate.  It remains open whether the endpoint $\rho_{\rm rad}^*$ is
attained.  Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and \ref{thm:superradial} concern only
radial solutions and do not exclude nonradial solutions of arbitrarily large
mass.  Finally, for powers above $2^*-1$, the critical Fowler cancellation in the nonlinear term is
lost; the conclusion in Theorem~\ref{thm:superradial} is
instead obtained from the separate dynamical systems argument of
Section~\ref{sec:supercritical}.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} concerns all radial solutions: they may change sign,
have arbitrarily many nodal regions, and need not be variational critical
points selected by any particular minimax procedure.  It does \emph{not}
exclude nonradial solutions of arbitrarily large mass.  Theorem~\ref{thm:superradial}
settles the corresponding question for strictly supercritical powers only
within the radial class; the nonradial problem remains open.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:lambda}
If $(u,\lambda)$ is a nontrivial solution of \eqref{eq:problem}, then
$\lambda<0$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Set $A=\int_{B_1}|\nabla u|^2dx$, $M=\int_{B_1}u^2dx$, and
$D=\int_{B_1}|u|^{2^*}dx$.  Testing the equation against $u$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nehari}
 A+\lambda M=D.
\end{equation}
The Pohozaev identity on the ball gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poho-global}
 \frac{N-2}{2}A+\frac{N\lambda}{2}M-
 \frac{N}{2^*}D+\frac12\int_{\partial B_1}(\partial_\nu u)^2\,dS=0.
\end{equation}
Since $\frac{N}{2^*}=\frac{N-2}{2}$, inserting \eqref{eq:nehari} into
\eqref{eq:poho-global} gives
\[
 \lambda M=-\frac12\int_{\partial B_1}(\partial_\nu u)^2\,dS.
\]
Since $u$ is radial, vanishing of the boundary integral would imply
$u(1)=u_r(1)=0$.  Uniqueness for the radial ODE with Cauchy data prescribed
at $r=1$ would then give $u\equiv0$, contradicting nontriviality.  Hence the
boundary integral is strictly positive and $\lambda<0$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:masszero}
Let $R=e^T$ be a zero of $v$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:massq}
 \int_{B_1}|u|^2\,dx
 =|\mathbb S^{N-1}|\frac{H(T)}{\eta(T)}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The radial Pohozaev identity for \eqref{eq:shoot}, integrated on $(0,R)$,
is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialpoho}
 \gamma\int_0^R v(r)^2r^{N-1}\,dr
 =\frac12R^Nv'(R)^2.
\end{equation}
At a zero $T$, that is $w(T)=0$, \eqref{eq:energy} gives
\[
 H(T)=\frac12w'(T)^2=\frac12R^Nv'(R)^2.
\]
On the other hand, \eqref{eq:rescale-back} gives
\[
 \int_{B_1}|u|^2\,dx
 =|\mathbb S^{N-1}|R^{-2}\int_0^R v(r)^2r^{N-1}\,dr.
\]
Combining these equalities with \eqref{eq:radialpoho} proves
\eqref{eq:massq}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:quotient}
There exists $C_N>0$ such that every solution of \eqref{eq:fowler} arising
from \eqref{eq:shoot} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient}
 0\leq \frac{H(t)}{\eta(t)}\leq C_N\qquad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb R.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since $\eta(t)=\gamma e^{2t}$ is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism from $\mathbb R$ onto $(0,\infty)$, we may regard both $H$ and $w$ as continuous functions of $\eta$.  From \eqref{eq:energyderivative} and \eqref{eq:fowlerdef},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hdeta}
 \frac{dH}{d\eta}=\frac12w^2.
\end{equation}
Fix $\eta_0=1+2\alpha^2$.  For $0\leq\eta\leq\eta_0$,
\[
 F_\eta(s)=-\frac{\alpha^2-\eta}{2}s^2+\frac1{2^*}|s|^{2^*}
\]
obey a coercive lower bound which is uniform in $\eta$.  Indeed,
$|\alpha^2-\eta|\leq C_N$ on this interval, and, since $2^*>2$, Young's
inequality gives, for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 s^2\leq \varepsilon |s|^{2^*}+C_{N,\varepsilon}.
\]
Choosing $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small yields constants $c_N,C_N>0$
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coercive-compact}
 F_\eta(s)\geq c_N|s|^{2^*}-C_N,
 \qquad s\in\mathbb R,\quad 0\leq\eta\leq\eta_0.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
 c_N|w|^{2^*}\leq H+C_N,
\]
because $H=\frac12w'^2+F_\eta(w)$.  A second application of
$|w|^2\leq 1+|w|^{2^*}$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wH}
 w^2\leq C_N(1+H),\qquad 0\leq\eta\leq\eta_0.
\end{equation}
Notice that this estimate does not require any upper bound for $w'$; the
nonnegative kinetic term is simply discarded.
Since $\eta(t)\rightarrow0$ and $H(t)\to0$ as $t\to-\infty$, we can extend
$H$ continuously to $\eta=0$ by setting $H(0)=0$.  Integrating
\eqref{eq:Hdeta} and using \eqref{eq:wH}, for $0\leq\eta\leq\eta_0$ we obtain
\[
 H(\eta)=\frac12\int_0^\eta w(\xi)^2\,d\xi
 \leq C_N\eta+C_N\int_0^\eta H(\xi)\,d\xi,
\]
where, with a harmless abuse of notation, $w(\xi)$ denotes $w$ written as
a function of the strictly increasing variable $\eta$. Therefore, by using the Gronwall's inequality, we obtain
\[
 H(\eta)\leq C_N\eta e^{C_N\eta}
 \leq C_N'\eta,\ 0\leq\eta\leq\eta_0 .
\]
Hence $\frac{H}{\eta}$ is bounded in this range.

For $\eta\geq\eta_0$, $F_\eta(s)$ is positive and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coercive-large}
 F_\eta(s)\geq\frac{\eta-\alpha^2}{2}s^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently, by \eqref{eq:Hdeta},
\[
 \frac{dH}{d\eta}\leq\frac{H}{\eta-\alpha^2}.
\]
For $\eta\geq\eta_0$, we therefore have
\[
 \frac{d}{d\eta}\log H(\eta)
 \leq \frac{1}{\eta-\alpha^2}
\]
whenever $H(\eta)>0$.  In fact, by \eqref{eq:Hdeta} and $H(0)=0$,
\[
 H(\eta)=\frac12\int_0^\eta (w(\xi))^2\,d\xi.
\]
Hence, if $H(\eta_*)=0$ for some $\eta_*>0$, continuity and nonnegativity of $(w(\xi))^2$ imply $w(\xi)=0$ for every $0<\xi<\eta_*$.  Since $\eta\mapsto t=\frac12\log(\frac{\eta}{\gamma})$ is one to one, $w$ vanishes on a nonempty open $t$ interval.  At any point of that interval one has $w=w'=0$; uniqueness for the smooth Fowler ODE then gives $w\equiv0$, and therefore $v\equiv0$, contradicting $v(0)=1$.  Thus $H(\eta)>0$ for every
$\eta>0$, and integration from $\eta_0$ to $\eta$ gives
\[
 H(\eta)\leq H(\eta_0)
 \frac{\eta-\alpha^2}{\eta_0-\alpha^2}.
\]
Using the preceding estimate $H(\eta_0)\leq C_N\eta_0$, and recalling
that $\eta_0=1+2\alpha^2$ depends only on $N$, we obtain
\[
 H(\eta)\leq C_N\frac{\eta_0}{\eta_0-\alpha^2}
 (\eta-\alpha^2)\leq C_N'\eta,
 \qquad \eta\geq\eta_0.
\]
Dividing by $\eta$ proves \eqref{eq:quotient}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:criticaloscillation}
For every $\gamma>0$, the shooting solution $v_\gamma$ of
\eqref{eq:shoot} has infinitely many positive, simple zeros.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Put $m=\frac{N-1}{2}$, $k=\frac{(N-1)(N-3)}{4}$, and
$\psi(r)=r^mv_\gamma(r)$.  Since $\gamma>0$ is fixed, choose
$R_0=R_0(\gamma)$ so large that
\[
 \gamma-\frac{k}{r^2}\ge\frac\gamma2,\ r\ge R_0 .
\]
On $[R_0,\infty)$ one has
\[
 \psi''+\left(\gamma-\frac{k}{r^2}\right)\psi+
 r^{-a}|\psi|^{\frac4{N-2}}\psi=0,
 \qquad a=\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}>2.
\]
Define
\[
 E(r)=\frac12\psi'^2+\frac12\left(\gamma-\frac{k}{r^2}\right)\psi^2
 +\frac1{2^*}r^{-a}|\psi|^{2^*}.
\]
More precisely,
\[
 E(r)\ge \frac12\psi'(r)^2+\frac{\gamma}{4}\psi(r)^2
 \ge c_\gamma\bigl(\psi'(r)^2+\psi(r)^2\bigr),
 \qquad c_\gamma=\min\left\{\frac12,\frac{\gamma}{4}\right\}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 E'(r)=\frac{k}{r^3}\psi^2-
 \frac{a}{2^*}r^{-a-1}|\psi|^{2^*}\le C_\gamma r^{-3}E(r).
\]
Gronwall's inequality therefore yields
\[
 E(r)\le E(R_0)\exp\left(C_\gamma\int_{R_0}^r \tau^{-3}\,d\tau\right)
 \le C_\gamma E(R_0),\qquad r\ge R_0.
\]
Thus $E$ is bounded above, and hence so are $\psi$ and $\psi'$.  We now
spell out the oscillation argument.  Put $\omega=\sqrt\gamma$ and introduce
Pr$\mathrm{\ddot{u}}$fer variables by
\[
 \psi=\varrho\sin\theta,\qquad
 \psi'=\omega\varrho\cos\theta,
 \qquad \varrho>0.
\]
They are well defined on $[R_0,\infty)$ after a continuous choice of
$\theta$, since $(\psi,\psi')$ cannot vanish simultaneously.  Writing the
ODE as
\[
 \psi''+\bigl(\omega^2+Q(r)\bigr)\psi=0,\qquad
 Q(r)=-\frac{k}{r^2}+r^{-a}|\psi(r)|^{\frac4{N-2}},
\]
and differentiating the two Pr$\mathrm{\ddot{u}}$fer identities gives the exact phase equation
\[
 \theta'(r)
 =\omega+\frac{Q(r)}{\omega}\sin^2\theta(r)
 =\sqrt\gamma-\frac{k}{\sqrt\gamma\,r^2}\sin^2\theta(r)
 +\frac{r^{-a}}{\sqrt\gamma}|\psi(r)|^{\frac4{N-2}}
   \sin^2\theta(r).
\]
Since $\psi$ is bounded on $[R_0,\infty)$, with a bound depending only on
the fixed parameter $\gamma$, we obtain
\[
 |\theta'(r)-\sqrt\gamma|
 \le C_\gamma(r^{-2}+r^{-a}).
\]
Here $a=\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}>2$, hence the right-hand side is integrable on
$(R_0,\infty)$.  Consequently
$\theta(r)=\sqrt\gamma r+O_\gamma(1)$ and tends to $+\infty$.  Therefore
$\psi$, and hence $v_\gamma$, has infinitely many zeros.  A zero cannot
satisfy simultaneously $v_\gamma=v_\gamma'=0$, by uniqueness for the
regular radial ODE; all zeros are simple.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:largegamma}
For every fixed $j\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:largegamma}
 M_j(\gamma)\longrightarrow0\qquad\text{as }\gamma\to+\infty.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Put $z_\gamma(s)=v_\gamma(\frac{s}{\sqrt\gamma})$.  It satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:besselperturbation}
 z_\gamma''+\frac{N-1}{s}z_\gamma'+z_\gamma+
 \frac1\gamma|z_\gamma|^{\frac4{N-2}}z_\gamma=0,
 \qquad z_\gamma(0)=1,\quad z_\gamma'(0)=0.
\end{equation}
For $\gamma\geq1$, its radial energy
\[
 E_\gamma(s)=\frac12 (z_\gamma'(s))^2+\frac12(z_\gamma(s))^2
 +\frac{N-2}{2N\gamma}|z_\gamma(s)|^{2^*}
\]
satisfies
\[
 E_\gamma'(s)=-\frac{N-1}{s}(z_\gamma'(s))^2\leq0,
 \qquad
 E_\gamma(0)=\frac12+\frac{N-2}{2N\gamma}\leq C_N.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bessel-uniform}
 \sup_{\gamma\geq1}\sup_{s\geq0}
 \bigl(|z_\gamma(s)|+|z_\gamma'(s)|\bigr)\leq C_N.
\end{equation}
To handle the regular singular point $s=0$ explicitly, rewrite
\eqref{eq:besselperturbation} in divergence form:
\[
 (s^{N-1}z_\gamma')'
 =-s^{N-1}\left(z_\gamma+
 \frac1\gamma|z_\gamma|^{\frac4{N-2}}z_\gamma\right).
\]
Thus, for $s>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bessel-derivative-integral}
 z_\gamma'(s)=-s^{1-N}\int_0^s\tau^{N-1}
 \left(z_\gamma(\tau)+\frac1\gamma
 |z_\gamma(\tau)|^{\frac4{N-2}}z_\gamma(\tau)\right)d\tau.
\end{equation}
In particular, \eqref{eq:bessel-uniform} implies $|z_\gamma'(s)|\leq C_Ns$
near the origin, uniformly for $\gamma\geq1$.  Integrating once more gives
\[
 z_\gamma(s)=1-\int_0^s\sigma^{1-N}\int_0^\sigma\tau^{N-1}
 \left(z_\gamma(\tau)+\frac1\gamma
 |z_\gamma(\tau)|^{\frac4{N-2}}z_\gamma(\tau)\right)d\tau\,d\sigma.
\]
Hence, on every fixed interval $[0,L]$, the family $\{z_\gamma\}_{\gamma\geq1}$
is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous.  Arzel\`a--Ascoli gives, along any sequence
$\gamma_n\to\infty$, a subsequence converging uniformly on $[0,L]$ to a
function $z$.  Passing to the limit in \eqref{eq:bessel-derivative-integral}
then gives uniform convergence of the derivatives as well.  Therefore
$z_{\gamma_n}\to z$ in $C^1([0,L])$, and the limit solves
\[
 z''+\frac{N-1}{s}z'+z=0,\qquad z(0)=1,\quad z'(0)=0.
\]
The regular solution of this Bessel equation is unique; denote it by $J$.
Since every sequence has the same limit, we have
\[
 z_\gamma\longrightarrow J\qquad\text{in }C^1_{\rm loc}([0,\infty))
 \quad(\gamma\to\infty).
\]
If $\zeta_j$ is the $j$th positive zero of $J$, then $\zeta_j$ is simple and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeroscaling}
 \sqrt\gamma R_j(\gamma)\longrightarrow\zeta_j.
\end{equation}
Indeed, fix $j$ and choose pairwise disjoint intervals
$I_k=(\zeta_k-\delta_k,\zeta_k+\delta_k)$, $1\le k\le j$, so small that
$|J'(s)|\ge c_k>0$ on $I_k$.  The $C^1$ convergence implies that, for all
sufficiently large $\gamma$, $|z_\gamma'(s)|\ge c_k/2$ on $I_k$ and
$z_\gamma$ has opposite signs at the two endpoints of $I_k$.  Hence
$z_\gamma$ has exactly one zero in each $I_k$.  On the compact part of
$[0,\zeta_j+\delta_j]$ left after removing these intervals, $J$ is bounded
away from zero; uniform convergence therefore excludes any additional zeros
there.  Thus the zero in $I_j$ is precisely the $j$th positive zero of
$z_\gamma$, proving \eqref{eq:zeroscaling}.

Writing $s_j(\gamma)=\sqrt\gamma R_j(\gamma)$ and using
\eqref{eq:massbranches},
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_j(\gamma)
 &=|\mathbb S^{N-1}|\,\gamma^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}
 s_j(\gamma)^{N-2}
 \int_0^1 z_\gamma(s_j(\gamma)r)^2r^{N-1}\,dr\\
 &=C_{N,j}\gamma^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}
 +o\!\left(\gamma^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}\right),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
 C_{N,j}=|\mathbb S^{N-1}|\,\zeta_j^{N-2}
 \int_0^1J(\zeta_jr)^2r^{N-1}\,dr>0.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:largegamma}.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:exchange}
For every $R>0$, a constant $C_{N,p,R}$ exists such that solutions of
\eqref{eq:helmholtzA} satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corebound}
 \sup_{A>0}\sup_{0<s\le R}
 s^\alpha\bigl(|z_A(s)|+s|z_A'(s)|\bigr)\le C_{N,p,R}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
We first establish the uniform estimate which is needed over a time interval
of length comparable with $\log A$.  Write $q=w_A'$ and $\eta=e^{2t}$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:superenergy}
 \mathcal H_A(t)=\frac12q(t)^2-\frac c2w_A(t)^2+
 \frac{\eta(t)}2w_A(t)^2+\frac1{p+1}|w_A(t)|^{p+1}.
\end{equation}
Differentiation, using \eqref{eq:superfowler}, gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:superenergyderivative}
 \mathcal H_A'(t)=-\beta q(t)^2+\eta(t)w_A(t)^2.
\end{equation}
Fix $R>0$.  We make the coercivity used below explicit.  For
$0\leq\eta\leq R^2$, write
\[
 V_\eta(s)=-\frac c2s^2+\frac\eta2s^2+\frac1{p+1}|s|^{p+1}.
\]
Since $p+1>2$, Young's inequality gives
\[
 \frac c2s^2\leq \frac1{2(p+1)}|s|^{p+1}+C_{N,p}.
\]
The term $\frac{\eta s^2}{2}$ is nonnegative, and hence
\[
 V_\eta(s)\geq \frac1{2(p+1)}|s|^{p+1}-C_{N,p}
 ,\ 0\leq\eta\leq R^2 .
\]
Choose $K_R$ (in fact a constant depending only on $N,p$ would suffice)
so large that $F_A:=\mathcal H_A+K_R\geq1$.  Then
\[
 F_A\geq \frac12q^2+\frac1{2(p+1)}|w_A|^{p+1}+1.
\]
Since $w_A^2\leq C(1+|w_A|^{p+1})$, this proves that there is
$C_R>0$, independent of $A$ and $0\leq\eta\leq R^2$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supercoercivity}
 1+w_A^2+q^2+|w_A|^{p+1}\leq C_RF_A.
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:superenergyderivative}, discarding the nonpositive term
$-\beta q^2$, and applying \eqref{eq:supercoercivity}, we obtain for
$t\leq\log R$,
\[
 F_A'(t)\leq e^{2t}w_A(t)^2\leq C_Re^{2t}F_A(t),
\]
that is,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supergronwall}
 F_A'(t)\leq C_Re^{2t}F_A(t),\  t\leq\log R .
\end{equation}
For each fixed $A>0$, regularity at the origin gives
$z_A(s)=A+O_A(s^2)$ and $z_A'(s)=O_A(s)$ as $s\rightarrow0$; hence
$w_A(t),q(t)\to0$ as $t\to-\infty$ and therefore $F_A(t)\to K_R$.
No convergence uniform in $A$ is needed here.  Indeed, for an arbitrary but
fixed $A$, integration of \eqref{eq:supergronwall} from $T<t$ gives
\[
 F_A(t)\leq F_A(T)\exp\left(C_R\int_T^t e^{2\tau}\,d\tau\right).
\]
We now let $T\to-\infty$ for this fixed $A$.  Since the limiting value
$K_R$ and the coefficient $C_R$ are independent of $A$, the resulting
estimate is automatically uniform after the limit has been taken:
\[
 F_A(t)\leq K_R\exp\left(\frac{C_R}{2}e^{2t}\right)
 \leq K_R\exp\left(\frac{C_RR^2}{2}\right).
\]
Combining this estimate with \eqref{eq:supercoercivity} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniformfowlerbound}
 \sup_{A>0}\sup_{t\leq\log R}(|w_A(t)|+|w_A'(t)|)\leq C_{N,p,R}.
\end{equation}
The crucial point is that $e^{2t}$ is integrable at $-\infty$; hence the
estimate is uniform even though the transition time tends to $-\infty$ as
$A\to\infty$.

The estimate \eqref{eq:uniformfowlerbound} is the core input used below.
Finally,
\[
 s^\alpha z_A(s)=w_A(t),\qquad
 s^{\alpha+1}z_A'(s)=w_A'(t)-\alpha w_A(t),\qquad t=\log s.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:uniformfowlerbound} implies \eqref{eq:corebound} for all
$A>0$.  The proof is complete.
