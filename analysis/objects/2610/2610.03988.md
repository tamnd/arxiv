<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:local-spectrum}
Let $R\in\overline{\mathbb Q}(x)\cap\mathbb R(x)$ be nonconstant, put $d=\deg R$ and
$e=[K_R:\mathbb Q]$, and let $m\geq 1$ be an integer. For every nonempty open interval
$J\subseteq\mathbb R$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-spectrum}
  \bigl\{\operatorname{type}(R(\xi)):\xi\in J\cap\mathscr U_m\bigr\}
  =\bigl\{r\in\mathbb Z:\lceil m/(ed)\rceil\leq r\leq em\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:classification}
Let $m\geq 1$ be an integer and let
$R\in\overline{\mathbb Q}(x)\cap\mathbb R(x)$ be nonconstant.
Then $R(\mathscr U_m)\subseteq\mathscr U_m$ if and only if $R\in\mathbb Q(x)$ and either
$m=1$ or $\deg R=1$. In particular, for $m\geq 2$, the preservers are
precisely the M\"obius transformations over $\mathbb Q$, and then
$R(\mathscr U_m)=\mathscr U_m$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The field of definition matters even in degree one. For example,
$R(x)=x+\sqrt2$ attains each of the types $1,2,3,4$ on $J\cap\mathscr U_2$
for every nonempty open interval $J$. More generally, for every pair of
types permitted by Theorem~\ref{thm:local-spectrum}, the corresponding
points $(\xi,R(\xi))$ are dense on the real graph of $R$ away from its
poles.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
We write $A\ll_{\mathcal P}B$ or
$A=O_{\mathcal P}(B)$ if $|A|\leq CB$, where $B\geq 0$ and $C>0$
depends only on the indicated parameters $\mathcal P$. For nonnegative
$A,B$, the notation $A\asymp_{\mathcal P}B$ means both
$A\ll_{\mathcal P}B$ and $B\ll_{\mathcal P}A$. Dependence on fixed data
may be omitted. All algebraic degrees are taken over $\mathbb Q$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:type-bounds}
Let $R\in\overline{\mathbb Q}(x)\cap\mathbb R(x)$ be nonconstant, with $d=\deg R$ and
$e=[K_R:\mathbb Q]$. If $\xi\in\mathscr U_m$, then $R(\xi)\in\mathscr U_j$ for some integer
$j$ satisfying
\[
  \left\lceil\frac{m}{ed}\right\rceil\leq j\leq em.
\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Put $\eta=R(\xi)$. Since the poles and critical points of $R$ are algebraic and $\xi$ is
transcendental, $\xi$ is neither a pole nor a critical point of $R$.
Hence $R$ has a Lipschitz local inverse $S$ near $\eta$. Moreover,
$\eta$ is transcendental, for otherwise $P(X)-\eta Q(X)$ would be a
nonzero polynomial with algebraic coefficients vanishing at $\xi$.

Choose real algebraic numbers $\alpha_k$ of degree $m$ such that
$\mathsf h(\alpha_k)\to\infty$ and
\[
0<|\xi-\alpha_k|\leq \exp\bigl(-k\mathsf h(\alpha_k)\bigr).
\]
For large $k$, the points $\alpha_k$ lie in a fixed neighborhood on which
$R$ is Lipschitz. By \eqref{eq:extension-degree} and
\eqref{eq:weil-heights},
\[
\deg R(\alpha_k)\leq em,
\qquad
\mathsf h(R(\alpha_k))
=d\mathsf h(\alpha_k)+O_R(1)\to\infty.
\]
Hence $R(\alpha_k)$ approximates $\eta$ with exponents tending to
infinity. Passing to a subsequence of constant degree $b\leq em$, we
obtain $w_b^*(\eta)=\infty$. Let
\[
j=\min\{\ell\geq1:w_\ell^*(\eta)=\infty\}.
\]
Then $\eta\in\mathscr U_j$ and $j\leq b\leq em$.

Conversely, take arbitrarily good algebraic approximants $\beta_k$ of
degree exactly $j$ to $\eta$ and put
$\widetilde{\alpha}_k=S(\beta_k)$ for all sufficiently large $k$. By
\eqref{eq:extension-degree},
\[
\deg\widetilde{\alpha}_k\leq edj,
\]
while the Lipschitz property of $S$ and
\[
\mathsf h(\beta_k)
=d\mathsf h(\widetilde{\alpha}_k)+O_R(1)
\]
show that $\widetilde{\alpha}_k$ approximates $\xi$ with exponents tending
to infinity. Passing again to a subsequence of constant degree, we obtain
$w_a^*(\xi)=\infty$ for some $a\leq edj$. Since $\xi\in\mathscr U_m$, the
minimality of $m$ gives $m\leq a$, and therefore
\[
\left\lceil\frac{m}{ed}\right\rceil\leq j\leq em.
\]

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:local-hilbert}
Let $F(T,X)\in\mathbb Q[T,X]$ be irreducible in $\mathbb Q(T)[X]$, with
$n=\deg_X F\geq 1$. Let $c\in\mathbb R$ and $0<\lambda<1$. Then there exist
rational numbers $t$ of arbitrarily large height such that
\[
  0<|t-c|\leq H(t)^{-\lambda}
\]
and $F(t,X)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ of degree $n$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By~\cite[Proposition~3.3.5]{Serre}, the exceptional specializations are
contained in a thin set $\Omega\subset\mathbf P^1(\mathbb Q)$, including the
zeros of the leading coefficient of $F$ in $X$. Serre's estimate
\cite[Proposition~3.4.2]{Serre} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thin-count}
  \#\{t\in\Omega\cap\mathbb Q:H(t)\leq B\}\ll_\Omega B.
\end{equation}

Choose $\lambda<\kappa<1$. For an integer $Q\geq 2$, let
$\mathcal T_Q$ consist of the reduced fractions $a/q$ such that
\[
  Q\leq q<2Q,
  \qquad c<\frac aq<c+Q^{-\kappa}.
\]
For each $q$, the elementary coprimality count gives
\[
  \#\{a\in\mathbb Z:cq<a<(c+Q^{-\kappa})q,\ \gcd(a,q)=1\}
  =Q^{-\kappa}\varphi(q)+O(\tau(q)),
\]
where $\varphi$ is Euler's totient function and $\tau$ counts the positive
divisors. We use the classical estimates
\[
  \sum_{q\leq x}\varphi(q)
  =\frac{3}{\pi^2}x^2+O(x\log x),
  \qquad
  \sum_{q\leq x}\tau(q)\ll x\log x;
\]
see~\cite[Chap.~3]{Apostol}. Summing the coprimality count over
$Q\leq q<2Q$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:many-rationals}
  \#\mathcal T_Q
  =\frac9{\pi^2}Q^{2-\kappa}+O(Q\log Q)
  \gg Q^{2-\kappa}.
\end{equation}

Every $t\in\mathcal T_Q$ satisfies
\[
  Q\leq H(t)\leq CQ,
  \qquad C=2(1+|c|).
\]
Thus~\eqref{eq:thin-count} excludes at most $O_{\Omega,c}(Q)$ elements
of $\mathcal T_Q$. Since $2-\kappa>1$, the count in
\eqref{eq:many-rationals} is larger for all sufficiently large $Q$.
We may therefore choose $t_Q\in\mathcal T_Q\setminus\Omega$.

By construction, $F(t_Q,X)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ of degree $n$.
Moreover,
\[
  0<(t_Q-c)H(t_Q)^\lambda
  \leq C^\lambda Q^{\lambda-\kappa}<1
\]
for all sufficiently large $Q$, since $\lambda<\kappa$. This gives the
required approximation, while $H(t_Q)\geq Q\to\infty$.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:gap}
Let $N\geq 1$, and let $z$ be a transcendental real number. Let
$(a_k)_{k\geq 1}$ be a sequence of real algebraic numbers of degree at
most $N$ converging to $z$, with $\mathsf h(a_k)\to\infty$. Suppose that
there are positive real numbers $u_k\to\infty$ and constants $C,A>0$
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-hypotheses}
  |z-a_k|\leq C\exp\bigl(-u_k\mathsf h(a_k)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \mathsf h(a_{k+1})\leq A u_k\mathsf h(a_k)
\end{equation}
for every $k\geq 1$. Then there are constants $c,E>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-conclusion}
  |z-\beta|\geq c\exp\bigl(-E\mathsf h(\beta)\bigr)
\end{equation}
for every real algebraic number $\beta$ of degree at most $N$ which is
distinct from every term of $(a_k)$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:conjugates}
For every integer $n\geq 1$, there is a real algebraic number $\theta$ of
degree $n$ whose minimal polynomial remains irreducible over $L$. For
any such $\theta$, with conjugates $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n$, the
functions in each of the two families
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conjugate-families}
  \bigl\{R^\sigma(X+\theta_i)\bigr\}_{\sigma,i},
  \qquad
  \bigl\{R^\sigma(X)-\theta_i\bigr\}_{\sigma,i}
\end{equation}
are pairwise distinct, where $\sigma$ ranges over the embeddings
$K\hookrightarrow L$ and $1\leq i\leq n$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Choose a rational prime $p$ unramified in $L$ and take the positive real
root $\theta=p^{1/n}$. At every prime of $L$ above $p$, the valuation of
$p$ is one. Thus $X^n-p$ is Eisenstein at such a prime and is irreducible
over $L$.

Now let $\theta$ be any number as in the statement, and let
$g\in\mathbb Q[X]$ be its monic minimal polynomial. We first show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conjugate-difference}
  \theta_j-\theta_i\in L\quad\Longrightarrow\quad i=j.
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $a=\theta_j-\theta_i\in L$, then $g(X)$ and $g(X+a)$ have
the common root $\theta_i$. Since $g$ is irreducible over $L$ and both
polynomials are monic of degree $n$, we obtain $g(X+a)=g(X)$.
Comparing the coefficients of $X^{n-1}$ gives $na=0$, so $a=0$.
The conjugates are distinct, and~\eqref{eq:conjugate-difference} follows.

We also need a simple observation about translations. A nonconstant
rational function has no nonzero additive period: a finite pole would
generate infinitely many poles, while a nonconstant polynomial cannot be
periodic. Suppose that $f,g\in L(X)$ are nonconstant and
$f(X+a)=g(X)$ for some $a\in\overline{\mathbb Q}$. For every
$\rho\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/L)$, we have
\[
  f(X+\rho(a))=g(X)=f(X+a).
\]
Hence $\rho(a)-a$ is an additive period of $f$, and therefore
$\rho(a)=a$. It follows that $a\in L$.

Suppose now that two members of the first family in
\eqref{eq:conjugate-families} coincide. After translating the variable,
this gives
\[
  R^\sigma(X)=R^\tau(X+\theta_j-\theta_i).
\]
The preceding observation implies $\theta_j-\theta_i\in L$.
By~\eqref{eq:conjugate-difference}, $i=j$, and then the minimality of
$K$ gives $\sigma=\tau$.

Finally, an equality in the second family gives
\[
  R^\sigma(X)-R^\tau(X)=\theta_i-\theta_j.
\]
The left-hand side belongs to $L(X)$ and is constant, so this constant
lies in $L$. Once again,~\eqref{eq:conjugate-difference} gives $i=j$,
and then $\sigma=\tau$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:translates}
Let $I$ be as above, let $m\geq 1$, and let $b\in I$. For every
$0<\sigma<1$, there are elements $\alpha\in A_m(I)$ of arbitrarily
large height such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:translate-height-distance}
  0<|b-\alpha|\leq\exp\bigl(-\sigma\mathsf h(\alpha)\bigr).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Choose $\theta$ of degree $m$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:conjugates}. Its
minimal polynomial remains irreducible over $L$, and hence over $K$.
Therefore
\[
  [K(\theta):\mathbb Q]
  =[K(\theta):K]\,[K:\mathbb Q]=em.
\]
Every embedding $\tau:K\hookrightarrow L$ extends to $m$ embeddings of
$K(\theta)$, sending $\theta$ to each of its conjugates
$\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_m$. For $t\in\mathbb Q$, the conjugate values of
$R(\theta+t)$ are consequently
\[
  R^\tau(\theta_i+t),
  \qquad \tau:K\hookrightarrow L,\quad 1\leq i\leq m.
\]

By Lemma~\ref{lem:conjugates}, the corresponding rational functions of
$t$ are pairwise distinct. Each equality between two of their values
therefore occurs at only finitely many specializations. Excluding these
specializations and the poles, we obtain a finite exceptional set
$\mathcal E\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}$ outside which all $em$ values are
finite and distinct. Since $R(\theta+t)\in K(\theta)$, this proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:translate-degrees}
  \deg(\theta+t)=m,
  \qquad \deg R(\theta+t)=em
  \qquad(t\in\mathbb Q\setminus\mathcal E).
\end{equation}
Here the first equality follows from $\mathbb Q(\theta+t)=\mathbb Q(\theta)$.

For every integer $q\geq 1$, set
\[
  t_q=\frac{\lfloor q(b-\theta)\rfloor+1}{q},
  \qquad \alpha_q=\theta+t_q.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:translate-distance}
  0<\alpha_q-b\leq q^{-1},
  \qquad H(t_q)\ll_{b,\theta}q.
\end{equation}
The first inequality is strict even when $q(b-\theta)$ is an integer.
Thus $t_q\to b-\theta$ without equality, so the finite set $\mathcal E$
is avoided for all sufficiently large $q$. Also, $\alpha_q\in I$
eventually. Hence $\alpha_q\in A_m(I)$ for all sufficiently large $q$.

By Northcott's theorem, $\mathsf h(\alpha_q)\to\infty$. Otherwise a
bounded-height subsequence would take values in a finite set, which is
impossible since $\alpha_q\to b$ and $\alpha_q\ne b$. On the other hand,
\eqref{eq:sum-height} and~\eqref{eq:translate-distance} give
\[
  \mathsf h(\alpha_q)
  \leq\mathsf h(\theta)+\log H(t_q)+\log2
  \leq\log q+O_{b,\theta}(1).
\]
For a suitable constant $C>0$, we therefore have
\[
  0<|\alpha_q-b|\exp\bigl(\sigma\mathsf h(\alpha_q)\bigr)
  \leq Cq^{\sigma-1}<1
\]
for all sufficiently large $q$, since $\sigma<1$. This proves
\eqref{eq:translate-height-distance}.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:generic-irreducible}
The polynomial $\Phi(T,X)$ is irreducible in $\mathbb Q(T)[X]$ and has degree
$edr$ in $X$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $x$ be transcendental over $E$, and set $\tau=R(x)-\vartheta$.
Since $R$ is nonconstant, $\tau$ is transcendental over $E$. The $er$
embeddings of $E(x)$ over $\mathbb Q(x)$ send $\tau$ to the values
\[
  R^\sigma(x)-\vartheta_i,
  \qquad \sigma:K\hookrightarrow L,\quad 1\leq i\leq r.
\]
These are pairwise distinct by Lemma~\ref{lem:conjugates}. Consequently,
\[
  [\mathbb Q(x,\tau):\mathbb Q(x)]=er=[E(x):\mathbb Q(x)].
\]
Together with the inclusion $\mathbb Q(x,\tau)\subseteq E(x)$, this proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primitive-function-field}
  \mathbb Q(x,\tau)=E(x).
\end{equation}

We now compute the degree over $\mathbb Q(\tau)$. Since
$E(\tau)=E(R(x))$, the degree formula for rational maps gives
$[E(x):E(\tau)]=d$. Also, adjoining the transcendental element $\tau$
preserves the degree of the constant field extension, so
$[E(\tau):\mathbb Q(\tau)]=[E:\mathbb Q]=er$. Using
\eqref{eq:primitive-function-field} and the tower law, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generic-degree}
  [\mathbb Q(\tau,x):\mathbb Q(\tau)]
  =[E(x):E(\tau)]\,[E(\tau):\mathbb Q(\tau)]
  =edr.
\end{equation}

By the definition of $\tau$, the factor
$P(x)-(\tau+\vartheta)Q(x)$ vanishes, and hence $\Phi(\tau,x)=0$.
The polynomial $\Phi(\tau,X)$ is nonzero, because $\tau$ is
transcendental over $\mathbb Q$, and its degree in $X$ is at most $edr$.
By~\eqref{eq:generic-degree}, the minimal polynomial of $x$ over
$\mathbb Q(\tau)$ has degree $edr$ and divides $\Phi(\tau,X)$. The two
polynomials are therefore scalar multiples of one another. Identifying
$\mathbb Q(T)$ with $\mathbb Q(\tau)$ proves both assertions.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:full-degree}
Let $x_0\in\mathbb R$ be neither a pole nor a critical point of $R$, and let
$r\geq 1$ be an integer. For every $0<\sigma<d$, there are real algebraic numbers
$\beta$, not poles of $R$ and of arbitrarily large height, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-degree-assertion}
\begin{gathered}
  \deg\beta=edr,\qquad \deg R(\beta)=r,\\
  0<|\beta-x_0|\leq\exp\bigl(-\sigma\mathsf h(\beta)\bigr).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For these numbers,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-degree-height}
  H(R(\beta))\asymp_{R,r}H(\beta)^{1/e}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Choose a bounded open interval $I$ containing $x_0$ whose closure contains
no pole or critical point of $R$, and let $S:R(I)\to I$ be the inverse
from~\eqref{eq:lipschitz}. Fix $0<\sigma<d$. With $\vartheta$ and
$\Phi$ as above, put $c=R(x_0)-\vartheta$ and choose
$\sigma/d<\lambda<1$. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:generic-irreducible} and~\ref{lem:local-hilbert},
there are rational numbers $t$ of arbitrarily large height such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t-approximation}
  0<|t-c|\leq H(t)^{-\lambda}
\end{equation}
and $\Phi(t,X)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ of degree $edr$.

Set $\delta=\vartheta+t$. Since $t\in\mathbb Q$, we have
$\mathbb Q(\delta)=\mathbb Q(\vartheta)$ and hence $\deg\delta=r$. Moreover,
\[
  0<|\delta-R(x_0)|=|t-c|\leq H(t)^{-\lambda}.
\]
Thus $\delta\to R(x_0)$, and $\delta\in R(I)$ for all sufficiently
large heights. Define $\beta=S(\delta)\in I$. Then $R(\beta)=\delta$
and $\beta\ne x_0$. The nonzero polynomial
$P(X)-\delta Q(X)$ vanishes at $\beta$, so $\beta$ is algebraic.
Furthermore,
\[
  P(\beta)-(t+\vartheta)Q(\beta)=0.
\]
This is one factor in the norm defining $\Phi(t,\beta)$, and therefore
$\Phi(t,\beta)=0$. Since $\Phi(t,X)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ of degree
$edr$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preimage-degrees}
  \deg\beta=edr,
  \qquad \deg R(\beta)=r.
\end{equation}
The exponent in~\eqref{eq:height-map} is $dr/(edr)=1/e$, which proves
\eqref{eq:full-degree-height}.

It remains to compare the approximation error with the height of $\beta$.
Apply~\eqref{eq:sum-height} to both $\delta=\vartheta+t$ and
$t=\delta-\vartheta$. Since $\mathsf h(t)=\log H(t)$, this bounds
$\mathsf h(\delta)-\log H(t)$ independently of $t$. Together with
\eqref{eq:weil-heights}, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preimage-parameter-height}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf h(\delta)&=\log H(t)+O_\vartheta(1),\\
  \mathsf h(\beta)&=\frac1d\log H(t)+O_{R,\vartheta}(1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\mathsf h(\beta)\to\infty$. The Lipschitz property of
$S$ and~\eqref{eq:t-approximation} give
\[
  0<|\beta-x_0|
  \leq c_0^{-1}|\delta-R(x_0)|
  \leq c_0^{-1}H(t)^{-\lambda}.
\]
Hence there is a constant $C>0$, independent of $t$, such that
\[
  0<|\beta-x_0|\exp\bigl(\sigma\mathsf h(\beta)\bigr)
  \leq C H(t)^{\sigma/d-\lambda}<1
\]
for all sufficiently large heights, since $\sigma/d<\lambda$.
This proves~\eqref{eq:full-degree-assertion}. The center $x_0$ is fixed
before the specializations are chosen; no height threshold uniform in
$x_0$ is required.

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
If $e>1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:local-spectrum} realizes the image type
$em>m$, so preservation requires $e=1$, or equivalently $R\in\mathbb Q(x)$.
If also $m,d\geq 2$, the same theorem realizes the type $m-1$, and
preservation again fails. Conversely, Proposition~\ref{prop:type-bounds}
gives preservation when $e=1$ and either $m=1$ or $d=1$. In the
M\"obius case, applying it also to $R^{-1}$ gives equality.
