<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:circle-area-kernels}
For $u>1$, $\beta>-1$ and $\lambda>\beta+2$,
\[
 \int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{|1-re^{i\theta}|^u}
       \le C(1-r)^{1-u},\qquad 0\le r<1,
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-area-kernel}
 \int_{\mathbb D}\frac{(1-|z|^2)^\beta}{|1-\overline wz|^\lambda}\,dA(z)
       \le C(1-|w|^2)^{\beta+2-\lambda},\qquad w\in\mathbb D.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
On $[-\pi,\pi]$, $|1-re^{i\theta}|\approx1-r+|\theta|$,
which proves the circle estimate by integration. For the area estimate,
angular integration reduces the question to
$\int_0^1(1-t)^\beta(1-|w|t)^{1-\lambda}\,dt$.
Use $1-|w|t\ge((1-|w|)+(1-t))/2$ and substitute
$u=(1-t)/(1-|w|)$. The resulting integral is finite because
$\beta>-1$ and $\lambda>\beta+2$; see also \cite{DurenSchuster2004}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:space-basics}\label{lem:F-dilations}
Each of $H^r$, $S^r$, $H(p,q,\alpha)$, $S(p,q,\alpha)$ and
$F(p,q,s)$, with the parameters specified above, is complete and
has the Fatou property: if $f_k\to f$ locally uniformly, then
$\|f\|\le\liminf_k\|f_k\|$, with infinity allowed.
For any of these spaces $X$, every $0<R<1$ and integer $m\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-local-interior}
 \max_{|z|\le R}|f^{(m)}(z)|\le C_{R,m,X}\|f\|_X.
\end{equation}
The dilation $C_\rho f(z)=f(\rho z)$ is a compact contraction on $X$
for $0<\rho<1$, and
\[
 \lim_{\rho\uparrow1}\sup_{\|f\|_X\le1}
       \max_{|z|\le R}|(f-C_\rho f)^{(m)}(z)|=0.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For $H^r$ and $H(p,q,\alpha)$, subharmonicity and the integral-mean
bound give local pointwise estimates; at an infinite exponent the
supremum bound suffices. For $F(p,q,s)$, the defining integral at
$a=0$ controls $f'$ in $L^p((1-|z|^2)^{q+s}dA)$ and hence locally
by subharmonicity. In a derivative space integrate from $0$ to bound
$f$ itself. Cauchy's formula then gives \eqref{eq:source-local-interior}.
Fatou's lemma in each defining integral, followed by the required
suprema, proves the Fatou property; the same assertion for a supremum
norm is immediate. A quasi-norm Cauchy sequence therefore has a local
analytic limit, and applying Fatou to its differences proves
completeness.

Monotonicity of integral means makes $C_\rho$ contractive on the Hardy
and mixed-norm spaces, and also on their derivative spaces since
$(C_\rho f)'=\rho f'(\rho\,\cdot)$. For $F(p,q,s)$, let $P_\rho$
be the Poisson kernel. Subharmonicity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-Poisson-dilation}
 |f'(\rho z)|^p\le\int_0^{2\pi}P_\rho(\theta)
                 |f'(e^{i\theta}z)|^p\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
Integrating against the weight defining the $F$ quasi-norm and
rotating $z$ replaces $a$ by $e^{i\theta}a$. Tonelli's theorem and
$\int P_\rho\,dm=1$, together with
$(C_\rho f)'=\rho f'(\rho\,\cdot)$, yield
\[
 \|C_\rho f\|_{F(p,q,s)}^p
 \le |f(0)|^p+\rho^p\bigl(\|f\|_{F(p,q,s)}^p-|f(0)|^p\bigr)
 \le \|f\|_{F(p,q,s)}^p.
\]
This uses subharmonicity and remains valid for $p<1$.

For later use, \eqref{eq:F-squares} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-bounded-derivative}
 \|h\|_{F(p,q,s)}\le C\bigl(|h(0)|+\|h'\|_\infty\bigr)
                        \quad\text{when }h'\in H^\infty,
\end{equation}
since the weighted area of $S(I)$ is at most $C|I|^{q+s+2}$.
A bounded sequence in any of the stated spaces has, by Montel and
Fatou, a locally convergent subsequence with its limit in that space.
After a fixed dilation, the functions and their first derivatives
converge uniformly on $\mathbb D$. The defining norms, or
\eqref{eq:F-bounded-derivative}, turn this into norm convergence.
Thus $C_\rho$ is compact. Finally,
$(f-C_\rho f)^{(m)}=f^{(m)}-\rho^mf^{(m)}(\rho\,\cdot)$;
\eqref{eq:source-local-interior} at orders $m$ and $m+1$ proves the
last assertion.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:essential-test}
Let $X$ be a quasi-Banach space and let $Y\subset H(\mathbb D)$ be a
complete quasi-normed space whose norm convergence implies local
uniform convergence and whose quasi-norm has the Fatou property.
Let $A:X\to Y$ be bounded. If $\sup_k\|f_k\|_X\le B<\infty$,
$B>0$, and $Af_k\to0$ locally uniformly, then
\[
 \limsup_k\|Af_k\|_Y\le C_Y B\|A\|_e.
\]
Here $C_Y$ depends only on the quasi-triangle constant of $Y$.
Compact operators into any complete quasi-normed space are closed in
operator quasi-norm; in particular, essential norm zero is equivalent
to compactness.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Choose a subsequence realizing the limit superior. For a compact
$K:X\to Y$, pass to a further subsequence with $Kf_k\to h$ in $Y$.
The local limit of $(A-K)f_k$ is $-h$, so
$\|h\|_Y\le\liminf_k\|(A-K)f_k\|_Y\le B\|A-K\|$.
Let $\kappa$ be a quasi-triangle constant of $Y$. Since $Kf_k\to h$,
$\limsup_k\|Kf_k\|_Y\le\kappa\|h\|_Y$. Hence
$\limsup_k\|Af_k\|_Y\le\kappa(1+\kappa)B\|A-K\|$.
Taking the infimum over $K$ proves the estimate.
For closedness, operator-quasi-norm approximation by compact maps
makes the image of the unit ball totally bounded; completeness makes
its closure compact.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:primitive-reduction}
Let $X\subset H(\mathbb D)$ and $Y$ be quasi-Banach spaces, and set
$S(X)=\{f\in H(\mathbb D):f'\in X\}$, with $\|f\|_{S(X)}=|f(0)|+\|f'\|_X$.
If a linear analytic formula $A$ on $S(X)$ annihilates constants,
then $A$ and $AJ:X\to Y$, where $Jg(z)=\int_0^zg(\zeta)\,d\zeta$,
are bounded simultaneously, with equal norms. They are compact
simultaneously and have equal essential norms whenever bounded.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Differentiation $\partial:S(X)\to X$ is contractive, $J$ is isometric,
and $\partial J=I$. Since $A=(AJ)\partial$, the two factorizations
give equality of norms and equivalence of compactness. Composing
compact approximants with $J$ or $\partial$ gives both inequalities
for the essential norms.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:F-little-approximation}
Let $0<p<\infty$, $q>-2$, $s>0$ and $q+s>-1$.
For $f\in F(p,q,s)$, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
 \renewcommand{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}
 \setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
 \item $f\in F_0(p,q,s)$.
 \item $\displaystyle\lim_{|I|\downarrow0}\frac{1}{|I|^s}
       \int_{S(I)}|f'(z)|^p(1-|z|^2)^{q+s}\,dA(z)=0$.
 \item $\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\uparrow1}
                         \|f-C_\rho f\|_{F(p,q,s)}=0$.
 \item $f$ is in the closure of the polynomials in $F(p,q,s)$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, $F_0(p,q,s)$ is closed and complete, and every
function analytic on a neighborhood of $\overline{\mathbb D}$ belongs
to it. Each $C_\rho$, $\rho<1$, maps $F(p,q,s)$ compactly into
$F_0(p,q,s)$. For $s=0$ (and hence $q>-1$), polynomials are dense
in $F(p,q,0)$ as well.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Put $d\mu_f(z)=|f'(z)|^p(1-|z|^2)^{q+s}\,dA(z)$. This is a finite
$s$-Carleson measure by \eqref{eq:F-squares}. The defining integral
for the little space is
\[
 \int_{\mathbb D}\frac{(1-|a|^2)^s}{|1-\overline az|^{2s}}\,d\mu_f(z).
\]
If it tends to zero as $|a|\uparrow1$, choose
$a=(1-|I|)\zeta_I$ as in the proof of \eqref{eq:F-squares}. The
kernel is at least $c|I|^{-s}$ on $S(I)$, proving (2).
Conversely, suppose (2) holds. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose
$h_0>0$ so that $\mu_f(S(I))\le\varepsilon|I|^s$ when
$|I|\le h_0$. For $h=1-|a|$ small, use the concentric dyadic squares
of lengths $h,2h,4h,\ldots$ from the proof of
\eqref{eq:F-squares}. The terms whose outer square has length at
most $h_0$ have sum at most $C\varepsilon\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-ks}$.
In the remaining region the kernel is at most
$C h^s h_0^{-2s}$, after changing the constant for the single
transition square. Its contribution tends to zero, since $\mu_f$
is finite. Letting first $|a|\uparrow1$ and then
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves (1).

The space described by (2) is closed. Indeed, the inequality
$|v+w|^p\le\max\{1,2^{p-1}\}(|v|^p+|w|^p)$ and
\eqref{eq:F-squares} give
\[
 \frac{\mu_f(S(I))}{|I|^s}
 \le C\|f-g\|_{F(p,q,s)}^p
               +C\frac{\mu_g(S(I))}{|I|^s}.
\]
Approximate $f$ in quasi-norm by members $g$ satisfying (2), and
then let $|I|\downarrow0$. Completeness follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:F-dilations}. If $f'$ is bounded, then
$\mu_f(S(I))/|I|^s\le C\|f'\|_\infty^p|I|^{q+2}\to0$.
This proves the assertion about functions analytic past the closed
disk, including the polynomials and all dilations $C_\rho f$.

Assume (2). Integrating \eqref{eq:F-Poisson-dilation} over $S(I)$
and rotating the variable gives
\[
 \frac{\mu_{C_\rho f}(S(I))}{|I|^s}
 \le\rho^p\int_0^{2\pi}P_\rho(\theta)
       \frac{\mu_f(S(e^{i\theta}I))}{|I|^s}\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Thus the small-arc bound in (2) holds uniformly for $C_\rho f$,
$0<\rho<1$. By the elementary inequality for a $p$th power, it also
makes $\mu_{f-C_\rho f}(S(I))/|I|^s$ uniformly small on sufficiently
short arcs.

We claim that $\mu_{f-C_\rho f}(\mathbb D)\to0$ as $\rho\uparrow1$.
On each compact disk this follows from local uniform convergence of
the derivatives. On $R<|z|<1$, monotonicity of integral means gives
\[
 M_p((f-C_\rho f)',t)^p
 \le C\bigl(M_p(f',t)^p+\rho^pM_p(f',\rho t)^p\bigr)
 \le C' M_p(f',t)^p.
\]
After integration in $t$ with the weight
$2t(1-t^2)^{q+s}$, the last bound is uniformly small for $R$ close
to $1$, because $\mu_f(\mathbb D)<\infty$. This proves the claim.
For arcs with $|I|\ge h_0$, we now have
\[
 \frac{\mu_{f-C_\rho f}(S(I))}{|I|^s}
       \le h_0^{-s}\mu_{f-C_\rho f}(\mathbb D)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Together with the small-arc bound and
$(f-C_\rho f)(0)=0$, equation \eqref{eq:F-squares} proves (3).

For fixed $\rho<1$, the Taylor polynomials of $C_\rho f$ and their
first derivatives converge uniformly on $\overline{\mathbb D}$.
Equation \eqref{eq:F-bounded-derivative} turns this into convergence
in $F(p,q,s)$. Hence (3) implies (4). Closedness and the polynomial
case already proved show that (4) implies (1). Finally,
$C_\rho:F(p,q,s)\to F(p,q,s)$ is compact by
Lemma~\ref{lem:F-dilations}, its range is in $F_0(p,q,s)$, and this
subspace is closed with the inherited quasi-norm. It is therefore
compact also with this smaller codomain.
For $s=0$ and $q>-1$, the same global weighted-integral argument,
which needs no small-square assumption, gives $\|f-C_\rho f\|_{F(p,q,0)}\to0$ directly.
Taylor approximation of $C_\rho f$ then proves polynomial density.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:two-kernels}
Fix $A>0$, positive integers $M$ and $n$, and $0<r<\infty$. For
$u=0,1$, put
\[
 g_{j,l,u}(z)=\frac{\delta_j^{M+u-A}}
                    {(1-\overline{a_{j,l}}z)^{M+u}}.
\]
Then, for $w\in Q_{j,l}$ and $x,y\in\mathbb C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-kernels}
 \frac{|x|^r}{\delta_j^{r(A+n-1)}}
 +\frac{|y|^r}{\delta_j^{r(A+n)}}
 \le C\sum_{u=0}^1
       |xg_{j,l,u}^{(n-1)}(w)+yg_{j,l,u}^{(n)}(w)|^r.
\end{equation}
The constant is independent of $j,l,w,x,y$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Write $a=a_{j,l}$ and $\delta=\delta_j$. If $(b)_k=b(b+1)\cdots(b+k-1)$,
with $(b)_0=1$, then
\[
 g_{j,l,u}^{(n-1)}(w)
 =\frac{(M+u)_{n-1}\overline a^{\,n-1}\delta^{M+u-A}}
             {(1-\overline aw)^{M+u+n-1}},
 \qquad
 g_{j,l,u}^{(n)}(w)
 =\frac{(M+u+n-1)\overline a}{1-\overline aw}
       g_{j,l,u}^{(n-1)}(w).
\]
Since $|a|\ge1/4$ and $|1-\overline aw|\approx\delta$ on $Q_{j,l}$,
we have $|\delta^{A+n-1}g_{j,l,u}^{(n-1)}(w)|\approx1$.
Set $x_0=\delta^{-A-n+1}x$, $y_0=\delta^{-A-n}y$, and
$\gamma=\overline a\delta/(1-\overline aw)$. Both $|\gamma|$ and
$|\gamma|^{-1}$ are bounded by fixed constants. After multiplication by
factors whose moduli are bounded above and below, the two expressions
on the right of \eqref{eq:two-kernels} become
$x_0+(M+n-1)\gamma y_0$ and $x_0+(M+n)\gamma y_0$. Their difference is
$\gamma y_0$, which first controls $y_0$ and then $x_0$. Taking $r$th powers
proves the assertion, using
$|v+w|^r\le\max\{1,2^{r-1}\}(|v|^r+|w|^r)$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:compact-inclusion}
Let $0<p,q\le\infty$ and $0<\alpha<1$. The inclusion
$S(p,q,\alpha)\to H^p$, equal to $D_{1,\operatorname{id}}^0$, is compact.
For $1\le p\le\infty$ it also satisfies
\[
 \|f\|_{H^p}\le |f(0)|+\frac{\|f'\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)}}{1-\alpha},
 \qquad f\in S(p,q,\alpha).
\]
If, in addition, $p<\infty$, $1<q\le\infty$, $1/p+1/q\le1$ and
$\alpha+1/p>1$, then $\phi\equiv1$ and $\varphi(z)=z$ satisfy all
hypotheses of \cite[Theorem~3.1]{Borgohain2026}, but not its asserted
necessary integral condition. In particular, $p=q=2$ and $\alpha=3/4$
give a counterexample in that range.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For $1\le p\le\infty$, radial integration and Minkowski's inequality
(the supremum inequality when $p=\infty$) give
\[
 M_p(f,r)\le |f(0)|+\int_0^rM_p(f',t)\,dt
 \le |f(0)|+\|f'\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)}\int_0^r(1-t)^{-\alpha}\,dt.
\]
This proves the stated norm bound. Integrating instead from $r_J$
gives
$\|f\|_{H^p}\le M_p(f,r_J)+(1-\alpha)^{-1}
2^{-J(1-\alpha)}\|f'\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)}$.

For $0<p<1$, put
$A_j(\theta)=\sup_{r_j\le u\le r_{j+1}}|f'(ue^{i\theta})|$.
The Poisson inequality on the circle of radius $r_{j+2}$ gives
\[
 \int_0^{2\pi}A_j(\theta)^p\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
       \le C M_p(f',r_{j+2})^p.
\]
Indeed, for $u\in[r_j,r_{j+1}]$, the kernels $P_{u/r_{j+2}}$
are bounded by a fixed multiple of $P_{r_{j+1}/r_{j+2}}$:
their distances from $1$ are comparable. This also holds for the
initial interval, whose ratios lie in $[0,2/3]$.
The radial increment over $[r_j,r_{j+1}]$, or over a subinterval,
is bounded by $\delta_j A_j(\theta)/2$. Subadditivity of the $p$th
power, followed by \eqref{eq:mean-growth}, therefore yields
\[
 \|f\|_{H^p}^p\le M_p(f,r_J)^p+
 C\|f'\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)}^p\sum_{j\ge J}2^{-jp(1-\alpha)}
 \le M_p(f,r_J)^p+C_{p,\alpha}2^{-Jp(1-\alpha)}
                      \|f'\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)}^p.
\]
For radii below $r_J$, the first term suffices by monotonicity of
integral means. Taking $J=0$ also proves boundedness.

For every $p$, a bounded source sequence has a locally uniformly
convergent subsequence with limit in the source, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:space-basics}. Apply the appropriate tail estimate to
the difference from its limit. For fixed $J$ the integral mean at
$r_J$ tends to zero, while the remaining term tends uniformly to zero
as $J\to\infty$. Thus the subsequence converges in $H^p$, proving
compactness.

Under the additional hypotheses, $\alpha p+1-p>0$. For the indicated
symbols, the proposed necessary quantity is
$2\pi\sup_{t<1}(1-t^2)^{-(\alpha p+1-p)}=\infty$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:radial-majorant}
Let $0<p<\infty$, $0<q\le\infty$ and $\alpha>0$ satisfy
$\alpha+1/p>1$. Let $\phi\in H(\mathbb D)$ and let $\varphi$ be an analytic
self-map of $\mathbb D$. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
 \renewcommand{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}
 \setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
 \setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
 \item There is a nonnegative Borel function $G$ on $\mathbb D$ such that
 \[
  \sup_{0\le r<1}\int_0^{2\pi}G(re^{i\theta})^p\,d\theta<\infty,
 \]
 and $|D_{\phi,\varphi}^{0}f(z)|\le G(z)$ for every $z\in\mathbb D$ and
 every $f\in S(p,q,\alpha)$ with $\|f\|_{S(p,q,\alpha)}\le1$.
 \item The symbols satisfy
 \[
  \sup_{0\le r<1}\int_0^{2\pi}
  \frac{|\phi(re^{i\theta})|^p}
  {(1-|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|^2)^{\alpha p+1-p}}\,d\theta<\infty.
 \]
\end{enumerate}
Either condition implies that
$D_{\phi,\varphi}^{0}:S(p,q,\alpha)\to H^p$ is bounded. The converse fails in general,
even when the operator is compact.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Set $b=\alpha+1/p>1$. We first establish the point-evaluation estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point-evaluation}
 \sup_{\|f\|_{S(p,q,\alpha)}\le1}|f(w)|
 \approx\frac{1}{(1-|w|^2)^{b-1}},
 \qquad w\in\mathbb D.
\end{equation}
For the upper bound, take $g\in H(p,q,\alpha)$, $z\in\mathbb D$ and
$R=(1+|z|)/2$. The Poisson estimate for the subharmonic function $|g|^p$
(see \cite[Chapter~1]{Duren1970}) and \eqref{eq:mean-growth} give
\[
 |g(z)|^p\le\frac{R+|z|}{R-|z|}M_p(g,R)^p
 \le C\frac{\|g\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)}^p}{(1-|z|)^{\alpha p+1}}.
\]
Applying this to $g=f'$ and integrating along a radius, we obtain
\[
 |f(w)| \le |f(0)|+C\|f'\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)} \int_0^{|w|}(1-t)^{-b}\,dt \le
 C\|f\|_{S(p,q,\alpha)}(1-|w|)^{1-b} \le
 C\frac{\|f\|_{S(p,q,\alpha)}}{(1-|w|^2)^{b-1}}.
\]
The last inequality uses $1-|w|^2\le2(1-|w|)$.

For the lower bound, consider
\[
 f_w(z)=\frac{1-|w|^2}{(1-\overline w z)^b},
 \qquad
 f_w'(z)=\frac{b\overline w(1-|w|^2)}{(1-\overline w z)^{b+1}}.
\]
The analytic power is chosen to equal $1$ at $z=0$. We verify directly
that $\sup_{w\in\mathbb D}\|f_w\|_{S(p,q,\alpha)}<\infty$; these are the
usual mixed-norm kernel estimates, as in
\cite[Proposition~2]{Arevalo2015} and \cite{ShieldsWilliams1971}.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:circle-area-kernels}, the circle integral
has the required bound for every exponent greater than one.
With $\gamma=p(b+1)>1$, this gives
$M_p(f_w',r)\le C(1-|w|^2)(1-r|w|)^{-\alpha-1}$.
Suppose first that $q<\infty$. Since
$1-r|w|\ge((1-r)+(1-|w|))/2$, the substitution
$u=(1-r)/(1-|w|)$ gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|f_w'\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)}^q
 &\le C(1-|w|^2)^q\int_0^1
     \frac{(1-r)^{\alpha q-1}}{(1-r|w|)^{(\alpha+1)q}}\,dr\\
 &\le C\left(\frac{1-|w|^2}{1-|w|}\right)^q
       \int_0^\infty\frac{u^{\alpha q-1}}{(1+u)^{(\alpha+1)q}}\,du
 \le C.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last integral is finite because $\alpha q>0$ and $q>0$.
For $q=\infty$, the same integral-mean estimate yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|f_w'\|_{H(p,\infty,\alpha)}
 &\le C\sup_{0\le r<1}
       \left(\frac{1-r}{1-r|w|}\right)^\alpha
       \frac{1-|w|^2}{1-r|w|}
 \le 2C,
 \end{aligned}
\]
since $1-r\le1-r|w|$ and $1-|w|^2\le2(1-r|w|)$.
Also $|f_w(0)|=1-|w|^2\le1$, so the asserted uniform bound follows.

 G(z)\ge |\phi(z)|\sup_{\|f\|_{S(p,q,\alpha)}\le1}|f(\varphi(z))|
 \ge c\frac{|\phi(z)|}{(1-|\varphi(z)|^2)^{b-1}}.
\]
Raising this inequality to the power $p$ and integrating on each circle

Conversely, assume (2) and let $C$ be the upper-bound constant in
\eqref{eq:point-evaluation}. The continuous nonnegative function

This proves boundedness. Finally,

\label{lem:discrete-estimates}
Let $0<p,q\le\infty$, $\alpha>0$ and $n\ge0$ be an integer. Then
\[
 \left\|\left\{\delta_j^{\alpha+1/p+n}
          \sup_{w\in Q_{j,l}}|f^{(n)}(w)|\right\}_{j,l}\right\|_{\ell^{p,q}}

       \frac{\delta_j^{M-\alpha-1/p}}{(1-\overline{a_{j,l}}z)^M}

Suppose first that $p<\infty$. Let $Q_{j,l}^*$ be the Euclidean
$\delta_j/16$-neighborhood of $Q_{j,l}$. It lies in $\mathbb D$.
Cauchy's formula, followed by the submean inequality for $|f|^p$, gives
\[

 \le C\delta_j^{-np-2}\int_{Q_{j,l}^*}|f(z)|^p\,dA(z).
\]
Indeed, one can first use Cauchy's
formula on the circle of radius $\delta_j/64$ about $w$, and then bound
$|f|^p$ on that circle by its averages over disks of radius
$\delta_j/64$, all contained in $Q_{j,l}^*$. At each fixed $j$ these

most $C\delta_j$, inside $|z|<r_{j+2}$. Consequently,
\[

 \le C\delta_j^{\alpha p}M_p(f,r_{j+2})^p.
\]
If $p=\infty$, Cauchy's formula gives instead

 \le C\delta_j^\alpha M_\infty(f,r_{j+2})$.

 M_p(F_j,t)^p

 \le C(d+\delta_j)^{1-M}.

 M_p(F_j,t)
 \le C u_j\delta_j^{M-\alpha-1}(d+\delta_j)^{1-M}.

 M_p(F_j,t)

 k}2^{-(k-j)\alpha\tau}u_j^\tau +C\sum_{j>k}2^{-(j-k)(M-\alpha-1/\tau)\tau}u_j^\tau.
\]
Both coefficient sequences decay geometrically. Convolution with either

 \|F_c\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)}

 \le C\|u\|_{\ell^q}.

 \le C\delta_j^{-(1/\tau-1/p)}u_j.

 \le C_{R,m}2^{-J(M-\alpha-1/\tau)},
\]
because $u_j\le1$. This completes the proof.

\label{lem:measure-embedding}
Let $0<p,q\le\infty$, $0<r<\infty$, $\alpha>0$, and let $n\ge0$ be

 \approx\|d\|_{\ell^{P,Q}}.
\end{equation}

 \ge c_0\|\{d_{j,l}:j\ge J\}\|_{\ell^{P,Q}},
\]
where $c_0>0$ depends only on the fixed parameters. The notation for the

\label{thm:mixed-hardy}
Let $0<p,q\le\infty$, $0<r<\infty$, $\alpha>0$ and $n\ge0$ be an
integer. Let $\phi\in H(\mathbb D)$ and let $\varphi$ be an analytic
self-map of $\mathbb D$. Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
 \renewcommand{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}
 \setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
 \item $D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}:H(p,q,\alpha)\to H^r$ is bounded.
 \item The following condition holds in the applicable parameter range:
 \[
 \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle\sup_{t,j,l} d_{t,j,l}^{(n)}<\infty,&p\le r,\ q\le r,\\[1mm]
  \displaystyle\sup_t\left[\sum_j(\sup_l d_{t,j,l}^{(n)})^Q\right]^{1/Q}<\infty,
                                 &p\le r<q,\\[1mm]
  \displaystyle\sup_{t,j}\left(\sum_l \bigl(d_{t,j,l}^{(n)}\bigr)^P\right)^{1/P}<\infty,
                                 &q\le r<p,\\[1mm]
  \displaystyle\sup_t\left[\sum_j
           \left(\sum_l \bigl(d_{t,j,l}^{(n)}\bigr)^P\right)^{Q/P}\right]^{1/Q}<\infty,
                                 &r<\min\{p,q\}.
 \end{cases}
 \]
 Here $0\le t<1$. In the rows where they are used,
 $P=p/(p-r)$ and $Q=q/(q-r)$, with $P=1$ for $p=\infty$
 and $Q=1$ for $q=\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
When these conditions hold,
\[
 \|D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)\to H^r}^{\,r}
 \approx\sup_{0\le t<1}\|\{d_{t,j,l}^{(n)}\}_{j,l}\|_{\ell^{P,Q}},
\]
and
\[
 \|D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}\|_{e,H(p,q,\alpha)\to H^r}^{\,r}
 \approx\lim_{J\to\infty}\sup_{0\le t<1}
       \|\{d_{t,j,l}^{(n)}:j\ge J\}\|_{\ell^{P,Q}}.
\]
In particular, the bounded operator is compact if and only if the last
limit is zero. All comparison constants depend only on
$p,q,r,\alpha,n$ and the fixed partition, not on $\phi$ or $\varphi$.

$f_\rho=C_\rho f$ has bounded derivatives on $\mathbb D$.
Thus $\phi(f_\rho^{(n)}\circ\varphi)\in H^r$, and its boundary
values give \eqref{eq:boundary-norm-identity} with $f_\rho$ in place
of $f$. The measure-embedding lemma and the contraction property of
$C_\rho$ yield
\[
 \|D_{\phi,\varphi}^nf_\rho\|_{H^r}^r
     \le C\|d_{\partial}^{(n)}\|_{\ell^{P,Q}}
                                      \|f\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)}^r.
\]
Letting $\rho\uparrow1$ and applying the Hardy Fatou inequality proves
boundedness for the undilated functions.

For the essential norm, put
$L_J=\|\{d_{\partial,j,l}^{(n)}:j\ge J\}\|_{\ell^{P,Q}}$ and
$L=\lim_JL_J$. The operators $D_{\phi,\varphi}^nC_\rho$ are compact
by the dilation argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-hardy}.
For $\|f\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)}\le1$, split the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:boundary-norm-identity}, applied to $f-C_\rho f$, at
$|w|=r_J$. On the inner disk the derivatives tend uniformly to zero,
and $\mu_{\partial}$ is finite. On the outer part,
Lemma~\ref{lem:measure-embedding} bounds the integral by $CL_J$.
Taking first $\rho\uparrow1$ and then $J\to\infty$ gives
$\|D_{\phi,\varphi}^n\|_e^r\le CL$.

If $L>0$, the tail assertion of that lemma supplies functions $f_J$
with uniformly bounded source quasi-norms, tending locally uniformly
to zero with their fixed derivatives, and satisfying
\[
 \|D_{\phi,\varphi}^nf_J\|_{H^r}^r\ge cL_J\ge cL.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:essential-test} gives the reverse essential-norm bound.
Closedness of compact operators gives the compactness criterion.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:mixed-derivative}
Let $0<p,q\le\infty$, $0<r<\infty$, $\alpha>0$ and $n\ge1$ be an
integer. Let $\phi\in H(\mathbb D)$ and let $\varphi$ be an analytic
self-map of $\mathbb D$. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
 \renewcommand{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}
 \setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
 \item $D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}:S(p,q,\alpha)\to S^r$ is bounded.
 \item The following condition holds in the applicable parameter range:
 \[
 \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle\sup_{t,j,l} v_{t,j,l}<\infty,&p\le r,\ q\le r,\\[1mm]
  \displaystyle\sup_t\left[\sum_j(\sup_l v_{t,j,l})^Q\right]^{1/Q}<\infty,
                                 &p\le r<q,\\[1mm]
  \displaystyle\sup_{t,j}\left(\sum_l v_{t,j,l}^P\right)^{1/P}<\infty,
                                 &q\le r<p,\\[1mm]
  \displaystyle\sup_t\left[\sum_j
           \left(\sum_l v_{t,j,l}^P\right)^{Q/P}\right]^{1/Q}<\infty,
                                 &r<\min\{p,q\}.
 \end{cases}
 \]
 Here $0\le t<1$. In the rows where they are used,
 $P=p/(p-r)$ and $Q=q/(q-r)$, with $P=1$ for $p=\infty$
 and $Q=1$ for $q=\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
When these conditions hold,
\[
 \|D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}\|_{S(p,q,\alpha)\to S^r}^{\,r}
 \approx
 \frac{|\phi(0)|^r}{(1-|\varphi(0)|^2)^{rb_n}}
       +\sup_{0\le t<1}\|v_t\|_{\ell^{P,Q}},
\]
and
\[
 \|D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}\|_{e,S(p,q,\alpha)\to S^r}^{\,r}
 \approx\lim_{J\to\infty}\sup_{0\le t<1}
             \|\{v_{t,j,l}:j\ge J\}\|_{\ell^{P,Q}}.
\]
Thus the bounded operator is compact if and only if the last limit is
zero. All comparison constants depend only on the fixed parameters and
the partition, not on the two symbols.

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
We first record the derivative point-evaluation estimate
\[
 \sup_{\|f\|_{S(p,q,\alpha)}\le1}|f^{(n)}(w)|
       \approx(1-|w|^2)^{-b_n},\qquad w\in\mathbb D.
\]
The upper bound follows by applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:discrete-estimates} to $f'$ at order $n-1$.
For the lower bound, take the primitive of a single kernel from that
lemma in the rectangle containing $w$. Its source quasi-norm is bounded,
and its $n$th derivative at $w$ has modulus comparable to
$\delta_j^{-b_n}\approx(1-|w|^2)^{-b_n}$.
Consequently the
norm of the scalar map $f\mapsto\phi(0)f^{(n)}(\varphi(0))$ is
comparable to $|\phi(0)|(1-|\varphi(0)|^2)^{-b_n}$.

Suppose (2) holds. At a fixed $t$, use the pull-back measures obtained
from $|\phi'|^r\,d\theta/(2\pi)$ and
$|\phi\varphi'|^r\,d\theta/(2\pi)$ on the circle of radius $t$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:measure-embedding} to $f'$ with orders $n-1$ and
$n$, respectively. Each associated sequence is bounded coordinatewise
by $v_t$. The derivative formula and the elementary inequality for the
$r$th power of a sum therefore give
\[
 M_r((D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}f)',t)^r
       \le C\|v_t\|_{\ell^{P,Q}}
                    \|f'\|_{H(p,q,\alpha)}^r.
\]
Together with the scalar estimate, this proves boundedness and the
upper operator-norm estimate.

For the converse, suppose (1) holds. Fix $A=\alpha+1/p$ and an integer
$M>\alpha+1/\min\{1,p\}$, and for finitely supported $c$ define
\[
 f_{c,u}(z)=\int_0^z\sum_{j,l}c_{j,l}g_{j,l,u}(\zeta)\,d\zeta,
                  \qquad u=0,1.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:discrete-estimates}, applied with $M$ and $M+1$, shows
that $\|f_{c,u}\|_{S(p,q,\alpha)}\le C\|c\|_{\ell^{p,q}}$.
Let $\varepsilon_{j,l}$ be independent random signs and write
$\varepsilon c=\{\varepsilon_{j,l}c_{j,l}\}$. For each fixed $t$,
Khintchine's inequality and \eqref{eq:two-kernels} give
\[
 \sum_{j,l}v_{t,j,l}|c_{j,l}|^r \le C\sum_{u=0}^1\mathbb E
 M_r((D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}f_{\varepsilon c,u})',t)^r \le
 C\|D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}\|^r\|c\|_{\ell^{p,q}}^r.
\]
To justify the first inequality, apply Khintchine pointwise, retain the
$(j,l)$th summand only on
$\{\theta:\varphi(te^{i\theta})\in Q_{j,l}\}$, and add over the
disjoint sets. Then apply \eqref{eq:two-kernels} with
$x=\phi'(te^{i\theta})$ and
$y=\phi(te^{i\theta})\varphi'(te^{i\theta})$.
The kernels are fixed before integration. Taking the supremum in
\eqref{eq:sequence-duality} and then over $t$ proves (2). The scalar
estimate gives the remaining part of the lower norm bound.

Assume henceforth that the operator is bounded, and put
\[
 L_J=\sup_{0\le t<1}\|\{v_{t,j,l}:j\ge J\}\|_{\ell^{P,Q}},
                 \qquad L=\lim_{J\to\infty}L_J.
\]
The limit exists and is finite. Testing on $z^n/n!$ and
$z^{n+1}/(n+1)!$ gives $\phi,\phi\varphi\in S^r$. Hence
$\phi'\in H^r$ and
$\phi\varphi'=(\phi\varphi)'-\phi'\varphi\in H^r$.
In particular, the total masses of both pull-back measures are bounded
uniformly in $t$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:space-basics} makes
$D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}C_\rho$ compact for $\rho<1$.
For $f$ in the unit ball,
set $h_\rho=f-C_\rho f$. Its $S(p,q,\alpha)$ quasi-norm is uniformly
bounded, and its fixed derivatives tend uniformly to zero on every
compact disk, uniformly over that unit ball. Split the two derivative
integrals into $|\varphi(te^{i\theta})|<r_J$ and its complement.
On the first set, their integrals tend to zero as $\rho\uparrow1$,
uniformly in $t$, by the total-mass bounds. The scalar value at zero
also tends to zero. On the second set,
Lemma~\ref{lem:measure-embedding}, applied to the restricted measures,
bounds the sum of the integrals by $CL_J$. Therefore
\[
 \|D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}\|_e^r
 \le\limsup_{\rho\uparrow1}
       \|D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}(I-C_\rho)\|^r
 \le CL_J.
\]
Letting $J\to\infty$ proves the upper essential-norm estimate.

For the lower bound, suppose $L>0$. Choose $t_J$ such that its tail
norm is at least $L_J/2$. By \eqref{eq:sequence-duality}, choose
finitely supported $c$ with $\|c\|_{\ell^{p,q}}\le1$, supported in
$j\ge J$, for which
$\sum v_{t_J,j,l}|c_{j,l}|^r\ge L_J/4$.
The same random-sign estimate as above, now integrated only where
$|\varphi(t_Je^{i\theta})|\ge r_J$, selects one value of $u$ and one
choice of signs giving a function $f_J$ such that
\[
 \|f_J\|_{S(p,q,\alpha)}\le C_0,
       \qquad \|D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}f_J\|_{S^r}^r\ge cL_J\ge cL.
\]
The last estimate in Lemma~\ref{lem:discrete-estimates} shows that
$f_J$ and its fixed derivatives tend locally uniformly to zero.
The same is true of $D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}f_J$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:essential-test}, with target $S^r$, now gives
$L^{1/r}\le C\|D_{\phi,\varphi}^n\|_e$. The upper bound and closedness
of compact operators complete the proof.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:positive-Carleson-kernel}
Let $s\ge0$, $\beta>-1$, $\beta+2>s$ and $\lambda>\beta+2$.
If $\|\mu\|_s<\infty$, define a positive measure $\nu$ by
\[
 d\nu(z)=(1-|z|^2)^\beta
       \left(\int_{\mathbb D}
          \frac{(1-|w|^2)^{\lambda-\beta-2}}
               {|1-\overline wz|^\lambda}\,d\mu(w)\right)dA(z).
\]
Then $\|\nu\|_s\le C\|\mu\|_s$.

<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
For $s=0$, integrate over $\mathbb D$ and apply
\eqref{eq:weighted-area-kernel}; the result is
$\nu(\mathbb D)\le C\mu(\mathbb D)$. Suppose $s>0$ and fix an arc
$I$ of length $h<1/4$. Let $I_k$ be its concentric dilate of length
$\min\{2^k h,1\}$, and stop when this is the full circle.
For $w\in S(I_2)$, use \eqref{eq:weighted-area-kernel} to bound the
inner $z$-integral over $S(I)$ by its integral over $\mathbb D$.
After multiplication by $(1-|w|^2)^{\lambda-\beta-2}$, this contribution
is at most $C\mu(S(I_2))\le C\|\mu\|_s h^s$.
For $w\in S(I_{k+1})\setminus S(I_k)$, $k\ge2$, and $z\in S(I)$,
the geometry of the squares gives
$|1-\overline wz|\ge c2^k h$ and $1-|w|^2\le C2^k h$.
Since $\lambda-\beta-2>0$ and
$\int_{S(I)}(1-|z|^2)^\beta\,dA(z)\le C h^{\beta+2}$,
the contribution from this difference is at most

 C h^{\beta+2}(2^k h)^{-\beta-2}
                  \mu(S(I_{k+1}))
 \le C\|\mu\|_s h^s\,2^{-k(\beta+2-s)}.
\]
The series converges because $\beta+2>s$. For $h\ge1/4$, the global
bound from \eqref{eq:weighted-area-kernel} suffices. This proves the
lemma. All interchanges are between nonnegative integrands.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:F-sampling-synthesis}
Let $0<p<\infty$, $-2<q<\infty$, $0\le s<\infty$, $q+s>-1$, and put $A=(q+2)/p$.
For each integer $m\ge1$ and $f\in F(p,q,s)$,
\[
 \left\|\left\{\delta_j^{A+m-1}
          \sup_{w\in Q_{j,l}}|f^{(m)}(w)|\right\}_{j,l}
       \right\|_{\mathcal E_{p,s}}
       \le C\|f\|_{F(p,q,s)}.
\]
Choose a positive integer $M$ such that $M>A+1$ and
$Mp>q+s+2+2\max\{p-1,0\}$. For every finitely supported $c$, put
\[
 G_c(z)=\sum_{j,l}c_{j,l}
            \frac{\delta_j^{M-A}}{(1-\overline{a_{j,l}}z)^M},
            \qquad f_c(z)=\int_0^zG_c(\zeta)\,d\zeta.
\]
Then $\|f_c\|_{F(p,q,s)}\le C\|c\|_{\mathcal E_{p,s}}$.
If the coefficients vanish for $j<J$ and
$\|c\|_{\mathcal E_{p,s}}\le1$, then, for each fixed $R<1$ and integer
$k\ge0$,
\[
 \max_{|z|\le R}|G_c^{(k)}(z)|
                   \le C_{R,k}2^{-J(M-A-1)}.
\]

\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-derivative-evaluation}
 \sup_{\|f\|_{F(p,q,s)}\le1}|f^{(m)}(w)|
       \approx(1-|w|^2)^{-A-m+1},\qquad w\in\mathbb D.
\end{equation}

Lemma~\ref{lem:discrete-estimates} to $f'$ with order $m-1$.

The sets $Q_{j,l}^*$ have bounded overlap, also when all rows are
included: at a given point their row sizes are comparable to its
distance from the boundary. If $a_{j,l}\in S(I)$, then $Q_{j,l}^*$ is

Summing the displayed inequality and using \eqref{eq:F-squares}

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:explicit-sequence}
For every $0<p,r<\infty$, $s\ge0$ and nonnegative array $v$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sequence-explicit-equivalence}
       \mathfrak D_{p,s;r}(v)^r\approx\mathcal C_{p,s;r}(v),
\end{equation}
with constants independent of $v$ and of finite truncations.
The equality in the cases $s=0$ is exact.
For $0<s\le1$ and $r\ge p$, the finite-depth integrand in
\eqref{eq:explicit-sequence-functional} is the least dyadic
$s$-covering cost of the arcs satisfying $v_I/b_I>t$.
In particular, when $s=1$ and $r\ge p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-shadow-maximal}
 \mathcal C_{p,1;r}(v)
     =\int_{\partial\mathbb D}
        \sup_{\substack{j,l\ge0\,;\ I_{j,l}\ni\zeta}}
                    \frac{v_{j,l}}{|I_{j,l}|}\,dm(\zeta),
\end{equation}
where $l$ always has its prescribed finite range in row $j$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
The cases $s=0$ follow from the sequence duality already proved.
For $s>1$, the coordinate estimate gives
$\|c\|_{\ell^\infty}\le C\|c\|_{\mathcal E_{p,s}}$.
Conversely, each square $S(I)$ contains at most $C|I|/\delta_j$
marked points from a row with $\delta_j\le C|I|$, so
\[
 \sum_{a_{j,l}\in S(I)}\delta_j^s
    \le C|I|\sum_{\delta_j\le C|I|}\delta_j^{s-1}
    \le C'|I|^s.
\]
Thus $\mathcal E_{p,s}$ has quasi-norm equivalent to
$\ell^\infty$, and its multiplier expression is comparable to
$\sum v_{j,l}$.

We turn to $0<s\le1$. Attach $c_{j,l}$ to $I_{j,l}$, with zero
coefficients on the first three levels. The geometric comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tree-Carleson-comparison}
 \|c\|_{\mathcal E_{p,s}}^p\approx
   \sup_{I\in\mathcal T}\frac{1}{|I|^s}
         \sum_{J\subseteq I}|c_J|^p|J|^s
\end{equation}
has constants independent of the support. Indeed, all marked points
attached to descendants of $I$ lie in a square over a concentric arc
of length at most $C|I|$, or in the full disk. This gives one
inequality, since $\delta_j=8|I_{j,l}|$.
Conversely, if $a_{j,l}\in S(K)$ for an arbitrary arc $K$ of length
$h$, then $1-|a_{j,l}|=3\delta_j/4\le h$, so
$|I_{j,l}|\le h/6$. Choose a dyadic length between $h$ and $2h$
(or the full circle when $h>1/2$). The ancestor of $I_{j,l}$ at that
level intersects $K$, and at most three arcs at that level can do so.
Their subtree bounds control the square over $K$. This proves the
other inequality.

It suffices, therefore, to work on a finite dyadic tree with the
constraints
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tree-mass-constraints}
 x_I\ge0,\qquad \sum_{J\subseteq I}x_J\le b_I\quad(I\in\mathcal T),
       \qquad x_I=|c_I|^p b_I.
\end{equation}
Coordinates on the first three levels, and coordinates with $v_I=0$,
may be set to zero. The quantity to be evaluated is the maximum of
$\sum_I v_I(x_I/b_I)^\theta$ under these constraints.
The maximum exists because the feasible set is a finite compact
polytope. All constants obtained below are independent of its depth.

Suppose first that $\theta\ge1$, and put $h_I=v_I/b_I$.
For a collection $\mathcal A$ of nodes, let its covering cost be the
minimum of $\sum_{K\in\mathcal B}b_K$ over collections of dyadic
ancestors such that every $I\in\mathcal A$ is contained in some
$K\in\mathcal B$. Redundant nested covering arcs can be removed.
For the set $\{I:h_I>t\}$, this cost is
$R_{\partial\mathbb D}^{(N)}(t)$ in
\eqref{eq:tree-superlinear-rule}. To see this, at a node already in
the set its whole arc must be covered, at cost $b_I$; otherwise one
chooses between that cost and the sum of the optimal child costs.
Because $0<s\le1$, covering a whole arc by its proper dyadic
subarcs cannot cost less than $|I|^s$: their lengths sum to at
least $|I|$, and $\sum_j u_j^s\ge(\sum_j u_j)^s$.
Thus this is also the usual dyadic $s$-content of the finite union
of the selected arcs.

Every feasible $x$ assigns mass at most the covering cost to any
selected collection. Also $x_I\le b_I$ and $\theta\ge1$.
Layer integration consequently gives
\[
 \sum_Iv_I(x_I/b_I)^\theta
    \le\sum_Ih_Ix_I
    =\int_0^\infty\sum_{h_I>t}x_I\,dt
    \le\int_0^\infty R_{\partial\mathbb D}^{(N)}(t)\,dt.
\]
For the reverse comparison, order the nodes with $h_I>0$ by
nonincreasing $h_I$. Starting with the empty collection, accept a
node $I$ exactly when assigning it mass $b_I$ would leave the total
accepted mass in every node $K\supseteq I$, including $K=I$,
at most $2b_K$.
Let $\mathcal A_t$ be the accepted nodes with $h_I>t$.
If a node with $h_I>t$ was rejected, at that time one of its ancestors
$K$ had accepted mass greater than $2b_K-b_I\ge b_K$.
This remains true for $\mathcal A_t$. Choose the inclusion-maximal
such witnessing ancestors; they are disjoint. They cover all rejected
nodes above level $t$ and cost no more than the accepted mass inside
them. Cover the remaining accepted nodes by their own arcs. This
proves
\[
 R_{\partial\mathbb D}^{(N)}(t)
           \le\sum_{I\in\mathcal A_t}b_I.
\]
The final accepted collection satisfies all constraints with the
factor $2$. Assigning $x_I=b_I/2$ on that collection and zero
elsewhere is feasible. Integrating the preceding inequality gives
\[
 \max_x\sum_Iv_I(x_I/b_I)^\theta
    \ge2^{-\theta}\sum_{I\text{ accepted}}v_I
    \ge2^{-\theta}\int_0^\infty
                         R_{\partial\mathbb D}^{(N)}(t)\,dt.
\]
This proves the comparison for $r\ge p$.

Now suppose $0<\theta<1$, and put $w_I=v_I/b_I^\theta$.
We prove an exact finite-tree identity. For a subtree rooted at $I$,
let $\Psi_I(t)$ be the maximum of
$\sum_{J\subseteq I}w_Jx_J^\theta-t\sum_{J\subseteq I}x_J$
under its constraints \eqref{eq:tree-mass-constraints}.
Zero-weight variables are discarded. Strict concavity makes the
maximizer unique on the remaining coordinates, and write $D_I(t)$
for its total mass.
Without the constraint at $I$, the local variable has optimal mass
$(\theta w_I/t)^{1/(1-\theta)}$, and the two subtrees contribute
$D_{I_-}(t)$ and $D_{I_+}(t)$. If the sum is at most $b_I$, the allocation is feasible. Otherwise
choose a common penalty $u>t$ making its total mass equal to $b_I$.
The child demands are continuous, nonincreasing and tend to zero by
the induction hypothesis; the local demand has the same properties.
Thus such a $u$ exists.
The resulting allocation maximizes the constrained objective at the
original price: it maximizes the objective at price $u$, and among
allocations of mass at most $b_I$ the added term $(u-t)$ times the
mass is largest at mass $b_I$. Hence
\[
 D_I(t)=\min\left\{b_I,
        (\theta w_I/t)^{1/(1-\theta)}
                           +D_{I_-}(t)+D_{I_+}(t)\right\}.
\]
If the unconstrained sum never exceeds $b_I$, no adjustment is
needed. This also covers subtrees with zero weights.
Induction proves that $D_I=R_I^{(N)}$ from
\eqref{eq:tree-sublinear-rule}, and that this function is continuous
for $t>0$.

Comparing optimal allocations at $t$ and $t+h$, for $h>0$, yields
\[
 -hD_I(t)\le\Psi_I(t+h)-\Psi_I(t)\le-hD_I(t+h).
\]
Thus $\Psi_I'(t)=-D_I(t)$. The feasible set is compact,
$\Psi_I(t)\to\Psi_I(0)$ as $t\downarrow0$, and
$\Psi_I(t)\to0$ as $t\to\infty$.
For the last limit one may omit all constraints and maximize the
finitely many expressions $w_Jx^\theta-tx$ separately; each such
maximum tends to zero. Integrating the derivative gives
\[
 \max_x\sum_Iw_Ix_I^\theta
      =\Psi_{\partial\mathbb D}(0)
      =\int_0^\infty R_{\partial\mathbb D}^{(N)}(t)\,dt.
\]
This proves the required finite-tree identity for $r<p$.

Increasing the depth or an entry of $v$ increases both recursions.
Every finite coefficient support is contained in a depth truncation,
so passage to increasing depths and
\eqref{eq:tree-Carleson-comparison} prove
\eqref{eq:sequence-explicit-equivalence} for infinite arrays as well.
For $s=1$, the least covering cost of a finite union of dyadic arcs
is its normalized length. Layer integration then gives
\eqref{eq:critical-shadow-maximal}, first at finite depth and then
by monotone convergence. This completes the proof.

\label{thm:F-boundedness}
Let $0<p_i<\infty$, $q_i>-2$, $0\le s_i<\infty$ and $q_i+s_i>-1$
for $i=1,2$. Let $n\ge1$ be an integer, let
$\phi\in H(\mathbb D)$ and let $\varphi$ be an analytic self-map of
$\mathbb D$. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
 \renewcommand{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}
 \setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
 \item $D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}:F(p_1,q_1,s_1)\to F(p_2,q_2,s_2)$ is
       bounded.
 \item $\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb D}
                     \mathcal C_{p_1,s_1;p_2}(v_a)<\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
The quantity in \textup{(2)} is given explicitly by
\eqref{eq:explicit-sequence-functional}: its middle range uses the
covering rule for $p_2\ge p_1$ and the power rule for $p_2<p_1$.
When these conditions hold,
\[
 \|D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}\|_{F(p_1,q_1,s_1)\to F(p_2,q_2,s_2)}
 \approx
 \frac{|\phi(0)|}{(1-|\varphi(0)|^2)^{b_n}}
       +\left(\sup_{a\in\mathbb D}\mathcal C_{p_1,s_1;p_2}(v_a)\right)^{1/p_2}.
\]
The comparison constants depend only on the two parameter triples,
$n$ and the fixed partition. In particular, neither $p_1\le p_2$ nor
$p_i\ge1$ is required.

\label{thm:F-essential-norm}
Let $0<p_i<\infty$, $q_i>-2$, $0\le s_i<\infty$ and $q_i+s_i>-1$
for $i=1,2$. Let $n\ge1$ and let $\phi,\varphi$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:F-boundedness}. Suppose
$D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}:F(p_1,q_1,s_1)\to F(p_2,q_2,s_2)$ is bounded.
For $J=0,1,\ldots$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-tail-limit}
 L_J=\left[\sup_{a\in\mathbb D}\mathcal C_{p_1,s_1;p_2}
          (\{v_{a,j,l}:j\ge J\})\right]^{1/p_2},
              \qquad L=\lim_{J\to\infty}L_J,
\end{equation}
where the coordinates with $j<J$ are set equal to zero. Then
\[
 \|D_{\phi,\varphi}^{n}\|_{e,F(p_1,q_1,s_1)\to F(p_2,q_2,s_2)}
                    \approx L.
\]
In particular, the bounded operator is compact if and only if $L=0$.
All comparison constants depend only on the parameter triples, $n$
and the fixed partition, not on the two symbols.

\[

 H(p,q,\alpha)&n\ge0&\alpha+1/p+n\\

\label{lem:Dirichlet-derivative-reduction}
Let $\beta\in\mathbb R$, let $m\ge0$ be an integer, and suppose
$\beta+2m>1$. Differentiation $f\mapsto f^{(m)}$ maps

\end{equation}

\[
 \sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal D_\beta}\le1}|f^{(m)}(w)|
       \approx(1-|w|^2)^{-(\beta+2m)/2}.
\]

\label{lem:Schatten-kernel-tail}
Let $A>0$, $n\ge1$, $t>0$, and let $M$ be a positive integer with
$(M-A)t>1$. Put
\[
 H_{ik}=\frac{\delta_i^{A+n-1}\delta_k^{M-A}}
                   {|1-\overline{a_k}a_i|^{M+n-1}}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Schatten-kernel-tail}
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_i
       \sum_{d(a_i,a_k)>R}H_{ik}^{\,t}=0.
\end{equation}
For each $R>0$, the marked points can be divided into finitely many
sets within each of which distinct points have distance greater
than $R$.
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-36: body_md -->
The spacing of the marked points gives a fixed $r_0>0$ such that the
disks $\{w:d(w,a_k)<r_0\}$ are disjoint. On each disk,
$1-|w|^2\approx\delta_k$,
$|1-\overline wa_i|\approx|1-\overline{a_k}a_i|$, and its area is
comparable to $\delta_k^2$, with constants independent of $i,k$.
Consequently, with $b=A+n-1>0$ and $R>r_0$,
\[
 \sum_{d(a_i,a_k)>R}H_{ik}^{\,t}
 \le C(1-|a_i|^2)^{bt}
       \int_{d(a_i,w)>R-r_0}
       \frac{(1-|w|^2)^{(M-A)t-2}}
            {|1-\overline wa_i|^{(M+n-1)t}}\,dA(w).
\]
Under the substitution $w=\sigma_{a_i}(\zeta)$, the right-hand side
becomes
\[
 C\int_{|\zeta|>\tanh(R-r_0)}
    \frac{(1-|\zeta|^2)^v}{|1-\overline{a_i}\zeta|^\lambda}\,dA(\zeta),
 \qquad v=(M-A)t-2,\quad \lambda=(M-2A-n+1)t.
\]
All factors involving $1-|a_i|^2$ cancel. Here $v>-1$ and
$v+2-\lambda=bt>0$. Choose
$0<\varepsilon<\min\{v+1,bt\}$. The elementary subcritical kernel
estimate gives
\[
 \sup_{a\in\mathbb D}\int_{\mathbb D}
       \frac{(1-|\zeta|^2)^{v-\varepsilon}}
            {|1-\overline a\zeta|^\lambda}\,dA(\zeta)<\infty.
\]
For completeness, this follows by angular integration: for
$\lambda>1$, the angular integral is bounded by
$C(1-r)^{1-\lambda}$; for $\lambda=1$ it is bounded by
$C\log(e/(1-r))$; and for $\lambda<1$ it is uniformly bounded.
The remaining radial integrals converge because
$v-\varepsilon>-1$ and $\lambda<v-\varepsilon+2$.
Factoring $(1-|\zeta|^2)^\varepsilon$ out on the restricted region
now bounds the preceding tail by
$C(1-\tanh^2(R-r_0))^\varepsilon$, uniformly in $i$. This proves
\eqref{eq:Schatten-kernel-tail}.

Finally, any disk of a fixed hyperbolic radius contains at most a
fixed number of marked points. Indeed, the disjoint disks of radius
$r_0$ about these points lie in a slightly larger disk, and their
areas with respect to $(1-|z|^2)^{-2}\,dA(z)$ are equal and positive.
Join two marked points when their distance is at most $R$. The
resulting graph has uniformly bounded degree. A greedy coloring of
its countable set of vertices uses finitely many colors, and each
color class has the required separation.

\label{thm:monomial-exact}
Let $\beta_1,\beta_2\in\mathbb R$, let $m,n\ge0$ be integers, and
let $\rho\in\mathbb C$ with $|\rho|\le1$. Take
$\phi(z)=z^m$ and $\varphi(z)=\rho z$.

\textup{(a)} Suppose $0<|\rho|\le1$. For $k\ge n$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monomial-weights}
 \sigma_k=\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}|\rho|^{k-n}
        (k+1)^{(\beta_1-1)/2}
        (k-n+m+1)^{(1-\beta_2)/2}.
\end{equation}
Then $D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n}:\mathcal D_{\beta_1}\to
\mathcal D_{\beta_2}$ is bounded if and only if
$\sup_{k\ge n}\sigma_k<\infty$. Under this condition,
\[
 \|D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n}\|=\sup_{k\ge n}\sigma_k,
 \qquad
 \|D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n}\|_e=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sigma_k.
\]
It is compact if and only if $\sigma_k\to0$. In the compact case,
its nonzero singular values are the nonincreasing rearrangement of
$(\sigma_k)_{k\ge n}$. For every $t>0$,
\[
 D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n}\in\mathcal S_t
       \iff\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\sigma_k^t<\infty,
 \qquad
 \|D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n}\|_{\mathcal S_t}^t
       =\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\sigma_k^t
\]
when the sum is finite.

\textup{(b)} If $0<|\rho|<1$, the operator belongs to every
$\mathcal S_t$, $t>0$, and
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}a_N(D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n})^{1/N}=|\rho|.
\]
If $|\rho|=1$, the exact parameter conditions are
\begin{enumerate}
 \renewcommand{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}{\textup{(\arabic{enumi})}}
 \setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
 \item boundedness $\iff\beta_2-\beta_1\ge2n$;
 \item compactness $\iff\beta_2-\beta_1>2n$;
 \item membership in $\mathcal S_t$
          $\iff\beta_2-\beta_1>2n+2/t$.
\end{enumerate}
At $\beta_2-\beta_1=2n$, the essential norm is exactly $1$.

\textup{(c)} If $\rho=0$, the operator has rank one, with its only
nonzero singular value equal to
\[
 n!(n+1)^{(\beta_1-1)/2}(m+1)^{(1-\beta_2)/2}.
\]
In particular it belongs to every Schatten class. All assertions
include $n=0$, with $0!=1$.

The functions $e_k^{(\beta)}(z)=(k+1)^{(\beta-1)/2}z^k$ form an
orthonormal basis of $\mathcal D_\beta$. For $k<n$ the image of
$e_k^{(\beta_1)}$ is zero, whereas for $k\ge n$ and $\rho\ne0$,
\[
 D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n}e_k^{(\beta_1)}
   =\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\rho^{k-n}(k+1)^{(\beta_1-1)/2}
       (k-n+m+1)^{(1-\beta_2)/2}e_{k-n+m}^{(\beta_2)}.
\]
The target basis indices are distinct. Orthogonality therefore gives,
for every finite sum $f=\sum_kc_ke_k^{(\beta_1)}$,
\[
 \|D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n}f\|_{\mathcal D_{\beta_2}}^2
             =\sum_{k\ge n}|c_k|^2\sigma_k^2.
\]
This proves the boundedness criterion and the exact norm. When the
sequence is bounded, density of polynomials yields the bounded
extension, which agrees with the analytic formula by local uniform
convergence.

Keeping the first finitely many nonzero columns gives a finite-rank
approximation, and the norm of the discarded part is the supremum
of the remaining $\sigma_k$. Hence the essential norm is at most
$\limsup_k\sigma_k$. Conversely, for any compact operator $K$,
$\|Ke_k^{(\beta_1)}\|\to0$, since an orthonormal sequence is weakly
null. Thus
\[
 \|D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n}-K\|
   \ge\limsup_{k\to\infty}
         \|(D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n}-K)e_k^{(\beta_1)}\|
   \ge\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sigma_k.
\]
Taking the infimum over $K$ proves the asserted essential norm and
compactness criterion. In the compact case, the positive operator
$(D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n})^*D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n}$ is diagonal in the
source basis, with diagonal entries $0$ for $k<n$ and $\sigma_k^2$
for $k\ge n$. Its eigenvalues give the singular-value and Schatten
formulas in (a).

Put $\gamma=n+(\beta_1-\beta_2)/2$. Directly from
\eqref{eq:monomial-weights},
\[
 \frac{\sigma_k}{|\rho|^{k-n}k^\gamma}\longrightarrow1
                                    \qquad(k\to\infty).
\]
For $|\rho|=1$, this gives all three conditions in (b), since
$\sum_k k^{t\gamma}<\infty$ if and only if $t\gamma<-1$. If $\gamma=0$, then $\sigma_k\to1$,
which proves the exact essential norm at the boundary.

If $0<|\rho|<1$, exponential decay makes
$\sum_k\sigma_k^t$ finite for every $t>0$. Moreover,
$\sigma_{k+1}/\sigma_k\to|\rho|<1$, so the sequence is eventually
strictly decreasing. It is positive and tends to zero; consequently,
a sufficiently late tail lies below every earlier term. Its
nonincreasing rearrangement therefore satisfies
$a_N(D_{z^m,\rho z}^{n})=\sigma_{n+N-1}$ for all sufficiently large
$N$. Taking $N$th roots proves the limit in (b).

Finally, if $\rho=0$, the formula is simply
$D_{z^m,0}^{n}f=f^{(n)}(0)z^m$. The functional $f\mapsto f^{(n)}(0)$
has norm $n!(n+1)^{(\beta_1-1)/2}$ on the source, and $z^m$ has
norm $(m+1)^{(1-\beta_2)/2}$ in the target. Their product is the
only nonzero singular value of this rank-one operator. This proves
(c) without assigning a value to $0^0$.
