<!-- mainthm-1: body_md -->
\label{mainthm:Mumford-Tate conjecture for HK}
\label{mainthm:semisimplicity conjecture}
Let $X$ be a hyper-K\"ahler variety over a field $F$ finitely generated over ${\mathds Q}$.
Then $H^i_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline F},{\mathds Q}_\ell)$ is a semisimple $G_F$-representation for every $i$ and every prime $\ell$, and the Mumford--Tate conjecture holds for $X$ in every degree.
<!-- end mainthm-1 -->
<!-- mainthm-2: body_md -->
\label{Corollary:introduction WMC for HK}
Let $K$ be a finite extension of ${\mathds Q}_p$, and let $X$ be a hyper-K\"ahler variety over $K$.
The weight--monodromy conjecture holds for $H^i_\mathrm{\acute{e}t}(X_{\overline K},{\mathds Q}_\ell)$ for every $i$ and every prime $\ell$, including $\ell=p$.
<!-- end mainthm-2 -->
<!-- mainthm-3: body_md -->
\label{Theorem:introduction rational compatibility for HK}
After replacing $F$ by a further finite extension, the family $\{\rho^{\operatorname{MT}}_{\ell,v}\}_\ell$ is rationally strongly compatible for every finite place $v$ of $F$.
More precisely, there exists a $\mathbf{G}$-valued Weil--Deligne representation $\rho_v$ of $F_v$ over $\overline{\mathds Q}$ whose $\mathbf{G}(\overline{\mathds Q})$-conjugacy class is invariant under $\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}\nolimits(\overline{\mathds Q}/{\mathds Q})$ and whose base change to $\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell$ is equivalent to $\rho^{\operatorname{MT}}_{\ell,v}$ for every $\ell$, including $\ell=p$.
<!-- end mainthm-3 -->
<!-- mainthm-4: body_md -->
\label{Theorem:introduction integral compatibility for HK}
We keep the notation of \Cref{Theorem:introduction rational compatibility for HK} and fix a finite place $v\mid p$ of $F$.
There exist a positive integer $D$ divisible by $p$ and a reductive model $\mathcal{G}$ of $\mathbf{G}$ over ${\mathds Z}[1/D]$ such that $\rho_v$ extends to a $\mathcal{G}$-valued Weil--Deligne representation $\rho_v^{\mathrm{int}}$ of $F_v$ over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}[1/D]$ and, for every prime $\ell\nmid D$, its base change $(\rho_v^{\mathrm{int}})_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}$ to $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$ is equivalent to the $\mathcal{G}$-valued Weil--Deligne representation $\rho^{\operatorname{MT},\mathrm{int}}_{\ell,v}$ of $F_v$ over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$ associated with the Galois action on $H^\bullet_\mathrm{\acute{e}t}(X_{\overline{F}_v},{\mathds Z}_\ell)$.
<!-- end mainthm-4 -->
<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:global additive rigidity}
If $V_c$ is Hodge--Tate at every place $v\mid\ell$, then $c=0$.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $v\mid\ell$.
The representation $V_c|_{G_{F_v}}$ is an extension of the trivial representation ${\mathds Q}_\ell$ by itself.
Since it is Hodge--Tate by hypothesis, additivity of the multiplicities of Hodge--Tate weights in short exact sequences shows that all its weights are zero.
Applying Sen's theorem \cite[Corollary~1]{Sen1973a} over the completion of the maximal unramified extension of $F_v$ shows that $I_v$ has finite image on $V_c$.
Thus $c(I_v)$ is finite and hence zero, since the additive group of ${\mathds Q}_{\ell}$ is torsion-free.

Now let $v\nmid\ell$, and let $p$ be the residue characteristic of $F_v$.
Since $c$ is additive, its restriction to $G_{F_v}$ factors through the abelianization.
Local class field theory therefore identifies $c(I_v)$ with the image of a continuous homomorphism $\mathcal{O}_{F_v}^{\times}\to {\mathds Q}_{\ell}$.
The group $\mathcal{O}_{F_v}^{\times}$ has an open pro-$p$ subgroup, whereas every compact subgroup of ${\mathds Q}_{\ell}$ is pro-$\ell$.
Since $p\ne\ell$, this open subgroup has trivial image, so $c(I_v)$ is again finite and hence zero.

We have shown that $c$ is unramified at every finite place of $F$.
Since the target of $c$ is abelian, global class field theory makes $c$ factor through the narrow class group of $F$, which is finite.
As ${\mathds Q}_{\ell}$ has no nonzero finite subgroup, we conclude that $c=0$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:no-unipotent quotient for global representation}
Let $ \rho \colon G_F\to \mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(V) $ be a continuous finite-dimensional representation over ${\mathds Q}_\ell$.
Suppose that $V$ is Hodge--Tate at every place $v \mid \ell$.
If $G$ is the algebraic monodromy group of $V$, then $G^\circ$ has no nontrivial unipotent algebraic quotient.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By replacing $F$ by a finite extension, we may assume that $G=G^\circ$.
Let $c\colon G\to\mathbb{G}_a$ be an algebraic homomorphism and consider the two-dimensional representation $V_c$ of $G$ as above.
By \cite[Theorem~4.14]{Milne17}, $V_c$ is a subquotient of a finite direct sum of tensor products of $V$ and its dual $V^\vee$, and hence $V_c$ is Hodge--Tate at every place $v\mid\ell$.
It then follows from \Cref{lem:global additive rigidity} that $c$ is trivial.
A nontrivial connected unipotent quotient of $G$ would admit $\mathbb{G}_a$ as a quotient \cite[Propositions~14.21 and~14.22]{Milne17}, contradicting what we have just proved.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:no-unipotent quotient for geometric representation}
Let $F$ be a finitely generated field over ${\mathds Q}$ and let $X$ be a smooth proper variety over $F$.
Let $\mathrm{G}_\ell(X)$ be the algebraic monodromy group of the representation
\[
 \rho_\ell \colon G_F\longrightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(H^\bullet_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline F},{\mathds Q}_\ell)).
\]
Then $\mathrm{G}_\ell(X)^\circ$ has no nontrivial unipotent algebraic quotient.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By the standard specialization argument using Hilbert irreducibility \cite[Section~10.6]{SerreMW1997} (see also \cite[Section~5]{CadoretMotivated2013}), it suffices to treat the case where $F$ is a number field.
In this case, $H^\bullet_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline F},{\mathds Q}_\ell)$ is Hodge--Tate at every place above $\ell$ by \cite{Faltings88,Tsuji}.
The assertion therefore follows from \Cref{prop:no-unipotent quotient for global representation}.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm: semisimple MT conjecture}
Under the comparison isomorphism
\[
H^\bullet_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline F},{\mathds Q}_\ell)
\simeq H^\bullet_B(X_{\mathds C},{\mathds Q})\otimes_{\mathds Q}{\mathds Q}_\ell,
\]
we have $\operatorname{MT}(X_{\mathds C})_{{\mathds Q}_\ell}\subset\mathrm{G}_\ell(X)^\circ$.
Moreover, $\mathrm{R}_{u,\ell}$ commutes with $\operatorname{MT}(X_{\mathds C})_{{\mathds Q}_\ell}$, and multiplication induces a canonical decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semisimple MTC}
\mathrm{G}_\ell(X)^\circ
=
\operatorname{MT}(X_{\mathds C})_{{\mathds Q}_\ell}\times\mathrm{R}_{u,\ell}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
See \cite[Lemma~3.3.2 and Proposition~5.2.2]{TZ26}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm: MT conjecture}
For every prime $\ell$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq: MT conjecture}
\mathrm{G}_\ell(X)^\circ=\operatorname{MT}(X_{\mathds C})_{{\mathds Q}_\ell}
\end{equation}
under the \'etale--Betti comparison.
Moreover, for each $0\leq i\leq 2\dim X$, $H^i_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline F},{\mathds Q}_\ell)$ is a semisimple $G_F$-representation and the Mumford--Tate conjecture $\textup{(MTC)}_i$ holds for $X$.
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Projection onto the second factor in \eqref{eq:semisimple MTC} makes $\mathrm{R}_{u,\ell}$ a unipotent algebraic quotient of $\mathrm{G}_\ell(X)^\circ$.
Thus $\mathrm{R}_{u,\ell}=1$ by \Cref{thm:no-unipotent quotient for geometric representation}, which proves \eqref{eq: MT conjecture}.
For every $i$, the degree-$i$ projection satisfies $p_i(\mathrm{G}_\ell(X)^\circ)=\mathrm{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^\circ$.
Applying $p_i$ to \eqref{eq: MT conjecture} therefore gives
\[
\mathrm{G}_{\ell,i}(X)^\circ
=p_i(\operatorname{MT}(X_{\mathds C})_{{\mathds Q}_\ell})
=\operatorname{MT}_i(X_{\mathds C})_{{\mathds Q}_\ell},
\]
which proves $\textup{(MTC)}_i$ in every degree.
Reductivity of Mumford--Tate groups then implies semisimplicity by \cite[Corollary~22.43]{Milne17}.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- ex-1: body_md -->
\label{Example: WD rep ell adic case}
Assume that $\ell \neq p$.
Let $ \phi \colon G_K \to \mathbf{G}({\mathds Q}_\ell) $ be a continuous homomorphism.
We fix an isomorphism ${\mathds Z}_\ell(1) \simeq {\mathds Z}_\ell$ (as ${\mathds Z}_\ell$-modules).
Let $t_\ell\colon I_K \to {\mathds Z}_\ell(1) \simeq {\mathds Z}_\ell$ be the usual maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient.
By Grothendieck's monodromy theorem, there exists a unique nilpotent element $N \in \mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}\nolimits(\mathbf{G})$ such that $\phi(\sigma)=\exp(t_\ell(\sigma)N)$ for all elements $\sigma$ of some open subgroup of $I_K$.
We fix a geometric Frobenius lift $\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}\nolimits_q \in W_K$.
We define $r \colon W_K \to \mathbf{G}({\mathds Q}_\ell)$ by $\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}\nolimits^n_q \sigma \mapsto \phi(\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}\nolimits^n_q \sigma)\exp(-t_\ell(\sigma)N)$.
After passing to $\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell$, we obtain the $\mathbf{G}$-valued Weil--Deligne representation $\rho(\phi) \coloneqq (r,N)$ of $K$ over $\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell$, whose equivalence class is independent of the choices of ${\mathds Z}_\ell(1) \simeq {\mathds Z}_\ell$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}\nolimits_q$ (see \cite[Lemma~8.4.3 and Variant~8.11]{Deligne73}).
<!-- end ex-1 -->
<!-- ex-2: body_md -->
\label{Example: WD rep p adic case}
Assume that $\ell =p$.
Let $ \phi \colon G_K \to \mathbf{G}({\mathds Q}_\ell) $ be a continuous homomorphism.
We assume that $\phi$ is potentially semistable, that is, for every finite-dimensional representation $V \in \mathrm{Rep}_{{\mathds Q}_p}(\mathbf{G})$, the induced $G_K$-action on $V$ is potentially semistable\footnote{It suffices to check this condition for one faithful representation; see \cite[Lemma 2.8]{IM20} for example.}
Let $K_0$ be the maximal unramified subfield of $K$ and $K^{\operatorname{ur}}_0 \subset \overline{K}$ the maximal unramified extension of $K_0$.
Every $V \in \mathrm{Rep}_{{\mathds Q}_p}(\mathbf{G})$ gives rise to the $(\varphi,N)$-module
\[
D_{\operatorname{pst}}(V) \coloneqq \varinjlim_{L}(V\otimes_{{\mathds Q}_p} B_{\operatorname{st}})^{G_L}
\]
over $K^{\operatorname{ur}}_0$, where $L \subset \overline{K}$ runs over all finite Galois extensions of $K$.
We choose an embedding $\tau \colon K^{\operatorname{ur}}_0 \hookrightarrow \overline{{\mathds Q}}_p$ (over ${\mathds Q}_p$).
The construction $ V \mapsto D_{\operatorname{pst}}(V) \otimes_{K^{\operatorname{ur}}_0} \overline{{\mathds Q}}_p $ induces an exact tensor functor $\omega' \colon \mathrm{Rep}_{{\mathds Q}_p}(\mathbf{G}) \to \mathop{\mathrm{Vect}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Q}}_p}$, where $\mathop{\mathrm{Vect}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Q}}_p}$ is the category of finite-dimensional $\overline{{\mathds Q}}_p$-vector spaces.
Let $\omega \colon \mathrm{Rep}_{{\mathds Q}_p}(\mathbf{G}) \to \mathop{\mathrm{Vect}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Q}}_p}$ be the canonical fiber functor $V \mapsto V_{\overline{{\mathds Q}}_p}$.
By Tannakian formalism, there exists an isomorphism $\omega \simeq \omega'$ of tensor functors, and this induces an isomorphism $\mathbf{G}_{\overline{{\mathds Q}}_p} \simeq \underline{\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits}^{\otimes}(\omega')$.
For each $V \in \mathrm{Rep}_{{\mathds Q}_p}(\mathbf{G})$, the Weil group $W_K$ acts on $D_{\operatorname{pst}}(V)$ by
\[
  \sigma \otimes \sigma \varphi^{-\alpha(\sigma)[{\mathds F}_q:{\mathds F}_p]},\quad \forall\sigma \in W_K\,.
\]
This action and the monodromy operators on the $(\varphi,N)$-modules are compatible with tensor operations in $V$.
Hence, via the chosen tensor isomorphism $\omega \simeq \omega'$, they define a continuous homomorphism $ r\colon W_K\to \mathbf{G}(\overline{{\mathds Q}}_p) $ together with an element $ N\in \mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}\nolimits(\mathbf{G})_{\overline{{\mathds Q}}_p}$ such that $\rho(\phi) \coloneqq (r,N)$ is a $\mathbf{G}$-valued Weil--Deligne representation of $K$ over $\overline{{\mathds Q}}_p$.
The equivalence class of $\rho(\phi)$ is independent of the choices of $\tau$ and $\omega \simeq \omega'$.

\label{Example: integral Weil-Deligne construction}
Assume that $\ell\neq p$.
Let
\[
\mathcal{G}\hookrightarrow\prod_i\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(V_i)
\]
be a reductive closed subgroup scheme over ${\mathds Z}_\ell$, where $\prod_i\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(V_i)$ is a finite product and each $V_i$ is a finite free ${\mathds Z}_\ell$-module of rank $n_i$.
Assume that $\ell>\max_i n_i$.
Let $ \phi\colon G_K\to\mathcal G({\mathds Z}_\ell) $ be a continuous homomorphism, and let $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$ denote the integral closure of ${\mathds Z}_\ell$ in $\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell$.
We choose an isomorphism ${\mathds Z}_\ell(1)\simeq{\mathds Z}_\ell$ and a geometric Frobenius lift $\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}\nolimits_q\in W_K$.
Let $(r, N)$ be the $\mathcal G$-valued Weil--Deligne representation of $K$ over $\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell$ associated with the generic fiber of $\phi$ using our fixed choices ${\mathds Z}_\ell(1)\simeq{\mathds Z}_\ell$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}\nolimits_q$.
We shall show that $r \colon W_K \to \mathcal G(\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell)$ takes values in $\mathcal G(\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell)$ and $N$ belongs to $\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}\nolimits(\mathcal G)_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}$.

Choose $\sigma\in I_K$ with $t_\ell(\sigma)=1$, and write $g_i$ and $N_i$ for the actions of $\phi(\sigma)$ and $N$ on $V_i\otimes_{{\mathds Z}_\ell}\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell$.
The eigenvalues of $g_i$ are roots of unity, and its unipotent part is $\exp(N_i)$.
If an eigenvalue has order divisible by $\ell$, then the inequality $n_i<\ell$ forces the characteristic polynomial of $g_i$ to be irreducible over ${\mathds Q}_\ell$.
Thus $g_i$ is semisimple and $N_i=0$.
If the semisimple part of $g_i$ has order $e_i$ prime to $\ell$, then $g_i^{e_i}=\exp(e_iN_i)$ is unipotent.
This, together with $(g_i^{e_i}-1)^{n_i}=0$ and $n_i<\ell$, implies that
\[
N_i=\frac{1}{e_i}\log(g_i^{e_i})
=\frac{1}{e_i}\sum_{j=1}^{n_i-1}
\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}(g_i^{e_i}-1)^j
\in\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}(V_i\otimes_{{\mathds Z}_\ell}\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell).
\]
Similarly, $\exp(tN_i)$ is defined over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$ for every $t\in{\mathds Z}_\ell$.
Since $\mathcal G$ is closed in $\prod_i\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(V_i)$, it follows that
\[
N\in\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}\nolimits(\mathcal G)_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell},
\quad
r(W_K)\subset\mathcal G(\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell),
\]
as desired.
The resulting $\mathcal G$-valued Weil--Deligne representation of $K$ over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$ is also denoted by $\rho(\phi)=(r, N)$.
In contrast to the rational case, its equivalence class may depend on the choices of ${\mathds Z}_\ell(1)\simeq{\mathds Z}_\ell$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}\nolimits_q$.
The following proposition shows that it is independent of these choices under an additional hypothesis.

We follow the arguments of \cite[Lemma~8.4.3 and Variant~8.11]{Deligne73}.
If $N=0$, then $r=\phi|_{W_K}$, and the assertion is immediate.
We henceforth assume that $N\neq0$.

Since $q-1\in{\mathds Z}_\ell^\times$, the same argument as in \cite[Lemma~8.4.3]{Deligne73} shows that, with the identification ${\mathds Z}_\ell(1)\simeq{\mathds Z}_\ell$ fixed, changing the geometric Frobenius lift yields an equivalent $\mathcal{G}$-valued Weil--Deligne representation over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$.

Changing the identification ${\mathds Z}_\ell(1)\simeq{\mathds Z}_\ell$ leaves $r$ unchanged and replaces $N$ by $aN$ for some $a\in{\mathds Z}_\ell^\times$.

\[

\longrightarrow
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits({\mathds Z}\psi,\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell^\times)\simeq \overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell^\times.

Since $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell^\times$ is divisible, every homomorphism on ${\mathds Z}\psi\subset X^*(D)$ extends to $X^*(D)$.

Z_{\mathbf{G}_{\overline{{\mathds Q}}_{\ell}}}(r)\subset Z_{\mathbf{G}_{\overline{{\mathds Q}}_{\ell}}}(\lambda)\subset P,

\label{Definition:integral G-valued purity}
Let $\rho=(r, N)$ be a $\mathcal{G}$-valued Weil--Deligne representation of $K$ over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$.
We say that $\rho$ is \textit{pure} (with respect to $\mu$) if the following conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The generic fiber $\rho_{\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell}$ is pure with respect to $\mu$.
\item The Frobenius-weight cocharacter $w \colon \mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbf{G}_{\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell}$ of $r$ extends over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$.
\item The scheme-theoretic centralizer $H\coloneqq  Z_{\mathcal{G}_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}}(r)$ of $r(W_K)$ is smooth over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$.
\item Put $\lambda\coloneqq w^{-1}$ and
\[
\mathcal V\coloneqq 
\{
X\in\bigoplus_{n\geq2}\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}(\lambda,n) \mid
\operatorname{Ad}(r(\sigma))(X)=q^{\alpha(\sigma)}X
\text{ for every }\sigma\in W_K
 \},
\]
where $\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}=\bigoplus_{n \in {\mathds Z}}\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}(\lambda,n)$ is the weight decomposition with respect to $\lambda$.
Then the map $ \mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}\nolimits(H)\to \mathcal V $ defined by $Y\mapsto[Y,N]$ is surjective.
\end{enumerate}

\label{lem:tensor-square defector of purity}
    Let $\rho=(r,N)$ be a Weil--Deligne representation of $K$ over $\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell$.
    If $\rho\otimes\rho$ is pure of weight $2i$ for some integer $i$, then $\rho$ is pure of weight $i$.
<!-- end lem-7 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Let $V$ be the underlying vector space of $\rho$ and let $M_\bullet V$ be the monodromy filtration of $N$.
The monodromy operator on $V\otimes V$ is $N\otimes 1+1\otimes N$.
Its monodromy filtration is the convolution of the two filtrations, so
\[
 \text{\rm gr}_m^M(V\otimes V)\simeq\bigoplus_{a+b=m}\text{\rm gr}_a^M V\otimes\text{\rm gr}_b^M V.
\]
This follows, for instance, from the Jacobson--Morozov theorem; see \cite[Proposition~(1.6.9)]{WeilII}.

Let $a$ be an eigenvalue of geometric Frobenius on $\text{\rm gr}_j^M V$.
Then $a^2$ is an eigenvalue on the summand $\text{\rm gr}_j^M V\otimes\text{\rm gr}_j^M V$ of $\text{\rm gr}_{2j}^M(V\otimes V)$.
Purity of $V\otimes V$ says that $a^2$ is a Weil $q^{2(i+j)}$-number.
It follows that $a$ is a Weil $q^{i+j}$-number, as desired.

\label{Theorem:purity of Weil-Deligne representations}

\label{Remark:difference Kisin-Zhou}
Extension of scalars from $\overline{{\mathds Q}}$ to ${\mathds C}$ induces a bijection between the $G_{\mathds Q}$-invariant equivalence classes of $\mathbf{G}$-valued Weil--Deligne representations of $F_v$ over $\overline{{\mathds Q}}$ and the $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits({\mathds C}/{\mathds Q})$-invariant equivalence classes of $\mathbf{G}$-valued Weil--Deligne representations of $F_v$ over ${\mathds C}$.
The latter are called ``defined over ${\mathds Q}$'' in \cite[Definition~4.1.7]{KZb}.
Indeed, after fixing a finite quotient through which the inertia subgroup acts, $\mathbf{G}$-valued Weil--Deligne representations are parametrized by an affine scheme of finite type over ${\mathds Q}$.
An $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits({\mathds C}/{\mathds Q})$-invariant $\mathbf{G}$-conjugacy orbit is locally closed and descends to $\overline{{\mathds Q}}$, so it has a $\overline{{\mathds Q}}$-point.
Moreover, two $\mathbf{G}$-valued Weil--Deligne representations over $\overline{{\mathds Q}}$ that are equivalent over ${\mathds C}$ are already equivalent over $\overline{{\mathds Q}}$ since their transporter is a scheme of finite type over $\overline{{\mathds Q}}$, and hence has a $\overline{{\mathds Q}}$-point whenever it has a ${\mathds C}$-point.
This proves the desired bijection.

Thus \Cref{Definition:rational strong compatibility} is equivalent to the formulation of strong compatibility used in \cite{KZb}.

Finally, let $U\subset V^2_B(1)$ be the orthogonal complement of $\mathop{\mathrm{Pic}}\nolimits(X)_{\mathds Q}=\mathop{\mathrm{Pic}}\nolimits(X_{\overline{F}})_{\mathds Q}$.

T\simeq \mathop{\mathrm{GSpin}}\nolimits(U)\longrightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{GSpin}}\nolimits(V^2_B(1)).
\]

\theta \colon H^1_B((C^\epsilon)_{{\mathds C}},{\mathds Q})^{\oplus 64} \stackrel{\textstyle\sim}{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0pt}{0.4ex}} \mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}\nolimits(V^2_B(1)).

Thus the character in \Cref{Proposition:LLV and Kuga-Satake} is nontrivial.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]

<!-- prop-7: body_md -->
\label{Proposition: ell independence of Weil part of integral MT representation}
Let $\ell\nmid D_0$ be a prime.
The representations $(r^{\mathrm{int}}_v)_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}$ and $r^{\operatorname{MT},\mathrm{int}}_{\ell,v}$ are $\mathcal{G}(\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell)$-conjugate.
Here $(r^{\mathrm{int}}_v)_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}$ denotes the base change of $r^{\mathrm{int}}_v$ along the map $\overline{{\mathds Z}}[1/D_0] \to \overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$ induced by $\iota_\ell$.
Moreover, their scheme-theoretic centralizers in $\mathcal{G}_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}$ are smooth over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$.
<!-- end prop-7 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
Fix a geometric Frobenius lift $\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}\nolimits_q\in W_{F_v}$, and put
\[
x\coloneqq (r^{\mathrm{int}}_v)_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}(\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}\nolimits_q),
\quad
y\coloneqq r^{\operatorname{MT},\mathrm{int}}_{\ell,v}(\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}\nolimits_q).
\]
Their generic fibers are semisimple and $\mathbf{G}(\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell)$-conjugate by rational strong compatibility.
The centralizers of $x$ and $y$ in $\mathcal{G}_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}$ are smooth and contain maximal tori $T_x$ and $T_y$ of $\mathcal{G}_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}$ by \Cref{Lemma: smoothness of Frobenius centralizer}.
Since a maximal torus is its own centralizer, we have $x\in T_x(\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell)$ and $y\in T_y(\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell)$.
By \cite[Theorem~3.2.6]{Conrad2014}, these tori are $\mathcal{G}(\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell)$-conjugate, so we may assume that $x,y\in T(\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell)$ for a common maximal torus $T$.
Their generic fibers then lie in the same orbit with respect to the action of the Weyl group associated with $T$.
The corresponding element in the Weyl group lifts to an element $g \in N_{\mathcal{G}_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}}(T)(\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell)$ since the Weyl group (scheme) is finite \'etale and $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$ is strictly henselian.
It follows that $gxg^{-1}=y$.

The images of the inertia subgroup for both representations are central, and rational strong compatibility implies that their restrictions to the inertia subgroup are the same.
It follows that $(r^{\mathrm{int}}_v)_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}$ and $r^{\operatorname{MT},\mathrm{int}}_{\ell,v}$ are conjugate by the element $g$.
Their centralizers equal those of $x$ and $y$, respectively, and hence are smooth over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$.

<!-- prop-8: body_md -->
\label{Proposition: torsion freeness of cohomological monodromy}
Let $K$ be a finite extension of ${\mathds Q}_p$, and let $X$ be an abelian variety or a hyper-Kähler~ variety over $K$.
Fix integers $i,m,n \geq 0$, and consider the $G_K$-module
\[
H^i_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline K},{\mathds Z}_\ell)^{\otimes m}\otimes_{{\mathds Z}_\ell}
H^i_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline K},{\mathds Z}_\ell)^{\vee, \otimes n}.
\]
For all but finitely many $\ell \neq p$, the monodromy operator $N_\ell$ of the Weil--Deligne representation $\rho_\ell=(r_\ell, N_\ell)$ of $K$ over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$ associated with this $G_K$-module (\Cref{Example: integral Weil-Deligne construction}) has the following property \textup{(t-f)}: For all $j \geq 0$, the cokernel of $(N_\ell)^{j}$ is torsion-free.
<!-- end prop-8 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
The property \textup{(t-f)} is preserved by duals and tensor products after excluding finitely many further primes.
Indeed, by \cite[Lemma~2.10]{Ito21} and Nakayama's lemma,
\textup{(t-f)} is equivalent to compatibility of the monodromy
filtration with reduction to $\overline{{\mathds F}}_\ell$.
This compatibility is preserved by duals and tensor products,
since the formulas given in \cite[Proposition~(1.6.9)]{WeilII}
remain valid over $\overline{{\mathds F}}_\ell$
outside a finite set of primes depending only on
the ranks of the underlying modules.
Thus it suffices to consider the Weil--Deligne representation $\rho_\ell=(r_\ell, N_\ell)$ of $K$ over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$ associated with $H^i_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline K},{\mathds Z}_\ell)$.
If $X$ is an abelian variety, then the assertion is proved in \cite{Ito21}; more precisely, this follows from \cite[Theorem~3.7 and Proposition~3.10(ii)]{Ito21}.
We assume that $X$ is a hyper-Kähler~ variety over $K$.
If $i=2$, then this again follows from \cite{Ito21}.
We shall deduce the general case from this case.

We choose an embedding $K \hookrightarrow {\mathds C}$ and let $\mathbf{G}\coloneqq \operatorname{MT}(X_{\mathds C})$.
We may assume that $K=K^c$ and obtain the representation $\phi^{\operatorname{MT}}_\ell \colon G_K \to \mathbf{G}({\mathds Q}_\ell)$ for all $\ell$ as in \Cref{Subsection:MT-WD representation}.
By \Cref{lem:weighted tensor generation}, $H^i_B(X_{\mathds C},{\mathds Q})^{\otimes2}$ is a direct summand of a finite direct sum of $H^2_B(X_{\mathds C},{\mathds Q})^{\otimes a} \otimes H^2_B(X_{\mathds C},{\mathds Q})^{\vee, \otimes b}$ for some $a, b$ as a $\mathbf{G}$-representation.
From this, we see that $H^i_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline K},{\mathds Z}_\ell)^{\otimes2}$ is a direct summand of a finite direct sum of $H^2_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline K},{\mathds Z}_\ell)^{\otimes a} \otimes H^2_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline K},{\mathds Z}_\ell)^{\vee, \otimes b}$ as a $G_K$-module for all but finitely many $\ell \neq p$.
It follows that $\rho_\ell^{\otimes 2}$ satisfies property \textup{(t-f)}.
Finally, property \textup{(t-f)} can be detected on tensor squares
for sufficiently large $\ell$.
Indeed, the monodromy filtration on a tensor square is the
tensor product filtration, both in characteristic zero and,
for all but finitely many $\ell$, after reduction to
$\overline{{\mathds F}}_\ell$.
Since a filtration is determined by its tensor square,
compatibility of the monodromy filtration with reduction
for $\rho_\ell^{\otimes2}$ implies the same compatibility
for $\rho_\ell$.
Thus $\rho_\ell$ also has property \textup{(t-f)}.
This completes the proof.

\label{Proposition: integral purity of MT representations}
There exists a positive integer $D$ divisible by $D_0$, such that, for every prime $\ell\nmid D$, both
\[
(\rho_v^{\mathrm{int}})_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\rho^{\operatorname{MT},\mathrm{int}}_{\ell,v}
\]
are pure with respect to $\mu$ in the sense of \Cref{Definition:integral G-valued purity}.
<!-- end prop-9 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
We want to apply \Cref{Lemma: sufficient criterion for integral purity}.
By the preceding results (and the corresponding known results for abelian varieties), the generic fibers are Frobenius semisimple and pure with respect to $\mu$.
Condition (d) implies that the Frobenius-weight cocharacters are defined over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$ for every $\ell \nmid D_0$.
Moreover, the scheme-theoretic centralizers of 
the corresponding actions of $W_{F_v}$ are smooth over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$ by \Cref{Proposition: ell independence of Weil part of integral MT representation}.
Thus conditions \textup{(1)--(3)} of \Cref{Definition:integral G-valued purity} hold.

Let $\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits\coloneqq  \mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}\nolimits(\mathcal{G})$.
Since $\rho_v$ is defined over $\overline{{\mathds Q}}$, it is easy to see that there exists a positive integer $D$ divisible by $D_0$, such that, for every prime $\ell\nmid D$, the isotypic decomposition of $\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Q}}_\ell}$ for the action of $r^{\mathrm{int}}_v(W_{F_v})$ is defined over $\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell$.
By \Cref{Proposition: ell independence of Weil part of integral MT representation}, the same holds for $r^{\operatorname{MT},\mathrm{int}}_{\ell,v}$.

It remains to show that for all but finitely many $\ell$, the cokernels of
\[
d_1 \colon \mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell} \longrightarrow \mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}, \quad Y \mapsto [Y, N^{\mathrm{int}}_v]
\]
and
\[
d_2 \colon \mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell} \longrightarrow \mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\overline{{\mathds Z}}_\ell}, \quad Y \mapsto [Y, N^{\operatorname{MT}, \mathrm{int}}_{\ell, v}]
\]
are torsion-free.
Since $\rho_v$ is defined over $\overline{{\mathds Q}}$, one can easily check the assertion for $d_1$.
For $d_2$, we note that
the natural
$\mathbf{G}$-equivariant inclusion
\[
\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\mathds Q}\hookrightarrow
\bigoplus_i\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(H^i_B(X_{\mathds C},{\mathds Q}))
\]
splits since $\mathbf{G}$ is reductive.
After enlarging $D$, we may assume that the inclusion and splitting extend
over ${\mathds Z}[1/D]$.
Under the \'etale--Betti comparison, this splitting is also $G_{F_v}$-equivariant.
Now \Cref{Proposition: torsion freeness of cohomological monodromy} implies that after enlarging $D$ further, the cokernel of $d_2$ is torsion-free for every $\ell \nmid D$.

All the hypotheses of \Cref{Lemma: sufficient criterion for integral purity} are now satisfied for both representations.
