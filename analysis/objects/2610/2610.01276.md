\label{thm:main}
There is a smooth projective threefold $X$ and a flat morphism
\[
 \pi:X\longrightarrow\mathcal B
\]
with connected fibers and a regular section such that the following assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K_X\simeq\mathcal O_X$ and $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=H^2(X,\mathcal O_X)=0$.
\item The generic fiber of $\pi$ is the elliptic curve \eqref{eq:mainW}. Its Mordell--Weil group over $\mathbb C(v,t)$ is torsion-free of rank $12$.
\item The only reducible fibers over generic points of discriminant curves are two split fibers of type $\textup{\textbf{IV}}$.
\item The Hodge numbers and Euler characteristic are
\[
 h^{1,1}(X)=25,\qquad h^{2,1}(X)=1,\qquad e(X)=48.
\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- pro-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:anticanonical}
The hypersurface $\mathcal Q\subset M$ defined by \eqref{eq:quartic} is integral and anticanonical. In particular, $\omega_{\mathcal Q}\simeq\mathcal O_{\mathcal Q}$.
<!-- end pro-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
On the chart $w=1/v$, use the transition functions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bundletransition}
 X'=w^3X,\qquad Y'=w^3Y,\qquad Z'=w^4Z,\qquad W'=W.
\end{equation}
Since $w^4\alpha(1/w)=\alpha(w)$ and $w^4\beta(1/w)=\beta(w)$, both sides of \eqref{eq:quartic} are multiplied by $w^{12}$. Thus the equation is global, including the fiber over infinity.

\label{lem:weightedblowup}

\end{equation}

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->

\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighteddiscrepancy}
 (1+1+1+2)-1-4=0.
\end{equation}

\[
 D=\{\lambda z^2+\mu s^4-q(x,y)=0\}\subset\mathbb P(1,1,1,2).

otherwise its kernel contains $E_v[m]$ for some $m>1$, and the isogeny
factors through $[m]$, contradicting minimality. An isogeny would therefore

\[
 \Phi_N\bigl(j(E_v),j(E_2)\bigr)=0

\[

\]

\[
 H^2(S,\mathbb Q)^\iota\oplus\mathbb Q(-1)^8
\]
as its rational Hodge structure. Both surfaces are K3 surfaces, so the anti-invariant part of $H^2(S,\mathbb Q)$ has dimension eight. Finally, the N\'eron--Severi group of a product of elliptic curves has rank

Its Picard number, and hence that of $S$, is $2+16=18$.

\label{lem:smallresolution}
Let $W$ be a threefold with an ordinary double point and a local Weierstrass equation $y^2=x^3+f(s,t)x+g(s,t)$. Suppose a regular section $(p(s,t),q(s,t))$ passes through the double point. Blowing up the ideal of its graph gives a smooth small crepant resolution near that point.
<!-- end lem-9 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Put $U=x-p$, $V=y-q$, and $h_*=3p^2+f$. The exact equation becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sectionfactor}
 V(V+2q)=U(U^2+3pU+h_*).
\end{equation}
At the double point $q=h_*=0$. The determinant of the Hessian of \eqref{eq:sectionfactor} is
\[
 4\left(\det\frac{\partial(q,h_*)}{\partial(s,t)}\right)^2.
\]
Since the point is ordinary double, $\mathop{}\!\mathrm d q$ and $\mathop{}\!\mathrm d h_*$ are independent base differentials.

Blow up $(U,V)$. On the chart $V=\lambda U$, the strict transform is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smallchartone}
 U\lambda^2+2q\lambda-U^2-3pU-h_*=0.
\end{equation}
Along the exceptional fiber, its base differential is $2\lambda\mathop{}\!\mathrm d q-\mathop{}\!\mathrm d h_*$, which cannot vanish. On the other chart $U=\mu V$, the equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smallcharttwo}
 V+2q-\mu^3V^2-3p\mu^2V-\mu h_*=0,
\end{equation}
and the corresponding differential is $2\mathop{}\!\mathrm d q-\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm d h_*$, again nonzero. These charts are smooth and the exceptional fiber is a single $\mathbb P^1$. Thus the resolution is small. A small resolution of this Gorenstein singularity is crepant, since there is no exceptional divisor on which a discrepancy could be supported.

<!-- pro-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:globalflat}
The Weierstrass model $\mathscr W\to\mathcal B$ admits a projective crepant resolution $X\to\mathscr W$ such that $X\to\mathcal B$ is flat and has a regular zero section.
<!-- end pro-7 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
The Weierstrass hypersurface is Cohen--Macaulay and its singular locus has codimension at least two, by Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary} and Proposition~\ref{prop:nodes}. It is therefore normal. First apply Lemma~\ref{lem:A2resolve}. The resulting projective normal threefold is smooth away from the ninety-six double points. Take the closures of the six section images in \eqref{eq:sixsections}. These are global effective Weil divisors. At every smooth point their ideals are invertible, so blowing them up is an isomorphism there. Near a double point on a given section, the ideal is the graph ideal $(U,V)$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:smallresolution}; hence its blow-up is a smooth small resolution.

Blow up successively the ideal sheaves of the strict transforms of these six divisors. Once a double point has been resolved, every later divisor is Cartier in its smooth neighborhood and later blow-ups are isomorphisms there. Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} shows that all double points are resolved. Each blow-up is a projective morphism defined by a global ideal sheaf, and every step is crepant.

The zero section of the Weierstrass model is smooth and is disjoint from all singular centers. The later divisorial ideals are invertible near it, even where their supports meet it. The zero section is therefore unchanged and remains regular.

Every fiber of the resolved morphism has dimension one. The curve blow-ups add curves over the two discriminant curves, and the small resolutions add only $\mathbb P^1$'s over the isolated base points. They introduce no surface over a point of $\mathcal B$. Since $X$ is Cohen--Macaulay and $\mathcal B$ is smooth, the equidimensional morphism is flat by miracle flatness. Its fibers are connected: its generic fiber is geometrically connected, and Stein factorization over the normal base has a finite birational factor, which is an isomorphism.

\label{rem:constructionmethod}
One may seek further examples by changing the constant elliptic curve or
the varying family in the product construction. The associated Kummer
surfaces and a suitable base change must provide an explicit K3 model
whose coefficient degrees allow an anticanonical compactification,
as in Section~\ref{sec:origin}. The singularities of the
compactification must then admit a projective crepant resolution, and the
vanishing of $H^1(\mathcal O)$ and $H^2(\mathcal O)$ must be checked.
To obtain a flat elliptic model, one must also control the degenerations
over the base. In the present example, the sections used for the rank
calculation provide global divisors for the small resolutions. For another
family, their field of definition, independence, and incidence with the
singularities require separate verification.

<!-- pro-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:Picard}
One has $\rho(X)=25$.
<!-- end pro-8 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Let $K=\mathbb C(\mathcal B)$. Restrict divisors on the smooth threefold $X$ to the generic elliptic curve $\mathscr E/K$. Every closed point of $\mathscr E$ extends by closure to a divisor on $X$, which is Cartier because $X$ is smooth. Restriction therefore surjects onto $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathscr E)$. The rational zero splits its degree map, so its degree-zero part is $\mathscr E(K)$ and
\[
 \operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Pic}(\mathscr E)=1+\operatorname{rank}\mathscr E(K)=13.
\]
The kernel is generated by vertical divisors: a rational function giving a principal divisor on $\mathscr E$ belongs to $K(X)$, and subtracting its divisor leaves a vertical divisor.

Flatness excludes divisors over points of $\mathcal B$. Every vertical prime divisor therefore lies over a curve. Over all curves other than the two split $\textup{\textbf{IV}}$ curves the generic fiber is irreducible; the zero section excludes multiple fibers. Their vertical classes are pullbacks from the base. Over each split $\textup{\textbf{IV}}$ curve there are exactly two further independent component classes. Their independence modulo pullbacks follows by intersecting with the nonidentity components of a transverse surface fiber: the resulting matrix is the nonsingular negative $\mathrm A_2$ Cartan matrix. Pullback from $\mathcal B$ is injective because it has a left inverse given by the zero section. Since $\rho(\mathcal B)=2+6=8$, the vertical rank is $8+2+2=12$.

Finally, $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:XCY}, so $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)=0$. Taking ranks in the restriction sequence gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flatPicard}
 \rho(X)=\rho(\mathcal B)+1+\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{MW}(X/\mathcal B)+2+2
        =8+1+12+2+2=25.
\end{equation}

\label{lem:residualdiscriminant}
The curve $D$ is reduced and disjoint from $C_+\cup C_-$. It has arithmetic genus $177$, exactly $96$ ordinary nodes and $64$ ordinary cusps, and no other singularities.
<!-- end lem-10 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
The blow-up description and adjunction give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:baseintersections}
 K_{\mathcal B}^2=2,\qquad C_+^2=C_-^2=-3,\qquad
 K_{\mathcal B}\cdot C_+=K_{\mathcal B}\cdot C_-=1,\qquad C_+\cdot C_-=0.
\end{equation}
The discriminant is a section of $-12K_{\mathcal B}$, and its multiplicity along each $C_\pm$ is four. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residualclass}
 D=-12K_{\mathcal B}-4C_+-4C_-.
\end{equation}
Intersecting \eqref{eq:residualclass} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residualnumbers}
 D^2=384,\qquad K_{\mathcal B}\cdot D=-32,\qquad
 D\cdot C_+=D\cdot C_-=0,\qquad
 p_a(D)=1+\frac{384-32}{2}=177.
\end{equation}

The degree-sixteen residual discriminant over $\mathbb C(v)$ is square-free by Lemma~\ref{lem:K3fibers}. There is no component over a point of the ruling: on the finite $v$-chart its leading coefficient in $t$ is a nonzero constant; the chart at $v=\infty$ is identical under \eqref{eq:Wtransition}. Over a base exceptional curve the discriminant of \eqref{eq:firstEchart} is not identically zero. Hence $D$ is reduced. Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary} shows that it is disjoint from $C_+\cup C_-$ and smooth at its remaining boundary points. More explicitly, in \eqref{eq:firstEchart}, a discriminant zero has $z\ne0$ and $g_E\ne0$, and
$\partial g_E/\partial z=11664\delta(0)^4z^3\ne0$; hence the discriminant is smooth there.

Consider first a discriminant point with $(f,g)\ne(0,0)$. The plane cubic has an ordinary node, with nondegenerate Hessian in its two fiber coordinates. The holomorphic Morse lemma with parameters puts the total equation locally in the form
\[
 u_1^2+u_2^2-h(s_1,s_2)=0;
\]
up to a unit its discriminant is $h=0$. The total space is singular precisely when $h=\mathop{}\!\mathrm d h=0$. At each of the $96$ points of Proposition~\ref{prop:nodes}, the full Hessian is nondegenerate, so the Hessian of $h$ is nondegenerate. The corresponding discriminant point is therefore an ordinary node. Conversely, every singular discriminant point with $(f,g)\ne(0,0)$ gives a singularity of the total Weierstrass space and is on that complete list.

It remains to consider $f=g=0$ away from the boundary. The polynomial $A(v)$ has eight simple roots and none is a root of $B$: indeed
$A'(v)=8v^3(v^4+7)$, while $A(0)=1$ and $A(v)=-48$ if $v^4=-7$; also $A=16$ if $v^4=1$. At a zero of $A$, the equation $g=0$ with $t\ne0$ becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cuspquadratic}
 d\theta^2+p\theta+h=0,\qquad
 \theta=t^4,\quad d=54b^4,\quad p=cC(v),\quad h=54B(v)^4.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:ABCidentity} and $a^3-c^2=108b^4$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cuspdiscriminant}
 p^2-4dh\equiv-108a^3B(v)^4\not\equiv0\pmod{A(v)}.
\end{equation}
Thus the quadratic has two distinct nonzero roots, and each gives four distinct nonzero values of $t$. There are exactly $8\cdot2\cdot4=64$ such points. At each of them $\partial f/\partial v\ne0$, $\partial f/\partial t=0$, and $\partial g/\partial t\ne0$. Hence $f,g$ are local base coordinates and the discriminant has the ordinary cusp equation $4f^3+27g^2=0$. The node list and the boundary analysis exclude any other singularity of $D$.

<!-- pro-9: body_md -->
\label{prop:Euler}
One has $e(X)=48$ and $(h^{1,1}(X),h^{2,1}(X))=(25,1)$.
<!-- end pro-9 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
For any reduced projective curve with $n$ ordinary nodes and $c$ ordinary cusps, its normalization $\nu:\widetilde D\to D$ satisfies
\[
 \chi(\mathcal O_{\widetilde D})=\chi(\mathcal O_D)+n+c.
\]
Every node identifies two points of the normalization; a cusp does not change the number of points. Since $e(\widetilde D)=2\chi(\mathcal O_{\widetilde D})$, this gives, without assuming irreducibility,
\[
 e(D)=2-2p_a(D)+n+2c.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:residualdiscriminant} therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residualEuler}
 e(D)=2-2\cdot177+96+2\cdot64=-128,\qquad
 e(D^{\mathrm{sm}})=-128-96-64=-288.
\end{equation}

Over $D^{\mathrm{sm}}$ the fiber has type $\textup{\textbf{I}}_1$ and Euler characteristic one. At a cusp the fiber is a cuspidal cubic, with Euler characteristic two; the total space is smooth there. At each discriminant node the small resolution replaces the node of a nodal cubic by $\mathbb P^1$, so the fiber has Euler characteristic $1-1+2=2$. Along each $C_\pm\simeq\mathbb P^1$ the fiber has type $\textup{\textbf{IV}}$ everywhere, with Euler characteristic four, by the uniform boundary resolution and the disjointness of the residual discriminant. Smooth elliptic fibers contribute zero. Additivity of Euler characteristic over these strata gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flatEuler}
 e(X)=4\bigl(e(C_+)+e(C_-)\bigr)+e(D^{\mathrm{sm}})+2\cdot96+2\cdot64
      =16-288+192+128=48.
\end{equation}
Since $X$ is Calabi--Yau, Proposition~\ref{prop:Picard} gives $h^{1,1}(X)=\rho(X)=25$. Finally $e(X)=2(h^{1,1}(X)-h^{2,1}(X))$, so $h^{2,1}(X)=1$.

\label{rem:quarticEuler}
The Euler characteristic can also be computed on the quartic resolution $Y$. The singularity on each special quartic fiber is analytically $z^2-q(x,y)=0$, after absorbing the unit multiplying $z^2$. Its Milnor number is
\[
 \dim_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[[x,y]]/(q_x,q_y)=3\cdot3=9,
\]
since the two cubic partial derivatives form a homogeneous complete intersection. The Milnor-fiber formula \cite[Theorems~6.5 and~7.2]{Milnor} gives $e(\mathcal Q_{v_0})=24-9=15$. A degree-two del Pezzo exceptional surface is a double plane branched over a smooth genus-three quartic and has Euler characteristic $2\cdot3-(2-2\cdot3)=10$. Thus $e(Y_{v_0})=15-1+10=24$, and
\[
 e(Y)=(2-6)\cdot24+6\cdot24=48.
\]
