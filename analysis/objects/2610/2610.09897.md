<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A \emph{finite chain complex over $\mathbb F_2$} is a sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces and linear maps
\[
\cdots\longrightarrow C_{q+1} \xrightarrow{\partial_{q+1}} C_q \xrightarrow{\partial_q} C_{q-1} \longrightarrow\cdots
\]
such that $\partial_q\partial_{q+1}=0$ for every $q$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A \emph{filtered chain complex} is a finite chain complex $C_*$ together with a chosen basis $\mathcal E$ and a map $f:\mathcal E\to\mathbb R$ such that every basis element appearing with nonzero coefficient in $\partial e$ has filtration value at most $f(e)$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:same value-cancellation}
Let $C_*$ be a finite filtered based chain complex over $\mathbb F_2$. Let $x\in C_q$ and $y\in C_{q+1}$ be basis elements with
\[
f(x)=f(y)=t,
\]
and suppose the coefficient of $x$ in $\partial y$ equals one. Then there exists a filtered based chain complex $C'_*$ obtained by removing $x$ and $y$ such that
\[
C_*\cong C'_*\oplus A_*
\]
by a filtration-preserving chain isomorphism, where $A_*$ is the two-generator chain complex
\[
0\longrightarrow \mathbb F_2 y
\xrightarrow{\partial} \mathbb F_2 x'
\longrightarrow0
\]
given $\partial y=x'$. Consequently, $C_*$ and $C'_*$ have naturally isomorphic persistence modules.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Suppose $\partial y=x+a$, where $x$ does not occur in $a$. We replace the basis element $x$ of $C_q$ by
\[
x':=\partial y=x+a.
\]
This change of basis is invertible because the coefficient of $x$ is one. This change of basis also preserves the filtration. For each $s$, let $F_sC_q$ denote the span of basis elements with filtration value at most $s$. Since every term of $a$ occurs in $\partial y$, it has filtration value at most $f(y)=t$. Hence, both
\[
x'=x+a \qquad\text{and}\qquad x=x'+a
\]
involve only elements of filtration value at most $t$. Thus, for $s < t$, neither $x$ nor $x'$ is present, while for $s\ge t$, replacing $x$ by $x'$ does not change the span. Therefore, every filtered subspace $F_sC_q$ is unchanged.

For the new basis, we have $\partial y=x'$. Consider any other basis element $z\in C_{q+1}$ whose boundary $\partial z$ contains $x'$. If we replace $z$ by $z+y$, then $x'$ is removed from $\partial z$. This change of basis also preserves the filtration. Since $x'$ occurs in $\partial z$, we have
\[
f(x')\le f(z).
\]
As $f(x')=f(y)=t$, it follows that $f(y)\le f(z)$. Thus $y$ is already present whenever $z$ is present, so replacing $z$ by $z+y$ does not change any filtered subspace. We can repeat the process for all such $z$. This ensures that $x'$ appears in the boundary of $y$ and in no other boundary of a $(q+1)$-chain. 

Next, we show that $y$ cannot appear in the boundary of a basis element $w\in C_{q+2}$. If $y$ appears in the boundary of $w \in C_{q+2}$, then the coefficient of $x'$ in $\partial^2w$ would be one, because $\partial y=x'$ and no other basis element of $C_{q+1}$ has a boundary containing $x'$. Hence, 
\[
\partial^2w \neq 0.
\]
This contradicts the property $\partial^2=0$.

We have now shown that the span of $y$ and $x'$ is a direct filtered subcomplex $A_*$. The subcomplex $A_*$ has trivial homology. Indeed, since
\[
\partial y=x'\neq 0,
\]
the element $y$ is not a cycle, while $x'$ is a cycle but also a boundary, namely $x'=\partial y$. Hence, $A_*$ has no nontrivial homology classes.

Since $A_*$ is isolated from the remaining generators, the transformed chain complex splits as
\[
C_*\cong C'_*\oplus A_*,
\]
where $C'_*$ is obtained by omitting the two generators $y$ and $x'$. Thus, $A_*$ may be removed as a contractible filtered summand.

For $s<t$, the summand $A_*$ is absent from the filtered complex, while for $s\ge t$, it is present but has trivial homology. Hence, at every filtration value,
\[
H_k(F_sC_*)\cong H_k(F_sC'_*).
\]
These isomorphisms are compatible with the inclusion maps between filtration levels, so $C_*$ and $C'_*$ define naturally isomorphic persistence modules.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:block-preserves-persistence}
The final filtered chain complex obtained after all same value cancellations has a persistence module naturally isomorphic to that of the original filtered chain complex.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
At each step, the algorithm cancels two basis elements of the same filtration value whose incidence coefficient is nonzero in the current transformed complex. By \cref{lem:same value-cancellation}, removing such a pair does not change the persistence module.

Since the chain complex is finite and each cancellation removes two generators, only finitely many cancellations occur. Composing the persistence-module isomorphisms obtained at each step gives a natural isomorphism between the persistence module of the original filtered chain complex and that of the final compressed complex.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
In every homological dimension, the positive-length and essential persistence intervals of the compressed complex are exactly those of the original filtered complex, with the same multiplicities.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:generator-count}
Suppose a finite filtered based chain complex has the property that the boundary of every basis generator has strictly smaller filtration value. Let $c_q(t)$ be the number of dimension-$q$ basis generators with filtration value $t$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:relative-generator-count}
c_q(t)=b_q(t)+d_{q-1}(t).
\end{equation}
If the barcode has $N_{\mathrm{fin}}$ finite positive-length intervals and $N_{\mathrm{ess}}$ essential intervals, then the total number of basis generators is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:two-fin-plus-ess}
\boxed{2N_{\mathrm{fin}}+N_{\mathrm{ess}}}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Consider the relative chain complex $C^t/C^{t^-}$. Since every boundary strictly lowers the filtration value, the boundary of a generator with filtration value $t$ lies entirely in $C^{t^-}$. Therefore the relative boundary map is zero. Hence
\[
H_q(C^t,C^{t^-})
\cong
C_q^t/C_q^{t^-},
\]
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:relative-homology-dimension}
\dim H_q(C^t,C^{t^-})=c_q(t).
\end{equation}

The short exact sequence of chain complexes
\[
0\longrightarrow C^{t^-}
\longrightarrow C^t
\longrightarrow C^t/C^{t^-}
\longrightarrow0
\]
induces the standard long exact sequence in homology \cite[Section~2.1]{hatcher2002algebraic}. The part relevant here is
\[
H_q(C^{t^-})
\xrightarrow{i_t^q}
H_q(C^t)
\xrightarrow{j}
H_q(C^t,C^{t^-})
\xrightarrow{\delta}
H_{q-1}(C^{t^-})
\xrightarrow{i_t^{q-1}}
H_{q-1}(C^t).
\]
Exactness means
\[
\ker j=\operatorname{im} i_t^q,
\qquad
\ker\delta=\operatorname{im} j,
\qquad
\operatorname{im}\delta=\ker i_t^{q-1}.
\]
By the first isomorphism theorem,
\[
\operatorname{im} j
\cong
H_q(C^t)/\ker j
=
H_q(C^t)/\operatorname{im} i_t^q
=
\operatorname{coker} i_t^q.
\]
Also, $\delta$ maps $H_q(C^t,C^{t^-})$ onto $\ker i_t^{q-1}$ and has kernel $\operatorname{im} j$. Thus the displayed part of the long exact sequence yields the short exact sequence
\[
0
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{coker} i_t^q
\longrightarrow
H_q(C^t,C^{t^-})
\longrightarrow
\ker i_t^{q-1}
\longrightarrow
0.
\]
Taking dimensions over $\mathbb F_2$ and using \cref{eq:relative-homology-dimension} gives
\[
c_q(t)
=
\dim\operatorname{coker} i_t^q+\dim\ker i_t^{q-1}
=
b_q(t)+d_{q-1}(t),
\]
which proves \cref{eq:relative-generator-count}.

Finally, sum this identity over all homological dimensions $q$ and filtration values $t$:
\[
\sum_{q,t}c_q(t)
=
\sum_{q,t}b_q(t)
+
\sum_{q,t}d_q(t).
\]
Every barcode interval contributes exactly one birth, so
\[
\sum_{q,t}b_q(t)=N_{\mathrm{fin}}+N_{\mathrm{ess}}.
\]
Every finite interval contributes exactly one death and every essential interval contributes none, so
\[
\sum_{q,t}d_q(t)=N_{\mathrm{fin}}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{q,t}c_q(t)
=
(N_{\mathrm{fin}}+N_{\mathrm{ess}})
+
N_{\mathrm{fin}}
=
2N_{\mathrm{fin}}+N_{\mathrm{ess}},
\]
which is \cref{eq:two-fin-plus-ess}.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:streaming-equivalence}
Consider a materialized and a streaming implementation of the same value block contraction over $\mathbb F_2$. Suppose that both implementations
\begin{enumerate}
\item process the cubical cells in the same deterministic order \cref{eq:cell-order};
\item generate the same boundary for each processed cell;
\item use the same replacement relation for every previously cancelled generator; and
\item choose the largest remaining same value boundary element as the cancellation pivot.
\end{enumerate}
Then, after each processed cell, the two implementations have the same surviving generators, the same cancellation relations, and the same rewritten boundary columns. Consequently, they produce identical compressed filtered chain complexes.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We proceed by induction over the common processing order of the cells. Before any cell is processed, both implementations have empty sets of surviving generators, cancellation relations, and compressed boundary columns, so their states agree.

Assume that the two states agree after the first $p-1$ cells have been processed, and let $\sigma$ be the $p$-th cell. By assumption~1, both implementations process the same cell $\sigma$, and by assumption~2 they generate the same initial boundary $\partial\sigma$.

This boundary may contain generators that were cancelled during earlier steps. By the induction hypothesis, every such generator has the same stored replacement relation in both implementations. Consequently, both perform the same substitutions and obtain the same rewritten boundary.

If this boundary contains an uncancelled element of the same filtration value as $\sigma$, assumption~4 selects the same largest such element $x$ in both implementations. Hence, both cancel the same pair $x,\sigma$. Since the rewritten boundary is identical, they also store the same replacement relation for $x$.

If no same value element remains, both implementations retain $\sigma$ and store the same rewritten boundary as its compressed boundary column.

Thus, in either case, the surviving generators, cancellation relations, and compressed boundary columns agree after processing the $p$-th cell. By induction, the two implementations agree after every processed cell and therefore produce identical compressed filtered chain complexes.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
Any implementation change that affects only the storage representation preserves the deterministic value block output, provided that the four hypotheses of \cref{thm:streaming-equivalence} remain satisfied.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:operation-log}
Let $x$ be a coordinate vector in the original cell basis. During a left-to-right reduction, the vector $V^{-1}x$ can be computed from the operation log without forming $V$ or $V^{-1}$. For each target column $j$ that belongs to the original support of $x$, apply in chronological order all source toggles stored for target $j$. A source index that is created by such a toggle does not need recursive processing.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Consider an elementary column operation, for $i < j$,
\[
R_j\leftarrow R_j+R_i.
\]
When the corresponding elementary matrix acts on a coordinate vector, it toggles coordinate $i$ exactly when coordinate $j$ is present. Thus, each operation can only create or remove a coordinate with smaller index than its target.

Because the reduction proceeds from left to right, all operations whose target is $i$ occur before any operation whose target is a larger index $j$. Hence, if processing the log of target $j$ toggles a new coordinate $i<j$, the operations associated with target $i$ have already occurred in chronological order. They must therefore not be applied recursively.

It remains to determine which target indices $j$ should have their logs replayed. Before the reduction reaches column $j$, no earlier operation can change coordinate $j$: such a change would require $j$ to be the source of an operation with some larger target, and that operation occurs only later. Therefore, coordinate $j$ is present when its operations are reached if and only if $j$ belonged to the original support of $x$.

Consequently, replaying, in chronological order, the stored source toggles for exactly the target indices in the original support of $x$ reproduces the action of $V^{-1}$, without constructing either $V$ or $V^{-1}$.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:triangular-relations}
Let a projected kernel relation correspond to a dying birth label $i$. Its coefficient at $i$ is one, and every other active birth label occurring in the relation is smaller than $i$ in the fixed filtration-compatible order.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Before projection, the relation associated with the dying birth label $i$ has coefficient one at $i$, and every other nonzero coordinate has label smaller than $i$. Suppose that one such label $k<i$ died earlier. Its stored replacement relation contains $k$ together with only labels smaller than $k$. Eliminating $k$ therefore introduces only labels smaller than $k$, and hence smaller than $i$, while leaving the coefficient of $i$ unchanged. Repeating this substitution for every earlier-dead label yields a projected relation whose coefficient at $i$ is still one and whose remaining active labels are all smaller than $i$.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:unit-lower}
Order the $m$ dying labels at value $t$ by increasing birth index
\[
i_1<i_2<\cdots<i_m.
\]
Then the dying block $C_D$ in \cref{eq:event-matrix} is lower triangular with every diagonal entry equal to one. In particular, $C_D$ is invertible over $\mathbb F_2$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Row $r$ is the projected relation whose leading dying label is $i_r$. By \cref{lem:triangular-relations}, the coefficient of $i_r$ is one, while every other dying label appearing in that row has birth index smaller than $i_r$.

Since the columns are ordered
\[
i_1<i_2<\cdots<i_m,
\]
any such smaller dying label must occur in one of the columns $1,\ldots,r-1$. Thus all entries above the diagonal are zero, and every diagonal entry is one. Hence $C_D$ is unit lower triangular.

A triangular matrix over a field is invertible if and only if all of its diagonal entries are nonzero. Therefore, $C_D$ is invertible over $\mathbb F_2$.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
Since $C_D$ is unit lower triangular, $W_t$ can be obtained by solving
\[
C_DW_t=C_S
\]
by forward substitution without matrix inversion or pivot search.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:support-restriction}
Let $v\in\mathcal S_t$ be a surviving label that does not occur in any projected dying relation at $t$. Then omitting the column corresponding to $v$ from $C_S$ does not change any other transition coefficient and removes no persistence pairing graph edge.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Since $v$ does not occur in any projected relation, its column in $C_S$ is zero. Hence, the corresponding column of
\[
W_t=C_D^{-1}C_S
\]
is also zero. It therefore contributes no graph edge. Moreover, multiplication by $C_D^{-1}$ acts independently on each column of $C_S$, so removing this zero column does not affect any of the remaining transition coefficients.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
