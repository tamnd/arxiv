<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:topdual}
Let $D$ be Demu\v{s}kin and let $M$ be a discrete $\mathbb F_p[D]$-module. Then there is a natural isomorphism
\[H^2(D,M)^\vee \simeq \operatorname{Hom}_D(M,\mathbb F_p).\]
If $M=X^\vee$ for a compact $\mathbb F_p\llbracket D\rrbracket$-module $X$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:topdualfixed}
H^2(D,X^\vee)^\vee \simeq X^D.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For a finite discrete $M$, top-degree duality is the usual dualizing-module identity for a profinite duality group; see \cite[p.~1]{SchmidtWingberg}. Since $M$ is killed by $p$, the relevant homomorphisms into the dualizing module $I(D)$ take values in $I(D)[p]$. For a Demu\v{s}kin group this is a one-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-space carrying the mod-$p$ orientation action. That action is trivial, and hence 
\[I(D)[p]\simeq\mathbb F_p\]
as a $D$-module. Therefore
\[H^2(D,M)^\vee\simeq \operatorname{Hom}_D(M,\mathbb F_p)\]
for finite $M$.
		
Now let $M$ be arbitrary and discrete. Every element of $M$ has open stabilizer and hence finite orbit, so $M$ is the filtered union of its finite-dimensional $D$-submodules:
\[M=\varinjlim_\lambda M_\lambda.\]
Continuous cohomology commutes with filtered direct limits of discrete coefficient modules; see \cite[Proposition~1.5.1]{NSW}. Thus
\[H^2(D,M)\simeq \varinjlim_\lambda H^2(D,M_\lambda).\]
Taking Pontryagin duals gives
\[H^2(D,M)^\vee\simeq \varprojlim_\lambda H^2(D,M_\lambda)^\vee \simeq \varprojlim_\lambda \operatorname{Hom}_D(M_\lambda,\mathbb F_p) \simeq \operatorname{Hom}_D(M,\mathbb F_p).\]
		
If $M=X^\vee$, Pontryagin biduality identifies $\operatorname{Hom}_D(X^\vee,\mathbb F_p)$ with the $D$-fixed subgroup of $X^{\vee\vee}\simeq X$, which proves \eqref{eq:topdualfixed}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:tdim}
For a compact elementary abelian pro-$p$ group $X$, define the topological $\mathbb{F}_p$-rank on $X$ as
\[\operatorname{tdim}_{\mathbb F_p}X:=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}X^\vee.\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:topedge}
There is a natural isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge}
H^3(G,\mathbb F_p) \simeq H^2(D,V).
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[H^i(G,\mathbb F_p)=0 \qquad (i>3).\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The unique $E_2$-term of total degree three is
\[E_2^{2,1}=H^2(D,V).\]
		
For $r\ge2$, a differential entering this term would have source $E_r^{2-r,r}$. Such a source either lies outside the first quadrant or has second index at least two, where the spectral sequence vanishes.
		
A differential leaving $E_r^{2,1}$ has target $E_r^{2+r,2-r}$. For $r=2$, the target lies in column four and vanishes because $\operatorname{cd} D=2$; for $r>2$, the target lies outside the nonzero rows. Hence
\[E_\infty^{2,1}=E_2^{2,1}.\]
This is the only graded piece of the filtration of $H^3(G,\mathbb F_p)$, proving
\eqref{eq:edge}. The same support argument gives
\[H^i(G,\mathbb F_p)=0\qquad(i>3).\]
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:topfixed}
For every extension \eqref{eq:mainext},
\begin{equation}
H^3(G,\mathbb F_p)^\vee\simeq W^D =\bigl(N/\Phi(N)\bigr)^D.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:topdimension}
\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^3(G,\mathbb F_p)=\operatorname{tdim}_{\mathbb F_p} \bigl(N/\Phi(N)\bigr)^D.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Proposition~\ref{prop:topedge} gives
\[H^3(G,\mathbb F_p)\simeq H^2(D,W^\vee).\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:topdual} therefore yields
\[H^3(G,\mathbb F_p)^\vee\simeq H^2(D,W^\vee)^\vee \simeq W^D. \]
Equation \eqref{eq:topdimension} follows from Definition~\ref{def:tdim}.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:equiv}
Let
\[1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow1\]
be exact, with $N\neq1$ free pro-$p$ and $D$ Demu\v{s}kin. Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\operatorname{cd} G=3$;
\item $H^3(G,\mathbb F_p)\neq0$;
\item $H^2(D,H^1(N,\mathbb F_p))\neq0$;
\item $(N/\Phi(N))^D\neq0$;
\item there exists $n\in N\setminus\Phi(N)$ such that $[g,n]\in\Phi(N) \text{ for every }g\in G$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By \eqref{eq:upperbound}, $\operatorname{cd} G\le3$. Thus (i) and (ii) are equivalent by \eqref{eq:cdcriterion}. Proposition~\ref{prop:topedge} gives (ii) $\Longleftrightarrow$ (iii), and Theorem~\ref{thm:topfixed} gives (iii) $\Longleftrightarrow$ (iv). Finally, $n\Phi(N)$ is fixed by $D=G/N$ if and only if 
\[gng^{-1}n^{-1}\in\Phi(N)\qquad \text{for every }g\in G,\]
which is equivalent to
\[[g,n]\in\Phi(N).\]
The fixed class is nonzero precisely when $n\notin\Phi(N)$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:kappa}
Let
\[ 1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow1\]
be an exact sequence of pro-$p$ groups, where $N\neq1$ is free pro-$p$ and $D$ is Demu\v{s}kin. Writing 
\[W=N/\Phi(N),\]
define the kernel fixed-point rank of the extension by
\[\kappa(G,N):= \operatorname{tdim}_{\mathbb F_p} W^D =\operatorname{tdim}_{\mathbb F_p} \bigl(N/\Phi(N)\bigr)^D.\]
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:kappa}
For every free-by-Demu\v{s}kin extension,
\[\kappa(G,N)=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^3(G,\mathbb F_p).	\]
In particular, $\operatorname{cd} G=3 \text{ if and only if } \kappa(G,N)>0$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:open}
Let $U\le G$ be open, put
\[N_U=N\cap U,\]
and let $D_U$ be the image of $U$ in $D$. Then
\[\operatorname{cd} G=3 \iff \operatorname{cd} U=3 \iff \bigl(N_U/\Phi(N_U)\bigr)^{D_U}\neq0.\]
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The group $N_U$ is open in $N$ and hence is again free pro-$p$; see, for example, \cite{RZ}. Since $N_U$ has finite index in the nontrivial torsion-free pro-$p$ group $N$, it is nontrivial. The image $D_U$ is open in $D$, hence is again Demu\v{s}kin.
		
Cohomological dimension is invariant under passage to open subgroups in this finite-cohomological-dimension setting, so $\operatorname{cd} U=\operatorname{cd} G$. Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:equiv} to
\[1\longrightarrow N_U\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow D_U\longrightarrow1\]
gives the result.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:fixedfinite}
Let $P$ be a pro-$p$ group acting continuously on a nonzero finite-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-vector space $M$. Then $M^P\neq0$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The action has finite image. Since $P$ is pro-$p$, its image is a finite $p$-group $\overline P$. The group algebra $\mathbb F_p[\overline P]$ is local and has the trivial module as its unique simple module. Every nonzero finite-dimensional module has nonzero socle and therefore contains a nonzero trivial submodule.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:finite}
Suppose $N\neq1$ is finitely generated free pro-$p$. Then every extension
\[1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow1\]
with $D$ Demu\v{s}kin satisfies $\operatorname{cd} G=3$. Moreover,
\[\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^3(G,\mathbb F_p)=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(N/\Phi(N))^D\ge1.\]
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The Frattini quotient $	N/\Phi(N)$ is a nonzero finite-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-vector space. Lemma~\ref{lem:fixedfinite} gives
\[(N/\Phi(N))^D\neq0,\]
and Theorem~\ref{thm:equiv} applies.

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor:infiniterank}
If $\operatorname{cd} G\le2$, then $N$ has infinite rank and $(N/\Phi(N))^D=0$.
<!-- end corollary-4 -->

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:kappa-realizability}
Let $D$ be an infinite Demu\v{s}kin group. Then every cardinal $\lambda$ occurs as $\kappa(G,N)$ for some free-by-Demu\v{s}kin extension
\[1\longrightarrow N \longrightarrow G \longrightarrow D\longrightarrow 1\]
with $N$ of infinite rank. More precisely, for every finite integer $k\ge 0$, there exists such an extension with $N$ free pro-$p$ of countably infinite rank and
\[\kappa(G,N)=k.\]
For every infinite cardinal $\lambda$, one may take $N$ to be free pro-$p$ of rank $\lambda$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
We first realize the finite values. Fix $k\ge 0$. By the classification of infinite Demu\v{s}kin groups, the abelianization of $D$ has positive $\mathbb Z_p$-rank; see \cite{Labute} or \cite[Chapter~III, \S~9]{NSW}. Hence there is a continuous epimorphism
\[q:D \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb Z_p.\]
	
Let $N$ be a free pro-$p$ group of countably infinite rank with a convergent free basis
\[x_1,x_2,x_3,\ldots \longrightarrow 1.\]
For each $n\ge1$, let
\[K_n=\overline{\langle x_{n+1},x_{n+2},\ldots\rangle}^{\,N}\]
be the closed normal subgroup generated by the tail beginning with $x_{n+1}$. Then
\[F_n:=N/K_n\]
is the free pro-$p$ group on the images of $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, and the natural quotient maps give \(N\simeq\varprojlim_n F_n\). 
	
For each $n$, define an automorphism $f_n\in\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$ by
\[\begin{cases} 
f_n(x_j)=x_j&(j\le k),\\
f_n(x_j)=x_jx_{j+1}&(k<j<n),\; \; \text{ and }\\
f_n(x_n)=x_n&(n>k).
\end{cases}\]
If $n\le k$, this simply means that $f_n$ is the identity. Each $f_n$ is an automorphism. Indeed, it is triangular with respect to the free basis $x_1,\ldots,x_n$: beginning with $x_n$, which is fixed, one can recover successively $x_{n-1},x_{n-2},\ldots,x_{k+1}$ from their images. Equivalently, these are finite compositions of elementary Nielsen automorphisms.
	
The automorphisms $f_n$ are compatible with the natural projections $F_{n+1}\to F_n$. Hence they determine an automorphism
\[f\in\operatorname{Aut}(N)\]
satisfying
\[\begin{cases}
f(x_i)=x_i& (i\le k),\;\; \text{ and }\\
f(x_i)=x_ix_{i+1}&(i>k).
\end{cases}\]
	
We next show that the closed subgroup generated by $f$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p$. Put
\[H=\overline{\langle f\rangle}\le\operatorname{Aut}(N).\]
For every $n$, let
\[H_n=\overline{\langle f_n\rangle}\le\operatorname{Aut}(F_n).\]
The action of $f_n$ on the Frattini quotient $\displaystyle F_n/\Phi(F_n)\simeq\mathbb F_p^n$ is represented by a unitriangular matrix. Its image therefore lies in a finite $p$-subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_p)$. The kernel of the natural homomorphism
\[\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}\bigl(F_n/\Phi(F_n)\bigr)\]
is pro-$p$, and consequently $H_n$ is a pro-$p$ group.
	
For $n\ge k+2$, the automorphism $f_n$ has infinite order. Indeed, on the abelianization $F_n^{\mathrm{ab}}\simeq\mathbb Z_p^n$ we have
\[f_n(x_{n-1})=x_{n-1}+x_n,\;\; f_n(x_n)=x_n\]
in additive notation. Therefore, for every positive integer $m$,
\[f_n^m(x_{n-1})=x_{n-1}+mx_n.\]
Since $\mathbb Z_p$ is torsion-free, this is different from $x_{n-1}$ for every $m>0$. Thus $f_n$ has infinite order. Since $H_n$ is an infinite procyclic pro-$p$ group, it follows that
\[H_n\simeq\mathbb Z_p\qquad(n\ge k+2).\]
	
The actions on the quotients $F_n$ separate automorphisms of $N$. Hence the natural map
\[H\longrightarrow\prod_{n\ge1}H_n\]
is injective. Since each $H_n$ is pro-$p$, the group $H$ is pro-$p$. Moreover, the projection $H\to H_{k+2}$ is surjective, because the image contains the dense cyclic subgroup generated by $f_{k+2}$ and is compact, hence closed. Therefore $H$ is infinite. Since $H$ is by definition procyclic, we conclude that
\[H\simeq\mathbb Z_p.\]
	
We now compute the induced action on the Frattini module $W=N/\Phi(N)$. Write
\[e_i=x_i\Phi(N).\]
Since the free basis of $N$ converges to $1$, the elements $e_i$ form a topological basis and $\displaystyle W\simeq\prod_{i\ge1}\mathbb F_p e_i$. Let $\gamma=f$ denote the distinguished topological generator of $H$, and put
\[\Lambda=\mathbb F_p\llbracket H\rrbracket\simeq\mathbb F_p\llbracket T\rrbracket,\qquad T=\gamma-1.\]
The action of $f$ on $W$ is
\[\begin{cases} 
\gamma e_i=e_i&(i\le k),\;\; \text{ and }\\
\gamma e_i=e_i+e_{i+1}&(i>k).\end{cases}\]
Hence
\[\begin{cases} 
Te_i=0&(i\le k),\;\; \text{ while }\\
Te_i=e_{i+1}&(i>k).\end{cases}\]
	
Let $\displaystyle W_1=\overline{\langle e_{k+1},e_{k+2},\ldots\rangle}$. Then
\[W=\mathbb F_p^k\times W_1,\]
where $H$ acts trivially on the first factor. Define
\[\theta:\mathbb F_p\llbracket T\rrbracket\longrightarrow W_1\]
by
\[\theta\left(\sum_{j\ge0}a_jT^j\right)=\sum_{j\ge0}a_je_{k+1+j}.\]
This map is continuous and bijective. Moreover,
\[\theta\left(T\sum_{j\ge0}a_jT^j\right)=T\theta\left(\sum_{j\ge0}a_jT^j\right),\]
because $Te_{k+1+j}=e_{k+2+j}$. Thus $\theta$ is an isomorphism of compact $\mathbb F_p\llbracket H\rrbracket$-modules. Consequently,
\[W\simeq\mathbb F_p^k\times\mathbb F_p\llbracket H\rrbracket\simeq\mathbb F_p^k\times\mathbb F_p\llbracket T\rrbracket.\]
	
The regular module $\mathbb F_p\llbracket T\rrbracket$ has no nonzero $H$-fixed element. Indeed, an element $u\in\mathbb F_p\llbracket T\rrbracket$ is fixed by $H$ if and only if it is fixed by $\gamma$, equivalently \(Tu =0\). Since $\mathbb F_p\llbracket T\rrbracket$ is an integral domain, multiplication by $T$ is injective, and therefore $u=0$. Hence
\[W^H\simeq\mathbb F_p^k.\]
	
Choose an isomorphism \(\mathbb Z_p\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H\) sending $1$ to $f$, let $D$ act on $N$ through the composite
\[D\overset{q}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathbb Z_p\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H\le\operatorname{Aut}(N),\]
and form the semidirect product
\[G=N\rtimes D.\]
Then
\[1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow1\]
is a free-by-Demu\v{s}kin extension. Since the action of $D$ on $W$ factors surjectively through $H$,
\[W^D=W^H\simeq\mathbb F_p^k.\]
Therefore
\[\kappa(G,N)=\operatorname{tdim}_{\mathbb F_p}W^D=k.\]
This realizes every finite value $k\ge0$ with $N$ of countably infinite rank.
	
It remains to realize the infinite cardinal values. Let $\lambda$ be an infinite cardinal, let $N_\lambda$ be a free pro-$p$ group of rank $\lambda$ and let $D$ act trivially on $N_\lambda$, and put
\[G_\lambda=N_\lambda\times D.\]
Then
\[\bigl(N_\lambda/\Phi(N_\lambda)\bigr)^D=N_\lambda/\Phi(N_\lambda).\]
By the definition of the rank of a free pro-$p$ group, $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^1(N_\lambda,\mathbb F_p)=\lambda$. Since
\[H^1(N_\lambda,\mathbb F_p)=\bigl(N_\lambda/\Phi(N_\lambda)\bigr)^\vee,\]
Definition~\ref{def:tdim} gives $\operatorname{tdim}_{\mathbb F_p}\bigl(N_\lambda/\Phi(N_\lambda)\bigr)=\lambda$. Hence
\[\kappa(G_\lambda,N_\lambda)=\lambda.\]
	
Thus every cardinal occurs as the kernel fixed-point rank of a free-by-Demu\v{s}kin extension with free kernel of infinite rank, and every finite value already occurs with a countably infinite-rank kernel.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:duality}
If $N\neq1$ is finitely generated free pro-$p$ and $D$ is Demu\v{s}kin, then $G$ is a profinite duality group at $p$ of dimension three.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Schmidt--Wingberg's condition $FC(N,p)$ requires $H^i(U,\mathbb F_p)$ to be finite for every open subgroup $U\le N$ and every $i\ge0$; see \cite[p.~2]{SchmidtWingberg}. If $N$ is finitely generated free pro-$p$, then every open subgroup $U$ is again finitely generated free pro-$p$. Therefore
\[H^i(U,\mathbb F_p)=0\qquad(i\ge2),\]
while $H^0(U,\mathbb F_p)\simeq\mathbb F_p$ and $H^1(U,\mathbb F_p)$ is finite-dimensional. Hence $FC(N,p)$ holds.
		
The group $N$ is a duality group at $p$ of dimension one, and the Demu\v{s}kin group $D$ is a duality group at $p$ of dimension two. Schmidt--Wingberg's extension theorem \cite[Theorem~1(ii)]{SchmidtWingberg} therefore gives that $G$ is a duality group at $p$, with
\[\operatorname{cd} G=\operatorname{cd} N+\operatorname{cd} D= 1+2=3.\]

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:PDnecessary}
Assume that $N\neq1$ is finitely generated free pro-$p$, $D$ is Demu\v{s}kin, and $G$ is a pro-$p$ $PD^3$ group. Then $N\simeq\mathbb Z_p$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
By \cite[Corollary~1.5]{GJPPZ}, a finitely generated normal subgroup of a pro-$p$ $PD^3$ group with infinite quotient is either infinite cyclic with virtually Demu\v{s}kin quotient, or Demu\v{s}kin with virtually infinite-cyclic quotient.
		
Here $N$ is free pro-$p$ and $G/N=D$ is Demu\v{s}kin. The second alternative is impossible because a nontrivial free pro-$p$ group has cohomological dimension one. Hence $N\simeq\mathbb Z_p$.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:defect}
Let
\[1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow1\]
be as above, let
\[\tau:H^1(N,\mathbb F_p)^D\longrightarrow H^2(D,\mathbb F_p)\]
be the Hochschild--Serre transgression, and let
\[W_D=W/\overline{I_DW}.\]
Choose a nonzero element $\eta^\vee\in H^2(D,\mathbb F_p)^\vee$ and define
\[\delta_G:= \tau^\vee(\eta^\vee) \in W_D. \]
The class $\delta_G$ is well defined up to multiplication by a nonzero scalar, so the condition $\delta_G\neq0$ is intrinsic.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:relationdefect}
Let
\[1\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow1\]
be a minimal pro-$p$ presentation of $D$, with $F$ free pro-$p$, and write
\[R=\overline{\langle\!\langle r\rangle\!\rangle}\]
for a defining Demu\v{s}kin relation. Choose lifts in $G$ of a free basis of $F$. They determine a homomorphism
\[\alpha:F\longrightarrow G\]
lifting the identity on $D$. Put
\[w:=\alpha(r)\in N.\]
Then, up to the scalar normalization in Definition~\ref{def:defect},
\[\delta_G=\overline{w}\in W_D. \]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Because the presentation of $D$ is minimal, inflation induces an isomorphism
\[H^1(D,\mathbb F_p)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H^1(F,\mathbb F_p). \]
Since $F$ is free pro-$p$, $H^2(F,\mathbb F_p)=0$. The Hochschild--Serre five-term sequence \cite[Proposition~1.6.7]{NSW} therefore identifies the presentation transgression
\[\operatorname{trg}_R: H^1(R,\mathbb F_p)^D \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} H^2(D,\mathbb F_p).\]
		
The standard relation/transgression pairing is
\[ \frac{R}{R^p[R,F]} \times H^1(R,\mathbb F_p)^D \longrightarrow \mathbb F_p, \qquad
(\overline{s},\psi) \longmapsto \psi(s). \]
The canonical identification
\[H^1(R,\mathbb F_p)^D\simeq \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb F_p} \left(R/R^p[R,F],\mathbb F_p\right)\]
gives the perfect evaluation pairing
\[\frac{R}{R^p[R,F]} \times H^1(R,\mathbb F_p)^D \longrightarrow \mathbb F_p, \qquad (\overline{s},\psi) \longmapsto \psi(s). \]
For a minimal pro-$p$ presentation, the images of a minimal system of normal generators of $R$ form an $\mathbb F_p$-basis of $R/R^p[R,F]$; see \cite[Corollary~3.9.3]{NSW}. Since $D$ is one-relator and 
\[R=\overline{\langle\!\langle r\rangle\!\rangle},\]
the class of $r$ is a basis of this relation module.
		
		
The chosen lifts give a morphism of extensions
\[\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
1&\longrightarrow&R&\longrightarrow&F&\longrightarrow&D&\longrightarrow&1\\
&&\downarrow&&\downarrow\alpha&&\Vert\\
1&\longrightarrow&N&\longrightarrow&G&\longrightarrow&D&\longrightarrow&1
\end{array}
\]
Naturality of the five-term sequence gives the commutative square
\[\begin{array}{ccc}
H^1(N,\mathbb F_p)^D & \overset{\tau}{\longrightarrow} & H^2(D,\mathbb F_p)\\
\downarrow && \Vert\\
H^1(R,\mathbb F_p)^D & \overset{\operatorname{trg}_R}{\longrightarrow} & H^2(D,\mathbb F_p),
\end{array}\]
where the left vertical map is restriction along $R\longrightarrow N$. Let
\[\varphi\in H^1(N,\mathbb F_p)^D.\] Under the relation pairing, the presentation transgression is evaluated on the defining relation by
\[(\varphi\circ\alpha)(r)= \varphi(\alpha(r)) = \varphi(w). \]
Hence the functional on $V^D$ defined by the transgression is evaluation at the image of $w$.
		
Every $D$-invariant continuous functional on $W$ vanishes on $\displaystyle \overline{I_DW}$ and therefore factors through $W_D$. Under
\[(V^D)^\vee\simeq W_D,\]
the dual transgression is consequently represented by the class of $w$ in $W_D$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:lower}
Assume $W^D=0$. Then $\operatorname{cd} G=1$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lowerconditions}
H^1(D,W^\vee)=0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \delta_G\neq0.
\end{equation}
In every other case, $\operatorname{cd} G=2$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Since $W^D=0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:equiv} gives $\operatorname{cd} G\le2$. From \eqref{eq:H2low}, $H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)=0$ if and only if $H^1(D,V)=0$ and $\operatorname{coker}\tau=0$. The target of
\[\tau:V^D\longrightarrow H^2(D,\mathbb F_p)\]
is one-dimensional. Therefore
\[\operatorname{coker}\tau=0 \iff \tau\neq0 \iff \tau^\vee\neq0 \iff \delta_G\neq0.\]
Since $V=W^\vee$, we obtain
\[H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)=0 \text{ if and only if } H^1(D,W^\vee)=0\text{ and } \delta_G\neq0.\]
		
A nontrivial pro-$p$ group has cohomological dimension one precisely when $H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)=0$. If $H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)\neq0$, the inequality $\operatorname{cd} G\le2$ gives $\operatorname{cd} G=2$.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:projectsuff}
Let $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p\llbracket D\rrbracket$. If $W$ is projective as a pseudocompact left $\Lambda$-module, then
\[H^1(D,W^\vee)=0.\]
Consequently, if
\[W^D=0, \qquad W\text{ is projective}, \qquad \delta_G\neq0, \]
then $\operatorname{cd} G=1$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Pontryagin duality between continuous cohomology and profinite homology gives
\[H^1(D,W^\vee)^\vee \simeq H_1(D,W). \]
In the pseudocompact module category,
\[H_1(D,W) \simeq \operatorname{Tor}_1^\Lambda(\mathbb F_p,W). \]
Projectivity of $W$ makes this Tor group vanish, and hence $H^1(D,W^\vee)=0$. The final assertion follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:PDn}
Let
\[1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow1\]
be exact, where $N\neq1$ is free pro-$p$ and $Q$ is a pro-$p$ Poincar\'e-duality group of dimension $n$. Then
\[H^{n+1}(G,\mathbb F_p)^\vee \simeq \bigl(N/\Phi(N)\bigr)^Q. \]
Consequently, $\operatorname{cd} G=n+1$ if and only if $\bigl(N/\Phi(N)\bigr)^Q\neq0$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Since $\operatorname{cd} N=1$ and $\operatorname{cd} Q=n$, we have that $\operatorname{cd} G\le n+1$. In the Hochschild--Serre spectral sequence, the only possibly nonzero term of total degree $n+1$ is 
\[E_2^{n,1}=H^n\bigl(Q,H^1(N,\mathbb F_p)\bigr).\]
As in Proposition~\ref{prop:topedge}, no differential can enter or leave this term. Hence
\[H^{n+1}(G,\mathbb F_p)\simeq H^n\bigl(Q,H^1(N,\mathbb F_p)\bigr).\]
		
Put \[W=N/\Phi(N).\]
Top-degree $PD^n$ duality gives
\[H^n(Q,W^\vee)^\vee \simeq \operatorname{Hom}_Q(W^\vee,I(Q)[p]),\]
where $I(Q)$ is the dualizing module. The same passage from finite to arbitrary discrete coefficients used in Lemma~\ref{lem:topdual} applies here, using \cite[Proposition~1.5.1]{NSW}.
		
The mod-$p$ orientation action is trivial because $Q$ is pro-$p$, so
\[I(Q)[p]\simeq\mathbb F_p\]
as a $Q$-module. Therefore
\[H^{n+1}(G,\mathbb F_p)^\vee \simeq \operatorname{Hom}_Q(W^\vee,\mathbb F_p) \simeq W^Q.\]
The cohomological-dimension criterion completes the proof.
