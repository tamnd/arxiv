<!-- conjecture-1: body_md -->
\label{conj:original}
Let a finite group $G$ act effectively on a smooth
quasiprojective variety $X$. Suppose that every geometric
quotient $X^g/C_G(g)$ is smooth. Then $D^{\mathrm b}_G(X)$ admits a
semiorthogonal decomposition with one subcategory
$\mathcal A_{[g]}$ for each conjugacy class, such that
\[
 \mathcal A_{[g]}\simeqD^{\mathrm b}(X^g/C_G(g)).
\]
<!-- end conjecture-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For every integer $h\geq1$, let $C_h$ be the smooth
projective model of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family}
 y^2=t^{12h-5}+t.
\end{equation}
There is an effective $S_3$ action on $C_h$ for which:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\operatorname{genus}(C_h)=6h-3$ and $\operatorname{genus}(C_h/S_3)=h$;
\item there is exactly one nonfree orbit, with stabilizer
      of order three;
\item the fixed quotient for the three-cycle class is
      $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C\amalg\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C$, and that for the transposition class
      is empty;
\item there is no $\mathbb C$-linear exact fully faithful functor
      \[
       D^{\mathrm b}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C\amalg\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C)\longrightarrowD^{\mathrm b}_{S_3}(C_h).
      \]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, all the smoothness and effectivity hypotheses
of Conjecture~\ref{conj:original} hold, but its conclusion
fails.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:capacity}
An object $E$ of a $\mathbb C$-linear triangulated category is
\emph{exceptional} if $\operatorname{RHom}(E,E)\simeq\mathbb C$.
Exceptional objects $E_1,\ldots,E_m$ are
\emph{completely orthogonal} if
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(E_i,E_j[q])=0
 \qquad(q\in\mathbb Z,\ i\ne j).
\]
Write $\operatorname{oc}(\mathcal T)$ for the supremum of the lengths
of such collections in $\mathcal T$, allowing the empty
collection.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:capacity}
Let $G$ act effectively on a smooth connected projective
complex curve $C$, and put $Y=C/G$. Let $e_1,\ldots,e_b$
be the inertia orders at the branch points.
If $\operatorname{genus}(Y)\geq1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacity}
 \operatorname{oc}\bigl(D^{\mathrm b}_G(C)\bigr)
 =
 \sum_{i=1}^b\left\lfloor\frac{e_i}{2}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
In particular, a $\mathbb C$-linear exact fully faithful functor
$D^{\mathrm b}(\mathbb C^n)\longrightarrowD^{\mathrm b}_G(C)$ exists if and only if
$n$ does not exceed the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:capacity}.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:torsion}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity}, every exceptional
object of $D^{\mathrm b}_G(C)$ is a shift of an indecomposable finite-length
equivariant sheaf supported on one nonfree orbit.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The category $\operatorname{Coh}_G(C)$ is hereditary. For coherent sheaves $F,H$,
one has $\operatorname{Ext}^q_G(F,H)=\operatorname{Ext}^q_C(F,H)^G$; invariants are exact over
$\mathbb C$. On a smooth curve the latter Ext groups vanish for $q>1$.
For example, this follows from the local-to-global spectral sequence:
the sheaf $\mathcal E xt^1(F,H)$ has finite support, and higher local
Ext and second sheaf cohomology vanish. In a hereditary category
every bounded complex splits as the direct sum of its shifted
cohomology sheaves, by the truncation triangles. An exceptional
object has no nontrivial direct-sum decomposition, so it is a shift
of one indecomposable sheaf.

A coherent equivariant sheaf splits as a torsion sheaf and a vector
bundle. In fact its torsion-free quotient $V$ is locally free, and
for a finite-length sheaf $T$,
$\operatorname{Ext}^1_G(V,T)=H^1(C,V^\vee\otimes T)^G=0$.
If $V$ has positive rank, the identity and normalized trace split
$\mathcal O_C$ off $\mathcal E nd(V)$ equivariantly. Therefore
\[
 H^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y)
 \cong H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)^G
 \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Ext}^1_G(V,V).
\]
The first isomorphism follows from the finite quotient map and exact
invariants. Since $\operatorname{genus}(Y)\geq1$, a vector bundle cannot be
exceptional.

A torsion sheaf splits equivariantly over the $G$-orbits in its
support. Indecomposability leaves just one orbit. At a free orbit,
equivariant finite-length sheaves are equivalent to finite-length
modules over a discrete valuation ring. Its unique simple module
has one-dimensional Hom and Ext$^1$ with itself, so the Euler
pairing is zero on the finite-length Grothendieck group. No object
supported on a free orbit can consequently be exceptional.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:localbound}
Among the exceptional objects supported on $D$, the maximum number
that can be pairwise completely orthogonal is $\lfloor e/2\rfloor$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $v$ is the class of an exceptional object, then $\chi(v,v)=1$.
By \eqref{eq:euler}, the integral vector $\Delta v$ has squared
length two and coordinate sum zero. Thus
$\Delta v=\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_b$ for two distinct indices
$a,b$, where $\varepsilon_i$ is a standard basis vector of $\mathbb Z^e$.

Two vectors of this form have zero dot product exactly when their
two-element endpoint sets are disjoint: identical endpoint sets give
dot product $2$ or $-2$, and exactly one common endpoint gives $1$
or $-1$. Complete orthogonality implies zero symmetric Euler pairing,
so \eqref{eq:euler} forces disjoint endpoint sets. A collection of
length $m$ therefore uses $2m$ distinct indices, proving $2m\leq e$.

The bound is attained. The simples with indices
$0,2,\ldots,2\lfloor e/2\rfloor-2$ are exceptional, and no two
chosen indices are adjacent in the cyclic order. Equation~\eqref{eq:simples}
shows complete orthogonality. Inducing these modules to $D$ gives
the desired equivariant sheaves.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:torsion}, each exceptional object lies on one
nonfree orbit. Lemma~\ref{lem:localbound} gives the upper bound at
each orbit. Objects with disjoint supports are completely orthogonal,
so choosing the local collections just constructed attains the sum
in \eqref{eq:capacity}.

The images of the $n$ simple objects of $D^{\mathrm b}(\mathbb C^n)$ under any exact
fully faithful functor are completely orthogonal exceptional objects.
This gives necessity. Conversely, any collection of $n$ such objects
defines a fully faithful functor: on the $i$th summand of
$D^{\mathrm b}(\mathbb C^n)$ send a bounded complex of vector spaces $V$ to
$V\otimes_\mathbb C E_i$, and take the direct sum. This proves sufficiency.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:necessary}
For a curve as in Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity}, let
$n_g=\#(C^g/C_G(g))$ for $g\ne1$. If Conjecture~\ref{conj:original}
holds, then $n_g\leq\sum_i\lfloor e_i/2\rfloor$ for every such $g$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Each required subcategory supplies a fully faithful embedding of
$D^{\mathrm b}(\mathbb C^{n_g})$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity}.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:action}
The maps in \eqref{eq:action} extend to automorphisms of $C_h$ and
generate an effective $S_3$ action. The only nonfree orbit is
$D=\{P_0,P_\infty\}$, where $P_0$ and $P_\infty$ lie above
$t=0$ and $t=\infty$. Its stabilizers are $\langle r\rangle$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Put $N=12h-5$ and $\ell=6h-2$. The polynomial $t^N+t$ is
squarefree: its derivative is $1$ at zero, and at a nonzero root
$t^{N-1}=-1$ the derivative is $1-N\ne0$. Since $N$ is odd,
the smooth projective hyperelliptic curve has genus
$(N-1)/2=6h-3$ and a unique point at infinity.

The congruence $N\equiv1\pmod3$ shows that $r$ preserves the
equation. The identity
$(t^N+t)/t^{N+1}=t^{-N}+t^{-1}$ and the equality $2\ell=N+1$
show the same for $s$. These function-field automorphisms extend
uniquely to the smooth projective model. They satisfy $r^3=s^2=1$.
For the remaining relation, substitution gives
\[
 srs(t,y)=(\omega^{-1}t,\omega^{2-\ell}y)
          =(\omega^{-1}t,\omega y)=r^{-1}(t,y),
\]
because $\ell\equiv1\pmod3$. The transformations $r^i$ and $r^is$
for $0\leq i<3$ are distinct already on $t$. Thus the action is
effective and the generated group is $S_3$.

The maps $r$ and $r^2$ fix exactly $P_0$ and $P_\infty$.
The involution $s$ exchanges these points. A fixed point of $s$
elsewhere would have $t=1$ or $t=-1$. Since $\ell$ is even, $s$
negates $y$ at these two values, but $y^2=2$ and $y^2=-2$,
respectively. It has no fixed point. All transpositions are
conjugate to $s$, so they have no fixed points either. This proves
the asserted stabilizers and exhausts the nonfree locus.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:quotient}
The quotient $Y_h=C_h/S_3$ has genus $h$. More explicitly, it is
the smooth projective model of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient}
 b^2=(u^2-4)P_{2h-1}(u),
\end{equation}
where $P_0(u)=2$, $P_1(u)=u$, and
$P_{n+1}(u)=uP_n(u)-P_{n-1}(u)$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Set $x=t^3$ and $v=ty$. They are $r$-invariant and satisfy
$v^2=x^{4h-1}+x$. They generate the fixed field of
$\langle r\rangle$: adjoining $t$ recovers $y=v/t$ and has degree
three. On this intermediate field, $s$ sends
$(x,v)$ to $(x^{-1},-v/x^{2h})$.

Now put $u=x+x^{-1}$ and $b=v x^{-h}(x-x^{-1})$. Both are
$s$-invariant. The recurrence gives
$P_n(x+x^{-1})=x^n+x^{-n}$, so their relation is
\eqref{eq:quotient}. Conversely,
$x^2-ux+1=0$ and $v=b x^h/(x-x^{-1})$. The extension of
$\mathbb C(u,b)$ obtained by adjoining $x,v$ therefore has degree at most
two, and exactly two because $s$ is nontrivial. Consequently
$\mathbb C(u,b)=\mathbb C(C_h)^{S_3}$.

For $n=2h-1$, the identity
$P_n(2\cos\theta)=2\cos(n\theta)$ gives $n$ distinct roots
strictly between $-2$ and $2$. Also $P_n(2)=2$ and $P_n(-2)=-2$.
Thus the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:quotient} is squarefree of
degree $2h+1$, and its smooth projective model has genus $h$.
The same genus follows from Riemann--Hurwitz using
Proposition~\ref{prop:action}:
\[
 2(6h-3)-2=6\bigl(2\operatorname{genus}(Y_h)-2\bigr)+2(3-1).
\]
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Propositions~\ref{prop:action} and \ref{prop:quotient} establish
the action, genera, and stabilizers. The three conjugacy classes
are represented by $1,r,s$. Their fixed quotients are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 g & C_h^g & C_h^g/C_{S_3}(g)\\ \hline
 1 & C_h & Y_h\\
 r & \{P_0,P_\infty\} & \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C\amalg\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C\\
 s & \varnothing & \varnothing.
\end{array}
\]
Indeed $C_{S_3}(r)=\langle r\rangle$ fixes the two points
individually. These are smooth geometric quotients. There is one
branch point of $Y_h$, with inertia order three, and
$\operatorname{genus}(Y_h)=h\geq1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity} gives
$\operatorname{oc}(D^{\mathrm b}_{S_3}(C_h))=1$. The derived category of two disjoint
points would require two completely orthogonal exceptional objects,
which is impossible.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:ordered}
There is a semiorthogonal decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordered}
 D^{\mathrm b}_G(C)=
 \left\langle\pi^*D^{\mathrm b}(Y),\mathcal B_1,\ldots,\mathcal B_b\right\rangle,
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_i=\left\langle M_{i,e_i-1},\ldots,M_{i,1}\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
The $\mathcal B_i$ are pairwise completely orthogonal. Each indicated
collection in $\mathcal B_i$ is strong exceptional, and
$\mathcal B_i\simeqD^{\mathrm b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbb C A_{e_i-1})$, where
$A_{e_i-1}$ is a linearly oriented quiver of type $A$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The finite quotient map is flat because both curves are smooth.
Its equivariant direct image followed by invariants is an exact
right adjoint to $\pi^*$, and
$(\pi_*\mathcal O_C)^G=\mathcal O_Y$. The projection formula
therefore proves that $\pi^*$ is fully faithful.

At an orbit of order $e$, omit the index $i$ and use the local
coordinates of Section~\ref{sec:capacity}. The module for $M_l$ is
$R/(t^l)$ with trivial character on its generator. Its resolution is
\[
 0\longrightarrow R\otimes\alpha^l
 \xrightarrow{\ t^l\ }R\longrightarrow M_l\longrightarrow0.
\]
For $1\leq l,m<e$, applying equivariant Hom to $M_m$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thickenings}
 \operatorname{Hom}_G(M_l,M_m)=
 \begin{cases}\mathbb C&l\geq m,\\0&l<m,\end{cases}
 \qquad \operatorname{Ext}^q_G(M_l,M_m)=0\quad(q>0).
\end{equation}
To see this explicitly, the invariant subspace of $M_m$ is
$\mathbb C\cdot1$. Its $\alpha^l$ subspace is zero when $l\geq m$,
and is $\mathbb C\cdot t^l$ when $l<m$. In the latter case the
differential sends $1$ to $t^l$ and is an isomorphism. This proves
\eqref{eq:thickenings}. For $l\geq m$ the nonzero Hom is generated
by the canonical quotient map, and compositions of these maps are
nonzero. Thus the descending collection is strong exceptional,
and the endomorphism algebra of its direct sum is the path algebra
of the stated linearly oriented quiver, up to taking the opposite
orientation. Derived Morita equivalence on the subcategory generated
by this direct sum gives the asserted equivalence for $\mathcal B_i$.

Objects supported on distinct $D_i$ have no Ext. Also
$\operatorname{RHom}_G(M_{i,l},\pi^*F)=0$ for $l<e_i$ and every
$F\inD^{\mathrm b}(Y)$. It suffices to take $F$ locally free, since $Y$
is smooth and quasiprojective. Locally its pullback is a direct sum
of copies of $R$ with invariant generators. There is no Hom from
$M_l$ to $R$. The cokernel computing Ext$^1$ from the displayed
resolution has no invariant part when $0<l<e$, so this Ext also
vanishes. Completion preserves these finite-support computations,
giving the required semiorthogonality globally.

For generation, the scheme-theoretic fiber at $y_i$ has multiplicity
$e_i$, hence
\[
 \pi^*\mathcal O_{y_i}=M_{i,e_i}.
\]
The category generated by the proposed components contains all
$M_{i,l}$ for $1\leq l\leq e_i$. Locally $M_1=S_0$, and the
short exact sequences
\[
 0\longrightarrow S_{l-1}\longrightarrow M_l
 \longrightarrow M_{l-1}\longrightarrow0\qquad(2\leq l\leq e_i)
\]
recover every character simple at $D_i$. Induction identifies these
with the global equivariant simples. They generate every
finite-length sheaf supported on $D_i$. At a free orbit, finite-length
equivariant sheaves descend to the corresponding finite-length
sheaves on $Y$, and are already in $\pi^*D^{\mathrm b}(Y)$. Thus every
torsion sheaf belongs to the generated category.

Finally let $E$ be an equivariant vector bundle and put
$V=(\pi_*E)^G$. This is locally free on $Y$, since it is a direct
summand of the locally free sheaf $\pi_*E$. The counit
$\pi^*V\to E$ is an isomorphism off the branch orbits by descent
for the free action. It is consequently injective, being a
generically invertible map between vector bundles of equal rank.
Its cokernel is a torsion sheaf supported on the branch orbits.
It follows that $E$ is generated as well. Every coherent sheaf is
an extension of a vector bundle by a torsion sheaf, proving full
generation of $D^{\mathrm b}_G(C)$.

Each exceptional subcategory here is admissible: Hom to and from
its generator has finite-dimensional total cohomology because the
generator has proper finite support. The exceptional projection
triangles, together with the established orthogonality and
generation, yield the semiorthogonal decomposition
\eqref{eq:ordered}.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:refinedcurve}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:refined} holds for every effective finite group
action on a smooth connected quasiprojective complex curve $C$.
The embedding for the identity component is $\pi^*$, and all the
embeddings can be chosen $\operatorname{Perf}(Y)$-linear for their natural maps
to $Y=C/G$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The identity fixed quotient is the connected curve $Y$. Every
connected component of a nonidentity fixed quotient is a point.
By \eqref{eq:sectors}, there are exactly $e_i-1$ such points over
$y_i$, across all nonidentity classes. Match them bijectively with
the exceptional objects $M_{i,e_i-1},\ldots,M_{i,1}$, and keep the
order in \eqref{eq:ordered}. This gives the claimed decomposition.
Only a labeling of the separate point components is used; no
canonical identification of a character with a group element is
required.

For linearity, $\pi^*$ is $\operatorname{Perf}(Y)$-linear by the projection
formula. If $l<e_i$, then $M_{i,l}$ is annihilated by the pullback
of the maximal ideal of $y_i$, since
$\pi^{-1}(y_i)=e_iD_i$ scheme-theoretically. It therefore defines
a coherent equivariant kernel on
$\{y_i\}\times_Y C$. The point embedding
\[
 D^{\mathrm b}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C)\longrightarrowD^{\mathrm b}_G(C),
 \qquad V\longmapsto V\otimes_\mathbb C M_{i,l},
\]
is fully faithful by exceptionality. For $P\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)$ the
kernel structure gives the natural derived isomorphism
\[
 (V\otimes M_{i,l})\otimes^{\mathbf L}\pi^*P
 \simeq (V\otimes^{\mathbf L}P|_{y_i})\otimes M_{i,l}.
\]
Its associativity and unit compatibilities follow from those of
derived tensor product. This proves the asserted linearity.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:linear}
For a linear action on a vector space $V$, the variety
$V^g/C_G(g)$ is irreducible, being a finite quotient of a vector
space. Thus the original and connected-component formulations
coincide in that setting. The curve counterexamples do not give
counterexamples for linear reflection-group actions.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:localfusion}
Let $G$ act effectively on a smooth connected quasiprojective
complex curve $C$, and let $g\ne1$. If there is a fully
faithful $\operatorname{Perf}(Y)$-linear exact functor
\[
 D^{\mathrm b}(C^g/C_G(g))\longrightarrowD^{\mathrm b}_G(C)
\]
for the natural maps to $Y=C/G$, then $|[g]\cap H_i|
 \leq\left\lfloor\frac{e_i}{2}\right\rfloor$
for every branch orbit $D_i$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $p$ be a point of $C^g/C_G(g)$ mapping to $y_i$, and
let $E$ be the image of $\mathcal O_p$. For every closed
point $z\ne y_i$ of $Y$, the sheaf $\mathcal O_z$ is
perfect and $\mathcal O_p\otimes_Y^{\mathbf L}\mathcal O_z=0$.
Linearity therefore gives
$E\otimes^{\mathbf L}\pi^*\mathcal O_z=0$.
Derived Nakayama at the points over $z$ shows that $E$
has no support there. Thus $E$ is supported on $D_i$.

By \eqref{eq:sectors}, there are $|[g]\cap H_i|$ source
points over $y_i$. Their images are completely orthogonal
exceptional objects supported on $D_i$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:localbound} gives the asserted inequality.
No genus assumption is used.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:typeAblocks}
Let $m_1,\ldots,m_s$ be nonnegative integers with
$\sum_{j=1}^s m_j=n\geq1$. Suppose that
$\max_j m_j\leq\left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil$.
Then there is a semiorthogonal decomposition
\[
 D^{\mathrm b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbb C A_n)
 =
 \left\langle\mathcal U_1,\ldots,\mathcal U_s\right\rangle,
 \qquad
 \mathcal U_j\simeqD^{\mathrm b}(\mathbb C^{m_j}).
\]
The order of the labelled blocks may be prescribed in
advance. Blocks with $m_j=0$ are omitted.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
We first colour the $n$ vertices of a path so that colour
$j$ occurs $m_j$ times and adjacent vertices have different
colours. Put $d=\lceil n/2\rceil$. List the colour blocks
in decreasing order of multiplicity and fill the positions
$1,3,\ldots,2d-1,\;2,4,\ldots,2\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.
Occurrences of a colour lying entirely in odd positions or
entirely in even positions are nonadjacent. At most one
colour crosses between the two sets of positions. If it
starts at position $2a+1$ and occupies the first $b$ even
positions, its multiplicity is $d-a+b$. A crossing colour
has multiplicity strictly less than $d$: otherwise, an
earlier colour would already occupy all $d$ odd positions.
Hence $b<a$, so its even positions lie strictly before its
odd positions and no two occurrences are adjacent.

Write $c(v)$ for the colour of vertex $v$, with colours
ordered by the prescribed block order. Orient each edge
from the endpoint of smaller colour to the endpoint of
larger colour, obtaining a quiver $Q$. Let $S_v$ be its
vertex simples in $\mathrm{rep}_\mathbb C Q$. For distinct vertices,
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(S_u,S_v)=0,\qquad
 \dim_\mathbb C\operatorname{Ext}^1(S_u,S_v)
 =
 \#\{\text{arrows }u\longrightarrow v\},
\]
and all higher Ext groups vanish. Thus the simples of a
fixed colour are completely orthogonal exceptional objects.
If $b>a$, then
$\operatorname{RHom}(S_v,S_u)=0$, whenever $c(v)=b, c(u)=a$.
The simples generate $D^{\mathrm b}(\mathrm{rep}_\mathbb C Q)$, so grouping
them by colour gives the required ordered blocks.

Any two orientations of a path are connected by source
and sink reflections. Their derived reflection functors
are equivalences; see \cite[Section~3.1]{Ladkani}.
Transporting the blocks along such an equivalence proves
the assertion for $D^{\mathrm b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbb C A_n)$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:fusioncriterion}
Let a finite group $G$ act effectively on a smooth connected
quasiprojective complex curve $C$. The following conditions
are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Conjecture~\ref{conj:original} admits a realization
whose embeddings are $\operatorname{Perf}(Y)$-linear for the natural
maps of the fixed quotients to $Y=C/G$.
\item For every branch inertia group $H_i$ and every
nonidentity conjugacy class,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fusioncriterion}
 |[g]\cap H_i|
 \leq\left\lfloor\frac{e_i}{2}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
When these conditions hold, the identity block may be chosen
as $\pi^*D^{\mathrm b}(Y)$, and the nonidentity blocks may occur in any
prescribed total order. All embeddings can be given by
$G$-equivariant kernels on the natural fibre products
\[
 (C^g/C_G(g))\times_Y C.
\]
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
The implication from (1) to (2) follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:localfusion}.

Conversely, suppose \eqref{eq:fusioncriterion} holds.
Fix a total order $[g_1],\ldots,[g_s]$ of the nonidentity
conjugacy classes, and put
$m_{i,[g]}=|[g]\cap H_i|$.
By equation~\eqref{eq:count}, we have 
$\sum_{[g]\ne[1]}m_{i,[g]}=e_i-1$.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:ordered},
\[
 \mathcal B_i
 \simeqD^{\mathrm b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbb C A_{e_i-1}).
\]
Since
$\lceil(e_i-1)/2\rceil=\lfloor e_i/2\rfloor$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeAblocks} gives
\[
 \mathcal B_i
 =
 \left\langle
   \mathcal B_{i,[g_1]},\ldots,\mathcal B_{i,[g_s]}
 \right\rangle,
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_{i,[g]}
 \simeqD^{\mathrm b}(\mathbb C^{m_{i,[g]}}),
\]
in the chosen class order.

Different branch blocks are completely orthogonal.
Consequently, the subcategories
\[
 \mathcal A_{[g]}
 =
 \bigoplus_i\mathcal B_{i,[g]}
\]
satisfy
\[
 \mathcal A_{[g]}
 \simeqD^{\mathrm b}(\mathbb C^{n_g})
 \simeqD^{\mathrm b}(C^g/C_G(g)),
 \qquad
 n_g=\sum_i m_{i,[g]}.
\]
Regrouping the local decompositions in the chosen class
order preserves semiorthogonality and generation.
Together with \eqref{eq:ordered}, this gives
\[
 D^{\mathrm b}_G(C)
 =
 \left\langle
   \pi^*D^{\mathrm b}(Y),
   \mathcal A_{[g_1]},\ldots,\mathcal A_{[g_s]}
 \right\rangle.
\]

To construct the kernels, put
$T_i=\bigoplus_{l=1}^{e_i-1}M_{i,l}$.
Derived Morita equivalence for the strong exceptional
collection in Proposition~\ref{prop:ordered} shows that
every object of $\mathcal B_i$ has a bounded representative
with terms in $\operatorname{add}(T_i)$.

The scheme-theoretic fibre over $y_i$ is $e_iD_i$.
Each $M_{i,l}$ with $l<e_i$ is annihilated by its ideal.
Hence every exceptional generator constructed above can
be represented by a bounded complex of coherent
$G$-equivariant sheaves on
$F_i=\{y_i\}\times_Y C$.
For a fixed class $[g]$, the bijection \eqref{eq:sectors}
identifies its $m_{i,[g]}$ source points over $y_i$.
Match them with the exceptional generators of
$\mathcal B_{i,[g]}$. Placing the corresponding complexes
on the components $\{p\}\times_Y C$ gives a kernel on
$(C^g/C_G(g))\times_Y C$. Its Fourier--Mukai functor sends
the point simples to the chosen completely orthogonal
exceptional generators and is therefore fully faithful.

For a source point $p$ over $y_i$, denote its assigned
generator by $E_p$. The projection formula gives, for
$P\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)$,
\[
 E_p\otimes^{\mathbf L}\pi^*P
 \simeq
 E_p\otimes_\mathbb C^{\mathbf L}P|_{y_i},
\]
where $P|_{y_i}$ denotes derived restriction.
The unit and associativity compatibilities follow from
derived tensor product. Thus the point embeddings are
$\operatorname{Perf}(Y)$-linear. The identity embedding $\pi^*$ is
linear by the projection formula.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:nofusion}
Suppose that $|[g]\cap H_i|\leq1$ for every branch inertia
group $H_i$ and every nonidentity conjugacy class $[g]$.
Then Conjecture~\ref{conj:original} holds, with
$\operatorname{Perf}(Y)$-linear embeddings given by kernels on the
natural fibre products. In particular, this applies to
finite abelian groups acting effectively on smooth
connected quasiprojective complex curves.
