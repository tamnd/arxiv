\label{II:thm:L3}
For $L=3$ put $\Pi_3=-yA$ and $\delta_0=H^2/2$. Then
\[
 I^{(3)}_\circleddash(s)=\Pi_3\bigl[4R^3+f_2R^2+f_1R+f_0-16\zeta(3)\bigr],
\]
with
\[
 f_2=-\theta_Q\mathcal D_0^\Delta,\qquad
 f_1=-2\mathcal D_0^\Delta-\theta_Q\mathcal D_1^\Delta,\qquad
 f_0=-\mathcal D_1^\Delta-\theta_Q\mathcal D_2^\Delta,
\]
and $\mathcal D_0^\Delta=-12K_2$, $\mathcal D_1^\Delta=-24K_3$, $\mathcal D_2^\Delta=-24K_4$.
In particular the coefficient of $R^2$ is $-\theta_Q\mathcal F_{\gamma_3}$, where
$\mathcal F_{\gamma_3}=\mathcal D_0^\Delta$ is the one-point
potential~\eqref{II:eq:FgammaL} of the local Calabi--Yau fourfold $X_3$ with insertion
$\gamma_3=H^2/2$.

\label{II:cor:L3F}
Write $\mathcal F=\mathcal F_{\gamma_3}$, and let $\theta_Q^{-1}$ act on power series
without constant term by $\theta_Q^{-1}\sum_{m\ge1}c_mQ^m=\sum_{m\ge1}(c_m/m)Q^m$.

 \mathcal D_2^\Delta=\frac{\mathcal F^2}{12},\qquad

\[
 I^{(3)}_\circleddash(s)=-yA\bigl[4(R-u)^3+4u^3-\mathcal D_1^\Delta-2u\mathcal F
 -16\zeta(3)\bigr].
\]

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{II:cor:L4F}

$h_3=-\theta_Q\mathcal F_{\gamma_4}$ and
$h_2=-3\mathcal F_{\gamma_4}-\theta_Q\mathcal D_1^\Delta$, while

$\mathcal D_0^\Delta=\mathcal F_{\gamma_4}$. Thus the four-loop Feynman integral is
determined by the pair
$
\left(\mathcal F_{\gamma_4},\mathcal D_1^\Delta\right),
$
or equivalently by
$
\left(\mathcal F_{\gamma_4},\mathcal T\right),
$
where $\mathcal T$ is the diagonal two-point primary series of

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{l3:prop:convergence}
The integral~\eqref{l3:eq:schwinger3} is absolutely convergent for
$\operatorname{Re}s\le16$ and holomorphic for $\operatorname{Re}s<16$.
Absolute convergence fails for $\operatorname{Re}s>16$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Since $|e^{sW}|=e^{aW}$ with $a=\operatorname{Re}s$, absolute convergence
depends only on $a$.  Write $x_i=ru_i$, where $r>0$, $u_i>0$, and $\sum u_i=1$;
the Jacobian of $(r,u_1,u_2,u_3)\mapsto x$ is $r^3$, so
$\prod_i\,\mathrm d x_i/x_i=(\,\mathrm d r/r)\,\,\mathrm d^3u/(u_1u_2u_3u_4)$ on the open simplex
$\Delta_3$.  Both $V$ and $W$ are homogeneous of degree one:
$V(x)=r$, $W(x)=rw(u)$ with $w(u)=(\sum u_i^{-1})^{-1}$.  The
arithmetic--harmonic mean inequality gives $w\le1/16$ on $\Delta_3$, with
equality only at $u_*=(1/4,\ldots,1/4)$.  Hence for $a\le16$ one has
$1-aw(u)>0$ for every $u\in\Delta_3\setminus\{u_*\}$, and on that set the radial
integral is $\int_0^\infty e^{-r(1-aw)}rw\,\,\mathrm d r/r=w/(1-aw)$.  By Tonelli
(the integrand is nonnegative, and $\{u_*\}$ is a null set), absolute
convergence is equivalent to finiteness of
\[
 \int_{\Delta_3}\frac{w(u)}{u_1u_2u_3u_4[1-aw(u)]}\,\,\mathrm d^3u .
\]
For $a<16$ the integrand is bounded by the one for $a=16$, so it suffices to
treat $a=16$.  Put $e_3(u)=\sum_j\prod_{k\ne j}u_k$ and $e_4(u)=u_1u_2u_3u_4$,
so that $w=e_4/e_3$ and the integrand equals $1/P(u)$ with
$P=e_3(1-16w)=e_3-16e_4$.  On the simplex,
\begin{equation}\label{l3:eq:P-identity}
 P(u)=\sum_{i<j}u_ku_l(u_i-u_j)^2,\qquad \{k,l\}=\{1,2,3,4\}\setminus\{i,j\};
\end{equation}
indeed, expanding the right side gives $\sum_iu_i^2e_2(u_{\hat\imath})-12e_4$
(where $e_2(u_{\hat\imath})$ is the second elementary symmetric function of the
three variables other than $u_i$), and $\sum_iu_i=1$ gives
$e_3=\sum_iu_ie_3(u)=\sum_iu_i^2e_2(u_{\hat\imath})+4e_4$.
Also $\sum_{i<j}(u_i-u_j)^2=4\sum_iu_i^2-1=4\lVert u-u_*\rVert^2$ on the simplex.
In the neighborhood $U=\{\max_iu_i-\min_iu_i<1/8\}$ of $u_*$ all $u_i$ are
bounded below by $1/8$ (since $\max_iu_i\ge1/4$); hence the weights $u_ku_l$ in
\eqref{l3:eq:P-identity} lie in $[1/64,1]$ and $P\asymp\lVert u-u_*\rVert^2$ on
$U$.  Thus $1/P$ is locally integrable near $u_*$ in dimension three.  On the
ordered chamber $u_1\ge u_2\ge u_3\ge u_4$, outside $U$ one has
$u_1-u_4\ge1/8$ and, keeping only the term $(i,j)=(1,4)$ of
\eqref{l3:eq:P-identity}, $P\ge u_2u_3(u_1-u_4)^2\ge u_2u_3/64$; enlarging the
domain of integration to $0\le u_4\le u_3\le u_2\le1$ gives
\[
 \int\frac{\,\mathrm d u_2\,\,\mathrm d u_3\,\,\mathrm d u_4}{P}
 \le64\int_0^1\,\mathrm d u_2\int_0^{u_2}\,\mathrm d u_3\int_0^{u_3}
 \frac{\,\mathrm d u_4}{u_2u_3}=64.
\]
The $S_4$-symmetry of the integrand completes the estimate.  Holomorphy on
$\operatorname{Re}s<16$ follows by dominated convergence on compact subsets.
For $a>16$, the factor $1-aw(u)$ is negative on an open neighborhood of
$u_*$, and the radial integral diverges there, so absolute convergence fails.

\label{l3:lem:frob-form}
There are unique convergent power series $\hat f_0,\hat f_1,\hat f_2\in\mathbb C\{Q\}$
such that, on the Euclidean germ $s=-t$ with $t$ sufficiently large,

\end{equation}

$\hat f_r-\hat f_r(0)\in Q\,\mathbb Q\{Q\}$ for $r=0,1,2$.  (The hats are removed in
Theorem~\ref{l3:thm:vanhove}, where the constant term is displayed separately.)

By Lemma~\ref{l3:lem:ode}, $I^{(3)}_\circleddash-I_{\rm part}$ solves $\mathcal L_3u=0$ on the simply

\emph{Step 1: a priori form.}  By Lemma~\ref{l3:lem:frob-form}, since
$\pi(-t)=-t^{-1}(1+O(t^{-1}))$, $R(-t)=-\log t+O(t^{-1})$ and
$Q=O(t^{-1})$, there are constants $\kappa_0,\ldots,\kappa_3$ with
\begin{equation}\label{l3:eq:apriori}
 I^{(3)}_\circleddash(-t)=\frac1t\sum_{j=0}^3\kappa_j\log^jt+O\!\left(\frac{\log^3t}{t^2}\right),
 \qquad
 \kappa_3=4,\ \kappa_2=-\hat f_2(0),\ \kappa_1=\hat f_1(0),\ \kappa_0=-\hat f_0(0).
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 2: Mellin transform.}  For $\operatorname{Re}\nu>0$ put
$M(\nu)=\int_{\mathbb R_+^4}e^{-V}W^\nu\prod_i\,\mathrm d x_i/x_i$.  As in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{l3:lem:ode},
\begin{equation}\label{l3:eq:M-bessel}
 M(\nu)=\frac{8\cdot4^{1-\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu)}\,\mathcal K(2\nu),\qquad

\end{equation}
For $0<\operatorname{Re}z<1$, Fubini and $\int_0^\infty t^{z-1}e^{-tW}\,\mathrm d t=
\Gamma(z)W^{-z}$ give
\begin{equation}\label{l3:eq:mellin-I}
 \int_0^\infty t^{z-1}I^{(3)}_\circleddash(-t)\,\,\mathrm d t=\Gamma(z)M(1-z).
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 3: two continuations.}  Fix once and for all a $t_0>16$ large enough
that~\eqref{l3:eq:apriori} is valid for $t\ge t_0$; such a $t_0$ exists because
\eqref{l3:eq:apriori} was derived from the convergent expansion of
Lemma~\ref{l3:lem:frob-form}, which holds on a germ at infinity.  Split the left
side of~\eqref{l3:eq:mellin-I} as $\int_0^{t_0}+\int_{t_0}^\infty$.  The first
integral is holomorphic for $\operatorname{Re}z>0$, since $I^{(3)}_\circleddash$ is continuous on
the compact interval $[-t_0,0]$ by Proposition~\ref{l3:prop:convergence} and
$\int_0^{t_0}t^{\operatorname{Re}z-1}\,\mathrm d t<\infty$ there.  By
\eqref{l3:eq:apriori}, the second equals

 +\int_{t_0}^\infty t^{z-1}\Bigl(I^{(3)}_\circleddash(-t)-\frac1t\sum_j\kappa_j\log^jt\Bigr)\,\mathrm d t
 \cr=\sum_{j=0}^3\frac{\kappa_j\,j!}{(1-z)^{j+1}}+h(z),
\end{multline*}
with $h$ holomorphic on $\operatorname{Re}z<2$.  Hence the left side of
\eqref{l3:eq:mellin-I} continues meromorphically to $0<\operatorname{Re}z<2$ with a
single pole at $z=1$, and the polar part there is $\sum_j\kappa_jj!\,\nu^{-j-1}$
in the variable $\nu=1-z$.  On the other hand, the right side continues via
\eqref{l3:eq:M-bessel}. We have near $x=0$,
$K_0(x)=-(\log(x/2)+\gamma_E)+O(x^2\log x)$, so
$K_0(x)^4=(\log(x/2)+\gamma_E)^4+O(x^2\log^4x)$ and therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal K(w)&=\int_0^1x^{w-1}\bigl(\log(x/2)+\gamma_E\bigr)^4\,\mathrm d x+H_0(w)\\
 &=\int_0^\infty e^{-wu}(u+c)^4\,\,\mathrm d u+H_0(w),
 \qquad \text{with}\qquad c=\log2-\gamma_E,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $H_0$ is holomorphic on $\operatorname{Re}w>-2$, using $x=e^{-u}$ in the second line.  Thus $\mathcal K$ is meromorphic on $\operatorname{Re}w>-2$
with a single pole at $w=0$ and polar part
$\mathcal K_{\rm pol}(w)=\sum_{j=0}^4\binom4j\,j!\,c^{4-j}w^{-j-1}$.  By the
identity theorem, the two continuations of~\eqref{l3:eq:mellin-I} agree, so
\begin{equation}\label{l3:eq:polar-match}
 \sum_{j=0}^3\frac{\kappa_j\,j!}{\nu^{j+1}}
 =\text{polar part at }\nu=0\text{ of }\;
 \Gamma(1-\nu)\,\frac{8\cdot4^{1-\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu)}\,\mathcal K(2\nu).
\end{equation}

\[
 \frac{\Gamma(1-\nu)}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}
 =\exp\Bigl(2\gamma_E\nu+\tfrac23\zeta(3)\nu^3+O(\nu^5)\Bigr),
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 4^{1-\nu}=4\,e^{-2\nu\log2}.
\]
Only the polar part of $\mathcal K(2\nu)$ contributes, and
$\mathcal K_{\rm pol}(2\nu)=\int_0^\infty e^{-2\nu u}(u+c)^4\,\mathrm d u$.  Hence the
right side of~\eqref{l3:eq:polar-match} is the polar part of
\[
 32\,\nu\,\exp\bigl(b\nu+\tfrac23\zeta(3)\nu^3\bigr)
 \Bigl(\frac{24}{(2\nu)^5}+\frac{24c}{(2\nu)^4}+\frac{12c^2}{(2\nu)^3}
 +\frac{4c^3}{(2\nu)^2}+\frac{c^4}{2\nu}\Bigr),\] 
with
\[b=2\gamma_E-2\log2=-2c .
\]
Expanding $$e^{b\nu+\frac23\zeta(3)\nu^3}=1+b\nu+\frac{b^2}2\nu^2
+\left(\frac{b^3}6+\frac23\zeta(3)\right)\nu^3+O(\nu^4)$$ and collecting, the polar part

\[
 32\Bigl[\frac{3}{4\nu^4}+\frac{3(b+2c)}{4\nu^3}+\frac{3(b+2c)^2}{8\nu^2}

 =\frac{24}{\nu^4}+\frac{16\zeta(3)}{\nu},
\]
because $b+2c=0$, then every term involving $\gamma_E$ or $\log2$ cancels.
Comparing with the left side of
\eqref{l3:eq:polar-match}, $\kappa_3\cdot3!=24$, $\kappa_2=\kappa_1=0$ and
$\kappa_0=16\zeta(3)$.  This is~\eqref{l3:eq:leading}, and the values of the
$\hat f_r(0)$ follow from~\eqref{l3:eq:apriori}.

\label{l3:thm:coefficient-bridge}

\emph{Convergence.}  Neither $\tau^\sharp=B/A$ nor $T$ is a holomorphic
germ at $Q=0$, since both contain $\log y$; their difference is.  Indeed
$\tau^\sharp-T=\widetilde B/A-S$ by Lemmas~\ref{l3:lem:T} and~\ref{l3:lem:B-expansion},
and $A$, $\widetilde B$, $S$ are holomorphic germs in $y$ of radius $\ge1/16$
with $A(0)=1$; composing with the holomorphic inverse germ $y(Q)$ shows that
every term of~\eqref{l3:eq:bridge} is a convergent germ at $Q=0$.

\emph{The scalar equation in the coordinate $R$.}  On the germ, $Q$ and $s$
determine each other (Convention~\ref{l3:conv:branches}), and $\partial_R$ acts on
functions of $Q$ as $\theta_Q$, since $Q=e^R$.  Since $R'(s)=-\pi(s)$, the chain rule reads
$\partial_s=-\pi\,\partial_R$ on functions of $R$.  Writing
$\mathcal L_3=\mathsf A_3\partial_s^3+\mathsf A_2\partial_s^2+\mathsf A_1\partial_s
+\mathsf A_0$ (the coefficients read off~\eqref{l3:eq:L3}; the letter $\mathsf A$
avoids the $a_n$ of Lemma~\ref{l3:lem:Bop}) and $\pi',\pi''$
for $s$-derivatives, one finds for any $\mathcal H$
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal L_3(\pi\mathcal H)&=\pi\bigl(L_3\partial_R^3+L_2\partial_R^2
 +L_1\partial_R\bigr)\mathcal H,\\
 L_3&=-\mathsf A_3\pi^3,\qquad L_2=6\mathsf A_3\pi\pi'+\mathsf A_2\pi^2,\\
 L_1&=-\mathsf A_3\Bigl(\frac{3\pi'^2}{\pi}+4\pi''\Bigr)-3\mathsf A_2\pi'-\mathsf A_1\pi.
\end{aligned}
\]
with no zeroth-order term because $\mathcal L_3\pi=0$.  The $L_i$ are analytic
on a punctured disc at $Q=0$ and meromorphic at $Q=0$; explicitly
$L_3=-s^2(s-4)(s-16)\pi^3\sim -s\sim Q^{-1}$, so they are \emph{not} power
series in $Q$.  All the statements below are equalities in the
field of meromorphic germs, and the operator
$L(Q,\theta_Q)=L_3\theta_Q^3+L_2\theta_Q^2+L_1\theta_Q$ is used only through its

$\partial_R(gR^k)=(\theta_Q g)R^k+kgR^{k-1}$, the coefficient of $R^2$ is
\begin{equation}\label{l3:eq:f2rec}
 L(Q,\theta_Q)f_2+12L_1=0,
\end{equation}
the term $12L_1$ coming from $L_1\partial_R(4R^3)=12L_1R^2$ and the constancy of
the leading coefficient $4$ (Lemma~\ref{l3:lem:frob-form}).  Comparison of
coefficients of powers of $R$ is legitimate by the transcendence of $R$ over the
field of convergent Laurent series in $Q$, proved in Lemma~\ref{l3:lem:frob-form}.

\emph{The A-model solution of the same equation.}  By Lemmas~\ref{l3:lem:qserre}
and~\ref{l3:lem:Bop}, $\pi\tau^\sharp=\pi(R+\theta_Q K_2)$ solves
$\mathcal L_3u=0$.  Dividing by $\pi$ and collecting the coefficient of $R^0$
gives $L_1+L(Q,\theta_Q)\theta_Q K_2=0$; multiplying by $12$ shows that
$12\theta_Q K_2$ also satisfies~\eqref{l3:eq:f2rec}.

\emph{Uniqueness.}  Put $\Delta=f_2-12\theta_Q K_2$.  Then
$L(Q,\theta_Q)\Delta=0$, i.e.\ $\pi\Delta$ solves $\mathcal L_3u=0$; and
$\pi\Delta$ is log-free, being $\pi$ times a convergent power series in $Q$.
The coefficient recurrence~\eqref{l3:eq:frob-rec} at $\rho=-1$ has nonzero
coefficient $(-n)^3$ for every subsequent unknown, $n\ge1$; therefore the space
of log-free solutions of this form is one-dimensional and spanned by $\pi$; hence
$\Delta$ is constant.  Both $f_2$ and $\theta_Q K_2$ vanish at $Q=0$ --- for
$f_2$ this is Proposition~\ref{l3:prop:leading}, for $K_2$ it is
Lemma~\ref{l3:lem:K2-welldefined} --- so $\Delta=0$.  The last equality in
\eqref{l3:eq:bridge} is Proposition~\ref{l3:prop:potential}.

A class $\beta$ with $|\beta|=1$ satisfies $p_i\cdot\beta=\delta_{ij}$ for
exactly one $j$; call it $\beta_j$.  A stable map of class $\beta_j$ has
irreducible image, so its image is a curve meeting the $j$-th ruling once and
contracted by the other three projections, that is
$L_b=\mathbb P^1\times\{b\}$ with $b\in(\mathbb P^1)^3$ (coordinates in the
factors other than $j$).  Writing the defining multilinear equation of $F_3$ as
$A(b)x_j^{(0)}+B(b)x_j^{(1)}$, one has $L_b\subset F_3$ if and only if
$A(b)=B(b)=0$.  Thus the lines of class $\beta_j$ are parametrized by the
complete intersection $C_j=\{A=B=0\}$, of class $h^2$ in $(\mathbb P^1)^3$,
where $h$ is the sum of the three remaining point classes.

At a point of $C_j$, dependence of $dA$ and $dB$ would give
$[x_j^{(0)}:x_j^{(1)}]$ for which the base differential of
$Ax_j^{(0)}+Bx_j^{(1)}$ vanishes. Its fiber differential already vanishes
there; this would be a singular point of $F_3$. Hence $dA,dB$ are independent,
so $C_j$ is a smooth regular codimension-two zero locus.
The ambient one-marked degree-$\beta_j$ moduli is
$\mathbb P^1\times(\mathbb P^1)^3$; the section cutting out the
hypersurface moduli cuts out $\mathbb P^1\times C_j$ over arbitrary bases.

Since $L=L_b\subset F_3\subset Y_3$ and $N_{L/Y_3}=\mathcal O_L^{\oplus3}$, the normal
sequence of $L\subset F_3\subset Y_3$ reads
$0\to N_{L/F_3}\to\mathcal O_L^{\oplus3}\to\mathcal O_L(H\cdot\beta_j)=
\mathcal O_L(1)\to0$.  A surjection $\mathcal O_L^{\oplus3}\twoheadrightarrow
\mathcal O_L(1)$ is given by three sections of $\mathcal O_L(1)$ without common
zero, hence spanning $H^0(\mathcal O_L(1))$; after a change of basis the map is
$(f_1,f_2,f_3)\mapsto f_1s_0+f_2s_1$ for a basis $s_0,s_1$, so
\[
 N_{L/F_3}\cong\mathcal O_L\oplus\mathcal O_L(-1).
\]
Therefore $H^1(L,N_{L/F_3})=0$, and from $0\to T_L\to T_{F_3}|_L\to N_{L/F_3}\to0$ with
$H^1(\mathcal O_L(2))=0$ we get $H^1(L,T_{F_3}|_L)=0$.  Consequently
$\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,1}(F_3,\beta_j)$ is unobstructed of dimension
$1+1=2=\operatorname{vdim}$, its virtual class is its fundamental class, and it has no boundary, because a contracted subtree with $v\geq1$ components,
one attachment, and at most one marking has at most $2v$ special points
in total, whereas stability requires at least $3v$. Thus it is the universal line

\mathcal V=|\beta_j|-1=0$, Proposition~\ref{l3:prop:virtual-reduction} identifies
