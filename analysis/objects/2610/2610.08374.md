<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
Let $u\in L^\infty(0,T)$ satisfy
\[
0\le u(t)\le u_{\max}
\qquad\text{a.e. on }(0,T).
\]
If $B_0\ge0$, then the initial value problem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced_model}
\frac{\,\mathrm d B}{\,\mathrm d t}
=
\frac{aB}{b+B}
-\alpha u(t)B
-mB,
\qquad
B(0)=B_0,
\end{equation}
admits a unique global nonnegative solution on $[0,T]$. Moreover,
\[
B(t)\ge0
\qquad\text{for all } t\in[0,T],
\]
and
\[
0\le B(t)\le
\max\left\{B_0,\frac{a}{m}\right\}
\qquad\text{for all } t\in[0,T].
\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:reduced_model} is measurable in $t$ and
locally Lipschitz continuous in $B$. Hence a unique local solution exists.
From Equation \eqref{eq:ReducedBiomass}, $\dot B=0$ whenever $B=0$.
Therefore, the nonnegative half-line
is positively invariant, and thus $B(t)\ge0$ for all $t\in[0,T]$.

Furthermore, using $u(t)\ge0$ and
\[
\frac{aB}{b+B}\le a,
\]
we obtain
\[
\frac{dB}{dt}\le a-mB.
\]
A comparison argument with the solution of
\[
y'=a-my, \qquad y(0)=B_0,
\]
yields
\[
B(t)\le \max\left\{B_0,\frac{a}{m}\right\}
\qquad \text{for all } t\in[0,T].
\]
The boundedness of $B$ excludes finite-time blow-up and therefore the
local solution extends globally.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
Consider \eqref{eq:reduced_model} under a constant harvesting effort
$u(t)\equiv u_c\ge0$. Then the equilibrium points are given by
\[
B^*_0=0
\]
and
\[
B^*_1(u_c)=\frac{a}{m+\alpha u_c}-b.
\]

Moreover, a positive equilibrium, $B^*_1(u_c)>0$, exists if and only if
\[
u_c<u_{\mathrm{crit}},
\qquad
u_{\mathrm{crit}}
:=
\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{a}{b}-m\right).
\]
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Equilibria satisfy
\[
0=\frac{aB}{b+B}-(m+\alpha u_c)B.
\]
Factoring out $B$ gives
\[
B\left(\frac{a}{b+B}-(m+\alpha u_c)\right)=0.
\]
Hence $B^*_0=0$ and
\[
B^*_1(u_c)=\frac{a}{m+\alpha u_c}-b.
\]
The condition $B^*_1(u_c)>0$ is equivalent to
\[
a>b(m+\alpha u_c),
\]
or, equivalently,
\[
u_c<\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{a}{b}-m\right)
=u_{\mathrm{crit}}.
\]
The conclusion follows.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:positivity}
Assume that $B_0\in L^\infty(\Omega)$, $B_0\ge0$, and
$u\in L^\infty((0,T)\times\Omega)$ satisfies
\[
0\le u(x,t)\le u_{\max}
\qquad\text{a.e. in }\Omega\times(0,T).
\]
Then every classical solution of
\eqref{eq:spatial_model}--\eqref{eq:initial_spatial} satisfies
\[
B(x,t)\ge0
\qquad\text{for all }x\in\Omega,\; t\in[0,T].
\]
Moreover,
\[
0\le B(x,t)
\le
\|B_0\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}+aT
\qquad\text{for all }x\in\Omega,\; t\in[0,T].
\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let
\[
F(x,t,B)
=
\frac{aB}{b+B}
-\alpha u(x,t)B
-mB.
\]
Since
\[
F(x,t,0)=0,
\]
the zero function is a subsolution of \eqref{eq:spatial_model}.
Because
\[
0=B_{\mathrm{sub}}(x,0)\le B_0(x),
\]
the parabolic comparison principle implies
\[
B(x,t)\ge0
\qquad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T].
\]

Furthermore, using $B\ge0$,
\[
\frac{aB}{b+B}\le a,
\qquad
-\alpha u(x,t)B\le0,
\qquad
-mB\le0.
\]
From Equation \eqref{eq:spatial_model}, we have
\[
\partial_t B-D\Delta B\le a.
\]

Let $y(t)$ solve
\[
y'(t)=a,
\qquad
y(0)=\|B_0\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}.
\]
Then
\[
y(t)=\|B_0\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}+at.
\]
Since $y$ is independent of $x$,
\[
\Delta y=0,
\]
and therefore
\[
\partial_t y-D\Delta y=a.
\]
Moreover,
\[
B_0(x)\le \|B_0\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}
= y(0)
\qquad\text{for all }x\in\Omega.
\]
Applying the parabolic comparison principle gives
\[
B(x,t)\le y(t)
=
\|B_0\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}+at.
\]
Since $t\le T$,
\[
B(x,t)
\le
\|B_0\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}+aT.
\]
This completes the proof.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
Assume that $B_0\in L^\infty(\Omega)$, $B_0\ge0$, and
\[
u\in L^\infty((0,T)\times\Omega),
\qquad
0\le u(x,t)\le u_{\max}
\]
almost everywhere. Then the problem
\eqref{eq:spatial_model}--\eqref{eq:initial_spatial}
admits a unique global mild solution
\[
B\in C([0,T];L^2(\Omega))
\cap
L^2(0,T;H^1(\Omega)).
\]
Furthermore, if $B_0$ and $\partial\Omega$ are sufficiently regular,
then the solution is classical for $t>0$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Consider the operator
\[
A:=D\Delta
\]
subject to the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition
\[
\partial_\nu v=0
\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.
\]
Its domain is
\[
\mathcal D(A)
=
\{v\in H^2(\Omega):\partial_\nu v=0
\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}.
\]
It is well known that $A$ generates an analytic semigroup
$\{e^{tA}\}_{t\ge0}$ on $L^2(\Omega)$.

The difficulty in applying the standard semilinear theory directly to
the biological reaction term is that the Beverton--Holt function
\[
B\mapsto \frac{aB}{b+B}
\]
is singular at $B=-b$. We therefore introduce a globally defined
extension of the reaction term.

Let
\[
R:=\|B_0\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}+aT
\]
and choose a smooth function
$\rho:\mathbb R\to[0,1]$ such that
\[
\rho(B)=1
\quad\text{for }B\ge0,
\qquad
\rho(B)=0
\quad\text{for }B\le-\frac{b}{2}.
\]
Define the truncated recruitment function by
\[
\widetilde{G}(B)
=
\rho(B)\frac{aB}{b+B}.
\]
Since the support of $\rho$ is separated from the singular point $B=-b$, the function $\widetilde{G}$ is continuously differentiable and globally Lipschitz on $\mathbb R$. Consequently, for
\[
\widetilde F(x,t,B)
=
\widetilde G(B)
-\alpha u(x,t)B
-mB,
\]
we have
\[
|\widetilde F(x,t,B_1)
-\widetilde F(x,t,B_2)|
\le C|B_1-B_2|
\]
for all $B_1,B_2\in\mathbb R$, with a constant $C>0$ independent of $(x,t)$, since
$0\le u(x,t)\le u_{\max}$ almost everywhere.

We therefore consider the extended problem
\[
\partial_t B=AB+\widetilde F(x,t,B),
\]
with the same initial and boundary conditions as
\eqref{eq:spatial_model}--\eqref{eq:initial_spatial}. Its mild formulation is
\[
B(t)
=
e^{tA}B_0
+
\int_0^t
e^{(t-s)A}
\widetilde F(\cdot,s,B(s))
\,ds .
\]
Since $\widetilde F$ is globally Lipschitz in $B$, standard semilinear parabolic theory yields a unique global mild solution
\[
B\in C([0,T];L^2(\Omega))
\cap
L^2(0,T;H^1(\Omega)).
\]

It remains to verify that this solution also solves the original biological problem. Since $B_0\ge0$ and the extended reaction satisfies
\[
\widetilde F(x,t,0)=0,
\]
the comparison principle gives
\[
B(x,t)\ge0
\]
almost everywhere in $\Omega\times(0,T)$. Hence, throughout the solution trajectory,
\[
\rho(B(x,t))=1,
\]
and therefore
\[
\widetilde G(B(x,t))
=
\frac{aB(x,t)}{b+B(x,t)}.
\]
Thus the extended equation coincides with the original equation on the
nonnegative region, and the obtained mild solution is a mild solution
of \eqref{eq:spatial_model}--\eqref{eq:initial_spatial}.

Moreover, by Theorem~\ref{thm:positivity},
\[
0\le B(x,t)
\le
\|B_0\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}+aT
\]
for all $t\in[0,T]$. Hence the solution remains uniformly bounded on
the entire time interval and cannot exhibit finite-time blow-up.

Finally, the smoothing property of the analytic semigroup implies that, whenever $B_0$ and $\partial\Omega$ possess sufficient regularity, the mild solution becomes classical for every $t>0$.
This completes the proof.

Assume that $u(x,t)\equiv u_c$ and that a positive homogeneous equilibrium $B^*=B^*(u_c)>0$ exists. Then $B^*$ is linearly asymptotically stable under homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions.
Moreover, the zero equilibrium is linearly asymptotically stable when
$u_c>u_{\mathrm{crit}}$, where

    u_{\mathrm{crit}}
=
\frac{1}{\alpha}

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For a spatially homogeneous equilibrium under the constant control
$u_c$, we have
\[
f(B^*)=0.
\]
Since $B^*>0$, it follows that
\[
\frac{a}{b+B^*}
=
m+\alpha u_c
=:q.
\]
Differentiating $f$ gives
\[
f'(B)
=
\frac{ab}{(b+B)^2}-q.
\]
Evaluating at $B^*$ and using
\[
\frac{a}{b+B^*}=q,
\]
we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
f'(B^*)
&=
\frac{ab}{(b+B^*)^2}-q\\
&=
q\left(
\frac{b}{b+B^*}-1
\right)\\
&=
-q\frac{B^*}{b+B^*}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $q>0$, $B^*>0$, and $b+B^*>0$, we conclude that
\[
f'(B^*)<0.
\]


To study the spatial stability, consider a small perturbation of the equilibrium,
\[
B(x,t)=B^*+z(x,t).
\]
Since $B^*$ is spatially homogeneous,
\[
\Delta B^*=0,
\qquad
f(B^*)=0.
\]
Substituting $B=B^*+z$ into the spatial model gives
\[
\partial_t z
=
D\Delta z+f(B^*+z).
\]
Using the first-order Taylor expansion
\[
f(B^*+z)
=
f(B^*)+f'(B^*)z+O(z^2),
\]
and neglecting the higher-order terms, we obtain the linearized problem
\[
\partial_t z
=
D\Delta z+f'(B^*)z,
\qquad
\partial_\nu z=0.
\]

We expand the perturbation in eigenfunctions of the Neumann Laplacian.
Let $\{\phi_k\}_{k\ge0}$ satisfy
\[
-\Delta\phi_k=\mu_k\phi_k,
\qquad
\partial_\nu\phi_k=0,
\]
with eigenvalues
\[
0=\mu_0\le\mu_1\le\mu_2\le\cdots.
\]
Since the eigenfunctions form an orthogonal basis of $L^2(\Omega)$, the perturbation can be represented as
\[
z(x,t)
=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_k(t)\phi_k(x).
\]
Substituting this expansion into the linearized equation and using
\[
\Delta\phi_k=-\mu_k\phi_k
\]
yields
\[
c_k'(t)
=
\bigl(f'(B^*)-D\mu_k\bigr)c_k(t).
\]
Hence, the growth rate associated with the $k$th spatial mode is
\[
\lambda_k
=
f'(B^*)-D\mu_k.
\]
Since $\mu_k\ge0$ and $D\ge0$,
\[
\lambda_k
\le
f'(B^*)
<0.
\]
Therefore, every spatial perturbation mode decays exponentially in time, and
\[
z(x,t)\to0
\qquad\text{as }t\to\infty.
\]
Thus, the positive homogeneous equilibrium $B^*$ is linearly asymptotically stable under homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions.

We next consider the zero equilibrium $B=0$, which satisfies $f(0)=0$. The corresponding linearized growth rate is
\[
f'(0)
=
\frac{a}{b}-m-\alpha u_c.
\]
We define the critical harvesting level by Equation \eqref{eq:ucrit_spatial}.
Then
\[
f'(0)
=
\alpha
\left(
u_{\mathrm{crit}}-u_c
\right).
\]

If $u_c>u_{\mathrm{crit}}$, then we get
$f'(0)<0$.
For the linearized spatial problem around $B=0$, the modal growth rates are
\[
f'(0)-D\mu_k.
\]
Since $\mu_k\ge0$, all these growth rates are negative. Hence the zero equilibrium is linearly asymptotically stable.

If $u_c<u_{\mathrm{crit}}$,
then we obtain $f'(0)>0$,
so the spatially homogeneous mode corresponding to $\mu_0=0$ grows
exponentially. Thus, the zero equilibrium is linearly unstable. In this regime, the positive homogeneous equilibrium exists and, as shown above, is linearly asymptotically stable.

At the critical value $u_c=u_{\mathrm{crit}}$,
we have $f'(0)=0$, so linearization alone does not determine the stability of the zero
equilibrium.

Therefore, $u_{\mathrm{crit}}$ identifies the transition between the persistence and extinction regimes of the spatially homogeneous equilibria.

\label{the:optimal}

Assume that $B_0\in L^\infty(\Omega)$, $B_0\ge0$, and
$U_{\mathrm{ad}}\neq\emptyset$. Then there exists at least one control $u^*\in U_{\mathrm{ad}}$ maximizing \eqref{eq:objective_spatial} subject to
the state equation \eqref{eq:spatial_model}.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $(u_n)$ be a maximizing sequence in $U_{\mathrm{ad}}$, namely
\[
J(u_n)
\longrightarrow
\sup_{u\in U_{\mathrm{ad}}} J(u).
\]

Since
\[
0\le u_n(x,t)\le u_{\max}
\qquad\text{a.e. in }\Omega\times(0,T),
\]
the sequence $(u_n)$ is uniformly bounded in
$L^\infty((0,T)\times\Omega)$.

By the Banach--Alaoglu theorem, there exist a subsequence, still denoted by
$(u_n)$, and a function $u^*\in L^\infty((0,T)\times\Omega)$ such that
\[
u_n \rightharpoonup^* u^*
\qquad\text{in }L^\infty((0,T)\times\Omega).
\]
Here, $\rightharpoonup^*$ denotes weak-* convergence in
$L^\infty((0,T)\times\Omega)$.

We now verify that $u^*\in U_{\mathrm{ad}}$. Let
$\varphi\in L^1((0,T)\times\Omega)$ be nonnegative. Since
$u_n\ge0$ almost everywhere,
\[
\int_0^T\int_\Omega u_n\varphi\,dx\,dt\ge0.
\]
Passing to the weak-* limit gives
\[
\int_0^T\int_\Omega u^*\varphi\,dx\,dt\ge0.
\]
Hence $u^*\ge0$ almost everywhere. Similarly, since
$u_{\max}-u_n\ge0$ almost everywhere,
\[
\int_0^T\int_\Omega
(u_{\max}-u_n)\varphi\,dx\,dt\ge0.
\]
Passing to the limit yields
\[
\int_0^T\int_\Omega
(u_{\max}-u^*)\varphi\,dx\,dt\ge0.
\]
Therefore $u^*\le u_{\max}$ almost everywhere. Thus
\[
0\le u^*\le u_{\max}
\qquad\text{a.e. in }\Omega\times(0,T),
\]
and consequently $u^*\in U_{\mathrm{ad}}$.

For each $u_n$, let $B_n$ be the corresponding state solution of
\eqref{eq:spatial_model}. By Theorem~\ref{thm:positivity},
\[
0\le B_n(x,t)\le C
\]
for some constant $C>0$ independent of $n$. Consequently,
\[
\|B_n\|_{L^\infty((0,T)\times\Omega)}
\le C.
\]

We define
\[
F_n
=
\frac{aB_n}{b+B_n}
-\alpha u_nB_n
-mB_n .
\]

Multiplying \eqref{eq:spatial_model} by $B_n$ and integrating over $\Omega$, we obtain
\[
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}
\|B_n(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
+
D\|\nabla B_n(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
=
\int_\Omega F_n B_n\,dx.
\]

Since $(u_n)$ and $(B_n)$ are uniformly bounded in
$L^\infty((0,T)\times\Omega)$, there exists a constant $C>0$ such that
\[
|F_n|\le C.
\]
Hence
\[
\left|
\int_\Omega F_n B_n\,dx
\right|
\le C|\Omega|.
\]

Integrating with respect to time over $(0,T)$ yields
\[
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}
\|B_n(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
+
D\int_0^T
\|\nabla B_n(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2\,dt
\le C,
\]
where $C$ is independent of $n$.

Therefore
\[
(B_n)
\quad\text{is bounded in}\quad
L^2(0,T;H^1(\Omega)).
\]

Furthermore, for every $\varphi\in H^1(\Omega)$,
Green's formula and the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition imply
\[
\langle \Delta B_n,\varphi\rangle
=
-\int_\Omega
\nabla B_n\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx.
\]

Hence
\[
|\langle \Delta B_n,\varphi\rangle|
\le
\|\nabla B_n\|_{L^2(\Omega)}
\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^2(\Omega)},
\]
which gives
\[
\|\Delta B_n\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}
\le
\|\nabla B_n\|_{L^2(\Omega)}.
\]

Consequently,
\[
(\Delta B_n)
\quad\text{is bounded in}\quad
L^2(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega)).
\]

Since $(F_n)$ is uniformly bounded in
$L^\infty((0,T)\times\Omega)$, it is bounded in
$L^2(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))$. From
\[
\partial_t B_n
=
D\Delta B_n
+
F_n,
\]
it follows that $(\partial_t B_n)$ is bounded in
$L^2(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))$.

The Aubin--Lions compactness lemma therefore yields the existence of a
subsequence, still denoted by $(B_n)$, and a function $B^*$ such that
\[
B_n \to B^*
\qquad\text{strongly in }
L^2((0,T)\times\Omega).
\]

Moreover, up to a further subsequence,
\[
B_n(x,t)\to B^*(x,t)
\qquad\text{a.e. in }\Omega\times(0,T).
\]

Since
\[
u_n \rightharpoonup^* u^*
\quad\text{in }L^\infty((0,T)\times\Omega)
\]
and
\[
B_n \to B^*
\quad\text{strongly in }L^2((0,T)\times\Omega),
\]
it follows that
\[
u_n B_n
\rightharpoonup
u^*B^*
\qquad\text{weakly in }L^2((0,T)\times\Omega).
\]

Furthermore, the function $B\mapsto \frac{aB}{b+B}$
is continuous on the bounded interval
$0\le B\le C$,
and therefore
\[
\frac{aB_n}{b+B_n}
\to
\frac{aB^*}{b+B^*}
\qquad\text{strongly in }L^2((0,T)\times\Omega).
\]

Passing to the limit in the weak formulation of
\eqref{eq:spatial_model}, we conclude that $B^*$ satisfies the state equation associated with the control $u^*$. Hence $(u^*,B^*)$ is an admissible control--state pair.

It remains to show that $u^*$ attains the maximal value of the objective functional $J$. To this end, we use the convergence properties established above together with the weak lower semicontinuity of the $L^2$-norm.

Moreover, since $u_n\rightharpoonup^* u^*$ weakly in
$L^\infty((0,T)\times\Omega)$, it also follows that
$u_n\rightharpoonup u^*$ weakly in
$L^2((0,T)\times\Omega)$. Thus, the weak lower semicontinuity of the
$L^2$-norm yields
\[
\|u^*\|_{L^2((0,T)\times\Omega)}^2
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\|u_n\|_{L^2((0,T)\times\Omega)}^2.
\]

Hence,
\[
-\frac{\gamma}{2}
\|u^*\|_{L^2((0,T)\times\Omega)}^2
\ge
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\left(
-\frac{\gamma}{2}
\|u_n\|_{L^2((0,T)\times\Omega)}^2
\right).
\]

Combining the convergence of the benefit term with the above estimate for the harvesting-cost term, we obtain
\[
J(u^*)
\ge
\limsup_{n\to\infty} J(u_n).
\]

Since $(u_n)$ is a maximizing sequence,
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty} J(u_n)
=
\sup_{u\in U_{\mathrm{ad}}} J(u).
\]

It follows that
\[
J(u^*)
=
\sup_{u\in U_{\mathrm{ad}}} J(u),
\]
and $u^*$ is an optimal distributed harvesting control.
