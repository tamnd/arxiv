<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label[assumption]{ass:model}
The set $\mathcal W\subset\mathbb R^d$ is nonempty, compact, and convex, with Euclidean diameter at most $D>0$.  Every $f_t$ is $\eta$-exp-concave on $\mathcal W$ and satisfies
$\left\lVert \nabla f_t(w)\right\rVert_2\le G$ for all $w\in\mathcal W$.
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- assumption-2: body_md -->
\label[assumption]{ass:exact-oracle}
The oracle returns an exact solution to
\[
 \min_{w\in\mathcal W}(w-a)^\top A(w-a)
\]
for every $a\in\mathbb R^d$ and $A\succ0$, at arithmetic cost $\mathsf C_{\rm proj}(d)$.
<!-- end assumption-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label[theorem]{thm:curved}
Under \Cref{ass:model,ass:exact-oracle}, fix $D,G,\eta>0$ independently of $d$ and $T$. The proper full-information learner \textup{\textsc{SparseGauss}}{} does not require $P_T(u)$ and, for every comparator path with $P_T(u)\le cT/(\sqrt d\,\Lambda_T^2)$, guarantees
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curved-summary}
 \operatorname{Reg}_T(u_{1:T})
 \le O\!\left(d\Lambda_T^2
 +d^{1/3}\Lambda_T^{4/3}T^{1/3}P_T(u)^{2/3}\right).
\end{equation}
Here $c>0$ and the constant hidden by $O(\cdot)$ depend only on $D,G,\eta$.
The learner uses one gradient query per round, requires $O((d^3+\mathsf C_{\rm proj}(d))\log^2(2T))$ operations per round in the dense spectral model, and stores $O(d^2\log^2(2T))$ real numbers. If an additional primitive performs the complete rank-one update and covariance flooring in $O(d^2)$ operations, including reconstruction in the original coordinates, the $d^3$ term becomes $d^2$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label[corollary]{cor:frontier}
Under \Cref{ass:model,ass:exact-oracle}, fix $D,G,\eta>0$ independently of $d$ and $T$. A proper full-information learner that does not require $P_T(u)$ guarantees
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-frontier}
 \operatorname{Reg}_T(u_{1:T})
 \le O\!\left(d\Lambda_T^2+\min\left\{
 d^{1/3}\Lambda_T^{4/3}T^{1/3}P_T(u)^{2/3},\,
 \sqrt{T(1+P_T(u))},\,T\right\}\right).
\end{equation}
for every comparator path. The constant hidden by $O(\cdot)$ depends only on $D,G,\eta$. The learner uses $O(\log T)$ loss evaluations, gradient evaluations, and Euclidean projection calls per round in addition to the curved branch's internal work.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label[theorem]{thm:lower}
There are universal constants $c,c_0>0$ such that the following holds.  Let
\[
 \mathcal W_d=\{w\in\mathbb R^d:\left\lVert w\right\rVert_2\le1/2\}
\]
and let the adversary use only the losses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hard-loss}
 f_{j,y}(w)=\frac12(w_j-y)^2,
 \qquad j\in[d],\quad y\in\{-1,+1\}.
\end{equation}
This class has diameter $D=1$, gradient bound $G\le3/2$, and exp-concavity coefficient at least $4/9$, uniformly in $d$.  If $T\ge c_0d^2$, then for every $P\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-frontier}
 \inf_{\mathsf A}\sup_{\substack{f_{1:T}\text{ of the form }\eqref{eq:hard-loss}\\
 u_{1:T}\in\mathcal W_d^T,\ P_T(u)\le P}}
 \mathbb E_{\mathsf A}\operatorname{Reg}_T(u_{1:T})
 \ge
 c\left[
 d+\min\left\{
 d^{1/3}T^{1/3}P^{2/3},\sqrt{TP},T
 \right\}
 \right].
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label[corollary]{cor:minimax}
For $D=1$, $G=3/2$, and $\eta=4/9$, take the worst case over compact convex domains of diameter at most one and losses satisfying \Cref{ass:model}. For horizons $T\gtrsim d^2$, the proper full-information minimax dynamic regret with path budget $P_T$ has, up to polylogarithmic factors, the frontier
\[
 \boxed{
 d+\min\{d^{1/3}T^{1/3}P_T^{2/3},\sqrt{TP_T},T\}.
 }
\]
With a Mahalanobis-projection oracle, the upper frontier is attained in near-linear time in $T$.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The lower bound follows from \Cref{thm:lower}, since its ball and coordinate-square losses belong to the stated class. For the upper bound, \Cref{cor:frontier} gives the curved branch, $\sqrt{T(1+P_T)}$, and the linear cap, together with an additive $d$, up to logarithms. For $P_T\ge1$, the square-root branch is within a constant factor of $\sqrt{TP_T}$. For $0<P_T<1$ and $T\ge d^2$, the ratio of the curved term to $\sqrt{TP_T}$ is $(d^2P_T/T)^{1/6}\le1$, so the curved term is already the smaller branch. The case $P_T=0$ follows directly from the static term. This proves the displayed normalized frontier.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label[lemma]{lem:surrogate}
For every $v\in\mathcal W$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domination}
 f_t(w_t)-f_t(v)
 \le \widetilde f_t(w_t)-\widetilde f_t(v)
 =-\widetilde f_t(v).
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\widetilde f_t$ is $\lambda$-exp-concave on $\mathcal W$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Because $2\gamma\le\eta$, the loss is also $2\gamma$-exp-concave.  Put
$a=\langle g_t,v-w_t\rangle$.  Concavity of $e^{-2\gamma f_t}$~gives
\[
 e^{-2\gamma f_t(v)}
 \le e^{-2\gamma f_t(w_t)}(1-2\gamma a).
\]
The choice of $\gamma$ implies $|2\gamma a|\le2\gamma GD\le1/2$.
For $|x|\le1/2$, $-\log(1-x)\ge x+x^2/4$.  Taking logarithms yields
\[
 f_t(v)-f_t(w_t)
 \ge a+\frac\gamma2a^2=\widetilde f_t(v),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:domination}.

For exp-concavity, write $s=\langle g_t,v-w_t\rangle$.  Then
\[
 \nabla\widetilde f_t(v)=(1+\gamma s)g_t,
 \qquad
 \nabla^2\widetilde f_t(v)=\gamma g_tg_t^\top.
\]
Since $|\gamma s|\le1/4$,
\[
 \nabla^2\widetilde f_t(v)
 \succeq\frac\gamma2(1+\gamma s)^2g_tg_t^\top
 =\lambda\nabla\widetilde f_t(v)\nabla\widetilde f_t(v)^\top.
\]
This is the Hessian criterion for $\lambda$-exp-concavity.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label[lemma]{lem:schedule}
The sets \eqref{eq:active-set} satisfy:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(H\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
\item $t\in\mathcal S_t$ and $|\mathcal S_t|\le c_H\log(2t)$ for a universal $c_H$;
\item once $i$ leaves the active set, it never returns;
\item for every interval $[r,s]\subseteq[T]$, there is an
$i\in\mathcal S_s\cap[r,\lfloor(r+s)/2\rfloor]$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, the history born at such an $i$ is active on every round from $i$ through $s$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The first assertion in (H1) and (H2) are immediate from \eqref{eq:active-set}.  For the size bound, fix $k$.  Integers with $\nu_2(i)=k$ are separated by $2^{k+1}$, and an active such integer lies in an interval of length $2^{k+2}$.  Hence only a universal constant number occur for each $k$, and $k\le\lfloor\log_2t\rfloor$.

For (H3), put $\Delta=s-r$.  If $\Delta<2$, the claim is immediate.  Otherwise choose the largest $2^k\le\Delta/2$.  The interval $[r,(r+s)/2]$ has length at least $2^k$, so it contains an integer $i$ divisible by $2^k$.  Thus $\ell(i)\ge2^{k+2}>\Delta\ge s-i$, and $i\in\mathcal S_s$.  Property (H2) ensures activity throughout $[i,s]$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label[lemma]{lem:meta}
If $\mathcal E_{i,k}$ is active through round $b\ge i$, then every prefix $[i,q]\subseteq[i,b]$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:meta-overhead}
 \sum_{t=i}^q
 [\widetilde f_t(w_t)-\widetilde f_t(x_t^{i,k})]
 \le a_k,
 \qquad
 a_k=\frac1\lambda\log\frac{T+1}{q_k}.
\end{equation}
For the uniform scale prior, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a}
 a=\lambda^{-1}\log((T+1)(K+1)).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By exp-concavity of the surrogate and \eqref{eq:master-prediction},
\[
 e^{-\lambda\widetilde f_t(w_t)}
 \ge\sum_{j,\ell}p_t^{j,\ell}e^{-\lambda\widetilde f_t(x_t^{j,\ell})}.
\]
Rearranging this inequality gives the one-step comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:meta-one-step}
 \lambda[\widetilde f_t(w_t)-\widetilde f_t(x_t^{i,k})]
 \le\log\frac{\widehat p_{t+1}^{i,k}}{p_t^{i,k}}.
\end{equation}
At birth, pruning renormalization can only increase mass, so
$p_i^{i,k}\ge q_k/i$.  For $t>i$, survival and renormalization give
$p_t^{i,k}\ge((t-1)/t)\widehat p_t^{i,k}$.  Summing
\eqref{eq:meta-one-step},
\begin{align*}
 \lambda\sum_{t=i}^q
 [\widetilde f_t(w_t)-\widetilde f_t(x_t^{i,k})]
 &\le -\log p_i^{i,k}+\log\widehat p_{q+1}^{i,k}
 +\sum_{t=i+1}^q\log\frac{\widehat p_t^{i,k}}{p_t^{i,k}}\\
 &\le \log\frac{i}{q_k}+0+\sum_{t=i+1}^q\log\frac t{t-1}\\
 &=\log\frac q{q_k}
 \le\log\frac{T+1}{q_k}.
\end{align*}
The terminal term is nonpositive because
$\widehat p_{q+1}^{i,k}\le1$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label[lemma]{lem:projection-monotone}
For every $u\in\mathcal W$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection-monotone}
 \mathrm{KL}(Q_u\|\mathcal N(m_{t+1},\Sigma_{t+1}))
 \le \mathrm{KL}(Q_u\|R_{t+1}).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
First keep the mean $a=a_{t+1}$ fixed and replace $C=C_{t+1}$ by
$C^\flat=C_{t+1}^\flat$.  In an eigenbasis of $C$, twice the covariance and mean-dependent part of the KL divergence is a sum of
\[
 h_j(c)=\frac{\tau^2+r_j^2}{c}+\log c,
\]
where $r_j$ is the corresponding coordinate of $u-a$.  If $c\le\tau^2$,
\[
 h_j'(c)=\frac{c-\tau^2-r_j^2}{c^2}\le0.
\]
Thus increasing every eigenvalue below $\tau^2$ to $\tau^2$ cannot increase KL.

Now put $A=(C^\flat)^{-1}$ and let $m=m_{t+1}$ be the metric projection of $a$ onto $\mathcal W$.  Its variational inequality is
\[
 (a-m)^\top A(u-m)\le0
 \qquad (u\in\mathcal W).
\]
Expanding squared norms gives
\[
 \left\lVert u-m\right\rVert_A^2
 \le\left\lVert u-a\right\rVert_A^2-\left\lVert a-m\right\rVert_A^2.
\]
The covariance terms are unchanged during mean projection, so KL again cannot increase.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label[lemma]{lem:mixgap}
The history mean satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixgap}
 \widetilde f_t(m_t)\le m_t^{\rm mix}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Under $P_t$, the scalar
$X=\langle g_t,U-w_t\rangle$ is Gaussian with mean
$a=\langle g_t,m_t-w_t\rangle$ and variance $v=g_t^\top\Sigma_tg_t$.
Since $\lambda=\gamma/2$,
$-\lambda\widetilde f_t(U)=-\lambda X-\lambda^2X^2$.
With $s=2\lambda^2v$, direct Gaussian integration~gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-integral}
 \log\frac{\mathbb E e^{-\lambda X-\lambda^2X^2}}
 {e^{-\lambda a-\lambda^2a^2}}
 =-\frac12\log(1+s)
 +\frac{s(1+2\lambda a)^2}{4(1+s)}.
\end{equation}
Both $m_t$ and $w_t$ lie in $\mathcal W$, so
$|\lambda a|\le\gamma GD/2\le1/8$ and
$(1+2\lambda a)^2\le25/16$.  Since
$\log(1+s)\ge s/(1+s)$, the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:gaussian-integral} is at most
$-7s/[64(1+s)]\le0$.  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E e^{-\lambda\widetilde f_t(U)}
 \le e^{-\lambda\widetilde f_t(m_t)},
\]
which is equivalent to \eqref{eq:mixgap}.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label[lemma]{lem:one-step}
Every history update satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-step}
 \lambda r_t\le F_t(u_t)-F_{t+1}(u_t).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The exponential-update identity gives
\[
 m_t^{\rm mix}-\mathbb E_{Q_t}\widetilde f_t
 =\frac1\lambda\left[
 \mathrm{KL}(Q_t\|P_t)-\mathrm{KL}(Q_t\|R_{t+1})
 \right].
\]
Use \cref{lem:mixgap} on the left and
\cref{lem:projection-monotone} on the right.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label[lemma]{lem:root-movement}
For every $u,v\in\mathcal W$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-movement}
 \sqrt{F_{t+1}(v)}
 \le\sqrt{F_{t+1}(u)}
 +\frac{\left\lVert v-u\right\rVert_2}{\sqrt2\,\tau}.
\end{equation}
At a history's birth time $i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-KL}
 F_i(u_i)\le B_\tau,
 \qquad
 B_\tau=\frac12\left[
 1+d(\rho^2-1-\log\rho^2)
 \right],
 \quad \rho=\frac\tau D.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
For fixed $P_{t+1}=\mathcal N(m,\Sigma)$,
\[
 F_{t+1}(u)
 =c+\frac12(u-m)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(u-m),
 \qquad
 c=\frac12\left[
 \tau^2\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{-1})-d+
 \log\frac{\det\Sigma}{\tau^{2d}}
 \right]\ge0.
\]
Define the augmented vector
\[
 z(u)=\left(
 \sqrt c,\frac1{\sqrt2}\Sigma^{-1/2}(u-m)
 \right).
\]
Then $\sqrt{F_{t+1}(u)}=\left\lVert z(u)\right\rVert_2$.  By the triangle inequality and
$\Sigma\succeq\tau^2I$,
\[
 \sqrt{F_{t+1}(v)}
 \le\sqrt{F_{t+1}(u)}
 +\frac1{\sqrt2}\left\lVert \Sigma^{-1/2}(v-u)\right\rVert_2
 \le\sqrt{F_{t+1}(u)}+\frac{\left\lVert v-u\right\rVert_2}{\sqrt2\tau}.
\]
At birth, $P_i=\mathcal N(w_0,D^2I)$; the Gaussian KL formula and
$\left\lVert u_i-w_0\right\rVert\le D$ give \eqref{eq:initial-KL}.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label[lemma]{lem:drawdown}
Consider a fresh scale-$\tau$ history on $[i,b]$.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drawdown-condition}
 \sum_{t=i}^q r_t\ge-H
 \qquad\text{for every }q\in[i,b],
\end{equation}

\label[lemma]{lem:low-variation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:low-var}

\end{equation}

Recursively decompose $I=[r,s]$.  By \cref{lem:schedule}, choose
$i\in[r,\lfloor(r+s)/2\rfloor]$ whose history remains active through $s$.

\[
 S_q=\sum_{t=i}^q
 [\widetilde f_t(x_t^{i,k})-\mathbb E_{Q_t}\widetilde f_t].
\]

$P_{i:s}\le P_I\le V_\tau$ imply
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal R_\tau([i,s])
 &\le a+\frac1\lambda
 \left(\sqrt{C_\tau}+\frac{V_\tau}{\sqrt2\tau}\right)^2-H_\tau\\
 &\le\frac{H_\tau}{64L}.
\end{align*}
Indeed, with
$\varepsilon=V_\tau/(\sqrt2\tau\sqrt{C_\tau})
=1/(512\sqrt2L)$, the first line equals
\[

\]
Since $C_\tau/\lambda=B_\tau/\lambda+H_\tau
\le(1+128L)A_\tau$, this is at most
$2A_\tau=H_\tau/(64L)$.

\[
 a-H_\tau\le-\frac{127}{128}H_\tau.
\]
Remove all bad nodes from the recursion tree.  Every remaining connected component consists only of left-child edges, because a right child is created only by a bad node.  Along a left edge the interval length at least halves, so each component has at most $L$ good nodes.  Removing $N_{\rm bad}$ binary-tree nodes creates at most $2N_{\rm bad}+1$ components.  Hence
\[
 N_{\rm good}\le L(2N_{\rm bad}+1).
\]
The total charge is at most
\[
 \frac{H_\tau}{64L}L(2N_{\rm bad}+1)
 -\frac{127H_\tau}{128}N_{\rm bad}
 \le\frac{H_\tau}{64}.
\]

We control local update errors at the implemented state, so their contribution is additive through \Cref{lem:robust}. Write $N=O(\log^2(2T))$ for a uniform bound on the number of active states and set the target local errors to $\varepsilon_t,\zeta_t\le\lambda\delta/(2T)$.

\emph{Uniform conditioning.}
Work in coordinates translated by the rational initial point $w_0$. Every feasible mean then has norm at most $D$. The stored covariances satisfy $\tau^2I\preceq\Sigma\preceq D^2I$, with $\tau\ge D/T$. Before flooring, the precision matrix is bounded between $D^{-2}I$ and $(\tau^{-2}+\lambda\gamma G^2)I$. The tilt mean is obtained by solving this positive-definite system with a bounded right-hand side. Consequently, all relevant magnitudes, inverse eigenvalues, and derivatives of the Gaussian KL formula have bounds of the form $2^{\operatorname{poly}(L_0)}\operatorname{poly}(d,T,1+1/\gamma,1+D+G)$. This includes inverse powers of $D$, since its positive rational encoding has at most $L_0$ bits. Thus polynomially many guard bits in the stated parameters suffice for any prescribed local KL error.

\emph{A rational covariance floor.}
The scale $\tau=D$ has the exact stored covariance $D^2I$. For the other dyadic scales, $\tau\le D/2$. Compute a rational approximation to the spectral clipping of the tilted covariance into $[\tau^2,D^2]$, first moving the two clipping thresholds inward by a guard margin $h$ and then approximating the matrix to operator-norm error at most $h/2$. The stored rational covariance then remains in the certified interval $[\tau^2,D^2]$ and differs from the exact floor by $O(h)$.

This operation has polynomial bit complexity without an eigenvalue separation assumption. One explicit construction replaces the scalar positive part $x_+$ by $(x+\sqrt{x^2+\epsilon^2})/2$, with uniform error at most $\epsilon/2$, and applies this formula successively at the lower and upper thresholds. For a symmetric matrix $M$, the required positive-definite square root is that of $M^2+\epsilon^2I$. Starting from a scalar upper bound times the identity, Newton's iteration $X\leftarrow(X+X^{-1}(M^2+\epsilon^2I))/2$ reduces to the scalar square-root iteration in the eigenbasis of $M$. Its iteration count is polynomial in the logarithmic spectral bounds and the requested precision. Rational rounding with guard bits gives the required operator-norm error; entrywise error $e$ contributes at most $de$ in operator norm. Choosing the smoothing, iteration, and rounding errors below fixed fractions of $h$ proves the claimed polynomial construction. The uniform KL bounds above turn its $O(h)$ covariance error into the desired local KL error.

\emph{Feasible mean updates.}
Round the tilt mean and the positive-definite projection metric to the working precision before calling the projection oracle. Their input encodings therefore have at most $B$ bits per coordinate, independent of earlier oracle-output lengths. Perturbing these inputs changes the quadratic objective uniformly on $\mathcal W$ by a quantity controlled by the same magnitude bounds. Hence a sufficiently accurate oracle output has objective gap at most $\xi$ for the exact floored metric $A_*=(C^\flat)^{-1}$. If $m^*$ is its exact projection and $\widehat m$ is the feasible returned point, the projection variational inequality gives
\[
 \|\widehat m-m^*\|_{A_*}^2\le\xi,
 \qquad
 \|u-\widehat m\|_{A_*}^2-\|u-m^*\|_{A_*}^2
 \le2(D/\tau)\sqrt\xi+\xi.
\]
The covariance and mean errors therefore yield \eqref{eq:approx-kl} uniformly over $u\in\mathcal W$. The oracle's output-length bound makes each stored feasible mean polynomial in the working precision. At the next update, its derived projection inputs are rounded again to $B$ bits, so encoding lengths do not grow with the number of rounds.

\emph{Master weights.}
Absolute rounding alone can erase a small expert weight. We instead round each normalized probability upward. For an exact normalized vector $v$ with at most $N$ entries, compute rational upper approximations $b_j$ such that
\[
 v_j+h\le b_j\le v_j+3h,
 \qquad q_j=\frac{b_j}{\sum_\ell b_\ell}.
\]
Certified absolute approximations to the exponentials suffice to construct these bounds. They imply
\[
 q_j\ge\frac{v_j}{1+3Nh},
 \qquad q_j\ge\frac{h}{1+3Nh}.
\]
Apply this operation after the exponential update and after birth, deletion, and normalization. Each operation decreases an individual weight by at most a factor $1+3Nh$ relative to its ideal value at that implemented state. In the telescoping proof of \Cref{lem:meta}, this adds at most $C Nh$ per round to the logarithmic overhead, including the birth weight. It proves \eqref{eq:approx-master} with $\zeta_t\le C Nh$. All stored probabilities have polynomial encoding length and a known positive lower bound. Since $|\lambda\widetilde f_t(x)|$ is uniformly bounded on $\mathcal W$, the required normalized exponential probabilities can be evaluated to this absolute accuracy in polynomial bit complexity.

The prediction is the exact rational convex combination of feasible means and the stored rational weights. The loss oracle is queried at this actual prediction, so surrogate domination and the Gaussian mix-gap argument apply directly to the implemented sequence. Choose $h$ and the projection objective accuracy so that $\varepsilon_t,\zeta_t\le\lambda\delta/(2T)$. The preceding bounds require only the displayed polynomial values of $B,p$. \Cref{lem:robust} then bounds the additional regret by $\delta$. If $n\theta^2\le1/32$, then every sequential prediction $X_r\in[-1,1]$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-point-risk}
 \sum_{r=1}^n\mathbb E(X_r-S\theta)^2\ge c n\theta^2
\end{equation}
for a universal $c>0$, where $X_r$ is measurable with respect to $Y_1,\ldots,Y_{r-1}$ and auxiliary randomness independent of $(S,Y_{1:n})$. Independent observations from other hidden coordinates may be included in this auxiliary randomness.
<!-- end lemma-15 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
The one-sample KL divergence between the two sign models is
\[
 \theta\log\frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}\le4\theta^2.
\]
Before round $r$, Pinsker's inequality bounds the total variation between the two histories by
$\sqrt{2(r-1)}\,\theta\le1/4$.  Thus every sign test errs with probability at least $3/8$.  The test $\operatorname{sign}(X_r)$ has
$|X_r-S\theta|\ge\theta$ on a sign error.  Sum over $r$.

<!-- lemma-16: body_md -->
\label[lemma]{lem:effective-lower}
There are universal $c,C>0$ such that the following holds.  Let $1\le m\le d$ and suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-regime}
 C\frac m{\sqrt T}\le P\le c\frac T{m^2}.
\end{equation}
Then the loss class \eqref{eq:hard-loss} admits a distribution and a comparator path of length at most $P$ for which every learner suffers
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-rate}
 \mathbb E\operatorname{Reg}_T\ge c m^{1/3}T^{1/3}P^{2/3}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-16 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Use only the first $m$ coordinates.  Choose
\[
 K=\left\lceil C_1\left(\frac{TP^2}{m^2}\right)^{1/3}\right\rceil,
 \qquad
 n=\left\lfloor\frac{T}{Km}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 a=\frac{P}{4K},
 \qquad
 \theta=\frac a{\sqrt m}.
\]
For suitable constants in \eqref{eq:effective-regime},
$2\le K\le T/(2m)$ and $a\le1/2$.
Partition $Knm$ rounds into $K$ blocks.  In block $k$, draw independent signs
$S_{k,1},\ldots,S_{k,m}$ and set
\[
 u^{(k)}=\frac a{\sqrt m}(S_{k,1},\ldots,S_{k,m},0,\ldots,0).
\]
This lies in $\mathcal W_d$, and the total path is at most
$2a(K-1)\le P$.
Within each block, cycle through the $m$ active coordinates $n$ times.  When coordinate $j$ is queried, draw $Y\in\{-1,+1\}$ with mean
$\theta S_{k,j}$ and reveal $f_{j,Y}$.  Keep the last block comparator fixed on any leftover rounds and continue the same conditional-mean square-loss experiment.  Those leftover rounds have nonnegative conditional expected excess, so they may be discarded in the lower-bound calculation.

Conditional on the signs and the past observations $\mathcal H_{t-1}$, every prediction $w_j$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-risk}
 \mathbb E\left[f_{j,Y}(w)-f_{j,Y}(u^{(k)})\mid S_{k,j},\mathcal H_{t-1}\right]
 =\frac12(w_j-\theta S_{k,j})^2.
\end{equation}
The information available for each hidden sign is controlled by
\[
 n\theta^2
 \le\frac{TP^2}{16K^3m^2},
\]
which is at most $1/32$ for a sufficiently large $C_1$.  Apply
\cref{lem:two-point} independently to every block-coordinate pair.  Since
$Knm\ge T/2$,
\[
 \mathbb E\operatorname{Reg}_T\ge cT\theta^2
 =c\frac{TP^2}{mK^2}
 \ge c m^{1/3}T^{1/3}P^{2/3}.
\]
The calculation holds after conditioning on any learner randomness. Averaging over the generated loss sequences and comparator paths therefore implies that, for every randomized learner, at least one admissible deterministic pair attains the same expected lower bound.

<!-- lemma-17: body_md -->
\label[lemma]{lem:static-lower}
If $T\ge c_0d^2$, then the same class has static minimax regret $\Omega(d)$.
<!-- end lemma-17 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Draw independent fixed signs $S_1,\ldots,S_d$ and set the fixed comparator
$u_j=S_j/(8\sqrt d)$. Query each coordinate for $n=d$ initial rounds with labels of mean $S_j/(8\sqrt d)$.  On all remaining rounds continue to use losses of the form \eqref{eq:hard-loss} with the same conditional means.  Their conditional expected excess over the fixed comparator is nonnegative, so it is enough to lower-bound the initial observations.  The product $n\theta^2=1/64$ satisfies \Cref{lem:two-point}, and \cref{lem:two-point,eq:square-risk} yield constant regret per coordinate and hence $\Omega(d)$ total regret.  Averaging over the signs and observations yields an admissible deterministic loss sequence and fixed comparator with at least this regret in expectation over learner randomness.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label[theorem]{thm:lower-restated}
There are universal constants $c,c_0>0$ such that the following holds.  Let
\[
 \mathcal W_d=\{w\in\mathbb R^d:\left\lVert w\right\rVert_2\le1/2\}
\]
and let the adversary use only the losses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hard-loss-restated}
 f_{j,y}(w)=\frac12(w_j-y)^2,
 \qquad j\in[d],\quad y\in\{-1,+1\}.
\end{equation}
This class has diameter $D=1$, gradient bound $G\le3/2$, and exp-concavity coefficient at least $4/9$, uniformly in $d$.  If $T\ge c_0d^2$, then for every $P\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-frontier-restated}
 \inf_{\mathsf A}\sup_{\substack{f_{1:T}\text{ of the form }\eqref{eq:hard-loss}\\
 u_{1:T}\in\mathcal W_d^T,\ P_T(u)\le P}}
 \mathbb E_{\mathsf A}\operatorname{Reg}_T(u_{1:T})
 \ge
 c\left[
 d+\min\left\{
 d^{1/3}T^{1/3}P^{2/3},\sqrt{TP},T
 \right\}
 \right].
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
We first verify the stated regularity. For \eqref{eq:hard-loss},
\[
 \nabla f_{j,y}(w)=(w_j-y)e_j,
 \qquad
 \nabla^2f_{j,y}(w)=e_je_j^\top.
\]
On $\mathcal W_d$, $|w_j-y|\le3/2$, so
\[
 \nabla^2f_{j,y}(w)
 \succeq\frac49\nabla f_{j,y}(w)\nabla f_{j,y}(w)^\top,
\]
and the claimed uniform regularity follows.
Let $C_*,c_*$ be the constants in \Cref{lem:effective-lower}, decreasing $c_*$ so that $c_*\le1$. Fix $\kappa=\sqrt{c_*}/2$ and $b=\kappa^2/4$. We may enlarge the universal constant in $T\ge c_0d^2$ so that
\[
 C_*d/\sqrt T\le\min\{c_*T/d^2,bT\},
 \qquad c_*T/d^2\ge(C_*\kappa)^{2/3}.
\]
If $P<C_*d/\sqrt T$, the curved expression is at most $C_*^{2/3}d$, so \Cref{lem:static-lower} suffices. For
$C_*d/\sqrt T\le P\le c_*T/d^2$, use \Cref{lem:effective-lower} with $m=d$.

Suppose next that $c_*T/d^2<P\le bT$ and choose
\[
 m=\left\lfloor\kappa\sqrt{T/P}\right\rfloor.
\]
The upper bound on $P$ ensures that the quantity inside the floor is at least two, so
$\frac\kappa2\sqrt{T/P}\le m\le\kappa\sqrt{T/P}<d$.
In particular, $P\le c_*T/m^2$. The second displayed condition gives
$C_*m/\sqrt T\le C_*\kappa/\sqrt P\le P$.
Thus both conditions of \Cref{lem:effective-lower} hold and its lower bound is $\Omega(\sqrt{TP})$.

At $P=bT$, one of the preceding cases gives $\Omega(T)$: the all-coordinate bound is at least a constant times $T$ if its regime reaches $bT$, and otherwise the effective-dimension bound gives $\sqrt b\,T$. Monotonicity of the admissible comparator class extends this lower bound to every $P\ge bT$. These cases cover every budget, including the constant-factor transition ranges. Finally, the static and moving constructions belong to the same loss class; taking their maximum and using $\max\{a,b\}\ge(a+b)/2$ yields the additive $d$ term.
