Write $M=M_\lambda$ and $U=U_\lambda$. The stabilizer of the
standard point of $X_\lambda$ is
\[
H_\lambda=(U\times U)\rtimes\operatorname{diag}M.
\]
Since $U\times U$ has no nontrivial algebraic characters,
characters of $H_\lambda$ identify with characters of $M$.
Thus it suffices to compute the $M$-characters on the fibers
of ${\mathcal{L}}_\lambda$ and ${\mathcal{D}}_\lambda$ at this point. Here
${\mathcal{L}}_\lambda$ denotes the descent of the restriction of the
canonical bundle of $\operatorname{Bun}_G(\mathbb P^1)_{0,\infty}$
to the stratum indexed by $\lambda$.

Consider the grading defined by $\lambda$:
\[
\mathfrak g=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}\mathfrak g_n,
\qquad
\mathfrak g_n=\{v:\operatorname{Ad}(\lambda(a))v=a^nv\}.
\]
Each $\mathfrak g_n$ is an $M$-module, and
\[
\mathfrak g_0=\mathfrak m,
\qquad
\mathfrak u=\bigoplus_{n>0}\mathfrak g_n.
\]
Let $d_n=\det(\mathfrak g_n)$, regarded as a character of $M$.
Then
\[
\delta_\lambda=\prod_{n>0}d_n,
\qquad
\varphi_\lambda=\prod_{n>0}d_n^{\,n}.
\]
The second identity follows by restriction to $T$ and also
shows that the displayed formula for $\varphi_\lambda|_T$
extends to a character of $M$.

Let $E_\lambda$ be the bundle representing the stratum, with
the standard framings at $0$ and $\infty$. With the convention
that the positive-degree root spaces correspond to $U$, its
adjoint bundle has the $M$-equivariant decomposition
\[
\operatorname{ad}(E_\lambda)
\simeq\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}
\mathfrak g_n\otimes\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(n).
\]
Put
\[
A_\lambda=\operatorname{ad}(E_\lambda)(-0-\infty).
\]
The Lie algebra of the automorphism group preserving the
two framings is $H^0(\mathbb P^1,A_\lambda)$. Consequently,
the fiber of ${\mathcal{D}}_\lambda$ has character
\[
\psi({\mathcal{D}}_\lambda)
=\prod_{n>0}d_n^{\,n-1}
=\varphi_\lambda\delta_\lambda^{-1},
\]
because $h^0(\mathcal O(n-2))=n-1$ for $n\geq1$, and
$h^0(\mathcal O(n-2))=0$ for $n\leq0$.

The tangent complex of the framed-bundle stack at
$E_\lambda$ is $R\Gamma(\mathbb P^1,A_\lambda)[1]$.
Its canonical-line fiber is therefore
\[
\det H^0(\mathbb P^1,A_\lambda)
\otimes\det H^1(\mathbb P^1,A_\lambda)^{-1}.
\]
Since $\chi(\mathcal O(n-2))=n-1$, this yields
\[
\psi({\mathcal{L}}_\lambda)=\prod_{n\in\mathbb Z}d_n^{\,n-1}.
\]
The adjoint representation of $M$ has trivial determinant,
so $d_0=1$. Moreover, $d_{-n}=d_n^{-1}$: their restrictions
to $T$ are products of opposite roots. Pairing the factors
indexed by $n$ and $-n$, we obtain
\[
\psi({\mathcal{L}}_\lambda)
=\prod_{n>0}d_n^{\,n-1}d_{-n}^{-n-1}
=\prod_{n>0}d_n^{\,2n}
=\varphi_\lambda^2.
\]
This proves both formulas.

Write $P=P_\lambda=MU$, and let $\overline P=M\overline U$
be the opposite parabolic. All induction and Jacquet
functors below are normalized. We take Ext groups in the
category of smooth complex representations of the indicated groups of $F$-points.

First, we have
\[
{\mathcal{S}}_{1/2}(X_\lambda)
\simeq i_{P(F)\times P(F)}^{G(F)\times G(F)}{\mathcal{S}}(M),
\]
where ${\mathcal{S}}(M)$ carries the $M(F)\times M(F)$-action
\[
((m_1,m_2)\cdot f)(m)=f(m_1^{-1}mm_2).
\]
Indeed, the projection
\[
X_\lambda(F)\longrightarrow G(F)/P(F)\times G(F)/P(F)
\]
has fiber $(M(F)\times M(F))/\operatorname{diag}M(F)\simeq M(F)$.
The half-density character on the stabilizer
\[
(U(F)\times U(F))\rtimes\operatorname{diag}M(F)
\]
is $\Delta_P$ on the diagonal Levi, where
\[
\Delta_P(m)=|\delta_P(m)|_F,\qquad
\delta_P(m)=\det\bigl(\operatorname{Ad}(m)|_{\mathfrak u}\bigr),\quad m\in M(F).
\]
This is precisely the restriction of
$\Delta_P^{1/2}\boxtimes\Delta_P^{1/2}$, the character
normalizing induction from $P(F)\times P(F)$.

By second adjointness, $i_{P(F)\times P(F)}^{G(F)\times G(F)}$ is left
adjoint to $r_{\overline P(F)\times\overline P(F)}^{G(F)\times G(F)}$.
Both functors are exact, so induction preserves projectives
and the adjunction extends to all Ext groups. Thus, setting
\[
R=r_{\overline P(F)\times\overline P(F)}^{G(F)\times G(F)}\Pi,
\]
we obtain
\[
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{G(F)\times G(F)}
\bigl({\mathcal{S}}_{1/2}(X_\lambda),\Pi\bigr)
\simeq
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{M(F)\times M(F)}\bigl({\mathcal{S}}(M),R\bigr).
\]

Choose a uniformizer $\varpi$ of $F$, and set
\[
a=\lambda(\varpi^{-1})\in Z(M)(F),
\qquad
z=(a,a)\in Z(M\times M)(F).
\]
For every root $\beta$ occurring in
$\operatorname{Lie}(\overline U)$, we have $|\beta(a)|<1$.
Hence $z$ lies in the strictly contracting chamber for
$\overline P(F)\times\overline P(F)$. Since $G$ is semisimple and
$\lambda\neq 0$, its image modulo the center of $G(F)\times G(F)$
does not belong to a compact subgroup.

Casselman's square-integrability criterion implies that
every generalized central exponent $\xi$ of $R$ satisfies
\[
|\xi(z)|<1.
\]
Since $R$ has finite length, its generalized
central-character decomposition shows that $z-1$ acts
invertibly on $R$. On the other hand, $z-1$ acts as zero
on ${\mathcal{S}}(M)$, because
\[
(z\cdot f)(m)=f(a^{-1}ma)=f(m).
\]

The actions of a central element on an Ext group induced
from its two arguments coincide. Consequently, the
endomorphism induced by $z-1$ on
\[
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{M(F)\times M(F)}({\mathcal{S}}(M),R)
\]
is simultaneously zero, by its action on the first
argument, and invertible, by its action on the second.
This Ext group therefore vanishes for every $i\geq 0$,
proving the theorem.

Let $j:G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Bun}_G(\mathbb{P}^1)_{0,\infty}$ be the open
stratum of trivial bundles. Let ${\mathcal{Z}}_D$ be the Hecke correspondence,
with input and output maps $p_1,p_2:{\mathcal{Z}}_D\to\operatorname{Bun}_G(\mathbb{P}^1)_{0,\infty}$, and set
\[
 Z=p_1^{-1}(G),\qquad p=p_1|_Z:Z\to G,\qquad
 q=p_2|_Z:Z\to\operatorname{Bun}_G(\mathbb{P}^1)_{0,\infty}.
\]
Since the modification is minuscule, $p$ is proper and $q$ is smooth;
the canonical-line identification defining $h_D$ gives, after the
fixed trivialization of the line attached to $D$,
                                
       \begin{equation} \label{E:hecke-relative-line} 
 |p^*\omega_G|^{1/2}
 \simeq |q^*\omega_{\operatorname{Bun}_G(\mathbb{P}^1)_{0,\infty}}|^{1/2}\otimes|\omega_{Z/\operatorname{Bun}_G(\mathbb{P}^1)_{0,\infty}}|.
\end{equation}
Thus $h_D(f)=q_!p^*f$, where \eqref{E:hecke-relative-line} is understood
in this formula; see \cite[Section 5.1]{BKP}.

Let ${\mathcal{U}}=q(Z)$ and $Z^\circ=q^{-1}(G)$. The stack ${\mathcal{U}}$ is an
invariant open substack of finite type containing $G$, and
$q:Z\to{\mathcal{U}}$ is a smooth cover. This cover is also surjective on
$F$-points up to isomorphism. Indeed, in the present $\operatorname{PGL}(N)$
situation, its fiber over a framed bundle is a nonempty open subset
of the space of inverse modifications
$\operatorname{Res}_{E/F}\mathbb{P}^{N-1}_E$. The latter is an $F$-rational
variety with Zariski-dense $F$-points. The open subset $Z^\circ$ is
dense in $Z$.

For compact open subgroups $K_1,K_2\subset G(F)$, put
\[
 A_{K_1,K_2}(g)=E_{K_1}\pi(g)E_{K_2}
 \in\operatorname {Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{K_2},V^{K_1}).
\]
Both fixed-vector spaces are finite-dimensional. On $G$, integration
against $A_{K_1,K_2}(g)|dg|^{1/2}$ gives the corresponding block of
the map $f\mapsto\pi(f)$. We pull this operator-valued half-density
back to $Z^\circ$ and show that it is locally integrable on all of
$Z$, coordinate by coordinate.

The analytic input is that matrix coefficients of a square-integrable
representation belong to the Harish-Chandra Schwartz space, and
regular semisimple orbital integrals of such functions converge
absolutely. The matrix entries of $A_{K_1,K_2}$ are such coefficients.
The open-correspondence identification underlying Lemma ~\ref{L:conv} is
$Z^\circ\simeq G\times G/T$, where
$p(g,aT)=g$ and $q(g,aT)=aya^{-1}g$; the relative density is the
one defining $\mu_y$. Consequently, for any nonnegative
$\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(G)$ and any matrix entry, the absolute integral
against $p^*\varphi$ is
                                
       \begin{equation} \label{E:orbital-absolute} 
 \int_{G(F)}\varphi(g)
       \int_{\Omega_y}
       |[A_{K_1,K_2}(xg)]_{ij}|\,d\mu_y(x)\,dg<\infty.
\end{equation}
Here and below a function on $G(F)$ is viewed as a half-density using
$|dg|^{1/2}$. To see the finiteness in \eqref{E:orbital-absolute}, note
that $\pi(g)E_{K_2}$ takes only finitely many values as $g$ ranges
over $\operatorname{supp}(\varphi)$, since it is constant on right
$K_2$-cosets.

This proves the local integrability claim. More explicitly, for any
compact subset $C\subset Z(F)$, choose $\varphi\geq0$ which is positive
on $p(C)$. Under \eqref{E:hecke-relative-line}, $p^*\varphi$ is a
nonvanishing positive test section near $C$, and every test section
supported in $C$ is bounded in absolute value by a constant multiple
of it. The complement $Z\setminus Z^\circ$ has measure zero, so the
operator-valued half-density defines a locally integrable
distribution on $Z$ with values in
$\operatorname {Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{K_2},V^{K_1})$.

These distributions descend along $q$. Indeed, the two pullbacks to
the smooth variety $Z\times_{{\mathcal{U}}}Z$ agree on the dense open subset
lying above $G$. Both are locally integrable, since smooth pullback
preserves local integrability. Their complement has measure zero,
so the two distributions agree everywhere. Smooth descent therefore
gives a linear map
\[
 \Psi_{K_1,K_2}:{\mathcal{S}}_{1/2}({\mathcal{U}})
 \longrightarrow\operatorname {Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{K_2},V^{K_1}).
\]
Equivalently, one may use an admissible presentation of ${\mathcal{U}}$:
the descended distributions are invariant and hence define
maps on the coinvariants defining its Schwartz space.

The maps $\Psi_{K_1,K_2}$ are compatible with averaging and with
the $G(F)\times G(F)$-action. These compatibilities hold on $G$ and
hence hold for their locally integrable extensions. If
$s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1/2}({\mathcal{U}})$ is fixed by $J\times J$, its blocks are
therefore those of the single operator $\Psi_{J,J}(s)$, extended
from $V^J$ to $V$ by $E_J$. Explicitly, with this extension understood,
\[
 \Psi_{K_1,K_2}(s)=E_{K_1}\Psi_{J,J}(s)E_{K_2}.
\]
In particular, these blocks define an actual element of $\operatorname{End\,}'(V)$,
independent of $J$. Since every $s$ is fixed by some $J\times J$,
we obtain a $G(F)\times G(F)$-equivariant map
\[
 \Psi_\pi:{\mathcal{S}}_{1/2}({\mathcal{U}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{End\,}'(V)
\]
extending $f\mapsto\pi(f)$.

The quotient ${\mathcal{S}}_{1/2}({\mathcal{U}})/{\mathcal{S}}(G)$ has a finite filtration
whose successive quotients are the nontrivial stratum modules
${\mathcal{S}}_{1/2}(X_\lambda)$ occurring in ${\mathcal{U}}$. By Theorem ~\ref{T:exti},
\[
 \operatorname {Hom}_{G(F)\times G(F)}
 \bigl({\mathcal{S}}_{1/2}({\mathcal{U}})/{\mathcal{S}}(G),\operatorname{End\,}'(V)\bigr)=0.
\]
Thus the extension to ${\mathcal{S}}_{1/2}({\mathcal{U}})$ is unique, and
$\Psi_\pi=\Phi_\pi|_{{\mathcal{S}}_{1/2}({\mathcal{U}})}$.

Choose $K$ such that $f$ is bi-$K$-invariant. Then $h_D(f)$ is
$K\times K$-invariant, so its image is determined by its block on
$V^K$. Since $h_D(f)=q_!p^*f$, the construction above gives
\[
 \Phi_\pi(h_D(f))
 =\int_{G(F)}f(g)\int_{\Omega_y}
       E_K\pi(xg)E_K\,d\mu_y(x)\,dg
 =\pi(\mu_y)\pi(f).
\]
Here all integrals take values in $\operatorname{End\,}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^K)$ and converge
absolutely by \eqref{E:orbital-absolute};
the resulting operators are extended to $V$ using $E_K$. The last
equality follows from the definition of $\pi(\mu_y)$ and the
bi-$K$-invariance of $f$. This proves \eqref{E:orbital-comparison}.

The part of the Hecke correspondence where both projective bundles are
trivial is parametrized by a point of $G$ and a conjugate of the
multiplication operator $y$, with the latter parameter retained in $G/T$.
Indeed, using vector bundles, the modification has the form
\[
 {\mathcal{O}}^N\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(1)^N,\qquad A(t)=tI-C,
\]
after normalizing its invertible leading coefficient; its determinant
prescribes the characteristic polynomial of $C$ to be the minimal
polynomial of $y$. All such $C$ form the homogeneous space $G/T$.
Its two endpoint values give the two framed projective bundles.

Compute the canonical-line comparison after passing to a splitting field,
where
\[
 A(t)=\operatorname{diag}(t-y_1,\ldots,t-y_N),\qquad y_i\ne y_j\quad(i\ne j).
\]
This is an algebraic determinant calculation; its result will subsequently
be descended to $F$, before taking absolute values.
In the common meromorphic endomorphism bundle put
\[
 \mathcal K=\operatorname{End\,}(\mathcal E_1)\cap
 A^{-1}\operatorname{End\,}(\mathcal E_2)A,
 \qquad \mathcal E_1={\mathcal{O}}^N,\quad\mathcal E_2={\mathcal{O}}(1)^N.
\]
Twist all these sheaves by ${\mathcal{O}}(-0-\infty)$. Since the two endpoint
endomorphism bundles are trivial and have no framed infinitesimal
automorphisms, the quotient exact sequences identify the two relative
tangent spaces $R_1,R_2$ with the respective spaces of local quotients by
$\mathcal K$. Scalar directions occur identically on both sides and cancel.

We use the canonical-line isomorphism of \cite[Theorem 1.1 and
Section 5.4]{EFK}. Its square compares the determinants of the two
deformation complexes. In the present model, the common terms cancel
in the two quotient exact sequences above, leaving the determinants
of the local quotients. The trace pairing and the residue description
of Serre duality identify these quotient determinants. Thus the squared
canonical-line isomorphism is computed by the determinant of their
local residue pairing. To check its normalization, in the local minuscule
model $A(z)=\operatorname{diag}(z,1,\ldots,1)$ the paired quotient generators are
$E_{i1}$ and $E_{1i}$, and
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(E_{i1} A(z)^{-1}E_{1i} A(z)\bigr)\,dz=1.
\]
The matrix units are dual for the trace pairing, and the displayed
residue is the Serre-duality evaluation in these local generators.
Taking determinants therefore introduces no additional scalar; taking
the absolute square root gives the half-density isomorphism. The
cotangent-line factor is the negative power specified in Section 1.2;
the chosen differential trivializes its density line and fixes the
numerical normalization used below.

For the matrix $A(t)$ above, the first quotient in the $ij$-direction is
supported at $y_j$. It pairs with the $ji$-direction in the second quotient.
Using $dt/t$, their pairing has coefficient
\begin{align}
 \beta(E_{ij},E_{ji})
 &=\operatorname{Res}_{t=y_j}
    \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(E_{ij}A(t)^{-1}E_{ji}A(t)\bigr)\frac{dt}{t}\notag\\
 &=\operatorname{Res}_{t=y_j}\frac{t-y_i}{t-y_j}\frac{dt}{t}
   =\frac{y_j-y_i}{y_j}.\label{eq:residue}
\end{align}
In this expression the residue evaluates the tangent-line-valued local
pairing against $dt/t$ at the modification point.

For completeness, the base tangent determinant introduces no extra
factor. Using the two framing actions, the tangent space to the
correspondence at this point is
$(\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g)/\operatorname{diag}\mathfrak t$, and the two
projection differentials may be written
\[
 (u,v)\longmapsto v-u,
 \qquad (u,v)\longmapsto v-\operatorname{Ad}(y)u.
\]
In a root direction on which $\operatorname{Ad}(y)$ acts by $a$, bases for their kernels
are $(1,1)$ and $(a^{-1},1)$, respectively; the vector $(0,1)$ is a lift
of a base tangent generator for both projections. The comparison of
these two exact-sequence determinants therefore contributes a power
$a^{\pm1}$. The product over all root directions is $1$, because
$\det(\operatorname{Ad}(y)\mid\mathfrak g/\mathfrak t)=1$. On the torus directions
the two projections coincide.

Relative to the trace pairing on $V$, the determinant of
\eqref{eq:residue} is consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weyl}
 \prod_{i\ne j}\frac{y_j-y_i}{y_j}
 =\prod_{i\ne j}(1-y_i/y_j)=\mathcal D_G(y).
\end{equation}
One can also see the direction of this determinant factor directly from
the tangent exact sequences. They identify the ratio of the two base
canonical lines with $\det R_1/\det R_2$. The residue pairing identifies
$\det R_1$ with $(\det R_2)^{-1}$, with coefficient
\eqref{eq:weyl}; hence the coefficient in the relative density is its
absolute square root, rather than its inverse.

The quotient sheaves, trace-residue pairing, and determinant
identifications are defined over $F$. Descending this calculation and
taking absolute square roots therefore gives
$|\mathcal D_G(y)|_F^{1/2}\nu_E$ on the parameter space $G/T$.
This proves \eqref{eq:intrinsic}.

On $\operatorname{GL}(N,F)$ the invariant trace-pairing density is
\[
 dm_{\operatorname{GL}(N)}(g)=\frac{\prod_{i,j}dg_{ij}}{|\det g|_F^N},
\]
where additive measure on each matrix entry gives ${\mathcal{O}}_F$ volume $1$.
Indeed, the trace pairing in the matrix-unit basis has determinant $\pm1$.
On $E^*$ the corresponding density is
\[
 dm_{E^*}(a)=\delta_E^{1/2}
                  \frac{da}{|N_{E/F}(a)|_F},
 \qquad da({\mathcal{O}}_E)=1.
\]
The factor $\delta_E^{1/2}$ is the square root of the absolute determinant
of the field trace pairing in an integral basis. Orthogonality of
$M_N(F)=E\oplus E^\perp$ shows that
\[
 \nu_E=\frac{dm_{\operatorname{GL}(N)}}{dm_{E^*}}
 \quad\text{on}\quad\operatorname{GL}(N,F)/E^*\simeq G(F)/T(F).
\]

Divide both group measures by $dm_{F^*}=dx/|x|_F$. This produces
measures $dg_{\mathrm{can}}$ on $G(F)$ and $dt_{\mathrm{can}}$ on $T(F)$, with
\[
 \nu_E=dg_{\mathrm{can}}/dt_{\mathrm{can}}.
\]
Counting invertible matrices modulo the maximal ideal gives
\[
 dm_{\operatorname{GL}(N)}(\operatorname{GL}(N,{\mathcal{O}}_F))=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-q^{-j}),
 \qquad dm_{F^*}({\mathcal{O}}_F^*)=1-q^{-1}.
\]
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:group-volume}
 dg_{\mathrm{can}}(K)=\prod_{j=2}^{N}(1-q^{-j}).
\end{equation}
Likewise
\[
 dm_{E^*}({\mathcal{O}}_E^*)=\delta_E^{1/2}(1-q^{-f}).
\]
The quotient $E^*/(F^*{\mathcal{O}}_E^*)$ has order $e$.
Its cosets have equal quotient measure, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:torus-volume}
 dt_{\mathrm{can}}(T(F))=
 e\,\delta_E^{1/2}\frac{1-q^{-f}}{1-q^{-1}}.
\end{equation}
Let $dt_T$ be probability Haar measure on the compact group $T(F)$.
Equations \eqref{eq:group-volume} and \eqref{eq:torus-volume} imply,
for arbitrary $dg$,
\[
 \nu_E=
 \frac{dg_{\mathrm{can}}(K)}{dt_{\mathrm{can}}(T(F))\,dg(K)}\frac{dg}{dt_T}
 =\frac{A_E}{dg(K)}\frac{dg}{dt_T}.
\]
Finally, quotient integration and $dt_T(T(F))=1$ give
\[
 (q_y)_*(dg/dt_T)=(p_y)_*dg.
\]
Combining this identity with \eqref{eq:intrinsic} proves
Theorem ~\ref{T:cy}.
