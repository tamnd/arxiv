<!-- maintheorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}\label{fi:thm:iteration}
Let $X$ be a compact complex manifold of dimension $n$, let $(L,h)$
be a smooth Hermitian holomorphic line bundle, and let $E$ be a fixed
holomorphic vector bundle on $X$. Set
$r=\max_{x\in X}\operatorname{rank}(\Theta_L)_x$. Then, for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-endpoint}
 h^q(X,L^p\otimes E)=O_\varepsilon(p^{r+\varepsilon})
 \qquad(0\le q\le n),
\end{equation}
and $h^0(X,L^p\otimes E),h^n(X,L^p\otimes E)=O(p^r)$.
<!-- end maintheorem-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:quantitative}
If $1\le r<n$, then for each integer $k\ge0$ there are constants
$C_k,N_k>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{fi:eq:iteration}
 h^q(X,L^p\otimes E)
 \le C_kp^{r+2(n-r)/(2k+5)}(1+\log p)^{N_k}
 \qquad(0\le q\le n,\ p\ge1).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:kodaira}
On every connected component $X_0$ of $X$,
$\kappa(L|_{X_0})\le r$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{geo:semipositive-maximum}
Let $p\ge1$, $0\ne W\subset H^0(X,L^p)$, and
$d=\max\operatorname{rank}d\Phi_W$. There is $x_*\notin B_W$ with
\[
 \operatorname{rank}d\Phi_W|_{x_*}=d,\qquad
 (\Theta_L)_{x_*}\ge0,\qquad \operatorname{rank}(\Theta_L)_{x_*}\ge d.
\]
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Assume first that $X$ is connected. Put $N=\dim W-1$ and choose a basis $s_0,\ldots,s_N$
of $W$, so that $\Phi_W=[s_0:\cdots:s_N]$ in a common local frame.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 1: resolve the section map.}
Analytic principalization of their common
coefficient ideal gives a modification $\pi:Y\to X$, with $Y$
smooth and compact, and an effective divisor $D$ such that
\[
 \pi^*s_j=s_Dv_j,\qquad
 v_j\in H^0(Y,M),\qquad
 M=\pi^*L^p\otimes\mathcal O_Y(-D),
\]
where the $v_j$ have no common zero and $\pi$ is an isomorphism
over $X\setminus B_W$. These assertions follow from
\cite[Theorem~2.0.3 and Lemma~4.0.3]{Wlodarczyk2009}: compactness
makes the sequence of blowups finite, and equality of the transformed
ideal with $(s_D)$ makes the divided coefficients generate the unit
ideal. Thus $f=[v_0:\cdots:v_N]:Y\to\mathbb P^N$ is holomorphic.
On the dense open set $U=\pi^{-1}(X\setminus B_W)$ it equals
$\Phi_W\circ\pi$, so $\max_Y\operatorname{rank}df=d$.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 2: force the maximum away from critical values.}
The analytic critical locus
$C_f=\{y:\operatorname{rank}df_y<d\}$ is compact. By the proper
mapping and image-dimension theorems \cite{Remmert1957},
$Z=f(Y)$ and $T=f(C_f)$ are compact analytic sets, with
$\dim Z=d$ and $\dim T\le d-1$ if $T\ne\varnothing$.
For the latter bound apply the dimension theorem on each irreducible
component of $C_f$: on its regular locus the restricted differential
has rank at most $d-1$. By Chow's theorem \cite{Chow1949}, $T$ is
projective algebraic. If $T\ne\varnothing$, choose a homogeneous
polynomial $P$ of degree $e\ge1$ vanishing on $T$ but not on $Z$.
If $T=\varnothing$, take $P=1$ and $e=0$; this includes $d=0$. Then
\[
 \sigma_P=P(s_0,\ldots,s_N)\in H^0(X,L^{pe}),\qquad
 \pi^*\sigma_P=s_D^eP(v_0,\ldots,v_N).
\]
Writing $v=(v_0,\ldots,v_N)$, the open set where $P(v)\ne0$
meets the dense set $U$, so
$\sigma_P\not\equiv0$. The smooth function
\[
 \Psi=\sum_j|\sigma_P s_j|_{h^{p(e+1)}}^2
      =|\sigma_P|_{h^{pe}}^2\sum_j|s_j|_{h^p}^2
\]
has a positive maximum $x_*$. Here $\sigma_P\ne0$ and some $s_j\ne0$, so
$x_*\notin B_W$ and its lift is outside $C_f$; hence
$\operatorname{rank}d\Phi_W|_{x_*}=d$.
No bound on the degree $e$, which may depend on $p,W$, is needed.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 3: compare the Hessians at the maximum.}
The tuples $(\sigma_P s_j)$ and $(s_j)$ define the same map near $x_*$.
With $\omega_{\rm FS}=i\partial\bar\partial\log(1+|w|^2)$,
the Hessian test at this positive maximum gives
\[
 0\le\beta_{x_*}\le p(e+1)(\Theta_L)_{x_*},\qquad
 \beta=\Phi_W^*\omega_{\rm FS},\quad
 \operatorname{rank}\beta_{x_*}=d.
\]
Thus $\Theta_L$ is semipositive at $x_*$ and its kernel is contained
in the kernel of $\beta_{x_*}$, proving the claimed rank inequality.


For disconnected $X$, choose a component on which the section map
has maximal rank and restrict $W$ to its nonzero image there.
The original map differs from this subsystem map by a linear
projective inclusion, so the ranks agree. Finally apply the bound
to complete linear systems. The same maximum argument for a single
nonzero section proves the assertion when the semipositive locus
is empty.

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Work on a connected component $X_0$. For every $p\ge1$ for which
$H^0(X_0,L^p|_{X_0})\ne0$,
Proposition~\ref{geo:semipositive-maximum} bounds the maximal rank
of the complete section map by the maximal curvature rank on $X_0$,
and hence by $r$. Taking the maximum over all such $p$ gives
$\kappa(L|_{X_0})\le r$. If no such $p$ exists, then
$\kappa(L|_{X_0})=-\infty$ and the assertion is immediate.

\label{ep:scalar}\label{cor:scalar}
The scalar section spaces satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-bound}
 h^0(X,L^p)\le Cp^r\qquad(p\ge1).
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{ep:line-twist}
For every fixed holomorphic line bundle $A$ on $X$,
\[
 h^0(X,L^p\otimes A)\le C_Ap^r\qquad(p\ge1).
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Induct on $n$, simultaneously for all compact complex manifolds
and all integer rank bounds $r\ge0$. Dimension zero is immediate;
we may assume $X$ connected. If every $H^0(X,L^a\otimes A)$
vanishes there is nothing to prove. Otherwise fix one nonzero
section for one $a\ge1$. Resolve the support of its zero divisor
\cite[Theorem~2.0.3]{Wlodarczyk2009} to obtain a fixed modification
$\mu:Y\to X$ and an effective divisor $D$ with smooth components.
With $M=\mu^*L$,
\[
 \mu^*A=M^{-a}\otimes\mathcal O_Y(D),\qquad
 h^0(X,L^p\otimes A)
 \le h^0(Y,M^{p-a}\otimes\mathcal O_Y(D)).
\]
Pullback is injective on sections and does not increase curvature
rank. Write $D=H_1+\cdots+H_s$, allowing repeated components, and
$D_j=H_1+\cdots+H_j$, with $D_0=0$. For $m=p-a>0$, use
\begin{equation}\label{ep:eq:divisor-sequence}
 0\longrightarrow M^m(D_{j-1})\longrightarrow M^m(D_j)
 \longrightarrow M^m(D_j)|_{H_j}\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
where $M^m(D_j)=M^m\otimes\mathcal O_Y(D_j)$. Taking sections and
summing yields
\begin{equation}\label{ep:eq:divisor-filtration}
\begin{split}
 h^0(Y,M^m\otimes\mathcal O_Y(D))
 \le{}&h^0(Y,M^m)+\sum_{j=1}^s h^0\bigl(H_j,(M|_{H_j})^m
                  \otimes\mathcal O_Y(D_j)|_{H_j}\bigr).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The first term is $O(m^r)$ by Corollary~\ref{ep:scalar}; each
summand is $O(m^r)$ by induction on the smooth $(n-1)$-fold $H_j$,
since $M|_{H_j}$ has curvature rank at most $r$ by
\eqref{eq:pullback-rank}. The section, the modification $\mu$, the
divisor $D$ and its components, the exponent $a$ and all twists are
fixed before $p$ varies; only $m=p-a$ varies, and the number of
summands is fixed. The induction
includes rank bounds $r\ge n-1$, and $D=0$ gives an empty sum.
Absorb the finitely many powers $p\le a$ in the constant.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{ep:vector-twist}\label{cor:section-growth}
For the fixed bundle $E$,
\[
 h^0(X,L^p\otimes E)\le C_Ep^r\qquad(p\ge1).
\]
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For $\operatorname{rank} E>0$, let $\pi:Y=\mathbb P(E^*)\to X$ parametrize lines
in $E^*$, and let $\mathcal O_Y(1)$ be the dual tautological bundle.
Fiberwise linear forms give $\pi_*\mathcal O_Y(1)=E$, hence
\[
 H^0(X,L^p\otimes E)
 =H^0\bigl(Y,(\pi^*L)^p\otimes\mathcal O_Y(1)\bigr).
\]
Since $\operatorname{rank}\pi^*\Theta_L\le r$, apply Lemma~\ref{ep:line-twist}
on $Y$, with $A=\mathcal O_Y(1)$. The lemma applies in dimension $n+\operatorname{rank} E-1$ because its induction
was completed in all dimensions before this reduction. The case $E=0$ is immediate.

\label{cor:top}
The top-degree cohomology satisfies
\[
 h^n(X,L^p\otimes E)=O(p^r).
\]
<!-- end corollary-4 -->

Serre duality \cite{Serre1955}, in the form of
\cite{Wel08}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:duality}
 H^n(X,L^p\otimes E)^*
 \simeq H^0\bigl(X,(L^*)^p\otimes E^*\otimes K_X\bigr).
\end{equation}
The dual metric has curvature $-\Theta_L$ and hence the same rank.
Apply Corollary~\ref{cor:section-growth} to $L^*$ with the fixed auxiliary bundle
$E^*\otimes K_X$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:euler-polynomial}
The Euler characteristic $\chi(X,L^p\otimes E)$ is a polynomial
in $p$ of degree at most $r$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Set $\alpha=\Theta_L/(2\pi)$, so $\alpha^{r+1}=0$ pointwise. Write $\operatorname{ch}(E)$ and $\operatorname{td}(T^{1,0}X)$
for the Chern character and Todd forms of fixed Hermitian metrics.
Analytic Hirzebruch--Riemann--Roch
\cite[Theorem~(3.4), p.~163]{Atiyah1964} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HRR}
 \chi(X,L^p\otimes E)=
 \sum_{j=0}^r\frac{p^j}{j!}
 \int_X\alpha^j\wedge\operatorname{ch}(E)
                       \wedge\operatorname{td}(T^{1,0}X).
\end{equation}
The integral selects real degree $2n$; the remaining factors are
fixed Chern--Weil forms. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euler-bound}
 |\chi(X,L^p\otimes E)|=O(p^r).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- corollary-5: body_md -->
\label{cor:surface-all}
If $n=2$, then
\[
 h^q(X,L^p\otimes E)=O(p^r)\qquad(0\le q\le2).
\]
<!-- end corollary-5 -->

In dimension two, $\chi=h^0-h^1+h^2$, so
\[

 \le h^0(X,L^p\otimes E)+h^2(X,L^p\otimes E)
       +|\chi(X,L^p\otimes E)|=O(p^r)
\]
by the degree-zero and top-degree estimates just proved, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:euler-polynomial}.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{fi:prop:localization}\label{fi:new:cor:observation}
Suppose that, on the global maximal Dolbeault domains of $F_p$,
\[
 I-\sum_{\iota=1}^{B_p}B_\iota\mathscr H_\iota A_\iota
       =\bar\partial T+T\bar\partial.
\]
$T_q:\mathscr L_p^q(X)\to\mathscr D_p^{q-1}(X)$ lowers degree,
$\mathscr H_\iota$ are buffered holomorphic restrictions in bounded
auxiliary dimensions, with bounded defining-list length and coefficient
multiplicity, and polynomial defining-map and norm-comparison bounds. If
$A_\iota,B_\iota$ and $B_p$ have uniform polynomial bounds, then
\begin{equation}\label{fi:eq:localization}
 h^q(X,L^p\otimes E)\le CB_p(1+\log p)^M\qquad(0\le q\le n)
\end{equation}
for fixed $C,M$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{fi:lem:restriction}
Fix $0<a<A$, $m\ge1$, $d\ge0$ and $a/A<\theta<1$.
Let $G:\Delta_A^m\to\mathbb C^d$ extend holomorphically past the closure,
with $\sup|G|\le B$, and set
\[
 \Omega_t=\{z\in\Delta_t^m:G(z)\in\Delta_t^d\},\qquad t=a,A.
\]
For every integer $N\ge0$, the restriction
$R:A^2(\Omega_A)\to A^2(\Omega_a)$ has a linear approximant $R_N$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{fi:eq:restriction}
 \operatorname{rank} R_N\le \binom{N+m+d}{m+d},\qquad
 \|R-R_N\|\le C(1+B)^d\theta^N,
\end{equation}
where $C$ depends only on $a,A,m,d,\theta$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The triangular biholomorphism
\[
 T(z,w)=(z,w-G(z))
\]
maps $\Delta_A^{m+d}$ to a bounded pseudoconvex domain $V$ of diameter
at most $C(1+B)$ and has complex Jacobian determinant one. The
coordinate slice $V\cap\{w'=0\}$ is $(\Omega_A,0)$ with its base
measure. Apply the hyperplane $L^2$ extension theorem of
Ohsawa--Takegoshi in the form proved in
\cite[Theorem~A]{Ber96}, successively in the $d$ normal coordinates.
At each step the intermediate slice is pseudoconvex. Dilation of a
domain of bounded diameter shows that the one-step \emph{squared}
norm bound is $CD^2$ for diameter $D$. Thus $f\in A^2(\Omega_A)$
extends to $V$ with norm at most $C(1+B)^d\|f\|$.
Disconnected slices cause no problem: the theorem prescribes the
holomorphic function on the entire hyperplane slice.

Pullback by $T$ gives $F\in A^2(\Delta_A^{m+d})$ satisfying
$F(z,G(z))=f(z)$ with the same norm bound. The subspace of Bergman
functions vanishing on this graph is closed: Bergman convergence
implies local uniform convergence. Orthogonal projection onto its
orthogonal complement therefore selects the minimum-norm extension
linearly, with the preceding bound. This does not assert boundedness
of unrestricted graph evaluation on the whole ambient Bergman space.

Let $T_NF$ be the Taylor polynomial of $F$ at the origin of total
degree at most $N$. Orthogonality
of monomials and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \sup_{\Delta_a^{m+d}}|F-T_NF|\le C\theta^N\|F\|_{A^2(\Delta_A^{m+d})}.
\]
The polynomial factor in the number of monomials of each degree is
absorbed by $\theta^N$. Evaluate on $(z,G(z))$ and integrate over
$\Omega_a$, whose volume is bounded. The approximating range is
spanned by restrictions of ambient monomials of total degree at most
$N$. This proves both estimates.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{fi:def:restrictionmodel}
For an integer $q_{\max}\ge0$, an order-$q_{\max}$ restriction model for $R$ consists of
maps $K_b:\mathscr L_+^b\to\mathscr L_-^b$ for $0\le b\le q_{\max}$
and $T_b:\mathscr L_+^b\to\mathscr D_-^{b-1}$ for
$0\le b\le q_{\max}+1$, with $T_0=0$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{fi:new:eq:modelpackage}
 R-K_b=\bar\partial_-T_b+T_{b+1}\bar\partial_+
 \quad\hbox{on }\mathscr D_+^b,\qquad 0\le b\le q_{\max}.
\end{equation}
The maps $T_b,\bar\partial_-T_b$ are polynomially bounded through degree
$q_{\max}+1$, and $T_{b+1}\bar\partial_+$ has a polynomially bounded extension
through degree $q_{\max}$. Each $K_b$ has a finite factorization
\[
 K_b=\sum_\iota B_{b\iota}\mathscr H_\iota A_{b\iota},\qquad
 \mathscr H_\iota:\mathscr H_\iota^+\longrightarrow\mathscr H_\iota^-.
\]
Here $\mathscr H_\iota$ is a fully buffered holomorphic restriction
between Bergman spaces, allowing finite coefficient direct sums;
$A_{b\iota}:\mathscr L_+^b\to\mathscr H_\iota^+$ and
$B_{b\iota}:\mathscr H_\iota^-\to\mathscr L_-^b$ are polynomially
bounded. The auxiliary dimensions are bounded. Each label records
the degree, tuple and layer of that occurrence, so repeated maps
are counted separately.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{fi:lem:composition}
Let $R_j:C_j\to C_{j+1}$ preserve maximal domains and commute
with $d_j$ and $\delta_j$. At each layer $j$, the tuple restrictions
admit order-$(q_{\max}+1)$ models with polynomial bounds uniform over
tuples and labels. Let $M_j$ be the total number of labeled restriction
occurrences over all tuples and required Dolbeault degrees at that
layer. Assume that $R_j$, $\delta_j$ and the tuple counts have uniform
polynomial bounds.
Put $\mathcal R^J=R_{J-1}\cdots R_0$.

There are $S:C_0^q\to C_J^q$ for $q\le q_{\max}$ and
$\mathcal T:C_0^q\to\operatorname{Dom}D_J^{q-1}$ for $q\le q_{\max}+1$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{fi:sc:layeridentity}
 \mathcal R^J=S+D_J\mathcal T+\mathcal T D_0\quad\hbox{on }\operatorname{Dom}D_0^q,
 \qquad q\le q_{\max}.
\end{equation}
The operators $\mathcal T,D_J\mathcal T$ are polynomially bounded through degree
$q_{\max}+1$, and $\mathcal T D_0$ has a polynomially bounded extension through
degree $q_{\max}$. The map $S$ factors through at most $\sum_jM_j$
labeled holomorphic restriction occurrences, with polynomial input
and output norms.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Give each tuple homotopy the sign $(-1)^a$ and take direct sums.
The resulting $T_j$ has bidegree $(0,-1)$, and
\[
 R_j-K_j=d_{j+1}T_j+T_jd_j,\qquad
 K_j=\sum_{\iota=1}^{M_j}B_{j\iota}\mathscr H_{j\iota}A_{j\iota}.
\]
Each label includes its tuple and Dolbeault degree; the corresponding
projections and inclusions into the direct sums are absorbed into
$A_{j\iota}$ and $B_{j\iota}$. The model definition supplies the
uniform polynomial operator and differential-product bounds through
the required degrees.

Define
\[
 W_j=\delta_{j+1}T_j+T_j\delta_j,\qquad
 F_j=K_j-W_j=R_j-D_{j+1}T_j-T_jD_j.
\]
All these maps are bounded through total degree $q_{\max}+1$. The products
$dW_j=-\delta_{j+1}dT_j+dT_j\delta_j$ are bounded, so $W_j$
takes $L^2$ into the maximal domain. For
$u\in\operatorname{Dom}D_j$ of degree $q\le q_{\max}+1$, both
$R_ju$ and $T_jD_ju$ lie in the target maximal domain, and
$D_{j+1}T_ju$ is closed. Thus $F_j$ preserves maximal domains;
the term $T_jD_ju$ uses homotopy degree $q+1\le q_{\max}+2$.
For $q\le q_{\max}$, applying the child identity to $D_ju$ gives
\[
 D_{j+1}F_ju=R_jD_ju-D_{j+1}T_jD_ju=F_jD_ju.
\]
This uses the child identity only through degree $q_{\max}+1$ and
requires no commutation of $K_j$ with $\delta_j$.

Set $F_{<j}=F_{j-1}\cdots F_0$ and
$R_{>j}=R_{J-1}\cdots R_{j+1}$, with empty products equal to the
identity. Product telescoping gives
\[
 \mathcal R^J-F_{J-1}\cdots F_0=D_J\mathcal T+\mathcal T D_0,\qquad
 \mathcal T=\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}R_{>j}T_jF_{<j}.
\]
On inputs through degree $q_{\max}+1$, each summand has left differential
$R_{>j}(D_{j+1}T_j)F_{<j}$ and takes values in the maximal domain.
On inputs through degree $q_{\max}$, the chain identity for $F_{<j}$
rewrites its right differential as
$R_{>j}(T_jD_j)F_{<j}$. Both products are bounded in the stated
degrees; no holomorphic restriction factor has been differentiated.

A second telescoping identity is
\[
 F_{J-1}\cdots F_0=(-W_{J-1})\cdots(-W_0)
   +\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}(-W_{J-1})\cdots(-W_{j+1})K_jF_{<j}.
\]
Each $W_j$ lowers Dolbeault degree by one, so the first term vanishes
on Dolbeault degrees at most $q_{\max}$. The remaining sum defines $S$ and
proves \eqref{fi:sc:layeridentity}. Substituting the factorization
of $K_j$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{fi:sc:additivefactorization}
 S=\sum_{j,\iota}
 \bigl[(-W_{J-1})\cdots(-W_{j+1})B_{j\iota}\bigr]
 \mathscr H_{j\iota}\bigl[A_{j\iota}F_{<j}\bigr].
\end{equation}
Each child label contributes one summand, with polynomially bounded
operator factors on its left and right, so the count is at most
$\sum_jM_j$. Replacing $\mathscr H_{j\iota}$ by a rank-$s_{j\iota}$
approximant gives rank at most $\sum_{j,\iota}s_{j\iota}$.
The error is bounded by the sum of the individual errors multiplied
by the norms of those two operator factors. These norms are polynomial
because $J$ is fixed.

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Write $S_p=\sum_{\iota=1}^{B_p}B_\iota\mathscr H_\iota A_\iota$ and
let $\Pi_p$ be the orthogonal projection onto the $\bar\partial$-harmonic
forms for the actual metrics. Hermitian Hodge theory
\cite[Chapters~IV--V]{Wel08} identifies its range in degree $q$
with $H^q(X,F_p)$, and $\|\Pi_p\|=1$. For harmonic input,
$T\bar\partial\Pi_p=0$, while $T\Pi_p$ lies in the maximal domain.
Thus $\bar\partial T\Pi_p$ is exact and orthogonal to harmonic forms. The
assumed identity gives
\[
 \Pi_pS_p\Pi_p=\Pi_p.
\]

By Lemma~\ref{fi:lem:restriction} and the norm comparisons, each
$\mathscr H_\iota$ has an approximant of rank at most $C(1+N)^M$ and
error at most $Cp^A\theta^N$, where $0<\theta<1$ and $C,M,A$
are independent of the label and $p$. The input and output norms
and $B_p$ are polynomially bounded, so replacing the designated
restrictions gives an operator $S_{p,N}$ with
\[
 \operatorname{rank}S_{p,N}\le CB_p(1+N)^M,\qquad
 \|S_p-S_{p,N}\|\le Cp^{A'}\theta^N.
\]
Choose $N=\lceil C_{\rm Tay}(1+\log p)\rceil$ with a sufficiently
large fixed $C_{\rm Tay}$. The error is less than $1/2$.
On the harmonic space, $\Pi_pS_{p,N}\Pi_p$ then differs from the
identity by norm less than $1/2$ and is injective. Its rank bounds
the dimension of that space, proving \eqref{fi:eq:localization}.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{fi:new:lem:fekete}
Let $D_{\rm ext}$ be a polydisc and let $K\Subset D_{\rm ext}$ be
compact with nonempty interior. Let $\mathcal M$ be a Hermitian
holomorphic-antiholomorphic kernel on $D_{\rm ext}\times D_{\rm ext}$,
with section space
\[
 \mathcal V=\operatorname{span}\{\mathcal M(\,\cdot\,,\bar w):w\in D_{\rm ext}\}.
\]
If $d=\dim\mathcal V\le m$ and $\sup_{K\times K}|\mathcal M|\le B$, there
are a holomorphic map $Z:D_{\rm ext}\to\mathbb C^d$ and a diagonal
signature matrix $\Sigma$ of size $d\times d$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{fi:new:eq:fekete}
 \mathcal M(z,\bar w)=Z(w)^*\Sigma Z(z),\qquad
 \sup_K|Z|\le C_m\sqrt B.
\end{equation}
Each component of $Z$ belongs to $\mathcal V$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
The assertion is immediate for $d=0$. Otherwise choose a basis of the
section space and nodes $z_1,\ldots,z_d\in K$ maximizing its absolute
evaluation determinant. The maximum is positive by the identity theorem.
Let $\ell=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_d)^T$ be the Lagrange basis, characterized
by $\ell_j(z_i)=\delta_{ij}$. Replacing one node in the determinant
gives $|\ell_j|\le1$ on $K$. Define the $d\times d$ Hermitian matrix
$A$ by $A_{ij}=\mathcal M(z_j,\bar z_i)$. Interpolation in both variables gives
\[
 \mathcal M(z,\bar w)=\ell(w)^*A\ell(z),\qquad
 \|A\|\le dB.
\]
The matrix $A$ has rank $d$. Choose a unitary $d\times d$ matrix
$U$ and a real diagonal matrix $\Lambda$ with $A=U^*\Lambda U$.
Put $\Sigma=\operatorname{sgn}\Lambda$ and
$Z=|\Lambda|^{1/2}U\ell$. Then
\[
 \mathcal M(z,\bar w)=Z(w)^*\Sigma Z(z),\qquad
 \sup_K|Z|\le\sqrt{dB}\sqrt d=d\sqrt B.
\]
Since $\ell_j\in\mathcal V$, every component of $Z$ is a finite constant
linear combination of kernel sections and is holomorphic on
$D_{\rm ext}$. The determinant is used only to choose the nodes; its size does not
enter the bound.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{fi:oa:parabolic}
Let $\phi$ be real and $C^5$ on a fixed neighborhood of $0$, with
locally constant Levi rank $m>0$ at $0$. In unitary coordinates
$z=(x,y)$, $x\in\mathbb C^m$, with $H_\phi(0)=\operatorname{diag}(A_0,0)$ and $A_0$
invertible, there are holomorphic polynomials $g_{t,\alpha}$ and
$Q_\alpha(u,v)=C_\alpha u+G_\alpha(v)\in\mathbb C^m$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{fi:oa:parabolic-error}
 \phi(t\alpha+t^2u,tv)
 =2\operatorname{Re}g_{t,\alpha}(u,v)
   +t^4Q_\alpha(u,v)^*\Sigma_\alpha Q_\alpha(u,v)+O(t^5).
\end{equation}
Here $C_\alpha$ is invertible, $\Sigma_\alpha$ is a signature matrix,
$\deg Q_\alpha\le2$, and $Q_\alpha(0)=0$.
The error and coefficient bounds are uniform for $0<t\le t_0$ in
terms of the fixed sets and the $C^5$ bound for $\phi$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
The argument uses the constant-rank hypothesis
only to identify finite Taylor coefficients at the origin.

\emph{Step 1: the limiting Levi form.}
Write the Levi matrix in the $x,y$ coordinates as below; its
$x,x$, $x,y$ and $y,y$ blocks $A,B,D$ have sizes
$m\times m$, $m\times(n-m)$ and $(n-m)\times(n-m)$:
\[
 H=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^*&D\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A subscript $j$ denotes the homogeneous Taylor term of real degree $j$. On the constant-rank germ the Schur complement vanishes.
Since $B_0=0$, comparison through degree two gives
\begin{equation}\label{fi:oa:Schur-jets}
 D=B^*A^{-1}B,\qquad D_0=D_1=0,\qquad
 D_2=B_1^*A_0^{-1}B_1.
\end{equation}
For $1\le a\le m$ and $1\le i,j\le n-m$, commutation of scalar
derivatives gives
\[
 \partial_{\bar y_i}B_{aj}(0)
 =\partial_{\bar x_a}D_{ij}(0)=0,\qquad
 \partial_{y_i}(B_1)_{aj}=\partial_{y_j}(B_1)_{ai}.
\]
Thus $\mathcal B_\alpha(v):=B_1(\alpha,v)$ is affine and holomorphic
in $v$, and its rows are closed one-forms. They have quadratic
primitives $\mathcal G_\alpha(0)=0$ with
$D_v\mathcal G_\alpha=\mathcal B_\alpha$. Explicitly, a row
$c_j+\sum_i c_{ji}v_i$, with $c_{ji}=c_{ij}$, has primitive
$\sum_jc_jv_j+\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}c_{ji}v_iv_j$.
All these coefficients are bounded by the given derivative bounds.

For $\Psi_{t,\alpha}(u,v)=(t\alpha+t^2u,tv)$, the chain rule and
\eqref{fi:oa:Schur-jets} yield, uniformly on fixed normalized boxes,
\begin{align}
 H_{t^{-4}\phi\circ\Psi_{t,\alpha}}
 &=\begin{pmatrix}
 A(\Psi_{t,\alpha})&t^{-1}B(\Psi_{t,\alpha})\\
 t^{-1}B(\Psi_{t,\alpha})^*&t^{-2}D(\Psi_{t,\alpha})
 \end{pmatrix}\longrightarrow L_\alpha:=
 \begin{pmatrix}A_0&\mathcal B_\alpha(v)\\
 \mathcal B_\alpha(v)^*&D_2(\alpha,v)\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{fi:oa:Levi-limit}
\end{align}
Indeed $B(\Psi)=tB_1(\alpha,v)+O(t^2)$ and
$D(\Psi)=t^2D_2(\alpha,v)+O(t^3)$.

\emph{Step 2: identify the mixed fourth-order polynomial.}
Expand the ordinary fourth Taylor polynomial as
\[
 (T_4\phi)\circ\Psi_{t,\alpha}
       =\sum_{j=0}^{8}t^jP_{j,\alpha}(u,v).
\]
Its coefficients are uniformly bounded. Because
$H_\phi-H_{T_4\phi}=O(|z|^3)$ and the largest factor in the scaled
pullback is $t^{-2}$, its Levi matrix has the same limit as
\eqref{fi:oa:Levi-limit}. Comparing powers of $t$ gives
\[
 H_{P_{j,\alpha}}=0\quad(j<4),\qquad H_{P_{4,\alpha}}=L_\alpha.
\]
By \eqref{fi:oa:Schur-jets}, the same limiting Levi form is obtained
from
\begin{equation}\label{fi:oa:qualitative-square}
 W_\alpha^*A_0W_\alpha,
 \qquad W_\alpha(u,v)=u+A_0^{-1}\mathcal G_\alpha(v).
\end{equation}
A polynomial with zero Levi matrix has no mixed monomials:
differentiating a monomial containing both $z$ and $\bar z$ once
in each type detects its coefficient, with no cancellation between
distinct monomials. A real such polynomial therefore equals
$2\operatorname{Re}h$ for a holomorphic polynomial $h$.
Since $W_\alpha(0)=0$, \eqref{fi:oa:qualitative-square} is entirely
mixed and equals the mixed part of $P_{4,\alpha}$.

\emph{Step 3: bounded holomorphic functions.}
Let $M_\alpha(z,\bar\zeta)$ be the polarization of the mixed part
of $P_{4,\alpha}$, where $z=(u,v)$. Its coefficients are Taylor
coefficients of $\phi$ at the center, combined polynomially with
$\alpha$; hence its norm on every fixed product is bounded. The
identity \eqref{fi:oa:qualitative-square} gives
\[
 M_\alpha(z,\bar\zeta)=W_\alpha(\zeta)^*A_0W_\alpha(z).
\]
This formula contains $A_0^{-1}$ and is used only qualitatively.
Its section space has dimension exactly $m$: the formula gives
the upper bound, and its $u$ Levi block $A_0$ gives the lower bound.
Lemma~\ref{fi:new:lem:fekete} therefore supplies a bounded holomorphic
column $Q_\alpha$ of length $m$ and a signature matrix $\Sigma_\alpha$.
Each component of $Q_\alpha$ is a
linear combination of kernel sections, which are polynomials of
degree at most two and affine in $u$. Thus
$Q_\alpha=C_\alpha u+G_\alpha(v)$; evaluation at the origin gives
$Q_\alpha(0)=0$. Cauchy estimates on a fixed polydisc inside the
Fekete compact bound every polynomial coefficient. Finally,
\[
 C_\alpha^*\Sigma_\alpha C_\alpha=A_0
\]
makes $C_\alpha$ invertible. The bound for $Q_\alpha$ comes from
$M_\alpha$, not from this last identity.

Finally, write $P_{j,\alpha}=2\operatorname{Re}h_{j,\alpha}$ for
$j<4$, and do the same for the pure part of $P_{4,\alpha}$.
Extracting pure coefficients is a bounded operation, and their
weighted sum defines $g_{t,\alpha}$. Terms with $j\ge5$ and the
ordinary scalar Taylor remainder are $O(t^5)$ on the whole normalized
box. This proves \eqref{fi:oa:parabolic-error} with all asserted bounds.

The constant-rank hypothesis entered only through the jet identities
\eqref{fi:oa:Schur-jets} at the origin. The working box need not lie
inside the constant-rank germ, and $t_0$ depends on the fixed
neighborhood carrying the $C^5$ bound, not on that germ.

Let $C$ be an algebraic complex cell of free dimension $d$ and of
the fixed complexity, and let
$F:C^\lambda\times P^+\to\Delta_M^{N_{\rm out}}$ be holomorphic.

 A(1+\log(1/\epsilon))^d

 \forall c\in C\quad\exists j,v\text{ in the inner core}:\quad
 \sup_{\omega\in P^-}|F(c,\omega)-H_j(v,\omega)|\le\epsilon.
\end{equation}
All $s$ parameters $\omega$ are retained. If $Q$ is holomorphic on a
larger ambient polydisc and

 Q(F(c,\omega))=\mathcal Q(c',\omega_I),

$\omega_I$ consists of $b$ parameter coordinates, there are

 Q(H_j(\xi))=q_j(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{a_j}),\qquad a_j\le a+b.

keeping all parameters $\omega$ throughout. Each recursive map is
obtained from $F$ by composition and removable extension, with the

the Euclidean norm.

\emph{Disc fibers.}
For a final disc $D(g(b))$, put $z=g(b)\zeta$. The available domain
contains $B^\lambda\times D(\lambda^{-1})$. Retain $\zeta$ as an additional
parameter and apply the induction to the base. If a prescribed
unit-core, doubled-disc convention is desired, cover the normalized
core disc by a fixed finite family of affine discs whose doubled
discs lie in $D(\lambda^{-1})$. This uses only the fixed extension
margin. For a punctured disc the bounded composed map extends jointly
across $\zeta=0$, by the Cauchy integral formula with parameters.
The polynomial or holomorphic identity involving $Q$ extends as well.
Thus the disc argument applies. Filling a puncture is permitted here
because the composed map takes values in the ambient polydisc;
its image need not lie in the unfilled cell.

\emph{The two ends of an annulus.}
For $A(a(b),g(b))$, normalize by $z=g(b)\zeta$ and put $h=a/g$. On the extended base, the extension convention gives
\begin{equation}\label{fi:cell:eq:annulusextension}
 0<|h|<1,\qquad \lambda|h|<|\zeta|<\lambda^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Choose fixed $e>0$, $\beta>1$ and $\lambda_1\in(\lambda,1)$ so that
\begin{equation}\label{fi:cell:eq:annulusconstants}
 1+2e<\lambda^{-1},\qquad

\end{equation}
These choices are possible by first taking $\lambda_1$ close to one
and $\beta$ close to one, and then taking $e$ sufficiently small.

$O(1+\log(1/\vartheta))$ radii $\rho\in[\vartheta,1]$, with a fixed ratio
close to one, and a fixed number of phases $\theta$ give affine discs
\begin{equation}\label{fi:cell:eq:affineend}

full enlarged $w$ disc and all previously retained parameter factors. Induction
applies to this map on the base, whose free dimension is at most
$d-1$.

For the inner end use $\xi=h/\zeta$ and the same affine discs for
$\vartheta\le|\xi|<1$. The reconstruction is
\[

\]
The identical base refinement works. Its bounds imply
$\lambda|h|<|\zeta|<\lambda^{-1}$ throughout the enlarged product.
Thus the inner end has the same count and the same induction.

\emph{The middle of an annulus.}
Here $|\zeta|<\vartheta$ and $|h|/|\zeta|<\vartheta$. For fixed $b,\omega$,
expand in a Laurent series.
Cauchy estimates on $|h|<|\zeta|<1$ give
\begin{gather*}
 F=c_0+\sum_{k\ge1}c_k\zeta^k+\sum_{k\ge1}c_{-k}\zeta^{-k},

 \quad(k\ge1,\ 1\le j\le N_{\rm out}).
\end{gather*}

\begin{equation}\label{fi:cell:eq:laurent}

\end{equation}

continuity and adds no variable. No dependence on other retained
parameters is introduced. Thus the count stays at most $a+b$.

\label{fi:it:lem:complexity}
In fixed dimensions, a fixed number of sums, products, holomorphic
quotients, compositions, derivatives and constant-node restrictions
of algebraic holomorphic branches preserves bounded graph complexity,
independently of all scalar constants used in these operations.

global branches. For sums, products, quotients and compositions, take their fiber
products with the polynomial equations of the relevant operations,
select the component containing the actual holomorphic graph, and
project away auxiliary variables. B\'ezout and algebraic projection
bound its degree in terms of the old bounds and fixed dimensions.
This also gives the composition bound used in
\cite[equation~(31)]{BN19}.

\emph{Derivatives.} Choose an irreducible minimal equation
$P(z,w)=0$ of a scalar branch. In characteristic zero $P_w$ is
generically nonzero. Adjoin
\[
 P_w(z,w)v+P_{z_i}(z,w)=0
\]
on that dense locus. Algebraic closure and projection give a
bounded-degree derivative relation. The actual derivative is
holomorphic on the whole larger domain, so its graph continues as
the chosen analytic branch across the exceptional locus. Repeat
for each fixed-order derivative. Analytic norm bounds come from
Cauchy estimates, without estimating $1/P_w$.

\emph{Exceptional restrictions.} For possibly exceptional frozen nodes, take nearby
generic constant-node slices of a degree-bounded polynomial graph
relation. Their nonzero specialized relations have the same degree
bound. Normalize their finite coefficient vectors to norm one and
pass to a convergent subsequence. The restricted holomorphic
branches converge locally uniformly, so the limiting nonzero
polynomial vanishes on the desired restricted graph. It cannot be
independent of the value variable, since it would then vanish on
an open base. The graph is an analytic component of that
bounded-degree zero set. In zero base dimension a constant graph
has degree one. Thus direct specialization need not stay nonzero;
the limiting argument supplies the required relation even at an
exceptional node.

This also applies to the finitely many constant-node evaluations
and linear combinations in Fekete interpolation. Complexity controls
degree; the separate analytic bounds below come from Taylor
coefficients, Fekete interpolation and Cauchy estimates.

for the defining maps and their fixed-order derivatives on the
reserved extensions. The same holds for ordinary cutoffs, affine
coordinate changes, their inverses and their Jacobians. Thus the
face and collation maps in the cited results have polynomial bounds.

\emph{Metric comparison.}
On a terminal outer cell, $|Q_{k,a}-q_{*,a}|\le CP_k^{-1/2}$
for every component $a$. Add to $\Gamma_k$ the holomorphic function
$P_k(q_*^*\Sigma_kQ_k-\tfrac12q_*^*\Sigma_kq_*)$.
The remaining scalar weight is exactly
\[
 P_k(Q_k-q_*)^*\Sigma_k(Q_k-q_*)+P_ktR^{t,(k)}_t+E_k.
\]
The first term is bounded in absolute value by $mC^2$, since the
Euclidean norm of $Q_k-q_*$ is at most
$\sqrt m\,CP_k^{-1/2}$. The second is bounded because $P_kt=1$,
and the third by \eqref{fi:it:eq:errorrecurrence}.
For a rank-zero center, Lemma~\ref{fi:it:lem:initial} gives the
bounded remainder directly, since $pt^{2k+5}=1$.
These are whole-domain bounds, including all retained variables,
and give \eqref{fi:eq:gaugebound}. The fixed $E$-metric and its
inverse are bounded on the atlas enlargement. Together with the
polynomial coordinate and Jacobian bounds and the Euclidean metric
in parameter directions, this proves
\eqref{fi:sc:normequivalence}. The coefficient multiplicity is
bounded by the fixed rank of $E$ and the bounded source dimensions.

\emph{Recursive construction.}
Terminal models of every required finite order follow from
\eqref{fi:sc:terminalmodel} and Lemma~\ref{fi:lem:restriction}.
Apply Proposition~\ref{fi:new:prop:analyticinduction} backwards
through the ordinary grids and relative covers. By
\eqref{fi:new:eq:budget}, an order-$q_{\max}$ parent receives
order-$(q_{\max}+1)$ children, whose homotopies are defined through degree
$q_{\max}+2$. Lemmas~\ref{fi:lem:composition} and~\ref{fi:it:lem:relative}
therefore apply on the full maximal Dolbeault domains in all source
variables, including retained variables and every parameter copy.
At a relative step the first copy is
$\xi_0=(b_0,V_0,W_0)$, and only $b_0$ is gridded. All other
coordinates remain source variables.
The induction reaches the atlas cover and gives
\eqref{fi:it:eq:assembledidentity}, with the asserted polynomial
bounds and full buffers. At each insertion,
\eqref{fi:new:eq:childsum} adds the child occurrences; the grid
counts above give \eqref{fi:it:eq:countrecurrence} and hence
\eqref{fi:it:eq:global}.

\label{ex:sharpness}
Let $0\le r\le n$ and let $Y$ be a connected compact complex manifold
of dimension $n-r$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-example}
 X=\mathbb P^r\times Y,\qquad
 L=\operatorname{pr}_1^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^r}(1).
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{pr}_1$ is projection to the first factor.
The pullback Fubini--Study metric has curvature rank $r$.
Fix $y_0\in Y$. Holomorphic functions on $Y$ are constant, so
restriction to $\mathbb P^r\times\{y_0\}$ is inverse to pullback on sections. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-growth}
 h^0(X,L^p)=h^0(\mathbb P^r,\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^r}(p))=\binom{p+r}{r}.
\end{equation}
The last equality counts homogeneous monomials in $r+1$ variables
\cite[Chapter~V, Theorem~15.5 and Corollary~15.6]{DemaillyCADG}.
For $r\ge1$ the dimension is $p^r/r!+O(p^{r-1})$; for $r=0$ it is
one. Hence the exponent in \eqref{eq:main-bound} cannot be reduced.

\label{ex:variable-rank}
Blow up $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ at a point and set
\[
 \pi:X\longrightarrow\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1,\qquad
 f=\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\pi,\qquad
 L=f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1).
\]
The induced metric has curvature $f^*\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$.
In coordinates centered at the blown-up point, the two charts are
\[
 \pi(u,v)=(uv,v),\quad f(u,v)=uv;
 \qquad
 \pi(s,t)=(s,st),\quad f(s,t)=s.
\]
Thus $f$ has exactly one critical point, represented by $(u,v)=(0,0)$,
where the curvature rank drops from one to zero. Along the punctured
axes,
\[
 \ker df_{(u,0)}=\mathbb C\partial_u,\qquad
 \ker df_{(0,v)}=\mathbb C\partial_v.
\]
Since $\ker\Theta_L=\ker df$, the kernel line has two different
limits at the critical point. Off that point the kernel is a complex
line, that is, a real two-plane. Hence a continuous field of real
planes of dimension at least two contained in the curvature kernel
would have dimension two and coincide with this line off the critical
point, which is impossible near it. The kernel therefore contains the
tangent spaces of no smooth foliation of codimension one, and this
metric fails Bouche's foliation hypothesis.

Nevertheless $h^0(X,L^p)=p+1$. Indeed, a section descends away from
the blown-up point and extends across it in a local frame by Hartogs'
theorem \cite[Chapter~I, Theorem~3.28]{DemaillyCADG}, giving the same
section space as on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$.

For fixed geometric data, $q$, and $s\ge1$, the quantifier in

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:vanishing}
Let $Y$ be a connected compact complex manifold, let $A$ be a
holomorphic line bundle, and fix $y_0\in Y$. There is $c>0$ such that
every nonzero $\sigma\in H^0(Y,A^p)$, $p\ge1$, satisfies
$\operatorname{ord}_{y_0}\sigma\le cp$.
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
Fix a smooth metric on $A$ and rescale $\sigma$ so that
$\sup_Y|\sigma|=1$.
On a finite fixed trivializing cover the local coefficients of
$\sigma$ are bounded by $e^{Cp}$, and changes of frame on compact
overlaps have the same bound. Fix a small coordinate ball $B$ at
$y_0$, and let $f$ be the coefficient of $\sigma$ in that frame.
For concentric balls in a trivializing chart, let $M(\rho)$ be the
supremum of the local coefficient on the ball of radius $\rho$.
If $0<\rho_1<\rho_2<\rho_3$, the three-circle theorem on complex
lines gives
\[
 M(\rho_2)\le M(\rho_1)^\theta M(\rho_3)^{1-\theta},
 \qquad \theta=\frac{\log(\rho_3/\rho_2)}{\log(\rho_3/\rho_1)}>0.
\]
Join $B$ to the centers of the finite cover by finitely many paths,
and subdivide each into overlapping coordinate balls. At each step
choose the next small ball inside the preceding intermediate ball.
The displayed inequality and the frame bounds propagate an upper
bound from $B$ to every member of the cover. Since this finite system
is independent of $p$ and $\sigma$, for some fixed $C,\theta_0>0$,
\[
 1\le e^{Cp}(\sup_B|f|)^{\theta_0}
 \quad\text{if }\sup_B|f|\le1.
\]
Taking the smallest of the finitely many products of exponents
justifies a common $\theta_0$. Thus $\sup_B|f|\ge e^{-C'p}$,
also when that supremum exceeds one.

Choose $B$ with twice its radius still in its trivializing chart.
If $f$ vanishes to order $v$ at $y_0$, Schwarz's lemma on radial
complex lines gives $\sup_B|f|\le 2^{-v}e^{C''p}$. Comparing the
two bounds proves $v\le cp$. Vanishing order is invariant under
a nonvanishing change of frame.

<!-- lemma-14: body_md -->
\label{lem:serre-siegel}
Let $A$ be a holomorphic line bundle on a connected compact complex
manifold $Y$. If $\kappa(A)\ge0$, then
\[
 h^0(Y,A^p)\le C p^{\kappa(A)}\qquad(p\ge1).
\]
Moreover, for some fixed integer $a\ge1$ and every $m\ge1$,
\[
 h^0(Y,A^{am})\ge\binom{m+\kappa(A)}{\kappa(A)}.
\]
If $\kappa(A)=-\infty$, all these spaces are zero.
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
Write $\kappa=\kappa(A)\ge0$. Choose a power $a$ and sections
$s_0,\ldots,s_\kappa$ whose ratios $f_i=s_i/s_0$ have independent
differentials at a point $y_0$ where $s_0\ne0$. Put
$f=(f_1,\ldots,f_\kappa)$ and complete it to product coordinates
$(f,w)$, with $w\in\mathbb C^{\dim Y-\kappa}$. Locally choose a
frame $e$ with $e^a=s_0$.
For $\sigma=e^pu\in H^0(Y,A^p)$, the functions
\[
 f_i=\frac{s_i s_0^{p-1}}{s_0^p},\qquad
 u^a=\frac{\sigma^a}{s_0^p}
\]
are ratios of sections of $A^{ap}$. By maximality of $\kappa$,
the map $(f,u^a)$ has rank at most $\kappa$. Its $w$ derivatives
therefore vanish. The same holds for $u$ on $\{u\ne0\}$ and hence
everywhere, so $u$ depends only on $f$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:vanishing} shows that the jet of $u$ in these
$\kappa$ coordinates through degree $\lfloor cp\rfloor$ determines
$\sigma$ injectively. Indeed, a zero jet gives vanishing order
greater than $cp$; a zero germ gives a zero global section by
connectedness. The coordinates and frame are independent of
$\sigma$, so this is a linear injection into a space of dimension
$\binom{\lfloor cp\rfloor+\kappa}{\kappa}=O(p^\kappa)$.

For the lower bound, the local submersion $f$ has open image.
Consequently no nonzero polynomial in its components vanishes, and
the sections
\[
 s_0^{m-|\alpha|}s_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots s_\kappa^{\alpha_\kappa},
 \qquad |\alpha|\le m,
\]
are linearly independent. Their number is
$\binom{m+\kappa}{\kappa}$. For $\kappa=0$ both arguments use a
one-dimensional space; the case $\kappa=-\infty$ is the definition.
