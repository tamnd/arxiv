<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:errrep}
Let Assumption~(A) hold, let $u$ be the solution of \eqref{poisson},
and let $u_h$ be its finite volume approximation, $-\Delta_h u_h = \mu_h$.
Then $e_h := P_h u - u_h$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:errrep}
a_h(e_h, w) = \sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}} \tau_e\, D_e w
\qquad\text{for all grid functions } w.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $\mathbf{x}_0\notin\partial K$ for all $K\in\mathcal{T}_h$, the solution $u$ is smooth in a neighborhood of each face,
and the divergence theorem together with $-\Delta u=\delta_{\mathbf{x}_0}$ gives the exact flux balance
$\sum_{e\subset\partial K}\Phi_{K,e}(u) = 1$ if $K=K_0$ and $=0$ otherwise.
The scheme reads $\sum_{e\subset\partial K}\Phi^h_{K,e}(u_h) = |K|\,(\mu_h)_K$, which is the same right-hand side.
Subtracting and using the linearity of $\Phi^h_{K,e}$ yields
$\sum_{e\subset\partial K}\Phi^h_{K,e}(e_h) = \sum_{e\subset\partial K}\tau_{K,e}$ for every $K$.
Multiplying by $w_K$, summing over $K$, and collecting the two contributions of each interior face gives \eqref{eq:errrep}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:consistency}
Let Assumption~(A) hold. Then there are constants $C$ and $h_0>0$ independent of $h$ such that for $h\le h_0$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau-bounds}
|\tau_e| \le
\begin{cases}
C, & \operatorname{dist}(e,\mathbf{x}_0) < 2h,\\
C\,h^{d+1}\operatorname{dist}(e,\mathbf{x}_0)^{-d-1}, & \operatorname{dist}(e,\mathbf{x}_0)\ge 2h,\ e\not\subset\partial\Omega,\\
C\,h^{d}, & e\subset\partial\Omega,
\end{cases}
\;\text{and hence}\;
\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}|\tau_e| \le C.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By odd reflection across the sides of $\Omega$, the solution $u$ is smooth on $\overline\Omega\setminus\{\mathbf{x}_0\}$,
and $|\nabla^k u(\mathbf{x})|\le C|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|^{2-d-k}$ for $k=1,2,3$.
Let $e$ be an interior face between $K$ and $L$ with $\mathbf{n}_{K,e}=\mathbf{e}_i$.
A direct computation shows that the discrete flux density is a weighted average of $\partial_i u$ over $Z_e:=K\cup L$,
\[
\frac{(P_hu)_L-(P_hu)_K}{h} = \frac{1}{|e|}\int_{e}\int_{-h}^{h}\phi_h(\sigma)\,\partial_i u(\mathbf{y}+\sigma\mathbf{e}_i)\,d\sigma\,ds(\mathbf{y}),
\qquad \phi_h(\sigma)=\frac{h-|\sigma|}{h^2},
\]
with the symmetric weight $\phi_h\ge0$, $\int_{-h}^h\phi_h\,d\sigma=1$, whereas the exact flux density is $|e|^{-1}\int_e\partial_iu\,ds$.
If $\operatorname{dist}(e,\mathbf{x}_0)\ge 2h$, then $|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|\ge\operatorname{dist}(e,\mathbf{x}_0)/2$ on $Z_e$;
a Taylor expansion in $\sigma$, in which the linear term vanishes by the symmetry of $\phi_h$, gives
$|\tau_e|\le C|e|\,h^2\sup_{Z_e}|\nabla^3u|\le Ch^{d+1}\operatorname{dist}(e,\mathbf{x}_0)^{-d-1}$.
If $\operatorname{dist}(e,\mathbf{x}_0)<2h$, then $e$ does not intersect the open cell $K_0$, so that
$\operatorname{dist}(e,\mathbf{x}_0)\ge\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x}_0,\partial K_0)\ge c_0h$ and
$|\Phi_{K,e}(u)|\le|e|\sup_e|\nabla u|\le Ch^{d-1}(c_0h)^{1-d}\le C$;
moreover, $Z_e\subset B_{5h}(\mathbf{x}_0)$ and $\phi_h\le h^{-1}$ give
$|\Phi^h_{K,e}(P_hu)|\le h^{-1}\int_{Z_e}|\nabla u|\,d\mathbf{x}\le Ch^{-1}\int_{B_{5h}(\mathbf{x}_0)}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|^{1-d}\,d\mathbf{x}\le C$.
For a boundary face $e\subset\partial K\cap\partial\Omega$, a Taylor expansion in the normal direction, using $u=0$ on $e$
and $d_e=h/2$, gives $|\tau_e|\le C|e|\,h\sup_K|\nabla^2u|\le Ch^d$, since $K$ stays away from $\mathbf{x}_0$ for $h\le h_0$.
Finally, there are at most $C$ faces with $\operatorname{dist}(e,\mathbf{x}_0)<2h$,
at most $Ck^{d-1}$ interior faces with $kh\le\operatorname{dist}(e,\mathbf{x}_0)<(k+1)h$, and at most $Ch^{1-d}$ boundary faces.
Hence $\sum_e|\tau_e|\le C\big(1+\sum_{k\ge2}k^{d-1}k^{-d-1}+h\big)\le C$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:dual}
Let Assumption~(A) hold, let $\psi$ be a grid function, and let $z_h$ be the solution of
$a_h(w,z_h) = (\psi,w)_h$ for all grid functions $w$, i.e., $-\Delta_h z_h=\psi$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dualstab}
\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}}|D_e z_h| \le C\,h\,\ell_h\,\|\psi\|_h,
\qquad
\ell_h :=
\begin{cases}
(1+|\log h|)^{1/2}, & d=2,\\
h^{-1/2}, & d=3,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $C$ independent of $h$ and $\psi$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Under Assumption~(A), the matrix of $-\Delta_h$ is the Kronecker sum
$\sum_{i=1}^d I\otimes\cdots\otimes T_i\otimes\cdots\otimes I$ of symmetric positive definite tridiagonal matrices $T_i$,
one for each coordinate direction.
Since these summands commute, expanding $\|\Delta_h z_h\|_h^2$ yields
$\|(I\otimes\cdots\otimes T_i\otimes\cdots\otimes I)z_h\|_h\le\|\Delta_h z_h\|_h=\|\psi\|_h$,
and similarly for the mixed terms involving $T_i^{1/2}\otimes T_j^{1/2}$, $i\neq j$.
In terms of difference quotients, this is a discrete $H^2$-regularity estimate:
all second difference quotients of $z_h$, including the mixed ones, are bounded by $C\|\psi\|_h$ in the discrete $L^2$ norm.
Consequently, each first difference quotient $\partial_{h,i}z_h$,
regarded as a grid function on the faces normal to the $i$-th coordinate direction,
has a discrete $H^1$ norm bounded by $C\|\psi\|_h$.
The discrete Sobolev inequality $\|v\|_{\infty}\le C(1+|\log h|)^{1/2}\|v\|_{1,h}$ in two dimensions \cite{Bramble1966},
and the discrete Sobolev inequality $\|v\|_{L^6_h}\le C\|v\|_{1,h}$ combined with the inverse estimate
$\|v\|_\infty\le h^{-1/2}\|v\|_{L^6_h}$ in three dimensions, give $\max|\partial_{h,i}z_h|\le C\ell_h\|\psi\|_h$.
Since $|D_e z_h| = h\,|\partial_{h,i}z_h|$ on the faces normal to the $i$-th direction
($h/2$ instead of $h$ on boundary faces), \eqref{eq:dualstab} follows.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:Poisson}
Let Assumption~(A) hold, let $u$ be the solution of \eqref{poisson},
and let $u_h$ be its finite volume approximation, identified with a piecewise constant function.
Then there are constants $C$ and $h_0>0$ independent of $h$ such that for all $h\le h_0$
\begin{alignat}{2}
\|u-u_h\|_{L^2(\Omega)} &\le C\,h\,|\log h|^{1/2}, &\qquad& d=2, \label{equ:ResultPois2D}\\
\|u-u_h\|_{L^2(\Omega)} &\le C\,h^{1/2}, && d=3. \label{equ:ResultPois3D}
\end{alignat}
The same bounds hold for the discrete error $\|P_hu-u_h\|_h$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We split $u-u_h = (u-P_hu) + e_h$ with $e_h = P_hu-u_h$, and note that
$h\le h\ell_h$, $h\ell_h = h^{1/2}$ for $d=3$, and $h\ell_h\le C h|\log h|^{1/2}$ for $d=2$ and $h\le h_0<1$.

\emph{Projection error.} For cells $K$ with $\operatorname{dist}(K,\mathbf{x}_0)\ge h$,
the Poincar\'e inequality on $K$ gives $\|u-(P_hu)_K\|_{L^2(K)}\le Ch\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(K)}$, and summation yields
\[
\sum_{\operatorname{dist}(K,\mathbf{x}_0)\ge h}\|u-(P_hu)_K\|_{L^2(K)}^2
\le C h^2\int_{\Omega\cap\{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|\ge h\}}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|^{2-2d}\,d\mathbf{x}
\le C h^2\ell_h^2 .
\]
For each of the boundedly many remaining cells, which lie in $B_{3h}(\mathbf{x}_0)$,
the scaling argument used for the first term in \eqref{eq:nearfield-split} gives
$\|u-(P_hu)_K\|_{L^2(K)}\le Ch$ for $d=2$, and
$\|u-(P_hu)_K\|_{L^2(K)}\le\|u\|_{L^2(K)}\le C\big\||\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|^{-1}\big\|_{L^2(B_{3h}(\mathbf{x}_0))}+Ch^{3/2}\le Ch^{1/2}$ for $d=3$.
Hence $\|u-P_hu\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le Ch\ell_h$.

\emph{Discrete error.} Let $z_h$ be the solution of the dual problem of Lemma~\ref{lem:dual} with $\psi=e_h$.
By Lemmas~\ref{lem:errrep}, \ref{lem:consistency} and~\ref{lem:dual},
\[
\|e_h\|_h^2 = a_h(e_h,z_h) = \sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\tau_e\,D_e z_h
\le \Big(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}|\tau_e|\Big)\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}}|D_e z_h|
\le C\,h\,\ell_h\,\|e_h\|_h .
\]
Hence $\|P_hu-u_h\|_h=\|e_h\|_h \le C h\ell_h$, and the triangle inequality completes the proof.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:nearfield}
Since $|K_0|^{1/2}|\overline{u}_{K_0}-u_{K_0}| = \|e_h\|_{L^2(K_0)}\le\|e_h\|_h$,
the proof yields $|K_0|^{1/2}|\overline{u}_{K_0}-u_{K_0}|\le Ch\ell_h$,
which is the bound for the second term in \eqref{eq:nearfield-split}
used in Subsections~\ref{subsec:Pois2D} and~\ref{subsec:Pois3D}.
For $d=2$, this means $|\overline{u}_{K_0}-u_{K_0}|\le C|\log h|^{1/2}$, whereas $\overline{u}_{K_0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}|\log h|+O(1)$:
the discrete solution reproduces the logarithmic growth of $u$ in the singular cell to leading order.
For $d=3$, the error on $K_0$ is bounded from below by $\|u-\overline{u}_{K_0}\|_{L^2(K_0)}\ge c\,h^{1/2}$,
so that the rate in \eqref{equ:ResultPois3D} cannot be improved.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:mechanism}
The proof makes the propagation of local errors explicit.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:consistency}, the far-field flux errors are summable,
while the finitely many faces near $\mathbf{x}_0$ carry flux errors of order one, which do not decrease under mesh refinement.
Through \eqref{eq:errrep}, these errors are propagated by the discrete dual solution, i.e., by the discrete Green's function,
and they enter the global error multiplied by the discrete gradients $D_ez_h$ near $\mathbf{x}_0$.
The size $h\ell_h$ of these gradients reflects that the dual solution is only in $H^2$,
which does not embed into $W^{1,\infty}$ for $d=2,3$.
This is the mechanism that reduces the convergence order from the nominal value $2$ to $2-d/2$
(up to a logarithmic factor for $d=2$), in analogy with the finite element result of Scott \cite{Scott1973},
who proved the $L^2$ error bound $O(h)$ for piecewise linear finite elements in two dimensions.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:extensions}
Assumption~(A) enters only through the summability of the flux errors and the dual stability \eqref{eq:dualstab}.
On general quasi-uniform admissible meshes, the two-point flux approximation is in general only first-order consistent
\cite{EymardGallouetHerbin2000}, so that the far-field flux errors are of order $h^d\operatorname{dist}(e,\mathbf{x}_0)^{-d}$
and their sum grows like $|\log h|$; provided a dual stability estimate like \eqref{eq:dualstab} is available,
the argument then yields the same rates up to an additional logarithmic factor.
Moreover, if the dual problem with data $\psi\in L^\infty(\Omega)$ satisfies the discrete $W^{1,\infty}$ estimate
$\max_e|D_ez_h|\le Ch\|\psi\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}$, which for the five- and seven-point schemes
is a consequence of discrete $W^{2,p}$-regularity with $p>d$,
the same duality argument in $L^1$ gives $\|P_hu-u_h\|_{L^1(\Omega)}\le Ch$.
Since $\|u-P_hu\|_{L^1(\Omega)}\le Ch\|\nabla u\|_{L^1(\Omega)}$ with $\nabla u\in L^1(\Omega)$,
this implies $\|u-u_h\|_{L^1(\Omega)}\le Ch$ for $d=2,3$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ ($d\ge2$) be a bounded domain and $\mathbf{x}_0\in\Omega$.
Consider
\begin{equation}
\partial_t u - \Delta u = \delta_{\mathbf{x}_0}\,\mathbf{1}_{t\ge t_0}
\quad \text{in } \Omega \times (0,T)
\qquad
u=0 \text{ on } \partial\Omega,
\qquad
u(0)=0.
\end{equation}

Let $\mathcal{T}_h$ be a quasi-uniform admissible finite volume mesh and let $K_0\in\mathcal{T}_h$ be the control volume containing $\mathbf{x}_0${                \color{black}   ;
for the estimate of the discretization error (Step~5 of the proof), we assume that $\Omega$, $\mathcal{T}_h$, and $\mathbf{x}_0$
satisfy Assumption~(A) of Subsection~\ref{subsec:PoisRigorous}}. Define the regularized source
\[
\mu_h := \frac{1}{|K_0|}\mathbf{1}_{K_0}.
\]

Let $u^{(h)}$ solve
\begin{equation}
\partial_t u^{(h)} - \Delta u^{(h)} = \mu_h\,\mathbf{1}_{t\ge t_0},
\qquad
u^{(h)}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad u^{(h)}(0)=0,
\end{equation}
and let $u_h(t)$ be the semidiscrete finite volume approximation{                \color{black}   ,
$u_h'-\Delta_hu_h=\mu_h\mathbf{1}_{t\ge t_0}$, $u_h(0)=0$, see Subsection~\ref{subsec:FVnotation}}.

Then for every $\tau>0$ there exists $C>0$ such that for all $t\in[t_0+\tau,T]$,
\begin{align} \label{equ:ResultL1L2}
\|u(t)-u_h(t)\|_{L^1(\Omega)} &\le C h,\\
\|u(t)-u_h(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)} &\le  \label{equ:ResultHeatPersistentSemi}
\begin{cases}
C h |\log h|^{1/2}, & d=2,\\
C h^{1/2}, & d=3.
\end{cases}
\end{align}

Moreover, for any compact $K\Subset \Omega\setminus\{\mathbf{x}_0\}$,
\[
\|u(t)-u_h(t)\|_{L^p(K)} \le C (h^r),
\]
where $r$ is the order of the underlying FV scheme for smooth solutions.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We split the error as
\[
u - u_h = (u - u^{(h)}) + (u^{(h)} - u_h) =: \eta_h + \xi_h.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: representation formula.}
Let $G_D(t, \mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$ be the Dirichlet heat kernel. Then
\[
u(\mathbf{x},t) = \int_{t_0}^t G_D(t-s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_0)\,ds,
\quad
u^{(h)}(\mathbf{x},t) = \int_{t_0}^t \frac{1}{|K_0|}\int_{K_0} G_D(t-s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\,d\mathbf{y}\,ds.
\]
Hence
\[
\eta_h(\mathbf{x},t)
= \int_{t_0}^t \left(
G_D(t-s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_0) - \frac{1}{|K_0|}\int_{K_0} G_D(t-s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\,d\mathbf{y}
\right) ds.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: pointwise estimate.}
{                \color{black}   For $|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|\ge 2h$,} by the mean value theorem in $\mathbf{y}$,
\[
|\eta_h(\mathbf{x},t)|
\le C h \int_{t_0}^t \sup_{\mathbf{y}\in K_0} |\nabla_\mathbf{y} G_D(t-s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\,ds.
\]
Using the classical estimate
\[
\int_0^\infty |\nabla_\mathbf{y} G_D(\tau,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\,d\tau \lesssim |\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|^{1-d},
\]
we obtain
\[
|\eta_h(\mathbf{x},t)| \lesssim h |\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|^{1-d}{                \color{black}   ,\qquad |\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|\ge 2h}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: $L^1$ estimate.}
Integrating in polar coordinates,
\[
\|\eta_h(t)\|_{L^1(\Omega)} \lesssim h \int_h^1 r^{1-d} r^{d-1} dr \lesssim h.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 4: $L^2$ estimate.}
Similarly,
\[
\|\eta_h(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2 \lesssim h^2 \int_h^1 r^{1-d} dr,
\]
which gives
\[
\|\eta_h(t)\|_{L^2} \lesssim 
\begin{cases}
h |\log h|^{1/2}, & d=2,\\
h^{1/2}, & d=3.
\end{cases}
\]
\begin{revblock}
Here the pointwise bound of Step~2 has been used for $|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|\ge 2h$ only.
In the remaining ball $B_{2h}(\mathbf{x}_0)$, we use that $\int_0^\infty G_D(\sigma,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\,d\sigma = G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$
is the Dirichlet Green's function of the Laplacian, so that Step~1 gives
$\eta_h(t) = b_h - E(t-t_0)b_h$ with $b_h(\mathbf{x}) := G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_0)-|K_0|^{-1}\int_{K_0}G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\,d\mathbf{y}$,
where $E(s)$ denotes the Dirichlet heat semigroup.
For $s=t-t_0\ge\tau$, we have $\|E(s)b_h\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}\le C(\tau)\|b_h\|_{L^1(\Omega)}\le C$,
and on $B_{2h}(\mathbf{x}_0)$ we have $|b_h(\mathbf{x})|\le C(1+|\log(|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|/h)|)$ for $d=2$
(the constant $\frac{1}{2\pi}|\log h|$ cancels in $b_h$, as in \eqref{eq:nearfield-split})
and $|b_h(\mathbf{x})|\le C(|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|^{-1}+h^{-1})$ for $d=3$.
Hence the near field contributes at most $Ch$ for $d=2$ and $Ch^{1/2}$ for $d=3$ to the $L^2$ norm,
and $O(h^2)$ to the $L^1$ norm, so that the bounds of Steps~3 and~4 hold on all of $\Omega$.
\end{revblock}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 5: discretization error.}
\begin{revblock}
The function $u^{(h)}$ solves a heat equation with a bounded source, but this bound degenerates:
$\|\mu_h\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}=|K_0|^{-1}\sim h^{-d}$, and the higher derivatives of $u^{(h)}$ blow up near $K_0$ as $h\to0$;
for instance, $\|u^{(h)}(t)\|_{H^2(\Omega)}\sim\|\mu_h\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\sim h^{-d/2}$.
Standard consistency estimates of the form $\|\xi_h(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le Ch\sup_{s\le t}\|u^{(h)}(s)\|_{H^2(\Omega)}$
would therefore only give the useless bound $O(h^{1-d/2})$.
We avoid derivatives of $u^{(h)}$ near $K_0$ altogether.
Since $\xi_h = (u-u_h)-\eta_h$ and $\eta_h$ has been estimated in Steps~3 and~4, it suffices to estimate $u-u_h$.

Let $u_\infty := G(\cdot,\mathbf{x}_0)$ be the solution of the Poisson problem \eqref{poisson},
and let $u_{\infty,h}$ be its finite volume approximation, $-\Delta_h u_{\infty,h}=\mu_h$;
recall from Subsection~\ref{subsec:FVnotation} that the finite volume source vectors of $\delta_{\mathbf{x}_0}$ and of $\mu_h$ coincide.
With the semigroup $E(s)$ of Step~4 and its semidiscrete counterpart $E_h(s):=e^{s\Delta_h}$,
Duhamel's formula gives for $s:=t-t_0\ge\tau$
\[
u(t) = u_\infty - E(s)u_\infty,
\qquad
u_h(t) = u_{\infty,h} - E_h(s)u_{\infty,h},
\]
and hence, with $v(t):=E(s)u_\infty$ and $\varepsilon_h(t):=P_hv(t)-E_h(s)u_{\infty,h}$,
\[
u(t)-u_h(t) = (u_\infty-u_{\infty,h}) - \big(v(t)-P_hv(t)\big) - \varepsilon_h(t).
\]

(a) The first term is the error of the stationary problem, $\|u_\infty-u_{\infty,h}\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le Ch\ell_h$
by Theorem~\ref{thm:Poisson}. This is the only place where the singularity at $\mathbf{x}_0$ enters,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:Poisson} controls it through the flux errors of order one at the finitely many faces near $\mathbf{x}_0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:consistency}) and the stability of the dual problem (Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}),
not through derivatives near $\mathbf{x}_0$.

(b) For $s\ge\tau$, $v(t)$ is smooth, and $\|v(t)-P_hv(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le Ch\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le C(\tau)h$.

(c) The function $\varepsilon_h$ starts from $\varepsilon_h(t_0)=P_hu_\infty-u_{\infty,h}$,
with $\|\varepsilon_h(t_0)\|_h\le Ch\ell_h$ by the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:Poisson},
and satisfies $\varepsilon_h'-\Delta_h\varepsilon_h=\rho_h$, where, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:errrep},
$(\rho_h(t),w)_h=\sum_{e}\tau_e(t)\,D_ew$ with the flux consistency errors $\tau_e(t)$ of $v(t)$.
The standard first-order flux consistency of the two-point scheme \cite{EymardGallouetHerbin2000} gives
$\|\rho_h(t)\|_{-1,h}\le Ch\,|v(t)|_{H^2(\Omega)}$, where $\|\cdot\|_{-1,h}$ is the norm dual to $|\cdot|_{1,h}$.
Since $u_\infty\in H^{1/4}(\Omega)$ (in fact, $u_\infty\in H^\beta(\Omega)$ for all $\beta<2-d/2$),
parabolic smoothing and $H^2$-regularity on the convex domain $\Omega$ yield
$|v(t)|_{H^2(\Omega)}\le C\|\Delta v(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le C (t-t_0)^{-7/8}\|u_\infty\|_{H^{1/4}(\Omega)}$.
Using that $E_h(s)$ is a contraction in $\|\cdot\|_h$ and the discrete smoothing property
$\|E_h(s)\rho\|_h\le Cs^{-1/2}\|\rho\|_{-1,h}$, which follows from the spectral representation of the symmetric operator $\Delta_h$,
Duhamel's formula gives
\[
\|\varepsilon_h(t)\|_h
\le \|\varepsilon_h(t_0)\|_h + Ch\int_0^{s}(s-\sigma)^{-1/2}\sigma^{-7/8}\,d\sigma
\le Ch\ell_h + C(\tau)h .
\]

Combining (a)--(c) shows $\|u(t)-u_h(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le Ch\ell_h$ for $t\in[t_0+\tau,T]$, and thus
$\|\xi_h(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le\|u(t)-u_h(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}+\|\eta_h(t)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le Ch\ell_h$,
which is the asserted order.
For the $L^1$ bound, the stationary errors in (a) and in $\varepsilon_h(t_0)$ are estimated in $L^1(\Omega)$
as in Remark~\ref{rem:extensions}, the propagation of $\varepsilon_h(t_0)$ is controlled since $E_h(s)$ is a contraction
in the discrete $L^1$ norm (the matrix of $-\Delta_h$ is an M-matrix, and the scheme is conservative),
and the remaining terms are bounded by $Ch$ in $L^2(\Omega)$ and hence in $L^1(\Omega)$.
\end{revblock}
Standard finite volume consistency yields optimal rates on compact subsets $K\Subset \Omega\setminus\{\mathbf{x}_0\}${                \color{black}   , which gives the local estimate}.

\medskip
Combining both terms gives the result.

Let $U_h(t)$ denote the semidiscrete solution. Then
\[
u(t_n)-u_h^n
=
(u-u^{(h)})(t_n)
+
(u^{(h)}(t_n)-U_h(t_n))
+

\]

\noindent\textbf{Step 1: source regularization.}
From the semidiscrete analysis,
\[
\|u(t_n)-u^{(h)}(t_n)\|_{L^1} \lesssim h,
\quad
\|u(t_n)-u^{(h)}(t_n)\|_{L^2}
\lesssim
\begin{cases}
h |\log h|^{1/2}, & d=2,\\
h^{1/2}, & d=3.
\end{cases}
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: spatial discretization.}
The term $u^{(h)}-U_h$ is estimated as in the semidiscrete case and does not exceed the same order.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: time discretization.}
Since $U_h$ solves a linear parabolic problem with {                \color{black}   a source that is constant for $t\ge t_0$}, standard backward Euler estimates give
\[
\|U_h(t_n)-u_h^n\| \le C \Delta t,
\]
uniformly for $t_n \ge t_0+\tau${                \color{black}   , with $C$ independent of $h$.
Indeed, as in Step~5 of the previous proof, $U_h(t)=u_{\infty,h}-E_h(t-t_0)u_{\infty,h}$, and
$u_h^n = u_{\infty,h}-r(-\Delta t\,\Delta_h)^{k}u_{\infty,h}$ with $r(\lambda)=(1+\lambda)^{-1}$,
where $k$ is the number of time steps since the source has been switched on.
The nonsmooth-data estimate $\sup_{\lambda\ge0}|r(\lambda)^k-e^{-k\lambda}|\le C/k$ for the backward Euler method \cite{Thomee2006}
gives $\|U_h(t_n)-u_h^n\|_h\le C\,\Delta t\,(t_n-t_0)^{-1}\|u_{\infty,h}\|_h$,
up to a shift of the switching-on time by at most one step, which causes an additional error of the same order.
Since $\|u_{\infty,h}\|_h\le\|P_hu_\infty\|_h+\|P_hu_\infty-u_{\infty,h}\|_h\le C$ uniformly in $h$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:Poisson},
the factor $h^{-d}$ in $\mu_h$ does not enter the constant}.
