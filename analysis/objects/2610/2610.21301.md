<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:grid}
Suppose that \(\boldsymbol g\) is continuously differentiable on a neighborhood of a convex region separated from the sensors and containing each source \(z_j\) and its nearest candidate \(\xi_j\), with \(\left\|D\boldsymbol g(z)\right\|_{\rm op}\leq C_1\) on that region. If \(|z_j-\xi_j|\leq h_\Gamma\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grid}
 \left\|\sum_ja_j\boldsymbol g(z_j)-\sum_ja_j\boldsymbol g(\xi_j)\right\|_2
 \leq C_1h_\Gamma\sum_ja_j.
\end{equation}
For a Cartesian grid covering the source region with coordinate spacing \(h\), \(h_\Gamma\leq\sqrt d\,h/2\).
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Set \(\gamma_j(s)=z_j+s(\xi_j-z_j)\), \(0\leq s\leq1\). Convexity keeps the segment in the region where the derivative bound holds. The fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 \boldsymbol g(\xi_j)-\boldsymbol g(z_j)=\int_0^1D\boldsymbol g(\gamma_j(s))(\xi_j-z_j)\,ds.
\]
The induced operator-norm bound implies \(\left\|\boldsymbol g(\xi_j)-\boldsymbol g(z_j)\right\|_2\leq C_1|\xi_j-z_j|\leq C_1h_\Gamma\). Multiply by \(a_j>0\), sum, and apply the triangle inequality. If several true sources use the same node, their amplitudes are added, so total strength is preserved. The farthest point in a Cartesian cell is at distance \(\sqrt d\,h/2\) from a nearest node.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:count}
Let the true distinct-source configuration have \(K_*\geq1\) positive atoms satisfying the fitting bounds, and put \(y=f_*+e+m\), where \(f_*\in\mathcal F_{K_*}\), \(\left\|e\right\|_2\leq\eta\), and \(\left\|m\right\|_2\leq b_{\rm mod}\). Here \(m\) denotes a deterministic forward-model discrepancy. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count_gap}
 \eta+b_{\rm mod}\leq\epsilon,
 \qquad d_{K_*-1}(f_*)>\epsilon+\eta+b_{\rm mod},
\end{equation}
then the smallest \(k\) satisfying \(d_k(y)\leq\epsilon\) is \(K_*\). If a converged attempted \(K_*\)-source fit attains residual at most \(\epsilon\), a sequential local-fit procedure with \(K_*\leq k_{\max}\) that tests every smaller size also returns \(K_*\) under these conditions.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Distance to a nonempty set is 1-Lipschitz, so
\(|d_k(y)-d_k(f_*)|\leq\eta+b_{\rm mod}\).
Nestedness implies \(d_k(f_*)\geq d_{K_*-1}(f_*)\) for \(k<K_*\); therefore every smaller model has residual greater than \(\epsilon\).
The true model has \(d_{K_*}(y)\leq\eta+b_{\rm mod}\leq\epsilon\).
An attempted fit cannot have a residual smaller than its global model distance. The additional local-fit assumption thus suffices for the sequential procedure.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:single}
For \(0<t_1<t_2\), \(H_d(r,t_1)/H_d(r,t_2)\) is strictly decreasing for \(r>0\). Exact readings at these two times at \(d+1\) affinely independent sensors therefore determine the location and strength of a single positive source uniquely, provided it does not coincide with a sensor.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Set \(x=r^2/(4\kappa)>0\) and \(p=d/2-2\). Changing variables \(v=1/s\) gives the ratio
\[
 Q(x)=\frac{\int_{1/t_1}^\infty v^pe^{-xv}\,dv}{\int_{1/t_2}^\infty v^pe^{-xv}\,dv}.
\]
On \([1/t_2,\infty)\), normalize \(v^pe^{-xv}\) to a probability density and let \(V\) have that density. The threshold is \(\tau=1/t_1>1/t_2\) and \(Q(x)=P_x(V\geq\tau)\). Differentiating the numerator and denominator gives \(Q'(x)=-\operatorname{Cov}_x(V,\boldsymbol1_{\{V\geq\tau\}})\). If \(p_+=P_x(V\geq\tau)\), this covariance equals
\[
 p_+(1-p_+)\{E_x[V\mid V\geq\tau]-E_x[V\mid V<\tau]\}>0.
\]
Here \(P_x\), \(E_x\), and \(\operatorname{Cov}_x\) denote probability, expectation, and covariance under this normalized density. Both conditional events have positive probability, and all required moments are finite for \(x>0\). Since \(x\) increases with \(r\), the ratio is strictly decreasing in \(r\).

At sensor \(i\), the reading ratio cancels the strength and determines \(r_i=|b_i-z|\). Subtracting \(|b_1-z|^2=r_1^2\) from \(|b_i-z|^2=r_i^2\) yields
\[
 2(b_i-b_1)^Tz=|b_i|^2-|b_1|^2-r_i^2+r_1^2,\qquad i=2,\ldots,d+1.
\]
Affine independence makes this system invertible. A nonzero reading then determines the strength.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:geometry}
If \(\boldsymbol g\) is \(C^3\) near \(z\) and nonzero there, then for a unit direction \(v\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
 1-\rho(z,z+\delta v)=\frac{\delta^2}{2}v^TG(z)v+O(\delta^3),
 \qquad
 Dq=\frac{(I-qq^T)D\boldsymbol g}{\left\|\boldsymbol g\right\|_2}.
\end{equation}
The remainder is uniform on compact source regions with bounded derivatives and \(\left\|\boldsymbol g\right\|_2\) bounded away from zero.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Differentiation of \(q^Tq=1\) gives \(q^TDq\,v=0\) and \(q^TD^2q[v,v]=-\left\|Dq\,v\right\|_2^2\). Taylor expansion of \(q(z+\delta v)\) and inner product with \(q(z)\) yield the result. Direct differentiation of \(\boldsymbol g/\left\|\boldsymbol g\right\|_2\) gives the derivative identity. Standard uniform Taylor bounds give the compact-region statement.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:profile}
Suppose that \(\boldsymbol g\) is continuously differentiable near an interior
parameter point \((z,a)\), with \(a>0\) and \(\boldsymbol g(z)\ne0\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile}
 \mathcal I_z(z,a)
 =\frac{a^2}{\sigma^2}
   D\boldsymbol g(z)^T(I-q(z)q(z)^T)D\boldsymbol g(z)
 =\frac{a^2\left\|\boldsymbol g(z)\right\|_2^2}{\sigma^2}G(z).
\end{equation}
For every \(v\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 v^T\mathcal I_z(z,a)v
 =\frac{1}{\sigma^2}
  \min_{s\in\mathbb R}
  \left\|aD\boldsymbol g(z)v+s\boldsymbol g(z)\right\|_2^2.
\]
Moreover, \(\mathcal I_z(z,a)\) is positive definite if and only if
\(J_1(z,a)\) has full column rank.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The blocks of the joint information matrix are
\[
 \mathcal I_{zz}=\frac{a^2}{\sigma^2}D\boldsymbol g^TD\boldsymbol g,\qquad
 \mathcal I_{za}=\frac{a}{\sigma^2}D\boldsymbol g^T\boldsymbol g,\qquad
 \mathcal I_{aa}=\frac{\left\|\boldsymbol g\right\|_2^2}{\sigma^2},
\]
where all responses and derivatives are evaluated at \(z\).
Substitution into the Schur complement yields the first equality
in \eqref{eq:profile}. The derivative identity in \eqref{eq:rho}
yields the second.

For any \(v\), the minimizing strength perturbation is
\[
 s_*=-\frac{a\boldsymbol g^TD\boldsymbol g\,v}{\left\|\boldsymbol g\right\|_2^2}.
\]
Substituting \(s_*\) proves the variational formula.
Its value vanishes for a nonzero \(v\) exactly when
\(aD\boldsymbol g\,v+s\boldsymbol g=0\) for some \(s\).
Since \(\boldsymbol g\ne0\), every nonzero vector in the null space of
\(J_1\) has a nonzero position component.
Thus \(\mathcal I_z\) is positive definite exactly when
\(J_1\) has full column rank.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:merge}
Suppose that \(\boldsymbol g\) is \(C^2\) on a convex neighborhood containing the cluster and separated from the sensors, and that \(\left\|D^2\boldsymbol g(z)[v,w]\right\|_2\leq C_2|v||w|\) there for all \(v,w\in\mathbb R^d\). Then

 \left\|\sum_ja_j\boldsymbol g(z_j)-A_c\boldsymbol g(c)\right\|_2
 \leq\frac{C_2}{2}\sum_ja_j|z_j-c|^2.
\end{equation}
For a pair separated by \(\delta\), the right-hand side is \(C_2a_1a_2\delta^2/(2A_c)\).
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
With \(v_j=z_j-c\), Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives
\[
 \boldsymbol g(z_j)=\boldsymbol g(c)+D\boldsymbol g(c)v_j+
 \int_0^1(1-s)D^2\boldsymbol g(c+sv_j)[v_j,v_j]\,ds.
\]
The first-order sum vanishes because \(\sum_ja_jv_j=0\). Bounding the remaining integrals proves the inequality. For a pair, direct substitution of its weighted center gives \(\sum_ja_j|z_j-c|^2=a_1a_2\delta^2/A_c\).

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:curvature}
Suppose that \(\theta_c\) is interior to the one-source fitting
region, that \(\boldsymbol g\) is \(C^4\) near \(c\), and that \(J_c\) has
full column rank. For all sufficiently small \(\delta\geq0\),
there is a strict local one-source least-squares minimizer
\(\theta_\delta\) near \(\theta_c\), with \(\theta_\delta\to\theta_c\),
whose residual norm satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curvature}
 R_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(\delta)
 :=\left\|f(\theta_\delta)-y_\delta\right\|_2
 =C\delta^2+O(\delta^3).
\end{equation}
For equal strengths, the remainder is \(O(\delta^4)\).
If \(C>0\), the leading residual along this branch is quadratic.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Cancellation of the first spatial moment gives the pair data
\[
 y_\delta=f(\theta_c)+\delta^2\boldsymbol d_2+O(\delta^3).
\]
The stationary equation is
\[
 Df(\theta)^T\{f(\theta)-y_\delta\}=0.
\]
At \((\theta,\delta)=(\theta_c,0)\), its derivative with respect
to \(\theta\) is \(J_c^TJ_c\), which is positive definite.
The implicit function theorem yields a nearby stationary branch
\(\theta_\delta\). By continuity of the least-squares Hessian,
this branch consists of strict local minimizers for sufficiently
small \(\delta\). Expansion of the stationary equation gives
\[
 \theta_\delta
 =\theta_c+\delta^2(J_c^TJ_c)^{-1}J_c^T\boldsymbol d_2
 +O(\delta^3).
\]
Substitution yields
\[
 f(\theta_\delta)-y_\delta
 =-\delta^2P_c^\perp\boldsymbol d_2+O(\delta^3).
\]
Taking norms proves \eqref{eq:curvature}.
For equal strengths, the pair data satisfy
\[
 y_\delta=f(\theta_c)+\delta^2\boldsymbol d_2+O(\delta^4).
\]
Since \(\theta_\delta-\theta_c=O(\delta^2)\), the same
stationary-equation calculation improves both the parameter
and residual remainders to \(O(\delta^4)\).

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:cubic}
Let \(z_1=c_{\rm mid}-\delta v/2\), \(z_2=c_{\rm mid}+\delta v/2\), with
\(\delta>0\), \(|v|=1\), and \(a_1,a_2>0\).
Suppose that \(\boldsymbol g\) is \(C^3\) on a neighborhood of the segment

\]
For the two-source parameter
\(\theta=(z_1,a_1,z_2,a_2)\), use \(J_2(\theta)\) from \eqref{eq:physical_jacobian} and define

 \left(\frac{\delta v}{2a_1},1,

\]
Then, writing \(J_2=J_2(\theta)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic}
 \inf_{|w|=1}\left\|J_2w\right\|_2
 \leq\frac{\left\|J_2w_\delta\right\|_2}{|w_\delta|}
 \leq\frac{C_3\delta^3}{12|w_\delta|}

 =\frac12\int_{-h}^{h}(h^2-s^2)\varphi^{(3)}(s)\,ds.

 \leq\frac{C_3}{2}\int_{-h}^{h}(h^2-s^2)\,ds

<!-- proposition-9: body_md -->
\label{prop:aggregate}
Let \(\theta_c=(c,A_c)\) be the aggregate parameter of a positive
cluster, and suppose that
\[
 s_c=\inf_{|w|=1}\left\|J_cw\right\|_2>0.
\]
Suppose that \(f\) is continuously differentiable on an open
convex neighborhood \(U\) of \(\theta_c\), contained in the
interior of the admissible parameter region, and that
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in U}
 \left\|Df(\theta)-Df(\theta_c)\right\|_{\rm op}
 \leq s_c/2.
\]
Let
\[
 y=\sum_j a_j\boldsymbol g(z_j)+e,\qquad \left\|e\right\|_2\leq\eta,
\]
and assume the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:merge}.
If a fitted one-source parameter \(\widehat\theta\in U\)
satisfies
\(\left\|f(\widehat\theta)-y\right\|_2\leq\epsilon\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aggregate_stability}
 |\widehat\theta-\theta_c|
 \leq\frac{2}{s_c}
 \left(
  \epsilon+\eta
  +\frac{C_2}{2}\sum_j a_j|z_j-c|^2
 \right).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-9 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Write \(h=\widehat\theta-\theta_c\).
Since \(U\) is convex,
\[
 f(\widehat\theta)-f(\theta_c)
 =Df(\theta_c)h
  +\int_0^1
   \{Df(\theta_c+th)-Df(\theta_c)\}h\,dt.
\]

\[
 \left\|f(\widehat\theta)-f(\theta_c)\right\|_2
 \geq s_c|h|-\frac{s_c}{2}|h|
 =\frac{s_c}{2}|h|.
\]
On the other hand, the triangle inequality and
Proposition~\ref{prop:merge} give
\[
 \left\|f(\widehat\theta)-f(\theta_c)\right\|_2
 \leq\epsilon+\eta
     +\frac{C_2}{2}\sum_j a_j|z_j-c|^2.
\]
Combining these inequalities proves
\eqref{eq:aggregate_stability}.

<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{prop:prob}
Let \(M>d+1\) and retain the assumptions and fixed quantities above. Suppose that \(\delta\to0\) and \(\sigma\to0\), with
\[
 \frac{\delta^2}{\sigma}\to\ell\in[0,\infty).
\]
For
\[
 y=a_1\boldsymbol g(z_1)+a_2\boldsymbol g(z_2)+\sigma Z,
 \qquad Z\sim N(0,I_M),
\]
let \(\widehat\theta\) be the local one-source least-squares fit
on the branch near \(\theta_c\).
Writing \(\chi^2_r(\lambda)\) for a noncentral chi-square random
variable with \(r\) degrees of freedom and noncentrality
\(\lambda\), we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ncx}
 \frac{\left\|f(\widehat\theta)-y\right\|_2^2}{\sigma^2}
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 \chi^2_{M-d-1}(\ell^2C^2).
\end{equation}
Here \(\Longrightarrow\) denotes convergence in distribution.
Consequently,
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{
 \left\|f(\widehat\theta)-y\right\|_2^2>\sigma^2T_M
 \right\}
 \longrightarrow
 \Pr\!\left\{
 \chi^2_{M-d-1}(\ell^2C^2)>T_M
 \right\}.
\]
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Set \(y_0=f(\theta_c)\).
The stationary equation for a one-source fit is
\[
 Df(\theta)^T\{f(\theta)-y\}=0.
\]
At \((\theta,y)=(\theta_c,y_0)\), its derivative with respect
to \(\theta\) is \(J_c^TJ_c\), which is positive definite.
The implicit function theorem therefore defines a twice continuously differentiable
local parameter map \(\widehat\theta(y)\).
By continuity of the least-squares Hessian, this map gives
strict local minimizers for \(y\) sufficiently close to \(y_0\).

The corresponding residual map
\[
 \boldsymbol r(y)=f(\widehat\theta(y))-y
\]
satisfies
\[
 \boldsymbol r(y_0)=0,\qquad D\boldsymbol r(y_0)=-P_c^\perp.
\]
The pair-response expansion gives
\[
 \boldsymbol u_\delta:=y-y_0
 =\delta^2\boldsymbol d_2+\sigma Z+O(\delta^3),
\]
and hence
\[
 \boldsymbol r(y)=-P_c^\perp \boldsymbol u_\delta+O(\left\|\boldsymbol u_\delta\right\|_2^2).
\]
Since \(\delta^2/\sigma\) is bounded,
\[
 \left\|\boldsymbol u_\delta\right\|_2
 =O_p(\delta^2+\sigma)
 =O_p(\sigma).
\]
Also, \(\delta^3/\sigma\to0\). Thus
\[
 \frac{\boldsymbol r(y)}{\sigma}
 =-P_c^\perp\left(
 Z+\frac{\delta^2}{\sigma}\boldsymbol d_2
 \right)+o_p(1)
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 -P_c^\perp(Z+\ell\boldsymbol d_2).
\]
Here \(O_p\) denotes boundedness in probability after scaling,
and \(o_p(1)\) denotes convergence to zero in probability.
The local fit is defined on a fixed neighborhood reached with
probability tending to one; any extension outside that
neighborhood leaves the limit unchanged.

The matrix \(P_c^\perp\) is an orthogonal projector of rank
\(M-d-1\). Therefore, the squared norm of the limiting vector
has a noncentral chi-square distribution with noncentrality
\[
 \ell^2\left\|P_c^\perp\boldsymbol d_2\right\|_2^2=\ell^2C^2.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:ncx}.
The limiting distribution is continuous at \(T_M\), which
also proves convergence of the rejection probability.

<!-- proposition-11: body_md -->
\label{prop:scale}
Retain the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:prob} and
suppose that \(C>0\).
Choose
\[
 \pi\in
 \left(
 \Pr\{\chi^2_{M-d-1}(0)>T_M\},\,1
 \right).
\]
Let \(\lambda_\pi>0\) be the unique solution of
\[
 \Pr\{\chi^2_{M-d-1}(\lambda_\pi)>T_M\}=\pi.
\]
Define the local reference separation by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scale}
 \delta_\pi(\sigma)
 =\left(\frac{\lambda_\pi\sigma^2}{C^2}\right)^{1/4}.
\end{equation}
For \(\delta=\delta_\pi(\sigma)\), the probability of rejecting
the local one-source fit tends to \(\pi\) as \(\sigma\to0\).
<!-- end proposition-11 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
At a fixed positive threshold, the noncentral chi-square
upper-tail probability is continuous and strictly increasing
in the noncentrality parameter, from its central value to one.
Thus \(\lambda_\pi\) exists and is unique.
Equation~\eqref{eq:scale} gives
\[
 \frac{\delta_\pi(\sigma)^2}{\sigma}
 =\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_\pi}}{C}.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:prob} then gives the stated limit.
