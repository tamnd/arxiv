<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
The converter parameters $L$ and $R_L$ are constant but unknown, with
$L>0$ and $R_L\geq0$.
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- assumption-2: body_md -->
The reference $I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ is continuously differentiable, and
both $I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ and $\dot I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ are bounded.
<!-- end assumption-2 -->
<!-- assumption-3: body_md -->
The averaged converter model \eqref{eq:UD} is valid and the commanded
converter voltage $U_D$ can be applied without actuator saturation. The arc
voltage $U_{\mathrm{arc}}$ used for feedforward compensation is assumed to be
measured without error or delay.
<!-- end assumption-3 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Consider the closed-loop error system
\eqref{eq:error_dynamics} under Assumptions~1--3. Then the following
properties hold:

\begin{enumerate}

\item For any initial condition, the error signals
$e_I(t)$, $e_{R_L}(t)$, and $e_L(t)$ remain bounded for all $t\geq0$.

\item The current tracking error is square integrable and converges
asymptotically to zero, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
\int_0^\infty e_I^2(t)\,\mathrm{d}t < \infty,
\qquad
\lim_{t\to\infty} e_I(t)=0.
\label{eq:tracking_convergence}
\end{equation}

\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Consider the Lyapunov candidate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:V_candidate}
V
=
\frac{1}{2}L e_I^2
+
\frac{1}{2\gamma_R}e_{R_L}^2
+
\frac{1}{2\gamma_L}e_L^2,
\end{equation}
with $\gamma_R>0$ and $\gamma_L>0$. Since $L>0$, the function $V$
is positive definite and radially unbounded with respect to the error vector
\(
e:=\begin{bmatrix}e_I & e_{R_L} & e_L\end{bmatrix}^{\top}.
\)

Differentiating $V$ along the trajectories of the closed-loop error system
\eqref{eq:error_dynamics} gives
\begin{align}
\dot V
&=
L e_I\dot e_I
+
\frac{1}{\gamma_R}e_{R_L}\dot e_{R_L}
+
\frac{1}{\gamma_L}e_L\dot e_L
\nonumber\\
&=
L e_I
\left(
-\frac{K_e}{L}e_I
+
\frac{I_L}{L}e_{R_L}
+
\frac{\dot I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}}{L}e_L
\right)
\nonumber\\
&\quad
-
e_{R_L}I_L e_I
-
e_L\dot I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}e_I
\nonumber\\
&=
-K_e e_I^2
\leq 0.
\label{eq:Vdot_negative}
\end{align}

Hence, $V(t)$ is non-increasing and satisfies
$V(t)\leq V(0)$ for all $t\geq0$. Since $V$ is positive definite and
radially unbounded with respect to the error vector, the error signals
$e_I(t)$, $e_{R_L}(t)$, and $e_L(t)$ remain bounded for all $t\geq0$,
which proves statement~(i).

Furthermore, integration of \eqref{eq:Vdot_negative} over $[0,T]$ yields
\begin{equation}
V(T)-V(0)
=
-\int_0^T K_e e_I^2(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.
\end{equation}
Since $V(T)\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
\int_0^\infty e_I^2(t)\,\mathrm{d}t
\leq
\frac{V(0)}{K_e}
<\infty,
\label{eq:e_square_integrable}
\end{equation}
and hence the tracking error is square integrable.

It remains to establish asymptotic convergence. Since
$I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}$ and $e_I$ are bounded, the relation
\(
I_L=I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}-e_I
\)
implies that $I_L$ is bounded. Together with boundedness of
$e_{R_L}$, $e_L$, and $\dot I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}$, the closed-loop error
dynamics imply that $\dot e_I$ is bounded. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\ddot V
=
-2K_e e_I\dot e_I
\label{eq:Vddot}
\end{equation}
is bounded, and thus $\dot V$ is uniformly continuous.

Since $V(t)$ is non-increasing and bounded from below, it converges to a
finite limit. Hence, by Barbalat's lemma \citep[Lemma~8.2]{khalil2002},
\(
\lim_{t\to\infty}\dot V(t)=0.
\)
Using \eqref{eq:Vdot_negative} and $K_e>0$ then yields
\(
\lim_{t\to\infty}e_I(t)=0,
\)
which proves statement~(ii).
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:RL_convergence}

Assume that the current reference is constant and nonzero,
\begin{equation}
I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}(t)\equiv \bar{I}_{L,\mathrm{ref}}\neq0,
\qquad
\dot I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}(t)\equiv0.
\end{equation}
Under the conditions of the preceding theorem, the resistance estimation error
converges asymptotically to zero, and hence
\begin{equation}
\lim_{t\to\infty}e_{R_L}(t)=0,
\qquad
\lim_{t\to\infty}\hat R_L(t)=R_L.
\end{equation}
Moreover, since $\dot I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}=0$, the inductance adaptation law
satisfies $
\dot{\hat L}=0,$
so that $\hat L$ remains constant during operation at a constant current
reference.\footnote{Convergence of the inductance estimate to its true value
is not addressed in the present work. In principle, parameter convergence can
be obtained under additional excitation conditions, such as persistent
excitation. This would require sufficiently persistent excitation of $I_L$ and $\dot I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}$. The design of such excitation signals is outside
the scope of this work.}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For $\dot I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}(t)\equiv0$, the closed-loop error dynamics
\eqref{eq:error_dynamics} reduce to
\begin{align*}
\dot e_I
&=
-\frac{K_e}{L}e_I
+
\frac{I_L}{L}e_{R_L},\\
\dot e_{R_L}
&=
-\gamma_R I_L e_I,\\
\dot e_L
&=0.
\end{align*}
Since
\(
I_L=\bar I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}-e_I,
\)
the resulting error system is autonomous. Consider the positive definite and radially unbounded Lyapunov function
\[
V
=
\frac{1}{2}L e_I^2
+
\frac{1}{2\gamma_R}e_{R_L}^2
+
\frac{1}{2\gamma_L}e_L^2
\]
in $(e_I,e_{R_L},e_L)$. Differentiating $V$ along the reduced error
dynamics yields
\[
\dot V
=
-K_e e_I^2
\leq0.
\]
Hence, all trajectories are bounded and approach the largest invariant set
contained in
\[
\mathcal S
:=
\{(e_I,e_{R_L},e_L):\dot V=0\}
=
\{(e_I,e_{R_L},e_L):e_I=0\}.
\]
On $\mathcal S$, $e_I=0$, which implies
$I_L=\bar I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}$. Substituting this into the current-error
dynamics gives
\[
\dot e_I
=
\frac{\bar I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}}{L}\,e_{R_L}.
\]

For a trajectory to remain in $\mathcal S$, it must satisfy
$\dot e_I=0$. Since $\bar I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}\neq0$ and $L>0$,
this requires $e_{R_L}=0$. Hence, the largest invariant subset of
$\mathcal S$ is
\[
\mathcal M
=
\{(e_I,e_{R_L},e_L):e_I=0,\ e_{R_L}=0\},
\]
where $e_L$ may take any constant value.
By LaSalle's invariance principle \citep{khalil2002}, every trajectory
converges to $\mathcal M$. Consequently,
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}e_I(t)=0,
\qquad
\lim_{t\to\infty}e_{R_L}(t)=0.
\]
Since $e_{R_L}=R_L-\hat R_L$, it follows that
\(
\lim_{t\to\infty}\hat R_L(t)=R_L.
\)
Finally, $\dot I_{L,\mathrm{ref}}=0$ implies from
\eqref{eq:L_adaptation} that
\(
\dot{\hat L}=0,
\)
and therefore $\hat L(t)\equiv\hat L(0)$.
