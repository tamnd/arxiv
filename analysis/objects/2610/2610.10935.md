\label{thm:main}
Let $a\colon\mathbb{R}^d\times\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ be a  Borel function such that there exist $0<\alpha\leq\beta$ for which $\alpha\leq a(x,y)\leq\beta$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^d$, and $1$-periodic; that is, $a(x+k,y+j)= a(x,y)$ for all $k,j\in\mathbb Z^d$. Let $\varepsilon>0$ and $s\coloneqq s(\varepsilon)\in(0,1)$. Let
\begin{equation}\label{intro:fe}
F_\varepsilon(u)\coloneqq s\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}a\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^2}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy
\end{equation}
be defined in $L^2(\mathbb{R}^d)$. If $\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}s=0$ and
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\varepsilon^{-2s}=\lambda\in[1,+\infty],
\end{equation*}
then
\begin{equation*}
\Gamma\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}F_\varepsilon(u)=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}\,a_\lambda\|u\|^2_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^d)},
\end{equation*}
for all $u\in L^2(\mathbb{R}^d)$, where the $\Gamma$-limit is computed with respect to the sequential weak topology of $L^2(\mathbb{R}^d)$, $a_\lambda$ is given by 
\begin{eqnarray}\label{eq:a-lambda-0}\nonumber
&&\hskip-1.3cm a_\lambda\coloneqq\inf\bigg\{\int_{Q}\int_{Q}(a(x,y)+a(y,x))|1+\varphi(x)|^2\,dx\,dy\\&&\ \quad+(\lambda-1)\int_{Q}\int_{Q} a(x,y)|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^2\,dx\,dy:\varphi\in L^2(Q)\text{                  
and }\int_Q\varphi(x)\,dx=0\bigg\}
\end{eqnarray}
if $\lambda<+\infty$, while
\begin{eqnarray}\label{eq:a-lambda-infty}
a_{+\infty}\coloneqq\ 2\int_{Q}\int_{Q}a(x,y)dx\,dy,
\end{eqnarray}
where $Q=(0,1)^d$ is the unit cube in $\mathbb{R}^d$, and $\sigma_{d-1}$ is the $(d-1)$-Hausdorff measure of the unit sphere ${\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:PiniSantilli}
In logarithmic scale $t=\log|x-y|$, the measure $s|h|^{-d-2s}\,dh$ becomes $\sigma_{d-1}s\,e^{-2st}\,dt$, which is asymptotically uniform as $s\to0^+$. Hence, the scales $t>0$, responsible for the interactions with infinity, carry mass $\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}$, while the scales $\log\varepsilon<t<0$, corresponding to the region in \eqref{penalization_term}, carry mass
\[
\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}\big(\varepsilon^{-2s}-1\big)\to\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}(\lambda-1).
\]
On the latter scales, the macroscopic profile is essentially frozen, $u(y)\approx u(x)$, whereas $y/\varepsilon$ is equidistributed in $Q$. Testing $F_\varepsilon$ on $u_\varepsilon(x)=u(x)\big(1+\varphi(x/\varepsilon)\big)$, the corresponding energy is therefore the periodic functional of \cite{PiniSantilli2026}, weighted by $a$ and evaluated on the corrector $\varphi$. For a periodic function, as in \cite{PiniSantilli2026}, the two ranges of scales play the same role, since its values at infinity are distributed as its values on the periodicity cell. The separation between the scale $\varepsilon$ and the macroscopic scale splits them.
Hence, the coefficient $a_\lambda$ in Theorem \ref{thm:main} interpolates between homogenization at infinity and homogenization on the torus.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:a-infty}
The map $\lambda\mapsto a_\lambda$ is continuous, increasing and concave (an infimum of affine functions of $\lambda$), and interpolates between the harmonic and the arithmetic mean of $\overline a^c$.

Indeed, if $\lambda=1$, then it is well known that the minimization gives the harmonic mean of $\overline a^c$; that is
$$
a_1=\Big(\int_Q \frac{1}{\overline a^c(x)}dx\Big)^{-1}.
$$
If $\lambda=+\infty$, then the only test function $\varphi\in L^2(Q)$ with $A_{\infty}(1,\varphi)<+\infty$ is $\varphi=0$. Therefore, 
                                                                
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a-infty}
a_{+\infty}=\int_Q\int_Q (a(x,y)+a(y,x))\,dx\,dy =\int_Q\overline a^c(x)\,dx=2\int_Q\int_Q a(x,y)\,dx\,dy;
\end{equation}
that is, the average of $\overline a^c$ (or twice the average of $a$).
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:symmetrx}
The replacement of $a(x,y)$ by its symmetric part $\frac12\big(a(x,y)+a(y,x)\big)$, which satisfies the same bounds and periodicity assumptions, does not change $F_\varepsilon$ nor $a_\lambda$. Hence, from now on we assume, without loss of generality, that $a$ is symmetric.
In such a case, we define 
$$\overline{a}^{y}(x)\coloneqq\int_Q a(x,y)\,dy.$$
Then we have $\overline{a}^{\rm c}=2\overline{a}^{y}$,
and we may use $2\overline{a}^{y}$ for the contracted function instead of $\overline{a}^{\rm c}$.
In this case, \eqref{eq:a-lambda-z} reads
\begin{equation*}
A_\lambda(z,\varphi)=2\int_Q\overline{a}^{y}(x)|z+\varphi(x)|^2\,dx+(\lambda-1)\int_Q\int_Q a(x,y)|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^2\,dx\,dy.
\end{equation*}
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:separated}
We consider $a(x,y)$ of the form $\frac12\big(a_0(x)+a_0(y)\big)$, with $a_0\colon\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ a $1$-periodic, bounded Borel function and satisfies $a_0\geq\alpha>0$ a.e. This corresponds (in the non-symmetric setting) to considering the coefficient $a_0(x/\varepsilon)$ alone in $F_\varepsilon$.
Then the coefficient $a_\lambda$ can be explicitly computed for every $\lambda\in[1,+\infty]$. Note that, by Remark \ref{rem:symmetrx}, this is the symmetric kernel equivalent to the choice $a(x,y)=a_0(x)$ discussed in Section \ref{sec:introduction}. By \eqref{def:oac}, the contracted function is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separated-ac}
\overline{a}^{\rm c}(x)=a_0(x)+\overline{a}_0,\qquad\text{where }\overline{a}_0\coloneqq\int_Q a_0(\xi)\,d\xi,
\end{equation}
while, expanding the square and using that $\int_Q\varphi(x)\,dx=0$, for every test function $\varphi$ in \eqref{eq:a-lambda} we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separated-nonlocal}
\int_Q\int_Q a(x,y)|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^2\,dx\,dy=\int_Q\overline{a}^{\rm c}(x)|\varphi(x)|^2\,dx;
\end{equation}
that is, the nonlocal term in \eqref{eq:a-lambda-z} is local. Hence, if $\lambda<+\infty$, by \eqref{eq:separated-nonlocal} and after completing the square, \eqref{eq:a-lambda-z} reads
\begin{align*}
A_\lambda(1,\varphi)&=\int_Q\overline{a}^{\rm c}(x)\Big(|1+\varphi(x)|^2+(\lambda-1)|\varphi(x)|^2\Big)dx\\
&=\lambda\int_Q\overline{a}^{\rm c}(x)\Big|\varphi(x)+\frac1\lambda\Big|^2dx+\Big(1-\frac1\lambda\Big)\int_Q\overline{a}^{\rm c}(x)\,dx,
\end{align*}
so that \eqref{eq:a-lambda} reduces to minimizing the first term under the constraint $\int_Q\big(\varphi(x)+\frac1\lambda\big)dx=\frac1\lambda$. This amounts to saturating a Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, which produces the unique minimizer
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separated-phi}
\varphi_\lambda(x)=\frac1\lambda\bigg(\frac{a_1}{\overline{a}^{\rm c}(x)}-1\bigg),\qquad\text{where }a_1=\bigg(\int_Q\frac{dx}{\overline{a}^{\rm c}(x)}\bigg)^{-1}
\end{equation}
is the harmonic mean of $\overline{a}^{\rm c}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separated-alambda}
a_\lambda=\Big(1-\frac1\lambda\Big)\int_Q\overline{a}^{\rm c}(x)\,dx+\frac1\lambda\,a_1=\Big(1-\frac1\lambda\Big)a_{+\infty}+\frac1\lambda\,a_1,
\end{equation}
where we used \eqref{eq:a-infty} and \eqref{eq:separated-ac}. With the convention $\frac1\lambda=0$ for $\lambda=+\infty$, both \eqref{eq:separated-phi} and \eqref{eq:separated-alambda} hold for $\lambda=+\infty$ also, in accordance with \cref{rem:a-infty}.

Thus, for such kernels, $a_\lambda$ is the convex combination, with parameter $\frac1\lambda$, of the harmonic mean $a_1$ and the arithmetic mean $a_{+\infty}=2\overline{a}_0$ of the contracted function.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:annuli}
For every $s\in(0,1)$, every measurable set $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ with $|A|<+\infty$, every $0<r_1<r_2$ and every $u\in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$, it holds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annuli}
s\int_A\int_{\{r_1<|x-y|<r_2\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^2}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\,dx\leq 2\sigma_{d-1}\|u\|^2_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)}|A|\big(r_1^{-2s}-r_2^{-2s}\big),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{d-1}$ is as in \eqref{eq:Flambda}. In particular, if $(u_\varepsilon)\subseteq L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$ is a bounded sequence and $r_1\coloneqq r_1(\varepsilon)$, $r_2\coloneqq r_2(\varepsilon)$ satisfy $0<r_1<r_2$ and
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}r_1^{-2s}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}r_2^{-2s}\in[0,+\infty),
\end{equation*}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annu-0}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}s\int_A\int_{\{r_1<|x-y|<r_2\}}\frac{|u_\varepsilon(x)-u_\varepsilon(y)|^2}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\,dx=0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Using $|u(x)-u(y)|^2\leq4\|u\|^2_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)}$ and integrating in polar coordinates, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
s\int_A\int_{\{r_1<|x-y|<r_2\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^2}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\,dx\leq4s\|u\|^2_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)}|A|\,\sigma_{d-1}\int_{r_1}^{r_2}t^{-1-2s}\,dt,
\end{equation*}
which gives \eqref{eq:annuli}.

Applying \eqref{eq:annuli},  with $u=u_\varepsilon$, we see that the quantity in the limit in \eqref{eq:annu-0} is bounded by $2\sigma_{d-1}\sup_\varepsilon\|u_\varepsilon\|^2_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)}|A|\big(r_1^{-2s}-r_2^{-2s}\big)$, which vanishes as $\varepsilon\to0$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:jump}
Let $\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ be a closed set such that there exist $C,\ell>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minkowski}
\big|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^d:\operatorname{dist}(x,\Sigma)<\tau\}\big|\leq C\tau\qquad\text{for every }\tau\in(0,\ell].
\end{equation}
Then, for every $s\in(0,1/2)$ and every $u\in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$, it holds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jump}
s\iint_{\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^d\times\mathbb{R}^d:\,\operatorname{dist}(x,\Sigma)\leq|x-y|<\ell\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^2}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\,dx\leq4\sigma_{d-1}C\|u\|^2_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)}\,\frac{s\,\ell^{1-2s}}{1-2s}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Set $\tau(x)\coloneqq\operatorname{dist}(x,\Sigma)$ and $f(t)\coloneqq\big(t^{-2s}-\ell^{-2s}\big)_+$ for $t>0$. Note that $|\Sigma|=0$ by \eqref{eq:minkowski}. For every $x\notin\Sigma$, using $|u(x)-u(y)|^2\leq4\|u\|^2_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)}$ and integrating in polar coordinates, we get
\begin{eqnarray*}
s\int_{\{\tau(x)\leq|x-y|<\ell\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^2}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dy&\leq& 4\|u\|^2_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)}\,\sigma_{d-1}s\int_{\tau(x)}^{\ell}t^{-1-2s}\,dt
\\&=&2\sigma_{d-1}\|u\|^2_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)}f\big(\tau(x)\big),
\end{eqnarray*}
where the integral is understood to be $0$ if $\tau(x)\geq\ell$. Since $f$ is decreasing on $(0,\ell]$ and vanishes on $[\ell,+\infty)$, by Cavalieri's principle and \eqref{eq:minkowski} we have
\begin{align*}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}f\big(\tau(x)\big)\,dx&=\int_0^{+\infty}\big|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^d:\tau(x)<f^{-1}(\xi)\}\big|\,d\xi\\
&\leq C\int_0^{+\infty}f^{-1}(\xi)\,d\xi\\
&=C\int_0^\ell f(t)\,dt=2C\,\frac{s\,\ell^{1-2s}}{1-2s},
\end{align*}
where $f^{-1}$ denotes the inverse of $f$ restricted to $(0,\ell)$. Integrating the previous estimate in $x$ gives \eqref{eq:jump}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:averaging}
Let $b\in L^1_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^d\times\mathbb{R}^d)$ be $1$-periodic.
Then there exists $C=C_d>0$ such that
\begin{multline}\label{eq:averaging}
\bigg|s\int_A\int_{\{c\varepsilon<|x-y|<\ell\}}\frac{b\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\big)}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\,dx-\overline{b}\,\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}\big((c\varepsilon)^{-2s}-\ell^{-2s}\big)|A|\bigg|\\
\leq C\|b\|_{L^1(Q^2)}\,(c\varepsilon)^{-2s}\Big(s\big(1+c^{-1}\big)^{d+4}|A|+\big(1+c^{-1}\big)^{d}|\partial_{2\varepsilon}A|\Big)
\end{multline}
for every $s\in(0,1)$, every $c,\ell>0$, every $\varepsilon>0$ such that $c\varepsilon<\ell$ and $4\sqrt d\,\varepsilon\leq\ell$, and every Borel set $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ with $|A|<+\infty$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
In the following, $C=C_d>0$ may change from line to line. Set
\begin{equation*}
\kappa(x,y)\coloneqq\frac{s}{|x-y|^{d+2s}},\qquad\Lambda\coloneqq\big(A\times\mathbb{R}^d\big)\cap\{c\varepsilon<|x-y|<\ell\}.
\end{equation*}
Integrating in polar coordinates, we get
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar-kappa}
\int_\Lambda\kappa(x,y)\,dy\,dx=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}\big((c\varepsilon)^{-2s}-\ell^{-2s}\big)|A|,
\end{equation}
so that the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:averaging} equals the absolute value of
\begin{equation*}
\int_\Lambda\Big(b\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)-\overline{b}\Big)\kappa(x,y)\,dy\,dx.
\end{equation*}
We cover $\Lambda$ by the sets
\begin{align*}
\Lambda_{1,\varepsilon}&\coloneqq\bigcup\big\{Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}:Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}\subseteq\Lambda,\ Q_{\varepsilon,j}\subseteq A\big\},\\
\Lambda_{2,\varepsilon}&\coloneqq\Lambda\cap\big(\partial_\varepsilon A\times\mathbb{R}^d\big),\\
\Lambda_{3,\varepsilon}&\coloneqq\Lambda\setminus\big(\Lambda_{1,\varepsilon}\cup\Lambda_{2,\varepsilon}\big).
\end{align*} By the $1$-periodicity of $b$, for all $j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^d$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cube-L1}
\int_{Q_{\varepsilon,j}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon,k}}\Big|b\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)-\overline{b}\Big|\,dy\,dx=\varepsilon^{2d}\,\|b-\overline{b}\|_{L^1(Q^2)}\leq2\varepsilon^{2d}\|b\|_{L^1(Q^2)}.
\end{equation}
Since the sets $\Lambda_{2,\varepsilon}$ and $\Lambda_{3,\varepsilon}$ are not unions of products of cubes, we estimate the corresponding contributions by covering them with products of cubes, and using \eqref{eq:cube-L1} on each of them.

Let $Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}\subseteq\Lambda_{1,\varepsilon}$, fix $(\overline x,\overline y)\in Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}$, and set $$m_{jk}\coloneqq\inf\{|x-y|^{-d-1-2s}:(x,y)\in Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}\}.$$ Since $b$ is $1$-periodic, we have
\begin{equation*}
\int_{Q_{\varepsilon,j}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon,k}}\Big(b\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)-\overline{b}\Big)\kappa(x,y)\,dy\,dx=\int_{Q_{\varepsilon,j}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon,k}}\Big(b\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)-\overline{b}\Big)\big(\kappa(x,y)-\kappa(\overline x,\overline y)\big)\,dy\,dx.
\end{equation*}
For $x\neq y$ we have $|\nabla_{(x,y)}\kappa(x,y)|\leq\sqrt2(d+2)s|x-y|^{-d-1-2s}$. Moreover, any two points $(x,y),(x',y')\in Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}$ satisfy $|x'-y'|\geq c\varepsilon$ and
\begin{equation*}
|x-y|\leq|x'-y'|+2\sqrt d\,\varepsilon\leq\big(1+2\sqrt d\,c^{-1}\big)|x'-y'|\leq2\sqrt d\big(1+c^{-1}\big)|x'-y'|,
\end{equation*}
so that, by the mean-value theorem,
\begin{equation*}
|\kappa(x,y)-\kappa(\overline x,\overline y)|\leq C\big(1+c^{-1}\big)^{d+3}\varepsilon s\,m_{jk}\qquad\text{for }(x,y)\in Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k},
\end{equation*}
where we used that $d+1+2s<d+3$ since $s<1$. Hence, by \eqref{eq:cube-L1} and using $\varepsilon^{2d}m_{jk}\leq\int_{Q_{\varepsilon,j}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon,k}}|x-y|^{-d-1-2s}\,dy\,dx$, summing over the cubes of $\Lambda_{1,\varepsilon}$ and integrating in polar coordinates we obtain
\begin{align*}
\bigg|\int_{\Lambda_{1,\varepsilon}}\Big(b\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)-\overline{b}\Big)\kappa(x,y)\,dy\,dx\bigg|&\leq C\big(1+c^{-1}\big)^{d+3}\varepsilon s\|b\|_{L^1(Q^2)}|A|\,\sigma_{d-1}\int_{c\varepsilon}^{+\infty}t^{-2-2s}\,dt\\
&\leq C\big(1+c^{-1}\big)^{d+4}\|b\|_{L^1(Q^2)}\,s\,(c\varepsilon)^{-2s}|A|.
\end{align*}

Let now $Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}$ intersect $\Lambda_{2,\varepsilon}\cup\Lambda_{3,\varepsilon}$. Since $\Lambda\subseteq\{|x-y|>c\varepsilon\}$ and $\big||x-y|-|x'-y'|\big|\leq2\sqrt d\,\varepsilon$ for $(x,y),(x',y')\in Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sup-kappa}
\sup_{(Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k})\cap\Lambda}\kappa\leq s\,\max\big\{c\varepsilon,|x-y|-2\sqrt d\,\varepsilon\big\}^{-d-2s}\qquad\text{for every }(x,y)\in Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}.
\end{equation}

If $Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}$ intersects $\Lambda_{2,\varepsilon}$, then $Q_{\varepsilon,j}$ intersects $\partial_\varepsilon A$, so that $Q_{\varepsilon,j}\subseteq\partial_{2\varepsilon}A$; moreover, $|x-y|<\ell+2\sqrt d\,\varepsilon$ on $Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}$. Hence, by \eqref{eq:cube-L1} and \eqref{eq:sup-kappa}, the contribution of $\Lambda_{2,\varepsilon}$ is at most
\begin{equation*}
2\|b\|_{L^1(Q^2)}\int_{\partial_{2\varepsilon}A}\int_{\{|x-y|<\ell+2\sqrt d\,\varepsilon\}}s\,\max\big\{c\varepsilon,|x-y|-2\sqrt d\,\varepsilon\big\}^{-d-2s}\,dy\,dx.
\end{equation*}
Integrating in polar coordinates, and using that $t\leq(1+2\sqrt d\,c^{-1})(t-2\sqrt d\,\varepsilon)$ for $t\geq(c+2\sqrt d)\varepsilon$, the inner integral is bounded by
\begin{equation*}
\sigma_{d-1}s\,(c\varepsilon)^{-d-2s}\frac{\big((c+2\sqrt d)\varepsilon\big)^d}{d}+\big(1+2\sqrt d\,c^{-1}\big)^{d-1}\sigma_{d-1}s\int_{c\varepsilon}^{+\infty}t^{-1-2s}\,dt\leq C\big(1+c^{-1}\big)^{d}(c\varepsilon)^{-2s},
\end{equation*}
so that the contribution of $\Lambda_{2,\varepsilon}$ is bounded by $C(1+c^{-1})^d\|b\|_{L^1(Q^2)}(c\varepsilon)^{-2s}|\partial_{2\varepsilon}A|$.

If $Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}$ intersects $\Lambda_{3,\varepsilon}$ at a point $(x,y)$, then $x\in A$ and $\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial A)>\sqrt d\,\varepsilon$, so that $Q_{\varepsilon,j}\subseteq A$. Since $(x,y)\notin\Lambda_{1,\varepsilon}$, the set $Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}$ contains a pair $(x',y')$ with $|x'-y'|\leq c\varepsilon$ or $|x'-y'|\geq\ell$. As $\big||x-y|-|x'-y'|\big|\leq2\sqrt d\,\varepsilon$ and $|x-y|<\ell$, we get that either $|x-y|\leq(c+2\sqrt d)\varepsilon$ on $Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}$, or $\ell-2\sqrt d\,\varepsilon\leq|x-y|\leq\ell+2\sqrt d\,\varepsilon$ on $Q_{\varepsilon,j}\times Q_{\varepsilon,k}$. In the first case, the supremum in \eqref{eq:sup-kappa} is at most $s(c\varepsilon)^{-d-2s}$; in the second case, since $\ell-2\sqrt d\,\varepsilon\geq\ell/2$, it is at most $s(\ell/2)^{-d-2s}$. Hence, by \eqref{eq:cube-L1}, the contribution of $\Lambda_{3,\varepsilon}$ is at most
\begin{align*}
&2\|b\|_{L^1(Q^2)}|A|\Big(s(c\varepsilon)^{-d-2s}\,\omega_d\big((c+2\sqrt d)\varepsilon\big)^d+s(\ell/2)^{-d-2s}\,\sigma_{d-1}\,4\sqrt d\,\varepsilon\,(2\ell)^{d-1}\Big)\\
&\qquad\leq C\|b\|_{L^1(Q^2)}\,s\Big(\big(1+c^{-1}\big)^d(c\varepsilon)^{-2s}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\ell}\ell^{-2s}\Big)|A|,
\end{align*}
where $\omega_d$ denotes the volume of the unit ball. Since $\varepsilon\leq\ell$ and $\ell^{-2s}\leq(c\varepsilon)^{-2s}$, the right-hand side is bounded by $C(1+c^{-1})^{d+4}\|b\|_{L^1(Q^2)}\,s\,(c\varepsilon)^{-2s}|A|$, and \eqref{eq:averaging} follows.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:long-range}
Let $s\in(0,1)$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Let $\overline{a}^{y}$ be as in Remark \ref{rem:symmetrx}. Then there exists $C=C_d>0$ such that
\begin{multline}\label{eq:long-range}
\bigg|s\int_A\int_{\mathbb{R}^d\setminus B_R}a\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\frac{|u(x)|^2}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\,dx-\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}R^{-2s}\int_A\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)|u(x)|^2\,dx\bigg|\\
\leq C\beta\,s\,\frac{\rho+\varepsilon}{R}R^{-2s}\|u\|^2_{L^2(A)}
\end{multline}
for every $\rho>0$, every Borel set $A\subseteq B_\rho$, every $u\in L^2(A)$ and every $R\geq\max\{2\rho,\sqrt d\,\varepsilon\}$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
In the following, $C=C_d>0$ may change from line to line. Set $\kappa(y)\coloneqq s|y|^{-d-2s}$ and
\begin{equation*}
J\coloneqq s\int_A|u(x)|^2\int_{\mathbb{R}^d\setminus B_R}a\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\frac{1}{|y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\,dx.
\end{equation*}

We first replace $|x-y|$ with $|y|$. Let $x\in A$ and $|y|\geq R$. For every $\theta\in[0,1]$ we have $|y-\theta x|\geq|y|-\rho\geq|y|/2$, since $R\geq2\rho$. Hence, by the mean-value theorem applied to $z\mapsto|z|^{-d-2s}$ on the segment joining $y-x$ and $y$,
\begin{equation*}
\Big||x-y|^{-d-2s}-|y|^{-d-2s}\Big|\leq(d+2)\,\rho\,\big(|y|/2\big)^{-d-1-2s}\leq C\rho|y|^{-d-1-2s},
\end{equation*}
where we used again that $d+1+2s<d+3$ since $s<1$. Using $a\leq\beta$ and integrating in polar coordinates, we get
\begin{equation}\label{eq:long-range-step1}
\begin{aligned}
\bigg|s\int_A\int_{\mathbb{R}^d\setminus B_R}a\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\frac{|u(x)|^2}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\,dx-J\bigg|&\leq C\beta s\rho\|u\|^2_{L^2(A)}\sigma_{d-1}\int_R^{+\infty}t^{-2-2s}\,dt\\
&\leq C\beta\,s\,\frac{\rho}{R}R^{-2s}\|u\|^2_{L^2(A)}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

We now average in $y$. Fix $x\in A$ and set $g(y)\coloneqq a(x/\varepsilon,y/\varepsilon)$. Integrating in polar coordinates, we have
\begin{equation*}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d\setminus B_R}\kappa(y)\,dy=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}R^{-2s},
\end{equation*}
so that
\begin{equation*}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d\setminus B_R}g(y)\kappa(y)\,dy-\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}R^{-2s}\,\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^d\setminus B_R}\Big(g(y)-\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)\kappa(y)\,dy.
\end{equation*}
Note that $|g-\overline{a}^{y}(x/\varepsilon)|\leq\beta$. We partition $\mathbb{R}^d\setminus B_R$ into the sets
\begin{equation*}
\Gamma_{1,\varepsilon}\coloneqq\bigcup\big\{Q_{\varepsilon,k}:Q_{\varepsilon,k}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d\setminus B_R\big\},\qquad\Gamma_{2,\varepsilon}\coloneqq\big(\mathbb{R}^d\setminus B_R\big)\setminus\Gamma_{1,\varepsilon}.
\end{equation*}
Let $Q_{\varepsilon,k}\subseteq\Gamma_{1,\varepsilon}$ and fix $\overline y\in Q_{\varepsilon,k}$. Since $a$ is $1$-periodic in the second variable, we have
\begin{equation*}
\int_{Q_{\varepsilon,k}}g(y)\,dy=\varepsilon^d\,\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big),
\end{equation*}
so that
\begin{equation*}
\int_{Q_{\varepsilon,k}}\Big(g(y)-\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)\kappa(y)\,dy=\int_{Q_{\varepsilon,k}}\Big(g(y)-\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)\big(\kappa(y)-\kappa(\overline y)\big)\,dy.
\end{equation*}
For $y\neq0$ we have $|\nabla\kappa(y)|\leq(d+2)s|y|^{-d-1-2s}$. Moreover, any two points $y,y'\in Q_{\varepsilon,k}$ satisfy $|y'|\geq R\geq\sqrt d\,\varepsilon$ and $|y|\leq|y'|+\sqrt d\,\varepsilon\leq2|y'|$. Hence, by the mean-value theorem,
\begin{equation*}
|\kappa(y)-\kappa(\overline y)|\leq C\varepsilon s|y|^{-d-1-2s}\qquad\text{for }y\in Q_{\varepsilon,k},
\end{equation*}
where we used again that $d+1+2s<d+3$ since $s<1$. Summing over the cubes of $\Gamma_{1,\varepsilon}$ and integrating in polar coordinates, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\bigg|\int_{\Gamma_{1,\varepsilon}}\Big(g(y)-\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)\kappa(y)\,dy\bigg|\leq C\beta\varepsilon s\,\sigma_{d-1}\int_R^{+\infty}t^{-2-2s}\,dt\leq C\beta\,s\,\frac{\varepsilon}{R}R^{-2s}.
\end{equation*}
If $y\in\Gamma_{2,\varepsilon}$, the cube $Q_{\varepsilon,k}$ containing $y$ intersects $B_R$, so that $|y|<R+\sqrt d\,\varepsilon$. Hence, integrating in polar coordinates,
\begin{equation*}
\int_{\Gamma_{2,\varepsilon}}\kappa(y)\,dy\leq\sigma_{d-1}s\int_R^{R+\sqrt d\,\varepsilon}t^{-1-2s}\,dt\leq\sqrt d\,\sigma_{d-1}s\,\varepsilon R^{-1-2s}=C s\,\frac{\varepsilon}{R}R^{-2s}.
\end{equation*}
Using $|g-\overline{a}^{y}(x/\varepsilon)|\leq\beta$ once more, we conclude that
\begin{equation*}
\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^d\setminus B_R}a\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\kappa(y)\,dy-\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}R^{-2s}\,\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)\bigg|\leq C\beta\,s\,\frac{\varepsilon}{R}R^{-2s}
\end{equation*}
for every $x\in A$. Multiplying by $|u(x)|^2$ and integrating over $A$, we get
\begin{equation*}
\bigg|J-\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}R^{-2s}\int_A\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)|u(x)|^2\,dx\bigg|\leq C\beta\,s\,\frac{\varepsilon}{R}R^{-2s}\|u\|^2_{L^2(A)},
\end{equation*}
which, together with \eqref{eq:long-range-step1}, gives \eqref{eq:long-range}.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:bis-competitor}
Let $S\coloneqq Q_\rho\setminus Q_{\rho-\delta}$, where $Q_{\rho-\delta}$ denotes the closed hypercube of side $\rho-\delta$ centred at $x_0$, and let
\begin{equation*}
c_\varepsilon\coloneqq\frac{1}{|S|}\int_Su^M_\varepsilon(x)\,dx,\qquad v^M_\varepsilon\coloneqq u^M_\varepsilon\,{\mathpalette\irchi\relax}_{Q_{\rho-\delta}}+c_\varepsilon\,{\mathpalette\irchi\relax}_S,
\end{equation*}
extended to $\mathbb{R}^d$ by $\rho$-periodicity. Let $N$ be as in \eqref{eq:bis-rho} and
\begin{equation*}
w^M_\varepsilon\coloneqq\frac{1}{N^d}\sum_{k\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}^d}v^M_{\varepsilon,k},\qquad v^M_{\varepsilon,k}\coloneqq v^M_\varepsilon(\cdot+\varepsilon k).
\end{equation*}
Then $w^M_\varepsilon$ is $\varepsilon$-periodic, $\|w^M_\varepsilon\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)}\leq M$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bis-mean}
\frac{1}{\rho^d}\int_{Q_\rho}w^M_\varepsilon(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{\rho^d}\int_{Q_\rho}u^M_\varepsilon(x)\,dx,
\end{equation}
\begin{eqnarray}\label{eq:bis-energy}\nonumber
G_\varepsilon(w^M_\varepsilon)&\leq& G_\varepsilon(u^M_\varepsilon)+4d\,\sigma_{d-1}\beta M^2\big(\varepsilon^{-2s}-(\sqrt d\,\rho)^{-2s}\big)\frac{\delta}{\rho}\\
&&\hskip2cm+2\sigma_{d-1}\beta M^2\Big(\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)^{-2s}-(\sqrt d\,\rho)^{-2s}\Big),
\end{eqnarray}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bis-long-range}
\int_{Q_\rho}\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)|w^M_\varepsilon(x)|^2\,dx\leq\int_{Q_\rho}\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)|u_\varepsilon(x)|^2\,dx+d\,\beta M^2\rho^{d-1}\delta.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By construction $w^M_\varepsilon$ is $\varepsilon$-periodic and, since $|u^M_\varepsilon|\leq M$ and $|c_\varepsilon|\leq M$, also $|v^M_\varepsilon|\leq M$ and $|w^M_\varepsilon|\leq M$. By the definition of $c_\varepsilon$ we have $\int_{Q_\rho}v^M_\varepsilon dx=\int_{Q_\rho}u^M_\varepsilon dx$, while the $\rho$-periodicity of $v^M_\varepsilon$ gives $\int_{Q_\rho}v^M_{\varepsilon,k}=\int_{Q_\rho}v^M_\varepsilon$ for every $k\in\mathbb{Z}^d$; averaging over $k$ gives \eqref{eq:bis-mean}.

We prove \eqref{eq:bis-energy}. We first compare $v^M_\varepsilon$ with $u^M_\varepsilon$. Splitting the domain of integration in $G_\varepsilon(v^M_\varepsilon)$ according to whether $x$ and $y$ belong to $Q_{\rho-\delta}$ or to $S$, the contribution of $Q_{\rho-\delta}\times Q_{\rho-\delta}$ is at most $G_\varepsilon(u^M_\varepsilon)$, the contribution of $S\times S$ vanishes because $v^M_\varepsilon$ is constant on $S$, and, using $|v^M_\varepsilon(x)-v^M_\varepsilon(y)|\leq2M$, $a\leq\beta$ and $|S|\leq d\,\rho^{d-1}\delta$, the contribution of the remaining pairs is at most
\begin{equation*}
\frac{8\beta M^2s}{\rho^d}|S|\,\sigma_{d-1}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\sqrt d\,\rho}t^{-1-2s}\,dt\leq4d\,\sigma_{d-1}\beta M^2\big(\varepsilon^{-2s}-(\sqrt d\,\rho)^{-2s}\big)\frac{\delta}{\rho}.
\end{equation*}

We then compare $w^M_\varepsilon$ with $v^M_\varepsilon$. Since $a$ is $1$-periodic, the integrand of $G_\varepsilon$ is invariant under the simultaneous translation $(x,y)\mapsto(x+\varepsilon k,y+\varepsilon k)$; hence, setting $Q'\coloneqq Q_\rho+\varepsilon k$ and assuming without loss of generality that $\varepsilon k\in[0,\rho)^d$, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
G_\varepsilon(v^M_{\varepsilon,k})=\frac{s}{\rho^d}\int_{Q'}\int_{Q'\cap\{|x-y|>\varepsilon\}}a\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\frac{|v^M_\varepsilon(x)-v^M_\varepsilon(y)|^2}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\,dx.
\end{equation*}
We write $Q'=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\{0,1\}^d}A_\sigma$ with $A_\sigma\coloneqq Q'\cap(Q_\rho+\rho\sigma)$; the sets $A_\sigma-\rho\sigma$ are then pairwise disjoint subsets of $Q_\rho$ covering it, and we split the double integral accordingly. Since $\rho/\varepsilon\in\mathbb{N}$ (recall \eqref{eq:bis-rho}) and $v^M_\varepsilon$ is $\rho$-periodic, the integrand is invariant under $(x,y)\mapsto(x+\rho\sigma,y+\rho\sigma)$, so that the contributions of the sets $A_\sigma\times A_\sigma$ add up to at most $G_\varepsilon(v^M_\varepsilon)$. If instead $\sigma\neq\tau$, $x\in A_\sigma$ and $y\in A_\tau$, the segment joining $x$ and $y$ meets $\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^d}\partial(Q_\rho+\rho j)$, on whose $\delta/2$-neighbourhood $v^M_\varepsilon$ is constant; hence $v^M_\varepsilon(x)=v^M_\varepsilon(y)$ whenever $|x-y|<\delta/2$, and the contributions of the sets $A_\sigma\times A_\tau$ with $\sigma\neq\tau$ add up to at most
\begin{equation*}
\frac{4\beta M^2s}{\rho^d}\int_{Q'}\int_{\{\delta/2\leq|x-y|\leq\sqrt d\,\rho\}}\frac{dy\,dx}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}=2\sigma_{d-1}\beta M^2\Big(\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)^{-2s}-(\sqrt d\,\rho)^{-2s}\Big).
\end{equation*}
Since $G_\varepsilon$ is convex, averaging over $k\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}^d$ gives \eqref{eq:bis-energy}.

Finally we prove \eqref{eq:bis-long-range}. Since $|u^M_\varepsilon|\leq|u_\varepsilon|$ pointwise, it is enough to prove it with $u^M_\varepsilon$ in place of $u_\varepsilon$. As $v^M_\varepsilon=u^M_\varepsilon$ on $Q_{\rho-\delta}$ and $|v^M_\varepsilon|\leq M$, using $\overline{a}^{y}\leq\beta$ and $|S|\leq d\,\rho^{d-1}\delta$ we get
\begin{equation*}
\int_{Q_\rho}\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)|v^M_\varepsilon(x)|^2\,dx\leq\int_{Q_\rho}\overline{a}^{y}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)|u^M_\varepsilon(x)|^2\,dx+d\,\beta M^2\rho^{d-1}\delta.
\end{equation*}
Moreover $\overline{a}^{y}$ is $1$-periodic, so that, arguing as above but without the contributions of the sets $A_\sigma\times A_\tau$ with $\sigma\neq\tau$, we obtain $\int_{Q_\rho}\overline{a}^{y}(x/\varepsilon)|v^M_{\varepsilon,k}|^2\,dx=\int_{Q_\rho}\overline{a}^{y}(x/\varepsilon)|v^M_\varepsilon|^2\,dx$ for every $k\in\mathbb{Z}^d$; the convexity of $t\mapsto t^2$ concludes the proof.

\label{thm:main2}
Let $a\colon\mathbb{R}^d\times\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ be a  Borel $1$-periodic function such that there exist $0<\alpha\leq\beta$ for which $\alpha\leq a(x,y)\leq\beta$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^d$. Let $1<p<\infty$, $\varepsilon>0$ and $s\coloneqq s(\varepsilon)\in(0,1)$. Let
\begin{equation}
F_{\varepsilon,p}(u)\coloneqq s\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}a\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^p}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,dx\,dy
\end{equation}
be defined in $L^p(\mathbb{R}^d)$. If $\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}s=0$ and
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\varepsilon^{-sp}=\lambda\in[1,+\infty],
\end{equation*}
then
\begin{equation*}
\Gamma\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}F_{\varepsilon,p}(u)=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{p}\,a_{\lambda,p}\|u\|^p_{L^p(\mathbb{R}^d)},
\end{equation*}
for all $u\in L^p(\mathbb{R}^d)$, where the  sequential $\Gamma$-limit is computed with respect to the weak topology of $L^p(\mathbb{R}^d)$, and $a_{\lambda,p}$ is given by 
\begin{eqnarray}\nonumber
&&\hskip-1.3cm a_{\lambda,p}\coloneqq\inf\bigg\{\int_{Q}\int_{Q}(a(x,y)+a(y,x))|1+\varphi(x)|^p\,dx\,dy\\&&\ \quad+(\lambda-1)\int_{Q}\int_{Q} a(x,y)|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^p\,dx\,dy:\varphi\in L^p(Q)\text{                  
and }\int_Q\varphi(x)\,dx=0\bigg\}
\end{eqnarray}
if $\lambda<+\infty$, while
\begin{eqnarray}
a_{+\infty,p}\coloneqq\ 2\int_{Q}\int_{Q}a(x,y)dx\,dy.
\end{eqnarray}
