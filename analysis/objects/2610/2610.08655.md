<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:numerics}
For a genus-two Lefschetz fibration $f:X\to S^2$ of type $(n,s)$,
\[
\mathrm{e}(X)=n+s-4,
\qquad
\sigma(X)=-\frac35n-\frac15s.
\]
Consequently
\[
\chi_h(X)=\frac{\mathrm{e}(X)+\sigma(X)}4,
\qquad
c_1^2(X)=2\mathrm{e}(X)+3\sigma(X).
\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The Euler characteristic formula is the standard Lefschetz-fibration formula
$\mathrm{e}(X)=\mathrm{e}(S^2)\mathrm{e}(\Sigma_2)+N=-4+N$.  Since every genus-two mapping class is hyperelliptic, the signature formula is Endo's formula.  The remaining identities are the standard almost-complex relations.

\label{prop:polizzi-branch}
In the sense of Polizzi's Proposition~9.2, the finite morphism

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:bisection-quotient}
Let $\xi_j\in E[2]\setminus\{0\}$.  Under the normalization
\[
\widetilde B_j^\sharp\cong E/\{\pm1\},\qquad
[x]\longmapsto \xi_j+x+(-x),
\]
the deck involution of
$\rho_j:\widetilde B_j^\sharp\to\mathbb P^1$ is
\[
\tau_j([x])=[x+\xi_j].
\]
Equivalently, $\rho_j$ is the quotient by $\tau_j$, followed by an
automorphism of its target $\mathbb P^1$.

More explicitly, write
\[
E:\quad y^2=(x-e_1)(x-e_2)(x-e_3),
\qquad \xi_j=(e_j,0),
\]
and let $\{j,k,\ell\}=\{1,2,3\}$.  On the coordinate
$x:E/\{\pm1\}\to\mathbb P^1$, the involution is
\[
x\longmapsto e_j+
\frac{(e_j-e_k)(e_j-e_\ell)}{x-e_j}.
\]
Thus an invariant degree-two coordinate is
\[
q_j(x)=x+e_j+
\frac{(e_j-e_k)(e_j-e_\ell)}{x-e_j},
\]
and the two ramification points are characterized by
\[
(x-e_j)^2=(e_j-e_k)(e_j-e_\ell).
\]
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Choose generators $\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_4$ for the four order-three cyclic
subgroups of $E[3]$; these subgroups correspond bijectively to the four
inversion orbits $\{\pm\eta_r\}$.  The following formulas are read from
Polizzi's descriptions of $A_{\xi_j},M_r,N_r$ and the reducible fibers
\cite[Sections~5 and~9]{Polizzi}.  Polizzi's four reducible fibers
$G_r^*=M_r+N_r$ meet $A_{\xi_j}$ in the two divisors
\[
\xi_j+\eta_r+2\eta_r,
\qquad
\xi_j+(\xi_j+\eta_r)+(\xi_j+2\eta_r).
\]
On the normalization these are the points $[\eta_r]$ and
$[\eta_r+\xi_j]$, respectively.  Polizzi's transverse intersection
calculation shows that both occur with multiplicity one.  They are therefore
the two reduced intersections
with $G_r^*$ and hence the entire degree-two fiber of $\rho_j$ over that
value.  Translation by $\xi_j$ commutes with inversion
and therefore descends to the involution $\tau_j$ of $E/\{\pm1\}$.
The four points $[\eta_r]$ are distinct in $E/\{\pm1\}$.  The deck
involution sends each $[\eta_r]$ to $[\eta_r+\xi_j]$, exactly as $\tau_j$
does.  Since two automorphisms of $\mathbb P^1$ agreeing on three distinct
points coincide, the deck involution is $\tau_j$.

The addition law on the Weierstrass model gives the displayed fractional
linear formula for translation by $(e_j,0)$.  Adding $x$ produces an
invariant rational function of degree two.  The ramification points of the
quotient are the fixed points of the involution, which satisfy the final
equation.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:polizzi-six-nonseparating}
Let $\tau$ be one of the six ramification values.  Exactly two of the six points in \eqref{eq:Polizzi-hyperelliptic-fiber} coalesce at $\tau$.  For a positively oriented local coordinate $s=t-\tau$, suitable fiber coordinates give
\[
u_\pm(s)=u_0\pm c\sqrt{s}+O(s),\qquad c\ne0.
\]
A positive circuit around $\tau$ therefore gives a positive half twist $h_{\alpha_\tau}$ about an arc joining the two colliding branch points.  Its hyperelliptic lift is the positive Dehn twist
\[
t_{a_\tau},\qquad a_\tau=q^{-1}(\alpha_\tau),
\]
where $q:\Sigma_2\to S^2$ is the hyperelliptic quotient.  The curve $a_\tau$ is nonseparating.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
A ramification point of a degree-two map in characteristic zero is simple.  The local form is $s=w^2$ after holomorphic coordinate changes, giving the displayed square-root exchange of the two local sheets.  The positive loop in $s$ is the positive geometric half twist.  The inverse image under the genus-two hyperelliptic cover of an arc joining two branch points is a nonseparating simple closed curve, and the half twist lifts to the positive Dehn twist about that curve.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:polizzi-scaling}
Suppose three points in a disk have expansions
\[
z_k(s)=z_0+s\gamma_k+O(s^2),\qquad k=1,2,3,
\]
with the $\gamma_k$ pairwise distinct.  For sufficiently small $|s|$, a positive circuit of $s$ around zero gives one positive full twist on the three points.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For small $|s|$ the $O(s^2)$ terms can be removed through a homotopy in the configuration space of three distinct points.  The resulting motion $z_0+s\gamma_k$ is a rigid positive $2\pi$ rotation of the three-point configuration, hence the central full twist $\Delta_3^2$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:vertical-branch}
At $s=0$ the fiber $L_i$ itself is a component of the branch divisor, so one should not interpret the six-point equation for a regular ruling fiber as a finite double-cover equation obtained by simply substituting $s=0$.  The braid calculation is performed over the punctured disk $0<|s|\ll1$, where the branch on the ruling fiber consists of the six intersections with $B^\sharp$.  The canonical resolution of the double cover then supplies the central fiber.  Polizzi identifies that central fiber, after returning to the relatively minimal genus-two fibration, as $W_i+Z_i$.  Consequently the spherical $3+3$ braid and the Picard--Lefschetz nodal model are two descriptions of the same degeneration, but the latter is what fixes the lift in $\operatorname{Mod}(\Sigma_2)$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:polizzi-separating-lift}
Let $d_i=q^{-1}(\delta_i)\subset\Sigma_2$.  Then $d_i$ is connected and separating, and the actual local Lefschetz monodromy of Xiao's $K^2=3$ pencil around $L_i$ is the single positive twist
\[
t_{d_i}.
\]
It maps to $T_{\delta_i}^2$ under the Birman--Hilden quotient
\cite{BirmanHilden}.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The curve $\delta_i$ separates the six branch points into two sets of three.  The double cover of a disk branched at three interior points is a one-holed torus: its Euler characteristic is $2-3=-1$, and its boundary is connected because the number of branch points is odd.  Consequently $q^{-1}(\delta_i)=d_i$ is connected and separates $\Sigma_2$ into two one-holed tori.

Over a punctured neighborhood of the distinguished value, the three branch points near $x_i$ undergo one positive full twist and the three near $y_i$ undergo another.  The boundaries of the two complementary three-punctured disks represent the same unoriented isotopy class $\delta_i$, and both motions have positive complex orientation.  Hence the spherical monodromy is $T_{\delta_i}^2$.

This does not by itself determine the Lefschetz lift, because the Birman--Hilden projection has the hyperelliptic involution in its kernel.  The lift is fixed by the local geometry of the singular fiber.  Polizzi's corresponding fiber is $W_i+Z_i$, where $W_i$ and $Z_i$ are smooth elliptic curves meeting transversely at one point.  Near that point the holomorphic map has, after analytic changes of coordinates, the ordinary nodal form
\[
xy=s.
\]
Picard--Lefschetz theory therefore gives one positive Dehn twist about the neck separating the two genus-one components.  This neck is precisely $d_i$.  Thus the geometric local monodromy is $t_{d_i}$.  Since the quotient map has angular degree two on an annular neighborhood of $d_i$, the twist $t_{d_i}$ projects to $T_{\delta_i}^2$.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:polizzi-geometric-factorization}
For the genus-two fibration determined by Polizzi's branch pair, and for
every distinguished basis chosen with the stated product convention,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Polizzi-global-product}
M_1M_2\cdots M_{13}=1\qquad\text{in }\operatorname{Mod}(\Sigma_2).
\end{equation}
Exactly six factors are positive Dehn twists about nonseparating curves and exactly seven are positive Dehn twists about separating curves.  More precisely, the former are the lifts of the six simple ramification half twists of the three bisections, and the latter are the geometric lifts $t_{d_i}$ of the seven $3+3$ spherical monodromies $T_{\delta_i}^2$.  This is a geometric monodromy relation of type $(6,7)$; no explicit marked Hurwitz factorization is asserted.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->

Theorem~\ref{thm:pi1-polizzi},
\[
\pi_1(S_E)\cong\pi_1(E)\cong\mathbb Z^2,

$\alpha:S_E\to E$.  Hence $h$ induces a surjection on $H_1$.  Since a

The last equality follows directly from the adjunction identity.

A rank-two alternating integral lattice has matrix

therefore form a monodromy-invariant rank-two local subsystem, so the
monodromy preserves $K_0$.  Equivalently, each nonseparating transvection
preserves $K_0$ because its defining vector lies there, while the seven
separating twists act trivially on first homology.  Retain the relative
order of the six nonseparating factors after deleting the seven homologically

\[
T_{v_1}T_{v_2}\cdots T_{v_6}=I.
\]

abelianization the only relation among the oriented peripheral classes is
$e_1+\cdots+e_6=0$.

generated by the corresponding width-four positive parabolic.  Since each
$T_{v_i}$ is primitive and positive, it is that generator after a choice
of peripheral path.  The positive sign is fixed by the complex orientation
of the Lefschetz singularity and the convention that a right-handed Dehn
twist gives the positively oriented peripheral generator; its class is
therefore $+e_j$, not $-e_j$.

the identity.  If the $j$th cusp occurs $n_j$ times, abelianization gives
\[
n_1e_1+\cdots+n_6e_6=0.
\]
Thus $(n_1,\ldots,n_6)$ is a multiple of $(1,\ldots,1)$.  Since
$\sum n_j=6$, every $n_j$ equals one.

The cusps of $\Gamma(4)$ are naturally indexed by
$\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Z/4)$, whose six elements may be represented by
\[
[1:0],\ [0:1],\ [1:1],\ [1:2],\ [1:3],\ [2:1].
\]

$SL(2,\mathbb Z)$ change of the basis $(p,q)$.  In particular no two
$v_i$ are equal up to sign in homology.

It remains to account for the choice of distinguished paths.  One may pass
between distinguished path systems by Hurwitz moves and simultaneous
conjugation.  On $K_0$, every nonseparating Picard--Lefschetz transformation
lies in $\Gamma(4)$ and is therefore the identity modulo $4$, while every
separating twist acts trivially.  More explicitly, a Hurwitz move replaces a
transported class $v_i$ by $T_{v_j}^{\pm1}(v_i)$; since
$T_{v_j}\equiv I\pmod4$ on $K_0$, its cusp label is unchanged.  Thus Hurwitz
moves preserve the cusp labels up to permutation.  A simultaneous
conjugation by $g\in\operatorname{Mod}(\Sigma_2)$ changes the marking and transports the
entire pair
\[
(K_0,\{v_i\})\longmapsto(g_*K_0,\{g_*v_i\}).
\]
The induced lattice isomorphism carries reduction modulo four bijectively
from $K_0/4K_0$ to $g_*K_0/4g_*K_0$.  Thus pairwise distinctness of the
cusp labels is invariant under simultaneous conjugation, although $g_*$
need not preserve the originally marked sublattice $K_0$.  A matching path
can be completed to a distinguished path system and gives two equal
transported unoriented curves, hence two equal cusp labels.  This
contradicts the six distinct labels above.

intersection number is the product of the base and fiber intersection

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:nullhomologous-surgery}
Let $Y$ be obtained from $S_E$ by orientation-preserving torus surgeries
with primitive filling slopes along the two
nullhomologous tori $T_x$ and $T_y$.  Then
\[
b_1(Y)\ge2.
\]
In particular, no choice of the two unit Luttinger surgeries above can
produce a simply connected manifold.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Put
\[
C_T=S_E\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu(T_x\cup T_y).
\]
With rational coefficients, excision and the Thom isomorphism give
\[
H_2(S_E,C_T;\mathbb Q)\cong
H_0(T_x\sqcup T_y;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q^2,
\qquad
H_1(S_E,C_T;\mathbb Q)=0.
\]
The relevant part of the homology sequence of the pair is therefore
\[
H_2(S_E;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow\mathbb Q^2
\longrightarrow H_1(C_T;\mathbb Q)
\longrightarrow H_1(S_E;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The first arrow records intersection with $[T_x]$ and $[T_y]$.  Both
classes vanish, so this arrow is zero.  Since
$H_1(S_E;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q^2$, we obtain
\[
0\longrightarrow
\mathbb Q\langle\mu_{T_x},\mu_{T_y}\rangle
\longrightarrow H_1(C_T;\mathbb Q)
\longrightarrow H_1(S_E;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow0,
\]
and hence $b_1(C_T)=4$.

For the two boundary components, Mayer--Vietoris presents $H_1(Y;\mathbb Q)$
as $H_1(C_T;\mathbb Q)\oplus\mathbb Q^4$ modulo the image of
$H_1(T^3\sqcup T^3;\mathbb Q)$.  On each component two boundary classes
eliminate the two core generators of the filling, while the attaching
meridian contributes at most one additional independent relation in
$H_1(C_T;\mathbb Q)$.  Thus the two fillings impose at most two relations
on the rank-four group $H_1(C_T;\mathbb Q)$, and $b_1(Y)\ge2$.  For the
unit Luttinger fillings these relations are
\[
[\mu_{T_x}]+\varepsilon_x[x^L],\qquad
[\mu_{T_y}]+\varepsilon_y[y^L].
\]
The conclusion is independent of signs, basing arcs, and possible
cross-terms in the push-offs.

\label{prop:dual-sphere-criterion}
Let $T_1,T_2\subset S_E$ be disjoint Lagrangian tori.  Suppose that, for
each $i$, there is an embedded sphere $D_i\subset S_E$ meeting $T_i$
transversely once and disjoint from $T_j$ for $j\ne i$.  Put
\[
C=S_E\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu(T_1\cup T_2).
\]
Then inclusion induces an isomorphism
\[
\pi_1(C)\cong\pi_1(S_E)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Consequently, if framed Lagrangian push-offs $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ map to
a basis of $\pi_1(S_E)$, unit Luttinger surgeries along them produce a
simply connected manifold.
<!-- end proposition-11 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Van Kampen identifies $\pi_1(S_E)$ with the quotient of $\pi_1(C)$ by the
normal closure of the two meridians.  The punctured sphere
$D_i\setminus\operatorname{int}D^2\subset C$ is a disk bounding $\mu_i$,
so both meridians are already trivial in $\pi_1(C)$.  Inclusion is
therefore an isomorphism.  The two surgery relations reduce to
$\lambda_1^{\varepsilon_1}=\lambda_2^{\varepsilon_2}=1$ and kill a basis
of $\mathbb Z^2$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:cq-fiber-quotient}
Let $\Pi=\pi_1(F)$ and let $N\triangleleft\Pi$ be the normal closure of
based representatives of the seven vanishing cycles of
$f_{\mathrm{cq}}$.  Then inclusion of a regular fiber induces
\[
 \Pi/N\cong\pi_1(X_{\mathrm{cq}}\setminus\nu F)
 \cong\pi_1(X_{\mathrm{cq}})\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Under abelianization, this quotient is identified with $\mathbb Z^2$ by
$Q$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Delete a small disk about a regular value of the fibration.  The remaining
base is a disk containing all seven critical values.  Van Kampen, using
the section to fix basing paths, presents the fundamental group of the
total space over this disk as the fiber group modulo the normal closure of
the seven vanishing cycles.  This total space is
$X_{\mathrm{cq}}\setminus\nu F$.  A punctured $(-1)$-section is a disk
whose boundary is the meridian of $F$; restoring $\nu F$ therefore changes
neither the group nor the quotient presentation.  Finally,
\[
X_{\mathrm{cq}}\cong(T^2\times S^2)\#3\overline{\mathbb CP}^{\,2},
\]
so its fundamental group is $\mathbb Z^2$; see
\cite[Sections~2.5 and~5.1]{AkhmedovMonden}.

The separating vanishing cycles have zero homology class, while the four
nonseparating classes $-p,q,p-q,-(p+q)$ span
$\langle p,q\rangle$.  Hence the abelianization of $\Pi/N$ is
$H_1(F;\mathbb Z)/\langle p,q\rangle$.  Directly from the displayed
coordinates,
\[
 \ker Q=\langle(0,1,0,1),(2,0,1,0)\rangle=\langle p,q\rangle,
\]
and this sublattice is primitive.  Thus $Q$ gives the stated
identification.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:twisted-double-simply-connected}
The twisted double $Z_\phi$ is a simply connected minimal symplectic
four-manifold.  Its numerical invariants
are
\[
e(Z_\phi)=10,\qquad \sigma(Z_\phi)=-6,\qquad
\chi_h(Z_\phi)=1,\qquad c_1^2(Z_\phi)=2.
\]
In particular, it is homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to
$\mathbb CP^2\#7\overline{\mathbb CP}^{\,2}$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Let $\Pi=\pi_1(F)$ and let $N\triangleleft\Pi$ be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:cq-fiber-quotient}.  That lemma supplies the nonabelian
identification $\Pi/N\cong\mathbb Z^2$ and identifies it by $Q$ after
abelianization.  Van Kampen, with the boundary convention above, gives the
amalgamated-product quotient
\[
\pi_1(Z_\phi)
\cong (\Pi/N)*_{\Pi}(\Pi/N)
\cong\Pi/\langle\!\langle N,\phi(N)\rangle\!\rangle.
\]
Changing basing paths only conjugates the generators of the two normal
subgroups.  After quotienting by $N$, the group is already abelian and is
identified with $\mathbb Z^2$ by $Q$.
The second block contains the vanishing cycles
$-\phi(p)$ and $\phi(q)$.  Their images under $Q$ form the unimodular basis
displayed above, so their normal closure kills the remaining group.  Hence
$\pi_1(Z_\phi)=1$.

The fiber-sum formulas give
\[
e(Z_\phi)=2e(X_{\mathrm{cq}})-2e(F)=2\cdot3-2(-2)=10,
\qquad
\sigma(Z_\phi)=2\sigma(X_{\mathrm{cq}})=-6.
\]
The remaining identities follow from
$4\chi_h=e+\sigma$ and $c_1^2=2e+3\sigma$.  Since $Z_\phi$ is simply
connected, its rank-eight intersection form is unimodular.  An even
unimodular form has signature divisible by $8$, whereas this signature is
$-6$.  The form is therefore odd and hence isomorphic to $I_{1,7}$; its
Kirby--Siebenmann invariant vanishes because $Z_\phi$ is smooth.

Choose area-preserving representatives in the gluing class.  Gompf's sum
construction makes $Z_\phi$ symplectic \cite{Gompf}.  Both summands carry
relatively minimal genus-two Lefschetz fibrations; equivalently, neither
pair $(X_{\mathrm{cq}},F)$ contains a symplectic exceptional sphere disjoint
from $F$.  This is the same relative-minimality input used for the Xiao
fiber sums in \cite[Section~5]{AkhmedovMonden}.  The exceptional ruled case
in Usher's theorem would require the genus-two summing surface to be a
section of an $S^2$-bundle, whereas such a section has the genus of the
base, here one.  Hence \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Usher} applies and $Z_\phi$ is
symplectically minimal.  Freedman's
classification \cite{Freedman} gives the asserted homeomorphism.  Finally, the
classification of symplectic structures on rational surfaces rules out a
minimal symplectic form on the standard seven-fold blowup
\cite{McDuffRational}.  A hypothetical diffeomorphism would transport the
minimal symplectic form on $Z_\phi$ to such a form, a contradiction.

<!-- proposition-12: body_md -->
\label{prop:blowdown-pi1}
Let $S\subset Z_\phi$ be a symplectically embedded sphere with $S^2=-4$,
put $C=Z_\phi\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu S$, and let $\mu$ be the image
in $\pi_1(C)$ of a generator of
$\pi_1(\partial\nu S)=\mathbb Z/4$.  If $Z_\phi(S)$ denotes the rational
blowdown, then
\[
\pi_1(Z_\phi)=\pi_1(C)/\langle\!\langle\mu\rangle\!\rangle,
\qquad
\pi_1(Z_\phi(S))=
\pi_1(C)/\langle\!\langle\mu^2\rangle\!\rangle.
\]
Consequently $Z_\phi(S)$ is simply connected precisely when $\mu^2$
normally generates $\pi_1(C)$.  Since $Z_\phi$ is simply connected, if
$\mu$ is nullhomotopic in $C$, the first quotient formula forces
$\pi_1(C)=1$, and the blowdown is simply connected.  This applies when
$S$ has a geometric dual sphere.
<!-- end proposition-12 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
The first equality is van Kampen for restoring the disk bundle of Euler
number $-4$.  If $B_2$ denotes the rational ball, the inclusion
$L(4,1)=\partial B_2\to B_2$ induces the quotient
$\mathbb Z/4\to\mathbb Z/2$ and sends $\mu$ to its generator.  This
generator is supplied by the boundary and can be eliminated in the van
Kampen pushout, leaving precisely the relation $\mu^2=1$.  This gives the
second equality.  If $S$ has a geometric dual sphere, puncturing the dual
at its intersection with $S$ gives a disk in $C$ bounded by $\mu$.

<!-- proposition-13: body_md -->
\label{prop:conditional-six-blowup}
Suppose the doubled factorization of $Z_\phi$ is Hurwitz equivalent to one
containing, as a contiguous subword, the boundary multitwist of an embedded
four-holed sphere, and suppose that the corresponding configuration
determines a symplectically embedded $(-4)$-sphere $S\subset Z_\phi$.  Assume
that replacing the four boundary twists by the three interior twists in the
lantern relation realizes the rational blowdown of $S$
\cite{EndoGurtas}, and that $S$ satisfies the
normal-generation condition of Proposition~\ref{prop:blowdown-pi1}.
Then $Z_\phi(S)$ is homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to
$\mathbb CP^2\#6\overline{\mathbb CP}^{\,2}$.
<!-- end proposition-13 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
Rational blowdown along the sphere $S$ changes the
invariants by
\[
(e,\sigma,c_1^2)=(10,-6,2)\longmapsto(9,-5,3).
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:blowdown-pi1} gives simple connectivity.  The
intersection form is unimodular of rank seven and signature $-5$; it is odd
because an even unimodular form has signature divisible by eight.  Hence it
is $I_{1,6}$, and its Kirby--Siebenmann invariant vanishes because the
manifold is smooth.  Freedman's theorem \cite{Freedman} gives an orientation-preserving
homeomorphism.  The rational
blowdown is the symplectic sum of $Z_\phi$ along $S$ with $\mathbb CP^2$
along a conic.  Since $Z_\phi$ is minimal, it contains no exceptional
sphere and, in particular, no pair of disjoint exceptional spheres each
meeting $S$ once.  Thus the exceptional-sphere alternatives in
Dorfmeister's rational-blowdown theorem do not occur, and that theorem shows
that $Z_\phi(S)$ is minimal \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Dorfmeister}.  The
classification of symplectic structures on rational surfaces
\cite{McDuffRational} excludes a minimal symplectic form on the standard
connected sum.  A hypothetical diffeomorphism would transport the form on
$Z_\phi(S)$ to such a form, a contradiction.
