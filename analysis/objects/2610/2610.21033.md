<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}$ be two domains, and $\varepsilon>0$ be a parameter. A randomized function $\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow \mathcal{Y}$ is called an $\varepsilon$-local randomizer if for all $x,x'\in \mathcal{X}$, and any measurable subset $Y\subseteq \mathcal{Y}$, it holds that $\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{M}(x)\in Y] \leq e^\varepsilon \mathbb{P}[\mathcal{M}(x')\in Y]$,
    where the probabilities are over the randomness of $\mathcal{M}$.

Let $\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}$ be two domains, $\varepsilon>0$, and $n\in\mathbb{N}$.

Set $L:=\lceil\log_2D\rceil$. We first prove \textbf{(1)}.
Fix a user $u\in[n]$, and let $T_u:=\mathcal{M}(X_u,\varepsilon,D)$.
Note that the depth $\ell_u$ and Hadamard coordinate $r_u$
are sampled independently of the input. Therefore, it is enough
to compare the conditional probabilities of the reported bit $Y_u$.
For any $x,x'\in[D]$ and any possible transcript $(\ell,r,y)$:
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\mathbb{P}\left[T_u=(\ell,r,y)\mid X_u=x\right]}
         {\mathbb{P}\left[T_u=(\ell,r,y)\mid X_u=x'\right]}
    &=
    \frac{\mathbb{P}\left[Y_u=y\mid\ell_u=\ell,r_u=r,X_u=x\right]}
         {\mathbb{P}\left[Y_u=y\mid\ell_u=\ell,r_u=r,X_u=x'\right]}.
\end{align*}
For any $(\ell,r)$, the unperturbed signs corresponding to $x$
and $x'$ are $b=\operatorname{Had}_\ell(v_\ell(x),r)$ and
$b'=\operatorname{Had}_\ell(v_\ell(x'),r)$, respectively.
If $b=b'$, then the two conditional distributions are identical.
If $b=-b'$, then the largest possible likelihood ratio is
$\frac{p_\varepsilon}{1-p_\varepsilon}=e^\varepsilon$, which proves that $\mathcal{M}$ is
$\varepsilon$-LDP.

Also, note that each user produces its transcript $T_u$ using
only its own input and independent local randomness.
All calls to $\mathsf{PrefixQuery}$ are performed by the server
after the transcript $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{M}}(X)=(T_1,\ldots,T_n)$ has been
collected. These calls only post-process $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{M}}(X)$,
so the protocol is non-interactive $\varepsilon$-LDP, regardless
of the number of queries.

Now, we prove \textbf{(2)}. The prefixes $j=0$ and $j=D$ are exact by definition. Let $j\in\{1,\cdots,D-1\}$ be any remaining query index, and $x\in[D]$ be any domain point. Also, let $\mathcal{C}(j)=\{(\ell_i,v_i): i\in[k]\}$ and
\begin{align*}
    Z_u
    :=
    \frac{L}{\theta_\varepsilon}
    \sum_{(\ell_i,v_i)\in\mathcal{C}(j)}
    \boldsymbol 1\{\ell_u=\ell_i\}Y_u\operatorname{Had}_{\ell_i}(v_i,r_u)
\end{align*}
be defined as in Algorithm~\ref{alg:hierarchical-ldp-prefix-query}.

We show that:
\begin{quote}
    \textbf{(I)} $\mathbb{E}[Z_u\mid X_u=x]=\boldsymbol 1\{x\leq j\}$;

    \textbf{(II)} $|Z_u|\leq L/\theta_\varepsilon$ almost surely.
\end{quote}
Here, all expectations are over the depth sampling ($\ell_u$), the Hadamard-coordinate sampling ($r_u$), and randomized response ($Y_u$).

Conditioned on $X_u=x$, $\ell_u=\ell$, and $r_u=r$, randomized response satisfies:
\[
    \mathbb{E}[Y_u\mid X_u=x,\ell_u=\ell,r_u=r]
    =
    \theta_\varepsilon\operatorname{Had}_\ell(v_\ell(x),r).
\]
Since $\ell_u$ is uniform over $\{1,\ldots,L\}$, it follows from Hadamard orthogonality (Claim~\ref{claim:hadamard-orthogonality-cdf}) that
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbb{E}[Z_u\mid X_u=x]
    &=
    \frac{L}{\theta_\varepsilon}
    \sum_{(\ell_i,v_i)\in\mathcal C(j)}
    \frac{1}{L}\frac{1}{2^{\ell_i}}
    \sum_{r=0}^{2^{\ell_i}-1}
    \theta_\varepsilon\operatorname{Had}_{\ell_i}(v_{\ell_i}(x),r)\operatorname{Had}_{\ell_i}(v_i,r)\\
    &=
    \sum_{(\ell_i,v_i)\in\mathcal C(j)}
    \boldsymbol 1\{v_{\ell_i}(x)=v_i\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Recall that $v_{\ell_i}(x)=\left\lfloor\frac{x-1}{2^{L-\ell_i}}\right\rfloor$ denotes the index of the unique depth-$\ell_i$ node containing $x$. Since the intervals in $\mathcal C(j)$ are disjoint and their union is $[1,j]$, the last expression equals $\boldsymbol 1\{x\leq j\}$, proving \textbf{(I)}.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:dyadic-prefix-cover-cdf}, the cover contains at most one node at every depth. Since the user samples only one depth, at most one term in the definition of $Z_u$ can be nonzero. Furthermore, $Y_u$ and every Hadamard entry belong to $\{-1,+1\}$. Hence $|Z_u|\leq L/\theta_\varepsilon$, proving \textbf{(II)}.

For the fixed dataset $X$, the variables $Z_1,\cdots,Z_n$ are independent, although they need not be identically distributed. Let $\widetilde Z_j = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum\limits_{u\in [n]}Z_u$. By \textbf{(I)},
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde Z_j\mid X\right]
    &=
    \frac{1}{n}\sum_{u\in[n]}\mathbb{E}[Z_u\mid X_u]\\
    &=
    \frac{1}{n}\sum_{u\in[n]}\boldsymbol 1\{X_u\leq j\}
    =Z_j(X).
\end{align*}
Using \textbf{(II)} and Hoeffding's inequality for independent bounded random variables implies:
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{P}\left[
        |\widetilde Z_j-Z_j(X)|>\alpha\mid X
    \right]
    &\leq
    2\exp\left(
        -\frac{2n^2\alpha^2}{\sum_{u\in[n]}(2L/\theta_\varepsilon)^2}
    \right)\\
    &=
    2\exp\left(
        -\frac{n\theta_\varepsilon^2\alpha^2}{2L^2}
    \right).
\end{align*}
Taking a union bound over the at most $D$ prefix queries results in:
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{P}\left[
        \max_{j\in\{1,\cdots,D-1\}}
        |\widetilde Z_j-Z_j(X)|>\alpha\mid X
    \right]
    \leq
    2D\exp\left(
        -\frac{n\theta_\varepsilon^2\alpha^2}{2L^2}
    \right).
\end{align*}
Since $\theta_\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon/4$ for $0<\varepsilon\leq1$ and $L\leq3\log D$ for $D\geq2$, the right-hand side is at most $\beta$ under the stated sample-size condition. Finally, clipping $\widetilde Z_j$ to $[0,1]$ cannot increase its distance from $Z_j(X)\in[0,1]$, which concludes the proof for \textbf{(2)}.


Finally, we prove \textbf{(3)}. Each user constructs and stores its report in $O(1)$ time and $O(1)$ words, so user preprocessing takes $O(n)$ total time. For each prefix query, the server constructs the dyadic cover in $O(L)$ time and scans the $n$ reports in $O(n)$ time, using a cover representation indexed by depth. Thus, one query takes $O(n+\log D)$ time, and $q\geq1$ queries take $O(q(n+\log D))$ total time including user preprocessing, without materializing the full tree or a length-$D$ vector.

Putting together, we are ready to prove the utility and privacy guarantee of our LDP synthetic data generator in Algorithm~\ref{alg:synthetic-dataset}.

Set $D:=\lceil2R/\delta\rceil+2$ and $L:=\lceil\log_2D\rceil$. Since $\delta\leq R/2$, we have $R/\delta\geq2$, and $\frac{2R}{\delta}
    \leq D
    \leq\frac{4R}{\delta}$.
In particular, $\log D=O(\log(R/\delta))$ and $L=O(\log(R/\delta))$. 

Let the real line partitioning $B_1=(-\infty,-R), B_j=[c_{j-1},c_j), B_D=[R,+\infty)$, be as in Definition~\ref{def:quantization-map}, where $c_0= -R-\delta$, $c_j= -R+\min\{(j-1)\delta,2R\}$, $c_D=R+\delta$.

We first prove \textbf{(1)}.
Each user computes the interval index $\widetilde X_u=\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(X_u)$ locally and applies the randomizer in Algorithm~\ref{alg:hierarchical-ldp-local-randomizer}. For any two real inputs, their indices belong to $[D]$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:prefix_oracle} proves $\varepsilon$-local differential privacy. The protocol is non-interactive, and all remaining steps are post-processing of the transcripts $\operatorname{Tr}$.

Now, we prove \textbf{(2)}.
Let $\widetilde P=\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(P)$, and write $F_{\widetilde P}(x)= \mathbb{P}_{X\sim \widetilde P}(X\leq x)$ for its CDF. By Definition~\ref{def:quantization-map}, $F_{\widetilde P}$ is continuous and linear on each interval $[c_{j-1},c_j]$, and
\begin{align*}
    F_{\widetilde P}(c_j)
    =
    \sum_{i=1}^j P(B_i)
    =
    \mathbb{P}_{X\sim P}\left[\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(X)\leq j\right],
    \qquad j\in\{0,\cdots,D\}.
\end{align*}
Let $\widetilde X=\left(\widetilde X_1,\cdots,\widetilde X_n\right)$ be the discretized dataset, and define its prefix frequencies by
\begin{align*}
    Z_j\left(\widetilde X\right)
    :=
    \frac{1}{n}\sum_{u\in[n]}\boldsymbol 1\left\{\widetilde X_u\leq j\right\},
    \qquad j\in\{0,\cdots,D\}.
\end{align*}
Let $\alpha':=\alpha/2$. Conditioned on any realization of $\widetilde X$, Theorem~\ref{thm:prefix_oracle}, with accuracy parameter $\alpha'/2$ and failure probability $\beta/2$, gives
\begin{align*}
    \max_{j\in\{0,\cdots,D\}}
    \left|\widehat Z_j-Z_j\left(\widetilde X\right)\right|
    \leq\frac{\alpha'}{2}
\end{align*}
with probability at least $1-\beta/2$ over the local randomizers, provided that
$n
    \geq
    \frac{288(\log D)^2}{\varepsilon^2(\alpha'/2)^2}
    \log\left(\frac{4D}{\beta}\right)$.

Since the prefix-oracle guarantee holds for every fixed dataset, the same probability bound holds over the joint sampling and local randomness.

Furthermore, since $X_1,\cdots,X_n$ are drawn i.i.d. from $P$, we have $\mathbb{E}_{X\sim P}\left[Z_j\left(\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(X)\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{X\sim P}\left[Z_j\left(\widetilde X\right)\right]=F_{\widetilde P}(c_j)$. Using Hoeffding's inequality, followed by a union bound over the prefixes, gives
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{P}\left[
        \max_{j\in\{0,\cdots,D\}}
        \left|Z_j\left(\widetilde X\right)-F_{\widetilde P}(c_j)\right|>\frac{\alpha'}{2}
    \right]
    &\leq
    2D\exp\left(-\frac{n(\alpha')^2}{2}\right)\leq\frac{\beta}{2}
\end{align*}
 Combining the two bounds, with probability at least $1-\beta$ over the sampling and local randomizers, the triangle inequality gives
\begin{align*}
    \left|\widehat Z_j-F_{\widetilde P}(c_j)\right|
    &\leq
    \left|\widehat Z_j-Z_j\left(\widetilde X\right)\right|
    +\left|Z_j\left(\widetilde X\right)-F_{\widetilde P}(c_j)\right|\leq\frac{\alpha'}{2}+\frac{\alpha'}{2}
    =\alpha'
\end{align*}
for every $j\in\{0,\cdots,D\}$ simultaneously. We condition on this event throughout the rest of the proof. Furthermore, $\widehat Z_0=0$ and $\widehat Z_D=1$, but the $\widehat Z_j$'s are not necessarily monotone.

For each $\ell\in[M]$, the binary search maintains the condition $A=\widehat Z_a<u_\ell\leq\widehat Z_b=B$. At termination, $a=b-1$, so that $B-A>0$ and $\lambda_\ell=(u_\ell-A)/(B-A)\in(0,1]$. Thus, the interpolation step is well-defined, and it holds that
    $u_\ell
    =(1-\lambda_\ell)\widehat Z_{b-1}+\lambda_\ell\widehat Z_b$, and
    $F_{\widetilde P}(z_\ell)
    =(1-\lambda_\ell)F_{\widetilde P}(c_{b-1})+\lambda_\ell F_{\widetilde P}(c_b)$.
Therefore, the simultaneous accuracy bound implies:
\begin{align*}
    |F_{\widetilde P}(z_\ell)-u_\ell|
    &\leq
    (1-\lambda_\ell)|F_{\widetilde P}(c_{b-1})-\widehat Z_{b-1}|
    +\lambda_\ell|F_{\widetilde P}(c_b)-\widehat Z_b|\leq\alpha'.
\end{align*}

Write $F_H$ for the CDF of $H$. Fix any $x\in\mathbb{R}$. By the preceding accuracy bound and monotonicity of $F_{\widetilde P}$, if $z_\ell\leq x$, then $u_\ell
    \leq F_{\widetilde P}(z_\ell)+\alpha'
    \leq F_{\widetilde P}(x)+\alpha'$.
Similarly, if $z_\ell>x$, then $u_\ell
    \geq F_{\widetilde P}(z_\ell)-\alpha'
    \geq F_{\widetilde P}(x)-\alpha'$.
    
Since $u_\ell=(\ell-1/2)/M$, these inequalities give
\begin{align*}
    F_H(x)
    &\leq
    \frac{1}{M}\sum_{\ell\in[M]}
    \boldsymbol 1\{u_\ell\leq F_{\widetilde P}(x)+\alpha'\}
    \leq F_{\widetilde P}(x)+\alpha'+\frac{1}{2M},\\
    1-F_H(x)
    &\leq
    \frac{1}{M}\sum_{\ell\in[M]}
    \boldsymbol 1\{u_\ell\geq F_{\widetilde P}(x)-\alpha'\}
    \leq 1-F_{\widetilde P}(x)+\alpha'+\frac{1}{2M}.
\end{align*}
Putting together, gives
\begin{align*}
    \|F_H-F_{\widetilde P}\|_\infty
    =\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|F_H(x)-F_{\widetilde P}(x)|
    \leq \alpha'+\frac{1}{2M}
    \leq\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}
    =\alpha.
\end{align*}
This argument does not require the estimated prefix values to be monotone.

Finally, to see \textbf{(3)}, computing the interval index and generating the local randomized response each take $O(1)$ time per user. Thus, user preprocessing takes $O(n)$ total time. Generating the synthetic dataset requires $M$ binary searches, each with at most $L$ prefix queries and constant additional work per step. By Theorem~\ref{thm:prefix_oracle}, each query takes $O(n+L)$ time. Thus, the total run-time of the algorithm is
\begin{align*}
    O\left(n+ML(n+L)\right)
    =O\left(
        \frac{1}{\alpha}
        \log\left(\frac{R}{\delta}\right)
        \left(n+\log\left(\frac{R}{\delta}\right)\right)
    \right),
\end{align*}
where we used $M=\lceil1/\alpha\rceil=O(1/\alpha)$ and $L=O(\log(R/\delta))$.
The assumption $\delta\leq R/2$ is used only to simplify the bounds.

We now prove the guarantees of our LDP agnostic learning algorithm.

Let $C\geq3$ be a universal constant for which Proposition~\ref{prop:deterministic-robust-general-merging} holds, and set
$t=(C+1)(d+1)s$,
    and $M=\left\lceil\frac{2t}{\alpha}\right\rceil$.

Let $\widetilde P:=\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(P)$ with support $I_{R,\delta}:=[-R-\delta,R+\delta]$.
Using Theorem~\ref{thm:ldp-synthetic}, with accuracy parameter $\alpha/(2t)$, the stated sample size suffices to obtain a synthetic multiset $\mathcal{Z}\subseteq I_{R,\delta}$ of size $M$, with empirical probability measure $H$, such that
\begin{align}
    \left\|F_H-F_{\widetilde P}\right\|_\infty
    \leq\frac{\alpha}{2t}
    =\frac{\alpha}{2(C+1)(d+1)s} \label{eq:main:1}
\end{align}
with probability at least $1-\beta$. We condition on this event for the rest of the proof.

Run the deterministic algorithm from Proposition~\ref{prop:deterministic-robust-general-merging} on $I_{R,\delta},\mathcal{Z},s,d$, with parameter $\eta=\alpha$.
Since $\widetilde P$ has a density supported on $I_{R,\delta}$, the Kolmogorov bound~\eqref{eq:main:1} satisfies the requirement of the proposition for $P^\star=\widetilde P$.
Thus, the output of the deterministic piece-wise polynomial learner $Q\in\mathcal{P}_{Cs,d}$ is nonnegative and satisfies:
\begin{align}
    \|Q-\widetilde P\|_1
    \leq
    4\inf_{P'\in\mathcal{P}_{s,d}}\|P'-\widetilde P\|_1+4\alpha. \label{eq:main:2}
\end{align}
Fix any $F\in\mathcal{F}$. We can write:
\begin{align}
    \|F-\widetilde P\|_1
    &\leq
    \left\|F-\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(F)\right\|_1
    +\left\|
        \operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(F)
        -\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(P)
    \right\|_1\\
    &\leq
    \operatorname{Disc\text{-}Err}_{R,\delta}(\mathcal{F})
    +\|F-P\|_1,
\end{align}
where the first inequality follows from the triangle inequality,
and the second follows from Definition~\ref{def:discretization-error}
and the fact that $\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}$ is a Markov
operator and does not increase $L_1$ distance
(see Lemma~\ref{lem:forward-bound}).

Consequently, we can write:
\begin{align*}
    \inf_{P'\in\mathcal{P}_{s,d}}\|P'-\widetilde P\|_1
    &\leq
    \inf_{P'\in\mathcal{P}_{s,d}}\|P'-F\|_1+\|F-\widetilde P\|_1\\
    &\leq
    \operatorname{Poly\text{-}Approx}_{s,d}(\mathcal{F})
    +\operatorname{Disc\text{-}Err}_{R,\delta}(\mathcal{F})+\|F-P\|_1.
\end{align*}
Putting together with \eqref{eq:main:2}, we get:
\begin{align}
    \|Q-\widetilde P\|_1
    \leq
    4\alpha+4\operatorname{Poly\text{-}Approx}_{s,d}(\mathcal{F})
    +4\operatorname{Disc\text{-}Err}_{R,\delta}(\mathcal{F})+4\|F-P\|_1. \label{eq:main:3}
\end{align}

Since $Q$ is a piece-wise polynomial supported on the bounded interval $I_{R,\delta}$, it is integrable. Let $m_0:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)\,dx < \infty$, and define the normalized output distribution by:
\begin{align*}
    \widehat P:=
    \begin{cases}
        Q/m_0,&m_0>0,\\
        \operatorname{Unif}(I_{R,\delta}),&m_0=0.
    \end{cases}
\end{align*}
If $m_0>0$, then
\begin{align}
    \|\widehat P-\widetilde P\|_1
    \leq
    \|\widehat P-Q\|_1+\|Q-\widetilde P\|_1
    =|1-m_0|+\|Q-\widetilde P\|_1\leq2\|Q-\widetilde P\|_1. \label{eq:main:4}

If $m_0=0$, then $\|Q-\widetilde P\|_1=1$ and $\|\widehat P-\widetilde P\|_1\leq2$. Therefore, in both cases, we have $\|\widehat P-\widetilde P\|_1\leq2\|Q-\widetilde P\|_1$.
Note that $\widehat P$ remains a piece-wise polynomial, since
normalization preserves the number and degrees of the polynomial
pieces, and the fallback density $\operatorname{Unif}(I_{R,\delta})$
has at most three pieces on $\mathbb{R}$.

where the first two inequalities follow from the triangle
inequality, the third follows because
$\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}$ is a Markov operator and
does not increase $L_1$ distance
(see Lemma~\ref{lem:forward-bound}), and the last follows
from Definition~\ref{def:discretization-error}.

Finally, we can write:
\begin{align*}
    \|\widehat P-P\|_1
    &\leq
    \|\widehat P-\widetilde P\|_1+\|\widetilde P-P\|_1\\
    &\leq

    +8\operatorname{Poly\text{-}Approx}_{s,d}(\mathcal{F}),

Taking the infimum over $F\in\mathcal{F}$ gives

We prove that bounding the polynomial approximation and discretization errors of mixture classes reduces to bounding the corresponding errors for the single-component classes.

Fix an arbitrary mixture $F=\sum_{i\in[m]}w_iF_i\in{m}\text{-}\text{mix}(\mathcal{F})$.

First we prove \textit{(1)}. By Definition~\ref{def:quantization-map},
$\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(F)
    =
\sum_{i\in[m]}w_i\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(F_i)$.
Therefore, the triangle inequality gives
\begin{align*}
    \left\|\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(F)-F\right\|_1
    &\leq
    \sum_{i\in[m]}w_i
\left\|\operatorname{Disc}_{R,\delta}(F_i)-F_i\right\|_1\\
    &\leq
    \operatorname{Disc\text{-}Err}_{R,\delta}(\mathcal{F}).
\end{align*}
Taking the supremum over $F\in{m}\text{-}\text{mix}(\mathcal{F})$ proves one direction. The reverse inequality follows from $\mathcal{F}\subseteq{m}\text{-}\text{mix}(\mathcal{F})$.

Next, we show \textit{(2)}. Fix $\eta>0$. For each $i\in[m]$, choose $P_i\in\mathcal{P}_{s,d}$ satisfying
\begin{align*}
    \|P_i-F_i\|_1
    \leq
    \operatorname{Poly\text{-}Approx}_{s,d}(\mathcal{F})+\eta,
\end{align*}
and set $P:=\sum_{i\in[m]}w_iP_i$.
Each $P_i$ has at most $s-1$ breakpoints. Their common refinement therefore has at most $m(s-1)+1$ interval pieces, and taking weighted sums does not increase the degree of the underlying polynomials. Since $P$ is nonnegative and integrable, we have $P\in\mathcal{P}_{m(s-1)+1,d}$. Moreover,
\begin{align*}
    \|P-F\|_1
    &\leq
    \sum_{i\in[m]}w_i\|P_i-F_i\|_1\leq
    \operatorname{Poly\text{-}Approx}_{s,d}(\mathcal{F})+\eta.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{align*}

    \leq
    \operatorname{Poly\text{-}Approx}_{s,d}(\mathcal{F})+\eta.
\end{align*}
Taking the supremum over $F\in{m}\text{-}\text{mix}(\mathcal{F})$ and letting $\eta$ tend to zero proves the result.

Next, we prove a bound on the discretization error of log-concave distributions.

Fix an arbitrary distribution $F\in\mathcal{L}(R,\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max})$, and let $f$ be its density, with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$.

Define $g(x):=\sigma f(\mu+\sigma x)$ for $x\in\mathbb{R}$.
The function $g$ is log-concave, and the change of variables $y=\mu+\sigma x$ gives
\begin{align*}
    \int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\,dx
    &=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\,dy=1,\\
    \int_{\mathbb{R}}xg(x)\,dx
    &=\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(y-\mu)f(y)\,dy=0,\\
    \int_{\mathbb{R}}x^2g(x)\,dx
    &=\frac{1}{\sigma^2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(y-\mu)^2f(y)\,dy=1.
\end{align*}
Thus, $g$ is a log-concave probability density with mean zero and variance one. By Lemma 5.5(a) of \citet{lovasz2007geometry}, $\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}g(x)\leq1$.
Since $f(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma}g\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)$, we obtain $\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}f(x)
    =\frac{1}{\sigma}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}g(z)
    \leq\frac{1}{\sigma}$.

We next bound the total tail mass. For $X\sim f$, the random variable $Y=(X-\mu)/\sigma$ has density $g$. Since $\mathbb{E}[Y^2]=1$, Lemma 5.17 of \citet{lovasz2007geometry} gives, for every $t>1$,
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{P}\left[|X-\mu|>t\sigma\right]
    =\mathbb{P}\left[|Y|>t\right]
    \leq e^{1-t}.
\end{align*}
Since $|\mu|\leq R$ and $\sigma\leq\sigma_{\max}$, it follows that
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{P}[|X|>T]
    &\leq
    \mathbb{P}\left[
        \frac{|X-\mu|}{\sigma}
        >\frac{T-R}{\sigma_{\max}}
    \right]
    \leq
    \exp\left(1-\frac{T-R}{\sigma_{\max}}\right)
    =\frac{\eta}{4}.
\end{align*}
Therefore, $F(\mathbb{R}\setminus[-T,T])\leq\eta/4$.

Let $D=J+2$, and let $B_j$'s and $c_j$'s be the intervals and endpoints from Definition~\ref{def:quantization-map}, with parameters $(T,\delta)$.
For each $j\in\{2,\cdots,D-1\}$, let $h_j=c_j-c_{j-1}\leq\delta$ and
\begin{align*}
    \bar f_j:=\frac{1}{h_j}\int_{B_j}f(y)\,dy.
\end{align*}
For every $x\in B_j$, we have
\begin{align*}
    |f(x)-\bar f_j|
    &\leq
    \frac{1}{h_j}\int_{B_j}|f(x)-f(y)|\,dy\\
    &\leq
    \sup_{y\in B_j}f(y)-\inf_{y\in B_j}f(y).
\end{align*}

Since $f$ is nonnegative and unimodal, the sum of its binwise differences between the supremum and infimum is bounded by its total increase plus its total decrease, each of which is at most $\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}f(x)$. Therefore,
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{j=2}^{D-1}
    \left(\sup_{x\in B_j}f(x)-\inf_{x\in B_j}f(x)\right)
    \leq2\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}f(x)
    \leq\frac{2}{\sigma}.
\end{align*}
Integrating over $x\in B_j$ and summing over the internal intervals yields
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{j=2}^{D-1}\int_{B_j}|f(x)-\bar f_j|\,dx
    &\leq
    \delta
    \sum_{j=2}^{D-1}
    \left(\sup_{x\in B_j}f(x)-\inf_{x\in B_j}f(x)\right)\leq
    \frac{2\delta}{\sigma}
    \leq\frac{2\delta}{\sigma_{\min}}.
\end{align*}
The original and discretized measures restricted to the two tails each have total mass $F(\mathbb{R}\setminus[-T,T])$. Their $L_1$ difference is therefore at most twice this mass. Combining the two bounds gives
\begin{align*}
    \left\|\operatorname{Disc}_{T,\delta}(F)-F\right\|_1
    &\leq
    \frac{2\delta}{\sigma_{\min}}+\frac{\eta}{2}=\frac{4T}{J\sigma_{\min}}+\frac{\eta}{2}
    \leq\eta.
\end{align*}
Taking the supremum over $F$ proves the result.

    K_\sigma=1+\left\lfloor\frac{\log(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})}{\log16}
    \right\rfloor,
    K=\max\{K_\mu,K_\sigma\}$,  and $M=\min\{m,K\}$.

Taking $C_0\geq100(1+\log16)$, the assumption gives
$K
    \geq
    \max\left\{
        \frac{R}{\sigma_{\min}},
        \frac{\log(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})}{\log16}
    \right\}
    \geq
    \frac{\Lambda}{1+\log16}
    \geq100m$.
Thus, $M=m$ and $2\leq M\leq K/100$, as required by the sparse estimation lower bound in 
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower-sparse}.

We construct $K$ Gaussian components $g_1,\ldots,g_K\in\mathcal{G}(R,\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max})$ and disjoint measurable sets $B_1,\ldots,B_K\subset\mathbb{R}$ such that $g_j(B_j)\geq2/3$ for all $j\in[K]$.
Let $\Phi$ denote the standard normal CDF. We use the larger of the following two constructions.

\textit{(1) Location Gaussians.}
For $j\in[K_\mu]$, set
$\mu_j=(2j-K_\mu-1)\sigma_{\min}, g_j=\mathcal{N}(\mu_j,\sigma_{\min}^2)$, and $B_j=[\mu_j-\sigma_{\min},\mu_j+\sigma_{\min})$. Then, the means satisfy $|\mu_j|\leq(K_\mu-1)\sigma_{\min}\leq R$. The set $B_j$'s are disjoint, and
\begin{align*}
    g_j(B_j)=2\Phi(1)-1>\frac{2}{3}.
\end{align*}

\textit{(2) Scale Gaussians.}
For $j\in[K_\sigma]$, set
$\sigma_j=16^{j-1}\sigma_{\min},
    g_j=\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_j^2)$, and
    $B_j=\{x:\sigma_j/4\leq|x|<4\sigma_j\}$.
We have $\sigma_j\in[\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max}]$. Then, the sets $B_j$'s are disjoint, and
\begin{align*}
    g_j(B_j)=2\bigl(\Phi(4)-\Phi(1/4)\bigr)>\frac{2}{3}.
\end{align*}

For $p\in\Delta_K$, write $f_p:=\sum_{j\in[K]}p_jg_j$, and let $\gamma=1/3$.
Since the sets are disjoint, $\sum_{i\neq j}g_j(B_i)\leq1-g_j(B_j)$.
Thus, the reverse triangle inequality gives, for any $p,q\in\Delta_K$,
\begin{align*}
    \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(f_p,f_q)
    &\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in[K]}
    \left|\sum_{j\in[K]}(p_j-q_j)g_j(B_i)\right|\\
    &\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in[K]}|p_j-q_j|
    \left(g_j(B_j)-\sum_{i\neq j}g_j(B_i)\right)\\
    &\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in[K]}|p_j-q_j|
    \bigl(2g_j(B_j)-1\bigr)\\
    &\geq\gamma\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(p,q).
\end{align*}

We reduce learning $\mathcal{S}_{K,M}$ to learning the Gaussian mixture class.
Given a discrete sample $Y=j$, the corresponding user independently draws $X\sim g_j$ and applies the local randomizer of the Gaussian mixture learner to $X$.
For any such randomizer $Q$ and measurable message event $A$, integrating its privacy inequality gives
\begin{align*}
    \int Q(A\mid x)\,dg_i(x)
    \leq e^\varepsilon\int Q(A\mid x)\,dg_j(x)
\end{align*}
for all $i,j\in[K]$. Hence, the resulting protocol remains non-interactive $\varepsilon$-LDP.
If $Y\sim p\in\mathcal{S}_{K,M}$, then $X\sim f_p$, and $f_p$ has at most $M\leq m$ Gaussian components.

Let $\widehat f$ be the output of the learner, and define
$\widehat p\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{q\in\mathcal{S}_{K,M}}
    \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widehat f,f_q)$.
A minimizer exists because $\mathcal{S}_{K,M}$ is compact and the objective is continuous in $q$.

On the event $\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widehat f,f_p)\leq\alpha$, we have
\begin{align*}
    \gamma\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widehat p,p)
    &\leq\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(f_{\widehat p},f_p)\\
    &\leq\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(f_{\widehat p},\widehat f)+\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widehat f,f_p)\\
    &\leq2\alpha.
\end{align*}
This decoding is post-processing and returns an $M$-sparse probability vector.
The resulting discrete learner has accuracy $2\alpha/\gamma$ and succeeds with probability at least $0.9$.
Choosing $\alpha_0$ so that $2\alpha_0/\gamma\leq c_0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:lower-sparse} therefore gives
$n=\Omega\left(

    \right)$.
Finally, since $K=\Theta(\Lambda)$ and $K/m\geq100$, $\log(K/m)=\Theta\left(\log\left(e+\frac{\Lambda}{m}\right)\right)$,
which gives the stated bound.

This ensures that
\[
    \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widehat P,\widetilde P)
    \leq
    \min_{G\in\mathcal{C}}\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widehat P,G)+\alpha.
\]

Let $P$ be the sampling distribution.
With probability at least $1-\beta$, the guarantee of $\mathcal{A}$ gives
\[
    \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widehat P,P)
    \leq C\inf_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(P,F)+\alpha.
\]
Condition on this event. Fix any $F^\star\in\mathcal{F}$.
Since $\mathcal{C}$ is an $\alpha$-cover, there exists $G^\star\in\mathcal{C}$
such that $\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(F^\star,G^\star)\leq\alpha$.
Therefore,
\begin{align*}
    \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widetilde P,P)
    &\leq
    \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widetilde P,\widehat P)+\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widehat P,P)\\
    &\leq
    \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(G^\star,\widehat P)+\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widehat P,P)+\alpha\\
    &\leq
    \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(F^\star,\widehat P)+\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widehat P,P)+2\alpha\\
    &\leq
    \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(F^\star,P)+2\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(\widehat P,P)+2\alpha\\
    &\leq
    (2C+1)\operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(P,F^\star)+4\alpha.
\end{align*}
Taking the infimum over $F^\star\in\mathcal{F}$ proves the accuracy
guarantee. The additional errors account for the cover and
the approximate minimization.

The conversion computes $|\mathcal{C}|$ distance approximations
and makes $O(|\mathcal{C}|)$ additional comparisons, giving
$O(|\mathcal{C}|(T_{\mathrm{dist}}+1))$ additional running time.
It uses only the output of $\mathcal{A}$, so it requires no additional
samples and preserves privacy and non-interactivity by
post-processing.

For a target additive error $\tau\in(0,1)$, applying this
construction with $\alpha=\tau/4$ and a cover of radius
$\tau/4$ gives a proper $(2C+1,\tau,\beta)$-agnostic learner
using $n_{\mathcal{A}}(\tau/4,\beta)$ samples.

<!-- claim-2: body_md -->
\label{lemma:weightcover}
Let $\alpha\in(0,1]$.
There is an $\alpha$-cover for the $(m-1)$-dimensional probability simplex
$\Delta_m=\left\{(w_1,w_2,\ldots,w_m)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^m\,:\,
\sum_{i\in[m]}w_i=1\right\}$
with respect to $\ell_\infty$ of size at most
$\lceil1/\alpha\rceil^m$.
<!-- end claim-2 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Partition $[0,1]$ into $\lceil1/\alpha\rceil$ intervals of length at most $\alpha$, and use their Cartesian products to partition $[0,1]^m$.
For every cell that intersects $\Delta_m$, select one point from the intersection.
Any two points in the same cell have $\ell_\infty$ distance at most $\alpha$, so the selected points form an $\alpha$-cover of $\Delta_m$.
There are at most $\lceil1/\alpha\rceil^m$ cells.

<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
We prove the cover construction for GMMs.

Let $f=\sum_{i\in[m]}w_i\mathcal{N}(\mu_i,\sigma_i^2)$ be an arbitrary member of the class.
We construct a cover by discretizing the mixture weights, means, and standard deviations.

For the weights, let $C_w$ be an $\alpha/m$-cover of $\Delta_m$ in $\ell_\infty$, of size at most $\lceil m/\alpha\rceil^m$, as given by Claim~\ref{lemma:weightcover}.

For the means, let $\mathcal{C}_\mu$ be a grid on $[-R,R]$ containing both endpoints, with consecutive points at distance at most $\alpha\sigma_{\min}/2$.
When $R=0$, take $\mathcal{C}_\mu=\{0\}$.
The grid can be chosen to have at most
$1+\lceil4R/(\alpha\sigma_{\min})\rceil$ points, and every $\mu\in[-R,R]$ has a grid point $\tilde\mu$ satisfying
$|\mu-\tilde\mu|\leq\alpha\sigma_{\min}/4$.

For the standard deviations, take
\begin{align*}
    \mathcal{C}_\sigma
    =
    \left\{
        \sigma_{\min}(1+\alpha/4)^j:
        j=0,\ldots,
        \left\lfloor
            \frac{\log(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})}
                 {\log(1+\alpha/4)}
        \right\rfloor
    \right\}.
\end{align*}
For every $\sigma\in[\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max}]$, there is a grid point $\tilde\sigma$ such that
$\tilde\sigma\leq\sigma<(1+\alpha/4)\tilde\sigma$, and hence
$|\log(\sigma/\tilde\sigma)|\leq\alpha/4$.

Let $\mathcal{C}$ contain all mixtures formed from weights in $C_w$, means in $\mathcal{C}_\mu$, and standard deviations in $\mathcal{C}_\sigma$.
Every member of $\mathcal{C}$ belongs to the target class.

For univariate Gaussians, varying the mean and then the standard deviation gives
\begin{align*}
    \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}\left(
        \mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2),
        \mathcal{N}(\tilde\mu,\tilde\sigma^2)
    \right)
    \leq
    \frac{|\mu-\tilde\mu|}{2\sigma}
    +\left|\log\left(\frac{\sigma}{\tilde\sigma}\right)\right|.
\end{align*}

Choose the corresponding grid parameters for $f$, and write
$\tilde f=\sum_{i\in[m]}\tilde w_i
\mathcal{N}(\tilde\mu_i,\tilde\sigma_i^2)\in\mathcal{C}$.
Then
\begin{align*}
    \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}(f,\tilde f)
    &\leq
    \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in[m]}|w_i-\tilde w_i|
    +\sum_{i\in[m]}\tilde w_i
        \operatorname{d_{\textsc{TV}}}\left(
            \mathcal{N}(\mu_i,\sigma_i^2),
            \mathcal{N}(\tilde\mu_i,\tilde\sigma_i^2)
        \right)\\
    &\leq
    \frac{\alpha}{2}
    +\sum_{i\in[m]}\tilde w_i
        \left(
            \frac{\alpha\sigma_{\min}/4}{2\sigma_{\min}}
            +\frac{\alpha}{4}
        \right)\\
    &=\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{3\alpha}{8}
    \leq\alpha.
\end{align*}
Finally,
\begin{align*}
    |\mathcal{C}|
    &\leq |C_w||\mathcal{C}_\mu|^m|\mathcal{C}_\sigma|^m\\
    &\leq
    \left\lceil\frac{m}{\alpha}\right\rceil^m
    \left(1+\left\lceil
        \frac{4R}{\alpha\sigma_{\min}}
    \right\rceil\right)^m
    \left(1+\left\lfloor
        \frac{\log(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})}
             {\log(1+\alpha/4)}
    \right\rfloor\right)^m\\
    &\leq
    \left[
        \frac{c m}{\alpha}
        \left(1+\frac{R}{\alpha\sigma_{\min}}\right)
        \left(1+\frac{\log(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})}{\alpha}\right)
    \right]^m
\end{align*}
for a universal constant $c>0$.
