<!-- head-1: body_md -->
\emph{Let $S$ be a signature of arity at least $2$ that is neither a
binary signature with an odd multiplicity nor a permutation of $\ensuremath{(2k,1,1)}$.  Then $N(S)$ admits a Hamiltonian cycle.}
\medskip
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{prelim:stutter-parity}
For every signature $S$, the graph $G(S)$ is bipartite with classes
the words of even and of odd inversion count.  Every stutter lies in
the even class, and
\[
  \#\{p : \operatorname{inv}(p) \text{ even}\}
  - \#\{p : \operatorname{inv}(p) \text{ odd}\}
  = \#\{\text{stutters of } S\}.
\]
In particular the two bipartition classes of $N(S)$ have equal size.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
A swap transposes two adjacent \emph{distinct} colors and changes
the inversion count by exactly $\pm 1$; hence every edge of $G(S)$
joins the two parity classes.

A stutter has even inversion count.  Its positions fall into its
doubles and, when $n$ is odd, the trailing single.  The two positions
of one double hold equal colors and form no inversion.  For two
different doubles $cc$ and $dd$, the four pairs of positions with one
position in each are all inversions if the earlier double carries the
larger color, and none are otherwise; so the two doubles contribute
$0$ or $4$.  Likewise a double and the trailing single contribute $0$
or $2$.

For the census, define $\iota(p)$, for a non-stutter $p$, as the word
obtained by transposing the colors of the \emph{first} mixed domino.
The first mixed domino of $\iota(p)$ is the same domino (earlier
dominoes are untouched and double), so $\iota$ is an involution on the
non-stutters, and it transposes two adjacent distinct colors, so it
flips the inversion parity.  The non-stutters therefore cancel in the
signed count, leaving one $+1$ for each stutter; removing the
stutters, all in the even class, balances the classes of $N(S)$
exactly.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{prelim:stutter-distance}
Two distinct stutters are at distance at least $4$ in $G(S)$.  In
particular, no word is adjacent to two distinct stutters.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Label the occurrences of each color $c$ in a word from left to right
as $c_1, c_2, \ldots, c_{k_c}$.  A swap exchanges two adjacent
occurrences of distinct colors, so it keeps every label and reverses
the relative order of exactly one pair of labeled occurrences.  For
words $p$ and $q$ let $\delta(p,q)$ be the number of pairs of labeled
occurrences whose relative order differs between $p$ and $q$.  A swap
changes $\delta(\cdot, q)$ by exactly one, so the distance between $p$
and $q$ is at least $\delta(p,q)$.

Let $s \ne s'$ be stutters.  In both, the occurrences $c_{2t-1},
c_{2t}$ of each color $c$ fill one double, and when $n$ is odd the
trailing position holds the last occurrence of the odd color.  Two
occurrences of the same color, or two involving the trailing
position, have the same relative order in $s$ and $s'$.  The
occurrences of two different doubles are ordered as the doubles are,
so each pair of doubles contributes $0$ or $4$ to $\delta(s,s')$.
Since $s \ne s'$, some pair of doubles is ordered differently, and
$\delta(s,s') \ge 4$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{prelim:restriction}
If $N(S)$ admits a Hamiltonian path or cycle, then $G(S)$ admits a
Lehmer path with spur tips among the stutters.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
A stutter of arity at least two is adjacent in $G(S)$ to a non-stutter:
either two consecutive dominoes carry different colors, or the last
domino and the trailing color differ; flipping that shifted domino
gives a non-stutter.  Choose for every stutter one such
adjacent non-stutter and attach the stutter to it as a spur tip.  By
Lemma~\ref{prelim:stutter-distance} no non-stutter is adjacent to two
stutters, so each chosen non-stutter carries exactly one spur.
Traversing the Hamiltonian path or cycle of $N(S)$ and making an
excursion to each attached stutter therefore visits every stutter
exactly once and every spur base exactly twice.

<!-- conjecture-1: body_md -->
\label{prelim:trichotomy}
Let $S$ be a signature of arity at least $2$ that is neither a binary
signature with an odd multiplicity nor a permutation of $\ensuremath{(2k,1,1)}$.  Then $N(S)$ admits a Hamiltonian cycle.
<!-- end conjecture-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prelim:path-only-2k11}
For every $k \ge 1$, $N(2k,1,1)$ admits a Hamiltonian path but no
Hamiltonian cycle.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The signature has two odd multiplicities, so it has no stutters,
and $N(2k,1,1)$ equals $G(2k,1,1)$.  A Hamiltonian path is constructed by
Verhoeff \cite[\S5.4]{verhoeff2017}; the construction is also
recovered by the general path theory of
Stachowiak \cite{stachowiak1992}, whose main theorem covers every
signature with at least two odd multiplicities.

The absence of a Hamiltonian cycle is Stachowiak's
Lemma~12 \cite{stachowiak1992}; we give a short parity argument.  Let
$A$ be the set of words in which $1$ precedes $2$, and $B$ the set in
which $2$ precedes $1$.  The only edges between $A$ and $B$ are the
$2k+1$ swaps $0^i 1 2 0^{2k-i} \sim 0^i 2 1 0^{2k-i}$,
$0 \le i \le 2k$.  Their ends in $A$ have inversion count
$2(2k-i)$, so they all lie in the even class.  Replacing the $2$ by
a $1$ maps $A$ bijectively onto the words of $\ensuremath{(2k,2)}$ and
preserves the inversion count, because within $A$ the $1$ and the
$2$ form no inversion.  By Lemma~\ref{prelim:stutter-parity}, $A$
therefore has $k+1$ more words in the even class than in the odd
class, one for each stutter of $\ensuremath{(2k,2)}$ (the double $11$ can
occupy any of its $k+1$ dominoes).  Suppose a Hamiltonian
cycle uses $2t$ edges between $A$ and $B$.  It then covers $A$ by $t$
vertex-disjoint paths, each with both ends in $A$ on such an edge,
hence both ends even.  A path in a bipartite graph whose ends lie in
the same class has exactly one more vertex in that class than in the
other, so $t = k+1$.  The cycle would need $2k+2$ edges between $A$
and $B$, but there are only $2k+1$.  The impossibility halves of this proposition and of
Theorem~\ref{inherited:binary-theorem} are used only to show that the
exception set of Theorem~\ref{assembly:theorem} is minimal; the
constructions of this article consume only the existence halves.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{prelim:parallel-edges}
In $G(S)$, edges $a \sim b$ and $c \sim d$ are \emph{parallel} if
also $a \sim c$ and $b \sim d$ are edges.  The edges $a \sim c$ and
$b \sim d$ are then called the \emph{cross edges} of the parallel
pair, and the
four vertices $a,b,c,d$ with the four edges form a $4$-cycle.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{prelim:gluing-lemma}
Let $C_1$ and $C_2$ be vertex-disjoint cycles in $G(S)$ carrying
parallel edges $a \sim b \in C_1$ and $c \sim d \in C_2$.  Then
replacing these two edges by the cross edges $a \sim c$ and
$b \sim d$ yields a single cycle on $V(C_1) \cup V(C_2)$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
$C_1$ minus $a \sim b$ is a Hamiltonian path in $C_1$ from $a$ to
$b$, and $C_2$ minus $c \sim d$ is one in $C_2$ from $c$ to $d$.
Concatenating $a \sim c$, the path from $c$ to $d$ in $C_2$,
$d \sim b$, and the path from $b$ to $a$ in $C_1$ gives a cycle on
all vertices of both.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{prelim:tree-gluing}
Let a graph have its vertex set partitioned into units, each carrying
a Hamiltonian cycle $H_U$.  Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a family of gadgets
forming a tree on the units, such that the unit edges of all gadgets
are pairwise distinct and a digon carries at most one of them.  Then
\[
  \bigcup_U H_U \;-\; \{\text{all unit edges}\} \;+\;
  \{\text{all cross edges}\}
\]
(as multisets; a digon with a unit edge contributes no remaining
edge) is a Hamiltonian cycle of the graph.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Add the gadgets in any order; in a tree, the two units of a gadget
are joined by no path of other gadgets, so they always lie in
different components.  Keep the invariant that every component of the
constructed subgraph is spanned by a single cycle, which contains
every unit edge of a later gadget inside it.  When the next gadget
with unit edges $e_1 = v_0 \sim v_1$ and $e_2 = v_2 \sim v_3$ joins
two components, delete $e_1$ from the cycle of the first and $e_2$
from the cycle of the second: each deletion turns a cycle into a
Hamiltonian path of that component, with ends $v_0,v_1$ respectively
$v_2,v_3$.  Adding the cross edges $v_1 \sim v_2$ and
$v_3 \sim v_0$ splices the two paths into one cycle.  Later unit
edges survive by distinctness; cross edges join different units, so
they lie on no $H_U$ and are never deleted.  A cross edge is added only once: a
gadget whose cross edge had been added earlier would have both its
units inside the component created by that earlier gadget, contrary
to the tree property.  At the end one cycle spans all units.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{cube:lemma1}
\mbox{}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},itemsep=0pt]
  \item The words with a given skeleton form a set of $2^r$
  words, where $r$ is the number of mixed dominoes.  The even
  edges make this set a copy of the hypercube $Q_r$ (the graph on the
  $2^r$ bit strings of length $r$, adjacent when they differ in one
  coordinate), with one coordinate per mixed domino, recording the order
  of its content.  We
  call the set a \emph{hypercube}, its coordinate directions the
  \emph{directions} of the hypercube, and $r$ its \emph{dimension}; the
  direction of a mixed domino $j$ is denoted $j$ as well.  Every odd
  edge joins two \emph{different} hypercubes.
  \item If all multiplicities of $S$ are even, no skeleton has exactly
  one mixed domino; hence $N(S)$ is the disjoint union of the hypercubes of
  dimension $r \ge 2$, together with the odd edges between them.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
(a) Words with the same skeleton differ only in the order of the two
colors within each mixed domino, and $s_{2i}$ flips domino $i$; the
content of each mixed domino can independently be ordered in two ways,
giving $2^r$ words, and the even swaps $s_{2i}$ at mixed dominoes are
the flips of the $r$ coordinates of a hypercube.  An odd swap at
position $2i+1$ exchanges two colors $b \ne c$ in
$\ldots a b \mid c d \ldots$ and replaces the domino contents
$\{a,b\},\{c,d\}$ by $\{a,c\},\{b,d\}$, and $\{a,c\} \ne
\{a,b\}$, so the skeleton changes and the swap leads to a different
hypercube.

(b) Every occurrence of a color $c$ lies either in a double $cc$ or in
a mixed domino containing $c$, so $k_c = 2 \cdot \#\{\text{doubles }cc\}
+ \#\{\text{mixed dominoes containing } c\}$.  If all $k_c$ are even,
every color lies in an even number of mixed dominoes, which is impossible
if there is exactly one mixed domino.  A non-stutter is a word
with at least one mixed domino; by what we just proved, in the all-even
case it has at least two, so it lies in a hypercube of dimension $r \ge 2$.
The hypercubes with $r = 0$ are the stutters, and those with $r = 1$ do not
occur; the even edges stay inside a hypercube and the odd edges join
different hypercubes, so the non-stutter graph is exactly the union of the
hypercubes with $r \ge 2$ plus the odd edges.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{cube:stitch}
Let shifted domino $i$ of $p$ be mixed (so $p_{2i+1} \ne p_{2i+2}$),
and let domino $j$ be mixed with $j \notin \{i, i+1\}$ (so domino $j$
is disjoint from the positions of the shifted domino).  Then the flips
$s_{2i+1}$ and $s_{2j}$ move disjoint positions and commute, so
\[
  q(p;i,j) \;=\; (\,p,\; s_{2j}(p),\;
    s_{2i+1}(s_{2j}(p)),\; s_{2i+1}(p)\,)
\]
is a $4$-cycle of the graph, a \emph{stitch}.  Its two edges in
direction $j$ are its \emph{hypercube edges}, lying in the hypercubes
of $p$ and of $s_{2i+1}(p)$; its two odd edges $s_{2i+1}$ are its
\emph{cross edges}.  Domino $j$ stays mixed in all four words, so all
four are non-stutters and a stitch is a gadget
(Lemma~\ref{prelim:tree-gluing}) with the two hypercubes as units and
its hypercube edges as unit edges.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{cube:rung}
Let $C$ be a hypercube and let $l(C)$ be the index of its rightmost
mixed domino.
The edges of $C$ in direction $l(C)$ are its \emph{rungs}.  A word
$p$ is \emph{anchored} if every shifted domino is double, that is, if
it has no odd edge.  Stutters and anchored words are independent
notions: $0011$ is a stutter with an odd edge, and $0110$ is anchored
but not a stutter.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{cube:lemmaN}
No stitch contains an anchored word.  All four words of $q(p;i,j)$
carry two distinct colors at positions $2i+1, 2i+2$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The words of $q(p;i,j)$ arise from $p$ by the swaps $s_{2j}$, which
does not touch positions $2i+1, 2i+2$, and $s_{2i+1}$, which exchanges
the two colors at those positions.  Since $p$ has distinct colors
there, so has every word of the stitch, and none of them is
anchored.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{cube:boustrophedon}
Let $r \ge 2$, let $d$ be a direction of the hypercube $Q_r$, and let
$f$ be an edge of $Q_r$ not in direction $d$.  Then $Q_r$ has a
Hamiltonian cycle that contains all $2^{r-1}$ edges in direction $d$,
and $f$.  We call it a \emph{boustrophedon cycle in direction $d$}.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Induction on $r$.  $Q_2$ is a $4$-cycle, which contains every edge.
For $r \ge 3$ write $Q_r = Q_{r-1} \times \{0,1\}$, with the last
factor in direction $d$, and let $f = \{(u,0),(u',0)\}$ (exchange the
two levels if necessary).  By the lemma for $Q_{r-1}$, in the
direction of the edge $uu'$ and with any edge in another direction,
$Q_{r-1}$ has a Hamiltonian cycle that contains $uu'$; write it as
$u_0 u_1 \cdots u_{2^{r-1}-1}$ with $u_0 = u$ and $u_1 = u'$.  The
walk
\[
  (u_0,1),\ (u_0,0),\ (u_1,0),\ (u_1,1),\ (u_2,1),\ (u_2,0),\ \ldots
\]
uses the edge in direction $d$ at every $u_t$, and it uses the edge
$u_t u_{t+1}$ at level $0$ for even $t$ and at level $1$ for odd $t$.
Since $2^{r-1}$ is even, the walk closes up; it is Hamiltonian and
contains $f$ (the step $t=0$).

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{tools:lemmaM}
Let $S$ be a signature of even length $2m \ge 2$.  There exists a set
$M(S)$ of odd edges of $G(S)$ that is a \emph{matching} (the edges are
pairwise vertex-disjoint) and, viewed as edges between the hypercubes of
$V(S)$ (the $0$-dimensional ones, the stutters, included), a
\emph{spanning tree} of those hypercubes.  The induction of the proof
establishes a strengthening of this claim, as is common: the set can be
chosen so that the following invariant holds.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0pt]
  \item[\textup{(INV)}] for every color $z$ of $S$, some word
  ending in $z$ lies on no edge of $M(S)$.  We call such a
  word \emph{free}.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Induction on $m$.  If $m = 1$, or $S$ has one color, there is a
single hypercube and $M(S) = \emptyset$.  Let $m \ge 2$, let
$a < b$ be the two smallest colors of $S$, and let $R$ be
the set of the other colors.

\emph{Blocks and groups.}  For colors $c \ne d$ put
\[
  X_{cd} = \{\, t \cdot cd,\ t \cdot dc \;:\; t \in V(S - c - d) \,\},
\]
and if $k_c \ge 2$ put
$X_{cc} = \{\, t \cdot cc : t \in V(S - c - c) \,\}$.  These are the
\emph{blocks}; the block $X_{cd}$ depends only on the pair $\{c,d\}$,
so $X_{cd} = X_{dc}$.
The content of the last domino is part of the skeleton, so each hypercube
lies in exactly one block, and $t \mapsto t \cdot cd$ is a bijection
between the hypercubes of $V(S-c-d)$ and the hypercubes of $X_{cd}$.  Odd
edges of $G(S-c-d)$ never move the last color of $t$; that color
is the \emph{group} of $t \cdot cd$.

\emph{Lifts.}  For every non-double block $X_{cd}$ and every group
$g$, choose an orientation $o(cd,g) \in \{cd, dc\}$ (fixed below).
Lift every edge $\{t_1, t_2\}$ of $M(S-c-d)$ whose two
words have group $g$ to $\{t_1 \cdot o, t_2 \cdot o\}$, where
$o = o(cd,g)$, and lift every edge of $M(S-c-c)$ to
$\{t_1 \cdot cc,\, t_2 \cdot cc\}$.  Inside each block the lifts form
a matching and a spanning tree of the block's hypercubes, by the induction
hypothesis.

\emph{Bridges.}  For $c \ne d$, the odd swap at position $2m-3$ maps
$v \cdot c \cdot dc'$ to $v \cdot d \cdot cc'$, and so joins the block
$X_{dc'}$ to the block $X_{cc'}$.  Add the following bridges; each is
\emph{owned} by the block named first:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item[\textup{(T1)}, \textup{(T2)}] for $d \in R$: let $t_d = v_d \cdot d$ be a free word
  of $V(S-a-b)$ (INV).  Add $v_d d \cdot ba \sim
  v_d b \cdot da$, joining $X_{ad}$ to
  $X_{ab}$, and $v_d d \cdot ab \sim v_d a
  \cdot db$, joining $X_{bd}$ to $X_{ab}$;
  \item[\textup{(T3)}] for $c < d$ in $R$: add $v a \cdot cd \sim v c \cdot
  ad$, joining $X_{cd}$ to $X_{ad}$, for any
  $v \in V(S-a-c-d)$;
  \item[\textup{(T4)}] for $k_c \ge 2$: put $y_c = a$ if $c \ne a$, and
  $y_a = b$.  Let $v \cdot y_c$ be a free word of
  $V(S-c-c)$ (INV), and add $v y_c \cdot cc \sim v c \cdot y_c c$,
  joining $X_{cc}$ to $X_{y_c c}$.
\end{itemize}
Every block other than $X_{ab}$ owns exactly one bridge.  Its
bridge leads to $X_{ab}$, or to a block $X_{ad}$ whose
own bridge leads to $X_{ab}$, so the bridges form a spanning
tree of the blocks.  Lifts stay inside blocks, and the bridges join
pairwise different pairs of blocks, so no two edges of $M(S)$ join
the same two hypercubes, and the lifts together with the bridges form a
spanning tree of the hypercubes (Figure~\ref{tools:block-tree}).

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:all-even}
Let $S$ be a signature of arity at least $2$ in which every multiplicity
is even.  Then $N(S)$ admits a Hamiltonian cycle.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{all-even:lemmaI}
Every hypercube meets at most one descent or root stitch.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
The hypercubes involved are those of the words $v_F$, $v'_F$,
$v_{ab}$, $v'_{ab}$, so it suffices to show that these words
have pairwise distinct skeletons.  In such a word let $\Lambda$
be the rightmost mixed domino and $\Lambda'$ the domino before it,
both taken as unordered pairs.  Both, and the doubles after $\Lambda$, can be
read off the skeleton.  By construction:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0pt]
  \item in $v_F$, $\Lambda' = \Lambda = \{a,b\}$;
  \item in $v_{ab}$, $\Lambda' = cc$ with $c \notin \Lambda = \{a,b\}$;

  exactly the color $y$ in common;
  \item in $v'_{ab}$, $\Lambda' = \{c,b\}$ and $\Lambda = \{c,a\}$
  have exactly the color $c$ in common.
\end{itemize}

skeleton.  The
$v_F$ lie in distinct families $F$, so they are pairwise distinct, and
so are the $v_{ab}$.  For a word of the last two kinds, let $y$
be the color common to $\Lambda'$ and $\Lambda$, let $e$ be the other
color of $\Lambda$, let $e'$ be the other color of $\Lambda'$
($e' = y$ if $\Lambda'$ is a double), and let $u'$ be the trailing
word after $\Lambda$.  If the word is $v'_F$, then
$F = F((yy)u', \{e,e'\})$.  If it is $v'_{ab}$, then $u'$ is
empty and $(c,a,b) = (y,e,e')$.  So the $v'_F$ are pairwise distinct,
and so are the $v'_{ab}$.  Finally, $v'_F = v'_{ab}$ would give
$F = F(cc, ab)$ with $c \notin \{a,b\}$ and $e = a < b$.  But $e$ is
the largest color of $\{a,b\} \setminus \{c\} = \{a,b\}$, which is
$b$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{one-odd:assembly}

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{assembly:theorem}
Let $S$ be a signature of arity at least $2$ that is neither a binary
signature with an odd multiplicity nor a permutation of $\ensuremath{(2k,1,1)}$.  Then $N(S)$ admits a Hamiltonian cycle.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Dispatch on the number of odd multiplicities of $S$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},itemsep=2pt]
  \item None: all multiplicities are even, and
  Theorem~\ref{thm:all-even} supplies a Hamiltonian cycle of $N(S)$.
  This includes the all-even binary signatures, so no clause of the
  binary classification is needed for existence here.
  \item One: the arity is at least $3$ by the first hypothesis (a
  binary signature with one odd multiplicity is excluded), and
  Theorem~\ref{thm:one-odd} supplies the cycle.
  \item At least two: such a signature has no stutter, so
  $N(S) = G(S)$.  Its arity is at least $3$: arity $2$ would mean two
  odd multiplicities, the excluded binary family.  It is not a
  permutation of $\ensuremath{(2j,1,1)}$ by the second hypothesis, so the
  inherited theorem (Theorem~\ref{inherited:stachowiak}) supplies a
  Hamiltonian cycle of $G(S) = N(S)$.
\end{enumerate}

\label{assembly:exact}
At each excluded family, $N(S)$ admits a Hamiltonian path but no
Hamiltonian cycle: for the binary signatures with an odd multiplicity
this is Theorem~\ref{inherited:binary-theorem}, and for the
permutations of $\ensuremath{(2k,1,1)}$ it is
Proposition~\ref{prelim:path-only-2k11}.  So the exception set of
Theorem~\ref{assembly:theorem} is minimal: outside it a cycle exists,
and at each member a path exists and no cycle does.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{assembly:lehmer}
By Verhoeff's reduction (Theorem~\ref{prelim:restriction}), a
Hamiltonian cycle or path of $N(S)$ yields a Lehmer path of $G(S)$.
Combining Theorem~\ref{assembly:theorem} with the paths of
Remark~\ref{assembly:exact} therefore gives Lehmer's word
conjecture in full: every neighbor-swap graph $G(S)$ admits a walk
that visits every word, with spur tips exactly at the
stutters.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{assembly:wellfounded}
The dispatch above is not recursive, and Theorems~\ref{thm:all-even}
and~\ref{thm:one-odd} are not mutually inductive.
Theorem~\ref{thm:all-even} is a direct construction: given the
matching of Lemma~\ref{tools:lemmaM} --- whose proof is an induction
on the number $m = n/2$ of dominoes, and nothing else --- the stitches
are glued along an explicit spanning tree, with no recursion on the
length.  Theorem~\ref{thm:one-odd} reduces a one-odd signature to the
strictly shorter all-even signature $S^x$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:all-even}) and to the inherited two-odd theorem for
its blocks; the family $\ensuremath{(2j,2,1)}$ likewise reduces to
Theorem~\ref{thm:all-even} on $\ensuremath{(2j,2)}$ and to the inherited
theorem on $\ensuremath{(2j-1,2,1)}$, both strictly shorter.  The dependency
is therefore acyclic --- the one-odd proof uses the all-even proof
and never the converse --- every chain of appeals is finite, and the
inherited theorem introduces no recursion from this article's side.

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
\label{assembly:machine}
The assembled claim has its own standing gate, independent of the
producers' gates: it re-derives $N(S)$ from the definition, checks
that the dispatch of the proof is exhaustive and disjoint and that its
exception predicate agrees with the classification of
Section~\ref{prelim:trichotomy-section}, and then runs the route end
to end at every signature in the gate's window --- the all-even
construction, the one-odd construction (whose two-odd blocks are
built by the Stachowiak implementation), and, at two or more odd
multiplicities, Stachowiak's construction itself --- verifying every
returned cycle vertex by vertex and edge by edge against the gate's
own graph.  Appendix~\ref{appendix:machine-verification} states the
exact claims and parameters.
