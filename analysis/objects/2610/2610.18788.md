\label{geo-square}
For $\Omega=\widetilde\omega I-F_D$,
\[
 \frac{\Omega^2}{\widetilde\omega^2}
 =\operatorname{diag}(\mathcal A_0+i\mathcal B_0,
                       \mathcal A_1+i\mathcal B_1,
                       \mathcal A_2+i\mathcal B_2),
\]
where
\begin{equation}\label{geo-AB}
 \mathcal A_j=I-\tfrac12(H_jS_j+S_jH_j)+K_j,\qquad
 \mathcal B_j=H_j+S_j.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Here $\widetilde\omega^2=4\omega_X\wedge\omega_{FS}$ and
$\Omega_{jj}=(I+iH_j)\omega_X+2(I+iS_j)\omega_{FS}$. Thus the diagonal
square, before including adjacent off-diagonal products, is
\[
 \tfrac12\big[(I+iH_j)(I+iS_j)+(I+iS_j)(I+iH_j)\big]
 =I+i(H_j+S_j)-\tfrac12\{H_j,S_j\}.
\]
For $\gamma_i=\xi_i\wedge\alpha$, graded multiplication gives
\[
 \gamma_i\wedge\gamma_i^\dagger
 =-2i\xi_i\wedge\xi_i^\dagger\wedge\omega_{FS},\qquad
 \gamma_i^\dagger\wedge\gamma_i
 =2i\xi_i^\dagger\wedge\xi_i\wedge\omega_{FS}.
\]
After division by $\widetilde\omega^2$ these contribute exactly
\eqref{geo-K}. A pure base or fiber $(1,1)$-form wedges to zero with each
mixed $\xi_i\wedge\alpha$, and the two consecutive mixed forms wedge to
zero because $\alpha\wedge\alpha=0$; the adjoint products behave likewise.
Products involving both $R_1$ and $P_2$ vanish by fiber form degree. These
facts remove every off-diagonal square block and retain the ordered middle
anticommutator. No commutation of the endomorphisms has been used.

below, with $O=0$, $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$,

\[

 (a,b,\epsilon)&A&B&Q(z)\\\hline
 (0,0,0)&O&O&0\\(0,1,0)&O&D&0\\(0,2,0)&O&I&0\\
 (1,0,0)&D&O&0\\(1,1,0)&D&D^\perp&0\\

 (2,0,0)&I&O&0\\(2,1,1)&I&D&z_0^2\\
 (2,2,0)&I&J&0\\(2,2,1)&I&I&z_0^2+z_1^2
\end{array}
\]
If $a=2,b=1$, the original lower map has the form
$\psi=v\sigma$, with saturated line $L=\mathcal O(2)v$,
$\operatorname{im}\psi=L(-p)$, and
$\operatorname{div}(\phi|_L)=q_0$ for simple divisor points $p,q_0$.
Then $\operatorname{div}Q=p+q_0$, allowing $p=q_0$.

Put $C=A^TB$. Expanding $z^TCz$ shows that $Q\equiv0$ exactly when
$C+C^T=0$. Every skew $2\times2$ matrix is $\lambda J$, of rank zero
if $\lambda=0$ and rank two otherwise. If a matrix has rank zero the
composition vanishes. If the coefficient ranks are $(1,2)$ or $(2,1)$,
$C$ has rank one, so its quadratic form is nonzero. In the $(1,1)$ case
write $A=u\alpha_c^T$, $B=v\beta_c^T$; then
\[
 C=\alpha_c(u^Tv)\beta_c^T,\qquad
 Q=(u^Tv)(\alpha_c^Tz)(\beta_c^Tz).
\]
Since the polynomial ring has no zero divisors, $Q=0$ precisely when
$u^Tv=0$. If both ranks are two, $C$ is invertible and can be skew or
have a nonzero symmetric part. This proves exhaustion, and the table
proves nonemptiness. The seven impossible triples are the five zero-rank
pairs with $\epsilon=1$, together with $(1,2,0)$ and $(2,1,0)$.

For $a=2,b=1$, factor the actual matrix $B=v\beta_c^T$ with nonzero
vectors. The nonzero section $\sigma=\beta_c^Tz$ has one simple zero

fiber map does vanish at $p$. Also
$\phi|_L=(A^Tv)^Tz\ne0$, since $A$ is invertible. This section of
$\operatorname{Hom}(L,E_0)=\mathcal O(1)$ has one simple zero $q_0$.
Their product is nonzero with divisor $p+q_0$. For any prescribed
$p,q_0$, choose nonzero linear sections $\sigma,\tau$ with those
divisors and put
$v=(A^T)^{-1}\operatorname{coeff}(\tau)$,
$B=v\operatorname{coeff}(\sigma)^T$. Thus no ordered pair is omitted.
For $A=I$, $v=e_0$, $\sigma=z_0$ gives $p=q_0=[0:1]$, while
$\sigma=z_1$ gives $p=[1:0]\ne q_0=[0:1]$.
Inverse scalar rescaling of $v,\sigma$ does not alter the original map
or the divisors. No metric splitting along $L$ or base change is used.

Repeated primary roots also remain: $A=I$ and
$B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ have
both ranks two and $Q=z_0^2$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{geo-metric-covariance}
An allowed holomorphic chain isomorphism gives a bijection of all smooth
positive metric triples, preserves the ordered equation and strict
calibration, and also intertwines the invariant equation on $\mathcal X$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Transport metrics by
$h'_j=(g_j^{-1})^*h_jg_j^{-1}$, with inverse
$h_j=g_j^*h'_jg_j$. Then $g_j:(E_j,h_j)\to(E_j,h'_j)$ is an isometry.
For an arrow $\Phi:E_s\to E_t$,
\[
 \Phi'=g_t\Phi g_s^{-1},\qquad
 (\Phi')^{\dagger_{h'}}=g_s\Phi^{\dagger_h}g_t^{-1}.
\]
Hence each of $P_1,R_1,P_2,R_2$ is conjugated at its own vertex. The
Chern connections and curvatures obey
\[
 D'_j=g_jD_jg_j^{-1},\quad F'_j=g_jF_jg_j^{-1},\quad
 H'_j=g_jH_jg_j^{-1},\quad
 \xi'_i=g_{i-1}\xi_i g_i^{-1}.
\]
The last formula follows by applying the transported source and target
connections; their derivative terms cancel. The allowed automorphisms
are constant, so no local $\partial g_j$ term is needed. Wedge
composition and metric adjoints give
\[
 K'_j=g_jK_jg_j^{-1},\quad S'_j=g_jS_jg_j^{-1},\quad
 \mathcal A'_j=g_j\mathcal A_jg_j^{-1},\quad
 \mathcal B'_j=g_j\mathcal B_jg_j^{-1}.
\]
In particular the entire anticommutator is conjugated, with no change in
order. The calibration is also conjugated, and for $w'\ne0$,
\[
 h'_j(\mathcal C'_jw',w')
 =h_j(\mathcal C_jg_j^{-1}w',g_j^{-1}w').
\]
This proves positivity in the transported metric.
The induced $\widetilde g=\bigoplus_jp_1^*g_j\otimes I_{\mathcal O(2j)}$
intertwines \eqref{geo-dolbeault}, commutes with the vertical $SU(2)$
action, and fixes the K\"ahler form. It conjugates $F_D$ and
$(\widetilde\omega I-F_D)^2$, including their Hermitian real and imaginary
parts, proving invariant transfer of the equation and calibration.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{geo-bridge}
Restriction from $SL_2(\mathbb C)$ to $SU(2)$ gives a bijection on coherent
equivariant subobjects of every bundle \eqref{geo-dolbeault}. The underlying
image sheaf, monomorphism, quotient, nonsaturation, and nonreduced structure
are preserved.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
An equivariant monomorphism is determined by its invariant image
$\mathcal S\subset\mathcal E$: injectivity forces its linearization to
be the restricted one. An invariant image and its quotient inherit their
linearizations. It is therefore enough to extend invariance of
$\mathcal S$ from $K=SU(2)$ to its complexification $G=SL_2(\mathbb C)$.

For $\xi\in\mathfrak{su}(2)$, consider $\exp(z\xi)$, $z\in\mathbb C$.
Since $\mathcal X$ is projective, GAGA
\cite[\S12, Theorems 2--3]{SerreGAGA} places the coherent quotient
$q:\mathcal E\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal E/\mathcal S$ at a point of the
projective Quot scheme with its Hilbert polynomial, by Grothendieck's
construction \cite[Theorem 5.1]{NitsureQuot}. The given
$G$-linearization induces a holomorphic action on this scheme. The orbit
map $z\mapsto\exp(z\xi)\cdot[q]$ is constant on the real axis by
$K$-invariance. The holomorphic identity theorem, applied in local
coordinates and then using separatedness, makes it constant on
$\mathbb C$. Equality of Quot points gives equality of kernels. Thus
$\mathcal S$ is invariant under $\exp(i\xi)$ for every $\xi$.
Polar decomposition $G=K\exp(i\mathfrak{su}(2))$ proves $G$-invariance.
The inherited $G$-linearization is unique and gives the quotient
linearization in the unchanged exact coherent sequence. No saturation or
local freeness on the surface was required.

One can also see the extension in a finite-dimensional representation.
Take the naturally linearized ample bundle
$L_a=p_1^*\mathcal O(1)\otimes p_2^*\mathcal O(1)$. For $n$ sufficiently
large, Serre global generation, as recalled in
\cite[\S2, Lemma 2.1(c)]{NitsureQuot}, gives
\[
 W=H^0(\mathcal X,\mathcal S\otimes L_a^n)
       \subset H^0(\mathcal X,\mathcal E\otimes L_a^n)=V.
\]
This is a $K$-invariant complex subspace of a finite-dimensional
holomorphic $G$-representation. Differentiating real one-parameter
invariance gives $d\rho(\xi)W\subset W$; complex linearity gives
$d\rho(i\xi)W\subset W$. Polar decomposition then makes $W$
$G$-invariant. The equivariant evaluation map
$W\otimes L_a^{-n}\to\mathcal E$ has image exactly $\mathcal S$.
Both arguments preserve all finite-support quotient data and apply to
every boundary map, whose induced linearization is unchanged.

$T_j=\chi I+\eta S_j$ and $L_j=\chi I+\eta H_j$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{geo-two-lyapunov}
 \tfrac12\{T_j,\mathcal C_j\}=I+S_j^2+K_j>0,\qquad
 \tfrac12\{T_j,L_j\}=I+\eta^2K_j>0,
 \qquad T_j>0,\quad L_j>0.
\end{equation}
The metric-linearized principal symbol is, up to the common curvature
sign, $g^{z\bar z}\zeta_z\bar\zeta_z\{T_j,u_j\}/2$ at each vertex.
Every strictly calibrated solution is therefore positively strongly
elliptic.

The phase equation gives $\mathcal A_j=\chi\mathcal C_j$ and

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{geo-euler-exclusion}
No original pair in the stratum $(2,2,0)$ admits a smooth strictly
calibrated solution.

Suppose such a solution exists. Full pointwise rank and zero composition

\[
 0\longrightarrow I_b:=\operatorname{im}\psi=\ker\phi\simeq\mathcal O(1)
 \longrightarrow E_1\longrightarrow\mathcal Q\simeq\mathcal O(3)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Let $P$ be the actual $h_1$-orthogonal projection onto $I_b$ and
$P^\perp=I-P$. For smooth positive functions $x,y$ the rank-one arrow
products are
\begin{equation}\label{geo-euler-products}
 P_1=xI_{E_0},\quad R_1=xP^\perp,\qquad
 P_2=yP,\quad R_2=yI_{E_2}.
\end{equation}
Thus $S_1=(1+y)P+(1-x)P^\perp$. The quotient eigenvalue of $T_1$ is
$(5-6x)/\sqrt{37}$, so its positivity implies
\begin{equation}\label{geo-euler-x}
 0<x<5/6.
\end{equation}
This is the metric-orthogonal splitting, not a holomorphic splitting or
an assumption that $H_1$ preserves $I_b$.

Let $\beta=P^\perp D_1^{1,0}P|_{I_b}$ be the second fundamental form.
The Chern connection in this smooth splitting is
\[
 D_1=\begin{pmatrix}D_{I_b}&-\beta^\dagger\\
                         \beta&D_{\mathcal Q}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Gauss--Codazzi in the fixed curvature convention
\cite[Ch.~V, \S14, equations (14.6)--(14.7)]{Demailly2012} gives
$P^\perp H_1P^\perp=H_{\mathcal Q}-\beta\beta^\dagger$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{geo-euler-gauss}
 \int_X\operatorname{tr}(P^\perp H_1)\omega_X
       =6\pi-\int_X|\beta|^2\omega_X.
\end{equation}
Differentiating $\phi P=0$ and $P^\perp\psi=0$ yields
$\xi_1P=-\phi\beta$ and $P^\perp\xi_2=\beta\psi$. By
\eqref{geo-euler-products},
\begin{equation}\label{geo-euler-derivatives}
 |\xi_1P|^2=x|\beta|^2,\qquad
 |P^\perp\xi_2|^2=y|\beta|^2.
\end{equation}

Set $V=2\pi$, $\ell_Q=\operatorname{tr}(P^\perp L_1)$ and
$k_Q=\operatorname{tr}(P^\perp K_1)$. The upper identity in
\eqref{geo-endpoint-integrals}, with $R_1=xP^\perp$, is
\begin{equation}\label{geo-euler-top}
 \frac{9V}{\sqrt{37}}
       =\int_Xx\ell_Q\omega_X+\frac\eta2\int_X|\xi_1|^2\omega_X.
\end{equation}
Project the second identity of \eqref{geo-two-lyapunov} at vertex one
onto $P^\perp$. Although $L_1$ need not preserve the splitting, $T_1$
does; cyclicity of trace gives
\begin{equation}\label{geo-euler-middle}
 [\chi+\eta(1-x)]\ell_Q=1+\eta^2k_Q,\qquad
 x\ell_Q=\tfrac56\ell_Q-\tfrac1\eta-\eta k_Q.
\end{equation}
Both kinetic halves are retained:
\begin{equation}\label{geo-euler-kinetics}
 k_Q=\tfrac12\bigl(|\xi_1P^\perp|^2+|P^\perp\xi_2|^2\bigr),\qquad
 \int_X\ell_Q\omega_X
       =\frac{17V-6\int_X|\beta|^2\omega_X}{\sqrt{37}},
\end{equation}
where the last identity uses \eqref{geo-euler-gauss} and
$L_1=\chi I+\eta H_1$.

\eqref{geo-euler-top}, splitting
$|\xi_1|^2=|\xi_1P|^2+|\xi_1P^\perp|^2$. The quotient half of the
upper derivative cancels its actual half in $k_Q$. The remaining equality
is
\[
 9V=8V-5\int_X|\beta|^2\omega_X
           +3\int_X|\xi_1P|^2\omega_X
           -3\int_X|P^\perp\xi_2|^2\omega_X.
\]
Using \eqref{geo-euler-derivatives} gives
\begin{equation}\label{geo-euler-contradiction}
 2\pi=\int_X(3x-3y-5)|\beta|^2\omega_X.
\end{equation}
Since $x<5/6$ and $y>0$, the coefficient is strictly less than $-5/2$.
The right side is nonpositive, also when $\beta=0$, while the left side
is positive. This excludes arbitrary metric triples, without a
representative normalization or base automorphism.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{geo-main}
For every $A,B\in\operatorname{Mat}_2(\mathbb C)$, on the fixed original
chain and background of \eqref{geo-chain}, the following are equivalent:
\begin{align*}
 &\mathrm{Ex}(A,B),\\
 &\mathcal E_{A,B}\text{ is }SU(2)\text{-equivariantly }Z\text{-stable},\\
 &\mathcal E_{A,B}\text{ is }SU(2)\text{-equivariantly }Z\text{-semistable},\\
 &\mathcal E_{A,B}\text{ is }SU(2)\text{-equivariantly }Z\text{-polystable},\\
 &\operatorname{rank}A=2\quad\text{and}\quad Q\not\equiv0.
\end{align*}
Thus all three stability predicates and smooth strictly calibrated
existence hold on $(2,1,1)$ and $(2,2,1)$, and fail on each of the nine
other nonempty triples of Lemma~\ref{geo-domain}. In particular there
are no strictly semistable objects in this fixed family.

The proof separates the gradient bound from the symbol gaps. A bounded
scalar potential first localizes the energy near a putative gradient
blow-up. On each boundary face, rescaling then forces a constant value
limit. To contradict blow-up, however, the argument must also preserve a
nonzero derivative at the rescaling center; the exact projected equations
and scalar transforms below provide that step, including both extreme
mixed ratios. Only after gradients are bounded do we estimate the gaps
at their separate global minima.
