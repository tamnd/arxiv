<!-- maintheorem-1: body_md -->
There exist a one-dimensional local Gorenstein Pr\"ufer domain $D$ and
nonzero elements $a,b\in D$ with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The integral closure $B$ of $D$ in $\operatorname{Frac} D$ is not Pr\"ufer.
In fact, $(a,b)T$ is not invertible for any integral overring $T$ of $D$.
\item For every integer $r\ge1$, each nonzero ideal of $D$ generated
by $r$ elements is a cycle of a two-periodic totally acyclic complex
whose terms are $D^r$.
\item Put $z=b/a$ and $S=B[z,z^{-1}]$. For every injective cogenerator
$E_0$ of $B$-modules,
\[
 \operatorname{Gfd}_DB=\operatorname{Gfd}_DS=0,
 \qquad \operatorname{fd}_DB=\infty,
 \qquad \operatorname{Tor}_1^B(E_0,S)\ne0.
\]
Thus $S$ is Gorenstein flat over $D$ but not over $B$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end maintheorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:fgv}
An ideal is \emph{divisorial} if $I=I_v$. We call $A$ an
\emph{FGV-domain} if every nonzero finitely generated integral ideal
is divisorial. Scaling shows that the same then holds for nonzero
finitely generated fractional ideals. This condition does not require
all ideals of $A$ to be divisorial.
A ring is \emph{coherent} if every finitely generated ideal is finitely
presented, and a domain is \emph{Pr\"ufer} if every nonzero finitely
generated ideal is invertible.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:normalization-gf}
A module is Gorenstein flat if it is a cycle in an
exact, doubly infinite complex of flat modules which remains exact
after tensoring with every injective module. Its Gorenstein flat
dimension is the least length of a resolution by such modules, or
infinity if there is no finite resolution; see \cite{CFH}. We put $\operatorname{Gfd}_A0=0$ and write $\operatorname{wGgldim}(A)=\sup_M\operatorname{Gfd}_AM$.
A domain is \emph{Gorenstein Pr\"ufer}, or $G$-Pr\"ufer, if it is
coherent and $\operatorname{wGgldim}(A)\le1$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:criterion}
For a domain $A$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ is $G$-Pr\"ufer.
\item $A$ is coherent and FGV.
\item Every torsion-free $A$-module is Gorenstein flat.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:inverse}
Let $A$ be a coherent domain and let $I$ be a nonzero finitely
generated fractional ideal. Then $I^{-1}$ is a finitely generated
fractional ideal. If $S\subseteq A\setminus\{0\}$ is multiplicatively
closed, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localv}
 (I^{-1})_S=(A_S:I_S),\qquad (I_v)_S=(I_S)_v,
\end{equation}
where the final $v$-operation is taken over $A_S$. Consequently,
every localization of a coherent FGV-domain is a coherent FGV-domain.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
After scaling, assume $I=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\subseteq A$, with each
$a_i\ne0$. Then $I^{-1}=\bigcap_i a_i^{-1}A$. A common nonzero
multiple of this intersection is an intersection of finitely many
principal integral ideals, which is finitely generated by coherence.
This proves the first assertion.

For the first equality in \eqref{eq:localv} follows from \cite[Proposition 1.5.13(3)]{WangKim2024}.
Apply it again to the finitely generated fractional
ideal $I^{-1}$ to obtain the second equality. Coherence localizes,
and every finitely generated ideal of $A_S$ is the extension of a
finitely generated ideal of $A$. The last assertion follows.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:ultraproduct-ideal-membership}
Let $\mathcal U$ be an ultrafilter on a set $\Lambda$, let
$(A_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ be a family of commutative rings
with identity, and put $A=\prod_{\mathcal U}A_\lambda$.
Fix a positive integer $r$ and elements
$a_i=[a_{i,\lambda}]\in A$ for $1\le i\le r$. Set
$I=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)A$ and
$I_\lambda=(a_{1,\lambda},\ldots,a_{r,\lambda})A_\lambda$.
For every $z=[z_\lambda]\in A$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:membership}
 z\in I
 \quad\text{if and only if}\quad
 z_\lambda\in I_\lambda
 \quad\text{for $\mathcal U$-almost all $\lambda\in\Lambda$}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Suppose $z=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_i b_i$ for elements
$b_i=[b_{i,\lambda}]\in A$. Equality in the ultraproduct means that
$z_\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i,\lambda}b_{i,\lambda}$ for
$\mathcal U$-almost all $\lambda$. Hence $z_\lambda\in I_\lambda$ on a
member of $\mathcal U$.

Conversely, suppose $S=\{\lambda:z_\lambda\in I_\lambda\}$ belongs
to $\mathcal U$. For each $\lambda\in S$, choose coefficients
$b_{1,\lambda},\ldots,b_{r,\lambda}\in A_\lambda$ such that
$z_\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i,\lambda}b_{i,\lambda}$, and set all
these coefficients equal to zero when $\lambda\notin S$.
Then $b_i=[b_{i,\lambda}]\in A$ satisfies
$z=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_i b_i$, so $z\in I$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:ultra-fgv}
Let $\Lambda$ be a nonempty set, let $\mathcal U$ be an ultrafilter on
$\Lambda$, and let each $A_\lambda$ be an FGV-domain. Then
$\prod_{\mathcal U}A_\lambda$ is an FGV-domain.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $A=\prod_{\mathcal U}A_\lambda$, with each $A_\lambda$ FGV, and take a
nonzero finitely generated ideal $I=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$ of $A$.
For $z\in A\setminus I$, put
$I_\lambda=(a_{1,\lambda},\ldots,a_{r,\lambda})A_\lambda$.
By \eqref{eq:membership}, there is a member $E$ of $\mathcal U$ on which
$I_\lambda\ne0$ and $z_\lambda\notin I_\lambda$.

For each $\lambda\in E$, divisoriality supplies
$q_\lambda\in\operatorname{Frac}(A_\lambda)$ such that
$q_\lambda I_\lambda\subseteq A_\lambda$ but
$q_\lambda z_\lambda\notin A_\lambda$. Write
$q_\lambda=b_\lambda/c_\lambda$, with $c_\lambda\ne0$, and put
$b_\lambda=0$, $c_\lambda=1$ outside $E$. Then
$q=[b_\lambda]/[c_\lambda]\in\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ satisfies $qI\subseteq A$.
If $qz\in A$, representatives for this product would give
$q_\lambda z_\lambda\in A_\lambda$ almost everywhere, a
contradiction. Hence $z\notin I_v$.

Since $1\in I^{-1}$, one has $I_v\subseteq A$. The separation
argument proves $I_v\subseteq I$, and the reverse inclusion is
automatic.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:uniform}
Let $\Lambda$ be a nonempty set, let $\mathcal U$ be an ultrafilter on
$\Lambda$, and let $(A_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ be domains.
Suppose that for
each $r\geq1$ there is an integer $h(r)$ such that every relation
module on $r$ elements of every $A_\lambda$ can be generated by at
most $h(r)$ elements. Then $\prod_{\mathcal U}A_\lambda$ is coherent.
If all $A_\lambda$ are FGV-domains, the ultraproduct is $G$-Pr\"ufer.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For a row over the ultraproduct, choose $h(r)$ generators of the
relation module in each coordinate, padding with zeros. Their
classes are relations over the ultraproduct. An arbitrary relation
holds coordinatewise on a member of $\mathcal U$ and there is a linear
combination of the chosen generators on that set. Extend its
coefficients by zero elsewhere. This proves that the chosen
$h(r)$ classes generate the relation module. The finite-row
criterion proves coherence. The final assertion follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:ultra-fgv} and Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:divided}
A prime ideal $P$ of a domain $A$ will be called \emph{divided} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divided}
 aP=P\qquad\text{for every }a\in A\setminus P.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $P$ is comparable by inclusion with every principal
ideal of $A$. We use the explicit condition \eqref{eq:divided}
throughout.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:pullback}
Let $P$ be a divided prime of a domain $A$, put $B=A/P$, and let
$\pi:A_P\to\operatorname{Frac}(B)$ be the residue map. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullback}
 PA_P=P\quad\text{inside }\operatorname{Frac}(A),
 \qquad \pi^{-1}(B)=A\quad\text{inside }A_P.
\end{equation}
Moreover, for any nonzero finitely generated ideal
$J=(\bar a_1,\ldots,\bar a_r)$ of $B$, the lifted ideal
$I=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)A$ contains $P$ and $J=I/P$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For $p\in P$ and $s\notin P$, the identity $sP=P$ gives
$p/s\in P$, proving $PA_P=P$. Localizing $A/P$ at all its nonzero
elements identifies $A_P/PA_P$ with $\operatorname{Frac}(B)$.
If $x\in A_P$ and $\pi(x)=\bar a\in B$, choose $a\in A$ lifting
$\bar a$. Then $x-a\in PA_P=P\subseteq A$, so $x\in A$.
This proves the preimage identity. Finally, some $a_j\notin P$,
and $P=a_jP\subseteq I$, yielding the last assertion.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:quotient}
Let $A$ be a domain and let $P$ be a divided prime.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $A$ is FGV, then $A/P$ is FGV.
\item If $A$ is coherent, then $A/P$ is coherent.
\item If $A$ is $G$-Pr\"ufer, then $A/P$ and every localization
of $A/P$ are $G$-Pr\"ufer.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Set $B=A/P$. For (i), take a nonzero finitely generated ideal
$J=(\bar a_1,\ldots,\bar a_r)$ of $B$, and lift it to
$I=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)A$. Choose $a_j\notin P$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:pullback}, $P\subseteq I$ and $J=I/P$.
Given $\bar z\in B\setminus J$, a lift $z\in A$ lies outside $I$.
As $I$ is divisorial, there exists $q\in(A:I)$ with $qz\notin A$.
Since $qa_j\in A$ and $a_j\notin P$, one has $q\in A_P$.
The element $\pi(q)\in\operatorname{Frac}(B)$ maps $J$ into $B$.
If $\pi(q)\bar z$ belonged to $B$, then $qz$ would belong to
$\pi^{-1}(B)=A$, a contradiction. Thus $\bar z\notin J_v$.
As before, $J_v\subseteq B$, so $J$ is divisorial.

For (ii), take a nonzero row $(\bar a_1,\ldots,\bar a_r)$ over
$B$ and a lift with $a_j\notin P$. A lifted relation
$(b_1,\ldots,b_r)$ has error $p=\sum_i a_ib_i\in P$.
Write $p=a_jc$ with $c\in P$. Replacing $b_j$ by $b_j-c$ gives
an exact relation over $A$ with the same reduction. Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Rel}_A(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Rel}_B(\bar a_1,\ldots,\bar a_r)
\]
is surjective. Coherence of $A$ makes the target finitely generated.
The zero row has relation module $B^r$, so the finite-row criterion
proves coherence of $B$. Notice that no finite generation of $P$
has been assumed.

The relation map also preserves any uniform bound for nonzero rows;
for a zero row of length $r$, the relation module is $B^r$.
Part (iii) follows from (i), (ii), Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion},
and Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse}.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:uniform-periodic}
Let $\Lambda$ be a nonempty set and let
$(B_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ be domains of the form
$B_\lambda=k_\lambda[[X,Y]]/(f_\lambda)$, where $k_\lambda$ is a field
and $f_\lambda$ is a nonzero nonunit. Let $\mathcal U$ be an ultrafilter and put
$A=\prod_\mathcal U B_\lambda$. Every nonzero ideal $I$ of $A$ generated by
$r\ge1$ elements admits a two-periodic totally acyclic complex with
all terms $A^r$ and with $I$ as a cycle. In particular, $A$ is coherent
and $G$-Pr\"ufer.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 1: matrix factorizations of the prescribed size.}\enspace Write $I=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)A$ and choose coordinate rows
$(a_{1,\lambda},\ldots,a_{r,\lambda})$ with image $I_\lambda$.
Let $S_\lambda=k_\lambda[[X,Y]]$. If $I_\lambda\ne0$, then
$I_\lambda$ is a maximal Cohen--Macaulay module of dimension one over
$B_\lambda$. Viewed over $S_\lambda$, it has depth one: it is nonzero torsion-free over the one-dimensional
local domain $B_\lambda$. The regular local ring $S_\lambda$ has global
dimension two, so the Auslander--Buchsbaum formula gives projective
dimension one
\cite[Tag~090U]{Stacks}. Hence the kernel of the prescribed
surjection $S_\lambda^r\to I_\lambda$ is a free module. Its rank is
$r$, since $f_\lambda I_\lambda=0$. Choosing a basis gives
\[
 0\longrightarrow S_\lambda^r
 \xrightarrow{\Phi_\lambda}S_\lambda^r
 \longrightarrow I_\lambda\longrightarrow0.
\]
As $f_\lambda S_\lambda^r\subseteq\operatorname{Im}\Phi_\lambda$, there is
$\Psi_\lambda$ with
\[
 \Phi_\lambda\Psi_\lambda
 =\Psi_\lambda\Phi_\lambda=f_\lambda I_r.
\]
The second equality follows from injectivity of $\Phi_\lambda$.
For a zero ideal use $\Phi_\lambda=I_r$ and
$\Psi_\lambda=f_\lambda I_r$.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 2: exactness and dual exactness.}\enspace Reducing modulo $f_\lambda$ gives matrices
$\varphi_\lambda,\psi_\lambda$ over $B_\lambda$ and an exact
alternating complex with cokernel $I_\lambda$. For example,
$\Phi_\lambda v=f_\lambda w$ implies
$\Phi_\lambda(v-\Psi_\lambda w)=0$, proving
$\ker\varphi_\lambda=\operatorname{Im}\psi_\lambda$; the other equality is
similar. Both lifted matrices are injective, and the same argument
applies to their transposes. This is the matrix-factorization
construction of \cite{Eisenbud}.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 3: ultraproducts and coherence.}\enspace Take the ultraproduct of the entries of the fixed-size matrices.
If a vector lies in a kernel over $A$, its representative lies in the
corresponding coordinate kernel on a set in $\mathcal U$. Choose preimages
coordinatewise on that set, and put zero entries elsewhere. This
proves exactness of the alternating complex over $A$. The same
argument proves exactness of its dual and of its augmentation onto
$I$. Thus $\operatorname{coker}\varphi\cong I$, and the alternating
complex has a cycle isomorphic to $I$.

In particular, the kernel of $A^r\to I$ is generated by at most $r$
columns. The zero row also has a finitely generated kernel.
Thus $A$ is coherent. Every finitely generated ideal is Gorenstein
flat; equivalently, by the criterion in \cite[Corollary~2.4]{HLZ},
$A$ is $G$-Pr\"ufer.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:flat-quotient-complex}
Let $A$ be a domain and $P$ a flat prime ideal. Put $B=A/P$.
If $\boldsymbol F$ is a totally acyclic complex of finitely generated
free $A$-modules, then $B\otimes_A\boldsymbol F$ is a totally acyclic
complex of finitely generated free $B$-modules. Its cycles are the
modules $B\otimes_A Z$ for the corresponding cycles $Z$ of
$\boldsymbol F$. Periodicity and the ranks of the terms are preserved.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The case $P=0$ is immediate. For a torsion-free $A$-module $M$, the
multiplication map $P\otimes_A M\to M$ is injective. Indeed,
$M\hookrightarrow K\otimes_A M$, with $K=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$, remains injective
after tensoring with the flat module $P$, and
$P\otimes_A K\cong K$. Consequently the flat resolution
$0\to P\to A\to B\to0$ gives
$\operatorname{Tor}_i^A(B,M)=0$ for every $i>0$.

All cycles of $\boldsymbol F$ and $\boldsymbol F^*$ are torsion-free,
since they are submodules of free modules. Tensor their short exact
cycle sequences with $B$. The Tor vanishing gives exactness and the
asserted description of cycles. Degreewise finite free duality gives
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_B(B\otimes_A\boldsymbol F,B)
 \cong B\otimes_A\operatorname{Hom}_A(\boldsymbol F,A),
\]
which is exact by the same argument. Periodicity and ranks are
unchanged by this scalar extension.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:periodic-divided}
Let $A$ be a domain, let $P$ be a flat divided prime, and fix an integer
$r\ge1$. Suppose every nonzero $r$-generated ideal of $A$ has a two-periodic totally acyclic complex
with terms $A^r$. Then every nonzero $r$-generated ideal of $A/P$, and
of every localization of $A/P$, has a two-periodic totally acyclic
complex with free terms of rank $r$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Lift a nonzero ideal $J=(\bar a_1,\ldots,\bar a_r)$ of $A/P$ to
$I=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)A$, with some $a_j\notin P$. Dividedness gives
$P=a_jP\subseteq PI\subseteq P$, hence $PI=P$. Therefore
$B\otimes_A I=I/P=J$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:flat-quotient-complex}.
For a localization, clear a common denominator from the prescribed
$r$ generators and localize the complex; multiplying by that
invertible denominator preserves the isomorphism class of the ideal.

\label{thm:TVN}
Fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter $\mathcal U$ on $\{2,3,\ldots\}$ and
form $A$, $P$, $Q$, $D$, $\mathfrak m$, $\bar u$, $\bar v$, and $z$

\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:cut-flat}
Let $A=\prod_\mathcal U\mathbb Q[[t^n,t^{n+1}]]$ for a nonprincipal
ultrafilter $\mathcal U$ on $\{2,3,\ldots\}$, and let $P$ be given by
\eqref{eq:P}. Then $P$ is a flat idempotent ideal of $A$.
More precisely, every finite subset of $P$ is contained in a
principal ideal $cA$ with $c\in P$, and may be factored with its
quotients also in $P$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Take $p_1,\ldots,p_r\in P$, not all zero, with representatives
$p_{i,n}$. On a member of $\mathcal U$, their minimum order
$s_n=\min_i\nu_n(p_{i,n})$ is finite and greater than $4n^2$.
There put $c_n=t^{\lfloor s_n/2\rfloor}$, and put $c_n=0$
outside that set. Both $c_n$ and every $p_{i,n}/c_n$ on this set
have order at least $n^2$, so they belong to $A_n$ by its conductor
inclusion. Define the quotient representatives to be zero elsewhere.

Since $s_n/n^2$ exceeds every fixed bound almost everywhere, both
$c=[c_n]$ and $q_i=[p_{i,n}/c_n]$ belong to $P$, and $p_i=cq_i$.
This proves the factorization assertion and $P^2=P$.
The principal subideals $cA\subseteq P$ with $c\ne0$ form a directed
family whose union is $P$. Each is a free rank-one $A$-module, so
$P$ is flat as their filtered colimit.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $\mathcal U$ be a nonprincipal ultrafilter on $\{2,3,\ldots\}$ and
let $D=(A/P)_{Q/P}$ be the domain defined in
\eqref{eq:factors}--\eqref{eq:D}. Then $D$ is a one-dimensional local
$G$-Pr\"ufer domain. For every integer $r\ge1$, every nonzero ideal of $D$ generated by $r$
elements admits a two-periodic complete free resolution with terms
$D^r$. Its integral closure $B=\overline D$ is not Pr\"ufer.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 1: the Gorenstein property and its periodic refinement.}\enspace Each $A_n\cong\mathbb Q[[X,Y]]/(Y^n-X^{n+1})$ is a plane-curve
domain. Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-periodic} makes $A$ coherent and
$G$-Pr\"ufer and supplies the uniform periodic complexes.
Theorem~\ref{thm:TVN} makes $P$ divided, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:cut-flat} makes it flat. Thus
Corollary~\ref{cor:periodic-divided} transfers the complexes to
$D$. Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient} and localization also give that
$D$ is coherent and FGV, hence $G$-Pr\"ufer. Its dimension and
locality are in Theorem~\ref{thm:TVN}.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 2: failure of Pr\"ufer normalization.}\enspace The primes of the polynomial
fiber \eqref{eq:polynomial-fiber} give
\[
 0\subsetneq\mathfrak m D[z]\subsetneq\mathfrak m D[z]+zD[z].
\]
Thus $\dim D[z]\ge2$. If $B$ were Pr\"ufer, its integral extension
over $D$ would give $\dim B=1$. Every overring of a one-dimensional
Pr\"ufer domain has dimension at most one. But $B[z]$ is integral
over $D[z]$, so dimension preservation would give
$2\le\dim D[z]=\dim B[z]\le1$, a contradiction. These dimension
arguments are the ones in \cite[Lemma~3.2 and the proof of
Theorem~1.1]{TVN}.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:polynomial-fiber-ideal}
Let $(R,\mathfrak m,k)$ be a local domain, let $0\ne a,b\in R$, and
put $z=b/a$. Suppose the natural map
$k[Z]\to R[z]/\mathfrak mR[z]$ is an isomorphism.
Then $(a,b)T$ is not invertible for any integral overring $T$ of $R$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 1: comparable primes over one prime of the integral overring.}\enspace The ideals
$\mathfrak q_0=\mathfrak mR[z]\subsetneq
\mathfrak q_1=\mathfrak mR[z]+zR[z]$
are prime and both contract to $\mathfrak m$ in $R$.
The extension $R[z]\subseteq T[z]$ is integral. Lying-over and
going-up give primes $\mathfrak Q_0\subsetneq\mathfrak Q_1$ of
$T[z]$ above them. Their contractions to $T$ coincide, say with
$\mathfrak p$, by incomparability for the integral extension
$R\subseteq T$; see \cite[Section~10.36]{Stacks}.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 2: invertibility contradicts the prime chain.}\enspace Suppose $(a,b)T$ is invertible. Over the local domain $T_{\mathfrak p}$
it is generated by either $a$ or $b$. If $b$ generates it, then
$z^{-1}=a/b\in T_{\mathfrak p}$, so $z$ is a unit in
$T_{\mathfrak p}[z]$. This contradicts the survival of
$\mathfrak Q_1$, which contains $z$, after localizing at
$T\setminus\mathfrak p$.
If $a$ generates the ideal, then $z\in T_{\mathfrak p}$, so
$(T\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}T[z]=T_{\mathfrak p}$.
Both $\mathfrak Q_0$ and $\mathfrak Q_1$ survive this localization
and contract to the maximal ideal $\mathfrak pT_{\mathfrak p}$.
They must therefore become the same prime, contradicting the
prime correspondence for localization. This excludes invertibility.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:normal-tor-obstruction}
Let $B$ be an integrally closed domain, let $0\ne a,b\in B$, and
suppose $I=(a,b)B$ is not invertible. Put $z=b/a$ and
$S=B[z,z^{-1}]$. For every injective cogenerator $E_0$ of $B$-modules (that is,
$\operatorname{Hom}_B(N,E_0)\ne0$ for every nonzero $B$-module $N$),
\[
 \operatorname{Tor}_1^B(E_0,S)\ne0.
\]
In particular, $S$ is not Gorenstein flat over $B$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Normality gives $B[z]\cap B[z^{-1}]=B$, and their sum is $S$.
Thus \cite[Tag~052J]{Stacks} gives
\[
 0\longrightarrow B\xrightarrow{c\mapsto(c,-c)}
 B[z]\oplus B[z^{-1}]\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow0.
\]
Suppose $\operatorname{Tor}_1^B(E_0,S)=0$. Then the induced map from $E_0$ to
$(E_0\otimes_B B[z])\oplus(E_0\otimes_B B[z^{-1}])$ is injective.
Since $II^{-1}\ne B$, the cogenerator property supplies
$0\ne e\in E_0$ with $(II^{-1})e=0$.

Every relation $ra+sb=0$ has the form
$(r,s)=(bc,-ac)$ for $c\in I^{-1}$. Both coordinates annihilate
$e$, so the assignments
$a\mapsto e,b\mapsto0$ and $a\mapsto0,b\mapsto e$
define maps $I\to E_0$. Extend them to $B$ by injectivity.
There are $e_1,e_2\in E_0$ with
\[
 ae_1=e,\quad be_1=0,\qquad ae_2=0,\quad be_2=e.
\]
Since $b=az$, the image of $e$ in $E_0\otimes_B B[z]$ is
$be_2\otimes1=ae_2\otimes z=0$.
Using $a=bz^{-1}$ and $e_1$ proves that its image in the other
summand is also zero, a contradiction. Positive Tor against
injectives vanishes for a Gorenstein flat module, proving the last
assertion.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:specified-overring}
Let $\mathcal U$ be a nonprincipal ultrafilter on $\{2,3,\ldots\}$,
let $D=(A/P)_{Q/P}$ be the domain in
\eqref{eq:factors}--\eqref{eq:D}, and put $B=\overline D$.
Use $u=[t^{n^2}]$, $v=[t^{n^2+\lfloor(\sqrt2-1)n\rfloor}]$,
and $z=\bar v/\bar u$ as in Section~\ref{sec:construction}. Then
$(\bar u,\bar v)T$ is not invertible in any integral overring $T$
of $D$. For the overring $S=B[z,z^{-1}]$, one has
\[
 \operatorname{Gfd}_D B=\operatorname{Gfd}_D S=0,\qquad \operatorname{fd}_D B=\infty,
 \qquad \operatorname{Tor}_1^B(E_0,S)\ne0
\]
for every injective cogenerator $E_0$ of $B$-modules. Hence $S$ is
Gorenstein flat over $D$ but not over $B$; $B$ is not a $G$-Pr\"ufer domain.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-fiber-ideal} to
\eqref{eq:polynomial-fiber}, with $a=\bar u,b=\bar v$.
The nonvanishing Tor assertion follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:normal-tor-obstruction} over $B$.
Both $B$ and $S$ are torsion-free $D$-modules, so their Gorenstein
flatness follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}.

If $B$ were $G$-Pr\"ufer, its torsion-free module $S$ would be
Gorenstein flat, contrary to the nonvanishing just proved. Thus $B$
is not $G$-Pr\"ufer. In particular $B\ne D$, since $D$ is $G$-Pr\"ufer.
This proper integral overring is not flat over $D$. Otherwise it
would be faithfully flat, by lying-over. For $a,b\in D$, $a\ne0$,
and $b/a\in B$, faithful flatness would give
$b\in aB\cap D=aD$, forcing $b/a\in D$. This would make $B=D$,
contrary to its non-Pr\"ufer property. Since finite flat dimension
agrees with Gorenstein flat dimension \cite[Theorem~2.2]{Bennis},
$\operatorname{Gfd}_D B=0$ and nonflatness imply $\operatorname{fd}_D B=\infty$.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:dimensions}
Let $D$ be the domain defined in \eqref{eq:factors}--\eqref{eq:D}.
Then $D$ is not Noetherian, and
\[
 \operatorname{wGgldim}(D)=1,\qquad \operatorname{w.gl.dim}(D)=\infty.
\]
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
If $D$ were Noetherian, the Krull--Akizuki theorem would make its
one-dimensional normalization Dedekind, contrary to
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; see \cite[Tag~09IG]{Stacks}.
The $G$-Pr\"ufer property gives $\operatorname{wGgldim}(D)\le1$.
For a nonzero nonunit $a\in D$, the nonzero torsion module $D/aD$
cannot be Gorenstein flat, since a Gorenstein flat module over a
domain embeds in a flat, hence torsion-free, module. Thus the weak
Gorenstein global dimension is exactly one. The module $B$ has
infinite flat dimension by Theorem~\ref{thm:specified-overring},
so $\operatorname{w.gl.dim}(D)=\infty$.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:cusp}
For a field $k$, let $R=k[[t^2,t^3]]$. Then $R$ is a non-Pr\"ufer
Gorenstein Pr\"ufer domain, but its normalization is the DVR $k[[t]]$.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
The presentation $R\cong k[[u,v]]/(v^2-u^3)$ is a one-dimensional
Noetherian hypersurface, so $R$ is Gorenstein and hence $G$-Pr\"ufer
\cite{CFH,HLZ}. Its quotient field contains $t=t^3/t^2$, which is
integral over $R$ but not in $R$. Hence $R$ is not normal and is not
Pr\"ufer. The ring $k[[t]]=R+Rt$ is integral over $R$, is integrally
closed, and has the same quotient field. It is therefore the
normalization, and is a DVR.

<!-- example-2: body_md -->
\label{ex:nonperiodic-ideal}
Let $k$ be a field of characteristic zero, set
$R=k[[t^4,t^6,t^9]]$, and put $\mathfrak n=(t^4,t^6,t^9)R$.
Then $R$ is Gorenstein Pr\"ufer, but $\mathfrak n$ has no eventually
periodic resolution by finitely generated free $R$-modules.
More precisely, $\beta_i^R(\mathfrak n)=2i+3$ for every $i\ge0$.
<!-- end example-2 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
The assignments $u\mapsto t^4$, $v\mapsto t^6$, and $w\mapsto t^9$
give
\[
 R\cong k[[u,v,w]]/(v^2-u^3,\ w^2-v^3).
\]
Indeed, reduction by the two relations writes every class as
$a(u)+b(u)v+c(u)w+d(u)vw$. The four exponents $0,6,9,15$ have
 distinct residues modulo four, so their substituted series are
independent over $k[[t^4]]$. The first relation defines a domain,
and the second is a nonzero element in that quotient; the displayed
relations are a regular sequence. Thus $R$ is a one-dimensional
Noetherian complete intersection and is $G$-Pr\"ufer \cite{CFH,HLZ}.

Let $\beta_i^R(M)=\dim_k\operatorname{Tor}_i^R(k,M)$ be the $i$th Betti number of a
finitely generated module. The complete-intersection resolution of the
residue field \cite{Tate} gives its Poincar\'e series
\[
 P_k^R(s)=\sum_{i\ge0}\beta_i^R(k)s^i
 =\frac{(1+s)^3}{(1-s^2)^2}
 =\frac{1+s}{(1-s)^2}.
\]
Consequently $\beta_i^R(k)=2i+1$. Since $\mathfrak n$ is the first
syzygy of $k$ in a minimal free resolution,
$\beta_i^R(\mathfrak n)=\beta_{i+1}^R(k)=2i+3$.
An eventually periodic resolution with finitely generated free terms
has bounded ranks and hence bounded Betti numbers, contradicting this
formula. The ideal still has a complete resolution; the conclusion
excludes periodic resolutions of finite rank, not complete resolutions
in general.
