\label{thm:main}
In the reversible setting above, assume the Stroock--Varopoulos
comparison \eqref{eq:SV-assumption}.  Suppose that, for some $s>0$
and $C\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:24-main}
 \|T_s:L^2(\sigma)\to L^4(\sigma)\|\le C.
\end{equation}
If $C>1$, let $\lambda>0$ denote the spectral gap, which is
positive by the endpoint bound and primitivity. Then every state $\rho$
satisfies, for $t\ge0$,
\[
 D(\Phi_t(\rho)\|\sigma)
 \le e^{-\alpha_{\rm HC}t}D(\rho\|\sigma),
 \qquad
 \alpha_{\rm HC}
 =\left(s+\frac{\log C+\frac12}{\lambda}\right)^{-1}.
\]
If $C=1$, the same conclusion holds with $\alpha_{\rm HC}=s^{-1}$
without a gap assumption.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
\emph{Step 1: a spectral form core.}
Let $E_R=\mathbf1_{[0,R]}(L)$ and set
$\mathcal C=\bigcup_{R>0}E_R\mathscr T_2(\mathcal H)$.
For $X\in D(\mathcal E)$, the spectral truncations $X_R=E_RX$ satisfy
\[
 \|X-X_R\|_{\mathcal E}^2
 =\|(I-E_R)X\|_2^2+\|(I-E_R)L^{1/2}X\|_2^2\longrightarrow0,
\]
where $\|X\|_{\mathcal E}^2=\|X\|_2^2+\mathcal E(X)$.  Thus
$\mathcal C$ is a form core.  If $X\in E_R\mathscr T_2(\mathcal H)$,
then $e^{sL}X$ is well defined and has norm at most $e^{sR}\|X\|_2$.
The assumed endpoint therefore gives
\[
 \|X\|_{4\leftarrow2,\sigma}
 =\|e^{-sL}e^{sL}X\|_{4\leftarrow2,\sigma}
 \le C\|e^{sL}X\|_2\le Ce^{sR}\|X\|_2.
\]
Thus the transported $L^4$ norm is bounded by a constant times
the Hilbert--Schmidt norm on each spectral subspace. We first prove the estimates for a nonzero
$X\in E_R\mathscr T_2(\mathcal H)$; the case $X=0$ is immediate.

\emph{Step 2: endpoint interpolation.}
Apply Stein interpolation \cite[Chapter~V]{SteinWeiss1971},
in its weighted noncommutative form
\cite[Theorem~2.10]{Jencova2018Renyi}, on
$0\le\operatorname{Re}z\le1$ to the analytic family $e^{-szL}$.  On the first boundary $e^{-isyL}$ is
unitary on $L^2$, and on the second boundary the factorization
$e^{-s(1+iy)L}=e^{-sL}e^{-isyL}$ gives the $L^2$-to-$L^4$ bound
$C$, for every $y\in\mathbb R$.  Consequently, for $0\le t\le s$,
\[
 \|e^{-tL}X\|_{q(t)\leftarrow2,\sigma}\le C^{t/s}\|X\|_2,
 \qquad \frac1{q(t)}=\frac{1-t/s}{2}+\frac{t/s}{4}.
\]
The exponent is $q(t)=4s/(2s-t)$. Apply the estimate to
$e^{tL}X$, which is well defined on the spectral subspace from
Step~1. Since $e^{-tL}e^{tL}X=X$, it follows that
\[
 \log\frac{\|X\|_{q(t)\leftarrow2,\sigma}}{\|X\|_2}
 \le\frac{t}{s}\log C+
 \log\frac{\|e^{tL}X\|_2}{\|X\|_2},\qquad0<t\le s.
\]

\emph{Step 3: finite cutoffs and the defect.}
Let $P_n$ be the finite spectral projections of $\sigma$ used above
and put $X_n=P_nXP_n$. For all sufficiently large $n$, $X_n\ne0$.
Fix $0<t\le s$ and define, for $0\le u\le t$,
\[
 g_n(u)=\log\|X_n\|_{q(u)\leftarrow2,\sigma}.
\]
Weighted interpolation makes $g_n$ convex, since $1/q(u)$ is affine
in $u$; see \cite{Kosaki1984Interpolation,Jencova2018Renyi}.
We differentiate only finite matrices on the range of $P_n$. The finite-matrix entropy formula and $q'(0)=1/s$ give
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X_n)}{4s\|X_n\|_2^2}
 =g_n'(0)\le\frac{g_n(t)-g_n(0)}{t}.
\]
The weight here is the restriction of $\sigma$, without renormalizing
its trace. Thus this is precisely $\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X_n)$, with no
normalization correction.
By the definition of the transported norm,
\[
 \|X_n\|_2\longrightarrow\|X\|_2,\qquad
 \|X_n\|_{q(t)\leftarrow2,\sigma}
 \longrightarrow\|X\|_{q(t)\leftarrow2,\sigma}<\infty.
\]
Lower semicontinuity of the entropy and the bound on $g_n'(0)$ give
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X)
 &\le\liminf_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X_n)\\
 &\le\frac{4s\|X\|_2^2}{t}
 \log\frac{\|X\|_{q(t)\leftarrow2,\sigma}}{\|X\|_2}\\
 &\le4\log C\|X\|_2^2+
 \frac{4s\|X\|_2^2}{t}
 \log\frac{\|e^{tL}X\|_2}{\|X\|_2}.
\end{align*}
Only the last Hilbert--Schmidt norm needs to be differentiated.
The operator $L$ is bounded on $E_R\mathscr T_2(\mathcal H)$, so
\[
 \left.\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm d t}\log\|e^{tL}X\|_2\right|_{t=0}
 =\frac{\operatorname{Re}\langle X,LX\rangle}{\|X\|_2^2}
 =\frac{\mathcal E(X)}{\|X\|_2^2}.
\]
Letting $t\downarrow0$ proves \eqref{eq:defective-general} on
$\mathcal C$. The argument applies to complex $X$ even when the spectral
projections of $L$ and $\sigma$ do not commute.

\emph{Step 4: the centred estimate.}
Write $a=\langle\sqrt\sigma,X\rangle$ and
$X_0=X-a\sqrt\sigma$.  Since $\|\sqrt\sigma\|_2=1$ and
$\sqrt\sigma\in\ker L$, we have
\[
 \langle\sqrt\sigma,X_0\rangle=0,\qquad
 \|X\|_2^2=|a|^2+\|X_0\|_2^2,\qquad \mathcal E(X_0)=\mathcal E(X).
\]
Moreover $X_0\in\mathcal C$, because every $E_R$ fixes
$\sqrt\sigma$.  The entropy-domain Rothaus inequality proved in
\cref{lem:rothaus-entropy-domain} below gives
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X)\le\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X_0)+2\|X_0\|_2^2.
\]
Applying \eqref{eq:defective-general} to $X_0$ and using
$\mathcal E(X_0)=\mathcal E(X)$ now gives
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X)\le4s\mathcal E(X)+(4\log C+2)\|X_0\|_2^2.
\]
Thus the defect depends only on the component orthogonal to the
invariant vector.

\emph{Step 5: extension to the form domain and the gap.}
For general $X\in D(\mathcal E)$, use the truncations $X_R=E_RX$
from Step~1.  They satisfy
$\mathcal E(X_R)\to\mathcal E(X)$ and $\|X_R\|_2\to\|X\|_2$.
Since $E_R\sqrt\sigma=\sqrt\sigma$, their centred components are
$(X_R)_0=E_RX_0$ and converge to $X_0$ in $L^2$.
For the defective bound, lower semicontinuity gives
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X)
 &\le\liminf_{R\to\infty}\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X_R)\\
 &\le\lim_{R\to\infty}\bigl(4s\mathcal E(X_R)+4\log C\|X_R\|_2^2\bigr)
 =4s\mathcal E(X)+4\log C\|X\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
Using the centred bound from Step~4 instead gives
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X)
 \le\lim_{R\to\infty}\bigl(4s\mathcal E(X_R)+(4\log C+2)\|E_RX_0\|_2^2\bigr)
 =4s\mathcal E(X)+(4\log C+2)\|X_0\|_2^2.
\]
Thus both inequalities hold throughout $D(\mathcal E)$.  With $X=\sqrt\rho$, the identity
$\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(\sqrt\rho)=D(\rho\|\sigma)$ then gives
\eqref{eq:Rothaus}.

If $\lambda>0$, the definition of the spectral gap on the orthogonal
complement of $\sqrt\sigma$ gives
$\lambda\|X_0\|_2^2\le\mathcal E(X_0)=\mathcal E(X)$.
Substitution into the centred bound, again with $X=\sqrt\rho$,
yields
\[
 D(\rho\|\sigma)\le
 \left(4s+\frac{4\log C+2}{\lambda}\right)\mathcal E(\sqrt\rho),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:tight-LSI}.  Finally, when $C=1$, the defect
$4\log C\|X\|_2^2$ in \eqref{eq:defective-general} vanishes.
Applying that inequality directly to $\sqrt\rho$ gives the constant
$4s$, without centering or a spectral-gap assumption.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:rothaus-entropy-domain}
Let $\sigma$ be a faithful density matrix on $\mathcal H$.  For every
$Y\in\mathscr T_2(\mathcal H)$ and $a\in\mathbb C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rothaus-entropy-domain}
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Y+a\sqrt\sigma)
 \le\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Y)+2\|Y\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Infinite values are allowed.  In particular, the estimate applies to
the centred vector $Y=X-\langle\sqrt\sigma,X\rangle\sqrt\sigma$
whenever its entropy is finite.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
\emph{Finite dimension.}
For $2\le q\le4$, set
$B_q=\sigma^{1/(2q)-1/4}Y\sigma^{1/(2q)-1/4}$.
The first variation of the squared Schatten norm at
$a\sigma^{1/q}$ is
\[
 \left.\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm d u}\|a\sigma^{1/q}+uB_q\|_q^2\right|_{u=0}
 =2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\bar a\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma^{1-1/q}B_q)\bigr)
 =2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\bar a\langle\sqrt\sigma,Y\rangle\bigr).
\]
For $a=0$ both sides vanish.  The Ball--Carlen--Lieb smoothness
inequality \cite{BallCarlenLieb1994} therefore gives
\[
 \|Y+a\sqrt\sigma\|_{q\leftarrow2,\sigma}^2
 \le |a|^2+2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\bar a\langle\sqrt\sigma,Y\rangle\bigr)
 +(q-1)\|Y\|_{q\leftarrow2,\sigma}^2.
\]
This is an equality at $q=2$, without a centering assumption.
The middle term is independent of $q$.  For any fixed $Z$,
\[
 \left.\partial_q\|Z\|_{q\leftarrow2,\sigma}^2\right|_{q=2+}
 =2\|Z\|_2^2\left.\partial_q\log\|Z\|_{q\leftarrow2,\sigma}\right|_{q=2+}
 =\frac12\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Z).
\]
Since equality holds at $q=2$, the right derivatives satisfy
\[
 \frac12\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Y+a\sqrt\sigma)
 \le\|Y\|_2^2+\frac12\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Y).
\]
This is \eqref{eq:rothaus-entropy-domain}.  When $Z=0$ its squared
norm is identically zero, so the same argument includes $Y=0$ and
$Y+a\sqrt\sigma=0$.

\emph{Entropy recovery under spectral cutoffs.}
For a positive trace-class operator $A$ of trace $m>0$, write
\[
 \mathfrak D_\sigma(A):=mD(A/m\|\sigma)+m\log m,
 \qquad \mathfrak D_\sigma(0):=0.
\]
This definition avoids subtracting possibly infinite energy and
entropy traces.  The functional is lower semicontinuous in trace
norm \cite[Theorem~4.1(i)]{Hiai2018Standard} and satisfies
$\mathfrak D_\sigma(A)\ge m\log m$.
Choose increasing finite-rank spectral projections $P_n$ of $\sigma$
with $P_n\to I$ strongly, and put $Q_n=I-P_n$, $Y_n=P_nYP_n$.
We claim that finite $\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Y)$ implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-cutoff-recovery}
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Y_n)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Y).
\end{equation}
To see this, set
\[
 A=Y^\dagger Y,\qquad A_n=Y_n^\dagger Y_n,\qquad
 R_n=P_nY^\dagger Q_nYP_n,\qquad S_n=Q_nAQ_n.
\]
Then $A_n+R_n=P_nAP_n$.  On the finite block, the reference-logarithm
terms cancel in the difference
\begin{align*}
 \mathfrak D_\sigma(A_n+R_n)-\mathfrak D_\sigma(A_n)-\mathfrak D_\sigma(R_n)
 ={}&\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(A_n[\log(A_n+R_n)-\log A_n]\bigr)\\
 &+\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(R_n[\log(A_n+R_n)-\log R_n]\bigr)\ge0.
\end{align*}
The last inequality follows from operator monotonicity of the logarithm
\cite[Chapter~V]{Bhatia1997Matrix}, with regularization at zero.
Data processing \cite[Theorem~4.1(iv)]{Hiai2018Standard} for the
pinching $A\mapsto P_nAP_n+Q_nAQ_n$, which fixes $\sigma$, gives the second:
\[
 \mathfrak D_\sigma(A_n)+\mathfrak D_\sigma(R_n)
 +\mathfrak D_\sigma(S_n)
 \le\mathfrak D_\sigma(P_nAP_n)+\mathfrak D_\sigma(S_n)
 \le\mathfrak D_\sigma(A).
\]
The discarded masses tend to zero because
\[
 r_n:=\operatorname{Tr} R_n=\|Q_nYP_n\|_2^2\le\|Q_nY\|_2^2\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad s_n:=\operatorname{Tr} S_n=\|YQ_n\|_2^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since $\mathfrak D_\sigma(R_n)\ge r_n\log r_n$ and
$\mathfrak D_\sigma(S_n)\ge s_n\log s_n$, it follows that
\[
 \mathfrak D_\sigma(A_n)
 \le\mathfrak D_\sigma(A)-r_n\log r_n-s_n\log s_n.
\]
The trace-norm convergence follows from the ideal inequality:
\[
 \|A_n-A\|_1
 \le(\|Y_n\|_2+\|Y\|_2)\|Y_n-Y\|_2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Combining the upper bound with lower semicontinuity gives
\[
 \mathfrak D_\sigma(A)
 \le\liminf_n\mathfrak D_\sigma(A_n)
 \le\limsup_n\mathfrak D_\sigma(A_n)\le\mathfrak D_\sigma(A).
\]
Applying this argument to $Y^\dagger$ also yields
$\mathfrak D_\sigma(Y_nY_n^\dagger)\to\mathfrak D_\sigma(YY^\dagger)$.
The identity
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Y)
 =\tfrac12\mathfrak D_\sigma(Y^\dagger Y)
 +\tfrac12\mathfrak D_\sigma(YY^\dagger)
 -\|Y\|_2^2\log\|Y\|_2^2
\]
and $\|Y_n\|_2\to\|Y\|_2$ prove
\eqref{eq:entropy-cutoff-recovery}.

\emph{Passage to the entropy domain.}
Assume $\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Y)<\infty$, since otherwise the inequality is immediate.  Put $z_n=\operatorname{Tr}(P_n\sigma)$ and
$\sigma_n=P_n\sigma P_n/z_n$, considered on $P_n\mathcal H$.
For every $Z=P_nZP_n$,
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma_n}(Z)
 =\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Z)+\|Z\|_2^2\log z_n.
\]
With $X=Y+a\sqrt\sigma$, its compression is
$X_n=Y_n+a\sqrt{z_n}\sqrt{\sigma_n}$.  The finite-dimensional
inequality gives
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma_n}(X_n)
 \le\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma_n}(Y_n)+2\|Y_n\|_2^2.
\]
Returning to the original reference state, this reads
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X_n)
 \le\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Y_n)+2\|Y_n\|_2^2
 +(\|Y_n\|_2^2-\|X_n\|_2^2)\log z_n.
\]
Here $z_n\to1$, both norm sequences are bounded, and $X_n\to X$
in Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  Thus \eqref{eq:entropy-cutoff-recovery} gives
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X)
 \le\liminf_n\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(X_n)
 \le\operatorname{Ent}_{2,\sigma}(Y)+2\|Y\|_2^2,
\]
as required.

Let $T_t$ be a $\sigma$-preserving Markov semigroup with scalar fixed
space and let $\lambda>0$.  Suppose that, for every $\sigma$-centred $Y\in L^2(\sigma)$,

Write $X=aI+Y$, where $a=\langle X\rangle_\sigma$ and

 \|X\|_{2,\sigma}^2=|a|^2+\|Y\|_{2,\sigma}^2.

\label{lem:entropy-approximation}
For every state $\rho$ there are $\rho_k\in B(\sigma)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-approximation}
 \|\rho_k-\rho\|_1\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad D(\rho_k\|\sigma)\longrightarrow D(\rho\|\sigma)
\end{equation}
in the extended nonnegative reals.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
In finite dimension, put $\rho_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)\rho+
\varepsilon\sigma$.  Faithfulness of $\sigma$ makes
$\rho_\varepsilon\in B(\sigma)$, and
\[
 \|\rho_\varepsilon-\rho\|_1\le2\varepsilon,\qquad
 D(\rho_\varepsilon\|\sigma)\le(1-\varepsilon)D(\rho\|\sigma).
\]
Lower semicontinuity gives the matching lower limit.

In infinite dimension, choose increasing finite-rank spectral
projections $E_n\to I$ of $\sigma$, and put $Q_n=I-E_n$.
Faithfulness gives $\operatorname{Tr}(Q_n\sigma)>0$.  Define the normal channel
\[
 \mathsf E_n(\rho)=E_n\rho E_n+
 \operatorname{Tr}(Q_n\rho)\frac{Q_n\sigma Q_n}{\operatorname{Tr}(Q_n\sigma)}.
\]
It replaces the discarded block by the corresponding Gibbs tail.
It preserves the trace and fixes $\sigma$:
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\mathsf E_n(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}(E_n\rho)+\operatorname{Tr}(Q_n\rho)=1,
 \qquad \mathsf E_n(\sigma)=\sigma.
\]
Moreover
\[
 \|\mathsf E_n(\rho)-\rho\|_1
 \le\|E_n\rho E_n-\rho\|_1+\operatorname{Tr}(Q_n\rho)\longrightarrow0.
\]
To control the operator order, let $m_n>0$ be the smallest eigenvalue
of $E_n\sigma E_n$ on $E_n\mathcal H$, and set
\[
 c_n=\max\left\{\frac{\|E_n\rho E_n\|_\infty}{m_n},
                  \frac{\operatorname{Tr}(Q_n\rho)}{\operatorname{Tr}(Q_n\sigma)}\right\}.
\]
The two blocks separately satisfy
\[
 E_n\rho E_n\le c_nE_n\sigma E_n,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}(Q_n\rho)\frac{Q_n\sigma Q_n}{\operatorname{Tr}(Q_n\sigma)}
 \le c_nQ_n\sigma Q_n,
\]
so $\mathsf E_n(\rho)\le c_n\sigma$.
Choose $\varepsilon_n=1/(n+1)$ and put
$\rho_n=(1-\varepsilon_n)\mathsf E_n(\rho)+\varepsilon_n\sigma$.
Then
\[
 \varepsilon_n\sigma\le\rho_n
 \le\bigl((1-\varepsilon_n)c_n+\varepsilon_n\bigr)\sigma,
 \qquad
 \|\rho_n-\rho\|_1\le\|\mathsf E_n(\rho)-\rho\|_1+2\varepsilon_n\to0.
\]
Thus $\rho_n\in B(\sigma)$.  If $D(\rho\|\sigma)<\infty$,
convexity, data processing, and lower semicontinuity yield
\begin{align*}
 D(\rho_n\|\sigma)
 &\le(1-\varepsilon_n)D(\mathsf E_n(\rho)\|\sigma)
 \le(1-\varepsilon_n)D(\rho\|\sigma),\\
 D(\rho\|\sigma)&\le\liminf_nD(\rho_n\|\sigma).
\end{align*}
These bounds prove entropy convergence.  If $D(\rho\|\sigma)=\infty$,
the second line alone forces $D(\rho_n\|\sigma)\to\infty$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:weighted-SDPI}
Let $\Phi$ be a normal channel with faithful invariant state $\sigma$,
let $T=\Phi^*$, and assume that $T$ is $\sigma$-KMS symmetric and extends
consistently to positive contractions on $L^r(\sigma)$ for
$1\le r\le\infty$. Suppose that, for some $1\le K<\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pq-SDPI-input}
 \|T:L^p(\sigma)\to L^q(\sigma)\|\le K,
 \qquad 1<p\le q<\infty.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-SDPI}
 D(\Phi(\rho)\|\sigma)
 \le \frac{(p-1)q}{p(q-1)}D(\rho\|\sigma)
 +\frac q{q-1}\log K
\end{equation}
for every state $\rho$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For $\rho\in B(\sigma)$, the observable
$X=\iota_{1,\sigma}^{-1}(\rho)$ is bounded and positive.
We use the inverse only on this range.  KMS symmetry gives
$\Phi\iota_{1,\sigma}=\iota_{1,\sigma}T$.  Weighted complex interpolation
\cite{Kosaki1984Interpolation} of \eqref{eq:pq-SDPI-input} with
the $L^1$ contraction yields, for
$0\le\vartheta<1$,
\[
 \frac1{p_\vartheta}=\vartheta+\frac{1-\vartheta}{p},
 \quad
 \frac1{q_\vartheta}=\vartheta+\frac{1-\vartheta}{q},
 \quad
 \|TX\|_{q_\vartheta,\sigma}
 \le K^{1-\vartheta}\|X\|_{p_\vartheta,\sigma}.
\]
For $r>1$, define the sandwiched R\'enyi relative entropy
\cite[Section~3]{Jencova2018Renyi} by
\[
 \widetilde D_r(\rho\|\sigma)
 :=\frac1{r-1}\log\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
 \left(\sigma^{(1-r)/(2r)}\rho
 \sigma^{(1-r)/(2r)}\right)^r\right].
\]
For $\rho\in B(\sigma)$, the positive product belongs to the
required Schatten class, and
\[
 \log\|\iota_{1,\sigma}^{-1}(\rho)\|_{r,\sigma}
 =\frac{r-1}{r}\widetilde D_r(\rho\|\sigma).
\]
Since $\Phi$ preserves
$\sigma$ and order, $\Phi(\rho)\in B(\sigma)$ as well.  The interpolated
bound therefore becomes
\[
 \widetilde D_{q_\vartheta}(\Phi(\rho)\|\sigma)
 \le\frac{(p_\vartheta-1)q_\vartheta}{p_\vartheta(q_\vartheta-1)}
 \widetilde D_{p_\vartheta}(\rho\|\sigma)
 +\frac{(1-\vartheta)q_\vartheta}{q_\vartheta-1}\log K.
\]
The interpolation identities imply
\[
 \frac{p_\vartheta-1}{p_\vartheta}
 =(1-\vartheta)\frac{p-1}{p},\qquad
 \frac{q_\vartheta-1}{q_\vartheta}
 =(1-\vartheta)\frac{q-1}{q}.
\]
The coefficients are therefore independent of $\vartheta$:
\[
 \frac{(p_\vartheta-1)q_\vartheta}{p_\vartheta(q_\vartheta-1)}
 =\frac{(p-1)q}{p(q-1)},\qquad
 \frac{(1-\vartheta)q_\vartheta}{q_\vartheta-1}=\frac q{q-1}.
\]
Letting $\vartheta\uparrow1$ proves
\eqref{eq:weighted-SDPI} on $B(\sigma)$ because
$\widetilde D_r(\rho\|\sigma)\to D(\rho\|\sigma)$ as $r\downarrow1$
for comparable states \cite[Proposition~3.8(ii)]{Jencova2018Renyi}.

For a general state with finite input entropy, choose $\rho_k$ from
\cref{lem:entropy-approximation}.  Trace-norm contractivity gives
$\|\Phi(\rho_k)-\Phi(\rho)\|_1\le\|\rho_k-\rho\|_1\to0$.
Consequently
\begin{align*}
 D(\Phi(\rho)\|\sigma)
 &\le\liminf_kD(\Phi(\rho_k)\|\sigma)\\
 &\le\lim_k\left(\frac{(p-1)q}{p(q-1)}D(\rho_k\|\sigma)
                 +\frac q{q-1}\log K\right)\\
 &=\frac{(p-1)q}{p(q-1)}D(\rho\|\sigma)+\frac q{q-1}\log K.
\end{align*}
For infinite input entropy the right-hand side is infinite, so the
assertion holds in the extended sense.

\label{thm:bounded-perturbation}
Let $\sigma$ be a faithful density matrix, fix $t_*>0$ and
$2\le p_*<\infty$, and let $T_t^{(r)}$ and $S_t^{(r)}$ be strongly
continuous, consistent semigroups on $L^r(\sigma)$ for
$2\le r\le p_*$.  Fix $\omega\in\mathbb R$ and a continuous
nondecreasing exponent curve $p:[0,t_*]\to[2,p_*]$ with $p(0)=2$.
Suppose that
\begin{equation*}
 \|T_{t-s}:L^{p(s)}(\sigma)\to L^{p(t)}(\sigma)\|
 \le e^{\omega(t-s)},\qquad0\le s\le t\le t_*.
\end{equation*}
Let $\mathscr C$ be a common dense subspace of
$\bigcap_{2\le r\le p_*}L^r(\sigma)$ on which the realizations of
the semigroups agree.  One may take $B(\mathcal H)$ when all maps are
initially defined there.  Let $\mathcal V$ be a linear map with bounded
realizations $\mathcal V^{(r)}:L^r(\sigma)\to L^r(\sigma)$ that
agree on $\mathscr C$.  We assume that adding $\mathcal V$ to the
generator of $T_t$ generates the semigroup $S_t$, in the precise sense
that, for $0\le t\le t_*$, the following $L^2$ Trotter formula holds:
\begin{equation*}
 S_t^{(2)}=\operatorname*{s-lim}_{n\to\infty}
 \bigl(T_{t/n}^{(2)}e^{(t/n)\mathcal V^{(2)}}\bigr)^n.
\end{equation*}
Here $\operatorname*{s-lim}$ denotes convergence on each vector of
$L^2(\sigma)$ in its norm.  Assume that the perturbation bound
\[
 M_{t_*}:=\sup_{2\le r\le p(t_*)}
 \|\mathcal V^{(r)}:L^r(\sigma)\to L^r(\sigma)\|
\]
is finite.
Then, for $0\le s\le t\le t_*$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-perturbation}
 \|S_{t-s}:L^{p(s)}(\sigma)\to L^{p(t)}(\sigma)\|
 \le e^{(\omega+M_{t_*})(t-s)}.
\end{equation}
In particular,
$\|S_t:L^2(\sigma)\to L^{p(t)}(\sigma)\|
\le e^{(\omega+M_{t_*})t}$.  If the reverse Trotter formula holds with
$T$ and $S$ interchanged and perturbation $-\mathcal V$, the same
statement transfers a curve from $S$ back to $T$.

\label{lem:finite-core-entropy}

 \le\|K\|_\infty\|Y\|_2^2.

\label{thm:mixed-polynomial}
Let $h$ be a real polynomial of degree $m\ge2$ with positive leading
coefficient, let $D\subset\{1,2,\ldots\}$ be finite and nonempty, and put
$d_*=\max D$.  For the sampler with bare jumps $\mathcal A_D$ and filter
\eqref{eq:filter}, the following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $m>d_*$, the sampler has no bounded $L^2$-to-$L^4$ map at any finite
positive time.
\item If $m=d_*$ and $a_m>0$ is the leading coefficient of $h$,
then, for every $p>2$, the sampler is bounded
$L^2(\sigma_\beta)\to L^p(\sigma_\beta)$ for
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-threshold}
 t>\beta a_m(1/2-1/p),
\end{equation}
and unbounded between these spaces for
$0<t<\beta a_m(1/2-1/p)$.
The boundary time is excluded from this statement.
\item If $m<d_*$, it is bounded $L^2(\sigma_\beta)\to
L^p(\sigma_\beta)$ for every $p>2$ and every $t>0$.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Quantitative bound.} Fix a slope $\delta>0$ and define
\[
 p_\delta(u)=(1/2-\delta u)^{-1},\qquad
 0\le u<(2\delta)^{-1}.
\]

\begin{equation*}
 \omega_{D,\delta,s}:=
 \max\!\left\{0,\,
 \delta\log Z_\beta+
 \sup_{n\ge0}\{\beta\delta h(n)-2A_D(n)\}
 +M_{D,p_\delta(s)}\right\}.
\end{equation*}
Whenever the supremum is finite, the full semigroup $S_t^D$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-full-curve}
 \|S_{t-u}^D:L^{p_\delta(u)}(\sigma_\beta)
 \to L^{p_\delta(t)}(\sigma_\beta)\|
 \le e^{\omega_{D,\delta,s}(t-u)},
 \qquad0\le u\le t\le s.
\end{equation}

\emph{Kernel and entropy decay.} The mixed semigroup is conservative,
and its nonnegative standard-form operator $L_D=-\mathscr L_{2,h,D}$
has compact resolvent. Put $g=\gcd(D)$ and let $P_r$ project onto
occupations $n\equiv r\pmod g$. Then
\[
 \ker L_D=\operatorname{span}\{\sqrt{\sigma_\beta}P_r:0\le r<g\}.
\]
In particular, the sampler is primitive exactly when $g=1$.
Assume henceforth $g=1$, and let $\lambda_D>0$ be its smallest
positive eigenvalue. If $d_*=m$, \eqref{eq:mixed-full-curve} reaches
$p=4$ for every $s>\beta a_m/4$; if $d_*>m$, it does so for every $s>0$.
For either of these two regimes, fix such an admissible $s$
and, with $\delta=1/(4s)$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-entropy-constant}
 C_D(s):=e^{s\omega_{D,1/(4s),s}}.
\end{equation}
Then every state $\rho$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-entropy-decay}
 D(\Phi_t^D(\rho)\|\sigma_\beta)
 \le e^{-\alpha_D(s)t}D(\rho\|\sigma_\beta),
 \qquad
 \alpha_D(s)=\left(
 2s+\frac{2\log C_D(s)+1}{\lambda_D}
 \right)^{-1}.
\end{equation}

the same ladder estimates give the common energy-bound exponents $\gamma=d_*/(2m)$ and $\mu=d_*/m$.

\]
Indeed, nonnegativity of the $w_{ij}$ and $w_{ii}>0$ bounds the first
factor $(n_i+1)^{3/2}$ by $CD_i(\mathbf n)^{3/2}$ and gives
\[
 |w_{ir}-w_{is}|\sqrt{(n_r+1)n_s}
 \le C D_i(\mathbf n)^{1/2}\sqrt{n+1}.
\]
Each term is a weighted occupation shift, so a bound on its
coefficients is an operator-norm bound. Summing the finitely many hopping terms therefore proves
\[
 \|K_zQ_n\|+\|[T,K_z]Q_n\|\le C\sqrt{n+1},
 \qquad \operatorname{Re}z\ge-1.
\]

On maps from $\mathcal H^{[n]}$ to $\mathcal H^{[n-3]}$, let
$\mathcal D_nX=H_{n-3}X-XH_n$, where $H_n=H|_{\mathcal H^{[n]}}$.
Since $H_0$ commutes with $D_i$, we have
\begin{align*}
 [H,K_z]
 &=-A(D_i-c_iI)(z+D_i)^{-1}+[T,K_z]\\
 &=-A+zK_z+c_iK_z+[T,K_z].
\end{align*}
Thus
\[
 (z-\mathcal D_n)K_zQ_n=AQ_n-R_zQ_n,\qquad
 R_z=c_iK_z+[T,K_z],\qquad \|R_zQ_n\|\le C\sqrt{n+1}.
\]
In particular, with $K=AD_i^{-1}$ and $R=c_iK+[T,K]$,
\[
 A=-[H,K]+R,\qquad
 \|KQ_n\|+\|RQ_n\|\le C\sqrt{n+1}.
\]
The finitely many sectors $n<3$ contribute zero to $A$.

\emph{Step 3:}
We use the resolvent identity to estimate the energy filter on
each half of the frequency axis. The group
$e^{it\mathcal D_n}X=e^{itH_{n-3}}Xe^{-itH_n}$ consists of
operator-norm isometries. Hence
\[
 \|(z-\mathcal D_n)^{-1}\|_{\infty\to\infty}
 \le|\operatorname{Im}z|^{-1},\qquad
 \|g(\mathcal D_n)\|_{\infty\to\infty}
 \le\|\check g\|_{L^1(\mathbb R)},
\]
where $g(\nu)=\int_\mathbb R\check g(t)e^{it\nu}\,\mathrm d t$ in the second
inequality. Also $\operatorname{spec}(\mathcal D_n)\subset[-M_n,M_n]$,
where $M_n=C(n+1)^2$.

Choose a smooth $\chi$ equal to zero on $(-\infty,-1]$ and to
one on $[0,\infty)$. Restricted to this spectral interval, it is a
sum of $O(1+\log(n+2))$ smooth functions $\chi_\ell$ supported
in $[-1,\infty)$ on dyadic intervals of length $2^\ell$, with
$|\chi_\ell^{(r)}|\le C_r2^{-\ell r}$. Each has an almost analytic
extension $\widetilde\chi_\ell$ with
\[
 \int_{\mathbb C}\frac{|\partial_{\bar z}\widetilde\chi_\ell(z)|}
 {|\operatorname{Im}z|}\,\mathrm d x\,\mathrm d y\le C,
 \qquad\operatorname{supp}\widetilde\chi_\ell
 \subset\{\operatorname{Re}z\ge-1\}.
\]
For example, use the first-order extension
$(\chi_\ell(x)+iy\chi_\ell'(x))\eta(y/2^\ell)$ with a fixed
smooth cutoff $\eta$. On $|y|\lesssim2^\ell$, its derivative obeys
\[
 |\partial_{\bar z}\widetilde\chi_\ell(x+iy)|
 \le C|y|2^{-2\ell}
 +C2^{-\ell}\mathbf1_{\{|y|\asymp2^\ell\}},
\]
on an $x$-interval of length $O(2^\ell)$. Dividing by $|y|$ and
integrating therefore gives a constant independent of $\ell$.
The Cauchy--Green, or Helffer--Sj\"ostrand, formula
\cite[Section~2]{GesztesyNichols2015} and Step~2 give
\begin{align*}
 \chi_\ell(\mathcal D_n)AQ_n
 &=A\chi_\ell(-D_i)Q_n
 -\frac1\pi\int_{\mathbb C}\partial_{\bar z}\widetilde\chi_\ell(z)
 (z-\mathcal D_n)^{-1}R_zQ_n\,\mathrm d x\,\mathrm d y,\\
 \|\chi_\ell(\mathcal D_n)AQ_n\|&\le C\sqrt{n+1}.
\end{align*}
The first term vanishes because $D_i\ge4I$. The formula can also
be checked entry by entry in the two finite energy eigenbases;
the resolvent estimate makes its bound independent of their dimensions.
Summing the dyadic pieces yields
\[
 \|\chi(\mathcal D_n)AQ_n\|
 \le C\sqrt{n+1}\log(n+2).
\]

Write $f=\widehat f_{\!\mathscr M}$ in this proof and set
$g_-(\nu)=f(\nu)-1+\chi(\nu)$ and
$g_+(\nu)=f(-\nu)-\chi(\nu)$. For every integer $r\ge0$,
\[
 |g_\pm^{(r)}(\nu)|\le C_r(1+|\nu|)^{-1-r}.
\]
A dyadic partition and the Fourier estimate above imply
\[
 \|g_\pm(\mathcal D_n)\|_{\infty\to\infty}\le C,
 \qquad
 \|(\nu g_\pm)(\mathcal D_n)\|_{\infty\to\infty}
 \le C\log(n+2).
\]
Here is the dyadic estimate. For a smooth $u$ supported in a fixed
bounded interval, put $u_\ell(\nu)=u(2^{-\ell}\nu)$.
Plancherel, Cauchy--Schwarz, and a change of variables give
\[
 \|\check u\|_1\le C(\|u\|_2+\|u'\|_2),\qquad
 \check u_\ell(t)=2^\ell\check u(2^\ell t),\qquad
 \|\check u_\ell\|_1=\|\check u\|_1.
\]
The rescaled pieces of $g_\pm$ have size $O(2^{-\ell})$, while
those of $\nu g_\pm$ have size $O(1)$. If $L_n$ is the number of
dyadic scales meeting $[-M_n,M_n]$, the two bounds follow from
\[
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{L_n}C2^{-\ell}\le2C,
 \qquad \sum_{\ell=0}^{L_n}C\le C\log(n+2).
\]
Apply the multiplier to $A=-\mathcal D_nK+R$ on this block:
\begin{align*}
 g_\pm(\mathcal D_n)AQ_n
 &=-(\nu g_\pm)(\mathcal D_n)KQ_n+g_\pm(\mathcal D_n)RQ_n,\\
 \|g_\pm(\mathcal D_n)AQ_n\|
 &\le C\log(n+2)\|KQ_n\|+C\|RQ_n\|.
\end{align*}
Step~2 now gives
\[
 \|g_\pm(\mathcal D_n)AQ_n\|\le C\sqrt{n+1}\log(n+2).
\]
Thus, denoting the filtered cubic lowering and raising jumps by
$L_{i,3}^-$ and $L_{i,3}^+$,
\[
 \|(L_{i,3}^--a_i^3)Q_n\|
 +\|(L_{i,3}^+)^\dagger Q_n\|
 \le C\sqrt{n+1}\log(n+2).
\]
The second term uses
$(L_{i,3}^+)^\dagger Q_n=f(-\mathcal D_n)AQ_n$.
Taking adjoints and shifting $n$ by three bounds $L_{i,3}^+Q_n$
as well. The same dyadic Fourier argument for $f$ itself gives
$\|L_{i,1}^{\pm}Q_n\|\le C\sqrt{n+1}\log(n+2)$ for the linear
jumps. Consequently, for $\psi\in\mathcal H^{[n]}$,
\begin{align*}
 \langle\psi,C_H\psi\rangle
 &\ge\frac12\sum_i\|a_i^3\psi\|^2
       -C(n+1)\log^2(n+2)\|\psi\|^2,\\
 \sum_i(a_i^\dagger)^3a_i^3
 &=\sum_iN_i(N_i-I)(N_i-2I)
 \ge k^{-2}\mathsf N^3-C(\mathsf N+I)^2.
\end{align*}
The last lower bound uses
$\sum_i n_i^3\ge k^{-2}(\sum_i n_i)^3$ and
$n_i(n_i-1)(n_i-2)=n_i^3-3n_i^2+2n_i$.
For every $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 (n+1)^2+(n+1)\log^2(n+2)
 \le\varepsilon(n+1)^3+C_\varepsilon.
\]
This absorbs the error into the cubic term. For the upper bound,
the filtered lowering jumps have norm at most $C(n+1)^{3/2}$,
and the remaining jumps satisfy the smaller bounds above. Thus
\[
 c(\mathsf N+I)^3-CI\le C_H\le C(\mathsf N+I)^3.
\]

\emph{Step 4:}
For a bare jump $A^\alpha$, its standard-form gain operator is
\[
 B_\alpha=e^{\beta H/4}L^\alpha e^{-\beta H/4}
 =F_0(\operatorname{ad}_H)A^\alpha,\qquad
 F_\theta(\nu)=\exp\!\left[-\frac{\sqrt{1+(\beta\nu)^2}}4+\theta\nu\right].
\]
Here $\operatorname{ad}_H(X)=[H,X]$ is used only on the finite
particle blocks. Fix $0\le\theta_*<\beta/4$.
Both $F_\theta$ and $\nu F_\theta$ have inverse Fourier transforms
with uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms for $|\theta|\le\theta_*$.
For a cubic lowering jump, the identity in Step~2 gives
\begin{align*}
 B_{\alpha,\theta}Q_n
 &:=(e^{\theta H}B_\alpha e^{-\theta H})Q_n
 =F_\theta(\mathcal D_n)AQ_n=-(\nu F_\theta)(\mathcal D_n)KQ_n
   +F_\theta(\mathcal D_n)RQ_n.
\end{align*}
The Fourier bounds therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{|\theta|\le\theta_*}\|B_{\alpha,\theta}Q_n\|
 \le C_{\theta_*}(\|KQ_n\|+\|RQ_n\|)
 \le C_{\theta_*}\sqrt{n+1}.
\]
For cubic raising jumps this follows by adjunction and
$F_\theta(-\nu)=F_{-\theta}(\nu)$; for linear jumps it follows
directly from their $C\sqrt{n+1}$ bare bound.

The correction $B$ in the statement is essential when hopping is
present. Transporting the full generator, including this correction,
gives on finite particle matrices
\[
 LX=DX+XD-\sum_\alpha B_\alpha X B_\alpha^\dagger,
 \qquad
 D=\frac1{2\cosh((\beta/4)\operatorname{ad}_H)}(C_H).
\]
To check the drift and its sign, set
$M=-iB-C_H/2$ and $x=\beta(E-E')/4$. The definition of $B$ gives
\[
 P_EMP_{E'}=-\frac{1-\tanh x}{2}P_EC_HP_{E'}
 =-\frac{e^{-x}}{2\cosh x}P_EC_HP_{E'}.
\]
Conjugating by $e^{\beta H/4}$ multiplies this block by $e^x$.
Hence $e^{\beta H/4}Me^{-\beta H/4}=-D$, and the two drift terms
in the nonnegative standard-form generator are $DX+XD$.
For $D_\theta=e^{\theta H}De^{-\theta H}$, its Hermitian part is
\[
 \operatorname{Re}D_\theta
 =\frac{\cosh(\theta\operatorname{ad}_H)}
 {2\cosh((\beta/4)\operatorname{ad}_H)}(C_H)
 =\int_\mathbb R m_\theta(t)e^{itH}C_He^{-itH}\,\mathrm d t,
\]
where an elementary Fourier transform gives
\[
 m_\theta(t)=
 \frac{\cos(2\pi\theta/\beta)\cosh(2\pi t/\beta)}
 {\beta\,[\sinh^2(2\pi t/\beta)+\cos^2(2\pi\theta/\beta)]}
 \ge0,\qquad \int_\mathbb R m_\theta(t)\,\mathrm d t=\frac12.
\]
Since $H$ commutes with $\mathsf N$, Step~3 implies, uniformly for
$|\theta|\le\theta_*$,
\[
 \operatorname{Re}D_\theta\ge c(\mathsf N+I)^3-CI,
 \qquad \|D_\theta Q_n\|\le C_{\theta_*}(n+1)^3.
\]
For the norm estimate, use the integrable inverse Fourier transform
of $e^{\theta\nu}/(2\cosh(\beta\nu/4))$ and the upper bound on $C_H$.

\emph{Step 5:}
On Hilbert--Schmidt space set
\[
 \mathcal K_rX=(\mathsf N+I)^rX+X(\mathsf N+I)^r
 \text{ for }r=1,2,3,\qquad
 W_\theta X=e^{\theta\widetilde H}Xe^{\theta\widetilde H}.
\]
The loss operator $X\mapsto DX+XD$ is a self-adjoint direct sum
on the blocks $Q_nXQ_m$, with domain $D(\mathcal K_3)$ and finite
particle matrices as an operator core. The gain operator and its
conjugates are lower order. In fact, each bare row has a fixed
particle-number change $d_\alpha\in\{-3,-1,1,3\}$, and
\[
 W_\theta\bigl(B_\alpha X B_\alpha^\dagger\bigr)
 =B_{\alpha,\theta}(W_\theta X)B_{\alpha,\theta}^\dagger.
\]
Write $X_{nm}=Q_nXQ_m$, with a block understood to be zero if
an index is negative. For one gain term,
\[
 \|B_{\alpha,\theta}X_{nm}B_{\alpha,\theta}^\dagger\|_2
 \le C\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}\|X_{nm}\|_2,
\]
and its output lies in block $(n+d_\alpha,m+d_\alpha)$.
For fixed $\alpha$, distinct input blocks have orthogonal outputs.
Squaring and summing gives
\[
 \|B_{\alpha,\theta}XB_{\alpha,\theta}^\dagger\|_2^2
 \le C\sum_{n,m}(n+1)(m+1)\|X_{nm}\|_2^2
 \le C\|\mathcal K_1X\|_2^2.
\]
For the form bound, put $x_{nm}=\|X_{nm}\|_2$ and use
\begin{align*}
 |\langle X,B_{\alpha,\theta}XB_{\alpha,\theta}^\dagger\rangle|
 &\le C\sum_{n,m}\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}\,
 x_{nm}x_{n+d_\alpha,m+d_\alpha}\\
 &\le C\sum_{n,m}(n+m+2)x_{nm}^2
 =C\langle X,\mathcal K_1X\rangle.
\end{align*}
The second inequality uses $2ab\le a^2+b^2$ and the comparability
of the weights before and after a shift of at most three.
Summing over $\alpha$ proves
\begin{align*}
 \left\|\sum_\alpha B_{\alpha,\theta}X
 B_{\alpha,\theta}^\dagger\right\|_2
 &\le C_{\theta_*}\|\mathcal K_1X\|_2,\\
 \left|\sum_\alpha\langle X,B_{\alpha,\theta}X
 B_{\alpha,\theta}^\dagger\rangle\right|
 &\le C_{\theta_*}\langle X,\mathcal K_1X\rangle.
\end{align*}
At $\theta=0$ the gain is symmetric because each $B_\alpha$ is
paired with its adjoint. For every $\varepsilon>0$, the scalar
inequality $r\le\varepsilon r^3+C_\varepsilon$ gives
\[
 \|\mathcal K_1X\|_2
 \le\varepsilon\|\mathcal K_3X\|_2+C_\varepsilon\|X\|_2.
\]
Thus the gain has relative operator bound zero with respect to
the loss operator. The self-adjoint
perturbation theorem \cite{Schmdgen2012} therefore identifies the closure on finite
particle matrices with the standard-form generator from Step~1;
the Gibbs vector belongs to its domain by the Gaussian particle-number
tail. Its form domain is $D(\mathcal K_3^{1/2})$. For the weighted form, Step~4 first gives
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Re}\langle X,W_\theta LW_\theta^{-1}X\rangle
 &\ge c\langle X,\mathcal K_3X\rangle
       -C\langle X,\mathcal K_1X\rangle-C\|X\|_2^2,\\
 \langle X,\mathcal K_1X\rangle
 &\le\varepsilon\langle X,\mathcal K_3X\rangle
       +C_\varepsilon\|X\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
Choose $\varepsilon$ so that $C\varepsilon<c/2$. We obtain
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle X,W_\theta LW_\theta^{-1}X\rangle
 \ge c_*\langle X,\mathcal K_3X\rangle-C_*\|X\|_2^2,
 \qquad 0\le\theta\le\theta_*<\beta/4,
\]
on the finite particle core. The constants are uniform in $\theta$
on this interval.

\emph{Step 6: smoothing at every positive time.}
Fix $s>0$ and $2<p<\infty$, and choose
\[
 \delta_p=\frac12-\frac1p,\qquad
 \theta_*=\frac{\beta\delta_p}{2}<\frac\beta4,\qquad
 \theta(t)=\frac{\theta_*t}{s}\text{ for }0\le t\le s.
\]
Let $\Pi_MX=Q_{\le M}XQ_{\le M}$, with
$Q_{\le M}=\sum_{n=0}^MQ_n$, and let $L_M$ be the form compression
of $L$ to its range. These projections commute with $W_\theta$.
For $U_M(t)=W_{\theta(t)}e^{-tL_M}\Pi_MX$, differentiation
in the finite-dimensional range gives
\[
 U_M'(t)=\frac{\theta_*}{s}
 (\widetilde H U_M(t)+U_M(t)\widetilde H)
 -W_{\theta(t)}L_MW_{\theta(t)}^{-1}U_M(t).
\]
The compression keeps every loss term: it is $\Pi_ML\Pi_M$ on
its range, rather than a new sampler with truncated jumps.
Since $\Pi_M$ commutes with the weight, Step~5 gives
\begin{align*}
 \frac12\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm d t}\|U_M(t)\|_2^2
 &\le\frac{\theta_*}{s}
 \langle U_M,\widetilde H U_M+U_M\widetilde H\rangle
 -c_*\langle U_M,\mathcal K_3U_M\rangle+C_*\|U_M\|_2^2\le b_s\|U_M(t)\|_2^2,
\end{align*}
where one may take
\[
 b_s=C_*+2\sup_{r\ge1}
 \left\{\frac{C\theta_*}{s}r^2-c_*r^3\right\}<\infty.
\]
Here $\widetilde H\le C(\mathsf N+I)^2$ is from Step~1.
Integration gives $\|U_M(s)\|_2\le e^{sb_s}\|X\|_2$,
uniformly in $M$. To remove $M$, set $u=(L+I)^{-1}X$ and
$u_M=(L_M+I)^{-1}\Pi_MX$. The variational equations give
\[
 \mathcal E(u-u_M,v)+\langle u-u_M,v\rangle=0
 \quad(v\in\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_M),\qquad
 \|u-u_M\|_{\mathcal E}
 \le\inf_{v\in\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_M}\|u-v\|_{\mathcal E}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here $\mathcal E(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the sesquilinear form associated with
$\mathcal E$. The limit follows because finite particle matrices are a form
core. Strong resolvent convergence then gives
$e^{-sL_M}\Pi_MX\to e^{-sL}X$ in Hilbert--Schmidt norm
\cite[Theorem~III.4.8]{engel1999one}.
Choose a joint eigenbasis $(\psi_a)_a$ of $\widetilde H$ and
$\mathsf N$, with $\widetilde H\psi_a=\widetilde E_a\psi_a$.
If $y_{ab}^{(M)}$ and $y_{ab}$ are the corresponding coefficients
of these two outputs, Fatou's lemma gives
\[
 \sum_{a,b}e^{2\theta_*(\widetilde E_a+\widetilde E_b)}|y_{ab}|^2
 \le\liminf_M\sum_{a,b}
 e^{2\theta_*(\widetilde E_a+\widetilde E_b)}|y_{ab}^{(M)}|^2
 \le e^{2sb_s}\|X\|_2^2.
\]
In particular, the weighted output is Hilbert--Schmidt and
\[
 \|e^{\theta_*\widetilde H}e^{-sL}X
 e^{\theta_*\widetilde H}\|_2\le e^{sb_s}\|X\|_2.
\]
The bound therefore holds for the full semigroup. Put $\widetilde Z=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta\widetilde H})$.
Since $\|Y\|_p\le\|Y\|_2$ for $p\ge2$,
\[
 \|e^{-sL}X\|_{p\leftarrow2,\sigma_\beta}
 =\widetilde Z^{\delta_p}
 \|e^{\theta_*\widetilde H}e^{-sL}X
 e^{\theta_*\widetilde H}\|_p
 \le\widetilde Z^{\delta_p}e^{sb_s}\|X\|_2.
\]
This proves the required bounds for every $s>0$ and finite $p>2$.

\emph{Step 7: the kernel and the gap.}
Step~5 gives a quantitative tail estimate:
\[
 \|(I-\Pi_M)Y\|_2^2
 \le\frac{\langle Y,\mathcal K_3Y\rangle}{(M+2)^3}
 \le\frac{\mathcal E(Y)+C_*\|Y\|_2^2}{c_*(M+2)^3}.
\]
Every $\Pi_M$ has finite rank. Thus the form unit ball is relatively
compact in Hilbert--Schmidt space, and $L$ has compact resolvent.
Moreover, the upper loss and gain bounds in Steps~4--5 give the
matching upper form bound, so
\[
 c\mathcal K_3-CI\le L\le C\mathcal K_3+CI.
\]
There are $\binom{n+k}{k}\asymp n^k$ occupation states with at most
$n$ particles. Counting pairs of such states gives
$\#\{\text{eigenvalues of }\mathcal K_3\le E\}\asymp E^{2k/3}$.
The min--max principle yields $1+\lambda_j\asymp j^{3/(2k)}$;
summing $e^{-t\lambda_j}$ gives the asserted heat-trace bounds.
These are matching powers, not exact Weyl constants.

To identify its kernel, put
$J_\alpha(t)=e^{itH}L^\alpha e^{-itH}$. The paired Dirichlet-form
identity \eqref{eq:E-paired-generator}, at $\sigma_E=\infty$, reads
\[
 \mathcal E(Y)=\sum_\alpha\int_\mathbb R
 \frac{\|J_\alpha(t)Y-YJ_{\bar\alpha}(t)^\dagger\|_2^2}
 {\beta\cosh(2\pi t/\beta)}\,\mathrm d t.
\]
We first justify the identity for complex finite particle matrices.
For a self-adjoint $Y$, the difference between the two sides is a
real quadratic polynomial in its matrix entries. It vanishes on
the positive definite matrices by homogeneity and \cref{lem:paired-generator},
and hence on all self-adjoint matrices. One may work in a particle
cutoff larger than the support of $Y$ by three, so all jump images
and loss terms are retained. For complex $Y=Y_1+iY_2$, with
$Y_j=Y_j^\dagger$, put
$\mathcal D_\alpha(t)Y=J_\alpha(t)Y-YJ_{\bar\alpha}(t)^\dagger$.
Adjoint pairing gives
\[
 \mathcal D_\alpha(t)(Y^\dagger)
 =-\bigl(\mathcal D_{\bar\alpha}(t)Y\bigr)^\dagger,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E(Y)=\mathcal E(Y_1)+\mathcal E(Y_2).
\]
The mixed terms on the right also cancel after summing over
$\alpha$. This proves the identity on the complex finite particle core.

The growth bounds from Step~3 give, uniformly in $t$,
\[
 \sum_\alpha\|\mathcal D_\alpha(t)Y\|_2^2
 \le C\langle Y,\mathcal K_3Y\rangle.
\]
Both sides therefore extend continuously to
$D(\mathcal K_3^{1/2})$. Approximation by finite particle matrices
also shows that each $\mathcal D_\alpha(t)Y$ is continuous in $t$.
The integration weight is strictly positive. Hence
\[
 Y\in\ker L\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal D_\alpha(t)Y=0
 \quad\text{for every }\alpha\text{ and }t\in\mathbb R.
\]
On each block $Q_n(\cdot)Q_m$, this is a finite trigonometric sum,
because a jump has a fixed particle-number change. Indeed, the finite frequency expansion on such a block is
\[
 \mathcal D_\alpha(t)Y
 =\sum_\nu e^{it\nu}
 \left[f(\nu)A_\nu^\alpha Y-f(-\nu)YA_\nu^\alpha\right].
\]
Distinct exponentials are linearly independent. Since
$f(-\nu)=e^{\beta\nu/2}f(\nu)$ and $f(\nu)>0$, each coefficient
vanishes and
\[
 A_\nu^\alpha Y=e^{\beta\nu/2}YA_\nu^\alpha.
\]
Define $X=\sigma_\beta^{-1/4}Y\sigma_\beta^{-1/4}$ blockwise;
these are finite matrices, so no boundedness of $X$ is assumed.
The modular relation gives, blockwise,
\[
 A_\nu^\alpha\sigma_\beta^{1/4}
 =e^{\beta\nu/4}\sigma_\beta^{1/4}A_\nu^\alpha,
 \qquad
 \sigma_\beta^{1/4}A_\nu^\alpha
 =e^{-\beta\nu/4}A_\nu^\alpha\sigma_\beta^{1/4}.
\]
Substituting $Y=\sigma_\beta^{1/4}X\sigma_\beta^{1/4}$ into the
preceding identity and cancelling the finite invertible weights
therefore gives $[A_\nu^\alpha,X]=0$. Summing the finitely many frequencies in each
particle block, the linear rows yield
\[
 [a_j,X]=[a_j^\dagger,X]=0,\qquad [N_j,X]=0\quad(1\le j\le k).
\]
Writing $X_{\mathbf n\mathbf m}=\langle\mathbf n|X|\mathbf m\rangle$,
the number commutators give
\[
 (n_j-m_j)X_{\mathbf n\mathbf m}=0\quad\text{for all }j.
\]
Thus $X$ is diagonal. If $x_{\mathbf n}=X_{\mathbf n\mathbf n}$,
the creation commutators give
\[
 0=\langle\mathbf n+e_j|[a_j^\dagger,X]|\mathbf n\rangle
 =\sqrt{n_j+1}\,(x_{\mathbf n}-x_{\mathbf n+e_j}).
\]
Every occupation vector is connected to the vacuum by these steps.
Hence $X$ is scalar and $\ker L=\mathbb C\sqrt{\sigma_\beta}$.
Compact resolvent now gives a positive gap $\lambda$.

\emph{Step 8: entropy decay and uniqueness among states.}
Apply \cref{thm:KMS-endpoint} with $p=4$ and
$C(s)=\max\{1,\widetilde Z^{1/4}e^{sb_s}\}$ to obtain the claimed
relative-entropy estimate. To check uniqueness also for invariant
states of infinite relative entropy, approximate any state $\tau$ in
trace norm by its normalized finite particle compressions $\rho_M$.
These have finite relative entropy because each particle cutoff
has finite dimension and the Gibbs state is faithful there. Pinsker's
inequality and the entropy estimate give
\[
 \|\Phi_t(\rho_M)-\sigma_\beta\|_1^2
 \le2D(\Phi_t(\rho_M)\|\sigma_\beta)
 \le2e^{-\alpha(s)t}D(\rho_M\|\sigma_\beta)
 \longrightarrow0.
\] If $\Phi_t(\tau)=\tau$, trace-norm contractivity gives
\[
 \|\tau-\sigma_\beta\|_1
 \le\|\tau-\rho_M\|_1+\|\Phi_t(\rho_M)-\sigma_\beta\|_1.
\]
Letting $t\to\infty$ and then $M\to\infty$ proves $\tau=\sigma_\beta$.

\emph{Step 9: the quadratic-jump alternative.}
We record the changes needed when each cubic pair is replaced by a
quadratic pair. Step~1 applies with common energy exponents $1/2$ and
$1$. In Step~2 take
\[
 A=a_i^2,\qquad D_i=b_iI+2\sum_jw_{ij}N_j,\qquad
 c_i=b_i+3U_i+2\mu_i.
\]
Then $[H_0,A]=-A(D_i-c_iI)$ exactly, and
$\|A(z+D_i)^{-1}Q_n\|\le C$ for $\operatorname{Re}z\ge-1$.
For a hopping term the direct commutator is
$[a_r^\dagger a_s,a_i^2]=-2\mathbf1_{\{r=i\}}a_ia_s$.
The resolvent-commutator coefficient is bounded by
\[
 C\frac{(n_i+1)|w_{ir}-w_{is}|\sqrt{(n_r+1)n_s}}
 {D_i(\mathbf n)^2}\le C\sqrt{n+1},
\]
using the same nonnegative-interaction estimate as in Step~2.
Thus its resolvent identity holds with a remainder bounded by
$C\sqrt{n+1}$. Step~3 consequently gives
\[
 \|(L_{i,2}^--a_i^2)Q_n\|+\|L_{i,2}^+Q_n\|
 \le C\sqrt{n+1}\log(n+2).
\]
For every $\varepsilon>0$, expanding the squared lowering norms and
using $2ab\le\varepsilon a^2+\varepsilon^{-1}b^2$ now gives
\[

Put $R_\theta=e^{\theta\mathcal K}Re^{-\theta\mathcal K}$ on
$\mathscr F$.  The conjugated free operator is
\[
 e^{\theta\mathcal K}L_0e^{-\theta\mathcal K}X
 =c\mathcal KX+kr_+X
 -v\sum_j\bigl(e^{-2\theta}a_jXa_j^\dagger
              +e^{2\theta}a_j^\dagger Xa_j\bigr).
\]
The two gain maps are Hilbert--Schmidt adjoints.  Their common
quadratic-form estimate follows from
\begin{align*}
 2\left|\sum_j\langle X,a_jXa_j^\dagger\rangle\right|
 &=2\left|\sum_j\langle a_j^\dagger X,Xa_j^\dagger\rangle\right|\le\sum_j\bigl(\|a_j^\dagger X\|_2^2+\|Xa_j^\dagger\|_2^2\bigr)
 =\langle X,\mathcal KX\rangle+k\|X\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
Consequently
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Re}\langle X,e^{\theta\mathcal K}L_0e^{-\theta\mathcal K}X\rangle
 \ge{}&(c-v\cosh(2\theta))\langle X,\mathcal KX\rangle+k(r_+-v\cosh(2\theta))\|X\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
Hence, uniformly for $0\le\theta\le\theta_*$,
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle X,e^{\theta\mathcal K}L_0
 e^{-\theta\mathcal K}X\rangle
 \ge \kappa_*\langle X,\mathcal KX\rangle
 -kv\cosh(2\theta_*)\|X\|_2^2.
\]
For the perturbation, Cauchy--Schwarz and Young's inequality give
\begin{align*}
 |\langle X,R_\theta X\rangle|
 &\le C_R\|X\|_2\|(\mathcal K+I)^{1/2}X\|_2\\
 &\le C_R\|X\|_2\langle X,\mathcal KX\rangle^{1/2}+C_R\|X\|_2^2\\
 &\le\frac{\kappa_*}{2}\langle X,\mathcal KX\rangle
    +\left(C_R+\frac{C_R^2}{2\kappa_*}\right)\|X\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
Thus one may take
$C_*=kv\cosh(2\theta_*)+C_R+C_R^2/(2\kappa_*)$, or any larger
positive constant, to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-oscillator-coercivity}
 \operatorname{Re}\langle X,e^{\theta\mathcal K}Le^{-\theta\mathcal K}X\rangle
 \ge \frac{\kappa_*}{2}\langle X,\mathcal KX\rangle-C_*\|X\|_2^2.
\end{equation}

We justify the evolution estimate by finite-dimensional approximation.
Let $\mathsf P_J=\mathbf1_{[0,J]}(\mathcal K)$ and let
$L_J=\mathsf P_JL\mathsf P_J$ on its finite-dimensional range.
For $X_J(t)=e^{-tL_J}\mathsf P_JX$ and
$\theta(t)=\kappa_*t/2$, set $U_J(t)=e^{\theta(t)\mathcal K}X_J(t)$.
Since $\mathsf P_J$ commutes with $\mathcal K$,
\cref{eq:weighted-oscillator-coercivity} applies to the compressed
operator with the same constants.  For $0\le t\le s_*$ it yields
\[
 \frac12\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm d t}\|U_J(t)\|_2^2
 =\theta'(t)\langle U_J(t),\mathcal KU_J(t)\rangle
 -\operatorname{Re}\langle U_J(t),
 e^{\theta(t)\mathcal K}L_Je^{-\theta(t)\mathcal K}U_J(t)\rangle
 \le C_*\|U_J(t)\|_2^2.
\]
Multiplying by $e^{-2C_*t}$ and integrating gives
\[
 \|U_J(t)\|_2^2\le e^{2C_*t}\|\mathsf P_JX\|_2^2
 \le e^{2C_*t}\|X\|_2^2,\qquad0\le t\le s_*.
\]
To pass to the limit, put $u=(L+I)^{-1}X$ and
$u_J=(L_J+I)^{-1}\mathsf P_JX$.
The two variational equations imply orthogonality of $u-u_J$ to
$\operatorname{Ran}\mathsf P_J$ for the inner product of the form
$\mathcal E+\|\cdot\|_2^2$.  Therefore
\[
 \|u-u_J\|_{\mathcal E}
 \le\inf_{v\in\operatorname{Ran}\mathsf P_J}\|u-v\|_{\mathcal E}
 \longrightarrow0,
\]
because the increasing ranges exhaust the form core $\mathscr F$.
Strong resolvent convergence gives
$e^{-s_*L_J}\mathsf P_JX\to e^{-s_*L}X$ in Hilbert--Schmidt norm
\cite[Theorem~III.4.8]{engel1999one}.
Write the respective matrix coefficients as $y^{(J)}_{\mathbf m\mathbf n}$
and $y_{\mathbf m\mathbf n}$.  Since $\theta(s_*)=\theta_*$, Fatou's lemma yields
\[
 \sum_{\mathbf m,\mathbf n}e^{2\theta_*(|\mathbf m|+|\mathbf n|)}
 |y_{\mathbf m\mathbf n}|^2
 \le\liminf_J\sum_{\mathbf m,\mathbf n}e^{2\theta_*(|\mathbf m|+|\mathbf n|)}
 |y^{(J)}_{\mathbf m\mathbf n}|^2
 \le e^{2C_*s_*}\|X\|_2^2.
\]
The finite left-hand side shows that the output belongs to
$D(e^{\theta_*\mathcal K})$, and its square root proves
\eqref{eq:oscillator-smoothing}.

Write $H=H_{0,n}+W$, $\sigma=\sigma_\beta(H)$, and
$\sigma_0=\sigma_\beta(H_{0,n})$.
Choose $c_0$ so that $\widetilde H=H+c_0I\ge I$. Outside the
finite oscillator block, $\widetilde H$ agrees with $H_{0,n}+c_0I$.
On the finite block all powers of $\widetilde H$ are bounded.
Since a coordinate ladder changes the oscillator number by one,
each bare jump $A$ satisfies
\[
 \|A\widetilde H^{-1/2}\|
 +\|\widetilde H^{1/2}A\widetilde H^{-1}\|<\infty.
\]
The finite Hermite span is a common invariant core, and
$\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-b\widetilde H})<\infty$ for every $b>0$.
These bounds verify the energy and domain assumptions of
\cite[Condition~A and Proposition~2.4]{BeckerRouzeSalzmannToAppearcmp}.
The Gaussian filter satisfies the required KMS and boundedness
conditions. The construction in that paper therefore gives a
conservative KMS-symmetric sampler.
Its standard-form operator $L\ge0$ is obtained by closing the form on
$\mathscr F+\mathbb C\sqrt\sigma$.  Here the finite $H$-spectral matrices
span $\mathscr F$, since $W$ acts inside a fixed finite oscillator

\emph{Step 1: finite-energy changes of the coefficients.}
Let $L^\ell$ and $L_0^\ell$ denote the filtered jumps for $H$ and
$H_{0,n}$, respectively, with $\ell$ ranging over the $2k$ bare
ladders.  There is a finite oscillator spectral projection $Q$ such

\begin{equation}\label{eq:coulomb-finite-coefficients}

For every bare coordinate ladder $A$,
\[
 (I-Q)A\Pi_M=0,\qquad \Pi_MA(I-Q)=0,
\]

\[
 (I-Q)(L^\ell-L_0^\ell)=0,

\]
This proves \eqref{eq:coulomb-finite-coefficients}.  Diagonalizing
$H$ inside $\Pi_M\mathcal H_n$ does not enlarge these spaces.
We enlarge $Q$ below when further ladder actions occur.

Write the state generator as
$\mathcal L(\rho)=\sum_\ell L^\ell\rho (L^\ell)^\dagger
+M_H\rho+\rho M_H^\dagger$.
We also need the finite-energy property for the drift, including
its correction Hamiltonian.  For the bare ladder $A^\ell$, let
$A^\ell_{\nu,H}$ denote its frequency component relative to $H$.
Its free frequency is $\nu_\ell\in\{-2,2\}$.  The same support argument gives, for each frequency,
\[

 \qquad G^\ell_\nu=QG^\ell_\nu Q,

\]

\begin{align*}
 (A^\ell_{\nu,H})^\dagger A^\ell_{\mu,H}
 &-\mathbf1_{\{\nu=\nu_\ell\}}\mathbf1_{\{\mu=\nu_\ell\}}
       (A^\ell)^\dagger A^\ell=\mathbf1_{\{\nu=\nu_\ell\}}(A^\ell)^\dagger G^\ell_\mu
   +\mathbf1_{\{\mu=\nu_\ell\}}(G^\ell_\nu)^\dagger A^\ell

 \le\|B_*\|_\infty^2\|e^{(\beta/4)\mathcal K}Y\|_2.

 \le\frac{\mathcal E(X)+c_2\|X\|_2^2}{c_1(J+1)}.

$\ker L=\mathbb C\sqrt\sigma$.  Hence $\lambda=\operatorname{gap}(L)>0$.

\[
 s_{n,M}=s_*+\frac{\log C+\frac12\log3}{\lambda}.
\]
Finally \cref{thm:KMS-endpoint} gives the stated entropy estimate,
for example with
$\alpha_{n,M}=(2s_*+(2\log C+1)/\lambda)^{-1}>0$.
