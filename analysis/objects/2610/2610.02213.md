<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:final-power}
Let $\Lambda=\langle S'\mid R'\rangle$ be given by a finite reducible presentation without proper powers. Let $s\notin S'$ and let $u\in F(S'\sqcup\{s\})$ have image in $\Lambda*\langle s\rangle$ which is not conjugate into $\Lambda$ and is not a proper power. Then, for every $m\ge1$,

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:trace-realization}
For every finite $U\subseteq F(S)$ and every $\eta>0$, there are $d\ge1$ and a homomorphism
\[
 \sigma:F(S)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}(d)
\]
such that
\[
 \left|\operatorname{tr}_d(\sigma(u))-\mu\{x:P_u(x)=1_{F(S)}\}\right|<\eta
 \qquad(u\in U).
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Choose a free atomless pmp action $F(S)\curvearrowright(Y,\nu)$, for instance a Bernoulli action, and define
\begin{equation}
 \rho(u)(x,y)=\bigl(u\cdot x,\,P_u(x)\cdot y\bigr).
\end{equation}
For the canonical choice $p_a\equiv a$ discussed above, this is just the diagonal action; the repair changes these fixed-point statistics for relators. The cocycle identity makes $\rho$ an $F(S)$-action. It is pmp as well: for fixed $u$, partition $X$ into the countably many Borel sets $X_g=\{x:P_u(x)=g\}$; on $X_g\times Y$ the map is $(x,y)\mapsto(u\cdot x,g\cdot y)$. Each such piece preserves measure and the images $u\cdot X_g$ are pairwise disjoint, so summing over $g\in F(S)$ preserves $\mu\times\nu$.

Let $\Delta$ be the diagonal action $\Delta(g)(x,y)=(g\cdot x,g\cdot y)$. Write $E_\rho$ and $E_\Delta$ for the orbit equivalence relations of $\rho$ and $\Delta$, respectively. The action $\Delta$ is free because the action on $Y$ is free, so its usual Cayley graphing is a treeing of $E_\Delta$. The endpoint identity \eqref{eq:endpoint} gives
\[
 \rho(u)(x,y)=\Delta(P_u(x))(x,y),
\]
and hence $E_\rho\subseteq E_\Delta$. By Gaboriau's theorem that measured subequivalence relations of treeable relations are treeable \cite[Th\'eor\`eme~IV.4]{Gaboriau2000}, $E_\rho$ is treeable.

Enumerate $S=\{a_1,\ldots,a_{|S|}\}$, regard these as the first $|S|$ generators of $F_\infty$, and extend $\rho$ to an action $\widetilde\rho$ of $F_\infty$ by letting all remaining generators act trivially. Then $E_{\widetilde\rho}=E_\rho$.

Since $E_\rho$ is treeable, Elek--Lippner's Theorem~4 implies that $E_\rho$ is a sofic equivalence relation. By their definition, there is therefore a sofic $F_\infty$-action $\beta$ whose orbit relation is $E_\rho$. The actions $\beta$ and $\widetilde\rho$ are measured orbit equivalent, so Elek--Lippner's Theorem~1 implies that $\widetilde\rho$ is sofic. Definition~1.3 then gives finite $F_\infty$-models whose rooted labelled neighbourhood distributions converge to those of $\widetilde\rho$ \cite[Definition~1.3 and Theorems~1,~4]{ElekLippner2010}.

For the fixed finite set $U$, write $|u|_S$ for reduced word length in the basis $S$, and take
\[
 \operatorname{rad}(U):=\max\bigl(\{|S|\}\cup\{|u|_S:u\in U\}\cup\{1\}\bigr).
\]
In Elek--Lippner's convention, a rooted radius-$r$ neighbourhood uses only the first $r$ generators of $F_\infty$. Thus an Elek--Lippner rooted $\operatorname{rad}(U)$-neighbourhood displays every generator in $S$ and contains the labelled path of every reduced word $u\in U$. Whether the $u$-path returns to the root is therefore determined by this neighbourhood. Restricting each finite $F_\infty$-model to the first $|S|$ generators gives a finite $F(S)$-set. These models may be chosen so that, simultaneously for all $u\in U$,
\[
 \left|\frac{|\{z\in V:u\cdot z=z\}|}{|V|}-(\mu\times\nu)(\operatorname{Fix}(\rho(u)))\right|<\eta
\]
for some finite $F(S)$-set $V$.

If $P_u(x)=1_{F(S)}$, then \eqref{eq:endpoint} gives $u\cdot x=x$, so $\rho(u)$ fixes every point over $x$. Conversely, if $\rho(u)(x,y)=(x,y)$, then $P_u(x)\cdot y=y$, and freeness of the action on $Y$ forces $P_u(x)=1_{F(S)}$. Thus
\[
 (\mu\times\nu)(\operatorname{Fix}(\rho(u)))=\mu\{x:P_u(x)=1_{F(S)}\}.
\]
Taking $d=|V|$ and writing the finite $F(S)$-action as a homomorphism $\sigma:F(S)\to\operatorname{Sym}(d)$ proves the lemma.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:criterion}
Let $S$ and $R$ be finite, put $\Gamma=\langle S\mid R\rangle$, and let $q:F(S)\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma$ be the quotient map. Suppose that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exist a free pmp action $\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)$ and a cocycle satisfying \eqref{eq:lift} such that, with $\operatorname{err}_\alpha$ defined from this cocycle as above,
\[
 \operatorname{err}_\alpha(r)<\varepsilon\qquad(r\in R).
\]
Then $\Gamma$ is sofic.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix a finite $W\subseteq F(S)$ and $\delta>0$. For each $u\in W\cap\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle$ choose an integer $\ell(u)\ge0$ and an expression
\[
 u=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell(u)} c_j r_j^{\epsilon_j} c_j^{-1},
 \qquad c_j\in F(S),\quad r_j\in R,\quad \epsilon_j\in\{\pm1\}.
\]
Let
\[
 L=\max\bigl(\{\ell(u):u\in W\cap\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle\}\cup\{1\}\bigr)
\]
and choose the cocycle so that $\operatorname{err}_\alpha(r)<\delta/(4L)$ for all $r\in R$. Equations \eqref{eq:error-basic} and \eqref{eq:error-conj} give
\[
 \operatorname{err}_\alpha(u)<\delta/4\qquad(u\in W\cap\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle).
\]

If $u\in W\setminus\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle$, then $q(u)\ne1$. Freeness of the $\Gamma$-action and \eqref{eq:endpoint} imply $P_u(x)\ne1_{F(S)}$ for every $x\in X$. Apply \cref{lem:trace-realization} to $W$ with error smaller than $\delta/4$. Words in $W\cap\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle$ then have permutation trace greater than $1-\delta/2$, whereas words in $W\setminus\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle$ have trace smaller than $\delta/4$. These estimates are stronger than the finite-word criterion above with tolerance $\delta$. Hence $\Gamma$ is sofic.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:terminal-repair}
Assume that $\theta$ is Borel and that for some $N\ge1$,
\[
 \theta^N(e_x)
\]
contains no deleted edge for every $x\in A$. Then the prescribed maps $p_a$, $a\in T^{\pm1}$, extend to Borel maps $p_s,p_{s^{-1}}$ satisfying \eqref{eq:generator-compatibility} such that
\[
 P_u(x)=1_{F(S)}\qquad(x\in A).
\]
The maps attached to $T^{\pm1}$ are unchanged.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Put
\[
 \tau_x=\theta^N(e_x)\qquad(x\in A).
\]
The path $\tau_x$ runs from $s\cdot x$ to $x$ and contains no deleted edge. Since $N$ is fixed, $\theta$ is Borel, and the prescribed maps on $T^{\pm1}$ are Borel, the map
\[
 x\longmapsto \operatorname{val}_p(\tau_x)
\]
is Borel. Define
\[
 p_s(x)=
 \begin{cases}
  \operatorname{val}_p(\tau_x)^{-1},&x\in A,\\
  s,&x\notin A,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 p_{s^{-1}}(s\cdot x)=p_s(x)^{-1}.
\]
Since $x\mapsto s\cdot x$ is a Borel bijection, the second formula defines $p_{s^{-1}}$ on all of $X$. By \eqref{eq:relative-path-endpoint}, for $x\in A$ the word $\operatorname{val}_p(\tau_x)^{-1}$ carries $x$ to $s\cdot x$. Hence the new maps satisfy \eqref{eq:generator-compatibility}.

Let $\gamma_x=\theta(e_x)$ be the complementary path from $s\cdot x$ to $x$. Evaluate the cocycle product $P_\omega(s\cdot x)$ using the new maps. A prescribed $T$-edge contributes its prescribed value. An undeleted $s$-edge contributes the ordinary letter. If a deleted occurrence is based at $y\in A$, then an occurrence of $e_y$ contributes
\[
 p_s(y)^{-1}=\operatorname{val}_p(\tau_y),
\]
and an occurrence of $e_y^{-1}$ contributes its inverse. Thus replacing each deleted edge in $\gamma_x$ by its terminal path $\tau_y=\theta^N(e_y)$ does not change the evaluated product. Since $\theta$ respects concatenation and $\tau_x$ contains no deleted edge,
\[
 \theta^N(\gamma_x)=\theta^{N+1}(e_x)=\theta(\tau_x)=\tau_x.
\]
In particular $\theta^N(\gamma_x)$ contains no deleted edge, so its relative value is defined, and
\[
 P_\omega(s\cdot x)
 =\operatorname{val}_p\bigl(\theta^N(\gamma_x)\bigr)
 =\operatorname{val}_p(\tau_x)
\]
and hence
\[
 P_u(x)=P_\omega(s\cdot x)p_s(x)=1_{F(S)}.
\]

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:pw-terminal}
In the preceding normalization one has $|\langle\bar v\rangle|=m$. For every $\kappa>0$ there exist a free pmp action $\Gamma_m\curvearrowright(X,\mu)$, a Borel set $A\subseteq X$, a Borel replacement operator $\widehat\theta$ for the deleted $s$-edges based in $A$, and an integer $N\ge1$ such that
\[
 \mu(A)\ge \frac1m-\kappa,
 \qquad
 A\text{ is contained in a Borel transversal for }\langle\bar v\rangle,
\]
and $\widehat\theta^N(e_x)$ contains no deleted edge for every $x\in A$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The proof of Poulin--Wr\'obel's one-relator product theorem verifies the hypotheses of their edge-cutting theorem and performs the required cyclic normalization in this setting \cite[proof of Theorem~4.1]{PoulinWrobel2026}. The proof of their Theorem~3.4 also records that the image of the root word has order exactly $m$. Their edge-cutting theorem and its proof then give, for every $\kappa>0$, a free pmp action together with a deleted-edge basepoint set of measure at least $1/m-\kappa$ contained in a transversal for the order-$m$ subgroup $\langle\bar v\rangle$ \cite[Theorem~3.4 and its proof]{PoulinWrobel2026}. We denote this deleted-edge basepoint set by $A$. If $\theta$ denotes their replacement operator in the alphabet $\Lambda\cup\{s\}$, Lemma~3.3 and Claim~3.5 in that proof provide a single integer $N$, independent of $x$, such that $\theta^N(e_x)$ contains no deleted edge for every $x\in A$ \cite[Lemma~3.3 and Claim~3.5]{PoulinWrobel2026}.

Expand every $\Lambda$-syllable occurrence in $w_0$ by its chosen word over $T^{\pm1}$. For a fixed occurrence of $s^{\pm1}$, the product of the preceding letters is unchanged as an element of $\Lambda*\langle s\rangle$; hence the corresponding $s$-edge has the same basepoint before and after expansion. The inserted letters lie in $T^{\pm1}$, so no new $s$-edge is created. It follows that expansion carries the complementary relator path for a deleted edge to the complementary path for the expanded relator and preserves the ordered deleted $s$-edge occurrences, their orientations, and their basepoints. Define $\widehat\theta$ by making the corresponding complementary-path substitution for the relator $v^m$, fixing all surviving generator edges, respecting inversion, and extending by concatenation, as in \cref{sec:repair}. The substitution is finite and fixed, so $\widehat\theta$ is Borel. By induction on $n$, expansion of $\theta^n(e_x)$ is $\widehat\theta^n(e_x)$ for every $n\ge0$. The termination bound $N$ therefore persists after expansion.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:transport}
Let $T\subset S$, let $\Lambda\le\Gamma$, and suppose the chosen maps $F(T)\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda$ and $F(S)\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma$ are compatible with the inclusion $F(T)\le F(S)$. Let $\Lambda\curvearrowright(Y,\nu)$ be a free pmp action equipped with an $F(T)$-valued cocycle satisfying the lifting condition. Suppose $\Gamma\curvearrowright(Z,\eta)$ is a pmp action and $\pi:Z\to Y$ is a $\Lambda$-equivariant factor map. For $a\in T^{\pm1}$ define
\[
 \widetilde p_a(z)=p_a(\pi z),
\]
viewing $F(T)$ as the subgroup $F(T)\le F(S)$. Then the maps $\widetilde p_a$ satisfy \eqref{eq:generator-compatibility} for the restricted $\Lambda$-action on $Z$, and for every $v\in F(T)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport}
 \widetilde P_v(z)=P_v(\pi z).
\end{equation}
In particular the measure of $\{\widetilde P_v\ne1\}$ equals the measure of $\{P_v\ne1\}$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The lifting condition on $Y$ says that $p_a(y)$ and $a$ represent the same element of $\Lambda$. Since $\Lambda\le\Gamma$, this gives
\[
 \widetilde p_a(z)\cdot z=a\cdot z.
\]
Using $\Lambda$-equivariance of $\pi$ also gives
\[
 \widetilde p_{a^{-1}}(a\cdot z)
 =p_{a^{-1}}(a\cdot\pi z)
 =p_a(\pi z)^{-1}.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:transport} follows from the word formula for the cocycle, and the equality of measures follows from $\pi_*\eta=\nu$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:relative-step}
Keep the setting and normalization preceding \cref{prop:pw-terminal}. Let $\Lambda\curvearrowright(Y,\nu)$ be a free pmp action equipped with an $F(T)$-valued cocycle satisfying the lifting condition for $F(T)\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda$, and denote its generator maps by $p_a^Y$. Then for every $\kappa>0$ there exist a free pmp action $\Gamma_m\curvearrowright(\Omega,\lambda)$, a $\Lambda$-equivariant factor map $\pi:\Omega\to Y$, and an $F(T\sqcup\{s\})$-valued cocycle satisfying the lifting condition such that
\[
 p_a=p_a^{\,Y}\circ\pi\qquad(a\in T^{\pm1})
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{err}_\alpha(v^m)\le m\kappa.
\]
In particular, under the natural inclusion $F(T)\le F(T\sqcup\{s\})$, every cocycle value of a word in $F(T)$ has the same distribution as before the extension.

By Howie's Freiheitssatz, $\Lambda$ embeds in $\Gamma_m$ \cite{Howie1981}. Coinduce the given $\Lambda$-action to $\Gamma_m$ and, if necessary, take its product with a free Bernoulli $\Gamma_m$-action. This gives a free pmp action $\Gamma_m\curvearrowright(Z,\eta)$ with a $\Lambda$-equivariant factor map $\pi_Z:Z\to Y$. Apply \cref{prop:pw-terminal} with the given $\kappa$. It gives independently a free pmp action $\Gamma_m\curvearrowright(X_0,\mu_0)$, a deleted-edge basepoint set $A_0$, a replacement operator $\widehat\theta_0$, and a uniform termination bound $N$, with

and $A_0$ contained in a transversal for $\langle\bar v\rangle$.

 P_{v^m}(x)=1_{F(T\sqcup\{s\})}\qquad(x\in A).

 \lambda(B_v)\ge m\lambda(A)=m\mu_0(A_0)\ge1-m\kappa,

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:induction}
Let $\Gamma=\langle S\mid R\rangle$ be a finite reducible presentation with no proper powers. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there are a free pmp action $\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)$ and a cocycle satisfying \eqref{eq:lift} such that
\[
 \operatorname{err}_\alpha(r)<\varepsilon\qquad(r\in R).
\]
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
We induct on $|R|$. If $R=\varnothing$, take any free pmp action and the canonical cocycle $P_v(x)=v$.

Assume $R\ne\varnothing$. Reducibility gives $s\in S$ and $r\in R$ such that, with
\[
 S'=S\setminus\{s\},\qquad R'=R\setminus\{r\},\qquad
 \Lambda=\langle S'\mid R'\rangle,
\]
the lower presentation is reducible and the image of $r$ in $\Lambda*\langle s\rangle$ is not conjugate into $\Lambda$. The no-proper-power condition passes to the lower presentation and says that the image of $r$ is not a proper power in $\Lambda*\langle s\rangle$. The lower group $\Lambda$ is locally indicable by Howie's theorem \cite[Corollary~4.5]{Howie1982}.

Let
\[
 \pi_0:F(S)\longrightarrow\Lambda*\langle s\rangle
\]
be the quotient obtained by imposing the lower relations $R'$.

If $\pi_0(r)$ is conjugate into $\langle s\rangle$, then, since it is not a proper power, it is conjugate to $s$ or $s^{-1}$. The final group is isomorphic to $\Lambda$ and $s$ acts trivially. Choose $g\in\Lambda*\langle s\rangle$ and $\zeta\in\{\pm1\}$ with
\[
 s=g\pi_0(r)^\zeta g^{-1},
\]
and choose $c\in F(S)$ with $\pi_0(c)=g$. Then
\[
 k_0=r c^{-1}s^{-\zeta}c\in\langle\!\langle R'\rangle\!\rangle.
\]
Fix an expression
\[
 k_0=\prod_{j=1}^{M_0}h_j(r_j')^{\delta_j}h_j^{-1},
 \qquad r_j'\in R',\quad \delta_j\in\{\pm1\}.
\]
Apply the induction hypothesis to the lower presentation with tolerance $\xi>0$ such that $(M_0+1)\xi<\varepsilon$. Regard the resulting action as an action of $\Gamma\cong\Lambda$, embed the lower cocycle values in $F(S)$, and put
\[
 p_s(x)=p_{s^{-1}}(x)=1_{F(S)}.
\]
The lower relator errors are unchanged and $\operatorname{err}_\alpha(s)=0$. By \eqref{eq:error-basic} and \eqref{eq:error-conj},
\[
 \operatorname{err}_\alpha(r)
 \le \sum_{j=1}^{M_0}\operatorname{err}_\alpha(r_j')
 \le M_0\xi<\varepsilon.
\]
This proves the induction step in this case.

Assume now that $\pi_0(r)$ is conjugate into neither free-product factor. Use the normalization preceding \cref{prop:pw-terminal}, with $m=1$, for the conjugacy class of $\pi_0(r)$ or its inverse. Let $v\in F(S)$ be the resulting finite-alphabet word, so that
\[
 v=\omega s.
\]
There are $g\in\Lambda*\langle s\rangle$ and $\zeta\in\{\pm1\}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-top-element}
 \pi_0(v)=g\pi_0(r)^\zeta g^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Choose $c\in F(S)$ with $\pi_0(c)=g$ and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-kernel-word}
 k=r c^{-1}v^{-\zeta}c.
\end{equation}
Then $k\in\ker\pi_0=\langle\!\langle R'\rangle\!\rangle$. Fix an expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-kernel-expression}
 k=\prod_{j=1}^{M}h_j(r_j')^{\delta_j}h_j^{-1},
 \qquad r_j'\in R',\quad \delta_j\in\{\pm1\}.
\end{equation}

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and choose
\[
 0<\xi<\frac{\varepsilon}{2(M+1)}.
\]
By induction, there are a free pmp action $\Lambda\curvearrowright(Y,\nu)$ and a lower cocycle with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-errors}
 \operatorname{err}_\alpha(r')<\xi\qquad(r'\in R').
\end{equation}
Choose $\kappa>0$ with $\kappa<\varepsilon/2$. Apply \cref{prop:relative-step} to the lower action and cocycle, with $m=1$. The resulting cocycle on a free pmp $\Gamma$-action preserves all estimates \eqref{eq:lower-errors} and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v-error}
 \operatorname{err}_\alpha(v)\le\kappa<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\end{equation}

From \eqref{eq:lower-kernel-word},
\[
 r=k c^{-1}v^\zeta c.
\]
The words $k$ and $v$ act trivially in the final group. Thus \eqref{eq:error-basic}, \eqref{eq:error-conj}, \eqref{eq:lower-kernel-expression}, and \eqref{eq:v-error} give
\[
 \operatorname{err}_\alpha(r)
 \le \operatorname{err}_\alpha(k)+\operatorname{err}_\alpha(v)
 \le \sum_{j=1}^{M}\operatorname{err}_\alpha(r_j')+\operatorname{err}_\alpha(v)
 <M\xi+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\varepsilon.
\]
This completes the induction.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:finite-subpresentation-test}
Let $\Gamma=\langle S\mid R\rangle$ be a presentation, with $S$ and $R$ arbitrary, and let $W\subset F(S)$ be finite. Then there is a finite subpresentation
\[
 \Gamma_0=\langle S_0\mid R_0\rangle
\]
with $S_0\subset S$ and $R_0\subset R$ such that every word of $W$ is a word over $S_0$ and
\[
 u=1\text{ in }\Gamma_0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u=1\text{ in }\Gamma
 \qquad(u\in W).
\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Put into $S_0$ every generator occurring in a word of $W$. For each $u\in W$ that is trivial in $\Gamma$, choose a finite expression
\[
 u=\prod_{j=1}^{m(u)}h_{u,j}r_{u,j}^{\epsilon_{u,j}}h_{u,j}^{-1},
 \qquad r_{u,j}\in R,\quad \epsilon_{u,j}\in\{\pm1\}.
\]
Add the finitely many relators $r_{u,j}$ to $R_0$ and enlarge $S_0$ to contain every generator occurring in them and in the conjugating words. Then every word of $W$ that is trivial in $\Gamma$ is already trivial in $\Gamma_0$. Conversely, the inclusion of generators and relators gives a homomorphism $\Gamma_0\to\Gamma$, so a word trivial in $\Gamma_0$ is trivial in $\Gamma$.

The finite case follows from \cref{prop:induction} and \cref{prop:criterion}. Let $\Gamma=\langle S\mid R\rangle$ have a reducible presentation without proper powers, with $S$ and $R$ arbitrary. Fix a finite $K\subset\Gamma$ and $\delta>0$, enlarge $K$ to contain $1$, and choose words $\widehat g\in F(S)$ representing every element of $K\cup K^2$, with $\widehat1=1$. Let $W$ consist of

\]
and
\[
 \widehat g\widehat h^{-1}\qquad(g,h\in K,\ g\ne h).
\]
Apply \cref{lem:finite-subpresentation-test}. In the resulting finite subpresentation $\Gamma_0$, the first family is trivial and the second family is nontrivial. Thus the elements represented by the $\widehat g$ preserve the products from $K$ exactly and are distinct. The finite subpresentation is reducible and has no proper powers, so it is sofic by the finite case. A sufficiently good finite sofic approximation of these finitely many elements gives the approximation required in the definition for $K$ and $\delta$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:arbitrary-final-power}
Let $\Lambda=\langle S'\mid R'\rangle$ be given by a reducible presentation without proper powers. Let $s\notin S'$ and let $u\in F(S'\sqcup\{s\})$ have image in $\Lambda*\langle s\rangle$ which is not conjugate into $\Lambda$ and is not a proper power. Then, for every $m\ge1$,
\[
 (\Lambda*\langle s\rangle)/\langle\!\langle u^m\rangle\!\rangle
\]
is sofic.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Fix a finite soficity test in the displayed group and apply \cref{lem:finite-subpresentation-test}. Enlarge the resulting finite subpresentation to contain the relation $u^m$ and all generators occurring in $u$. The truth values of the finitely many test words are unchanged by this enlargement: words already trivial remain trivial, while a word nontrivial in the original group cannot become trivial in the enlarged subpresentation because the latter still maps to the original group. Its lower part is a finite subpresentation of the reducible presentation of $\Lambda$, hence is reducible and has no proper powers. If the image of $u$ in the corresponding finite lower free product were conjugate into the lower group, or were a proper power, its image in $\Lambda*\langle s\rangle$ would have the same property. Thus \cref{thm:final-power} applies to the finite subpresentation. A sufficiently good approximation of the chosen finite test transfers to the original group.
