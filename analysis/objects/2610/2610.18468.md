<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:postwelldefined}
Assume that ${\mathcal G}:{\mathcal H}\to{\mathcal H}'$ is continuous and let $y\in{\mathcal H}'$. Then\\
(a) The measure $\pi^y(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)$ given by \eqref{eq:solutionIP} is a well-defined probability measure.\\
(b) The measure $\pi^y(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)$ has a well-defined mean  and covariance operator
\[
\langle m, f\rangle = \int\langle \theta, f\rangle \frac{1}{Z(y)}\ell(\theta,y)\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta),\quad
\langle {\mathcal C} f, g\rangle = \int\langle \theta-m, f\rangle \langle \theta-m, g\rangle \frac{1}{Z(y)}\ell(\theta,y)\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)
\]
for $f,g\in{\mathcal H}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
(a) First, the continuity of ${\mathcal G}$ implies that  $\ell(\theta,y)$ is measurable with respect to $\theta$.
Second, $\|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2 \geq 0$, so $\ell(\theta,y) \leq 1$ and $Z(y) \leq \int\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta) = 1 <\infty$, yielding the upper bound.
Third, the lower bound follows from the continuity of ${\mathcal G}$ at zero, according to which there is $r>0$ such that $\|{\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'} \leq 1 + \|{\mathcal G}(0)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}$
whenever $\|\theta\|_{\mathcal H} < r$. In particular, $\|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}$ is bounded for fixed $y\in{\mathcal H}'$ and so $\ell(\theta,y) \geq \exp(-K)$ for $\|\theta\|_{\mathcal H} < r$ and some $K>0$. We conclude that
\[
   Z(y) \geq \int_{\{\|\theta\|_{\mathcal H} < r\}} \exp(-K) \pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta) = \exp(-K)\pi_0(\{\|\theta\|_{\mathcal H} < r\}) > 0,
\]
because any nondegenerate Gaussian measure is full, i. e., $\pi_0(B) > 0$ for any non-empty open subset of ${\mathcal H}$ \cite[Proposition 1.25]{DaPrato:2006}.

(b) Since $\pi_0$ is a Gaussian measure, $\int \|\theta\|_{\mathcal H}^p\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)$ is finite for every $p\in\mathbb N$ \cite[Theorem 6.6]{Stuart_2010_acta_numerica}. Since $|\langle \theta, f \rangle| \leq \|\theta\|\|f\|$, the integral defining $\langle m, f\rangle$ is finite. A similar argument applies to the quadratic form defining the covariance. \qed

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:conv}
(a) Under assumptions (A1) and (A2), the posterior measures $\pi^{y_n}_n(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)$ given by \eqref{eq:approx} converge to $\pi^y(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)$ weakly in the sense of measures.\\[1pt]
(b) The means $m_n$ converge to $m$ in norm and the covariance operators converge in operator norm.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- head-3: body_md -->
(a) Assumption (A2) and the fact that the sequence $y_n$ is bounded implies that there is a constant $K'$ such that
\[
    \ell(\theta,y) \geq  \exp(-K'), \quad \ell_n(\theta,y_n) \geq  \exp(-K')
\]
for all $n\in\mathbb N$, whenever $\|\theta\|_{\mathcal H} < r$.
As in the proof of Proposition \ref{prop:postwelldefined}, this gives uniform bounds from below for $Z(y)\geq a > 0$ and all $Z_n(y_n)\geq a > 0$.

Now let $h:{\mathcal H}\to \mathbb R$ be a continuous function such that $|h(\theta)| \leq c(1+\|\theta\|_{\mathcal H}^p)$ for some $c>0$, $p \geq 0$ and all $\theta\in{\mathcal H}$. First note that
\[
  Z_n(y_n) = \int\exp\Big(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\|y_n - {\mathcal G}_n(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2\Big)\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)
    \to \int\exp\Big(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2\Big)\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta) = Z(y)
\]
as $n\to\infty$ by Lebesgue's dominated convergence theorem, since the integrands converge pointwise and are bounded by 1. Similarly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convergence}
\begin{array}{rcl}
   \displaystyle\int h(\theta)\pi^{y_n}_n(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta) & = & \displaystyle \int h(\theta)\frac{1}{Z_n(y_n)}
        \exp\Big(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\|y_n - {\mathcal G}_n(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2\Big)\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)\\
        &&\\
   & \to & \displaystyle\int h(\theta)\frac{1}{Z(y)}\exp\Big(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2\Big)\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta) \ =\  \int h(\theta)\pi^y(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)
    \end{array}
\end{equation}
because the integrands converge pointwise and are bounded by the $\pi_0$-integrable function $c(1+\|\theta\|_{\mathcal H}^p)/a$ (proof of part (b) in Proposition \ref{prop:postwelldefined}).

In particular, \eqref{eq:convergence} holds for any bounded continuous function ${\mathcal H}\to\mathbb R$, implying the weak convergence of $\pi^{y_n}_n(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)$ to $\pi^y(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)$.
Taking $h(\theta) = \langle\theta, f\rangle$ for $f\in{\mathcal H}$, we see that the means converge in the sense that
\[
   \langle m_n,f\rangle = \int\langle\theta,f\rangle \pi^{y_n}_n(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta) \to \int\langle\theta,f\rangle \pi^y(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta) = \langle m,f\rangle
\]
for every $f\in{\mathcal H}$. Similarly, the covariance operators converge when evaluated at fixed $f,g\in{\mathcal H}$.

(b) The norm convergence of the means $m_n$ to $m$ follows from the next argument. Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $\|m_n-m\|_{\mathcal H} = \sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal H} \leq 1}|\langle m_n -m,f\rangle|$, we can find $f_n$, $\|f_n\|_{\mathcal H} \leq 1$, such that
\[
   \|m_n-m\|_{\mathcal H} - \frac{\varepsilon}2 \leq |\langle m_n -m,f_n\rangle|.
\]
But $|\langle m_n -m,f_n\rangle|$ equals
\begin{eqnarray*}
 \Big\vert\int \langle\theta,f_n\rangle\left(\frac{1}{Z_n(y_n)}\exp\Big(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\|y_n - {\mathcal G}_n(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2\Big)
     - \frac{1}{Z(y)}\exp\Big(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2\Big)\right)\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)\Big\vert\\
   \leq \int \|\theta\|_{\mathcal H}\left|\frac{1}{Z_n(y_n)}\exp\Big(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\|y_n - {\mathcal G}_n(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2\Big)
     - \frac{1}{Z(y)}\exp\Big(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2\Big)\right|\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)
\end{eqnarray*}
As above, dominated convergence gives that the right-hand side of the inequality converges to zero. Thus, for sufficiently large $n$,
$\|m_n-m\|_{\mathcal H} - \frac{\varepsilon}2 \leq \frac{\varepsilon}2$ and so $\|m_n-m\|_{\mathcal H} \leq \varepsilon$ as desired. In a similar way one can show that the covariance operators converge in operator norm.
\qed

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:rates}
Assume (A1) -- (A3) and let $h:{\mathcal H}\to\mathbb R$ be measurable with respect to the Borel $\sigma$-algebras on ${\mathcal H}$ and $\mathbb R$ such that $|h(\theta)| \leq c(1+\|\theta\|_{\mathcal H}^p)$ for some $c>0, p \geq 0$ and all $\theta\in{\mathcal H}$. Then there is a constant $C''$ such that
\[
\Big\vert\int h(\theta)\pi^{y_n}_n(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta) - \int h(\theta)\pi^y(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)\Big\vert
    \leq  C''\big(\| y - y_n\|_{{\mathcal H}'} + \psi(n)\big).
\]
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- head-4: body_md -->
Making use of the inequality
\[
   |{\rm e}\hspace{1pt}^{-b} - {\rm e}\hspace{1pt}^{-a}| \leq |b-a|\quad\text{for}\ a,b\geq 0,
\]
we can estimate
\begin{eqnarray*}
|\ell_n(\theta,y_n) - \ell(\theta,y)| & = &
                          \Big\vert\exp\Big(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\|y_n - {\mathcal G}_n(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2\Big) - \exp\Big(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2\Big)\Big\vert\\
                         & \leq & \frac1{2\sigma^2}\Big\vert \|y_n - {\mathcal G}_n(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2 - \|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}^2\Big\vert\\
       & = & \frac1{2\sigma^2}\Big\vert \|y_n - {\mathcal G}_n(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'} - \|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}\Big\vert\Big(\|y_n - {\mathcal G}_n(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'} + \|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}\Big)
\end{eqnarray*}
Assuming that $\|y_n\|_{{\mathcal H}'}, \|y\|_{{\mathcal H}'}\leq r$, we can bound the second factor on the right-hand side by
\[
\|y_n - {\mathcal G}_n(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'} + \|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'} \leq 2\Big(r + C\big(1 + \|\theta\|_{\mathcal H}^q\big)\Big).
\]
Using the reverse triangle inequality, the first factor is estimated by
\begin{eqnarray*}
\big\vert \|y_n - {\mathcal G}_n(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'} - \|y - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'}\big\vert &\leq& \|y_n - {\mathcal G}_n(\theta) - (y - {\mathcal G}(\theta))\|_{{\mathcal H}'} \\
&\leq & \| y - y_n\|_{{\mathcal H}'} + \|{\mathcal G}_n(\theta) - {\mathcal G}(\theta)\|_{{\mathcal H}'} \\
& \leq & \| y - y_n\|_{{\mathcal H}'} + C\psi(n)\|\theta\|_{\mathcal H}^q.
\end{eqnarray*}
Since all powers of $\|\theta\|_{{\mathcal H}}$ are integrable with respect to $\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)$, we get
\[
  |Z_n(y_n) - Z(y)| \leq \int\big\vert\ell_n(\theta,y_n) - \ell(\theta,y)\big|\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)   \leq C'\big(\| y - y_n\|_{{\mathcal H}'} + \psi(n)\big)
\]
for some constant $C'$. Since $Z_n(y_n)$ and $Z(y)$ are bounded from below by some constant $a>0$, we also have
\[
\Big\vert\frac1{Z_n(y_n)} - \frac1{Z(y)}\Big\vert \leq \frac{C'}{a^2}\big(\| y - y_n\|_{{\mathcal H}'} + \psi(n)\big).
\]
Given a Borel measurable function $h:{\mathcal H}\to\mathbb R$ such that $|h(\theta)| \leq c(1+\|\theta\|_{\mathcal H}^p)$, we combine the estimates to get
\begin{eqnarray*}
\Big\vert\int h(\theta)\pi^{y_n}_n(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)\!\!\! &-&\!\!\! \int h(\theta)\pi^y(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)\Big\vert
    \ = \ \Big\vert\int h(\theta)\left(\frac{1}{Z_n(y_n)}\ell_n(\theta,y_n) - \frac1{Z(y)}\ell(\theta,y)\right)\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)\Big\vert\\[4pt]
    & \leq & \int |h(\theta)|\left(\frac{1}{Z_n(y_n)}\big\vert\ell_n(\theta,y_n) - \ell(\theta,y)\big\vert + \Big\vert\frac1{Z_n(y_n)} - \frac1{Z(y)}\Big\vert\big\vert\ell(\theta,y)\big\vert\right)\pi_0(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}\theta)\\[4pt]
    & \leq &  C''\Big(\| y - y_n\|_{{\mathcal H}'} + \psi(n)\Big)
\end{eqnarray*}
where we have used the previous estimates, the lower bound $a$ for $Z_n(y_n)$ and the fact that $\ell(\theta, y) \leq 1$.
This is the desired rate dependent version of \eqref{eq:convergence}. \qed

If the transition kernel $p$ of a Markov chain $(X_n)$ satisfies the detailed balance condition with a probability measure $\pi$, then $\pi$ is an invariant probability measure of the chain.

The assertion is easily proved by the observation that
\[

for every bounded measurable function $f$ on $S$, since $\int \pi(y,\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt} x) = 1$ for all y. Thus \eqref{eq:invariantprobability} holds. \qed

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:ET}
Let the Markov chain $(X_n)$ be $\pi$-irreducible with invariant probability measure $\pi$ and Harris recurrent. Let $f(x)$ be any function integrable with respect to $\pi(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt} x)$. Then
\[
  \lim_{N\to\infty} \frac1{N} \sum_{n=1}^N f(X_n) = \int_S f(x)\pi(\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt} x),
\]
$P_{x_0}$-almost surely for every initial value $x_0$, where $P_{x_0}$ is the measure on the space of trajectories introduced after \eqref{eq:Px}.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:CL}
Suppose that the chain $(X_n)$ is stationary and $\alpha$-mixing with $\alpha(n) = {\mathcal O}(n^{-5})$ and that $\ensuremath{\text{E}}(X_n) = 0$ and $\ensuremath{\text{E}}(X_n^{12}) < \infty$. Let
$S_N = X_1 + \cdots + X_N$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma2}
   \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\ensuremath{\text{V}}(S_N)}{N} = \gamma^2 = {\rm V}(X_1) + 2\sum_{k=1}^\infty{\rm COV}(X_1,X_{1+k})
\end{equation}
where the series is absolutely convergent. If $\gamma > 0$, the random variable $S_N/\gamma\sqrt{N}$ converges (in distribution) to the standard normal distribution.

The theorem is taken from \cite[Theorem 27.4]{Billingsley:1995}. \qed

To illustrate the top-down approach, consider the Hilbert space ${\mathcal H} = L^2(D)$ on the domain $D = [0,1]^d\subset {\mathbb R}^d$. The operator ${\mathcal L}$ on ${\mathcal H}$ is the Laplacian $-\Delta$ with operator domain ${\mathcal D}({\mathcal L}) = H^2(D)\cap H_0^1(D)$. The operator ${\mathcal L}$ is selfadjoint, positive and an isomorphism between ${\mathcal D}({\mathcal L})$ and ${\mathcal H}$. There is a complete orthonormal system $(\lambda_k, \varphi_k)$, indexed by $k = (k_1,\ldots, k_d)\in{\mathbb N}^d$ with eigenvalues and eigenfunctions given by
\[
    \lambda_k = \pi^2 |k|^2 = \pi^2(k_1^2 + \cdots + k_d^2),\qquad \varphi_k(x) = \prod_{i=1}^d\big(\sqrt{2}\sin(k_i\pi x_i)\big).
\]
We have that ${\mathcal L}g = \sum_{k\in{\mathbb N}^d} \lambda_k\langle g,\varphi_k\rangle\varphi_k$. For $\alpha \geq 0$, the sequence $\lambda_k^{-\alpha}$ is bounded and so
${\mathcal C} = {\mathcal L}^{-\alpha}$ maps ${\mathcal H} = L^2(D)$ continuously into itself via
${\mathcal C}f = \sum_{k\in{\mathbb N}^d} \lambda_k^{-\alpha}\langle f,\varphi_k\rangle\varphi_k$. The operator ${\mathcal C}$ is selfadjoint and positive. Further, for $\alpha > d/2$ it is trace class, because $\sum_{k\in{\mathbb N}^d} \lambda_k^{-\alpha} = \pi^{-2\alpha}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb N}^d}|k|^{-2\alpha}$ is finite. Thus ${\mathcal C}$ is the covariance operator of a Gaussian measure on ${\mathcal H}$. The operator ${\mathcal C}$ is an integral operator with kernel
\[
   C(x,y) = \sum_{k\in{\mathbb N}^d} \lambda_k^{-\alpha} \varphi_k(x)\varphi_k(y) \in L^2(D\times D).
\]
The eigenfunctions $\varphi_k$ are uniformly bounded by $2^{d/2}$ and the sequence $\lambda_k^{-\alpha}$ is summable, thus the convergence is actually uniform in $(x,y)$ and the kernel is a continuous function on $D\times D$.

Consider the Hilbert space ${\mathcal H} = L^2(0,1)$. The operator ${\mathcal L}$ will be the Laplacian $-\partial_x^2$ with operator domain
${\mathcal D}({\mathcal L}) = H^2_{\rm per}(0,1) = \{f\in H^2(0,1): f(0) = f(1), f'(0) = f'(1)\}$. The operator ${\mathcal L}$ is selfadjoint, positive and an isomorphism between ${\mathcal D}({\mathcal L})$ and its range ${\mathcal R}({\mathcal L}) = \{g\in L^2(D): \int_0^1 g(x){\rm d}x = 0\}$.
The operator ${\mathcal L}$ has a sequence of eigenvalues $\lambda_0 = 0$, $\lambda_k = (2\pi k)^2$ of multiplicity two for $k\geq 1$ with eigenfunctions $\psi_0 = 1$,
$\varphi_k(x) = \sqrt{2}\sin 2k\pi x$, $\psi_k(x) = \sqrt{2}\cos 2k\pi x$.

For $\alpha = 1$ one can get an explicit formula for the kernel $C(x,y)$ of ${\mathcal C}$. Comparing with the Fourier series for
\[
    \frac12\Big(z-\frac12\Big)^2 = \frac{1}{24} + 2 \sum _{k=1}^\infty (2k\pi)^{-2}\cos 2 k\pi z
\]
we find that
\begin{eqnarray*}
  C(x,y) = c + 2 \sum _{k=1}^\infty (2k\pi)^{-2}\cos 2 k\pi (x-y) = \frac12\Big(x-y-\frac12\Big)^2 + c - \frac{1}{24}.
\end{eqnarray*}
In the bottom-up approach, we would have started with the Fourier expansion of $C(x,0)$ and then would have worked upwards to construct a Gaussian, trigonometric random field.

The covariance operator may be taken as a negative power of a general second order elliptic partial differential operator ${\mathcal L}$ satisfying suitable conditions
\cite[Assumption 2.9]{Stuart_2010_acta_numerica}. This gives us the opportunity to have a glimpse at a different road to covariances, the \emph{SPDE approach} \cite{Lindgren:2022}. Informally speaking, if a random field $\Theta$ on a domain $D$ is defined as $\Theta = {\mathcal L}^{-1}\dot{W}$, where $\dot{W}$ is Gaussian white noise on $D$, then $\Theta$ has covariance operator ${\mathcal L}^{-2}$. To exemplify this, consider the Sturm-Liouville operator
\[
   {\mathcal L} f(x) = - \frac{\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}}{\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt} x}\left(p(x)\frac{\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt}}{\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt} x}f(x)\right) + q(x)f(x)
\]
on $D = (0,1)$ where $p(x) > 0$, $q(x)\geq 0$. We assume that the coefficient functions $p,q$ are sufficiently regular so that ${\mathcal L}$ is an isomorphism from
$H^1_0(D)$ to $H^{-1}(D)$. Gaussian white noise $\dot{W}$ on $D = (0,1)$ can be seen as the pathwise weak derivative of Wiener process, thus certainly belongs to $H^{-1}(D)$. The equation ${\mathcal L}(\Theta) = \dot{W}$ has a unique solution $\Theta \in H^1_0(D)$.
It can be written as an It\^o integral in terms of the Green function $G$ of ${\mathcal L}$,
\[
   \Theta(x) = \int_0^1 G(x,z)\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt} W(z).
\]
The random field $\Theta$ has mean zero and covariance (using the It\^o isometry)
\[
  \ensuremath{\text{E}}(\Theta(x)\Theta(y)) = \ensuremath{\text{E}}\left(\int_0^1 G(x,z)\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt} W(z)\int_0^1 G(y,z)\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt} W(z)\right) = \int_0^1 G(x,z)G(y,z)\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt} z.
\]
By the symmetry of Green's function, the latter term equals $\int_0^1 G(x,z)G(z,y)\hspace{0.0pt}{\rm d}\hspace{0.0pt} z$ and hence coincides with the integral kernel of ${\mathcal L}^{-2}$.

<!-- head-15: body_md -->
The concept of the SPDE approach goes back at least to \cite{Whittle:1954} and was notably used to construct random fields with a Mat\'{e}rn covariance $C(x,y) \sim (\kappa(|x-y|)^\nu K_\nu(\kappa|x-y|)$ as solutions to $(\kappa^2 - \Delta)^{\alpha/2}\Theta = \dot{W}$ on $\mathbb R^d$, where $\kappa, \nu > 0$, $\alpha = \nu + d/2$ and $K_\nu$ is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. We refer to \cite{Lindgren:2011} and the survey paper \cite{Lindgren:2022}, which also discusses an extension to the non-constant, anisotropic case obtained through $$\tau(x)\big(\kappa(x)^2 - \nabla\cdot H(x)\nabla\big)^{\alpha/2}\Theta(x) = \dot{W}(x).$$ Further, $\kappa(x)$ may be taken as a random field itself, as done in \cite{Roininen:2019} with $\alpha = 2$. Mat\'{e}rn covariances together with a Karhunen-Lo\`{e}ve expansion were also used in \cite{Taghizadeh:2025}.
