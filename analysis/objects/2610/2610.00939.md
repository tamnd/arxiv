<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:p5640}
  Let
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:p5640-data}
    n=2,\qquad m=2,\qquad
    A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
    b=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
    x_0=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1/3\end{pmatrix}.
  \end{equation}
  Every stepsize recomputed by CSD is $1/2$, and the complete orbit is
  \begin{align}
    g_k&=2^{-k}\begin{pmatrix}1\\(-1)^k\end{pmatrix},
      \label{eq:p5640-gradient}\\
    x_k=e_k&=2^{-k}\begin{pmatrix}1\\(-1)^k/3\end{pmatrix}.
      \label{eq:p5640-error}
  \end{align}
  It is nonterminating and satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:p5640-ratio}
    \frac{\lVerte_{k+1}\rVert_2}{\lVerte_k\rVert_2}=\frac12
    \qquad(k\ge0).
  \end{equation}
  Consequently, the assertion of universal $R$-superlinear convergence for
  $m\ge\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil$ does not hold.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Here $x_*=0$ and $g_0=(1,1)^{\mathsf{T}}$.  Whenever a cycle starts with two
gradient components of equal magnitude, its recomputed stepsize is
\[
  \frac{g^{\mathsf{T}}g}{g^{\mathsf{T}}Ag}=\frac12.
\]
For this stepsize,
\[
  I-\frac12 A=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\0&-1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The two component magnitudes therefore remain equal after each of the two
updates in a cycle.  Induction over the cycles gives
\eqref{eq:p5640-gradient}; applying $A^{-1}$ gives
\eqref{eq:p5640-error}, and~\eqref{eq:p5640-ratio} follows.  In particular
$\lVerte_k\rVert_2=(\sqrt{10}/3)\,2^{-k}$, so the error sequence has
exactly $R$-linear convergence with factor $1/2$ and hence, by the
observation of Section~\ref{sec:csd}, is not $R$-superlinearly convergent.  Finally, $m=2=\lceil(2+1)/2\rceil$ is the
smallest admissible integer cycle length, so the universal assertion fails at
its threshold.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:invariant-orbit}
  Let $A\succ0$ have orthonormal eigenvectors $u,v$ with distinct eigenvalues
  $0<\lambda<\mu$, respectively.  Fix any cycle length $m\ge1$, any
  $c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$, and any $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$.  Initialize CSD so
  that
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-gradient-general}
    g_0=c(u+\sigma v).
  \end{equation}
  Define
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
    \rho=\frac{\mu-\lambda}{\mu+\lambda}\in(0,1).
  \end{equation}
  Then every stepsize recomputed at a cycle boundary is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-step}
    \alpha_{m\ell}^{\mathrm{SD}}=\frac{2}{\lambda+\mu},
    \qquad \ell=0,1,\ldots,
  \end{equation}
  and the full orbit is
  \begin{align}
    g_k&=c\rho^k\bigl(u+(-1)^k\sigma v\bigr),
      \label{eq:gradient-orbit}\\
    e_k&=c\rho^k\bigl(\lambda^{-1}u
      +(-1)^k\sigma\mu^{-1}v\bigr).
      \label{eq:error-orbit}
  \end{align}
  In particular,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-ratios}
    \frac{\lVertg_{k+1}\rVert_2}{\lVertg_k\rVert_2}
    =\frac{\lVerte_{k+1}\rVert_2}{\lVerte_k\rVert_2}
    =\rho
    \qquad(k\ge0).
  \end{equation}
  None of the full gradient sequence, the full error sequence, and their
  cycle-boundary subsequences converges $R$-superlinearly.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For a vector of the form $s(u+\tau v)$ with $s\ne0$ and
$\tau\in\{-1,1\}$, orthonormality and the eigenvalue relations give
\[
  \frac{g^{\mathsf{T}}g}{g^{\mathsf{T}}Ag}
  =\frac{2s^2}{(\lambda+\mu)s^2}
  =\frac{2}{\lambda+\mu}.
\]
For this stepsize,
\[
  (I-\alpha A)u=\rho u,
  \qquad
  (I-\alpha A)v=-\rho v.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:gradient-update} therefore changes the sign of the
$v$-component and multiplies both component magnitudes by $\rho$.  After any
number of updates, and hence at every cycle boundary, the two components
still have equal magnitude.  Induction proves~\eqref{eq:constant-step} and
\eqref{eq:gradient-orbit}.  Applying $A^{-1}$ gives
\eqref{eq:error-orbit}.

The norms of the two parenthesized vectors in
\eqref{eq:gradient-orbit} and~\eqref{eq:error-orbit} are independent of $k$,
so~\eqref{eq:constant-ratios} follows.  The full sequences are geometric with
factor $\rho>0$, while their cycle-boundary subsequences are geometric with
factor $\rho^m>0$ in the cycle index; by the observation of
Section~\ref{sec:csd}, none of them converges $R$-superlinearly.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:three-spectrum}
  Let
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:three-spectrum-data}
    n=3,\qquad m=2,\qquad A=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,3),\qquad b=0,
  \end{equation}
  and set
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:three-spectrum-initial}
    g_0=\begin{pmatrix}(2+\sqrt2)/4\\[2pt]1/2\\[2pt](2-\sqrt2)/4\end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    x_0=A^{-1}g_0
      =\begin{pmatrix}(2+\sqrt2)/4\\[2pt]1/4\\[2pt](2-\sqrt2)/12\end{pmatrix}.
  \end{equation}
  Define
  \[
    \mu_- =2-\frac{\sqrt2}{2},\qquad
    \mu_+ =2+\frac{\sqrt2}{2},\qquad
    \alpha_\pm=\frac1{\mu_\pm}.
  \]
  The CSD orbit is nonterminating, has full support (every component of
  $g_k$ is nonzero for every $k$), and is nonresonant: no cycle-boundary
  stepsize annihilates a spectral component, i.e., $\mu_\pm\ne\lambda_i$ for
  every $i$.  The orbit has projective period two at cycle boundaries: writing
  $w_{k,i}=g_{k,i}^2/\sum_{j=1}^3g_{k,j}^2$, the normalized squared component
  weights $w_{2\ell}$ alternate between two distinct interior points of the simplex.
  The stepsize pattern is
  $\alpha_-,\alpha_-,\alpha_+,\alpha_+,\ldots$, and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:three-spectrum-contraction}
    g_{k+4}=\frac1{49}g_k,\qquad e_{k+4}=\frac1{49}e_k
    \qquad(k\ge0).
  \end{equation}
  Consequently the orbit is $R$-linearly convergent with root
  factor $49^{-1/4}=1/\sqrt7$, but not $R$-superlinearly convergent.  It is an
  interior, nonresonant obstruction and is not a two-dimensional
  balanced-zigzag orbit.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For a nonzero gradient $g$, write
  $w_i=g_i^2/\sum_{j=1}^3g_j^2$ and
  $\mu=\sum_{i=1}^3\lambda_iw_i$, where
  $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)=(1,2,3)$.
  At a cycle boundary the exact stepsize is
  $\alpha=1/\mu$; after two updates the $i$th gradient component is multiplied
  by $(1-\lambda_i/\mu)^2$.  Therefore the normalized squared component
  weights obey the map
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:three-spectrum-weight-map}
    T_i(w)=\frac{w_i|\mu-\lambda_i|^4}
      {\sum_jw_j|\mu-\lambda_j|^4}.
  \end{equation}
  Let $w^-=(g_{0,1}^2,g_{0,2}^2,g_{0,3}^2)$, whose entries sum to one, so
  that $w^-$ is the initial weight vector; let $w^+$ be obtained by
  interchanging $\sqrt2$ with $-\sqrt2$ in the three entries.  Explicitly,
  \[
    w^-=\left(\frac{3+2\sqrt2}{8},\frac14,\frac{3-2\sqrt2}{8}\right),
    \qquad
    w^+=\left(\frac{3-2\sqrt2}{8},\frac14,\frac{3+2\sqrt2}{8}\right).
  \]
  Both vectors are positive and sum to one, so they are interior points of the
  simplex.  Their weighted means are $\mu_-$ and $\mu_+$, respectively.
  Since
  \[
    (1-\mu_-)(1-\mu_+)=\frac12,\quad
    (2-\mu_-)(2-\mu_+)=-\frac12,\quad
    (3-\mu_-)(3-\mu_+)=\frac12,
  \]
  direct substitution gives
  \[
    w_i^-|\mu_--\lambda_i|^4=\frac14w_i^+,
    \qquad
    w_i^+|\mu_+-\lambda_i|^4=\frac14w_i^-.
  \]
  Hence $T(w^-)=w^+$ and $T(w^+)=w^-$, so
  $w_{4\ell}=w^-$ and $w_{4\ell+2}=w^+$ for every $\ell\ge0$.
  This proves the cycle-boundary projective period two
  and the displayed stepsize pattern.  Since $\mu_\pm\notin\{1,2,3\}$, each
  update multiplies every gradient component by a nonzero factor
  $1-\lambda_i\alpha_\pm$; hence all three components are nonzero at every
  iteration, so the orbit is nonterminating, has full support, and is
  nonresonant.  Moreover
  $\mu_-\mu_+=7/2$, so over the four updates the $i$th gradient component is
  multiplied by
  \[
    \left[\frac{(\mu_--\lambda_i)(\mu_+-\lambda_i)}
      {\mu_-\mu_+}\right]^2=\frac1{49}
  \]
  for each $i$.  Any four consecutive updates contain two factors of each
  type, and these diagonal update matrices commute.  Thus the identity holds
  for every starting index $k$, proving~\eqref{eq:three-spectrum-contraction}; applying
  $A^{-1}$ gives the error relation.  The subsequence $\{\lVerte_{4k}\rVert_2\}$ is
  positive geometric with ratio $1/49$, so by the observation of
  Section~\ref{sec:csd} the orbit is not $R$-superlinearly convergent, while
  \eqref{eq:three-spectrum-contraction} gives the stated $R$-linear root
  factor $49^{-1/4}$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
For every $n\ge2$ and $m\ge1$, augment
$\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\mu)$ by arbitrary positive diagonal entries and
initialize the added gradient components at zero.  They remain zero, so the
active plane follows Proposition~\ref{prop:invariant-orbit}.  The obstruction
thus occurs in every dimension and at every cycle length as a lower-dimensional
support phenomenon.  Theorem~\ref{thm:three-spectrum} is different: all three
spectral components are nonzero, so its obstruction is full-support in the
three-dimensional problem.  Either theorem alone disproves the universal
statement.
