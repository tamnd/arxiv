<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{lplq}
Let $ 1\leqslant p< \infty$ and denote by $\|\cdot\|_{p}$ the norm in the space $L^{p}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d))$. 
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item For $t>0$  
we have 
\begin{equation}
\label{dispersive}
\left\| e^{t\Delta} x\right\|_{L^p} \leqslant Me^{-\alpha t}\left\| x \right\|_{L^p}\hbox{ for all }x \in L^p(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d)),
\end{equation}
 where $M$ and $\alpha$ are certain positive constants.  
                                                                                                                           
                 
                                                                                                                                                                                           
               
                                                               
\item  For $p$ and $q$ such that $1<p\leqslant q <\infty$, and for all $t>0$ we have
\begin{equation} \label{lplq1}
\left\| e^{t\mathbf{\Delta}}\operatorname{div} f \right\|_{L^q} \leqslant [h_d(t)]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{d}}e^{-t\beta} \left\| f\right\|_{L^p}
\hbox{ for all  $f \in L^p(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d))$}
\end{equation} 
 where $h_d(t): = C\max\left( \frac{1}{t^{d/2}},1 \right)$, 
   and $\beta$ is certain positive constant depending only on $p, q$, and  $d$.
                                           
\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{Inertial} Consider the projection $P$ as above, and 
                                                                    
  let $\hat{u}$ be a periodic mild solution to \eqref{NSESd}.
An \emph{inertial manifold}  for mild solutions to \eqref{NSESd} around  $\hat{u}$ is a Lipschitz surface $\mathbb{M}$ in $X:=L_\sigma^2(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d))$ which  is the graph of a Lipschitz function $\Phi: \mathrm{Im} P \rightarrow (I-P)X$, i.e.,
$$\mathbb{M} = \left\{ x+\Phi x \mid x\in \mathrm{Im}P \right\},$$
and the following conditions are satisfied:
\begin{itemize}
                                                                                                                                     
                                                                                                 
            
                                                                                                                                                                                                    
                                                                                         
\item[(i)] $\mathbb{M}$ is positively $\hat{u}$-invariant under Eq. \eqref{NSESd}, i.e., if a mild  solution $x(t), t\geqslant s$, of Eq. \eqref{NSESd} satisfies $x(s)-\hat{u}(s) \in \mathbb{M}$, 
then we have that $x(t)-\hat{u}(t) \in  \mathbb{M}$ for $t\geqslant s$.
\item[(ii)]  $\mathbb{M}$  exponentially $\hat{u}$-attracts all the mild solutions to Eq. \eqref{NSESd} in the sense that for each mild solution $u(\cdot)$ to  Eq. \eqref{NSESd} and any fixed $s\in \mathbb{R}$,                                                                       
 there is a mild solution $u^* (\cdot)$ of \eqref{NSESd} such that $u^*(t)\in \mathbb{M}$ for $t\geqslant s$ and
\begin{equation}\label{attract}
\left\Vertu(t)-\hat{u}(t)-u^*(t)\right\Vert \leqslant Ce^{-\gamma(t-s)} \hbox{  for all  } t\geqslant s \hbox{  and some constants  } \gamma, C > 0.
\end{equation}
                                                                                                          
                                                                                                                                                                          
\end{itemize}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{mainlin}\mbox{}\\
 Suppose that  $f\in C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))$ is 
   $T$-periodic. Then,  there exists a unique initial vector field   $x^*\in L_\sigma^p(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d)) $ such that
    the mild solution $\hat{u}$  to  \eqref{oelin} with initial vector field $x^*$ is 
  $T$-periodic. Moreover, the $T$-periodic mild solution $\hat{u}$  satisfies 
\begin{equation}\label{bdd}
  \|\hat{u}\|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, L_{\sigma}^{p}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d))}\leqslant \tilde{M}\|f\|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))}\hbox{ for  }\tilde{M}:=\tilde C\beta^{ \frac{d-p}{2p}}\mathbf{\Gamma}(1-\theta)+\frac{1}{\beta}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{\Gamma}$ standing for the Gamma function.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The proof of this lemma may be done using Serrin's method (see \cite{Ser59} for the original approach, and  
see \cite{NV2025} for implementation  of Serrin's  method in framework of diffusion equations on spheres).   
Take 
 $u(t)=\int_s^te^{(t- \tau)\mathbf{\Delta}}\mathbb P\operatorname{div} f(\tau)d\tau $ for all $t\ge s$, 
 $t, s \in\mathbb{R} $. 
 Firstly, setting $X:=L_\sigma^p(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d))$ and  using inequality \eqref{lplq1} 
we have
                                                                                                                  
   
      
                                                                                                                      
$$\|u(t)\|_{X}=\|\int^{t}_se^{(t-\tau)\mathbf{\Delta}}\mathbb P\operatorname{div} f(\tau)d\tau\|_{X}\leqslant C_1\int^{t}_s\|e^{(t-\tau)\mathbf{\Delta}}\operatorname{div} f(\tau)\|_{X}d\tau
$$
$$\leqslant
 C_1\int_s^{t}
 [h_d(t-\tau)]^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{d}}e^{-\beta(t-\tau)} \left\| f(\tau) \right\|_{L^{p/2}}d\tau \hbox{ for all }t\ge s.$$
 Since $h_d(t)= C \max \left( \frac{1}{t^{d/2}},1 \right)$,  it follows  from above 
inequality that
\begin{align*}
   \| u(t)\|_{X}&\leqslant 
   C_1\int_s^{t} 
   \left([C\tau]^{-\frac d2 (\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{d})}+1\right) e^{-\beta(t-\tau)}d\tau\|f\|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))}\\
   &\leqslant  \left(\tilde C\beta^{ \frac{d-p}{2p}}\mathbf{\Gamma}(1-\theta)+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\|f\|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))}\hbox{ for all }t\ge s
   \end{align*}
   where  $\mathit{\mathbf{\Gamma}}$ is  the Gamma function,
   $\theta:=\frac d2 (\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{d})<1$ since  $p>d$, and
   $\tilde C:=C_1C^{-\frac d2 (\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{d})}$.
 Hence,  $u\in C_b([s, \infty), X)$ and
 \begin{equation}\label{esu}
 \| u\|_{C_b([s, \infty), X)}\leqslant \tilde{M}\|f\|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))}\hbox{ for }\tilde M:= \tilde C\beta^{ \frac{d-p}{2p}}\mathbf{\Gamma}(1-\theta)+\frac{1}{\beta}.
\end{equation}

Next, we will prove that the sequence $\{{u}(s+nT)\}_{n\in \mathbb{N}}$ is a Cauchy sequence in $X$.   
Indeed,  set $w(t)={u}(t+(m-n)T)$ for any fixed couple of natural  numbers  $m>n \in \mathbb{N}$.  The periodicity of $f$ implies that 
 $w$ can be rewritten as
\begin{equation}\label{eqw}
w(t)=e^{(t- s)\mathbf{\Delta}}{u}(s+(m-n)T)+\int_s^te^{-(t- \tau)\mathbf{\Delta}}\mathbb P \operatorname{div} f(\tau)d\tau\hbox{ for all }t\ge s.
\end{equation}

\def\mathbf{\Delta}{\Delta_H}
 Now, it follows from \eqref{dispersive} that 
$$
\|{u}(t)-w(t)\|=\|e^{(t-s)\mathbf{\Delta}}(u(s)-w(s))\|\leqslant Me^{-\alpha (t-s)}\|u(s)-w(s)\|\leqslant Ce^{-\alpha (t-s)}, \; \; t>s$$
for $C:=\frac{2M }{\alpha}\|f\|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))}$ which is independent of $m$ and $ n$.

Substituting $t:=s+nT$ in the above inequality we have 
$
\|{u}(s+nT)-{u}(s+mT)\|\leqslant Ce^{-\alpha nT}$ 
for all $m>n\in \mathbb{N}$. Therefore,
 $\{{u}(s+nT)\}_{n\in \mathbb{N}}$ is a Cauchy sequence in  $X$ which is a Banach space.
Hence,   
 $\{{u}(s+nT)\}_{n\in \mathbb{N}}$ is convergent in $X$, and we put
$x^*:= \lim_{n\to \infty}{u}(s+nT)\in X.$

\def\mathbf{\Delta}{\mathbf{\Delta}}
\medskip
Plugging  $x^*$ as the initial vector field, we
 now show that the mild solution 
$$\hat{u}(t)=
e^{(t-s)\mathbf{\Delta}}{x^*}+\int_s^te^{(t-\tau)\mathbf{\Delta}}g(\tau)d\tau$$
is $T$-periodic. 
To do this, we set
${v(t)}:=\int_s^{t+nT}e^{(t+nT-\tau)\mathbf{\Delta}}\mathbb P\operatorname{div} g(\tau)d\tau $ 
for arbitrary fixed $n\in \mathbb{N}$ and all $t\ge s$, that is, $v(t)=u(t+nT)$ for 
\begin{equation}\label{defu}
u(t)=\int_s^{t}e^{(t-\tau)\mathbf{\Delta}}\mathbb P\operatorname{div} f(\tau)d\tau.
\end{equation}
 Once again,  it follows from periodicity of $g$  that $v$ satisfies
$${v(t)}=e^{(t-s)\mathbf{\Delta}}u(s
+nT)+\int_s^{t}e^{(t-\tau)\mathbf{\Delta}}\mathbb P\operatorname{div} f(\tau)d\tau $$
 for $u$ defined as in \eqref{defu}.

Thus, 
$
\|\hat{u}(s+T)-v(s+T)\|_X=\left\Verte^{T\mathbf{\Delta}}({\hat{u}(s)}-v(s))\right\Vert_X\leqslant
 \left\Verte^{T\mathbf{\Delta}}\right\Vert\|{\hat{u}(s)}-v(s)\|_X.$

This implies 
 $
\|\hat{u}(s+T)-{u}(s+(n+1)T)\|_X\leqslant \left\Verte^{T\mathbf{\Delta}}\right\Vert\|{x^*}-{u}(s+nT)\|_X.
$
Letting $n\to \infty$ and  using the limit   
$\lim\limits_{n\to \infty}{u}(s+nT)=x^*=\hat{u}(s)$ in $X$ (see above) we have
 $\hat{u}(s+T)=\hat{u}(s).$  
We next prove  that  $\hat{u}(t+T)=\hat{u}(t)$ for all $t\ge s$. 
In fact, set  
$\hat{w}:=\hat{u}(t+T)$ for all $t\ge s$.  The periodicity of $f$  yields 
 $$\hat{w}(t)=e^{(t-s)\mathbf{\Delta}}\hat{u}(s+T)+\int_s^te^{(t-s)\mathbf{\Delta}}\mathbb P\operatorname{div} f(\tau)d\tau\hbox{ for all }t\ge s.$$
 Since $\hat{u}(s+T)=\hat{u}(s)$ we have 
 $$\hat{w}(t)=e^{-(t-s)\mathbf{\Delta}}\hat{u}(s)+\int_s^te^{-(t-s)\mathbf{\Delta}}\mathbb P\operatorname{div} f(\tau)d\tau=\hat{u}(t)\hbox{ for all }t\ge s.$$
 That is, $\hat{u}(t)=\hat{w}(t)=\hat{u}(t+T)$ for all $t\ge s$, implying that  
  $\hat{u}(t)$ is  $T$-periodic. The inequality $\eqref{bdd}$ can be obtained from  \eqref{esu} in combination with the properties  $\|u^*\|_X\leqslant \|u\|_{C_b}$ and 
$\|\hat{u}\|_{C_b}=\sup_{s\leqslant t\leqslant s+T}\|\hat{u}(t)\|_X$ because of the periodicity of $\hat{u}$.

\noindent 
The uniqueness of the initial vector field $x^*$ and therefore of the $T$-periodic solution  follows from \eqref{dispersive}. 
Namely, suppose that $u$ and $v$ are two $T$-periodic mild  solutions of Equation  \eqref{oelin} subject to initial vector fields    $x_1^*=u_s$ and $x_2^*=v_s$, respectively. Then 
$u(t)-v(t)=e^{(t-s)\mathbf{\Delta}}(x_1^*-x_2^*)$. From  \eqref{dispersive} we arrive at  $$\|u(t)-v(t)\|_X=\|e^{(t-s)\mathbf{\Delta}}(x_1^*-x_2^*)\|_X\leqslant Me^{-\alpha (t-s)}\|x_1^*-x_2^*\|_X.$$

Therefore, $\lim_{t\to \infty}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_X=0$. In combination with periodicity and continuity of $u$ and $v$, we have that $u(t)=v(t)$ for all $t\in \mathbb{R}$.

We consider the following ball
$$ 
\mathcal{B}^{per}_{\rho} =\{ v \in C_b(\mathbb{R}, L_\sigma^p(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d))) :  v \, \text{ is $T$-periodic and } \, \|v \|_{C_b} \leqslant \rho  \}. 
$$
Clearly, for each $v\in \mathcal{B}^{per}_{\rho}$, $v\otimes v$ belongs to $C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))$  and is  $T$-periodic.  
On $\mathcal{B}^{per}_{\rho}$,  we determine the transformation $\mathcal{T}$ as follows:
Consider the equation 
\begin{equation}\label{ns1}
                                 
u(t)= e^{(t-s)\mathbf{\Delta}}x+\int_s^t e^{-(t-\tau)\mathbf{\Delta}}\mathbb P \, \operatorname{div}(-(v\otimes v)(\tau)+ f(\tau))d\tau\hbox{\ for all }t\ge s.
\end{equation}
                                                                                                                                         
Then, for $v\in  \mathcal B_\rho^{T}$ we put 
\begin{equation}\label{defphi}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{T}(v)&=u\hbox{ where 
$u\in C_b(\mathbb{R}, X)$ is the unique $T$-periodic solution}\cr
& \hskip 5cm\hbox{  to Equation \eqref{ns1},}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $X:=L_\sigma^p(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d))$, as previously. 
The existence and uniqueness of the above  periodic solution $u$ is proved in Lemma  \ref{mainlin}. Hence, $\mathcal{T}$ is well-defined.

                                                                                                    
                              
                                          
               
                                                                                                          
Furthermore,  
using the fact that $v\in \mathcal{B}^{per}_\rho$, by \eqref{bdd} and H\"older inequality we derive
$$
\| u \|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, X}) \leqslant  \tilde{M}(\| v\otimes v \|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))} + \|f\|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))})\leqslant$$

$$
Hence, $\mathcal{T}$ maps from $\mathcal{B}^{per}_{\rho}$ into itself provided $\| f \|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))}$ and  $\rho$ are sufficiently small. 


For $v_1, v_2 \in \mathcal{B}^{per}_{\rho}$ set  $\mathcal{T} v_1:=z_1$,  $\mathcal{T} v_2:=z_2$.  From H\"older's inequality it follows that   
 \begin{align}
\| z_1 - z_2 \|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, X)} &\leqslant \tilde{M}\| v_1\otimes v_1 - v_2\otimes v_2 \|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))}  \notag \\

&\leqslant  2 \tilde{M}  \rho  \| v_1 - v_2 \|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, X)}. \notag
\end{align} 
Thus, if  $\rho$ and $\| f \|_{C_b(\mathbb{R}, L^{p/2}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))}$  
are sufficiently small, then $\mathcal{T}$ is a selfmap in $\mathcal{B}^{per}_{\rho}$, and  a contraction. Therefore, $\mathcal{T}$ has a unique fixed point, and this is the unique  periodic mild solution to equation \eqref{NSESd}
in a small closed ball of $C_b(\mathbb{R}, L_\sigma^{p}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d)))$.

We remark first that, since $\mathbb{S}^d$ is compact, the Navier-Stokes equations on $S^d$ is dissipative as proved below:
                                                 
                                                                    
                  
Indeed, for the Navier-Stokes equation
\begin{equation}
    u_t+\nabla_u u+\operatorname{grad}\pi
    =\Delta u+\operatorname{div}f,
    \qquad
    \operatorname{div}u=0,
\end{equation}
taking the \(L^2(\mathbb{S}^d)\)-inner product with \(u\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{3.171}
    \frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
    +\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
    =-(f(t),\nabla u(t)).
\end{equation}

Indeed,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    (\nabla_u u,u)
    &=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^d}u\cdot\nabla |u|^2\,dS =-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^d}
(\operatorname{div}u)|u|^2\,dS
    =0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Since \(S^d\) is compact and the  Ebin-Marsden
Laplacian has strictly positive first eigenvalue,
\[
    \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2
    \geq \lambda_1\|u\|_{L^2}^2\hbox{ for }\lambda_1>0.
\]
Hence
\begin{equation}    \label{3.18}
    \frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
+\lambda_1\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
    \leqslant C\|f(t)\|_{L^2}^2.
\end{equation}
Thus the equation is dissipative in \(L^2\), that is, bounded sets are
eventually absorbed into a bounded \(L^2\)-ball.

For \(f=0\), in particular,
\begin{equation}
    \|u(t)\|_{L^2}
    \leqslant e^{-\lambda_1t/2}\|u(0)\|_{L^2}.
    \tag{3.19}
\end{equation}
This is valid for every \(d\geq2\). 



Then, since $f\in C_b(\mathbb{R}, H^{1,1}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d\otimes T\mathbb{S}^d)))$, in a similar way as in Theorem \ref{mainnon}, we have that the periodic solution $\hat{u}$ belongs to $C_b(\mathbb{R}, H^{1}(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d)))$
We next  consider equation for the perturbation around the periodic solution. As previously, take any solution
 $u$ of Equation \eqref{NSESd}  and put 
$
w(t):=u(t)-\widehat u(t).
$
Since both \(u\) and \(\widehat u\) satisfy the Navier-Stokes equation \eqref{NSESd},  subtraction gives
$$
\partial_t w
+
P\operatorname{div}
\left[
(w+\widehat u)\otimes(w+\widehat u)
-\widehat u\otimes\widehat u
\right]
=
\mathbf{\Delta} w.
$$
Hence, with
$
A:=-\mathbf{\Delta},
$ 
we obtain 
\begin{equation}\label{3.9}
\partial_t w+Aw=F(w)
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{3.10}
F(w)
=
-P\operatorname{div}
\left[
w\otimes w+w\otimes\widehat u
+\widehat u\otimes w
\right].    
\end{equation}
Clearly,
 $
F(0)=0.
 $ 
We next prove the local Lipschitz continuity of $F$. To this purpose,
 for \(v,z\in H^1(\Gamma(T\mathbb{S}^d))\), Sobolev embedding yields
 $
H^1(\Gamma(TS^d))
\hookrightarrow
L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}(\Gamma(TS^d)).
$ 
Therefore,
\begin{equation}\label{3.11
}
    \begin{aligned}
\|P\operatorname{div}(v\otimes z)\|_{H^{-1}}
&\leqslant C\|v\otimes z\|_{L^2}\leqslant
C\|v\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}}
\|z\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}}\leqslant C\|v\|_{H^1}\|z\|_{H^1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}


Consequently, for \(w_1,w_2\in H^1\),

\begin{equation}\label{3.12}
\begin{aligned}
\|F(w_1)-F(w_2)\|_{H^{-1}}
\leqslant
C\bigl(
\|w_1\|_{H^1}
+\|w_2\|_{H^1}
+\|\widehat u\|_{H^1}
\bigr)
\|w_1-w_2\|_{H^1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Thus \(F\) is locally Lipschitz from \(H^1\) into
\(H^{-1}\).

                                
                                                                                     
   
               
   
          
   
                               
     
                     
                 
                              
   

We then consider the cut-off equations \eqref{CutEq} and  
prove that the cut-off nonlinear operator $F_R$ defined as in \eqref{CutEq} is  globally  Lipschitz. To do this,  
fix \(R>0\). Let
$\chi_R$ by the cut-off function as in \eqref{cut}. Recall that  
$
F_R(w)
=
\chi_R(\|w\|_{H^1})F(w).
$ 
By construction,
$
F_R(w)=F(w)
\text{ if }\|w\|_{H^1}\leqslant R.
           
$

                                                                     
Let \(w_1,w_2\in H^1\). We write
\begin{equation}\label{3.16}
    \begin{aligned}
F_R(w_1)-F_R(w_2)
={}&
\chi_R(\|w_1\|_{H^1})
\bigl(F(w_1)-F(w_2)\bigr)
+
\bigl[
\chi_R(\|w_1\|_{H^1})
-\chi_R(\|w_2\|_{H^1})
\bigr]F(w_2).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
If both \(\|w_1\|_{H^1}\) and \(\|w_2\|_{H^1}\) are at most \(2R\), then (3.12) gives
\begin{equation}\label{3.17}
    \|F(w_1)-F(w_2)\|_{H^{-1}}
\leqslant
C(4R+K)\|w_1-w_2\|_{H^1},
\hbox{ where }
K:=\|\widehat u\|_{C_b(\mathbb R,H^1)}.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&
\left|
\chi_R(\|w_1\|_{H^1})
-\chi_R(\|w_2\|_{H^1})
\right|
\leqslant
\frac{C_\chi}{R}
\|w_1-w_2\|_{H^1}.
\end{aligned}
           
$$

Since the second term in (\ref{3.16}) is non-zero only when
\(\|w_2\|_{H^1}\leqslant2R\), we also have
\begin{equation}\label{3.19}
\|F(w_2)\|_{H^{-1}}
\leqslant C(2R+K)^2.
\end{equation}
Combining (\ref{3.16})--(\ref{3.19}), we obtain
\begin{equation}
 \label{3.20}   
{
\|F_R(w_1)-F_R(w_2)\|_{H^{-1}}
\leqslant
L_R\|w_1-w_2\|_{H^1},
}
\end{equation}
where
$
L_R
=
C(4R+K)
+
\frac{C_\chi}{R}C(2R+K)^2.
           
$ 
Thus \(F_R\) is globally Lipschitz from \(H^1\) into
\(H^{-1}\).

                             
We now can apply the  Zelik's theorem \cite[Theorem 2.15]{Ze14} to
 $$
\partial_t w+Aw=F_R(w).
           
$$
 Here the phase space is
$
H=L^2_\sigma(\Gamma(TS^d)),
$ 
and
 $
H^1=D(A^{1/2}),
\qquad
H^{-1}=D(A^{-1/2}).
$  
Thus the nonlinearity \(F_R\) is globally Lipschitz from \(H^1\) into
\(H^{-1}\), corresponding to the case \(\beta=-1\) in Zelik's theorem.

The spectral-gap condition (2.65) of \cite[Theorem 2.15]{Ze14} therefore becomes
   
                               
                                        
      
           
   
              
$$
\sqrt{\lambda_{N+1}}
-
\sqrt{\lambda_N}
>L_R.
           
$$
For the Ebin--Marsden Laplacian,
$$
\lambda_k=k^2+(k-1)(d-1),
\qquad k=1,2,\ldots.
           
$$
Hence
\begin{equation} \label{3.26}
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{\lambda_{N+1}}-\sqrt{\lambda_N}
=
\frac{2N+d}
{\sqrt{(N+1)^2+N(d-1)}
+
\sqrt{N^2+(N-1)(d-1)}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Whenever \(N\) and \(d\) are chosen so that
$$
\sqrt{\lambda_{N+1}}-\sqrt{\lambda_N}>L_R,
           
$$

Zelik's Theorem \cite[Theorem 2.15]{Ze14} yields an \(N\)-dimensional inertial manifold
$$
\mathcal M_R
=
\{w_++\Phi_R(w_+):w_+\in P_NH\}
$$
for the cut-off equation (3.22), where
$$
\Phi_R:P_NH\to Q_NH
$$

Lastly, we would like to explain 
the relation with the original Navier-Stokes equation. To this end,

Therefore, every trajectory of the cut-off equation which remains in \(B_R\) also solves the original perturbation equation.

In particular, if $
\mathcal U\subset B_R
$ 
is a positively invariant neighborhood of \(w=0\), then the part 
$
\mathcal M_R\cap\mathcal U
$ 
is invariant for the original perturbation equation and provides a finite-dimensional reduction of the Navier-Stokes dynamics in \(\mathcal U\).
 This completes the proof.
