<!-- head-1: body_md -->
We work over $\mathbb C$.
A Calabi--Yau threefold $X$ is a smooth projective threefold with $K_X\sim0$ and $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=H^2(X,\mathcal O_X)=0$.
``General'' means on a dense Zariski-open subset of the relevant parameter space.
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- head-2: body_md -->
Let $B$ be a smooth projective fourfold and let $L,R$ be line bundles on $B$ with
\begin{equation}
2L+R=-K_B
\label{eq:CYcondition}
\end{equation}
in additive notation.
When $B$ is a product of projective spaces, $X_+$ below is a gCICY in the sense of \cite{AAGGL}; for general $B$ we keep the same configuration notation, with the row of $B$ recording line bundles on $B$.
We write $F$ for the pullback to $\mathbb P^1\times B$ of the hyperplane class of $\mathbb P^1_{[u:v]}$ and use the same letter for its restrictions.
Take
\begin{equation}
P(u,v)=f_0u^3+f_1u^2v+f_2uv^2+f_3v^3\in H^0\bigl(\mathbb P^1\times B,\mathcal O(3)\boxtimes L\bigr), \qquad f_i\in H^0(B,L),
\label{eq:cubicP}
\end{equation}
and let $M=Z(P)\subset\mathbb P^1\times B$.
The generalized phase is the zero locus
\[
X_+=Z_M(s),\qquad s\in H^0\bigl(M,\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\boxtimes(L+R)|_M\bigr),
\]
that is, the gCICY with configuration
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbb P^1&3&-1\\ B&L&L+R\end{smallmatrix}\right]$.
Since $K_{\mathbb P^1\times B}=\mathcal O(-2)\boxtimes K_B$ and $\mathcal O(3)\boxtimes L+\mathcal O(-1)\boxtimes(L+R)=\mathcal O(2)\boxtimes(2L+R)$, the condition \eqref{eq:CYcondition} is exactly the adjunction condition for $X_+$ to have trivial canonical bundle.
The section $s$ is the
\emph{generalized section} of the block: $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\boxtimes(L+R)$
has no nonzero sections on $\mathbb P^1\times B$, and $s$ exists only after restriction to $M$.
<!-- end head-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:allsections}
Assume that $f_0,\ldots,f_3$ are linearly independent and that $Z_B(f_0)$ is a reduced divisor; both hold for general $f_i$ if $h^0(B,L)\ge4$ and $L$ is globally generated.
Then $y_1,y_2,y_3$ form a basis of $H^0(M,\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\boxtimes L|_M)$, and every generalized section has the form
\begin{equation}
s=\ell_1y_1+\ell_2y_2+\ell_3y_3, \qquad \ell_j\in H^0(B,R).
\label{eq:ellgeneral}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For a line bundle $N$ on $B$, the Koszul sequence of $M$ twisted by $\mathcal O(-1)\boxtimes N$ is
\[
0\to\mathcal O(-4)\boxtimes(N-L)\xrightarrow{\ \times P\ }\mathcal O(-1)\boxtimes N\to
\mathcal O(-1)\boxtimes N|_M\to0 .
\]
The middle term has no cohomology by the K\"unneth formula, since $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)$ is acyclic.
Hence the connecting map
\[
\delta_N\colon H^0\bigl(M,\mathcal O(-1)\boxtimes N|_M\bigr)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
H^1\bigl(\mathcal O(-4)\boxtimes(N-L)\bigr)=H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-4))\otimes H^0(B,N-L)
\]
is an isomorphism.
For $N=L$ the target is 3-dimensional, and for $N=L+R$ it is $H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-4))\otimes H^0(B,R)$.
Multiplication by $\ell\in H^0(B,R)$ maps the sequence for $N=L$ to the sequence for $N=L+R$, so $\delta_{L+R}(\ell\sigma)=\ell\,\delta_L(\sigma)$.
Therefore multiplication induces an isomorphism $H^0(M,\mathcal O(-1)\boxtimes L|_M)\otimes H^0(B,R)\to H^0(M,\mathcal O(-1)\boxtimes(L+R)|_M)$.

It remains to see that the $y_j$ are linearly independent.
On $M\cap\{v=0\}=\{[1:0]\}\times Z_B(f_0)$, equation \eqref{eq:auxgeneral} gives $y_j=f_j$.
If $\sum_{j=1}^3c_jy_j=0$, then $\sum_{j=1}^3c_jf_j$ vanishes on the reduced divisor $Z_B(f_0)$ of class $L$, hence is a constant multiple of $f_0$.
By linear independence of the $f_i$, all $c_j$ vanish.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:cubicsmooth}
Assume that $L$ and $R$ are globally generated, and let $f_i\in H^0(B,L)$ and $\ell_j\in H^0(B,R)$ be general.
Then $M$, $X_+$ and $X_-$ are smooth of the expected dimensions and $X_\pm$ have trivial canonical bundle.
The common zero locus $Z_B(f_0,\ldots,f_3)$ is empty or a reduced set of $\int_Bc_1(L)^4$ points, and the $\ell_j$ have no common zero on it.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The sections of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(3)\boxtimes L$ of the form \eqref{eq:cubicP}, and the sections of $(\mathcal Q\boxtimes L)\oplus(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(1)\boxtimes R)$ induced by the data $(\mathbf f,\ell)$ as in \eqref{eq:generalminus}, 
generate these bundles, because $L$ and $R$ are globally generated and $\mathcal Q$ is a quotient of $\mathcal O^{\oplus4}$; hence $M$ and $X_-$ are smooth of the expected dimension by Bertini's theorem for a generating linear system.
General sections $f_0,\ldots,f_3$ of the globally generated $L$ on the fourfold $B$ meet in a reduced set of $\int_Bc_1(L)^4$ points (possibly none), and general $\ell_j$ do not vanish simultaneously at these finitely many points.
At a point of $M$ with $y_j\ne0$, choosing $\ell_j$ nonzero at the image in $B$ and the other $\ell$'s zero shows that the linear system $\{\ell\cdot y\}$ is base-point free on $M\setminus(\mathbb P^1\times Z_B(f))$; by Bertini, $X_+$ is smooth there.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:stabilizers}
In $\mathcal A_{-,i_0}$ the only points with nontrivial stabilizer lie on the stratum $\{x^{(i_0)}=0\}$, which is nonempty only if $a_{i_0}\ge1$.
There the stabilizer is $\mu_{a_{i_0}}$, acting with weight 1 on the $n_{i_0}+1$ transverse coordinates $x^{(i_0)}_l$ and trivially on all other coordinates.
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal A_{-,i_0}$ is smooth away from this stratum, and along it
has quotient singularities of type $\frac1{a_{i_0}}(1,\ldots,1)$ with $n_{i_0}+1$ entries;
\item a class $(d,\sum_{i=1}^kd_iH_i)$ is Cartier on $\mathcal A_{-,i_0}$ if and
only if $a_{i_0}\mid d_{i_0}$;
\item in particular $L_1,\ldots,L_4$ are always Cartier, while $L_5$ and
$-K_{\mathcal A_{-,i_0}}$ are Cartier if and only if $a_{i_0}\mid r_{i_0}$, and $\mathcal A_{-,i_0}$ is Gorenstein if and only if $a_{i_0}\mid r_{i_0}$.
\end{enumerate}

A point is semistable for $\vartheta$ exactly when $\vartheta$ lies in the cone generated by the weights of its nonzero coordinates.
If all coordinates of two factors $x^{(i)},x^{(j)}$ vanish, the remaining weights span a cone meeting $\mathfrak C_-^{\rm tot}$ only on the wall $c_i/a_i=c_j/a_j$; if $y=0$ the remaining weights have nonnegative first component; 
and if $u=v=0$ together with $x^{(i)}=0$ the remaining weights force $c_i=0$.
Thus a semistable point with some $x^{(i)}=0$ has $(u,v)\ne0$, $y\ne0$, $x^{(j)}\ne0$ for $j\ne i$, and by the criterion above $i=i_0$.

It acts on $x^{(i_0)}_l$ by $\mu_{i_0}$ and trivially on the other coordinates.
Every other semistable point has $(u,v)\ne0$ or $u=v=0$ with all $x^{(j)}\ne0$ and $y\ne0$, and in both cases the stabilizer is trivial.

and a general complete intersection $V\subset\mathcal A_{-,i_0}$ is the strict transform of a general (i.e.\ $\Delta$-regular) complete intersection $V_\Delta$ of the nef partition, with $V\to V_\Delta$ crepant.
The $L_j$ are base-point free, so $V$ is smooth away from the singular stratum of Lemma~\ref{lem:stabilizers}; 
that stratum has dimension $8-(n_{i_0}+1)$, and $a_{i_0}\ge2$ forces $n_{i_0}+1=2a_{i_0}+r_{i_0}\ge4$, so a general codimension-5 $V$ misses it.
Hence $V$ is smooth and $h^{p,q}(V)=h^{p,q}_{\rm st}(V_\Delta)$.
The threefold $X_-$ is also a complete intersection of sections of the $L_j$: the sections of $(0,L)$ are $f(x)+(\text{bilinear in }(u,v)\text{ and }y)$, and the equations $D(u,v)y=\mathbf f$, $\ell\cdot y=0$ are such sections.
Moreover $X_-$ misses the singular stratum, since $x^{(i_0)}=0$ forces $\mathbf f=0$ and then $y=0$ by injectivity of $D(u,v)$; so $X_-$ is smooth.
(The family of all complete intersections is in general larger than the family of auxiliary systems: for instance, when $R-L$ is effective the sections of $L_5$ contain additional terms $u\,y_jy_l\,g(x)$.) 
The complete intersections that are smooth of dimension 3 form a nonempty Zariski open subset of an irreducible parameter space, hence a connected one, 
so $X_-$ is a smooth fiber of a smooth projective family containing $V$, and $h^{p,q}(X_-)=h^{p,q}(V)$.
In case (2) of Theorem~\ref{thm:cubicblock}, $X_+\simeq X_-$.
In case (3), $X_\pm$ are isomorphic in codimension 1, so $h^{1,1}(X_+)=\rho(X_+)=\rho(X_-)=h^{1,1}(X_-)$, and the flop replaces disjoint smooth rational curves by smooth rational curves, 
so $e(X_+)=e(X_-)$; since both are Calabi--Yau threefolds, all Hodge numbers agree.
(More generally, birational Calabi--Yau manifolds have equal Hodge numbers \cite{BatyrevBetti,DenefLoeser}.)

(3) The Batyrev--Borisov mirror duality theorem \cite{BBH} gives
\[
E_{\rm st}(V^*;u,v)=(-u)^3E_{\rm st}(V_\Delta;u^{-1},v),
\]
i.e.\ $h^{p,q}_{\rm st}(V^*)=h^{3-p,q}_{\rm st}(V_\Delta)$.
The dual Gorenstein toric Fano variety admits a maximal projective crepant partial desingularization whose singular locus has codimension at least 4
\cite{Batyrev}; a general 3-dimensional complete intersection of the
pulled-back nef partition avoids it and gives a smooth crepant resolution $Y$ of $V^*$, with $h^{p,q}(Y)=h^{p,q}_{\rm st}(V^*)$ \cite{BBH}.
Combining with (2) proves the claim.

Use $(z_1,z_2,w)$ with Euler operators $\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_w$, and put $S=\theta_1+\theta_2$.
Then
\begin{align*}
\mathcal L^{(4)}_A={}&S^2-3S\theta_w+4\theta_w^2-4w(S-\theta_w)^2\\
&-z_1(\theta_1^2+5\theta_1\theta_2+10\theta_2^2+2\theta_1+5\theta_2+1)
 -z_2(10\theta_1^2+5\theta_1\theta_2+\theta_2^2+5\theta_1+2\theta_2+1),\\
\mathcal L^{(4)}_B={}&\theta_1^2-\theta_1\theta_2+\theta_2^2
-(z_1+z_2)\bigl((S+1)^2+3(S+1)\theta_w+5\theta_w^2\bigr)\\
&+(z_1+z_2)w\bigl(5\theta_w^2+(3-2S)\theta_w-(7S+2)(S+1)\bigr),\\
\mathcal L^{(4)}_C={}&(\theta_1-\theta_2)(3S-5\theta_w)
-\bigl(z_1(\theta_1+2\theta_2+1)-z_2(2\theta_1+\theta_2+1)\bigr)(3S+3+4\theta_w)\\
&+4w\bigl(z_1(\theta_1+2\theta_2+1)-z_2(2\theta_1+\theta_2+1)\bigr)(S+\theta_w+1),\\
\mathcal L^{(4)}_w={}&\theta_w^3-w(S+\theta_w+1)(S-\theta_w)^2 .
\end{align*}
The last operator is the $w$-recurrence of \eqref{eq:period4}.
The first 3 were found by solving the linear equations for second-order operators with coefficients of degree at most 2; we now prove that they annihilate $\Phi_4$ and the $\mathcal R_4$-valued Frobenius series.

Write $c(n_1,n_2,m)$ for the coefficients of \eqref{eq:period4} and $N=n_1+n_2$.
The Gamma function gives the meromorphic identities
\[
\frac{c(n_1,n_2,m-1)}{c(n_1,n_2,m)}=\frac{m^3}{(N+m)(N-m+1)^2},\qquad
\frac{c(n_1-1,n_2,m)}{c(n_1,n_2,m)}=\frac{n_1^3(N-m)^2}{N^4(N+m)},
\]
and similarly for $n_2$; the shifts in \eqref{eq:period4} used by the operators are products of these.
After division by $c(n_1,n_2,m)$, the coefficient of $z_1^{n_1}z_2^{n_2}w^m$ in $\mathcal L\Phi_4$ becomes a rational function of $(n_1,n_2,m)$.
For each of the 4 operators this rational function vanishes identically: after clearing denominators it is a polynomial of degree at most 16 in each variable, and it vanishes on an $18\times18\times18$ grid of generic rational points.
This proves the annihilation for all coefficients at which all the Gamma quotients involved are holomorphic, which excludes only the rows $n_1+n_2\le1$.
There the coefficients are finite in number, since $c(n_1,n_2,m)=0$ for $m>n_1+n_2$, and they are checked directly.

The same identities, with $(n_1,n_2,m)$ replaced by $(n_1+H_1,n_2+H_2,m+G)$, give the annihilation of the $\mathcal R_4$-valued series, again except in the rows $n_1+n_2\le1$.
In the row $n_1=n_2=0$ the series is the boundary series $\sum_{m\ge0}r_m w^m=1+F^2\operatorname{Li}_2(w)-12[\mathrm{pt}]\operatorname{Li}_3(w)$ of Section~\ref{sec:mirror4}, with $F=H_1+H_2-G$.
Only the terms of $\mathcal L^{(4)}_A$ without $z_1,z_2$ contribute to this row, and the check is the computation of \cite[Appendix~D.1]{KanCompanion}, using $H_iF^2=0$ and $GF^2=6[\mathrm{pt}]$.
The indicial parts of $\mathcal L^{(4)}_B$ and $\mathcal L^{(4)}_C$ annihilate $F^2$ and $[\mathrm{pt}]$ by the relations of $\mathcal R_4$, and the $w$-recurrence $\mathcal L^{(4)}_w$ is treated like the operator $\mathcal L_3$ there.
The 2 rows with $n_1+n_2=1$ and $m=0$ are checked directly.
