<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
Let
	\[
	C_0=\{(z,0)\in\mathfrak H:|z|=1\}.
	\]
Every finite $\mathbb{C}$-circle is a ${\rm Sim}(\mathfrak H)$-image of $C_0.$
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $C$ be a finite $\mathbb{C}$-circle and the projected Euclidean circle have a centre $z_0 = x_0 + iy_0 \in \mathbb{C}$ and a radius $r > 0$. Let $t_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ dictate the base vertical height.  We claim that any finite $\mathbb{C}$-circle is 
 uniquely parametrised by the angle $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$ as follows:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{cases}
x(\theta) = x_0 + r\cos\theta \\
y(\theta) = y_0 + r\sin\theta \\
t(\theta) = t_0 - 2r(x_0\sin\theta - y_0\cos\theta).
\end{cases}.
\end{equation*}
             
                                                                               
      
    
              
   
         
                      
              
                             
          
    
                                                          
           
 
              
Indeed, by definition, $C$ is the boundary of a
	complex geodesic $L$. Using the standard Hermitian form
	\[
	\langle Z,W\rangle
	=
	Z_1\overline{W_3}
	+
	Z_3\overline{W_1}
	+
	Z_2\overline{W_2},
	\]
	the complex geodesic $L$ is the intersection of
	$\mathbf H^2_{\mathbb C}$ with a complex projective line. Hence there
	exists a positive vector $
	v=[a\quad b\quad c]^t$
	such that
	\[
	L=\{[Z]\in\mathbf H^2_{\mathbb C}:\langle Z,v\rangle=0\}.
	\]
	A boundary point $(z,t)\in\mathfrak H$ is represented by
	\[
	\hat p(z,t)
	=
	\begin{bmatrix}
		-\dfrac{|z|^2-it}{2}\\[2mm]
		z\\[2mm]
		1
	\end{bmatrix}.
	\]
	Therefore $(z,t)\in C=\partial_\infty L$ if and only if
	\[
	\langle \hat p(z,t),v\rangle=0.
	\]
	Substituting the above lift yields
	\[
	-\frac{|z|^2-it}{2}\,\overline c
	+z\overline b
	+\overline a
	=0.
	\]
                     
    
                        
                
               
     
    
Since $C$ is finite, the corresponding complex geodesic does not pass
	through $\infty$. Hence $c\neq0$ and we may divide by $\overline c$:
	\[
	-|z|^2+it
	+2z\frac{\overline b}{\overline c}
	+2\frac{\overline a}{\overline c}
	=0.
	\]
Setting $z_0=b/c, A=2\overline a/\overline c, $ we have
	\[
	-|z|^2+it+2z\overline{z_0}+A=0.
	\]
Writing $A=\alpha+i\beta,$ and separating real and imaginary parts, we obtain
	\[
	|z|^2-2\Re(z\overline{z_0})=\alpha,
	\qquad
	t+\beta
	=
	-2\Im(z\overline{z_0}).
	\]
Thus, we obtain
	\[
	|z-z_0|=r, \qquad  t=t_0-2\Im(z\overline{z_0}),
	\]
by setting $r=\sqrt{\alpha+|z_0|^2},\, t_0=-\beta.$
	                                        
Consequently,
	\[
	C
	=
	\Bigl\{
	(z,t)\in\mathfrak H:
	|z-z_0|=r,\;
	t=t_0-2\Im(z\overline{z_0})
	\Bigr\},
	\]
	as desired.
	
Finally, our result follows immediately by 
	\[
	C=
	L_{(z_0,t_0)}\circ D_r(C_0).
	\]
