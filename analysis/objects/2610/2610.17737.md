For every complex semisimple symmetric pair and every $r\ge2$,
\[
 \dim C_r(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))
 \le\left\lfloor\frac{r\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}\right\rfloor.

Every irreducible component of $C_r(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$ is simultaneously
isotropic for all the forms $B|_\mathfrak{p}\otimes J$,
$J\in\Lambda^2(\mathbb{C}^r)^*$.

<!-- theoremb-1: body_md -->
Let $(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})$ be a nonzero simple complex Hermitian symmetric
pair. Put $d=\dim\mathfrak{p}^{+}$ and $\ell=\operatorname{rk}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})$, and choose a
Cartan subspace $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak{p}$.
For every $r\ge2$,
\[
 \dim C_r(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))=rd,\qquad
 \dim C_r(\mathfrak{p})=\max\{rd,\,2d+(r-1)\ell\}.
\]
The subvarieties $(\mathfrak{p}^{+})^r$ and $(\mathfrak{p}^{-})^r$ are distinct
maximal-dimensional irreducible components of
$C_r(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$ for every $r\ge2$.
For $r\ge3$, they are the only such components.

For every $r\ge2$, the maximal-dimensional irreducible
components of $C_r(\mathfrak{p})$ are precisely those among
\[
 (\mathfrak{p}^{+})^r,\qquad(\mathfrak{p}^{-})^r,\qquad
 \overline{K\cdot\mathfrak a^r}
\]
whose dimensions attain the displayed maximum. Their
dimensions are $rd$, $rd$, and $2d+(r-1)\ell$, respectively.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:pair-decomposition}
Every complex semisimple symmetric pair is a direct sum of indecomposable
symmetric pairs of the following two kinds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item a pair of simple type, obtained by restricting \(\theta\) to a
  \(\theta\)-stable simple ideal of \(\mathfrak{g}\);
\item a pair of group type
  \[
    (\mathfrak h \oplus\mathfrak h,\Delta\mathfrak h),
    \qquad \Delta\mathfrak h:=\{(x,x):x\in\mathfrak h\},
  \]
  where \(\mathfrak h\) is simple and the involution interchanges the two
  factors.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:hermitian}
A symmetric pair \((\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})\) is of \emph{Hermitian type} if \(\mathfrak{g}\) admits
a three-grading
\[
  \mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_0\oplus\mathfrak{g}_1,
  \qquad
  [\mathfrak{g}_i,\mathfrak{g}_j]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{i+j},
  \qquad
  \mathfrak{g}_i=0\ \text{for }|i|>1,
\]
such that
\[
  \mathfrak{g}_0=\mathfrak{k},\qquad
  \mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_1.
\]
We write
\[
  \mathfrak{p}^{-}:=\mathfrak{g}_{-1},\qquad \mathfrak{p}^{+}:=\mathfrak{g}_1.
\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:hermitian-basic}
Let \(\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}\) be a Hermitian symmetric pair. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \(\mathfrak{p}^{+}\) and \(\mathfrak{p}^{-}\) are abelian subalgebras of \(\mathfrak{g}\);
\item every element of \(\mathfrak{p}^{+}\cup\mathfrak{p}^{-}\) is nilpotent;
\item \(B(\mathfrak{p}^{+},\mathfrak{p}^{+})=B(\mathfrak{p}^{-},\mathfrak{p}^{-})=0\) and \(B(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{p}^{+})=B(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{p}^{-})=0\);
\item \(B\) induces a perfect pairing \(\mathfrak{p}^{+}\times\mathfrak{p}^{-}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}\);
\item \(\dim\mathfrak{p}^{+}=\dim\mathfrak{p}^{-}=\frac12\dim\mathfrak{p}\).
\item If $\mathfrak{g}$ is simple and $\mathfrak{p}\ne0$, then $\mathfrak{p}^{+}$ and $\mathfrak{p}^{-}$
are irreducible $K$-modules; the grading element $\xi$ acts on
them with eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$, respectively.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:Cr}
For \(r\geq1\), let \(\mathcal C_r(\mathfrak{p})\) be the affine scheme in \(\mathfrak{p}^r\)
defined by the equations
\[
  [x_i,x_j]=0\qquad(1\leq i<j\leq r),
\]
and define the nilpotent higher commuting scheme by
\[
  \mathcal C_r^{\mathrm{nil}}(\mathfrak{p})
  :=
  \mathcal C_r(\mathfrak{p})
  \times_{\mathfrak{p}^r}
  \mathcal N_{\mathrm{sch}}(\mathfrak{p})^r.
\]
We write
\[
  C_{r}(\mathfrak{p}):=\mathcal C_r(\mathfrak{p})_{\mathrm{red}},
  \qquad
  C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))
  :=\mathcal C_r^{\mathrm{nil}}(\mathfrak{p})_{\mathrm{red}}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))
  =
  \left\{
    (x_1,\ldots,x_r)\in\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p})^r
    \ \middle|\
    [x_i,x_j]=0\ \text{for all }i,j
  \right\}.
\]

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:symmetries}
The variety \(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))\) is a closed cone in \(\mathfrak{p}^r\). It is stable under
the diagonal action of \(K\) and under the action of \(\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb{C})\) given by
\[
  g\cdot(x_1,\ldots,x_r)
  =
  \left(
    \sum_jg_{1j}x_j,\ldots,\sum_jg_{rj}x_j
  \right).
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The closedness and \(K\)-stability follow directly from
\cref{def:Cr}. For a point
\((x_1,\ldots,x_r)\inC_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))\), we put
\[
  A=\operatorname{span}\{x_1,\ldots,x_r\}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.
\]
The endomorphisms
\(\operatorname{ad}x_1,\ldots,\operatorname{ad}x_r\) commute and are
nilpotent. They are therefore simultaneously strictly
upper triangular in a suitable basis.  Every linear combination of them is
therefore nilpotent.  It follows that every element of \(A\) is nilpotent, so
every \(\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb{C})\)-transform of the tuple again lies in
\(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))\).  Finally, restricting this action to the subgroup of scalar matrices 
\(\mathbb{C}^\times I_r \subseteq \operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb{C})\) shows that \(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))\) is stable under 
simultaneous dilation, and is therefore a cone.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:GLr-symmetry}
In the case \(r=2\), this \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{C})\)-symmetry was utilized by Bulois 
\cite[proof of Lemma~1.8]{Bulois}. For general \(r\), one may canonically identify 
\(\mathfrak{p}^r\) with the tensor product \(\mathfrak{p}\otimes U\), where \(U = \mathbb{C}^r\). Under this 
identification, the group \(\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb{C}) \cong \operatorname{GL}(U)\) acts naturally on the second 
tensor factor. For each alternating form \(J \in \Lambda^2 U^*\), the bilinear form
\[
  \omega_J := B|_\mathfrak{p} \otimes J
\]
defines an alternating 2-form on \(\mathfrak{p}\otimes U\) satisfying the equivariance property 
\(g^*\omega_J = \omega_{g^*J}\) for every \(g \in \operatorname{GL}(U)\). Because \(\operatorname{GL}(U)\) acts 
transitively on the set of alternating forms on \(U\) of any given rank, this 
symmetry clarifies why the subsequent isotropy properties hold simultaneously 
for the entire family \(\{\omega_J : J \in \Lambda^2 U^*\}\).

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:products}
Suppose
\[
  (\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
  =
  (\mathfrak{g}^{(1)},\mathfrak{k}^{(1)})
  \oplus
  (\mathfrak{g}^{(2)},\mathfrak{k}^{(2)}),
  \qquad
  \mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}^{(1)}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}.
\]
Then
\[
  \mathcal C_r^{\mathrm{nil}}(\mathfrak{p})
  \simeq
  \mathcal C_r^{\mathrm{nil}}(\mathfrak{p}^{(1)})
  \times
  \mathcal C_r^{\mathrm{nil}}(\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}).
\]
Consequently,
\[
  C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))
  \simeq
  C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}^{(1)}))
  \times
  C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}^{(2)})),
\]
and dimensions add. The same product decomposition holds for the full commuting schemes:
\[
 \mathcal C_r(\mathfrak{p})\cong
 \mathcal C_r(\mathfrak{p}^{(1)})\times\mathcal C_r(\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}),
 \qquad
 C_r(\mathfrak{p})\cong C_r(\mathfrak{p}^{(1)})\times C_r(\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}).
\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We write $x_i=x_i^{(1)}+x_i^{(2)}$. Since the two summands
are commuting ideals, the bracket equations split into the
independent equations $[x_i^{(1)},x_j^{(1)}]=0$ and
$[x_i^{(2)},x_j^{(2)}]=0$. This proves the assertion for the
full commuting schemes.

Let $K^{(j)}$ be the connected fixed-point group for the $j$th
factor. The product decomposition of the action gives
\[
 \mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{p}]^{K^{(1)}\times K^{(2)}}
 =\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{p}^{(1)}]^{K^{(1)}}\otimes_\mathbb{C}
   \mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}]^{K^{(2)}}.
\]
Hence the invariant quotient morphism is the product of the
two quotient morphisms. Taking the fibre over the image of
zero shows that the null schemes also factor as products.
Intersecting these null-fibre conditions with the bracket
equations proves the nilpotent scheme isomorphism. Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, the Cartesian product of 
any two reduced schemes of finite type is again reduced. Passing to the underlying 
reduced subschemes yields the isomorphisms \(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p})) \cong C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}^{(1)})) \times C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}))\) 
and \(C_r(\mathfrak{p}) \cong C_r(\mathfrak{p}^{(1)}) \times C_r(\mathfrak{p}^{(2)})\). The additivity of Krull 
dimensions follows immediately.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:groupcase}
Let \(\mathfrak h\) be complex semisimple and let \(\theta\) interchange the
two factors of \(\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak h\). Then
\[
  \mathfrak{k}=\Delta\mathfrak h,\qquad
  \mathfrak{p}=\{(x,-x):x\in\mathfrak h\}\simeq\mathfrak h.
\]
Under this identification,
\[
  [(x,-x),(y,-y)]=([x,y],[x,y]),
\]
so two elements of \(\mathfrak{p}\) commute precisely when the corresponding elements
of \(\mathfrak h\) commute. Moreover, \((x,-x)\) is nilpotent if and only if
\(x\) is nilpotent. Therefore
\[
  C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))\simeqC_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak h)),
  \qquad
  \dim\mathfrak{p}=\dim\mathfrak h.
\]
The restriction of the Killing form of \(\mathfrak{g}\) to \(\mathfrak{p}\) is
\(2B_{\mathfrak h}\). Consequently, general results established for complex semisimple symmetric pairs 
specialize directly to statements concerning the classical nilpotent higher commuting 
varieties of semisimple Lie algebras. Note that a nonzero group-type pair is never Hermitian, because
\(\mathfrak{k}\simeq\mathfrak h\) is semisimple and has trivial center.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:span-strata}
Let \(r\geq1\) and \(0\leq d\leq\min\{r,\dim\mathfrak{p}\}\). There is a natural isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:span-bundle}
  C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))^{(d)}
  \simeq
  \left\{
    (A,x_1,\ldots,x_r)
    \in
    \left.\mathcal S_d^{\oplus r}\right|_{\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})}
    \ \middle|\
    \langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\rangle=A
  \right\}.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is the fibrewise spanning open subset of the vector
bundle \(\left.\mathcal S_d^{\oplus r}\right|_{\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})}\). Consequently,
whenever \(\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})\neq\varnothing\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:span-stratum-dim}
  \dimC_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))^{(d)}
  =
  rd+\dim\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p}).
\end{equation}

Moreover,
\[
  \bigcup_{e=0}^{d}C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))^{(e)}
\]
is the closed rank-at-most-\(d\) locus. Hence \(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))^{(d)}\) is locally
closed and is open in the rank-at-most-\(d\) locus. In particular, the stratum
of maximal possible rank \(\min\{r,a(\mathfrak{p})\}\) is open in \(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))\).
Finally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:span-dimension}
  \dimC_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))
  =
  \max_{0\leq d\leq\min\{r,a(\mathfrak{p})\}}
  \left(
    rd+\dim\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})
  \right).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let \(\mathbf x=(x_1,\ldots,x_r)\inC_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))^{(d)}\), and put
\[
  A_{\mathbf x}:=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\rangle.
\]
The operators \(\operatorname{ad}x_1,\ldots,\operatorname{ad}x_r\) are
commuting nilpotent endomorphisms and hence are simultaneously strictly
triangularisable. Every element of
\(A_{\mathbf x}\) is therefore nilpotent. Since the \(x_i\) commute, this
proves \(A_{\mathbf x}\in\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})\). Thus the map
\[
  \mathbf x
  \longmapsto
  (A_{\mathbf x},x_1,\ldots,x_r)
\]
has the target displayed in \eqref{eq:span-bundle}.

On the rank-$d$ locus in $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^r,\mathfrak{p})$, the map
$f\mapsto\operatorname{im}f$ is a morphism to $\operatorname{Gr}(d,\mathfrak{p})$.
Indeed, on a chart where a fixed $d\times d$ minor is
nonzero, its Grassmannian coordinates are ratios of minors
with that nonvanishing denominator. For $d=0$, the map is
constant. Applying this to the linear map $e_i\mapsto x_i$,
where $(e_i)$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb{C}^r$, shows that
$\mathbf x\mapsto(A_{\mathbf x},\mathbf x)$ is a morphism.
Its inverse is the projection $(A,\mathbf x)\mapsto\mathbf x$,
which proves \eqref{eq:span-bundle}.

The condition that \(r\) vectors span a \(d\)-dimensional fibre is open and,
since \(d\leq r\), nonempty. Over every irreducible component of
\(\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})\), the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:span-bundle} is therefore a
dense open subset of a vector bundle of rank \(rd\). This proves
\eqref{eq:span-stratum-dim}.

The rank of the linear map
\[
  \mathbb{C}^r\longrightarrow\mathfrak{p},\qquad e_i\longmapsto x_i,
\]
is at most \(d\) precisely when all \((d+1)\times(d+1)\) minors vanish.
Thus the rank-at-most-\(d\) locus is closed. The remaining assertions follow
from the finite disjoint decomposition
\[
  C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))
  =
  \bigsqcup_{0\leq d\leq\min\{r,a(\mathfrak{p})\}}
  C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))^{(d)}.
\]

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:asymptotic}
Set $a:=a(\mathfrak{p})$ and $m:=\dim\mathcal{A}_a(\mathfrak{p})$.
With the convention \(\dim\mathcal{A}_0(\mathfrak{p})=0\), there is an integer
\(r_0\geq\max\{1,a\}\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eventual-dimension}
  \dimC_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))=ra+m
  \qquad(r\geq r_0).
\end{equation}
One valid choice is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-r0}
  r_0
  :=
  \max\left(
    \{1,a\}
    \cup
    \left\{
      1+\left\lfloor
        \frac{\dim\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})-m}{a-d}
      \right\rfloor
      :0\leq d<a
    \right\}
  \right),
\end{equation}
where the second set is omitted when \(a=0\).

For every \(r\geq r_0\), the maximal-dimensional irreducible components of
\(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))\) are in natural bijection with the irreducible components
\(T\) of \(\mathcal{A}_a(\mathfrak{p})\) satisfying \(\dim T=m\). The component associated
with \(T\) is
\[
  \overline{
    \left\{
      (x_1,\ldots,x_r)\in A^r
      \ \middle|\
      A\in T,\ 
      \langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\rangle=A
    \right\}
  }.
\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
If $a=0$, then $\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p})=\{0\}$: any nonzero nilpotent
element would span a member of $\mathcal{A}_1(\mathfrak{p})$.
Thus $C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$ and $\mathcal{A}_0(\mathfrak{p})$ are points, $m=0$,
and all assertions hold with $r_0=1$.
We may therefore assume $a>0$.

If \(0\leq d<a\), then the definition of \(r_0\) implies
\[
  r > \frac{\dim\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})-m}{a-d}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  ra+m > rd+\dim\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p}).
\]
Since \(r\geq a\), all ranks \(0,\ldots,a\) occur in the maximum
\eqref{eq:span-dimension}. The preceding strict inequalities show that the
maximum is uniquely achieved at \(d=a\), proving
\eqref{eq:eventual-dimension}.

Set \(U_a:=C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))^{(a)}\). By \cref{prop:span-strata}, \(U_a\) is open in
\(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))\), and it is the fibrewise spanning open subset of
\(\left.\mathcal S_a^{\oplus r}\right|_{\mathcal{A}_a(\mathfrak{p})}\). The complement of
\(U_a\) is the closed locus \(\bigcup_{d<a}C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))^{(d)}\), which has
dimension
\[
  \max_{0\le d<a}\dimC_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))^{(d)} < ra+m.
\]
Therefore, every irreducible component of \(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))\) of maximal dimension
\(ra+m\) must intersect \(U_a\), and no maximal component is contained in the
boundary.

Since \(U_a\to\mathcal{A}_a(\mathfrak{p})\) is a fibre bundle with irreducible fibres of
constant dimension \(ra\), its irreducible components are indexed by the
irreducible components of the base \(\mathcal{A}_a(\mathfrak{p})\). Specifically, if
\(T\subseteq\mathcal{A}_a(\mathfrak{p})\) is an irreducible component, the preimage of \(T\) in
\(U_a\) is irreducible of dimension \(ra+\dim T\). It follows that the
components of \(U_a\) of maximal dimension correspond precisely to the
components \(T\subseteq\mathcal{A}_a(\mathfrak{p})\) with \(\dim T=m\). Taking closures yields
the asserted bijection and description of components.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:pair-projection}
Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over an algebraically closed
field $k$, let $r\ge2$, and let $Z\subseteq E^r$ be a nonempty irreducible
closed subvariety. Write $\pi_i$ and $\pi_{ij}$ for the projections onto
the indicated factors. Then
\[
 (r-1)\dim Z
 \le \sum_{1\le i<j\le r}\dim\overline{\pi_{ij}(Z)}.
\]
If $r\ge3$, $d\ge0$ is an integer, $\dim Z=rd$, and
$\dim\overline{\pi_{ij}(Z)}\le2d$ for all $i<j$, then
\[
 Z=Y_1\times\cdots\times Y_r,
 \qquad Y_i=\overline{\pi_i(Z)},\qquad \dim Y_i=d.
\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We choose linear coordinates on each factor of $E^r$.
Their restrictions generate the function field $k(Z)$ over
the ground field $k$. We choose a transcendence basis from
these restrictions and let $b_i$ be the number chosen from
the $i$th factor. Then
\[
 \dim Z=\sum_{i=1}^r b_i.
\]
The coordinates chosen from factors $i$ and $j$ are
algebraically independent on $\overline{\pi_{ij}(Z)}$.
Therefore
\[
 b_i+b_j\le\dim\overline{\pi_{ij}(Z)}.
\]
Summing over all pairs proves the inequality, since each
$b_i$ appears exactly $r-1$ times.

Under the additional hypotheses, we obtain
\[
 (r-1)rd
 =\sum_{i<j}(b_i+b_j)
 \le\sum_{i<j}\dim\overline{\pi_{ij}(Z)}
 \le\binom r2(2d)
 =(r-1)rd.
\]
Hence every pair satisfies $b_i+b_j=2d$.
Since $r\ge3$, these equations imply $b_i=d$ for every $i$.
This conclusion holds for every transcendence basis selected
from the coordinate functions.

Fix $i$, and put $Y_i=\overline{\pi_i(Z)}$.
We first select a transcendence basis of $k(Y_i)$ from the
coordinates on the $i$th factor. Dominance of $Z\to Y_i$
makes these functions algebraically independent in $k(Z)$,
so we can extend them to a transcendence basis of $k(Z)$
using the remaining coordinate functions. No additional
coordinate from the $i$th factor can be selected: all such
coordinates are already algebraic over the chosen basis of
$k(Y_i)$. Thus this choice has $b_i=\dim Y_i$, and the
preceding argument gives $\dim Y_i=d$.

Finally, $Z$ is a closed irreducible subvariety of
$Y_1\times\cdots\times Y_r$. The product is irreducible
over the algebraically closed ground field, and both varieties
have dimension $rd$. Consequently, they are equal.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:universal-isotropy}
Let $Z$ be an irreducible component of $C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$.
Every form $\omega_{ij}$ vanishes on the smooth locus of $Z$.
Equivalently, $Z$ is isotropic for every form $B|_\mathfrak{p}\otimes J$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Write $x_i:Z_{\mathrm{sm}}\to\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p})$ for the $i$th coordinate map,
and consider the regular one-form
\[
  \alpha_{ij}=B(x_j,dx_i)\in
             H^0(Z_{\mathrm{sm}},\Omega^1_{Z_{\mathrm{sm}}}).
\]
There are finitely many nilpotent $K$-orbits in $\mathfrak{p}$
\cite{KostantRallis}; see also \cite[\S1]{Bulois}.
Their inverse images under $x_i$ give a finite partition of
$Z_{\mathrm{sm}}$ into locally closed subsets. Since $Z_{\mathrm{sm}}$ is
irreducible, one such subset contains a dense open subset
$U\subseteq Z_{\mathrm{sm}}$. Thus $x_i(U)$ lies in a single orbit.

For $z\in U$ and $u\in T_zZ$, the differential satisfies
\[
  (dx_i)_z(u)\in T_{x_i(z)}(K\cdot x_i(z))
              =[\mathfrak{k},x_i(z)].
\]
We may therefore write $(dx_i)_z(u)=[a,x_i(z)]$ for some
$a\in\mathfrak{k}$. Invariance of $B$ and commutativity give
\[
  \alpha_{ij,z}(u)
  =B(x_j(z),[a,x_i(z)])
  =B([x_i(z),x_j(z)],a)=0.
\]
Hence $\alpha_{ij}$ vanishes on $U$. The sheaf
$\Omega^1_{Z_{\mathrm{sm}}}$ is locally free on the integral variety
$Z_{\mathrm{sm}}$, so a section vanishing on a dense open subset
vanishes everywhere. Thus $\alpha_{ij}=0$ on $Z_{\mathrm{sm}}$.

The one-form $\alpha_{ij}$ is the pullback of the ambient
one-form $B(x_j,dx_i)$ on $\mathfrak{p}^r$, whose exterior derivative
is $-\omega_{ij}$  (with the convention $(\varphi\wedge\psi)(u,v)=\varphi(u)\psi(v)-\varphi(v)\psi(u)$). Since exterior differentiation commutes
with pullback, the restriction of $\omega_{ij}$ to
$Z_{\mathrm{sm}}$ equals $-d\alpha_{ij}=0$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:abelian}
Let $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}$ be a complex semisimple symmetric pair and put
$D=\dim\mathfrak{p}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})=\varnothing$ for every integer $d>D/2$. Equivalently, every
abelian linear subspace contained in $\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p})$ has dimension at most $D/2$.
\item If $D$ is even, then $\mathcal{A}_{D/2}(\mathfrak{p})$ is a finite set.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
For~(i), let $A\in\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})$. Then $A^2\subseteq C_2(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$, and hence \cref{prop:r2-nilpotent} gives
\[
  2d = \dim A^2 \le \dim C_2(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p})) = D,
\]
so $d\le D/2$.

For~(ii), suppose $D=2d_0$. If $\mathcal{A}_{d_0}(\mathfrak{p})$ is empty, there is nothing to
prove. Otherwise, choose an integer $r\ge\max\{2,d_0\}$. By
\cref{prop:span-strata}, the rank-$d_0$ stratum has dimension
\[
  rd_0 + \dim\mathcal{A}_{d_0}(\mathfrak{p}).
\]
On the other hand, \cref{thm:universal} bounds the dimension of the ambient
variety by
\[
  \dimC_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p})) \le \Bigl\lfloor\frac{rD}{2}\Bigr\rfloor = rd_0.
\]
Since the stratum is contained in $C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$, we obtain
$rd_0 + \dim\mathcal{A}_{d_0}(\mathfrak{p}) \le rd_0$, which forces $\dim\mathcal{A}_{d_0}(\mathfrak{p}) = 0$.
Since $\mathcal{A}_{d_0}(\mathfrak{p})$ is a projective algebraic variety, it 
consists of only finitely many closed points.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:Ab-bound}
For every integer $d\ge2$,
\[
  \dim\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})
  \le
  \Bigl\lfloor\frac{d\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}\Bigr\rfloor - d^2,
\]
whenever $\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p})$ is nonempty. Moreover,
\[
  \mathcal{A}_1(\mathfrak{p}) \simeq \mathbb P\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}),
\]
and hence, whenever this variety is nonempty,
\[
  \dim\mathcal{A}_1(\mathfrak{p})
  = \dim\mathfrak{p} - \operatorname{rk}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k}) - 1
  \le \dim\mathfrak{p} - 2.
\]

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
For $d\ge2$, we apply \cref{prop:span-strata} with $r=d$. The rank-$d$
stratum in $C_d(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$ has dimension
\[
  d^2 + \dim\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p}).
\]
Since $d\ge2$, \cref{thm:universal} applies to $C_d(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$ and gives
\[
  d^2 + \dim\mathcal{A}_d(\mathfrak{p}) \le \dim C_d(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p})) \le \Bigl\lfloor\frac{d\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}\Bigr\rfloor.
\]
Subtracting $d^2$ yields the asserted inequality.

$\mathbb P\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p})$. The dimension formula then follows from
\eqref{eq:KR}. Nonemptiness of $\mathcal{A}_1(\mathfrak{p})$ implies $\mathfrak{p}\ne0$, so $\operatorname{rk}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})\ge1$,
giving the upper bound $\dim\mathfrak{p} - 2$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:hermitian-max-components}
Let $(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})$ be a nonzero complex Hermitian symmetric pair
of simple type, and put $d=\dim\mathfrak{p}^{+}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item For every $r\ge2$, the subvarieties $(\mathfrak{p}^{+})^r$ and
$(\mathfrak{p}^{-})^r$ are distinct maximal-dimensional irreducible
components of $C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$, each of dimension $rd$.
\item For every $r\ge3$, these are the only
maximal-dimensional irreducible components of $C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
For \textup{(i)}, both subvarieties are closed and irreducible,
and each has dimension $rd$. By \cref{thm:hermitian}, this is
the dimension of $C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$. Each is therefore an
irreducible component: an irreducible closed subvariety
properly contained in an irreducible component has strictly
smaller dimension. The two subvarieties are distinct because
$\mathfrak{p}^{+}\cap\mathfrak{p}^{-}=0$ and $d>0$. This proves \textup{(i)},
including the case $r=2$.

For \textup{(ii)}, let $r\ge3$, and let $Z$ be an irreducible
component of dimension $rd$. Each two-coordinate projection
has closure contained in $C_2(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$, which has dimension
$2d$. The equality statement in \cref{lem:pair-projection}
therefore gives
\[
 Z=Y_1\times\cdots\times Y_r,
 \qquad Y_i=\overline{\pi_i(Z)},\qquad \dim Y_i=d.
\]

Both $K$ and $\operatorname{GL}_r$ are connected and preserve the ambient
variety by \cref{lem:symmetries}. Hence each group preserves
$Z$. Indeed, if a connected algebraic group $H$ acts on a
variety, the closure of the image of $H\times Z$ is an
irreducible closed subset containing the irreducible component
$Z$; it must consequently equal $Z$.

Permutation matrices show that $Y_1=\cdots=Y_r$; write
$Z=Y^r$. The diagonal matrices in $\operatorname{GL}_r$ show that $Y$ is
stable under multiplication by nonzero scalars. Since $Y$ is
nonempty and closed, it also contains zero. For $x,y\in Y$,
the tuple $(x,y,0,\ldots,0)$ belongs to $Z$. Applying the
matrix that replaces its first coordinate by the sum of its
first two coordinates shows that $x+y\in Y$. Thus $Y=A$ is
a vector subspace of dimension $d$.

The inclusion $A^r\subseteqC_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$ implies that every
element of $A$ is nilpotent and that $[A,A]=0$.
Moreover, $K$-stability of $Z$ implies $K$-stability of $A$.
Let $\xi$ be the grading element. Since $A$ is stable under
$\operatorname{ad}\xi$, its decomposition into eigenspaces is
\[
 A=(A\cap\mathfrak{p}^{+})\oplus(A\cap\mathfrak{p}^{-}).
\]
Each intersection is a $K$-submodule. By
\cref{lem:hermitian-basic}, the modules $\mathfrak{p}^{+}$ and $\mathfrak{p}^{-}$
are irreducible, so each intersection is either zero or the
whole corresponding summand. Since both summands have
dimension $d$ and $\dim A=d$, precisely one is present.
Therefore $A=\mathfrak{p}^{+}$ or $A=\mathfrak{p}^{-}$, proving \textup{(ii)}.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:pLevi} 
A \emph{\(\mathfrak{p}\)-Levi subalgebra} of \((\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})\) is a subalgebra of the form
\(\mathfrak l=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(h)\) with \(h\in\mathfrak{p}\) semisimple. Such an \(\mathfrak l\) is
reductive and \(\theta\)-stable (since \(\theta(\mathfrak l) = \mathfrak{z}_\mathfrak{g}(\theta h) = \mathfrak{z}_\mathfrak{g}(-h) = \mathfrak l\)), so
\[
\mathfrak l = \mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak l)\oplus[\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l],
\qquad
\mathfrak l\cap\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l},
\]
where
\[
\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l} = \mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak l)\cap\mathfrak{p},
\qquad
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l} = [\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l]\cap\mathfrak{p},
\qquad
c_{\mathfrak l} = \dim\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l},
\qquad
\delta_{\mathfrak l} = \dim\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}.
\]
We use the notation
\[
  \mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l})
  :=
  \mathcal{N}(\mathfrak l_{\mathrm{ss}})
  \cap\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l},
  \qquad
  \mathfrak l_{\mathrm{ss}}
  :=
  [\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l].
\]
Thus \(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l})\) is the nilpotent cone of the
\((-1)\)-eigenspace of the induced semisimple symmetric pair.
In particular, if
\(\mathfrak{k}_{\mathfrak l}=\mathfrak l_{\mathrm{ss}}\cap\mathfrak{k}\), then
\((\mathfrak l_{\mathrm{ss}},\mathfrak{k}_{\mathfrak l})\) is again a complex
semisimple symmetric pair (with the convention that if \(\mathfrak l_{\mathrm{ss}}=0\), then \(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}=0\) and \(\dimC_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}))=0\)). Its \((-1)\)-eigenspace is
\(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}\), so \cref{thm:universal} applies to it.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:jordan-plevi}
Let \(\mathbf x=(x_1,\dots,x_r)\inC_{r}(\mathfrak{p})\) and let \(x_i=s_i+n_i\) be the Jordan
decomposition in \(\mathfrak{g}\). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \(s_i,n_i\in\mathfrak{p}\) for every \(i\);
\item the \(2r\) elements \(s_1,\dots,s_r,n_1,\dots,n_r\) commute pairwise;
\item \(\mathfrak l:=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(s_1,\dots,s_r)\) is a \(\mathfrak{p}\)-Levi subalgebra;
\item \(s_i\in\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l}\) and \(n_i\in\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}\) for every
\(i\); in particular,
\((n_1,\dots,n_r)\inC_{r}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l})\bigr)\).
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
(i) We have \(-x_i=\theta(x_i)=\theta(s_i)+\theta(n_i)\), where \(\theta(s_i)\) is
semisimple and \(\theta(n_i)\) is nilpotent and commutes with \(\theta(s_i)\).
By uniqueness of the Jordan decomposition of \(-x_i = (-s_i) + (-n_i)\),
we obtain \(\theta(s_i)=-s_i\) and \(\theta(n_i)=-n_i\), so \(s_i, n_i \in \mathfrak{p}\).

(ii) In the faithful adjoint representation,
\[
  \operatorname{ad}(x_i) = \operatorname{ad}(s_i) + \operatorname{ad}(n_i)
\]
is the Jordan decomposition of \(\operatorname{ad}(x_i)\). In characteristic zero,
the semisimple and nilpotent parts of an endomorphism are polynomials without
constant term in that endomorphism. Consequently, the semisimple and nilpotent
parts of commuting endomorphisms commute pairwise. Since \(\mathfrak{g}\) is
semisimple, \(\operatorname{ad}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{g})\) is injective, and the assertion
follows.

(iii) The span \(\mathfrak a'=\langle s_1,\dots,s_r\rangle\) is an abelian
subspace of \(\mathfrak{p}\) consisting of commuting semisimple elements, so every element
of \(\mathfrak a'\) is semisimple. By the conjugacy theorem for Cartan
subspaces \cite{KostantRallis}, \(\mathfrak a'\) is contained in a Cartan
subspace of \(\mathfrak{p}\). After \(K\)-conjugation, we may assume that
\(\mathfrak a'\subseteq\mathfrak a\). By \eqref{eq:restricted-decomp},
\[
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak a') = \mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak a)\oplus
\bigoplus_{\alpha|_{\mathfrak a'}=0}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},
\qquad
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(h) = \mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak a)\oplus
\bigoplus_{\alpha(h)=0}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\qquad(h\in\mathfrak a').
\]
Because \(\Sigma\) is finite, a generic element \(h=\sum_i c_i s_i\in\mathfrak a'\)
satisfies \(\alpha(h)\ne0\) for every \(\alpha\in\Sigma\) that does not vanish
identically on \(\mathfrak a'\). For such an \(h\), the two right-hand sides
coincide, showing that \(\mathfrak l=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak a')=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(h)\)
is a \(\mathfrak{p}\)-Levi subalgebra.

(iv) By definition of \(\mathfrak l = \mathfrak{z}_\mathfrak{g}(s_1,\dots,s_r)\), every
element of \(\mathfrak l\) commutes with each \(s_i\). Since the \(s_j\) commute
pairwise, each \(s_i \in \mathfrak l\), which implies
\(s_i \in \mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak l) \cap \mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l}\).

Each \(n_i\) lies in \(\mathfrak l\cap\mathfrak{p}\) by (ii). Write \(n_i=z+y\) with
\(z\in\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l}\) and \(y\in\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}\), and let \(y=y_s+y_n\)
be the Jordan decomposition of \(y\) inside the semisimple algebra
\([\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l]\), which coincides with its Jordan decomposition in \(\mathfrak{g}\).
Because \(\mathfrak l = \mathfrak{z}_\mathfrak{g}(h)\) with \(h\) semisimple in semisimple \(\mathfrak{g}\),
its center \(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak l)\) is a toral subalgebra, so \(z\) is semisimple.
Since \(z \in \mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak l)\), it commutes with \(y_s\) and \(y_n\).
Thus, \(z+y_s\) is semisimple, \(y_n\) is nilpotent, the two commute, and
\(n_i=(z+y_s)+y_n\). Uniqueness of the Jordan decomposition of the nilpotent
element \(n_i\) forces \(z+y_s=0\). By directness of
\(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak l)\oplus[\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l]\), this yields \(z=0\)
and \(y_s=0\). Hence \(n_i=y_n\in\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}\). Finally, since the \(n_i\)
commute and are nilpotent in \([\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l]\), we have
\((n_1,\dots,n_r)\inC_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}))\).

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:plevi-roots}
Every \(\mathfrak{p}\)-Levi subalgebra of \((\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})\) is \(K\)-conjugate to a
subalgebra of the form
\[
\mathfrak l_I = \mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak a)\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma_I}
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\qquad I\subseteq\Delta.
\]
Conversely, every such \(\mathfrak l_I\) is a \(\mathfrak{p}\)-Levi subalgebra. In
particular, there are only finitely many \(K\)-conjugacy classes of
\(\mathfrak{p}\)-Levi subalgebras. Moreover, writing \(s=|I|\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-delta}
\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l_I} = \mathfrak a^{I},
\qquad
c_{\mathfrak l_I} = \ell-s,
\qquad
\delta_{\mathfrak l_I}
= s + \sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_I^{+}}\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
The classification is the restricted-root form of the description
of semisimple centralizers by Satake subdiagrams; see
\cite[Proposition~1.5]{PanyushevYakimova}.
For completeness, after $K$-conjugation let the defining semisimple
element lie in $\mathfrak a$. Choose a real form
$\mathfrak a_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq\mathfrak a$ on which all restricted
roots are real-valued, and write $h=u+iv$ with
$u,v\in\mathfrak a_{\mathbb{R}}$.
Then $\alpha(h)=0$ if and only if
$\alpha(u)=\alpha(v)=0$.
For a generic real number $t$, the element $h'=u+tv$
has exactly the same vanishing restricted roots as $h$,
and hence the same centralizer in $\mathfrak{g}$.
We may now move $h'$ into a closed real Weyl chamber.
Its vanishing roots form $\Sigma_I$ for a subset
$I\subseteq\Delta$, proving the assertion.
Conversely, a generic element of $\mathfrak a^I$ has centralizer
$\mathfrak l_I$.

It remains to establish the formulas in \eqref{eq:c-delta}. If
\(u\in\mathfrak a^{I}\), then \(u\) centralizes
\(\mathfrak{z}_\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak a)\) and acts by zero on each root space
\(\mathfrak{g}_\beta\) for \(\beta\in\Sigma_I\subseteq\mathbb{Z} I\) (since \(\beta(u)=0\));
thus \(u\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak l_I)\cap\mathfrak{p}\). Conversely, if
\(u\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak l_I)\cap\mathfrak{p}\), then \(u\) commutes with
\(\mathfrak a\) (since \(\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak{z}_\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak a)\subseteq\mathfrak l_I\)),
so \(u\in\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak a)=\mathfrak a\) by \ref{R1}.
Furthermore, \(u\) centralizes \(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\) for every \(\alpha\in I\),
so \(\alpha(u)=0\). Since the simple roots in \(I\) are linearly
independent, \(\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l_I} = \mathfrak a^I\) and
\(c_{\mathfrak l_I} = \dim\mathfrak a^{I} = \ell-s\).

Finally, by \ref{R2}, the subspace
\[
\mathfrak l_I\cap\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak a \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma_I^{+}}
(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha})\cap\mathfrak{p}
\]
has dimension \(\ell + \sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_I^{+}}\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\).
Because \(\mathfrak l_I\cap\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l_I}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l_I}\),
we obtain
\[
\delta_{\mathfrak l_I}
= \dim(\mathfrak l_I\cap\mathfrak{p}) - c_{\mathfrak l_I}
= \Bigl(\ell + \sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_I^{+}}\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\Bigr) - (\ell - s)
= s + \sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_I^{+}}\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},
\]
which completes the proof.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:plevi-orbit-dim}
For every \(\mathfrak{p}\)-Levi subalgebra \(\mathfrak l\),
\[
\dim K - \dim N_K(\mathfrak l) = \dim\mathfrak{p} - c_{\mathfrak l} - \delta_{\mathfrak l}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Write \(\mathfrak l = \mathfrak{z}_\mathfrak{g}(h)\) with \(h\in\mathfrak{p}\) semisimple. First,
the Lie algebra of \(N_K(\mathfrak l)\) is
\(\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak n_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak l) = \mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak l\), since
\(\mathfrak l\) is self-normalizing in \(\mathfrak{g}\): if \(y\in\mathfrak n_\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak l)\),
then \([y,h]\in\mathfrak l = \mathfrak{z}_\mathfrak{g}(h)\), so \((\operatorname{ad} h)^2(y) = 0\);
since \(\operatorname{ad} h\) is semisimple, this forces \([y,h]=0\), whence
\(y\in\mathfrak l\).

Next, \(\operatorname{ad} h\) maps \(\mathfrak{k}\to\mathfrak{p}\) and \(\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{k}\). These two
linear maps are mutual adjoints up to sign with respect to the nondegenerate
symmetric forms \(B|_{\mathfrak{k}}\) and \(B|_{\mathfrak{p}}\), since invariance gives
\[
B([h,x],y) = -B(x,[h,y]) \qquad (x\in\mathfrak{k},\, y\in\mathfrak{p}).
\]
Consequently, they have equal rank:
\(\operatorname{rk}(\operatorname{ad} h|_{\mathfrak{k}}) = \operatorname{rk}(\operatorname{ad} h|_{\mathfrak{p}})\).
Their kernels are
\(\ker(\operatorname{ad} h|_\mathfrak{k}) = \mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak l\) and
\(\ker(\operatorname{ad} h|_\mathfrak{p}) = \mathfrak{p}\cap\mathfrak l\). Therefore,
\[
\dim\mathfrak{k} - \dim(\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak l)
= \dim\mathfrak{p} - \dim(\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathfrak l)
= \dim\mathfrak{p} - c_{\mathfrak l} - \delta_{\mathfrak l}.
\]
Since \(\dim K = \dim\mathfrak{k}\) and \(\dim N_K(\mathfrak l) = \dim(\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak l)\),
the left-hand side equals \(\dim K - \dim N_K(\mathfrak l)\).

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:plevi-family-bound}
For every complex semisimple symmetric pair, every \(r\ge2\), and every
\(\mathfrak{p}\)-Levi subalgebra \(\mathfrak l\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plevi-family-bound}
  \dim X_{\mathfrak l}
  \le
  \dim\mathfrak{p}
  +(r-1)c_{\mathfrak l}
  +
  \left\lfloor
    \frac{(r-2)\delta_{\mathfrak l}}{2}
  \right\rfloor .
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
  \dim X_{\mathfrak l}
  \le
  \dim\mathfrak{p}
  +(r-1)c_{\mathfrak l}
  +\frac{r-2}{2}\delta_{\mathfrak l}.
\]
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
We abbreviate \(c=c_{\mathfrak l}\), \(\delta=\delta_{\mathfrak l}\), and put
\(N=N_K(\mathfrak l)^{\circ}\). The connected group \(N\) preserves
\(\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l}\) and \(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}\), and acts trivially on
the finite set of irreducible components of \(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}))\);
hence \(N\) preserves every individual component.

Let \(Y\) be an irreducible component of \(C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}))\). The group
\(N\) acts freely on \(K\times\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l}^{\,r}\times Y\) by
\[
  u\cdot(k,\mathbf s,\mathbf n)
  =
  (ku^{-1},u\mathbf s,u\mathbf n).
\]
This action preserves the fibers of \(\Phi_{\mathfrak l}\). Therefore,
\[
  \dim\operatorname{im}
  \bigl(
    \Phi_{\mathfrak l}
    |_{K\times\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak l}^{\,r}\times Y}
  \bigr)
  \le
  \dim K-\dim N+rc+\dim Y.
\]
Since \(\dim N=\dim N_K(\mathfrak l)\), taking the maximum over all irreducible
components \(Y\) yields
\[
  \dim X_{\mathfrak l}
  \le
  \dim K-\dim N_K(\mathfrak l)
  +rc+\dimC_{r}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l})\bigr).
\]
By \cref{def:pLevi}, the pair \(([\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l],[\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l]\cap\mathfrak{k})\)
is a complex semisimple symmetric pair with \((-1)\)-eigenspace \(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l}\).
Applying \cref{thm:universal} to this pair gives
\[
  \dimC_{r}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak l})\bigr)
  \le
  \left\lfloor\frac{r\delta}{2}\right\rfloor .
\]
Combining this with \cref{lem:plevi-orbit-dim}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
  \dim X_{\mathfrak l}
  &\le
  (\dim\mathfrak{p}-c-\delta)+rc
  +\left\lfloor\frac{r\delta}{2}\right\rfloor\\
  &=
  \dim\mathfrak{p}+(r-1)c
  +\left\lfloor\frac{(r-2)\delta}{2}\right\rfloor ,
\end{align*}
where the last equality follows from \(-\delta + \lfloor r\delta/2 \rfloor = \lfloor (r-2)\delta/2 \rfloor\).

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:cartan-component}
For every complex semisimple symmetric pair and every $r\ge1$,
the subvariety $\overline{K\cdot\mathfrak a^r}$ is an
irreducible component of $C_{r}(\mathfrak{p})$ of dimension
$\dim\mathfrak{p}+(r-1)\ell$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Let
\[
 \mathfrak a_{\mathrm{reg}}
 =\{h\in\mathfrak a:\alpha(h)\ne0 \text{ for every }\alpha\in\Sigma\}.
\]
The restricted-root decomposition gives
$\mathfrak z_\mathfrak{p}(h)=\mathfrak a$ for
$h\in\mathfrak a_{\mathrm{reg}}$.
By \cite{KostantRallis}, the set
\[\mathfrak{p}_{\mathrm{rss}}:=K\cdot\mathfrak a_{\mathrm{reg}}
\]
is a nonempty Zariski-open subset of $\mathfrak{p}$.
Its elements are precisely the semisimple $x\in\mathfrak{p}$ satisfying
$\dim\mathfrak z_\mathfrak{p}(x)=\ell$; these are the regular semisimple
elements of the isotropy representation.
The open subset
\[
 U=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_r)\inC_{r}(\mathfrak{p}):x_1\in\mathfrak{p}_{\mathrm{rss}}\}
\]
is the image of
\[
 K\times\bigl(\mathfrak a_{\mathrm{reg}}\times
                    \mathfrak a^{r-1}\bigr)
 \longrightarrow\mathfrak{p}^r,
 \qquad (k,\mathbf h)\longmapsto k\mathbf h.
\]
Indeed, after conjugating $x_1$ into $\mathfrak a_{\mathrm{reg}}$,
commutativity forces every other entry into its centralizer
$\mathfrak a$ in $\mathfrak{p}$.
Thus $U$ is irreducible, and its closure is
$\overline{K\cdot\mathfrak a^r}$. The closure of a nonempty
irreducible open subset is an irreducible component.

We compute the fibres of the displayed morphism.
If $k\mathbf h=k'\mathbf h'$, then $u=k^{-1}k'$ satisfies
$uh'_1=h_1$. Both first entries are regular, so
\[
 u\mathfrak a=u\mathfrak z_\mathfrak{p}(h'_1)
              =\mathfrak z_\mathfrak{p}(h_1)=\mathfrak a.
\]
Hence $u\in N_K(\mathfrak a)$. Conversely, every element of
this normalizer gives a point of the same fibre by changing
$(k,\mathbf h)$ to $(ku^{-1},u\mathbf h)$.
Every fibre therefore has dimension $\dim N_K(\mathfrak a)$,
which equals $\dim Z_K(\mathfrak a)$ because the little Weyl
group is finite.

For $\mathfrak l=\mathfrak z_\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak a)$, we have
$c_{\mathfrak l}=\ell$ and $\delta_{\mathfrak l}=0$.
Moreover, the Lie algebras of $N_K(\mathfrak l)$ and
$Z_K(\mathfrak a)$ both equal
$\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak z_\mathfrak{g}(\mathfrak a)$.
By \cref{lem:plevi-orbit-dim},
\[
 \dim K-\dim Z_K(\mathfrak a)=\dim\mathfrak{p}-\ell.
\]
The fibre-dimension theorem now gives
\[
 \dim\overline{K\cdot\mathfrak a^r}
 =\dim K-\dim Z_K(\mathfrak a)+r\ell
 =\dim\mathfrak{p}+(r-1)\ell.
\]

For every complex semisimple symmetric pair,

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:hermitian-plevi-codim}
Let \(\mathfrak l\) be a \(\mathfrak{p}\)-Levi subalgebra of a simple Hermitian
symmetric pair, and write \(c=c_{\mathfrak l}\), \(\delta=\delta_{\mathfrak l}\).
Then
\[
  \delta=0 \iff c=\ell,
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \dim\mathfrak{p}-\delta \ge 4c \quad\text{whenever } 0<c<\ell.
\]
Moreover, equality $\dim\mathfrak{p}-\delta=4c$ with $0<c<\ell$
can occur only for the pair of type $\mathrm{CI}(2)$,
equivalently $\mathrm{BDI}_{\mathrm{Herm}}(3)$.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
By \cref{lem:plevi-roots}, after \(K\)-conjugation we may assume that
\(\mathfrak l = \mathfrak l_I\) for some subset of simple roots \(I\subseteq\Delta\).
Set \(s=|I|\). By \eqref{eq:c-delta},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:codim-c-delta}
  c = \ell-s,
  \qquad
  \delta = s + \sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_I^+}\dim\mathfrak{g}_\alpha.
\end{equation}
Since every term in the sum is strictly positive, \(\delta=0\) if and only if
\(s=0\), which is equivalent to \(c=\ell\). This establishes the first
equivalence.

We now assume that \(0<c<\ell\), which is equivalent to \(1\le s\le\ell-1\) and
\(c=\ell-s\). The simple restricted roots of \(\Sigma\) are ordered as
\[
  \alpha_1 = e_1-e_2,\ \ldots,\ \alpha_{\ell-1} = e_{\ell-1}-e_\ell,
\]
together with the special end root \(\alpha_\ell\), which is \(2e_\ell\) in
type \(C_\ell\) and \(e_\ell\) in type \(BC_\ell\).

Any subset \(I\subseteq\Delta\) decomposes into connected components of the
restricted Dynkin diagram. Each connected component of \(I\) that does not
contain the special node \(\alpha_\ell\) is a path of type \(A_t\) for some
\(t\ge1\). Its contribution to the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:codim-c-delta}
is precisely
\[
  t + a_\Sigma\binom{t+1}{2} = t + \frac{a_\Sigma}{2}t(t+1) = A(t).
\]
If a connected component of \(I\) contains the special node \(\alpha_\ell\) and
has rank \(u\ge1\), it is of type \(C_u\) or \(BC_u\), and its contribution to
\eqref{eq:codim-c-delta} is
\[
  u + \Bigl(u + a_\Sigma u(u-1) + 2b_\Sigma u\Bigr) = 2u + a_\Sigma u(u-1) + 2b_\Sigma u = H(u).
\]
Thus, if the components not containing \(\alpha_\ell\) have ranks
\(t_1,\ldots,t_v\), and if \(u\) denotes the rank of the component containing
\(\alpha_\ell\) (with \(u=0\) when no such component exists), then
\[
  s = u + t_1 + \cdots + t_v,
\]
and \(\delta\) decomposes as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:codim-block-decomposition}
  \delta =
  \begin{cases}
    \displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^v A(t_j), & \text{if } u=0,\\[3mm]
    \displaystyle H(u) + \sum_{j=1}^v A(t_j), & \text{if } u>0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

The following two elementary identities allow us to merge these blocks:
\begin{align}
  A(x+y) - A(x) - A(y)
  &= a_\Sigma xy \ge 0,
  \label{eq:codim-A-merge}\\
  H(u+t) - H(u) - A(t)
  &= \frac{t}{2}\bigl(a_\Sigma(t+4u-3) + 4b_\Sigma + 2\bigr) \ge 0,
  \label{eq:codim-H-merge}
\end{align}
valid for all \(x,y\ge0\) and \(u,t\ge1\), since \(a_\Sigma\ge1\) and
\(b_\Sigma\ge0\).

If \(u=0\), repeated application of \eqref{eq:codim-A-merge} yields
\(\delta \le A(s)\). If \(u>0\), repeated application of \eqref{eq:codim-H-merge}
yields \(\delta \le H(s)\). Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:codim-delta-max}
  \delta \le \max\{A(s), H(s)\}.
\end{equation}

It remains to compare \(A(s)\) and \(H(s)\) with \(H(\ell) = \dim\mathfrak{p}\). For
every \(t\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:codim-H-increment}
  H(t+1) - H(t) = 2(a_\Sigma t + b_\Sigma + 1) \ge 2(1\cdot 1 + 0 + 1) = 4.
\end{equation}
Since \(\ell = s+c\), telescoping across the \(c\) steps gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:codim-H-bound}
  H(\ell) - H(s) = \sum_{t=s}^{\ell-1}\bigl(H(t+1) - H(t)\bigr) \ge 4c.
\end{equation}

For the term \(A(s)\), a direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:codim-A-first-step}
  H(s+1) - A(s) - 4
  = \frac12\bigl(a_\Sigma s(s+1) + 4b_\Sigma(s+1) + 2s - 4\bigr).
\end{equation}
For \(s=1\), the parenthetical expression evaluates to
\(2a_\Sigma + 8b_\Sigma - 2 \ge 2(1) - 2 = 0\). For \(s\ge2\), we have
\(2s-4\ge0\) and \(a_\Sigma s(s+1) > 0\). Hence \(H(s+1) - A(s) \ge 4\) for all
\(s\ge1\). Combining this with \eqref{eq:codim-H-increment} for the remaining
\(c-1\) increments yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:codim-A-bound}
  H(\ell) - A(s)
  = \bigl(H(s+1) - A(s)\bigr) + \sum_{t=s+1}^{\ell-1}\bigl(H(t+1) - H(t)\bigr)
  \ge 4 + 4(c-1) = 4c.
\end{equation}

Combining \eqref{eq:codim-delta-max}, \eqref{eq:codim-H-bound}, and
\eqref{eq:codim-A-bound}, we conclude that
\[
  \dim\mathfrak{p} - \delta
  = H(\ell) - \delta
  \ge H(\ell) - \max\{A(s), H(s)\}
  \ge 4c.
\]
For the equality assertion, equality in
$H(\ell)-H(s)\ge4c$ forces every increment
$2(a_\Sigma t+b_\Sigma+1)$, for $s\le t\le\ell-1$,
to equal $4$. Thus $s=c=1$, $a_\Sigma=1$, and $b_\Sigma=0$.
Equality in $H(\ell)-A(s)\ge4c$ first requires
\[
 a_\Sigma s(s+1)+4b_\Sigma(s+1)+2s-4=0.
\]
This again forces $s=1$, $a_\Sigma=1$, and $b_\Sigma=0$;
any further increment is strictly larger than $4$, so $c=1$.
Since $\delta\le\max\{A(s),H(s)\}$, these are the only
possible parameters for $\dim\mathfrak{p}-\delta=4c$.
The restricted-root table identifies the pair as $\mathrm{CI}(2)$.
This completes the proof.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:full-hermitian}
Let \(\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}\) be a nonzero complex simple Hermitian
symmetric pair, and set \(d=\dim\mathfrak{p}^{+}\) and \(\ell=\operatorname{rk}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})\). Then, for every
\(r\ge2\),
\[
  \dimC_{r}(\mathfrak{p})
  =
  \max\bigl\{
    rd,\,
    2d+(r-1)\ell
  \bigr\}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  \dimC_{r}(\mathfrak{p})
  =
  \max\bigl\{
    \dim (\mathfrak{p}^{+})^r,\,
    \dim \overline{K\cdot\mathfrak a^r}
  \bigr\}.
\]
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
For \(r=2\), since \(\dim\mathfrak{p}=2d\) and \(\ell\ge1\), we have
\[
  \max\{2d,\, 2d+\ell\} = 2d+\ell = \dim\mathfrak{p}+\ell.
\]
The formula therefore follows from \cref{cor:r2-general}. We may therefore assume
\(r\ge3\).

We first establish the upper bound. Let \(\mathfrak l\) be an arbitrary
\(\mathfrak{p}\)-Levi subalgebra, and set \(c=c_{\mathfrak l}\) and
\(\delta=\delta_{\mathfrak l}\). Then \(0\le c\le\ell\) and \(\dim\mathfrak{p}=2d\).
By \cref{prop:plevi-family-bound}, the family closure \(X_{\mathfrak l}\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-family-bound}
  \dim X_{\mathfrak l}
  \le
  2d+(r-1)c+\frac{r-2}{2}\delta.
\end{equation}
We analyze three cases based on the value of \(c\):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Case \(c=\ell\).} By \cref{lem:hermitian-plevi-codim},
\(\delta=0\). Substituting into \eqref{eq:main-family-bound} gives
\[
  \dim X_{\mathfrak l} \le 2d+(r-1)\ell.
\]
\item \textbf{Case \(c=0\).} By \cref{lem:plevi-roots}, \(\mathfrak l=\mathfrak{g}\).
Consequently, \(\delta=\dim\mathfrak{p}=2d\) and \(X_{\mathfrak l}=C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))\).
Then \eqref{eq:main-family-bound} yields
\[
  \dim X_{\mathfrak l} \le 2d+\frac{r-2}{2}(2d) = rd.
\]
(In fact, equality holds by \cref{thm:hermitian}.)
\item \textbf{Case \(0<c<\ell\).} By \cref{lem:hermitian-plevi-codim}, the
codimension satisfies
\[
  2d-\delta = \dim\mathfrak{p}-\delta \ge 4c.
\]
Rewriting \eqref{eq:main-family-bound} by subtracting \(rd\), we obtain
\begin{align*}
  \dim X_{\mathfrak l} - rd
  &\le
  (2-r)d + (r-1)c + \frac{r-2}{2}\delta \\
  &=
  (r-1)c - \frac{r-2}{2}(2d-\delta) \\
  &\le
  (r-1)c - \frac{r-2}{2}(4c) \\
  &=
  (r-1)c - 2(r-2)c \\
  &=
  (3-r)c.
\end{align*}
Since \(r\ge3\) and \(c>0\), we have \((3-r)c \le 0\). Thus,
\(\dim X_{\mathfrak l} \le rd\) for every intermediate \(\mathfrak{p}\)-Levi subalgebra.
\end{enumerate}

Taking the maximum over the finite closed cover
\(C_{r}(\mathfrak{p}) = \bigcup_{\mathfrak l} X_{\mathfrak l}\) from \eqref{eq:plevi-cover},
we conclude that
\[
  \dimC_{r}(\mathfrak{p})
  \le
  \max\bigl\{
    rd,\,
    2d+(r-1)\ell
  \bigr\}.
\]

For the matching lower bound, \((\mathfrak{p}^{+})^r\) is a closed irreducible subvariety
of \(C_{r}(\mathfrak{p})\), so \(\dimC_{r}(\mathfrak{p})\ge\dim(\mathfrak{p}^{+})^r = rd\). Furthermore,
\cref{lem:cartan-component} shows that \(\overline{K\cdot\mathfrak a^r}\) is a
closed irreducible subvariety of \(C_{r}(\mathfrak{p})\) of dimension
\(\dim\mathfrak{p}+(r-1)\ell = 2d+(r-1)\ell\). Hence
\[
  \dimC_{r}(\mathfrak{p}) \ge \max\bigl\{rd,\, 2d+(r-1)\ell\bigr\},
\]
which proves the equality.

<!-- corollary-7: body_md -->
\label{cor:threshold}
Let \((\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})\) be a nonzero complex simple Hermitian symmetric pair.
For every integer \(r\ge2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
  rd < 2d+(r-1)\ell &\iff r < \tau,\\
  rd = 2d+(r-1)\ell &\iff r = \tau,\\
  rd > 2d+(r-1)\ell &\iff r > \tau.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
  \dimC_{r}(\mathfrak{p})
  =
  \begin{cases}
    \dim\overline{K\cdot\mathfrak a^r} = 2d+(r-1)\ell, & \text{if } r<\tau,\\[2mm]
    \dim(\mathfrak{p}^{+})^r = rd, & \text{if } r>\tau,
  \end{cases}
\]
and the two dimensions coincide precisely when \(\tau\) is an integer and
\(r=\tau\).

Up to isomorphism, the simple Hermitian symmetric pairs partition into the
following three cases:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \(\rho=1\) occurs uniquely for \((\mathfrak{sl}_2,\mathfrak{so}_2)\); in
this case \(\tau=+\infty\), and the Cartan family has strictly larger dimension
than the linear nilpotent families for every \(r\ge2\).

\item \(\rho=\tfrac32\) occurs uniquely for
\((\mathfrak{sp}_4,\mathfrak{gl}_2) \cong (\mathfrak{so}_5, \mathfrak{so}_3\oplus\mathfrak{so}_2)\);
in this case \(\tau=4\), so the Cartan family strictly dominates for \(r\in\{2,3\}\),
an exact dimensional tie occurs at \(r=4\), and the nilpotent families strictly
dominate for all \(r\ge5\).

\item Every other simple Hermitian symmetric pair satisfies
\[
  \rho\ge2,
  \qquad
  \tau\le3.
\]
In particular, the linear nilpotent families strictly dominate for all \(r\ge4\),
and they dominate or tie with the Cartan family at \(r=3\).
\end{enumerate}

For $\rho>1$, this has the sign of $r-\tau$.

<!-- corollary-8: body_md -->
\label{cor:full-components}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:full-hermitian}, put
\[
 L_r=rd,\qquad S_r=2d+(r-1)\ell,\qquad
 \mathcal T_r=\overline{K\cdot\mathfrak a^r},
\]
where $\mathfrak a$ is a Cartan subspace of $\mathfrak{p}$.
The maximal-dimensional irreducible components of $C_{r}(\mathfrak{p})$
are precisely:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $(\mathfrak{p}^{+})^r$ and $(\mathfrak{p}^{-})^r$ if $L_r>S_r$;
\item $\mathcal T_r$ if $L_r<S_r$;
\item $(\mathfrak{p}^{+})^r$, $(\mathfrak{p}^{-})^r$, and $\mathcal T_r$ if $L_r=S_r$.
\end{enumerate}
Equality occurs exactly in the following cases, up to isomorphism:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
r=3:&
\mathrm{AIII}(1,2),\quad
\mathrm{AIII}(2,2)\cong\mathrm{BDI}_{\mathrm{Herm}}(4),\quad
\mathrm{CI}(3),\\[1mm]
r=4:&\mathrm{CI}(2).
\end{array}
\]
<!-- end corollary-8 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
The two linear varieties have dimension $L_r$, and
$\mathcal T_r$ has dimension $S_r$ by
\cref{lem:cartan-component}. They are pairwise distinct:
$\mathfrak{p}^{+}\cap\mathfrak{p}^{-}=0$, while $\mathcal T_r$ contains tuples
with a nonzero semisimple entry.

For $r=2$, the unique maximal-dimensional component is
$\mathcal T_2$ by \cref{cor:r2-general}.
Let $r\ge3$. By \cref{lem:intermediate-levi}, the intermediate
$\mathfrak{p}$-Levi families have dimension strictly less than
$\dimC_{r}(\mathfrak{p})$. The family with $c=\ell$ is $\mathcal T_r$,
and the family with $c=0$ is $C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$.
Any maximal-dimensional irreducible component of $C_{r}(\mathfrak{p})$
is contained in one member of the finite closed cover
\eqref{eq:plevi-cover}. The intermediate families have just
been excluded. If it is contained in the nilpotent family,
it must have dimension $L_r$ and hence must be one of the two
linear components, by \cref{thm:hermitian-max-components}.
If it is contained in $\mathcal T_r$, irreducibility forces
it to equal $\mathcal T_r$.
Conversely, each listed subvariety whose dimension equals
$\max\{L_r,S_r\}$ is an irreducible component.
This proves the three alternatives.

Finally, equality is impossible for $r=2$. For $r\ge3$,
$L_r=S_r$ is equivalent to
$d/\ell-1=1/(r-2)$. By \eqref{eq:d-ab}, $d/\ell-1=\tfrac{a_\Sigma}{2}(\ell-1)+b_\Sigma$ lies in
$\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}$, so $1/(r-2)\in\{1,\tfrac12\}$ and $r\in\{3,4\}$. Substitution from \eqref{eq:ab-table}
gives the displayed list.

\label{ex:sl2herm}
Consider $(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k}) = (\mathfrak{sl}_2,\mathfrak{so}_2)$,
the Hermitian symmetric pair of type $\mathrm{AIII}(1,1)$, where
$\mathfrak{k} = \mathbb{C} h \cong \mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak{gl}_1\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_1)$ and
$\mathfrak{p} = \mathbb{C} e\oplus\mathbb{C} f$ in terms of the standard $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple $(e,h,f)$.
The three-grading is given by
\[
  \mathfrak{p}^{+} = \mathbb{C} e, \qquad \mathfrak{p}^{-} = \mathbb{C} f.
\]
For any two elements $x = ae+bf$ and $y = a'e+b'f$ in $\mathfrak{p}$, their Lie bracket is
\[
  [x,y] = (ab'-a'b)h.
\]
Thus a tuple $(x_1,\dots,x_r)\in\mathfrak{p}^r$, with $x_i = a_i e + b_i f$, commutes if
and only if
\[
  a_i b_j - a_j b_i = 0 \qquad \text{for all } 1\le i<j\le r.
\]
Consequently, the full commuting variety $C_{r}(\mathfrak{p})$ is canonically isomorphic to
the determinantal variety of $2\times r$ matrices of rank at most $1$:
\[
  C_{r}(\mathfrak{p})
  \cong
  \left\{
    \begin{pmatrix} a_1 & \cdots & a_r \\ b_1 & \cdots & b_r \end{pmatrix}
    \in \operatorname{Mat}_{2\times r}(\mathbb{C}) :
    \operatorname{rk}\begin{pmatrix} a_1 & \cdots & a_r \\ b_1 & \cdots & b_r \end{pmatrix} \le 1
  \right\}.
\]
Because the variety of $2\times r$ matrices of rank at most $1$ has dimension
$2 + r - 1 = r + 1$, we obtain
\[
  \dimC_{r}(\mathfrak{p}) = r+1,
\]
in exact agreement with \cref{thm:full-hermitian} (since $d=1$ and $\ell=1$,
giving $\max\{r,\, 2+(r-1)\} = r+1$).

On the other hand, an element $ae+bf \in \mathfrak{p}$ has determinant $-ab$, so it is
nilpotent if and only if $ab=0$. It follows that a commuting tuple of
nilpotents must have all its entries in $\mathbb{C} e$ or all its entries in $\mathbb{C} f$.
Therefore,
\[
  C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p})) = (\mathbb{C} e)^r \cup (\mathbb{C} f)^r = (\mathfrak{p}^{+})^r \cup (\mathfrak{p}^{-})^r.
\]
Thus $C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{p}))$ has dimension $r$ and has exactly two irreducible
components. Moreover, the variety of $1$-dimensional abelian nilpotent
subspaces is
\[
  \mathcal{A}_1(\mathfrak{p}) = \{\mathfrak{p}^{+},\,\mathfrak{p}^{-}\},
\]
providing the smallest instance of \cref{prop:Ab-hermitian} and
\cref{thm:hermitian-max-components}.

\label{thm:equivariant-quot}
Let $r\ge2$, $m,n,s_+,s_-\ge0$, and $m+n\ge1$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equiv-quot-bound}
 \dim\mathsf Q^{m,n;s_+,s_-}_{r,0}
 \le rmn+s_+m+s_-n-m^2-n^2.
\end{equation}
Equality holds if at least one of the following conditions holds:
\begin{align}
 &s_-\ge n,\qquad m\le s_++rn,
 \label{eq:plus-stability}\\
 &s_+\ge m,\qquad n\le s_-+rm.
 \label{eq:minus-stability}
\end{align}
If $r\ge3$ and $m,n\ge1$, these conditions are also necessary:
equality holds if and only if at least one holds.
When equality holds in this range, there is exactly one
maximal-dimensional component for each of
\eqref{eq:plus-stability} and \eqref{eq:minus-stability}
that holds.

For every $r\ge2$, if $m,n\ge1$, $s_+\ge m$, and $s_-\ge n$,
the scheme has at least two maximal-dimensional components, two of which are the closed loci
\[
 \mathfrak mH^0(\mathcal F)\subseteq H^0(\mathcal F)_+,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak mH^0(\mathcal F)\subseteq H^0(\mathcal F)_-.
\]
For $r\ge3$ there are no others; for $r=2$ there may be
(\cref{rem:equivariant-quot-surface}).

Put

The generating locus

 \mathcal U

is open. To see this, put $L=m+n$. Simultaneous strict
triangularisation implies that every product of $L$ coordinate
operators is zero. Generation is therefore equivalent to
surjectivity of the finite matrix whose columns are
$X_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots X_r^{\alpha_r}\phi(w)$, for basis
vectors $w$ in $W$ and nonnegative integers $\alpha_i$ with
$\sum_i\alpha_i<L$. This is an open rank condition.
The map from $\mathcal U$ to $\mathsf Q^{m,n;s_+,s_-}_{r,0}$ is the

bundles, hence a Zariski locally trivial $G_{m,n}$-torsor.
The action is

   =\bigl((gX_ig^{-1})_{i=1}^r,g\phi\bigr)

\]
An automorphism commuting

of the framing. On $(\mathfrak p_{m,n}^+)^r$, all operators kill $V_+$.
The framing generates precisely when
\[
 \phi_-(W_-)=V_-,
 \qquad
 \phi_+(W_+)+\sum_iX_i(V_-)=V_+.
\]
Such a framing and tuple exist if and only if
$s_-\ge n$ and $s_++rn\ge m$:
after choosing a surjection $W_-\to V_-$, the second condition

The analogous calculation on $(\mathfrak p_{m,n}^-)^r$

Each nonempty intersection is open in a variety of dimension

The symmetric pair of type $\mathrm{AIII}$ has simple ambient

By \cref{thm:hermitian-max-components}, the only

 Z_+=(\mathfrak p_{m,n}^+)^r,\qquad
 Z_-=(\mathfrak p_{m,n}^-)^r.
\]
Therefore $\dim\mathcal U=rmn+\dim F$ if and only if

are nonempty. The two linear components are distinct, and, by \cref{rem:equivariant-stack}, their images under the torsor are exactly the two stated loci. 
These loci are closed because the conditions are the vanishing
of the induced maps between the tautological character bundles.

\label{rem:equivariant-quot-degenerate}
The assumptions $m,n\ge1$ in the component count are essential.
If $n=0$ and $s_+\ge m$, then the coordinate operators vanish,
and taking the kernel of the framing gives
\[
 \mathsf Q^{m,0;s_+,s_-}_{r,0}\cong\operatorname{Gr}(s_+-m,W_+).
\]
Here $\operatorname{Gr}(k,W)$ denotes the Grassmannian of $k$-dimensional
linear subspaces of $W$. The Quot scheme is empty when $s_+<m$.
The analogous statement holds
when $m=0$, with the two parities interchanged.
In these cases the two linear families coincide.

\label{rem:equivariant-quot-surface}
For $r=2$, the preceding construction has a standard quiver
interpretation. Write
\[
  X_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_1\\B_1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 X_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_2\\B_2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The equation $[X_1,X_2]=0$ is equivalent to
$A_1B_2-A_2B_1=0$ and $B_1A_2-B_2A_1=0$.
The Kronecker quiver has two vertices and two arrows from
one vertex to the other; its double adds a reverse arrow
for each arrow. Assigning $A_1,A_2$ to the two forward arrows
and $B_2,-B_1$ to the reverse arrows turns the displayed equations
into the preprojective relations.

Consider the Nakajima quiver variety for this doubled quiver,
with dimension vector $(m,n)$, framing vector $(s_+,s_-)$,
and complex moment-map parameter zero. Choose the generic
stability condition requiring the images of the framing maps
$W_\pm\to V_\pm$ to generate the representation.
Its Lagrangian fibre is the reduced fibre over zero of the
projective morphism from the stable quiver variety to its
affine quotient. By \cite[Lemma~5.9]{NakajimaALE}, transposed to the stability convention just specified, this fibre consists
of jointly nilpotent representations with the reverse framing
maps $V_\pm\to W_\pm$ equal to zero. It is therefore
isomorphic to $\mathsf Q^{m,n;s_+,s_-}_{2,0}$.
Nakajima's theorem \cite[Theorem~5.8]{NakajimaALE} consequently
gives the stronger known statement that every nonempty
$\mathsf Q^{m,n;s_+,s_-}_{2,0}$ is equidimensional of dimension
\[
  2mn+s_+m+s_-n-m^2-n^2.
\]
In particular, when $r=2$, nonemptiness alone implies equality
in \eqref{eq:equiv-quot-bound}. The conditions
\eqref{eq:plus-stability} and \eqref{eq:minus-stability}
are sufficient but need not be necessary in this case.
For example, the invariant quotient $\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(x^4,y)$ has
character multiplicities $(2,2)$ and trivial one-dimensional
framing, although neither condition holds.
The necessity and exact component count in
\cref{thm:equivariant-quot} concern $r\ge3$.

\label{thm:equivariant-gorenstein}
Let $r\ge2$. For $N\geq2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gorenstein-bound}
  \dim\mathcal H_{r,N}
  \leq
  \left\lfloor
    \frac r2\left(\frac{N(N+1)}2-1\right)
  \right\rfloor
  -\frac{N(N-1)}2.
\end{equation}
For $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-gorenstein-bound}
  \dim\mathsf G^{\mathrm{odd}}_{r,2n}
  \leq\frac{rn(n+1)}2-n^2.
\end{equation}
For $m\geq2$ and $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:even-gorenstein-bound}
  \dim\mathsf G^{\mathrm{even}}_{r,(m,n)}
  \leq
  \left\lfloor\frac{rmn}{2}\right\rfloor
  -\binom m2-\binom n2.
\end{equation}
The ordinary estimate also holds at any smooth point of a complex
$r$-dimensional variety. The hypotheses in
\eqref{eq:even-gorenstein-bound} are not restrictive: the length-one
Gorenstein locus is a point, and every invariant even-socle quotient of
length greater than one has $m\ge2$ and $n\ge1$, as the proof shows.

\emph{The ordinary locus.}
Fix a nondegenerate symmetric form $b$ on $V=\mathbb{C}^N$.
Write $\mathrm O(V,b)$ for its isometry group, consisting
of $g\in\mathrm{GL}(V)$ such that $b(gu,gw)=b(u,w)$ for
all $u,w\in V$. Put
\[
  \mathfrak p_{\mathrm{AI}}
  =\{X\in\mathfrak{sl}(V):b(Xu,w)=b(u,Xw)
                                      \text{ for all }u,w\in V\}.
\]
This is the $(-1)$-eigenspace of the symmetric pair
$(\mathfrak{sl}(V),\mathfrak{so}(V,b))$, and
\[
  \dim\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{AI}}=\frac{N(N+1)}2-1.
\]
Consider the open cyclic locus
\[
  \mathcal U_{\mathrm{AI}}
  =\{(X_\bullet,v)\in
       C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{AI}}))\times V:
       \mathbb{C}[X_1,\ldots,X_r]v=V\}.
\]
For $(X_\bullet,v)\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{AI}}$, the kernel
$I$ of $R\to V$, $f\mapsto f(X_\bullet)v$, is an ideal:
if $f(X_\bullet)v=0$, commutativity gives
$(gf)(X_\bullet)v=g(X_\bullet)f(X_\bullet)v=0$.
Cyclicity identifies $A=R/I$ with $V$ as an $R$-module,
and the class of $1$ corresponds to $v$.
Since the $X_i$ are commuting nilpotent operators, $A$ is
supported at the origin. Define
\[
  \lambda(\bar f)=b\bigl(v,f(X_\bullet)v\bigr).
\]
Self-adjointness and commutativity imply
\[
  \lambda(\bar f\bar g)
  =b\bigl(f(X_\bullet)v,g(X_\bullet)v\bigr).
\]
Thus the Frobenius pairing on $A$ is identified with $b$,
so it is nondegenerate and $A$ is Gorenstein.
Conversely, a quotient $A=R/I$ with a Frobenius functional
$\lambda$ and an isometry $(A,b_\lambda)\simeq(V,b)$
gives such a tuple, with $v$ the image of $1$.
The multiplication operators are nilpotent and hence traceless,
so they do lie in $\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{AI}}$.

Over $\mathcal H_{r,N}$, the Frobenius functionals form an
open subset of the dual of the rank-$N$ tautological bundle,
with every fibre nonempty and of dimension $N$.
Over this space, the isometries form an orthogonal frame
torsor, locally trivial in the \'etale topology, with relative
dimension $\dim\mathrm O(V,b)=N(N-1)/2$.
The functional and the frame can be recovered from
$(X_\bullet,v)$ by the displayed formulas. The same
constructions use the tautological multiplication maps in
families and identify this frame torsor, after reduction,
with $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{AI}}$.
Its group action is free: an automorphism commuting with all
$X_i$ and fixing $v$ fixes every $f(X_\bullet)v$ and hence
all of $V$. Consequently,
\[
  \dim\mathcal U_{\mathrm{AI}}
  =\dim\mathcal H_{r,N}+N+\frac{N(N-1)}2.
\]
On the other hand, \cref{thm:universal} gives
\[
  \dim\mathcal U_{\mathrm{AI}}
  \le\left\lfloor\frac r2\dim\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{AI}}\right\rfloor+N.
\]
Subtracting proves \eqref{eq:gorenstein-bound}.
At a smooth point of an $r$-dimensional complex variety,
the completed local ring is $\mathbb{C}[[x_1,\ldots,x_r]]$.
Every local quotient of length $N$ is annihilated by the
$N$th power of the maximal ideal. The reduced punctual
length-$N$ loci therefore depend only on this finite
truncation, which gives the same estimate in formal local
coordinates.

\emph{Odd socle.} Let $A=R/I=A_+\oplus A_-$ be an invariant Gorenstein quotient with odd
socle, choose an odd Frobenius functional $\lambda$, and write
$b_\lambda(u,w)=\lambda(uw)$. Let $\varepsilon$ be the grading
involution. The form
\[
  \omega(u,w):=b_\lambda(\varepsilon u,w)
\]
is alternating and nondegenerate. Indeed, $\varepsilon$ is an algebra
automorphism and $\lambda\circ\varepsilon=-\lambda$, so
\[
  b_\lambda(\varepsilon u,w)
  =-\lambda\bigl(\varepsilon(\varepsilon(u)w)\bigr)
  =-b_\lambda(u,\varepsilon w),
\]
which proves skew-symmetry; composition with the invertible map
$\varepsilon$ preserves nondegeneracy.
Both $A_+$ and $A_-$ are isotropic of half the dimension,
so they are Lagrangian subspaces of dimension $n$.

Each coordinate multiplication $X_i$ is odd and $b_\lambda$-self-adjoint.
Therefore
\[
  \omega(X_i u,w)
  =-b_\lambda(X_i\varepsilon u,w)
  =-b_\lambda(\varepsilon u,X_i w)
  =-\omega(u,X_i w).
\]
Thus the $X_i$ lie in the odd part
$\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{CI}}$ of
\[
  \bigl(
    \mathfrak{sp}(A,\omega),
    \mathfrak{gl}(A_+)
  \bigr),
\]
the Hermitian pair of type $\mathrm{CI}$. Its positive and
negative grading pieces are
\[
  \mathfrak p_{\mathrm{CI}}^{+}
  =\mathfrak{sp}(A,\omega)\cap\operatorname{Hom}(A_-,A_+),
  \qquad
  \mathfrak p_{\mathrm{CI}}^{-}
  =\mathfrak{sp}(A,\omega)\cap\operatorname{Hom}(A_+,A_-),
\]
where each block is extended by zero on the other summand, and
\[
  \dim\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{CI}}^+
  =\frac{n(n+1)}2.
\]
Since $\omega(\varepsilon u,\varepsilon w)=-\omega(u,w)$, conjugation by
$\varepsilon$ preserves $\mathfrak{sp}(A,\omega)$ and is an involution
of it. Its fixed algebra consists of
the endomorphisms preserving the two Lagrangian summands;
the action on $A_+$ determines the negative dual action on
$A_-$. This identifies the fixed algebra with
$\mathfrak{gl}(A_+)$, as required for type $\mathrm{CI}$.

Conversely, on a fixed symplectic space $V=V_+\oplus V_-$
with $n$-dimensional Lagrangian summands, let
$\varepsilon|_{V_\pm}=\pm\mathrm{id}$. We now use the notation
\[
 \mathfrak p_{\mathrm{CI}}
 :=\{X\in\mathfrak{sp}(V,\omega):\varepsilon X=-X\varepsilon\}
\]
for the corresponding space on this fixed vector space and $\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{CI}}^{\pm}$ are defined as above, with $V_\pm$ in place of $A_\pm$.
Take a commuting nilpotent tuple in $\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{CI}}$
and an even cyclic vector $v\in V_+$.
The symmetric form
\[
  b(u,w)=\omega(\varepsilon u,w)
\]
makes every $X_i$ self-adjoint. Evaluation at $v$ identifies
$A=\mathbb{C}[X_1,\ldots,X_r]$ with $V$: an element annihilating $v$
annihilates its cyclic span and is therefore zero.
Equivalently, $A=R/I$ for the kernel of
$f\mapsto f(X_\bullet)v$.
This kernel is invariant under $\mu_2$ because the operators
are odd and $v$ is even. The functional
\[
  \lambda(a)=b(v,av)
\]
is Frobenius: self-adjointness gives $\lambda(aa')=b(av,a'v)$ for $a,a'\in A$, which
is nondegenerate under the identification $A\simeq V$. It is odd because
$b(V_+,V_+)=0$ and $v\in V_+$.

It follows that the even cyclic locus
\[
  \mathcal U_{\mathrm{odd}}
  =
  \left\{
  (X_\bullet,v)
  \in
  C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{CI}}))\times V_+
  \ \middle|\
  \mathbb{C}[X_1,\ldots,X_r]v=V
  \right\}
\]
parametrises these algebras with an odd Frobenius functional and a
graded symplectic frame, meaning a symplectic isomorphism
preserving the two character spaces. A change of frame on
$V_+$ determines the inverse dual change on $V_-$.
The frame group is therefore
$\mathrm{GL}(V_+)$, of dimension $n^2$, and the odd Frobenius
functionals form an open subset of $(A_-)^*$, of dimension $n$.
Hence
\[
  \dim\mathcal U_{\mathrm{odd}}
  =
  \dim\mathsf G^{\mathrm{odd}}_{r,2n}+n+n^2.
\]
By \cref{thm:hermitian}, the ambient commuting variety has
dimension $rn(n+1)/2$. Bounding the cyclic locus by its ambient
product and subtracting $n+n^2$ proves
\eqref{eq:odd-gorenstein-bound}.

\emph{Even socle.}
First, the even-socle locus with $m=1$ and $n\ge1$ is empty.
Indeed, its nonzero socle would equal $A_+=\mathbb{C}\cdot1$,
forcing $\mathfrak m_A=0$ and $A=\mathbb{C}$, contrary to $n\ge1$.
If $n=0$, every coordinate multiplication is zero, so the
only quotient algebra is $A=\mathbb{C}$, with $(m,n)=(1,0)$.
Thus every even-socle quotient of length greater than one
has $m\ge2$ and $n\ge1$, as in the statement.

We choose an even Frobenius functional and put
$b_\lambda(u,w)=\lambda(uw)$. Its character spaces are
orthogonal and separately nondegenerate.
We fix $V=V_+\oplus V_-$ with dimensions $m,n$ and a
nondegenerate symmetric form $b$ making the summands
orthogonal. A graded isometry $(A,b_\lambda)\simeq(V,b)$
transports the multiplication operators to $V$.
Let $\varepsilon|_{V_\pm}=\pm\mathrm{id}$.
For the coordinate multiplications $X_i$, set
\[
  Y_i=\varepsilon X_i.
\]
Oddness and self-adjointness of the $X_i$ imply
\[
  b(Y_i u,w)=-b(u,Y_i w),
  \qquad
  Y_iY_j=-X_iX_j.
\]
In particular, $Y_i^{2q}=(-1)^qX_i^{2q}$ for $q\ge1$,
so nilpotence is preserved. The $Y_i$ form a commuting nilpotent tuple in the
odd part
\[
 \mathfrak p_{\mathrm{BDI}}
 :=\{Y\in\mathfrak{so}(V,b):\varepsilon Y=-Y\varepsilon\}
\]
of the symmetric pair
\[
  \bigl(
    \mathfrak{so}(V,b),
    \mathfrak{so}(V_+)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(V_-)
  \bigr),
  \qquad
  \dim\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{BDI}}=mn.
\]

The transformation is inverted by $X_i=\varepsilon Y_i$.
For an even vector $v$ and $q\ge0$, we have
\[
 Y_{i_1}\cdots Y_{i_q}v
 =(-1)^{q(q+1)/2}X_{i_1}\cdots X_{i_q}v.
\]
Thus the two cyclic spans coincide.
Conversely, start with a commuting nilpotent tuple
$Y_\bullet$ in $\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{BDI}}$ and an even
cyclic vector $v$. The endomorphisms $X_i=\varepsilon Y_i$
are commuting, nilpotent, odd, and $b$-self-adjoint.
The kernel of $f\mapsto f(X_\bullet)v$ is an invariant
ideal $I$, and the resulting algebra $A=R/I$ is identified
with $V$.
The functional $\lambda(a)=b(v,av)$ is Frobenius by
self-adjointness and cyclicity. It is even because
$b(V_+,V_-)=0$, so the socle is even.

Thus the open locus
\[
 \mathcal U_{\mathrm{even}}
 :=\{(Y_\bullet,v)\in
       C_{r}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak p_{\mathrm{BDI}}))\times V_+:
       \mathbb{C}[Y_1,\ldots,Y_r]v=V\}
\]
parametrises the even-socle quotients with an even Frobenius
functional and a graded orthogonal frame.
The even Frobenius functionals form an open subset of
$(A_+)^*$, of dimension $m$. Over this functional space,
the graded orthogonal frames form a torsor under
$\mathrm O(V_+,b|_{V_+})\times\mathrm O(V_-,b|_{V_-})$,
locally trivial in the \'etale topology and of relative
dimension $\binom m2+\binom n2$.
The action is free by the cyclicity argument used above.
Therefore
\[
  \dim\mathcal U_{\mathrm{even}}
  =
  \dim\mathsf G^{\mathrm{even}}_{r,(m,n)}
  +m+\binom m2+\binom n2.
\]
We now apply \cref{thm:universal} to the ambient commuting
variety and subtract the last three terms, obtaining
\eqref{eq:even-gorenstein-bound}.
Here $m+n\geq3$, so the orthogonal Lie algebra is semisimple;
the case $m+n=4$ is covered by the semisimple form of that theorem.

\label{prop:gorenstein-sharp}
Let $r\ge2$. The following statements hold:

\item $\mathcal H_{r,2}=\mathsf G^{\mathrm{odd}}_{r,2}\cong\mathbb P^{r-1}$;

\cref{cor:odd-socle-strict} is sharp at $r=3$.
\item $\mathsf G^{\mathrm{even}}_{r,(m,2)}=\varnothing$ for $m\ge4$.

(i) A punctual quotient $A$ of length two has $\dim\mathfrak m_A=1$, so
$\mathfrak m_A^2=0$ by Nakayama's lemma. Hence $\mathfrak m^2\subseteq I$,
and $I$ is determined by the one-dimensional quotient
$\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\to\mathfrak m_A$; these quotients form
$\mathbb P^{r-1}$. Such an ideal is homogeneous, hence invariant, and $A$
is Gorenstein with socle $\mathfrak m_A$, which is odd. Thus
$\mathcal H_{r,2}=\mathsf G^{\mathrm{odd}}_{r,2}\cong\mathbb P^{r-1}$,
and both bounds in question equal $r-1$.

(ii) Let $x_i$ act on $\mathbb{C}[y_1,\ldots,y_r]$ as $\partial/\partial y_i$.
A quadratic form $Q$ of rank $n$ has apolar ideal
$\operatorname{Ann}(Q)
=\{f\in R:f(\partial_{y_1},\ldots,\partial_{y_r})Q=0\}$.
It is homogeneous, hence invariant, and $A=R/\operatorname{Ann}(Q)$ is
Gorenstein with Hilbert function $(1,n,1)$, that is,
$\dim\mathfrak m_A^i/\mathfrak m_A^{i+1}=1,n,1$ for $i=0,1,2$
\cite{IarrobinoKanev}. Its character multiplicities are $(2,n)$, and its
socle $\mathfrak m_A^2$ is even. The line $\mathbb{C} Q$ is recovered from the
degree-two part of $\operatorname{Ann}(Q)$, so distinct projective forms
give distinct ideals. Since the differentiation maps have constant rank
on the rank-$n$ locus, the apolar quotients form an algebraic family of
constant length $n+2$. A rank-$n$ form is a nondegenerate form in the
variables of a unique $n$-dimensional subspace of
$\langle y_1,\ldots,y_r\rangle$, so the projective rank-$n$ forms have
dimension $n(r-n)+\binom{n+1}2-1=rn-\binom n2-1$. This is the value of
\eqref{eq:even-gorenstein-bound} for $m=2$.

(iii) Let $A$ be an invariant Gorenstein quotient with even socle and
character multiplicities $(3,2)$; then $e(A)\le\dim A_-=2$. Suppose
$e(A)=2$. Then $A_-\to\mathfrak m_A/\mathfrak m_A^2$ is an isomorphism,
so $A_-\cap\mathfrak m_A^2=0$. Every monomial of degree three in the
$x_i$ is odd and lies in $\mathfrak m_A^2$, hence vanishes. Thus
$\mathfrak m_A^3=0$ and $\mathfrak m_A^2\subseteq\operatorname{soc}(A)$,
so $\dim A\le1+2+1=4$, a contradiction. Hence $e(A)=1$, and an odd lift
$t$ of a generator of $\mathfrak m_A/\mathfrak m_A^2$ gives
$A\simeq\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^5)$ with $t$ odd. Conversely, this algebra has
character multiplicities $(3,2)$ and even socle $\mathbb{C} t^4$.

Equivariant surjections $R\to\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^5)$ are given by
$x_i\mapsto\alpha_it+\beta_it^3$ with $(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r)\ne0$.
They form an open subset of $\mathbb A^{2r}$, which carries a flat family
of quotients and hence a morphism to the locus, surjective by the
preceding paragraph. Two surjections have the same kernel exactly when
they differ by a parity-preserving automorphism $t\mapsto ct+dt^3$ with
$c\ne0$, and this two-dimensional group acts freely because the maps are
surjective. The fibre-dimension theorem gives
$\dim\mathsf G^{\mathrm{even}}_{r,(3,2)}=2r-2$, which is less than the
value $3r-4$ of \eqref{eq:even-gorenstein-bound} when $r\ge3$.

(iv) Let $A$ be an invariant Gorenstein quotient of length four with odd
socle, so with character multiplicities $(2,2)$. If $e(A)=2$, then $A$
has Hilbert function $(1,2,1)$, and
$\operatorname{soc}(A)=\mathfrak m_A^2$ is spanned by the images of the
even monomials $x_ix_j$, a contradiction. Hence $A\simeq\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^4)$
with $t$ odd, and the parametrisation of (iii), taken modulo $t^4$,
gives $\dim\mathsf G^{\mathrm{odd}}_{r,4}=2r-2$. For $r=3$ this equals
the bound $4$ of \cref{cor:odd-socle-strict}, whereas
\eqref{eq:odd-gorenstein-bound} gives $5$.

(v) Let $A$ be an invariant Gorenstein quotient of length $N$ with
$\dim A_-=2$. If $e(A)=2$, the argument of (iii) gives $N\le4$. If
$e(A)\le1$, then $A\simeq\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^N)$ with $t$ odd, whose odd part has
dimension $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$; hence $N\le5$. In either case
$m=N-2\le3$.

\label{rem:scheme-theoretic-applications}
The cyclic locus can avoid all maximal-dimensional matrix
components, as in \cref{cor:odd-socle-strict}.
Consequently, the Gorenstein bounds need not be equalities.
These arguments concern dimensions and components of reductions;
they do not establish reducedness or local smoothness of the
original moduli schemes.

\label{rem:applications-fields}
The results of this section hold over every algebraically closed field

defined over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$: the punctual moduli spaces, their
cyclic loci, and the frame bundles.
