<!-- head-1: body_md -->
For a given query, a pool of $n$ items has unknown binary relevance
$g\in\{0,1\}^n$. A fitted predictor provides scores $m\in[0,1]^n$,
and the residual vector $r=g-m$ records its prediction errors.
We observe the relevance of $B$ items and use their residuals to
update predictions for the remaining items. We evaluate both
prediction error and ranking quality. The covariance we seek to
estimate is
\begin{equation}
   \Sigma^{\star}
   = \operatorname{Cov}(r)
   = \mathbb{E}[rr^\top]-\mathbb{E}[r]\mathbb{E}[r]^\top .
   \label{eq:sigdef}
\end{equation}
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:delta}
Let $r$ satisfy $\mathbb{E}[r]=0$, with covariance
$\Sigma^{\star}=\mathbb{E}[rr^\top]$. Let $S$ be a set of nodes fixed before
observing the residuals, and let $U$ be its complement.
Suppose the observed residuals are
$z_S=r_S+\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon$ is independent
of $r$, $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon]=0$, and
$\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon)=\sigma^2I$.
Write $V_S=\Sigma^{\star}_{SS}+\sigma^2I\succ0$.

For any fixed matrix $C$, consider the linear update
$\hat r_U=Cz_S$. Its reduction in total expected squared
prediction error is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
   \Delta(C;S)
   &:= \mathbb{E}\bigl[\|r_U\|_2^2\bigr]
       -\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|r_U-Cz_S\|_2^2\bigr]\\
   &=2\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(C\Sigma^{\star}_{SU}\bigr)
     -\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(CV_SC^\top\bigr).
\end{split}
   \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
The unique maximiser is $C^\star=\Sigma^{\star}_{US}V_S^{-1}$.
For every $C$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
   \Delta(C^\star;S)-\Delta(C;S)
   &=\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl[
      (C-C^\star)V_S(C-C^\star)^\top
      \bigr]\\
   &=\bigl\|(C-C^\star)V_S^{1/2}\bigr\|_F^2 .
\end{split}
   \label{eq:quad}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:scalar}
For a single revealed node $a$ and a single target node $i$,
write $c=C_{ia}$ and $V=\Sigma^{\star}_{aa}+\sigma^2>0$.
The reduction in expected squared error is
\[
   \Delta_i(c)=2c\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}-Vc^2.
\]
It is maximised at $c^\star=\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}/V$, and
\[
   \Delta_i(c^\star)-\Delta_i(c)
   =V(c-c^\star)^2.
\]
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:hurt}
For a single revealed node $a$ and target node $i$, suppose
$\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}=0$ and $V=\Sigma^{\star}_{aa}+\sigma^2>0$.
Then every nonzero propagation coefficient $c$ gives
\[
   \Delta_i(c)=-Vc^2<0.
\]
Thus, any nonzero linear adjustment increases expected squared
error when the true residual cross-covariance is zero.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:rigid}
Let $L$ be the combinatorial Laplacian of an undirected graph
with nonnegative edge weights. For $\lambda>0$, define
$K=(I+\lambda L)^{-1}$. Let $S$ be the revealed set and
$U$ its complement. The linear update based on $K$ for
exact observations has coefficient matrix
\[
   C(K)=K_{US}K_{SS}^{-1}.
\]
Every entry $c_{ij}$ of $C(K)$ is nonnegative.

For a single reveal $S=\{j\}$ and a target node $i$, if
$c_{ij}>0$ and $\Sigma^{\star}_{ij}<0$, then
Corollary~\ref{cor:scalar} gives $\Delta_i(c_{ij})<0$.
Thus, for every $\lambda>0$, a nonzero update of this form
increases expected squared error when the true residual
covariance between the two nodes is negative.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:linear}
For a fixed coefficient $c$, the single-reveal gain satisfies
\[
   \mathbb{E}[\Delta_i(c)\mid\mathcal{D}]
   =2c\,\mathbb{E}[\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}\mid\mathcal{D}]
    -c^2\bigl(\mathbb{E}[\Sigma^{\star}_{aa}\mid\mathcal{D}]+\sigma^2\bigr).
\]
The same property holds for a fixed matrix $C$ in
\eqref{eq:delta}. Holding the posterior mean covariance
fixed, changing posterior dispersion does not change the
expected gain. Uncertainty affects this decision through
the posterior mean, without a separate dispersion penalty.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:cb}
Conditional on $\mathcal{D}$ and $\Sigma^{\star}$, suppose the new residuals
have mean zero and covariance $\Sigma^{\star}$, with the independent
observation noise of Theorem~\ref{thm:delta}.
Assume finite posterior mean covariance and a reveal set
fixed before observing the new residuals.

For a single reveal, write
$\bar V=\mathbb{E}[\Sigma^{\star}_{aa}\mid\mathcal{D}]+\sigma^2>0$.
The coefficient maximising posterior expected gain is
\begin{equation}
   c^B_{ia}
   =\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}\mid\mathcal{D}]}
          {\mathbb{E}[\Sigma^{\star}_{aa}\mid\mathcal{D}]+\sigma^2},
   \label{eq:cb}
\end{equation}
and, for every coefficient $c$ chosen before observing $z_a$,
\[
   \mathbb{E}[\Delta_i(c^B_{ia})-\Delta_i(c)\mid\mathcal{D}]
   =\bar V(c-c^B_{ia})^2.
\]
For multiple reveals, let
$\bar V_S=\mathbb{E}[\Sigma^{\star}_{SS}\mid\mathcal{D}]+\sigma^2I\succ0$.
Then
\[
   C^B=\mathbb{E}[\Sigma^{\star}_{US}\mid\mathcal{D}]\,\bar V_S^{-1},
\]
with
\[
   \mathbb{E}[\Delta(C^B;S)-\Delta(C;S)\mid\mathcal{D}]
   =\bigl\|(C-C^B)\bar V_S^{1/2}\bigr\|_F^2
\]
for every coefficient matrix $C$ chosen before observing $z_S$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:linscope}
The optimum is over rules linear in $z_S$, with coefficients
fixed before $z_S$ is observed. A full posterior predictive
calculation can also update model probabilities and parameter
distributions using $z_S$, producing a prediction that need
not be linear. Proposition~\ref{prop:cb} does not establish
optimality over that larger class. Updating family weights within a query changes them by at most $0.01$ over $B=20$ reveals
(supplement).
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
Let $S$ be revealed and $U$ unrevealed, with $S$ and the coefficient matrix $C$ fixed before the
residuals are observed, and $z_S=r_S+\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon\sim(0,\sigma^2I)$
independent of $r$, let $\mathbb{E}[r]=0$, and let $V_S=\Sigma^{\star}_{SS}+\sigma^2I\succ0$. For any
$C\in\mathbb{R}^{|U|\times|S|}$ and $\hat r_U=Cz_S$,
\[
   \Delta(C;S):=\mathbb{E}\|r_U\|^2-\mathbb{E}\|r_U-Cz_S\|^2
   =2\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(C\Sigma^{\star}_{SU}\bigr)-\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(CV_SC^\top\bigr),
\]
uniquely maximised at $C^\star=\Sigma^{\star}_{US}V_S^{-1}$, and for every $C$,
\[
   \Delta(C^\star;S)-\Delta(C;S)
   =\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl[(C-C^\star)V_S(C-C^\star)^\top\bigr]
   =\bigl\|(C-C^\star)V_S^{1/2}\bigr\|_F^2 .
\]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Expanding the square,
$\mathbb{E}\|r_U-Cz_S\|^2=\mathbb{E}\|r_U\|^2-2\,\mathbb{E}[r_U^\top Cz_S]+\mathbb{E}\|Cz_S\|^2$, so
$\Delta(C;S)=2\,\mathbb{E}[r_U^\top Cz_S]-\mathbb{E}\|Cz_S\|^2$. For the cross term, $r_U^\top Cz_S$ is a scalar, so it
equals its own trace and
\[
   \mathbb{E}[r_U^\top Cz_S]=\mathbb{E}\operatorname{tr}(Cz_Sr_U^\top)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(C\,\mathbb{E}[z_Sr_U^\top]\bigr)
   =\operatorname{tr}(C\Sigma^{\star}_{SU}),
\]
using $\mathbb{E}[z_Sr_U^\top]=\mathbb{E}[(r_S+\varepsilon)r_U^\top]=\Sigma^{\star}_{SU}$ because $\varepsilon\perp r$ and
$\mathbb{E}\varepsilon=0$. For the quadratic term,
$\mathbb{E}\|Cz_S\|^2=\mathbb{E}\operatorname{tr}(Cz_Sz_S^\top C^\top)=\operatorname{tr}(CV_SC^\top)$ with
$V_S=\mathbb{E}[z_Sz_S^\top]=\Sigma^{\star}_{SS}+\sigma^2I$. This gives the identity.

Since $V_S\succ0$, $C\mapsto-\operatorname{tr}(CV_SC^\top)$ is strictly concave
and the stationary point is the unique maximiser. Differentiating,
$\partial\Delta/\partial C=2{\Sigma^{\star}_{SU}}^{\!\top}-2CV_S=2\Sigma^{\star}_{US}-2CV_S$, which vanishes iff
$C=\Sigma^{\star}_{US}V_S^{-1}=C^\star$.

For the last claim, symmetry of $V_S$ gives ${\Sigma^{\star}_{SU}}={\Sigma^{\star}_{US}}^{\!\top}=(C^\star V_S)^\top=V_SC^{\star\top}$,
so $\operatorname{tr}(C\Sigma^{\star}_{SU})=\operatorname{tr}(CV_SC^{\star\top})$ and
\[
   \Delta(C;S)=2\operatorname{tr}(CV_SC^{\star\top})-\operatorname{tr}(CV_SC^\top),
   \qquad
   \Delta(C^\star;S)=\operatorname{tr}(C^\star V_SC^{\star\top}).
\]
Subtracting,
$\Delta(C^\star;S)-\Delta(C;S)
=\operatorname{tr}(C^\star V_SC^{\star\top})-2\operatorname{tr}(CV_SC^{\star\top})+\operatorname{tr}(CV_SC^\top)
=\operatorname{tr}[(C-C^\star)V_S(C-C^\star)^\top]$,
which is $\|(C-C^\star)V_S^{1/2}\|_F^2$ since $V_S\succ0$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The proof uses $\mathbb{E}[z_Sr_U^\top]=\Sigma^{\star}_{SU}$, which requires $\mathbb{E}[r]=0$. This is also what makes
$\Sigma^{\star}=\mathbb{E}[rr^\top]$ a covariance rather than a second moment. Cross-fitting does not deliver it, since
it prevents the mean from being fitted on the labels it is scored against but does not make it the
conditional mean. In the experiments the bias is estimated on training queries and applied as a
recalibration to held-out ones, which uses no labels from the query being scored.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
With $S=\{a\}$, $U=\{i\}$, $c=C_{ia}$ and $V=\Sigma^{\star}_{aa}+\sigma^2$, the identity reads
$\Delta_i(c)=2c\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}-Vc^2$, maximised at $c^\star=\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}/V$, with
$\Delta_i(c^\star)-\Delta_i(c)=V(c-c^\star)^2$. If $\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}=0$ but $c\neq0$ then
$\Delta_i(c)=-Vc^2<0$.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
Let $L$ be the combinatorial Laplacian of an undirected graph with nonnegative edge weights, let
$\lambda>0$, put
$K=(I+\lambda L)^{-1}$, and let $c_{ij}$ denote the entries of $C(K)=K_{US}K_{SS}^{-1}$. Then
$c_{ij}\ge0$ for all $i,j$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Write $A=I+\lambda L$. Its off-diagonal entries are $-\lambda A^{\mathrm{adj}}_{ij}\le0$, its diagonal
entries are $1+\lambda d_i>0$, and $A_{ii}-\sum_{j\ne i}|A_{ij}|=1>0$, so $A$ is a nonsingular symmetric
$M$-matrix and $K=A^{-1}\ge0$ entrywise.

Non-negativity of $K$ is not by itself enough. A nonnegative positive-semidefinite kernel can induce coefficients of either sign. For
$K=\begin{pmatrix}1&0.5&0.1\\0.5&1&0.5\\0.1&0.5&1\end{pmatrix}$, which is positive definite and entrywise
positive, taking $S=\{1,2\}$ and $U=\{3\}$ gives $K_{US}K_{SS}^{-1}=(-0.2,\;0.6)$. What gives the
conclusion is the $M$-matrix structure of the precision. Partition $A$ by the unrevealed and revealed sets.
For a Gaussian with precision $A$ the conditional mean of $r_U$ given $r_S$ is $-A_{UU}^{-1}A_{US}r_S$, and
comparing it with $K_{US}K_{SS}^{-1}r_S$ gives
\[
   C(K)\;=\;K_{US}K_{SS}^{-1}\;=\;-A_{UU}^{-1}A_{US}.
\]
Principal submatrices of a nonsingular $M$-matrix are nonsingular $M$-matrices, so $A_{UU}^{-1}\ge0$
entrywise, while $A_{US}\le0$ entrywise. The product is therefore entrywise nonnegative.

The same holds with the soft-update nugget, where the coefficient is $K_{US}(K_{SS}+\sigma^2I)^{-1}$. This is
still the conditional mean under the working model with noisy observations: writing $Q=K^{-1}$ and letting
$E_S$ select the revealed coordinates, the update matrix is
$(Q+\sigma^{-2}E_SE_S^\top)^{-1}\sigma^{-2}E_S$, whose rows on $U$ coincide with
$K_{US}(K_{SS}+\sigma^2I)^{-1}$. Adding a nonnegative diagonal to $Q$ preserves the $M$-matrix property, so
this matrix is entrywise nonnegative as well. The argument is specific to the Laplacian kernel and does not
extend automatically to every family in the library.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
Only non-negativity is claimed. On irregular graphs the entries of $K$ need not decay monotonically in graph
distance, and the impossibility argument in the main text does not need them to. If $c_{ia}>0$ and $\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}<0$ then $\Delta_i<0$ for a single reveal by the Corollary. At $c_{ia}=0$ the
gain is zero.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
Under the assumptions of Theorem~1 of the main paper, with a finite posterior mean covariance and a
reveal set fixed in advance, let $\bar V=\mathbb{E}[\Sigma^{\star}_{aa}\mid\mathcal{D}]+\sigma^2>0$. Then
$\mathbb{E}[\Delta_i(c)\mid\mathcal{D}]$ is maximised over $c\in\mathbb{R}$ at
$c^{B}=\mathbb{E}[\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}\mid\mathcal{D}]/\bar V$, and $\mathbb{E}[\Delta_i(c^{B})-\Delta_i(c)\mid\mathcal{D}]=\bar V(c-c^{B})^2$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
$\Delta_i(c)=2c\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}-c^2(\Sigma^{\star}_{aa}+\sigma^2)$ is affine in $\Sigma^{\star}$ for fixed $c$, so
$\mathbb{E}[\Delta_i(c)\mid\mathcal{D}]=2c\,\mathbb{E}[\Sigma^{\star}_{ia}\mid\mathcal{D}]-c^2\bar V$. This is the scalar identity with $\Sigma^{\star}$
replaced by its posterior mean, and the Corollary applies verbatim. The matrix statement follows the
same way from the Theorem, with $\bar V_S=\mathbb{E}[\Sigma^{\star}_{SS}\mid\mathcal{D}]+\sigma^2I$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
The optimum is over rules linear in $z_S$ whose coefficient is fixed before $z_S$ is observed. Under
model uncertainty a fully Bayesian predictor may update $\mathbb{P}(M_m\mid\mathcal{D},z_S)$ and induce an effective
coefficient depending on the realised $z_S$. The proposition makes no claim against that class.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
With $D=C-C^\star$, $u_i=D_iV_S^{1/2}$, $p_{ij}=(D_i-D_j)z_S$, $g_{ij}=|\mu_i(C^\star)-\mu_j(C^\star)|$ and
$\sigma_{ij}^2=\|u_i-u_j\|^2$, we have $\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}^2\le|U|\,\mathcal R(C)$, and for every constant
$t>0$,
\[
   \mathbb{E}\,\#\{\text{misordered}\}\;\le\;\mathbb{E}\,\#\{g_{ij}<t\}\;+\;\sum_{i<j}2\Phi(-t/\sigma_{ij})
\]
if $z_S$ is Gaussian, and without that assumption the same bound holds with the last term replaced by
$|U|\,\mathcal R(C)/t^2$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
The proposition controls pairwise disagreement with the oracle \emph{linear score}. It is not a recall@$k$ guarantee against true labels. Such a guarantee would also require assumptions
linking the oracle scores to relevance, which we do not make.
