<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{P}=\{P_1,P_2,\ldots,\\P_n\}$ denote the set of all participants. The secret space is denoted by $\mathcal{S}$, while $\mathcal{S}_\theta$ stands for the share space of the $\theta$-th participant. A secret sharing scheme of $n$ participants generally comprises two procedures: share distribution and secret reconstruction.
	\begin{enumerate}     
	  \item Share Distribution Phase: For any secret drawn from $\mathcal{S}$ and a certain random input selected from $\mathcal{R}$, the dealer invokes the share generation algorithm
		\[\mathsf{SHAR}\colon \mathcal{S}\times \mathcal{R}\to \mathcal{S}_1\times \mathcal{S}_2\times\dotsb\times \mathcal{S}_n\]
		to allocate shares among the participants in $\mathcal{P}$.
	  \item Secret Reconstruction Phase: Any authorized subset $\mathcal{A}\subseteq \mathcal{P}$ is capable of reconstructing the secret through the secret reconstruction algorithm
		\[\mathsf{RECO}\colon\prod_{P_\theta\in \mathcal{A}} \mathcal{S}_\theta\to \mathcal{S},\]
		whereas any unauthorized subset is not.
	\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def: CSS}
Let $\mathcal{P}=\{P_1,P_2,\ldots,P_n\}$ be the set of all participants, partitioned by the dealer into $q$ disjoint compartments $\gamma_1, \gamma_2, \ldots, \gamma_q$, i.e.,
                       \[\mathcal{P}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{q} \gamma_i,~\mathrm{and}~\gamma_i\cap\gamma_j=\emptyset~\mathrm{for~all}~1\leq i<j\leq q.\]
Denote by $|\cdot|$ the cardinality of a set. Let $t_1, t_2, \ldots, t_q,t$ be positive integers such that
                      \[t_i\leq |\gamma_i|=n_i (1\le i\le q),~\mathrm{and}~\sum_{i=1}^q t_i\leq t\leq n.\]
The compartmented access structure with lower bounds (LCAS) is
                 \[\Gamma_L=\{\mathcal{A} \subseteq \mathcal{P}: \forall 1\leq i\leq q,\ |\mathcal{A}\cap \gamma_i|\geq t_i~\mathrm{and}~|\mathcal{A}|\geq t\}.\]
A Compartmented Secret Sharing Scheme (CSSS) for the LCAS with perfect security is a secret sharing scheme satisfying the following correctness and privacy requirements.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Correctness. The secret can be reconstructed from any authorized subset $\mathcal{A}\in\Gamma_L$.
\item Privacy. No information about the secret can be obtained from any subset $\mathcal{B}\notin \Gamma_L$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{le: CRT}
Let $p$ be a prime, $\mathbb{F}_p$ the finite field with $p$ elements, and $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$ the univariate polynomial ring over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Let $m_1(x),m_2(x),\ldots,m_k(x)\in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ be pairwise coprime, let $M(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_i(x)$, $M_i(x)=M(x)/m_i(x)$, and $\lambda_i(x)\equiv M_i^{-1}(x)\pmod{m_i(x)}$. Then, for any $f_1(x),\ldots,f_k(x)\in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$, the system of congruences $f(x)\equiv f_i(x)\pmod{m_i(x)}, i\in[k]$, has the solution
               \[f(x)\equiv \sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_i(x)M_i(x)f_i(x)\pmod{M(x)}.\]
If $\deg(f(x))<\deg(M(x))$, then the solution is unique, and we denote it by
               \[f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_i(x)M_i(x)f_i(x)\bmod{M(x)}.\]

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lemma:1}
Let $\mathcal{X}\subseteq [n]$, $u\in [q]$ and let $k$ be a positive integer. Then the following properties hold in the scheme of \cite{YangCSS2026}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
   \item $\deg(s'_u(x))<T_u$ and $\deg(s'_{q+1}(x))<T$.
   \item $D_{\mathcal{X}}\geq \sum_{\theta=1}^{k}d_{\theta}$ if $|\mathcal{X}|\geq k$.
   \item $T\geq T_u$.
   \item $D_{\gamma_u}\geq T_u$.
   \item $s(x)=\big(\sum_{u=1}^{q}s'_u(x)+s'_{q+1}(x)\big)\bmod{m_0(x)}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
(i) Recall that $s'_u(x)=s_u(x)+\alpha'_u(x)x^{d_0} (u\in [q])$ and $s'_{q+1}(x)=s_{q+1}(x)+\alpha'_{q+1}(x)x^{d_0}$,
   where $s_u(x)\in \mathcal{S}=\{g(x)\in \mathbb{F}_p[x]: \deg(g(x))<d_0\}$, $\alpha'_u(x)\in \mathcal{G}'_u=\{g(x)\in \mathbb{F}_p[x]: \deg(g(x))<(\sum_{\theta^{(u)}=1}^{t_u}d_{\theta^{(u)}})-d_0=T_u-d_0\}$ for $u\in [q]$, $s_{q+1}(x)=(s(x)-s_1(x)-\cdots-s_q(x))\bmod{m_0(x)}$, and $\alpha'_{q+1}(x)\in\mathcal{G}'_{q+1}=\{g(x)\in \mathbb{F}_p[x]: \deg(g(x))<(\sum_{\theta=1}^{t}d_{\theta})-d_0=T-d_0\}$. Consequently, $\deg(s'_u(x))\leq\deg(\alpha^{\prime}_u(x))+d_0<\sum_{\theta^{(u)}=1}^{t_u}d_{\theta^{(u)}}=T_u$ for $u\in [q]$, and $\deg(s'_{q+1}(x))\leq\deg(\alpha^{\prime}_{q+1}(x))+d_0<\sum_{\theta=1}^{t}d_{\theta}=T$.

(ii) Since $d_1\leq d_2\leq \cdots \leq d_n$, every $\mathcal{X}\subseteq [n]$ satisfies $D_{\mathcal{X}}\geq \sum_{\theta=1}^{|\mathcal{X}|}d_{\theta}$. Therefore, $D_{\mathcal{X}}\geq \sum_{\theta=1}^{k}d_{\theta}$ if $|\mathcal{X}|\geq k$.

(iii) Since $t\geq \sum_{i=1}^{q}t_i\geq t_u$ for $u\in [q]$, then
                \[T=\sum\limits_{\theta=1}^{t}d_{\theta}\geq \sum\limits_{\theta=1}^{t_u}d_{\theta}=T_u.\]
(iv)Since $|\gamma_u|=n_u\geq t_u$, then $D_{\gamma_u}\geq T_u$ by the above item (ii).

(v) Recall that $m_0(x)=x^{d_0}$, $s_u(x)=s'_u(x)\bmod{m_0(x)}$ and $s_{q+1}(x)=\big(s(x)-\sum_{u=1}^{q}s_u(x)\big)\bmod{m_0(x)}$. Therefore,
       \begin{displaymath}
         \begin{aligned}
         s(x)=&\left(\sum_{u=1}^{q}s_u(x)+s_{q+1}(x)\right)\bmod{m_0(x)}\\
           =&\left(\sum_{u=1}^{q}s^{\prime}_u(x)+s^{\prime}_{q+1}(x)\right)\bmod{m_0(x)}.
       \end{aligned}
       \end{displaymath}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{le: leakage}
Denote by $g_u(x)=s'_{q+1}(x)-s'_u(x)$ for $u\in [q]$, then the following properties hold in the scheme of \cite{YangCSS2026}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
   \item $\deg(g_u(x))<T$ and $g_u(x)\equiv \omega_{u,v}(x)\pmod{m_{u,v}(x)}$ for $v\in [n_u]$.
   \item Every participant $P_{\theta}=P_{u,v}$ can compute
        \begin{displaymath}
         \begin{aligned}
         c_{\theta}(x)&=(s_{u,v}(x)+\omega_{u,v}(x))\bmod{m_{\theta}(x)}\\
                      &=s'_{q+1}(x)\bmod{m_{\theta}(x)}.
          \end{aligned}
       \end{displaymath}
   \item If $n_u\geq t$ for some $u\in [q]$, then $g_u(x)$ is computable from the public polynomials alone.
   \item If $s'_{q+1}(x)$ is known, then every compartment secret polynomial $s'_u(x)$, and hence the secret $s(x)$, is reconstructed from the public polynomials and $s'_{q+1}(x)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
(i) According to Lemma \ref{lemma:1} item (i) and item (iii), we have $\deg(s'_u(x))<T_u$, $\deg(s'_{q+1}(x))<T$, and $T\geq T_u$. Consequently, $\deg(g_u(x))<T$. Recall that $s_{u,v}(x)=s'_u(x)\bmod{m_{u,v}(x)}$ for $v\in [n_u]$, hence
        \begin{displaymath}
         \begin{aligned}
              \omega_{u,v}(x)&=(s'_{q+1}(x)-s_{u,v}(x))\bmod{m_{u,v}(x)}\\
              &=(s'_{q+1}(x)-s'_u(x))\bmod{m_{u,v}(x)}\\
              &=g_u(x)\bmod{m_{u,v}(x)}.
         \end{aligned}
        \end{displaymath}
Namely, $g_u(x)\equiv \omega_{u,v}(x)\pmod{m_{u,v}(x)}$ for $v\in [n_u]$.

(ii) Since $s_{u,v}(x)=s'_u(x)\bmod{m_{u,v}(x)}$ for $v\in [n_u]$, then by the above item (i) we have
        \begin{displaymath}
         \begin{aligned}
         c_{\theta}(x)&=(s_{u,v}(x)+\omega_{u,v}(x))\bmod{m_{\theta}(x)}\\
                      &=(s^{\prime}_{u}(x)+g_u(x))\bmod{m_{\theta}(x)}\\
                      &=s'_{q+1}(x)\bmod{m_{\theta}(x)}.
          \end{aligned}
        \end{displaymath}

(iii) If $n_u\geq t$ for some $u\in [q]$, then $D_{\gamma_u}\geq \sum_{\theta=1}^{t}d_{\theta}=T$ by Lemma \ref{lemma:1} item (ii). According to the above item (i), we have $\deg(g_u(x))<T\leq D_{\gamma_u}$ and $g_u(x)\equiv \omega_{u,v}(x)\pmod{m_{u,v}(x)}$ for $v\in [n_u]$. Therefore, $g_u(x)$ is computable from the public polynomials alone by Lemma \ref{le: CRT}.

(iv) From the above item (i) we have $g_u(x)\equiv \omega_{u,v}(x)\pmod{m_{u,v}(x)}$ for $v\in [n_u]$. This shows that
               \[s'_u(x)\equiv (s'_{q+1}(x)-\omega_{u,v}(x)) \pmod{m_{u,v}(x)} ~\mathrm{for}~v\in [n_u].\]
By Lemma \ref{lemma:1} item (i) and item (iv), it holds that $\deg(s'_u(x))<T_u\leq D_{\gamma_u}$. Therefore, compartment secret polynomials $s'_u(x), u\in [q]$ are recovered by Lemma \ref{le: CRT} if $s'_{q+1}(x)$ is known. Consequently, the secret $s(x)$ is reconstructed by Lemma \ref{lemma:1} item (v).

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{Th: attack}
In the scheme of \cite{YangCSS2026}, the participants in $\mathcal{B}$ can reconstruct the secret $s(x)$ if one of the following conditions holds, where $\mathcal{B}\subseteq [n]$ and $\mathcal{B}^{(u)}=\mathcal{B}\cap \gamma_u$ for $u\in [q]$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
   \item $|\mathcal{B}|\geq t$;
   \item $|\mathcal{B}^{(u)}|\geq t_u$ and $|\mathcal{B}\setminus \gamma_u|+n_u\geq t$ for some $u\in [q]$;
   \item $n_u\geq t$ and $|\mathcal{B}|\geq t_u$ for some $u\in [q]$.
\end{enumerate}
Note that every $\mathcal{B}\in \Gamma_L$ satisfies the conditions (i) and (ii), whereas each of the three conditions may also be satisfied by some unauthorized subset $\mathcal{B}\notin \Gamma_L$ (see Corollary \ref{Cor: witnesses}). Such an unauthorized subset can reconstruct the secret, although Yang, Zhu, Fu and Xia \cite{YangCSS2026} claim that no unauthorized subset obtains any information about the secret.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Recall that $g_u(x)=s'_{q+1}(x)-s'_u(x)$ and let $c_{\theta}(x)$ be as in Lemma \ref{le: leakage} item (ii). According to Lemma \ref{le: leakage} item (iv),  it suffices to prove that the global secret polynomial $s^{\prime}_{q+1}(x)$ can be recovered from any one of the conditions.

\emph{Case} (i). By Lemma \ref{le: leakage} item (ii), the participants in $\mathcal{B}$ establish the system of congruences
\[s'_{q+1}(x)\equiv c_{\theta}(x) \pmod{m_{\theta}(x)}, \theta\in \mathcal{B}.\]
Since $|\mathcal{B}|\geq t$, from Lemma \ref{lemma:1} item (i) and item (ii), we have
                 \[\deg(s'_{q+1}(x))<T=\sum\limits_{\theta=1}^{t}d_{\theta}\leq D_{\mathcal{B}}.\]
Therefore, $s'_{q+1}(x)$ is recovered by Lemma \ref{le: CRT}.

\emph{Case} (ii). For a given $u\in [q]$, the participants in $\mathcal{B}^{(u)}$ establish the system of congruences
          \[s_{u,v}(x)=s'_u(x)\bmod{m_{u,v}(x)}~\mathrm{for}~P_{u,v}\in \mathcal{B}^{(u)}.\]
Since $|\mathcal{B}^{(u)}|\geq t_u$, from Lemma \ref{lemma:1} item (i) and item (ii), we have
               \[\deg(s'_{u}(x))<T_u=\sum\limits_{\theta=1}^{t_u}d_{\theta}\leq D_{\mathcal{B}^{(u)}}.\]
Therefore, $s'_{u}(x)$ is recovered by Lemma \ref{le: CRT}. Based on this, the set $\mathcal{B}$ can determine $g_u(x)\equiv \omega_{u,v}(x) \!\!\pmod{m_{u,v}(x)}~(v\in [n_u])$ and $g_u(x)\equiv (c_{\theta}(x)-s'_u(x)) \pmod{m_{\theta}(x)}~(\theta\in \mathcal{B}\setminus \gamma_u)$,
where the first family of congruences is publicly known by Lemma \ref{le: leakage} item (i), and the second family uses $c_{\theta}(x), \theta\in \mathcal{B}\setminus \gamma_u$ in Lemma \ref{le: leakage} item (ii) and the recovered $s'_u(x)$. Since $\gamma_u$ and $\mathcal{B}\setminus \gamma_u$ are disjoint and $|\mathcal{B}\setminus \gamma_u|+n_u\geq t$, these $n_u+|\mathcal{B}\setminus \gamma_u|$ pairwise coprime moduli have total degree, by Lemma \ref{lemma:1} item (ii),
                      \[D_{\gamma_u}+D_{\mathcal{B}\setminus \gamma_u}\geq \sum\limits_{\theta=1}^{t}d_{\theta}=T>\deg(g_u(x)).\]
Consequently, the polynomial $g_u(x)$ is recovered by Lemma \ref{le: CRT}, and hence $s'_{q+1}(x)=s'_u(x)+g_u(x)$.

\emph{Case} (iii). Since $n_u\geq t$ for some $u\in [q]$, the polynomial $g_u(x)$ is publicly known by Lemma \ref{le: leakage} item (iii). Recall that $g_u(x)=s'_{q+1}(x)-s'_u(x)$. Hence every $\theta\in \mathcal{B}$, \emph{of whatever compartment}, produces a residue of $s'_u(x)$ by Lemma \ref{le: leakage} item (ii), namely
      \begin{displaymath}
         \begin{aligned}
              s'_u(x)&\equiv (s^{\prime}_{q+1}(x)-g_u(x)) \pmod{m_{\theta}(x)}\\
                     &\equiv (c_{\theta}(x)-g_u(x)) \pmod{m_{\theta}(x)}, \theta\in \mathcal{B}.\\
         \end{aligned}
        \end{displaymath}
Since $|\mathcal{B}|\geq t_u$, we have $\deg(s'_u(x))<T_u\leq D_{\mathcal{B}}$ by Lemma \ref{lemma:1} item (i) and (ii). Therefore, the compartment secret polynomial $s'_u(x)$ is recovered, and hence $s'_{q+1}(x)=s'_u(x)+g_u(x)$.

Finally, let $\mathcal{B}\in \Gamma_L$. Then $|\mathcal{B}|\geq t$, so the condition (i) holds. Moreover, for every $u\in [q]$, $|\mathcal{B}^{(u)}|\geq t_u$ together with
         \[|\mathcal{B}\setminus \gamma_u|+n_u\geq |\mathcal{B}\setminus \gamma_u|+|\mathcal{B}^{(u)}|=|\mathcal{B}|\geq t\]
shows that the condition (ii) holds as well.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{Cor: witnesses}
Each of the three conditions of Theorem \ref{Th: attack} is satisfied by some unauthorized subset, in the following sense.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If $n-n_u+t_u-1\geq t$ for some $u\in [q]$, then the subset consisting of all the participants outside $\gamma_u$ together with $t_u-1$ participants of $\gamma_u$ is unauthorized and satisfies Theorem \ref{Th: attack} item (i).
  \item If $t_u<n_u$ for some $u\in [q]$, then the subset consisting of $t_u$ participants of $\gamma_u$ together with $\max\{0,t-n_u\}$ participants outside $\gamma_u$ is unauthorized and satisfies Theorem \ref{Th: attack} item (ii).
  \item If $n_u\geq t$ for some $u\in [q]$, then any subset consisting of $t_u$ participants of $\gamma_u$ is unauthorized and satisfies Theorem \ref{Th: attack} item (iii).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, the scheme of \cite{YangCSS2026} is insecure unless $t_u=n_u$ for all $u\in [q]$, in which case $t=n$ and $\Gamma_L=\{\mathcal{P}\}$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Denote the subsets constructed in items (i)-(iii) be $\mathcal{B}_1, \mathcal{B}_2$ and $\mathcal{B}_3$, respectively.

(i) Since $|\mathcal{B}_1|=(n-n_u)+(t_u-1)\geq t$, Theorem \ref{Th: attack} item (i) holds, while $|\mathcal{B}_1\cap \gamma_u|=t_u-1<t_u$ shows that $\mathcal{B}_1\notin \Gamma_L$.

(ii) Since $t\leq n$, we have $0\leq\max\{0,t-n_u\}\leq n-n_u$, which ensures that the subset $\mathcal{B}_2$ is well defined. Since $|\mathcal{B}_2\cap \gamma_u|=|\mathcal{B}_2^{(u)}|=t_u$ and $|\mathcal{B}_2\setminus \gamma_u|+n_u=\max\{0,t-n_u\}+n_u\geq t$, Theorem \ref{Th: attack} item (ii) holds. Now we show that $\mathcal{B}_2\notin \Gamma_L$. If $n_u\geq t$, then $\mathcal{B}_2\subseteq \gamma_u$, and $|\mathcal{B}_2\cap \gamma_w|=0<t_w$ for any $w\neq u$ (such a $w$ exists since $q\geq 2$). If $n_u<t$, then $|\mathcal{B}_2|=t_u+t-n_u<t$ because $t_u<n_u$. In both cases $\mathcal{B}_2\notin \Gamma_L$.

(iii) Since $n_u\geq t$ for some $u\in [q]$ and $|\mathcal{B}_3|=t_u$, Theorem \ref{Th: attack} item (iii) holds. Moreover, $\mathcal{B}_3\subseteq \gamma_u$, which implies that $|\mathcal{B}_3\cap \gamma_w|=0<t_w$ for any $w\neq u$ (such a $w$ exists since $q\geq 2$), so $\mathcal{B}_3\notin \Gamma_L$.

Finally, assume that the scheme is secure. By the above item (ii), $t_u=n_u$ for all $u\in [q]$. Hence $\sum_{u=1}^{q}t_u=\sum_{u=1}^{q}n_u=n$, and $\sum_{u=1}^{q}t_u\leq t\leq n$ implies $t=n$. Since every authorized subset $\mathcal{A}$ satisfies $|\mathcal{A}\cap \gamma_u|\geq t_u=n_u$ for all $u\in [q]$, it follows that $\mathcal{A}\supseteq \gamma_u$ for all $u\in [q]$. Therefore $\Gamma_L=\{\mathcal{P}\}$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{Re: degeneration}
Theorem \ref{Th: attack} item (i) shows that the compartment thresholds play no role at all: any $t$ participants reconstruct the secret, so the family of sets that can reconstruct the secret contains $\{\mathcal{B}\subseteq \mathcal{P}: |\mathcal{B}|\geq t\}$, and the scheme realises at most a $(t,n)$-threshold access structure. Theorem \ref{Th: attack} item (iii) is more damaging still: as soon as one compartment satisfies $n_u\geq t$, any $t_u$ participants reconstruct the secret, and the scheme realises at most the $(t_u,n)$-threshold access structure. Since $t_u$ may be as small as $1$, the scheme may be broken by a single participant.
