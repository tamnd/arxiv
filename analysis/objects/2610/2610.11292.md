<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:GO}
A connected homogeneous sub-Riemannian manifold $(M,\mathcal{D},Q)$ is geodesic orbit
(abbreviated GO) with respect to $G$ if every normal extremal in $T^*M$ is an orbit of a
one-parameter subgroup of $G$ under the cotangent lift of the $G$-action. In this case every
normal geodesic is an orbit $\exp(tX)\cdot p$ of a one-parameter subgroup, and a nonzero vector
$X\in\mathfrak{g}$ with this property is called a geodesic vector.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:twostep}
If $(M,\mathcal{D},Q)$ is geodesic orbit with respect to $G$, then
\[ \mathcal{D}_p+[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}]_p=T_pM\qquad\text{for every }p\in M. \]
Equivalently, for an $\mathrm{Ad}(H)$-invariant reductive decomposition
$\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}$ and the horizontal subspace
$\Delta=\mathcal{D}_{eH}\subset\mathfrak{m}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twostep}
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}+\Delta+[\Delta,\Delta],
\end{equation}
where $[\Delta,\Delta]$ denotes the linear span of the indicated Lie brackets. Consequently, every
constant-speed locally length-minimizing geodesic on a sub-Riemannian geodesic orbit manifold has
a normal lift; in other words, there are no strictly abnormal length minimizers.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $(M,\mathcal{D},Q)=(G/H,\Delta,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\Delta)$ be a compact, connected,
simply connected homogeneous sub-Riemannian manifold, where the action of $G$ is almost effective
and $H$ is a connected simple Lie group. Then every proper sub-Riemannian $G$-GO structure
belongs to one of the following two families, and every structure listed is GO and bracket
generating.
\begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\upshape(\Alph{enumi})}{\upshape(\Alph{enumi})}
\item Take one of the simple-group structures in Tables~\ref{tab:two} and~\ref{tab:rem},
optionally in product with a compact simply connected semisimple group carrying any positive
bi-invariant Riemannian metric.
\item Let $K$ be a compact simply connected semisimple Lie group, $H\subset K$ a connected simple
subgroup that is not normal, and $b$ any positive bi-invariant metric on $K$. On $K$ take the
left-invariant structure
\[ \Delta=\mathfrak{h}^{\perp_b}\subset\mathfrak{k},\qquad Q=b|_{\Delta}, \]
where $\mathfrak{h}^{\perp_b}$ is the $b$-orthogonal complement of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h}$
of $H$ in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{k}$ of $K$. Its acting group is $K\times H$ by left and right
translations, with isotropy group $\operatorname{diag} H$. Its corank is $\dim H$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:geod}
Let $(G/H,\Delta,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\Delta)$ be a connected homogeneous sub-Riemannian
manifold, let $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ be an $\mathrm{Ad}(H)$-invariant Riemannian extension of
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\Delta$ on $\mathfrak{m}$, and let $\Delta^{\perp}$ be the
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$-orthogonal complement of $\Delta$ in $\mathfrak{m}$. Then
$(G/H,\Delta,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\Delta)$ is geodesic orbit with respect to $G$ if and only
if, for every $X\in\Delta$ and every $Y\in\Delta^{\perp}$, there exists $Z\in\mathfrak{h}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geodlemma}
\langle X+Y,[X+Z,W]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle=0\qquad\text{for all }W\in\mathfrak{g}.
\end{equation}
In this case the normal geodesic through the base point $eH$ is of the form
$\exp t(X+Z)\cdot eH$, $t\in\mathbb{R}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:covector}
$(G/H,\Delta,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\Delta)$ is geodesic orbit with respect to $G$ if and only
if, for every $\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^0$, there exists $Z\in\mathfrak{h}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covector}
\lambda\big([\mathcal{A}\lambda+Z,W]\big)=0\qquad\text{for all }W\in\mathfrak{g}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Writing $\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^0$ as $\lambda=\langle X+Y,\cdot\rangle$ with $X=\mathcal{A}\lambda\in\Delta$
its horizontal part and $Y\in\Delta^{\perp}$ its vertical part, equation \eqref{eq:geodlemma} is
equivalent to \eqref{eq:covector}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since the sub-Riemannian structure is homogeneous, it suffices to verify \eqref{eq:twostep} at the
base point $p=eH$; the passage from $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}+\Delta+[\Delta,\Delta]$ to
$\mathcal{D}_p+[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}]_p=T_pM$ for arbitrary $p$ is the
homogeneous-distribution correspondence of Berestovskii \cite{Berestovskii}, followed by
translation by $G$.

Let $\pi:\mathfrak{g}\to\Delta^{\perp}$ be the natural projection along $\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Delta$. Define
\[ V:=\operatorname{span}\{\pi([X_1,X_2]):X_1,X_2\in\Delta\},\qquad
U:=V^{\perp}\cap\Delta^{\perp}. \]
Both $V$ and $U$ are $\mathrm{Ad}(H)$-invariant. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:claim}
[\Delta,V]\subset\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Delta\oplus V.
\end{equation}
Fix $X\in\Delta$, $v\in V$ and $u\in U$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:geod}, there exists $Z\in\mathfrak{h}$ such
that
\[ \langle X+u,[X+Z,W]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle=0\qquad\forall\,W\in\mathfrak{g}. \]
Taking $W=v$ gives
\[ 0=\langle X+u,[X+Z,v]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle=\langle X+u,[X,v]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle
+\langle X+u,[Z,v]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle. \]
Since $[\mathfrak{h},V]\subset V\subset\mathfrak{m}$, we have $[Z,v]=[Z,v]_{\mathfrak{m}}\in V$. Hence
$\langle X+u,[Z,v]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle=0$, and we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:step8}
\langle X+u,[X,v]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle=0.
\end{equation}
This identity holds for every $u\in U$, including $u=0$. Subtracting the case $u=0$ from
\eqref{eq:step8} yields
\[ \langle u,[X,v]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle=0,\qquad\forall\,u\in U. \]
Thus $[X,v]_{\mathfrak{m}}$ has no $U$-component. Since
$\mathfrak{m}=\Delta\oplus\Delta^{\perp}=\Delta\oplus V\oplus U$, this means
$[X,v]_{\mathfrak{m}}\in\Delta\oplus V$, and therefore $[X,v]\in\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Delta\oplus V$, proving
\eqref{eq:claim}. Set
\[ F:=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Delta\oplus V. \]
By construction,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Feq}
F=\mathfrak{h}+\Delta+[\Delta,\Delta].
\end{equation}
Indeed, $[\Delta,\Delta]\subset\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Delta\oplus V=F$ and
$\mathfrak{h}+\Delta+[\Delta,\Delta]\subset F$, while conversely $V\subset[\Delta,\Delta]+\mathfrak{h}+\Delta$,
so that $F\subset\mathfrak{h}+\Delta+[\Delta,\Delta]$. Note also that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Finv}
[\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Delta,F]\subset F.
\end{equation}
Now $F$ contains $\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Delta$, and we prove that $F$ contains every iterated Lie bracket
of elements of $\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Delta$. By the Jacobi identity and antisymmetry, every such iterated
Lie bracket is a linear combination of left-nested brackets
$[y_1,[y_2,[\cdots,[y_{k-1},y_k]\cdots]]]$ with $y_i\in\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Delta$. We argue by induction
on $k$. For $k=1$ we have $y_1\in\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Delta\subset F$. For $k\ge2$, the induction
hypothesis gives $[y_2,[\cdots,[y_{k-1},y_k]\cdots]]\in F$, and since $y_1\in\mathfrak{h}\oplus\Delta$,
equation \eqref{eq:Finv} gives $[y_1,[y_2,[\cdots]]]\in F$. Hence all iterated Lie brackets lie in
$F$, and so $F=\mathfrak{g}$. By \eqref{eq:Feq} we obtain
$\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}+\Delta+[\Delta,\Delta]$, which is \eqref{eq:twostep}. The final assertion follows
from the Goh condition \eqref{eq:goh}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:abelian}
Suppose that $(G/H,\Delta,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\Delta)$ is geodesic orbit with respect to
$G$. Every abelian ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ has zero intersection with $\mathfrak{h}$. Every
$\mathrm{Ad}(H)$-invariant positive definite inner product on $\mathfrak{a}$ is
$\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{g})$-invariant. In particular, $[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{a}]=0$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For $X\in\mathfrak{a}$, one has $(\mathrm{ad}\,X)^2=0$ because $\mathfrak{a}$ is an abelian ideal. If also
$X\in\mathfrak{h}$, then the compactness of $H$ provides an $\mathrm{Ad}(H)$-invariant positive
definite inner product on $\mathfrak{g}$ for which $\mathrm{ad}\,X$ is skew-adjoint. Hence
$\mathrm{ad}\,X$ is both nilpotent and skew-adjoint, so $\mathrm{ad}\,X=0$. Effectiveness of the
$G$-action implies $\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=0$, where $\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})$ is the center, so
$X=0$. This proves $\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{h}=0$.

Fix an $\mathrm{Ad}(H)$-invariant inner product $b$ on $\mathfrak{a}$. Take $v\in\mathfrak{a}$ and let
$\mu=b(v,\cdot)\in\mathfrak{a}^*$. Since $\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{h}=0$, the restriction map
$\mathfrak{h}^0\to\mathfrak{a}^*$ is surjective. Hence we may choose $\lambda_0\in\mathfrak{h}^0$ extending
$\mu$, that is, $\lambda_0|_{\mathfrak{a}}=\mu$. Define
\[ \eta(X)=\mu([X,v])\ (X\in\mathfrak{g}),\qquad \lambda_t=\lambda_0+t\eta\ (t\in\mathbb{R}). \]
The ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ is abelian, so $\eta|_{\mathfrak{a}}=0$. Also $\eta|_{\mathfrak{h}}=0$, since $b$ is
$\mathrm{Ad}(H)$-invariant. Thus $\eta\in\mathfrak{h}^0$ and $\lambda_t\in\mathfrak{h}^0$ with
$\lambda_t|_{\mathfrak{a}}=\mu$ for all $t$.

Apply the covector form \eqref{eq:covector} to $\lambda_t$, there exists
$Z\in\mathfrak{h}$ such that
\[ 0=\lambda_t\big([\mathcal{A}\lambda_t+Z,v]\big). \]
Both $[\mathcal{A}\lambda_t,v]$ and $[Z,v]$ lie in the ideal $\mathfrak{a}$. Hence
$\lambda_t([Z,v])=\mu([Z,v])=b(v,[Z,v])=0$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lem23}
0=\lambda_t\big([\mathcal{A}\lambda_t,v]\big).
\end{equation}
Now $[\mathcal{A}\lambda_t,v]\in\mathfrak{a}$ and $\lambda_0|_{\mathfrak{a}}=\mu$, so
$\lambda_0([\mathcal{A}\lambda_t,v])=\mu([\mathcal{A}\lambda_t,v])=\eta(\mathcal{A}\lambda_t)$. Also
$\eta([\mathcal{A}\lambda_t,v])=\mu([[\mathcal{A}\lambda_t,v],v])=0$ because $(\mathrm{ad}\,v)^2=0$ on $\mathfrak{a}$.
Using $\lambda_t=\lambda_0+t\eta$ and the linearity of $\mathcal{A}$, equation \eqref{eq:lem23} gives
\[ 0=\lambda_t\big([\mathcal{A}\lambda_t,v]\big)=\eta(\mathcal{A}\lambda_t)
=\eta(\mathcal{A}\lambda_0)+t\,\eta(\mathcal{A}\eta)
\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}). \]
Taking $t=0$ yields $\eta(\mathcal{A}\lambda_0)=0$, and then $t\,\eta(\mathcal{A}\eta)=0$ for all $t$, so
$\eta(\mathcal{A}\eta)=0$. But $\mathcal{A}\eta\in\Delta$ is the horizontal velocity of the covector $\eta$, and by
\eqref{eq:cometric},
\[ \eta(\mathcal{A}\eta)=Q(\mathcal{A}\eta,\mathcal{A}\eta)=Q^{*}(\eta|_{\Delta},\eta|_{\Delta})=0. \]
Thus $\eta|_{\Delta}=0$, that is, $b(v,[X,v])=\eta(X)=0$ for all $X\in\Delta$ and
$v\in\mathfrak{a}$. Polarizing in $v$, we get $b(v,[X,w])+b(w,[X,v])=0$, so
$\mathrm{ad}\,X|_{\mathfrak{a}}$ is skew-adjoint with respect to $b$ for every $X\in\Delta$. The same
holds for $X\in\mathfrak{h}$ by the $\mathrm{Ad}(H)$-invariance of $b$. Since $\mathfrak{h}+\Delta$ generates
$\mathfrak{g}$ and the skew-adjoint endomorphisms of $\mathfrak{a}$ form a Lie algebra, it follows that
$\mathrm{ad}\,X|_{\mathfrak{a}}$ is skew-adjoint for all $X\in\mathfrak{g}$; that is, $b$ is
$\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{g})$-invariant.

Finally, if $X\in\mathfrak{n}$, then $\mathrm{ad}\,X$ on $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent. Its restriction to
$\mathfrak{a}$ is both nilpotent and skew-adjoint, hence zero. Thus $[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{a}]=0$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:nilradical}
Suppose that $(G/H,\Delta,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\Delta)$ is geodesic orbit with respect to
$G$. Then $[\mathfrak{n},[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]]=0$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Write the lower central series $C^1\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}$, $C^{j+1}\mathfrak{n}=[\mathfrak{n},C^j\mathfrak{n}]$.
Suppose the nilpotency class were $r\ge3$, that is, $C^{r+1}\mathfrak{n}=0$ but $C^r\mathfrak{n}\neq0$.
Then the ideal $\mathfrak{a}=C^{r-1}\mathfrak{n}$ would be abelian, since
\[ [C^{r-1}\mathfrak{n},C^{r-1}\mathfrak{n}]\subset C^{2r-2}\mathfrak{n}=0. \]
It is an ideal of $\mathfrak{g}$ because the terms of the lower central series are characteristic in
$\mathfrak{n}$ and $\mathfrak{n}$ is an ideal of $\mathfrak{g}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:abelian} gives
$[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{a}]=0$, contradicting $[\mathfrak{n},C^{r-1}\mathfrak{n}]=C^r\mathfrak{n}\neq0$. Hence $r\le2$,
that is, $[\mathfrak{n},[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]]=0$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:reg}
The cotangent GO condition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GO}
\text{for every }p\in\mathfrak{m}\text{ there is }Z\in\mathfrak{h}\text{ such that }[p,Ap+Z]=0.
\end{equation}
For every $t>0$, this holds for $A$ if and only if it holds for $A+tI$. Hence $(\Delta,Q)$ is GO
if and only if the positive definite Riemannian metric with metric operator $(A+tI)^{-1}$ is
$G$-GO.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Represent $\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^0$ by $p\in\mathfrak{m}$ using $B$, that is, $\lambda=B(p,\cdot)$. Then
by the ad-invariance of $B$, we obtain for all $W\in\mathfrak{g}$
\[ \lambda([A\lambda+Z,W])=B(p,[Ap+Z,W])=B([p,Ap+Z],W).\]
It follows that  \eqref{eq:covector} is equivalent to \eqref{eq:GO}, since $B$ is nondegenerate.

Finally,
\[ [p,(A+tI)p+Z]=[p,Ap+t\,p+Z]=[p,Ap+Z]+t\,[p,p]=[p,Ap+Z], \]
so the same isotropy vector $Z$ works on both sides for every real $t$. Since $A+tI$ is positive
definite for $t>0$, it defines a Riemannian metric (with cometric $A+tI$ and metric operator
$(A+tI)^{-1}$) whose GO condition is exactly \eqref{eq:GO} for $A+tI$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:quadform}
Let $V\neq0$ be a real inner product space and $r\ge2$. The form $\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_i|z_i|^2$ on
$\sum_i z_i=0$ is nonnegative if and only if either all $c_i\ge0$, or exactly one coefficient $c_j$
is negative, every other coefficient is positive, and
\[ c_j\ge-\Bigl(\sum_{i\neq j}c_i^{-1}\Bigr)^{-1}. \]
In the first case its kernel consists of vectors supported on the zero coefficients and having
coordinate sum zero. In the second case it is positive definite for strict inequality; at equality
its kernel is $\{(c_i^{-1}u)_i:u\in V\}$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Two negative coefficients, or one negative and one zero coefficient, give a negative value on an
opposite pair of nonzero coordinates. In the all-nonnegative case the assertion about the kernel
follows term by term. In the remaining case put $S=\sum_{i\neq j}c_i^{-1}>0$. Subject to
$\sum_{i\neq j}z_i=-z_j$, the weighted Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\[ \sum_{i\neq j}c_i|z_i|^2\ge\frac{|z_j|^2}{S}, \]
with equality exactly when $z_i=-z_j/(Sc_i)$. Hence nonnegativity is equivalent to
$c_j+1/S\ge0$, and the equality case gives the stated kernel. For strict inequality, vanishing
requires $z_j=0$ and then every other coordinate zero.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:criterion}
Under the preceding hypotheses, the three properties
\[ A_c\ge0,\qquad \Delta=\operatorname{Im} A_c\neq\mathfrak{m},\qquad \mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}+\Delta+[\Delta,\Delta] \]
hold simultaneously if and only if the coefficients satisfy, for some $j\in F$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:criterion}
c_i>0\ (i\neq j),\qquad c_j=-\Bigl(\sum_{i\in J\setminus\{j\}}c_i^{-1}\Bigr)^{-1}<0,\qquad
P\neq\varnothing\ \text{or}\ r\ge3.
\end{equation}
Moreover, the kernel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
\operatorname{Ker} A_c=\{n(u):u\in\mathfrak{h}\},\qquad
n(u)_i=c_i^{-1}\rho_i(u),\quad i\in J.
\end{equation}
Consequently the corank is $\dim\mathfrak{h}$ and the horizontal rank is
$\dim\mathfrak{g}-2\dim\mathfrak{h}$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
\emph{Necessity.} Nonnegativity and bracket generation force $c_i>0$ for every $i\in P$. Indeed, a
negative coefficient makes $A_c$ negative on the nonzero summand $\mathfrak{p}_i$ in
\eqref{eq:decomp}. If such a coefficient is zero, the projection of $\mathfrak{h}+\operatorname{Im} A_c$ to
$\mathfrak{g}_i$ is contained in the proper subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}_i$; brackets cannot generate
$\mathfrak{g}_i$.

If all active coefficients are nonnegative, singularity requires at least two zero coefficients,
and their indices must be full. Choose distinct such indices $a,b$. For every $u\in\mathfrak{h}$, the
vector with active coordinates $z_a=u$, $z_b=-u$ and all others zero belongs to $\operatorname{Ker} A_c$. Since
$A_c$ is self-adjoint, its image is orthogonal to this kernel, and therefore its $a$ and $b$
coordinates agree under the identifications with $\mathfrak{h}$. The same is true of every element of
$\mathfrak{h}$. The equality of these two coordinates defines a proper Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$,
contradicting bracket generation.

There is consequently one negative active coefficient, at some $j\in F$. Lemma~\ref{lem:quadform}
implies that every other active coefficient is positive; hence $c_i>0$ for all $i\neq j$. The
strict inequality of that lemma would make $A_c$ positive definite. Singularity therefore forces
\[ c_j=-1/S,\qquad S=\sum_{i\in J\setminus\{j\}}c_i^{-1},\qquad \sum_{i\in J}c_i^{-1}=0. \]
The kernel is precisely \eqref{eq:kernel}.

If $r=2$ and $P=\varnothing$, both active factors are full and the last summand in
\eqref{eq:decomp} is the entire kernel. Thus $\Delta$ has no active component. The projection of
$\mathfrak{h}+\Delta$ onto the two active factors is diagonal, so it cannot generate their direct sum.
Necessity of the final condition in \eqref{eq:criterion} follows.

\emph{Sufficiency.} Assume \eqref{eq:criterion} holds. Lemma~\ref{lem:quadform} proves
nonnegativity, singularity and \eqref{eq:kernel}. Write $N=\operatorname{Ker} A_c$; since
$(\mathfrak{h}+\Delta)^{\perp_B}=N$, it suffices to prove that a vector $n(u)\in N$ orthogonal to
$[\Delta,\Delta]$ must vanish.

First suppose $P\neq\varnothing$ and choose $k\in P$. We have $\mathfrak{p}_k\subset\Delta$. The span
of the $\mathfrak{h}_k$-projections of $[\mathfrak{p}_k,\mathfrak{p}_k]$ is an $\mathfrak{h}_k$-ideal, by equivariance.
It is nonzero: otherwise invariance of $B_k$ would give
\[ B_k([U,X],Y)=B_k(U,[X,Y])=0\qquad(U\in\mathfrak{h}_k,\ X,Y\in\mathfrak{p}_k). \]
Because $[\mathfrak{h}_k,\mathfrak{p}_k]\subset\mathfrak{p}_k$, this would imply $[\mathfrak{h}_k,\mathfrak{p}_k]=0$ and
make $\mathfrak{h}_k$ a nonzero proper ideal of the simple $\mathfrak{g}_k$. The span is therefore all of
$\mathfrak{h}_k$. Orthogonality of $n(u)$ to these brackets says
\[ 0=B(n(u),[X,Y])=c_k^{-1}B_k(\rho_k(u),[X,Y])\qquad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{p}_k), \]
which forces $u=0$.

Now suppose $P=\varnothing$, so $r\ge3$ and all active factors are full. Put $v_i=c_i^{-1}$ for
$i\in F$ and choose a nonzero real vector
\[ a\in W=\Bigl\{(a_i)_{i\in F}:\sum_i a_i=0,\ \sum_i v_ia_i=0\Bigr\}. \]
This is possible because $\dim W=r-2$; the vectors $(1)_i$ and $(v_i)_i$ are independent. For
every $x\in\mathfrak{h}$, the vector $a\otimes x:=(a_i\rho_i(x))_{i\in F}$ lies in
$\Delta=\mathfrak{m}\cap N^{\perp_B}$. The identities $\sum_i v_i=\sum_i v_ia_i=0$ give
\[ \kappa:=\sum_i v_ia_i^2=\sum_{i\neq j}v_i(a_i-a_j)^2>0. \]
Strict positivity follows because all $v_i$ for $i\neq j$ are positive; equality would make all
$a_i$ equal, and $\sum_i a_i=0$ would then imply $a=0$. For $x,y\in\mathfrak{h}$,
\[ B\big(n(u),[a\otimes x,a\otimes y]\big)=\kappa B_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,[x,y]). \]
Since $[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}]=\mathfrak{h}$, orthogonality forces $u=0$. This proves
$\mathfrak{h}+\Delta+[\Delta,\Delta]=\mathfrak{g}$ in both cases. The metric is positive definite on the
image, and formula \eqref{eq:kernel} gives the rank and corank.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:isom}
Under the above hypotheses. Let $(G/H,\Delta,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\Delta)$ be a compact, connected, simply connected
 sub-Riemannian geodesic orbit manifold with $G$ semisimple and $H$ simple. Then it is isometric to
$((K\times H)/\operatorname{diag} H,\Delta,B|_{\Delta})=(K,\Delta,b)$, where $K$ is a compact simply connected semisimple Lie group,  $b$ is a positive bi-invariant metric on $K$, and
$\Delta=\mathfrak{h}^{\perp_b}\subset\mathfrak{k}$ is the $b$-orthogonal complement of the Lie algebra
$\mathfrak{h}$ of $H$ in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{k}$ of $K$,
$H\simeq (\rho_{i}(H))_{i\neq j}\subset K$ is a
connected simple subgroup that is not normal.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Notation as in Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}. Put $\mathfrak{k}=\bigoplus_{i\neq j}\mathfrak{g}_i$ and
$b=\sum_{i\neq j}c_i^{-1}B_i$. The remaining projection embeds $\mathfrak{h}$ into $\mathfrak{k}$. At the
group level, replace $H$ by its image in $K$, dividing out the possible finite ineffective kernel.
The homogeneous quotient $(K\times H)/\operatorname{diag} H$ is $K$, with tangent map
\[ T\big((x_i)_{i\neq j},z_j\big)=\big(x_i-\rho_i(z_j)\big)_{i\neq j}, \]
where $\rho_i$ is the isotropy embedding. For $p\in\mathfrak{m}$ with active
components $z_i$, orthogonality to $\operatorname{Ker} A_c$ reads
\[ \sum_{i\neq j}z_i/c_i-Sz_j=0. \]
This is precisely $b(Tp,\mathfrak{h})=0$. Also $T(A_cp)_i=c_ip_i-c_j\rho_i(z_j)$, and
$\sum_{i\neq j}z_i=-z_j$, $c_jS=-1$ give
\[ b\big(T(A_cp),T(A_cp)\big)=\sum_{i\neq j}c_i|p_i|^2+(2c_j+c_j^2S)|z_j|^2=B(p,A_cp). \]
Polarization proves that the resulting horizontal metric is exactly $Q=b|_{\Delta}$.

It remains to determine bracket generation. The Lie algebra generated by $\mathfrak{h}^{\perp_b}$ is
$\mathfrak{h}$-invariant, hence is an ideal of $\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{h}^{\perp_b}$. Its
$b$-orthogonal complement is also an ideal and lies in $\mathfrak{h}$. Simplicity of $\mathfrak{h}$ makes
this complement either zero or $\mathfrak{h}$. The latter occurs exactly when $\mathfrak{h}$ is an ideal of
$\mathfrak{k}$. This proves the stated condition that $H$ be non-normal.
