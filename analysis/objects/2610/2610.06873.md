<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:scaling}
Let $\alpha > 0$ and $x \in \mathbb{R}$, then
\begin{equation}
\big[\exp k(x)\big]^{\alpha} = \exp_{\kappa/\alpha}(\alpha x).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
First, recall that $\exp k(x) = (\sqrt{1 + \kappa^2 x^2} + \kappa x)^{1/\kappa}$. Thus, we obtain $[\exp k(x)]^\alpha = (\sqrt{1 + \kappa^2 x^2} + \kappa x)^{\alpha/\kappa}$, which is indeed the case. Now let us define a new symbol $\kappa' = \kappa/\alpha$ and set $y = \alpha x$. In this case, remember that $\kappa' y = \kappa x$ and $\kappa'^2 y^2 = \kappa^2 x^2$; therefore,
when we evaluate the expression $\exp_{\kappa'}(y) = (\sqrt{1+\kappa'^2 y^2}+\kappa'y)^{1/\kappa'}$, the result is
$(\sqrt{1+\kappa^2 x^2}+\kappa x)^{\alpha/\kappa}$.
Moreover, this matches exactly, up to a constant factor, the expression we obtained for $[\exp k(x)]^\alpha$; thus, the claim is proven.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:variance-threshold}
Assume \(X\sim f_\kappa(\,\cdot\,;\mu,\Sigma)\) is the isotropic standardized \(\kappa\)-Gaussian from Section \(\ref{sec:mep-derivation}\). Here \(\Sigma=I_m\).  
We look at when \(\mathbb{E}\|X\|^2\) is finite. This happens exactly when  
\[
\kappa < \frac{2}{m+2}.
\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $R = \|X\|$. According to the tail equivalence, when $\|x\| \to \infty$, $f_\kappa(x) \sim C\|x\|^{-2/\kappa}$ (Section~\ref{sec:tail-behaviour}), provided that the radial density of $R$ satisfies $\omega_{m-1}r^{m-1}f_\kappa(r) \sim C'r^{m-1-2/\kappa}$ as $r \to \infty$. We therefore have
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[R^2] = \int_0^\infty r^2\cdot \omega_{m-1}r^{m-1}f_\kappa(r)\,dr
\end{equation}
has an integrand that behaves like $C'r^{m+1-2/\kappa}$ for values of $r$ that are large, and the integral approaches zero at infinity if and only if the exponent satisfies the condition $m + 1 - 2/\kappa < -1$, that is, $m + 2 < 2/\kappa$, and, $\kappa < 2/(m + 2)$; if $\kappa \geq 2/(m + 2)$, the integral diverges. Since the integral function is bounded near the origin (because $f_\kappa$ are bounded), convergence at infinity is the only necessary condition.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Proposition~\ref{prop:variance-threshold} is the $\kappa$-Gaussian analogue of the classical requirement $\nu>2$ for the multivariate Student-$t_\nu$ distribution to have finite covariance. This was confirmed numerically in the simulations underlying Section~\ref{sec:simulation}: for $\kappa$ close to but below the threshold, $\mathbb{E}[R^2]$ is finite but large and slow to stabilize in finite samples; for $\kappa$ at or above the threshold, the empirical variance fails to converge as $N$ grows. All simulations in Section~\ref{sec:simulation} restrict $\kappa$ to a fixed fraction of this threshold (as a function of $m$) to stay within the regime where the standardization $\Sigma=I_m$ is meaningful. This constraint applies throughout the parameter space $(\mu,\Sigma,\kappa)$ wherever $\Sigma$ is interpreted as the covariance matrix (as opposed to a general scale matrix), as reflected in Assumption~\ref{ass:A1} and in the statement of the GOF test in Section~\ref{sec:test}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:nn}
Suppose that $X_1,\dots,X_n$ are independent and identically distributed random vectors in $\mathbb{R}^m$ with density $f$, and let $k \in \{1,\dots,n-1\}$. For $i = 1, \dots, n$, let us arrange the Euclidean distances $\{\|X_i - X_j\|: j \neq i\}$ in ascending order as follows:
\begin{equation}
\rho_{1,i} \leq \rho_{2,i} \leq \cdots \leq \rho_{n-1,i}.
\end{equation}
The \emph{$k$-th nearest neighbour distance} of $X_i$ is $\rho_{k,i} = \rho_{k,i}(X_1,\dots,X_n) := \rho_{k,i}$, equivalently
\begin{equation}
\rho_{k,i} = \min\Big\{ r>0 \;:\; \big|\{j\neq i : \|X_i-X_j\|\leq r\}\big| \geq k \Big\},
\end{equation}
meaning the radius of the smallest closed sphere centered at point $X_i$ and containing at least $k$ of the remaining $n-1$ sampling points.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:nn-density}
With $V_m = \pi^{m/2}/\Gamma(m/2+1)$ the volume of the unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^m$, the standard $k$-NN density estimator at $X_i$ is
\begin{equation}
\hat f_{k,i} = \frac{k}{(n-1)\,V_m\,\rho_{k,i}^m}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:A1}
The function $f$ is bounded; the support of $f$ is a set such that $\operatorname{supp}(f) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$, $f$ is bounded away from $0$ by every compact subset of its (open) support, where $\int f(x)^{1-\kappa}dx,\ \int f(x)^{1+\kappa}dx < \infty$. This additionally requires the condition $\kappa < 2/(m+2)$ when $f = f_\kappa$ itself is a $\kappa$-Gaussian distribution and $\Sigma$ is taken as its covariance matrix (Proposition~\ref{prop:variance-threshold}).
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- assumption-2: body_md -->
\label{ass:A2}
The function $f$ is twice continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of every point in its support, $\nabla^2 f$ has a bounded value, and there exist values of $\gamma\in(0,1]$ and $L<\infty$ such that the following equality applies:
\begin{equation}
\Big|f(x+u) - \sum_{|\ell|\le 1} \tfrac{D^\ell f(x)}{\ell!}u^\ell\Big| \le L\|u\|^{1+\gamma} \qquad \text{for } u \text{, } \text{in a neighborhood of } 0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end assumption-2 -->
<!-- assumption-3: body_md -->
\label{ass:A3}
The constant $k \geq 1$ does not increase with $n$.
<!-- end assumption-3 -->
<!-- assumption-4: body_md -->
\label{ass:A4}
Because $\kappa \in (0,1)$, $1-\kappa, 1+\kappa \in (0,2)\setminus\{1\}$, which falls within the acceptable range of exponents for Assumption~\ref{ass:A1}.
<!-- end assumption-4 -->
<!-- assumption-5: body_md -->
\label{ass:A5}
$\mathbb{E}\big[\rho_{k,1}^{m(2+\delta)}\big] < \infty$ applies uniformly with respect to $n$ for some $\delta > 0$.
<!-- end assumption-5 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:lps}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:A1}--\ref{ass:A2}, for fixed $k\geq1$ and $\alpha$ in a neighbourhood of $1$ with $\alpha\neq1$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\Big[C_{k,\alpha}\big((n-1)V_m\rho_{k,i}^m\big)^{1-\alpha}\Big] = I^{(\alpha)}[f] + O\!\big(n^{-\gamma/m}\big),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}\Big[C_{k,\alpha}\big((n-1)V_m\rho_{k,i}^m\big)^{1-\alpha}\Big] = O(1),
\end{equation}
uniformly over $i=1,\dots,n$, where $\gamma$ is the H\"older exponent of Assumption~\ref{ass:A2}.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:marginal}
Given the assumptions $\ref{ass:A1}$–$\ref{ass:A3}$, we have that for every constant $\alpha \in \{1-\kappa,\,1+\kappa\}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\big[\widehat I_n^{(\alpha)}\big] = I^{(\alpha)}[f] + O(n^{-\gamma/m}), \qquad
\operatorname{Var}\big(\widehat I_n^{(\alpha)}\big) = O(n^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Thus, when $n \to \infty$, $\widehat I_n^{(\alpha)} \to I^{(\alpha)}[f]$ in the $L^2$ space.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
When the deviation bound obtained from Lemma $\ref{lem:lps}$ is averaged over $i = 1, \dots, n$, the specified deviation is obtained; for this value holds uniformly for all values of $i$. Let $\widehat I_n^{(\alpha)} = \frac1n\sum_i Y_i$ denote the variance, where $Y_i := C_{k,\alpha}\big((n-1)V_m\rho_{k,i}^m\big)^{1-\alpha}$. According to Lemma~\ref{lem:lps}, we have $\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)=O(1)$; the $Y_i$’s have no independence, however $\rho_{k,i}$ has only depends on the $k$ points closest to $X_i$ . Thus, when the $k$-NN sets of $i$ and $j$ intersect, the maximum degree of the dependency graph over $\{1,\dots,n\}$ is bounded by a constant $c(k,m)$ independent of $n$ (this is a standard consequence of the bounded-degree property of $k$-NN graphs in $\mathbb{R}^m$; \citealp{redmond1996,penrose2003}). Therefore
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}\big(\widehat I_n^{(\alpha)}\big) = \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i,j}\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Y_j) = \frac{1}{n^2}\Big[\sum_i \operatorname{Var}(Y_i) + \sum_{i\neq j: i\sim j}\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Y_j)\Big] = \frac{1}{n^2}\big[O(n) + O(n)\big] = O(n^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Here, we have $O(n)$ nonzero terms in the second sum (its order is bounded); by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, each term is $O(1)$, and $\operatorname{Var}(Y_i) = O(1)$. Since the bias$^2 + $variance$\to0$, $L^2$-convergence follows.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:joint}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:A1}--\ref{ass:A4},
\begin{equation}
\big\|(\widehat I_n^{(1-\kappa)},\widehat I_n^{(1+\kappa)}) - (I^{(1-\kappa)}[f],I^{(1+\kappa)}[f])\big\|_2 \to 0,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Cov}\big(\widehat I_n^{(1-\kappa)},\widehat I_n^{(1+\kappa)}\big) = O(n^{-1}).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $\widehat I_n^{(1-\kappa)}=\frac1n\sum_i Y_i$; where $Y_i := C_{k,1-\kappa}\big((n-1)V_m\rho_{k,i}^m\big)^{\kappa}$ and $\widehat I_n^{(1+\kappa)} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i Z_i$; where $Z_i := C_{k,1+\kappa}\big((n-1)V_m\rho_{k,i}^m\big)^{-\kappa}$; both $Y_i$ and $Z_i$ have the same $k$-NN distance $\rho_{k,i}$ as a function (raised to different powers), therefore, they are not \emph{independent} for even a single $i$; also, for $i \neq j$, $\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i, Z_j) \neq 0$ applies only when the $k$-NN neighborhoods of $i$ and $j$ overlap again, due to the finite-degree property, there are $O(n)$ such pairs. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Cov}\big(\widehat I_n^{(1-\kappa)},\widehat I_n^{(1+\kappa)}\big) = \frac{1}{n^2}\Big[\sum_i \operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Z_i) + \sum_{i\neq j: i\sim j}\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Z_j)\Big].
\end{equation}
Following the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality as well as Lemma~\ref{lem:lps}, $|\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Z_i)| \le \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)\operatorname{Var}(Z_i)} = O(1)$, and applies similarly to the cross-pairs; therefore both sums equal $O(n)$, resulting in$\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat I_n^{(1-\kappa)},\widehat I_n^{(1+\kappa)}) = O(n^{-1})$. The common $L^2$-convergence is obtained from the marginal $L^2$-convergence (Lemma~\ref{lem:marginal}) and the fact that $\operatorname{Cov} \to 0$ as following:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\big[\|(\widehat I_n^{(1-\kappa)},\widehat I_n^{(1+\kappa)}) - (I^{(1-\kappa)}[f],I^{(1+\kappa)}[f])\|_2^2\big] = \mathrm{MSE}(\widehat I_n^{(1-\kappa)}) + \mathrm{MSE}(\widehat I_n^{(1+\kappa)}) \to 0. \qedhere
\end{equation}

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:consistency}
Assuming the Assumptions $\ref{ass:A1}$–$\ref{ass:A4}$, if $k \geq 1$ is a constant and $\kappa \in (0,1)$, then
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\big[(\widehat S_{\kappa,n} - S_\kappa[f])^2\big] = O\!\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^2}\Big(n^{-2\gamma/m} + n^{-1}\Big)\right) \longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{as $n \to \infty$}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
From \eqref{eq:decomposition},
\begin{equation}
\widehat S_{\kappa,n} - S_\kappa[f] = \frac{1}{2\kappa}\Big[\big(\widehat I_n^{(1-\kappa)} - I^{(1-\kappa)}[f]\big) - \big(\widehat I_n^{(1+\kappa)} - I^{(1+\kappa)}[f]\big)\Big] =: \frac{D_n}{2\kappa}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[D_n^2] = \operatorname{Var}\big(\widehat I_n^{(1-\kappa)}\big) + \operatorname{Var}\big(\widehat I_n^{(1+\kappa)}\big) - 2\operatorname{Cov}\big(\widehat I_n^{(1-\kappa)},\widehat I_n^{(1+\kappa)}\big) + \big(\mathrm{bias}_{1-\kappa} - \mathrm{bias}_{1+\kappa}\big)^2,
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{bias}_\alpha := \mathbb{E}[\widehat I_n^{(\alpha)}] - I^{(\alpha)}[f] = O(n^{-\gamma/m})$, and this value is determined by Lemma~\ref{lem:marginal}. According to Lemmas $\ref{lem:marginal}$–$\ref{lem:joint}$, both the variance and covariance terms have a complexity of $O(n^{-1})$, and the squared error term has a complexity of $O(n^{-2\gamma/m})$. Therefore, $\mathbb{E}[D_n^2] = O(n^{-2\gamma/m} + n^{-1})$, which is divided by $4\kappa^2$ to obtain the specified ratio.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The rate in Theorem~\ref{thm:consistency} degrades as $\kappa\to0$ through the $1/\kappa^2$ prefactor; this is a distinct phenomenon from the bias growth observed empirically in Section~\ref{sec:sim-gof-heatmap} as $\kappa$ approaches the variance-existence threshold of Proposition~\ref{prop:variance-threshold} \emph{from below} -- i.e.\ as $\kappa$ grows, not shrinks. That empirical growth is not accounted for by the constants appearing in the proof above, and we do not offer a rate for it here; a plausible mechanism is that the H\"older constant $L$ in Assumption~\ref{ass:A2} degrades as the density becomes harder to approximate locally near the boundary, but we have not made this precise, and flag it as an open question. Separately, whether the bias terms of the two power-sum estimators partially cancel, rather than combining via the crude triangle bound used above, merits further investigation; a sharper rate for the $\kappa\to0$ behaviour may follow from a joint second-order expansion of the two moment functionals around $\alpha=1$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:limit}
As $\kappa\to0^+$, $\widehat S_{\kappa,n}\to\widehat H_n$, the Kozachenko--Leonenko Shannon entropy estimator \citep{kozachenko1987}, and $S_\kappa[f]\to H[f]=-\int f\log f$, recovering the $L^2$-consistency result of \citet{cadirci2022entropy} as a limiting special case.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $\alpha = 1\mp\kappa$; thus, as $\kappa \to 0$, $1 - \alpha = \pm\kappa \to 0$. The function $C_{k,\alpha} = \Gamma(k)/\Gamma(k+1-\alpha)$ is regular with respect to $\alpha$ in the vicinity of $\alpha = 1$, and $C_{k,1} = 1$ and $\frac{d}{d\alpha}\ log C_{k,\alpha}\big|_{\alpha=1} = \psi(k)$ (digamma function; from $\Gamma'(k)/\Gamma(k)=\psi(k)$), we obtain the first-order Taylor series expansion $C_{k,\alpha} = 1 + (1-\alpha)\psi(k) + o(1-\alpha)$. In a similar way, $[(n-1)V_m\rho_{k,i}^m]^{1-\alpha} = 1 + (1-\alpha)\log[(n-1)V_m\rho_{k,i}^m] + o(1-\alpha)$. When we multiply these and retain the first-order terms in the expression $(1-\alpha) = \pm\kappa$.
\begin{equation}
Y_i - Z_i = 2\kappa\Big[\log\big((n-1)V_m\rho_{k,i}^m\big) + \psi(k)\Big] + o(\kappa).
\end{equation}
The result is that $\widehat{S}_{\kappa,n} = (\widehat{I}_n^{(1-\kappa)} - \widehat{I}_n^{(1+\kappa)})/(2\kappa) = \frac{1}{2\kappa n}\sum_i (Y_i - Z_i) \to \frac{1}{n}\sum_i\big[\log((n-1)V_m\ rho_{k,i}^m) + \psi(k)\big] $ when $\kappa \to 0$, and this is exactly the Kozachenko–Leonenko Shannon entropy estimator (taking into account the general sign rule; since $S_\kappa \to H = -\int f \log f$, while the sum shown consists of averaged $+\log$ terms, which matches $\widehat{H}_n$ as traditionally described using the digamma correction).
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:clt}
Under assumptions $\ref{ass:A1}$–$\ref{ass:A5}$,
\begin{equation}
\sqrt {n} \Big[\big(\widehat{I}_n^{(1-\kappa)}, \widehat{I}_n^{(1+\kappa)}\big) - \big(I^{(1-\kappa)}[f], I^{(1+\kappa)}[f]\big)\Big] \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}_2(0, \Gamma),
\end {equation}
here $\Gamma_{\alpha\alpha'} = \lim_{n\to\infty} n\,\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat I_n^{(\alpha)},\widehat I_n^{(\alpha')})$ and $\alpha,\alpha' \in \{1-\kappa,1+\kappa\}$. By applying the delta method to $\phi(a,b)=(a-b)/(2\kappa)$, we obtain the following:
\begin {equation}
\sqrt{n}\,\big(\widehat{S}_{\kappa,n} - S_{\kappa}[f]\big) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\kappa}^2),
\qquad \sigma_{\kappa}^2 = \frac{\Gamma_{11} - 2\Gamma_{12} + \Gamma_{22}}{4\kappa^2}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Fix $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$ and consider $W_n := a\widehat I_n^{(1-\kappa)}+b\widehat I_n^{(1+\kappa)} = \frac1n\sum_i (aY_i+bZ_i)$. Each summand $aY_i+bZ_i$ is a function of $\rho_{k,i}$ alone, and $\rho_{k,i}$ is determined by the local configuration of points within the $k$-NN neighbourhood of $X_i$ -- i.e.\ $W_n$ is (a normalized sum of) a \emph{stabilizing} functional of the $k$-NN graph on $\{X_1,\dots,X_n\}$ in the sense of \citet{penrose2001,penrose2003}: changing points far from $X_i$ does not change $\rho_{k,i}$. Given the assumptions ~\ref{ass:A1}--\ref{ass:A2}, \ref{ass:A5}, we have the general central limit theorem for stabilizing Euclidean NN-functionals \citep [Thm.~2.1] {penrose2003} holds for $W_n$, which in turn implies that for some $\sigma_{a,b}^2 = a^2\Gamma_{11}+2ab\Gamma_{12}+b^2\Gamma_{22}\geq0$, $\sqrt n (W_n - \mathbb{E} W_n) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal N(0,\sigma_{a,b}^2)$; here, $\Gamma_{\alpha\alpha'}$ denotes the corresponding asymptotic (co)variance ratio determined using Lemma~\ref{lem:joint}. Because $a$ and $b$ have arbitrary values, the Cramér–Wold approach provides a joint bivariate CLT. Because $\phi(a,b) = (a - b)/(2\kappa)$ is linear (and thus, for $\kappa \neq 0$, $\nabla\phi = (1, -1)/(2\kappa) \neq 0$), which gives $\sigma_\kappa^2 = \nabla\phi^T\Gamma\nabla\phi = (\Gamma_{11}-2\Gamma_{12}+\Gamma_{22})/(4\kappa^2)$.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Unlike the case of the single-exponent R’en, where the marginal variance is determined solely by $\Gamma_{11}$, here $\Gamma_{12}$ generally does not have a simple closed-form expression. Practically, as in the variance estimate in \citet{cadirci2025tsallis}, $\sigma_\kappa^2$ can be estimated via bootstrap or subsampling; The normality checks in Section~\ref{sec:sim-normality} empirically confirm this estimate by comparing it to the finite-sample distribution of $\widehat S_{\kappa,n}$. In order to verify the $\sigma_\kappa^2$ formula term by term, however, we did not decompose the estimate into $\Gamma_{11}$, $\Gamma_{12}$, and $\Gamma_{22}$ separately; doing so would be a natural check for future work.
