<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:informal-A}
Fix an order $d$ at which the quotient Boolean moment SDP of
\cref{sec:degeneracy} has a unique rank-one optimum and at least one
strictly complementary optimal SOS certificate.
Then, for every fixed admissible step choice, the canonical DR residual
satisfies a uniform local error bound around its entire fixed-point set;
consequently, exact ADMM, exact sGS--ADMM, and fixed-step PDHG converge
eventually linearly from every initialization, and the limiting KKT point
they select need not be strictly complementary. Moreover, outside a
lower-dimensional semialgebraic exceptional set in the $2^n$-dimensional
space of Boolean objectives, both hypotheses hold simultaneously at every
uniquely rank-one exact order. The formal statements are
\cref{thm:boolean-fixed,thm:main}, with rates in \cref{cor:admm,cor:pdhg}.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
All spaces are finite-dimensional and real. $\mathbb S^p$ is the space of
symmetric $p\times p$ matrices with the trace inner product
$\langle H_1,H_2\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(H_1H_2)$, Frobenius norm $\lVert \cdot\rVert_F$, and PSD cone
$\mathbb S_+^p$; $\Pi_+$ is the Frobenius projection onto $\mathbb S_+^p$ and
$\Pi_-:=\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_+$. For $S\subseteq[n]$, $x^S:=\prod_{i\in S}x_i$; optima
carry star subscripts ($x_\star$, $X_\star$), optimal sets are calligraphic
($\mathcal S_\star$, $\mathcal Z_\star$), and we distinguish the number of Boolean variables $n$,
a general SDP matrix size $p$, and the moment matrix size $N_d$ (with
$p=N_d$ in the Boolean specialization). The remaining conventions are
standard---directional derivatives $F'(z;h)$; tangent and normal cones
$T_C(z)$, $N_C(z)$; $\operatorname{ri}$, $\operatorname{aff}$, $\operatorname{cl}$; vector Euclidean and operator
norms---and are collected in \cref{app:spectral}.
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:gauge}
A unique Boolean minimizer genuinely restricts symmetric models, since a
MAX-CUT or zero-field Ising objective satisfies $f(x)=f(-x)$ and its
minimizers come in $\pm$ pairs. Such models enter our framework after a standard gauge
fixing, such as fixing one spin in a connected instance; we use them as
motivation rather than as literal instances.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:degeneracy}
For the independent encoding $\mathcal A_d$, primal nondegeneracy of
$X_\star$~\cite{alizadeh9mp-complementarity-nondegeneracy} holds if and only
if $m=N$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dimL}
  \dim L=m-N=\frac{N(N-1)}2-|\mathcal I_{2d}|+1 .
\end{equation}
At $d=1$ one has $m=N$; at every $2\le d\le n$ one has $m>N$
(indeed $w_1w_1^\top-w_2w_2^\top\in L\setminus\{0\}$), so the rank-one
primal optimum is degenerate. If, in addition, $\mathcal S_\star$ contains a
\emph{strictly complementary} certificate $S_{\mathrm{sc}}$, i.e.\
$\operatorname{rank}(S_{\mathrm{sc}})=N-1$, then $\operatorname{aff}\mathcal S_\star=S_{\mathrm{sc}}+L$, so $\mathcal S_\star$ is
positive-dimensional with $\dim\mathcal S_\star=m-N$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:one-sided}
The primal optimizer $X_\star$ is unique with $1\le r:=\operatorname{rank} X_\star<p$; the
optimal slack set $\mathcal S_\star$ is nonempty and compact; and there exists
$S_{\mathrm{sc}}\in\mathcal S_\star$ with $X_\starS_{\mathrm{sc}}=0$ and $X_\star+S_{\mathrm{sc}}\succ0$.
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:rigidity}
Assume \cref{ass:one-sided} and $\mu_{\mathrm{ri}}>0$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item $\mu_{\mathrm{nor}}>0$: the modulus extends to every boundary normal.
\item There exist $c_\sigma,t_\sigma>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-eb}
 \lVert \delta_\sigma(Z)\rVert_F\ \ge\ c_\sigma\,\operatorname{dist}_F(Z,\mathcal Z_\star)
 \qquad\text{whenever }\operatorname{dist}_F(Z,\mathcal Z_\star)\le t_\sigma .
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
No positive lower bound on the nonzero eigenvalues of the varying slack
$\bar S$ is imposed.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- head-2: body_md -->
We argue by contradiction: suppose endpoints $\bar Z_k=X_\star-\sigma\bar
S_k$ and unit normals $H_k\in N_{\mathcal Z_\star}(\bar Z_k)$ satisfy
$\delta_\sigma'(\bar Z_k;H_k)\to0$, and set $E_k:=\Pi_+'(\bar Z_k;H_k)$.
\emph{First}, the certificate kills the $V$ block: $P_VE_kP_V$ is PSD by
\eqref{eq:positive-deriv}, and
$\langle P_VE_kP_V,S_{\mathrm{sc}}\rangle=\langle \mathsf P E_k,S_{\mathrm{sc}}-\bar S_k\rangle\to0$ by
\eqref{eq:EF-identities}, so $P_VE_kP_V\to0$. \emph{Second}, the remainder
of $E_k$ has no $VV$ component, so \cref{lem:stable-directions} pushes it
to relative-interior endpoints, where the modulus $\mu_{\mathrm{ri}}$
forces it to vanish; hence $E_k\to0$, and $H_k$ approaches
$L=\mathcal R\cap\mathbb S(V)$. \emph{Third}, decomposing $H_k=Y_k+G_k$ by
\eqref{eq:normal-sum} with the bounds of \cref{lem:normal-bound} gives
$\langle H_k,Y_k\rangle\to0$ and $\limsup_k\langle H_k,G_k\rangle\le0$, contradicting
$\lVert H_k\rVert_F=1$.
The full proof is in \cref{app:rigidity-i}.

\begin{corollary}[Qualitative rigidity on $L^\perp$]\label{cor:qualitative}
Under the assumptions of \cref{thm:rigidity},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qualitative}
 \delta_\sigma'(\bar Z;H)\ne0
 \qquad\text{for every }\bar Z\in\mathcal Z_\star,\ H\in L^\perp\setminus\{0\},
\end{equation}
since $L^\perp\subseteq N_{\mathcal Z_\star}(\bar Z)$ by \eqref{eq:normal-sum}.
\end{corollary}

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:boolean-fixed}
Suppose the order-$d$ Boolean moment relaxation has a unique rank-one
optimum and one strictly complementary optimal SOS certificate. Then, for
every fixed $\sigma>0$, the canonical DR residual satisfies the setwise
error bound \eqref{eq:canonical-eb} around its entire fixed-point set
$\mathcal Z_\star$. The algorithmic conclusions of \cref{cor:admm,cor:pdhg} hold for
the independent encoding \eqref{eq:Ad}, at every endpoint,
regardless of the rank of the certificate selected by the iteration.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
\Cref{prop:encoding} supplies surjectivity of $\mathcal A_d$;
\cref{lem:slack-trace} supplies compactness of $\mathcal S_\star$; the instantiation
above places us under \cref{ass:one-sided};
\cref{prop:boolean-modulus} verifies $\mu_{\mathrm{ri}}>0$. Apply
\cref{thm:rigidity}, then \cref{sec:algorithms}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:admm}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:boolean-fixed} (or, abstractly, of
\cref{thm:rigidity}), for every fixed $\sigma>0$ and $0<\tau<2$, the shadow
orbits of exact ADMM and exact sGS--ADMM converge to an endpoint of
$\mathcal Z_\star$; the distance contracts eventually Q-linearly in the
$\mathsf G_\tau$ metric, and the shadow and the raw primal--dual iterates
converge R-linearly (raw-variable recovery: \cref{app:shadow-rates}).
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:pdhg}
Under the same hypotheses with $\sigma=\eta_x$ and
$\eta_x\eta_y\lVert \mathcal A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2<1$, every PDHG orbit converges to a KKT pair;
the distance to $\mathcal W_\star$ contracts eventually Q-linearly in the
preconditioned metric, and the iterates and the slack
$S^k=C-\mathcal A^*\lambda^k$ converge R-linearly.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:eventual}
At $d=n$ the moment body $\mathcal X_n$ is the affine image, with unique
coefficients, of the probability simplex on $\{\pm1\}^n$:
$X=\sum_{a\in\{\pm1\}^n}\pi_a\,b_n(a)b_n(a)^\top$ with $\pi_a\ge0$,
$\sum_a\pi_a=1$. Consequently every uniquely minimized $f$ is uniquely
rank-one exact at $d=n$, so $d_\star(f)\le n$; and unique rank-one exactness
persists upward from any order at which it holds. Moreover, for every
uniquely minimized $f$ the full-order relaxation admits a strictly
complementary optimal certificate, of rank $2^n-1$, given explicitly in
\cref{app:full-order}.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:cell-generic}
$\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sc}}(a,d)$ is open in $\mathcal Q(a,d)$ and dense relative to
$\mathcal C(a,d)$; both cells are semialgebraic; and
$\dim\bigl(\mathcal C(a,d)\setminus\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sc}}(a,d)\bigr)<\dim\mathcal C(a,d)$.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
There exists an open dense semialgebraic set $\mathcal G_{\mathrm{gen}}\subseteq\mathcal P_n$ whose
complement has Lebesgue measure zero such that every $f\in\mathcal G_{\mathrm{gen}}$ has a
unique Boolean minimizer, $d_\star(f)\le n$ is well defined, and at every
order $d_\star(f)\le d\le n$ the moment relaxation has the unique optimum
$b_d(x_\star)b_d(x_\star)^\top$ and an optimal certificate of rank $N_d-1$.
Consequently, for every such $f$, every such order $d$, and every fixed
admissible parameter choice, the conclusions of
\cref{thm:boolean-fixed,cor:admm,cor:pdhg} hold: the three splitting
methods converge eventually linearly from every initialization.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
\Cref{prop:simultaneous} (\cref{app:ambient}) constructs $\mathcal G_{\mathrm{gen}}$ as above;
for $f\in\mathcal G_{\mathrm{gen}}$ and $d\in[d_\star(f),n]$ the hypotheses of
\cref{thm:boolean-fixed} hold and \cref{sec:algorithms} applies; no
parameter-dependent exceptional set is needed, because the modulus
argument of \cref{sec:boolean} covers every fixed $\sigma>0$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- head-3: body_md -->
First, a \emph{quantitative} rigidity: our proof produces $\mu_{\mathrm{nor}}$,
$c_\sigma$, and $t_\sigma$ by compactness; we conjecture that for the
Boolean class they admit explicit instance-parameter bounds---the missing
step from rate separation to complexity separation. Second, \emph{beyond
the Boolean quotient}: identify the weakest algebraic structure under which
one strictly complementary certificate rigidifies the entire residual
field; constrained binary hierarchies with localizing matrices are the
natural first target. We believe residual-field rigidity is the right
abstraction for exact relaxations whose certificates, rather than
solutions, are degenerate, and we hope it extends well beyond the Boolean
cube.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:encoding}
The matrices $D_1,\ldots,D_{m-1}$ form a basis of $\ker(\mathcal G_d)=\mathcal H_d^\perp$;
the operator $\mathcal A_d$ has full row rank; and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:encoding-identities}
 \{X:\mathcal A_dX=e_1\}=I_N+\mathcal T_d,\qquad
 \ker(\mathcal A_d)=\mathcal T_d,\qquad
 \operatorname{ran}(\mathcal A_d^*)=\mathcal R_d,\qquad
 \mathcal A_d^\dagger e_1=I_N .
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The $F_{A,B}$ form a basis of $\mathbb S^N$. Within one XOR class, $\mathcal G_d$ maps
each basis element to the same monomial, so its kernel in that class
consists of coefficient vectors summing to zero, with basis given by
differences from the reference pair; distinct classes have disjoint
supports. This proves the kernel assertion and independence of the $D_i$.
Since $\mathcal G_d(E_{\emptyset,\emptyset})=1$, the normalization row is not in
their span, so $\mathcal A_d$ has full row rank. The equalities $\mathcal A_dX=e_1$ state
that the entries of $X$ are constant on each XOR class with common
diagonal equal to one, which is the affine-set identity; its direction space
is $\mathcal T_d$, and orthogonal complementation gives the row space. Finally
$I_N\in\mathcal R_d$ (matrices in $\mathcal T_d$ have zero diagonal) and $\mathcal A_dI_N=e_1$,
so $I_N$ is the unique feasible point of $\operatorname{ran}(\mathcal A_d^*)$, namely
$\mathcal A_d^\dagger e_1$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:slack-trace}
Every $S\in\mathcal S_\star$ satisfies $\operatorname{tr} S=f_\emptyset-f_\star$; consequently
$\mathcal S_\star$ is compact.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
$\operatorname{tr} S=\langle A_\emptyset,S\rangle$ is the constant coefficient of $\mathcal G_d(S)=f-f_\star$,
which is $f_\emptyset-f_\star$. The set is closed, and
$\lVert S\rVert_F\le\operatorname{tr} S$ for PSD matrices, so it is bounded.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By the constant-diagonal identity \eqref{eq:transverse-intersection},
$\ker(\mathcal A_d)\cap\mathbb S(V)^\perp=\mathcal T_d\cap\mathbb S(V)^\perp=\{0\}$, so $\mathcal A_d$ is
injective on the $N$-dimensional tangent space
\eqref{eq:nondeg-definition}; hence nondegeneracy holds if and only if
$m=N$.
For the dimension formula, $\mathcal R_d+\mathbb S(V)=\mathbb S^N$ because the orthogonal
complement of the sum is $\mathcal T_d\cap\mathbb S(V)^\perp=\{0\}$; therefore
\[
 \dim L=\dim\bigl(\mathcal R_d\cap\mathbb S(V)\bigr)
 =m+\frac{(N-1)N}2-\frac{N(N+1)}2=m-N,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:dimL} by \eqref{eq:Ad}. At $d=1$, $N=n+1$ and
$|\mathcal I_2|=1+n+\binom n2$, so
$m=\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}2-\binom n2-n-1+1=n+1=N$. For $d\ge2$, the two Gram
vectors $w_1=e_\emptyset-a\,e_{\{i,j\}}$ and $w_2=e_{\{i\}}-a\,e_{\{j\}}$ of
\cref{sec:degeneracy} are orthogonal to $u$ and represent the same
polynomial \eqref{eq:same-poly}, so
$w_1w_1^\top-w_2w_2^\top$ is a nonzero element of
$\mathbb S(V)\cap\mathcal H_d^\perp=L$ by \eqref{eq:boolean-L}; hence $\dim L\ge1$ and
$m>N$. For the final clause, \cref{lem:slack-slice} gives
$\mathcal S_\star=(S_{\mathrm{sc}}+L)\cap\mathbb S_+(V)$ with $S_{\mathrm{sc}}$ positive definite on $V$, so a
relative neighborhood of $S_{\mathrm{sc}}$ in $S_{\mathrm{sc}}+L$ stays inside $\mathcal S_\star$; hence
$\operatorname{aff}\mathcal S_\star=S_{\mathrm{sc}}+L$ and $\dim\mathcal S_\star=\dim L=m-N$.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- head-4: body_md -->
If $E\subseteq\mathbb R^p$ is a subspace with orthogonal projector $P_E$, then
$\mathbb S(E):=\{H\in\mathbb S^p:H=P_EHP_E\}$ and
$\mathbb S_+(E):=\mathbb S(E)\cap\mathbb S_+^p$; the complement $\mathbb S(E)^\perp$ is taken in
$\mathbb S^p$. For a nonempty closed convex set $C$ and $z\in C$,
\[
 T_C(z):=\{h:\exists t_k\downarrow0,\ z_k\in C,\ (z_k-z)/t_k\to h\},
 \qquad
 N_C(z):=\{h:\langle h,w-z\rangle\le0\ \forall w\in C\},
\]
and $N_C(z):=\emptyset$ for $z\notin C$; $\operatorname{ri}$, $\operatorname{aff}$, and $\operatorname{cl}$ denote
relative interior, affine hull, and closure. Vectors take the Euclidean
norm $\lVert \cdot\rVert_2$, and $\lVert \cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}$ is the operator norm.
Directional derivatives are
$F'(z;h):=\lim_{t\downarrow0}(F(z+th)-F(z))/t$, positively homogeneous in
$h$ but not necessarily linear. For a linear map $B:E_1\to E_2$ between
Euclidean spaces with $E_1\ne\{0\}$, set
$\sigma_{\min}(B):=\min_{\|h\|=1}\|Bh\|$; it is positive if and only if $B$ is
injective, and
$|\sigma_{\min}(B_1)-\sigma_{\min}(B_2)|\le\lVert B_1-B_2\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:drs}
$T_{\sigma,1}$ is the DR map, with the PSD projection first, for
$F(X)=\langle C,X\rangle+\iota_{\{\mathcal A X=b\}}(X)$ and $G=\iota_{\mathbb S_+^p}$. Its fixed
points decode KKT points via $X=\Pi_+(Z)$, $S=-\sigma^{-1}\Pi_-(Z)$. In
particular, if the primal optimizer $X_\star$ is unique, then
$\mathcal Z_\star:=\operatorname{Fix} T_{\sigma,1}=\{X_\star-\sigma S:S\in\mathcal S_\star\}$, where $\mathcal S_\star$ is
the optimal slack set.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The affine proximal map is
$\operatorname{prox}_{\sigma F}(W)=X_b+\mathsf P^\perp(W-\sigma C)$. Substituting it
into $Z^+=Z+\operatorname{prox}_{\sigma F}(2\Pi_+(Z)-Z)-\Pi_+(Z)$ gives
\eqref{eq:res-components}; this is the standard DR
construction~\cite{eckstein92mp-drs,bauschke17springer-convex-analysis-hilbert-spaces}.
The Moreau decomposition of $Z$ gives $X:=\Pi_+(Z)\succeq0$,
$S:=-\sigma^{-1}\Pi_-(Z)\succeq0$, and $XS=0$. The two orthogonal equations
$r^{\parallel}=r^{\perp}_\sigma=0$ are equivalent to $\mathcal A X=b$ and
$C-S\in\operatorname{ran}(\mathcal A^*)$, hence to KKT feasibility plus complementarity;
surjectivity of $\mathcal A$ makes the multiplier unique. Conversely any KKT pair
$(X,S)$ gives the fixed point $Z=X-\sigma S$. When the primal optimizer is
unique, this gives the fixed-point identity stated in \cref{lem:drs}.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:slack-slice}
Under \cref{ass:one-sided},
$\mathcal S_\star=(S_{\mathrm{sc}}+L)\cap\mathbb S_+(V)$ with $L:=\mathcal R\cap\mathbb S(V)=\operatorname{span}(\mathcal S_\star-\mathcal S_\star)$.
Consequently $\mathcal Z_\star$ is compact and convex with affine direction space $L$,
$S_{\mathrm{sc}}\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal S_\star$, and every $S\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal S_\star$ is positive definite on $V$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Any optimal slack is complementary to $X_\star$, hence supported on $V$;
subtracting its dual equality from that of $S_{\mathrm{sc}}$ shows
$S-S_{\mathrm{sc}}\in\mathcal R$, proving $\mathcal S_\star\subseteq(S_{\mathrm{sc}}+L)\cap\mathbb S_+(V)$. Conversely
$S=S_{\mathrm{sc}}+H\succeq0$ with $H\in\mathcal R\cap\mathbb S(V)$ is dual feasible and
complementary to the feasible $X_\star$, hence optimal. For every such $H$,
$S_{\mathrm{sc}}\pm tH$ is positive definite on $V$ for small $t>0$, so these
directions span the affine hull, $L=\operatorname{span}(\mathcal S_\star-\mathcal S_\star)$, and
$S_{\mathrm{sc}}\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal S_\star$. If $S\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal S_\star$, extending the segment from $S_{\mathrm{sc}}$
through $S$ slightly stays in $\mathcal S_\star$, so $S$ is a convex combination with
positive weight on $S_{\mathrm{sc}}$ and is positive definite on $V$. The assertions
for $\mathcal Z_\star=X_\star-\sigma\mathcal S_\star$ follow from the affine bijection.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:sylvester}
Set $X_U:=B_U^\top X_\star B_U=\Lambda_U\succ0$ and
$\bar S_V:=B_V^\top\bar SB_V\succeq0$. For $H\in\mathbb S^p$, put
$H^0_{UU}:=B_U^\top HB_U$, $M:=B_U^\top HB_V$, $H^0_{VV}:=B_V^\top HB_V$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coarse-derivative}
 [B_U\ B_V]^\top\,\Pi_+'(\bar Z;H)\,[B_U\ B_V]
 =\begin{bmatrix}H^0_{UU}&Y\\Y^\top&
 O_K\Pi_+(O_K^\top H^0_{VV}O_K)O_K^\top\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 X_UY+\sigma Y\bar S_V=X_UM .
\end{equation}
The operator $\mathscr L_{\bar S}:Y\mapsto X_UY+\sigma Y\bar S_V$ is
invertible with
$\|\mathscr L_{\bar S}^{-1}\|_{F\to F}\le a_U^{-1}$,
$a_U:=\lambda_{\min}(X_U)>0$, uniformly in $\bar S\in\mathcal S_\star$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
In eigencoordinates the Sylvester equation reads
$(\xi_i+\sigma s_j)\widetilde Y_{ij}=\xi_i\widetilde M_{ij}$, allowing
$s_j=0$ on $K$; this reproduces the $UK$ and $UJ$ blocks of
\eqref{eq:positive-deriv}, and the $VV$ block is the displayed
$K$-embedding. For every $Y$,
$\langle Y,\mathscr L_{\bar S}Y\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(Y^\top X_UY)+\sigma\operatorname{tr}(Y^\top Y\bar S_V)
\ge a_U\lVert Y\rVert_F^2$, and Cauchy--Schwarz gives

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:cones}
Under \cref{ass:one-sided}, at $\bar Z=X_\star-\sigma\bar S$,
\begin{align}
 T_{\mathcal S_\star}(\bar S)&=\{H\in L:Q_K^\top HQ_K\succeq0\},\label{eq:slack-tangent}\\
 T_{\mathcal Z_\star}(\bar Z)&=\{H\in L:\widetilde H_{KK}\preceq0\},\label{eq:shadow-tangent}\\
 N_{\mathcal Z_\star}(\bar Z)&=L^\perp+\mathcal K(\bar S),\qquad
 \mathcal K(\bar S)=\{W\in\mathbb S_+^p:\operatorname{ran}(W)\subseteq K\},\label{eq:shadow-normal}
\end{align}
where the sum is a Minkowski sum and all cones are taken in $\mathbb S^p$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
For the cone supported on $V$, the tangent and ambient normal formulas are
\[
 T_{\mathbb S_+(V)}(\bar S)=\{H\in\mathbb S(V):Q_K^\top HQ_K\succeq0\},
 \qquad
 N_{\mathbb S_+(V)}(\bar S)=\mathbb S(V)^\perp-\mathcal K(\bar S).
\]
For completeness: the tangent condition is necessary by evaluating feasible
secants on vectors of $\ker(\bar S)\cap V$; it is sufficient because adding
$\varepsilon P_K$ to a direction makes its $KK$ block positive definite, after
which the Schur complement shows $\bar S+t(H+\varepsilon P_K)$ is PSD for small
$t>0$, and $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ closes the cone. The normal formula is the
polar: the free $KJ$ and $JJ$ tangent blocks force the corresponding normal
blocks to vanish, and the polar of the PSD $KK$ cone is its negative.
For the affine slice $\mathcal S_\star=(S_{\mathrm{sc}}+L)\cap\mathbb S_+(V)$, the same approximation
can be performed \emph{inside $L$}: replace $H$ by $H+\varepsilon(S_{\mathrm{sc}}-\bar S)$,
whose $KK$ block is positive definite because $S_{\mathrm{sc}}\succ0$ on $V$; this
proves \eqref{eq:slack-tangent}. The relative Slater point $S_{\mathrm{sc}}$ justifies
the normal-cone intersection rule~\cite{rockafellar15book-convex-analysis},
so
$N_{\mathcal S_\star}(\bar S)=L^\perp+\mathbb S(V)^\perp-\mathcal K(\bar S)=L^\perp-\mathcal K(\bar S)$,
using $L\subseteq\mathbb S(V)$. Finally the affine bijection
$S\mapsto X_\star-\sigma S$ maps tangents by $-\sigma$ and normals by
$-\sigma^{-1}$; positive scalars do not change cones, and the sign flip
gives \eqref{eq:shadow-tangent}--\eqref{eq:shadow-normal}.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:psd-cauchy}
If $S\succeq0$ and $B_E,B_F$ are orthonormal frames for orthogonal
subspaces, then
$\lVert B_E^\top SB_F\rVert_F^2\le\operatorname{tr}(B_E^\top SB_E)\,\operatorname{tr}(B_F^\top SB_F)$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The block is $(S^{1/2}B_E)^\top(S^{1/2}B_F)$, so
$\lVert B_E^\top SB_F\rVert_F\le\lVert S^{1/2}B_E\rVert_F\,\lVert S^{1/2}B_F\rVert_F$; square and
use $\lVert S^{1/2}B_E\rVert_F^2=\operatorname{tr}(B_E^\top SB_E)$, likewise for $B_F$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:pv-bound}
Let $W\in\mathbb S_+^p$ and let $P_V$ be the orthogonal projector onto a
subspace $V\subseteq\mathbb R^p$. Then $W\succeq aP_V$ for some $a>0$ if and only
if $V\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(W)$; in that case, when $W\ne0$, one may take $a=\lambda_+(W)$, the
smallest positive eigenvalue of $W$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Necessity: if $W\succeq aP_V$ and $z\in\ker(W)$, then
$0=z^\top Wz\ge a\lVert P_Vz\rVert_2^2$, so $P_Vz=0$; hence $\ker(W)\perp V$,
i.e.\ $V\subseteq\ker(W)^\perp=\operatorname{ran}(W)$. Sufficiency: for every $x$,
$x^\top Wx\ge\lambda_+(W)\lVert P_{\operatorname{ran}(W)}x\rVert_2^2$, and $V\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(W)$
gives $\lVert P_{\operatorname{ran}(W)}x\rVert_2\ge\lVert P_Vx\rVert_2$, so
$x^\top Wx\ge\lambda_+(W)\,x^\top P_Vx$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:pv}
The condition $V\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(W)$ in \cref{lem:pv-bound} cannot be replaced
by ``$W$ is positive definite on $V$'', which only says
$V\cap\ker(W)=\{0\}$. For
$W=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and
$V=\operatorname{span}\{e_1\}$, the quadratic form $(te_1)^\top W(te_1)=t^2$ is
positive on $V$, yet $\det(W-aP_V)=-a<0$ for every $a>0$, so no inequality
$W\succeq aP_V$ holds. In \cref{lem:normal-bound} the stronger condition is
available because $\operatorname{ran}(S_{\mathrm{sc}})=V$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:normal-bound}
Let $a_s:=\lambda_{\min}(B_V^\topS_{\mathrm{sc}} B_V)>0$. For every $\bar S\in\mathcal S_\star$
and every decomposition $H=Y+W$ with $Y\in L^\perp$, $W\in\mathcal K(\bar S)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-normal-bound}
 \lVert W\rVert_F\le \frac{\lVert S_{\mathrm{sc}}-\bar S\rVert_F}{a_s}\,\lVert H\rVert_F,
 \qquad
 \lVert Y\rVert_F\le\Bigl(1+\frac{\lVert S_{\mathrm{sc}}-\bar S\rVert_F}{a_s}\Bigr)\lVert H\rVert_F .
\end{equation}
These bounds are uniform over $\bar S\in\mathcal S_\star$; in particular
$L^\perp\cap\mathcal K(\bar S)=\{0\}$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Since $W\succeq0$, $\operatorname{tr} W\ge\lVert W\rVert_F$. Because $W$ is supported on $V$ and
$S_{\mathrm{sc}}\succeq a_sP_V$---by \cref{lem:pv-bound}, since $\operatorname{ran}(S_{\mathrm{sc}})=V$ and
$a_s$ is the smallest positive eigenvalue of $S_{\mathrm{sc}}$---and because
$\bar SW=0$ and $S_{\mathrm{sc}}-\bar S\in L$,
\[
 a_s\lVert W\rVert_F\le a_s\operatorname{tr} W\le\langle W,S_{\mathrm{sc}}\rangle
 =\langle W,S_{\mathrm{sc}}-\bar S\rangle
 =\langle H,S_{\mathrm{sc}}-\bar S\rangle
 \le\lVert H\rVert_F\,\lVert S_{\mathrm{sc}}-\bar S\rVert_F.
\]
The bound on $Y=H-W$ follows, compactness of $\mathcal S_\star$ bounds the factor, and
$H=0$ gives the intersection statement.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:stable-directions}
For $\bar S\in\mathcal S_\star$ and $0<\varepsilon\le1$, set
$\bar S_\varepsilon:=(1-\varepsilon)\bar S+\varepsilon S_{\mathrm{sc}}$ and

\end{equation}

$\bar S_\varepsilon\succ0$ on $V$ and \cref{lem:slack-slice}. For the convergence,

If the full-normal modulus were zero, compactness would allow endpoints
$\bar Z_k=X_\star-\sigma\bar S_k$ and unit normals

$\bar Z_k\to\bar Z$, $H_k\to H$, and

\begin{equation}\label{eq:vanishing-components}
 \mathsf P E_k\to0,\qquad \mathsf P^\perp F_k\to0 ,
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 1.} The ambient $V$-supported block $E_k^V:=P_VE_kP_V$ is PSD by

$\Pi_-'(\bar Z_k;W_k)=-R_k$,
where $R_k$ has only $UJ_k$ and $J_kU$ blocks and
$(\widetilde R_k)_{UJ_k}=-\Omega_k\circ(\mathbf1-\Omega_k)
\circ(\widetilde H_k)_{UJ_k}$. By \eqref{eq:EF-identities},
$\lVert R_k\rVert_F^2\le\langle E_k,F_k\rangle\to0$. Since $\mathsf P W_k\to0$ as well,

$\bar S_{k,\varepsilon_k}\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal S_\star$.

$\lVert \delta_\sigma'(X_\star-\sigma\bar S_{k,\varepsilon_k};W_k)\rVert_F
\ge\mu_{\mathrm{ri}}\lVert W_k\rVert_F$, a contradiction. Thus
$E_k=W_k+E_k^V\to0$.

\emph{Step 3.} Now \eqref{eq:vanishing-components} gives
$\operatorname{dist}_F(H_k,\mathcal R)\to0$. Also $\operatorname{dist}_F(H_k,\mathbb S(V))\to0$: the $UU$ block of

\cref{lem:sylvester} gives
$M_k=Y_k^c+\sigma X_U^{-1}Y_k^c(\bar S_k)_V$, with
$M_k=B_U^\top H_kB_V$ and $Y_k^c=B_U^\top E_kB_V$,
and the right side tends to zero because $(\bar S_k)$ is bounded and
$Y_k^c\to0$. The fixed subspaces $\mathcal R$ and $\mathbb S(V)$ therefore give
$\operatorname{dist}_F(H_k,L)\to0$: both distance functions are invariant under
translations along $L$, so the claim reduces to positivity of the minimum
of $\operatorname{dist}_F(\cdot,\mathcal R)+\operatorname{dist}_F(\cdot,\mathbb S(V))$ on the unit sphere of
$L^\perp$, and a zero there would produce a unit vector of $L^\perp$ inside
$\mathcal R\cap\mathbb S(V)=L$.

\emph{Step 4.} Choose $\ell_k\in L$ with $H_k-\ell_k\to0$. By
\cref{prop:cones,lem:normal-bound}, write $H_k=Y_k+G_k$ with

$\langle H_k,Y_k\rangle=\langle H_k-\ell_k,Y_k\rangle\to0$. Because $G_k$ is PSD and supported
on $K_k$, spectral coordinates give
\[

the inequality uses $M\preceq\Pi_+(M)$ and $G_k\succeq0$ and does not
require commutativity. Since $E_k^V\to0$ and $(G_k)$ is bounded,
$\limsup_k\langle H_k,G_k\rangle\le0$. Hence
$1=\lVert H_k\rVert_F^2=\langle H_k,Y_k\rangle+\langle H_k,G_k\rangle$ has limit superior at most
zero, a contradiction.

 \qquad\text{so}\quad \mathsf P E_k\to0,\quad \mathsf P^\perp F_k\to0,

identification with $\Pi_+'(\bar Z;H)$ has been made.

$\langle E_k,F_k\rangle\to0$, we get $\langle X_\star,S_k\rangle=o(t_k^2)$. As $X_\star\succ0$

\[
