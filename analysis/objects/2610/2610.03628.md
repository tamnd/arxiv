<!-- introtheorem-1: body_md -->
\label{T: intro gen diff}
    Let ${\mathcal K}$ be an expansion of a real closed field with a weakly o-minimal theory. Let $f: I\to K$ be a definable function. Then, away from finitely many points in $I$, $f'(a)$ exists in $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$: We say that  $f$ is generically $nv$-differentiable.

    In particular, if ${\mathcal K}$ is definably Cauchy complete (namely has no definable nonvaluational cuts) then every definable function is generically differentiable (in the usual sense).

<!-- introtheorem-2: body_md -->
Let ${\mathcal K}$ be an expansion of a real closed field $K$ whose theory is weakly o-minimal.  Let $f:K\to \mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$ be a definable function. Then: 
   \begin{enumerate}
       \item If $\mathrm{Im}(f)$ consists of nonvaluational cuts and $f$ is locally constant  then $Im(f)$ is finite.
       \item If $\mathrm{Im}(f)$ consists of valuational cuts then $f$ is locally constant. 
      \end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{D: Stab}
Given a cut ${\mathcal C}=(C_1,C_2)$ in a linearly ordered group $(M,<,+)$, the stabilizer group of ${\mathcal C}$, also known as \emph{the invariance group of ${\mathcal C}$} is  $G(C_1)=G({\mathcal C})=\{\delta\in M: \delta+C_1=C_1\}$.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{D: cuts}
	   A cut ${\mathcal C}=(C_1,C_2)$ is {\em valuational} if $G({\mathcal C})\neq \{0\}$ and {\em nonvaluational}, otherwise.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let ${\mathcal M}$ be a weakly o-minimal expansion of an ordered group. The \textit{definable nonvaluational completion}, denoted $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$ is the subset of $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$ consisting of all definable nonvaluational (possibly rational) cuts in $M$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Some historical and terminology-related remarks are in order. It seems that nonvaluational cuts in ordered abelian groups were first studied by Baer in \cite{Baer}; these were called by Baer: \emph{Dedekindean cuts}. Cohen and Goffman constructed the nonvaluational completion \cite{CoGo} by means of ``Cauchy'' sequences indexed by ordinals and showed that it is a group (as was done in a general context by Bourbaki, \cite[Chapter II, \S3, no. 7]{Bourbaki}). Independently, Scott constructed the completion for any ordered field by a different method \cite{ScottCompletion}. 

    Note that all their different constructions are for general cuts, i.e. not necessarily definable.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- fact-1: body_md -->
\label{F: M*}
    Let ${\mathcal M}$ be a weakly o-minimal expansion of a linearly ordered group. 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $(M,<)$ naturally embeds in $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4mu(\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv},<)$ as a dense subset. 
        \item The group operations on $M$ extend continuously to $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$. 
        \item If $M$ expands a (real closed) field                              
        then $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$ is an ordered field extension of $M$ and it is a real closed field. 
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end fact-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\widehat M$ be the Cauchy completion of $M$ as defined in \cite{CoGo}. (1) and (2) follow from \cite[Section 2]{CoGo}, once we restrict from $\widehat M$ to $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$. Although the authors do not prove that $M$ is dense in its Cauchy completion, it is standard to verify and noted in the historical remarks of \cite{ScottCompletion}

    For (3), it is convenient to use the results in \cite{ScottCompletion}, namely Theorems 1 and 2 there. $M$ is dense in $\widehat M$ and as $M$ is real closed then so is $\widehat M$. To see that $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$ is real closed, note that if $p(x)$ is a polynomial over $ \mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$ and $a\in \widehat M$ is a root of $p$ then, by taking derivatives, we may replace $p$ by a polynomial $q(x)$ over $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$ which vanishes at $a$, while its derivative does not.
But then, the cut $a$ is an endpoint of one of the convex components of $\{x\in M: q(x)>0\}$, thus it is a definable cut, so $a\in \mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$. It follows that $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$ is also real closed.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- fact-2: body_md -->
\label{F:monotonicity}\cite[Theorem 3]{MocTan}
    Let $M$ be weakly o-minimal but not necessarily dense and $f: M\to D$ be a definable partial function, where $D$ is any definable linear order. Then away from finitely many points $f$ is locally constant, locally strictly increasing or locally strictly decreasing.
<!-- end fact-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{L: images are WOM}
    Let $f: M\to \mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$ be a definable partial function, with $\mathrm{Im}(f)$ infinite. 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $\mathrm{Im}(f)$ is weakly o-minimal (possibly, not densely ordered), with respect to some definable linear order.
        \item There exists a finite subset $F\subseteq \mathrm{Im}(f)$ such that, locally around each point of $\mathrm{Im}(f)\setminus F$, this order agrees with either the $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$-induced order  or with its inverse. 
        \item There is an interval $I_0\subseteq \operatorname{dom}(f)$ such that $f\upharpoonright I_0$ is weakly monotone with convex fibers and $f(I_0)$  infinite.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $I$ be the domain of $f$. Let $I_0\subseteq I$ consist of all those $x\in I$ such that $x$ belongs to the leftmost convex component of $f^{-1}(f(x))$. Then $f(I_0)=f(I)$ and the fibers of $f\upharpoonright I_0$ are convex. If $E$ is the associated (convex) equivalence relation $xEy \Leftrightarrow f(x)=f(y)$, then $f(I)=f(I_0)$ is in definable bijection with $I_0/E$, inducing a linear order on it, whose weak o-minimality follows from that of ${\mathcal M}$. Let $\trianglelefteq$ be the order which $I_0/E$ induces on $\mathrm{Im}(f)$ and let $\leq$ be the order induced from $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muM\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$.

    Consider the identity map $(\mathrm{Im}(f),\trianglelefteq)\to (\mathrm{Im}(f),\leq)$. By Fact \ref{F:monotonicity} applied to the weakly o-minimal structure $(\mathrm{Im}(f),\trianglelefteq)$, this map, away from finitely many points, is locally strictly increasing or decreasing (it cannot be constant by injectivity). The result follows.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Recall that given an $\aleph_1$-saturated structure ${\mathcal M}$, a definable set $S \subseteq M^n$  is
		{\em stably embedded} if for every definable $X \subseteq  M^ {nk}$  the set $X \cap S^k$  is definable using only parameters from $S$. Thus, in this case the language of the ${\mathcal M}$-induced structure on $S$ does not change when we move to elementary extensions.  
        
        
       
		Our interest below is with definable sets $D$ with an induced linear ordering with respect to which the ${\mathcal M}$-induced structure  is weakly o-minimal. 
		However, weakly o-minimal definable sets need not be stably embedded. In fact, even if ${\mathcal M}$ itself is weakly o-minimal and  $D$ is an interval (thus a weakly o-minimal sort), $D$ is not necessarily stably embedded. <!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{Johnson convexity}
    For $I\subseteq K$ an open interval, let $f:I\to K$ be a definable function which is nowhere locally affine. Then $f$ is locally convex (or concave) at any generic point.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Absorbing parameters into the language, we may assume that $f$ is $\0$-definable. 
                                                                                                             
    Let $a$ be a generic element in $I$. By our assumption on $f$ and weak o-minimality, for every $x\in I$, the intersection of the affine line connecting the points $(a,f(a))$ and $(x,f(x))$ with the graph of $f$ is finite. Thus, there is an  $\epsilon>0$, such that for all $x\in (a,a+\epsilon)$, the affine line through the points $(a,f(a))$ and $(x,f(x))$ does not intersect the graph of $f$ in the interval $(a,a+\epsilon)$.  By INP, we may assume that $\dim(a/\epsilon)=1$. It follows that there exists an interval $I_0\ni a$, such that for all $x,y\in I_0$, the affine line connecting the points $(x,f(x))$ and $(y,f(y))$ intersects the graph of $f$ only at $x$ and $y$. In particular, $m(a,-)$ is injective on $I_0$.
    

    Consider, in an elementary extension,  the convex sets  $J_0(a) =m(a,a^-)$ and $J_1(a)=m(a,a^+)$ (the images under $m(a,x)$ of $a^+$ and $a^-$). By our assumption on $I_0$, these two sets are disjoint. Thus, either $J_0(a)<J_1(a)$ or $J_0(a)>J_1(a)$. For concreteness, assume the former. 
    This implies that there is an interval $I_1\ni a$ such that for all $x,z\in I_1$, if $x<a<z$ then
    $m(x,a)<m(a,z)$. By INP, we may assume that $a$ is generic over the parameters defining $I_1$. Hence, $a$ is generic in \[\{y\in I_1: \forall x,z\in I_1(x<y<z\to m(x,y)<m(y,z)\}.\] 
    
    
    By weak o-minimality, there is some open interval $I_2$, $a\in I_2\subseteq I_1$, such that for all $x<y<z$ in $I_2$, $m(x,y)<m(y,z)$, so $f$ is convex there.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{L:f' constant is locally affine}
    Let $I$ be an open interval and $f:I\to K$ a definable function.                                                                                                                         

                    
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item If $f'_+(x)\equiv c\in \mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$  on $I$ then $c\in K$ and away from finitely many points, $f$ is locally of the form $f(x)=cx+b$, for $b\in K$. The same is true for $f_-'(x)$.
        \item there are at most finitely many points such that $f_+'(x)= \pm \infty$.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Assume that $f_+'(x)\equiv c\in \mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$  on $I$ (the proof for $f_-'(x)$ is the same).
It is sufficient to show that there is a subinterval $J\subseteq I$ on which $f$ is $K$-affine with slope $c$.

If $f$ is locally constant, then $c=0$ and there is nothing to show; so assume that $f$ is locally strictly monotone. By Proposition \ref{Johnson convexity}, we may assume that $f$ is convex: For all $x<y<z$: $m(x,y)<m(y,z)$. It follows that for all $x<y<z$: 
\[
    m(x,y)< m(x,z)<m(y,z).
\]

This implies that $f'_+(x)\le m(x,z)$ and  $m(x,z)\leq f'_+(y)$. We conclude that for all $x,z$ we have $c\le m(x,z)\le c$, i.e., $m(x,z)$ is constant, for $x<z$ in $I$. But $m(x,z)\in K$, so $c\in K$, and $f$ is $K$-affine, with slope $c$.

(1) follows from the above. As for (2), if, for example, $f_+'(x)=\infty$ on infinitely many points then by weak o-minimality it is constant and equal to $\infty$ on an interval, contradicting (1).

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{T:n=1 gen-dif}

<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Assume everything is definable over $\emptyset$.
    Fix some generic $a\in \operatorname{dom}(f)$. We have to show that $f$ is nv-differentiable at $a$. If $f$ is locally constant at $a$, there is nothing to prove, so we assume that is not the case; i.e. that it is strictly monotone around $a$. 

    The limit $f_+'(a)$ (and $f_-'(a)$) lies in $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$; it cannot be $\pm\infty$ by Lemma \ref{L:f' constant is locally affine}(2) and genericity of $a$.
    
    Next, we note that  $f'_+(a), f'_-(a)\in \mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$.     So both lateral derivatives are nonvaluational (possibly rational). If $f'_+(a)=f'_-(a)$, we are done. Towards a contradiction and without loss of generality, assume that $f'_-(a)<f'_+(a)$. Note that for no $f'_-(a)<d<f'_+(a)$ can we get $\dim(a/d)=1$. Indeed, if such a $d$ existed we would get that for the function $g(x)=f(x)-dx$, $g_+'(a)>0$ and $g_-'(a)<0$.  But, since $a$ is generic over the parameters defining $g$, the function $g$ must be continuous and either constant, strictly increasing or strictly decreasing around $a$. Neither option is compatible with both  $g_+'(a)>0$ and $g_-'(a)<0$, so we have reached a contradiction. But then  for all $f'_-(a)<d<f'_+(a)$ we have $a\in \operatorname{dcl}(d)$, which, since ${\mathcal K}$ satisfies exchange, is absurd.

By Lemma \ref{L: F in nv is lsi to K}, there exists an infinite definable subset $S\subseteq F$ and a definable injection $S\hookrightarrow K$; in the language of \cite{HaHaPeVF}: $F$ is locally strongly internal to $K$. Also, in the language of \cite{HaHaPeVF}, $K$ is an SW-uniformity (\cite[Definition 3.2]{HaHaPeVF}). The only difference, not allowing us to apply \cite[Theorem 4.21]{HaHaPeVF} directly is that $K$ does not satisfy (Gen-Dif) per-se, i.e. the derivatives take values in $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$ and not $K$. Nevertheless, the proof structure still applies almost verbatim. We highlight the main differences.

    In the above terminology, for a definable field $F$ that is locally strongly internal to an SW-uniformity, the proof of \cite[Theorem 4.21]{HaHaPeVF} produces a type-definable additive subgroup $\nu$ of $(F,+)$ (the so called infinitesimal subgroup). The group $\nu$ is invariant under scalar multiplication in the following sense: For $\widehat {\mathcal K}\succ {\mathcal K}$ a sufficiently saturated extension and $a\in F({\mathcal K})$, $a\cdot \nu(\widehat {\mathcal K})=\nu(\widehat {\mathcal K})$. Furthermore, $\nu(\widehat {\mathcal K})$ has a structure of a $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$-differential group  (see \cite[Proposition 4.19]{HaHaPeVF}), 
                                                                
    specifically, for $a\in F({\mathcal K})$, the Jacobian matrix of the map $a\mapsto a\cdot x$, at $0$, is a matrix with coefficients in $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$, i.e. an element of $M_n(\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv})$ for some $n$. The chain-rule (which is applicable here as well) implies that there is an embedding of $F$, as a subring, into $M_n(\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv})$. One may definably embed $K$ into $M_n(\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv})$ as well, as scalar matrices. Inside $M_n(\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv})$, $K\cap F\subseteq K$ is a definable subfield, but by dimension considerations, $K$ has no proper subfields hence $K\subseteq F$. Since $K$ is real closed and $F$ has finite dp-rank, $F$ must be either $K$ or $K^{alg}$.

<!-- lemma-15: body_md -->
\label{L:curves and Diag v2}
Assume that $I\subseteq K$ is a convex set and $f:I \to K$ a convex nv-differentiable function. Assume further that $f'(I)$ is dense in $\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(f'(I))$. Then, for every $d\in m(I\times I)$ outside a finite set, there exists a unique $c\in \mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$ such that $(c,c)\in \operatorname{cl}_{\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu}(L_d)$. Furthermore, $c$ is nonvaluational, and for $(x,y)\in L_d$ close enough to $(c,c)$, we have $x<c<y$.

<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
The convexity of $f$, together with the non affiness, implies that for all $a<b\in I$,   $f'(a)<m(a,b)<f'(b)$. Thus, $m(I\times I)$ is contained in $\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(f'(I))$ and  is dense in it.

For $e>0$ let $D_e=\{m(x,y):x<y\in I,\, y-x<e\}$. As above, $D_e$ is contained in $\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(f'(I))$ and dense  in it, and thus $\operatorname{cl}_K(D_e)$ consists of a single convex component which equals $\operatorname{cl}_{\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu}(f'(I))\cap K$. Also, by definition of derivative, $f'(I)\subseteq \operatorname{cl}_{\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu}(D_e)$. Thus, $$\operatorname{cl}_{\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu}(m(I\times I))=\operatorname{cl}_{\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu}(f'(I))=\operatorname{cl}_{\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu}(D_e),$$ for every $e>0$.

It follows that  there is a finite set $F_e$, such that $m(I\times I)=D_e\cup F_e$, and $F_{e'}\supseteq F_e$, for $e'<e$. By weak o-minimality, for $e$ small enough, $F:=F_e$ remains the same. 

Fix $d\in m(I\times I)\setminus F$, and then $d\in D_e$ for all $e>0$.
the convexity of $f$ implies that $L_d^+:=L_d\cap \{(x,y)\in K^2: y>x\}$ is the graph of a strictly decreasing function  (note that $L_d$ is symmetric with respect to the diagonal of $K$). 

For $e>0$, let $$\Delta_e=\{(x,y)\in K^2:|x-y|<e\},$$ a tube around the diagonal, and then $D_e=m(\Delta_e)$, so $L_d\cap \Delta_e\neq \emptyset$, for all $e>0$. It follows that the frontier in $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu^2$ of $L_d$ belongs to the diagonal, $\Delta_{\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu}$,  of $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$. In fact, if $c=\inf_{\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu}(\pi_2(L_d))$ (the projection onto the $y$-coordinate of $L_d$), then $Fr_{\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu}(L_d)\cap \Delta_{\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu}=(c,c)$.

Since $L_d$ is the graph of a strictly decreasing function, it follows from weak o-minimality, that for sufficiently small $e$, $L_e\cap \Delta_e$ is contained in the ``third quadrant'' with respect to $(c,c)$, namely in the set $\{(x,y)\in K^2: x<c<y\}$. In particular, since we have such $x<c<y$, with $y-x<e$,   for all sufficiently small $e>0$, it follows that $c$ is nonvaluational.

<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
A definable function $f:K\to K$ is differentiable at a nonvaluational cut  $c$ if $f$ can be extended continuously to $c$ and $\lim\limits_{x\to c} \frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}$ exists in $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu $.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
It can be shown that for any definable function $f: K\to K$ there is a partition of $K$ into finitely many convex sets $I_1,\dots, I_k$ such that for all $1\le i\le k$ $f|I_i$ can be extended continuously to a function on the convex hull of $I_i$ in $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$ (and into $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$). So the assumption that $f$ can be extended to $c$ is harmless. In the application below, it will be automatic. Also note that, for a fixed $c$, the function $m_f(x,c):K\to K_{nv}$ is definable and therefore the lateral limits, at least, exist as $x$ tends to $c$.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{T: MVT}
    Let $f: I\to K$ be a $\0$-definable, convex ${nv}$-differentiable function,  with $I\subseteq K$ convex.                                                                          

    Then, there is a $\0$-definable partition $I=\bigcup I_j$ into definable  convex sets and a finite set $D\subseteq K$,  such that for each $j$, for all $a<b\in I_j$, if $m(a,b)\notin D$ then there exists $c\in \mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$, $a<c<b$, such that
    $$f'(c)=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}.$$

<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
First, we claim that after partitioning $I$ we may assume that for every $c\in I_{nv}$, either $f$ is strictly increasing on $c^-$ and $c^+$ or $f$ is strictly decreasing on both $c^-$ and $c^+$. Indeed, since $f$ is convex, it is strictly monotone, locally, at generic points of $K$, so by omitting finitely many points we may assume that $f$ is locally monotone at every point of $I$. Define the equivalence relation on $I$: $xEy$ if $f$ is strictly monotone on some open interval containing $[x,y]$. The $E$-classes must be convex subsets of $K$, and  by Theorem \ref{T:no lc into nv v2}, there are only finitely many $E$-classes whose endpoints (in $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$) are nonvaluational. Hence, after removing finitely many points from $I_{nv}$, and partitioning $I$ accordingly,  we may assume, without loss of generality, that $f$ is strictly increasing or decreasing on both $c^-$ and $c^+$, for all $c\in I_{nv}$.

By Corollary \ref{C:partition of nv}, we may partition $f'(I)$ into finitely many sets such that each one is dense in its $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$-convex hull. Using a weak o-minimality, we can therefore partition $I$ into finitely many convex $I_j$, such that on each $I_j$, the image $f'(I_j)$ is dense in $\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(f'(I_j))$, as in the assumption of Lemma \ref{L:curves and Diag v2}. Thus, we assume now that $I$ satisfies these assumptions.

By the same lemma, for all $d\in m(I\times I)$, outside a finite set. there exists a nonvaluational $c$ such that for arbitrarily close $x,y$, with $x<c<y$, we have $m(x,y)=d$. (Note that, geometrically, this means that there are secant lines on the graph of $f$, arbitrarily close to $c$, whose slope is $d$). 


We  claim that away from finitely many cuts $c\in I_{nv}$, $f$ can be extended continuously to $c$, as a function into $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu_{nv}$, and in addition $f'(c)=d$, in the above sense. First note that the lateral limits of $f$ at $c$ exist in $\mkern 4mu\overline{\mkern-4muK\mkern-2mu}\mkern 2mu$ (by weak o-minimality) and must be equal. Indeed, if  the limits are not equal then for $x<c<y$, with $|x-y|\to 0$  we have $m(x,y)\to \infty$, contradicting the assumption that $(c,c)\in \operatorname{cl}(L_d)$.
Similarly, if the limit were a valuational cut, then since $f$ is, say, strictly increasing on both $c^-$ and $c^+$, then $m(x,y)\to \infty$, for $x<c<y$, with $|x-y|\to 0$. There can be at most finitely many $c\in I_{nv}$ where this can happen, by weak o-minimality. 


Thus, $f$ can be continuously extended to $c$ and $f(c)$ (we still call this extension $f$), is nonvaluational. Next we claim that $f'(c)=d$.


By continuity of $f$,  for every $x_1<c$, $m(x_1,c)=\lim\limits_{x_2\to c^-}m(x_1,x_2)$, and also for every $x_2<c$, $m(x_1,x_2)<d=m(x_2,y_2)$, for some $y_2>c$. Hence, $f'(c^-)\leq d$.
Similarly, $f'(c^+)\geq d$. 

By the convexity of $f$, for each $c_1,c_2\in K$, if $c_1<c<c_2$ then $f'(c_1)< f'(c^-)\leq f'(c^+)< f'(c_2)$. Since $f'(I)$ is dense in $\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(f'(I))$ it follows that $f'(c^-)=f'(c^+)=d$.
