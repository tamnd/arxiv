<!-- theoremalphabetic-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:A}
If a reaction network is positively transverse, then 
no bounded trajectory of any associated mass-action system with initial condition in ${\mathbb{R}}^n_{>0}$ has an $\omega$-limit point at the boundary of ${\mathbb{R}}^n_{\geq 0}$.
<!-- end theoremalphabetic-1 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
We let ${\mathbb{R}}_{>0}$ and ${\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}$ denote the sets of positive and nonnegative real numbers, respectively. For a set $B\subseteq {\mathbb{R}}^n$ we let $B^\circ$ denote its relative interior. 
For $a\in {\mathbb{Z}}^\ell_{\geq 0}$  and $x\in {\mathbb{R}}^\ell_{\geq 0}$, we let   $x^a = \prod_{i=1}^\ell x_i^{a_i}$ with the convention that $0^0 = 1$.  For $v,w\in {\mathbb{R}}^n$, $v\cdot w$ refers to their scalar product.
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:bad}
Let $x(t)$ be a  positive trajectory of \eqref{eq:mass},  $Z\subseteq \{1,\dots,n\}$ nonempty, and   $h\colon D\rightarrow {\mathbb{R}}$ be a   function defined on $D\coloneqq \{x\in {\mathbb{R}}^n_{> 0} : \prod_{i\in Z} x_i \neq 1\}$. A point $a_\infty\in \Delta_Z$ is called a \bfemph{negative logarithmic limit} for $(x(t),Z,h)$ if there 
  exists  a sequence $\{t_j\}_{j\in {\mathbb{N}}}\subset {\mathbb{R}}_{>0}$ satisfying
\begin{itemize}
\item $\{t_j\}$ tends to $\infty$ as $j$ tends to $\infty$.
\item $x_i(t_j)<1$ for all $i\in Z$ and $j\in {\mathbb{N}}$.
\item  $\{x(t_j)\}$ converges to a point in ${\mathbb{R}}^n_{\geq 0}$ with zero support $Z$.
\item $\{a(x(t_j))\}$ converges to $a_\infty$.
\item $h(x(t_j)) <0$ for all $j\in {\mathbb{N}}$.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:Wprimepos}
Let $x(t)$ be a bounded positive trajectory of \eqref{eq:mass}, $Z\subseteq \{1,\dots,n\}$ be nonempty, and consider the associated function $g$ from \eqref{eq:ga}. 

If $Z$ is inclusion-maximal among all possible zero supports of points in $\omega(x(t))$, then there exists a negative logarithmic limit for $(x(t),Z,g)$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $x^*$ be  an $\omega$-limit point of the trajectory with zero support $Z$ (which exists by hypothesis). 
Consider the following function on ${\mathbb{R}}^n_{>0}$:
\[ W(x)\coloneqq  \sum_{i\in Z}\, (x_i\log x_i - x_i)  \,.\]
Observe that $W$ can be extended continuously to ${\mathbb{R}}^n_{\geq 0}$. 
Using the definition of $g(x)$ and that $\dot{x}_i(t)=f_i(x(t))$, provided that $\prod_{k\in Z} x_k(t) \neq 1$ it holds that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wg}
\tfrac{d}{dt} W(x(t)) =     \sum_{i\in Z}  (\log x_i(t))  \, f_i(x(t))  =  \Big(\sum_{k\in Z}  \log x_k(t) \Big) g(x(t))  \, . 
\end{equation}
If a sequence $x(t_j)$ converges to $x^*$, then $ \sum_{k\in Z}  \log x_k(t_j)<0$ for $j$ sufficiently large. Using \eqref{eq:Wg} and \Cref{def:bad}, we  aim to find a sequence of points in the trajectory at which $\tfrac{d}{dt} W$ is positive. This will be obtained by constructing two suitable sequences  $\{x(d_j)\}$ and $\{x(s_j)\}$ such that $d_j<s_j$ and $W(x(d_j)) < W(x(s_j))$ for all $j\in {\mathbb{N}}$. 

To this end,  choose a sequence $\{\delta_j\}_{j\in {\mathbb{N}}}$ converging to zero such that 
\[0<\delta_j<\min \{x_i(0), 1 :  i\in Z\}\,. \] 
Consider the box
\[B_j \coloneqq \{x\in {\mathbb{R}}^n_{>0} : 0<x_i\leq \delta_j,\ i\in Z\}\, \]
and let 
\[ \eta_j \coloneqq    \delta_j  \log \delta_j -\delta_j  <0\, . \]

For a fixed $j$, if $x\in B_j$, then  $x_i \log x_i  - x_i  < 0$ for all $i\in Z$ as $x_i<1$. 
If   additionally   $x_i=\delta_j$ for some $i\in Z$, then 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WRbound}
W(x) = \delta_j  \log \delta_j -\delta_j   + \sum_{k\in Z\setminus \{i\}}  (x_k \log x_k  - x_k)  \leq   \eta_j <0\,.
\end{equation}

Choose a sequence $\{x(r_j)\}$ converging to $x^*$ with $\{r_j\}$ converging to $\infty$. As $x_i(r_j)$ converges to zero if $i\in Z$ and $\{W(x(r_j))\}$ also converges to zero, 
there exists a subsequence $\{x(s_j)\}$ satisfying
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:choosetj}
 x(s_j)\in B_j^\circ\,, \qquad \text{ and } \qquad W(x(s_j))> \frac{\eta_j}{2}\,. 
\end{equation}


As $x(0) \notin B_j$ by construction and $x(s_j)\in B_j^\circ$, there exists a last time $d_j<s_j$ at which $x(s)$ enters the box $B_j$. Specifically,   for $i\in Z$ let
\[ d_{j,i} \coloneqq  \max\{s\in [0,s_j] : x_i(s)= \delta_j \} \quad \text{and}\quad d_{j} \coloneqq  \max_{i\in Z} \{d_{j,i}\}\,.\]
This construction gives that  
\[ d_j<s_j \,, \quad  x(s)\in B_j^\circ\quad \text{ for all }s\in (d_j,s_j] \,,\quad\text{and}\quad \max_{i\in Z} \, x_i(d_j)=\delta_j\,.\] 
In particular  \eqref{eq:WRbound} gives that $W(x(d_j))\leq \eta_j$. Together with 
 \eqref{eq:choosetj} and using that $\eta_j<0$ we obtain that
\[  W(x(d_j))\leq \eta_j < \frac{\eta_j}{2} <    W(x(s_j))\,. \]
The mean value theorem now gives the existence of   $t_j\in(d_j,s_j)$ such that
\[\tfrac{d}{dt} W(x(t_j))>0\, \qquad j\in {\mathbb{N}}\,. \]
Since $x(t_j)\in  B_j^\circ$, $\{x_i(t_j)\}$ converges to zero for all $i\in Z$, and \eqref{eq:Wg} gives that $g(x(t_j))<0$. 

Additionally, $\{t_j\}$ tends to $\infty$ because $\{d_j\}$ tends to $\infty$. To see this, let $M>0$. As the trajectory remains in ${\mathbb{R}}^n_{>0}$ by forward invariance, continuity of the trajectory and compactness give that
\[ \alpha_M \coloneqq \min \{ x_i(t) : i\in Z , 0\leq t\leq M \} >0\, . \]
As $\delta_j$ converges to zero, there exists $j_0$ such that $\delta_j<\alpha_M$ for all $j>j_0$. As $x(d_j)\in B_j$, this implies that $d_j>M$ for all $j>j_0$. Hence $\{d_j\}$ tends to $\infty$. 
 
 
As the trajectory is bounded,  $\{(x_i(t_{j}))_{i\notin Z}\}$ has a convergent subsequence. 
By passing to a subsequence if necessary, $x(t_j)$ converges to a point $x^{**}$, which  in particular is an $\omega$-limit point. We have by construction that $x_i^{**}=0$ for $i\in Z$, and by the maximality of $Z$,  $x_i^{**}\neq 0$ if $i\notin Z$. 
Finally, as by construction $a(x(t_j))\in \Delta_Z$, which is compact, there exists a subsequence of $\{x(t_j)\}$ such that 
$\{a(x(t_j))\}$ converges to some $a_\infty\in \Delta_Z$. 
This gives the desired negative logarithmic limit $a_\infty$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:selectpos}
Consider a reaction network $G=(V,E)$ with $n$ species, $Z\subseteq \{1,\dots,n\}$ nonempty, and  let $x(t)$ be a  positive trajectory of \eqref{eq:mass}. Let $G'=(V',E')$ be a nonempty subnetwork of $G_Z$ consisting of a union of linkage classes and let $P'$ be the convex hull of the complexes in $V'$. 

If $a_\infty\in \Delta_Z$ is a negative logarithmic limit for $(x(t),Z,g_{G'})$, then 
there exists a face $\Gamma$ of $P'$ such that 
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Gamma$ is not  $Z$-transverse for $G'$.
\item $\Gamma=\operatorname{argmin}  \{ a' \cdot \bar{y} : y \in P'\}$ for some $a'\in  \Delta^\circ_Z$.
\item $\Gamma$  is a face of the face $\operatorname{argmin}  \{ a_\infty \cdot \bar{y}: y \in P'\}$ of $P'$. 
 \end{itemize}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $\{t_j\}$ be a sequence witnessing \Cref{def:bad} for $a_\infty$. 
Let $u_j\coloneqq \prod_{i\in Z}  x_i(t_j) >0$ and observe that $\{u_j\}$ converges to zero because $\{x_i(t_j)\}$ converges to zero for all $i\in Z$. Also, we have that   $a_j\coloneqq a(x(t_j))\in \Delta^\circ_Z$ and $x_i(t_j) =   u_j^{a_{ji}}$ for $i\in Z$ by \eqref{eq:xR}.
Let 
\[ \Gamma_0 \coloneqq  \operatorname{argmin}  \{ a_\infty \cdot \bar{y} : y \in P'\} \,. \]


For each $j\in {\mathbb{N}}$, let  $\beta_{j,y}  \coloneqq  \prod_{i\notin Z} x_{i}(t_j)^{y_i}$, consider the sum $\widetilde{u}_j\coloneqq  \sum_{y\in V'}u_j^{a_j\cdot \bar{y}}$ and let 
\begin{align*}
 \tilde{g}_j &\coloneqq  \frac{g_{G'}(x(t_j))}{\widetilde{u}_j}
 =   \sum_{y\rightarrow y'\in E'}    (a_j\cdot (\bar{y}' -   \bar{y})) \kappa_{y\rightarrow y'} \beta_{j,y}  \frac{u_j^{a_j\cdot \bar{y}}}{\widetilde{u}_j}\,.
 \end{align*} 
 The signs of  $\tilde{g}_j$ and of $g_{G'}(x(t_j)) $ agree and  by the choice of $t_j$ it follows that $\tilde{g}_j<0$  (cf. \Cref{def:bad}). 
 
Consider the vector of quotients 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:z}
z_j\coloneqq  \left(\frac{u_j^{a_j\cdot \bar{y}}}{\widetilde{u}_j}\right)_{y\in V'}\,,
\end{equation}
which we   view in  ${\mathbb{R}}^{V'}$. Then
\[ \tilde{g}_j =  \sum_{y\in V'}  \Big(\sum_{y\rightarrow y'\in E'}    (a_j\cdot (\bar{y}' -   \bar{y})) \kappa_{y\rightarrow y'} \Big) \beta_{j,y} \,z_{j,y} \,. \]
Each $z_j$ belongs to the relative interior of the standard simplex in ${\mathbb{R}}^{V'}$, and hence by compactness of the simplex, by passing to a subsequence, we can assume that $\{z_j\}$ converges to some $z_\infty$ in the simplex. Let 
\[ \Gamma_{V'} \coloneqq \{y\in V' : z_{\infty,y}>0\}\]
 and let us inspect the term $z_{\infty,y_0}$ for $y_0\in V'$ more closely. We have that 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zy}
 z_{j,y_0}= \frac{u_j^{a_j\cdot \bar{y}_0}}{\sum_{y\in V'} u_j^{a_j\cdot \bar{y}}} 
=  \frac{1}{ 1+ \sum_{y\in V', y\neq y_0} u_j^{a_j\cdot (\bar{y}-\bar{y}_0)}}\, . 
\end{equation}

If $y_0\notin \Gamma_0$, then by definition of $\Gamma_0$ and $P'$,  $\eta\coloneqq a_\infty\cdot  (\bar{y}-\bar{y}_0)<0$ for some $y\in \Gamma_0\cap V'$. 
Since $a_j$ converges to $a_\infty$ (by \Cref{def:bad}) we have
\[ a_j\cdot  (\bar{y}-\bar{y}_0)< \eta/2<0 \quad \text{for $j$ sufficiently large, and hence } \quad
u_j^{a_j\cdot (\bar{y} -   \bar{y}_0)} \geq u_j^{\eta/2}\,.\]
We conclude from this that
$u_j^{a_j\cdot (\bar{y} -   \bar{y}_0)}$
 tends to infinity as $u_j$ tends to zero. This implies that there is at least  one term in the denominator of \eqref{eq:zy}
 that tends to infinity. As all terms in the denominator are positive, it follows that $z_{\infty,y_0}=0$ and hence  $y_0\notin \Gamma_{V'}$. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:face_inclusion}
\Gamma_{V'}\subseteq \Gamma_0 \,.
\end{equation}



Observe  now that 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:au}
a_j\cdot(\bar y'-\bar y) = \frac{-1}{\log u_j} \log\frac{z_{j,y}}{z_{j,y'}}\,,
\end{equation}
for all $y,y'\in V'$, and hence we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gj_rewrite}
(- \log u_j)\, \tilde{g}_j =\sum_{y\in V'}   \sum_{y\rightarrow y'\in E'}  
 \kappa_{y\rightarrow y'} \beta_{j,y} \, \Big(z_{j,y}  \log\frac{z_{j,y}}{z_{j,y'}}\Big)\,. 
 \end{equation}
Note that this expression has the same sign as $\tilde{g}_j$  and that   the coefficients $ \kappa_{y\rightarrow y'} \beta_{j,y}$ are bounded away from $0$ and $\infty$ (as every $x_i(t_j)$, for $i\notin Z$, converges to a positive number by \Cref{def:bad}). 
 
For a reaction $y\rightarrow y'\in E'$, we look at the term it contributes  in \eqref{eq:gj_rewrite}: 
 \begin{itemize}
 \item 
 If $y\in \Gamma_{V'}$ and $y'\notin\Gamma_{V'}$, 
 then
\[ z_{j,y}\ \text{ tends to } z_{\infty,y}>0 \qquad \text{and}\qquad z_{j,y'}\ \text{ tends to } 0\, ,\]
so
\[ z_{j,y}\log\frac{z_{j,y}}{z_{j,y'}} \text{ tends to } \infty\, .\]
Thus this term tends to $\infty$.
\item If $y,y'\in\Gamma_{V'}$, then  $z_{j,y}\log\frac{z_{j,y}}{z_{j,y'}}$ has a finite limit and hence the sequence stays bounded.

\item 
If $y\notin\Gamma_{V'}$, then  $z_{j,y}>0$ converges to zero and in particular so does $z_{j,y} \log z_{j,y} $. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $j_0$ such that $-\varepsilon<z_{j,y} \log z_{j,y} <0$ for all $j>j_0$. 
Hence, as $z_{j,y'}\leq 1$, we have 
\[ -\varepsilon < z_{j,y} \log z_{j,y}  \leq z_{j,y}\log\frac{z_{j,y}}{z_{j,y'}} \,,\] 
and  the summand is bounded from below  for all sufficiently large $j$. 
\end{itemize}

We conclude that the existence of a reaction from a complex in $\Gamma_{V'}$ to a complex not in $\Gamma_{V'}$ forces $- \log(u_j)\, \tilde{g}_j$ in  \eqref{eq:gj_rewrite}   to tend to $\infty$ and hence  become positive for all $j$ large enough. Therefore the fact that $\tilde{g}_j<0$  for all $j$ sufficiently large implies that there is no such reaction. 



\medskip
All that is left is to show that 
\[ \Gamma_{V'}  = \operatorname{argmin} \{ a'\cdot \bar{y}: y\in V'\}\]
for some $a'\in \Delta^\circ_Z$. From this we will obtain that $\Gamma\coloneqq \operatorname{conv} (\Gamma_{V'})$  is the face of $P'$ in the statement: it is not  $Z$-transverse since $\Gamma\cap V'=\Gamma_{V'}$ and \eqref{eq:face_inclusion} gives that $\Gamma$ is a face of $\Gamma_0$. 

 
 To this end, choose $y_0\in\Gamma_{V'}$ and consider the vector subspace
\[ U \coloneqq   \langle\bar{y}-\bar{y}_0 : y\in \Gamma_{V'} \rangle  \subseteq {\mathbb{R}}^Z \,.\]
Let
\[ w_j \coloneqq  -(\log u_j) a_j  \in {\mathbb{R}}^Z_{> 0}\,\]
so that by \eqref{eq:au} we have
\[ w_j \cdot   (\bar y-\bar y_0)= \log\frac{z_{j,y_0}}{z_{j,y}} \, . \] 
Using the earlier arguments, we observe that: 
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $\mu \in U$,    then
$w_j \cdot \mu$ remains bounded as $j$ tends to $\infty$, as $w_j \cdot   (\bar y-\bar y_0)$ remains   bounded when $y\in \Gamma_{V'}$. 
\item For all $i\in Z$, $w_{ji} =-\log  x_i(t_j)$ tends to $\infty$ as $x_i(t_j)$ converges to zero.  
\item $w_j \cdot   (\bar y-\bar y_0)$  tends to $\infty$ if $y\in V'\setminus \Gamma_{V'}$. 
 \end{enumerate}

Choose  an orthonormal basis $\{\mu_1,\dots,\mu_{s}\}$ of $U$ and  consider   
\[\mu(j)= (w_j\cdot \mu_1) \mu_1+ \dots + (w_j\cdot \mu_s) \mu_s \in U \,, \quad \text{so that } w_j = \mu(j)+\nu(j)\ \text{ with }\nu(j)\in U^\perp  \, . \]
For $y\in \Gamma_{V'}$, as $\bar y-\bar y_0 \in U$, we have that $\nu(j)\cdot  (\bar y-\bar y_0) =0$ for all $j$. 


By point (i), $w_j\cdot \mu_l$ remains bounded for all $l=1,\dots,s$ and hence  each entry of $\mu(j)$ remains bounded.
By point (ii) it then follows that all entries of $\nu(j)$  tend to $\infty$. 
 In particular, there exists $j_0$ such that  $\nu(j)\in {\mathbb{R}}^Z_{>0}$ for all $j>j_0$.

Similarly, for $y\in V'\setminus \Gamma_{V'}$, as $\mu(j)\cdot  (\bar y-\bar y_0)$ remains bounded, point (iii) implies that 
$\nu(j) \cdot   (\bar y-\bar y_0)$ tends to $\infty$. 
Using now that  the number of complexes in $V'$ is finite, there exists $j_1>j_0$ such that  $\nu(j) \cdot (\bar y-\bar y_0)>0$ for all $y\in V'\setminus \Gamma_{V'}$ and $j>j_1$. 

For any such $j$, by defining 
\[ a'\coloneqq \frac{\nu(j)}{\sum_{i\in Z} \nu(j)_i} \in\Delta_Z^\circ \quad \text{we have}\quad  
 a'\cdot\bar y \  \begin{cases}  =  a'\cdot\bar y_0 & \text{if } y\in\Gamma_{V'}  \\
>a'\cdot\bar y_0  & \text{if }  y\in V'\setminus \Gamma_{V'} \,. 
\end{cases}
\]
As $\Gamma$ is the convex hull of $\Gamma_{V'}$,  $a'\in \Delta_Z^\circ$ determines $\Gamma$ as desired.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:bergman}
Consider a reaction network $G=(V,E)$ with $n$ species, $Z\subseteq \{1,\dots,n\}$ nonempty, and  let $x(t)$ be a  positive trajectory of \eqref{eq:mass}. If $a_\infty\in \Delta_Z$ is a negative logarithmic limit for $(x(t),Z,g)$, then 
\[ a_\infty \in  \operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z) \,.\]
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\{t_j\}$ be  a sequence witnessing \Cref{def:bad} for $a_\infty$. Let $x^*\in {\mathbb{R}}^n_{\geq 0}$ be the limit of $\{x(t_j)\}$, which by hypothesis has zero support $Z$. All points $x(t_j)$ belong to the same stoichiometric compatibility class, which is a closed set, and hence so does $x^*$. It follows that $x(t_j)-x^* \in S$ for all $j$. Projecting onto the coordinates in $Z$, we obtain that 
\[ \overline{x(t_j)} \in S_Z \subseteq {\mathbb{R}}^Z\, . \]

Let $u_j\coloneqq \prod_{i\in Z}  x_i(t_j)<1$, which converges to zero because $x_i(t_j)$ converges to zero. By construction $a_j \coloneqq a(x(t_j))\in \Delta_Z^\circ$ and using \eqref{eq:xR} we have  $x_i(t_j) =   u_j^{a_{ji}}$ for $i\in Z$. 

Let $v\in S_Z^\perp$ be nonzero and have inclusion-minimal support among all nonzero vectors of $S_Z^\perp$. 
We now proceed similarly to the proof of \Cref{prop:selectpos}. 
As $0 = v\cdot \overline{x(t_j)}$, after dividing by $\widetilde{u}_j\coloneqq \sum_{k\in Z: v_k\neq 0} u_j^{a_{jk}}>0$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
0 & =  \sum_{i\in Z}  v_i\,  \frac{u_j^{a_{ji}}}{\widetilde{u}_j} \,\quad   \text{ for all $j$}\,.  
\end{align*}
For every $j$, the vector
$z_j\in {\mathbb{R}}^Z$ such that $z_{ji}=0$ if $v_i=0$, and $u_j^{a_{ji}}/\widetilde{u}_j$ otherwise, 
 belongs to $\Delta_Z$ and satisfies $0=v\cdot z_j$. 
As $\Delta_Z$ is compact, by passing to a subsequence if necessary, $\{z_j\}$ has a limit $z_\infty \in \Delta_Z$ for $j$ tending to $\infty$, which also satisfies 
$0 =    v \cdot  z_{\infty}.$ 

Let $m_{\infty} \coloneqq \min \{a_{\infty,i} : i\in Z, v_i\neq 0\}$. If $a_{\infty,i}>m_{\infty}$ and $v_i\neq 0$ for $i\in Z$, then 
\[ \frac{u_j^{a_{ji}}}{\widetilde{u}_j}  = \frac{1}{ 1+ \sum_{k\neq i, v_k\neq 0} u_j^{a_{jk}-a_{ji}}} \] 
tends to zero: by choosing $k$ such that $a_{\infty, k}=m_{\infty}$ and $v_k\neq 0$, we have that  $a_{\infty,k}-a_{\infty, i}<0$, which implies that $u_j^{a_{jk}-a_{ji}}$ tends to $\infty$ and hence $z_{\infty,i}=0$. 

Hence, $\{i\in Z : z_{\infty,i}>0\} \subseteq \{i\in Z :a_{\infty,i}=m_{\infty},v_i\neq 0\}$. In particular, 
\[ 0 =  \sum_{i\in Z:\, a_{\infty,i}=m_{\infty}} v_i \, z_{\infty,i}\,. \]
As $z_{\infty}$ is nonnegative and nonzero, it follows that there must exist indices $i,k\in Z$ such that  $z_{\infty,i}, z_{\infty,k}\neq 0$ and $v_i>0,v_k<0$. 
In particular 
$m_{\infty}= \min_{s : v_s\neq 0} \, a_{\infty,s} = a_{\infty,i}  = a_{\infty,k} $ with $i \in \{s: v_s>0\}$ and $k \, \in  \{s: v_s<0\}$. 
Since this holds for all nonzero vectors $v$ with minimal support, this holds for all signed circuits and hence $a_\infty\in \operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z)$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:gooda}  Consider a reaction network $G$ with $n$ species. Let $Z\subseteq \{1,\dots,n\}$ be nonempty, $g$  the associated function from \eqref{eq:ga}, and $x(t)$ a  positive trajectory of \eqref{eq:mass}. 
 
If $a_\infty\in \Delta_Z$ is a negative logarithmic limit for $(x(t),Z,g)$, then  $G_Z\neq \varnothing$ and there exists  a face $\Gamma$ of $P_Z$ that is not  $Z$-transverse for $G_Z$, and such that 
\[ a_\infty \in   N_Z(\Gamma)  \cap \operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z)\, \quad\text{and}\quad N_Z^*(\Gamma)\cap {\mathbb{R}}^{Z}_{> 0} \neq \varnothing \,.\]
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By \Cref{prop:bergman}, $a_\infty \in  \operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z)$.  
If $G_Z=\varnothing$, then $g$ is identically zero and $a_\infty$ cannot be a negative logarithmic limit. 
Now apply \Cref{prop:selectpos} to $G'=G_Z$ to obtain the face $\Gamma$  that is not  $Z$-transverse for $G_Z$ and that is determined by a positive vector, hence $N_Z^*(\Gamma)\cap {\mathbb{R}}^{Z}_{> 0} \neq \varnothing$. As $\Gamma$ is a face of $\operatorname{argmin} \{a_\infty\cdot \bar y : y \in P_Z\}$ by \Cref{prop:selectpos}, 
it follows  that $a_\infty\in N_Z(\Gamma)$.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:siphon}
Consider a reaction network with $n$ species and a siphon $Z\subseteq \{1,\dots,n\}$. Then $Z$  is said to be:
\begin{itemize}
\item  A \bfemph{relevant siphon} if  $Z$ does not contain the support of a   nonnegative conservation vector.
\item A \bfemph{positively transverse siphon}  if for every face $\Gamma$ of $P_Z$, at least one of the following holds:
\begin{itemize}
\item $N_Z^*(\Gamma)\cap {\mathbb{R}}^{Z}_{> 0} = \varnothing$.
\item $\Gamma$ is  $Z$-transverse for $G_Z$.
\item $N_Z(\Gamma)\cap \operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z) \cap \Delta_Z = \varnothing$.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\label{def:positive}
A reaction network is said to be \bfemph{positively transverse} if  every relevant siphon is positively transverse.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:main}
Consider a reaction network and let $x(t)$ be a bounded positive trajectory  of \eqref{eq:mass}.  If $x(t)$ has a  boundary $\omega$-limit point whose zero support $Z$ is a positively transverse siphon, then there exists $Z\subsetneq Z'$ such that $Z'$ is the zero support of an $\omega$-limit point of $x(t)$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Suppose, for contradiction, that $Z\neq \varnothing$ is inclusion-maximal among the zero supports of the $\omega$-limit points of the trajectory. 
 By \Cref{prop:Wprimepos},  there exists  a negative logarithmic limit $a_\infty \in \Delta_Z$ for $(x(t),Z,g)$. 
By \Cref{cor:gooda},  there exists  a face $\Gamma$ of $P_Z$ that is not  $Z$-transverse for $G_Z$,  
$a_\infty \in   N_Z(\Gamma) \cap \operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z) \cap \Delta_Z$, and $N_Z^*(\Gamma)\cap {\mathbb{R}}^{Z}_{> 0} \neq \varnothing$. 
 Hence $Z$ is not positively transverse by \Cref{def:siphon}, a contradiction.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For a positively transverse reaction network,  
no bounded positive trajectory of \eqref{eq:mass} has a boundary $\omega$-limit point.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By \cite[Prop. 5.4]{AngeliDeLeenheerSontag2007}, the zero support $Z$ of an $\omega$-limit point is a siphon, and as noted above, $Z$ must be a relevant siphon. The statement now follows 
by applying  \Cref{prop:main} to all inclusion-maximal zero supports of the $\omega$-limit set of a bounded trajectory.

<!-- example-2: body_md -->
\label{ex:trop}
 Consider the following network with three species:
 \begin{align*}
 X_2  & \ce{->} 2X_2+X_3   &     X_1+X_2+2X_3 & \ce{->} X_1 +X_3 \\[5pt]
 X_1+X_2 & \ce{<=>} 2X_1+2X_2 &
  X_1+2X_3   \ce{->} 2X_1+ X_3  & \ce{->} 4X_1+X_2 \,.
 \end{align*}
We show that the network is positively transverse and hence by \Cref{thm:main}, no bounded positive trajectory has a  boundary $\omega$-limit point. 
 The stoichiometric subspace $S$ has dimension $2$ and is generated by $(1,1,0),(0,1,1)$. Hence, 
 \[ S^\perp=\langle (1,-1,1)\rangle\,.\]
From this we obtain that all siphons are relevant. There are four siphons: $\{1\}$, $\{1,2\}$, $\{2,3\}$ and $\{1,2,3\}$. 
                                                               
 We start by studying $Z\coloneqq \{1,2,3\}$ and consider $a\in {\mathbb{R}}^3_{>0}$. Since all entries of $a$ are positive, the face  of $P_Z$ determined by $a$ is:
 \[  \begin{cases}
   \operatorname{conv} (X_2,X_1+X_3)   & \text{if } a_2=a_1+a_3  \\
 \{X_2\} & \text{if } a_2<a_1+a_3 \\
  \{X_1+X_3\} & \text{if } a_2>a_1+a_3\,. 
 \end{cases}
 \]
 In the first two cases, the reaction $X_2   \ce{->} 2X_2+X_3$  shows that  the face is   $Z$-transverse. For the face $\Gamma\coloneqq \{X_1+X_3\}$, for which $N_Z(\Gamma)= \{a\in {\mathbb{R}}^3_{\geq 0} : a_2\geq a_1+a_3\}$, there is no outgoing reaction, so we need to compute the positive tropicalization of $S_Z$. 
As   $S_Z^\perp=\langle (1,-1,1)\rangle$, there are two signed circuits $(C^+,C^-)$ of  $S_Z^\perp$: 
\[ (\{1,3\},\{2\}) \qquad \text{and}\qquad (\{2\},\{1,3\}) \,.\]
 This gives that 
 \[ \operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z)= \{a\in {\mathbb{R}}^3 : a_1=a_2\leq a_3 \text{ or }a_2=a_3 \leq a_1 \} = \{a\in {\mathbb{R}}^3 : a_2=\min \{a_1,a_3\}\}\,. \]
 From this we obtain that if $a\in \operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z)\cap N_Z(\Gamma)$, then
 \[ a_2\geq a_1+a_3  \geq \min \{a_1,a_3\} = a_2\]
 and hence all inequalities are equalities. 
In particular the equality $a_1+a_3 = \min \{a_1,a_3\}$ with $a_1,a_3\geq 0$ forces $a_1=a_3=0$, hence $a_2=0$, and $a=0\notin \Delta_Z$. We conclude that $\operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z)\cap N_Z(\Gamma)\cap \Delta_Z=\varnothing$ and hence by \Cref{def:siphon}, $Z$ is positively transverse. 
  The sets $\operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z)\cap \Delta_Z$ and $N_Z(\Gamma)\cap \Delta_Z$  are illustrated in \Cref{fig:2}.  
 
It is more straightforward to show that the siphons  $\{1\}$, $\{1,2\}$, $\{2,3\}$ are positively transverse, as in this case all faces determined by a positive vector are  $Z$-transverse.

The strategy of proof of \Cref{thm:main} follows the same principles as several earlier works, notably \cite{GMS2,Anderson11}: on the one hand, convergence to the boundary forces some Lyapunov-type function to be decreasing along a sequence of points of the trajectory; on the other hand, the study of the logarithmic limit of these points in conjunction with the geometry of the convex hull of the complexes of the network gives that the function has nonnegative derivative. 
  
 The function $g$ used in this work arises from the evaluation of the derivative of the function $-W$ in the proof of \Cref{prop:Wprimepos}, which was  also used in previous work, e.g., \cite[Thm. 7.5 and \S8.2]{GMS2}. Our chosen evaluation  allows us to more easily control the simultaneous convergence of the coefficients and  the monomials through the normalization of the monomials. 
 This is done at the cost of including complexes that only appear as products of reactions in our analysis of the monomials. 
  With this, we can avoid the tier \cite{Anderson11} and jet   \cite{GMS2} decompositions and provide a direct study of negative logarithmic limits in \Cref{prop:selectpos}.

Every nonrelevant siphon $Z$ satisfies $\operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z)=\varnothing$ and hence the third condition in \Cref{def:siphon} holds. Requiring all siphons to be positively transverse in \Cref{def:positive} is  thus equivalent to requiring only the relevant siphons to be positively transverse. Checking relevance however is an easy verification worth performing as a first step and the chosen formulation emphasizes this.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
In order to  computationally decide whether a network is positively transverse, one can start by identifying all siphons and selecting the relevant ones. Several algorithms in the Petri net literature allow for siphon identification, e.g.,\cite{KinuyamaMurata1986,NabliFagesMartinezSoliman2012}; see also \cite{ShiuSturmfels} for an algebraic approach. After every relevant siphon $Z$ has been identified, the next step is to decide whether $Z$ is positively transverse. To this end, one can start by finding $N_Z^*(\Gamma)$ and $N_Z(\Gamma)$ for all faces of $P_Z$. Among the faces with $N_Z^*(\Gamma)\cap {\mathbb{R}}^{Z}_{> 0} \neq \varnothing$, find  the set $\mathcal{P}$ of faces that are not  $Z$-transverse by simply identifying the complexes that belong to the face. 

If $\mathcal{P}=\varnothing$, then $Z$ is positively transverse. Otherwise, $Z$ is positively transverse if and only if 
$N_Z(\Gamma)\cap \operatorname{Trop}^+(S_Z)\cap \Delta_Z = \varnothing$ for all $\Gamma\in \mathcal{P}$.  The positive tropicalization of $S_Z$ agrees with the positive Bergman fan of the oriented matroid of $S_Z$. This  can be computed by first computing the Bergman fan of the matroid of $S_Z$ and then following \cite[Alg. 1]{RoseTelek24}. 

All polyhedral computations such as finding inner normal cones and the Bergman fan can be performed, 
for instance, using \texttt{polymake} \cite{polymake} or \texttt{oscar.jl} in Julia \cite{OSCAR,OSCAR-book}.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rem:literature}
 In \cite{CNP}   \emph{lower endotactic} networks were introduced and extended in \cite{Pantea} (see also \cite{JohnstonPanteaDonnell2016} for a computational approach to decide lower endotacticity). 
 For every nonnegative vector $a$, consider the subnetwork $G_a=(V_a,E_a)$ of $G$ obtained by removing the reactions $y\rightarrow y'$  satisfying $a\cdot (y'-y)= 0$. 
Then a network is said to be lower endotactic if $a\cdot (y'-y)> 0$ for every reaction $y\rightarrow y'$ of $G_a$ whenever $y\in \operatorname{argmin} \{a \cdot \tilde{y}:  \tilde{y}\rightarrow \tilde{y}'\in E_a\}$.   This is a ``nonnegative version'' of endotactic networks, where this condition is required for all direction vectors. 

Networks (a) and (b) in \Cref{fig:1} are lower endotactic, but (c) is not. Network (c) is in particular not endotactic, and we note also that it is not covered by the siphon-based persistence criterion of \cite{AngeliDeLeenheerSontag2007}, since it has a relevant siphon (called \emph{critical siphon} in loc. cit.), namely $\{2\}$.


Pantea shows that if the network has a two-dimensional stoichiometric subspace and lower endotactic stoichiometric subnetworks, then any bounded positive trajectory  is persistent and in particular does not have boundary $\omega$-limit points \cite[Thm. 5.1]{Pantea}. This was used to show that the GAC holds if the stoichiometric subspace has dimension three. 



In \cite{GMS2}, the authors studied  \emph{strongly endotactic networks}, which are endotactic networks such that, for any vector $a\in {\mathbb{R}}^n$ not in $S^\perp$, there exists a reaction $y\rightarrow y'$ such that 
$a\cdot  y'> a\cdot y$ and $y\in \operatorname{argmin} \{a \cdot \tilde{y}:  \tilde{y}\rightarrow \tilde{y}'\in E\}$.  They proved that strongly endotactic mass-action systems are permanent. In particular their positive trajectories are bounded and persistent, and consequently the GAC holds for strongly endotactic complex-balancing systems.
The approach is revisited in \cite{anderson:cappelletti:tier}, where  consequences of the tier structure of strongly endotactic networks for the stochastic modeling of the network are also obtained.  
 
 Positive transversality may look like a strengthening of lower endotacticity, since we also require a reaction to point into the polytope whenever the face is selected by a positive vector. However, the two classes are incomparable: network (b) in \Cref{fig:1} is lower endotactic but not positively transverse, while network (c)  is  positively transverse but not lower endotactic.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:gac1}
 Consider a   reaction network such that \eqref{eq:mass} admits a positive complex-balancing steady state. 
Assume that  every relevant siphon $Z\neq \{1,\dots,n\}$ that is not a singleton is positively transverse.

Then every positive trajectory $x(t)$  converges to the unique complex-balancing positive steady state  in the stoichiometric compatibility class of $x(0)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
By \cite[Theorem 3.2]{SiegelMacLean}, see also \cite{TDS,Sontag01}, the $\omega$-limit set of a trajectory $x(t)$ with positive initial condition $x(0)$  either  consists of the unique complex-balancing steady state $c^*$  in the stoichiometric compatibility class of $x(0)$ or is contained in the boundary of ${\mathbb{R}}^n_{\geq 0}$. Since the trajectory is bounded by \cite[Lemma 3.5]{SiegelMacLean}, the trajectory converges to $c^*$ if we can exclude the latter scenario.
  
By \cite[Prop. 20, Rem. 21]{TDS} the origin is not an $\omega$-limit point. 
By \cite[Cor. 3.3]{AndersonShiu10}, a singleton cannot be an inclusion-maximal zero support of an $\omega$-limit point  (as any such point will necessarily lie in the relative interior of a facet of the stoichiometric compatibility class).  
If $x(t)$ has a boundary $\omega$-limit point,  then there exists an inclusion-maximal zero support $Z$ of $\omega$-limit points, which is a relevant siphon, and  is neither a singleton nor $\{1,\dots,n\}$.  By hypothesis, $Z$ is positively transverse, and we obtain a contradiction by \Cref{prop:main}.
