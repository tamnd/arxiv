<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Automorphisms preserve element orders, so  $\omega(G)\geq 1+|\pi(G)|.$ Since $\omega(G)=5$, it follows that $|\pi(G)|\leq 4.$

    $$
    1=F_0(G)<F_1(G)<F_2(G)<F_3(G)<F_4(G)=G
    .$$

        $h(G)\leq 3,$ contradicting our assumption that $h(G)=4$. Therefore $h(G)\leq 3$.

    \[

\label{lem:frobenius-orbits}
    Let $ G=N\rtimes H $ be a Frobenius group with kernel $N$ and complement $H$. Define $ A_N=\{\alpha|_N:\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\}\leq\operatorname{Aut}(N) $ and $ A_H=\{\alpha|_H:\alpha\in N_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}(H)\}\leq\operatorname{Aut}(H). $ Then 
    $$ \omega(G)=\omega_{A_N}(N)+\omega_{A_H}(H)-1. $$
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
The number of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$-orbits contained in $N$ is $ \omega_{A_N}(N). $ Since $G$ is Frobenius, every $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$-orbit contained in $G\setminus N$ therefore contains an element of $H^\#$.

    Let $h_1,h_2\in H^{\#}$. We show that if $ h_1^\alpha=h_2 $ for some $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, then $h_1,h_2$ belong to the same $A_H$-orbit. Distinct Frobenius complements have trivial intersection, and hence  $ H^\alpha=H. $ Thus $ \alpha\in N_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}(H), $ and therefore $h_1$ and $h_2$ belong to the same $A_H$-orbit.

    It follows that the number of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$-orbits contained in $G\setminus N$ is exactly  $ \omega_{A_H}(H)-1, $ and the result follows.

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor:converse-main2}
    Let $G=K\rtimes H$ be a Frobenius group, where
    $K=\gamma_\infty(G)$ is a $p$-group and $H\cong C_q$. Assume that $\omega_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}(K)=4$ and that $A_H=\{\alpha|_H:\alpha\in N_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}(H)\}$ acts transitively on $H^\#$. Then
    \[
    \omega(G)=5.
    \]
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Since $K=\gamma_\infty(G)$ is characteristic in $G$, the number
    of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$-orbits contained in $K$ is $\omega_{A_K}(K)=\omega_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}(K)=4$, where $A_K=\{\alpha|_K:\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\}$.
    
    Moreover, by hypothesis, $A_H$ acts transitively on $H^\#$, and hence $\omega_{A_H}(H)=2$. Therefore, by Lemma~\ref{lem:frobenius-orbits},  $\omega(G) = \omega_{A_K}(K)+\omega_{A_H}(H)-1 = 4+2-1 = 5$.

If $G$ is a Frobenius $AT$-group, then $ \omega(G) = \omega(N)+\omega(H)-1. $

<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
For a Frobenius group, $ \omega(G)=\omega_{A_N}(N)+\omega_{A_H}(H)-1. $ Since $G$ is an AT-group, we conclude that any two elements of $N$ having the same order are conjugate under an automorphism of $G$. Since $N$ is characteristic, such an automorphism induces an element of $A_N$. Hence $ \omega_{A_N}(N)=\omega(N). $ 
        
        Similarly, let $h_1,h_2\in H^\#$ have the same order. There exists $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ such that $h_1^\alpha=h_2$. Then $ 1\neq h_2\in H^\alpha\cap H. $ Since distinct Frobenius complements intersect trivially, it follows that we have $H^\alpha=H$. Thus $\alpha|_H\in A_H$, and consequently $ \omega_{A_H}(H)=\omega(H). $ Therefore $ \omega(G)=\omega(N)+\omega(H)-1. $

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:3primes-AT}
        Let $G$ be a finite solvable group such that
        $\omega(G)=5\text{ and }|\pi(G)|=3$. Then $G$ is an $AT$-group.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Let $\pi(G)=\{p,q,r\},$ where $p$, $q$, and $r$ are distinct primes. Note that $G$ contains elements of orders $p$, $q$, and $r$. By solvability, $G$ contains an element whose order is the product of two distinct primes in $\pi(G)$ (see \cite[Proposition~1]{Luc99}). Without loss of generality, we may assume that $G$ contains an element of order $pq$.
    
    Hence $G$ contains elements of the five distinct orders, say, $1, p, q, r, pq.$  Thus any two elements of $G$ having the same order belong to the same orbit. Hence $G$ is an $AT$-group.

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:5-orbit-3-primes}
        Let $G$ be a finite solvable group such that $\omega(G)=5\text{ and }|\pi(G)|=3.$ Then $G$ has an elementary abelian  characteristic Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$, and $G=P\rtimes H,$ where $H$ is a Hall $\{q,r\}$-subgroup satisfying $\omega(H)=3\text{ or }\omega(H)=4.$ Moreover, one of the following holds:
        \begin{enumerate}
            \item
            $G=P\rtimes C_{qr}$, where $C_{qr}$ acts fixed-point-freely on $P$. The orbit representatives have orders $1,\ p,\ q,\ r,\ qr.$
            \item
            $G=(P\times Q)\rtimes R$, where $R\cong C_2$ acts fixed-point-freely on both $P$ and $Q$. The orbit representatives have orders $1,\ p,\ q,\ pq,\ 2.$ 
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:3primes-AT}, the group $G$ is an $AT$-group, 
    
    By Zhang's description of finite solvable AT-groups \cite{Zha92}, we may write $ G=P\rtimes H, $ where $P$ is an elementary abelian normal Sylow $p$-subgroup, the Sylow subgroups of $H$ are cyclic, and $ |H:F(H)|\leq2. $ 
    
    Suppose first that $H$ is nilpotent. Since its Sylow subgroups are cyclic, we may write $ H=Q\times R, $ where $Q$ and $R$ are cyclic Sylow $q$- and $r$-subgroups, respectively. Since $G$ has exactly five element orders, neither $Q$ nor $R$ can contain elements of order $q^2$ or $r^2$. Consequently, $ Q\cong C_q, R\cong C_r, $ and hence $ H\cong C_q\times C_r\cong C_{qr}. $ 

    Thus $G$ contains elements of orders $ 1,\ p,\ q,\ r,\ qr. $ Since these are already five distinct element orders, no further order can occur. In particular, no nonidentity element of $H$ can centralize a nonidentity element of $P$. Indeed, if $1\neq x\in P$ and $1\neq h\in H$ satisfy $[x,h]=1$, then $xh$ has order divisible by $p$ and by a prime divisor of $|h|$, yielding an additional element order. Therefore  $ C_P(h)=1 $ for every $1\neq h\in H$. Hence $H$ acts fixed-point-freely on $P$, and  $ G=P\rtimes C_{qr}. $  This gives case~(1). Moreover, in this case $\omega(H)=4$.
    
    Suppose now that $H$ is not nilpotent. Then $ |H:F(H)|=2, $ so, after relabelling if necessary, we may assume that $r=2$. Let $R$ be a Sylow $2$-subgroup of $H$. Since the Sylow subgroups of $H$ are cyclic and $G$ has no elements of order $4$, we have $ R\cong C_2. $ Similarly, a Sylow $q$-subgroup $Q$ of $H$ satisfies $ Q\cong C_q. $ Since $H$ is not nilpotent, $ H=Q\rtimes R\cong C_q\rtimes C_2\cong D_{2q}. $ 

    We claim that $[P,Q]=1$. Suppose, to the contrary, that $[P,Q]\neq1$. Since $P$ is characteristic in $G$ and $ F(G/P)=QP/P, $ the subgroup $PQ$ is characteristic in $G$. Hence $ C_P(Q)=C_P(PQ) $ is characteristic in $G$. Since $G$ is an $AT$-group and every nonidentity element of $P$ has order $p$, the set $P^\#$ is a single $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$-orbit. Therefore $ C_P(Q)=1$ or $ C_P(Q)=P. $ The second possibility would imply $[P,Q]=1$, contrary to our assumption. Hence $ C_P(Q)=1. $ 
    
    We may therefore apply Corollary~\ref{cor:counting} to $ G=P\rtimes(Q\rtimes R), $ with $R\cong C_2$. Thus, for some positive integer $d$, $ p^{2d}-1=q(p^d-1), $ and hence $ q=p^d+1. $ Since $p$ and $q$ are odd primes, this is impossible. Therefore $ [P,Q]=1. $ It follows that $$ G=(P\times Q)\rtimes R,\qquad R\cong C_2. $$ Since $P$ and $Q$ commute, $G$ contains elements of order $pq$. Thus the five element orders occurring in $G$ are $ 1,\ p,\ q,\ pq,\ 2. $ 
    
    The subgroup $R$ acts fixed-point-freely on $Q$, since $Q\rtimes R\cong D_{2q}$. We claim that it also acts fixed-point-freely on $P$. Otherwise, there would exist $1\neq x\in C_P(R)$. If $1\neq y\in R$, then $xy$ would have order $2p$, since $p$ is odd. This would give a sixth element order, contradicting $\omega(G)=5$. Hence  $  C_P(R)=1.  $ 
    
    Therefore $ G=(P\times Q)\rtimes C_2, $ where $C_2$ acts fixed-point-freely on both $P$ and $Q$. This is case~(2). Moreover, $H\cong D_{2q}$ has three automorphism orbits, and hence $\omega(H)=3$.

<!-- ex-1: body_md -->
For every integer $k\geq 3$, there exists a finite non-abelian EPPO-group $G$ such that $\omega(G)=k.$
<!-- end ex-1 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Let $p$ be an odd prime and set $n=k-2$. Consider the dihedral group
    $$ D_{2p^n}=C_{p^n}\rtimes C_2, $$ 
    where  $C_2$ acts by inversion on $C_{p^n}$. Since $p$ is odd, it follows that this action is fixed-point-free on $C_{p^n}$, and hence $D_{2p^n}$ is a Frobenius group with kernel $C_{p^n}$ and complement $C_2$. 
    
    Every element outside $C_{p^n}$ has order $2$, while the elements of $C_{p^n}$ have orders $ 1,p,p^2,\ldots,p^n. $ Thus $G$ is an EPPO-group. Moreover, $G$ is an $AT$-group, since elements of the same order in $C_{p^n}$ are in the same $\operatorname{Aut}(C_{p^n})$-orbit and all elements outside $C_{p^n}$ are thus involutions and form a single automorphism orbit. Therefore, by Lemma~\ref{lem:frobenius-orbits}, $ \omega(D_{2p^n}) = \omega(C_{p^n})+\omega(C_2)-1. $ Since $ \omega(C_{p^n})=n+1 \text{ and } \omega(C_2)=2$, we conclude that $ \omega(D_{2p^n})=n+2=k. $

<!-- ex-2: body_md -->
For every integer $k\geq 5$, there exists a finite non-CPPO-group $G$ such that $\omega(G)=k.$
<!-- end ex-2 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Set $m=k-3$ and choose distinct primes $p$ and
    $q$ such that
    $p\equiv q\equiv1\pmod m.$ Then there are elements of order $m$ in both $\operatorname{Aut}(C_p)$ and $\operatorname{Aut}(C_q)$, say $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_p^{\times}$ and $\mu\in\mathbb{F}_q^{\times}$.
        Let 
    $$\langle a\rangle\cong C_p, \quad \langle b\rangle\cong C_q,\quad N=\langle a\rangle\times \langle b\rangle  , \quad H=\langle h\rangle\cong C_m.$$
    Define the action $a^h=a^{\lambda}$ and $b^h=b^{\mu}$. 
    Since every
    nonidentity element of $H$ acts fixed-point-freely on $N$,  
    $G=N\rtimes H$
    is a Frobenius group, which is not CPPO.
    
    Now we compute $\omega(G)$. Note that $\operatorname{Aut}(N)\cong\operatorname{Aut}(C_p)\times\operatorname{Aut}(C_q)$ is abelian. Hence every automorphism of $N$ extends to an automorphism of $G$ fixing $H$. Thus, $\omega_{A_N}(N)=4$. Moreover, since $H$ acts faithfully on $N$ and $\operatorname{Aut}(N)$ is abelian, every automorphism of $G$ normalizing $H$ induces the identity on $H$. Hence
    \[
    \omega_{A_H}(H)=m.
    \]

    By Lemma~\ref{lem:frobenius-orbits}, $\omega(G)=4+m-1=m+3=k$.
