\label{thm:implications}
The above six properties are related as follows:
\[
(1^{\rm hol})\Longrightarrow (2^{\rm hol}) \Longrightarrow (1^{\rm top}) \Longleftrightarrow (2^{\rm top}) \Longrightarrow (3^{\rm top})\Longleftrightarrow (3^{\rm hol}).
\]
Moreover, none of the one-way implications can be reversed.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
In Properties (3$^{\rm hol}$) and (3$^{\rm top}$), we require the local systems $V$ to be semisimple since non-abelian Hodge theory (see \cite{Simpson, BudurWang}) only applies to semisimple local systems. This assumption is also necessary for some of the implications. Indeed, if arbitrary local systems are allowed, then both implications $(1^{\rm top})\Rightarrow (3^{\rm hol})$ and $(1^{\rm top})\Rightarrow (3^{\rm top})$ fail. For example, let $X$ be an elliptic curve. For any $\omega\in H^1(X,\mathbb{C})$, let $V_\omega$ be the extension of $\mathbb{C}_X$ by itself corresponding to $\omega$. Then the Aomoto complex $(H^*(X, V_{\omega}), \wedge \omega)$ has a nontrivial cohomology group in degree 0. Thus, $X$ satisfies $(1^{\rm top})$ but satisfies neither $(3^{\rm top})$ nor $(3^{\rm hol})$ when arbitrary local systems are allowed.

\label{thm:KP.rightway.thm}
Let $\Sigma$ be a (possibly non-compact) Riemann surface with $\pi_1(\Sigma)\neq 0$.                            
Let $g\colon X\to \Sigma$ be a proper morphism between complex spaces. 
Assume that $g$ has simply connected fibers. 
Then the following are equivalent.
   \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item $g$ is a $\mathbb Z$-homology fiber bundle. \label{item:KP.rightway.thm.1}
    \item $g$ is a homotopy fiber bundle.\label{item:KP.rightway.thm.2}
    \item  The universal cover $\widetilde X$ is homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex. \label{item:KP.rightway.thm.3}
    \end{enumerate}
    If, in addition,   $\dim X\geq 4$ and $X$ is smooth, then these are also equivalent to
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)] \setcounter{enumi}{3}
    \item \label{item:KP.rightway.thm.4} There exists a proper $C^\infty$-fiber bundle $g'\colon X\to\Sigma$ which is homotopic to $g$ and agrees with $g$ outside a small neighborhood of the singular fibers of $g$. 
    \end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The following was introduced in \cite{S}  to study Kotschick's question: 
\begin{enumerate}
\item[$(3^{\rm \acute{e}tale})$] There exists $\omega\in H^1(X, \mathbb{C})$ such that, for every finite \'etale cover $\pi\colon X'\to X$, the Aomoto complex
$$
H^0(X',\mathbb C) \xrightarrow{\wedge\pi^*\omega} H^1(X',\mathbb C)  \xrightarrow{\wedge\pi^*\omega} H^2(X',\mathbb C)  \xrightarrow{\wedge\pi^*\omega} \cdots
$$
is exact.
\end{enumerate}
It was proved in \cite{S} that $(1^{\rm hol})\Rightarrow (3^{\rm \acute{e}tale})$. Moreover, \cite{HS,pietig} proved that, when $\dim X\leq 3$, $(3^{\rm \acute{e}tale})$ implies $(2^{\rm top})$. Together, these results give a positive answer to Kotschick's question for K\"ahler manifolds of dimension at most three.

For any finite \'etale cover $\pi\colon X'\to X$, the local system $\pi_*(\mathbb{C}_{X'})$ is semisimple. Thus, by the isomorphism $H^i(X',\mathbb C)\cong H^i(X, \pi_*(\mathbb{C}_{X'}))$, Property $(3^{\rm hol})$ implies $(3^{\rm \acute{e}tale})$. Since Theorem \ref{thm:Aomoto-intro} shows that $(3^{\rm hol})$ does not imply $(2^{\rm top})$, it follows that $(3^{\rm \acute{e}tale})$ does not imply $(2^{\rm top})$ in higher dimensions.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{sub-Hodge}
    Assume that $X$ is a compact K\"ahler manifold and $V$ is a semisimple local system of finite rank on $X$. Then, the resonance variety $R(X, V)$ is a finite union of sub-Hodge structures in $H^1(X, \mathbb{C})$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The resonance variety $R(X, V)$ is closely related to the cohomology support locus, whose definition we now recall. Let $\mathrm{M}_B(X, 1)$ denote the moduli space of rank-one local systems on $X$, which is a commutative algebraic group. We define the \emph{cohomology support locus} of $V$ by
    \[
    \Sigma(X, V)\coloneqq \{L\in \mathrm{M}_B(X, 1)\mid H^i(X, V\otimes L)\neq 0 \text{ for some $i$}\}.
    \]
    By \cite[Theorem 7.3]{Schnell}, $\Sigma(X, V)$ is a finite union of linear subvarieties.\footnote{In \cite{Schnell}, the result assumes that $X$ is projective. However, the argument also generalizes to the compact K\"ahler case (see also \cite{Arapura2}). The idea is that $\mathrm{M}_B(X, 1)$ has a natural hyperK\"ahler structure and $\Sigma(X, V)$ is a quaternion subvariety, which implies that each of its irreducible components is a linear subvariety.} In other words, up to a translate, every irreducible component of $\Sigma(X, V)$ is of the form 
    \[
    \operatorname{Image}\left(\varphi^*\colon \mathrm{M}_B(A, 1)\to \mathrm{M}_B(X, 1)\right)
    \]
    where $\varphi\colon X\to A$ is a map to a compact complex torus.\footnote{When $X$ is a smooth projective variety, $A$ can be chosen to be an abelian variety.}

    Notice that the Lie algebra of the algebraic group $\mathrm{M}_B(X, 1)$ is naturally isomorphic to $H^1(X, \mathbb{C})$. Hence there is an exponential map $\exp\colon  H^1(X, \mathbb{C})\to \mathrm{M}_B(X, 1)$. By \cite[Theorem 1.4]{BudurWang}, the exponential map induces an isomorphism of analytic germs
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:germ}
    R(X, V)_{(0)}\cong \Sigma(X, V)_{(1)}.
    \end{equation}
    Since $R(X, V)$ is a cone, the linearity of $\Sigma(X, V)$ in the previous paragraph implies that every irreducible component of $R(X, V)$ is a sub-Hodge structure in $H^1(X, \mathbb{C})$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:D-finite}
Let $X$ be a complex space and let $f\colon X\to \Sigma$ be a proper morphism to a Riemann surface $\Sigma$ such that $b_1(\Sigma)<\infty$.
Then the groups $H^i(X,\mathbb Z)$ are finitely generated if and only if there is a finite subset $D\subseteq \Sigma$ such that $f$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-homology fiber bundle over $\Sigma\setminus D$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
There is a smallest discrete subset $D\subseteq \Sigma$ such that the sheaves $R^if_*\mathbb Z_X$ are local systems on $\Sigma\setminus D$.
If $D$ is finite, then the groups
$$
H^j\bigl(\Sigma\setminus D,R^if_*\mathbb Z_X\bigr)
$$
are finitely generated, and hence so are the groups
$$
H^j\bigl(X\setminus f^{-1}(D),\mathbb{Z}\bigr)
$$
by the Leray spectral sequence.
The Mayer--Vietoris sequence now gives finite generation of the groups $H^j(X,\mathbb{Z})$.


If $D$ is infinite, then $\Sigma$ is noncompact, hence Stein. 
Artin's vanishing theorem for constructible sheaves on Stein manifolds therefore gives
$$
H^j\bigl(\Sigma,R^if_*\mathbb Z_X\bigr)=0 \qquad\text{for }j>1,
$$
see \cite[Theorem~10.3.8]{KS90}. 
Thus all differentials in the Leray spectral sequence vanish.
Therefore, the groups $H^i(X,\mathbb Z)$ are finitely generated if and only if each group
$$
H^j\bigl(\Sigma,R^if_*\mathbb Z_X\bigr)
$$
is finitely generated.
The proposition therefore follows from Lemma \ref{lem:D-finite} below.

\label{lem:D-finite}
Let $\Sigma$ be a connected orientable topological surface with $b_1(\Sigma)<\infty$.
Let ${\mathcal F}$ be a sheaf of abelian groups on $\Sigma$ whose stalks are finitely generated. 
Let
$$
\Sigma^\circ=\{x\in \Sigma \mid {\mathcal F}\text{ is a local system on a neighborhood of }x\}
$$
and put $D=\Sigma\setminus \Sigma^\circ$.  Assume that $D$ is discrete.  Then the groups
$H^i(\Sigma,\mathcal{F})$ are finitely generated for every $i$ if and only if $D$ is finite.

Conversely, assume that $D$ is infinite.
Let ${\mathcal F}_{sky}\subset {\mathcal F}$ be the maximal skyscraper subsheaf. 
If ${\mathcal F}_{sky}$ has infinitely many nonzero stalks, $H^0(\Sigma,\mathcal{F})$ is not finitely generated.
We may thus assume that this is not the case.
Then we replace ${\mathcal F}$ by ${\mathcal F}/{\mathcal F}_{sky}$ and assume that ${\mathcal F}$ does not contain skyscraper subsheaves. 


                                                                                                      
                                                        
                                                                                                                                  
                                                                               
                                                                                                                                         
                
    
                                                
    
                                                                                                                            
                                          

                                                        
                   
   
                                   
    
                                                           
                                                                                                   
                               
                                                                                                                                    
                                                                                                                                        

Note that $D$ is closed and discrete, hence is countable and has no accumulation points in $\Sigma$.
In particular, we can write
$$
D=\{0_i\mid i=1,2,\ldots\}.
$$
Since $\Sigma$ is orientable and $b_1(\Sigma)<\infty$, it is given by attaching to a compact orientable surface (called compact core) with boundary an infinite annulus to each of the finitely many boundary components.
We can therefore choose a closed embedded tree $\Gamma\subset\Sigma$ which contains $D$ and is obtained from a finite tree contained in the compact core by attaching finitely many proper rays, one for each of the attached ends.  

Let $W_1$ be a sufficiently small open thickening of $\Gamma$, chosen
so that $\Gamma$ is a deformation retract of $W_1$, and put
$
W_2\coloneq\Sigma\setminus\Gamma.
$
Then $W_1$ is connected and contractible, while $W_2$ and $W_1\cap W_2$ have the homotopy type of finite CW complexes and do not contain any points of $D$.
The Mayer--Vietoris sequence therefore reduces us to the case where $\Sigma=W_1$ is a connected contractible surface without boundary.

We then choose an open covering
$\Sigma=V\cup\bigsqcup_{i=1}^\infty U_i$, where the $U_i$ are pairwise
disjoint open discs containing $0_i$, $V\cap U_i$ is nonempty and

$R_i=\{i\}\times(-\infty,0]$, and
$V=\mathbb R^2\setminus\bigcup_{i\geq1}R_i$.
Then $\mathbb R^2=V\cup\bigcup_{i\geq1}U_i$, and each $V\cap U_i$ is nonempty
and contractible.)
By the definition of $\Sigma^\circ$ and $D$,
the sheaf ${\mathcal F}$ is locally constant outside of $D$, but ${\mathcal F}$ is not locally constant in the neighborhood of any of the points $0_i$.

\textbf{Claim.} The map $H^0(U_i,{\mathcal F})\to H^0(V\cap U_i,{\mathcal F})$ is not surjective.
 
\begin{proof} 
Set
$$
j\colon U_i\setminus\{0_i\}\hookrightarrow U_i \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathcal L\coloneq \mathcal F|_{U_i\setminus\{0_i\}}.
$$
There is a natural morphism
\begin{align} \label{eq:claim:map:1}
\mathcal F|_{U_i}\longrightarrow j_\ast \mathcal L.
\end{align}
Its kernel is supported at $0_i$, hence is a skyscraper subsheaf of $\mathcal F$. 
By our reduction, this kernel is zero.

Suppose, for contradiction, that
\begin{align} \label{eq:claim:map:2}
H^0(U_i,\mathcal F)\longrightarrow H^0(V\cap U_i,\mathcal F)
\end{align}
is surjective. 
Since $V\cap U_i$ is nonempty and contractible and $\mathcal F$ is locally constant there, its space of sections identifies with any stalk of $\mathcal L$ on $V\cap U_i$. 
                                                                                                                                     
Surjectivity of \eqref{eq:claim:map:2} thus implies that $\mathcal L$ is a trivial local system on $U_i\setminus\{0_i\}$.
But then $j_\ast \mathcal L$ is a trivial local system on $U_i$ and the injective map \eqref{eq:claim:map:1} is surjective by surjectivity of  \eqref{eq:claim:map:2}.
Hence, \eqref{eq:claim:map:1} is an isomorphism, which contradicts our assumptions.  
                                                                                                                                                                                                
                                                                                                                                                                                                                             
\end{proof}

Let $U=\bigsqcup_{i\geq 1} U_i$ and consider the Mayer--Vietoris sequence
$$
H^0(\Sigma,{\mathcal F}) \to \prod_{i=1}^{\infty} H^0(U_i,{\mathcal F}) \oplus H^0(V,{\mathcal F})\to \prod_{i=1}^{\infty} H^0(U_i\cap V,{\mathcal F})\to H^1(\Sigma,{\mathcal F}) .
$$
By the above claim, the cokernel of $\prod_{i=1}^{\infty} H^0(U_i,{\mathcal F}) \to \prod_{i=1}^{\infty} H^0(U_i\cap V,{\mathcal F})$ is not finitely generated.

Hence, the above exact sequence shows that $H^1(\Sigma,{\mathcal F})$ is not finitely generated.
This concludes the proof of the lemma.

\label{lem:derived-blow-up-formula}
Let $M$ be a complex manifold, let $\iota\colon Z\hookrightarrow M$ be a
closed complex submanifold of codimension $c$, and let
\[
\pi\colon M'=\operatorname{Bl}_ZM\longrightarrow M
\]
be the blow-up of $M$ along $Z$. Then there is an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derived-blow-up-formula}
R\pi_{*}\mathbb Z_{M'}
\simeq
\mathbb Z_M\oplus\bigoplus_{r=1}^{c-1}\iota_*\mathbb Z_Z[-2r]
\end{equation}
in the derived category of sheaves of abelian groups on $M$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->

$p\colon E=\mathbb{P}(N_{Z/M})\to Z$ be its natural projection. The morphism in
\eqref{eq:derived-blow-up-formula} is obtained from the pullback
$ \mathbb Z_M\to R\pi_*\mathbb Z_{M'}$
together with, for $1\leq r\leq c-1$, the morphisms
$\iota_*\mathbb Z_Z[-2r]\to R\pi_*\mathbb Z_{M'}
$
defined by pulling back to $E$, taking cup product with
$c_1(\mathcal O_E(1))^{r-1}$, and applying the Gysin morphism for
$j\colon E\hookrightarrow M'$.

It suffices to check that the resulting morphism is an isomorphism on
stalks. 
Away from $Z$, this is clear. 
At a point of $Z$, the assertion is precisely the projective-bundle formula for the fiber $\mathbb{P}^{c-1}$.
                                                  
   
                                                                               
   
This proves \eqref{eq:derived-blow-up-formula}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:vanishing}
Let $V$ be any semisimple local system on $X$ and assume $\omega\in H^1(X, \mathbb{C})$ is nonzero. 
Then the Aomoto complex
$$
(H^*(X, V), \wedge\omega)\coloneqq H^0({X}, V)\xrightarrow{\wedge \omega} H^1({X}, V)\xrightarrow{\wedge \omega}H^2({X}, V)\xrightarrow{\wedge \omega}\cdots

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lemma:pushforward}
The map $p\colon S\to E$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trivial LS}
Rp_* \mathbb{Q}_S \cong \mathbb{Q}_E\oplus \mathbb{Q}_E[-1]^{\oplus 2}\oplus \mathbb{Q}_E[-2].
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
First, we prove that $p\colon S\to E$ is a $\mathbb Q$-homology fiber bundle. 
    In fact, away from the branch locus of $p_C$, $p$ is an $F$-fiber bundle. 
    Let $x\in E$ be a branch point of $p_C$, and let $x'\in E$ be a point different from but very close to $x$. 
    Then there is a specialization map between the fibers of $p$, $\mathrm{sp}\colon S_{x'}\to S_{x}$, well-defined up to homotopy. 
    This map can be chosen to be the finite \'etale double cover $F\to F/\langle \sigma_1 \rangle$. 
    Hence, it induces an isomorphism on rational (co-)homology.
    Therefore, $p\colon S\to E$ is a $\mathbb Q$-homology fiber bundle. 
    In particular, $R^ip_* \mathbb{Q}_S$ is a local system for every $i$. 

    Away from the branch locus of $p_C$, $p$ is an $F$-fiber bundle, and the monodromy action is given by translation. 
    Thus, the restriction of $R^ip_* \mathbb{Q}_S$ to $E^\circ\coloneq E\setminus \operatorname{Br}(p_C)$ is the trivial local system. 
    Since the inclusion $E^\circ\to E$ induces a surjective map on fundamental groups, $R^ip_* \mathbb{Q}_S$ is a trivial local system for all $i$. 
    Thus, the isomorphism \eqref{eq:trivial LS} follows from the decomposition theorem \cite{BBD} and the fact that a general fiber of $p$ is an elliptic curve.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:sum} 
There are $a_i\in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ with  
\[
R{h}_* \mathbb{Q}_{X} \cong \bigoplus_{0\leq i\leq 2N}\mathbb{Q}_{E}[-i]^{\oplus a_i} .
\]
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Recall that ${h}\colon {X}\to {E}$ is equal to the composition of the blow-up map ${X}\to \mathbb{P}^N\times {E}$ and the projection $\mathbb{P}^N\times {E}\to {E}$. 
Taking the derived pushforward of $\mathbb{Q}_{X}$ to $\mathbb{P}^N\times {E}$, we obtain a direct sum of the constant sheaf $\mathbb{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^N\times {E}}$ with a direct sum of shifted trivial local systems on ${S}$, cf.~\eqref{eq:derived-blow-up-formula}. 
Note further that the derived pushforwards of $\mathbb{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^N\times {E}}$ and $\mathbb{Q}_{S}$ to ${E}$ are direct sums of shifted trivial local systems on ${E}$. 
Hence, $R{h}_*(\mathbb{Q}_{X})$ is isomorphic to a direct sum of shifted trivial local systems on ${E}$.
The precise version in the corollary follows because $h$ has relative dimension $N$.
<!-- end proof-11 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Notice that $h: X\to E$ induces an isomorphism on the fundamental groups. Thus, $\pi_1(X)\cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ is abelian, and it suffices to consider rank-one local system $V=h^*V_E$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:sum} and the projection formula, 
\[
Rh_*(V)\cong \bigoplus_{0\leq j\leq 2N}V_{E}[-j]^{\oplus a_j}.
\]
If $V_E$ is nontrivial, then $H^*(E, V_E)=0$. Hence, $H^{*}(X, V)=0$, and the assertion follows. 

If $V_E$ is trivial, then the above decomposition gives
\[
H^*(X, V)\cong \bigoplus_{0\leq j\leq 2N}H^{*}(E, \mathbb{C})[-j]^{\oplus a_j}
\]
as graded $H^{*}(E, \mathbb{C})$-modules. In particular, $H^*(X, V)$ is a free graded $H^*(E, \mathbb{C})$-module. 

Since $h^*: H^1(E, \mathbb{C})\to H^1(X, \mathbb{C})$ is an isomorphism, we can write $\omega=h^*\alpha$ for some nonzero $\alpha\in H^1(E, \mathbb{C})$. Since the complex $(H^*(E, \mathbb{C}), \wedge \alpha)$ is exact, the complex $(H^*(X, V), \wedge \omega)$ is also exact.

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Let $X$ be as in Section \ref{sec:example}.
By Lemma \ref{lem:relative-generic-projection}, we can choose $N=4$ and so $\dim X=5$.
Let $V$ be any semisimple local system on $X$. 
By Proposition \ref{prop:vanishing}, for any nonzero $\omega\in H^0(X,\Omega^1_{X})$, the Aomoto complex $(H^\ast(X,V),\wedge \omega)$ is exact.
It remains to show that any real closed one-form on $X$ has a zero.
Assume, for contradiction, that $X$ admits a real closed one-form without zeros.
Then $X$ fibers smoothly over $S^1$, see \cite{T}.  
We can pick such a $C^\infty$-fibration $X\to S^1$ such that $\pi_1(X)\to \pi_1(S^1)$ is surjective. 
Let $\widetilde X\to X$ be the infinite cyclic cover corresponding to the kernel of $\pi_1(X)\to \pi_1(S^1)$.
Then $\widetilde X$ is homotopy equivalent to a fiber of $X\to S^1$, hence has the homotopy type of a finite CW complex.

Recall that $h\colon X\to E$ induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$ and let $\widetilde E\to E$ be the infinite cyclic covering, induced by the kernel of $\pi_1(E)\cong \pi_1(X)\to \pi_1(S^1)$.
Then $h$ induces a natural map $\tilde h\colon \widetilde X\to \widetilde E$. 
This map is not a $\mathbb Z$-homology fiber bundle. 
Indeed, $R^{4}\widetilde h_\ast \mathbb Z$ fails to be locally constant at every
point of $\widetilde E$ lying above the branch locus of $p_C\colon C\to E$, and this is an infinite set. 
Proposition \ref{prop:D-finite} therefore implies that $H^{i}(\widetilde X,\mathbb Z)$ is not finitely generated for some $i$.
In particular, $\widetilde X$ does not have the homotopy type of a
finite CW complex. 
This contradiction completes the proof of the theorem.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:foliated-geometry}
With the notation above, the following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Over the regular values of $h$, the fibers of $h$ are compact leaves of $\mathcal F_\omega$.
\item Every regular leaf of $\ker\alpha_c$ is an immersed Levi-flat hypersurface \cite{BrunellaLevi}, and its Levi foliation is the restriction of $\mathcal F_\omega$.
\item If $\Gamma_c$ is discrete, then, after rescaling, there is a submersion
$
q_c\colon E\longrightarrow S^1
$
such that $q_c^*d\vartheta=\beta_c$. For $H_c=q_c\circ h$, every regular fiber of $H_c$ is a compact Levi-flat leaf of $\ker\alpha_c$, fibered over a circle by compact leaves of $\mathcal F_\omega$.
\item If $\Gamma_c$ is dense in $\mathbb R$ and $\ell$ is a leaf of $\ker\beta_c$ which does not contain a critical value of $h$, then $h^{-1}(\ell)$ is a leaf of $\ker\alpha_c$ and is dense in $X$. In particular, this holds for all but finitely many leaves of $\ker\beta_c$.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover,
$
\operatorname{Sing}(\ker\alpha_c)=Z(\omega)=\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal F_\omega)=\operatorname{Crit}(h).
$
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Since $\eta$ is nowhere vanishing, $Z(\omega)$ is the critical locus of $h$ and $\ker\omega=\ker dh$ away from $Z(\omega)$. Corollary~\ref{cor:sum} also gives
$
H^0(h^{-1}(t),\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Q}
$
for every $t\in E$. Thus the fibers of $h$ are connected, and (i) follows.

Since
$
\ker\omega=\ker\alpha_c\cap J\ker\alpha_c,
$
the complex tangent bundle of every regular leaf of $\ker\alpha_c$ is $\mathcal F_\omega$. This proves (ii).

The form $\beta_c$ defines a linear foliation on the real two-torus underlying $E$. If $\Gamma_c$ is discrete, a nonzero multiple of $\beta_c$ has primitive integral periods. Integration gives a submersion $q_c\colon E\to S^1$ with $q_c^*d\vartheta=\beta_c$, as in Tischler's theorem \cite{T}. Since $\alpha_c=h^*\beta_c$, we have
$
H_c^*d\vartheta=\alpha_c.
$
This proves (iii).

If $\Gamma_c$ is dense in $\mathbb R$, every leaf of $\ker\beta_c$ is dense in $E$ \cite{CandelConlon}. Let $B\subset E$ be the finite set of critical values of $h$ and let $\ell$ be a leaf disjoint from $B$. Since $h$ is a smooth fibration with connected fibers over $E\setminus B$, $h^{-1}(\ell)$ is a leaf of $\ker\alpha_c$. Let $U\subset E\setminus B$ be any sufficiently small disc over which $h$ is a product. Since $\ell\cap U$ is dense in $U$, the closure of $h^{-1}(\ell)$ contains $h^{-1}(U)$.
Since this holds for every such $U$, the closure contains $h^{-1}(E\setminus B)$, and hence $h^{-1}(\ell)$ is dense in $X$. Since every point of $B$ lies on one leaf of $\ker\beta_c$, this proves (iv).

Finally, $\operatorname{Re}(c\omega)$ vanishes at a point if and only if $\omega$ does. Together with $Z(\omega)=\operatorname{Crit}(h)$, this proves the last assertion.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
Propositions~\ref{prop:vanishing} and~\ref{prop:foliated-geometry} show that the semisimple Aomoto condition does not detect the singularities or the transverse dynamics of the foliation defined by a holomorphic one-form. Indeed, for every semisimple local system $V$ on $X$ and every nonzero $\omega\in H^1(X,\mathbb C)$, the corresponding Aomoto complex is exact, whereas for a nonzero holomorphic one-form $\omega=h^*\eta$,
$
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal F_\omega)=Z(\omega)=\operatorname{Crit}(h)\neq\varnothing.
$
Moreover, if $\rho_\omega\colon\pi_1(X)\to\mathbb C$ denotes the period homomorphism, then the characters $\gamma\mapsto\exp(t\rho_\omega(\gamma))$, $t\in\mathbb C$, generally have infinite image. Such characters cannot be reduced to finite monodromy by passing to a finite \'etale cover. Thus the semisimple condition contains information beyond that coming from finite \'etale covers. It also does not distinguish the compact and dense behavior of the real foliations $\ker\operatorname{Re}(c\omega)$, which is governed by the period group $\Gamma_c$.
