<!-- question-1: body_md -->
\label{q:forget-only-possible}
Is forget-only unlearning always possible? If not, what conditions
make it impossible?
<!-- end question-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
Let \(\mathcal{A}\) be hard-margin linear SVM in dimension \(d\geq 2\).
Then, no forget-only unlearner with non-trivial utility can satisfy
\((\alpha,\varepsilon)\)-R\'{e}nyi unlearning with a fixed finite
\(\varepsilon\) uniformly over all datasets and deletion requests.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- question-2: body_md -->
\label{q:memorization-needed}
How much information about the training dataset must the learned model
memorize in order to support future forget-only unlearning requests?
<!-- end question-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Let \(S\) be a training dataset, \(M=\mathcal{A}(S)\),
\((\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{\bar{A}})\) satisfy \(\left({\alpha,\varepsilon}\right)\)-Rényi unlearning,
and \(U_1,\ldots,U_K\) be the unlearning requests. For each request,
let \(W_i=\mathcal{A}(S\setminus U_i)\) be the corresponding
retrained model. Then, for any ordering \(\pi\) of the requests,
\[
\begin{aligned}
I(M;S)
\gtrsim{}&
\sum_{i=1}^K
H\!\left(
W_{\pi(i)}
\mid W_{\pi(<i)}, U_{\pi(\leq i)}
\right)-
K\cdot\mathrm{err}(\alpha,\varepsilon).
\end{aligned}
\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:renyi-unlearning}
Fix \(\alpha>1\) and \(\varepsilon\geq0\). We say that
\(\left({\mathcal{A},\mathcal{\bar{A}}}\right)\) satisfies
\(\left({\alpha,\varepsilon}\right)\)-Rényi unlearning (RU) over deletion sets of
size at most \(m\) if, for every dataset \(S\in\cX^n\) and every
request \(U\subseteq S\) with \(|U|\leq m\),
\[
    d_\alpha\left({
        \mathcal{\bar{A}}\left({\mathcal{A}(S),U}\right),
        \mathcal{\bar{A}}\left({\mathcal{A}(S\setminus U),\emptyset}\right)
    }\right)
    \le \varepsilon .
\]
When \(m\) is clear from context, we simply say that
\(\left({\mathcal{A},\mathcal{\bar{A}}}\right)\) satisfies
\(\left({\alpha,\varepsilon}\right)\)-R\'enyi unlearning.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:gamma-utility}
Fix a metric \(d_{\cW}\) on \(\cW\) and a non-increasing function
\(\Gamma:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]\). For each \(w\in\cW\), let
\(Q_w\coloneqq\mathrm{Law}(\mathcal{\bar{A}}(w,\emptyset))\). We say that \(\mathcal{\bar{A}}\)
has \((d_{\cW},\Gamma)\)-utility on empty requests if, for every
\(w\in\cW\) and every radius \(r\geq0\),
\[
    \@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{P}\right)_{W^\emptyset\sim Q_w}
    \!\left[d_{\cW}(W^\emptyset,w)>r\right]\leq\Gamma(r).
\]
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:gaussian-utility}
Suppose \(\cW=\mathbb R^d\) and
\(\mathcal{\bar{A}}(w,\emptyset)=w+Z\), where
\(Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_d)\). Then \(\mathcal{\bar{A}}\) has
\(\left({\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_2,\Gamma}\right)\)-utility with
\(\Gamma(r)=1\) for \(r<\sigma\sqrt d\), and
\(\Gamma(r)=\exp\left({-(r-\sigma\sqrt d)^2/(2\sigma^2)}\right)\) otherwise.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:deletion-gap}
Fix a learning algorithm \(\mathcal{A}\), a norm \(\phi\), integers
\(N\ge2\) and \(m\ge0\), and parameters \(\alpha>1\), \(\Delta>0\),
\(\delta_\alpha\ge0\), and \(\kappa\in[0,1]\).  We call
\((S_1,\ldots,S_N,U)\), with \(S_i\in\cX^n\), an
\((N,m,\Delta,\phi;\delta_\alpha,\kappa)\)-\emph{shared-deletion
example} (s.d.e.) for \(\mathcal{A}\) if:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \(U\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^N S_i\) and \(|U|\le m\).

    \item For every distinct \(i,j\in[N]\),
    \(
        d_\alpha\bigl(\mathcal{A}(S_i),\mathcal{A}(S_j)\bigr)
        \le \delta_\alpha.
    \)

    \item There exist measurable sets \(E_1,\ldots,E_N\subseteq\cW\)
    such that, for every distinct \(i,j\in[N]\),
    \(
        \phi(x-z)\ge\Delta\) for all \(x\in E_i,\ z\in E_j,
    \)
    and, for every \(i\in[N]\),
    \(

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:concentration-impossibility}
Fix \(\alpha >1\). Let \(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{\bar{A}}\) be
\(\left({\alpha,\varepsilon}\right)\)-RU for deletion requests of size at most
$m$ and let \(\mathcal{\bar{A}}\) have $(\phi, \Gamma)$-utility on empty
requests. If \(\mathcal{A}\) admits an \((N, m, \Delta, \phi;
\delta_\alpha, \kappa)\)-s.d.e., then it must hold that:

\noindent \textbf{Exact collision} ($\delta_\alpha = 0$):
    \[\varepsilon \geq
        \sup_{r \in R}
        \max \left\{{
            \log N + \frac{1}{\gamma} \log (1 - u(r)),
            \frac{1}{\gamma} \log \frac{N-1}{N} - \log u(r)
        }\right\}.
        \]
\noindent \textbf{Approximate collision} ($\delta_\alpha > 0$):
    \[
        \varepsilon \geq
        \sup_{r \in R}
        \max \left\{{
            \gamma \left({\log N - \delta_\alpha}\right)
            + \frac{1}{\gamma} \log (1 - u(r)),
            \frac{1}{\gamma^2} \log \frac{N-1}{N} - \frac1\gamma \delta_\alpha - \log u(r)
        }\right\},
    \]
where $R \coloneqq \left\{{r \in (0, \Delta/2): 0 < u(r) < 1}\right\}$, $u(r) = \kappa + \Gamma(r)$, and \(\gamma=\frac{\alpha -1}{\alpha}\).

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Assume first $\delta_\alpha = 0$. Write $W_i \coloneqq \mathcal{A}(S_i
\setminus U)$ and $Q_i \coloneqq \mathrm{Law}(\mathcal{\bar{A}}(W_i, \emptyset))$.
Since $\mathcal{A}(S_i)$ has the same law for all $i$ and $\mathcal{\bar{A}}$
is forget-only, all $N$ deletion requests have the same output law $T
\coloneqq \mathrm{Law}(\mathcal{\bar{A}}(M, U))$, $M \sim \mathrm{Law}\left({\mathcal{A}(S_1)}\right)$, and the
unlearning guarantee gives $ 
  D_{\IfNoValueTF{Q_i\,\|\,T}{\alpha}{Q_i\,\|\,T}}\!\left( \le \right) 
 \varepsilon$ and
$ 
  D_{\IfNoValueTF{T\,\|\,Q_i}{\alpha}{T\,\|\,Q_i}}\!\left( \le \right) 
 \varepsilon$ for every $i$.

Fix $r \in (0, \Delta/2)$ with $0 < u(r) < 1$. By the separation
property of shared deletion examples (property $(3)$) and
\Cref{lem:renyi-from-concentration}, there are disjoint sets $B_i$
with $Q_i(B_i) \ge 1 - u(r)$ such that each retraining target
concentrates on its own set, yet the single law $T$ must stay close to
all of them.  Then, using the R\'enyi unlearning inequality (\Cref{lem:renyi-event-transfer}), we get
that \(T\) must give some probability mass to every \(B_i\):
\[
    1 - u(r) \le Q_i(B_i) \le e^{\gamma\varepsilon}\, T(B_i)^{\gamma}
    \implies
    T(B_i) \ge e^{-\varepsilon} \left({1 - u(r)}\right)^{1/\gamma}.
\]
However, since $T$ has only one unit of mass to share among the disjoint
sets:
\[
    1 \ge \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{N} T(B_i) \ge N e^{-\varepsilon} \left({1 - u(r)}\right)^{1/\gamma}
    \implies
    \varepsilon \ge \log N + \tfrac{1}{\gamma} \log\left({1 - u(r)}\right) .
\]
This is the first term of the maximum. The second follows from the same steps
applied to $B_i^c$ with $ 
  D_{\IfNoValueTF{T\,\|\,Q_i}{\alpha}{T\,\|\,Q_i}}\!\left( \le \right) 
 \varepsilon$, which forces $T$
to stay inside every $B_i$, giving $T(B_i) \ge 1 - e^{\gamma\varepsilon} u(r)^{\gamma}$.
Taking the supremum over $r$ gives the exact collision lower bound.

For $\delta_\alpha > 0$, the unlearned laws $T_i$ may differ, but data
processing gives $d_\alpha(T_i, T_1) \le \delta_\alpha$. Each estimate then
passes through $T_i$ first; this extra application
of~\Cref{lem:renyi-event-transfer} replaces $\gamma$ by $\gamma^2$ and adds the
$\delta_\alpha$ penalties in the approximate collision lower bound.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
We would like to highlight a consequence of our lower bounds, which can hold for a variety of algorithms. For instance, in the case of the canonical threshold ERM learner, the algorithm is simply outputting a real number - the threshold - from a finite set of possible thresholds. Unlearning, on the other hand, imposes the requirement to retain significantly more information than just this single number. However, there is no way to encode all of this additional information into that one number while remaining within the original model space. This implies that we may need to move to a different output space in order to output this additional information, and possibly design a different algorithm for the unlearning problem.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:renyi-event-transfer}
Let \(\alpha>1\), let \(\gamma=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha},\) and suppose
\(D_\alpha(P\|Q)\le \varepsilon\). Then, for every measurable event
\(E\),
\(
    P(E)
    \le
    e^{\gamma\varepsilon} Q(E)^\gamma .
\)
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let \(\mu\) be a common dominating measure for \(P\) and \(Q\), meaning that \(P\) and \(Q\) are both absolutely continuous with respect to \(\mu\): if \(\mu(A)=0\), then \(P(A)=Q(A)=0\). Such a common dominating measure always exists; for example, one may take \(\mu = 1/2(P+Q)\). Define
    \[
    p \coloneqq \frac{dP}{d\mu},
    \qquad
    q \coloneqq \frac{dQ}{d\mu}.
    \]
    The Rényi divergence can be written as,
    \begin{equation}
         
  D_{\IfNoValueTF{P\,\|\,Q}{\alpha}{P\,\|\,Q}}\!\left( = \right) 
 \frac{1}{\alpha-1} \log \int p(x)^\alpha q(x)^{1-\alpha} d\mu(x).
    \end{equation}
    Define the Radon-Nikodym derivative of $P$ w.r.t. $Q$ as,
    \begin{equation}
        L(x) \coloneqq \frac{dP}{dQ} (x) = \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}.
    \end{equation}
    Then we have,
    \begin{equation}
        \int L(x)^\alpha dQ(x) 
        = \int \left( \frac{p(x)}{q(x)} \right)^\alpha q(x) d\mu(x)
        = \int p(x)^\alpha q(x)^{1 - \alpha} d \mu(x),
    \end{equation}
    which implies that,
    \begin{gather}
    \begin{aligned}
         
  D_{\IfNoValueTF{P\,\|\,Q}{\alpha}{P\,\|\,Q}}\!\left( = \right) 
 \frac{1}{\alpha-1} & \log \int L(x)^\alpha dQ(x)
        = \frac{1}{\alpha - 1} \log \@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\right)_Q (L^\alpha)
        \leq \varepsilon \\
        & \implies \@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\right)_Q (L^\alpha) \leq e^{(\alpha-1)\varepsilon}.
    \end{aligned}
    \end{gather}
    For any event $A$, we have,
    \begin{equation}
        P(A) = \int_A dP(x) = \int_A L(x) \; dQ(x) = \@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\right)_Q (L \; \mathds{1}_A).
    \end{equation}
    Using  H\"{o}lder's inequality with $m=\alpha$ and $n=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}$ ($\frac{1}{m} + \frac{1}{n} = 1$), we finally get, 
    \begin{gather}
    \begin{aligned}
        P(A)
        &= \@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\right)_Q (L \; \mathds{1}_A) 
        \leq (\@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\right)_Q L^m)^{1/m} \; (\@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\right)_Q \mathds{1}_A^n)^{1/n} \\
        &= (\@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\right)_Q L^\alpha)^{1/\alpha} \; (\@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\right)_Q \mathds{1}_A)^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha}
        \leq e^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha \; \varepsilon} \; Q(A)^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha}.
    \end{aligned}
    \end{gather}
    This completes the proof.

        }\right\},
    \)
    and $\gamma = \frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}$.

    For each pair $i \neq j$, the sets $E_i, E_j$ from the
    shared deletion example satisfy
    \[
        \left({
            \forall z_i \in E_i
        }\right)
        \left({
            \forall z_j \in E_j
        }\right)
        \quad
        \phi( z_i - z_j ) \geq \Delta > 2r,

    \(
        \@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{P}\right)_{W_k \sim P_k} \left({W_k \in E_k}\right) \geq 1 - \kappa
    \)
    for $k \in \left\{{i, j}\right\}$.
    Applying~\Cref{lem:renyi-from-concentration} to the laws $P_i, P_j$ and the
    sets $E_i, E_j$, we get that $B_i \cap B_j = \emptyset$ and
    $Q_i(B_i) \geq 1 - u(r)$, implying $Q_i(B_i^c) \leq u(r)$.
    Since this holds for every pair $i \neq j$, the sets $B_1, \dots, B_N$ are
    pairwise disjoint.

    The unlearning assumption gives $d_\alpha (Q_i, T_i) \leq \varepsilon$ for all $i \in [N]$.
    As $T_i$ is the image of $M_i$ under the map $w \to \mathcal{\bar{A}}(w, U)$, by the data processing inequality, we have that, for $i \neq j$,
    \[
        d_\alpha(T_i, T_j) \leq d_\alpha(M_i, M_j) \leq \delta_\alpha.
    \]
    Specifically, we will use $d_\alpha(T_1, T_i) \leq \delta_\alpha$.

    As the sets $B_1, \dots, B_N$ are disjoint, it holds that,
    \(
        \sum_{i=1}^N T_1(B_i) \leq 1.
    \)

    We first analyze the exact collision case, which also covers the deterministic learners. The zero-collision condition forces $T_i = T_1$, which implies $d_\alpha(Q_i, T_1) \leq \varepsilon$ for all $i \in [N]$.
    Using~\Cref{lem:renyi-event-transfer} in two directions, together with the unlearning assumption, we have,
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
        1 - u(r) \leq Q_i(B_i) \leq e^{\gamma \varepsilon} T_1(B_i)^\gamma
        &\implies
        T_1(B_i) \geq e^{-\varepsilon} \left({1 - u(r)}\right)^{1 / \gamma}, \\
        1 - T_1(B_i) = T_1(B_i^c) \leq e^{\gamma \varepsilon} Q_i(B_i^c)^\gamma \leq e^{\gamma \varepsilon} u(r)^\gamma
        &\implies
        T_1(B_i) \geq 1 - e^{\gamma \varepsilon} u(r)^\gamma
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    Summing each lower bound over $i$ and using
    \(
        \sum_{i=1}^N T_1(B_i) \leq 1,
    \)
    we get the final two terms depending on $r$.

    In the approximate collision case, we use~\Cref{lem:renyi-event-transfer} twice to get,
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
        &1 - u(r) 
        \leq Q_i(B_i) 
        \leq e^{\gamma \varepsilon} T_i(B_i)^\gamma
        \leq e^{\gamma \varepsilon + \gamma^2 \delta_\alpha} T_1(B_i)^{\gamma^2}
        \implies
        T_1 (B_i) \geq \left({
            1 - u(r)}\right)^{1 / \gamma^2}
         e^{-\varepsilon/\gamma - \delta_\alpha}, \\
        &T_1(B_i^c) 
        \leq e^{\gamma \delta_\alpha} T_i(B_i^c)^\gamma
        \leq e^{\gamma^2 \varepsilon + \gamma \delta_\alpha}
        Q_i(B_i^c)^{\gamma^2}
        \leq e^{\gamma^2 \varepsilon + \gamma \delta_\alpha} u(r)^{\gamma^2}
        \implies
        T_1(B_i) \geq 1 - e^{\gamma^2 \varepsilon + \gamma \delta_\alpha} u(r)^{\gamma^2},
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    Here we used that $x \mapsto x^\gamma$ is increasing. Summing the first lower bound over the disjoint sets $B_i$ gives
    \[
        1 \geq \sum_{i=1}^N T_1(B_i)
        \geq N \left({1-u(r)}\right)^{1/\gamma^2}
        e^{-\varepsilon/\gamma-\delta_\alpha}.
    \]
    Taking logarithms and rearranging,
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}
        &\geq \log N - \delta_\alpha
        + \frac{1}{\gamma^2}\log(1-u(r)), \\
        \varepsilon
        &\geq \gamma\left({\log N-\delta_\alpha}\right)
        + \frac{1}{\gamma}\log(1-u(r)).
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    Similarly, summing the second lower bound gives
    \[
        1 \geq \sum_{i=1}^N T_1(B_i)
        \geq N\left({1-e^{\gamma^2\varepsilon+\gamma\delta_\alpha}u(r)^{\gamma^2}}\right),
    \]
    hence
    \[
        e^{\gamma^2\varepsilon+\gamma\delta_\alpha}u(r)^{\gamma^2}
        \geq \frac{N-1}{N}.
    \]
    Taking logarithms and dividing by $\gamma^2$ yields
    \[
        \varepsilon \geq
        \frac{1}{\gamma^2}\log\frac{N-1}{N}
        -\frac{\delta_\alpha}{\gamma}-\log u(r).
    \]

    In both cases, taking the supremum over $\left\{{r \in (0, \Delta/2): 0 < u(r) < 1}\right\}$ gives the final bounds.

\label{def:teaching-dim}
    Let $\cX$ be an instance space, and let $\cH \subseteq \left\{{0,1}\right\}^{\cX}$ be a hypothesis class over $\cX$.
    For a hypothesis $h \in \cH$, a set $T \subseteq \cX \times \left\{{0,1}\right\}$ is called a \textit{teaching set} for $h$ if $h$ is consistent with $T$ and no other hypothesis $h' \in \cH \setminus \{h\}$ is consistent with $T$.
    Let $\mathcal{T}(h)$ denote the collection of all teaching sets for $h$.
    The \textit{teaching dimension} of $\cH$ is defined as
    \[
        \mathrm{TD}(\cH)
        \coloneqq
        \max_{h \in \cH}
        \min_{T \in \mathcal{T}(h)}
        |T|.
    \]

Let $h_0 \in \cH$ have a teaching set $T_0$. A teaching set size is upper-bounded by the teaching dimension, hence $|T_0| \leq \tau$. Similarly, let $R_i$ be teaching sets for $h_i$, with $|R_i| \leq \tau$ as well.
    Construct the common unlearning request as $\tau+1$ copies of $T_0$: $U = \uplus_{i=1}^{\tau+1} T_0$, so
    $|U| \leq \tau (\tau + 1)$. Set $S_i = R_i \cup U$, so $|S_i| \leq \tau (\tau + 2)$.

    Consider first the hypothesis $h_0$. On $S_i$, it errs only on the retain part $R_i$. $U$ consists of copies of $T_0$, which is perfectly fitted by $h_0$ by construction. As $|R_i| \leq \tau$, $h_0$ can make at most $\tau$ mistakes on it. On the other hand, $h_i \neq h_0$ perfectly fits the retain part $R_i$. However, on each of the $\tau+1$ copies of $T_0$ in $U$, it must make at least one mistake, as $h_0$ is the only hypothesis that perfectly fits $U$. Therefore, $h_i$ makes at least $\tau + 1$ mistakes on $S_i$. Combining these two results, we obtain that $\mathcal{A}(S_i) = h_0$ for any $i \in [N]$. The retrained targets are $\mathcal{A}(S_i \setminus U) = \mathcal{A}(R_i) = h_i$, again by the teaching set property.

    Therefore, the above implies that $N$ different datasets exactly collide under any ERM $\mathcal{A}$, but are strictly separated under retraining.

\mathcal{A}_c^{\mathrm{th}}(S_2\setminus U)-\mathcal{A}_c^{\mathrm{th}}(S_1\setminus U)
\le
\frac{c-b_2}{2}
+
\frac{a_1+c}{2}.

\((c-b_2)/2\le c-\frac12\), since \(b_2\ge -c+1\).

\(a_1+b_1=a_2+b_2\), these inequalities imply \(a_1=a_2\) and
\(b_1=b_2\).

\[
\mathcal{A}_c^{\mathrm{th}}(S_2\setminus U)-\mathcal{A}_c^{\mathrm{th}}(S_1\setminus U)
\le
\frac{a+c}{2}+\frac{c-b}{2}
=
c-\frac{b-a}{2}
\le
c-\frac12,
\]
because \(a,b\in\mathcal X_c\) and \(a<b\). Thus the largest admissible
separation in a \((2,m,\Delta,\left| {\cdot} \right|;0,0)\)-s.d.e. is
\(\Delta=c-\frac12\).

To prove the memorization lower bound, we start from the result of~\Cref{pp:information-lb} and will bound the term,
    \[
        I \left(
            W_{\pi(i)}; \overline{W}_{\pi(i)} \mid
            W_{\pi(<i)}, U_{\pi(\le i)}
        \right).
    \]
    Assuming the model space \( \mathcal{W} \) is discrete and finite, we have that,
    \begin{gather}
    \begin{aligned}
        I \left(
            W_{\pi(i)}; \overline{W}_{\pi(i)} \mid
            W_{\pi(<i)}, U_{\pi(\le i)}
        \right)
        &=
        H \left(
            W_{\pi(i)} |  W_{\pi(<i)}, U_{\pi(\le i)}
        \right)
        -
        H \left(
            W_{\pi(i)} |  \overline{W}_{\pi(i)}, W_{\pi(<i)}, U_{\pi(\le i)}
        \right) \\
        &\overset{(a)}{\geq}
        H \left(
            W_{\pi(i)} |  W_{\pi(<i)}, U_{\pi(\le i)}
        \right)
        -
        H \left(
            W_{\pi(i)} |  \overline{W}_{\pi(i)}
        \right),
    \end{aligned}
    \end{gather}
    where $(a)$ uses that conditioning reduces entropy.

    By Fano's inequality applied to the pair
    \[
        \left(
            W_{\pi(i)},\,
            \overline{W}_{\pi(i)}
        \right),
    \]
    we get
    \[
        H\left(
            W_{\pi(i)}
            \mid
            \overline{W}_{\pi(i)}
        \right)
        \leq
        h(P_{e,\pi(i)})
        +
        P_{e,\pi(i)}\log(|\mathcal W|-1)
        =:
        F(P_{e,\pi(i)})  
        ,
    \]
    where
    \[
        P_{e,\pi(i)}
        \coloneqq
        \@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{P}\right){
            \overline{W}_{\pi(i)}\neq W_{\pi(i)}
        }.
    \]
    Moreover,
    \[
        P_{e,\pi(i)}
        =
        \@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\right)_{S,U_{\pi(i)}}
        \left[
        \@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{P}\right){
            \overline{W}_{\pi(i)}\neq W_{\pi(i)}
            \mid S,U_{\pi(i)}
        }
        \right]
        \leq
        \@ifnextchar\bgroup\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\right)_{S,U_{\pi(i)}}[\beta_{\mathrm{un}}]
        =
        \beta_{\mathrm{un}}
        \leq
        \bar\beta .
    \]
    The function $F$ is increasing on $[0, 1 - \frac{1}{|\mathcal W|}]$, since
    $F'(p) = \log\frac{(1-p)(|\mathcal W|-1)}{p} \geq 0$ there, and it attains its maximum
    $\log|\mathcal W|$ at $p = 1 - \frac{1}{|\mathcal W|}$. Hence, with
    $\tilde\beta \coloneqq \min\{\bar\beta,\, 1 - \frac{1}{|\mathcal W|}\}$, we have
    $F(P_{e,\pi(i)}) \leq F(\tilde\beta)$: by monotonicity if $\bar\beta \leq 1 - \frac{1}{|\mathcal W|}$,

    \[
        H\left(
            W_{\pi(i)}
            \mid
            \overline{W}_{\pi(i)}
        \right)
        \le
        h(\tilde\beta) + \tilde\beta\log(|\mathcal W|-1).

        I\left(
            W_{\pi(i)};\overline{W}_{\pi(i)}
            \mid
            W_{\pi(<i)},U_{\pi(\le i)}
        \right)
        \ge
        H\left(
            W_{\pi(i)}
            \mid
            W_{\pi(<i)},U_{\pi(\le i)}
        \right)
        -
        h(\tilde\beta)  
        -
        \tilde\beta\log(|\mathcal W|-1).  
    \]
    Summing over \(i=1,\ldots,K\) and using~\Cref{pp:information-lb} gives the claim.

The main idea is that, for one-hot inputs, \(\left\langle{w},{e_a}\right\rangle=w_a\)
    for every \(a\in[d]\). Therefore, the ridge objective separates
    into independent one-dimensional problems. For a dataset \(D\),
    its unique minimizer satisfies
    \[\left[{\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ridge}}(D)}\right]_a=T_a(D)/(N_a(D)+\lambda),\]
    where \(N_a(D)\) is the number of examples with input \(e_a\),
    and \(T_a(D)\) is the sum of their labels. We construct datasets
    with equally many copies of \((e_a,+1)\) and \((e_a,-1)\), so
    all fitted coefficients are zero regardless of \(N_a(D)\).
    Deleting one copy of \((e_a,+1)\) changes the corresponding label
    sum to \(-1\), making the retrained coefficient depend on
    \(N_a(D)\). Accurate unlearning therefore requires retaining
    information about these counts. The remaining examples in the
    construction ensure that the total dataset size stays fixed
    despite this randomness.

    Draw \(C_1,\ldots,C_b\) independently and uniformly from
    \(\left\{{1,2,3}\right\}\). Block \(j\) contains \(C_j\) copies of each of
    \((e_{2j-1},+1)\) and \((e_{2j-1},-1)\), and \(4-C_j\) copies
    of each of \((e_{2j},+1)\) and \((e_{2j},-1)\). Each block
    therefore has eight examples, giving \(n=8b\). Place one copy
    of \((e_{2j-1},+1)\) first in each block, order the remaining
    examples deterministically, and concatenate the blocks to obtain
    \(S=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\). Since all label sums are zero,
    \(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ridge}}(S)=0\).

    For each \(i\in[b]\), let
    \(U_i=\left\{{z_{8(i-1)+1}}\right\}=\left\{{(e_{2i-1},+1)}\right\}\). Both its index
    and value are fixed, so this singleton request reveals nothing
    about \(C_i\). After deletion, there are \(2C_i-1\) examples
    with input \(e_{2i-1}\), with label sum \(-1\), while all other
    label sums remain zero. Thus
    \[
        W_i=\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ridge}}(S\setminus U_i)
        =-\frac{1}{2C_i-1+\lambda}\,e_{2i-1}.
    \]
    The three values of \(C_i\) give three distinct retraining
    models it needs to match. Let \(\mathcal H_i=(W_{<i},U_{\le i})\).
    Previous targets reveal only \(C_1,\ldots,C_{i-1}\), and the

    \(H(W_i\mid\mathcal H_i)=\log3\). For \(\beta_3<2/3\),

    \]
    For \(\beta_3=2/3\), the bound follows from nonnegativity.
    Finally, the bare fitted vector is identically zero on this
    distribution, so \(I(M;S)_{\mathrm{bare}}=0\).

Set \(r=r_\star\). By definition of \(r_\star\), we have
    \(r^2\le d\) and \(r(m+1)\le n\). Since \(d>4\) and \(n>3m\), we have

    measurements of the form \((1,0_r,0)\). These impose no constraint
    and do not affect the fitted matrix.

    Draw a uniformly random permutation \(\sigma\) of \(\left[{r}\right]\). For
    each row \(i\in\left[{r}\right]\), include \(m\) indexed copies of the row-sum
    measurement
    \[
        c_i^{(a)}=(i,\mathbf 1_r,r),

    \[
        z_i=(i,e_{\sigma(i)},1).

    request may delete all \(m\) row-sum copies in a row.

    the data are consistent and \(\lambda\to0\), the learner selects,
    among interpolating matrices, one minimizing
    \[
        \sum_{i=1}^r \left\lVertx_i\right\rVert_2.

    \[
        \sum_{\ell=1}^r (x_i)_\ell=r,
        \qquad
        (x_i)_{\sigma(i)}=1.

    Cauchy--Schwarz,
    \[
        \sum_{\ell\neq\sigma(i)} (x_i)_\ell^2
        \ge
        \frac{(r-1)^2}{r-1}
        =
        r-1,
    \]
    with equality iff all remaining coordinates are equal to \(1\). Hence the
    unique minimum-norm interpolating row is
    \[
        x_i=\mathbf 1_r.
    \]

    \[
        M_{\mathrm{bare}}
        =
        \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fac}}(S)

        \mathbf 1_r\mathbf 1_r^\top.

        U_i=\left\{{c_i^{(1)},\ldots,c_i^{(m)}}\right\}.

    Finally, $M$ is deterministic on this distribution, so
    $I(M;S)_{\mathrm{bare}} = 0$.
