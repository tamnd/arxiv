<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:Complete}
Let $\mathrm{E}$ be a spectrum with each $\pi_i(\mathrm{E})$ a finitely-generated abelian group, and $\Gamma$ be a group admitting a finite $\underline{E}\Gamma$ and a torsion-free subgroup of finite index, then $\mathrm{E}^*(B\Gamma)$ is complete Hausdorff.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We use a theorem of Jankovski \cite{Jankovski} (see Yosimura \cite[Theorem 6 vii)]{Yosimura} for the form of this theorem we will use). This theorem requires $B\Gamma$ to have finite skeleta, which follows from the fact that there is a finite-index $\Gamma' \leq \Gamma$ which is torsion-free and hence acts freely on $\underline{E}\Gamma$, which is a contractible finite $\Gamma'$-CW-complex. Thus $B\Gamma$ has a finite cover which has finite skeleta, so $B\Gamma$ has finite skeleta too.

Then \cite[Theorem 6 vii)]{Yosimura} says that as long as for each $\ell$ the map
$$\mathrm{E}^i(B\Gamma)\otimes \mathbb{Q} \longrightarrow \prod_{k \leq \ell} \mathrm{H}^k(B\Gamma ; \pi_{i-k}(\mathrm{E})\otimes \mathbb{Q}),$$
given by the Chern--Dold character \cite[p.\ 172]{AdamsGuide} with target $\prod_k \mathrm{H}^k(- ; \pi_{i-k}(\mathrm{E})\otimes \mathbb{Q})$ composed by projection to the factors having $k \leq \ell$, 
is surjective, then $\mathrm{E}^*(B\Gamma)$ is complete Hausdorff. 

To verify this condition, we consider the map $B\Gamma = E\Gamma \times_\Gamma \underline{E}\Gamma \to \underline{B}\Gamma = * \times_\Gamma \underline{E}\Gamma$ and form the induced commutative diagram
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathrm{E}^i(\underline{B}\Gamma)\otimes \mathbb{Q} \rar \dar & \prod_{k \leq \ell} \mathrm{H}^k(\underline{B}\Gamma ; \pi_{i-k}(\mathrm{E})\otimes \mathbb{Q}) \dar\\
\mathrm{E}^i(B\Gamma)\otimes \mathbb{Q} \rar & \prod_{k \leq \ell} \mathrm{H}^k(B\Gamma ; \pi_{i-k}(\mathrm{E})\otimes \mathbb{Q}).
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
The right-hand vertical map is an isomorphism, as the fibres of $B\Gamma \to \underline{B}\Gamma$ are classifying spaces of finite groups and hence are rationally acyclic. As $\underline{B}\Gamma$ is a finite complex, the Chern--Dold character $\mathrm{ch} : \mathrm{E}^i(\underline{B}\Gamma)\otimes \mathbb{Q} \to \prod_{k} \mathrm{H}^k(\underline{B}\Gamma ; \pi_{i-k}(\mathrm{E})\otimes \mathbb{Q})$ is an isomorphism, so the top horizontal map is surjective. Thus the clockwise composition is surjective, and hence the bottom horizontal map is too.
<!-- end proof-1 -->

\label{lem:AnnOfx}
If $a \in \mathrm{MU}^*(BC_n)$ has $x_n \cdot a =0$, then $a = A \cdot \emptyset_n(x_n)$ for some $A \in \mathrm{MU}^{|a|}$.
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Representing $a$ by a series $f(x_n) \in \mathrm{MU}^*[[x_n]]$, the relation $x_n \cdot a =0$ means that $x_n \cdot f(x_n)$ is divisible by $[n](x_n) = x_n \cdot \emptyset_n(x_n)$ in $\mathrm{MU}^*[[x_n]]$, and so dividing by $x_n$  it shows that 
$$a = g(x_n) \cdot \emptyset_n(x_n)  \in \mathrm{MU}^*[[x_n]]/([n](x_n)) = \mathrm{MU}^*(BC_n)$$
for some $g(x_n) = \sum_{i=0}^\infty g_i x_n^i \in \mathrm{MU}^*[[x_n]]$.

We want to exploit that $x_n \cdot \emptyset_n(x_n)  =0 \in \mathrm{MU}^*(BC_n)$. As $\mathrm{MU}^*(BC_n)$ is complete and Hausdorff by Lemma \ref{lem:Complete}, the element $\left(\sum_{i=0}^\infty g_i x_n^i \right) \cdot \emptyset_n(x_n)$ agrees with the unique limit as $j \to \infty$ of the Cauchy sequence
$$\left(\sum_{i=0}^j g_i x_n^i \right) \cdot \emptyset_n(x_n) = g_0 \cdot \emptyset_n(x_n),$$
which is constant and so has limit $g_0  \cdot\emptyset_n(x_n)$. Thus $a$ has the claimed form.

<!-- rem-2: body_md -->

$$\kappa^{E}_{-1}(\pi) = \pi_!^{E}(1) \in \mathrm{E}^{-2}(B)$$
need not be zero. In fact, under Quillen's geometric interpretation \cite{QuillenCob} of complex cobordism, $\kappa^{MU}_{-1}(\pi) \in \mathrm{MU}^{-2}(B)$ is the class of the complex oriented map $\pi : E \to B$ itself.

<!-- lem-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:FiltEstimate}
Restricting along $\iota: \{*\} \to \mathcal{M}_g$ we have
$$\iota^*\kappa^E_{-1} = (1-g)[\mathbb{P}^1]_E \in \mathrm{E}^{-2} \quad\quad \iota^*\kappa^E_{0} = 2-2g \in \mathrm{E}^0 \quad\quad \iota^*\kappa^E_j=0 \in \mathrm{E}^{2j} \text{ for $j>0$}.$$
More precisely, we have
\begin{align*}
\kappa^E_{-1} - (1-g)[\mathbb{P}^1]_E &\in F^1 \mathrm{E}^{-2}(\mathcal{M}_g)\\
\kappa^E_{0} - (2-2g) &\in F^1 \mathrm{E}^{0}(\mathcal{M}_g)\\
\kappa^E_{j}  &\in F^{2j} \mathrm{E}^{2j}(\mathcal{M}_g) \text{ for $j>0$}.
\end{align*}
<!-- end lem-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We may establish this universally in the theory $\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{MU}$. Restricted to a point, we are forming the pushforwards along $\pi : \Sigma_g \to \{*\}$ of powers of the class $c_1^{MU}(T\Sigma_g) \in \mathrm{MU}^2(\Sigma_g)$. Tautologically $\pi_!(1) = [\Sigma_g] \in \mathrm{MU}^{-2} = MU_2$ is the cobordism class of the oriented surface of genus $g$, and by computing the first Chern number this is cobordant to $(1-g)$ copies of the projective line $\mathbb{P}^1$. The number $\pi_!(c_1^{MU}(T\Sigma_g)) \in \mathrm{MU}^0=\mathrm{MU}_0=\mathbb{Z}$ can be determined by applying $\mathrm{MU} \to \mathrm{H}$, so it is $\int_{\Sigma_g} c_1^H(T\Sigma_g)=2-2g$. For $j>0$ we have $\mathrm{MU}^{2j} = \mathrm{MU}_{-2j}=0$, so necessarily $\iota^*\kappa_j^{MU}=0$. 

The class $x \in \mathrm{MU}^2(\mathbb{CP}^\infty)$ vanishes when restricted to the 1-skeleton, so we have $c_1^{MU}(T_\pi) = \ell^*(x) \in F^2 \mathrm{MU}^2(\mathcal{C}_g)$, and so $c_1^{MU}(T_\pi)^{j+1} \in F^{2j+2} \mathrm{MU}^{2j+2}(\mathcal{C}_g)$. If $\mathcal{M}_g^{(p)}$ is a $p$-skeleton then $\mathcal{C}_g\vert_{\mathcal{M}_g^{(p)}}$ is a surface bundle over it so it has dimension $\leq p+2$, and hence $c_1^{MU}(T_\pi)^{j+1}$ vanishes on it if $p+2 < 2j+2$. But then $\kappa^{MU}_j\vert_{\mathcal{M}_g^{(p)}}$ vanishes if $p+2 < 2j+2$, so $\kappa^{MU}_j$ lies in $F^{2j} \mathrm{MU}^{2j}(\mathcal{M}_g)$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lem-5: body_md -->
Stable $\mathrm{E}$-theory cohomology operations preserve $R\mathrm{E}^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$.
<!-- end lem-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
If $\psi$ is a stable $\mathrm{E}$-theory cohomology operation then
$$\psi(\kappa^E_i) = c^* \mathrm{Th}(\ell)^*(\psi(x^{i+1} \cdot u_{-\gamma}^E)),$$
and $\psi(x^{i+1} \cdot u_{-\gamma} ) =  f_{\psi, i}(x) \cdot u_{-\gamma}^E  \in \mathrm{E}^*(\mathrm{MTSO}(2)) =   \mathrm{E}^{*+2}(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) \cdot u_{-\gamma}^E$ for a unique power series $f_{\psi, i}(x) = \sum_{j=0}^\infty a_j \cdot x^j \in \mathrm{E}^*(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) = \mathrm{E}^*[[x]]$. Hence
$$\psi(\kappa^E_i) = \sum_{j=0}^\infty a_j \cdot \kappa^E_{j-1} \in \mathrm{E}^*(\mathcal{M}_g),$$
where we use the completeness of $\mathrm{E}^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ and the filtration estimates in Lemma~\ref{lem:FiltEstimate} to interpret the right-hand side as a convergent Cauchy sequence.

<!-- lem-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:LNOps}
We have 
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $s_{i+1}(\kappa_{-1}^{MU}) = (-1)^{i+1} \kappa_i^{MU} \in \mathrm{MU}^{2i}(\mathcal{M}_g)$,

\item $s_j(\kappa_n^{MU}) = \binom{n}{j} \kappa_{n+j}^{MU} \in \mathrm{MU}^{2n+2j}(\mathcal{M}_g)$ for $n \geq 0$.

\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For (i), in $\mathrm{MU}^*(\mathrm{MTSO}(2)) = \mathrm{MU}^*(\mathbb{CP}^\infty) \cdot u_{-\gamma}$ we have

Let $\mathrm{E}$ be a complex oriented cohomology theory. Then the map

<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $\Gamma' \leq \Gamma$ be a torsion-free finite-index subgroup, which we may assume to be normal. Then $\underline{E}\Gamma$ is an $E\Gamma'$, and we let $G := \Gamma/\Gamma'$. Then we may take $EG \times \underline{E}\Gamma$ with the diagonal $\Gamma$-action as an $E\Gamma$, so that $B\Gamma = EG \times_G (\underline{E}\Gamma/\Gamma')$. Let us write $Y := \underline{E}\Gamma/\Gamma'$, which is a $B\Gamma'$ and a finite CW-complex. We now apply \cite[Theorem A]{HKR}, which says that the natural maps
$$\mathrm{E}^*(EG \times_G Y) \longrightarrow \lim_{A \in \mathcal{A}(G)} \mathrm{E}^*(EG \times_A Y) \longrightarrow \int_{A \in \mathcal{A}(G)} \mathrm{E}^*(BA \times Y^A)$$
are both isomorphisms after inverting $\vert G \vert$. (This uses the observation of \cite[Remark 3.5 (1)]{HKR} to restrict to abelian groups of prime power order.)

Consider $\mathrm{E}^*(BA \times Y^A)$ equipped with the Leray--Serre filtration with respect to the map $BA \times Y^A \to Y^A$. The edge homomorphism for the associated Atiyah--Hirzebruch--Serre spectral sequence has the form
$$\mathrm{E}^*(BA \times Y^A) \longrightarrow \mathrm{H}^0(Y^A ; \mathrm{E}^*(BA)),$$
where we may think of the target as the set $\mathrm{Map}(Y^A, \mathrm{E}^*(BA))$ of locally constant functions. This edge homomorphism is split surjective, by choosing a splitting of $Y^A \to \pi_0(Y^A)$. Its kernel consists of elements which have Serre filtration $\geq 1$, so their $d$-th powers have Serre filtration $\geq d$ and hence vanish as long as $d > \mathrm{dim}(Y^A)$: in particular this kernel is nilpotent. Using that the category $\mathcal{A}(G)$ is finite, it follows that
$$\int_{A \in \mathcal{A}(G)} \mathrm{E}^*(BA \times Y^A) \longrightarrow \int_{A \in \mathcal{A}(G)} \mathrm{Map}(Y^A, \mathrm{E}^*(BA))$$
is (split) surjective with nilpotent kernel. Finally, the argument of \cite[Lemma 15.4]{QuillenSpec}, translated into the language of ends, shows that the natural map
\begin{equation*}
\int_{A \in \mathcal{A}(G)} \mathrm{Map}(Y^A, \mathrm{E}^*(BA)) \longrightarrow \int_{A \in \mathcal{A}(\Gamma)} \mathrm{Map}(\underline{E}\Gamma^A, \mathrm{E}^*(BA))
\end{equation*}
is an isomorphism. As each $A \in \mathcal{A}(\Gamma)$ is in particular a finite group, the fixed points $\underline{E}\Gamma^A$ are contractible and in particular connected so the right hand side is $\int_{A \in \mathcal{A}(\Gamma)} \mathrm{E}^*(BA)$, which is precisely the limit in the statement of the theorem.

\label{thm:LocalisationTheorem}
Let $G$ be finite and $\rho$ be a faithful action. Let $S \subset \Sigma$ be the (finite) set of points having nontrivial isotropy, and write $G_p \leq G$ for the isotropy group at a point $p \in \Sigma$ (which acts faithfully on $T_p\Sigma$ so is cyclic). 
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item If $y \in \mathrm{MU}^i_G(\Sigma) := \mathrm{MU}^i(EG \times_G \Sigma)$ has degree $i > 2$ then
$$\pi_!^{MU}(y) = \sum_{[p] \in S/G} \mathrm{Ind}_{G_p}^G\left(\frac{y\vert_p}{c_1^{MU}(T_p \Sigma)}\right) \in \mathrm{MU}^{i-2}(BG).$$
Part of the statement is that the restriction $y\vert_p \in \mathrm{MU}^i_{G_p}(\{p\})$ is divisible by $c_1^{MU}(T_p \Sigma)$, and the fraction denotes any choice of quotient.

\item If $y \in \mathrm{MU}^2_G(\Sigma)$ then
$$\pi_!^{MU}(y) = C \cdot \mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^G(1) + \sum_{[p] \in S/G} \mathrm{Ind}_{G_p}^G\left(\frac{y\vert_p}{c_1^{MU}(T_p \Sigma)}\right) \in \mathrm{MU}^{0}(BG)$$
where $C \in \mathbb{Z}$ is determined by requiring that the two sides be equal after restricting along $\{*\} \to BG$. (This will depend on the choices of quotients $\frac{y\vert_p}{c_1^{MU}(T_p \Sigma)}$.)
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let us first deal with the case $S=\emptyset$, i.e.\ $G$ acts freely on $\Sigma$. Then $\mathrm{MU}^i_G(\Sigma) = \mathrm{MU}^i(\Sigma/G)$. If $i>2$ then this group vanishes, so $y=0$ and hence (i) holds trivially. If $i=2$ then Poincar{\'e} duality $[\Sigma/G]_{MU} \frown - : \mathrm{MU}^2(\Sigma/G) \overset{\sim}\to \mathrm{MU}_0(\Sigma/G)=\mathbb{Z}$ identifies $y$ with the integer $d := [\Sigma/G]_{MU} \frown y$. If $\iota : \{*\} \to \Sigma/G$ is a point, then $\iota^{MU}_!(1) \in \mathrm{MU}^2(\Sigma/G)$ is a generator and $y = d \cdot \iota^{MU}_!(1)$. Then $q : \{*\} \overset{\iota}\to \Sigma/G \overset{\pi}\to BG$ are complex-oriented maps, and $\pi^{MU}_!\iota^{MU}_!(1) = q^{MU}_!(1) = \mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^G(1)$, so $\pi^{MU}_!(y) = d \cdot \mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^G(1)$ as required.

Suppose now that $S$ is nonempty. We may choose a $G$-invariant Riemannian metric on $\Sigma$, and hence cover it $G$-equivariantly by $\Sigma - S$ and a small open disc bundle of $T\Sigma\vert_S$. Using that this disc bundle is ($G$-equivariantly) homotopy equivalent to $S$, and that the intersection is ($G$-equivariantly) homotopy equivalent to the unit sphere bundle $S(T\Sigma\vert_S)$, we develop the following commutative  diagram.
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd} 
\mathrm{MU}^{i-1}_G(S(T\Sigma\vert_S)) \arrow[rd, "\partial"] \arrow[d, "\delta"] \\
\bigoplus_{[p] \in S/G} \mathrm{MU}^{i-2}_{G_p}(\{p\}) \rar{i^{MU}_!} \arrow[rdd, swap, "\bigoplus c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma) \cdot -"]& \mathrm{MU}^i_G(\Sigma) \rar \dar & \mathrm{MU}^i_G(\Sigma - S) \dar\\
  & \mathrm{MU}^i_G(S) \rar \arrow[d, equals] & \mathrm{MU}^i_G(S(T\Sigma\vert_S)) \arrow[d, equals]\\
 & \bigoplus_{[p] \in S/G} \mathrm{MU}^i_{G_p}(\{p\}) & \bigoplus_{[p] \in S/G} \mathrm{MU}^i_{G_p}(S(T_p\Sigma))
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
The row is part of the long exact sequence for the pair $(\Sigma, \Sigma - S)$ together with excision and the Thom isomorphism. The square and the map $\partial$ can be viewed as part of the Mayer--Vietoris sequence for the open cover of $\Sigma$ by $\Sigma - S$ and the small open disc bundle of $T\Sigma\vert_S$.

As $G$ acts freely on $\Sigma-S$ we have $\mathrm{MU}^i_G(\Sigma-S) = \mathrm{MU}^i((\Sigma-S)/G)$ and $(\Sigma-S)/G$ has dimension 1 as $S \neq \emptyset$. In particular this vanishes if $i \geq 2$. Similarly, as $G_p$ acts freely on $S(T_p \Sigma)$ we have $\mathrm{MU}^i_{G_p}(S(T_p\Sigma)) =  \mathrm{MU}^i(S(T_p\Sigma)/G_p)$ and $S(T_p\Sigma)/G_p = S^1$ so this vanishes if $i \geq 2$.

Suppose then that we are given $y \in \mathrm{MU}^i_G(\Sigma)$ with $i \geq 2$. It restricts to 0 in $\mathrm{MU}^i_G(\Sigma - S)$ so lifts along $i_!^{MU}$, and hence its restriction $\bigoplus_{[p]} y\vert_p$ to $\mathrm{MU}^i_G(S) = \bigoplus_{[p] \in S/G} \mathrm{MU}^i_{G_p}(\{p\})$ admits a lift $\bigoplus_{[p]} y\vert_p / c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma)$ to $\bigoplus_{[p] \in S/G} \mathrm{MU}^{i-2}_{G_p}(\{p\})$. Thus the class
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ModifiedY}
y - \sum_{[p]} i^{MU}_! \left( \frac{y\vert_p }{ c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma)}\right) \in \mathrm{MU}^i_G(\Sigma)
\end{equation}
restricts to 0 in both $\mathrm{MU}^i_G(\Sigma - S)$ and $\mathrm{MU}^i_G(S)$, so by exactness of the Mayer--Vietoris sequence it is in the image of the map $\partial$. If $i > 2$ then $\mathrm{MU}^{i-1}_G(S(T\Sigma\vert_S))=0$ so \eqref{eq:ModifiedY} is zero and applying $\pi^{MU}_!$ to it gives (i).

If $i=2$ then $\mathrm{MU}^{1}_G(S(T\Sigma\vert_S)) = \bigoplus_{[p]} \mathrm{MU}^1(S(T_p\Sigma)/G_p)$ is non-zero. The diagram shows that there are classes $\epsilon_p \in \mathrm{MU}^0_{G_p}(\{p\})$ so that \eqref{eq:ModifiedY} is $\sum_{[p]} i^{MU}_!(\epsilon_p)$ and so
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:ModifiedYFormula}
y = \sum_{[p]} i^{MU}_!\left( \frac{y\vert_p }{ c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma)} + \epsilon_p\right) \in \mathrm{MU}^2_G(\Sigma).
\end{equation}
As the $i^{MU}_!(\epsilon_p)$ come from $\partial$ they vanish in $\mathrm{MU}^i_G(S)$, and so $c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma) \cdot \epsilon_p  = 0$. Thus the element $\frac{y\vert_p }{ c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma)} + \epsilon_p$ is just another choice of a quotient of $y\vert_p$ by $c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma)$ in the ring 
$$\mathrm{MU}^*_{G_p}(\{p\}) = \mathrm{MU}^*[[c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma)]]/([|G_p|](c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma))).$$

By Lemma \ref{lem:AnnOfx} the non-uniqueness involved in dividing by $c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma)$ in the ring $\mathrm{MU}^*_{G_p}(\{p\})$ consists only of $\mathrm{MU}^*$-multiples of $\emptyset_{|G_p|}(c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma)) = \mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^{G_p}(1)$.
Thus $\epsilon_p  = E_p \cdot \mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^{G_p}(1)$ for some $E_p \in \mathbb{Z} = \mathrm{MU}^0$. Applying $\pi_!^{MU}$ to \eqref{eq:ModifiedYFormula} gives
\begin{align*}
\pi_!^{MU}(y) &= \sum_{[p]} \pi^{MU}_! i^{MU}_! \left( \frac{y\vert_p }{ c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma)} + E_p \cdot \mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^{G_p}(1)\right) \\
&= \sum_{[p]} \mathrm{Ind}_{G_p}^G \left(\frac{y\vert_p }{ c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma)}\right) + E_p \cdot \mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^{G}(1)\\
&= \left(\sum_{[p]} E_p\right) \cdot \mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^{G}(1) + \sum_{[p]} \mathrm{Ind}_{G_p}^G \left(\frac{y\vert_p }{ c_1^{MU}(T_p\Sigma)}\right) \in \mathrm{MU}^0(BG).
\end{align*}
This gives a formula of the required kind, with $C := \sum_{[p]} E_p$. This integer can be determined by restricting to a point.

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
If $n=p_1^{e_1} \cdots p_r^{e_r}$ is the factorisation into distinct prime powers, so that $C_n = C_{p_1^{e_1}} \times \cdots \times C_{p_r^{e_r}}$, then the map
$$BC_{p_1^{e_1}} \vee \cdots \vee BC_{p_r^{e_r}} \longrightarrow BC_n,$$
induced by the canonical inclusion of groups on each factor, is a stable homotopy equivalence. It follows that the classes $\kappa^E_i(\rho) \in \mathrm{E}^*(BC_n)$ are completely determined by their restrictions to the Sylow subgroups $C_{p_i^{e_i}}$ of $C_n$. Thus to prove the equivalence of (i)---(iii) we may as well suppose that $C_n = C_{p^k}$ is a cyclic $p$-group, and also show these properties are equivalent to (iv). 

Clearly (iv) implies (i). Using Landweber--Novikov operations in the form of $s_{i+1}(\kappa^{MU}_{-1}) = (-1)^{i+1} \kappa_{i}^{MU}$ from Lemma \ref{lem:LNOps}, it follows that (i) implies (ii). Clearly (ii) implies (iii).  We shall show that (iii) implies (iv) using Nielsen's classification described above.

Recall that our convention for the complex orientation of $\mathrm{K}$ is such that $x_n := c_1^K(L_n) = 1-[L_n^\vee] \in \mathrm{K}^0(BC_n)$, where $L_n$ is the tautological representation of $C_n = \mu_n$ on $\mathbb{C}$ in which the generator acts as $e^{2\pi i/n}$. If $k$ divides $n$ then $\mu_{n/k}$ is a subgroup of $\mu_n$, and the restriction of $x_n$ is $x_{n/k}$.


In Nielsen's classification $\alpha_i$ divides $n=p^k$ so is a $p$-th power, and is the order of a nontrivial stabiliser so is divisible by $p$. Furthermore $\beta_i$ is coprime to $\alpha_i$, so we can write $A_i \alpha_i + B_i \beta_i = 1$ for integers $A_i$ and $B_i$ and furthermore $B_i$ and $\beta_i$ are not divisible by $p$. Given $\alpha_i$, the data $[B_i] = [\beta_i]^{-1} \in \mathbb{Z}/\alpha_i$ is equivalent to the data $[\beta_i] \in \mathbb{Z}/\alpha_i$, and hence to the data $\beta_i/\alpha_i \in \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$. 


By (c) there is an isomorphism $(T_{x_i}\Sigma)^{\otimes \beta_i} \cong L_{\alpha_i}$ of representations of $G_{x_i} = \mu_{\alpha_i}$, and so an isomorphism 
$$T_{x_i}\Sigma \cong (T_{x_i}\Sigma)^{\otimes(A_i \alpha_i + B_i \beta_i)} \cong ((T_{x_i}\Sigma)^{\otimes \beta_i})^{\otimes B_i} \cong L_{\alpha_i}^{\otimes B_i}$$
of representations of $G_{x_i} = \mu_{\alpha_i}$. It follows that
$$c_1^{K}(T_{x_i} \Sigma) = [B_i](x_{\alpha_i}) \in \mathrm{K}^0(BG_{x_i})$$
where $x_{\alpha_i} = c_1^{K}(L_{\alpha_i})$ as above.  Thus for all $j \geq 1$, using Lemma \ref{lem:QuillenTrfLem} we have
$$\kappa_j^{K}(\rho) = \sum_{i=1}^q \mathrm{Ind}_{G_{x_i}}^G([B_i](x_{\alpha_i})^j) = \sum_{i=1}^q [B_i](x_{n})^j \cdot \frac{[n](x_n)}{[\alpha_i](x_n)} \in \mathrm{K}^0(BC_n).$$

Now $\mathrm{K}^0(BC_n)$ is the completion of the complex representation ring $R(C_n) = \mathbb{Z}[L_n]/(L_n^{n}-1)$ at its augmentation ideal $I=(L_n-1) = (1-L_n^\vee)$, and as $n=p^k$ it follows from \cite[III Proposition 1.1]{AtiyahTall} that the $I$-adic topology on $I$ coincides with the $p$-adic topology. Thus
$$\mathrm{K}^0(BC_n) = \mathbb{Z} \times_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathbb{Z}_p[x_n]/(1-(1-x_n)^{p^k}),$$
so that there is a homomorphism $\Phi_k$ to $\mathbb{Z}_p[\xi_{p^k}]$, the extension of the $p$-adics obtained by adjoining a primitive $p^k$-th root of unity, given by by $1-x_n \mapsto \xi_{p^k}$. This homomorphism sends $[B](x_n)$ to $1-\xi_{p^k}^B$, so applying it to $\kappa^K_j(\rho)=\kappa^K_j(\rho')$ gives identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PS}
\sum_{\substack{i \text{ such that } \\ p^k \mid \alpha_i}} (1-\xi_{p^k}^{B_i})^j = \sum_{\substack{i \text{ such that } \\ p^k \mid \alpha'_i}} (1-\xi_{p^k}^{B'_i})^j \in \mathbb{Z}_p[\xi_{p^k}] \quad \text{ for all $j \geq 1$}.
\end{equation}
Here we have used that $\Phi_k$ annihilates $\frac{[n](x_n)}{[\alpha_i](x_n)}$ if $p^k  \nmid \alpha_i$. Of course $p^k \mid \alpha_i$ just means $p^k = \alpha_i$, but we write the argument this way so that below it will easily generalise.

If $p_j(z_1, \dots, z_N) = \sum_{i=1}^N z_i^j$ denotes the power sum, there is an identity
$$\exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^\infty \tfrac{1}{j} p_j(z_1, \ldots, z_N) T^j\right) = \prod_{i=1}^N (1-z_i T) \in \mathbb{Q}[z_1, \ldots, z_N][[T]].$$
By functoriality, the same holds in $R[[T]]$ for any $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra $R$ and sequence of elements $z_1, \ldots, z_N \in R$. Note that extending a sequence by taking $z_{N+1}=0$ gives exactly the same identity.

This applies to the above considered as lying in the $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra given by the field of fractions $\mathbb{Q}_p(\xi_{p^k})$, where the identity \eqref{eq:PS} of power sums shows that
$$\prod_{\substack{i \text{ such that } \\ p^k = \alpha_i}} (1-(1-\xi_{p^k}^{B_{i}}) T) = \prod_{\substack{i \text{ such that } \\ p^k = \alpha'_i}} (1-(1-\xi_{p^k}^{B'_{i}}) T) \in \mathbb{Q}_p(\xi_{p^k})[[T]].$$
Both sides define elements of the subring $\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{p^k})[T]$, so they are equal there. As $p$ does not divide $B_i$ or $B'_i$, both $1-\xi_{p^k}^{B_{i}}$ and $1-\xi_{p^k}^{B'_{i}}$ are nonzero. By unique factorisation of polynomials over a field it follows that the degrees of these polynomials are equal, so
$$\#\{i \text{ such that } p^k = \alpha_i\} = \#\{i \text{ such that } p^k = \alpha'_i\},$$
and we may order the fixed points with stabilisers having order $p^k$ so that
$$1-\xi_{p^k}^{B_{i}} = 1-\xi_{p^k}^{B'_{i}} \in \mathbb{Z}[\xi_{p^k}] \subset \mathbb{Q}_p(\xi_{p^k}).$$
This is only possible if $B_{i} \equiv B'_{i} \mod p^k$, so the fixed point data of the two actions at points of maximal isotropy are the same.

We generalise the above as follows. For each $m = 1,2,\ldots, k$ there is a homomorphism $\Phi_m : \mathrm{K}^0(BC_n) \to \mathbb{Z}_p[\xi_{p^m}]$ given by $1-x_n \mapsto \xi_{p^m}$. This satisfies
$$\Phi_m\left(\frac{[n](x_n)}{[\alpha_i](x_n)}\right) = \Phi_m(\emptyset_{n/\alpha_i}([\alpha_i](x_n))) = \begin{cases}
0 & p^m \nmid \alpha_i\\
\frac{n}{\alpha_i} & p^m \mid \alpha_i
\end{cases}$$
and so applying it to $\kappa^K_j(\rho)=\kappa^K_j(\rho')$ gives 
\begin{equation*}
\sum_{\substack{i \text{ such that } \\ p^m \mid \alpha_i}} \tfrac{n}{\alpha_i} (1-\xi_{p^m}^{B_i})^j = \sum_{\substack{i \text{ such that } \\ p^m \mid \alpha'_i}} \tfrac{n}{\alpha'_i} (1-\xi_{p^m}^{B'_i})^j \in \mathbb{Z}_p[\xi_{p^m}] \quad \text{ for all $j \geq 1$}.
\end{equation*}
By downwards induction on $m$, with the base case $m=k$ proved above, the terms with $p^{m+1} \mid \alpha_i$ and $p^{m+1} \mid \alpha'_i$ already match up, leaving
\begin{equation*}
\sum_{\substack{i \text{ such that } \\ p^m = \alpha_i}} (1-\xi_{p^m}^{B_i})^j = \sum_{\substack{i \text{ such that } \\ p^m = \alpha'_i}} (1-\xi_{p^m}^{B'_i})^j \in \mathbb{Z}_p[\xi_{p^m}] \quad \text{ for all $j \geq 1$}.
\end{equation*}
Proceeding with the power-sum method as above, we find that the two actions have the same number of points with isotropy of order $p^m$, and that these may be ordered so that $B_{i} \equiv B'_{i} \mod p^m$.

$$\kappa^{MU}_{-1}(\rho) = A[\mathbb{P}^1] \cdot \emptyset_n(x_n) +  \sum_{i=1}^q \emptyset_{n/\alpha_i}([\alpha_i](x_n)) \cdot \frac{\emptyset_{\beta_i}([B_i](x_{n})) - \beta_i/\alpha_i\cdot \emptyset_{\alpha_i}(x_{n})}{x_n}$$
where the quotients are formed in $\mathrm{MU}^*[[x_n]] \otimes \mathbb{Q}$, and $A \in \mathbb{Q}$ is determined by

We apply Theorem \ref{thm:LocalisationTheorem} with $y = \pi^*(x_n) \in \mathrm{MU}^2_{C_n}(\Sigma_g)$, giving
$$x_n \cdot \kappa^{MU}_{-1}(\rho) = x_n \cdot \pi^{MU}_!(1) = \pi^{MU}_!(y) = C \cdot \mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^{C_n}(1) + \sum_{i=1}^q \mathrm{Ind}_{C_{\alpha_i}}^{C_n}\left(\frac{x_{\alpha_i}}{[B_i](x_{\alpha_i})}\right).$$
We have $x_{\alpha_i} = [\beta_i]([B_i](x_{\alpha_i}))$ mod $[\alpha_i](x_{\alpha_i})$, so we can choose to form the quotient
$$\frac{x_{\alpha_i}}{[B_i](x_{\alpha_i})} = \emptyset_{\beta_i}([B_i](x_{\alpha_i}))$$
Restricting to a point this gives $0 = C \cdot |C_n| + \sum_{i=1}^q |C_n/C_{\alpha_i}| \beta_i$, so $C = - \sum_{i=1}^q \beta_i/\alpha_i$ (which will indeed be an integer). With some rearranging, in $\mathrm{MU}^*(BC_n) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ this gives
$$x_n \cdot \kappa^{MU}_{-1}(\rho) = \sum_{i=1}^q \emptyset_{n/\alpha_i}([\alpha_i](x_n)) \cdot \left( \emptyset_{\beta_i}([B_i](x_{n})) - \beta_i/\alpha_i\cdot \emptyset_{\alpha_i}(x_{n}) \right).$$
By Lemma \ref{lem:AnnOfx}, in $\mathrm{MU}^*(BC_n) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ we may divide by $x_n$ uniquely up to adding on $\mathrm{MU}^*$-multiples of $\emptyset_n(x_n)$, so
$$\kappa^{MU}_{-1}(\rho) = A[\mathbb{P}^1] \cdot \emptyset_n(x_n) +  \sum_{i=1}^q \emptyset_{n/\alpha_i}([\alpha_i](x_n)) \cdot \frac{\emptyset_{\beta_i}([B_i](x_{n})) - \beta_i/\alpha_i\cdot \emptyset_{\alpha_i}(x_{n})}{x_n}$$
for some $A[\mathbb{P}^1] \in \mathrm{MU}^{-2} \otimes \mathbb{Q}$, which depends on how we choose to form the quotients on the right-hand terms in the ring $\mathrm{MU}^*(BC_n)$. and $[\Sigma_g] = (1-g)[\mathbb{P}^1]$ so $A$ is determined by
$$1-g = nA - \sum_{i=1}^q n/\alpha_i \cdot (\binom{\beta_i}{2} B_i - \tfrac{\beta_i}{\alpha_i} \binom{\alpha_i}{2}).$$

Let $G$ be finite and $\rho$ be a faithful action on a surface $\Sigma$, and keep the notation of Section \ref{sec:FinGpAct}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Burnside}
\pushQED{\qed} 
\kappa_0^{S}(\rho) = \frac{\chi(\Sigma) - \vert S \vert}{\vert G \vert} \cdot [G/e] + \sum_{[p] \in S/G} [G/G_p] \in \pi^{0}_S(BG) = A(G)^{\wedge}_I. \qedhere
\popQED
\end{equation}

<!-- cor-5: body_md -->
\mbox{}
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item The class $\kappa_0^{S}(\rho) \in \pi_S^0(BG)$ is determined by the numbers $\chi(\Sigma^H)$ for every subgroup $H \leq G$. 

\item The class $\kappa_0^{MU}(\rho)\otimes \mathbb{Q} \in \mathrm{MU}^0(BG) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ is determined by the numbers $\chi(\Sigma^H)$ for every abelian subgroup $H \leq G$ of prime power order.

\item The class $\kappa_0^{K}(\rho)\otimes \mathbb{Q} \in \mathrm{K}^0(BG) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ is determined by the numbers $\chi(\Sigma^H)$ for every cyclic subgroup $H \leq G$ of prime power order.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
The formula
$$\mathcal{K} := \frac{\chi(\Sigma) - \vert S \vert}{\vert G \vert} \cdot [G/e] + \sum_{[p] \in S/G} [G/G_p]$$
gives an element of the Burnside ring $A(G)$. For a subgroup $H \leq G$ we have $\vert \mathcal{K}^H \vert = \chi(\Sigma^H)$, and it is classical that an element  $\mathcal{K} \in A(G)$ is determined by the function $H \mapsto \vert \mathcal{K}^H \vert$. As $\mathcal{K}$ determines $\kappa_0^S(\rho)$, the first statement follows.

For the second statement, it follows from \eqref{eq:hkr} that $\kappa_0^{MU}(\rho)\otimes \mathbb{Q}$ is determined by $\kappa_0^{MU}(\rho\vert_A)\otimes \mathbb{Q}$ for all abelian subgroups $A \leq G$ of prime power order; to these we then apply the first case of this statement.

For the third statement, consider the image of $\mathcal{K}$ under the linearisation map $A(G) \to R(G)$ to the complex representation ring. By character-theory, elements here are detected by restriction to cyclic subgroups. On the other hand, as in the beginning of the proof of Corollary \ref{cor:DistinguishCylic} elements of $\mathrm{K}^0(BC_n)$ are determined by their restrictions to the prime-power cyclic subgroups.

Let $p(z) \in \mathbb{Z}[z]$ be the monic polynomial which has a root at $\chi(\Sigma_g^F)$ for every finite $p$-subgroup $F \leq \Gamma_g$. Then some power of $p(\kappa_0^{S}) \in \pi_S^0(\mathcal{M}_g)$ vanishes.

By Theorem \ref{thm:FinSubgpDetNilp} it suffices to show that $p(\kappa_0^S(F)) \in \pi^0_S(BF)$ vanishes for every finite subgroup $F \leq \Gamma_g$, and by Kerckhoff's theorem these groups are realised by actual actions on surfaces. By the Segal conjecture $\pi^0_S(BF) = A(F)^\wedge_I$, the completion of the Burnside ring of $F$ at the augmentation ideal. The formula \eqref{eq:Burnside} describes a lift $\mathcal{K}_F \in A(F)$ of $\kappa_0^S(F)$ to the uncompleted Burnside ring. Now, Laitinen \cite[Proposition 1.10]{Laitinen} has shown that the kernel of the completion map $A(F) \to A(F)^\wedge_I \cong \pi^0_S(BF)$ coincides with the intersection of the kernels of the restriction maps $A(F) \to A(F_p)$ to all Sylow subgroups $F_p \leq F$. $$|p(\mathcal{K}_P)^{P}| = p(|\mathcal{K}_P^P|) = p(\chi(\Sigma_g^P))$$
which vanishes as $p$ has a root at $\chi(\Sigma_g^P)$ by design. Thus $p(\mathcal{K}_F) \in A(F)$ vanishes after restriction to any $p$-subgroup, so is in the kernel of the completion map and hence $p(\kappa_0^S(F)) =0 \in \pi^0_S(BF)$ for all finite subgroup $F \leq \Gamma_g$.

<!-- lem-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:powers}
Consider $\frac{[kl](x)}{[l](x)} = \emptyset_k([l](x)) \in \mathrm{MU}^0(BC_{kl})$. Then
$$\emptyset_k([l](x))^2 = k \cdot \emptyset_k([l](x)),$$
and so $(\emptyset_k([l](x)) - k)^2 =  - k \cdot (\emptyset_k([l](x)) - k)$.
<!-- end lem-7 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Note that $\emptyset_k([l](x)) = k + ([l](x))$, and $[l](x) \cdot \emptyset_k([l](x))=0$.
<!-- end proof-15 -->
<!-- lem-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:units}
In $\mathrm{MU}^0(BC_n)$ we have $\emptyset_n(x) = \emptyset_n([k](x))$ for all $k$ coprime to $n$.
<!-- end lem-8 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
If $k$ is coprime to $n$, we may write $Ak + Bn = 1$ for integers $A$ and $B$. Hence
$$x = [A]([k](x)) +_F [B]([n](x)) = [A]([k](x))$$
so $x \in ([k](x))$ (and of course $[k](x) \in (x)$ so $(x)=([k](x))$, though we will not need this). Thus $x \cdot \emptyset_n([k](x))=0$, as $\emptyset_n([k](x))$ is annihilated by multiplication by $[k](x)$, and so $\emptyset_n([k](x))$ is a multiple of $\emptyset_n(x)$. As $x$ annihilates $\emptyset_n(x)$ this multiple is just an integer, and setting $x=0$ shows that this integer is 1.
<!-- end proof-16 -->
<!-- lem-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:products}
If $n=kl$ with $k$ and $l$ coprime, then in $\mathrm{MU}^0(BC_n)$ we have 
$$\emptyset_k([l](x)) \cdot \emptyset_{l}([k](x)) = \emptyset_{n}(x).$$
More generally, if $k$ and $l$ divide $n$ then
$$\emptyset_k([\tfrac{n}{k}](x)) \cdot \emptyset_l([\tfrac{n}{l}](x)) = \gcd(k, l) \cdot \emptyset_{kl/\gcd(k,l)}([\tfrac{n}{kl /\gcd(k,l)}](x)).$$
<!-- end lem-9 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
In the first case as $k$ is coprime to $l$, we may write $Ak + Bl = 1$ for integers $A$ and $B$. Hence
$$x = [A]([k](x)) +_F [B]([l](x)) \in ([k](x), [l](x)).$$
As $\emptyset_k([l](x)) \cdot \emptyset_{l}([k](x))$ is annihilated by multiplication by $[k](x)$ or $[l](x)$, it follows that it is also annihilated by $x$, so is a multiple of $\emptyset_n(x)$. As in the previous lemma, this multiple is 1.

In the second case consider the quotient map $\phi : C_n \to C_{kl /\gcd(k,l)}$. If $z \in \mathrm{MU}^2(BC_{kl /\gcd(k,l)})$ denotes the canonical generator then $\phi^*(z) = [\tfrac{n\cdot \gcd(k,l)}{kl}](x)$ and so
$$[\tfrac{n}{k}](x) = \phi^*([\tfrac{l}{\gcd(k,l)}](z)) \quad\text{and}\quad [\tfrac{n}{l}](x) = \phi^*([\tfrac{k}{\gcd(k,l)}](z)).$$
Thus the identity we wish to establish is
$$\phi^*\left(\emptyset_k([\tfrac{l}{\gcd(k,l)}](z)) \cdot \emptyset_l([\tfrac{k}{\gcd(k,l)}](z))\right) = \phi^*\left(\gcd(k,l) \cdot \emptyset_{kl/\gcd(k,l)}(z)\right),$$
so we may as well show that it holds before pulling back via $\phi$.

As $\gcd(\tfrac{k}{\gcd(k,l)}, \tfrac{l}{\gcd(k,l)})=1$ we have $z \in ([\tfrac{k}{\gcd(k,l)}](z), [\tfrac{l}{\gcd(k,l)}](z))$ and so $z$ annihilates the product $\emptyset_k([\tfrac{l}{\gcd(k,l)}](z)) \cdot \emptyset_l([\tfrac{k}{\gcd(k,l)}](z))$. As above, it follows that the product is a multiple of $\emptyset_{kl/\gcd(k,l)}(z)$, and we can determine which multiple by setting $x$ to zero: the multiple is $\gcd(k,l)$.

<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
There are bundle maps
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}
E C_6 \times_{C_6} T^2 \rar \dar& E\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}) \times_{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})} T^2 \rar \dar& E\mathrm{Sp}(2g, \mathbb{Z}) \times_{\mathrm{Sp}(2g, \mathbb{Z})} T^{2g} \dar\\
BC_6 \rar & B\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z}) \rar & B\mathrm{Sp}(2g, \mathbb{Z}).
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
It is enough to show that $\Omega^{MU}$ pulled back to $E C_6 \times_{C_6} T^2$ has the property that no power is torsion. This pull back still satisfies (i) and (ii).

Let us write $\pi : E \to B$ for the bundle $E C_6 \times_{C_6} T^2 \to BC_6$, $s : B \to E$ for the canonical section, and $L \to B$ for the canonical complex line bundle (where $C_6$ acts as the sixth roots of unity). As we explained in the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:elliptic}, there is an isomorphism $s^*(T_\pi E) \cong L$, and so we may find an embedding $e : L \to E$ as a tubular neighbourhood of the zero section. There is then a corresponding collapse map
$$c : E \longrightarrow L^+$$
to the fibrewise 1-point compactification of $L$ over $B$. There is furthermore a cofibre sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CofSeq}
B \overset{s_\infty}\longrightarrow L^+ \overset{q}\longrightarrow \mathrm{Th}(L \to B)
\end{equation}
where $s_\infty$ is the section at infinity. We define a class
$$\nu := c^*(q^*(u)) \in \mathrm{H}^2(E;\mathbb{Z})$$
where $u \in \mathrm{H}^2(\mathrm{Th}(L);\mathbb{Z})$ is the Thom class. Restricted to a fibre $T$ of $E$ the class $\nu$ is a generator (as $c$ had degree 1 on each fibre). 

Recall that $C_6 \subset \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$ is generated by the element $T=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}
0&-1\\ 1&1
\end{smallmatrix} \bigr)$, and the matrix $T - I = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}
-1&-1\\ 1&0
\end{smallmatrix} \bigr)$ is invertible. It then follows from the standard periodic resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$ over $\mathbb{Z}[C_6]$ that
$$\mathrm{H}^*(C_6 ; \mathrm{H}^1(T;\mathbb{Z}))=0,$$
and hence the Serre spectral sequence for $\pi : E \to B$ collapses. In addition we see that $c : E \to L^+$ induces an isomorphism on $E^2$-pages of the associated Serre spectral sequences, so $c$ induces an isomorphism on cohomology. Thus it is a stable homotopy equivalence, so also induces an isomorphism in $\mathrm{MU}$-theory. The cofibre sequence \eqref{eq:CofSeq} is split by the projection $L^+ \to B$, so we have stable homotopy equivalences
$$\Sigma^\infty_+ E \simeq \Sigma^\infty_+ L^+ \simeq \Sigma^\infty_+ B \vee \Sigma^\infty \mathrm{Th}(L)$$
and so $\mathrm{MU}^i(E) \cong \mathrm{MU}^i(BC_6) \oplus \mathrm{MU}^{i-2}(BC_6)$, which is torsion-free (Theorem \ref{thm:MUCnFlat}). Thus it will be enough to show that the pullback of $\Omega^{MU}$ to $\mathrm{MU}^*(E)$ is not nilpotent, and for this it is enough to show that the pullback of $\Omega$ to $\mathrm{H}^*(E;\mathbb{Z})$ is not nilpotent.

By the collapse of the Serre spectral sequence for $\pi$, we have that $\mathrm{H}^2(E;\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}\{\nu\} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\{\pi^*(x)\}$ where $x = c_1(L) \in \mathrm{H}^2(C_6;\mathbb{Z})$ and $\nu$ we constructed above. The identity $s^*(\nu) = x$ follows from the definition of $\nu$, and hence $\omega := 2(\nu - \pi^*(x))$ is the unique class satisfying
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $s^*(\omega)=0$,

\item $\omega \vert_{T^2}$ is twice a generator of $\mathrm{H}^2(T^2;\mathbb{Z})$,
\end{enumerate}
so is the pullback of $\Omega$ to $E$. The identity $\nu^2 = \pi^*(x) \cdot \nu$ holds (by definition of the Euler class in terms of the Thom class), and so
$$\omega^d = -(-2)^d\nu\cdot\pi^*(x)^{d-1} +(-2\pi^*(x))^{d} \in \mathrm{H}^{2d}(E;\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/6\{\pi^*(x)^d, \nu \cdot \pi^*(x)^{d-1}\}$$
which is not zero, so $\omega$ is not nilpotent.
