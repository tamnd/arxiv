\label{thm:cth-stability}
Let $V_e$ and $\widetilde V_e$ be the updates of Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:radius})} for a family of $E$ matrices, computed from $(O_e,G_e)$ and $(\widetilde O_e,\widetilde G_e)$ with the same shape factor $s$ and schedule multiplier $\lambda\in[0,1]$. Let
\[
 \delta_O=\max_e\|O_e-\widetilde O_e\|_F,\qquad
 \delta_a=\max_e|a_e-\widetilde a_e|,\qquad
 \delta_b=\max_{e,r}|b_{er}-\widetilde b_{er}|,\qquad
 R=\max_e\|\widetilde O_e\|_F,
\]
$A=\min\{\mu,\widetilde\mu\}$ and $B=\min_e\min\{\nu_e,\widetilde\nu_e\}$. Then $A,B>0$ and
\begin{equation}
 \frac1E\sum_e\|V_e-\widetilde V_e\|_F
 \leq s\left(3\delta_O+\frac{2R\delta_a}{A}
                 +\frac{2\lambda R\delta_b}{B}\right).
 \label{eq:cth-stability}
\end{equation}

<!-- head-1: body_md -->
The models have six layers, eight experts per layer, and top-2 routing, and they train with context length 256, 16 sequences per step, and 2,000 steps (8.192M training tokens). Widths 1,024, 1,536, 2,048, and 2,560 give 329.8M, 664.5M, 1,112.5M, and 1,673.8M total parameters. We train on a 60M-token FineWeb-Edu sample (95\% training and 5\% validation tokens) and on a DCLM-Baseline sample of the same size \citep{li2024dclm}. Parameters, gradients, and optimizer states are in FP32, and each run uses one NVIDIA H100 GPU with PyTorch 2.7. Heavy-ball Muon has the shape factor $0.2\sqrt{\max(m,n)}$ and the AdamW settings of the variants. We tuned its learning rate on a grid at each width and obtained 0.00125 at width 1,024 on both corpora and 0.000625 at the larger widths (the width-1,536 grid had the two rates 0.000625 and 0.00125). All variants use this rate. Variants that we also swept over two or three learning rates on seeds 0 to 2 picked the same rate. The heavy-ball grids include seeds that the comparisons also use. This favors the baseline.
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- head-2: body_md -->
The family-factor variant multiplies the orthogonalized update of expert $e$ by $c_e$ (Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:expertgate})}) with the radius set to one, and its uncapped form uses $u_e$. Row weighting multiplies row $r$ of $O_e$ by $q_{er}/\overline q_e$ (Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:rowgate})} with $\lambda_t=1$). This changes the direction of the update, and the scalar radius of Compass keeps the direction. The combined variant (family $\times$ row) applies both. In these runs every matrix uses heavy-ball momentum, so $O_e$ comes from $M_e$, and Excitation follows its published utilization-based equations. The controls replace the score with the gradient norm, the momentum cosine $\cos(M_e, G_e)$ or the reciprocal of $a_e$, permute the capped factors among the experts of each family at every step, or drop the mean-one normalization, which multiplies each expert step by $a_e$.

<!-- compasslemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:cth-normalization}
For both families, $\overline c=1$, $c_e>0$, $0<q_{er}\leq1$ and $0<h_{er}\leq1$. Also $A,B\geq\epsilon_{\rm score}$, and with $\delta_a$ and $\delta_b$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:cth-stability},
\begin{align}
 |c_e-\widetilde c_e|&\leq\frac{\delta_a}{A}(1+\widetilde c_e),&
 \frac1E\sum_e|c_e-\widetilde c_e|&\leq\frac{2\delta_a}{A},
 \label{eq:cth-cpert}\\
 |q_{er}-\widetilde q_{er}|&\leq\frac{2\delta_b}{B},&
 \|h_e-\widetilde h_e\|_\infty&\leq\frac{2\lambda\delta_b}{B}.
 \label{eq:cth-hpert}
\end{align}
<!-- end compasslemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
\begin{equation}
 |\operatorname{cos}_\epsilon(X,Y)|\leq\frac{\|X\|\,\|Y\|}{\|X\|\,\|Y\|+\epsilon_{\rm cos}}<1,
 \qquad\text{so}\qquad
 \epsilon_{\rm score}\leq a_e,\,b_{er}<1+\epsilon_{\rm score}.
 \label{eq:cth-score-range}
\end{equation}
Means and minima of numbers in this interval also lie in it. Hence $\overline a$, $\check a_e$, $\mu$ and $\nu_e$ lie in this interval for both families, and $A,B\geq\epsilon_{\rm score}$. Summing $c_e=\check a_e/\mu$ over the family gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_ec_e=\frac1\mu\sum_e\check a_e=\frac{E\mu}{\mu}=E,
 \label{eq:cth-csum}
\end{equation}
so $\overline c=1$, and $c_e>0$ because $\check a_e>0$. Also $0<q_{er}\leq1$ because $b_{er}/\nu_e>0$, and $h_{er}=(1-\lambda)+\lambda q_{er}$ lies between $q_{er}$ and one because $\lambda\in[0,1]$.

A minimum changes by at most the change of its arguments. If $|x-x'|\leq\delta$ and $|y-y'|\leq\delta$, then $\min(x,y)\leq\min(x'+\delta,y'+\delta)=\min(x',y')+\delta$, and exchanging the two pairs gives
\begin{equation}
 |\min(x,y)-\min(x',y')|\leq\delta .
 \label{eq:cth-min}
\end{equation}
The same argument proves this inequality for the maximum. Averaging $|a_j-\widetilde a_j|\leq\delta_a$ over the family, Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-min})} with $x=a_e$ and $y=\overline a$, and averaging again give, in turn,
\begin{equation}
 |\overline a-\overline{\widetilde a}|\leq\delta_a,\qquad
 |\check a_e-\widetilde{\check a}_e|\leq\delta_a,\qquad
 |\mu-\widetilde\mu|\leq\delta_a .
 \label{eq:cth-aerr}
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
 c_e-\widetilde c_e
 =\frac{\check a_e-\widetilde{\check a}_e}{\mu}+\widetilde{\check a}_e\Bigl(\frac1\mu-\frac1{\widetilde\mu}\Bigr)
 =\frac{\check a_e-\widetilde{\check a}_e}{\mu}+\widetilde c_e\,\frac{\widetilde\mu-\mu}{\mu}
 \label{eq:cth-cdiff}
\end{equation}
and $\mu\geq A$, we get
\begin{equation}
 |c_e-\widetilde c_e|\leq\frac{\delta_a}{\mu}+\widetilde c_e\,\frac{\delta_a}{\mu}\leq\frac{\delta_a}{A}(1+\widetilde c_e).
\end{equation}
Averaging over $e$ with $\overline{\widetilde c}=1$ gives the second bound in Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-cpert})}.

For the rows of one matrix, $q_{er}=y_{er}/\nu_e$ with $y_{er}=\min(b_{er},\nu_e)$. As above,
\begin{equation}
 |\nu_e-\widetilde\nu_e|\leq\delta_b,\qquad |y_{er}-\widetilde y_{er}|\leq\delta_b .
\end{equation}
Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-cdiff})}, with $y_{er}$, $\nu_e$ and $\widetilde q_{er}$ in place of $\check a_e$, $\mu$ and $\widetilde c_e$, gives
\begin{equation}
 |q_{er}-\widetilde q_{er}|\leq\frac{\delta_b}{B}\,(1+\widetilde q_{er})\leq\frac{2\delta_b}{B},
\end{equation}
because $\nu_e\geq B$ and $\widetilde q_{er}\leq1$. Finally, $h_{er}-\widetilde h_{er}=\lambda(q_{er}-\widetilde q_{er})$.

<!-- compasslemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:cth-radial}
For $\tau\geq0$, $h,\widetilde h\in[0,1]^m$ and $X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$,
\begin{align}
 \|\Phi_{\tau,h}(X)\|_F&\leq\|X\|_F,\label{eq:cth-phi-norm}\\
 \|\Phi_{\tau,h}(X)-\Phi_{\tau,\widetilde h}(Y)\|_F
 &\leq3\|X-Y\|_F+\|Y\|_F\|h-\widetilde h\|_\infty.
 \label{eq:cth-phi-lip}
\end{align}
<!-- end compasslemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let
\begin{equation}
 u_h(X)=\|\operatorname{diag}(h)X\|_F,\qquad
 J(X)=\frac{X}{\max(\|X\|_F,\tau)}\quad(X\neq0),\qquad J(0)=0 .
\end{equation}
Then $\Phi_{\tau,h}=u_hJ$ and $\|J(X)\|_F\leq1$. For any vector $k$,
\begin{equation}
 \|\operatorname{diag}(k)X\|_F^2=\sum_rk_r^2\|X_r\|_2^2\leq\|k\|_\infty^2\|X\|_F^2 .
 \label{eq:cth-rows}
\end{equation}
With $k=h$, this gives $u_h(X)\leq\|X\|_F$ and hence Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-phi-norm})}. With $k=h-\widetilde h$ and the reverse triangle inequality, it gives $|u_h(Y)-u_{\widetilde h}(Y)|\leq\|h-\widetilde h\|_\infty\|Y\|_F$, so
\begin{equation}
 \|\Phi_{\tau,h}(Y)-\Phi_{\tau,\widetilde h}(Y)\|_F=|u_h(Y)-u_{\widetilde h}(Y)|\,\|J(Y)\|_F\leq\|h-\widetilde h\|_\infty\|Y\|_F .
\end{equation}
By the triangle inequality, it remains to show that $\|\Phi_{\tau,h}(X)-\Phi_{\tau,h}(Y)\|_F\leq3\delta$ for $\delta=\|X-Y\|_F$. This difference is symmetric in $X$ and $Y$, so we may assume $\rho=\|X\|_F\geq\rho'=\|Y\|_F$. If $Y=0$, the bound follows from Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-phi-norm})}, because $\|X\|_F=\delta$. Otherwise, $d=\max(\rho,\tau)$ and $d'=\max(\rho',\tau)$ are positive, and $d\geq d'$. Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-min})} for the maximum and the reverse triangle inequality give
\begin{equation}
 0\leq d-d'\leq\rho-\rho'\leq\delta .
\end{equation}
From
\begin{equation}
 J(X)-J(Y)=\frac{X-Y}{d}-\frac{d-d'}{d\,d'}\,Y
\end{equation}
and $\|Y\|_F\leq d'$, we get
\begin{equation}
 \|J(X)-J(Y)\|_F\leq\frac{\delta}{d}+\frac{\|Y\|_F}{d'}\cdot\frac{d-d'}{d}\leq\frac{2\delta}{d}.
\end{equation}
Finally,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{\tau,h}(X)-\Phi_{\tau,h}(Y)=\bigl(u_h(X)-u_h(Y)\bigr)J(X)+u_h(Y)\bigl(J(X)-J(Y)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $|u_h(X)-u_h(Y)|\leq u_h(X-Y)\leq\delta$ and $u_h(Y)\leq\rho'\leq d$. The norm of this difference is therefore at most
\begin{equation}
 \delta+d\cdot\frac{2\delta}{d}=3\delta .\qedhere
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $F_e=\Phi_{\tau,h_e}(O_e)$ and $\widetilde F_e=\Phi_{\tau,\widetilde h_e}(\widetilde O_e)$, so that $V_e=sc_eF_e$ and $\widetilde V_e=s\widetilde c_e\widetilde F_e$. Then
\begin{equation}
 V_e-\widetilde V_e=sc_e(F_e-\widetilde F_e)+s(c_e-\widetilde c_e)\widetilde F_e,
\end{equation}
and since $c_e>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \|V_e-\widetilde V_e\|_F\leq sc_e\|F_e-\widetilde F_e\|_F+s|c_e-\widetilde c_e|\,\|\widetilde F_e\|_F .
 \label{eq:cth-thm-split}
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:cth-normalization} gives $h_e,\widetilde h_e\in(0,1]^m$. Lemma~\ref{lem:cth-radial} with $X=O_e$ and $Y=\widetilde O_e$, together with Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-hpert})}, then gives
\begin{equation}
 \|F_e-\widetilde F_e\|_F\leq3\delta_O+\frac{2\lambda R\delta_b}{B},\qquad
 \|\widetilde F_e\|_F\leq\|\widetilde O_e\|_F\leq R .
\end{equation}
We insert these bounds in Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-thm-split})} and average over the family. With $\overline c=1$ and Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-cpert})},
\begin{equation}
 \frac1E\sum_e\|V_e-\widetilde V_e\|_F
 \leq s\Bigl(3\delta_O+\frac{2\lambda R\delta_b}{B}\Bigr)+sR\,\frac{2\delta_a}{A},
\end{equation}
which is Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-stability})}. Lemma~\ref{lem:cth-normalization} also gives $A,B>0$. The proof compares two fixed sets of inputs, so the bound also holds when the scores depend on the same gradient as the direction.

<!-- compasslemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:cth-cosine}
For vectors or vectorized matrices $x,y,x',y'$ and $\epsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \left|\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{\|x\|\|y\|+\epsilon}
       -\frac{\langle x',y'\rangle}{\|x'\|\|y'\|+\epsilon}\right|
 \leq\frac{2(\|y\|\|x-x'\|+\|x'\|\|y-y'\|)}{\|x\|\|y\|+\epsilon}.
 \label{eq:cth-cosine-lip}
\end{equation}
Taking positive parts and adding $\epsilon_{\rm score}$ to both cosines does not increase the left side.
<!-- end compasslemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $N=\langle x,y\rangle$, $P=\|x\|\|y\|$, $N'=\langle x',y'\rangle$, $P'=\|x'\|\|y'\|$ and $\Delta=\|y\|\|x-x'\|+\|x'\|\|y-y'\|$. The identities
\begin{equation}
 N-N'=\langle x-x',y\rangle+\langle x',y-y'\rangle,\qquad
 P-P'=(\|x\|-\|x'\|)\|y\|+\|x'\|(\|y\|-\|y'\|)
\end{equation}
and the Cauchy-Schwarz and reverse triangle inequalities give
\begin{equation}
 |N-N'|\leq\Delta,\qquad |P-P'|\leq\Delta .
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
 \frac{N}{P+\epsilon}-\frac{N'}{P'+\epsilon}
 =\frac{N-N'}{P+\epsilon}+\frac{N'}{P'+\epsilon}\cdot\frac{P'-P}{P+\epsilon}
\end{equation}
and $|N'|\leq P'$, the left side of Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-cosine-lip})} is at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Delta}{P+\epsilon}+\frac{\Delta}{P+\epsilon}=\frac{2\Delta}{P+\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
The positive part $[t]_+=\max(t,0)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 |[t]_+-[t']_+|\leq|t-t'|
\end{equation}
by Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-min})} for the maximum, and adding the same constant to two numbers does not change their difference.

<!-- compassproposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:cth-polynomial}
Let $p(x)=3.4445x-4.7750x^3+2.0315x^5$ be the polynomial of Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:ns})}, and let $P_5=p\circ p\circ p\circ p\circ p$. Let $D\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ have the singular value decomposition $D=L\Sigma R^\top$ \citep[Chapter~2]{horn2013matrix} with singular values $\sigma_i$, and let $\hat\sigma_i=\sigma_i/(\|D\|_F+\epsilon_{\rm NS})$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{N}_5(D)=L\operatorname{diag}\bigl(P_5(\hat\sigma_i)\bigr)R^\top,
 \label{eq:cth-spectral}
\end{equation}
where $0\leq P_5(x)\leq1.21$ for $0\leq x\leq1.21$ and $P_5(x)>0$ for $x>0$. Hence $\mathcal{N}_5(D)$ has the rank of $D$, $\|\mathcal{N}_5(D)\|_{\rm op}\leq1.21$ and $\|\mathcal{N}_5(D)\|_F\leq1.21\sqrt{\operatorname{rank}(D)}$. For $D'\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$,
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal{N}_5(D)-\mathcal{N}_5(D')\|_F
 \leq\frac{2(4.25)^5\|D-D'\|_F}{\min(\|D\|_F,\|D'\|_F)+\epsilon_{\rm NS}}.
 \label{eq:cth-nslip}
\end{equation}
<!-- end compassproposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Consider first $m\leq n$, where no transpose is taken. Let $X=L\operatorname{diag}(x_i)R^\top$ with orthogonal $L$ and $R$, where $\operatorname{diag}(x_i)$ is the $m\times n$ matrix with diagonal entries $x_i$. Then
\begin{equation}
 XX^\top=L\operatorname{diag}(x_i^2)L^\top,
\end{equation}
and one iteration of Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:ns})} maps $X$ to
\begin{equation}
 F(X)=3.4445X-4.7750(XX^\top)X+2.0315(XX^\top)^2X=L\operatorname{diag}\bigl(p(x_i)\bigr)R^\top .
 \label{eq:cth-oneiter}
\end{equation}
The first iterate is
\begin{equation}
 X_0=\frac{D}{\|D\|_F+\epsilon_{\rm NS}}=L\operatorname{diag}(\hat\sigma_i)R^\top,
\end{equation}
so five iterations give Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-spectral})}. For $m>n$, the map runs on $D^\top=R\Sigma^\top L^\top$, and transposing the result gives the same formula. Repeated and zero singular values are allowed.

We write
\begin{equation}
 p(x)=x\,k(x^2),\qquad k(y)=2.0315y^2-4.7750y+3.4445 .
\end{equation}
The discriminant of $k$ is
\begin{equation}
 4.7750^2-4(2.0315)(3.4445)=-5.189382<0,
\end{equation}
so $k>0$ and $p(x)>0$ for $x>0$. The derivative is
\begin{equation}
 p'(x)=g(x^2),\qquad g(y)=10.1575y^2-14.325y+3.4445,
\end{equation}
and the discriminant of $g$ is
\begin{equation}
 14.325^2-4(10.1575)(3.4445)=65.25559>0 .
\end{equation}
The two roots $y_-<y_+$ of $g$ are positive because their sum and product are positive. The leading coefficient of $g$ is positive, so $g<0$ exactly between the roots. On $[0,\infty)$, $p$ therefore increases up to $\xi=\sqrt{y_-}$, decreases from $\xi$ to $\sqrt{y_+}$, and increases after $\sqrt{y_+}$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 p(x)\leq\max\{p(\xi),p(1.21)\}\qquad(0\leq x\leq1.21).
 \label{eq:cth-pmax}
\end{equation}
Exact evaluation gives $p'(0.5545)>0>p'(0.5546)$, so $\xi\in(0.5545,0.5546)$. The positive terms of $p$ increase with $x$ and the negative term decreases, so
\begin{equation}
 p(\xi)\leq3.4445(0.5546)-4.7750(0.5545)^3+2.0315(0.5546)^5=1.2028\ldots<1.21 .
\end{equation}
Since also $p(1.21)=0.9778\ldots<1.21$, $p$ maps $[0,1.21]$ into itself, and so does $P_5$. Together with $p(x)>0$ for $x>0$, this proves the bounds on $P_5$. By Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-spectral})}, the singular values of $\mathcal{N}_5(D)$ are the numbers $P_5(\hat\sigma_i)$. They lie in $[0,1.21]$ because $0\leq\hat\sigma_i<1$ (each $\sigma_i\leq\|D\|_F$), and they are positive exactly when $\sigma_i>0$. This gives the rank and the two norm bounds.

For Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-nslip})}, we first bound $p'$. Since $g$ is convex,
\begin{equation}
 \max_{0\leq y\leq1.21^2}g(y)=\max\{g(0),g(1.21^2)\}=\max\{3.4445,\,p'(1.21)\}=4.2448\ldots,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \min_{y\in\mathbb{R}}g(y)=3.4445-\frac{14.325^2}{4\cdot10.1575}=-1.6061\ldots .
\end{equation}
Hence $|p'|\leq4.25$ on $[-1.21,1.21]$, and the mean value theorem \citep[Chapter~5]{rudin1976principles} gives
\begin{equation}
 |p(x)-p(x')|\leq4.25\,|x-x'|\qquad(x,x'\in[-1.21,1.21]).
 \label{eq:cth-plip}
\end{equation}
Let $K_1=Q_1\Theta Q_1^\top$ and $K_2=Q_2\Gamma Q_2^\top$ be symmetric matrices with orthogonal $Q_1,Q_2$ and eigenvalues $\theta_i,\gamma_j\in[-1.21,1.21]$ \citep[Chapter~2]{horn2013matrix}. Since $K_1^k=Q_1\Theta^kQ_1^\top$ for every $k$,
\begin{equation}
 p(K_1)=Q_1p(\Theta)Q_1^\top,\qquad p(K_2)=Q_2p(\Gamma)Q_2^\top .
\end{equation}
With $C=Q_1^\top Q_2$, the matrices
\begin{equation}
 Q_1^\top\bigl(p(K_1)-p(K_2)\bigr)Q_2=p(\Theta)C-Cp(\Gamma),\qquad
 Q_1^\top(K_1-K_2)Q_2=\Theta C-C\Gamma
\end{equation}
have the entries $(p(\theta_i)-p(\gamma_j))C_{ij}$ and $(\theta_i-\gamma_j)C_{ij}$. Orthogonal factors do not change the Frobenius norm, so Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-plip})} gives
\begin{equation}
 \|p(K_1)-p(K_2)\|_F\leq4.25\,\|K_1-K_2\|_F .
 \label{eq:cth-symlip}
\end{equation}

For $X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$, the symmetric matrix
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D(X)=\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\X^\top&0\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D(X)^3=\mathcal D(XX^\top X),\qquad \mathcal D(X)^5=\mathcal D\bigl((XX^\top)^2X\bigr),
\end{equation}
and $\mathcal D(X)^2$ is block diagonal with the blocks $XX^\top$ and $X^\top X$. Since $\mathcal D$ is linear,
\begin{equation}
 p(\mathcal D(X))=\mathcal D(F(X)),\qquad \|\mathcal D(X)-\mathcal D(Y)\|_F=\sqrt2\,\|X-Y\|_F .
\end{equation}
An eigenvalue $\theta$ of $\mathcal D(X)$ satisfies $\theta^2\leq\|X\|_{\rm op}^2$, because $\theta^2$ is an eigenvalue of $\mathcal D(X)^2$, whose eigenvalues are those of $XX^\top$ and $X^\top X$. If $\|X\|_{\rm op}\leq1.21$ and $\|Y\|_{\rm op}\leq1.21$, Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-symlip})} therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \|F(X)-F(Y)\|_F=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\|p(\mathcal D(X))-p(\mathcal D(Y))\|_F\leq4.25\,\|X-Y\|_F .
 \label{eq:cth-rectlip}
\end{equation}
The $k$th iterates $X_k$ of $D$ and $X_k'$ of $D'$ have singular values in $[0,1.21]$, by the first part of the proof. Five applications of Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-rectlip})} give
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal{N}_5(D)-\mathcal{N}_5(D')\|_F\leq4.25^5\,\|X_0-X_0'\|_F .
\end{equation}
With $\rho=\|D\|_F$, $\rho'=\|D'\|_F$ and $\epsilon=\epsilon_{\rm NS}$,
\begin{equation}
 X_0-X_0'=\frac{D-D'}{\rho+\epsilon}+\frac{(\rho'-\rho)\,D'}{(\rho+\epsilon)(\rho'+\epsilon)},
\end{equation}
and $\|D'\|_F=\rho'$ and $|\rho-\rho'|\leq\|D-D'\|_F$ give
\begin{equation}
 \|X_0-X_0'\|_F\leq\frac{\|D-D'\|_F}{\rho+\epsilon}+\frac{|\rho-\rho'|}{\rho+\epsilon}\leq\frac{2\|D-D'\|_F}{\rho+\epsilon} .
\end{equation}
Since $\rho\geq\min(\rho,\rho')$, this proves Equation~\textup{(\ref{eq:cth-nslip})}. For $m>n$, the same argument applies to the transposes.
