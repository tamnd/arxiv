<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $M$ be a compact orientable simple $3$--manifold, and let $F$ be a component of $\partial M$ with $g(F)\ge2$. Suppose that $\alpha,\beta\subset F$ are separating slopes and that both $M(\alpha)$ and $M(\beta)$ are reducible. Then $\Delta(\alpha,\beta)=0$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:number}
Under the hypotheses on $M$ and $F$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, there are at most $2g(F)-3$ isotopy classes of separating slopes $\gamma\subset F$ for which $M(\gamma)$ is reducible.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:QZ}
Let $M$ be a simple $3$--manifold, and let $F$ be a boundary component of $\partial M$ of genus at least two. If $\alpha,\beta\subset F$ are separating slopes and both $M(\alpha)$ and $M(\beta)$ are reducible, then $\Delta(\alpha,\beta)\le2$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:SW}
Let $M$ be a simple $3$--manifold, and let $F$ be a boundary component of $\partial M$ of genus at least two. Then the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item There are only finitely many isotopy classes of separating degenerating slopes on $F$.
\item Suppose that $\alpha,\beta\subset F$ are slopes, $M(\alpha)$ is reducible, and $M(\beta)$ is $\partial$-reducible. If either $\alpha$ or $\beta$ is separating, then $\Delta(\alpha,\beta)=0$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:filled-boundary-irred}
Let $M$ and $F$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:SW}, and let $\alpha,\beta\subset F$ be separating slopes such that both $M(\alpha)$ and $M(\beta)$ are reducible. If $\Delta(\alpha,\beta)>0$, then both $M(\alpha)$ and $M(\beta)$ are boundary-irreducible.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:reducibility-preserved}
Let $N$ be a reducible $3$--manifold, let $G\subset\partial N$ be a boundary component of genus at least two, and let $\delta$ be a slope on $G$. Then $N(\delta)$ is reducible.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $S\subset\operatorname{int} N$ be a reducing sphere. The attached $2$--handle is disjoint from $S$. If $S$ is nonseparating, then it remains nonseparating in $N(\delta)$ and hence remains a reducing sphere. If $S$ is separating, let $X$ and $Y$ be the closures of the components of $N-S$, with $G\subset\partial X$. The component $Y$ is unchanged and is not a $3$--ball. Since $g(G)\ge2$ and $\delta$ is essential, compressing $G$ along $\delta$ leaves a positive-genus boundary component of $X(\delta)$. Thus $X(\delta)$ is not a $3$--ball either, and $S$ remains a reducing sphere in $N(\delta)$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:Taylor}
Let $(N,\gamma)$ be a taut sutured manifold, and let $b$ be a component of $\gamma$ whose two adjacent components of $R(\gamma)$ are thrice-punctured spheres. Let $Q\subset N$ be a parameterizing surface with $|Q\cap b|\ge1$, and suppose that the intersection of $Q$ with each of the two components of $R(\gamma)$ adjacent to $b$ contains no inessential arcs or circles. Let $\kappa_b\subset N[b]$ be the cocore arc of the attached $2$--handle. Then at least one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Q$ has a compressing disk or a $b$--boundary-compressing disk;
\item $(N[b],\kappa_b)=(M'_0,\kappa'_0)\#(M'_1,\kappa'_1)$, where $M'_1$ is a lens space and $\kappa'_1$ is a core of a genus-one Heegaard splitting of $M'_1$;
\item $(N[b],\gamma-b)$ is taut;
\item $-2\chi(Q)+|Q\cap\gamma|\ge2|Q\cap b|$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rmk:Taylor-summand}
In alternative~(2), the symbol $\#$ denotes the connected sum of pairs. Thus there is a separating sphere $S\subset N[b]$, transverse to $\kappa_b$, with $|S\cap\kappa_b|=2$. It is important to note that the lens-space/core pair is a nontrivial summand. In particular, the corresponding punctured summand is not a $3$--ball containing a boundary-parallel arc of $\kappa_b$; see \cite[Lemma~8.3 and its proof]{Taylor}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:genus4}
For the chosen quadruple, $\Delta(\alpha,\beta)=2$ and $g(F)=4$. Moreover, after isotopy,
\[
F-\operatorname{int}\mathcal N_F(\alpha\cup\beta)=X_1\sqcup X_2\sqcup X_3\sqcup X_4,
\]
where each $X_i$ is a once-punctured torus.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $\alpha$ is separating, $\Delta(\alpha,\beta)$ is even. As $\Delta(\alpha,\beta)>0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:QZ} therefore gives $\Delta(\alpha,\beta)=2$. Without loss of generality, assume that $|\alpha\cap\beta|=2$, and set $P=\mathcal N_F(\alpha\cup\beta)$. Then $P$ is a four-punctured sphere and
\[
F-\operatorname{int} P=X_1\sqcup X_2\sqcup X_3\sqcup X_4,
\]
where each $X_i$ has one boundary component and genus $h_i\ge1$. Label the components so that $\alpha$ separates $X_1\cup X_2$ from $X_3\cup X_4$, while $\beta$ separates $X_1\cup X_3$ from $X_2\cup X_4$. In particular,
\[
g(F)=h_1+h_2+h_3+h_4.
\]

It remains to show that $h_i=1$ for every $i$. Suppose otherwise. Without loss of generality, assume that $h_1\ge2$. There are infinitely many separating curves $\delta\subset\operatorname{int} X_1$, pairwise nonisotopic in $F$, each of which bounds a once-punctured torus in $X_1$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:SW}(1), we may choose such a $\delta$ so that $M'=M(\delta)$ is simple. Compressing $F$ along $\delta$ yields a torus and a boundary component $F'$ of genus $g(F)-1$.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Claim 1.} $(M',F',\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal C$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Proof of Claim 1.}
The component $F'$ is obtained from $F$ by replacing $X_1$ with a surface of genus $h_1-1\ge1$ having one boundary component; $P$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $X_2,X_3,X_4$ are unchanged. Consequently $F'$ contains $\alpha$ and $\beta$ as essential separating slopes. Any new bigon would give a disk component of $F'-(\alpha\cup\beta)$, whereas all four complementary components of $P$ have positive genus. Thus $\Delta_{F'}(\alpha,\beta)=2$.

It remains to verify that $M'(\alpha)$ and $M'(\beta)$ are reducible. Since $\delta$ is disjoint from $\alpha$, the handle additions commute:
\[
M'(\alpha)\cong M(\alpha)(\delta).
\]
The $\alpha$--handle attachment caps the boundary of the component of $F-\alpha$ containing $X_1\cup X_2$, producing a boundary component of $M(\alpha)$ of genus $h_1+h_2\ge3$. The curve $\delta$ remains essential on this component. Lemma~\ref{lem:reducibility-preserved} therefore shows that $M(\alpha)(\delta)$, and hence $M'(\alpha)$, is reducible. The same argument with $\beta$ in place of $\alpha$ shows that $M'(\beta)$ is reducible. Thus $(M',F',\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal C$. \qed

Since $g(F')=g(F)-1$, Claim~1 contradicts the minimality of $g(F)$. Hence $h_i=1$ for every $i$, and therefore $g(F)=4$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:boundary-normalized}
There exists a quadruple $(M,F,\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal C$ such that $g(F)=4$ and $F$ is the only non-toral component of $\partial M$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $(M,F,\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal C$ be the minimal counterexample considered in Proposition~\ref{prop:genus4}. Since $M$ is irreducible and has a boundary component of positive genus, $\partial M$ has no sphere components. Suppose that $G\ne F$ is a boundary component with $h=g(G)\ge2$. There are infinitely many separating slopes on $G$, pairwise nonisotopic in $G$, each bounding a once-punctured torus. By Lemma~\ref{lem:SW}(1), choose one, say $\delta$, such that $M'=M(\delta)$ is simple.

Since $M'$ is simple by construction and the data $F,\alpha,\beta$ are unchanged, it remains only to verify reducibility of $M'(\alpha)$ and $M'(\beta)$. Since $\delta\subset G$ and $\alpha,\beta\subset F$, the handle additions commute:
\[
M'(\alpha)=M(\delta)(\alpha)\cong M(\alpha)(\delta),\qquad
M'(\beta)=M(\delta)(\beta)\cong M(\beta)(\delta).
\]
Because the handle additions along $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are supported on $F$, the boundary component $G$ is unchanged in both filled manifolds. Lemma~\ref{lem:reducibility-preserved} therefore shows that $M(\alpha)(\delta)$ and $M(\beta)(\delta)$ are reducible. It follows that $M'(\alpha)$ and $M'(\beta)$ are reducible, and hence $(M',F,\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal C$.

Repeating the construction finitely many times yields a quadruple in $\mathcal C$ for which every boundary component other than $F$ is a torus.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:taut}
The sutured manifold $(M,\gamma)$ is taut.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Since $M$ is simple, it is irreducible and boundary-irreducible; in particular, the torus components in $T(\gamma)$ are incompressible. All boundary curves of the components of $R(\gamma)$ are essential in $F$, so these components are incompressible in $F$. Since $F$ is incompressible in $M$, they are also incompressible in $M$. It remains only to prove that $R_+(\gamma)$ and $R_-(\gamma)$ are Thurston-norm minimizing.

Let $S\subset M$ be an oriented properly embedded surface with the same oriented boundary as $R_+(\gamma)$ and representing the same relative homology class as $R_+(\gamma)$. Its boundary consists of five pairwise nonparallel essential curves parallel to $\alpha,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4$. Write $S=\bigsqcup_jS_j$, with each $S_j$ connected.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Claim 1.} Every component $S_j$ with nonempty boundary has positive norm.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Proof of Claim 1.}
A disk component would give a compressing disk for $F$, contrary to boundary-irreducibility. An annular component is incompressible, for otherwise a compression would yield a disk in $M$ bounded by one of its boundary components, contradicting the incompressibility of $F$. The two boundary curves of such an annulus are among the five pairwise nonparallel components of $\partial S$, so it cannot be boundary-parallel. It would therefore be an essential annulus in $M$, contradicting anannularity. Hence $\chi(S_j)<0$, and $x(S_j)>0$. \qed

Suppose, for contradiction, that $x(S)\le2$. By Claim~1 and the fact that $\partial S\ne\varnothing$, $S$ has either one or two components with nonempty boundary. We distinguish these two cases.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Case 1.} Exactly one component of $S$ has nonempty boundary. It has all five boundary components of $S$. If its genus is $h$, then its norm is $2h+3\ge3$, contradicting $x(S)\le2$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Case 2.} Exactly two components of $S$ have nonempty boundary. Since $x(S)\le2$, each has norm $1$. A connected orientable surface of norm $1$ has an odd number of boundary components. Hence the total number of boundary components of these two surfaces is even, contradicting $|\partial S|=5$.

Therefore $x(S)\ge3=x(R_+(\gamma))$, so $R_+(\gamma)$ is Thurston-norm minimizing. The same argument applies to $R_-(\gamma)$. Thus $(M,\gamma)$ is taut.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $H_\beta$ be the $2$--handle attached along $\beta$. Choose a reducing sphere $\widehat Q\subset M(\beta)$ minimizing the number of meridian disks in $\widehat Q\cap H_\beta$, and put $Q=\widehat Q\cap M$. The surface $Q$ is connected and planar, with all boundary components parallel to $\beta$. Writing $q=|\partial Q|$, we have $\chi(Q)=2-q$; irreducibility of $M$ implies $q\ge1$. The proof of Lemma~2.1 in \cite{QZ} shows that the minimality of $|\widehat Q\cap H_\beta|$, together with the boundary-irreducibility of $M(\beta)$, implies that $Q$ is incompressible and boundary-incompressible.

To verify the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:Taylor}, isotope the components of $\partial Q$ to parallel copies of $\beta$ in $P$, disjoint from the annuli about $c_1,\ldots,c_4$ and meeting the annulus about $\alpha$ in two spanning arcs. Thus condition~(P1) holds. Since $q\ge1$, $Q$ is not a sphere; if $Q$ is a disk, then its boundary meets $\alpha\subset\gamma$. Hence condition~(P2) holds, and $Q$ is a parameterizing surface, with
\[
Q\cap(\gamma-\alpha)=\varnothing,\qquad
|Q\cap\gamma|=|Q\cap\alpha|=2q.
\]
By construction, the portions of $\partial Q$ lying in the two components of $R(\gamma)$ adjacent to $\alpha$ are essential arcs. These observations, together with Lemma~\ref{lem:taut}, verify all the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:Taylor}, which we apply with $N=M$ and $b=\alpha$.

The first three alternatives are ruled out in turn. Since $Q$ is incompressible, alternative~(1) would yield an $\alpha$--boundary-compressing disk $D$ with $\partial D=a\cup d$, where $a\subset Q$ and $d\subset\alpha$. The arc $a$ cannot be essential in $Q$, for otherwise $D$ would be an ordinary boundary-compressing disk for $Q$. Thus $a$ cobounds a disk $E\subset Q$ with an arc $e\subset\partial Q$. After a small perturbation, $D\cup_aE$ is a properly embedded disk in $M$ with boundary $d\cup e$. Boundary-irreducibility of $M$ implies that $d\cup e$ is inessential in $F$. Choose the disk $B\subset F$ bounded by $d\cup e$ innermost with respect to $\alpha\cup\partial Q$. Then $B$ is a bigon between $\alpha$ and the corresponding component of $\partial Q$, contradicting minimal position. Thus alternative~(1) is impossible.

If alternative~(2) holds, let $S\subset M(\alpha)$ be the corresponding summing sphere, so that $|S\cap\kappa_\alpha|=2$, and let $H$ denote the attached $2$--handle. Isotope $S$ so that $S\cap H$ consists of two meridian disks. Then $A=S\cap M$ is a properly embedded annulus whose boundary components are parallel to $\alpha$. The incompressibility of $F$ and essentiality of $\alpha$ imply that $A$ is incompressible; anannularity of $M$ therefore forces $A$ to be boundary-parallel to an annulus $A_0\subset F$. The product region between $A$ and $A_0$ is a solid torus in which $\alpha$ is a longitude. Attaching the portion of $H$ between the two disks of $S\cap H$ gives a $3$--ball bounded by $S$, in which the corresponding subarc of $\kappa_\alpha$ is boundary-parallel. This contradicts Remark~\ref{rmk:Taylor-summand}, so alternative~(2) is impossible.

Alternative~(3) is also impossible: $M(\alpha)$ is reducible, whereas the underlying manifold of a taut sutured manifold is irreducible. Only alternative~(4) remains. Substituting $\chi(Q)=2-q$ and the intersection counts above gives
\[
4q-4=-2\chi(Q)+|Q\cap\gamma|
   \ge2|Q\cap\alpha|=4q,
\]
a contradiction. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
