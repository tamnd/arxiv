<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A policy \(\pi\) is consistent if, for every \(\theta^\ast\in\Theta\),
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb M_{\theta^\ast,\pi}(A_{\mathrm{out}}\neq a_{\theta^\ast})=0.
\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Consider two such reward tables $r,r'$. We say that they are neighboring if there exists at most one pair $(i,t)\in[K]\times[T]$ such that $r_{i,t} \neq r'_{i,t}$, while all other entries are equal. We say that $\pi$ is $\epsilon$-DP if for all neighboring $r,r'$, for all instances $\theta^{*}$, and for all $S\subseteq[K]^{T+1}$, we have
\[\mathbb{M}_{\theta^{*},\pi}(\pi(r)\in S) \leq e^{\epsilon}\mathbb{M}_{\theta^{*},\pi}(\pi(r')\in S).\]
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:LowerBound}
Suppose a policy \(\pi\) is consistent and \(\epsilon\)-DP. Assume further that there exists an allocation \(\lambda^*=(\lambda^*_1,\ldots,\lambda^*_K)\in\Delta_{[K]}\) such that, for every problem instance \(\theta\in\Theta\), the arm counts generated by \(\pi\) satisfy
$ 
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_{a,T}}{T}=\lambda_a^*
$  a.s.\ for all $a\in[K]$.
Then
\[

We now provide a proof sketch of Theorem \ref{thm:UpperBound}. The proof relies on arguing that six particular good events will happen with high probability. We elaborate on these good events one by one. Their formal statements and proofs are deferred to Appendix \ref{apx:GE}.

Good Event 1, stated in Lemma \ref{lem:GE1}, is used to prove a concentration property of the private estimators $m_{a,t}$ for all arms $a\in[K]$ and across all $t\in[T]$. Together with a lower bound on the number of arm pulls $N_{a,t}$ asserted by Good Event 3 (Lemma \ref{lem:GE3}), it ensures that the private mean estimators converge to the corresponding true mean, which plays a crucial role in the error probability analysis.



Good Event 2, stated in Lemma \ref{lem:GE2}, demonstrates the boundedness property of the private empirical estimator, the alternative instance sampled each round, as well as the final recommendation. This is to ensure that the algorithm operates and terminates without producing any private estimator or final recommendation having an arm mean that is smaller than $0$ or greater than $1$.



Good Event 3, stated in  Lemma \ref{lem:GE3}, shows that the forced exploration, implemented by a mixture of uniform design each round, ensures that each arm can get $\Omega(t^{1-\alpha})$ arm pulls and gather enough exploration by spending $\mathrm{polylog}(T)$ arm pulls in total. 



Good Event 4, stated in Lemma \ref{lem:GE4}, proves that with the support of Good Event 1 (Lemma \ref{lem:GE1}) and Good Event 3 (Lemma \ref{lem:GE3}), after a burn-in period of order $\mathrm{polylog}(T)$, the private empirical best arm agrees with the true best arm. This result carries importance into the discussion of the subsequent good events, where we show that the max-learner and the min-learner in \textsc{AO-Pri-BAI} eventually solve this game.



Good Event 5, stated in Lemma \ref{lem:GE5} and with the associated Corollary \ref{col:GE5}, is to establish that after taking rounds of order $\mathrm{polylog}(T)$, the logarithm of the ratio between $q_{\mathrm{Final}}(\theta)$ and $q_{\mathrm{Final}}(\theta^{*})$ is asymptotically equal to the an empirical approximation of the hardness term $\tau^{*}(\theta^{*})$. This result assists in the proof of the upper bound because to prove the upper bound is exactly to control the probability of sampling an instance where the best arm differs from the true one, which is exactly $q_{\mathrm{Final}}(\mathrm{Alt}(\theta^{*}))$. Since $q_{\mathrm{Final}}(\mathrm{Alt}(\theta^{*}))$ is the integral of density $q_{\mathrm{Final}}(\theta)$ over $\mathrm{Alt}(\theta^{*})$, having control over the density allows us to control the integral as well as to later relate it to the optimal error exponent $\tau^{*}(\theta^{*})$.




The final Good Event 6 stated in Lemma \ref{lem:GE6} shows that the final empirical design $\bar{e}_{T}=\{\bar{e}_{a,T}\}_{a=1}^{T}$, is asymptotically achieving the hardness term $\tau^{*}(\theta^{*})$ by bounding their difference with an error term $\varepsilon_{6,T}(\delta)$, which we define by a telescoping sum and then Lemma \ref{lem:GE6control}, \ref{sublem:GE6control1}, \ref{sublem:GE6control2}, \ref{sublem:GE6control3}, \ref{sublem:GE6control4}, \ref{sublem:GE6control5}, \ref{sublem:GE6control6} argue that each of the summand, and thus $\varepsilon_{6,T}(\delta)$, is of order $o(1)$.

The final argument on the upper bound, stated in Theorem \ref{thm:UpperBound}, is then established by assembling the good events proved. With the choice of $\delta_T=T^{-3}$, since the failure probabilities of the primitive good events are summable, the
Borel--Cantelli lemma implies that, with probability one, all six good events
hold for all sufficiently large \(T\).  Hence, the rest of the argument may be
carried out pathwise on a combination of them.

By construction of the final recommendation rule,

\bigl(a_{\mathrm{out}}\neq a_{\theta^\ast}\bigr)
=
q_{\mathrm{Final}}\bigl(\mathrm{Alt}(\theta^\ast)\bigr).
\]
Thus, it remains to upper bound the probability mass of the alternative set.

Good Event~5 gives the large-deviation approximation
\[
q_{\mathrm{Final}}\bigl(\mathrm{Alt}(\theta^\ast)\bigr)
\doteq

\label{thm:Lipschitz-dber}
The map \(q\mapsto d_\epsilon^{\mathrm{Ber}}(p,q)\) is \(C_\mu\)-Lipschitz on \([3\xi_\mu,1-3\xi_\mu]\), uniformly over \(p\in[0,1]\), where
\[
C_\mu=\frac{1}{3\xi_\mu(1-3\xi_\mu)},
\qquad
\xi_\mu=\frac14\min_{a\in[K]}\{\mu_a,1-\mu_a\}.
\]

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix $p\in[0,1]$ and $q,q'\in[3\xi_{\mu}, 1-3\xi_{\mu}]$, for $u\in(0,1)$,
    \[|(\epsilon|p-u|+\mathrm{kl}(u,q))-(\epsilon|p-u|+\mathrm{kl}(u,q'))|=|\mathrm{kl}(u,q)-\mathrm{kl}(u,q')|.\]

    Notice that
    \[\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\mathrm{kl}(u,q)=\frac{q-u}{q(1-q)}.\]

    Since $q(1-q) \geq 3\xi_{\mu}(1-3\xi_{\mu})$ and $|q-u| \leq 1$, we have
    \[ \bigg|\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\mathrm{kl}(u,q)\bigg| \leq \frac{1}{3\xi_{\mu}(1-3\xi_{\mu})}=C_{\mu}.\]

    By the mean-value theorem, 
    \[|\mathrm{kl}(u,q)-\mathrm{kl}(u,q')| \leq C_{\mu}|q-q'|.\]

    If we define $f(p,q,u)=\epsilon|p-u|+\mathrm{kl}(u,q)$, then it shows that
    \[|f_(p,q,u)-f(p,q',u)| \leq C_{\mu}|q-q'|,\]
    then for every fixed $u\in(0,1)$, it implies $f$ is $C_{\mu}$-Lipschitz with respect to second argument.

    For any $u\in(0,1)$, we have
    \[f(p,q,u) \leq f(p,q',u) + C_{\mu}|q-q'|.\]

    Take infimum over $u$ on both sides, we have
    \[d_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(p,q)=\inf_{u\in(0,1)}f(p,q,u) \leq \inf_{u\in(0,1)}f(p,q',u)+C_{\mu}|q-q'|=d_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(p,q')+C_{\mu}|q-q'|.\]

    We swap $q$ and $q'$ to obtain
    \[d_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(p,q')\inf_{u\in(0,1)}f(p,q',u) \leq \inf_{u\in(0,1)}f(p,q,u)+C_{\mu}|q'-q|=d_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(p,q)+C_{\mu}|q'-q|.\]

    and combining the two inequalities leads to
    \[|d_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(p,q)-d_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(p,q')| \leq C_{\mu}|q-q'|.\]

We fix a policy $\pi$ consisting of a sampling rule $\{\lambda_t\}_{t=1}^T$ and a recommendation rule $I$. The goal of the proof is to upper bound
\[
\mathrm{KL} \bigl(\mathbb{M}_{\theta',\pi} \,\|\, \mathbb{M}_{\theta^{*},\pi}\bigr).
\]
Following the approach of Theorem~2 in \citet{jourdan2025optimalbestarmidentification}, let $\theta' \in \mathrm{Alt}(\theta^{*})$ be arbitrary, and let $\theta'' \in \Theta$ be an auxiliary (``intermediate'') bandit instance.

For each arm $a \in [K]$, define                                                                             
\[
c_a \in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\gamma\in\Gamma(P_{\theta'}^a,P_{\theta''}^a)} ,\mathbb{P}_{r,r''\sim\gamma}(r'=r''),\]
where  $\Gamma(P_{\theta'}^a,P_{\theta''}^a)$ is the set of all couplings between $P_{\theta'}^a$ and $P_{\theta''}^a$,
                                                                               
as the maximal coupling between $P_{\theta'}^a$ and $P_{\theta''}^a$, such that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The marginals are $P_{\theta'}^a$ and $P_{\theta''}^a$ respectively, i.e.,
    \[\sum_{r'}c_{a}(r', r'')=P_{\theta''}^{a}(r''),\]
    \[\sum_{r''}c_{a}(r',r'')=P_{\theta'}^{a}(r').\]
    \item Among all couplings between $P_{\theta'}^a$ and $P_{\theta''}^a$, it maximizes the probability $\mathbb{P}(r'=r'')$.       
    As a consequence, 
    \[\mathbb{P}_{r,r''\sim c_{a}}(r'\neq r'')=TV(P_{\theta'}^a || P_{\theta''}^a).\]
\end{enumerate}

We construct a coupled environment $\gamma$ associated with $(\theta', \theta'')$ over a time horizon $T$, which generates an augmented history
\[
\bigl\{(A_t, r'_t, r''_t)\bigr\}_{t=1}^T \;\text{and}\; A_{\mathrm{out}}.
\]
The interaction proceeds iteratively as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item At each time $t$, the arm $A_t$ is drawn according to the sampling rule $\lambda_t$, based only on the history
    \[
    (A_1, r'_1, A_2, r'_2, \dots, A_{t-1}, r'_{t-1}).
    \]
    In particular, the policy does not depend on $\{r''_i\}_{i=1}^{t-1}$ when selecting arms.
    
    \item Conditionally on $A_t$, the reward pair $(r'_t, r''_t)$ is sampled from the maximal coupling $c_{A_t}$, and is conditionally independent of the past history
    \[
    \{(A_i, r'_i, r''_i)\}_{i=1}^{t-1}.
    \]

    \item The recommended arm $A_{\mathrm{out}}$ is drawn according to the recommendation rule $I$, based only on the same history up to time $T$
    \[
    (A_1, r'_1, A_2, r'_2, \dots, A_{T}, r'_{T}),
    \]
    and is independent of $\{r''_i\}_{i=1}^{T-1}$.
\end{enumerate}
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        
                       
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                
                                                                                                                                                                                         
                     

    This interaction between $\pi$ and $\gamma$ mirrors the standard interaction between the policy $\pi$ and the instance $\theta'$, with the addition that, at each round $t$, an auxiliary reward $r''_t$ is sampled via the maximal coupling of $P_{\theta'}^{A_t}$ and $P_{\theta''}^{A_t}$.

The distribution of the augmented history induced by $(\gamma,\pi)$ is given by
\[
p_{\gamma,\pi}(A_{1:T}, r'_{1:T}, r''_{1:T}, A_{\mathrm{out}})
= \Biggl[ \prod_{t=1}^T 
\lambda_t\bigl(A_t \mid A_{1:t-1}, r'_{1:t-1}\bigr)
\, c_{A_t}(r'_t, r''_t) \Biggr]
I\bigl(A_{\mathrm{out}} \mid A_{1:T}, r'_{1:T}\bigr).
\]
                                                                                                                                                      

For notational convenience, define
\[
\mathbf{A} = (A_1,\dots,A_T), \quad
\mathbf{r}' = (r'_1,\dots,r'_T), \quad
\mathbf{r}'' = (r''_1,\dots,r''_T),
\]
\[
c_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r}', \mathbf{r}'')
:= \prod_{t=1}^T c_{A_t}(r'_t, r''_t),
\]
and
\[
\lambda(\mathbf{A}, A_{\mathrm{out}} \mid \mathbf{r}')
:= \Biggl[ \prod_{t=1}^T 
\lambda_t\bigl(A_t \mid A_{1:t-1}, r'_{1:t-1}\bigr) \Biggr]
I\bigl(A_{\mathrm{out}} \mid A_{1:T}, r'_{1:T}\bigr).
\]
Then
\[
p_{\gamma,\pi}(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{r}', \mathbf{r}'', A_{\mathrm{out}})
= \lambda(\mathbf{A}, A_{\mathrm{out}} \mid \mathbf{r}')
\, c_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r}', \mathbf{r}'').
\]

By construction of the coupling, the distribution $\mathbb{M}_{\theta',\pi}$ is the marginal of $p_{\gamma,\pi}$ after integrating out $(\mathbf{r}', \mathbf{r}'')$, i.e.,
\[
\mathbb{M}_{\theta',\pi}(\mathbf{A}, A_{\mathrm{out}})
= \int p_{\gamma,\pi}(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{r}', \mathbf{r}'', A_{\mathrm{out}})
\, \mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}' \, \mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}''.
\]

Next, define the auxiliary joint distribution
\[
q_{\gamma,\pi}(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{r}', \mathbf{r}'', A_{\mathrm{out}})
= \lambda(\mathbf{A}, A_{\mathrm{out}} \mid \mathbf{r}'')
\frac{p_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r}'')}{p''_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r}'')}
\, c_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r}', \mathbf{r}''),
\]
where
\[
p_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r}'')
:= \prod_{t=1}^T P_{\theta^*}^{A_t}(r''_t),
\qquad
p''_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{r}'')
:= \prod_{t=1}^T P_{\theta''}^{A_t}(r''_t).
\]

    \]

    Now define $\mu_{t}$ as the uniform probability measure on $\overline{\mathrm{Alt}(\hat{\theta}_{t})}$. Since $\overline{\mathrm{Alt}(\hat{\theta}_{t})} \subset [0,1]^{K}$, its volume is at most $1$, hence
    \[\mu_{t}\bigl(B_{t}(r)\bigr) \geq \bigl|B_{t}(r)\bigr|\geq c_{K}r^{K}, c_{K}=2^{-(K+2)}.\]

    For $\theta\in B_{t}(r)$, we have $\bigl\|\theta-\theta^{*}_{t}\bigr\|_{1} \leq Kr$, so by $\epsilon$-Lipschitz,
    \[F_{t}(\theta) \leq F_{t}(\theta^{*}_{t}) + \epsilon Kr = \tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})+\epsilon Kr.\]

    Thus the set $A_{t}(r)=\bigl\{\theta\in\overline{\mathrm{Alt}(\hat{\theta}_{t})}:F_{t}(\theta) \leq \tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})+\epsilon Kr\bigr\}$ satisfies
    \[\mu_{t}\bigl(A_{t}(r)\bigr) \geq \mu_{t}\bigl(B_{t}(r)\bigr) \geq c_{K}r^{K}.\]

    Let $Z_{t}$ be the normalization constant of $q_{t}$, therefore for $\theta_{t}$,
    \[
    q_{t}(\theta)=\frac{\exp\bigl(-\beta_{t}F_{t}(\theta)\bigr)}{Z_{t}}, \,
    Z_{t}=\int_{\overline{\mathrm{Alt}(\hat{\theta}_{t})}}
    \exp\bigl(-\beta_{t}F_{t}(\theta)\bigr)d\mu_{t}(\theta).
    \]

    Because $A_{t}(r) \subseteq \overline{\mathrm{Alt}(\hat{\theta}_{t})}$ and on $A_{t}(r)$,
    \[F_{t}(\theta) \leq \tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})+\epsilon Kr,\]
    we have
\begin{align*}
    Z_{t}&\geq\int_{A_{t}(r)}\exp\bigl(-\beta_{t}F_{t}(\theta)\bigr)d\mu_{t}(\theta)\\
    &\geq \int_{A_{t}(r)}
    \exp\Bigl(-\beta_{t}\bigl(\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})+\epsilon Kr\bigr)\Bigr)d\mu_{t}(\theta)\\
    &=\mu_{t}\bigl(A_{t}(r)\bigr)
    \exp\Bigl(-\beta_{t}\bigl(\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})+\epsilon Kr\bigr)\Bigr)\\
    &\geq c_{K}r^{K}
    \exp\Bigl(-\beta_{t}\bigl(\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})+\epsilon Kr\bigr)\Bigr).
\end{align*}

Note that
\[\log q_{t}(\theta)=-\beta_{t}F_{t}(\theta)-\log Z_{t}.\]

Take expectation over $q_{t}$:
\[
\int q_{t}(\theta)\log q_{t}(\theta) d\mu_{t}(\theta)
=-\beta_{t}\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{t}(\theta_{t})\bigm|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr]-\log Z_{t}.
\]

LHS is $KL\bigl(q_{t}\bigm\|\mu_{t}\bigr) \geq 0$, therefore
\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{t}(\theta_{t})\bigm|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr]
\leq -\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\log Z_{t}
\leq \tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})+\epsilon Kr
+\frac{K\log \frac{1}{r}-\log c_{K}}{\beta_{t}}.
\]

Thus
\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{t}(\theta_{t})\bigm|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr]-\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})
\leq\epsilon Kr+\frac{K\log \frac{1}{r}-\log c_{K}}{\beta_{t}}.
\]

By our specification that $\beta_{t} \rightarrow\infty$, we fix $\tilde{t}$ such that $\beta_{t} > 2$ for $t\geq \tilde{t}$. For $t\geq \tilde{t}$, choose $r=\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}$, then
\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{t}(\theta_{t})\bigm|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr]-\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})
\leq\frac{\epsilon K}{\beta_{t}}+\frac{K\log \beta_{t}-\log c_{K}}{\beta_{t}}.
\]

Since $c_{K}=2^{-(K+2)}$, we have
\[-\log c_{K}=(K+2)\log 2,\]

thus,
\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{t}(\theta_{t})\bigm|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr]-\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})
\leq\frac{\epsilon K+K\log \beta_{t}+(K+2)\log 2}{\beta_{t}},
\]

and hence,
\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{t}(\theta_{t})\bigm|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr]-\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})
\in O\Bigl(\frac{\log \beta_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\Bigr).
\]

Now, define the martingale difference
\[Y_{t}=F_{t}(\theta_{t})-\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{t}(\theta_{t})\bigm|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr],\]

since $F_{t}(\theta_{t}) \in [0,\epsilon]$, we have $|Y_{t}| \leq \epsilon$.

Note that
\[R_{T}^{\min}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl(F_{t}(\theta_{t})-\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})\bigr).\]

When $t<\tilde{t}$, we apply the trivial bound
\[F_{t}(\theta_{t})-\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t}) \in [0,\epsilon],\]

thus
\begin{align*}
    R_{T}^{\min}&=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl(F_{t}(\theta_{t})-\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})\bigr)\\
    &\leq\frac{\tilde{t}\epsilon}{T}
    +\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T}\bigl(F_{t}(\theta_{t})-\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})\bigr)\\
    &=\frac{\tilde{t}\epsilon}{T}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T}Y_{t}
    +\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T}
    \Bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{t}(\theta_{t})\bigm|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr]
    -\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})\Bigr).
\end{align*}

By Azuma-Hoeffding, for $b>0$,
\[
\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\Bigl|\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T}Y_{t}\Bigr| >b T\Biggr)
\leq 2\exp\Bigl(-\frac{b^{2}T^{2}}{2\epsilon^{2}(T-\tilde{t}+1)}\Bigr)
\leq 2\exp\Bigl(-\frac{b^{2}T}{2\epsilon^{2}}\Bigr), \, \forall \,T\geq\tilde{t}.
\]

Therefore,
\[
\sum_{T=\tilde{t}}^{\infty}
\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\biggl|\frac{\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T}Y_{t}}{T}\biggr| >b\Biggr)
<\infty.
\]

By Borel-Cantelli,
\[
\mathbb{P}\Biggl(
\biggl|\frac{\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T}Y_{t}}{T}\biggr|
>b \text{ for infinitely many }T
\Biggr)=0.
\]

We consider the countable sequence $b_{n}=\frac{1}{n}, n\in\mathbb{N}$, therefore we have for $n\in\mathbb{N}$,
\[
\mathbb{P}\Biggl(
\biggl|\frac{\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T}Y_{t}}{T}\biggr|
>\frac{1}{n} \text{ for infinitely many }T
\Biggr)=0.
\]

Taking complement of this event and taking intersection over $n$, we have
\[
\mathbb{P}\Biggl(
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
\biggl\{\Bigl|\frac{\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T}Y_{t}}{T}\Bigr|
<\frac{1}{n} \text{ for sufficiently large }T\biggr\}
\Biggr)=1,
\]

which implies
\[\frac{\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T}Y_{t}}{T} \rightarrow 0 \text{ a.s.}.\]

Furthermore, $\beta_{t}\rightarrow \infty$ is a sufficient condition for $\frac{\log\beta_{t}}{\beta_{t}} \rightarrow 0$. If we let
\[
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T}
\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{t}(\theta_{t})\bigm|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr]
-\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})\Bigr)
\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T} s_{t},
\]
where
\[
s_{t}=\frac{\epsilon K+K\log\beta_{t}+(K+2)\log2}{\beta_{t}}
\in O\Bigl(\frac{\log \beta_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\Bigr)\in o(1),
\]

then, by Ces\`aro Mean Theorem,
\[
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=\tilde{t}}^{T}
\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{t}(\theta_{t})\bigm|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr]
-\tilde{G}_{t}(\lambda_{t})\Bigr)
\in o(1).
\]

Combining above, we have
\[R_{T}^{\min} \in o(1) \text{ a.s.}.\]
