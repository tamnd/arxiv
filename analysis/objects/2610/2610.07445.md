<!-- question-1: body_md -->
\label{q:main-question}
  Let $f \in H^1(\Gamma_0 (\mathfrak{n}), \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ be an ethereal mod $p$ Bianchi eigenform.
  Does there exist an $\mathcal{O}_K$-ideal $\mathfrak{r}$ and a Hecke eigenvalue system $F$ in $ H^1(\Gamma_0 (\mathfrak{nr}), \mathbb{C})$ such that $a_\lambda (F) \equiv a_\lambda (f) \ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)$ for all primes $(\lambda, \mathfrak{nr} \cdot p )= 1$?\footnote{Here we are a bit imprecise about what we mean by reduction mod $p$. For a precise statement, see Definition \ref{def:liftable}.}
<!-- end question-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Let $p$ be $2$, $3$, or $5$, and let $f$ be a mod $p$ Bianchi eigenform over $K$ with Hecke eigenvalues in $\mathbb{F}_{p}$.
  Suppose that the associated Galois representation $\rho_f$ satisfies $\det \rho_f = \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$, and assume that the rank of $X_{0}(15)(K)$ is $0$.
  Then $f$ lifts at higher level.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
Across the fields $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, levels $\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n})$ and characteristics $p$ with
  $d \in \{1,2,3,7,11\}$, $\operatorname{Nm}(\mathfrak{n}) \le 500$ and $2 < p < 20$, over $78.6\%$ of all ethereal mod $p$ Bianchi modular forms admit higher-level characteristic $0$ lifts.

  With no restrictions on $p$ and $\operatorname{Nm}(\mathfrak{n}) \le 1000$, at least $43.8\%$ lift.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
We restrict to these particular fields because they are exactly the Euclidean imaginary quadratic fields, giving a simple word decomposition algorithm for the groups $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$. We do not pursue the same question of lifting ethereal eigenvalue systems over other fields in this paper.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- conjecture-1: body_md -->
Any ethereal mod $p$ Bianchi modular form over a norm-Euclidean imaginary quadratic field $K$ admits a higher-level characteristic $0$ lift.
<!-- end conjecture-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
One can reformulate Conjecture \ref{conj:existence} as a statement about global deformation rings attaining characteristic $0$ points after adding sufficient local conditions.
  In our situation, the expected dimension of the corresponding deformation space equals $0$, but since the spaces tend to be highly singular, it is difficult to obtain concrete results.
  For further details on this point of view, see \cite{calegari2009}.
<!-- end remark-2 -->

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
Suppose $M$ is a $\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n})]$-module which is free of finite rank over $\mathbb{Z}$, and let $M^{\vee} \mathrel{\vcenter{\baselineskip0.5ex \lineskiplimit0pt
      \hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}} 
  = \operatorname{Hom}_\mathbb{Z}(M, \mathbb{Z})$.
  Then $H^{j}(\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n}), M^{\vee})_\mathrm{tors} \cong H_{j-1} {(\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n}), M)}_\mathrm{tors}$ for any $j \ge 1$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Choose a free resolution $P_\bullet \to \mathbb{Z}$ of $\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n})]$-modules, where $\mathbb{Z}$ has the trivial action.
  Then the homology of the complex $C_\bullet \mathrel{\vcenter{\baselineskip0.5ex \lineskiplimit0pt
      \hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}} 
  = P_\bullet \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n})]} M$ is $H_\bullet (\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n}), M)$.
  Notice that $C_j$ is free over $\mathbb{Z}$ since $P_j$ is free over $\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n})]$ and $M$ is free over $\mathbb{Z}$.
  It follows that $d(C_j)$ is a free submodule of $C_{j-1}$, being a submodule of a free module over a PID.
  We may then apply the universal coefficient theorem~\cite[3.6.5]{weibel1994}, giving a split exact sequence
  \begin{equation}
    0 \to \operatorname{Ext}^{1}_\mathbb{Z} (H_{j-1}(C_\bullet), \mathbb{Z}) \to H^{j}(\operatorname{Hom}_\mathbb{Z}(C_\bullet, \mathbb{Z})) \to \operatorname{Hom}_\mathbb{Z} (H_j(C_\bullet), \mathbb{Z}) \to 0.
  \end{equation}
  By the tensor-hom adjunction, we have an isomorphism of complexes
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:26}
    \operatorname{Hom}_\mathbb{Z}(C_{\bullet}, \mathbb{Z})  \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n})]}(P_\bullet, M^{\vee}),
  \end{equation}
  so the middle term computes $H^{j}(\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n}), M^{\vee})$.
  For the term on the left, note that $H_{j-1}(\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n}), M)$ is finitely generated since $\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n})$ is a Bianchi group, so a standard computation yields that $\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_\mathbb{Z}(H_{j-1}(\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n}), M), \mathbb{Z}) \cong H_{j-1}(\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n}), M)_\mathrm{tors}$.
  On the other hand, $\operatorname{Hom}_\mathbb{Z} (H_j(C_\bullet), \mathbb{Z})$ is torsion-free.
  Since the sequence is split, the result follows.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:small-prime-liftability}
  Let $p \in \{2,3,5\}$, and let $f$ be a mod $p$ Bianchi eigenform over $K$ with Hecke eigenvalues in $\mathbb{F}_{p}$, with associated Galois representation $\rho$.
  Suppose the rank of $X_{0}(15)(K)$ is $0$.
  Then $f$ is liftable.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The strategy is to apply the recent result of Caraiani--Newton \cite{caraiani2023}, which states that elliptic curves over $K$ where $X_{0}(15)(K)$ has rank $0$ are modular.
  If we can find an elliptic curve $E /K$ satisfying $E[p] \simeq \rho$, then by modularity there exists a characteristic $0$ Bianchi modular form $F$ such that $\overline{\rho}_{F} \simeq \rho$.
  When $p$ is $2$, this is quite explicit.
  The associated mod $2$ Galois representation $\rho$ has image contained in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_2) \simeq S_{3}$.
  Note that $\rho$ factors through a finite extension $L = \overline{K}^{\ker(\rho)}$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K) \simeq \mathrm{im}(\rho)$.
  If $g \in K[x]$ is a separable cubic polynomial whose splitting field is $L$, then the elliptic curve $E_{g} : y^{2} = g(x)$ satisfies $E[2] \simeq \rho$.

  For the remaining two primes, the existence of such elliptic curves is ensured by \cite[Theorem 2]{shepherd-barron1997}. The argument boils down to noting that such elliptic curves are
  classified by $K$-points of the twisted modular curves $X_{\rho}(p)$, and for $p=3,5$, these curves are isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
Out of the norm-Euclidean imaginary quadratic fields, the condition on $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ holds for $d \in \{1,2,3\}$, but not for $d \in \{7,11\}$.
  For individual elliptic curves over these fields, it is possible to check modularity using the Faltings--Serre--Livné method.
  This is recorded in the LMFDB \cite{lmfdb}.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:ethereal-ellcurve-steinberg}
  Suppose $f$ is an ethereal mod $p$ Bianchi modular form of level $\Gamma_0(\mathfrak{n})$, and that $F$ is a characteristic $0$ lift of level $\mathfrak{nr}$ associated to an elliptic curve $E$ over $K$.
  If $\mathfrak{q} \mid \mathfrak{r}$ is a divisor such that $(\mathfrak{q}, \mathfrak{n}p) =1$ and $F$ is new at $\mathfrak{q}$, then one of the following two statements is true.
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item We have $p=2$, the curve $E$ is a ramified quadratic twist of an elliptic curve with good or multiplicative reduction at $\mathfrak{q}$, and $\mathfrak{q}$ divides $\mathfrak{r}$ exactly twice.     
  \item The curve $E$ has multiplicative reduction at $\mathfrak{q}$, and $\mathfrak{q}$ divides $\mathfrak{r}$ exactly once. Furthermore,
  \begin{equation}
    a_{\mathfrak{q}}(f) \equiv \pm (1 + \operatorname{Nm}(\mathfrak{q})) \ (\mathrm{mod}\ p).
  \end{equation}
  \end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\rho_f$ and $\rho_F$ denote the Galois representations associated to $f$ and $F$, respectively, with $\rho_f \cong \overline{\rho}_F \ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)$.
  We may realise $\rho_F$ as $T_p E \cong \mathbb{Z}_p \oplus \mathbb{Z}_p$, and observe that since $\rho_f$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{q}$ but $\rho_F$ is not, the subgroup $\rho_F(I_{\mathfrak{q}}) \subset \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ is in the kernel of reduction mod $p$.
  Accordingly, $\rho_F(I_{\mathfrak{q}})$ is a closed subgroup of a pro-$p$ group, hence itself pro-$p$. 

  By the work of Serre--Tate \cite{serre1968}, the size of the inertia image $\rho_F(I_{\mathfrak{q}})$ is determined by the reduction type of $E$ over $K_{\mathfrak{q}}$: it is trivial in the case of good reduction, finite in the case of potentially good reduction, and infinite in the case of potentially multiplicative reduction.
  The good reduction case is ruled out by assumption.

  In the potentially good reduction case, we know that $\rho_F(I_{\mathfrak{q}})$ is one of the groups
  \begin{equation}
\{C_2,C_3,C_4,C_6, Q_8, C_3 \rtimes C_4, \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3) \},
  \end{equation}
  by \cite{kraus1990}.
  None of these are $p$-groups when $p\ge 5$.
  For $p=3$, the only $3$-group in the list is $C_3$.
  But an elementary argument shows that $\ker(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_3) \to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3))$ contains no element of order $3$, so $\rho_F(I_{\mathfrak{q}})$ must be trivial.
  A similarly simple computation shows that when $p=2$ there are no elements of order $4$ in $\ker(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_2) \to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_2))$, so the only $2$-group possibly isomorphic to $\rho_F (I_{\mathfrak{q}})$ is $C_2$.
  This case does occur, and by \cite[Lemma 4.2]{coppola2026}, the base change $E_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}$ is then a ramified quadratic twist of a curve over $K_{\mathfrak{q}}$ with good reduction.

  It remains to rule out additive reduction in the potentially multiplicative case.
  This happens precisely when $E$ attains multiplicative reduction over a ramified quadratic extension $K'_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}$, in which case a twist $E'$ of $E$ has multiplicative reduction over $K_{\mathfrak{q}}$.
  Write $\rho_F \cong \rho_{E'} \otimes \psi$ with $\psi$ the corresponding quadratic character.
  We claim that $\rho_{E'}|_{G_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}}$ is upper-triangular.
  Indeed, we have an isomorphism of $G_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}$-modules $E'(\overline{K}_{\mathfrak{q}}) \cong \overline{K}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\times}/q^{\mathbb{Z}}$ where $q \in K_{\mathfrak{q}}$ is the Tate parameter for $E'$.
  A basis for the $p^{n}$-torsion of $E'$ is given by $\{\zeta_{p^{n}}, q^{1/p^{n}}\}$, and $\rho_{E'}$ acts like the $p$-adic cyclotomic character $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ on the first basis vector and trivially on the quotient since $q \in K_{\mathfrak{q}}$, hence the decomposition.
  Since $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{q}$, we find that
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:15}
    \rho_F |_{I_{\mathfrak{q}}} \cong \begin{pmatrix}
      \psi & \ast \\ 0 &\psi
    \end{pmatrix},
  \end{equation}
  so projection onto the diagonal gives a surjection $\rho_F(I_{\mathfrak{q}}) \to \mathbb{Z}/2$ as $\psi$ is ramified.
  But this is impossible when $p$ is odd since $\rho_F(I_{\mathfrak{q}})$ is pro-$p$. The claimed reduction types then follow, and the valuations of the conductors are well-known consequences.
  

  Finally, we prove the formula for $a_{\mathfrak{q}}(f)$ in the case of multiplicative reduction.
  The argument above with $E$ in place of $E'$ shows that the restriction of $\overline{\rho}_F$ to the decomposition group at $\mathfrak{q}$ has the shape
  \begin{equation}
    \overline{\rho}_F|_{G_{K_\mathfrak{q}}} = \begin{pmatrix}
      \eta \overline{\chi}_{\mathrm{cyc}} & \ast \\ 0 & \eta
    \end{pmatrix},
  \end{equation}
  where $\eta$ is an unramified quadratic character, and $\overline{\chi}_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ is the mod $p$ cyclotomic character.
  We then compute  
  \begin{equation}
    a_{\mathfrak{q}} (f) = \operatorname{Tr} \rho_f (\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}}) = \operatorname{Tr} \overline{\rho}_F(\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}}) =  \eta(\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}})(\overline{\chi}_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}}) + 1) = \pm (\operatorname{Nm}(\mathfrak{q}) + 1),
  \end{equation}
  as claimed.

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
\label{rmk:blocking}
  There exist many ethereal forms in our dataset whose Galois representations we do not expect to lift to an elliptic curve, on the basis of Proposition \ref{prop:ethereal-ellcurve-steinberg} and the Ramanujan bound.
  For example, over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$, we have a mod $19$ ethereal form $f$ at level $\mathfrak{n} = (1 - 6\omega)$ (label \texttt{73.1}), with $\omega = \sqrt{-2}$.
  Its Hecke eigenvalue at the prime $\mathfrak{p}_{3} = (1 - \omega)$ (label \texttt{3.2}) is $5$; a lift $F$ associated to an elliptic curve (i.e. one with rational Hecke eigenvalues) must have $\lvert a_{\mathfrak{p}_3}(F) \rvert \leq 2\sqrt{3}$.
  However, there are no integers $n \equiv 5 \ (\mathrm{mod}\ 19)$ satisfying this inequality.
  This problem can be avoided if $\mathfrak{p}_3$ appears in the level of $F$, as the Hecke eigenvalues for primes in the level of $F$ need not match those of $f$ per our conjecture.
  But we have $a_{\mathfrak{p}_3}(f)^2 - (1+\operatorname{Nm}(\mathfrak{p}_3))^2 \equiv 9 \ (\mathrm{mod}\ 19)$, and so this cannot happen.
	
  For ethereal forms in large characteristic, there are very few possible Hecke eigenvalues for primes of small norm for which an integer lift can be chosen that also satisfies the Ramanujan bound.
  As primes satisfying the level-raising condition are also expected to be rare (see Section \ref{sec:feasible}), we typically do not expect lifts to be associated to elliptic curves for large characteristic.

\label{prop:level-raising-over-Q}
  Let $p > 3$ and $\ell$ be distinct primes, and fix $N \in \mathbb{N}$ coprime to both.
  Suppose $f \in H^{1}(\Gamma_0(N), \mathbb{F}_p)$  is a Hecke eigenform satisfying $a_\ell (f) = \pm (1+\ell) \ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)$ with $\rho_f$ irreducible.

  \begin{equation}
\alpha_1 \oplus \alpha_\ell : H^{1}(\Gamma_0(N), \mathbb{F}_p)^{2} \to H^{1}(\Gamma_0(N\ell), \mathbb{F}_p).
  \end{equation}

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->

<!-- example-4: body_md -->
When $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$, the principal Bianchi group is $\Gamma = \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}])$.
  A presentation can be obtained from that of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}])$ in \cite[Proposition 4.3.4]{Fine1989}, and is given by
  \begin{equation*}
    \Gamma = \langle T, T_\omega, S \mid S^2 = (TS)^3 = [T,T_\omega] = [S, T_\omega]^2 = 1  \rangle, 
  \end{equation*}
  where $[g,h] = g^{-1}h^{-1}gh$ denotes the commutator.
  This isomorphism can be realised explicitly with the identifications $T = \begin{psmallmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1\end{psmallmatrix}$, $T_{\omega} = \begin{psmallmatrix}1 & \omega \\ 0 & 1\end{psmallmatrix}$, and $S = \begin{psmallmatrix}0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{psmallmatrix}$, where $\omega = \sqrt{-2}$.
  A $1$-cocycle $\phi \in Z^1\left( \Gamma , V \right)$ must therefore satisfy the following relations:
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item $\phi(S)( 1 + S ) = 0$,
  \item $\phi(T)S( 1 + TS + (TS)^2 ) + \phi(S)(1 + TS + (TS)^2) = 0$,
  \item $\phi(T)T^{-1}(1-T_{\omega}^{-1}) + \phi(T_{\omega})(T^{-1}-1)T_{\omega}^{-1} = 0$,
  \item $\phi(T_{\omega}) (-S + S T_{\omega}^{-1} S T_{\omega} - T_{\omega}^{-1} S T_{\omega} + 1)  + \phi(S)(S + ST_{\omega}S + T_{\omega}^{-1} S T_{\omega} + T_{\omega}) = 0 $.
  \end{enumerate}

  Fixing a basis for $V$ over $R$, each of these conditions gives a condition satisfied by the values $\phi(T)$, $\phi(T_\omega)$ and $\phi(S)$ as elements of $V$. Conversely, any triple satisfying all four conditions specifies a $1$-cocycle, and so the space of cocycles is computable as a kernel of a block matrix. Similarly, a $1$-coboundary is of the form $\phi(g) = vg - v$ for some $v \in V$, and so the subspace $B^1\left(\Gamma, V \right)$ is expressible in terms of the images of $T-1$, $T_\omega-1$ and $S-1$ in $R[G]$ on $V$. The quotient of these two spaces gives $H^1\left( \Gamma , V \right)$.

\label{lemma:K-group}
  A right $G$-action on $R = H'\backslash H$ extending the natural $H$-action is equivalent to the choice of a subgroup $K \subset G$ such that $H \cap K = H'$, $K H = G$ and $R \cong K \backslash G$.

Suppose $G$ acts on $R$, and define $K \mathrel{\vcenter{\baselineskip0.5ex \lineskiplimit0pt
      \hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}} 
  = \operatorname{Stab}_{G}(H'e)$. Transitivity immediately implies that $R \cong K \backslash G$, and evidently $H \cap K = H'$. For any $g \in G$, write $H'g = H' h$ for some $h \in H$, so $h g^{-1} \in K$. Choosing $k$ such that $h g^{-1} = k^{-1}$, we get $g = k h$, so $G = KH$.

  For the converse, simply transfer the right action from $K \backslash G$ to $R$.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
Fix a cocycle $f \in Z^{1}(G,\mathrm{CoInd}^{G}_{H}(V))$.
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item $\Phi(f) \in Z^{1}(G, \mathrm{CoInd}^{G}_{H'}(V))$.
  \item If $f$ is a coboundary, then so is $\Phi(f)$. 
  \end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
(1) We need to verify the cocycle condition for $\Phi(f)$, namely $\Phi(f) (g_{1}g_{2}) = \Phi(f)(g_{1})\cdot  g_{2} + \Phi(f)(g_{2})$ for $g_{1},g_{2} \in G$.
  By Equation \ref{eq:weird-action}, notice that
  \begin{align}
  \label{eq:10}
    \Phi(f)(g_{1}) \cdot g_{2} &= \sum_{r} \sigma_{g_{2}}^{-1}(r) \otimes \left(f(\gamma\sigma_{g_{1}}(r) g_{1} r^{-1} \gamma^{-1}) \cdot \gamma r g_{2}\right) \\
  &= \sum_{r} r\otimes \left(f(\gamma \sigma_{g_{1}}(\sigma_{g_{2}}(r)) g_{1} \sigma_{g_{2}}(r)^{-1} \gamma^{-1}) \cdot \gamma \sigma_{g_{2}}(r) g_{2}\right),
\end{align}
by reindexing the sum under the map $r \mapsto \sigma_{g_{2}}(r)$.
Next we manipulate the inner expressions; write
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:11}
  \gamma \sigma_{g_{1}}(\sigma_{g_{2}}(r)) &g_{1} \sigma_{g_{2}}(r)^{-1}\gamma^{-1} = \\
   &\underbrace{\left(\gamma \sigma_{g_{1}g_{2}}(r) g_{1}g_{2}r^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\right)}_{=\mathrel{\vcenter{\baselineskip0.5ex \lineskiplimit0pt
      \hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}} 
   A} \underbrace{\left(  \gamma r g_{2}^{-1} \sigma_{g_{2}}(r)^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\right)}_{=\mathrel{\vcenter{\baselineskip0.5ex \lineskiplimit0pt
      \hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}} 
   B}.
\end{align}
By the cocycle identity and the fact that $f$ satisfies $f(B) = - f(B^{-1}) \cdot B$,
we get
\begin{align}
  f(AB) &= f(A) \cdot B + f(B) \\
  &= (f(A) - f(B^{-1})) \cdot B.
\end{align}
Now note that
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:8}
B \cdot \gamma \sigma_{g_{2}}(r) g_{2} = \gamma r,
\end{equation}
and that $B^{-1} = \gamma \sigma_{g_{2}}(r)g_{2} r^{-1} \gamma^{-1}$, so
\begin{align}
  \Phi(f) (g_1) \cdot g_2
  =& \sum_{r} r \otimes (f(A) - f(B^{-1})) \cdot \gamma r \\
  =& \sum_{r} r \otimes f(\gamma \sigma_{g_{1}g_{2}}(r) g_{1}g_{2}r^{-1}\gamma^{-1}) \cdot \gamma r \\
  &-
    \sum_{r} r \otimes f(\gamma \sigma_{g_{2}}(r)g_{2} r^{-1} \gamma^{-1}) \cdot \gamma r \\
  =& \Phi(f) (g_1g_2) - \Phi(f) (g_2).
\end{align}
Rearranging, we find
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:12}
\Phi(f)(g_{1}g_{2}) = \Phi(f)(g_{1}) \cdot g_{2} + \Phi(f)(g_{2}),
\end{equation}
as required.


(2) If $f(g) = m \cdot g - m$ for some $m \in \mathrm{CoInd}^{G}_{H}(V)$, define $w = \sum_{r} r \otimes (m \cdot \gamma r)$.
Then, unfolding definitions and applying Equation \ref{eq:weird-action}, we find

\begin{align}
  \label{eq:13}
  \Phi(f)(g)
  &= \sum_{r} r \otimes \left(f(\gamma \sigma_{g}(r) g r^{-1} \gamma^{-1}) \cdot \gamma r\right) \\
  &= \sum_{r} r \otimes \left(m \cdot \gamma \sigma_{g}(r) g r^{-1} \gamma^{-1} \cdot \gamma r - m \cdot \gamma r\right) \\
  &= \sum_{r} r \otimes \left(m \cdot \gamma \sigma_{g}(r) g - m \cdot \gamma r\right) \\
  &= \Big(\sum_{r}  \sigma_{g}^{-1}(r) \otimes (m \cdot \gamma r g )\Big) - \sum_{r} r \otimes (m \cdot \gamma r)\\
  &= w \cdot g - w,
\end{align}
which proves the second claim.

\label{conj:existence}
  Let $p$ be a rational prime, and let $f$ be an ethereal mod $p$ Bianchi eigenform with trivial coefficients over a norm-Euclidean imaginary quadratic field $K$ of level $\mathfrak{n}$.

<!-- remark-13: body_md -->
\label{rmk:hard-lifts}
  While we were able to lift only around $4$ in every $10$ ethereal forms across all fields up to norm $1000$, we nevertheless conjecture that lifts can always be found in general.
  The dataset consists of many eigenvalue systems in large characteristic, and these are expected to be very hard to lift, as noted in Section \ref{sec:feasible}.
  When we restrict to primes in the range $2$ to $20$, the proportion of lifts jumps significantly.
