<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
There is a unique probability measure \(\mathfrak m\) on \(\Gamma\) that
satisfies the (intensity-one) recovery property.  It is the scaling-limit law, in the
unparametrized Hausdorff trace topology, of three-dimensional loop-erased
random walk from \(0\) to \(\partial B\).
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:blc-main}
For each fixed \(0<\lambda\leq1\), the laws of \(K_\lambda^\delta\)
converge as \(\delta=1/N\downarrow0\), in the Hausdorff topology, to a law
\(\nu_\lambda\) on \(\mathfrak C_B\).  If
\(K_\lambda^B\sim\nu_\lambda\), then \(K_\lambda^B\) has empty interior and,
for every compact \(A\Subset B\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blc-recovery-intro}
 \mathbb E\!\left[
  \mathbf 1_{\{K_\lambda^B\cap A=\varnothing\}}
  e^{-\lambda m_B(K_\lambda^B,A)}\right]
 =\mathbb P^0\!\left(W[0,\tau_B]\cap A=\varnothing\right).
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:looprecovery}
 \mathcal D_\lambda(K_\lambda^B)
 \stackrel d= W[0,\tau_B].
\end{equation}
Moreover, \(\nu_\lambda\) is the unique probability law on
\(\mathfrak C_B\) satisfying \eqref{eq:blc-recovery-intro}.  For \(\lambda=1\), $\nu_1$ reduces to \(\mathfrak m\) in \cref{thm:main}.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\Cref{thm:main} gives an intrinsic justification for calling the
three-dimensional LERW scaling limit \emph{loop-erased Brownian motion} (LEBM):
its law is uniquely characterized by the Brownian recovery property.
This is uniqueness in distribution. Whether a Brownian trajectory almost
surely has a unique measurable loop-erasure, up to increasing reparametrization,
remains open in both two and three dimensions.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:inversion}
Let \(K_\infty\) be the trace of the scaling limit of infinite
three-dimensional LERW from \(0\), as in
\cite[Theorem~10.1]{LiShiraishi}, with its endpoint at infinity adjoined.
Regard this as a random compact subset of
\(\widehat{\mathbb R^3}=\mathbb R^3\cup\{\infty\}\), and extend
\(\iota(x)=x/|x|^2\) by \(\iota(0)=\infty\) and \(\iota(\infty)=0\).
Then
\[
 \iota(K_\infty)\stackrel{\mathrm d}=K_\infty.
\]
For oriented curves from \(0\) to \(\infty\), inversion is accompanied
by reversal of orientation. The assertion is modulo reparametrization.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The problem of defining Laplacian-\(b\) motion in three dimensions
was already formulated by Lawler~\cite[Section~1]{LawlerLaplacian}.
At \(b=1\), it would be interesting to characterize the loop-erased
Brownian motion constructed here by this local growth rule. In the discrete model, conditional on an initial
segment \(\eta\), the next-step weights are proportional to
\(p(\eta_{\mathrm{tip}},y)h_\eta(y)\), where \(h_\eta(y)\) is the
probability of reaching the target before hitting \(\eta\).
In a continuum domain \(D\), the corresponding function is
\[
 h_t(x)=\mathbb P^x\bigl(\tau_{\partial D}
                         <\tau_{\gamma[0,t]}\bigr).
\]
The problem is to define the normalized first-power harmonic weights
at the random tip, prove independence from the tip regularization,
and establish existence and uniqueness of the resulting growth law.
One possible route to identification is to derive the intensity-one
recovery property \eqref{eq:lerw-recovery} from this local rule and then apply
\cref{thm:main}. Let us remark that the comparison of harmonic functions in the proof of the boundary Harnack principle \cite[Theorem~1.5 and Remark~1.3]{GLLLL24} may help control the normalized
weights near the tip.
 This question is left for our future work.

<!-- fact-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:closed-decoration-avoidance}
Let \(D\subset\mathbb R^3\) be bounded and open, let
\(K,A\subset\overline D\) be compact, and let
\(\mathcal L_D^\lambda\) be a Brownian loop soup of intensity
\(0<\lambda<\infty\). Let \(H\) be the union in \eqref{eq:decoration} before taking closure, so that \(\overline H=\mathcal D_\lambda(K)\):
\[
 H=K\cup\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathcal L_D^\lambda:
                         \,\ell\cap K\ne\varnothing}\ell.
\]
Almost surely, \(\overline H\cap A=\varnothing\) if and only if
\(H\cap A=\varnothing\).  Consequently, for a random compact \(K\)
independent of the soup and every deterministic compact \(A\),
\[
 \mathbb P(\overline H\cap A=\varnothing\mid K)
 =\mathbf 1_{\{K\cap A=\varnothing\}}e^{-\lambda m_D(K,A)}.
\]
Here the closure is taken in \(\overline D\).

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:no-polar-subarc}\label{lem:blc-nonpolarity}
Fix \(0\le\lambda<\infty\). For a random \(K\in\mathfrak C_B\),
the identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shared-recovery-transform}
 \mathbb E H_\lambda(K,A)
 =\mathbb P^0(W[0,\tau_B]\cap A=\varnothing),
 \qquad A\Subset B\text{ compact},
\end{equation}
are equivalent to
\(\mathcal D_\lambda(K)\stackrel d=W[0,\tau_B]\).
Either condition implies
\(K\in\mathfrak C_B^{\mathrm{np}}\) almost surely; if
\(K\in\Gamma\), it implies \(K\in\Gamma^+\) almost surely.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We give the local argument outlined in \cite[Section~1.5]{Cai26}.
First, \(K\subset\mathcal D_\lambda(K)\) and a three-dimensional
Brownian range has empty interior, so \(K\) has empty interior.
If local nonpolarity fails with positive probability, countability of
\(\mathscr T\) gives a fixed triple \((W,V,U)\) for which
\[
 K\cap W\ne\varnothing,\qquad \operatorname{Cap}(K\cap\overline U)=0
\]
with positive probability. Conditional on such a \(K\), Brownian loops
almost surely avoid the polar set \(K\cap\overline U\), by local
absolute continuity of bridges with respect to Brownian motion.
Consequently every attached loop entering \(V\) also hits
\(K\setminus U\), and with conditional probability at least
\[
 \exp\{-\lambda m_B(\overline V,\overline B\setminus U)\}>0
\]
no such loop occurs, by \eqref{eq:separated-loop-finite}.
On this event the decoration meets \(W\), but its intersection with
\(\overline W\) is polar.
This is impossible for a stopped Brownian range: whenever it meets the
open set \(W\), it contains there the range of a nondegenerate time
interval, which has dimension two and positive Newtonian capacity
\cite[Theorems~4.29(b) and~4.32]{MortersPeres}.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:borel-inverse}
The Lusin--Suslin theorem states that an injective Borel map between
standard Borel spaces has Borel image and a Borel inverse on its
image; see \cite[Theorem~15.1 and Corollary~15.2]{Kechris}. Applied to \(\Phi\) on

of the coordinate vector \(\Phi(K)\) determines the law of \(K\)
as a compact set. This is the only measurable reconstruction result
needed below.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:mixed-moments}\label{lem:blc-mixed-moments}
Fix \(0\le\lambda<\infty\). Suppose that, for each finite list
\((x_i,y_i)_{i\le r}\subset\mathcal Z^{(2)}\), with repetitions
allowed, there is a random compact \(C\Subset B\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shared-probe-hypothesis}
 \mathbb E_C H_\lambda(C,A)
 =\prod_{i=1}^rX_{x_i,y_i}(A),
 \qquad A\subset\overline B\text{ compact}.
\end{equation}
Then at most one law on \(\mathfrak C_B\) satisfies
\eqref{eq:shared-recovery-transform}. In particular, any two such
laws \(\mu_1,\mu_2\) have the same mixed Green function moments:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-moment-equality}
 \mathbb E_{\mu_1}\prod_{i=1}^rX_{x_i,y_i}(K)
 =\mathbb E_{\mu_2}\prod_{i=1}^rX_{x_i,y_i}(K).
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:green-path-limit}
The interpolated paths \((\omega_i^\delta)_{i\le r}\) converge jointly to
independent normalized Brownian bridges
\((\Gamma_D^{x_i,y_i})_{i\le r}\).  In
particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raw-union-limit}
 C^{0,\delta}\Rightarrow
 C^0:=\bigcup_{i=1}^r\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_D^{x_i,y_i})
\end{equation}
in Hausdorff distance, and \(C^0\Subset D\) almost surely.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We give only the additional points needed for path convergence and the
three-dimensional normalization. For one pair of endpoints, let \(N_\omega\) be the number of steps of the bridge. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discrete-duration-law}
 \mathbb P_{D^\delta}^{x_\delta,y_\delta}(N_\omega=n)
 =\frac{Q^{\delta,n}(x_\delta,y_\delta)}
        {\mathcal G_{D^\delta}(x_\delta,y_\delta)}.
\end{equation}
On a fixed window \(n\delta^2/3\in[t_0,T]\), the killed local limit
gives convergence of bridge finite-dimensional distributions.
The bridge density relative to walk through time \(k\le n/2\) is
\[
 \frac{Q^{\delta,n-k}(S_k,y_\delta)}
      {Q^{\delta,n}(x_\delta,y_\delta)}\le C_{t_0,T},
\]
by \eqref{eq:lclt} and \eqref{eq:killed-lclt}. Tightness from Donsker's invariance principle and reversibility gives tightness in the uniform topology 
on both halves. Thus, on each fixed duration window, 
the laws of the fixed-duration bridges converge 
weakly in the uniform topology.

By \eqref{eq:killed-lclt} and \eqref{eq:green-normalization}, the duration law
converges to \(p_D(t,x,y)\,\,\mathrm d t/G_D(x,y)\), since admissible times
have mesh \(2\delta^2/3\). The Gaussian and spectral bounds make the
short- and long-duration tails uniformly negligible, so removing the
window gives the claimed variable-duration path convergence.
Independence gives the joint statement; taking traces gives
\eqref{eq:raw-union-limit}, and each limiting bridge has a compact trace
contained in \(D\).

\emph{Step 1: scales, lattice cover, and quantifiers.}
Put \(d_i^0=|a_i-b_i|>0\).  Fix an integer \(M\ge1\), to be chosen at
the end, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-scales}
 s=\varepsilon^2R_*,\qquad h=\varepsilon^{5/4}R_*,\qquad
 L=\varepsilon^{1/2}R_*.
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scale-ratios}
 \frac{s}{h}=\frac{h}{L}=\varepsilon^{3/4}.
\end{equation}
The order of all choices below is important.  We first choose \(\varepsilon>0\)
so small that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scale-separation}
 32s<h,\qquad 32h<L,
\end{equation}
and that \(L\) is smaller than one twentieth of
\[
 \min_{i\le r}\big\{\operatorname{dist}(a_i,\partial D),\operatorname{dist}(b_i,\partial D),
                         |a_i-b_i|\big\}.
\]
We then choose \(\delta_0(\varepsilon)>0\) so small that, for every
\(0<\delta<\delta_0(\varepsilon)\), one has \(\delta<s/100\), and the same
inequalities, with a factor \(1/2\) of slack, hold for all lattice
approximations of the endpoints.  Shrinking \(\delta_0(\varepsilon)\) once more,
we also require, for every \(i\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-uniform-separation}
 |a_i^\delta-b_i^\delta|\ge\frac12d_i^0,
 \qquad R+\delta\le\frac14d_i^0,
 \quad R:=L/2.
\end{equation}
From now on \(\delta\) is arbitrary in this range.  This is exactly the
order of limits in \eqref{eq:H2}.

Since \(\operatorname{dist}(U,\partial D)>10R_*\), the closed \(2R_*\)-neighbourhood
\(U^+\) of \(U\) is compactly contained in \(D\).  Put
\[
 s_\delta=2\delta\left\lceil\frac{s}{2\delta}\right\rceil.
\]
The half-open cubes
\[
 Q=v_Q+[-s_\delta/2,s_\delta/2)^3,\qquad
 v_Q\in s_\delta\mathbb Z^3\subset\delta\mathbb Z^3,
\]
partition \(\mathbb R^3\).  Let \(\mathcal Q_\delta\) consist of the cubes
meeting the closed \((s+\delta)\)-neighbourhood of \(U\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cube-cover-count}
 |\mathcal Q_\delta|\le C_U s^{-3},\qquad
 Q\subset B(v_Q,s)\quad(Q\in\mathcal Q_\delta),
\end{equation}
for all \(\delta<s/100\).  Indeed,
\(s\le s_\delta<s+2\delta\), and the half diagonal of \(Q\) is
\(\sqrt3s_\delta/2<s\).  The second inclusion also shows that all
\(B(v_Q,h/2)\) occurring below are contained in \(U^+\), hence in \(D\).

For \(Q\in\mathcal Q_\delta\) and a nearest-neighbour path \(\gamma\),
set
\begin{align}
 \sigma_Q(\gamma)&=\inf\{j:\gamma_j\in \mathbb B_\delta(v_Q,4s)\},
       \notag\\
 \Lambda_Q(\gamma)&=\operatorname{supp}(\gamma[0,\sigma_Q])\cap
       \big(\mathbb B_\delta(v_Q,h/2)\setminus\mathbb B_\delta(v_Q,4s)\big),
       \label{eq:Gamma-Q}\\
 \Psi_Q(\gamma)&=\sup_{z\in \mathbb B_\delta(v_Q,3s)}
 \mathbb P^z\big(S[0,\tau_{v_Q,h/2}]\cap\Lambda_Q(\gamma)=\varnothing\big).
 \notag
\end{align}
When \(\sigma_Q(\gamma)=\infty\), we set \(\Psi_Q(\gamma)=0\).

\emph{Step 2: the off-center initial-segment estimate.}
The following is \cite[Lemma~3.3]{SS18}, in the notation needed below.

\begin{claim}\label{clm:off-center-prefix}
There are \(A_M,C_M<\infty\) and \(\kappa_M>0\), depending only on
\(M\), such that, whenever \(|a-v_Q|>2h\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SS-off-center}
 \mathbb P^a\big(\exists T<\tau_{D^\delta}:S_T\in Q,\
 \Psi_Q(\operatorname{LE}(S[0,T]))>A_M(s/h)^{\kappa_M}\big)
 \le C_M(s/h)^M.
\end{equation}
\end{claim}


\begin{proof}
Apply \cite[Lemma~3.3]{SS18} after translation and scaling, with inner
and outer radii \(4s/\delta\) and \(h/(2\delta)\).  The portion of the loop-erased path used in the
annular avoidance event of that lemma
is contained in \(\Lambda_Q\), and interior Harnack replaces the
starting point \(v_Q\) by the supremum over \(\mathbb B_\delta(v_Q,3s)\).
The radii have ratio \(8s/h\); absorbing the fixed factors into
\(A_M,C_M\) gives \eqref{eq:SS-off-center}.
\end{proof}


We also record the following identity for stopped normalized-random-walk bridges.

\begin{claim}\label{clm:stopped-Green}
Let \(\tau\) be a stopping time for the killed walk in \(D^\delta\), and
let \(F\in\mathcal F_\tau\).  If \(N\) is the number of steps of a
normalized random-walk bridge from \(a\) to \(b\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped-Doob-exact}
 \mathbb P_{D^\delta}^{a,b}(F,\tau<N)
 =\frac{\mathbb E^a\!\left[
   \mathbf 1_{\{F,\tau<\tau_{D^\delta}\}}
   \big(\mathcal G_{D^\delta}(S_\tau,b)-\mathbf 1_{\{S_\tau=b\}}\big)\right]}
 {\mathcal G_{D^\delta}(a,b)}.
\end{equation}
\end{claim}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that \(\tau\) is bounded.  Fix a stopped initial segment
\(\xi=(a=\xi_0,\ldots,\xi_k=x)\) on which \(F\) occurs.  Every full path
counted by \(\{\tau<N\}\) is obtained uniquely by appending to \(\xi\) a
positive-length path from \(x\) to \(b\).  The total weight of all such
continuations is
\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}Q^{\delta,n}(x,b)
 =\mathcal G_{D^\delta}(x,b)-\mathbf 1_{\{x=b\}}.
\]
Multiply by the weight of \(\xi\), sum over stopped initial segments, and divide
by \(\mathcal G_{D^\delta}(a,b)\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:stopped-Doob-exact} for bounded \(\tau\).  Applying it to
\(\tau\wedge m\) on \(\{\tau\le m\}\), and then using monotone
convergence, proves the general case.
\end{proof}

For any path \(\xi\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau-Q-definition}
 \tau_Q(\xi)=\inf\big\{T\ge0:\xi_T\in Q,\
 \Psi_Q(\operatorname{LE}(\xi[0,T]))>A_M(s/h)^{\kappa_M}\big\}.
\end{equation}
It is a stopping time because the expression tested at time \(T\) is a
deterministic function of \(\xi[0,T]\).  Call \(Q\) \emph{\(i\)-far} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:i-far}
 |v_Q-a_i^\delta|>2h\quad\hbox{and}\quad
 |v_Q-b_i^\delta|>2h.
\end{equation}
On \(\{\tau_Q<\tau_{D^\delta}\}\), the stopped point is in
\(Q\subset B(v_Q,s)\).  For an \(i\)-far cube,
\[
 |S_{\tau_Q}-b_i^\delta|>2h-s>h,
\]
so the indicator correction in \eqref{eq:stopped-Doob-exact} vanishes.
The stopped identity and \cref{clm:off-center-prefix} now give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stopped-Doob}
 \mathbb P_{D^\delta}^{a_i^\delta,b_i^\delta}(\tau_Q<N_i)
 \le
 \frac{\sup_{x\in Q\cap\delta\mathbb Z^3}\mathcal G_{D^\delta}(x,b_i^\delta)}
      {\mathcal G_{D^\delta}(a_i^\delta,b_i^\delta)}
 \mathbb P^{a_i^\delta}(\tau_Q<\tau_{D^\delta}).
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:green-3d-bounds}, the numerator is at most
\(C\delta/h\), whereas the denominator is at least \(c\delta\).
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:far-cube-bound}
 \mathbb P_{D^\delta}^{a_i^\delta,b_i^\delta}(\tau_Q<N_i)
 \le C_Mh^{-1}(s/h)^M.
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 3: cubes near the two endpoints.}
For \(x\in D^\delta\) and
\(0<\rho<R\), define

\begin{equation*}
 \Psi^{\mathrm{cen}}_{x;\rho,R}(\gamma)=
 \sup_{z\in\mathbb B_\delta(x,\rho/2)}
 \mathbb P^z\big(S[0,\tau_{x,R}]\cap\operatorname{supp}(\gamma)\cap(\mathbb B_\delta(x,R)\setminus\mathbb B_\delta(x,\rho))=\varnothing\big).
\end{equation*}

The next claim gives the centered analogue of \cref{clm:off-center-prefix}, controlling avoidance of the annular part of a loop-erased initial segment that exits the outer ball.

\begin{claim}\label{clm:centered-prefix}
There are \(A_M^{\mathrm e},C_M<\infty\) and
\(\kappa_{\mathrm e,M}>0\) such that, whenever
\(0<\rho<R\), \(\delta\le\rho/10\), and \(B(x,R)\subset D\),
\[
 \mathbb P^x\big(\exists T<\tau_{D^\delta}:\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{LE}(S[0,T])
 \not\subset\mathbb B_\delta(x,R),
 \Psi^{\mathrm{cen}}_{x;\rho,R}(\operatorname{LE}(S[0,T]))
 >A_M^{\mathrm e}(\rho/R)^{\kappa_{\mathrm e,M}}\big)
 \le C_M(\rho/R)^M.
\]
\end{claim}


\begin{proof}
This is \cite[Lemma~3.2]{SS18} after translation and scaling, followed
by interior Harnack to allow all starting points in
\(\mathbb B_\delta(x,\rho/2)\).  Its annular target is contained in ours,
so the same bound applies after adjusting the constants.
\end{proof}


From now on set
\begin{equation*}
 \rho=8h,\qquad R=L/2,\qquad \frac{\rho}{R}=\frac{16h}{L},
\end{equation*}
and put
\begin{equation*}
 B_M=A_M^{\mathrm e}16^{\kappa_{\mathrm e,M}}.
\end{equation*}

Let \(\eta_i=\operatorname{LE}(\omega_i)\).  For
\(e\in\{a_i^\delta,b_i^\delta\}\), let \(\mathcal B_{i,e}\) be the event
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-bad}
 \sup_{z\in D^\delta:\,|z-e|\le3h}\mathbb P^z
       (S[0,\tau_{z,L}]\cap\operatorname{supp}(\eta_i)=\varnothing)
 >B_M(h/L)^{\kappa_{\mathrm e,M}}.
\end{equation}

The next claim transfers this centered estimate to normalized random-walk bridges, uniformly near either marked endpoint.
\begin{claim}\label{clm:endpoint-bound}
Uniformly in sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\) and then sufficiently small
\(\delta\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-bound}
 \mathbb P(\mathcal B_{i,a_i^\delta}\cup\mathcal B_{i,b_i^\delta})
 \le C_M(h/L)^M.
\end{equation}
\end{claim}

\begin{proof}
We first treat \(a=a_i^\delta\), \(b=b_i^\delta\), and suppress \(i\).
The scale choice in Step~1 ensures
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-geometric-room}
 B(a,L)\Subset D,\qquad |a-b|>4L.
\end{equation}
Since \(\eta_i\) joins \(a\) to \(b\), it leaves the closed lattice ball
\(\mathbb B_\delta(a,R)\), where \(R=L/2\).  Let
\begin{equation*}
 k=\inf\{j:\eta_i(j)\notin\mathbb B_\delta(a,R)\},\qquad u=\eta_i(k).
\end{equation*}
The
nearest-neighbour property gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-exit-shell}
 R<|u-a|\le R+\delta.
\end{equation}
By \cref{lem:last-exit-prefix}, applied to the final loop erasure, there
is a time index \(\sigma\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-last-exit-witness}
 \omega_{i,\sigma}=u,\qquad
 \operatorname{LE}(\omega_i[0,\sigma])=\eta_i[0,k].
\end{equation}
Because \(u\ne b\) by \eqref{eq:endpoint-geometric-room},
\(\sigma<N_i\).

Suppose \(\mathcal B_{i,a}\) occurs.  The supremum in
\eqref{eq:endpoint-bad} is over a finite set, so there is
\(z\in D^\delta\), \(|z-a|\le3h\), for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-witness-z}
 \mathbb P^z\big(S[0,\tau_{z,L}]\cap\operatorname{supp}(\eta_i)=\varnothing\big)
 >B_M(h/L)^{\kappa_{\mathrm e,M}}.
\end{equation}
The scale separation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-ball-inclusions}
 z\in\mathbb B_\delta(a,4h)=\mathbb B_\delta(a,\rho/2),\qquad
 \mathbb B_\delta(a,R)\subset\mathbb B_\delta(z,L),
\end{equation}
because \(R+3h<L\).  Let
\[
 \mathcal A_z=\{S[0,\tau_{z,L}]\cap\operatorname{supp}(\eta_i)=\varnothing\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal A_z\), the walk avoids every subset of \(\eta_i\) up to the
earlier exit time \(\tau_{a,R}\).  In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-event-inclusion-detailed}
 \mathcal A_z\subset\big\{
 S[0,\tau_{a,R}]\cap\operatorname{supp}(\eta_i[0,k])
 \cap(\mathbb B_\delta(a,R)\setminus\mathbb B_\delta(a,\rho))
 =\varnothing\big\}.
\end{equation}
Here \(\tau_{a,R}\le\tau_{z,L}\) follows from the second inclusion in
\eqref{eq:endpoint-ball-inclusions}.  Combining
\eqref{eq:endpoint-witness-z}--\eqref{eq:endpoint-event-inclusion-detailed}
and then taking the supremum over starts in \(\mathbb B_\delta(a,\rho/2)\) gives
\begin{equation}\Psi^{\mathrm{cen}}_{a;\rho,R}
       (\operatorname{LE}(\omega_i[0,\sigma]))\ge \mathbb P^z(\mathcal A_z)
 >B_M(h/L)^{\kappa_{\mathrm e,M}}
 =A_M^{\mathrm e}(\rho/R)^{\kappa_{\mathrm e,M}}.
 \label{eq:endpoint-forces-centered}
\end{equation}

Define the random-walk stopping time
\begin{multline*}
 \tau^{\mathrm{end}}_{i,a}(\xi)=\inf\big\{T\ge0:\
  \xi_T\in\mathbb B_\delta(a,R+\delta)\setminus\mathbb B_\delta(a,R),\\
  \operatorname{supp}\operatorname{LE}(\xi[0,T])\not\subset\mathbb B_\delta(a,R),\
  \Psi^{\mathrm{cen}}_{a;\rho,R}(\operatorname{LE}(\xi[0,T]))
     >A_M^{\mathrm e}(\rho/R)^{\kappa_{\mathrm e,M}}\big\}.
\end{multline*}
It is a stopping time, since every object tested at \(T\) is determined
by \(\xi[0,T]\).  Equations
\eqref{eq:first-exit-shell}, \eqref{eq:endpoint-last-exit-witness}, and
\eqref{eq:endpoint-forces-centered} show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-event-inclusion}
 \mathcal B_{i,a}
 \subset\{\tau^{\mathrm{end}}_{i,a}(\omega_i)
              \le\sigma<N_i\}.
\end{equation}

The event that defines \(\tau^{\mathrm{end}}_{i,a}\) is a subevent of
the event in \cref{clm:centered-prefix}.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-raw-bound}
 \mathbb P^a(\tau^{\mathrm{end}}_{i,a}<\tau_{D^\delta})
 \le C_M(\rho/R)^M
 \le C_M(h/L)^M,
\end{equation}
where \(16^M\) has been absorbed in \(C_M\).  At the stopped point
\(x\), \(|x-a|\le R+\delta\), and
\eqref{eq:endpoint-uniform-separation} gives
\begin{equation*}
 |x-b|\ge |a-b|-|x-a|
 \ge\frac12d_i^0-(R+\delta)\ge\frac14d_i^0.
\end{equation*}
Consequently \eqref{eq:green-3d-bounds} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-Doob-factor}
 \sup_{x\in\mathbb B_\delta(a,R+\delta)}
 \frac{\mathcal G_{D^\delta}(x,b)}
      {\mathcal G_{D^\delta}(a,b)}\le C.
\end{equation}
In particular \(x\ne b\), so the correction term in
\eqref{eq:stopped-Doob-exact} vanishes.  Applying
\cref{clm:stopped-Green} to
\eqref{eq:endpoint-event-inclusion} and using 
\eqref{eq:endpoint-raw-bound} and~\eqref{eq:endpoint-Doob-factor} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-endpoint-bound}
 \mathbb P_{D^\delta}^{a,b}(\mathcal B_{i,a})
 \le C_M(h/L)^M.
\end{equation}


For the other endpoint, use the standard reversal identity for LERW
under a symmetric kernel; it follows directly from the one-path formula
in \cite[Propositions~3.1 and~3.5]{Lawler18}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LERW-reversal-law}
 \operatorname{LE}(\omega)^\leftarrow\text{ under }\mathbb P_{D^\delta}^{a,b}
 \quad\stackrel d=\quad
 \operatorname{LE}(\widetilde\omega)\text{ under }\mathbb P_{D^\delta}^{b,a}.
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal B_{i,b}$ depends only on the trace, it is the the corresponding bad event near the starting
endpoint for the reversed path. Applying
\eqref{eq:first-endpoint-bound} with $a,b$ interchanged gives
$\mathbb P_{D^\delta}^{a,b}(\mathcal B_{i,b})\le C_M(h/L)^M$.
Together with the estimate for the starting endpoint, this proves the claim.
\end{proof}


\emph{Step 4: transfer from ordinary initial segments to the entangled
multipath LERW.}
For each vertex \(w\in C_r^\delta\), \cref{lem:prefix-witness} already
provides \(i,T\) with
\(\omega_{i,T}=w\) and
\(\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{LE}(\omega_i[0,T])\subset C_r^\delta\).
If \(w\ne b_i^\delta\), then \(T<N_i\).


Let \(\mathsf{Bad}\) be the union, over \(i\), of
\(\mathcal B_{i,a_i^\delta}\), \(\mathcal B_{i,b_i^\delta}\), and all
\(\{\tau_Q(\omega_i)<N_i\}\) with \(Q\) \(i\)-far in the sense of
\eqref{eq:i-far}.  By \eqref{eq:far-cube-bound},
\eqref{eq:endpoint-bound}, and \eqref{eq:cube-cover-count},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bad-union}
 \mathbb P(\mathsf{Bad})
 \le C_{M,r}\left((h/L)^M+s^{-3}h^{-1}(s/h)^M\right).
\end{equation}
The next claim combines the off-center and endpoint estimates to control avoidance of the entire entangled range.

\begin{claim}
\label{clm:good-event-geometry}
On \(\mathsf{Bad}^c\),
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal H_\delta(C_r^\delta;s,L;U)
 \le C_M\max\{(s/h)^{\kappa_M},
                 (h/L)^{\kappa_{\mathrm e,M}}\}.
\end{equation*}
\end{claim}


\begin{proof}
Fix \(z\in U\cap\delta\mathbb Z^3\) within distance \(s\) of \(C_r^\delta\),
and choose a vertex \(w\in C_r^\delta\) with \(|z-w|\le s+\delta\).
By \cref{lem:prefix-witness}, some
\(\gamma=\operatorname{LE}(\omega_i[0,T])\) ends at \(w\) and satisfies
\(\operatorname{supp}\gamma\subset C_r^\delta\).  For the cube \(Q\) containing
\(w\), we have \(|z-v_Q|<3s\).

If \(Q\) is \(i\)-far, then \(w\ne b_i^\delta\), hence \(T<N_i\).
On \(\mathsf{Bad}^c\), the definition of the first bad time
\(\tau_Q\) therefore gives
\(\Psi_Q(\gamma)\le A_M(s/h)^{\kappa_M}\).
Since \(\Lambda_Q(\gamma)\subset C_r^\delta\) and
\(\mathbb B_\delta(v_Q,h/2)\subset\mathbb B_\delta(z,L)\), this bounds the
probability of avoiding \(C_r^\delta\) until \(\tau_{z,L}\).

Otherwise \(|z-e|<3h\) for an endpoint
\(e\in\{a_i^\delta,b_i^\delta\}\).  The complement of
\(\mathcal B_{i,e}\) bounds that probability by
\(B_M(h/L)^{\kappa_{\mathrm e,M}}\), because
\(\operatorname{supp}\eta_i\subset C_r^\delta\).
Taking the supremum over \(z\) proves the claim.
\end{proof}


\emph{Step 5: exponent bookkeeping and conclusion.}
We now make the earlier choice \(M=12\).  From
\eqref{eq:three-scales} and \eqref{eq:scale-ratios},
\begin{equation*}
 (h/L)^{12}=\varepsilon^9,
\end{equation*}
whereas the number of cubes and the \(h^{-1}\) cost of the Doob
transform give
\[
 s^{-3}h^{-1}(s/h)^{12}=R_*^{-4}\varepsilon^{-6}\varepsilon^{-5/4}\varepsilon^9=R_*^{-4}\varepsilon^{7/4}.
\]
Since \(R_*\) is fixed, \eqref{eq:bad-union} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bad-final}
 \sup_{0<\delta<\delta_0(\varepsilon)}
 \mathbb P(\mathsf{Bad})
 \le C(M,r,R_*)(\varepsilon^9+\varepsilon^{7/4})\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
On \(\mathsf{Bad}^c\), \cref{clm:good-event-geometry} and
\eqref{eq:scale-ratios} give
\[
 \mathcal H_\delta(C_r^\delta;s,L;U)\le C\varepsilon^\alpha,
\]
where
\begin{equation*}
 \alpha=\frac34\min\{\kappa_{12},\kappa_{\mathrm e,12}\}>0.
\end{equation*}
Set \(\eta_H=\alpha/2\).  There is \(\varepsilon_0>0\), independent of
\(\delta\), such that \(C\varepsilon^\alpha\le\varepsilon^{\eta_H}\) whenever
\(\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0\).  Hence, for every such \(\varepsilon\) and all
\(\delta<\delta_0(\varepsilon)\),
\[
 \left\{\mathcal H_\delta(C_r^\delta;\varepsilon^2R_*,
             \sqrt\varepsilon R_*;U)>\varepsilon^{\eta_H}\right\}
 \subset\mathsf{Bad}.
\]
Take first \(\limsup_{\delta\downarrow0}\), use
\eqref{eq:bad-final}, and then let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).  This is exactly
\eqref{eq:H2}.

\label{lem:macro-soup-coupling}

\label{lem:near-miss}
Let \(U_0\Subset U_1\Subset D\).  There is a finite constant \(C\), depending
only on \(t_0,T,U_0,U_1,D\), with the following property.  Suppose a
random lattice set \(C^\delta\subset U_0\) is independent of the test loop,
and define the target-measurable event
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-event}
 E^\delta:=\left\{
 \sup_{\substack{z\in U_1\cap\delta\mathbb Z^3\\
                  \operatorname{dist}(z,C^\delta)\le 2s}}
 \mathbb P^z\!\left(S[0,\tau_{z,L}]\cap C^\delta=\varnothing\right)
 \le q\right\}.
\end{equation}
Thus \(E^\delta\in\sigma(C^\delta)\).  Assume \(0<s<L\),
\(U_0^{+3L}\Subset U_1\), and \(\delta=o(s)\).  Then, on \(E^\delta\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-miss-mass}
 m_{D^\delta}^{\delta,[t_0,T]}
 \{\ell:\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{tr}\ell,C^\delta)\le s,
           \ \operatorname{tr}\ell\cap C^\delta=\varnothing\}
 \le C\left(q+\frac{L^2}{t_0}\right)+o_\delta(1).
\end{equation}
The same bound holds after imposing any further trace-measurable
restriction on the loops.
It also holds for an arbitrary target \(C^\delta\subset D^\delta\)
if only loops whose entire trace lies in \(U_0\) are counted, with the
same hittability event \(E^\delta\).
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
This is the rooted-loop argument of
\cite[proof of Proposition~5.2]{SS18}, with its LERW hittability input
replaced by \(E^\delta\). Condition on the target and keep the original
root and the factor \(1/n\) in loop measure. By time reversal, at a
cost of two we may assume the first approach occurs before \(n/2\).
After that approach, avoidance until exiting the ball of radius \(L\)
costs at most \(q\); failure to exit within \(n/4\) steps costs at most
\(CL^2/t_0\). In either case, a final interval of at least \(n/4\)
steps gives a return factor \(Cn^{-3/2}\). Thus the probability for a
loop rooted at any fixed vertex is at most

 C\left(q+L^2/t_0\right)n^{-3/2}.

Summing with weight \(1/n\) over \(O(\delta^{-3})\) roots and
\(3t_0\delta^{-2}\le n\le3T\delta^{-2}\) gives
\eqref{eq:near-miss-mass}. The condition \(\delta=o(s)\) allows us to
pass from the interpolated trace to a vertex within distance \(2s\).
For the localized version, the approach point lies in \(U_0\), which
is the only place where hittability is used.

\label{prop:blc-subsequential-geometry}
For fixed \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), the family \((K_\lambda^\delta,R^\delta)\)
is tight.  Along every convergent subsequence it has a Skorokhod realization
such that
\begin{equation*}
 (K_\lambda^\delta,R^\delta)\longrightarrow(K,R)
 \quad\text{a.s.},
 \qquad
 K\subset R\stackrel d=W[0,\tau_B].
\end{equation*}
Here \(K\) is compact and connected, \(0\in K\),
\(K\cap\partial B\) is a singleton, and \(\operatorname{int}K=\varnothing\).

For a fixed finite endpoint list, every mesh sequence has a subsequence on

which \(C_{\lambda,\times}^\delta\Rightarrow C\), jointly with
mutually independent normalized Brownian bridges
\((\Gamma_B^{a_i,b_i})_{i\le r}\), and

\begin{equation}\label{eq:blc-probe-subset}
 C\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^r\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_B^{a_i,b_i})\Subset B.
\end{equation}
The limit has at most \(r\) connected components, and every
component contains both endpoints of at least one labelled pair.

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:blc-continuum-identities}
Every radial subsequential limit \(K\) in
\cref{prop:blc-subsequential-geometry} satisfies, for every compact
\(E\Subset B\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blc-continuum-recovery}
 \mathbb E\!\left[
  \mathbf 1_{\{K\cap E=\varnothing\}}e^{-\lambda m_B(K,E)}\right]
 =\mathbb P^0(W[0,\tau_B]\cap E=\varnothing).
\end{equation}
For every finite ordered list \((a_i,b_i)_{i\le r}\) with
\(a_i,b_i\in B\), \(a_i\ne b_i\), and repeated pairs allowed, there is a
subsequential multipath limit \(C=C_\lambda(a_1,b_1;\ldots;a_r,b_r)\)
satisfying, for every compact \(E\subset\overline B\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blc-continuum-probe}
 \mathbb E_C\!\left[
  \mathbf 1_{\{C\cap E=\varnothing\}}e^{-\lambda m_B(C,E)}\right]
 =\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{G_{B\setminus E}(a_i,b_i)}{G_B(a_i,b_i)}.
\end{equation}
Here a numerator is zero if an endpoint lies in \(E\) or the two endpoints
belong to different components of \(B\setminus E\).

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:whole-space}
The law of \(K_\infty\) is the unique law on infinite curves from
\(0\) to \(\infty\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:whole-space-recovery}
 \mathcal D_\infty(K)\stackrel d=W[0,\infty)\cup\{\infty\},
\end{equation}
where \(W\) is Brownian motion started at \(0\).

<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
We record the exhaustion details so that no whole-space uniqueness
statement is being assumed. We use the crossing-loop estimate
\cite[Lemma~5.1]{JegoLupu26}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:whole-space-loop-tail}
 m_\infty(\overline B_L,B_R^c)\le cL/R,\qquad R\ge2L.
\end{equation}

First we prove uniqueness using only the bounded-domain probes already
constructed in \cref{prop:continuum-probe}. Fix finitely many pairs of distinct endpoints \((x_i,y_i)\), with repetitions allowed, and take the
corresponding probe \(C_R\) in \(B_R\). For two candidate laws
\(\mu_1,\mu_2\), set
\[
 X^R_{x,y}(K)=\frac{G_{B_R\setminus K}(x,y)}{G_{B_R}(x,y)},
 \qquad
 F^\infty_j(A)=
 \mathbb E_{\mu_j}\bigl[\mathbf 1_{\{K\cap A=\varnothing\}}
                         e^{-m_\infty(K,A)}\bigr].
\]
The closed-decoration avoidance argument of
Fact \ref{lem:closed-decoration-avoidance}, localized using
\eqref{eq:whole-space-loop-tail}, applies to every compact
\(A\subset\mathbb R^3\). Hence \eqref{eq:whole-space-recovery} implies
\(F^\infty_1(A)=F^\infty_2(A)\).
Apply \cref{prop:continuum-probe} with the compact obstacle
\(K\cap\overline B_R\) and use Fubini as in
\cref{lem:mixed-moments}. On \(\{C_R\subset B_L\}\), the replacement
of \(m_{B_R}(K,C_R)\) by \(m_\infty(K,C_R)\) changes the integrand by
at most \(cL/R\). Therefore
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_{\mu_1}\prod_iX^R_{x_i,y_i}(K)
       -\mathbb E_{\mu_2}\prod_iX^R_{x_i,y_i}(K)\right|
 \le 2cL/R+2\mathbb P(C_R\not\subset B_L).
\]
The probe is contained in the union of its normalized Brownian
bridges. Each bridge in \(B_R\) is a whole-space normalized bridge
conditioned to stay in \(B_R\). Such whole-space bridges have finite
lifetime and compact trace, since \(G_{\mathbb R^3}(x,y)<\infty\).
Consequently the last probability tends to zero when first
\(R\to\infty\) and then \(L\to\infty\).
Monotone convergence of Green functions and bounded convergence
now give equality of all mixed moments of
\(G_{\mathbb R^3\setminus K}(x,y)/G_{\mathbb R^3}(x,y)\).
The local nonpolarity argument in
\cref{lem:no-polar-subarc} and the Green-coordinate argument cited before
\cref{prop:green-injective} apply to infinite curves: the required
local finiteness of crossing loops follows from
\eqref{eq:whole-space-loop-tail}. The condition of local
nonpolarity is again Borel, using rational balls in \(\mathbb R^3\).
The moment-determinacy and measurable-reconstruction arguments of
\cref{lem:mixed-moments,rem:borel-inverse} therefore identify the
law of the infinite curve. This proves uniqueness.

For existence, let \(K^{(R)}\) have the radial LERW scaling-limit law
in \(B_R\). The finite/infinite LERW comparison
\cite[(10.2), proof of Theorem~10.1]{LiShiraishi}
couples initial segments through scale \(S\) with error \(O(S/R)\).
After first leaving that scale, either LERW can return to \(B_L\)
only if its underlying random walk does so; the standard
three-dimensional return bound is \(O(L/S)\).
By \cite[Proposition~10.2]{LiShiraishi} and lower semicontinuity of
total variation under weak convergence, the comparison passes to the
limiting stopped curves. The return estimate passes to the limit first
with slightly enlarged observation balls. Outside these return events,
the full traces in \(B_L\) agree with their stopped traces. Thus
\[
 d_{\rm TV}\bigl(\mathop{\rm Law}(K^{(R)}\cap B_L),
                 \mathop{\rm Law}(K_\infty\cap B_L)\bigr)
 \le c(S/R+L/S),\qquad L\ll S\ll R.
\]
The comparison in the cited source uses cubes for the intermediate
scale; enclosing balls give the displayed bound with adjusted constants.
In particular the local trace laws converge in total variation.
The same comparison makes every stopped initial segment of
\(K_\infty\) simple. Applying the return bound at increasing dyadic
scales shows that it eventually leaves every bounded set. Thus adjoining
\(\infty\) gives a simple compact path in $\widehat{\mathbb R^3}$, as required by the candidate class.
Couple these traces on \(B_S\), independently of one whole-space soup.
On agreement there, their decorations inside \(B_L\), \(L<S\),
can differ only through a loop meeting both \(B_L\) and \(B_S^c\);
its probability is at most \(cL/S\) by
\eqref{eq:whole-space-loop-tail}.
But the \(B_R\)-decoration of \(K^{(R)}\) has the law of
\(W[0,\tau_{B_R}]\), by \cref{thm:main} and dilation.
Letting \(R\to\infty\) and then \(S\to\infty\) proves
\eqref{eq:whole-space-recovery} on every bounded observation set.
The endpoint at infinity is deterministic, completing the proof.

The Brownian inversion and time-reversal identities
\cite{Yor85}, equivalently
\cite[Lemmas~3.3 and~3.5]{JegoLupu26}, give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverted-brownian-range}
 \iota\bigl(W[0,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}\bigr)
 \stackrel d=W[0,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}.
\end{equation}
For clarity concerning the endpoints, apply those identities first
to Brownian motion from \(x\ne0\) to infinity. Its inverted and
reversed trace is a normalized Brownian bridge from \(0\) to
\(\iota(x)\). Writing \(y=\iota(x)\), the bridge stopped upon leaving
\(B_S\) has density
\[
 \frac{G_{\mathbb R^3}(W_{\tau_{B_S}},y)}{G_{\mathbb R^3}(0,y)}
 =1+O(S/|y|)
\]
relative to stopped Brownian motion, uniformly for \(|y|>2S\).
The same Doob-transform formula bounds its subsequent return probability
to \(B_L\) by \(CL/S\). Thus its compactified trace converges to the
Brownian trace as \(|y|\to\infty\). On the other side, Brownian traces
started at \(x\to0\) converge by translation, and inversion is continuous
on the compactification. This proves
\eqref{eq:inverted-brownian-range}.

By \cite[Lemma~4.1]{JegoLupu26},
\(\iota(\mathcal L_\infty^1)\) is again an intensity-one Brownian
loop soup and remains independent of \(\iota(K_\infty)\).
(Their inversion has an additional minus sign, removed by reflection
invariance.) Since \(\iota\) is a homeomorphism of
\(\widehat{\mathbb R^3}\), it preserves intersections and commutes with
closure. Thus
\[
 \mathcal D_\infty\bigl(\iota(K_\infty)\bigr)
 \stackrel d=\iota\bigl(\mathcal D_\infty(K_\infty)\bigr)
 \stackrel d=W[0,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}.
\]
The inverted path, with reversed orientation, is an infinite simple curve
from \(0\) to \(\infty\). Apply \cref{lem:whole-space}.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rem:bdeddomain}
Let $D\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a bounded connected Lipschitz domain.
All laws below concern unparametrized traces, and all decorations use an
independent intensity-one Brownian loop soup in $D$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Interior point-to-point paths.}
For distinct $a,b\in D$, let $K_D^{a,b}$ denote the scaling limit of the
loop erasure of the normalized random-walk bridge from $a^\delta$ to
$b^\delta$ in $D^\delta$. It is a simple curve compactly contained in $D$,
and its law is uniquely characterized, among such curves with endpoints
$a,b$, by
\[
  \mathcal D_1^D(K_D^{a,b})
  \overset{d}=\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_D^{a,b}).
\]

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Exit excursions.}
Let $K_D^a$ have the radial law of \cref{thm:main}, with starting point $a$,
and write $\zeta(K)$ for the unique point of $K\cap\partial D$.
For a continuous $f:\partial D\to[0,\infty)$ with
\[
  Z_D^{a,f}:=\mathbb E^a[f(W_{\tau_D})]>0,
\]
define the law of $K_D^{a;f}$ by
\[
 \frac{d\operatorname{Law}(K_D^{a;f})}
      {d\operatorname{Law}(K_D^a)}(K)
 =\frac{f(\zeta(K))}{Z_D^{a,f}}.
\]
Its decoration has the law of the stopped Brownian range weighted by
$f(W_{\tau_D})/Z_D^{a,f}$, and this recovery property uniquely
characterizes its law among simple curves from $a$ with a single boundary
contact. Equivalently, it is the scaling limit obtained by weighting the
discrete exit excursion by $f$ of its exit point. 
The case
$f\equiv1$ is the unconditioned exit excursion. Disintegration by the
common exit point also gives the fixed-exit-point characterization for
harmonic-measure-almost every $z\in\partial D$; it does not, by itself,
assert convergence for arbitrary lattice approximations of a prescribed
boundary point.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Inversion covariance.}
Suppose $0\notin\overline D$, set $\iota(x)=x/|x|^2$, and put
$D'=\iota(D)$. Then $D'$ is again a bounded connected Lipschitz domain,
and
\[
 \iota(K_D^{a,b})\overset{d}=
 K_{D'}^{\iota(a),\iota(b)}.
\]
For exit excursions the corresponding statement is
\[
 \iota(K_D^{a;f})\overset{d}=
 K_{D'}^{\iota(a);f^\iota},
 \qquad
 f^\iota(z)=\frac{f(\iota(z))}{|z|},
 \quad z\in\partial D'.
\]
Multiplying the exit weight by a positive constant does not change the
normalized law. For the conditional laws with a fixed exit point, this
endpoint factor cancels, giving
\[
 \iota(K_D^{a,z})\overset{d}=
 K_{D'}^{\iota(a),\iota(z)}
\]
for harmonic-measure-almost every $z$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Justification.}
For interior endpoints, take one pair in \cref{prop:continuum-probe}.
Compact containment and recovery along
subsequences follow from 
\cref{lem:green-path-limit,prop:multipath-hittability,prop:common-decoration-stability}.
The stopped change of measure \eqref{eq:stopped-Doob-exact}, localized away
from the terminal point and combined with the reversal identity
\eqref{eq:LERW-reversal-law}, transfers the quasi-loop argument of
\cite[Section~6]{SS18}; the return estimates at the two fixed interior
endpoints allow us to remove the restrictions near those
endpoints. Thus these subsequential
limits are simple paths. The local-nonpolarity and Green function moment arguments of
\cref{lem:no-polar-subarc,lem:mixed-moments,prop:green-injective} apply with
the bridge range in place of the stopped Brownian range: the probes are
unchanged, and only the prescribed right-hand side of the recovery
transform changes. They give uniqueness and hence convergence of the full
sequence.

For exit excursions, direct decoration leaves the unique boundary point
unchanged. Weighting by $f$ therefore preserves the recovery property,
with the same weight on the Brownian exit point; continuity of $f$ also
passes this weighting through the joint discrete limit. The same
identification argument gives uniqueness, and disintegration gives the
almost-everywhere conditional statement.

Finally, the inversion identities of \cite[Lemmas~3.5 and~4.1]{JegoLupu26},
restricted to $D$, transport normalized bridges and the independent loop
soup. For a stopped Brownian path, the transformed law is instead the Doob
$h$-transform in $D'$, where $h(x)=|x|^{-1}$. Its density relative to
Brownian motion started at $\iota(a)$ and stopped on exiting $D'$ is
$h(W_{\tau_{D'}})/h(\iota(a))$. This gives exactly the exit weight
$f^\iota$ above. Inversion preserves intersections and closure, so
transporting the recovery identities and applying uniqueness proves the
covariance statements.
