<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:coeff}
Let $0<q<1$ and suppose that $\Omega_q(A)\subseteq\overline\mathbb D$. For every pair of unit vectors $x,y\in H$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coeffbound}
\begin{aligned}
|\langle Ax,y\rangle|
&\leq
\frac{q}{1+\sqrt{1-q^2}}
\left(1+\sqrt{1-|\langle x,y\rangle|^2}\right)
&&\text{if }|\langle x,y\rangle|\leq q,\\
|\langle Ax,y\rangle|
&\leq |\langle x,y\rangle|
&&\text{if }|\langle x,y\rangle|\geq q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Multiplying $y$ by a unimodular scalar, we may assume that $\langle x,y\rangle\geq0$. This does not change either modulus in \eqref{eq:coeffbound}. If $\langle x,y\rangle\geq q$, then
\[
\frac{\langle Ax,y\rangle}{\langle x,y\rangle}
\in \Omega_{\langle x,y\rangle}(A)
\subseteq \Omega_q(A)
\subseteq \overline{\mathbb D},
\]
where the inclusion $\Omega_t(A)\subseteq\Omega_r(A)$ for $0<r\leq t\leq1$ follows from the proof of \cite[Theorem~2.5]{li1994generalized}, which extends straightforwardly from matrices to bounded operators. Hence
$
|\langle Ax,y\rangle|
\leq |\langle x,y\rangle|,
$
which proves the second estimate in \eqref{eq:coeffbound}. 

Suppose that $0\leq\langle x,y\rangle<q$. Set
\[
e=\frac{x+y}{\sqrt{2(1+\langle x,y\rangle)}},
\qquad
f=\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2(1-\langle x,y\rangle)}},
\qquad
b=\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{1+\sqrt{1-\langle x,y\rangle^2}}.
\]
Then $e$ and $f$ are orthonormal. For $z\in\mathbb C$, put
$$
X_z=(1+z)e+(1-z)f, \quad Y_z=(1+\overline z)e-(1-\overline z)f,$$
and define $F(z)=\frac{\langle AX_z,Y_z\rangle}{4}$. A direct computation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:XzYz}
\|X_z\|^2=\|Y_z\|^2=2(1+|z|^2),
\qquad
\langle X_z,Y_z\rangle=4z.
\end{equation}






If
$
|z|=\frac{q}{1+\sqrt{1-q^2}},
$
then using \eqref{eq:XzYz}, a computation gives
$$
\frac{|\langle X_z,Y_z\rangle|}
{\|X_z\|\|Y_z\|}
=q.
$$
Thus, since by assumption
$
W_q(A)\subseteq q\overline{\mathbb D},
$
we have
\[
\frac{|\langle AX_z,Y_z\rangle|}
{\|X_z\|\|Y_z\|}
\leq q.
\]
Hence, when
$
|z|=\frac{q}{1+\sqrt{1-q^2}},
$
\[
|F(z)|
\leq
\frac{q}{4}\|X_z\|\|Y_z\|.
\]
In this case a further direct computation from \eqref{eq:XzYz} yields $\frac{q}{4}\|X_z\|\|Y_z\|
=\frac{q}{1+\sqrt{1-q^2}}$. Noting $\frac{s}{1+\sqrt{1-s^2}}$ is increasing on $[0,1)$, shows $
b\leq \frac{q}{1+\sqrt{1-q^2}}
$
and clearly $F(z)$ is a polynomial, so applying the maximum-modulus principle on the disk of radius $\frac{q}{1+\sqrt{1-q^2}}$ yields \begin{equation}\label{maxmod}
    |F(b)|\leq \frac{q}{1+\sqrt{1-q^2}}.
\end{equation}
A straightforward computation from the definitions gives
\[
X_b=\sqrt{2(1+b^2)}\,x,
\qquad
Y_b=\sqrt{2(1+b^2)}\,y,
\]
and thus
\[
F(b)=\frac{1+b^2}{2}\langle Ax,y\rangle.
\]
Since
\[
\frac{2}{1+b^2}
=
1+\sqrt{1-|\langle x,y\rangle|^2},
\]
it follows from \eqref{maxmod} that
\[
|\langle Ax,y\rangle|
=
\left(1+\sqrt{1-|\langle x,y\rangle|^2}\right)|F(b)|
\leq
\frac{q}{1+\sqrt{1-q^2}}
\left(1+\sqrt{1-|\langle x,y\rangle|^2}\right).
\]

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:dilation}
Let $A\in\mathcal B(H)$ and let $0<|q|\leq1$. If $\Omega_q(A)\subseteq\overline\mathbb D$, then $A\in C_{\rho_q}$, where $\rho_q$ is given by \eqref{eq:rhoq}.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $\Omega_q(A)=\Omega_{|q|}(A)$, we may assume that $q>0$.
If $q=1$, then $\Omega_1(A)=W(A)$ and the result is the classical
disk theorem of Berger \cite{berger1965strange}. So we assume throughout that $0<q<1$, which means $1\leq\rho_q<2$, and consequently by \eqref{eq:rhocharacterization} it is enough to prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:targetnorm}
\|(2-\rho_q)A+(\rho_q-1)\zeta I\|\leq1,
\qquad \zeta\in\mathbb T.
\end{equation}
Fix $\zeta\in\mathbb T$ and unit vectors $x,y\in H$. The triangle inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangle}
\left|\left\langle\big((2-\rho_q)A+(\rho_q-1)\zeta I\big)x,y\right\rangle\right|
\leq
(2-\rho_q)|\langle Ax,y\rangle|+(\rho_q-1)|\langle x,y\rangle|.
\end{equation}
If $|\langle x,y\rangle|\geq q$, Lemma~\ref{lem:coeff} then gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(2-\rho_q)|\langle Ax,y\rangle|
+(\rho_q-1)|\langle x,y\rangle|
&\leq
(2-\rho_q)|\langle x,y\rangle|
+(\rho_q-1)|\langle x,y\rangle|\\
&=
|\langle x,y\rangle|\\
&\leq 1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Suppose that $|\langle x,y\rangle|\leq q$. Since
$$
\frac{q}{1+\sqrt{1-q^2}}\leq\frac{\rho_q}{2},
$$
Lemma~\ref{lem:coeff} bounds the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:triangle} by
\begin{equation}\label{EQCS}
    \frac{\rho_q(2-\rho_q)}{2}
    \left(1+\sqrt{1-|\langle x,y\rangle|^2}\right)
    +(\rho_q-1)|\langle x,y\rangle|.
\end{equation}
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to the vectors
\[
\left(
\frac{\rho_q(2-\rho_q)}{2},
\,\rho_q-1
\right)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\left(
\sqrt{1-|\langle x,y\rangle|^2},
\,|\langle x,y\rangle|
\right)
\]
shows the expression in \eqref{EQCS} is bounded above by
\[
\frac{\rho_q(2-\rho_q)}{2}
+
\sqrt{
\frac{\rho_q^2(2-\rho_q)^2}{4}
+(\rho_q-1)^2
}.
\]
Then noting $ \rho_q(2-\rho_q)=1-(\rho_q-1)^2$, we have
\[
\sqrt{
\frac{\rho_q^2(2-\rho_q)^2}{4}
+(\rho_q-1)^2
}
=
\frac{1+(\rho_q-1)^2}{2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{\rho_q(2-\rho_q)}{2}
+
\sqrt{
\frac{\rho_q^2(2-\rho_q)^2}{4}
+(\rho_q-1)^2
}
=
\frac{1-(\rho_q-1)^2}{2}
+
\frac{1+(\rho_q-1)^2}{2}
=1.
\]
Hence the expression in \eqref{EQCS} is at most $1$ and therefore
\[
\left|
\left\langle
\big((2-\rho_q)A+(\rho_q-1)\zeta I\big)x,y
\right\rangle
\right|
\leq 1
\]
for all unit vectors $x,y\in H$ so taking the supremum over unit $x$ and $y$ shows \eqref{eq:targetnorm} holds.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:affine}
The relation $\Omega_q(\alpha A+\beta I)=\alpha\Omega_q(A)+\beta$ shows immediately that if $\Omega_q(A)\subseteq c+R\overline\mathbb D$, with $R>0$, then $\frac{A-cI}{R}\in C_{\rho_q}$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:sharpdilation}
The coefficient in Theorem~\ref{thm:dilation} is sharp. For $\frac{4}{5}<q\leq1$, let
\[
A=\frac{2q}{1+\sqrt{1-q^2}}
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A direct $2\times2$ calculation gives
$$
W_q\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\right)=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-q^2}}{2}\overline\mathbb D,$$
and hence $\Omega_q(A)=\overline\mathbb D$ and $\|A\|=\rho_q$. Any unitary $\rho$-dilation implies $\|A\|\leq\rho$ by taking $m=1$ in \eqref{eq:rhodilation}, so no smaller coefficient is possible. For $0<q\leq\frac{4}{5}$, we have $\rho_q=1$, which is already the least value allowed in the classes $C_\rho$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:complete}
Let $A\in\mathcal B(H)$, let $0<|q|\leq1$ and let $\Omega_q(A)\subseteq\overline\mathbb D$. Then the closed unit disk is a complete $\rho_q$-spectral set for $A$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:dilation}, $A\in C_{\rho_q}$ and so by
\cite[Theorem~2]{okubo1975constants}, there exists a bounded invertible
operator $S$ such that
\[
\|S^{-1}AS\|\leq1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\|S\|\|S^{-1}\|\leq\rho_q.
\]
Set $T=S^{-1}AS$ and let $F$ be an $M_m(\mathbb C)$-valued rational function
with poles off $\overline{\mathbb D}$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:similarityF}
F(A)=(I_m\otimes S)F(T)(I_m\otimes S^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Since $\|T\|\leq1$, the complete von Neumann inequality
\cite{paulsen2002completely} gives
\[
\|F(T)\|\leq\sup_{|z|\leq1}\|F(z)\|.
\]
Combining this with \eqref{eq:similarityF} and using
$\|I_m\otimes S\|=\|S\|$ and
$\|I_m\otimes S^{-1}\|=\|S^{-1}\|$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completebound}
\|F(A)\|
\leq
\|S\|\|S^{-1}\|\,\|F(T)\|
\leq
\rho_q\sup_{|z|\leq1}\|F(z)\|.
\end{equation}
Thus $\overline{\mathbb D}$ is a complete $\rho_q$-spectral set for $A$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:circular}
If $\overline{\Omega_q(A)}=c+R\overline\mathbb D$, then Corollary~\ref{cor:complete} shows that the closure of the scaled $q$-numerical range is a complete $\rho_q$-spectral set for $A$. The coefficient is sharp: for $\frac{4}{5}<|q|\leq1$, the $2\times2$ operator in Remark~\ref{rem:sharpdilation}, together with the scalar polynomial $p(z)=z$, forces the constant $\rho_q$, while for $0<|q|\leq\frac{4}{5}$ the constant polynomial $p(z)=1$ shows that no constant below $1$ is possible.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
In finite dimensions, Corollary~\ref{cor:complete} can also be obtained from the similarity results in \cite[Theorem~2.2 and Corollary~2.3]{aouichaouioloughlin2026}, together with \cite[Proposition~9.1]{ahagczyzperalavirtanen2026}.
