<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
There is a rule, depending only on the fixed principalization
procedure, assigning to every characteristic-zero field $k$ and every
smooth proper $k$-variety $X$ of dimension at most four a finite
ordered sequence
\[
 X=X_0 \longleftarrow X_1\longleftarrow\cdots\longleftarrow X_r
\]
with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Each arrow is the blowup of a smooth center of codimension at
least two, and $X_r$ is smooth and projective over $k$.
\item Every composite $X_i\to X$ is an isomorphism over $U(X)$.
\item The rule is natural under isomorphisms, including semilinear ones.
\item If $X$ is projective, the sequence is empty.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:admissible}
The linear system $(L,H)$ is \emph{admissible over $W$} if its base ideal is
the unit ideal on $W$ and the induced morphism
$P\to\mathbb P(H^\vee)$ is a closed immersion.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:existence}
An admissible system exists over every dense quasi-projective open
of a smooth proper variety. The same holds for a finite disjoint
union of such varieties, with an open dense in every component.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The open-preserving Chow lemma gives a projective modification
$\rho:P\to Y$ that is an isomorphism over $W$
\citep[Theorem~3.5]{Rydh}. Take $P$ integral by retaining
the reduced closure of $W$.
Choose a very ample line bundle $A$ on $P$ and set
$L=(\rho_*A)^{\vee\vee}$. The pushforward is torsion-free of
rank one, and regularity of $Y$ makes its reflexive hull an
invertible sheaf. Its global sections contain
$H=H^0(P,A)$. Over $W$ these sections generate $L$.

The graph of the resulting rational map is $P$: the morphism
$(\rho,\iota):P\to Y\times\mathbb P(H^\vee)$ is a closed immersion,
where $\iota$ is the embedding given by $A$, and its image is the
closure of the graph over $W$. The graph of a linear system is
its ordinary base-ideal blowup. This proves admissibility.
For a finite disjoint union, choose a very ample bundle on the
projective disjoint union and use the same argument.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:boundary}
Every maximal quasi-projective open of a smooth proper $k$-variety $Y$
has complement of codimension at least two. Consequently $U(Y)$
contains every codimension-one point of $Y$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For such an open $W$, take an admissible base ideal $\mathcal I$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:existence}. Its invertibility locus is isomorphic to
an open of the projective variety $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}Y$, so is
quasi-projective. It contains $W$ and every codimension-one point,
since the local ring at such a point is a discrete valuation ring.
Maximality identifies this locus with $W$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:sum}
Let $F$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero,
$Y$ a smooth proper $F$-variety, and $B\subsetneq Y$ a closed subset.
Let $\{(L_i,H_i)\}_{i\in I}$ be a nonempty family of admissible
systems over $Y\setminus B$, with base ideals $\mathcal I_i$.
Suppose
\[
 L_i\cdot C=L_j\cdot C
 \quad\text{for every integral curve }C\subset B
 \text{ and all }i,j.
\]
Then the coherent ideal $\mathcal J=\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal I_i$ has projective
ordinary blowup. The assertion also holds componentwise on a
finite disjoint union of smooth proper varieties.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Noetherianness makes $\mathcal J$ a finite subsum, so assume $I$ is finite.
Put $\pi:T=\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal J}Y\to Y$ and
$\mathcal J\mathcal O_T=\mathcal O_T(-D)$. For each $i$ set
\[
 M_i=\pi^*L_i-D,\qquad
 R_i=\mathcal I_i\mathcal O_T\otimes\mathcal O_T(D).
\]
The ideals $R_i$ sum to $\mathcal O_T$. Their unit loci $\Omega_i$ therefore
cover $T$, and all contain $\pi^{-1}(Y\setminus B)$.

Write $q_i:P_i=\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I_i}Y\to Y$ and
$A_i=q_i^*L_i\otimes\mathcal I_i\mathcal O_{P_i}$; $A_i$ is very ample.
Let $Q_i$ be the reduced closure of the common dense open in
$T\times_Y P_i$, with projections
\[
\begin{CD}
 Q_i @>{b_i}>> P_i\\
 @V{a_i}VV @VV{q_i}V\\
 T @>{\pi}>> Y.
\end{CD}
\]
The variety $Q_i$ is projective, since $b_i$ is projective and
$P_i$ is projective. This supplies a projective variety for intersection
calculations before projectivity of $T$ is known.
The morphism $a_i$ is an isomorphism over
$\Omega_i$. Pulling back $\mathcal I_i\subset\mathcal J$ gives an effective Cartier
divisor $\Delta_i$ on $Q_i$, disjoint from $a_i^{-1}(\Omega_i)$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison}
 a_i^*M_i=b_i^*A_i+\Delta_i.
\end{equation}

We first show that every $M_i$ is nef, meaning that it has
nonnegative degree on every integral curve. If $C\subset T$ is
contracted by $\pi$, then $M_i\cdot C>0$ because $-D$ is
$\pi$-ample. Otherwise choose $i$ with the generic point of $C$
in $\Omega_i$. Its strict transform in $Q_i$ is not contracted by
$b_i$, so \eqref{eq:comparison} gives $M_i\cdot C>0$.
If $\pi(C)\subset B$, the hypothesis and the projection formula
give the same degree for every $M_j$. If $\pi(C)\not\subset B$,
the generic point of $C$ belongs to every $\Omega_j$, and the same
argument applies to each index. Thus every $M_j$ has positive
degree on every curve.

It remains to check higher-dimensional subvarieties. Let
$Z\subset T$ be integral of dimension $d>0$, choose $i$ with
its generic point in $\Omega_i$, and let $Z'\subset Q_i$ be its strict
transform. On $Z'$ write
\[
 M'=a_i^*M_i|_{Z'},\quad N'=b_i^*A_i|_{Z'},\quad
 G=\Delta_i|_{Z'},\quad e=\dim b_i(Z').
\]
Here $M',N'$ are nef and $G$ is effective Cartier, with $M'=N'+G$.
For a point $b\in P_i$ above $y\in Y$, the fiber of $b_i$
is a closed subscheme of
$T_y\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(y)}\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(b)$, where $\kappa(-)$
denotes the residue field. On that fiber $a_i^*M_i$ is the restriction
of the ample bundle $-D$, up to a trivial factor.
In particular $M'$ is ample on the generic fiber of
$Z'\to b_i(Z')$.

The projection formula now gives
\[
 (M')^{d-e}(N')^e\cdot Z'>0.
\]
Indeed the pushforward of
$c_1(M')^{d-e}\cap[Z']$ is a positive multiple of $[b_i(Z')]$:
its coefficient is the positive top degree on the generic
fiber, or the degree of the generically finite map if $d=e$.
Intersecting with the ample class $c_1(A_i)^e$ gives the
displayed positivity. This also covers $e=0$.
Effectivity of $G$ and nefness on the projective variety $Z'$
therefore imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed}
 (M')^d\cdot Z'\ \ge\ (M')^{d-1}N'\cdot Z'\ \ge\ \cdots\
 \ge\ (M')^{d-e}(N')^e\cdot Z'\ >0.
\end{equation}
Since $a_i:Z'\to Z$ is birational, this is $M_i^d\cdot Z>0$
\citep[Tag~0BET]{Stacks}.
If $\pi(Z)\subset B$, the boundary-degree hypothesis makes
$a_i^*M_j|_{Z'}$ numerically equivalent to $M'$ for every $j$.
Their top intersections agree on the projective $Z'$, and the
projection formula gives $M_j^d\cdot Z=M_i^d\cdot Z>0$.
Otherwise the generic point of $Z$ belongs to every $\Omega_j$, and each index
can be used from the start.

Nakai--Moishezon on complete schemes makes every $M_j$ ample
\citep{Kleiman}; see also \citet[Theorem~1.3]{FujinoMiyamoto}.
Hence $T$ is projective.
The proof applies separately to the disjoint integral components.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:preparation}
For every smooth proper $k$-variety $X$, there is a finite natural
sequence of smooth blowups $f:Y\to X$, preserving $U(X)$, such
that either $Y$ is projective or some maximal quasi-projective open
$W\subset Y$ contains $f^{-1}U(X)$ and every component of
$Y\setminus W$ has image of codimension at least three in $X$.
The sequence is empty if $X$ is projective.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Set $V=U(X)$. At the beginning of a round, let
$f_i:Y_i\to X$ be the sequence already constructed and put
$V_i=f_i^{-1}V$. Let $\mathcal G_i$ consist of all maximal
quasi-projective opens of $Y_i$ containing $V_i$.
It is finite by Benoist's theorem and nonempty since
$V_i\simeq V$ is quasi-projective.
Stop if $Y_i$ is projective or if some $W\in\mathcal G_i$ satisfies
the required image condition. Otherwise principalize
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preparation-ideal}
 \mathcal J_i=\prod_{W\in\mathcal G_i}\mathcal I_{(Y_i\setminus W)_{\mathrm{red}}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal I_Z$ denotes the defining ideal of a closed subscheme
$Z$. The product uses all members of $\mathcal G_i$, so no open is chosen.
Append the whole principalization sequence and begin the next round.
All these ideals are nonzero and unit on $V_i$.

We prove termination using an auxiliary ideal that is not part of
the rule. By Lemma~\ref{lem:existence}, there is a nonzero ideal
$\mathcal I$ unit on $V$ with projective blowup $P=\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}X$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary}, its support
$\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal O_X/\mathcal I)$ has no divisorial component.
Let $S$ range over its finitely many codimension-two components,
and let $\eta_S$ be the generic point of $S$.
The ring $R_S=\mathcal O_{X,\eta_S}$ is regular local of dimension two.

The localized sequence
$T_{i,S}=Y_i\times_X\operatorname{Spec} R_S$ consists, after identity steps
are omitted, of point blowups on a regular surface. The centers
avoid $V_i$, and $V$ contains every codimension-one point of $X$.
On $\operatorname{Spec} R_S$ their images therefore lie over its closed point
$\eta_S$. A center component meeting $T_{i,S}$ must dominate $S$; its
dimension is at most $\dim X-2=\dim S$, so its generic fiber
over $S$ is a finite reduced set of closed points. Divide
$\mathcal I\mathcal O_{T_{i,S}}$ by its full divisorial factor. The residual
ideal has finite colength at finitely many points. Here the
\emph{colength} of an ideal $J$ in a local ring $R$ is the module
length $\operatorname{length}_R(R/J)$, the number of factors in a composition series
when this length is finite. Let $\delta_i$ be the sum of these local
colengths, over all $S$. It is a nonnegative integer.

The local inequality is as follows. Let $J$ be a nonunit ideal
of finite colength in a regular local ring $R$ of dimension two.
On the blowup of its closed point, let $J'_q$ be the residual
ideals after removing the exceptional factor, and let $R'_q$ be
the local rings at their support points. The surface inequality
of \citet[Tag~0AGT]{Stacks} gives
\[
 \sum_q\operatorname{length}_{R'_q}(R'_q/J'_q)<\operatorname{length}_R(R/J).
\]
Applied to the residual ideals above,
this says that blowing up a residual base point strictly decreases
the total colength. Blowing up elsewhere does not increase it,
and previously invertible factors simply pull back.

Suppose a round is nonterminal. The invertibility locus $O$
of $\mathcal I\mathcal O_{Y_i}$ contains $V_i$. It is quasi-projective:
the blowup of $\mathcal I\mathcal O_{Y_i}$ is an isomorphism over $O$
and maps projectively to $P$, by its closed graph in the
fiber product over $X$. Enlarge $O$ to a maximal
quasi-projective $W_{\mathcal I}\in\mathcal G_i$.
Failure of the stopping condition gives a component
$C$ of $Y_i\setminus W_{\mathcal I}$ with codimension-two image $S$.
There can be no codimension-one image by
Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary}. The image is contained in the support
of $\mathcal O_X/\mathcal I$, so this $S$ belongs to the fixed finite list.
Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary} on $Y_i$ gives
$\dim S\le\dim C\le\dim X-2=\dim S$. Thus the generic
point of $C$ becomes a closed point $p$ of $T_{i,S}$ where
$\mathcal I$ is not invertible.

Each reduced complement occurring in
\eqref{eq:preparation-ideal} is either absent or a reduced
zero-dimensional subscheme after localization at $p$. Its
ideal at $p$ is therefore either $\mathcal O_{T_{i,S},p}$ or
the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_p$. The factor from
$W_{\mathcal I}$ is the latter. Thus
$(\mathcal J_i)_p=\mathfrak m_p^a$ for some $a>0$.
Since $\mathfrak m_p^a$ is not invertible, principalization must
eventually have a nonidentity step whose center meets the fiber over $p$. Before the first such step, all centers are
disjoint from that fiber, so the localized surface and residual ideal
are unchanged near $p$. That first step therefore blows up $p$ while
the residual ideal of $\mathcal I$ is still nonunit, and strictly lowers the
total colength. The other steps cannot increase the total colength, so
$\delta_{i+1}<\delta_i$.
A nonnegative integer cannot decrease through infinitely many
rounds, proving termination.

The ideals and stopping conditions are invariant under
isomorphisms of the input, so the fixed principalization
procedure makes the entire sequence natural. Its centers
avoid the inverse image of $V$ throughout.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:bounds}
The sets $K_B(N)$ are finite, nested, and exhaust $\Gamma_B$.
Their definition is invariant under isomorphisms, including
semilinear isomorphisms of the geometric data.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
In $N^1(Z_j)_{\mathbb R}$ the nef cone is closed and pointed,
and $h_j$ lies in its interior. The closed set of classes
$u$ satisfying $Nh_j\pm u\in\operatorname{Nef}(Z_j)$ is bounded.
Otherwise a normalized unbounded sequence would have a
nonzero limit for which both $u$ and $-u$ are nef,
contradicting pointedness. It is therefore compact.
The product of these compact sets meets the lattice
\eqref{eq:lattice} in finitely many points.
For any fixed class $\gamma$, openness of the ample cone
gives $Nh_j\pm\gamma|_{Z_j}$ ample for all sufficiently
large $N$. This proves exhaustion. Nestedness follows
by adding the nef class $h_j$. All defining conditions
are intrinsic to the indicated data.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:descent}
The ideal $\mathcal K_F$ descends uniquely to a coherent ideal
$\mathcal K\subset\mathcal O_Y$. Principalizing $\mathcal K$ over $k$ gives a
smooth variety projective over $k$, and all its centers
avoid $f^{-1}V$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Each $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(F/k)$ preserves $V_F$ and $A_F$ and
permutes $\mathcal G$. It transports boundary normalizations, the classes
$h_j$, the bounds $K_B(N)$ and admissible systems to the corresponding
data for $\sigma(W)$. Hence $N_{\sigma(W)}=N_W$ and
$S_{\sigma(W)}=\sigma(S_W)$, and it carries $\mathcal J_{W,\gamma}$
to $\mathcal J_{\sigma(W),\sigma(\gamma)}$.
Thus $\sigma$ permutes the factors of \eqref{eq:final-ideal}:
$\mathcal K_F$ is an invariant ideal, not merely an invariant
isomorphism class of sheaves.

Descend its finite presentation to a
finite Galois extension $E/k$ inside $F$, obtaining
$\mathcal K_E$. Invariance descends by faithful flatness.
On an affine open $\operatorname{Spec} R\subset Y$, choose a $k$-basis
$e_1,\ldots,e_t$ of $E$ and its trace-dual basis
$d_1,\ldots,d_t$. For any element
$u=\sum_\ell r_\ell\otimes e_\ell$ of the ideal $\mathcal K_E$,
\[
 \sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/k)}\tau(d_j u)=r_j\otimes1.
\]
The right side therefore belongs to $\mathcal K_E$.
It follows that $\mathcal K_E$ is the extension of its contraction
to $R$. These contractions are coherent and glue.
Faithful flatness also proves uniqueness, and hence
independence of the algebraic closure used in the construction.

Principalize $\mathcal K$ over $k$, with endpoint $Y'\to Y$, and put
$Y'_F=Y'\times_k F$.
After base change to $F$, the pullback of
\eqref{eq:final-ideal} to $Y'_F$ is invertible. In a local domain,
if a product of nonzero ideals is invertible, each factor is invertible: after
dividing the product of the other factors by a generator
of the product, one obtains an inverse fractional ideal.
Since $Y'_F$ is smooth, this applies on every component.
Thus $Y'_F$ maps to each
$\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal J_{W,\gamma}}Y_F$.
The map is projective, by its closed graph in the fiber
product over $Y_F$, because $Y'_F\to Y_F$ is projective.
Its target is projective, so $Y'_F$ is projective.
Existence of an ample line bundle descends from $F$ to $k$
\citep[Tags~0BDC and 0BDE]{Stacks}; properness of $Y'$
then gives projectivity over $k$.
Finally, $\mathcal K$ is unit on $f^{-1}V$, so every
principalization center avoids that open.

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Run Proposition~\ref{prop:preparation}. If the endpoint is
projective, stop. Otherwise the relevant boundary images
have codimension at least three in $X$. Since $\dim X\le4$,
they have dimension at most one, so the construction of
Section~\ref{sec:completion} applies. Lemma~\ref{lem:descent}
gives the projective endpoint over $k$.

The preparation, the numerical selection and the final
principalization are finite. All centers avoid the inverse image of the original
$U(X)$. Since the ideals are generically nonzero, no center
contains an ambient component. After omitting the identity blowups
as agreed above, all centers have codimension at least two.

An isomorphism of inputs transports the preparation, its
ordered exceptional divisors, the finite numerical sets,
and the actual descended ideal. Functorial principalization
then transports every stage. The lifts respect identities and
composition by uniqueness on
the dense unchanged open. For an isomorphism over $k\simeq k'$,
extend the field isomorphism to algebraic closures; invariance and
uniqueness of descent in Lemma~\ref{lem:descent} make the descended
ideal independent of this extension.
The initial stopping condition makes the sequence empty
on projective inputs.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The passage to $F$ is used only to select an ideal, which is
then descended and principalized over $k$. The construction
keeps the pullback of $U(X)$ throughout; it does not
replace it by the core computed over $F$.
In dimension at least five, preparation still terminates,
but boundary images may be surfaces. The degree-one
numerical class used in \eqref{eq:boundary-class} then has
no corresponding construction here. The present argument
does not give projectivization in that dimension range.
