<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
There exist a field $K$, a rank-one nondiscrete valuation domain $A$ of $K$
with maximal ideal $M$, and B\'ezout domains $S_j\subsetneq A$, $j\in\mathbb Z$,
each having quotient field $K$, with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item\label{item:strict} $S_j\subsetneq S_{j+1}$ for every $j\in\mathbb Z$.
 \item\label{item:primecompat} If $Q_j=M\cap S_j$, then $Q_j$ is a nonzero
 prime and, whenever $j\leq\ell$,
 \[
   Q_\ell\cap S_j=Q_j,
   \qquad Q_jS_\ell=Q_\ell.
 \]
 \item\label{item:localization} $(S_0)_{Q_0}=A$.
 \item\label{item:parity} The prime $Q_j$ is divisorial in $S_j$ if and
 only if $j$ is even.
 \item\label{item:limits} The rings
 \[
   S^+=\bigcup_{j\geq0}S_j,
   \qquad S^-=\bigcap_{j\leq0}S_j
 \]
 are B\'ezout domains with quotient field $K$, and $S^+\subsetneq A$.
 Moreover,
 \[
   M\cap S^+=Q_0S^+,
   \qquad M\cap S^-=Q_0\cap S^-,
 \]
 and both displayed primes are nondivisorial in their respective rings.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:limit-alternatives}
For both the ascending and descending questions, there are chains with a
divisorial limit prime and chains with a nondivisorial limit prime.  In the
ascending case, the opposite limit behaviors may occur with the same initial
pair $(R,P)$ and the same localization $R_P$.  Every finite stage may be
chosen to be B\'ezout.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:intersection}
Let $k$ be an infinite field of cardinality $\kappa$, let $L/k$ be a field
extension, and let $(V_i)_{i\in I}$ be valuation rings of $L$, trivial on
$k$, with $|I|<\kappa$.  Then
\[
 D=\bigcap_{i\in I}V_i
\]
is a B\'ezout domain with quotient field $L$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The assertion is immediate if the family is empty, so assume otherwise and
write $v_i$ for the valuation belonging to $V_i$.  Fix $z\in L^\times$.
For a fixed $i$ with $v_i(z)=0$, there is at most one $c\in k$ for which
$v_i(c+z)>0$: triviality on $k$ makes the residue map
$k\longrightarrow\kappa(v_i)$ injective, so two distinct constants cannot
cancel the residue of $z$.  Fewer than $\kappa$ constants are therefore
excluded by all the valuations.  After also excluding $0$ and, if $z\in k$,
$-z$, choose $c\in k^\times$ such that $c+z\neq0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:avoid-zero}
 v_i(c+z)=0\quad\text{whenever }v_i(z)=0.
\end{equation}
If $v_i(z)\neq0$, the two summands $c$ and $z$ have distinct values, because
$v_i(c)=0$; hence the ultrametric inequality is an equality.  Together with
\cref{eq:avoid-zero}, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:min-value}
 v_i(c+z)=\min\{0,v_i(z)\}\qquad(i\in I).
\end{equation}
It follows that
\[
 \frac1{c+z},\qquad\frac z{c+z}
\]
belong to every $V_i$, and therefore to $D$.  Their quotient is $z$, so
$\operatorname{qf}(D)=L$.

Now let $0\neq a,b\in D$, put $z=b/a$, choose $c$ as above, and set
$d=ca+b=a(c+z)$.  For every $i$, \cref{eq:min-value} yields
\[
 v_i(d)=v_i(a)+\min\{0,v_i(b)-v_i(a)\}
       =\min\{v_i(a),v_i(b)\}.
\]
Thus $a/d,b/d\in D$, so $a,b\in dD$.  Conversely,
$d=ca+b\in aD+bD$.  Hence $aD+bD=dD$.  Induction shows that every
finitely generated ideal is principal, and $D$ is B\'ezout.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:prime-extension}
Let $D$ be a Pr\"ufer domain and
$D\subseteq E\subseteq\operatorname{qf}(D)$.  If $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(E)$ and
$\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap D$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-extension}
 \mathfrak q=\mathfrak pE.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We first recall why every overring of a Pr\"ufer domain is flat.  Let $V$ be
a valuation domain and let $V\subseteq C\subseteq\operatorname{qf}(V)$.  The intermediate
ring $C$ is itself a valuation domain: for $0\neq x\in\operatorname{qf}(V)$, either
$x\in V\subseteq C$ or $x^{-1}\in V\subseteq C$.  Let $\mathfrak n$ be the
maximal ideal of $C$ and put $\mathfrak r=\mathfrak n\cap V$.  Then
$\mathfrak r$ is prime.  Every element of $V\setminus\mathfrak r$ is a unit
of $C$, so $V_{\mathfrak r}\subseteq C$.  Conversely, if
$x\in C\setminus V$, then $x^{-1}\in V$; because $x$ is its inverse in $C$,
$x^{-1}\notin\mathfrak n$, and hence $x^{-1}\notin\mathfrak r$.  Therefore
$x\in V_{\mathfrak r}$, proving $C=V_{\mathfrak r}$.  Thus every overring of
a valuation domain is flat.  For each maximal ideal $N$ of $D$, the ring
$D_N$ is a valuation domain and $E_{D\setminus N}$ is an overring of $D_N$.
Flatness is local, so $E$ is flat over $D$.

Set $S=D\setminus\mathfrak p$ and $V=D_{\mathfrak p}$.  By the preceding
paragraph, $S^{-1}E=V_{\mathfrak r}$ for some prime
$\mathfrak r\subseteq\mathfrak pV$.  The prime
$S^{-1}\mathfrak q$ survives and its contraction to $V$ is
$\mathfrak pV$.  Every element of $V\setminus\mathfrak pV$ is already
inverted in $S^{-1}E$, so $\mathfrak r\subseteq\mathfrak pV$; on the other
hand, the contraction to $V$ of any prime of $V_{\mathfrak r}$ is contained
in $\mathfrak r$.  Hence $\mathfrak pV\subseteq\mathfrak r$, and consequently
\[
 \mathfrak r=\mathfrak pV,\qquad S^{-1}E=V,
 \qquad S^{-1}\mathfrak q=\mathfrak pV.
\]
Moreover,
$S^{-1}(\mathfrak pE)=\mathfrak p(S^{-1}E)=\mathfrak pV$.
If $x\in\mathfrak q$, there is therefore an $s\in S$ such that
$sx\in\mathfrak pE$.  Since $E$ is flat over $D$, the
$D/\mathfrak p$-module $E/\mathfrak pE$ is flat and hence torsion-free.
The class of $s$ in the domain $D/\mathfrak p$ is nonzero, so
$s(x+\mathfrak pE)=0$ forces $x\in\mathfrak pE$.  This proves
$\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak pE$; the reverse inclusion follows from
$\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:limiting}
For every $i\in\Lambda$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limiting-inclusion}
 A\cap\bigcap_{m\geq1}W_{i,m}\subseteq B_i.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Fix $0\neq z\in K$.  Choose $N\geq1$ such that $z\in F(Z)$, where
$Z=T^{1/N}$, and write
\[
 z=\frac{f(Z)}{g(Z)},\qquad
 f(Z)=\sum_{\ell=r_0}^{r_1}a_\ell Z^\ell,
 \qquad g(Z)=\sum_{\ell=s_0}^{s_1}b_\ell Z^\ell,
\]
with $a_{r_0}b_{s_0}\neq0$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v-ac-polynomials}
 v(z)=\frac{r_0-s_0}{N},
 \qquad \operatorname{ac}(z)=\frac{a_{r_0}}{b_{s_0}}.
\end{equation}
For every $\ell>r_0$ with $a_\ell\neq0$,
\begin{align*}
 &u_i\bigl(a_\ell X_i^{m\ell/N}\bigr)
   -u_i\bigl(a_{r_0}X_i^{mr_0/N}\bigr)\\
 &\hspace{4em}=u_i(a_\ell)-u_i(a_{r_0})
       +\frac{m(\ell-r_0)}N,
\end{align*}
which is positive for all sufficiently large $m$.  Hence the $r_0$-term is
the unique term of least $u_i$-value in $f(X_i^{m/N})$ for all large $m$;
in particular, this evaluation is nonzero.  The same argument applies to
$g$ and its $s_0$-term.  Therefore $z$ has neither a zero nor a pole at
$T=X_i^m$ for all sufficiently large $m$, and
\begin{align}
 u_i\bigl(z(X_i^m)\bigr)
 &=u_i(a_{r_0})+\frac{mr_0}N
   -u_i(b_{s_0})-\frac{ms_0}N \notag\\
 &=m v(z)+u_i(\operatorname{ac}(z)).\label{eq:asymptotic-value}
\end{align}

Now suppose $z$ belongs to the left side of
\cref{eq:limiting-inclusion}.  If $v(z)>0$, then $z\in B_i$ by
\cref{eq:B-i}.  If $v(z)=0$, membership in $W_{i,m}$, together with
\cref{eq:ordinary-specialization,eq:asymptotic-value} for large $m$, gives
$u_i(\operatorname{ac}(z))\geq0$.  Again $z\in B_i$ by \cref{eq:B-i}.  The case $z=0$ is
automatic.

$v(xT^e)=v(x)+e<0$.  Thus $xQ\nsubseteq A$, proving
\cref{eq:A-colon-odd}.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:even}
For every $n\in\mathbb Z$, the prime $Q_{2n}=M\cap E_n$ is divisorial in $E_n$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->

\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-colon-Q}
 (B_n:Q)=A.

$v(xq)>0$ and $xq\in B_n$ by \cref{eq:B-i}.  Hence

$e\in\mathbb Q$ with $0<e<-v(x)$.  Then $T^e\in Q$ by
\cref{eq:T-e-universal}, but $v(xT^e)<0$, so
$xT^e\notin B_n$.  This proves the reverse inclusion in

$p_{i,m}\in Q$ gives $(W_{i,m}:Q)=W_{i,m}$.  Therefore

$v(qh)>0$ and therefore $qh\in B_n$.  Thus $QH_n\subseteq E_n$, proving

Thus $Q_{2n}$ is divisorial.

\label{prop:filtered-chain}
The sequence

 D_0\supsetneq D_1\supsetneq D_2\supsetneq\cdots
\]
is a strictly descending chain of B\'ezout domains with common quotient
field $K_0$.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtered-parity}
 \mathfrak p_m\text{ is divisorial in }D_m
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\text{ is even}.
\end{equation}

The inclusions follow from \cref{eq:discrete-nesting}, which gives
$S^0_{-(m+1)}\subseteq S^0_{-m}$, and from
$G_{m+1}\subseteq G_m$.  We first prove they are strict.  Suppose $m$ is
even and write $-m=2n$.  Choose $c\in k_0^\times$ with
$t=c\notin\mathcal C_{m+1}$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtered-even-witness}
 y=\frac{tX_n^{-2}}{t-c}.
\end{equation}
The numerator is the witness in \cref{eq:discrete-strict-witness} for
$E_n^0\setminus H_{n-1}^0$.  The denominator has value zero, with
second value zero, at every special valuation $W^0_{i,s}$.  Consequently
$y\in E_n^0=S^0_{-m}$, but $y\notin H_{n-1}^0=S^0_{-(m+1)}$; explicitly,
its second value at $W^0_{n,1}$ is $-1$.  As a rational function of $t$,
$y$ has its only pole at $t=c$ and is regular at infinity because its
numerator and denominator have the same degree.  Hence
$y\in G_{m+1}\subseteq G_m$.  It follows that
$y\in D_m\setminus D_{m+1}$.

If $m$ is odd, write $-m=2n+1$.  The constant rational function
$X_n^{-1}$ has no geometric pole and belongs to
$H_n^0\setminus E_n^0$.  It therefore lies in
$D_m\setminus D_{m+1}$.  This proves strictness in both cases.

Fix $m$ and choose $c\in k_0^\times$ with
$t=c\notin\mathcal C_m$.  The element
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geometric-unit}
 q_c=\frac{t}{t-c}
\end{equation}
has $a$-value $1$.  At $W^0_{i,s}$ its first value is zero and its second
value is
\[
 u_i\left(\frac{X_i^s}{X_i^s-c}\right)=s.
\]
Its divisor on $\mathbf P^1_{F_0}$ consists of a zero at $0$ and a pole at
$c$, and it is a unit at infinity.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qc-properties}
 q_c\in\mathfrak p_m,
 \qquad q_c\in\mathcal O_\xi^\times
       \quad(\xi\in\mathcal C_m).
\end{equation}
In particular, every $\mathfrak p_m$ is nonzero.

\cref{eq:discrete-test-element} by putting
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regularized-test}
 r_{i,s,c}=\frac{p^0_{i,s}}{(t-c)^s}.
\end{equation}
The polynomial $p^0_{i,s}$ has degree $s$, so $r_{i,s,c}$ is regular at
infinity and its only possible pole is $t=c$.  It therefore belongs to
$G_m$.  The denominator $t-c$ is a unit, of second value zero, at every
$W^0_{j,h}$, so \cref{eq:discrete-test-properties} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regularized-properties}
 r_{i,s,c}\in M_0\cap D_m
 \quad\text{whenever }W^0_{i,s}\text{ occurs in }S^0_{-m},
 \qquad
 r_{i,s,c}\in(W^0_{i,s})^\times.
\end{equation}
The first assertion follows because $p^0_{i,s}$ belongs to every special
valuation, and its positive $a$-value puts it in either $A_0$ or $B_n^0$.

Suppose first that $m$ is odd, so $S^0_{-m}=H_n^0$ for some $n$.  Put
$\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_m$.  Since all elements of $\mathfrak p$ have
positive integral $a$-value, while $q_c\in\mathfrak p$ has value $1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtered-A-colon}
 (A_0:\mathfrak p)=t^{-1}A_0.
\end{equation}
For every special valuation in $H_n^0$, the appropriate element
$r_{i,s,c}$ is a $\mathfrak p$-unit, so
$(W^0_{i,s}:\mathfrak p)=W^0_{i,s}$.  For every
$\xi\in\mathcal C_m$, the element $q_c$ is a $\mathfrak p$-unit, so
$(\mathcal O_\xi:\mathfrak p)=\mathcal O_\xi$.  Taking the colon through
the defining intersection for $D_m$ gives
\[
 (D_m:\mathfrak p)=t^{-1}A_0\cap
   \bigcap_{\substack{i>n\\s\geq1}}W^0_{i,s}
   \cap G_m.
\]

applies and gives
\[
 u_i\bigl(x(X_i^s)\bigr)=-s+u_i(\operatorname{ac}_0(x))<0.
\]

this strict inequality says $x\notin W^0_{i,s}$, contradicting the
displayed intersection.  Thus every element in it has nonnegative
$a$-value.  Its remaining special and geometric conditions are exactly
those defining $D_m$, so the displayed intersection is $D_m$.  Therefore
\[
 \mathfrak p^{v_{D_m}}=(D_m:(D_m:\mathfrak p))=D_m\neq\mathfrak p,
\]
and the odd prime is nondivisorial.

Suppose now that $m$ is even, so $S^0_{-m}=E_n^0$.  Again write
$\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_m$.  We claim
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtered-B-colon}
 (B_n^0:\mathfrak p)=A_0.
\end{equation}

$A_0\subseteq(B_n^0:\mathfrak p)$.  Since the $t$-adic valuation $a$ is

$E_n^0$, \cref{eq:discrete-test-properties} says that $p^0_{n,s}$ belongs
to every retained special valuation, and the denominator is a unit there.

$u_n$-unit.  Consequently
\[

 \qquad

   =u_n(\operatorname{ac}_0(x))-s<0,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtered-even-first-colon}
 (D_m:\mathfrak p)=L_m,
 \qquad
 L_m:=H_n^0\cap G_m.
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtered-even-conductor}
 (D_m:L_m)=\mathfrak p.
\end{equation}

in $B_n^0$.  Conversely, suppose $xL_m\subseteq D_m$.  Since $1\in L_m$,

\[
 \mathfrak p^{v_{D_m}}
  =(D_m:(D_m:\mathfrak p))=(D_m:L_m)=\mathfrak p.

The parity assertion follows because $m$ and $-m$ have the same parity.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:outer-pullback}
Let $K$ be a field, let $V$ be a rank-one valuation domain with quotient
field $L$, nondiscrete value group, maximal ideal $N$, residue field $K$,
and residue map $\varphi:V\to K$.  Suppose that $D$ is a Pr\"ufer subdomain
of $K$ with quotient field $K$, and put
\[
 R=\varphi^{-1}(D).
\]
Then $R$ is a Pr\"ufer domain with quotient field $L$; if $D$ is B\'ezout,
then $R$ is B\'ezout.  If
$0\neq\mathfrak a$ is an ideal of $D$ and
$I=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak a)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullback-v-transfer}
 I^{v_R}=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak a^{v_D}).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Write $w$ for the valuation belonging to $V$.
We first prove the Pr\"ufer assertion by examining the localizations of
$R$.  A maximal ideal of $R$ which contains $N$ has the form
$\mathfrak q=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak m)$ for a maximal ideal
$\mathfrak m$ of $D$.  We claim
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullback-localization}
 R_{\mathfrak q}=\varphi^{-1}(D_{\mathfrak m}).
\end{equation}
The inclusion from left to right is clear.  Conversely, let $x$ belong to
the right side.  If its residue is $d/s$ with $d\in D$ and
$s\in D\setminus\mathfrak m$, choose a lift $\widetilde s\in R$ with
residue $s$.  Then $\widetilde s x$ has residue $d$, so
$\widetilde s x\in R$, while $\widetilde s\notin\mathfrak q$.  Hence
$x\in R_{\mathfrak q}$.  Since
$D_{\mathfrak m}$ is a valuation domain, its inverse image on the right of
\cref{eq:pullback-localization} is the composite valuation domain: for an
element of $L^\times$ of outer value zero, membership is decided by the
residue valuation, and elements of positive or negative outer value are
decided by $V$.

If a maximal ideal $\mathfrak q$ of $R$ does not contain $N$, choose
$y\in N\setminus\mathfrak q$.  For every $x\in V$, one has
$yx\in N\subseteq R$, and hence $x=(yx)/y\in R_{\mathfrak q}$.  Thus
$R_{\mathfrak q}$ is an overring of the valuation domain $V$ and is itself
a valuation domain.  Every localization of $R$ at a maximal ideal is
therefore a valuation domain, so $R$ is Pr\"ufer.

Choose $0\neq y\in N$.  For every $x\in V$, both $y$ and $yx$ lie in $R$,
so $x\in\operatorname{qf}(R)$.  It follows that $V\subseteq\operatorname{qf}(R)$ and hence
$\operatorname{qf}(R)=\operatorname{qf}(V)=L$.

Suppose now that $D$ is B\'ezout, and take $0\neq x,y\in R$.  If
$w(x)<w(y)$, then $y/x\in N\subseteq R$, so $xR+yR=xR$; the case
$w(y)<w(x)$ is symmetric.  Assume $w(x)=w(y)=\alpha$.  Choose
$z\in L^\times$ with $w(z)=\alpha$ and set
\[
 a=\varphi(x/z),\qquad b=\varphi(y/z)\in K^\times.
\]
When $\alpha=0$ we take $z=1$, and then $a,b\in D$.  In all cases the
two-generated fractional ideal $aD+bD$ is principal.  Indeed, choose
$0\neq h\in D$ with $ha,hb\in D$; the integral ideal
$haD+hbD$ is $\eta D$ for some $\eta\in D$, and hence
$aD+bD=(\eta/h)D$.  Write
$aD+bD=\delta D$ with $\delta\in K^\times$.  Since
$\delta\in aD+bD$, choose $r,s\in D$ such that $\delta=ra+sb$, choose
lifts $\widetilde r,\widetilde s\in R$ of $r,s$, and put
$d=\widetilde r x+\widetilde s y$.  The residue of $d/z$
is the nonzero element $\delta$, so $w(d)=\alpha$.  Moreover, $x/d$ and
$y/d$ have outer value zero and residues $a/\delta,b/\delta\in D$.
Therefore $x/d,y/d\in R$, while $d\in xR+yR$, and hence
$xR+yR=dR$.  Induction shows that every finitely generated ideal of $R$ is
principal.  Thus $R$ is B\'ezout.

It remains to compute the two colons.  Because the value group is
nondiscrete, it has positive elements arbitrarily close to zero.  If
$x\notin V$, choose $y\in N$ with
$0<w(y)<-w(x)$.  Since
$N\subseteq I$, the element $y$ belongs to $I$, but $xy\notin V$ and hence
$xy\notin R$.  Thus $(R:I)\subseteq V$.  For $x\in V$, reducing products
modulo $N$ gives
\[
 xI\subseteq R
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \varphi(x)\mathfrak a\subseteq D.
\]
Here the implication also covers $x\in N$, whose residue is zero.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullback-first-colon}
 (R:I)=\varphi^{-1}((D:\mathfrak a)).
\end{equation}
Put $J=(R:I)=\varphi^{-1}((D:\mathfrak a))$; in particular,
$N\subseteq J$.  If $x\notin V$, nondiscreteness again supplies
$y\in N$ with $0<w(y)<-w(x)$.  Then $y\in J$ but $xy\notin V$, so
$x\notin(R:J)$.  Hence $(R:J)\subseteq V$.  For $x\in V$, reduction
modulo $N$ gives
\[
 xJ\subseteq R
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \varphi(x)(D:\mathfrak a)\subseteq D.
\]
Consequently
\[
 (R:(R:I))=(R:J)
  =\varphi^{-1}((D:(D:\mathfrak a)))
  =\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak a^{v_D}),
\]
which is \cref{eq:pullback-v-transfer}.

<!-- proposition-13: body_md -->
\label{prop:divisorial-negative-limit}
The rings in \cref{eq:outer-chain} form a strictly descending chain
\[
 R_0\supsetneq R_1\supsetneq R_2\supsetneq\cdots
\]
of B\'ezout domains with common quotient field $L$.  The ideal $P$ is a
nonzero prime of $R_0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outer-parity}
 P\cap R_m\text{ is divisorial in }R_m
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\text{ is even}.
\end{equation}
If
\[
 R_\infty=\bigcap_{m\geq0}R_m,
\]
then $P\cap R_\infty=N$, and this is a nonzero divisorial prime of
$R_\infty$.
<!-- end proposition-13 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Each $D_m$ is B\'ezout with quotient field $K_0$, so
\cref{lem:outer-pullback} makes $R_m$ B\'ezout (and hence Pr\"ufer) with
quotient field $L$.
Taking inverse images preserves the inclusions, and strictness is preserved
as well: if $d\in D_m\setminus D_{m+1}$, its constant lift in $V$ belongs
to $R_m\setminus R_{m+1}$.  Thus the chain is strictly descending.

The ideal $\mathfrak p_0=M_0\cap D_0$ is a nonzero prime, so its inverse
image $P$ is a nonzero prime of $R_0$.  Since $D_m\subseteq D_0$,
\begin{align}
 P\cap R_m
 &=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p_0\cap D_m)\notag\\
 &=\varphi^{-1}(M_0\cap D_m)
  =\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p_m).
  \label{eq:outer-contraction}
\end{align}
Every $\mathfrak p_m$ is nonzero by \cref{eq:qc-properties}.  Therefore
\cref{lem:outer-pullback,eq:filtered-parity} give
\[
 (P\cap R_m)^{v_{R_m}}
  =\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p_m^{v_{D_m}}),
\]
and this equals $P\cap R_m$ exactly for even $m$.  This proves
\cref{eq:outer-parity}.

Inverse images commute with arbitrary intersections, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outer-limit-ring}
 R_\infty=\varphi^{-1}(D_\infty).
\end{equation}
Combining \cref{eq:base-prime-zero,eq:outer-contraction} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outer-limit-prime}
 P\cap R_\infty
 =\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p_0\cap D_\infty)
 =\varphi^{-1}(0)=N.
\end{equation}
The ring $R_\infty$ has quotient field $L$: if $0\neq y\in N$, then for
every $x\in V$, both $y$ and $yx$ lie in $N\subseteq R_\infty$, so
$V\subseteq\operatorname{qf}(R_\infty)$ and hence $L=\operatorname{qf}(V)\subseteq\operatorname{qf}(R_\infty)$.
Conversely, $\operatorname{qf}(R_\infty)\subseteq L$ because
$R_\infty\subseteq V\subseteq L$.  Thus $\operatorname{qf}(R_\infty)=L$.

We finish by computing the two colons in this quotient field.  Since $N$ is
an ideal of $V$,
\[
 V\subseteq(R_\infty:N).
\]
If $x\notin V$, choose $e\in\mathbb Q$ with $0<e<-w(x)$.  Then
$Z^e\in N$, but $xZ^e\notin V$ and hence $xZ^e\notin R_\infty$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limit-N-first-colon}
 (R_\infty:N)=V.
\end{equation}
Also $NV=N\subseteq R_\infty$, so
$N\subseteq(R_\infty:V)$.  Conversely, suppose $xV\subseteq R_\infty$.
Since $1\in V$, one has $x\in R_\infty\subseteq V$.  If $x\notin N$, its
residue is a nonzero element $b\in D_\infty\subseteq F_0$ by
\cref{lem:base-prime-vanishes}.  The constant lift of the element
$t\in K_0$ is a unit of $V$, but the residue of $xt$ is $bt\notin F_0$;
in particular, it is not in $D_\infty$.  Hence $xt\notin R_\infty$, a
contradiction.  We have proved
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limit-N-second-colon}
 (R_\infty:V)=N.
\end{equation}
Combining \cref{eq:limit-N-first-colon,eq:limit-N-second-colon},
\[
 N^{v_{R_\infty}}
  =(R_\infty:(R_\infty:N))=(R_\infty:V)=N.
\]
Thus the limit prime is nonzero and divisorial.
