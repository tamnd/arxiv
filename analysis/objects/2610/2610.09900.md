<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:symmetric-as}
For \(d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), a symmetric association scheme of class \(d\) on \(X\) consists of nonzero \(0,1\)-matrices \(B_0,\ldots,B_d\) indexed by \(X\) and satisfying
\[
\begin{gathered}
B_0=I,\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^dB_i=J,\qquad
B_i^T=B_i,\\
B_iB_j\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{B_0,\ldots,B_d\}
\quad(0\leq i,j\leq d).
\end{gathered}
\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:classical-distributions}
For \(\varnothing\ne C\subseteq X\), let \(\chi_C\in\mathbb{C}^X\) be its characteristic vector. Its inner distribution \(a=(a_i)_{i=0}^d\) and dual distribution \(b=(b_j)_{j=0}^d\) are
\[
a_i:=\frac1{|C|}\chi_C^\dagger B_i\chi_C,
\qquad
b_j:=\frac v{|C|}\chi_C^\dagger E_j\chi_C
\qquad(0\le i,j\le d).
\]
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:classical-distributions}
For every nonempty \(C\subseteq X\), its distributions satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_0&=1,& \sum_{i=0}^da_i&=|C|,\\
b_0&=|C|,& \sum_{j=0}^db_j&=v,
\end{aligned}
\]
the MacWilliams identity and its inverse,
\[
b_j=\sum_{i=0}^dQ_{ij}a_i,\qquad
a_i=\frac1v\sum_{j=0}^dP_{ji}b_j,
\]
and the nonnegativity conditions
\[
a_i\geq0\quad(0\leq i\leq d),\qquad
b_j\geq0\quad(0\leq j\leq d).
\]
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The characteristic vector satisfies \(\chi_C^\dagger\chi_C=|C|\) and \(\chi_C^\dagger J\chi_C=|C|^2\). Applying \(B_0=I\), \(\sum_iB_i=J\), \(E_0=v^{-1}J\), and \(\sum_jE_j=I\) in the distribution formulas gives the four normalizations.
Substituting \eqref{eq:classical-eigenmatrices} into the distribution formulas gives the MacWilliams identity and its inverse~\cite[Section~2]{MartinTanaka2009}.
The inner distribution is nonnegative because the \(B_i\) have nonnegative entries; the dual distribution is nonnegative because \(E_j\succeq0\).
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:classical-code-design}
Let
\[
0\in\Delta\subseteq\{0,\ldots,d\},
\qquad T\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}.
\]
A nonempty subset \(C\subseteq X\) is a \(\Delta\)-code or a \(T\)-design if, respectively,
\[
a_i=0\quad(i\notin\Delta),
\qquad
b_j=0\quad(j\in T).
\]
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
A finite quantum space is a pair \((A,h_A)\), where \(A\ne\{0\}\) is a finite-dimensional unital \(C^\ast\)-algebra and
\[
A\cong\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^rM_{n_\alpha}(\mathbb{C}),
\qquad
h_A((x_\alpha)_{\alpha=1}^r)
:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^rn_\alpha\operatorname{Tr}(x_\alpha).
\]
Here \(r,n_\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\), and \(\operatorname{Tr}\) is the usual matrix trace.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:qas}
A quantum coherent algebra on \(A\) is a complex linear subspace \(W\subseteq\operatorname{End}(A)\) such that
\[
I,J\in W,\qquad
ST,\ S\bullet T,\ S^\dagger,\ S^\ast\in W
\qquad(S,T\in W).
\]
It is a quantum association scheme (QAS) if
\[
\left\{
p\in W:
p\bullet p=p=p^\ast,\ 
p\bullet I=I\bullet p=p
\right\}=\{0,I\}.
\]
A QAS is commutative or cocommutative, respectively, if
\[
ST=TS\quad(S,T\in W),
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
S\bullet T=T\bullet S\quad(S,T\in W).
\]
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{def:effective-size}
For \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\), its effective size is
\[
\operatorname{esize}(\chi):=\frac{h_A(\chi)^2}{h_A(\chi^2)}.
\]
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def:inner-distribution}\label{def:dual-distribution}
For \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\), its inner distribution \(A^\chi=(A_a^\chi)_{a\in\mathcal C}\) and dual distribution \(B^\chi=(B_i^\chi)_{i\in\mathcal S}\) are defined by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner-distribution}
A_a^\chi:V_a\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad
A_a^\chi(x):=\frac{\langle\chi,x(\chi)\rangle}{h_A(\chi^2)}
\qquad(x\in V_a)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-distribution}
B_i^\chi:U_i\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad
B_i^\chi(u):=\frac{N\langle\chi,u(\chi)\rangle}{h_A(\chi^2)}
\qquad(u\in U_i).
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:distribution-normalization}
Multiplying \(\chi\) by a positive scalar leaves its effective size and distributions unchanged.
The distributions satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_0^\chi(I)&=1,&
\sum_{a\in\mathcal C}A_a^\chi(z_a)&=A^\chi(J)=\operatorname{esize}(\chi),\\
B_0^\chi(E_0)&=\operatorname{esize}(\chi),&
\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}B_i^\chi(e_i)&=N,
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
A_a^\chi(x^\ast)=\overline{A_a^\chi(x)}
\quad(x\in V_a),\qquad
B_i^\chi(u^\dagger)=\overline{B_i^\chi(u)}
\quad(u\in U_i).
\]
Every nonzero projection \(p\in A\) has effective size \(\operatorname{esize}(p)=h_A(p)\).
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since \(\chi\ne0\), the denominator \(h_A(\chi^2)=\langle\chi,\chi\rangle\) is positive.
The numerators and denominator all scale by the square of the positive scalar, proving invariance under rescaling.
The normalization at \(I\) follows from \(I(\chi)=\chi\), while \(J(\chi)=h_A(\chi)1_A\) and \(E_0=N^{-1}J\) give the two values equal to \(\operatorname{esize}(\chi)\).
The sums follow by linearity from \(\sum_a z_a=J\) and \(\sum_i e_i=I\).
For the involution \(\ast\), traciality and \(\chi=\chi^\ast\) give
\[
\overline{\langle\chi,x(\chi)\rangle}
=h_A\bigl(x(\chi)^\ast\chi\bigr)
=h_A\bigl(\chi x(\chi)^\ast\bigr)
=\langle\chi,x^\ast(\chi)\rangle.
\]
The identity for \(B_i^\chi\) follows from the defining property of the Hilbert-space adjoint \(\dagger\).
Finally, \(p^2=p\) gives \(h_A(p^2)=h_A(p)\), proving the projection formula.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:macwilliams}
The maps \(Q'\) and \(N^{-1}P'\) are inverse complex linear maps.
For every \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-macwilliams}
B^\chi=Q'A^\chi,\qquad
A^\chi=N^{-1}P'B^\chi.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Dualizing \eqref{eq:inverse-block-maps} gives \(Q'P'=N\operatorname{id}\) and \(P'Q'=N\operatorname{id}\).
To identify the dual distribution, let \(u\in U_i\).
Since \(Q_{ai}(u)=Nz_a\bullet u\) and \(\sum_a z_a=J\),
\[
(Q'A^\chi)_i(u)=NA^\chi(u)
=\frac{N\langle\chi,u(\chi)\rangle}{h_A(\chi^2)}
=B_i^\chi(u).
\]
Applying the inverse map \(N^{-1}P'\) gives the second identity.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{def:qas-code}\label{def:qas-design}
Let \(0\in\Delta\subseteq\mathcal C\) and \(T\subseteq\mathcal S\setminus\{0\}\).
An element \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\) is a \(\Delta\)-code if
\[
A_a^\chi=0\qquad(a\notin\Delta),
\]
and a \(T\)-design if
\[
B_i^\chi=0\qquad(i\in T).
\]
<!-- end definition-8 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:spectral-vanishing}
For \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\) and \(i\in\mathcal S\),
\[
B_i^\chi=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e_i(\chi)=0.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
If \(e_i(\chi)=0\), then every \(u\in U_i\) annihilates \(\chi\), since \(u=u\circ e_i\).
This proves \(B_i^\chi=0\).
For the converse, it suffices to evaluate the functional at the orthogonal projection \(e_i\in U_i\):
\[
B_i^\chi(e_i)=\frac{N}{h_A(\chi^2)}\langle\chi,e_i(\chi)\rangle
=\frac{N}{h_A(\chi^2)}\|e_i(\chi)\|^2.
\]
Thus \(B_i^\chi=0\) implies \(e_i(\chi)=0\).
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:distribution-positivity}
The sets \(R_a^+\) and \(K_i^+\) are closed convex cones in the respective self-adjoint real subspaces, and \(K_i^+\) consists of the positive operators in \(U_i\).
For every \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\),
\[
A_a^\chi(x)\geq0\quad(x\in R_a^+),\qquad
B_i^\chi(u)\geq0\quad(u\in K_i^+).
\]
Every pair satisfying \eqref{eq:conic-feasibility} has equal nonnegative objectives,
\[
\mathsf B_0(E_0)=N\mathsf A(E_0)=\mathsf A(J)\geq0,
\]
and satisfies \(\mathsf A(w)\geq0\) for every \(w\in W\) that is positive as an operator on \(A\).
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
In their self-adjoint real subspaces, the cones are intersections of closed homogeneous half-spaces.
The quadratic evaluations defining \(R_a^+\) are real by traciality, as in the proof of Proposition~\ref{lem:distribution-normalization}.
The description of \(K_i^+\) is its defining quadratic-form condition.
Applying the defining inequalities to \(\chi\) in \eqref{eq:inner-distribution} and \eqref{eq:dual-distribution} and using \(h_A(\chi^2)>0\) gives nonnegativity of the distributions.
For a feasible pair, \(\mathsf B=Q'\mathsf A\) and \(E_0=N^{-1}J\) give the objective identities.
Their common value is nonnegative because \(E_0\in K_0^+\).
It remains to show positivity of \(\mathsf A\) on positive operators.
If \(w\in W\) is positive on \(A\), each central orthogonal projection \(e_i\) commutes with \(w\), so \(e_i\circ w\in K_i^+\).
The inverse MacWilliams relation therefore gives
\[
\mathsf A(w)=\frac1N\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}\mathsf B_i(e_i\circ w)\geq0.
\]
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
For \(0\in\Delta\subseteq\mathcal C\), let \(\operatorname{Dels}_{\mathrm{code}}(W,\Delta)\) be the supremum of
\begin{equation}\label{def:code-conic-bound}
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{maximize} & \mathsf A(J),\\
\text{subject to} & \text{the conditions in }\eqref{eq:conic-feasibility},\\
& \mathsf A_a=0\quad(a\notin\Delta).
\end{array}
\end{equation}
For \(T\subseteq\mathcal S\setminus\{0\}\), let \(\operatorname{Dels}_{\mathrm{des}}(W,T)\) be the infimum of
\begin{equation}\label{def:design-conic-bound}
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{minimize} & \mathsf B_0(E_0),\\
\text{subject to} & \text{the conditions in }\eqref{eq:conic-feasibility},\\
& \mathsf B_i=0\quad(i\in T).
\end{array}
\end{equation}
The variables are \((\mathsf A,\mathsf B)\), and we use \(\sup\varnothing=-\infty\) and \(\inf\varnothing=+\infty\).

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:conic-bounds}
Let \(0\in\Delta\subseteq\mathcal C\) and \(T\subseteq\mathcal S\setminus\{0\}\).
Every \(\Delta\)-code \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{esize}(\chi)\leq\operatorname{Dels}_{\mathrm{code}}(W,\Delta),
\]
and every \(T\)-design \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{esize}(\chi)\geq\operatorname{Dels}_{\mathrm{des}}(W,T).
\]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
It suffices to use the actual distributions as feasible points.
Proposition~\ref{lem:distribution-normalization}, Theorem~\ref{thm:macwilliams}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:distribution-positivity} show that \((A^\chi,B^\chi)\) satisfies \eqref{eq:conic-feasibility}.
If \(\chi\) is a \(\Delta\)-code, its distribution also satisfies the additional constraints in \eqref{def:code-conic-bound}; its objective value \(\operatorname{esize}(\chi)\) is therefore at most the supremum.
If \(\chi\) is a \(T\)-design, the same pair satisfies \eqref{def:design-conic-bound}, and its objective value is at least the infimum.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:classical-noncommutative-specialization}
Let \(W\) be the adjacency algebra of a homogeneous coherent configuration on a finite set \(X\), with adjacency basis \(\{B_a\}_{a\in\mathcal C}\).
Regard \(W\) as a QAS on \(A=\mathbb{C}^X\), with the simple-ideal decompositions above.
Then
\[
V_a=\mathbb{C} B_a,\qquad R_a^+=\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}B_a.
\]
For a nonempty \(Y\subseteq X\) with characteristic vector \(\chi_Y\),
\[
A_a^{\chi_Y}(B_a)=\frac1{|Y|}\chi_Y^\dagger B_a\chi_Y,\qquad
B_i^{\chi_Y}(u)=\frac{|X|}{|Y|}\chi_Y^\dagger u\chi_Y
\quad(u\in U_i),
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{esize}(\chi_Y)=|Y|,\qquad
B_i^{\chi_Y}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e_i(\chi_Y)=0.
\]
After identifying \((U_i,\circ,\dagger)\) with \(M_r(\mathbb{C})\) by a \(C^\ast\)-algebra isomorphism, its complex linear functionals nonnegative on \(K_i^+\) are precisely
\[
u\longmapsto\operatorname{Tr}(Du),\qquad D\in M_r(\mathbb{C}),\quad D\succeq0.
\]
The code and design bounds therefore become semidefinite programs with scalar variables for the \(V_a\) and positive semidefinite matrix variables for the \(U_i\).
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We first identify the ideals and cones for the Schur product.
The adjacency algebra is a cocommutative QAS~\cite{Higman1970,Gromada2024}.
The \(B_a\) are minimal projections for the entrywise Schur product, so \(V_a=\mathbb{C} B_a\).
Their entries are nonnegative, giving \(\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}B_a\subseteq R_a^+\).
Conversely, if \(cB_a\in R_a^+\), self-adjointness for \(\ast\) gives \(c\in\mathbb{R}\), and testing on the constant function with value \(1\) gives
\[
c\sum_{x,y\in X}(B_a)_{xy}\geq0.
\]
The sum is positive because \(B_a\) is a nonzero \(0,1\)-matrix; hence \(c\geq0\).

Substituting \(N=|X|\) and \(h_A(\chi_Y^2)=|Y|\) in \eqref{eq:inner-distribution}--\eqref{eq:dual-distribution} gives the distribution formulas.
Proposition~\ref{lem:distribution-normalization} gives \(\operatorname{esize}(\chi_Y)=h_A(\chi_Y)=|Y|\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral-vanishing} gives the vanishing criterion.
For composition, the \(C^\ast\)-algebra isomorphism identifies \(K_i^+\) with the positive semidefinite cone in \(M_r(\mathbb{C})\).
Self-duality under the trace pairing gives the stated representation of its positive functionals~\cite[Section~2.4]{BoydVandenberghe2004}.
The cone constraints are therefore scalar nonnegativity and positive semidefiniteness; all other constraints and both objectives are linear.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:classical-programs}
For the Bose--Mesner algebra of a symmetric association scheme, the conic programs~\eqref{def:code-conic-bound} and~\eqref{def:design-conic-bound} reduce to the linear programs~\eqref{def:classical-code-program} and~\eqref{def:classical-design-program}, respectively.

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Proposition~\ref{prop:classical-noncommutative-specialization} gives \(V_a=\mathbb{C} B_a\) and \(R_a^+=\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}B_a\).
Symmetry also gives \(U_i=\mathbb{C} E_i\) and \(K_i^+=\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}E_i\).
Thus the functionals are determined by real scalars \(a_i:=\mathsf A_i(B_i)\) and \(b_j:=\mathsf B_j(E_j)\), and the cone constraints are \(a_i,b_j\geq0\).
Evaluating the MacWilliams relation on \(E_j\) and \(B_i\), using \eqref{eq:classical-eigenmatrices}, gives the eigenmatrix relations in Proposition~\ref{prop:classical-distributions}.
Since \(I=B_0=\sum_j E_j\), the common normalization is \(a_0=1\), equivalently \(v^{-1}\sum_jb_j=1\).
The objectives are \(\mathsf A(J)=\sum_i a_i\) and \(\mathsf B_0(E_0)=b_0\), and the respective code and design conditions set exactly the prescribed components to zero.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:schurian-design-orthogonality-bound}
Suppose that \(T\subseteq\mathcal S\setminus\{0\}\) and \(\varnothing\ne Z\subseteq\Omega\) satisfy
\[
e_i\chi_Z=0\qquad(i\notin T\cup\{0\}).
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{Dels}_{\mathrm{des}}(W,T)\geq\frac{|\Omega|}{|Z|}.
\]
Every \(T\)-design \(Y\subseteq\Omega\) satisfies
\[
|Y|\geq\frac{|\Omega|}{|Z|},\qquad
|Y\cap gZ|=\frac{|Y||Z|}{|\Omega|}\quad(g\in G),
\]
and hence \(|\Omega|\mid |Y||Z|\).
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
To bound the design program, average the rank-one operators associated with the translates of \(Z\):
\[
M:=\frac{|\Omega|}{|Z||G|}
\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{gZ}\chi_{gZ}^{\dagger}\in W.
\]
By transitivity, each point belongs to \(|G||Z|/|\Omega|\) translates, counted with multiplicity.
Thus \(M\) has diagonal entries one, nonnegative entries, and \(M1=|Z|1\).
In particular, \(M-I\) is a nonnegative linear combination of nondiagonal adjacency matrices.
Since each \(e_i\) commutes with the group action, the hypothesis also gives \(e_i\chi_{gZ}=0\) for \(i\notin T\cup\{0\}\).
Consequently,
\[
e_0\circ M=|Z|E_0,\qquad
e_i\circ M=0\quad(i\notin T\cup\{0\}).
\]
For any feasible pair \((\mathsf A,\mathsf B)\) in the design program, Proposition~\ref{prop:classical-noncommutative-specialization} gives nonnegativity of \(\mathsf A\) on adjacency matrices.
Using \(\mathsf B_i=0\) for \(i\in T\) and the inverse MacWilliams relation, we obtain
\[
1=\mathsf A(I)\leq\mathsf A(M)
=\frac1{|\Omega|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}\mathsf B_i(e_i\circ M)
=\frac{|Z|}{|\Omega|}\mathsf B_0(E_0).
\]
Taking the infimum proves the program bound; Theorem~\ref{thm:conic-bounds} then gives the bound on \(|Y|\).

For the intersection formula, Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral-vanishing} gives \(e_i\chi_Y=0\) for \(i\in T\), while \(e_i\chi_{gZ}=0\) for \(i\notin T\cup\{0\}\).
The orthogonal decomposition by the \(e_i\) therefore gives
\[
|Y\cap gZ|
=\langle\chi_Y,\chi_{gZ}\rangle
=\langle E_0\chi_Y,E_0\chi_{gZ}\rangle
=\frac{|Y||Z|}{|\Omega|}.
\]
The left-hand side is an integer, proving divisibility.
This intersection formula also appears in~\cite[Definition~5.3 and Proposition~5.5]{BambergLansdown2026}.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:moore-standard-block}
The multiplicity of \(V\) in \(\mathbb{C}^\Omega\) is two, and the sum of all submodules isomorphic to \(V\) is \(\Phi(V)\oplus\Psi(V)\).
Put
\[
T:=\{i\in\mathcal S\setminus\{0\}:
\operatorname{im}e_i=\Phi(V)\oplus\Psi(V)\}.
\]
Then
\[
|T|=1,\qquad U_i\cong M_2(\mathbb{C})\quad(i\in T).
\]
For every nonempty \(Y\subseteq\Omega\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y\text{ is a }T\text{-design}
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi_Y\in\bigl(\Phi(V)\oplus\Psi(V)\bigr)^\perp\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle |\{B:(x,B)\in Y\}|=\frac{|Y|}{v},\\[5pt]
\displaystyle |\{(y,B)\in Y:x\in B\}|=\frac{k|Y|}{v}
\end{cases}
\quad(\forall x\in X).
\end{aligned}
\]
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The stabilizer of \((x,B)\in\Omega\) has three orbits on \(X\):
\[
\{x\},\qquad B,\qquad X\setminus(\{x\}\cup B).
\]
Its fixed vectors in \(V\) are constant on these orbits and have coordinate sum zero, so their space has dimension two.
Frobenius reciprocity gives multiplicity two for \(V\) in \(\mathbb{C}^\Omega\)~\cite{Serre1977}.
The maps \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\) are nonzero and not proportional: choose \(p\in B\), \(q\notin B\cup\{x\}\), and \(a\in V\) with \(a_p=1\), \(a_q=-1\), and all other coordinates zero.
Then
\[
\Phi(a)(x,B)=0,\qquad \Psi(a)(x,B)=1.
\]
By irreducibility of \(V\) and Schur's lemma, their images are distinct irreducible submodules with zero intersection.
They account for both copies of \(V\), and~\eqref{eq:schurian-commutant} gives \(|T|=1\) and the corresponding ideal \(M_2(\mathbb{C})\).

Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral-vanishing} gives the first equivalence.
For \(a\in V\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle\chi_Y,\Phi(a)\rangle
&=\sum_{x\in X}|\{B:(x,B)\in Y\}|a_x,\\
\langle\chi_Y,\Psi(a)\rangle
&=\sum_{z\in X}|\{(x,B)\in Y:z\in B\}|a_z.
\end{aligned}
\]
Each expression vanishes for every \(a\) with coordinate sum zero if and only if its count vector is constant.
Summing the two count vectors over \(X\) gives \(|Y|\) and \(k|Y|\), respectively, yielding the stated values.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:moore-correspondence}
For a finite simple \(k\)-regular graph \(\Gamma\) on \(X\), let \(N_\Gamma(x)\) be the neighborhood of \(x\) and put
\[
Y_\Gamma:=\{(x,N_\Gamma(x)):x\in X\}.
\]
The map \(\Gamma\mapsto Y_\Gamma\) is a bijection between Moore graphs of degree \(k\) and diameter two on \(X\) and nonempty subsets of \(\Omega\) that are both \(\Delta\)-codes and \(T\)-designs.
Every such subset has size \(v\).
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Let \(Y\) be a nonempty \(\Delta\)-code and \(T\)-design.

E_s&=\frac1{D^2}\sum_{r=0}^n\mathsf K_s(r)B_r.

<!-- definition-10: body_md -->
\label{def:quantum-code-purity}
A quantum code is a nonzero subspace of \(\mathcal H\), represented by its orthogonal projection \(\Pi\).
For \(1\le\delta\le n+1\), it detects all errors of weight less than \(\delta\) if
\[
\Pi W_\alpha\Pi=c_\alpha\Pi
\qquad(0<\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)<\delta)
\]
for scalars \(c_\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\).
It is pure to distance \(\delta\) if
\[
\Pi W_\alpha\Pi=0
\qquad(0<\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)<\delta).
\]

<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{prop:weyl-code-distributions}
Let \(\Pi\in A\) be a nonzero projection of rank \(K\).
Write its distributions as \(a_r:=A_r^\Pi(B_r)\) and \(b_s:=B_s^\Pi(E_s)\).
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_r&=\frac1K\sum_{\alpha\in G_r}\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi W_\alpha\Pi W_\alpha^\ast),\\
b_s&=\frac DK\sum_{\beta\in G_s}|\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi W_\beta)|^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_s&=\sum_{r=0}^n\mathsf K_s(r)a_r,\\
a_r&=\frac1{D^2}\sum_{s=0}^n\mathsf K_r(s)b_s.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their normalizations are
\[
a_0=1,\qquad \sum_{r=0}^na_r=b_0=DK,\qquad \sum_{s=0}^nb_s=D^2.
\]
The projection \(\Pi\) is a \(\Delta\)-code if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weyl-code-condition}
\Pi W_\alpha\Pi=0
\qquad(0<\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)<\delta).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->

=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left((\Pi W_\alpha\Pi)(\Pi W_\alpha\Pi)^\ast\right)\ge0.
\]
The sum defining \(a_r\) therefore vanishes exactly when every \(\Pi W_\alpha\Pi\), \(\alpha\in G_r\), is zero.

<!-- definition-11: body_md -->
\label{def:qoa}
Let \(R\) be a positive integer and \(t\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\).
A quantum orthogonal array \(\operatorname{QOA}(R,n,q,t)\) is a family \((\varphi_j)_{j=1}^R\in\mathcal H^R\) with \(\|\varphi_j\|=1\) for \(j\in[R]\), satisfying
\[
\sum_{j,l=1}^R\operatorname{Tr}_{\Lambda^c}(\varphi_j\varphi_l^\dagger)
=\frac{R}{q^t}I_\Lambda
\qquad(\Lambda\subseteq[n],\ |\Lambda|=t).
\]
The integer \(t\) is called its strength.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:qoa-qas-design}
Let \(t\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\) and \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\).
Then \(\chi\) is a \(\{1,\ldots,t\}\)-design for \(W\) if and only if
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\Lambda^c}(\chi)
=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}\chi}{q^t}I_\Lambda
\qquad(\Lambda\subseteq[n],\ |\Lambda|=t).
\]
For \((\varphi_j)_{j=1}^R\in\mathcal H^R\) with \(\|\varphi_j\|=1\), put
\[
\varphi=\frac1{\sqrt R}\sum_{j=1}^R\varphi_j,
\qquad p=\varphi\varphi^\dagger.
\]
The family is a \(\operatorname{QOA}(R,n,q,t)\) if and only if \(\|\varphi\|=1\) and \(p\) is a \(\{1,\ldots,t\}\)-design.
For \(\|\varphi\|=1\),
\[
p\text{ is a }\{1,\ldots,t\}\text{-design}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\text{ is a }\{0\}\cup\{t+1,\ldots,n\}\text{-code}.
\]
In particular, every QOA determines a rank-one projection with \(\operatorname{esize}(p)=D\).
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
We first express each partial-trace condition in Weyl coefficients.
Fix \(\Lambda\subseteq[n]\) with \(|\Lambda|=t\).
The Weyl operators on these factors form an orthogonal basis, whose weight-zero element is the identity.
Its coefficient in \(\operatorname{Tr}_{\Lambda^c}(\chi)\) is fixed by \(\operatorname{Tr}\chi\).
By the defining property of the partial trace, the reduced operator is therefore the required scalar identity exactly when
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\chi W_\beta^\ast)=0
\qquad(\varnothing\ne\operatorname{supp}(\beta)\subseteq\Lambda).
\]
Every nonempty support of size at most \(t\) is contained in such a \(\Lambda\).
Thus all the partial-trace conditions hold exactly when
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\chi W_\beta^\ast)=0
\qquad(1\le\operatorname{wt}(\beta)\le t).
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:dual-distribution} gives
\[
B_s^\chi(E_s)=\frac{D}{\operatorname{Tr}(\chi^2)}
\sum_{\beta\in G_s}|\operatorname{Tr}(\chi W_\beta^\ast)|^2.
\]
Each sum vanishes exactly when its Weyl coefficients vanish.
Since \(U_s=\mathbb{C} E_s\), this is equivalent to \(B_s^\chi=0\) for \(1\le s\le t\), proving the first assertion.

For the row vectors in the theorem, the cross terms are collected by \(p\).
Linearity of the partial trace gives
\[
\sum_{j,l=1}^R\operatorname{Tr}_{\Lambda^c}(\varphi_j\varphi_l^\dagger)
=R\operatorname{Tr}_{\Lambda^c}(p).
\]
Taking the full trace of a QOA identity gives \(R\|\varphi\|^2=R\), hence \(\|\varphi\|=1\).
With this normalization, the displayed identity is exactly the partial-trace condition for \(p\).
Conversely, that condition and \(\|\varphi\|=1\) give the QOA identities.

To compare the rank-one design with the code condition, observe that
\[
pW_\alpha p=(\varphi^\dagger W_\alpha\varphi)p
=\operatorname{Tr}(pW_\alpha)p.
\]
Since \(p=p^\dagger\), we have \(\operatorname{Tr}(pW_\alpha^\ast)=\overline{\operatorname{Tr}(pW_\alpha)}\).
Hence the Weyl coefficients of weights \(1,\ldots,t\) vanish if and only if \(pW_\alpha p=0\) for those weights.
Proposition~\ref{prop:weyl-code-distributions}, with \(\delta=t+1\), gives the code equivalence.
Finally, \(p\) is a rank-one projection and \(h_A(p)=D\), so \(\operatorname{esize}(p)=D\).

<!-- proposition-11: body_md -->
\label{prop:qoa-bound}
Let \(t\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\).
The value \(\operatorname{Dels}_{\mathrm{des}}(W,\{1,\ldots,t\})\) is the optimum of
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{minimize} & b_0,\\[1mm]
\mathrm{subject\ to} & b_s=0 \quad (1\le s\le t),\\

& \displaystyle\frac1{D^2}\sum_{s=0}^n\mathsf K_r(s)b_s\ge0 \quad (1\le r\le n),\\[3mm]
& \displaystyle\frac1{D^2}\sum_{s=0}^nb_s=1.
\end{array}
\]

\[
\operatorname{Dels}_{\mathrm{des}}(W,\{1,\ldots,t\})
\le D
\le\operatorname{Dels}_{\mathrm{code}}(W,\{0\}\cup\{t+1,\ldots,n\}).
\]
The resulting constraints on \(n,q,t\) are independent of \(R\).

The two QOA definitions differ even for classical rows.
Let \(t\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\) and \(\varnothing\ne C\subseteq\mathbb F_q^n\).
Put \(|x\rangle:=e_{x_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{x_n}\) and \(x_\Lambda:=(x_j)_{j\in\Lambda}\) for \(x\in C\) and \(\Lambda\subseteq[n]\).
The diagonal projection
\[
\Pi=\sum_{x\in C}|x\rangle\langle x|
\]
is a \(\{1,\ldots,t\}\)-design if and only if \(C\) is a classical orthogonal array of strength \(t\), since its partial traces are diagonal with entries
\[
\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}_{\Lambda^c}(\Pi)\bigr)_{u,u}
=\bigl|\{x\in C:x_\Lambda=u\}\bigr|,
\qquad(u\in\mathbb F_q^\Lambda).
\]
For \(|\Lambda|=t\), the design condition requires these counts to equal \(|C|/q^t\)~\cite[Chapter~1]{HedayatSloaneStufken1999}.

For the definition with cross terms, put
\[
\varphi=\frac1{\sqrt{|C|}}\sum_{x\in C}|x\rangle.
\]
Its partial trace is
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\Lambda^c}(\varphi\varphi^\dagger)
=\frac1{|C|}
\sum_{\substack{x,y\in C\\x_{\Lambda^c}=y_{\Lambda^c}}}
|x_\Lambda\rangle\langle y_\Lambda|.
\]
The diagonal entries give the same counts divided by \(|C|\).
Every matrix-entry contribution has coefficient \(1/|C|>0\), so an off-diagonal entry is nonzero exactly when two distinct rows agree outside \(\Lambda\).
Thus every off-diagonal entry vanishes for every \(|\Lambda|=t\) if and only if
\[
\bigl|\{j\in[n]:x_j\ne y_j\}\bigr|\ge t+1
\qquad(x,y\in C,\ x\ne y).
\]
Consequently, the basis vectors indexed by \(C\) form a QOA if and only if \(C\) is an orthogonal array of strength \(t\) and its distinct rows have Hamming distance at least \(t+1\).
The distance condition says that deleting any \(t\) columns leaves pairwise distinct rows~\cite[Section~IV]{GoyenecheZyczkowski2014}.

\begin{gather}
\operatorname{Dels}_{\mathrm{code}}(W_n,\Delta_\delta)
=n\left\lceil\frac n\delta\right\rceil,\label{eq:wn-code-bound}\\*
\operatorname{Dels}_{\mathrm{des}}(W_n,T_t)
=\frac{n}{\left\lceil n/(t+1)\right\rceil}.
\label{eq:wn-design-bound}
\end{gather}
Every \(\Delta_\delta\)-code projection \(p\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{rank}(p)\le\left\lceil\frac n\delta\right\rceil,
\]
and equality is attained.
The minimum effective size of a \(T_t\)-design is
\[
\min\{\operatorname{esize}(\chi):\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\},\
\chi\text{ is a }T_t\text{-design}\}=n.
\]

<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
We first derive a matrix estimate common to both conic programs, then construct feasible families attaining the bounds.
For a feasible pair \((\mathsf A,\mathsf B)\), with \(\mathsf B=Q'\mathsf A\), put
\[
X_s:=\bigl(\mathsf A_s(\mathsf F_{ji}^{(s)})\bigr)_{i,j\in D_s}
\qquad(|s|<n).
\]
The formula for \(Q\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wn-transition}
(Q'\mathsf A)_r(\mathsf E_{ij}^{(r)})
=n^2(X_{i-j})_{i-r,i}
\qquad(i,j\in D_r).
\end{equation}
The involution condition makes \(X_s\) Hermitian.
Since
\[
\mathsf A_s\!\left(\sum_{i,j\in D_s}c_{ij}\mathsf F_{ij}^{(s)}\right)
=\operatorname{Tr}(X_sC)
\qquad\bigl(C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in D_s}\bigr),
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:matrix-difference-cones} and self-duality of the positive semidefinite cone give \(X_s\succeq0\)~\cite[Section~2.4]{BoydVandenberghe2004}.
The MacWilliams relation, normalization, and positivity of \(\mathsf B\) also give
\[
(X_0)_{ij}
=n^{-2}\mathsf B_{j-i}(\mathsf E_{jj}^{(j-i)})\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{i,j\in[n]}(X_0)_{ij}
=\mathsf A_0(I)=1,
\]
and
\[
\mathsf B_0(E_0)=\mathsf A(J)
=n\sum_{|s|<n}\operatorname{Tr}(X_s).
\]

The estimate needed below follows by grouping nearby indices.
For an integer \(1\le d\le n\), every matrix \(M=(M_{ab})_{a,b\in[n]}\succeq0\) with \(M_{ab}=0\) for \(0<|a-b|<d\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wn-interval-estimate}
\sum_{a,b\in[n]}M_{ab}
\le\left\lceil\frac nd\right\rceil\operatorname{Tr}(M).
\end{equation}
Write \(M_{ab}=\langle u_a,u_b\rangle\) and partition \([n]\) into consecutive intervals \(I_1,\ldots,I_{\lceil n/d\rceil}\), each of size at most \(d\).
The vanishing entries make the vectors within each interval pairwise orthogonal, so Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\left\|\sum_{a\in[n]}u_a\right\|^2
\le\left\lceil\frac nd\right\rceil
\sum_{j=1}^{\lceil n/d\rceil}
\left\|\sum_{a\in I_j}u_a\right\|^2
=\left\lceil\frac nd\right\rceil
\sum_{a\in[n]}\|u_a\|^2.
\]

For the code bound, apply this estimate to the matrix representing \(n^{-2}\mathsf B_0\):
\[
M:=\bigl((X_{b-a})_{bb}\bigr)_{a,b\in[n]},
\qquad M_{ab}=n^{-2}\mathsf B_0(\mathsf E_{ba}^{(0)}).
\]
Positivity on \(K_0^+\) gives \(M\succeq0\), and the code constraints give \(M_{ab}=0\) for \(0<|a-b|<\delta\).
The entries of \(X_0\) are nonnegative and sum to \(1\), so
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(M)=\operatorname{Tr}(X_0)\le1,
\qquad
\frac{\mathsf A(J)}n
=\sum_{|s|<n}\operatorname{Tr}(X_s)
=\sum_{a,b\in[n]}M_{ab}.
\]
Set \(m:=\lceil n/\delta\rceil\).
By \eqref{eq:wn-interval-estimate} with \(d=\delta\),
\[
\frac{\mathsf A(J)}n
\le m\operatorname{Tr}(M)\le m.
\]
The projection
\[
p:=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\varepsilon_{1+j\delta,1+j\delta}
\]
is a \(\Delta_\delta\)-code of rank \(m\), by Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix-difference-code-design}.
Since \(\operatorname{esize}(p)=n\operatorname{rank}(p)=nm\), it attains the code bound and also proves that the rank bound is attained.
For any code projection, \(\operatorname{esize}(p)=n\operatorname{rank}(p)\) gives the stated rank inequality.

For a feasible design family, the design constraints and \eqref{eq:wn-transition} give
\[
(X_0)_{ab}=0\qquad(1\le|a-b|\le t).
\]
Set \(m:=\lceil n/(t+1)\rceil\).
Applying \eqref{eq:wn-interval-estimate} to \(X_0\), with \(d=t+1\), gives
\[
1=\sum_{a,b\in[n]}(X_0)_{ab}
\le m\operatorname{Tr}(X_0).
\]
Since every \(X_s\) is positive semidefinite,
\[
\mathsf B_0(E_0)
=n\sum_{|s|<n}\operatorname{Tr}(X_s)
\ge n\operatorname{Tr}(X_0)\ge\frac nm.
\]

To attain \(n/m\), choose indices at least \(t+1\) apart and put
\[
S:=\{1+j(t+1):j=0,\ldots,m-1\}\subseteq[n],
\qquad
c:=\sum_{i,j\in S}\varepsilon_{ij},
\]
and define
\[
\mathsf A_s:=0\quad(s\ne0),
\qquad
\mathsf A_0(x):=\frac{\langle c,x(c)\rangle}{nm^2}
\quad(x\in V_0).
\]
Since \(c\in A_+\) and \(\operatorname{Tr}(c^2)=m^2\), the defining properties of \(R_0^+\) and the involution identity for distributions give positivity, Hermitian symmetry, and \(\mathsf A_0(I)=1\).
For \(\mathsf B:=Q'\mathsf A\), \eqref{eq:wn-transition} gives
\[
\mathsf B_r(\mathsf E_{ij}^{(r)})=
\begin{cases}
n^2m^{-2},&i=j,\ i\in S,\ i-r\in S,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
The matrices representing these functionals are diagonal and positive semidefinite, so every \(\mathsf B_r\) is Hermitian and nonnegative on \(K_r^+\).
The separation of the indices in \(S\) gives \(\mathsf B_r=0\) for \(1\le|r|\le t\).
Thus the pair is feasible, with objective
\[
\mathsf B_0(E_0)
=\frac1n\sum_{i\in[n]}\mathsf B_0(\mathsf E_{ii}^{(0)})
=\frac nm.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:wn-design-bound}.

To determine the minimum for actual designs, the nonnegative eigenvalues of every \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\) give
\[
\operatorname{esize}(\chi)
=n\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(\chi)^2}{\operatorname{Tr}(\chi^2)}
\ge n.
\]
Equality holds for the \(T_t\)-design \(\varepsilon_{11}\), by Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix-difference-code-design}.

\[
\begin{aligned}
G&=S_v,\\
H&=\{g\in S_v:g([k])=[k]\}
   \cong S_k\times S_{v-k}.
\end{aligned}

G/H&\xrightarrow{\sim}\{B\subseteq[v]:|B|=k\},\\
gH&\longmapsto g([k]).
\end{aligned}
\]

\]

\quad[v]\setminus(B\cup C).
\]

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:johnson-pointwise-characterization}
Let \(0\le t\le k\) and \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\).
Then \(\chi\) is a quantum block \(\tau\)-design of strength \(t\) if and only if it is a \(T_t\)-design in \(\mathfrak J_\tau(v,k)\).
Equivalently,
\[
e_\lambda\chi=0
\qquad(\lambda\vdash v,\ 1\le v-\lambda_1\le t).
\]
For the one-dimensional trivial representation \(\tau\), these conditions are precisely those of a weighted \(t\)-\((v,k)\) design.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
First we identify the components excluded by the averaging condition.
For each \(C\in\binom{[v]}t\), the branching rule for \(K_C\cong S_{v-t}\) gives
\[
(S^\lambda)^{K_C}\ne0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\lambda_1\ge v-t
\]
for every partition \(\lambda\vdash v\)~\cite[Section~2.8]{Sagan2001}.
Indeed, the trivial representation indexed by \((v-t)\) occurs on restriction exactly when this one-row partition is contained in \(\lambda\), which is equivalent to \(\lambda_1\ge v-t\).
For an irreducible \(S_v\)-submodule \(U\subseteq A\), the translates of \(U^{K_C}\) span an invariant subspace.
By irreducibility, this span is \(U\) when \(U^{K_C}\ne0\), and is zero otherwise.
Conjugation gives \(gK_Cg^{-1}=K_{gC}\), so applying this observation to each irreducible summand yields
\[
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigcup_{C\in\binom{[v]}t} A^{K_C}
=\mathbb{C}1_A\oplus\bigoplus_{\lambda\in T_t}\operatorname{im}e_\lambda.
\]
Since
\[
E_0\chi=\frac{h_A(\chi)}{h_A(1_A)}1_A,
\qquad \chi-E_0\chi\perp\mathbb{C}1_A,
\]
the decomposition shows that \(\chi-E_0\chi\perp A^{K_C}\) for every \(C\) exactly when \(e_\lambda\chi=0\) for every \(\lambda\in T_t\).
Averaging over \(K_C\) projects onto \(A^{K_C}\), so this orthogonality is precisely \eqref{eq:tau-quantum-block-design}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral-vanishing} then identifies it with the \(T_t\)-design condition.

It remains to compare the averaging condition with the classical incidence condition.
For trivial \(\tau\), write \(\chi=(w_B)_{B\in\mathcal B}\) and put
\[
c:=\frac1{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}w_B.
\]
For fixed \(C\), the nonempty sets
\[
\{B\in\mathcal B:B\cap C=S\},\qquad S\subseteq C,
\]
are the \(K_C\)-orbits: permutations of \([v]\setminus C\) act transitively on the remaining elements of a block.
Thus \eqref{eq:tau-quantum-block-design} requires mean weight \(c\) on each nonempty orbit.
The blocks containing \(C\) form one such orbit, so their total weight is
\[
c\binom{v-t}{k-t}
=\frac{\binom{k}{t}}{\binom{v}{t}}\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}w_B,
\]
as required.

Conversely, suppose that \(w\) is a weighted \(t\)-\((v,k)\) design.
The incidence condition gives \(\sum_{B\supseteq C}(w_B-c)=0\) whenever \(|C|=t\).
We first obtain the same vanishing for smaller prescribed subsets by counting their \(t\)-subset extensions.
For \(S\subseteq[v]\) with \(|S|\le t\),
\[
\binom{k-|S|}{t-|S|}\sum_{B\supseteq S}(w_B-c)
=\sum_{\substack{C\supseteq S\\|C|=t}}\sum_{B\supseteq C}(w_B-c)=0.
\]
Since \(t\le k\), the coefficient is positive, giving \(\sum_{B\supseteq S}(w_B-c)=0\).
We can now isolate blocks having a specified intersection with \(C\).
For \(|C|=t\) and \(S\subseteq C\), inclusion--exclusion gives
\[
\sum_{B:B\cap C=S}(w_B-c)
=\sum_{S\subseteq R\subseteq C}(-1)^{|R|-|S|}\sum_{B\supseteq R}(w_B-c)=0.
\]
Every nonempty orbit therefore has mean weight \(c\), proving the averaging condition.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:johnson-zauner-orbits}
For projections, the averaging condition in Definition~\ref{def:quantum-block-tau-design} can be expressed through Zauner's coherence condition.
Fix \(0\le t\le k\), and let
\[
\mathcal H:=\bigoplus_{B\in\mathcal B}V_B,
\qquad
\rho:S_v\longrightarrow U(\mathcal H)
\]
be the induced representation, and identify \(A\) with the block diagonal subalgebra of \(\operatorname{End}(\mathcal H)\).
For \(P\in A\) satisfying
\[
P^2=P=P^\ast,
\qquad
0\ne P\ne1_A=I_{\mathcal H},
\]
put
\[
D_C(P):=\{\rho(g)P\rho(g)^\ast:g\in K_C\}
\qquad\left(C\in\binom{[v]}t\right).
\]
Each orbit element occurs equally often in the group average, so
\[
\frac1{|D_C(P)|}\sum_{Q\in D_C(P)}Q
=\frac1{|K_C|}\sum_{g\in K_C}\rho(g)P\rho(g)^\ast.
\]
Since \(h_A=d\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal H}\), we also have
\[
\frac{h_A(P)}{h_A(1_A)}
=\frac{\operatorname{rank}P}{\dim\mathcal H}.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:johnson-zauner-coherence}
\begin{aligned}
&P\text{ is a quantum block }\tau\text{-design of strength }t\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_{Q\in D_C(P)}Q
=\frac{|D_C(P)|\operatorname{rank}P}{\dim\mathcal H}I_{\mathcal H}
\quad\left(C\in\binom{[v]}t\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The projections in each \(D_C(P)\) have the same rank, and the right-hand side is Zauner's coherence condition~\cite[Definition~1.1]{Zauner1999}.
Under this condition, each \(D_C(P)\) is regular and coherent in his sense: a singleton orbit would force \(P\) to be a scalar projection, contradicting \(0\ne P\ne I_{\mathcal H}\).
Here the strength \(t\) specifies the subgroups \(K_C\), while the coherence condition on each orbit concerns the first tensor moment in Zauner's definition.

\label{prob:johnson-zauner-reconstruction}
Fix \(2\le t\le k\le v-1\) and an irreducible unitary representation \(\tau:H\to U(V)\).
For a finite set \(D\subseteq\operatorname{End}(\mathcal H)\) of at least two projections that is regular and coherent in Zauner's sense, determine necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of
\[
u\in U(\mathcal H),\qquad
C\in\binom{[v]}t,\qquad
P\in A,\qquad
P^2=P=P^\ast,\quad0\ne P\ne1_A,
\]
such that \(P\) is a quantum block \(\tau\)-design of strength \(t\) and
\[
uDu^\ast=D_C(P).
\]
Give a construction of such \(P\).

<!-- proposition-16: body_md -->
\label{prop:johnson-module-decomposition}
The \(S_5\)-module and composition algebra decompose as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:johnson-module-decomposition}
\begin{aligned}
A&\cong S^{(5)}\oplus2S^{(4,1)}\oplus2S^{(3,2)}
       \oplus2S^{(3,1,1)}
       \oplus S^{(2,2,1)}\oplus S^{(2,1,1,1)},\\
(W,\circ,\dagger)&\cong\mathbb{C}^{\oplus3}\oplus M_2(\mathbb{C})^{\oplus3}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In particular, composition is noncommutative and \(\dim W=15\).

Let \(\mathbf1\) denote the trivial representation of either group factor, let \(\operatorname{sgn}\) and \(\operatorname{Std}\) denote the sign and standard representations of \(S_3\), and let \(\boxtimes\) denote the external tensor product.
Conjugation on \(\operatorname{End}(V_{\{0,1\}})\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{End}(V_{\{0,1\}})
&\cong\mathbf1\boxtimes
(\mathbf1\oplus\operatorname{sgn}\oplus\operatorname{Std}),\\
A&\cong\operatorname{Ind}_H^{S_5}\operatorname{End}(V_{\{0,1\}}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The Littlewood--Richardson rule~\cite[Chapter~4]{Sagan2001} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Ind}_{S_2\times S_3}^{S_5}
(\mathbf1\boxtimes\mathbf1)
&\cong S^{(5)}\oplus S^{(4,1)}\oplus S^{(3,2)},\\
\operatorname{Ind}_{S_2\times S_3}^{S_5}
(\mathbf1\boxtimes\operatorname{sgn})
&\cong S^{(3,1,1)}\oplus S^{(2,1,1,1)},\\
\operatorname{Ind}_{S_2\times S_3}^{S_5}
(\mathbf1\boxtimes\operatorname{Std})
&\cong S^{(4,1)}\oplus S^{(3,2)}
       \oplus S^{(3,1,1)}\oplus S^{(2,2,1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Schur's lemma gives the composition algebra from the multiplicities~\cite[Chapter~2]{Serre1977}.

we have \((S^\lambda)^K=\{0\}\) for every \(\lambda\in T_2\).

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:johnson-minimum-size}
\label{prop:johnson-size-twenty-design}
The projections \(P^\pm\in A\) are quantum block \(\tau\)-designs of strength \(2\) and are not central in \(A\).
They satisfy
\[
\begin{gathered}
P^+P^-=0,\qquad P^++P^-=1_A,\\
\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}\operatorname{rank}(P_B^\pm)=10,
\qquad \operatorname{esize}(P^\pm)=20.
\end{gathered}
\]
They attain
\[
\min\{\operatorname{esize}(\chi):
      \chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\},\
      e_\lambda\chi=0\ (\lambda\in T_2)\}
=20
\]
and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:johnson-pointwise-average}
\frac16\sum_{g\in K_C}g\cdot P^\pm=\frac12 1_A
\qquad(C\in\mathcal B).
\end{equation}
For \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\) central in \(A\),
\[
\chi\text{ is a }T_2\text{-design}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}1_A.
\]
Every such design has effective size \(40\).
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-29: body_md -->
First we prove the lower bound.
For a \(T_2\)-design \(\chi\in A_+\setminus\{0\}\), Proposition~\ref{prop:johnson-fiber-traces} gives
\(\operatorname{Tr}(\chi_B)=s>0\) for all \(B\).
Nonnegative eigenvalues then give
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\chi_B^2)\le s^2,\qquad
h_A(\chi)=20s,\qquad
h_A(\chi^2)\le20s^2,\qquad
\operatorname{esize}(\chi)\ge20.
\]
To attain the bound, note that affine maps preserve midpoints, so \(g u_B=u_{gB}\) for \(g\in K\).
Thus \(P^+\) is \(K\)-fixed, and equivariance of \(e_\lambda\), together with Lemma~\ref{lem:agl-fixed-vectors}, gives
\[
e_\lambda P^+=0\qquad(\lambda\in T_2).
\]
The same vanishing holds for \(P^-=1_A-P^+\).
Both projections have rank one in each \(M_2(\mathbb{C})\) component, hence are not central in \(A\) and satisfy
\[
h_A(P^\pm)=2\cdot10=20=\operatorname{esize}(P^\pm).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:johnson-pointwise-characterization} gives strength \(2\) and~\eqref{eq:johnson-pointwise-average}, since \(|K_C|=6\) and \(h_A(1_A)=40\).
If \(\chi\) is central in \(A\), its components are scalar matrices; their equal traces force \(\chi=c1_A\) with \(c>0\).
Conversely, every such element satisfies the averaging condition and has effective size \(40\).

<!-- proposition-17: body_md -->
\label{prop:johnson-schur-decomposition}
For the quantum Schur product,
\[
(W,\bullet,\ast)
\cong\mathbb{C}^{\oplus7}\oplus M_2(\mathbb{C})^{\oplus2}.
\]
The pair orbits \(|B\cap C|=2,1,0\) contribute \(\mathbb{C}^{\oplus3}\), \(M_2(\mathbb{C})^{\oplus2}\), and \(\mathbb{C}^{\oplus4}\), respectively.
Moreover,
\[
\{u^\ast\mathbin\bullet u:u\in V_a\}\subseteq R_a^+
\qquad(a\in\mathcal C).
\]
<!-- end proposition-17 -->
<!-- proof-30: body_md -->
The quantum Schur product acts separately on each \((B,C)\)-component.
Taking \(a,b\) to be matrix units gives bases on both sides of the correspondence.
For matrix units \(E_{pq}\) in an orthonormal basis of \(V_C\), the trace normalization gives
\[
m^\dagger(E_{pq})
=\frac12\sum_{r=1}^2E_{pr}\otimes E_{rq},
\]
so, for \(a,b,c,d\in\operatorname{Hom}(V_C,V_B)\), direct multiplication gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(2a(\cdot)b^*\bigr)\bullet
\bigl(2c(\cdot)d^*\bigr)
&=\operatorname{Tr}(b^*c)\,2a(\cdot)d^*,\\
\bigl(2a(\cdot)b^*\bigr)^\ast
&=2b(\cdot)a^*.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(w_{BC}\mapsto[w_{BC}]\) is a \(\ast\)-isomorphism onto \(\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Hom}(V_C,V_B))\).
The group transports \(a\) to \(gag^{-1}\); consequently, the algebra on a pair orbit is the commutant of its stabilizer on \(\operatorname{Hom}(V_C,V_B)\).
Indeed, a commuting operator extends equivariantly to the orbit, independently of the chosen transporter.

For \(B=C\), the stabilizer representation on \(\operatorname{End}(V_B)\) is \(\mathbf1\oplus\operatorname{sgn}\oplus\operatorname{Std}\).
For \(|B\cap C|=1\), the stabilizer interchanges the two points outside \(B\cup C\); its eigenvalues \(1,-1\) each occur twice on \(\operatorname{Hom}(V_C,V_B)\).
For disjoint \(B,C\), the stabilizer \(S_2\times S_2\) has each of its four characters occurring once.
Schur's lemma gives the asserted algebras.

Positive rank-one operators correspond to the maps \(2a(\cdot)a^*\), which preserve positivity.
Hence every positive element \(w\) of \((W,\bullet,\ast)\) satisfies
\[
\langle\chi,w(\chi)\rangle
=h_A\bigl(\chi^{1/2}w(\chi)\chi^{1/2}\bigr)\ge0
\qquad(\chi\in A_+).
\]

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:johnson-distribution-matrices}
The feasible functionals of the design program~\eqref{def:design-conic-bound} are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:johnson-functional-parameters}
\mathsf A(w)=\operatorname{Tr}_A\!\left(
\left(
xE_0+\frac y5e_{(2,2,1)}+\frac z4e_{(2,1,1,1)}
\right)w\right),
\qquad \mathsf B=Q'\mathsf A,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:johnson-feasible-parameters}
x,y,z\ge0,\qquad x+y+z=1,\qquad
y\le\frac12,\qquad z\le x,\qquad y+z\le2x.
\end{equation}
The objective is \(40x\).
Replacing only \(\mathsf A_a(v)\ge0\) for \(v\in R_a^+\) by \(\mathsf A(z_a)\ge0\), where \(z_a\) are the minimal central projections of \((W,\bullet,\ast)\), replaces the last inequality by \(y\le2x\).
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-31: body_md -->
The design conditions \(\mathsf B_i=0\) for \(i\in T_2\) leave the multiplicity-one modules \(S^{(5)},S^{(2,2,1)},S^{(2,1,1,1)}\), of dimensions \(1,5,4\).
The MacWilliams relation gives \(\mathsf B_i=40\mathsf A|_{U_i}\), so positivity on \(K_i^+\) and normalization give~\eqref{eq:johnson-functional-parameters} with \(x,y,z\ge0\) and \(x+y+z=1\).
Since \(J=40E_0\), the objective is \(40x\).
We compute the three projection maps to determine the remaining constraints.

The map \(E_0\) has coefficient \(1/10\) between any two \(F_0\) coordinates and is zero elsewhere.
The \(F_2\) coordinates carry \(\operatorname{Ind}_{S_2\times S_3}^{S_5}(\mathbf1\boxtimes\operatorname{sgn})\).
Identify them with the exterior basis indexed by the increasing triples \(X\setminus B\).
The projection \(\bigwedge^3(I_5-J_5/5)\) has image \(\bigwedge^3S^{(4,1)}\cong S^{(2,1,1,1)}\)~\cite{Sagan2001}, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:johnson-exterior-projection}
\begin{aligned}
(e_{(2,1,1,1)})_{BC}(F_2)
&=\det(I_5-J_5/5)_{X\setminus B,\,X\setminus C}\,F_2,\\
(e_{(2,1,1,1)})_{BC}(F_j)
&=0\qquad(j\ne2),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(J_5\) is the all-ones matrix.
For \(B=\{0,1\}\) and \(C=\{0,1\},\{0,2\},\{2,3\}\), these determinants are \(2/5,-1/5,0\).

For \(e_{(2,2,1)}\), use the orbit of \(P^+\).
Extend \(P_B^+\) by zero on \(V_B^\perp\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{X\setminus B}\).
Its unique diagonal entry \(2/3\) occurs at \(m_B\), so any permutation stabilizing \(P^+\) preserves all midpoints.
Hence
\[
g(a+b)+g(0)
=2g((a+b)/2)
=g(a)+g(b),
\]
so \(x\mapsto g(x)-g(0)\) is additive on \(\mathbb F_5\).
Thus \(g(x)=g(0)+(g(1)-g(0))x\), and \(g\) is affine.
The stabilizer is therefore \(K\), and the orbit has six elements.
Define
\[
\{H_1,\ldots,H_6\}
:=\{g\cdot P^+-\tfrac12 1_A:g\in S_5\}.
\]
These elements are real symmetric and componentwise traceless, so they lie in \(\operatorname{Ind}_{S_2\times S_3}^{S_5}(\mathbf1\boxtimes\operatorname{Std})\).
They satisfy \(e_\lambda H_r=0\) for \(\lambda\in T_2\), by Theorem~\ref{thm:johnson-minimum-size}; their nonzero invariant span is therefore the single copy of \(S^{(2,2,1)}\).
For the ordinary Hilbert--Schmidt inner product, the equivariant sum of their rank-one operators is scalar on this five-dimensional module by Schur's lemma.
Since \(\sum_B\operatorname{Tr}(H_{r,B}^*H_{r,B})=5\), its trace is \(6\cdot5\), giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:johnson-five-projection}
e_{(2,2,1)}(\chi)
=\frac16\sum_{r=1}^6H_r
\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}\operatorname{Tr}(H_{r,B}^*\chi_B)
\qquad(\chi\in A).
\end{equation}
The factor \(2\) in \(h_A\) does not change orthogonal projections.

The following coordinates make this calculation explicit:
\[
2H_{r,B}
=n_{r,B}^{\mathsf T}\binom{\sigma_1}{\sigma_3},
\qquad n_{r,B}\in\mathbb{R}^2.
\]
For complement \(\{c<d<e\}\), midpoint choices \(c,d,e\) give
\[
n_{r,B}
=\binom{\sqrt3/2}{1/2},\quad
\binom{-\sqrt3/2}{1/2},\quad
\binom0{-1},
\]
respectively.
The six permutations fixing \(0,1\) individually give distinct orbit elements, with midpoint choices
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
r&\{0,1\}&\{0,2\}&\{2,3\}\\ \hline
1&2&3&1\\
2&2&4&0\\
3&3&1&0\\
4&3&4&1\\
5&4&1&4\\
6&4&3&4
\end{array}
\]
because only the identity in \(K\) fixes both points.
For the three pair representatives, respectively,
\[
\sum_rn_{r,B}n_{r,C}^{\mathsf T}
=3I_2,\quad
-\tfrac32I_2,\quad
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:johnson-five-projection} divides these by \(12\): by \(6\) for the orbit sum and by \(2\) for the normalized basis \((F_1,F_3)\).

To express \(\mathsf A\) on the ideals for \(\bullet\), define coefficients \(d_{\mu\nu}\) and operators \(D_{BC}\in\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Hom}(V_C,V_B))\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(
xE_0+\frac y5e_{(2,2,1)}+\frac z4e_{(2,1,1,1)}
\right)_{CB}(F_\nu)
&=\sum_{\mu=0}^3 d_{\mu\nu}F_\mu,\\
D_{BC}(a)
&:=2\sum_{\mu,\nu=0}^3d_{\mu\nu}F_\nu aF_\mu.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(0\le\mu,\nu\le3\), \(F_\nu\in\operatorname{End}(V_B)\), and \(F_\mu\in\operatorname{End}(V_C)\).
Tracing the composition of this \((C,B)\)-component with \(2a(\cdot)b^*\) gives \(\operatorname{Tr}(b^*D_{BC}(a))\), hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:johnson-component-pairing}
\mathsf A(w)
=\sum_{B,C}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(D_{BC}[w_{BC}]\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here the trace is on \(\operatorname{Hom}(V_C,V_B)\).
At the three representatives above,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:johnson-component-operators}
\begin{aligned}
D_{BB}(a)
&=\frac{x}{10}a
 +\frac y{20}(\sigma_1a\sigma_1+\sigma_3a\sigma_3)
 +\frac z{10}\sigma_2a\sigma_2,\\
D_{BC}(a)
&=\frac{x}{10}a
 -\frac y{40}(\sigma_1a\sigma_1+\sigma_3a\sigma_3)
 -\frac z{20}\sigma_2a\sigma_2
&&(|B\cap C|=1),\\
D_{BC}(a)
&=\frac{x}{10}a+\frac y{20}\sigma_3a\sigma_3
&&(B\cap C=\varnothing).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For \(B=\{0,1\}\) and \(C=\{0,2\}\), choose a real orthonormal eigenbasis of \(V_B\) for the nonidentity element of the pair stabilizer, with eigenvalues \(1,-1\), and transport it to \(V_C\) by \((1\,2)\).
This transposition matches the initial bases, so the same real orthogonal change applies in both components.
It preserves \(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\sigma_1,\sigma_3\}\) orthogonally and sends \(\sigma_2\) to \(\pm\sigma_2\), leaving the middle expression in~\eqref{eq:johnson-component-operators} unchanged.
In these bases, identify \(\operatorname{Hom}(V_C,V_B)\) with \(M_2(\mathbb{C})\), and let \(E_{ij}\) denote its matrix units.
The nonidentity element of the pair stabilizer has eigenspaces with ordered bases \((E_{11},E_{22})\) and \((E_{12},E_{21})\).
In these bases, the restrictions of \(D_{BC}\) have matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:johnson-two-matrices}
\frac1{40}
\begin{pmatrix}
4x-y&-y-2z\\
-y-2z&4x-y
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\frac1{40}
\begin{pmatrix}
4x+y&-y+2z\\
-y+2z&4x+y
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Diagonalizing gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:johnson-density-eigenvalues}
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
|B\cap C|&\text{eigenvalues}&\text{multiplicities}\\ \hline
2&
\dfrac1{10},\
\dfrac{x-y+z}{10},\
\dfrac{x-z}{10}
&1,1,2\\[4pt]
1&
\dfrac{2x-y-z}{20},\
\dfrac{2x+z}{20},\
\dfrac{2x+y-z}{20}
&1,2,1\\[4pt]
0&
\dfrac{2x+y}{20},\
\dfrac{2x-y}{20}
&2,2
\end{array}
\end{equation}

For the functionals in~\eqref{eq:johnson-functional-parameters}, we now verify that these eigenvalue tests are equivalent to positivity on \(R_a^+\).
For \((V_a,\bullet,\ast)\cong\mathbb{C}\), Proposition~\ref{prop:johnson-schur-decomposition} gives \(z_a\in R_a^+\).
The nonzero positive map \(z_a\) cannot annihilate \(1_A\), so
\(\langle1_A,z_a(1_A)\rangle>0\) and
\[
R_a^+=\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}z_a.
\]
For either simple ideal of \((W,\bullet,\ast)\) isomorphic to \(M_2(\mathbb{C})\), the unit eigenvectors of \(D_{BC}\) in~\eqref{eq:johnson-two-matrices} are
\[
q=\frac{E_{11}\pm E_{22}}{\sqrt2}
\quad\text{or}\quad
q=\frac{E_{12}\pm E_{21}}{\sqrt2}.
\]
Take \(u=(1,1)^{\mathsf T}\in V_B\) and \(v=(1,\pm1)^{\mathsf T}\in V_C\) in the chosen bases, with the sign such that the projection of \(uv^*\) onto the relevant eigenspace is \(\pm\sqrt2q\), and set
\[
\chi_B:=uu^*,\qquad
\chi_C:=vv^*,\qquad
\chi_D:=0\quad(D\notin\{B,C\}).
\]
Equivariance under the transposition exchanging \(B,C\) identifies \([w_{BC}]\) with \([w_{CB}]\) in these bases.
The two ordered-pair contributions, with the trace and correspondence normalizations, give
\[
0\le\langle\chi,w(\chi)\rangle
=16\langle q,[w_{BC}]q\rangle
\qquad(w\in R_a^+).
\]
Thus each functional \(w\mapsto\langle q,[w_{BC}]q\rangle\) is nonnegative on \(R_a^+\).
Equivariance and~\eqref{eq:johnson-component-pairing} then show that nonnegative eigenvalues suffice for \(\mathsf A_a\ge0\) on \(R_a^+\).
Conversely, the rank-one projection onto each displayed \(q\) commutes with the stabilizer and extends equivariantly to an element positive for \(\bullet\), hence in \(R_a^+\).
Testing it forces the corresponding eigenvalue to be nonnegative.
This proves the equivalence; the Hermitian operators in~\eqref{eq:johnson-functional-parameters} and~\eqref{eq:johnson-component-operators} give the required involution identities.

Using \(x+y+z=1\), nonnegativity in~\eqref{eq:johnson-density-eigenvalues} is equivalent to~\eqref{eq:johnson-feasible-parameters}.
Testing only the minimal central projections of \((W,\bullet,\ast)\) replaces positive semidefiniteness of the two matrices in~\eqref{eq:johnson-two-matrices} by their trace conditions \(4x-y\ge0\) and \(4x+y\ge0\).
The retained inequality \(2x-y\ge0\) from \(B\cap C=\varnothing\) implies both, leaving exactly the weaker inequality \(y\le2x\).
