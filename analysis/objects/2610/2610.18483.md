<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $\kappa\mapsto(  1-\mu(  d)  \,K_{\kappa})  $ is
analytic and $\kappa\mapsto\mu(  d)  \,K_{\kappa}$ compact-operator
valued, the meromorphic Fredholm theory applies, see \cite[Theorem
XIII.13]{ReSi IV}. Hence, either the map (\ref{Op_pencil_inv}) is finitely
meromorphic in $\mathbb{C}$ or $(1-\mu(  d)  \,K_{\kappa})$ is
nowhere invertible. We distinguish the two cases: 1) $1\notin\sigma\left(
\mu(  d)   K_{0}\right)  $; 2) $1\in\sigma\left(  \mu\left(
d\right)   K_{0}\right)  $ is a simple eigenvalue.

1) If $1\notin\sigma\left(  \mu(  d)   K_{0}\right)  $, then
$\ker\left(  1-\mu(  d)   K_{0}\right)  =\varnothing$. Hence
$(  1-\mu(  d)  \,K_{\kappa})  ^{-1}\in\mathscr{B} 
(H^{-1/2}(\Gamma))$ exists by the Fredholm alternative and, by continuity of
$\kappa\mapsto(  1-\mu(  d)  \,K_{\kappa})  $, the
operators $(  1-\mu(  d)  \,K_{\kappa})  ^{-1} 
\in\mathscr{B}(H^{-1/2}(\Gamma))$ exist in an open neighbourhood of $\kappa=0$.
Henceforth (\ref{Op_pencil_inv}) is finitely meromorphic in $\mathbb{C}$.

2) Let $1\in\sigma\left(  \mu(  d)   K_{0}\right)  $ be simple and
assume that $(  1-\mu(  d)  \,K_{\kappa})  $ is not
invertible at any $\kappa\in\mathbb{C}$. We show that this statement leads to
a contradiction. By the Fredholm alternative, $\ker\left(  1-\mu\left(
d\right)  \,K_{\kappa}\right)  \neq\left\{  0\right\}  $, which is equivalent
to say that, for each $\kappa$, there exists a non-trivial solution
$\varphi(\kappa)\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)$ of the eigenvalue problem 
\[
\mu(  d)  \,K_{\kappa}\varphi(\kappa)=\varphi(  \kappa)
\,.
\]
We work in a disk ${D}_{\delta}$ with $\delta>0$ small and denote 
\[
{D}_{\delta}^{+}:=\left\{  \kappa\in\mathbb{C}_{+}:\left\vert \kappa
\right\vert <\delta\right\}  \,.
\]
Since $\kappa\mapsto\mu(  d)  \,K_{\kappa}$ is analytic and
$1\in\sigma\left(  \mu(  d)   K_{0}\right)  $ is not-degenerate,
the Kato-Rellich theorem applies (e.g. \cite[Theorem XII.8]{ReSi IV}).
Therefore, the eigencouple $(1,\varphi(  \kappa)  )$ is analytic in
$D_{\delta}$. Let us define $\psi(\kappa):=S\!L_{\kappa}\varphi(\kappa)$. By
the trace maps properties of the single-layer operator, one gets
\begin{equation}
\left[  \gamma_{0}\right]  \psi(\kappa)=0\,,\qquad\left[  \gamma_{1}\right]
\psi(\kappa)=-\varphi(  \kappa)  =-\mu(  d)
\,\gamma_{1}\psi(\kappa)\,. \label{transmission_bc_id} 
\end{equation}
For $\kappa\in{D}_{\delta}^{+}$, the well-known mapping properties (see e.g.
in \cite[Theorem 6.12 and successive remarks]{McLe}), the first jump condition
in (\ref{transmission_bc_id}) and the identity 
\begin{equation}
-\Delta S\!L_{\kappa}\varphi(\kappa)|\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma=\kappa^{2}S\!L_{\kappa}\varphi
(\kappa)|\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma\,,\label{SL_id} 
\end{equation}
imply $\psi(\kappa)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L_{\Delta}^{2}(\mathbb{R} 
^{3}\backslash\Gamma)$. Define $L_{1,d}$ as in (\ref{L_cd_def})-(\ref{L_d_dom})
with $c_{\ell}=1$ for all $\ell$ and $d=\left(  d_{1},...d_{\ell}\right)  $.
Then, (\ref{transmission_bc_id}) corresponds to the boundary conditions
(\ref{transmission_bc}). It follows that $\psi(\kappa)\in\mathcal{D}_{d}$ and,
using (\ref{L_cd_def}) and (\ref{SL_id}), one obtains 
\[
L_{1,d}\psi(\kappa)|\Omega_{\mathrm{ex}}=\kappa^{2} 
\psi(\kappa)|\Omega_{\mathrm{ex}}\,,\qquad L_{1,d} 
\psi(\kappa)|\Omega_{\ell}=\kappa^{2}d_{\ell}\psi(\kappa)|\Omega_{\ell}\,.
\]
Therefore, for any $\kappa\in{D}_{\delta}^{+}$, one gets the identities

\label{Rem-res}
Case 1) If $\rho_{0}/\lambda_{\circ}<0$ then 
$$

$$
\kappa_{\pm}(\varepsilon)=
\pm i\sqrt{2}\,\left({\rho_{0}}/
\lambda_{\circ}\right)^{1/2} \|\varphi_{\circ}\| _{\hat{H}^{-1/2}(
\Gamma)  }\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,(1+O(  \varepsilon))\pm \sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\xi(\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,)
$$
We take in account Remark \ref{rem-res}. In Case 1, since there are no positive eigenvalues, both $\kappa^{2}_{\pm}(\varepsilon)$ are resonances.
In Case 2, $\kappa^{2}_{-}(\varepsilon)$ is a resonance and $\kappa^{2}_{+}(\varepsilon)$ is a negative eigenvalue. Hence, one has the following scenery:

\item{case $\lambda_{\circ}>0$)}  the shift of  $\widetilde d^{-1}$ from $\lambda_{\circ}$ to the right ($\rho_{0}<0$)  generates two branches of resonances,  emanating from the zero eigenvalue. Conversely, the shift of  $\widetilde d^{-1}$ from $\lambda_{\circ}$ to the left ($\rho_{0}>0$)  generates two branches, one consisting of negative eigenvalues and the other of resonances. 

\item{case $\lambda_{\circ}<0$)} the previous results are reversed: the shift to the left   generates two branches of resonances and the shift to the right generates one branch consisting of negative eigenvalues and another one consisting of resonances.
\vskip5pt\noindent
Summing up: if $\widetilde d^{-1}(\varepsilon)/\lambda_{\circ}>1$, then two branches of resonances are created. Otherwise,  if $\widetilde d^{-1}(\varepsilon)/\lambda_{\circ}<1$,  then two branches, one consisting of negative eigenvalues and the other of resonances, are created.
