\label{p:proprieta hsigma}
Assume \textup{(H3)-(H4)}. Then $h_{\sigma_0} \in C^{0}(\mathbb R^{N\times n})$ (see \eqref{eq:def-h-varsigma}) for every $\sigma_0 \in (0,\infty)$, and it satisfies the bounds
\begin{equation}\label{e:la h al variare di sigma}
        h_{\sigma_0}(\xi)\leq h_{\sigma_1}(\xi)\leq (\sigma_1/\sigma_0)^2h_{\sigma_0}(\xi) \quad \quad \textup{for every $\xi\in \mathbb R^{N\times n}$},
    \end{equation}
for all pairs $\sigma_0,\sigma_1 \in (0,\infty)$ with $\sigma_0\leq \sigma_1$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We fix $\sigma_0 \in (0,\infty)$ and first verify the continuity of $h_{\sigma_0}$. Let $\xi_0 \in \mathbb R^{N \times n}$ and consider an arbitrary sequence $\xi_k \to \xi_0$ as $k \to \infty$. For each index $k$, we can select $\tau_k \in [0,\infty)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{e:prima eq prop di h}
\varphi\left(\tfrac{1}{\tau_k}\right)\Phi(\xi_k) + \tfrac{\sigma_0^2}{4}\tau_k - \tfrac{1}{k} < h_{\sigma_0}(\xi_k).
\end{equation}
Since $\sup_{k \in \mathbb N} \Phi(\xi_k) < \infty$ and $\Phi$ is continuous, definition \eqref{eq:def-h-varsigma} ensures that the sequence $(\tau_k)$ is bounded, i.e., $\limsup_{k \to \infty} \tau_k < \infty$. Hence, we may extract a convergent subsequence $(\tau_{k_j})$ such that $\tau_{k_j} \to \tau_0$ for some $\tau_0 \in [0,\infty)$. Relying on the continuity of both $\varphi$ and $\Phi$, passing to the limit in \eqref{e:prima eq prop di h} yields
\begin{equation*}
h_{\sigma_0}(\xi_0) \leq \varphi\left(\frac{1}{\tau_0}\right)\Phi(\xi_0) + \frac{\sigma_0^2}{4}\tau_0
\leq \liminf_{j \to \infty} h_{\sigma_0}(\xi_{k_j}).
\end{equation*}
Since the point $\xi_0$ and the converging sequence $(\xi_k)$ were chosen arbitrarily, we conclude that $h_{\sigma_0}$ is lower semicontinuous. The upper semicontinuity is a direct consequence of the definition of $h_{\sigma_0}$ as an infimum of continuous functions and the continuity of $\Phi$.

Now consider $\sigma_0, \sigma_1 \in (0,\infty)$ with $\sigma_0 \leq \sigma_1$. The first inequality in \eqref{e:la h al variare di sigma} follows trivially from the definition \eqref{eq:def-h-varsigma}. To verify the second, fix any $\xi \in \mathbb R^{N \times n}$. By the continuity of $\varphi$, the infimum is attained, so there exists $\tau_{\sigma_0,\xi} \in [0,\infty)$ satisfying
\begin{equation*}
h_{\sigma_0}(\xi) = \varphi\left(\frac{1}{\tau_{\sigma_0,\xi}}\right)\Phi(\xi) + \frac{\sigma_0^2}{4}\tau_{\sigma_0,\xi}.
\end{equation*}
Exploiting the fact that $(\sigma_1/\sigma_0)^2 \geq 1$, we can easily deduce
\begin{equation*}
h_{\sigma_1}(\xi) \leq \varphi\left(\frac{1}{\tau_{\sigma_0,\xi}}\right)\Phi(\xi) + \frac{\sigma_1^2}{4}\tau_{\sigma_0,\xi}
\leq \left(\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_0}\right)^2 h_{\sigma_0}(\xi),
\end{equation*}
which validates the claimed inequality.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:bulk-growth-section2}
Assume \textnormal{(H3)}-\textnormal{(H5)} and let $\sigma\in (0,\infty)$. Then there exists a positive constant $K_{\sigma}>0$ such that the following growth estimates hold:
\begin{equation}\label{e:bulk-growth-h}
 \Big(\frac1{K_{\sigma}}|\xi|-K_{\sigma}\Big)\vee 0
 \le h_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{qc}}(\xi)
 \le h_{\sigma}(\xi)
 \le K_{\sigma}(1+|\xi|)
 \qquad\text{for all }\xi\in \mathbb R^{N\times n},
\end{equation}
and correspondingly for the recession function,
\begin{equation}\label{e:bulk-growth-hrecession}
 \frac1{K_{\sigma}}|\xi|\le h_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{qc},\infty}(\xi)\le K_{\sigma}|\xi|
 \qquad\text{for all }\xi\in \mathbb R^{N\times n}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let us introduce the auxiliary scalar function defined for $t\in \mathbb R$ as
\begin{equation*}
\tilde h_{\textup{scal}}(t):=\inf_{\tau\in [0,\infty)}
\left\{\varphi\left(\frac1\tau\right)\frac{1}{C_\Phi}t^2 +\frac{\sigma^2}{4}\tau\right\},
\end{equation*}
where $C_\Phi$ is the coercivity constant appearing in \eqref{eq:growth-Phi}. From this definition, it immediately follows that
\begin{equation*}
\tilde h_{\textup{scal}}(|\xi|)\leq h_{\sigma}(\xi)+C_\Phi\,\varphi(\infty)
\qquad\text{for every }\xi\in \mathbb R^{N\times n}.
\end{equation*}
Invoking \cite[Proposition 2.1, item ii]{ACF26}, we can find a sufficiently large constant $K_{\sigma}$ such that
\begin{equation*}
    \Big(\frac1{K_{\sigma}}|\xi|-K_{\sigma}\Big)\lor 0
 \le  ({\tilde h}^{**}_{\textup{scal}}(|\xi|)-C_\Phi\varphi(\infty))\lor 0 \leq h_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{qc}}(\xi),
\end{equation*}
where $\tilde h^{**}_{\textup{scal}}$ denotes the convex envelope of $\tilde h_{\textup{scal}}$. In a similar way, we define another scalar function to govern the upper bound:
\begin{equation*}
\hat h_{\textup{scal}}(t):=\inf_{\tau\in[0,\infty)}
\left\{\varphi\left(\frac1\tau\right)C_\Phi t^2 +\frac{\sigma^2}{4}\tau\right\},
\qquad t\in\mathbb R.
\end{equation*}
Thanks to \eqref{eq:growth-Phi}, we trivially have $h_{\sigma}(\xi)\leq \hat h_{\textup{scal}}(|\xi|)+C\varphi(\infty)$ for every $\xi\in\mathbb R^{N\times n}$. Furthermore, applying again \cite[Proposition 2.1, item ii]{ACF26}, we infer that $\hat h_{\textup{scal}}(|\xi|)\leq K_\sigma\bigl(|\xi|+1\bigr)$ for all $\xi\in\mathbb R^{N\times n}$, slightly enlarging the constant $K_{\sigma}$ if necessary. This establishes the third inequality in \eqref{e:bulk-growth-h}. 

Finally, combining these growth conditions with the definition of the recession function $h^{\mathrm{qc},\infty}_\sigma$ stated in \eqref{eq:def-h-recession}, we deduce \eqref{e:bulk-growth-hrecession}, completing the proof.

$\varsigma\in(0,\infty)$, and suppose that $\Phi^{1/2}$ is convex.

\quad\text{for }\tau>0,

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By definition \eqref{e:h norm generic} we have that $h_\varsigma^{\mathrm{scal}}$ is non-decreasing. Moreover, from the identity 
\begin{equation*}
    2zt-z^2q(\tau)= \frac{t^2}{q(\tau)}- q(\tau)\left(z-\frac{t}{q(\tau)}\right)^2,
\end{equation*}
and the positivity of $q$, we deduce the following
\[
r(\tau)t^2
=
\frac{t^2}{q(\tau)}
=
\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
\bigl\{2zt-z^2q(\tau)\bigr\}
\]
showing the jointly convexity of $(t,\tau)\mapsto r(\tau)t^2$.
Thus the map $(t,\tau)\longmapsto
r(\tau)t^2+\frac{\varsigma^2}{4}\tau$ is jointly convex as well, and its partial infimum with respect

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{l:5.3}
Let $\varepsilon>0$ and $S_\varepsilon:H^1(A;\mathbb{R}^N) \times L^1(A;[0,1])\to [0,\infty]$ be the functional defined by
\begin{equation*}
    S_\varepsilon(u,v) := \int_A \varphi(\varepsilon f^2(v))\Phi(\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x.
\end{equation*}
where $A\in \mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Then for every $M \in \mathbb{N}$ and $(u,v) \in H^1(A;\mathbb{R}^N) \times L^1(A;[0,1])$ there exists $k \in \{M + 1, \dots, 2M\}$ such that 
\begin{equation*}
    S_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_k(u),v) \leq \left(1 + \frac{C_\Phi^2}{M}\right) S_{\varepsilon}(u,v) + \varphi(\infty)(\Phi(0)+C_\Phi) \mathcal{L}^n(|u| > a_M).
\end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let us fix $M \in \mathbb{N}$ and $(u,v) \in H^1(A;\mathbb{R}^N) \times L^1(A;[0,1])$ with $S_\varepsilon(u,v) < \infty$, otherwise the claim follows trivially. By averaging, there exists $k \in \{M + 1, \dots, 2M\}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{e:5.11}
    \int_{A \cap \{a_k \leq |u| < a_{k+1}\}} \varphi(\varepsilon f^2(v))\Phi(\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x \leq \frac{1}{M} S_\varepsilon(u,v).
\end{equation}
By the properties of $GBV$ functions and the very definition of $\mathcal{T}_k$, we have that $\mathcal{T}_k(u)$ belongs to $BV \cap L^\infty(A;\mathbb{R}^N)$. Furthermore, being $\mathrm{Lip}(\mathcal{T}_k) \leq 1$, we can check for every $y,w \in \mathbb{R}^N$ with $|w| = 1$ that $|(\nabla \mathcal{T}_k(y))w| = |\partial_w \mathcal{T}_k(y)| \leq 1$ that provides $\|\nabla \mathcal{T}_k(y)\|_2 \leq 1$ for every $y \in \mathbb{R}^N$ (here $\|\cdot\|_2$ stands for the matrix norm on $\mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ induced by $|\cdot|$ on $\mathbb{R}^N$) and consequently
\begin{equation}\label{e:5.12}
    |\nabla(\mathcal{T}_k(u))(x)| \leq |\nabla u(x)| \quad \text{for } \mathcal{L}^n\text{-a.e.\ } x \in A.
\end{equation}
In particular, in virtue of $\mathcal{T}_k(y)= y$ on $\{|y| < a_k\}$, $\mathcal{T}_k(y)= 0$ on $\{|y| \geq a_{k+1}\}$ and \eqref{eq:growth-Phi}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
    S_\varepsilon(\mathcal{T}_k(u),v) &= \int_{A \cap \{|u| < a_k\}} \varphi(\varepsilon f^2(v))\Phi(\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x 
    + \int_{A \cap \{a_k \leq |u| < a_{k+1}\}} \varphi(\varepsilon f^2(v))\Phi(\nabla (\mathcal{T}_k(u)))\,\mathrm{d}x \\
    &\quad + \int_{A \cap \{|u| \geq a_{k+1}\}} \varphi(\varepsilon f^2(v))\Phi(0)\,\mathrm{d}x \\
    &\leq S_\varepsilon(u,v) + C_\Phi \int_{A \cap \{a_k \leq |u| < a_{k+1}\}} \varphi(\varepsilon f^2(v))|\nabla(\mathcal{T}_k(u))|^2\,\mathrm{d}x + \varphi(\infty)(\Phi(0)+C_\Phi)\mathcal{L}^n(|u| \geq a_{k}) \\
    &\leq S_\varepsilon(u,v) + C_\Phi\int_{A \cap \{a_k \leq |u| < a_{k+1}\}} \varphi(\varepsilon f^2(v))|\nabla u|^2\,\mathrm{d}x + \varphi(\infty)(\Phi(0)+C_\Phi)\mathcal{L}^n(|u| \geq a_{k+1}) \\
    &\leq \left(1 + \frac{C_\Phi^2}{M}\right) S_\varepsilon(u,v) + \varphi(\infty)(\Phi(0)+C_\Phi) \mathcal{L}^n(|u| > a_M),
\end{align*}
concluding the proof.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{l:quasi monotonia funzione di degradamento}
Assume \textnormal{(H1)}, \textnormal{(H3)}, and \textnormal{(H4)}. Then, there exists a constant $K\ge 1$ such that, for every $\varepsilon>0$ and every pair $t_1,t_2\in [0,1)$ satisfying $t_1\le t_2$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison-f-phi-surface}
 f^2(t_1)\le K f^2(t_2),
 \qquad
 \varphi\big(\varepsilon f^2(t_1)\big)\le K\varphi\big(\varepsilon f^2(t_2)\big).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By assumption \textnormal{(H1)}, there exist $0<r_1<r_2<1$ such that $f$ is nondecreasing on both $[0,r_1]$ and $[r_2,1)$. Since $f$ is continuous and strictly positive on $[r_1,r_2]$, one can find a constant $c_0\ge 1$ guaranteeing $f^2(t_1)\le c_0 f^2(t_2)$ whenever $t_1\le t_2$.

We then introduce $\widehat\varphi(t):=\varphi(c_0t)/\varphi(t)$ for $t>0$. Owing to \textnormal{(H3)} and \textnormal{(H4)}, $\widehat\varphi$ is continuous on $(0,\infty)$ and exhibits the limits
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow 0}\widehat\varphi(t)=c_0,
 \qquad
 \lim_{t\to\infty}\widehat\varphi(t)=1.
\]
Consequently, $c_1:=\sup_{t>0}\widehat\varphi(t)<\infty$, which implies $\varphi(c_0 t)\le c_1\varphi(t)$ for all $t\ge 0$. The second inequality in \eqref{eq:comparison-f-phi-surface} is thus an immediate consequence.

\label{l:g limitata}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-anisotropic-gamma-convergence}, there exists a constant $L>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-uniform-bound}

\end{equation}

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Fix $(\zeta,\nu)\in \mathbb R^N\times \mathbb S^{n-1}$ and let $\varepsilon_j\downarrow 0$. We construct the test functions
\[
 u_j(x):=\widehat u_j(x\cdot\nu),
 \qquad
 v_j(x):=\widehat v_j(x\cdot\nu),
\]
where the one-dimensional profiles are defined as
\[
 \widehat u_j(t):=
 \begin{cases}
 0,& t\le -\varepsilon_j,\\
 \zeta,& t\ge \varepsilon_j,\\
 \textup{affine interpolation},& -\varepsilon_j<t<\varepsilon_j,
 \end{cases}
\qquad
 \widehat v_j(t):=
 \begin{cases}
 0,& |t|\le \varepsilon_j,\\
 1,& |t|\ge 2\varepsilon_j,\\
 \textup{affine interpolation},& \varepsilon_j<|t|<2\varepsilon_j.
 \end{cases}
\]
It is evident that $u_j\to u_{\zeta,\nu}$ in $L^1(Q^\nu;\mathbb R^N)$ and $0\le v_j\le 1$. Since the support of $\nabla u_j$ is strictly contained within the strip $\{|x\cdot\nu|<\varepsilon_j\}$, where $v_j=0$, the bulk term in $\mathcal F^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(u_j,v_j;Q^\nu)$ vanishes identically. Hence, taking into account that $\omega$ is non-decreasing and the equivalence of $\gamma$ with the Euclidean norm (cf. \eqref{e:gamma lower euclidean suppenergy}), we obtain
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal F^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(u_j,v_j;Q^\nu)
 &=\int_{Q^\nu\cap\{|x\cdot\nu|\le 2\varepsilon_j\}}\frac{\omega(1-v_j)}{4\varepsilon_j}\,dx
 +\int_{Q^\nu\cap\{\varepsilon_j\le |x\cdot\nu|\le 2\varepsilon_j\}}\varepsilon_j\gamma(\nabla v_j)^2\,dx\\
 &\le \omega(1)+C_\gamma^2\int_{Q^\nu\cap\{\varepsilon_j\le |x\cdot\nu|\le 2\varepsilon_j\}}\varepsilon_j|\nabla v_j|^2\,dx\leq \omega(1)+2C_\gamma^2.
\end{align*}
Passing to the lower limit in the definition of $g$ immediately yields the claim.

\label{proplbboundary-gamma}
Let $(\zeta,\nu)\in\mathbb{R}^N\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ and suppose that an
optimizing sequence in \eqref{eq:def-g-gamma} converges in
$L^2(Q^\nu,\mathbb{R}^{N+1})$. Then, under the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-anisotropic-gamma-convergence}, there exist
a sequence $\varepsilon_j\to0$ and a sequence
\[
 (\tilde u_j,\tilde v_j)\to(u_{\zeta,\nu},1)
 \qquad\text{in }L^2(Q^\nu,\mathbb{R}^{N+1}),
\]
with $\tilde v_j\in[0,1]$ for $\mathcal{L}^n$-a.e.\ in $Q^\nu$,
such that
\begin{equation*}
 \lim_{j\to\infty}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (\tilde u_j,\tilde v_j;Q^\nu)
 =g(\zeta,\nu),
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eqpropboundrybv-gamma}
 \tilde u_j=(u_{\zeta,\nu})\ast\phi_{\varepsilon_j},
 \qquad
 \tilde v_j=
 \mathbf 1_{\{|x\cdot\nu|\ge2\varepsilon_j\}}\ast\phi_{\varepsilon_j}
 \qquad\text{on }\partial Q^\nu.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Step 1. Construction of $\tilde u_j$ and $\tilde v_j$.}

By hypothesis, there exist a sequence $\varepsilon_j\to0$ and functions
\[
 (u_j,v_j)\to(u_{\zeta,\nu},1)
 \qquad\text{in }L^2(Q^\nu,\mathbb{R}^{N+1}),
\]
with $0\le v_j\le1$ a.e.\ in $Q^\nu$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{e:successione-minimizzante-gamma-boundary}
 g(\zeta,\nu)
 =
 \lim_{j\to\infty}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (u_j,v_j;Q^\nu).
\end{equation}
For notational convenience, set
\begin{equation}\label{eqdefUjVj-gamma}
 U_j:=(u_{\zeta,\nu})\ast\phi_{\varepsilon_j},
 \qquad
 V_j:=\mathbf 1_{\{|x\cdot\nu|\ge2\varepsilon_j\}}\ast\phi_{\varepsilon_j}.
\end{equation}
Then $\|u_j-U_j\|_{L^2(Q^\nu,\mathbb{R}^N)}\to0$.
Moreover, $U_j=u_{\zeta,\nu}$ on $\{|x\cdot\nu|\ge\varepsilon_j\}$,
whereas $V_j=0$ on $\{|x\cdot\nu|\le\varepsilon_j\}$ and $V_j=1$
on $\{|x\cdot\nu|\ge3\varepsilon_j\}$.
In particular, $V_j=0$ on $\{\nabla U_j\ne0\}$.
Since $f(0)=0$ and $\Phi_\infty(0)=0$, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-energy-gamma}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(U_j,V_j;Q^\nu)
 =
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(0,V_j;Q^\nu)\leq c,
\end{equation}
because $\gamma(\nabla V_j)
 \le C_\gamma|\nabla V_j|
 \le\frac{c}{\varepsilon_j}$, by \eqref{e:gamma lower euclidean suppenergy}, and $\nabla V_j$ is supported in a strip of thickness of order $\varepsilon_j$.
We now choose a sequence $\eta_j\downarrow0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:choice-eta-gamma}
 \frac{\varepsilon_j}{\eta_j}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{f^2(1-\eta_j)}{\eta_j}
 \|u_j-U_j\|_{L^2(Q^\nu)}^2\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
and we set
$K_j:=\left\lfloor\frac{\eta_j}{\varepsilon_j}\right\rfloor$.
For $k=K_j+1,\ldots,2K_j$, define
\[
 R_k^j
 :=
 Q^\nu_{1-k\varepsilon_j}
 \setminus
 Q^\nu_{1-(k+1)\varepsilon_j},
 \qquad
 S_k^j
 :=
 Q^\nu_{1-k\varepsilon_j}
 \setminus
 Q^\nu_{1-(k+3)\varepsilon_j}.
\]
For $j$ sufficiently large, these layers are contained in $Q^\nu$.
The layers $R_k^j$ are pairwise disjoint, while the layers $S_k^j$
have overlap multiplicity at most three a.e.\ in $Q^\nu$.
By \eqref{e:successione-minimizzante-gamma-boundary},
\eqref{eq:canonical-energy-gamma}, and an averaging argument,
there exists $k_j\in\{K_j+1,\ldots,2K_j\}$,
such that, setting $R_j:=R_{k_j}^j$ and $S_j:=S_{k_j}^j$,
one has
\begin{align}
 &\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(u_j,v_j;S_j)
 +\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(U_j,V_j;S_j)
 +\frac{f^2(1-\eta_j)}{\varepsilon_j}
   \|u_j-U_j\|_{L^2(R_j)}^2
 \le
 \frac{c}{K_j}
 +\frac{f^2(1-\eta_j)}{K_j\varepsilon_j}
  \|u_j-U_j\|_{L^2(Q^\nu)}^2,
 \label{eqchoice1-gamma}
\end{align}
where $K_j\to\infty$ and $K_j\varepsilon_j/\eta_j\to1$, so the
right-hand side converges to zero by \eqref{eq:choice-eta-gamma}.
Let $\theta_j\in C_c^1(Q^\nu_{1-k_j\varepsilon_j},[0,1])$ be such that
\[
 \theta_j=1
 \quad\text{on }Q^\nu_{1-(k_j+1)\varepsilon_j},
 \qquad
 |\nabla\theta_j|\le\frac{3}{\varepsilon_j},
\]
and define $\tilde u_j
 :=
 \theta_j u_j+(1-\theta_j)U_j.$ 
 
 We next construct $\tilde v_j$. First, define
\begin{equation}\label{eqdefhatv-gamma}
 \hat v_j(x)
 :=
 \min\left\{
 1,\,
 1-\eta_j+\frac{\textup{dist}(x,R_j)}{\varepsilon_j}
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
Thus,
\[
 \hat v_j=1-\eta_j\quad\text{on }R_j,
 \qquad
 \hat v_j=1
 \quad\text{if }\textup{dist}(x,R_j)\ge\eta_j\varepsilon_j.
\]
Next, set
\begin{equation}\label{eqdefhatV-gamma}
 \hat V_j(x)
 :=
 \min\left\{
 1,\,
 V_j(x)
 +\frac{
   \textup{dist}\bigl(
    x,Q^\nu\setminus Q^\nu_{1-(k_j+1)\varepsilon_j}
   \bigr)
  }{\varepsilon_j}
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
Then $\hat V_j=V_j$ outside
$Q^\nu_{1-(k_j+1)\varepsilon_j}$, while $\hat V_j=1$ sufficiently far
inside this cube, and $\hat V_j=1$ on
$\{|x\cdot\nu|\ge3\varepsilon_j\}$.
Finally, define
\begin{equation*}
 v_j^\ast(x)
 :=
 \min\left\{
 1,\,
 v_j(x)
 +\frac{2}{k_j\varepsilon_j}
  \textup{dist}\bigl(x,Q^\nu_{1-k_j\varepsilon_j}\bigr)
 \right\}
\end{equation*}
and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:def-tilde-v-gamma}
 \tilde v_j:=v_j^\ast\wedge\hat V_j\wedge\hat v_j.
\end{equation}
By construction $0\le\tilde v_j\le1$.
Moreover, on $\partial Q^\nu$, both $v_j^\ast$ and $\hat v_j$
are equal to $1$ for $j$ sufficiently large, consequently
$\tilde v_j=\hat V_j=V_j$ on $\partial Q^\nu$.
Together with the definition of $\tilde u_j$, this proves
the boundary condition \eqref{eqpropboundrybv-gamma}.

\noindent
\textbf{Step 2. Estimate of the bulk term.}

On $R_j$ we have
\[
 \nabla\tilde u_j
 =
 \theta_j\nabla u_j
 +(1-\theta_j)\nabla U_j
 +(u_j-U_j)\otimes\nabla\theta_j,
\]
therefore,
\[
 |\nabla\tilde u_j|
 \le
 |\nabla u_j|+|\nabla U_j|
 +\frac{3}{\varepsilon_j}|u_j-U_j|.
\]
By the quadratic growth of $\Phi_\infty$
(cf.\ \eqref{eq:growth-Phi-infty}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-growth-tilde-u-gamma}
 \Phi_\infty(\nabla\tilde u_j)
 \le
 c\Phi_\infty(\nabla u_j)
 +c\Phi_\infty(\nabla U_j)
 +\frac{c}{\varepsilon_j^2}|u_j-U_j|^2
 \qquad\text{on }R_j.
\end{equation}
Moreover on $R_j$, $\tilde v_j\le v_j$, $\tilde v_j\le V_j$
and $\tilde v_j\le1-\eta_j$, thus
Lemma~\ref{l:quasi monotonia funzione di degradamento} gives
\[
 f^2(\tilde v_j)\le c f^2(v_j),
 \qquad
 f^2(\tilde v_j)\le c f^2(1-\eta_j).
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 \tilde v_j\le V_j=0
 \qquad\text{on }\{\nabla U_j\ne0\}\cap R_j,
\]
and hence
\[
 f^2(\tilde v_j)\Phi_\infty(\nabla U_j)=0
 \qquad\text{a.e.\ on }R_j.
\]
Multiplying \eqref{eq:bulk-growth-tilde-u-gamma} by
$\varepsilon_jf^2(\tilde v_j)$ yields
\begin{align}
 \varepsilon_j f^2(\tilde v_j)
 \Phi_\infty(\nabla\tilde u_j)
 \le
 c\varepsilon_j f^2(v_j)\Phi_\infty(\nabla u_j)
 +c\frac{f^2(1-\eta_j)}{\varepsilon_j}|u_j-U_j|^2
 \qquad\text{on }R_j.
 \label{eq:bulk-transition-gamma}
\end{align}
Integrating and using \eqref{eqchoice1-gamma}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 \int_{R_j}
 \varphi'(0)\varepsilon_j f^2(\tilde v_j)
 \Phi_\infty(\nabla\tilde u_j)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x
 \le
 \frac{c}{K_j}
 +c\frac{f^2(1-\eta_j)}{K_j\varepsilon_j}
 \|u_j-U_j\|_{L^2(Q^\nu)}^2,
\end{align*}
and hence by \eqref{eq:choice-eta-gamma},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-transition-vanishes-gamma}
 \limsup_{j\to\infty}
 \int_{R_j}
 \varphi'(0)\varepsilon_j f^2(\tilde v_j)
 \Phi_\infty(\nabla\tilde u_j)\,\textup{d}x
 =0.
\end{equation}
On $S_j\setminus R_j$, we have $\tilde u_j=u_j$ and
$\tilde v_j\le v_j$. Hence, by
Lemma~\ref{l:quasi monotonia funzione di degradamento} and
\eqref{eqchoice1-gamma},
\[
 \int_{S_j\setminus R_j}
 \varphi'(0)\varepsilon_j f^2(\tilde v_j)
 \Phi_\infty(\nabla\tilde u_j)\,\textup{d}x
 \le
 c\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(u_j,v_j;S_j)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Moreover, on $Q^\nu_{1-(k_j+3)\varepsilon_j}$ we have
$\hat V_j=\hat v_j=1$ for $j$ sufficiently large, and therefore
\[
 \tilde u_j=u_j,
 \qquad
 \tilde v_j=v_j.
\]
On the exterior region $\tilde u_j=U_j$, $\tilde v_j\le V_j$
and the supports of $\nabla U_j$ and $V_j$ are disjoint, thus
\[
 \int_{Q^\nu\setminus Q^\nu_{1-k_j\varepsilon_j}}
 \varphi'(0)\varepsilon_jf^2(\tilde v_j)
 \Phi_\infty(\nabla\tilde u_j)\,\textup{d}x=0.
\]
Combining these estimates, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eqfinelastcu-gamma}
 \int_{Q^\nu}
 \varphi'(0)\varepsilon_j f^2(\tilde v_j)
 \Phi_\infty(\nabla\tilde u_j)\,\textup{d}x
 \le
 \int_{Q^\nu}
 \varphi'(0)\varepsilon_j f^2(v_j)
 \Phi_\infty(\nabla u_j)\,\textup{d}x+o(1).
\end{equation}

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Step 3. Estimate of the anisotropic phase-field energy.}

From the monotonicity of $\omega$ we obtain
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,\tilde v_j;Q^\nu)
 \le
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,v_j^\ast;Q^\nu)
 +
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,\hat V_j;Q^\nu)
 +\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,\hat v_j;Q^\nu).
 \label{eqcalFst0-gamma}
\end{align}
We start with $\hat v_j$. By \eqref{eqdefhatv-gamma}, we have $0\le1-\hat v_j\le\eta_j$ and $|\nabla\hat v_j|\le\frac1{\varepsilon_j}$, with
\[
 \mathcal{L}^n(\{\hat v_j\ne1\})\le c\varepsilon_j,
 \qquad
 \mathcal{L}^n(\{\nabla\hat v_j\ne0\})
 \le c\eta_j\varepsilon_j,
 \qquad
 \gamma(\nabla\hat v_j)
 \le\frac{C_\gamma}{\varepsilon_j}.
\]
Setting $\tau_j:=\max_{t\in[0,\eta_j]}\omega(t)$, we have
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,\hat v_j;Q^\nu)
 =\int_{Q^\nu}
 \left(
  \frac{\omega(1-\hat v_j)}{4\varepsilon_j}
  +\varepsilon_j\gamma(\nabla\hat v_j)^2
 \right)\,\textup{d}x
 \le c\tau_j+c\eta_j.
 \label{eq:hat-v-energy-gamma}
\end{align}
Likewise, the definition of $\hat V_j$ gives
\[
 |\nabla\hat V_j|
 \le|\nabla V_j|+\frac1{\varepsilon_j},
 \qquad
 \gamma(\nabla\hat V_j)\le\frac{c}{\varepsilon_j},
 \qquad
 \mathcal{L}^n(\{\hat V_j\ne1\})\le c\eta_j\varepsilon_j,
\]
and consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hat-V-energy-gamma}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,\hat V_j;Q^\nu)
 \le c\eta_j.
\end{equation}
It remains to estimate $v_j^\ast$.
Set $E_j:=Q^\nu\setminus Q^\nu_{1-k_j\varepsilon_j}$ and observe that $v_j^\ast=v_j$ on $Q^\nu_{1-k_j\varepsilon_j}$ and $v_j^\ast\ge v_j$ on $Q^\nu$. Thus, by the monotonicity of $\omega$,
$\omega(1-v_j^\ast)\le\omega(1-v_j)$, and being the
distance function 1-Lipschitz,
\[
 \gamma(\nabla v_j^\ast)^2
 \le
 \gamma(\nabla v_j)^2
 +\frac{c}{k_j\varepsilon_j}\gamma(\nabla v_j)
 +\frac{c}{k_j^2\varepsilon_j^2}
 \qquad\text{on }E_j.
\]
Therefore,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,v_j^\ast;Q^\nu)
 \le
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,v_j;Q^\nu)
 +\frac{c\mathcal{L}^n(E_j)}{k_j^2\varepsilon_j}
 +
 \frac{c}{k_j}
 \int_{E_j}\gamma(\nabla v_j)\,\textup{d}x,
 \label{eq:vstar-preliminary-gamma}
\end{align}
where, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
\begin{align*}
 \frac1{k_j}\int_{E_j}\gamma(\nabla v_j)\,\textup{d}x
 \le
 \frac{\mathcal{L}^n(E_j)^{\frac12}}{k_j\varepsilon_j^{\frac12}}
 \left(
  \varepsilon_j
  \int_{E_j}\gamma(\nabla v_j)^2\,\textup{d}x
 \right)^{\frac12}
 \le
 \frac{\mathcal{L}^n(E_j)^{\frac12}}{k_j\varepsilon_j^{\frac12}}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,v_j;Q^\nu)^{\frac12}.
\end{align*}
Since $E_j$ is a boundary layer of thickness of order
$k_j\varepsilon_j$, we have $\mathcal{L}^n(E_j)\le ck_j\varepsilon_j$.
The energies
$\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(u_j,v_j;Q^\nu)$
are uniformly bounded by
\eqref{e:successione-minimizzante-gamma-boundary}, hence
\eqref{eq:vstar-preliminary-gamma} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vstar-final-gamma}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,v_j^\ast;Q^\nu)
 \le
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,v_j;Q^\nu)
 +\frac{c}{k_j}
 +\frac{c}{k_j^{1/2}},
\end{equation}
and, since $k_j\ge K_j+1\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vstar-limsup-gamma}
 \limsup_{j\to\infty}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,v_j^\ast;Q^\nu)
 \le
 \limsup_{j\to\infty}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,v_j;Q^\nu).
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eqcalFst0-gamma},
\eqref{eq:hat-v-energy-gamma},
\eqref{eq:hat-V-energy-gamma}, and
\eqref{eq:vstar-final-gamma}, and using
$\eta_j,\tau_j\to0$, we conclude that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-limsup-gamma}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,\tilde v_j;Q^\nu)
 \le
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (0,v_j;Q^\nu)+o(1).
\end{equation}
Finally, \eqref{eqfinelastcu-gamma} and
\eqref{eq:phase-limsup-gamma} imply
\[
 \limsup_{j\to\infty}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (\tilde u_j,\tilde v_j;Q^\nu)
 \le g(\zeta,\nu).
\]
By construction, $\tilde u_j\to u_{\zeta,\nu}$ in
$L^2(Q^\nu,\mathbb{R}^N)$.
Since $\tilde v_j=v_j$ on
$Q^\nu_{1-(k_j+3)\varepsilon_j}$, $0\le\tilde v_j\le1$, and
$(k_j+3)\varepsilon_j\to0$, we also have $\tilde v_j\to1$ in
$L^2(Q^\nu)$.
The reverse inequality follows from
\eqref{eq:def-g-gamma}, completing the proof.

\textbf{Step 1. Reduction to an optimal sequence converging in
$L^2$.} Consider a vanishing sequence $\varepsilon_j\to0$ and a pair $(u_j,v_j)$
such that $(u_j,v_j)\to (u_{\zeta,\nu},1)
 \text{ in }L^1(Q^\nu;\mathbb{R}^{N+1})$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimal-L1-periodicity-gamma}
 g(\zeta,\nu)
 = \lim_{j\to\infty}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(u_j,v_j;Q^\nu).
\end{equation}
Since $0\le v_j\le1$, we have that $v_j\to 1$ strongly in $L^2(Q^{\nu})$. We assert that, for every $j,M\in\mathbb{N}$, there exists $ k_{M,j}\in\{M+1,\ldots,2M\}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{e:truncation g}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 \bigl(\mathcal T_{k_{M,j}}(u_j),v_j;Q^\nu\bigr)
 \le
 \left(1+\frac{C^2}{M}\right)
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 (u_j,v_j;Q^\nu),
\end{equation}
where the functions $\mathcal{T}_k$ are defined in \eqref{e:Tk}, and we assume that
\[
 a_M>1+|\zeta|
 =1+\|u_{\zeta,\nu}\|_{L^\infty(Q^\nu)}.
\]
For fixed
$M$, the functions
$\mathcal T_{k_{M,j}}(u_j)$ are uniformly bounded and converge
to $u_{\zeta,\nu}$ in $L^2(Q^\nu,\mathbb{R}^N)$. Applying \eqref{e:truncation g}, we obtain
\[
 \limsup_{j\to\infty}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 \bigl(\mathcal T_{k_{M,j}}(u_j),v_j;Q^\nu\bigr)
 \le
 \left(1+\frac{C^2}{M}\right)g(\zeta,\nu).
\]
Letting $M\to\infty$ and using a diagonal argument, we deduce
\begin{align}
 g(\zeta,\nu)
 =\inf\Bigl\{
 \liminf_{j\to\infty}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(u_j,v_j;Q^\nu):
 \varepsilon_j\to0,\,
 \|u_j-u_{\zeta,\nu}\|_{L^2(Q^\nu)}\to0,\quad
 \|v_j-1\|_{L^2(Q^\nu)}\to0
 \Bigr\}.
 \label{eq:g-characterization-L2-gamma}
\end{align}
We now justify \eqref{e:truncation g}. For every
$k\in\{M+1,\ldots,2M\}$, since $\Phi_{\infty}(0)=0$,
\begin{align}
 &\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 \bigl(\mathcal T_k(u_j),v_j;Q^\nu\bigr)
 =
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 \bigl(u_j,v_j;\{|u_j|\le a_k\}\bigr)
 \notag\\
 &+
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 \bigl(\mathcal T_k(u_j),v_j;
       \{a_k<|u_j|<a_{k+1}\}\bigr)+
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 \bigl(0,v_j;\{|u_j|\ge a_{k+1}\}\bigr).
 \label{e:Feps-Tk-gamma}
\end{align}
On the intermediate region, the quadratic growth of
$\Phi_\infty$ (cf. \eqref{eq:growth-Phi-infty}), together with
$\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal T_k)\le1$, give
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}
 \bigl(\mathcal T_k(u_j),v_j;
       \{a_k<|u_j|<a_{k+1}\}\bigr)
&\le

 a_\zeta\cdot\zeta=\rho(\zeta)=s,
\end{equation}
and $q_\nu\in\mathbb{R}^n$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-calibrating-vector}
 \gamma^\circ(q_\nu)=1,
 \qquad
 q_\nu\cdot\nu=\gamma(\nu)=c_\nu.
\end{equation}
Decompose $q_\nu$ as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-normal-tangential-decomposition}
 q_\nu=q_\nu^\tau+c_\nu\nu,
 \qquad
 q_\nu^\tau\in\Pi_\nu.
\end{equation}
Fix $T>0$ and let $ (u,v)\in\mathcal{U}_{\zeta,\nu}^T
$. Defining
the scalar function $ \alpha:=a_\zeta\cdot u$, then $ \nabla\alpha=(\nabla u)^{\mathsf T}a_\zeta$.
Since $\rho^\circ(a_\zeta)=1$, inequality
\eqref{eq:Phi-projection-inequality} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-projection-estimate}
 \Phi_\infty(\nabla u)
 \ge
 \gamma(\nabla\alpha)^2
 \qquad\text{a.e.\ in }Q^\nu_T.
\end{equation}
Moreover, by linearity of convolution and
$a_\zeta\cdot\zeta=s$, the boundary condition for $u$ implies
\[
 \alpha=u_{s,\nu}\ast\phi_1
 \qquad\text{on }\partial Q^\nu_T,
\]
where
\[
 u_{s,\nu}(x)
 :=
 \begin{cases}
  s,&x\cdot\nu>0,\\
  0,&x\cdot\nu\le0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Therefore, using \eqref{eq:bulk-projection-estimate},
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{F}^\infty_1(u,v;Q^\nu_T)
 \ge
 \int_{Q^\nu_T}
 \left(
 \varphi'(0)f(v)^2\gamma(\nabla\alpha)^2
 +
 \frac{\omega(1-v)}{4}
 +
 \gamma(\nabla v)^2
 \right)\,\textup{d}x.
 \label{eq:vector-to-scalar-energy-lower}
\end{align}
We now reduce the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:vector-to-scalar-energy-lower} to one dimension. As in Proposition~\ref{p:chargT}, set $ \widetilde Q^\nu_T:=Q^\nu_T\cap\Pi_\nu.$ Choose $R_\nu
 :=
 1+\frac{3|q_\nu^\tau|}{c_\nu}
$ and define the interior part of the tangential section by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-tangential-section-direct}
 \widetilde Q^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}_T
 :=
 \left\{
 y\in\widetilde Q^\nu_T\colon
 \textup{dist}\bigl(y,\partial\widetilde Q^\nu_T\bigr)>R_\nu
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
Since $R_\nu$ is independent of $T$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-section-asymptotic-measure}
 \frac{
 \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\widetilde Q^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}_T)
 }{T^{n-1}}
 \longrightarrow1
 \qquad\text{as }T\to\infty.
\end{equation}
For every $y\in\widetilde Q^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}_T$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:def-line-interval-Iy}
 I_y
 :=
 \left\{
 t\in\mathbb{R}\colon y+tq_\nu\in Q^\nu_T
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
Since $Q^\nu_T$ is open and convex, $I_y$ is an open interval.
We write $I_y=(a_y,b_y)$. For $T>6$, the choice of
$\widetilde Q^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}_T$ implies that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:line-contains-fixed-interval}
 \textstyle \left[-\frac{3}{c_\nu},\frac{3}{c_\nu}\right]
 \subset I_y.
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $|t|\le3/c_\nu$, then $ (y+tq_\nu)\cdot\nu= t(q_\nu\cdot\nu)
 =tc_\nu,$ and hence $|(y+tq_\nu)\cdot\nu|\le3.$ Moreover, by definition of $R_\nu$,
the tangential displacement satisfies $|tq_\nu^\tau| \le\frac{3|q_\nu^\tau|}{c_\nu}< R_\nu,$ proving \eqref{eq:line-contains-fixed-interval}. Define now
\begin{equation}\label{eq:def-direct-slicing-region}
 E_T
 :=
 \left\{
 y+tq_\nu\colon
 y\in\widetilde Q^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}_T,\ 
 t\in I_y
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
By construction $ E_T\subset Q^\nu_T$ and the map
\[
 (y,t)\longmapsto y+tq_\nu,
 \qquad
 y\in\Pi_\nu,\quad t\in\mathbb{R},
\]
is injective. Indeed, if $y_1+t_1q_\nu=y_2+t_2q_\nu$, then, taking the scalar product with $\nu$ and using
$y_1,y_2\in\Pi_\nu$, we obtain $(t_1-t_2)q_\nu\cdot\nu
 =(t_1-t_2)c_\nu=0.$ Since $c_\nu>0$, it follows that $t_1=t_2$, and hence $y_1=y_2$. 
Consequently, for every non-negative measurable function
$h\colon E_T\to[0,+\infty]$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direct-line-change-variables}
 \int_{E_T}h(x)\,\textup{d}x
 =
 c_\nu
 \int_{\widetilde Q^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}_T}
 \int_{I_y}
 h(y+tq_\nu)\,\textup{d}t\,\textup{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y).
\end{equation}
For $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-a.e.\
$y\in\widetilde Q^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}_T$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:def-direct-slices-alpha-v}
 \alpha_y(t):=\alpha(y+tq_\nu),
 \qquad
 v_y(t):=v(y+tq_\nu),
 \qquad t\in I_y.
\end{equation}
By the Sobolev slicing theorem, $\alpha_y,v_y\in H^1(I_y)$ and the chain rule yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direct-slice-derivatives}
 \alpha_y'(t)
 =
 \nabla\alpha(y+tq_\nu)\cdot q_\nu,
 \qquad
 v_y'(t)
 =
 \nabla v(y+tq_\nu)\cdot q_\nu
\end{equation}
for a.e.\ $t\in I_y$. By the dual inequality for $\gamma$ and $\gamma^\circ$ in \eqref{e:polar inequality},
\[
 |p\cdot q_\nu|
 \le
 \gamma(p)\gamma^\circ(q_\nu)
 =
 \gamma(p)
 \qquad
 \text{for every }p\in\mathbb{R}^n.
\]
It follows from \eqref{eq:direct-slice-derivatives} that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-controls-direct-alpha-slice}
 |\alpha_y'(t)|
 \le
 \gamma\bigl(\nabla\alpha(y+tq_\nu)\bigr) \qquad \textup{and} \qquad |v_y'(t)|
 \le
 \gamma\bigl(\nabla v(y+tq_\nu)\bigr)
\end{equation}
for a.e.\ $t\in I_y$. We next verify the endpoint conditions of the slices. Since
\[
 y+a_yq_\nu,\ y+b_yq_\nu\in\partial Q^\nu_T
\]
and,
\[
 (y+a_yq_\nu)\cdot\nu=c_\nu a_y\le-3, \qquad (y+b_yq_\nu)\cdot\nu=c_\nu b_y\ge3
\]
from \eqref{eq:line-contains-fixed-interval},
the canonical boundary conditions defining
$\mathcal{U}^T_{\zeta,\nu}$ imply
\[
 \alpha_y(a_y)=0,
 \qquad
 v_y(a_y)=1, \qquad \alpha_y(b_y)=s,
 \qquad
 v_y(b_y)=1.
\]
Extend $\alpha_y$ and $v_y$ to the whole real line by setting
\[
 \overline\alpha_y(t)
 :=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&t\le a_y,\\
  \alpha_y(t),&a_y<t<b_y,\\
  s,&t\ge b_y,
 \end{cases} \qquad \textup{and} \qquad \overline v_y(t)
 :=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&t\le a_y,\\
  v_y(t),&a_y<t<b_y,\\
  1,&t\ge b_y.
 \end{cases}
\]
Therefore, by definition of
$g_\textup{scal}(s)$,
\begin{align}
 &\int_{I_y}
 \left(
 \varphi'(0)f^2(v_y)|\alpha_y'|^2
 +
 \frac{\omega(1-v_y)}{4}
 +
 |v_y'|^2
 \right)\,\textup{d}t
 \ge
 g_\textup{scal}(s).
 \label{eq:one-dimensional-slice-lower-direct}
\end{align}
We now use the non-negativity of the energy density, the inclusion
$E_T\subset Q^\nu_T$, and
\eqref{eq:vector-to-scalar-energy-lower}. We obtain
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{F}^\infty_1(u,v;Q^\nu_T)
 &\ge
 \int_{E_T}
 \left(
 \varphi'(0)f^2(v)\gamma(\nabla\alpha)^2
 +
 \frac{\omega(1-v)}{4}
 +
 \gamma(\nabla v)^2
 \right)\,\textup{d}x.
 \label{eq:restriction-to-ET}
\end{align}
Applying \eqref{eq:direct-line-change-variables} and
\eqref{eq:gamma-controls-direct-alpha-slice}, we find
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{F}^\infty_1(u,v;Q^\nu_T)
 \ge
 c_\nu
 \int_{\widetilde Q^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}_T}
 \int_{I_y}
 \left(
 \varphi'(0)f^2(v_y)|\alpha_y'|^2
 \right.
  +
 \frac{\omega(1-v_y)}{4}
 +
 |v_y'|^2)
 \,\textup{d}t\,\textup{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y).
 \label{eq:direct-slicing-energy-lower}
\end{align}
Using \eqref{eq:one-dimensional-slice-lower-direct}, we conclude that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direct-lower-before-normalization}
 \mathcal{F}^\infty_1(u,v;Q^\nu_T)
 \ge
 c_\nu
 \mathcal{H}^{n-1}
 \bigl(\widetilde Q^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}_T\bigr)
 g_\textup{scal}(s).
\end{equation}

After division by $T^{n-1}$, this yields
\begin{align}
 \frac{1}{T^{n-1}}
 \mathcal{F}^\infty_1(u,v;Q^\nu_T)
 \ge
 c_\nu
 \frac{
 \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\widetilde Q^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}_T)
 }{T^{n-1}}
 g_\textup{scal}(s).
 \label{eq:direct-lower-normalized}
\end{align}
Taking the infimum over
$(u,v)\in\mathcal{U}^T_{\zeta,\nu}$ and using
Proposition~\ref{p:chargT}, from \eqref{eq:interior-section-asymptotic-measure} we obtain
\begin{align*}
 g(\zeta,\nu)
 =
 \lim_{T\to\infty}
 \inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{U}^T_{\zeta,\nu}}
 \frac{1}{T^{n-1}}
 \mathcal{F}^\infty_1(u,v;Q^\nu_T)
 \ge
 c_\nu
 \lim_{T\to\infty}
 \frac{
 \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\widetilde Q^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}_T)
 }{T^{n-1}}
 g_\textup{scal}(s)= c_\nu g_\textup{scal}(s).
\end{align*}
Recalling that $ c_\nu=\gamma(\nu)$ and $s=\rho(\zeta)$ we finally obtain
\[
 g(\zeta,\nu)
 \ge
 \gamma(\nu)g_\textup{scal}\bigl(\rho(\zeta)\bigr).
\]

\textbf{Step 3. The upper bound.}

We now prove that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-rho-gamma-upper}
 g(\zeta,\nu)
 \le
 \gamma(\nu)g_\textup{scal}\bigl(\rho(\zeta)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Assume that $\zeta\ne0$, otherwise the claim is trivial, and set again $s:=\rho(\zeta)$, $c_\nu:=\gamma(\nu)$. Fix $L>0$ and $(\alpha,\beta)
 \in
 \mathcal{U}_s(-L/2,L/2).$ Extending the functions by constants outside
$(-L/2,L/2)$ and truncating, we may assume that
\[
 \alpha(t)=0,\quad \beta(t)=1
 \qquad\text{for }t\le-L/2, \qquad \textup{and} \qquad  \alpha(t)=s,\quad \beta(t)=1
 \qquad\text{for }t\ge L/2,
\]
with $0\le\alpha\le s$, $0\le\beta\le1$. Let $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$ be any sequence such that $\varepsilon_jc_\nu L<1$ for every $j$. Define, for $x\in Q^\nu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direct-recovery-u-rho-gamma}
 u_j(x)
 :=
 \frac{\zeta}{s}
 \alpha\left(
 \frac{x\cdot\nu}{\varepsilon_jc_\nu}
 \right) \qquad \textup{and} \qquad v_j(x)
 :=
 \beta\left(
 \frac{x\cdot\nu}{\varepsilon_jc_\nu}
 \right).
\end{equation}
Since $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are constant outside
$(-L/2,L/2)$, the pair $(u_j,v_j)$ differs from
$(u_{\zeta,\nu},1)$ only in 
\[
 \left\{
 x\in Q^\nu\colon
 |x\cdot\nu|
 <
 \frac{\varepsilon_jc_\nu L}{2}
 \right\}.
\]
The measure of this region is $\varepsilon_jc_\nu L$. Since $u_j$ and $v_j$
are uniformly bounded, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direct-recovery-convergence-rho-gamma}
 (u_j,v_j)\to(u_{\zeta,\nu},1)
 \qquad\text{in }L^2(Q^\nu,\mathbb{R}^{N+1}).
\end{equation}
We now compute the energy: for a.e.\ $x\in Q^\nu$,
\[
 \nabla u_j(x)
 =
 \frac{\zeta}{s}\otimes
 \frac{\nu}{\varepsilon_jc_\nu}
 \alpha'\left(
 \frac{x\cdot\nu}{\varepsilon_jc_\nu}
 \right),
\]
and by the rank-one identity \eqref{eq:Phi-rank-one-identity},
\begin{align}
 \Phi_\infty(\nabla u_j(x))
 =
 \rho\left(\frac{\zeta}{s}\right)^2
 \gamma\left(\frac{\nu}{\varepsilon_jc_\nu}\right)^2
 \left|
 \alpha'\left(
 \frac{x\cdot\nu}{\varepsilon_jc_\nu}
 \right)
 \right|^2
=
 \frac{1}{\varepsilon_j^2}
 \left|
 \alpha'\left(
 \frac{x\cdot\nu}{\varepsilon_jc_\nu}
 \right)
 \right|^2,
 \label{eq:direct-recovery-bulk-computation}
\end{align}
because $\rho\left(\frac{\zeta}{s}\right)=1$ and $\gamma\left(\frac{\nu}{c_\nu}\right)=1$. Similarly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direct-recovery-phase-gradient}
 \gamma(\nabla v_j(x))^2
 =
 \frac{1}{\varepsilon_j^2}
 \left|
 \beta'\left(
 \frac{x\cdot\nu}{\varepsilon_jc_\nu}
 \right)
 \right|^2.
\end{equation}
Since the tangential section
$Q^\nu\cap\Pi_\nu$ has $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-measure one, Fubini's theorem
and the change of variables $ t=\frac{x\cdot\nu}{\varepsilon_jc_\nu}$ gives
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{F}^\infty_{\varepsilon_j}(u_j,v_j;Q^\nu)
 &=
 c_\nu
 \int_{-\frac L2}^{\frac L2}
 \bigg(
 \varphi'(0)f^2(\beta)|\alpha'|^2
 +
 \frac{\omega(1-\beta)}{4}
 +
 |\beta'|^2
 \bigg)\,\textup{d}t.
 \label{eq:direct-recovery-energy-identity}
\end{align}
By \eqref{eq:direct-recovery-convergence-rho-gamma} and the
definition of $g$ in \eqref{eq:def-g-gamma}, we infer that
\begin{align}
 g(\zeta,\nu)
 \le
 \liminf_{j\to\infty}
 \mathcal{F}^\infty_{\varepsilon_j}(u_j,v_j;Q^\nu)
 =
 c_\nu
 \int_{-\frac L2}^{\frac L2}
 \bigg(
 \varphi'(0)f^2(\beta)|\alpha'|^2
 +
 \frac{\omega(1-\beta)}{4}
 +
 |\beta'|^2
 \bigg)\,\textup{d}t.
 \label{eq:upper-before-infimum}
\end{align}
Taking the infimum over
$(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{U}_s(-L/2,L/2)$ gives
\[
 g(\zeta,\nu)\le c_\nu m_L(s).
\]
Letting $L\to\infty$ and using
\eqref{eq:gscalar-large-interval}, we conclude the proof.

v_j^1(M_jx+\frac12\nu), & \text{if } x\cdot\nu<0,\\[3pt]
v_j^2(M_jx-\frac12\nu), & \text{if } x\cdot\nu\ge0.
\end{cases}
\]
Because of periodicity in the tangential directions, $(u_j,v_j)$
belongs to $H^1(Q^\nu;\mathbb{R}^{N+1})$.
Moreover, $u_j=0$ and $v_j=1$ on
$\{x\cdot\nu\le-\frac1{M_j}\}$, while
$u_j=\zeta_1+\zeta_2$ and $v_j=1$ on
$\{x\cdot\nu\ge\frac1{M_j}\}$, and the pair is
$\frac1{M_j}$--periodic along the directions of
$\Pi_{\nu}\cap Q^\nu$.

By a change of variables,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
\|u_j-u_{\zeta_1+\zeta_2,\nu}\|_{L^1(Q^\nu)}

\|u_j-u_{\zeta_1+\zeta_2,\nu}\|_

\|u_j^1-u_{\zeta_1,\nu}\|_{L^1(Q^\nu)}
+
\|u_j^2-u_{\zeta_2,\nu}\|_{L^1(Q^\nu)}
\Big)
+\frac{|\zeta_1|+|\zeta_2|}{2M_j},
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
hence $u_j\to u_{\zeta_1+\zeta_2,\nu}$ in
$L^1(Q^\nu;\mathbb{R}^N)$.

Furthermore, using also the positive one-homogeneity of the norm
$\gamma$, an immediate computation yields
\[
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j/M_j}(u_j,v_j;Q^\nu)
=
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(u_j^1,v_j^1;Q^\nu)
+
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{\varepsilon_j}(u_j^2,v_j^2;Q^\nu).
\]
Taking the limit as $j\to\infty$ gives the desired inequality in
\ref{lemmapropgsubadd}.

\noindent\textit{(ii) Continuity.}
From \ref{lemmapropgsubadd} and
Proposition~\ref{p:crescita della gvettoriale}, we deduce that
\[
g(\zeta,\nu)
\le
g(\zeta',\nu)+K|\zeta-\zeta'|,
\]
where $K$ (cf.~\eqref{e:crescita della g vettoriale}) does not depend
on $\nu$.
Interchanging $\zeta$ and $\zeta'$ therefore gives
\[
|g(\zeta,\nu)-g(\zeta',\nu)|
\le K|\zeta-\zeta'|.
\]
Thus it remains only to show the continuity with respect to $\nu$.

Since $\Phi_{\infty}$ is continuous and strictly positive on the
compact unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^{N\times n-1}$, there exists a nondecreasing
modulus of continuity $\vartheta_\Phi$, with
$\vartheta_\Phi(0)=0$, such that
\[
\Phi_{\infty}(\xi)
\le
\bigl(1+\vartheta_\Phi(|\xi-\xi'|)\bigr)
\Phi_{\infty}(\xi')
\qquad
\text{whenever }|\xi|=|\xi'|=1.
\]
By the $2$-homogeneity of $\Phi_\infty$, this implies, after possibly
enlarging $\vartheta_\Phi$, that
\begin{equation}\label{eqpsiinfunifcont}
\Phi_\infty(\eta R)
\le
\bigl(1+\vartheta_\Phi(|R-\mathrm{Id}|)\bigr)
\Phi_\infty(\eta)
\qquad
\text{for every }\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n},\ R\in O(n).
\end{equation}
Thanks to \eqref{e:gamma lower euclidean suppenergy}, for every $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^n$ and $R\in O(n)$,
\[
\begin{split}
\gamma(\xi R)
\le
\gamma(\xi)+\gamma\bigl(\xi(R-\mathrm{Id})\bigr)
\le
\gamma(\xi)+C_\gamma|\xi|\,|R-\mathrm{Id}|
\le
\left(1+\frac{C_\gamma}{c_\gamma}|R-\mathrm{Id}|\right)\gamma(\xi).
\end{split}
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gammarotation}
\gamma^2(\xi R)
\le
\bigl(1+\vartheta_\gamma(|R-\mathrm{Id}|)\bigr)\gamma^2(\xi),
\end{equation}
where, 
\[
\vartheta_\gamma(t)
:=
2\frac{C_\gamma}{c_\gamma}t
+
\left(\frac{C_\gamma}{c_\gamma}\right)^2t^2.
\]
In particular, $\vartheta_\gamma$ is nondecreasing and
$\vartheta_\gamma(t)\to0$ as $t\to0$. Setting $\vartheta(t):=
\max\{\vartheta_\Phi(t),\vartheta_\gamma(t)\}
$, then both \eqref{eqpsiinfunifcont} and
\eqref{eq:gammarotation} hold with $\vartheta$ in place of
$\vartheta_\Phi$ and $\vartheta_\gamma$, respectively.

Fix $\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ and a sequence $\varepsilon_j\to0$, and let
$(u_j,v_j)$ be the sequence given by Proposition~\ref{periodicity},
extended periodically along $\Pi_\nu$ and constantly in the
$\nu$--direction as in part~\ref{lemmapropgsubadd}.
Let $\tilde\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$, $\tilde\nu\neq\nu$, and choose
$R\in O(n)$ such that
\[

\qquad
|R-\mathrm{Id}|\le c|\nu-\tilde\nu|
\]
(for instance, $R$ acts as the identity on the subspace orthogonal to
$\operatorname{span}\{\nu,\tilde\nu\}$ and rotates $\tilde\nu$ to
$\nu$ in that plane). Let $M_j\to\infty$ and define
\[
\tilde u_j(x):=u_j(M_jRx),
\qquad
\tilde v_j(x):=v_j(M_jRx).
\]
Since
\[
(Rx)\cdot\nu
=
x\cdot R^T\nu
=
x\cdot\tilde\nu,
\]
and $u_j\to u_{\zeta,\nu}$ locally, it follows that
\[
\tilde u_j\to u_{\zeta,\tilde\nu}
\qquad\text{in }L^1(Q^{\tilde\nu};\mathbb{R}^N).
\]
Moreover,
\[
\nabla\tilde u_j(x)
=
M_j\nabla u_j(M_jRx)R,
\qquad
\nabla\tilde v_j(x)
=
M_j\nabla v_j(M_jRx)R.
\]
Using \eqref{eqpsiinfunifcont}, we obtain
\[
\Phi_{\infty}(\nabla\tilde u_j)(x)
=
M_j^2
\Phi_{\infty}\bigl(\nabla u_j(M_jRx)R\bigr)
\le
M_j^2
\bigl(1+\vartheta(|R-\mathrm{Id}|)\bigr)
\Phi_{\infty}(\nabla u_j)(M_jRx).
\]
Likewise, by \eqref{eq:gammarotation},
\[
\gamma^2(\nabla\tilde v_j(x))
=
M_j^2
\gamma^2\bigl(\nabla v_j(M_jRx)R\bigr)
\le
M_j^2
\bigl(1+\vartheta(|R-\mathrm{Id}|)\bigr)
\gamma^2(\nabla v_j(M_jRx)).
\]
Therefore, substituting these estimates into the definition of
$\mathcal{F}^\infty_{\varepsilon_j/M_j}
(\tilde u_j,\tilde v_j;Q^{\tilde\nu})$
and performing the change of variables $y=M_jRx$, we obtain
\[
\mathcal{F}_{\frac{\varepsilon_j}{M_j}}^{\infty}
(\tilde u_j,\tilde v_j;Q^{\tilde\nu})
\le
\bigl(1+\vartheta(|R-\mathrm{Id}|)\bigr)
M_j^{1-n}
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_j}^{\infty}
(u_j,v_j;M_jRQ^{\tilde\nu}).
\]

Since $(u_j,v_j)$ are periodic in the directions orthogonal to $\nu$,
the set
$\Pi_{\nu}\cap M_jRQ^{\tilde\nu}$ can be covered by at most $M_j^{n-1}+cM_j^{n-2}$ disjoint copies of $\Pi_{\nu}\cap Q^\nu$. Hence,
\begin{align*}
 g(\zeta,\tilde\nu)
&\le
\limsup_{j\to\infty}
\mathcal{F}_{\frac{\varepsilon_j}{M_j}}^{\infty}
(\tilde u_j,\tilde v_j;Q^{\tilde\nu})
\le
\bigl(1+\vartheta(|R-\mathrm{Id}|)\bigr)
\limsup_{j\to\infty}
\left(1+\frac{c}{M_j}\right)
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_j}^{\infty}(u_j,v_j;Q^\nu)
\\
&=
\bigl(1+\vartheta(|R-\mathrm{Id}|)\bigr)g(\zeta,\nu)\le
\bigl(1+\vartheta(c|\nu-\tilde\nu|)\bigr)g(\zeta,\nu).
\end{align*}
Interchanging the roles of $\nu$ and $\tilde\nu$, we also obtain
\[
g(\zeta,\nu)
\le
\bigl(1+\vartheta(c|\nu-\tilde\nu|)\bigr)
g(\zeta,\tilde\nu).
\]
Since $\vartheta(t)\to0$ as $t\to0$, this proves the continuity of
$g(\zeta,\cdot)$ on $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ and hence, together with the
Lipschitz continuity with respect to $\zeta$, the continuity of $g$
on $\mathbb{R}^N\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$.

Fix an arbitrary $A\in\mathcal A(\Omega)$.
We may initially assume that the inferior limit on the right-hand side
of \eqref{lbjumpvectoriale} is finite, since otherwise the claim holds
trivially.
Consequently, up to extracting a subsequence, we can suppose that
\[
(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon)\in H^1(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N\times[0,1])
\]
and that the aforementioned $\liminf$ is actually a limit.

For every $\varepsilon>0$, we introduce the localized energy Radon measure
$\mu_\varepsilon$ on $A$ defined by
\begin{equation*}
\mu_\varepsilon
:=
\left(
\varphi(\varepsilon f^2(v_\varepsilon))\Phi(\nabla u_\varepsilon)
+
\frac{\omega(1-v_\varepsilon)}{4\varepsilon}
+
\varepsilon\gamma^2(\nabla v_\varepsilon)
\right)
\mathcal{L}^n\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt \vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits A.
\end{equation*}
By weak$^*$ compactness, extracting a further subsequence if necessary,
we obtain
\[
\mu_\varepsilon\rightharpoonup\mu
\]
for some limiting Radon measure $\mu$ on $A$. This weak convergence
guarantees that
\begin{equation*}
\liminf_{\varepsilon\to0}
\mathcal F_\varepsilon(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon,A)
\geq \mu(A).
\end{equation*}
To prove \eqref{lbjumpvectoriale}, it is therefore sufficient to
establish the pointwise bound
\begin{equation}\label{e:claim lower sup}
\frac{d\mu}{d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt \vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits J_u}(x_0)
\geq
g([u](x_0),\nu_u(x_0))
\end{equation}
for $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-a.e.\ $x_0\in J_u\cap A$.

Let us fix a point $x_0\in J_u\cap A$ that simultaneously satisfies
the following regularity conditions:
\begin{equation*}
\frac{d\mu}
{d(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt \vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits(J_u\cap A))}(x_0)
=
\lim_{\rho\to0}
\frac{\mu(Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))}
{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(J_u\cap Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))}
<\infty,
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{\rho\to0}
\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
(J_u\cap Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))}
{\rho^{n-1}}
=1,
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{\rho\to0}
\frac1{\rho^n}
\int_{Q^\nu_\rho(x_0)}
|u(y)-u_{x_0,\zeta,\nu}(y)|\,dy
=0,
\end{equation*}
where we have set
\[
\nu:=\nu_u(x_0),
\qquad
\zeta:=[u](x_0).
\]
By standard theory on $BV$ functions, these conditions identify a set
of full $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-measure within $J_u\cap A$.

From the weak$^*$ convergence of $\mu_\varepsilon$, we can express the density
at $x_0$ as
\begin{equation}\label{e:doppio limite radon-nyk parte sup}
\frac{d\mu}{d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt \vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits J_u}(x_0)
=
\lim_{\rho\to0}
\frac{\mu(Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))}{\rho^{n-1}}
=
\lim_{\substack{\rho\to0\\ \rho\in I(x_0)}}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}
\frac{\mu_\varepsilon(Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))}
{\rho^{n-1}},
\end{equation}
where $I(x_0)$ is the set of ``good'' radii
\begin{equation*}
I(x_0)
:=
\left\{
\rho\in
\left(
0,\frac{2}{\sqrt n}\textup{dist}(x_0,\partial A)
\right):
\mu(\partial Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))=0
\right\}.
\end{equation*}

Next, as in the proof of \cite[Proposition~3.3]{ACF26}, we fix a
threshold parameter $\delta\in(0,1)$ and, for every $\varepsilon>0$, define
the truncation level
\[
\delta_\varepsilon
:=
\sup\left\{
t\in[0,1):
\varepsilon f^2(t)\leq\delta
\right\}.
\]
Since $f(0)=0$ and $f(t)\to\infty$ as $t\to1^-$,
$\delta_\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ is actually a maximum by the continuity of $f$
on $[0,1)$. Moreover, for all $t\in(\delta_\varepsilon,1)$,
\[
\delta<\varepsilon f^2(t),
\qquad
\varepsilon f^2(\delta_\varepsilon)=\delta.
\]
In particular, the latter identity implies
\[
\delta_\varepsilon\to1
\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to0.
\]
Moreover, by (H1), there exists $t_0\in(0,1)$ such that $f$ is
increasing on $[t_0,1)$. Defining $M:=\max_{[0,t_0]}f$, we have for all $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small, $\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}>M^2
\text{ and }
\delta_\varepsilon\in(t_0,1).$ In particular, when $\varepsilon$ is small enough,
$t\in[0,\delta_\varepsilon]$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{e:bounddeltaciro bis}
\varepsilon f^2(t)\leq\delta.
\end{equation}

We then introduce the truncated phase-field $\hat v_\varepsilon:=v_\varepsilon\land\delta_\varepsilon$ and decompose the energy as follows:
\begin{align*}
\mathcal F_\varepsilon
(u_\varepsilon,\hat v_\varepsilon,Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
&=
\mathcal F_\varepsilon
\bigl(
u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon,
Q^\nu_\rho(x_0)\setminus\{v_\varepsilon>\delta_\varepsilon\}
\bigr)+
\int_{Q^\nu_\rho(x_0)\cap\{v_\varepsilon>\delta_\varepsilon\}}
\varphi(\delta)\Phi(\nabla u_\varepsilon)\,dx
\\
&\quad+
\frac{\omega(1-\delta_\varepsilon)}{4\varepsilon}
\mathcal{L}^n
\bigl(
Q^\nu_\rho(x_0)\cap\{v_\varepsilon>\delta_\varepsilon\}
\bigr).
\end{align*}
Given that $\varphi$ is non-decreasing and using
\eqref{e:bounddeltaciro bis}, it follows that $\varphi(\delta)
\leq
\varphi(\varepsilon f^2(v_\varepsilon))\text{ on }\{v_\varepsilon>\delta_\varepsilon\}.$ Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\mathcal F_\varepsilon
(u_\varepsilon,\hat v_\varepsilon,Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
\leq
\mathcal F_\varepsilon
(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon,Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
+
\frac{\omega(1-\delta_\varepsilon)}{4\varepsilon}
\mathcal{L}^n
\bigl(
Q^\nu_\rho(x_0)\cap\{v_\varepsilon>\delta_\varepsilon\}
\bigr).
\end{align*}
Appealing to the definition of $\delta_\varepsilon$, condition
\eqref{eq:def-varsigma}, and the fact that
$\delta_\varepsilon\to1$ as $\varepsilon\to0$, we conclude that, for $\varepsilon$
sufficiently small (depending on $\delta$),
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal F_\varepsilon
(u_\varepsilon,\hat v_\varepsilon,Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
\leq
\mathcal F_\varepsilon
(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon,Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
+
\frac{(\varsigma^2+\delta)\rho^n}{4\delta}.
\end{equation*}
Substituting this into
\eqref{e:doppio limite radon-nyk parte sup} and then letting
$\rho\to0$ provides the key lower estimate
\begin{equation}\label{e:penultima stima con der radon-nyk lower sup}
\frac{d\mu}{d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt \vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits J_u}(x_0)
\geq
\limsup_{\substack{\rho\to0\\\rho\in I(x_0)}}
\limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}
\frac{
\mathcal F_\varepsilon
(u_\varepsilon,\hat v_\varepsilon,Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
}{\rho^{n-1}}.
\end{equation}

For an arbitrary $\eta\in(0,1)$, conditions
\eqref{eq:uniform-recession-estimate} and
\eqref{eq:growth-Phi-infty} guarantee that $\Phi(\xi)\geq(1-\eta)\Phi_\infty(\xi)$
for sufficiently large $|\xi|$. Thus, by the continuity of the
recession function $\Phi_\infty$, there exists a constant
$C(\eta)>0$ ensuring the global bound
\begin{equation*}
\Phi(\xi)+C(\eta)
\geq
(1-\eta)\Phi_\infty(\xi)
\qquad
\text{for every }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}.
\end{equation*}
For each ``good'' radius $\rho\in I(x_0)$, we select
$\eta_\rho\in(0,1)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{e:cicciomaccio}
\eta_\rho\to0,
\qquad
\rho C(\eta_\rho)\to0
\qquad
\text{as }\rho\to0.
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{e:bounddeltaciro bis} and hypothesis (H4), we can write
\begin{equation*}
\varphi(\varepsilon f^2(\hat v_\varepsilon))
\geq
(1-\tilde\vartheta(\delta))
\varphi'(0^+)\varepsilon f^2(\hat v_\varepsilon),
\end{equation*}
where $\tilde\vartheta(\delta)\to0^+$ as $\delta\to0^+$. Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\varphi(\varepsilon f^2(\hat v_\varepsilon))
\Phi(\nabla u_\varepsilon)
\geq
(1-\tilde\vartheta(\delta))
\varphi'(0^+)\varepsilon f^2(\hat v_\varepsilon)
\bigl(
(1-\eta_\rho)\Phi_\infty(\nabla u_\varepsilon)
-
C(\eta_\rho)
\bigr).
\end{align*}
Integrating this bound over the cube and recalling
\eqref{e:bounddeltaciro bis}, we deduce
\begin{align*}
\frac{
\mathcal F_\varepsilon
(u_\varepsilon,\hat v_\varepsilon,Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
}{\rho^{n-1}}
&\geq
\frac{
(1-\tilde\vartheta(\delta))
(1-\eta_\rho)
\mathcal F^\infty_\varepsilon
(u_\varepsilon,\hat v_\varepsilon,Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
}{\rho^{n-1}}-
\frac{
C(\eta_\rho)
\mathcal{L}^n(Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
(1-\tilde\vartheta(\delta))
\varphi'(0^+)\delta
}{\rho^{n-1}},
\end{align*}
where the asymptotic functional
$\mathcal F^\infty_\varepsilon$ is defined in
\eqref{eq:def-recession-energy}.

Indeed, the potential and damage terms are nonnegative and therefore
may be multiplied by the factor
\[
(1-\tilde\vartheta(\delta))(1-\eta_\rho)\leq1.
\]

Therefore, taking the superior limits, we arrive at
\begin{equation}\label{e:ultima stima der radon nyk lowe sup}
\frac{d\mu}{d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt \vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits J_u}(x_0)
\geq
(1-\tilde\vartheta(\delta))
\limsup_{\substack{\rho\to0\\\rho\in I(x_0)}}
\limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}
\frac{
\mathcal F^\infty_\varepsilon
(u_\varepsilon,\hat v_\varepsilon,Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
}{\rho^{n-1}}.
\end{equation}

Finally, we perform the standard blow-up procedure. For $\rho>0$ set
\[
u^\rho_\varepsilon(y)
:=
u_\varepsilon(\rho y+x_0),
\qquad
\hat v^\rho_\varepsilon(y)
:=
\hat v_\varepsilon(\rho y+x_0).
\]
By the $2$-homogeneity of $\Phi_\infty$ and the $1$-homogeneity of the
norm $\gamma$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{e:blowup Finfty gamma}
\frac{
\mathcal F^\infty_\varepsilon
(u_\varepsilon,\hat v_\varepsilon,Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
}{\rho^{n-1}}
=
\mathcal F^\infty_{\varepsilon/\rho}
(u^\rho_\varepsilon,\hat v^\rho_\varepsilon,Q^\nu).
\end{equation}
Because $u_\varepsilon\to u$ strongly in $L^1(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N)$ and
$v_\varepsilon\to1$ strongly in $L^1(\Omega)$, while
$\delta_\varepsilon\to1$, a standard diagonal argument allows us to choose
$\rho_j\to0$, with $\rho_j\in I(x_0)$, and
$\varepsilon_j\to0$ such that
\[
\tilde\varepsilon_j
:=
\frac{\varepsilon_j}{\rho_j}
\longrightarrow0, \quad \textup{and} \quad (u^{\rho_j}_{\varepsilon_j},\hat v^{\rho_j}_{\varepsilon_j})
\longrightarrow
(u_{\zeta,\nu},1)
\qquad\text{in }L^1(Q^\nu;\mathbb{R}^{N+1}),
\]
and 
\begin{align}
\limsup_{\substack{\rho\to0\\\rho\in I(x_0)}}
\limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}
\frac{
\mathcal F^\infty_\varepsilon
(u_\varepsilon,\hat v_\varepsilon,Q^\nu_\rho(x_0))
}{\rho^{n-1}}=
\lim_{j\to\infty}
\mathcal F^\infty_{\tilde\varepsilon_j}
\bigl(
u^{\rho_j}_{\varepsilon_j},
\hat v^{\rho_j}_{\varepsilon_j},
Q^\nu
\bigr).
\label{e:ultima stima lower sup}
\end{align}
By definition of $g$ in \eqref{eq:def-g-gamma}, we
therefore obtain
\[
\lim_{j\to\infty}
\mathcal F^\infty_{\tilde\varepsilon_j}
\bigl(
u^{\rho_j}_{\varepsilon_j},
\hat v^{\rho_j}_{\varepsilon_j},
Q^\nu
\bigr)
\geq
g(\zeta,\nu).
\]
Coupling this with
\eqref{e:ultima stima der radon nyk lowe sup} and \eqref{e:cicciomaccio} yield
\[
\frac{d\mu}{d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt \vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits J_u}(x_0)
\geq
(1-\tilde\vartheta(\delta))
g(\zeta,\nu),
\]
concluding the proof, taking $\delta\to0$.

Fix an arbitrary open set $A\in \mathcal{A}(\Omega)$. We can assume without loss of generality that the inferior limit on the right-hand side of \eqref{e:liminf diffuse sigmabarra finito vettoriale} is finite, as the inequality holds trivially otherwise. Moreover, since $u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$, thanks to Lemma \ref{l:5.3} we may assume that $(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon)\in H^1\cap L^\infty(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N+1})$ for every $\varepsilon>0$.

Let $\eta\in(0,1\wedge \varsigma)$ and invoking the notation and results established in Lemma~\ref{l:suppenergy}, we can find $\delta_{\eta}\in(0,1)$ such that, for every $\delta\in(0,\delta_{\eta})$, the truncated function $u^\delta_\varepsilon$ belongs to the space $SBV(A,\mathbb{R}^N)$.
This allows us to bound the effective energy from below as follows:
\begin{align*}
 H_\delta(\tilde u_{\varepsilon}^\delta,A)&=\zeta_1(\delta)\int_A h^{\mathrm{qc}}_{\varsigma_\eta}(\nabla \tilde u^{\delta}_\varepsilon) \textup{d}x 
+ \zeta_2(\delta)\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(A\cap J_{\tilde u^\delta_\varepsilon}) 
 \leq\mathcal{F}_\varepsilon(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon,A)
+ h_{\varsigma_\eta}(0)\mathcal{L}^{n}(A\cap \{v_{\varepsilon}\leq 1-\delta\})\,,
\end{align*}
where the shifted parameter $\varsigma_{\eta}:=(\varsigma-\eta)(1-\eta)$ as in Lemma \ref{l:suppenergy}. 

Taking the inferior limit as $\varepsilon\to0$ and applying the lower semicontinuity result of Theorem~\ref{t:CFI_sci}, we deduce:
\begin{equation*}
    \zeta_1(\delta)\int_A h_{\varsigma_\eta}^{\mathrm{qc}}(\nabla u)\textup{d}x 
    + \zeta_1(\delta)\int_A h^{\mathrm{qc},\infty}_{\varsigma_\eta}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} D^c u)
    \leq \liminf_{\varepsilon \to 0} \mathcal{F}_\varepsilon(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon},A)\,,
\end{equation*}
because $v_\varepsilon\to 1$ in $L^1(\Omega)$. Finally, by passing to the limit first as $\delta\to0$ and subsequently as $\eta\to0$, we recover the desired lower bound \eqref{e:liminf diffuse sigmabarra finito vettoriale}. This sequential limit is fully justified by the asymptotic behavior in \eqref{e:limittruncat}, the monotonicity property \eqref{e:la h al variare di sigma}, and a direct application of Beppo Levi's Monotone Convergence Theorem.

\label{prop:domain direct}
Let $(u,v)\in L^1(\Omega;\mathbb R^{N+1})$ be such that $\displaystyle\big(\Gamma\textup{-}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0} \mathcal{F}_\varepsilon\big)(u,v;\Omega)<\infty$. Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-anisotropic-gamma-convergence}, it necessarily follows that $v=1$ $\mathcal{L}^n$-a.e. in $\Omega$, and $u\in GBV_{\star}(\Omega,\mathbb R^N)$ with $\mathcal{F}_{\varsigma}(u,1;\Omega)<\infty$.

Consider a sequence $(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon)\to(u,v)$ in $L^1(\Omega,\mathbb R^{N+1})$ verifying 
\begin{equation}\label{e:prima equazione nella prop sul dominio del gamma limite}
\sup_\varepsilon\mathcal{F}_\varepsilon(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon;\Omega)<\infty.
\end{equation}
Due to the strict penalization induced by the term $\frac{\omega(1-v_\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}$, it follows that $v=1$ $\mathcal{L}^n$-a.e. in $\Omega$.  
Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$. Using standard truncation properties for phase-field approximations and exploiting the coercive growth conditions on $h^{\mathrm{qc}}_{\varsigma}$ (cf. \eqref{e:bulk-growth-h}), we obtain the uniform bound
\begin{align*}
\int_\Omega|\nabla {u}_\varepsilon^\delta|\textup{d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(J_{{u}_\varepsilon^\delta})
\leq c\big(\mathcal{F}_\varepsilon(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon;\Omega)+1\big),
\end{align*}
where $u_\varepsilon^\delta$ represents a properly thresholded version of $u_\varepsilon$, and $c>0$ is a constant depending on $\delta$ and the measure of $\Omega$. In particular, each component $({u}_\varepsilon^\delta)_i$ belongs to $GSBV(\Omega)$ and satisfies
\begin{align*}
\int_\Omega|\nabla ({u}_\varepsilon^\delta)_i|\textup{d}x+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(J_{({u}_\varepsilon^\delta)_i})
\leq c\big(\mathcal{F}_\varepsilon(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon;\Omega)+1\big).
\end{align*}
For any $M>0$, the truncated function $\tau_M(({u}_\varepsilon^\delta)_i)$ is in $BV_{\textup{loc}}(\Omega)$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. The previous estimate enforces that 
\[
|D(\tau_M(({u}_\varepsilon^\delta)_i))|(\Omega)\leq C_M,
\]
for some constant $C_M>0$ dependent on $M$ but strictly independent of $\varepsilon$. Hence, up to a subsequence, $\tau_M(({u}_\varepsilon^\delta)_i)$ converges weakly in $BV(\Omega)$. Since $v_\varepsilon \to 1$, we also have ${u}_\varepsilon^\delta \to u$ strongly in $L^1(\Omega,\mathbb R^N)$, implying that $\tau_M(u_i) \in BV(\Omega)$ for every $M>0$. Consequently, $u_i\in GBV(\Omega)$ for each $i$, ensuring that $u\in (GBV(\Omega))^N$.

Furthermore, letting $\tilde \tau_M(w):=(\tau_M(w_1),\dots,\tau_M(w_N))$ for every $w\in \mathbb R^N$, there exists a constant $L\in(0,\infty)$ such that 
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{M>0} \sup_{\varepsilon>0} \mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde \tau_M(u_\varepsilon),v_\varepsilon;\Omega)\leq L.
\end{equation*}
Because $\tilde \tau_M(u_{\varepsilon})\to \tilde \tau_M(u)$ in $L^1(\Omega,\mathbb R^N)$, we can leverage Theorem~\ref{thm:lbcompBV}, along with the growth properties \eqref{e:bulk-growth-h} and \eqref{e:crescita della g vettoriale}, to deduce that for all $i$ and $M>0$:
\begin{equation*}
\int_\Omega |\nabla \tau_M(u_i)| \textup{d}x 
+ |D^c(\tau_M(u_i))|(\Omega)
+ \int_{J_{\tau_M(u_i)}} (|[\tau_M(u_i)]|\wedge1) \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq L.
\end{equation*}
This explicitly classifies $u\in GBV_{\star}(\Omega,\mathbb R^N)$. By exploiting the fundamental structural properties of $GBV$ functions (see, for instance, \cite{Bra98}) and applying the Monotone Convergence Theorem, we infer that
\begin{equation}\label{e:funz finito su GBV alla N}
\int_\Omega |\nabla u_i| \textup{d}x 
+ |D^cu_i|(\Omega)
+  \int_{J_{u_i}} (|[u_i]|\wedge1) \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}<\infty.
\end{equation}
Coupling this integrability with the growth constraints of $h_{\varsigma}^{\mathrm{qc}}$, $h_{\varsigma}^{\mathrm{qc},\infty}$, and $g$ (cf. \eqref{e:bulk-growth-h} and \eqref{e:crescita della g vettoriale}), we finally conclude that $\mathcal{F}_{\varsigma}(u,1;\Omega)<\infty$.

\label{prop:contF0}
 Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-anisotropic-gamma-convergence}, for all $u\in (GBV(\Omega))^N$ with $\mathcal{F}_{\varsigma}(u,1;\Omega)<\infty$ (i.e., $u\in GBV_{\star}(\Omega)^N$), we have

\]

<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
We assess the convergence of the volume, Cantor, and surface components separately.  

For the bulk part, \eqref{e:bulk-growth-h} ensures that $|\nabla u|\in L^1(\Omega)$. Since $\nabla(\mathcal{T}_k(u))=\nabla u$ for $\mathcal{L}^n$-a.e. $x\in \Omega_k:=\{|u|\le a_k\}$, applying the linear growth bound yields
\[
\Big|\int_\Omega h_{\varsigma}^\mathrm{qc}\big(\nabla(\mathcal{T}_k(u))\big)\textup{d}x-
\int_\Omega h_{\varsigma}^\mathrm{qc}(\nabla u)\textup{d}x\Big|
\leq K_{\varsigma}\int_{\Omega\setminus \Omega_k}(1+|\nabla u|)\textup{d}x.
\]
As $a_k\to\infty$, the domain $\Omega\setminus \Omega_k$ vanishes in measure, producing
\[
\lim_{k\to\infty} \int_\Omega h_{\varsigma}^\mathrm{qc}\big(\nabla(\mathcal{T}_k(u))\big)\textup{d}x
=\int_\Omega h_{\varsigma}^\mathrm{qc}(\nabla u)\textup{d}x.
\]

Concerning the surface contribution, we note that $J_{\mathcal{T}_k(u)}\subseteq J_u$ for every $k$, and $\nu_{\mathcal{T}_k(u)}=\nu_u$ for $\mathcal H^{n-1}$-a.e. $x\in J_{\mathcal{T}_k(u)}$. Because $\mathcal{F}_{\varsigma}(u,1;\Omega)<\infty$, condition \eqref{e:funz finito su GBV alla N} is satisfied. Consequently, the left and right traces verify $(\mathcal{T}_k(u))^\pm\to u^\pm$ and $\chi_{J_{\mathcal{T}_k(u)}}\to \chi_{J_u}$ as $k\to\infty$, with the uniform domination $|[\mathcal{T}_k(u)]|\le |[u]|$ on $J_u$. Thus,
\begin{align*}
\lim_{k\to\infty} \int_{J_{\mathcal{T}_k(u)}}g([\mathcal{T}_k(u)],\nu_{\mathcal{T}_k(u)})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}
&=\lim_{k\to\infty} \int_{J_u}g([\mathcal{T}_k(u)],\nu_u)\chi_{J_{\mathcal{T}_k(u)}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{J_u}g([u],\nu_u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^{n-1},
\end{align*}
relying on the continuity of $g$ (Lemma~\ref{lemmapropg2}) and the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

For the Cantor part, the chain rule in $GBV$ guarantees
\[
D^c(\mathcal{T}_k(u))\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt \vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits \Omega_k=D^c u\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height 7pt width .5pt depth 0pt \vrule height .5pt width 6pt depth 0pt}}\nolimits \Omega_k,
\qquad
|D^c(\mathcal{T}_k(u))|\ll |D^c u|,
\qquad
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|D^c(\mathcal{T}_k(u))|}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|D^c u|}\le 1.
\]
Then, exploiting the recession bound \eqref{e:bulk-growth-hrecession}, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\Big|\int_{\Omega}h_{\varsigma}^{\mathrm{qc},\infty}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} D^c(\mathcal{T}_k(u)))-
\int_{\Omega}h_{\varsigma}^{\mathrm{qc},\infty}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} D^cu)\Big|
\leq K_{\varsigma}\int_{\Omega\setminus \Omega_k}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} |D^cu|
= K_{\varsigma}|D^cu|(\Omega\setminus \Omega_k).
\end{equation*}
Taking the limit as $k\to\infty$ nullifies the right-hand side, yielding
\[
\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}h^{\mathrm{qc},\infty}_{\varsigma}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} D^c(\mathcal{T}_k(u)))=
\int_{\Omega}h^{\mathrm{qc},\infty}_{\varsigma}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} D^cu),
\]
which completes the proof.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{thm:lbcomp}
Let $u\in (GBV(\Omega))^N$. Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-anisotropic-gamma-convergence}, it holds that
\begin{equation}\label{e:lbcomp}
\mathcal{F}_{\varsigma}(u,1;\Omega) \le \Gamma(L^1)\hbox{-}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to0} \mathcal{F}_\varepsilon(u, 1;\Omega).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Let $(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon)\in H^1(\Omega, \mathbb R^{N}\times [0,1])$ be a sequence converging to $(u,1)$ in $L^1(\Omega,\mathbb R^{N+1})$. We may seamlessly assume that $\liminf_{\varepsilon\to0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon;\Omega)<\infty$, that this limit is attained along a subsequence, and that $(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon)\to(u,1)$ pointwise $\mathcal{L}^n$-a.e. in $\Omega$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:domain direct}, this guarantees that $u\in(GBV(\Omega))^N$ and $\mathcal{F}_{\varsigma}(u,1;\Omega)<\infty$. 

Recalling the truncation operator $\mathcal{T}_k$, we recognize that $\mathcal{T}_k(u_\varepsilon)\to \mathcal{T}_k(u)$ strongly in $L^1(\Omega,\mathbb R^N)$ for any fixed $k$, and that $\mathcal{T}_k(u)\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb R^N)$. Thus, invoking Theorem~\ref{thm:lbcompBV}, we possess the bound
\begin{equation}\label{estTkA}
\mathcal{F}_{\varsigma}(\mathcal{T}_{k_{M}}(u),1;\Omega)\leq \liminf_{\varepsilon\to0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_{k_{M}}(u_\varepsilon),v_\varepsilon;\Omega),
\end{equation}
for every integer choice $k_M$. 

We claim that for each $M\in\mathbb N$, there exists an optimal index $k_{M}\in\{M+1,\dots,2M\}$ (independent of $\varepsilon$) such that, upon passing to a suitable subsequence,
\begin{equation}\label{estTk}
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_{k_{M}}(u_\varepsilon),v_\varepsilon;\Omega)\leq 
\Big(1+\frac{c}{M}\Big)\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon;\Omega)
+c\,\mathcal{L}^n(\{|u_\varepsilon|>a_M\}),
\end{equation}
for some uniform constant $c>0$. Accepting this claim momentarily, substituting \eqref{estTk} into \eqref{estTkA} and noting that the measure of the super-level sets decays to zero, we infer that
\[
\limsup_{M\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varsigma}(\mathcal{T}_{k_M}(u),1;\Omega)
\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon;\Omega).
\]
Applying the truncation continuity of $\mathcal{F}_{\varsigma}$ proven in Proposition~\ref{prop:contF0}, we effortlessly recover the general lower bound \eqref{e:lbcomp}. 

It remains to justify \eqref{estTk}. We execute De Giorgi's averaging-slicing method across the codomain levels. For each $k\in\mathbb N$, the energy partitions additively:
\begin{multline}\label{e:Feps* Tk2}
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_k(u_\varepsilon),v_\varepsilon;\Omega)
=\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon;\{|u_\varepsilon|\leq a_k\})\\
+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_k(u_\varepsilon),v_\varepsilon;\{a_k<|u_\varepsilon|< a_{k+1}\})
+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(0,v_\varepsilon;\{|u_\varepsilon|\geq a_{k+1}\}).
\end{multline}
Due to the structural growth limits \eqref{eq:growth-Phi} and the non-expansiveness of $\mathcal{T}_k$, the transition layer energy is bounded by
\begin{align}\label{e:Feps* Tk 22}
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_k(u_\varepsilon),v_\varepsilon;\{a_k<|u_\varepsilon|<a_{k+1}\})
&\leq c\int_{\{a_k<|u_\varepsilon|< a_{k+1}\}}\varphi(\varepsilon f^2(v_\varepsilon))\Phi(\nabla u_\varepsilon)\textup{d}x\notag\\
&\quad +c\mathcal{L}^n(\{a_k<|u_\varepsilon|<a_{k+1}\})
+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(0,v_\varepsilon;\{a_k<|u_\varepsilon|< a_{k+1}\}).
\end{align}
Summing \eqref{e:Feps* Tk2} and \eqref{e:Feps* Tk 22}, and subsequently averaging over $k \in \{M+1, \dots, 2M\}$, ensures the existence of a highly favorable index $k_{M,\varepsilon}$ fulfilling
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_{k_{M,\varepsilon}}(u_\varepsilon),v_\varepsilon;\Omega)
&\leq \frac{1}{M}\sum_{k=M+1}^{2M}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{T}_k(u_\varepsilon),v_\varepsilon;\Omega)\leq \Big(1+\frac{c}{M}\Big)\mathcal{F}_\varepsilon(u_\varepsilon,v_\varepsilon;\Omega)
+c\,\mathcal{L}^n(\{|u_\varepsilon|>a_M\}).\end{align*}
As $\varepsilon\to0$, the pigeonhole principle allows us to extract a subsequence for which $k_{M,\varepsilon} \equiv k_M$ locks into a constant value independent of $\varepsilon$, verifying \eqref{estTk} and concluding the proof.

the monotonicity of $\omega$ and the triangle inequality for
$\gamma$ give
\[
\mathcal{G}_\varepsilon(v_\varepsilon^i;D_i)
\le
c\big(\mathcal{G}_\varepsilon(v_\varepsilon^A;D_i)
+\mathcal{G}_\varepsilon(v_\varepsilon^B;D_i)\big)
+\frac{cM^2\varepsilon}{\delta^2}
\int_{D_i}|v_\varepsilon^A-v_\varepsilon^B|^2\,\textup{d}x.
\]
Since $\eta_\varepsilon\to0$ as $\varepsilon\to0$, summing up
\eqref{e:stima iniziale limsup}--\eqref{e:i+1i}, using the preceding
estimate, and averaging over $i=2,\dots,M-1$, we find an index
$i_\varepsilon\in\{2,\ldots,M-1\}$ such that
\begin{align*}
&\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}
(u_\varepsilon^{i_\varepsilon},v_\varepsilon^{i_\varepsilon};A'\cup B)
\\
&\le
\left(1+\frac{c}{M-2}\right)
\left(
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(u_\varepsilon^A,v_\varepsilon^A;A)
+\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(u_\varepsilon^B,v_\varepsilon^B;B)
\right)
+\frac{c}{M-2}\mathcal{L}^n(B\cap(A\setminus A'))
\\
&\quad+
\frac{cM^2}{\delta^2(M-2)}
\int_{B\cap(A\setminus A')}
\big(|u_\varepsilon^A-u_\varepsilon^B|^2
+\varepsilon|v_\varepsilon^A-v_\varepsilon^B|^2\big)\,\textup{d}x.
\end{align*}
We apply this estimate below to suitably truncated recovery sequences.

\medskip
\noindent
{\bf Step 2. Truncation and diagonal argument.}

Using the same truncation argument as in Theorem~\ref{thm:lbcomp},
for every $M\ge4$ and $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small there exists
$k_{\varepsilon,M}\in\{M+1,\dots,2M\}$ such that
\begin{align}\label{e:truncation Psik}
&\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_\varepsilon
(\mathcal{T}_{k_{\varepsilon,M}}(u_\varepsilon^A),v_\varepsilon^A;A)
+\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_\varepsilon
(\mathcal{T}_{k_{\varepsilon,M}}(u_\varepsilon^B),v_\varepsilon^B;B)
\notag\\
&\le
\Big(1+\frac{c}{M}\Big)
\Big(
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_\varepsilon(u_\varepsilon^A,v_\varepsilon^A;A)
+\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_\varepsilon(u_\varepsilon^B,v_\varepsilon^B;B)
\Big)
\notag\\
&\quad+
c\mathcal{L}^n\big(
\{|u_\varepsilon^A|\ge a_{M+1}\}
\cup\{|u_\varepsilon^B|\ge a_{M+1}\}
\big),
\end{align}
for some constant $c$ independent of $\varepsilon$ and $M$.
Set
\[
U_\varepsilon^{J,M}:=\mathcal{T}_{k_{\varepsilon,M}}(u_\varepsilon^J),
\qquad J\in\{A,B\}.
\]
Since the same truncation is used for both families,
$\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{T}_k)\le1$, and
$\|\mathcal{T}_k\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^N)}\le a_{k+1}$, we have
\[
\|U_\varepsilon^{A,M}-U_\varepsilon^{B,M}\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
\le
2a_{2M+1}\|u_\varepsilon^A-u_\varepsilon^B\|_{L^1(\Omega)}
\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every fixed }M.
\]
Apply the construction and the averaged estimate of Step~1, with
$M$ layers, to $(U_\varepsilon^{A,M},v_\varepsilon^A)$ and
$(U_\varepsilon^{B,M},v_\varepsilon^B)$, and denote the resulting pair by
$(\bar u_\varepsilon^M,\bar v_\varepsilon^M)$.
By \eqref{e:rec1},
\[
\limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}
\mathcal{L}^n\big(
\{|u_\varepsilon^A|\ge a_{M+1}\}
\cup\{|u_\varepsilon^B|\ge a_{M+1}\}
\big)
\le \mathcal{L}^n(\{|u|\ge a_{M+1}\}).
\]
Thus, using \eqref{e:rec2} and \eqref{e:truncation Psik}, and
absorbing constants, we obtain
\begin{align}\label{e:truncation Psik 2}
\limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_\varepsilon(\bar u_\varepsilon^M,\bar v_\varepsilon^M;A'\cup B)
&\le
\Big(1+\frac{c}{M}\Big)
\Big(
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}''(u,1;A)
+\widehat{\mathcal{F}}''(u,1;B)
\Big)
\notag\\
&\quad+
\frac{c}{M}\mathcal{L}^n(B\cap(A\setminus A'))
+c\mathcal{L}^n(\{|u|\ge a_{M+1}\}).
\end{align}
Moreover, by the Lipschitz property of the truncations and the
definition of the glued displacement,
\[
\|\bar u_\varepsilon^M-u\|_{L^1(\Omega)}
\le
\|u_\varepsilon^A-u\|_{L^1(\Omega)}
+\|u_\varepsilon^B-u\|_{L^1(\Omega)}
+2\int_{\{|u|>a_{M+1}\}}|u|\,\textup{d}x.
\]
The phase-field construction also gives
\[
\|\bar v_\varepsilon^M-1\|_{L^1(\Omega)}
\le
\|v_\varepsilon^A-1\|_{L^1(\Omega)}
+\|v_\varepsilon^B-1\|_{L^1(\Omega)}.
\]
Since $u\in L^1(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$ and $a_{M+1}\to\infty$,
the tail terms vanish as $M\to\infty$.
A diagonal choice $M=M(\varepsilon)\to\infty$ sufficiently slowly yields $(\bar u_\varepsilon^{M(\varepsilon)},\bar v_\varepsilon^{M(\varepsilon)})
\to(u,1)$ in $L^1(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N+1})$ and
\[
\limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_\varepsilon
(\bar u_\varepsilon^{M(\varepsilon)},\bar v_\varepsilon^{M(\varepsilon)};A'\cup B)
\le
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}''(u,1;A)
+\widehat{\mathcal{F}}''(u,1;B).
\]
This proves \eqref{e:subadd} by the definition of
$\widehat{\mathcal{F}}''$.

Given a subsequence $(\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k})$ of $(\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_\varepsilon)$, we can extract (without relabeling) a further subsequence that $\overline{\Gamma}$–converges to some functional $\overline{\mathcal{F}}$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{e:additivity}
\overline{\mathcal{F}}=(\mathcal{F}')_-=(\mathcal{F}'')_-,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{F}'$ and $\mathcal{F}''$ denote the $\Gamma(L^1)$–lower and upper limits of $\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k}$, and the subscript $_-$ stands for the inner regular envelope of the corresponding functional (see \cite[Definition~16.2 and Theorem~16.9]{dalmaso1993introduction}).

We observe that $\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u,v;\cdot)$ is the restriction to open sets of a Borel measure by \cite[Theorem~14.23]{dalmaso1993introduction}. Indeed, by construction it is increasing and inner regular; additivity follows from \eqref{e:additivity} once one verifies that $(\mathcal{F}')_-$ is superadditive and $(\mathcal{F}'')_-$ is subadditive. The former is a direct consequence of the additivity of $\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_\varepsilon(u,v;\cdot)$ together with \cite[Proposition~16.12]{dalmaso1993introduction}. The latter is obtained from Lemma~\ref{l:wsub}, along the same lines as \cite[Proposition~18.4]{dalmaso1993introduction}, replacing \cite[(18.6)]{dalmaso1993introduction} with Lemma~\ref{l:wsub}.

We split the proof of \eqref{e:gammalisup estimate} into several steps. Note first that it suffices to treat the case $v=1$ $\mathcal{L}^n$–a.e. in $\Omega$.

{\bf Step 1. Diffuse estimate for $u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$.}
We first derive a global upper bound for $\mathcal{F}''$ which is sharp on the diffuse part when $u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$. Define $H:L^1(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)\times\mathcal A(\Omega)\to[0,\infty]$ by
\begin{equation}\label{e:H2}
 H(u;A):=\int_A h_{\varsigma}(\nabla u)\,\textup{d}x
\end{equation}
for $u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N)$, and $H(u;A)=\infty$ otherwise, where $h_{\varsigma}$ is given by \eqref{eq:def-h-varsigma}. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{linearest}
\mathcal{F}''(u,1;A)\leq H(u;A)
\end{equation}
for $u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N)$ and $A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)$. Assuming \eqref{linearest}, set $H^{*}:L^1(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N)\times\mathcal A(\Omega)\to[0,\infty]$ as
\begin{equation}\label{e:Hqc}
 H^{*}(u;A):=\int_A h_{\varsigma}^{\mathrm{qc}}(\nabla u)\,\textup{d}x
\end{equation}
for $u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N)$, and $H^{*}(u;A)=\infty$ otherwise. By the $L^1$–lower semicontinuity of $\mathcal{F}''$ and the relaxation theorem in \cite[Statement~III.7]{AcerbiFusco1984semicontinuity} (whose assumptions follow from Proposition~\ref{prop:bulk-growth-section2}), we deduce $\mathcal{F}''(u,1;A)\leq H^{*}(u;A).$ Then \cite[Theorem~4.1]{AmbrosioDalmaso1992} yields, for every $u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N)$,
\begin{equation}\label{e:lsc envelope H}
\mathcal H(u;A):=\mathrm{sc}^-(L^1)\hbox{-}H^{*}(u;A)
=\int_A h_{\varsigma}^{\mathrm{qc}}(\nabla u)\,\textup{d}x+\int_A h_{\varsigma}^{\mathrm{qc},\infty}\big(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} D^su\big)\,,
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{e:stima limsup diffusa}
\mathcal{F}''(u,1;A)\leq \mathcal H(u;A)
\end{equation}
for all $u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$ and $A\in\mathcal A(\Omega)$.

To verify \eqref{linearest}, consider first an affine map $u(x)=\xi x+b$ with $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}$ and $b\in\mathbb{R}^N$. By \eqref{eq:def-h-varsigma} there exists $\tau_{\xi}\in[0,\infty)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{e:optimal tauxi vettoriale}
    h_{\varsigma}(\xi)=\varphi\Big(\frac{1}{\tau_{\xi}}\Big)\Phi(\xi)+\frac{\varsigma^2}{4}\tau_\xi,
\end{equation}
with the convention that $\varphi(\frac1\tau)$ is extended continuously at $\tau=0$ by $\varphi(\infty)\in(0,\infty)$. Define $(u_k,v_k):=(u,\lambda_k)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{e:def di tau kappa}
\varepsilon_kf^2(\lambda_k)=\varepsilon_k\frac{\ell(\lambda_k)}{Q(1-\lambda_k)}=\frac{1}{\tau_k},
\end{equation}
where $\tau_k\to \tau_\xi$ as $k\to \infty$. Then {$\lambda_k\to 1$ as $k\to\infty$, thus} $(u_k,v_k)\to(u,1)$ in $L^1(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N+1})$, and
\begin{align}\label{e:gamma limsup vettoriale affine functions}
  \widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k}(u_k,v_k,A)
 &= \Big[\varphi\Big(\frac{1}{\tau_k}\Big)\Phi(\xi)+\frac{\omega(1-\lambda_k)}{4\varepsilon_k}+\eta_{\varepsilon_k} \Phi(\xi)\Big]\mathcal{L}^n(A) \nonumber\\
 & = \Big[\varphi\Big(\frac{1}{\tau_k}\Big)\Phi(\xi)+\frac{\omega(1-\lambda_k)}{4Q(1-\lambda_k)}{\ell(\lambda_k)}\tau_k+\eta_{\varepsilon_k}\Phi(\xi)\Big]\mathcal{L}^n(A).
\end{align}
Using \eqref{eq:def-varsigma}, $\tau_k\to \tau_\xi$ and $\eta_\varepsilon=o(\varepsilon)$, we get
\[
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k}(u_{k},v_{k};A)
{=} h_{\varsigma}(\xi)\mathcal{L}^n(A)=H(u,A),
\]
which proves \eqref{linearest} for affine $u$ by \eqref{e:optimal tauxi vettoriale}–\eqref{e:gamma limsup vettoriale affine functions}.

Next, let $u\in C^0(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$ be piecewise affine, say $u(x)=\sum_{i=1}^M(\xi_i x+b_i)\mathbf 1_{\Omega_i}(x)$ with pairwise disjoint $\Omega_i\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)$ of Lipschitz boundary and $\mathcal{L}^n(\Omega\setminus\cup_i \Omega_i)=0$. Set $u_k:=u$ and $v_k:=\sum_{i=1}^M \lambda_k^i\chi_i$, where for each $i$ and $k$, $\varepsilon_k f^2(\lambda_{k}^i)= \frac{1}{\tau^i_{k}}$ as in \eqref{e:def di tau kappa}, and $\{\chi_i\}$ is a partition of unity subordinate to the cover $\{\Omega_i^\delta\}$: $\sum_{i=1}^M \chi_i(x)=1$ on $\Omega$,
\begin{equation*}
\chi_i\in C_c^\infty(\Omega^\delta_i), \qquad 0\leq \chi_i\leq 1, \qquad \chi_i\equiv 1 \, \textup{on $\Omega_i^{-\delta}$} \qquad \textup{and} \qquad |\nabla \chi_i| \leq \frac{2}{\delta} \quad \textup{$i=1,\dots,M,$}
\end{equation*}
adopting the following conventions
\begin{equation}
    B^{\delta}:=\{x\in \mathbb{R}^n \, :\, \operatorname{dist}(x,B)<\delta\} \qquad \textup{and} \qquad B^{-\delta}:=\{x\in B \, :\, \operatorname{dist}(x,\partial B)>\delta\},
\end{equation}
for every $\delta>0$ and $B\subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$. Since $\sum_{i=1}^M\chi_i=1$ on $\Omega$, we have $1-v^k=\sum_{i=1}^M (1-\lambda^i_k)\chi_i\le 1-\lambda^{i_k}_k$, for some $i_k$. Arguing as in \eqref{e:gamma limsup vettoriale affine functions}, using the monotonicity of $\omega$ and \eqref{e:gamma lower euclidean suppenergy},
\begin{align}\label{e:stima lim sup caso affine generale}
& \limsup_{k\to\infty}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k}(u_{k},v_{k};A)
\le \sum_{i=1}^M \mathcal{L}^n(\Omega_i^{-\delta}\cap A)h_{\varsigma}(\xi_i)\nonumber\\
&\quad+\limsup_{k\to \infty} \Big[\Big(C_u+\ell(\lambda^{i_k}_k)\tau^{i_k}_{k}\frac{\omega(1-\lambda^{i_k}_k)}{Q(1-\lambda^{i_k}_k)}+\frac{4\varepsilon_kMC^2_\gamma}{\delta^2}\Big)\sum_{i=1}^M \mathcal{L}^n((\Omega^\delta_i\setminus \Omega^{-\delta}_i)\cap A)\Big],
\end{align}
where $C_u:= (\varphi(\infty)+1)\max_{i=1,\dots,M}\Phi(\xi_i)$. Since $\lambda^{i_k}_k\to1$, $(u_k,v_k)\to(u,1)$ in $L^1(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N+1})$, and letting $\delta\downarrow0$ in \eqref{e:stima lim sup caso affine generale} gives
\[
 \mathcal{F}''(u,1;A)\le \sum_{i=1}^M \mathcal{L}^n(\Omega_i\cap A)h_{\varsigma}(\xi_i)=H(u;A).
\]
Finally, for general $u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$, extend $u$ (still denoted by $u$) to $W^{1,1}_0(\Omega',\mathbb{R}^N)$ for some bounded $\Omega'\supset\supset \Omega$ (using that $\Omega$ is Lipschitz), approximate by piecewise affine $u_k\to u$ in $W^{1,1}(\Omega',\mathbb{R}^N)$ (see \cite[Chapter X, Prop.~2.1]{ekeland1999convex}), and conclude by the continuity of $H$ in $W^{1,1}$ and the $L^1$–lower semicontinuity of $\mathcal{F}''$.

\medskip
{\bf Step 2. Inner regularity of $\mathcal{F}''(u,1;\cdot)$ and existence of the $\Gamma(L^1)$–limit for $u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$.}
We claim that for $u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$,
\begin{equation}\label{inner}
\mathcal{F}''(u,1;\cdot)=(\mathcal{F}'')_-(u,1;\cdot).
\end{equation}
Given $A$ open and $\delta>0$, choose $A'\subset\subset A''\subset\subset A$ and an open set $C$ with $A\setminus A'\subset C$ so that $\mathcal H(u;C)\le\delta$, where $\mathcal H$ is defined in \eqref{e:lsc envelope H}. By Lemma~\ref{l:wsub} and \eqref{e:stima limsup diffusa},
\[
\mathcal{F}''(u,1;A)\le \mathcal{F}''(u,1;A'\cup C)\le \mathcal{F}''(u,1;A'')+\mathcal H(u;C)\le \mathcal{F}''(u,1;A'')+\delta,
\]
which gives \eqref{inner}. Hence, from \eqref{e:additivity},
\begin{equation}\label{e:Gammabarlimite uguale al gammalimite}
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u,1;\cdot)\le \mathcal{F}'(u,1;\cdot)\le \mathcal{F}''(u,1;\cdot)=\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u,1;\cdot),
\end{equation}
so the $\Gamma$–limit of $\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k}(u,1;\cdot)$ exists and coincides with $\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u,1;\cdot)$ for every $u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$.

\medskip
{\bf Step 3. Integral representation of the $\Gamma(L^1)$–limit on $BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)\times\{1\}$.}
To represent $\overline{\mathcal{F}}$ in integral form and to estimate its densities, we consider the coercive perturbation
\[
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_\lambda(u,1;A):=\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u,1;A)+\lambda{|Du|(A)},
\]
for $u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$, $A\in \mathcal{A}(\Omega)$ and $\lambda\in(0,1]$. By \eqref{e:bulk-growth-h}, \eqref{e:lbcomp}, \eqref{e:stima limsup diffusa}, and \eqref{e:Gammabarlimite uguale al gammalimite},
\[
{\lambda|Du|(\Omega)-c\mathcal{L}^n(\Omega)\le
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_\lambda(u,1;\Omega)\le
c(|Du|(\Omega)+\mathcal{L}^n(\Omega))\,,}
\]
for some $c>0$ and all $u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$. Moreover, translation invariance holds:
\[
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k}(u(\cdot-z),v(\cdot-z);z+A)=\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k}(u,v;A),\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k}(u+b,v;A)=\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k}(u,v;A),
\]
whence the analogous properties for $\overline{\mathcal{F}}$.
By \cite[Theorem~3.12]{bouchitte1998global}, for $u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$ and $A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)$,
\[
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}(u,1;A)=
\int_A h_\lambda(\nabla u)\textup{d}x
+\int_A h_\lambda^\infty({\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} D^cu})
+\int_{J_u\cap A}g_\lambda([u],\nu_u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1},
\]
with
\begin{align}\label{e:flambdadff}
h_\lambda(\xi):=\limsup_{r\downarrow0}\frac{1}{r^n}
\inf\Big\{\overline{\mathcal{F}}_\lambda(w,1;Q_r):
\,w\in BV(Q_r,\mathbb{R}^N),\;
w(x)=\xi x\text{ on }\partial Q_r\Big\},
\end{align}
for $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}$,
\begin{equation}\label{e:flambda}
g_\lambda(\zeta,\nu):=\limsup_{r\downarrow0}\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}
\inf\Big\{\overline{\mathcal{F}}_\lambda(w,1;Q_r^\nu):
\,w\in BV(Q_r^\nu,\mathbb{R}^N),\;
w=u_{\zeta,\nu}\text{ on }\partial Q_r^\nu\Big\},
\end{equation}
for $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^N$, $\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$, and $h^\infty_\lambda(\xi):=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{h_\lambda(t\xi)}{t}$. Using \eqref{e:stima limsup diffusa},
\begin{equation}\label{lambdalin}
h_\lambda(\xi)\leq
\frac{1}{r^n}\overline{\mathcal{F}}_\lambda(\xi x,1;Q_r)
\leq h_\varsigma^{\mathrm{qc}}(\xi)+\lambda|\xi|,
\end{equation}
hence
\begin{equation}\label{recessione}
h_\lambda^\infty(\xi)\leq
h_\varsigma^{{\mathrm{qc},\infty}}(\xi)+\lambda|\xi|.
\end{equation}
We now prove
\begin{equation}\label{e:upbvsalto}
g_\lambda(\zeta,\nu)\leq g(\zeta,\nu)+\lambda|\zeta|,
\end{equation}
for all $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^N$, $\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$. From \eqref{e:flambda},
\begin{align}\label{sfc}
g_\lambda(\zeta,\nu)
\leq\limsup_{r\downarrow0}\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_\lambda(u_{\zeta,\nu},1;Q_r^\nu)
=\limsup_{r\downarrow0}\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u_{\zeta,\nu},1;Q_r^\nu)
+\lambda|\zeta|.
\end{align}
By the definition of $\overline\mathcal{F}$, for any
$(\tilde u_k,\tilde v_k)\to(u_{\zeta,\nu},1)$
in $L^1(Q_r^\nu;\mathbb{R}^{N+1})$,
\begin{equation}\label{recupp}
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u_{\zeta,\nu},1;Q_r^\nu)
\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k}(\tilde u_k,\tilde v_k;Q_r^\nu).
\end{equation}
Thus, it suffices to construct a suitable recovery sequence
$(\tilde u_k,\tilde v_k)$ depending on $r$, $\zeta$, and $\nu$.
By applying Proposition~\ref{propuvjump} with
$\varepsilon_k^*:=\varepsilon_k/r$ and $\eta_k^*:=\eta_{\varepsilon_k}$,
we obtain sequences
$(u_k^*,v_k^*)\to(u_{\zeta,\nu},1)$
in $L^2(Q^\nu,\mathbb{R}^{N+1})$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{estg}
\lim_{k\to\infty}
\mathcal{F}^\infty_{\varepsilon_k^*}(u_k^*,v_k^*;Q^\nu)
=g(\zeta,\nu)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lim_{k\to\infty}
\eta_k^*\|\nabla u_k^*\|_{L^2(Q^\nu)}^2=0.
\end{equation}
Define $\tilde u_k(y):=u_k^*\!\left(\frac{y}{r}\right)$ on $Q_r^\nu$, so that $\tilde u_k\to u_{\zeta,\nu}$ in $L^2(Q_r^\nu,\mathbb{R}^N)$. Fix $\tau>0$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$.
By \eqref{eq:uniform-recession-estimate}, we have
$\Phi(\xi)\leq(1+\tau)\Phi_\infty(\xi)$ for $|\xi|$ large,
and hence, for all $\xi$,
\begin{equation}\label{e:stima con recessione quadratica in upperbound vettoriale}
\Phi(\xi)\leq(1+\tau)\Phi_\infty(\xi)+C(\tau).
\end{equation}
Let $K\geq1$ be the constant in
Lemma~\ref{l:quasi monotonia funzione di degradamento}.
For every $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small (depending on $\delta$), define
\[
\textstyle \delta_\varepsilon:=
\max\Big\{t\in[0,1):
\varepsilon f^2(t)\leq\frac{\delta}{K}\Big\}.
\]
Since $f^2$ is continuous on $[0,1)$ and diverges as $t\uparrow1$,
this maximum is well defined, belongs to $(0,1)$, and satisfies $\varepsilon f^2(\delta_\varepsilon)=\frac{\delta}{K}.$ Moreover, $\delta_\varepsilon\to1$ as $\varepsilon\to0$, since every fixed $t<1$
belongs to the above sublevel set for $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small.
By Lemma~\ref{l:quasi monotonia funzione di degradamento},
\[
\varepsilon f^2(t)\leq K\varepsilon f^2(\delta_\varepsilon)=\delta
\qquad\text{for every }t\in[0,\delta_\varepsilon],
\]
whereas the maximality of $\delta_\varepsilon$ gives
$f^2(t)>f^2(\delta_\varepsilon)$ for every $t\in(\delta_\varepsilon,1)$.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{e:stima di soglia per f in upperbound vettoriale}
\varepsilon f^2(t\wedge\delta_\varepsilon)\leq\delta,
\qquad
f^2(t\wedge\delta_\varepsilon)\leq f^2(t)
\qquad\text{for every }t\in[0,1).
\end{equation}

Setting $v^*_{k,\delta}:=v_k^*\wedge\delta_{\varepsilon_k}$,
the bulk term does not increase by
\eqref{e:stima di soglia per f in upperbound vettoriale}.
The gradient term does not increase either, since
\[
\nabla v^*_{k,\delta}
=\mathbf1_{\{v_k^*<\delta_{\varepsilon_k}\}}\nabla v_k^*
\qquad\text{a.e. in }Q^\nu.
\]
Thus, by the monotonicity of $\omega$ and $\mathcal{L}^n(Q^\nu)=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{e:prima approssimazione per troncamento in upperbound vettoriale}
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_k^*}^\infty(u_k^*,v_{k,\delta}^*;Q^\nu)
\leq
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_k^*}^\infty(u_k^*,v_k^*;Q^\nu)
+r\frac{\omega(1-\delta_{\varepsilon_k})}{4\varepsilon_k}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, using
$\varepsilon_k f^2(\delta_{\varepsilon_k})=\delta/K$
and \eqref{eq:def-varsigma},
\begin{equation}\label{e:potential cost threshold upperbound vettoriale}
\frac{\omega(1-\delta_{\varepsilon_k})}{4\varepsilon_k}
=\frac{K}{4\delta}\ell(\delta_{\varepsilon_k})
\frac{\omega(1-\delta_{\varepsilon_k})}{Q(1-\delta_{\varepsilon_k})}
\longrightarrow\frac{K\varsigma^2}{4\delta}
\qquad\text{as }k\to\infty.
\end{equation}
Now letting
$\tilde v_{k,\delta}(y):=v^*_{k,\delta}\!\left(\frac{y}{r}\right)$,
we have $\nabla\tilde v_{k,\delta}(y)
=\frac{1}{r}(\nabla v^*_{k,\delta})\!\left(\frac{y}{r}\right)$, thus from the $2$-homogeneity of $\gamma^2$, we get
\begin{equation*}
\textstyle \int_{Q_r^\nu}
\varepsilon_k\gamma(\nabla\tilde v_{k,\delta})^2\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} y
=
r^{n-1}\int_{Q^\nu}
\varepsilon_k^*\gamma(\nabla v^*_{k,\delta})^2\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d} x.
\end{equation*}
Using analogously the $2$-homogeneity of $\Phi_\infty$,
changing variable we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eqdeltaresc}
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_k}^\infty
(\tilde u_k,\tilde v_{k,\delta};Q_r^\nu)
=
r^{n-1}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_k^*}^\infty
(u_k^*,v_{k,\delta}^*;Q^\nu),
\quad
\|\nabla\tilde u_k\|_{L^2(Q_r^\nu)}^2
=
r^{n-2}\|\nabla u_k^*\|_{L^2(Q^\nu)}^2.
\end{equation}

Since $\varphi$ is differentiable at $0$ (cf. (H4)),
there exists a modulus of continuity $\vartheta$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{e:modulo continuità derivata di varphi in upperbound vettoriale}
\varphi(\varepsilon_k f^2(\tilde v_{k,\delta}))
\leq
(1+\vartheta(\delta))\varphi'(0^+)
\varepsilon_k f^2(\tilde v_{k,\delta}),
\end{equation}
for every $k$ sufficiently large.
Combining \eqref{eqdeltaresc},
\eqref{e:modulo continuità derivata di varphi in upperbound vettoriale},
\eqref{e:prima approssimazione per troncamento in upperbound vettoriale},
and \eqref{e:stima con recessione quadratica in upperbound vettoriale},
and using $\varepsilon_k f^2(\tilde v_{k,\delta})\leq\delta<1$, we deduce
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_k}(\tilde u_k,\tilde v_{k,\delta};Q_r^\nu)
&\leq
(1+\vartheta(\delta))
\int_{Q_r^\nu}\!\Big(
\varphi'(0^+)\varepsilon_k f^2(\tilde v_{k,\delta})
\Phi(\nabla\tilde u_k)
+\frac{\omega(1-\tilde v_{k,\delta})}{4\varepsilon_k}
+\varepsilon_k\gamma(\nabla\tilde v_{k,\delta})^2
\Big)\,\textup{d}x
\\
&\leq
(1+\vartheta(\delta))\Big(
r^{n-1}(1+\tau)
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_k^*}^\infty(u_k^*,v_{k,\delta}^*;Q^\nu)
+r^n C(\tau)
\Big)
\\
&\leq
(1+\vartheta(\delta))\Big(
r^{n-1}(1+\tau)
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_k^*}^\infty(u_k^*,v_k^*;Q^\nu)
+r^n(1+\tau)\frac{\omega(1-\delta_{\varepsilon_k})}{4\varepsilon_k}
+r^n C(\tau)
\Big),
\end{align*}
where $C(\tau)$ may be enlarged and is independent of $k$, $r$,
and $\delta$. Moreover, from \eqref{eq:growth-Phi} and \eqref{eqdeltaresc},
\[
\textstyle \eta_{\varepsilon_k}\!\int_{Q_r^\nu}
\Phi(\nabla\tilde u_k)\,\textup{d}x
\leq
C_\Phi\eta_{\varepsilon_k}r^{n-2}
\|\nabla u_k^*\|_{L^2(Q^\nu)}^2
+C_\Phi\eta_{\varepsilon_k}r^n.
\]

For every fixed $r>0$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$,
since $\delta_{\varepsilon_k}\to1$, we have $(\tilde u_k,\tilde v_{k,\delta})
\to(u_{\zeta,\nu},1)$ in $L^2(Q_r^\nu,\mathbb{R}^{N+1})$. Summing up and passing to the limit as $k\to\infty$,
by \eqref{recupp}, \eqref{estg}, and
\eqref{e:potential cost threshold upperbound vettoriale}, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u_{\zeta,\nu},1;Q_r^\nu)
\leq
(1+\vartheta(\delta))\Big(
r^{n-1}(1+\tau)g(\zeta,\nu)
+r^n(1+\tau)\frac{K\varsigma^2}{4\delta}
+C(\tau)r^n
\Big).
\end{equation*}
Dividing by $r^{n-1}$ and letting $r\downarrow0$
with $\delta$ and $\tau$ fixed, in view of \eqref{sfc}, 
\[
g_\lambda(\zeta,\nu)
\leq
(1+\vartheta(\delta))(1+\tau)g(\zeta,\nu)
+\lambda|\zeta|.
\]
Letting first $\delta\downarrow0$ and then $\tau\downarrow0$,
inequality~\eqref{e:upbvsalto} follows. Finally, letting $\lambda\downarrow0$,
inequalities \eqref{lambdalin}, \eqref{recessione},
and \eqref{e:upbvsalto} yield, for every $u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^N)$, $\overline{\mathcal{F}}(u,1;\Omega)
\leq\mathcal{F}_\varsigma(u,1;\Omega).$ Combined with the lower bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:lbcompBV}),
this identifies the $\Gamma$–limit of the subsequence
$\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon_k}$.
By Urysohn’s property~\cite[Proposition~8.3]{dalmaso1993introduction},
the result extends to the entire family $\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_\varepsilon$.

\medskip
{\bf Step 4. Extension to $GBV$.}
To extend \eqref{e:gammalisup estimate} to $u\in (GBV\cap L^1(\Omega))^N$, we argue by truncation. For $k\in\mathbb{N}$, let $\mathcal{T}_k$ be as in \eqref{e:Tk}. By Steps~1–3,
\[
 \mathcal{F}''(\mathcal{T}_k(u),1;\Omega)\le \mathcal{F}_{\varsigma}(\mathcal{T}_k(u),1;\Omega).
\]
The claim follows by the $L^1$–lower semicontinuity of $\mathcal{F}''$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:contF0}.
