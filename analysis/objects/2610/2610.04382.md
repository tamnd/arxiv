The projection $p:\mathrm{M}_{n,k}\to \mathrm{R}_n$ is a locally trivial fibration, where the fiber is $F:=\operatorname{Conf}_k(M)$, with $M=\mathrm{Int}(\mathbb{B}^3)\setminus C$.

<!-- propositionx-1: body_md -->
For every $k\geq 1$ we have the following short exact sequence:

    $$1\longrightarrow F_n^k\overset{\iota_*}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{M}_{n,k}\overset{p_*}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{LB}_n\longrightarrow1.$$
    The short exact sequence splits, under the section $s_*$, and thus we have $\mathcal{M}_{n,k}\cong F_n^k\rtimes\mathrm{LB}_n$, where $\mathrm{LB}_n$ acts on $F_n^k$ in the following way: for $\beta\in\mathrm{LB}_n$, whose induced element in $\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$ is denoted by $\phi_\beta$, and for $(u_1,\dots,u_k)\in F_n^k$ we have $$s_*(\beta)^{-1}(u_1,\dots,u_k)s_*(\beta)=(\phi_\beta(u_1), \dots, \phi_\beta(u_k)).$$

    In particular, for $k=1$ we have $$\mathcal{M}_{n,1}\cong U\rtimes\mathrm{LB}_n,\ \text{where}\ U\cong F_n.$$

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
The \emph{mapping class group} of $M$ with respect to $N$, denoted $\mathrm{MCG}(M,N)$, is the group of
isotopy classes of elements of $\operatorname{Homeo}(M,N)$. The \emph{pure mapping class group} $\mathrm{PMCG}(M,N)$ is
the subgroup of classes sending each component of $N$ to itself.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
The loop braid group on $n$ components is $\mathrm{LB}_n:=\mathrm{MCG}(\mathbb{B}^3,C)$, and the \emph{pure loop
braid group} is $\mathrm{PLB}_n:=\mathrm{PMCG}(\mathbb{B}^3,C)$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $\mathrm{R}_n$ be the space of configurations of $n$ disjoint, pairwise unlinked, unordered Euclidean
circles contained in $\mathrm{Int}(\mathbb{B}^3)$, lying in planes parallel to a fixed plane $P_0$, and
let $\widetilde{\mathrm{R}}_n$ be the corresponding space of ordered configurations, so that
$\mathrm{R}_n=\widetilde{\mathrm{R}}_n/S_n$, where $S_n$ is the symmetric group of degree $n$. We set $\mathrm{LB}_n:=\pi_1(\mathrm{R}_n,C)$.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{bre}
The group $\mathrm{LB}_n$ admits the presentation with generators
$\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1},\rho_1,\dots,\rho_{n-1}$ and relations
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}$, for $i=1,\dots,n-2$,
\item $\sigma_i\sigma_j=\sigma_j\sigma_i$, for $|i-j|>1$,
\item $\rho_i\rho_{i+1}\rho_i=\rho_{i+1}\rho_i\rho_{i+1}$, for $i=1,\dots,n-2$,
\item $\rho_i\rho_j=\rho_j\rho_i$, for $|i-j|>1$,
\item $\rho_i^2=1$, for $i=1,\dots,n-1$,
\item $\sigma_i\rho_j=\rho_j\sigma_i$, for $|i-j|>1$,
\item $\sigma_i\rho_{i+1}\rho_i=\rho_{i+1}\rho_i\sigma_{i+1}$, for $i=1,\dots,n-2$,
\item $\rho_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\rho_{i+1}$, for $i=1,\dots,n-2$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{Nielsen}
Let $x,y\in\operatorname{Fix}(f)$ and $R(x)=(f\circ \ell)\ell^{-1}\in \pi$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Let $\gamma\in\pi$. Replacing $\ell$ by $\gamma\ell$,
replaces $R(x)$ by $f_{\pi}(\gamma)R(x)\gamma^{-1}$. Thus, the class of $R(x)$ in $\mathcal{R}(f_\pi)$
is independent of the choice of $\ell$.
\item[(ii)] The fixed points $x$ and $y$ are Nielsen equivalent in $X$ if and only if $R(x)$ and $R(y)$ are Reidemeister equivalent.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
(i) We have $f\circ(\gamma\ell)=(f\circ\gamma)(f\circ\ell)$ and
$(\gamma\ell)^{-1}=\ell^{-1}\gamma^{-1}$. Thus, we get
$(f\circ(\gamma\ell))(\gamma\ell)^{-1}=(f\circ\gamma)(f\circ\ell)\ell^{-1}\gamma^{-1}$. It follows that these two loops are Reidemeister equivalent and thus they represent the same Reidemeister class.

(ii) Suppose $l$ is a path from $x$ to $y$ such that $l\simeq f\circ\ell$ keeping the endpoints fixed. We choose a path $\ell_x$ from $\ast_1$ to $x$ and we choose the path $\ell_y$ from $\ast_1$ to $y$ to be $\ell_xl$. Then
$
(f\circ\ell_y)\ell_y^{-1}=(f\circ\ell_x)(f\circ l)l^{-1}\ell_x^{-1}$. But $(f\circ\ell_x)(f\circ l)l^{-1}\ell_x^{-1}\simeq (f\circ\ell_x)\ell_x^{-1}$. Thus, $R(y)=R(x)$. Conversely, if
$R(y)=f_{\pi}(\gamma)R(x)\gamma^{-1}$, for $\gamma\in \pi$, then by replacing $\ell_x$ by $\gamma^{-1}\ell_x$ we get that $R(x)=R(y)$ and hence $(f\circ\ell_x)\ell_x^{-1}=(f\circ\ell_y)\ell_y^{-1}$. Hence, we can define the path
$l:=\ell_x^{-1}\ell_y$ from $x$ to $y$ that satisfies the relation $l\simeq f\circ l$ while keeping the
endpoints fixed.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
For $n, k\in \mathbb{N}$ we define the $n,k$ \textit{mixed configuration space} to be the configuration space that consists of distinct circles and distinct ordered points. That is, $$\mathrm{M}_{n,k}:=\{(c, p_1,\dots, p_k)\ |\ c\in \mathrm{R}_n,\ p_i\neq p_j\ \text{for}\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq k\ \text{and}\ p_i \in \mathrm{Int}(\mathbb{B}^3)\setminus c\}.$$

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{fibration}
The projection $p:\mathrm{M}_{n,k}\to \mathrm{R}_n$ is a locally trivial fibration, with fiber $F:=\operatorname{Conf}_k(M)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We want to prove that for every $c\in \mathrm{R}_n$ there exists a neighborhood $V$ of $c$ and a homeomorphism $\phi: V\times F\to p^{-1}(V)$, with $p\circ\phi=pr_V$, where $pr_V:V\times F\to V$ is the projection to $V$. From this it follows that $p^{-1}(c)\cong F$ for every $c\in \mathrm{R}_n$.

Suppose that for some neighborhood $V$ of $c_0\in \mathrm{R}_n$ we have, for each $c\in V$, a homeomorphism $\Phi_c$ of $\mathbb{B}^3$, such that $\Phi_c(c_0)=c$ as subsets of $\mathbb{B}^3$ and such that the map from $V$ to $\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{B}^3)$ that takes $c$ to $\Phi_c$ is continuous. Let $F_0:=\operatorname{Conf}_k(\mathrm{Int}(\mathbb{B}^3)\setminus c_0)$. We can then define the map $\phi_0: V\times F_0\to p^{-1}(V)$ given by $$\phi_0(c,y_1,\dots,y_k)=(c,\Phi_c(y_1),\dots,\Phi_c(y_k)).$$
We note that the image of $V\times F_0$ under $\phi_0$ is indeed in $p^{-1}(V)\subset\mathrm{M}_{n,k}$. For $y_i\notin c_0$ then $\Phi_c(y_i)\notin \Phi_c(c_0)=c$, since $\Phi_c$ is injective. Thus, the points $\Phi_c(y_i)$ are pairwise distinct and do not lie on $c$. Moreover, $\phi_0$ is a homeomorphism with inverse
$\phi_0^{-1}(c,y_1,\dots,y_k)=(c,\Phi^{-1}_c(y_1),\dots,\Phi^{-1}_c(y_k))$ and $p\circ\phi_0=pr_V$. Indeed, the
evaluation map $\mathrm{ev}:\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{B}^3)\times\mathbb{B}^3\to\mathbb{B}^3$, given by $\mathrm{ev}(\Psi,y):=\Psi(y)$, is continuous for the
compact-open topology, $\mathbb{B}^3$ being compact, so each coordinate 
of $\phi_0$ is continuous. Since inversion is also continuous on $\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{B}^3)$, the same argument applies to $\phi_0^{-1}$. We conclude that $\phi_0$ is a trivialization of $p$ over $V$ with fiber $F_0$. In addition, note that $\mathrm{R}_n$ is path connected, since an appropriate shrinking of the radii followed by a translation of the centers joins any configuration to a standard one, the circles remaining disjoint throughout. So isotopy extension provides a homeomorphism $h$ of $\mathbb{B}^3$ with $h(C)=c_0$ from which we can deduce a homeomorphism $\tilde{h}$ from $F$ to $F_0$, where $(y_1,\dots,y_k)$ is mapped to $(h(y_1),\dots,h(y_k))$. As a result, we can construct the desired homeomorphism $\phi:=\phi_0\circ(\mathrm{id}_V\times\tilde{h})$  from $V\times F\to p^{-1}(V)$ such that $p\circ\phi=pr_V$. Therefore, it suffices to construct the family of homeomorphisms $\{\Phi_c\}_{c\in V}$ of $\mathbb{B}^3$.

Fix an ordering $c_0=(\rho^0_1,\dots,\rho^0_n)$ of the circles of $c_0$, that is, a preimage of
$c_0$ under the covering $\widetilde{\mathrm{R}}_n\to\mathrm{R}_n$. Since the map is a covering, there is a neighborhood
$V_1$ of $c_0$ and a unique continuous way of ordering the circles of every $c\in V_1$ that agrees
with the chosen ordering at $c=c_0$. For $c\in V_1$ we may therefore write
$c=(\rho_1,\dots,\rho_n)$. Note that each $\rho_i^0$ is a circle determined by its center $z_i^0$ and its radius $r_i^0$. That is, $\rho_i^0=(z_i^0, r_i^0)$. 
We choose pairwise disjoint compact balls $T_1,\dots,T_n$ in the interior of $\frac{3}{4}\mathbb{B}^3$, such that $\rho_i^0$ lives in the interior of $T_i$. Moreover, we choose bump functions $\chi_i:\mathbb{R}^3\to[0,1]$ supported in $T_i$ and equal to $1$ on a neighborhood of $\rho_i^0$. In addition, for any $c=(\rho_1,\dots,\rho_n)\in V_1$ we define the affine transformation $A_i^c$, which sends $\rho_i^0$ of $c_0$ onto $\rho_i$ of $c$. That is, $A_i^c:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^3$ is given by $$A_i^c(y):=z_i+\frac{r_i}{r_i^0}(y-z_i^0),$$ which is actually a dilation followed by a translation that carries $(z_i^0, r_i^0)$ to $(z_i, r_i)$. Note that $A_i^c$ has no rotational part and it maps each plane to a parallel plane. Finally, for each $c\in V_1$ we define $\Phi_c:\mathbb{B}^3\to\mathbb{B}^3$ by $$\Phi_c(y):=y+\sum_{i=1}^n\chi_i(y)(A_i^c(y)-y).$$

By construction, $\Phi_c$ is continuous, $\Phi_{c_0}=\mathrm{id}$, $\Phi_c$ is the identity outside the set $\bigcup_iT_i$ and thus also near $\partial\mathbb{B}^3$. Since on a neighborhood of $\rho^0_i$ the map $\chi_i$ equals $1$, then $\Phi_c$ agrees with $A^c_i$. As a result we have $\Phi_c(\rho^0_i)=A^c_i(\rho^0_i)=\rho_i$ for each $1\leq i\leq n$. That is, $\Phi_c(c_0)=c$ as subsets of $\mathbb{B}^3$.

In what follows we will prove that $\Phi_c$ is a local diffeomorphism, injective and surjective. As $c$ tends to $c_0$, the maps $A_i^c$ converge to the identity in $C^1$ on the compact set
$\bigcup_iT_i$, since $A^c_i(y)-y=(z_i-z^0_i)+\left(\frac{r_i}{r^0_i}-1\right)(y-z^0_i)$ has both its
values and its derivative tending to $0$. As the $\chi_i$ are fixed, it follows that
$\|D\Phi_c-I\|$ tends to $0$ as well, so there is a neighborhood $V:=V_2\subseteq V_1$ of $c_0$ and a constant
$\lambda<1$ with $\|D\Phi_c-I\|\le\lambda$ for all $c\in V$. Thus, $\Phi_c$ has invertible derivative and so it is a local diffeomorphism. Moreover, $\Phi_c$ is injective. Since $\|D\Phi_c-I\|\le\lambda$, by the mean value inequality, it follows that $|(\Phi_c(y)-y)-(\Phi_c(y')-y')|\leq\lambda|y-y'|$ and therefore
$
|\Phi_c(y)-\Phi_c(y')|\ \ge\ |y-y'|-\lambda\,|y-y'|\ =\ (1-\lambda)\,|y-y'| .
$
Since $\lambda<1$, the equality $\Phi_c(y)=\Phi_c(y')$ forces $y=y'$. Finally, $\Phi_c$ is surjective. The image of $\mathbb{B}^3$ under $\Phi_c$ is both open and closed. It is open because the points of $\partial\mathbb{B}^3$ are fixed by
$\Phi_c$, and at interior points $\Phi_c$ is a local homeomorphism, hence an open map. It is also
closed, because it is the image of the compact set $\mathbb{B}^3$ under the continuous map $\Phi_c$. As $\mathbb{B}^3$ is connected and the image is
nonempty, $\Phi_c(\mathbb{B}^3)=\mathbb{B}^3$.

All together, we deduce that $\Phi_c$ is a diffeomorphism of $\mathbb{B}^3$, since it is a bijective local diffeomorphism. In
particular it is a homeomorphism of $\mathbb{B}^3$, which is all we need. Moreover, the map from $V$ to $\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{B}^3)$ that takes $c$ to $\Phi_c$ is continuous since $\Phi_c(y)$ depends continuously on $(c,y)$ and $\mathbb{B}^3$ is compact. This is the required family
$\{\Phi_c\}_{c\in V}$ and this completes the proof.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{section}
The marked base-points $\ast_1,\dots,\ast_k$ in the collar of $\partial\mathbb{B}^3$ are chosen once and for all and are
independent of the configuration in $\mathrm{M}_{n,k}$. Thus, the map
$s\colon \mathrm{R}_n\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}_{n,k}$ given by $s(c)=(c,\ast_1,\dots,\ast_k),$
is continuous and $p\circ s=\mathrm{id}$. Hence, $s$ is a section for the map $p: \mathrm{M}_{n,k}\to \mathrm{R}_n$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{free}
 For $k\geq1$, the following isomorphism hold: $$\pi_1(\operatorname{Conf}_k(M))\cong \pi_1(M)^k.$$
 In particular, $\pi_1(\operatorname{Conf}_k(M))\congF_n^k$, where $F_n$ denotes the free group of rank $n$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We will use transversality to prove the claim. The fat diagonal $\Delta$ of $M^k$, that is the subspace of a $M^k$ where at least two of the coordinate components are equal, is a finite union of submanifolds of dimension $3(k-1)$. The inclusion $i$ of $M^k\setminus\Delta$ into $M^k$ induces a surjective map $i^*:\pi_1(\operatorname{Conf}_k(M))=\pi_1(M^k\setminus\Delta)\to \pi_1(M^k)$. This holds because, by transversality, every map of a $1$-complex into $M^k$ may be perturbed away from the fat diagonal $\Delta$, since $1+3(k-1)<3k$. Moreover, the induced map $i^*$ is injective because, again by transversality, every map of a $2$-complex, which corresponds to null-homotopies, into $M^k$ can be perturbed away from $\Delta$, since $2+3(k-1)<3k$. Therefore, $\pi_1(\operatorname{Conf}_k(M))\cong \pi_1(M^k)$, where the isomorphism is induced by the coordinate projection. Note that $\pi_1(M^k)=\pi_1(M)^k$ and since $\pi_1(M)=F_n$ it follows that $\pi_1(\operatorname{Conf}_k(M))\congF_n^k$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{thickening}
There is a homomorphism $t:\mathcal{M}_{n,1}\to \mathrm{LB}_{n,1},$
that satisfies $q\circ t=p_*$, where $p_*:\mathcal{M}_{n,1}\to \mathrm{LB}_n$, the map induced by the projection $p:\mathrm{M}_{n,1}\to \mathrm{R}_n$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The homomorphism $t$ is induced from the map $T$ which is defined by replacing the marked point in $\mathrm{M}_{n,1}$ by a circle $c_{n+1}$ of small radius centered at the point and lying in a plane parallel to the fixed one. 
For $(c,y)\in \mathrm{M}_{n,1}$ let $T(c,y)\in\mathrm{R}_{n,1}$ be the configuration consisting of $c$
together with the circle $c_{n+1}$ of radius $\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dist}(y,c)$ centered at $y$ and
lying in the plane through $y$ parallel to $P_0$. The element $T(c,y)$ lies indeed in $\mathrm{R}_{n,1}$ since the new circle
lies in a plane parallel to $P_0$, it is contained in a ball disjoint from $c$, and it is unlinked
from the other circles. As
$\operatorname{dist}(y,c)$ is continuous and strictly positive on $\mathrm{M}_{n,1}$, the map $T$ is
continuous. Thus, it induces a homomorphism on fundamental groups, that is $t:\mathcal{M}_{n,1}\to \mathrm{LB}_{n,1}$. Finally, by construction of $T$ the equality $q\circ t=p_*$ follows and this completes the proof.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{map g}
The map $g:F_n\to \mathrm{PLB}_{n+1}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n+1})$ defined by $g(u)=\alpha_u$ is an injective homomorphism with image $U_{n+1}:=g(F_n)$ isomorphic to $F_{n}$. Moreover, for $\phi\in \mathrm{LB}_n\subset \operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$, seen as permutation-conjugacy automorphism of the free group, we have $\alpha_{\phi(u)}=\bar{\phi}\alpha_u\bar{\phi}^{-1}$, where $\bar{\phi}\in \mathrm{LB}_{n+1}\subset \operatorname{Aut}(F_{n+1})$ is the canonical extension of $\phi$, that is, $\bar{\phi}(x_i)=\phi(x_i)$ for $1\leq i \leq n$ and $\bar{\phi}(x_{n+1})=x_{n+1}$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For $u,v\in F_n$ one can verify that $\alpha_{uv}=\alpha_u\circ\alpha_v$. Moreover, $g$ is injective because if $\alpha_u=\alpha_v$ then $u^{-1}x_{n+1}u=v^{-1}x_{n+1}v$, which means that $vu^{-1}$ is in the centralizer of $x_{n+1}$ in $F_{n+1}$, which is the cyclic subgroup generated by $\langle x_{n+1}\rangle$ and thus $vu^{-1}\in \langle x_{n+1}\rangle\cap F_n=\{1\}$. Finally, $\bar{\phi}\alpha_u\bar{\phi}^{-1}(x_{n+1})=\bar{\phi}\alpha_u(x_{n+1})=\bar{\phi}(u^{-1}x_{n+1}u)=(\bar{\phi}(u))^{-1}x_{n+1}\bar{\phi}(u)$ and $\bar{\phi}\alpha_u\bar{\phi}^{-1}(x_i)=x_i$, for all $1\leq i\leq n$. Therefore, $\alpha_{\phi(u)}=\bar{\phi}\alpha_u\bar{\phi}^{-1}$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{correspondance}
Let $u\in\pi_1(M, *_1)\congF_n$ and let $a_u\in  \mathcal{M}_{n,1}$. Then, $t(a_u)=\alpha_u\in \mathrm{PLB}_{n+1}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n+1})$. In particular, $U$ is isomorphic to $U_{n+1}$ via $t$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Under the identification of $\mathrm{LB}_n$ as a subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n+1})$ we have that $t(a_u)$ is the automorphism of $F_{n+1}$ induced by the terminal homeomorphism $h$ of an ambient isotopy realizing the "thickened" motion of $a_u$, where the marked point is replaced by a circle. Thus, it suffices to compute the images of the meridians $x_1,\dots,x_{n+1}$ of $C\sqcup c_{n+1}$ under $h$, each joined to a base-point $*$ in the collar of $\partial\mathbb{B}^3$ by a fixed path $\tau$. 

 Let $u\in\pi_1(M, *_1)\congF_n$ be represented by the loop $\gamma$ in $M$ based at $*_1$. The element $t(\alpha_u)$ in $\mathrm{PLB}_{n+1}$ is the loop braid whose last circle follows $\gamma$, while the other circles $C$ stay fixed. Thus, it is realized by an ambient isotopy supported in a neighborhood $N$ of $\gamma$, containing $c_{n+1}$, which is disjoint form $C$. Hence, $h$ is the identity outside $N$.

 We represent the meridians $x_1,\dots,x_n$ by the loops $m_1,\dots,m_n$ based at $*$. The loops $\gamma$ and $m_1,\dots, m_n$ have different base-points and thus we can assume that they are disjoint and moreover we can assume that they are not inside $N$. Therefore, $x_1,\dots,x_n$ are fixed by $h$ and thus $t(a_u)(x_i)=x_i$, for all $1\leq i\leq n$.

 We represent the meridian $x_{n+1}$ by the loop $\tau m_{n+1}\bar{\tau}$, where $m_{n+1}$ is a small meridian of $c_{n+1}$. The isotopy returns $m_{n+1}$ to a meridian of $c_{n+1}$ but it drags the path $\tau$ along $\gamma$ and so $x_{n+1}$ is sent to $u^{-1}x_{n+1}u$. That is, $h(x_{n+1})=u^{-1}x_{n+1}u$. 

 Hence, we have $t(a_u)=\alpha_u$. Moreover, $a_u\in U, \alpha_u\in U_{n+1}$ and since by Lemma \ref{map g} the map that takes $u\in F_n$ to $\alpha_u\in U_{n+1}$ is an isomorphism, it follows that $U$ and $U_{n+1}$ are isomorphic via $t$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{ses}
    For every $k\geq 1$ we have the following short exact sequence:

    $$1\longrightarrow F_n^k\overset{\iota_*}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{M}_{n,k}\overset{p_*}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{LB}_n\longrightarrow1.$$
    The short exact sequence splits, under the section $s_*$, and thus we have $\mathcal{M}_{n,k}\cong F_n^k\rtimes\mathrm{LB}_n$, where $\mathrm{LB}_n$ acts on $F_n^k$ in the following way: for $\beta\in\mathrm{LB}_n$, whose induced element in $\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$ is denoted by $\phi_\beta$, and for $(u_1,\dots,u_k)\in F_n^k$ we have $$s_*(\beta)^{-1}(u_1,\dots,u_k)s_*(\beta)=(\phi_\beta(u_1), \dots, \phi_\beta(u_k)).$$

    In particular, for $k=1$ we have $$\mathcal{M}_{n,1}\cong U\rtimes\mathrm{LB}_n,\ \text{where}\ U\cong F_n.$$
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
By Theorem \ref{fibration} we have the locally trivial fibration $p:\mathrm{M}_{n,k}\to \mathrm{R}_n$ with fiber $\operatorname{Conf}_k(M)$, from which we deduce the following long exact sequence:
    $$\dots\longrightarrow\pi_2(\mathrm{R}_n)\overset{\partial}{\longrightarrow}\pi_1(\operatorname{Conf}_k(M))\overset{\iota_*}{\longrightarrow}\pi_1(\mathrm{M}_{n,k})\overset{p_*}{\longrightarrow}\pi_1(\mathrm{R}_n)\longrightarrow\pi_0(\operatorname{Conf}_k(M))\longrightarrow\dots.$$
We know that $\pi_1(\operatorname{Conf}_k(M))\cong F_n^k$ by Lemma \ref{free}, $\pi_1(\mathrm{M}_{n,k})=\mathcal{M}_{n,k}$ and $\pi_1(\mathrm{R}_n)=\mathrm{LB}_n$. The fiber $\operatorname{Conf}_k(M)$ is path connected and thus $\pi_0(\operatorname{Conf}_k(M))=1$, which implies also that $p_*$ is surjective. Therefore, for the existence of the short exact sequence it remains to prove the injectivity of $\iota_*$, since the exactness at $\mathcal{M}_{n,k}$ follows from the exactness of the long exact sequence. 

In order to prove injectivity of $\iota_*$ we will not make any claim about $\pi_2(\mathrm{R}_n)$, since $\mathrm{R}_n$ it is not aspherical because $\pi_1(\mathrm{R}_n)=\mathrm{LB}_n$ has torsion elements. Instead, we will simply prove injectivity of $\iota_*$ by proving injectivity of the map $t\circ\iota_*$, where $t$ is the map defined in Lemma \ref{thickening}. Let $k=1$ and $u,v\in F_n$. By Lemma \ref{correspondance} we have $t(\iota_*(u))=\alpha_u\in \mathrm{LB}_{n,1}\subset \operatorname{Aut}(F_{n+1})$ and $t(\iota_*(v))=\alpha_v\in \mathrm{LB}_{n,1}\subset \operatorname{Aut}(F_{n+1})$. If $\alpha_u=\alpha_v$ then $u^{-1}x_{n+1}u=v^{-1}x_{n+1}v$, which means that $vu^{-1}$ is in the centralizer of $x_{n+1}$ in $F_{n+1}$, which is the cyclic subgroup generated by $\langle x_{n+1}\rangle$ and thus $u=v$. Now let $k\geq 2$. For $1\leq i\leq k$ consider the map $q_i:\mathrm{M}_{n,k}\to \mathrm{M}_{n,1}$ that "forgets" all marked points but the $i^{th}$ one. This map is well defined and continuous because the marked points are ordered. Thus, it induces the map $(q_i)_*: \mathcal{M}_{n,k}\to \mathcal{M}_{n,1}$. Hence, we obtain $(q_i)_*\circ\iota_*=\iota_*^1\circ \mathrm{pr}_i$, where $\mathrm{pr}_i: F_n^k\to F_n$ is the projection to the $i^{th}$ coordinate and $\iota_*^1:F_n\to\mathcal{M}_{n,1}$. Therefore, for $(u_1,\dots,u_k)\in \ker\iota_*$ then $\iota_*(u_i)=1$ for every $1\leq i\leq k$, which implies that $u_i=1$ by the case $k=1$. Thus, $\iota_*$ is injective.

Finally it follows that we indeed have the short exact sequence  $$1\longrightarrow F_n^k\overset{\iota_*}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{M}_{n,k}\overset{p_*}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{LB}_n\longrightarrow1,$$
which splits since the map $s$ defined in Remark \ref{section} is a section of $p$. As a result, the homomorphism $p_*$ has as section the map $s_*:\mathrm{LB}_n\to\mathcal{M}_{n,k}$, induced by $s$, as section and $\mathcal{M}_{n,k}\cong \iota_*(F_n^k)\rtimes s_*(\mathrm{LB}_n),\ \text{where}\ \iota_*(F_n^k)\cong F_n^k\ \text{and}\ s_*(\mathrm{LB}_n)\cong \mathrm{LB}_n.$

It remains to show the action of $\mathrm{LB}_n$ on $F_n^k$. Let $\beta\in \mathrm{LB}_n$ be realized by $f$ with an isotopy $h_t$ from $\mathrm{id}$ to $f$ and let $\phi_\beta$ be the induced element in $\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$. Moreover, we fix $i$ and we let $\alpha_u^i$ to be the fiber element in which the circles stay fixed, the $i^{th}$ marked point traverses a loop $l$ that represents $u$, while the other marked points are fixed. We consider $\Sigma(s,t):=(h_t(C),*_1,\dots,h_t(l(s)),\dots,*_k)$, which is an element in $\mathrm{M}_{n,k}$, for all $s,t\in[0,1]$. Using the fact that $h_0=\mathrm{id}$, $h_1=f$ and $f$ fixes the marked points, we have for $s=0$ and $s=1$ the loop $s_*(\beta)$ and the loops $\alpha_u^i$, for $t=0$, and $\alpha_{\phi_\beta(u)}^i$, for $t=1$, which correspond to the fiber. Therefore we obtain 
\begin{equation}\label{eq}
    s_*(\beta)\alpha_{\phi_\beta(u)}^i=\alpha_{u}^is_*(\beta),
\end{equation}
simply by the fact that the boundary paths of the square from $(0,0)$ to $(1,1)$ are homotopic relative to their fixed endpoints. Thus, $s_*(\beta)^{-1}\alpha_u^is_*(\beta)=\alpha_{\phi(u)}^i$. The automorphism $\phi$ is the same for each $i$, and as a result we have that $s_*(\beta)^{-1}(u_1,\dots,u_k)s_*(\beta)=(\phi_\beta (u_1),\dots,\phi_\beta (u_k))$

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm1}
    Let $u,w\in F_n$. Then, $u,w$ are $\phi_\beta$-conjugate if and only if $\beta_u$ and $\beta_w$ are conjugate by an element of $U$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let $u,v\in F_n$.
    From \eqref{eq} we have that $\alpha_vs_*(\beta)=s_*(\beta)\alpha_{\phi_\beta(v)}$ and by definition we have $\beta_u:=s_*(\beta)\alpha_u$. Thus, $\alpha_v\beta_u\alpha_v^{-1}=\alpha_vs_*(\beta)\alpha_u\alpha_v^{-1}=s_*(\beta)\alpha_{\phi_\beta(v)}\alpha_u\alpha_v^{-1}=s_*(\beta)\alpha_{\phi_\beta(v)}\alpha_{uv^{-1}}=s_*(\beta)\alpha_{\phi_\beta(v)uv^{-1}}$. The last two equalities follow from Lemma \ref{map g}. Therefore, $\alpha_v\beta_u\alpha_v^{-1}=\beta_{\phi_\beta(v)uv^{-1}}$. Hence, if $w$ and $u$ are $\phi_\beta$-conjugate, that is, there exists an element $v\in F_n$ such that $w=\phi_\beta(v)uv^{-1}$, then $\beta_w=\alpha_v\beta_u\alpha_v^{-1}$. Thus, $\beta_w$ and $\beta_u$ are conjugate by an element in $U$. Conversely, if $\beta_w=\alpha_v\beta_u\alpha_v^{-1}$ then $\beta_w=\beta_{\phi_\beta(v)uv^{-1}}$ and thus $w=\phi_\beta(v)uv^{-1}$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
It is important to note that in the classical case of fixed points of homeomorphisms of the punctured disk in \cite{Guaschi}, Guaschi proves that the corresponding subgroup $U_{n+1}$ is the kernel of the map that forgets the last strand. In our case we can not replace the group $U\cong U_{n+1}$ by the kernel of the map that forgets the last circle, that is, $\mathrm{LB}_{n,1}\to \mathrm{LB}_n$ because the statement would be simply false. Let $q:\mathrm{LB}_{n,1}\to \mathrm{LB}_n\subset\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})$ be the forgetful map, that forgets the last circle, which algebraically is induced by killing the $x_{n+1}$ generator. It holds that $U_{n+1}\subsetneq\ker q$, since for example $\alpha_{i,n+1}\in \ker q \setminus U_{n+1}$, for all $i\leq n$, which is the element whose $i^{th}$ circle passes through the last $(n+1)^{th}$ circle and goes back to its position. Indeed, $\alpha_{i,n+1}\notin U_{n+1}$ because every element of $U_{n+1}$ fixes $x_i$, for all $1\leq i\leq n$, whereas $\alpha_{i,n+1}(x_i)=x_{n+1}^{-1}x_ix_{n+1}\neq x_i$. It is also interesting to note that $t(\mathcal{M}_{n,1})\subsetneq \mathrm{LB}_{n,1}$ and that the difference between $\mathrm{LB}_{n,1}$ and $t(\mathcal{M}_{n,1})$ is that in $\mathrm{LB}_{n,1}$ we have elements where circles different than the last one pass through the last circle which can not happen for elements in $t(\mathcal{M}_{n,1})$, since they are obtained from elements in $\mathcal{M}_{n,1}$, where the last component is a point and not a circle. This is what forces Theorem \ref{thm1} to be stated with $U_{n+1}$ rather than $\ker q$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
For $x\in\operatorname{Fix}(f)\cap M$ choose a path $\lambda_x$ from $*_1$ to $x$ in $M$ and let $\Lambda_x$ be the path $s\to(C,\lambda_x(s))$ in $\mathrm{M}_{n,1}$ from $(C,*_1)$ to $(C,x)$. Let $\hat{l}_{x}$ be the mixed loop $(h_t(C),h_t(x))$ based at $(C,x)$ and set $l_{x}:=\Lambda_x\hat{l}_x\bar{\Lambda}_x\in \mathcal{M}_{n,1}$. Thus, $l_x\in \mathcal{M}_{n,1}$ is the motion of the circles together with the marked point $x$ based at $(C,*_1)$ under $h_t$.
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{coordinate}
    We have $l_x=\beta_{R(x)}$, where $R(x)\in F_n$ is the coordinate of $x$. Moreover, by choosing a different path from $*_1$ to $x$, that is, replacing $\lambda_x$ by $\gamma\lambda_x$, we are replacing $l_x$ by $\alpha_\gamma l_x\alpha_\gamma^{-1}$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We will use the same argument as in the proof of Proposition \ref{ses}. Consider $$\Sigma(s,t):=(h_t(C), h_t(\lambda_x(s))),$$ which takes values in $\mathrm{M}_{n,1}$, since $h_t$ is a homeomorphism and $\lambda_x(s)\notin C$. For $s=0$ and $s=1$ we get the loops $\hat{l}_{*_1}$ and $\hat{l}_x$ respectively, while for $t=0$ and $t=1$ we get the paths $\Lambda_x$ and $\Lambda_{f\circ \lambda_x}$ respectively, from $(C,*_1)$ to $(C,x)$. Thus, by homotopy relative to the endpoints of the square from $(0,0)$ to $(1,1)$ we get $\hat{l}_{*_1}\cdot \Lambda_{f\circ \lambda_x}=\Lambda_x\cdot \hat{l}_x$ and since $\hat{l}_{*_1}=s_*(\beta)$, we finally obtain $s_*(\beta)\cdot \Lambda_{f\circ \lambda_x}=\Lambda_x\cdot \hat{l}_x$. Therefore, $l_x=\Lambda_x\hat{l}_x\bar{\Lambda}_x=s_*(\beta)\cdot \Lambda_{f\circ \lambda_x}\bar{\Lambda}_x=s_*(\beta)\alpha_{(f\circ \lambda_x)\bar{\lambda}_x}=\beta_{R(x)}$. 

Moreover, by Lemma \ref{Nielsen}, we see that replacing $\lambda_x$ by $\gamma\lambda_x$, we replace $R(x)$ by $\phi_\beta(\gamma)R(x)\gamma^{-1}$, which in turn tells us, by Theorem \ref{thm1}, that we replace $\beta_{R(x)}$ by $\alpha_\gamma\beta_{R(x)}\alpha_\gamma^{-1}$.
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm2}
Let $x,y\in\operatorname{Fix}(f)\cap M$. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] the fixed points $x$ and $y$ are Nielsen equivalent in M, 
\item[(ii)] the coordinates $R(x)$ and R(y) are $\phi_\beta$-conjugate in $F_n$,
\item[(iii)] the loops $l_x$ and $l_y$ are conjugate in $\mathcal{M}_{n,1}$ by an element of $U$,
\item[(iv)] the loop braids $t(l_x)$ and $t(l_y)$ are conjugate in $\mathrm{LB}_{n,1}$ by an element of $U_{n+1}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The equivalence between (i) and (ii) follows from Lemma \ref{Nielsen}, which is a well known fact. 
   
   The equivalence between (ii) and (iii) follows from Lemma \ref{coordinate} together with Theorem \ref{thm1}.
   
   Suppose that (iii) holds, that is, $l_y=\alpha l_x \alpha^{-1}$, with $\alpha\in U$. Then, $t(l_y)=t(\alpha)t(l_x)t(\alpha)^{-1}$, with $t(\alpha)\in U_{n+1}$, since $t$ is a homomorphism and $t(U)=U_{n+1}$, by Lemma \ref{thickening} and Lemma \ref{correspondance}. Thus, (iii) implies (iv). Conversely, suppose that (iv) holds. That is, $t(l_y)=\bar{\alpha}t(l_x)\bar{\alpha}^{-1}$, where $\bar{\alpha}\in U_{n+1}$. By Lemma \ref{correspondance}, there exists an element $\alpha\in U$ such that $t(\alpha)=\bar{\alpha}$. Thus, $t(l_y)=t(\alpha l_x\alpha^{-1})$ and it suffices to show that the homomorphism $t$ is injective. This is indeed the case and it follows from Lemma \ref{thickening} and Proposition \ref{ses}. If $t(m)=1$, for $m\in\mathcal{M}_{n,1}$, then $p_*(m)=q(t(m))=1$, where $q:\mathrm{LB}_{n,1}\to \mathrm{LB}_n$ and so $m\in\ker p_*=U$. We know that the map $t$ is already injective on $U$ by Lemma \ref{correspondance}. Thus, $m=1$, which implies that $t$ is injective and therefore $l_y=\alpha l_x\alpha^{-1}$. Therefore, (iv) implies (iii).
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Theorem \ref{thm2} asserts the existence of a conjugating element but we note that one can be written down explicitly. Indeed, let $\ell$ be a path from $x$ to $y$ with $\ell$ homotopic to $f\circ\ell$ relative to the endpoints,
realizing the Nielsen equivalence, and set $w:=\lambda_x\,\ell\,\bar\lambda_y\inF_n$, where $\lambda_x$ is a path from $*_1$ to $x$ and $\lambda_y$ is a path from $*_1$ to $y$. Computing
the coordinate of $y$ with the path $\lambda_x\ell$ instead of $\lambda_y$ gives $R(x)$. Moreover, since the
two paths differ by $w$, Lemma \ref{Nielsen} gives $R(x)=\phi_\beta(w)R(y)w^{-1}$. Applying Theorem \ref{thm1} we get $\beta_{R(x)}=\alpha_w\beta_{R(y)}\alpha_{w}^{-1}$.
Since $l_x=\beta_{R(x)}$ and $l_y=\beta_{R(y)}$, by Lemma \ref{coordinate}, it follows that
$l_x=\alpha_w\,l_y\,\alpha_w^{-1}$.
Thus the conjugating element is built directly from the Nielsen path $\ell$ and the two connecting
paths.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
We notice that Theorems \ref{thm1} and \ref{thm2} apply equally to the iterates of $f$. The loop braid of $f^k$
becomes $\beta^k$ and its induced automorphism $\phi_\beta^k$. In particular, each Nielsen class of
$\operatorname{Fix}(f^k)$ in $M$ carries a loop braid, up to $U$-conjugacy, and the points of a
periodic orbit of period $k$ are fixed points of $f^k$ to which the criterion applies.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{realization}
    Let $\beta\in \mathrm{LB}_n$ and let $u_1,\dots,u_m\in F_n$. There exists $f\in \operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{B}^3, C)$ that is the identity on the collar of $\partial\mathbb{B}^3$ with $b_{f,C}=\beta$ and with fixed points $x_1,\dots,x_m\in M$, such that $R(x_i)=u_i$ for every $1\leq i \leq m.$
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
We consider the element $s_*(\beta)\alpha_{(u_1,\dots,u_m)}$ in $\mathcal{M}_{n,m}\cong F_n^m\rtimes\mathrm{LB}_n$, where $\alpha_{(u_1,\dots,u_m)}$ is the element in $F_n^m$ that has the $i^{th}$ marked point traversing $u_i$ while the $n$ circles remain at their position. Note that $\alpha_{(u_1,\dots,u_m)}$ represents a loop $(c_t,y_1(t),\dots, y_m(t))$ based at $(C,*_1,\dots,*_m)$, which gives an isotopy of $C\cup\{*_1,\dots,*_m\}$ in $\mathrm{Int}(\mathbb{B}^3)$. Thus, by the isotopy extension theorem it extends to an ambient isotopy $h_t$ of $\mathbb{B}^3$, which is the identity on the collar of the boundary. We define $f:=h_1$ and thus $f(C)=c_1=C$, $b_{f,C}=\beta$ and $f(*_i)=y_i(1)=*_i$. Therefore, each $x_i:=*_i$ is a fixed point of $f$ in $M$. Moreover, the motion of $C$ together with $x_i$ determined by $h_t$ corresponds to the $u_i$-extension of $\beta$, that is, $\beta_{u_i}$. Thus, by Lemma \ref{coordinate}, $l_{x_i}=\beta_{u_i}$ and hence $R(x_i)=u_i$.
<!-- end proof-11 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
By Theorem \ref{thm2} the $U$-conjugacy class of $l_x$ completely determines the Nielsen class
of $x$. The linking data extracted from the abelianized generalized Lefschetz number is not. That is, two
fixed points may have the same linking numbers with every sublink of $C$ and still lie in distinct
Nielsen classes, as the following example shows.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{example}
 Consider $\beta=1\in \mathrm{LB}_n$. Hence $\phi_\beta=\mathrm{id}$, which implies that the $\phi_\beta$-conjugacy is the standard conjugacy. Applying Proposition \ref{realization}, with $m=2$, $u_1=1\in F_n$ and $u_2=x_1x_2x_1^{-1}x_2^{-1}\in F_n$, we have a homeomorphism $f$ with two fixed points $x$ and $y$ such that $R(x)=1$ and $R(y)=x_1x_2x_1^{-1}x_2^{-1}$. Both coordinates $R(x), R(y)$ have trivial abelianization and thus all linking numbers of $x$ and $y$ vanish. While the fixed point $x,y$ have the same linking numbers we see that they are not Nielsen equivalent because $u_1=1$ and $u_2=x_1x_2x_1^{-1}x_2^{-1}\neq 1$ are not conjugate in $F_n$.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{wecken}
Let $\bar B$ be the compactification of $B=\mathbb{B}^3\setminus C$ obtained by replacing each component of $C$ by the
boundary torus of a tubular neighborhood, and let $\bar f$ be the homeomorphism induced by $f$.
Then
$N(\bar f)=MF(\bar f).$
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
The trace formula, before passing to the abelianization, presented in \cite{Makri} computes the generalized Lefschetz number of $\bar f$, whose
nonzero coefficients correspond to the essential fixed point classes and hence give the Nielsen
number $N(\bar f)$. By Proposition \ref{wecken} this number is attained, which means that it is not just a lower
bound for the number of fixed points of a map homotopic to $\bar f$, but the exact minimum. This has
no analogue in the two-dimensional case. By Theorem~\ref{thm2}, each essential class is labeled by a $U_{n+1}$-conjugacy class of loop braids on $n+1$
strands.
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
Given $\beta\in \mathrm{LB}_n$ and given $u,v\in F_n$ it is decidable whether $\beta_u$ and $\beta_v$ are $U$-conjugate. That is, it is solvable whether two fixed points with coordinates $u$ and $v$ are Nielsen equivalent.
