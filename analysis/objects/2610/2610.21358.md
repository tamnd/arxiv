\label{introthm-induction-coreg}
Fix an integer $c\ge0$. Assume that for every projective klt Calabi-Yau pair
of dimension $c$ with standard coefficients the regional orbifold
fundamental group is virtually abelian of rank at most $2c$.
Then every projective klt pair $(X,D)$ of absolute coregularity
$a\le c$ has virtually abelian regional orbifold fundamental group
of rank at most $2a$.

\label{intro:cy-all}\label{intro:cy-standard}
Let $(X,D)$ be a projective klt pair of dimension $n$ with
$K_X+D\equiv0$.  Let $s$ be the smallest positive integer such that
$s(K_X+D)\sim0$. Then $\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(X,D)$ is virtually abelian of rank
at most $2n$. In particular:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item If $D$ has standard coefficients, there is a connected
	finite cyclic log index-one cover $p:Y\to X$ of degree $s$, with $Y$ canonical

	\[
	1\longrightarrow\pi_1(Y_{\mathrm{reg}})\longrightarrow\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(X,D)
	\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/s\mathbb Z\longrightarrow1.
	\]
	In this case the virtual rank is exactly $2\widetilde q(Y)$. 
	\item If $D=0$, the virtual rank is exactly $2\widetilde q(X)$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:virtually-abelian}
We say that a group $G$ is \textit{virtually abelian of rank $n$} if it has a subgroup of finite index which is abelian of rank $n$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
A group $G$ being virtually abelian of rank $n$ is equivalent to having a \textit{normal} subgroup of finite index which is abelian of rank $n$, since one can take the normal core of the abelian subgroup from Definition~\ref{def:virtually-abelian}, which is again abelian of the same rank and has finite index in $G$.

All groups occurring here are indeed finitely generated: a complex algebraic
variety has the homotopy type of a finite CW complex, and orbifold fundamental
groups are quotients of fundamental groups of algebraic open sets. 

For these groups, the above definition is equivalent to having a finite-index subgroup $\mathbb Z^n$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem-virtual-groups}
Let $F, G$ and $H$ be groups fitting in an exact sequence 
$$F \xrightarrow{\phi} G \xrightarrow{\psi} H\rightarrow 1.$$ 
If $H$ is finitely generated and virtually abelian of rank $n$ and $F$ is finite, then $G$ is virtually abelian of rank at most $n$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By replacing $F$ with its image $\phi(F)$ we can assume that $F$ is a subgroup of $G$ with $f$ elements.
Since $H$ is virtually abelian, there exists an abelian group $A \leqslant H$ of rank $n$ and of finite index in $H$. After replacing $A$ by a subgroup of finite index, we may assume that $A\simeq\mathbb Z^n$. Let $A$ be generated by $\{a_i\}_{\{1 \leq i\leq n\}}$. Let $g_i$ be elements in $G$ mapping to $a_i$ via $\psi$. For any $x$ in $F$ and $g_i$, we have that $g_i x g_i^{-1}$ gets mapped to the identity $e\in H$ via $\psi$, hence $g_i x g_i^{-1} \in F$. Thus conjugation by elements  $y \in \langle g_i \rangle$ gives a group action  on $F$. Since $F$ is a finite set, there exists a uniform constant $m$ such that $m$-iterations of all of these actions are the identity, in particular $y^{m} x y^{-m} =x$ for any $x \in F$ and $y \in \langle g_i \rangle$. 

On the other hand, for any $y \in \langle g_i \rangle$, there exists $x\in F$, such that $g_i^{m} y g_i^{-m}=x y$, since it maps to the same element of $A$ as $y$. Therefore 
$$
g_i^{fm}g_j^{fm}g_i^{-fm}=g_i^{(f-1)m}xg_j^{fm}g_i^{-(f-1)m}=xg_i^{(f-1)m}g_j^{fm}g_i^{-(f-1)m}= \ldots=x^{f}g_j^{fm}=g_j^{fm}.
$$
We conclude that $\langle  g_i^{fm}\rangle_i$ is an abelian subgroup of rank $n$ of $G$. This subgroup has finite index in $G$, as it surjects onto $\langle a_i^{fm}\rangle_i$, a finite index subgroup of $H$, via a homomorphism with finite kernel.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A group is virtually solvable of derived length at most $\ell$ and index
at most $i$ if it has a solvable subgroup of derived length at most
$\ell$ and index at most $i$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem-fin-sol}
    Let $F, G$ and $H$ be groups fitting in an exact sequence 
$$F \xrightarrow{\phi} G \xrightarrow{\psi} H\rightarrow 1.$$ 
If $H$ is virtually solvable of derived length at most $n$ and index at most $i$ and $F$ is finite, then $G$ is virtually solvable of derived length at most $n+1$ and index at most $i(|F|-1)!$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $H'$ be the solvable subgroup of $H$ of derived length at most $n$ and index at most $i$. By letting $G'$ be the preimage of $H'$ under $\psi$, we obtain the exact sequence
$$F \xrightarrow{\phi} G' \xrightarrow{\psi} H'\rightarrow 1,$$ 
where $[G:G']=[H:H']\le i$.

By taking a quotient if needed, we may assume that $F$ is a subgroup of $G'$.
Let $Z$ be the centralizer of $F$ in $G'$. The index of $Z$ in $G'$ is at most $|{\rm Aut}(F)|$, which is at most $(|F|-1)!$.

Since $F\cap Z$ is abelian and $\psi(Z) \leqslant H'$ is solvable of derived length at most $n$, the group $Z$ is solvable of derived length at most $n+1$. Hence $G$ is virtually solvable of derived length at most $n+1$ and index at most $i|{\rm Aut}(F)|\leq i(|F|-1)!$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:characteristic}
If a finite group $F$ has an abelian subgroup of index at most $J$,
then it has a characteristic abelian subgroup of index at most $J^2$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For $H\le F$ put
$m(H)=|H|\,|C_F(H)|$. For subgroups $H,K\le F$, counting the
products $HK$ and $C_F(H)C_F(K)$ gives
\[
 m(H)m(K)\le m(H\cap K)m(\langle H,K\rangle).
\]
Thus the subgroups maximizing $m$ are closed with respect to intersection.
Their unique smallest member $A$ is characteristic. The centralizer
of a maximizing subgroup is also maximizing, since
$H\subseteq C_F(C_F(H))$. Hence $A\subseteq C_F(A)$, so $A$ is
abelian. If $B$ is the given abelian subgroup, then
\[
 |A|\,|F|\ge m(A)\ge m(B)\ge |B|^2\ge |F|^2/J^2.
\]
This proves the assertion.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem-virt-sol}
Suppose $F\to G\to H\to1$ is exact, $F$ is finite and has an
abelian subgroup of index at most $J$, and $H$ has a solvable
subgroup of derived length at most $\ell$ and index at most $i$.
Then $G$ has a solvable subgroup of derived length at most $\ell+2$
and index at most $i(J^2)!$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Replace $F$ by its image; the same index bound persists. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:characteristic}, choose a characteristic abelian
subgroup $A\le F$ with $[F:A]\le J^2$. It is normal in $G$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem-fin-sol} to
$F/A\to G/A\to H\to1$, and lift the resulting solvable subgroup
through the abelian kernel $A$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:interpolation}
Let $(X,P)$ be a $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt pair and let
$(X,B,\mathbf M)$ be generalized lc, with $P\le B$.
For rational $0<t\le1$, the generalized pair
\[
 (X,(1-t)B+tP,(1-t)\mathbf M)
\]
is generalized klt. On any higher model its generalized log
discrepancies are
\[
 (1-t)A_{X,B,\mathbf M}(E)+tA_{X,P}(E)>0.
\]
In particular, for any fixed finite collection of lc places
of $(X,B,\mathbf M)$, the pair $(X,(1-t)B+tP,(1-t)\mathbf M)$  has discrepancies in $(0,1)$ for $t>0$ sufficiently
small.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Pull back the identity
\[
 K_X+(1-t)B+tP+(1-t)\mathbf M_X
 =(1-t)(K_X+B+\mathbf M_X)+t(K_X+P).
\]
On a common resolution the resulting boundary is $(1-t)B_V+tP_V$,
where $K_V+P_V=f^*(K_X+P)$. Its log discrepancies are therefore
the stated convex combinations. For an lc place they equal
$tA_{X,P}(E)$, which belongs to $(0,1)$ when $t>0$ is sufficiently small.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem-big-klt}
Let $(X,B,\mathbf M)$ be a projective generalized klt pair and let $H$
be an ample $\mathbb Q$-Cartier divisor. For every rational $\epsilon>0$
there is an ordinary klt boundary $\Delta\ge B$ with
\[
 K_X+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb Q}K_X+B+\mathbf M_X+\epsilon H.
\]
If finitely many prescribed prime divisors $E_i$ over $X$ satisfy
$0<A_{X,B,\mathbf M}(E_i)<1$, we may also arrange
$0<A_{X,\Delta}(E_i)<1$ for every $i$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Take a log resolution $f:V\to X$ on which $\mathbf M$ descends and all
the prescribed divisors occur. Write
$K_V+B_V+\mathbf M_V=f^*(K_X+B+\mathbf M_X)$.
The divisor $\mathbf M_V+\epsilon f^*H$ is nef and big. By Kodaira's lemma write it
as $H_\delta+\delta E$, up to $\mathbb Q$-linear equivalence,
where $E\ge0$ is fixed and $H_\delta$ is ample for sufficiently small rational
$\delta>0$. Choose a general effective representative $G_\delta\sim_{\mathbb Q}H_\delta$
with sufficiently small coefficients. We may arrange that
$(V,B_V+G_\delta+\delta E)$ is sub-klt and that $G_\delta$ contains
none of the prescribed divisors. Their boundary coefficients tend to
those of $B_V$ as $\delta\to0$ and therefore remain in $(0,1)$.
Set $\Delta=B+f_*(G_\delta+\delta E)\ge B$.
Then $K_X+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb Q}K_X+B+\mathbf M_X+\epsilon H$ is
$\mathbb Q$-Cartier, and
\[
 K_V+B_V+G_\delta+\delta E=f^*(K_X+\Delta).
\]
Indeed, the difference is exceptional and $\mathbb Q$-linearly trivial
over $X$, so it vanishes by the negativity lemma.
The equality proves both that $(X,\Delta)$ is klt and that
$A_{X,\Delta}(E_i)=1-\operatorname{coeff}_{E_i}(B_V+G_\delta+\delta E)$
belongs to $(0,1)$. Negative components of $B_V$ are exceptional
and disappear under $f_*$; the added divisor is effective.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem-BCHM-generalized-pairs}
In Lemma~\ref{lem:interpolation}, assume in addition that $X$ is projective.
For any finite collection $E_1,\ldots,E_r$ of exceptional prime divisors
over $X$ with $A_{X,B,\mathbf M}(E_i)=0$, there is a projective birational
morphism $\mu:Y\to X$, with $Y$ $\mathbb Q$-factorial, whose
exceptional prime divisors are exactly $E_1,\ldots,E_r$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Choose $t>0$ sufficiently small in Lemma~\ref{lem:interpolation}
so that all the prescribed generalized log discrepancies belong to $(0,1)$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem-big-klt} to this generalized klt pair, using an
ample divisor on $X$, to obtain an ordinary klt boundary $\Delta$
with $0<A_{X,\Delta}(E_i)<1$ for every $i$.
Now~\cite[Corollary~1.4.3]{BCHM10} extracts exactly these divisors
on a $\mathbb Q$-factorial model. Its discrepancy hypotheses hold
because $(X,\Delta)$ is klt and all the specified discrepancies are smaller than one.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:cbf}
Let $f:Z\to W$ be a projective contraction and let $(Z,B,\mathbf M)$
be generalized lc, with $K_Z+B+\mathbf M_Z\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$.
There is an induced generalized lc pair $(W,B_W,\mathbf N)$ with
$B_W\ge0$, $\mathbf N$ b-nef and b-$\mathbb Q$-Cartier, and
$K_W+B_W+\mathbf N_W\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$.
The coefficient of a prime divisor $E$ in $B_W$ is $1-t_E$,
where $t_E$ is the generalized lc threshold of $f^*E$ over its
generic point. If the total pair is generalized klt, so is the base.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem-image-lcc}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:cbf}, the image of a generalized lc
center is a generalized lc center of the base, or it is the whole base.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let $C$ have proper image $T$, and suppose $T$ is not a generalized
lc center of the base. Near the generic point of $T$, choose a
general effective Cartier divisor containing $T$ but containing
no generalized lc center of the base. Such a choice is possible:
centers properly contained in $T$ can be discarded on this open
set, and centers properly containing $T$ need not lie in the divisor.
On a common log resolution this divisor has zero order along every
discrepancy-zero divisor, since it contains no generalized lc center.
A sufficiently small positive multiple can therefore be added while
preserving generalized lc singularities. Adding its pullback upstairs makes the discrepancy
of any lc place centered on $C$ negative. The discriminant changes
by exactly that divisor and the moduli part is unchanged. This
contradicts the lc equivalence in~\cite[Proposition~4.16]{Fil20}.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:absolute-coregularity} 
Let $(X,B, {\bf M})$ be a generalized log canonical pair. Let $g:(Y,B_Y, {\bf M}) \rightarrow (X,B, {\bf M})$ be a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial gdlt modification, with $\lfloor B_Y \rfloor=E_1+\ldots +E_r$. 
We define $\mathcal{D}(Y,B_Y,{\bf M})$, the \textit{dual complex} of $(Y,B_Y,{\bf M})$, to be:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item For each $E_i$, there is a vertex $s_{E_i}$ in $\mathcal{D}(Y,B_Y)$,
    \item For any subset $I \subseteq \{1,\ldots, r\}$, and any irreducible component $Z$ of $\cap_{i \in I} E_i$, there is a $(|I|-1)$-dimensional simplex $s_Z$.
    \item For any 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $I\subseteq \{1, \ldots r\}$,
        \item  $j \in I$,
        \item  any irreducible component $Z$ of $\cap_{i \in I} E_i$,
        \item and $W$ the unique irreducible component of $\cap_{i \in I\setminus \{j\}} E_i$ containing $Z$,
    \end{itemize}
    there is a gluing map given by the inclusion of $s_W$ into $s_Z$ as the unique face that does not contain the vertex $s_{E_j}$.
\end{enumerate}
We define the \textit{dimension of the dual complex} to be the smallest dimension of a maximal simplex in $\mathcal{D}(Y,B_Y,{\bf M})$ (with respect to inclusion). The empty complex has dimension $-1$ by definition. By~\cite{FMP22}, the dimension of $\mathcal{D}(Y,B_Y, {\bf M})$ does not depend on the gdlt modification.
Hence, we can define the \textit{coregularity} of a generalized pair $(X,B, {\bf M})$, to be:

$$\text{coreg}(X,B, {\bf M}):= \text{dim}\, X-\text{dim}\, \mathcal{D}(Y,B_Y, {\bf M})-1.$$


For a generalized pair $(X,D, {\bf M})$ admitting a generalized log Calabi-Yau completion, we define the \textit{absolute coregularity} ${\rm \hat{coreg}}(X,D, {\bf M})$ to be the smallest value of $\text{coreg}(X,B,{\bf M+ N})$, where $(X,B, {\bf M+N})$ is generalized log Calabi-Yau, with $B\geq D$ and $\mathbf N$ a nef b-divisor.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:coreg-facts}
The coregularity of a projective generalized lc Calabi-Yau pair is
invariant under crepant birational changes. Its dual complex is
equidimensional, and on a generalized dlt model all minimal lc

the total space.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:cy-coreg}
Let $(X,P)$ be a projective klt pair admitting a generalized lc
Calabi-Yau completion. If $K_X+P$ is pseudo-effective, then
$K_X+P\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$ and
$\widehat{\operatorname{coreg}}(X,P)=\dim X$.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
The case $\dim X=0$ is immediate. Pass to a small $\mathbb Q$-factorialization;
completions pull back and push forward crepantly with effective boundaries,
so absolute coregularity is unchanged. For any completion
$(X,B,\mathbf M)$ we have
$-(K_X+P)\sim_{\mathbb Q}B-P+\mathbf M_X$.
The trace of a b-nef divisor is pseudo-effective. The pseudo-effective
cone contains no line, so $K_X+P\equiv0$ and
$B-P+\mathbf M_X\equiv0$. Since both summands are pseudo-effective,
they are numerically trivial. The effective divisor $B-P$ has zero
intersection with an ample complete-intersection curve, hence $B=P$;
also $\mathbf M_X\equiv0$.
On a model $f:V\to X$ where $\mathbf M$ descends,
$E=f^*\mathbf M_X-\mathbf M_V\ge0$ is exceptional by negativity.
If $E\ne0$, choose an ample divisor $H$ on $V$ and put $n=\dim V$.
Then
\[
 \mathbf M_V\cdot H^{n-1}=-E\cdot H^{n-1}<0,
\]
contradicting nefness. Thus we get $E=0$.

The nef part descends, and the completion has the ordinary klt
discrepancies. Its dual complex is empty. This holds for every
completion, proving the coregularity claim. Finally, numerical-triviality
abundance~\cite[Theorem~1.2]{Gon13} gives $K_X+P\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:standard}
A divisor $D$ is said to have \textit{standard} coefficients, when the coefficients are in the set $\{1-\frac{1}{n}\}_{\{n \in \mathbb{N}\}}\cup \{1\}$.
For an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D$, the \textit{standard approximation} $D_{\text{st}}$ is the largest effective divisor with standard coefficients, such that $D_{\text{st}}\leq D$.

<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:orb}
For a generalized  pair $(X,D,{\bf M})$ with $D_\text{st}=\sum_{j \in J} D_j + \sum_{i \in I} (1-\frac{1}{n_i})D_i$, we define the \textit{orbifold fundamental group of the smooth locus} $\pi_1^{\text{reg}}(X,D,{\bf M})$ to be 
$$
\pi_1(X_{\text{reg}}\setminus \text{supp}(D_{\text{st}}))/N,
$$
where $X_{\text{reg}}$ is the smooth locus of $X$ and $N$ is the normal subgroup of $\pi_1(X_{\text{reg}}\setminus \text{supp}(D_{\text{st}}))$ generated by $\gamma_i^{n_i}$, where $\gamma_i$ is a loop around $D_i$ for $i \in I$.

\label{thm:fano}\label{thm-Bra21-gen-pair}

$A$ ample and $E\ge0$. Choose $\epsilon>0$ small enough that
$(V,C+\epsilon E,\mathbf M)$ is generalized klt. Ordinary approximation

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop-dim1}
For projective generalized klt curve pairs of finite absolute
coregularity, coregularity zero implies a finite regional orbifold
group, and coregularity one implies virtual abelianity of rank at
most two. These groups have abelian subgroups of index at most $60$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
On a smooth curve the nef part has nonnegative degree. A completion
therefore forces either an elliptic curve with zero boundary and
degree-zero nef part, or $\mathbb P^1$ with $\deg D_{\mathrm{st}}\le2$.
In coregularity zero the completion has a coefficient-one point;
the klt boundary has strictly smaller degree, so
$\deg D_{\mathrm{st}}<2$. 

Write $D_{\mathrm{st}}=\sum_{i=1}^r(1-1/m_i)p_i$, with $m_i\ge2$.
The orbifold Euler characteristic on $\mathbb P^1$ is
$2-\deg D_{\mathrm{st}}$. If it is positive and $r\ge3$, the orbifold is
spherical: its signature $(m_1,\ldots,m_r)$, the list of orders at the points, is $(2,2,m)$, $(2,3,3)$, $(2,3,4)$ or $(2,3,5)$.
The corresponding groups are dihedral, tetrahedral, octahedral or
icosahedral. With at most two cone points, the presentation directly
gives a finite cyclic group (possibly trivial). In all these cases it is well known that
there is an abelian subgroup of index at most $60$.

If $\deg D_{\mathrm{st}}=2$, the orbifold Euler characteristic is zero,
and the Euclidean signatures are $(2,2,2,2)$, $(2,3,6)$,
$(2,4,4)$ and $(3,3,3)$; see~\cite[Proposition~5.3]{GLM23}.
The corresponding orbifold fundamental groups contain their translation
subgroups $\mathbb Z^2$ as normal subgroups of indices
$2,6,4,3$, respectively.
In the elliptic-curve case the boundary is zero, so the regional
orbifold fundamental group is the ordinary fundamental group of the
elliptic curve, namely $\mathbb Z^2$.

\label{lem-Fano-extraction}

their discrepancy coefficients. All remaining exceptional and
indeterminacy loci meet a sufficiently general fibre in codimension
at least two.

<!-- lemma-15: body_md -->
\label{lem-fibration}
Let $f:Z\to W$ be a projective contraction of normal varieties and
let $R$ be a boundary with standard coefficients smaller than one.
For a prime divisor $E$ on $W$, write, over its generic point,
\[
 f^*E=\sum_j m_{E,j}T_{E,j},\qquad
 \operatorname{coeff}_{T_{E,j}}R=1-1/n_{E,j}.
\]
Set $k_E=\gcd_j(m_{E,j}n_{E,j})$ and
$R_W=\sum_E(1-1/k_E)E$. If $T_{E,j}$ is not a component of
$\operatorname{Supp}R$, its coefficient in $R$ is zero, so $n_{E,j}=1$.
There is a smooth open subset $V\subset W$ with complement of
codimension at least two such that, for $U=f^{-1}(V)$ and a general
fibre $F$, we have an exact sequence
\[
 \pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(F,R|_F)\longrightarrow\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(U,R|_U)
 \longrightarrow\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(W,R_W)\longrightarrow1.
\]
Moreover, $\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(U,R|_U)$ surjects onto $\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(Z,R)$.
<!-- end lemma-15 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Remove from the base its singular locus and the images of those prime
divisors on $Z$ whose images in $W$ have codimension at least two. Denote the resulting big open subset by $V$ and put
$U=f^{-1}(V)$. By~\cite[Lemma~1.5.A]{Nor83}, we can choose a smaller
nonempty open set $V^\circ\subset V$, with divisorial complement, such that
\[
 U^\circ:=f^{-1}(V^\circ)_{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus\operatorname{Supp} R\longrightarrow V^\circ
\]
is a topological fibre bundle. By generic flatness and generic smoothness,
we may assume its connected fibre is $F_{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus\operatorname{Supp}(R|_F)$,
and that $V^\circ$ avoids the images of the vertical boundary components.

Now, the loops of horizontal boundary components are represented, up to
conjugation, by loops in a general fibre. 
Quotienting the homotopy sequence by their prescribed orbifold relations 
therefore gives an exact sequence
\[
 \pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(F,R|_F)\xrightarrow{\psi_1}H\xrightarrow{\psi_2}Q\longrightarrow1,
\]
where $H=\pi_1(U^\circ)/N_{\mathrm{hor}}$ and $Q=\pi_1(V^\circ)$.

As noted by~\cite[Fact~1.2]{Nor83}, reinserting a divisor $T_{E,j}$ with its
orbifold coefficient is nothing else than imposing the relation $\gamma_{E,j}^{n_{E,j}}=1$.
At a general smooth point, the image of its loop is conjugate to
$\delta_E^{m_{E,j}}$, where $\delta_E$ is a loop around $E$.
Thus, these relations map to powers $\delta_E^{m_{E,j}n_{E,j}}$ and
their normal closure is generated by $\delta_E^{k_E}$: each exponent
is divisible by $k_E$, and B\'ezout's identity expresses $k_E$ as
an integral linear combination of these exponents.
All components of $U_{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus U^\circ$ reinserted at this step,
other than the horizontal boundary components already treated, dominate divisors of $V$;
any removed subsets of codimension at least two in $U_{\mathrm{reg}}$ do not
change the fundamental group. We obtain a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
 \pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(F,R|_F)\arrow[r,"\psi_1"]\arrow[d,equal]&
 H\arrow[r,"\psi_2"]\arrow[d,"\phi_X"]&
 Q\arrow[r]\arrow[d,"\phi_C"]&1\\
 \pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(F,R|_F)\arrow[r,"\phi_1"]&
 \pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(U,R|_U)\arrow[r,"\phi_2"]&
 \pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(W,R_W)\arrow[r]&1.
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The vertical maps are surjective by the definition of the orbifold
fundamental groups of pairs.

Let $\alpha\in\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(U,R|_U)$ such that $\phi_2(\alpha)=1$.
We take $\alpha'$ a preimage of $\alpha$ by $\phi_X$. Then
$\psi_2(\alpha')\in\ker\phi_C$, so it is a product of conjugates of
$\delta_E^{k_E}$ and their inverses.
Loops around a divisor are well-defined up to conjugation. As $\psi_2$
is surjective, we may choose conjugates $\eta_{E,j}$ of the loops
around $T_{E,j}$ such that $\psi_2(\eta_{E,j})=\delta_E^{m_{E,j}}$.
Choose integers $a_{E,j}$ satisfying
$\sum_j a_{E,j}m_{E,j}n_{E,j}=k_E$. Then
\[
 \lambda_E:=\prod_j\eta_{E,j}^{a_{E,j}n_{E,j}}\in\ker\phi_X,
 \qquad \psi_2(\lambda_E)=\delta_E^{k_E}.
\]
Therefore, taking a suitable product $\gamma'$ of conjugates of these
$\lambda_E$ and their inverses, we have
$\psi_2(\gamma')=\psi_2(\alpha')$ and $\phi_X(\gamma')=1$.
Hence, by exactness of the top row, there exists
$\beta\in\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(F,R|_F)$ such that $\psi_1(\beta)=\alpha'\gamma'^{-1}$.
Thus
\[
 \phi_1(\beta)=\phi_X(\psi_1(\beta))
 =\phi_X(\alpha'\gamma'^{-1})=\alpha,
\]
which proves $\operatorname{im}\phi_1\supseteq\ker\phi_2$.
The reverse inclusion follows from commutativity, and $\phi_2$ is
surjective because $\psi_2$ and $\phi_C$ are surjective.
Hence the bottom sequence is also exact.
Finally, adding the "missing divisors" in $Z_{\mathrm{reg}}$ possibly imposes further
loop relations, yielding $\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(U,R|_U)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(Z,R)$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm-induction-coreg}\label{thm:replacement-reduction}
Fix an integer $c\ge0$. Assume that for every projective klt Calabi-Yau pair
of dimension $c$ with standard coefficients the regional orbifold
fundamental group is virtually abelian of rank at most $2c$.
Then every projective klt pair $(X,D)$ of absolute coregularity
$a\le c$ has virtually abelian regional orbifold fundamental group
of rank at most $2a$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
\ \\
\noindent
\emph{Preparation.}
If the assumption holds in dimension $c$, then it also holds in every dimension $k\le c$: apply the
hypothesis to the product of the given $k$-dimensional Calabi-Yau pair
$(V,C)$ with standard coefficients and $E^{c-k}$, where $E$ is an
elliptic curve, with boundary $\operatorname{pr}_V^*C$.

If $G$ is the regional orbifold fundamental group of the given
$k$-dimensional pair, then that of the product is 
$G\times\mathbb Z^{2(c-k)}$. Virtual abelianity and the bound
$2c$ imply that $G$ is virtually abelian of rank at most $2k$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:cy-coreg} the product has absolute coregularity $c$.

We perform an induction on $n=\dim X$ (simultaneously for all absolute
coregularities at most $c$). 
Choose a generalized lc completion
$(X,B,\mathbf M)$ of coregularity
$a=\widehat{\operatorname{coreg}}(X,D)\le c$.
First take a small $\mathbb Q$-factorialization. The regional fundamental group
and the completion's coregularity are unchanged. Put $P=D_{\mathrm{st}}$,
then $(X,P)$ is klt, $P\le B$, and $\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(X,P)=\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(X,D)$.
\ \\
\noindent
\emph{Case 1: $K_X+P$ is pseudo-effective.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:cy-coreg} shows that $(X,P)$ is a klt Calabi-Yau pair
with standard coefficients and 
forces $a=n$. The assumption in dimensions $n\le c$ proves the
claim. 
\ \\
\noindent
\emph{Case 2: $K_X+P$ is not pseudo-effective.}
Run a $(K_X+P)$-MMP with scaling to a Mori fibre space
$f:Z\to W$. The completion is carried crepantly through the MMP,
since its log canonical divisor is $\mathbb Q$-linearly trivial.
It remains generalized lc of coregularity $a$. We have
$\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(Z,P_Z)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(X,P)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem-MMP-group}. If $W$ is a point, $(Z,P_Z)$ is log
Fano, so $\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(Z,P_Z)$ is finite by Theorem~\ref{thm:fano}.

Otherwise apply the canonical bundle formula, take a generalized
dlt modification $W'\to W$ of the base, and use
Lemma~\ref{lem-dlt-mod-Fano-fibration} to obtain $f':Z'\to W'$.
Choose the boundary $R_{Z'}$ with standard coefficients using
Lemma~\ref{lem-dlt-group}, so that
\[
 \pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(Z',R_{Z'})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(Z,P_Z).
\]
It satisfies $R_{Z'}\le B_{Z'}$ and has all coefficients smaller
than one. Since the completion on $Z'$ is generalized dlt, the
ordinary pair $(Z',R_{Z'})$ is klt.

Let $R_{W'}$ be the gcd boundary from Lemma~\ref{lem-fibration}.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:discriminant}, the pair $(W',R_{W'})$ is klt
and its generalized lc completion has coregularity at most $a$, by
Remark~\ref{rem:coreg-facts}. Thus its absolute coregularity is
$a'\le a$. Since $\dim W'<n$, by induction we get virtual abelianity
of rank at most $2a'\le2a$ for $\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(W',R_{W'})$.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:cy-cover}
Let $(X,D)$ be a projective klt Calabi-Yau pair of dimension $n$
with standard coefficients. Write $D=\sum_i(1-1/m_i)D_i$, and let
$s$ be the smallest positive integer such that $s(K_X+D)\sim0$.
There is a connected finite cyclic cover $p:Y\to X$ of degree $s$,
with ramification index exactly $m_i$ over $D_i$ and no other
divisorial ramification, such that $Y$ is canonical, $K_Y\sim0$
is Cartier, and
\[
 1\longrightarrow\pi_1(Y_{\mathrm{reg}})\longrightarrow\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(X,D)
 \longrightarrow\mathbb Z/s\mathbb Z\longrightarrow1.
\]
Consequently, $\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(X,D)$ is virtually abelian of rank
$2\widetilde q(Y)\le2n$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Choose a canonical Weil divisor $K_X$ and a rational function
$g\in\mathbb C(X)^*$ such that
$s(K_X+D)=\operatorname{div}_X(g)$.
Let $t$ be an $s$-th root of $g$ in an algebraic closure of
$\mathbb C(X)$, and set $L=\mathbb C(X)(t)$.
This is a cyclic Galois extension of degree $e$ dividing $s$, and
$t^e\in\mathbb C(X)$. If $e<s$, then
$\operatorname{div}_X(t^e)=e(K_X+D)$, contrary to the minimality
of $s$. Thus $[L:\mathbb C(X)]=s$.

Let $Y$ be the normalization of $X$ in $L$. 
At a prime divisor $P$ on $X$, the ramification index of this
root extension is
\[
 e_P=\frac{s}{\gcd(s,\operatorname{ord}_P(g))}.
\]
The coefficient of $K_X$ at $P$ is an integer. Also $m_i$ divides
$s$, because $s(K_X+D)$ is integral. At $D_i$, writing that
integer as $a_i$, we obtain
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{D_i}(g)=s(a_i+1)-s/m_i,
 \qquad e_{D_i}=m_i.
\]
At a prime divisor $P$ outside $\operatorname{Supp}D$, the order
of vanishing $\operatorname{ord}_P(g)$ 
is divisible by $s$, so there is no other divisorial ramification.

Choose the canonical divisor on $Y$ using the pullback of the
rational top form defining $K_X$. At a divisor over $D_i$, the
ramification coefficient is $m_i-1=m_i(1-1/m_i)$.
The canonical divisor formula therefore gives
\[
 K_Y=p^*(K_X+D)=\frac1s\operatorname{div}_Y(g)
     =\operatorname{div}_Y(t).
\]
Only the sum $K_X+D$ is pulled back as a $\mathbb Q$-Cartier
divisor; the equality is checked in codimension one. Thus $K_Y$
is Cartier and linearly trivial. Finite crepant pullback preserves
klt singularities by~\cite[Proposition~5.20]{KM98}, and since $K_Y$ is Cartier, $Y$ is in fact canonical.

Remove a codimension-two subset of $X$ so that the remaining open
set $U$ is smooth and the branch divisors are smooth and disjoint.
Locally along $D_i\cap U$, the normalized cover is a disjoint union
of maps $(u,z_2,\ldots,z_n)\mapsto(u^{m_i},z_2,\ldots,z_n)$.
Away from the boundary it is \'etale. Thus $p^{-1}(U)$ is smooth
and the cover is an orbifold covering with the exact prescribed
ramification. The complement of $p^{-1}(U)$ in $Y$ has codimension at least two,
because $p$ is finite. Removing these codimension-two subsets from
the regular loci changes neither the fundamental group nor the
orbifold loop relations. This proves the claimed exact sequence.
Now apply~\cite[Theorem~1.3]{FGSW26} to $Y$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:irregularity}

\[

subgroup of $\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(X,D)$. It therefore has the same virtual rank.

<!-- lemma-17: body_md -->
\label{lem:regular-rank}
Let $(X,D)$ be a projective klt pair. If $\pi_1(X_{\mathrm{reg}})$ is
virtually abelian of rank $r$, then $r=2\widetilde q(X)$.
There is a connected finite quasi-\'etale cover $X_0\to X$ with
\[
 \pi_1((X_0)_{\mathrm{reg}})\xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }\pi_1(X_0)
 \simeq\mathbb Z^r.
\]

<!-- end lemma-17 -->
<!-- proof-28: body_md -->
Choose a finite-index subgroup $\mathbb Z^r$ of $\pi_1(X_{\mathrm{reg}})$.
Its connected finite topological cover is algebraic. Normalize $X$ in its function field to
obtain a finite quasi-\'etale cover $X_0\to X$.

The preimage of $X_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is a smooth open subset of $X_0$
whose complement has codimension at least two. Thus
$\pi_1((X_0)_{\mathrm{reg}})=\mathbb Z^r$.
The pulled-back pair is moreover klt. By the local finiteness
theorem~\cite{Bra21}, its regional fundamental groups
are finite, hence have trivial image in $\mathbb Z^r$.
The kernel of the surjection
$\pi_1((X_0)_{\mathrm{reg}})\to\pi_1(X_0)$ is normally generated by
these local images, so this map is an isomorphism.

For a projective log resolution $V\to X_0$ that is an
isomorphism over $(X_0)_{\mathrm{reg}}$, the kernel of
$\pi_1((X_0)_{\mathrm{reg}})\to\pi_1(V)$ is normally generated by
loops around the exceptional divisors. Each loop lies
in the image of a regional fundamental group and is therefore
trivial. Consequently $\pi_1(V)=\mathbb Z^r$.
Rational singularities and Hodge theory now give
$r=b_1(V)=2q(V)=2q(X_0)$, as in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:irregularity}.

$\pi_1(W_{\mathrm{reg}})$ is a finite-index subgroup of
$\pi_1(X_{\mathrm{reg}})$. Its surjection onto the first homology
modulo torsion of a resolution of $W$ gives $2q(W)\le r$.
Together with the equality for $X_0$, this proves
$r=2\widetilde q(X)$.

$\pi_1((X_0)_{\mathrm{reg}})\simeq\pi_1(X_0)$ implies that every
finite \'etale cover of $(X_0)_{\mathrm{reg}}$ extends to a finite \'etale
cover of $X_0$. The uniqueness of normalization finally shows that a
finite quasi-\'etale cover of $X_0$ is this \'etale extension.

\label{prop-induction-solvable}
Fix an integer $c_0\ge0$. Suppose that for every integer $1\le k\le c_0$
there is a positive integer $I_k$ such that the regional orbifold
fundamental group $\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(V,\Delta)$
of every projective klt Calabi-Yau pair $(V,\Delta)$ of dimension $k$
with standard coefficients
has a solvable subgroup of derived length at most $2k-1$ and index
at most $I_k$, independently of the pair.

$B(d,c_0)$, depending only on $d,c_0$ and the constants $I_1,\ldots,I_{c_0}$,
such that for every projective klt pair $(X,D)$ of dimension $d$
and absolute coregularity at most $c_0$, the group $\pi_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(X,D)$ has

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:terminalization}
For boundary-free klt Calabi-Yau varieties, the criterion
$\widetilde q(X)=0$ is equivalent to finiteness of $\pi_1(X_{\mathrm{reg}})$
in every dimension, by~\cite{FGSW26}. 
For a terminal threefold with $K_X\sim0$ Cartier, one also has
the following alternative:
$X$ is smooth, or is a finite quasi-\'etale quotient of an abelian
threefold, or has finite regional fundamental group. Indeed,
$\widetilde q(X)=0$ gives the last case, and $\widetilde q(X)=3$ gives the
abelian cover. In the remaining case, the torus cover has a terminal
factor of dimension at most two, hence is smooth. Thus $X$ has
quotient singularities. Since $K_X$ is Cartier, a local quotient
presentation may be chosen with a finite group $G\subset\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb C)$
and no nontrivial quasi-reflections.
For an element $g$ of order $r$ with eigenvalues
$\exp(2\pi i a_j/r)$, $0\le a_j<r$, define
$\operatorname{age}(g)=\sum_{j=1}^3a_j/r$.
The Reid-Tai criterion says that a quotient without quasi-reflections
is terminal if and only if $\operatorname{age}(g)>1$ for every
nonidentity element $g$; see~\cite[Theorem~4.11]{Rei87}.
For $g\in\mathrm{SL}_3$, both $\operatorname{age}(g)$ and
$\operatorname{age}(g^{-1})$ are integers, and
\[
 \operatorname{age}(g)+\operatorname{age}(g^{-1})
 =\#\{j:a_j\ne0\}\le3.
\]
Terminality would make both integers at least two, so their sum would
be at least four, contradicting the displayed bound.
Consequently $G$ is trivial, and a Gorenstein terminal quotient threefold is smooth.
