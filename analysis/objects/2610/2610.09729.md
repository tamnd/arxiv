<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:stationary-product}
The map
\[
 \Psi:\mathbb R\times\Sigma_1\longrightarrow\mathbf E,
 \qquad \Psi(t,x)=\Psi_{t,\mathbf T}(x)
\]
is a diffeomorphism. In particular $H^1(\mathbf E;\mathbb R)=0$,
and $H_2(\mathbf E;\mathbb R)$ is generated by a large coordinate sphere.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By GR, every stationary orbit meets $\Sigma_1$ transversally,
so $\Psi$ is a surjective local diffeomorphism. We prove that
it is proper and then use achronality to show that the resulting
covering has only one sheet.

Fix a compact set $C\subset\mathbf E$. By the definition of $\mathbf E$
and openness of $\ll$, finitely many open sets $U_i$ cover $C$,
with points $p_i^\pm=(t_i^\pm,x_i^\pm)$ in the asymptotic region satisfying
\[
 p_i^-\ll q\ll p_i^+\qquad(q\in U_i).
\]
Choose $R>\max_i(|t_i^-|+|t_i^+|)$. Timelikeness of the
stationary orbits through $p_i^\pm$ allows us to compare their
large translates with the zero-time slice. Since the flow
preserves $\ll$, for $q\in U_i$ we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationary-flow-chronology}
 \begin{aligned}
 (0,x_i^-)&\ll\Psi_{s,\mathbf T}(p_i^-)\ll\Psi_{s,\mathbf T}(q),&&s\ge R,\\
 \Psi_{s,\mathbf T}(q)&\ll\Psi_{s,\mathbf T}(p_i^+)\ll(0,x_i^+),&&s\le-R.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Because the zero-time points in
\eqref{eq:stationary-flow-chronology} belong to the achronal
hypersurface $\overline{\Sigma}_1$, no translate
$\Psi_{s,\mathbf T}(C)$ with $|s|\ge R$ can meet that hypersurface.
Consequently, $\Psi(t,x)\in C$ implies $|t|\le R$ and
$x=\Psi_{-t,\mathbf T}(\Psi(t,x))$, whence
\[
 \Psi^{-1}(C)\subset[-R,R]\times
 \left(\Sigma_1\cap
       \{\Psi_{s,\mathbf T}(q):|s|\le R,\ q\in C\}\right).
\]
The set on the right is compact, since $\Sigma_1$ is closed
relative to $\mathbf E$ and the flow image of $[-R,R]\times C$
is compact. As a closed subset of this set, $\Psi^{-1}(C)$
is compact as well, proving properness.

The proper local diffeomorphism $\Psi$ is therefore a covering
of $\mathbf E$, which is connected as the image of
$\mathbb R\times\Sigma_1$. For a point of the asymptotic end,
its entire stationary orbit is timelike, and achronality of
$\overline{\Sigma}_1$ shows that its fiber has exactly one
point. The covering consequently has one sheet, so $\Psi$
is a diffeomorphism.

The cohomology conclusions now follow from
$\mathbf E\simeq\mathbb R\times\Sigma_1$ and
$\Sigma_1\simeq(1,\infty)\times\mathbb S^2$. In particular,
every closed one-form on $\mathbf E$ has a global potential, while
a closed two-form has a global primitive if and only if its
integral over a large coordinate sphere vanishes.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:charging}
Under GR, SBS, and the maximal-hypersurface assumption, there are
Lorentz metrics $\mathbf g_q$ and real two-forms $\mathbf F_q$ on
$\mathbf E\cup\mathbf O$, for a neighbourhood $\mathbf O$ of $\mathcal S_0$, such that

 (\mathbf g_1,\mathbf F_1)=(\mathbf g,0),\qquad
 \mathcal L_\mathbf T\mathbf g_q=\mathcal L_\mathbf T\mathbf F_q=0 .
\]
The metrics depend smoothly on $q\in[0,1]$ on every compact subset.
The fields $\mathbf F_q$ are continuous in $q$ and smooth in spacetime.
On $\mathbf E$ the pair
$(\mathbf g_q,\mathbf F_q)$ satisfies \eqref{eq:Einstein-Maxwell} and
\[
 \frac1{4\pi}\int_{S_r}{}^*_{\mathbf g_q}\mathbf F_q=\sqrt{1-q^2},\qquad
 \frac1{4\pi}\int_{S_r}\mathbf F_q=0 .
\]
The fixed fields $\mathbf K,\mathbf Z$ remain Killing near $\mathcal S_0$, and both horizon
branches remain Killing horizons with the same generator $\mathbf K$
and surface gravity. Moreover
$\operatorname{sign}\mathbf g_q(\mathbf T,\mathbf T)=\operatorname{sign}\mathbf g(\mathbf T,\mathbf T)$,

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:optical-completion}
A mass-one asymptotically flat stationary vacuum end has a
stationary gauge in which $\overline\mathbf g$ extends analytically
across $\mathbb R\times\{i^0\}$. Here $i^0$ is the point added
to the orbit space. In normal coordinates $y$ for
$\widehat\gamma$ at $i^0$, with $\rho=|y|$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completion-coordinates}
 \widehat\gamma_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+O(\rho^2),\qquad
 \Omega=\rho^2+O(\rho^3),\qquad
 (\vartheta_0)_i=O(\rho),\qquad y^i(\vartheta_0)_i=0.
\end{equation}
For each remainder $R=O(\rho^k)$ in
\eqref{eq:completion-coordinates}, its normal-coordinate derivatives
satisfy $\partial_y^I R=O(\rho^{k-|I|})$ for every fixed multiindex $I$.
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optical-normal-data}
 \overline\mathbf g=-\mathrm d\tau^2+\sum_i(\mathrm dy^i)^2+O(\rho).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We use the analytic completion of Beig and Simon~\cite{BeigSimon},
after the stationary regularity improvement of Kennefick and
\'O Murchadha~\cite{KOM}, in the formulation of
\cite{DainInfinity}.
The completed mass and twist potentials of
Geroch and Hansen~\cite{GerochMoments,Hansen} are
\[
 \widehat m=\frac{1-f^2-\psi^2}{4f\sqrt\Omega},\qquad
 \widehat s=\frac{\psi}{2f\sqrt\Omega}.
\]
Together with $\widehat\gamma$ and $\Omega$, they are analytic
at $i^0$ and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completed-potential-data}
 \begin{gathered}
 \Omega(i^0)=0,\quad \mathrm d\Omega(i^0)=0,\quad
 \widehat\nabla^2\Omega(i^0)=2\widehat\gamma(i^0),\\
 \widehat m(i^0)=1,\quad \widehat s(i^0)=0,\quad
 \mathrm d\widehat s(i^0)=S_i\,\mathrm dy^i,\qquad
 \widehat m^2+\widehat s^2=1+\Omega.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $\widehat\nabla$ is the connection of $\widehat\gamma$,
and the last derivative follows from the twist expansion
$\psi=2(S\cdot\omega)r^{-2}+O(r^{-3})$ in
\eqref{eq:ernst-end-expansion}.
See also Ace\~na~\cite{Acena} for convergent expansions from
the multipole data. On the punctured end, the definitions and
\eqref{eq:harrison} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completed-vacuum-potentials}
 \Phi_0=-\frac{\widehat m+i\widehat s}{\sqrt\Omega},\qquad
 f^{-1}=1+2\Omega+2\widehat m\sqrt\Omega,\qquad
 \psi=2f\widehat s\sqrt\Omega.
\end{equation}
The Hessian normalization in
\eqref{eq:completed-potential-data} gives, in normal coordinates,
$\widehat\gamma_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+O(\rho^2)$ and
$\Omega=\rho^2+O(\rho^3)$. At $q=0$, the transformed twist equation
\eqref{eq:charged-twist} reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-twist}
 f_0^2 *_\gamma\mathrm d\vartheta_0
       =-2\operatorname{Im}(\overline\Phi_0\,\mathrm d\Phi_0).
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:harrison} at $q=0$ into
\eqref{eq:endpoint-twist}, we express $\mathrm d\vartheta_0$
in terms of the mass and twist potentials, as in
Dain~\cite{DainInfinity},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limiting-connection}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathrm d\vartheta_0
 &=-\tfrac14*_\gamma
       \left(\frac{2\psi}{f}\,\mathrm df
           +\frac{1-f^2+\psi^2}{f^2}\,\mathrm d\psi\right)\\
 &=-2*_{\gamma}\left\{
 \frac{1-f^2-\psi^2}{4f}\,
             \mathrm d\left(\frac\psi{2f}\right)
 -\frac\psi{2f}\,
             \mathrm d\left(\frac{1-f^2-\psi^2}{4f}\right)
                         \right\}\\
 &=-2*_{\gamma}\bigl\{
   \widehat m\sqrt\Omega\,\mathrm d(\widehat s\sqrt\Omega)
  -\widehat s\sqrt\Omega\,\mathrm d(\widehat m\sqrt\Omega)\bigr\}\\
 &=-2\Omega*_{\gamma}
       (\widehat m\,\mathrm d\widehat s
                      -\widehat s\,\mathrm d\widehat m)\\
 &=-2*_{\widehat\gamma}
       (\widehat m\,\mathrm d\widehat s
                      -\widehat s\,\mathrm d\widehat m).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $*_{\gamma}=\Omega^{-1}*_{\widehat\gamma}$ on one-forms.
It follows from the last expression in
\eqref{eq:limiting-connection} that $\mathrm d\vartheta_0$ extends
analytically to the full ball and remains closed there, by
continuity from the punctured ball. We can therefore choose
its radial primitive
\[
 (\vartheta_0)_i(y)
   =\int_0^1 t y^j(\mathrm d\vartheta_0)_{ji}(ty)\,\mathrm dt
\]
which is analytic, is $O(\rho)$, and satisfies
$y^i(\vartheta_0)_i=0$. Since the punctured ball is simply connected,
replacing the original connection by this primitive amounts
to a stationary time change. In this gauge,
\eqref{eq:completion-coordinates} holds and
\eqref{eq:optical-completion} extends analytically across $i^0$.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The inverse Harrison relation \eqref{eq:inverse-harrison}
requires $\Phi_0$ relative to the normalized stationary field
$\partial_\tau$. The Bertotti--Robinson product in
Cl\'ement's construction~\cite{Clement1998} is locally the same
for every $J$, while its stationary field depends on $J$.

by the Maxwell equations.

Equality of the area with $4\pi$ forces $A=1$ at every point
of the cut, since $A\le1$. By \eqref{eq:integrated-cone-focusing},
we therefore have $\chi=0$ and $\mathbf F_0(L,e_A)=0$ along each
generator from the cut to the vertex. To use these identities
on an open set, translate the cone by the stationary flow.
The geodesics in \eqref{eq:cone-geodesic-data} then give the map
\[
 (u,s,\omega)\longmapsto
 \bigl(u-s+O(s^2),\ s\omega+O(s^2)\bigr),\qquad
 \partial_u=\partial_\tau.
\]
The pullback of $\mathrm d\tau\wedge\mathrm dy^1\wedge
\mathrm dy^2\wedge\mathrm dy^3$ by this map is
$s^2(1+O(s))\,\mathrm du\wedge\mathrm ds\wedge\mathrm d\omega$.
Together with the spatial derivative $I+O(s)$ in $s\omega$,
this shows that $(u,s,\omega)$ are coordinates on a punctured
neighbourhood of the end. In these coordinates the Killing and
affine geodesic equations, followed by the rescaling
$L=-\Omega k$ from \eqref{eq:cone-affine-normalization}, give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-null-normalization}
 \partial_s\overline\mathbf g(\partial_u,k)=0,\qquad
 \overline\mathbf g(\partial_u,k)|_{s=0}=1,
 \qquad \mathbf g_0(\partial_u,\partial_r)=-1.
\end{equation}

Choose the additive constant in $r$ independently of $u$.
Since $\chi=0$ and the angular coordinates are constant
along each ray, the angular metric satisfies the transport
equation with limiting value supplied by \eqref{eq:cone-cut-metric}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-angular-metric}
 \partial_r(\mathbf g_0)_{AB}
       =2\mathbf g_0(\mathbf D^{(0)}_{\partial_A}L,\partial_B)=0,
 \qquad \lim_{r\to\infty}(\mathbf g_0)_{AB}=\overset{\circ}{\gamma}_{AB}.
\end{equation}
Using $\mathbf g_0(\partial_u,\partial_r)=-1$ from
\eqref{eq:equality-null-normalization} and the angular metric
from \eqref{eq:equality-angular-metric}, we write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-metric-Maxwell-form}
 \mathbf g_0=\overset{\circ}{\gamma}-2\,\mathrm du\,\mathrm dr-f_0\,\mathrm du^2
                         +2a_A\,\mathrm du\,\mathrm dx^A.
\end{equation}
Here $a$ is an angular one-form and the coefficients are independent
of $u$. We use the potential to determine $\mathbf F_0$ and fix the
remaining freedom in the affine parameter.
The inversion $y\mapsto y/|y|^2$ and the change from $s$ to
$r$ both reverse orientation, leaving the volume form
$\mathrm du\wedge\mathrm dr\wedge\mathrm d\omega$.
Thus \eqref{eq:charged-potential} and $(\mathbf F_0)_{rA}=0$ give
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \partial_r\operatorname{Im}\Phi_0=({}^*\mathbf F_0)_{ur}
       =\tfrac12(\mathrm d\omega)^{AB}(\mathbf F_0)_{AB},\\
 \mathbf F_0=\mathrm du\wedge\mathrm d\operatorname{Re}\Phi_0
       +(\partial_r\operatorname{Im}\Phi_0)\,\mathrm d\omega.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Here angular indices are raised with $\overset{\circ}{\gamma}$.
The determinant and inverse of \eqref{eq:equality-metric-Maxwell-form}
give $\sqrt{|\det\mathbf g_0|}=\sqrt{\det\overset{\circ}{\gamma}}$,
$(\mathbf F_0)^{ru}=\partial_r\operatorname{Re}\Phi_0$, and
$(\mathbf F_0)^{Au}=0$. The radial Maxwell equations therefore reduce to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-radial-Maxwell}
 \begin{aligned}
 0&=\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{|\det\mathbf g_0|}\,(\mathbf F_0)^{\mu u}\bigr)
       =\sqrt{\det\overset{\circ}{\gamma}}\,\partial_r^2\operatorname{Re}\Phi_0,\\
 0&=(\mathrm d\mathbf F_0)_{rAB}
       =(\partial_r^2\operatorname{Im}\Phi_0)(\mathrm d\omega)_{AB}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The values and derivatives in \eqref{eq:completed-potential-data}
give $\widehat s=S\cdot y+O(\rho^2)$ and
$\widehat m=1+O(\rho^2)$. Formula~\eqref{eq:completed-vacuum-potentials}
therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-potential-limits}
 \frac{\operatorname{Re}\Phi_0}{r}\longrightarrow-1,\qquad
 \operatorname{Im}\Phi_0\longrightarrow-S\cdot\omega.
\end{equation}
Indeed \eqref{eq:cone-geodesic-data} gives $\rho/s\to1$,
and \eqref{eq:cone-affine-asymptotics} gives $rs\to1$
along each ray.
Equations~\eqref{eq:equality-radial-Maxwell} make $\Phi_0$
affine along each ray, and \eqref{eq:equality-potential-limits}
forces $\partial_r\Phi_0=-1$. We may therefore fix the affine
origin by $r=-\operatorname{Re}\Phi_0$. This change is independent
of $u$ and preserves $\partial_r$ and the normalization
\eqref{eq:equality-null-normalization}. It adds multiples of the
null generator to the angular tangents, so it also preserves
the metric \eqref{eq:equality-angular-metric}. We obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-angular-potential}
 \Phi_0=-r+ic,\qquad c=-S\cdot\omega,\qquad
 \mathbf F_0=\mathrm dr\wedge\mathrm du.
\end{equation}
The inverse metric now gives $(\mathbf F_0)^{rA}=a^A$, so the angular
part of $\mathrm dc=i_{\partial_u}{}^*\mathbf F_0$ reads
$\mathrm dc=*_{\overset{\circ}{\gamma}}a$. Applying $*_{\overset{\circ}{\gamma}}^2=-1$, and using
\eqref{eq:harrison} at $q=0$, determines both remaining coefficients,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-endpoint-data}
 a=-*_{\overset{\circ}{\gamma}}\mathrm dc,\qquad
 f_0=|\Phi_0|^2-1=r^2+c^2-1.
\end{equation}

Rotating the angular coordinates so that $S=(0,0,J)$, we have
\[
 c=-J\cos\theta,\qquad
 a=-J\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\phi.
\]
Put $R=r+1$ and $\Delta=R^2-2R+J^2$.
Applying the inverse
Harrison relation \eqref{eq:inverse-harrison} to
\eqref{eq:equality-angular-potential}, and extracting the unchanged
orbit metric from \eqref{eq:equality-metric-Maxwell-form}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-inverse-data}
 \sigma=\frac{\Phi_0+1}{\Phi_0-1}
          =1-\frac2{R+iJ\cos\theta},\qquad
 \gamma=(\mathrm dr-a)^2+f_0\overset{\circ}{\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Using $f_0=R^2-2R+J^2\cos^2\theta$ and the angular change
$\mathrm d\varphi=\mathrm d\phi+J\Delta^{-1}\mathrm dR$,
we complete the square in \eqref{eq:equality-inverse-data},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-orbit-metric}
 \begin{aligned}
 \gamma
 &=f_0\,\mathrm d\theta^2+\mathrm dR^2
       +2J\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm dR\,\mathrm d\phi
       +\Delta\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\phi^2\\
 &=f_0\left(\frac{\mathrm dR^2}{\Delta}
                    +\mathrm d\theta^2\right)
       +\Delta\sin^2\theta
          \left(\mathrm d\phi+\frac J\Delta\mathrm dR\right)^2\\
 &=f_0\left(\frac{\mathrm dR^2}{\Delta}
                    +\mathrm d\theta^2\right)
                  +\Delta\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi^2.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Taking real and imaginary parts of the Ernst potential in
\eqref{eq:equality-inverse-data} gives the norm and twist
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-vacuum-potentials}
 f=\frac{f_0}{R^2+J^2\cos^2\theta},\qquad
 \psi=\frac{2J\cos\theta}{R^2+J^2\cos^2\theta}.
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:equality-vacuum-potentials} into the first
identity in \eqref{eq:stationary-orbit-form-identities}, with the
Hodge star of \eqref{eq:equality-orbit-metric}, determines the
stationary connection through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-vacuum-connection}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathrm d\left(\frac{2JR\sin^2\theta}{f_0}\,\mathrm d\varphi\right)
 &=\frac{2J\sin\theta}{f_0^2}
   \Bigl[-\sin\theta(R^2-J^2\cos^2\theta)\,\mathrm dR
         \\
 &\hspace{7em}{}+2R\Delta\cos\theta\,\mathrm d\theta\Bigr]
                                      \wedge\mathrm d\varphi\\
 &=f^{-2}\sin\theta\bigl(\psi_\theta\,\mathrm dR
                 -\Delta\psi_R\,\mathrm d\theta\bigr)
                                      \wedge\mathrm d\varphi\\
 &=-f^{-2}*_{\gamma}\mathrm d\psi.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:equality-vacuum-connection}, the difference from
the original vacuum connection is closed and hence exact on
the exterior sphere region. After a stationary time change,
substituting this connection, \eqref{eq:equality-vacuum-potentials},
and \eqref{eq:equality-orbit-metric} into
\eqref{eq:stationary-reduction} gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbf g={}&-\frac{f_0}{R^2+J^2\cos^2\theta}
       \left(\mathrm dt+\frac{2JR\sin^2\theta}{f_0}
                                      \mathrm d\varphi\right)^2\\
 &+(R^2+J^2\cos^2\theta)
       \left(\frac{\mathrm dR^2}{\Delta}+\mathrm d\theta^2
             +\frac{\Delta\sin^2\theta}{f_0}\,\mathrm d\varphi^2\right).
 \end{split}
\]
This is the Kerr metric with mass one and angular momentum $S$.

\label{lem:zero-spin-staticity}
Let $\mathbf g$ and $\mathbf T$ be $C^5$ stationary vacuum fields near a spacelike
maximal hypersurface
$\overline\Sigma_1\simeq[1,\infty)\times\mathbb S^2$ with boundary
$\mathcal S_0$. Write $\mathbf T=nT_0+X$, where $T_0$ is the future
unit normal, and $f=-\mathbf g(\mathbf T,\mathbf T)$.
Assume that its end is the zero-time section of
a stationary chart satisfying \eqref{eq:AF} through five
derivatives with $M=1$, that $\mathbf T$ is tangent to $\mathcal S_0$, and
that $n>0$ on $\Sigma_1$. Let $U$ be the component of
$\{f>0\}\cap\Sigma_1$ containing the end and set
$F=\tfrac12\mathrm d\mathbf T^\flat$. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:staticity-hypotheses}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathbf T^\flat\wedge F=0\quad\hbox{on }U,\\
 \left.\mathrm df\right|_{T\Sigma_1}\ne0
       \quad\hbox{on }\partial_{\Sigma_1}U,
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
then
\[
 \mathbf T|_{\mathcal S_0}=0,\qquad k=0,\qquad X=0
       \quad\hbox{on }\overline\Sigma_1.
\]
In particular $f=n^2>0$ throughout $\Sigma_1$, and the stationary
metric is static in the region swept out by this slice.

We first prove $\overline U\cap\mathcal S_0\ne\varnothing$.
The $C^5$ fields satisfy \eqref{eq:KID}, with $n=0$ on
$\mathcal S_0$ by tangency of $\mathbf T$ and $n\to1$ at infinity.
The maximum-principle argument proving \eqref{eq:lapse-boundary}
therefore gives $\partial_{\nu_{\mathcal S_0}}n>0$.
On $U$, contracting $\mathbf T^\flat\wedge F=0$ with $\mathbf T$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:static-two-form}
 F=-\frac1{2f}\mathbf T^\flat\wedge\mathrm df,
 \qquad F\wedge F=0\quad\hbox{on }U.
\end{equation}
By continuity, $F\wedge F=0$ also holds on $\overline U$.
Suppose now that $\overline U\cap\mathcal S_0=\varnothing$,
and set $B=\partial_{\Sigma_1}U$. This is a nonempty compact
subset of the open slice, with $f=0$ on $B$. Since $n>0$
there, $\mathbf T$ does not vanish, and the first condition in
\eqref{eq:staticity-hypotheses}, extended by continuity, gives
$F=\mathbf T^\flat\wedge Y$ at each point of $B$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:static-zero-level-relations}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathrm df=2i_\mathbf T F=-2Y(\mathbf T)\mathbf T^\flat,\\
 \mathrm df|_{T\Sigma_1}=-2Y(\mathbf T)X^\flat,
 \qquad |X|_h=n>0\quad\text{on }B.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The second condition in \eqref{eq:staticity-hypotheses} makes $f$
a local coordinate near $B$. Its positive side belongs to $U$,
so $B$ is a smooth level surface, and
\eqref{eq:static-zero-level-relations} makes $X/n$ its unit normal.
We now compute the flux through this surface. Since
$\operatorname{div}_hX=0$ and $X$ is tangent to $\mathcal S_0$,
\[
 \mathrm d(i_X\mathrm{vol}_h)=(\operatorname{div}_hX)\mathrm{vol}_h=0,
 \qquad \int_{\mathcal S_0} i_X\mathrm{vol}_h=0.
\]
Because $\overline{\Sigma}_1\simeq[1,\infty)\times\mathbb S^2$,
the integral over $\mathcal S_0$ determines the cohomology class
of every closed two-form. The preceding identities therefore
make $i_X\mathrm{vol}_h$ exact, so its flux through each connected
component $B_0$ of $B$ must vanish. But with the unit normal $X/n$
from \eqref{eq:static-zero-level-relations}, this says
\[
 0=\int_{B_0}i_X\mathrm{vol}_h=\int_{B_0}n\,\mathrm d A>0,
\]
a contradiction. Hence there is a point
$p\in\overline U\cap\mathcal S_0$.

We use the point $p$ to prove that $X$ vanishes on the whole
sphere. Its restriction is Killing, since $n=0$ and
$\mathcal L_Xh=2nk$ imply $\mathcal L_X(h|_{T\mathcal S_0})=0$, and
$p\in\overline U$ together with $f|_{\mathcal S_0}=-|X|^2$
gives $X(p)=0$. It remains to show that its tangential derivative
at $p$, a rotation with coefficient $\omega_p$, also vanishes.
The flow of $\mathbf T$ fixes $p$ and preserves $\mathcal S_0$, so its
derivative preserves both the tangent and normal planes.
Since $n=X=0$ at $p$,
\[
 \mathbf g(\mathbf D_{\nu_{\mathcal S_0}}\mathbf T,T_0)
       =-\partial_{\nu_{\mathcal S_0}}n.
\]
Preservation of the normal plane and the Killing equation give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:static-normal-boost}
 \mathbf D_{\nu_{\mathcal S_0}}\mathbf T
       =(\partial_{\nu_{\mathcal S_0}}n)T_0,
 \qquad
 \mathbf D_{T_0}\mathbf T
       =(\partial_{\nu_{\mathcal S_0}}n)\nu_{\mathcal S_0}.
\end{equation}
The normal derivatives in \eqref{eq:static-normal-boost} and the
tangential rotation give, in an adapted orthonormal coframe,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:static-boundary-wedge}
 \begin{aligned}
 F|_p&=(\partial_{\nu_{\mathcal S_0}}n)\,
                   \theta^0\wedge\theta^1
                  +\omega_p\,\theta^2\wedge\theta^3,\\
 (F\wedge F)|_p
   &=2\omega_p(\partial_{\nu_{\mathcal S_0}}n)\,
                   \theta^0\wedge\theta^1\wedge\theta^2\wedge\theta^3
     =0.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The vanishing of $F\wedge F$ on $\overline U$ from
\eqref{eq:static-two-form}, together with the positive normal
lapse derivative in \eqref{eq:lapse-boundary}, forces
$\omega_p=0$ in \eqref{eq:static-boundary-wedge}. Thus the
Killing field $X|_{\mathcal S_0}$ and its derivative vanish at
$p$. Restricting it to geodesics gives Jacobi fields with zero
initial data, so it vanishes on the connected sphere and
$\mathbf T|_{\mathcal S_0}=0$.

We integrate the Sudarsky--Wald identity
\eqref{eq:stationary-divergence}. On $S_R=\{r=R\}$, with outward
unit normal $\nu_R$, the decay $k=O(r^{-3})$, $X=O(r^{-2})$
from \eqref{eq:slice-asymptotic-data} makes the flux tend to zero.
Since $X=0$ on $\mathcal S_0$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:staticity-integral}
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{\Sigma_1}n|k|^2\,\mathrm d\mu_h
   &=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{S_R}k(X,\nu_R)\,\mathrm d A\\
   &\quad-\int_{\mathcal S_0}k(X,\nu_{\mathcal S_0})\,\mathrm d A=0.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since $n>0$, \eqref{eq:staticity-integral} gives $k=0$, and
\eqref{eq:KID} makes $X$ Killing on the slice. Its tangential
derivatives vanish on $\mathcal S_0$, and the Killing equation
then gives the normal derivative as well. The same uniqueness
argument along geodesics gives $X=0$ on the connected slice.
Thus $\mathbf T=nT_0$ and $f=n^2>0$, and the stationary translates of
the slice are orthogonal to $\mathbf T$.

We prove \eqref{eq:horizon-smallness}. If this estimate has no
positive threshold $j_*(A)$ for a fixed $A$, there are smooth
spacetimes $(\mathbf g_j,\mathbf T_j,\Sigma_{1,j})$ satisfying the hypotheses with
$M_j=1$ and $\overline A_j\le A$, and angular momenta $S_j$ of
magnitude $J_j=|S_j|$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-spin-contradiction}
 J_j\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \sup_{\mathcal S_0}\mathbf g_j(\mathbf T_j,\mathbf T_j)
             \ge\varepsilon_{\mathrm{AIK}}(A)^2>0.
\end{equation}
Pull back the fields by the charts of Section~\ref{subsec:assumptions}.
To retain a Lorentzian limit, we need a uniform bound for the
inverse metric. Suppress $j$ and let $h^{ij}$ denote the inverse of the spatial block
$\mathbf g_{ij}$ in these coordinates. The bounds for $h$ at

 \mathbf g^{ij}&=h^{ij}-a^{-2}h^{ik}\mathbf g_{0k}h^{j\ell}\mathbf g_{0\ell}.
 \end{aligned}

\label{lem:harrison-collar}
For each $q\in[0,1]$, there is an outward unit spacelike normal
field along $\mathcal S_0$ whose geodesics form a spacelike
exterior collar, parametrized by length $\rho$ and base point $y$.

 \begin{gathered}
 \mathbf g_q=\mathrm d\rho^2+
 (h_\rho)_{AB}(\mathrm dy^A+\beta^A\mathrm dt)
                    (\mathrm dy^B+\beta^B\mathrm dt)-a^2\mathrm dt^2,\\
 \partial_t=\mathbf K,\qquad -\infty<t<\infty .
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The slices $t=\mathrm{const}$ are spacelike, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collar-orders}
 \begin{gathered}
 a=\kappa\rho+O(\rho^3),\qquad
 \beta=O(\rho^2),\qquad \partial_\rho\beta=O(\rho),\\

\label{lem:temporal-gradient}
Let $\mathbf g_j$ be smooth Lorentz metrics converging in $C^2$ to a
Lorentz metric near a fixed compact set $Q$.
Let $\tau$ be a fixed smooth function with
past timelike gradient and
\[
 \alpha_j^2=-\mathbf g_j^{-1}(\mathrm d\tau,\mathrm d\tau)\ge a_0^2>0
       \quad\hbox{on }Q\cap\{\tau\ge0\}.
\]
Suppose $\Sigma_j$ are smooth spacelike maximal hypersurfaces
with future unit normals $T_{0,j}$, whose portions $\Sigma_j\cap\{\tau\ge0\}$ are compact and
contained in $Q$. Put
\[
 V_j=-\alpha_j^{-1}\operatorname{grad}_{\mathbf g_j}\tau,
 \qquad v_j=-\mathbf g_j(T_{0,j},V_j),
 \qquad \tau_{\max}=\max_Q\max(\tau,0).
\]
There is an exponent $\mu>4$, depending only on $a_0$,
$\sup_j(\|\mathbf g_j\|_{C^2(Q)}+\|\mathbf g_j^{-1}\|_{C^0(Q)})$, and
$\|\tau\|_{C^3(Q)}$ in a fixed finite coordinate cover, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:temporal-gradient}
 \sup_{\Sigma_j\cap\{\tau>0\}}\tau^\mu v_j
 \le2\max\left\{2\tau_{\max}^\mu,
       \sup_{\partial\Sigma_j\cap\{\tau>0\}}\tau^\mu v_j\right\}.
\end{equation}
The boundary supremum is zero when its set is empty.

Timelike displacement of a causal curve, with the displacement
chosen by a scalar differential equation, is used in the proof of
\cite{ChruscielGrant}. Here we need a displacement
that stays uniformly small along an inextendible curve while
producing infinite proper time. We construct it to show that
\eqref{eq:compact-proper-time} excludes a causal curve confined
to a compact set with no future endpoint in $\mathcal B$.
Suppose that $\gamma$ is such a curve.
Choose a $C^2$ future unit timelike field $V$ on a neighbourhood of
its compact closure, and use the Riemannian metric
$\mathbf g+2V^\flat\otimes V^\flat$ to measure tangent vectors.
Parametrize $\gamma$ so that its
almost-everywhere tangent $l$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:causal-tangent-normalization}
 \mathbf g(l,V)=-1,\qquad \mathbf g(l,l)\le0,\qquad
 |l|_{\mathbf g+2V^\flat\otimes V^\flat}^2\le2 .
\end{equation}
The parameter in \eqref{eq:causal-tangent-normalization}
ranges over $[0,\infty)$, since a finite range would give
finite Riemannian length in a compact set and hence an endpoint,
contrary to inextendibility. Put
\[
 b(s)=\mathbf g(l,\mathbf D_lV),\qquad |b|\le K,\qquad K\ge1.
\]
The bound follows from $|l|\le\sqrt2$ in
\eqref{eq:causal-tangent-normalization} and the bound for $\mathbf D V$
on the compact closure of $\gamma$.
Under a displacement $uV$, the linear change in the squared
norm of the tangent is $2bu-2u'$. The coefficient $b$ has no
sign, so a constant displacement does not suffice. We choose
$u'-bu=c_0u^2$ so that $2bu-2u'=-2c_0u^2$ dominates
the quadratic error. Infinite proper time will then follow from
$\int_0^\infty|u|\,\mathrm ds=\infty$, while a small bound for
$|u|$ keeps the displaced curve in a compact neighbourhood.
Put
\[
 \Lambda'(s)=\exp\left(\int_0^sb(t)\,\mathrm dt\right),
 \qquad \Lambda(s)=\int_0^s\Lambda'(t)\,\mathrm dt.
\]
The equation for the displacement becomes
\[
 \left(\frac{\Lambda'}u\right)'=-c_0\Lambda'.
\]

according to whether $\Lambda$ is bounded gives, for $c_0$
sufficiently large,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:causal-displacement}
 u(s)=
 \begin{cases}
 -\dfrac{\Lambda'(s)}{c_0(1+\Lambda(s))},
       &\Lambda(s)\longrightarrow\infty,\\[7pt]
 \dfrac{\Lambda'(s)}{c_0(\Lambda_\infty-\Lambda(s))},
       &\Lambda(s)\longrightarrow\Lambda_\infty<\infty .
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:Riccati}
 u'=b u+c_0u^2,\qquad
 |u|\le\frac K{c_0},\qquad |u'|\le2K|u|,
 \qquad \int_0^\infty|u|\,\mathrm ds=\infty .
\end{equation}

$\Lambda'\le1+K\Lambda\le K(1+\Lambda)$.

 \Lambda_\infty-\Lambda(s)
 =\int_s^\infty\Lambda'(t)\,\mathrm dt
 \ge\Lambda'(s)\int_s^\infty e^{-K(t-s)}\,\mathrm dt

\]

\[
 c_0^{-1}\log(1+\Lambda(s))\qquad\text{and}\qquad
 c_0^{-1}\log[\Lambda_\infty/(\Lambda_\infty-\Lambda(s))].

 (\Psi_{u,V}^*\mathbf g)(V,V)&=-1.

 &\le-2c_0u^2-(u')^2+O(u^2)
 \le-c_0u^2<0 .

       =-1-u'+O(|u|)<-\tfrac12.
\]
Thus each finite segment of $\widetilde\gamma$ is future
timelike and satisfies the hypotheses of
\eqref{eq:compact-proper-time}. Indeed, its tangent is bounded,
and the positive minimum of $|u|$ on that interval makes
\eqref{eq:displaced-curve-norm} a uniform timelike bound.
But \eqref{eq:displaced-curve-norm} and \eqref{eq:Riccati}
give
\[
 L_\mathbf g(\widetilde\gamma|_{[0,S]})
       \ge\sqrt{c_0}\int_0^S|u|\,\mathrm ds
       \longrightarrow\infty .
\]
This is a contradiction. Time reversal excludes
past-inextendible causal curves confined to compact sets.

Having excluded future- and past-inextendible causal curves
in compact sets, we apply Beem's theorem to an open
$\mathcal O$ with $Q\Subset\mathcal O\Subset\mathcal B$.
The theorem makes $\mathcal O$ stably causal, meaning that
its cones can be widened while preserving
causality~\cite{MinguzziCausality}. It therefore admits a
smooth temporal function with past timelike
gradient~\cite{MinguzziCausality}.
