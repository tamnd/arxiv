<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{setup:main}
Fix $0<W<2$.  For every $s>0$,
\[
 \Phi_\gamma\longrightarrow\phi_W
 \quad\text{in probability in }H^{-s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal S)
 \qquad(\gamma\downarrow0).
\]
Equivalently, for every
$F\in C_b(H^{-s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal S))$,
\[
 \lim_{\gamma\downarrow0}\mathbb E^{(1)}[F(\Phi_\gamma)]=F(\phi_W).
\]
Moreover, in probability,
\begin{equation}\label{setup:measure}
 \mu_{h_\gamma^{(1)}}^\gamma
                  \Longrightarrow e^{\phi_W(z)}\,d^2z.
\end{equation}
The measure convergence also holds on the stated compactification;
the limiting area measure gives no mass to its boundary.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{setup:geometry}
The deterministic metric $e^{\phi_W}|dz|^2$ has area one and satisfies
\[
 -\Delta\phi_W=2\pi W e^{\phi_W},\qquad
 \partial_n\phi_W=0\quad\text{on }\partial\mathcal S.
\]
Its Gaussian curvature is $\pi W$, its horizontal boundary is geodesic,
and each marked boundary point has opening angle $\pi W/2$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The identity $\int_\mathbb R\operatorname{sech}^2(Wt/2)\,dt=4/W$ gives area one.
Differentiation gives
$\Delta\phi_W=-(W^2/2)\operatorname{sech}^2(Wt/2)=-2\pi W e^{\phi_W}$,
while $\phi_W$ is independent of $\theta$.
The curvature formula $-e^{-\phi_W}\Delta\phi_W/2$ gives $\pi W$;
the zero normal derivative along the straight horizontal boundary gives
geodesic boundary.  Near the negative end use $\zeta=e^{t+i\theta}$.
Then
\[
 e^{\phi_W}|dz|^2
 \sim\frac W\pi|\zeta|^{W-2}|d\zeta|^2.
\]
The distance to the end is asymptotic to
$\sqrt{W/\pi}(2/W)|\zeta|^{W/2}$, and the semicircle length is
asymptotic to $\pi\sqrt{W/\pi}|\zeta|^{W/2}$.  Their ratio is
$\pi W/2$.  The coordinate $\zeta=e^{-(t+i\theta)}$ gives the same
calculation at the positive end.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{setup:Pitman}
For $0<\gamma^2<2W$, the process $R_\gamma$ on either half-line
has the law of
\[
 B(t)-\left(\frac W\gamma-\frac\gamma2\right)t
 -2\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}
       \left\{B(s)-\left(\frac W\gamma-\frac\gamma2\right)s\right\},
 \qquad t\geq0.
\]
The two half-lines in this representation are independent.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
On one half-line put $M_t=\sup_{s\leq t}B(s)$ and
$Y_t=2M_t-B(t)$.  Pitman's theorem \cite{Pitman75} says that $Y$
is the variance-two three-dimensional Bessel process, with generator
$\partial_y^2+(2/y)\partial_y$ and entrance point zero.  Brownian
recurrence gives the pathwise identity
\[
 M_t=\inf_{s\geq t}Y_s.
\]
Indeed, $Y_s\geq M_s\geq M_t$ for $s\geq t$, and the first future
time at which $B$ hits $M_t$ gives equality.  The probability that
this Bessel process, started from $y$, hits $x\in(0,y)$ is $x/y$:
solve $g''+(2/y)g'=0$ with value one at $x$ and zero at infinity.
The Markov property therefore shows that, conditional on the path
of $Y$ up to time $t$, $M_t$ is uniform on $[0,Y_t]$.  Accordingly
$B(t)=2M_t-Y_t$ is conditionally uniform on $[-Y_t,Y_t]$.

Giving $B$ drift $-W/\gamma+\gamma/2$ up to time $t$ multiplies its
law by
\[
 \exp\!\left[-\frac12\left(\frac W\gamma-\frac\gamma2\right)B(t)
       -\frac t4\left(\frac W\gamma-\frac\gamma2\right)^2\right].
\]
Conditional expectation given the path of $Y$ up to time $t$ is
\[
 e^{-\frac t4(W/\gamma-\gamma/2)^2}
 \frac{\sinh((W/\gamma-\gamma/2)Y_t/2)}
      {(W/\gamma-\gamma/2)Y_t/2}.
\]
The ratio has value one at zero and is an eigenfunction of the
Bessel generator with eigenvalue $(W/\gamma-\gamma/2)^2/4$.
Its Doob transform has generator
\[
 \partial_y^2+
 \left(\frac W\gamma-\frac\gamma2\right)
 \coth\!\left[\frac12\left(\frac W\gamma-\frac\gamma2\right)y\right]
 \partial_y.
\]
Negating this process gives the transform in the statement, with
its entrance law at zero.  On the other hand, variance-two
Brownian motion with drift $-W/\gamma+\gamma/2$, started from
$x<0$, avoids zero with probability
$1-e^{(W/\gamma-\gamma/2)x}$.  Its survival Doob transform has drift
\[
 -\left(\frac W\gamma-\frac\gamma2\right)
 -\frac{2(W/\gamma-\gamma/2)e^{(W/\gamma-\gamma/2)x}}
              {1-e^{(W/\gamma-\gamma/2)x}}
 =-\left(\frac W\gamma-\frac\gamma2\right)
       \coth\!\left[-\frac12\left(\frac W\gamma-\frac\gamma2\right)x\right].
\]
This is the same diffusion generator.  The entrance law is the
limit as $x\uparrow0$, proving the stated conditioning convention.
Applying the construction to independent Brownian halves proves
the last assertion.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{ldp:gaussian}
The laws of $(\gamma B,\gamma X^\perp)$ satisfy a good large deviation
principle on $\mathcal B\times E_s$, at speed $\gamma^{-2}$,
with the rate specified in \eqref{ldp:gaussian-cost}.
These laws are exponentially tight.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The Brownian motion belongs to $\mathcal B$ almost surely.  Indeed, the
reflection principle gives, for each $\epsilon>0$,
\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}\mathbb P\!\left(\sup_{|t|\le n+1}|B(t)|>
                                  \epsilon n^{3/4}\right)
 \le 8\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-\epsilon^2 n^{3/2}/(4(n+1))}<\infty.
\]
Borel--Cantelli, for rational $\epsilon>0$, proves the required weighted
tail limit.  The resulting law is a centered Radon Gaussian law on the
separable space $\mathcal B$. Its Cameron--Martin squared norm is
$\frac12\int|u'|^2$, because its variance on each half-line is $2t$.
Fernique's theorem states that a centered Gaussian random variable $Y$
in a separable Banach space satisfies
$\mathbb E\exp(\epsilon\|Y\|^2)<\infty$ for some $\epsilon>0$.
Applied to the Brownian path as a random element of $\mathcal B$, it gives
\begin{equation}\label{ldp:fernique}
 \mathbb E e^{\epsilon\|B\|_*^2}<\infty
 \quad\text{for some }\epsilon>0.
\end{equation}
Here this exponential integrability also has an elementary Brownian
proof. If $\|B\|_*>r$, either $\sup_{|t|\le1}|B(t)|>r$ or, for some
$k\ge0$, $\sup_{|t|\le2^{k+1}}|B(t)|>r2^{3k/4}$.
The reflection principle and the Gaussian tail bound, on the two
half-lines, therefore give for $r\ge1$
\[
 \mathbb P(\|B\|_*>r)
 \le8e^{-r^2/4}+8\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-r^2 2^{k/2}/8}
 \le C e^{-c r^2}
\]
for constants $C,c>0$. In the last inequality one can use
$2^{k/2}\ge1+(\sqrt2-1)k$ and sum a geometric series.
Integrating this tail proves \eqref{ldp:fernique} for $0<\epsilon<c$.
In particular, it gives
$\mathbb P(\|\gamma B\|_*>r)\le C_\epsilon e^{-\epsilon r^2/\gamma^2}$,
where $C_\epsilon=\mathbb E e^{\epsilon\|B\|_*^2}$; this is the estimate
used later to control exceptional radial paths.

The construction of $X^\perp$ from independent angular modes gives, for
$0<|h|\le1$,
\[
 \mathbb E\|X^\perp(t+h)-X^\perp(t)\|_{H_N^{-s}}^2
 \lesssim_s\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-1-2s}\min\{1,n|h|\}.
\]
For every $0<r<\min\{2s,1\}$, splitting this sum at $|h|^{-1}$
bounds it by a constant times $|h|^r$. Gaussian higher moments and
the continuity criterion, applied on each bounded time interval,
give an $E_s$-valued version. More explicitly, for an integer $k$
large enough that $kr/2>1$, a centered Hilbert-valued Gaussian variable
satisfies
$\mathbb E\|Y\|^k\lesssim_k(\mathbb E\|Y\|^2)^{k/2}$.
The increment bound is then $O(|h|^{kr/2})$, so Kolmogorov's
continuity criterion applies. Taking a countable exhaustion of the
time line produces compatible continuous versions. In the normalized
cosine coordinates,
the $n$th covariance is $(\pi/n)e^{-n|t-t'|}$. Applying
$-d^2/dt^2+n^2$ to this kernel gives $2\pi\delta_0$; hence the
Cameron--Martin squared norm of $X^\perp$ is
$\frac1{2\pi}\int|\nabla v|^2$. To apply the Banach-space Gaussian
LDP \cite{Baldi,DZ}, first restrict the lateral field to $[-j,j]$.
The pair $Y_j=(B,X^\perp|_{[-j,j]})$ is a centered Radon Gaussian
random variable in the separable Banach space
$\mathcal B\times C([-j,j];H_N^{-s}(0,\pi))$, with zero angular
mean in its second coordinate. Its small-noise rate is one half
of its squared Cameron--Martin norm. The restricted lateral norm
is the infimum of the global norm over extensions. The
Dawson--G\"artner projective-limit theorem \cite{DZ} therefore
gives an LDP on $\mathcal B\times E_s$ with rate the supremum
of these restricted rates. This supremum equals
\eqref{ldp:gaussian-cost}: if it is finite, choose global
Cameron--Martin extensions on $[-j,j]$ with uniformly bounded
energies. A weakly convergent subsequence in $\mathcal H_\perp$
agrees with the specified field on every bounded interval and
has no larger energy. The converse inequality follows by restricting
any global extension. Independence adds the radial cost, giving
the factors $1/4$ and $1/(4\pi)$.

Exponential tightness in the projective-limit topology can be checked
separately. By the compactly embedded Gaussian Banach space of
\cite[Theorem 1.1 and Section 2]{Baldi}, each $Y_j$ has an almost
surely finite norm $\|\cdot\|_j^\sharp$ whose balls have compact
closure in its ambient Banach space, and
$C_j:=\mathbb E\exp(\epsilon_j\|Y_j\|_j^{\sharp\,2})<\infty$ for some
$\epsilon_j>0$. Given $L>0$, choose $r_j$ so that
$\epsilon_jr_j^2\ge L+j+\log C_j$, and let $K_j$ be the closure
of that norm ball. For $0<\gamma\le1$, Markov gives
\[
 \mathbb P(\gamma Y_j\notin K_j)
 \le C_j e^{-\epsilon_jr_j^2/\gamma^2}
 \le e^{-(L+j)/\gamma^2}.
\]
The set of pairs whose restriction belongs to $K_j$ for every $j$
is compact in $\mathcal B\times E_s$, by a diagonal subsequence
and compatibility of the restrictions. A union bound bounds its
complement by $\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-(L+j)/\gamma^2}$, whose logarithmic
upper rate is at most $-L$. This proves exponential tightness
without inferring it merely from compactness of rate sublevels.

The rate sublevels are compact in the stated topology. A radial
energy bound gives local equicontinuity and $|u(t)|\lesssim\sqrt{|t|}$;
the latter makes the weighted tails uniformly small. For the
lateral component, zero angular mean gives
$\|v\|_{L^2(\mathcal S)}\le\|v_\theta\|_{L^2(\mathcal S)}$. A bounded
energy set therefore satisfies
\[
 \sup_t\|v(t)\|_{L^2(0,\pi)}^2
 \le2\|v\|_{L^2(\mathcal S)}\|v_t\|_{L^2(\mathcal S)},\qquad
 \|v(t+h)-v(t)\|_{L^2(0,\pi)}
 \le |h|^{1/2}\|v_t\|_{L^2(\mathcal S)}.
\]
The compact embedding $L^2(0,\pi)\hookrightarrow H_N^{-s}(0,\pi)$
and Arzela--Ascoli give compactness in $E_s$. Weak lower
semicontinuity of both energies gives closedness of the sublevels.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{var:onofri}
For $W>0$, suppose that $g$ is $H^1$ on every bounded full strip,
has finite Dirichlet energy on the strip, and
$\int_{\mathcal S}g^2e^{\phi_W}\,\mathrm d^2 z<\infty$.  Then
$\int e^{g+\phi_W}\,\mathrm d^2 z$ is finite and
\begin{equation}
\label{var:onofri-formula}
 W\log\int_{\mathcal S}e^{g+\phi_W}\,\mathrm d^2 z
 \le W\int_{\mathcal S}g e^{\phi_W}\,\mathrm d^2 z
    +\frac1{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal S}|g_t|^2\,\mathrm d^2 z
    +\frac{W^2}{16\pi}\int_{\mathcal S}|g_\theta|^2\,\mathrm d^2 z.
\end{equation}
For $g$ independent of $\theta$, equality holds exactly when
\begin{equation}
\label{var:onofri-equality}
 g(t)=c-2\log\{1+\kappa\tanh(Wt/2)\},
 \qquad c\in\mathbb R,\quad -1<\kappa<1.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The sharp spherical Onofri inequality \cite{Onofri,Mancini}, with
$\,\mathrm d\omega=\,\mathrm d A/(4\pi)$ on the unit sphere, is
\begin{equation}
\label{var:sphere}
 \log\int_{\mathbb S^2}e^h\,\mathrm d\omega
 \le\int_{\mathbb S^2}h\,\mathrm d\omega
       +\frac1{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb S^2}|\nabla h|^2\,\mathrm d A.
\end{equation}
In cylinder coordinates its metric is
$\operatorname{sech}^2 s\,(\,\mathrm d s^2+\,\mathrm d\theta^2)$.  Apply this inequality to the
even reflection, in the angular variable, of $g(2s/W,\theta)$ from
$0<\theta<\pi$ to the circle of length $2\pi$.
Reflection requires no boundary condition on the normal derivative.
Conformal invariance of Dirichlet energy and the weighted $L^2$
assumption give finite spherical $L^2$ norm and finite Dirichlet energy
away from the poles. To justify extension across a pole, first truncate
the function between $-M$ and $M$. In a conformal coordinate about the
pole, use a radial cutoff that is zero for $r\le\varepsilon^2$, is
$\log(r/\varepsilon^2)/\log(1/\varepsilon)$ for
$\varepsilon^2<r<\varepsilon$, and is one for $r\ge\varepsilon$.
Its Dirichlet energy is $2\pi/\log(1/\varepsilon)$.
Multiplying the truncated function by this cutoff changes its $H^1$
norm by a quantity tending to zero: the extra gradient term is bounded
by $M^2$ times this energy, and the original gradient and $L^2$ terms
on the shrinking disk tend to zero by integrability. Do this at both
poles, and then let $M\to\infty$. The truncations converge in $H^1$
by dominated convergence applied to the function and its gradient.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{mom:covariance-bound}
For distinct points $z,w\in\mathcal S$,
\begin{equation}
 |G_{\perp,N}(z,w)|\leq2\log^+\frac1{|z-w|}+O(1),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[X_M^\perp(z)X_N^\perp(w)]=G_{\perp,\min(M,N)}(z,w),
 \label{mom:covariance-formula}
\end{equation}
where the bounded term is absolute and uniform in the cutoffs and
horizontal translations.  The same bound holds for $G_\perp$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The mixed covariance follows directly from independence of the
modes.  To bound a single cutoff, for $|q|\leq1$, $q\ne1$, first
suppose $N|1-q|\leq1$.  Then
\[
 \left|\Re\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{q^n}{n}\right|
       \leq1+\log N\leq1+\log(1/|1-q|).
\]
If $N|1-q|>1$, summation by parts and
$|\sum_{n=N+1}^{m}q^n|\leq2/|1-q|$ give
$|\sum_{n>N}q^n/n|\leq2/((N+1)|1-q|)\leq2$.
The full series has real part $-\log|1-q|$; when $|q|=1$, use
Dirichlet convergence.  Since $|1-q|\leq2$, we have in either case
\[
 \left|\Re\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{q^n}{n}\right|
                     \leq\log^+(1/|1-q|)+O(1).
\]
For $s\geq0$,
\[
 |1-e^{-s+ix}|^2=(1-e^{-s})^2+2e^{-s}(1-\cos x)
 \gtrsim\min\{1,s^2+\operatorname{dist}(x,2\pi\mathbb Z)^2\}.
\]
Use this with $s=|t-t'|$ and $x=\theta-\theta'$ or
$x=\theta+\theta'$.  The corresponding angular distances satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(\theta-\theta',2\pi\mathbb Z)=|\theta-\theta'|,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{dist}(\theta+\theta',2\pi\mathbb Z)
       =\min(\theta+\theta',2\pi-\theta-\theta')\geq|\theta-\theta'|.
\]
The identity $2\cos(n\theta)\cos(n\theta')
=\cos(n(\theta-\theta'))+\cos(n(\theta+\theta'))$ proves the bound.
The same argument gives the off-diagonal limit
\begin{equation}
 G_\perp(z,w)=-\log|1-e^{-|t-t'|+i(\theta-\theta')}|
         -\log|1-e^{-|t-t'|+i(\theta+\theta')}|.
 \label{mom:full-covariance}
\end{equation}
Convergence is absolute if $t\ne t'$ and follows from Dirichlet's
test otherwise.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{mom:collision-integral}
For a bounded measurable $D\subset\mathbb R^2$, an integer $q\geq2$ and
$0<s<4/q$,
\begin{equation}
 \int_{D^q}\prod_{i<j}(1\wedge|z_i-z_j|)^{-s}\,\,\mathrm d\mathbf z
 \leq |D|\left(|D|+\frac{2\pi}{2-sq/2}\right)^{q-1}.
 \label{mom:collision-bound}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Every unordered pair occurs in two of the products below, so
\[
 \prod_{i<j}(1\wedge|z_i-z_j|)^{-s}
 =\prod_{i=1}^q
   \left(\prod_{j\ne i}(1\wedge|z_i-z_j|)^{-sq/2}\right)^{1/q}.
\]
Apply H\"older on this product space with $q$ equal exponents.
The integral of the left side is at most
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^q\left[
 \int_{D^q}\prod_{j\ne i}(1\wedge|z_i-z_j|)^{-sq/2}
                   d\mathbf z\right]^{1/q}.
\]
In its $i$th factor, hold $z_i$ fixed and integrate the other
$q-1$ variables first. There are no pair interactions between
those variables in this factor, so their integrals multiply.  Each inner integral is
at most
$|D|+2\pi\int_0^1\rho^{1-sq/2}\,\,\mathrm d\rho
=|D|+2\pi/(2-sq/2)$.
Integration in $z_i$ supplies the remaining factor $|D|$.
This treats all simultaneous collisions and all collections of
clusters with an explicit dependence on $q$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{mom:high-moment}
Fix $0<W<2$ and a bounded interval $I$.  For all sufficiently small
$\gamma>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{N\geq1}\sup_{0\leq a\leq1}
 \big\|Z_{\gamma,N;D}(\log a)\big\|_{L^{\,1+(W+2)/(2\gamma^2)}(\mathbb P)}
                     \lesssim_{W,|I|}1.
 \label{mom:high-moment-bound}
\end{equation}
The cutoff masses converge in this norm to
$Z_{\gamma;D}(\log a)$, with the same bound and with mean
$\int_Da(z)(2\sin\theta)^{-\gamma^2/2}\,\,\mathrm d^2 z$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
For an integer $q\geq2$, positivity and Tonelli allow the $q$
spatial integrals and expectation to be interchanged.  The variance
of $\sum_iX_N^\perp(z_i)$ is
$\sum_iG_{\perp,N}(z_i,z_i)+2\sum_{i<j}G_{\perp,N}(z_i,z_j)$.
Consequently the subtracted Wick self-variances cancel exactly, and
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E[Z_{\gamma,N;D}(\log a)^q]
 =\int_{D^q}\prod_i a(z_i)(2\sin\theta_i)^{-\gamma^2/2}
       \exp\!\left(\gamma^2\sum_{i<j}G_{\perp,N}(z_i,z_j)\right)
                                             \,\mathrm d\mathbf z.
 \label{mom:replica-formula}
\end{equation}
The bounded part of the covariance contributes
$\exp(O(\gamma^2q(q-1)))$.  After taking a $q$-th root it is
$\exp(O(\gamma^2q))$, with an absolute implied constant.
To integrate the remaining factors, fix
$1<p<4/(W+2)$.  H\"older with exponents $p$ and $p/(p-1)$,
separates the factors
$\prod_{i<j}(1\wedge|z_i-z_j|)^{-2\gamma^2}$ and
$\prod_i(2\sin\theta_i)^{-\gamma^2/2}$.
For the first factor use Lemma~\ref{mom:collision-integral} with
$s=2p\gamma^2$; the second factor is a product of $q$ identical
one-point integrals. Taking the $q$th root yields
\begin{align}
 \big\|Z_{\gamma,N;D}(\log a)\big\|_{L^q(\mathbb P)}
 &\leq e^{O(\gamma^2q)}|D|^{1/(pq)}
 \left(|D|+\frac{2\pi}{2-p\gamma^2q}\right)^{(q-1)/(pq)}
 \notag\\[-2pt]
 &\quad\times
 \left[|I|\int_0^\pi(2\sin\theta)^{-p\gamma^2/(2(p-1))}
                                  \,\,\mathrm d\theta\right]^{(p-1)/p},
 \label{mom:explicit-moment}
\end{align}
provided $p\gamma^2q<2$ and $p\gamma^2/(2(p-1))<1$.
The first condition is exactly the collision-integral condition
with exponent $2p\gamma^2$; the second integrates the boundary
weight.  This also proves that the quantities in
\eqref{mom:replica-formula} are finite.

Now take $q=\lceil1+(W+2)/(2\gamma^2)\rceil$.  Then
\[
 \frac{W+2}{2}<\gamma^2q\leq\frac{W+2}{2}+2\gamma^2,
 \qquad
 2-p\gamma^2q\longrightarrow2-p(W+2)/2>0.
\]
For small $\gamma$, the integrand in the one-point boundary
integral is bounded by
$\max\{1,(2\sin\theta)^{-1/2}\}$, an integrable function.
Every factor of \eqref{mom:explicit-moment} is thus bounded
independently of $\gamma,N,a$.  Lyapunov's inequality passes from
this integer $q$ to the asserted real exponent.

Let $\mathcal G_N=\sigma(U_1,\ldots,U_N)$, including their entire
paths.  Independence and mean-one Wick normalization give, for
$M\geq N$,
\[
 \mathbb E[Z_{\gamma,M;D}(\log a)\mid\mathcal G_N]
                         =Z_{\gamma,N;D}(\log a).
\]
The nonnegative martingale is bounded in $L^q(\mathbb P)$, with $q>1$ fixed
once $\gamma$ is fixed.  Uniform integrability gives an almost
sure and $L^1(\mathbb P)$ limit with the stated mean; Fatou gives its $L^q(\mathbb P)$
bound.  The martingale then equals the conditional expectation of
this limit, and approximation by bounded cylinder functions proves
convergence in $L^q(\mathbb P)$. More explicitly, if the limit is $Y$ and $Y_j$ is a
bounded $\mathcal G_j$-measurable approximation in $L^q(\mathbb P)$, then for
$N\ge j$ conditional expectation is a contraction and
\[
 \|\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal G_N]-Y\|_{L^q(\mathbb P)}
 \le 2\|Y-Y_j\|_{L^q(\mathbb P)}.
\]
Such approximations exist because
$\sigma(\bigcup_j\mathcal G_j)$ contains $Y$. Sending $j\to\infty$
proves convergence in $L^q(\mathbb P)$ and therefore in the asserted norm.  The domination in \eqref{mom:explicit-moment} also permits
$N\to\infty$ in \eqref{mom:replica-formula}, replacing $G_{\perp,N}$ by
$G_\perp$, for every fixed $\gamma,q$ satisfying its two strict
integrability conditions.

\label{mom:measure}
For each fixed sufficiently small $\gamma>0$, there is a positive
random Radon measure $\mu_\gamma$ on the strip such that, for every
fixed bounded box $D$ and deterministic Borel density $0\le a\le1$,
\[
 \int_D a(z)\,\mu_\gamma(dz)
 = Z_{\gamma;D}(\log a)
 =\lim_{N\to\infty}Z_{\gamma,N;D}(\log a)
 \quad\text{almost surely and in }L^1(\mathbb P).
\]
Here the limit on the right is the real-valued martingale limit from
Lemma~\ref{mom:high-moment}. The expectation measure is
$(2\sin\theta)^{-\gamma^2/2}d^2z$. In particular, for every fixed
Lebesgue-null set $A$, $\mu_\gamma(A)=0$ almost surely.

On each closed
box $[-T,T]\times[0,\pi]$, take simultaneous limits against a
countable uniformly dense rational vector space of continuous
functions containing the constants.  Signed tests are handled by
their positive and negative parts.  The cutoff total masses
converge, and
$|\int f\,\,\mathrm d\mu_N|\leq\|f\|_\infty\mu_N(D)$.
The limit therefore extends to a bounded positive functional on

the Riesz representation theorem.  Its expectation is
$(2\sin\theta)^{-\gamma^2/2}\,\mathrm d^2 z$, first on continuous tests and

therefore agree on overlaps and define a positive Radon measure

Continuous approximation in $L^1((2\sin\theta)^{-\gamma^2/2}\,\mathrm d^2 z)$
identifies each bounded Borel integral with the limit of the
corresponding real-valued cutoff masses.  The common-coupling comparison in Subsection~\ref{reg:section}
identifies this measure with the geometric chaos used in the disk model.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{mom:positive}
Almost surely the limiting measure assigns positive mass to every
nonempty open set in the strip.

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Fix a compact interior rectangle of positive area. We prove that
its mass is almost surely nonzero. For each positive integer $m$,
delete the first $m$ modes.  The resulting
cutoff masses form a nonnegative martingale whose covariance is
$G_{\perp,N}-G_{\perp,m}$.  On a fixed interior rectangle $G_{\perp,m}$ and the boundary
weight are bounded.  Lemma~\ref{mom:covariance-bound} and the
two-replica formula give a uniform second moment when $\gamma^2<1$,
because $\int_{D^2}(1\wedge|z-w|)^{-2\gamma^2}\,\mathrm d^2 z\,\mathrm d^2 w<\infty$.
The remaining-mode mass thus has an almost sure and $L^2(\mathbb P)$ limit.
The full density is exactly this density multiplied by
$\exp(\gamma X_m^\perp(z)-\gamma^2G_{\perp,m}(z,z)/2)$, a continuous function
bounded above and below by positive random constants on the
rectangle.  After integration and passage to the limit, the full
mass is zero if and only if the remaining-mode mass is zero.
For every $m$ the latter event is measurable with respect to
$\sigma(U_n:n>m)$. Its limit inferior as $m\to\infty$ belongs to
the intersection of these sigma-fields: for each fixed $j$, all
terms with $m\ge j$ are measurable with respect to
$\sigma(U_n:n>j)$. The limit inferior agrees almost surely with
the full zero-mass event. Independence of the modes and Kolmogorov's
zero--one law show that its probability is zero or one. Probability
one would force the full mass to have expectation zero, contrary
to its preserved positive mean. Its probability is therefore zero.  A countable basis of
rectangles then gives positive mass to every nonempty open set.

The assumed envelope reads
\begin{equation}
 e^{f_\gamma(t)}
 \le\min\{1,4e^{-W|t|/2+r(1+|t|)^{3/4}}\}.
 \label{mom:radial-envelope}
\end{equation}
If $|t|\geq\max\{1,(2^{11/4}r/W)^4\}$, then
$r(1+|t|)^{3/4}\leq W|t|/4$.
There are $O_W((1+r)^4)$ unit intervals meeting the complementary

summable geometric sequence from $4e^{-W|t|/4}$.
Their total is therefore $\lesssim_W(1+r)^4$.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{mom:cutoff-error}
Fix a bounded strip box $D$.  There is a deterministic sequence
$\varepsilon_N\to0$ such that, for all sufficiently small
$\gamma$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{0\leq a\leq1}
 \big\|Z_{\gamma;D}(\log a)-Z_{\gamma,N;D}(\log a)
       \big\|_{L^{\,1+(W+2)/(2\gamma^2)}(\mathbb P)}\leq\varepsilon_N.
 \label{mom:cutoff-norm}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\limsup_{\gamma\downarrow0}\gamma^2\log
 \sup_{0\leq a\leq1}
 \mathbb P\left(\left|Z_{\gamma;D}(\log a)
              -Z_{\gamma,N;D}(\log a)\right|>\varepsilon\right)
                         =-\infty.
 \label{mom:cutoff-probability}
\end{equation}
The corresponding signed integrals obey the same estimates for
deterministic densities of absolute value at most one.  The bounds
also hold conditionally for independent random densities.
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
A direct expansion at the diverging target moment order would have
an increasing number of signed terms. Instead we first prove convergence
at one fixed even moment order, and then use hypercontractivity to
reach the target order. Choose an even integer $k>4/(2-W)$. This is
equivalent to $k(W+2)/(2(k-1))<2$, so one can choose $p>1$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \frac{pk(W+2)}{2(k-1)}<2.
 \label{mom:fixed-replica-parameters}
\end{equation}
Temporarily use the noise parameter
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{W+2}{2(k-1)}}
\]
and keep the base weight $(2\sin\theta)^{-\gamma^2/2}$ unchanged.
Expand the even $k$-th
power of the difference of two finite-cutoff masses.  In each
replica choose $n_i=M$ or $n_i=N$ according to a subset
$J\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}$, with $n_i=M$ exactly when $i\in J$.
The mixed covariance in \eqref{mom:covariance-formula} gives the
signed kernel
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{J\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}}(-1)^{k-|J|}
 \exp\!\left\{\frac{W+2}{2(k-1)}
                    \sum_{i<j}G_{\perp,\min(n_i,n_j)}(z_i,z_j)\right\}.
 \label{mom:mixed-replica-kernel}
\end{equation}
Its absolute value is bounded, uniformly in $M\geq N$, by a
constant depending only on $W,k$ times
\begin{equation}
 \prod_{i<j}(1\wedge|z_i-z_j|)^{-(W+2)/(k-1)}.
 \label{mom:fixed-replica}
\end{equation}
This bound is integrable after multiplication by the $k$
boundary weights, uniformly for small $\gamma$.  Indeed,
H\"older with $p,p/(p-1)$ and
Lemma~\ref{mom:collision-integral} integrate its $p$-th power by
\eqref{mom:fixed-replica-parameters}.  Fix a sufficiently small
$\varepsilon>0$ with $p\varepsilon^2/(2(p-1))<1$; for
$0<\gamma\leq\varepsilon$ the remaining weight is dominated by
$\prod_i\max\{1,(2\sin\theta_i)^{-\varepsilon^2/2}\}$, whose
$p/(p-1)$-th power is integrable.

For a configuration of distinct points, all mixed covariances
converge to $G_\perp(z_i,z_j)$ uniformly over $M\geq N$ as
$N\to\infty$.  Every term of \eqref{mom:mixed-replica-kernel}
then has the same limit, while its coefficients sum to zero.
The supremum over the countably many $M\geq N$ of its absolute
value therefore tends to zero almost everywhere.  Dominated
convergence with the preceding envelope proves
\begin{align}
 \sup_{\substack{0<\gamma\leq\varepsilon\\0\leq a\leq1,\ M\geq N}}
 \Bigg\|\int_D a(z)(2\sin\theta)^{-\gamma^2/2}
 \Big(&:e^{\sqrt{(W+2)/(2(k-1))}\,X_M^\perp(z)}:
 \notag\\[-2pt]
 &-:e^{\sqrt{(W+2)/(2(k-1))}\,X_N^\perp(z)}:\Big)\,\mathrm d^2 z
       \Bigg\|_{L^k(\mathbb P)}\longrightarrow0.
 \label{mom:fixed-replica-convergence}
\end{align}
The signed expansion is legitimate term by term because each
term has this same integrable bound.  Evenness of $k$ makes the
expanded power the absolute $k$-th power.  No full-chaos estimate
was assumed in this two-cutoff argument.

For $0\le\rho\le1$, let $X^{\perp\prime}$ be an independent copy of
$X^\perp$ and let $\mathbb E'$ integrate this copy. For a Gaussian functional
$Y$, the Mehler operator is
$T_\rho Y(X^\perp)=\mathbb E'[Y(\rho X^\perp+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}X^{\perp\prime})]$.  Gaussian hypercontractivity \cite{BBG} states
\[
 \|T_\rho Y\|_{L^q(\mathbb P)}\leq\|Y\|_{L^k(\mathbb P)},
 \qquad q\geq k>1,\quad \rho^2\leq(k-1)/(q-1).
\]
It holds on the present Gaussian space by finite-dimensional
approximation.  The action on a Wick exponential can be checked
without a series expansion. If $A$ is a centered Gaussian linear
functional of variance $\sigma^2$ and $A'$ is its independent copy,
then
\[
 \mathbb E'\exp\left\{\alpha(\rho A+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}A')
                         -\frac{\alpha^2\sigma^2}{2}\right\}
 =\exp\left\{\alpha\rho A-\frac{\alpha^2\rho^2\sigma^2}{2}\right\}.
\]
Thus $T_\rho:e^{\alpha A}:=:e^{\rho\alpha A}:$.
Integration against the deterministic density commutes with $T_\rho$
by integrability of the finite-cutoff masses. Take
\[
 \rho=\gamma\sqrt{\frac{2(k-1)}{W+2}},
 \qquad q=1+\frac{W+2}{2\gamma^2}.
\]
For small $\gamma$, $q\geq k$ and the hypercontractive condition
is an equality.  Applying it to the difference in
\eqref{mom:fixed-replica-convergence} replaces the noise
parameter by $\gamma$ and preserves the deterministic boundary
weight.  It gives the norm bound in \eqref{mom:cutoff-norm}
first between cutoffs $M,N$ and then, by
Lemma~\ref{mom:high-moment}, with $M\to\infty$.
Finally Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \gamma^2\log\sup_a\mathbb P\left(
 |Z_{\gamma;D}(\log a)-Z_{\gamma,N;D}(\log a)|>\varepsilon\right)
 \leq\left(\gamma^2+\frac{W+2}{2}\right)
                          \log(\varepsilon_N/\varepsilon),
\]
once $\varepsilon_N<\varepsilon$, proving
\eqref{mom:cutoff-probability}.  Absolute values of products of
signed densities give the same bounds.  Conditioning proves the
independent-density assertion; the supremum is always outside
the norm or probability, not inside the random variable.

\label{reg:mixed-covariance}
All Fourier/Fourier, Fourier/smooth, and smooth/smooth mixed covariances
satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Cov}(U(z),V(w))\le2\log^+\frac1{|z-w|}+O(1),
 \label{reg:covariance-bound}
\end{equation}
uniformly in their cutoffs.  For distinct interior points they converge

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
The common mode coupling gives
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Cov}(X^\perp_N(z),X^\perp_\varepsilon(w))
 &=\int K_\varepsilon(w,y)G_{\perp,N}(z,y)\,\,\mathrm d y,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X^\perp_\varepsilon(z),X^\perp_{\varepsilon'}(w))
 &=\iint K_\varepsilon(z,x)K_{\varepsilon'}(w,y)G_\perp(x,y)\,\,\mathrm d x\,\,\mathrm d y.
\end{align*}
The passage from finite modes is justified by logarithmic domination.
For any such probability kernel,
\[
 \int K_\varepsilon(w,x)\log^+\frac1{|z-x|}\,\,\mathrm d x
 \le\log^+\frac1{|z-w|}+O_\rho(1).
\]
If $|z-w|\ge2\varepsilon$, use $|z-x|\ge|z-w|/2$.
Otherwise scaling bounds the integral by
$\log(1/\varepsilon)+O_\rho(1)$, using
$\int_{|u|<1}\log(1/|u|)\,\,\mathrm d u=\pi/2$.

\emph{Step 1: continuous approximating maps.}
Let $D_T=[-T,T]\times(0,\pi)$ and let $P_N$ project onto the first
$N$ nonconstant cosine modes. Define
\begin{equation}\label{ldp:finite-map}
 f_{T,N}^g(u,v)=\int_{D_T}e^{d_0(u+g;t)+P_Nv(t,\theta)}\,dt\,d\theta,
 \qquad G_{T,N}^g(u,v)=(u,v,f_{T,N}^g(u,v)).
\end{equation}
For fixed $T,N$, this map is continuous on $\mathcal E$:
the running maximum is locally Lipschitz and the finitely many
coefficients of $P_Nv$ converge uniformly on $[-T,T]$.
The ordinary contraction principle therefore applies to
$G_{T,N}^g(X_\gamma)$.

Write $A_{\gamma,T}^g$ for the restriction of $A_\gamma^g$ to $D_T$,
and $A_{\gamma,T,N}^g$ for its Fourier-cutoff version. To distinguish
this last random variable from $f_{T,N}^g(X_\gamma)$, put
\[
 c_{\gamma,N}(\theta)=(2\sin\theta)^{-\gamma^2/2}
             e^{-\gamma^2G_{\perp,N}((0,\theta),(0,\theta))/2},
 \qquad
 f_{\gamma,T,N}^g(u,v)=
 \int_{D_T}e^{d_\gamma(u+g;t)+P_Nv(t,\theta)}
              c_{\gamma,N}(\theta)\,dt\,d\theta.
\]
Thus $A_{\gamma,T,N}^g=f_{\gamma,T,N}^g(X_\gamma)$, including
the boundary factor and Wick correction. There are three errors:
\begin{align}\label{ldp:three-errors}
 |A_\gamma^g-f_{T,N}^g(X_\gamma)|
 &\le (A_\gamma^g-A_{\gamma,T}^g)
       +|A_{\gamma,T}^g-A_{\gamma,T,N}^g|\notag\\
 &\quad+|f_{\gamma,T,N}^g(X_\gamma)-f_{T,N}^g(X_\gamma)|.
\end{align}

\emph{Step 2: the Fourier error at a fixed box.}
Conditionally on $B$, the density $e^{d_\gamma(\gamma B+g)}$ is
between zero and one and is independent of the lateral field.
Lemma~\ref{mom:cutoff-error} gives numbers
$\varepsilon_{N,T}\to0$ as $N\to\infty$, for each fixed $T$, such that
\[
 \|A_{\gamma,T}^g-A_{\gamma,T,N}^g\|_{L^{q_\gamma}(\mathbb P)}
 \le\varepsilon_{N,T},\qquad
 q_\gamma=1+\frac{W+2}{2\gamma^2}.
\]
For every $\eta>0$, Markov's inequality yields
\[
 \limsup_{\gamma\downarrow0}\gamma^2\log
 \mathbb P(|A_{\gamma,T}^g-A_{\gamma,T,N}^g|>\eta)
 \le\frac{W+2}{2}\log\frac{\varepsilon_{N,T}}{\eta}.
\]
The right side tends to $-\infty$ with $N$. The box is fixed
throughout this assertion; uniformity in $T$ is neither claimed nor
needed.

\emph{Step 3: the horizontal tail.}
On $\|\gamma B\|_*\le r$, the envelope
\eqref{ldp:tail-envelope}, the unit-cell bound in
Lemma~\ref{mom:high-moment}, and Minkowski's inequality give a
conditional $L^{q_\gamma}(\mathbb P)$ bound $\varepsilon_T(r,g)$ for
$A_\gamma^g-A_{\gamma,T}^g$. For every fixed $r,g$ this bound tends
to zero as $T\to\infty$, uniformly for sufficiently small $\gamma$:
it is bounded by a constant times the sum of the cell suprema of
the envelope outside $[-T,T]$. Fernique's bound
\eqref{ldp:fernique} and conditional Markov therefore give
\[
 \mathbb P(A_\gamma^g-A_{\gamma,T}^g>\eta)
 \le \left(\frac{\varepsilon_T(r,g)}{\eta}\right)^{q_\gamma}
        +C e^{-\epsilon r^2/\gamma^2}.
\]
Here $C,\epsilon>0$ are independent of $T,r,\gamma$. For nonnegative
$p_\gamma,q_\gamma'$, the inequality
$p_\gamma+q_\gamma'\le2\max(p_\gamma,q_\gamma')$ shows that
the logarithmic rate of their sum is at most the maximum of their
logarithmic upper rates. Consequently
\[
 \limsup_{\gamma\downarrow0}\gamma^2\log
 \mathbb P(A_\gamma^g-A_{\gamma,T}^g>\eta)
 \le\max\left\{
 \frac{W+2}{2}\log\frac{\varepsilon_T(r,g)}{\eta},
 -\epsilon r^2\right\}.
\]
First let $T\to\infty$ with $r$ fixed, then let $r\to\infty$.
This proves
\begin{equation}\label{ldp:tail-superexponential}
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\limsup_{\gamma\downarrow0}\gamma^2\log
 \mathbb P(A_\gamma^g-A_{\gamma,T}^g>\eta)=-\infty.
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 4: the correction at fixed $T,N$.}
The diagonal covariance is bounded for fixed $N$ and is nonnegative.
Thus $c_{\gamma,N}\to1$ in $L^1(0,\pi)$, by dominated convergence
with majorant $\max\{1,(2\sin\theta)^{-1/2}\}$ for $\gamma^2\le1$.
For a compact $K\subset\mathcal E$, let
$M_K=\sup_{(u,v)\in K,z\in D_T}|P_Nv(z)|<\infty$.
Since $d_\gamma,d_0\le0$ and
$|d_\gamma(u+g;t)-d_0(u+g;t)|\le\gamma^2|t|/2$, we have
\[
 \sup_K|f_{\gamma,T,N}^g-f_{T,N}^g|
 \le e^{M_K}\left(2T\|c_{\gamma,N}-1\|_{L^1(0,\pi)}
                       +\pi\gamma^2T^2/2\right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Given $L>0$, Gaussian exponential tightness supplies such a compact
$K$ with complement of logarithmic upper rate at most $-L$.
For each fixed $\eta>0$, the correction exceeds $\eta$ only outside
$K$ once $\gamma$ is sufficiently small. Since $L$ is arbitrary,
\begin{equation}\label{ldp:finite-correction}
 \limsup_{\gamma\downarrow0}\gamma^2\log
 \mathbb P(|f_{\gamma,T,N}^g(X_\gamma)-f_{T,N}^g(X_\gamma)|>\eta)
 =-\infty.
\end{equation}
Both cutoffs remain fixed when taking this limit.

\emph{Step 5: deterministic approximation on energy sublevels.}
Let $K_L=\{I_G\le L\}$, which is compact by
Lemma~\ref{ldp:gaussian}. On $K_L$,
$|u(t)+g(t)|\le(2\sqrt L+\|g'\|_2)\sqrt{|t|}$, so
\[
 d_0(u+g;t)\le -W|t|+(2\sqrt L+\|g'\|_2)\sqrt{|t|}.
\]
The unit-cell exponential bound of Lemma~\ref{var:cell-exponential}
then gives a finite, uniformly summable envelope for the deterministic
area, hence uniformly vanishing horizontal tails. It also gives, on
each fixed $D_T$, uniform bounds on $\int e^{2v}$ and
$\int e^{2P_Nv}$, because projection does not increase the energy.
Moreover,
\[
 \|(1-P_N)v\|_{L^2(\mathcal S)}
 \le(N+1)^{-1}\|v_\theta\|_{L^2(\mathcal S)}.
\]
Using $e^{d_0}\le1$, $|e^x-e^y|\le|x-y|(e^x+e^y)$ and
Cauchy--Schwarz, we obtain
\[
 \sup_{K_L}\left|
 \int_{D_T}e^{d_0(u+g)+v}-f_{T,N}^g(u,v)\right|
 \le\frac{C_{L,T}}{N+1}\longrightarrow0.
\]
These two estimates prove finiteness of $A_0^g$ and permit uniform
approximation on $K_L$, first by choosing $T$, then $N$.
Positivity follows because the integrand is positive almost everywhere.

\emph{Step 6: one sequence for every error threshold.}
We specify the diagonal construction to avoid exchanging limits.
For each integer $j\ge1$ put $\delta_j=1/(3j)$. Choose $T_j\ge j$
so large that the logarithmic upper rate of
$\mathbb P(A_\gamma^g-A_{\gamma,T_j}^g>\delta_j)$ is at most $-4j$
and the deterministic horizontal tail is at most $1/(2j)$ on $K_j$.
Steps 3 and 5 ensure that both requirements hold for all sufficiently
large $T_j$. With this $T_j$ fixed, choose $N_j\ge j$ so large that
the logarithmic upper rate of
$\mathbb P(|A_{\gamma,T_j}^g-A_{\gamma,T_j,N_j}^g|>\delta_j)$
is at most $-4j$ and the deterministic Fourier error on $D_{T_j}$
is at most $1/(2j)$ on $K_j$. Steps 2 and 5 give these choices.
There is no subsequent enlargement of $T_j$.

Set $G_j=G_{T_j,N_j}^g$. For this fixed pair of cutoffs, Step 4
assigns rate $-\infty$ to the third error at threshold $\delta_j$.
The union bound in \eqref{ldp:three-errors} therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{ldp:diagonal-error}
 \limsup_{\gamma\downarrow0}\gamma^2\log
 \mathbb P\left(|A_\gamma^g-f_{T_j,N_j}^g(X_\gamma)|>1/j\right)
 \le-4j.
\end{equation}
The factor three in the union bound disappears because
$\gamma^2\log3\to0$. Equip the product space with the sum of a
compatible metric on $\mathcal E$ and $1\wedge|a-a'|$ on the mass
coordinate. The first two coordinates of $Y_\gamma^g$ and
$G_j(X_\gamma)$ agree exactly. Given any fixed $\eta>0$, for all
large $j$ the event that their distance exceeds $\eta$ is contained
in the event in \eqref{ldp:diagonal-error}. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{ldp:exponentially-good}
 \lim_{j\to\infty}\limsup_{\gamma\downarrow0}\gamma^2\log
 \mathbb P\bigl(d(Y_\gamma^g,G_j(X_\gamma))>\eta\bigr)=-\infty.
\end{equation}
In particular, the sequence of cutoffs does not depend on $\eta$.
Each $j$ is fixed before $\gamma\downarrow0$; no estimate uniform in
all $j$ at a common positive $\gamma$ is required.

Our simultaneous deterministic choices also ensure
\[
 \sup_{K_j}|f_{T_j,N_j}^g-A_0^g|\le1/j.
\]
For every fixed $L$, this proves uniform convergence on $K_L$ as
$j\to\infty$. Since the approximating maps are continuous, $A_0^g$
is continuous on $K_L$.

\emph{Step 7: identification of the joint rate.}
The extended contraction principle for exponentially good continuous
approximations \cite[Theorem 4.2.23]{DZ} applies to the Gaussian LDP,
\eqref{ldp:exponentially-good}, and the uniform convergence on every
energy sublevel. Its rate is
\[
 \inf\{I_G(x): I_G(x)<\infty,\ (x,A_0^g(x))=(u,v,a)\}.
\]
The retained Gaussian coordinates force $x=(u,v)$, so this is
precisely \eqref{ldp:unshifted-joint-cost}. The limiting map need
only be specified on finite-energy inputs; if a map on all of
$\mathcal E$ is required, extend its mass coordinate by zero elsewhere.
This is measurable since the finite-energy set is a countable union
of compact sublevels on each of which $A_0^g$ is continuous.
The rate sublevel of height $L$ is exactly the continuous image
$\{(x,A_0^g(x)):x\in K_L\}$, and is therefore compact.

For completeness, exponential tightness of the mass also follows
directly. On $\|\gamma B\|_*\le r$, Proposition~\ref{mom:whole-strip}
bounds its conditional $L^{q_\gamma}(\mathbb P)$ norm by
$C_W(1+r+\|g\|_*)^4$. Thus
\[
 \mathbb P(A_\gamma^g>M)
 \le\left(\frac{C_W(1+r+\|g\|_*)^4}{M}\right)^{q_\gamma}
       +C e^{-\epsilon r^2/\gamma^2}.
\]
For any desired exponential cost choose $r$, then $M$, sufficiently
large. Combining this with Gaussian exponential tightness gives
exponential tightness of $Y_\gamma^g$.

On the set $q=m$, their derivatives agree almost everywhere, since
the derivative of an absolutely continuous function vanishes almost
everywhere on each of its level sets, applied to $q-m$. Thus whenever
$m_\sigma'>0$, outside a null set,
\[
 m_\sigma'(t)=-W\tanh(Wt/2)
                  +\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm d t}u(\sigma t)>0.
\]
Thus $0\le m_\sigma'\le|\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm d t}u(\sigma t)|$, which proves
square integrability of the reflected derivative.  On and off the
record set the same algebra gives
\[
 \left|\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm d t}f(\sigma t)\right|^2
 -\left|\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm d t}u(\sigma t)\right|^2
 =-4W\tanh(Wt/2)m_\sigma'(t).
\]
The difference of the source terms on this half-line is
$-\frac{W^2}{2}\int_0^\infty m_\sigma(t)\operatorname{sech}^2(Wt/2)\,\mathrm d t$.
Adding it to one quarter of the preceding identity and integrating
gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm d t}
       \{-W\tanh(Wt/2)m_\sigma(t)\}\,\mathrm d t
 =-Wm_\sigma(\infty).
\]
All terms are integrable: $m_\sigma$ is bounded and eventually constant,
and $\operatorname{sech}^2(Wt/2)$ decays exponentially.  Summing proves
\eqref{var:reflection-formula}.  The derivative identity and
$0\le\tanh(Wt/2)\le1$ prove \eqref{var:energy-comparison}.

\label{ldp:weighted-upper}
For every closed $\mathcal C\subset\mathcal B\times E_s\times[0,\infty)$,
\begin{align}

<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
By exponential tightness in Lemma~\ref{ldp:joint}, for each $T>0$
there is a compact set outside which the triple has logarithmic
probability at most $-T$. Holder's inequality with exponents
$q,q/(q-1)$ and Lemma~\ref{ldp:weight-moment} bound the weighted
contribution of that complement by logarithmic rate
\[
 \frac1q\limsup_{\gamma\to0}\gamma^2\log
 \mathbb E\left[\left(\frac W{4\pi}Z_\gamma(b_\gamma(\gamma B))\right)^ 
 {q(W/\gamma^2-1/2)}
 e^{-\frac{qW^2}{4\gamma^2}\int_\mathbb R\gamma B(t)\operatorname{sech}^2(Wt/2)\,dt}
 \right]-\left(1-\frac1q\right)T.
\]
This tends to $-\infty$ as $T\to\infty$.

On a fixed such compact set, the radial norms are bounded.
For $0<a\le\epsilon<1$ and sufficiently small $\gamma$,
\begin{align*}
 &(W-\gamma^2/2)\log\frac{Wa}{4\pi}
 -\frac{W^2}{4}\int_\mathbb R u(t)\operatorname{sech}^2(Wt/2)\,dt\\
 &\hspace{30mm}\le\frac W2\log\epsilon+O(1),
\end{align*}
where the bound is uniform on that compact set. This removes
$a$ close to zero by letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$.
On the remaining compact set $a\ge\epsilon$, the logarithm of the
weight multiplied by $\gamma^2$ converges uniformly to
\[
 W\log\frac{Wa}{4\pi}
 -\frac{W^2}{4}\int_\mathbb R u(t)\operatorname{sech}^2(Wt/2)\,dt.
\]
Cover the compact set by finitely many neighborhoods on which this
continuous expression oscillates by at most any prescribed positive
amount. Bound the weight on each neighborhood by its supremum and
apply the LDP upper bound to the closed intersections with the
compact set and $\mathcal C$. The logarithmic upper bound for this
finite sum is the largest of the corresponding bounds. Letting the
oscillation tend to zero, then $\epsilon\downarrow0$, and finally
$T\to\infty$ proves \eqref{ldp:weighted-upper-eq}.
For the lower bound, fix a finite-rate point $(u,v,a)$ in the open

logarithmic weight differs from its value at $(u,v,a)$ by less than

uniformly on this interval. Restrict the expectation to $V$ and
bound the weight below by the exponential of its value at the
chosen point minus $2\eta$, divided by $\gamma^2$.
The probability LDP lower bound for $V$ is at least the negative
Gaussian energy of $(u,v)$, because $V$ contains that point.
Let $\eta\downarrow0$ and take the supremum over finite-rate points
in $\mathcal C$. This proves the stated open-set bound.

\label{ldp:area-limit}
The limiting measure to be identified is
\begin{equation}\label{ldp:classical-area}
 \frac W{4\pi}\operatorname{sech}^2(Wt/2)\,dt\,d\theta.
\end{equation}

For finitely many $f\in C_c(\mathcal S)$, adjoin to the triple
\eqref{ldp:triple} the coordinates
\[
 \int_{\mathcal S}f(z)e^{b_\gamma(\gamma B;t)}
 (2\sin\theta)^{-\gamma^2/2}:e^{\gamma X^\perp(z)}:\,d^2z.
\]
The proof of Lemma~\ref{ldp:joint} applies to these coordinates:
the cutoff estimates apply to the positive and negative parts of
$f/\|f\|_\infty$; the finite maps are continuous; and uniform
integrability on bounded energy sets gives deterministic limit
$\int f(z)e^{b_0(u;t)+v(z)}\,d^2z$. The additional coordinates are
bounded in absolute value by $\|f\|_\infty$ times the total mass,
so exponential tightness is retained. The weighted estimates and
uniqueness argument therefore give joint convergence of these
coordinates to $\int f(z)\operatorname{sech}^2(Wt/2)\,d^2z$, while the denominator
in \eqref{ldp:area-representation} tends to $4\pi/W$.

To prevent escape of mass, choose $0\le f\le1$ in $C_c(\mathcal S)$
whose integral against \eqref{ldp:classical-area} exceeds
$1-\epsilon$. The measures in \eqref{ldp:area-representation} have
total mass one; their mass outside $\operatorname{supp}f$ is at
most one minus their integral of $f$, and is therefore less than
$2\epsilon$ with probability tending to one. This establishes
tightness away from both angular boundaries and both horizontal
ends. Here is the corresponding weak-convergence conclusion in explicit
test-function form. Let $g$ be any bounded continuous function on the
open strip, and let $f$ be the compactly supported cutoff just chosen.
The integral of $gf$ converges because $gf\in C_c(\mathcal S)$.
For each of the random and limiting probability measures, the absolute
integral of $g(1-f)$ is at most $\|g\|_\infty$ times its integral
of $1-f$. The latter is at most $2\epsilon$ with probability tending
to one for the random measure and at most $\epsilon$ for the limit.
Sending $\epsilon\downarrow0$ proves convergence of the integral
of $g$ in probability.

To express convergence in the weak topology itself, choose a sequence
$(f_j)_{j\ge1}$ of compactly supported continuous functions dense in
the unit ball of $C_0(\mathcal S)$ for the uniform norm, where $C_0$
denotes continuous functions vanishing at infinity. For probability
measures $\mu,\nu$, use the metric
\[
 d(\mu,\nu)=\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}
       \left(1\wedge\left|\int f_jd\mu-\int f_jd\nu\right|\right).
\]
Density shows that convergence in this metric is convergence against
all $C_0$ tests. The cutoff argument in the preceding paragraph,
using total mass one, then upgrades it to weak convergence.
A finite initial sum in this metric tends to zero in probability
by the joint finite-test convergence; the remaining sum is at most
$\sum_{j>J}2^{-j}=2^{-J}$. This proves convergence in probability
in the weak topology. The same test
argument applies to restrictions of continuous functions on
$[-\infty,+\infty]\times[0,\pi]$, and therefore proves weak
convergence on the compactification as well. The limiting density integrates to one and assigns
zero mass to all added boundary points.

 =\int_\mathbb R|\overline\varphi'-\phi_W'|^2dt
 \le\frac1\pi\int_{\mathcal S}
                  |\partial_t(\varphi-\phi_W)|^2dz<\infty.

$\int|\nabla v|^2\le\int|\nabla(\varphi-\phi_W)|^2$.

finite-energy preimage.

Put $h=\varphi-\phi_W$.  Its angular mean obeys
$|\overline h(t)|\le|\overline h(0)|+
\sqrt{|t|/\pi}\,\|\nabla h\|_2$.

These bounds show $h\in L^2(e^{\phi_W}\,\mathrm d z)$, so
Lemma~\ref{var:onofri} proves finiteness and positivity of
$Z_0(\varphi)$ as well.

Expanding the square and integrating by parts in the angular mean
gives
\begin{align*}
 &\frac1{4\pi}\int_{[-T,T]\times(0,\pi)}
                    (|\nabla\varphi|^2-|\nabla\phi_W|^2)\,\mathrm d z\\
 &\quad=\frac1{4\pi}\int_{[-T,T]\times(0,\pi)}|\nabla h|^2\,\mathrm d z
 -\frac W2\tanh(WT/2)\bigl(\overline h(T)+\overline h(-T)\bigr)\\
 &\qquad+\frac{W^2}{4}\int_{-T}^{T}\overline h(t)\operatorname{sech}^2(Wt/2)\,\mathrm d t.
\end{align*}
After adding the difference of the boundary terms in
\eqref{act:counterterms}, the remaining boundary contribution is
$\frac W2(1-\tanh(WT/2))(\overline h(T)+\overline h(-T))$, which
tends to zero by the square-root growth bound.  The other terms
converge to
$\frac1{4\pi}\int|\nabla h|^2+W\int h e^{\phi_W}$.

 \frac1{4\pi}\int_{[-T,T]\times(0,\pi)}|\nabla\phi_W|^2\,\mathrm d z

 \phi_W(T)=-WT+\log(W/\pi)+o(1).
\]
Therefore $\mathscr S_W(\phi_W)=W(\log(W/\pi)-1)$, and all stated
identities follow.  This computation requires no convergence of $h$
