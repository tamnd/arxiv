<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:PTR}
A submanifold $N$ is called \emph{partially totally real} if there is a totally real distribution $\mathscr H^{\perp}$ such that
\[
TN=\mathscr H^{\perp}\oplus\widetilde{\mathscr H},
\qquad
\mathscr H^{\perp}\perp\widetilde{\mathscr H},
\qquad
P(\widetilde{\mathscr H})\subseteq\widetilde{\mathscr H}.
\]
The orthogonal complementary distribution $\widetilde{\mathscr H}$ is called the ambiguous distribution.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A PTR-submanifold is called \emph{proper} if the ambiguous distribution
$\widetilde{\mathscr H}$ is neither totally real nor complex, slant, or
pointwise slant.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:canonicalPTR}
The bare PTR condition is deliberately broad.  On an open set where
\(\operatorname{rank}P\) is constant and \(\ker P\) has positive rank,
skew-adjointness of \(P\) gives
\[
TN=\ker P\oplus(\ker P)^\perp
=\ker P\oplus\operatorname{Im}P.
\]
If \(X\in\ker P\), then the tangential component of \(JX\) vanishes, hence
\(JX\) is normal; thus \(\ker P\) is totally real.  Moreover,
\[
P(\operatorname{Im}P)\subseteq\operatorname{Im}P.
\]
Consequently, on every constant-rank region for which \(\ker P\) has
positive rank, the maximal choice
\(\mathscr H^\perp=\ker P\) and
\(\widetilde{\mathscr H}=\operatorname{Im}P\) automatically satisfies the
PTR conditions of Definition~\ref{def:PTR}.  Accordingly, the geometric
content considered here concerns the restrictions imposed by the gradient
Ricci soliton equation on this maximal splitting and on the nonzero spectrum
of \(P^2\); in the proper PTR case, this includes the nontrivial spectral
structure of the ambiguous distribution.

This maximal choice is an additional standing convention on the constant-rank
region under consideration; it is not part of the PTR definition itself.  To
remove the nonuniqueness coming from smaller totally real subdistributions,
throughout the spectral discussion we use
\[
\mathscr H^\perp=\ker P,
\qquad
\widetilde{\mathscr H}=\operatorname{Im}P=(\ker P)^\perp.
\]
Hence
\[
q=\dim\widetilde{\mathscr H}=\operatorname{rank}P
\]
is intrinsic to the immersion on each constant-rank component.  Since the
rank of a real skew-adjoint endomorphism is even, this maximal ambiguous
dimension \(q\) is automatically even.  The geometric content studied below
is therefore not the mere existence of a PTR splitting, but the restrictions
imposed by the gradient soliton equation on the kernel and on the nonzero
spectrum of \(P^2\).
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
If $P=0$ on all of $TN$, then $N$ is totally real. If $\widetilde{\mathscr H}$ is $J$-invariant, then $P^2=-I$ on $\widetilde{\mathscr H}$, and the structure reduces to the CR case. If
\[
P^2=-\rho^2I\quad\text{on }\widetilde{\mathscr H}
\]
for a pointwise function $0<\rho<1$, then the ambiguous distribution has one pointwise slant level; in the terminology already contained in the PTR framework, this is the pointwise hemi-slant special situation. Pointwise slant immersions in complex space forms have an independent existence and uniqueness theory; see, for example, \cite{AlghanemiEtAl2021}.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:P2basic}
On $TN$, the operator $P^2$ is self-adjoint and nonpositive. Moreover, every eigenvalue of $-P^2$ lies in $[0,1]$. Equivalently, if
\[
P^2X=-\rho^2X,
\qquad X\ne0,
\]
then $0\le \rho^2\le1$.  The rank of \(P\) is even; more precisely, every
positive eigenvalue of \(-P^2\) has even multiplicity.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We verify the assertions one at a time.

From
\[
g(PX,Y)=-g(X,PY)
\]
for all tangent vectors \(X,Y\), the adjoint of \(P\) satisfies
\[
P^*=-P.
\]
Hence
\[
(P^2)^*=(P^*)^2=(-P)^2=P^2,
\]
so \(P^2\) is self-adjoint.

Next, put \(Y=PX\) in the skew-adjointness relation:
\[
g(PX,PX)=-g(X,P(PX))=-g(X,P^2X).
\]
Therefore
\[
g(P^2X,X)=-\|PX\|^2\le0,
\]
which proves that \(P^2\) is nonpositive.

Now suppose
\[
P^2X=-\rho^2X,\qquad X\ne0.
\]
Taking the inner product with \(X\),
\[
g(P^2X,X)=-\rho^2\|X\|^2.
\]
Using the preceding identity,
\[
-\|PX\|^2=-\rho^2\|X\|^2,
\]
and hence
\[
\|PX\|^2=\rho^2\|X\|^2.
\]
Since
\[
JX=PX+FX
\]
is the orthogonal decomposition of \(JX\) into tangent and normal parts,
\[
\|JX\|^2=\|PX\|^2+\|FX\|^2\ge \|PX\|^2.
\]
Because \(J\) is an isometry,
\[
\|JX\|=\|X\|.
\]
Thus
\[
\rho^2\|X\|^2=\|PX\|^2\le\|X\|^2.
\]
Dividing by \(\|X\|^2>0\) gives
\[
0\le\rho^2\le1.
\]

It remains to prove the parity statement.  Let \(E_{\rho}\) be the eigenspace
of \(-P^2\) corresponding to \(\rho^2>0\).  Since \(P\) commutes with \(P^2\),
the space \(E_{\rho}\) is \(P\)-invariant.  On \(E_{\rho}\), define
\[
J_{\rho}=\frac1{\rho}P.
\]
Then
\[
J_{\rho}^2=\frac1{\rho^2}P^2=-I.
\]
Thus \(E_{\rho}\) is a complex vector space with respect to \(J_{\rho}\), so
\(\dim_{\mathbb R}E_{\rho}\) is even. Summing over the nonzero spectral
blocks shows that \(\operatorname{rank}P\) is even.
<!-- end proof-1 -->

At points where the multiplicities of the eigenvalues of $P^2|_{\widetilde{\mathscr H}}$ are locally constant, the ambiguous distribution admits the corresponding orthogonal local spectral decomposition. We use this only pointwise below; no integrability assumption on the eigendistributions is required.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
If $N$ is totally geodesic, then $h=0$ and $\mathcal Q_h=0$. If $N$ is minimal, then $H=0$ and
\[
g(\mathcal Q_hX,X)
=-\sum_i\|h(X,e_i)\|^2\le0.
\]
Thus in the minimal case $\mathcal Q_h$ is nonpositive in the quadratic-form sense.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:flatPTR}
Work in $\mathbb C^4\cong\mathbb R^8$ with an orthonormal real frame
\[
\{e_1,Je_1,e_2,Je_2,e_3,Je_3,e_4,Je_4\}.
\]
Fix $0<\theta<\pi/2$, and define
\[
u=e_2,
\qquad
v=\cos\theta\,Je_2+\sin\theta\,e_3.
\]
Let
\[
\widetilde{\mathscr H}
=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,Je_1,u,v\},
\qquad
\mathscr H^\perp=\operatorname{span}\{e_4\},
\]
choose $x_0\in\mathbb C^4$, and let
$N=x_0+(\widetilde{\mathscr H}\oplus\mathscr H^\perp)$ be the affine
linear subspace with this direction space.
Then $J\mathscr H^\perp$ is normal. A direct projection gives
\[
Pu=\cos\theta\,v,
\qquad
Pv=-\cos\theta\,u.
\]
Hence
\[
P^2=-I
\quad\text{on }\operatorname{span}\{e_1,Je_1\},
\qquad
P^2=-\cos^2\theta\,I
\quad\text{on }\operatorname{span}\{u,v\}.
\]
Thus the ambiguous distribution has two distinct nonzero tangency levels; in particular this is a genuine multi-level proper PTR model.

Because $N$ is affine linear, $h=0$ and $\operatorname{Ric}=0$. Fix
$\lambda\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$ and define
\[
f(x)=\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x-x_0\|^2,\qquad x\in N,
\]
where $\|\cdot\|$ is the Euclidean norm induced on the affine subspace.
Then $\nabla f=\lambda(x-x_0)$ under the canonical identification with the
direction space and $\operatorname{Hess}f=\lambda g$. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Ric}+\operatorname{Hess}f=\lambda g.
\]
This gives a proper PTR gradient Ricci soliton with nonconstant potential in the flat case. Notice that the example also explains why the nonflat assumption will matter later: when $c=0$, the soliton equation does not directly detect the different $P^2$-levels.

\label{ex:SegrePTR}
Consider the standard Segre \(CR\)-product
\[
\Phi:
\mathbb{CP}^{1}(4)\times\mathbb{RP}^{5}(1)
\longrightarrow
\mathbb{CP}^{11}(4),
\]
obtained by composing
\[
\mathbb{CP}^{1}(4)\times\mathbb{RP}^{5}(1)
\hookrightarrow
\mathbb{CP}^{1}(4)\times\mathbb{CP}^{5}(4)
\]
with the Segre embedding \(S_{1,5}\). Standard \(CR\)-products of this form
were studied by Chen \cite{Chen1981b}.

Let
\[
\mathscr D=T\mathbb{CP}^{1},
\qquad
\mathscr D^\perp=T\mathbb{RP}^{5}.
\]
Then \(\mathscr D\) is \(J\)-invariant and \(\mathscr D^\perp\) is totally
real.  With the canonical maximal decomposition of
Remark~\ref{rem:canonicalPTR},
\[
\widetilde{\mathscr H}=\mathscr D,
\qquad
\mathscr H^\perp=\mathscr D^\perp,
\]
and hence
\[
P^2=-I\quad\text{on }\mathscr D,
\qquad
P=0\quad\text{on }\mathscr D^\perp.
\]
Since \(\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathscr D=2\),
\[
n=7,\qquad \|P\|^2=2.
\]

The induced metric is the product metric.  The factors satisfy
\[
\operatorname{Ric}_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}(4)}=4g,
\qquad
\operatorname{Ric}_{\mathbb{RP}^{5}(1)}=4g,
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{Ric}_N=4g.
\]
Taking \(f\) constant gives
\[
\operatorname{Hess}f=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Ric}+\operatorname{Hess}f=4g.
\]
Thus this is a nonflat, non-totally-geodesic PTR gradient Ricci soliton (in the
$CR$ special case, hence not a proper PTR example) with
\(\lambda=4\), but the soliton is \emph{trivial} in the standard sense because
\(f\) is constant.  It is therefore a benchmark for the nonflat identities,
not an example of a nontrivial nonflat soliton.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:Ricci}
For tangent vector fields $X,Y$ on a PTR-submanifold of $\widetilde M(c)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ricci}
\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)
=
\frac c4\bigl\{(n-1)g(X,Y)-3g(P^2X,Y)\bigr\}
+g(\mathcal Q_hX,Y).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let \(\{e_1,\ldots,e_n\}\) be a local orthonormal tangent frame. With our convention,
\[
\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(R(e_i,X)Y,e_i).
\]
Apply the Gauss equation to each summand:
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)
={}&
\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(\widetilde R(e_i,X)Y,e_i)\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(h(e_i,e_i),h(X,Y))\\
&-\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(h(e_i,Y),h(X,e_i)).
\end{align*}
Since
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(e_i,e_i)=nH
\]
and \(h\) is symmetric, the last two lines equal
\[
n\,g(H,h(X,Y))
-\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(h(X,e_i),h(Y,e_i))
=
g(\mathcal Q_hX,Y).
\]

It remains to contract the ambient curvature. Substitute
\[
U=e_i,\qquad V=X,\qquad W=Y
\]
into \eqref{eq:ambientcurv}:
\begin{align*}
\widetilde R(e_i,X)Y
=\frac c4\{&
g(X,Y)e_i-g(e_i,Y)X
+g(JX,Y)Je_i\\
&-g(Je_i,Y)JX
+2g(e_i,JX)JY\}.
\end{align*}
Taking the inner product with \(e_i\) and summing, the first term gives
\[
n\,g(X,Y),
\]
while the second gives
\[
-\sum_i g(e_i,Y)g(X,e_i)=-g(X,Y).
\]
Thus these two terms contribute
\[
(n-1)g(X,Y).
\]
The third term vanishes because
\[
g(Je_i,e_i)=0.
\]

For the fourth term,
\begin{align*}
-\sum_i g(Je_i,Y)g(JX,e_i)
&=\sum_i g(e_i,JY)g(JX,e_i)\\
&=\sum_i g(e_i,PY)g(PX,e_i)\\
&=g(PX,PY).
\end{align*}
For the fifth term,
\begin{align*}
2\sum_i g(e_i,JX)g(JY,e_i)
&=2\sum_i g(e_i,PX)g(PY,e_i)\\
&=2g(PX,PY).
\end{align*}
Hence the Kähler contribution is
\[
3g(PX,PY).
\]
Using \(P^*=-P\),
\[
g(PX,PY)
=-g(X,P^2Y)
=-g(P^2X,Y),
\]
because \(P^2\) is self-adjoint. Therefore
\[
\sum_i g(\widetilde R(e_i,X)Y,e_i)
=
\frac c4\bigl\{(n-1)g(X,Y)-3g(P^2X,Y)\bigr\}.
\]
Adding the extrinsic term \(g(\mathcal Q_hX,Y)\) proves \eqref{eq:Ricci}.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:scalar}
Let $N^n$ be a PTR-submanifold of a complex space form $\widetilde M(c)$ carrying a gradient Ricci soliton. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalarPTR}
\Delta f+n^2\|H\|^2-\|h\|^2
=
n\lambda-\frac{cn(n-1)}4-\frac{3c}{4}\|P\|^2.
\end{equation}
Here $\|P\|^2=\sum_i\|Pe_i\|^2$ and $\|h\|^2=\sum_{i,j}\|h(e_i,e_j)\|^2$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let \(\{e_1,\ldots,e_n\}\) be a local orthonormal tangent frame. By definition,
\[
\operatorname{Scal}_N=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\operatorname{Ric}(e_j,e_j).
\]
Using Lemma~\ref{lem:Ricci},
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Scal}_N
&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}
\left[
\frac c4\{(n-1)g(e_j,e_j)-3g(P^2e_j,e_j)\}
+g(\mathcal Q_he_j,e_j)
\right]\\
&=\frac c4
\left[
n(n-1)-3\sum_{j=1}^{n}g(P^2e_j,e_j)
\right]
+\sum_{j=1}^{n}g(\mathcal Q_he_j,e_j).
\end{align*}
Hence
\[
\operatorname{Scal}_N=\frac c4\{n(n-1)-3\operatorname{tr}P^2\}
+\operatorname{tr}\mathcal Q_h.
\]

We compute the two traces separately. Since \(P\) is skew-adjoint,
\[
g(P^2e_j,e_j)=-g(Pe_j,Pe_j)=-\|Pe_j\|^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{tr}P^2
=\sum_{j=1}^{n}g(P^2e_j,e_j)
=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|Pe_j\|^2
=-\|P\|^2.
\]

From the definition of \(\mathcal Q_h\),
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{tr}\mathcal Q_h
&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}g(\mathcal Q_he_j,e_j)\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[
n\,g(H,h(e_j,e_j))
-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|h(e_j,e_i)\|^2
\right]\\
&=n\,g\left(H,\sum_{j=1}^{n}h(e_j,e_j)\right)
-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|h(e_i,e_j)\|^2.
\end{align*}
Since
\[
H=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{n}h(e_j,e_j),
\]
we have
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{n}h(e_j,e_j)=nH.
\]
Thus
\[
\operatorname{tr}\mathcal Q_h
=n\,g(H,nH)-\|h\|^2
=n^2\|H\|^2-\|h\|^2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{Scal}_N=
\frac{cn(n-1)}4
+\frac{3c}{4}\|P\|^2
+n^2\|H\|^2-\|h\|^2.
\]

Finally, trace the soliton equation
\[
\operatorname{Ric}+\operatorname{Hess}f=\lambda g.
\]
The three traces are
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{Ric})=\operatorname{Scal}_N,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{Hess}f)=\Delta f,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(\lambda g)=n\lambda.
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{Scal}_N+\Delta f=n\lambda.
\]
Substitution of the expression for \(\operatorname{Scal}_N\) gives
\[
\Delta f+n^2\|H\|^2-\|h\|^2
=
n\lambda-\frac{cn(n-1)}4-\frac{3c}{4}\|P\|^2.
\]

\label{ex:scalarflat}
For the affine proper PTR submanifold of Example~\ref{ex:flatPTR}, the ambient curvature and second fundamental form vanish:
\[
c=0,\qquad h=0,\qquad H=0,\qquad \mathcal Q_h=0.

The Gaussian potential is $f(x)=\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x-x_0\|^2$, so
\[

Hence both sides of \eqref{eq:scalarPTR} are exactly $n\lambda$.  This verifies the scalar identity on an actual nonconstant gradient Ricci soliton and also shows why the PTR tangency energy disappears from the trace formula in the flat case.

<!-- example-4: body_md -->
\label{ex:SegreScalar}
Continue Example~\ref{ex:SegrePTR}.  For the standard Segre $CR$-product, the pure factor components of the second fundamental form vanish; see Chen \cite{Chen1981b}. Hence
the pure components of \(h\) vanish and only mixed components remain.  For
orthonormal frames
\[
\{X_1,X_2\}\subset\mathscr D,
\qquad
\{Z_1,\ldots,Z_5\}\subset\mathscr D^\perp,
\]
consider a mixed plane \(\operatorname{span}\{X_i,Z_a\}\). Its sectional
curvature in the product metric is zero, because in a Riemannian product a
$2$-plane spanned by vectors tangent to two different factors has zero mixed
sectional curvature. Since \(JZ_a\) is normal, the ambient
complex-space-form contribution is \(c/4=1\). The Gauss equation gives
\[
0=1-\|h(X_i,Z_a)\|^2,
\]
so
\[
\|h(X_i,Z_a)\|=1.
\]
Thus \(H=0\), and with the ordered-pair Hilbert--Schmidt convention,
\[
\|h\|^2
=
2\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{a=1}^{5}\|h(X_i,Z_a)\|^2
=20.
\]
Since \(c=4\), \(n=7\), \(\lambda=4\), \(f\) is constant, and
\(\|P\|^2=2\), the two sides of \eqref{eq:scalarPTR} are
\[
-20
\]
and
\[
7\cdot4-\frac{4\cdot7\cdot6}{4}
-\frac{3\cdot4}{4}\cdot2
=28-42-6=-20,
\]
respectively.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:compact}
If $N$ is compact without boundary, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact}
 n\lambda\operatorname{Vol}(N)
=
\frac{cn(n-1)}4\operatorname{Vol}(N)
+\frac{3c}{4}\int_N\|P\|^2\,dV
+\int_N\bigl(n^2\|H\|^2-\|h\|^2\bigr)\,dV.
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Integrate the scalar identity over \(N\):
\begin{align*}
&\int_N\Delta f\,dV
+n^2\int_N\|H\|^2\,dV
-\int_N\|h\|^2\,dV\\
&\qquad=
n\lambda\operatorname{Vol}(N)
-\frac{cn(n-1)}4\operatorname{Vol}(N)
-\frac{3c}{4}\int_N\|P\|^2\,dV.
\end{align*}
Because \(N\) is compact and has no boundary,
\[
\int_N\Delta f\,dV
=
\int_N\operatorname{div}(\nabla f)\,dV
=0
\]
by the divergence theorem. Moving the remaining terms to the right-hand side gives exactly \eqref{eq:compact}.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:maxbound}
Let $N^n$ be a compact minimal PTR-submanifold of a complex space form
$\widetilde M(c)$, $c>0$, carrying a gradient Ricci soliton. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:properbound}
\boxed{\lambda\le\frac{c(n-1)}4.}
\end{equation}
The bound follows from the following pointwise estimate. Put
\[
a=\lambda-\frac{c(n-1)}4.
\]
If $p$ is a maximum point of $f$ and $X_\alpha\in T_pN$ is a unit eigenvector
of $-P^2$ with eigenvalue $\rho_\alpha^2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maxpoint}
a\le \frac{3c}{4}\rho_\alpha^2
-\sum_{i=1}^n\|h(X_\alpha,e_i)\|^2
\le\frac{3c}{4}\rho_\alpha^2.
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:maxpoint} gives the directionwise refinement, including the
second-fundamental-form contribution, while \eqref{eq:properbound} is the
global PTR bound under the convention adopted after Definition~\ref{def:PTR}.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
At a maximum point $p$ of $f$, $\operatorname{Hess}f\le0$.  From
Lemma~\ref{lem:Ricci} and the soliton equation,
\[
S_f+\mathcal Q_h
=
\left(\lambda-\frac{c(n-1)}4\right)I+\frac{3c}{4}P^2.
\]
Since $H=0$,
\[
g(\mathcal Q_hX,X)=-\sum_i\|h(X,e_i)\|^2.
\]
For $P^2X_\alpha=-\rho_\alpha^2X_\alpha$,
\[
\operatorname{Hess}f(X_\alpha,X_\alpha)
=
a-\frac{3c}{4}\rho_\alpha^2
+\sum_i\|h(X_\alpha,e_i)\|^2\le0,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:maxpoint}.

By the PTR convention used in this paper, $\mathscr H^\perp$ has positive
rank.  Since it is totally real, $\mathscr H^\perp_p\subset\ker P_p$ at every
point.  Choose a unit vector $X\in\mathscr H^\perp_p$.  Then $PX=0$, so
\eqref{eq:maxpoint} with $\rho^2=0$ gives
\[
\lambda-\frac{c(n-1)}4
\le -\sum_i\|h(X,e_i)\|^2\le0.
\]
This is exactly \eqref{eq:properbound}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:constantPhi}
Let $N^n$ be a compact connected PTR-submanifold of $\widetilde M(c)$ carrying
a gradient Ricci soliton. Define
\[
\Phi=\frac{3c}{4}\|P\|^2+n^2\|H\|^2-\|h\|^2.
\]
If $\Phi$ is constant, then $f$ is constant and $N$ is Einstein:
\[
\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda g,\qquad
\lambda=\frac{c(n-1)}4+\frac{\Phi}{n}.
\]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The scalar-curvature calculation in Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar} gives
\[
\operatorname{Scal}_N=\frac{cn(n-1)}4+\Phi,
\]
so $\operatorname{Scal}_N$ is constant. Tracing the soliton equation yields
\[
\Delta f=n\lambda-\operatorname{Scal}_N,
\]
which is constant because $\lambda\in\mathbb R$. Compactness gives
\[
0=\int_N\Delta f\,dV=(n\lambda-\operatorname{Scal}_N)\operatorname{Vol}(N),
\]
hence $\Delta f=0$. Since $N$ is compact and connected, $f$ is constant.
The soliton equation reduces to $\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda g$, and
$n\lambda=\operatorname{Scal}_N$ gives the stated formula.

$\|h\|^2$ are all constant. Thus many high-symmetry compact candidates can
support only trivial gradient solitons. It does not exclude noncompact
examples or compact examples for which $\Phi$ varies.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:reconstruction}
Let $N^n$ be a PTR-submanifold of a complex space form $\widetilde M(c)$ carrying a gradient Ricci soliton. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator}
\boxed{
S_f+\mathcal Q_h
=
\left(\lambda-\frac{c(n-1)}4\right)I
+\frac{3c}{4}P^2.
}
\end{equation}
If $c\ne0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reconstruct}
P^2
=
\frac4{3c}
\left[
S_f+\mathcal Q_h
-\left(\lambda-\frac{c(n-1)}4\right)I
\right].
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Lemma~\ref{lem:Ricci} gives, for all tangent vectors \(X,Y\),
\[
\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)
=
\frac{c(n-1)}4g(X,Y)
-\frac{3c}{4}g(P^2X,Y)
+g(\mathcal Q_hX,Y).
\]
Therefore the Ricci endomorphism is
\[
\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}
=
\frac{c(n-1)}4I-\frac{3c}{4}P^2+\mathcal Q_h.
\]
The soliton equation is equivalent to
\[
\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}+S_f=\lambda I.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
\frac{c(n-1)}4I-\frac{3c}{4}P^2+\mathcal Q_h+S_f
=
\lambda I.
\]
Move the scalar term and the \(P^2\)-term to the opposite sides:
\[
S_f+\mathcal Q_h
=
\left(\lambda-\frac{c(n-1)}4\right)I
+\frac{3c}{4}P^2.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:operator}.

If \(c\ne0\), subtract the scalar multiple of the identity:
\[
S_f+\mathcal Q_h
-\left(\lambda-\frac{c(n-1)}4\right)I
=
\frac{3c}{4}P^2.
\]
Multiplication by \(4/(3c)\) yields \eqref{eq:reconstruct}.

\label{cor:interval}
Assume $q=\dim\widetilde{\mathscr H}>0$ and use the maximal constant-rank
choice of Remark~\ref{rem:canonicalPTR}. If $c>0$, every eigenvalue $\sigma$
of $(S_f+\mathcal Q_h)|_{\widetilde{\mathscr H}}$ satisfies

\lambda-\frac{c(n+2)}4
\le \sigma<

\end{equation}

$PX=0$, whose eigenspace in the full tangent bundle is
$\mathscr H^\perp=\ker P$. For $c<0$, the order reverses:
\[
\lambda-\frac{c(n-1)}4

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->

vector in $\widetilde{\mathscr H}$ cannot lie in $\ker P$. Lemma~\ref{lem:P2basic}

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:anisotropy}
Assume $c\ne0$ and $q=\dim\widetilde{\mathscr H}>0$. On the ambiguous distribution,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tracefree}
\left[(S_f+\mathcal Q_h)|_{\widetilde{\mathscr H}}\right]^{\circ}
=
\frac{3c}{4}
\left[P^2|_{\widetilde{\mathscr H}}\right]^{\circ}.
\end{equation}
Let the eigenvalues of $P^2|_{\widetilde{\mathscr H}}$ be
$-\rho_1^2,\dots,-\rho_q^2$, repeated according to multiplicity, and put
\[
\overline{\rho^2}
=\frac1q\sum_{\alpha=1}^{q}\rho_\alpha^2.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anisotropy}
\boxed{
\left\|
\left[(S_f+\mathcal Q_h)|_{\widetilde{\mathscr H}}\right]^{\circ}
\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=
\frac{9c^2}{16}
\sum_{\alpha=1}^{q}
\left(\rho_\alpha^2-\overline{\rho^2}\right)^2.
}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Set
\[
A=(S_f+\mathcal Q_h)|_{\widetilde{\mathscr H}},
\qquad
B=P^2|_{\widetilde{\mathscr H}},
\qquad
a=\lambda-\frac{c(n-1)}4.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:operator} becomes
\[
A=aI+\frac{3c}{4}B.
\]
Taking the trace on the \(q\)-dimensional space \(\widetilde{\mathscr H}\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}A
=
aq+\frac{3c}{4}\operatorname{tr}B.
\]
Therefore
\begin{align*}
A^\circ
&=
A-\frac{\operatorname{tr}A}{q}I\\
&=
aI+\frac{3c}{4}B
-\left(
a+\frac{3c}{4q}\operatorname{tr}B
\right)I\\
&=
\frac{3c}{4}
\left(
B-\frac{\operatorname{tr}B}{q}I
\right)\\
&=
\frac{3c}{4}B^\circ.
\end{align*}
This proves \eqref{eq:tracefree}.

Now choose an orthonormal eigenbasis
\[
BE_\alpha=-\rho_\alpha^2E_\alpha.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{tr}B
=
-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{q}\rho_\alpha^2
=
-q\,\overline{\rho^2}.
\]
Hence
\begin{align*}
B^\circ E_\alpha
&=
\left(
B-\frac{\operatorname{tr}B}{q}I
\right)E_\alpha\\
&=
-\rho_\alpha^2E_\alpha
+\overline{\rho^2}\,E_\alpha\\
&=
-\left(\rho_\alpha^2-\overline{\rho^2}\right)E_\alpha.
\end{align*}
Since \(B^\circ\) is self-adjoint,
\[
\|B^\circ\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=
\sum_{\alpha=1}^{q}
\left(\rho_\alpha^2-\overline{\rho^2}\right)^2.
\]
Finally,
\[
A^\circ=\frac{3c}{4}B^\circ
\]
implies
\[
\|A^\circ\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=
\frac{9c^2}{16}\|B^\circ\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:anisotropy}.

then $\nabla f=0$ and hence $f$ is constant and the soliton is Einstein,
without any compactness assumption. If instead $N$ is compact and connected,
then the same conclusion follows without assuming zero curvature nullity. In
particular, on a compact connected
$N$, the stronger geometric assumptions
\[
\nabla P^2=0,
\qquad
\nabla h=0
\]
also force the soliton to be trivial.
<!-- end corollary-7 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
The curvature-nullity assertion follows directly from
Proposition~\ref{prop:differential}. If the curvature-nullity distribution is
zero at every point, then the inclusion $\nabla f$ in that distribution forces
$\nabla f=0$, so $f$ is constant and the soliton equation reduces to
$\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda g$; no compactness is needed for this conclusion.
For the compact alternative, contract $R(X,Y)\nabla f=0$ in the first and
fourth slots with an orthonormal frame
$\{e_i\}$:
\[
\operatorname{Ric}(Y,\nabla f)
=\sum_i g(R(e_i,Y)\nabla f,e_i)=0.
\]
For a gradient Ricci soliton satisfying
$\operatorname{Ric}+\operatorname{Hess}f=\lambda g$, the contracted Bianchi
identity gives the standard scalar-curvature identity
\[
\frac12\,\nabla\operatorname{Scal}_N
=\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla f,\cdot).
\]
Hence $\operatorname{Scal}_N$ is constant. Taking the trace of the soliton
equation,
\[
\operatorname{Scal}_N+\Delta f=n\lambda,
\]
shows that $\Delta f$ is constant as well. If $N$ is compact, integration
gives
\[
0=\int_N\Delta f\,dV
=(\Delta f)\operatorname{Vol}(N),
\]
so $\Delta f=0$. Compactness and connectedness then imply that the harmonic
function $f$ is constant. The soliton equation reduces to
$\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda g$.

Finally, $\nabla h=0$ implies that $H$ and all contractions entering
$\mathcal Q_h$ are parallel, hence $\nabla\mathcal Q_h=0$. This proves the
last assertion.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:realhyp}
Let \(m\ge2\), and let \(M^{2m-1}\) be a compact real hypersurface of a
nonflat complex space form.  Denote its shape operator by \(A\).  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shapecombination}
(\operatorname{tr}A)^2-\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
\end{equation}
is constant, then \(M\) admits no gradient Ricci soliton.  In particular,
compact real hypersurfaces with constant principal curvatures, including the
standard homogeneous families, admit no gradient Ricci soliton.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
For a real hypersurface with unit normal \(\nu\), put \(\xi=-J\nu\) and define \(\eta(X)=g(X,\xi)\). Then
\[
P^2=-I+\eta\otimes\xi,
\qquad
\|P\|^2=2m-2.
\]
If \(n=2m-1\), then
\[
H=\frac{\operatorname{tr}A}{n}\nu,
\qquad
\|h\|^2=\operatorname{tr}(A^2).
\]
Hence the quantity in Theorem~\ref{thm:constantPhi} is
\[
\Phi
=
\frac{3c}{4}(2m-2)
+
(\operatorname{tr}A)^2
-
\operatorname{tr}(A^2).
\]
Under the hypothesis, \(\Phi\) is constant.  If a gradient Ricci soliton
existed, Theorem~\ref{thm:constantPhi} would force \(f\) to be constant and
\(M\) to be Einstein.  However, real hypersurfaces of nonflat complex space
forms are not Einstein for \(m\ge2\); see
Cecil--Ryan \cite[Theorem~8.69]{CecilRyan2015}.  This obstruction is also
used in the Ricci-soliton setting by Cho--Kimura \cite{ChoKimura2009}.
For the classical projective and hyperbolic results, see also Cecil--Ryan
\cite{CecilRyan1982}, Montiel \cite{Montiel1985}, and Kon \cite{Kon1979}.  This contradiction proves
the nonexistence.

<!-- corollary-8: body_md -->
\label{cor:realhypvariation}
If a compact real hypersurface \(M^{2m-1}\), \(m\ge2\), of a nonflat complex
space form carries a gradient Ricci soliton, then
\[
(\operatorname{tr}A)^2-\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
\]
must be nonconstant.
