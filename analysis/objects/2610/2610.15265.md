<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:enumeration}
With probability $1 - o(1)$ over $\mathcal{L} \sim \mu_n$, every quasi-HKZ basis $\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{HKZ}}$ of $\mathcal{L}$ has enumeration tree size:
\begin{align}\label{eq:main-enum}
T(\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{HKZ}}) = n^{c_{\mathrm{HKZ}} \cdot n + o(n)}, \qquad c_{\mathrm{HKZ}} = \frac{1}{2e} = 0.18393\dots.
\end{align}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:two-way}
With probability $1 - o(1)$, $\mathcal{L} \sim \mu_n$ has a primitive sublattice $\mathcal{M}$ of rank $m = \lfloor(n-1) / 2\rfloor$ with $\det\mathcal{M} \leq e^{O(n)} n^{n/8}$. Combining a reversed dual-HKZ basis of $\mathcal{M}$ with lifts of an HKZ basis of its orthogonal quotient gives a basis $\mathbf{B}_2$ of $\mathcal{L}$ with enumeration tree size:
\begin{align}\label{eq:two-way-main}
T(\mathbf{B}_2) = n^{c_2 \cdot n + o(n)}, \qquad c_2 = \frac{1}{4\sqrt e} = 0.15163\dots.
\end{align}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:eight-blocks}
With probability $1 - o(1)$, $\mathcal{L} \sim \mu_n$ contains a sequence of dense sublattices $\mathcal{M}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{M}_1 \subseteq \dots \subseteq \mathcal{L}$ such that, combining (lifted) HKZ and reversed dual-HKZ bases of (orthogonal quotients of) these blocks, we obtain a basis $\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{rec}}$ of $\mathcal{L}$ with enumeration tree size:
\begin{align}\label{eq:eight-block-main}
T(\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{rec}}) \leq n^{c_{\mathrm{rec}} \cdot n + o(n)}, \qquad c_{\mathrm{rec}} < 0.14740.
\end{align}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- heuristicassumption-1: body_md -->
\label{heur:assumption}
Consider nested chains of primitive sublattices, with prescribed ranks and determinant bounds. Suppose that, for some $\delta > 0$, the expected number of such chains in a Haar random lattice is at least $n^{\delta n^2 + o(n^2)}$, and the same bound holds for every non-empty subchain obtained by omitting intermediate sublattices. Then a Haar-random lattice contains such a complete chain with probability $1 - o(1)$.
<!-- end heuristicassumption-1 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:heuristic}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{heur:assumption} holds, and let $\varepsilon > 0$ be fixed. Then with probability $1 - o(1)$, $\mathcal{L} \sim \mu_n$ contains a basis $\mathbf{B}$ satisfying:
\begin{align}\label{eq:heuristic}
T(\mathbf{B}) \leq n^{(c_{\mathrm{flat}} + \varepsilon) \cdot n + o(n)}, \qquad c_{\mathrm{flat}} = \frac{2-\sqrt2}{4} = 0.14644\dots.
\end{align}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:any-basis}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:unfold}
Let $d\geq2$ and let $f(s,\mathcal Q)$ be nonnegative, measurable and rotation-invariant in $\mathcal Q$. Then
\begin{align}\label{eq:unfold-measure}
 \mathbb E_{\mu_d}\!\left[\sum_{[\boldsymbol v]\in\mathcal L_{\rm prim}/\{\pm1\}}f(\|\boldsymbol v\|,\mathcal Q_{\boldsymbol v})\right]
 =d\kappa_d\int_0^\infty s^{d-1}\mathbb E_{\mu_{d-1}}[f(s,\mathcal Q)]\,\mathrm d s.
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal X_1=\{\mathbb Z\}$ and $\mu_1$ is its point mass.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We apply the primitive component ($k=1$) of Str\"ombergsson's averaging formula~\cite[Proposition~5.1 and (5.1)--(5.4)]{strom}. For a specified primitive vector of length $s$, the normalized quotient carries Haar measure $\mu_{d-1}$, while the choices of lifts have total measure one. Counting $\boldsymbol v$ and $-\boldsymbol v$ separately gives primitive-vector intensity $\zeta(d)^{-1}\,\mathrm d\boldsymbol v$. Passing to polar coordinates and dividing by two to count primitive pairs gives the factor $d\kappa_d$.
\qed

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:block-average}
For $\mathcal M\in\operatorname{Gr}(\mathcal L,r)$, let $D=\det\mathcal M$ and $\mathcal Q=\pi_{\mathcal M^\perp}(\mathcal L)$, so that $\det\mathcal Q=D^{-1}$. For every nonnegative measurable function $F$ that is rotation-invariant in both lattice arguments:
\begin{align}\label{eq:marked-disintegration}
 \mathbb E_{\mu_n}\!\left[\sum_{\mathcal M\in\operatorname{Gr}(\mathcal L,r)}F(D,D^{-1/r}\mathcal M,D^{1/(n-r)}\mathcal Q)\right] &=na(n,r)\int_0^\infty D^{n-1}\mathbb E_{\substack{\mathcal L_1\sim\mu_r\\\mathcal L_2\sim\mu_{n-r}}}[F(D,\mathcal L_1,\mathcal L_2)]\,\mathrm d D,
\end{align}
where $\mathcal L_1$ and $\mathcal L_2$ on the right are independent.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Kim's decomposition of Haar measure~\cite[Lemma~10 and Sections~4--5]{kimflags} separates the determinant, the two normalized lattices, rotations, and choices of lifts. The determinant density is proportional to $D^{n-1}\,\mathrm d D$, and the two lattice factors carry Haar probability measure. Integrating out the rotations and lifts leaves a constant; taking $F=\mathbf 1_{D\leq H}$ and using \eqref{eq:sublattice-mean} identifies it as $na(n,r)$.
\qed

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:tree-prefix}
With probability $1-e^{-\Omega(n)}$ over $\mathcal L\sim\mu_n$, simultaneously for all bases at radius $R=\lambda_1(\mathcal L)$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:tree-prefix}
 \ln T(\mathbf B)=\max_{0\leq k\leq n}P_k(\mathbf B)+O(n),\qquad
 \ln N_{n-k}(\mathbf B,R)=P_k(\mathbf B)+O(n)\quad(k<n).
\end{align}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We first prove the lower bound, with $d=n-k$. Averaging over a fundamental domain of $\mathcal Q_k$ shows that some translate of $\mathbb B_d(R/2)$ contains at least $V_d(R/2)^d/\det\mathcal Q_k$ lattice points. Subtracting one of these points from all of them gives distinct points in $\mathcal Q_k\cap\mathbb B_d(R)$, because distances within a ball of radius $R/2$ are at most $R$.

For the upper bound, every point with $\|\boldsymbol v\|\leq R$ contributes at least one to $e^{\pi R^2}\rho(\mathcal Q_k)$. Equivalently, the Gaussian function $e^{\pi(R^2-\|\boldsymbol v\|^2)}$ bounds the ball's indicator from above. Applying Poisson summation and then $\mathcal Q_k^*\subseteq\mathcal L^*$ gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:width-sandwich}
 e^{P_k}V_d(R/2)^d\leq N_d(\mathbf B,R)
 &\leq e^{\pi R^2}\rho(\mathcal Q_k)
 =e^{\pi R^2+P_k}\rho(\mathcal Q_k^*) \leq e^{\pi R^2+P_k}\rho(\mathcal L^*).
\end{align}
By \eqref{eq:short-tail} and Stirling's formula, with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(n)}$ we have $R\geq a\sqrt n$ for an absolute $a>0$. Markov's inequality and \eqref{eq:theta-input} also give $\rho(\mathcal L^*)\leq e^n$ with probability at least $1-2e^{-n}$. Both bounds therefore hold with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(n)}$. Using $R\leq\sqrt n$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:geometry} and the cube inside the unit ball,
\begin{align}
 V_d(R/2)^d\geq a^d(n/d)^{d/2}\geq e^{-O(n)},\qquad
 e^{\pi R^2}\rho(\mathcal L^*)\leq e^{(\pi+1)n}.
\end{align}
Taking logarithms gives the claimed estimate for each level of the tree. As there are only $n+1$ levels, the logarithm of the total tree size differs from that of the widest level by at most $\ln(n+1)$. This proves the proposition.
\qed

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:hkz-bounds}
For every HKZ basis $\mathbf B_{\mathrm H}$ of a unimodular rank-$d$ lattice, uniformly for $0\leq t\leq1$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:hkz-bounds}
 \frac{P_{\lfloor td\rfloor}(\mathbf B_{\mathrm H})}{d\ln d}\leq p_{\mathrm H}(t)+o(1),\qquad\qquad
 p_{\mathrm H}(t)=-\frac{1-t}{2}\ln(1-t).
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Minkowski's bound gives $h_j\leq\frac{1}{2}\ln j$. For $r=d-k\geq1$, \eqref{eq:prefix} implies
\begin{align}
 P_{d-r}\leq\frac{r}{2}\ln d\sum_{j=r+1}^d\frac{1}{j-1}

\end{align}
After dividing by $d\ln d$, the rounding error is uniform in $k$. At the remaining endpoint $k=d$, the claim follows from $P_d=0$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:error}
For $\mathcal{L}\sim\mu_n$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:exp-moment}
 \mathbb E_{\mu_n}[e^{Z_n(\mathcal L)/2}]=e^{O(n^2)}.
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We prove the claim by induction on the dimension, writing $M_d=\mathbb E_{\mu_d}[e^{Z_d/2}]$ and starting from $M_1=1$. For a fixed unimodular rank-$d$ lattice, let $\mathcal S(\mathcal L)$ be its finite nonempty set of shortest-vector pairs. Once a first vector is chosen, all later HKZ choices are choices in its projected quotient. Multiplying every lattice vector by the same factor changes its length and $(\det\mathcal L)^{1/j}$ by that factor, so $h_j$ and $e_j$ stay unchanged. We may therefore rescale the quotient to determinant one without changing the remaining deficits:
\begin{align}
 Z_d(\mathcal L)=\max_{[\boldsymbol v]\in\mathcal S(\mathcal L)}
 \left(d\ln\frac{2r_d}{\|\boldsymbol v\|}+Z_{d-1}(\mathcal Q_{\boldsymbol v})\right).
\end{align}
Every shortest vector is primitive and has length at most $2r_d$. After exponentiating, we can bound the maximum by the sum over all shortest-vector pairs, and then enlarge this sum to all primitive pairs in the ball of radius $2r_d$. All summands are nonnegative, so sums and integrals may be interchanged without first proving integrability (Tonelli's theorem~\cite[Theorem~2.37]{folland}). Proposition~\ref{prop:unfold} then gives:
\begin{align}\label{eq:moment-recursion}
 M_d\leq d\kappa_d M_{d-1}\int_0^{2r_d}s^{d-1}\left(\frac{2r_d}{s}\right)^{d/2}\,\mathrm d s
 =\frac{2^d}{\zeta(d)}M_{d-1}\leq2^dM_{d-1}.
\end{align}
Here $V_dr_d^d=1$. The factor $1/2$ in the moment ensures convergence at $s=0$; removing it would give the divergent integral $\int_0^{2r_d}s^{-1}\,\mathrm d s$. Iteration gives $\ln M_n\leq(\ln2)\sum_{d=2}^n d=O(n^2)$. Because we average over all primitive vectors, the argument does not require the quotients selected by HKZ to be Haar-random or independent.
\qed

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:profile}
With probability $1-e^{-\Omega(n)}$ over $\mathcal L\sim\mu_n$, simultaneously for all quasi-HKZ bases,
\begin{align}\label{eq:haar-prefix-profile}
 \max_{0\leq k\leq n}\left|\frac{P_k(\mathbf B)}{n\ln n}-p_{\mathrm H}(k/n)\right|=o(1).
\end{align}
With the same probability, for every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:bulk-error}
 \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i:\,x_i\leq1-\varepsilon}
 \left|\frac{y_i}{\ln n}-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(1+\ln(1-x_i)\bigr)\right|=o(1).
\end{align}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $m=n-1$, and let $W_n$ be the maximum of $Z_m(\mathcal Q_{\boldsymbol v})$ over primitive pairs with $\|\boldsymbol v\|\leq4r_n$. Proposition~\ref{prop:unfold} and Lemma~\ref{lem:error} give
\begin{align}\label{eq:tail-moment}
 \mathbb E_{\mu_n}[e^{W_n/2}]\leq\kappa_n(4r_n)^n\mathbb E_{\mu_m}[e^{Z_m/2}]=e^{O(n^2)}.
\end{align}
Hence $W_n=O(n^2)$ with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(n^2)}$. Together with \eqref{eq:short-tail} and Proposition~\ref{prop:tree-prefix}, this gives an event of probability $1-e^{-\Omega(n)}$ on which all three bounds hold. In particular, every quasi-HKZ basis then has $|\ln a_1|=O(\ln n)$.

For its normalized HKZ tail $\mathbf B'$, put $g_j=\ln(2r_j)$ for $j\geq2$, with $g_1=e_1=0$. The prefix identity gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:reconstruct}
 y'_{m-d+1}=h_d-\sum_{j=d+1}^m\frac{h_j}{j-1},\qquad
 R_{m,d}=g_d-\sum_{j=d+1}^m\frac{g_j}{j-1}.
\end{align}
Using $h_j=g_j-e_j$ and the triangle inequality,
\begin{align}\label{eq:weighted-error}
 \sum_{d=1}^m d|y'_{m-d+1}-R_{m,d}|
 \leq\sum_{j=2}^m\left(j+\sum_{d<j}\frac{d}{j-1}\right)e_j
 \leq\frac{3}{2}W_n=O(n^2).
\end{align}
For fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and $d\geq\varepsilon m$, Stirling's formula and harmonic sums give $R_{m,d}=\frac{1}{2}\ln m\,(1+\ln(d/m))+O(1)$, where the constant may depend on $\varepsilon$. The weighted bound above therefore gives an average coordinate error $O(1)$ in this range. Rescaling by $y_i=y'_{i-1}-\ln a_1/m$ for $i\geq2$ and dividing by $\ln n$ proves \eqref{eq:bulk-error}.

Summing the coordinate errors and using $\frac{1}{2}\int_0^x(1+\ln(1-t))\,\mathrm d t=p_{\mathrm H}(x)$ proves \eqref{eq:haar-prefix-profile} for $k/n\leq1-\varepsilon$.

For the remaining prefixes, Lemma~\ref{lem:hkz-bounds} applied to the tail gives $P_k/(n\ln n)\leq p_{\mathrm H}(k/n)+o(1)$, while Proposition~\ref{prop:tree-prefix} and $N_{n-k}\geq1$ give $P_k\geq-O(n)$. Since $P_n=0$ and $p_{\mathrm H}(x)\to0$ as $x\to1$, letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the uniform claim.
\qed

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
With probability $1-e^{-\Omega(n)}$, Propositions~\ref{prop:tree-prefix} and~\ref{prop:profile} both hold, giving uniformly over all quasi-HKZ bases:
\begin{align}\label{eq:quasi-critical}
 \frac{\ln T(\mathbf B)}{n\ln n}
 =\max_{0\leq x\leq1}p_{\mathrm H}(x)+o(1)
 =\frac{1}{2e}+o(1).
\end{align}
The maximum occurs at $x=1-1/e$.
\qed

\label{lem:marked-dense}
Fix $0<\alpha<1$. Take $m=\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor$ for $\alpha\ne1/2$, and $m=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ for $\alpha=1/2$. There is a threshold

\end{align}
with the following property. Prescribe any rotation-invariant measurable properties that hold with Haar probability $1-o(1)$ in dimensions $m$ and $n-m$. <!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:split-retention}
The reduced count $Z$ satisfies $\mathbb E[Z]=(1-e^{-\Omega(n)})\mu$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
For a candidate of determinant $D$, rescaling to determinant one changes the cutoff to $U_rD^{-r/m}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:block-average}, the mean number of violations at rank $r$ in this normalized lattice is $a(m,r)U_r^mD^{-r}$. Summing over the ranks and integrating over $D$, we obtain:
\begin{align}\label{eq:retention-bound}
 \frac{\mathbb E[N_H-Z]}{\mu}
 &\leq\frac{na(n,m)}{\mu}\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}a(m,r)U_r^m\int_0^H D^{n-r-1}\,\mathrm d D =\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}\frac{n}{n-r}a(m,r)U_r^mH^{-r}.
\end{align}
To estimate these terms uniformly in $r$, we write $b_j=\ln(V_j/\zeta(j))$, with $\zeta(1)=1$. Stirling's formula gives $b_j=-j\ln n/2+O(n)$ uniformly for $1\leq j\leq n$, since $j\ln(n/j)=O(n)$. The product for $a(m,r)$ has $r$ numerator and $r$ denominator terms, so
\begin{align}\label{eq:retention-coefficient}
 \ln a(m,r)=-\frac{r(m-r)}{2}\ln n+O(rn).
\end{align}
Consequently, the logarithm of the $r$th summand in \eqref{eq:retention-bound} is
\begin{align}\label{eq:retention-decay}
 -\frac{r(m-r)(n-m)}{2n}\ln n-r\ln\frac{H}{n^{m(n-m)/(2n)}}+O(rn).
\end{align}
Choosing the constant in $H$ sufficiently large makes this at most $-\kappa nr$ for some $\kappa>0$. Summing over $r$ proves the claim.
\qed

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:split-pairs}
For the same choice of $H$, $\mu\geq e^{\Omega(n^2)}$ and $\mathbb E[Z^2]\leq(1+e^{-\Omega(n)})\mu^2$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
We distinguish pairs according to the rank of their intersection, and write $s=n-2m\geq1$. Pairs with zero intersection have mean $\mu^2$ before removing candidates, by Kim's pair formula in Section~\ref{sec:mean-inputs}. The diagonal contributes at most $\mu$. It remains to count distinct candidates intersecting in a rank-$r$ lattice $\mathcal C$, where $1\leq r<m$.

The intersection is primitive and has $D:=\det\mathcal C\geq U_r$. Projecting away $\mathcal C$ gives two primitive rank-$(m-r)$ sublattices with zero intersection in a rank-$(n-r)$ quotient of determinant $D^{-1}$. Each projected sublattice determines its original candidate as its full inverse image, so there is no additional multiplicity. Normalizing the quotient changes the two determinant ceilings to $HD^{-(n-m)/(n-r)}$.

Kim's pair formula applies because $2(m-r)\leq n-r-1$. Integrating its value against the intersection density $na(n,r)D^{n-1}\,\mathrm d D$ bounds the number of these pairs by
\begin{align}\label{eq:overlap-integral}
 &na(n,r)a(n-r,m-r)^2H^{2(n-r)}\int_{U_r}^{\infty}D^{2m-n-1}\,\mathrm d D =\frac{n}{s}a(n,r)a(n-r,m-r)^2H^{2(n-r)}U_r^{-s}.
\end{align}
The cutoff removes the divergence at zero, while $s\geq1$ ensures convergence at infinity. This is why, for $\alpha=1/2$, we chose $m=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ rather than $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.

Combining the diagonal, the pairs with zero intersection, and the remaining intersecting pairs gives:
\begin{align}\label{eq:variance}
 \frac{\mathbb E[Z^2]}{\mu^2}\leq1+\frac{1}{\mu}
 +\frac{n}{s}\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}\frac{a(n,r)a(n-r,m-r)^2}{a(n,m)^2}H^{-2r}U_r^{-s}.
\end{align}
Using the same $b_j$ as above, cancellation in the products for $a(n,r)$ gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:overlap-coefficient}
 \ln\frac{a(n,r)a(n-r,m-r)^2}{a(n,m)^2}
 &=2\sum_{j=m-r+1}^m b_j-\sum_{j=n-r+1}^n b_j-\sum_{j=1}^r b_j+O(r\ln n)\\
 &=\frac{r(s+r)}{2}\ln n+O(rn).
\end{align}
The logarithm of the $r$th contribution to \eqref{eq:variance} is therefore
\begin{align}\label{eq:overlapdecay}
 -\frac{r(m-r)(n-m)}{n}\ln n-2r\ln\frac{H}{n^{m(n-m)/(2n)}}+O(rn).
\end{align}
The error term also absorbs the factor $n/s\leq n$. As in Lemma~\ref{lem:split-retention}, increasing the constant in $H$ makes the sum $e^{-\Omega(n)}$. Finally, $\ln a(n,m)=-m(n-m)\ln n/2+O(n^2)$, so the same choice makes $\ln\mu=\Omega(n^2)$.
\qed

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:heuristic-moments}
For fixed $K\geq3$, the ranks and bounds in \eqref{eq:heuristic-boundaries} satisfy \eqref{eq:heuristic-moment-condition} with $\delta=(1-2a)^2/(8(K-2))$.

Consider any nonempty set of boundaries $t_1<\cdots<t_s$. Kim's chain formula~\cite[Theorem~5]{kimflags} and Stirling's formula give $\mathbb E[C_{J,n}]=n^{(\Gamma_J+o(1))n^2}$. Using $c=a/2$ and $a-a^2/2=1/4$, the exponent becomes a sum of squares:
\begin{align}\label{eq:heuristic-square-slack}
 \Gamma_J&=c(1+t_s-t_1)-\frac{1-t_1^2-(1-t_s)^2-\sum_{\nu=1}^{s-1}(t_{\nu+1}-t_\nu)^2}{4}\\
 &=\frac{(t_1-a)^2+(1-a-t_s)^2+\sum_{\nu=1}^{s-1}(t_{\nu+1}-t_\nu)^2}{4}.
\end{align}
The distances in the last line are nonnegative integer multiples of $w$ whose sum is $(K-2)w$. Their squared sum is at least $(K-2)w^2$, since $j^2\geq j$ for every nonnegative integer $j$. Thus $\Gamma_J\geq(1-2a)^2/(4(K-2))=2\delta$. There are finitely many subchains for fixed $K$, so the same bound applies to all of them for sufficiently large $n$.
\qed

The normalized HKZ blocks used in Section~\ref{sec:two-way} have dimensions $d=n/2+O(1)$ and shortest-vector lengths $\rho_1\asymp\sqrt d$, by the shortest-vector properties imposed in the construction. \begin{align}
  -\sum_{i\in I}\ln\rho_i
  \le \frac{k}{2}\ln(d/k)\ln n+O(n).
\end{align}
Here and below the error is uniform in $I$, and $0\ln(d/0)=0$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
  \sum_{i=1}^d\left[\frac14\ln n-\ln\rho_i\right]_+
  &\le \ln n\max_{0\le k\le d}
       \left\{\frac{k}{4}+\frac{k}{2}\ln(d/k)\right\}+O(n) =\left(\frac1{2\sqrt e}+o(1)\right)d\ln n.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:fgl-hkz-subset}
\end{equation}

We use the two-block basis from Section~\ref{sec:two-way}, together with the product and coordinate estimates proved above. With probability $1-o(1)$, this basis exists and the shortest-vector bounds in \eqref{eq:short-tail} hold for both the lattice and its dual. We work on this event, on which $\lambda_1(\mathcal L)\asymp\sqrt n$. By Section~\ref{sec:prefix-nodes}, the fixed radius $R=10\sqrt n$ contains a closest vector to every target. To account for this radius, define:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_R(\mathbf B)=\prod_i\max\left\{1,\frac{R}{\sqrt n\,a_i}\right\}.
 \label{eq:fgl-radius-product}
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathcal P(\mathbf B)\le\mathcal P_R(\mathbf B)\le 10^n\mathcal P(\mathbf B)$, and the maximum coordinate scale remains $n^{o(n)}$.

Fix any target $\boldsymbol t$. If $\boldsymbol t\in\mathcal L$, return $\boldsymbol t$. Otherwise, let $\boldsymbol z^*$ be a closest lattice point and put $\boldsymbol v^*=\boldsymbol t-\boldsymbol z^*$, so $\|\boldsymbol v^*\|\le R$. We sample one implicit array in $\mathcal L$ and another in $\boldsymbol t+\mathcal L$, both using rounded Gram--Schmidt coordinates centered at the origin:
\begin{align}
 \kappa_i(\boldsymbol x)=
 \left\lfloor\frac{\langle \boldsymbol x,\boldsymbol b_i^*\rangle}{a_i^2}\right\rceil.
\end{align}
Here $\lfloor u\rceil=\lfloor u+1/2\rfloor$. Restricted to either coset, $\kappa$ is a bijection with $\mathbb Z^n$. Indeed, for $\boldsymbol x=\boldsymbol t+\mathbf B\boldsymbol z$, each integer $z_i$ is recovered uniquely from $\kappa_i(\boldsymbol x)$ once $z_{i+1},\ldots,z_n$ are known. The same applies with $\boldsymbol t=\boldsymbol0$. We apply the FGL product distribution to these integer coordinates, using its rounded weights $\alpha_i\le\min\{1,na_i^2/R^2\}$. This gives distributions $\nu_0$ on $\mathcal L$ and $\nu_{\boldsymbol t}$ on $\boldsymbol t+\mathcal L$.

Translation by $\boldsymbol v^*$ maps $\mathcal L$ bijectively onto $\boldsymbol t+\mathcal L$. The rounding error in the difference of each coordinate is at most one, and hence, for every $\boldsymbol x\in \mathcal L$,
\begin{align}
 \sum_i\alpha_i
       |\kappa_i(\boldsymbol x+\boldsymbol v^*)-\kappa_i(\boldsymbol x)|^2
 \le \frac{2n}{R^2}\|\boldsymbol v^*\|^2+2n\le4n.
\end{align}
The central-box argument of~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{fgl2026} therefore applies to these two coset bijections without change. It gives maximum point masses at most $2^{O(n)}/\mathcal P_R(\mathbf B)$ and
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\boldsymbol x\in \mathcal L}\nu_0(\boldsymbol x)\nu_{\boldsymbol t}(\boldsymbol x+\boldsymbol v^*)
 \ge\frac{2^{-O(n)}}{\mathcal P_R(\mathbf B)}.
 \label{eq:fgl-affine-overlap}
\end{equation}
Neither sampler requires knowledge of $\boldsymbol z^*$ or $\boldsymbol v^*$. Independent arrays of length $2^{O(n)}\mathcal P_R(\mathbf B)$, generated from short seeds as in FGL, consequently have the same representation and second-moment bounds, up to exponential factors. Those arguments use only the point-mass and overlap bounds and remain valid for the two different marginal distributions.

For rationally encoded bases and targets, a common scaling clears their denominators and places both cosets in $\mathbb Z^n$. We scale the search radius by the same factor, leaving the coordinate scales and all ratios used below unchanged. This permits the exact discrete-grid implementation of FGL for arbitrary encoded targets.

We then apply the FGL random grid at scale $W=O(R\sqrt{\ln n})$. A pair with difference $\boldsymbol v^*$ shares a cell with probability $2^{-O(n)}$. Packing each coset separately bounds the number of distinct points of their union in a cell by
\begin{align}
 2\left(1+\frac{2W}{\lambda_1(\mathcal L)}\right)^n
 =\exp(O(n\ln\ln n)).
\end{align}
This bound is uniform in $\boldsymbol t$, even when the two cosets are very close. The collision lemma~\cite[Lemma~4]{fgl2026} can therefore be applied with the good pairs restricted to cross-array pairs whose difference is $\boldsymbol v^*$. We initialize the retained candidate with $\boldsymbol0$ and ignore pairs from the same array. For every reported cross-array pair $X\in\mathcal L$, $Y\in\boldsymbol t+\mathcal L$, the vector $\boldsymbol t-(Y-X)$ belongs to $\mathcal L$. Throughout the prescribed search budget, we retain the candidate closest to $\boldsymbol t$. With constant probability a pair with difference $\boldsymbol v^*$ is reported, making the retained candidate exact. A constant number of independent runs raises this probability above $2/3$.

As in FGL, the resulting query time is at most $n^{o(n)}\sqrt{\mathcal P_R(\mathbf B)} =n^{(\tau_2+o(1))n}$, with polynomial space. Every geometric bound is independent of the target, so one basis supports all targets, with fresh randomness for each query.

It remains to describe the preprocessing and the size of the retained advice. Preprocessing may enumerate primitive half-rank sublattices, perform the two block reductions, and test the resulting product and coordinate bounds. The estimates above have $e^{O(n)}$ slack: for example, thresholds $\mathcal P(\mathbf B)\le n^{2\tau_2n}e^{n\sqrt{\ln n}}$ and $\max_i\max\{1,a_i^{-1}\}\le e^{n\sqrt{\ln n}}$ therefore suffice for all sufficiently large $n$. Rounding these thresholds upward to powers of two makes the tests exact and changes the query exponent only by $o(1)$.

The product bound also gives polynomial encoding length after size reduction: since $\sum_i y_i=0$, one has $|y_i|\leq\ln\mathcal P(\mathbf B)=O(n\ln n)$ and $\|\boldsymbol b_i\|\leq\sqrt n\,\mathcal P(\mathbf B)$. As in Section~\ref{sec:prefix-nodes}, the corresponding integer basis changes have polynomial bit length relative to the input. One may therefore restrict preprocessing to a finite search over such changes; the bounds above guarantee success on the stated event. Only the chosen basis is retained as advice. Preprocessing has unrestricted running time and is independent of the target.
\qed
