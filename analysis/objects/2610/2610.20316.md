\label{def:S-squareful}
    Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $S$ a set of primes. We say that $n$ is  ${S}$\emph{-squareful} if $p$ divides $n$ implies $p^2$ divides $n$ for all primes $p$ that are not in $S$. We denote the set of $S$-squareful numbers by $\mathbb{Z}_{S\text{-ful}}$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->

\label{thm:theorem_1}

<!-- end theorem-1 -->

\label{thm:constants_equal}

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A \emph{Campana orbifold} is a pair $(X,D)$, where $X$ is a smooth variety over $K$ and $$D=\sum_{\alpha \in \mathscr{A}}\epsilon_\alpha D_\alpha$$ is an effective Weil $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor of $X$ over $K$, where the $D_\alpha$  are prime divisors such that for all $\alpha \in \mathscr{A},$ either $\epsilon_\alpha = 1 $ or $\epsilon_\alpha$ takes the form $1-\frac{1}{m_\alpha}$ for some $m_\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}$ and the support $D_{\text{red}}=\sum_{\alpha \in \mathscr{A}}D_\alpha$ of $D$ has strict normal crossings on $X$.
    A Campana orbifold is called \emph{klt} if $\epsilon_\alpha \neq 1$ for all $\alpha \in \mathscr{A}$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $(X,D)$ be a Campana orbifold.  A \emph{good integral model of} $(X,D)$ \emph{over} $\mathscr{O}_{K,S}$ is defined to be a pair $(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{D})$ where $\mathscr{X}$ is a regular, flat, proper $\mathscr{O}_{K,S}$-scheme which is a model of $X$ and $\mathscr{D}=\sum_{\alpha \in \mathscr{A}}\epsilon_\alpha \mathscr{D}_\alpha,$ where $\mathscr{D}_\alpha$ denotes the Zariski closure of $D_\alpha$ in $\mathscr{X}$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Let $(X,D)$ be a Campana orbifold with  a good integral model $(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{D})$ over $\mathscr{O}_{K,S}$. A point $P\in (X \setminus D_{\text{red}})(K)$ is a \emph{Campana $\mathscr{O}_{K,S}$ point of $(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{D})$ } if for all $v \notin S$ and all $\alpha \in \mathscr{A}$, we have $n_v(\mathscr{D}_\alpha,P)=0$ if $\epsilon_\alpha = 1.$ If $\epsilon_\alpha \neq 1,$ so that $\epsilon_\alpha = 1- \frac{1}{m_\alpha}$ for some $m_\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}$, then either $n_v(\mathscr{D}_\alpha,P)=0$ or $n_v(\mathscr{D}_\alpha,P) \geq m_\alpha$.
    We denote the set of Campana $\mathscr{O}_{K,S}$-points of $(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{D})$ by $(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{D})(\mathscr{O}_{K,S})$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
Let $(X,D)$ be a Campana orbifold, let $S\subset \Omega_K$ be a finite set of places containing all archimedean places, and let $(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{D})$ be a good integral model of $(X,D)$ over $\mathscr{O}_{K,S}$. \prod_{p \vert \mathbf{p}^\mathbf{k}} \left( \frac{ \frac{av\mathbf{p}^\mathbf{k}\mathbf{q}^{\mathbf{l}}}{p^2}}{p} \right)\\
&=\left(\frac{bv\mathbf{p}^k\mathbf{q}^\mathbf{l}}{\frac{a}{\mathbf{p}^\mathbf{k}}} \right) \left(\frac{av\mathbf{p}^k\mathbf{q}^\mathbf{l}}{\frac{b}{\mathbf{q}^\mathbf{l}}} \right)\left(\frac{-ab}{v} \right)\prod_{p \vert \mathbf{q}^\mathbf{l}} \left( \frac{ \frac{bv\mathbf{p}^\mathbf{k}\mathbf{q}^{\mathbf{l}}}{p^2}}{p} \right)
\prod_{p \vert \mathbf{p}^\mathbf{k}} \left( \frac{ \frac{av\mathbf{p}^\mathbf{k}\mathbf{q}^{\mathbf{l}}}{p^2}}{p} \right),
\end{align*}}
where we have in the last line jacobi symbols. 
Together with quadratic reciprocity we get the lemma.

\label{remark:constants_equal}
    That (\ref{eq:C_final}) and (\ref{eq:new_conjecture_c_final}) are the same can be obtained by the following argument.
In both constants write $\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in \{0,1\}^n}\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in \{0,1\}^m}$ as $\sum_{\mathbf{p}^\mathbf{k}\vert a}\sum_{\mathbf{q}^\mathbf{l}\vert b}$. Then 
\begin{align*}
    (\mathbf{p}^\mathbf{k}, \mathbf{q}^\mathbf{l},d,e,r,s)\mapsto \left(dr,es,\frac{\mathbf{p}^\mathbf{k}}{r},\frac{\mathbf{q}^\mathbf{l}}{s},r,s\right)
\end{align*}
is a bijective change of variables. This bijection  transforms the weight $\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathbf{p}^{\mathbf{k}}\mathbf{q}^{\mathbf{l}}}}$ into $(ders)^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ and converts the character occurring in the Euler product. If $\mathbf{p}^{\mathbf{k}}\mathbf{q}^{\mathbf{l}}\equiv 1\pmod 4$, the required form follows directly from quadratic reciprocity. In the case $\mathbf{p}^{\mathbf{k}}\mathbf{q}^{\mathbf{l}}\equiv 3\pmod 4$, one additionally applies the complementary involution \begin{align*}
(\mathbf{p}^{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{q}^{\mathbf{l}},d,e,r,s)\mapsto\left(\frac{a}{\mathbf{p}^\mathbf{k}},\frac{b}{\mathbf{q}^\mathbf{l}},r,s,d,e\right).
\end{align*} Expanding the absolutely convergent Euler product shows that the factor $\left(1+\bigl(\frac{-ab}{\ell}\bigr)\right)$ for every prime $l \nmid 2ab$ precisely compensates for the additional signs arising from quadratic reciprocity. Hence both congruence classes lead to the same desired expression for the complete sum, although the corresponding identity does not in general hold term wise.
