<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{measurecon}
Let $A \subset \mathbb{S}^n$ be a measurable domain satisfying $\operatorname{Vol}(A) \ge \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)$. Then, for any $\epsilon \in (0, \pi/2]$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(A_\epsilon)}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)} \ge 1 - \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}} e^{-\epsilon^2 (n-1)/2}.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(A_\epsilon)}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)} = 1.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
For every equatorial tube $T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}) \subset \mathbb{S}^n$ of radius $\delta \in (0, \pi/2)$,
\begin{equation}\label{ineqfat}
    \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}))}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)} \ge 1 - 2 e^{-\frac{n-2}{2} \delta^2},
\end{equation}
which implies that
\begin{equation}\label{ineqfat2}
    \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}))}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)} = 1.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem1}
We observe at this point that the existence, for every dimension $n \in N$,  of the tube  $T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})$, (which provides a precise meaning to the limit \eqref{ineqfat2}), is given by the following considerations. For each $n \in N$ we are considering the totally geodesic  immersion $\tilde\varphi_n: (\mathbb{S}^{n-1}, \tilde\varphi_{n}^*(g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}})) \rightarrow (\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}) $. Then, its minimal focal distance in $\mathbb{S}^{n}$, $\tilde{\mathbb{C}}_n=\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})$, (see definition \ref{minfoc} in the following section \S.2) do not depends on the dimension $n$ and we have, for all $n \in N$, that
$$\tilde{\mathbb{C}}_n=\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})=\frac{\pi}{2}$$ 

Hence, the radius $\delta$ in equation \eqref{ineqfat2} is bounded above by 
$$\delta \leq \inf_{n \in N}\{\tilde{\mathbb{C}}_n\}=\frac{\pi}{2}$$

\noindent We shall return on these considerations in the statement of our main result.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:main_intro}\

Let $\mathcal{F}=\{\varphi_n: (\Sigma^{n-1}, \varphi_n^*(g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}})) \rightarrow (\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}})\}_{n\in N}$ be a family of  closed and minimal isometric immersions. Then, for every positive and sufficiently small  $\delta \in \mathbb{R}^+$ and every $\epsilon \in (0, \pi/2 - \delta)$, we have that there exists $n_\delta$ such that for every $n \geq n_\delta$,
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{Vol}(T_{\delta+\epsilon}(\Sigma^{n-1})) \ge \left( 1 - \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}} e^{-\epsilon^2 (n-1)/2} \right) \operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n).
\end{equation}
\noindent 
Consequently, for every $r \in (0, \pi/2)$,
\begin{equation}\label{limit}
    \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T_r(\Sigma^{n-1}))}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)} = 1.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{goodef2}
Concentration of measure in tubes around submanifolds occurs as the dimension of these immersions increases toward infinity. Consequently, the existence of a positive radius that allows these tubes to be defined for all dimensions must be guaranteed, rendering the limit set forth in \eqref{limit} well-defined, (see remark \ref{rem1}). This prior consideration is what underlies the expression {\em sufficiently small  $\delta \in \mathbb{R}^+$}, that we have used in the statement of the above theorem, avoiding some technicalities concerning its precise meaning, which is related with the family of immersions $\mathcal{F}$ in a way we shall explain in the next section \S.2, (see definition \ref{goodef3}). We shall state in a precise way Theorem \ref{thm:main_intro} in Theorem \ref{thm_main} in section \S.3.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\

Let us define the focal distance $c\colon ST\Sigma^{\perp}=\{(p,\xi)\in T\Sigma^{\perp}\colon \Vert \xi\Vert=1\}\to \mathbb{R}$  as $$c(p, \xi)=\sup_{t>0}\{t\colon {\rm dist}_{_M}(p, \gamma_{\xi} (t))=t\} $$
\noindent Then we have that $$ \exp^{\perp}\big(\Omega_{\Sigma}\big)= \{ \exp^{\perp}(p,s\xi)=\gamma_{\xi}(s),\,\,\, (p,\xi)\in ST\Sigma^{\perp}\,,\,0\leq s\leq c(p,\xi)\}.  $$
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{minfoc}

The minimal focal distance $\mathbb{C}_M(\Sigma)$ of $\Sigma$ in $M$ is defined as $$\mathbb{C}_M(\Sigma)=\inf\{c(p, \xi), (p, \xi) \in ST\Sigma^{\perp}\}.$$
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\

Let us consider $\varphi: (\Sigma^m,\varphi^*g) \longrightarrow (M^n, g)$ a submanifold of a Riemannian manifold $M$, and a number  $r\leq \mathbb{C}_M(\Sigma)$. The {\em tube of radius} $r$ about $\Sigma$ in $M$ is the set
\begin{equation*}
                
  T_r(\Sigma):=\left\{ x \in M\, :\, \text{dist}_M(x, \Sigma) \leq r \right\}.
              
\end{equation*}

\noindent and {\em the tubular hypersurface} of radius $r$ about $\Sigma$ in $M$ is the set
\begin{equation*}
                
  \partial T_r(\Sigma):=\left\{ x \in M\, :\, \text{dist}_M(x, \Sigma) = r \right\}.
              
\end{equation*}
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\

 If $M$ is complete and $\Sigma$ is closed then $\mathbb{C}_M(\Sigma) >0$. Thus  we have  for all  $0\leq r\leq \mathbb{C}_M(\Sigma)$ that
\begin{equation*}
                
T_r(\Sigma)=\exp^{\perp}\Big(\left\{(p, \xi)\in T\Sigma^\bot, \text{ with }\, \Vert \xi\Vert \leq r\right\}\Big) \subseteq  \exp^{\perp}\left(\Omega_\Sigma\right).
              
\end{equation*}
\noindent so, as $\Sigma$ is closed, the points in the tube of radius $r$ are those whose distance to the submanifold is less than or equal to $r$, that is, those that can be reached by a minimizing geodesic that starts from the submanifold orthogonally.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{goodef3}\

Let us consider $\mathcal{F}:=\{\varphi_n:(\Sigma^{n-1},\varphi_{n}^*g_{\mathbb{S}^n})\rightarrow (\mathbb{S}^n, \varphi^*g_{\mathbb{S}^n}) \}_{n\in N}$ a family of codimension $1$ isometric immersions in the sphere of sectional curvature $1$. The minimal focal distance of $\Sigma^{n-1}$ in $\mathbb{S}^{n}$, $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\Sigma^{n-1})$, (see definition \ref{minfoc}) depends on the immersion $\varphi_n$, (on the metric $\varphi_{n}^*g_{\mathbb{S}^n}$), and, in particular, of the dimension $n$. We shall denote it as $\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\Sigma^{n-1})=\mathbb{C}_n$. \begin{itemize}
\item[i)] The mean exit time function $E_{T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})} $ defined on the tube $T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})$, is bounded from below by the function $F_{\delta,n}\circ s$ as
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})} (x)=E_{T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})} (s(x))&\geq (F_{\delta,n}\circ s)(x)\,\,\,\forall x \in T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
\end{aligned} $$
\noindent where $s(x)=\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(x,\mathbb{S}^{n-1})$ and  $F_{\delta,n}: (0,\delta) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is the function defined as
$$F_{\delta,n}(r)=\frac{(\delta-r)}{\sin(\delta)}\cdot\frac{1-\cos^{n}(r)}{n\cos^{n-1}(r)}$$
\noindent which satisfies  that
$$ \lim_{n\to \infty}F_{\delta,n}(r)=\infty \,\,\,\forall r \in (0,\delta)$$
\noindent Hence
$$\max E_{T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})} \longrightarrow \infty \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$

\item[ii)] The mean exit time function $E_{B_{\pi/2 - \delta}(\mathbb{S}^n)}$ from the ball  $B_{\pi/2-\delta}(p_N) \subseteq \mathbb{S}^{n}$ is uniformly  bounded from above as $$E_{B_{\pi/2 - \delta}(\mathbb{S}^n)}(y)=E_{B_{\pi/2 - \delta}(\mathbb{S}^n)}(t(y))\leq G_{\delta,n}:= \frac{1-\cos(\pi/2-\delta)}{n\cos(\pi/2-\delta)}\cdot$$\label{thmprelim}
\noindent where $t(y)=\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(y,p_N)$, and we have that the uniform bound $G_{\delta,n}$ satisfies
 $$\lim_{n\to \infty}G_{\delta,n}=0\, .$$
 \noindent Hence 
 $$\max E_{B_{\pi/2 - \delta}(\mathbb{S}^n)} \longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$
 \end{itemize}

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:met_comp}
Let $M^n$ be a Riemannian manifold with $\text{Ric}_M \ge n-1$, and let $\Sigma^{n-1} \subset M$ be a closed minimal hypersurface. Then, given $\delta \leq \mathbb{C}_n$, the MET function defined on the tubular neighborhood $T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})$ is bounded below pointwise by the MET solution transplanted from the model equatorial tube in $\mathbb{S}^n$:
\begin{equation}
    E_{T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})}(x) \ge E_{T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}(r_\Sigma(x)) \quad \forall x \in T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1}),
\end{equation}
where $r_\Sigma(x) := \text{dist}_M(x, \Sigma^{n-1})$.

<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:Symmcompact}\

	Let $(M^n, g)$ be a closed Riemannian manifold. Suppose that $D \subseteq M$ is a precompact open connected domain in $M^n$. Then the \emph{relative $\mathbb{S}^n$- symmetrization of \, $D$} is denoted by $D^{*}$ and is defined to be the unique $s(D)$-ball in $\mathbb{S}^n$,  $D^{*}:=B^{\mathbb{S}^n}_{s(D)}(p_N)$, (where we fix $p_N$ the north-pole of $\mathbb{S}^n$), having the same relative volume:
	
	\begin{equation*}
		V_{\operatorname{rel}}^{M}(D)=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(D)}{\operatorname{Vol}(M)}=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}\left(B_{s(D)}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)}=V_{\operatorname{rel}}^{\mathbb{S}^n}(B^{\mathbb{S}^n}_{s(D)})
	\end{equation*}
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{decrea}\

Define $u^\sharp : [0, V_{\operatorname{rel}}(D)] \to [0, \infty)$ by
    \begin{equation}
        u^\sharp(s) := 
        \begin{cases}
            \operatorname{ess\,sup}_D u & \text{if } s = 0, \\
            \inf \{ t \ge 0 : \mu_M(t) \le s \} & \text{if } s > 0.
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
    
     Note that, by its definition, $u^\sharp$ is non-increasing and attains its maximum at $s=0$, 
$$max_{[0, V_{\operatorname{rel}}(D)]} u^\sharp=u^\sharp(0)=\operatorname{ess\,sup}_D u $$
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
Let $u : D \to [0, \infty)$ be a nonnegative measurable function on $D \subseteq M$. Its $\mathbb{S}^n$-Schwarz symmetrization $u^* : D^* \to [0, \infty)$ is defined as the unique radial non-increasing function with respect to $r_{p_N}(x^*) = \text{dist}_{\mathbb{S}^n}(x^*, p_N)$ given by
    \begin{equation}
        u^*(x^*) = u^\sharp \left( \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(B_{r_{p_N}(x^*)}^{\mathbb{S}^n}(p_N))}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)} \right)=u^\sharp(V_{\operatorname{rel}}^{\mathbb{S}^n}(B_{r_{p_N}(x^*)}^{\mathbb{S}^n}(p_N)).
    \end{equation}
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
Equivalently, $u^*$ is the unique radial, non-increasing function on $D^*$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
    V_{\operatorname{rel}}^{\mathbb{S}^n}(\{ u^* \ge t \}) = V_{\operatorname{rel}}^M(D(t)) \quad \forall t \ge 0.
\end{equation}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\

The Schwarz symmetrization of a function satisfies the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $u^*$ is radial and non-increasing with respect to the distance from the target pole $p_N$.
    \item Symmetrization preserves relative $L^1$ integrals:
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(M)} \int_D u \, d\sigma = \frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)} \int_{D^*} u^* \, d\tilde{\sigma}.
    \end{equation}
    \item For any two smooth nonnegative functions $u_1, u_2$ on $D$,
    \begin{equation}
        u_1 \le u_2 \implies u_1^* \le u_2^*.
    \end{equation}
                                                                                                         
                     
                                                                                     
                   
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{maximums}\

Let $(M,g)$ be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension $n$ without boundary ($\partial M = \emptyset$), and let $D \subset M$ be a bounded open domain with smooth boundary $\partial D$. Let $E_D \in C^2(\overline{D})$ be the unique classical solution to the Poisson problem
$$
\begin{cases}
-\Delta_g E_D = 1 & \text{in } D, \\
E_D = 0 & \text{on } \partial D.
\end{cases}
$$
Let $E_D^*$ be the Schwarz symmetrization of $E_D$ on the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^n(1)$, constructed via the relative volume measure. Then,
$$
\max_{x \in \overline{D}} E_D(x) = \max_{x \in \overline{D}^*} E_D^*(x).
$$
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
To start the proof, and as  the data function $f \equiv 1$ is smooth ($C^\infty(D)$) and $\partial D$ is of class $C^\infty$, standard boundary elliptic regularity ensures that $E_D \in C^\infty(D) \cap C^2(\overline{D})$. 

Since $\overline{D}$ is compact and $E_D$ is continuous, $E_D$ attains its maximum on $\overline{D}$. Furthermore, since $-\Delta_g E_D = 1 > 0$ in $D$ and $\left.E_D\right|_{\partial D} = 0$, the Strong Maximum Principle implies that $E_D(x) > 0$ for all $x \in D$ and that $E_D$ achieves its maximum at an interior point $x_0 \in D$. Define
$$
M_0 := \max_{x \in \overline{D}} E_D(x) = E_D(x_0) > 0.
$$

                                                                

                                                                                                                                                                                                           
   
                                                                                                      
   
By definition of the decreasing rearrangement, (see Definition \ref{decrea}), $E_D^\sharp(0)$ represents the essential supremum of $E_D$ on $D$:
$$
E_D^\sharp(0) = \mathrm{ess\,sup}_{x \in D} E_D(x).
$$

To show that $E_D^\sharp(0) = M_0$, fix any $\varepsilon > 0$ and consider the superlevel set:
$$
\Omega_{M_0 - \varepsilon} = \{ x \in D : E_D(x) > M_0 - \varepsilon \}.
$$
Since $E_D$ is continuous at $x_0$ and $E_D(x_0) = M_0$, there exists an open neighborhood $U \subset D$ of $x_0$ such that $U \subseteq \Omega_{M_0 - \varepsilon}$. On a Riemannian manifold, every non-empty open set has strictly positive volume measure, which implies:
$$
\mu_M\left( \Omega_{M_0 - \varepsilon} \right) \ge \mu_M(U) > 0.
$$
Consequently, for any $0<M_0-\epsilon=t < M_0$, the measure $\mu_M(\{ x \in D : E_D(x) > t \})$ is strictly positive. Therefore, by definition of $M_0$, we have that
$\inf \{ t \ge 0 : \mu_M(\{ E_D > t \}) = 0 \} = M_0$, establishing that:
\begin{equation}\label{eqt1}
E_D^\sharp(0) = M_0 = \max_{x \in \overline{D}} E_D(x).
\end{equation}

                                                               

Now, let us fix the North pole $p_N \in \mathbb{S}^n$. Let $v : [0, \pi] \to [0, 1]$ be the relative volume function of geodesic balls in $\mathbb{S}^n$:
$$
v(r) = \frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{S}^n}(B_r(p_0))}{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)} = \frac{\int_0^r \sin^{n-1}(\theta) \, d\theta}{\int_0^\pi \sin^{n-1}(\theta) \, d\theta}.
$$
The function $v(r)$ is continuous, strictly increasing, and satisfies $v(0) = 0$. The symmetrized domain $D^* \subset \mathbb{S}^n$ is the geodesic ball $B_{R^*}(p_N)$ with radius $R^* = v^{-1}\left(\mu_M(D)\right)$. 

The Schwarz symmetrization $E_D^* : \mathbb{S}^n \to [0, \infty)$ is defined by:
$$
E_D^*(x) = E_D^\sharp \left( v(r_{p_{N}}(x)) \right).
$$

Because $E_D^\sharp$ is non-increasing on $[0, \mu_M(D)]$ and $v(r)$ is strictly increasing in $r = r_{p_{N}}$, the composite function $r \mapsto E_D^\sharp(v(r))$ achieves its maximum when $r$ is minimized, which occurs at $r = 0$ (the pole $x = p_N$):
\begin{equation}\label{eqt2}
\max_{x \in \overline{D}^*} E_D^*(x) = \max_{[0,V_{\operatorname{rel}}^m(D)]}(E^\sharp \circ v)=E_D^*(p_N) = (E_D^\sharp\circ v)(0)=E_D^\sharp(v(0)) = E_D^\sharp(0).
\end{equation}

                            

Combining the equalities established in \eqref{eqt1} and \eqref{eqt2}, we obtain
$$
\max_{x \in \overline{D}^*} E_D^*(x) = E_D^\sharp(0) = \max_{x \in \overline{D}} E_D(x).
$$

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:talenti}\

Let $(M^n, g)$ be a closed Riemannian manifold satisfying $\text{Ric}_M \ge n-1$. Consider the Poisson problem on $D \subseteq M$ with $f \in L^2(D)$:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{cases}
        \Delta^M u + f = 0 & \text{in } D, \\
        u|_{\partial D} = 0,
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and its corresponding symmetrized problem on the geodesic ball $D^* \subseteq \mathbb{S}^n$:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{cases}
        \Delta^{\mathbb{S}^n} v + f^* = 0 & \text{in } D^*, \\
        v|_{\partial D^*} = 0.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Then, the rearranged solution $u^*$ satisfies pointwise:
\begin{equation}
    u^* \le v \quad \text{on } D^*.
\end{equation}

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{thm_main}\

Let $\mathcal{F}=\{\varphi_n:\Sigma^{n-1} \rightarrow \mathbb{S}^n\}_{n\in N}$ be a family of closed and minimal isometric immersions, and let us assume that the minimal focal distance associated to the family is
$$\mathbb{C}(\mathcal{F}):=\inf_{n \in N} \{\mathbb{C}_n\} > 0$$

Then, given $\delta \leq \mathbb{C}(\mathcal{F})$, and given $\epsilon \in (0, \pi/2 - \delta)$, there exists $n_\delta \in N$ such that, if $n \geq _\delta$, we have
\begin{equation} \label{eq:main_bound}
    \operatorname{Vol}(T_{\delta+\epsilon}(\Sigma^{n-1})) \ge \left( 1 - \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}} e^{-\epsilon^2 (n-1)/2} \right) \operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n).
\end{equation}
Hence, for this familiy of closed and minimal immersions, we have that
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T_r(\Sigma^{n-1}))}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)} = 1.
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
\

Let us consider the family of closed and minimal isometric immersions $\mathcal{F}=\{\varphi_n:\Sigma^{n-1} \rightarrow \mathbb{S}^n\}_{n\in N}$, and let us assume that the minimal focal distance associated to the family $\mathbb{C}(\mathcal{F}):=inf_{n \in N} \{\mathbb{C}_n\} > 0$.  Let us consider now $\delta \leq \mathbb{C}(\mathcal{F})$.

The proof proceeds in three main steps.

\medskip
                                                              

First, and by inequality  \eqref{ineq1} in Corollary \ref{cor:met_divergence}, since $\Sigma^{n-1} \subset \mathbb{S}^n$ is closed and minimal, the MET solution $E_{T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})}$ satisfies, for all $n \in N$,
\begin{equation}
    E_{T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})} \ge E_{T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.
\end{equation}
Applying the order-preserving property of Schwarz symmetrization yields
\begin{equation}
    E_{T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})}^* \ge E_{T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^* \quad \forall n \in \mathbb{N}.
\end{equation}
Taking the maximum on both sides, and applying Proposition \ref{maximums}, (because, as $\delta$ is less than the focal distance of $\Sigma$ in $\mathbb{S}^n(1)$, the tube $T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})$ is an open and bounded domain in $\mathbb{S}^n$ with smooth boundary $\delta T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})$), we obtain
\begin{equation} \label{eq:step1_div}
    \max E_{T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})}^* \ge \max E_{T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^* = \max E_{T_\delta(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})} \longrightarrow \infty \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.
\end{equation}
Thus, the symmetrized MET sequence $\{ \max E_{T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})}^*\}_{n \in N}$ is a non-bounded sequence diverging to infinity, so it cannot contain infinite and convergent subsequences.



         
                                                                    

The second step of the proof consist in to get an estimation of the volume of the tube for large dimensions.  Given our family of closed and minimal isometric immersions $\mathcal{F}=\{\varphi_n:\Sigma^{n-1} \rightarrow \mathbb{S}^n\}_{n\in N}$, and given our radius $\delta \leq \mathbb{C}(\mathcal{F})$,  we claim that there exists $n_\delta \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $n \ge n_\delta$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:step2_vol}
    \operatorname{Vol}(T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})) \ge \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n).
\end{equation}
We establish \eqref{eq:step2_vol} by contradiction. Let us suppose that for all $n \in N$, there exists $m_n \geq n$ such that 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:step2_vol2}
\operatorname{Vol}(T_\delta(\Sigma^{{m_{n}}-1})) < \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{m_{n}})
\end{equation} 
\noindent for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

Namely, inequality \eqref{eq:step2_vol2} holds along an unbounded subsequence of natural numbers $\{m_n\}_{n\in N}$. We are going to study the convergence of the subsequence $\{ \max E_{T_\delta(\Sigma^{m_{n}-1})}^*\}_{n \in N}$. 

Let $B_{r_{m_{n}}(\delta)}^{\mathbb{S}^{m_{n}}}$ be the symmetrization ball corresponding to $T_\delta(\Sigma^{m_{n}-1})$. Since $\operatorname{Vol}(T_\delta(\Sigma^{m_{n}-1})) < \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{m_{n}})$, the radius $r_{m_{n}}(\delta)$ satisfies $r_{m_{n}}(\delta) < \pi/2$, meaning that $B_{r_{m_{n}}(\delta)}^{\mathbb{S}^{m_{n}}}$ is strictly contained in a polar hemisphere. By Corollary \ref{cor:upper_bound_met},
\begin{equation}
    E_{T_\delta(\Sigma^{m_{n}-1})}^* \le E_{B_{r_{m_{n}}(\delta)}^{\mathbb{S}^{m_{n}}}} \longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.
\end{equation}
This implies that $\max E_{T_\delta(\Sigma^{m_{n}-1})}^* \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, namely, that we have a convergent-to-zero subsequence $\{ \max E_{T_\delta(\Sigma^{m_{n}-1})}^*\}_{n \in N}$ which directly contradicts the divergence result \eqref{eq:step1_div} established in the first step of the proof. 

Therefore, there must exist $n_\delta \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $\operatorname{Vol}(T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})) \ge \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)$ for all $n \ge n_\delta$.

         
                                                                             

Finally, we apply the Measure Concentration Theorem. Set $A := T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1}) \subset \mathbb{S}^n$. For $n \ge n_\delta$, the second step of the proof \eqref{eq:step2_vol} guarantees that $\operatorname{Vol}(A) \ge \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)$.

The $\epsilon$-fattening of $A$ is precisely the larger tubular neighborhood:
\begin{equation}
    A_\epsilon = \left\{ x \in \mathbb{S}^n : \text{dist}_{\mathbb{S}^n}(x, T_\delta(\Sigma^{n-1})) < \epsilon \right\} = T_{\delta+\epsilon}(\Sigma^{n-1}).
\end{equation}
Applying the classical Measure Concentration Theorem on the sphere (Theorem 1.1) to $A$, we obtain, for all $\epsilon \in (0, \pi/2 - \delta)$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T_{\delta+\epsilon}(\Sigma^{n-1}))}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)} \ge 1 - \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}} e^{-\epsilon^2 (n-1)/2}.
\end{equation}
Taking $n \to \infty$ for any radius $r = \delta + \epsilon \in (0, \pi/2)$, we conclude that
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T_r(\Sigma^{n-1}))}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^n)} = 1,
\end{equation}
which completes the proof.
