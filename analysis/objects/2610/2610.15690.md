<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:score}
Under the preceding assumptions, for every $W,W'\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}$,
$s\in\mathcal S$, and $a\in\mathcal A$,
\begin{align}
S_W(s,a)
&=\Sigma^{-1}\bigl(a-m(\mu_W(s))\bigr)\phi(s)^\top,
\label{eq:score}\\
\lVertS_W(s,a)\rVert_*
&\le R_\pi,
\qquad R_\pi:=BD\lVert\Sigma^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
\label{eq:bounded}\\
\lVertS_W(s,a)-S_{W'}(s,a)\rVert_*
&\le L_\pi\lVertW-W'\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
\qquad L_\pi:=\frac{B^2D^2}{4}\lVert\Sigma^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{\,2}.
\label{eq:lipschitz}
\end{align}

These are sufficient constants, with no claim of sharpness. In particular,
no bound on $\lVertW\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}$ or on $\lVert\mu_W(s)\rVert_2$ is required.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix a state $s$ and abbreviate $\phi=\phi(s)$.
On a compact set of mean parameters, the Gaussian kernel and its derivatives
are uniformly bounded on the bounded set $\mathcal A$. Since $\mathcal A$
has finite measure, differentiation under the integral is justified by
dominated convergence. Positivity of $Z$ then permits differentiation of
normalized moments and logarithms at every mean parameter.

We first differentiate the normalizer. The mean gradient of the unnormalized log kernel is $\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu)$. Hence, we obtain
\begin{align}
\nabla_\mu Z(\mu)
&=\int_{\mathcal A}\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu)
 \exp\!\left[-\tfrac12(x-\mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu)\right]\,dx,
\nonumber\\
\nabla_\mu\log Z(\mu)
&=\Sigma^{-1}(m(\mu)-\mu).
\label{eq:normalizer}
\end{align}

For $a\in\mathcal A$, the support indicator is constant with respect to
the parameter. Subtracting \eqref{eq:normalizer} from the gradient of the
log kernel gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-score}
\nabla_\mu\log\pi_\mu(a)
=\Sigma^{-1}(a-\mu)-\Sigma^{-1}(m(\mu)-\mu)=\Sigma^{-1}(a-m(\mu)).
\end{equation}

The chain rule for $\mu=W\phi$ proves \eqref{eq:score}.
Thus the truncated mean, rather than the untruncated mean parameter,
centers the score.

We next differentiate the truncated mean. For a direction $h\in\mathbb R^m$, differentiation of the normalized expectation,
using \eqref{eq:mean-score}, gives
\begin{align}
D_\mu m(\mu)[h]
&=\mathbb E_\mu\!\left[X(X-m(\mu))^\top\right]\Sigma^{-1}h
=C(\mu)\Sigma^{-1}h,
\label{eq:mean-derivative}\\
D_W S_W(s,a)[U]
&=-\Sigma^{-1} C(\mu_W(s))\Sigma^{-1}\,U\phi\phi^\top,
\qquad U\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}.
\label{eq:score-derivative}
\end{align}

The matrix order in these identities matters:
$D_\mu m(\mu)=C(\mu)\Sigma^{-1}$.
In \eqref{eq:score-derivative}, $\Sigma^{-1}C\Sigma^{-1}$ is an $m\times m$ matrix,
$U\phi$ is an $m$-vector, and the output is an $m\times d$ matrix.
The mean/covariance derivative identity is also the standard
exponential-family identity under the natural parameter $\Sigma^{-1}\mu$;
see Fithian~\cite{fithian2023}. The norm constants below are derived here.

To control the covariance, we use the support diameter. If a scalar random variable $Y$ lies in $[\alpha,\beta]$ and
$\bar y=\mathbb E Y$, then
\[
0\le\mathbb E[(Y-\alpha)(\beta-Y)]
=(\beta-\bar y)(\bar y-\alpha)-\operatorname{Var}(Y).
\]

Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-variance}
\operatorname{Var}(Y)\le(\beta-\bar y)(\bar y-\alpha)
\le\frac{(\beta-\alpha)^2}{4}.
\end{equation}

For $v\in\mathbb R^m$, the range of $v^\top X$ has width at most
$D\lVertv\rVert_2$. Applying \eqref{eq:scalar-variance} with the infimum
and supremum of this range yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covariance}
v^\top C(\mu)v=\operatorname{Var}_\mu(v^\top X)
\le\frac{D^2}{4}\lVertv\rVert_2^{\,2},
\qquad
0\preceq C(\mu)\preceq\frac{D^2}{4}I_m.
\end{equation}

This argument does not require the extremal values to be attained.

It remains to bound the score and its derivative in the required norm. For vectors $u$ and $v$, the matrix $uv^\top$ has at most one nonzero
singular value, equal to $\lVertu\rVert_2\lVertv\rVert_2$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-one}
\lVertuv^\top\rVert_*=\lVertu\rVert_2\lVertv\rVert_2.
\end{equation}

For every $a\in\mathcal A$,
\[
\lVerta-m(\mu)\rVert_2
=\lVert\mathbb E_\mu[a-X]\rVert_2
\le\mathbb E_\mu\lVerta-X\rVert_2\le D.
\]

This remains valid for a nonconvex support, even if its mean is not in
$\mathcal A$. Eqs. \eqref{eq:score} and \eqref{eq:rank-one} now give
\[
\lVertS_W(s,a)\rVert_*
=\lVert\Sigma^{-1}(a-m(\mu_W(s)))\rVert_2\lVert\phi\rVert_2
\le BD\lVert\Sigma^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:bounded}.

Similarly, \eqref{eq:score-derivative}--\eqref{eq:rank-one} imply
\begin{align}
\lVertD_W S_W(s,a)[U]\rVert_*
&=\lVert\Sigma^{-1}C(\mu_W(s))\Sigma^{-1}\,U\phi\rVert_2\lVert\phi\rVert_2
\nonumber\\
&\le\lVert\Sigma^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{\,2}\lVertC(\mu_W(s))\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
       \lVertU\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert\phi\rVert_2^{\,2}
\nonumber\\
&\le\frac{B^2D^2}{4}\lVert\Sigma^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{\,2}\lVertU\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\label{eq:derivative-bound}
\end{align}

Finally, let $W_t=W'+t(W-W')$. The fundamental theorem of calculus
and the triangle inequality for the nuclear norm give
\begin{align*}
S_W(s,a)-S_{W'}(s,a)
&=\int_0^1 D_W S_{W_t}(s,a)[W-W']\,dt,\\
\lVertS_W(s,a)-S_{W'}(s,a)\rVert_*
&\le\int_0^1 L_\pi\lVertW-W'\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\,dt
=L_\pi\lVertW-W'\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{align*}

All bounds are uniform in $s$, $a$, and the endpoints of this segment,
which proves \eqref{eq:lipschitz}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:cauchy}
For \eqref{eq:cauchy-density}, the score is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cauchy-score}
S_W(s,a)=
\left(\frac{2(a_i-(W\phi(s))_i)}
{\gamma_i^2+(a_i-(W\phi(s))_i)^2}\right)_{i=1}^m\phi(s)^\top,
\end{equation}
where the parenthesized entries form a column vector.
The two regularity requirements hold with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cauchy-constants}
R_\pi=B\left(\sum_{i=1}^m\gamma_i^{-2}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad L_\pi=\frac{2B^2}{\min_i\gamma_i^2}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix $s$, abbreviate $\phi=\phi(s)$, and write
$r_i=a_i-\mu_i$ with $\mu=W\phi$ within this proof.
Differentiating the log density with respect to each location and applying
the chain rule gives \eqref{eq:cauchy-score}.

To bound the score, note that
$2\gamma_i|r_i|\le\gamma_i^2+r_i^2$. The rank-one identity therefore gives
\[
\lVertS_W(s,a)\rVert_*
=\lVert\phi\rVert_2\left(\sum_i
\left(\frac{2r_i}{\gamma_i^2+r_i^2}\right)^2\right)^{1/2}
\le B\left(\sum_i\gamma_i^{-2}\right)^{1/2}.
\]

For Lipschitz continuity, the derivative of each location-score component is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cauchy-derivative}
\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_i}
\frac{2(a_i-\mu_i)}{\gamma_i^2+(a_i-\mu_i)^2}
=-\frac{2(\gamma_i^2-r_i^2)}{(\gamma_i^2+r_i^2)^2}.
\end{equation}

Its absolute value is at most $2/(\gamma_i^2+r_i^2)\le2/\gamma_i^2$.
Thus, for any $U\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}$,
\begin{align*}
D_WS_W(s,a)[U]
&=\left(-\frac{2(\gamma_i^2-r_i^2)(U\phi)_i}
{(\gamma_i^2+r_i^2)^2}\right)_{i=1}^m\phi^\top,\\
\lVertD_WS_W(s,a)[U]\rVert_*
&\le\frac{2}{\min_i\gamma_i^2}\lVertU\phi\rVert_2\lVert\phi\rVert_2
\le\frac{2B^2}{\min_i\gamma_i^2}\lVertU\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{align*}

The output is an $m\times d$ matrix of rank at most one.
Integrating this uniform derivative bound along the segment from $W'$ to
$W$, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:score}, proves the Lipschitz inequality.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:boltzmann}
Under these assumptions, \eqref{eq:boltzmann-density} is normalizable for
every $W$, and its score is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boltzmann-score}
S_W(s,a)=\frac{F(s,a)-\mathbb E_W[F(s,A)]}{\tau}.
\end{equation}

Here $\mathbb E_W$ denotes expectation over an action drawn from the current
policy at the fixed state $s$. The two regularity requirements hold with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boltzmann-constants}
R_\pi=\frac{2B}{\tau},\qquad
L_\pi=\frac{B^2}{\tau^2}.
\end{equation}

The score matrix need not have rank one.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Nuclear--spectral duality gives
$|\langle W,F(s,a)\rangle_{\mathrm F}|\le\lVertW\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVertF(s,a)\rVert_*$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boltzmann-finite}
0<e^{-\lVertW\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}B/\tau}\nu_s(\mathcal A)
\le Z_s(W)\le e^{\lVertW\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}B/\tau}\nu_s(\mathcal A)<\infty.
\end{equation}

On every compact set of parameter matrices, the integrand and its first
two parameter derivatives are dominated by constants integrable with
respect to the finite measure $\nu_s$. Differentiation under the integral
is therefore justified. Differentiating the log normalizer yields
$\nabla_W\log Z_s(W)=\mathbb E_W[F(s,A)]/\tau$, proving
\eqref{eq:boltzmann-score}. Jensen's inequality and the triangle inequality
give $\lVertF(s,a)-\mathbb E_W[F(s,A)]\rVert_*\le 2B$ and the bounded-score claim.

Fix $s$ and abbreviate the random feature matrix by $F=F(s,A)$.
All expectations, variances, and covariances below use the current policy.
Differentiating its normalized expectation in the direction
$U\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}$ gives
\begin{align}
D_W\mathbb E_W[F][U]
&=\frac{1}{\tau}\mathbb E_W[(F-\mathbb E_W F)\langle U,F-\mathbb E_W F\rangle_{\mathrm F}],
\label{eq:boltzmann-mean-derivative}\\
D_WS_W(s,a)[U]
&=-\frac{1}{\tau^2}\mathbb E_W[(F-\mathbb E_W F)\langle U,F-\mathbb E_W F\rangle_{\mathrm F}].
\label{eq:boltzmann-score-derivative}
\end{align}

Both outputs have the same $m\times d$ dimensions as $W$.
These are the exponential-family covariance identities
\cite{fithian2023}, here justified directly for bounded features.

To bound the derivative in the required norms, take
$V\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}$ and pair \eqref{eq:boltzmann-score-derivative} with $V$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boltzmann-dual-cov}
\langle V,D_WS_W(s,a)[U]\rangle_{\mathrm F}
=-\frac{1}{\tau^2}\operatorname{Cov}(\langle V,F\rangle_{\mathrm F},\langle U,F\rangle_{\mathrm F}).
\end{equation}

The scalar $\langle U,F\rangle_{\mathrm F}$ lies in
$[-B\lVertU\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},B\lVertU\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}]$. Thus \eqref{eq:scalar-variance} gives
$\operatorname{Var}(\langle U,F\rangle_{\mathrm F})\le B^2\lVertU\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{\,2}$, and the same argument applies to
$V$. Cauchy--Schwarz for covariance now implies
\[
\bigl|\langle V,D_WS_W(s,a)[U]\rangle_{\mathrm F}\bigr|
\le\frac{B^2}{\tau^2}\lVertV\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVertU\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]

Finally, nuclear--spectral duality states that for every real matrix $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-norm}
\lVertX\rVert_*=\sup_{\lVertV\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le1}|\langle V,X\rangle_{\mathrm F}|.
\end{equation}

Indeed, if $X=\sum_i\sigma_i p_iq_i^\top$ is a thin singular value
decomposition, then
$|\langle V,X\rangle_{\mathrm F}|\le\sum_i\sigma_i|p_i^\top Vq_i|
\le\lVertV\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\sum_i\sigma_i$.
Equality is attained by $V=\sum_i p_iq_i^\top$; the zero matrix is immediate.
Taking this supremum proves
\[
\lVertD_WS_W(s,a)[U]\rVert_*
\le\frac{B^2}{\tau^2}\lVertU\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]

Integration along the parameter segment proves the global Lipschitz
claim. This argument avoids both a rank assumption and a dimension factor.

\label{prop:softmax}
For \eqref{eq:softmax-density},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:softmax-score}
S_W(s,a)=\frac{(e_a-p)\phi(s)^\top}{\tau}.
\end{equation}

The two regularity requirements hold with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:softmax-constants}
R_\pi=\frac{\sqrt{2}B}{\tau},\qquad
L_\pi=\frac{B^2}{2\tau^2}.
\end{equation}

These constants are independent of the number of actions. For $K=1$,
the score is identically zero and both constants may instead be zero.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Differentiating the log probability gives \eqref{eq:softmax-score}.
For any probability vector $p$,
\[
\lVerte_a-p\rVert_2^{\,2}
=(1-p_a)^2+\sum_{i\ne a}p_i^2
\le2(1-p_a)^2\le2.
\]

The rank-one identity proves the bounded-score assertion.

For Lipschitz continuity, we consider the Jacobian of $p$ with respect to
the scaled logits $W\phi(s)/\tau$, which is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:softmax-jacobian}
\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top.
\end{equation}

For $v\in\mathbb R^K$, $v^\top (\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top)v$ is the variance of $v_I$ when
$\Pr(I=i)=p_i$, and is nonnegative. If $K\ge2$, its range has width at most
$\max_{i,j}|v_i-v_j|\le\sqrt{2}\lVertv\rVert_2$. Applying
\eqref{eq:scalar-variance} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:softmax-jacobian-bound}
0\le v^\top (\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top)v\le\tfrac12\lVertv\rVert_2^{\,2},
\qquad\lVert(\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\tfrac12.
\end{equation}

For a direction $U\in\mathbb R^{K\times d}$, differentiating the score gives
\[
D_WS_W(s,a)[U]=-
\frac{(\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top)U\phi(s)\phi(s)^\top}{\tau^2}.
\]

This $K\times d$ matrix has rank at most one, so
\begin{align*}
\lVertD_WS_W(s,a)[U]\rVert_*
&=\frac{\lVert(\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top)U\phi(s)\rVert_2\lVert\phi(s)\rVert_2}{\tau^2}\\
&\le\frac{B^2}{2\tau^2}\lVertU\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{align*}

Integrating along the line segment between the parameters gives the
Lipschitz claim. The case $K=1$ is immediate.
