Let $\gamma$ be an arc on the unit disk having length 
$\beta$ 
and $\ell$ be the line segment joining the endpoints of $\beta$. Let $V$ be the subset of the disk having its boundary as $\ell$ and the complement of $\beta$ with respect
to the boundary of the disk.

Extend $\ell$ symmetrically to length 1, and let $\tilde\ell$ be the line formed by translating
the extended $\ell$ parallel to itself 10 units in the direction of the origin. Now construct
a rectangle $R$ by joining the endpoints of the extended $\ell$ to the endpoints of 
$\tilde\ell$.  Then $V\subset R$ and it follows from \cite[p. 322]{JS}
that there exists a solution $v(z)$ to the minimal surface equation in $R$ having boundary values $+\infty$ on the extended $\ell$ and $\tilde\ell$, and 0 on the other two sides.



 Let $\eta$ be the distance from the origin to $\ell$
and $\Delta=\{|z|<\eta/2\}$.  Let $M$ denote the maximum of $v(z)$ in $\Delta$.

Now, for any given $r_n$, the function $u(r_nz)/r_n$ satisfies the minimal surface equation
and we consider any of the components of the set where $u(r_nz)>0$ in
 $|z|<1$.  In any such component $u(r_nz)/r_n$
is bounded by the corresponding $v(z)$ with the arc $\gamma$ referred to above, being
an arc containing the portion of the component on the boundary.  Thus, if the component
extends to $\Delta$, then for those points in $\Delta$ we have
$u(r_nz)/r_n\leq M$.  Applying this to all such components we then get $u(z)
\leq Mr_n$ for $z\in D\cap\{|z|=\eta\, r_n/2\}$ from which the result follows.
