<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:boundary}
For every $\gamma\neq\alpha$ such that $\mathcal{C}_\alpha\cap\,\mathcal{C}_\gamma\neq\emptyset$, the common curve between the cells $\mathcal{C}_\alpha$ and $\mathcal{C}_\gamma$ is
\begin{align}
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha\gamma}:=\mathcal{C}_\alpha\cap\mathcal{C}_\gamma\subseteq\{\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^2,\,d_\alpha(\underline{x})=d_\gamma(\underline{x})\}.
\end{align}
The boundary of $\mathcal{C}_\alpha$ is the union $\mathcal{I}_\alpha:=\partial\mathcal{C}_\alpha=\bigcup_{\gamma}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha\gamma}$ of its common curves.
The points of $\mathcal{I}_\alpha$ that belong to more than one common curve are referred to as vertices, or common points of the cell.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:star}
A Voronoi cell $\mathcal{C}_\alpha$ is said to be star convex at a point $\underline{y}\in\mathcal{C}_\alpha$ if, for every $\underline{x}\in\mathcal{C}_\alpha$, the line segment between $\underline{y}$ and $\underline{x}$ is contained in $\mathcal{C}_\alpha$.
That is, $\mathcal{C}_\alpha$ is star convex at $\underline{y}$ if and only if
\begin{align}
d_\alpha((1-s)\underline{y}+s\underline{x})\leq d_\gamma((1-s)\underline{y}+s\underline{x})
\;\;\forall\;
(\underline{x},\gamma,s)\in \mathcal{C}_\alpha\times I\times[0,1].
\end{align}
In what follows, we consider star convexity at the nucleation point $\underline{x}_\alpha$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:visible}
An open, connected subset $\delta\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\alpha\gamma}$ that contains no vertex is said to be visible from $\underline{x}_\alpha$ if, for every $\underline{y}\in\delta\mathcal{I}$, the half-open segment $[\underline{x}_\alpha,\underline{y})$ is contained in the interior of $\mathcal{C}_\alpha$, and the ray from $\underline{x}_\alpha$ through $\underline{y}$ is not tangent to $\mathcal{I}_{\alpha\gamma}$ at $\underline{y}$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{theo:diffeo}
Let $\delta \mathcal{I}\subseteq \mathcal{I}_{\alpha\gamma}$ be a boundary segment of an anisotropic Voronoi cell $\mathcal{C}_\alpha$ that is visible from $\underline{x}_\alpha$, and let $\delta S^1:=\{\underline{s}\in S^1,\,\exists\,t>0,\;\varphi_\alpha(\underline{s},t)\in\delta\mathcal{I}\}$.
Then, $\delta S^1$ is an open arc of the unit circle, and $\varphi_\alpha^{-1}$ maps $\delta\mathcal{I}$ diffeomorphically onto the graph $\{(\underline{s},\chi_{\alpha\gamma}(\underline{s})):\,\underline{s}\in\delta S^1\}$ of the smooth contact function $\chi_{\alpha\gamma}:\delta S^1\rightarrow(0,\infty)$ given by
\begin{align}\label{eq:contact}
\chi_{\alpha\gamma}(\underline{s}):=
\frac{c}{b(\underline{s})+\sqrt{b(\underline{s})^2-a(\underline{s})\,c}}
\;\;\forall\;
\underline{s}\in\delta S^1,
\end{align}
where
\begin{align}
a(\underline{s}):=\underline{s}\cdot\mathbf{Z}_\gamma^\alpha\cdot\underline{s}-1,\quad
b(\underline{s}):=\underline{s}\cdot\mathbf{Z}_\gamma^\alpha\cdot\underline{x}_\gamma^\alpha,\quad
c:=\underline{x}_\gamma^\alpha\cdot\mathbf{Z}_\gamma^\alpha\cdot\underline{x}_\gamma^\alpha,
\end{align}
with $\underline{x}_\gamma^\alpha:=\mathbf{Z}_\alpha^{1/2}\cdot(\underline{x}_\gamma-\underline{x}_\alpha)$ and $\mathbf{Z}_\gamma^\alpha:=\mathbf{Z}_\alpha^{-1/2}\cdot\mathbf{Z}_\gamma\cdot\mathbf{Z}_\alpha^{-1/2}$.
In particular,
\begin{align*}
\delta \mathcal{I}=\{\varphi_\alpha(\underline{s},\chi_{\alpha\gamma}(\underline{s})):\,\underline{s}\in\delta S^1\}.
\end{align*}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\underline{s}\in S^1$ and $t>0$, and let $\underline{y}=\varphi_\alpha(\underline{s},t)$.
Then $d_\alpha(\underline{y})=t$ and $\underline{y}-\underline{x}_\gamma=\mathbf{Z}_\alpha^{-1/2}\cdot(t\,\underline{s}-\underline{x}_\gamma^\alpha)$, so that
\begin{align}
d_\gamma(\underline{y})^2-d_\alpha(\underline{y})^2
=a(\underline{s})\,t^2-2\,b(\underline{s})\,t+c
=:g(\underline{s},t).
\end{align}
Since $\mathbf{Z}_\gamma^\alpha$ is SPD and $\underline{x}_\gamma\neq\underline{x}_\alpha$, we have $g(\underline{s},0)=c>0$.
By Definition~\ref{def:visible}, for every $\underline{y}\in\delta\mathcal{I}$ with $\varphi_\alpha^{-1}(\underline{y})=(\underline{s},\tau)$, $\tau$ is the smallest positive root of $g(\underline{s},\cdot)$, and this root is simple, since the ray is not tangent to $\mathcal{I}_{\alpha\gamma}$ at $\underline{y}$.
A simple root requires $b(\underline{s})^2-a(\underline{s})\,c>0$, and an elementary discussion of the signs of $a(\underline{s})$ and $b(\underline{s})$ shows that the smallest positive root is given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:contact}, so that $\tau=\chi_{\alpha\gamma}(\underline{s})$.
Moreover, since a ray meets $\delta\mathcal{I}$ only at the point where it first leaves $\mathcal{C}_\alpha$, the map $\delta\mathcal{I}\ni\underline{y}\mapsto\underline{s}$ is injective, and its image $\delta S^1$ is connected and open, i.e., an open arc.
Finally, $\partial_t g(\underline{s},\chi_{\alpha\gamma}(\underline{s}))=-2\sqrt{b(\underline{s})^2-a(\underline{s})\,c}\neq 0$, so that, by the implicit function theorem, $\chi_{\alpha\gamma}$ is smooth on $\delta S^1$.
Hence, $\varphi_\alpha^{-1}$, which is a diffeomorphism, maps $\delta\mathcal{I}$ onto the graph of the smooth function $\chi_{\alpha\gamma}$, which is diffeomorphic to $\delta S^1$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
