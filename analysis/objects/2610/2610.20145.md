<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The formulation considered here is related to the approach of
\cite{vidal2015model,vidal2016empirical}, where a two-field formulation involving only
$(p_h,\lambda_h)$ is used and the flux $\boldsymbol{v}_h$ can be recovered by
back-substitution. While their formulation preserves the affine frequency
dependence and reduces the dimension of the variables involved during the
offline stage; Recovering the flux variables when they are required adds
an additional online computation involving the high-fidelity volume
operators. In our setting, the velocity field is part of the quantities of
interest and is therefore retained explicitly in the reduced formulation.

Accordingly, our approach differs from \cite{vidal2015model,vidal2016empirical} in two respects. First, we retain the
three-field formulation $(p_h,\boldsymbol{v}_h,\lambda_h)$ and compensate for the
increased offline cost through parallel computation. Second, we construct
distinct reduced spaces for the volume variables
$u_h=(p_h,\boldsymbol{v}_h)$ and the trace variable $\lambda_h$. This separation is
required with the well-posedness analysis as explained in Remark
\ref{rem:wellposdeness_ROM}, which relies on independently constructed
approximation spaces for the volume and trace variables. It also preserves
the block structure of the high-fidelity formulation in the reduced system,
as discussed in \cref{sec:galerkin}.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:local_proj}
We note that the uncondensed system \eqref{eq:full_block} is neither assembled nor solved
globally in our implementation. Indeed, solving the system \eqref{eq:full_block} would
introduce unnecessary global volume degrees of freedom that the HDG method aims to mitigate.  During the training stage, the condensed
system \eqref{eq:condensed} is therefore solved to compute the high-fidelity snapshots.
During the projection stage, only the local contributions
$\mathbb{A}_e$, $\mathbb{B}_e$, $\mathbb{C}_e$, $\mathbb{L}_e$, and
$\mathbb{S}_e$ are accessed element-wise and projected onto the corresponding
reduced spaces. The global uncondensed operator therefore never needs to be
assembled. This element-wise construction preserves the HDG structure,
avoids unnecessary memory requirements, and enables the projection operations
to be performed in parallel.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
Assume $s>0$, $\tau\ge\tau_{\min}>0$ and
$\mu\ge\mu_{\min}>0$. Then, $\exists \beta >0 $ such that for every $x\in \mathcal{V}_h$ we have, 
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Re}\,\mathcal{A}(x,Tx)\ \ge\ \beta\,\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}^2 .
\label{eq:Tcoerc}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $x=(p,\boldsymbol v,\lambda), y=(\phi,\boldsymbol\psi,q) \in \mathcal{V}_h$ , we have
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{A}(x,y) = \;&
  \sum_{K_e}\Bigl(-\sigma\,\rho(\boldsymbol v,\boldsymbol\psi)_{K_e}
    -(p,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\psi)_{K_e}
    +\langle\lambda,\boldsymbol\psi\cdot\boldsymbol \nu\rangle_{\partial K_e}\Bigr)
  \\
  &+\sum_{K_e}\Bigl(-\sigma\,\mu\,(p,\phi)_{K_e}
    +(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol v,\phi)_{K_e}
    +\tau\,\langle p-\lambda,\phi\rangle_{\partial K_e}\Bigr)
  \\
  &+\sum_{K_e}\bigl\langle\boldsymbol v\cdot\boldsymbol \nu
    +\tau\,(p-\lambda),\ q\bigr\rangle_{\partial K_e}
     -\sum_{\mathfrak f^{\mathrm A}}c_e\,\langle\lambda,q\rangle_{\mathfrak f} .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:calA_expanded}
\end{equation}
where $\mathfrak f^{\mathrm A}$ denotes the faces on $\Gamma_A$; Neumann faces carry no additional term.\\
Take  $y=Tx$, we get the energy identity
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Re}\,\mathcal{A}(x,Tx) = E(x) :=
  \sum_{K_e}\Bigl(s\,\rho\|\boldsymbol v\|_{K_e}^2
  + s\,\mu\,\|p\|_{K_e}^2
  + \tau\,\|p-\lambda\|_{\partial K_e}^2\Bigr)
   + \sum_{\mathfrak f^{\mathrm A}}c_e\,\|\lambda\|_{\mathfrak f}^2 ,
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
where we used $\operatorname{Re}(-\sigma)=s>0$ and the fact that for any complex number $z\in\mathbb{C}$, we have $\operatorname{Re}(\bar{z} - z)= 0$. \\
Next, we bound the energy identity. It is straightforward that 
\begin{equation}
    \|\boldsymbol v\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2\le(s\,\rho_{\min})^{-1}E(x)  \quad \text{and} \quad
\|p\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2\le(s\mu_{\min})^{-1}E(x),
\end{equation}
For the face terms,  we have $\|\lambda\|_{\mathfrak f}^2
 \le 2\|p-\lambda\|_{\mathfrak f}^2+2\|p\|_{\mathfrak f}^2$
 and 
 \begin{equation}
  2\sum_{K}\|p-\lambda\|_{\partial K}^2
  \;\le\; \frac{2}{\tau_{\min}}\sum_{K}\tau\,\|p-\lambda\|_{\partial K}^2
  \;\le\; \frac{2}{\tau_{\min}}\,E(x) .
\label{eq:face_term1}
\end{equation}
and using
 the discrete trace inequality (see for example \cite{ern2021finite})
$\|q\|_{\partial K}^2\le C_{\mathrm{tr}}h_K^{-1}\|q\|_K^2$ on each cell with $C_{\mathrm{tr}}$ denoting the inequality constant,
followed by $\|p\|_\Omega^2\le E(x)/(s\,\mu_{\min})$, gives
\begin{equation}
  2\sum_{K}\|p\|_{\partial K}^2
  \;\le\; \frac{2\,C_{\mathrm{tr}}}{h_{\min}}\,\|p\|_\Omega^2
  \;\le\; \frac{2\,C_{\mathrm{tr}}}{h_{\min}\,s\,\mu_{\min}}\,E(x) .
\label{eq:face_term2}
\end{equation}
Finally we get 
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\mathfrak f^{\mathrm I}}\|\lambda\|_{\mathfrak f}^2
  \;\le\; \Bigl(\frac{2}{\tau_{\min}}
        + \frac{2\,C_{\mathrm{tr}}}{h_{\min}\,s\,\mu_{\min}}\Bigr)E(x) .
\label{eq:face_final}
\end{equation}
While on ABC faces, with $c_{\min}:=\displaystyle \min_{\Gamma_A}(c\rho)^{-1}>0$, we have
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\mathfrak f^{\mathrm A}}\|\lambda\|_{\mathfrak f}^2
\le c_{\min}^{-1}E(x).
\end{equation}
Adding the five bounds gives
$\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}^2\le\beta^{-1}E(x)\le \, \operatorname{Re}\,  \mathcal{A}(x,Tx) $ with $\beta$ defined as
\begin{equation}
  \beta^{-1} := \frac{1}{s\rho_{\min}}
  + \frac{1}{s\,\mu_{\min}}
  + \frac{2}{\tau_{\min}}
  + \frac{2\,C_{\mathrm{tr}}}{h_{\min}\,s\,\mu_{\min}}
  + \frac{1}{c_{\min}} ,
\label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
Assume $s>0$, $\tau\ge\tau_{\min}>0$ and
$\mu\ge\mu_{\min}>0$. Then,
\begin{equation}
  \inf_{x\in \mathcal{V}_h\setminus\{0\}}\ \sup_{y\in \mathcal{V}_h\setminus\{0\}}
  \frac{|\mathcal{A}(x,y)|}{\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}\,\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}}\ \ge\ \beta ,
  \qquad
  \inf_{y\in \mathcal{V}_h\setminus\{0\}}\ \sup_{x\in \mathcal{V}_h\setminus\{0\}}
  \frac{|\mathcal{A}(x,y)|}{\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}\,\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}}\ \ge\ \beta .
\label{eq:infsup}
\end{equation}
In particular, the HDG system \eqref{eq:full_block} is well-posed.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix $x\in \mathcal{V}_h$, since $Tx\in \mathcal{V}_h$ and $\|Tx\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}=\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}$, the first inequality  in  \eqref{eq:infsup} is straightforward. 
For the second one, fix
$y\in \mathcal{V}_h\setminus\{0\}$ and we take $X=TY$.  We get 
$\mathcal{A}(Ty,y)=\mathcal{A}(x,Tx)\ge\beta\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}^2=\beta\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}^2$. Well-posedness follows from Banach-Nečas-Babuška theorem (see for instance \cite{fortin1991mixed}).

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
Under the same assumptions, $\mathbb{A}_e$ is coercive, hence invertible
for every cell, and the condensation   \eqref{eq:backsubstitution} is well defined.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $u_e=(p,\boldsymbol v)\in W_h(K_e)\times\boldsymbol W_h(K_e)$  and apply the energy identity   \eqref{eq:energy} to
$x=(u_e,0)$, we get  $\mathcal{A}(x,Tx)=a_e(u_e,u_e)$ and  \eqref{eq:energy}
reduces to
\[
  \operatorname{Re}\,a_e(u_e,u_e)
  = s\,\rho\|\boldsymbol v\|_{K_e}^2
  + s\,\mu\,\|p\|_{K_e}^2
  + \tau\,\|p\|_{\partial K_e}^2
  \ \ge\ \alpha_a\|u_e\|_{L^2(K_e)}^2 ,
  \qquad \alpha_a := \min(s\,\rho,\,s\,\mu) > 0 ,
\]
so $\mathbb{A}_e$ is coercive and therefore invertible.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
Under the same assumptions, $-\mathcal{K}$ is coercive,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Re}\bigl(-\mathcal{K}(\lambda,\lambda)\bigr)
  \ \ge\ \beta\,\|\lambda\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}^2
  \qquad\forall\,\lambda\in M_h.
\label{eq:Kcoerc}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $\lambda\in M_h$ and $x_\lambda:=(u,\lambda)$ with
$U=-\mathbb{A}^{-1}\mathbb{C}\Lambda$, which yields $\mathcal{A}\bigl(x_\lambda,(u,0)\bigr)=0$, we obtain thus 
\[
  \mathcal{A}(x_\lambda,Tx_\lambda)
  = \mathcal{A}\bigl(x_\lambda,(u,0)\bigr) + \mathcal{A}\bigl(x_\lambda,(0,-\lambda)\bigr) 
  = \mathcal{A}\bigl(x_\lambda,(0,-\lambda)\bigr)  =  -\,\mathcal{K}(\lambda,\lambda),
\]
which implies that 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coercivity_K}
    \operatorname{Re}(-\mathcal{K}(\lambda,\lambda))
=\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{A}(x_\lambda,Tx_\lambda)
\ge\beta\|x_\lambda\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}^2\ge\beta\|\lambda\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}^2.
\end{equation}

The coercivity constant $\beta_k$ of $-\mathcal{K}$ satisfies  $\beta_k>\beta$ in practice, indeed
the constant $\beta$ in \eqref{eq:coercivity_K} must hold 
for all variables $x_\lambda=(u,\lambda)$, whereas the coercivity of
$-\mathcal{K}$ only needs to control $\lambda$. Indeed, from \eqref{eq:coercivity_K}, we have
\begin{equation}
     \operatorname{Re}(-\mathcal{K}(\lambda,\lambda))\ge\beta\|x_\lambda\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}^2  = \beta\|\lambda\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}^2 + \beta\|u_\lambda\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2.
\end{equation}

The well-posedness of the reduced model  \eqref{eq:reduced_block} is inherited from
Section  \ref{sec:wellposedness}. Since the two bases are constructed separately, the reduced
space $\mathcal{Z}_n=\mathcal{Z}_n^U\times\mathcal{Z}_n^\Lambda$ is $T$-invariant: for
$x_r=(u_r,\lambda_r)\in\mathcal{Z}_n$ we have $Tx_r=(u_r,-\lambda_r)\in\mathcal{Z}_n$, so
$Tx_r$ is an admissible test function and the energy identity  \eqref{eq:energy} still applies 
on $\mathcal{Z}_n$. Hence $\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{A}(x_r,Tx_r)\ge\beta\|x_r\|^2_{\mathcal{V}_h}$
with the same constant $\beta$ as the full-order system. In contrast, using  a single basis stacking the volume and skeleton components, $Tx_r$ would
in general leave $\mathcal{Z}_n$ and the argument breaks. 
Moreover, the coercivity of the sesquilinear   form $a$ defined in \eqref{eq:form_ae} justifies the invertibility of
the reduced matrix in  \eqref{eq:reduced_block} which in turn justifies \cref{eq:reduced_backsub}. Finally, since $\beta$ is the inf-sup constant of the full-order system, it is independent of the reduced dimension $n_U$ and $n_\Lambda$ : meaning that the reduced model remains uniformly stable as the reduced bases are enriched.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:cea}
Let $\gamma$ denote the continuity constant of the sesquilinear form
$\mathcal{A}$ and $\beta$ its inf-sup constant defined in \eqref{eq:beta}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cea}
  \bigl\|(e_U,e_\Lambda)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}
  \le \Bigl(1+\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\Bigr)
  \inf_{(v_r,q_r)\in\mathcal{Z}_n}
  \bigl\|(u_h-v_r,\,\lambda_h-q_r)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For $(v_r,q_r)\in\mathcal Z_n$ set $d_U:=u_h-v_r$,
$d_\Lambda:=\lambda_h-q_r$, $\zeta_U:=u_r-v_r$, $\zeta_\Lambda:=\lambda_r-q_r$,
so that we get  $(\zeta_U,\zeta_\Lambda)\in\mathcal Z_n$ and
$(e_U,e_\Lambda)=(d_U,d_\Lambda)-(\zeta_U,\zeta_\Lambda)$.\\ For any
$(w_r,\varphi_r)\in\mathcal Z_n$, by linearity and since $(u_h,\lambda_h)$
solves  \eqref{eq:full_block} on $\mathcal Z_n$ while $(u_r,\lambda_r)$
solves  \eqref{eq:reduced_block},
\[
  \mathcal A\bigl((\zeta_U,\zeta_\Lambda),(w_r,\varphi_r)\bigr)
  =\mathcal A\bigl((u_h,\lambda_h),(w_r,\varphi_r)\bigr)
   -\mathcal A\bigl((v_r,q_r),(w_r,\varphi_r)\bigr)
  =\mathcal A\bigl((d_U,d_\Lambda),(w_r,\varphi_r)\bigr) .
\]
Inf-sup stability and continuity of $\mathcal A$ then give
\[
  \beta\|(\zeta_U,\zeta_\Lambda)\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}
  \le\sup_{(w_r,\varphi_r)\in\mathcal Z_n\setminus\{0\}}
  \frac{\mathcal A((\zeta_U,\zeta_\Lambda),(w_r,\varphi_r))}{\|(w_r,\varphi_r)\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}}
  \le\gamma\|(d_U,d_\Lambda)\|_{\mathcal{V}_h} ,
\]
so $\|(\zeta_U,\zeta_\Lambda)\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}\le(\gamma/\beta)\|(d_U,d_\Lambda)\|_{\mathcal{V}_h}$.
We use the triangle inequality and the infimum over $(v_r,q_r)$ to conclude.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:trace}
Let
$\gamma_k$ be the continuity constant of the sesquilinear form $\mathcal{K}$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sum_lambda}
\displaystyle
  \left\| e_\Lambda \right\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}
  \leq\underbrace{\Bigl(1+\frac{\gamma_k}{\beta}\Bigr)
       \inf_{q_r\in \mathcal{Z}_n^\Lambda}\left\| \lambda_h-q_r \right\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}}
       _{\text{best-approx of }\lambda_h\text{ in }\mathcal{Z}_n^\Lambda}
  +\underbrace{\frac{\delta_K\left\| \lambda_h \right\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}+\delta_F}{\beta}}
       _{\text{gap between the two condensations}},
\end{equation}
where $\delta_K, \delta_F>0$ are respectively the operator norms of $\delta \mathbb{K}$ and $\delta \mathbb{F}$.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By  \eqref{eq:commutation}, we get 
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathbb{K}} = \mathbb{Z}_\Lambda^{*}\mathbb{K}\mathbb{Z}_\Lambda-\delta\mathbb{K},
    \qquad
    \widehat{\mathbb{F}} = \mathbb{Z}_\Lambda^{*}\mathbb{F}-\delta\mathbb{F}.
\end{equation}
Fix $q_r\in\mathcal{Z}^\Lambda_n$ and split
$e_\Lambda=(\lambda_h-q_r)+\underbrace{(q_r-\lambda_r)}_{\zeta_r}$; we estimate the
second term. Applying $\widehat{\mathbb{K}}$ to
$\widehat\zeta=\widehat q-\widehat\Lambda$, using $\widehat{\mathbb{K}}\widehat\Lambda=\widehat{\mathbb{F}}$ and $\mathbb{K}\Lambda=\mathbb{F}$, we get
\begin{equation}
   \widehat{\mathbb{K}}\,\widehat\zeta
   =\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_\Lambda^{*}\mathbb{K}\mathbb{Z}_\Lambda-\delta\mathbb{K}\bigr)\widehat q
     -\mathbb{Z}_\Lambda^{*}\mathbb{F}+\delta\mathbb{F}
   =\mathbb{Z}_\Lambda^{*}\mathbb{K}\,(\mathbb{Z}_\Lambda\widehat q-\Lambda)
     -\delta\mathbb{K}\,\widehat q+\delta\mathbb{F}.
\end{equation}
Let $\varphi_r=\boldsymbol{Z}_\Lambda\widehat\varphi\in\mathcal{Z}^\Lambda_n$, we get
\begin{equation}
   \widehat\varphi^{*}\widehat{\mathbb{K}}\widehat\zeta
   = \mathcal{K}(q_r-\lambda_h,\varphi_r)
     -\widehat\varphi^{*}\delta\mathbb{K}\,\widehat q
     +\widehat\varphi^{*}\delta\mathbb{F} .
\end{equation}
The first term is bounded by the continuity of $\mathcal{K}$, with constant $\gamma_k$.
For the two others, we introduce
\begin{equation}
   \delta_K:=\sup_{\widehat q,\widehat\varphi\,\in\,\mathbb{C}^{n_\Lambda}\setminus\{0\}}
      \frac{|\widehat\varphi^{\,*}\delta\mathbb{K}\,\widehat q|}
           {\|\widehat q\|\,\|\widehat\varphi\|},
   \qquad
   \delta_F:=\sup_{\widehat\varphi\,\in\,\mathbb{C}^{n_\Lambda}\setminus\{0\}}
      \frac{|\widehat\varphi^{\,*}\delta\mathbb{F}|}{\|\widehat\varphi\|} ,
   \label{eq:deltas}
\end{equation}
the operator norm of $\delta\mathbb{K}$ and the norm of $\delta\mathbb{F}$
on $\mathcal{Z}^\Lambda_n$.  Since $\mathbb{Z}_\Lambda$ has $\mathbb{M}_\Lambda$-orthonormal columns
($\|\widehat q\|=\|q_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}$,
$\|\widehat\varphi\|=\|\varphi_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}$),
\[
   |\widehat\varphi^{*}\delta\mathbb{K}\,\widehat q|
   \le\delta_K\,\|q_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}\,\|\varphi_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)} ,
   \qquad
   |\widehat\varphi^{*}\delta\mathbb{F}|
   \le\delta_F\,\|\varphi_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)} .
\]
Altogether,
\begin{equation}
   |\widehat\varphi^{*}\widehat{\mathbb{K}}\widehat\zeta|
   \le\Bigl(\gamma_k\|\lambda_h-q_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}
      +\delta_K\|q_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}+\delta_F\Bigr)\,
      \|\varphi_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)} .
\end{equation}
Taking the supremum over $\widehat\varphi\neq0$ and using the inf-sup
constant of $\widehat{\mathbb{K}}$, which is the same $\beta$ as in  \eqref{eq:Kcoerc},
\begin{equation}  
   \beta\,\|\zeta_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}
   \le\gamma_k\|\lambda_h-q_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}
      +\delta_K\|q_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}+\delta_F .
\end{equation}
Choosing $q_r=\Pi_{\mathcal{Z}^\Lambda_n}\lambda_h$, which realizes the infimum
of the first term and satisfies $\|q_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}\le\|\lambda_h\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}$,
the triangle inequality $\|e_\Lambda\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}
\le\|\lambda_h-q_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}+\|\zeta_r\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}$
gives  \eqref{eq:sum_lambda}.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:primal}
Let $\gamma_c$ be the continuity constant of $c(q,w):=W^{*}\mathbb{C}Q$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sum_primal}
  \left\| e_U \right\|_{L^2(\Omega)}
  \leq\underbrace{\frac{\gamma_a}{\alpha_a}
  \inf_{v_r\in \mathcal{Z}_n^U}\left\| u_h-v_r \right\|_{L^2(\Omega)}}_{\text{best-approx of }u_h\text{ in }\mathcal{Z}_n^U}
  +\underbrace{C_{\mathrm{coupl}}\left\| e_\Lambda \right\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}}_{\text{propagation of trace error}},
  \qquad
\end{equation}
where $C_{\mathrm{coupl}}=\gamma_c/\alpha_a$.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
By  \eqref{eq:local}, the coefficient vector of $u_h$ satisfies $U=\mathbb{A}^{-1}(\mathbb{S}-\mathbb{C}\Lambda)$ while the reduced
back-substitution
$\widehat{\mathbb{A}}\widehat U
=\mathbb{Z}_U^{*}(\mathbb{S}-\mathbb{C}\mathbb{Z}_\Lambda\widehat\Lambda)$ that is $\mathbb{Z}_U \hat{U}=\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{S}-\mathbb{C}\mathbb{Z}_\Lambda\widehat\Lambda)$. Inserting in $E_U$ the coefficient vector of $e_U$, we get 
\[   E_U   =\underbrace{\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{-1}-\mathcal{P}\bigr)      \bigl(\mathbb{S}-\mathbb{C}\Lambda\bigr)}_{\text{(i)}}   +\underbrace{\mathcal{P}\bigl(-\mathbb{C}E_\Lambda\bigr)}_{\text{(ii)}} .\]
Term (i) is  \eqref{eq:cea_local} with $f=\mathbb{S}-\mathbb{C}\Lambda$, it is bounded by$(\gamma_a/\alpha_a)\inf_{v_r}\|u_h-v_r\|_{L^2(\Omega)}$. \\
For term (ii), define the test function $w_r$ with coefficient vector $\mathcal{P}(-\mathbb{C}E_\Lambda)$, it satisfies $a(w_r,v_r)=-c(e_\Lambda,v_r)$ for all $v_r\in\mathcal{Z}^U_n$; choosing $v_r=w_r$ and using coercivity,
\[   \alpha_a\|w_r\|^2_{L^2(\Omega)}\le\operatorname{Re}a(w_r,w_r)   =-\operatorname{Re}c(e_\Lambda,w_r)   \le\gamma_c\|e_\Lambda\|_{L^2(\Sigma)}\|w_r\|_{L^2(\Omega)} .\]
Adding the two contributions gives  \eqref{eq:sum_primal}.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
At each training frequency $\sigma_\ell$ for $\ell \in \{1,\ldots,n_{\rm lift}\}$, the two conditions
\[
\mathbb{A}^{-1}(\sigma_\ell)\mathbb{C}\,\mathcal{Z}^\Lambda_n
\subset \mathcal{Z}^U_n,
\qquad
\mathbb{A}^{-1}(\sigma_\ell)\mathbb{S}
\in \mathcal{Z}^U_n
\]
are not independent. Indeed, by construction, the high-fidelity
snapshot $U_h(\sigma_\ell)$ belongs to $\mathcal{Z}^U_n$ and satisfies
\[
U_h(\sigma_\ell)
=
\mathbb{A}^{-1}(\sigma_\ell)\mathbb{S}
-
\mathbb{A}^{-1}(\sigma_\ell)\mathbb{C}\Lambda_h(\sigma_\ell),
\qquad
\Lambda_h(\sigma_\ell)\in\mathcal{Z}^\Lambda_n.
\]
Consequently, if $\mathcal{Z}^U_n$ is enriched with  $
\mathbb{A}^{-1}(\sigma_\ell)\mathbb{C}\,\mathcal{Z}^\Lambda_n
\subset \mathcal{Z}^U_n,
$ then $ \mathbb{A}^{-1}(\sigma_\ell)\mathbb{S}
\in\mathcal{Z}^U_n.$\\
As a result,
both $\delta_K$ and $\delta_F$ vanish at these frequencies. We assess
this lifting strategy numerically in Sections~\ref{sec:marmousi} and
\ref{sec:3d}.
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
In practice, enforcing the lifting condition
$
\mathbb{A}^{-1}(\sigma_\ell)\mathbb{C}\mathcal{Z}^\Lambda_n
\subset \mathcal{Z}^U_n
$ at $n_{\rm lift}$ selected training frequencies enriches the primal space by at most $n_{\rm lift}n_\Lambda$ additional dimensions, so that $ \dim\mathcal{Z}^U_n \le  n_U+n_{\rm lift}n_\Lambda. $\\
Although these additional basis vectors are inexpensive to compute,
since $\mathbb{A}$ is block diagonal and the required local inverse
matrices are already available from the high-fidelity solves, the
resulting increase in reduced dimension may become significant. This
growth can be controlled in two ways: (i) by performing the lifting
only at $n_{\rm lift}$ selected training frequencies, so that the gaps
vanish exactly at these frequencies and remain small in their
neighborhood, and (ii) by compressing the lifted vectors with a POD
before orthonormalizing them against $\mathcal{Z}^U_n$.

An alternative that keeps the dimension of $\mathcal{Z}^U_n$ fixed is a
Petrov--Galerkin projection of the volume equation, in which the test space
$\mathcal{Y}_n\neq\mathcal{Z}^U_n$ is spanned by the dual liftings
$\mathbb{A}^{-*}(\sigma_\ell)\mathbb{B}^*\mathcal{Z}^\Lambda_n$,
$\ell=1,\dots,n_{\rm lift}$, while the trial space $\mathcal{Z}^U_n$ is left
unchanged. The gaps $\delta_K$ and $\delta_F$ then vanish at the lifting
frequencies without enlarging $\mathcal{Z}^U_n$. However, this approach
requires the analysis of a new inf-sup constant, which is not controlled by
construction since the trial and test spaces are built independently.
Moreover, the reduced system is square only if
$\dim\mathcal{Y}_n=\dim\mathcal{Z}^U_n$  which couples the two reduced spaces. We do not pursue this
approach here and instead consider the two strategies described above.

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
The reduced model \eqref{eq:condensed_reduced} is closely related to localized model order reduction methods discussed in the introduction. In both settings, the interior unknowns are approximated in a reduced space and subsequently eliminated by static condensation, resulting in a reduced Schur complement for the interface variables. In the present HDG setting, the volume unknowns play the role of the component interiors, while the trace unknowns on the skeleton play the role of what is referred to as ports in localized model reduction.  In \cite{phuong2013static}, however, the interior reduced space is constructed by lifting the modes of the port reduced space. Consequently, $
\mathbb{A}^{-1}\mathbb{C}\,\mathcal{Z}^\Lambda_n
\subset \mathcal{Z}^U_n $ holds by construction, so that projection and static condensation commute. In the present work, in contrast, the reduced spaces $\mathcal{Z}^U_n$ and $\mathcal{Z}^\Lambda_n$ are constructed independently, and this inclusion is not guaranteed. As a result, condensation and projection do not generally commute, which motivates the analysis presented above.

Moreover, localized methods construct local reduced bases that are valid on a component of the domain and typically rely on local a posteriori error estimators to assess and adapt these bases. For instance, \cite{smetana2015new} develops an estimator accounting for the combined reduction of interior and port variables, while \cite{buhr2017arbilomod,ohlberger2015error} employ localized estimators to drive the adaptive enrichment of local reduced spaces. In the present work, the reduced spaces are instead global over the physical and parameter domains. Accordingly, the estimates established in \cref{prop:trace,prop:primal,prop:gaps} are a priori estimates for the global reduced formulation, rather than localized a posteriori estimators for component-wise enrichment.
