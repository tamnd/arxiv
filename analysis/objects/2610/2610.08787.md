Let $ {\cal R} $ be a unitary fusion category. The tube category associated with
    $ {\cal R} $, denoted by $\mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} )$, is a unitary linear category defined by
    the following data.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[-] The objects of $\mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} )$ are the objects of $ {\cal R} $;
        \item[-] For $A,B\in {\cal R} $, the morphism space in $\mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} )$ is
        defined by
        \[
        \mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} )(A, B) := \bigoplus_{i\in\mathrm{Irr} {\cal R} } {\cal R} (A\otimes i, i\otimes B).
        \]
        That is, any morphism $u$ in $\mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} )(A,B)$ is an
        $\mathrm{Irr} {\cal R} $-graded vector of the form
        \[
        \left(\raisebox{-28.65274pt}{\includegraphics{figures/tikz/tube-morphism-component.pdf}}\right)_{i\in\mathrm{Irr} {\cal R} },
        \]
        where each $u_i$ is a morphism in $ {\cal R} (A\otimes i, i\otimes B)$;
        \item[-] For $u\in \mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} )(B,C)$ and
        $v\in \mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} )(A,B)$, their composition is defined by
        \[
        (u\circ v)_k = \sum_{i,j\in\mathrm{Irr} {\cal R} }\sum_{\alpha = 1}^{N^{ji}_k}
        \raisebox{-57.10547pt}{\includegraphics{figures/tikz/tube-morphism-composition.pdf}}.
        \]
        Here
        \[
            \left\{q_\alpha\in  {\cal R} (k, j\otimes i): \alpha = 1,2,\ldots, N^{ji}_k\right\}
        \]
        is an orthonormal basis of $ {\cal R} (k, j\otimes i)$;
        \item[-] The dagger structure~\cite{Green_2024} is defined by
        \[
        \left(\raisebox{-28.65274pt}{\includegraphics{figures/tikz/tube-morphism-before-adjoint.pdf}}\right)^\dagger :=
    \raisebox{-28.65274pt}{\includegraphics{figures/tikz/tube-morphism-after-adjoint.pdf}}.
        \]
    \end{itemize}

For $x=(X,\gamma_{X,-})$ and $A \in  {\cal R} $, define $x^\sharp(A):= {\cal R} (X,A)$. For
$A,B\in {\cal R} $, define the action of $x^\sharp$ on tube morphisms by the linear
map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anyon-insertion-rep}
    x^\sharp_{A,B}:\mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} )(A,B)
    \rightarrow\mathsf{Hilb}\bigl( {\cal R} (X,A), {\cal R} (X,B)\bigr)
\end{equation}
determined as follows. For $V\in {\cal R} $ and
$u:A\otimes V\rightarrow V\otimes B$, its action on
$\phi:X\rightarrow A$ is defined algebraically by~\cite{Lan_2014,Green_2024}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anyon-insertion-partial-trace}
    x^\sharp_{A,B}\left(\pi_V^{A,B}(u)\right)(\phi)
    :=\operatorname{ptr}^{\ell}_V\!\left[
        u\circ(\phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_V)\circ\gamma_{X,V}^{-1}
    \right].
\end{equation}
This action is graphically represented by
\[
\raisebox{-52.21042pt}{\includegraphics{figures/tikz/central-object-action.pdf}}.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:anyon-insertion-partial-trace} uses the inverse half-braiding to move the
$V$-string across the $X$-string.
We call $x^\sharp$ the \emph{anyon-insertion representation functor}, associated with $x$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:hardiman-tube-center-equivalence}
For every $x\in\mathfrak{Z}( {\cal R} )$, the construction above defines a $*$-functor
$x^\sharp:\mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} )\rightarrow\mathsf{Hilb}$. Moreover, the assignment
$x\mapsto x^\sharp$ extends to a unitary equivalence
\begin{equation}
    (-)^\sharp: \mathfrak{Z}( {\cal R} )^{\mathrm{op}}
    \cong \mathsf{Fun}_*\bigl(\mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} ),\mathsf{Hilb}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where the right-hand side is the category of $*$-functors to finite-dimensional
Hilbert spaces and natural transformations.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
For all $A,B\in {\cal R} $, the anyon-insertion maps associated with the simple
objects of $\mathfrak{Z}( {\cal R} )$ assemble into a linear isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tube-sector-decomposition}
\begin{aligned}
    \Phi_{A,B}:\mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} )(A,B)
    &\xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }
    \bigoplus_{z\in\mathrm{Irr}\mathfrak{Z}( {\cal R} )}
    \mathsf{Hilb}\bigl(z^\sharp(A),z^\sharp(B)\bigr),\\
    u&\longmapsto
    \bigl(z^\sharp_{A,B}(u)\bigr)_{z}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $A=B$, this is an isomorphism of finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebras.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop:hardiman-tube-center-equivalence}, the
anyon-insertion functors $z^\sharp$, with $z\in\mathrm{Irr}\mathfrak{Z}( {\cal R} )$, form a complete
set of simple tube representations. The semisimple Yoneda
decomposition of the tube category then gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:tube-sector-decomposition}.
Under this isomorphism, composition and the dagger operation act
componentwise.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The half-braiding construction for resolving topological sectors was
implemented numerically for the non-braided Haagerup fusion category
$\mathcal H_3$ in Ref.~\cite{Vanhove_2022}. An alternative procedure using
central idempotents of the MPO tube algebra was applied to
strange-correlator transfer matrices in Ref.~\cite{Bal_2018}, building on
Ref.~\cite{Bultinck_2017}. Here we formulate the anyon-insertion construction
as the $*$-functor $x^\sharp:\mathsf{Tube}_{\mathrm v}( {\cal R} )\to\mathsf{Hilb}$, establish the sector
decomposition of general tube morphisms, and apply it to transfer morphisms
produced by Loop-TNR.
\label{rem:hardiman-numerics}

and coevaluation. No braiding in $ {\cal R} $ is involved.
Indeed, horizontal dinaturality together with the two snake identities gives
\begin{align*}
    \operatorname{swap}_{X,Y}^{-1}
      \circ_{\mathrm h}\operatorname{swap}_{X,Y}
    &=\mathrm{id}_{YX}^{\mathrm h},\\
    \operatorname{swap}_{X,Y}
      \circ_{\mathrm h}\operatorname{swap}_{X,Y}^{-1}
    &=\mathrm{id}_{XY}^{\mathrm h}.
\end{align*}

Apply horizontal dinaturality to
\begin{equation*}
    p:A\otimes X\longrightarrow X\otimes B
\end{equation*}
and the morphism
\begin{equation*}
    \mathrm{id}_X\otimes q\otimes\mathrm{id}_X:
    (XB)\otimes(YX)\longrightarrow(XY)\otimes(AX).
\end{equation*}
This gives the intertwining relation
\begin{multline*}
    \operatorname{swap}_{X,Y}\circ_{\mathrm h}
    \varpi_B^{YX,YX}\!\left(\mathrm{ct}_{\mathrm v}(q\otimes p)\right)\\
    =\varpi_A^{XY,XY}\!\left(\mathrm{ct}_{\mathrm v}(p\otimes q)\right)
    \circ_{\mathrm h}\operatorname{swap}_{X,Y}.
\end{multline*}
Thus the two horizontal endomorphisms are conjugate by
$\operatorname{swap}_{X,Y}$.  Since categorical trace is invariant under
conjugation,
\begin{equation*}
    \Theta_A\!\left(Q\circ_{\mathrm v}P\right)
    =\Theta_B\!\left(P\circ_{\mathrm v}Q\right).
\end{equation*}
In the cut-square picture, this equality of horizontal trace classes is
\begin{equation*}

\end{equation*}
This is the cowedge condition for $(\Theta_A)_A$.  The universal property of
the vertical torus coend therefore induces the unique map
$\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{cut}}$ with Eq.~\eqref{eq:appendix-change-of-cut}.

Interchanging the horizontal and vertical variables gives a cowedge in the
opposite direction and hence an inverse map.  Both composites are the identity
on the universal square generators, so $\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{cut}}$ is an
isomorphism.
