\label{lemma: properties of six-canonical bundle}
The line bundle $\mathcal{L}$ is strongly relatively very ample. Furthermore, $\mathcal{E}$ is locally free of rank $5g-5|6g-6$, and its formation commutes with base change.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The line bundle $\mathcal{L}$ is strongly relatively very ample (see, e.g., \cite{ott2026algebraicity}). 

For every point $t \in T$, let $X_t$ denote the fiber, and let $(C_t,L_t)$ be the spin curve determined by $X_t$. Over a bosonic base, we have the well-known identifications
\[
\mathcal{O}_{X_t}=\mathcal{O}_{C_t}\oplus\Pi L_t,
\qquad
\mathcal{L}\vert_{X_t}=L_t^6\oplus\Pi L_t^7,
\qquad
L_t^2\cong\omega_{C_t}.
\]
Since $\deg L_t=g-1$, both summands of $\mathcal{L}\vert_{X_t}$ have vanishing $H^1$. The ordinary Riemann--Roch formula gives
\[
h^0(C_t,L_t^6)=5g-5,
\qquad
h^0(C_t,L_t^7)=6g-6.
\]
Cohomology and base change now show that $\mathcal{E}$ is locally free of this rank and commutes with base change.

\underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{\mathcal{O}_T}(V_T,\mathcal{E}_{X/T})
\big/\mathcal{O}_{T,0}^{*},
\]
where invertible even functions on the base act by scalar multiplication on $V_T$. We write $\operatorname{Fr}(X/T)$ for the set of all frames on $X/T$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:frame-torsor}
For any family of genus $g>1$ super Riemann surfaces
$\pi:X\to T$, its framing sheaf $\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}$
is a $G_T$-torsor on the big Zariski site of $T$ and is
represented by a smooth superscheme of finite type over $T$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We need to check that $\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}$ satisfies the two conditions
of a $G_T$-torsor on the big Zariski site.

\medskip
\emph{Locally non-empty:}
We must show that for every morphism $f:S\to T$ there exists
a Zariski cover $\bigcup_i S_i=S$ such that
$\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}(S_i)=\operatorname{Fr}(X_{S_i}/S_i)\neq\emptyset$. This is equivalent to giving an open cover of $S$ on which 
the vector bundle $f^*\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{X_S/S}$ is trivial, which exists because the bundle is locally free.
\medskip

\emph{$G_T$ acts freely and transitively:}
Suppose $S\to T$ is a morphism for which
$\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}(S)\neq\emptyset$.
Let $\rho\in\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}(S)$, and suppose that $\rho\cdot g=\rho$
for some $g\in G_T(S)$.
Choose local representatives $(S_i,\rho_i)$ of $\rho$ and lifts
$\tilde{g}_i\in\operatorname{GL}(V)(S_i)$ of $g\vert_{S_i}$. 
Since $\rho\cdot g=\rho$, there are even invertible functions $\lambda_i$ on $S_i$ such that
\[
\rho_i\circ\tilde{g}_i=\lambda_i\rho_i.
\]
Composing with $\rho_i^{-1}$ gives
$\tilde{g}_i=\lambda_i\operatorname{id}_{V_{S_i}}$.
Hence $g\vert_{S_i}=\operatorname{id}$ for every $i$, and thus $g=\operatorname{id}$ on $S$.

For transitivity, let $\rho,\rho'\in\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}(S)$, and choose representatives
$(S_i,\rho_i)$ and $(S_i,\rho_i')$.
Set
$\tilde{g}_i:=\rho_i'^{-1}\circ\rho_i
\in\operatorname{Aut}(V_{S_i})$, and let $g_i\in G_T(S_i)$
be its projective class. 
Changing the representatives multiplies $\tilde{g}_i$
by an even invertible scalar, so it does not change $g_i$.

On $S_i\cap S_j$, write
$\rho_i=\lambda_{ij}\rho_j$ and
$\rho_i'=\lambda_{ij}'\rho_j'$, where
$\lambda_{ij}$ and $\lambda_{ij}'$ are even invertible
functions. Then
\[
\tilde{g}_i
=
(\lambda_{ij}')^{-1}\lambda_{ij}\tilde{g}_j.
\]
Thus $g_i$ and $g_j$ agree on $S_i\cap S_j$ and glue to
$g\in G_T(S)$.
The identities $\rho_i'\circ\tilde{g}_i=\rho_i$ give
$\rho'\cdot g=\rho$.

\medskip
For representability, take $S=T$ and choose a Zariski cover
$\bigcup_i T_i=T$ with frames $\rho_i\in\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}(T_i)$.
Freeness and transitivity give isomorphisms of sheaves
\[
\theta_i:G\times T_i\xrightarrow{\sim}\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}\vert_{T_i}.
\]


Write $T_{ij}:=T_i\cap T_j$.
On $T_{ij}$, let $g_{ji}\in G(T_{ij})$ be the unique
section satisfying $\rho_j\cdot g_{ji}=\rho_i$.
The transition map $\theta_j^{-1}\circ\theta_i$ is
left multiplication by $g_{ji}$, hence is an isomorphism
of superschemes
$\psi_{ji}:G\times T_{ij}\to G\times T_{ij}$.
On $T_i\cap T_j\cap T_k$, we have
\[
\rho_k\cdot(g_{kj}g_{ji})
=
\rho_j\cdot g_{ji}
=
\rho_i
=
\rho_k\cdot g_{ki}.
\]
Freeness gives $g_{kj}g_{ji}=g_{ki}$, so
$\psi_{kj}\circ\psi_{ji}=\psi_{ki}$.
Also $\psi_{ii}=\operatorname{id}$ and $\psi_{ij}=\psi_{ji}^{-1}$.

The superschemes $G\times T_i$ therefore glue along the
maps $\psi_{ji}$ to a superscheme $Y\to T$.
The identities $\theta_j\circ\psi_{ji}=\theta_i$ show that
the $\theta_i$ glue to an isomorphism
$\operatorname{Hom}_T(-,Y)\cong\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}$.
Thus $Y$ represents $\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}$.

Finally, each restriction $Y\times_T T_i\to T_i$ is
isomorphic to $G\times T_i\to T_i$, which is smooth and
of finite type because $G$ is smooth and of finite type.
Both properties are Zariski local on the target, so
$Y\to T$ is smooth and of finite type.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop: P is DM}
The morphism $F$ is smooth, of finite type, and representable
by superschemes. In particular, $P$ is a smooth
Deligne--Mumford superstack of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We must check that for every morphism $T\to\mathfrak{M}_g$ from a
superscheme $T$, the base change $P_T:=P\times_{\mathfrak{M}_g}T$
is represented by a superscheme smooth and of finite type
over $T$. We will show that $P_T$ is naturally equivalent
to the framing sheaf $\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}$ of the family $X/T$
classified by $T\to\mathfrak{M}_g$, viewed as a stack with only
identity arrows.

For any $S\to T$, $P_T(S)$ is the groupoid whose objects
are triples $(Y/S,\rho,\alpha)$ consisting of a framed
super Riemann surface $(Y/S,\rho)$ and a superconformal
isomorphism $\alpha:X_S\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} Y$ over $S$.
Define
\[
\Phi_S:P_T(S)\longrightarrow\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}(S),
\qquad
(Y/S,\rho,\alpha)\longmapsto\alpha^*\rho.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Essentially surjective.}
For any $\rho'\in\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}(S)$, the object
$(X_S/S,\rho',\operatorname{id}_{X_S})$ maps to $\rho'$.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Fully faithful.}
A morphism from $(Y/S,\rho,\alpha)$ to
$(Y'/S,\rho',\alpha')$ is an isomorphism
$\phi:(Y/S,\rho)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}(Y'/S,\rho')$ of framed super
Riemann surfaces satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{expression for phi}
\phi\circ\alpha=\alpha'
\iff
\phi=\alpha'\circ\alpha{}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Thus there is at most one such morphism.
The superconformal isomorphism
$\phi=\alpha'\circ\alpha{}^{-1}$ preserves the frames
precisely when $\phi^*\rho'=\rho$.
Since $\alpha^*$ is a bijection on frames, we have
\[
\phi^*\rho'=\rho
\iff
\alpha^*(\phi^*\rho')=\alpha^*\rho
\iff
\alpha'^*\rho'=\alpha^*\rho.
\]
Consequently, the morphism set between these two objects
is a singleton exactly when their images under $\Phi_S$
are equal, and is empty otherwise. This proves full
faithfulness.


By Lemma~\ref{lem:frame-torsor}, $\underline{\operatorname{Fr}}_{X/T}$ is represented
by a superscheme smooth and of finite type over $T$.
This proves the assertions about $F$.

Finally, an \'etale atlas $U\to\mathfrak{M}_g$ pulls back to an
\'etale atlas $P\times_{\mathfrak{M}_g}U\to P$ by a superscheme.
Hence $P$ is Deligne--Mumford. Its smoothness and finite
type over $\mathbb{C}$ follow by composing $F$ with the smooth
finite-type structure morphism of $\mathfrak{M}_g$.

\label{P is alg}

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop: P is DM}, it suffices to show that every automorphism of a framed family is the identity.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:bosonic-monomorphism}
The morphism
\[
I_{\operatorname{bos}}:P_{\operatorname{bos}}\longrightarrow Q'_{\operatorname{bos}}
\]
is a monomorphism of algebraic spaces.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $T$ be an ordinary scheme, and suppose
\[
(\mathcal{Z}_{\rho},d\psi_{\rho}(D))
=(\mathcal{Z}_{\rho'},d\psi_{\rho'}(D'))
\]
in $Q'(T)$. The equality $\mathcal{Z}_{\rho}=\mathcal{Z}_{\rho'}$ defines an isomorphism
\[
\phi:=\psi_{\rho'}{}^{-1}\circ\psi_{\rho}:X\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} X'.
\]
The equality of the transported distributions makes $\phi$ superconformal, and the factorizations of $j_{\rho}$ and $j_{\rho'}$ give
\[
j_{\rho}=j_{\rho'}\circ\phi.
\]

We must show that $\phi^*\rho'=\rho$. This can be checked Zariski locally on $T$, so choose local linear representatives of both frames, and denote their evaluation maps by $q_{\rho}$ and $q_{\rho'}$.

To compare the frames, we express the equality of embeddings in terms of these evaluation maps. By the quotient description of $\mathbb{P}(V)$, the equality $j_{\rho}=j_{\rho'}\circ\phi$ gives a unique isomorphism $\lambda:\phi^*\mathcal{L}'\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{L}$ satisfying
\[
\lambda\circ\phi^* q_{\rho'}=q_{\rho}.
\]
The evaluation map corresponding to the pulled-back frame is $\gamma_{\phi}\circ\phi^* q_{\rho'}$, where $\gamma_{\phi}:\phi^*\mathcal{L}'\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{L}$ is the canonical isomorphism. Thus $\phi^*\rho'=\rho$ if and only if
\[
\lambda=\pi^*(a)\gamma_{\phi}
\]
for some $a\in\Gamma_T^*:=H^0(T,\mathcal{O}_T)^{\times}$.

The automorphism $\gamma_{\phi}\circ\lambda{}^{-1}$ of $\mathcal{L}$ is multiplication by an element
\[
s\in\Gamma_X^*:=H^0(X,\mathcal{O}_X)_{\overline{0}}^{\times}.
\]
The framing condition is therefore equivalent to $s$ lying in the image of $\pi^*:\Gamma_T^*\to\Gamma_X^*$.

Let $C=X_{\operatorname{bos}}$, and let $J$ be the odd ideal of $\mathcal{O}_X$. Since $T$ is ordinary and $X/T$ has relative dimension $1|1$, we have $J^2=0$ and the canonical decomposition
\[
\mathcal{O}_X=\mathcal{O}_C\oplus J,
\qquad
(\mathcal{O}_X)_{\overline{0}}=\mathcal{O}_C.
\]
Moreover, $C\to T$ is a smooth proper family of geometrically connected curves, so $(\pi|_C)_*\mathcal{O}_C=\mathcal{O}_T$. It follows that pullback gives an isomorphism
\[
\pi^*:
H^0(T,\mathcal{O}_T)^{\times}
\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} H^0(X,\mathcal{O}_X)_{\overline{0}}^{\times}.
\]
Thus $s=\pi^*(a)$ for some $a\in\Gamma_T^*$, and $\lambda=\pi^*(a^{-1})\gamma_{\phi}$. Hence $\phi$ preserves the frames locally, and therefore globally.

Consequently, equality of the images in $Q'(T)$ implies that the two objects are isomorphic in $P(T)$. Since this holds for every ordinary scheme $T$, $I_{\operatorname{bos}}$ is a monomorphism.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{slice is kuranishi}
The family \eqref{kuranishi} is a Kuranishi family for $X$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
We first note that the base $U$ has the correct dimension: $\dim(U)=3g-3|2g-2$. Indeed, this follows from the equivalence $\mathfrak{M}_g\cong[P/G]$, together with the isomorphism $T_p \frak{o}(p) \cong T_e G$ which follows from $T_e \operatorname{Stab}_G(p)=0$.  

We also note that the differential of the restricted action $
a|_{G\times U}:G\times U\to P
$
is invertible at $(e,p)$. Indeed,
\[
da_{e,p}:T_eG\oplus T_pU\to T_pP,
\qquad
(\xi,v)\mapsto d(a(-,p))_e(\xi)+v,
\]
and its two summands map isomorphically onto the complementary subspaces
$T_p\frak{o}(p)$ and $T_pU$.
The inverse function theorem gives open neighborhoods $W\subset G$ of $e$ and $V\subset P$ of $p$ such that
\[
a|_{W\times U}:W\times U\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} V.
\]

Let $(\eta:Y\to(S,s_0),\psi)$ be a deformation of $X$. On a neighborhood $S'$ of $s_0$, choose a frame $\rho_Y$ satisfying
\[
\psi^*(\rho_Y|_{s_0})=\rho.
\]
The corresponding map $\alpha:S'\to P$ sends $s_0$ to $p$.

                                                                             

After shrinking $S'$, assume that $\alpha(S')\subset V$, and write
\[
(g,f):=(a|_{W\times U})^{-1}\circ\alpha:
S'\to W\times U.
\]
Then $g(s_0)=e$, $f(s_0)=p$, and $\alpha=a(g,f)$. Thus the two framed families classified by $\alpha$ and $a(g,f)$ admit a unique framed isomorphism
\[
\theta:(Y|_{S'},\rho_Y)
\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}(f^*\mathcal{X},f^*\rho_U\circ g).
\]
At $s_0$, both $\theta_{s_0}\circ\psi$ and $\phi$ are framed isomorphisms from $(X,\rho)$ to $(\mathcal{X}_p,\rho_U|_p)$. Since framed objects have no non-trivial automorphisms,
\[
\theta_{s_0}\circ\psi=\phi.
\]
Set $F:=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\theta:Y|_{S'}\to\mathcal{X}$. Then $(f,F)$ is the required cartesian pair.

For uniqueness, first replace $\rho_Y$ by another extending frame $\rho_Y'$ with the same restriction to the central fiber. The torsor property gives a unique map $h:S'\to G$ satisfying
\[
\rho_Y'=\rho_Y\circ h,
\qquad
h(s_0)=e.
\]
Its classifying map is
\[
\alpha'=a(h,\alpha)=a(gh,f).
\]
After shrinking $S'$ so that $gh$ takes values in $W$, the inverse of $a|_{W\times U}$ therefore gives the pair $(gh,f)$. In particular, the base map $f$ is unchanged.

The isomorphism constructed from $\rho_Y'$ satisfies

The diagonal morphism is representable if for every map $(f,g): S \to \mathfrak{T}_g \times \mathfrak{T}_g$ with corresponding marked families denoted $(X\to S, [f_s])$ and $(Y\to S, [g_s])$, the marked isomorphism functor

\]
is representable by a complex analytic superspace. 

It is known that the superconformal isomorphism functor 
\[
I:=\underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{\mathfrak{M}_g(S)}(X,Y): \operatorname{sSch}/S \to \operatorname{Set}
\]
is a
finite and unramified complex analytic superspace over $S$ \cite[Proposition~4.3]{CV}, and so it suffices to prove that the obvious inclusion $I' \subset I$ is representable by open immersions. That is, for an $S$-space $j: T \to S$ and every map $T \to I$, the base change map $p: I_T':=I' \times_I T \to T$ is an open immersion.

\[
U:=\{t\in|T|:[g_{j(t)} \circ \alpha_{j(t)}]=[f_{j(t)}]\}.
\]
Note that $U$ is open: Indeed, for every $u_0 \in U$ the fact that the two markings $[g_{j(u_0)} \circ \alpha_{j(u_0)}]$ and $[f_{j(u_0)}]$ agree implies that they agree on some  
 path-connected open neighborhood of $u_0$, and this open neighborhood is contained in $U$ by definition. 

Let $T'$ be an analytic superspace admitting a map $\beta': T' \to I_T'$ and set $\beta= p \circ \beta': T' \to T$.  Then by definition of $I_T'$, $\beta^* \alpha: X_{T'} \cong Y_{T'}$ is marking-preserving, so

By definition of $U$, $\beta(t') \in U$, and thus $\beta$ factors through $U$. 
Conversely, suppose that a map $\beta: T' \to T$ factors through $U$, then $\beta^* \alpha$ satisfies the above equality which is exactly the marking-preserving condition, and so $\beta$ lifts to a map $\beta': T' \to I'$.

        \xrightarrow{\sim}(L_0,\alpha_0).
    \] 
    This extension is essentially unique in the sense that any other extension agrees with it after restriction to some common neighborhood. 

    \item \emph{Extension of isomorphisms.}
    Let $(L,\alpha_L)$ and $(M,\alpha_M)$ be continuous spin structures

    \[
        \gamma_0:M|_{Y_0}\xrightarrow{\sim}L|_{Y_0}

    continuous isomorphism of families of spin curves
    \[
        \gamma:M|_{Y_U}\xrightarrow{\sim}L|_{Y_U}.
    \]

    The extension $\gamma$ is essentially unique. Furthermore, the same holds when $\pi:Y\to S$ is an analytic
    family of Riemann surfaces over a complex analytic space $S$,
    and the two spin structures are analytic. The extension $\gamma$
    is then analytic.

    \item \emph{Extension of complex structure.}
    Let $X$ be a complex manifold, let $N$ be a holomorphic line
    bundle on $X$, and let $L$ be a continuous complex line bundle
    on $X$, equipped with an isomorphism of continuous line bundles
    \[
        \alpha:L^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\sim}N.
    \]
    Then $L$ admits a unique holomorphic structure for which
    $\alpha$ is holomorphic.

    In particular, if $\pi:Y\to S$ is an analytic family of Riemann
    surfaces with $Y$ and $S$ complex manifolds, every continuous
    spin structure
    \[
        \alpha:L^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\sim}\omega_{Y/S}
    \]
    acquires a unique analytic spin structure compatible with
    $\alpha$. Its restriction to each fiber agrees with the
    original holomorphic spin structure on that fiber.

One applies statement~(2) on $K$ to the spin structures $L$ and $F^*M$.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{teich theorem for spin} The classifying map 
 $B \to ST_g'$ of the family in \eqref{cont fams of marked spins} is surjective, representable and \'etale.

We first prove that $B \to ST_g'$ is representable. The arguments used to prove representability also show that the map is \'etale. 
It suffices to prove that the component maps $\mathbb{B}_{\sigma} \to  ST_g'$ are representable. 
\medskip 

For $S \to ST_g'$ let $(Y \to S, M)$ denote the corresponding continuous family of marked spin curves. Set $E:= \mathbb{B}_{\sigma} \times_{ST_g'} S$, and let $p:E \to S$ and $q: E \to \mathbb{B}_{\sigma}$ denote the natural projection maps. 

Fix a point $e \in E$, and let $(b=q(e), \ s= p(e), \ \theta_e: (K_{\sigma})_b \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} Y_s)$ denote the corresponding object, where $\theta_e$ is an isomorphism of marked spin curves. 

We claim that there exist an open neighborhood $U \subset S$ containing $s$, a map $f:U \to \mathbb{B}_{\sigma}$, and an isomorphism
\[ F: f^* K_{\sigma} \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} Y \vert_U\]
of continuous families of marked spin curves over $U$ such that $F_s= \theta_e$. 

Let $c$ denote the composition $S \to ST_g' \to T_g'=|T_g|$ and let $\Phi_{\sigma}: \mathbb{B}_{\sigma} \to T_g'=|T_g|$ be the Teichm\"uller homeomorphism, and let $C_{\sigma} \to \mathbb{B}_{\sigma} $ be the corresponding continuous family of Riemann surfaces. Set
\[ f:= \Phi_{\sigma} {}^{-1} \circ c\]
and note that $f(s)=b$ since $c(s)=\Phi_{\sigma}(b)$ as elements in $T_g'$. Indeed, these correspond to $(C_{\sigma})_b$ (the continuous curves underlying $(K_{\sigma})_b$) and $Y_s$ respectively, and we know these are isomorphic via $\theta_e$. 
Indeed, $\theta_e$ is an isomorphism of spins so in particular one of the underlying curves. 
By the same logic, for every $t \in S$, the identity $c(t)=\Phi_{\sigma}(f(t))$ implies that there exists an isomorphism
\[ F_t: (C_{\sigma})_{f(t)} \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} Y_t \]
of marked Riemann surfaces, and since these isomorphisms are unique, they glue to a global isomorphism of continuous families of marked Riemann surfaces
\[ F: f^* C_{\sigma} \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} Y, \qquad F \vert_s = F_s\]
over $S$ by \cite[Theorem~5.6]{AC}. By Lemma~\ref{the extension lemmas}, there exists an open neighborhood $U \subset S$ of $s$ and an isomorphism of continuous families of marked spin curves
\[ \Theta: (f|_U)^* K_{\sigma} \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} Y|U, \qquad \Theta_s= \theta_e\]
and the underlying continuous isomorphism is $F|_U$. Redefine $f:= f |_U:U \to \mathbb{B}_{\sigma}$, and let 
\[\psi: U \to E\]

We will now prove that $\psi$ is representable by an open immersion. That is, for every $\tau: T \to E$ we will show that there exists an open subset $W \subset T$ which represents $U \times_E T$. Let $a$ denote the composition $T \xrightarrow{\tau} E \xrightarrow{p} S$, and define
\[ W:= \{ t \in a{}^{-1}(U) \mid \tau(t) = \psi(a_U(t)) \}  \subset T \times_S U\]

We first show that $W$ is open. Fix $t_0 \in W$, and let 
\[ G: (q \circ \tau) K_{\sigma} \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} (p \circ \tau)Y\]
be the isomorphism picked out by $\tau$, and 
\[ a_U^* \Theta: a_U^* f^* K_{\sigma} \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} a_U^*(Y \vert_U)\]
the pullback of the isomorphism $\Theta$ picked out by $\psi$. 
Then by definition of $W$, $G_{t_0} = (a_U^*\Theta)_{t_0}$, and by uniqueness of extension of isomorphisms of marked spin curves on the fibers, there must exist some open subset $N$ containing $t_0$ such that $G \vert_N = (a_U^*\Theta) \vert_N$, and hence $N \subset W$. We now verify that $W$ represents $U \times_E T$. Indeed, a map $h: R \to T$ lifts to $R \to U \times_E T$ if and only if $R$ admits a map $u: R \to U$ satisfying $\tau \circ h= \psi \circ u$. The definition of $W$ and the fiber product $E=S \times_{ST_g'} \mathbb{B}_{\sigma}$ then immediately imply that the map $h$ must factor through $W$. 

The next step is to prove that $E$ is representable by a topological manifold. Note that once we have established this, we have also proved that the map $E \to S$ is \'etale. Indeed, we have shown that for every $e \in E$, there exists an open subset $V:=\psi(U) \subset E$ which $p$ maps isomorphically onto the open subset $U \subset S$. 
We will use the following descent statement from the open-gluing and Hausdorff criteria in \cite[Theorems~1.1 and~1.3]{conradGluing}: A sheaf $E$ covered by open immersions $\psi_e: U_e \to E$ is represented by a topological manifold if it admits a countable subcover and each canonical map
\[ \rho:=(\operatorname{pr}_1, \operatorname{pr}_2): R \to U_e \cap U_{e'}=U_e \times_S U_{e'}, \]
where $R:=U_e \times_{\psi_e, E, \psi_{e'}} U_{e'}$ denotes the fiber product in the diagram 
\[ \begin{tikzcd}

    U_{e'} \arrow[r,"\psi_{e'}"] & E,
\end{tikzcd}\]
is a closed immersion. 

First note that both $\operatorname{pr}_1$ and $\operatorname{pr}_2$ are open immersions, since they are base changes of the open immersions $\psi_{e'}$ and $\psi_e$ respectively. Set $O:= \operatorname{pr}_2(R) \subset U_{e'}$, and let $W_{ee'}:= j{}^{-1}(O)$, where
$j: U_e \cap U_{e'} \to U_{e'}$ is the inclusion map. The map
\[ j \vert_{W_{ee'}}: W_{ee'} \to O\]
is a homeomorphism, and hence  $\rho: R \to W_{ee'}$ is a homeomorphism. 

We are left to show that $W_{ee'}$ is closed. Let 
\[ f_e: U_e \to \mathbb{B}_{\sigma}, \qquad f_{e'}: U_{e'} \to \mathbb{B}_{\sigma'}\] 
and note that $U_e \cap U_{e'} = \emptyset$ if $\sigma \neq \sigma'$, so we may assume that $\sigma=\sigma'$. Then clearly 
\[ f_e \vert_{U_e \cap U_{e'}} = f_{e'} \vert_{U_e \cap U_{e'}}\]
since  $f_e:= \Phi_{\sigma} {}^{-1} \circ c \circ i_{U_e}$ and $f_{e'}:= \Phi_{\sigma} {}^{-1} \circ c \circ i_{U_{e'}}$. Let us denote the common map by
\[f: U_e \cap U_{e'} \to \mathbb{B}_{\sigma},\]
and let $Z= f^* C_{\sigma} \to U_e \cap U_{e'}$ be the underlying continuous family of marked curves.  
Recall the isomorphisms of continuous families of marked Riemann surfaces underlying $\Theta_e$ and $\Theta_{e'}$: 
\[ F_e: f_e^* C_{\sigma} \to Y, \qquad F_{e'}: f_{e'}^* C_{\sigma} \to Y. \]
Since isomorphisms of marked Riemann surfaces are unique, $F_e \vert_{U_e \cap U_{e'}}= F_{e'} \vert_{U_e \cap U_{e'}}: Z \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} Y \vert_{U_e \cap U_{e'}}$.   

Write $V:=U_e\cap U_{e'}$, let $\pi:Z\to V$ be the projection, and denote the common underlying isomorphism by
\[
    F:Z\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} Y_V, \qquad Y_V:=Y\vert_V.
\]
Let $(L,\alpha_L)$ denote the spin structure on $Z$ belonging to $f^*K_{\sigma}$, and write $M_V:=M\vert_{Y_V}$. The restrictions of $\Theta_e$ and $\Theta_{e'}$ to $V$ determine isomorphisms of line bundles on $Z$
\[
    \gamma_e,\gamma_{e'}:F^*M_V\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} L
\]
compatible with the spin structures. Their ratio
\[
    h:=\gamma_{e'}\circ\gamma_e^{-1}:L\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} L
\]
is therefore an automorphism of the spin structure $(L,\alpha_L)$. Since $L$ is a line bundle, there is a unique continuous, fiberwise holomorphic function $u:Z\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}$ such that $h=u\,\operatorname{id}_L$. Compatibility with $\alpha_L$ gives $u^2=1$, so $u$ is locally constant with values $1$ and $-1$.

Define the open subset
\[
    D:=\{z\in Z:u(z)\neq 1\}\subset Z.
\]
We claim that
\[
    V\setminus W_{ee'}=\pi(D).
\]
Indeed, for $s\in V$, the objects $\psi_e(s)$ and $\psi_{e'}(s)$ have the same base point $s$, the same point $f(s)\in\mathbb{B}_{\sigma}$, and the same underlying marked-curve isomorphism $F_s$. Thus, writing $Z_s:=\pi^{-1}(s)$, the definition of the fiber product gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s\in W_{ee'}
    &\Longleftrightarrow \psi_e(s)=\psi_{e'}(s)\\
    &\Longleftrightarrow \gamma_e\vert_{Z_s}=\gamma_{e'}\vert_{Z_s}\\
    &\Longleftrightarrow u\vert_{Z_s}\equiv 1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently, $s\notin W_{ee'}$ if and only if there exists $z\in Z_s$ with $u(z)\neq 1$, which is precisely the condition $s\in\pi(D)$. Since $Z_s$ is connected, in this case $u\vert_{Z_s}\equiv -1$.

Finally, the projection $\pi:Z\to V$ is open: the local product charts in the definition of a continuous family identify its restrictions with projections from products, which are open maps. Hence $\pi(D)$ is open in $V$. The displayed equality shows that $W_{ee'}$ is closed in $V=U_e\cap U_{e'}$. Under the identification $R\cong W_{ee'}$ established above, the map $\rho:R\to U_e\cap U_{e'}$ is therefore a closed immersion, as required.

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:analytic-spin-atlas}
The map \eqref{analytic spin atlas} is surjective, representable and \'etale.
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Representability follows from the representable diagonal of the analytic Deligne--Mumford stack $ST_g$. We prove \'etaleness componentwise. Given an analytic family $\mathcal{Y}=(Y\to S,M,\alpha_M)$ classified by $S\to ST_g$, set $E:=\mathbb{B}_{\sigma}^{\operatorname{an}}\times_{ST_g}S$, with projections $p:E\to S$ and $q:E\to\mathbb{B}_{\sigma}^{\operatorname{an}}$.

Fix $e=(s,b,\theta_e)\in E$, where $s=p(e)$, $b=q(e)$ and $\theta_e:(K_\sigma)_b\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{Y}_s$. If $c:S\to T_g$ is the analytic classifying map of the underlying marked curve family, then
\[
    f:=(\Phi_\sigma^{\operatorname{an}})^{-1}\circ c:S\to\mathbb{B}_{\sigma}^{\operatorname{an}}
\]
is analytic and satisfies $f(s)=b$. We obtain an isomorphism $F:f^*C_\sigma^{\operatorname{an}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} Y$ of families of marked curves over $S$ whose restriction at $s$ is  $\theta_e$. By Lemma~\ref{the extension lemmas} we can find an open neighborhood $U\subset S$ of $s$, and an isomorphism
\[
    \Theta:(f\vert_U)^*K_\sigma\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{Y}\vert_U,
    \qquad \Theta_s=\theta_e.
\]
of marked families of spin curves. 
The triple $(i_U,f\vert_U,\Theta)$ defines $\psi_e:U\to E$, with $i_U:U\hookrightarrow S$ the inclusion and $p\circ\psi_e=i_U$. The same open-immersion argument as in Theorem~\ref{teich theorem for spin} can then be used to show that each $\psi_e$ is an open immersion, and thus $E \to S$ is \'etale.  Finally, the surjectivity in Theorem~\ref{teich theorem for spin}, together with these local analytic lifts, proves surjectivity of \eqref{analytic spin atlas}.

The super Teichm\"uller stack $\mathfrak{T}_g$ admits an \'etale atlas
