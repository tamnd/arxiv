\label{prop:onestep}
Let $\epsilon=(\epsilon^u,\epsilon^y)$ be a random vector with finite
second moments $\Sigma_{uu}=\mathbb{E}[\epsilon^u\epsilon^{u\top}]$,
$\Sigma_{uy}=\mathbb{E}[\epsilon^u\epsilon^{y\top}]=\Sigma_{yu}^\top$
and $\Sigma_{yy}=\mathbb{E}[\epsilon^y\epsilon^{y\top}]$, let
$\gamma\ge0$ be such that $\Sigma_{yy}+\gamma I$ is invertible, and let
$L=\Sigma_{uy}M$ with $M=(\Sigma_{yy}+\gamma I)^{-1}$. Then
\begin{align*}
    &\mathbb{E}\|\epsilon^u\|^2-\mathbb{E}\|\epsilon^u-L\epsilon^y\|^2\\
    &\qquad=
    \operatorname{tr}\bigl[\Sigma_{uy}M(\Sigma_{yy}+2\gamma I)M\Sigma_{yu}\bigr]
    \ \ge\ 0.
\end{align*}
For $\gamma=0$, $L$ minimizes $\mathbb{E}\|\epsilon^u-L'\epsilon^y\|^2$
over all matrices $L'$, and the second moment of $\epsilon^u-L\epsilon^y$
is the Schur complement $\Sigma_{uu}-\Sigma_{uy}\Sigma_{yy}^{-1}\Sigma_{yu}$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For a matrix $L'$ let
$F(L')=\mathbb{E}\|\epsilon^u-L'\epsilon^y\|^2
=\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{uu}-2\operatorname{tr}(L'\Sigma_{yu})
+\operatorname{tr}(L'\Sigma_{yy}L'^{\top})$.
With $L=\Sigma_{uy}M$ and $M=(\Sigma_{yy}+\gamma I)^{-1}$,
\begin{equation*}
    F(0)-F(L)
    =
    \operatorname{tr}\bigl[\Sigma_{uy}(2M-M\Sigma_{yy}M)\Sigma_{yu}\bigr],
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
    2M-M\Sigma_{yy}M
       
                                                       
    =
    M(\Sigma_{yy}+2\gamma I)M,
\end{equation*}
which gives the stated expression. Since $M$ is symmetric and
$\Sigma_{yy}+2\gamma I$ is positive semidefinite,
$F(0)-F(L)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl[(M\Sigma_{yu})^{\top}(\Sigma_{yy}+2\gamma I)(M\Sigma_{yu})\bigr]\ge0$.
For $\gamma=0$, $F$ is a convex quadratic function of $L'$ with
gradient $-2\Sigma_{uy}+2L'\Sigma_{yy}$, which vanishes exactly at
$L=\Sigma_{uy}\Sigma_{yy}^{-1}$; the second moment of
$\epsilon^u-L\epsilon^y$ is
$\Sigma_{uu}-L\Sigma_{yu}-\Sigma_{uy}L^{\top}+L\Sigma_{yy}L^{\top}
=\Sigma_{uu}-\Sigma_{uy}\Sigma_{yy}^{-1}\Sigma_{yu}$.
