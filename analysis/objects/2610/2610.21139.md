\label{def:contraction-routine}
Consider an optimization subroutine
$
\mathsf{Contract}(y,R;\mathrm{SO}_\lambda),
$
which takes an initial point $y$, a target radius $R$, and a stochastic oracle $\mathrm{SO}_\lambda$ as inputs. Associated with the subroutine are a bias-admissibility condition $\mathsf{Adm}_{\rm C}(R,\mathcal{B}_\lambda)$ and an oracle complexity bound $T(R,\lambda)$.
          
    
                                      
                 
               
    
We say that $\mathsf{Contract}$ is a valid distance-contraction subroutine if, whenever
\[
\mathbb{E}\|y-x^*\|^2\le R^2
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathsf{Adm}_{\rm C}(R,\mathcal{B}_\lambda)\text{ holds},
\]
it uses at most $T(R,\lambda)$ calls to $\mathrm{SO}_\lambda$ and returns $y^+$ satisfying $\mathbb{E}\|y^+-x^*\|^2\le \frac{R^2}{2}.$

\label{thm:sge}
Let $\{x_t\}$ be generated by Algorithm~\ref{alg:sge}. 
Set $\alpha_t=\frac{t-1}{t}$, $\tau_t=\frac{t-1}{3}$, $\eta_t=\frac{\eta}{t}$ for $t\le2$, and $\eta_t=\frac{\eta}{t}+\frac{\mu(t-3)}{2}$ for $t\ge3$, where

\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}[f(x_k)-f^*]
\le &\left(
\tfrac{72L}{k(k+2)}
+\tfrac{162\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}}{mk(k+2)}
+\tfrac{486\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}^2}{\mu m^2k(k+2)}
\right)\|x_0-x^*\|^2 + \tfrac{2}{9k(k+2)}[f(x_0)-f^*]+\tfrac{192\sigma_*^2}{\mu m(k+2)}
+\tfrac{216\mathcal{B}^2}{\mu}\nonumber\\
\le&
\left(
\tfrac{73L}{k(k+2)}
+\tfrac{162\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}}{mk(k+2)}
+\tfrac{486\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}^2}{\mu m^2k(k+2)}
\right)\|x_0-x^*\|^2 +\tfrac{192\sigma_*^2}{\mu m(k+2)}
+\tfrac{216\mathcal{B}^2}{\mu}. \label{eq:sge-rate}
\end{align}
In particular,  for $m = 1$ and $k \geq \max \{98\sqrt{\tfrac{L}{\mu}}, 144\sqrt{\tfrac{\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}}{\mu}}, \tfrac{102\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}}{\mu}, \tfrac{3072\sigma_*^2}{\mu^2\|x_0-x^*\|^2}\}$, we have
\begin{align}\label{sge_contraction}
\mathbb{E}\|x_k - x^*\|^2 \leq \tfrac{2}{\mu}\mathbb{E}[f(x_k)-f^*] \leq \tfrac{1}{4}\|x_0-x^*\|^2 + \tfrac{432 \mathcal{B}^2}{\mu^2}.
\end{align}

<!-- head-1: body_md -->
To prove the claim, we need to control the state-dependent
variance, and also bound the error introduced by the persistent
oracle bias. We begin with the SGE recursion stated in Proposition \ref{prop:general_convergence}, whose proof follows from a similar analysis of SGE as in \cite{ilandarideva2025state} (see Appendix \ref{subsec:proof_of_proposition}).
With this recursion, we can handle the state-dependent variance and systematic bias transparently. Our parameter choices ensure that the state-dependent variance
terms can be absorbed into the converging function-value terms in this
recursion. Taking the telescoping sum over the recursions yields
\[
A_k \mathbb{E}[f(x_k)-f^\ast]
+\frac{P_k}{2}\mathbb{E}\|z_k-x^\ast\|^2
\leq
\Gamma_k+B\sum_{t=1}^k(3t-2)\mathbb{E}\|z_t-x^\ast\|,
\]
where $A_k$ and $P_k$ grow quadratically in $k$, and $\Gamma_k$
contains the initial-error and residual-variance terms. To bound the accumulated bias, observe that the same inequality
controls the distance of each $z_t$ from $x^\ast$. Thus, the
distances appearing on the right-hand side can themselves be
bounded in terms of the accumulated error. Applying Young's
inequality provides a bound of the form
$2\Gamma_k+O(k^2B^2/\mu)$, and dividing both sides of the inequality by $A_k$ yields the stated
function-value bound. By further using the strong convexity property, we can obtain the
distance-contraction guarantee. The full proof is deferred to Appendix~\ref{sec:proof_sge_thm}.\qed

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{corollary_known}
Suppose all oracles are unbiased, i.e., $B_\lambda = 0,~\forall \lambda$. Define $A_\lambda=c_\lambda \max \left\{98\sqrt{\frac{L}{\mu}}, 144\sqrt{\tfrac{\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}_\lambda}{\mu}}, \tfrac{102\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}_\lambda}{\mu}\right\}$ and $B_\lambda=c_\lambda \cdot \frac{3072\sigma^2_{\lambda,*}}{\mu^2R_0^2}$. For $\epsilon >0$, let $K=\lceil\log_2(R_0^2/\epsilon)\rceil$. Then the total costs of  Algorithm \ref{alg:known} and using the best-fixed oracle throughout satisfy
\begin{align}\label{total_cost_results}
\mathrm{Cost}_{\rm adap}(\epsilon)&=\sum_{s=1}^K\min_\lambda\max\{A_\lambda,B_\lambda2^{s-1}\}, \nonumber\\
\mathrm{Cost}_{\rm fixed}(\epsilon)&=\min_\lambda\sum_{s=1}^K\max\{A_\lambda,B_\lambda2^{s-1}\} = \mathcal{O}\left( \min_{\lambda} \left\{c_\lambda \cdot \left(\sqrt{\tfrac{L}{\mu}}\log_2\tfrac{R_0^2}{\epsilon} +\tfrac{\ensuremath{\mathcal{L}}_\lambda}{\mu}\log_2\tfrac{R_0^2}{\epsilon} + \tfrac{\sigma_{\lambda,*}^2}{\mu^2 \epsilon}\right)\right\}  \right),
\end{align}
hence $\mathrm{Cost}_{\rm adap}(\epsilon)\le\mathrm{Cost}_{\rm fixed}(\epsilon)$. 
Moreover, there exist instances for which $ \frac{\mathrm{Cost}_{\rm fixed}(\epsilon)}
     {\mathrm{Cost}_{\rm adap}(\epsilon)}= \Theta\!\left(\tfrac{K}{\log K}\right) = \Theta\!\left(
\tfrac{\log(R_0^2/\epsilon)}
{\log\log(R_0^2/\epsilon)}
\right) $, and this order is tight for stage costs of the form
$\max\{A_\lambda,B_\lambda2^{s-1}\}$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- head-2: body_md -->
The comparison with the best fixed oracle follows directly
from minimizing the cost at each stage: the sum of the
stagewise minima is no larger than the minimum of the sums.
It remains to show how large this improvement can be.

We first construct two oracles. The first has a smaller
coarse-scale cost but a larger residual-variance term, whereas
the second has a constant stage cost of $M$ throughout the
optimization horizon. The parameters are chosen so that the
first oracle is cheaper until the final
$m=\lceil\log_2 K\rceil$ stages. Thus, the adaptive policy
incurs a total cost of $\Theta(M\log K)$, while the best
fixed oracle incurs a cost of $MK$.

To show that this order cannot be improved, let $d_s$ denote
the minimum cost at stage $s$, and consider an oracle attaining
$d_{K-m}$. Its cost over all $K$ stages is at most
$O(Kd_{K-m})$. On the other hand, because $d_s$ is
nondecreasing, the adaptive policy pays at least
$(m+1)d_{K-m}$ over the final $m+1$ stages. Combining these
bounds gives an improvement of at most $O(K/\log K)$.
The construction with tight examples, and the complete bounds are presented
in Appendix~\ref{proof_corollary_known}. \qed

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:domination}
Oracle $\nu$ dominates oracle $\lambda$ if
\[
\mathcal{B}_\nu\le\mathcal{B}_\lambda,
\quad
c_\nu D_\nu\le c_\lambda D_\lambda,
\quad
c_\nu\sigma_{\nu,*}^2\le c_\lambda\sigma_{\lambda,*}^2.
\]
Then $\nu$ is admissible whenever $\lambda$ is, and $C_\nu(R)\le C_\lambda(R)$ for every $R$; hence Algorithm~\ref{alg:known} with SGE subroutine never selects $\lambda$.

$
4^{-\mathcal R}\le\tfrac{\delta}{K+\ell}.

$

$

\right)
\le
\tfrac{\sqrt{2}\mu R/16}{\sqrt{2}\mu R/4}

\tfrac14,

which proves the second inequality in Ineq.~\eqref{eq:cert-constant-prob} and completes the proof.

First, note that the fully successful blocks cover disjoint certified level intervals. By \eqref{eq:block-vs-pg-switching}, every contraction stage in such blocks costs no more than the corresponding stage of our benchmark, while the cost of the PG stage is at most $\zeta$ times its SGE-equivalent stage cost by \eqref{eq:pg-cost-comparable}. \begin{align}\label{eq:successful_blocks}

\end{align}

For a failed block, by the budget constraint rule in \eqref{eq:block-stop-budget}, the uncertified training cost of all contraction stages is at most $B$, as shown in Ineq.~\eqref{eq:block-budget}.  Meanwhile, in case $B$ is very large or even $\infty$, we can always bound the block's contraction cost by $C_{\rm sw}^{\rm PG}$ due to \eqref{eq:block-vs-pg-switching}. \begin{align}\label{eq:failed_blocks}

\[

=
\mathcal{R}\sum_{b\ {\rm failed}}
\widetilde C_{k_b}(\lambda_b),
\]
