\label{thm:full-global}
		Let $0<\alpha<1$. Suppose $0\leq n_{\mathrm{in}}\in L^1\cap H^3(\mathbb T\times\mathbb R), v_{\mathrm{in}}\in H^3(\mathbb T\times\mathbb R)$ and $\nabla\cdot v_{\mathrm{in}}=0$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The nonlinear diffusion introduces a difficulty
		in estimating $n_{\neq}$ separately.
		Testing its equation produces a mixed term involving
		$\partial_yn_0\,\partial_yn_{\neq}$.
		Since its coefficient depends on the full density,
		this term does not in general vanish after integration
		in $x$ and has no definite sign.
		We therefore estimate the full density together with
		$\omega_{\neq}$ in the joint energy
		\eqref{eq:full-energy}.
		This retains the positive nonlinear diffusion integral
		and controls the density--velocity coupling.
		The density Fourier correction is divided by an
		upper bound for the diffusion coefficient.
		Its diffusion contribution can then be absorbed
		into the same positive integral without
		differentiating the coefficient.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The method also applies at $\alpha=0$.
		In this case the diffusion coefficient equals one
		and the chemotactic term is
		$-\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c)$.
		The zero-mode production satisfies
		\[
		\int_{\mathbb R}n_0^3dy
		\le C(M)
		\bigl(\|\partial_yn_0\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2\bigr)^{\frac23}
		\le\varepsilon\|\partial_yn_0\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2
		+C(M,\varepsilon).
		\]
		The joint energy and the zero-mode vorticity estimate
		remain valid.
		The gradient term used in the density iteration
		retains a positive coefficient, and the iteration
		exponents become $r_j=2^{j+1}$.
		Thus the argument also covers the classical linear
		sensitivity studied in \cite{ZZZ}.
		Theorem \ref{thm:full-global} is stated for
		$0<\alpha<1$ to focus on the intermediate
		superlinear sensitivities.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
At $\alpha=1$, the diffusion coefficient equals one
		and the sensitivity is $n^2+n$, as in \cite{WWZ}.
		The distinction from $0<\alpha<1$ appears in
		two estimates.
		First, the zero-mode production contains a quartic
		term satisfying
		\[
		\int_{\mathbb R}n_0^4 dy
		\le C\left(\frac M{2\pi}\right)^2
		\|\partial_yn_0\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2.
		\]
		The gradient exponent is now one, with a
		mass-dependent coefficient.
		Consequently, the arbitrary-mass absorption estimate
		\eqref{eq:all-mass-absorption} in
		Lemma \ref{lem:mean-production}
		does not extend to this endpoint.
		Increasing $A$ does not improve this absorption,
		since both production and diffusion carry
		the factor $A^{-1}$.
		Second, the higher-power gradient term in
		\eqref{eq:full-density-Moser-energy},
		used in Lemma \ref{lem:full-density-Moser},
		has a coefficient proportional to $1-\alpha$.
		At $\alpha=1$, this term disappears, while
		the same Young estimate produces
		$n^{r+2}|\nabla c|^2$.
		For $0<\alpha<1$, arbitrary-mass absorption
		and the positive higher-power gradient term
		are both available.
		These are the two estimates that allow the
		global argument to close without restricting
		the initial cell mass.

			\le C_\alpha\bigl(s^2+s^{2+\alpha}\bigr).

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix $0<T<T_{\max}$.
	By the local existence assertion of
	Proposition \ref{prop:H3-continuation},
	proved in Appendix \ref{sec:local-continuation},
	we have
	$n,v\in C([0,T];H^3(\mathbb T\times\mathbb R))$.
	The chemical equation gives
	\[
	\widehat c(t,k,\eta)
	=\frac{\widehat n(t,k,\eta)}{1+k^2+\eta^2},
	\qquad
	\|c(t)\|_{H^5}\le C\|n(t)\|_{H^3}.
	\]
	Consequently, Sobolev embedding yields
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass-regularity-bounds}
		\begin{aligned}
			\sup_{0\le t\le T}\bigl(
			&\|n(t)\|_{L^\infty}
			+\|v(t)\|_{L^\infty}
			+\|\nabla c(t)\|_{L^\infty}\\
			&+\|n(t)\|_{L^2}
			+\|\nabla n(t)\|_{L^2}
			+\|v(t)\|_{L^2}
			+\|\nabla c(t)\|_{L^2}
			\bigr)\le C_T.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	The constants $C_T$ below may depend on the solution
	on $[0,T]$, but are independent of $R$.
	Choose an even function $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$
	such that $0\le\chi\le1$, $\chi=1$ on $[-1,1]$,
	$\chi=0$ outside $[-2,2]$, and $\chi$ is nonincreasing
	on $[0,\infty)$.
	Then $\chi(\frac yR)$ increases pointwise to one as
	$R\to\infty$.
	Since
	$\partial_y\left[\chi\left(\frac yR\right)\right]
	=\frac1R\chi'\left(\frac yR\right)$,
	a change of variables gives
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff-derivative-bounds}
		\left\|
		\partial_y\left[\chi\left(\frac yR\right)\right]
		\right\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}
		=R^{-\frac12}\|\chi'\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)},
		\quad
		\left\|
		\partial_y\left[\chi\left(\frac yR\right)\right]
		\right\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
		=R^{-1}\|\chi'\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}.
	\end{equation}
	
	\noindent\textbf{Nonnegativity.}
	The continuity of $n$ in $H^3$, supplied by
	Proposition \ref{prop:H3-continuation},
	and Sobolev embedding give
	$$\|n(t)-n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty}
	\le C\|n(t)-n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^3}
	\to0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to0^+.$$
	Due to $n_{\mathrm{in}}\ge0$, it follows that
	\[
	\inf_{(x,y)\in\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n(t,x,y)
	\ge-\|n(t)-n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty}>-\frac12
	\]
	on a sufficiently short initial time interval.
	We first prove nonnegativity as long as this lower
	bound holds.
	For $-\frac12<n<0$, the explicit coefficients satisfy
	\begin{equation}\label{diffion coe}
		\frac12
		\le(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]
		\le2^{1-\alpha},
		\qquad
		|n(1+n)^\alpha|\le|\min\{n,0\}|.
	\end{equation}
	The bounds in \eqref{diffion coe} apply to all terms
	below containing $\min\{n,0\}$ or its gradient,
	since both vanish almost everywhere on $\{n\ge0\}$.
	
	Test $\eqref{eq:rescaled-system}_1$ with
	$\chi(\frac yR)\min\{n,0\}$.
	For almost every $t$, integration by parts,
	periodicity in $x$ and $\nabla\cdot v=0$ give
	\begin{equation}\label{energy ineq}
		\begin{aligned}
			&\frac12\frac d{dt}
			\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			\chi\left(\frac yR\right)
			|\min\{n,0\}|^2 dxdy\\
			&+
			\frac1A\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			\chi\left(\frac yR\right)
			(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]
			|\nabla\min\{n,0\}|^2 dxdy\\=~&
			\frac1A\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			\chi\left(\frac yR\right)n(1+n)^\alpha
			\nabla c\cdot\nabla\min\{n,0\}dxdy\\
			&-\frac1{A}\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			\partial_y\left[\chi\left(\frac yR\right)\right]
			(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]
			\partial_yn\min\{n,0\}dxdy\\
			&+\frac1{A}\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			\partial_y\left[\chi\left(\frac yR\right)\right]
			n(1+n)^\alpha
			\partial_yc\,\min\{n,0\}dxdy\\
			&+\frac1{2A}\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			\partial_y\left[\chi\left(\frac yR\right)\right]
			v_2|\min\{n,0\}|^2 dxdy
			=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	Using the bound for $n(1+n)^\alpha$ in
	\eqref{diffion coe}, the bound for $\nabla c$ in
	\eqref{eq:mass-regularity-bounds}
	and Young's inequality, we obtain
	\begin{align*}
		I_{1}\le~&
		\frac{\|\nabla c(t)\|_{L^\infty}}A
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		\chi\left(\frac yR\right)
		|\min\{n,0\}||\nabla\min\{n,0\}|dxdy\\\le~&
		\frac1{4A}\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		\chi\left(\frac yR\right)
		|\nabla\min\{n,0\}|^2 dxdy+
		\frac{C_T}{A}\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		\chi\left(\frac yR\right)
		|\min\{n,0\}|^2 dxdy.
	\end{align*}
	For the three terms containing the cutoff derivative,
	we first estimate each term in absolute value.
	The coefficient bounds in \eqref{diffion coe} give
	\begin{align*}
		&I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}
		\le |I_2|+|I_3|+|I_4|\\\le~&
		\frac CA
		\left\|
		\partial_y\left[\chi\left(\frac yR\right)\right]
		\right\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		\left[
		|\partial_yn|\,|\min\{n,0\}|
		+\bigl(|\partial_yc|+|v_2|\bigr)
		|\min\{n,0\}|^2
		\right]dxdy\\\le~&
		\frac{C_T}{AR}\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		\left(
		|\partial_yn|\,|\min\{n,0\}|
		+|\min\{n,0\}|^2
		\right)dxdy\\\le~&
		\frac{C_T}{AR}
		\left(
		\|\partial_yn(t)\|_{L^2}
		\|\min\{n(t),0\}\|_{L^2}
		+\|\min\{n(t),0\}\|_{L^2}^2
		\right)\\\le~&
		\frac{C_T}{AR}
		\left(
		\|\nabla n(t)\|_{L^2}\|n(t)\|_{L^2}
		+\|n(t)\|_{L^2}^2
		\right).
	\end{align*}
	Here the third line uses the $L^\infty$ derivative
	bound in \eqref{eq:cutoff-derivative-bounds}
	and the bounds for $\nabla c$ and $v$ in
	\eqref{eq:mass-regularity-bounds}.
	The fourth line follows from Cauchy--Schwarz,
	and the last uses $|\min\{n,0\}|\le|n|$.
	Substituting these estimates into \eqref{energy ineq}
	and using the diffusion lower bound in
	\eqref{diffion coe}, we retain the gradient term
	with coefficient $\frac1{4A}$.
	Integrating from $0$ to $t$ and using
	$n_{\mathrm{in}}\ge0$, we obtain

		\begin{aligned}
			&\frac12\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			\chi\left(\frac yR\right)
			|\min\{n(t,x,y),0\}|^2 dxdy\\&+
			\frac1{4A}\int_0^t\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			\chi\left(\frac yR\right)
			|\nabla\min\{n(s,x,y),0\}|^2 dxdyds\\\le~&
			\frac{C_T}{A}\int_0^t\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			\chi\left(\frac yR\right)
			|\min\{n(s,x,y),0\}|^2 dxdyds\\&+
			\frac{C_T}{AR}\int_0^t

			\|n(s)\|_{L^2}\|\nabla n(s)\|_{L^2}

\label{lem:mass-elliptic}

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Fix a time $t$. The elliptic equation gives
	$\widehat c(t,k,\eta)=\frac{\widehat n(t,k,\eta)}{1+k^2+\eta^2}$.
	Parseval's identity proves the Sobolev estimate.
	The same formula gives the representation
	\begin{equation}\label{heat}
		c(t)=\int_0^\infty {\rm e}^{-s}{\rm e}^{s\Delta}n(t)ds
	\end{equation}
	in $L^2$. Since $n(t)\ge0$ and the heat kernel is nonnegative,
	we obtain $c(t)\ge0$.
	
	The one-dimensional Green function gives
	$c_0(t,y)=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}
	{\rm e}^{-|y-z|}n_0(t,z)dz$.
	Its derivative is
	\[
	\partial_yc_0(t,y)
	=-\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}
	\operatorname{sgn}(y-z){\rm e}^{-|y-z|}
	n_0(t,z)dz.
	\]
	Therefore
	\[
	|\partial_yc_0(t,y)|
	\le\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}n_0(t,z)dz
	=\frac{M}{4\pi},
	\]
	which proves \eqref{eq:mass-chemical}.
	The heat kernel on $\mathbb T\times\mathbb R$ is
	\[
	\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
	\frac1{4\pi s}
	\exp\left(
	-\frac{(x+2\pi j)^2+y^2}{4s}
	\right),
	\qquad s>0.
	\]
	By the triangle inequality, the $L^1$ norm of its
	gradient on $\mathbb T\times\mathbb R$ is bounded by
	the $L^1$ norm of the gradient of the Gaussian on
	$\mathbb R^2$. Scaling gives
	\[
	\int_{\mathbb R^2}
	\left|
	\nabla\left[
	\frac1{4\pi s}
	\exp\left(-\frac{x^2+y^2}{4s}\right)
	\right]
	\right|dxdy
	\le Cs^{-\frac12}.
	\]
	Since the heat semigroup acts by convolution with
	this kernel, Young's convolution inequality yields
	\[
	\|\nabla e^{s\Delta}f\|_{L^\infty}
	\le Cs^{-\frac12}\|f\|_{L^\infty},
	\qquad s>0.
	\]
	Recall the heat representation in \eqref{heat}.
	The preceding estimate gives
	\[
	e^{-s}\|\nabla {\rm e}^{s\Delta}n(t)\|_{L^\infty}
	\le C{\rm e}^{-s}s^{-\frac12}\|n(t)\|_{L^\infty}.
	\]
	We therefore differentiate the heat representation 
	under the integral sign to obtain
	$\nabla c(t)
	=\int_0^\infty {\rm e}^{-s}\nabla {\rm e}^{s\Delta}n(t)ds$.
	Consequently,
	\[
	\|\nabla c(t)\|_{L^\infty}
	\le
	C\|n(t)\|_{L^\infty}
	\int_0^\infty {\rm e}^{-s}s^{-\frac12}ds
	\le C\|n(t)\|_{L^\infty}.
	\]

If $M=0$, nonnegativity and mass conservation give $n_0=0$,
	so all assertions are immediate.
	It remains to consider the case $M>0$.
	
The one-dimensional interpolation inequality gives
	\[
	\int_{\mathbb R}n_{0}^4 dy
	\le
	C\left(\int_{\mathbb R}n_{0}dy\right)^2
	\|\partial_y n_{0}\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2.

	\begin{align*}

		&\le

		\left(\int_{\mathbb R}n_{0}^4 dy\right)^{\frac{s-1}{3}}\le

 Taking $s=3$ and $s=3+\alpha$, we similarly obtain

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The zero mode of the vorticity, given by $\eqref{eq:rescaled-system}_3$, satisfies 
	\[
\partial_t\omega_0-A^{-1}\partial_y^2\omega_0
=-A^{-1}\partial_y^2(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0,
\]
where we used $\partial_y(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0=(v_{2,\neq}\omega_{\neq})_0$
by incompressibility and the definition of vorticity.
Duhamel's formula therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{Duhamel}
\omega_0(t)
=
{\rm e}^{\frac tA\partial_y^2}(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0
-\frac1A\int_0^t
\partial_y^2{\rm e}^{\frac{t-s}{A}\partial_y^2}
(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0(s)ds.
\end{equation}
By the definition of the zero mode, H\"older's inequality
and \eqref{eq:Biot-bound}, we arrive at
\begin{equation}\label{v w}
	\begin{aligned}
		\|(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0\|_{L^1(\mathbb R)}
		&\le C\|v_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2\le C\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2,\\
		\|\partial_y(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0
		\|_{L^{\frac43}(\mathbb R)}&\le
		C\|v_{2,\neq}\|_{L^4}
		\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}
		\le C\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The one-dimensional heat kernel is
$\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi\tau}}
\exp\left(-\frac{y^2}{4\tau}\right)$ for $\tau>0$.
Scaling gives
\[
\left\|
\partial_y\left[
\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi\tau}}
\exp\left(-\frac{y^2}{4\tau}\right)
\right]
\right\|_{L^4(\mathbb R)}
\le C\tau^{-\frac78},\quad
\left\|
\partial_y^2\left[
\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi\tau}}
\exp\left(-\frac{y^2}{4\tau}\right)
\right]
\right\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
\le C\tau^{-\frac32}.
\]
Young's convolution inequality consequently yields
\begin{equation}\label{heat estimates}
	\|\partial_y{\rm e}^{\tau\partial_y^2}h
	\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
	\le
	C\tau^{-\frac78}\|h\|_{L^{\frac43}(\mathbb R)},
	\qquad
	\|\partial_y^2{\rm e}^{\tau\partial_y^2}h
	\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
	\le
	C\tau^{-\frac32}\|h\|_{L^1(\mathbb R)}.
\end{equation}
Fix $t\in(0,T]$. We distinguish the cases
$t\le A$ and $t>A$.

\noindent\textbf{Case 1: $0<t\le A$.}
For every $s\in[0,t)$, we have
$0<\frac{t-s}{A}\le\frac tA\le1$.
Thus the entire time integral can be estimated
using the derivative of the averaged product.
Since spatial differentiation commutes with the
heat semigroup, setting $\tau=\frac{t-s}{A}$ and
using $\eqref{v w}_2$ and the first estimate in
\eqref{heat estimates}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
	\left\|
	\partial_y^2{\rm e}^{\tau\partial_y^2}
	(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0(s)
	\right\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}&=
	\left\|
	\partial_y{\rm e}^{\tau\partial_y^2}
	\partial_y(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0(s)
	\right\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}\\&\le
	C\tau^{-\frac78}
	\|\partial_y(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0(s)
	\|_{L^{\frac43}(\mathbb R)}\le
	C\tau^{-\frac78}\|\omega_{\neq}(s)\|_{L^2}^2.
\end{align*}
Integrating this estimate and changing variables
from $s$ to $\tau=\frac{t-s}{A}$ give
\begin{align*}
	&\frac1A\int_0^t
	\left\|
	\partial_y^2{\rm e}^{\frac{t-s}{A}\partial_y^2}
	(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0(s)
	\right\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}ds\le
	C\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|\omega_{\neq}(s)\|_{L^2}^2
	\frac1A\int_0^t
	\left(\frac{t-s}{A}\right)^{-\frac78}ds\\&=
	C\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|\omega_{\neq}(s)\|_{L^2}^2
	\int_0^{\frac{t}{A}}\tau^{-\frac78}d\tau\le
	8C\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|\omega_{\neq}(s)\|_{L^2}^2,
\end{align*}
where we used $\frac{t}{A}\le1$ and
$\int_0^1\tau^{-\frac78}d\tau=8$.

\noindent\textbf{Case 2: $t>A$.}
We split the time integral into the intervals
$[0,t-A]$ and $[t-A,t]$.
On $[t-A,t)$, we have
$0<\frac{t-s}{A}\le1$.
The same pointwise estimate as in Case 1 gives
\begin{align*}
	\frac1A\int_{t-A}^t
	\left\|
	\partial_y^2{\rm e}^{\frac{t-s}{A}\partial_y^2}
	(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0(s)
	\right\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}ds\le
	8C\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|\omega_{\neq}(s)\|_{L^2}^2.
\end{align*}
On $[0,t-A]$, we have $\frac{t-s}{A}\ge1$.
We now use the $L^1$ bound for the averaged product,
keeping both derivatives on the heat kernel.
By $\eqref{v w}_1$ and the second estimate in
\eqref{heat estimates}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
	\left\|
	\partial_y^2{\rm e}^{\frac{t-s}{A}\partial_y^2}
	(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0(s)
	\right\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}&\le
	C\left(\frac{t-s}{A}\right)^{-\frac32}
	\|(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0(s)\|_{L^1(\mathbb R)}\\&\le
	C\left(\frac{t-s}{A}\right)^{-\frac32}
	\|\omega_{\neq}(s)\|_{L^2}^2.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
	&\frac1A\int_0^{t-A}
	\left\|
	\partial_y^2{\rm e}^{\frac{t-s}{A}\partial_y^2}
	(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0(s)
	\right\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}ds\le
	C\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|\omega_{\neq}(s)\|_{L^2}^2
	\frac1A\int_0^{t-A}
	\left(\frac{t-s}{A}\right)^{-\frac32}ds\\&=
	C\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|\omega_{\neq}(s)\|_{L^2}^2
	\int_1^{\frac{t}{A}}\tau^{-\frac32}d\tau\le
	2C\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|\omega_{\neq}(s)\|_{L^2}^2,
\end{align*}
where we used $\int_1^\infty\tau^{-\frac32}d\tau=2$.

Combining the two cases, we conclude that
\[
\frac1A\int_0^t
\left\|
\partial_y^2{\rm e}^{\frac{t-s}{A}\partial_y^2}
(v_{1,\neq}v_{2,\neq})_0(s)
\right\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}ds
\le
C\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|\omega_{\neq}(s)\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
This together with \eqref{Duhamel} yields
\[
\|\omega_0(t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
\le
\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
+C\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|\omega_{\neq}(s)\|_{L^2}^2,
\qquad 0\le t\le T.
\]
Taking the supremum over $t\in[0,T]$ proves
\eqref{eq:mean-vorticity-uniform}.

For $k\ne0$, we use the unitary Fourier transforms
	\[
	f_k(y)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
	\int_{\mathbb T}f(x,y){\rm e}^{-\mathrm ikx}dx,
	\qquad
	\widehat f(k,\eta)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
	\int_{\mathbb R}f_k(y){\rm e}^{-\mathrm i\eta y}dy.
	\]
The analysis is based on the integral representation
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-transport-arctan}
		\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
		=
		\int_0^\infty
		{\rm e}^{-A^{\frac13}|k|^{\frac13}r}
		\frac{\sin(\eta r)}r dr.
	\end{equation}
	Indeed, both sides vanish at $\eta=0$, and
	differentiating the right-hand side gives
	\[
	\int_0^\infty
	{\rm e}^{-A^{\frac13}|k|^{\frac13}r}
	\cos(\eta r)dr
	=\frac{A^{\frac13}|k|^{\frac13}}
	{A^{\frac23}|k|^{\frac23}+\eta^2},
	\]
	which is also the derivative of the left-hand side.
	Using \eqref{eq:mean-transport-arctan} and Fourier
	inversion, we obtain
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-transport-kernel}
		\begin{aligned}
			&\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb R}
			{\rm e}^{\mathrm i\eta y}
			\operatorname{sgn}(k)
			\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
			\widehat f(k,\eta)d\eta\\=~&
			\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(k)}{2\mathrm i}
			\int_0^\infty
			{\rm e}^{-A^{\frac13}|k|^{\frac13}r}
			\frac{f_k(y+r)-f_k(y-r)}rdr\\=~&
			\frac{\mathrm i\operatorname{sgn}(k)}2
			\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\frac{{\rm e}^{-A^{\frac13}|k|^{\frac13}|y-z|}
			}{y-z}f_k(z)dz.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	To estimate the expression on the left-hand side of
\eqref{eq:mean-transport-correction}, consider
$\partial_tf=-A^{-1}v_{1,0}\partial_xf$.
Then
$\partial_t f_k=-\frac{\mathrm ik}{A}v_{1,0}f_k$,
and the expression inside the absolute value is
the time derivative of the quadratic correction. 
	Substituting it when
	differentiating the correction, and using Parseval's
	identity and the kernel representation
	\eqref{eq:mean-transport-kernel}, gives
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-transport-pairing}
		\begin{aligned}
			&\frac d{dt}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\operatorname{sgn}(k)
			\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
			|\widehat f(k,\eta)|^2 d\eta\\=~&
			2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\operatorname{sgn}(k)
			\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
			\overline{\widehat f(k,\eta)}
			\partial_t\widehat f(k,\eta)d\eta\\=~&
			\frac{|k|}{A}\operatorname{Re}
			\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}
			\int_{\mathbb R}
			\int_{\{z\in\mathbb R:\,|y-z|>\varepsilon\}}
			\frac{
				{\rm e}^{-A^{\frac13}|k|^{\frac13}|y-z|}
			}{y-z}
			v_{1,0}(z)\overline{f_k(y)}f_k(z)dzdy.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
For each $\varepsilon>0$, interchange $y$ and $z$
	in the truncated double integral in
	\eqref{eq:mean-transport-pairing}.
	Since $v_{1,0}$ is real,
	$\operatorname{Re}(\overline{f_k(y)}f_k(z))$
	is unchanged under this interchange, whereas
	$(y-z)^{-1}$ changes sign.
	Averaging the original and interchanged expressions
	therefore gives
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-transport-symmetrized}
		\begin{aligned}
			&\frac d{dt}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\operatorname{sgn}(k)
			\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
			|\widehat f(k,\eta)|^2 d\eta\\
			&\quad=
			-\frac{|k|}{2A}\operatorname{Re}
			\int_{\mathbb R}\int_{\mathbb R}
			{\rm e}^{-A^{\frac13}|k|^{\frac13}|y-z|}
			\frac{v_{1,0}(y)-v_{1,0}(z)}{y-z}
			\overline{f_k(y)}f_k(z)dzdy.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	The passage to the limit is justified by the
	Lipschitz bound
	\[
	\left|
	\frac{v_{1,0}(y)-v_{1,0}(z)}{y-z}
	\right|
	\le
	\|\partial_yv_{1,0}\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}\quad {\text{for}}\quad
	y\ne z
	\]
	and the following integrability estimate:
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-transport-kernel-bound}
		\begin{aligned}
			&\int_{\mathbb R}\int_{\mathbb R}
			{\rm e}^{-A^{\frac13}|k|^{\frac13}|y-z|}
			|f_k(y)||f_k(z)|dzdy\\\le~&
			\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}\int_{\mathbb R}
			{\rm e}^{-A^{\frac13}|k|^{\frac13}|y-z|}
			\bigl(|f_k(y)|^2+|f_k(z)|^2\bigr)dzdy=
			2A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}
			\|f_k\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	In the last equality, we used
	$\int_{\mathbb R}
	{\rm e}^{-A^{\frac13}|k|^{\frac13}|y-z|}dz
	=2A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}$.
	Thus the double integral in
	\eqref{eq:mean-transport-symmetrized}
	is absolutely convergent.
	Combining \eqref{eq:mean-transport-symmetrized}
	with \eqref{eq:mean-transport-kernel-bound}, we obtain
		\begin{align*}
			\left|
			\frac d{dt}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\operatorname{sgn}(k)
			\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
			|\widehat f(k,\eta)|^2 d\eta
			\right|\le
			\frac1A
			\|\partial_yv_{1,0}\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
			A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
			\|f_k\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2.
		\end{align*}
	By Parseval's identity, $A\ge1$ and $|k|\ge1$, there holds
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\sum_{k\ne0}A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
	\|f_k\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2\le
	\sum_{k\ne0}k^2\|f_k\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2=
	\|\partial_xf\|_{L^2(\mathbb T\times\mathbb R)}^2
	<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the sum of the preceding mode estimates
is absolutely convergent.
Summing over $k\ne0$ and applying Parseval's identity
in $y$ proves \eqref{eq:mean-transport-correction}.

\eqref{eq:joint-density-fourier-equation}.
Moreover, at $k\ne0$, inserting $P_{\neq}$
does not change the Fourier coefficient.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-density-fourier-square}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\partial_t|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2
		-k\partial_\eta|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2\\=~&
		-\frac2A\operatorname{Re}\left\{
		\overline{[\nabla n]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)}
		\cdot
		\left[
		(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]\nabla n
		\right]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)
		\right\}\\
		&+
		\frac2A\operatorname{Re}\left\{
		\overline{[\nabla n]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)}
		\cdot
		\left[
		P_{\neq}\bigl(n(1+n)^\alpha\nabla c+vn\bigr)
		\right]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)
		\right\}.
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We now multiply \eqref{eq:joint-density-fourier-square}
by
$\operatorname{sgn}(k)
\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)$,
integrate over $\eta\in\mathbb R$,
and sum over $k\ne0$.
The time derivative gives the derivative of
the quadratic Fourier expression.
For the Couette term, integration by parts in $\eta$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-density-couette-calculation}
	\begin{aligned}
		&-\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		\operatorname{sgn}(k)
		\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
		k\partial_\eta|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta\\
		&=
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		k\partial_\eta\left[
		\operatorname{sgn}(k)
		\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
		\right]
		|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta\\&=
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		\frac{
			A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
			|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2
		}{
			1+A^{-\frac23}|k|^{-\frac23}\eta^2
		}d\eta.
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Finally, multiply the resulting identity by
the density correction coefficient
$$
\frac1{
	8\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
	[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}.$$
Using \eqref{eq:joint-density-fourier-square}
and \eqref{eq:joint-density-couette-calculation},
we obtain
\begin{align}
	&\frac1{
		8\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
		[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
	\frac d{dt}
	\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
	\operatorname{sgn}(k)
	\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
	|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta
	\notag\\&+
	\frac1{
		8\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
		[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
	\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
	\frac{
		A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
		|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2
	}{
		1+A^{-\frac23}|k|^{-\frac23}\eta^2
	}\,d\eta
	\notag\\&=
	-\frac2{
		8\pi A(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
		[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
	\operatorname{Re}
	\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
	\operatorname{sgn}(k)
	\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
	\notag\\&\times
	\overline{[\nabla n]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)}
	\cdot
	\left[
	(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]\nabla n
	\right]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)d\eta
	\notag\\&+
	\frac2{
		8\pi A(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
		[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
	\operatorname{Re}
	\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
	\operatorname{sgn}(k)
	\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
	\notag\\&\times
	\overline{[\nabla n]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)}
	\cdot\left[
	P_{\neq}\left(n(1+n)^\alpha\nabla c+vn\right)
	\right]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)d\eta.
	\label{eq:joint-density-correction-identity}
\end{align}
We next estimate the diffusion, chemotactic
and velocity terms on the right-hand side
of \eqref{eq:joint-density-correction-identity}
separately. 
For the diffusion term, Cauchy--Schwarz, Parseval's identity, 	\eqref{eq:joint-diffusion-range} and
	\eqref{eq:joint-multiplier-gradient}
	bound its absolute value by
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-density-diffusion-correction}
		\begin{aligned}
			\frac1{8A}
			\|\nabla n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}\|\nabla n\|_{L^2}
			\le
			\frac1{8A}\|\nabla n\|_{L^2}^2.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	For the chemotactic term, we first estimate
	$P_{\neq}(n(1+n)^\alpha\nabla c)$.
	Since $n_0(1+n_0)^\alpha\nabla c_0$ is independent
	of $x$, we have
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-chemotactic-flux-decomposition}
		\begin{aligned}
			P_{\neq}\bigl(n(1+n)^\alpha\nabla c\bigr)
			=P_{\neq}\Bigl([n(1+n)^\alpha-n_0(1+n_0)^\alpha]\nabla c_0+n(1+n)^\alpha\nabla c_{\neq}
			\Bigr).
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	On $[0,2K_\infty]$, the direct calculation implies
	\[
	\frac d{ds}[s(1+s)^\alpha]
	=
	(1+s)^\alpha+\alpha s(1+s)^{\alpha-1}
	\le
	(1+\alpha)(1+2K_\infty)^\alpha.
	\]
	Thus the mean value theorem gives
	\[
	|n(1+n)^\alpha-n_0(1+n_0)^\alpha|
	\le
	(1+\alpha)(1+2K_\infty)^\alpha|n_{\neq}|.
	\]
	Using this bound,
	$n(1+n)^\alpha\le2K_\infty(1+2K_\infty)^\alpha$, \eqref{eq:nonzero-chemical-gradient},
	\eqref{eq:mass-chemical} and
	\eqref{eq:joint-chemotactic-flux-decomposition},
	we obtain
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-chemotactic-flux-bound}
		\begin{aligned}
			\bigl\|
			P_{\neq}\bigl(n(1+n)^\alpha\nabla c\bigr)&
			\bigr\|_{L^2}\le
			(1+\alpha)(1+2K_\infty)^\alpha
			\frac M{4\pi}\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}
			\\&+
			2K_\infty(1+2K_\infty)^\alpha
			\|\nabla c_{\neq}\|_{L^2}\le
			C(\alpha,M,K_\infty)\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	Apply Cauchy--Schwarz and
	\eqref{eq:joint-multiplier-gradient}
	to the part of \eqref{eq:joint-density-correction-identity}
	containing this product.
	By \eqref{eq:joint-chemotactic-flux-bound},
	its absolute value is bounded by
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-corrected-chemotaxis-bound}
		\begin{aligned}
			&\frac{
				\|\nabla n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}
				\|P_{\neq}(n(1+n)^\alpha\nabla c)\|_{L^2}
			}{
				8A(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
				[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]
			}\\\le~&
			\frac{C(\alpha,M,K_\infty)}A
			\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}\|\nabla n\|_{L^2}\le
			\frac1{32A}\|\nabla n\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac{C(\alpha,M,K_\infty)}A
			\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	For the velocity term, $v_{2,0}=0$ gives
	$-\frac1A\nabla\cdot(vn)=
	-\frac1Av_{1,0}\partial_xn_{\neq}
	-\frac1A\nabla\cdot(v_{\neq}n)$.
	We treat these two terms separately.
	For the zero-mode velocity term, apply
	\eqref{eq:mean-transport-correction}
	with $f=n_{\neq}$ and multiply by the density
	correction coefficient.
	Using $\partial_yv_{1,0}=\omega_0$ and
	\eqref{eq:joint-proof-mean-bound},
	we obtain the absolute bound
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-density-mean-transport-bound}
		\begin{aligned}
			\frac{C\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
				A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
				|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta}{
				8\pi A(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
				[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
			\left(
			\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
			+CK_{\neq}
			\right).
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	For the non-zero mode velocity term,
	\eqref{eq:Biot-bound} implies
	\[
	\|P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}n)\|_{L^2}
	\le
	\|n\|_{L^\infty}\|v_{\neq}\|_{L^2}
	\le
	CK_\infty\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}.
	\]
	Applying this bound and
	\eqref{eq:joint-multiplier-gradient}
	to its gradient pairing in
	\eqref{eq:joint-density-correction-identity}
	gives
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-density-velocity-bound}
		\begin{aligned}
			&\frac{
				\|\nabla n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}
				\|P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}n)\|_{L^2}
			}{
				8A(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
				[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]
			}\\\le~&
			\frac{CK_\infty}{A}
			\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}\|\nabla n\|_{L^2}\le
			\frac1{32A}\|\nabla n\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac{C(K_\infty)}A
			\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
Combining the ordinary density estimate \eqref{eq:joint-density-basic-bound} with the correction identity \eqref{eq:joint-density-correction-identity}, and using the estimates \eqref{eq:joint-density-diffusion-correction}, \eqref{eq:joint-corrected-chemotaxis-bound}, \eqref{eq:joint-density-mean-transport-bound}, and \eqref{eq:joint-density-velocity-bound}, we obtain
	\begin{align}
		&\frac d{dt}\left[
		\|n\|_{L^2}^2
		+\frac{\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\operatorname{sgn}(k)
			\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
			|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta}{
			8\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
			[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
		\right]
		\notag\\
		&+
		\frac{57}{32A}\|\nabla n\|_{L^2}^2
		+
		\frac1{
			8\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
			[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		\frac{
			A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
			|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2
		}{
			1+A^{-\frac23}|k|^{-\frac23}\eta^2
		}d\eta\notag\\&\le
		\frac{C(\alpha,M)}A
		+\frac{C(\alpha,M,K_\infty)}A
		\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
		+\frac{C(K_\infty)}A
		\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
		\notag\\
		&+
		\frac{C	\left(
			\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
			+CK_{\neq}
			\right)
			\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
			A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
			|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta}{
			8\pi A(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
			[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}.
		\label{eq:joint-density-energy-bound}
	\end{align}

	\noindent{\bf Step II. The vorticity energy.}
	Applying $P_{\neq}$ to $\eqref{eq:rescaled-system}_3$
	and using $v_{2,0}=0$, we obtain
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-vorticity-equation}
		\begin{aligned}
			\partial_t\omega_{\neq}
			+y\partial_x\omega_{\neq}
			-\frac{\Delta\omega_{\neq}}A
			=-\frac1A\partial_xn_{\neq}
			-\frac1A v_{1,0}\partial_x\omega_{\neq}-\frac1A P_{\neq}\nabla\cdot
			\bigl(v_{\neq}\omega_0
			+v_{\neq}\omega_{\neq}\bigr).
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	
	\noindent{\bf (i) The ordinary $L^2$ estimate.}
	Testing \eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-equation}
	with $2\omega_{\neq}$, and using integration by parts together with $\nabla\cdot v=0$, gives
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-vorticity-L2-identity}
		\begin{aligned}
			&\frac d{dt}\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac{2}{A}\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2\\=~&
			\frac2A\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			n_{\neq}\partial_x\omega_{\neq}dxdy
			+\frac2A\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}\omega_0)
			\cdot\nabla\omega_{\neq}dxdy.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	For the first term on the right-hand side, using
	Cauchy--Schwarz and Young's inequalities, we obtain
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-vorticity-density-bound}
		\begin{aligned}
			\left|
			\frac2A\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			n_{\neq}\partial_x\omega_{\neq}dxdy
			\right|\le
			\frac2A\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}
			\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}\le
			\frac1{64A}\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac CA\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	For the second term, \eqref{eq:Biot-bound} and
	\eqref{eq:joint-proof-mean-bound} imply
		\begin{align}\label{eq:joint-vorticity-mean-product}
			\|P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}\omega_0)\|_{L^2}\le
			\|\omega_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
			\|v_{\neq}\|_{L^2}\le
			C\left(
			\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
			+CK_{\neq}
			\right)\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}.
		\end{align}
	Thus
		\begin{align*}
			&\left|
			\frac2A\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}\omega_0)
			\cdot\nabla\omega_{\neq}dxdy
			\right|\le
			\frac2A
			\|P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}\omega_0)\|_{L^2}
			\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}\\\le~&
			\frac1{64A}\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac CA
			\left(
			\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty}
			+CK_{\neq}\right)^2
			\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
		\end{align*}
	Substituting this and
	\eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-density-bound}
	into \eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-L2-identity}, we obtain
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-vorticity-basic-bound}
		\begin{aligned}
			\frac d{dt}\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac{63}{32A}
			\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2\le
			\frac CA\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac CA
			\left(
			\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty}
			+CK_{\neq}
			\right)^2
			\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	
	\noindent{\bf (ii) The Fourier correction estimate.}
By \eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-equation}, the Fourier
differentiation identities and \eqref{eq:correction-shear},
differentiation of the vorticity correction in
\eqref{eq:full-energy} gives
		\begin{align}\label{eq:joint-vorticity-correction-identity}
		&\frac1{8\pi}\frac d{dt}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		\operatorname{sgn}(k)
		\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
		|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2d\eta\notag\\&+
		\frac1{8\pi}\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		\frac{
			A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
			|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2
		}{
			1+A^{-\frac23}|k|^{-\frac23}\eta^2
		}d\eta\notag\\=~&
		-\frac2{8\pi A}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		\operatorname{sgn}(k)
		\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
		(k^2+\eta^2)|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta\notag\\&-
		\frac2{8\pi A}\operatorname{Re}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		\operatorname{sgn}(k)
		\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
		\mathrm ik\,
		\overline{\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)}
		\widehat n(t,k,\eta)d\eta\\&+
		\frac2{8\pi A}\operatorname{Re}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		\operatorname{sgn}(k)
		\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)\notag\\&\times
		\overline{[\nabla\omega_{\neq}]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)}
		\cdot
		\left[
		P_{\neq}\bigl(v_{\neq}\omega_0
		+v_{\neq}\omega_{\neq}\bigr)
		\right]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)d\eta\notag\\&-
		\frac2{8\pi A}\operatorname{Re}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		\operatorname{sgn}(k)
		\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
		\overline{\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)}
		[v_{1,0}\partial_x\omega_{\neq}]^{\wedge}
		(t,k,\eta)d\eta\notag\\\notag&=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.
	\end{align}
	We estimate the terms on the right-hand side in order.
	The bound $|\arctan z|\le\frac{\pi}{2}$ and
	Parseval's identity yield
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-vorticity-diffusion-correction}
		\begin{aligned}
			|I_{1}|\leq\frac1{8A}\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	For the density term $I_{2}$, by Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
	Parseval's identity and
	\eqref{eq:joint-multiplier-gradient}, we have
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-vorticity-corrected-density-bound}
		\begin{aligned}
		|I_{2}|\le
			\frac1{8A}\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}
			\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}\le
			\frac1{64A}\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac CA\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	For the product $v_{\neq}\omega_0$, the same
	Cauchy--Schwarz estimate and
	\eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-mean-product} give
		\begin{align*}
			&\frac2{8\pi A}
			\left|
			\operatorname{Re}
			\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\operatorname{sgn}(k)
			\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
			\overline{[\nabla\omega_{\neq}]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)}
			\cdot
			[P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}\omega_0)]^{\wedge}
			(t,k,\eta)d\eta
			\right|\\
			&\le
			\frac1{8A}
			\|P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}\omega_0)\|_{L^2}
			\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}\le
			\frac1{64A}\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac CA
			\left(
			\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty}
			+CK_{\neq}
			\right)^2
			\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
		\end{align*}
	For the product $v_{\neq}\omega_{\neq}$,
	H\"older's inequality, \eqref{eq:Biot-bound} and interpolation inequality imply
		\begin{align*}
			\|P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}\omega_{\neq})\|_{L^2}\le
			\|v_{\neq}\|_{L^4}\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^4}\le
			C\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^{\frac32}
			\left(
			\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}
			+\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}
			\right)^{\frac12}.
		\end{align*}
	Applying this and \eqref{eq:joint-multiplier-gradient},
	we obtain
	\begin{align*}
		&\frac2{8\pi A}
		\left|
		\operatorname{Re}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		\operatorname{sgn}(k)
		\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
		\overline{[\nabla\omega_{\neq}]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)}
		\cdot
		[P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}\omega_{\neq})]^{\wedge}
		(t,k,\eta)d\eta
		\right|\\
		&\le
		\frac1{8A}
		\|P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}\omega_{\neq})\|_{L^2}
		\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}\le
		\frac CA\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
		\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}
		+\frac CA\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^{\frac32}
		\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^{\frac32}\\
		&\le
		\frac1{32A}\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
		+\frac CA
		\left(
		\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^4
		+\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^6
		\right).
	\end{align*}
	By \eqref{eq:joint-proof-bootstrap-consequences}, we arrive at
	\[
	\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^4
	+\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^6
	\le
	\left[
	\frac{8K_{\neq}}3+
	\left(\frac{8K_{\neq}}3\right)^2
	\right]\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
	\]
	Therefore,
		\begin{align*}
			&\frac2{8\pi A}
			\left|
			\operatorname{Re}
			\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\operatorname{sgn}(k)
			\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
			\overline{[\nabla\omega_{\neq}]^{\wedge}(t,k,\eta)}
			\cdot
			[P_{\neq}(v_{\neq}\omega_{\neq})]^{\wedge}
			(t,k,\eta)d\eta
			\right|\\&\le
			\frac1{32A}\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac{C(K_{\neq})}A
			\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
		\end{align*}
	Collecting the above estimates, $|I_{3}|$ is bounded by
	\begin{equation}\label{I3 estimate}
	 \frac{3}{64A}\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C(K_{\neq},\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^{\infty}} )}{A}\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
	\end{equation}
Finally, applying
	\eqref{eq:mean-transport-correction}
	with $f=\omega_{\neq}$, and using
	$\partial_yv_{1,0}=\omega_0$ and
	\eqref{eq:joint-proof-mean-bound}, we find
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-vorticity-mean-transport-bound}
		\begin{aligned}
		|I_{4}|\le
			\frac C{8\pi A}
			\left(
			\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty}
			+CK_{\neq}
			\right)
			\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
			A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
			|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	Substituting
	\eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-diffusion-correction},
	\eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-corrected-density-bound},
	\eqref{I3 estimate}
	and \eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-mean-transport-bound}
	into \eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-correction-identity}
	gives
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-vorticity-correction-bound}
		\begin{aligned}
			&\frac1{8\pi}\frac d{dt}
			\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\operatorname{sgn}(k)
			\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
			|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta\\&\quad+
			\frac1{8\pi}\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\frac{
				A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
				|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2
			}{
				1+A^{-\frac23}|k|^{-\frac23}\eta^2
			}d\eta\\
			&\le
			\frac{3}{16A}\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac CA\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac{
				C(K_{\neq},
				\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)})
			}A\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2\\
			&\quad+
			\frac C{8\pi A}
			\left(
			\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
			+CK_{\neq}
			\right)
			\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
			A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
			|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	Adding \eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-basic-bound}
	and \eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-correction-bound},
	and moving the gradient term on the right-hand
	side to the left, we obtain
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-vorticity-energy-bound}
			\begin{aligned}
			&\frac d{dt}\left[
			\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac1{8\pi}
			\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\operatorname{sgn}(k)
			\arctan\bigl(A^{-\frac13}|k|^{-\frac13}\eta\bigr)
			|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta
			\right]\\
			&\quad+
			\frac{57}{32A}\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac1{8\pi}
			\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
			\frac{
				A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
				|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2
			}{
				1+A^{-\frac23}|k|^{-\frac23}\eta^2
			}d\eta\\
			&\le
			\frac CA\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
			+\frac{
				C(K_{\neq},
				\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)})
			}A\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2\\
			&\quad+
			\frac C{8\pi A}
			\left(
			\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
			+CK_{\neq}
			\right)
			\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
			A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
			|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}

	\noindent{\bf Step III. Combining the estimates.}
	Add \eqref{eq:joint-density-energy-bound}
	and \eqref{eq:joint-vorticity-energy-bound}.
	By \eqref{eq:full-energy}, the sum of their
	time derivatives is $E'(t)$.
	We next combine the Couette dissipation with
	a portion of the gradient dissipation.
	For the density, apply
	\eqref{eq:fourier-coercivity} with
	\[
	a=
	\frac1{
		8\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
		[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]},
	\]
	multiply that inequality by
	$|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2$,
	integrate over $\eta\in\mathbb R$ and sum over $k\ne0$.
	For the vorticity, use $a=\frac{1}{8\pi}$ and
	$|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2$.
	Both coefficients belong to $(0,1]$.
	For the density gradient, we also use
	$\|\nabla n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}\le\|\nabla n\|_{L^2}$. Therefore,
	\begin{align}\label{eq:joint-after-frequency-coercivity}
		&E'(t)+\frac5{4A}
		\left(
		\|\nabla n\|_{L^2}^2
		+\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
		\right)+\frac1{32\pi}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
		|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta
		\notag\\
		&+\frac1{
			32\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
			[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
		|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta
		\notag\\&\le
		\frac{C(\alpha,M)}A
		+\frac{C(\alpha,M,K_\infty)}A
		\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2+
		\frac{
			C(\alpha,K_{\neq},K_\infty,
			\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)})
		}A\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2\\&+
		\frac CA
		\left(
		\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
		+CK_{\neq}
		\right)
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
		\notag\\\notag&\times
		\left(
		\frac{|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2}{
			8\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
			[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
		+\frac{|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2}{8\pi}
		\right)d\eta.
	\end{align}
	It remains to absorb the three nonconstant terms
	on the right-hand side.
	Fix their constants and choose $A$ sufficiently
	large that
	\begin{align}
		&\frac CA
		\left(
		\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
		+CK_{\neq}
		\right)
		\le\frac1{16},
		\label{eq:joint-absorb-mean-transport}\\
		&C(\alpha,M,K_\infty)A^{-\frac23}
		(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
		[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]
		\le\frac1{128\pi},
		\label{eq:joint-absorb-density}\\
		&C(\alpha,K_{\neq},K_\infty,
		\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)})
		A^{-\frac23}
		\le\frac1{128\pi}.
		\label{eq:joint-absorb-vorticity}
	\end{align}
	First, \eqref{eq:joint-absorb-mean-transport}
	bounds the coefficients of the density and
	vorticity Fourier integrals in the last term
	of \eqref{eq:joint-after-frequency-coercivity}
	by
	\[
	\frac1{
		128\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
		[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}\quad\text{and}\quad
	\frac1{128\pi},
	\]
	respectively.
	Second, Parseval's identity and $|k|^{\frac23}\ge1$
	for $k\ne0$ give
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-nonzero-norm-comparison}
		\frac1A\|f_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
		\le
		A^{-\frac23}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
		|\widehat f(k,\eta)|^2 d\eta,
		\qquad f=n\quad\text{or}\quad \omega.
	\end{equation}
	Applying \eqref{eq:joint-nonzero-norm-comparison}
	with $f=n$ and using
	\eqref{eq:joint-absorb-density}, we obtain
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		&\frac{C(\alpha,M,K_\infty)}A
		\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
		\le
		C(\alpha,M,K_\infty)A^{-\frac23}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
		|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta\\
		&\le
		\frac1{
			128\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
			[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
		|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta.
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	The same calculation with $f=\omega$ and
	\eqref{eq:joint-absorb-vorticity} gives
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\frac{
			C(\alpha,K_{\neq},K_\infty,
			\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_0\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)})
		}A\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2\le
		\frac1{128\pi}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
		|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta.
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	Substituting these three bounds into \eqref{eq:joint-after-frequency-coercivity}, we conclude that
	\begin{align*}
		&E'(t)+\frac1A
		\left(
		\|\nabla n\|_{L^2}^2
		+\|\nabla\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
		\right)+\frac1{64\pi}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
		|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta\\
		&+\frac1{
			64\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
			[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
		\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
		A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
		|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta
		\le\frac{C(\alpha,M)}A.
	\end{align*}
	This proves \eqref{eq:joint-differential}.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:joint-uniform}
Under the assumptions of Proposition \ref{prop:joint-energy},
after increasing its threshold for $A$ if necessary, we have
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-ODE}
		E'(t)+\frac1A E(t)\le\frac{C(\alpha,M)}A,

	\end{equation}

Suppose first that $M>0$.
By \eqref{eq:ordinary-mean-GN} and Young's inequality, we get
\[
4\pi\|n_0\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2
\le
2\pi\|\partial_yn_0\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2+C(M).
\]
This implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\|\nabla n\|_{L^2}^2=
	2\pi\|\partial_yn_0\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2
	+\|\nabla n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2\ge
	4\pi\|n_0\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2-C(M).
\end{aligned}
\]
Choose $A$ sufficiently large that
\[
A^{\frac23}\ge
128\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty].
\]
Since
$(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]\ge1$
and $|k|^{\frac23}\ge1$ for $k\ne0$,
Parseval's identity yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\frac1{
		64\pi(1+2K_\infty)^{\alpha-1}
		[1+2(1-\alpha)K_\infty]}
	&\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
	A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}
	|\widehat n(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta\ge\frac2A\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2,\\
	\frac1{64\pi}
	\sum_{k\ne0}\int_{\mathbb R}
	A^{-\frac13}|k|^{\frac23}&
	|\widehat\omega(t,k,\eta)|^2 d\eta
	\ge\frac2A\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Substituting these bounds into
\eqref{eq:joint-differential}, and using
$\|n\|_{L^2}^2=
2\pi\|n_0\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2
+\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2$,
we obtain
\[
E'(t)+\frac2A
\left(
\|n\|_{L^2}^2+\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
\right)
\le\frac{C(\alpha,M)}A.
\]
Hence it follows from \eqref{eq:full-energy-equivalence} that
\[E'(t)+\frac1A E(t)\le\frac{C(\alpha,M)}A.\]
Multiplying it by ${\rm e}^{\frac{t}{A}}$ and integrating over
$[0,t]$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
	E(t)\le
	{\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{A}}E(0)
	+\bigl(1-{\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{A}}\bigr)C(\alpha,M)\le
	\max\{E(0),C(\alpha,M)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
If $M=0$, nonnegativity and mass conservation imply
$n=0$. Under the same choice of $A$,
\eqref{eq:joint-differential} therefore gives
\[
E'(t)+\frac2A\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
\le\frac{C(\alpha,0)}A.
\]
The energy equivalence yields the same differential
inequality and bound for $E(t)$.
This proves \eqref{eq:joint-ODE}.

Let $[0,T]$ be a compact interval of a classical

Assume that \eqref{eq:full-bootstrap} holds and that
$A$ satisfies the threshold in

		\le

		\alpha,M,\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty},

	\end{equation}

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
If $M=0$, nonnegativity and mass conservation give $n=0$.
	We therefore assume $M>0$.

\noindent{\bf Step I. The $L^r$ energy estimate.}
For $r\ge2$, testing $\eqref{eq:rescaled-system}_1$
with $rn^{r-1}$ gives
	\begin{align*}
		\frac{d}{dt}
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n(t,x,y)^r dxdy~+&~
		\frac{r(r-1)}A
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		(1+n)^{\alpha-1}\bigl[1+(1-\alpha)n\bigr]
		n^{r-2}|\nabla n|^2 dxdy\\=~&
		\frac{r(r-1)}A
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^{r-1}(1+n)^\alpha\nabla c\cdot\nabla n dxdy.
	\end{align*}
	Young's inequality and
	Lemma \ref{lem:revised-coefficients} imply
	\begin{equation}\label{nr energy}
			\begin{aligned}
			\frac{d}{dt}
			\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n^r dxdy&+
			\frac{r(r-1)}{2A}
			\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			(1+n)^{\alpha-1}\bigl[1+(1-\alpha)n\bigr]
			n^{r-2}|\nabla n|^2 dxdy\\&\le
			\frac{C_\alpha r^2}A
			\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			\bigl(n^r+n^{r+\alpha}\bigr)|\nabla c|^2 dxdy.
		\end{aligned}	
	\end{equation}
The two lower bounds in Lemma \ref{lem:revised-coefficients} yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
	2(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]
	\ge 1+(1-\alpha)(1+n)^\alpha\ge 1+(1-\alpha)n^\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]
\ge\frac12\bigl[1+(1-\alpha)n^\alpha\bigr].
\]
Using
\[
\begin{aligned}
	n^{r-2}|\nabla n|^2=\frac4{r^2}|\nabla n^{\frac{r}{2}}|^2,\qquad
	n^{r+\alpha-2}|\nabla n|^2
	=\frac4{(r+\alpha)^2}
	|\nabla n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}|^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
we obtain, for $r\ge2$,
\begin{align}\label{result 1}
	&r(r-1)\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]
	n^{r-2}|\nabla n|^2dxdy\nonumber\\
	&\ge \frac{2(r-1)}r
	\|\nabla n^{\frac{r}{2}}\|_{L^2}^2
	+\frac{2(1-\alpha)r(r-1)}{(r+\alpha)^2}
	\|\nabla n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}\|_{L^2}^2\\\nonumber&\ge
	\|\nabla n^{\frac{r}{2}}\|_{L^2}^2
	+\frac{4(1-\alpha)}9
	\|\nabla n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}\|_{L^2}^2.
\end{align}
The last inequality follows from
$\frac{r-1}r\ge\frac12$ and $r+\alpha\le\frac32r$ with $r\ge2$.
For $q=\frac{r}{2}$ or $q=\frac{r+\alpha}{2}$, using
H\"older's inequality,
\eqref{eq:full-elliptic-gradient} and
\eqref{eq:cylinder-interpolation}, we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
	&\frac{C_\alpha r^2}{A}
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n^{2q}|\nabla c|^2 dxdy\\\le~&
	\frac{C_\alpha r^2}{A}
	\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}^2
	\|n^q\|_{L^2}\|\nabla n^q\|_{L^2}
	+\frac{C_\alpha r^2}{A}
	\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}^2
	\|n^q\|_{L^2}^2\\\le~&
	\frac{\varepsilon}{A}
	\|\nabla n^q\|_{L^2}^2
	+\frac{C(\alpha,\varepsilon)r^4}{A}
	\left(
	\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}^4
	+\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}^2
	\right)\|n^q\|_{L^2}^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
where we choose $\varepsilon=\frac14$ for $q=\frac{r}{2}$ and
$\varepsilon=\frac{1-\alpha}{9}$ for
$q=\frac{r+\alpha}{2}$.
Substituting \eqref{result 1} and the preceding
estimates into \eqref{nr energy}, and discarding the nonnegative term
$(4A)^{-1}\|\nabla n^{\frac{r}{2}}\|_{L^2}^2$, we obtain
	\begin{align}\label{eq:full-density-Moser-energy}
		&\frac d{dt}
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n^r dxdy
		+\frac{1-\alpha}{9A}
		\|\nabla n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}\|_{L^2}^2\nonumber\\\le~&
		\frac{C_\alpha r^4}{A}
		\left(
		\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}^4
		+\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}^2
		\right)
		\left(
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n^r dxdy
		+\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n^{r+\alpha}dxdy
		\right)\\\nonumber\le~&
		\frac{Cr^4}{A}
		\left(
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n^r dxdy
		+\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^{r+\alpha}dxdy
		\right),
	\end{align}
	where we used \eqref{eq:full-energy-equivalence} and \eqref{eq:joint-ODE}. Moreover, the constant
 $C$ depends only on $\alpha$, $M$,
$\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^2}$ and
$\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_{\neq}\|_{L^2}$,
and is independent of $r$, $A$ and $T$.
	
For nonzero $f\in L^1\cap H^1(\mathbb T\times\mathbb R)$,
the cylinder Nash inequality gives
\begin{equation*}
	\|\nabla f\|_{L^2}^2
	\ge
	\frac{\|f\|_{L^2}^4}{C\|f\|_{L^1}^2}
	-C\|f\|_{L^1}^2.
\end{equation*}
Applying the above inequality
to $f=n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}$, for each fixed $r\ge2$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
	&\|\nabla n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}\|_{L^2}^2\ge
	\frac1C
	\frac{
		\left(
		\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^{r+\alpha}dxdy
		\right)^2
	}{
		\left(
		\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}dxdy
		\right)^2}-
	C\left(
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}dxdy
	\right)^2\\
	&\ge
	\frac1C
	\frac{
		\left(
		\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^{r+\alpha}dxdy
		\right)^2
	}{
		\left(
		\displaystyle\sup_{0\le s\le T}
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}dxdy
		\right)^2}-C\left(
	\sup_{0\le s\le T}
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}dxdy
	\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Substituting this bound into
\eqref{eq:full-density-Moser-energy} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:review-Moser-ODE}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\frac d{dt}
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^rdxdy\le
		\frac{Cr^4}{A}
		\left(
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^rdxdy
		+\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^{r+\alpha}dxdy
		\right)\\&+
		\frac{C_\alpha}{A}
		\left(
		\sup_{0\le s\le T}
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}dxdy
		\right)^2-
		\frac1{C_\alpha A}
		\frac{\left(
			\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			n^{r+\alpha}dxdy
			\right)^2}{
			\left(
			\displaystyle\sup_{0\le s\le T}
			\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
			n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}dxdy
			\right)^2}.
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Noting that $n^r\le n^2$ on $\{n\le1\}$ and
$n^r\le n^{r+\alpha}$ on $\{n>1\}$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moser-moment-comparison}
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n^rdxdy
	\le\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}^2
	+\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n^{r+\alpha}dxdy.
\end{equation}
Choose $C_1\ge2$ sufficiently large.
If
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n^rdxdy
	>C_1r^4\biggl[1+\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}^2+
	\left(
	\sup_{0\le s\le T}
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}dxdy
	\right)^2
	\biggr],
\end{aligned}
\]
then \eqref{eq:moser-moment-comparison} implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moser-above-threshold}
	\begin{aligned}
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^{r+\alpha}dxdy
		>\frac{C_1r^4}{2}\biggl[1+\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}^2+
		\left(
		\sup_{0\le s\le T}
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}dxdy
		\right)^2
		\biggr].
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n^rdxdy\le
2\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n^{r+\alpha}dxdy$ and
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\left(
	\sup_{0\le s\le T}
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}dxdy
	\right)^2\le
	\frac2{C_1r^4}
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n^{r+\alpha}dxdy.
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, \eqref{eq:moser-above-threshold} gives
\[
\frac{
	\left(
	\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n^{r+\alpha}dxdy
	\right)^2
}{\left(
	\displaystyle\sup_{0\le s\le T}
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n(s,x,y)^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}dxdy
	\right)^2}\ge
\frac{C_1r^4}{2}
\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
n^{r+\alpha}dxdy.
\]
Consequently, \eqref{eq:review-Moser-ODE} yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\frac d{dt}
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n^rdxdy
	&\le
	\frac1A
	\left(
	3Cr^4+\frac{2C_\alpha}{C_1r^4}
	-\frac{C_1r^4}{2C_\alpha}
	\right)
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n^{r+\alpha}dxdy\\
	&\le
	\frac{r^4}{A}
	\left(
	3C+C_\alpha-\frac{C_1}{2C_\alpha}
	\right)
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n^{r+\alpha}dxdy.
\end{aligned}
\]
Choose $C_1>2C_\alpha(3C+C_\alpha)$.
The preceding estimate then gives
\[
\frac d{dt}
\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}n^rdxdy<0.
\]
Since the integral is absolutely continuous in time,
it is decreasing on every interval where it exceeds
that threshold. Consequently, its value is bounded
by the maximum of its initial value and the threshold.
Writing $C$ for the chosen constant $C_1$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-density-Moser-recursion}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\sup_{0\le t\le T}
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^rdxdy\\\le~&
		\max\biggl\{
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n_{\mathrm{in}}^rdxdy, 
		Cr^4\biggl[
		1+\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}^2+
		\left(
		\sup_{0\le s\le T}
		\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
		n^{\frac{r+\alpha}{2}}dxdy
		\right)^2
		\biggr]\biggr\}.
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent{\bf Step II. The Moser iteration.}
Set
$r_j=\alpha+(2-\alpha)2^j$ for $j\in\{0,1,\ldots\}$.
Then
$r_0=2$ and $\frac{r_j+\alpha}{2}=r_{j-1}$ with $j\ge1$.
For every $j\ge0$, mass conservation at the initial
time gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
	n_{\mathrm{in}}(x,y)^{r_j}dxdy\le
	M\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty}^{r_j-1}\le
	\max\bigl\{
	1,M,\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty}
	\bigr\}^{r_j}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Applying \eqref{eq:full-density-Moser-recursion}
with $r=r_j$ and taking $r_j$-th roots, for $j\geq 1$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\max\biggl\{
		1,M,\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty},\|n\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}, \sup_{0\le t\le T}\|n(t)\|_{L^{r_j}}
		\biggr\}\\\le~&
		(Cr_j^4)^{\frac{1}{r_j}}
		\max\biggl\{
		1,M,\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty},
		\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)},
		\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|n(t)\|_{L^{r_{j-1}}}
		\biggr\}^{\frac{2r_{j-1}}{r_j}},
		\label{eq:moser-norm-recursion}
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The products of the iteration exponents satisfy
$\prod_{\ell=1}^j\frac{2r_{\ell-1}}{r_\ell}
=\frac{2^{j+1}}{r_j}
\le\frac2{2-\alpha}$.
Similarly, for $1\le i\le j$,
\[
\prod_{\ell=i+1}^j
\frac{2r_{\ell-1}}{r_\ell}
=
\frac{2^{j-i}r_i}{r_j}
\le\frac2{2-\alpha},
\]
where the product equals one when $i=j$.
Taking logarithms in
\eqref{eq:moser-norm-recursion} and iterating,
we therefore obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
	&\log\max\biggl\{
	1,M,\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty},
	\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)},\;
	\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|n(t)\|_{L^{r_j}}
	\biggr\}\\\le~&
	\frac2{2-\alpha}
	\log\max\bigl\{
	1,M,\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty},
	\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}
	\bigr\}+
	\frac2{2-\alpha}
	\sum_{i=1}^j
	\frac{\log C+4\log r_i}{r_i}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Due to $(2-\alpha)2^i\le r_i\le2^{i+1}$,
we have
\[
\sum_{i=1}^\infty
\frac{\log C+4\log r_i}{r_i}
\le
\frac1{2-\alpha}
\sum_{i=1}^\infty
\frac{\log C+4(i+1)\log2}{2^i}
<\infty.
\]
The preceding logarithmic estimate and the bound
for the series imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|n(t)\|_{L^{r_j}}
	\le
	\exp\left(
	\frac{2(\log C+12\log2)}{(2-\alpha)^2}
	\right)
	\max\bigl\{
	1,M,\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty},
	\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}
	\bigr\}^{\frac2{2-\alpha}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since the right-hand side is independent of $j$, there holds
\[
\sup_{j\ge0}\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\|n(t)\|_{L^{r_j}}
\le
C\left(
\alpha,M,\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty},
\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}
\right).
\]
Letting $j\to\infty$ yields
\[
\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^\infty)}
\le
C\left(
\alpha,M,\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty},
\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}
\right).
\]
By \eqref{eq:full-energy-equivalence}, \eqref{eq:joint-ODE} and
$\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^2}^2
\le M\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty}$, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\|n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}^2&\le
	\frac43\max\{E(0),C(\alpha,M)\}\\&\le
	\frac43\max\left\{
	\frac54\left(
	M\|n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty}
	+\|(\omega_{\mathrm{in}})_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2
	\right),C(\alpha,M)
	\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Substituting this bound into the preceding
$L^\infty$ estimate proves
\eqref{eq:full-density-Moser-bound}.

\noindent\textbf{1. Local existence.}
The perturbation velocity satisfies
\[
\partial_tv+y\partial_xv+(v_2,0)
+\frac1A v\cdot\nabla v+\frac1A\nabla p
=\frac1A\Delta v+\frac1Ane_2,
\qquad \nabla\cdot v=0.
\]
We make the coordinate change $(x,y)\mapsto(x+ty,y)$.
For a scalar function $f$,
\[
\partial_t\bigl[f(t,x+ty,y)\bigr]
=(\partial_tf+y\partial_xf)(t,x+ty,y).
\]
We compose $n$, $c$ and $p$ with this map and replace
the velocity by $(v_1-tv_2,v_2)(t,x+ty,y)$.
The latter transformation preserves incompressibility, since
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\partial_x\bigl[(v_1-tv_2)(t,x+ty,y)\bigr]
	+\partial_y\bigl[v_2(t,x+ty,y)\bigr]=(\partial_xv_1+\partial_yv_2)(t,x+ty,y)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
We denote the transformed functions by the same symbols.
The original spatial derivatives become $\partial_x$ and
$\partial_y-t\partial_x$, and therefore
\begin{equation*}
	\partial_x^2+(\partial_y-t\partial_x)^2
	=\nabla\cdot\left[
	\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&-t\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}\nabla
	\right].
\end{equation*}
The matrix has determinant one and trace $2+t^2$. Thus, for
$0\le t\le T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-transformed-ellipticity}
	\frac{k^2+\eta^2}{2+T^2}
	\le k^2+(\eta-tk)^2
	\le(2+T^2)(k^2+\eta^2).
\end{equation}
In particular, the transformed diffusion is uniformly elliptic
on finite time intervals. The coordinate and velocity
transformations, and their inverses, are bounded on $H^j$
and $W^{j,6}$, with bounds uniform on such intervals.
The coordinate change also preserves Lebesgue measure.
The transformed equations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-transformed-system}
	\left\{
	\begin{aligned}
		~&\partial_tn=\frac1A\nabla\cdot\left[
		\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&-t\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}
		(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]\nabla n
		\right]\\
		&\quad\quad\quad-\frac1A\nabla\cdot\left[
		\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&-t\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}
		n(1+n)^\alpha\nabla c+vn\right],\\~&
		\bigl[1-\partial_x^2-(\partial_y-t\partial_x)^2\bigr]c
		=n,\\~&
		\partial_tv-\frac1A
		\bigl[\partial_x^2+(\partial_y-t\partial_x)^2\bigr]v=-2(v_2,0)-\frac1A v\cdot\nabla v\\
		&\quad\quad\quad+\frac1A n(-t,1)-\frac1A
		\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&-t\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}\nabla p,
		\\~&
		\nabla\cdot v=0.
	\end{aligned}
	\right.
\end{equation}
The term $-2(v_2,0)$ combines the term $-(v_2,0)$ in
the original equation with the term $-(v_2,0)$ obtained
by differentiating $v_1-tv_2$ in time.
At $t=0$, both transformations are the identity.
To eliminate the pressure, we apply the Fourier multiplier
with symbol
\begin{equation*}
	I-\frac{
		\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&-t\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}
		\binom{k}{\eta}(k,\eta)
	}{k^2+(\eta-tk)^2},
	\qquad (k,\eta)\ne(0,0),
\end{equation*}
and value $I$ at the origin.
Incompressibility gives
$(k,\eta)\widehat v=0$ and $(k,\eta)\partial_t\widehat v=0$,
so this multiplier leaves $v$ and $\partial_tv$ unchanged.
Moreover,
\[
(k,\eta)
\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&-t\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}
\binom{k}{\eta}=k^2+(\eta-tk)^2.
\]
Consequently, it annihilates the transformed pressure
gradient, whose Fourier transform is
$\mathrm i
\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&-t\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}
\binom{k}{\eta}\widehat p$.
It commutes with the transformed Laplacian because the
latter has a scalar Fourier symbol. The multiplier is the
usual Leray projection conjugated by the coordinate and
velocity transformations. It is therefore bounded on $L^6$
and $H^j$, uniformly on finite time intervals.

We apply the local parabolic theory with time integrability
exponent six and spatial spaces $L^6$ and $W^{2,6}$.
The corresponding initial-data space is $W^{\frac53,6}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-initial-embedding}
	H^3(\mathbb T\times\mathbb R)
	\hookrightarrow W^{\frac53,6}(\mathbb T\times\mathbb R)
	\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb T\times\mathbb R).
\end{equation}
For the velocity, we use the divergence-free subspaces.
Since $n_{\mathrm{in}}\ge0$, a sufficiently small bounded
neighborhood of $n_{\mathrm{in}}$ in $W^{\frac53,6}$ satisfies
$n>-1/2$. On this neighborhood the coefficient functions
are smooth with bounded derivatives, and
$(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]\ge\frac12$.
Together with \eqref{eq:local-transformed-ellipticity}, this
gives uniform ellipticity of the density equation.
Expanding its diffusion and chemotactic terms gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
	&\nabla\cdot\left[
	\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&-t\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}
	(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]\nabla n\right]=(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]\\&\times
	\bigl[\partial_x^2+(\partial_y-t\partial_x)^2\bigr]n+\alpha(1-\alpha)n(1+n)^{\alpha-2}
	\left(|\partial_xn|^2
	+|(\partial_y-t\partial_x)n|^2\right),\\[1mm]
	&\nabla\cdot\left[
	\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&-t\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}
	n(1+n)^\alpha\nabla c\right]\\
	&=\bigl[(1+n)^\alpha
	+\alpha n(1+n)^{\alpha-1}\bigr]
	\left[\partial_xn\,\partial_xc
	+(\partial_y-t\partial_x)n\,(\partial_y-t\partial_x)c\right]+n(1+n)^\alpha(c-n).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the last identity uses the chemical equation in
\eqref{eq:local-transformed-system}. The elliptic estimates
on each finite interval give
\[
\|c\|_{W^{2,6}}\le C_T\|n\|_{L^6},
\qquad
\|\nabla c\|_{L^\infty}\le C_T\|n\|_{L^\infty}.
\]
The product estimates
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\||\nabla n|^2\|_{L^6}
	\le\|\nabla n\|_{L^\infty}\|\nabla n\|_{L^6},\qquad
	\|v\cdot\nabla n\|_{L^6}
	\le\|v\|_{L^\infty}\|\nabla n\|_{L^6},
\end{aligned}
\]
$$\|v\cdot\nabla v\|_{L^6}
\le\|v\|_{L^\infty}\|\nabla v\|_{L^6} $$
and the same estimates for differences show that the
terms other than the principal diffusion are locally
Lipschitz from $W^{\frac53,6}\times W^{\frac53,6}$ into
$L^6\times L^6$. The principal density operator also
depends locally Lipschitz continuously on $n$ as an
operator from $W^{2,6}$ into $L^6$.
Freezing its coefficient at $n_{\mathrm{in}}$ gives
\[
-\frac1A(1+n_{\mathrm{in}})^{\alpha-1}
[1+(1-\alpha)n_{\mathrm{in}}]
\bigl[\partial_x^2+(\partial_y-t\partial_x)^2\bigr].
\]
Its coefficients have bounded spatial H\"older derivatives
by $n_{\mathrm{in}}\in H^3$, are smooth in time, and are
uniformly elliptic. The linear maximal regularity theorem
\cite[Theorem 1.23]{Amann2017} applies on the flat cylinder.
It also applies to the transformed heat operator, which
preserves the divergence-free subspace.
For $n$ sufficiently close to $n_{\mathrm{in}}$ in
$C([0,T_*];W^{\frac53,6})$, the coefficient difference satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
	&\left\|\left\{
	(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]
	-(1+n_{\mathrm{in}})^{\alpha-1}
	[1+(1-\alpha)n_{\mathrm{in}}]
	\right\}
	\bigl[\partial_x^2+(\partial_y-t\partial_x)^2\bigr]h
	\right\|_{L^6}\\
	&\le C\|n-n_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^\infty}
	\|h\|_{W^{2,6}},\qquad h\in W^{2,6}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The principal density operator is therefore a small
perturbation of the frozen operator from $W^{2,6}$ to $L^6$,
uniformly in time, and retains maximal regularity.
The remaining terms belong to $L^\infty(0,T_*;L^6)$
for continuous $W^{\frac53,6}$ trajectories.
Thus the hypotheses of \cite[Theorem 2.1]{Amann2005}
hold with time exponent six and forcing exponent infinity.
This theorem yields a unique local
solution satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-maximal-regularity}
	n,v\in W^{1,6}(0,T_*;L^6)
	\cap L^6(0,T_*;W^{2,6})
	\cap C([0,T_*];W^{\frac53,6}).
\end{equation}
We choose $T_*$ strictly inside this existence interval.
Then \eqref{eq:local-initial-embedding} bounds $n$, $v$
and their first spatial derivatives on $[0,T_*]$.

\noindent\textbf{2. The $H^3$ estimate and local regularity.}
We first prove an estimate for smooth solutions in the
original coordinates. Suppose that $n>-\frac12$ and that
$n$, $\nabla n$ and $\nabla v$ are bounded on the interval
under consideration. In this estimate, $C$ depends on
$\alpha$, the fixed value of $A$, and these bounds, but
not on higher Sobolev norms. For $|\ell|\le3$, the Sobolev and commutator estimates give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-H3-product-estimates}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\|(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]-1\|_{H^3}
		+\|n(1+n)^\alpha\|_{H^3}\le C\|n\|_{H^3},\\
		&\left\|\left[\partial^\ell,
		(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]\right]\nabla n
		\right\|_{L^2}\le C\|n\|_{H^3},\\
		&\|[\partial^\ell,v\cdot\nabla]n\|_{L^2}
		\le C\left(
		\|\nabla v\|_{L^\infty}\|n\|_{H^3}
		+\|\nabla n\|_{L^\infty}\|v\|_{H^3}\right),\\
		&\|[\partial^\ell,v\cdot\nabla]v\|_{L^2}
		\le C\|\nabla v\|_{L^\infty}\|v\|_{H^3}.
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The subtraction of one in the diffusion-coefficient
estimate is necessary because a nonzero constant does
not belong to $L^2(\mathbb T\times\mathbb R)$.
The elliptic estimates and the Sobolev product estimate
further imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-H3-chemotaxis}
	\begin{aligned}
		\|n(1+n)^\alpha\nabla c\|_{H^3}
		&\le C\|n(1+n)^\alpha\|_{L^\infty}\|\nabla c\|_{H^3}
		+C\|n(1+n)^\alpha\|_{H^3}\|\nabla c\|_{L^\infty}\\
		&\le C\|n\|_{H^3},
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where
$\|\nabla c\|_{H^3}\le C\|n\|_{H^2}$ and
$\|\nabla c\|_{L^\infty}\le C\|n\|_{L^\infty}$.
Apply $\partial^\ell$ to the density and velocity
equations, test with $2\partial^\ell n$ and
$2\partial^\ell v$, and sum over $|\ell|\le3$.
Incompressibility eliminates the leading velocity
transport and pressure terms. For the Couette transport, we have
\[
[\partial^\ell,y\partial_x]f
=\ell_2\partial_x^{\ell_1+1}\partial_y^{\ell_2-1}f
\quad\text{if}\quad\ell_2\ge1,
\]
and the commutator vanishes if $\ell_2=0$.
These terms are therefore bounded by the $H^3$ energy.
Using \eqref{eq:local-H3-product-estimates},
\eqref{eq:local-H3-chemotaxis} and the diffusion lower
bound $\frac12$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
	&\frac d{dt}\bigl(\|n\|_{H^3}^2+\|v\|_{H^3}^2\bigr)
	+\frac1A\|\nabla n\|_{H^3}^2
	+\frac2A\|\nabla v\|_{H^3}^2\\\le~& C\bigl(\|n\|_{H^3}^2+\|v\|_{H^3}^2\bigr)
	+\frac CA\|n\|_{H^3}\|\nabla n\|_{H^3}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Young's inequality bounds the last term by
$(2A)^{-1}\|\nabla n\|_{H^3}^2+CA^{-1}\|n\|_{H^3}^2$.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuation-H3-energy}
	\begin{aligned}
		\frac d{dt}\bigl(\|n\|_{H^3}^2+\|v\|_{H^3}^2\bigr)
		+\frac1{2A}\bigl(
		\|\nabla n\|_{H^3}^2+\|\nabla v\|_{H^3}^2\bigr)\le C\bigl(\|n\|_{H^3}^2+\|v\|_{H^3}^2\bigr).
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We now approximate the initial data in $H^3$ by smooth
data, preserving nonnegativity and incompressibility.
By \cite[Theorem 3.1]{Amann2005}, the corresponding
solutions exist on a common interval $[0,T_*]$ and
have uniform bounds in \eqref{eq:local-maximal-regularity}.
After decreasing $T_*$ if necessary, their densities
remain greater than $-\frac12$. The embedding
\eqref{eq:local-initial-embedding} gives uniform bounds
for their first spatial derivatives.
Applying \eqref{eq:continuation-H3-energy} and
Gronwall's inequality yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-H3-uniform}
	\begin{aligned}
		\sup_{0\le t\le T_*}
		\bigl(\|n(t)\|_{H^3}^2+\|v(t)\|_{H^3}^2\bigr)+\frac1A\int_0^{T_*}
		\bigl(\|n(t)\|_{H^4}^2+\|v(t)\|_{H^4}^2\bigr)dt
		\le C,
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $C$ independent of the approximation. 
Continuous dependence identifies the limit with the
solution in \eqref{eq:local-maximal-regularity}.
Weak compactness and \eqref{eq:local-H3-uniform} give
$n,v\in L^\infty(0,T_*;H^3)\cap L^2(0,T_*;H^4)$.
The transformed equations, after eliminating the
pressure, then give
$\partial_tn,\partial_tv\in L^2(0,T_*;H^2)$.
The time-continuity theorem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-H3-time-continuity}
	L^2(0,T_*;H^4)\cap W^{1,2}(0,T_*;H^2)
	\hookrightarrow C([0,T_*];H^3)
\end{equation}
therefore proves
\[
n,v\in C([0,T_*];H^3)\cap L^2(0,T_*;H^4).
\]
Returning to the original variables preserves this
regularity. Parabolic regularity gives a classical
solution for positive times, and
Lemma \ref{lem:revised-mass} gives nonnegativity and
mass conservation.

\noindent\textbf{3. Finite-time estimates.}
Suppose that $T_{\max}<\infty$ and
\eqref{eq:continuation-density-assumption} holds, that is,
\[
\sup_{0\le t<T_{\max}}
\bigl(\|n(t)\|_{L^2}+\|n(t)\|_{L^\infty}\bigr)<\infty.
\]
In the remainder of the proof, constants may depend
on $\alpha$, $A$, $T_{\max}$, the initial data and these
density bounds, but are independent of $T<T_{\max}$.

\noindent{\bf (a) Velocity and vorticity energy estimates.}
Testing the full vorticity equation with $2\omega$ gives
\[
\frac d{dt}\|\omega\|_{L^2}^2
+\frac2A\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}^2
=\frac2A\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
n_{\neq}\partial_x\omega dxdy.
\]
Here incompressibility eliminates the transport term.
Young's inequality yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuation-vorticity}
	\frac d{dt}\|\omega\|_{L^2}^2
	+\frac1A\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}^2
	\le\frac1A\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^2}^2.
\end{equation}
Testing the perturbation velocity equation with $2v$
similarly gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\frac d{dt}\|v\|_{L^2}^2
	+\frac2A\|\nabla v\|_{L^2}^2&=-2\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}v_1v_2dxdy
	+\frac2A\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}nv_2 dxdy\\
	&\le\left(1+\frac1A\right)\|v\|_{L^2}^2
	+\frac1A\|n\|_{L^2}^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
For divergence-free $v$, Parseval's identity gives
\[
\|\nabla v\|_{L^2}^2=\|\omega\|_{L^2}^2,
\qquad
\|\nabla^2v\|_{L^2}^2=\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}^2,
\]
where the second-derivative norm sums over all ordered
second derivatives. Integrating
\eqref{eq:continuation-vorticity} and applying
Gronwall's inequality to the velocity estimate gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuation-velocity-energy}
	\sup_{0\le t<T_{\max}}
	\bigl(\|v(t)\|_{H^1}^2+\|\omega(t)\|_{L^2}^2\bigr)
	+\int_0^{T_{\max}}\|v(t)\|_{H^2}^2 dt<\infty.
\end{equation}

\noindent{\bf (b) H\"older estimates.}
Fix $0<\tau<T_{\max}$ and use the coordinate
transformation from part 1. The bounds in
\eqref{eq:continuation-velocity-energy} hold in these
variables as well. The two-dimensional Sobolev
embeddings imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\|v\cdot\nabla v\|_{L^2(0,T_{\max};L^6)}
	&\le\|v\|_{L^\infty(0,T_{\max};L^{12})}
	\|\nabla v\|_{L^2(0,T_{\max};L^{12})}\\
	&\le C\|v\|_{L^\infty(0,T_{\max};H^1)}
	\|v\|_{L^2(0,T_{\max};H^2)}<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
Also,
$\|n(t)\|_{L^6}^6\le
\|n(t)\|_{L^\infty}^4\|n(t)\|_{L^2}^2$.
Hence the right-hand side of the velocity equation
after eliminating the pressure belongs to
$L^2(0,T_{\max};L^6)$.
The evolution operator for its transformed heat part
has Fourier symbol
\[
\exp\left[-\frac1A\int_s^t
\bigl(k^2+(\eta-\sigma k)^2\bigr)d\sigma\right].
\]
By \eqref{eq:local-transformed-ellipticity}, its kernel
is a periodized Gaussian with covariance comparable
to $(t-s)I$. For $0<\gamma<\frac23$, its
$L^6$ to $C^\gamma$ norm is bounded by
$C(t-s)^{-\frac16-\frac{\gamma}{2}}$. Noting that
$\int_0^{T_{\max}}r^{-\frac13-\gamma}dr<\infty$,
Duhamel's formula and Cauchy--Schwarz give a uniform
spatial $C^\gamma$ bound for $v$ on
$[\frac{\tau}{4},T_{\max})$.

For the difference of these evolution operators at
final times $t+h$ and $t$, with the same initial time
$s<t$, Gaussian differentiation gives the
$L^6$ to $L^\infty$ bound
$C\min\{(t-s)^{-\frac16}, h(t-s)^{-\frac76}\}$.
The first bound follows by estimating the operators
separately; the second follows by integrating their
final-time derivatives over an interval of length $h$.
Splitting the Duhamel integral at $t-s=h$ and including
the integral over $[t,t+h]$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\|v(t+h)-v(t)\|_{L^\infty}
	\le C_\tau h
	+C\left[
	\int_0^h r^{-\frac13}dr
	+h^2\int_h^{T_{\max}}r^{-\frac73}dr
	\right]^{\frac12}\le C_\tau h^{\frac13}
\end{aligned}
\]
for $t\ge\frac{\tau}{4}$, $t+h<T_{\max}$ and
$0<h\le\min\{1,T_{\max}\}$. The initial heat evolution
is bounded by the first term. Larger time increments
are controlled by the uniform supremum bound.
Thus $v$ is uniformly bounded in
$C^{\gamma,\frac{\gamma}{2}}$ on this interval.
The transformed full vorticity satisfies
\[
\partial_t\omega-\frac1A
\bigl[\partial_x^2+(\partial_y-t\partial_x)^2\bigr]\omega
+\frac1A v\cdot\nabla\omega
=-\frac1A\partial_xn.
\]
Its diffusion is uniformly elliptic, its drift is
bounded on $[\frac{\tau}{4},T_{\max})$, and its divergence
forcing has the bounded vector $(-\frac{n}{A},0)$.
The scalar local boundedness and H\"older estimates
in \cite{LSU}, together with the $L^2$ bound in
\eqref{eq:continuation-velocity-energy}, therefore
give boundedness and H\"older continuity of $\omega$
on $[\frac{\tau}{2},T_{\max})$.
The density diffusion matrix is
\[
\frac1A(1+n)^{\alpha-1}[1+(1-\alpha)n]
\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&-t\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is bounded and uniformly elliptic because $n$ is
bounded and nonnegative. Its remaining divergence
terms are bounded, since $n$ and $v$ are bounded and
$\|\nabla c(t)\|_{L^\infty}\le C\|n(t)\|_{L^\infty}$.
The same scalar H\"older estimates apply to $n$.
After reducing the exponent, we obtain some
$0<\beta<\gamma$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuation-holder-bounds}
	\|n\|_{C^{\beta,\frac{\beta}{2}}}
	+\|\omega\|_{C^{\beta,\frac{\beta}{2}}}
	+\|v\|_{C^{\beta,\frac{\beta}{2}}}<\infty
\end{equation}
on $[\frac{\tau}{2},T_{\max})\times\mathbb T\times\mathbb R$.
These estimates are uniform in space and in
$T<T_{\max}$: they are applied on backward cylinders
of a fixed radius whose bottom times exceed $\frac{\tau}{4}$.

\noindent{\bf (c) Bounds for the first derivatives.}
In the transformed variables, the chemical equation
has the representation
\[
c(t)=\int_0^\infty {\rm e}^{-r}
{\rm e}^{r[\partial_x^2+(\partial_y-t\partial_x)^2]}
n(t)dr.
\]
The gradient heat kernel has $L^1$ norm at most
$Cr^{-\frac12}$, so spatial differences give
$\|\nabla c(t)\|_{C^\beta}\le C\|n(t)\|_{C^\beta}$.
For time differences, use
\[
\partial_t
{\rm e}^{r[\partial_x^2+(\partial_y-t\partial_x)^2]}
=-2r\partial_x(\partial_y-t\partial_x)
{\rm e}^{r[\partial_x^2+(\partial_y-t\partial_x)^2]}.
\]
After one further spatial derivative, the kernel
has $L^1$ norm at most $Crr^{-\frac32}=Cr^{-\frac12}$.
Since $\int_0^\infty {\rm e}^{-r}r^{-\frac12}dr<\infty$,
both the gradient screened kernel and its time
derivative have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms.
Consequently,
\[
\|\nabla c(t)-\nabla c(s)\|_{L^\infty}
\le C\|n(t)-n(s)\|_{L^\infty}
+C|t-s|\|n(s)\|_{L^\infty}.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:continuation-holder-bounds},
this proves a uniform $C^{\beta,\frac{\beta}{2}}$ bound for
$\nabla c$ on $[\frac{\tau}{2},T_{\max})$.
The density diffusion matrix and the vector
\[
n(1+n)^\alpha
\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&-t\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}\nabla c+nv
\]
are now uniformly bounded in $C^{\beta,\frac{\beta}{2}}$.
The interior divergence-form parabolic Schauder
estimate in \cite{LSU}, applied on smaller backward
cylinders, yields
\[
\sup_{\tau\le t<T_{\max}}
\|\nabla n(t)\|_{L^\infty}<\infty.
\]
The coordinate change preserves this spatial
gradient bound.
For the velocity gradient, return to the original
coordinates. At each fixed time the spatial H\"older
bounds for $\omega$ remain uniform. The identities
$v_{\neq}=\nabla^\perp\Delta^{-1}\omega_{\neq}$,
$\partial_yv_{1,0}=\omega_0$,
$v_{2,0}=0$
and the heat representation on non-zero modes give
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^\infty}
	\le\|\omega_0(t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
	+C[\omega_{\neq}(t)]_{C^\beta}
	\int_0^1 r^{-1+\frac{\beta}{2}}dr+C\|\omega_{\neq}(t)\|_{L^2}
	\int_1^\infty {\rm e}^{-\frac{r}{2}}dr.
\end{aligned}
\]
For $0<r\le1$, cancellation of the second derivative
of the heat kernel permits subtraction of the
vorticity value at the evaluation point, giving the
factor $r^{\frac{\beta}{2}}$. For $r>1$, the non-zero $x$
frequencies give exponential decay.
Both integrals are finite. We have therefore proved,
in the original variables,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuation-gradient-bounds}
	\sup_{\tau\le t<T_{\max}}
	\bigl(\|\nabla n(t)\|_{L^\infty}
	+\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^\infty}\bigr)<\infty.
\end{equation}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4. Continuation.}
The density bounds and
\eqref{eq:continuation-gradient-bounds} allow us to
apply \eqref{eq:continuation-H3-energy} on $[\tau,T]$,
with $C$ independent of $T<T_{\max}$.
Gronwall's inequality gives
\[
\|n(t)\|_{H^3}^2+\|v(t)\|_{H^3}^2
\le\bigl(\|n(\tau)\|_{H^3}^2+\|v(\tau)\|_{H^3}^2\bigr)
{\rm e}^{C(t-\tau)}.
\]
Integrating \eqref{eq:continuation-H3-energy} then yields
\[
\sup_{\tau\le t<T_{\max}}
\bigl(\|n(t)\|_{H^3}^2+\|v(t)\|_{H^3}^2\bigr)
+\int_\tau^{T_{\max}}
\bigl(\|n(t)\|_{H^4}^2+\|v(t)\|_{H^4}^2\bigr)dt
<\infty.
\]
In the transformed variables, the equations imply
$\partial_tn,\partial_tv\in L^2(\tau,T_{\max};H^2)$.
Applying the time-continuity theorem used in
\eqref{eq:local-H3-time-continuity} on
$[\tau,T_{\max}]$ gives strong $H^3$ limits at
$T_{\max}$. The inverse coordinate and velocity
transformations are uniformly bounded and strongly
continuous on $H^3$ for finite times, so the original
fields have the same terminal regularity.
The terminal velocity is divergence free, and the
terminal density is nonnegative. Fatou's lemma gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb T\times\mathbb R}
n(T_{\max},x,y)dxdy\le M.
\]
Thus the terminal data satisfy the hypotheses of
the local existence result in parts 1--2.
Restarting the original system at $T_{\max}$ extends
the solution beyond $T_{\max}$, contradicting
maximality. This proves the continuation assertion.
