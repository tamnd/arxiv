<!-- question-1: body_md -->
\label{que1.1}
Let $n\geq3$ and let $f\colon\mathbb S^n\to\mathbb S^n$ be an orientation-preserving branched cover. Does there exist a metric $d$ inducing the original topology on the source sphere such that $(\mathbb S^n,d)$ is Ahlfors $n$-regular and linearly locally contractible, and $f\colon(\mathbb S^n,d)\to(\mathbb S^n,\rho)$ has bounded length distortion?
<!-- end question-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm1.2}
For every $n\geq3$ there is an orientation-preserving degree-two branched cover
\[
 F_n\colon\mathbb S^n\longrightarrow\mathbb S^n
\]
for which no compatible source metric is simultaneously quasiconvex, linearly locally contractible, and makes $F_n$ BLD to the fixed round target.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor1.3}
For every $n\geq3$ there is a branched cover $\mathbb S^n\to\mathbb S^n$ admitting no compatible Ahlfors $n$-regular, linearly locally contractible source metric for which it is BLD. Thus the answer to Heinonen--Semmes Question~27 is negative in every stated dimension.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm1.4}
Let $f\colon M\to N$ be as above.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $d_f$ is a compatible geodesic metric, $f\colon(M,d_f)\to(N,\rho_N)$ is $1$-BLD, and $(M,d_f)$ is Ahlfors $n$-regular with constants depending only on $n$, $m$ and $N$.
\item If $d$ is a compatible $C$-quasiconvex metric on $M$ for which $f$ is $L$-BLD, then $L^{-1}d\le d_f\le LC\,d$.
\item $f$ admits a compatible quasiconvex, linearly locally contractible metric for which it is BLD if and only if $(M,d_f)$ is linearly locally contractible. If $M$ is homeomorphic to a smooth manifold, the same holds with ``quasiconvex'' replaced by ``Ahlfors $n$-regular''. In particular, for $M=N=\mathbb S^n$, Question~\ref{que1.1} has a positive answer for $f$ if and only if $(\mathbb S^n,d_f)$ is linearly locally contractible.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm1.5}
Let $(M,D)$ be a closed connected oriented topological $n$-manifold with a compatible length metric and contractibility data $(\lambda,r_*)$, let $(N,\rho_N)$ be a closed connected oriented Riemannian $n$-manifold, and let $f\colon(M,D)\to(N,\rho_N)$ be an orientation-preserving $L$-BLD branched cover of degree $m\ge2$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the branch-value set $f(B_f)$ is uniformly $\delta$-porous in $N$ at small scales, with $\delta=\delta(n,L,\lambda,m)>0$; consequently $\dim_{\mathcal H}f(B_f)\le\dim_Af(B_f)\le n-c(n,L,\lambda,m)<n$ and $\operatorname{vol}_N(f(B_f))=0$;
\item $f$ satisfies the Newman property of Luisto~\cite{L2019} at every branch point, with a constant depending only on $n,L,\lambda,m$, and the branch set $B_f$ is uniformly porous in $(M,D)$ at small scales.
\end{enumerate}
No measure on $M$ is specified as an assumption or used in the obstruction argument. In this finite degree setting, however, Ahlfors regularity of $M$ follows from Theorem~\ref{thm1.4}(i),(ii); thus the absence of an explicit doubling assumption is not an enlargement beyond all doubling source spaces. The scale thresholds depend in addition on $r_*$ and on $N$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor1.6}
For every $n\ge3$, the degree-two cover $F_n$ of Theorem~\ref{thm1.2} can be chosen so that its branch value set $A_n=F_n(B_{F_n})$ has the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A_n$ is ambiently homeomorphic to the standard equator $\mathbb S^{n-2}\subset\mathbb S^n$ and is contained in a round great $(n-1)$-sphere;
\item $A_n$ is uniformly $1/4$-porous at all scales below $\pi$, and $\dim_{\mathcal H}A_n=n-2$;
\item $(\mathbb S^n,d_{F_n})$ is a compatible Ahlfors $n$-regular geodesic space and $F_n$ is $1$-BLD from it, but this space is not linearly locally contractible.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, uniform porosity of branch values, even together with the dimension and ambient topology in \textup{(i)}--\textup{(ii)}, does not imply admissibility.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def2.1}
Following~\cite{BMW2025,S1996}, $(X,d)$ is \emph{linearly locally contractible} with contractibility data $(\lambda,r_*)$, where $\lambda\ge1$ and $r_*>0$, if for every $x\in X$ and $0<r<r_*$ the inclusion $B(x,r)\hookrightarrow B(x,\lambda r)$ is null-homotopic in $B(x,\lambda r)$.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm2.2}
Let $X$ be a metric space homeomorphic to a closed orientable smooth $n$-manifold, $n\ge2$. If $X$ is Ahlfors $n$-regular and linearly locally contractible, then $(X,d,\mathcal H^n_d)$ supports a weak $(1,1)$-Poincar\'e inequality.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor2.3}
Every compatible Ahlfors $n$-regular, linearly locally contractible metric on a closed connected oriented manifold homeomorphic to a smooth manifold is quasiconvex.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The space is compact, connected and complete, its Hausdorff measure is doubling, and Theorem~\ref{thm2.2} gives a Poincar\'e inequality. Quasiconvexity follows from~\cite[Proposition~4.8 and Lemma~4.9]{BB2018}; a ball of radius larger than the diameter gives one global constant.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem2.4}
Let $(X,d)$ be compact and $C$-quasiconvex, and let
\[
 D(x,x')=\inf\{\ell_d(\gamma)\colon\gamma\text{ joins }x,x'\}.
\]
Then $D$ is a compatible compact length metric, $d\le D\le Cd$, and $\ell_D(\gamma)=\ell_d(\gamma)$ for every path. If $d$ has contractibility data $(\lambda_0,r_*)$ then $D$ has data $(C\lambda_0,r_*)$. Replacing $d$ by $D$ changes no BLD constant.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The bounds $d\le D\le Cd$ are the definition of length and quasiconvexity; the triangle inequality follows by concatenation, and the topology is unchanged. Since $D\ge d$, $\ell_D\ge\ell_d$; conversely, for $\ell_d(\gamma)<\infty$ and any partition, $\sum D(\gamma(t_{j-1}),\gamma(t_j))\le\sum\ell_d(\gamma|_{[t_{j-1},t_j]})=\ell_d(\gamma)$. Hence $\ell_D=\ell_d$, so $D$ is a length metric. For contractibility use $B_D(x,s)\subset B_d(x,s)$ and $B_d(x,\lambda_0s)\subset B_D(x,C\lambda_0s)$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem2.5}
Let $f\colon(X,D)\to(Y,d_Y)$ be an $L$-BLD map between complete locally compact length spaces. Every rectifiable path $\beta\colon[0,1]\to Y$ has a full lift $\alpha$ starting at any prescribed $x\in f^{-1}(\beta(0))$, and $\ell_D(\alpha)\le L\ell_{d_Y}(\beta)$. The map $f$ is $L$-Lipschitz.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We first recall the topological input: a branched cover between locally compact complete length spaces has local path lifts in normal neighborhoods~\cite[Proposition~2.6]{L2017}; this is the local form of the path-lifting results of~\cite{F1950,R1973}. We explain why a rectifiable path has a full lift and why no rectifiability of the lift needs to be assumed in advance.

Starting from $x$, extend a local lift as far as possible, obtaining a maximal lift $\alpha\colon[0,T)\to X$, $0<T\le1$. Such a maximal lift can be obtained by ordering extensions of the initial lift by restriction; the union of a chain is again a lift on the union of its parameter intervals. For $0\le u<v<T$, the lower BLD inequality, which applies to every continuous path, gives
\[
 \ell_D(\alpha|_{[u,v]})
 \le L\ell_{d_Y}(\beta|_{[u,v]}),\qquad
 D(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))\le L\ell_{d_Y}(\beta|_{[u,v]}).
\]
The length function of a continuous rectifiable path is continuous. Hence the right-hand side tends to zero as $u,v\uparrow T$. Completeness of $X$ supplies a limit $x_T$, and continuity of $f$ gives $f(x_T)=\beta(T)$. Defining $\alpha(T)=x_T$ extends the lift continuously. If $T<1$, a local lift of the remaining path starting at $x_T$ extends it further, contrary to maximality. Thus $T=1$ and the extension is a full lift. Applying the same BLD inequality on $[0,1]$ proves $\ell_D(\alpha)\le L\ell_{d_Y}(\beta)$. This also explains the completeness step in~\cite[Lemma~2.8]{L2017}.

Finally, for $u,v\in X$ and $\varepsilon>0$, choose a path $\gamma$ joining them with $\ell_D(\gamma)<D(u,v)+\varepsilon$. Then
\[
 d_Y(f(u),f(v))\le\ell_{d_Y}(f\circ\gamma)
 \le L\ell_D(\gamma)<L(D(u,v)+\varepsilon).
\]
Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves that $f$ is $L$-Lipschitz.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem3.1}
Let $f\colon X\to(Y,\rho)$ be a fixed continuous map. Suppose that there are continuous loops $\gamma_j\colon\mathbb S^1\to X$, points $x_j$ on these loops, open sets $U_j\subset Y$, and numbers $b_j>0$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B_\rho(f(x_j),b_j)\subset U_j$ and $\gamma_j(\mathbb S^1)\subset f^{-1}(U_j)$;
\item $[\gamma_j]\ne0$ in $H_1(f^{-1}(U_j);\mathbb Z)$;
\item $0<\ell_j:=\ell_\rho(f\circ\gamma_j)<\infty$, $\ell_j\to0$, and $\ell_j/b_j\to0$.
\end{enumerate}
Then no compatible metric $d$ on $X$ is simultaneously quasiconvex, satisfies one-dimensional homological linear local contractibility, and makes $f$ BLD. In particular, there is no compatible quasiconvex, linearly locally contractible BLD source metric.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Suppose that such a metric exists, and let $C,L,\lambda,r_*$ be its quasiconvexity, BLD, and homological contractibility constants. First, quasiconvexity and the upper BLD inequality imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq3.1}
 \rho(f(u),f(v))\leq LC\,d(u,v)\qquad(u,v\in X).
\end{equation}
Indeed, apply the upper length inequality to a quasiconvex path joining $u$ to $v$.

The lower BLD inequality, which applies to all continuous paths, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq3.2}
 \ell_d(\gamma_j)\leq L\ell_j.
\end{equation}
Thus the rectifiability of the loops in the unknown source metric is a consequence, not an extra assumption. Set $r_j=2L\ell_j$. Parameterizing $\gamma_j$ from $x_j$, every point of the loop has distance at most $L\ell_j<r_j$ from $x_j$. For all sufficiently large $j$, we have
\[
 r_j<r_*\quad\hbox{and}\quad LC\lambda r_j=2L^2C\lambda\ell_j<b_j.
\]
Homological linear local contractibility makes $[\gamma_j]=0$ in $H_1(B_d(x_j,\lambda r_j);\mathbb Z)$. By~\eqref{eq3.1},
\[
 B_d(x_j,\lambda r_j)\subset
 f^{-1}\bigl(B_\rho(f(x_j),b_j)\bigr)\subset f^{-1}(U_j).
\]
The inclusion-induced map therefore sends $[\gamma_j]$ to zero in $H_1(f^{-1}(U_j);\mathbb Z)$, contrary to (ii).
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem3.2}
Let $I$ be an open interval, let $0<a<R$, and let
\[
 p\colon W\longrightarrow I\times D_R
\]
be a proper branched double cover whose branch-value set is $I\times\{-a,a\}$, with simple branching along both components. Assume that each branch value has its unique ramification preimage. Then, as a branched cover, $p$ is equivalent to
\[
 \operatorname{id}_I\times\pi\colon I\times\Sigma\longrightarrow I\times D_R,
\]
where $\Sigma$ is an open annulus and $\pi$ is the double cover of the disk branched at $-a,a$. For every $s_0\in I$, the inverse image of
\[
 \{s_0\}\times[-a,a]
\]
is a simple closed curve representing a generator, up to sign, of $H_1(W;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
An explicit model for the disk cover is
\[
 \Sigma=\{(w,\zeta)\in D_R\times\mathbb C:\zeta^2=w^2-a^2\},
 \qquad \pi(w,\zeta)=w.
\]
It is a smooth surface: the two complex partial derivatives of $\zeta^2-w^2+a^2$ cannot both vanish on its zero set, since $a>0$. Projection is a local homeomorphism when $\zeta\ne0$, and near $\zeta=0$, $w=\pm a$, it has a simple square-map branch. The map is proper, as both $w$ and $\zeta$ are bounded over compact subsets of $D_R$. There is exactly one preimage of each of $-a,a$ and two preimages of every other point.

The complement of the two branch-value arcs in $I\times D_R$ has fundamental group freely generated by meridians about the two punctures. To determine their actions, choose a small target product neighborhood $V$ about one branch value and the normal neighborhood $U$ of its unique ramification preimage supplied by the local topological square-map model. The set $U\setminus B_p$ is connected: in these charts it is the product of a punctured disk with an interval. Consequently
\[
 p\colon U\setminus B_p\longrightarrow V\setminus p(B_p)
\]
is a connected two-sheeted covering. The branch-value arc in $V$ is tame, so $\pi_1(V\setminus p(B_p))\cong\mathbb Z$, generated by a meridian. Connectedness makes its action transitive on the two sheets; hence the meridian acts by the nontrivial transposition. The same argument applies to the other arc. Thus the global monodromy is transitive and specifies a unique connected double cover of the complement, with exactly the monodromy of $\operatorname{id}_I\times\pi$. The classification of covering spaces, for example~\cite[Theorem~1.38]{Ha2002}, gives an equivalence away from the branch values.

This equivalence extends over the branch locus. One can see this in the local square-map model: the two sheets merge into the unique point above the branch value. Equivalently, properness and the uniqueness of that preimage force every lifted sequence approaching a branch value to converge to that point. Applying the same argument to the inverse equivalence proves that the extension is a homeomorphism. Consequently it suffices to compute the disk cover.

We calculate the topology of $\Sigma$ without invoking a surface-classification theorem. The identity
\[
 (w+\zeta)(w-\zeta)=a^2
\]
shows that $u=(w+\zeta)/a$ is nonzero. It is a coordinate with inverse
\[
 w=\frac a2(u+u^{-1}),\qquad
 \zeta=\frac a2(u-u^{-1}).
\]
Writing $u=e^{s+i\theta}$, the constraint $|w|<R$ becomes
\[
 a^2(\sinh^2s+\cos^2\theta)<R^2,
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 |s|<b(\theta):=\operatorname{arsinh}
       \sqrt{(R/a)^2-\cos^2\theta}.
\]
The function $b$ is positive and continuous because $R>a$. Thus $(\theta,s)\mapsto(\theta,s/b(\theta))$ identifies $\Sigma$ with the open annulus $\mathbb S^1\times(-1,1)$. The homotopy $(\theta,s)\mapsto(\theta,(1-\tau)s)$, $0\leq\tau\leq1$, stays in $\Sigma$ and is a strong deformation retraction onto $s=0$.

This core circle is exactly
\[
 (w,\zeta)=(a\cos\theta,ia\sin\theta),\qquad
 \pi^{-1}([-a,a])=\{(a\cos\theta,ia\sin\theta):\theta\in\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z\}.
\]
It therefore generates $H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$. As a direct check, the continuous map $(w,\zeta)\mapsto(w+\zeta)/|w+\zeta|$ sends this circle to $e^{i\theta}$ with winding number one, so the circle cannot be null-homotopic. Taking the product with $I$ proves the lemma.

If $v,w$ span a simplex, the lift from $x_v$ of the minimizing geodesic from $p(v)$ to $p(w)$ ends at some $x'_w\in C\cap f^{-1}(p(w))$ (Lemma~\ref{lem4.2}) at $D$-distance at most $L\rho_N(p(v),p(w))\le2L\varepsilon_0r$ from $x_v$, so $D(x_v,x_w)\le(2L+1)\varepsilon_0r=:a$. By~\eqref{eq5.2}, $(C_n+2)a<r_*$, and Lemma~\ref{lem5.1} gives $g\colon Q_r\to M$ with $g(\sigma)\subset B_D(x_v,C_na)$ for every simplex $\sigma$ and vertex $v$ of $\sigma$. Any point within $D$-distance $C_na$ of $x_v$ is joined to $x_v$ by a path whose image has length less than $LC_n(2L+1)\varepsilon_0r<r/8$ and therefore stays in $B(y_0,\sqrt n\,r+r/8)\subset B(y_0,s)$; hence $g(Q_r)\subset C$. As in the estimate just made,
\begin{equation}\label{eq5.4}
 \rho_N\bigl(f(g(z)),p(z)\bigr)\le L\,D(g(z),x_v)+2\varepsilon_0r\le A_0\varepsilon_0r=r/8\qquad(z\in Q_r).
\end{equation}

Let $V=B(y_0,s)$ and $\overline B=\overline B(y_0,r/4)$. The minimizing geodesics from $p(z)$ to $f(g(z))$ give a homotopy from $p$ to $f\circ g$ whose points lie in $V$ and, for $z\in\partial Q_r$, at distance at least $r-r/8>r/4$ from $y_0$. Hence $p$ and $f\circ g$ are homotopic as maps of pairs $(Q_r,\partial Q_r)\to(V,V\setminus\overline B)$, and $p_*[Q_r,\partial Q_r]=o_{\overline B}$, the generator of $H_n(V,V\setminus\overline B)\cong\mathbb Z$ (Lemma~\ref{lem5.3} in $N$), because $p$ is an orientation-preserving embedding of $Q_r\supset p^{-1}(\overline B)$ and both classes localize to $o_{y_0}$. On the source side, $g(\partial Q_r)\cap K=\emptyset$ by~\eqref{eq5.4}, and $f(C\setminus K)\subset V\setminus\overline B$ by the definition of $K$, so
\[
 (Q_r,\partial Q_r)\xrightarrow{\ g\ }(C,C\setminus K)\xrightarrow{\ f\ }(V,V\setminus\overline B)
\]
are maps of pairs. Write $g_*[Q_r,\partial Q_r]=q\,o_K$. Fix $z\in B(y_0,r/4)$ and $F=C\cap f^{-1}(z)\subset K$. Naturality of the inclusions $(C,C\setminus K)\to(C,C\setminus F)$ and $(V,V\setminus\overline B)\to(V,V\setminus\{z\})$ together with Lemma~\ref{lem5.3} gives
\[
 f_*o_K=\Bigl(\sum_{x\in F}i(x,f)\Bigr)o_{\overline B}=d_C\,o_{\overline B}.
\]
Therefore $(f\circ g)_*[Q_r,\partial Q_r]=d_Cq\,o_{\overline B}=o_{\overline B}$, i.e.\ $d_Cq=1$, impossible for $d_C\ge2$.

\label{thm7.5}

\[
 u(r)=\max_{\rho(a,y)\le r}\rho(b,\Psi(y)),\qquad
 v(r)=\min_{\rho(a,y)\ge r}\rho(b,\Psi(y)).
\]
For small positive $r$, both are positive, and
\[
 B(b,v(r))\subset\Psi(B(a,r))\subset B(b,2u(r)).
\]
For $\kappa\ge1$ with $\kappa r<\pi$, quasisymmetry gives
\[
 u(r)\le\eta'(1/\kappa)v(\kappa r),\qquad
 u(r)\le\pi\eta'(r/\pi).
\]
The second estimate follows by comparing each $y\in\overline B(a,r)$ with the antipode of $a$. Choose $\kappa$ so that $5m\lambda_0\eta'(1/\kappa)\le1$. Choose $r_1>0$, depending only on the stated data, so that for $r<r_1$,
\[
 2u(r)<r_N,\qquad 5mu(r)<r_0,\qquad \kappa r<r_N.
\]
The preceding bound on $u(r)$ makes this choice uniform in $x$ and in $\Phi$ with the given quasisymmetry function.

 B(b,5m\lambda_0u(r))\subset B(b,v(\kappa r))

 \overline B_{D_1}(x,2m\kappa r)
 \subset B_{D_1}(x,3m\kappa r).
\]
We have proved contractibility data $(3m\kappa,r_1)$ for $D_1$. Theorem~\ref{thm1.4} now gives admissibility.

\label{rem7.7}
The homeomorphism $h$ of Proposition~\ref{prop7.3} carries the null set $E$ onto a set of positive volume and is therefore not quasisymmetric. Theorem~\ref{thm7.5} shows that post-composition with a quasisymmetric map preserves admissibility, while Theorem~\ref{thm1.7}(i) shows that post-composition with a homeomorphism does not; this does not assert that every non-quasisymmetric target change destroys admissibility. Also, admissibility of $\Phi\circ q_m$ for quasiconformal $\Phi$ shows that no condition of local bi-Lipschitz flatness on the branch-value set is necessary: the necessary condition of Corollary~\ref{cor6.3} (uniform porosity) allows branch-value sets of dimension greater than the topological branching dimension, as the dimension-three examples demonstrate.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop7.8}
Every orientation-preserving branched cover $f\colon\mathbb S^2\to\mathbb S^2$ admits an admissible metric, namely $d_f$.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-34: body_md -->
By Sto\"ilow's theorem~\cite{LP2020,St1928} there are a homeomorphism $\varphi\colon\mathbb S^2\to\widehat\mathbb C$ and a nonconstant rational map $R$ with $f=R\circ\varphi$. Since $\varphi$ is a homeomorphism, $(\mathbb S^2,d_f)$ is isometric to $(\widehat\mathbb C,d_R)$. Near a point $w$ with $i(w,R)=k$ there are holomorphic coordinates in which $R(\zeta)=\zeta^k$, and the round metric is bi-Lipschitz to $|d\xi|$ on a target disk. On a small disk around $w$, $d_R$ is therefore comparable to the length metric of $k|\zeta|^{k-1}|d\zeta|$, the Euclidean cone of angle $2\pi k$: paths leaving the disk have image length bounded below, and paths inside have image length comparable to their cone length. A Euclidean cone of angle at least $2\pi$ is $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$~\cite[Theorem~II.3.14]{BH1999}, so its balls are convex and contractible in themselves. Linear local contractibility is local and bi-Lipschitz invariant, so finitely many such charts and compactness give contractibility data for $d_R$. Ahlfors regularity is Theorem~\ref{thm4.5}, and $R$ is $1$-BLD from $d_R$ by Proposition~\ref{prop4.1}.
<!-- end proof-34 -->
<!-- proof-35: body_md -->
(i) was proved above, (ii) is Theorem~\ref{thm7.5} with Corollary~\ref{cor7.6}, and (iii) is Proposition~\ref{prop7.8}.
<!-- end proof-35 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex8.1}
For $w_k(z)=z^k$ on $\widehat\mathbb C$, $(\widehat\mathbb C,d_{w_k})$ is the spherical cone surface with two cone points of angle $2\pi k$. It is locally $\mathrm{CAT}(1)$ by the cone criterion. There is no nonconstant closed local geodesic of length less than $2\pi$: one avoiding the two cone points projects to a closed round geodesic; a segment between the two cone points has length at least $\pi$; and a geodesic segment from one cone point back to itself with no cone point in its interior would project to a round geodesic loop of length at least $2\pi$. The globalization criterion~\cite[Chapters~II.3--II.4]{BH1999} therefore gives $\mathrm{CAT}(1)$. Balls of radius less than $\pi/2$ are convex and contract to their centers along their unique geodesics. Thus $(\widehat\mathbb C,d_{w_k})$ has contractibility data $(1,\pi/2)$ while $i(0,w_k)=k$, so no bound $i(x,f)\le C(n,L,\lambda)$ holds.

<!-- construction-1: body_md -->
\label{con8.2}
Let $A=\{a_1,\dots,a_d\}\subset\{0<|z|<1\}$ have pairwise distinct arguments, ordered so that $\arg a_1<\dots<\arg a_d$. Take $d+1$ copies $\Sigma_1,\dots,\Sigma_{d+1}$ of $\widehat\mathbb C$; for each $k$ cut $\Sigma_k$ and $\Sigma_{k+1}$ along $\mathcal R_{a_k}$ and reglue crosswise by the identity of the arc. Let $X_A$ be the result and $f_A\colon X_A\to\widehat\mathbb C$ the projection.
<!-- end construction-1 -->
<!-- lemma-14: body_md -->
\label{lem8.3}
$X_A\cong\mathbb S^2$, and $f_A$ is an orientation-preserving branched cover of degree $d+1$ with one branch point of index $2$ over each $a_k$, one of index $d+1$ over $\infty$, and no others. The pullback metric $d_A=d_{f_A}$ is a spherical cone surface with cone angles $4\pi$ over the $a_k$ and $2\pi(d+1)$ over $\infty$, and $f_A$ is $1$-BLD from it.
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- proof-36: body_md -->
The monodromy around $a_k$ is $\sigma_k=(k\ k{+}1)$. The monodromy around $\infty$ is, up to conjugation and inversion, the product $\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_d$, and hence is a $(d+1)$-cycle. The transpositions act transitively, so $X_A$ is connected. Counting the lifted cells of a target triangulation whose vertices contain all branch values gives the Riemann--Hurwitz identity $\chi(X_A)=2(d+1)-d-d=2$. Thus the closed connected oriented surface $X_A$ is a sphere. The metric statements follow from Proposition~\ref{prop4.1} since the gluings are isometries.
<!-- end proof-36 -->
<!-- lemma-15: body_md -->
\label{lem8.4}
Let $B=B_\rho(y,r)$, $0<r<\pi/4$, with $\partial B\cap(A\cup\{\infty\})=\emptyset$. Every component of $f_A^{-1}(B)$ is an open disk, and its closure is a compact disk with no branch points on its boundary.
<!-- end lemma-15 -->
<!-- proof-37: body_md -->
First suppose $\infty\notin B$. Since $\infty\notin\partial B$, the round ball $B$ is a bounded Euclidean disk in the stereographic coordinate. By Sto\"ilow factorization and uniformization~\cite{LP2020,St1928}, there are a homeomorphism $\varphi\colon X_A\to\widehat\mathbb C$ and a rational map $P$ with $f_A=P\circ\varphi$. The unique point over $\infty$ has index $d+1$. Choosing the coordinate $\varphi$ to send that point to $\infty$, we see that $P$ has only one pole, at $\infty$, and hence is a polynomial.

Write $B=\{\zeta:|\zeta-b|<s\}$ and let $V$ be a component of $P^{-1}(B)$. It is bounded. If $\gamma\subset V$ is a Jordan curve, the maximum principle applied to $P-b$ on the bounded Jordan domain enclosed by $\gamma$ gives $|P-b|<s$ throughout that domain. The domain and $\gamma$ belong to the same component of $P^{-1}(B)$, so the domain is contained in $V$. The Jordan-curve criterion for planar domains implies that $V$ is simply connected. Moreover, the circle $\partial B$ contains no critical value. Its inverse image is therefore a finite union of smooth circles. Thus $\overline V$ is a compact surface with boundary; simple connectedness makes it a disk. Transport this conclusion by $\varphi^{-1}$.

If $\infty\in B$, then $B\cap A=\emptyset$. Indeed $\rho(\infty,a)>\pi/2$ for $|a|<1$, whereas two points of $B$ have distance less than $2r<\pi/2$. The fiber over $\infty$ is a singleton, so lifting paths to $\infty$ shows that $f_A^{-1}(B)$ is connected. Off that point it is the connected degree-$(d+1)$ cover of a punctured disk. Filling the puncture gives a disk, and the unbranched boundary again gives a compact disk closure. Notice that the argument here uses $2r<\pi/2$, not merely $r<\pi/2$.
<!-- end proof-37 -->
<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop8.5}
For every $A$ as in Construction~\ref{con8.2}, $(X_A,d_A)$ has contractibility data $(5,1/10)$.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-38: body_md -->
Let $x\in X_A$, $0<r<1/10$, and $y=f_A(x)$. Choose $r'\in(r,21r/20)$ such that $\partial B(y,r')$ misses every branch value, including $\infty$. Let $W$ be the component of $f_A^{-1}(B(y,r'))$ containing $x$. A path from $x$ of $d_A$-length less than $r$ projects inside $B(y,r')$, so $B_{d_A}(x,r)\subset W$. By Lemma~\ref{lem8.4}, $\overline W$ is a compact disk.

On $\overline W$ pull back the Euclidean metric of the normal-coordinate disk $\exp_y^{-1}(\overline B(y,r'))$, and let $\sigma$ be its intrinsic length metric. This is a compact flat surface with cone angle $2\pi i(z,f_A)\ge2\pi$ at each branch point. The angle can be $2\pi(d+1)$ if the component contains the point over $\infty$. There are no branch points on the boundary, which is locally the convex side of a Euclidean circle. Consequently $\overline W$ is locally $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$, including at its boundary; the cone criterion and the Cartan--Hadamard theorem~\cite[Chapters~II.3--II.4]{BH1999} make the complete simply connected length space $(\overline W,\sigma)$ globally $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$.

The exponential chart on this small round ball is $2$-bi-Lipschitz. For $z\in B_{d_A}(x,r)$, a $d_A$-geodesic from $x$ to $z$ stays in $W$ and has $\sigma$-length less than $2r$. Thus $B_{d_A}(x,r)\subset B_\sigma(x,2r)$. Conversely a path in $\overline W$ of $\sigma$-length less than $2r$ has $d_A$-length less than $4r$, so $B_\sigma(x,2r)\subset B_{d_A}(x,4r)$. Convexity of balls in a $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ space gives a continuous geodesic contraction to $x$ inside $B_\sigma(x,2r)$. Its restriction to $B_{d_A}(x,r)$ is the required null-homotopy in $B_{d_A}(x,5r)$. All constants are independent of $A$ and $d$.
<!-- end proof-38 -->
<!-- proof-39: body_md -->
\textup{(i)} We first work in dimension two. Fix $c$ with $|c|=1/2$. Choose $R_0>0$ so small that $B_\rho(c,3R_0)\subset\{0<|z|<1\}$ and that $R_0$ is below the threshold of Theorem~\ref{thm6.1} for the data $n=2$, $L=1$, $\lambda=5$, $r_*=1/10$ and the round target. This threshold is independent of $m$.

Fix $0<R<R_0$ and put $d=m-1$. For sufficiently large $d$, the round ball $B_\rho(c,2R)$ has an $h$-net of at most $d$ points, containing $c$, with
\[
 h=C R/\sqrt d,
\]
where $C$ is an absolute constant. To justify the cardinality, take a maximal $h$-separated set containing $c$. Disjoint balls of radius $h/2$ about its points lie in $B_\rho(c,3R)$, so the round area bounds give at most $C_1(R/h)^2$ points when $h<R$. Choose $C$ with $C_1/C^2<1/2$, and enlarge the lower threshold on $d$ if necessary. Maximality supplies the net property. Perturb the other points by less than $h/10$ so that their arguments, and the argument of $c$, are distinct. Add further points with distinct arguments, if needed, to obtain exactly $d$ points in $B_\rho(c,3R)$. The resulting set $A$ remains a $2h$-net of $B_\rho(c,2R)$ and contains $c$.

Lemma~\ref{lem8.3} and Proposition~\ref{prop8.5} give a degree-$m$ $1$-BLD map from a geodesic sphere with data $(5,1/10)$ and branch values $A\cup\{\infty\}$. Every ball of radius greater than $2h$ contained in $B_\rho(c,R)$ meets $A$, since its center lies in the region covered by the net. This gives the required bound $C_0m^{-1/2}R$, after changing the absolute constant. The finitely many smaller values of $d$ are covered by increasing $C_0$ so that the assertion about contained balls is vacuous for those values, and using any $d$ distinct points including $c$. Because $R$ was arbitrary, the obstruction applies below any positive threshold controlled only by the listed data.

For $n>2$, let $T=\mathbb R^{n-2}/\mathbb Z^{n-2}$ with its fixed flat metric, and use the $\ell^2$ product length metrics on
\[
 M_m=X_A\times T,\qquad N_n=\widehat\mathbb C\times T,
 \qquad F_m=f_A\times\operatorname{id}_T.
\]
For $r<1/10$, a product ball lies in the product of the radius-$r$ balls in its factors. Contracting both factors contracts it inside the radius-$5\sqrt2 r$ product ball. Thus the data $(5\sqrt2,1/10)$ are uniform. The map is open, discrete and orientation-preserving of degree $m$, and its branch-value set is $(A\cup\{\infty\})\times T$. For a rectifiable product path, preservation of the lengths of every subpath in the first factor preserves its metric speed almost everywhere; the product speed formula proves length preservation. If a product path is nonrectifiable, at least one coordinate path is nonrectifiable, and its image still has infinite length. Hence $F_m$ is $1$-BLD in the convention of~\eqref{eq2.3}. A product ball projects onto a round ball of the same radius, so a ball avoiding the branch-value set projects onto one avoiding $A\cup\{\infty\}$. The two-dimensional net estimate applies. Decrease $R_0$ for each $n$ to be below the threshold of Theorem~\ref{thm6.1} for these product data.

\textup{(ii)} For a degree-$m$ branched cover of $\mathbb S^2$ by $\mathbb S^2$, Sto\"ilow's theorem and the Riemann--Hurwitz formula give
\[
 \#f(B_f)\le\#B_f\le\sum_{x\in B_f}(i(x,f)-1)=2m-2.
\]
At every fixed sufficiently small round scale $R$, at least $c_1(R/\varrho)^2$ disjoint balls of radius $\varrho$ can be packed in $B(y_0,R)$. With $\varrho=cm^{-1/2}R$ and $c>0$ small enough, their number exceeds $2m-2$, so one ball contains no branch value. This proves the claimed uniform lower bound. The examples in \textup{(i)} prove its sharp order for the sphere-to-sphere class.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem8.6}
For sphere-to-sphere covers the exponential dependence in the particular constant of Theorem~\ref{thm6.1} is therefore not sharp; the optimal order in that class is $m^{-1/2}$. This does not identify the optimal constant for general closed surface sources or for $n\ge3$. Lemma~\ref{lem6.5} distinguishes a lower bound for the diameter of some fiber from a lower bound for the separation of all its points. The recursion supplies the latter indirectly by splitting inverse-image components, and can lose quantitative information at every step.

<!-- lemma-16: body_md -->
\label{lem9.2}
If $h'\colon\mathbb S^n\to\mathbb S^n$ is another orientation-preserving homeomorphism with $h'(E)=A$, there is a source homeomorphism $H$ such that $h\circ q_m=h'\circ q_m\circ H$ and
\[
 d_{h\circ q_m}(x,x')=d_{h'\circ q_m}(H(x),H(x')).
\]
Thus admissibility depends only on the embedded set $A$ and the degree $m$.
<!-- end lemma-16 -->
<!-- proof-40: body_md -->
Put $\Phi=h'^{-1}\circ h$, so $\Phi(E)=E$. The complement $\mathbb S^n\setminus E$ has fundamental group $\mathbb Z$, and its winding cover corresponds to $m\mathbb Z$. Since $\Phi_*(m\mathbb Z)=m\mathbb Z$, the covering-space lifting criterion~\cite[Proposition~1.33]{Ha2002} gives a covering isomorphism $H$ with $\Phi\circ q_m=q_m\circ H$ off $E$. Each fiber over $E$ is a singleton, so compactness extends $H$ and its inverse continuously over $E$. The resulting homeomorphism satisfies the asserted factorization, and bijecting paths by $H$ in~\eqref{eq1.1} proves the metric identity.
