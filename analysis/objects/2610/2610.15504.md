<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lm:normalizing-constants}
		If $g\in\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n}$ and $A$ is given by~\eqref{eq:admissible-family}, then

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The upper estimate follows from convexity and~\eqref{eq:admissibility}:
		\[
		A=\sum_{j\in E}\phi_j(\tau r_j) \leq\tau\sum_{j\in E}\phi_j(r_j)=\tau.
		\]
		
		For the lower estimate, first suppose that for some $j\in E$ and some $0\leq k<m$ one has $\phi_j(2^k\tau r_j)>\delta$, and take the smallest such $k$. Iterating the $\delta_2$ inequality up to this first crossing gives
		\[
		\delta<K^k\phi_j(\tau r_j)+D_kc_j \leq K^m A+D_m\sum_{q=n}^{\infty}c_q.
		\]
		
		By~\eqref{eq:choice-n0}, we get $A\geq\delta/(2K^m)\geq a$.
		
		If there is no such crossing, the $\delta_2$ inequality may be iterated $m$ times for every $j\in E$. Since $2^m\tau=1$, summing the resulting estimates and using~\eqref{eq:admissibility} gives
		\[
		1\leq K^mA+D_m\sum_{j\in E}c_j \leq K^mA+D_m\sum_{q=n}^{\infty}c_q.
		\]
		
		Thus $A\geq1/(2K^m)\geq a$ in this case as well.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lm:compactness}
		Let \(\Phi=\{\phi_n\}\) be a normalized Musielak--Orlicz function satisfying the \(\delta_2\) condition, with the parameters chosen above. Then \(\mathcal C_{\Phi,\tau}\) is a nonempty compact subset of \(C[0,1]\). Every element of \(\mathcal C_{\Phi,\tau}\) is the restriction to \([0,1]\) of a normalized Orlicz function satisfying the \(\Delta_2\) condition at zero.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let \(g\in\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n}\) be represented as in~\eqref{eq:admissible-family}, and put \(t_j=\tau r_j\) for \(j\in E\). Denote by \(\phi'_{j,+}\) the right derivative of the convex function \(\phi_j\). Since \(\phi_j(t_j)\leq A\leq\tau\leq\delta\), convexity and the \(\delta_2\) condition give \(t_j\phi'_{j,+}(t_j) \leq\phi_j(2t_j)-\phi_j(t_j) \leq(K-1)\phi_j(t_j)+c_j.\) Moreover, convexity implies
		\[
		|\phi_j(t_jx)-\phi_j(t_jy)| \leq t_j\phi'_{j,+}(t_j)|x-y|, \qquad x,y\in[0,1].
		\]
		
		Consequently,
		\[
		|g(x)-g(y)| \leq\frac{1}{A}\sum_{j\in E}t_j\phi'_{j,+}(t_j)|x-y| \leq\left(K-1+\frac{1}{a}\sum_{j=n_0}^{\infty}c_j\right)|x-y|.
		\]
		
		Also, $0\leq g(x)\leq1$ and $g(1)=1$. Thus the families $\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n}$ are uniformly bounded and equicontinuous. Their closures are compact by the Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem.
		Each \(\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n}\) is nonempty: taking \(E=\{n\}\) and \(r_n=1\) gives the admissible function \(t\mapsto\phi_n(\tau t)/\phi_n(\tau)\). Their closures form a nested sequence of nonempty compact sets, so \(\mathcal C_{\Phi,\tau}\) is nonempty and compact.
		
		Every $f\in \mathcal C_{\Phi,\tau}$ is convex, nonnegative, and satisfies $f(0)=0$ and $f(1)=1$. For $g\in\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n}$ and $0\leq x\leq1/2$, the $\delta_2$ condition gives
		\[
		g(2x)\leq Kg(x)+\frac{1}{A}\sum_{j\in E}c_j \leq Kg(x)+\frac{1}{a}\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}c_j.
		\]
		
		Fix \(n\geq n_0\). Since \(f\in\overline{\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n}}\), choose a sequence \(\{g_k^{(n)}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\) in \(\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n}\) such that \(g_k^{(n)}\to f\) uniformly on \([0,1]\) as \(k\to\infty\). Applying the preceding estimate to each \(g_k^{(n)}\) and passing to this uniform limit gives \(f(2x)\leq Kf(x)+a^{-1}\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}c_j\) for \(0\leq x\leq1/2\). This inequality holds for every \(n\geq n_0\). Since \((c_j)\in\ell^1\), letting \(n\to\infty\) now yields
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:limit-doubling}
			f(2x)\leq Kf(x),\qquad 0\leq x\leq\frac12.
		\end{equation}
		
		If $0<x\leq 1$, choose $q$ such that $2^{-q}\leq x$. From~\eqref{eq:limit-doubling} and monotonicity it follows that \(1=f(1)\leq K^qf(2^{-q})\leq K^qf(x).\) Thus \(f(x)>0\) for every \(x\in(0,1]\). Let \(L\geq1\) be a Lipschitz constant of \(f\) on \([0,1]\), and extend \(f\) to \([0,\infty)\) by setting \(f(x)=1+L(x-1)+(x-1)^2\) for \(x>1\). This extension is continuous and convex, and hence is a normalized Orlicz function. Inequality~\eqref{eq:limit-doubling} shows that it satisfies the \(\Delta_2\) condition at zero.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lm:summable-uniform-perturbations}
		Let \(\psi\) be a normalized Orlicz function, and let \(\Psi=\{\psi_n\}\) be a normalized Musielak--Orlicz function. Set \(\varepsilon_n=\|\psi_n-\psi\|_\infty\), where the uniform norm is taken over \([0,1]\), and assume that \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_n<\infty\). Then the canonical unit-vector sequences of \(\ell_\psi\) and \(\ell_\Psi\) are equivalent. Consequently, the identity on finitely supported sequences extends to an isomorphism between \(h_\psi\) and \(h_\Psi\).
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Set \(E=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_n.\) Let \(x=(x_n)\) be a finitely supported sequence. Since all the functions are normalized, both Luxemburg norms dominate the supremum norm: \(\|x\|_\infty\leq\|x\|_\psi\) and \(\|x\|_\infty\leq\|x\|_\Psi\).
		
		Let \(r>\|x\|_\psi\). Then \(\sum_n\psi(|x_n|/r)\leq1\), and \(|x_n|/r\leq1\) for every \(n\). Therefore,
		\[
		\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \psi_n\left(\frac{|x_n|}{r}\right) \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \psi\left(\frac{|x_n|}{r}\right) + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_n \leq 1+E.
		\]
		
		By convexity and the equality \(\psi_n(0)=0\), \(\psi_n\left( \frac{|x_n|}{(1+E)r} \right) \leq \frac{1}{1+E} \psi_n\left(\frac{|x_n|}{r}\right).\) Hence \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \psi_n\left( \frac{|x_n|}{(1+E)r} \right) \leq1,\) and consequently \(\|x\|_\Psi \leq (1+E)\|x\|_\psi.\)
		
		Interchanging the two modulars gives \(\|x\|_\psi \leq (1+E)\|x\|_\Psi.\) Thus, for every finitely supported scalar sequence \(x\), \(\frac{1}{1+E}\|x\|_\psi \leq \|x\|_\Psi \leq (1+E)\|x\|_\psi.\) These inequalities establish the equivalence of the canonical unit-vector sequences. Since the finitely supported sequences are dense in \(h_\psi\) and \(h_\Psi\), the identity extends to an isomorphism between these subspaces.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
\ref{th:1.1} Let \(J:X\longrightarrow\ell_\Phi\) be an isomorphic embedding, and let \((d_i)\) be a normalized symmetric basis of \(X\). Since \(\Phi\in\delta_2\), the canonical unit-vector sequence \((e_n)\) is a Schauder basis of \(\ell_\Phi\). The sequence \((Jd_i)\) is a symmetric basic sequence in \(\ell_\Phi\), equivalent to \((d_i)\).
		
		By Proposition~\ref{prop:peiratz1}, there exists a normalized block basic sequence $u_i=\sum_{j=n_i}^{n_{i+1}-1}s_je_j$, $ n_i<~n_{i+1},$ which is equivalent to \((Jd_i)\), and hence to \((d_i)\). Since \(u_i\) has finite support and \(\|u_i\|_\Phi=1\), continuity of the modular on this finite-dimensional coordinate subspace gives
		
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-normalization}
			\sum_{j=n_i}^{n_{i+1}-1}\phi_j(|s_j|)=1.
		\end{equation}
		
		Since \(n_i\to\infty\), after discarding finitely many initial blocks and relabeling the remaining sequence, we may assume that \(n_i\geq n_0\) for every \(i\). Since a symmetric basis is equivalent to each of its tails, this does not affect the equivalence class of the sequence. For every \(i\in\mathbb N\), set
		\[
		A_i=\sum_{j=n_i}^{n_{i+1}-1}\phi_j(\tau|s_j|), \qquad \psi_i(x)=\frac{1}{A_i} \sum_{j=n_i}^{n_{i+1}-1}\phi_j(\tau|s_j|x).
		\]
		
		Disjointness of the block supports gives, for every finitely supported scalar sequence \((x_i)\),
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-modular-identity}
			\rho_\Phi\left(
			\sum_i x_i\tau u_i
			\right)
			=
			\sum_i A_i\psi_i(|x_i|).
		\end{equation}
		
		For each \(i\), let \(E_i=\{j:n_i\leq j<n_{i+1},\ s_j\neq0\}\). Since \(\|u_i\|_\Phi=1\), the set \(E_i\) is nonempty. Setting \(r_j=|s_j|>0\) for \(j\in E_i\), identity~\eqref{eq:block-normalization} gives \(\sum_{j\in E_i}\phi_j(r_j)=1\). Omitting the zero terms in the definitions of \(A_i\) and \(\psi_i\), we therefore obtain \(\psi_i\in\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n_i}\).
		By the proof of Lemma~\ref{lm:compactness}, the set \(\overline{\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n_0}}\) is compact in \(C[0,1]\). Since \(n_i\geq n_0\), every \(\psi_i\) belongs to this set. Hence there is a subsequence \(\{\psi_{i_m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\) converging uniformly on \([0,1]\) to a function \(\psi\).
		
		To verify that \(\psi\in\mathcal C_{\Phi,\tau}\), fix \(n\geq n_0\). Since \(n_{i_m}\to\infty\), for all sufficiently large \(m\) we have \(n_{i_m}\geq n\), and therefore \(\psi_{i_m}\in\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n_{i_m}}\subseteq\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n}\). Passing to the uniform limit gives \(\psi\in\overline{\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n}}\). As this holds for every \(n\geq n_0\), definition~\eqref{eq:Cphi-tau} yields \(\psi\in\mathcal C_{\Phi,\tau}\).
		
		Passing to a further subsequence and relabeling, we may assume that \(\|\psi_{i_m}-\psi\|_\infty\leq2^{-m}\) for every \(m\in\mathbb N\).
		
		Let \(\Psi_0=\{\psi_i\}\), and let \(\widetilde\Psi_0=\{A_i\psi_i\}\). By Lemma~\ref{lm:normalizing-constants}, \(a\leq A_i\leq\tau\leq1.\) Consequently, for every finitely supported scalar sequence \(x\), \(\rho_{\widetilde\Psi_0}(x) \leq \rho_{\Psi_0}(x).\) Conversely, by convexity,
		\[
		\rho_{\Psi_0}(ax) = \sum_i\psi_i(a|x_i|) \leq a\sum_i\psi_i(|x_i|) \leq \sum_i A_i\psi_i(|x_i|) = \rho_{\widetilde\Psi_0}(x).
		\]
		
		These modular inequalities imply \(\|x\|_{\widetilde\Psi_0} \leq \|x\|_{\Psi_0} \leq \frac1a\|x\|_{\widetilde\Psi_0}.\)
		
		Thus the canonical unit-vector sequences of \(\ell_{\Psi_0}\) and \(\ell_{\widetilde\Psi_0}\) are equivalent. Moreover, identity~\eqref{eq:block-modular-identity}, applied to \(x/r\) for every \(r>0\), gives \(\left\|\sum_i x_i\tau u_i\right\|_\Phi =\|x\|_{\widetilde\Psi_0}\) for every finitely supported scalar sequence \(x=(x_i)\). Hence the canonical unit-vector sequence of \(\ell_{\widetilde\Psi_0}\) is isometrically equivalent to \((\tau u_i)\), which is equivalent to \((u_i)\).

The preceding norm estimates also show that \(h_{\Psi_1}\) is isomorphic to the closed linear span of \((u_{i_m})\).

		\ref{th:1.2} Necessity. Assume that \(\ell_\psi 
	\mathrel{\vtop{\offinterlineskip\ialign{##\cr

		B^{-1}\left\|\sum_i a_i e_i\right\|_\psi \leq \left\|\sum_i a_i e_i\right\|_\varphi \leq B\left\|\sum_i a_i e_i\right\|_\psi
		\]
		for every finitely supported scalar sequence \((a_i)\). By the equivalence criterion for canonical unit-vector sequences in Orlicz sequence spaces, the functions \(\psi\) and \(\varphi\) are equivalent at zero. Thus \(\varphi\sim\psi\).

	Sufficiency. Suppose that \(\psi\sim\varphi\) for some \(\varphi\in\mathcal C_{\Phi,\tau}\). We first construct an isomorphic embedding of \(\ell_\varphi\) into \(\ell_\Phi\). By Lemma~\ref{lm:compactness}, the function \(\varphi\) is normalized and satisfies the \(\Delta_2\) condition at zero; in particular, \(h_\varphi=\ell_\varphi\).

Starting with \(q_1=n_0\), construct inductively a sequence \(\{g_k\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\), choosing \(g_k\in\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,q_k}\) with \(\|g_k-\varphi\|_\infty\leq2^{-k}\). Write its admissible representation as
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_k(t)&=\frac1{A_k}\sum_{j\in E_k}\phi_j(\tau r_{k,j}t),
& A_k&=\sum_{j\in E_k}\phi_j(\tau r_{k,j}),\\
\sum_{j\in E_k}\phi_j(r_{k,j})&=1,
& r_{k,j}&>0,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(E_k\) is a nonempty finite subset of \(\{q_k,q_k+1,\ldots\}\). After choosing \(E_k\), take \(q_{k+1}=1+\max E_k\). The next approximation exists because \(\varphi\in\overline{\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,q_{k+1}}}\). Thus \(\max E_k<\min E_{k+1}\). In the remainder of this argument, each \(g_k\) is defined on all of \([0,\infty)\) by the displayed finite-sum formula, so it is a normalized Orlicz function.

Set \(u_k=\sum_{j\in E_k}r_{k,j}e_j\). The admissibility equality gives \(\rho_\Phi(u_k)=1\), and hence \(\|u_k\|_\Phi=1\). The vectors \(u_k\) form a block basic sequence with successive finite supports. Lemma~\ref{lm:normalizing-constants} gives \(a\leq A_k\leq\tau\leq1\) for every \(k\).

Let \(\Theta=\{g_k\}\) and \(\widetilde\Theta=\{A_kg_k\}\), and define \(Tx=\tau\sum_kx_ku_k\) for every finitely supported scalar sequence \(x=(x_k)\). Disjointness of the supports gives, for every \(r>0\),
\[
\rho_\Phi(Tx/r)=\sum_k A_kg_k(|x_k|/r)
=\rho_{\widetilde\Theta}(x/r).
\]
Consequently, \(\|Tx\|_\Phi=\|x\|_{\widetilde\Theta}\). Moreover, convexity and the bounds on \(A_k\) imply
\[
\rho_\Theta(ax)\leq\rho_{\widetilde\Theta}(x)
\leq\rho_\Theta(x),
\qquad
a\|x\|_\Theta\leq\|Tx\|_\Phi\leq\|x\|_\Theta.
\]

Since \(\sum_k\|g_k-\varphi\|_\infty\leq\sum_k2^{-k}=1\), the norm estimates in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lm:summable-uniform-perturbations} yield
\[
\frac12\|x\|_\varphi\leq\|x\|_\Theta\leq2\|x\|_\varphi.
\]
Combining these estimates gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:converse-block-embedding}
\frac a2\|x\|_\varphi\leq\|Tx\|_\Phi\leq2\|x\|_\varphi
\end{equation}
for every finitely supported \(x\). These sequences are dense in \(h_\varphi=\ell_\varphi\), so \(T\) extends to a bounded linear operator from \(\ell_\varphi\) into \(\ell_\Phi\). The lower estimate in \eqref{eq:converse-block-embedding} shows that the extension is injective and has closed range, equal to the closed linear span of \((u_k)\). Hence \(\ell_\varphi 
	\mathrel{\vtop{\offinterlineskip\ialign{##\cr
				$\subset$\cr
				\noalign{\kern 0.2ex}
				$\sim$\cr}}}
\ell_\Phi\).

Finally, equivalence at zero preserves the \(\Delta_2\) condition at zero. Since \(\psi\sim\varphi\), we have \(h_\psi=\ell_\psi\), and the equivalence of the canonical basic sequences gives \(\ell_\psi\cong\ell_\varphi\). Composing this isomorphism with \(T\) proves \(\ell_\psi 
	\mathrel{\vtop{\offinterlineskip\ialign{##\cr
				$\subset$\cr
				\noalign{\kern 0.2ex}
				$\sim$\cr}}}
\ell_\Phi\). Taking \(\psi(t)=t^p\), where \(1\leq p<\infty\), in the proved equivalence gives the asserted characterization of embedded \(\ell^p\)-spaces.

		g(t)=\frac1A\sum_{j\in E}\phi_j(\tau r_jt), \qquad A=\sum_{j\in E}\phi_j(\tau r_j), \qquad \sum_{j\in E}\phi_j(r_j)=1,

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{th:upper-index}
		Let \(\ell_\Phi\) be a Musielak--Orlicz sequence space generated by a normalized Musielak--Orlicz function \(\Phi=\{\phi_n\}\) satisfying the \(\delta_2\) condition, and fix \(\delta_2\)-data \(K\), \(\delta\), and \((c_n)\) as above. Assume that every \(\phi_n\) is differentiable on \((0,\gamma_\delta]\), where \(\gamma_\delta=\inf_n\phi_n^{-1}(\delta)\) and the derivative at \(\gamma_\delta\) is taken from the left. If \(\ell^p\), where \(1\leq p<\infty\), is isomorphic to a subspace of \(\ell_\Phi\), then \(p\leq b_{\Phi,(c_n),\delta}.\)
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Suppose, to the contrary, that \(p>b_{\Phi,(c_n),\delta}\). Choose \(r\) such that \(\max\{b_{\Phi,(c_n),\delta},0\}<r<p.\) By the definition of \(b_{\Phi,(c_n),\delta}\), there exists \(N\in\mathbb N\) such that \(\beta_{n,\delta}<r\) for every \(n\geq N\). Hence, for every \(n\geq N\) and every \(t\in(0,\gamma_\delta]\), \(\frac{t\phi_n'(t)-c_n}{\phi_n(t)} \leq\beta_{n,\delta}<r.\) Equivalently,
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:index-differential-inequality}
			t\phi_n'(t)-r\phi_n(t)-c_n<0.
		\end{equation}
		
		Since \(\ell^p 
	\mathrel{\vtop{\offinterlineskip\ialign{##\cr
				$\subset$\cr
				\noalign{\kern 0.2ex}
				$\sim$\cr}}}
 \ell_\Phi\), Theorem~\ref{prop:1} \ref{th:1.2} yields an Orlicz function \(\psi\in \mathcal C_{\Phi,\tau}\) such that \(\psi\sim x^p\). Set \(n_k=\max\{n_0,N\}+k\). Since \(\psi\in\overline{\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n_k}}\) for every \(k\), choose a sequence \(\{\psi_k\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\) with \(\psi_k\in\mathcal B_{\Phi,\tau,n_k}\) such that \(\|\psi_k-\psi\|_\infty\leq2^{-k}\).
		For every \(k\), write
		\[
		\psi_k(x) = \frac{1}{A_k} \sum_{j\in E_k}\phi_j(\tau r_jx), \qquad A_k = \sum_{j\in E_k}\phi_j(\tau r_j),
		\]
		where \(E_k\) is a nonempty finite subset of \(\{n_k,n_k+1,\ldots\}\), \(r_j>0\), and \(\sum_{j\in E_k}\phi_j(r_j)=1.\) In particular, \(\phi_j(r_j)\leq1=\phi_j(1)\). Since every \(\phi_j\) is increasing, it follows that \(0<r_j\leq1\). Moreover, for \(0<x\leq1\), \(0<\tau r_jx\leq\tau\leq\delta\leq\gamma_\delta.\) Therefore, inequality~\eqref{eq:index-differential-inequality} can be applied with \(t=\tau r_jx\).
		
		Set \(d_k=(rA_k)^{-1}\sum_{j\in E_k}c_j\). Summing inequality~\eqref{eq:index-differential-inequality} with \(t=\tau r_jx\) over \(j\in E_k\), and dividing by \(A_k\), gives \(x\psi_k'(x)-r\psi_k(x)-rd_k<0,  0<x\leq1.\) Consequently,
		\[
		\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{\psi_k(x)+d_k}{x^r}\right) =\frac{x\psi_k'(x)-r\psi_k(x)-rd_k}{x^{r+1}}<0.
		\]
		
		Thus \(x\mapsto(\psi_k(x)+d_k)/x^r\) is decreasing on \((0,1]\).
		
		By Lemma~\ref{lm:normalizing-constants}, \(A_k\geq a>0\). Hence \(0\leq d_k \leq\frac{1}{ra}\sum_{j=n_k}^{\infty}c_j \longrightarrow0.\) Since \(\psi_k(1)=1\), monotonicity yields \(\psi_k(x)+d_k\geq(1+d_k)x^r\) for every \(x\in(0,1]\). Passing to the limit, using \(\psi_k\to\psi\) uniformly and \(d_k\to0\), we obtain \(\psi(x)\geq x^r\).
		
		On the other hand, \(\psi\sim x^p\) implies that \(\psi(x)\leq Bx^p\) for some \(B>0\) and all sufficiently small \(x>0\). Therefore, \(1\leq Bx^{p-r}\), which is impossible as \(x\to0+\), since \(p-r>0\). This contradiction proves that \(p\leq b_{\Phi,(c_n),\delta}\).

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:cotype-upper-index}
Let \(\Phi=\{\phi_n\}\) be normalized and satisfy \(\delta_2\). Fix \(\delta_2\)-data \(K\geq2\), \(0<\delta\leq1\), and \((c_n)\in\ell^1\) with \(c_n\geq0\). Assume that every \(\phi_n\) is differentiable on \((0,\gamma_\delta]\), with the derivative at \(\gamma_\delta\) taken from the left. Put \(b=b_{\Phi,(c_n),\delta}\). Then
\[
\Phi\in\delta^r\qquad\text{for every }r>\max\{1,b\}.
\]
Consequently, \(\ell_\Phi\) has cotype \(q\) for every \(q\geq2\) such that \(q>b\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cotype-upper-index}
\operatorname{cot}(\ell_\Phi)
\leq\max\{2,b_{\Phi,(c_n),\delta}\}.
\end{equation}
In particular, if \(b_{\Phi,(c_n),\delta}<2\), then \(\ell_\Phi\) has cotype \(2\).
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Fix \(r>\max\{1,b\}\). Choose \(N\geq n_0\) such that \(\beta_{n,\delta}<r\) for every \(n\geq N\). The definition of \(\beta_{n,\delta}\) gives
\[
t\phi_n'(t)\leq r\phi_n(t)+c_n,
\qquad n\geq N,\quad 0<t\leq\gamma_\delta.
\]
For \(t>0\) and \(\lambda\geq1\) with \(\lambda t\leq\gamma_\delta\), integrating
\[
\left(\frac{\phi_n(u)}{u^r}\right)'
\leq c_nu^{-r-1}
\]
over \([t,\lambda t]\) yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:index-integrated-growth}
\phi_n(\lambda t)
\leq\lambda^r\phi_n(t)+\frac{c_n}{r}(\lambda^r-1)
\leq\lambda^r\left(\phi_n(t)+\frac{c_n}{r}\right).
\end{equation}

Let \(\tau,n_0,a\) be chosen from the fixed \(\delta_2\)-data as above. Applying Lemma~\ref{lm:normalizing-constants} to the admissible singleton \(E=\{n\}\), \(r_n=1\), gives \(\phi_n(\tau)\geq a\) for every \(n\geq n_0\). Set \(\eta=a/2\). If \(n\geq N\) and \(\phi_n(\lambda t)\leq\eta\), then \(\lambda t<\tau\leq\delta\leq\gamma_\delta\), so~\eqref{eq:index-integrated-growth} applies whenever \(t>0\).

Define the nonnegative summable sequence
\[
d_n^{(r)}=
\begin{cases}
\eta,&n<N,\\
c_n/r,&n\geq N.
\end{cases}
\]
For \(n<N\), the condition \(\phi_n(\lambda t)\leq\eta\) directly implies
\[
\phi_n(\lambda t)\leq\eta
\leq\lambda^r\bigl(\phi_n(t)+d_n^{(r)}\bigr).
\]
For \(n\geq N\), the same inequality follows from~\eqref{eq:index-integrated-growth}; the case \(t=0\) is immediate. Thus~\eqref{eq:delta-power-cotype} holds with \(H=1\), threshold \(\eta\), and errors \((d_n^{(r)})\), proving \(\Phi\in\delta^r\).

For every \(q\geq2\) with \(q>b\), we may take \(r=q\) and apply~\eqref{eq:cotype-characterization}. Taking the infimum over these \(q\) proves~\eqref{eq:cotype-upper-index}. If \(b<2\), the choice \(r=2\) gives cotype \(2\) itself.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:nonuniform-growth}
		We construct a normalized differentiable Musielak--Orlicz function \(\Phi=\{\phi_n\}\) to which Theorem~\ref{th:upper-index} applies, although the uniform upper growth assumption imposed in \cite{Peiratz92} fails.

		\[
		\phi_n(t)=
		\begin{cases}
			\displaystyle \frac{\varepsilon_n}{p_nd_n}
			\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon_n}\right)^{p_n},
			&0\leq t\leq\varepsilon_n,\\[6pt]
			\displaystyle \frac{t-a_n}{d_n},
			&t\geq\varepsilon_n.
		\end{cases}
		\]
		
		Then \(\Phi\) satisfies the \(\delta_2\) condition with \(K=2\), \(\delta=1\), and \(c_n=a_n/d_n\). For these data, \(b_{\Phi,(c_n),1}=1\), whereas \(\sup_n\sup_{0<t\leq1}\frac{t\phi_n'(t)}{\phi_n(t)}=\infty.\) Moreover, \(\ell_\Phi\cong\ell^1\), and hence, for \({1\leq s<\infty}\), \(\ell^s 
	\mathrel{\vtop{\offinterlineskip\ialign{##\cr
				$\subset$\cr
				\noalign{\kern 0.2ex}
				$\sim$\cr}}}
\ell_\Phi\Longleftrightarrow s=1.\)

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
At \(t=\varepsilon_n\), the two defining expressions have the same value \(\varepsilon_n/(p_nd_n)\) and the same derivative \(1/d_n\). The derivative is increasing on \((0,\varepsilon_n)\) and constant on \((\varepsilon_n,\infty)\). Thus each \(\phi_n\) is convex and continuously differentiable, with \(\phi_n(0)=0\), \(\phi_n(t)>0\) for \(t>0\), and \(\phi_n(1)=1\).
		
		For \(0<t\leq\varepsilon_n\), we have \(t\phi_n'(t)/\phi_n(t)=p_n\). Since \(p_n\to\infty\), the uniform upper growth assumption of \cite{Peiratz92} is not satisfied. In addition, \(\phi_n(2t)/\phi_n(t)=2^{p_n}\) whenever \(0<t\leq\varepsilon_n/2\), so no uniform doubling estimate without additive terms holds for this sequence.
		
		On the other hand, \(0<a_n<\varepsilon_n\leq1/2\), and therefore \(0<c_n=a_n/d_n\leq2\varepsilon_n\). In particular, \((c_n)\in\ell^1\). Direct calculation gives
		\[
		t\phi_n'(t)-\phi_n(t)=
		\begin{cases}
			(p_n-1)\phi_n(t),&0<t\leq\varepsilon_n,\\
			c_n,&t\geq\varepsilon_n.
		\end{cases}
		\]
		
		For \(0<t\leq\varepsilon_n\), the first expression is at most \((p_n-1)\phi_n(\varepsilon_n)=c_n\) and consequently,
		\[
		\left(\frac{\phi_n(t)+c_n}{t}\right)' =\frac{t\phi_n'(t)-\phi_n(t)-c_n}{t^2}\leq0.
		\]
		
		Comparing the values at \(t\) and \(2t\), we obtain \(\phi_n(2t)\leq2\phi_n(t)+c_n\) for every \(t>0\). Thus the \(\delta_2\) condition holds with \(K=2\), \(\delta=1\), and the specified sequence \((c_n)\).
		
		Since \(\phi_n^{-1}(1)=1\), we have \(\gamma_1=1\). Furthermore, \(\frac{t\phi_n'(t)-c_n}{\phi_n(t)}\leq1\) for every \(t>0\), with equality whenever \(t\geq\varepsilon_n\). Hence \(\beta_{n,1}=1\) for every \(n\), and \(b_{\Phi,(c_n),1}=1\). Theorem~\ref{th:upper-index} now shows that an embedding \(\ell^s 
	\mathrel{\vtop{\offinterlineskip\ialign{##\cr
				$\subset$\cr
				\noalign{\kern 0.2ex}
				$\sim$\cr}}}
\ell_\Phi\), with \(1\leq s<\infty\), requires \(s=1\).
		
		To verify that this bound is attained, we identify \(\ell_\Phi\) with \(\ell^1\) up to equivalent norms. Convexity and normalization give \(\phi_n(t)\leq t\) for \(0\leq t\leq1\). The monotonicity established above also yields \(\phi_n(t)+c_n\geq t(1+c_n)\) for \(0<t\leq1\). Therefore, \(0\leq t-\phi_n(t)\leq c_n, 0\leq t\leq1.\) Write \(C=\sum_n c_n\). If \(x\in\ell^1\setminus\{0\}\), then \(\sum_n\phi_n(|x_n|/\|x\|_1)\leq1\), so \(\|x\|_\Phi\leq\|x\|_1\). Conversely, let \(x\in\ell_\Phi\) and \(\lambda>\|x\|_\Phi\). Then \(\sum_n\phi_n(|x_n|/\lambda)\leq1\), and normalization implies \(|x_n|/\lambda\leq1\) for every \(n\). Thus
		\[
		\frac1\lambda\sum_n|x_n| \leq\sum_n\phi_n\left(\frac{|x_n|}{\lambda}\right)+\sum_n c_n \leq1+C.
		\]
		
		Letting \(\lambda\downarrow\|x\|_\Phi\), we obtain \(\|x\|_1\leq(1+C)\|x\|_\Phi\). Hence the identity map is an isomorphism between \(\ell_\Phi\) and \(\ell^1\). Moreover, the estimate \(0\leq t-\phi_n(t)\leq c_n\) for \(0\leq t\leq1\) implies \(\sup_{0\leq t\leq1}|\phi_n(t)-t|\leq c_n\). Since \(\sum_n c_n<\infty\) and \(\Phi\in\delta_2\), Lemma~\ref{lm:power-singleton-family} applies with \(p=1\) and yields \(\mathcal C_{\Phi,\tau}=\{t\mapsto t\}\).

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:nakano-cotype-exact}
Let \(2\leq q<\infty\). The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The space \(\ell^{(p_n)}\) has cotype \(q\).
\item There exist \(C\geq1\) and a nonnegative sequence \((a_n)\in\ell^1\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nakano-cotype-errors}
t^q\leq Ct^{p_n}+a_n
\qquad(0\leq t\leq1,\ n\in\mathbb N).
\end{equation}
\item There exists \(\vartheta\in(0,1)\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nakano-cotype-series}
\sum_{\{n:\,p_n>q\}}\vartheta^{1/(p_n-q)}<\infty.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
For fixed \(C\geq1\), the least possible error in~\eqref{eq:nakano-cotype-errors} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nakano-minimal-error}
d_n(q,C)=\max_{0\leq t\leq1}(t^q-Ct^{p_n})
=\begin{cases}
0,&p_n\leq q,\\[4pt]
\displaystyle
\left(1-\frac{q}{p_n}\right)
\left(\frac{q}{Cp_n}\right)^{q/(p_n-q)},&p_n>q.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus cotype \(q\) is also equivalent to \(\sum_n d_n(q,C)<\infty\) for some \(C\geq1\).
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
By~\eqref{eq:cotype-characterization}, condition~(i) is equivalent to \(\Phi\in\delta^q\). Suppose that~\eqref{eq:delta-power-cotype} holds with constants \(H\), \(\eta\), and errors \((e_n)\in\ell^1\). For \(0<s\leq1\), take \(v_n=\eta^{1/p_n}\), \(t=sv_n\), and \(\lambda=1/s\). Since \(\phi_n(\lambda t)=\eta\), we obtain
\[
\eta s^q\leq H\eta s^{p_n}+He_n.
\]
Hence~(ii) holds with \(C=H\) and \(a_n=He_n/\eta\).

Conversely, suppose~(ii) holds. For \(0<u\leq v\) with \(v^{p_n}\leq1\), apply~\eqref{eq:nakano-cotype-errors} at \(u/v\) and multiply by \(v^{p_n}\). This gives
\[
\left(\frac uv\right)^qv^{p_n}
\leq Cu^{p_n}+a_nv^{p_n}
\leq Cu^{p_n}+a_n.
\]
Taking \(u=t\) and \(v=\lambda t\) proves~\eqref{eq:delta-power-cotype} with \(H=C\), \(\eta=1\), and errors \(a_n/C\); the case \(t=0\) is immediate. Thus~(i) and~(ii) are equivalent.

For fixed \(C\), condition~(ii) holds exactly when the maxima \(d_n(q,C)\) form a summable sequence. If \(p_n\leq q\), then \(t^q\leq t^{p_n}\) on \([0,1]\), and the maximum is zero. If \(p_n>q\), differentiating \(t^q-Ct^{p_n}\) shows that its maximum occurs at
\[
t_n=\left(\frac{q}{Cp_n}\right)^{1/(p_n-q)}\in(0,1),
\]
which proves~\eqref{eq:nakano-minimal-error}.

To compare these errors with~\eqref{eq:nakano-cotype-series}, put \(a=p_n-q>0\) and \(x=q/a\). Then
\[
d_n(q,C)=\frac{1}{1+x}\left(1+\frac1x\right)^{-x}C^{-x}.
\]
The inequalities \((1+x)^{-1}\geq e^{-x}\) and \((1+1/x)^{-x}\geq e^{-1}\), together with the upper bound of one for both factors, give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nakano-error-bounds}
e^{-1}(eC)^{-q/(p_n-q)}
\leq d_n(q,C)
\leq C^{-q/(p_n-q)}
\qquad(p_n>q).
\end{equation}
If the errors are summable, the left inequality yields~(iii) with \(\vartheta=(eC)^{-q}\). Conversely, given~(iii), choose \(C=\vartheta^{-1/q}>1\); the right inequality makes the errors summable. This proves all the equivalences.

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor:nakano-optimal-cotype}
Let \(L=\limsup_{n\to\infty}p_n\), with \(L=\infty\) allowed. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nakano-optimal-cotype}
\operatorname{cot}(\ell^{(p_n)})=\max\{2,L\}.
\end{equation}
If \(L<2\), the space has cotype \(2\). If \(2\leq L<\infty\), the optimal exponent is attained if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nakano-cotype-endpoint}
\sum_{\{n:\,p_n>L\}}\vartheta^{1/(p_n-L)}<\infty
\quad\text{for some }\vartheta\in(0,1).
\end{equation}
If \(L=\infty\), the space has no finite cotype.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:weighted-cotype-defect}
Let \(2\leq q<\infty\). Then
\(\ell_M(w)\) has cotype \(q\)
if and only if there exist \(C\geq 1\) and \(\eta>0\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-cotype-test}
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}D_n(q,C,\eta)<\infty.
\end{equation}

Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-optimal-cotype}
\operatorname{cot}(\ell_M(w))
=\inf\left\{q\geq2:\exists C\geq1,\ \eta>0,
\ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}D_n(q,C,\eta)<\infty\right\}.
\end{equation}

Use the generators \(\phi_n^w(t)=w_nM(t)\) from~\eqref{eq:weighted-musielak-function}. Suppose first that \(\ell_M(w)\) has cotype \(q\). By~\eqref{eq:cotype-characterization}, \(\Phi^w\in\delta^q\). For \(0<u\leq v\) with \(w_nM(v)\leq\eta\), take \(t=u\) and \(\lambda=v/u\) to obtain

Thus \(D_n(q,H,\eta)\leq H d_n\), proving the required summability.

			0\leq t^p-\psi_n(t)
			\leq a_nt^{p-1}\leq a_n,

		By~\eqref{eq:prescribed-cotype-linear-error},

			\geq z^q(y^p-a_n)-Cz^py^p
			=\frac12y^pz^q-z^qa_n
			\geq\frac12y^pz^q-a_n.
		\]
		The positive constant \(c=\frac12y^pz^q\)
		is independent of \(n\).
		Since \(a_n\to0\), we have
		\(D_n(q,C,\eta)\geq c/2\) for all sufficiently
		large \(n\). Thus
		\(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}D_n(q,C,\eta)=\infty\).
		
		As \(C\) and \(\eta\) were arbitrary,
		Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-cotype-defect}
		excludes cotype \(q\) for every \(2\leq q<p\).
		
		Therefore
		\(\operatorname{cot}\bigl(\ell_{M_p}(w)\bigr)=p\),
		and this exponent is attained.
<!-- end proof-17 -->
<!-- corollary-5: body_md -->
\label{cor:weighted-cotype-A}
Let \(w\in\mathcal A\) and \(2\leq q<\infty\). Then \(\ell_M(w)\) has cotype \(q\) if and only if there exists \(C\geq1\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-global-cotype}
M(\lambda t)\leq C\lambda^qM(t)
\qquad(t>0,\ \lambda\geq1).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-cotype-global-index}
\operatorname{cot}(\ell_M(w))
=\max\{2,\beta_M^{\mathrm{glob}}\}.
\end{equation}
If \(\beta_M^{\mathrm{glob}}<2\), the space has cotype \(2\). If \(2\leq\beta_M^{\mathrm{glob}}<\infty\), it has cotype \(\beta_M^{\mathrm{glob}}\) exactly when~\eqref{eq:weighted-global-cotype} holds with \(q=\beta_M^{\mathrm{glob}}\).
<!-- end corollary-5 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Suppose that \(\ell_M(w)\) has cotype \(q\), and choose \(C\) and \(\eta\) as in Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-cotype-defect}. By the definition of \(\mathcal A\), there is a subsequence \((w_{n_k})\) tending to zero with \(\sum_kw_{n_k}=\infty\). Fix \(0<u\leq v\). If
\[
B=\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^qM(v)-CM(u)>0,
\]
then \(w_{n_k}M(v)\leq\eta\) for all sufficiently large \(k\), and consequently
\[
D_{n_k}(q,C,\eta)\geq B w_{n_k}.
\]
This contradicts \(\sum_nD_n(q,C,\eta)<\infty\). Hence \(B\leq0\) for every \(0<u\leq v\), which is~\eqref{eq:weighted-global-cotype} with \(t=u\) and \(\lambda=v/u\).

Conversely,~\eqref{eq:weighted-global-cotype} makes every term \(D_n(q,C,\eta)\) zero, for any \(\eta>0\). Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-cotype-defect} then gives cotype \(q\).

The set of exponents in~\eqref{eq:global-upper-index} is upward closed. If \(\beta_M^{\mathrm{glob}}<\infty\), every \(q>\beta_M^{\mathrm{glob}}\) belongs to that set, and every member is at least \(\beta_M^{\mathrm{glob}}\). Taking the infimum over \(q\geq2\) proves~\eqref{eq:weighted-cotype-global-index}, and also gives the stated endpoint conclusions. If \(\beta_M^{\mathrm{glob}}=\infty\), the equivalence just proved excludes every finite cotype, so the same formula holds.

<!-- example-6: body_md -->
\label{ex:weighted-cotype-dependence}
Fix \(p>2\), and put
\[
M_p(t)=
\begin{cases}
t^p,&0\leq t\leq1,\\
pt-(p-1),&t>1.
\end{cases}
\]
Then \(M_p\) is a continuously differentiable convex Orlicz function, and
\[
\operatorname{cot}(\ell_{M_p}(w))=
\begin{cases}
p,&w\in\mathcal A,\\
2,&\sum_nw_n<\infty.
\end{cases}
\]
Both displayed cotypes are attained. In particular, the same function \(M_p\) gives optimal cotype \(p\) for \(w_n=1/n\), and optimal cotype \(2\) for \(w_n=2^{-n}\).
<!-- end example-6 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
The values and first derivatives match at \(t=1\), and the derivative is nondecreasing. Moreover,
\[
\frac{tM_p'(t)}{M_p(t)}
=\begin{cases}
p,&0<t\leq1,\\
\displaystyle\frac{pt}{pt-(p-1)},&t>1,
\end{cases}
\]
so this quotient is at most \(p\). Integration gives \(M_p(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^pM_p(t)\) for \(\lambda\geq1\). No global upper growth exponent smaller than \(p\) is possible: for \(0<u\leq1\), a bound \(M_p(1)\leq C u^{-r}M_p(u)\) with \(r<p\) would give \(1\leq C u^{p-r}\), a contradiction as \(u\to0\). Thus \(\beta_{M_p}^{\mathrm{glob}}=p\), with the bound attained, and Corollary~\ref{cor:weighted-cotype-A} applies when \(w\in\mathcal A\).

For summable weights, observe that
\[
pt-(p-1)\leq M_p(t)\leq pt\qquad(t\geq0).
\]
Therefore, for \(0<u\leq v\),
\[
\left(\frac uv\right)^2M_p(v)-M_p(u)
\leq pu-M_p(u)\leq p-1.
\]
It follows that \(D_n(2,1,\eta)\leq(p-1)w_n\) for every \(\eta>0\). The sum is finite, so Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-cotype-defect} gives cotype \(2\), and hence optimal cotype \(2\).
