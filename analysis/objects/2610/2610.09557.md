<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ be a nonempty bounded open convex domain with smooth boundary $S=\partial\Omega$. Let $\Gamma_0\subset\overline{\Omega}$ be a properly embedded smooth arc meeting $S$ orthogonally, and let $\Gamma=\{\Gamma_t\}_{t\in[0,T_{\mathrm{max}})}$ be its maximal smooth free boundary curve shortening flow. Exactly one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $T_{\mathrm{max}}=\infty$, and $\Gamma_t$ converges smoothly to a single chord meeting $S$ orthogonally at both endpoints;
\item $T_{\mathrm{max}}<\infty$, and there is $p_0\in S$ such that
\begin{equation}                      
 \frac{\Gamma_t-p_0}{\sqrt{2(T_{\mathrm{max}}-t)}}\to\mathbb{S}^1_+
 \quad\text{smoothly as }t\uparrowT_{\mathrm{max}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{S}^1_+$ is the unit semicircle centred at the origin in $T_{p_0}\overline{\Omega}$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, in case \textup{(ii)}, the whole curve shrinks to $p_0$ and
                                  
 $\sqrt{2(T_{\mathrm{max}}-t)}\,\|\kappa(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty(\Gamma_t)}\to 1$,
               
where $\kappa(\cdot,t)$ denotes the curvature of $\Gamma_t$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
The family $\Gamma=\{\Gamma_t\}_{t\in[0,T)}$ is a \textit{free boundary curve shortening flow} in $\Omega$ if
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:definition-FBCSF}
		\begin{cases}
			\partial_t\gamma(x,t)=-\kappa(x,t)\nu(x,t),
			&x\in[0,1],\ t\in(0,T),\\
			\gamma_t(\{0,1\})\subset S,\quad \nu\perp\nu_S,
			&x\in\{0,1\},\ t\in[0,T).
		\end{cases}
	\end{equation}
	where $\kappa(x,t)$ denotes the curvature of $\Gamma_t$ at $\gamma(x,t)$ with respect to the unit normal field $\nu$, and $\nu_S$ is the outward unit normal to $S$. Notice that the evolution equation can be written as $\partial_t\gamma= \partial^2_s\gamma$, where $s$ denotes the arclength parameter.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:properness}
Let $\gamma:[0,1]\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^2$ be a family of smooth embeddings satisfying \eqref{eq:definition-FBCSF}. If $\gamma_0((0,1))\subset\Omega$, then $\gamma_t((0,1))\subset\Omega$ for every $t<T$. In particular, $\gamma_t^{-1}(S)=\{0,1\}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix $q\in S$ and set $h_q(x,t)=\langle q-\gamma(x,t),\nu_S(q)\rangle$. Since $\partial_t\gamma=\partial_s^2\gamma$, where $s$ denotes arclength, we have
\[
(\partial_t-\partial_s^2)h_q=0.
\]
By convexity of the domain $\Omega$, we have $h_q(0,t),h_q(1,t)\ge0$ for all $t$, while initial properness gives $h_q(x,0)>0$ for $x\in(0,1)$. On each compact time interval contained in $[0,T)$, the equation is uniformly parabolic, so, by the maximum principle, $h_q\ge0$ everywhere and $h_q(x,t)>0$ for $x\in(0,1)$ and $t>0$. Intersecting the supporting half-planes gives $\Gamma_t\subset\overline{\Omega}$. If $\gamma(x,t)=q\in S$ for some $x\in(0,1)$, then $h_q(x,t)=0$, a contradiction. Thus $\gamma_t((0,1))\subset\Omega$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:zero}
	Let $I$ be an open time interval.
	                       
	                                      
	   
	                                    
	                                     
	   
	                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
	   
	                               
	   
	                                                                                                                
	       
	Let $\Gamma_t^1,\Gamma_t^2\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ be smooth families of compact embedded arcs with curvature-vector normal velocity. Each endpoint either lies on $S$ for every $t\in I$, with the arc orthogonal to $S$ and its adjacent subarc in $\Omega$, or remains disjoint from the other arc for every $t\in I$.
	                                                                                                                                                          
	Suppose also that
	\[
	\partial\Gamma_t^1\cap(\Gamma_t^2)^\circ
	=\partial\Gamma_t^2\cap(\Gamma_t^1)^\circ=\emptyset,
	\quad t\in I.
	\]
	Assume that the arcs share no subarc of positive length at any time. Then the total number of intersections
	\[
	N(t)=\#(\Gamma_t^1\cap\Gamma_t^2)
	\]
	is finite and non-increasing, and decreases strictly across an interior tangency or a common endpoint. 
	
	In particular, if all intersections at $t_1,t_2\in I$, $t_1<t_2$, are transverse and interior, and $N(t_1)=N(t_2)$, then every intersection on $[t_1,t_2]$ is transverse and interior, and $N$ is constant there.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:immortal}
	If $T_{\mathrm{max}}=\infty$, then $\Gamma_t$ converges smoothly to a single orthogonal chord.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For simplicity, let $L(t)\coloneqq \mathcal{H}^1(\Gamma_t)$ for $t\in[0, T_{\mathrm{max}})$. Since length is non-increasing along the flow by \eqref{eq:derivative-length},  $L(t)$ has a limit $L_\infty\ge0$ as $t\to \infty$.
	
	We first notice that the limit length $L_\infty$ cannot be 0. Indeed, if $L_\infty$ were zero, then, for large $t$, the diameter of $\Gamma_t$ would be arbitrarily small, and $\Gamma_t$ would be forced to go extinct in finite time by comparison with a sufficiently small shrinking half-circle. Hence $L_\infty>0$.
	
	Now let $t_i\to\infty$ be arbitrary and set $\Gamma^i_\tau=\Gamma_{t_i+\tau}$. Since $L(t)\to L_\infty$, \eqref{eq:derivative-length} implies
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:immortal-translate}
		\int_{-2}^{-1}\int_{\Gamma^i_\tau}\kappa^2\,ds\,d\tau
		=L(t_i-2)-L(t_i-1)\to 0.
	\end{equation}
	By a standard real analysis argument, there exists $\tau_*\in(-2,-1)$ with $\int_{\Gamma_{t_i+\tau_*}}\kappa^2\,ds\to0$. After constant-speed reparametrisation, we have
	\[
	|\gamma_i'|=L(t_i+\tau_*),\quad
	\int_0^1|\gamma_i''|^2\,dx
	=L(t_i+\tau_*)^3
	\int_{\Gamma_{t_i+\tau_*}}\kappa^2\,ds\to 0.
	\]
	By the compact embedding $W^{2,2}(I)\Subset C^{1,\alpha}(I)$, $0<\alpha<1/2$, there is a subsequence of $(\gamma_i)_i$ converging to an affine parametrisation of a segment $\sigma$ of length $L_\infty$. Orthogonality passes to the limit. Thus $\sigma$ is a proper orthogonal chord. By parabolic regularity \cite{Lieberman},
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:immortal-subsequence}
		\Gamma_{t_i}\to\sigma\quad\text{smoothly along a subsequence}.
	\end{equation}
	
	It remains to prove uniqueness of the limiting chord. 
	                                                                                                                                                                   
	                                                                                                                                
	Let $K=\bigcap_{T>0}\overline{\{p_-(t):t\ge T\}}\subset S$ be the set of all possible limit left endpoints and notice it is nonempty, compact, and connected. The key estimate (somewhat inspired by arguments of Lauer in \cite{Lauer13}) is the following:  if $p_-(t)\in K$ for all $t\in [a,b]\subset[0, T_{\mathrm{max}})$, then
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-estimate-uniqueness}
		\int_a^b\sqrt{-L'(t)}\, dt\leq \sqrt{2} L(0)\sqrt{L(a)-L_\infty}.
	\end{equation}
	                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               
	To prove \eqref{eq:main-estimate-uniqueness}, for every $q\in K$, consider the orthogonal chord
	\[
	\sigma_q=[q,r_q],\quad
	r_q=q+L_\infty e(q),\quad e(q)=-\nu_S(q)=\nu_S(r_q).
	\]
	Whenever $p_-(t)=q\in K$, by looking at the tangent line to $S$ at $r_q$, we have by convexity 
	\[
	\langle p_+(t)-q,e(q)\rangle\le L_\infty.
	\]
	Therefore, since $|T_t(s)-T_t(0)|^2=2-2\langle T_t(s), T_t(0)\rangle$, we have
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:ODE-excess}
		L(t)-L_\infty \leq L(t)-\langle p_+(t)-q,e(q)\rangle=\frac12\int_0^{L(t)}|T_t(s)-T_t(0)|^2\,ds
		\leq -\frac{L(0)^2}{2} L'(t),
	\end{equation}
	where the last inequality follows from Cauchy--Schwarz. Integrating \eqref{eq:ODE-excess}, we obtain \eqref{eq:main-estimate-uniqueness}.
	
	We shall now show that if $K$ contains a nontrivial arc, then \eqref{eq:main-estimate-uniqueness} leads to a contradiction. Choose $q_0$ in the relative interior of $K$ and consider the orthogonal chord $\sigma_{q_0}=[q_0, r_0]$ starting at $q_0$; then fix a sufficiently small relatively open arc $J$ containing $q_0$, with $\overline J$ contained in the relative interior of $K$ and disjoint from $r_0$. By definition of $K$, there exists $a_i \to \infty$ such that $p_-(a_i)\to q_0$, and, after passing to a subsequence, $\Gamma_{a_i}\to \sigma_{q_0}$ smoothly. For all large $i$, let $b_i$ be the first time after $a_i$ such that $p_-(t)$ leaves $J$ and notice that $b_i$ is finite because $K\not\subset \overline{J}$. Then $p_-(b_i)\in \partial J$.                                                                                                                   
	
	We will show that \eqref{eq:main-estimate-uniqueness} forces the curve's squared-height integral above $\sigma_{q_0}$ to tend to zero at $b_i$, whereas exiting $J$ implies a fixed positive lower bound, providing the desired contradiction. Fix a unit normal $n_0$ to the line containing $\sigma_{q_0}$, and set
	\[
	h(\cdot)=\langle\cdot-q_0, n_0\rangle,\quad F(t)=\int_{\Gamma_t}h^2\, ds.
	\]
	Since $\Gamma_{a_i}\to \sigma_{q_0}$ smoothly, $F(a_i)\to0$. By the orthogonality condition at the endpoints and Cauchy--Schwarz, we have
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:height-evolution}
		F'(t) =-2\int_{\Gamma_t}h\kappa\langle n_0, \nu\rangle\, ds -\int_{\Gamma_t}h^2\kappa^2\, ds\leq-2\int_{\Gamma_t}h\kappa\langle n_0, \nu\rangle\, ds\leq  2\sqrt{F(t)}\sqrt{-L'(t)}.
	\end{equation}
	Therefore, $F(b_i)^{1/2}\leq F(a_i)^{1/2}+\int_{a_i}^{b_i}\sqrt{-L'(t)}\, dt\to 0$ by \eqref{eq:main-estimate-uniqueness}.
	
	On the other hand, if $\tilde\gamma_{b_i}$ is an arclength parametrisation of $\Gamma_{b_i}$ starting at $p_-(b_i)$, there is $\delta>0$ such that $|h(p_-(b_i))|\geq\delta$, $|\partial_s h|\le1$, and $L(b_i)\ge L_\infty\ge\delta$, so that $|h(\tilde\gamma_{b_i}(s))|\ge\delta-s\ge\frac\delta2$ for $0\le s\le\delta/2$. Consequently,
	\[
	F(b_i)\ge\int_0^{\delta/2}\frac{\delta^2}{4}\, ds
	=\frac{\delta^3}{8}>0,
	\]
	contradicting $F(b_i)\to0$. This provides the desired contradiction.
	
	Thus $K=\{q_0\}$ and $p_-(t)\to q_0$. Every subsequential limiting chord is then $\sigma_{q_0}$, which shows the desired uniqueness.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:tangent-flow-classification}
	Let $X_0=(p_0,T_{\mathrm{max}})\in\operatorname{spt}\Gamma$. Every tangent flow $\Gamma^\infty$ at $X_0$ has negative-time slices $q|\Sigma_\tau|$ for a fixed positive integer $q$. If $p_0\in\Omega$, then $\Sigma_\tau=\Sigma$ is a line through the origin. If $p_0\in S$, then one of the following holds:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item[\textup{(B1)}] the shrinking semicircle $\Sigma_\tau=\sqrt{-2\tau}\,\mathbb{S}^1_+$ in $H$, with $q=1$;
		\item[\textup{(B2)}] the static orthogonal half-line $\Sigma=L_+$;
		\item[\textup{(B3)}] the static boundary line $\Sigma=P$.
	\end{enumerate}
	Moreover, if $p_0\in S$, the tangent flow at $X_0$ is unique.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
As mentioned above, up to a subsequence (not relabelled), $\Gamma^i$ converge as Brakke flows to some self-shrinking free boundary Brakke flow $\Gamma^\infty$. This limit is nonzero by \cite[Theorem~6.4 and Corollary~7.3]{edelen18}.
	
	By the proof of Edelen's monotonicity formula \cite[Theorem 5.1]{edelen18}, there is a set $G\subset(-\infty,0)$ of full measure such that, for every $\tau\in G$ and $R>0$,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
		\begin{split}
			\sup_i\int_{\Gamma^i_\tau\cap B_R}(1+\kappa_i^2)\,ds<\infty,\quad \int_{\Gamma^i_\tau\cap B_R}\left|-\kappa_i\nu_i-\frac{p^\perp}{2\tau}\right|^2\,ds\to 0.
		\end{split}
	\end{equation}
	where $\kappa_i$ denotes the curvature of the smooth embedded curve $\Gamma_\tau^i$ and $\nu_i$ its unit normal. By the Sobolev embedding $W^{2,2}(I)\hookrightarrow C^{1, 1/2}(I)$, for every $\tau \in G$ and $\alpha\in(0, 1/2)$, up to a subsequence (depending on $\tau$), the maps $\gamma^i_\tau$ converge in $C^{1, \alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ to a proper and connected smooth immersion $\gamma^\tau_\infty$. Moreover, by passing to the limit in \eqref{eq:defect}, $\gamma^\tau_\infty$ is a weak solution of the shrinker equation
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:shrinker-equation}
		\kappa-\frac{p\cdot \nu}{-2\tau}=0
	\end{equation}
	and, if $p_0\in S$, then $\gamma^\tau_\infty$ satisfies the Neumann condition in \eqref{eq:definition-FBCSF} on the limit boundary $P$, so that its image $\Sigma_\tau$ meets $P$ orthogonally at any boundary points. By elliptic regularity, $\Sigma_\tau$ is a smooth immersed curve and it is locally a $C^{1, \alpha}$ limit of embedded curves, and therefore it cannot have any transverse self-intersections.
	
	By the classification in \cite{al86}, the only smooth immersed planar self-shrinkers with no transverse self-intersections are lines through the origin and the circle $\sqrt{-2\tau}\,\mathbb{S}^1$. Since every embedded free boundary solution in $H$ gives rise to a smooth embedded solution in $\mathbb{R}^2$ after reflection about $P$, we conclude the following.
	\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=20pt]
		\item \emph{The interior case.} If $p_0\notin S$,  
		\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=36pt]
			\item[\textup{(I1)}] if $\Sigma_\tau$ is compact, then $\Sigma_\tau=\sqrt{-2\tau}\,\mathbb{S}^1$;
			\item[\textup{(I2)}] if $\Sigma_\tau$ is non-compact, then $\Sigma_\tau=\Sigma$ is a line through the origin.
		\end{itemize}
		\item \emph{The boundary case.} If $p_0\in S$,
		\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=36pt]
			\item[\textup{(B1)}] if $\Sigma_\tau$ is compact, then $\Sigma_\tau=\sqrt{-2\tau}\,\mathbb{S}^1_+$;
		\end{itemize}
		and, if $\Sigma_\tau$ is non-compact, then $\Sigma_\tau=\Sigma$, where
		\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=36pt]
			\item[\textup{(B2)}] either $\Sigma=L_+$, the orthogonal half-line, 
			\item[\textup{(B3)}] or $\Sigma=P$, the boundary line.
		\end{itemize}
	\end{itemize}

	Let us now rule out \textup{(I1)}. Let $R>\sqrt{-2\tau}$ and notice that if $\Sigma_\tau=\sqrt{-2\tau}\,\mathbb{S}^1$, then local Hausdorff convergence of the spacetime supports gives $\Gamma^i_\tau\cap\partial B_R(0)=\varnothing$ for all large $i$; on the other hand, since $\Gamma^i_\tau$ is connected, this means that $\Gamma^i_\tau\subset B_R(0)$ for all large $i$, contradicting the fact that $\Gamma^i_\tau$ is an arc with endpoints on $S_i$, and $\operatorname{dist}(0,S_i)=\lambda_i\operatorname{dist}(p_0,S)\to\infty$.
	
	Next, let us rule out higher multiplicity in case \textup{(B1)}. Let $\tau \in G$ and let $\ell=\mathcal{H}^1(\Sigma_\tau)=\pi\sqrt{-2\tau}$ be the length of the limiting semicircle. Similarly to the proof of case \textup{(I1)}, for any large $i$ the whole of $\Gamma^i_\tau$ lies in a fixed ball. Therefore, as $\tau$ is a good time, up to a subsequence, the constant-speed parametrisations $\tilde \gamma_i:[0,1]\to \Gamma^i_\tau$ converge in $C^{1, \alpha}$ to an immersion $\tilde \gamma_\infty$ of constant speed $q\ell$ with image $\Sigma_\tau$. Now express the limiting map in the arclength coordinate of the semicircle, namely, take an arclength parametrisation $\alpha:[0,\ell]\to\Sigma_\tau$ and let $f\coloneqq \alpha^{-1}\circ\tilde \gamma_\infty:[0,1]\to[0, \ell]$. Notice that $|f'|=q\ell>0$, so that $f'$ has a fixed sign. Thus,
	\[
	0<q\ell=\int_0^1|f'|\,dx=|f(1)-f(0)|\le\ell,
	\]
	so that $q=1$, as required.
	
	Finally, we prove that boundary tangent flows are unique. The argument is an instance of a general principle: the set of limits of a precompact continuous connected family of rescalings is connected, so that, if it is finite, then it has to be a singleton. For $\lambda\ge\lambda_0$ with $T_{\mathrm{max}}-\lambda_0^{-2}>0$, let
	\[
	\zeta_\lambda=\mathcal{H}^1\mathbin{\vrule height 1.6ex depth 0pt width
0.13ex\vrule height 0.13ex depth 0pt width 1.3ex}\bigl[\lambda(\Gamma_{T_{\mathrm{max}}-\lambda^{-2}}-p_0)\bigr].
	\]
	This is the time-$(-1)$ measure of the original flow $\Gamma$, parabolically rescaled about $X_0$ by the factor $\lambda$. By Edelen's local mass bound \cite[Lemma~6.1]{edelen18}, every set $\{\zeta_\lambda:\lambda\geq a\}$, for $a\ge\lambda_0$, has compact closure in the local weak topology of Radon measures, and \cite[Theorem~4.14 and Proposition~6.2]{edelen18} identify $ \mathcal{K}=\bigcap_{a\ge\lambda_0}\overline{\{\zeta_\lambda:\lambda\geq a\}}$ as the set of all possible time-$(-1)$ tangent measures. Notice that by definition $\mathcal{K}$ is nonempty, compact, and connected.
	
	On the other hand, we have already shown that
	\[
	\mathcal{K}\subset \bigl\{q\mathcal{H}^1\mathbin{\vrule height 1.6ex depth 0pt width
0.13ex\vrule height 0.13ex depth 0pt width 1.3ex} P,\; q\mathcal{H}^1\mathbin{\vrule height 1.6ex depth 0pt width
0.13ex\vrule height 0.13ex depth 0pt width 1.3ex} L_+,\; \mathcal{H}^1\mathbin{\vrule height 1.6ex depth 0pt width
0.13ex\vrule height 0.13ex depth 0pt width 1.3ex}(\sqrt2\,\mathbb{S}^1_+) :q\in\mathbb{N},\ q\ge1\bigr\},
	\]
	i.e. each measure in $\mathcal{K}$ is induced by one of $P$ and $L_+$ with some positive integer multiplicity $q$, or $\sqrt2\,\mathbb{S}^1_+$ with multiplicity one. The local mass bound \cite[Lemma~6.1]{edelen18} bounds the possible multiplicities, so $\mathcal{K}$ is finite, and its connectedness implies $\mathcal{K}$ is a singleton.  Backward self-similarity determines every negative-time slice from
    the slice at $-1$, proving uniqueness for $\tau<0$. This concludes the proof.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:sheeting}
	Suppose the rescalings \eqref{eq:rescale} converge to a static tangent flow with negative-time slices $q|\Sigma|$. For every $R>0$ and $I=[a,b]\Subset(-\infty,0)$, $a<b$, and all large $i$, there are smooth ordered functions $u_i^1<\cdots<u_i^q$ on $(\Sigma\cap B_{2R}(0))\times I$ such that
	\[
	\Gamma^i_\tau\cap B_R(0)
	=B_R(0)\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^q\operatorname{graph}_i u_i^j(\cdot,\tau)
	\qquad(\tau\in I),
	\]
	and $u_i^j\to0$ in $C^\infty((\Sigma\cap\overline{B}_R(0))\times I)$, including $\partial\Sigma$ when present.
	
	If $\Sigma$ is a line, $B_R(0)$ contains no endpoints; if $\Sigma=P$, moreover, $u_i^j>0$. If $\Sigma=L_+$, each graph starts at a distinct endpoint of $\Gamma^i_\tau$ and satisfies $\partial_xu_i^j(0,\tau)=0$; in particular, $q\le2$.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Fix $R>0$ and $I=[a,b]\Subset(-\infty,0)$ and choose good times $a'<a<b<b'<0$. Let $I'=[a',b']$. Notice that, by local Hausdorff convergence of the spacetime supports \cite[Theorem~4.14 and Corollary~7.3]{edelen18}, for every compact $K\subset\mathbb{R}^2$, we have
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:static-support-control}
		\sup_{\tau\in I'}\sup_{p\in\Gamma^i_\tau\cap K}
		\operatorname{dist}(p,\Sigma)\rightarrow0.
	\end{equation}
		
\textit{Step 1: Sheeting at the good times $a', b'$}.
Let $n$ be a unit normal to the line/half-line $\Sigma$ and let $\tau$ be either one of $a',b'$. Notice that, since $\tau$ is a good time, by $C^{1, \alpha}$-convergence (cf.~ Remark \ref{rmk:good-times}), for every compact $K\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ we have
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:good-time-uniform-tilt}
		\sup_{p\in\Gamma^i_\tau\cap K}|\langle T_i(p),n\rangle|\to 0,
	\end{equation}
where $T_i$ is the unit tangent to $\Gamma^i_\tau$. Let $\rho, h>0$ be fixed.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
	\item \textit{The interior case.} Use Euclidean coordinates $(x,y)$ with $\Sigma=\{x=0\}$ and let $Q_{h, \rho}\coloneqq (-h, h)\times(-\rho, \rho)\subset \mathbb{R}^2$. First notice that for all sufficiently large $i$ there are no endpoints in $Q_{h, \rho}$. Indeed, as $p_0\in\Omega$ and the endpoints of $\Gamma^i_\tau$ lie in the boundary $S_i$, for all sufficiently large $i$, they lie outside $Q_{h, \rho}$. By \eqref{eq:static-support-control}, the sides $x=\pm h$ avoid the flow for all large $i$. 
	By \eqref{eq:good-time-uniform-tilt}, on each of the $N_i$ connected components of $\Gamma^i_\tau\cap Q_{h, \rho}$, $|\partial_s y|\geq 1/2$ and therefore the derivative has a fixed sign for all large $i$. Hence, every connected component of $\Gamma^i_\tau\cap Q_{h, \rho}$ joins the two sides $y=\pm \rho$ and is a graph. The components are disjoint since $\Gamma^i_\tau$ is an embedded curve.
	\item \textit{$\Sigma = P$.} The argument is nearly identical to the one in the previous case. The only difference is in how we establish that there are no endpoints of $\Gamma^i_\tau$ in $Q_{h, \rho} = (-h, h)\times(-\rho, \rho)$. In the case $\Sigma=P$, this follows from \eqref{eq:good-time-uniform-tilt}: indeed, if $\Gamma^i_\tau$ had an endpoint in $Q_{h, \rho}$, the orthogonality condition in \eqref{eq:definition-FBCSF} would contradict \eqref{eq:good-time-uniform-tilt}.
	\item \textit{$\Sigma=L_+$.} In $\Phi_i([0,\rho)\times(-h,h))$, support convergence and $\Phi_i\to\mathrm{Id}$ show that the faces $y=\pm h$ avoid the flow for all large $i$. By the same argument as above, the inward distance coordinate satisfies $|\partial_s x|\ge1/2$ for all large $i$. Thus each of the $N_i$ connected components of $\Gamma^i_\tau \cap \Phi_i([0,\rho)\times(-h,h))$ joins $x=0$ to $x=\rho$: the positive lower bound on $|\partial_s x|$ excludes two ends on the same face and properness prevents the curve from touching $x=0$ outside of an endpoint. Thus the components are graphs over $\{0\leq x<\rho\}$.
\end{itemize}
In all cases, $C^0$-convergence of the functions $u_i^1<\dots<u_i^{N_i}$ representing the graphical sheets to $0$ follows from \eqref{eq:static-support-control}; then \eqref{eq:good-time-uniform-tilt} implies $C^1$-convergence. In the case $\Sigma=L_+$, the orthogonality condition at the endpoints implies the homogeneous Neumann condition $\partial_xu_i^j(0,\tau)=0$ for all $j$.
Finally, each graph contributes a uniform positive amount of mass on a smaller fixed rectangle, so the local mass bound bounds $N_i$. Since each graph converges in $C^1$ to the same segment, varifold convergence to $q|\Sigma|$ then forces $N_i=q$ for all sufficiently large $i$. In the case $\Sigma=L_+$, in particular $q\leq 2$, because the graphs start at distinct endpoints and each $\Gamma^i_\tau$ has exactly two endpoints.
	
	\vspace{1ex}
	
	\emph{Step 2: Extending sheeting to the closed time interval $[a', b']$.} As in 	\emph{Step 1}, we shall divide the argument into three cases.
	\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
		\item \textit{The interior case.} The argument for the interior case follows \cite[Theorem~7.2 and Lemma~7.4]{White_lectures}. We record it here for completeness.
		
		As in \emph{Step 1}, use Euclidean coordinates $(x,y)$ with $\Sigma=\{x=0\}$, and fix the rectangles
		\[
		Q=(-1,1)\times(-2R,2R),\quad Q'=(-2,2)\times(-2R-1,2R+1).
		\] 
		Again, as in \emph{Step 1}, for all sufficiently large $i$ (depending on $R$ only) $Q'$ cannot contain any endpoints of $\Gamma^i_\tau$. Let us now apply Lemma \ref{lem:zero} to line segments transverse to $\Sigma$. At times $a',b'$, \emph{Step 1} gives exactly $q$ Euclidean graphs in $Q'$ with small $C^1$-norm. Thus, for each $c\in (-2R-1, 2R+1)$, the segment $\sigma_c=[-2,2]\times\{c\}$ has exactly $q$ transverse intersections with both $\Gamma^i_{a'}$ and $\Gamma^i_{b'}$, and its endpoints avoid the flow for all times $\tau\in I'$ by \eqref{eq:static-support-control}. Hence, by Lemma \ref{lem:zero}, for all $c\in (-2R-1, 2R+1)$
		\[
		\tau \mapsto  \#(\Gamma^i_{\tau}\cap \sigma_c) \  \text{is constant on } I'=[a', b'],
		\]
		and  $\sigma_c$ has exactly $q$ transverse intersections with $\Gamma^i_{\tau}$ throughout $I'$. Ordering these intersections gives $q$ smooth graphs $u_i^1(\cdot, \tau)<\dots<u_i^{q}(\cdot, \tau)$ in $Q'$ throughout $I'$.
		
		Their slopes also tend uniformly to zero in $Q$. To see this, fix $\epsilon\in(0,\pi/2)$ and choose $h>0$ so small that any line through a point of $Q\cap\{|x|\le h\}$ and making an angle of at least $\epsilon$ with $\Sigma$ meets both lines $x=\pm h$ inside $Q'$.
		For large $i$, by \eqref{eq:static-support-control}, $\Gamma^i_\tau\cap Q'\subset\{|x|<h/2\}$ for every $\tau\in I'$. Moreover, by \emph{Step~1}, at both good times the graph slopes have absolute value less than $\tan\epsilon$.
		Suppose that at some intermediate time a tangent line at a point of $\Gamma^i_\tau\cap Q$ makes an angle of at least $\epsilon$ with $\Sigma$. Consider the fixed segment of this line between $x=-h$ and $x=h$. At each good time, it meets every sheet exactly once and transversely. Thus its total intersection number is $q$ at both $a'$ and $b'$. Since $Q'$ contains no curve endpoints, Lemma~\ref{lem:zero} excludes any tangential intersection, giving the required contradiction. Thus the tangent lines in $Q$ converge uniformly to the direction of $\Sigma$ throughout $I'$.
		
		\item \textit{$\Sigma =P$.} Again, use Euclidean coordinates $(x,y)$ with $\Sigma=\{x=0\}$, and fix the rectangles
		\[
		Q=(-1,1)\times(-2R,2R),\quad Q'=(-2,2)\times(-2R-1,2R+1).
		\] 
		We shall prove that for all sufficiently large $i$ the larger rectangle $Q'$ does not contain any endpoints of $\Gamma^i_\tau$ for any $\tau \in I'$. Then the interior argument of the previous paragraph applies verbatim.
		
		In order to rule out $\Gamma^i_\tau$ having any endpoints in $Q'$, we again use Lemma \ref{lem:zero} with segments normal to $S_i$ foliating a small collar neighbourhood of $S_i$ around the origin. Parametrise $S_i$ by $\beta_i$ as in \eqref{eq:fermi-chart} and for $|c|\le2R+2$ consider the segments $\ell_{i,c}=\{\beta_i(c)-s\nu_{S_i}(\beta_i(c)):0\le s\le1\}$.
		As $S_i\to P$ smoothly, for large $i$, they lie in a uniform collar neighbourhood of $S_i$ and meet $S_i$ only at their endpoints at $s=0$. By \eqref{eq:static-support-control}, their endpoints at $s=1$ remain disjoint from the flow. At both good times,
		\begin{equation}\label{eq:preservation-intersection}
			\#(\Gamma^i_{a'}\cap\ell_{i,c})
			=\#(\Gamma^i_{b'}\cap\ell_{i,c})=q,
		\end{equation}
		with all intersections transverse and interior, since the graphs and $S_i$ are $C^1$-close to $P$.
	
		If, for some $\tau$ in $(a', b')$, the curve $\Gamma^i_\tau$ had an endpoint at $\beta_i(c)$, then $\Gamma^i_\tau$ would be tangent to $\ell_{i,c}$ at $\beta_i(c)$ and Lemma \ref{lem:zero} would force a strict decrease in the total intersection number between $a'$ and $b'$, contradicting \eqref{eq:preservation-intersection}.

        Since $P=\{x=0\}$ is a supporting line of the convex domain $\Omega_i$, with $\Omega_i\subset\{x>0\}$, properness and the absence of endpoints in $Q'$ imply that every sheet lies strictly in $\{x>0\}$; hence $u_i^j>0$ throughout $I'$.
		
		\item \textit{$\Sigma = L_+$.} Finally, in the half-line case, we first apply the interior argument away from $S_i$ and then use the maximum principle to propagate graphicality all the way to the endpoints.
		
		Consider the fixed collar $\Phi_i([0,2R]\times(-1,1))$. Applying the interior argument in a neighbourhood of the outer side $\Phi_i(\{2R\}\times(-1,1))$ gives exactly $q$ transverse crossings, with tangent lines tending uniformly to the horizontal. At $a'$, \emph{Step~1} identifies the curve in the collar as $q$ subarcs, each joining an endpoint to the outer side. Since the crossings remain transverse, their order along the curve cannot change; this preserves the decomposition throughout $I'$.
		
		Orient each subarc from its boundary endpoint towards the outer side, and set $f^i=\langle T_i,e_x\rangle$, where $e_x=(1,0)$. At the intersection with the outer side, by the above, $f^i\to 1$. The same holds at the boundary endpoint, because of orthogonality. Together with \eqref{eq:good-time-uniform-tilt} at $a'$, these estimates give constants $c_i>0$, with $c_i\to1$, such that $f^i\ge c_i$ initially and at both ends throughout $I'$. Writing $s$ for arclength and $\eta$ for the tangential parametrisation speed, we have
		\[
		f^i_\tau=f^i_{ss}+\eta f^i_s+\kappa_i^2f^i.
		\]
		Positive constants are subsolutions, so the maximum principle gives $f^i\ge c_i$ along every subarc throughout $I'$. Since the gradient of the inward distance coordinate converges uniformly to $e_x$ in the collar, that coordinate is strictly increasing along each subarc for all large $i$.
		
		Thus the subarcs are the graphs $\operatorname{graph}_i u_i^j$ of smooth ordered functions $u_i^1<\cdots<u_i^q$ on $[0,2R)\times I'$. Their heights tend uniformly to zero by \eqref{eq:static-support-control}, and $\partial_xu_i^j=(\partial_s y)/(\partial_s x)\to0$ since $f^i\geq c_i\to 1$ and $\Phi_i$ converges smoothly to the identity. At the endpoints, orthogonality gives $\partial_xu_i^j(0,\tau)=0$.
	\end{itemize}

	
	\emph{Smooth convergence.}
	Finally, we upgrade $C^1$ convergence to smooth convergence by standard parabolic regularity. If $\Sigma$ is a line, then the functions $u_i^j$ solve the equation $u_\tau=u_{yy}/(1+u_y^2)$. \\
	If $\Sigma = L_+$, then in  Fermi coordinates \eqref{eq:fermi-chart}, we have
	$\Phi_i^*\delta=dx^2+a_i(y,x)^2dy^2$, where $a_i(y,x)\rightarrow1$ locally smoothly.
	Therefore, the functions $u_i^j$ solve a parabolic equation with principal coefficient $(1+a_i^2u_x^2)^{-1}$, and homogeneous Neumann data at $x=0$. The $C^1$ bounds  give uniform parabolicity, so interior and boundary estimates \cite{Lieberman} give smooth convergence to zero as required.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:static-exclusion}
Let $X_0=(p_0,T_{\mathrm{max}})\in\operatorname{spt}\Gamma$. If $p_0\in\Omega$, every tangent flow at $X_0$ has multiplicity one. If $p_0\in S$, a tangent flow of type \textup{(B2)} has multiplicity one, and type \textup{(B3)} does not occur.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Suppose that a tangent flow at $X_0$ is an interior line of multiplicity $q\ge2$, the line $P$ of multiplicity $q\ge1$, or the half-line $L_+$ of multiplicity $q\ge2$. At a boundary point the tangent flow is unique by Proposition~\ref{prop:tangent-flow-classification}. In the interior, all tangent flows have the same Gaussian density by \cite[Proposition~6.2 and Theorem~6.4]{edelen18}. Since the density of a static line is its multiplicity, every interior tangent line has multiplicity $q$, although its direction may depend on the sequence.

Put $\theta(t)=\sqrt{T_{\mathrm{max}}-t}$. Any sequence of rescalings by $\theta(t)^{-1}$ as $t\uparrowT_{\mathrm{max}}$ has a subsequence converging to a static flow with multiplicity $q$. Proposition~\ref{prop:sheeting} therefore gives the following descriptions for all sufficiently late times, and we define the separation function $d(t)$ as follows.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
	\item \textit{The interior case.} On each of the two outermost sheets in $\overline B_{\theta(t)}(p_0)$, choose the point closest to $p_0$, and let $d(t)$ be the smaller of their distances to the opposite sheet. Notice that $d(t)=o(\theta(t))$ by Proposition \ref{prop:sheeting}.
	
	\item \textit{$\Sigma=P$.} Let $d(t)$ be the distance from $0$ to the first intersection of $\Gamma_t$ with $\{(x,0):0\le x\le\theta(t)\}$. In the fixed coordinates centred at $p_0$, Proposition~\ref{prop:sheeting} gives exactly $q$ transverse intersections. By convexity, all intersections have $x>0$ (see Remark \ref{rmk:convexity}).
	
	\item \textit{$\Sigma=L_+$.} Here $q=2$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:sheeting}. Let $d(t)$ be the arclength separation of the two endpoints along the short boundary arc about $p_0$.
\end{itemize}
By sheeting and embeddedness, $d(t)$ is positive and continuous, and by Proposition \ref{prop:sheeting}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalised-separation}
	\delta(t)\coloneqq\frac{d(t)}{\theta(t)}\to 0
	\quad\text{as }t\uparrowT_{\mathrm{max}}.
\end{equation}
Choose $t_i\uparrowT_{\mathrm{max}}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-max}
	\sup_{t_i\le t<T_{\mathrm{max}}}\delta(t)
	\le(1+i^{-1})\delta(t_i),
	\qquad \lambda_i=\theta(t_i)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
After passing to a subsequence, the rescaled flows \eqref{eq:rescale} converge to $q|\Sigma|$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:sheeting} gives ordered graphs $u_i^1<\cdots<u_i^q$ on domains exhausting $I\times(-2,0)$, where $I=\mathbb{R}$ for lines and $I=[0,\infty)$ for the half-line. Also, $u_i^j\to0$ locally smoothly. Set
\[
h_i=
\begin{cases}
	u_i^1,&\Sigma=P,\\
	u_i^q-u_i^1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The graphical equation for $u_i^1$ in the first case, and the difference of the equations for the outermost sheets in the other cases, give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-collapse}
	(h_i)_\tau=A_i(h_i)_{zz}+B_i(h_i)_z+C_ih_i, 
\end{equation}
where $(A_i,B_i,C_i)\to(1,0,0)$ locally smoothly and $z=y$ in the interior and $\Sigma=P$ cases, and $z=x$ for the Fermi half-line graphs in the case $\Sigma=L_+$. The functions $h_i$ are positive (in the case $\Sigma=P$ this is a consequence of convexity as pointed out in Remark \ref{rmk:convexity}), and in the half-line case they satisfy homogeneous Neumann data at $0$.

We now relate $h_i$ to $d(t)$. Let $t=T_{\mathrm{max}}+\lambda_i^{-2}\tau$. In both boundary cases, by definition, $h_i(0,\tau)=\lambda_i d(t)$.
In the interior case, let $s_i(\tau)$ be the $y$-coordinate of the closest point to the origin realising $d(t)$. Sheeting gives $s_i\to0$ uniformly on compact time intervals. Moreover, since the graphs have slopes $o(1)$ by Proposition \ref{prop:sheeting}, projecting the segment defining $\lambda_i d(t)$ onto the fixed normal gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-gap}
 h_i(s_i(\tau),\tau)=(1+o(1))\lambda_i d(t)
 =(1+o(1))\sqrt{-\tau}\,\delta(t).
\end{equation}
uniformly on compact time intervals in $(-2,0)$.

Normalise by setting $w_i=h_i/\delta(t_i)$. Equations \eqref{eq:tail-max} and \eqref{eq:relative-gap} yield
\[
w_i(s_i(-1),-1)\to1,\qquad
w_i(s_i(\tau),\tau)\le\sqrt{-\tau}+o(1)
\quad(-1\le\tau<0).
\]
Harnack's inequality and parabolic estimates \cite{Lieberman} give locally uniform bounds for $w_i$ and its spatial derivative, including at the Neumann boundary. Therefore, up to a subsequence, the $w_i$ converge locally smoothly to a non-negative solution $w$ of $w_\tau=w_{zz}$, with homogeneous Neumann data when $I=[0,\infty)$. Since $s_i\to0$,
\[
w(0,-1)=1,\qquad w(0,\tau)\le\sqrt{-\tau}
\quad(-1\le\tau<0).
\]
This contradicts Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}.

Compactness of $\overline{\Omega}$ gives a subsequence with $p_i\to p_0$ and the point $(p_0,T_{\mathrm{max}})$ is singular. If $p_0\in\Omega$, Propositions~\ref{prop:tangent-flow-classification} and~\ref{prop:static-exclusion} imply that every tangent flow there is a multiplicity-one static line. The Gaussian density is therefore $1$, and White's local regularity theorem \cite[Theorems~3.1 and~3.5]{whi05localreg}, applied in a neighbourhood disjoint from $S$, would make the point regular. Thus $p_0\in S$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-sing} identifies the unique tangent flow as the multiplicity-one shrinking semicircle.
	
	Let $t_i\uparrowT_{\mathrm{max}}$ now be arbitrary and put $\lambda_i=(T_{\mathrm{max}}-t_i)^{-1/2}$. The rescaled flows \eqref{eq:rescale} about $(p_0,T_{\mathrm{max}})$ converge subsequentially to
	$\Sigma_\tau=\sqrt{-2\tau}\,\mathbb{S}^1_+$
	with multiplicity one. Choose a good time $\tau_0\in(-2,-1)$. As in the compact case of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:tangent-flow-classification}, local support convergence and connectedness imply that the whole curve $\Gamma^i_{\tau_0}$ lies in a fixed ball for all large $i$. Thus Remark~\ref{rmk:good-times} gives, after a further subsequence and reparametrisation, global $C^{1,\alpha}$ convergence to $\Sigma_{\tau_0}$, for $0<\alpha<1/2$.
	
	The boundaries $S_i$ converge smoothly to $P$ near the compact limiting flow on $[\tau_0,-1/2]$. Therefore, by continuous dependence and positive-time smoothing for free boundary curve shortening flow, for every $0<\epsilon<-1/2-\tau_0$, $\Gamma^i_\tau \to \Sigma_\tau$ smoothly on $[\tau_0+\epsilon,-1/2]$. 
	In particular, we obtain smooth convergence of $\Gamma^i_{-1}$ to $\Sigma_{-1}$.
	Finally, dividing by $\sqrt2$ gives the convergence asserted in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}\textup{(ii)} along this subsequence.
	
	Since the sequence $t_i\uparrowT_{\mathrm{max}}$ was arbitrary and the tangent flow is unique, the rescaled curves converge smoothly to the unit semicircle.
