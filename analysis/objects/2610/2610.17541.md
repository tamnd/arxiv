<!-- head-1: body_md -->
We write $\brc*{\cdot}_+ = \max\set{\cdot, 0}$.
For $d\ge1$, we write $\triangle_d\coloneqq\Set{p\in[0, 1]^d}{\sum_{i=1}^d p_i=1}$ for the probability simplex and $\triangle_d^{\le}\coloneqq\Set{p\in[0, 1]^d}{\sum_{i=1}^d p_i\leq 1}$ for the subdistribution simplex. Given a finite set $A$, we write $\mathcal{D}(A)$ for the set of distributions $\nu\colon A\rightarrow [0, 1]$ over $A$, that is, $\sum_{a\in A} \nu(a) = 1$.
A set $X\subseteq\mathbb{R}_+^d$ is \emph{downward closed}
if $x\in X$ and $0\le y\le x$ imply $y\in X$, where the inequality is coordinatewise.
For $X\subseteq\mathbb{R}_+^d$, its \emph{downward closure} is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{dw}(X)\coloneqq
\Set{y\in\mathbb{R}_+^d}{y\le x\text{ for some }x\in X}.
\end{equation}
For compact nonempty $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ and $w\in\mathbb{R}^d$, the \emph{support function} is $h_A\prn{w}\coloneqq\max_{x\in A}\inpr{w,x}\in\mathbb{R}$;
the \emph{gauge function} is $\gamma_A\prn{x}\coloneqq\inf\Set{\lambda\ge0}{x\in\lambda A}$.
Support functions are convex and positively homogeneous, and $h_{A+B}=h_A+h_B$ holds for the Minkowski sum $A+B$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Given an MDP $\mathcal{M}$ and a function $w\in \mathcal{D}(T)$,
 the \emph{maximum weighted reachability} $\mathrm{WRPr}^{\max}_{w}\in [0, 1]$ is defined by 
 \footnote{
   For any rational-valued $w$,
   $\mathrm{WRPr}^{\max}_{w}$ can be computed in polynomial time by solving
   a linear program~\citep{baier2008principles}.
   Moreover, this value is attained by a deterministic
   memoryless scheduler.
 }
\[
     \mathrm{WRPr}^{\max}_{w} = \sup_{\sigma\in \Theta}\sum_{t\in T} w(t)\cdot \mathrm{RPr}^{\sigma}_{t}. 
\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
An \emph{upper bound} $u$ of $\mathrm{WRPr}^{\max}_{w}$ is a scalar $u\in[0, 1]$ such that $\mathrm{WRPr}^{\max}_{w} \leq u$.
    Writing $T=\set{t_1,\dots,t_d}$, a \emph{componentwise lower bound} $l$ of $\mathrm{WRPr}^{\max}_{w}$ is a vector $l\in [0, 1]^d$ such that there exists a scheduler $\sigma\in\Theta$ satisfying $l_i\le\mathrm{RPr}^{\sigma}_{t_i}$ for $\forall i\in[d]$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{definition:verification}

Given an MDP $\mathcal{M}$ with the target states $T = \{t_1,\cdots, t_d\}$ and a safe region $S\subseteq [0, 1]^d$ (that is nonempty closed convex, and moreover, downward closed), \emph{safety verification} asks if the following set containment holds:
\[
    \big(\mathrm{RPr}^{\sigma}_{t_1},\dots, \mathrm{RPr}^{\sigma}_{t_d}\big)\in S \quad \forall \sigma\in\Theta. 
\]  
We say that $\mathcal{M}$ is \emph{safe} (w.r.t.~$S$) if the above set containment holds, and otherwise \emph{unsafe}.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
Consider the MDP in~\cref{fig:exMDP}, with state set
$Q\coloneqq\{q_{\mathtt{i}},q_{\mathrm{ok}},t_1,t_2\}$.
The transition probabilities for action $a_1$ from the initial state
$q_{\mathtt{i}}$ are given by
$P(q_{\mathtt{i}},a_1,t_1) \coloneqq 0.005$, $P(q_{\mathtt{i}},a_1,q_{\mathrm{ok}}) \coloneqq 0.99$, and $P(q_{\mathtt{i}},a_1,t_2) \coloneqq 0.005$.
The corresponding probabilities for action $a_2$ are
$0.009$, $0.99$, and $0.001$, respectively.
The target states $t_1$ and $t_2$ represent two undesirable outcomes.
Actions are selected by, e.g., user inputs.


Suppose that we execute the system twice under the same
fixed environmental choice with independent randomness.
We require the probability that both executions reach the same target state to be at most a threshold $\theta>0$,
regardless of user inputs.
This requirement is equivalent to safety verification
with the safe region
    $S\coloneqq
    \left\{
        (p_1,p_2)\in[0,1]^2
        \;\middle|\;
        p_1^2+p_2^2\leq\theta
    \right\}$.

<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:sets}
The safe region $S\subseteq\triangle_d^{\le}$ is nonempty, compact, convex, and downward closed. The outer approximation $U\subseteq\triangle_d^{\le}$ is nonempty, compact, and convex.
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:containment}
Let $B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d_+$ be nonempty, compact, convex, and downward closed in $\mathbb{R}^d_+$, and let $U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d_+$ be nonempty and compact. Then $U\subseteq B$ holds if and only if $h_U\prn{w}\le h_B\prn{w}$ holds for all $w\in\triangle_d$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The forward implication follows from the definition of the support function. For the reverse implication, let $x\in U\setminus B$ and let $q$ be its Euclidean nearest point in $B$. We have $q\le x$: lowering any coordinate of $q$ that exceeds the corresponding coordinate of $x$ preserves membership in $B$ and strictly decreases the distance. The projection inequality gives $\inpr{x-q,b-q}\le0$ for all $b\in B$. Thus $x-q$ is nonzero and nonnegative, and $w\coloneqq\prn{x-q}/\norm{x-q}_1$ satisfies $h_B\prn{w}=\inpr{w,q}<\inpr{w,x}\le h_U\prn{w}$,
where the strict inequality follows by $x\ne q$.
This is a contradiction.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:containment}
Under \cref{ass:sets}, let $E\subseteq\mathbb{R}_+^d$ be a given error unit that is nonempty, compact, convex, downward closed, and satisfies $h_E>0$ over $\triangle_d$.
For every $\epsilon\ge0$, we have $U\subseteq S+\epsilon E$ if and only if \mbox{$v_E\prn{w}\le\epsilon$} holds for all $w\in\triangle_d$. Consequently, we have
\begin{equation}
V_E\prn{U,S}=\min\Set{\epsilon\ge0}{U\subseteq S+\epsilon E}.
\label{eq:containment}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Apply \cref{lem:containment} to $B=S+\epsilon E$. By $h_B=h_S+\epsilon h_E$, the containment $U\subseteq S+\epsilon E$ is equivalent to $h_U\le h_S+\epsilon h_E$ over $\triangle_d$. Dividing by $h_E(w)$ gives $v_E\prn{w}\le\epsilon$. Minimizing over $\epsilon\ge0$ gives \eqref{eq:containment}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:infconv}
From the convex-analytic viewpoint, the excess generalizes the directed Hausdorff distance from $U$ to $S$.
To see this, we introduce a distance function
\[
  d_E(x) \coloneqq (\chi_S \,\square\, \gamma_E)(x) = \min_{q \in S}\gamma_E(x-q),
\]
which is the \emph{infimal convolution} between the indicator function of $S$, $\chi_S$, and the gauge function of $E$.
Then $S+\epsilon E=\Set{x}{d_E\prn{x}\le\epsilon}$ and $V_E\prn{U,S}=\max_{x\in U}d_E\prn{x}$ (\cref{prop:infconv} in \cref{app:proofs}).
As seen later, $d_{E_2}$ and $d_{E_\infty}$ correspond to the $L^2$ and $L^\infty$ point-to-set distances, respectively.
Hence $V_E$ is exactly the directed Hausdorff distance when $E=E_2$. 
\footnote{
  A similar notion to the direction score (called the quality indicator) is also used to measure the approximation quality of sandwich algorithms.
  The quality indicator is equivalent to the directed Hausdorff distance but only up to multiplicative factors~\citep{kamenev1994analysis,lammel2023convergence},
  which is weaker than the exact correspondence of $V_{E_p}$ and the directed Hausdorff distance.
}

This idea of the set distance by an error unit dates back to \citet{Hormander1955}.
For safety verification, we
(i) focus on downward-closed sets and
(ii) allow asymmetric error units $E$.
These points confine queries to $\triangle_d$ (\cref{lem:containment}), enabling query direction synthesis.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:l2}
Under \cref{ass:sets}, we have $V_{E_2}\prn{U,S}=\max_{x\in U}\operatorname{dist}_2\prn{x,S}$. If this value is positive, let $x_*$ be a (non-unique) maximizer of the distance and let $q_*\coloneqq\operatorname{proj}_S^2\prn{x_*}$. Then
\begin{equation}
w_*\coloneqq\frac{x_*-q_*}{\norm{x_*-q_*}_1}
\label{eq:l2-direction}
\end{equation}
belongs to $\triangle_d$ and maximizes $v_{E_2}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:linf}
Under \cref{ass:sets}, we have $V_{E_\infty}\prn{U,S}=\max_{x\in U}\operatorname{dist}_\infty\prn{x,S}$. If this value is positive, let $x_*$ be a (non-unique) distance maximizer, $\delta\coloneqq\operatorname{dist}_\infty\prn{x_*,S}$, and $q_*\coloneqq\brc*{x_* - \delta\mathbf1}_+$, where $\mathbf1$ is the all-ones vector. Then $q_*$ is an $L^\infty$-nearest point in $S$ to $x_*$. 
There exists $w_*\in\triangle_d$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
h_S\prn{w_*}=\inpr{w_*,q_*},\qquad
\inpr{w_*,x_*-q_*}=\delta,
\label{eq:linf-conditions}
\end{equation}
and every such $w_*$ maximizes $v_{E_\infty}$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:linf-smooth}
In the setting of \cref{prop:linf},
let $S=\Set{x\in\triangle_d^\le}{f(x)\le\theta}$
for a differentiable convex $f$ that is
nondecreasing in each coordinate
and a threshold $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$ with $f(0)<\theta$.
Then $\nabla f(q_*)\odot\mathbf1_I\neq0$, and
$
  w_*\coloneqq
  {\nabla f(q_*)\odot\mathbf1_I}/{\norm{\nabla f(q_*)\odot\mathbf1_I}_1}
$
satisfies the certificate~\eqref{eq:linf-conditions},
where $\odot$ denotes the coordinatewise product.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:gauge}
Under \cref{ass:sets} and \eqref{eq:positivity},
\begin{equation}
V_S\prn{U,S}=\brc*{\max_{x\in U}\gamma_S\prn{x}-1}_+
\label{eq:gauge-excess}
\end{equation}
holds.
If \eqref{eq:gauge-excess} is positive, let $x_*\in U$ maximize $\gamma_S$, $\lambda_*\coloneqq\gamma_S\prn{x_*}$, and $q_*\coloneqq x_*/\lambda_*$.
Then $q_*$ lies on the boundary of $S$, and every $w_*\in\triangle_d$ satisfying $h_S\prn{w_*}=\inpr{w_*,q_*}$ also satisfies $\inpr{w_*,x_*}=h_U\prn{w_*}$ and maximizes $v_S$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:gauge-smooth}
Let $S=\Set{x\in\triangle_d^{\le}}{f\prn{x}\le\theta}$
for a differentiable convex $f$ that is nondecreasing in each coordinate
and a threshold $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$ with $f\prn{0}<\theta$.
Then \eqref{eq:positivity} holds.
Moreover, in the setting of \cref{prop:gauge}, we have $f\prn{q_*}=\theta$, and if $\nabla f\prn{q_*}\neq0$, then
$
  w_*\coloneqq
  {\nabla f\prn{q_*}}/{\norm{\nabla f\prn{q_*}}_1}
$
belongs to $\triangle_d$ and maximizes $v_S$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The pair $\prn{x_*,w_*}$ in \cref{prop:gauge} admits a dual interpretation via the \emph{polar set} of $S$:
$$S^\circ\coloneqq\Set{y\in\mathbb{R}^d}{\inpr{y,x}\le 1\;\forall x\in S}.$$
Since $0\in S$ by downward closedness, we have $\gamma_S=h_{S^\circ}$ by the duality~\citep[Theorem~14.5]{rockafellar1970convex}, and hence $\max_{x\in U}\gamma_S\prn{x}=\max_{y\in S^\circ}h_U\prn{y}$.
In \cref{prop:gauge}, $y_*\coloneqq w_*/h_S\prn{w_*}$ belongs to $S^\circ$ and satisfies $\inpr{y_*,x_*}=h_U\prn{y_*}=h_{S^\circ}\prn{x_*}$.
That is, $x_*$ is a supporting point of $U$ in direction $y_*$, and $y_*$ is a supporting point of $S^\circ$ in direction $x_*$; the query $w_*$ is the $\ell_1$-normalization of the latter.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- example-2: body_md -->
\label{ex:boxes}
Consider $S=\Set{x\in\mathbb{R}_+^2}{x_1+x_2\le1/4}$ and
$U=\brc{0,3/4}\times\brc{0,1/4}$.
The safe region bounds the sum of the two failure probabilities by $1/4$.
The vertex $x_*=\prn{3/4,1/4}$ is farthest from $S$ in Euclidean distance,
with projection $q_*=\prn{1/4,0}$ and residual $\prn{1/2,1/4}$.
Thus the $L^2$ rule selects $w_*=\prn{2/3,1/3}$.
In the $L^\infty$ distance, $x_*$ is again a farthest point,
with $\delta=1/2$ and $q_*=\prn{x_*-\delta\mathbf1}_+$.
The direction $w_*=\prn{1,0}$ satisfies \eqref{eq:linf-conditions}.
For the gauge, $\gamma_S\prn{x}=4\prn{x_1+x_2}$ is maximized at $x_*$ with $\lambda_*=4$, and the gauge rule selects
$w_*=\prn{1/2,1/2}$ supporting $S$ at $q_*=x_*/4$.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:infconv}
Under \cref{ass:sets}, let $E\subseteq\mathbb{R}_+^d$ be an error unit that is nonempty, compact, convex, downward closed, and satisfies $h_E>0$ over $\triangle_d$. For every $x\in\mathbb{R}^d_+$ and $\epsilon\ge0$, we have $x\in S+\epsilon E$ if and only if $d_E\prn{x}\le\epsilon$, and
\begin{equation}
d_E\prn{x}=\brc*{\max_{w\in\triangle_d}\frac{\inpr{w,x}-h_S\prn{w}}{h_E\prn{w}}}_+.
\label{eq:infconv-dual}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $d_E$ is finite and continuous on $\mathbb{R}^d_+$, and $V_E\prn{U,S}=\max_{x\in U}d_E\prn{x}$.
Moreover, under $h_S>0$ on $\triangle_d$ assumed in \eqref{eq:positivity}, we have $d_S\prn{x}=\brc*{\gamma_S\prn{x}-1}_+$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^d_+$.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
To characterize the sublevel of $d_E$, we first characterize the sublevel set of $\gamma_E$.
Since $E$ is nonempty and downward closed, we have $0\in E$, so $\mu E\subseteq\lambda E$ for $0\le\mu\le\lambda$ by convexity. For $\lambda>0$, closedness of $E$ then gives $\gamma_E\prn{z}\le\lambda$ if and only if $z\in\lambda E$; for $\lambda=0$, $\gamma_E\prn{z}\le0$ if and only if $z\in\bigcap_{\lambda>0}\lambda E=\set{0}=0E$ by boundedness of $E$. Thus $\Set{z}{\gamma_E\prn{z}\le\epsilon}=\epsilon E$ for all $\epsilon\ge0$. In particular, $s\mapsto\gamma_E\prn{x-s}$ has closed sublevel sets, so it is lower semicontinuous and attains its minimum (possibly $+\infty$) on the compact set $S$, and $d_E$ is well-defined.

If $d_E\prn{x}\le\epsilon$, a minimizer $s\in S$ satisfies $x-s\in\epsilon E$, so $x\in S+\epsilon E$. Conversely, $x=s+\epsilon t$ with $s\in S$ and $t\in E$ gives $d_E\prn{x}\le\gamma_E\prn{\epsilon t}\le\epsilon$.
This proves the first claim: $x\in S+\epsilon E$ if and only if $d_E\prn{x}\le\epsilon$.
Applying \cref{lem:containment} with $U=\set{x}$ and $B=S+\epsilon E$, together with $h_B=h_S+\epsilon h_E$, shows that $x\in S+\epsilon E$ if and only if $\inpr{w,x}-h_S\prn{w}\le\epsilon h_E\prn{w}$ for all $w\in\triangle_d$, that is, $\epsilon\ge M\prn{x}\coloneqq\max_{w\in\triangle_d}\prn{\inpr{w,x}-h_S\prn{w}}/h_E\prn{w}$.
This maximum exists because the ratio is continuous in $w$ and $\triangle_d$ is compact.
Combined with the first claim, $d_E\prn{x}\le\epsilon$ if and only if $\brc*{M\prn{x}}_+\le\epsilon$ for every $\epsilon\ge0$, so $d_E\prn{x}=\brc*{M\prn{x}}_+$. This $d_E\prn{x}$ is finite.

Next, we show that $d_E$ is continuous.
The support function $h_E$ is continuous because $E$ is compact, so it attains its minimum $\mu$ on the compact set $\triangle_d$, which is positive because of the assumption $h_E>0$.
For each $w\in\triangle_d$ and $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^d_+$, H\"older's inequality gives $\abs{\inpr{w,x}-\inpr{w,y}}\le\norm{x-y}_\infty$, so the function $x\mapsto\prn{\inpr{w,x}-h_S\prn{w}}/h_E\prn{w}$ is $\prn{1/\mu}$-Lipschitz in $\norm{\cdot}_\infty$.
The pointwise maximum $M$ of these functions over $w\in\triangle_d$ is again $\prn{1/\mu}$-Lipschitz, and so is $d_E=\brc*{M}_+$.
In particular, $d_E$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}^d_+$.

Since $U$ is compact and $d_E$ is continuous, $\max_{x\in U}d_E\prn{x}$ exists, and interchanging the two maxima, together with the monotonicity of $\brc*{\cdot}_+$, gives $\max_{x\in U}d_E\prn{x}=V_E\prn{U,S}$ by the definition of the excess in~\eqref{eq:score}.

Finally, we focus on the case $E=S$.
The convexity gives $S+\epsilon S=\prn{1+\epsilon}S$, and the gauge property above, applied to $S$ with $\lambda=1+\epsilon>0$, gives $x\in\prn{1+\epsilon}S\iff\gamma_S\prn{x}\le1+\epsilon$. Hence $d_S\prn{x}\le\epsilon\iff\brc*{\gamma_S\prn{x}-1}_+\le\epsilon$ for all $\epsilon\ge0$, and the two values coincide.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:norm-enlargement}
Under \cref{ass:sets}, let $p\in\set{2,\infty}$ and $x\in\mathbb{R}^d_+$. Then there is a point $q\in S$ nearest to $x$ in $\norm{\cdot}_p$ with $q\le x$ coordinatewise, and $d_{E_p}\prn{x}=\operatorname{dist}_p\prn{x,S}$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Compactness of $S$ ensures the existence of a nearest point $s_*$.
Replacing $s_*$ by its coordinatewise minimum with $x$ preserves membership in $S$ by downward closedness, and cannot increase the distance because each coordinate
of $x-s_*$ either stays the same or becomes zero.
Since $\gamma_{E_p}\prn{z}=\norm{z}_p$ if $z\ge0$ and $\gamma_{E_p}\prn{z}=+\infty$ otherwise (which is basic), we have $d_{E_p}\prn{x}=\min\Set{\norm{x-q}_p}{q\in S,\ q\le x}$, which equals $\operatorname{dist}_p\prn{x,S}$ by the first claim.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:certificate}
Under \cref{ass:sets}, let $E\subseteq\mathbb{R}_+^d$ be an error unit that is nonempty, compact, convex, downward closed, and satisfies $h_E>0$ over $\triangle_d$. Let $x_*\in U$ maximize $d_E$ over $U$, and let $w\in\triangle_d$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
\inpr{w,x_*}-h_S\prn{w}\ge d_E\prn{x_*}\,h_E\prn{w}.
\label{eq:certificate}
\end{equation}
Then we have $h_U\prn{w}=\inpr{w,x_*}$, the inequality~\eqref{eq:certificate} holds with equality, and $w$ maximizes $v_E$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We have $h_U\prn{w}\ge\inpr{w,x_*}$ by definition of the support function.
This together with the assumption \eqref{eq:certificate} and \cref{prop:infconv} leads to
\[
v_E\prn{w}=\frac{h_U\prn{w}-h_S\prn{w}}{h_E\prn{w}}\ge\frac{\inpr{w,x_*}-h_S\prn{w}}{h_E\prn{w}}\ge d_E\prn{x_*}=V_E\prn{U,S}\ge v_E\prn{w},
\]
where the last inequality follows from the definition of the excess~\eqref{eq:score}.
Hence all inequalities are equalities, which gives $V_E(U,S) = v_E(w)$, or equivalently, $w$ maximizes $v_E$.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By \cref{prop:infconv,lem:norm-enlargement}, we have the reduction of the excess to the directed Hausdorff distance: $V_{E_2}\prn{U,S}=\max_{x\in U}d_{E_2}\prn{x}=\max_{x\in U}\operatorname{dist}_2\prn{x,S}$.

Let this value be positive and fix a maximizer $x_*$.
Since the Euclidean projection $q_*=\operatorname{proj}_S^2(x_*)$ is the unique nearest point, it coincides with the nearest point below $x_*$ given by \cref{lem:norm-enlargement}, that is, $q_*\le x_*$.
Moreover, $x_*\neq q_*$ because $\norm{x_*-q_*}_2=V_{E_2}\prn{U,S}>0$.
Thus $x_*-q_*$ is a nonzero nonnegative vector, and its $\ell_1$-normalization $w_*$ in \eqref{eq:l2-direction} belongs to $\triangle_d$. The projection inequality gives $\inpr{x_*-q_*,s-q_*}\le0$ for all $s\in S$, and dividing both sides by $\norm{x_*-q_*}_1$ gives $h_S\prn{w_*}=\inpr{w_*,q_*}$.
Since $w_*$ and $x_*-q_*$ are parallel, and we have $h_{E_2}\prn{w_*}=\norm{w_*}_2$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\inpr{w_*,x_*}-h_S\prn{w_*}=\inpr{w_*,x_*-q_*}=\norm{w_*}_2\norm{x_*-q_*}_2=h_{E_2}\prn{w_*}\,d_{E_2}\prn{x_*},
\end{equation}
so $w_*$ maximizes the direction score $v_{E_2}$ by \cref{lem:certificate}.

By \cref{prop:infconv,lem:norm-enlargement}, we have the reduction of the excess to the directed Hausdorff distance: $V_{E_\infty}\prn{U,S}=\max_{x\in U}d_{E_\infty}\prn{x}=\max_{x\in U}\operatorname{dist}_\infty\prn{x,S}$.

We check that $q_*\coloneqq\brc*{x_*-\delta\mathbf1}_+$ lies in $S$ and is a nearest point to $x_*$ in the $L^\infty$ sense.
By \cref{lem:norm-enlargement}, some nearest point $q\le x_*$ exists, and $q_*\le q$ holds coordinatewise because $q_{*,i}=\max\set{x_{*,i}-\delta,0}\le q_i$ follows from $q_i\ge0$ and $x_{*,i}-q_i\le\norm{x_*-q}_\infty=\delta$.
Downward closedness then gives $q_*\in S$. Since $x_{*,i}-q_{*,i}=\min\set{x_{*,i},\delta}\le\delta$ for every $i$, we have $\norm{x_*-q_*}_\infty\le\delta=\operatorname{dist}_\infty\prn{x_*,S}$, so $q_*$ is also a nearest point.

Next, we show the existence of a direction $w_*\in\triangle_d$ satisfying the certificate~\eqref{eq:linf-conditions}.
By the choice of $x_*$, the excess equals $\delta$; additionally, $\delta>0$ holds by assumption.
Since $h_{E_\infty}=1$ on $\triangle_d$ and $\delta>0$, \eqref{eq:infconv-dual} at $x_*$ reads $\delta=d_{E_\infty}\prn{x_*}=\max_{w\in\triangle_d}\prn{\inpr{w,x_*}-h_S\prn{w}}$, and we let $w_*\in\triangle_d$ attain this maximum.
Since $q_*\in S$, the definition of the support function and H\"older's inequality give
\begin{equation}
\delta=\inpr{w_*,x_*}-h_S\prn{w_*}
\le\inpr{w_*,x_*-q_*}
\le\norm{w_*}_1\norm{x_*-q_*}_\infty=\delta.
\label{eq:linf-upper}
\end{equation}
Both inequalities are therefore equalities, which gives the two conditions in the certificate~\eqref{eq:linf-conditions}.

Conversely, every $w_*\in\triangle_d$ satisfying \eqref{eq:linf-conditions} has $\inpr{w_*,x_*}-h_S\prn{w_*}=\inpr{w_*,x_*-q_*}=\delta=h_{E_\infty}\prn{w_*}\,d_{E_\infty}\prn{x_*}$,
where the last identity follows from $h_{E_\infty}=1$ and $\delta=d_{E_\infty}(x_*)$.
Therefore it maximizes $v_{E_\infty}$ by \cref{lem:certificate}.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:linf-saturated}
In the setting of \cref{prop:linf}, the set $I$ is nonempty and $q_{*,i}=0$ for every $i\notin I$.
Moreover, $w\in\triangle_d$ satisfies \eqref{eq:linf-conditions} if and only if
$h_S\prn{w}=\inpr{w,q_*}$ and $w_i=0$ for every $i\notin I$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
By the definition of $q_*$, we have $x_{*,i}-q_{*,i}=\min\set{x_{*,i},\delta}\le\delta$ for every $i$, with equality exactly on $I$.
Since $\norm{x_*-q_*}_\infty=\delta$, the set $I$ is nonempty.
For $i\notin I$, we have $x_{*,i}<\delta$ and hence $q_{*,i}=0$.
For $w\in\triangle_d$, we have
$\inpr{w,x_*-q_*}=\sum_iw_i\prn{x_{*,i}-q_{*,i}}\le\delta\sum_iw_i=\delta$,
with equality if and only if $w_i=0$ for every $i\notin I$.

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Based on \cref{lem:linf-saturated}, we consider the characterization of $w_*$ by the normal cone $N_S$.
By definition, $w\in N_S\prn{q_*}$ if and only if $q_*$ solves
\begin{equation}
\max_{s\in\mathbb{R}^d}\ \inpr{w,s}
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
f\prn{s}\le\theta,\quad
\inpr{\mathbf1,s}\le1,\quad
s_i\ge0\ \ \prn{i\in[d]}.
\label{eq:linf-normal-problem}
\end{equation}
All constraint functions in \eqref{eq:linf-normal-problem} are convex and differentiable, and the point $\eta\mathbf1$ satisfies all of them strictly for small $\eta>0$ by $f\prn{0}<\theta$ and continuity; that is, Slater's condition holds for \eqref{eq:linf-normal-problem}.
Hence the KKT conditions are necessary and sufficient for the optimality of $q_*$, which gives
\[
N_S\prn{q_*}=\Set{\lambda\nabla f\prn{q_*}+\nu\mathbf1-\textstyle\sum_{i\in Z}\mu_ie_i}{\lambda,\nu\ge0,\ \mu\ge0},
\]
where $e_i\in\mathbb{R}^d$ is the $i$-th standard basis vector and $Z\coloneqq\Set{i}{q_{*,i}=0}$.
By complementary slackness,
$\lambda=0$ if $f\prn{q_*}<\theta$,
and $\nu=0$ if $\inpr{\mathbf1,q_*}<1$.
Since $f$ is nondecreasing in each coordinate, $\nabla f\prn{q_*}\ge0$.

We first show that the simplex constraint is inactive at $q_*$.
By definition, $q_*\le x_*$, and $q_{*,i}=x_{*,i}-\delta<x_{*,i}$ for $i\in I$, where $I$ is nonempty by \cref{lem:linf-saturated}.
Since $x_*\in U\subseteq\triangle_d^{\le}$, we obtain $\inpr{\mathbf1,q_*}<\inpr{\mathbf1,x_*}\le1$, and thus $\nu=0$.

Next, we show $\nabla f\prn{q_*}\odot\mathbf1_I\neq0$.
Take $w$ from \cref{prop:linf} and write $w=\lambda\nabla f\prn{q_*}-\sum_{i\in Z}\mu_ie_i$ with $\lambda,\mu\ge0$.
By \cref{lem:linf-saturated}, $w$ is supported on $I$, so
\[
1=\sum_{i\in I}w_i\le\lambda\sum_{i\in I}\nabla_if\prn{q_*}.
\]
Hence $\nabla f\prn{q_*}\odot\mathbf1_I\neq0$.
In particular, $\lambda>0$, so $f\prn{q_*}=\theta$ and $\nabla f\prn{q_*}\in N_S\prn{q_*}$.

Since $\set{1,\dots,d}\setminus I\subseteq Z$ by \cref{lem:linf-saturated}, we have
\[
\nabla f\prn{q_*}\odot\mathbf1_I=\nabla f\prn{q_*}-\sum_{i\notin I}\nabla_if\prn{q_*}e_i\in N_S\prn{q_*},
\]
with $\nabla_if\prn{q_*}\ge0$.
Hence $w_*$ belongs to $N_S\prn{q_*}\cap\triangle_d$ and is supported on $I$, and \cref{lem:linf-saturated} gives \eqref{eq:linf-conditions}.

Let $g\prn{x}\coloneqq\max_{w\in\triangle_d}\inpr{w,x}/h_S\prn{w}$ for $x\in\mathbb{R}^d_+$.
Since $h_E=h_S$ for $E=S$, \eqref{eq:infconv-dual} and \cref{prop:infconv} give $\brc*{\gamma_S\prn{x}-1}_+=d_S\prn{x}=\brc*{g\prn{x}-1}_+$, so $\gamma_S\prn{x}=g\prn{x}$ whenever either exceeds $1$.
Both functions are positively homogeneous and positive at every $x\ne0$, because $S$ is bounded and $h_S>0$ on $\triangle_d$; hence scaling $x$ gives $\gamma_S=g$ on $\mathbb{R}^d_+$.
As in the proof of \cref{prop:infconv}, $g$ is Lipschitz continuous, so $\gamma_S$ attains its maximum over the compact set $U$, and $V_S\prn{U,S}=\max_{x\in U}d_S\prn{x}=\brc*{\max_{x\in U}\gamma_S\prn{x}-1}_+$ by \cref{prop:infconv}. This proves \eqref{eq:gauge-excess}.

If the excess is positive, then $\lambda_*=\gamma_S(x_*)>1$ and $d_S\prn{x_*}=\lambda_*-1$. By positive homogeneity, $\gamma_S\prn{q_*}=\gamma_S\prn{x_*}/\lambda_*=1$, so $d_S\prn{q_*}=0$ and $q_*\in S$ by \cref{prop:infconv}. For $t>1$, $\gamma_S\prn{tq_*}=t>1$ gives $tq_*\notin S$; letting $t\downarrow1$ shows that $q_*$ lies on the boundary of $S$.

Every $w\in\triangle_d$ satisfying $h_S\prn{w}=\inpr{w,q_*}$ obeys, by $x_*=\lambda_*q_*$,
\begin{equation}
\inpr{w,x_*}-h_S\prn{w}=\prn{\lambda_*-1}h_S\prn{w}=d_S\prn{x_*}\,h_S\prn{w},
\end{equation}
so \cref{lem:certificate} with $E=S$ gives $\inpr{w,x_*}=h_U\prn{w}$ and the maximality of $w$.

Continuity and $f\prn{0}<\theta$ give $\rho\triangle_d^{\le}\subseteq S$ for some $\rho>0$, so \eqref{eq:positivity} holds. By \cref{prop:gauge}, $q_*\in S$, and $\sum_i q_{*,i}=\lambda_*^{-1}\sum_i x_{*,i}<1$. If $f\prn{q_*}<\theta$, continuity gives a number $\eta>1$ sufficiently close to one that $\eta q_*\in\triangle_d^{\le}$ and $f\prn{\eta q_*}<\theta$. Then $\eta q_*\in S$ and $x_*\in\prn{\lambda_*/\eta}S$, contradicting the definition of $\lambda_*$. Thus $f\prn{q_*}=\theta$.

Monotonicity of $f$ gives $\nabla f\prn{q_*}\ge0$. For each $x\in S$ and $t\in\brc{0,1}$, convexity of $S$ implies $f\prn{q_*+t\prn{x-q_*}}\le\theta=f\prn{q_*}$. Taking the right derivative at $t=0$ yields $\inpr{\nabla f\prn{q_*},x-q_*}\le0$. If the gradient is nonzero, its normalization therefore belongs to $\triangle_d$ and supports $S$ at $q_*$. The conclusion follows from \cref{prop:gauge}.
