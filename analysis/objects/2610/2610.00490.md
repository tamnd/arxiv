<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:transverse}
Write $\iota_D:D\hookrightarrow\mathfrak X$, put
$Z_{[0]}=Z$, and regard $f_{[0]}=\iota_D\circ f$
as a morphism to $\mathfrak X$.  For $r\geq1$, an
\emph{order-$r$ transverse extension} of $f$ consists
of a superscheme $Z_{[r]}$, a closed immersion
$i_r:Z\hookrightarrow Z_{[r]}$, and a morphism
$f_{[r]}:Z_{[r]}\to\mathfrak X$, with a specified
$2$-commutative square
$$
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
 Z\arrow[r,hook,"i_r"]\arrow[d,"f"']&
 Z_{[r]}\arrow[d,"f_{[r]}"]\\
 D\arrow[r,hook,"\iota_D"']&\mathfrak X.
\end{tikzcd}
$$
We require this square to be $2$-Cartesian.  Explicitly, the
specified comparison
$f_{[r]}\circ i_r\simeq\iota_D\circ f$ must induce
an isomorphism
$$
 Z\xrightarrow{\sim}Z_{[r]}\times_{\mathfrak X}D.
$$
The adjective $2$-Cartesian records that this is a fibre
product of stacks.  For a superscheme $T$, an object of
$Z_{[r]}\times_{\mathfrak X}D$ is a triple
$$
 (a:T\to Z_{[r]},\ b:T\to D,\
   \varphi:f_{[r]}\circ a\xRightarrow{\sim}\iota_D\circ b).
$$
The isomorphism $\varphi$ is part of the datum; see
\cite[Tag~0040]{StacksProject}.  For superschemes viewed as
stacks this recovers the ordinary Cartesian fibre product.
Moreover, $\iota_D$ is a representable closed immersion,
so its base change here is the closed subsuperscheme cut out by
the pulled-back ideal of $D$.  Thus the displayed condition
identifies the entire inverse-image subsuperscheme with $Z$,
including its odd functions and nilpotent structure.

Let $\mathcal I_{[r]}=f_{[r]}^{-1}\mathcal I_D\cdot\mathcal O_{Z_{[r]}}$.
\'Etale-locally on $Z$, we further require an isomorphism of
augmented superalgebras with ideals
$$
 (\mathcal O_{Z_{[r]}},\mathcal I_{[r]})
 \simeq (\mathcal O_Z[t]/(t^{r+1}),(t)),
 \qquad |t|=\bar0,
$$
inducing the given identification with $\mathcal O_Z$ modulo
$\mathcal I_{[r]}$.  
Here $|t|=\bar0$ says that the new
parameter $t$ is even.  On a chosen \'etale-local lift to
a target atlas, as described above, the morphism is an immersion
and a local equation of $D$ pulls back to the generator
$t$.  Transversality means this identification of the new
even direction with the boundary-normal direction.

To extend a \emph{given} order-$(r-1)$ object, we also specify
an identification
$$
 (|Z|,\mathcal O_{Z_{[r]}}/\mathcal I_{[r]}^r)
 \xrightarrow{\sim}Z_{[r-1]},
$$
together with a compatible $2$-isomorphism between the
restricted map and $f_{[r-1]}$.  All isomorphisms of
extensions must respect these identifications.  In particular,
we keep the preceding-order extension fixed, but choose the
next source thickening as part of the extension.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:transverse-extension}
Fix $r\geq1$ and an order-$(r-1)$ transverse extension.
There is a canonical obstruction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transverse-obstruction}
 \operatorname{ob}_r(f_{[r-1]})\in
 H^1(Z,\mathcal M_r)
 =H^1\bigl(Z,(Q_f\otimes A_f^r)_{\bar0}\bigr).
\end{equation}
An order-$r$ extension exists if and only if this class vanishes.
When it exists, the set of its isomorphism classes is a torsor
under $H^0(Z,\mathcal M_r)$.  An automorphism inducing the identity
on the specified order-$(r-1)$ object is the identity.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:etale-local-normal-form}
In the setting of \cref{prop:transverse-extension},
\'etale-locally near any point of $Z$, there is a target
neighbourhood $V\to\mathfrak X$ and an \'etale morphism
$$
 (\pi_Z,w,t):
 V\longrightarrow Z\times\mathbb A^{a+1\mid b}
$$
such that, writing $D_V=D\times_{\mathfrak X}V$,
$$
 D_V=(t=0),\qquad
 f(Z)=(w=t=0),\qquad
 \pi_Z\circ f=\operatorname{Id}_Z.
$$

For every $r\geq1$, projection to $(\pi_Z,t)$
identifies each local order-$r$ transverse extension with
$Z\times\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb C[t]/(t^{r+1}))$.  Its map to $V$
is uniquely determined by a graph
$$
 w=\Phi(z,t),\qquad
 t^{r+1}=0,\qquad
 \Phi(z,0)=0,
$$
where each component of $\Phi$ has the parity of the
corresponding component of $w$.  Conversely, every such
graph determines a local transverse extension.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Choose an \'etale atlas $V\to\mathfrak X$ and pull back
the source.  We may then work with a morphism from a smooth
superscheme $Z$ to the smooth divisor $D_V\subset V$.
This is the super-coordinate counterpart of the ordinary
\'etale-local embedding statement recalled in
\cite[Section~1]{RydhEtale}.                                     
                                  

Dualising the split differential gives a locally split
surjection $f^*\Omega_{D_V}\twoheadrightarrow\Omega_Z$.
Choose homogeneous functions $z$ on $D_V$ whose
pulled-back differentials form a homogeneous basis at the
point under consideration.  After shrinking, their restrictions
give an \'etale morphism
{$h:Z\to\mathbb A^{1\mid q}$}.  Complete these functions
by homogeneous functions $\xi$ so that $(z,\xi)$
are \'etale coordinates on $D_V$.  Lift them to $V$
and choose a local equation $t$ of $D_V$.
The differentials of $(z,\xi,t)$ form a homogeneous
basis at the chosen point.  The super Jacobian criterion
therefore makes these \'etale coordinates near that point
in $V$.

Let $\pi:V\to\mathbb A^{1\mid q}$ be defined by
the lifted functions $z$, and form the Cartesian square
$$
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
 V'=V\times_{\mathbb A^{1\mid q}}Z
   \arrow[r]\arrow[d,"\pi_Z"']&V\arrow[d,"\pi"]\\
 Z\arrow[r,"h"']&\mathbb A^{1\mid q}.
\end{tikzcd}
$$
The upper horizontal map is \'etale, and $f$ lifts to
$f'=(f,\operatorname{Id}_Z):Z\to V'$.  On $V'$, subtract the
pullbacks of $f^*\xi$ from the functions $\xi$,
and denote the resulting tuple by $w$.  Translation in
these relative homogeneous coordinates shows that
$$
 (\pi_Z,w,t):V'\longrightarrow
 Z\times\mathbb A^{a+1\mid b}
$$
is \'etale and carries $f'$ to the zero section.
The inverse image of that section is \'etale over $Z$
and has the section $f'$, which is an open immersion.
The complement of its image is closed in this inverse image,
and the inverse image is closed in $V'$.  Removing that
complement from $V'$ leaves precisely the image of the
chosen section $f'$ over the zero section.  Thus this
local image is cut out by $w,t$, including the odd
components of $w$.


Now consider a transverse extension of order $r$.

\label{lem:graph-torsor}

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Apply \cref{lem:etale-local-normal-form}.  The coordinates
$z$ parametrise the source, $w$ are the complementary
directions within the divisor, and $t$ is normal to the
divisor.  In our curve setting the even dimension of $z$
is one.  The given order-$(r-1)$ extension is represented
uniquely by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:graphs}
 w=\Phi(z,t),\qquad t^r=0,\qquad \Phi(z,0)=0.
\end{equation}
For $r=1$, this means the central graph $w=0$.
Lifting its coefficients modulo $t^{r+1}$ gives a local
order-$r$ extension.  With one such lift as origin, every
other graph is obtained uniquely by adding
$$
 t^r h(z),\qquad |h_a|=|w_a|.
$$
Its parametrisation is immaterial: the graph determines the
source together with its map.                              
                                                      

We next check how the difference $t^r h(z)$ transforms on an
overlap.  This will identify its intrinsic coefficient bundle
and show that the construction does not depend on the adapted
coordinates.  Write the change of coordinates as
$$
 z_j=F_{ij}(z_i,w_i,t_i),\qquad
 w_j=G_{ij}(z_i,w_i,t_i),\qquad
 t_j=a_{ij}(z_i,w_i,t_i)t_i,
$$
where $a_{ij}$ is an even unit and
$G_{ij}(z_i,0,0)=0$.  Put
$$
 a_{ij}^0=a_{ij}|_Z,\qquad
 B_{ij}=\left.\frac{\partial G_{ij}}{\partial w_i}\right|_Z.
$$
The supermatrix $B_{ij}$ represents the induced differential
on $Q_f$.  Compare two graphs differing by $t_i^r h_i$.
Their $t_j$-coordinates differ by
$t_i\,\delta a_{ij}\in(t_i^{r+1})$, so they agree to
the order under consideration.  Their $z_j$-coordinates
may differ in order $r$.  However, the reference graph
vanishes at $t_j=0$, so its derivative in $z_j$ is
divisible by $t_j$.  Re-expressing both graphs at the same
$(z_j,t_j)$ therefore changes their difference only in
order $r+1$.  The remaining difference is
$t_i^r B_{ij}h_i$ modulo $t_i^{r+1}$.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:graph-difference-transition}
 h_j=(a_{ij}^0)^{-r}B_{ij}h_i,
\end{equation}
where the central change of the $z$-coordinates is understood.

Equation~\eqref{eq:graph-difference-transition} is exactly the
transition rule for an even section of $Q_f\otimes A_f^r$:
the local generator $t_j^r$ of $A_f^r$ is
$(a_{ij}^0)^r t_i^r$.  The calculation holds on the full
superscheme $Z$, including its odd functions, and also holds
for $r=1$.  Subtraction of graphs is additive at order $r$, since
products of two differences vanish modulo $t^{r+1}$.
This proves the torsor assertion and its naturality.

We first construct the obstruction class from the local

$R=V\times_{\mathfrak X}V$, with source and target

nilpotent extension; \'etale morphisms admit unique such lifts.

 Z_{ij,[r-1]} \arrow[r,"\gamma_{ij}"] \arrow[d,hook]
   & R \arrow[d,"s"]\\

   \arrow[ur,dashed,"\widetilde\gamma_{ij}" description]

\end{tikzcd}

arrows lift the same preceding arrow with the same first endpoint.

immersion.  The same argument shows that a relative automorphism

\label{cor:equivariant-formal}

pair $(\mathfrak X,D)$, and that $f:Z\to D$ is

the action on $\mathfrak X$ preserves $D$, and the
equivariance of $f$ includes specified comparison
$2$-isomorphisms satisfying the group law.  They induce
actions on $Q_f$, $A_f$, and the obstruction groups.

with its action and comparison isomorphisms restricting to

$\operatorname{ob}_r(f_{[r-1]})$ is $H$-invariant,
and its vanishing suffices for a coherent equivariant extension.

 H^1\bigl(Z,(Q_f\otimes A_f^r)_{\bar0}\bigr)^H=0
 \qquad(r\geq1).
\end{equation}

$$

maps, commute with reduction from order $a+1$ to
order $a$.  For a vector bundle $E$ on $X$,
write
$$
 \widehat{f^*E}=\varprojlim_a f_a^*E.
$$
These complete modules are locally free: local frames of
the target bundle pull back compatibly on the formal charts.
Equivalently, one can lift frames successively as in
\cref{lem:complete-filtration}.

To identify the completed twist, consider first the line
bundle $\mathcal O_X(-D_0)$ and its map to $\mathcal O_X$.
On compatible charts, \cref{thm:formal-map} gives
$\widehat{f^*u}=e t$, with $e$ invertible and

Consequently the completed map
$$

$$

is a non-zero-divisor in $\widehat\mathcal A$, this map
identifies the completed pullback line bundle with $\mathcal I$.

but multiplication by the nilpotent image of $e t$ is
not injective.  Thus the identification with an ideal is
being made in the complete ring, not in a finite-order
truncation.

Taking dual tensor powers and using local frames therefore gives
$$
 \varprojlim_a f_a^*B(mD_0)
 \simeq\widehat{f^*B}\otimes_{\widehat\mathcal A}\mathcal I^{-m}.
$$
The compatible sections $f_a^*\beta_m$ define a section

 \widetilde\beta^\times\in
 H^0\!\left(C,\widehat{f^*B}
              \otimes_{\widehat\mathcal A}\mathcal A^\times\right).
$$

be the limit of the maps $f_a^*p$.  The image of this

This can also be checked directly in the local expression above:
$$
 \widetilde\beta^\times=(e t)^{-m}\widehat{f^*v},
 \qquad
 \widehat p^{\,\times}(\widetilde\beta^\times)
 =(e t)^{-m}\widehat{f^*(u^m)}=1.
$$
In particular, substitution by the formal map preserves a
finite pole bound.

This construction explains the order of operations.  We
extended a section of $B|_U$ to a regular section of
$B(mD_0)$, pulled that section back to all finite

Cartier ideal.  Localising $\mathcal A_{[a]}$ itself at

nilpotent there.  The regular twisted pullbacks are what
allow us to pass through those finite orders.

It remains to transfer the section to the comparison extension.
The compatible middle arrows in
\eqref{eq:comparison-pushout} give a map
$\widehat\psi:\widehat{f^*B}\to\widehat\mathcal P$.

$$
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
 \widehat{f^*B}\otimes_{\widehat\mathcal A}\mathcal A^\times
 \arrow[r,"\widehat\psi^\times"]
 \arrow[d,"\widehat p^{\,\times}"']
 &\widehat\mathcal P\otimes_{\widehat\mathcal A}\mathcal A^\times
 \arrow[d,"\widehat q^\times"]\\
 \mathcal A^\times\arrow[r,equal]&\mathcal A^\times.
\end{tikzcd}
$$
Hence $\beta^\times=\widehat\psi^\times
(\widetilde\beta^\times)$ satisfies
$\widehat q^\times(\beta^\times)=1$.
This section is global: $\beta_m$ descended on $X$,
and pullback, the compatible inverse limits, and the maps
in the pushout preserve its gluing identities.
Multiplication by $\beta^\times$ gives the required
$\mathcal A^\times$-linear splitting.

Finally, the local sections $\lambda_i$ of
\cref{lem:compatible-splittings} also map to $1$.
Thus
$$
 b_i=\lambda_i-\beta^\times|_{U_i}
 \in\mathcal G^\times(U_i).
$$
Their differences on overlaps are
$b_j-b_i=\lambda_j-\lambda_i=\Gamma_{ij}$, so
$\delta b=\Gamma$.  This is the asserted Laurent
primitive.

<!-- lemma-15: body_md -->
\label{lem:reflexive-Hartogs}
Suppose that $X$ is normal and
$\operatorname{codim}_X Z\geq2$.  For a vector bundle $F$
on $U$, the sheaf $E=j_*F$ is coherent and reflexive, and
the canonical restriction morphism is an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reflexive-analytic-extension}
 E^{\mathrm{an}}\xrightarrow{\sim}j^{\mathrm{an}}_*F^{\mathrm{an}}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for every analytic open subset $W\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}$,
restriction induces an isomorphism
$$
 H^0(W,E^{\mathrm{an}})\xrightarrow{\sim}
 H^0(W\cap U^{\mathrm{an}},F^{\mathrm{an}}).
$$
<!-- end lemma-15 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
We first construct the algebraic extension and explain why it
is canonical.  Choose a coherent extension $G$ of $F$
to $X$, and form its double dual $G^{\vee\vee}$.
On a normal variety this hull is reflexive, and its restriction
to $U$ is $F^{\vee\vee}=F$.  A reflexive sheaf on
a normal variety is recovered from its restriction away from
a subset of codimension at least two
\cite[Proposition~1.6]{Hartshorne}; see also
\cite[Tag~0EBJ]{StacksProject} for the normal case used here.
Applying this fact to
the hull gives
$$
 G^{\vee\vee}\simeq j_*\bigl(G^{\vee\vee}|_U\bigr)
 \simeq j_*F.
$$
Thus $E=j_*F$ is coherent and reflexive, independently of
the initial choice of $G$.

We next verify that this same extension has the required
Hartogs property on analytic open subsets.  The analytic
local ring is flat over the algebraic local ring
\cite[Section~6, Proposition~3 and its corollary]{SerreGAGA}.
Duals of finitely presented modules commute with flat base
change \cite[Proposition~21]{SerreGAGA}, so the isomorphism
$E\simeq E^{\vee\vee}$ analytifies to
$E^{\mathrm{an}}\simeq(E^{\mathrm{an}})^{\vee\vee}$.  In particular,
$E^{\mathrm{an}}$ is reflexive.  The space $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is
normal, and $Z^{\mathrm{an}}$ has codimension at least two.

To see explicitly how sections extend, work locally on
$X^{\mathrm{an}}$ and dualise a finite presentation of
$(E^{\mathrm{an}})^\vee$.  Reflexivity expresses $E^{\mathrm{an}}$
as the kernel of a map between finite free sheaves:
$$
 0\longrightarrow E^{\mathrm{an}}\longrightarrow
 \mathcal O_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\oplus a}
 \xrightarrow{\Phi}\mathcal O_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\oplus b}.
$$
A section of $E^{\mathrm{an}}$ away from $Z^{\mathrm{an}}$ is
therefore a tuple of holomorphic functions satisfying
$\Phi(s)=0$.  The Hartogs theorem on normal complex
spaces extends each function uniquely across $Z^{\mathrm{an}}$.
The relation $\Phi(s)=0$ continues to hold by density, so
the extended tuple is a section of $E^{\mathrm{an}}$.  Uniqueness
makes these local extensions agree on overlaps.  They hence
glue on every analytic open subset, proving
\eqref{eq:reflexive-analytic-extension} and its stated
interpretation in terms of sections.

<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
Let $\alpha\in H^1(U,F)$ have zero analytic image.
The identification
$H^1(U,F)=\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal O_U}(\mathcal O_U,F)$ represents it by an
algebraic extension
$$
 0\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow B
 \xrightarrow{p}\mathcal O_U\longrightarrow0.
$$
Its quotient is locally free, so the sequence splits locally
and $B$ is a vector bundle.  Analytification preserves this exact sequence, and the
resulting extension represents the image
$\alpha^{\mathrm{an}}$ under the natural comparison map. 
The vanishing of
$\alpha^{\mathrm{an}}$ gives a holomorphic section
$s^{\mathrm{an}}\in H^0(U^{\mathrm{an}},B^{\mathrm{an}})$ satisfying
$p^{\mathrm{an}}(s^{\mathrm{an}})=1$.

Apply \cref{lem:reflexive-Hartogs} to $B$.  The sheaf
$\overline B=j_*B$ is coherent reflexive, and
$\overline B^{\mathrm{an}}\simeq j^{\mathrm{an}}_*B^{\mathrm{an}}$.
Proper GAGA \cite{SerreGAGA} gives the natural isomorphisms
$$
\begin{aligned}
 H^0(U,B)&=H^0(X,\overline B)\\
 &\xrightarrow{\sim}H^0(X^{\mathrm{an}},\overline B^{\mathrm{an}})
 =H^0(U^{\mathrm{an}},B^{\mathrm{an}}).
\end{aligned}
$$

section $s\in H^0(U,B)$.  The same comparison for
$\mathcal O_U$, and naturality with respect to $p$, give
the commutative square
$$
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
 H^0(U,B)\arrow[r,"\sim"]\arrow[d,"p"']&
 H^0(U^{\mathrm{an}},B^{\mathrm{an}})\arrow[d,"p^{\mathrm{an}}"]\\
 H^0(U,\mathcal O_U)\arrow[r,"\sim"]&
 H^0(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathcal O_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}).
\end{tikzcd}
$$
Since the bottom arrow sends $1$ to $1$ and is
injective, $p(s)=1$.  The map $a\mapsto as$ therefore
splits the algebraic extension, so $\alpha=0$.

<!-- lemma-16: body_md -->
\label{lem:coarse-Torelli}
One has $\overline\tau^{-1}(A_g^{\rm ind})=M_g$.
The Torelli morphism $\tau:M_g\to J_g$ is finite and
surjective.  Moreover, $J_g$ is closed in $A_g^{\rm ind}$
and open in its reduced Satake closure $\overline J_g^{\rm Sat}$.
<!-- end lemma-16 -->
<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
The principally polarised Jacobian of a smooth curve is
indecomposable.  Conversely, suppose that a stable curve
has image in $A_g^{\rm ind}$.  Its abelian part must
have dimension $g$, so
\eqref{eq:stable-Satake-image} forces
$b_1(\Gamma_C)=0$.  Thus $\Gamma_C$ is a tree
and the curve is of compact type.  Indecomposability of
the product then forces exactly one component to have
positive genus.

There can be no rational components.  Indeed, a tree
with at least two vertices has at least two leaves.
If only one vertex has positive genus, at least one
leaf is rational.  That component would have only one
attaching point and no markings, contradicting stability.
The graph therefore has a single vertex and no edges,
so the curve is smooth.

This identifies the inverse image of the indecomposable
open locus and gives the Cartesian square
$$
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
 M_g\arrow[r,hook]\arrow[d,"\tau"']&
 \overline M_g\arrow[d,"\overline\tau"]\\
 A_g^{\rm ind}\arrow[r,hook]&A_g^{\rm Sat}.
\end{tikzcd}
$$
Consequently $\tau:M_g\to A_g^{\rm ind}$ is proper,
being a base change of $\overline\tau$.  The classical
Torelli theorem makes it injective on geometric points
and hence quasi-finite.  A proper quasi-finite morphism
is finite.  Its reduced image $J_g$ is therefore
closed in $A_g^{\rm ind}$, and the induced morphism
to that image is finite and surjective.  Finally,
closedness in this open subset gives
$$
 \overline J_g^{\rm Sat}\cap A_g^{\rm ind}=J_g,
$$
which proves that $J_g$ is open in its Satake closure.

<!-- lemma-18: body_md -->
\label{lem:gerbe-trace}
Let
$$
 r:\mathcal S_g^\varepsilon\longrightarrow\mathcal R_g^\varepsilon,
 \qquad q:U_{g,n}^\varepsilon\longrightarrow\mathcal R_g^\varepsilon
$$
be the scalar $\boldsymbol\mu_2$-gerbe and the

the summand $F_{\rm inv}$ descends to a vector bundle
$F_{\rm rig}$ on $\mathcal R_g^\varepsilon$, with
$r^*F_{\rm rig}\simeq F_{\rm inv}$. Pullback
followed by the summand inclusion induces an isomorphism
$$
 H^1(\mathcal R_g^\varepsilon,F_{\rm rig})
 \xrightarrow{\sim}H^1(\mathcal S_g^\varepsilon,F).
$$
The anti-invariant summand satisfies
$$
 H^k(\mathcal S_g^\varepsilon,F_{\rm anti})=0
 \qquad(k\geq0).
$$
\item For every vector bundle $G$ on
$\mathcal R_g^\varepsilon$, pullback is injective:
$$
 q^*:H^1(\mathcal R_g^\varepsilon,G)
 \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow H^1(U_{g,n}^\varepsilon,q^*G).
$$
\end{enumerate}

decomposition and the comparison maps commute with
analytification.}

The central involution gives the two global idempotents
displayed above.  Their images are direct summands of a locally
free sheaf, hence are vector bundles.  These constructions are
canonical and commute with pullback.

Work \'etale-locally on $\mathcal R_g^\varepsilon$, where the
gerbe is $B\boldsymbol\mu_2$ over the base.  A vector
bundle there is a bundle with a $\boldsymbol\mu_2$-action;
its invariant summand is a bundle with trivial action and thus
comes from the base.  This description is compatible with
descent and produces $F_{\rm rig}$, with
$r^*F_{\rm rig}\simeq F_{\rm inv}$.

On an \'etale base chart $T$ over which the gerbe is
$T\times B\boldsymbol\mu_2$, let $E$ be the
$\boldsymbol\mu_2$-equivariant bundle on $T$
corresponding to the pullback of $F$ to this gerbe.
Then
$$
 (r_*F)|_T \simeq E^{\boldsymbol\mu_2},
$$
where $E^{\boldsymbol\mu_2}\subset E$ is the subsheaf
of sections fixed by the scalar involution.
Thus the direct image functor is locally identified
with the invariants functor
$E\mapsto E^{\boldsymbol\mu_2}$.
This functor is exact over $\mathbb C$, by the averaging operator
$\frac12(\operatorname{Id}+\kappa)$.  Its derived functors therefore
vanish in positive degrees; this is the linearly reductive
case of tame descent \cite{AOV}.  Since $F_{\rm anti}$
has no invariant sections, the resulting identities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 r_*F_{\rm inv}
   &\simeq F_{\rm rig},\\
 R^k r_*F_{\rm inv}
   &=0 \qquad (k>0),\\
 R^k r_*F_{\rm anti}
   &=0 \qquad (k\geq0).
\end{aligned}
$$
These are local identities on the base and hence hold globally.
The Leray spectral sequence now gives both the acyclicity of
$F_{\rm anti}$ and the isomorphism
$$
 H^1(\mathcal R_g^\varepsilon,F_{\rm rig})
 \simeq H^1(\mathcal S_g^\varepsilon,F_{\rm inv})
 \simeq H^1(\mathcal S_g^\varepsilon,F).
$$

The same proof works analytically on local charts neutralising
the gerbe.  There the relative cohomology is computed by the
cohomology of the finite group $\mu_2$; averaging makes
invariants exact and kills its positive-degree cohomology.
Thus the same direct-image identities and Leray argument apply.
The idempotents and the descent identifications commute with
analytification, giving the stated compatibility.  This proves
(i) in both categories.

$$
 G\longrightarrow q_*q^*G

 \xrightarrow{\operatorname{Id}\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_q}G
$$
whose composite is $d\,\operatorname{Id}_G$.  Since $q$

cohomology therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-trace-injection}
 \operatorname{Tr}_q\circ q^*=d\,\operatorname{Id}.
\end{equation}
Division by $d$, on each connected component

map is locally a finite disjoint union of copies

copies.  The same identity proves injectivity.
The unit, trace and gerbe averaging commute with
analytification, and so do the induced cohomology
maps.

summand from first cohomology in both categories, compatibly
with analytification.  Its gerbe isomorphisms therefore reduce

$$
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
 H^1(R,G)\arrow[r,hook,"q^*"]\arrow[d]&
 H^1(U,q^*G)\arrow[d,hook]\\
 H^1(R^{\mathrm{an}},G^{\mathrm{an}})\arrow[r,hook]&

\end{tikzcd}

A holomorphic splitting makes its analytic image zero, so the
injectivity just proved makes the algebraic extension split.
The converse follows by analytifying an algebraic splitting.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For every $g\geq3$ and $\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}$, the primary
obstruction of the smooth unpunctured supermoduli component
$\mathfrak M_g^\varepsilon$ is nonzero both algebraically and holomorphically:
$$
 \omega_2^{\rm alg}(\mathfrak M_g^\varepsilon)\ne0,
 \qquad
 \omega_2^{\rm hol}((\mathfrak M_g^\varepsilon)^{\mathrm{an}})\ne0.
$$
Consequently both components are non-projected and non-split in
both categories.

<!-- proof-32: body_md -->
By Section~\ref{sec:transverse}, the algebraic primary class
belongs to $H^1(\mathcal S_g^\varepsilon,K_{2,g}^\varepsilon)$, where
$$
 K_{2,g}^\varepsilon=
 \operatorname{Hom}(\bigwedge^2T_-\mathfrak M_g^\varepsilon,T_+\mathfrak M_g^\varepsilon).
$$
This is an algebraic vector bundle, so
\cref{prop:spin-comparison} gives an injection on its first
cohomology under analytification.

We next identify this analytic image with the holomorphic
primary obstruction. On smooth charts, analytification
identifies algebraic differentials with holomorphic
differentials and preserves the odd ideal and its powers.
The definitions in Section~\ref{sec:transverse} therefore
give canonical identifications
$$
 \bigl(T_\pm\mathfrak M_g^\varepsilon\bigr)^{\mathrm{an}}
 \simeq T_\pm\bigl((\mathfrak M_g^\varepsilon)^{\mathrm{an}}\bigr).
$$
Since analytification also commutes with duals, tensor
products and exterior powers of vector bundles,
$(K_{2,g}^\varepsilon)^{\mathrm{an}}$ is precisely the coefficient
bundle of the holomorphic primary obstruction.

Choose an \'etale atlas by smooth superschemes and,
after refinement, local split coordinates on its charts.
As recalled in Section~\ref{sec:transverse}, the quadratic
terms in the odd variables in the even coordinate changes
define a cocycle representing
$\omega_2^{\rm alg}(\mathfrak M_g^\varepsilon)$.
Analytification turns these coordinate changes into
holomorphic ones and preserves both their linear
transition maps and their quadratic coefficients.
The resulting cocycle is therefore the one defining
the holomorphic primary obstruction.

The cocycle identities on the atlas groupoid, as well as
the coboundary identities arising from changes of local
splittings, are preserved by analytification. Thus this
identification is independent of the chosen charts and
descends to the spin stack. Under the natural comparison
map, it gives
$$
 \omega_2^{\rm alg}(\mathfrak M_g^\varepsilon)
 \longmapsto
 \omega_2^{\rm hol}\bigl((\mathfrak M_g^\varepsilon)^{\mathrm{an}}\bigr).
$$

Finally, Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic} says that
the class on the left is nonzero.  Injectivity
of Proposition~\ref{prop:spin-comparison} makes
its image on the right nonzero as well.
Projectedness would force the relevant primary
class to vanish, and a splitting would supply
a projection.  Both are therefore excluded in
both categories, proving the theorem.
