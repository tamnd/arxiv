<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{defn:signed_burning}
Let $\Gamma = (G, \sigma)$ be a signed graph. At any given time, each vertex is in exactly one of three states: unburned, burned yellow, or burned green. Initially ($t=0$) every vertex is unburned; once a vertex is burned (yellow or green), it remains in that state. For each time $t\geq 1$, the following two steps are performed in order.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spread:} For each unburned vertex $v$, compute the yellow influence
    $$s_t^{y}(v) = |\{u \in N^{+}(v) : u \text{ is yellow}\}| + |\{u \in N^{-}(v) : u \text{ is green}\}|,$$
    and the green influence
    $$s_t^{g}(v) = |\{u \in N^{+}(v) : u \text{ is green}\}| + |\{u \in N^{-}(v) : u \text{ is yellow}\}|.$$
 Then update simultaneously every unburned vertex $v$ as follows:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item burn $v$ yellow if $s_t^{y}(v) > s_t^{g}(v)$,
        \item burn $v$ green if $s_t^{y}(v) < s_t^{g}(v)$,
        \item leave $v$ unburned if $s_t^{y}(v) = s_t^{g}(v)$.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ignition:} Pick an unburned vertex, if one exists, and burn it yellow or green.
\end{enumerate}
This process terminates when every vertex is either burned yellow or burned green.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:arithmetic}
Identify yellow with $1$, green with $-1$, unburned with $0$, and let $\nu_t \colon V(G) \to \{0,1,-1\}$ record the state of every vertex at the end of time $t$, so that $\nu_0 \equiv 0$. A burned neighbour $u$ of $v$ contributes to $s_t^{y}(v)$ iff $\sigma(vu)\nu_{t-1}(u) = 1$ and to $s_t^{g}(v)$ iff $\sigma(vu)\nu_{t-1}(u) = -1$, while an unburned neighbour contributes
$\sigma(vu)\nu_{t-1}(u) = 0$ to neither. Hence, writing
\[
  h_t(v) \; :=\; \sum\nolimits_{u \in N(v)} \sigma(vu)\,\nu_{t-1}(u),
\]
we have $h_t(v) = s_t^{y}(v) - s_t^{g}(v)$ for unburned $v$, and the spread step results in 
\[
  \nu_t(v) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \nu_{t-1}(v), & \text{if } \nu_{t-1}(v) \neq 0,\\[2pt]
    \operatorname{sgn} (h_t(v)), & \text{if } \nu_{t-1}(v) = 0,
  \end{cases}
\]
with 
$\operatorname{sgn} (0) := 0$. The ignition step then changes a single remaining $0$ to either $1$ or $-1$, producing $\nu_t$. The process ends once $\nu_T \in \{\pm 1\}^{V(G)}$ for some~$T$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:switching_burning}
Let $\Gamma = (G,\sigma)$ be a signed graph, $U \subseteq V(G)$, and $\mathcal{I} \in \mathcal{B}(\Gamma)$. Then $\mathcal{I}^U \in \mathcal{B}(\Gamma^U)$, $T(\mathcal{I}^U) = T(\mathcal{I})$, and  for all $0 \leq t \leq T = T(\mathcal{I})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:switching_set_identities}
  \nu_{\mathcal{I}^U\!,\,t} \;=\; \Phi_U \cdot \nu_{\mathcal{I},t}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
$
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I}^U\!,\,t} = \bigl(\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I},t} \setminus U\bigr) \cup \bigl(\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{I},t} \cap U\bigr)$, and
$
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{I}^U\!,\,t} = \bigl(\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{I},t} \setminus U\bigr) \cup \bigl(\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I},t} \cap U\bigr).
$
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Write $\nu_t = \nu_{\mathcal{I},t}$ for the process $\mathcal{I}$ on $\Gamma$ and
$\nu_t' = \nu_{\mathcal{I}^U\!,\,t}$ for the process $\mathcal{I}^U$ on $\Gamma^U$, and let
$h_t$ and $h_t'$ be the corresponding influence sums from Remark~\ref{rem:arithmetic}. We prove
\eqref{eqn:switching_set_identities} by induction on $t$, the case $t = 0$ being clear since
$\nu_0 = \nu_0' \equiv 0$. Assume $\nu_{t-1}' = \Phi_U \cdot \nu_{t-1}$ for some $t \geq 1$. By
\eqref{eqn:switch_product} and $\Phi_U(u)^2 = 1$,
\begin{align*}  
h_t'(v) &= \sum_{u \in N(v)} \sigma^U(vu)\,\nu_{t-1}'(u) = \sum_{u \in N(v)} \Phi_U(v)\Phi_U(u)\sigma(vu)\,\Phi_U(u)\nu_{t-1}(u)\\
&= \Phi_U(v)\,h_t(v).
\end{align*}

A vertex is unburned in one process exactly when it is unburned in the other, since
$\nu_{t-1}'(v) = 0$ if and only if $\nu_{t-1}(v) = 0$. If $\nu_{t-1}(v) \neq 0$ then both
processes keep the colour of $v$, so $\nu_t'(v) = \nu_{t-1}'(v) = \Phi_U(v)\nu_{t-1}(v) = \Phi_U(v)\nu_t(v)$ by the induction hypothesis. If $\nu_{t-1}(v) = 0$ the spread step gives $\nu_t'(v) = \operatorname{sgn} (h_t'(v)) = \Phi_U(v)\operatorname{sgn} (h_t(v)) = \Phi_U(v)\nu_t(v)$, because $\operatorname{sgn}$ is an odd function. In particular, the two processes have the same unburned set after the spread step, so a vertex is available for ignition in one precisely when it is available in the other. If an ignition occurs at time $t$ it is at the same vertex $v_t$ in both processes, receiving the colour $c_t$ in $\mathcal{I}$ and $\Phi_U(v_t)c_t$ in
$\mathcal{I}^U$, which again agrees with \eqref{eqn:switching_set_identities}. This completes the induction. Consequently the two processes burn their last vertex at the same time and ignite at the same
rounds, so $T(\mathcal{I}^U) = T(\mathcal{I})$ and
$\mathcal{I}^U \in \mathcal{B}(\Gamma^U)$. Moreover, the equivalence follows immediately.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:R_switching}
For every signed graph $\Gamma$ and every $U \subseteq V(G)$ we have
$\mathcal{R}(\Gamma^U) = \Phi_U \cdot \mathcal{R}(\Gamma)$. In particular
$\mathcal{R}(\Gamma) = -\mathcal{R}(\Gamma)$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The map $\mathcal{I} \mapsto \mathcal{I}^U$ is a bijection $\mathcal{B}(\Gamma) \to \mathcal{B}(\Gamma^U)$, since $(\mathcal{I}^U)^U = \mathcal{I}$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:switching_burning} applies for both implications; the first claim is now \eqref{eqn:switching_set_identities} at $t = T$. Taking $U = V(G)$ gives $\Phi_U \equiv -1$, and $\Gamma^{V(G)} = \Gamma$ by \eqref{eqn:switch_product}, whence $\mathcal{R}(\Gamma) = -\mathcal{R}(\Gamma)$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{defn:parameters}
Let $\Gamma = (G,\sigma)$ be a signed graph. Then the signed burning number is $b(\Gamma)= \min_{\mathcal{I}\,\in\,\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)} T(\mathcal{I})$ and the plurality number is $Y(\Gamma) = \max_{\mathcal{I}\,\in\,\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)}\left(\max\bigl\{|\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I}}|,\, |\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{I}}|\bigr\}\right)$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:symmetry}
For any signed graph $\Gamma$ on $n$ vertices,
\[
    Y(\Gamma)
    \;=\; \max_{\mathcal{I}\,\in\,\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)} |\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I}}|
    \;=\; \max_{\mathcal{I}\,\in\,\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)} |\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{I}}|
    \;\geq\; \lceil n/2 \rceil.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By Corollary~\ref{cor:R_switching}, $\nu \mapsto -\nu$ is a bijection of $\mathcal{R}(\Gamma)$ onto itself,  exchanging $\{v : \nu(v) = 1\}$ with $\{v : \nu(v) = -1\}$. Hence $\max_{\mathcal{I}} |\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I}}| = \max_{\mathcal{I}} |\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{I}}|=Y(\Gamma)$.
The bound $Y(\Gamma) \geq \lceil n/2 \rceil$ follows since $|\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I}}| + |\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{I}}| = n$ for every $\mathcal{I} \in \mathcal{B}(\Gamma)$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:burning_bound}
Let $G$ be a graph, and let $\Gamma = (G, \sigma)$ be a signed graph.
\begin{enumerate}[{\upshape(i)}]
    \item If $\Gamma' = (G, \sigma')$ is a signed graph with $\Gamma' \sim \Gamma$, then $b(\Gamma') = b(\Gamma)$.
    \item The (unsigned) burning number satisfies $b(G) \leq b(\Gamma)$.
    \item If $\Gamma$ is a balanced signed graph, then $b(G) = b(\Gamma)$. In particular, if $G$ is a forest, then $b(G) = b(\Gamma)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
\begin{enumerate}[{\upshape(i)}]
\item We have $\Gamma' = \Gamma^U$ for some $U \subseteq V(G)$ since $\Gamma' \sim \Gamma$. Consider a burning sequence $\mathcal{I} \in \mathcal{B}(\Gamma)$ with $T(\mathcal{I}) = b(\Gamma)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:switching_burning} implies that $\mathcal{I}^U \in \mathcal{B}(\Gamma')$ with $T(\mathcal{I}^U) = T(\mathcal{I}) = b(\Gamma)$, so $b(\Gamma') \leq b(\Gamma)$. The reverse inequality follows by symmetry. Therefore, $b(\Gamma') = b(\Gamma)$.

\item Fix an optimal burning sequence $\mathcal{I} = (v_1^{c_1}, \ldots, v_k^{c_k})$ for $\Gamma$. So $T = T(\mathcal{I}) = b(\Gamma)$, and write $\nu_t = \nu_{\mathcal{I},t}$ and $h_t$ for the associated configurations and influence sums. Run standard burning of $G$ with the ignitions $(v_1,\ldots,v_k)$. When some $v_t$ is already burned, ignite an arbitrary unburned vertex instead. We show that this process burns every vertex by time $T$. Let $Z_t$ denote the set of burned vertices after time $t$ in that standard process, and let $W_t = \mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I},t} \cup \mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{I},t}$ denote the set of all burned vertices in the signed burning process after time $t$. We claim $W_t \subseteq Z_t$ for all $t$, by induction on $t$. The base case $t = 0$ is immediate. Suppose $W_{t-1} \subseteq Z_{t-1}$. Let $v$ burn during the spread step of the signed process at time $t$. Then $h_t(v)\neq0$, so some term $\sigma(uv)\nu_{t-1}(u)$ is nonzero. Thus
$v$ has a burned neighbour in $W_{t-1}\subseteq Z_{t-1}$. Standard burning at time $t$ burns every unburned neighbour of $Z_{t-1}$, so $v\in Z_t$. At time $t \leq k$ the signed process ignites $v_t$. The standard process ignites $v_t$ as well if it is still unburned, and otherwise ignites any unburned vertex. In both cases $v_t \in Z_t$, since $v_t \in Z_{t-1}$ whenever it is already burned. Hence $W_t \subseteq Z_t$. Since $W_T = V(G)$, we get $Z_T = V(G)$ and $b(G) \leq b(\Gamma)$.

\item If $\Gamma$ is balanced then $\Gamma \sim \Gamma_+$ and by (i), $b(\Gamma) = b(\Gamma_+)$. By (ii), $b(G) \leq b(\Gamma_+)$. Fix an optimal burning sequence $(v_1, \ldots, v_k)$ for $G$, so that standard burning terminates at time $T = b(G)$. Run the signed burning process on $\Gamma_+$ with ignition sequence $(v_1^y, \ldots, v_k^y)$. Since all edges of $\Gamma_+$ are positive, an easy induction on $t$ shows that every configuration of this process is nonnegative: the ignitions are yellow, and $\operatorname{sgn} (h_t) \geq 0$ whenever $\nu_{t-1} \geq 0$. Hence $h_t(v)$ is the number of burned neighbours of $v$ and no green-burned vertex ever arises; the spreading rule thus reduces to that of standard graph burning. Hence $b(\Gamma_+) \leq b(G)$, giving $b(\Gamma_+) = b(G)$. Consequently, $b(G) = b(\Gamma)$. A forest has no cycles, so any signed forest is balanced. \qedhere
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:switching_distance}
Let $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma'$ be signed graphs on the same underlying graph $G$ on $n$ vertices, and suppose $\Gamma' = \Gamma^{\,U}$ for some $U \subseteq V(G)$. Then
\[
  Y(\Gamma') = \max\nolimits_{\,\nu \,\in\, \mathcal{R}(\Gamma)} \bigl|\{v : \nu(v) = \Phi_U(v)\}\bigr|
  = n - \min\nolimits_{\,\nu \,\in\, \mathcal{R}(\Gamma)} d_H\bigl(\nu, \Phi_U\bigr),
\]
where $d_H(\nu,\nu') = |\{v : \nu(v) \neq \nu'(v)\}|$ denotes the Hamming distance.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:switching_burning} and Corollary~\ref{cor:R_switching} the map $\nu \mapsto \mu:=\Phi_U \cdot \nu$ is a bijection $\mathcal{R}(\Gamma) \to \mathcal{R}(\Gamma')$, and $\mu(v)=\Phi_U(v)\nu(v) = 1$ if and only if $\nu(v) = \Phi_U(v)$. Hence
\[
  Y(\Gamma') = \max\nolimits_{\mu \in \mathcal{R}(\Gamma')} \bigl|\{v : \mu(v) = 1\}\bigr|
  = \max\nolimits_{\nu \in \mathcal{R}(\Gamma)} \bigl|\{v : \nu(v) = \Phi_U(v)\}\bigr|,
\]
since $\nu$ and $\Phi_U$ agree at exactly $n - d_H(\nu,\Phi_U)$ vertices.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:covering_radius}
(i) Identify $\{\pm1\}$ with the field $\mathbb{F}_2$ via $x\mapsto(1-x)/2$. Then $R(\Gamma)$ becomes a binary code of length $n$. Proposition~\ref{prop:switching_distance} says
that $n-Y(\Gamma^U)$ is the distance from the word $\Phi_U$ to this code. As $U$ ranges over all subsets of $V(G)$, the vector $\Phi_U$ ranges over all of $\{\pm 1\}^{V(G)}$ and the signed graph $\Gamma^{\,U}$ ranges over the whole switching class of $\Gamma$, so
    \[
    \min\nolimits_{\Gamma' \sim \Gamma} Y(\Gamma')
    = n - \max\nolimits_{U \subseteq V(G)} \ \min\nolimits_{\nu \in \mathcal{R}(\Gamma)} d_H\bigl(\nu, \Phi_U\bigr)
    = n - \operatorname{cr}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(\Gamma)\bigr),
    \]
    where $\operatorname{cr}(C) = \max_{\Phi \in \{\pm 1\}^{V(G)}} \min_{\nu \in C} d_H(\nu,\Phi)$ denotes the covering radius of a code $C \subseteq \{\pm 1\}^{V(G)}$. Determining the signature that minimises the plurality number within a switching class is thus a covering radius problem for $\mathcal{R}(\Gamma)$.
    
    (ii) Taking $\Gamma' = \Gamma$ and $U = \emptyset$ recovers the definition of $Y(\Gamma)$, so the content of Proposition~\ref{prop:switching_distance} lies entirely in changing the representative of the switching class. By Corollary~\ref{cor:R_switching} the set $\mathcal{R}$ is determined by that class up to coordinate-wise multiplication by a sign vector, so the choice of representative is immaterial.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:path-lower}
Let $\Gamma=(P_n,\sigma)$ be a signed path with segments $S_1,\ldots,S_r$, and let $m_1,\ldots,m_s$ be the orders of its negative segments. Set $\varepsilon=1$ if $S_1$ and $S_r$ are negative segments whose orders are congruent to $2$ modulo $3$, and $\varepsilon=0$ otherwise. Then
\[
  Y(\Gamma)\;\ge\; n-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\Big\lfloor \frac{m_i}{3}\Big\rfloor-\varepsilon .
\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:upper}
Let $\Gamma=(P_n,\sigma)$ be a signed path with segments $S_1,\ldots,S_r$, and let $m_1,\ldots,m_s$ be the orders of its negative segments. Then
\[
  Y(\Gamma)\le n-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\Big\lfloor\frac{m_i-1+e_i}{3}\Big\rfloor .
\]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:exact}
Let $\Gamma=(P_n,\sigma)$ be a signed path with segments $S_1,\ldots,S_r$, let $m_1,\ldots,m_s$ be the orders of its negative segments, and let $\varepsilon$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:path-lower}. Then 
\[
  Y(\Gamma)=n-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\Big\lfloor \frac{m_i}{3}\Big\rfloor-\varepsilon = n-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\Big\lfloor\frac{m_i-1+e_i}{3}\Big\rfloor
\]
if and only if either $\Gamma=\Gamma_-$, or both of the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}[{\upshape(i)}]
\item every negative segment other than $S_1$ and $S_r$ has order not divisible by $3$;
\item $\varepsilon=0$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, $Y(\Gamma_-) = \Big\lfloor \frac{2n+1}{3}\Big\rfloor$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Write $\mathrm{LB}$ and $\mathrm{UB}$ for the bounds in Theorems~\ref{thm:path-lower} and~\ref{thm:upper}, respectively. Since $\mathrm{LB}\le Y(\Gamma)\le\mathrm{UB}$, the two equalities in the statement hold exactly when $\mathrm{LB}=\mathrm{UB}$. Put $\delta_i=\lfloor m_i/3\rfloor-\lfloor (m_i-1+e_i)/3\rfloor$, so that
\[
  \mathrm{UB}-\mathrm{LB}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\delta_i+\varepsilon .
\]
Checking the residues of $m_i$ modulo $3$ gives
\[
  \delta_i=\begin{cases}
    1 & \text{if } e_i=0 \text{ and } m_i\equiv 0 \, (\bmod \, 3),\\
   -1 & \text{if } e_i=2 \text{ and } m_i\equiv 2 \, (\bmod \, 3),\\
    0 & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\]
If $s=0$, then $\Gamma=\Gamma_+$ and $\mathrm{LB}=\mathrm{UB}=n$, and (i) and (ii) hold trivially. If $s\ge 1$ and $r=1$, then $\Gamma=\Gamma_-$. The only negative segment has $m_1=n$ and $e_1=2$. Moreover $\varepsilon=1$ exactly when $n\equiv 2\, (\bmod \, 3)$, that is, exactly when $\delta_1=-1$. So $\mathrm{LB}=\mathrm{UB}$. If $s\geq 1$ and $r\ge 2$, then every $e_i$ is at most $1$. Then every $\delta_i$ is nonnegative, and so is $\varepsilon$. Hence $\mathrm{LB}=\mathrm{UB}$ if and only if every $\delta_i$ vanishes and $\varepsilon=0$. Since $e_i=0$ exactly for the negative segments other than $S_1$ and $S_r$, this is the conjunction of (i) and (ii). For $\Gamma=\Gamma_-$ we obtain $Y(\Gamma_-)=n-\lfloor (n+1)/3\rfloor$, which equals $\lfloor (2n+1)/3\rfloor$ in each of the cases $n\equiv 0,1,2\, (\bmod \, 3)$.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:minimum}
Let $\Gamma=(P_n,\sigma)$ be a signed path. Then
$Y(\Gamma)\;\ge\;Y(\Gamma_-)\;=\;\big\lfloor\frac{2n+1}{3}\big\rfloor$. If $n\equiv 2\, (\bmod \, 3)$, then equality holds if and only if $\Gamma=\Gamma_-$.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:exact} we have $Y(\Gamma_-)=n-\lfloor (n+1)/3\rfloor$, so by
Theorem~\ref{thm:path-lower} it suffices to show that
$\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lfloor m_i/3\rfloor+\varepsilon\le\lfloor (n+1)/3\rfloor$.
The negative segments are pairwise vertex disjoint, so $\sum_{i=1}^{s} m_i\le n$.
If $\varepsilon=0$, then $\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lfloor m_i/3\rfloor\le\lfloor\sum_{i=1}^{s} m_i/3\rfloor\le\lfloor n/3\rfloor$.
If $\varepsilon=1$ and $s=1$, then $\Gamma=\Gamma_-$ and the two sides are equal.
If $\varepsilon=1$ and $s\ge 2$, then $S_1$ and $S_r$ are distinct negative segments with
$m_1\equiv m_s\equiv 2\, (\bmod \, 3)$, whence
\[
  \sum_{i=1}^{s}\Big\lfloor\frac{m_i}{3}\Big\rfloor+\varepsilon
  \;\le\;\frac{m_1-2}{3}+\frac{m_s-2}{3}+\sum_{i\neq 1,s}\frac{m_i}{3}+1
  \;\le\;\frac{n-1}{3}.
\]
Since the left side is an integer, it is at most $\lfloor (n-1)/3\rfloor$, which proves the inequality. Now let $n\equiv 2\, (\bmod \, 3)$ and $\Gamma\neq\Gamma_-$. Then
$\lfloor n/3\rfloor=\lfloor (n-1)/3\rfloor=(n-2)/3=\lfloor (n+1)/3\rfloor-1$, so the first and
the third case give $\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lfloor m_i/3\rfloor+\varepsilon\le\lfloor (n+1)/3\rfloor-1$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:path-lower} now gives $Y(\Gamma)\ge Y(\Gamma_-)+1$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:delivery}
Let $w$ be an entry of $L$ and let $z$ be a neighbour of $w$. Then
\[
  c(w)\,\Phi_U(z)=
  \begin{cases}
    -1, &\parbox[t]{4.95in}{if $w$ is the closing entry of a negative segment and $z$ is its left neighbour,}\\
    \phantom{-}1, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, if $z$ is burned by spreading with the colour $c(w)$, then
$\nu(z)=-\Phi_U(z)$ in the first case and $\nu(z)=\Phi_U(z)$ in the second.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let $S=u_1\cdots u_m$ be the segment containing $w$. Write $c(w)=\theta\,\Phi_U(w)$, so that $\theta=-1$ exactly when $w$ is a running entry of a negative segment and $\theta=1$ otherwise, by \eqref{eq:cw}. Now
$
c(w)\,\Phi_U(z)=\theta\,\Phi_U(w)\Phi_U(z)=\theta\,\sigma(wz)
$. 

Suppose first that $w=u_j$ is a running entry. By the definition of $L(S)$ we have $2\le j\le m-1$, so both neighbours of $w$ lie in $S$ and $wz$ is an edge of $S$. Hence $\sigma(wz)=-1$ exactly when $S$ is negative, which is exactly when $\theta=-1$. In both cases $\theta\,\sigma(wz)=1$. 

Suppose now that $w=u_m$ is the closing entry, so that $\theta=1$ and $c(w)\Phi_U(z)=\sigma(wz)$. If $z$ is the right neighbour of $w$, then $z$ is the first vertex of the segment following $S$. So $wz$ joins two consecutive segments, and $\sigma(wz)=1$. Let $z$ be the left neighbour of $w$. If $m\ge 2$, then $z=u_{m-1}$ and $wz$ is an edge of $S$. So $\sigma(wz)=-1$ when $S$ is negative and $\sigma(wz)=1$ when $S$ is positive. If $m=1$, then $S$ is positive, since negative segments have order at least $2$. Then $z$ lies in the segment preceding $S$, so again $\sigma(wz)=1$. Thus $c(w)\Phi_U(z)=-1$ exactly in the stated case. 

The last assertion follows by using $\Phi_U(z)^2 = 1$.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Run $\mathcal{I}$ on $\Gamma_+$, and for $1\le k<\ell$ let $x_k$ be the right neighbour of
$w_k$, which exists because $w_k$ lies to the left of $w_{k+1}$. We prove by induction on
$k\le\ell-1$:

\smallskip
\noindent $(\mathrm{A}_k)$ $\beta(w_k)=k$ and $\beta(x_k)=k+1$. At the end of the spread step of time step $k+1$, every vertex to the left of $x_k$ is burned or held,
and no vertex to its right is burned. Finally, $\nu(z)=\Phi_U(z)$ for every burned vertex $z$ that is not a running entry of a negative segment.

\smallskip
\noindent Two amendments are needed though, both concerning $v_1$ and both affecting $(A_1)$ only. $(\mathrm{a})$ If $m_1\ge5$ and $m_1\equiv2\pmod 3$, that is, if $w_1$ is the third vertex of $S_1$, then $v_1$ is neither burned nor held at the end of the spread step of time step $2$, its burn time being $\beta(v_1)=3$. In this case $L(S_1)$ has a running and a closing entry, so $\ell\ge2$ and $v_1$ is burned by the end of the spread step of time step $\ell+1$. $(\mathrm{b})$ If $S_1$ is negative with $m_1\equiv2\pmod 3$, hence only if $\varepsilon=1$, then $\nu(v_1)=-\Phi_U(v_1)$.

Nothing is burned before the ignition step of time step~$1$. So $w_1$ is ignited there, $\beta(w_1)=1$ and $\nu(w_1)=c(w_1)$. At the spread step of time step $2$ the neighbours of $w_1$ are the only unburned vertices with a burned
neighbour, and each takes the colour $\nu(w_1)$. In particular, the right neighbour $x_1$ of $w_1$, if it exists, burns there with colour $\Phi_U(x_1)$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:delivery}. For $\ell\ge2$ this gives
$\beta(x_1)=2$ and $\nu(x_1)=\Phi_U(x_1)$. By (K3) we have $w_1=u_j$ in $S_1$ with $j\le3$.

Let $j=1$. Then $w_1=v_1$, and no vertex lies to its left. Let $j=2$. Then $\beta(v_1)=2$ and
$\nu(v_1)=\nu(w_1)$, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:delivery} equals $\Phi_U(v_1)$, unless $w_1$ is
the closing entry of a negative segment, that is unless $S_1$ is negative of order $2$. Let
$j=3$. Then $m_1\ge5$ and $m_1\equiv2\,(\bmod\,3)$ by (K3), so $\beta(u_2)=2$ and
$\nu(u_2)=\Phi_U(u_2)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:delivery}, while $v_1$ has no burned neighbour. Here
$L(S_1)$ has a running entry and a closing entry, so $\ell\ge2$. At the spread step of time
step $3$ the only burned neighbour of $v_1$ is $u_2$, whence $\beta(v_1)=3$ and
$\nu(v_1)=\nu(u_2)=\Phi_U(u_2)$. The edge $v_1u_2$ lies in $S_1$, so this is $\Phi_U(v_1)$ when
$S_1$ is positive and $-\Phi_U(v_1)$ when $S_1$ is negative.

In both cases calling for the two amendments, $S_1$ is negative with $m_1\equiv2\,(\bmod\,3)$, as
announced. Moreover $\nu(w_1)=\Phi_U(w_1)$ unless $w_1$ is a running entry of a negative
segment, by \eqref{eq:cw}. So $(\mathrm{A}_1)$ holds when $\ell\ge2$. When $\ell=1$, the same computation gives the following at the end of the spread step of time step $2$. Every vertex to the left of $w_1$ is burned or held, and so is the right neighbour of $w_1$, if it exists. No other vertex is burned.

Let $1\le k<\ell$ and assume $(\mathrm{A}_k)$. Put $v_p=x_k$, let $w=w_{k+1}$ and let
$S=u_1\cdots u_m$ be the segment containing $w$. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:position}
  w=v_{p+\delta} \text{ with } 1\le\delta\le 3, \text{ and } \delta=3 \text{ only if } w=u_3
  \text{ and } v_p \text{ is the vertex preceding } u_1 .
\end{equation}
Suppose first that $w$ is not the first entry of $L(S)$. Then its predecessor $w_k$ lies in
$S$. By (K2) it is three places to the left of $w$, or two when $w$ is the closing entry. As
$v_p$ is the right neighbour of $w_k$, we get $\delta=2$ or $\delta=1$.

Suppose now that $w$ is the first entry of $L(S)$. Then $S\ne S_1$ by (K1), so $S'$ exists. We
claim that $v_p$ is $u_1$ or the vertex preceding $u_1$. Let $L(S')$ be nonempty. Its last entry is $w_k$, which by (K4) is the last vertex of $S'$, or the second to last one when $3$ divides $|S'|$. So $v_p$ is $u_1$ in the first case and the last vertex of $S'$ in the second. Let $L(S')$ be empty. Then $S'$ has order $1$ and is positive, and $S'\neq S_1$ by (K1). So the segment preceding $S'$ exists and is negative, hence of order at least $2$, and its order is not divisible by $3$ by the definition of $L(S')$. By (K4) its list ends at its last vertex, that vertex is $w_k$, and $v_p$ is the vertex of $S'$. This proves the claim. By (K3) we have $w=u_j$ with $j\le3$. Hence $\delta=j-1$ when $v_p=u_1$, and $\delta=j$ when $v_p$ precedes $u_1$. It remains to show $\delta\neq 0$. By the above, $\delta=0$ would require $v_p=u_1$ and $j=1$. Suppose $j=1$. By (K3), $m=1$. Since $S\neq S_1$ and $L(S)$ is nonempty, the definition of $L(S)$ gives $|S'|\equiv 0 \pmod 3$. Thus $|S'|\ge 3$, so $L(S')$ is nonempty and its last entry is $w_k$. By
(K4), $w_k$ is the second to last vertex of $S'$. Hence $v_p$ is the last vertex of $S'$, which precedes $u_1$. So $v_p\neq u_1$, and $\delta\ge 1$. This proves \eqref{eqn:position}.

By $(\mathrm{A}_k)$ no vertex to the right of $v_p$ is burned at the end of the spread step of time step $k+1$. So $w$ is ignited at time step $k+1$, $\beta(w)=k+1$ and $\nu(w)=c(w)$. Moreover $\nu(w)=\Phi_U(w)$ unless $w$ is a running entry of a negative segment, by \eqref{eq:cw}. Consider the spread step of time step $k+2$. Three kinds of unburned vertex have a burned neighbour there. The held ones stay unburned. The $\delta-1\le2$ vertices between $v_p$ and
$w$ are treated below. The right neighbour $z$ of $w$, if it exists, has $w$ as its only burned neighbour, so it is burned at time step $k+2$ with $\nu(z)=\Phi_U(z)$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:delivery}.

Let $\delta=1$. Then no vertex lies between $v_p$ and $w$. Let $\delta=3$. Then $w=u_3$ and
$v_p$ precedes $u_1$ by \eqref{eqn:position}. So the edge $v_pu_1$ joins two consecutive
segments, and $\Phi_U(u_1)=\Phi_U(v_p)$. At the start of this spread step $u_1$ has $v_p$ as
its only burned neighbour, and $u_2$ has $w$ as its only burned neighbour. Hence
$\nu(u_1)=\nu(v_p)=\Phi_U(u_1)$, and $\nu(u_2)=\nu(w)=\Phi_U(u_2)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:delivery}, $w$ being a running entry.

Let finally $\delta=2$. Then $v_{p+1}$ lies between the burned vertices $v_p$ and $w$, which
deliver the colours $\Phi_U(v_p)$ and $\nu(w)$. If the two deliveries are opposite, then
$v_{p+1}$ is held and there is nothing to prove. If they agree, then $v_{p+1}$ is burned with
the colour $\nu(w)$, and we must check that $\nu(w)=\Phi_U(v_{p+1})$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:delivery} this holds unless $w$ is the closing entry of a negative segment $S$
and $v_{p+1}$ is its left neighbour. Assume that it is. Then $w$ is the first entry of $L(S)$. Otherwise, by (K2), the closing entry $w$ lies two places after its predecessor, which gives $\delta=1$. So $L(S)$ has no running entry, whence $m\le2$ by (K1), and $m=2$ because $S$ is negative. Thus $w=u_2$ and $v_{p+1}=u_1$. Also $v_p$ precedes $u_1$, because $\delta=2$, so the edge $v_pu_1$ joins two consecutive segments and $\Phi_U(v_p)=\Phi_U(u_1)$. On the other
hand $\nu(w)=\Phi_U(u_2)$ by \eqref{eq:cw}, and $\Phi_U(u_2)=-\Phi_U(u_1)$ because
the edge $u_1u_2$ lies in the negative segment $S$. The two deliveries are therefore opposite,
and this case does not arise.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:wave}
Let $v$ be a vertex of $P_n$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(W1)}] If $v$ is not ignited, then $\beta(v)=g(v)+1$, and every neighbour $u$ of $v$ with $\beta(u)=g(v)$ has $\nu(u)=\nu(v)$.
\item[\textup{(W2)}] If $v$ is free, then $\beta(v)\le g(v)$.
\item[\textup{(W3)}] Distinct ignited vertices have distinct values of $\beta$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Put $g=g(v)$ and suppose that $\beta(v)\geq g+1$. Then $v$ is unburned before the spread step of time $g+1$, and its burned neighbours at that moment are the neighbours $u$ with $\beta(u)=g$. There is at least one such neighbour. If all of them have the same colour $c$, then $v$ burns by spreading at time $g+1$ with colour $c$. In that case $v$ is not ignited, because the ignition step only chooses unburned vertices. Otherwise $v$ has two neighbours $u,u'$ with $\beta(u)=\beta(u')=g$ and $\nu(u)\neq\nu(u')$. These are all the neighbours of $v$, and they remain burned, so their influences cancel at time $g+1$ and afterwards; and $v$ never burns by spreading. Hence $v$ is ignited, and it is held.

(W1) A vertex burned by spreading at time $t$ has a burned neighbour at the start of time $t$, so $\beta(v)\geq g+1$ when $v$ is not ignited. The first alternative above then occurs, and it gives both assertions.

(W2) If $v$ were ignited with $\beta(v)\geq g+1$, the first alternative would be impossible, so $v$ would be held. Hence a free vertex satisfies $\beta(v)\le g$.

(W3) At most one vertex is ignited per time step.

\label{lem:short}
\begin{enumerate}[{\upshape(i)}]
\item If $\{x\}$ is a singleton block, then $x$ is free, and $\beta(x)\le\beta(u)$ for every neighbour $u$ of $x$.
\item Let $B$ be a block of order $3$ with middle $w$, put $M=\beta(w)$, let $u$ be an end of

$u$ is held if and only if $u$ is ignited with $\beta(u)\geq M+1$, and in that case $\beta(u')=M$.
If $u$ is not held, then $\beta(u)\le\beta(u')$; moreover $\beta(u)=M+1$ if $u$ is not ignited, and $\beta(u)\le M$ if $u$ is free. In particular, $\beta(u)\geq M+1$ if and only if $u$ is not free.
\item With $B$, $w$ and $M$ as in (ii), $w$ is never held, and $w$ is ignited if and only if $\beta(u)\ge M$
for both ends $u$ of $B$.
\item Let $u$ and $u'$ be adjacent vertices in different blocks. Suppose that each of $u$ and $u'$ is a singleton block or an end of a block of order 3. If neither $u$ nor $u'$ is held, then $\beta(u)=\beta(u')$. In particular, no two singleton blocks are adjacent.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:chain}
Let $\{x\}$ and $\{y\}$ be singleton blocks, with $x$ to the left of $y$ and no singleton block between them. Then $x$ and $y$ are not adjacent, and some block between $x$ and $y$ has order $2$ or at least $4$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:short}(iv), $x$ and $y$ are not adjacent. So the vertices strictly between them form blocks $B_1,\ldots,B_k$, listed from left to right, where $k\ge1$ and each $B_i$ has order more than 1. Suppose, for a contradiction, that all of them have order $3$, and write $B_i=\{a_i,b_i,c_i\}$ from left to right. Put $c_0=x$, $a_{k+1}=y$ and $M_i=\beta(b_i)$ for $1\le i\le k$. For $0\le i\le k$ the vertices $c_i$ and $a_{i+1}$ are adjacent and lie in different blocks, and each of them is a singleton block or an end of a block of order $3$. So Lemma~\ref{lem:short}~(ii) and (iv) apply to the pair $c_i,a_{i+1}$.

Let $X_i=\{c_0\}\cup B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_i$, and let $T_i$ be the set of values $\beta(z)$ over the ignited vertices $z\in X_i$. By (W3),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain_distinct}
  \text{if } z\notin X_i \text{ is ignited, then } \beta(z)\notin T_i .
\end{equation}
Moreover, for $1\le i\le k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain_middle}
  \text{if neither } a_i \text{ nor } c_i \text{ is free, then } b_i \text{ is ignited, and hence } M_i\in T_i .
\end{equation}
Indeed, the last assertion of Lemma~\ref{lem:short}~(ii) gives $\beta(a_i)\geq M_i+1$ and $\beta(c_i)\geq M_i+1$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:short}~(iii) then shows that $b_i$ is ignited.

We prove by induction on $i\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}$ the two statements
\[
  (\mathrm{A}_i)\ \ a_i \text{ is not free, when } i\ge 1;
  \qquad\qquad
  (\mathrm{B}_i)\ \ \beta(c_i)\in T_i .
\]
Statement $(\mathrm{A}_0)$ is empty, and $(\mathrm{B}_0)$ holds because $c_0=x$ is ignited by Lemma~\ref{lem:short}(i).

Assume $(\mathrm{A}_i)$ and $(\mathrm{B}_i)$ for some $0\le i\le k$. We first show
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain_next}
  \text{if } a_{i+1} \text{ is not held, then } \beta(a_{i+1})\in T_i .
\end{equation}
If $c_i$ is not held, Lemma~\ref{lem:short}(iv) gives $\beta(a_{i+1})=\beta(c_i)$, which lies in $T_i$ by $(\mathrm{B}_i)$. If $c_i$ is held, then $i\ge1$, since $c_0$ is free. Lemma~\ref{lem:short}(ii) gives $\beta(a_{i+1})=M_i$. As $c_i$ is held and $a_i$ is not free by $(\mathrm{A}_i)$, \eqref{eq:chain_middle} gives $M_i\in T_i$.

Next, $a_{i+1}$ is not free. Otherwise $a_{i+1}$ is ignited and not held, so $\beta(a_{i+1})\in T_i$ by \eqref{eq:chain_next}. Since $a_{i+1}\notin X_i$, this contradicts \eqref{eq:chain_distinct}. When $i<k$ this is $(\mathrm{A}_{i+1})$.

Let $i<k$. It remains to prove $(\mathrm{B}_{i+1})$. If $c_{i+1}$ is ignited, then $\beta(c_{i+1})\in T_{i+1}$ by definition. So assume $c_{i+1}$ is not ignited. Then $c_{i+1}$ is not free, and \eqref{eq:chain_middle} together with $(\mathrm{A}_{i+1})$ shows that $b_{i+1}$ is ignited, with $\beta(b_{i+1})=M_{i+1}$. If $a_{i+1}$ were held, Lemma~\ref{lem:short}(ii) would give $\beta(c_i)=M_{i+1}=\beta(b_{i+1})$. This is impossible by \eqref{eq:chain_distinct}, since $\beta(c_i)\in T_i$ and $b_{i+1}\notin X_i$. So $a_{i+1}$ is not held, and since it is not free, it is not ignited. Lemma~\ref{lem:short}(ii), applied to $a_{i+1}$ and to $c_{i+1}$, gives $\beta(a_{i+1})=M_{i+1}+1=\beta(c_{i+1})$. By \eqref{eq:chain_next}, $\beta(c_{i+1})=\beta(a_{i+1})\in T_i\subseteq T_{i+1}$. This completes the induction.

Now $(\mathrm{A}_k)$ and $(\mathrm{B}_k)$ hold, so the argument above with $i=k$ shows that $a_{k+1}=y$ is not free. This contradicts Lemma~\ref{lem:short}(i).

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:DSGBwD_hardness}
Unless $\textsf{P} = \textsf{NP}$, there is no polynomial-time $(1 - \frac{1}{e} + \varepsilon)$-approximation algorithm for computing $Y_k(\Gamma)$ for any $\varepsilon > 0$, given a directed signed graph $\Gamma$ and integer $k$ as input.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
We reduce from the Maximum Coverage problem. An instance consists of a set system $(U, \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace)$ and an integer $k$, where $U = \{e_1, e_2, \ldots, e_n \}$ is the ground set and $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace = \{S_1, S_2, \ldots, S_m \}$ is a family of non-empty subsets of $U$. The task is to select a subfamily $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace^* \subseteq \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ with $\ensuremath{\left|S^*\right|} = k$ maximizing $\ensuremath{\left|\bigcup_{S \in \mathcal{S^*}} S\right|}$. Let $\textsf{OPT}_\textsf{MC}$ denote the maximum. We may assume that $k < m$, otherwise $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace^* = \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$. Given such an instance, we construct the directed signed graph $\Gamma = (D, \sigma)$. Label the sets in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ with indices from $[m]$ and let $\ell = (k + 1)^2$. Consider a bipartite directed graph $D = (V, A)$ with $V = V_\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace \sqcup V_{U}$, where $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace} = \{a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_m\}$ has one vertex per set $S_i \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ and $V_U = V_U^1 \sqcup V_U^2 \sqcup \ldots \sqcup V_U^\ell$, where for each $t \in [\ell]$, $V_{U}^t = \{b_1^t, b_2^t, \ldots, b_n^t\}$ contains one vertex per element $e_j \in U$. For every $t \in [\ell]$, we add a directed arc $(a_i, b^t_j)$ to $A$ whenever $e_j \in S_i$ and set $\sigma_{(a_i, b^t_j)} = +1$. Finally, set the deadline to $k + 1$. First, we show the following.
\begin{claim} 
$Y_{k + 1}(\Gamma) = \ell \cdot \textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{MC}} + (k + 1) = \ell \left(\textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{MC}} + \frac{1}{k + 1}\right)$ 
\end{claim}
    
\begin{proof}[Proof of Claim]
\renewcommand{$\Box$\ \emph{(Claim.)}}{$\Box$\ \emph{(Claim.)}}

We distinguish between two cases:
    
\textbf{Case 1:} Assume that $\textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{MC}} < n$. Clearly $Y_{k + 1}(\Gamma) \geq  \ell \cdot \textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{MC}} + (k + 1)$ since we can ignite $k$ vertices from $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ corresponding to the optimum of the Maximum Coverage problem for the first $k$ rounds and burn some arbitrary vertex in the last round (note that the fire ignited in round $T = k + 1$ does not spread), resulting in exactly  $\ell \cdot \textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{MC}} + (k + 1)$  yellow vertices at the end of round $k + 1$. Now assume that $Y_{k + 1}(\Gamma) >  \ell \cdot \textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{MC}} + (k + 1)$. If all of the vertices burned by the optimum in the first $k$ rounds are from the set $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$, then by selecting the corresponding sets from $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$, we cover more than $\textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{MC}}$ elements from $U$, which is a contradiction (this is possible because we assumed $\textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{MC}} < n$). Consider a scenario in which an unburned $b_j^t$ is ignited. If the vertex has an in-degree of at least $1$, then burning any $a_i$ with an arc $(a_i, b^t_j)$ strictly increases the number of vertices burned yellow (if $b^t_j$ is unburned, no other $b^{t'}_j$ could be burned in $D$ either, and $\ell > 1$). If the in-degree of $b_j^t$ is $0$, burning any arbitrary $a_i$ strictly improves the number of yellows since we assumed $S \neq \varnothing$ for all $S \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$, so $a_i$ has an out-degree of at least $\ell$ (note that this is possible since we assumed $k < m$). In any case, we get a contradiction.

\textbf{Case 2:} Next, let $\textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{MC}} = n$, and for the sake of contradiction, assume that $\textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{SGM}} > \ell \cdot n + k + 1$. Note that igniting the vertices corresponding to the sets in the optimum solution of the Maximum Coverage problem in the first $k$ rounds and igniting any arbitrary vertex from the set $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ in round $k + 1$ will burn exactly the $\ell \cdot n + k + 1$ vertices yellow (i.e., the entire $V^t_U$ sets for the $t \in [\ell]$ and $k + 1$ vertices of $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$). If the optimum burns the entirety of $V_{U}^1, V_{U}^2, \ldots, V_{U}^\ell$, then the maximum number of vertices that could burn yellow is clearly $\ell \cdot n + k + 1$. This implies that in the first $k$ rounds, the optimum ignites a set of $k' \leq k$ vertices of $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ that are distinct from the $k$ vertices corresponding to any optimum of the Maximum Coverage problem. Assume that these $k'$ sets cover at most $n' \le n - 1$ elements from $U$ in the Maximum Coverage problem. Therefore, the number of vertices burned yellow by the corresponding $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ vertices is at most $k' + \ell \cdot n'$. By doing this, the optimum can ignite $k + 1 - k' \leq k + 1$ many vertices from outside $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$, making the total number of yellow vertices $k' + \ell \cdot n' + k + 1 \leq 2k + 1 + \ell \cdot (n - 1) = 2k + 1 + \ell \cdot n -  (k + 1)^2 < \ell \cdot n + k + 1$ for $k >= 1$. This is a contradiction to its optimality.
\end{proof}
    
Feige \cite{Feige98} showed that for any $\varepsilon > 0$, the Maximum Coverage problem cannot be approximated in polynomial time within a ratio of $(1 - 1/e + \varepsilon)$ unless $\textsf{P} = \textsf{NP}$. To show the hardness of approximation, we look further into Feige's hardness proof, where he provides a gap-producing reduction from the 3\textsf{SAT}-5\footnote{An instance of the the 3\textsf{SAT}-5 problem consists of a set of literals $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ and a set of $3$-\textsf{CNF} clauses $C_1, C_2, \ldots, C_m$ with the promise that no literal appears, positively or negatively, more than $5$ times in the clauses. The task is to decide whether a satisfying assignment of boolean values to literals exists.} problem to Maximum Coverage as follows: If the instance of the 3\textsf{SAT}-5 problem is satisfiable, then the instance of the Maximum Coverage problem has a solution that covers the entire set $U$. If the 3\textsf{SAT}-5 instance is not satisfiable, then any choice of $k$ sets from $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ would cover at most a $1 - 1/e + \ensuremath{o\left(1\right)}$ fraction of the elements of $U$. Following the chain of reduction, if the 3\textsf{SAT}-5 instance is satisfiable, we get an instance of the Directed Signed Graph Burning problem where $Y_{k + 1}(\Gamma) = \ell \cdot n + k + 1 = \ell \cdot \left(n + \frac{1}{k + 1}\right) = \ell \cdot (n + \ensuremath{o\left(1\right)})$. Otherwise, $Y_{k + 1}(\Gamma) \leq \ell \left(\textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{MC}} + \frac{1}{k + 1}\right) = \ell \left((1 - 1/e + \ensuremath{o\left(1\right)}) \cdot n + \frac{1}{k + 1}\right) = \ell (1 - 1/e + \ensuremath{o\left(1\right)}) \cdot n$ (note that $\frac{1}{k + 1} = \ensuremath{o\left(1\right)}$ since $k$ tends to infinity). Therefore, any algorithm that approximates $Y_{k + 1}(\Gamma)$ within a factor of $(1 - 1/e + \varepsilon)$ for any $\varepsilon > 0$ could decide whether the 3\textsf{SAT}-5 instance is satisfiable or not, which is not possible unless $\textsf{P} = \textsf{NP}$.

\textbf{Case 2:} Next, let $\textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{MC}} = n$, and for the sake of contradiction, assume that $\textsf{OPT}_{\textsf{SGM}} > \ell \cdot n + k + 1$. Note that igniting the vertices corresponding to the sets in the optimum solution of the Maximum Coverage problem in the first $k$ rounds and igniting any arbitrary vertex from the set $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ in round $k + 1$ will burn exactly the $\ell \cdot n + k + 1$ vertices yellow (i.e., the entire $V^t_U$ sets for the $t \in [\ell]$ and $k + 1$ vertices of $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$). If the optimum burns the entirety of $V_{U}^1, V_{U}^2, \ldots, V_{U}^\ell$, then the maximum number of vertices that could burn yellow is clearly $\ell \cdot n + k + 1$. This implies that in the first $k$ rounds, the optimum ignites a set of $k' \leq k$ vertices of $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ that are distinct from the $k$ vertices corresponding to any optimum of the Maximum Coverage problem. Assume that these $k'$ sets cover at most $n' \le n - 1$ elements from $U$ in the Maximum Coverage problem. Therefore, the number of vertices burned yellow by the corresponding $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ vertices is at most $k' + \ell \cdot n'$. By doing this, the optimum can ignite $k + 1 - k' \leq k + 1$ many vertices from outside $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$, making the total number of yellow vertices $k' + \ell \cdot n' + k + 1 \leq 2k + 1 + \ell \cdot (n - 1) = 2k + 1 + \ell \cdot n -  (k + 1)^2 < \ell \cdot n + k + 1$ for $k >= 1$. This is a contradiction to its optimality.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:USGBwD_hardness}
    Unless $\textsf{P} = \textsf{NP}$, there is no polynomial-time exact algorithm for computing $Y_k(\Gamma)$, given a undirected signed graph $\Gamma$ and integer $k$ as input.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
The result follows in a straightforward manner from the hardness  of the standard (unsigned) graph burning. In the decision version of the standard unsigned graph burning, we are given an undirected graph $G$ on $n$ vertices and an integer $k$. The task is to decide if a sequence of at most $k$ ignitions exist that can burn the entire vertex set by round $k$, which is an \textsf{NP}-complete problem \cite{BessyBJRR17}.

One can reduce any arbitrary instance of the standard unsigned graph burning problem given as graph $G$ to an instance of computing $Y_k(\Gamma)$ on an undirected signed graph $\Gamma = (G, \sigma)$ with a deadline by letting $\sigma(e) = +1$ for all $e \in E(G)$ and setting the deadline to $k$. Clearly, $Y_k(\Gamma) = n$ if and only if an ignition sequence of size at most $k$ in the original unsigned graph can burn the entire vertex set.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:DSGBwoD_hardness}
    Unless $\textsf{P} = \textsf{NP}$, there is no polynomial-time exact algorithm for computing $Y(\Gamma)$, given a directed signed graph $\Gamma$ as input.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
We reduce from the Set Cover problem. In an instance of the Set Cover problem, we are given a set $U$ of $n$ elements, a family $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ of $m$ subsets of $U$, and a parameter $k$. The task is to decide whether there exists a set $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace'$ of exactly\footnote{The standard Set Cover definition only requires $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace'$ to be of size at most $k$, but it is well understood that it can be reduced to the case with cover size of exactly $k$ by adding new elements and singleton sets containing those elements.} $k$ sets in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ that collectively cover $U$, i.e., $\bigcup_{s \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace'} s = U$. Such an $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace'$ is said to ``cover'' $U$. Without loss of generality, we can assume that no set in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ contains another set. Furthermore, we can assume that every set in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ has a cardinality of at least $2$ since all the empty sets could be removed and singleton sets must be added to the solution. We can also assume that $U$ has no isolated (not in any $S \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$) elements.

We generate a directed signed graph $\Gamma$ with three main parts. The first is a bipartite digraph $H = (V, E)$ representing the set system with $V = V_U \sqcup V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$, where $V_U$ contains one vertex per element in $U$ and $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ contains one vertex per set in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$. The edges are directed from $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ to $V_U$. There is a positive edge from $s \in V_\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ to $u \in V_U$ if and only if $u \in s$. We let $v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_N$ collectively denote the nodes in $H$ with $N = m + n$. We add two gadgets to $H$ in this reduction to complete $\Gamma$. These gadgets are depicted in Figure \ref{fig:timer}. 
    
\emph{i) The timer gadget $T$:} It is depicted on the left side in Figure \ref{fig:timer}, consisting of a root node $r$ and directed paths $p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_N$, each of length $k - 1$ (i.e., $k$ vertices in each). For each $i \in [N]$, let $s_i$ and $t_i$ denote the first and last vertices of $p_i$, respectively. $r$ is connected to each $s_i$ via a positive edge and every edge of the path is also a positive edge. Each $t_i$ is connected to $v_i$ via a negative edge. Let $v_u \in V_U$ correspond to an element $u \in U$. We say a vertex $v_{u}$ is \emph{marked for coverage} in a given round if, in the previous round, a vertex $v_S \in V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ was ignited yellow, where $S \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ contains $u$. The function of the timer gadget is to ensure that every vertex of $V_U$ that has not been marked for coverage by round $k + 2$ turns green at the end of it. As a result, the timer gadget simulates a deadline for any burning sequence to burn vertices yellow by round $k + 2$. Note that the vertices in $V_U$ t hat are marked for coverage at time $k + 2$ remain unburned because of a tie in the influence they receive; a yellow influence from the $V_S$ that has marked them for coverage and a green influence from a $t_i$. The optimal burning sequence will then proceed to burn them yellow one by one, so they will eventually contribute to $Y(\Gamma)$.
    
\emph{ii) The bait gadget $B$:} It is shown on the right side in Figure \ref{fig:timer}, consisting of a large number $M$ of vertices (consider $M \geq N^3$). Every vertex $w \in (V(T) - \{r\}) \cup V(H) \cup V(B)$ is connected to $M/2$ arbitrarily chosen vertices of $B$ via positive edges and to the other $M/2$ in $B$ via negative edges. The root node $r$ is connected to all bait vertices via positive edges. The function of the bait is to force any optimal burning sequence to ignite $r$ yellow in the first round and set off a timer. In the following, we show that any other strategy would yield a much smaller objective value.

First, note that if $r$ is ignited yellow in the first round, one has all $M$  vertices of $B$ burned yellow by the second round. However, any strategy that ignites a vertex other than $r$ in the first round, regardless of its colour, will burn around $M/2$ vertices of $B$ green in the second round. When $M$ is large enough to satisfy 
$$M \geq  M/2 + \ensuremath{\left|T\right|} + N \Rightarrow M/2 \geq N (k + 1) + 1,$$
the optimal strategy will burn $r$ yellow in the first round (a loss of $M/2$ in objective value cannot be compensated by future ignitions). $M \geq N^3$ certainly satisfies this condition.
    
Next, we show that if we can compute $Y_k(\Gamma)$ exactly in polynomial time, then we can also decide any given instance of the Set Cover problem in polynomial time.

\begin{claim}
$Y_k(\Gamma) \geq M + N \cdot k + n + k + 1$ if and only if the set cover instance is a $\textsf{Yes}$ case, that is, there exists a subset $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace'$ of size $k$ for which $\bigcup_{s \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace'} s = U$.
\end{claim}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Claim]
\renewcommand{$\Box$\ \emph{(Claim.)}}{$\Box$\ \emph{(Claim.)}}
We distinguish between the $\textsf{Yes}$ and $\textsf{No}$ instances of the Set Cover problem:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\textsf{Yes}$ Case.} Assume there exists $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace' \subset \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ of size $k$ for which $\bigcup_{s \in \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace'} s = U$. Consider the strategy where we ignite $r$ yellow in the first round and ignite $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ vertices from $V(H)$ that correspond to the sets in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace'$ yellow in rounds $2, 3, \ldots, k + 1$. In round $k + 2$, a green fire burns every remaining vertex from $V_\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ as well as those $V_U$ vertices that have not been marked for coverage so far. The number of yellow vertices at the end of this strategy is at least $1 + N \cdot k$ (from $T$) plus $M$ (from $B$) plus $k$ (from $V_\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$-vertices in $H$ ignited yellow) plus $n$ (all of the $V_U$) since the sets in $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace'$ will cover the entirety of $U$, for a total of $M + N \cdot k + n + k + 1$.

\item \textbf{$\textsf{No}$ Case.} Assume that no $\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace' \subset \ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$ of size $k$ can cover the set  $U$. Consider an optimal sequence that burns the maximum number of nodes yellow. If multiple such optimal sequences exist, consider one with the least number of vertices ignited  yellow from $V_U$. This sequence ignites $r$ yellow in the first round, as discussed earlier. We argue that in this sequence, at most $k$ vertices from $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ get to spread their fire before the process ends. To see why, observe that the paths of length $k - 1$ will spread the fire to all vertices of $H$ (except those marked for coverage in previous rounds) by round $k + 2$. Therefore, even if all the nodes after $r$ in the sequence are chosen from $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$, at most $k$ of them get to pass the fire along to $V_U$. Note that no $V_U$ vertex is ignited yellow. The reason is that if a vertex $u \in V_U$ is ignited yellow in the optimum sequence, by optimality we have no vertex $v \in V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ with an edge to $u$ (such a $v$ exists because we assumed no isolated elements) ignited yellow so far. Replacing $u$ in the sequence with $v$ does not decrease the number of yellow vertices, but it decreases the number of $V_U$ vertices in the sequence ignited yellow, which is a contradiction to the choice of the optimal sequence. Next, note that since we have a $\textsf{No}$ instance of the Set Cover problem, the set of $V_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace}$ vertices ignited yellow can burn at most $n - 1$ vertices from $V_U$ yellow. Even assuming that all the path vertices are burned yellow, at most $M + N \cdot k + n + k$ vertices ($M$ from $B$, $N \cdot k + 1$ from paths and $r$, $n - 1$ from $V_U$, and $k$ from $V_\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}\xspace$) are burned yellow. Note that in this analysis, we do take the vertices of $V_U$ that are marked for coverage in the last round into account by assuming that the optimal sequence burns them all yellow one by one after round $k + 2$. Altogether, it would be at least one less than what is achievable in the $\textsf{Yes}$ case. \qedhere
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}
The gap of at least $1$ in the objective value of the $\textsf{Yes}$ and $\textsf{No}$ cases means that if we can exactly compute $Y_k(\Gamma)$ for any given $\Gamma$ and $k$ in polynomial time, we could also correctly decide the Set Cover problem in polynomial time, which is not possible unless $\textsf{P} = \textsf{NP}$.

\label{thm1:USGBwoD_hardness}
Given an undirected signed graph $\Gamma$ and an integer $\tau$, it is $\textsf{NP}$-complete to
decide whether $Y(\Gamma)\ge\tau$. In particular, unless $\textsf{P}=\textsf{NP}$, there is no
polynomial-time exact algorithm for computing $Y(\Gamma)$, given an undirected signed graph

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:burning_cover}
Let $G$ be a graph and let $m\ge1$. Then $b(G)\le m$ if and only if there are vertices
$y_1,\ldots,y_m$ of $G$ with $V(G)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}N^{m-i}_G[y_i]$, where
$N^{\rho}_G[y]=\{u\in V(G):d_G(y,u)\le\rho\}$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
By definition of graph burning.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:bait}
Let $\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)$ and suppose that $\mathcal{I}$ does not ignite $r$ in yellow
at time $1$. Then $\ensuremath{\left|\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I}}\right|}\le\tau-1$.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
At the end of time $1$ the vertex $z_1$ is the only burned vertex, and we write $c\in\{\pm1\}$ for
its colour. Suppose $z_1\in W$. At the spread step of time $2$ every $b\in B$ has $z_1$ as its only
burned neighbour, so $b$ is burned with the colour $\sigma(bz_1)c$, and by \eqref{eqn:bait_rows}
exactly $M/2=P+1$ vertices of $B$ are burned green. Suppose $z_1\in B$. At the spread step of time
$2$ every $x\in W$ has $z_1$ as its only burned neighbour, so $x$ is burned with the colour
$\sigma(xz_1)c$, and by \eqref{eqn:bait_columns} at least $P+1$ vertices of $W$ are burned green.
Suppose finally that $z_1=r$ and $c=-1$. Then all $M\ge P+1$ vertices of $B$ are burned green at
the spread step of time $2$. Burned vertices never change their colour, so in each case
$\ensuremath{\left|\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I}}\right|}\le\ensuremath{\left|V(\Gamma)\right|}-(P+1)=\tau-1$.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:after_r}
Let $\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)$ ignite $r$ in yellow at time $1$ and write
$\nu_t=\nu_{\mathcal{I},t}$. At the end of the spread step of time $2$ the burned vertices are
exactly $r$, the vertices of $B$, and the vertices $p^{v,i}_1$ with $v\in V(G)$ and $i\in[w]$, and
all of them are yellow. Moreover $z_s\in W$ for every $s\ge2$, and
$\sum_{b\in B}\sigma(xb)\nu_{t-1}(b)=0$ for every $x\in W$ and every $t\ge3$.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
At the spread step of time $2$ the vertex $r$ is the only burned vertex. Its neighbours are the
vertices of $B$ and the vertices $p^{v,i}_1$, and all the corresponding edges are positive. So each
of them has $h_2=1$ and is burned yellow, while every other vertex has $h_2=0$ and stays unburned.
The ignition step only chooses unburned vertices, and $r$ is burned at time $1$ while every vertex
of $B$ is burned at the spread step of time $2$. Since $V(\Gamma)=W\sqcup B\sqcup\{r\}$, this gives
$z_s\in W$ for every $s\ge2$. For $t\ge3$ every vertex of $B$ is burned yellow at the end of time
$t-1$, so the sum in question equals $\sum_{b\in B}\sigma(xb)$, which vanishes by
\eqref{eqn:bait_rows}.

\label{lem:witness}
Let $\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)$ ignite $r$ in yellow at time $1$ and let $u\notin B$ be a
burned vertex. Then there is a time $s$ with $d'(z_s,u)\le\beta(u)-s$.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:after_r} every ignited vertex lies in $W\cup\{r\}$.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{lem:distance}
Define $\pi\colon W\to V(G)$ by $\pi(x)=x$ for $x\in V(G)$, and $\pi(x)=v$ if $x\in Q(v)$ or $x$ is
an internal vertex of a timer path of $v$. Then $d'(r,u)=k$ for every $u\in V(G)$. Moreover, if
$x\in W$ and $u\in V(G)$ satisfy $d'(x,u)\le k$, then $d_G(\pi(x),u)\le d'(x,u)$.
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
In $\Gamma'$ the neighbours of $r$ are the vertices $p^{v,i}_1$, and the neighbours of an internal
vertex of a timer path lie on that path. So a walk in $\Gamma'$ from $r$ to a vertex of $G$
traverses a whole timer path and has length at least $k$, with equality along a timer path of $u$.
Hence $d'(r,u)=k$. Deleting $r$ from $\Gamma'$ leaves $G$ together with the paths
$p^{v,i}_1\cdots p^{v,i}_{k-1}$ and the pendants, each attached to $G$ at the single vertex $v$ with
$\pi$-value $v$. So a walk from $x$ to $u$ avoiding $r$ passes through $\pi(x)$ and has length at
least $d_G(\pi(x),u)$, while a walk from $x$ to $u$ through $r$ has length at least
$1+d'(r,u)=k+1$. Thus $d'(x,u)\le k$ forces a shortest $x,u$ walk to avoid $r$, and the claim
follows.

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:timer_wave}
Let $\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)$ ignite $r$ in yellow at time $1$, let $v\in V(G)$, let
$i\in[w]$ and write $p_j=p^{v,i}_j$. Let $1\le t\le k$ and suppose that $v$ is unburned at the end
of time $t-1$ and that no $p_j$ is ignited at a time in $\{2,\ldots,t\}$. Then at the end of time
$t$ the vertices $p_1,\ldots,p_{t-1}$ are burned yellow and the vertices $p_t,\ldots,p_{k-1}$ are
unburned.
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
Put $p_0=r$ and $p_k=v$, so that the neighbours of $p_j$ outside $B$ are $p_{j-1}$ and $p_{j+1}$
for $1\le j\le k-1$. We induct on $t$. For $t=1$ nothing but $r$ is burned at the end of time $1$,
which is the assertion. Let $2\le t\le k$. The induction hypothesis applies at $t-1$ and gives that
at the end of time $t-1$ the vertices $p_1,\ldots,p_{t-2}$ are burned yellow and
$p_{t-1},\ldots,p_{k-1}$ are unburned. Consider the spread step of time $t$. The vertices of $B$
are unburned at the end of time $1$, and they contribute $0$ to $h_t$ for $t\ge3$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:after_r}. So $h_t(p_j)$ is determined by $p_{j-1}$ and $p_{j+1}$. For $j=t-1$ the
vertex $p_{t-2}$ is yellow and joined to $p_{t-1}$ by a positive edge, while $p_t$ is unburned,
where for $t-1=k-1$ we use that $v$ is unburned at the end of time $t-1$. Hence $h_t(p_{t-1})=1$
and $p_{t-1}$ is burned yellow. For $j\ge t$ both $p_{j-1}$ and $p_{j+1}$ are unburned at the end
of time $t-1$, so $h_t(p_j)=0$ and $p_j$ stays unburned at the spread step. It is not ignited at
time $t$ by hypothesis.

<!-- lemma-14: body_md -->
\label{lem:yes_case}
If $b(G)\le m$, then $Y(\Gamma)\ge\tau$.
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
Let $y_1,\ldots,y_m$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:burning_cover}. Define $\mathcal{I}$ as follows.
Ignite $r$ yellow at time $1$. At a time $s\ge2$, ignite $y_{s-1}$ yellow if $s\le m+1$ and
$y_{s-1}$ is unburned; otherwise ignite an unburned vertex of $G$ yellow if one exists, and an
arbitrary unburned vertex yellow if not. Let $T$ be the first time at the end of which every vertex
of $G$ is burned. If $2\le s<T$, then some vertex of $G$ is unburned at the ignition step of time
$s$, so $z_s\in V(G)$.

We claim that for every $t\le k$, every vertex of $G$ that is burned at the end of time $t$ is
yellow. The case $t=1$ holds because only $r$ is burned then. Let $2\le t\le k$ and let $v\in V(G)$
be unburned at the end of time $t-1$. Then $t-1<T$, so no pendant and no internal vertex of a timer
path is ignited at a time in $\{2,\ldots,t-1\}$. Every $x\in Q(v)$ has $v$ as its only neighbour
outside $B$, and $B$ is unburned at the end of time $1$ and contributes $0$ to $h_{t'}$ for
$t'\ge3$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:after_r}. So every pendant of $v$ is unburned at the end of time $t-1$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:timer_wave}, applied with $t-1$ in place of $t$, shows that $p^{v,i}_{k-1}$ is
unburned at the end of time $t-1$ for every $i\in[w]$, because $t-1\le k-1$. Hence all burned
neighbours of $v$ at the spread step of time $t$ lie in $V(G)$ and are joined to $v$ by positive
edges. By the induction hypothesis they are yellow, so $h_t(v)$ is the number of burned neighbours
of $v$ in $G$. Therefore $v$ is burned yellow at time $t$ if it has a burned neighbour in $G$ at the
end of time $t-1$, and it stays unburned otherwise. As the ignitions are yellow, the claim follows.

The same reasoning shows that the fire spreads inside $G$ at unit speed up to time $k$. The
vertex $y_i$ is burned at the end of time $i+1$ at the latest, so every vertex of
$N^{m-i}_G[y_i]$ is burned at the end of time $(i+1)+(m-i)=k$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:burning_cover},
every vertex of $G$ is burned yellow at the end of time $k$.

Let $v\in V(G)$ and $x\in Q(v)$. If $x$ is ignited, then it is ignited yellow. Otherwise, $v$ is
its only neighbour outside $B$, so $x$ is burned at the spread step of time $\beta(v)+1\ge3$ with
the colour $\nu_{\mathcal{I}}(v)=1$, the edge $xv$ being positive. So every pendant is yellow.
Consequently $r$, the $M$ vertices of $B$, the $n$ vertices of $G$ and the $nL$ pendants are all
yellow, whence $\ensuremath{\left|\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I}}\right|}\ge M+1+n(L+1)=\tau$.

Let $\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:bait} we may assume that $\mathcal{I}$
ignites $r$ yellow at time $1$.

Suppose first that every vertex of $G$ is burned at the end of time $k$. Let $u\in V(G)$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:witness} there is a time $s$ with $d'(z_s,u)\le\beta(u)-s\le k-s$, so $s\le k$. If
$s=1$, then $z_1=r$ and $d'(r,u)\le k-1$, contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:distance}. So $s\ge2$, and
$z_s\in W$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:after_r}. Hence Lemma~\ref{lem:distance} gives
$d_G(\pi(z_s),u)\le k-s$. Put $y_i=\pi(z_{i+1})$ for $1\le i\le m$, which is defined by
Lemma~\ref{lem:after_r}, taking for $y_i$ an arbitrary vertex of $G$ if $\mathcal{I}$ has no
ignition at time $i+1$. Since $k-s=m-i$ for $s=i+1$, we obtain
$V(G)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}N^{m-i}_G[y_i]$, so $b(G)\le m$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:burning_cover}, a
contradiction. 

So let $v\in V(G)$ be unburned at the end of time $k$. Call a timer path of $v$ \emph{touched} if
one of its internal vertices is ignited at a time in $\{2,\ldots,k\}$. Let $a$ be the number of
touched timer paths of $v$ and let $b$ be the number of pendants of $v$ ignited at a time in
$\{2,\ldots,k\}$. Distinct timer paths of $v$ have disjoint sets of internal vertices, and these
are disjoint from $Q(v)$. So each of the $a+b$ objects counted uses up one of the $k-1$ ignitions
at times $2,\ldots,k$, and $a+b\le k-1$.

Let the $i$-th timer path of $v$ be untouched. Lemma~\ref{lem:timer_wave}, applied with $t=k$,
gives that $p^{v,i}_{k-1}$ is burned yellow at the end of time $k$. The edge $p^{v,i}_{k-1}v$ is
negative, so this vertex contributes $-1$ to $h_{k+1}(v)$. The neighbours of $v$ are its at most
$\Delta$ neighbours in $G$, its $L$ pendants, the $w$ ends of its timer paths, and the vertices of
$B$. The vertices of $B$ contribute $0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:after_r}. A pendant of $v$ has $v$ as its
only neighbour outside $B$, so it is burned at the end of time $k$ only if it is ignited at a time
in $\{2,\ldots,k\}$. Every contribution other than those of the $w-a$ untouched timer paths is at
most $1$. Hence
\[
  h_{k+1}(v)\;\le\;\Delta+b+a-(w-a)\;=\;\Delta+b+2a-w\;\le\;\Delta+2(k-1)-w\;=\;-1 ,
\]
and $v$ is burned green at the spread step of time $k+1$.

At most $k$ pendants of $v$ are ignited at times $2,\ldots,k+1$. Let $x\in Q(v)$ be a pendant that
is not one of them. At the spread step of time $k+1$ the vertex $x$ has no burned neighbour outside
$B$, since $v$ is unburned at the end of time $k$, so $x$ is unburned at the end of time $k+1$. At
the spread step of time $k+2$ its only burned neighbour outside $B$ is $v$, which is green, and the
edge $xv$ is positive. So $x$ is burned green. Hence at least $L-k$ pendants of $v$ are green, and
together with $v$ this gives
\[
  \ensuremath{\left|\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I}}\right|}\;\le\;\ensuremath{\left|V(\Gamma)\right|}-(L-k+1)\;=\;\tau+P-(P+1)\;=\;\tau-1 .
\]
As $\mathcal{I}$ was arbitrary, $Y(\Gamma)\le\tau-1$.

<!-- proof-28: body_md -->
A burning sequence of $\Gamma$ has at most $\ensuremath{\left|V(\Gamma)\right|}$ entries, and the burning process it
determines is simulated in polynomial time. So a burning sequence $\mathcal{I}$ with
$\ensuremath{\left|\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{I}}\right|}\ge\tau$ is a certificate, and the problem lies in $\textsf{NP}$.

For the hardness, map an instance $(G,m)$ of Graph Burning with $1\le m<n$ to the instance
$(\Gamma(G,m),\tau)$, and map every instance with $m\ge n$ to a fixed $\textsf{Yes}$ instance. The
construction takes polynomial time. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:yes_case} and \ref{lem:no_case} we have
$Y(\Gamma)\ge\tau$ if and only if $b(G)\le m$. Since Graph Burning is $\textsf{NP}$-complete
\cite{BessyBJRR17}, deciding whether $Y(\Gamma)\ge\tau$ is $\textsf{NP}$-complete as well. Finally,
a polynomial-time algorithm computing $Y(\Gamma)$ exactly would decide Graph Burning in polynomial
time, which is impossible unless $\textsf{P}=\textsf{NP}$.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{thm1:USGBwD_hardness}
Unless $\textsf{P} = \textsf{NP}$, there is no polynomial-time exact algorithm for computing
$Y_k(\Gamma)$, given an undirected signed graph $\Gamma$ and an integer $k$ as input.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-29: body_md -->
Let $\Gamma$ be an undirected signed graph on $n$ vertices and set $k=n$. Every burning sequence of
$\Gamma$ has at most $n$ entries, so $\mathcal{B}_{\le n}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)$ and
$Y_n(\Gamma)=Y(\Gamma)$. Now apply Theorem~\ref{thm1:USGBwoD_hardness}.

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{thm1:DSGBwoD_hardness}
Unless $\textsf{P} = \textsf{NP}$, there is no polynomial-time exact algorithm for computing
$Y(\Gamma)$, given a directed signed graph $\Gamma$ as input.
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- proof-30: body_md -->
Let $\Gamma=(G,\sigma)$ be an undirected signed graph, and let $D$ be the digraph obtained from $G$
by replacing every edge $uv$ by the two arcs $(u,v)$ and $(v,u)$, both of sign $\sigma(uv)$. Then
$N^{+}_{\textsf{in}}(v)$ and $N^{-}_{\textsf{in}}(v)$ in $(D,\sigma)$ coincide with $N^{+}(v)$ and
$N^{-}(v)$ in $\Gamma$ for every vertex $v$. So the two burning processes agree step by step, and
$Y((D,\sigma))=Y(\Gamma)$. Now apply Theorem~\ref{thm1:USGBwoD_hardness}.
