\label{conj:GL}
Let $f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{U}$ be multiplicative and suppose that
$|\alpha-a/q|\leqslant1/(qx)$, where $(a,q)=1$ and $1\leqslant q\leqslant x$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GL-conjecture}
 |L_f(x,\alpha)|\ll \frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}+\log q.
\end{equation}

<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{U}$ be multiplicative, and suppose that
$|\alpha-a/q|\leqslant 1/(qx)$, $(a,q)=1$, and $1\leqslant q\leqslant x$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 |L_f(x,\alpha)|
 \leqslant
 \left(\frac4{\sqrt6}+o(1)\right)
 \frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}
 +O(\log q),
\end{equation}
                                                                                                                               
                                            
If $f$ is completely multiplicative, then the constant $4/\sqrt{6}$ improves to $1$. Both constants are best possible in their respective classes.

<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:complete-small-q}
Let $f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{U}$ be completely multiplicative. Let $2\leqslant q<\log x$ and $|\alpha-a/q|\leqslant1/(qx)$ with $(a,q)=1$, we have
\[
 |L_f(x,\alpha)|
 \leqslant (1+o(1))\frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}.
\]
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By \cite[Corollary~7.1]{GranvilleLamzouri2026}, there is a primitive
character $\chi\pmod\ell$, with $\ell\mid q$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GL-complete}
 L_f(x,\alpha)
 =
 \frac{\overline{\chi}(a)\kappa(q/\ell)\tau(\chi)}{\phi(q)}
 \sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{f(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}n
 +O\bigg(\frac{(\log x)^{2/3+o(1)}}{\sqrt q}+e^{O(\sqrt{\log\log x})}\bigg),
\end{equation}
where $f=\kappa*\chi$. The error term is clearly 
$o((\log x)/\sqrt q)$ since $q<\log x$.

For every $n$ with $\chi(n)\neq0$, writing $n=dm$, where all prime factors of $d$ divide $q$ but not $\ell$,
and $(m,q)=1$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{f(n)\overline{\chi(n)}}n
 &=
 \sum_{\substack{d\leqslant x\\
                  p\mid d\Rightarrow p\mid q,\ p\nmid\ell}}
 \frac{f(d)\overline{\chi(d)}}d
 \sum_{\substack{m\leqslant x/d\\(m,q)=1}}
 \frac{f(m)\overline{\chi(m)}}m\\
 & 
 = \prod_{\substack{p\mid q\\p\nmid\ell}}
 \bigg(1-\frac{f(p)\overline{\chi(p)}}p\bigg)^{-1}
 \sum_{\substack{m\leqslant x\\(m,q)=1}}
 \frac{f(m)\overline{\chi(m)}}m 
 +O\left(\log^2q\right),
\end{aligned}
\]
where we replace $m\le x/d$ by $m\le x$ in the inner sum of the first line, at a cost
\(
 \sum_{x/d<m\leqslant x}m^{-1}
 \ll \log d\), and then extend the $d$-sum to infinity. A crude estimate gives  the error
 \[
 \sum_{\substack{d\ge1\\p\mid d\Rightarrow p\mid q}}
 \frac{\log d}{d}
 =
 \prod_{p\mid q}\bigg(1-\frac1p\bigg)^{-1}
 \sum_{p\mid q}\frac{\log p}{p-1}
 \ll
 \frac q{\phi(q)}\log q
 \ll \log^2 q,
 \]
while the contribution from $d>x$ is o(1) by Rankin's trick.

Moreover, by M\"obius inversion,
\[
 \Bigg|
 \sum_{\substack{m\leqslant x\\(m,q)=1}}
 \frac{f(m)\overline{\chi(m)}}m
 \Bigg|
 \leqslant
 \sum_{\substack{m\leqslant x\\(m,q)=1}}\frac1m
 =\sum_{r\le x,r|q}\frac{\mu(r)}{r}\sum_{n\le x/r}\frac{1}{n}
 =
 \frac{\phi(q)}q\log x+O(\log^2 q).
\]
Thus, using $|\tau(\chi)|=\sqrt\ell$, it remains to show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complete-local-bound}
 \frac{\sqrt\ell}{q}|\kappa(q/\ell)|
 \prod_{\substack{p\mid q\\p\nmid\ell}}
 \Bigg|1-\frac{f(p)\overline{\chi(p)}}p\Bigg|^{-1}
 \leqslant \frac1{\sqrt q}.                    
\end{equation}

We verify this prime by prime. Write $p^e\Vert q$ and $p^j\Vert\ell$,
where $0\leqslant j\leqslant e$. If $j\geqslant1$, then $\chi(p)=0$ and
$\kappa(p^{e-j})=f(p)^{e-j}$. Hence the corresponding local factor on
the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:complete-local-bound} is at most
$p^{j/2-e}\leqslant p^{-e/2}$.
If $j=0$, put $z:=f(p)\overline{\chi(p)}$. Since $f=\kappa*\chi$ and
$f$ is completely multiplicative, we have
\[
 \kappa(p^e)=f(p)^e-\chi(p)f(p)^{e-1}\implies |\kappa(p^e)|=|f(p)^{e-1}||1-z|\le|1-z|.
\]
The corresponding local factor on
the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:complete-local-bound} is therefore at most
\[
 \frac1{p^e}\frac{|1-z|}{|1-z/p|}\le p^{-e+1/2}\le p^{-e/2},
\]
where we used $|1-z|\leqslant\sqrt p |1-z/p|$, since $|z|\leqslant1$ and 
\(
 p|1-z/p|^2-|1-z|^2
 =(p-1)\left(1-{|z|^2}/{p}\right)\geqslant0.
\)
Multiplying over the local factor at $p^e\Vert q$ proves
\eqref{eq:complete-local-bound}.

Substituting \eqref{eq:complete-local-bound}  into \eqref{eq:GL-complete} we get
\[
 |L_f(x,\alpha)|
 \leqslant (1+o(1))\frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}.
\]
The two $O(\log^2 q)$ errors above contribute, after substitution into
\eqref{eq:GL-complete}, at most
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \log^2 q\left(
 \frac{\sqrt{\ell}}{\phi(q)}|\kappa(q/\ell)|
 +\frac{\sqrt q}{\phi(q)}
 \right)
 &\ll
 \frac{\sqrt q}{\phi(q)}
 \bigl(2^{\omega(q)}+1\bigr)\log^2 q=
 o\left(\frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}\right),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the second term follows from \eqref{eq:complete-local-bound} and the first term comes from
\(
 |\kappa(a)|
\le
 2^{\omega(a)}.
\)
This proves the lemma.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:sparse}
In the range $\log x\leqslant q\leqslant(\log x)^2$, we have
\[
 |L_0|+|L_1|
 \ll
 \log\log x
 +o\bigg(\frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}\bigg).
\]
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The contribution of $L_0$ is immediate from Mertens' theorem:
\[
 |L_0|=\bigg|
 \sum_{\substack{n\leqslant x\\P^+(n)\leqslant y}}
 \frac{f(n)}n\mathrm e(an/q)
 \bigg|
 \leqslant
 \prod_{p\leqslant y}\bigg(1-\frac1p\bigg)^{-1}
 \ll\log y
 \ll\log\log x.
\]

We now turn to $L_1$. After removing the terms divisible by $p^2$ for
some $p>y$, as in the previous section, every remaining integer counted
by $L_1$ can be written uniquely as $n=pm$, where $p>y$ and
$P^+(m)\leqslant y$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L1-formula}
 L_1
 =
 \sum_{p>y}\frac{f(p)}p
 \sum_{\substack{m\leqslant x/p\\P^+(m)\leqslant y}}
 \frac{f(m)}m\mathrm e(amp/q).
\end{equation}

We first consider $m\leqslant\sqrt q$. We may replace $p\leqslant x/m$ by  $p\leqslant x/\sqrt q$, at a cost
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{m\leqslant\sqrt q}\frac1m
 \sum_{x/\sqrt q<p\leqslant x/m}\frac1p
 &\ll
 \log q\bigg(-\log\big(1-\frac{\log q}{2\log x}\big) +O\big(\frac1{\log x}\big) \bigg) \ll
 \frac{\log^2 q}{\log x}
 =o(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $\sqrt q<y$, the condition $P^+(m)\leqslant y$ is automatic. 
Thus, after grouping the prime variable by its residue class modulo $q$,
the contribution from $m\leqslant\sqrt q$ is
\[
 \sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times} A(u)V(u)
 \ll \|A\|_2\|V\|_2,
\]
by Cauchy--Schwarz, where
\[
 A(u):=
 \sum_{\substack{y<p\leqslant x/\sqrt q\\p\equiv u\pmod q}}
 \frac{f(p)}p,
 \qquad
 V(u):=
 \sum_{m\leqslant\sqrt q}\frac{f(m)}m\mathrm e(amu/q).
\]

For $\|A\|_2$, let $I=(P,2P]$ be a dyadic interval.  We write $$A(u)=\sum_I A_I(u),\quad A_I(u):=
\sum_{\substack{p\in I\\ p\equiv u\pmod q}}\frac{f(p)}p.$$ 
Since
$P\geqslant y=q^2$, $\log (P/q)\asymp\log P$, then by the Brun--Titchmarsh inequality and partial summation formula, we get
\[
 \max_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times}|A_I(u)|\le \max_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times}
 \sum_{\substack{p\in I\\p\equiv u\pmod q}}\frac1p
 \ll
 \frac1{\phi(q)}
 \sum_{p\in I}\frac1p.
\]
Hence by Minkowski's inequality, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|A\|_2
 &\leqslant \sum_I\|A_I\|_2
 \leqslant
 \sum_I
 \bigg(
 \max_u|A_I(u)|
 \sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times}|A_I(u)|
 \bigg)^{1/2}\\
 &\leqslant
 \sum_I
 \bigg(
 \frac1{\phi(q)}
 \sum_{p\in I}\frac1p
 \sum_{p\in I}\frac1p
 \bigg)^{1/2}
 \ll
 \frac1{\sqrt{\phi(q)}}
 \sum_I\sum_{p\in I}\frac1p
 \ll
 \frac{\log\log x}{\sqrt{\phi(q)}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For $\|V\|_2$, let $c_q(h):= \sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times}\mathrm e(hu/q)$ denote the Ramanujan sum. For $m\neq n$, the standard bound $|c_q(h)|\le(q,h)$ gives
$|c_q(m-n)|\le(q,m-n)\le |m-n|<\sqrt q. $ Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|V\|_2^2
 &=
 \sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times}|V(u)|^2
 =
 \sum_{m,n\leqslant\sqrt q}
 \frac{f(m)\overline{f(n)}}{mn}\,c_q(m-n)\\
 &\ll
 \phi(q)\sum_{m\leqslant\sqrt q}\frac1{m^2}
 +
 \sqrt q\sum_{\substack{m,n\leqslant\sqrt q\\m\neq n}}\frac1{mn}\ll
 \phi(q)+\sqrt q \log^2 q
 \ll \phi(q).
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining this with the bound for $\|A\|_2$, the contribution from
$m\leqslant\sqrt q$ is \( \|A\|_2\|V\|_2\ll\log\log x.
\)

We now consider $m>\sqrt q$. Split the prime variable into dyadic
intervals $I=(P,2P]$. For $p\in I$, we replace $m\leqslant x/p$ by  $\sqrt q<m\leqslant x/(2P)$. The omitted boundary terms are
bounded by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_I\sum_{p\in I}\frac1p
 \sum_{x/(2P)<m\leqslant x/p}\frac1m
 &\ll
 \sum_I\sum_{p\in I}\frac1p
 \ll \log\log x.
\end{aligned}
\]

For a fixed $I$, grouping both variables by their residue classes
modulo $q$, the contribution from the interval $I$ is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigg|
 \sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times} &A_I(u)\sum_{v\bmod q}
C_I(v)\mathrm e(auv/q) \bigg|
\le 
\|A_I\|_2 \Bigg(\sum_{u\bmod q}
\bigg|
\sum_{v\bmod q}C_I(v)\mathrm e(auv/q)
\bigg|^2\Bigg)^{1/2}\\
 &\le
 \|A_I\|_2 \Bigg(\sum_{v,w\bmod q}
C_I(v)\overline{C_I(w)}
\sum_{u\bmod q}\mathrm e(au(v-w)/q)\Bigg)^{1/2}
\le
 \sqrt q\,\|A_I\|_2\|C_I\|_2
 \end{aligned}
\]
where 
\[
C_I(v):=
 \sum_{\substack{\sqrt q<m\leqslant x/(2P)\\
                  P^+(m)\leqslant y\\
                  m\equiv v\pmod q}}
 \frac{f(m)}m,
\]
and
$$ \begin{aligned} \|C_I\|_2^2 &\leqslant \max_{v\bmod q}|C_I(v)| \sum_{v\bmod q}|C_I(v)|
\ll
\frac{\log x}{q}  \sum_{\substack{m\geqslant1\\P^+(m)\leqslant y}}\frac1m\ll  \frac{\log x\log\log x}{q}. \end{aligned} $$

Now, summing over the dyadic intervals $I$ and using the same estimate for $\|A_I\|_2$ as above, we obtain the contribution from $m>\sqrt{q}$ is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sqrt q\sum_I\|A_I\|_2\|C_I\|_2
 &\ll
 \sqrt q\sum_I
 \frac1{\sqrt{\phi(q)}}
 \bigg(\sum_{p\in I}\frac1p\bigg)
 \bigg(\frac{\log x\log\log x}{q}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
 &\ll
 (\log\log x)^{3/2}
 \sqrt{\frac{\log x}{\phi(q)}}
                                                                                              
 =
 o\bigg(\frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}\bigg),
\end{aligned}
\]
where we used
\(
 \sum_I\sum_{p\in I}\frac1p\ll\log\log x
\)
and \eqref{eq:direct-facts}. Together with the boundary terms and the
range $m\leqslant\sqrt q$, this proves the proposition.

<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:gauss}
For every $\chi\pmod q$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gauss-l1}
 \sum_{d\mid q^\infty}
 \frac{|\tau(\overline{\chi},ad)|}{d}
 \leqslant
 \frac4{\sqrt6}\sqrt q.
\end{equation}
If $f$ is completely multiplicative, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gauss-complete}
 \bigg|
 \sum_{d\mid q^\infty}
 \frac{f(d)\tau(\overline{\chi},ad)}{d}
 \bigg|
 \leqslant
 \sqrt q.
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every $\varepsilon>0$ and $X\geqslant1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gauss-tail}
 \sum_{\substack{d>X\\d\mid q^\infty}}
 \frac{|\tau(\overline{\chi},ad)|}{d}
 \ll_\varepsilon
 \frac{q^{1/2+\varepsilon}}{\sqrt X}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By the Chinese remainder theorem, it is enough to estimate the
corresponding sums prime by prime. Let $q=p^e$, and suppose that $\chi$
is induced by a primitive character $\chi^\ast\pmod{p^j}$, where
$0\leqslant j\leqslant e$. If $j\geqslant1$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \tau(\overline{\chi},ap^v)
 &=
 \begin{cases}
  0, & v\neq e-j,\\[2mm]
  p^{e-j}\chi^\ast(a)\tau(\overline{\chi^\ast}),
  & v=e-j,
 \end{cases}\\
 \sum_{v\geqslant0}
 \frac{|\tau(\overline{\chi},ap^v)|}{p^v}
 &=
 \frac{p^{e-j}|\tau(\overline{\chi^\ast})|}{p^{e-j}}
 =
 p^{j/2}
 \leqslant p^{e/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here we used the standard formula
$|\tau(\chi^\ast)|=p^{j/2}$ for primitive characters.

If $j=0$, then $\chi$ is principal modulo $p^e$, and
$\tau(\overline{\chi},ap^v)=c_{p^e}(ap^v)$. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{v\geqslant0}\frac{|\tau(\overline{\chi},ap^v)|}{p^v}
 &=
 \frac{p^{e-1}}{p^{e-1}}
 +\phi(p^e)\sum_{v\geqslant e}\frac1{p^v}
 =2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Returning to general $q$, we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{d\mid q^\infty}\frac{|\tau(\overline{\chi},ad)|}{d}
 &\leqslant
 \sqrt q
 \prod_{\substack{p^e\Vert q\\
                   \chi\pmod{p^e}\ {\rm principal}}}
 \max\bigg(1,\frac2{p^{e/2}}\bigg)
 \leqslant
 \sqrt q\,\frac2{\sqrt2}\frac2{\sqrt3}
 =
 \frac4{\sqrt6}\sqrt q.
\end{aligned}
\]
                                              
This proves \eqref{eq:Gauss-l1}.

Suppose now that $f$ is completely multiplicative. By the Chinese
remainder theorem, the sum in \eqref{eq:Gauss-complete} factors prime
by prime, up to unit factors of modulus $1$. For $p^e\Vert q$, the
nonprincipal case contributes at most $p^{j/2}\leqslant p^{e/2}$. In the
principal case, after absorbing the unit factor into $f(p)$, the local
sum contributes, for some $|z|\le1$,
\[
 \bigg|z^{e-1}\frac{z-1}{1-z/p}\bigg|
 \leqslant\frac{|1-z|}{|1-z/p|}
 \leqslant\sqrt p
 \leqslant p^{e/2},
\]
by the same inequality $|1-z|\leqslant\sqrt p |1-z/p|$ we used in Lemma~\ref{lem:complete-small-q}.
Multiplying over $p^e\Vert q$ proves \eqref{eq:Gauss-complete}.

Finally,  we use Rankin's trick to bound
\[
 \sum_{\substack{d>X\\d\mid q^\infty}}
 \frac{|\tau(\overline{\chi},ad)|}{d}
 \leqslant
 X^{-1/2}
 \sum_{d\mid q^\infty}
 \frac{|\tau(\overline{\chi},ad)|}{\sqrt d}.
\]
The same calculation at prime power $p^e$ gives
\[
 \sum_{v\geqslant0}
 \frac{|\tau(\overline{\chi},ap^v)|}{p^{v/2}}
 \leqslant
 p^{e/2}\bigg(1+\frac2{\sqrt p}\bigg),
\]
and therefore \eqref{eq:Gauss-tail} follows by
\[
 \sum_{d\mid q^\infty}
 \frac{|\tau(\overline{\chi},ad)|}{\sqrt d}
 \ll
 \sqrt q\prod_{p\mid q}\bigg(1+\frac2{\sqrt p}\bigg)
 \ll_\varepsilon \sqrt q\,q^\varepsilon.
\]

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:two-large}
In the range $\log x\leqslant q\leqslant(\log x)^2$, we have
\[
 |L_{\geqslant2}|
 \leqslant
 \bigg(\frac4{\sqrt6}+o(1)\bigg)
 \frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}+O(1).
\]
If $f$ is completely multiplicative, then
\[
 |L_{\geqslant2}|
 \leqslant
 (1+o(1))\frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}+O(1).
\]
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Split the prime variables into dyadic intervals
$I=(P,2P]$ and $J=(R,2R]$, we define
\[
  X:=\frac{x}{4cPR},\quad
P_{\chi,c}(I):=
 \sum_{\substack{p\in I\\p\nmid c}}
 \frac{f(p)\chi(p)}p \quad \text{and}
 \quad
 D_\chi(X):=
 \sum_{\substack{d\leqslant X\\d\mid q^\infty}}
 \frac{f(d)\tau(\overline{\chi},ad)}d.
\]
The choice of $X$ separates the condition $dcpr\leqslant x$ on each dyadic
block.                                                                                                                 

If $d>X$, then
\(
 {x}/{4}<n=dcpr\leqslant x.
\)
After summing over $c,I,J$, taking absolute values and applying Lemma~\ref{lem:ramare}, the total
contribution of all such terms to $L_{\ge2}$ is at most
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\int_0^1(1-t)
 \sum_{\substack{x/4<n\leqslant x}}                     
 \frac1n
 \sum_{\substack{p,r>y,\ p\neq r\\pr\mid n}}
 t^{\omega_y(n/(pr))}\,\mathrm{d} t
 \leqslant
 \sum_{x/4<n\leqslant x}\frac1n
 \ll1.
\end{aligned}
\]


For $d\leqslant X$, we have $dcpr\leqslant x$ for every
$p\in I$ and $r\in J$. After temporarily including the diagonal \(p=r\) when \(I=J\), whose contribution is acceptable, we have, by plugging
\eqref{eq:character-expansion} into \ref{eq:ramare-decomposition},

\begin{align}
 L_{\geqslant2}
 =&
 \int_0^1(1-t)
 \sum_{\substack{c\geqslant1,\ (c,q)=1\\
                  v_\ell(c)\leqslant1\;(\ell>y)}}
 \frac{f(c)t^{\omega_y(c)}}{c\phi(q)}
 \sum_{I,J}\sum_{\chi\bmod q}
 \chi(c)D_\chi(X)
 P_{\chi,c}(I)P_{\chi,c}(J)\mathrm{d} t
 +O(1)+o\bigg(\frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}\bigg)\notag\\
 ={}&
 \int_0^1 (1-t) \sum_{\substack{c\geqslant1,\ (c,q)=1\\
                  v_\ell(c)\leqslant1\;(\ell>y)}}t^{\omega_y(c)}
 \sum_{I,J}
 L_{\geqslant2,\mathrm{block}}(c;I,J)\,\mathrm{d} t
 +O(1) +o\bigg(\frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}\bigg),\label{eq:block}
\end{align}
where
\[
 L_{\geqslant2,\mathrm{block}}(c;I,J)
 :=
 \frac{f(c)}{c\phi(q)}
 \sum_{\chi\bmod q}
 \chi(c)D_\chi(X)
 P_{\chi,c}(I)P_{\chi,c}(J).
\]
By character orthogonality and the same Brun--Titchmarsh estimate used
in Section~\ref{sec:sparse},
\begin{align}\label{eq:prime-sec}
 \sum_{\chi\bmod q}|P_{\chi,c}(I)|^2
 &=
 \phi(q)
 \sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times}
 \bigg|
 \sum_{\substack{p\in I,\ p\nmid c\\p\equiv u\pmod q}}
 \frac{f(p)}p
 \bigg|^2
 \ll
 \bigg(\sum_{p\in I}\frac1p\bigg)^2.
\end{align}
Then by \eqref{eq:Gauss-l1}, \eqref{eq:prime-sec}, and Cauchy--Schwarz, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BT-block}
 |L_{\geqslant2,\mathrm{block}}(c;I,J)|
 \le
\frac{1}{c\phi(q)}
\max_{\chi}|D_\chi(X)|
\sum_{\chi\bmod q}
|P_{\chi,c}(I)P_{\chi,c}(J)|
 \ll
 \frac{\sqrt q}{c\phi(q)}
 \bigg(\sum_{p\in I}\frac1p\bigg)
 \bigg(\sum_{r\in J}\frac1r\bigg).
\end{equation}

We now fix $0<\delta<1$ and put \( z:=\exp((\log x)^\delta).\)
We first consider the part of $L_{\geqslant2}$ for which at least one of the two marked
primes lies between $y$ and $z$.
 By \eqref{eq:BT-block} and \eqref{eq:block}, and extending the sums, their total contribution is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\ll
 \frac{\sqrt q}{\phi(q)}
 \bigg(\sum_{y<p\leqslant z}\frac1p\bigg)
 \bigg(\sum_{y<p\leqslant x}\frac1p\bigg)
 \int_0^1(1-t)\sum_{\substack{c\leqslant x,\ (c,q)=1\\
                  v_\ell(c)\leqslant1\;(\ell>y)}}
 \frac{t^{\omega_y(c)}}c\,\mathrm{d} t
 +O(1)\\
 &\ll
 \frac{\sqrt q}{\phi(q)}
 \bigg(\sum_{y<p\leqslant z}\frac1p\bigg)
 \bigg(\sum_{y<p\leqslant x}\frac1p\bigg)
 \frac{\phi(q)}q\log x
 \int_0^1(1-t)
 \exp\bigg(-(1-t)\sum_{y<p\leqslant x}\frac1p\bigg)\,\mathrm{d} t
 +O(1)\\
 &\ll
 \frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}
 \bigg(\sum_{y<p\leqslant z}\frac{1}{p}\bigg)
      \bigg(\sum_{y<p\leqslant x}\frac{1}{p}\bigg)^{-1}
 +O(1)\\
 &\ll
 \frac{\delta+o(1)}{1+o(1)}
 \frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}
 +O(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sum_{\substack{c\leqslant x,\ (c,q)=1\\
                  v_\ell(c)\leqslant1\;(\ell>y)}}
 \frac{t^{\omega_y(c)}}c\leqslant
 \prod_{\substack{p\leqslant y\\p\nmid q}}
 \bigg(1-\frac1p\bigg)^{-1}
 \prod_{y<p\leqslant x}\bigg(1+\frac tp\bigg)\\
 & =
 \prod_{\substack{p\leqslant x\\p\nmid q}}
 \bigg(1-\frac1p\bigg)^{-1}
 \prod_{y<p\leqslant x}
 \bigg(1-\frac1p\bigg)\bigg(1+\frac tp\bigg)
 \ll
 \frac{\phi(q)}q\log x\,
 \exp\bigg(-(1-t)\sum_{y<p\leqslant x}\frac1p\bigg).
\end{aligned}
\]


It remains to consider the part of $L_{\geqslant2}$ for which both marked
primes exceed $z$. Since $p\in(P,2P]$ and
$r\in(R,2R]$, we have $P,R\geqslant z/2$. Since \( q\leqslant(\log x)^2\leqslant(\log P)^{2/\delta},
\) we can apply the Siegel--Walfisz theorem to get
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-second-sharp}
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\chi\bmod q}|P_{\chi,c}(I)|^2
 &=
 \phi(q)
 \sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times}
 \bigg|
 \sum_{\substack{p\in I,\ p\nmid c\\p\equiv u\pmod q}}
 \frac{f(p)}p
 \bigg|^2\\
 &\leqslant
 \phi(q)
 \max_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times}
 \sum_{\substack{p\in I\\p\equiv u\pmod q}}\frac1p
 \sum_{p\in I}\frac1p
 \leqslant
 \bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)
 \bigg(\sum_{p\in I}\frac1p\bigg)^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Suppose $X=x/(4cPR)<\log x$, then \( \frac{x}{4\log x}<cpr\leqslant x.\)
Using \eqref{eq:BT-block} and Lemma~\ref{lem:ramare}, the total
contribution of these blocks is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\ll
 \frac{\sqrt q}{\phi(q)}
 \sum_{x/(4\log x)<m\leqslant x}\frac1m\ll
 \frac{\sqrt q}{\phi(q)}\log\log x
 =
 o\bigg(\frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}\bigg),
\end{aligned}
\]
by \eqref{eq:direct-facts}, which is negligible.

We are left with $X\geqslant\log x$. For a general multiplicative function,
\eqref{eq:Gauss-l1} gives
\[
 |D_\chi(X)|
 \leqslant
 \frac4{\sqrt6}\sqrt q.
\]
If $f$ is completely multiplicative, then
\eqref{eq:Gauss-complete} and \eqref{eq:Gauss-tail} give, for $\varepsilon=1/8$ (say),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |D_\chi(X)|
 &\leqslant
 \sqrt q+ O_\varepsilon\bigg(\frac{q^{1/2+\varepsilon}}{\sqrt X}\bigg)
 =
 (1+o_x(1))\sqrt q.
\end{aligned}
\]
                                           

Hence, by \eqref{eq:prime-second-sharp} and Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |L_{\geqslant2,\mathrm{block}}(c;I,J)|
 &\leqslant
 \frac{\max_\chi|D_\chi(X)|}{c\phi(q)}
 \bigg(\sum_{\chi\bmod q}|P_{\chi,c}(I)|^2\bigg)^{1/2}
 \bigg(\sum_{\chi\bmod q}|P_{\chi,c}(J)|^2\bigg)^{1/2}\\
 &\leqslant
 \bigg(\frac4{\sqrt6}+o_x(1)\bigg)
 \frac{\sqrt q}{c\phi(q)}
 \bigg(\sum_{p\in I}\frac1p\bigg)
 \bigg(\sum_{r\in J}\frac1r\bigg)
\end{aligned}
\]
in the multiplicative case. If $f$ is completely multiplicative, the same
estimate holds with $4/\sqrt6$ replaced by $1$.

To recombine the prime sums, we may drop the conditions $p\nmid c$ and
$r\nmid c$.                                                                                                                       
The corresponding loss is
\[
 \ll
 (\log x)(1+\log\log x)
 \sum_{p>y}\frac1{p^2}
 =
 o\bigg((\log x)\frac{\phi(q)}q\bigg),
\]
which is again negligible  to $L_{\geqslant2}$.
For the remaining terms, put $m=cpr$ and write
\[
 K:=\omega_y(m),
 \qquad
 K_z:=\#\{p>z:p\mid m\}.
\]
For each fixed $m$, the marked primes can be chosen in
$K_z(K_z-1)$ ordered ways, while the two-prime Ramar\'e weight gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 K_z(K_z-1)
 \int_0^1(1-t)t^{K-2}\,\mathrm{d} t
 &=
 \frac{K_z(K_z-1)}{K(K-1)}
 \leqslant1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, in the multiplicative case, the contribution from $p,r>z$ and
$X\geqslant\log x$ is at most
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\bigg(\frac4{\sqrt6}+o_x(1)\bigg)
 \frac{\sqrt q}{\phi(q)}
 \sum_{\substack{m\leqslant x\\(m,q)=1}}\frac1m
 =
 \bigg(\frac4{\sqrt6}+o_x(1)\bigg)
 \frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}
 +o\bigg(\frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}\bigg).
\end{aligned}
\]
For completely multiplicative $f$, the same argument gives
\(
 (1+o_x(1))\frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}.
\)

Combining this with the contribution from the range in which at least
one marked prime lies in $(y,z]$, we obtain
\[
 |L_{\geqslant2}|
 \leqslant
 \bigg(
 \frac4{\sqrt6}+O(\delta)+o_x(1)
 \bigg)
 \frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}
 +O(1)
\]
for general multiplicative functions, and $4/\sqrt{6}$ replaced by 1 for completely multiplicative functions. We finally let $x\to\infty$ first
with $\delta$ fixed, and then let $\delta\to0$. This proves the
proposition.

From \eqref{eq:decomposition},
Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:two-large}, we get
\[
 |L_f(x,a/q)|
 \leqslant
 \bigg(\frac4{\sqrt6}+o(1)\bigg)
 \frac{\log x}{\sqrt q}
 +O(\log\log x).
\]
Since $\log\log x\ll\log q$ in this range, this proves
\eqref{eq:main} at $a/q$, and \eqref{eq:phase-error} returns us to
$\alpha$.
If $f$ is completely multiplicative, the second part of
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-large} gives us the same result with $4/\sqrt{6}$ replaced by 1.

The constant $4/\sqrt6$ is best possible by
\cite[Example~1.7]{GranvilleLamzouri2026}. To see that the constant $1$
is also best possible, we fix a primitive character $\chi\pmod{q_0}$ and
take $f=\overline{\chi}$ and $\alpha=a/q_0$, where $(a,q_0)=1$. Then
\[
 L_f(x,a/q_0)
 =
 \frac{\chi(a)\tau(\overline{\chi})}{q_0}\log x+O(1) \implies
 |L_f(x,a/q_0)|
 =
 (1+o(1))\frac{\log x}{\sqrt{q_0}}.
\]
