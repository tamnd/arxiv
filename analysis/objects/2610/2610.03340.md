\textbf{An embedding ${\mathfrak{vect}}(n+1)\subset {\mathfrak{k}}(2n+1)$}

1) Let ${\mathfrak{g}}= {\mathfrak{vect}}(n+1)={\mathfrak{vect}}(q_1,\dots,q_n, y)$. Let us represent ${\mathfrak{g}}$ in the form 
\[
{\mathfrak{g}}=
{\mathbb  C}[y]{\mathfrak{vect}}(q_1,\dots, q_n) +{\mathbb  C}[q_1,\dots, q_n]{\mathfrak{vect}}(y).
\]


Let
\[
\text{ $\deg y=2$, \ $\deg q_i=1$ for all $i=1,\dots, n$.}
\]
 Then, the non-positive part of the ${\mathbb  Z}$-graded Lie algebra ${\mathfrak{g}}$ is of the form:
\[
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
${\mathfrak{g}}_{-2}$ & ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$ & ${\mathfrak{g}}_0$\\
\hline
${\partial}_y$ & ${\partial}_i, \; q_i{\partial}_y$ & $q_i{\partial}_j, q_iq_j{\partial}_y, y{\partial}_y$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\]

Let us describe the embedding of ${\mathfrak{g}}$ into ${\mathfrak{k}}={\mathfrak{k}}(2n+1)={\mathfrak{k}}(q_1,\dots, q_n, p_1,\dots, p_n, t)$ up to scalar coefficients: they are irrelevant for us here. 

Clearly, ${\partial}_y$ corresponds to the generating function ${\bf 1}$ (actually, to $\mathchoice
  {\raisebox{.25pt}{\relax$\dfrac{1}{2}$}}
  {\raisebox{.25pt}{\relax$\dfrac{1}{2}$}}
  {\raisebox{-.5pt}{\relax$\tfrac{1}{2}$}}
  {\raisebox{-.5pt}{\relax$\tfrac{1}{2}$}}{\bf 1}$, but we do not have to be so precise to prove the claim). Further, ${\partial}_i$ corresponds to $p_i$, whereas $q_i{\partial}_y$ corresponds to~ $q_i$.

For the elements of degree 0, we look how they commute with elements of degree~$-1$. We see that 
\[
\text{$q_i{\partial}_j \longleftrightarrow q_ip_j$ and $q_iq_j{\partial}_y\longleftrightarrow q_iq_j$.}
\]
The element $y{\partial}_y$ multiplies the subspace $\Span({\partial}_i)$ by $(-1)$ and annihilates $\Span(q_i{\partial}_y)$. The element $\mathchoice
  {\raisebox{.25pt}{\relax$\dfrac{1}{2}$}}
  {\raisebox{.25pt}{\relax$\dfrac{1}{2}$}}
  {\raisebox{-.5pt}{\relax$\tfrac{1}{2}$}}
  {\raisebox{-.5pt}{\relax$\tfrac{1}{2}$}}(t+\sum p_iq_i)$ acts precisely so. Therefore, the image of ${\mathfrak{g}}_0$ in ${\mathfrak{k}}_0$ contains all elements ${\mathfrak{k}}_0$, except those generated by $p_ip_j$ for all~$i,j$.

To single out this image, and ipso facto, the whole ${\mathfrak{g}}$ by means of differential equations, we use the operators $\tilde K_{q_i}=q_i{\partial}_t-{\partial}_{p_i}$ (called $Y$-vectors in \cite{ShVf}; the ones that commute with~ ${\mathfrak{k}}_-$). Accordingly, the image of ${\mathfrak{g}}$ in ${\mathfrak{k}}$ is generated by the functions $f$ satisfying the following system of equations
\begin{equation}\label{EQ}
\tilde K_{q_i}\tilde K_{q_j}(f)=0 \text{ for all } i,j=1,\dots n.
\end{equation}

Clearly, on the space of functions independent of $t$, the system \eqref{EQ} is equivalent to the simpler system 
$$
{\partial}_{p_i}{\partial}_{p_j}(f)=0 \text{ for all } i,j=1,\dots n,
$$
and hence, the solutions independent of $t$ are of the form 
\[
\text{$\varphi(q)\longleftrightarrow \varphi(q){\partial}_y$ and $\varphi(q)p_i\longleftrightarrow \varphi(q){\partial}_i$.}
\]

It remains to describe the images of the elements of the form $\varphi(q)y^k{\partial}_i, \varphi(q)y^k{\partial}_y\in {\mathfrak{g}}$ which should correspond to generating functions $f$ depending on~ $t$. 

First, observe that $({\partial}_y)^k (\varphi(q)y^k{\partial}_y)=k!\varphi(q){\partial}_y \longleftrightarrow k!\varphi(q)$, so the highest wrt $t$ summand of the function corresponding to $\varphi(q)y^k{\partial}_y$ is proportional to~$\varphi(q)t^k$. Observe also that
$$
\tilde K_{q_i}(t+\sum q_ip_i)= (q_i{\partial}_t-{\partial}_{p_i})(t+\sum q_sp_s)=0, \; (q_i{\partial}_t-{\partial}_{p_i})\varphi(q)=0,
$$
and hence
$$
\tilde K_{q_i}\tilde K_{q_j}\varphi(q)(t+\sum q_sp_s)^k=0 \Longrightarrow \varphi(q)(t+\sum q_sp_s)^k \longleftrightarrow \varphi(q)y^k{\partial}_y.
$$
Analogously,
$$
\tilde K_{q_j}(p_k(t+\sum q_ip_i))=\delta_k^j (t+\sum q_ip_i)\Longrightarrow \tilde K_{q_i}\tilde K_{q_j}(p_k(t+\sum q_ip_i))=0,
$$
and therefore to $\varphi(q)y^k{\partial}_i$ a function proportional to $\varphi(q)p_i(t+\sum q_sp_s)^k$ corresponds.

Thus, the image of ${\mathfrak{g}}$ in ${\mathfrak{k}}$ is spanned by the elements generated for every non-negative integer $k$ by functions of the form 
\begin{equation}\label{mainF}
\varphi(q), \; \varphi(q)p_i, \; \varphi(q)(t+\sum q_sp_s)^k \text{ and } \varphi(q)p_i(t+\sum q_sp_s)^k \text{ for }i= 1,\dots, n.
\end{equation}

This image is maximal in~${\mathfrak{k}}$. Indeed, in the grading used above we have ${\mathfrak{g}}_-={\mathfrak{k}}_-$, whereas
\[
{\mathfrak{g}}_0=\Span(q_ip_j, \; q_iq_j, \; t+\mathop{\sum}\limits_{1\leq s\leq n} q_sp_s)
\]
is the stabilizer of the Lagrangian subspace $\Span(q)\subset{\mathfrak{k}}_{-1}$ in ${\mathfrak{k}}_0={\mathfrak{c}}({\mathfrak{sp}}(2n))$,
and the stabilizer of a~Lagrangian subspace is a~maximal subalgebra of ${\mathfrak{c}}({\mathfrak{sp}}(2n))$. Let now
${\mathfrak{r}}$ be a~graded subalgebra such that ${\mathfrak{g}}\subsetneq{\mathfrak{r}}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}$. Since ${\mathfrak{r}}_0\supset{\mathfrak{g}}_0$,
either ${\mathfrak{r}}_0={\mathfrak{g}}_0$, and then ${\mathfrak{r}}$ is contained in the prolong of ${\mathfrak{k}}_-\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_0$ in ${\mathfrak{k}}$,
i.e., in the space of solutions of the system \eqref{EQ}, which is ${\mathfrak{g}}$ itself, a~contradiction;
or ${\mathfrak{r}}_0={\mathfrak{k}}_0$, and then ${\mathfrak{r}}_1$ is a~non-zero ${\mathfrak{k}}_0$-submodule of the irreducible
${\mathfrak{k}}_0$-module ${\mathfrak{k}}_1\simeq S^3({\mathfrak{k}}_{-1})$, so ${\mathfrak{r}}\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{k}}_0\oplus{\mathfrak{k}}_1$, which
generates~${\mathfrak{k}}$.

For the cases 2) and 3) below the argument is the same, with $\Span(Q)$ instead of $\Span(q)$: it
is a~maximal isotropic subsuperspace of ${\mathfrak{k}}_{-1}$, resp. of ${\mathfrak{m}}_{-1}$, and its stabilizer is
a~maximal subalgebra of ${\mathfrak{k}}_0$, resp. of ${\mathfrak{m}}_0$, see \cite{ShM}.

2) To embed ${\mathfrak{g}}= {\mathfrak{vect}}(n+1|k)={\mathfrak{vect}}(q_1,\dots,q_n, \xi_1, \dots, \xi_k; y)$ into ${\mathfrak{k}}(2n+1|2k)$ generated by the same functions as in case 1) and $2k$ odd indeterminates $\xi_i$ and $\eta_i$ and the contact form 
\[
dt+\sum (p_idq_i-q_idp_i)+\sum (\xi_jd\eta_j-\eta_jd\xi_j). 
\]
We use the same arguments as in case 1) considering new dual pairs $\xi_i\leftrightarrow\eta_i$. The only difference in the main formula \eqref{mainF} of the answer: we have to replace \eqref{mainF} with
\begin{equation}\label{mainF1}
