<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:descent-invertibility}
Under \eqref{eq:lyaprange},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lyapineq}
 V(\bar F_h(z))\le V(z)-c_h(\sin z)^TK\sin z,
\end{equation}
so $V(\bar F_h(z))<V(z)$ for $z\notin\operatorname{Crit}(V)$. Under
\eqref{eq:diffeorange}, $\bar F_h$ is a diffeomorphism of $\mathcal Z$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Taylor's inequality for $\Psi$, whose Hessian has norm at most one,
applied to the increment $hK\sin z$, gives
\[
 V(\bar F_h(z))\le V(z)-h(\sin z)^TK\sin z
             +\frac{h^2}{2}(\sin z)^TK^2\sin z.
\]
Since $K^2\le\Lambda_{n}K$ as quadratic forms, this proves
\eqref{eq:lyapineq}. Since $c_h>0$, the correction term vanishes exactly
when $K\sin z=0$, that is, on $\operatorname{Crit}(V)$ by \eqref{eq:critcondition}.

For \eqref{eq:diffeorange}, the derivative on $E$ is
\[
 D\bar F_h(z)|_E=I+hKD(z)|_E.
\]
Since $\|KD(z)|_E\|_2\le\Lambda_{n}$, it is invertible when
$h\Lambda_{n}<1$. Hence every map of the homotopy
$z\mapsto z+thK\sin z$, $0\le t\le1$, is a local diffeomorphism, and
$\bar F_h$ has degree one.  A local diffeomorphism of the compact
connected torus $\mathcal Z$ is a covering map; degree one makes it
one-sheeted and hence a global diffeomorphism.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:global}
Under \eqref{eq:lyaprange}, $\operatorname{Fix}(\bar F_h)=\operatorname{Crit}(V)$,
every periodic point is fixed, and every forward orbit converges to a
single critical point.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Critical points are fixed by \eqref{eq:gradientreduced}.  Conversely, a
fixed point leaves $V$ unchanged, so Theorem~\ref{thm:descent-invertibility}
forces criticality.  The same strict decrease excludes every non-fixed
periodic orbit.

Write $z_m=\bar F_h^{\,m}(z_0)$.  The descent estimate implies
$(\sin z_m)^TK\sin z_m\to0$.  Hence
$\operatorname{dist}(z_m,\operatorname{Crit}(V))\to0$, every accumulation point is
critical, and all accumulation points have the common potential value
$V_\infty=\lim_mV(z_m)$.  In the reduced metric,
\begin{equation*}
 \|\operatorname{grad}_g V(z)\|_g^2=(\sin z)^TK\sin z,
\end{equation*}
and the length of the update is
\begin{equation*}
 \delta_m=\|hK\sin z_m\|_g
         =h\|\operatorname{grad}_g V(z_m)\|_g.
\end{equation*}
The distance between successive points of $\mathcal Z$ is at most
$\delta_m$.

Let $\omega$ be the set of accumulation points of $(z_m)$. It is
nonempty and compact, $\operatorname{dist}(z_m,\omega)\to0$, and by
the above $\omega\subset\operatorname{Crit}(V)\cap V^{-1}(V_\infty)$. Near each
$p\in\omega$, analyticity gives the \L{}ojasiewicz gradient inequality
\begin{equation*}
 |V(z)-V_\infty|^{1-\vartheta_p}
 \le C_p\|\operatorname{grad}_g V(z)\|_g,
 \qquad C_p>0,\quad 0<\vartheta_p\le\tfrac12,
\end{equation*}
on a neighbourhood $U_p$ of $p$; see \cite{AbsilMahonyAndrews2005}.
By compactness, finitely many of these neighbourhoods,
$U_{p_1},\dots,U_{p_N}$, cover $\omega$; their union $U$ is a
neighbourhood of $\omega$, so $z_m\in U$ for all large $m$. With
$\vartheta=\min_i\vartheta_{p_i}$ and $C=\max_iC_{p_i}$, the inequality
\begin{equation*}
 |V(z)-V_\infty|^{1-\vartheta}\le C\|\operatorname{grad}_g V(z)\|_g
\end{equation*}
holds on $U\cap\{|V-V_\infty|\le1\}$, since lowering the exponent can
only decrease the left-hand side there. It therefore holds along the
tail of the orbit. Put $e_m=V(z_m)-V_\infty$.  If some
$e_m=0$, strict descent makes the orbit stationary.  Otherwise, for all
large $m$, $e_m<1$ and concavity of $t^\vartheta$ gives
\[
 e_m^\vartheta-e_{m+1}^\vartheta
 \ge\vartheta e_m^{\vartheta-1}(e_m-e_{m+1})
 \ge\frac{\vartheta(1-h\Lambda_{n}/2)}{C}\,\delta_m.
\]
Thus $\sum_m\delta_m<\infty$.  The orbit is Cauchy and therefore
converges to one critical point.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:hyperbolicity}
Under \eqref{eq:lyaprange}, every non-degenerate critical point $p$ of
$V$ is a hyperbolic fixed point of $\bar F_h$ with $\operatorname{ind}(p)$
multipliers outside the unit circle.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
At a critical point $p$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linearisation}
 D\bar F_h(p)|_E=I-hK_0H_p.
\end{equation}
The matrix $K_0H_p$ is similar to the symmetric matrix
$K_0^{1/2}H_pK_0^{1/2}$ and has the same inertia as $H_p$.  Its
eigenvalues $\eta$ are real and satisfy $|\eta|\le\Lambda_{n}$.  Under
\eqref{eq:lyaprange}, positive $\eta$ give $|1-h\eta|<1$, whereas negative
$\eta$ give $1-h\eta>1$.  Non-degeneracy excludes $\eta=0$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:full-convergence}
Under \eqref{eq:lyaprange}, every orbit of \(F_h\) converges to a
fixed point.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop:global}, the edge differences converge to a
critical configuration.  Every accumulation point of the full orbit
therefore lies on the corresponding common-phase fibre.  The quantity
\(\exp(i\mathbf1^T\theta)\) is constant by \eqref{eq:phasesum}; its level set
meets that fibre in only finitely many points.  Along a lifted orbit $x_{m+1}=\widetilde F_h(x_m)$,
$\widetilde F_h(x_m)-x_m=hB\sin(B^Tx_m)\to0$, since
$\sin z\in\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}$ at each reduced limit
and $B\mathbf1=0$.
A sequence on a compact metric space with a finite accumulation set and
successive distances tending to zero converges to one of its accumulation
points.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:null-preimages}
For every \(h>0\), \(\bar F_h^{-1}(N)\) is null whenever
\(N\subset\mathcal Z\) is null.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The function \(J_h\) is analytic on the connected torus \(\mathcal Z\)
and is not identically zero, since
\(J_h(0)=\det(I+hK_0)>0\) (compare
\cite[Lemma~2.4]{CheriditoJentzenRossmannek2024}).  Hence its zero set \(\Sigma_h\) has measure
zero.  The complement \(\mathcal Z\setminus\Sigma_h\) has a countable
cover by inverse-function neighbourhoods on which \(\bar F_h\) is a
local diffeomorphism.  Local inverse maps preserve null sets, while the
remaining part of every inverse image lies in \(\Sigma_h\).

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:classification}
If $n\not\equiv0\pmod4$, every critical point of $V$ is represented
exactly once by \eqref{eq:critconfig}--\eqref{eq:alphakr}, is
non-degenerate, and satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:index-general}
 \operatorname{ind}(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 n-1-k,&c_{k,r}>0,\\
 k,&c_{k,r}<0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 V(z)=d_kc_{k,r}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The observations preceding the theorem exclude equal multiplicities and
coincident branch values. Every critical point therefore has a unique
majority value $\alpha$, a unique exceptional set $A$, and
$\cos\alpha\ne0$. Equation~\eqref{eq:closurealpha} gives exactly the
angles in \eqref{eq:alphakr}. Conversely, each prescribed configuration
satisfies closure and has equal sines, so it is critical. Uniqueness of
$\alpha$ and $A$ shows that the parametrisation has no repetitions,
including for $k=0$.

Put $c=\cos\alpha$, so that $V(z)=(n-k)c-kc=d_kc$. The Hessian form on
$E$ is
\begin{equation*}
 Q_z(u):=u^TH_zu
 =-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\cos z_i\,u_i^2
 =c\sum_{i\in A}u_i^2-c\sum_{i\notin A}u_i^2.
\end{equation*}
For $k\ge1$, decompose $E$ into the zero-sum subspace supported on
$A$, the zero-sum subspace supported on its complement, and the line
generated by
\[
 w=n\mathbf1_A-k\mathbf1.
\]
These subspaces have dimensions $k-1$, $n-1-k$, and one, respectively,
and are mutually orthogonal for $Q_z$. The form has sign $c$ on the
first, sign $-c$ on the second, and
\[
 Q_z(w)=k(n-k)d_kc
\]
on the mixed direction. Since $d_k>0$ and $c\ne0$, this proves
non-degeneracy and \eqref{eq:index-general}. For $k=0$, the restricted
Hessian is $-cI$.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:critical-count}
If $n\not\equiv0\pmod4$, then $V$ is Morse with
$n\binom{n-1}{\lfloor (n-1)/2\rfloor}$ critical points.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
There are $\binom{n}{k}$ choices of $A$ and $d_k$ admissible angles
for each choice. Since
\[
 d_k\binom{n}{k}
 =n\left[\binom{n-1}{k}-\binom{n-1}{k-1}\right],
 \qquad \binom{n-1}{-1}=0,
\]
the sum telescopes:
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor (n-1)/2\rfloor}d_k\binom{n}{k}
 =n\binom{n-1}{\lfloor (n-1)/2\rfloor}.
\]
Non-degeneracy is part of Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:morseconsistency}
For $n\not\equiv0\pmod4$, the indices satisfy, as they must, the Morse
relation
\[
 \sum_{z\in\operatorname{Crit}(V)}(-1)^{\operatorname{ind}(z)}
 =\chi(\mathcal Z)=\chi(\mathbb T^{n-1})=0,
\]
where \(\chi\) is the Euler characteristic. The count in
Corollary~\ref{cor:critical-count} equals the cycle bound for isolated
complex solutions of the algebraic Kuramoto equations in
\cite{ChenDavisMehta2018}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:4l}
If $n\equiv0\pmod4$, then $\operatorname{Crit}(V)$ is the union of the
circles \eqref{eq:criticalcircle} and the configurations
\eqref{eq:critconfig}--\eqref{eq:alphakr} with $c_{k,r}\ne0$. These
configurations are non-degenerate, hence isolated, and satisfy
\eqref{eq:index-general}. Distinct circles meet exactly at $p_+$ and
$p_-$, and $V$ vanishes on every circle.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
For unequal multiplicities, closure gives \eqref{eq:alphakr}. The angles
$\pi/2$ and $3\pi/2$ solve it for every $k$, but their branch values
coincide and represent only $p_+$ and $p_-$. Every other angle gives a
uniquely represented critical point. Its non-degeneracy and index follow
from the Hessian calculation in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}, which uses only $d_k>0$ and
$\cos\alpha\ne0$.

Equal multiplicities give exactly \eqref{eq:criticalcircle}. Its
parametrisation is injective with nonzero derivative, hence an embedding.
Away from $p_\pm$, the two coordinate values determine the partition up
to interchange. Thus only complementary sets define the same circle, and
distinct circles have no other intersections. Finally, $V=d_{n/2}c=0$
on every circle.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:critical-counts-4l}
If $n\equiv0\pmod4$, there are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:numberisolated4l}
 \frac{n+2}{2}\binom{n}{n/2}-2^{n},
\end{equation}
isolated critical points and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:numbercircles}
 \frac12\binom{n}{n/2}
\end{equation}
critical circles.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Removing the two coincident-branch angles leaves
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{(n-2)/2}(d_k-2)\binom{n}{k}
\]
isolated points. The telescoping identity used in
Corollary~\ref{cor:critical-count}, together with
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{(n-2)/2}\binom{n}{k}
 =2^{n-1}-\frac12\binom{n}{n/2},
\]
gives \eqref{eq:numberisolated4l}. Complementary subsets define the same
critical circle and no others do, which gives \eqref{eq:numbercircles}.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:circle-hessian}
For $z\in\mathcal C_A\setminus\{p_+,p_-\}$, $H_z$ has $(n-2)/2$
positive and $(n-2)/2$ negative eigenvalues, and kernel
$T_z\mathcal C_A$. Moreover, $H_{p_\pm}=0$ and
$D\bar F_h(p_\pm)|_E=I$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Put $c=\cos\alpha\ne0$.  The zero-sum subspaces supported on $A$ and
$A^c$ each have dimension $(n-2)/2$, and the Hessian form is definite on
them with opposite signs.  The remaining direction is the tangent vector
\[
 w=\mathbf1-2\mathbf1_A.
\]
Writing $D=\operatorname{diag}(\cos z_i)$ gives
\[
 H_zw=-P_EDw=P_E(-c\mathbf1)=0.
\]
These three subspaces exhaust $E$.  At $p_\pm$ every cosine vanishes, so
$H_{p_\pm}=0$; equation~\eqref{eq:linearisation} then gives the identity
derivative.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Each critical circle is Morse--Bott away from $p_\pm$; the union of
circles is not a submanifold of $\mathcal Z$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:notextrema}
Near \(p_+\), write \(z=p_++y\) with \(y\in E\). Taylor expansion gives
\begin{equation*}
 V(p_++y)=\frac16\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_i^3+O(\|y\|^5).
\end{equation*}
The linear term vanishes because \(\mathbf1^Ty=0\). Taking
\(y=(2t,-t,-t,0,\ldots,0)\) gives \(V=t^3+O(t^5)\), with both signs
arbitrarily close to \(p_+\). At \(p_-\) the expansion has the opposite
sign. Hence neither intersection is a local extremum.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:circlemultipliers}
For \(z\in\mathcal C_A\setminus\{p_+,p_-\}\) and \(0<h<1/2\),
\(D\bar F_h(z)|_E\) has the multiplier one along \(T_z\mathcal C_A\),
\((n-2)/2\) multipliers of modulus less than one and \((n-2)/2\)
greater than one.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
The operator \(K_0H_z\) is similar to the symmetric matrix
\(K_0^{1/2}H_zK_0^{1/2}\), whose inertia is given by
Proposition~\ref{prop:circle-hessian}. Its eigenvalues \(\eta\) satisfy \(|\eta|\le4\),
since \(\|K_0\|_2=4\) and \(\|H_z\|_2\le1\).
By \eqref{eq:linearisation}, the corresponding multipliers are
\(1-h\eta\). A positive \(\eta\) gives modulus less than one, a
negative \(\eta\) gives a multiplier greater than one, and the kernel
gives the tangent multiplier one.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:degenerateunstable}
For \(0<h<1/2\), neither \(p_+\) nor \(p_-\) is Lyapunov stable.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Consider \(p_+\). By Theorem~\ref{thm:4l}, a sufficiently small closed
neighbourhood \(U\) contains no isolated critical point. Every critical
point in \(U\) therefore lies on a critical circle and has potential
zero. If \(p_+\) were Lyapunov stable, an initial condition sufficiently
close to it would have its entire forward orbit in \(U\).
Remark~\ref{rem:notextrema} allows such an initial condition \(z_0\)
with \(V(z_0)<0\). Proposition~\ref{prop:global} makes its orbit

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:strict-saddle-basins}
Under \eqref{eq:lyaprange}, \(\mathcal B(\mathcal S)\) has zero
measure.
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
We apply
\cite[Assumption~2.1, Theorem~2.2 and Corollary~2.3]{CheriditoJentzenRossmannek2024}
to the lift of the reduced map. Let $\varpi:E\to\mathcal Z$ be the
covering projection with deck group
$2\pi\mathbb Z^n\cap E$, and define
\[
 \widehat F_h(y)=y+hK\sin y,\qquad y\in E,
\]
so that $\varpi\circ\widehat F_h=\bar F_h\circ\varpi$. Put
$\widehat{\mathcal S}=\varpi^{-1}(\mathcal S)$, a set of fixed points of
$\widehat F_h$.

The cited assumption consists of conditions at each point of an
arbitrary set of fixed points; neither isolation nor countability is
required, and the cited corollary handles uncountable sets by
second countability. Since $\widehat F_h$ is analytic, it is
differentiable at every point of $\widehat{\mathcal S}$, with
first-order Taylor remainder locally $\varepsilon$-Lipschitz for every
$\varepsilon>0$. At lifts of isolated critical points,
Proposition~\ref{prop:hyperbolicity} shows that the derivative is
diagonalisable over $\mathbb R$ with an eigenvalue of modulus greater
than one; at lifts of points of critical circles other than $p_\pm$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:circlemultipliers} gives the same conclusion, the
tangent direction contributing a multiplier equal to one. By the cited
theorem, every point whose forward orbit remains near such a fixed
point lies in a Lipschitz graph over the centre-stable subspace, which
has codimension at least one. The cited corollary, applied on
$E\simeq\mathbb R^{n-1}$, yields a null set $\widehat N\subset E$ such
that every orbit of $\widehat F_h$ converging to a point of
$\widehat{\mathcal S}$ eventually enters $\widehat N$.

Now let $\bar F_h^{\,k}(z_0)\to p\in\mathcal S$, choose a lift $y_0$ of
$z_0$, and put $y_k=\widehat F_h^{\,k}(y_0)$, a lift of
$\bar F_h^{\,k}(z_0)$. Then $\|y_{k+1}-y_k\|=h\|K\sin y_k\|\to0$ and
$\operatorname{dist}(y_k,\varpi^{-1}(p))\to0$. Since $\varpi^{-1}(p)$
is a translate of a lattice,
let $\lambda_k\in\varpi^{-1}(p)$ be a nearest lattice
point to $y_k$. Then
\[
 \|\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_k\|
 \le \operatorname{dist}(y_{k+1},\varpi^{-1}(p))
    +\|y_{k+1}-y_k\|
    +\operatorname{dist}(y_k,\varpi^{-1}(p))
 \longrightarrow 0.
\]
The lattice has positive minimum separation, so $\lambda_k$ is
eventually constant. Hence $y_k$ converges to a single point of
$\widehat{\mathcal S}$, and therefore
$y_0\in\bigcup_{k\ge0}\widehat F_h^{-k}(\widehat N)$. As $\varpi$ is a
local diffeomorphism, $N:=\varpi(\widehat N)$ is null in $\mathcal Z$, and
\[
 \mathcal B(\mathcal S)\subset\bigcup_{k\ge0}\bar F_h^{-k}(N).
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:null-preimages} makes every set in this union null.

\label{lem:angular-limit}
For every such orbit, there is \(\kappa\ge0\) such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:angular-critical-limit}
 \phi(u_k)\to \kappa,

<!-- proof-20: body_md -->

for all \(k\).  By \eqref{eq:local-potential-cubic},
\[
 W(r_ku_k)=r_k^3\bigl(\phi(u_k)+O(r_k^2)\bigr),
\]
so every angular accumulation point has non-negative \(\phi\)-value.

\[
 \tau_k=\sum_{j<k}a_j.

\[
 u_{k+1}=u_k-a_k\operatorname{grad}_{S_g}\phi(u_k)+O(a_k^2),

\(\widehat u(\tau)\).  The vector field
\(-\operatorname{grad}_{S_g}\phi\) is smooth on the compact sphere.

a discrete Gronwall inequality gives
\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}
 d_g\bigl(\widehat u(\tau_k+t),
              \Phi_t(u_k)\bigr)
 \le C_T\sup_{j\ge k}a_j\longrightarrow0,
\]
where \(\Phi_t\) is the flow of

Indeed, the accumulated one-step error over any slow-time interval of
length at most \(T+\sup a_j\) is bounded by
\(C\sum a_j^2\le C_T\sup_{j\ge k}a_j\).

\(\Phi\).

chain transitive.  Since \(\phi\) decreases strictly along

\(\operatorname{Crit}(\phi)\) and that \(\phi\) is constant there.
The sequence \((u_k)\) has the same limit set as its interpolation,
because \(d_g(u_{k+1},u_k)=O(a_k)\to0\), and the constant value \(\kappa\)
is non-negative by the first paragraph.

<!-- proposition-9: body_md -->
\label{prop:local-degenerate-basin}
The set \(\mathcal B^+_{\rm loc}\) has zero measure.
<!-- end proposition-9 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Choose a convex ball \(U\) small enough that
\(\sup_U\|DG_h-I\|<1/2\).  Integrating \(DG_h-I\) along the
segment joining any two points of \(U\) shows that
\[
 \|G_h(x)-G_h(y)\|\ge\tfrac12\|x-y\|,
 \qquad x,y\in U.
\]
Thus \(G_h\) is injective on \(U\) with positive Jacobian, and no
nonzero orbit remaining in \(U\) reaches the origin in finite time. For
such an orbit in \(\mathcal B^+_{\rm loc}\), let \(\kappa\) be given by
Lemma~\ref{lem:angular-limit}.

Suppose first that \(\kappa>0\).  By \eqref{eq:radial-blowup},
\[
 r_{k+1}=r_k-3h\kappa\,r_k^2+o(r_k^2).
\]
Hence \(1/r_{k+1}-1/r_k\to3h\kappa\), so \(r_k\sim(3h\kappa\,k)^{-1}\) and
\(\sum_kr_k^2<\infty\). Write \(DG_h(y)=I+M(y)\) with
\(M(y)=-hK\operatorname{diag}(\sin y)|_E\). Then
\(\|M(y)\|=O(\|y\|_g)\) and
\[
 \operatorname{tr}_E M(y)
 =-h\operatorname{tr}(K\operatorname{diag}(\sin y))
 =-2h\sum_i\sin y_i
 =O(\|y\|_g^3),
\]
the cancellation of the linear term being the one behind
\eqref{eq:harmonic-cubic}. Since
\(\log\det(I+M)=\operatorname{tr}M+O(\|M\|^2)\), uniformly for
\(y\in U\),
\begin{equation*}
 \log\det DG_h(y)=O(\|y\|_g^2).
\end{equation*}
Hence the products
\(\mathcal J_k(y_0)=\det DG_h^k(y_0)\) converge to a number
\(\mathcal J_\infty(y_0)\in(0,\infty)\) along every orbit of this type.

Let \(\mathcal E_+\subset\mathcal B^+_{\rm loc}\) be the set of
initial points with \(\kappa>0\). If it had positive measure, some
\(\mathcal E_m=\{y\in\mathcal E_+:\mathcal J_\infty(y)\ge1/m\}\) would have positive
measure.
By Egorov's theorem, applied to \(\mathcal J_k\) and \(G_h^k\) on
\(\mathcal E_m\), both converge uniformly on a measurable subset
\(\mathcal E'\subset\mathcal E_m\) of positive measure; in particular
\(\mathcal J_k\ge1/(2m)\) on \(\mathcal E'\) for large \(k\). Since orbits stay
in \(U\), \(G_h^k\) is injective on \(\mathcal E'\), and the change of
variables formula gives
\[
 \operatorname{Leb}(G_h^k(\mathcal E'))
 =\int_{\mathcal E'} \mathcal J_k(y)\,dy
 \ge\frac{\operatorname{Leb}(\mathcal E')}{2m}>0
\]
for all large \(k\), whereas \(G_h^k(\mathcal E')\) lies in balls about
the origin with radii tending to zero. Hence \(\mathcal E_+\) is null.

It remains to exclude \(\kappa=0\). Then
\(\operatorname{dist}(u_k,\mathcal D_0)\to0\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:angular-limit}, \(\mathcal D_0\) is finite by
Lemma~\ref{lem:zero-directions}, and \(d_g(u_{k+1},u_k)\to0\) by
\eqref{eq:angular-blowup}. Since the points of \(\mathcal D_0\) are at
positive distance from one another, the vanishing increments prevent
\((u_k)\) from switching between neighbourhoods of different points;
hence \(u_k\to u_0\) for a single \(u_0\in\mathcal D_0\).
Write \(u_{0,i}=a\varepsilon_i\), with \(a>0\) and
\(\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}\).  The ray through \(u_0\) is
pointwise fixed by \(G_h\), since
\[
 G_h(ru_0)=ru_0+hK\bigl(\cos(ar)\mathbf1\bigr)=ru_0.
\]

Choose \(g\)-normal angular coordinates \(\eta\) near \(u_0\),
so that the Hessian matrix
\(H_\phi=D^2_{S_g}\phi(u_0)\) is symmetric and nonsingular by
Lemma~\ref{lem:zero-directions}.  Denote the radial and angular
components of the polar map by \(\rho(r,\eta)\) and
\(\mathcal U(r,\eta)\).  They extend smoothly to \(r=0\), where
\(\rho(0,\eta)=0\) and \(\mathcal U(0,\eta)=\eta\).
The angular part fixes \(\eta=0\) for every small
\(r\), and \eqref{eq:angular-blowup} gives
\[
 \mathcal U(r,0)=0,\qquad
 D_\eta\mathcal U(r,0)=I-hrH_\phi+O(r^2),
\]
with \(D_\eta^2\mathcal U(r,\eta)=O(r)\), uniformly near
\((0,0)\).  Taylor's theorem therefore yields
\begin{equation*}
 \eta_{k+1}=\bigl[I-hr_kH_\phi+R_k\bigr]\eta_k,
 \qquad
 \|R_k\|\le C\bigl(r_k\|\eta_k\|+r_k^2\bigr)=o(r_k).
\end{equation*}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:slow-dichotomy} with \(a_k=hr_k\)
and \(H_*=H_\phi\).  Because \(\eta_k\to0\) and
\(\sum_k r_k=\infty\), there are \(C,\gamma>0\) such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:angular-exponential}
 \|\eta_k\|\le Ce^{-\gamma\tau_k},
 \qquad \tau_k=h\sum_{j<k}r_j.
\end{equation}

The fixed ray gives \(\rho(r,0)=r\); moreover
\(D_\eta\rho(r,0)=0\) for small \(r\). Indeed, let
\(v\in T_{u_0}S_g\). Since \(\sin(ru_0)=\sin(ar)\varepsilon\) and
\(G_h(ru_0)=ru_0\ne0\), the \(g\)-norm is differentiable at
\(G_h(ru_0)\), and differentiating \(\|G_h(ru)\|_g\) at \(u=u_0\)
gives
\[
 D_\eta\rho(r,0)v
 =r\langle u_0,v\rangle_g
  -hr\sin(ar)
   \langle u_0,K\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon)v\rangle_g=0.
\]
The first inner product vanishes because \(v\) is tangent to \(S_g\),
and the second equals
\[
 \langle u_0,K\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon)v\rangle_g
 =u_0^T\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon)v
 =a\sum_i v_i=0,
\]
as \(v\in E\).  Writing \(G_h(ru)=ru+r^2Q(r,u)\) with \(Q\) smooth for small
\(r\ge0\), we get
\[
 \rho(r,\eta)=r\|u(\eta)+rQ(r,u(\eta))\|_g
             =r+r^2q(r,\eta),
\]
where \(q\) is smooth with uniformly bounded angular second
derivatives.  The two identities above give \(q(r,0)=0\) and \(D_\eta q(r,0)=0\),
so Taylor's theorem yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:radial-quadratic-angle}
 |\rho(r,\eta)-r|\le Cr^2\|\eta\|^2.
\end{equation}
Since $r_{k+1}=\rho(r_k,\eta_k)$, \eqref{eq:radial-quadratic-angle}
gives
\[
 \Bigl|\frac{r_{k+1}}{r_k}-1\Bigr|\le Cr_k\|\eta_k\|^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Using $|\log(1+t)|\le2|t|$ for $|t|\le\frac12$ and
\eqref{eq:angular-exponential}, we obtain, for large $k$,
\[
 |\log r_{k+1}-\log r_k|\le 2Cr_k\|\eta_k\|^2
 \le C'r_ke^{-2\gamma\tau_k}.
\]
Since $\tau_{k+1}-\tau_k=hr_k\to0$ and $1-e^{-x}\ge x/2$ for
$0\le x\le1$, for large $k$
\[
 r_ke^{-2\gamma\tau_k}
 \le\frac{1}{h\gamma}
     \bigl(e^{-2\gamma\tau_k}-e^{-2\gamma\tau_{k+1}}\bigr),
\]
and the right-hand side telescopes. Hence
$\sum_k|\log r_{k+1}-\log r_k|<\infty$, so $\log r_k$ converges to a
finite limit, contradicting $r_k\to0$.

The mechanism is the following. On the invariant ray the radius is
preserved exactly; off the ray, the radial change is quadratic in the
angular deviation $\eta$; and $\eta$ decays exponentially in slow time.
At the zero level the cubic term provides no radial contraction, so
nothing can drive $r_k$ to zero. Hence no nonzero orbit in
$\mathcal B^+_{\rm loc}$ has $\kappa=0$, and the origin itself has zero
measure.

index order. For sufficiently large indices these operators satisfy
\[
 \|\Phi_s(k,j)\|\le C e^{-\lambda(\sigma_k-\sigma_j)},
 \qquad j\le k,
\]
and the inverse products on the unstable block satisfy
\[
 \|\Phi_u(k,j)\|\le C e^{-\lambda(\sigma_j-\sigma_k)},
 \qquad j\ge k.
\]

Let \(x_k\to0\).  Variation of constants gives, for sufficiently large
\(k_0\),
\begin{align*}
 P_sx_k
 &=\Phi_s(k,k_0)P_sx_{k_0}
   +\sum_{j=k_0}^{k-1}\Phi_s(k,j+1)P_sR_jx_j,\\
 P_ux_k
 &=-\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\Phi_u(k,j+1)P_uR_jx_j.
\end{align*}
The second identity follows by writing the formula up to a finite
terminal index and letting that index tend to infinity, using
\(P_ux_k\to0\). Thus every convergent solution is a fixed point of the
associated Green operator on bounded sequences.

Fix \(0<\gamma<\lambda\), and increase \(k_0\) so that
\(\|R_j\|\le\varepsilon a_j\) for \(j\ge k_0\), with
\(\varepsilon>0\) arbitrarily small.  For every \(\beta>0\),
writing \(A_0=\sup_{j\ge k_0}a_j<\infty\), comparison with the
integral of an exponential over each
\([\sigma_j,\sigma_{j+1}]\) gives the two bounds
\begin{align*}
 \sup_{k\ge k_0}\sum_{j=k_0}^{k-1}
  a_j e^{-\beta(\sigma_k-\sigma_{j+1})}
 &\le \frac{e^{\beta A_0}}{\beta},\\
 \sup_{k\ge k_0}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}
  a_j e^{-\beta(\sigma_j-\sigma_k)}
 &\le \frac{e^{\beta A_0}}{\beta}.
\end{align*}
With $\beta=\lambda$, these bounds show that the Green operator has
norm $O(\varepsilon)$ on $\ell^\infty$. After multiplication by
\(e^{\gamma\sigma_k}\) the stable and unstable exponents become
\(\lambda-\gamma>0\) and \(\lambda+\gamma>0\), so the same holds on
\[
 X_\gamma=
 \{x:\sup_{k\ge k_0}e^{\gamma\sigma_k}\|x_k\|<\infty\}.
\]
Choosing \(\varepsilon\) sufficiently small makes both operators
strict contractions.
Since the inhomogeneous term $\Phi_s(k,k_0)P_sx_{k_0}$ belongs to
$X_\gamma$, the Green equation has a unique fixed point in $X_\gamma$.
As $X_\gamma\subset\ell^\infty$, this is also the unique bounded fixed
point, which is the given solution. Thus
\(
\sup_{k\ge k_0}e^{\gamma\sigma_k}\|x_k\|<\infty.
\)
This is \eqref{eq:slow-dichotomy-decay}; enlarging the constant absorbs
the finite initial segment.
