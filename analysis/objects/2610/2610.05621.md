\label{lem:GD}
Let
$
Y_i
\in
\{1,\ldots,\ell\},
$
be latent variables satisfying
$
\mathbb{P}(Y_i=r)
=
\pi_r,
\ 
r=1,\ldots,\ell.
$
Let
\[
T\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^m X_i,
\quad
V_i^*\coloneqq\frac{X_i}{T},
\quad i=1,\ldots,m.
\]
Then, 
conditional on
$
Y_1={j_1},
\ldots,
Y_m={j_m},
$
the random vector admits the stochastic representation
    
                       
                 
                         
    
\begin{align}\label{rep-est}
(X_1,\ldots,X_m)^\top
\stackrel{d}{=}
T(V_1^*,\ldots,V_m^*)^\top,
\end{align}
where
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]  $
T\mid 
\left\{
Y_1=j_1,\ldots,Y_m=j_m
\right\}
\sim
\text{Gamma}
\left(
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{j_i},
\lambda
\right),
$
    \item[(b)] 
$
(V_1^*,\ldots,V_m^*)^\top\mid 
\left\{
Y_1=j_1,\ldots,Y_m=j_m
\right\}
\sim
\operatorname{Dir}
(
\alpha_{j_1},
\ldots,
\alpha_{j_m}
)
$, and
    \item[(c)] 
\(T\) and
\((V_1^*,\ldots,V_m^*)^\top\)
being conditionally independent given
$Y_1=j_1,\ldots,Y_m=j_m$.
\end{enumerate}

S
=
\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i,
$

\frac{1}

\left(
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\pi_{j_i}

\left(
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{j_i}

(\alpha_{j_1},\ldots,\alpha_{j_m})}
\left[
g(V_1,\ldots,V_m)
\right],
\end{equation}

$
(V_1,\ldots,V_m)^\top
\sim
\operatorname{Dir}
(\alpha_{j_1},\ldots,\alpha_{j_m}).

\mathbb{E}

\mathbb{E}

g(X_1,\ldots,X_m)
\mid
Y_1,\ldots,Y_m
\right)
\right].

Y_1={j_1},

Y_m={j_m}.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The bias in Corollary \ref{cor:Bias} admits the following decomposition:
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Bias}(\widehat{I(g)},I(g))
&=\frac{1}{mC_g}
\Bigg\{
\mathbb{E}\Bigg[
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{Y_i}\right)
\mathbb{E}_{\operatorname{Dir}(\alpha_{Y_1},\ldots,\alpha_{Y_m})}
\left[g(V_1,\ldots,V_m)\right]
\Bigg]
\left(
n\mathbb{E}\left[
\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{Y_i}}
\right]
-\frac{1}{\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\pi_r\alpha_r}
\right)
\\[0,2cm]
&+
n\operatorname{Cov}\Bigg(
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{Y_i}\right)
\mathbb{E}_{\operatorname{Dir}(\alpha_{Y_1},\ldots,\alpha_{Y_m})}
\left[g(V_1,\ldots,V_m)\right],
\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{Y_i}}
\Bigg)
\Bigg\}.
\end{align*}
Since the function $x\mapsto 1/x$ is convex on $(0,\infty)$, Jensen's inequality gives
\[
\mathbb{E}\left[
\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{Y_i}}
\right]
\geqslant
\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{Y_i}\right]}
=
\frac{1}{n\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\pi_r\alpha_r}.
\]
Hence,
\[
n\mathbb{E}\left[
\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{Y_i}}
\right]
-\frac{1}{\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\pi_r\alpha_r}
\geqslant 0.
\]
Moreover, since $g\geqslant 0$, the expectation multiplying this nonnegative quantity is also nonnegative. Therefore, the first contribution to the bias is nonnegative. The sign of the bias as a whole, however, depends on the covariance term. In particular, a nonnegative covariance guarantees a nonnegative bias, whereas a negative covariance does not necessarily imply a negative bias: it must be sufficiently negative to offset the first contribution. Thus, the sign of the bias cannot, in general, be determined solely from the nonnegativity of $g$ or from Jensen's inequality.

\frac{1}{\mu_\alpha}.

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{j_i}

\frac{n}{
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{j_i}

$q_0=\max\{0,2m-n\}$ and $d_q=2m-q$.

Consequently,
$
\operatorname{MSE}[\widehat{I(g)}]
=
\operatorname{Var}[\widehat{I(g)}].
$
<!-- end corollary-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Since $\ell=1$, we have $j_1=\cdots=j_n=1$, $\pi_1=1$, and
$\alpha_1=\alpha$. The result then follows directly from
Theorem~\ref{thm:variance} and the identity
$
\sum_{q=q_0}^{m}
\binom{m}{q}
\binom{n-m}{m-q}
=
\binom{n}{m}.
$
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
When $m=n$, the only possible value of $q$ is
$q=q_0=n=d_q$. Therefore, by Corollary \ref{cor:variance-gamma},
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Var}[\widehat{I(g)}]
&=
\frac{1}{C_g^2}
\left\{
\mathbb E_{\operatorname{Dir}_{n}(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)}
\left[g(V_1,\ldots,V_n)^2\right]
-
\left[
\mathbb E_{\operatorname{Dir}_{n}(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)}
\left[g(V_1,\ldots,V_n)\right]
\right]^2
\right\}
\\[0,2cm]
&=
\operatorname{Var}_{\operatorname{Dir}_{n}(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)}\left[{g(V_1,\ldots,V_n)\over C_g}\right].
\end{align*}
Thus, when $m=n$, the variance reduces to the variance of
$g(V_1,\ldots,V_n)/C_g$ under the
$\operatorname{Dir}_{n}(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)$ distribution.
