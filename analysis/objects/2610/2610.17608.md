<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $M$ be a closed graph $3$-manifold. Then there are an integer
$N\geq 1$ and an injective homomorphism
\[
  \pi_1(M)\hookrightarrow \operatorname{GL}_N(\mathbb C).
\]
In fact, when $M$ is  nongeometric, it has a finite cover  whose fundamental group admits a faithful  representation in dimension $73$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:3-mfd-linear}
The fundamental group of every compact $3$-manifold is linear over
$\mathbb C$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
In \cite[Problem~3.37(b)]{K3}, it was further asked whether all $3$-manifold groups could have faithful linear representations with a uniform bound on the dimension. Our construction does not provide such a bound. One reason is that in \Cref{thm:main}, we do not have  good control over the index of the cover.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:reduced-junction}
Let $e,f$ be oriented edges with $t(e)=o(f)=v$, and let $g\in G_v$.
The triple $(e,g,f)$ is \emph{reduced} if
\[
f\neq\bar e,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
f=\bar e\ \text{ and }\ g\notin H_{\bar e}.
\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm-cyc-nor-form}
    Every
nontrivial element of $\pi_1(\mathcal G,v_*,T)$ is conjugate either
to a nontrivial element of a vertex group or to $\gamma_c$ for a
cyclically reduced closed path $c$ of positive edge length.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
To obtain such a representative, one first reduces it to a closed word.
If its closing junction is not reduced, a cyclic permutation,
which corresponds to conjugation, makes that junction an internal
one. Cancelling the resulting inverse edges and absorbing the
vertex-group factors gives a shorter closed word. Repeating this
procedure terminates at a vertex-group element or a cyclically
reduced closed path.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{defn-leading-block}
Let $k$ be a field.  A \emph{leading-block system of rank $d$} for
$\mathcal G$ consists of the following data.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\rm(\roman*)]

\item
For every vertex $v$, there is an $n$-dimensional $k$-vector space $V_v$ and a
faithful representation
\[
\sigma_v\colon G_v\longrightarrow \operatorname{GL}(V_v).
\]

\item
For every geometric edge $|e|\in\mathcal E$, there is an indeterminate $x_{|e|}$, such that the family
$\{x_{|e|}:|e|\in\mathcal E\}$ is algebraically independent over $k$.  Set
\[
K=k(x_{|e|}:|e|\in\mathcal E).
\]

\item
For every oriented edge $e$, there is a $K$-linear isomorphism
\[
B_e\colon V_{o(e)}\otimes_k K
\longrightarrow
V_{t(e)}\otimes_k K
\]
such that
\[
B_{\bar e}=B_e^{-1}
\]
and
\[
B_e\sigma_{o(e)}\bigl(\iota_e(h)\bigr)
=
\sigma_{t(e)}\bigl(\iota_{\bar e}(h)\bigr)B_e
\qquad (h\in G_e).
\]

\item
There is a fixed $d$-dimensional $k$-vector space $W$ such that, for
every oriented edge $e$,
\[
B_e\in
\operatorname{Hom}_k\bigl(V_{o(e)},V_{t(e)}\bigr)
   \otimes_k k[x_{|e|}^{\pm1}],
\qquad
\deg_{x_{|e|}}B_e\leq 1,
\]
and there are $k$-linear maps
\[
p_e\colon V_{o(e)}\longrightarrow W,
\qquad
i_e\colon W\longrightarrow V_{t(e)}
\]
satisfying
\[
[x_{|e|}]B_e=i_e\circ p_e.
\]
Here $[x_{|e|}]B_e$ denotes the coefficient of $x_{|e|}^{\,1}$ in
the Laurent-polynomial map $B_e$.

\item
For every reduced triple $(e,g,f)$, with $t(e)=o(f)=v$, 
the transition map
\[
p_f\sigma_v(g)i_e\colon W\longrightarrow W
\]
is invertible.

\end{enumerate}

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:leading-block}
If $\mathcal G$ admits a leading-block system of rank $d$ for some $d\geq 1$, then $\pi_1(\mathcal G,v_*,T)$ embeds in $\operatorname{GL}_n(K)$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We extend each representation $\sigma_v$ from $V_v$ to
$V_v\otimes_k K$ by extension of scalars, without changing the
notation.

For every vertex $v$, let $\tau_v$ be the unique path in the maximal
subtree $T$ from $v_*$ to $v$.  If $\tau_v$ traverses the oriented
edges
\[
a_1,\ldots,a_s
\]
in this order, define
\[
U_v=B_{a_s}\cdots B_{a_1}
  \colon V_{v_*}\otimes_k K
  \longrightarrow V_v\otimes_k K,
\]
and put
\[
U_{v_*}=I.
\]
Every $U_v$ is a $K$-linear isomorphism.

For $g\in G_v$, define
\[
\rho(g)=U_v^{-1}\sigma_v(g)U_v,
\]
and, for every oriented edge $e$, define
\[
\rho(t_e)=U_{t(e)}^{-1}B_eU_{o(e)}.
\]
Thus all the matrices $\rho(g)$ and $\rho(t_e)$ belong to
\[
\operatorname{GL}(V_{v_*}\otimes_kK)\cong\operatorname{GL}_n(K).
\]

We first verify the Bass--Serre relations.  Since
$B_{\bar e}=B_e^{-1}$, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho(t_{\bar e})
&=
U_{o(e)}^{-1}B_{\bar e}U_{t(e)}\\
&=
U_{o(e)}^{-1}B_e^{-1}U_{t(e)}
=
\rho(t_e)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Suppose that the geometric edge underlying $e$ belongs to $T$.  Then
\[
U_{t(e)}=B_eU_{o(e)}.
\]
Indeed, if the path from $v_*$ to $t(e)$ is obtained by appending $e$
to the path from $v_*$ to $o(e)$, this follows immediately from the
definition of $U_v$.  In the opposite case, the path from $v_*$ to
$o(e)$ is obtained by appending $\bar e$ to the path from $v_*$ to
$t(e)$, and hence
\[
U_{o(e)}
=
B_{\bar e}U_{t(e)}
=
B_e^{-1}U_{t(e)}.
\]
This gives the same identity.  Consequently,
\[
\rho(t_e)=I
\qquad (e\subset T).
\]

Finally, let $h\in G_e$.  Using the intertwining relation in
Definition~\ref{defn-leading-block}, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho(t_e)\rho\bigl(\iota_e(h)\bigr)\rho(t_e)^{-1}
&=
U_{t(e)}^{-1}
B_e\sigma_{o(e)}\bigl(\iota_e(h)\bigr)
B_e^{-1}U_{t(e)}\\
&=
U_{t(e)}^{-1}
\sigma_{t(e)}\bigl(\iota_{\bar e}(h)\bigr)
U_{t(e)}\\
&=
\rho\bigl(\iota_{\bar e}(h)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the defining Bass--Serre relations are satisfied.  Therefore
the preceding assignments determine a homomorphism
\[
\rho\colon
\pi_1(\mathcal G,v_*,T)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{GL}(V_{v_*}\otimes_k K)
\cong\operatorname{GL}_n(K).
\]

We now prove that $\rho$ is injective.  By the cyclic normal-form
theorem for graphs of groups (Theorem \ref{thm-cyc-nor-form}), every nontrivial element is conjugate
either to a nontrivial element of some vertex group or to an element
represented by a cyclically reduced closed Bass--Serre path of
positive edge length.  Since membership in $\ker\rho$ is invariant
under conjugation, it suffices to consider these two cases.

First suppose that the cyclically reduced representative has positive
edge length $m$.  Choose its initial vertex $v_0$ immediately before
an edge and write the corresponding closed path as
\[
c=e_1g_1e_2g_2\cdots e_mg_m.
\]
Put
\[
o(e_j)=v_{j-1},
\qquad
t(e_j)=v_j,
\qquad
g_j\in G_{v_j},
\]
where
\[
v_m=v_0.
\]
We read the indices cyclically and set $e_{m+1}=e_1$.  Cyclic
reducedness means that every cyclic junction
\[
e_j\,g_j\,e_{j+1}
\]
is reduced.  In particular,
\[
e_{j+1}=\bar e_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
g_j\notin H_{\bar e_j}.
\]


Note that the expression
\[
c=e_1g_1e_2g_2\cdots e_mg_m
\]
records the path in chronological order: one first traverses $e_1$,
then applies $g_1$ at $t(e_1)$, then traverses $e_2$, and so on.
Since our matrices act on column vectors on the left, compositions
are written from right to left.  Thus the group element corresponding
to $c$ is
\[
\gamma_c
=
g_mt_{e_m}\cdots g_2t_{e_2}g_1t_{e_1}.
\]
This does not mean that the path is traversed backward: the rightmost
factor $t_{e_1}$ acts first, followed by $g_1$, then $t_{e_2}$, and
so forth.



Substituting the definitions of $\rho(g_j)$ and $\rho(t_{e_j})$,
all intermediate factors $U_{v_j}^{\pm1}$ cancel.  Therefore
\[
\rho(\gamma_c)
=
U_{v_0}^{-1}A_cU_{v_0},
\]
where
\[
A_c
=
\sigma_{v_m}(g_m)B_{e_m}
\sigma_{v_{m-1}}(g_{m-1})B_{e_{m-1}}
\cdots
\sigma_{v_1}(g_1)B_{e_1}.
\]
It is therefore enough to prove that
\[
A_c\neq I.
\]
This is why we compute the leading coefficient of $A_c$, rather than
directly computing a coefficient of the conjugate
$\rho(\gamma_c)$.


Fix a geometric edge $|e|\in\mathcal E$.  For every $j$ such that
$|e_j|=|e|$, write
\[
B_{e_j}=\sum_{r\leq1}x_{|e|}^rB_{j,r}.
\]
Consider one term in the full expansion of the product defining $A_c$.
For every $j$ with $|e_j|=|e|$, suppose that the term
\[
x_{|e|}^{r_j}B_{j,r_j}
\]
is selected from the factor $B_{e_j}$.  Thus $r_j$ is the exponent
selected from the $j$th edge factor, and $r_j\leq1$.  Since the vertex
matrices contain no Laurent variables, the exponent of $x_{|e|}$ in the
resulting term is
\[
\sum_{\{j:|e_j|=|e|\}}r_j.
\]
Define $m_{|e|}:=\#\{j:|e_j|=|e|\}$, then there are $m_{|e|}$ summands in this expression, each at most $1$, so the
exponent is at most $m_{|e|}$.  Equality holds only when $r_j=1$ for every
$j$ satisfying $|e_j|=|e|$.


Consequently,
\[
M_c
=
\prod_{e\in\mathcal E}x_{|e|}^{m_{|e|}}
=
\prod_{j=1}^m x_{|e_j|}
\]
is componentwise maximal among the Laurent monomials occurring in
$A_c$.  Moreover, equality in every coordinate is possible only by taking
the coefficient of $x_{|e_j|}^{\,1}$ from every factor $B_{e_j}$.
Consequently, the coefficient of $M_c$ in $A_c$ is

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_c:=[M_c]A_c
={}&
\sigma_{v_m}(g_m)(i_{e_m}p_{e_m})
\sigma_{v_{m-1}}(g_{m-1})(i_{e_{m-1}}p_{e_{m-1}})
\\
&\qquad\cdots
\sigma_{v_1}(g_1)(i_{e_1}p_{e_1}) ~\\
={}&
\sigma_{v_m}(g_m)i_{e_m} 
\bigl(
p_{e_m}\sigma_{v_{m-1}}(g_{m-1})i_{e_{m-1}}
\bigr)\\
&\quad\cdots
\bigl(
p_{e_2}\sigma_{v_1}(g_1)i_{e_1}
\bigr)
p_{e_1},
\end{aligned}
\]
where $[M_c]A_c$ means that the coefficient of $M_c$ is taken in
every matrix entry.  When $m=1$, the product of the displayed middle
factors is understood to be the identity map on $W$.

For $1\leq j\leq m$, with cyclic indices, define
\[
T_j
=
p_{e_{j+1}}\sigma_{v_j}(g_j)i_{e_j}
\colon W\longrightarrow W.
\]
Since every junction $e_jg_je_{j+1}$ is reduced,
Definition~\ref{defn-leading-block}\textup{(v)} implies that every $T_j$ is invertible.
This includes the last-to-first transition
\[
T_m
=
p_{e_1}\sigma_{v_m}(g_m)i_{e_m},
\]
corresponding to the cyclic junction
\[
e_m\,g_m\,e_1.
\]

Using these transition maps, the leading coefficient becomes
\[
C_c
=
\sigma_{v_m}(g_m)i_{e_m}
T_{m-1}\cdots T_1p_{e_1}.
\]
Since
\[
T_m
=
p_{e_1}\sigma_{v_m}(g_m)i_{e_m}
\]
is invertible, the map $i_{e_m}$ is injective and the map $p_{e_1}$
is surjective.  Hence
\[
\sigma_{v_m}(g_m)i_{e_m}
\]
is injective, while $T_{m-1}\cdots T_1$ is invertible.  It follows
that
\[
\operatorname{rank}C_c=d.
\]
In particular,
\[
[M_c]A_c=C_c\neq0.
\]

Because $m>0$, the monomial $M_c$ is nonconstant.  The identity map
has zero coefficient at every nonconstant Laurent monomial, whereas
$A_c$ has the nonzero coefficient $C_c$ at $M_c$.  Therefore
\[
A_c\neq I,
\]
and consequently
\[
\rho(\gamma_c)\neq I.
\]

It remains to consider a cyclically reduced representative of edge
length zero.  Such an element is a nontrivial element $g\in G_v$ for
some vertex $v$.  Since $\sigma_v$ is faithful,
\[
\sigma_v(g)\neq I,
\]
and therefore
\[
\rho(g)
=
U_v^{-1}\sigma_v(g)U_v
\neq I.
\]
Thus every nontrivial element has nontrivial image under $\rho$.
Hence $\ker\rho=1$, and $\rho$ is faithful.

with $u_e$ a column vector and $\ell_e$ a row vector, then the coefficient of the

\label{prop:combination}
Let $k$ be a field and let $\mathcal G$ be a finite connected graph of groups with
injective edge maps.  For every vertex $v$, suppose that there is a faithful
representation
\[
 \sigma_v:G_v\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(V_v),
 \qquad \dim_{k}V_v=n.
\]
For every incidence $a$ at $v$, suppose that there are
\[
 Z_a^0\in V_v,
 \qquad
 \lambda_a^0\in V_v^*=\operatorname{Hom}_k(V_v,k)
\]
such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\lambda_a^0(Z_a^0)=1$;
\item $H_a$ fixes $Z_a^0$ and preserves $\ker\lambda_a^0$;
\item (pointed detector property) for every $g\in G_v$,

 \lambda_a^0\bigl(\sigma_v(g)Z_a^0\bigr)=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g\in H_a;
\]

 Q_e^0\sigma_{o(e)}(\iota_e(h))
 =\sigma_{t(e)}(\iota_{\bar e}(h))Q_e^0,
\]
and
\[
 Q_e^0Z_e^0=Z_{\bar e}^0,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\bar e}^0Q_e^0=\lambda_e^0.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Then there is a purely transcendental extension $\mathbb K/k$ and an injective
homomorphism
\[
 \rho:\pi_1(\mathcal G,v_*,\mathcal T)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb K).
\]

\label{lem:pointed-scaling}
Let $A=I_d+N\in\operatorname{GL}_d(k)$ with $N^2=0$, and let $c\in k^\times$.  There is a
$k$-linear automorphism $S_c\in\operatorname{GL}(\operatorname{Mat}_d(k))$ such that, for every $r\in k$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pointed-scaling}
S_c(A)=A,
\qquad
\tau_A\circ S_c=\tau_A,
\qquad
S_cU_A(r)S_c^{-1}=U_A(cr).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
The automorphism $S_c$ is noncanonical and depends on $c$; only its existence is used later.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Note that the operator $D=\operatorname{ad}(N)$ is nilpotent.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 1: a $D$-invariant decomposition.}
Because $A$ is a polynomial in $N$, one has $D(A)=\operatorname{ad}(N)(A)=0$.  Also $A^{-1}=I_d-N$
commutes with $N$, and the commutativity of trace gives, for every $Z\in \operatorname{Mat}_d(k)$, that
\begin{align*}
\,\tau_A(DZ)
 &=\frac{1}{d}\operatorname{tr}\bigl((NZ-ZN)A^{-1}\bigr)\\
 &=\frac{1}{d}(\operatorname{tr}(ZA^{-1}N)-\operatorname{tr}(ZNA^{-1}))=0.
\end{align*}
Hence $\tau_A\circ D=0$.  Moreover $\tau_A(A)=1$, so we have a decomposition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pointed-decomposition}
\operatorname{Mat}_d(k)=kA\oplus W,
\qquad W:=\ker\tau_A.
\end{equation}
Both summands are $D$-invariant. Indeed, $D(A)=0$, so $D$
vanishes on $kA$, while $\tau_A\circ D=0$ implies that
$D(W)\subseteq W$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 2: rescaling the Jordan chains of $D|_W$.}
By \eqref{eq:D-cube}, the nilpotent operator $D|_W$ satisfies
$D^3=0$.  Its minimal polynomial divides $x^3$, so it splits over $k$, and
$W$ has a basis partitioned into nilpotent Jordan chains of length at most
three.  On a chain
\[
v_0,v_1,\dots,v_{\ell-1},
\qquad
Dv_0=0,
\quad Dv_j=v_{j-1}\ (1\leq j<\ell),
\]
define
\[
S_c(v_j):=c^{-j}v_j.
\]
Then $S_cD|_WS_c^{-1}=cD|_W$ on that chain.  Doing this on every chain gives an
automorphism of $W$ with the same property.  Extend it to all of
$\operatorname{Mat}_d(k)$ by making it the identity on $kA$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:S-conjugates-D}
S_cDS_c^{-1}=cD,
\qquad
S_c(A)=A.
\end{equation}

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 3: preservation of the trace functional.}
Write $Z=\lambda A+w$ according to \eqref{eq:pointed-decomposition}.  Since
$S_c$ fixes $A$ and preserves $W$,
\[
\tau_A(S_cZ)=\tau_A(\lambda A+S_cw)=\lambda=\tau_A(Z).
\]

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Step 4: scaling the conjugation parameter.}
Using \eqref{eq:U-exponential}, \eqref{eq:S-conjugates-D}, and the fact that
the exponential is a finite polynomial in $D$,
\[
S_cU_A(r)S_c^{-1}
 =S_c\exp(rD)S_c^{-1}
 =\exp(rcD)=U_A(cr).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:pointed-scaling}.

S_r\in\operatorname{GL}(\operatorname{Mat}_6(\mathbb R))
\]
such that, for every $u\in\mathbb R$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scaling-identity}
S_rU(u)=U(ru)S_r,
\qquad
S_rK=K,
\qquad
\tau_KS_r=\tau_K.
\end{equation}

\smallskip
\noindent
\emph{Case 1: $b\neq0$.}
Set
\[
\mu_-=\frac{a}{b},
\qquad
\mu_+=-\frac{d}{b},
\]
and introduce the source parameters
\[
x:=p,
\qquad
y:=\mu_-p+q=\frac{a}{b}p+q.
\]
Then
\[
ap+bq=by,
\]
whereas
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_+(ap+bq)+cp+dq
&=
-\frac{d}{b}(ap+bq)+cp+dq\\
&=
\frac{bc-ad}{b}\,p
=
-\frac{\Delta}{b}\,x.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\widehat\sigma_-(p,q)=U(x)\oplus U(y)
\]
and
\[
\widehat\sigma_+^A(p,q)
=
U(by)\oplus U\left(-\frac{\Delta}{b}x\right).
\]

Since $b\neq0$ and $\Delta=\pm1$, both $b$ and $-\Delta/b$ are
nonzero. Define
\[
\widehat Q_e^0(P,Q)
:=
\left(S_bQ,S_{-\Delta/b}P\right).
\]
Using \eqref{eq:scaling-identity}, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat Q_e^0\widehat\sigma_-(p,q)(P,Q)
&=
\widehat Q_e^0\bigl(U(x)P,U(y)Q\bigr)\\
&=
\left(S_bU(y)Q,S_{-\Delta/b}U(x)P\right)\\
&=
\left(
U(by)S_bQ,
U\left(-\frac{\Delta}{b}x\right)S_{-\Delta/b}P
\right)\\
&=
\widehat\sigma_+^A(p,q)\widehat Q_e^0(P,Q).
\end{aligned}
\]
The map $\widehat Q_e^0$ is invertible, since it is the composite of
the block interchange with the invertible map
$S_{-\Delta/b}\oplus S_b$.

Moreover, writing
\[
\widehat Z=(K,K),
\qquad
\widehat\lambda(P,Q)
=
\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\tau_K(P)+\tau_K(Q)\bigr),
\]
the remaining identities in \eqref{eq:scaling-identity} give
\[
\widehat Q_e^0\widehat Z
=
(S_bK,S_{-\Delta/b}K)
=
(K,K)
=
\widehat Z
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\lambda\bigl(\widehat Q_e^0(P,Q)\bigr)
&=
\frac{1}{2}
\left(
\tau_K(S_bQ)+\tau_K(S_{-\Delta/b}P)
\right)\\
&=
\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\tau_K(Q)+\tau_K(P)\bigr)
=
\widehat\lambda(P,Q).
\end{aligned}
\]

\smallskip
\noindent
\emph{Case 2: $b=0$.}
In this case
\[
\Delta=ad\in\{\pm1\},
\]
so $a,d\in\{\pm1\}$. Set
\[
\mu_-=\frac{c}{d},
\qquad
\mu_+=0,
\]
and put
\[
x:=p,
\qquad
y:=\mu_-p+q=\frac{c}{d}p+q.
\]
Since $b=0$, the target parameters are
\[
ap+bq=ax
\]
and
\[
\mu_+(ap+bq)+cp+dq
=
cp+dq
=
dy.
\]
Therefore
\[
\widehat\sigma_-(p,q)=U(x)\oplus U(y),
\qquad
\widehat\sigma_+^A(p,q)=U(ax)\oplus U(dy).
\]

Define
\[
\widehat Q_e^0(P,Q):=(S_aP,S_dQ).
\]
Then \eqref{eq:scaling-identity} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat Q_e^0\widehat\sigma_-(p,q)(P,Q)
&=
\left(S_aU(x)P,S_dU(y)Q\right)\\
&=
\left(U(ax)S_aP,U(dy)S_dQ\right)\\
&=
\widehat\sigma_+^A(p,q)\widehat Q_e^0(P,Q).
\end{aligned}
\]
The map $\widehat Q_e^0$ is invertible, and again
\[
\widehat Q_e^0\widehat Z=\widehat Z,
\qquad
\widehat\lambda\widehat Q_e^0=\widehat\lambda,
\]
because $S_a$ and $S_d$ fix $K$ and preserve $\tau_K$.

Thus, in either case, we have constructed an invertible map
$\widehat Q_e^0$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:standardized-intertwining}
\widehat Q_e^0\widehat\sigma_-(p,q)
=
\widehat\sigma_+^A(p,q)\widehat Q_e^0,
\qquad
\widehat Q_e^0\widehat Z=\widehat Z,
\qquad
\widehat\lambda\widehat Q_e^0=\widehat\lambda.
\end{equation}

We now return to the original vertex coordinates. Define
\[
Q_e^0:=J_+^{-1}\widehat Q_e^0J_-.
\]
By definition of the standardized actions,
\[
\widehat\sigma_-(p,q)
=
J_-\sigma_{v_-}(\iota_e(h_{p,q}))J_-^{-1}
\]
and
\[
\widehat\sigma_+^A(p,q)
=
J_+\sigma_{v_+}(\iota_{\bar e}(h_{p,q}))J_+^{-1}.
\]
Therefore, using \eqref{eq:standardized-intertwining},
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_e^0\sigma_{v_-}(\iota_e(h_{p,q}))
&=
J_+^{-1}\widehat Q_e^0J_-
  \sigma_{v_-}(\iota_e(h_{p,q}))\\
&=
J_+^{-1}\widehat Q_e^0
  \widehat\sigma_-(p,q)J_-\\
&=
J_+^{-1}\widehat\sigma_+^A(p,q)
  \widehat Q_e^0J_-\\
&=
\sigma_{v_+}(\iota_{\bar e}(h_{p,q}))
  J_+^{-1}\widehat Q_e^0J_-\\
&=
\sigma_{v_+}(\iota_{\bar e}(h_{p,q}))Q_e^0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the edge-intertwining identity.

Finally, recall that
\[
Z_{a_\pm}^0=J_\pm^{-1}\widehat Z,
\qquad
\lambda_{a_\pm}^0=\widehat\lambda J_\pm.
\]
The pointed identities in
\eqref{eq:standardized-intertwining} therefore imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_e^0Z_{a_-}^0
&=
J_+^{-1}\widehat Q_e^0J_-
  (J_-^{-1}\widehat Z)\\
&=
J_+^{-1}\widehat Q_e^0\widehat Z
=
J_+^{-1}\widehat Z
=
Z_{a_+}^0
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{a_+}^0Q_e^0
&=
\widehat\lambda J_+J_+^{-1}
  \widehat Q_e^0J_-\\
&=
\widehat\lambda\widehat Q_e^0J_-
=
\widehat\lambda J_-
=
\lambda_{a_-}^0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves all the required identities.

For the reverse orientation, define
\[
Q_{\bar e}^0:=(Q_e^0)^{-1}.
\]
The reverse intertwining and pointed identities follow immediately by
inverting the preceding relations.

Write $g=wt_v^m$.  Note that $g\in H_{v,i}$ is the same as $w\in \langle c_i\rangle$, where $c_i$ is the group element represented by the corresponding boundary loop.  Conjugate the representation so that the fixed point of \(c_i\) is infinity and write
\[
\rho_v(w)=\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\\gamma&u\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U_i=\begin{pmatrix}1&\xi\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \xi\neq0.
\]
Since $\det\rho_v(w)=1$, direct multiplication gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Fuchsian-trace}
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\rho_v(w)U_i\rho_v(w)^{-1}U_i^{-1}\bigr)
 =2+\xi^2\gamma^2.
\end{equation}
The factor $\eta_v(w)$ cancels in the tensor commutator.  Recall that $Z_{v,i}^0 = (K_{v,i},K)$, so $
\sigma_v(g)(K_{v,i},K)
=
\bigl(\alpha_v(g)K_{v,i}\alpha_v(g)^{-1},
      \beta_v(g)K\beta_v(g)^{-1}\bigr)$. Consequently,

      \begin{equation}
\label{eq:act-trace}
\begin{aligned}
\lambda^0_{v,i}\bigl(\sigma_v(g)Z^0_{v,i}\bigr)
=\frac1{12}\Bigl(
&\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\alpha_v(g)K_{v,i}\alpha_v(g)^{-1}K_{v,i}^{-1}\bigr)\\
+&\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\beta_v(g)K\beta_v(g)^{-1}K^{-1}\bigr)
\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Recall that \[
\alpha_v(w)
=
\bigl(\rho_v(w)\otimes\eta_v(w)\bigr)\oplus I_2,
\qquad
K_{v,i}=(U_i\otimes I_2)\oplus I_2.
\]
Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_v(w)K_{v,i}\alpha_v(w)^{-1}K_{v,i}^{-1}
&=
\Bigl(
(\rho_v(w)\otimes\eta_v(w))
(U_i\otimes I_2)\\
&\hspace{1.5cm}\cdot
(\rho_v(w)^{-1}\otimes\eta_v(w)^{-1})
(U_i^{-1}\otimes I_2)
\Bigr)\oplus I_2\\
&=
\bigl(\rho_v(w)U_i\rho_v(w)^{-1}U_i^{-1}\bigr)
\otimes I_2
\ \oplus I_2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence the trace in
the first summand of (\ref{eq:act-trace}) 
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\alpha_v(g)K_{v,i}\alpha_v(g)^{-1}K_{v,i}^{-1}\bigr) = 2(2+\xi^2\gamma^2)+2=6+2\xi^2\gamma^2.
\]
The trace in the second $\operatorname{Mat}_6$-summand is $6$, because the image of $G_v$
under $\beta_v$ centralizes $K$; see the end of \S \ref{Section:rep-prod-group}.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:detector-value}
\lambda_{v,i}^0\bigl(\sigma_v(g)Z_{v,i}^0\bigr)
 =1+\frac{\xi^2\gamma^2}{6}.
\end{equation}
Over $\mathbb R$, this equals $1$ exactly when $\gamma=0$, which says that the
projective element $\bar\rho_v(w)$ fixes the cusp at infinity. But this is the same as saying $g\in\langle c_i,t_v\rangle$. In fact, let
$\Gamma:=\bar\rho_v(\pi_1(Q_v))\leq\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$ and let
$p(z)=z+\xi$ be the primitive parabolic corresponding to $c_i$.  Any element
of $\Gamma$ fixing infinity is represented by an affine map $h(z)=az+b$ with
$a>0$.  If $a\neq1$, then
\[
h^nph^{-n}(z)=z+a^n\xi.
\]
For one of the two directions $n\to\pm\infty$, these nontrivial translations
converge to the identity, contradicting discreteness of the Fuchsian group.
Hence $a=1$, so every element of the stabilizer is a translation.  The
translation parameters form a discrete subgroup of $(\mathbb R,+)$ and are therefore
cyclic.  Since we assume that $p$ is primitive (see the beginning of \S \ref{Section:rep-prod-group}), it generates that cyclic stabilizer.
Consequently
$\bar\rho_v(w)\in\langle\bar\rho_v(c_i)\rangle$.  Faithfulness of
$\bar\rho_v$ gives $w\in\langle c_i\rangle$.  There is no restriction on $m$,
so this is equivalent to $g\in\langle c_i,t_v\rangle=H_{v,i}$.

\label{lem:relative-product-cover}
Let $M$ be a closed, connected, orientable, irreducible nongeometric graph
manifold.   Then $M$ has a
finite connected cover
\[
\widehat M\longrightarrow M
\]
such that every Seifert block is a product $Q_v\times S^1$, where

By Wang and Yu \cite[Lemma 0.4]{WY}, the graph manifold $M$ is finitely covered by a graph manifold $\widehat M$ with each piece homeomorphic to $F_i \times S^1$, where $F_i$ is a compact orientable surface and the Euler characteristic $\chi (F_i)<0$. Kapovich--Leeb \cite[Lemma~2.1, especially Steps~1--3]{KapovichLeeb} prove that the graph manifold $\widehat M$ (as a manifold obtained by gluing finitely many Seifert manifolds with hyperbolic base orbifolds) has a further finite cover that has each piece a trivial circle bundle over orientable surfaces of genus $g\geq 2.$
