 N_{S_{r,R,\beta}}^{\mathrm D}\left(E\right)

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:order-continuity}
For $v\ge w\ge0$ and every $n\ge0$,
\[
 \frac{v^2-w^2}{R^2}
 \le\lambda_{v,n}-\lambda_{w,n}\le v^2-w^2.
\]
In particular, $n_v\left(t^2\right)$ decreases with $v$, vanishes for
$v>Rt$, and $M$ is continuous on $\left(0,+\infty\right)$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
{For every nonzero $u\in H^1\left(1,R\right)$, the difference between
the two Rayleigh quotients is}
{\[
 \frac{a_v\left[u\right]-a_w\left[u\right]}
      {\int_1^R s\left|u\right|^2\,\mathrm{d} s}
 =\left(v^2-w^2\right)
   \frac{\int_1^R s^{-1}\left|u\right|^2\,\mathrm{d} s}
        {\int_1^R s\left|u\right|^2\,\mathrm{d} s}.
\]}
{Since $R^{-2}\le s^{-2}\le1$, the last ratio lies in
$\left[R^{-2},1\right]$. Applying the min--max principle
to every finite-dimensional trial space gives both eigenvalue
bounds~\cite[Chapter~4]{Davies}. Taking $w=0$ and using
$\lambda_{0,n}\ge0$ gives $\lambda_{v,n}\ge v^2/R^2$.
Thus $n_v\left(t^2\right)=0$ when $v>Rt$.}

{We also need a uniform bound on the number of radial eigenvalues.
The inequalities $1\le s\le R$ give}
{\[
 \frac{a_0\left[u\right]}{\int_1^R s\left|u\right|^2\,\mathrm{d} s}
 \ge\frac1R\frac{\int_1^R\left|u'\right|^2\,\mathrm{d} s}
                         {\int_1^R\left|u\right|^2\,\mathrm{d} s},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{0,n}\ge\frac1R\left(\frac{\pi n}{\delta}\right)^2.
\]}
{Consequently, for $0<t\le B$ only
$n\le\delta B\sqrt R/\pi$ can contribute and the order support
is contained in $\left[0,RB\right]$.}

{Fix $t_*>0$. Each function $v\mapsto\lambda_{v,n}$ is continuous
by the two-sided bound and strictly increasing on
$\left[0,+\infty\right]$. Therefore the equation
$\lambda_{v,n}=t_*^2$ has at most one solution for each relevant
$n$, and these exceptional orders form a finite set. If
$t_j\to t_*$, then}
{\[
 n_v\left(t_j^2\right)\longrightarrow n_v\left(t_*^2\right)
 \quad\text{for almost every }v.
\]}
{The common support and radial-index bound just obtained allow
dominated convergence in the definition of $M$. This proves continuity.
It also shows why including a radial eigenvalue at its energy
threshold does not introduce a discontinuity after order integration.}

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:angular-transfer}
For normalized radii $1<R$ and $0<\beta\le2\pi$, let
$S=S_{1,R,\beta}$ and $p=\pi/\beta$.
For every $E>0$,
\[
 N_S^{<}\left(E\right)\ge\frac{M\left(\sqrt E\right)}{2p}.
\]
Consequently, Proposition~\ref{prop:continuous} implies
\[
 N_S^{<}\left(E\right)>\frac{\left|S\right|}{4\pi}E,
 \qquad \mu_k\left(S\right)<\frac{4\pi k}{\left|S\right|}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
More generally, any lower bound $M\left(t\right)>Dt^2/4+tB\left(t\right)$
transfers to
\[
 N_S^{<}\left(E\right)>\frac{\left|S\right|}{4\pi}E
              +\frac{\sqrt E}{2p}B\left(\sqrt E\right).
\]
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
{\color{revision}Write $N_S^{\le}\left(E\right)$ for the Neumann count including
eigenvalues equal to $E$. The angular Neumann eigenfunctions are
$\cos\left(jp\theta\right)$, $j\ge0$, each with multiplicity one.
Separation of variables gives}
{\[
 N_S^{\le}\left(t^2\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}n_{jp}\left(t^2\right).
\]}
{For $jp\le v\le\left(j+1\right)p$, monotonicity implies
$n_v\left(t^2\right)\le n_{jp}\left(t^2\right)$. Integrating on each
interval and summing gives the left-endpoint comparison}
{\[
 pN_S^{\le}\left(t^2\right)\ge\int_0^{+\infty}n_v\left(t^2\right)\,\mathrm{d} v
 =\frac12M\left(t\right).
\]}
{This comparison alone uses an inclusive energy cutoff. To obtain
strict eigenvalue inequalities, fix $E>0$ and let $E_j\uparrow E$
with $0<E_j<E$. The sector spectrum is discrete, so
$N_S^{\le}\left(E_j\right)\to N_S^{<}\left(E\right)$.
{\color{revision}Passing to the limit using the continuity of $M$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:order-continuity} gives the first inequality below;
Proposition~\ref{prop:continuous}, proved in Section~\ref{sec:neumann},
gives the strict second inequality:}}
{\[
 pN_S^{<}\left(E\right)
 \ge\frac12M\left(\sqrt E\right)
 >\frac D8 E.
\]}
{Since $D/\left(8p\right)=\left|S\right|/\left(4\pi\right)$, this gives the qualitative
strict counting inequality. Taking $E=4\pi k/\left|S\right|$ gives
$N_S^{<}\left(E\right)>k$: at least $k+1$ eigenvalues, starting with
$\mu_0$, lie strictly below $E$, and hence $\mu_k\left(S\right)<E$.
Conversely, the strict eigenvalue inequalities imply the strict
counting bound by choosing $k=\left\lfloor\left|S\right| E/\left(4\pi\right)\right\rfloor$
when $k\ge1$, and by the constant mode when $k=0$.
{\color{revision}For physical radii $r<rR$, the general remainder transfers as

 N_{S_{r,rR,\beta}}^{<}\left(E\right)
 >\frac{\left|S_{r,rR,\beta}\right|}{4\pi}E
   +\frac{r\sqrt E}{2p}B\left(r\sqrt E\right).
\]
This is the form used in Section~\ref{sec:neumann-quantitative}.}}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:saw}
Let $q\in C^1\left(\left[0,T\right]\right)$ have absolutely continuous
derivative. Suppose that $q'\left(0\right)=0$ and
$q''\le-\kappa<0$ almost everywhere. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-sided-rounding}
 -\frac{\sqrt2}{6\sqrt\kappa}
 \le\int_0^T\psi\left(q\left(v\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d} v
 \le\frac1{3\sqrt\kappa}.
\end{equation}
Both constants are sharp. More precisely, with
$a=\left\{q\left(0\right)\right\}$, the following phase-dependent bounds hold:
\begin{align}
 \int_0^T\psi\left(q\left(v\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d} v
 &\ge-\sqrt{\frac{2a}{\kappa}}
          \left(\frac12-\frac{2a}{3}\right),
 &&0\le a\le\frac12,\label{eq:rounding-phase-lower}\\
 \int_0^T\psi\left(q\left(v\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d} v
 &\le\frac{2\sqrt2}{3\sqrt\kappa}
          \left(a-\frac12\right)^{3/2},
 &&\frac12\le a<1.\label{eq:rounding-phase-upper}
\end{align}
Consequently, the bounds relevant to the initial phases $1/4$ and
$3/4$ are
\[
a=\frac14:\quad
 \int_0^T\psi\left(q\right)\,\mathrm{d} v\ge-\frac{\sqrt2}{6\sqrt\kappa},
 \qquad
 a=\frac34:\quad
 \int_0^T\psi\left(q\right)\,\mathrm{d} v\le\frac{\sqrt2}{12\sqrt\kappa}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
{The case $T=0$ is immediate, so assume $T>0$.
Put $s\left(v\right)=-q'\left(v\right)$ and $y\left(v\right)=q\left(0\right)-q\left(v\right)$. Absolute continuity gives
$s\left(v\right)\ge\kappa v$, since $s\left(0\right)=0$ and $s'\ge\kappa$ almost
everywhere. Hence $y'=s$ and $y$ is strictly increasing away from
zero. Applying the chain rule for absolutely continuous functions,}
{\[
 s\left(v\right)^2=2\int_0^v s\left(z\right)s'\left(z\right)\,\mathrm{d} z
 \ge2\kappa\int_0^v s\left(z\right)\,\mathrm{d} z=2\kappa y\left(v\right).
\]}
{Set $Y=y\left(T\right)$, and let $v=v\left(y\right)$ be the inverse map for $0<y\le Y$.
The phase integral becomes}
{\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounding-phase-change}
 \begin{aligned}
 I:=\int_0^T\psi\left(q\left(v\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d} v
   &=\int_0^Y g_a\left(y\right)w\left(y\right)\,\mathrm{d} y,\\
 g_a\left(y\right)&=\psi\left(a-y\right),\qquad w\left(y\right)=\frac1{s\left(v\left(y\right)\right)}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}}
{The weight is positive and nonincreasing, and}
{\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounding-weight}
 0<w\left(y\right)\le\frac1{\sqrt{2\kappa y}},\qquad y>0.
\end{equation}}
{One may first change variables on $\left[\varepsilon,T\right]$ and
then let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$: the majorant in
\eqref{eq:rounding-weight} is integrable at zero. Since $q$ is
strictly decreasing on $\left(0,T\right)$, each integer level is attained
at most once. Thus the values of $\psi$ at the jumps have no effect
on the integral, although they determine the filled points in
Figure~\ref{fig:quadratic-sawtooth}.}

{We record both pairing signs, including every possible truncation.
If a negative half-period begins at $y=b$, then, up to its isolated
jump value,}
{\[
 g_a\left(b+t\right)=
 \begin{cases}-t,&0<t<1/2,\\1-t,&1/2<t<1.\end{cases}
\]}
{Its negative value $-z$ at $b+z$ is followed by the positive value
$z$ at $b+1-z$. The complete pair therefore has integral}
{\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounding-negative-positive}
 \int_0^{1/2}z\left[w\left(b+1-z\right)-w\left(b+z\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d} z\le0.
\end{equation}}
{For a truncation of length $0\le h\le1/2$ the integrand is
nonpositive. If $1/2<h\le1$, the integral over the prefix of
length $h$ is exactly}
{\[
 \int_{1-h}^{1/2}z\left[w\left(b+1-z\right)-w\left(b+z\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d} z
 -\int_0^{1-h}z\,w\left(b+z\right)\,\mathrm{d} z\le0.
\]}
{The first term matches every included positive value to an earlier
negative value; the second consists of the unmatched negative values.}

{For a positive half-period followed by a negative one, the values
$z$ and $-z$ occur at $b+1/2-z$ and $b+1/2+z$, respectively.
The complete pair has integral}
{\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounding-positive-negative}
 \int_0^{1/2}z
 \left[w\left(b+1/2-z\right)-w\left(b+1/2+z\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d} z\ge0.
\end{equation}}
{A prefix of length at most $1/2$ is nonnegative. For a longer prefix,
put $d=h-1/2\in\left[0,1/2\right]$. Its integral equals}
{\[
 \int_0^d z\left[w\left(b+1/2-z\right)-w\left(b+1/2+z\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d} z
 +\int_d^{1/2}z\,w\left(b+1/2-z\right)\,\mathrm{d} z\ge0.
\]}
{Here every included negative value has its earlier positive match;
only positive values can remain unmatched. These identities also
hold when $b=0$, by the integrability established above. Any tail
made of successive pairs is a finite collection of complete pairs
and at most one truncated pair, so its sign is the same. The two
matching arrangements are illustrated in
Figure~\ref{fig:rounding-pairing}.}

$a=\left\{q\left(0\right)\right\}$. Define on $0\le a\le1$

\begin{aligned}
 C_+\left(a\right)&=

 \displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}\left(a-1/2\right)^{3/2},

 \displaystyle\sqrt{2a}\left(\frac12-\frac{2a}{3}\right),

 \displaystyle\int_{a-1/2}^{1/2}
       \frac{z}{\sqrt{2\left(a-1/2+z\right)}}\,\mathrm{d} z,
       &1/2\le a\le1.
 \end{cases}

 -R U\left(R,\lambda\right)\,

{Set $F_t\left(x\right)=h t g_R\left(px/t\right)$ on the real interval $\left(t/p\right)I$.
By \eqref{eq:Bq-action-curvature}, $F_t'$ is monotone with a

{\[
 \frac{a}{t}\le\left|F_t''\left(x\right)\right|\le\frac{b}{t}
\]}
{for positive constants $a,b$ depending only on the fixed data.
For $0<\varepsilon<1/4$, remove the set}
{\[
 \left\{x:\operatorname{dist}\left(F_t'\left(x\right),\mathbb Z\right)<\varepsilon\right\}.
\]}
{There are only a fixed number of its component intervals, and
their total length is $O\left(\varepsilon t\right)$, by the lower derivative
bound.  Discard every integer index $j$ whose edge $\left[j,j+1\right]$
meets that set, together with the endpoint indices whose edges
are not wholly contained in $\left(t/p\right)I$.  This discards
$O\left(\varepsilon t+1\right)$ terms.}

{The retained indices form a fixed number of consecutive blocks.
On each block the differences
$d_j=F_t\left(j+1\right)-F_t\left(j\right)$ are monotone and belong to a single interval
$\left[m+\varepsilon,m+1-\varepsilon\right]$ for an integer $m$.
Put}
{\[
 u_j=e^{2\pi\mathrm iF_t\left(j\right)},\qquad
 a_j=\frac1{e^{2\pi\mathrm i d_j}-1}
       =-\frac12-\frac{\mathrm i}2\cot\left(\pi d_j\right).
\]}
{Then $u_j=a_j\left(u_{j+1}-u_j\right)$.  Summation by parts on a block
$A\le j\le B$ gives}
{\[
 \sum_{j=A}^B u_j
 =a_Bu_{B+1}-a_Au_A+
       \sum_{j=A+1}^B\left(a_{j-1}-a_j\right)u_j.
\]}
{The cotangent is monotone between successive integers.  Hence
$\sup\left|a_j\right|+\sum\left|a_{j-1}-a_j\right|=O\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\right)$ on the
block.  Restoring the discarded terms proves}
{\[
 \left|\sum_{pj/t\in I}e^{2\pi\mathrm i h f_t\left(pj\right)}\right|
       =O\left(\varepsilon t+\varepsilon^{-1}+1\right).
\]}
{Choose $\varepsilon=t^{-1/2}$ for sufficiently large $t$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:Bq-exponential-sum}.}

{The number of indices is $t\left|I\right|/p+O\left(1\right)$.  The exponential-sum
estimate first proves \eqref{eq:Bq-discrete-mean} for trigonometric
polynomials.  Uniform approximation by the Fej\'er polynomials
proves it for continuous
periodic functions.  Continuous majorants and minorants for
interval indicators then give it for step functions; upper and
lower step approximations give the stated Riemann integrable case.

Neumann spectral-sum estimates~\cite{Kroger,Laptev}. Let

 &=\left(2\pi\right)^{-d}\left|\Omega\right|,\\

          \left|\left\langle e_\xi,\phi_\ell\right\rangle\right|^2
 &=\int_\Omega\left|\nabla e_\xi\right|^2\,\mathrm{d} y
