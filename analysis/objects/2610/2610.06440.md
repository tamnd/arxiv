<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:fundgrp}
(\cite{sf}, Section 1) Let $A=\begin{pmatrix} a & b\\c & d\end{pmatrix},B=\begin{pmatrix} p & q\\r & s\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbf Z)$ with $AB=BA$ and $m,n\in\mathbf Z$. Put $M=M(A,B;m,n)$. The homotopy exact sequence of a fiber bundle gives the following short exact sequence of groups:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ses}
1\to\pi_1F\to\pi_1M\to\pi_1S\to 1.
\end{equation}
$\pi_1M$ is generated by $\alpha,\beta,\sigma,\tau$ with the relations
\begin{align*}
	&\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma & &\alpha^{-1}\sigma\alpha=\sigma^a\tau^c & &\beta^{-1}\sigma\beta=\sigma^p\tau^r\\
	&\alpha\beta\alpha^{-1}\beta^{-1}=\sigma^m\tau^n & &\alpha^{-1}\tau\alpha=\sigma^b\tau^d & &\beta^{-1}\tau\beta=\sigma^q\tau^s.
\end{align*}
Moreover, $\sigma,\tau$ come from the inclusion $\pi_1F\to\pi_1M$ and generate $\pi_1F$, and under the projection $\pi_1M\to\pi_1S$, $\alpha,\beta$ map to generators.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:section}
Let $M=M(A,B;m,n)$, with $A=\begin{pmatrix} a & b\\c & d\end{pmatrix},B=\begin{pmatrix} p & q\\r & s\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbf Z)$. Then $M$ has a section if and only if $\binom{m}{n}\in\langle A-I,B-I \rangle$, the span of the columns of $A-I$ and $B-I$.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We will use the presentation for $\pi_1M$ given in Proposition \ref{prop:fundgrp}. From the relations between $\alpha$ and $\sigma$, we see that $\alpha^{-1}\sigma^d\alpha=\sigma^{ad}\tau^{cd}$ and $\alpha^{-1}\tau^{-c}\alpha=\sigma^{-bc}\tau^{-cd}$. Putting these together gives
\[
	\alpha^{-1}\sigma^d\tau^{-c}\alpha=\sigma^{ad-bc}=\sigma.
\]
Hence $\alpha\sigma\alpha^{-1}=\sigma^d\tau^{-c}$. Similarly, we can write $\alpha\tau\alpha^{-1},\beta\sigma\beta^{-1}$, and $\beta\tau\beta^{-1}$ in terms of $\sigma$ and $\tau$. Also recall that $\alpha\beta=\sigma^m\tau^n\beta\alpha$.

Note that every element in $\pi_1M$ can be written in the form $\sigma^x\tau^y\beta^w\alpha^z$. Given some product of generators, we can use the above observations to move all the $\alpha$'s to the right (at the expense of picking up extra $\sigma$'s and $\tau$'s), then move all the $\beta$'s to the right.

Now suppose $M$ has a section. This induces a section $f:\pi_1S\to\pi_1M$ in the short exact sequence (\ref{eq:ses}). Using our above observations and the fact that $f$ is a section, we see that $f(\alpha)=\sigma^x\tau^y\alpha$ and $f(\beta)=\sigma^w\tau^z\beta$. Moreover, since $\alpha$ and $\beta$ commute in $\pi_1S$, $f(\alpha)$ and $f(\beta)$ must commute in $\pi_1M$. Thus
\begin{align*}
	&(\sigma^x\tau^y\alpha)(\sigma^w\tau^z\beta)=(\sigma^w\tau^z\beta)(\sigma^x\tau^y\alpha)\\
	&\sigma^x\tau^y(\sigma^d\tau^{-c})^w(\sigma^{-b}\tau^a)^z\alpha\beta=\sigma^w\tau^z(\sigma^s\tau^{-r})^x(\sigma^{-q}\tau^p)^y\beta\alpha\\
	&\alpha\beta\alpha^{-1}\beta^{-1}=\sigma^w\tau^z(\sigma^s\tau^{-r})^x(\sigma^{-q}\tau^p)^y\sigma^{-x}\tau^{-y}(\sigma^d\tau^{-c})^{-w}(\sigma^{-b}\tau^a)^{-z}\\
	&\sigma^m\tau^n=\sigma^w\tau^z(\sigma^s\tau^{-r})^x(\sigma^{-q}\tau^p)^y\sigma^{-x}\tau^{-y}(\sigma^d\tau^{-c})^{-w}(\sigma^{-b}\tau^a)^{-z}.
\end{align*}
This equation now only involves $\sigma$'s and $\tau$'s, and since $\pi_1F\to\pi_1M$ is an inclusion, we see that the powers of $\sigma$ and $\tau$ must match up. Writing $\binom{k}{l}$ to denote $\sigma^k\tau^l$, the above equation simplifies to
\begin{align*}
	\begin{pmatrix}m\\n\end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix}w\\z\end{pmatrix}+x\begin{pmatrix}s\\-r\end{pmatrix}+y\begin{pmatrix}-q\\p\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}-w\begin{pmatrix}d\\-c\end{pmatrix}-z\begin{pmatrix}-b\\a\end{pmatrix}\\
	&=\begin{pmatrix}s & -q\\-r & p\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}d & -b\\-c & a\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}w\\z\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}w\\z\end{pmatrix}\\
	&=(B^{-1}-I)v_1-(A^{-1}-I)v_2\\
	&=(B-I)v_1'+(A-I)v_2',
\end{align*}
where $v_1=\binom{x}{y}$, $v_2=\binom{w}{z}$, $v_1'=-B^{-1}v_1$, and $v_2'=-A^{-1}v_2$. Hence $\binom{m}{n}\in\langle A-I,B-I \rangle$.

Conversely, if $\binom{m}{n}\in\langle A-I,B-I \rangle$, then according to Proposition 2(3) of \cite{sf}, $M(A,B;m,n)$ is isomorphic to $M(A,B;0,0)$ as bundles, and $M(A,B;0,0)$ has a section by construction.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- dfn-1: body_md -->
A symplectic 4-manifold $X$ is called a \textit{symplectic Calabi-Yau surface}, or SCY for short, if $\kappa(X)=0$. As observed by Li (\cite{li} Proposition 2.4), if $X_0$ is a minimal model of $X$ and $K_{X_0}$ its canonical class, then $\kappa=0$ is equivalent to saying that $K_{X_0}$ is torsion in $H^2(X_0;\mathbf Z)$.
<!-- end dfn-1 -->
<!-- rmk-1: body_md -->
\label{rmk:scy}
Note that if $X$ is a symplectic 4-manifold with torsion canonical class $K$, then either $K$ is actually trivial or $X$ is an integral homology Enriques surface (in which case $K$ has order two); see Proposition 6.3 in \cite{li}. In this paper, all SCYs under consideration have $b_1\ge 2$ (since $b_1\ge 2$ for torus bundles; see \cite{sf}, Proposition 1), which rules out integral homology Enriques surfaces and their blowups, so our SCYs all have minimal models with $K=0$.

Observe that if $X$ is a SCY and $X=X_0\#n\overline\mathbf C P^2$, with $X_0$ minimal, then $0=c_1^2(X_0)=c_1^2(X)+n$. Hence if $X$ is a SCY and $c_1^2(X)=0$, then $X$ is already minimal. In this paper, all our SCYs will have $c_1^2=0$ by construction, and thus our SCYs are minimal. Combining this with our previous observation, all our SCYs have $K=0$. This will be implicit every time we invoke Corollary \ref{cor:homeo}.

\end{pmatrix}.
\]

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Using the presentation of $\pi_1M$ given in Proposition \ref{prop:fundgrp}, we see that $\sigma,\tau$, and $\beta$ all commute. Observe that $\alpha\beta\alpha^{-1}\beta^{-1}=\sigma^m\tau^n$ implies
\begin{align*}
	\alpha\beta\alpha^{-1}&=\sigma^m\tau^n\beta\\
	\beta&=\alpha^{-1}\sigma^m\tau^n\beta\alpha\\
	&=(\sigma^a\tau^c)^m(\sigma^b\tau^d)^n\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha,
\end{align*}
and hence $\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha=\sigma^{-ma-nb}\tau^{-mc-nd}\beta$. Thus $\pi_1M$ is isomorphic to $\mathbf Z^3\rtimes\mathbf Z$, where the $\mathbf Z^3$ subgroup is generated by $\sigma,\tau,\beta$ and the $\mathbf Z$-action by $\alpha$ is specified by
\[
	\alpha^{-1}\sigma\alpha=\sigma^a\tau^c,\;\;\alpha^{-1}\tau\alpha=\sigma^b\tau^d,\;\;\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha=\sigma^{-ma-nb}\tau^{-mc-nd}\beta.
\]

If we are given $\tilde A$ as
\[\tilde A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b & x\\c & d & y\\0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix},\]

\[ \begin{pmatrix}m\\n\end{pmatrix} = -A^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}.\]

\label{lem:conj}
Let $A,P\in\operatorname{GL}(3,\mathbf Z)$ and set $B=PAP^{-1}$. Then
\[ \mathbf Z^3\rtimes_A\mathbf Z\cong\mathbf Z^3\rtimes_B\mathbf Z.\]
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Define $\phi: \mathbf Z^3\rtimes_A\mathbf Z\to\mathbf Z^3\rtimes_B\mathbf Z$ by $\phi(v,n)=(Pv,n)$. In $\mathbf Z^3\rtimes_B\mathbf Z$, we have
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
	\phi(v,n)\phi(w,m)&=(Pv,n)(Pw,m)\\
	&=(Pv+B^nPw,n+m)\\
	&=(Pv+PA^nw,n+m)\\
	&=\phi(v+A^nw,n+m)\\
	&=\phi((v,n)(w,m)),
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
so $\phi$ is a homomorphism. $\phi^{-1}(v,n)=(P^{-1}v,n)$ is the inverse, so $\phi$ is an isomorphism.

Cancelling the leftmost $b_3$'s gives a relation equivalent to ($\phi(\gamma)$), so we can eliminate the surface relation.

We are done simplifying the relations; the remaining relations are ($\alpha'$), ($E'$), ($\gamma'$), ($G'$), ($\phi(\alpha)$), ($\phi(D)$), ($\phi(\gamma)$), and ($\phi(F)$). The relations ($E'$) and ($G'$) eliminate the generators $a_2,b_2$, so we are left with the following presentation of $\pi_1(X_k)$:
\[ \pi_1(X_k)\cong\langle a_1,b_1,a_3,b_3|(\alpha'),(\gamma'),(\phi(\alpha)),(\phi(D)),(\phi(\gamma)),(\phi(F))\rangle,\]
with
\[ a_1^2b_1=b_1a_1^2 \tag{$\alpha'$} \]
\[ [a_1b_1a_1^{-1},b_1]=1 \tag{$\gamma'$} \]
\[ a_3^2b_3=b_3a_3^2 \tag{$\phi(\alpha)$} \]
\[ b_3=a_1b_1a_1^{-1} \tag{$\phi(D)$} \]
\[ b_3=a_3b_1a_3^{-1} \tag{$\phi(\gamma)$} \]
\[ a_3a_1^{-2}a_3=b_1^{-k} \tag{$\phi(F)$} \]

It is not immediately clear from this presentation whether $\pi_1(X_k)$ is the of the form in Proposition \ref{prop:fundgrp}, so we make a change-of-basis. Replace the generator $a_1$ by
\[ c=a_3^{-1}a_1, \]
so that $c,b_1,a_3,b_3$ is our new generating set for $\pi_1(X_k)$.

Using the relations between $a_1,b_1,a_3$, and $b_3$, we observe the following relations between $c,b_1,a_3$, and $b_3$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cb1}
\begin{split}
	cb_1c^{-1}&=a_3^{-1}a_1b_1a_1^{-1}a_3\\
	&=a_3^{-1}b_3a_3\\
	&=b_1
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cb3}
\begin{split}
	c^{-1}b_3c&=a_1^{-1}a_3b_3a_3^{-1}a_1\\
	&=a_1^{-1}b_1a_1\\
	&=b_3
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\[ b_1^{-k}=a_3(a_3c)^{-2}a_3=a_3c^{-1}a_3^{-1}c^{-1} \]
\[ a_3c^{-1}a_3^{-1}=b_1^{-k}c \]
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ca3}
	a_3ca_3^{-1}=c^{-1}b_1^k
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:b13}
	[b_1,b_3]=[b_1,a_1b_1a_1^{-1}]=1
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ab3}
	a_3b_3a_3^{-1}=a_3^2b_1a_3^{-2}=b_1
\end{equation}
\[ b_3=a_3b_1a_3^{-1} \tag{$\phi(\gamma)$} \]

Conversely, suppose we are only given the relations (\ref{eq:cb1}), (\ref{eq:cb3}), (\ref{eq:ca3}), (\ref{eq:b13}), (\ref{eq:ab3}), and ($\phi(\gamma)$). We will derive the relations ($\alpha'$), ($\gamma'$), ($\phi(\alpha)$), ($\phi(D)$), and ($\phi(F)$):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[$\phi(\alpha)$:] Starting with ($\phi(\gamma)$) and using (\ref{eq:ab3}), we get
\[ b_3=a_3b_1a_3^{-1}=a_3^2b_3a_3^{-2}, \]
which is ($\phi(\alpha)$).

\item[$\phi(D)$:] Using $c=a_3^{-1}a_1$ in (\ref{eq:cb1}), we get $a_3^{-1}a_1b_1=b_1a_3^{-1}a_1$, and thus $a_1b_1=a_3b_1a_3^{-1}a_1$. Using ($\phi(\gamma)$) then gives $a_1b_1=b_3a_1$, which is ($\phi(D)$).

\item[$\alpha'$:] Using $c=a_3^{-1}a_1$ in (\ref{eq:cb3}), we get $a_3^{-1}a_1b_3=b_3a_3^{-1}a_1$, and hence $a_1b_3=a_3b_3a_3^{-1}a_1$. Now use (\ref{eq:ab3}) to get $a_1b_3=b_1a_1$. Thus $b_3=a_1^{-1}b_1a_1$. We derived ($\phi(D)$) above, which says that $b_3=a_1b_1a_1^{-1}$. We see that $a_1^{-1}b_1a_1=a_1b_1a_1^{-1}$, which is equivalent to ($\alpha'$).

\item[$\gamma'$:] We derived above that $b_3=a_1b_1a_1^{-1}$. Hence the relation (\ref{eq:b13}) becomes $[b_1,a_1b_1a_1^{-1}]=1$, which is ($\gamma'$).

\item[$\phi(F)$:] In the process of deriving (\ref{eq:ca3}) from ($\phi(F)$), we only used $c=a_3^{-1}a_1$; no other relations were used. Hence (\ref{eq:ca3}) and ($\phi(F)$) are equivalent.
\end{enumerate}
Hence under the two sets of generators $a_1,b_1,a_3,b_3$ and $c=a_3^{-1}a_1,b_1,a_3,b_3$, the sets of relations ($\alpha'$)-($\phi(F)$) and (\ref{eq:cb1})-($\phi(\gamma)$) are equivalent. So
\begin{gather*}
	\pi_1(X_k)\cong \langle c,b_1,a_3,b_3|[c,b_1]=[c,b_3]=[b_1,b_3]=1,\\
	a_3b_3a_3^{-1}=b_1,a_3b_1a_3^{-1}=b_3,a_3ca_3^{-1}=c^{-1}b_1^k\rangle
\end{gather*}
From this presentation, it is clear that $\pi_1(X_k)\cong\mathbf Z^3\rtimes_A\mathbf Z$, with $\mathbf Z^3$ generated by $\{b_1,b_3,c\}$, $\mathbf Z$ generated by $a_3$, and
\[ A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & k\\1 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & -1\end{pmatrix}. \]

If $k=2t$ is even, take
\[ P = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0\\0 & 0 & 1\\1 & 1 & t\end{pmatrix}, \]
so that
\[ PAP^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -t & 1\\0 & -1 & 0\\0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}. \]
By Lemma \ref{lem:conj} and Proposotion \ref{prop:torusbndl}, $X_k\cong M(T,I;1,0)$, where
\[ T = \begin{pmatrix}-1 & -t\\0 & -1\end{pmatrix}. \]
By Proposition \ref{prop:section}, this torus bundle does not have a section.

If $k=2t+1$ is odd, take
\[ P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & t\\1 & 1 & t\\2 & 2 & 2t+1\end{pmatrix}, \]
so that
\[ PAP^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -k & t+1\\0 & -1 & 1\\0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}. \]
By Lemma \ref{lem:conj} and Proposition \ref{prop:torusbndl}, $X_k\cong M(T,I;-t,1)$, where
\[ T = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -k\\0 & -1\end{pmatrix}. \]
By Proposition \ref{prop:section}, this torus bundle does not have a section.

The relations coming from the primed curves imply that $a_2$ and $b_2$ commute. Thus, given any $\begin{pmatrix} p & q\\r & s\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbf Z)$ we can let $\phi$ be an appropriate product of $t_{a_2}$'s and $t_{b_2}$'s so that $\phi(a_2)=a_2^pb_2^r$, $\phi(b_2)=a_2^qb_2^s$, and $\phi$ fixes the other generators. It is then not too difficult to determine the relations coming from the unprimed curves, now conjugated by $\phi$. When combined with the relations coming from the primed curves we get the following presentation for $\pi_1(X_\phi)$, where $X_\phi$ is the total space of the pencil (\ref{eq:hamadabreed}):
\begin{gather*}
	\pi_1(X_\phi)\cong\langle a_1,b_1,a_3,b_3|b_3=a_1^{2q+1}b_1^sa_1^{-1},a_3^2=a_1^{2p+1}b_1^ra_1^{-1},\\
	[a_1^2,b_1]=[a_1b_1a_1^{-1},b_1]=[a_1,b_1][a_3,b_3]=[a_3b_3a_3^{-1},b_3]=1\rangle.
\end{gather*}
It is not clear to the author how to recognize this as the fundamental group of a torus bundle.
