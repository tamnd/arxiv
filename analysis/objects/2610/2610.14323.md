<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
Let $q\in B(Q)$ and $a\in\mathbb C$.  If $\operatorname{Im} z>0$, then
\[
 F_a(z;q)=0
\]
if and only if $z^2$ is an eigenvalue of the half-line realization of
\eqref{eq:schrodinger} subject to \eqref{eq:robin}.  Zeros in the lower
half-plane are resonances in the usual meromorphic continuation sense.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
If $\operatorname{Im} z>0$, then the Jost solution decays exponentially at infinity and
therefore belongs to $L^2(0,\infty)$.  The equation
$F_a(z;q)=0$ is exactly
\[
\psi_x(z,0;q)-a\psi(z,0;q)=0,
\]
which is the Robin boundary condition.  Hence $\psi(z,\cdot)$ is an
eigenfunction.

Conversely, an $L^2$ solution for $\operatorname{Im} z>0$ is a scalar multiple of the
Jost solution, so the Robin boundary condition implies $F_a(z;q)=0$.
The resonance statement follows from the standard analytic continuation
of the Jost solution, exactly as in the Dirichlet discussion in
\cite[Section 1]{MSW}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
Let $Q,A>0$.  There exists $C=C(Q,A)$ such that, for
$q\in B(Q)$ and $|a|\le A$,
\begin{align}
 |F_a(x;q)|&\le C(1+|x|),\qquad x\in\mathbb R,
 \label{eq:realbound}\\
 |F_a(z;q)|&\le C(1+|z|)\mathrm e^{2|z|},\qquad z\in\mathbb C.
 \label{eq:complexbound}
\end{align}
Moreover, for $\rho\ge\rho_0(Q,A)$ and
\[
 |z-3i\rho|\le\rho,
\]
one has
\begin{equation}
 \left|
 \frac{F_a(z;q)}{iz-a}-1
 \right|
 \le \frac{C(Q,A)}{\rho}.
 \label{eq:normalizedbound}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The estimates follow from the transformation representation and the
corresponding estimates for the Jost solution used in
\cite[Lemma 3.2]{MSW}.  The only additional contribution is the explicit
linear term $iz-a$.  On the circle centered at $3i\rho$ the integral
terms in \eqref{eq:Frepresentation} are uniformly $O(Q)$ (the interval
of integration has fixed length), while $|iz-a|\ge c\rho$ for $\rho$
sufficiently large relative to $A$.  After division by $iz-a$ these
integral terms are therefore $O(\rho^{-1})$, which gives
\eqref{eq:normalizedbound}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
For every $Q,A>0$ there exist $C=C(Q,A)$ and $R_0=R_0(Q,A)$ such that
the number $N_a(r)$ of zeros of $F_a$ in $|z|<r$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 N_a(r)\le C(1+r),\qquad r\ge1.
 \label{eq:zero-count}
\end{equation}
Moreover, if
\[
 \Pi_a(R,z)
 =
 \prod_{|z_n|\ge R}
 E\left(\frac z{z_n}\right),
\]
then, for $|z|\le R/2$,
\begin{equation}
 |\Pi_a(R,z)-1|
 \le
 \frac{C(Q,A)|z|^2}{R}
 \exp\left(\frac{C(Q,A)|z|^2}{R}\right).
 \label{eq:tailproduct}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The proof is the Jensen-formula argument of \cite[Lemma 3.2 and
the subsequent canonical-product estimates]{MSW}.  The factor $iz-a$
contributes only logarithmically to the maximum-modulus estimates.
For $|a|\le A$, this changes the constants but not the order or the
linear zero-counting bound.  The estimate \eqref{eq:tailproduct} then
follows from the standard estimate
$|E(w)-1|\le C|w|^2$ for $|w|\le1/2$.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Let $Q,A>0$.  There exist constants $C=C(Q,A)$ and
$R_0=R_0(Q,A)$ with the following property.

Let $q,\widetilde q\in B(Q)$ and $a\in\mathbb C$, $|a|\le A$.  Suppose
that the zeros of
\[
 F_a(z;q)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 F_a(z;\widetilde q)
\]
coincide in $|z|<R$, including multiplicities.  Then, for $R\ge R_0$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \sup_{|x|\le R^{1/3}}
 \frac{|F_a(x;q)-F_a(x;\widetilde q)|}{1+|x|}
 \le C R^{-1/3}.
 }
 \label{eq:finite-stability}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Write the Hadamard factorizations in the form
\[
 F_a(z;q)=z^{m}e^{g(z)}P_{<R}(z)P_{\ge R}(z),
 \qquad
 F_a(z;\widetilde q)=z^{m}e^{\widetilde g(z)}
 \widetilde P_{<R}(z)\widetilde P_{\ge R}(z),
\]
where the common zeros in $|z|<R$, including their multiplicities, have
been collected in $P_{<R}=\widetilde P_{<R}$ and
$g-\widetilde g$ is affine.  The integer $m$ is the common multiplicity of
any zero at the origin.  Notice that the free factor $iz-a$ is not inserted
into either Hadamard product; it is used only for the high-energy
normalization in \eqref{eq:normalizedbound}.

Consequently,
\[
 \frac{F_a(z;q)}{F_a(z;\widetilde q)}
 =e^{g(z)-\widetilde g(z)}
   \frac{P_{\ge R}(z)}{\widetilde P_{\ge R}(z)}
\]
whenever the denominator is nonzero.  On
$|z-3i\rho|\le\rho$, the normalized estimate
\eqref{eq:normalizedbound} gives
\[
 \frac{F_a(z;q)}{F_a(z;\widetilde q)}=1+O(\rho^{-1}),
\]
while the tail-product estimate gives
\[
 P_{\ge R}(z)=1+O(\rho^2/R),\qquad
 \widetilde P_{\ge R}(z)=1+O(\rho^2/R),
\]
provided $\rho\le R/4$.  For $R$ large these estimates imply
$|\widetilde P_{\ge R}(z)|\ge1/2$, so division is legitimate and the
same bound holds for their quotient.  Hence
\[
 e^{g(z)-\widetilde g(z)}-1
 =O\left(\rho^{-1}+\frac{\rho^2}{R}\right)
\]
on that disc.  Put $h=g-\widetilde g=c_1z+c_0$.  On the high-imaginary disc the
quotient is nonzero and is $1+O(\rho^{-1})$, so a single analytic branch of
$\log$ may be chosen there.  The normalization at infinity fixes the branch,
and hence $h(z)=O(\rho^{-1})$ on that disc.  Since $h$ is affine, evaluating
at two points of the disc gives $|c_1|\rho+|c_0|\le C\rho^{-1}$, and
therefore $h(z)=O(\rho^{-1})$ throughout $|z|\le\rho$.

Choose $\rho=R^{1/3}$.  Then
\[
 \rho^{-1}+\frac{\rho^2}{R}\le C R^{-1/3}.
\]
The preceding estimates therefore give
\[
 \frac{F_a(x;q)}{F_a(x;\widetilde q)}=1+O(R^{-1/3})
\]
for real $|x|\le R^{1/3}$, away from the finitely many common zeros; the
identity extends across those zeros by analyticity.  Hence, using
\eqref{eq:realbound},
\[
 |F_a(x;q)-F_a(x;\widetilde q)|
 \le C(1+|x|)R^{-1/3},
 \qquad |x|\le R^{1/3},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:finite-stability}.  This argument uses $iz-a$ only through the
normalization estimate and never treats $iz-a$ as a zero factor of $F_a$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The factor $(1+|x|)$ in \eqref{eq:finite-stability} is natural and should
not be omitted: the Robin characteristic function has linear high-energy
growth, whereas the Dirichlet Jost function is asymptotic to $1$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:Robin-Fourier-decomp}
There is a function $\eta_a\in L^p(0,2)$, depending on $q,\widetilde q$
(and on $a$), such that, for real $z$,
\begin{equation}
 D_a(z)=B(0,0)+\int_0^2 \eta_a(t)e^{izt}\,dt,
 \label{eq:Robin-Fourier-decomp}
\end{equation}
where $D_a(z)=F_a(z;q)-F_a(z;\widetilde q)$ and
\begin{equation}
 \|\eta_a\|_{L^p(0,2)}
 \le C(Q,A,p)\bigl(1+\|\widetilde q-q\|_{L^p(0,1)}\bigr).
 \label{eq:Robin-ha-bound}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 2B(0,0)=\int_0^1(\widetilde q(s)-q(s))\,ds.
 \label{eq:B00-integral}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We use the transformation representation with $q$ as the reference
potential.  Thus $\psi$ denotes the Jost solution for $q$, while
$\widetilde\psi$ is obtained from $\psi$ by the transformation kernel
$B$:
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde\psi(z,x)=\psi(z,x)
 +\int_x^{2-x}B(x,t)\psi(z,t)\,dt.
 \label{eq:Robin-Fourier-proof-B}
\end{equation}
At $x=0$, differentiation of this identity gives the boundary relation
\eqref{eq:RobinB}.  The important point is that the first term in
\eqref{eq:RobinB} cannot be discarded: it is the scalar
$-B(0,0)\psi(z,0)$.

For the Fourier decomposition it is preferable to return to the
transformation representation for the individual Jost solutions.  At
$x=0$ we have
\[
 \psi(z,0)=1+\int_0^2K_q(0,t)e^{izt}\,dt,
\]
and, in the weak sense sufficient for the Fourier inversion argument,
\[
 \psi_x(z,0)=iz-K_q(0,0)
 +\int_0^2K_{q,x}(0,t)e^{izt}\,dt.
\]
The corresponding identities hold for $\widetilde q$.  Hence
\begin{align}
 D_a(z)
 &=B(0,0)
   +\int_0^2 \eta_a(t)e^{izt}\,dt,
 \label{eq:Robin-D-decomp}
\end{align}
where, with the signs determined by the definition
$D_a=F_a(q)-F_a(\widetilde q)$,
\begin{equation}
 \eta_a(t)=
 K_{q,x}(0,t)-K_{\widetilde q,x}(0,t)
 -a\bigl(K_q(0,t)-K_{\widetilde q}(0,t)\bigr).
 \label{eq:Robin-ha-explicit}
\end{equation}
Here we have used
$K_{\widetilde q}(0,0)-K_q(0,0)=B(0,0)$, which follows from
\eqref{eq:B0} by putting $t=0$.

We prove the required trace estimate directly from the characteristic-coordinate
Volterra representation of the transformation kernel.  This avoids any
appeal to a nonexistent pointwise derivative of $q$.  Put

\[
 K_q(x,t)=K_{0,q}(x,t)+H_q(x,t),
 \qquad
 K_{0,q}(x,t)=\frac12\int_{(x+t)/2}^{1}q(s)\,ds .
\]
In the characteristic coordinates
\[
 \xi=\frac{x+t}{2},\qquad \eta=\frac{t-x}{2},
\]
the remainder satisfies
\[
 (H_q)_{\xi\eta}(\xi,\eta)
 =-q(\xi-\eta)K_q(\xi-\eta,\xi+\eta),
 \qquad H_q(\xi,0)=H_q(1,\eta)=0.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 H_q(\xi,\eta)
 =\int_\xi^1\int_0^\eta
 q(s-v)K_q(s-v,s+v)\,dv\,ds .
 \label{eq:H-characteristic-representation}
\end{equation}
The usual Volterra iteration gives
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{0\le x\le1,\ x\le t\le2-x}|K_q(x,t)|
 \le C(Q).
 \label{eq:K-uniform-bound}
\end{equation}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:H-characteristic-representation} with respect to
its integration limits is legitimate for almost every $t$: the resulting
boundary traces are absolutely continuous in $t$, and all integrands are
locally integrable because $q\in L^1$ and $K_q$ is bounded.  Since
$x=\xi-\eta$ and $t=\xi+\eta$, we have
$\partial_x=\frac12(\partial_\xi-\partial_\eta)$.  Thus, on the boundary
$x=0$ (so $\xi=\eta=t/2$),
\begin{align}
 H_{q,x}(0,t)
 ={}&-\frac12\int_0^{t/2}
 q(t/2-v)K_q(t/2-v,t/2+v)\,dv \\
 &-\frac12\int_{t/2}^{1}
 q(s-t/2)K_q(s-t/2,s+t/2)\,ds .
 \label{eq:Hx-explicit}
\end{align}
In particular, the first term in $K_q$ is a genuine primitive of $q$:
\[
 K_{0,q}(x,t)=\frac12\int_{(x+t)/2}^{1}q(s)\,ds,
 \qquad
 K_{0,q,x}(0,t)=-\frac14q(t/2)\quad\text{a.e.}
\]
Thus there is no differentiability assumption on $q$ beyond $q\in L^1$.
Using \eqref{eq:K-uniform-bound} and Minkowski's integral inequality (or,
equivalently, the boundedness of the corresponding Volterra operators),
we obtain
\begin{equation}
 \|H_{q,x}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^p(0,2)}
 \le C(Q,p)\|q\|_{L^p(0,1)}
 \le C(Q,p)Q,
 \label{eq:Hx-Lp}
\end{equation}
for $1\le p\le\infty$.  More importantly, subtracting the corresponding
formulae for $q$ and $\widetilde q$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \|H_{\widetilde q,x}(0,\cdot)-H_{q,x}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^p(0,2)}
 \le C(Q,p)\|\widetilde q-q\|_{L^p(0,1)},
 \label{eq:Hx-difference-Lp}
\end{equation}
where we used, in addition to \eqref{eq:K-uniform-bound}, the standard
Volterra estimate
\[
 \|K_{\widetilde q}-K_q\|_{L^\infty(\triangle)}
 \le C(Q)\|\widetilde q-q\|_{L^1(0,1)}.
\]
Indeed, after subtraction each integrand is a sum of a term containing
$\widetilde q-q$ times one bounded transformation kernel and a term
containing $q$ times $K_{\widetilde q}-K_q$; the displayed estimate and
$\|\widetilde q-q\|_1\le\|\widetilde q-q\|_p$ give the stated bound.
Finally,
\[
 K_{q,x}(0,t)= -\frac14q(t/2)+H_{q,x}(0,t),
\]
so \eqref{eq:Hx-Lp} and \eqref{eq:Hx-difference-Lp} imply
\begin{align}
 \|K_{q,x}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^p(0,2)}&\le C(Q,p),\\
 \|K_{\widetilde q,x}(0,\cdot)-K_{q,x}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^p(0,2)}
 &\le C(Q,p)\|\widetilde q-q\|_{L^p(0,1)}.
 \label{eq:Robin-Kx-trace-estimate}
\end{align}
The $L^p$ estimate for the undifferentiated trace follows in the same way
from the representation of $K_q$ and is bounded by $C(Q)$.  Therefore
\eqref{eq:Robin-ha-explicit} satisfies
\eqref{eq:Robin-ha-bound}, with the slightly stronger dependence
$C(Q,A,p)(1+\|\widetilde q-q\|_p)$.

Finally, the diagonal identity for the transformation kernel gives
\[
 2B(x,x)=\int_x^1(\widetilde q(s)-q(s))\,ds,
\]
and $x=0$ gives \eqref{eq:B00-integral}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:Robin-Fourier}
Let $q,\widetilde q\in B(Q)$, let
$\widetilde q-q\in L^p(0,1)$, $1<p\le2$, and let $|a|\le A$.
Suppose that the zeros of $F_a(z;q)$ and
$F_a(z;\widetilde q)$ coincide in $|z|<R$, including multiplicities.
Then, with
\[
\beta=\frac{p-1}{3(3p-1)},
\]
one has
\begin{equation}
 |B(0,0)|
 \le C(Q,A,p)
 \bigl(1+\|\widetilde q-q\|_p\bigr)R^{-\beta}.
 \label{eq:corrected-B00}
\end{equation}
In particular, the complete zero data control the scalar boundary
contribution $B(0,0)$ at the rate $R^{-\beta}$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The proof has an additional step compared with the Dirichlet argument
in \cite[Section 4]{MSW}.  In the Dirichlet case, equation (11) of
that paper is a direct inverse Fourier transform for
$K_{\widetilde q}(0,t)-K_q(0,t)$.  In the Robin case this is no longer
true because the boundary functional contains a derivative.  The
extra scalar term $B(0,0)\psi(z,0)$ in \eqref{eq:RobinB} must therefore
be isolated before any Fourier inversion is used.

Set
\[
 r=R^\alpha,
 \qquad
 \alpha=\frac{p}{3(3p-1)},
 \qquad
 \beta=\frac{\alpha(p-1)}p
       =\frac{p-1}{3(3p-1)}.
\]
Let $\chi\in C_c^\infty((-2,2))$ be nonnegative, with
$\chi=1$ on $[-1,1]$, and put
\[
 \chi_r(z)=\frac1r\chi\left(\frac zr\right).
\]
Since $\int\chi_r=\int\chi$, equation
\eqref{eq:Robin-Fourier-decomp} gives
\begin{equation}
 B(0,0)
 =\frac1{\int\chi}
   \int_{\mathbb R}\chi_r(z)D_a(z)\,dz
 -\frac1{\int\chi}
   \int_0^2\eta_a(t)\widehat{\chi_r}(-t)\,dt.
 \label{eq:B00-average}
\end{equation}
Because $\chi_r$ is supported in $|z|\le2r$ and $r\ll R^{1/3}$,
the finite-data estimate \eqref{eq:Robin-low-frequency} yields
\begin{align}
 \left|\int\chi_r(z)D_a(z)\,dz\right|
 &\le C R^{-1/3}
   \int_{|z|\le2r}(1+|z|)|\chi_r(z)|\,dz \\
 &\le C R^{-1/3}(1+r)
 \le C R^{-1/3+\alpha}.
 \label{eq:B00-low}
\end{align}
Since $-1/3+\alpha<-\beta$, this contribution is
$O(R^{-\beta})$.

For the second term in \eqref{eq:B00-average}, Hausdorff--Young and
scaling give, with $p'=p/(p-1)$,
\[
 \|\widehat{\chi_r}\|_{L^{p'}(\mathbb R)}
 \le C r^{-1/p'}.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{align}
 \left|\int_0^2\eta_a(t)\widehat{\chi_r}(-t)\,dt\right|
 &\le C\|\eta_a\|_p r^{-1/p'} \\
 &\le C(Q,A,p)
    \bigl(1+\|\widetilde q-q\|_p\bigr)r^{-1/p'}.
 \label{eq:B00-HY}
\end{align}
Since $r^{-1/p'}=R^{-\beta}$, \eqref{eq:corrected-B00} follows.

\label{prop:Robin-B0-direct}
Let $1<p\le2$, put
\[
 \alpha=\frac{p}{3(3p-1)},
 \qquad
 \beta=\frac{p-1}{3(3p-1)},
 \qquad r=R^\alpha,
\]
and assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:Robin-Fourier}.  Then, for
$R$ sufficiently large and $|a|\le A$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{0\le t\le2}|B(0,t)|
 \le C(Q,A,p)\bigl(1+\|\widetilde q-q\|_p\bigr)
 R^{-\beta}.
 \label{eq:Robin-B0-direct}
\end{equation}
In particular, no additional pointwise estimate for $c_a$ is required.

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Put
\[
 H_a(t):=\int_t^2 \eta_a(s)\,ds.
\]
Since
\[
 D_a(z)-B(0,0)=\int_0^2\eta_a(s)e^{izs}\,ds,
\]
and $\eta_a\in L^p(0,2)$ with $p>1$, its Fourier transform belongs to
$L^{p'}(\mathbb R)$ by Hausdorff--Young.  The following inversion identity
is therefore understood first in the distributional sense and then pointwise
for the primitive $H_a$, because multiplication by $1/(iz)$ away from the
origin makes the high-frequency part absolutely integrable in $L^p$--$L^{p'}$
duality.  Fourier inversion gives, for $0\le t\le2$,
\begin{equation}
 H_a(t)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 \bigl(D_a(z)-B(0,0)\bigr)
 \frac{e^{-izt}-e^{-2iz}}{iz}\,dz,
 \label{eq:Robin-H-Fourier}
\end{equation}
with the quotient at $z=0$ interpreted by continuity.
We split the integral at $|z|=r=R^\alpha$.

For the low-frequency part we exploit the cancellation in the kernel rather
than estimating it by its absolute value.  First,
\begin{equation}
 \int_{|z|\le r}\frac{e^{-izt}-e^{-2iz}}{iz}\,dz
 =2\int_t^2\frac{\sin(rs)}{s}\,ds,
 \label{eq:Robin-H-kernel}
\end{equation}
so its absolute value is bounded by an absolute constant, uniformly in
$0\le t\le2$ and $r\ge1$.  Moreover,
\[
 \int_{|z|\le r}(1+|z|)
 \left|\frac{e^{-izt}-e^{-2iz}}{z}\right|dz
 \le C(1+r).
\]
Consequently, by \eqref{eq:Robin-low-frequency} and
\eqref{eq:corrected-B00},
\begin{align}
 |H_a^{\rm low}(t)|
 &\le C R^{-1/3}(1+r)
 +C|B(0,0)|
 \\&\le C(Q,A,p)\bigl(1+\|\widetilde q-q\|_p\bigr)
 \left(R^{-1/3}r+R^{-\beta}\right).
 \label{eq:Robin-H-low}
\end{align}
Since $r=R^\alpha$ and
$\alpha-1/3< -\beta$, this gives
\begin{equation}
 |H_a^{\rm low}(t)|
 \le C(Q,A,p)\bigl(1+\|\widetilde q-q\|_p\bigr)
 R^{-\beta}.
 \label{eq:Robin-H-low-final}
\end{equation}

For the high-frequency part, use
\eqref{eq:Robin-Fourier-decomp} and Hausdorff--Young.  Since
\[
 \left|\frac{e^{-izt}-e^{-2iz}}{z}\right|\le\frac{2}{|z|},
\]
we obtain, with $p'=p/(p-1)$,
\begin{align}
 |H_a^{\rm high}(t)|
 &\le C\|\eta_a\|_p
 \left(\int_{|z|>r}|z|^{-p}\,dz\right)^{1/p}
 \\&\le C(Q,A,p)
 \bigl(1+\|\widetilde q-q\|_p\bigr)r^{-1/p'}
 \\&=C(Q,A,p)
 \bigl(1+\|\widetilde q-q\|_p\bigr)R^{-\beta}.
 \label{eq:Robin-H-high}
\end{align}

proves
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{0\le t\le2}|H_a(t)|
 \le C(Q,A,p)\bigl(1+\|\widetilde q-q\|_p\bigr)
 R^{-\beta}.
 \label{eq:Robin-H-final}
\end{equation}

\eqref{eq:Robin-c-from-eta},

\]
Together with \eqref{eq:corrected-B00} and \eqref{eq:Robin-H-final}, this proves
\eqref{eq:Robin-B0-direct}.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:Robin-perturbed-characteristic}
Let $Q,A>0$.  There are constants $C=C(Q,A)$ and $R_0=R_0(Q,A)$ such
that, under \eqref{eq:Robin-zero-perturbation}, the quotient of the finite
paired genus-one products satisfies, on the contour $\mathcal C_r$ described
above,
\begin{equation}
 \int_{\mathcal C_r}
 \left|\log W_R^0(z)\right|\,|dz|
 \le C\varepsilon r\log R,
 \label{eq:Robin-logW-integral}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{z\in\mathcal C_r}|W_R^0(z)|
 \le \exp(C\varepsilon r).
 \label{eq:Robin-W-sup}
\end{equation}
Here $W_R^0$ is the quotient of the paired genus-one factors for
$0<|z_n|<R$; the common factor $z^m$ at the origin is removed first.
Slightly abusing notation, throughout this factorization the cutoff at $R$
is understood pairwise: both members of any paired pair are assigned to the
same one of the finite product and the tail product.  Thus no pair is split
by the radial cutoff.  The affine Hadamard factor is treated separately below.
Moreover, if
\begin{equation}
 \rho=R^{1/3},
 \label{eq:Robin-rho}
\end{equation}
then, on the disk $|z-3i\rho|\le\rho$,
\begin{equation}
 |W_R^0(z)-1|\le C\varepsilon\log R\,e^{C\varepsilon\log R}.
 \label{eq:Robin-W-high}
\end{equation}
Finally, after increasing $R_0$ if necessary, the affine quotient
\begin{equation}
 E_R(z):=e^{g(z)-\widetilde g(z)}
\end{equation}
satisfies, on $|z|\le r$,
\begin{equation}
 |E_R(z)-1|
 \le C\left(\varepsilon\log R+R^{-1/3}\right)
 \exp\!\left(C\varepsilon\log R+C R^{-1/3}\right).
 \label{eq:Robin-affine-low}
\end{equation}
The same bound, with the right-hand side interpreted exponentially, is valid
without the smallness assumption $\varepsilon\log R\le c_0$; when
$\varepsilon\log R>c_0$ the final stability estimate follows from the
trivial $L^p$ bound after enlarging the constant.

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
After the common factor at zero is removed, Hadamard factorization gives
\begin{equation}
 F_a(z;q)=e^{g(z)}P_{<R}(z)P_{\ge R}(z),
 \qquad
 F_a(z;\widetilde q)=e^{\widetilde g(z)}
 \widetilde P_{<R}(z)\widetilde P_{\ge R}(z),
 \label{eq:Robin-Hadamard-split}
\end{equation}
where $g-\widetilde g$ is affine.  For a paired zero the genus-one ratio is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{E(z/z_n)}{E(z/\widetilde z_n)}
 =\frac{z-z_n}{z-\widetilde z_n}
   \frac{\widetilde z_n}{z_n}
   \exp\!\left[z\left(\frac1{z_n}-\frac1{\widetilde z_n}\right)\right].
 \label{eq:Robin-E-ratio}
\end{equation}
For $|z_n|\ge1$, the pairing assumption gives
$|\widetilde z_n|\ge |z_n|/4$ and hence
\begin{equation}
 \left|\frac1{z_n}-\frac1{\widetilde z_n}\right|
 \le C\frac{\varepsilon}{|z_n|^2}.
 \label{eq:Robin-reciprocal-difference}
\end{equation}
The finitely many zeros with $|z_n|<1$ are treated as complete factors; their
number is $O_{Q,A}(1)$ and they cause no singular reciprocal estimate.

On the horizontal part at height $1$, lower-half-plane zeros are at distance
at least one, and the fixed semicircle is at distance at least one from every
upper-half-plane zero of either characteristic function, by the preceding
uniform disk bound.  Thus
\[
 \left|\log\frac{z-z_n}{z-\widetilde z_n}\right|
 \le C\frac{\varepsilon}{|z-\widetilde z_n|}.
\]
Split the zeros into $|z_n|\le2r$ and the dyadic shells
$2^jr<|z_n|\le2^{j+1}r$.  The first group contains $O_{Q,A}(r)$ zeros.
For each such zero, the separation of the contour from the zeros and the
standard estimate for the integral of $|z-\widetilde z_n|^{-1}$ along a
piecewise smooth contour of length $O(r)$ give an $O(\log R)$ bound.
Thus the first group contributes $O(\varepsilon r\log R)$.  In the $j$th
shell, each factor contributes $O(\varepsilon/(2^jr))$ pointwise and the
shell contains $O_{Q,A}(2^jr)$ zeros.  Since the contour has length $O(r)$,
each nonempty shell contributes $O(\varepsilon r)$.  There are
$O(\log R)$ shells, giving
\eqref{eq:Robin-logW-integral}.  The exponential corrections in
\eqref{eq:Robin-E-ratio} are bounded by $C\varepsilon|z|$ because
\[
 \sum_{1\le|z_n|<R}
 \left|\frac1{z_n}-\frac1{\widetilde z_n}\right|
 \le C\varepsilon,
\]
so they contribute $O(\varepsilon r)$ on $\mathcal C_r$.
This proves \eqref{eq:Robin-W-sup}.

For the high-imaginary disk $D_\rho:=\{|z-3i\rho|\le\rho\}$,
the Robin high-energy estimate gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{F_a(z;q)}{F_a(z;\widetilde q)}=1+O(\rho^{-1}),
 \qquad z\in D_\rho,
 \label{eq:Robin-ratio-high}
\end{equation}
uniformly for $|a|\le A$.  The tail products satisfy, by the usual genus-one
estimate and the zero-counting bound,
\begin{equation}
 P_{\ge R}(z)=1+O(\rho^2/R),
 \qquad
 \widetilde P_{\ge R}(z)=1+O(\rho^2/R).
 \label{eq:Robin-tail-high}
\end{equation}
The same dyadic-shell argument as above, now centered at $3i\rho$, gives
\[
 |\log W_R^0(z)|\le C\varepsilon\log R,
 \qquad z\in D_\rho,
\]
and hence \eqref{eq:Robin-W-high}.  Combining
\eqref{eq:Robin-Hadamard-split}--\eqref{eq:Robin-tail-high} gives
\begin{equation}
 E_R(z)=1+O\!\left(\varepsilon\log R+R^{-1/3}\right),
 \qquad z\in D_\rho,
 \label{eq:Robin-affine-high}
\end{equation}
provided $\varepsilon\log R$ is below a fixed small constant.  This is the
only place where the affine Hadamard factor enters.

Write $\delta_R=C(\varepsilon\log R+R^{-1/3})$, with the constant
chosen so that \eqref{eq:Robin-affine-high} gives $|E_R(z)-1|\le\delta_R$ on
$D_\rho$.  In the smallness regime under consideration, take $R$ large
(and the threshold for $\varepsilon\log R$ small) so that $\delta_R<1/2$.
Then $E_R(D_\rho)$ lies in the disk $|w-1|<1/2$, where the principal
logarithm is analytic.  Consequently
\[
 h_R(z):=\operatorname{Log}E_R(z)
 \quad\text{is analytic on }D_\rho,
 \qquad |h_R(z)|\le 2|E_R(z)-1|\le 2\delta_R.
\]
Since $E_R(z)=\exp(c_1z+c_0)$, we have $h_R'(z)=c_1$ on this disk; the
possible additive multiple of $2\pi i$ between $h_R$ and $c_1z+c_0$ does
not affect the derivative or the exponential.  Cauchy's estimate therefore
gives
\[
 |c_1|\le C\rho^{-1}
       \left(\varepsilon\log R+R^{-1/3}\right).
\]
Using the bound on $h_R$ at the disk center and this derivative estimate,
we obtain $|h_R(z)|\le C(\varepsilon\log R+R^{-1/3})$ for $|z|\le
r=o(\rho)$.  Exponentiating yields \eqref{eq:Robin-affine-low}.  If $\varepsilon\log R$ is not small, the
right-hand side of the final stability estimate is already bounded below by
a fixed positive constant (after enlarging constants), so the trivial estimate
$\sup_x|\int_x^1(\widetilde q-q)|\le Q_p$ suffices.
