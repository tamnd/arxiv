<!-- theorema-1: body_md -->
For every $n\ge2$ and every non-zero harmonic cubic $p$ on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$, $R(p)\ge\frac{n+4}3$, with equality if and only if $p$ satisfies the eikonal equation $|Dp|^2=9\|p\|_\infty^2|x|^4$. Consequently $\kappa_n=\frac{n+4}3$ exactly for $n\in\{2,5,8,14,26\}$, the minimizers being $\operatorname{Re} z^3$ and Cartan's isoparametric cubics $\mathcal{C}_{d}$ (\Cref{sec:hsiang}); in particular $\kappa_5=3$.

<!-- theoremb-1: body_md -->
Let $u$ be a Hsiang cubic on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ with algebra $\mathbb{A}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $u$ attains the value $M$ exactly on $\widehat{\mathrm{Idm}}=\{\hat c:\ c\in\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})\}$, and $u|_{S^{n-1}}$ has no other local maxima;
\item[(ii)] $u=M\cos3\varphi=M\,T_3(\cos\varphi)$ on every great circle through $\hat c$ in a direction of the Peirce space $\mathbb{A}_c(-1)$;
\item[(iii)] the Chebyshev ratio of $u$ is $R(u)=\dfrac{2N_n}{9\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}}$, where $\mathcal{K}$ is the Killing operator \eqref{killing}.
\end{enumerate}

Let $u$ be a Hsiang cubic on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ with algebra $\mathbb{A}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $u$ is a stationary point of the Chebyshev ratio $R$ on $\mathscr{H}_3$ if and only if $u$ admits a Kolmogorov measure;

\end{enumerate}

<!-- theoremd-1: body_md -->
Let $u$ be a Hsiang cubic on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ with algebra $\mathbb{A}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If $\mathbb{A}\in\mathsf{Pol}(m_0,m_1)$ is polar, with polar decomposition $\mathbb V_0\oplus\mathbb V_1$, then the uniform measure on the sphere bundle $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ is, up to a positive factor, a representing measure of $u$. In particular, $u$ is stationary, $\mathcal{K}=m_1\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb V_0}\oplus2m_0\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb V_1}$, and
$$
R(u)=\frac{2N_n}{27\,m_0m_1}.
$$
\item[(ii)] If the group $G$ of orthogonal automorphisms of $\mathbb{A}$ acts transitively on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ and every $G$-invariant cubic form is a multiple of $u$, then the $G$-invariant probability measure on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ is, up to a positive factor, a representing measure of $u$. This applies to Cartan's cubics $\mathcal{C}_{d}$, to $\det X$ on $M_3(\mathbb{R}^{})$ and to $\operatorname{Re}\det z$ on the space $\mathrm{Sym}_3(\mathbb C)\cong\mathbb{R}^{12}$ of complex symmetric matrices.
\item[(iii)] If $u$ is a sum $\sum_b\pm x_{b_1}x_{b_2}x_{b_3}$ of monomials over the blocks of a partial Steiner triple system (in a suitable orthonormal basis), then $u$ has a representing measure with equal atoms at $4|B|$ explicit idempotents. This applies to the mutants, to $\det X$, to the $21$-dimensional exceptional algebra $\mathrm{trip}(\operatorname{Im}\mathbb O)$ and, more generally, to every exceptional Hsiang algebra with $n_{-1/2}\ne0$ known to exist, with the single exception of the $12$-dimensional algebra in \textup{(ii)}, which has no Steiner decomposition, as announced in \cite{HsiangStrongRigidity}.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, every Hsiang cubic known to exist is stationary. Stationarity is open only for hypothetical exceptional Hsiang algebras whose Peirce profiles are admissible but neither realized nor excluded; these lie in finitely many dimensions.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:metrized}
A bilinear form $Q$ on an algebra $\mathbb{A}$ is called \emph{invariant} if $Q(xy,z)=Q(x,yz)$ for all $x,y,z\in\mathbb{A}$. A \emph{metrized algebra} is a pair $(\mathbb{A},Q)$, where $\mathbb{A}$ is a commutative algebra and $Q$ is a nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form on $\mathbb{A}$ \cite{Bordemann}, \cite[Chap.~6]{Nadirashvili-Tkachev-Vladuts}. It is called \emph{Euclidean} if $Q$ is positive definite.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:critical}
Let $u\not\equiv0$ be a cubic form and $\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}(u)$. A point $x\in S$ is a critical point of $u|_S$ with $u(x)\ne0$ if and only if $c=x/(6u(x))$ is an idempotent. In particular, $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})\neq\emptyset$, and the maximum of $u$ on $S$ is attained at points $\hat c$, $c\in\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$, with $\max_Su=\frac1{6|c|}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By Lagrange's rule and \eqref{dict}, $x\in S$ is critical if and only if $\frac12x^2=\mu x$ for some $\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{}$; pairing with $x$ gives $\mu=\frac12\langle x^2, x\rangle=3u(x)$. Thus $x^2=6u(x)x$, and for $u(x)\ne0$ this is equivalent to $c^2=c$ with $c=x/(6u(x))$. Conversely, $u(\hat c)=\langle c^2, c\rangle/(6|c|^3)=1/(6|c|)$. Since $u$ is odd and $u\not\equiv0$, its maximum on $S$ is positive, and the maximum point is a critical point with $u\ne0$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:hsiang}
A Euclidean metrized algebra $\mathbb{A}$ with $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\ne\{0\}$ is called a \emph{Hsiang algebra} with \emph{weight} $\theta$ if it satisfies \eqref{hsiangid} \cite[Chap.~6]{Nadirashvili-Tkachev-Vladuts}, \cite{Tkachev-book}. It is called \emph{trivial} if $\dim\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}=1$; this happens if and only if $u=a\langle q, x\rangle^3$ for some $q\in\mathbb{A}$, $a\ne0$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{the:exact}
A non-trivial Hsiang algebra is exact. Equivalently, every Hsiang cubic which is not congruent to $ax_1^3$ is harmonic.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{the:peirce}
Let $c\in\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$. Then $L(c)$ has the simple eigenvalue $1$ with eigenvector $c$, and
\begin{equation}\label{peirce}
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{R}^{}c\oplus\mathbb{A}_c(-1)\oplus\mathbb{A}_c(-\tfrac12)\oplus\mathbb{A}_c(\tfrac12),\qquad \mathbb{A}_c(t)=\ker(L(c)-t),
\end{equation}
is an orthogonal decomposition. Moreover, $y_1y_2=-\frac43\langle y_1, y_2\rangle c$ for all $y_1,y_2\in\mathbb{A}_c(-1)$. The dimensions $n_t=\dim\mathbb{A}_c(t)$ do not depend on $c$; the triple
\begin{equation}\label{profile}
\mathsf{pr}(\mathbb{A})=(n_{-1},\,n_{-1/2},\,n_{1/2})
\end{equation}
is called the \emph{Peirce profile} of $\mathbb{A}$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:einstein}
A Hsiang algebra is called an \emph{Einstein Hsiang algebra} if its Killing form is proportional to the inner product, i.e.\ $\mathcal{K}=\nu\operatorname{Id}$ for some $\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{}$ (then $\nu=\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K} /n>0$).
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:polar}
A \emph{symmetric Clifford system} of size $(m_0,m_1)$ is a family $P_1,\dots,P_{m_0}$ of symmetric $m_1\times m_1$ matrices such that
\begin{equation}\label{cliffsys}
P_iP_j+P_jP_i=2\delta_{ij}\operatorname{Id},\qquad 1\le i,j\le m_0,
\end{equation}
where for $m_0=1$ we also require $\operatorname{tr} P_1=0$. With $P(y)=\sum_iy_iP_i$, so that $P(y)^2=|y|^2\operatorname{Id}$, the cubic form
\begin{equation}\label{clifford}
u(y,z)=\tfrac12\langle P(y)z, z\rangle,\qquad (y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{m_0}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_1}=\mathbb{R}^{n},
\end{equation}
is a Hsiang cubic \cite{tkachev2010minimal}, \cite[Chap.~6]{Nadirashvili-Tkachev-Vladuts}. Its algebra is called a \emph{polar algebra of type $(m_0,m_1)$}, and the orthogonal splitting $\mathbb V_0\oplus\mathbb V_1=\mathbb{R}^{m_0}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{m_1}$ is its \emph{polar decomposition}. We denote by $\mathsf{Pol}(m_0,m_1)$ the class of polar algebras of type $(m_0,m_1)$. A Hsiang algebra which is not similar to a polar one is called \emph{exceptional}.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:infeig}
A cubic form $u\not\equiv0$ on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is called a \emph{harmonic $\infty$-eigencubic} if $\Delta u=0$ and $\Delta_\infty u=\lambda|x|^2u$ for some $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{}$.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{pro:infeig}
A cubic form $u\not\equiv0$ is a non-trivial Hsiang cubic if and only if it is a harmonic $\infty$-eigencubic, i.e.
\begin{equation}\label{infeig2}
\Delta u=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta_\infty u=\lambda|x|^2u\quad\text{for some }\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{}.
\end{equation}
In this case $\lambda=-\theta$ and
\begin{equation}\label{lambdaM}
\lambda=54\,M^2,\qquad M=\max_{S}u.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $u$ is a non-trivial Hsiang cubic, then $\Delta u=0$ by \Cref{the:exact}, and \eqref{hsiangeq} reduces to \eqref{infeig2} with $\lambda=-\theta$. Conversely, \eqref{infeig2} implies \eqref{hsiangeq} with $\theta=-\lambda$, and $u$ is non-trivial because $\Delta(a\langle q, x\rangle^3)=6a|q|^2\langle q, x\rangle\not\equiv0$.

To prove \eqref{lambdaM}, let $x_0\in S$ be a maximum point of $u|_S$. By Euler's identities, $\langle Du(x), x\rangle=3u(x)$ and $D^2u(x)x=2Du(x)$. At $x_0$ the gradient is normal to the sphere, so $Du(x_0)=3Mx_0$ and $D^2u(x_0)x_0=6Mx_0$. Therefore
$$
\Delta_\infty u(x_0)=9M^2\langle D^2u(x_0)\,x_0, x_0\rangle=54M^3,
$$
while the right hand side of \eqref{infeig2} at $x_0$ equals $\lambda M$. Since $M>0$, \eqref{lambdaM} follows.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The computation in the proof shows that a cubic form $u\not\equiv0$ is never $\infty$-harmonic: $\Delta_\infty u(x_0)=54M^3>0$ at a maximum point on the sphere. The same argument, applied at the maximum and minimum points on the sphere, shows that no homogeneous polynomial $u\not\equiv0$ of degree $k\ge2$ satisfies $\Delta_\infty u=0$; this yields the non-vanishing property of the gradient of real analytic $\infty$-harmonic functions \cite[Prop.~4.1]{Tkachev-laplace}, which goes back to Fuglede \cite{Fuglede-nonvanishing}, cf.\ Lewis \cite{Lewis-smoothness}. Thus \eqref{infeig2} is the simplest non-trivial $\infty$-Laplace equation which cubic forms can satisfy, and the Hsiang cubics are exactly its harmonic solutions. Formula \eqref{lambdaM} is the analytic form of the normalization $|c|^2=-\frac3{2\theta}$: the eigenvalue in \eqref{infeig2} is determined by the Chebyshev level $M$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
For a harmonic function with $Du\ne0$, $\Delta_pu=\operatorname{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du)=(p-2)|Du|^{p-4}\Delta_\infty u$. Hence a non-trivial Hsiang cubic satisfies
$$
\Delta_pu=(p-2)\lambda\,|Du|^{p-4}|x|^2u\qquad\text{for every }p>1,
$$
an observation made in \cite[Sec.~4]{Tkachev-laplace}. This should be compared with the case $\lambda=0$: for $p\notin\{1,2\}$ the only cubic form satisfying $\Delta_pu=0$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is $u\equiv0$ \cite{Tkachev-laplace}, which answers, for cubic forms, a question of J.~L.~Lewis \cite{Lewis-smoothness}; for homogeneous polynomials of degree four and five see Lewis and Vogel \cite{Lewis-Vogel}, and for the algebraic structure of homogeneous (quasiradial) solutions in the plane see \cite{Tkachev-quasiradial}. Thus the Hsiang cubics are the natural non-trivial cubic solutions of the nonlinear eigenvalue equations for the whole family of $p$-Laplacians. For $p=1$ one has $\Delta_1u:=|Du|\operatorname{div}\frac{Du}{|Du|}=-\lambda\frac{|x|^2u}{|Du|^2}$, which is Hsiang's original reading of \eqref{hsiangeq}: the mean curvature of the level set $\{u=s\}$ at $x$ equals $-\lambda s|x|^2/|Du|^3$, i.e.\ it is proportional to the level. For harmonic functions the $1$-Laplacian and the $\infty$-Laplacian coincide up to the factor $-|Du|^{-2}$; this is why the geometric (mean curvature) and the variational ($L^\infty$) readings of \eqref{hsiangeq} agree.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:aronsson}
Aronsson's characterization of $\infty$-harmonic functions of class $C^2$ is that $|Du|$ is constant along the gradient curves $\dot\gamma=Du(\gamma)$ \cite{Aronsson-pde,Aronsson-Crandall-Juutinen}. For a Hsiang cubic,
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\,|Du(\gamma)|^2=2\Delta_\infty u(\gamma)=2\lambda\,|\gamma|^2u(\gamma),\qquad \frac{d}{dt}\,u(\gamma)=|Du(\gamma)|^2.
$$
Thus $|Du|$ is strictly increasing along gradient curves in $\{u>0\}$ and strictly decreasing in $\{u<0\}$, at a rate proportional to the level: the defect from Aronsson's condition is measured by $u$ itself. On the minimal cone $u^{-1}(0)$ (away from its singular set) the gradient is, to first order, of constant length along the flow.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:kellogg}
For a homogeneous polynomial $p$ of degree $k$, $\max_S|Dp|\le k\max_S|p|$ (Kellogg \cite{Kellogg-polynomials}); for $k=3$ it is a direct consequence of Banach's theorem \cite{Banach-homogene} (see, e.g., \cite[Prop.~1.44]{Dineen-book}, \cite{Agrachev-Kozhasov-Uschmajew}): every symmetric trilinear form $F$ on a Euclidean space satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{banach}
\sup_{|x|=|y|=|z|=1}|F(x,y,z)|=\sup_{|x|=1}|F(x,x,x)| .
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $p(x)=F(x,x,x)$, then $\langle Dp(x), y\rangle=3F(x,x,y)$ by the symmetry of $F$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{kellogg}
|Dp(x)|\le3\|p\|_\infty|x|^2,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n} .
\end{equation}
Equality in $\max_S|Dp|\le3\|p\|_\infty$ holds for every cubic form, by the computation in the proof of \Cref{pro:infeig}. Since $Dp$ is homogeneous of degree $2$, the Lipschitz constant of a cubic form on the unit ball $B$ is therefore
$$
\operatorname{Lip}(p,B)=\max_S|Dp|=3\|p\|_\infty .
$$
Consequently the Chebyshev problem \eqref{kappa0} is the same as the problem of minimizing the Lipschitz constant on $B$ of a harmonic cubic with prescribed $L^2$-norm on the sphere. This is a genuine $L^\infty$-variational problem in the sense of Aronsson, restricted to a finite-dimensional class.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{pro:firstint}
Let $u$ be a Hsiang cubic and $M=\|u\|_\infty$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{firstint}
Q(x)=9M^2|x|^4-|Du(x)|^2 .
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $Q\ge0$ on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$, and $Q$ is constant along every gradient curve $\dot\gamma=Du(\gamma)$.
\item[(ii)] $Q$ vanishes on the subspace $\mathbb{R}^{}c\oplus\mathbb{A}_c(-1)$ for every $c\in\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$. In particular, equality holds in \eqref{bsineq} on the sphere $S_c=S\cap\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{}c\oplus\mathbb{A}_c(-1)\bigr)$ of dimension $n_{-1}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
(i) The inequality $Q\ge0$ is \eqref{bsineq}. Along a gradient curve, \Cref{rem:aronsson} gives $\frac{d}{dt}|Du(\gamma)|^2=2\lambda|\gamma|^2u(\gamma)$. On the other hand, $\frac{d}{dt}|\gamma|^2=2\langle \gamma, Du(\gamma)\rangle=6u(\gamma)$ by Euler's identity, so $\frac{d}{dt}|\gamma|^4=12|\gamma|^2u(\gamma)$. Hence
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\,Q(\gamma)=(108M^2-2\lambda)\,|\gamma|^2u(\gamma)=0
$$
since $2\lambda=108M^2$ by \eqref{lambdaM}.

(ii) In the normalized scale $9M^2=\frac13$ and $|Du|^2=\frac14|x^2|^2$. Let $x=sc+y$ with $s\in\mathbb{R}^{}$ and $y\in\mathbb{A}_c(-1)$. By \Cref{the:peirce}, $cy=-y$ and $y^2=-\frac43|y|^2c$, so that
$$
x^2=\bigl(s^2-\tfrac43|y|^2\bigr)c-2s\,y .
$$
Since $|c|^2=\frac34$, this gives
$$
|x^2|^2=\tfrac34\bigl(s^2-\tfrac43|y|^2\bigr)^2+4s^2|y|^2=\tfrac43\bigl(\tfrac34s^2+|y|^2\bigr)^2=\tfrac43|x|^4,
$$
that is, $|Du(x)|^2=\frac13|x|^4=9M^2|x|^4$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{the:cartanmin}
For every $n\ge2$ and every $0\ne p\in\mathscr{H}_3(\mathbb{R}^{n})$,
\begin{equation}\label{cartanbound}
R(p)\ \ge\ \frac{n+4}{3}.
\end{equation}
Equality holds if and only if $|Dp|^2=9\|p\|_\infty^2|x|^4$ on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$, i.e.\ if and only if, up to rotations and scaling, either $n=2$ and $p=\operatorname{Re} z^3$, or $n=3d+2$ with $d\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ and $p$ is Cartan's cubic $\mathcal{C}_{d}$. In particular $\kappa_n\ge\frac{n+4}3$ for all $n$, with equality exactly for $n\in\{2,5,8,14,26\}$:
$$
\kappa_5=3,\qquad\kappa_8=4,\qquad\kappa_{14}=6,\qquad\kappa_{26}=10,
$$
and in these dimensions the minimum in \eqref{kappa0} is attained only on the orbit of Cartan's cubic.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $B$ be the unit ball, $|S|$ the area of $S$ and $M=\|p\|_\infty$. Since $p$ is harmonic and $\partial_rp=3p$ on $S$ by Euler's identity, Green's formula gives
$$
\int_B|Dp|^2\,dx=\int_Sp\,\partial_rp\,dS=3|S|\,\|p\|_2^2 .
$$
On the other hand, $|Dp(x)|\le3M|x|^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ by Kellogg's inequality \eqref{kellogg}. Since $\int_B|x|^4\,dx=|S|/(n+4)$,
$$
3|S|\,\|p\|_2^2\ \le\ 9M^2\int_B|x|^4\,dx=\frac{9M^2|S|}{n+4},
$$
which is \eqref{cartanbound}. Equality holds if and only if the continuous non-negative function $9M^2|x|^4-|Dp|^2$ vanishes on $B$, hence, by homogeneity, on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$. By \cite[Thm.~1.4]{Tkachev-isoparametric} with $m=3$ (see also \cite{Cartan-isoparametric,Tkachev-generalization}), a cubic form satisfying the eikonal equation $|Dp|^2=9M^2|x|^4$ is, up to rotations and scaling, either one of Cartan's isoparametric cubics $\mathcal{C}_{d}$, $n=3d+2$, $d\in\{1,2,4,8\}$, or the cubic $x_n^3-3x_n(x_1^2+\dots+x_{n-1}^2)$, i.e.\ $T_3(\langle x, e_n\rangle)$ on the sphere. The latter has Laplacian $-6(n-2)x_n$, so it is harmonic only for $n=2$, where it is $\operatorname{Re} z^3$. Conversely, these cubics are harmonic and satisfy the eikonal equation. The statement on $\kappa_n$ follows because the minimum in \eqref{kappa0} is attained.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:kelloggdefect}
The proof gives the identity

R(p)=\frac{n+4}{3}\Bigl(1-\frac{\langle Q_p\rangle}{9M^2}\Bigr)^{-1},\qquad \langle Q_p\rangle=(n+4)\int_B\bigl(9M^2|x|^4-|Dp|^2\bigr)\frac{dx}{|S|},

so that minimizing $R$ amounts to minimizing the mean defect in Kellogg's inequality. In dimensions $n\ne2,5,8,14,26$ no harmonic cubic is eikonal and the defect is bounded away from zero; for $n=3$ the value $\kappa_3=\frac{35}9$ of \Cref{conj:kappa} corresponds to the normalized mean defect $\langle Q_p\rangle/(9M^2)=\frac25$, attained by $x_1x_2x_3$ ($\mathsf{Pol}(1,2)$).

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{the:equi}
Let $u$ be a Hsiang cubic and let $M$ be as in \eqref{Mnorm}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The critical values of $u|_S$ are contained in $\{M,0,-M\}$. Every local maximum of $u|_S$ is a global maximum, and
\begin{equation}\label{altset}
\{x\in S:\ |u(x)|=\|u\|_\infty\}=\widehat{\mathrm{Idm}}\cup(-\widehat{\mathrm{Idm}}),\qquad \widehat{\mathrm{Idm}}=\{\hat c:\ c\in\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})\}.
\end{equation}
\item[(ii)] The Riemannian Hessian of $u|_S$ at $\hat c$ is
\begin{equation}\label{hessian}
\operatorname{Hess}(u|_S)(\hat c)=6M\bigl(L(c)-\tfrac12\bigr)\big|_{c^\perp},
\end{equation}
with eigenvalues $-9M$, $-6M$, $0$ on $\mathbb{A}_c(-1)$, $\mathbb{A}_c(-\frac12)$, $\mathbb{A}_c(\frac12)$.
\item[(iii)] If $n_{-1}\ge1$, $\xi\in\mathbb{A}_c(-1)$ and $|\xi|=1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{T3}
u(\cos\varphi\,\hat c+\sin\varphi\,\xi)=M\cos3\varphi=M\,T_3(\cos\varphi),\qquad\varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{}.
\end{equation}
The six points $\varphi\in\frac\pi3\mathbb{Z}$ of alternation on this circle are $\pm\hat c_1,\pm\hat c_2,\pm\hat c_3$, where $c_1=c$ and $\{c_1,c_2,c_3\}$ is a \emph{triad} of idempotents, $c_1+c_2+c_3=0$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
(i) By \Cref{lem:critical}, every critical point with $u(x)\ne0$ is $\pm\hat c$ with $c$ an idempotent, and $|u(\pm\hat c)|=\frac1{6|c|}$. Since all idempotents have the same length, these values are all equal to $M$. Let $x_0$ be a local maximum of $u|_S$. If $u(x_0)<0$, then $x_0=-\hat c$ is also a global minimum point, so $u|_S$ is constant near $x_0$ and $u=-M|x|^3$ on an open cone, which is impossible for a polynomial. If $u(x_0)=0$, then by homogeneity $u\le0$ on an open cone $\Gamma$ containing $x_0$, with $u(x_0)=0$; since $u$ is harmonic (\Cref{the:exact}), the strong maximum principle gives $u\equiv0$ on $\Gamma$, hence $u\equiv0$. Therefore every local maximum has the value $M$, and \eqref{altset} follows.

(ii) The Riemannian Hessian of the restriction of a function $f$ to the unit sphere at a critical point $x$ is $D^2f(x)-\langle Df(x), x\rangle\operatorname{Id}$ on $x^\perp$. With $f=u$, $x=\hat c$, we have $D^2u(\hat c)=L(\hat c)=L(c)/|c|$ and $\langle Du(\hat c), \hat c\rangle=3M=\frac1{2|c|}$, which gives \eqref{hessian}. The eigenvalues follow from \eqref{peirce}.

(iii) Put $\eta=|c|\xi$, so $|\eta|=|c|$. By \Cref{the:peirce}, $c\eta=-\eta$ and $\eta^2=-\frac43|\eta|^2c=-c$. Hence $\mathbb{R}^{}c\oplus\mathbb{R}^{}\eta$ is a subalgebra, and $(sc+t\eta)^2=(s^2-t^2)c-2st\,\eta$. Then
$$
u(sc+t\eta)=\tfrac16\langle (s^2-t^2)c-2st\,\eta, sc+t\eta\rangle=\tfrac{|c|^2}6(s^3-3st^2).
$$
Substituting $s=\cos\varphi/|c|$, $t=\sin\varphi/|c|$ gives $u=\frac{1}{6|c|}\cos3\varphi$. Finally, the elements $c_2=-\frac12c+\frac{\sqrt3}2\eta$, $c_3=-\frac12c-\frac{\sqrt3}2\eta$ satisfy $c_2^2=c_2$, $c_3^2=c_3$ by the same multiplication rule, and $c_1+c_2+c_3=0$.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
Part (i) is the exact analogue of the defining property of $T_k$: all local extrema of $u|_S$ have the same absolute value. Part (ii) says that \emph{the Peirce decomposition of an idempotent is the spectral decomposition of the Hessian of the cubic at its maximum}, and that $u|_S$ is Morse--Bott along its maximum set, with nullity $n_{1/2}=\dim\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$. Part (iii) shows that through every idempotent there passes an $(n_{-1}-1)$-parameter family of great circles on which $u$ is literally the Chebyshev polynomial $T_3$; the alternation points on these circles are hexagons of idempotents. For a generic cubic form none of these properties holds: its maximum on the sphere is attained at a single pair of points.
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
Equioscillation in the sense of (i) does not need the full Hsiang identity: it only uses that all idempotents of $\mathbb{A}(u)$ have the same length. Whether, conversely, a harmonic cubic whose critical values on the sphere are $\pm M$ and $0$ is necessarily a Hsiang cubic is open (\Cref{prob:converse}).

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
\label{rem:aku}
Following \cite{Agrachev-Kozhasov-Uschmajew}, the \emph{cubic Chebyshev form} in $m$ variables is
$$
\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,m}(s,y)=s^3-3s|y|^2,\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}^{},\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{m-1};
$$
on the unit sphere it coincides with $T_3(s)$, i.e.\ it restricts to $T_3(\cos\varphi)=\cos3\varphi$ on every great circle through $(1,0)$, and $\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,2}=\operatorname{Re}(s+iy)^3$. The computation in the proof of \Cref{the:equi}(iii) holds verbatim for an arbitrary $y\in\mathbb{A}_c(-1)$: by \Cref{the:peirce}, $(sc+y)^2=(s^2-\frac43|y|^2)c-2sy$, whence, with $s'=s|c|$,
\begin{equation}\label{chebform}
u(s'\hat c+y)=M\,\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,\,n_{-1}+1}(s',y),\qquad s'\in\mathbb{R}^{},\ y\in\mathbb{A}_c(-1).
\end{equation}
Thus \emph{the restriction of a Hsiang cubic to the subalgebra $\mathbb{R}^{}c\oplus\mathbb{A}_c(-1)$ is a cubic Chebyshev form}, and \eqref{T3} is its restriction to $2$-planes. For Cartan's cubic $\mathcal{C}_{1}$ one has $n_{-1}=2$, so its restriction to the $3$-dimensional subalgebra $\mathbb{R}^{}c\oplus\mathbb{A}_c(-1)$ is $M\,\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,3}$, which is exactly the ternary cubic of least deviation among \emph{all} ternary cubic forms found in \cite{Agrachev-Kozhasov-Uschmajew} (\Cref{pro:aku}). Note that $\Delta\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,m}=-6(m-2)s$, so $\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,m}$ is not harmonic for $m\ge3$, in accordance with the fact that $\mathbb{R}^{}c\oplus\mathbb{A}_c(-1)$ is a subalgebra but not an exact algebra. On the other hand, $|D\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,m}|^2=9|x|^4$, i.e.\ $\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,m}$ is a solution of the eikonal equation (for $m\ne2$ one of the reducible ones in the classification of \cite{Tkachev-generalization}, see also \cite{Tkachev-jordan}), and therefore
$$
\Delta_\infty\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,m}=\tfrac12\langle D\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,m}, D|D\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,m}|^2\rangle=54\,|x|^2\,\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,m}.
$$
Thus the Chebyshev forms satisfy the $\infty$-eigenvalue equation of \Cref{def:infeig} with $\lambda=54M^2$, $M=\max_S\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,m}=1$, exactly as in \eqref{lambdaM}, but they are harmonic only for $m=2$. In this sense the extremal form of \cite{Agrachev-Kozhasov-Uschmajew} is a non-exact $\infty$-eigencubic, and the Hsiang cubics are its harmonic counterparts (\Cref{pro:infeig}).

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:fischer}
If $p$ is a harmonic form of degree $k$ and $q$ is any form of degree $k$, then
$$
\int_Spq\,d\sigma=\frac{[p,q]}{n(n+2)\cdots(n+2k-2)}.
$$
In particular, $\int_Spq\,d\sigma=[p,q]/N_n$ for $p,q\in\mathscr{H}_3$, with $N_n$ as in \eqref{Nn}.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For harmonic $q$ both sides are $O(n)$-invariant inner products on the irreducible $O(n)$-module of harmonic forms of degree $k$, hence proportional; the constant is found on $p=q=x_1\cdots x_k$ if $k\le n$ (then $[p,p]=1$ and $\int_Sx_1^2\cdots x_k^2\,d\sigma=1/(n(n+2)\cdots(n+2k-2))$), and on $p=q=\operatorname{Re} z^3$ if $n=2$, $k=3$ (then $[p,p]=24$ and $\int\cos^23\varphi=\frac12$). For general $q=h+|x|^2r$ with $h$ harmonic, both sides vanish on $|x|^2r$: the left one because $r|_S$ is a sum of spherical harmonics of lower degree, the right one by \eqref{adj}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:zonal}
For $a\in S$ the harmonic part \eqref{harmdec} of $\langle x, a\rangle^3$ is
\begin{equation}\label{zonal}
Z_a(x)=\langle x, a\rangle^3-\frac{3}{n+2}|x|^2\langle x, a\rangle,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{zonalquad}
\int_SpZ_a\,d\sigma=\frac6{N_n}\,p(a)\qquad\text{for all } p\in\mathscr{H}_3.
\end{equation}
Hence $K_a=\frac{N_n}6Z_a$ is the reproducing kernel of $\mathscr{H}_3\subset L^2(\sigma)$, and $K_a(a)=\dim\mathscr{H}_3=\frac{n(n-1)(n+4)}6$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Since $\Delta\langle x, a\rangle^3=6\langle x, a\rangle$, \eqref{harmdec} gives $\ell=\frac{3}{n+2}\langle x, a\rangle$. By \Cref{lem:fischer} and \eqref{eval},
$$
\int_S pZ_a\,d\sigma=\int_S p\,\langle x, a\rangle^3\,d\sigma=\frac{[p,\langle x, a\rangle^3]}{N_n}=\frac{6\,p(a)}{N_n}.
$$
Finally $K_a(a)=\frac{N_n}6\cdot\frac{n-1}{n+2}=\frac{n(n-1)(n+4)}{6}$.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{the:ratio}
For a normalized Hsiang cubic $u$ on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$,
\begin{equation}\label{ratio}
\|u\|_2^2=\frac{\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}}{6N_n},\qquad R(u)=\frac{2N_n}{9\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}}.
\end{equation}
In the non-normalized scale $R(u)=-\theta N_n/(9\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K})$. If $\mathbb{A}$ is an Einstein Hsiang algebra, $\mathcal{K}=\nu\operatorname{Id}$, then $R(u)=\frac{2(n+2)(n+4)}{9\nu}$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Since $u$ is harmonic, \Cref{lem:fischer} gives $\|u\|_2^2=[u,u]/N_n$. Writing $u=\frac16\sum \mathcal{T}_{ijk}x_ix_jx_k$ and differentiating, each of the $3!$ permutations contributes $\sum \mathcal{T}_{ijk}^2$, so $[u,u]=\frac{6}{36}\|\mathcal{T}\|^2=\frac16\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}$ by \eqref{trA}. By \Cref{the:equi}, $\|u\|_\infty=M$ and $M^2=\frac1{27}$ (generally $M^2=-\theta/54$ by \eqref{lambdaM}).

<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
\label{rem:bombieri}
The proportionality \eqref{bombieri} holds only on $\mathscr{H}_3$. For a general cubic form write $p=h+|x|^2\ell$ as in \eqref{harmdec}. Since $[h,|x|^2\ell]=[\Delta h,\ell]=0$ and $[|x|^2\ell,|x|^2\ell]=[\ell,\Delta(|x|^2\ell)]=(2n+4)[\ell,\ell]$ by \eqref{adj}, and since $h$ and $\ell$ are $L^2$-orthogonal on $S$,
$$
\|p\|_B^2=\frac{[h,h]}{6}+\frac{(n+2)[\ell,\ell]}{3},\qquad
\|p\|_2^2=\frac{[h,h]}{N_n}+\frac{[\ell,\ell]}{n}.
$$
Thus the two norms are proportional on a cubic form only if it is harmonic or of the form $|x|^2\ell$, and the problem of minimizing $\|p\|_\infty^2/\|p\|_2^2$ over \emph{all} cubic forms differs from that of \cite{Agrachev-Kozhasov-Uschmajew}. For instance, on the unit sphere $\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,3}=4x_1^3-3x_1|x|^2$ coincides with $T_3(x_1)$; here $\|\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,3}\|_B^2=\frac{32}5+\frac35=7$, while $\|\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,3}\|_2^2=\frac{17}{35}$.

<!-- remark-10: body_md -->
\label{rem:akuconj}
In \cite[Conjecture~1]{Agrachev-Kozhasov-Uschmajew} it is conjectured that the Chebyshev forms are extremal in every dimension; for cubics this reads
$$
\frac{\|p\|_\infty^2}{\|p\|_B^2}\ \ge\ \frac{\|\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,n}\|_\infty^2}{\|\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,n}\|_B^2}=\frac1{3n-2}\qquad\text{for all cubic forms }p\ne0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n},
$$
the value on the right following from $\|\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,n}\|_\infty=1$ and $\|\mathord{\text{\usefont{U}{wncy}{m}{n}Ch}}_{3,n}\|_B^2=1+3(n-1)$. For a Hsiang cubic, \eqref{bombieri} and \Cref{the:ratio} give
\begin{equation}\label{hsiangbombieri}
\frac{\|u\|_\infty^2}{\|u\|_B^2}=\frac{6R(u)}{N_n}=\frac{4}{3\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}}.
\end{equation}
For Cartan's cubic $\mathcal{C}_{1}$ ($n=5$, $\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}=\frac{70}3$) this equals $\frac{2}{35}<\frac1{13}$, so the conjecture fails for cubic forms on $\mathbb{R}^{5}$. The same happens for the other Hsiang cubics with a large Killing trace in \Cref{tab:ratio}: $\det X$ gives $\frac1{27}<\frac1{25}$ ($n=9$), $\mathsf{Pol}(2,8)$ gives $\frac1{36}<\frac1{28}$ ($n=10$), and $\mathrm{trip}(\operatorname{Im}\mathbb O)$ gives $\frac1{189}<\frac1{61}$ ($n=21$). In $\mathbb{R}^{4}$, on the other hand, the Lawson cubic ($\mathsf{Pol}(2,2)$) gives $\frac19>\frac1{10}$, and the case $n=4$ remains open. In fact the conjecture fails for every $n\ge5$. If $p_1,p_2$ are cubic forms on orthogonal subspaces with $\|p_1\|_\infty=\|p_2\|_\infty$, then $\|p_1+p_2\|_\infty=\|p_1\|_\infty$ (since $s^3+t^3\le(s^2+t^2)^{3/2}$ for $s,t\ge0$) and the Bombieri norms add in squares, so the reciprocal ratios $\|p\|_B^2/\|p\|_\infty^2$ add. By \eqref{hsiangbombieri} and \Cref{cor:killingbound} this reciprocal equals $\frac12n(n+2)$ for Cartan's cubic on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ and $4$ for $\operatorname{Re} z^3$, and comparing with $3n-2$ one checks that $\mathcal{C}_{1}$ (for $n=5,6$), $\mathcal{C}_{1}\oplus\operatorname{Re} z^3$ ($n=7$), $\mathcal{C}_{2}$ ($8\le n\le13$), $\mathcal{C}_{4}$ ($14\le n\le37$) and orthogonal sums of $k$ copies of $\mathcal{C}_{8}$ ($26k\le n\le121k$) beat the Chebyshev form. Thus Hsiang algebras with much multiplication are flatter than the Chebyshev forms, even among all cubic forms.

<!-- remark-11: body_md -->
\label{rem:heuristics}
Several facts proved in this paper point towards Hsiang cubics as the extremal harmonic cubics.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Chebyshev principle.} A minimizer of \eqref{kappa0} is stationary, and by \Cref{the:kolm} and the remark following it, a stationary harmonic cubic which is not zonal must equioscillate on a large set: it must be a positive average of zonal harmonics with poles in its own extremal set. Hsiang cubics are the harmonic cubics in which this happens in the most rigid way: all positive critical values on the sphere coincide, and the extremal set is the whole idempotent variety (\Cref{the:equi}). For $n=2$ the analogy is complete, since $\kappa_2=2$ is attained exactly by $\operatorname{Re} z^3$.
\item \emph{Flatness is measured by the multiplication.} Among Hsiang cubics of a given dimension, \eqref{ratio} shows that $R$ decreases as $\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}=\|\mathcal{T}\|^2$ increases. In $\mathbb{R}^{5}$ Cartan's cubic has almost twice the value of $\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}$ of $\mathsf{Pol}(1,4)$, and \Cref{pro:polarsaddle} shows that $R$ indeed decreases from the cubic of $\mathsf{Pol}(1,4)$ in the direction of the Lawson component of Cartan's cubic, cf.\ \eqref{cartansplit}.
\item \emph{Rigidity.} For $x_1x_2x_3$ ($\mathsf{Pol}(1,2)$), the Lawson cubic ($\mathsf{Pol}(2,2)$) and Cartan's cubic $\mathcal{C}_{1}$ every direction which vanishes on the idempotent variety is an infinitesimal rotation, $D=o(u)$; for the first two this makes them strict local minimizers (\Cref{cor:strict}), and for $\mathcal{C}_{1}$ it is consistent with its global minimality (\Cref{rem:cartanlocal}), whereas the cubic of $\mathsf{Pol}(1,4)$ is not a local minimizer (\Cref{pro:polarsaddle}). Moreover, the alternation sets of $x_1x_2x_3$ and of Cartan's cubic $\mathcal{C}_{1}$ carry spherical $3$-designs (\Cref{cor:design}), the strongest form of equidistribution compatible with the degree.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{pro:peetre}
Let $u(x_1,x_2,x_3)=T(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ be a non-zero block-trilinear cubic on $\mathbb{R}^{n}=(\mathbb{R}^{k})^3$, so that $n=3k$ and $N_n=3k(3k+2)(3k+4)$. Then $u\in\mathscr{H}_3$ and
\begin{equation}\label{peetre}
R(u)=\frac{N_n}{27}\,\frac{\|T\|^2}{\|T\|_2^2}\ \ge\ \frac{N_n}{27\,k^2}.
\end{equation}
Equality holds if and only if $k\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ and, up to orthogonal changes of the variables $x_1,x_2,x_3$ and scaling, $u=\operatorname{Re}\bigl((x_1x_2)x_3\bigr)$ on $\mathbb K_k^3$, i.e.\ $u$ is a mutant Hsiang cubic.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
$u$ is harmonic by the remark after \Cref{def:blocktri}. Since $|T(x_1,x_2,x_3)|\le\|T\|\,|x_1||x_2||x_3|$ with equality for suitable unit vectors, and on $S^{n-1}$, by the inequality between the arithmetic and geometric means,
$$
\bigl(|x_1|^2|x_2|^2|x_3|^2\bigr)^{1/3}\le\frac{|x_1|^2+|x_2|^2+|x_3|^2}{3}=\frac13 ,
$$
i.e.\ $|x_1||x_2||x_3|\le3^{-3/2}$, with equality iff $|x_i|^2=\frac13$, we get $\|u\|_\infty=\|T\|/(3\sqrt3)$. The symmetric tensor of $u$ has six non-zero blocks, each equal to $\frac16T$ up to a permutation of the arguments, so $\|u\|_B^2=\frac16\|T\|_2^2$. Now \eqref{bombieri} gives the identity in \eqref{peetre}. For an orthonormal basis $e_1,\dots,e_k$ of $\mathbb{R}^{k}$ let $T_i=T(e_i,\cdot,\cdot)$, regarded as a $k\times k$ matrix. Then
$$
\|T\|_2^2=\sum_{i=1}^k\|T_i\|_2^2\le k\sum_{i=1}^k\|T_i\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\le k^2\|T\|^2,
$$
which is the inequality. Equality forces $T_i=\|T\|\,O_i$ with $O_i$ orthogonal, for every orthonormal basis, hence $T(x,\cdot,\cdot)$ is $\|T\|\,|x|$ times an orthogonal matrix for every $x$. Thus $(x,y)\mapsto\mu(x,y)$, $\langle \mu(x,y), z\rangle=T(x,y,z)/\|T\|$, is a bilinear map with $|\mu(x,y)|=|x||y|$. Fix unit vectors $a,b$; the maps $y\mapsto\mu(a,y)$ and $x\mapsto\mu(x,b)$ are orthogonal, and $x\circ y=\mu\bigl(\mu(\cdot,b)^{-1}x,\mu(a,\cdot)^{-1}y\bigr)$ is a composition with unit $\mu(a,b)$, $|x\circ y|=|x||y|$. By Hurwitz's theorem on composition algebras with unit \cite{Hurwitz1898}, see also \cite[Chap.~1]{Shapiro-compositions}, $k\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ and $(\mathbb{R}^{k},\circ)$ is isometrically isomorphic to $\mathbb K_k$; hence $\mu$ is isotopic to the multiplication of the normed division algebra $\mathbb K_k$, i.e.\ $\mu(x,y)=C\bigl((Ax)(By)\bigr)$ with orthogonal $A,B,C$, so that $u$ is congruent to $\langle x_1x_2, x_3\rangle=\operatorname{Re}\bigl((x_1x_2)\bar x_3\bigr)$; replacing $x_3$ by $\bar x_3$ gives the mutant cubic of \Cref{sec:polar}. Conversely, for the mutant cubic $\|T\|=1$ and $\|T\|_2^2=k^2$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{pro:zonalstat}
Every zonal harmonic $Z_a$, $a\in S$, is stationary.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
For $n=2$ every harmonic cubic is a rotated multiple of $\operatorname{Re} z^3$, so $R$ is constant on $\mathscr{H}_3\setminus\{0\}$. Let $n\ge3$. We have $Z_a(x)=G(\langle x, a\rangle)$ with $G(t)=t^3-\frac{3}{n+2}t$. The interior critical values of $G$ on $[-1,1]$ have modulus $2(n+2)^{-3/2}<\frac{n-1}{n+2}=G(1)$, so $|G|$ attains its maximum only at $t=\pm1$, i.e.\ $E(Z_a)=\{\pm a\}$. Since $h\in\mathscr{H}_3$ is odd, \eqref{danskingen} gives
$$
\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0^+}\|Z_a+\varepsilon h\|_\infty=h(a).
$$
If $h\perp Z_a$, then $h(a)=\int_S hK_a\,d\sigma=0$ by \Cref{lem:zonal}, and $\|Z_a+\varepsilon h\|_2^2=\|Z_a\|_2^2+\varepsilon^2\|h\|_2^2$. Hence the one-sided derivative of $R(Z_a+\varepsilon h)$ vanishes for every $h\perp Z_a$, and, by scale invariance, $Z_a$ is stationary.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{the:kolm}
For a Hsiang cubic $u$ the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $u$ is stationary;
\item[(ii)] there is a probability measure $\mu_0$ on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ with $\int h(\hat c)\,d\mu_0(c)=0$ for all $h\in\mathscr{H}_3$, $h\perp u$;
\item[(iii)] there is a finite positive measure $\mu$ on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{kolmrep}
u(x)=\int_{\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})}Z_{\hat c}(x)\,d\mu(c)=\int_{\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})}\Bigl(\langle x, \hat c\rangle^3-\frac{3}{n+2}|x|^2\langle x, \hat c\rangle\Bigr)d\mu(c);
\end{equation}
\item[(iv)] there is a finite positive measure $\mu$ on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{cubature}
\int_Sh\,u\,d\sigma=\frac6{N_n}\int_{\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})}h(\hat c)\,d\mu(c)\qquad\text{for all }h\in\mathscr{H}_3.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The measures in (iii) and (iv) are the same, and may be chosen with at most $\dim\mathscr{H}_3$ atoms. Their total mass is
\begin{equation}\label{mass}
\mu(\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A}))=\frac{|c|}6\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}=\frac{\sqrt3}{12}\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{def:harmrep}
A finite positive measure $\mu$ on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ satisfying \eqref{kolmrep}, equivalently \eqref{cubature}, is called a \emph{Kolmogorov measure} of $u$. In the language of the truncated moment problem, a Kolmogorov measure represents the functional $h\mapsto\int_Sh\,u\,d\sigma$ on $\mathscr{H}_3$, i.e.\ it represents $u$ modulo the trace part $|x|^2\mathscr H_1$ in \eqref{harmdec}.
<!-- end definition-8 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
(i)$\Leftrightarrow$(ii). Let $V=u^\perp\subset\mathscr{H}_3$ and let $\mathcal K\subset V^*$ be the image of the compact set $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ under $c\mapsto(h\mapsto h(\hat c))$. Since $\mathscr{H}_3$ is finite dimensional, $\mathcal K$ is a compact subset of the finite-dimensional space $V^*$, and its convex hull $\operatorname{conv}\mathcal K$ is compact by Carath\'eodory's theorem. By the strict separation theorem for compact convex sets in finite dimensions, either there is $h\in V$ with $\ell(h)<0$ for all $\ell\in\mathcal K$, i.e.\ \eqref{stat} fails, or $0\in\operatorname{conv}\mathcal K$, which is (ii). By Carath\'eodory's theorem on convex hulls, $0$ is then a convex combination of at most $\dim V+1=\dim\mathscr{H}_3$ points of $\mathcal K$; this is the elementary part of Tchakaloff's theorem on positive cubature formulas \cite{Putinar-Tchakaloff,Bayer-Teichmann}.

(ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii). Let $\mu_0$ be as in (ii) and put
$$
p=\int_{\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})}Z_{\hat c}\,d\mu_0(c).
$$
Since $c\mapsto Z_{\hat c}$ is a continuous map into the finite-dimensional space $\mathscr{H}_3$, $p$ is a well-defined element of $\mathscr{H}_3$. For every $h\in\mathscr{H}_3$, by Fubini's theorem and \eqref{zonalquad},
\begin{equation}\label{kolmpair}
\int_Sh\,p\,d\sigma=\int_{\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})}\Bigl(\int_ShZ_{\hat c}\,d\sigma\Bigr)d\mu_0(c)=\frac6{N_n}\int_{\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})}h(\hat c)\,d\mu_0(c).
\end{equation}
By (ii) the right hand side vanishes for $h\in V$, so $p\perp V$ and therefore $p=\gamma u$ for some $\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{}$. Taking $h=u$ in \eqref{kolmpair} and using $u(\hat c)=M$ on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ (\Cref{the:equi}),
$$
\gamma\|u\|_2^2=\frac6{N_n}\int u(\hat c)\,d\mu_0=\frac{6M}{N_n}\,\mu_0(\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A}))>0 .
$$
Hence $\gamma>0$, and $\mu=\mu_0/\gamma$ satisfies \eqref{kolmrep}. The second expression in \eqref{kolmrep} is just the formula \eqref{zonal} for $Z_{\hat c}$.

(iii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iv). If $\mu$ satisfies \eqref{kolmrep}, then \eqref{kolmpair} with $\mu$ in place of $\mu_0$ and $p=u$ is exactly \eqref{cubature}. Conversely, if $\mu$ satisfies \eqref{cubature}, then $p=\int Z_{\hat c}\,d\mu\in\mathscr{H}_3$ satisfies $\int_Sh\,p\,d\sigma=\int_Sh\,u\,d\sigma$ for all $h\in\mathscr{H}_3$ by \eqref{kolmpair}; since $p-u\in\mathscr{H}_3$, this forces $p=u$, which is \eqref{kolmrep}. In particular the measures in (iii) and (iv) are the same, and if $\mu_0$ in (ii) has at most $\dim\mathscr{H}_3$ atoms, so does $\mu$.

(iv)$\Rightarrow$(i). Taking $h=u$ in \eqref{cubature} gives $\|u\|_2^2=\frac{6M}{N_n}\mu(\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A}))$, so $\mu\ne0$. If $h\in\mathscr{H}_3$ and $h\perp u$, then \eqref{cubature} gives $\int h(\hat c)\,d\mu(c)=0$; since $\mu$ is a non-zero positive measure, $h(\hat c)\ge0$ for some $c\in\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$, i.e.\ \eqref{stat} holds.

Finally, the identity $\|u\|_2^2=\frac{6M}{N_n}\mu(\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A}))$ together with $\|u\|_2^2=\frac{\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}}{6N_n}$ from \eqref{ratio} and $6M=1/|c|$ gives \eqref{mass}.
<!-- end proof-13 -->
<!-- remark-13: body_md -->
For a generic $p\in\mathscr{H}_3$, $E(p)$ is a single pair $\pm x_0$, and the analogue of (iii) forces $p$ to be a multiple of $Z_{x_0}$. Thus a stationary harmonic cubic which is not zonal must have a large extremal set, large enough to reproduce $p$ as a positive average of zonal harmonics. In the classical case it is the $k+1$ alternation points of $T_k$ that play this role. For Hsiang cubics the extremal set is the whole idempotent variety, which explains the analogy.
<!-- end remark-13 -->
<!-- remark-14: body_md -->
We say that $u$ \emph{admits an idempotent quadrature} if \eqref{cubature} holds; thus a Hsiang cubic is stationary exactly when it admits an idempotent quadrature, in analogy with quadrature domains, where $\int_\Omega h\,dA$ is reproduced by finitely many point evaluations for all integrable harmonic $h$. Identity \eqref{cubature} is a \emph{quadrature identity with the weight $u$} (we use the word in the sense of quadrature domains; in numerical analysis multivariate formulas of this kind are called cubature formulas): the integral of every harmonic cubic against $u\,d\sigma$ is computed exactly from its values at the idempotents. It is the analogue of the positive quadrature formulas carried by the alternation points of Chebyshev polynomials. We return to this point of view in \Cref{sec:open}.

<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
Following the terminology of the truncated moment problem \cite{Curto-Fialkow-flat,Schmudgen-book,Putinar-positive}, a positive measure $\mu$ on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ is called a \emph{representing measure} of $u$ (or of the tensor $\frac16\mathcal{T}$) if
\begin{equation}\label{repmeas}
u(x)=\int_{\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})}\langle x, \hat c\rangle^3\,d\mu(c),\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad L(x)=6\int_{\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})}\langle x, \hat c\rangle\ \hat c\otimes\hat c\ d\mu(c).
\end{equation}

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{the:moments}
Let $\mu$ be a representing measure of $u$ supported on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$. Then
\begin{align}
\int d\mu&=\tfrac{|c|}6\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K},&\int\hat c\,d\mu&=0,\label{mom01}\\
\int\hat c\otimes\hat c\,d\mu&=\tfrac{|c|}{6}\,\mathcal{K},&\int\hat c\otimes\hat c\otimes\hat c\,d\mu&=\tfrac16\,\mathcal{T}.\label{mom23}
\end{align}
Moreover, every idempotent is in equilibrium with respect to $\mu$:
\begin{equation}\label{equil}
\int_{\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})}\langle \hat c_0, \hat c\rangle^2\,\hat c\,d\mu(c)=M\,\hat c_0,\qquad c_0\in\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A}).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
The third moment is \eqref{repmeas}, the first moment was computed above, and the mass is \eqref{mass}. For \eqref{equil}, apply the second identity in \eqref{repmeas} to $x=y=\hat c_0$: the left hand side becomes $\hat c_0^{\,2}=c_0/|c|^2=\hat c_0/|c|$, while the right hand side is $6\int\langle \hat c_0, \hat c\rangle^2\hat c\,d\mu$; and $\frac1{6|c|}=M$. For the second moment $\Sigma=\int\hat c\otimes\hat c\,d\mu$ we compute, using \eqref{repmeas} twice and then \eqref{equil},
\begin{align*}
\langle Ax, y\rangle=\operatorname{tr} L(x)L(y)&=36\iint\langle x, \hat c\rangle\langle y, \hat c'\rangle\langle \hat c, \hat c'\rangle^2\,d\mu(c)\,d\mu(c')\\
&=36\int\langle y, \hat c'\rangle\Bigl\langle x,\int\langle \hat c', \hat c\rangle^2\hat c\,d\mu(c)\Bigr\rangle d\mu(c')=36M\langle \Sigma x, y\rangle.
\end{align*}
Hence $\Sigma=\frac{1}{36M}\mathcal{K}=\frac{|c|}{6}\mathcal{K}$.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:design}
Let $\mu$ be a representing measure of $u$ supported on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\mathbb{A}$ is an Einstein Hsiang algebra;
\item[(ii)] $\mu/\mu(\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A}))$ is a spherical $2$-design;
\item[(iii)] the symmetrized measure $\frac12(\mu+(-1)_*\mu)$ on the alternation set $\pm\widehat{\mathrm{Idm}}$ is a spherical $3$-design.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, these properties do not depend on the choice of the representing measure.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
A probability measure on $S$ is a $2$-design iff its first moment vanishes and its second moment is $\frac1n\operatorname{Id}$ \cite{Delsarte-Goethals-Seidel}. By \eqref{mom01}--\eqref{mom23} this happens iff $\mathcal{K}$ is a multiple of $\operatorname{Id}$. Symmetrization kills the odd moments and keeps the second one.
<!-- end proof-15 -->
<!-- remark-15: body_md -->
By the quadratic trace identity of Hsiang algebras, the Einstein property (\Cref{def:einstein}) is equivalent to the vanishing of the mean curvature of $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ in the sphere of radius $|c|$ \cite{Tkachev-book}.
Combining this with \Cref{cor:design} gives, for algebras which admit a representing measure on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{A}\ \text{is an Einstein Hsiang algebra}&\iff\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})\ \text{is minimal in the sphere}\\
&\iff\mu\ \text{is a $2$-design}\iff\pm\widehat{\mathrm{Idm}}\ \text{carries a $3$-design}.
\end{align*}

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{the:clifford}
The normalized idempotents of $\mathbb{A}$ form the sphere bundle
\begin{equation}\label{cliffidm}
\widehat{\mathrm{Idm}}=\Bigl\{\Bigl(\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\,\eta,\ \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\zeta\Bigr):\ \eta\in S^{m_0-1},\ \zeta\in E_+(\eta),\ |\zeta|=1\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}
Let $\nu$ be the probability measure on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ defined by
\begin{equation}\label{cliffnu}
\int f(\hat c)\,d\nu(c)=\int_{S^{m_0-1}}\Bigl(\int_{S_\eta}f\bigl(\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\,\eta,\sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\zeta\bigr)\,d\sigma_\eta(\zeta)\Bigr)d\sigma_{m_0}(\eta),\qquad f\in C(S^{n-1}),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $\eta$ is chosen uniformly on $S^{m_0-1}$ and then $\zeta$ uniformly on $S_\eta$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{cliffwaring}
u(x)=\frac{\sqrt3\,m_0m_1}{4}\int\langle x, \hat c\rangle^3\,d\nu(c),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
\end{equation}
so that $\mu=\frac{\sqrt3}4m_0m_1\,\nu$ is a representing measure of $u$ and $u$ is stationary. Moreover $\mathcal{K}=m_1\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb V_0}\oplus2m_0\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb V_1}$, $\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}=3m_0m_1$, and $R(u)=\dfrac{2N_n}{27m_0m_1}$.
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
\emph{The idempotents.} Let $x=(y,z)\in S^{n-1}$ and $t=|y|$, so that $|z|^2=1-t^2$. Since $P(y)^2=|y|^2\operatorname{Id}$, the eigenvalues of $P(y)$ are $\pm|y|$, hence $\langle P(y)z, z\rangle\le|y||z|^2$ and
$$
u(x)\le\tfrac12|y||z|^2=\tfrac12t(1-t^2)\le\tfrac1{3\sqrt3}=M,
$$
the last inequality being elementary (the maximum of $t(1-t^2)$ on $[0,1]$ is attained at $t=\frac1{\sqrt3}$). Equality holds throughout if and only if $t=\frac1{\sqrt3}$ and $P(y)z=|y|z$, i.e.\ $y=\frac1{\sqrt3}\eta$ with $|\eta|=1$ and $z=\sqrt{\frac23}\zeta$ with $\zeta\in S_\eta$. On the other hand, by \Cref{lem:critical} and \Cref{the:equi}(i), the maximum points of $u|_S$ are exactly the points $\hat c$, $c\in\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$. This proves \eqref{cliffidm}.

\emph{The fibre integral.} Let $\hat c=(\frac1{\sqrt3}\eta,\sqrt{\frac23}\zeta)$ and $x=(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$. Then
$$
\langle x, \hat c\rangle=a+b\langle z, \zeta\rangle,\qquad a=\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\langle y, \eta\rangle,\quad b=\sqrt{\tfrac23}.
$$
Fix $\eta$. The measure $\sigma_\eta$ is invariant under $\zeta\mapsto-\zeta$, so the odd powers of $\langle z, \zeta\rangle$ have zero integral, while, since $\sigma_\eta$ is the normalized measure on the unit sphere of the $\ell$-dimensional space $E_+(\eta)$,
$$
\int_{S_\eta}\langle z, \zeta\rangle^2\,d\sigma_\eta(\zeta)=\frac1\ell\,|\Pi(\eta)z|^2=\frac1{2\ell}\bigl(|z|^2+\langle P(\eta)z, z\rangle\bigr).
$$
Hence, using $3ab^2=2a$,
\begin{equation}\label{cliff1}
\begin{aligned}
\int_{S_\eta}\langle x, \hat c\rangle^3\,d\sigma_\eta(\zeta)&=a^3+3ab^2\int_{S_\eta}\langle z, \zeta\rangle^2\,d\sigma_\eta(\zeta)\\
&=\frac{\langle y, \eta\rangle^3}{3\sqrt3}+\frac{\langle y, \eta\rangle}{\sqrt3\,\ell}\bigl(|z|^2+\langle P(\eta)z, z\rangle\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\emph{The base integral.} Now integrate \eqref{cliff1} against $\sigma_{m_0}$. The terms $\langle y, \eta\rangle^3$ and $\langle y, \eta\rangle|z|^2$ are odd in $\eta$ and have zero integral. For the remaining term, $\langle y, \eta\rangle\langle P(\eta)z, z\rangle=\sum_{i,j}y_i\eta_i\eta_j\langle P_jz, z\rangle$, and $\int\eta_i\eta_j\,d\sigma_{m_0}=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{m_0}$ gives
$$
\int_{S^{m_0-1}}\langle y, \eta\rangle\langle P(\eta)z, z\rangle\,d\sigma_{m_0}(\eta)=\frac1{m_0}\langle P(y)z, z\rangle=\frac2{m_0}\,u(x).
$$
Thus, by \eqref{cliffnu} and $\ell=\frac{m_1}2$,
$$
\int\langle x, \hat c\rangle^3\,d\nu(c)=\frac{2}{\sqrt3\,\ell m_0}\,u(x)=\frac{4}{\sqrt3\,m_0m_1}\,u(x),
$$
which is \eqref{cliffwaring}.

\emph{The Killing operator.} In the same way, the mixed terms $\eta\otimes\zeta$ have zero integral, $\int_{S_\eta}\zeta\otimes\zeta\,d\sigma_\eta=\frac1\ell\Pi(\eta)$ and $\int_{S^{m_0-1}}\Pi(\eta)\,d\sigma_{m_0}=\frac12\operatorname{Id}$, so that
$$
\int\hat c\otimes\hat c\,d\nu=\frac1{3m_0}\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb V_0}\oplus\frac2{3m_1}\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb V_1}.
$$
Since $\mu=\frac{\sqrt3}4m_0m_1\,\nu$ is a representing measure and $|c|=\frac{\sqrt3}2$, \eqref{mom23} gives
$$
\tfrac{\sqrt3}{12}\,\mathcal{K}=\int\hat c\otimes\hat c\,d\mu=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{12}\bigl(m_1\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb V_0}\oplus2m_0\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb V_1}\bigr),
$$
which is the formula for $\mathcal{K}$, and $\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}=m_0m_1+2m_0m_1=3m_0m_1$. The value of $R(u)$ now follows from \eqref{ratio}.

<!-- definition-10: body_md -->
\label{def:psts}
A cubic form $u$ on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ admits a \emph{Steiner decomposition} if there are an orthonormal basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$, a family $B$ of $3$-element subsets $b=\{b_1,b_2,b_3\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}$ (\emph{blocks}) such that any two distinct indices lie in at most one block, and signs $\varepsilon_b\in\{\pm1\}$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{psts}
u(x)=\sum_{b\in B}\varepsilon_b\,x_{b_1}x_{b_2}x_{b_3}.
\end{equation}

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{pro:psts}
Let $u$ be a cubic form on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$, not necessarily a Hsiang cubic, which admits a Steiner decomposition \eqref{psts}, and let $\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}(u)$ be its metrized algebra. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] for every block $b$, the span of $e_{b_1},e_{b_2},e_{b_3}$ is a subalgebra of $\mathbb{A}$ isomorphic to the algebra of $x_1x_2x_3$, and the four vectors of the set
$$
\mathcal I_b=\Bigl\{\tfrac12\bigl(s_1e_{b_1}+s_2e_{b_2}+s_3e_{b_3}\bigr):\ s\in\{\pm1\}^3,\ s_1s_2s_3=\varepsilon_b\Bigr\}
$$
are idempotents of $\mathbb{A}$, called the \emph{block idempotents} of $b$; the sets $\mathcal I_b$ are pairwise disjoint, and $\mathcal I_B=\bigcup_{b\in B}\mathcal I_b$ has $4|B|$ elements;
\item[(ii)] with $\hat c=c/|c|$,
\begin{equation}\label{pstswaring}
u(x)=\frac{\sqrt3}{8}\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_B}\langle x, \hat c\rangle^3 ,
\end{equation}
and the Killing operator is diagonal,
\begin{equation}\label{pstsA}
\mathcal{K}=2\operatorname{diag}\bigl(r(1),\dots,r(n)\bigr),\qquad \operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}=6|B|,
\end{equation}
where the \emph{replication number} $r(i)$ is the number of blocks containing $i$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, if $u$ is a Hsiang cubic, then the measure with mass $\frac{\sqrt3}8$ at each point of $\mathcal I_B$ is a representing measure: $u$ admits a finite idempotent quadrature with equal weights, and $u$ is stationary.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
The structure constants $\langle e_ie_j, e_k\rangle=\partial_i\partial_j\partial_ku$ equal $\varepsilon_b$ if $\{i,j,k\}=b\in B$ and vanish otherwise; in particular $e_i^2=0$, since no monomial in \eqref{psts} contains a square. Because a pair $\{i,j\}$ lies in at most one block, $e_ie_j=\varepsilon_be_k$ for $\{i,j,k\}=b$, and $e_ie_j=0$ if $\{i,j\}$ lies in no block. Thus the span of $e_{b_1},e_{b_2},e_{b_3}$ is closed under multiplication, and the map $e_{b_1}\mapsto e_{b_1}$, $e_{b_2}\mapsto e_{b_2}$, $e_{b_3}\mapsto\varepsilon_be_{b_3}$ identifies it with the algebra of $x_1x_2x_3$. This proves the first claim of (i). For $c\in\mathcal I_b$ as in (i),
$$
c^2=\tfrac14\cdot2\sum_{\{i,j,k\}=b}s_is_j\,\varepsilon_b\,e_{k}=\tfrac12\sum_{k\in b}s_k\,e_k=c,
$$
since $s_is_j\varepsilon_b=s_is_js_1s_2s_3=s_k$. Two distinct blocks differ in at least one index, so their block idempotents have different supports, and the sets $\mathcal I_b$ are disjoint.

For (ii), fix a block $b$ and write $\hat c=\frac1{\sqrt3}(s_1e_{b_1}+s_2e_{b_2}+s_3e_{b_3})$ for $c\in\mathcal I_b$. Expanding $(s_1x_{b_1}+s_2x_{b_2}+s_3x_{b_3})^3$ and summing over the four admissible sign vectors $s$, the monomials $x_{b_i}^3$ and $x_{b_i}^2x_{b_j}$ cancel, because each of $s_i$ and $s_j$ takes the values $\pm1$ equally often on this set (with $s_i^3=s_i$, $s_i^2=1$), while the monomial $6s_1s_2s_3\,x_{b_1}x_{b_2}x_{b_3}=6\varepsilon_b\,x_{b_1}x_{b_2}x_{b_3}$ is the same for all four. Hence
$$
\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_b}\langle x, \hat c\rangle^3=\frac{24}{3\sqrt3}\,\varepsilon_b\,x_{b_1}x_{b_2}x_{b_3},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \varepsilon_b\,x_{b_1}x_{b_2}x_{b_3}=\frac{\sqrt3}8\sum_{c\in\mathcal I_b}\langle x, \hat c\rangle^3,
$$
and summation over $b\in B$ gives \eqref{pstswaring}. Finally, for $x=\sum_ix_ie_i$ and fixed $j,k$ there is at most one $i$ with $\{i,j,k\}\in B$, so that
$$
\operatorname{tr} L(x)^2=\sum_{j,k}\Bigl(\sum_ix_i\langle e_ie_j, e_k\rangle\Bigr)^2=\sum_ix_i^2\,\#\{(j,k):\{i,j,k\}\in B\}=2\sum_ir(i)\,x_i^2,
$$
which is \eqref{pstsA}; summing, $\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}=2\sum_ir(i)=6|B|$. If $u$ is a Hsiang cubic, all idempotents have length $|c|=\frac{\sqrt3}2$, which is the length of the block idempotents, and \eqref{pstswaring} is \eqref{repmeas}; its total mass $\frac{\sqrt3}2|B|=\frac{|c|}6\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K}$ agrees with \eqref{mass}.

<!-- remark-17: body_md -->
\label{rem:nonhsiang}
Parts (i) and (ii) of \Cref{pro:psts} hold for every cubic form $u$ with a Steiner decomposition: the block idempotents have length $\frac{\sqrt3}2$, the value $m=u(\hat c)=3^{-3/2}$ at them is the same, and \eqref{pstswaring} is a positive quadrature on $4|B|$ tetrahedral nodes. Moreover, $u$ is harmonic, and the proof of \Cref{the:ratio} gives $\|u\|_2^2=\operatorname{tr} \mathcal{K} /(6N_n)=|B|/N_n$. Hence, with $M=\max_Su\ge m$,
\begin{equation}\label{nonhsiang}
R(u)=\frac{N_n\,M^2}{|B|}=\frac{N_n}{27|B|}\Bigl(\frac Mm\Bigr)^2\ \ge\ \frac{N_n}{27|B|},
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if the block idempotents are maximum points of $u|_S$. For a Hsiang cubic this is automatic by \Cref{the:equi}. In general it fails: for the Fano plane, $u=\sum x_ix_jx_k$ over its seven lines on $\mathbb{R}^{7}$, the point $x=(1,\dots,1)/\sqrt7$ gives $M\ge7^{-1/2}$, so $(M/m)^2\ge\frac{27}7$; the block idempotents are not maximum points, and the tetrahedral quadrature carries no information about the first variation of $R$.

                                                                                          
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              
    
                                                        
    
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   
                                     
The combinatorics alone is not sufficient: the sign decoration $(\varepsilon_b)$ of the blocks, which is determined by the algebra and not by the Steiner system, matters as well \cite{HsiangStrongRigidity}. Thus the Steiner system provides the scale $N_n/(27|B|)$, and the Hsiang identity, through the signs, is what makes this scale the actual value of $R$.

\label{the:homog}
Assume that

If $v\ne0$ were a $G$-fixed vector, then $|x|^2\langle x, v\rangle$ would be a $G$-invariant cubic form which is not a multiple of the harmonic form $u$; by (b), $G$ has no non-zero fixed vector. <!-- theorem-10: body_md -->
\label{the:strict}
Let $u$ be a Hsiang cubic which admits a Kolmogorov measure $\mu$ with $\operatorname{supp}\mu=\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$, and assume $D=o(u)$. Then $u$ is a strict local minimizer of $R$ modulo rotations and scaling: there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $u$ in $\mathscr{H}_3$ such that $R(p)\ge R(u)$ for $p\in U$, with equality only if $p=t\,u\circ g$ for some $t>0$ and $g\in O(n)$.
<!-- end theorem-10 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
Let $W=u^\perp\cap o(u)^\perp\subset\mathscr{H}_3$. For $h\in W\setminus\{0\}$ we have $h\notin D$; by \eqref{cubature}, $\int h(\hat c)\,d\mu=0$, and since $h$ does not vanish identically on $\operatorname{supp}\mu=\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$, it takes a positive value there. Hence $\phi(h)=\max_ch(\hat c)>0$ on $W\setminus\{0\}$, and by homogeneity and compactness $\phi(h)\ge\delta\|h\|_2$ for some $\delta>0$. Since $(u+h)(\hat c)=M+h(\hat c)$ exactly,
$$
R(u+h)\ge\frac{(M+\delta\|h\|_2)^2}{\|u\|_2^2+\|h\|_2^2}>\frac{M^2}{\|u\|_2^2}=R(u)\qquad\text{for }0<\|h\|_2<\varepsilon_0.
$$
The map $\Phi(t,g,h)=t\,(u+h)\circ g$ from $\mathbb{R}^{}_{>0}\times O(n)\times W$ to $\mathscr{H}_3$ has surjective differential at $(1,\operatorname{Id},0)$, since its image is $\mathbb{R}^{}u+o(u)+W=\mathscr{H}_3$. By the inverse function theorem, every $p$ near $u$ can therefore be written as $p=t\,(u+h)\circ g$ with $h\in W$ small, and $R(p)=R(u+h)$.

<!-- corollary-7: body_md -->
\label{cor:strict}
The cubic $x_1x_2x_3$ on $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ ($\mathsf{Pol}(1,2)$) and the Lawson cubic on $\mathbb{R}^{4}$ ($\mathsf{Pol}(2,2)$) are strict local minimizers of $R$ on $\mathscr{H}_3$ modulo rotations and scaling. Consequently
$$
\kappa_3\le\tfrac{35}9,\qquad \kappa_4\le\tfrac{32}9,
$$
and each of these values is a strict local minimum value of $R$.
<!-- end corollary-7 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
We apply \Cref{the:strict}. Representing measures with full support on $\mathrm{Idm}(\mathbb{A})$ are the four equal atoms on the tetrahedron for $x_1x_2x_3$ (\Cref{ex:x1x2x3}) and the arc length for the Lawson cubic (\Cref{pro:lawson}). It remains to verify $D=o(u)$, i.e.\ $\dim D=\dim o(u)$.

                                                                                          
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  
    
                                                                                                            
                          
                                       
\emph{$x_1x_2x_3$.} Here $G$ is finite, so $\dim o(u)=3$. The evaluation map $\mathscr{H}_3\to\mathbb{R}^{4}$, $h\mapsto(h(\hat c_\varepsilon))_\varepsilon$, is onto: on the four points $\hat c_\varepsilon=\varepsilon/\sqrt3$ the harmonic cubic $x_1x_2x_3$ is constant, and the zonal harmonics $Z_{e_i}$, $i=1,2,3$, are non-zero multiples of $\varepsilon_i$; the four resulting vectors are the rows of a Hadamard matrix. Hence $\dim D=7-4=3$.

\emph{Lawson.} A direct computation gives $\dim o(u)=5$ (so the Lie algebra of $G$ is one-dimensional), and $\dim\mathscr{H}_3=16$. Using the rational parametrization $\cos\frac\varphi2=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}$, $\sin\frac\varphi2=\frac{2t}{1+t^2}$ of \eqref{lawsonidm}, the condition $h|_{\widehat{\mathrm{Idm}}}=0$ becomes the vanishing of two polynomials in $t$ with rational coefficients (the coefficients of $1$ and $\frac1{\sqrt2}$). The resulting linear map $\mathscr{H}_3\to\mathbb Q[t]^2$ has rank $11$, as elimination over $\mathbb Q$ shows; hence $\dim D=5$.

<!-- remark-19: body_md -->
\label{rem:cartanlocal}
For $n=5$ local minimality is superseded by \Cref{the:cartanmin}: Cartan's cubic $\mathcal{C}_{1}$ is the global minimizer of $R$ on $\mathbb{R}^{5}$, unique up to rotations and scaling, so that $\kappa_5=3$. <!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:joint}
On $S^4$,
\begin{equation}\label{joint}
|u+i\,u_L|\le M,
\end{equation}
and equality at a point $x$ implies $u(x)=0$ or $u_L(x)=0$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
The torus $\mathbb T^2$ acts orthogonally on $\mathbb{R}^{}\times\mathbb C\times\mathbb C$ by $(v_0,z,w)\mapsto(v_0,e^{i\alpha}z,e^{i\beta}w)$. It preserves $u$ and rotates the phase of $zw^2$. Therefore the joint amplitude $|u+iu_L|$ is controlled by the orbit invariants $a=|z|^2$, $b=|w|^2$, $c=v_0^2$, which range over the simplex $a+b+c=1$:
$$
4|u+iu_L|^2=c\,(a-b)^2+\operatorname{Re}(zw^2)^2\le c\,(a-b)^2+ab^2 .
$$
Compare this with $u$ on its meridian $w=0$: at the point with $|z|^2=m$, $v_0^2=1-m$ it takes the value $p(m)=\frac12m\sqrt{1-m}$, and $\max_{[0,1]}p=M$, attained only at $m=\frac23$ (this is the equioscillation of $u$, \Cref{the:equi}). We claim that the joint amplitude is dominated by this meridian profile:
$$
c\,(a-b)^2+ab^2\le 4p(m)^2=m^2(1-m),\qquad m=\max(a,b).
$$
Indeed, if $a\ge b$ then $(a-b)^2\le a^2$ and $ab^2\le a^2b$, so the left hand side is at most $a^2(c+b)=a^2(1-a)$; if $b\ge a$ then $(a-b)^2\le b^2$ and the left hand side is at most $b^2(c+a)=b^2(1-b)$. Hence $|u+iu_L|\le p(m)\le M$. Equality forces $m=\frac23$ and equality in the elementary estimates, i.e.\ $(a,b,c)\in\{(\frac23,0,\frac13),(0,\frac23,\frac13),(\frac13,\frac23,0)\}$. In the first two cases $u_L(x)=0$, in the third $u(x)=0$.
<!-- end proof-25 -->
<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{pro:polarsaddle}
For every $\theta\notin\frac\pi2\mathbb Z$,
$$
R(\cos\theta\,u+\sin\theta\,u_L)<R(u)=R(u_L).
$$
In particular, the cubic $u$ of $\mathsf{Pol}(1,4)$ is stationary, but it is not a local minimizer of $R$.
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
First, $u\perp u_L$ in $L^2(\sigma)$, because $u$ is even and $u_L$ is odd under $z\mapsto-z$, and $\|u_L\|_2=\|u\|_2$ by \Cref{lem:fischer}, since $[u_L,u_L]=[u,u]=2$. Hence $p_\theta=\cos\theta\,u+\sin\theta\,u_L$ satisfies $\|p_\theta\|_2=\|u\|_2$ for all $\theta$, and $p_\theta=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(e^{-i\theta}(u+iu_L)\bigr)$. By \Cref{lem:joint}, $p_\theta\le M$ on $S^4$, and equality at $x$ would require $|u+iu_L|(x)=M$ and $\arg(u+iu_L)(x)=\theta$; for $\theta\notin\frac\pi2\mathbb Z$ this contradicts the second part of the lemma. By compactness, and since $p_\theta$ is odd, $\|p_\theta\|_\infty<M$, so $R(p_\theta)<M^2/\|u\|_2^2=R(u)$. For $\theta=\frac\pi2$ we get $R(u_L)=R(u)$, since $\max_{S^4}u_L=M$.
<!-- end proof-26 -->
<!-- remark-20: body_md -->
Thus on the great circle $\{p_\theta\}$ of the sphere $\{\|p\|_2=\|u\|_2\}\subset\mathscr{H}_3$ through the cubic of $\mathsf{Pol}(1,4)$ and the Lawson cubic, the Chebyshev ratio attains its maximum $R(u)$ exactly at $\pm u$ and $\pm u_L$ and is strictly smaller everywhere else. In particular, not every Hsiang cubic is a local minimizer of the Chebyshev problem. By \eqref{cartansplit}, the direction of decrease from $u$ points towards Cartan's cubic $\mathcal{C}_{1}$, the global minimizer in $\mathbb{R}^{5}$ (\Cref{the:cartanmin}), and it does so through the Lawson cubic.
