<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\normalfont\label{re1}
The motivation behind studying this optimization problem is somehow indirect, although the problem is interesting on its own as well. The starting point of our work was to solve portfolio optimization problems where the risk is modelled by the entropic value-at-risk by means of proximal point methods. Noticing a certain resemblance of the portfolio optimization problem considered in \cite{AJF} to the \textit{Fenchel-Lagrange dual problem} (see \cite{DVO}) obtained in \cite{BGW}, we consider a generalization of the primal entropy constrained linear optimization problem treated in the latter, that is $(P)$. Later we see that its \textit{Lagrange dual problem} turns out in a special case to be the mentioned portfolio optimization problem \cite[(4.7)]{AJF}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\normalfont\label{re2}
By using Lagrange duality, it can easily be verified that the optimal solution of the optimization problem that consists in maximizing the \textit{weighted Shannon entropy} (see, for instance, \cite{GUI}) subject to linear constraints
\begin{eqnarray*}
~~\max_{x_j\geq 0,~j=1,\hdots,m,\atop \sum\limits_{j=1}^m p_jx_j=1} \left\{-\sum_{j=1}^mp_jx_j\ln x_j\right\},
\end{eqnarray*}
is $\bar x_i=1/(\sum_{j=1}^mp_j),~i=1,\hdots,m$, and its optimal objective value is equal to $\ln (\sum_{j=1}^mp_j)$.
Hence, if $H>\ln (\sum_{j=1}^mp_j)$, then the feasible set of $(P)$ is empty. Moreover, if $H=\ln (\sum_{j=1}^mp_j)$, then the $x$-components of any optimal solution $(\bar x, \bar y)$ to $(P)$ are $\bar x_j=1/(\sum_{j=1}^mp_j)$, $j=1,\hdots,m$, and $(P)$ reduces to the unconstrained convex optimization problem
\begin{eqnarray}\label{pr2}
~~c_p^{\top} \bar x+\inf_{y\in \mathbb{R}^d,~By\leqq h-A_p\overline{x}} \sigma_D(y),
\end{eqnarray}
iteratively solvable by existing proximal splitting algorithms.    
                                                                              
                                   
                                                                                           
                                    
                
                                                         

Finally, to determine the minimal value of $-\sum_{j=1}^mp_jx_j\ln x_j$ subject to $\sum_{j=1}^mp_jx_j=1$, $x_j\geq 0$, $j=1,\hdots, m$, we set $z_j=p_jx_j,~j=1,\hdots,m$, and get the following minimization problem consisting in minimizing the \textit{Kullback-Leibler entropy} subject to the usual linear constraints
\begin{equation}\label{eq3}
~~\inf_{z_j\geq 0,~j=1,\hdots,m,\atop \sum\limits_{j=1}^mz_j=1}\left\{-\sum\limits_{j=1}^mz_j\ln\left(\frac{z_j}{p_j}\right)\right\}=\min_{z_j\geq 0,~j=1,\hdots,m,\atop \sum\limits_{j=1}^mz_j=1}\left\{-\sum\limits_{j=1}^m(z_j\ln z_j-z_j\ln p_j)\right\}.
\end{equation}
For attaining the minimum in \eqref{eq3}, the sum $-\sum_{j=1}^mz_j\ln z_j\geq 0$ must be equal to zero and, as $z_j\ln p_j\leq 0$ for all $j=1,\hdots,m$, an optimal solution to \eqref{eq3} is $\bar z_l=1$, $\bar z_j=0$ for all $j=1,\hdots,m,~j\neq l$, where $l = \min\{i\in \{1,\hdots,m\}: p_i=\min\{p_1,\hdots,p_m\}\}$. Hence, we have that $\bar x_l=1/p_l$, $\bar x_j=0$ for all $j=1,\hdots,m$, $j\neq l$, solves $(P)$, whose minimal value is $\min\{\ln p_1,\hdots,\ln p_m\}=\ln p_l$. In this case, the constraint involving entropy of $(P)$ is automatically fulfilled, and $(P)$ collapses into a convex optimization problem with affine constraints similar to \eqref{pr2}, which can be solved via existing splitting proximal point methods.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\normalfont\label{re3}
Note that the objective function of the problem $(P)$ is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous. For any fixed $y\in \mathbb{R}^n$, the set                                                                                                           
$$
\mathcal{A}=\left\{x\in \mathbb{R}_+^m:A_px+By\leqq h,~\sum\limits_{j=1}^mp_jx_j=1,~ \sum\limits_{j=1}^mp_jx_j\ln x_j\leq -H\right\}
$$
is closed, convex and bounded, thus also compact, so one gets by the generalized Weierstrass theorem that the infimum regarding $x\in \mathcal{A}$ is attained in the optimization problem $(P)$ for any $y\in \mathbb{R}^n$.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\normalfont\label{re5}\normalfont
One can show that $(D_L)$ coincides with the Fenchel-Lagrange dual problem to $(P)$ (see \cite{DVO}).

\end{eqnarray*}

which proves that the last bracket is also nonnegative. In summary, all brackets in \eqref{equal} are nonnegative and must therefore be equal to zero, i.e. the optimality conditions $(i)-(vi)$ are fulfilled. Clearly, optimality condition $(vi)$ is valid by feasibility.

\normalfont  Let $X^*\in L^q$, where $q\in (1, +\infty)$, $\alpha\in (0,1]$ and the function $g:L^q\rightarrow \overline{\mathbb{R}}$ be defined by
\begin{eqnarray*}
g(X^*)= \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} 0, \qquad
\textnormal{if } X^*\in L^q_+,~ \mathbb{E}(X^*\ln(X^*))\leq -\ln\alpha,~\mathbb{E}X^*=1,\\
+ \infty,  \textnormal{ otherwise}. \end{array} \right.
\end{eqnarray*}

It can be verified that $g$ is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous, and its conjugate function $g^*:L^p\rightarrow \overline{\mathbb{R}}$
(where $p\in (1, +\infty)$ is the \textit{H\"older conjugate} of $q$, i.e. it fulfills $1/p+1/q=1$) is given by
\begin{eqnarray}
g^*(X)=\sup_{X^*\in L^q_+,~\mathbb{E}X^*=1,\atop \mathbb{E}(X^*\ln(X^*))\leq -\ln\alpha} \langle X^*,X\rangle.\label{g_formula}
\end{eqnarray}
For a fixed $X\in L^p$, the negative of the right-hand side in \eqref{g_formula} is a convex optimization problem. By the corresponding weak Lagrange duality, one obtains
\begin{eqnarray}
g^*(X)\leq\inf_{\lambda\geq 0,~\gamma\in \mathbb{R}}\left\{\sup_{X^*\in L^q_+}\{\langle X^*,X\rangle-\lambda\mathbb{E}(X^*\ln(X^*))-\gamma\langle 1_{\Omega},X^*\rangle\}+\gamma-\lambda\ln\alpha\right\}.\label{g_formula1}
\end{eqnarray}
Moreover, applying \cite[Theorem 3.2.9.]{DVO} follows that the strong duality holds for this pair of primal-dual optimization problems, i.e. in \eqref{g_formula1} equality holds and the infimum in the right-hand side is attained. To see this, let $C=\mathbb{R}_+\times \{0\}$ and take $h:L^q\rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2\cup\{\infty_{\mathbb{R}^2_+}\}$,
\begin{eqnarray*}
h(X^*)= \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} (\mathbb{E}(X^*\ln(X^*))+\ln\alpha,\mathbb{E}X^*-1)^{\top},
\textnormal{ if } X^*\in L^q_+,\\
\infty_{\mathbb{R}^2_+},  \textnormal{ otherwise,} \end{array} \right.
\end{eqnarray*}
then it holds $h(\operatorname*{dom} \langle X,\cdot\rangle\cap \operatorname*{dom} h)=h(\operatorname*{dom} h)=[\ln\alpha+\min_{X^*\in L^q_+}\mathbb{E}(X^*\ln(X^*)),+\infty)\times[-1,+\infty)$, where it is obvious that $\min_{X^*\in L^q_+}\mathbb{E}(X^*\ln(X^*))<0$, and therefore,
\begin{eqnarray*}
0_{\mathbb{R}^2}\in\operatorname*{ri}\left(h(\operatorname*{dom} h)+C \right)=\operatorname*{ri} \left[\ln\alpha+\min_{X^*\in L^q_+}\mathbb{E}(X^*\ln(X^*)),+\infty\right)\times\operatorname*{ri}[-1,+\infty).
\end{eqnarray*}

\begin{eqnarray*}

(p^2z^2+p^2z+1)e^z-p\xi(z+1)-x=0
\end{eqnarray}
that fulfills $p\bar ze^{\bar z}\geq \xi$. More precisely, for $\xi\leq -p/e$ it holds $\bar z\in \mathbb{R}$, when $-p/e<\xi\leq 0$ and $x\leq 1/e$ one has $\bar z\in (-\infty,W_{-1}(\xi/p)]$, and when $-p/e<\xi\leq 0$ and $x> 1/e$ one has $\bar z\in [W(\xi/p),+\infty]$, while if $\xi>0$ it holds $\bar z\in [W(\xi/p),+\infty)$.

Note first that as the function $h$ is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous, its epigraph is nonempty, convex and closed, thus the projection onto it is well defined. Then, for any $(x,\xi), (\overline{y}, \overline{\theta})\in \mathbb{R}^2$ \cite[Proposition 6.46]{BCO} asserts that $(\overline{y}, \overline{\theta})=P_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(x,\xi)$ if and only if $(x-\overline{y}, \xi-\overline{\theta})\in N_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(\overline{y}, \overline{\theta})$.

Clearly, when $(x,\xi)\in \operatorname*{epi} h$ it holds $P_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(x,\xi)=(x,\xi)$. Assume further that $(x,\xi)\notin \operatorname*{epi} h$, that can happen in exactly two situations, either when $x<0$ or if $px\ln x>\xi$ when $x\geq 0$.

                                                                                                     

The first case we treat is when $x<0$ and $\xi \geq 0$. Then one has $(0, \xi)\in \operatorname*{epi} h$ and
$$\sqrt{(x-0)^2+(\xi-\xi)^2} \leq \sqrt{(x-y)^2+(\xi-\theta)^2}\quad  \forall (y, \theta)\in \operatorname*{epi} h,$$
that, combined, yield $P_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(x,\xi)=(0,\xi)$.

Let us assume now that $P_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(x,\xi)=(0,0)$, to see if other points $(x,\xi)$ besides those on the half-axe $(-\infty,0]\times \{0\}$ are projected on $\operatorname*{epi} h$ in the origin. By \cite[Proposition 6.46]{BCO} one gets the equivalent formulation $(x,\xi)\in N_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(0,0)$. If $\xi\neq 0$, then it holds by the definition of the normal cone that
$$xy+\xi \theta\leq 0 \text{ for all } (y,\theta)\in \operatorname*{epi} h \Rightarrow xy+\xi py\ln y\leq 0\quad  \forall y> 0$$
\begin{eqnarray}\label{epih}
\Leftrightarrow x+\xi p\ln y\leq 0\quad  \forall y> 0 \Leftrightarrow  x\leq-\xi p\ln y\quad  \forall y> 0.
\end{eqnarray}
For $\xi>0$ we get by \eqref{epih} that
$$
x\leq-\xi p\lim_{y\rightarrow +\infty}\ln y=-\infty \Rightarrow x=-\infty,
$$
while when $\xi<0$ one gets
$$
x\leq-\xi p\lim_{y\rightarrow 0}\ln y=-\infty \Rightarrow x=-\infty.
$$
In both these cases one cannot have $(x,\xi)\in N_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(0,0)$, therefore a contradiction has been reached and the only remaining possibility is $\xi=0$, when \eqref{epih} yields $x\leq 0$. Consequently $N_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(0,0)=(-\infty,0]\times\{0\}$.

Note that in a similar manner one can show that $N_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(0,\overline{\theta})=(-\infty,0]\times\{\overline{\theta}\}$ for any $\overline{\theta}> 0$, which guarantees that the only points having their projections onto $\operatorname*{epi} h$ on the half-axe $\{0\}\times (0, +\infty)$ are the ones identified above that lie in the set $(-\infty,0)\times (0, +\infty)$.

Due to the fact that $\operatorname*{dom} h=[0, +\infty)$, the only remaining possibility for some pair $(\overline{y},\overline{\theta})\in \mathbb{R}^2$ to be the projection of $(x,\xi)$
onto $\operatorname*{epi} h$ is to have $\overline{y}>0$, that is equivalent to $(x,\xi)-(\overline{y},\overline{\theta})\in N_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(\overline{y},\overline{\theta})$. Noticing that since $\overline{y}>0$ it holds $\partial h(\overline{y})=\{h'(y)\}$ where $h'(y)=p\ln y+p$, one gets for the normal cone that (see Section 2.1 in \cite{ABB})
\begin{eqnarray*}
N_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(\overline{y},\overline{\theta})=\{(\lambda\gamma,-\lambda):\gamma\in \partial h(\overline{y})\text{ and }\lambda\geq 0\}
\end{eqnarray*}
and as $(x,\xi)-(\overline{y},\overline{\theta})\in N_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(\overline{y},\overline{\theta})$, we have
\begin{eqnarray}
\left\{ \begin{array}{ll}x-\overline{y}=\lambda\gamma, \\
\xi-\overline{\theta}=-\lambda, \end{array} \right.\Leftrightarrow\left\{ \begin{array}{ll}x-\overline{y}=\lambda(p\ln \overline{y}+p), \\
\overline{\theta}=\xi+\lambda. \end{array} \right.\label{epih2}
\end{eqnarray}
If $\lambda=0$ one has by \eqref{epih2} that $\overline{y}=x$ and $\overline{\theta}=\xi$, i.e. $(x,\xi)\in \operatorname*{epi} h$, which is a contradiction to our assumption from the beginning of this proof. Therefore, $\lambda>0$. Moreover, notice that by \cite[Proposition 28.28]{BCO} it holds $\overline{\theta}=p\overline{y}\ln\overline{y}$. Further, by using the Lambert $W$ function and the property $z=W(z)e^{W(z)}$ for all $z\in \mathbb{R}$ (see \cite{BLI}) we have that
\begin{eqnarray}
\ln\overline{y}=\frac{\overline{\theta}}{p\overline{y}}\Leftrightarrow \overline{y}=e^{\frac{\theta}{p\overline{y}}}\Leftrightarrow \frac{\overline{\theta}}{p}=\frac{\overline{\theta}}{p\overline{y}}e^{\frac{\theta}{p\overline{y}}}\Leftrightarrow W\left(\frac{\overline{\theta}}{p}\right)=\frac{\overline{\theta}}{p\overline{y}}\Leftrightarrow \overline{y}=\frac{\frac{\overline{\theta}}{p}}{W\left(\frac{\overline{\theta}}{p}\right)}\Leftrightarrow\overline{y}=e^{W\left(\frac{\overline{\theta}}{p}\right)},\label{epih3}
\end{eqnarray}
hence, $\overline{\theta}=p\overline{y}\ln \overline{y}=pze^z$ where $z:=W(\overline{\theta}/p)$. Combining \eqref{epih2} and \eqref{epih3} reveals that
\begin{eqnarray*}
&&x-e^z=\lambda(pz+p)\Leftrightarrow \lambda=\frac{x-e^z}{p(z+1)}\Leftrightarrow \frac{\lambda+\xi}{p}=\frac{x-e^z+p\xi (z+1)}{p^2(z+1)}\notag\\
&\Leftrightarrow& \frac{\overline{\theta}}{p}=\frac{x-e^z+p\xi (z+1)}{p^2(z+1)}\Leftrightarrow W\left(\frac{\overline{\theta}}{p}\right)e^{W\left(\frac{\overline{\theta}}{p}\right)}=\frac{x-e^z+p\xi (z+1)}{p^2(z+1)}\notag\\
&\Leftrightarrow& ze^z=\frac{x-e^z+p\xi (z+1)}{p^2(z+1)}\Leftrightarrow (p^2z^2+p^2z+1)e^z-p\xi(z+1)-x=0,\label{epih4}
\end{eqnarray*}
which, taking into consideration \eqref{epih2} verifies the last case.

Finally, it remains to show the uniqueness of the solution of \eqref{root} when $(x, \xi) \notin \operatorname*{epi} h \cup (-\infty, 0]\times [0, +\infty)$. For this purpose, we define the function $F:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ by $$F(z)=(p^2z^2+p^2z+1)e^z-p\xi(z+1)-x=p(z+1)(pze^z-\xi)+e^z-x,$$
whose derivative is
$$
F'(z)=(2p^2z+p^2)e^z+(p^2z^2+p^2z+1)e^z-p\xi=e^zp^2(z+1)^2+e^z+p(pze^z-\xi),\ z\in \mathbb{R}.
$$
One has
\begin{equation}\label{eq21}
\lim_{z\rightarrow +\infty}F(z)= p^2\lim_{z\rightarrow +\infty}(z^2e^z) +p\lim_{z\rightarrow +\infty}z(pe^z-\xi)+\lim_{z\rightarrow +\infty} e^z-p\xi-x=+\infty,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq22}
\lim_{z\rightarrow -\infty}F(z) = p^2\lim_{z\rightarrow -\infty}(z^2e^z)+p\lim_{z\rightarrow -\infty}z(pe^z-\xi)+\lim_{z\rightarrow -\infty} e^z-p\xi-x=
\left\{
\begin{array}{cc}
 \operatorname*{sgn}(\xi) (+\infty), & \mbox{if } \xi\neq 0,\\
  -x, & \mbox{if } \xi=0,
\end{array}\right.
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname*{sgn} (\cdot)$ is the usual \textit{signum function}, as well as
$\lim_{z\rightarrow +\infty}F'(z)=+\infty$ and $\lim_{z\rightarrow -\infty}F'(z)=-p\xi$.
Moreover, $F''(z) = e^z\big(p^2z^2 + 5p^2z + 4p^2+1\big)$, $z\in \mathbb{R}$. Analyzing this function, one notices that $F''(z)>0$ when $0<p<2/3$, $F''(z)\geq 0$ when $p=2/3$, while when
$p>2/3$ $F''$ takes nonnegative values outside the interval $\big( (-5p-\sqrt{9p^2-4})/(2p), (-5p+\sqrt{9p^2-4})/(2p)\big)$, on which it is negative.

This shows that when $p\in (0, 2/3]$ the function $F'$ is always positive when $\xi<0$, in which situation $F$ is strictly increasing and, consequently,
the solution $\bar z\in \mathbb{R}$ to the equation \eqref{root} is unique and it can be shown that it verifies $p\bar ze^{\bar z}\geq \xi$. However, in case $p>2/3$ the calculations become more complicated and a different approach is required. Note also that, depending on the values of $p$, $x$ and $\xi$ (as considered above) equation \eqref{root} has at least one and up to four solutions.

                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           

In the following, we distinguish six cases identified by the possible values of $x$ and $\xi$, when $p>0$.

$(i)$ When $\xi\leq-p/e$, noting that
\begin{eqnarray}\label{eq19}
\min_{z\in \mathbb{R}}\{pze^z\}=-\frac pe,
\end{eqnarray}
it follows $F'(z)> 0$ $\forall z\in \mathbb{R}$, so $F$ is strictly monotone increasing in this situation, consequently \eqref{eq21} and \eqref{eq22} yield that the solution $\bar z\in \mathbb{R}$ to the equation \eqref{root} is unique. By \eqref{eq19}, it also verifies $p\bar ze^{\bar z}\geq \xi$.

$(ii)$ When $-p/e<\xi\leq0$ and $x<0$, one has
\begin{eqnarray}\label{eq20}
F'(z)=e^zp^2(z+1)^2+e^z+p(pze^z-\xi)>0 \quad  \forall z\in \mathbb{R} \text{ such that } pze^z\geq \xi.
\end{eqnarray}
Since $\lim_{z\rightarrow +\infty} pze^z = +\infty$ and $\lim_{z\rightarrow -\infty} pze^z = 0$, taking into consideration \eqref{eq19} one can show that the equation $pze^z=\xi$ has two solutions, say $\tilde{z}, \hat{z} \in \mathbb{R}$ with $\tilde{z}<-1 < \hat{z}<0$. Via \eqref{eq20} one concludes that $F$ is strictly increasing on $(-\infty, \tilde{z}]$ and on $[\hat{z}, +\infty)$, and can decrease only on a subinterval of $(\tilde{z}, \hat{z})$. Since $F(\tilde{z})>0$ and $F(\hat{z})>0$, by \eqref{eq21} and \eqref{eq22} follows the uniqueness of the solution $\bar z<\tilde{z}$ to \eqref{root} under the current hypotheses. As $p\bar ze^{\bar z}\geq \xi$ can be equivalently written as $\bar z=W_{-1}(\bar ze^{\bar z})\leq W_{-1}(\xi/p)$, one concludes $\bar z\in(-\infty,W_{-1}(\xi/p)]$.

$(iii)$ When $-p/e<\xi\leq0$ and $0\leq x\leq 1/e$, one determines again $\tilde{z}, \hat{z} \in \mathbb{R}$. Then $px\ln x>p\tilde{z}e^{\tilde{z}}=p\hat{z}e^{\hat{z}}=\xi$, which implies by monotonicity (as the function $h$ is strictly decreasing on $[0, 1/e]$) that $x<e^{\tilde{z}}<e^{\hat{z}}$, therefore $F(\tilde{z})>0$ and $F(\hat{z})>0$. The conclusion from the previous case can be drawn in this situation, too.

$(iv)$ When $-p/e<\xi\leq0$ and $1/e<x\leq 1$, one determines again $\tilde{z}, \hat{z} \in \mathbb{R}$. Then $px\ln x>p\overline{z}e^{\overline{z}}=\xi$, which implies by monotonicity (as the function $h$ is strictly increasing on $(1/e, +\infty)$) that $e^{\tilde{z}}<e^{\hat{z}}<x$, therefore $F(\tilde{z})<0$ and $F(\hat{z})<0$. Then equation \eqref{root} has a unique solution $\bar z>\hat{z}$ such that $\xi \leq p\bar ze^{\bar z}$. As $p\bar ze^{\bar z}\geq \xi$ can be equivalently written in this case as $\bar z=W(\bar ze^{\bar z})\geq W(\xi/p)$, one concludes $\bar z\in[W(\xi/p),+\infty)$.

$(v)$ When $-p/e<\xi\leq0$ and $x> 1$, one determines again $\tilde{z}, \hat{z} \in \mathbb{R}$. As $e^{\tilde{z}}<e^{\hat{z}}<x$, $F(\tilde{z})<0$ and $F(\hat{z})<0$, thus the conclusion from the previous case can be drawn in this situation, too.

$(vi)$ When $\xi \geq 0$, the only situation not covered yet in this analysis is when $px\ln x>\xi\geq 0$, that yields $x>1$. Moreover, one can show that the equation $pze^z=\xi$ has only one solution, say $\hat{z}\in \mathbb{R}$ with $\hat{z}>0$. Then again $x>e^{\hat{z}}$, thus $F(\hat{z})<0$ and the uniqueness of the solution of \eqref{root} that verifies $p\bar ze^{\bar z}\geq \xi$, i.e. lies in the interval $[W(\xi/p),+\infty)$, follows analogously to the previous case.

\label{co4} Let $0<p\leq 1$, then it holds for all $(x,\xi)\in \mathbb{R}\times \mathbb{R}$ that
\begin{eqnarray*}
P_{\operatorname*{epi} h}(x,\xi)=\begin{cases}
 (x,\xi), & \quad \text{if } px\ln x\leq \xi,\\
 (0,\xi), & \quad \text{if } x\leq0\text{ and }\xi\geq 0,\\
 (e^{\bar z},p\bar ze^{\bar z}),  & \quad \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{eqnarray*}
where $\bar z\in \mathbb{R}$ is the unique solution of \eqref{root} that fulfills $p\bar ze^{\bar z}\geq \xi$. More precisely, exactly one of the following six cases holds\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] $\xi\leq -\frac{p}{e}$ and $x<0$, then $\overline{z}\in \mathbb{R}$ is unique and $\overline{z}\in(-\infty,0]$;
  \item[(ii)] $\xi\leq -\frac{p}{e}$ and $x\geq 0$, then $\overline{z}\in \mathbb{R}$ is unique and $\overline{z}\in (-\infty,x]$;
    \item[(iii)] $-\frac{p}{e} <\xi \leq 0$ and $x<0$, then $\overline{z}\in \mathbb{R}$ is unique and $\bar z\in (-\infty,W_{-1}(\xi/p)]$;
  \item[(iv)] $-\frac{p}{e} <\xi \leq 0$ and $0\leq x\leq \frac{1}{e}$, then $\overline{z}\in \left[W_{-1}\left(\frac{\xi}{p}\right)+\ln(x)-1,W_{-1}\left(\frac{\xi}{p}\right)\right]$;
  \item[(v)] $-\frac{p}{e} <\xi \leq 0$ and $x> \frac{1}{e}$, then $\overline{z}\in\left[W\left(\frac{\xi}{p}\right),W\left(\frac{\xi}{p}\right)+\ln(x)+2\right]$;
  \item[(vi)] $\xi>0$, then $x>1$ and $\overline{z}\in \left[W\left(\frac{\xi}{p}\right),W\left(\frac{\xi}{p}\right)+\ln(x)+1\right]$.
\end{enumerate}

Theorem \ref{ThmEpi} implies immediately the formulae for $P_{\operatorname*{epi} h}$, the equation and the situation when $\xi\leq -\frac{p}{e}$ and $x\in \mathbb{R}$, then $\overline{z}\in \mathbb{R}$. When $x<0$, then $F(0)=1-p\xi-x>0$ and together with the fact that $F'(z)>0$ for all $z\in \mathbb{R}$ one gets case $(i)$.

If $x\geq 0$, then $F(x)=p(x+1)(pxe^x-\xi)+e^x-x>0$ and again by using the fact that $F'(z)>0$ for all $z\in \mathbb{R}$ follows case $(ii)$.

When $-p/e <\xi \leq 0$ and $x<0$, then it is mentioned in the proof of Theorem \ref{ThmEpi} that $F$ can decrease only on the subinterval of $(\tilde{z},\hat{z})$ where $\tilde{z}<-1<\hat{z}<0$. If one takes any $\breve{z}\in(\tilde{z},\hat{z})$ with $\breve{z}<-1$, then $p\breve{z}e^{\breve{z}}<\xi$ and
\begin{eqnarray*}
F(\breve{z})=\underbrace{p(\breve{z}+1)}_{<0}\underbrace{(p\breve{z}e^{\breve{z}}-\xi)}_{<0}+\underbrace{e^{\breve{z}}-x}_{>0}>0.
\end{eqnarray*}
Further, for any $\breve{z}\in(\tilde{z},\hat{z})$ with $\breve{z}>-1$ it also holds $p\breve{z}e^{\breve{z}}<\xi$ and
\begin{eqnarray*}
F(\breve{z})=\underbrace{(p^2\breve{z}^2+p^2\breve{z}+1)}_{>0, \text{ since } 0<p\leq 1}e^{\breve{z}}\underbrace{-p\xi(\breve{z}+1)-x}_{>0}>0.
\end{eqnarray*}
These two cases show that $F$ is strictly positive on the subinterval for all $z\in(\tilde{z},\hat{z})$ such that $\overline{z}$ is the unique solution of \eqref{root} on the whole space $\mathbb{R}$ and $\bar z\in (-\infty,W_{-1}(\xi/p)]$, i.e. case $(iii)$ is true.

Moreover, if $-p/e <\xi \leq 0$ and $0\leq x\leq 1/e$, then $\overline{z}\in(-\infty,W_{-1}(\xi/p)]$. From \eqref{eq21} and \eqref{eq22} one has that $\lim_{z\rightarrow +\infty}F(z)=+\infty$ as well as $\lim_{z\rightarrow -\infty}F(z)=-\infty$, hence, $F(W_{-1}(\xi/p))>0$. As $W_{-1}(\xi/p)<0$ and $\ln(x)<0$, it follows $W_{-1}(\xi/p)+\ln(x)-1<W_{-1}(\xi/p)<0$ such that
                  
$$\underbrace{p(W_{-1}(\xi/p)+\ln(x))}_{<0}\underbrace{(p(W_{-1}(\xi/p)+\ln(x)-1)e^{W_{-1}(\xi/p)+\ln(x)-1}-\xi)}_{\geq0, \text{ since }W_{-1}(\xi/p)+\ln(x)-1\in (-\infty,W_{-1}(\xi/p)]}
+\!\!\!\!\!\underbrace{x(e^{W_{-1}(\xi/p)-1}-1)}_{\leq 0, \text{ as } x\geq 0,\ W_{-1}(\xi/p)-1<0}\!\!\!\!\! <0,$$
                
i.e. $F(W_{-1}(\xi/p)+\ln(x)-1)<0$, which verifies $(iv)$.

Now, if $-p/e<\xi\leq 0$ and $x>1/e$, then $\overline{z}\in [W(p/e),+\infty)$. Using again \eqref{eq21} and \eqref{eq22} reveals that $F(W(\xi/p))<0$ and since $W(\xi/p)\geq-1$, it holds that $W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+2>0$ (notice also that $x>1/e$, i.e. $\ln(x)>-1$) and
                  
$$\underbrace{p(W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+3)}_{>0}\underbrace{(p(W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+2)e^{W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+2}-\xi)}_{>0, \text{ since }W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+2\in [W(\xi/p),+\infty)}
+\underbrace{x(e^{W(\xi/p)+2}-1)}_{\geq 0}>0,$$
                
which means that $F(W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+2)>0$ and thus, case $(v)$ is true.

Finally, let $\xi>0$, then $x>1$ and $\overline{z}\in [W(\xi/p),+\infty)$. As $px\ln(x)>\xi$, it holds $W(x\ln x)=\ln x>W(\xi/p)$, which implies that $x>e^{W(\xi/p)}$. Furthermore, since $\lim_{z\rightarrow+\infty}F(z)=+\infty$ and
                  
$$F(W(\xi/p))=\underbrace{p(W(\xi/p)+1)\left(p\frac{\xi}{p}-\xi\right)}_{=0}+e^{W(\xi/p)-x}<0,$$
                
we have that $W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+1>W(\xi/p)$ and hence,
                  
$$\underbrace{p(W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+2)}_{>0}\underbrace{(p(W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+1)e^{W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+1}-\xi)}_{>0, \text{ since }W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+1\in [W(\xi/p),+\infty)}
+\underbrace{x(e^{W(\xi/p)+1}-1)}_{\geq 0, \text{ since } W\left(\xi/p\right)\geq -1}>0,$$
                
i.e. $F(W(\xi/p)+\ln(x)+1)>0$, which verifies $(vi)$.

\normalfont
The graphs in Figure \ref{fig:n10m10cvobjvalEVaR-PDPS}$(b)$ and Figure \ref{fig:n15m15cvobjvalEVaR-PDPS}$(b)$ look somehow counterintuitive, as the objective values of the portfolio optimization problems (in this case maximization problems) abruptly decrease from very large values and then slightly increase toward the (approximate) optimal values of the problems, different from the expected monotonicity (induced by proximal point methods). These graphs are obtained by solving at each iteration of the parallel splitting method for solving $(PU')$ the optimality conditions system from Theorem \ref{theoremChapter1:3.2} and then plotting the corresponding dual objective values. While the inherent computational approximations of the employed computer certainly play a role in this phenomenon, its main cause seems to be the lack of dual feasibility of the derived dual solutions in the first iterations. One can see, for instance, in Figure \ref{fig:n10m10cvoptsolEVaR-PDPS} $(b)$ and Figure \ref{fig:n15m15cvoptsolEVaR-PDPS}$(b)$ that the variable represented in pink is not feasible from the beginning. However, this flaw is corrected as the algorithm advances and the (approximate) optimal values of the problems obtained via the two competing methods are (approximatively) the same.
