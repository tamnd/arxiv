<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $C$ be a complete algebraically closed non-archimedean extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, with ring of integers $\mathcal O_C$ and residue field $k$. Let $\mathfrak X$ be a proper smooth $p$-adic formal scheme, topologically of finite presentation over $\mathcal O_C$. Write $X_\eta$ for its rigid analytic generic fiber, $X_k=\mathfrak X\otimes_{\mathcal O_C}k$ for its special fiber, and $W=W(k)$. Fix $n\geqslant0$, and write
\[
 h^{i,n-i}=\dim_C H^{n-i}(X_\eta,\Omega^i_{X_\eta/C}).
\]
Let $h_W^{i,n-i}$ denote the Hodge--Witt numbers of $X_k$. Then for every integer $r$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 \sum_{i<r}(r-i)h^{i,n-i}\ \leqslant\ \sum_{i<r}(r-i)h_W^{i,n-i}.
\end{equation}
Thus the Hodge polygon of $X_\eta$ lies on or above the Hodge--Witt polygon of $X_k$, and the two polygons have the same endpoints. The Hodge--Witt numbers may be negative.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:support}
Let $a=(a_i)$ and $b=(b_i)$ be finitely supported sequences with $a_i\geqslant0$. The polygon of $a$ lies on or above the polygon of $b$ if and only if $S_r(a)\leqslant S_r(b)$ for every $r\in\mathbb{Z}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The inequalities imply $E(a)\subseteq E(b)$ by comparing the defining halfspaces. Conversely, since $a_i\geqslant0$, the supporting line of each integral slope $r$ meets the polygon of $a$, possibly at an endpoint. Thus
\[
 \inf_{(x,y)\in E(a)}(y-rx)=-S_r(a).
\]
If $E(a)\subseteq E(b)$, this infimum is at least $-S_r(b)$, proving the converse. The entries of $b$ may have either sign.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:telescope}
For every integer $r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hinge}
 S_r(h_W^{\bullet,n-\bullet})=\sum_{\lambda\leqslant r}(r-\lambda)m_{\lambda}+T^{r-1,n-r+1}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Substituting \eqref{eq:hwdef} into $S_r(h_W^{\bullet,n-\bullet})$, the coefficient of $T^{j,n-j}$ for $j\leqslant r-3$ is
\[
 (r-j)-2(r-j-1)+(r-j-2)=0.
\]
For the remaining values of $j$, direct calculation shows that the coefficient is $1$ when $j=r-1$ and $0$ otherwise. Thus the domino terms contribute $T^{r-1,n-r+1}$.

For a single Newton slope $\lambda\in[a,a+1)$, with $a\in\mathbb Z$, the contributions to $m^{a,n-a}$ and $m^{a+1,n-a-1}$ are $a+1-\lambda$ and $\lambda-a$, respectively. If $a<r$, their weighted sum is
\[
 (r-a)(a+1-\lambda)+(r-a-1)(\lambda-a)=r-\lambda.
\]
When $a=r-1$, the second summand has weight zero. If $a\geqslant r$, neither slope number contributes. Summing with multiplicities $m_{\lambda}$ gives $\sum_{\lambda\leqslant r}(r-\lambda)m_{\lambda}$, where the term with $\lambda=r$ is zero. Combining the two contributions proves the formula.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:filtration}
For $s\geqslant2$ there is a distinguished triangle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:QsTriangle}
 \varphi_*^{s-1}\mathcal Q_{1,r}\longrightarrow\mathcal Q_{s,r}
       \longrightarrow\mathcal Q_{s-1,r}\longrightarrow[1].
\end{equation}
Consequently $\mathcal Q_{s,r}$ has a finite filtration with factors
$\varphi_*^j\mathcal Q_{1,r}$, $0\leqslant j<s$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Forgetting the last factor gives a homotopy pullback square with right-hand vertical map $\varphi_*^{s-1}i$. In a stable category the cofiber of the other vertical map $E_{s,r}\to E_{s-1,r}$ is therefore $\varphi_*^{s-1}\mathcal Q_{1,r}$. Apply the cofiber triangle for the composite $E_{s,r}\to E_{s-1,r}\to\varphi^*({\mathlarger{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})$. Induction gives the filtration. Applying derived global sections preserves these triangles.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:perfectness}
For all integers $s,r\geqslant1$, the complex $P_{s,r}$ is perfect over $A$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support}
 P_{s,r}[1/q_s]=0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We first recall that $R\Gamma(\mathfrak X,\Omega^i_{\mathfrak X/\mathcal O_C})$ is perfect over $\mathcal O_C$ and satisfies derived base change to both fibers; here $\Omega^i$ denotes continuous differential forms. Put $\mathfrak X_a=\mathfrak X\bmod p^a$. Proper perfect pushforward and derived base change give compatible perfect complexes $R\Gamma(\mathfrak X_a,\Omega^i)$ over $\mathcal O_C/p^a$ \cite[Tag~0DJT]{Stacks}. Their derived inverse limit is perfect over $\mathcal O_C$ and recovers each of them by reduction \cite[Tag~09AW]{Stacks}.

To identify this limit with formal cohomology and compare it with the generic fiber, we use the natural quasi-isomorphisms
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R\Gamma(\mathfrak X,\Omega^i_{\mathfrak X/\mathcal O_C})
   &\simeq R\varprojlim_aR\Gamma(\mathfrak X_a,\Omega^i_{\mathfrak X_a/(\mathcal O_C/p^a)}),\\
 R\Gamma(\mathfrak X,\Omega^i_{\mathfrak X/\mathcal O_C})[1/p]
   &\simeqR\Gamma(X_\eta,\Omega^i_{X_\eta/C}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
These can be computed using a finite affine formal cover with affine intersections. On each reduction, affine acyclicity gives a \v{C}ech complex whose transition maps are termwise surjective, so its derived inverse limit is the formal \v{C}ech complex. Inverting $p$ gives the corresponding affinoid \v{C}ech complex on $X_\eta$, which computes coherent cohomology by affinoid acyclicity. Derived base change to $k$ follows from that for $\mathfrak X_1$ over $\mathcal O_C/p$.

The quotient $\mathcal Q_{1,r}$ has a finite Nygaard filtration, indexed by $0\leqslant a<r$, with graded terms in \eqref{eq:filteredfrobenius}. The Hodge--Tate comparison gives each such term a further finite canonical filtration with pieces $\Omega^i_{\mathfrak X/\mathcal O_C}[-i]$, with line twists, for $0\leqslant i\leqslant a$. Their global sections are perfect over $\mathcal O_C=A/(\xi)$. The two-term resolution $[A\xrightarrow{\xi}A]$ makes them perfect over $A$. Hence $P_{1,r}$ is perfect over $A$.

By \cite[Theorem~1.8]{BMS}, the complex $R\Gamma(\mathfrak X,\varphi^*({\mathlarger{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}))$ is perfect. The quotient triangle gives the same assertion for the global Nygaard stage. Restriction by the automorphism $\varphi$ and finite homotopy pullbacks preserve perfection, so $P_{s,r}$ is perfect. By Lemma~\ref{lem:filtration}, it has a finite filtration whose $j$-th factor is supported at $\xi_j$. Inverting $q_s$ therefore annihilates every factor, proving \eqref{eq:support}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:spectral}
Let $U\to T$ be a map of finitely filtered complexes over a DVR with residue field $L$. Suppose that the cohomology of each graded piece is finite-dimensional over $L$, that $H^n(\operatorname{gr}^aU)\to H^n(\operatorname{gr}^aT)$ is injective for all $a,n$, and that the spectral sequence of $T$ degenerates at $E_1$. Then the spectral sequence of $U$ also degenerates at $E_1$, and
\[
 \operatorname{length} H^n(U)=\sum_a\dim_L H^n(\operatorname{gr}^aU).
\]
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The map of $E_1$-pages is injective, and every differential in the target vanishes. Hence every differential in the source vanishes as well. Induction gives the same assertion on all subsequent pages. The length formula follows by adding the lengths of the graded pieces of the induced finite filtration on cohomology.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:genericlength}
For $s,r\geqslant1$ and every integer $n$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:genericlength}
 \operatorname{length}_{B_j}H^n(P_{s,r}\mathbin{\otimes^{\mathbf L}}_A B_j)=S_r(h^{\bullet,n-\bullet})
 \qquad(0\leqslant j<s).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We first treat $s=1$ and $j=0$. Apply the filtered Frobenius \eqref{eq:filteredfrobenius}, truncated at $r$, to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filteredmap}
 P_{1,r}\longrightarrow R\Gamma(\mathfrak X,{\mathlarger{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\mathbin{\otimes^{\mathbf L}}_A A/(\xi^r).
\end{equation}
After base change to $B_0$, its grade $a$, for $0\leqslant a<r$, is the natural map
\[
 R\Gamma\bigl(\mathfrak X,\tau_{\leqslant a}({\mathlarger{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}/\xi)\bigr)[1/p]\{a\}
 \longrightarrow
 R\Gamma\bigl(\mathfrak X,{\mathlarger{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}/\xi\bigr)[1/p]\{a\}.
\]
After inverting $p$, the Postnikov spectral sequence of the target identifies with the Hodge--Tate spectral sequence of $X_\eta$ \cite[Theorem~9.2(i)]{BMS}, which degenerates by \cite[Theorem~1.7(ii)]{BMS}.

In Postnikov degree $i$, the map above induces the identity on graded pieces for $i\leqslant a$ and has zero source for $i>a$. It is therefore injective on the cohomology of every graded piece. Since the target spectral sequence degenerates, Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral} implies that the source spectral sequence also degenerates. Consequently, its degree-$n$ cohomology has dimension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truncdim}
 \sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant a}h^{i,n-i}.
\end{equation}
The induced map on $E_\infty$ is injective, so the map on cohomology is injective as well. Here, as throughout, truncation precedes global sections.

After base change to $B_0$, the spectral sequence of the finite $\xi$-adic filtration on the target of \eqref{eq:filteredmap} degenerates at $E_1$. To see this, put $L=R\Gamma(\mathfrak X,{\mathlarger{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\mathbin{\otimes^{\mathbf L}}_A B_0$. By \cite[Theorem~1.8]{BMS} and Frobenius pullback, $R\Gamma(\mathfrak X,{\mathlarger{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})[1/p]$ has finite free cohomology over $A[1/p]$. It therefore splits noncanonically as the direct sum of its cohomology modules in their degrees. Base change gives the same splitting for $L$, with finite free $B_0$-cohomology. The splitting induces a splitting of the filtered complex $L\mathbin{\otimes^{\mathbf L}}_{B_0}B_0/(\xi^r)$ with its finite $\xi$-adic filtration, proving the claimed degeneration. This is a statement after inverting $p$, concerning the $\xi$-adic filtration; the Postnikov filtration on the sheaf ${\mathlarger{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}/\xi$ was used separately above.

Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral} over $B_0$ now gives the exact length
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinarylength}
 \operatorname{length}_{B_0}H^n(P_{1,r}\mathbin{\otimes^{\mathbf L}}_A B_0)
 =\sum_{a=0}^{r-1}\sum_{i=0}^a h^{i,n-i}
 =S_r(h^{\bullet,n-\bullet}).
\end{equation}
For $\ell\ne j$, the element $\xi_\ell$ is a unit in $B_j$. Indeed its image under $\theta\varphi^j$ is nonzero; the cyclotomic formula gives $p$ for a positive Frobenius shift and a nonzero ratio of successive cyclotomic differences for a negative shift. Hence the finite filtration of Lemma~\ref{lem:filtration} has exactly one surviving factor after tensoring with $B_j$, namely $\varphi_*^jP_{1,r}$. Frobenius transports its length to \eqref{eq:ordinarylength}, proving \eqref{eq:genericlength}.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:determinant}
Let $P$ be a perfect $A$-complex with $P[1/q_s]=0$. Then for every $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:detbound}
 \operatorname{length}_W H^n(P\mathbin{\otimes^{\mathbf L}}_A W)
 \ \geqslant\ \sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\operatorname{length}_{B_j}H^n(P\mathbin{\otimes^{\mathbf L}}_A B_j).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The ring $A$ is local. Choose a bounded finite free representative $C^\bullet$ of $P$, and let $t$ be the rank of $d^{n-1}$ over $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$. The complex is acyclic over this field. It is also acyclic over $W[1/p]$, because $\rho(q_s)=p^s$ and the specialization to $W[1/p]$ factors through $A[1/q_s]$. Similarly it is acyclic over $\operatorname{Frac}(B_j)$. In an acyclic complex of vector spaces with fixed term dimensions, ranks of differentials are determined recursively from the first term. Thus $d^{n-1}$ has rank $t$ over all these fields.

For any DVR $R$ and bounded finite free complex $C_R^\bullet$ acyclic over $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minorlength}
 \operatorname{length}_R H^n(C_R^\bullet)=v_R\bigl(I_t(d^{n-1})\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $I_t$ is the ideal of $t$-by-$t$ minors. To prove this, put $Z^n=\ker(d^n)$. It is saturated in $C_R^n$, since $C_R^n/Z^n=\operatorname{im}(d^n)$ is torsion-free. Over a DVR it is a direct summand. The nonzero Smith factors of $d^{n-1}$ computed in $C_R^n$ are consequently the same as those computed in $Z^n$. Their valuation sum is the length of $Z^n/\operatorname{im}(d^{n-1})$. This proves \eqref{eq:minorlength}. If $t=0$, then $Z^n$ has rank zero and vanishes, so the claimed inequality is immediate.

Put $\ell_j=\operatorname{length}_{B_j}H^n(P\mathbin{\otimes^{\mathbf L}}_A B_j)$. Every $t$-minor $f\in A$ lies in $\xi_j^{\ell_j}B_j$. We next contract this divisibility to $A$. For every $a\geqslant1$,
\[
 A[1/p]/(\xi_j^a)\simeq B_j/(\xi_j^a).
\]
It follows that $f\in\xi_j^{\ell_j}A[1/p]$; when $\ell_j=0$ there is no condition to contract. Since $A/(\xi_j)\simeq\mathcal O_C$ is a domain, $\xi_j$ is prime; it does not divide $p$. Successive prime-factor cancellation yields
\[
 \xi_j^aA[1/p]\cap A=\xi_j^aA.
\]
The $\xi_j$ are pairwise nonassociated prime elements. Successive cancellation therefore shows that divisibility by every $\xi_j^{\ell_j}$ implies divisibility by their product.

Consequently every $t$-minor is divisible in $A$ by $\prod_j\xi_j^{\ell_j}$. After $\rho$, every specialized minor is divisible by $p^{\sum_j\ell_j}$. At least one such minor is nonzero, since the specialized differential still has rank $t$. Formula \eqref{eq:minorlength} over $W$ proves \eqref{eq:detbound}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:growth}
Let $M\in D_c^b(R)$ and fix an integer $r$. The complexes $Q_s^r(M)$ have finite-length $W$-cohomology in a common bounded range and are perfect over $W$. For every $n$, there is a constant $C_{M,r,n}\geqslant0$, independent of $s\geqslant1$, such that
\[
 \left|\operatorname{length}_W H^n(Q_s^r(M))
       -s\,S_r\bigl(h_W^{\bullet,n-\bullet}(M)\bigr)\right|
       \leqslant C_{M,r,n}.
\]
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth}
 \operatorname{length}_W H^n(Q_s^r(M))
 =s\,S_r\bigl(h_W^{\bullet,n-\bullet}(M)\bigr)+O(1),
 \qquad s\to\infty.
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:typeIgrowth}
Let $E\in D_c^b(R)$ be a finite-type Dieudonn\'e module concentrated in internal degree $a$ and external degree $n-a$. Then the conclusions of Proposition~\ref{prop:growth} hold for $E$. Moreover, $H^q(Q_s^rE)=0$ for $q\notin\{n-1,n\}$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Put $b=n-a$. For $a\geqslant r$, the cone vanishes. For $a<r$,
\[
 Q_s^r(E)\simeq
 \bigl[\sigma_*^sE\xrightarrow{\ \iota_{s,r}\ }E\bigr],
 \qquad \iota_{s,r}=p^{s(r-a-1)}V^s,
\]
in total degrees $n-1,n$. Its cohomology is $\ker(\iota_{s,r})$ and $\operatorname{coker}(\iota_{s,r})$ in these degrees, respectively.

Suppose first that $E$ is torsion-free. Its only possibly nonzero Hodge--Witt numbers can be interpreted, after a simple rearrangement, as
\[
 h_W^{a,b}(E)=\operatorname{length}_W(E/VE),\qquad
 h_W^{a+1,b-1}(E)=\operatorname{length}_W(E/FE);
\]
see \cite[Lemma~2(ii) and the proof of Lemma~3]{Crew}. Since $FV=p$, these lengths sum to $\operatorname{rk}_W E$. The map $\iota_{s,r}$ is injective, and additivity of colength gives
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{length}_W H^n(Q_s^rE)
 &=s\bigl((r-a-1)\operatorname{rk}_W E+\operatorname{length}_W(E/VE)\bigr)\\
 &=s\sum_{i<r}(r-i)h_W^{i,n-i}(E).
\end{align*}

For general $E$, put $e(E)=\operatorname{length}_W E_{\mathrm{tor}}$ and $\overline E=E/E_{\mathrm{tor}}$. The snake lemma, applied to the torsion sequence and $\iota_{s,r}$, bounds both the kernel on $E$ and the difference of cokernel lengths on $E$ and $\overline E$ by $e(E)$. These modules have the same Hodge--Witt numbers, since their rational slopes agree and their domino terms vanish. Thus
\begin{gather*}
 0\leqslant\operatorname{length}_W H^n(Q_s^rE)-sS_r(h_W^{\bullet,n-\bullet}(E))\leqslant e(E),\\
 \operatorname{length}_W H^{n-1}(Q_s^rE)\leqslant e(E).
\end{gather*}
All other cohomology vanishes. For $a\geqslant r$, the same bounds hold with $S_r(h_W^{\bullet,n-\bullet}(E))=0$. Perfection follows since $W$ is a DVR.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:dominogrowth}
Let $U=U_t\in D_c^b(R)$ be an elementary domino of type $t$, concentrated in external degree $n-a$ and internal degrees $a,a+1$. Then the conclusions of Proposition~\ref{prop:growth} hold for $U$. Moreover, $H^q(Q_s^rU)=0$ for $q\notin\{n-1,n,n+1\}$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The source of $U$ is $k[[V]]$, its target is killed by $V$, and $pU=0$. By \cite[I, (2.14.3)--(2.14.4)]{IR}, its total cohomology has lengths
\[
 \operatorname{length}_W H^n(\operatorname{Tot} U)=\max(t,0),\qquad
 \operatorname{length}_W H^{n+1}(\operatorname{Tot} U)=\max(-t,0),
\]
and vanishes otherwise.

For $a\geqslant r$, the cone vanishes. For $a\leqslant r-2$, the modification is a scalar twist and $\iota_{s,r}=0$, since $p$ kills the source and $V$ kills the target. Hence
\[
 Q_s^r(U)\simeq\operatorname{Tot} U\oplus\sigma_*^s\operatorname{Tot} U[1],
\]
whose cohomology is supported in $[n-1,n+1]$ and has length at most $|t|$ in each degree. At $a=r-1$, the components of $\iota_{s,r}$ are $V^s$ and the identity. This map is injective, with quotient $k[[V]]/(V^s)$ in total degree $n$, so
\[
 Q_s^r(U)\simeq\bigl(k[[V]]/(V^s)\bigr)[-n],
 \qquad \operatorname{length}_W H^n(Q_s^rU)=s.
\]
The only nonzero Hodge--Witt numbers are
\[
 h_W^{a,n-a}(U)=1,\qquad
 h_W^{a+1,n-a-1}(U)=-2,\qquad
 h_W^{a+2,n-a-2}(U)=1.
\]
Thus
\[
 S_r\bigl(h_W^{\bullet,n-\bullet}(U)\bigr)
 =\sum_{i<r}(r-i)h_W^{i,n-i}(U)
 =\begin{cases}
 1,&r=a+1,\\
 0,&r\neq a+1.
 \end{cases}
\]
It follows that \eqref{eq:growth} holds, with error and off-degree lengths bounded by $|t|$; both vanish at $r=a+1$. The resulting bounded complexes with finite-length cohomology are perfect over $W$.

Put $D_j=\widetilde H^j(M)$, $L_j=D_j[-j]$, and $A_j=\widetilde\tau_{\leqslant j}M$. Recall that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:layeridentity}
 h_W^{i,n-i}(M)=h_W^{i,n-i}(L_n)\qquad\text{for every }i.
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:heartgrowth}, $Q_s^rL_j$ has cohomology in degrees $[j-1,j+1]$, with bounded lengths outside degree $j$, and
\[
 \operatorname{length} H^j(Q_s^rL_j)
 =sS_r\bigl(h_W^{\bullet,j-\bullet}(L_j)\bigr)+O(1).
\]
Apply $Q_s^r$ to the finite diagonal Postnikov tower
\[
 A_{j-1}\longrightarrow A_j\longrightarrow L_j\longrightarrow A_{j-1}[1].
\]
Every connecting map $H^q(Q_s^rL_j)\to H^{q+1}(Q_s^rA_{j-1})$ has image of bounded length: for $q\ne j$ its source has bounded length, while for $q=j$ its target vanishes, since all earlier layers have cohomology in degrees at most $j$. Thus \eqref{eq:leslength} and finite induction give
\[
 \operatorname{length} H^n(Q_s^rM)
 =\sum_j\operatorname{length} H^n(Q_s^rL_j)+O(1)
 =sS_r\bigl(h_W^{\bullet,n-\bullet}(M)\bigr)+O(1),
\]
where the last equality uses \eqref{eq:layeridentity}. All errors are bounded independently of $s$. The same finite tower gives finite-length cohomology in a common bounded range, hence perfection over $W$.
