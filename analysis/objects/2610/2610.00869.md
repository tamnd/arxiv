<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main-4d}
Let $H=-\Delta+V$ be a Schr\"odinger operator on ${\mathbb{R}}^4$ with
$|V(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta}$ for some
$\beta>10$. If $H$ has a zero-energy resonance, then the wave
operators $W_\pm^{\mathrm{low}}$ are unbounded on $L^1({\mathbb{R}}^4)$ for every sufficiently small cutoff scale $\lambda_0>0$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:main-infty}
Let $n\ge3$ and let $|V(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta}$, where
$\beta>n+4$. Fix a sufficiently small cutoff scale $\lambda_0>0$. Suppose zero is an eigenvalue of $H=-\Delta+V$ and
no zero-energy resonance is present. Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\emph{(i)}] $W_\pm^{\mathrm{low}}$ are bounded on
$L^p({\mathbb{R}}^n)$ for $1\le p<\frac{n}{2}$ when $n\ge5$, and for
$1\le p\le2$ when $n=3, 4$; if
$\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{E}_0\neq\emptyset$
(which is possible only when $n\ge5$), then they are unbounded on
$L^p({\mathbb{R}}^n)$ for every
$\frac{n}{2}\le p\le\infty$;
\item[\emph{(ii)}] assume $\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_0$; then
$W_\pm^{\mathrm{low}}$ are bounded on
$L^p({\mathbb{R}}^n)$ for $1\le p<n$; if moreover
$\mathcal{E}_0\setminus\mathcal{E}_1\neq\emptyset$, they are unbounded
on $L^p({\mathbb{R}}^n)$ for every $n\le p\le\infty$;
\item[\emph{(iii)}] assume $\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_1$; then
$W_\pm^{\mathrm{low}}$ are bounded on
$L^p({\mathbb{R}}^n)$ for $1\le p<\infty$; if moreover
$\mathcal{E}_1\setminus\mathcal{E}_2\neq\emptyset$, they are unbounded
on $L^\infty({\mathbb{R}}^n)$;
\item[\emph{(iv)}] assume $\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_2$; then
$W_\pm^{\mathrm{low}}$ are bounded on
$L^\infty({\mathbb{R}}^n)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
For $n=3,4$, under our assumptions on $V$, the identity $\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_0$
holds automatically; see \cite{JK, J84, Yajima-1995-even}. Thus part~\textup{(ii)} gives boundedness for $1\le p<n$
in these dimensions, including the usual $L^2$ bound.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:decay-and-endpoint-novelty}
In the presence of a zero-energy eigenvalue but no zero-energy
resonance, Theorem~\ref{thm:main-infty} establishes the low-energy
classification under the decay assumption $\beta>n+4$.
This is weaker than the assumption in \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Erd-Gre-arxiv},
which, for the classical Schr\"odinger operator, requires $\beta>n+6$
in odd dimensions and $\beta>n+5$ in even dimensions.
Moreover, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first
necessary and sufficient characterization of $L^\infty$-boundedness
in the eigenvalue case for every dimension $n\ge3$:
\[
W_\pm^{\mathrm{low}}\text{ are bounded on }L^\infty(\mathbb R^n)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal E=\mathcal E_2.
\]
In particular, after the zeroth- and first-order moment conditions
are imposed, the endpoint is characterized precisely by the
vanishing of the harmonic second-order moments.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The condition at $p=\infty$ is the higher-dimensional counterpart of
the one found in dimension two in \cite{CMY-2D}, where, in the
eigenvalue case, the $L^\infty$-endpoint was shown to depend on the
degree-two harmonic moments of $V\psi$, in addition to the presence
of an s-wave resonance and the absence of p-wave resonances. Thus, in
every dimension $n\ge2$, the $L^\infty$-endpoint of the wave
operators in the eigenvalue case is governed by second-order
threshold data of $H$.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
The statements here concern the low-energy wave operators.
They extend to the full wave operators whenever the corresponding
high-energy $L^p$ bounds are available under the chosen hypotheses
on $V$; see \cite{Yajima-JMSJ-95,Yajima-1995-even,Yajima-JMSJ-2006}. Indeed, it has been shown in \cite{Yajima-JMSJ-95,Yajima-1995-even,Yajima-JMSJ-2006} that $W_\pm^{\mathrm{high}}$ is bounded on $L^p$ for all $1\le p\le \infty$ and any fixed $\lambda_0$ if
\[
|V(x)|\lesssim \langle x\rangle^{-n-2-}, \quad \mathcal{F}(\langle x\rangle^{2\sigma}V)\in L^{\frac{n-1}{n-2}} ~\text{ with $\sigma>\frac{n-2}{n-1}$}.
\]
Under these additional high-energy assumptions, Theorem~\ref{thm:main-infty}
therefore gives the same classification for the full wave operators
when zero is an eigenvalue and no zero-energy resonance is present.

\label{lem:exp-of-in-M}
Assume that $|V(x)|\lesssim \langle x\rangle^{-\beta}$ for some $\beta>10$, and let $0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}$ with $\lambda_{0}>0$ fixed and sufficiently small.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.6ex]
\item If zero is a resonance but not an eigenvalue of $H$, then
\[

\]
\item If zero is both a resonance and an eigenvalue of $H$, then
\[
M^{+}(\lambda)^{-1}=f_{1}^{+}(\lambda)S_{1}SS_{1}+\frac{S_{2}D_{2}S_{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{g_{2}^{+}(\lambda)}{\lambda^{4}}S_2D_{2}\Gamma_{3}D_{2}S_2+K+\mathcal{L}(\lambda).

\end{enumerate}

\]
and the absolute values of the kernels of $K$ and $\mathcal{L}(\lambda)$ define $L^2$-bounded operators. Moreover, $\mathcal{L}(\lambda)=\widetilde{\mathcal O}(|\log\lambda|^{-1})$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
To prove this lemma, we use the identities  
\hyperref[{eq:kernel-est-2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:kernel-est-2}}}} 
-- 
\hyperref[{eq:kernel-est-4}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:kernel-est-4}}}} 
.
The kernel of  
\hyperref[{eq:rem-part}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:rem-part}}}} 
 can be written as a linear combination of terms of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ke-of-W0}
\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\iint_{\mathbb{R}^8} e^{i\lambda\rho_{\pm}}
\mathcal{G}_{j}^{\pm}(\lambda, x, z, w, y)\, v(w)(K+\mathcal{L}(\lambda))(z, w) v(z)\, dz\, dw\,
\lambda\chi_0(\lambda)\,d\lambda
\end{equation}
for $j=0,1$, where $\rho_{\pm}=|x-z|\pm |w-y|$ and
\[
\mathcal{G}_{j}^{\pm}(\lambda,x, z, w, y)
=\frac{1}{|x-z|^{2-j/2}}\,\frac{1}{|w-y|^{3/2}}\,
\lambda^{\frac{1+j}{2}}\omega_{j}(\lambda|x-z|)\,\omega_{\pm}(\lambda|w-y|).
\]
By the estimates  
\hyperref[{eq:est-for-omega}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:est-for-omega}}}} 
, for every integer $k\geq0$,
\[
\bigl|\partial_{\lambda}^{k}\bigl(\omega_{j}(\lambda|x-z|)\,\omega_{\pm}(\lambda|w-y|)\bigr)\bigr|
\lesssim\lambda^{-k}
\]
uniformly in $x,y,z,w\in\mathbb{R}^{4}$.

Set
$
N(z,w)=\sup_{0\le k\le3}\sup_{0<\lambda<\lambda_0}\bigl|\lambda^k(\log\lambda)\,\partial_{\lambda}^k\mathcal{L}(\lambda)(z,w)\bigr|.
$
By the absolute symbol estimate for $\mathcal L$, this common
majorant defines a bounded operator on $L^2(\mathbb R^4)$.
Fix $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Including the additional factor $\lambda$
in  
\hyperref[{eq:ke-of-W0}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:ke-of-W0}}}} 
, apply Lemma~\ref{lem-oscillatory estimate}
with $b=(3+j)/2-\varepsilon$. This requires at most three derivatives
and gives
\begin{align*}
&\biggl|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\lambda\rho_{\pm}}\lambda^{\frac{3+j}{2}}\omega_{j}(\lambda|x-z|)\omega_{\pm}(\lambda|w-y|) (K+\mathcal{L}(\lambda))(z, w)\,\chi_0(\lambda)\,d\lambda\biggr|
\\
&\qquad\qquad \lesssim \bigl(|K(z, w)|+|N(z,w)|\bigr)\langle\rho_{\pm}\rangle^{-\frac{5+j}{2}+\varepsilon}.
\end{align*}

Consequently, the $L^{1}$-norm of  
\hyperref[{eq:ke-of-W0}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:ke-of-W0}}}} 
 in the variable $x$ is bounded by
\begin{align*}
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}} \int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{|v(z)v(w)|\bigl(|K(z, w)|+|N(z,w)|\bigr)}{|x-z|^{2-j/2}\,\langle\rho_{\pm}\rangle^{(5+j)/2-\varepsilon}\,|w-y|^{3/2}}\,dz\, dw\,dx
\\
&\quad \lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{|v(z)v(w)|\bigl(|K(z, w)|+|N(z,w)|\bigr)}{|w-y|^{3/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{1+j/2}}{\langle r\pm|w-y|\rangle^{(5+j)/2-\varepsilon}}\,dr\,dz\, dw
\\
&\quad \lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|v(z)v(w)|\bigl(|K(z,w)|+N(z,w)\bigr)\bigl(|w-y|^{(j-1)/2}+|w-y|^{-3/2}\bigr)\, dz\, dw
\\
&\quad\lesssim \|v\|_{L^2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\langle w\rangle^{-\beta}\bigl(|w-y|^{j-1}+|w-y|^{-3}\bigr)\,dw\right)^{1/2}\lesssim 1,
\end{align*}
uniformly in $y\in\mathbb R^4$. Here polar coordinates give the
radial integral, which is bounded by $C(1+|w-y|^{1+j/2})$.
Cauchy--Schwarz and the $L^2$ bounds for $|K|$ and $N$ then give
the last displayed expression. Its uniform bound follows from
Lemma~\ref{lemma-GV} and the decay of $V$.
The $L^{1}$-boundedness of  
\hyperref[{eq:rem-part}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:rem-part}}}} 
 now follows from Schur's test.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:unbounded-leading}
Assume that $|V(x)|\lesssim \langle x\rangle^{-\beta}$ for some $\beta>8$. Then the operator
$\mathcal{W}_{m}^{\mathrm{low}}$ defined by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-part}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\lambda R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2})v
\Bigl(f_{1}^{+}(\lambda)S_{1}SS_{1}&+\frac{S_{2}D_{2}S_{2}}{\lambda^{2}}
+\frac{g_{2}^{+}(\lambda)}{\lambda^{4}}S_2D_{2}\Gamma_{3}D_{2}S_2\Bigr)v
\bigl(R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2})-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2})\bigr)
\chi_0(\lambda)\,d\lambda
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
is unbounded on $L^1(\mathbb{R}^4)$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By the definition of $S_1$ and $S_2$, compactness of $vG_0v$,
and the Riesz--Schauder theory, the projections $S_1$ and $S_2$ are finite-rank operators.
Furthermore, $S_1-S_2$ is a rank-one projection onto the one-dimensional space spanned by
$(S_1-S_2)v$ whenever a resonance is present. Recall that
$
S=S_1-S_2+(S_1-S_2)B_1S_2+S_2B_2(S_1-S_2)+S_2B_3S_2.
$
Set $\psi_0=(S_1-S_2)v/\|(S_1-S_2)v\|_{L^2}$ and let $\{\psi_j\}_{j=1}^{m}$ be an orthonormal
basis of $S_2L^2(\mathbb{R}^4)$. In view of the above, we can represent the operator
$
f_{1}^{+}(\lambda)S_{1}SS_{1}+\frac{S_{2}D_{2}S_{2}}{\lambda^{2}}
+\frac{g_{2}^{+}(\lambda)}{\lambda^{4}}S_2D_{2}\Gamma_{3}D_{2}S_2
$
as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-for-Op}
f_{1}^{+}(\lambda)\sum_{i,j=0}^{m}\alpha_{i,j}^{(1)}\psi_i\langle\cdot,\psi_j\rangle
+\frac{1}{\lambda^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\alpha_{i,j}^{(2)}\psi_i\langle\cdot,\psi_j\rangle
+\frac{g_{2}^{+}(\lambda)}{\lambda^{4}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\alpha_{i,j}^{(3)}\psi_i\langle\cdot,\psi_j\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{0,0}^{(1)}=1$ and the parameters $\alpha_{i,j}^{(u)}$ for $i,j=0,1,\ldots,m$ and $u=1,2,3$ are fixed constants.

Note that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Four_trans_on_S}
\mathcal{F}(R_0^+(\lambda^2)g)(\xi)=\frac{\widehat{g}(\xi)}{|\xi|^2-\lambda^2-i0}, \quad 
\frac{\lambda}{\pi i}\bigl(R_0^+(\lambda^{2})-R_0^-(\lambda^{2})\bigr)h(z)
=\bigl(\widehat{h}\,d\sigma_\lambda\bigr)^{\vee}(z),
\end{equation}
where $d\sigma_\lambda$ denotes the induced measure on the sphere $\lambda\mathbb{S}^3$.
Then, applying Plancherel's theorem together with  
\hyperref[{eq:exp-for-Op}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:exp-for-Op}}}} 
, we obtain for any Schwartz
function $h$ that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-repre-of-Wm}\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{W}_{m}^{\mathrm{low}}h)(\xi)
=&\sum_{i,j=0}^{m}\alpha_{i,j}^{(1)}\frac{\widehat{v\psi_i}(\xi)}{(2\pi)^4}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^4}
\frac{\overline{\widehat{v\psi_j}(\eta)}\,\widehat{h}(\eta)\chi_0(|\eta|)}{|\xi|^2-|\eta|^2-i\varepsilon}\,
f_{1}^{+}(|\eta|)\,d\eta \\
&+\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{\widehat{v\psi_i}(\xi)}{(2\pi)^4}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^4}
\frac{\overline{\widehat{v\psi_j}(\eta)}\,\widehat{h}(\eta)\chi_0(|\eta|)}{|\xi|^2-|\eta|^2-i\varepsilon}\,
\Bigl[\frac{\alpha_{i,j}^{(2)}}{|\eta|^2}+\frac{\alpha_{i,j}^{(3)}\,g_{2}^{+}(|\eta|)}{|\eta|^4}\Bigr]
d\eta.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Since $S_2v=0$, we have $\int_{\mathbb{R}^4}(\psi_j v)(x)\,dx=0$ for all $j=1,\dots,m$.
Noting that $|v(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-4-}$ and $\psi_j\in L^2(\mathbb{R}^4)$, we obtain
$\langle x\rangle(\psi_j v)(x)\in L^1(\mathbb{R}^4)$, and hence
$\widehat{v\psi_j}(\xi)=\mathcal{O}(|\xi|)$ for $j=1,\dots,m$.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonzero-vpsi0}
\widehat{v\psi_0}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^4}v(x)\psi_0(x)\,dx
=\frac{\langle v,(S_1-S_2)v\rangle}{\|(S_1-S_2)v\|_{L^2}}
=\|(S_1-S_2)v\|_{L^2}\neq0,
\end{equation}

Suppose, for a contradiction, that $\mathcal W_m^{\mathrm{low}}$ is $L^1$ bounded. For every Schwartz function $h$, the Fourier transform of
$\mathcal W_m^{\mathrm{low}}h$ is then continuous at the origin. Now let $\{h_k\}_{k\ge1}$ be the sequence of Schwartz functions defined by
$
\widehat{h_k}(\xi)=\chi_0(2|\xi|)\bigl(1-\chi(k|\xi|)\bigr).
$
The inverse Fourier transforms of the two smooth cutoffs have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms under dilation; hence $\sup_k\|h_k\|_{L^1}\lesssim 1$. Since
$\widehat{v\psi_j}(\xi)=\mathcal{O}(|\xi|)$ for $j=1,\dots,m$, the representation
 
\hyperref[{eq:F-repre-of-Wm}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:F-repre-of-Wm}}}} 
 can be evaluated at $\xi=0$ for each fixed
$k$. Indeed, $\widehat h_k$ vanishes in a neighborhood of zero,
so the denominator is uniformly separated from zero for sufficiently
small $|\xi|$, and dominated convergence applies before taking
$k\to\infty$. We obtain
\begin{align*}
\langle\mathcal{W}_{m}^{\mathrm{low}}h_k,1\rangle
&=-\sum_{j=0}^{m}\alpha_{0,j}^{(1)}\frac{\widehat{v\psi_0}(0)}{(2\pi)^4}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^4}
\frac{\overline{\widehat{v\psi_j}(\eta)}\,\widehat{h_k}(\eta)}{|\eta|^2+i\varepsilon}\,
f_{1}^{+}(|\eta|)\chi_0(|\eta|)\,d\eta \\
&=-\frac{\widehat{v\psi_0}(0)}{(2\pi)^4}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^4}
\frac{\overline{\widehat{v\psi_0}(\eta)}\,\widehat{h_k}(\eta)}{|\eta|^2+i\varepsilon}\,
f_{1}^{+}(|\eta|)\chi_0(|\eta|)\,d\eta
+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\int_{|\eta|\le\lambda_0}\frac{|f_{1}^{+}(|\eta|)|}{|\eta|}\,d\eta\right).
\end{align*}
Note that
$
f_{1}^{+}(|\eta|)=\frac{1}{|\eta|^2}\frac{1}{a\log|\eta|+z+\mathcal{O}(|\eta|^{0+})}
$
for a constant $a \neq 0$.
In four-dimensional polar coordinates the error is bounded by a constant times $\int_0^{\lambda_0}|\log r|^{-1}dr<\infty$.
Moreover,  
\hyperref[{eq:nonzero-vpsi0}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:nonzero-vpsi0}}}} 
 implies that there exists a nonzero constant $c$ such that
\[
\frac{\widehat{v\psi_0}(0)}{(2\pi)^4}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^4}
\frac{\overline{\widehat{v\psi_0}(\eta)}\,\widehat{h_k}(\eta)}{|\eta|^2+i\varepsilon}\,
f_{1}^{+}(|\eta|)\chi_0(|\eta|)\,d\eta=c\log\log k+\mathcal{O}(1),\qquad k\gg1.
\]
It follows that
\[
\big|\langle\mathcal{W}_{m}^{\mathrm{low}}h_k,1\rangle\big|\longrightarrow\infty\qquad(k\to\infty).
\]
This contradicts the $L^1$-boundedness of $\mathcal{W}_{m}^{\mathrm{low}}$, thereby completing the proof.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:bound-of-re-part}
Suppose that $|V(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta}$ with
$\beta>n+4$. Let $\mathcal N$ and $\mathcal R(\lambda)$ be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:exp-of-M-inv}. Then
\[
\mathcal W_R=\chi_0\big(\sqrt H/2\big)\circ
\frac1{\pi i}\int_0^\infty\lambda\chi_0(\lambda)
R_0^+(\lambda^2)v[\mathcal N+\mathcal R(\lambda)]v

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
It suffices to prove that the integral operator, without the left
spectral cutoff, has an admissible kernel. Write
$r=|x-z|$ and $s=|w-y|$.
Using the explicit kernels
\[
R_0^\pm(\lambda^2)(x,y)=\frac{e^{\pm i\lambda|x-y|}}{4\pi|x-y|},
\]
the integral kernel, up to a fixed constant, is
\[
\iint \frac{v(z)v(w)}{rs}\int_0^\infty
\lambda\chi_0(\lambda)
\big(e^{i\lambda(r+s)}-e^{i\lambda(r-s)}\big)
[\mathcal N(z,w)+\mathcal R(\lambda)(z,w)]\,d\lambda\,dz\,dw.
\]
Set
\[
\mathcal A_0(z,w)=|\mathcal N(z,w)|,\qquad
\mathcal A_1(z,w)=\sup_{0\le j\le3}\sup_{0<\lambda<\lambda_0}
\lambda^{j-1}|\partial_\lambda^j\mathcal R(\lambda)(z,w)|.
\]
Both kernels give bounded operators on $L^2$. We shall repeatedly use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:repart-n3-weight}
\sup_{y\in\mathbb R^3}
\big\|v(w)(1+|w-y|^{-1})\big\|_{L^2_w}<\infty.
\end{equation}
Indeed, on $|w-y|<1$ use $V\in L^\infty$ and
$\int_{|u|<1}|u|^{-2}du<\infty$, and on its complement use
$V\in L^1$.

First consider $\mathcal R(\lambda)$. Since
$\lambda\mathcal R(\lambda)=\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda^2)$,
we may apply Lemma~\ref{lem-oscillatory estimate} with $b=3/2$;
this uses only the three available derivatives and gives
\[
\left|\int_0^\infty e^{it\lambda}\lambda\chi_0(\lambda)
\mathcal R(\lambda)(z,w)\,d\lambda\right|
\lesssim\mathcal A_1(z,w)\langle t\rangle^{-5/2}.
\]
Thus its kernel is majorized by
\[
C\iint v(z)\mathcal A_1(z,w)v(w)
\frac{\langle r+s\rangle^{-5/2}+\langle r-s\rangle^{-5/2}}{rs}
\,dz\,dw.
\]
Polar integration gives
\[
\frac1s\int_0^\infty
r\big(\langle r+s\rangle^{-5/2}+\langle r-s\rangle^{-5/2}\big)\,dr
\lesssim1+s^{-1};
\]
the same estimate holds with $r$ and $s$ interchanged. Consequently
the integral of the absolute kernel in $x$ is bounded by
\[
C\iint v(z)\mathcal A_1(z,w)v(w)(1+|w-y|^{-1})\,dz\,dw
\le C\|v\|_2\|\mathcal A_1\|_{\mathbb B(L^2)}
\|v(\cdot)(1+|\cdot-y|^{-1})\|_2\lesssim1.
\]
The integral in $y$ is bounded in the same way, placing the
factor $1+|x-z|^{-1}$ on the left of $\mathcal A_1$. Hence the
$\mathcal R$ contribution is admissible.

Next consider the constant coefficient $\mathcal N$. Put
\[
F(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{it\lambda}\lambda\chi_0(\lambda)\,d\lambda,
\qquad k(r,s)=\frac{F(r+s)-F(r-s)}{rs}.
\]
We prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:repart-n3-main-Schur}
\int_0^\infty |k(r,s)|r^2\,dr\lesssim1+s^{-1},\qquad
\int_0^\infty |k(r,s)|s^2\,ds\lesssim1+r^{-1}.
\end{equation}
On $r+s\le1$, boundedness of $F$ gives $|k(r,s)|\lesssim(rs)^{-1}$,
which immediately has the two bounds in
 
\hyperref[{eq:repart-n3-main-Schur}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:repart-n3-main-Schur}}}} 
 on this region.

On $r+s>1$, first treat the region $|r-s|\le(r+s)/2$.
Here $r\sim s$ and both are bounded below by $1/4$.
The oscillatory integral lemma with $b=1$ gives
\[
|k(r,s)|\lesssim\frac{
\langle r+s\rangle^{-2}+\langle r-s\rangle^{-2}}{rs}.
\]
Since $r/s$ and $s/r$ are bounded on this region, both radial
integrals in  
\hyperref[{eq:repart-n3-main-Schur}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:repart-n3-main-Schur}}}} 
 are uniformly bounded.
This also handles the possible vanishing of $r-s$ without dividing
by that phase.

It remains to consider $r+s>1$ and $|r-s|>(r+s)/2$.
For $t\ne0$, two integrations by parts give the exact identity
\[
F(t)=-\frac1{t^2}+H(t),\qquad
H(t)=-\frac1{t^2}\int_0^\infty e^{it\lambda}
\big(2\chi_0'(\lambda)+\lambda\chi_0''(\lambda)\big)\,d\lambda.
\]
The boundary term is $-t^{-2}$ because
$(\lambda\chi_0(\lambda))'|_{\lambda=0}=1$.
All derivatives in the remaining integral fall on $\chi_0$;
its amplitude is smooth and compactly supported away from zero.
Therefore, for every fixed $M$,
\[
|H(t)|\lesssim_M\langle t\rangle^{-M},\qquad |t|\ge1/2.
\]
The two boundary terms must be subtracted before taking absolute
values. They give
\[
\frac1{rs}\left(\frac1{(r-s)^2}-\frac1{(r+s)^2}\right)
=\frac4{(r-s)^2(r+s)^2}\lesssim(r+s)^{-4}.
\]
For the cutoff terms, since $|r-s|\sim r+s$ in this region,
\[
\frac{|H(r+s)|+|H(r-s)|}{rs}
\lesssim_M\frac{\langle r+s\rangle^{-M}}{rs}.
\]
Taking $M>2$, both contributions satisfy
 
\hyperref[{eq:repart-n3-main-Schur}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:repart-n3-main-Schur}}}} 
: the boundary contribution has
uniformly bounded radial integrals on $r+s>1$, whereas the cutoff
contribution has radial integrals bounded by $C/s$ and $C/r$,
respectively. This proves  
\hyperref[{eq:repart-n3-main-Schur}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:repart-n3-main-Schur}}}} 
.

The kernel of the $\mathcal N$ contribution is
$\iint v(z)\mathcal N(z,w)v(w)k(|x-z|,|w-y|)\,dz\,dw$,
up to a constant. Applying  
\hyperref[{eq:repart-n3-main-Schur}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:repart-n3-main-Schur}}}} 
 and
 
\hyperref[{eq:repart-n3-weight}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:repart-n3-weight}}}} 
, with $\mathcal A_0$ in place of
$\mathcal A_1$, proves that its $L^1$-norm in either variable is uniformly bounded
with respect to the other variable, just as for the remainder term.
Thus the entire integral kernel is admissible. Schur's test gives
boundedness on $L^p$ for every $1\le p\le\infty$; composing with
the admissible spectral cutoff preserves these bounds.

The $L^1$-bound follows by the same argument as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:L1-bound-of-K}, using the free-resolvent
estimates in dimension $n$. We therefore concentrate on
the $L^\infty$-bound.

Put
\[
r=|x-z|,\qquad s=|w-y|,\qquad
k_0=\left[\frac{n+3}{2}\right].
\]
Recall that the nonnegative kernels
\[
\mathcal A_0(z,w)=|\mathcal N(z,w)|,\qquad
\mathcal A_1(z,w)
=
\sup_{0\le k\le k_0}\sup_{0<\lambda<\lambda_0}
\lambda^{k-1}
\bigl|\partial_\lambda^k\mathcal R(\lambda)(z,w)\bigr|
\]
define bounded operators on $L^2$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ge4-coefficient-bound}
\iint_{\mathbb R^{2n}}
|v(z)|\mathcal A_i(z,w)|v(w)|\,dz\,dw
\le \|\mathcal A_i\|_{2\to2}\|v\|_2^2,
\qquad i=0,1.
\end{equation}

We split the left free resolvent into its large- and
small-argument parts:
\[
R_0^+(\lambda^2)(x,z)
=
R_0^+(\lambda^2)(x,z)\widetilde\chi(\lambda r)
+
R_0^+(\lambda^2)(x,z)\chi(\lambda r).
\]

\medskip
\noindent\emph{The large-argument part.}
By the free-resolvent formulas in Subsection \ref{sec:free-r}, its kernel is a linear
combination of
\[
\begin{aligned}
\iint_{\mathbb R^{2n}}
\frac{v(z)v(w)}{r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}s^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}
\int_0^\infty
e^{i\lambda(r\pm s)}
\lambda^{n-2}\omega_1(\lambda r)\omega_\pm(\lambda s)
\bigl(\mathcal N+\mathcal R(\lambda)\bigr)(z,w)
\chi_0(\lambda)\,d\lambda\,dz\,dw,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\omega_1$ includes the large-argument cutoff.
Fix $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Since
$n-2\ge\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon$, from  
\hyperref[{eq:est-for-omega}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:est-for-omega}}}} 
, the symbol estimates give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\partial_\lambda^k\left[
\lambda^{n-2}\omega_1(\lambda r)\omega_\pm(\lambda s)
\bigl(\mathcal N+\mathcal R(\lambda)\bigr)(z,w)
\right]\right|
\lesssim
\bigl(\mathcal A_0(z,w)+\mathcal A_1(z,w)\bigr)
\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon-k}
\end{aligned}
\]
for $ 0<\lambda<\lambda_0$ and $
0\le k\le k_0.$
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem-oscillatory estimate} with
$b=\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon$, we obtain the kernel majorant
\[
C\iint_{\mathbb R^{2n}}
|v(z)v(w)|
\bigl(\mathcal A_0(z,w)+\mathcal A_1(z,w)\bigr)
\left(\frac{1}{r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\langle r+s\rangle^{\frac{n+1}{2}+\varepsilon}s^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
}+\frac{1}{r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\langle r-s\rangle^{\frac{n+1}{2}+\varepsilon}s^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
}\right)\,dz\,dw.
\]
Using polar coordinates and the elementary estimate
$
\int_0^\infty
\frac{t^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}
{\langle t\pm R\rangle^{\frac{n+1}{2}+\varepsilon}}
\,dt
\lesssim 1+R^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
$
for $R>0$,
we bound the integral of the absolute kernel in $y$ by
\[
C\iint_{\mathbb R^{2n}}
|v(z)v(w)|
\bigl(\mathcal A_0(z,w)+\mathcal A_1(z,w)\bigr)
\bigl(1+|x-z|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\bigr)\,dz\,dw.
\]
This is uniformly bounded in $x$ by Cauchy--Schwarz, since
\[
\sup_x
\bigl\|v(\cdot)
\bigl(1+|x-\cdot|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\bigr)\bigr\|_2
<\infty.
\]
Indeed, $|x-z|^{-(n-1)}$ is locally integrable and
$V\in L^1\cap L^\infty$. Interchanging $r$ and $s$
gives the corresponding bound for the integral in $x$.
Thus the large-argument contribution has an admissible
kernel.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{The small-argument expansion.}
By  
\hyperref[{eq:kernel-est-1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:kernel-est-1}}}} 
,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ge4-small-expansion}
\begin{aligned}
R_0^+(\lambda^2)(x,z)\chi(\lambda r)
&=
\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{n-3}{2}\right]}
b_j\lambda^{2j}r^{2j+2-n}
+r_n(\lambda,r).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here the cutoff has been removed from the finite sum and its
contribution absorbed into $r_n$. More precisely, $r_n$ consists
of the original small-argument remainder and
$-\sum_j b_j\lambda^{2j}r^{2j+2-n}\widetilde\chi(\lambda r)$.
For the latter terms, $\lambda r\gtrsim1$ and
$n-2-2j\ge1$, so the same bound holds. Thus, for fixed
$0<\varepsilon<1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ge4-small-remainder}
\bigl|\partial_\lambda^k r_n(\lambda,r)\bigr|
\lesssim_{k,\varepsilon}
\lambda^{n-2-\varepsilon-k}r^{-\varepsilon},
\qquad 0\le k\le k_0.
\end{equation}


Before estimating these terms, we record the effect of
the left spectral cutoff. Denote the kernel of
$\chi_0(\sqrt H/2)$ by $C(x,u)$. The estimate
stated before the lemma gives
\[
|C(x,u)|\lesssim_M\langle x-u\rangle^{-M}.
\]
Consequently, for every $0<d<n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ge4-smoothing}
\sup_{x,z}\int_{\mathbb R^n}
|C(x,u)|\,|u-z|^{-d}\,du<\infty.
\end{equation}
This follows from local integrability near $u=z$ and
the uniform integrability of $C(x,\cdot)$.

We will also use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ge4-right-integrability}
\int_{\mathbb R^n}
|w-y|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
\langle w-y\rangle^{-\frac{n+1}{2}-\varepsilon}\,dy
<\infty,
\end{equation}
uniformly in $w$. In polar coordinates, the integrand
with the radial measure behaves like
$s^{\frac{n-1}{2}}$ near zero and like
$s^{-1-\varepsilon}$ at infinity.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{The finite sum paired with $\mathcal N$.}
Let $B_j$ be the operator with kernel
\[
B_j(u,z)=b_j|u-z|^{-d_j},
\qquad d_j=n-2-2j\in(0,n).
\]
Moreover, writing
\[
C_j(x,z)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}C(x,u)B_j(u,z)\,du,
\]
we obtain from  
\hyperref[{eq:ge4-smoothing}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:ge4-smoothing}}}} 
 that
\[
\sup_x\|C_j(x,\cdot)v\|_2\lesssim\|v\|_2.
\]
The finite sum now has coefficients independent of $\lambda$.
The free spectral-measure identity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ge4-polynomial-multiplier}
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{\pi i}\int_0^\infty
\lambda^{2j+1}\chi_0(\lambda)
C_jv\mathcal Nv
(R_0^+-R_0^-)(\lambda^2)\,d\lambda
=C_jv\mathcal Nv(-\Delta)^j
\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The Fourier multiplier on the right has a Schwartz
kernel. Cauchy--Schwarz and the $L^2$-boundedness of $\mathcal N$
therefore show that  
\hyperref[{eq:ge4-polynomial-multiplier}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:ge4-polynomial-multiplier}}}} 

is bounded on $L^\infty$. The left spectral cutoff is already
included in $C_j$.


\medskip
\noindent\emph{The finite sum paired with
$\mathcal R(\lambda)$.}
Using  
\hyperref[{eq:kernel-est-4}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:kernel-est-4}}}} 
, we write the kernel as a sum of
\[
\frac1{\pi i}\iint C_j(x,z)v(z)v(w)s^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
\int_0^\infty e^{\pm i\lambda s}
\lambda^{2j+\frac{n-1}{2}}\chi_0(\lambda)
\omega_\pm(\lambda s)\mathcal R(\lambda)(z,w)
\,d\lambda\,dz\,dw.
\]

The scalar
oscillatory amplitudes are
\[
\lambda^{2j+\frac{n-1}{2}}
\omega_\pm(\lambda s)
\mathcal R(\lambda)(z,w).
\]
Since
$\mathcal R(\lambda)=\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda)$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\partial_\lambda^k\left[
\lambda^{2j+\frac{n-1}{2}}
\omega_\pm(\lambda s)
\mathcal R(\lambda)(z,w)
\right]\right|
    &\lesssim
\mathcal A_1(z,w)
\lambda^{2j+\frac{n+1}{2}-k}
\\
&\lesssim
\mathcal A_1(z,w)
\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon-k},
\qquad 0\le k\le k_0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem-oscillatory estimate} therefore yields
the kernel majorant, up to a constant,
\[
\iint_{\mathbb R^{2n}}
|C_j(x,z)v(z)|
\mathcal A_1(z,w)|v(w)|
s^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
\langle s\rangle^{-\frac{n+1}{2}-\varepsilon}
\,dz\,dw.
\]
Equations  
\hyperref[{eq:ge4-right-integrability}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:ge4-right-integrability}}}} 
 and
 
\hyperref[{eq:ge4-coefficient-bound}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:ge4-coefficient-bound}}}} 
 show that the $L^1$-norm of this kernel in $y$ is uniformly
bounded in $x$. This proves the $L^\infty$ bound.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{The remainder.}
We apply the same oscillatory estimate to $r_n$.
Including the factor $\lambda$ and the spectral
difference, the scalar amplitudes associated with
$r_n(\lambda,r)$ are
\[
\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
r_n(\lambda,r)\omega_\pm(\lambda s)\mathcal N(z,w)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
r_n(\lambda,r)\omega_\pm(\lambda s)
\mathcal R(\lambda)(z,w).
\]
By  
\hyperref[{eq:ge4-small-remainder}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:ge4-small-remainder}}}} 
, their $k$-th
derivatives are bounded, respectively, by
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal A_0(z,w)r^{-\varepsilon}
\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+n-2-\varepsilon-k},
\\
&\mathcal A_1(z,w)r^{-\varepsilon}
\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+n-1-\varepsilon-k}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $n\ge4$ and $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, both satisfy
the bound
\[
C\bigl(\mathcal A_0(z,w)+\mathcal A_1(z,w)\bigr)
r^{-\varepsilon}
\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon-k}.
\]
Another application of the oscillatory estimate gives, up to a constant,
\[
\iint_{\mathbb R^{2n}}
\left(\int_{\mathbb R^n}|C(x,u)|\,|u-z|^{-\varepsilon}\,du\right)|v(z)|
\bigl(\mathcal A_0(z,w)+\mathcal A_1(z,w)\bigr)|v(w)|
s^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
\langle s\rangle^{-\frac{n+1}{2}-\varepsilon}
\,dz\,dw
\]
as a kernel majorant. The integral in parentheses is uniformly bounded in $x,z$
by  
\hyperref[{eq:ge4-smoothing}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:ge4-smoothing}}}} 
.
Integrating in $y$ and applying
 
\hyperref[{eq:ge4-right-integrability}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:ge4-right-integrability}}}} 
 and
 
\hyperref[{eq:ge4-coefficient-bound}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:ge4-coefficient-bound}}}} 
 proves the
$L^\infty$-bound for the remainder.

All applications of the oscillatory lemma use
$b=\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon$, for which
$[b]+2=k_0$. Thus the available spectral derivatives
suffice. We have proved the $L^1$ and $L^\infty$ bounds
for $\mathcal W_R$, and interpolation completes the
proof for every $1\le p\le\infty$.

The estimates for each eigenfunction contribution in
\cite[Sections 2--3]{Goldberg-Green-2016},
\cite[Proposition 2.1]{Goldberg-Green-2017}, and
\cite[Lemma 4.3]{Yajima-2016-3d} give the $L^p$-boundedness of
$\mathcal W_e$ for $1\le p\le2$ in dimensions $n\ge5$, $n=4$,
and $n=3$, respectively. These are estimates for the low-energy
singular integrals and apply under the stated decay assumption.
In dimensions three and four, the required zeroth-moment
cancellation holds automatically because $\mathcal E=\mathcal E_0$.

We prove the remaining assertions in four steps. First, we isolate
a finite-rank term. We then show that its remainder is bounded
for $2\le p\le\infty$, compute the asymptotics of the finite-rank
input profiles, and determine the exact ranges of $p$.
Throughout the proof, the operator identities are initially
understood on test functions, or through the indicated
eigenfunction pairings.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Step 1: isolating the finite-rank term.}
Choose an orthonormal basis $\{\psi_j\}_{j=1}^k$ of
$\mathcal E=\mathcal P_eL^2(\mathbb R^n)$.
The zero-energy equation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eigen-rep-hd}
\begin{gathered}
\psi_j=-(-\Delta)^{-1}(V\psi_j),\\
(-\Delta)^{-1}V\mathcal P_e=-\mathcal P_e,
\qquad \mathcal P_eV(-\Delta)^{-1}=-\mathcal P_e.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Apply the free resolvent identity
\[
R_0^+(\lambda^2)=(-\Delta)^{-1}
+\lambda^2(-\Delta)^{-1}R_0^+(\lambda^2)
\]
to  
\hyperref[{eq:def-Wesing-hd}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:def-Wesing-hd}}}} 
. This gives
$\mathcal W_e=\mathcal N_{4,m}+\mathcal N_{4,r}$, where
\begin{align*}
\mathcal N_{4,m}
&=\frac1{\pi i}\int_0^\infty\lambda^{-1}\chi_0(\lambda)
(-\Delta)^{-1}V\mathcal P_eV
(R_0^+-R_0^-)(\lambda^2)\,d\lambda,\\
\mathcal N_{4,r}
&=\frac1{\pi i}\int_0^\infty\lambda\chi_0(\lambda)
(-\Delta)^{-1}R_0^+(\lambda^2)V\mathcal P_eV
(R_0^+-R_0^-)(\lambda^2)\,d\lambda.
\end{align*}
The free spectral-measure identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:free-measure}
\frac{\lambda}{\pi i}(R_0^+-R_0^-)(\lambda^2)\varphi
=\bigl(\widehat\varphi\,d\sigma_\lambda\bigr)^\vee,
\end{equation}
where $d\sigma_\lambda$ is surface measure on $\{|\xi|=\lambda\}$.
Consequently,
\[
\frac1{\pi i}\int_0^\infty\lambda^{-1}\chi_0(\lambda)
(R_0^+-R_0^-)(\lambda^2)\,d\lambda
=(-\Delta)^{-1}\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta}).
\]
Using this identity under the pairing with $V\psi_j$, together
with  
\hyperref[{eq:eigen-rep-hd}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:eigen-rep-hd}}}} 
, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\mathcal N_{4,m}f
=-\sum_{j=1}^k\psi_j
\bigl\langle(-\Delta)^{-1}\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta})f,V\psi_j\bigr\rangle
 =\sum_{j=1}^k\psi_j
\bigl\langle f,\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta})\psi_j\bigr\rangle.
\end{align*}
These pairings are justified by the decay of $V\psi_j$ and
$(-\Delta)^{-1}V\psi_j=-\psi_j\in L^2$.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N4m-identity-hd}
\mathcal N_{4,m}=\mathcal P_e\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta}).
\end{equation}

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Step 2: boundedness of the remainder for
$2\le p\le\infty$.}
Split the factor $(-\Delta)^{-1}$ in $\mathcal N_{4,r}$ as
\[
(-\Delta)^{-1}
=\frac{\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta})}{-\Delta}
+\frac{\widetilde\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta})}{-\Delta},
\]
and denote the resulting terms by $\mathcal N_{4,r,0}$ and
$\mathcal N_{4,r,1}$, respectively.

For $\mathcal N_{4,r,1}$, the symbol
$\widetilde\chi_0(|\xi|)|\xi|^{-2}$ has an integrable inverse
Fourier transform: its kernel is $\mathcal{O}(|x|^{2-n})$ near zero and
decays to arbitrary order at infinity.
The stationary integral to its right is also bounded on every
$L^p$. Indeed, boundedness of the eigenfunctions gives
\[
V\mathcal P_eV=\sum_{j=1}^k(V\psi_j)\langle\cdot,V\psi_j\rangle,
\qquad |V\mathcal P_eV(z,w)|\lesssim|V(z)||V(w)|.
\]
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:bound-of-re-part} therefore applies
without its left spectral cutoff. Every local singularity
$|x-z|^{-d}$, $0<d<n$, is now integrated directly against $|V(z)|$,
using
\[
\sup_x\int_{\mathbb R^n}|V(z)|\,|x-z|^{-d}\,dz<\infty.
\]
The terms independent of $\lambda$ are treated by the same spectral-multiplier
identity, and the remaining terms by the same oscillatory
estimates. This proves the required bound for $\mathcal N_{4,r,1}$.

For $\mathcal N_{4,r,0}$, we identify its adjoint.
By  
\hyperref[{eq:free-measure}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:free-measure}}}} 
 and the coarea formula,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Foure express-hd}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(\mathcal W_ef)(\xi)
={}&\sum_{j=1}^k\frac{\widehat{V\psi_j}(\xi)}{(2\pi)^n}
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\int_{\mathbb R^n}
\frac{\overline{\widehat{V\psi_j}(\eta)}\,
\chi_0(|\eta|)\widehat f(\eta)}
{|\eta|^2(|\xi|^2-|\eta|^2-i\varepsilon)}\,d\eta.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
On the other hand,
\[
\mathcal N_{4,r,0}^*
=\frac1{\pi i}\int_0^\infty\lambda\chi_0(\lambda)
(R_0^+-R_0^-)(\lambda^2)V\mathcal P_eV
R_0^-(\lambda^2)\frac{\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta})}{-\Delta}\,d\lambda.
\]
Applying  
\hyperref[{eq:free-measure}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:free-measure}}}} 
 to the outer spectral difference
sets $\lambda=|\xi|$ and yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-form-hd}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(\mathcal N_{4,r,0}^*f)(\xi)
={}&\sum_{j=1}^k
\frac{\chi_0(|\xi|)\widehat{V\psi_j}(\xi)}{(2\pi)^n}
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\int_{\mathbb R^n}
\frac{\overline{\widehat{V\psi_j}(\eta)}\,
\chi_0(|\eta|)\widehat f(\eta)}
{|\eta|^2(|\eta|^2-|\xi|^2+i\varepsilon)}\,d\eta.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Comparing the denominators in these two formulas gives
\[
\mathcal N_{4,r,0}^*
=-\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta})\mathcal W_e.
\]
The right-hand side is bounded on $L^p$ for $1\le p\le2$ by
the estimates recalled at the start. Duality therefore gives
the boundedness of $\mathcal N_{4,r,0}$, and hence
$\mathcal N_{4,r}$, for $2\le p\le\infty$.
At $p=\infty$, the extension is the adjoint of the corresponding
$L^1$ operator and agrees with the stationary expression on test
functions.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Step 3: moment asymptotics of the input profiles.}
We compare the large-$x$ expansions of $\psi_j$ and
$\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta})\psi_j$. Set
$b_0=((n-2)|\mathbb S^{n-1}|)^{-1}$.
By  
\hyperref[{eq:eigen-rep-hd}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:eigen-rep-hd}}}} 
,
\[
\psi_j(x)=-b_0\int_{\mathbb R^n}|x-y|^{2-n}(V\psi_j)(y)\,dy.
\]
The usual Newton-kernel estimate gives
$|\psi_j(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{2-n}$.
In particular, $\langle\cdot\rangle^{n+2}V\psi_j\in L^1$
under $\beta>n+4$. We use the moments
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments-hd}
\begin{aligned}
&m_0^{(j)}=\int_{\mathbb R^n}V\psi_j\,dy,
\qquad d_i^{(j)}=\int_{\mathbb R^n}y_iV\psi_j\,dy,\\
&m_{i,l}^{(j)}=\int_{\mathbb R^n}y_iy_lV\psi_j\,dy,
\qquad m_{i,l,m}^{(j)}=\int_{\mathbb R^n}y_iy_ly_mV\psi_j\,dy,\\
&\bar m^{(j)}=\sum_i m_{i,i}^{(j)},
\qquad \mu_{i,l}^{(j)}=m_{i,l}^{(j)}-\frac{\delta_{il}}n\bar m^{(j)}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus $m_1^{(j)}=(d_1^{(j)},\ldots,d_n^{(j)})$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-char-hd}
\begin{aligned}
\psi_j\in\mathcal E_0
&\iff m_0^{(j)}=0,\\
\psi_j\in\mathcal E_1
&\iff m_0^{(j)}=0\ \text{and}\ m_1^{(j)}=0,\\
\psi_j\in\mathcal E_2
&\iff \psi_j\in\mathcal E_1\ \text{and}\
\mu_{i,l}^{(j)}=0\ \text{for all }i,l.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The last equivalence follows because the trace-free quadratic
polynomials $y_iy_l-|y|^2\delta_{il}/n$ span $\mathcal A_2$.

For $|y|\le|x|/2$, expand the Newton kernel to second order:
\[
\begin{aligned}
|x-y|^{2-n}
={}&|x|^{2-n}+(n-2)\frac{x\cdot y}{|x|^n}\\
&+\frac{n(n-2)}2\sum_{i,l}
\frac{x_ix_l}{|x|^{n+2}}
\left(y_iy_l-\frac{|y|^2}n\delta_{il}\right)
+\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-n-1}|y|^3).
\end{aligned}
\]
The integral over $|y|>|x|/2$, as well as the tails of the
Taylor terms, is $\mathcal{O}(|x|^{2-\beta})$ by boundedness of $\psi_j$
and the decay of $V$. Since $\beta>n+4$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Psij-asymp-hd}
\begin{aligned}
\psi_j(x)=-b_0\bigg[m_0^{(j)}|x|^{2-n}
+(n-2)\frac{x\cdot m_1^{(j)}}{|x|^n}+\frac{n(n-2)}2\sum_{i,l}\mu_{i,l}^{(j)}
\frac{x_ix_l}{|x|^{n+2}}\bigg]
+\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-n-1}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $n=3,4$, the first term is incompatible with $\psi_j\in L^2$
unless $m_0^{(j)}=0$. This recovers $\mathcal E=\mathcal E_0$.

To identify the effect of the spectral cutoff, use
$\widehat\psi_j(\eta)=-|\eta|^{-2}\widehat{V\psi_j}(\eta)$ and
expand
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VPsi-taylor-hd}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{V\psi_j}(\eta)
=m_0^{(j)}-i\eta\cdot m_1^{(j)}
-\frac{\bar m^{(j)}}{2n}|\eta|^2
-\frac12\sum_{i,l}\mu_{i,l}^{(j)}\eta_i\eta_l+\frac i6\sum_{i,l,m}m_{i,l,m}^{(j)}\eta_i\eta_l\eta_m
+E_j(\eta).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Taylor's theorem gives
$|\partial_\eta^\alpha E_j(\eta)|\lesssim|\eta|^{4-|\alpha|}$
for $|\alpha|\le4$, while its derivatives of orders
$4<|\alpha|\le n+1$ are bounded near zero.

The constant and linear terms reproduce the first two terms of
 
\hyperref[{eq:Psij-asymp-hd}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:Psij-asymp-hd}}}} 
. For the quadratic term, use the
distributional identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Riesz-identity-hd}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F^{-1}(\eta_i\eta_l|\eta|^{-2})
&=-\partial_i\partial_l(b_0|x|^{2-n})\\
&=-b_0(n-2)\,\mathrm{p.v.}\,
\frac{nx_ix_l-\delta_{il}|x|^2}{|x|^{n+2}}
+\frac{\delta_{il}}n\delta_0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The delta coefficient follows by taking the trace and using
$-\Delta(b_0|x|^{2-n})=\delta_0$.
Contraction with the trace-free matrix $(\mu_{i,l}^{(j)})$
eliminates both terms containing $\delta_{il}$ and leaves the
harmonic quadratic term in  
\hyperref[{eq:Psij-asymp-hd}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:Psij-asymp-hd}}}} 
.
In contrast, the trace term in  
\hyperref[{eq:VPsi-taylor-hd}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:VPsi-taylor-hd}}}} 
 cancels
the denominator $|\eta|^2$ and contributes the Schwartz function
$\frac{\bar m^{(j)}}{2n}\mathcal F^{-1}(\chi_0(|\cdot|))$.

For these homogeneous symbols, insertion of $\chi_0$ changes
their kernels by a rapidly decaying term away from the origin.
The cutoff cubic terms are $\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-n-1})$ at infinity.
Finally, the compactly supported symbol
$E_j(\eta)\chi_0(|\eta|)|\eta|^{-2}$ has derivatives bounded by
$C|\eta|^{2-|\alpha|}$ for $|\alpha|\le n+1$.
Its derivatives of order $n+1$ are integrable, so integration by
parts gives the same $\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-n-1})$ bound for its inverse
Fourier transform. We conclude that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chiPsij-asymp-hd}
\begin{aligned}
\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta})\psi_j(x)
={}&-b_0m_0^{(j)}|x|^{2-n}
-b_0(n-2)\frac{x\cdot m_1^{(j)}}{|x|^n}\\
&-\frac{b_0n(n-2)}2\sum_{i,l}\mu_{i,l}^{(j)}
\frac{x_ix_l}{|x|^{n+2}}\\
&+\frac{\bar m^{(j)}}{2n}\mathcal F^{-1}(\chi_0(|\cdot|))(x)
+\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-n-1}),\qquad |x|\to\infty.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus the first three possible nonzero homogeneous terms have
orders $|x|^{2-n}$, $|x|^{1-n}$, and $|x|^{-n}$, respectively.
When $\psi_j\in\mathcal E_2$, all three vanish and the input
profile belongs to $L^1$.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Step 4: the $L^p$ classification.}
For $2\le p\le\infty$, every output profile $\psi_j$ belongs
to $L^p$, because $\psi_j\in L^2\cap L^\infty$.
Therefore  
\hyperref[{eq:N4m-identity-hd}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:N4m-identity-hd}}}} 
 implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:we-finite-rank-criterion}
\mathcal N_{4,m}:L^p\longrightarrow L^p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta})\psi_j\in L^{p'}
\ \text{for every }j.
\end{equation}
Sufficiency follows from H\"older's inequality.
For necessity, choose $\phi_l\in C_c^\infty$ such that
$\langle\psi_j,\phi_k\rangle=\delta_{jk}$, which is possible
by linear independence. Pairing $\mathcal N_{4,m}f$ with $\phi_k$
isolates the functional
$\langle f,\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta})\psi_k\rangle$.
Its boundedness on compactly supported test functions gives
the required $L^{p'}$ membership, including $L^1$ when $p=\infty$.
By Step~2, the same criterion holds for $\mathcal W_e$.

For use in the necessity statements, a nonzero homogeneous term
$|x|^{-\gamma}A(x/|x|)$, with continuous nonzero $A$, cannot
belong to $L^q$ when $\gamma q\le n$.
Indeed, $|A|$ is bounded below on an open subset of the sphere,
and the radial integral there diverges. The faster-decaying errors
in  
\hyperref[{eq:chiPsij-asymp-hd}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:chiPsij-asymp-hd}}}} 
 do not change this conclusion.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item In general, the input profiles are
$\mathcal{O}(\langle x\rangle^{2-n})$. For $n\ge5$, they belong to
$L^{p'}$ when $p<n/2$, giving the bounds for $2\le p<n/2$.
Together with the initial $1\le p\le2$ estimates, this proves
the asserted boundedness range. If
$\mathcal E\setminus\mathcal E_0\ne\varnothing$, then some
$m_0^{(j)}\ne0$, so that input profile fails to lie in $L^{p'}$
for $p'\le n/(n-2)$, equivalently $p\ge n/2$.
The criterion  
\hyperref[{eq:we-finite-rank-criterion}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:we-finite-rank-criterion}}}} 
 proves precisely
the claimed unboundedness range.

\item If $\mathcal E=\mathcal E_0$, the input profiles are
$\mathcal{O}(\langle x\rangle^{1-n})$ and belong to $L^{p'}$ for
$p'>n/(n-1)$, equivalently $p<n$.
This gives boundedness for $2\le p<n$ and, with the initial
estimates, for $1\le p<n$.
If $\mathcal E_0\setminus\mathcal E_1\ne\varnothing$, some
$m_1^{(j)}\ne0$. The corresponding nonzero dipole term excludes
$L^{p'}$ membership for $p'\le n/(n-1)$, and hence excludes
$L^p$-boundedness for every $p\ge n$.

\item If $\mathcal E=\mathcal E_1$, the input profiles are
$\mathcal{O}(\langle x\rangle^{-n})$, so they belong to every $L^{p'}$
with $p'>1$. This proves boundedness for $2\le p<\infty$;
the initial estimates include the remaining range.
If $\mathcal E_1\setminus\mathcal E_2\ne\varnothing$, some

\item If $\mathcal E=\mathcal E_2$, every input profile belongs
to $L^1$ by Step~3. In particular,
\[

\le\sum_{j=1}^k\|\psi_j\|_\infty
\|\chi_0(\sqrt{-\Delta})\psi_j\|_1\,\|f\|_\infty.
\]
Together with Step~2, this proves the $L^\infty$-bound.
\end{enumerate}
This completes the proof.

\begin{pmatrix}Qf\\\lambda^{-1}S_1f\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\sigma_\lambda^*(f_0,f_1)=f_0+\lambda^{-1}f_1.
\]
Here the adjoint is taken with respect to the usual direct-sum
inner product. The map $\sigma_\lambda$ is invertible. It becomes unitary when
the target is equipped with the weighted norm
$\|(f_0,f_1)\|_\lambda^2=\|f_0\|_2^2+\lambda^2\|f_1\|_2^2$.
All matrix adjoints below use the unweighted inner product.

By  
\hyperref[{eq:exp of M}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:exp of M}}}} 
 and  
\hyperref[{eq:lemma-inv-cancellation}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:lemma-inv-cancellation}}}} 
,
left and right multiplication give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma-inv-blocks}
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_\lambda M^+(\lambda)\sigma_\lambda^*
&=\begin{pmatrix}
QM^+Q&\lambda^{-1}QM^+S_1\\
\lambda^{-1}S_1M^+Q&\lambda^{-2}S_1M^+S_1
\end{pmatrix}\\[1mm]
&=\begin{pmatrix}QTQ&0\\0&S_1vG_1vS_1\end{pmatrix}
+E(\lambda),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, suppressing the argument $\lambda$ in the entries,
\[
E(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}
E_{00}&\lambda QvG_1vS_1+E_{01}\\
\lambda S_1vG_1vQ+E_{10}&E_{11}
\end{pmatrix}
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma-inv-block-orders}
\begin{aligned}
E_{00}(\lambda),\ E_{11}(\lambda)
&=\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda),\\
E_{01}(\lambda),\ E_{10}(\lambda)
&=\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda^2).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the rank-one terms in dimensions three and four vanish
whenever either outside projection is $S_1$.
The unscaled mixed blocks are consequently
$\lambda^2QvG_1vS_1$ and $\lambda^2S_1vG_1vQ$, modulo
$\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda^3)$, and the lower diagonal block is
$\lambda^2S_1vG_1vS_1+\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda^3)$.
For the upper diagonal block, use
$\lambda^2\log\lambda=\mathcal O_{[(n+3)/2]}(\lambda)$.
Thus $E(\lambda)=\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda)$, with the
sharper mixed-entry remainders in  
\hyperref[{eq:lemma-inv-block-orders}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:lemma-inv-block-orders}}}} 
.

The constant matrix in  
\hyperref[{eq:lemma-inv-blocks}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:lemma-inv-blocks}}}} 
 is invertible.
Write
\[
\mathcal D=\begin{pmatrix}D_0&0\\0&D_2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathcal B(\lambda)
=\bigl(\sigma_\lambda M^+(\lambda)\sigma_\lambda^*\bigr)^{-1}.
\]
For sufficiently small $\lambda_0>0$ and $0<\lambda<\lambda_0$,
the Neumann expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma-inv-normalized-inverse}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B(\lambda)
&=(I+\mathcal D E(\lambda))^{-1}\mathcal D\\
&=\mathcal D-\mathcal D E(\lambda)\mathcal D
+\mathcal D E(\lambda)\mathcal D E(\lambda)\mathcal B(\lambda),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal B(\lambda)=\widetilde{\mathcal O}(1)$.
To verify the absolute-kernel estimates, choose nonnegative
bounded kernel majorants for $\mathcal D$ and for
$\lambda^{k-1}\partial_\lambda^kE$, $0\le k\le[(n+3)/2]$.
The $\ell$-th Neumann term is then dominated by the corresponding
product of majorants times $\lambda^\ell$.
After $k$ derivatives and multiplication by $\lambda^k$, at most
a polynomial factor in $\ell$ is introduced. Taking $\lambda_0$
small makes these majorant series converge in operator norm.
Their sums supply the required common nonnegative kernels.
In particular, the last term in
 
\hyperref[{eq:lemma-inv-normalized-inverse}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq:lemma-inv-normalized-inverse}}}} 
 is
$\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda^2)$ in every entry.

Keeping the first-order mixed terms explicit, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma-inv-matrix-expansion}
\mathcal B(\lambda)=
\begin{pmatrix}
D_0+\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda)
&-\lambda D_0vG_1vD_2+\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda^2)\\[1mm]
-\lambda D_2vG_1vD_0+\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda^2)
&D_2+S_1G(\lambda)S_1
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $G(\lambda)=\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda)$ is defined
as the exact lower diagonal entry of $\mathcal B(\lambda)$ minus
$D_2$, extended by zero on $QL^2$.
Thus all higher-order terms in this entry are retained in $G$.
The other three remainders retain their respective outside projections.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Restoring the scaling.}
Since $\sigma_\lambda$ is invertible, the exact identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma-inv-scale-back}
\begin{aligned}
(M^+(\lambda))^{-1}
=\sigma_\lambda^*\mathcal B(\lambda)\sigma_\lambda
=\begin{pmatrix}Q&\lambda^{-1}S_1\end{pmatrix}
\mathcal B(\lambda)
\begin{pmatrix}Q\\\lambda^{-1}S_1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The upper diagonal entry contributes
$D_0+\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda)$.
Each mixed entry is multiplied by $\lambda^{-1}$, giving
\[
-D_0vG_1vD_2+\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda),
\qquad
-D_2vG_1vD_0+\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda),
\]
respectively. The lower diagonal entry is multiplied by
$\lambda^{-2}$. Consequently,
\[
(M^+(\lambda))^{-1}
=\frac{S_1(D_2+G(\lambda))S_1}{\lambda^2}
+\mathcal N+\mathcal R(\lambda),
\]
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma-inv-N}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal N=D_0-D_0vG_1vD_2-D_2vG_1vD_0,\quad
\mathcal R(\lambda)=\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The operator $\mathcal N$ is absolutely bounded by the estimates
above. Moreover, $S_1\mathcal R(\lambda)S_1=0$, since the entire
lower diagonal correction has been incorporated into $G$.
The $\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda^2)$ bounds on the mixed
remainders are precisely what makes their restored contributions
$\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\lambda)$. This proves the lemma.
