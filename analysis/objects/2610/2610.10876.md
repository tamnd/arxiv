<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\@gobble{rem:planar}
When $\max_s\theta_s\ll1$, we have $\varphi\simeq\Delta/R$, and \eqref{eq:conformal_satellite_x}-\eqref{eq:conformal_satellite_z} reduce to
 
\begin{align}
\mathbf{r}_s\simeq[i_s\Delta,j_s\Delta,0]^\mathsf{T}
\end{align}
 
recovering the regular planar grid commonly adopted in the literature \cite[Eqs.~(4)-(6)]{Bacci_2023}. The corresponding planar satellites are shown as dashed light-gray boxes in Fig.~\ref{fig:conformal}.

As an example, Fig.~\ref{fig:model_conf_vs_planar} compares the planar and conformal configurations for a \ac{LEO} formation with $h_\mathsf{sat}=550\,\mathrm{km}$ and $S=49$. For $\Delta=R/1000=6.92\,\mathrm{km}$, their maximum separation is only $62.3\,\mathrm{m}$, whereas for the illustrative large spacing $\Delta=R/10=692.1\,\mathrm{km}$, it reaches $620.1\,\mathrm{km}$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\@gobble{rem:flat_user}
When $\omega_{\rm max}\ll1$, the spherical cap is locally approximated by the plane tangent to the Earth at the nadir point, located at $z=h_\mathsf{sat}$. The coverage region becomes a disk of radius
 
\begin{align}
\@gobble{eq:flat_radius}
r_\mathsf{flat}=h_\mathsf{sat}\tan\theta_{\rm max}
\end{align}
 
and an area-uniform \ac{UT} position is generated as
 
\begin{align}
\@gobble{eq:flat_user}
\mathbf{m}_k
&=\left[\rho_k\cos(2\pi V_k),
\rho_k\sin(2\pi V_k),
h_\mathsf{sat}\right]^\mathsf{T}
\end{align}
with $\rho_k =r_\mathsf{flat}\sqrt{U_k}$.
The minimum-distance constraint is then evaluated using
$\|\mathbf{m}_k-\mathbf{m}_{k'}\|_2$. In this limit,
$r_\mathsf{cap}\simeq r_\mathsf{flat}$.

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage_radius}, the two models agree at large $\varepsilon_{\rm min}$. For the \ac{LEO} scenario with $\varepsilon_{\rm min}=40^\circ$, however, $r_\mathsf{cap}$ exceeds $r_\mathsf{flat}$ by $26\,\mathrm{km}$ (about $5\%$). The flat model therefore reduces the coverage of cell-edge \acp{UT} and artificially increases their spatial density, leading to the \ac{KPI} bias discussed in Sect.~\ref{results:geometry}.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The square reference grid provides a convenient baseline for more general formation geometries. For a nonsquare or irregular arrangement, the Fraunhofer distance can be estimated using an enclosing square aperture. Within the same transverse-aperture criterion, this gives a conservative estimate and avoids deriving a separate expression for every configuration. Configuration-specific expressions can nevertheless provide tighter estimates by accounting for the actual occupied positions, as in the case of the cross-shaped configuration considered in \cite{RdG_2025}.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
In distributed satellite systems, the low \acl{SNR} of individual radiating elements can make direct estimation of all channel coefficients impractical. Precoding therefore often relies on channel reconstruction from user-location and satellite-state information, using an assumed propagation model. The distinction between the reconstructed channel $\widetilde{\mathbf{h}}_k$ and the propagation channel $\mathbf{h}_k$ thus reflects a practical design issue. If reconstruction uses a global \ac{FF} approximation while users lie in the formation's radiative \ac{NF}, a modeling mismatch remains even with perfect geometric information. Our comparison isolates the performance penalty caused by this operationally relevant mismatch from that due to parameter-estimation errors.
