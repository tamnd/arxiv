Let $(R,\theta)$ satisfy the preceding assumptions.
Every automorphism of $E$, and every automorphism of $G$, has a
unique expression
\[
 \varphi=i_g\circ f_*,
 \qquad
 g\in G,\quad f\in\operatorname{Aut}(R,\theta).
\]
Every automorphism of $G_{\mathrm{sc}}=E_{\mathrm{sc}}$ is a
composition of a strictly inner automorphism, a diagonal
automorphism, and a ring automorphism induced by an element of
$\operatorname{Aut}(R,\theta)$.
Conversely, each such composition is an automorphism of the
corresponding group.

We separate normalization of a finite family of constant elements
from recovery of the arbitrary root parameters.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{1. Normalize the standard frame.}
For $\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}E$, consider
\[
 \mathcal F(R)=(x_\lambda(1))_{\lambda\in\Phi_\rho},
\]
called the standard frame.
Normal structure allows reduction modulo $J$, and the field
automorphism theorem describes the residual action.
We lift and remove only its conjugating factor: the residual
coefficient automorphism already fixes the frame.
The resulting automorphism $\varphi_1$ therefore has standard
reductions on $\mathcal F(R)$.

On the $78$-dimensional Chevalley module $L_R$, one averaging over
a finite $2$-subgroup fixes the toral involutions and a chosen family
of Weyl elements simultaneously.
We then normalize the remaining Weyl elements and one complete
constant short-root matrix.
Weyl conjugation gives the other constant short-root elements, and
short--short commutators determine the constant long-root elements
from their squares.
The accumulated change of basis
$C_0\in\operatorname{GL}_{78}(R)$ is compatible with the twisting

\noindent\emph{2. Replace the linear normalization by a group element.}
At this point, $C_0$ is not known to belong to $G$.

tensors, and the Jacobi identity reduces the transported bracket
to two scalar coefficients.

without changing the normalized frame.
A local bracket-stabilizer result places the corrected matrix in
the adjoint group.
The triviality of the frame centralizer in the adjoint group over

\noindent\emph{3. Recover the coefficients.}
The normalized map is now an automorphism of $E$ fixing the
standard frame. Its values preserve the Chevalley bracket.
Together with this bracket preservation, finite commutation
conditions recognize long-root elements and short-root elements
with skew parameters.
The $B_2$ relations and the fixed--skew decomposition of $R$ give
one additive coefficient map commuting with $\theta$.
A short $A_2$ relation proves multiplicativity; applying the same
argument to the inverse proves bijectivity.
Characteristicity and the trivial centralizer of $E$ give the full
adjoint result, while the central isogeny and perfectness give the
simply connected classification.

The remainder of Section~1 fixes the matrix conventions.
Sections~2--4 perform the linear normalization, and Section~5
corrects and descends the change of basis.
Section~6 recovers the coefficients and completes the classification.
It also gives coefficient recovery for frame-fixing homomorphisms
over arbitrary rings with involution, and relative normalization
for homomorphisms with a local target and prescribed standard
residual action.

it preserves the fundamental chamber, whose Weyl-group stabilizer
is trivial. Passing to a residue field therefore also excludes

Neither \'etaleness of $S_\chi/R$ nor invertibility of $3$ is needed.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{3. Eliminate the central correction.}
The automorphism
$\varphi^{\mathrm{st}}=i_x\circ d_\chi\circ\rho_*$
induces the same automorphism of $E$ as $\varphi$.
Consequently,
\[
 (\varphi^{\mathrm{st}})^{-1}\varphi(y)=yz(y),
 \qquad z(y)\in Z(\widetilde E),
\]
where centrality makes $z:\widetilde E\to Z(\widetilde E)$ a
group homomorphism.

The group $\widetilde E$ is perfect: long and short $A_2$
subsystems express root elements of the corresponding length as
commutators with parameters $\pm t,1$ and constants $\pm1$.
Here $t\in R_0$ for long roots and $t\in R$ for short roots.
Weyl conjugation reaches every root, so every elementary generator
is a commutator.
Thus $z=1$ and $\varphi=\varphi^{\mathrm{st}}$.
Strictly inner and ring automorphisms, together with the construction
of $d_\chi$ above, also prove the converse.

 q_*\circ\Psi=i_h\circ(\nu_0)_*,
 \qquad h\in G(\overline T),
\]

unital equivariant ring homomorphism $\rho:S\to T$ such that
\[
 q_*(g)=h,\qquad q\circ\rho=\nu_0,\qquad
 \Psi=i_g\circ\rho_*.
\]
The element $g$ is already uniquely determined by
\[
 g\,x_{\lambda,T}(1)\,g^{-1}
   =\Psi(x_{\lambda,S}(1))
 \qquad(\lambda\in\Phi_\rho),
\]
where the second subscript specifies the coefficient ring.
Neither $\Psi$ nor $\nu_0$ is assumed injective or surjective.
