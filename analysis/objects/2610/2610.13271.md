<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:intro-main}
Let $0<p<1$ and $n\ge2$.  A map
$f:\mathcal S_p^n\to\mathcal S_p^n$ is surjective and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maxidentity-intro}
 \mathfrak M_p(f(A),f(B))=\mathfrak M_p(A,B)\qquad(A,B\in\mathcal S_p^n)
\end{equation}
if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-form-intro}
 f(A)=\varepsilon(A)\Phi(A),
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon:\mathcal S_p^n\to\{-1,1\}$ is even and $\Phi$ is one of
$A\mapsto UAV$, $A\mapsto UA^{\mathsf t}V$,
$A\mapsto U\overline A V$, or $A\mapsto UA^*V$, with unitary matrices
$U,V$.  The value of $\varepsilon(0)$ is immaterial.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:p-additivity}
	Let $0<p<1$ and $A,B\in\mathcal S_p(H)$. Then
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-ptriangle}
		\lVert A+B\rVert_p^p
		=\lVert A\rVert_p^p+\lVert B\rVert_p^p
		\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\perp_{\!s} B.
	\end{equation}
	The same equivalence holds with $B$
	replaced by $-B$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The equivalence is a direct application of
	\cite[Lemma A.1]{HuangZhu2026} to the
	Schatten class $\mathcal S_p(H)$. The assertion
	for $-B$ follows by replacing $B$ with
	$-B$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:maxphase}
A map $f:\mathcal S_p(H_1)\to\mathcal S_p(H_2)$ is a \emph{max-phase isometry} if
\[
 \mathfrak M_p(f(A),f(B))=\mathfrak M_p(A,B)\qquad(A,B\in\mathcal S_p(H_1)),
\]
where $\mathfrak M_p$ is the max-phase kernel defined in the introduction.  No linearity,
continuity, or injectivity is included in the definition.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:norm-orth}
Let $0<p<1$ and let $f:\mathcal S_p(H_1)\to\mathcal S_p(H_2)$ be a surjective max-phase
isometry.  Then
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item $f(0)=0$ and $\lVert f(A)\rVert_p=\lVert A\rVert_p$ for every $A$;
 \item $A\perp_{\!s} B$ if and only if $f(A)\perp_{\!s} f(B)$;
 \item for $A\ne0$, the partially ordered family of orthogonality
 complements above $A^\perp$ is carried onto the corresponding family above
 $f(A)^\perp$.
\end{enumerate}
Here $A^\perp:=\{B\in\mathcal S_p(H_1):A\perp_{\!s} B\}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Putting $A=B=0$ in the max identity gives $f(0)=0$.  Putting $B=A$ gives
$2\lVert f(A)\rVert_p=2\lVert A\rVert_p$.

By \eqref{eq:p-triangle} and \Cref{lem:p-additivity},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orth-max-character}
 A\perp_{\!s} B
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathfrak M_p(A,B)^p=\lVert A\rVert_p^p+\lVert B\rVert_p^p.
\end{equation}
The max identity and norm preservation therefore prove (ii).

Finally, surjectivity and (ii) give
$f(A^\perp)=f(A)^\perp$.  If $A^\perp\subseteq B^\perp$ and
$W\in f(A)^\perp$, write $W=f(C)$.  Then $A\perp_{\!s} C$, hence $B\perp_{\!s} C$,
and therefore $f(B)\perp_{\!s} W$.  This proves
$f(A)^\perp\subseteq f(B)^\perp$; the converse follows in the same way.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:rankone-recognition}
Assume $\dim H\ge2$ and $0\ne A\in\mathcal S_p(H)$.  The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item $\operatorname{rank} A=1$.
 \item $A^\perp$ is maximal among the proper sets $B^\perp$ with $B\ne0$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, every surjective max-phase isometry preserves rank-one
operators in both directions.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Suppose first that $\operatorname{rank} A\ge2$.  Choose a rank-one partial isometry $e$
whose left and right supports are subprojections of $\ell(A)$ and
$r(A)$, respectively.  Every operator orthogonal to $A$ is orthogonal
to $e$, so $A^\perp\subseteq e^\perp$.  The inclusion is strict: choose a
second pair of initial and final unit vectors in the supports of $A$,
orthogonal to those of $e$, and form the corresponding rank-one operator.

Conversely, let $A$ have rank one and suppose
$A^\perp\subseteq B^\perp$ with $B\ne0$.  Testing the inclusion on
rank-one operators $\xi\otimes\eta$ with
$\xi\perp\ell(A)H$ and $\eta\perpr(A)H$ gives
\[
 \ell(B)\le\ell(A),\qquad r(B)\ler(A).
\]
Both right-hand projections have rank one, hence $B$ belongs to the same
one-dimensional complex corner as $A$, and $B^\perp=A^\perp$.  The final
assertion follows from \Cref{prop:norm-orth}(iii).
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:finite-rank-recognition}
For $A\ne0$, $\operatorname{rank} A$ is the largest cardinality of a family
$e_1,\dots,e_m$ of mutually support-orthogonal rank-one partial isometries
satisfying $A^\perp\subseteq e_j^\perp$ for every $j$.  In particular, a
surjective max-phase isometry preserves every finite rank.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The inclusion $A^\perp\subseteq e^\perp$ is equivalent to
$\ell(e)\le\ell(A)$ and $r(e)\ler(A)$.  Thus an orthogonal
family of such $e$'s is exactly a pair of orthonormal families in the left
and right support spaces.  Its maximal cardinality is $\operatorname{rank} A$.  Apply
\Cref{prop:norm-orth,lem:rankone-recognition} to obtain invariance.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:Phi-monotone}
	For fixed $D$, the function
	$s\mapsto\Phi_{p,D}(s)$ is strictly
	increasing on $[2\sqrt D,\infty)$.
	For fixed $s=2$, the function
	$D\mapsto\Phi_{p,D}(2)$ is strictly
	monotone when $p\ne2$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:rankone-formula}
For $e,v\in\mathcal U_{\min}(H)$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sym-rankone-formula}
 \mathfrak M_p(\lambda e,v)^p
 =\Phi_{p,D(e,v)}
   \left(2+2\lvert \Re\bigl(\lambda\overline{z(e,v)}\bigr)\rvert\right).
\end{equation}
Consequently, the function $\lambda\mapsto\mathfrak M_p(\lambda e,v)$ determines
both $D(e,v)$ and $|\operatorname{Tr}(e^*v)|$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Both $\lambda e-v$ and $\lambda e+v$ have rank at most two.  With respect
to orthonormal bases of $\operatorname{span}\{\xi,\zeta\}$ and
$\operatorname{span}\{\eta,\theta\}$, let $t_1,t_2$ be the eigenvalues of
$(\lambda e-v)^*(\lambda e-v)$.  A direct trace and determinant computation
(cf. \cite{HornJohnson2013}) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-det}
 t_1+t_2=2-2\Re\bigl(\lambda\overline z\bigr),
 \qquad t_1t_2=D,
\end{equation}
where $z=z(e,v)$ and $D=D(e,v)$.  Thus
\[
 \lVert \lambda e-v\rVert_p^p
 =\Phi_{p,D}\left(2-2\Re(\lambda\overline z)\right).
\]
Replacing $v$ by $-v$ changes the minus sign to a plus sign and leaves $D$
unchanged.  Since $\Phi_{p,D}$ is strictly increasing by
\Cref{lem:Phi-monotone}, taking the larger value gives
\eqref{eq:sym-rankone-formula}.

As $\lambda$ ranges over $\mathbb T$, the minimum and maximum of the right-hand
side are
\[
 \Phi_{p,D}(2),
 \qquad \Phi_{p,D}(2+2|z|),
\]
respectively.  The second part of \Cref{lem:Phi-monotone} recovers $D$
from the first number, and the first part then recovers $|z|$ from the
second.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:scalar}
	Let $\varphi:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ be a surjective
	map satisfying
	\[
	\max\{|\varphi(z)+\varphi(w)|,
	|\varphi(z)-\varphi(w)|\}
	=
	\max\{|z+w|,|z-w|\}
	\]
	for all $z,w\in\mathbb C$. Then
	\[
	\varphi(z)=\delta(z)Rz,
	\]
	where $R$ is a real-linear orthogonal
	map of $\mathbb C$ and
	$\delta:\mathbb C\to\{-1,1\}$ is even.
	If $\varphi(1)=1$, then, after
	absorbing a global sign,
	$Rz=z$ or $Rz=\overline z$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Identify $\mathbb C$ with the real Euclidean
	plane. By \cite{LiuLiuTan2025},
	$\varphi(z)=\delta(z)Rz$ for a
	real-linear orthogonal map $R$
	and a sign function $\delta$.
	
	Surjectivity forces
	$\delta(-z)=\delta(z)$; otherwise,
	the two points $z$ and $-z$ would
	have the same image, leaving its
	antipode outside the range.
	
	If $\varphi(1)=1$, a global sign
	adjustment gives $R(1)=1$.
	The only real orthogonal maps of
	$\mathbb C$ fixing $1$ are the identity
	and complex conjugation.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:rankone-rigidity}
	Let $n\ge2$ and let
	$f:\mathcal S_p^n\to\mathcal S_p^n$
	be a surjective max-phase isometry.
	There exists a standard real-linear Schatten
	isometry $T$, either complex linear or
	conjugate linear, such that
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:rankone-sign}
		f(ze)\in\{T(ze),-T(ze)\}
		\qquad(z\in\mathbb C,\ e\in\mathcal U_{\min}(\mathbb C^n)).
	\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Put $\mathcal U=\mathcal U_{\min}(\mathbb C^n)$.
	By \Cref{lem:rankone-recognition,prop:norm-orth},
	$f$ maps rank-one operators onto rank-one
	operators and preserves their orthogonality
	complements. Since two nonzero rank-one
	operators have the same orthogonality
	complement precisely when they span the
	same complex line, $f$ maps each such
	line onto another. Thus \Cref{lem:scalar}
	gives
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:line-orientation}
		f(\lambda e)
		=\delta_e(\lambda)
		\lambda^{\sigma(e)}f(e),
		\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb T,
	\end{equation}
	where $\delta_e(\lambda)\in\{-1,1\}$
	and $\sigma(e)\in\{1,-1\}$.
	
	By \Cref{lem:rankone-formula} and
	surjectivity on each line, $f$ preserves
	$D(e,v)$ and
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:absolute-transition}
		|\operatorname{Tr}(f(e)^*f(v))|
		=|\operatorname{Tr}(e^*v)|,\qquad e,v\in\mathcal U.
	\end{equation}
	Writing $z=\operatorname{Tr}(e^*v)$ and
	$w=\operatorname{Tr}(f(e)^*f(v))$, the same formula
	yields
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:orientation-identity}
		|\Re(\overline\lambda\mu z)|
		=
		\left|\Re\left(
		\overline{\lambda^{\sigma(e)}}
		\mu^{\sigma(v)}w\right)\right|
	\end{equation}
	for all $\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb T$.
	
	If $z\ne0$ and $\sigma(e)\ne\sigma(v)$,
	setting $\lambda=\mu$ makes the left-hand
	side constant while the right-hand side
	varies with $\mu$, a contradiction.
	Since the graph joining pairs with
	nonzero trace is connected, $\sigma(e)$
	has a common value $\sigma$.
	Varying $\lambda$ then gives
	\[
	w\in
	\begin{cases}
		\{z,-z\},&\sigma=1,\\
		\{\overline z,-\overline z\},&\sigma=-1.
	\end{cases}
	\]
	
	It remains to remove these pair-dependent
	signs. Equip
	$Q=\mathcal U/\{\pm1\}$ with
	\[
	d_Q([e],[v])^2
	=2-2|\Re\operatorname{Tr}(e^*v)|.
	\]
	The preceding identity shows that
	$[e]\mapsto[f(e)]$ is a surjective
	isometry of $Q$, hence a homeomorphism.
	
	The manifold $\mathcal U$ is simply
	connected for $n\ge2$, as follows
	from its circle-bundle representation
	\[
	\mathcal U\cong
	(S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1})/\mathbb T.
	\]
	Consequently, the homeomorphism of
	$Q$ lifts through the double covering
	$\mathcal U\to Q$ to a homeomorphism
	$g:\mathcal U\to\mathcal U$ satisfying
	$g(e)=\pm f(e)$.
	
	On the set
	\[
	\Omega=\{(e,v)\in\mathcal U^2:
	\operatorname{Tr}(e^*v)\ne0\},
	\]
	the continuous function
	\[
	(e,v)\longmapsto
	\begin{cases}
		\displaystyle
		\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(g(e)^*g(v))}{\operatorname{Tr}(e^*v)},
		&\sigma=1,\\[6pt]
		\displaystyle
		\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(g(e)^*g(v))}
		{\overline{\operatorname{Tr}(e^*v)}},
		&\sigma=-1
	\end{cases}
	\]
	takes values in $\{-1,1\}$.
	The set $\Omega$ is path connected:
	for fixed $e$, the condition
	$\operatorname{Tr}(e^*v)\ne0$ defines a path-connected
	subset containing $e$, and the diagonal
	of $\mathcal U^2$ is connected.
	Since the displayed function equals
	$1$ on the diagonal, it is identically
	$1$. Together with
	\eqref{eq:absolute-transition}, this
	proves
	\[
	\operatorname{Tr}(g(e)^*g(v))
	=
	\begin{cases}
		\operatorname{Tr}(e^*v),&\sigma=1,\\
		\overline{\operatorname{Tr}(e^*v)},&\sigma=-1
	\end{cases}
	\]
	for all $e,v\in\mathcal U$.
	
	We may therefore apply
	\cite[Theorem 2]{Molnar2002},
	composing with entrywise conjugation
	when $\sigma=-1$.
	It follows that
	$g(e)=T(e)$ for a standard
	real-linear Schatten isometry $T$
	of the asserted orientation.
	Since $f(e)=\pm g(e)$, the scalar
	classification in \Cref{lem:scalar}
	extends this relation to every
	complex multiple of $e$, proving
	\eqref{eq:rankone-sign}.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:functional-phase-retrieval}
Let $E,F$ be complex Hilbert spaces of the same finite dimension, and let
$C,D:E\to F$ be invertible.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:functional-phase}
 |\Re\operatorname{Tr}(C^*e)|=|\Re\operatorname{Tr}(D^*e)|
\end{equation}
for every rank-one operator $e:E\to F$, then $D=C$ or $D=-C$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Fix $\eta\in E$.  Operators of the form $\xi\otimes\eta$, with $\xi\in F$,
form a real linear space consisting of rank at most one.  On this space the
two sides of \eqref{eq:functional-phase} are the absolute values of two real
linear functionals.  Two real linear functionals with identical absolute
values are equal or opposite; hence
\[
 D\eta=\tau(\eta)C\eta,
 \qquad \tau(\eta)\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
If $\eta_1,\eta_2$ are linearly independent, invertibility of $C$ and the
identity for $\eta_1+\eta_2$ force
$\tau(\eta_1)=\tau(\eta_2)=\tau(\eta_1+\eta_2)$.  Collinear vectors are
handled directly.  Thus $\tau$ is constant and $D=\pm C$.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:max-profile}
Let $0<p<1$ and $A,B\in M_n(\mathbb C)$.  Suppose that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile-hyp}
 \mathfrak M_p(A,te)=\mathfrak M_p(B,te)
 \qquad(t\in\mathbb R,\ e\in\mathcal U_{\min}(\mathbb C^n)).
\end{equation}
Then $B=A$ or $B=-A$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Taking $t=0$ gives $\lVert A\rVert_p=\lVert B\rVert_p$.  If $A=0$, then $B=0$ and there
is nothing to prove; hence assume that both matrices are nonzero.  By
\eqref{eq:orth-max-character}, for a rank-one $e$,
\[
 A\perp_{\!s} e
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathfrak M_p(A,e)^p=\lVert A\rVert_p^p+1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B\perp_{\!s} e.
\]
Thus $A$ and $B$ have the same rank-one orthogonality locus.  If their rank
is less than $n$, this locus is the Segre product
\[
 \mathbb P(\ell(A)^\perp H)
 \times \mathbb P(r(A)^\perp H),
\]
and equality of the two products gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:same-supports}
 \ell(A)=\ell(B),\qquad r(A)=r(B).
\end{equation}
If the locus is empty, both matrices have full rank, and
\eqref{eq:same-supports} also holds.  Denote the common support spaces by
$F$ and $E$.  Restricted from $E$ to $F$, both $A$ and $B$ are invertible.

Write $A=u_A|A|$ and set
\[
 J_p(A)=u_A|A|^{p-1}:E\to F;
\]
define $J_p(B)$ similarly.  For a rank-one $e:E\to F$, finite-dimensional
trace differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:directional-derivative}
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\lVert A+te\rVert_p^p
 =p\Re\operatorname{Tr}(J_p(A)^*e).
\end{equation}
Indeed, this is the standard derivative of
$X\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}|X|^p$ at an invertible matrix; it follows either from singular
value perturbation or from the divided-difference formula for trace
functions.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:max-expansion}
 \mathfrak M_p(A,te)^p
 =\lVert A\rVert_p^p
  +p|t|\,|\Re\operatorname{Tr}(J_p(A)^*e)|+o(|t|).
\end{equation}
The identical expansion holds for $B$.  Comparing it with
\eqref{eq:profile-hyp} yields
\[
 |\Re\operatorname{Tr}(J_p(A)^*e)|
 =|\Re\operatorname{Tr}(J_p(B)^*e)|
\]
for every rank-one $e:E\to F$.  By
\Cref{lem:functional-phase-retrieval},
$J_p(B)=\pm J_p(A)$.

Finally, $J_p$ is injective up to the same real sign on the invertible
corner.  In fact, the polar part of $J_p(A)$ is $u_A$ and
$|J_p(A)|=|A|^{p-1}$, whence
\[
 A=u_A|J_p(A)|^{1/(p-1)}.
\]
It follows that $B=A$ in the plus case and $B=-A$ in the minus case.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:why-profile}
One value of the max-kernel is not sufficient to recognize a ray below one.
For example, noncollinear unit vectors in $\ell_p^2$ can satisfy
$\max\{\lVert x+y\rVert_p,\lVert x-y\rVert_p\}=2$.  The strength of
\Cref{thm:max-profile} is the use of the entire one-parameter rank-one
profile, not a single equality case.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:matrix-classification}
Let $0<p<1$ and $n\ge2$.  A map
$f:\mathcal S_p^n\to\mathcal S_p^n$ is a surjective max-phase isometry if
and only if there are a surjective real-linear Schatten isometry $T$ and an
even function $\varepsilon:\mathcal S_p^n\to\{-1,1\}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstract-classification}
 f(A)=\varepsilon(A)T(A)\qquad(A\in\mathcal S_p^n).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Assume first that $f$ is a surjective max-phase isometry.  By
\Cref{prop:rankone-rigidity}, choose a real-linear Schatten isometry $T$ for
which $f(te)=\pm T(te)$ on every rank-one line.  Fix $A$ and put
$B=T^{-1}f(A)$.  For $e\in\mathcal U_{\min}$ and $t\in\mathbb R$, the sign attached to $te$
only interchanges the two terms in a maximum.  Hence
\begin{align*}
 \mathfrak M_p(B,te)
 &=\mathfrak M_p(T(B),T(te))\\
 &=\mathfrak M_p(f(A),f(te))
  =\mathfrak M_p(A,te).
\end{align*}
By \Cref{thm:max-profile}, $B=\pm A$.  Thus
$f(A)=\varepsilon(A)T(A)$ for some sign $\varepsilon(A)$.

It remains to use surjectivity.  For $A\ne0$, the only possible preimages of
$T(A)$ under the displayed formula are $A$ and $-A$.  The two values
$f(A)$ and $f(-A)$ cover both $T(A)$ and $-T(A)$ exactly when
\[
 \varepsilon(-A)=\varepsilon(A).
\]
Since $f$ is onto, $\varepsilon$ is even.

Conversely, let \eqref{eq:abstract-classification} hold with $\varepsilon$ even.
For $A,B$ set $\alpha=\varepsilon(A)\varepsilon(B)\in\{-1,1\}$.  Then
\begin{align*}
 \mathfrak M_p(f(A),f(B))
 &=\max\{\lVert T(A)+\alpha T(B)\rVert_p,
          \lVert T(A)-\alpha T(B)\rVert_p\}\\
 &=\max\{\lVert A+\alpha B\rVert_p,\lVert A-\alpha B\rVert_p\}
  =\mathfrak M_p(A,B).
\end{align*}
To see surjectivity, let $C=T(A)$.  If $\varepsilon(A)=1$, then $f(A)=C$; if
$\varepsilon(A)=-1$, evenness gives $f(-A)=C$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:concrete}
Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:matrix-classification}, there exist
unitary matrices $U,V$ such that the isometry $T$ in
\eqref{eq:abstract-classification} is exactly one of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourforms}
 T(A)=UAV,\qquad UA^{\mathsf t}V,\qquad
 U\overline A V,\qquad UA^*V.
\end{equation}
Conversely, every map $f(A)=\varepsilon(A)T(A)$ with $T$ as in
\eqref{eq:fourforms} and $\varepsilon$ even is a surjective max-phase isometry.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
A surjective real-linear isometry of $\mathcal S_p^n$, $p\ne2$, is either
complex linear or conjugate linear.  In the complex-linear case, the
Arazy--Erdos theorem gives $UAV$ or $UA^{\mathsf t}V$.  Composing a
conjugate-linear isometry with entrywise conjugation reduces it to the
complex-linear case and gives the other two forms.  The converse is contained
in \Cref{thm:matrix-classification} and is also immediate from unitary
invariance of the Schatten quasi-norm.
<!-- end proof-11 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:scalar-case}
When $n=1$, the conclusion is \Cref{lem:scalar}: a surjective max-phase map
on $\mathbb C$, viewed as a real plane, is an even pointwise sign times a real
orthogonal map.  Rotations and reflections are precisely the scalar
instances of the complex-linear and conjugate-linear forms in
\eqref{eq:fourforms}.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:finite-rank-core}
Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space with $\dim H\ge2$, let $0<p<1$, and let
$f:\mathcal S_p(H)\to\mathcal S_p(H)$ be a surjective max-phase isometry.  Then there exists
a standard surjective real-linear Schatten isometry $T$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-rank-core}
 f(A)\in\{T(A),-T(A)\}
 \qquad(A\in\mathcal F(H)).
\end{equation}
Moreover, $f$ preserves every finite rank, both singular support dimensions,
and two-sided support orthogonality on all of $\mathcal S_p(H)$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
The last assertion about orthogonality follows from
\Cref{prop:norm-orth}; finite-rank preservation follows from
\Cref{lem:finite-rank-recognition}.  The proof of
\Cref{prop:rankone-rigidity} uses only rank-two spectral computations, the
projective Wigner theorem, and connectedness of the rank-one partial
isometries, all valid on an arbitrary Hilbert space.  It therefore supplies
a standard $T$ for which \eqref{eq:rankone-sign} holds.

Fix $A\in\mathcal F(H)$ and set $B=T^{-1}f(A)$.  Rank preservation makes $B$
finite rank.  The same calculation as in the proof of
\Cref{thm:matrix-classification} gives
$\mathfrak M_p(A,te)=\mathfrak M_p(B,te)$ for every rank-one $e$ and real $t$.  Equality of
rank-one orthogonality loci identifies the finite-dimensional left and right
support spaces of $A$ and $B$.  Restricting to their common finite support
corner, the proof of \Cref{thm:max-profile} applies verbatim and gives
$B=\pm A$.  This proves \eqref{eq:finite-rank-core}.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:singular-values}
Let $0<p<1$, $n\ge2$, and let $f$ be as in
\Cref{thm:matrix-classification}.  Then
\[
 s_j(f(A))=s_j(A)\qquad(1\le j\le n),
\]
and hence $\operatorname{rank} f(A)=\operatorname{rank} A$ for every $A$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Each of the four maps in \eqref{eq:fourforms} preserves the full singular
value sequence, and multiplication by a real sign has no effect on singular
values.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:continuous}
Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space, $\dim H\ge2$, and $0<p<1$.  Suppose
$f:\mathcal S_p(H)\to\mathcal S_p(H)$ is a continuous surjective max-phase isometry.  Then
there are a standard surjective real-linear Schatten isometry $T$ and a
constant $\delta\in\{-1,1\}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuous-form}
 f(A)=\delta T(A)\qquad(A\in\mathcal S_p(H)).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Let $T$ be supplied by \Cref{thm:finite-rank-core}.  On
$\mathcal F(H)\setminus\{0\}$ write $f(A)=\varepsilon(A)T(A)$.  Continuity implies that
$\varepsilon$ is locally constant there: otherwise, a sequence $A_k\to A\ne0$
with $\varepsilon(A_k)=-\varepsilon(A)$ would make $f(A_k)$ converge simultaneously to
$T(A)$ and $-T(A)$ up to the fixed sign.  The punctured finite-rank ideal is
path connected.  Indeed, any two of its nonzero elements lie in a
finite-dimensional real subspace of dimension at least two, and a polygonal
path can be chosen to avoid the origin.  Hence $\varepsilon$ is a single constant
$\delta$ on all nonzero finite-rank operators.

For arbitrary $A\in\mathcal S_p(H)$ choose finite-rank $A_k\to A$ in the Schatten
quasi-norm.  If $A\ne0$, continuity of $f$ and $T$ gives
\[
 f(A)=\lim_k f(A_k)=\delta\lim_k T(A_k)=\delta T(A).
\]
At zero the identity is automatic.
<!-- end proof-14 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:weaker-regularity}
The proof only uses continuity on the punctured class and sequential
continuity along finite-rank approximations.  Thus the conclusion remains
valid under that weaker pair of assumptions.  By contrast, measurability
alone cannot eliminate the phase: an even Borel sign function in
\eqref{eq:abstract-classification} still gives a Borel max-phase isometry.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:line-test}
In $\ell_p^2$, put
\[
 x=2^{-1/p}(1,1),\qquad y=2^{-1/p}(1,-1).
\]
Then $\lVert x\rVert_p=\lVert y\rVert_p=1$, but
\[
 \max\{\lVert x+y\rVert_p,\lVert x-y\rVert_p\}=2^{1-1/p}<1.
\]
Thus the longer diagonal of two unit vectors may be shorter than either
vector.  In particular, equality in an ordinary triangle inequality cannot
be used to recover collinearity.  The proof of the main theorem succeeds
because it detects orthogonality through $p$-additivity and then uses the
entire rank-one perturbation profile.
