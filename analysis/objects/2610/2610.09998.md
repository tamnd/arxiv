<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A composite DNA symbol $S$ over the alphabet $\mathcal{X}$ is defined by the integer counts
\[
S = (n_A, n_C, n_G, n_T), \qquad \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} n_x = k,
\]
where $k$ is called the resolution parameter and $n_x$ represents the count of nucleotide $x$ used to form the composite symbol. Equivalently, $S$ represents an empirical distribution $p_S(x) = n_x / k$ over $\mathcal{X}$. We denote the set of all composite symbols with resolution $k$ as the composite alphabet $\mathcal{A}_k$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:binning}
For a resolution parameter $k$, the \emph{GC probability mass} is defined as $q(S) = p_S(G) + p_S(C)$. As it takes discrete values in $\{0, \frac{1}{k}, \dots, 1\}$,  we partition $\mathcal{A}_k$ into $k + 1$ disjoint probability bins $\mathcal{Q} = \{\mathcal{B}_0, \dots, \mathcal{B}_k\}$, where all symbols in bin $\mathcal{B}_i$ share the same GC probability mass, meaning
$\mathcal{B}_i = \{S \in \mathcal{A}_k : q(S) = \frac{i}{k} \}$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:det_gc}

    Let \(\mathbf{S} = [S_1, \dots, S_n] \in \mathcal{A}_k^n\). We say \(\mathbf{S}\) complies with the deterministic GC constraint with limits \(\alpha \le \beta \in [0,1]\) if
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    \begin{align}
        n\alpha \leq \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{I}_{\min}(S_i) \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{I}_{\max}(S_i) \leq n \beta.
    \end{align}
    \vspace{-0.4cm} \\
    We further say that \(\mathbf{S}\) complies with the \(w\)-windowed deterministic GC constraint with limits \(\alpha \le \beta \in [0,1]\) if any subsequence \(\mathbf{S}' = [S_i, \ldots , S_{i+w-1}]\) for \(i \in \{ 1, \ldots , n-w+1 \} \) complies with the deterministic GC constraint.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
Let \(k=4\), and let the standard acceptable GC limits for a sequence of length \(n=10\) be \(\alpha = 0.4\) and \(\beta = 0.6\). This requires the physical strand to contain between 4 and 6 GC nucleotides. 
    Consider a composite sequence \(\mathbf{S}\) consisting of 4 pure G/C symbols (\(q(S)=1\)), 3 pure A/T symbols (\(q(S)=0\)), and 3 mixed symbols biased toward A/T (e.g., 3 adenines and 1 cytosine, yielding \(q=0.25\)).
    
    Under the deterministic framework, we evaluate the worst-case bounds. The 4 pure G/C symbols guarantee the lower bound (\(\sum \mathbb{I}_{\min} = 4 \ge 4\)). However, the deterministic upper bound assumes all 3 mixed symbols will, in the worst case, manifest as cytosine. This yields a maximum potential weight of \(\sum \mathbb{I}_{\max} = 4 + 3 = 7\). Since \(7 > 6\), the sequence \(\mathbf{S}\) is rejected as invalid.
    
    Analyzing the strand probabilistically reveals a different reality. As the pure symbols are fixed, the sequence only violates the upper bound if \textit{all three} mixed symbols simultaneously manifest as cytosine. The probability of this single worst-case realization is \((0.25)^3 = 1/64 \approx 0.016\). This means that \(98.4\%\) of the millions of physical strands synthesized from this sequence will have exactly 4, 5, or 6 GC nucleotides, perfectly complying with the strict 40\%--60\% thermodynamic requirements. Discarding this sequence represents a massive loss of coding capacity. 

    Allowing the sequence, on the other hand, would only slightly bias the composite pool. Therefore, the GC constraint is better evaluated probabilistically, setting the threshold on the probability $\epsilon$ of violation rather than the worst-case realization.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:prob_gc}
    Let \(\mathbf{S} = [S_1, \dots, S_n] \in \mathcal{A}_k^n\) be a composite sequence of length \(n\). Furthermore, let \(Y\) be the random variable depicting the total physical GC count of a DNA strand synthesized from $\mathbf{S}$. We say \(\mathbf{S}\) complies with the \(\epsilon\)-probabilistic GC constraint with limits \(\alpha \le \beta \in [0,1]\) and error tolerance $ \epsilon \in [0, 1) $ if
    \begin{equation}
        \Pr\left(Y < n\alpha\right) + \Pr\left(Y > n\beta\right) \leq \epsilon.
    \end{equation}
    We further say that \(\mathbf{S}\) complies with the \(w\)-windowed \(\epsilon\)-probabilistic GC constraint if any subsequence \(\mathbf{S}' = [S_i, \ldots , S_{i+w-1}]\) for \(i \in \{1, \ldots , n-w+1\}\) complies with the \(\epsilon\)-probabilistic GC constraint.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
For a strict error tolerance of \(\epsilon = 0\), the probabilistic approach exactly reduces to the deterministic worst-case baseline.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:type}
Given a sequence \(\mathbf{S} \in \mathcal{A}_k^n\), we define the type of \(\mathbf{S}\) as \(\mathbf{t}(\mathbf{S}) = (t_0(\mathbf{S}), \dots, t_k(\mathbf{S}))\), where \(t_i(\mathbf{S})\) denotes the number of positions of \(\mathbf{S}\) assigned to probability bin \(\mathcal{B}_i\) as defined in Definition \ref{def:binning}. Furthermore, given a vector \(\mathbf{t} = (t_0, \ldots , t_k)\) such that \(t_i \geq 0\) for all \(i \in \{0, \ldots , k \} \) and \(\sum_{i=0}^k t_i = n\), we denote by
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{T}_\mathbf{t} = \left\{ \mathbf{S} \in \mathcal{A}_k^n : \mathbf{t}(\mathbf{S}) = \mathbf{t} \right\}
\end{equation*}
the type-class of type \(\mathbf{t}\).
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:exact_pmf}
For a sequence of type \(\mathbf{t}=(t_0,\ldots,t_k)\), let
\(
Y_i \sim \ensuremath{\operatorname{Bin}}\left(t_i,\frac{i}{k}\right)
\)
denote the binomially distributed GC count contributed by the symbols in bin
\(\mathcal{B}_i\), and let \(f_i\) denote the \ac{pmf} of \(Y_i\). Then the \ac{pmf} of the total GC count
\(
Y=\sum_{i=0}^{k}Y_i
\)
is
\(
f_Y=f_0*f_1*\cdots*f_k.
\)
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:type_compliance}
For any two composite sequences \(\mathbf{S}_1, \mathbf{S}_2 \in \mathcal{T}_\mathbf{t}\), \(\mathbf{S}_1\) complies with the global \(\epsilon\)-probabilistic GC constraint if and only if \(\mathbf{S}_2\) complies.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
The set of all sequence types that satisfy the global \(\epsilon\)-probabilistic GC constraint is denoted as:
\vspace{-0.4cm}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}_{\epsilon} = \left\{ \mathbf{t} : \Pr\left(Y_\mathbf{t} < n\alpha\right) + \Pr\left(Y_\mathbf{t} > n\beta\right) \le \epsilon \right\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:class_cardinality}

For a given sequence type \(\mathbf{t} = (t_0, \dots, t_k)\), the total number of composite sequences in the type-class \(\mathcal{T}_\mathbf{t}\) is given by:
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    |\mathcal{T}_\mathbf{t}| = \frac{n!}{\prod_{i=0}^k t_i!} \prod_{i=0}^k |\mathcal{B}_i|^{t_i}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def:global_GC_capacity}
Let \(\mathsf{p}\) be a condition on vectors over \(\mathcal{A}_k^n\) and let \(\mathcal{V}_\mathsf{p} \subseteq \mathcal{A}_k^n\) be the set of all vectors fulfilling that condition. Then we define the capacity for codes constrained by \(\mathsf{p}\) as
\begin{align}
C_\mathsf{p} =
\lim_{n \rightarrow \infty}
\frac{\log_2 \left| \mathcal{V}_\mathsf{p} \right|}
{n}.
\end{align}
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:global_capacity}
Given a resolution parameter \(k\) and an error tolerance \(\epsilon\), let
\(\mathbf{p}_\mathbf{t} = (t_0/n, \dots, t_k/n)\) denote the empirical bin distribution of type \(\mathbf{t}\) with entropy \(H(\mathbf{p}_\mathbf{t}) = -\sum_{i=0}^k \frac{t_i}{n} \log_2 \frac{t_i}{n}\), where \(0 \log_2 0 = 0\), and let
\vspace{-0.4cm}
\begin{align*}
F(\mathbf{t}) = H(\mathbf{p}_\mathbf{t}) + \sum_{i=0}^k \frac{t_i}{n} \log_2 |\mathcal{B}_i|.
\end{align*}
Then the global \(\epsilon\)-probabilistic GC constrained capacity is
\begin{align}
    C_{\text{global-GC}}(\epsilon) 
    &= \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{1}{n} \log_2 \left( \sum_{\mathbf{t} \in \mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}} \frac{n!}{\prod_{i=0}^k t_i!} \prod_{i=0}^k |\mathcal{B}_i|^{t_i} \right) \nonumber
    \\
    &= \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \max_{\mathbf{t} \in \mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}} F(\mathbf{t}),
\end{align}
where \(\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}\) is the allowed type set for length \(n\), provided that \(\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon} \neq \emptyset\) and the limit exists.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The set \(\mathcal{V}_{\text{global-GC}}\) of valid sequences is the disjoint union of the type-classes \(\mathcal{T}_\mathbf{t}\), \(\mathbf{t} \in \mathcal{P}_\epsilon\). Each has size \(2^{nF(\mathbf{t})}\) up to a polynomial factor, and there are polynomially many types~\cite{cover_thomas_types}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:eps_asymptotic}
Let \(\mu(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{i=0}^k p_i \frac{i}{k}\) denote the expected GC fraction of a sequence with bin distribution \(\mathbf{p}\). Using Chebyshev's inequality (see Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}), for \(\alpha < \beta\) and any fixed \(\epsilon \in (0,1)\), the capacity does not depend on \(\epsilon\):
\vspace{-0.3cm}
\begin{align*}
C_{\text{global-GC}}(\epsilon) = \max_{\mathbf{p} : \, \alpha \leq \mu(\mathbf{p}) \leq \beta} \left( H(\mathbf{p}) + \sum_{i=0}^k p_i \log_2 |\mathcal{B}_i| \right).
\end{align*}
In particular, since \(|\mathcal{B}_i| = |\mathcal{B}_{k-i}|\), the unconstrained maximizer \(p_i = |\mathcal{B}_i|/|\mathcal{A}_k|\) satisfies \(\mu(\mathbf{p}) = \frac{1}{2}\), and thus \(C_{\text{global-GC}}(\epsilon) = \log_2 |\mathcal{A}_k|\) whenever \(\alpha \leq \frac{1}{2} \leq \beta\). This does not hold for the deterministic case \(\epsilon = 0\), see Corollary~\ref{cor:det_capacity} in Appendix~\ref{app:deterministic}.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:window_prob_capacity}
Let \(\mathcal{G}(V,E)\) be a directed graph whose vertices \(V\) are the set of all \((w-1)\)-tuples of probability-mass bins \(\mathcal{B}_i\), \(i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\). 
An edge from a vertex \(s=(b_1,\ldots,b_{w-1})\) to a vertex \(s'=(b'_1,\ldots,b'_{w-1})\) exists if the following two conditions hold: 
(i) \((b_2,\ldots,b_{w-1})=(b'_1,\ldots,b'_{w-2})\), and
(ii) the \(w\)-tuple \((b_1,\ldots,b_{w-1},b'_{w-1})\) satisfies the \(\epsilon\)-probabilistic GC constraint. Each valid edge corresponding to the appended bin \(\mathcal{B}_{j}\) is assigned weight \(|\mathcal{B}_{j}|\). 
Let \(T_\epsilon\) denote the resulting weighted transition matrix and let \(v^{(w)}_\epsilon(s)\) count the valid length-\(w\) composite sequences whose last \(w-1\) bins equal \(s\), i.e., \(v^{(w)}_\epsilon = u T_\epsilon\) with \(u(s) = \prod_{j=1}^{w-1} |\mathcal{B}_{b_j}|\). 
Then, for \(n \geq w\), the number of elements in the set \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{w-GC}}\) of composite sequences fulfilling the \(w\)-windowed \(\epsilon\)-probabilistic GC constraint is
\begin{equation}
    |\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{w-GC}}|
    =
    v^{(w)}_\epsilon T_\epsilon^{\,n-w}\mathbf{1}^{T},
\end{equation}
and, if the largest absolute eigenvalue of \(T_\epsilon\) is positive, i.e., \(\lambda(T_\epsilon) > 0\), the corresponding capacity is
\begin{equation}
    C_{\mathrm{w-GC}}(\epsilon)
    =
    \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty}
    \frac{1}{n} \log_2 |\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{w-GC}}|
    =
    \log_2 \lambda(T_\epsilon).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Valid sequences correspond to weighted walks in \(\mathcal{G}\), and the growth rate of \(T_\epsilon^{\,n-w}\) is \(\lambda(T_\epsilon)\) by Gelfand's formula.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:eps_window}
For \(k=4\), \(\alpha=0.4\), and \(\beta=0.6\), evaluating \(\lambda(T_\epsilon)\) gives normalized capacities \(C_{\mathrm{w-GC}}(\epsilon)/\log_2|\mathcal{A}_4| \approx 0.629, 0.629, 0.770\) for \(w=5\) and \(\epsilon = 0, 0.05, 0.2\), and \( C_{\mathrm{w-GC}}(\epsilon)/\log_2|\mathcal{A}_4| \approx 0.674, 0.678, 0.839\) for \(w=10\). In contrast to the global constraint (Remark~\ref{rem:eps_asymptotic}), \(\epsilon\) determines the capacity, since the window length \(w\) does not grow with $n$. For small \(\epsilon\), it coincides with the deterministic capacity, see Appendix~\ref{app:deterministic}.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:prefix_count}
Let \(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}\) be a prefix of length \(\ell\). For any allowed type \(\mathbf{t} = (t_0, \dots, t_k) \in \mathcal{P}_\epsilon\), let \(c_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}})\) denote the number of symbols currently in \(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}\) that belong to probability bin \(\mathcal{B}_i\). The remaining number of symbols required from bin \(\mathcal{B}_i\) to complete type \(\mathbf{t}\) is \(t_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}) = t_i - c_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}})\). The total number of valid length-\(n\) sequences extending the prefix \(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}\) is:
\begin{equation}
    N(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}) = \sum_{\mathbf{t} \in \mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}} N_\mathbf{t}(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}),
\end{equation}
where the per-type continuation count \(N_\mathbf{t}(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}})\) is given by the multinomial extension:
\begin{equation}
    N_\mathbf{t}(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}) \! = \!\!
    \begin{cases} 
        \frac{(n-\ell)!}{\prod_{i=0}^{k} t_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}})!} \prod\limits_{i=0}^{k} |\mathcal{B}_i|^{t_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}})}, 
        & \!\!\!\! \text{if } t_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}) \! \ge \! 0 \text{ for all } i, \!\!
        \\
        0, & \!\!\!\! \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
A type-\(\mathbf{t}\) sequence contains \(t_i\) symbols from
\(\mathcal{B}_i\). Their GC contributions are independent Bernoulli
trials with success probability \(\frac{i}{k}\), so
\(Y_i \sim \ensuremath{\operatorname{Bin}}(t_i,\frac{i}{k})\). Since the variables
\(Y_0,\ldots,Y_k\) are mutually independent and
\(Y=\sum_{i=0}^k Y_i\), the \ac{pmf} of \(Y\) is the convolution of their
individual \acp{pmf}~\cite{Grinstead2025Sums}.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
As established in Proposition~\ref{prop:exact_pmf}, the exact Poisson binomial \ac{pmf} of a sequence's total physical GC count \(Y\) is determined entirely by the convolution of the binomial distributions associated with each probability bin. These binomial distributions depend exclusively on the bin counts \((t_0, \dots, t_k)\) specified by the type \(\mathbf{t}\). Since \(\mathbf{S}_1\) and \(\mathbf{S}_2\) belong to the same type-class \(\mathcal{T}_\mathbf{t}\), their total GC counts are identically distributed. Consequently, their tail violation probabilities \(\Pr\left(Y < n\alpha\right) + \Pr\left(Y > n\beta\right)\) are exactly equal.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Constructing a sequence of type \(\mathbf{t}\) is a two-step process. First, we must assign each of the \(n\) sequence positions to one of the \(k+1\) probability bins such that exactly \(t_i\) positions are assigned to bin \(\mathcal{B}_i\). The number of distinct ways to partition these positions is given by the multinomial coefficient \(\frac{n!}{\prod_{i=0}^k t_i!}\). 

Second, for each of the \(t_i\) positions assigned to bin \(\mathcal{B}_i\), we must select a specific composite symbol from that bin. Since there are \(|\mathcal{B}_i|\) distinct symbols in bin \(\mathcal{B}_i\), there are \(|\mathcal{B}_i|^{t_i}\) possible symbol combinations for those positions and the proposition follows.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let \(\mathcal{V}_{\text{global-GC}}\) denote the set of valid sequences satisfying the global probabilistic GC constraint. 
A sequence satisfies the constraint if and only if its type belongs to \(\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}\). Consequently, \(\mathcal{V}_{\text{global-GC}}\) forms the disjoint union of valid type-classes, \(\mathcal{V}_{\text{global-GC}} = \bigcup_{\mathbf{t} \in \mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}} \mathcal{T}_\mathbf{t}\). We obtain the total number of valid sequences \(|\mathcal{V}_{\text{global-GC}}|\) by summing their cardinalities via Proposition \ref{prop:class_cardinality}. 

From \cite[Theorem 11.1.3]{cover_thomas_types}, applied to the sequence of probability bins over an alphabet of size \(k+1\), we know that
\begin{align*}
   \frac{1}{(n+1)^{k+1}} 2^{n H(\mathbf{p}_\mathbf{t})} \leq \frac{n!}{\prod_{i=0}^k t_i!} \leq 2^{n H(\mathbf{p}_\mathbf{t})}.
\end{align*}
Since \(\prod_{i=0}^k |\mathcal{B}_i|^{t_i} = 2^{n \sum_{i} \frac{t_i}{n} \log_2 |\mathcal{B}_i|}\), Proposition~\ref{prop:class_cardinality} gives
\begin{align*}
   \frac{1}{(n+1)^{k+1}} 2^{n F(\mathbf{t})} \leq \left| \mathcal{T}_\mathbf{t} \right| \leq 2^{n F(\mathbf{t})}.
\end{align*}
Let \(F^*_n = \max_{\mathbf{t} \in \mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}} F(\mathbf{t})\). As the number of types is at most \((n+1)^{k+1}\)~\cite[Theorem 11.1.1]{cover_thomas_types}, we have
\begin{align*}
   \frac{1}{(n+1)^{k+1}} 2^{n F^*_n} \leq |\mathcal{V}_{\text{global-GC}}| \leq (n+1)^{k+1} 2^{n F^*_n}.
\end{align*}
Taking \(\frac{1}{n}\log_2(\cdot)\), both bounds differ from \(F^*_n\) by at most \(\frac{(k+1)\log_2(n+1)}{n} \rightarrow 0\) as \(n \rightarrow \infty\), which proves the claim.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The Poisson binomial variable \(Y_\mathbf{t}\) has mean \(n\mu(\mathbf{p}_\mathbf{t})\) and variance at most \(n/4\). By Chebyshev's inequality, every type with
\begin{align*}
\alpha + \frac{1}{2\sqrt{n\epsilon}} \leq \mu(\mathbf{p}_\mathbf{t}) \leq \beta - \frac{1}{2\sqrt{n\epsilon}}
\end{align*}
belongs to \(\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}\), while for any fixed \(\delta > 0\) and sufficiently large \(n\), no type with \(\mu(\mathbf{p}_\mathbf{t}) < \alpha - \delta\) or \(\mu(\mathbf{p}_\mathbf{t}) > \beta + \delta\) does. Since \(F\) is continuous, the claim follows from Theorem~\ref{prop:global_capacity}.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
By construction, \(v^{(w)}_{\epsilon}\) counts valid length-\(w\)
sequences, while \(T_{\epsilon}\) extends them through valid weighted
transitions, with each transition weight accounting for the number of
composite symbols in the appended bin. Hence
\[
|\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{w-GC}}|
=
v^{(w)}_{\epsilon}T_{\epsilon}^{\,n-w}\mathbf{1}^{T}.
\]
Since sequences shorter than \(w\) are unconstrained, every length-\((w-1)\) prefix is valid, which gives \(v^{(w)}_\epsilon = u T_\epsilon\). Let \(m = n-w+1\) and let \(\sigma_m = \mathbf{1} T_\epsilon^{m} \mathbf{1}^T\) be the sum of all entries of \(T_\epsilon^m\). As \(1 \leq u(s) \leq |\mathcal{A}_k|^{w-1}\) for all \(s\), we have \(\sigma_m \leq |\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{w-GC}}| \leq |\mathcal{A}_k|^{w-1} \sigma_m\). Since \(T_\epsilon\) is non-negative, \(\|T_\epsilon^m\|_\infty \leq \sigma_m \leq (k+1)^{w-1} \|T_\epsilon^m\|_\infty\), where \(\|\cdot\|_\infty\) is the maximum row sum and \((k+1)^{w-1}\) is the number of states. By Gelfand's formula, \(\|T_\epsilon^m\|_\infty^{1/m} \rightarrow \lambda(T_\epsilon)\), and since \(|\mathcal{A}_k|^{w-1}\) and \((k+1)^{w-1}\) do not depend on \(n\), we obtain \(\frac{1}{n}\log_2 |\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{w-GC}}| \rightarrow \log_2 \lambda(T_\epsilon)\).
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Because the type-classes of the allowed types in \(\mathcal{P}_\epsilon\) are mutually disjoint, the total number of valid extensions \(N(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}})\) is exactly the sum of the extensions belonging to each valid type, \(N(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}) = \sum_{\mathbf{t} \in \mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}} N_\mathbf{t}(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}})\).

To evaluate the extensions for a specific type \(\mathbf{t}\), observe that the prefix \(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}\) of length \(\ell\) has already consumed \(c_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}})\) symbols from each bin \(\mathcal{B}_i\). Therefore, completing a sequence of exactly type \(\mathbf{t}\) requires filling the remaining \(n - \ell\) positions such that exactly \(t_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}) = t_i - c_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}})\) positions are assigned to bin \(\mathcal{B}_i\) for every \(i \in \{0, \ldots, k\}\).

If \(t_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}}) \leq 0\) for any bin, the prefix has already exceeded the allowed count for that type, yielding $0$ valid extensions. Otherwise, applying the combinatorial logic established in Proposition~\ref{prop:class_cardinality} 
                          
to the remaining \(n - \ell\) positions yields the product of the multinomial partition coefficient \(\frac{(n-\ell)!}{\prod_{i=0}^{k} t_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}})!}\) and the independent symbol combinations \(\prod_{i=0}^{k} |\mathcal{B}_i|^{t_i(\Tilde{\mathbf{S}})}\).
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:det_capacity}
Let \(k \geq 2\), \(\alpha \leq \beta\), and let \(m_k = |\mathcal{A}_k| - 2(k+1)\) denote the number of mixed symbols, i.e., symbols in \(\mathcal{B}_1, \dots, \mathcal{B}_{k-1}\). With \(p_m = 1 - p_0 - p_k\), define
\begin{align}
G(p_0, p_k) = H(p_0, p_k, p_m) &+ (p_0 + p_k) \log_2 (k+1)
\nonumber \\
&+ p_m \log_2 m_k.
\label{eq:def_G_deterministic}
\end{align}
For \(\epsilon = 0\), the capacity is
\begin{align}
C_{\text{global-GC}}(0) = \max_{p_0 \geq 1-\beta, \; p_k \geq \alpha, \; p_0 + p_k \leq 1} G(p_0, p_k).
\end{align}
If \(\frac{\alpha}{k+1} \geq \frac{\beta - \alpha}{m_k}\) and \(\frac{1-\beta}{k+1} \geq \frac{\beta - \alpha}{m_k}\), the maximum is attained at the boundary, i.e., \(C_{\text{global-GC}}(0) = G(1-\beta, \alpha)\).
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Since every symbol in \(\mathcal{B}_k\) always and every symbol in \(\mathcal{B}_1, \dots, \mathcal{B}_{k-1}\) possibly manifests as G or C, the smallest and largest values of \(Y_\mathbf{t}\) with nonzero probability are \(t_k\) and \(n - t_0\). Hence, \(\mathcal{P}_0 = \{\mathbf{t} : t_k \geq n\alpha, \; t_0 \geq n(1-\beta)\}\), in accordance with Definition~\ref{def:det_gc}. The constraint does not distinguish between the bins \(\mathcal{B}_1, \dots, \mathcal{B}_{k-1}\), so for a fixed total mass \(1 - p_0 - p_k\) on these bins, \(F\) is maximized by the uniform distribution over all \(m_k\) mixed symbols. Together with \(|\mathcal{B}_0| = |\mathcal{B}_k| = k+1\) and the continuity of \(F\), Theorem~\ref{prop:global_capacity} yields~(\ref{eq:def_G_deterministic}). The objective is the entropy of a distribution over \(\mathcal{A}_k\) that is uniform within the pure A/T, pure G/C, and mixed symbols, and is therefore concave. At \(p_0 = 1-\beta\), \(p_k = \alpha\), the stated conditions require the per-symbol probability of both constrained groups to be at least that of the mixed symbols, which is exactly the Karush--Kuhn--Tucker condition for both constraints to be active.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
Under the deterministic baseline (\(\epsilon = 0\)), intermediate probabilities are irrelevant: a window is admissible if and only if it contains at least \(w\alpha\) pure G/C symbols and at most \(w\beta\) symbols that are not pure A/T. It is therefore sufficient to partition the composite alphabet into three bins: pure A/T \(\mathcal{B}_{\text{AT}} = \mathcal{B}_0\), pure G/C \(\mathcal{B}_{\text{GC}} = \mathcal{B}_k\), and mixed \(\mathcal{B}_{\text{mix}} = \bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1} \mathcal{B}_i\) with \(|\mathcal{B}_{\text{mix}}| = m_k\) as in Corollary~\ref{cor:det_capacity}. The state space reduces to \(3^{w-1}\) states, and \(C_{\mathrm{w-GC}}(0) = \log_2 \lambda(T_0)\), where \(T_0\) is the weighted transition matrix over this 3-bin partition.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- example-2: body_md -->
Figure~\ref{fig:fsm_transition} illustrates the state transitions for a window of length \(w=3\) using an alphabet partitioned into three bins. Because \(w=3\), each state is defined by a length-\(2\) sequence of bins (representing the \(w-1\) overlap). If the system is currently in state \((b_1, b_1)\), appending a new bin creates a temporary length-\(3\) window. The transition is permitted if and only if this resulting window satisfies the \(\epsilon\)-probabilistic GC bounds. Assume that appending \(b_1\) or \(b_3\) violates the constraint, while appending \(b_2\) forms a valid window. Consequently, the oldest bin is discarded, and the system successfully transitions to the new state \((b_1, b_2)\).

To illustrate the step-by-step mechanics of Algorithm~\ref{alg:encoder}, consider a simplified scenario where not all possible compositions are present and the \(\epsilon\)-probabilistic constraint allows exactly two valid composition types for a strand of length \(n=3\): 
\(\mathcal{P}_\epsilon = \{\mathbf{t}_1, \mathbf{t}_2\}\), where \(\mathbf{t}_1 = (1,1,1)\) and \(\mathbf{t}_2 = (2,1,0)\). 


As shown in Figure \ref{fig:worked_example}, the composite alphabet is partitioned into three probability bins (\( \mathcal{B}_0, \mathcal{B}_1, \mathcal{B}_2\)) 
containing 2, 2, and 3 symbols, respectively. Note that this is a toy example: the bin sizes are chosen for illustration and do not correspond to a full composite alphabet \(\mathcal{A}_k\), for which \(|\mathcal{B}_i| = (i+1)(k-i+1)\) would hold.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\columnwidth]{figures/method2_example_2_H_alt.png}
    \caption{A numerical trace of the multi-type arithmetic encoder for a target fraction \(d = 0.1875\).}
    \label{fig:worked_example}
\end{figure}



By Proposition~\ref{prop:class_cardinality}, we evaluate the cardinality of each allowed type-class:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \(|\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{t}_1}| = \frac{3!}{1!1!1!} (2^1 \cdot 2^1 \cdot 3^1) = 72\) sequences.
    \item \(|\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{t}_2}| = \frac{3!}{2!1!0!} (2^2 \cdot 2^1 \cdot 3^0) = 24\) sequences.
\end{itemize}
The total valid codebook size is \(N(\emptyset) = 72 + 24 = 96\) valid sequences. Thus, the encoder can map \(m = \lfloor \log_2(96) \rfloor = 6\) bits. 

Suppose we wish to encode the binary message \(\mathbf{b} = 001100\). The target fraction is computed as \(d = \operatorname{NBC}(001100) / 2^6 = 12/64 = 18/96 = 0.1875\). 



The encoder initializes the interval \([0,1)\) and evaluates the candidate symbols for the first position. For candidate \(a_1 \in \mathcal{B}_0\), we must compute the unified prefix-completion count \(N(a_1)\) by summing the continuations from both types via Proposition~\ref{prop:prefix_count}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For \(\mathbf{t}_1\), the remaining requirement is \((0,1,1)\). The continuations are \(N_{\mathbf{t}_1}(a_1) = \frac{2!}{0!1!1!} (2^0 \cdot 2^1 \cdot 3^1) = 12\).
    \item For \(\mathbf{t}_2\), the remaining requirement is \((1,1,0)\). The continuations are \(N_{\mathbf{t}_2}(a_1) = \frac{2!}{1!1!0!} (2^1 \cdot 2^1 \cdot 3^0) = 8\).
\end{itemize}
Thus, the total valid continuations are \(N(a_1) = 12 + 8 = 20\). 

The corresponding interval width for \(a_1\) is proportional to this sum: \(P(a_1|\emptyset) = 20/96\). Because our target fraction \(d = 18/96\) falls within the sub-interval \([0, 20/96)\), the encoder successfully selects \(a_1\). This process recurses, dynamically pruning types when their requirements drop below zero, until the final length-$3$ codeword is isolated.
