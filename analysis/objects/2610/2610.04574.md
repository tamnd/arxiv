Bold capitals denote matrices, and sans-serif capitals denote local box or
coordinate index sets.  For index sets $\mathsf{I},\mathsf{J}$, $\mathbf{A}_{\mathsf{I},\mathsf{J}}$
denotes the submatrix of $\mathbf{A}$ with rows in $\mathsf{I}$
and columns in $\mathsf{J}$. We write $\left\lVert \cdot \right\rVert$ for the Euclidean norm of vectors,
and $\left\lVert \cdot \right\rVert_{2}$ and $\left\lVert \cdot \right\rVert_{F}$ for the spectral
and Frobenius norms of matrices, respectively. We use 
$\operatorname{range}(\mathbf{M})$, $\operatorname{null}(\mathbf{M})$, and $\mathbf{M}^\dagger$
for the column space, nullspace, and Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse of
$\mathbf{M}$.

All Gaussian test matrices introduced below have independent entries.
Over $\mathbb{R}$, the entries are standard real Gaussians; over $\mathbb{C}$, each entry
is $(g_1+\mathrm{i}g_2)/\sqrt{2}$, where $g_1$ and $g_2$ are independent
standard real Gaussians.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:one-level-recovery}
Suppose
\(
    s\geq\max_\tau (n_\tau+k_\tau+p).
\)
Form the Gaussian sketches
\[
    \mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Omega},
    \qquad
    \mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{A}^*\mathbf{\Psi}
\]
using $s$ applications of each operator. Using the same sketch pair, recover the box bases and neighboring
$\mathsf{R}$-containing coefficients. Let
$\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathcal M$ denote the approximation obtained
by completing these recovered coefficients with the exact
$\mathsf{SS}$ block in the recovered coordinates. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:one-level-quasioptimality}
    \mathbb{E}\left\lVert \mathbf{A}-\widetilde{\mathbf{A}} \right\rVert_{F}
    \leq
    \sqrt{2}
    \left(\max_\tau\beta_\tau\right)
    \operatorname{opt}(\mathbf{A}),
\end{equation}
where $\beta_\tau:= \alpha_{k_\tau,p} \sqrt{1+{n_\tau}/{(k_\tau+p-1)}}$ and $\alpha_{k,p}$ is the Gaussian range-recovery factor from
\cref{eq:appendix-range-alpha}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:orthogonality-levels}
The terminal component $\mathbf{W}_{\ell_0}$ and the exact level contributions
$\mathbf{D}_\ell$ are mutually orthogonal in the Frobenius inner product.
The same cross-level orthogonality holds for levelwise retained or recovered
contributions having the same incoming coordinate spaces and zero
$\mathsf{SS}$ block.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
At coarser levels, the carried test blocks are obtained by
applying $\mathcal V_\sigma^*$ and $\mathcal U_\sigma^*$
to the original Gaussian test blocks $\mathbf{\Omega}_\sigma$
and $\mathbf{\Psi}_\sigma$, respectively.
These maps have orthonormal rows but depend on the original
tests, so the usual Gaussian invariance argument does not apply.
We therefore check the numerical row rank of each local test
matrix when computing its pseudoinverse. All checks passed
in our experiments. If a rank deficiency is detected, one can
resketch the affected level and resume carried recovery.
\label{remark:carried_rank}

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:geometry-query-counts}
For a quadtree ($d=2$) or octree ($d=3$), assume leaf size
at most $2^d k$ and $2{:}1$ balance between adjacent leaves.
Because the number of near neighbors in $\mathcal B_\ell$ is bounded by $3^d$,
one may take $c_{\rm {geom}}=6^d+1$.
Actual neighborhood sizes can give smaller sample widths,
particularly for lower-dimensional point sets. The wiggly-torus experiment in
\cref{subsec:exp-wtorus} uses approximately $120k+p$ samples rather than the
three-dimensional bound $217k+p$.  For weak admissibility on a binary tree with leaf size
at most $2k$, one may take $c_{\mathrm{geom}}=3$,
without a balance assumption.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:resketched-quasioptimality}
Suppose $p\geq2$.
The resketched algorithm of \Cref{subsec:resketched-recovery} produces an approximation
$\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:resketched-error-bound}
    \mathbb{E}\left\lVert \mathbf{A}-\widetilde{\mathbf{A}} \right\rVert_{F}
    \leq
    \sqrt{2(L-\ell_0+1)}\,\beta\,\mathrm{OPT}.
\end{equation}
The numbers of queries to $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{A}^*$ are given in
\Cref{subsec:recovery-complexity}.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:weak-admissibility-recovery}
Consider a binary tree.  Take $\ell_0=1$ and
$\mathcal{N}_\ell(\tau)=\{\tau\}$, and assume $L=O(\log(N/k))$.  In the
uniform-rank case, leaf dimensions at most $2k$ give
\[
    \mathbb{E}\left\lVert \mathbf{A}-\widetilde{\mathbf{A}} \right\rVert_{F}
    \leq
    \sqrt{2L}\,\alpha_{k,p}
    \sqrt{1+{2k}/{(k+p-1)}}\,\mathrm{OPT}.
\]
Here $\mathrm{OPT}$ uses the corresponding weak-admissibility representation.
The query counts are at most $L(3k+p)+2k$ for $\mathbf{A}$ and $L(3k+p)$ for
$\mathbf{A}^*$.
With $p=O(k)$, both counts are $O(k\log(N/k))$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Suppose the rank is at most $k$. Set $p-1=\gamma k$ with fixed $\gamma>0$. 
Then $\alpha_{k,p}$ is bounded
independently of $k$, and
\[
    \beta\leq
    \alpha_{k,p}\sqrt{1+(c_{\rm geom}-1)/{(1+\gamma)}}.
\]
For fixed $c_{\mathrm{geom}}$ and $\gamma$, $\beta$ is
bounded independently of $N$ and $k$.
For trees of logarithmic depth, the factor multiplying $\mathrm{OPT}$
in \cref{eq:resketched-error-bound} is therefore
$O(\sqrt{\log(N/k)})$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:gaussian-range-estimate}
Let $\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}$, let $\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times n}$, and let
$1\leq k\leq m$ and $p\geq2$.  Draw a standard Gaussian matrix
$\mathbf{\Omega}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times(k+p)}$, and let $\mathbf{Q}$ contain the top $k$
left singular vectors of $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Omega}$.  Define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:appendix-range-alpha}
    \alpha_{k,p}^2
    :=
    1+
    \left(
        \sqrt{
            p\min\left\{
                {e^2(k+p)}/{(p^2-1)},
                {k}/{(p-1)}
            \right\}
        }
        +\sqrt{{k}/{(p-1)}}
    \right)^2.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:appendix-gaussian-range}
    \mathbb{E}\left\lVert (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^*)\mathbf{A} \right\rVert_{F}^2
    \leq
    \alpha_{k,p}^2
    \min_{\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{M})\leq k}\left\lVert \mathbf{A}-\mathbf{M} \right\rVert_{F}^2.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
If $n\leq k$, then
$\operatorname{range}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Omega})=\operatorname{range}(\mathbf{A})$ with
probability one, and
both sides of \cref{eq:appendix-gaussian-range} vanish.
Now assume $k<n$.  Let $\mathbf{A}_1=\mathbf{U}_1\mathbf{\Sigma}_1\mathbf{V}_1^*$ be a
best rank-$k$ approximation to $\mathbf{A}$, and let
$\mathbf{A}_2=\mathbf{U}_2\mathbf{\Sigma}_2\mathbf{V}_2^*=\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}_1$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:appendix-range-split}
    \begin{aligned}
        \left\lVert (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^*)\mathbf{A} \right\rVert_{F}^2
        &=
        \left\lVert (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^*)\mathbf{A}_1 \right\rVert_{F}^2
        +\left\lVert (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^*)\mathbf{A}_2 \right\rVert_{F}^2\\
        &\leq
        \left\lVert (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^*)\mathbf{A}_1 \right\rVert_{F}^2
        +\left\lVert \mathbf{\Sigma}_2 \right\rVert_{F}^2.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

It remains to bound the first term in
\cref{eq:appendix-range-split}, which measures the part of
$\mathbf{A}_1$ missed by the recovered basis. Set $\mathbf{\Omega}_i=\mathbf{V}_i^*\mathbf{\Omega}$ for $i=1,2$.  These are independent
standard Gaussian matrices, and
$\mathbf{\Omega}_1\mathbf{\Omega}_1^\dagger=\mathbf{I}$ with probability one.
Right-multiplying the identity
$\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Omega}
=\mathbf{U}_1\mathbf{\Sigma}_1\mathbf{\Omega}_1
+\mathbf{U}_2\mathbf{\Sigma}_2\mathbf{\Omega}_2$ by
$\mathbf{\Omega}_1^\dagger\mathbf{V}_1^*$ and rearranging gives
\[
        \mathbf{A}_1=
        \mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Omega}\mathbf{\Omega}_1^\dagger\mathbf{V}_1^*
        -\mathbf{U}_2\mathbf{\Sigma}_2\mathbf{\Omega}_2
        \mathbf{\Omega}_1^\dagger\mathbf{V}_1^*.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:appendix-range-two-terms}
    \left\lVert (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^*)\mathbf{A}_1 \right\rVert_{F}
    \leq
    \left\lVert (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^*)\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Omega} \right\rVert_{F}
    \left\lVert \mathbf{\Omega}_1^\dagger \right\rVert_{2}
    +\left\lVert \mathbf{\Sigma}_2\mathbf{\Omega}_2\mathbf{\Omega}_1^\dagger \right\rVert_{F}.
\end{equation}

Let $\mathbf{\Pi}=\mathbf{\Omega}_1^\dagger\mathbf{\Omega}_1$.  This is the orthogonal
projector onto $\operatorname{range}(\mathbf{\Omega}_1^*)$ and has rank $k$.
Since $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Omega}\mathbf{\Pi}$ has rank at most $k$,
the truncated SVD $\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^*\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Omega}$
has no larger Frobenius approximation error than this competitor.
Additionally, $\mathbf{\Omega}_1(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{\Pi})=\mathbf{0}$, and the competitor's
residual contains only trailing components. This gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:appendix-rank-p-competitor}
    \left\lVert (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^*)\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Omega} \right\rVert_{F}
    \leq
    \left\lVert \mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Omega}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{\Pi}) \right\rVert_{F}
    =
    \left\lVert \mathbf{\Sigma}_2\mathbf{\Omega}_2(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{\Pi}) \right\rVert_{F}.
\end{equation}
Since $\mathbf{\Omega}_2$ is independent of $\mathbf{\Omega}_1$, we can apply the fixed-matrix
Gaussian identity \cite[Proposition~A.1]{halko2011finding}. Additionally, the matrix $(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{\Pi})$ has rank $p$.
\[
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{\Omega}_2}
        \left\lVert \mathbf{\Sigma}_2\mathbf{\Omega}_2(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{\Pi}) \right\rVert_{F}^2
        &=
        \left\lVert \mathbf{\Sigma}_2 \right\rVert_{F}^2\left\lVert \mathbf{I}-\mathbf{\Pi} \right\rVert_{F}^2
        =p\left\lVert \mathbf{\Sigma}_2 \right\rVert_{F}^2,\\
        \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{\Omega}_2}
        \left\lVert \mathbf{\Sigma}_2\mathbf{\Omega}_2\mathbf{\Omega}_1^\dagger \right\rVert_{F}^2
        &=
        \left\lVert \mathbf{\Sigma}_2 \right\rVert_{F}^2
        \left\lVert \mathbf{\Omega}_1^\dagger \right\rVert_{F}^2.
    \end{aligned}
\]
The pseudoinverse of a Gaussian matrix has the following bounds
\[
 \mathbb{E}\left\lVert \mathbf{\Omega}_1^\dagger \right\rVert_{F}^2\leq k/(p-1), \qquad  \mathbb{E}\left\lVert \mathbf{\Omega}_1^\dagger \right\rVert_{2}^2
    \leq {e^2(k+p)}/{(p^2-1)},
\]
where the Frobenius norm bound is in \cite[Proposition~A.6]{halko2011finding}, and
the spectral norm bound is in \cite[Lemma~3.1]{nakatsukasa2020fast} for $k\geq2$.
For $k=1$, the spectral norm bound is true as well, since the spectral and Frobenius norms coincide.
Using Minkowski's inequality in $L^2$, applied to
\cref{eq:appendix-range-two-terms,eq:appendix-rank-p-competitor} and combined
with \cref{eq:appendix-range-split}, gives the result.

To compare recovery with an arbitrary admissible approximation,
fix $\overline{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathcal M$ and, for each box $\tau$, set
\[
\eta_{\tau,\mathrm{row}}
:=\left\lVert \mathbf{A}_{\mathsf{B},\mathsf{F}}
           -\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathsf{B},\mathsf{F}} \right\rVert_{F},\qquad
\eta_{\tau,\mathrm{col}}
:=\left\lVert \mathbf{A}_{\mathsf{F},\mathsf{B}}
           -\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathsf{F},\mathsf{B}} \right\rVert_{F}.
\]
The corresponding far-field blocks of $\overline{\mathbf{A}}$
have rank at most $k_\tau$.

Using unitary invariance, we work with transformed coordinates
\(\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{U}^*\mathbf{A}\mathbf{V}\).
Fix a box $\tau$ and consider
$
    [\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{B}}\quad
     \mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{N}}\quad
     \mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{F}}].
$
The first two blocks are recovered by block extraction, while
\(\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{F}}\) is discarded. 
Let $\mathbf{G}:=\mathbf{\Omega}_{\mathsf{B}\cup\mathsf{N}}$ and
$\widehat{\mathbf{G}}:=\widehat{\mathbf{\Omega}}_{\mathsf{B}\cup\mathsf{N}}$.
Let $\mathbf{H}_1$ contain the $k_\tau+p$ orthonormal nullspace
vectors of $\mathbf{G}$ used in block nullification, and let
$\mathbf{H}_2$ have orthonormal columns spanning
$\operatorname{range}(\mathbf{G}^*)$.
Conditional on $\mathbf{G}$ and $\mathbf{V}$,
\[
    \mathbf{\Omega}_{\mathsf{F},1}
    :=\mathbf{\Omega}_{\mathsf{F}}\mathbf{H}_1,
    \qquad
    \mathbf{\Omega}_{\mathsf{F},2}
    :=\mathbf{\Omega}_{\mathsf{F}}\mathbf{H}_2
\]
are independent standard Gaussian matrices.
Block nullification uses $\mathbf{\Omega}_{\mathsf{F},1}$ as its
test matrix. By \cref{lem:gaussian-range-estimate},
the discarded far-field block satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[
  \left\lVert \mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{F}} \right\rVert_{F}^2
  \mid \mathbf{G},\mathbf{V}
\right]\leq \alpha_{k_\tau,p}^2 \eta_{\tau,\rm {row}}^2.
\label{eq:error_tau_row} 
\end{equation}

It remains to account for the error introduced by block extraction. By \cref{eq:one-level-block-extraction},
\[ [\widetilde{\mathbf{C}}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{B}}\quad \widetilde{\mathbf{C}}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{N}}] - [\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{B}}\quad \mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{N}}] = \mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{F}} \widehat{\mathbf{\Omega}}_{\mathsf{F}} \widehat{\mathbf{G}}^\dagger. \] 
Since $\operatorname{range}(\mathbf{G}^\dagger)=\operatorname{range}(\mathbf{G}^*)=\operatorname{range}(\mathbf{H}_2)$, \[ \widehat{\mathbf{\Omega}}_{\mathsf{F}}\widehat{\mathbf{G}}^\dagger = \mathbf{V}_{\mathsf{F}}^* \mathbf{\Omega}_{\mathsf{F}}\mathbf{H}_2 \mathbf{H}_2^*\mathbf{G}^\dagger \mathbf{V}_{\mathsf{B}\cup\mathsf{N}}. \]
With $\mathbf{G}$, $\mathbf{V}$, and $\mathbf{\Omega}_{\mathsf{F},1}$ fixed,
$\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{F}}$ is fixed, and the remaining randomness in the
extraction term enters only through the independent Gaussian matrix
$\mathbf{\Omega}_{\mathsf{F},2}$. We take expectations first over $\mathbf{\Omega}_{\mathsf{F},2}$, then apply the conditional range estimate and take expectations over $\mathbf{G}, \mathbf{V}$. The  identities
\cite[Propositions~A.1 and A.6]{halko2011finding} give
 \[ \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \left\lVert \mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{F}} \right\rVert_{F}^2 + \left\lVert  \mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{R},\mathsf{F}} \widehat{\mathbf{\Omega}}_{\mathsf{F}} \widehat{\mathbf{G}}^\dagger  \right\rVert_{F}^2 \right] \leq \beta_\tau^2\eta_{\tau,\mathrm{row}}^2. \] 
 The adjoint argument gives the bound $\beta_\tau^2\eta_{\tau,\mathrm{col}}^2$ for the residual block column.

Using the forward estimate on overlapping $\mathsf{RR}$ blocks, every error
block is controlled by at least one of the row or column estimates.  Summing
these estimates counts each corresponding competitor block at most twice; therefore
\[ \mathbb{E}\left\lVert  \mathbf{A}- \widetilde{\mathbf{A}} \right\rVert_{F}^2 \leq 2 \left( \max_{\tau}\beta_\tau^2 \right) \left\lVert  \mathbf{A} -\overline{\mathbf{A}}  \right\rVert_{F}^2. \] Taking the infimum over all \(\overline{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathcal M\), then taking the square root, and applying Jensen's inequality gives the result.

By nestedness, the coarser contribution $\mathbf{D}_{\ell'}$ is supported
entirely in the $\mathsf{S}_{\ell}, \mathsf{S}_{\ell}$ block when expressed

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{D}_{\ell'}
&=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathcal U_{\mathsf{S}_\ell} & \mathcal U_{\mathsf{R}_\ell}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{B} & \mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathcal V_{\mathsf{S}_\ell} & \mathcal V_{\mathsf{R}_\ell}
\end{bmatrix}^{*},
\\
\mathbf{D}_{\ell}
&=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathcal U_{\mathsf{S}_\ell} & \mathcal U_{\mathsf{R}_\ell}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{S}_\ell,\mathsf{R}_\ell}\\

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Fix a level $\ell$, and condition on all test matrices drawn at finer levels.
Then the current core matrix $\mathbf{C}:=\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{S}_{\ell+1},\mathsf{S}_{\ell+1}}$
 is fixed.
Resketched recovery uses
$\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}=\mathbf{C}\widehat{\mathbf{\Omega}}$ and
$\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}=\mathbf{C}^*\widehat{\mathbf{\Psi}}$
with fresh independent Gaussian test matrices.
The one-level procedure produces completed transforms
$\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}$ and neighboring $\mathsf{SR}$, $\mathsf{RS}$,
and $\mathsf{RR}$ coefficients, which assemble the
physical-coordinate contribution $\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_\ell$.
Completing the recovered coefficients with the exact
$\mathsf{SS}$ block, as in \Cref{lem:one-level-recovery}, gives
\[
\mathbf{E}_\ell = \mathbf{D}_{\ell} - \widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{\ell}
=
\mathcal U_{\mathsf{S}_{\ell+1}}
\left(
\mathbf{C}-\mathbf{U}
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{S}_\ell,\mathsf{S}_\ell}
&
\widetilde{\mathbf{C}}_{\mathsf{S}_\ell,\mathsf{R}_\ell}
\\
\widetilde{\mathbf{C}}_{\mathsf{R}_\ell,\mathsf{S}_\ell}
&
\widetilde{\mathbf{C}}_{\mathsf{R}_\ell,\mathsf{R}_\ell}
\end{bmatrix}
\mathbf{V}^*
\right)
\mathcal V_{\mathsf{S}_{\ell+1}}^*,
\]
where the recovered blocks vanish on nonneighboring pairs.
Let $\operatorname{opt}_\ell:=\operatorname{opt}(\mathbf{C})$
as defined in \eqref{eq:one-level-opt} for the current
partition, with prescribed ranks on $\mathcal T_{\ell}$ and coarser leaves in $\mathcal B_{\ell} \setminus \mathcal T_{\ell}$ left uncompressed.
Since the surviving coordinate maps have orthonormal columns, the squared estimate in the proof of \Cref{lem:one-level-recovery} gives
\[
\mathbb E\ \left\lVert \mathbf{E}_\ell \right\rVert_{F}^2
\le 2\beta^2\operatorname{opt}_\ell^2.
\]

For any $\overline{\mathbf{A}}\in
\mathcal H^2$, set
$\overline{\mathbf{C}}:=
\mathcal U_{\mathsf{S}_{\ell+1}}^*
\overline{\mathbf{A}}
\mathcal V_{\mathsf{S}_{\ell+1}}$.
Because these transforms act boxwise, they preserve neighbor
support and rank bounds on each far-field block
row and column, so $\overline{\mathbf{C}}$
is an admissible one-level competitor.
Therefore,
\[
\operatorname{opt}_\ell
\le \left\lVert \mathbf{C}-\overline{\mathbf{C}} \right\rVert_{F}
\le \left\lVert \mathbf{A}-\overline{\mathbf{A}} \right\rVert_{F},
\]
and taking the infimum gives
$\operatorname{opt}_\ell\le\mathrm{OPT}$.
Orthogonality holds for every realization, and the terminal
block $\mathbf{C}_{\mathsf{S}_{\ell_0},\mathsf{S}_{\ell_0}}$
is recovered exactly. Taking expectations over all test
matrices therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E\left\lVert \mathbf{A}-\widetilde{\mathbf{A}} \right\rVert_{F}^2
=
\textstyle{\sum}_{\ell=\ell_0}^{L}\ \mathbb E\left\lVert \mathbf{E}_\ell \right\rVert_{F}^2
\le 2(L-\ell_0+1)\beta^2\mathrm{OPT}^2.
\]
Applying Jensen's inequality proves the claim.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Fix the box bases of bounded rank, and let $\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}$ denote the
approximation in \eqref{eq:levelwise-expansion}.
The regrouping below uses only the near-neighbor sparsity
pattern of the retained level contributions and the identity
$\mathbf{D}_\ell=\mathbf{W}_{\ell+1}-\mathbf{W}_\ell$. 
No orthogonality or nestedness of the box bases is used
in this regrouping.

Because the coordinate maps act boxwise, restriction to
neighboring coefficient blocks commutes with their embedding
in physical coordinates. Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:h-matrix-telescoping-form}
    \widetilde{\mathbf{A}}
    =
    \mathbf{W}_{\ell_0}
    +\displaystyle{\sum}_{\ell=\ell_0}^{L}\ 
        \left[\mathbf{D}_\ell\right]_{\mathcal N_\ell}.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal N_\ell$ denotes the full near-neighbor mask
on $\mathcal B_\ell$, identified with the corresponding
physical matrix entries. Pairs of coarser leaves contribute
zero, since $\mathbf{W}_{\ell+1}$ and $\mathbf{W}_\ell$ both equal
$\mathbf{A}$ on those blocks.

To compare this telescoping representation with the conventional
representation, we identify the level at which each pair
first becomes well separated. The near masks are nested, 
$
    \mathcal N_L
    \subseteq
    \mathcal N_{L-1}
    \subseteq\cdots\subseteq
    \mathcal N_{\ell_0},
$
since descendants of a well-separated pair remain well separated.  
For $\ell>\ell_0$, $\mathcal I_\ell$ consists of the pairs
that are still near on the preceding level but become
well separated on level $\ell$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:interaction_list}
    \mathcal I_\ell
    :=
    \mathcal N_{\ell-1}\setminus\mathcal N_\ell\quad \text{for}\ \ell_0<\ell\leq L
    ,\quad \text{and}\quad
    \mathcal I_{\ell_0}
    :=
    (\mathcal N_{\ell_0})^c.
\end{equation}
The masks $\mathcal I_{\ell_0},\ldots,\mathcal I_L,\mathcal N_L$ 
form a disjoint partition of the physical matrix entries.

With the same box bases, the conventional approximation
retains $\mathbf{A}$ on neighboring leaf pairs and uses
$\mathbf{W}_\ell$ on pairs first separated at level $\ell$.
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:h-matrix-interaction-form}
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathcal H}:=
\displaystyle{\sum}_{\ell=\ell_0}^{L}\ 
\left[\mathbf{W}_\ell\right]_{\mathcal I_\ell} + \left[\mathbf{A}\right]_{\mathcal N_L}.
\end{equation}
On the 2:1-balanced trees used here, this is the standard U-, V-, W-, and
X-list organization \cite[Sec.~3.3.1]{ying2004kernel}: the U-list contains
the retained leaf-neighbor interactions, while the V-, W-, and X-lists
assign each well-separated pair to the first level at which it becomes
admissible.

To show that $\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal H}=\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}$,
we regroup this interaction-list expression into the
telescoping form above. Expanding each interaction mask as a difference of near masks
and collecting terms restricted to the same mask gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    {\mathbf{A}}_{\mathcal H}
    &=
    \mathbf{W}_{\ell_0}
    +
    \left[
        \mathbf{W}_{\ell_0+1}-\mathbf{W}_{\ell_0}\right]_{\mathcal N_{\ell_0}}
    +\cdots 
    +
    \left[
        \mathbf{W}_L-\mathbf{W}_{L-1}\right]_{\mathcal N_{L-1}}
    +
    \left[
        \mathbf{A}-\mathbf{W}_L\right]_{\mathcal N_L}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Using
$\mathbf{D}_\ell=\mathbf{W}_{\ell+1}-\mathbf{W}_\ell$ and 
$\mathbf{W}_{L+1}=\mathbf{A}$,
we conclude that ${\mathbf{A}}_{\mathcal H}= \widetilde{\mathbf{A}}$.
<!-- end proof-5 -->

Only the coarse and fine interactions require both physical and transformed
box vectors, because the coarser leaf remains in physical coordinates.
Same-level interactions and the coarsest coefficient matrix use transformed
coordinates throughout.  To avoid storing both versions of the charge and
potential vectors, the local basis factors can instead be absorbed into the
coarse and fine interaction blocks.
