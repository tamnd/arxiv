<!-- setup-1: body_md -->
\label{setup:standing}
Throughout the paper, $\Bbbk$ denotes a non-trivial
commutative noetherian hereditary ring and $A$ a unital
$\Bbbk$-algebra. We assume
that the small $\Bbbk$-pre-additive category $Q$ satisfies the
following conditions; see \cite[Setup~2.5, Definition~7.3]{HJ22}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] \emph{Hom-finiteness:} Each hom $\Bbbk$-module $Q(p,q)$
is finitely generated and projective.
\item[(2)] \emph{Local boundedness:} For each $q\inQ$, the sets
\[
 N_-(q)=\{p\inQ\midQ(p,q)\ne0\}\quad\text{and}\quad
 N_+(q)=\{p\inQ\midQ(q,p)\ne0\}
\]
are finite.
\item[(3)] \emph{Existence of a Serre functor:} There is a
$\Bbbk$-linear auto-equivalence $\mathbb S:Q\toQ$ and natural
isomorphisms
\[
 Q(p,q)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(Q(q,\mathbb S(p)),\Bbbk).
\]
\item[(4)] \emph{Strong Retraction Property:} For any object $q$ in $Q$, the unit map $\Bbbk\toQ(q,q)$ (via $x\mapsto x\cdot \mathrm{id}_q$) has a $\Bbbk$-module retraction; whence there is a $\Bbbk$-module decomposition  $$Q(q,q)=(\Bbbk\cdot{\rm id}_q)\oplus\mathfrak{r}_q$$ for $q\in Q$ such that
\begin{itemize}
\item  $\mathfrak{r}_q\circ\mathfrak{r}_q\subseteq\mathfrak{r}_q$ for all $q$, and
\item $Q(q,p)\circ Q(p,q)\subseteq\mathfrak{r}_p$ for all $p\neq q$.
\end{itemize}
These submodules define the pseudo-radical ideal $\mathfrak r$ by
$\mathfrak r(q,q)=\mathfrak r_q$ and $\mathfrak r(p,q)=Q(p,q)$ for $p\ne q$.
\item[(5)] \emph{Nilpotence:} There is an integer $d\geq1$ such
that $\mathfrak r^d=0$.
\end{enumerate}
All results are understood under these hypotheses. The additional
assumption that $A$ is left noetherian is imposed in
Section~\ref{sec:compact} and wherever explicitly stated.

The category ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ of $\Bbbk$-linear functors from $Q$ to the
 category ${}_{A}\operatorname{Mod}$ of left $A$-modules is called the \emph{category of $Q$-shaped $A$-modules}.
When $A=\Bbbk$, we write ${}_{Q}\operatorname{Mod}$ instead of ${}_{Q,\Bbbk}\operatorname{Mod}$.
The functor category ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ is a Grothendieck category
with enough projectives.
Following \cite{HJ22}, we write ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Prj}$ and
${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}$ for its full subcategories of projective
and injective objects, respectively.
For left $A$-modules, the corresponding subcategories

We first recall the terminology used in this construction.
Following \cite{Buhler10}, an exact category is an additive
category equipped with a class of kernel--cokernel pairs
satisfying Quillen's axioms.
Such a pair $X\xrightarrow{i}Y\xrightarrow{p}Z$ is called
a conflation; the morphisms $i$ and $p$ are called an
inflation and a deflation, respectively.
An exact category is Frobenius if it has enough projectives
and enough injectives, and these two classes coincide.
Their common objects are called \emph{projective-injective}.
The stable category has the same objects as the Frobenius
category. Its morphism groups are obtained by taking the
quotient modulo morphisms that factor through projective-injective
objects.
It has a canonical triangulated structure, with
distinguished triangles induced by conflations
\cite[Chapter~I, Section~2]{Happel88}.

To define the $Q$-shaped homotopy category introduced by
Slaftsos and Vit\'{o}ria \cite{SV26}, we equip ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ with the
objectwise split exact structure.
Its conflations are the short exact sequences
\[
 0\longrightarrow X'\xrightarrow{u}X
 \xrightarrow{v}X''\longrightarrow0
\]
for which the evaluated sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow X'(q)\xrightarrow{u(q)}X(q)
 \xrightarrow{v(q)}X''(q)\longrightarrow0
\]
splits in ${}_{A}\operatorname{Mod}$ for every $q\inQ$.
These splittings are not required to be natural in $q$,
so the original sequence need not split in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$.
We denote the associated exact category by
${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}$.

Under the standing hypotheses, ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}$
is Frobenius \cite[Example~4.18(c)]{SV26}.
Its projective-injective objects are called contractible; their full subcategory is denoted by $\operatorname{Ctr}_{Q}(A).$ Further characterizations are given in Subsection~\ref{subsec:contractible}.
The stable category is the \emph{$Q$-shaped homotopy category},
denoted by $\mathcal K_{Q}(A)$; see \cite[Definition~6.9]{SV26}.
Thus \[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal K_{Q}(A)}(X,Y)
 =
 \operatorname{Hom}_{Q,A}(X,Y)/
 [\operatorname{Ctr}_{Q}(A)](X,Y),
\]
where $[\operatorname{Ctr}_{Q}(A)](X,Y)$ consists of the
morphisms that factor through a contractible object.
Distinguished triangles in $\mathcal K_{Q}(A)$ are induced, up to isomorphism, by objectwise split
short exact sequences.
By \cite[Construction 5.6 and Proposition 5.7]{HJ24} there are objectwise split sequences
\begin{align*}
 0&\longrightarrow\mathbb K(X)\longrightarrow\mathbb F(X)
 \xrightarrow{\epsilon_X}X\longrightarrow0,\\
 0&\longrightarrow X\xrightarrow{\eta_X}\mathbb G(X)
 \longrightarrow\mathbb C(X)\longrightarrow0,
\end{align*}
where \begin{equation*}
 \mathbb F(X)=\bigoplus_{q\inQ}F_q(X(q))
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathbb G(X)=\prod_{q\inQ}G_q(X(q)).
\end{equation*}
and $\epsilon$ and $\eta$ are the unique natural transformations defined by the universal properties of coproducts and products such that $\epsilon_{q}^{X}$ is the counit of $(F_{q},E_{q})$ at $X$ and $\eta_{q}^{X}$ is the unit of $(E_{q},G_{q})$ at $X$, respectively.
The remaining objects in the sequences are defined by $\mathbb C(X)=\operatorname{Cok} \eta_{X}$ and $\mathbb K(X)=\operatorname{Ker} \epsilon_{X}$.

These sequences above provide enough projectives and
enough injectives for ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}$. They also identify
suspension and inverse suspension in $\mathcal K_{Q}(A)$:
\[
 \Sigma X\cong\mathbb C(X)
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \Sigma^{-1}X\cong\mathbb K(X)
 \quad\text{in }\mathcal K_{Q}(A).
\]

We next recall the abelian model structures on ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$
whose common homotopy category is the $Q$-shaped derived
category $\mathcal D_{Q}(A)$. Their construction uses cotorsion pairs
with respect to the abelian exact structure, for which we
fix the following terminology.

\begin{dfn}\label{dfn:cotorsion}
Let $\mathcal C$ be an exact category, and let
$\operatorname{Ext}^n_{\mathcal C}$ denote the Yoneda extension groups
with respect to its exact structure. For a class
$\mathcal X$ of objects of $\mathcal C$, set
\[
\begin{aligned}
 {}^{\perp_1}\mathcal X
 &=
 \{U\in\mathcal C\mid
   \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal C}(U,X)=0
   \text{ for all }X\in\mathcal X\},\\
 \mathcal X^{\perp_1}
 &=
 \{V\in\mathcal C\mid
   \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal C}(X,V)=0
   \text{ for all }X\in\mathcal X\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
A pair $(\mathcal U,\mathcal V)$ of classes of objects is
a \emph{cotorsion pair} in $\mathcal C$ if
$\mathcal U={}^{\perp_1}\mathcal V$ and
$\mathcal V=\mathcal U^{\perp_1}$.
It is \emph{complete} if every $X\in\mathcal C$ admits
conflations
\[
 0\longrightarrow V_X\longrightarrow U_X
 \longrightarrow X\longrightarrow0
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 0\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow V^X
 \longrightarrow U^X\longrightarrow0
\]
with $U_X,U^X\in\mathcal U$ and
$V_X,V^X\in\mathcal V$.
The morphisms $U_X\longrightarrow X$ and
$X\longrightarrow V^X$ are called a special
$\mathcal U$-precover and a special
$\mathcal V$-preenvelope, respectively.
The pair is \emph{hereditary} if
$\operatorname{Ext}^n_{\mathcal C}(U,V)=0$ for all $n\geq1$,
$U\in\mathcal U$, and $V\in\mathcal V$.
We say that it is \emph{generated by a set}
$\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal C$ if
$\mathcal V=\mathcal S^{\perp_1}$.
\end{dfn}

Following \cite[Definition~4.1]{HJ22}, we define
\[
 \mathscr E=\{X\in{}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}\mid X^\natural
       \text{ has finite projective dimension in }{}_{Q}\operatorname{Mod}\},
\]
where $(-)^\natural$ is the forgetful functor.
Objects of $\mathscr E$ are called \emph{exact}, and they are precisely the objects whose
$Q$-shaped homology, equivalently cohomology, vanishes
in all positive degrees \cite[Theorem~7.1]{HJ22}.
In particular, by the proof of \cite[Theorem D]{HJ24}, one has $X\in\mathscr E$ if and only if $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{Q,A}(S_q(A),X)=0$ for every $q\inQ.$
By \cite[Theorems~4.4 and~6.1]{HJ22}, the class $\mathscr E$ is
thick, and the hereditary Hovey triples
\[
 ({}^{\perp_1}\mathscr E,\mathscr E,{}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod})
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 ({}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod},\mathscr E,\mathscr E^{\perp_1})
\]
define the projective and injective abelian model structures,
respectively. These model structures have the same weak
equivalences \cite[Proposition~6.3]{HJ22}.
Their common homotopy category is the
\emph{$Q$-shaped derived category of $A$}, denoted by $\mathcal D_{Q}(A)$.

\label{prp:contractible}
Let ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}$ denote ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ endowed with the
objectwise split exact structure. For an object
$X\in{}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$, the following statements are equivalent.
\begin{eqc}
\item $X$ is projective-injective in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}$.
\item There exists a family $(M_q)_{q\inQ}$ of left
$A$-modules such that
\[
 X\cong\prod_{q\inQ}G_q(M_q).
\]
\item There is an isomorphism
\[
 X\cong\prod_{q\inQ}G_qK_q(X).
\]
\item There exists a family $(N_q)_{q\inQ}$ of left
$A$-modules such that
\[
 X\cong\bigoplus_{q\inQ}F_q(N_q).
\]
\item There is an isomorphism
\[
 X\cong\bigoplus_{q\inQ}F_qC_q(X).
\]
\item For every $q\inQ$ and every $M\in{}_{A}\operatorname{Mod}$,
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_{{}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}}(S_q(M),X)=0.
\]
\item For every $q\inQ$ and every $M\in{}_{A}\operatorname{Mod}$,
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_{{}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}}(X,S_q(M))=0.
\]
\end{eqc}
<!-- end prp-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By \cite[Remark~4.8, Example~4.18(c)]{SV26}, the exact category
${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}$ is Frobenius. Thus its projective and
injective objects coincide.

Since every $A$-module is injective in the split exact structure,
it follows from \cite[Proposition~4.19]{SV26} that
$\textup{(i)}\Leftrightarrow\textup{(vi)}$, and its proof yields
\[
 X\cong\prod_{q\inQ}G_qK_q(X)
\]
whenever (i) holds. Hence $\textup{(i)}\Rightarrow\textup{(iii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(ii)}$.
Conversely, the adjunction $(E_q,G_q)$ shows that $G_q(M)$
is injective in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}$ for every $A$-module $M$.
Since products of injectives are injective, one has $\textup{(ii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(i)}$.

By \cite[Proposition~3.7 and Lemma~3.4]{HJ24}, one has
\[
 \prod_{q\inQ}G_q(M_q)
 \cong\bigoplus_{q\inQ}G_q(M_q)
 \cong\bigoplus_{q\inQ}F_{\mathbb S^{-1}(q)}(M_q),
\]
so $\textup{(ii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(iv)}$. The adjunction $(F_q,E_q)$ shows
that each $F_q(N)$ is projective in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}$.
Thus $\textup{(iv)}\Rightarrow\textup{(i)}$.
Moreover, by \cite[Lemma~7.28]{HJ22} we have natural isomorphisms
\begin{equation*}
 C_pF_q(M)\cong
 \begin{cases}
 M,&p=q,\\
 0,&p\ne q.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
Since $C_p$ preserves direct sums, the decomposition in (iv)
implies $C_p(X)\cong N_p$, proving $\textup{(iv)}\Rightarrow\textup{(v)}$.
The converse is immediate.

\smallskip
\noindent

The implication $\textup{(i)}\Rightarrow\textup{(vii)}$ follows from projectivity.
Assume (vii). We first show that each canonical quotient
$\pi_q:X(q)\to C_q(X)$ splits. Apply
$\operatorname{Hom}_{Q,A}(-,S_q(M))$ to the canonical objectwise split
exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathbb K(X)\xrightarrow{a}
 \mathbb F(X)\xrightarrow{\epsilon_X}X\longrightarrow0.
\]
By (vii) and the adjunction $(C_q,S_q)$, the morphism
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_A(C_q\mathbb F(X),M)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_A(C_q\mathbb K(X),M)
\]
is surjective for every $M$. Taking $M=C_q\mathbb K(X)$
shows that $C_q(a)$ is a split monomorphism. Right exactness
of $C_q$ and \cite[Lemma~7.28]{HJ22} therefore give a split
exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow C_q\mathbb K(X)\longrightarrow X(q)
 \xrightarrow{\pi_q}C_q(X)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Choose sections $s_q$ of $\pi_q$. By the adjunctions
$(F_q,E_q)$, they induce a morphism
\[
 \psi:Y:=\bigoplus_{q\inQ}F_qC_q(X)\longrightarrow X
\]
such that $C_q(\psi)=\pi_qs_q=\mathrm{Id}_{C_q(X)}$
under the natural identification $C_q(Y)\cong C_q(X)$ supplied by \cite[Lemma~7.28]{HJ22}.
Since $C_{q}$ is right exact for any $q\inQ$, we have that  $C_q(\operatorname{Cok}\psi)=\operatorname{Cok} C_{q}(\psi)=0$ for all $q$.
It follows from \cite[Proposition 7.19]{HJ22} that
 $\psi$ is an epimorphism.

It remains to show that $\operatorname{Ker}\psi=0$. Let $J$ be an injective
cogenerator of ${}_{A}\operatorname{Mod}$.
Every extension of $X$ by $S_q(J)$ is objectwise split.
Consequently, (vii) and the adjunction $(C_q,S_q)$ give
\[
 0=\operatorname{Ext}^1_{{}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}}(X,S_q(J))
  =\operatorname{Ext}^1_{Q,A}(X,S_q(J))
  \cong\operatorname{Hom}_A(L_1C_q(X),J).
\]
Here the last isomorphism follows by applying the adjunction
to a projective resolution of $X$ and using the exactness
of $\operatorname{Hom}_A(-,J)$. Since $J$ is a cogenerator in ${}_{A}\operatorname{Mod}$, one has
$L_1C_q(X)=0$ for every $q$.
Writing $V=\operatorname{Ker}\psi$. Then we have the following sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow C_q(V)\longrightarrow C_q(Y)
 \xrightarrow{C_q(\psi)}C_q(X)\longrightarrow0.
\]
As $C_q(\psi)$ is an isomorphism, $C_q(V)=0$ for every $q$,
and hence $V=0$ by \cite[Proposition 7.19]{HJ22}. Thus $\psi$ is an isomorphism, proving
$\textup{(vii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(v)}$.

under direct summands.
Since $\mathcal K_{Q}(A)$ is the stable category of this Frobenius

$X$ is contractible. This proves $\textup{(iii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(i)}$.

\label{prp:I-Frobenius}
The subcategory $\mathscr I$ is
extension-closed in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$, and the exact structures on $\mathscr I$ induced by the abelian and objectwise split exact structures on ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$
coincide. With this
exact structure, $\mathscr I$ is Frobenius and
\[
 \operatorname{Prj}(\mathscr I)=\operatorname{Inj}(\mathscr I)={}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}.
\]
<!-- end prp-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $0\to X\to Y\to Z\to0$ be a short exact sequence
in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ with $X,Z\in\mathscr I$.
For every $q\inQ$, the evaluated sequence splits
because $X(q)$ is injective. Hence
$Y(q)\cong X(q)\oplus Z(q)$ is injective.
This proves that $\mathscr I$ is extension-closed and that
the two induced exact structures coincide.

For each $X\in\mathscr I$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical-I}
there are conflations
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathbb K(X)
 \longrightarrow\mathbb F(X)
 \longrightarrow X\longrightarrow0
\]
and
\[
 0\longrightarrow X
 \longrightarrow\mathbb G(X)
 \longrightarrow\mathbb C(X)\longrightarrow0
\]
in $\mathscr I$ with $\mathbb F(X),\mathbb G(X)\in{}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}$.
By Proposition~\ref{prp:contractible}, $\mathbb F(X)$ and $\mathbb G(X)$
are projective-injective in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}$.
Thus $\mathscr I$ has enough projectives and enough injectives.

If $P$ is projective in $\mathscr I$, the deflation
$\mathbb F(P)\to P$ splits, so $P$ is a direct summand
of $\mathbb F(P)$.
Similarly, every injective object $J$ of $\mathscr I$ is a
direct summand of $\mathbb G(J)$.
Hence every projective or injective object of $\mathscr I$ belongs
to ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}$ by Lemma \ref{lem:canonical-I}.

Conversely, let $J\in{}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}$.
Then $J\in\mathscr I$.
The canonical monomorphism $J\to\mathbb G(J)$ splits
in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ by injectivity of $J$.
Consequently, $J$ is a direct summand of the projective-injective
object $\mathbb G(J)$ and is therefore projective-injective
in $\mathscr I$.
This proves the asserted equality and shows that $\mathscr I$
is Frobenius.

<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:factor-ideal}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be in $\mathscr I$. Then a morphism $X\to Y$ factors through a contractible object of ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ if and only if it factors through an object of ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}$.
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Since every object of ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}$ is contractible by the proof
of Proposition~\ref{prp:I-Frobenius},  a factorization through
an object of ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}$ is a factorization through a contractible object.
Conversely, suppose that $u:X\to Y$ admits a factorization
$u=ba$ with $a:X\to C$ and $b:C\to Y$ where $C$ is contractible.
Note that there is an inflation $$0\to X\to \mathbb{G}(X)\to \mathbb{C}(X)\to 0$$ in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}$.
Since $C$ is an injective object in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{os}}$ by Proposition \ref{prp:contractible}, there is a morphism
$\widetilde a:\mathbb G(X)\to C$ with
$\widetilde a\eta_X=a$. Therefore $
 u=(b\widetilde a)\eta_X.$
This is the required factorization, since $\mathbb G(X)$ is
injective in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical-I}.

<!-- lem-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:Ext-comparison}
For $X,Y\in\mathscr I$, there are natural isomorphisms
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_{Q,A}(X,Y)
 \cong\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathscr I}(X,Y)
 \cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}(X,\Sigma Y).
\]
<!-- end lem-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The first isomorphism follows because $\mathscr I$ is a full extension-closed subcate-
gory of ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ with the inherited exact structure; see Proposition~\ref{prp:I-Frobenius}.

Consider the conflation $0\to Y\to \mathbb{G}(Y)\xrightarrow{p} \mathbb{C}(Y)\to0$ in $\mathscr I$.
Since $\mathbb{G}(Y)$ is projective-injective in $\mathscr I$, applying $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr I}(X,-)$ gives an exact sequence
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr I}(X,\mathbb{G}(Y))\xrightarrow{p_*}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr I}(X,\mathbb{C}(Y))
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathscr I}(X,Y)\longrightarrow0.
\]
It is straightforward to check that a morphism $X\to \mathbb{C} (Y)$ factors through a projective-injective
object if and only if it factors through $p$ as every
morphism from a projective object to $\mathbb{C}(Y)$ lifts along $p$.
We therefore obtain natural isomorphisms
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\underline{\mathscr I}}(X,\Sigma Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\underline{\mathscr I}}(X,\mathbb{C}(Y))\cong\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathscr I}(X,Y),
\]
as desired.

\label{prp:two-cotorsion}
The pairs $(\mathscr W_{\mathrm{inj}},\mathscr I)$ and $(\mathscr U,\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}})$ are complete hereditary
cotorsion pairs in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$, generated respectively by
the sets
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal S_0
 &=\{F_q(A/J)\mid q\inQ,\ J\text{is a left ideal of }A\}\quad \text{and}\quad
 \mathcal S_1
 &=\mathcal S_0\cup\{S_q(A)\mid q\inQ\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, $\mathscr W_{\mathrm{inj}}\subseteq\mathscr E$ and $\mathscr W_{\mathrm{inj}}\cap\mathscr I={}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}.$
<!-- end prp-7 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Let $X\in{}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$. For every $q\inQ$ and every left
ideal $J$ of $A$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:ext-adjunction} one has
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_{Q,A}(F_q(A/J),X)
 \cong\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(A/J,X(q)).
\]
By Baer's criterion, these groups vanish for all $q$ and $J$
if and only if each $X(q)$ is injective. Thus
$\mathcal S_0^{\perp_1}=\mathscr I$.
Furthermore, the characterization of exact objects established in the proof
of \cite[Theorem~D]{HJ24} gives
\[
 \{S_q(A)\mid q\inQ\}^{\perp_1}=\mathscr E.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \mathcal S_1^{\perp_1}
 =\mathcal S_0^{\perp_1}
   \cap\{S_q(A)\mid q\inQ\}^{\perp_1}
 =\mathscr I\cap\mathscr E=\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}}.
\]
Since ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ is a Grothendieck category with enough
projectives, it follows from \cite[Corollary~2.15(3)]{SS11} that
$(\mathscr W_{\mathrm{inj}},\mathscr I)$ and $(\mathscr U,\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}})$ are complete cotorsion pairs
generated by $\mathcal S_0$ and $\mathcal S_1$, respectively.

By Proposition~\ref{prp:I-Frobenius}, the class $\mathscr I$ is
closed under extensions and contains ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}$.
If
\[
 0\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow Y
 \longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow0
\]
is exact with $X,Y\in\mathscr I$, then it is objectwise split.
Hence each $Z(q)$ is a direct summand of $Y(q)$ and is
therefore injective. Thus $\mathscr I$ is also closed under
cokernels of monomorphisms.
Since $\mathscr E$ is thick and contains ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}$,
the same closure properties hold for $\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}}=\mathscr E\cap\mathscr I$.
It follows that the two cotorsion pairs are hereditary.
The inclusion $\mathscr E^{\perp_1}\subseteq\mathscr I$ and the cotorsion
pair $(\mathscr E,\mathscr E^{\perp_1})$ yield
\[
 \mathscr W_{\mathrm{inj}}={}^{\perp_1}\mathscr I
 \subseteq{}^{\perp_1}(\mathscr E^{\perp_1})
 =\mathscr E.
\]
 Since $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{Q,A}(X,I)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathscr I}(X,I)$
for $X,I\in\mathscr I$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:Ext-comparison},
 the objects of $\mathscr W_{\mathrm{inj}}\cap\mathscr I$ are precisely the
projective objects of the exact category $\mathscr I$.
By Proposition~\ref{prp:I-Frobenius}, we obtain that $\mathscr W_{\mathrm{inj}}\cap\mathscr I=\operatorname{Prj}(\mathscr I)={}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Inj}.$

\label{prp:restricted-cotorsion}
The cotorsion pair $(\mathscr U,\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}})$ in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ restricts
to a complete hereditary and cohereditary cotorsion pair $(\mathscr F_{\mathrm{inj}},\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}})$ in the Frobenius
exact category $\mathscr I$.
<!-- end prp-8 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $X\in\mathscr I$. Since $(\mathscr U,\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}})$ is a complete cotorsion pair by Proposition \ref{prp:two-cotorsion},  we have two exact
sequences in ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$
\begin{equation*}
 0\to Z_X\to F_X\to X\to0
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 0\to X\to Z^X\to F^X\to0,
\end{equation*}
with $Z_X,Z^X\in\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}}$ and $F_X,F^X\in\mathscr U$.
Both sequences are  objectwise split, since $Z_X$ and $X$ are
in $\mathscr I$. Thus $F_X(q)\cong Z_X(q)\oplus X(q)$
and $Z^X(q)\cong X(q)\oplus F^X(q)$ for any $q\in Q.$
It follows that $F_X$ and $F^X$ are in $\mathscr I$,
so both belong to $\mathscr F_{\mathrm{inj}}$. Hence
both short exact sequences are conflations in $\mathscr I$.
Recall that
\[
 \mathscr F_{\mathrm{inj}}=\{F\in\mathscr I\mid
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathscr I}(F,Z)=0\text{ for every }Z\in\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}}\}.
\]
Suppose that $X\in\mathscr I$ satisfies
$\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathscr I}(F,X)=0$ for every $F\in\mathscr F_{\mathrm{inj}}$.
The conflation $0\to X\to Z^X\to F^X\to0$
splits because $F^X\in\mathscr F_{\mathrm{inj}}$. Thus $X$ is a direct summand
of $Z^X\in\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}}$, and hence belongs to $\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}}$.
Together with the two conflations constructed above, this shows that
$(\mathscr F_{\mathrm{inj}},\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}})$ is a complete cotorsion pair in $\mathscr I$.

It remains to prove that $(\mathscr F_{\mathrm{inj}},\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}})$ is hereditary and cohereditary.
For $Z\in\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}}$, we have two conflations in $\mathscr I$
\[
 0\to\mathbb K(Z)\to\mathbb F(Z)\to Z\to0
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 0\to Z\to\mathbb G(Z)\to\mathbb C(Z)\to0
\]
with $\mathbb F(Z)$ and $\mathbb G(Z)$ projective-injective in $\mathscr I$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical-I} and \cite[Theorem 4.4]{HJ22}, we obtain that
$\mathbb K(Z)$  and $\mathbb C(Z)$ belong to $\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}}$.
Moreover, these conflations give $\mathbb K(Z)\cong\Sigma^{-1}Z$ and $\mathbb C(Z)\cong\Sigma Z$ in $\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}).$
Now let $F$ be in $\mathscr F_{\mathrm{inj}}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical-I}, both
$\mathbb K(F)$ and $\mathbb C(F)$ belong to $\mathscr I$.
It follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:Ext-comparison} that
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathscr I}(\mathbb K(F),Z)
 &\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}(\Sigma^{-1}F,\Sigma Z)\\
 &\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}(F,\Sigma\mathbb C(Z))\\
 &\cong\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathscr I}(F,\mathbb C(Z))=0,
\end{align*}
and
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathscr I}(\mathbb C(F),Z)
 &\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}(\Sigma F,\Sigma Z)\\
 &\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}(F,\Sigma\mathbb K(Z))\\
 &\cong\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathscr I}(F,\mathbb K(Z))=0.
\end{align*}
These vanishings hold for every $Z\in\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}}$, so $\mathbb K(F),\mathbb C(F)\in\mathscr F_{\mathrm{inj}}$.
Thus $\mathscr F_{\mathrm{inj}}$ is closed under syzygies and cosyzygies,
proving that $(\mathscr F_{\mathrm{inj}},\mathscr E_{\mathrm{inj}})$ is hereditary and cohereditary.

\cite{Bondarko}
Let $\mathcal{D}$ be a triangulated category. A \emph{$t$-structure} in $\mathcal{D}$ is a pair $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y})$ of full subcategories satisfying the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(X [1],Y)=0$ for all $X\in\mathcal{X}$ and $Y\in\mathcal{Y};$
\item $\mathcal{X}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{X}$ (or $\mathcal{Y}[-1]\subseteq\mathcal{Y}$);
\item For each $M\in \mathcal{D}$, there is a triangle $X[1]\to M\to Y\to X[2]$
with $X\in \mathcal{X}$ and $Y\in \mathcal{Y}.$
\end{enumerate}
A $t$-structure $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})$ is called a \emph{Bousfield localizing pair} if $\mathcal{X}$ is a triangulated subcategory of $\mathcal{D}.$ Such pairs are also called semi-orthogonal decompositions.

\label{dfn:compact-object}
Let $\mathcal A$ be an additive category with arbitrary
set-indexed coproducts. An object $C\in\mathcal A$ is
\emph{compact} if, for every set-indexed family
$\{X_i\}_{i\in I}$ in $\mathcal A$, the canonical map
\[

 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}\left(C,\bigoplus_{i\in I}X_i\right)
\]
is an isomorphism. We denote by $\mathcal A^c$ the full
subcategory of compact objects.

<!-- dfn-7: body_md -->
\label{dfn:compact-generation}
Let $\mathcal T$ be a triangulated category with arbitrary
set-indexed coproducts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
The category $\mathcal T$ is \emph{compactly generated}
if there is a set $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal T^c$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal T}(G,\Sigma^nX)=0
 \quad\text{for all }G\in\mathcal G\text{ and }n\in\mathbb Z
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad X\cong0.
\]
Such a set $\mathcal G$ is called a \emph{set of compact
generators}. We allow shifts in the detection condition;
equivalently, all shifts of $\mathcal G$ together form a generating
set in the unshifted convention of the cited definition.

\item
A full triangulated subcategory $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal T$
is \emph{thick} if it is closed under direct summands in
$\mathcal T$, and \emph{localizing} if it is closed under
arbitrary set-indexed coproducts in $\mathcal T$.
Every localizing subcategory is thick.

\item
For a collection $\mathcal G$ of objects of $\mathcal T$,
write $\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal G)$ and $\operatorname{Loc}(\mathcal G)$ for the
smallest thick and localizing subcategories of $\mathcal T$
containing $\mathcal G$, respectively.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- lem-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:locally-noetherian}
The category ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ is locally noetherian. For
$M\in{}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{eqc}
\item $M$ is noetherian;
\item $M$ is finitely generated;
\item $M$ is finitely presented;
\item $\operatorname{supp} M=\{q\in Q \mid M(q)\neq 0\}$ is finite and each $M(q)$ is finitely generated over $A$.
\end{eqc}
Every such $M$ admits an exact sequence
\begin{equation*}
 0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow M
 \longrightarrow0,
\end{equation*}
where $P$ is a finite direct sum of objects $F_{q}(A)$
and $K$ is finitely generated. Moreover,
$\operatorname{Ext}^1_{Q,A}(M,-)$ preserves coproducts.
<!-- end lem-5 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
For each $q\inQ$, the object $F_{q}(A)$ has finite
support by local boundedness of $Q$. Each value $F_{q}(A)(p)=Q(q,p)\otimes_{\Bbbk}A$
is a finitely generated projective $A$-module, hence
noetherian since $A$ is left noetherian. Then any ascending
chain of subobjects of $F_{q}(A)$ therefore stabilizes at each
object, and finite support ensures that the chain itself
stabilizes. Thus every $F_{q}(A)$ is noetherian. Since $\{F_{q}(A)\}_{q\inQ}$ generate ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ by \cite[Proposition 3.12]{HJ22}, it follows that ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ is locally
noetherian.

Since ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ is locally noetherian, its noetherian,
finitely generated, and finitely presented objects coincide;
see \cite[Chapter~V, \S4]{Stenstrom75}.
Thus \textup{(i)}--\textup{(iii)} are equivalent. Since every finitely generated object is a quotient of a finite direct sum of the objects $F_q(A)$,
it is noetherian and has finite support with finitely generated values. This proves $\textup{(ii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(iv)}$.

Suppose that \textup{(iv)} holds.
Since $M(q)$ is finitely generated for  each $q\in\operatorname{supp} M$,
there is an epimorphism $u_q:A^{m_q}\to M(q)$
with $m_q$ finite. Under the adjunction $(F_q,E_q)$,
these maps induce a morphism
\[
 \phi:\bigoplus_{q\in\operatorname{supp} M}F_{q}(A)^{\oplus m_q}
 \longrightarrow M.
\]
For each $p\in\operatorname{supp} M$, the component $\phi_p$ satisfies $
 \phi_p(\mathrm{id}_p\otimes v)=u_p(v)$,
where $v\in A^{m_p}$ and $\mathrm{id}_p\otimes v$ lies in the summand indexed
by $p$. Thus $\phi_p$ is surjective, and so $\phi$ is an epimorphism.
Since $\operatorname{supp} M$ is finite, $M$ is finitely generated, proving \textup{(ii)}.

Suppose that  $M$ is finitely generated. There is an exact sequence
\begin{equation*}
 0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow M
 \longrightarrow0,
\end{equation*}
 with $P$ a finite direct sum of
the objects $F_q(A)$. Thus $K$ is noetherian, hence finitely
generated.
Thus we have the exact sequence
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{Q,A}(P,-)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{Q,A}(K,-)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^1_{Q,A}(M,-)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since $P$ and $K$ are finitely presented,
both $\operatorname{Hom}_{Q,A}(P,-)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_{Q,A}(K,-)$ preserve coproducts. Consequently, $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{Q,A}(M,-)$ preserves coproducts.

\label{lem:radical-filtration}
Every noetherian object $N\in{}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ has a finite filtration whose factors
are finite direct sums of stalks $S_q(M)$ with $M$ finitely generated
over $A$.
<!-- end lem-7 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Define the subobject $\mathfrak r N$ of $N$ by
\[
 (\mathfrak r N)(q)=\sum_{p\inQ,\,\alpha\in\mathfrak r(p,q)}\operatorname{Im} N(\alpha).
\]
For every morphism $\beta:q\to t$ and every
$\alpha\in\mathfrak r(p,q)$, $\beta\alpha\in\mathfrak r(p,t)$. Hence
\[
 N(\beta)\bigl(\operatorname{Im}N(\alpha)\bigr)
 =\operatorname{Im}N(\beta\alpha)
 \subseteq(\mathfrak rN)(t),
\]
and so
\[
 N(\beta)\bigl((\mathfrak rN)(q)\bigr)
 \subseteq(\mathfrak rN)(t).
\]
It follows that $\mathfrak rN$ is a subobject of $N$.
Iterating and using $\mathfrak r^d=0$ gives the finite filtration
\[
 N\supseteq\mathfrak r N\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak r^dN=0.
\]
Each quotient $N_j=\mathfrak r^jN/\mathfrak r^{j+1}N$
is noetherian. For every $\alpha\in\mathfrak r(p,q)$,
\[
 N(\alpha)\bigl((\mathfrak r^jN)(p)\bigr)
 \subseteq(\mathfrak r^{j+1}N)(q),
\]
so $N_j(\alpha)=0$. Thus $N_j$ factors through
$Q/\mathfrak r$. By the strong retraction property,
\[
 (Q/\mathfrak r)(p,q)\cong
 \begin{cases}
 \Bbbk,&p=q,\\
 0,&p\ne q.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently, the componentwise identity maps give an
isomorphism
\[
 N_j\cong
 \bigoplus_{q\in\operatorname{supp} N_j}S_q(N_j(q)).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:locally-noetherian}, the support of $N_j$
is finite and each $N_j(q)$ is finitely generated over $A$.
Hence every filtration factor has the required form.

By \cite[Proposition~3.2(a)]{HJ24}, we have $\mathscr E^{\bot_{1}}\subseteq\mathscr I$. Hence $\mathscr W_{\mathrm{inj}}\subseteq \mathscr E$, and so $Q_I\rho S_q(A)\cong S_q(A)$ in $\mathcal D_{Q}(A)$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:left-equivalence}, the restriction
\[
 Q_I|_{{}^\perp\mathcal K_{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}:{}^\perp\mathcal K_{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal D_{Q}(A)
\]
is a triangle equivalence with quasi-inverse $L_I$.
The two properties above therefore give $
 \rho S_q(A)\cong L_IQ_I\rho S_q(A)\cong L_IS_q(A)$ in $\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}).$

Since $Q_I$ has a right adjoint, it preserves coproducts.
Note that $(L_I, Q_I)$ is an adjoint pair. It follows from \cite[Lemma~3.5]{Krause05} that $L_I$ preserves compact objects.
By \cite[Theorem~D]{HJ24}, one has
\[
 (\mathcal D_{Q}(A))^c=\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal D_{Q}(A)}\{S_q(A)\mid q\inQ\}.
\]
Since $L_I$ is fully faithful and
$(\mathcal D_{Q}(A))^c$ is idempotent complete, its essential image $L_I((\mathcal D_{Q}(A))^c)$ is thick in $\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$.
 Using the isomorphisms
$L_IS_q(A)\cong\rho S_q(A)$ proved above, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 L_I((\mathcal D_{Q}(A))^c)
 &=\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}\{L_IS_q(A)\mid q\inQ\}\\
 &=\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}\{\rho S_q(A)\mid q\inQ\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $L_I$ preserves compact objects, we have
$L_I((\mathcal D_{Q}(A))^c)\subseteq(\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}))^c$.

\label{thm:compact-quotient}
Assume Setup~\ref{setup:standing} and let $A$ be left noetherian.
Let $i^*:\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})\to\mathcal K_{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$ and $L_I:\mathcal D_{Q}(A)\to\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$ be the functors

 =L_I((\mathcal D_{Q}(A))^c)
 =\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}\{\rho S_q(A)\mid q\inQ\}.

Then the functor $i^*$ preserves compact objects, and its restriction
$(\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}))^c\to(\mathcal K_{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}))^c$ has kernel $\mathcal P_{Q}(A)$.
It induces a triangle equivalence up to direct summands
\[
 \overline{i^*}:
 (\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}))^c/\mathcal P_{Q}(A)
 \longrightarrow(\mathcal K_{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}))^c,
 \qquad C\longmapsto i^*C.
\]
In particular, every object of $(\mathcal K_{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}))^c$ is a direct summand of $i^*C$
for some $C\in(\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}))^c$, and
$\overline{i^*}$ sends $\rho S_q(M)$ to an
object isomorphic to $\sigma S_q(M)$ for every $q\inQ$
and every finitely generated left $A$-module $M$.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:recollement},
$\operatorname{Ker} i^*=\operatorname{Im} L_I$, and $i^*$ induces a triangle equivalence
$\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})/\operatorname{Im} L_I\simeq\mathcal K_{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$.
Since the inclusion $i_*:\mathcal K_{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})\to\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$ preserves coproducts, it follows from \cite[Lemma 3.5]{Krause05} that $i^*$ preserves compact objects.
We next show that $\operatorname{Im} L_I$ is generated, as a localizing
subcategory of $\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$, by $\{L_IS_q(A)\mid q\inQ\}$. Set
\[
 \mathcal T=\operatorname{Loc}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}\{L_IS_q(A)\mid q\inQ\}.
\]
Since $L_I$ is fully faithful,
$\operatorname{Im} L_I$ is a triangulated subcategory of $\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$.
Moreover, $L_I$ preserves coproducts, so $\operatorname{Im} L_I$ is
localizing. Note that $\operatorname{Im} L_I$ contains $L_IS_q(A)$ for each $q\inQ$.
It follows that
$\mathcal T\subseteq\operatorname{Im} L_I$.
Conversely, consider the full subcategory
\[
 \mathcal U=\{Y\in\mathcal D_{Q}(A)\mid L_IY\in\mathcal T\}.
\]
Then $\mathcal U$ is a localizing subcategory of $\mathcal D_{Q}(A)$ containing
 $S_q(A)$ for each $q\inQ$.
Since $\mathcal D_{Q}(A)=\operatorname{Loc}\{S_{q}(A)\mid q\inQ\}$ by
\cite[Theorem~D]{HJ24}, it follows that $\mathcal U=\mathcal D_{Q}(A)$, proving
$\operatorname{Im} L_I\subseteq\mathcal T$. Consequently, $
 \operatorname{Im} L_I
 =\operatorname{Loc}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}\{L_IS_q(A)\mid q\inQ\}.$

By Proposition~\ref{prp:rho-L}, each $L_IS_q(A)$ is compact
in $\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$. We now identify the objects of $\operatorname{Im} L_I$ that
are compact in $\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$. Let $X\in\operatorname{Im} L_I\cap(\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}))^c$.
Then $X\cong L_I(Y)$ for some $Y\in\mathcal D_{Q}(A)$. For every set-indexed family
$\{Y_j\}_{j\in J}$ in $\mathcal D_{Q}(A)$, there are natural isomorphisms
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_{Q}(A)}\Bigl(Y,\bigoplus_{j\in J}Y_j\Bigr)
 &\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}\Bigl(L_IY,
 L_I\bigl(\bigoplus_{j\in J}Y_j\bigr)\Bigr)\\
 &\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})}\Bigl(X,\bigoplus_{j\in J}L_IY_j\Bigr)\\

 &\cong\bigoplus_{j\in J}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D_{Q}(A)}(Y,Y_j).
\end{aligned}
\]

$X\in L_I((\mathcal D_{Q}(A))^c)$.
Conversely, if $Y\in(\mathcal D_{Q}(A))^c$, then $L_I(Y)$ is compact in
$\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$ by Proposition~\ref{prp:rho-L}. Thus we obtain
\[
 \operatorname{Im} L_I\cap(\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}))^c
 =L_I((\mathcal D_{Q}(A))^c)
 =\mathcal P_{Q}(A).
\]
The $\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$ is compactly generated by Theorem~\ref{thm:compact-I}, and $\operatorname{Im} L_I$ is the localizing subcategory generated by the compact objects $L_IS_q(A)$.

 \longrightarrow(\mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})/\operatorname{Im} L_I)^c

functor $\overline{i^*}$.
The description of its action on $\rho S_q(M)$ follows from
Proposition~\ref{prp:sigma-preenvelope}.

\label{exa:ordinary}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)]  Let $Q$ be the $\Bbbk$-linear path category of the quiver
\[
 \cdots\longrightarrow q+1\longrightarrow q
 \longrightarrow q-1\longrightarrow\cdots
\]
modulo all paths of length two. Then ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ is
the category of chain complexes of left $A$-modules,
and there are natural identifications
\[
 \mathcal K_{Q}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})\simeq\mathcal K({}_A\operatorname{Inj}),\quad
 \mathcal K_{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})\simeq\mathcal K^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj}),
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \mathcal D_{Q}(A)\simeq\mathcal D(A).
\]
Under these identifications, $Q_I$ is the canonical
localization functor. Hence Theorem~\ref{thm:recollement}
gives the recollement
\[
\xymatrix@C=3pc{
 \mathcal K^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})\ar[r]^{i_*}&
 \mathcal K({}_A\operatorname{Inj})
 \ar@/_1.5pc/[l]_-{i^*}\ar@/^1.5pc/[l]^-{i^!}
 \ar[r]^{Q_I}&
 \mathcal D(A)
 \ar@/_1.5pc/[l]_-{L_I}\ar@/^1.5pc/[l]^-{R_I}}.
\]
Assume now that $A$ is left noetherian.
The preceding recollement recovers
\cite[Corollary~4.3]{Krause05}.
Theorems~\ref{thm:compact-I} and~\ref{thm:compact-acyclic}
show that $\mathcal K({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$ and
$\mathcal K^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$ are compactly generated.
By \cite[Proposition~2.3 and Lemma~5.2]{Krause05},
the restriction of $Q_I$ gives a triangle equivalence $
 \mathcal K({}_A\operatorname{Inj})^c
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal D^b({}_A\operatorname{mod})$
that identifies $L_I(\mathcal D(A)^c)$ with the full
subcategory $\mathcal K^b({}_A\operatorname{prj})$ of perfect complexes.
Theorem~\ref{thm:compact-quotient} therefore induces a
fully faithful triangle functor
\[
 \mathcal D^b({}_A\operatorname{mod})/\mathcal K^b({}_A\operatorname{prj})
 \longrightarrow
 \mathcal K^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})^c,
\]
and every compact object of
$\mathcal K^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$ is a direct summand
of an object in its essential image.
Thus $\mathcal K^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})^c$ is triangle
equivalent to the idempotent completion of the singularity
category of $A$, recovering \cite[Corollary~5.4]{Krause05}.

\item[(2)] For $m\geq1$, let $Q_m$ be the $\Bbbk$-linear path category of the quiver

\[
\begin{tikzpicture}[
  every node/.style={inner sep=2.5pt},
  every path/.style={->, bend right=18}
]
  \node (q0)    at (0:2.3)   {$0$};
  \node (qm1)   at (60:2.3)  {$m-1$};
  \node (qm2)   at (120:2.3) {$m-2$};
  \node (qdots) at (180:2.3) {$\vdots$};
  \node (q2)    at (240:2.3) {$2$};
  \node (q1)    at (300:2.3) {$1$};

  \draw (q0)    to (qm1);
  \draw (qm1)   to (qm2);
  \draw (qm2)   to (qdots);
  \draw (qdots) to (q2);
  \draw (q2)    to (q1);
  \draw (q1)    to (q0);
\end{tikzpicture}
\]
modulo all paths of length two. Then ${}_{Q_m,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ is
the category of $m$-periodic complexes of left $A$-modules.
By \cite[Example 1.5]{HJ242} and Theorem~\ref{thm:recollement}, we get the following recollement
\[
\xymatrix@C=3pc{
 \mathcal K_m^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})\ar[r]^{i_*}&
 \mathcal K_m({}_A\operatorname{Inj})
 \ar@/_1.5pc/[l]_-{i^*}\ar@/^1.5pc/[l]^-{i^!}
 \ar[r]^{Q_I}&
 \mathcal D_m(A)
 \ar@/_1.5pc/[l]_-{L_I}\ar@/^1.5pc/[l]^-{R_I}},
\]
with $Q_I$ the canonical periodic localization.
When $A$ is left noetherian, we recover Liu's recollement
\cite[Theorem~3.1]{Liu23}.
By Theorems~\ref{thm:compact-I} and~\ref{thm:compact-acyclic},
its middle and left terms are compactly generated, while
the stalk modules $\{S_q(A)\mid q\inQ\}$ form a set of
compact generators for its right term.

\item[(3)] Let $Q$ be the $\Bbbk$-linear path category of the quiver
\[
 \cdots\longrightarrow q+1\longrightarrow q
 \longrightarrow q-1\longrightarrow\cdots
\]
modulo all paths of length $N$.
The category ${}_{Q,A}\operatorname{Mod}$ identifies with the category
of $N$-complexes of left $A$-modules, and the objects of
$\mathscr E$ are precisely the $N$-acyclic complexes.
By \cite[Example 1.5]{HJ242} and Theorem~\ref{thm:recollement}, we get the following recollement
\[
\xymatrix@C=2.8pc{
 \mathcal K_N^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})
 \ar[r]^{i_*}
 &
 \mathcal K_N({}_A\operatorname{Inj})
 \ar@/_1.5pc/[l]_-{i^*}
 \ar@/^1.5pc/[l]^-{i^!}
 \ar[r]^{Q_I}
 &
 \mathcal D_N(A)
 \ar@/_1.5pc/[l]_-{L_I}
 \ar@/^1.5pc/[l]^-{R_I}.}
\]
Assume that $A$ is left noetherian, and write $S_i(M)$
for the stalk $N$-complex with $M$ in degree $i$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, the sets
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal G_I=\{\rho S_i(M)\mid i\in\mathbb Z,\ M\in\mathcal M_A\}\quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal G_{\mathrm{ac}}=\{\sigma S_i(M)\mid i\in\mathbb Z,\ M\in\mathcal M_A\}
\end{aligned}
\]
form sets of compact generators for
$\mathcal K_N({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$ and
$\mathcal K_N^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$, respectively.
For $\mathcal K_N({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$, this recovers the existence
of compact generators established in
\cite[Theorem~3.23]{IKM17}.

To describe the compact objects of
$\mathcal K_N^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$, recall that
$Q_I$ restricts to a triangle equivalence $
 \mathcal K_N({}_A\operatorname{Inj})^c
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal D_N^b({}_A\operatorname{mod})$
by \cite[Theorem~3.23]{IKM17}.
Furthermore,  by \cite[Theorem~4.2]{IKM17} and \cite[Proposition~4.15(i)]{IKM17}, we
identify $\mathcal D_N(A)^c$ with
$\mathcal K_N^b({}_A\operatorname{prj})$.
The isomorphism $Q_IL_I\simeq\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal D_N(A)}$
therefore shows that the preceding equivalence identifies
$L_I(\mathcal D_N(A)^c)$ with
$\mathcal K_N^b({}_A\operatorname{prj})$.
Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:compact-quotient}, we obtain
a fully faithful triangle functor
\[
 \mathcal D_N^b({}_A\operatorname{mod})/\mathcal K_N^b({}_A\operatorname{prj})
 \longrightarrow
 \mathcal K_N^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})^c.
\]
Every compact object of
$\mathcal K_N^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})$ is a direct
summand of an object in its essential image.
Thus $\mathcal K_N^{\mathrm{ac}}({}_A\operatorname{Inj})^c$ is
triangle equivalent to the idempotent completion of
the $N$-singularity category
$\mathcal D_N^b({}_A\operatorname{mod})/\mathcal K_N^b({}_A\operatorname{prj})$.
\end{enumerate}
