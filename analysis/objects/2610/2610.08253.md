<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{log-holder}
Let $g\in C(\Omega)$. We say that $g$ is locally log-H\"older continuous, abbreviated $g\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\log}(\Omega)$, if there exists $c_{\log}>0$ such that
$$
|g(x)-g(y)|\leq \frac{c_{\log}}{\log(e+1/|x-y|)} \quad \text{for all}\;\; x,y\in\Omega.
$$
We say that $g$ is globally log-H\"older continuous, abbreviated $g\in C^{\log}(\Omega)$,
if $g$ is locally log-H\"older continuous and there exists $g_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}$ such that
$$
|g(x)-g_{\infty}|\leq \frac{c_{\log}}{\log(e+|x|)} \quad \text{for all}\;\; x\in\Omega.
$$
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Let $s\in(0,1)$. If $u\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ for some $p\in \mathcal{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ with $1<p^-\leq p^+< \infty$ is such that $I_{1-s}*u$   is a well-defined locally integrable function, we define the distributional Riesz fractional gradient
\[
(\nabla^s u)_j := \frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s},
\qquad
j=1,\ldots,d,
\]
where
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s}
:=
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j} I_{1-s}u,
\end{equation}
in the sense that
\begin{align*}
\left\langle
\frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s},
v
\right\rangle
&=
(-1)
\left\langle
I_{1-s}u,
\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_j}
\right\rangle
=
-\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}
\bigl(I_{1-s}*u\bigr)
\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_j}
\,dx,
\end{align*}
for every $v\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:bd-Riesz} Let us shed some light on  what it  means for $I_{1-s}\ast u$ to be well defined in the setting of variable Lebesgue spaces.
The $L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)\rightarrow L^{q(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ boundedness of $I_{\alpha}$  was first considered in \cite{Sam98}. For the case of the whole
space $\mathbb{R}^d$ the following statement was proven in \cite{CCUF07} and can also be found in \cite[Thm.~2.2]{AS06} and \cite[Thm.~6.1.9]{DHHR}.
Let \(0<\alpha<d\) and let \(p(\cdot)\in \mathcal{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^d)\) such that
\(
1<p^- \le p^+ < \frac{d}{\alpha}.
\)
Then there exists a constant \(c>0\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bd-Riesz}
\|{I}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)}
\le
c\,\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)},
\qquad
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d),
\end{equation}
where \(q(\cdot)\) is  given by
\(
\frac{1}{q(x)}
=
\frac{1}{p(x)}
-
\frac{\alpha}{d},
\
x\in\mathbb{R}^d.
\)
In particular, under the hypothesis $p^+<\tfrac{d}{1-s}$, \eqref{eq:bd-Riesz} shows that $I_{1-s}u\in L^{q(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)$ with $q^->p^->1$, which in particular guarantees that $I_{1-s}u$ is locally integrable, as required in the definition above.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm-prop-frac-grad}
Let $s\in(0,1)$. Then the Riesz fractional gradient satisfies the following properties.

\begin{itemize}
    \item[(i)] If $u\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$, then
    \[
        \nabla^s u = I_{1-s}*\nabla u.
    \]

    \item[(ii)] If $u\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$, then the fractional Laplacian admits the representation
    \[
        (-\Delta)^s u
        =
        -\sum_{j=1}^d
        \frac{\partial^s}{\partial x_j^s}
        \frac{\partial^s}{\partial x_j^s}u.
    \]

    \item[(iii)] If $u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d)$, then the Fourier transform of the fractional partial derivatives is given by
    \[
        \widehat{\frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s}}(\xi)
        =
        (2\pi)^s i\xi_j |\xi|^{-1+s}\widehat{u}(\xi).
    \]
\end{itemize}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We provide a proof of (iii) since there appears to be a sign inconsistency  in \cite[Thm.~1.4]{SS15}. We compute
\begin{align*}
\widehat{\frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s}}(\xi)
=
\widehat{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}(I_{1-s}u)}(\xi)
=
2\pi i\xi_j\,\widehat{I_{1-s}* u}(\xi)
=
2\pi i\xi_j\,(2\pi)^{s-1}|\xi|^{s-1}\widehat{u}(\xi)
=
i(2\pi)^s|\xi|^{s-1}\xi_j\,\widehat{u}(\xi).
\end{align*}
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The first identity provides a geometric interpretation of the fractional gradient. While the classical gradient is the locally linear approximation of a function, the fractional gradient can be viewed as an \emph{averaged} linear approximation, incorporating nonlocal information through the Riesz potential. The second identity establishes the natural connection between the fractional gradient and the fractional Laplacian. It is the nonlocal analogue of the classical identity
\[
    -\Delta u
    =
    -\sum_{j=1}^d
    \frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x_j^2},
\]
showing that the fractional Laplacian can be recovered as the sum of the fractional second-order derivatives. Finally, the Fourier representation provides the spectral characterization of the fractional gradient. Together with the previous identities, it links the geometric and spectral viewpoints.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def-Xsp}
Let $p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ and let $s\in(0,1)$. For
$u\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$ we define the norm
\[
    \|u\|_{X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)}
    :=
    \|u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)}
    +
    \| \nabla^s u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)},
\]
where the latter norm is understood as the  norm  in $L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$
of the Euclidean norm of $\nabla^s u$, i.e., $|\nabla^s u|=(\sum_{j=1}^d|\partial^s_{x_j}u|^2)^{1/2}$.
The space $X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ is then defined by
\[ X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)
    :=
    \overline{C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)}
    ^{\|\cdot\|_{X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)}},
\]
the closure of $C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d)$ with respect to the norm of $X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
We restrict our attention to the case $s\in(0,1)$, although the construction extends naturally to arbitrary orders $s\ge0$. Writing
\(
    \tilde s=s-\lfloor s\rfloor,
\)
where $\lfloor s\rfloor$ denotes the integer part of $s$, one may define
\[
    \|u\|_{X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)}
    :=
    \|u\|_{W^{\lfloor s\rfloor,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)}
    +
    \|\nabla^{\tilde s}D^{\lfloor s\rfloor}u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;{\mathbb{R}^d})},
\]
where $W^{\lfloor s\rfloor,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ stands for the Sobolev space of order $\lfloor s\rfloor$ with variable exponent $p(\cdot)$.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm-frac-FTOC}
Let $0<s<1$, then  every \( u \in C_0^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^d) \) is expressible as
\[
u = I_s \sum_{j=1}^d \mathcal{R}_j \frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s},
\]
where \( \mathcal{R}_j \) is the Riesz transform, which can be characterized as a singular integral or \( 0 \)-order operator with symbol \( \frac{-i\xi_j}{|\xi|} \), i.e.,  $\widehat{\mathcal{R}_jf} =\frac{-i\xi_j}{|\xi|}\hat{f}$ for $f\in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $u \in C_0^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^d)$.  Since $u\in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d)$,
using
Theorem \ref{thm-prop-frac-grad}(iii), which gives
$
\widehat{\frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s}} =  (2\pi)^s i \xi_j |\xi|^{-1+s} \hat{u},
\  \text{and} \
\widehat{\mathcal{R}_jf} = -\frac{i \xi_j}{|\xi|}\hat{f}
$,
the result follows from the identity
\(
\hat{I}_s = (2\pi |\xi|)^{-s} \ \text{in } \mathcal{S}^\prime(\mathbb{R}^d)
\),  as
\[
\hat{u}=\hat{I}_s \sum_{j=1}^d -\frac{i \xi_j}{|\xi|}\widehat{\frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s}}.
\]

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm-char-bessel}
Assume that $p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ satisfies
\(
1<p^-\le p^+<\infty\)   and let  $s\in (0,1)$.
Then
\[
    X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)
    =
    L^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d).
\]
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We first prove the inclusion \( L^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \subset X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \).
Suppose \( u \in L^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \), which means that \( u = g_s * f \) for some \( f \in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \).
Then using \cite[Lem.~12.4.3]{DHHR}
(see also  \cite[Thm. 5.1]{AS06})
we have
\begin{equation}\label{help-1}
\|u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)} \leq c \|g_s\|_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^d)} \|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)},
\end{equation}
and thus \( u \in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \). It therefore remains to show that \( {\nabla^s u } \in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d) \) and the inclusion will be demonstrated. We begin by assuming \( f \in C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d) \), and then the general case will follow from density. Note that \( f \in C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d) \) and \( u \in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \) implies that it makes sense to write

\[
\frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s} = g_s * \frac{\partial^s f}{\partial x_j^s}.
\]
Then, using Theorem \ref{thm-prop-frac-grad}(iii) to compute the Fourier transform, we obtain
\[
\widehat{\frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s}}(\xi) = (1 + 4\pi^2 |\xi|^2)^{-\frac{s}{2}} ((2\pi)^s i \xi_j |\xi|^{-1+s}) \hat{f}(\xi),
\]
so that
\[
\widehat{\frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s}} = \frac{i \xi_j}{|\xi|} \frac{(2\pi |\xi|)^s}{(1 + 4\pi^2 |\xi|^2)^{\frac{s}{2}}} \hat{f}.
\]

On the other hand, it follows from \cite[Lem.~2.1, p.~247]{Miz96} (see also \cite[Lem.~2, p.~133]{Stein70}) that there exists a finite signed measure $\mu_s$ such that
\[
\hat{\mu}_s(\xi) = \frac{(2\pi |\xi|)^s}{(1 + 4\pi^2 |\xi|^2)^{\frac{s}{2}}},
\]
and therefore inverting the Fourier transform we have
\[
\frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s} = { -}
\mu_s * \mathcal{R}_j f,
\]
where \( \mathcal{R}_j \) is the Riesz transform. Now
\begin{equation}\label{help-2}
\|\nabla^s u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;{\mathbb{R}^d})} \leq c{ \max_{j=1,\dots, d}}\|\mathcal{R}_j f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)} \leq c' \|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)},
\end{equation}
where
in the second step we used the
  boundedness of the Riesz transform \( \mathcal{R}_j : L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \to L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \) for \( 1 < p^- \leq p^+ < \infty \), which also is stated in \cite[Lem.~12.4.3]{DHHR}. Now \eqref{help-1} together with \eqref{help-2} implies the desired result  is case of \( f \in C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d) \). Otherwise it follows by density, since \(C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d) \) in dense in \(L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)\), cf. e.g \cite[Lem.~4.6.5, p.~130]{DHHR}.

Conversely, suppose \( u \in X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \). Define
\[
f := \lambda_s * \left( u + \sum_{j=1}^d \mathcal{R}_j \frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s} \right),
\]
where \( \lambda_s \) is the finite signed measure for which
\[
(1 + 4\pi^2 |\xi|^2)^{\frac{s}{2}} = \hat{\lambda}_s(\xi) [1 + (2\pi |\xi|)^s],
\]
cf.  \cite[Lem.~2.2, p. 248]{Miz96}.
Moreover, we introduce a function $h_{s}$ via its Fourier transform as follows
\[
\frac{(1 + 4\pi^2 |\xi|^2)^{\frac{s}{2}}}{1 + (2\pi |\xi|)^s}=\hat{\lambda}_s(\xi)=:\hat{h}_s(\xi) +1.
\]
In  particular, this implies $\lambda_s=h_s+\delta_0$, where $\delta_0$ denotes the delta distribution.
It was shown in \cite[Lem.~5.4]{AS06} that $h_s$ admits the following bounds:
\[
|h_s(x)| \leq C
\begin{cases}
|x|^{-(d-s)}, & |x| < 1 \\
|x|^{-(d+s)}, & |x| \geq 1
\end{cases}.
\]
In what follows we will show that \( |h_s|\) has an integrable least bell shaped majorant, i.e., the function  \(\Psi_s(x) := \sup_{|y| \geq |x|} |h_s(y)|\in L^1(\mathbb{R}^d)\), so that \cite[Lem.~4.6.3]{DHHR} can be applied w.r.t. $h_s$. For
  \(|x| < 1\),
\[
\Psi_s(x) \leq \max\left\{ \sup_{|x| \leq |y| < 1} C|y|^{-(d-{s})},\, \sup_{|y| \geq 1} C|y|^{-(d+s)} \right\}
= C |x|^{-(d-{s})}.
\]
For \(|x| \geq 1\),
\[
\Psi_s(x) = \sup_{|y| \geq |x|} |h_s(y)| \leq C |x|^{-(d+s)}.
\]
Thus,
\[
\Psi_s(x) \leq C
\begin{cases}
|x|^{-(d-s)}, & |x| < 1, \\
|x|^{-(d+s)}, & |x| \geq 1.
\end{cases}
\]
Using spherical coordinates,
\[
\int_{|x| < 1} |x|^{-(d-{s})} \, dx
= C_d \int_0^1 r^{{s}-1} \, dr < \infty,
\]
and
\[
\int_{|x| \geq 1} |x|^{-(d+s)} \, dx
= C_d \int_1^\infty r^{-(s+1)} \, dr < \infty,
\]
since \(s > 0\). Hence \(\Psi_s \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)\).
We compute
\begin{align*}
    \|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}
    & =\left\|\lambda_s * \left( u + \sum_{j=1}^d \mathcal{R}_j \frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s} \right)\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}
     =\left\|(h_s+\delta_0) * \left( u + \sum_{j=1}^d \mathcal{R}_j \frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s} \right)\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}\\
     & \le \left\|h_s * \left( u + \sum_{j=1}^d \mathcal{R}_j \frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s} \right)\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}+ \left\| \delta_0 * \left( u + \sum_{j=1}^d \mathcal{R}_j \frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s} \right)\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}\\
    &\leq c\left\| u + \sum_{j=1}^d \mathcal{R}_j \frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}
    \leq C \left(\|u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}+\sum_{j=1}^d \left\|\frac{\partial^s u}{\partial x_j^s}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}\right),
\end{align*}
where we used the definition of $h_s$ in the second step, the identity $\delta_0 \ast f =f$    and \cite[Lem.~12.4.3]{DHHR}
in the fourth step, as well as  the boundedness of the  Riesz transform  in the last step (see, e.g., \cite[Lem.~3.3]{GHN07} or \cite[Lem.~12.4.3]{DHHR}).
The right-hand side is bounded since $u\in X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ which shows that \( f \in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \).
It remains to show that \( u = g_s * f \). Supposing that \( u \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d) \cap X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \), we have that
\[
\hat{g}_s \hat{f} = \hat{g}_s \hat{\lambda}_s \left( \hat{u} + \sum_{j=1}^d \frac{-{i}{\xi}_j}{|\xi|} ((2\pi)^s { i {\xi}_j} |\xi|^{-1+s}) \hat{u} \right),
\]
where we used Theorem \ref{thm-prop-frac-grad}(iii) and the fact that $\widehat{\mathcal{R}_j\varphi} (\xi)= -\frac{i \xi_j}{|\xi|}\hat{\varphi}(\xi)$, $\varphi\in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d)$.
Making simplifications in the summation on the right hand side, we have
\[
\hat{g}_s \hat{f} = \hat{g}_s \hat{\lambda}_s (\hat{u} + (2\pi |\xi|)^s \hat{u}) = \hat{u},
\]
where we  used the definition of \( \lambda_s \)  and  $g_s$ in the second equality.
Therefore \( u = g_s * f \) whenever \( u \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d) \). The general case then follows from the density of \( \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d) \) in \( X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \), since \( C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d) \subset \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d) \cap X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \subset X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \) and \( X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d) \) is defined as the completion of \( C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d) \) in the \( X^{s,p(\cdot)} \)-norm.

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Set
\(
\alpha(x):=s-\frac{d}{p(x)}\). Then
\(\alpha^{-} {=}s-\frac{d}{p^-}>0
\)
implying
\(
0<\alpha^{\color{cyan}-}\leq \alpha(x)<s<1.
\)
We first prove the assumption for
\(u\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb R^d)\). Using the fractional fundamental theorem of calculus (Theorem \ref{thm-frac-FTOC}) we write
\[
u= I_s g, \qquad \text{where}\quad
g=
\sum_{j=1}^d
\mathcal{R}_j\frac{\partial^su}{\partial x_j^s}.
\]
Since $g$ decays like $|z|^{-d-s}$ at infinity for $u\in C_0^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^d)$  which makes $I_sg$ a continuous function,   this is a pointwise identity, and
 for \(x\neq y\) we have
\[
u(x)-u(y)
=
\gamma(d,s)\int_{\mathbb R^d}
\left(
|x-z|^{s-d}-|y-z|^{s-d}
\right)g(z)\,dz.
\]

We use the generalized H\"older inequality from \cite[Lem.~3.2.20]{DHHR},
\[
\int_E|g(z)|\,dz
\leq
2\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}
 \|\chi_E\|_{L^{p'(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)},
\]
{where $E$ is any measurable subset of $\mathbb R^d$}, together with  the standard
characteristic-function estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:characteristic-small-ball}
\|\chi_{B(a,r)}\|_{L^{p'(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}
\leq
C_0r^{d/p'(a)},
\qquad 0<r\leq1,
\end{equation}
which holds uniformly with respect to \(a\in\mathbb R^d\), see \cite[Cor.~4.5.9 and above]{DHHR}. Let
\(
h:=|x-y|\leq\frac14.
\)
We split the integral into a near part over \(B(x,2h)\) and a
far part over its complement.
For \(a\in\{x,y\}\), consider
\[
J(a,h)
:=
\int_{B(a,3h)}
|a-z|^{s-d}|g(z)|\,dz.
\]
Decompose \(B(a,3h)\) into the dyadic annuli
\[
A_k(a)
:=
\left\{
z:
2^{-k-1}3h\leq |a-z|<2^{-k}3h
\right\},
\qquad k\geq0.
\]
Using the generalized H\"older inequality and
\eqref{eq:characteristic-small-ball}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
J(a,h)
&\leq
C_1\sum_{k=0}^\infty
(2^{-k}h)^{s-d}
\int_{A_k(a)}|g(z)|\,dz
\\
&\leq
C_2\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}{(\mathbb R^d)}}
\sum_{k=0}^\infty
(2^{-k}h)^{s-d}
(2^{-k}h)^{d/p'(a)}
=
C_2\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}{(\mathbb R^d)}}
\sum_{k=0}^\infty
(2^{-k}h)^{s-d/p(a)}
\leq
C_3 h^{\alpha(a)}
\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}{(\mathbb R^d)}}.
\end{align*}
The last series converges uniformly because
\(\alpha(a)\geq\alpha^{-}>0\). Since by definition of $h$ it holds
\[
B(x,2h)\subset B(y,3h),
\]
the near part therefore satisfies
\begin{align*}
\int_{B(x,2h)}
\left(
|x-z|^{s-d}+|y-z|^{s-d}
\right)|g(z)|\,dz
\leq
C_4\left(h^{\alpha(x)}+h^{\alpha(y)}\right)
\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}{(\mathbb R^d)}}.
\end{align*}
Moreover, local logarithmic H\"older continuity gives
\[
\left|\frac1{p(x)}-\frac1{p(y)}\right|
\leq
\frac{c_{\log}}{\log(e+1/h)}.
\]
and consequently,
\begin{align*}
\frac{h^{\alpha(y)}}{h^{\alpha(x)}}
=
h^{d(1/p(x)-1/p(y))}
\leq
\exp\left(
d\left|\frac1{p(x)}-\frac1{p(y)}\right|
\log\frac1h
\right)
\leq e^{dc_{\log}}.
\end{align*}
Thus, the near part is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-part}
\int_{B(x,2h)}
\left(

\right)|g(z)|\,dz\leq C_5 h^{\alpha(x)}
\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}.
\end{equation}

For \(z\notin B(x,2h)\), the mean value theorem yields
\begin{equation}\label{MVT}
\left|
|x-z|^{s-d}-|y-z|^{s-d}
\right|
\leq
C_6 h|x-z|^{s-d-1}.
\end{equation}
Here we used that  every point $\xi$ on the line segment joining \(x\) and \(y\)
has distance
\[

\geq |x-z|-t|x-y|
\geq \frac12|x-z|
\]
for every
\(t\in[0,1]\) and the fact that $s-d-1<0$.
First consider the region

A_k

\left\{

\right\}

& \leq C_7\sum_{\substack{k\geq1\\2^kh<1/2}}
 h(2^k h)^{s-d-1}

Here the second step is again a consequence of the characteristic function estimate \eqref{eq:characteristic-small-ball} because

and \(2^{k+1}h<1\).

\leq

\(E\) with \(|E|\geq1\), one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:characteristic-large-sets}
\|\chi_E\|_{L^{p'(\cdot)}{(\mathbb R^d)}}
\leq
\tilde{C}|E|^{1/(p')^{-}},
\qquad
{(p')}^{-}=(p^+)'.
\end{equation}
We consider dyadic annuli of radii \(2^k\),  i.e.,
\[
A_k:=
\left\{
z\in\mathbb R^d:
2^k\leq |x-z|<2^{k+1}
\right\}, \qquad k\geq 0
\]
together with the set $A_{-1}:=
\left\{
z\in\mathbb R^d:
\frac12\leq |x-z|<1
\right\}$.
Since \(A_k\subset B(x,2^{k+1})\) and
\(|A_k|\leq C_{11}2^{kd}\), estimate
\eqref{eq:characteristic-large-sets} gives
\[
\|\chi_{A_k}\|_{L^{p'(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}
\leq
C_{12}2^{kd/(p')^{-}}.
\]
The finitely many annuli for which \(|A_k|<1\) can be absorbed into
the constant. Therefore, since
$A_1$  has uniformly bounded measure, using \eqref{MVT} and the generalized H\"older inequality yield
\begin{align*}
\int_{|x-z|\geq1/2}
\left|
|x-z|^{s-d}-|y-z|^{s-d}
\right||g(z)|\,dz
& =C_6 h\sum_{k=-1}^{\infty}\int_{A_k}|x-z|^{s-d-1}|g(z)|dz
\\
&\leq
C_{13}h\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}
\left(
1+\sum_{k=0}^\infty
2^{k(s-d-1+d/(p')^-)}
\right)
\\
&=
C_{13}h\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}
\left(
1+\sum_{k=0}^\infty
2^{k(s-1-d/p^+)}
\right).
\end{align*}
The series converges because
\(
s-1-\frac{d}{p^+}<0.
\)
Since \(0<h<1\) and \(\alpha(x)<1\), we have
\(h\leq h^{\alpha(x)}\). Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:far-part}
\int_{|x-z|\geq1/2}
\left|
|x-z|^{s-d}-|y-z|^{s-d}
\right||g(z)|\,dz
\leq
C_{14} h^{\alpha(x)}
\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}.
\end{equation}

Combining \eqref{eq:near-part},
\eqref{eq:intermediate-part}, and \eqref{eq:far-part}, we find
\begin{equation}\label{est-1/4}
|u(x)-u(y)|
\leq
C_{15}|x-y|^{s-d/p(x)}
\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}
\end{equation}
whenever \(|x-y|\leq1/4\). Suppose now that \(1/4<|x-y|<1\), and set
\[
x_j:=x+\frac{j}{4}(y-x),\qquad j=0,\ldots,4.
\]
Then \(|x_{j+1}-x_j|=|x-y|/4<1/4\). Applying the preceding estimate \eqref{est-1/4} to each consecutive pair and using \(\alpha(x_j)=s-d/p(x_j)>0\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
|u(x)-u(y)|
&\le \sum_{j=0}^3|u(x_{j+1})-u(x_j)|\le C_{15} \sum_{j=0}^3\left(\frac{|x-y|}{4}\right)^{\alpha(x_{j})}\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}\leq 4C_{15}\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, since \(1/4<|x-y|<1\) and \(0<\alpha(x)<s\), we have
\(
|x-y|^{\alpha(x)}\ge 4^{-s} \iff    1\leq 4^s|x-y|^{\alpha(x)}.
\)
Therefore,
\[
|u(x)-u(y)|
\le
4^{s+1}C_{15}\,|x-y|^{s-d/p(x)}
\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}.
\]

As in \eqref{eq:est-g-Riesz} we have that
\(
\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)}
\leq
C'\|\nabla^su\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d;\mathbb R^d)}.
\)
Therefore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth-morrey}
|u(x)-u(y)|=|I_sg(x)-I_sg(y)|
\leq
C''|x-y|^{s-d/p(x)}
\|\nabla^su\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d;\mathbb R^d)}
\end{equation}
for every \(u\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb R^d)\) and
\(0<|x-y|<1\).
Let now \(u\in X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)\). By density, choose
\(u_k\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb R^d)\) such that
\(
u_k\to u
 \text{ in }X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d).
\)
We show that \((u_k)_k\) converges locally uniformly. Let \(Q\) be a ball
of radius at most \(1/4\), set \(v_{k,\ell}:=u_k-u_\ell\), and write
\[
(v_{k,\ell})_Q
:=
\frac1{|Q|}\int_Qv_{k,\ell}(z)\,dz,
\qquad
\operatorname*{osc}_Qv_{k,\ell}
:=
\sup_{z,w\in Q}|v_{k,\ell}(z)-v_{k,\ell}(w)|.
\]
By \eqref{eq:smooth-morrey}, using $|z-w|^{s-d/p(x)}\leq 1$ we obtain
\[
\operatorname*{osc}_Qv_{k,\ell}
\leq
C''\|\nabla^sv_{k,\ell}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)}.
\]
Moreover, the generalized H\"older  inequality gives
\[
|(v_{k,\ell})_Q|
\leq
\frac{2}{|Q|}
\|v_{k,\ell}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)}
\|\chi_Q\|_{L^{p'(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)}.
\]
Since
\[
\|v_{k,\ell}\|_{L^\infty(Q)}
\leq \sup_{z\in Q}|v_{k,\ell}(z)-(v_{k,\ell})_Q|+|(v_{k,\ell})_Q|
\leq
\operatorname*{osc}_Qv_{k,\ell}
+|(v_{k,\ell})_Q|,
\]
it follows that
\[
\|v_{k,\ell}\|_{L^\infty(Q)}
\leq
C_Q\|v_{k,\ell}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)}
+
C''\|\nabla^sv_{k,\ell}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)}.
\]
Thus \((u_k)\) is {a} locally uniformly Cauchy {sequence}  and  {consequently}  converges to a continuous
function \(\widetilde u\). Since \(u_k\to u\) in \(L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)\),
we have \(\widetilde u=u\) almost everywhere. Finally, passing to the limit in \eqref{eq:smooth-morrey} yields
\[
|\widetilde u(x)-\widetilde u(y)|
\leq
C''|x-y|^{s-d/p(x)}
\|\nabla^su\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d;\mathbb R^d)}
\]
for \(0<|x-y|<1\).  Rearranging and taking the supremum over
\(y\in B(x,1)\setminus\{x\}\) proves the assertion.

<!-- theorem-10: body_md -->
\label{thm-low-semicont}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ be an open set and let
$p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\Omega)$ satisfy
\(
1<p^- \le p^+<\infty.
\)
Let
\(
f:\Omega\times\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}
\)
be a Borel measurable function  satisfying
\begin{itemize}
\item \(
    \xi \mapsto f(x,\xi)
    \)
      is lower semicontinuous for almost every $x \in \Omega$,
\item  \(\xi\mapsto f(x,\xi)\) is convex,
\item  the growth condition
\[
f(x,\xi)\ge \langle a(x),\xi\rangle+b(x)
\]
for almost every $x\in\Omega$ and every $\xi\in\mathbb R^d$, where
\(
a\in L^{p'(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb R^d),
\
b\in L^1(\Omega),
\)
and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ denotes the Euclidean scalar product.
\end{itemize}
Then the functional
\[
J(\xi):=\int_\Omega f(x,\xi(x))\,dx
\]
  is weakly lower semicontinuous in $L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb R^d)$, i.e., if
\(
\xi_\nu\rightharpoonup\xi
\ \text{in }L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb R^d)\)
then
\[\liminf_{\nu\to\infty}J(\xi_\nu)\ge J(\xi).
\]

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
A function $f:\Omega\times\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}$ satisfying the
hypotheses of Theorem \ref{thm-low-semicont}, i.e.\ jointly Borel measurable in
$(x,\xi)$ and, for almost every $x\in\Omega$, lower semicontinuous and convex in
$\xi$, is commonly called a \emph{normal convex integrand}
(see e.g.\ \cite[Ch.~6]{FL07}). The joint measurability of $f$ is exactly what is needed to
ensure that $x\mapsto f(x,\xi(x))$ is Lebesgue measurable for every measurable
$\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb R^d$, so that the functional $J(\xi)=\int_\Omega
f(x,\xi(x))\,dx$ in Theorem \ref{thm-low-semicont} is well defined with values
in $\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}$.  If, in addition, $\xi\mapsto f(x,\xi)$ is continuous (rather
than merely lower semicontinuous) for a.e.\ $x$, one calls $f$ a
\emph{Carath\'eodory function}; in that case the measurability of
$x\mapsto f(x,\xi(x))$ is immediate. Note that  our class additionally allows $f(x,\cdot)$ to take the value $+\infty$, which continuity would rule out.

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We divide the proof into three steps.

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Step 1.}
Let
\[
h(x,\xi):=\langle a(x),\xi\rangle+b(x).
\]
Since
\(
a\in L^{p'(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb R^d),
\
b\in L^1(\Omega),
\)
$h$ is affine-linear in $\xi$,
and
\(
\xi_\nu\rightharpoonup\xi
\ \text{in }
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb R^d),
\)
it follows from the definition of weak convergence that
\[
\lim_{\nu\to\infty}
\int_\Omega h(x,\xi_\nu(x))\,dx
=
\int_\Omega h(x,\xi(x))\,dx.
\]

Define
\(
g(x,\xi):=f(x,\xi)-h(x,\xi).
\)
Then \(g\ge0\) and since
$h$ is affine and continuous in
$\xi$,
$g$ inherits lower semicontinuity and convexity in
$\xi$ from
$f$.  Therefore, if we prove that
\[
\liminf_{\nu\to\infty}
\int_\Omega g(x,\xi_\nu(x))\,dx
\ge
\int_\Omega g(x,\xi(x))\,dx,
\]
the theorem follows. Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume
\(f\ge0\).

\medskip


\noindent
\textbf{Step 2.}
We next prove that \(J\) is strongly lower semicontinuous. Assume that
\(
\xi_\nu\to\xi\quad\text{in } L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb R^d).
\)
Set \(\ell:=\liminf_{\nu\to\infty}J(\xi_\nu)\). By definition of the \(\liminf\), there exists a subsequence \(\{\xi_{\nu_k}\}_{k=1}^\infty\) such that
\[
J(\xi_{\nu_k})\longrightarrow \ell \quad\text{as } k\to\infty.
\]
Since \(\xi_{\nu_k}\to\xi\) in \(L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb R^d)\) as well, passing to a further subsequence (not relabeled), we may assume that
\[
\xi_{\nu_k}(x)\to\xi(x)\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega,
\]
see \cite[Lem. 3.2.10(a)]{DHHR}. Since \(f\ge0\), Fatou's lemma yields
\[
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_\Omega f(x,\xi_{\nu_k}(x))\,dx
\ge
\int_\Omega \liminf_{k\to\infty} f(x,\xi_{\nu_k}(x))\,dx.
\]
Thus, using the lower semicontinuity of \(\xi\mapsto f(x,\xi)\) for a.e. \(x\in\Omega\), we obtain
\[
\liminf_{k\to\infty} J(\xi_{\nu_k})\ge J(\xi).
\]
Since \(\big(J(\xi_{\nu_k})\big)_k\) converges to \(\ell\), every subsequence of it converges to \(\ell\) as well, so that
\[
\ell = \lim_{k\to\infty}J(\xi_{\nu_k}) = \liminf_{k\to\infty}J(\xi_{\nu_k})\ge J(\xi).
\]
Recalling \(\ell=\liminf_{\nu\to\infty}J(\xi_\nu)\) proves
\[
\liminf_{\nu\to\infty}J(\xi_\nu)\ge J(\xi).
\]

\textbf{Step 3.}
We now pass from strong to weak lower semicontinuity using Mazur's Theorem.
{Assume that \linebreak
\(
\xi_\nu \rightharpoonup \xi\quad\text{in } L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb R^d)
\) and set}
\[
L:=
\liminf_{\nu\to\infty}
J(\xi_\nu).
\]
Since \(f\ge0\), we have \(L\ge0\). We may assume that
\(L<\infty\), since otherwise there is nothing to prove.
Passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may further assume that
\[
L=
\lim_{\nu\to\infty}
J(\xi_\nu).
\]
Hence, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists
\(\nu_\varepsilon\) such that
\[
J(\xi_\nu)\le L+\varepsilon
\qquad
\text{for every }
\nu\ge\nu_\varepsilon.
\]

Applying Theorem \ref{thm-marzur}  in
\(L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb R^d)\),
there exists a sequence
\[
\{\eta_\mu\}_{\mu=1}^\infty
\subset
\operatorname{co}
\{\xi_\nu\}_{\nu=\nu_\varepsilon}^\infty
\]
such that
\(
\eta_\mu\to\xi
\ \text{strongly in }
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb R^d),
\)
and, for every \(\mu\),
\[
\eta_\mu
=
\sum_{i=\nu_\varepsilon}^{N_\mu}
\alpha_\mu^i\xi_i,
\qquad
\alpha_\mu^i \geq 0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=\nu_\varepsilon}^{N_\mu}
\alpha_\mu^i=1.
\]
Moreover, since \(f(x,\cdot)\) is convex, we deduce
\[
J(\eta_\mu)
=\int_\Omega f(x,\eta_{\mu}(x))\,dx
=\int_\Omega f\left(x,\sum_{i=\nu_\varepsilon}^{N_\mu}
\alpha_\mu^i\xi_i(x)\right)\,dx
\leq \sum_{i=\nu_\varepsilon}^{N_\mu}
\alpha_\mu^i\int_\Omega f (x,\xi_i(x))dx
=
\sum_{i=\nu_\varepsilon}^{N_\mu}
\alpha_\mu^i
J(\xi_i)
\le
L+\varepsilon.
\]

Combining this estimate with Step~2 yields
\[
J(\xi)
\le {\liminf_{\mu\to\infty}J(\eta_\mu) \le }
L+\varepsilon.
\]
Since \(\varepsilon>0\) is arbitrary, we get
\(
J(\xi)
\le
\liminf_{\nu\to\infty}
J(\xi_\nu),
\)
which proves the theorem.

\label{thm-minimizer}
Let $p(\cdot)\in \mathcal{P}^{\log}(\mathbb R^d)$ such that
\(
1<p^-\le p^+<\infty
\), $s\in(0,1)$ so that $s<\frac{d}{p^+}$, and define {$p^{\ast}$ as in \eqref{def-p-ast}. Moreover let $\Omega\subset \mathbb{R}^d$ be open and bounded,}
$q(\cdot)\in  \mathcal P(\Omega)$  be such that $1 < q^-\leq q(x) < p^{*}(x)$, {  $x\in \Omega$,} and let
$g\in L^{q'(\cdot)}(\Omega)$.
Let $\varphi\in X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)$ such that $I_{1-s} * \varphi$ is a well-defined locally integrable function.
Assume that
$f:\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R$
 is a Borel measurable function, which is   lower semicontinuous for a.e. $x\in \mathbb{R}^d$ (first variable) and convex in the second variable, and that there exist constants $c,C>0$ and functions
$\gamma_1,\gamma_2\in L^1(\mathbb R^d)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{cond-f}
c|z|^{p(x)}+\gamma_1(x)
\le
f(x,z)
\le
\gamma_2(x)+C|z|^{p(x)}
\end{equation}
for all $(x,z)\in\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R^d$.
Then the functional

F_s(u)
:=
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
f(x,\nabla^su)\,dx
-
\int_\Omega gu\,dx
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Define
\[
C_s
:=
\inf_{u\in X^{s,p(\cdot)}_{\varphi}(\Omega)}
\left(
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
f(x,\nabla^su)\,dx
-
\int_\Omega gu\,dx
\right).
\]
Since $\varphi\in X^{s,p(\cdot)}_\varphi(\Omega)$, we have $C_s\le F_s(\varphi)$, and using \eqref{cond-f} together with the generalized H\"older inequality we obtain
\begin{align*}
C_s
\le
F_s(\varphi)
&=
\int_{\mathbb R^d} f(x,\nabla^s\varphi)\,dx
-
\int_\Omega g\varphi\,dx
\\
&\le
\|\gamma_2\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^d)}
+
C\int_{\mathbb R^d}|\nabla^s\varphi|^{p(x)}\,dx
+
\int_\Omega |g\varphi|\,dx
\\
&\le
\|\gamma_2\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^d)}
+
C\int_{\mathbb R^d}|\nabla^s\varphi|^{p(x)}\,dx
+
2\|g\|_{L^{q'(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
\ <\ \infty,
\end{align*}
where the last line is finite since $\varphi\in X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)$ and $\varphi\in L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)$ by Theorem \ref{Iq}.
Hence there exists a minimizing sequence
$\{u_n\}\subset X^{s,p(\cdot)}_\varphi(\Omega)$ satisfying

\begin{equation}\label{Cs-limit}
C_s
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\left(
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
f(x,\nabla^su_n)\,dx
-
\int_\Omega gu_n\,dx
\right).
\end{equation}
We claim that the sequences $(u_n)$ and $(\nabla^s u_n)$ are  bounded in $L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)$ and
$L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$, respectively.
The first claim is a consequence of the second, by Theorem \ref{Iq}.
First we should note that, similarly as above and using Young's inequality with $\varepsilon>0$, it holds
\begin{eqnarray}
\int_{\Omega} g u_n \; dx
&\leq & 2 \|g\|_{L^{q'(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
\| u_n\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\notag\\
&\leq & c' \|g\|_{L^{q'(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
\|\nabla^s u_n\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)}
\le  \varepsilon \, \|\nabla^s u_n\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)}^{p^-}+  C(\varepsilon) \, \|g\|_{L^{q'(\cdot)}(\Omega)}^{(p^-)'} \label{help-ineq}
\end{eqnarray}
with positive constants   $c(\varepsilon)$ and $C(\varepsilon)$, such that $c(\varepsilon)$  can be chosen as small as we want.
Then, using \eqref{cond-f} we can deduce that, for any $n$ sufficiently large and in case $\|\nabla^su_n\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)} >1$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_s+1
&\ge
c
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
|\nabla^su_n|^{p(x)}\,dx
+
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
\gamma_1(x)\,dx
-
\int_\Omega gu_n\,dx
\\
&\ge
c\;
\|\nabla^s u_n\|^{p^-}_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)}
+
\|
\gamma_1  \|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^d)}
-\varepsilon \, \|\nabla^s u_n\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)}^{p^-} - C(\varepsilon) \, \|g\|_{L^{q'(\cdot)}(\Omega)}^{(p^-)'},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the second step we used  \cite[Lem.~3.2.5]{DHHR} to estimate the first term on the right hand side and  \eqref{help-ineq} for the last.
Choosing $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small yields the boundedness of the sequence $(\nabla^s u_n)$ in $L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$.


Since the spaces $L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)$ and
$L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ are reflexive,
cf. \cite[Theorem 3.4.7]{DHHR},  the above mentioned bounded sequences admit weak convergent subsequences (see e.g. \cite[Theorem 3.18]{Brezis2011}).

Since $u_n=\varphi$ on $\Omega^c$ by definition, we have $\operatorname{supp}(u_n-\varphi)\subset\overline\Omega$, so that $(u_n-\varphi)$, extended by zero, is also bounded in $L^{q(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$. Hence, up to a subsequence, there exist $u\in L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)$ and $v\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$ such that
\[
u_n-\varphi
\rightharpoonup
u-\varphi
\quad\text{in }
L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega) \quad \text{ and } \quad \nabla^su_n
\rightharpoonup
v
\quad\text{in }
L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d).
\]
We postpone asserting $u\in X^{s,p(\cdot)}_\varphi(\Omega)$ until we have shown $v=\nabla^su$ below; at this stage we only know $u\in L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)$, with $u=\varphi$ on $\Omega^c$.
To see that in fact $u\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ -- needed for $\nabla^su$ to be well-defined per Definition~\ref{def-Xsp} -- note that since $u_n-\varphi\in X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ with $\nabla^s(u_n-\varphi)$ bounded in $L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$, the global Sobolev-type inequality of Theorem~\ref{fractionalSI} gives
\[
\|u_n-\varphi\|_{L^{p^*(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)}\le C\,\|\nabla^s(u_n-\varphi)\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R}^d)}\le C',
\]
uniformly in $n$. Since $p(x)<p^*(x)$ for every $x$ and $\Omega$ is bounded, the embedding $L^{p^*(\cdot)}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)$ shows that $(u_n-\varphi)$ is also bounded in $L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)$; identifying the limit of a further subsequence with $u-\varphi$, we conclude $u-\varphi\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)$, hence $u=\varphi+(u-\varphi)\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^d)$.



It remains to show that $v=\nabla^su$,
after which weak lower semicontinuity yields the desired conclusion.  Precisely, if we can establish this claim, from \eqref{Cs-limit} we conclude
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_s=\lim_{n\to\infty}
\left(
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
f(x,\nabla^su_n)\,dx
-
\int_\Omega gu_n\,dx
\right)
&\ge
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
f(x,\nabla^su_n)\,dx
-
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\int_\Omega gu_n\,dx
\\
&\ge
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
f(x,\nabla^su)\,dx
-
\int_\Omega gu\,dx .
\end{aligned}
\]

Here we have used the weak lower semicontinuity of the first term,  guaranteed by Theorem \ref{thm-low-semicont}, together
with the weak continuity of the linear functional
\(
u\mapsto\int_\Omega gu\,dx
\),
which follows from the duality
\(
\big(L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)\big)^*
=
L^{q'(\cdot)}(\Omega).
\)

It remains to prove that
\[
v=\nabla^su.
\]

For
\(
w\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb R^d)
\),
the definition of the fractional gradient gives, for $j\in\{1, \dots, d\}$,
\[
\left\langle
\frac{\partial^s}{\partial x_j^s}(u_n-\varphi),
w
\right\rangle
=
-
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
I_{1-s}*(u_n-\varphi)
\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_j}\,dx
=
-
\int_\Omega
(u_n-\varphi)
I_{1-s}*\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_j}\,dx,{\footnotemark}
\]
 since
\(
\operatorname{supp}(u_n-\varphi)\subset\overline{\Omega}
\)\textcolor{red}{\footnotetext{The order of integration may be exchanged by Fubini's theorem: since the kernel of the Riesz potential $
\kappa(x):=\gamma(d,1-s)\,|x|^{(1-s)-d}\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^d)$ (since $1-s>0$) and $\partial_{x_j}w$ is bounded with compact support, $\int_{\mathbb R^d}|\kappa(x-y)|\,|\partial_{x_j}w(x)|\,dx$ is bounded uniformly for $y$ in the bounded set $\overline\Omega$; together with $u_n-\varphi\in L^1(\Omega)$, this gives absolute convergence of the double integral.}}.
Passing to the limit using the weak convergence in
\(
L^{q(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)
\),
we obtain
\[
-
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\int_\Omega
(u_n-\varphi)
I_{1-s}*\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_j}\,dx
=
-
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
(u-\varphi)
I_{1-s}*\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_j}\,dx.{\footnotemark}
\]
\footnotetext{Since $\partial_{x_j}w\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb R^d)$, the function $I_{1-s}*\partial_{x_j}w$ is bounded and continuous on $\mathbb R^d$ (its Fourier transform $c|\xi|^{-(1-s)}\widehat{\partial_{x_j}w}(\xi)$ is $L^1$, being locally integrable near $\xi=0$ since $1-s<d$, and rapidly decaying at infinity). Restricted to the bounded set $\Omega$, it therefore belongs to $L^{q'(\cdot)}(\Omega)$, so the limit passes through by definition of the weak convergence $u_n-\varphi\rightharpoonup u-\varphi$ in $L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)$ and the duality $(L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega))^*=L^{q'(\cdot)}(\Omega)$. The domain is then extended to $\mathbb R^d$ on the right using $u-\varphi=0$ a.e.\ on $\Omega^c$.}
Moving the Riesz potential back to the other factor yields
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\left\langle
\frac{\partial^s}{\partial x_j^s}(u_n-\varphi),
w
\right\rangle
=
-
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
I_{1-s}*u
\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_j}\,dx
+
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
I_{1-s}*\varphi
\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_j}\,dx.
\]

On the other hand,
\[
\left\langle
\frac{\partial^s}{\partial x_j^s}(u_n-\varphi),
w
\right\rangle
=
\left\langle
\frac{\partial^s u_n}{\partial x_j^s},
w
\right\rangle
-
\left\langle
\frac{\partial^s\varphi}{\partial x_j^s},
w
\right\rangle.
\]

Since
\(
\partial_{x_j}^su_n
\rightharpoonup
v_j
\)
in
\(
L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)
\),
we have
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\left\langle
\frac{\partial^s}{\partial x_j^s}(u_n-\varphi),
w
\right\rangle
=
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
v_jw\,dx
-
\left\langle
\frac{\partial^s\varphi}{\partial x_j^s},
w
\right\rangle.
\]

Comparing the two identities and cancelling the fractional derivatives of
\(
\varphi
\),
we conclude that
\[
v_j
=
\frac{\partial^su}{\partial x_j^s},
\]
for every
\(j=1,\dots,d\). Since $u\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)$ and $\nabla^su=v\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d;\mathbb{R}^d)$, we conclude $u\in X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)$; together with $u=\varphi$ on $\Omega^c$, this gives $u\in X^{s,p(\cdot)}_\varphi(\Omega)$, which completes the proof.

\label{rem-uniqueness}
If, in addition to the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm-minimizer}, the map
$z\mapsto f(x,z)$ is strictly convex for a.e.\ $x\in\mathbb R^d$, then the
minimizer of \eqref{functional-min} over $X^{s,p(\cdot)}_\varphi(\Omega)$ is
unique. Indeed, let $u_1,u_2$ be two minimizers. Since
$X^{s,p(\cdot)}_\varphi(\Omega)$ is convex, $w:=\frac12(u_1+u_2)\in
X^{s,p(\cdot)}_\varphi(\Omega)$, and $F_s(u_1)=F_s(u_2)=C_s$ is finite by
\eqref{cond-f}. If $\nabla^su_1\neq\nabla^su_2$ on a set of positive measure,
strict convexity of $f(x,\cdot)$ and linearity of the second term in
\eqref{functional-min} give $F_s(w)<\frac12\big(F_s(u_1)+F_s(u_2)\big)=C_s$,
a contradiction. Hence $\nabla^s(u_1-u_2)=0$ a.e., and since
$u_1-u_2\in X^{s,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb R^d)$, Theorem~\ref{fractionalSI} yields
$u_1=u_2$ a.e.

<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
\begin{itemize}
\item The  proof  of Theorem \ref{thm-Euler-Lagrange} differs from the corresponding one in \cite[Thm.~1.18]{SS18} by using  the dominated convergence theorem instead of G\^{a}teaux differentiability.
\item Note that (even in the constant exponent case) this is not to be confused with
existing notions of the fractional $p$-Laplace’s equation from the real interpolation
theory, see e.g. \cite{DCKP16,LL14}.
\item Although the exponent $p(x)$ depends on the spatial variable, no derivatives
of $p$ appear in the Euler--Lagrange equation. Indeed, the first variation is
taken with respect to the variable
\(
z=\nabla^su(x),
\)
while the point $x$ is regarded as a parameter. Consequently,
\(
\nabla_z\!\left(\frac{|z|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\right)
=
|z|^{p(x)-2}z,
\)
exactly as in the constant exponent case. The dependence of $p$ on $x$
therefore influences only the growth of the operator and not its formal
Euler--Lagrange structure.
\end{itemize}
