<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For every $r\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\setminus\{1\}$ and every real $\nu>2$,
there is a positive integer $Q(r,\nu)$ such that
\[
 \left|\log r-\frac pq\right|>q^{-\nu}
 \qquad(p\in\mathbb Z,\ q\in\mathbb Z,\ q\geq Q(r,\nu)).
\]
Consequently $\log r$ is irrational and $\mu(\log r)=2$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:MI}
Fix positive integers $m,K$, positive rational numbers $w_0,v_0$, and
a rational number $0<\theta<1$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:volume}
 K(w_0/v_0)\theta^m<1.
\end{equation}
There are successive lower thresholds for the positive rational weights
$w_1,\ldots,w_m$, depending only on these fixed data and earlier weights,
with the following property. For any pairwise distinct
$y_0,\ldots,y_{K-1}\in\mathbb C^*$ and any $c_{ji}\in\mathbb C$, the map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MI}
 \mathcal P_W(H)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j=0}^{K-1}\mathcal J_V(H),
 \qquad P\longmapsto
 \left(P\bigl(y_j(1+t),(c_{ji}+u_i+\log(1+t))_{i=1}^m\bigr)\right)_j
\end{equation}
is onto for every $H=nR$ with $n\geq n_0$. Here the positive integer
$R$ depends only on $W,V$; the threshold $n_0$ may depend on the centres.
The weight thresholds are independent of all centres.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:length}
In affine $d$-space, assign positive rational degree weights
$\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_d$ to the coordinates. On an open set let
$D_1,\ldots,D_d$ be commuting holomorphic vector fields which form a frame,
with positive costs $\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_d$.
Fix $\epsilon>0$.
Suppose $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ are polynomials of weighted degree at most $N>0$,
and $Z$ is a codimension-$k$ irreducible component of their common zeros.
If
\[
 D^\gamma f_l\big|_Z=0
 \quad\text{whenever}\quad\sum_b\kappa_b\gamma_b<\epsilon N,
\]
then, for every coordinate normal basis $A$ and every frame normal basis $B$
at a common general smooth point of $Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-sharp}
 \prod_{a\in A}\rho_a\leq k!\epsilon^{-k}\prod_{b\in B}\kappa_b.
\end{equation}
A normal basis means that the indicated vectors map to a basis modulo
$T_xZ$. The constant is independent of the polynomials, $Z$, the point,
and the sizes of the local coordinate neighbourhoods.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Choose $x$ smooth on $Z$, away from every other component, where the two
normal-basis conditions hold. Flow from $Z$ along the fields in $B$.
Their commutativity gives local coordinates $(z,b)$ in which these fields
are $\partial/\partial b_j$ and $Z$ is $b=0$. The Taylor expansion is
$f_l(z,b)=\sum_\alpha f_{l,\alpha}(z)b^\alpha$, with
$f_{l,\alpha}\equiv0$ for $\sum_{b\in B}\kappa_b\alpha_b<\epsilon N$.

Take the affine slice $S$ obtained by fixing coordinates outside $A$.
It is transverse to $Z$. The derivative of the map $b$ has kernel $T_xZ$,
so its restriction to $T_xS$ is an isomorphism. Thus $b|_S$ are analytic
local coordinates and the other flow coordinates have the form $z=z(b)$.
Every surviving summand
$f_{l,\alpha}(z(b))b^\alpha$ still has weight at least $\epsilon N$:
the coefficient is a power series with nonnegative exponents. No assumption
on coefficient magnitudes or convergence radii is used for this conclusion.

Let $\mathcal O$ be the analytic local ring of $S$ at $x$, let
$J=(f_1|_S,\ldots,f_r|_S)$, and let $\mathfrak a$ be the monomial ideal of
$b$-weight at least $\epsilon N$. We have $J\subseteq\mathfrak a$, so
\[
 \dim_\mathbb C(\mathcal O/J)\geq\dim_\mathbb C(\mathcal O/\mathfrak a)
 =\#\{\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^k:\textstyle\sum_{b\in B}\kappa_b\alpha_b<\epsilon N\}
 \geq\frac{(\epsilon N)^k}{k!\prod_{b\in B}\kappa_b}.
\]
For the last inequality, the simplex with the displayed strict inequality
is covered by the disjoint unit cubes indexed by the coordinatewise floors
of its points. Every such index is admissible. The number of cubes is at
least the volume of the simplex.

The zero of $J$ is isolated on $S$, hence $J$ is maximal-primary.
Choose $k$ constant linear combinations $g_1,\ldots,g_k$ of the $f_l|_S$
which still have an isolated common zero at $x$. To justify the choice,
at each step avoid the finitely many positive-dimensional components of
the previously chosen equations. None is contained in the common zeros
of all generators of $J$. The forbidden coefficient vectors form finitely
many proper linear subspaces over the infinite field $\mathbb C$.
With $J'=(g_1,\ldots,g_k)\subseteq J$, it follows that
$\dim_\mathbb C(\mathcal O/J)\leq\dim_\mathbb C(\mathcal O/J')$.

The restricted polynomials $g_i$ have degree at most $N$ in the variables
indexed by $A$ with weights $\rho_a$; fixing the other coordinates cannot
increase this degree. Choose an integer $d_0$ for which every $d_0\rho_a$
is a positive integer, and substitute $x_a=\lambda_a+z_a^{d_0\rho_a}$.
Choose $\lambda_a\ne x_a(x)$, so all preimages of $x$ are unramified.
There are $\prod_{a\in A}d_0\rho_a$ of them, and their local intersection
lengths equal that at $x$. Each substituted polynomial has ordinary degree
at most $d_0N$. The affine Bezout bound for isolated zeros, counted with
local intersection length, gives
\[
 \dim_\mathbb C(\mathcal O/J')\leq
 \frac{(d_0N)^k}{\prod_{a\in A}d_0\rho_a}
 =\frac{N^k}{\prod_{a\in A}\rho_a}.
\]
Other components elsewhere do not invalidate this isolated-zero bound.
Polynomial, analytic, and completed local rings give the same length here:
all finite-colength quotients are already determined modulo a power of the
maximal ideal. Combining the bounds and cancelling $N^k$ proves the lemma.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:moving-chart}
For every $a_j=(y_j,c_{j1},\ldots,c_{jm})$ with $y_j\ne0$, the formal
coordinates
\[
 t_j=Y/y_j-1,\qquad
 u_{ji}=X_i-c_{ji}-\log(Y/y_j)
\]
give
\[
 D_0=(1+t_j)\partial_{t_j},\qquad
 D_i=\partial_{u_{ji}},\qquad
 D_0=Y\partial_Y+\sum_i\partial_{X_i},\quad D_i=\partial_{X_i}.
\]
The fields on the right are the same at every centre. They preserve
$\mathcal P_W(N)$, and differentiation by $D_a$ decreases local $V$-order
by at most $v_a$. These statements require no bound on the sizes or mutual
distances of the centres.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The Jacobian of the displayed coordinate change at the centre has
determinant $1/y_j\ne0$. Direct differentiation gives
$D_0t_j=1+t_j$, $D_0u_{ji}=0$, $D_it_j=0$, and
$D_iu_{jl}=\delta_{il}$. The claimed order losses follow on each monomial
$t_j^s u_j^\beta$. The field $Y\partial_Y$ preserves monomial degree,
whereas every $\partial_{X_i}$ decreases it.
Alternatively, the affine change
$(\widetilde Y,\widetilde X)\mapsto(y_j\widetilde Y,c_j+\widetilde X)$
and its inverse preserve each weighted-degree polynomial space.
All logarithms here are formal expansions with constant term zero at
$Y/y_j=1$; no choice of a global branch of $\log Y$ is involved.
There is a different local chart for each centre. This lemma does not
assert the existence of one global change sending all $y_j$ to $1$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:affine-invariance}
If $x$ is irrational, $u\in\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\}$ and
$v\in\mathbb Q$, then $\mu(ux+v)=\mu(x)$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Write $u=A_1/B_1$ and $v=A_2/B_2$ with $B_1,B_2>0$.
A rational approximation $p/q$ to $x$ gives the rational number
\[
 \frac{A_1B_2p+A_2B_1q}{B_1B_2q}
\]
approximating $ux+v$ with error $|u|\,|x-p/q|$. Its reduced denominator
$Q$ is at most $B_1B_2q$. For any exponent $s>0$, an error at most
$q^{-s}$ consequently gives an error at most
$|u|(B_1B_2)^sQ^{-s}$. Along infinitely many approximations tending to
the irrational number $ux+v$, the denominators $Q$ are unbounded.
For every $s'<s$, the fixed factor is absorbed by $Q^{s-s'}$ for all
sufficiently large $Q$. Taking suprema of approximation exponents gives
$\mu(ux+v)\geq\mu(x)$. Applying the same argument to the inverse affine
map gives the opposite inequality, also when either exponent is infinite.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:log-combinations}
Let $r_1,\ldots,r_s$ be positive rational numbers and
$c_1,\ldots,c_s$ rational numbers. If
\[
 \xi=\sum_{k=1}^s c_k\log r_k\ne0,
\]
then $\mu(\xi)=2$. More generally, $\mu(u\xi+v)=2$ for every
$u\in\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\}$ and $v\in\mathbb Q$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Choose a positive integer $D$ with $Dc_k\in\mathbb Z$ for every $k$.
The number
\[
 r_* =\prod_{k=1}^s r_k^{Dc_k}
\]
is positive and rational, including when some exponents are negative.
The real logarithm identity gives $D\xi=\log r_*$ exactly. Since
$\xi\ne0$, $r_*\ne1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:affine-invariance} now give all the conclusions.
The nonzero condition cannot be replaced merely by requiring that some
$c_k\ne0$, because different logarithmic terms can cancel.
<!-- end proof-4 -->

\label{cor:rational-powers}
If $\alpha>0$, $\alpha\ne1$, and

$\mu(\log\alpha)=2$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:finite-degree}
In the setting of Section~\ref{sec:contradiction}, let
\[
 \gamma=\min\{g-E,cL-1-E\}>0,\qquad
 a_0=\max\{1,v_0^{-1}\},\qquad C_0=\log K+2\log a_0+3.
\]
Every $H$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:analytic-threshold}
 H>\max\left\{1,\frac{2cmLS}{A\gamma},
               \frac{16(3m+2)^2}{\gamma^2},\frac{4C_0}{\gamma}\right\}
\end{equation}
satisfies \eqref{eq:finite-conditions}. These bounds are independent of
the selected nonzero minor and its row expansion.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The box bounds and $w_i\geq1$ give
\begin{align*}
 M&\leq K(H/v_0+1)\prod_i(\theta H/w_i+1)
      \leq K a_0(H+1)^{m+1},\\
 Q_H&\leq a_0(H+1)^{2m+1}.
\end{align*}
Consequently, for $H\geq1$,
\[
 \delta_H\leq
 \frac{(3m+2)\log(H+1)+C_0}{H}
 \leq\frac{3m+2}{\sqrt H}+\frac{C_0}{H}.
\]
Here $\log(1+H)\leq\sqrt H$: the difference has nonnegative derivative
because $H+1\geq2\sqrt H$, and is positive at $H=1$.
The last two terms in the maximum \eqref{eq:analytic-threshold} imply
$\delta_H<\gamma/2$.
The elementary inequality $(1+x)^{-m}\geq1-mx$ for $x\geq0$ gives
\[
 c(L-L_H)\leq\frac{cmLS}{AH}<\frac\gamma2.
\]
It follows that
$cL_H>cL-\gamma/2\geq1+E+\gamma/2>1+E+\delta_H$ and
$E+\delta_H<E+\gamma/2<g$, as required.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:fibre-interpolation}
Fix integers $m,K\geq1$ and $1\leq\kappa\leq K$, and positive rational
$w_0,v_0,\theta$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-volumes}
 0<\theta<1,\qquad
 K(w_0/v_0)\theta^m<1,\qquad \kappa\theta^m<1.
\end{equation}
There are successive lower thresholds for positive rational
$w_1,\ldots,w_m$, depending only on these fixed data and earlier weights,
with the following property. Set
$W=(w_0,\ldots,w_m)$ and $V=(v_0,w_1/\theta,\ldots,w_m/\theta)$.
For any centres
\[
 a_j=(y_j,c_{j1},\ldots,c_{jm})\in\mathbb C^*\times\mathbb C^m,\qquad 0\leq j<K,
\]
assume that at most $\kappa$ indices have any given value of $y_j$ and that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:within-fibre}
 j\ne l,\quad y_j=y_l
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 c_{ji}\ne c_{li}\quad\hbox{for every }1\leq i\leq m.
\end{equation}
Then the map \eqref{eq:MI} is onto for every $H=nR$ with $n\geq n_0$.
Here a positive integer $R$ can be chosen using only $W,V$, before the
centres are specified, whereas $n_0$ may depend on the centres as well as
all previously fixed data.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Choose rational $\sigma>0$ so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-sigma}
 \begin{gathered}
 (1+3\sigma)^{m+1}K(w_0/v_0)\theta^m<1,\\
 (1+3\sigma)^m\kappa\theta^m<1,\qquad (1+\sigma)\theta<1.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Such a choice exists by the strict inequalities \eqref{eq:fibre-volumes}.
Using this choice of $\sigma$, take
\[
 \ell_0=m+2,\qquad
 C_*=\max_{1\leq k\leq m}2k!(\ell_0/\sigma)^k,
\]
as in Section~\ref{subsec:moving-curves}.
Enforce \eqref{eq:separation} by the
successive weight choices already justified there. This choice of weights
has no dependence on any centre.

We prove the curve inequality \eqref{eq:curve} for the present centres.
Suppose an irreducible curve $\Gamma$ violates it. Dimension counting
produces $F_N$ for large $N$: the ratio of conditions to coefficients
tends to
$(1+3\sigma)^{m+1}K(w_0/v_0)\theta^m<1$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:moving-chart}, the derivative costs and degree bounds are
identical at all the centres. The zero--pole argument, persistent-component
construction, and normal-basis comparison of
Section~\ref{subsec:moving-curves} therefore apply unchanged up to the
conclusion that $Y$ is constant on $\Gamma$.
Explicitly, the persistent component has
$\prod_{a\in A}w_a\leq C_*\prod_{b\in B}v_b$ for every pair of normal bases.
Together with \eqref{eq:separation}, this either puts all positive coordinate
directions in its tangent space or forces $dX_i=dY/Y$ for some $i$.
The residue argument in that section forces $Y$ to be constant in the
second case as well. No distinctness of the $y_j$ or of the $c_{ji}$ was
used in this part of the proof.

Write this constant as $y$. Since a violating curve meets a centre,
$y\ne0$ and
$J_y=\{j:y_j=y\}$ is nonempty, with cardinality at most $\kappa$.
On the fibre $Y=y$ the contacts are
\[
 h_P=\min_i\frac{\operatorname{ord}_P(X_i-c_{ji})}{v_i}
 \quad(P\text{ lying over }a_j,\ j\in J_y).
\]
All prescribed centres on $\Gamma$ belong to this one fibre. If
$|J_y|=1$, the final zero--pole comparison below applies immediately.
Otherwise use the following argument, which also works when $|J_y|=1$.

In the $m$ variables $X_i$, choose a nonzero polynomial $G_N$ of weighted
degree at most $N$ for the weights $(w_1,\ldots,w_m)$,
with $V'=(v_1,\ldots,v_m)$-order at least
$(1+3\sigma)N$ at all points $(c_{ji})_i$ with $j\in J_y$.
The ratio of conditions to coefficients tends to
\[
 |J_y|(1+3\sigma)^m
 \frac{\prod_i w_i}{\prod_i v_i}
 =|J_y|(1+3\sigma)^m\theta^m<1
\]
by \eqref{eq:fibre-sigma}. Every partial derivative of total cost at most
$\sigma N$ has order at least $(1+2\sigma)Nh_P$ on each relevant branch
and pole degree at most $Nd_W(\Gamma)$. It must vanish on $\Gamma$.
When $m=1$ this is impossible already for $G_N$ itself: a nonzero
polynomial on an affine line cannot vanish on a curve.

For $m\geq2$, use the common-zero sets in this fibre of all
$\partial_X^\gamma G_N$ with cost at most $r\sigma N/\ell_0$,
$0\leq r\leq\ell_0$. Choose nested irreducible components containing the
affine part of $\Gamma$, starting at the last level and proceeding
backwards. Their dimensions are between $1$ and $m-1$; two adjacent
components therefore coincide in $Z'$. The equations at the earlier
level, followed by any derivative of cost less than $\sigma N/\ell_0$,
vanish on $Z'$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:length} in this affine $m$-space,
with degree weights $w_i$, frame $\partial_{X_i}$, and costs $v_i$.
For all coordinate normal bases $A,B$ of $Z'$ it gives
\[
 \prod_{a\in A}w_a\leq C_*\prod_{b\in B}v_b.
\]
There can be only one normal-basis index set. Otherwise order two
different such sets so that the largest index in their symmetric
difference belongs to $A$; \eqref{eq:separation} is then a contradiction.
The unique set is nonempty because $Z'$ has positive codimension.
Choose $i$ in it. All other coordinate directions fail to span the
normal quotient, so $T Z'\subseteq\ker dX_i$ at its general smooth points.
Consequently $X_i$ is constant on $Z'$.
Condition \eqref{eq:within-fibre} implies that $Z'$ meets at most one
prescribed centre in this fibre. Hence $\Gamma$ also meets at most one.

Let that centre be $a_j$. Some $X_l$ is nonconstant on $\Gamma$, since
$Y$ is constant and $\Gamma$ is a curve. The rational function
$X_l-c_{jl}$ has pole degree at most $w_l d_W(\Gamma)$, and its zeros
over $a_j$ have total order at least
$(w_l/\theta)\sum_P h_P$. The zero--pole equality now gives
\[
 d_W(\Gamma)\geq\theta^{-1}\sum_Ph_P
 >(1+\sigma)\sum_Ph_P,
\]
contradicting the assumed violation. This proves the curve inequality
for every curve and every permitted centre configuration.

The centres are distinct as full points, by \eqref{eq:within-fibre}.
Choose $R$ with $R/w_a,R/v_a$ positive integers, using only the weights.
The Taylor cutoffs, weighted projective embedding, and local ideals
from Section~\ref{subsec:packets} are therefore available.
For each fixed configuration the curve inequality makes
$A-(1+\sigma)E$ nef, hence $A-E$ ample. Eventual recovery of the ordinary
Rees powers and Serre vanishing give
$H^1(X,I^n\otimes L^n)=0$ for all $n$ beyond a finite threshold.
Sections of $L^n$ come from embedding polynomials for all sufficiently
large $n$ as well. Taking the maximum of these thresholds gives an $n_0$
such that every $H=nR$, $n\geq n_0$, has the stated surjectivity.
All details of the ordinary-power comparison and passage to the
weighted coefficient quotient were proved in Section~\ref{subsec:packets}.
The possibly centre-dependent choices of $I$, the blow-up, and the
vanishing thresholds occur only after the weights and $R$ have been fixed.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:MI}
Theorem~\ref{thm:fibre-interpolation} recovers \cite[Theorem 2.1]{source}
by taking $y_j=1$ for all $j$ and $\kappa=K$.
If the $y_j$ are pairwise distinct, take $\kappa=1$.
Then $\kappa\theta^m<1$ is automatic and
\eqref{eq:within-fibre} is vacuous. Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:MI} is recovered with no restrictions
on the $c_{ji}$ beyond being complex numbers.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->

These substitutions give precisely the indicated hypotheses and maps.
In the distinct-$Y$ case, a curve with constant $Y$ already meets at most
one centre, so the second auxiliary polynomial $G_N$ can be omitted.
