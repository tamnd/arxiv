\label{clique-estable}
                                             
For each $P \in {\mathcal P}$, adjacencies among vertices in $P$ impose no restriction on the coloring, since exactly one vertex in P is assigned a color. 
This observation generalizes Proposition~2 in \cite{li2000partition} for the PCP. 
Therefore, throughout this paper, each set P is assumed to be either a clique or an independent set, whichever is more convenient for the arguments.

Each list coloring of $G$ induces a double-partition coloring of $(\tilde G, {\mathcal P}, {\mathcal Q})$ with the same number of colors, and conversely.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $c: V \longrightarrow C$ be a list coloring of $G$ and  $\tilde V' = \{(v,c(v)) : v \in V\} \subseteq \tilde{V}$. 
    Consider the color assignment $\tilde c: \tilde V' \to C$ defined by $\tilde c(v,c(v)) = c(v)$.
    Clearly, $\tilde c$ verifies constraints \ref{def:DPCP:C1} and \ref{def:DPCP:C2}, and uses as many colors as $c$.
    To prove that $\tilde c$ is a double-partition coloring of $\tilde G$, it remains to show that $\tilde c$ is a 
             
    proper coloring of $\tilde G$. Indeed, let $(v_1,k_1)$ and $(v_2,k_2)$ be two adjacent colored vertices of $\tilde G$. By the definition of $\tilde E$, either $v_1v_2 \in E$ or $k_1 \neq k_2$. Moreover, observe that $\tilde c(v_1,k_1) = k_1 = c(v_1)$ and $\tilde c(v_2,k_2) = k_2 = c(v_2)$. Thus, if $v_1v_2 \in E$, then $c(v_1) \neq c(v_2)$ as $c$ is a proper coloring of $G$. Otherwise, $k_1 \neq k_2$ must hold.
    In both cases, it follows that $\tilde c (v_1,k_1) \neq \tilde c(v_2,k_2)$.

    For the converse, let $\tilde c$ be a double-partition coloring for $\tilde G$.
    Due to constraint \ref{def:DPCP:C1}, for each $v\in V$ there is a unique $k_v \in L_v$ such that the vertex $(v, k_v) \in P_v$ is colored. 
    Observe that two vertices $(v_1, k_{v_1})$ and $(v_2, k_{v_2})$ have the same color if and only if $k_{v_1} = k_{v_2}$. Indeed, if $k_{v_1} = k_{v_2}$ then $(v_1, k_{v_1}), (v_2, k_{v_2}) \in Q_{k_{v_1}} = Q_{k_{v_2}}$ and constraint \ref{def:DPCP:C2} requires both vertices to have the same color. Otherwise, if $k_{v_1} \neq k_{v_2}$, the vertices are adjacent and must have different colors, as $\tilde c$ is proper. As a consequence, the number of colors required by $\tilde c$ is equal to $|\{k_v : v \in V\}|$.
    
    Now consider the color assignment $ c: V \longrightarrow C$ defined by $c(v)=k_v \in L_v$, which clearly requires the same number of colors as $\tilde c$.
    To prove that $c$ is a proper coloring of $G$, let $v_1,v_2$ be two adjacent vertices from $V$. 
    By the definition of $\tilde E$, $(v_1,k_{v_1})$ and $(v_2,k_{v_2})$ are adjacent in $\tilde G$. 
    Hence, $\tilde c(v_1,k_{v_1})\neq \tilde c(v_2,k_{v_2})$, as $\tilde c$ is a proper coloring of $\tilde G$. 
    Moreover, $k_{v_1} \neq k_{v_2}$ follows from the observation above and thus $c(v_1) \neq c(v_2)$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:3.1}
Given a hypergraph ${\mathcal H} = ({\mathcal V}, {\mathcal E})$ its \textit{conflict graph $G({\mathcal H})$} is the graph whose vertex set is 
$$
V(G({\mathcal H}))=\left\{(E,v): E \in \mathcal {\mathcal E}, v \in E\right\},
$$
and whose edge set is defined as follows:
$$
(E_1,v_1) (E_2,v_2) \in E(G({\mathcal H})) \Leftrightarrow v_1 \neq v_2 \mbox{ and }                                            
(v_1 \in E_2 \mbox{ or } v_2 \in E_1)
$$

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop.cfcp.as.dpcp}
Each conflict-free coloring of ${\mathcal H}$ induces a double-partition coloring of $(G({\mathcal H}), {\mathcal P}, {\mathcal Q})$ with the same number of colors, and conversely. Therefore, $$
\chi_{CF}({\mathcal H})=\chi_{DP}(G({\mathcal H}), {\mathcal P}, {\mathcal Q}).
$$
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $c:{\mathcal V}' \to C$, with ${\mathcal V}' \subseteq {\mathcal V}$, be a conflict-free coloring of ${\mathcal H}$. For each $E \in {\mathcal E}$, let $v_E \in E$ be a uniquely colored vertex in $E$ with respect to $c$. Define 
$
V' = \{(E,v_E) : E \in {\mathcal E}\} \subseteq V(G({\mathcal H})),
$
and let $\tilde c:V' \to C$ be the coloring of $G({\mathcal H})$ defined by $\tilde c(E,v_E) = c(v_E)$.
Clearly, $\tilde c$ uses no more colors than $c$. 
Moreover, by construction, $\tilde c$ satisfies conditions~\ref{def:DPCP:C1} and~\ref{def:DPCP:C2}.

We now show that $\tilde c$ is a proper coloring of $G({\mathcal H})$. 
Let $(E_1,v_{E_1})$ and $(E_2,v_{E_2})$ be adjacent vertices in $V'$. 
By Definition~\ref{def:3.1}, we have $v_{E_1} \neq v_{E_2}$ and either $v_{E_2} \in E_1$ or $v_{E_1} \in E_2$. 
Assume, without loss of generality, that $v_{E_2} \in E_1$. 
Since $v_{E_1}$ is the unique vertex of $E_1$ colored with $c(v_{E_1})$, it follows that $c(v_{E_1}) \neq c(v_{E_2})$, and therefore $\tilde c(E_1,v_{E_1}) \neq \tilde c(E_2,v_{E_2})$. Thus, $\tilde c$ is proper.

For the converse direction, let $\tilde c:\tilde{V}' \to C$, with $\tilde{V}'  \subseteq V(G({\mathcal H}))$, 
be a double-partition coloring of $(G({\mathcal H}),{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$. 
For each $v \in {\mathcal V}$, define
$Q'_v = Q_v \cap \tilde{V}' $,
and let $\mathcal{W} = \{ v \in {\mathcal V} : Q'_v \neq \emptyset \}$.
By condition~\ref{def:DPCP:C2}, all vertices in $Q'_v$ receive the same color. 
Hence the mapping $c:\mathcal{W} \to C$ defined by
$c(v) = \tilde c(E,v), \quad \text{for any } (E,v) \in Q'_v$,
is a well-defined coloring of ${\mathcal H}$ and uses the same number of colors as $\tilde c$.

Finally, we prove that $c$ is conflict-free. In fact, condition~\ref{def:DPCP:C1}states that, for any $E \in {\mathcal E}$, exactly one vertex $(E,v_E) \in P_E$ is colored. 
Thus, $v_E$ is colored in $c$. 
Suppose there exists another colored vertex $v' \in E$, $v' \neq v_E$. Then $(E',v') \in \tilde{V}'$ holds for some $E' \in {\mathcal E}$,  $E' \neq E$. Moreover,  observe that
$(E,v_E)$ and $(E',v')$ are adjacent in $G({\mathcal H})$.
Since $\tilde c$ is proper, $\tilde c(E,v_E) \neq \tilde c(E',v')$, and therefore $c(v_E) \neq c(v')$.
Hence, $v_E$ has a color distinct from all other colored vertices in $E$, 
and $c$ is a conflict-free coloring of ${\mathcal H}$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{nm_igual1}


Instances $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$ with ${\mathcal P} = \{V\}$ are always feasible and $\chi_{DP}(G, {\mathcal P}, {\mathcal Q}) = 1$. Indeed, assigning an arbitrary color to any vertex $v \in V$ yields an optimal double-partition coloring. For instances $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$ with ${\mathcal Q} = \{V\}$, since all vertices must share the same color and each $P \in {\mathcal P}$ is a clique, $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$ is feasible if and only if $\alpha(G) = n$. 
In this case, all colored vertices belong to the same part $Q=V$ and, by \ref{def:DPCP:C2}, must therefore receive the same color. Hence, exactly one color is used, and $\chi_{DP}(G, {\mathcal P}, {\mathcal Q}) = 1$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{th:singletons}
Given $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$, let $P\in{\mathcal P}$ such that $P=\{u\}$ and $v \in  Q_{j(u)}\setminus \{u\}$.

\begin{itemize}
\item If $v\in N(u)$ and $P_{i(v)}=\{v\}$, then $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$ is infeasible. 

\item If $v\in N(u)$ and  $|P_{i(v)}|\geq 2$,  then solving $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$ is equivalent to solving the instance $(G-v,\tilde {\mathcal P}, \tilde {\mathcal Q})$, where 
$
\tilde {\mathcal P}= \{P\setminus \{v\}: P\in {\mathcal P}\}$ and $\tilde {\mathcal Q}= \{Q\setminus \{v\}: Q\in {\mathcal Q}\}$.

\item If $v\notin N(u)$ and $P_{i(v)}=\{v\}$ then solving $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$ is equivalent to solving $(\tilde  G,\tilde{\mathcal P},\tilde {\mathcal Q})$    where $\tilde G$ is the graph obtained by contracting $u$ and  $v$, i.e. $\tilde G=(V\setminus \{v\}, \tilde  E)$, with $\tilde E=(E\setminus \{vw: w\in N(v)\}) \cup \{uw: w\in N(v)\})$,  
                                                        
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           
                            
  
 $\tilde {\mathcal P}={\mathcal P}\setminus \{\{v\}\}$ and $\tilde {\mathcal Q}= \{Q\setminus \{v\}: Q\in {\mathcal Q}\}$.
        
\end{itemize}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since $P=\{u\}$, node $u$ must be colored in order to satisfy condition \ref{def:DPCP:C1}. Moreover, since $v\in Q_{j(u)}$, if $v$ is colored, then it must be assigned to the same color as $u$. 

Therefore, if $v\in N(u)$, nodes $u$ and $v$ cannot both be colored, and hence $v$ must remain uncolored.
    
If, in addition, $P_{i(v)}=\{v\}$, then $v$ must be colored to satisfy condition \ref{def:DPCP:C1} for $P_{i(v)}$, which contradicts the fact that $v$ must remain uncolored. Thus, the instance is infeasible.

If $|P_{i(v)}|\geq 2$, the fact that $v$ must remain uncolored implies that $v$ can be removed from the graph and from all sets in ${\mathcal P}$ and ${\mathcal Q}$. The resulting instance is equivalent to $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$, proving the second statement.

Finally, suppose that $v\notin N(u)$ and $P_{i(v)}=\{v\}$. In this case, $v$ must be colored, and, since $v\in Q_{j(u)}$, both $u$ and $v$ must be assigned to the same color. Hence, $u$ and $v$ can be identified, yielding an equivalent instance obtained by contracting $u$ and $v$ as described in the third statement.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{connectedcomponentsandisolatedvertices}
Let $G$ be a graph with $\ell$ connected components, each denoted by $G_k=(V_k, E_k)$ with $k\in [\ell]$, such that, for every $Q \in {\mathcal Q}$, $Q \subseteq V_k$ for some $k \in [\ell]$.
For every $k \in [\ell]$, let ${\mathcal P}^k = \{P \cap V_k: P \in {\mathcal P}, \,P\cap V_k \neq \emptyset\}$ and ${\mathcal Q}^k = \{Q \cap V_k: Q \in {\mathcal Q}, \, Q\cap V_k \neq \emptyset\}$.
Then, $(G,\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})$ is feasible if and only if $(G_k, \mathcal{P}^k, \mathcal{Q}^k)$ is feasible for every $k\in [\ell]$. Moreover, if $(G,\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})$ is feasible, then
$\chi_{DP}(G, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{Q})=\max \{\chi_{DP}(G_k, \mathcal{P}^k, \mathcal{Q}^k):k\in [\ell]\}$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{Isolated}
Let $G$ be a graph with an isolated vertex $v$ and $|V| \geq 2$.
Define $G' = G \setminus \{v\}$, ${\mathcal P}' = {\mathcal P} \setminus \{\{v\}\}$, and ${\mathcal Q}' = \{ Q \setminus \{v\} : Q \in {\mathcal Q}\}$.
Then, $(G,\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})$ is feasible if and only if $(G', \mathcal{P}', \mathcal{Q}')$ is feasible.
Moreover, if $(G', \mathcal{P}', \mathcal{Q}')$ is feasible,
$\chi_{DP}(G, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{Q}) = \chi_{DP}(G', \mathcal{P}', \mathcal{Q}')$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma.feasible.solutions}
The set of colored vertices of any double-partition coloring of $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$ is a feasible set of representatives of $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$. Conversely, if $W$ is a feasible set of representatives,  assigning one color for each set $W \cap Q$, $Q \in {\mathcal Q}$, yields a double-partition coloring of $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$. Therefore, 
$(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$ is feasible if and only if                                                                                                              
there exists a feasible set of representatives.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop.2.step.heuristic}
For any feasible instance $(G, {\mathcal P}, {\mathcal Q})$ of DPCP, $$\chi_{DP}(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})=\min \{\chi(G[W]/{\mathcal Q}): W \text{ is a feasible set of representatives} \}.$$
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
It is enough to prove that the minimum number of colors in a double-partition coloring defined on a feasible set of representatives $W$ is $\chi(G[W]/{\mathcal Q})$. 

A double-partition coloring of $(G, {\mathcal P}, {\mathcal Q})$ defined on $W$ corresponds to a total proper coloring of $G[W]/{\mathcal Q}$ in which each vertex $w_j \in V(G[W]/{\mathcal Q})$ is assigned the unique color used in $Q_j$. 
Conversely, since $W\cap Q$ is a stable set of $G$ for every $Q\in {\mathcal Q}$, a total proper coloring of $G[W]/{\mathcal Q}$ corresponds to a double-partition coloring of $(G, {\mathcal P}, {\mathcal Q})$ in which each $v \in W$ is assigned the color of $w_{j(v)}$. 
Both constructions preserve the number of colors used.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{Gtilde} Given  $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$ with $G=(V,E)$, let $\tilde{G}=(V,\tilde{E})$, with 
$\tilde{E} = \{uv \in E: i(u) = i(v) \vee j(u) = j(v)\}$.
Then, $(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$ is feasible if and only if $\alpha(\tilde{G})= n$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
It is clear that $\alpha(\tilde G)\leq n$, as any set of more than $n$ vertices contains at least two vertices belonging to the same $P\in {\mathcal P}$, which are adjacent in $\tilde{G}$.

Let $(G, {\mathcal P}, {\mathcal Q})$ be a feasible instance of DPCP. By the previous lemma, there exists a set of representatives $W$ such that, for all $Q \in {\mathcal Q}$, $W \cap Q$ is a stable set of $G$. Then, for any $u, v \in W$ we have $i(u)\neq i(v) \text{ and } j(u)\neq j(v)$, i.e., $uv \notin \tilde{E}$. Therefore, $W$ is a stable set of $\tilde{G}$ and $\left| W \right| = n$. We can conclude that $\alpha(\tilde{G}) = n$.

Conversely, assume $\alpha(\tilde{G}) = n$ and let $S \subseteq V$ be a stable set of maximum cardinality in $\tilde{G}$. Thus, we have $\left| S \cap P \right| = 1$ for all $P \in {\mathcal P}$.
Furthermore, consider two nodes  $u, v \in S \cap Q$, for some $Q\in {\mathcal Q}$. Since $S$ is a stable set in $\tilde{G}$, $uv \notin \tilde{E}$ and it follows that $uv \notin E$. Therefore, $S \cap Q$ is a stable set of $G$ for each $Q \in {\mathcal Q}$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
The pricing problem is NP-complete.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
It is immediate that the pricing problem belongs to NP. We prove NP-hardness by reduction from the decision version of the Weighted Stable Set Problem (WSSP).

An instance $(G,w,k)$ of the WSSP consists of a graph $G=(V,E)$, a vector $w \in \mathbb{Z}_+^V$ of nonnegative integer vertex weights, and an integer $k \in \mathbb{Z}_+$. The problem asks whether $G$ contains a stable set of weight at least $k$.

Consider the pricing problem defined on the same graph $G$, with partitions ${\mathcal P} = \{\{v\} : v \in V\}$ and ${\mathcal Q} = \{V\}$, and dual variables defined by $\gamma_v = w_v/(k-1)$ for all $v \in V$, and $\mu_V = 0$.
Then, for any stable set $S \in {\mathscr S}(G)$, we have
$\bar{c}_S = 1 - \frac{w(S)}{k-1}$.
Since $w(S)$ and $k$ are integers, there exists a stable set with $w(S) \ge k$ if and only if there exists a stable set with $\bar{c}_S < 0$. This completes the reduction.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{remark.M}
For any $M \in {\mathbb Z}_+$, let $(x^*, t^*)$ be an optimal solution of $\text{LR}^{\text{M}}_{\text{ext}}$ with optimal value $z^*_{\mathrm{ext}}$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $t^* = 0$, then $x^*$ is an optimal solution of $\text{LR}$.
    
    \item Since $\min\{n,m\}$ is an upper bound on the optimal value of $\text{LR}$, it follows that if $t^* > 0$ and $z^*_{\mathrm{ext}} > \min\{n,m\}$, then $\text{LR}$ is infeasible.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{Prop.Equiv.Formulations}
\text{DPCP-SCF}\ is equivalent to the formulation $\mathrm{ILP}^{\mathrm{E}}$ proposed in \cite{Furini2018} for the PCP, and to the formulation WLCP-SC proposed in \cite{Lucci2024} for the LCP, in the following sense:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item \label{prop:formulations:a}
    Let $I=(G,{\mathcal P})$ be an instance of the PCP, with $G=(V,E)$, and let $I'=(G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$ be the corresponding DPCP instance with ${\mathcal Q}=\{\{v\}:v\in V\}$. Then, formulation $\mathrm{ILP}^{\mathrm{E}}$ for $I$ coincides with formulation \text{DPCP-SCF}\ for $I'$.

    \item \label{prop:formulations:b}
    Let $I=(G,C,\{L_v:v\in V\})$ be an instance of the LCP, with $G=(V,E)$, and assume that $I$ contains no indistinguishable colors, i.e., that the sets
    $V^k=\{v\in V:k\in L_v\}$
    are distinct for all $k\in C$. Let $I'$ be the corresponding DPCP instance constructed as described in Section~\ref{sec:definition:dpcp}. Then, formulation WLCP-SC for $I$ coincides with formulation \text{DPCP-SCF}\ for $I'$, provided that all color weights are equal to one.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For part~\ref{prop:formulations:a}, observe that $|Q|=1$ holds for every $Q\in{\mathcal Q}$ in instance $I'$. Therefore, the family of constraints~\eqref{SCFormulationB2} is empty, and \text{DPCP-SCF}\ coincides with the formulation $\mathrm{ILP}^{\mathrm{E}}$ proposed in \cite{Furini2018}.

For part~\ref{prop:formulations:b}, following the construction introduced in Section~\ref{sec:definition:dpcp}, let $I'$ denote the DPCP instance $(\tilde G,{\mathcal P},{\mathcal Q})$, where $\tilde G=(\tilde V,\tilde E)$ is the graph with vertex set
$\tilde V=\{(v,k):v\in V,\ k\in L_v\}$,
and edge set $\tilde E$ such that $(v_1,k_1)(v_2,k_2)\in\tilde E$ if and only if either $v_1v_2\in E$ or $k_1\neq k_2$. Moreover, 
${\mathcal P} = \{P_v:v\in V\}$ and  
${\mathcal Q} = \{Q_k:k\in C\}$, where
$P_v=\{(v',k)\in\tilde V: v'=v\}$ and 
$Q_k=\{(v,k')\in\tilde V: k' = k\}$.

For each $k\in C$, let $G_k=G[V^k]$ be the subgraph of $G$ induced by $V^k$.
Since $I$ has no indistinguishable colors, the set $K$ of equivalence classes introduced in \cite{Lucci2024} coincides with $C$, and $m(k)=1$ for every $k\in C$. Consequently, the decision variables of WLCP-SC are indexed by
$X=\{(S,k):k\in C,\ S\in{\mathscr S}(G_k)\}$.
We first establish a bijection between $X$ and ${\mathscr S}(\tilde G)$.

Let $\tilde S\in{\mathscr S}(\tilde G)$ with $\tilde S\neq\emptyset$. Then there exists a unique $k\in C$ such that $\tilde S\subseteq Q_k$. Indeed, if $(v_1,k_1),(v_2,k_2)\in\tilde S$ with $k_1\neq k_2$, then $(v_1,k_1)$ and $(v_2,k_2)$ are adjacent in $\tilde G$, contradicting the stability of $\tilde S$. Define $S=\{v\in V:(v,k)\in\tilde S\}$.
Then $S\subseteq V^k$ and $S\in{\mathscr S}(G_k)$, since adjacency in $G_k$ would imply adjacency in $\tilde G$. Hence, $(S,k)\in X$.

Conversely, let $(S,k)\in X$ and define
$
\tilde S=\{(v,k):v\in S\}
$.
                                                                                                          
Then $\tilde S\subseteq\tilde V$, and $\tilde S\in{\mathscr S}(\tilde G)$, since adjacency in $\tilde G$ would imply adjacency in $G_k$, contradicting the stability of $S$.

Thus, there is a bijection between the variables $x_{\tilde S}$ in \text{DPCP-SCF}\ and the variables $x_S^k$ in WLCP-SC.
Moreover, under this correspondence, the condition $\tilde S\cap P_v\neq\emptyset$ is equivalent to $v\in S$, so constraints~\eqref{SCFormulationA} correspond to constraints~(1) of WLCP-SC. Similarly, the condition $\tilde S\cap Q_k\neq\emptyset$ is equivalent to $S\in{\mathscr S}(G_k)$, and since $m(k)=1$, constraints~\eqref{SCFormulationB} correspond to constraints~(2) of WLCP-SC.

Finally, when all color weights in WLCP-SC are equal to one, the objective functions of the two formulations coincide.
