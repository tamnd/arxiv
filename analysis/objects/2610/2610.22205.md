\label{thm:main}
  \leavevmode
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Alph*)},leftmargin=*]
  \item
    The Rado graph and the countable symplectic space over $\mathbb{F}_2$, which is the Pauli group on countably many qubits modulo phases, are simple and unstable (\cref{thm:rado-simple,thm:symp-simple}).
  \item
    Let a stabilizer code be given, with stabilizer labels $S$ and logical operators $L$.
    An undetectable error is a logical error exactly when its type over $S\cup L$ forks over $S$, and all logical errors have the same type over $S$, which does not fork over the empty set.
     Clifford encoding circuits implement automorphisms of the Fraïssé limit, and the independence theorem is the gluing of syndromes (\cref{thm:witt-clifford,thm:forking-pauli}, and \cref{prop:logical,prop:glue}).
    The probes of the Pauli observable are the Pauli channels, and the noise of a memory on countably many qubits is carried by idealized errors (\cref{prop:channels}).
  \item
    For every diagonal observable whose pure responses have the independence property,
    and in particular for the Rado observable and the Pauli observable, the real-valued theory of its mixed preparations, probes and response has the tree property of the second kind, witnessed by the difference of two responses.
    Hence a simple diagonal observable is NIP (\cref{thm:collapse}).
  \item
    For the Pauli observable, the indicator of every syndrome sector is the difference of the responses of two uniform mixtures, and the tree property of the second kind is witnessed by actual preparations:
    the position of the first check to fire in a block, for instance the first domain wall of a bit flip in a repetition code, is a dividing condition (\cref{prop:sector}, \cref{thm:pauli-tp2} and \cref{exa:repetition}).
  \item
    For the Rado observable, and more generally for every observable whose pure responses are freely independent,
    such as the random bipartite graph, the conditions on a probe given by instances of a quantifier-free formula with actual mixtures as parameters are compatible along every sequence:
    no such sequence is uniformly $k$-inconsistent, for any $k$.
    Hence the tree property of the second kind in~(C) is witnessed only by idealized preparations (\cref{thm:free,thm:free-qf}, and \cref{cor:rado}).
  \end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:dividing}
  Let $\varphi(x,y)$ be a formula and let an integer $k\ge2$ be given.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
  \item
    A sequence $(b_j)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}$ is \emph{$k$-inconsistent for $\varphi$} if for all $j_1<\dots<j_k$ there is no $x$ with $\varphi(x,b_{j_1})\wedge\dots \wedge\varphi(x,b_{j_k})$.
  \item
    The formula $\varphi$ has the \emph{tree property} if, for some
    $k\ge2$, there is a tree $(b_s)_{s\in\mathbb{N}^{<\mathbb{N}}}$ such that for every $\eta\in\mathbb{N}^\mathbb{N}$ the set $\{\varphi(x,b_{\eta\restriction n}):n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ is consistent and for every $s$ the sequence $(b_{s^\frown j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}$ is $k$-inconsistent for $\varphi$.
  \item
    The formula $\varphi$ has the \emph{tree property of the first kind} if there is a tree $(b_s)_{s\in\mathbb{N}^{<\mathbb{N}}}$ such that for every $\eta\in\mathbb{N}^\mathbb{N}$ the set $\{\varphi(x,b_{\eta\restriction n}):n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ is consistent and for all incomparable $s,t\in\mathbb{N}^{<\mathbb{N}}$ the set $\{\varphi(x,b_s),\varphi(x,b_t)\}$ is inconsistent.
  \item
    The formula $\varphi$ has the \emph{tree property of the second kind}
    (TP$_2$) if, for some $k\ge2$, there is an array $(b_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}$ such that every row $(b_{ij})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}$ is $k$-inconsistent for $\varphi$ and for every $f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ the set $\{\varphi(x,b_{if(i)}):i\in\mathbb{N}\}$ is consistent.
  \item
    The theory $T$ is \emph{simple} if no formula has the tree property.
  \end{enumerate}

<!-- fact-1: body_md -->
\label{fact:kp}
  Let $\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}$ be a ternary relation on small subsets of $\mathbb{M}$, written $A\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_C B$, and assume that it satisfies the following:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
  \item
    \emph{invariance} under automorphisms of $\mathbb{M}$;
  \item
    \emph{symmetry}:
    the relation $A\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_C B$ holds if and only if $B\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_C A$ holds;
  \item
    \emph{monotonicity and transitivity}:
    the relation $A\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_C BD$ holds if and only if both $A\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_C B$ and $A\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_{CB}D$ hold;
  \item
    \emph{finite character}:
    the relation $A\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_C B$ holds if and only if $a\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_C b$ holds for all finite tuples $a$ from $A$ and $b$ from $B$;
  \item
    \emph{local character}:
    for every finite tuple $a$ and every set $B$ there is $B_0\subseteq B$ with $|B_0|\le|T|$ and $a\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_{B_0}B$;
  \item
    \emph{extension}:
    for every tuple $a$ and all sets $C\subseteq B$ there is $a'$ with $\operatorname{tp}(a'/C)=\operatorname{tp}(a/C)$ and $a'\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_C B$;
  \item
    \emph{independence theorem over models}:
    if $M$ is a model, $B\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_M D$, $a_1\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_M B$, $a_2\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_M D$ and $\operatorname{tp}(a_1/M)=\operatorname{tp}(a_2/M)$, then there is $a$ with $\operatorname{tp}(a/MB)=\operatorname{tp}(a_1/MB)$, $\operatorname{tp}(a/MD)=\operatorname{tp}(a_2/MD)$ and $a\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_M BD$.
  \end{enumerate}
  Then the theory $T$ is simple and the relation $\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}$ coincides with nonforking independence (see \cite[Theorem~4.2]{Kim-Pillay:1997}).

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:rado-simple}
  The theory of the Rado graph is simple, and the relation $A\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_C B$ holds if and only if $A\cap B\subseteq C$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We verify the hypotheses of \cref{fact:kp}.
  The first five are set algebra, and extension and the independence theorem follow from the extension property of $\mathbb{M}$.
  Invariance, symmetry and finite character are immediate.
  Monotonicity and transitivity hold together, since the conditions $A\cap B\subseteq C$ and $A\cap D\subseteq C\cup B$ say jointly that every element of $A\cap(B\cup D)$ lies in $C$.
  For local character, given a finite tuple $a$ and a set $B$, the finite set $B_0=B\cap a$ satisfies $a\cap B\subseteq B_0$, which is the relation $a\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_{B_0}B$.

  For extension, let $a$ and $C\subseteq B$ be given, and write $a=(c,a_0)$, where $c$ lists the entries of $a$ in $C$.
  The type of $a_0$ over $C$ prescribes only the equalities and edges of $a_0$ within itself and to $C$.
  Hence the extension property of $\mathbb{M}$, applied one entry at a time with the excluded set enlarged by $B\setminus C$, gives a tuple $a_0'$ disjoint from $B$ with the same type over $C$, and the tuple $a'=(c,a_0')$ satisfies $a'\cap B\subseteq C$.

  For the independence theorem, let $M$, $B$, $D$, $a_1$ and $a_2$ be as in \cref{fact:kp}(vii).
  Since $\operatorname{tp}(a_1/M)=\operatorname{tp}(a_2/M)$, the two tuples have the same entries in $M$ and the same remaining equalities;
  write $a_\ell=(m,a_\ell')$ with $a_\ell'$ disjoint from $M$.
  The hypotheses $a_1\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_MB$ and $a_2\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_MD$ say that $a_1'$ is disjoint from $B$ and $a_2'$ from $D$.
  We look for a tuple $x$, disjoint from $M\cup B\cup D$, whose edges within itself and to $M$ are those of $a_1'$ (equivalently, of $a_2'$), whose edges to $B\setminus M$ are those of $a_1'$, and whose edges to $D\setminus M$ are those of $a_2'$.
  These prescriptions concern disjoint sets of vertices, because $B\cap D\subseteq M$;
  the extension property of $\mathbb{M}$ therefore provides such an $x$, and by quantifier elimination the tuple $a=(m,x)$ is as required.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:generic}
  If $a_1,\dots,a_n\in V$ are linearly independent and $\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_n\in\mathbb{F}_2$, then there is $b\in V$ with $\omega(a_i,b)=\varepsilon_i$ for $i=1,\dots,n$.

The vectors $a_i$ lie in a finite-dimensional subspace $W$ spanned by part of the symplectic basis, and the form is nondegenerate on $W$.
  Hence the functionals $\omega(a_i,\cdot)$ restricted to $W$ are linearly independent, and the linear map $b\mapsto(\omega(a_i,b))_{i\le n}$ from $W$ to $\mathbb{F}_2^n$ is onto.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:symp-simple}
  The theory of $(V,\omega)$ is simple, and the relation $A\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_C B$ holds if and only if $\operatorname{span}(AC)\cap\operatorname{span}(BC)=\operatorname{span}(C)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The proof follows that of \cref{thm:rado-simple}, with linear span in place of equality:
  the first five axioms are linear algebra, and extension and the independence theorem come from the freedom to prescribe the form on a complement.
  We verify the hypotheses of \cref{fact:kp}.
  Invariance and symmetry are immediate, and finite character holds because a vector of $\operatorname{span}(AC)\cap\operatorname{span}(BC)$ outside $\operatorname{span}(C)$ is a combination of finitely many elements of $A$, $B$ and $C$.

  For monotonicity and transitivity, put $Q_1=\operatorname{span}(AC)$, $Q_2=\operatorname{span}(BC)$, $Q_3=\operatorname{span}(BCD)$ and $Q_0=\operatorname{span}(C)$;
  these subspaces satisfy $Q_2\subseteq Q_3$ and $\operatorname{span}(ABC)=Q_1+Q_2$.
  It remains to be shown that $Q_1\cap Q_3=Q_0$ if and only if $Q_1\cap Q_2=Q_0$ and $(Q_1+Q_2)\cap Q_3=Q_2$.
  Suppose that $Q_1\cap Q_3=Q_0$.
  Since $Q_2\subseteq Q_3$, the intersection $Q_1\cap Q_2$ is contained in $Q_0$.
  If $u_1+u_2\in Q_3$ with $u_1\in Q_1$ and $u_2\in Q_2$, then the vector $u_1$ also lies in $Q_3$, hence in $Q_1\cap Q_3=Q_0\subseteq Q_2$;
  thus the sum lies in $Q_2$.
  Conversely, every $u\in Q_1\cap Q_3$ lies in $(Q_1+Q_2)\cap Q_3=Q_2$, and therefore in $Q_1\cap Q_2=Q_0$.

  Local character follows from finite dimension.
  Given a finite tuple $a$ and a set $B$, the space $\operatorname{span}(a)\cap\operatorname{span}(B)$ is finite, so it is contained in $\operatorname{span}(B_0)$ for some finite $B_0\subseteq B$.
  If $u+w\in\operatorname{span}(B)$ with $u\in\operatorname{span}(a)$ and $w\in\operatorname{span}(B_0)$, then the vector $u$ lies in $\operatorname{span}(a)\cap\operatorname{span}(B)\subseteq\operatorname{span}(B_0)$.
  It follows that $\operatorname{span}(aB_0)\cap\operatorname{span}(B)=\operatorname{span}(B_0)$, which is the relation $a\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_{B_0}B$.

  For extension, let $a$ and $C\subseteq B$ be given.
  Replacing $a$ by a maximal subtuple linearly independent over $\operatorname{span}(C)$, we may assume that $a$ is linearly independent over $\operatorname{span}(C)$, since the remaining entries are then determined.
  Choose a complement $B'$ of $\operatorname{span}(C)$ in $\operatorname{span}(BC)$.
  By saturation, the alternating space $\operatorname{span}(BC)\oplus\operatorname{span}(x)$ in which $x$ has the values of $\omega$ prescribed by $\operatorname{tp}(a/C)$ on $\operatorname{span}(xC)$ and is orthogonal to $B'$ is realized over $\operatorname{span}(BC)$ by a tuple $a'$;
  this tuple has the type of $a$ over $C$ and is linearly independent over $\operatorname{span}(BC)$.

  The independence theorem is a gluing of functionals.
  Let $M$, $B$, $D$, $a_1$ and $a_2$ be as in \cref{fact:kp}(vii).
  As before, we may assume that $a_1$ is linearly independent over $M$;
  then so is $a_2$, and the hypotheses $a_1\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_MB$ and $a_2\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_MD$ make $a_1$ linearly independent over $\operatorname{span}(MB)$ and $a_2$ over $\operatorname{span}(MD)$.
  For each $k$, the functionals $\omega(a_{1,k},\cdot)$ on $\operatorname{span}(MB)$ and $\omega(a_{2,k},\cdot)$ on $\operatorname{span}(MD)$ agree on $M$, because $\operatorname{tp}(a_1/M)=\operatorname{tp}(a_2/M)$.
  Since $B\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_MD$, their domains meet in $\operatorname{span}(MB)\cap\operatorname{span}(MD)=M$, and the two functionals glue to a linear functional on $\operatorname{span}(MBD)$.
  Realize over $\operatorname{span}(MBD)$ a tuple $a$, linearly independent over $\operatorname{span}(MBD)$, with these functionals and with the values of $\omega$ on $a$ given by $\operatorname{tp}(a_1/M)$.
  By quantifier elimination, the type of $a$ over $MB$ is that of $a_1$, and its type over $MD$ is that of $a_2$;
  the relation $a\mathrel{\mathpalette\di@Ind{}}_MBD$ holds by construction.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:witt-clifford}
  Let $W$ and $W'$ be subspaces of $\mathbb{F}_2^{2n}$ and let $f\colon W\to W'$ be a linear bijection with $\omega(f(v),f(w))=\omega(v,w)$ for all $v,w\in W$.
  Then there is a Clifford unitary $U$ on $n$ qubits with $UP_vU^*=\pm P_{f(v)}$ for all $v\in W$.
  If, moreover, the subspace $W$ is isotropic and the vectors $v_1,\dots,v_m$ form a basis of $W$, then the unitary $U$ can be chosen with $UP_{v_i}U^*=P_{f(v_i)}$ for all $i\le m$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By the theorem of Witt, the map $f$ extends to a linear bijection $\tau$ of $\mathbb{F}_2^{2n}$ that preserves $\omega$.
  Every such $\tau$ is induced by conjugation by a Clifford unitary \cite{Calderbank-Rains-Shor-Sloane:1998,Dehaene-deMoor:2003};
  let $U$ be one.
  Since the operator $UP_vU^*$ is a Pauli string up to a phase, Hermitian and with square $I$, the phase is a sign.
  For the second statement, let $\varepsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}$ be the sign with $UP_{v_i}U^*=\varepsilon_iP_{f(v_i)}$.
  The vectors $f(v_i)$ are linearly independent, so by nondegeneracy there is $b\in\mathbb{F}_2^{2n}$ with $(-1)^{\omega(f(v_i),b)}=\varepsilon_i$ for all $i$.
  Conjugation by $P_b$ multiplies $P_{f(v_i)}$ by $(-1)^{\omega(f(v_i),b)}$, so the Clifford unitary $P_bU$ has the required property.

\label{def:family-tp}

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:channels}
  Let $\hat{V}$ be the group of labels of Pauli strings of arbitrary support, and for $0<p\le1$ let $\nu_p$ be the depolarizing noise of rate $p$.
  Then the following hold:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item
    the map $u\mapsto\omega(\cdot,u)$ is an isomorphism of topological groups from $\hat{V}$ onto $V^*$, which carries $V$ onto the functionals of the actual errors;
  \item
    the probes of the Pauli observable are the Pauli channels on countably many qubits, and the response of a mixture $\mu$ at a Pauli channel $\nu$ is the probability that a check drawn with law $\mu$ anticommutes with an independent error drawn with law $\nu$;
  \item
    the depolarizing noise $\nu_p$ is concentrated on errors of infinite support, and for a label $a\in V$ that acts nontrivially on $w$ qubits we have
    \[
      r_{\delta_a}(\nu_p)=\tfrac12\bigl(1-(1-\tfrac{4p}3)^w\bigr).
    \]
  \end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
(a) The map is a homomorphism.
  Write $X_k$ and $Z_k$ for the labels of the strings $X$ and $Z$ on the qubit $k$.
  Then the values $\omega(X_k,u)$ and $\omega(Z_k,u)$ are the two coordinates of
  $u$ at the qubit $k$, so the map is injective, and every
  $\lambda\in V^*$ is the image of the vector $u=(x,z)$ with $z_k=\lambda(X_k)$ and $x_k=\lambda(Z_k)$ for every $k$, because $\lambda$ and $\omega(\cdot,u)$ are linear and agree on the basis $\{X_k,Z_k:k\in\mathbb{N}\}$ of $V$.
  The value $\omega(a,u)$ depends on finitely many coordinates of $u$, so the map is continuous, and a continuous bijection between compact Hausdorff spaces is a homeomorphism.

  (b) By \cref{lem:probe-space}, \cref{lem:pauli-probes} and~(a), the probes are the Radon probability measures on $\hat{V}$, and $r_\mu(\nu)=\int\sum_a\mu(a)\,\omega(a,u)\,d\nu(u)$, which is the probability stated, by Fubini’s theorem.

  (c) Under $\nu_p$, the events “the error acts nontrivially on the qubit $k$” are independent and have probability $p$, so, by the lemma of Borel and Cantelli, almost every error acts nontrivially on infinitely many qubits.
  At each of the $w$ qubits where $P_a$ acts nontrivially, it anticommutes with two of the three nontrivial Pauli matrices, hence with the error with probability $2p/3$, independently of the other qubits.
  The strings anticommute exactly when an odd number of these $w$ events occur, and this has probability $\frac12\bigl(1-(1-\frac{4p}3)^w\bigr)$.

<!-- keytheorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:collapse}
  Let $C_\varphi$ be a diagonal observable, and assume that there are distinct labels
  $a_0,a_1,\ldots$ in $A$ such that for all finite disjoint $N_1,N_0\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ there is a label $b\in B$ with $\varphi(a_n,b)=1$ for $n\in N_1$ and $\varphi(a_n,b)=0$ for $n\in N_0$.
  Then the affine formula
  \[
    \psi(x;y_1,y_2)=R(y_1,x)-R(y_2,x),
  \]
  with $x$ of the sort of probes and $y_1,y_2$ of the sort of preparations, has the tree property of the second kind in the real-valued theory of $\mathcal{S}_\varphi$, with $k=2$, $\alpha=1/2$ and $\beta=1$.
  In particular, a simple diagonal observable has no independence property.
<!-- end keytheorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
We first build finite arrays in $\mathcal{S}_\varphi$ itself and then pass to an elementary extension by compactness.

  Let $N\ge1$ be given, and choose $t$ with $2^t>N$.
  Partition $2^t N$ of the labels $a_n$ into $N$ blocks $J_0,\dots,J_{N-1}$ of size $2^t$, and identify each block with $\mathbb{F}_2^t$.
  Choose distinct nonzero vectors $w_0,\dots,w_{N-1}$ in $\mathbb{F}_2^t$, and for $i,j<N$ let $H_{ij}\subseteq J_i$ be the set of $v\in\mathbb{F}_2^t$ with $w_j\cdot v=1$.
  Each $H_{ij}$ has $2^{t-1}$ elements, and for $j\ne j'$ the set $H_{ij}\cap H_{ij'}$ has $2^{t-2}$ elements, since $w_j$ and $w_{j'}$ are linearly independent.
  Let $\mu^+_{ij}$ and $\mu^-_{ij}$ be the uniform probability measures on $H_{ij}$ and on $J_i\setminus H_{ij}$.

  For a probe $\nu$, write $\pi_a(\nu)=\nu\{\lambda:\lambda(a)=1\}\in[0,1]$ for its marginal at the label $a$;
  by \cref{lem:probe-space}, the response is then $R(\mu,\nu)=\sum_a\mu(a)\pi_a(\nu)$.
  With $m=2^t$, we have
  \[
    \psi(\nu;\mu^+_{ij},\mu^-_{ij})=\frac2m\Bigl(\sum_{a\in H_{ij}}\pi_a(\nu)
    -\sum_{a\in J_i\setminus H_{ij}}\pi_a(\nu)\Bigr)\le1 .
  \]
  For $j\ne j'$ in the same row, adding the two values gives
  \[
    \frac4m\Bigl(\sum_{a\in H_{ij}\cap H_{ij'}}\pi_a(\nu)-\sum_{a\in J_i\setminus(H_{ij}\cup
      H_{ij'})}\pi_a(\nu)\Bigr)\le\frac4m\cdot\frac m4=1 ,
  \]
  so the two values are never both greater than $1/2$.
  For every $f\colon N\to N$, the hypothesis applied to the finite disjoint sets
  $\bigcup_iH_{if(i)}$ and $\bigcup_i(J_i\setminus H_{if(i)})$ gives a label $b$, and at the pure probe of $\varphi(\cdot,b)$ every value $\psi(\cdot;\mu^+_{if(i)},\mu^-_{if(i)})$ with $i<N$ equals $1$.

  Thus, for every $N$, the structure $\mathcal{S}_\varphi$ satisfies the following conditions on $2N^2$ preparations $y^\pm_{ij}$:
  for every $f\colon N\to N$, the value of $\sup_x\min_{i<N}\psi(x;y^+_{if(i)},y^-_{if(i)})$ is $1$;
  and for all $i$ and $j\ne j'$, the value of $\sup_x\min\bigl(\psi(x;y^+_{ij},y^-_{ij}),\psi(x;y^+_{ij'},y^-_{ij'})\bigr)$ is at most $1/2$.
  These are finitely many closed conditions for each $N$, and the conditions for $N$ are inherited by subarrays.
  By compactness, an $\aleph_1$-saturated elementary extension of $\mathcal{S}_\varphi$ contains an array $(y^\pm_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}$ satisfying them for all $N$;
  by saturation, the suprema along paths are attained.
  This is the tree property of the second kind, which is incompatible with simplicity in real-valued logic \cite{BenYaacov:2003b}.

  It remains to be shown that a simple diagonal observable has no independence property.
  The hypothesis of the theorem is the independence property of the pure responses, in the form used in the second paper:
  actual labels are dense in $K_\varphi$, and a finite pattern of answers to the labels
  $a_n$ prescribes a clopen subset of $K_\varphi$, so the hypothesis says exactly that the functions $\lambda\mapsto\lambda(a_n)$ form an independent sequence on $K_\varphi$.
  For a diagonal observable, the pure and the mixed responses have the independence property together, since a mixed response is an average of pure ones.
  Indeed, if the pure responses have no independent sequence, then, by the dichotomy of Rosenthal, every sequence of them has a subsequence that converges pointwise on $K_\varphi$, and this subsequence is weakly Cauchy in $C(K_\varphi)$ by dominated convergence;
  by the theorem of Stegall, as in the second paper, every sequence in the closed absolutely convex hull of the pure responses, which contains the mixed responses, then has a weakly Cauchy subsequence as well.
  Consequently, if a diagonal observable has the independence property, the hypothesis holds, and the real-valued theory of $\mathcal{S}_\varphi$ has the tree property of the second kind.

We first compute the response of $\mu_{ij}$ at a pure probe $\lambda\in V^*$.
  If $\lambda$ does not vanish on $W_{ij}$, then the functional $\lambda$
  takes the value $1$ on exactly half of the coset $g_{ij}+W_{ij}$, since a nonzero functional on a finite $\mathbb{F}_2$-space is balanced, and translation by $g_{ij}$ either preserves or exchanges its level sets.
  If $\lambda$ vanishes on $W_{ij}$, then the functional $\lambda$ is constant on the coset, with value $\lambda(g_{ij})$.
  Hence
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:first-check}
  r_{\mu_{ij}}(\lambda)=
  \begin{cases}
  1   & \text{if }\lambda(g_{im})=0\text{ for }m<j\text{ and }\lambda(g_{ij})=1, \\
  1/2 & \text{if }\lambda(g_{im})=1\text{ for some }m<j,                         \\
  0   & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  In words, the response is $1$ exactly when $g_{ij}$ is the first check of block $i$ to fire.

  (a) Let $f$ and $n$ be given.
  The conditions $\lambda(g_{im})=0$ for $m<f(i)$ and
  $\lambda(g_{if(i)})=1$, for $i<n$, concern finitely many linearly independent vectors, so \cref{lem:generic} gives an error $e$ with $\omega(\cdot,e)$ satisfying them, and by~\eqref{eq:first-check} the response $r_{\mu_{if(i)}}(e)$ equals $1$ for all $i<n$.
  The conditions for all $i$ together define a nonempty closed subset of $V^*$, since the vectors $g_{im}$ are linearly independent;
  any $\lambda$ in it works.

  (b) By symmetry, we may assume that $j<j'$.
  By~\eqref{eq:first-check}, the pair $\bigl(r_{\mu_{ij}}(\lambda),r_{\mu_{ij'}}(\lambda)\bigr)$ at a pure probe takes one of the values
  \[
    (1,\tfrac12),\quad(\tfrac12,\tfrac12),\quad(0,\tfrac12),\quad(0,1),\quad(0,0).
  \]
  Indeed, if the first check of block $i$ to fire among $g_{i0},\dots,g_{ij'}$ has index $m$, the pair is $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ for $m<j$, $(1,\tfrac12)$ for $m=j$, $(0,\tfrac12)$ for $j<m<j'$ and $(0,1)$ for $m=j'$;
  if none fires, the pair is $(0,0)$.
  Responses are affine in the probe, so at a mixed probe $\nu$ the pair lies in the convex hull of these five points.
  On that hull the minimum of the two coordinates is at most
  $2/3$, with equality only at $\tfrac23(1,\tfrac12)+\tfrac13(0,1)$, which is the pair of the probe $\tfrac23\delta_\lambda+\tfrac13\delta_{\lambda'}$ with $g_{ij}$ firing first under $\lambda$ and $g_{ij'}$ under $\lambda'$.

  Statements (a) and (b) are the two clauses of \cref{def:family-tp} for the array $(r_{\mu_{ij}})$, with $k=2$, $\alpha=2/3$ and $\beta=1$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:qudits}
  For qudits of prime dimension $p$, the labels form a symplectic space over the field $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p$ elements, and the response of a label at an error is $1$ when their commutation phase is nontrivial and $0$ otherwise.
  The proof of \cref{thm:pauli-tp2} adapts as follows.
  A nonzero functional on a finite $\mathbb{F}_p$-space takes each value equally often, so in~\eqref{eq:first-check} the response $\frac12$ becomes $\vartheta=\frac{p-1}p$.
  For $j<j'$, the pair of responses at a pure probe takes one of the values $(1,\vartheta)$, $(\vartheta,\vartheta)$, $(0,\vartheta)$, $(0,1)$ and $(0,0)$.
  The point $(\vartheta,\vartheta)$ lies below the segment from $(1,\vartheta)$ to $(0,1)$, since $\vartheta<1-\vartheta+\vartheta^2$, and on that segment the two coordinates are equal at the point with first coordinate $\frac p{p+1}$.
  Hence the bound $\frac23$ of \cref{thm:pauli-tp2}(b) becomes $\frac p{p+1}$, and it is attained.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- example-2: body_md -->
\label{exa:repetition}
  Index the qubits by pairs $(i,m)\in\mathbb{N}^2$, so that each block $i$ is a chain of qubits, and let $g_{im}=Z_{i,m}Z_{i,m+1}$ be the check of the bit-flip repetition code on the neighboring qubits $m$ and $m+1$ of the chain $i$.
  These checks commute pairwise and are linearly independent.
  For a bit flip $e=\prod X_{i,m}^{x_{i,m}}$, we have $\omega(g_{im},e)=x_{i,m}+x_{i,m+1}$, which is $1$ if and only if $e$ has a domain wall between the qubits $m$ and $m+1$ of the chain $i$.
  By~\eqref{eq:first-check}, the response of $\mu_{ij}$ at $e$ is $1$ exactly when the first domain wall of $e$ on the chain $i$ lies between the qubits $j$ and $j+1$.
  \cref{thm:pauli-tp2} then reads as follows:
  the chains are independent code blocks of a memory, an error cannot have two first domain walls on the same chain, and the positions of the first domain walls on finitely many chains can be prescribed independently by a single error.
  The bit flips on the chain $i$ with zero syndrome are the identity and the flip $X$ of the whole chain, which acts on infinitely many qubits:
  the logical bit flip of an infinite chain is an idealized error, an element of $\hat{V}\setminus V$.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:fourier}
  The mechanism behind \cref{thm:pauli-tp2} is Fourier analysis on the compact group $V^*$:
  since the characters of $V^*$ separate points and are closed under multiplication, the linear span of Alice’s responses and the constants is dense in $C(V^*)$ by the Stone--Weierstrass theorem;
  it is an algebra, because $2r_ar_b=r_a+r_b-r_{a+b}$ for the pure responses $r_a$.
  By \cref{prop:sector}, the indicators of syndrome sectors, which are functions of supremum norm one, lie in this span with coefficients of total absolute value at most $3$.
  On the pure labels, by contrast, a formula of $(V,\omega)$ speaks about a bounded number of checks, and \cref{thm:symp-simple} shows that no formula has the tree property.

<!-- keytheorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:free}
  Let $C_\varphi$ be a diagonal observable with freely independent pure responses, and let $\kappa>0$ be given.
  Let $\mathcal{R}_\kappa$ be the family of functions $\gamma+\sum_{i<n}c_ir_{\mu_i}$ on $\mathcal{P}(\{0,1\}^A)$, where $n\in\mathbb{N}$, the $\mu_i$ are mixtures of Alice and $|\gamma|+\sum_i|c_i|\le\kappa$.
  Then the family $\mathcal{R}_\kappa$ contains no uniformly $k$-inconsistent sequence, for any $k$.
  In particular, no tree property is witnessed in $\mathcal{R}_\kappa$.
<!-- end keytheorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Let $(h_j)$ be a sequence in $\mathcal{R}_\kappa$ and let $\alpha<\beta$ be such that each $h_j$ takes a value $\ge\beta$.
  We show that for every $k$ there are $j_1<\dots<j_k$ and a pure probe $\lambda$ with $h_{j_\ell}(\delta_\lambda)>\alpha$ for all $\ell$.

  Write $h_j=h_{c_j,\gamma_j}$ with $c_j=\sum_ic_{j,i}\mu_{j,i}$, whence $\|c_j\|_1\le\kappa$.
  Since $A$ is countable, a diagonal argument gives a subsequence, still denoted $(c_j)$, that converges pointwise on $A$ to some $c$;
  by Fatou’s lemma, the limit satisfies $\|c\|_1\le\kappa$.
  Put $\eta=(\beta-\alpha)/5$ and choose a finite set $F\subseteq A$ with $\|c\restriction(A\setminus F)\|_1<\eta$.
  Put $F_0=F$, and choose inductively indices $j_1<\dots<j_k$ and finite sets $F_0\subseteq F_1\subseteq\dots\subseteq F_k$ such that
  \[
    \|(c_{j_\ell}-c)\restriction F_{\ell-1}\|_1<\eta
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \|c_{j_\ell}\restriction(A\setminus F_\ell)\|_1<\eta;
  \]
  the first condition holds for all large indices by pointwise convergence, and the second for a large enough finite set.
  Define $\lambda\in\{0,1\}^A$ by $\lambda(a)=1$ exactly when $a\in F$ and $c(a)>0$, or $a\in F_\ell\setminus F_{\ell-1}$ and $c_{j_\ell}(a)>0$ for some $\ell\le k$.

  Let $\ell\le k$ be given.
  The deficit of $\lambda$ for $h_{j_\ell}$ is
  \[
    \sup h_{j_\ell}-h_{j_\ell}(\delta_\lambda)=
    \sum_{\lambda(a)=0}c_{j_\ell}(a)^++\sum_{\lambda(a)=1}c_{j_\ell}(a)^-,
  \]
  and we bound its contribution on four disjoint pieces of $A$.
  On $F_\ell\setminus F_{\ell-1}$ the sign pattern of $\lambda$ is that of $c_{j_\ell}$, and the contribution is $0$.
  On $F$ it is that of $c$, and a label where the signs of $c_{j_\ell}$ and $c$ disagree contributes at most $|c_{j_\ell}(a)-c(a)|$;
  the total is less than $\eta$.
  On $F_{\ell-1}\setminus F$ the contribution is at most $\|c_{j_\ell}\restriction(F_{\ell-1}\setminus F)\|_1 \le\|(c_{j_\ell}-c)\restriction F_{\ell-1}\|_1+\|c\restriction(A\setminus F)\|_1<2\eta$.
  On $A\setminus F_\ell$ it is less than $\eta$.
  Hence $h_{j_\ell}(\delta_\lambda)>\sup h_{j_\ell}-4\eta\ge\beta-4\eta>\alpha$.

\label{thm:free-qf}
  Let $C_\varphi$ be a diagonal observable with freely independent pure responses, let $n\ge1$ be an integer, and let $u\colon[0,1]^n\to\mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function.
  Let $\mathcal{R}_u$ be the family of functions
  \[
    h(\nu)=u\bigl(r_{\mu_1}(\nu),\dots,r_{\mu_n}(\nu)\bigr)
    \qquad(\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\{0,1\}^A)),
  \]
  where $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n$ range over the mixtures of Alice.
  Then the family $\mathcal{R}_u$ contains no uniformly $k$-inconsistent sequence, for any $k$.
  In particular, no tree property of the quantifier-free formula $u(R(y_1,x),\dots,R(y_n,x))$ in the probe variable $x$ is witnessed by actual preparations.
<!-- end keytheorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
For $\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\{0,1\}^A)$ write $\pi(\nu)\in[0,1]^A$ for its vector of marginals $(\pi_a(\nu))_{a\in A}$, defined before \cref{thm:free}.
  Every $h\in\mathcal{R}_u$ factors as $h(\nu)=H(\pi(\nu))$ with
  \[
    H(q)=u\bigl(\langle\mu_1,q\rangle,\dots,\langle\mu_n,q\rangle\bigr),
    \qquad \langle\mu,q\rangle=\sum_{a\in A}\mu(a)q_a ,
  \]
  and every $q\in[0,1]^A$ is the vector of marginals of a product measure.
  It therefore suffices to work on the cube $[0,1]^A$.

  Let $(h_j)$ be a sequence in $\mathcal{R}_u$, with parameters $\mu_{j,1},\dots,\mu_{j,n}$, and let $\alpha<\beta$ be such that each $h_j$ takes a value $\ge\beta$.
  We show that for every $k$ there are $j_1<\dots<j_k$ and $q\in[0,1]^A$ with $H_{j_\ell}(q)>\alpha$ for all $\ell$.
  Since $u$ is uniformly continuous, there is $\theta>0$ such that $|u(s)-u(t)|<\beta-\alpha$ whenever $\max_i|s_i-t_i|<\theta$.
  For each $j$ choose $q^{(j)}\in[0,1]^A$ with $H_j(q^{(j)})\ge\beta$.
  The set $A$ is countable and the cube is compact and metrizable, so a diagonal argument provides a subsequence, still indexed by $j$, along which $\mu_{j,i}\to\mu_i$ pointwise on $A$ for each $i\le n$ and $q^{(j)}\to q^\infty$ in $[0,1]^A$.
  By Fatou’s lemma, each limit $\mu_i$ has total mass at most $1$.
  Put $\eta=\theta/5$ and choose a finite set $F\subseteq A$ with $\mu_i(A\setminus F)<\eta$ for all $i\le n$.

  Put $F_0=F$, and choose inductively indices $j_1<\dots<j_k$ and finite sets $F_0\subseteq F_1\subseteq\dots\subseteq F_k$ such that, for all $i\le n$,
  \[
    \sum_{a\in F_{\ell-1}}|\mu_{j_\ell,i}(a)-\mu_i(a)|<\eta,\qquad
    \sum_{a\in F}|q^{(j_\ell)}_a-q^\infty_a|<\eta,\qquad
    \mu_{j_\ell,i}(A\setminus F_\ell)<\eta .
  \]
  The first two conditions hold for all large indices, by convergence on the finite sets $F_{\ell-1}$ and $F$, and the third for a large enough finite set $F_\ell$.
  Define $q\in[0,1]^A$ by $q_a=q^\infty_a$ for $a\in F$, $q_a=q^{(j_\ell)}_a$ for $a\in F_\ell\setminus F_{\ell-1}$ and $\ell\le k$, and $q_a=0$ for $a\notin F_k$.

  Let $\ell\le k$ and $i\le n$ be given.
  We compare $\langle\mu_{j_\ell,i},q\rangle$ with $\langle\mu_{j_\ell,i},q^{(j_\ell)}\rangle$, on the same four pieces of $A$ as in the proof of \cref{thm:free};
  on each piece the two vectors differ by at most $1$ in each coordinate.
  On $F_\ell\setminus F_{\ell-1}$ they agree.
  On $F$ the difference is at most $\sum_{a\in F}|q^\infty_a-q^{(j_\ell)}_a|<\eta$, since $\mu_{j_\ell,i}(a)\le1$.
  On $F_{\ell-1}\setminus F$ it is at most $\mu_{j_\ell,i}(F_{\ell-1}\setminus F)\le\sum_{a\in F_{\ell-1}}|\mu_{j_\ell,i}(a) -\mu_i(a)|+\mu_i(A\setminus F)<2\eta$.
  On $A\setminus F_\ell$ it is at most $\mu_{j_\ell,i}(A\setminus F_\ell)<\eta$.
  Hence $|\langle\mu_{j_\ell,i},q\rangle-\langle\mu_{j_\ell,i},q^{(j_\ell)}\rangle|<4\eta<\theta$ for every $i\le n$, and the choice of $\theta$ gives $H_{j_\ell}(q)>H_{j_\ell}(q^{(j_\ell)})-(\beta-\alpha)\ge\alpha$.
