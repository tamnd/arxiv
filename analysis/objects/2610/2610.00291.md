<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:frontier}
For every $q\in[0,1/2]$,
\begin{align}
 \max_{k:\ \sum w_dk_d=q}\left|\sum_dw_ds_dk_d\right|&=A_*(q),\\
 \min_{k:\ \sum w_dk_d=q}e(k)&=\tfrac12-A_*(q),\\
 \max_{k:\ \sum w_dk_d=q}I(Y;M)&=\Psi(q):=\mathcal I(q,A_*(q)).
 \label{eq:psi}
\end{align}
For $r\leq\epsilon<1/2$, let $\delta=1/2-\epsilon$. Then the exact task transport is
\begin{equation}
 q_T(\epsilon)=\min\{q\in[0,1/2]: A_*(q)\geq\delta\}.
 \label{eq:qT}
\end{equation}
The minimum target information among task-feasible kernels equals
\begin{equation}
 R_D^Y(\epsilon)=\ln2-h_2(\epsilon).
 \label{eq:RD}
\end{equation}
For the information-only relaxation at this same threshold,
\begin{equation}
 q_I(\epsilon)=\min\{q\in[0,1/2]:\Psi(q)\geq R_D^Y(\epsilon)\}.
 \label{eq:qI}
\end{equation}
These minima are attained. The quantity $R_D^Y$ concerns $I(Y;M)$, not data compression $I(D;M)$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For a fixed $q$, maximize $A=\sum_iw_is_ik_i$ under $0\leq k_i\leq1$ and $\sum_iw_ik_i=q$. If $s_i>s_j$ while $k_i<1$ and $k_j>0$, transferring an equal amount of selected probability from $j$ to $i$ strictly increases $A$ and preserves $q$. Repeating such exchanges gives the threshold kernel and \eqref{eq:confidence-frontier}. Symmetry yields the negative counterpart with the same $q$, so the maximum absolute value is also $A_*(q)$. The error formula in \eqref{eq:IA} proves the second claim.

For $A>0$ in the interior of the channel domain,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\mathcal I(q,A)}{\partial A}
 =\tfrac12\ln\frac{(q+A)(1-q+A)}{(q-A)(1-q-A)}>0.
\end{equation}
The function is even in $A$, with continuous boundary values. Thus maximizing absolute contrast also maximizes information at fixed $q$. This proves \eqref{eq:psi}. Encoders of cost $q>1/2$ can be complemented to encoders of cost $1-q<1/2$, preserving both Bayes error and mutual information. Hence no optimum requires $q>1/2$, and \eqref{eq:qT} follows.

For \eqref{eq:RD}, the binary Fano inequality gives $I(Y;M)\geq\ln2-h_2(\epsilon)$ for any task-feasible encoder. A symmetric Bayes rule, with a fair coin on posterior ties, induces a binary symmetric channel with crossover probability $r$. Flipping its decision independently with probability $(\epsilon-r)/(1-2r)$ produces a binary symmetric channel of crossover probability $\epsilon$, which achieves equality. This encoder factors through $D$, so the lower bound is achievable within the observation constraint. The fixed-cost information boundary now gives \eqref{eq:qI}. Compactness of the finite-dimensional kernel cube and continuity establish attainment.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
$A_*(q)$ is piecewise affine and nondecreasing on $[0,1/2]$, but $\Psi(q)$ need not be globally increasing. For example, observations with posterior score zero can leave $A_*$ constant while increasing $q$ reduces information. Thus \eqref{eq:qI} is a first-crossing definition, not an assumed global inverse. On each score segment the channel varies affinely, and mutual information is convex in that channel. The minimum feasible $q$ can be obtained by scanning segment endpoints and solving the first threshold crossing in a qualifying segment.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:stat-gap}
For every informative finite symmetric experiment and every
\begin{equation}
 r<\epsilon<1/2,
\end{equation}
one has $q_I(\epsilon)<q_T(\epsilon)$. In particular, the gap survives arbitrary nonzero observation noise in this class, provided the requested error lies strictly above the data Bayes floor.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $p_+=P(s_D>0)\leq1/2$. Since $0<\delta<S$, the threshold encoder reaches contrast $\delta$ before exhausting all positive scores. Therefore $0<q_T<p_+\leq1/2$, and its Bayes error is exactly $\epsilon$.

We show that Fano is strict for this encoder. If equality held in $H(Y\mid M)\leqh_2(\epsilon)$, the binary error indicator $E$ for a Bayes decoder would be independent of $M$. Since the error is less than $1/2$, the decoder is nonconstant. Uniformity of $Y$ would then give
\begin{equation}
 \tfrac12=\epsilon+(1-2\epsilon)P(g(M)=1),
\end{equation}
forcing $P(g(M)=1)=1/2$, hence $q_T=1/2$, a contradiction. Thus $\Psi(q_T)>R_D^Y(\epsilon)$. Continuity, $\Psi(0)=0<R_D^Y(\epsilon)$, and the intermediate value theorem yield a feasible information cost strictly below $q_T$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:binary}
Fix $0<\epsilon<1/2$ and define
\begin{equation}
 \phi(q)=h_2(q)-\tfrac12h_2(2q),\qquad 0\leq q\leq\tfrac12.
 \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
There is a unique $q_\epsilon\in(0,1/2)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \phi(q_\epsilon)=R(\epsilon)=\ln2-h_2(\epsilon).
 \label{eq:q-root}
\end{equation}
The exact optimal costs are
\begin{equation}
 W^*_{\mathrm{task}}(\epsilon)=\tfrac12-\epsilon,
 \qquad W^*_{\mathrm{info}}(R(\epsilon))=q_\epsilon,
\end{equation}
and their difference is strictly positive:
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_W(\epsilon)=\tfrac12-\epsilon-q_\epsilon>0.
 \label{eq:strict-gap}
\end{equation}
A task-optimal encoder is $(a,b)=(0,1-2\epsilon)$. An information-only optimal encoder is $(a,b)=(0,2q_\epsilon)$, which satisfies
\begin{equation}
 I(K)=R(\epsilon),\qquad e(K)=\tfrac12-q_\epsilon>\epsilon.
\end{equation}
Swapping the source labels gives equivalent optimizers without changing the physical reset state.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:regularization}
Fix an encoder with $0\leq k_d\leq\kappa<1$ and $q>0$. Put
\begin{equation}
 F=\frac q\mathsf{B},\qquad c=\frac1{e^F-1}.
\end{equation}
Suppose
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda>\frac c\tau,
 \qquad
 \Lambda\geq\frac{\kappa(1+c)}{\tau(1-\kappa)}.
 \label{eq:rate-threshold}
\end{equation}
There is a protocol starting exactly from reset, reaching the prescribed encoder exactly at $\tau$, with all rates bounded by $\Lambda$, for which
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M\leq\mathsf{B},\qquad
 \Sigma=\frac{q^2}{\mathsf{B}}+\frac q{\tau\Lambda}\ln(1+c).
 \label{eq:regularized-cost}
\end{equation}
Thus no singular transition rate is required. If $C_\Lambda(K)$ is the protocol cost infimum for this endpoint, then
\begin{equation}
 0\leq C_\Lambda(K)-q^2/\mathsf{B}
 \leq\frac q{\tau\Lambda}\ln(1+c)
\end{equation}
whenever \eqref{eq:rate-threshold} holds.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For $k_d=0$, keep both rates zero. For $k_d>0$, prescribe the conditional memory probability
\begin{equation}
 x_d(t)=\mathbb{P}(M_t=1\mid D=d)=\frac{k_dt}{\tau}.
\end{equation}
Define $\rho(t)=\min\{\Lambda t,c\}$ and rates
\begin{equation}
 r_d^-(t)=\begin{cases}\Lambda,&t=0,\\ \rho(t)/t,&t>0,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 r_d^+(t)=\frac{k_d}{\tau}\frac{1+\rho(t)}{1-k_dt/\tau}.
 \label{eq:explicit-rates}
\end{equation}
The two rates are finite and continuous across $t_0=c/\Lambda$. Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}
 (1-x_d)r_d^+-x_dr_d^-=k_d/\tau,
\end{equation}
so the master equation is satisfied exactly from $x_d(0)=0$. The backward rate is bounded by $\Lambda$. The forward rate obeys
\begin{equation}
 r_d^+(t)\leq\frac{k_d(1+c)}{\tau(1-k_d)}
 \leq\frac{\kappa(1+c)}{\tau(1-\kappa)}\leq\Lambda.
\end{equation}
The log ratio of the forward and backward rates is finite on every active sector, including $t=0$; hence the rates can be assigned a finite instantaneous energy difference under local detailed balance.

Writing $J_d=w_dk_d/\tau$, the joint fluxes are
\begin{equation}
 u_d=J_d(1+\rho(t)),\qquad v_d=J_d\rho(t).
\end{equation}
Every active sector has the same flux force
\begin{equation}
 f(t)=\ln(1+1/\rho(t))\geq\ln(1+1/c)=F\quad(t>0).
\end{equation}
Consequently $\mathcal M=(q/\tau)\int_0^\tau f(t)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d} t\leq q/F=\mathsf{B}$. The force diverges logarithmically as $t\downarrow0$, due to the pure initial distribution, but the rates do not diverge and the entropy integral is finite. Since $t_0<\tau$,
\begin{align}
 \Sigma
 &=\frac q\tau\left[\int_0^{c/\Lambda}\ln\left(1+\frac1{\Lambda t}\right)\mathrm{d} t
                  +(\tau-c/\Lambda)F\right]\\
 &=qF+\frac q{\tau\Lambda}\ln(1+c).
\end{align}
The last equality follows from
$\int_0^c\ln(1+1/z)\mathrm{d} z=(c+1)\ln(c+1)-c\ln c$.
Combining this explicit upper bound with \eqref{eq:lower} proves the claim.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:direct}
Let $C_{T,\Lambda}$ and $C_{I,\Lambda}$ be the actual protocol infima under the task and information constraints, respectively, with the same $\tau,\mathsf{B},\Lambda$. Put $c_I=(e^{q_I/\mathsf{B}}-1)^{-1}$. If the rate conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:regularization} hold for both $(0,2q_I)$ and $(0,2q_T)$, and
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda>\frac{\mathsf{B} q_I\ln(1+c_I)}{\tau(q_T^2-q_I^2)},
 \label{eq:strict-cap}
\end{equation}
then both task and information problems are feasible and
\begin{equation}
 C_{T,\Lambda}-C_{I,\Lambda}
 \geq\frac{q_T^2-q_I^2}{\mathsf{B}}
       -\frac{q_I\ln(1+c_I)}{\tau\Lambda}>0.
 \label{eq:cap-gap}
\end{equation}
All rate-cap thresholds in this corollary are finite for $0<\epsilon<1/2$.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:combined}
Fix an informative finite symmetric experiment, $r<\epsilon<1/2$, $\tau>0$, and $\mathsf{B}>0$. Use the physical model of Section~\ref{sec:control}, with rates controlled only by observed $D$. There exists a finite $\Lambda_0$ such that for every $\Lambda>\Lambda_0$, both optimization problems are feasible and
\begin{equation}
 C_{T,\Lambda}(\epsilon)>C_{I,\Lambda}(R_D^Y(\epsilon)).
\end{equation}
More explicitly, choose any encoder with cost $0<q<q_T$, information at least $R_D^Y(\epsilon)$, and $\max_d k_d=\kappa<1$. Put $c=(e^{q/\mathsf{B}}-1)^{-1}$. Whenever its rate conditions hold and a task-feasible encoder with all write probabilities below one is also realizable under the same cap,
\begin{equation}
 C_{T,\Lambda}-C_{I,\Lambda}
 \geq\frac{q_T^2-q^2}{\mathsf{B}}
       -\frac{q\ln(1+c)}{\tau\Lambda}.
 \label{eq:combined-certificate}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is positive for a sufficiently large finite cap.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Theorem~\ref{thm:stat-gap} proves that the task-optimal threshold encoder $k^T$ has information strictly above $R_D^Y(\epsilon)$. Replace it by $k_d=\alpha k_d^T$ for $0<\alpha<1$ sufficiently close to one. Continuity preserves information strictly above the threshold, while $q=\alpha q_T<q_T$ and $\max k_d\leq\alpha<1$. Thus the information witness required by the theorem always exists.

To ensure task feasibility with finite rates, put $\delta=1/2-\epsilon$ and let $k^B$ be a Bayes rule with contrast $S$, randomizing ties symmetrically. Choose $\beta\in(\delta/S,1)$ and set $k_d=\beta k_d^B$. This has contrast $\beta S>\delta$, hence error strictly below $\epsilon$, and all encoder probabilities are below one. Theorem~\ref{thm:regularization} realizes both witnesses at sufficiently large finite caps.

Every task-feasible protocol has $q\geq q_T$, and hence entropy production at least $q_T^2/\mathsf{B}$. The explicit information witness has entropy production given by \eqref{eq:regularized-cost}. Subtracting gives \eqref{eq:combined-certificate}. All rate and strictness thresholds are finite since both witnesses have maximum write probability below one and $q<q_T$. This also establishes a common $\Lambda_0$ valid for every larger cap.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
For every protocol with forward rate bounded by $\Lambda$ and duration $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
 e\geq r+e^{-\Lambda\tau}(1/2-r).
 \label{eq:feasibility}
\end{equation}
This lower bound remains valid with an additional mobility budget. In particular, an informative experiment cannot attain its exact data Bayes error in this reset-memory model at any finite $\Lambda\tau$.

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
\textit{Step 1: the information threshold is necessary and achievable.}
For a Bayes decoder $\widehat Y=g(M)$, define the error indicator $E=\mathbf 1_{\{Y\neq\widehat Y\}}$. Since $Y$ is binary, $Y$ is determined by $(M,E)$, and $E$ is determined by $(M,Y)$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 H(Y\mid M)=H(E\mid M)\leq H(E)=h_2(e(K)).
\end{equation}
As $e(K)\leq1/2$ and $h_2$ increases on $[0,1/2]$, task feasibility implies $I(Y;M)\geq\ln2-h_2(\epsilon)$. Conversely, the binary symmetric channel $(a,b)=(\epsilon,1-\epsilon)$ has Bayes error $\epsilon$ and mutual information $\ln2-h_2(\epsilon)$. Thus \eqref{eq:binary-rd} is exactly the minimum information among task-feasible binary encoders. This is the binary rate--distortion calculation; see also~\cite{PolyanskiyWu2022Notes}.

\textit{Step 2: solve the task-constrained transport problem.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:bayes} gives $|b-a|\geq1-2\epsilon$. Since $a,b\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 q=\frac{a+b}{2}\geq\frac{|b-a|}{2}\geq\tfrac12-\epsilon.
\end{equation}
The encoder $(a,b)=(0,1-2\epsilon)$ attains both inequalities and has error exactly $\epsilon$. Therefore $W^*_{\mathrm{task}}(\epsilon)=1/2-\epsilon$.

\textit{Step 3: maximize information at fixed transport.}
For $q\in[0,1/2]$, the constraint $a+b=2q$ gives $a\in[0,2q]$ and $b=2q-a$. The function
\begin{equation}
 f_q(a)=h_2(a)+h_2(2q-a)
\end{equation}
is concave on that interval; hence its minimum is achieved at an endpoint. Both endpoints have value $h_2(2q)$. Substituting into \eqref{eq:mi} proves
\begin{equation}
 I(K)\leq\phi(q),
\end{equation}
with equality at $(a,b)=(0,2q)$ or $(2q,0)$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \phi(0)=0,\quad \phi(1/2)=\ln2,\quad
 \phi'(q)=\ln\frac{2(1-q)}{1-2q}>0
 \quad(0<q<1/2).
 \label{eq:phi-derivative}
\end{equation}
Continuity and strict monotonicity establish existence and uniqueness of $q_\epsilon$.

If an encoder has $q<q_\epsilon$, then $q<1/2$ and $I(K)\leq\phi(q)<R(\epsilon)$, so it is infeasible for the information benchmark. Encoders with $q>1/2$ cannot improve on the candidate $q_\epsilon<1/2$. The endpoint encoder $(0,2q_\epsilon)$ attains the threshold and proves $W^*_{\mathrm{info}}(R(\epsilon))=q_\epsilon$. This also justifies the global optimization over all $a,b\in[0,1]$, rather than only those with $q\leq1/2$.

\textit{Step 4: establish strictness.}
Define the continuous function on $[0,1/2]$
\begin{equation}
 G(\epsilon)=\phi(1/2-\epsilon)-\ln2+h_2(\epsilon).
\end{equation}
Its endpoint values are $G(0)=G(1/2)=0$, while for $0<\epsilon<1/2$,
\begin{align}
 G'(\epsilon)
 &=-\ln\frac{1+2\epsilon}{2\epsilon}
   +\ln\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}\\
 &=\ln\frac{2(1-\epsilon)}{1+2\epsilon}.
\end{align}
This derivative is positive on $(0,1/4)$, zero at $1/4$, and negative on $(1/4,1/2)$. Hence $G(\epsilon)>0$ throughout the open interval. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \phi(1/2-\epsilon)>R(\epsilon)=\phi(q_\epsilon).
\end{equation}
Strict monotonicity of $\phi$ yields $q_\epsilon<1/2-\epsilon$. Finally, \eqref{eq:bayes} gives $e(0,2q_\epsilon)=1/2-q_\epsilon>\epsilon$.
