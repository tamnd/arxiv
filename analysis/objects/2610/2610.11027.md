\label{assumh}
For each \(i=1,2\), let \(h_i:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\) be a \(C^1\) function,
and define its primitive by
\[
H_i(s)=\int_0^s h_i(\tau)\,d\tau.
\]
We assume that there exist constants \(C_1,C_2>0\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{ass1.5}
\liminf_{|s|\to+\infty}\frac{H_i(s)}{s^2}\ge 0,~
\liminf_{|s|\to+\infty}\frac{s h_i(s)-C_1 H_i(s)}{s^2}\ge 0,~

\end{equation}
Condition \eqref{ass1.5} implies that for any \(\epsilon>0\), there exist
constants \(C_\epsilon,C_\epsilon'>0\) such that
\begin{align}
H_i(s)+\epsilon s^2&\ge -C_\epsilon,\quad \forall s\in\mathbb{R},\; i=1,2,\label{ass1.6}\\
s h_i(s)-C_1 H_i(s)+\epsilon s^2&\ge -C_\epsilon',\quad \forall s\in\mathbb{R},\; i=1,2.\label{ass1.7}
\end{align}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:attractor}
Let \(X\) be a Banach space and let \(\{S(t)\}_{t\ge0}\) be a continuous
semigroup on \(X\). A set \(\mathfrak{A}\subset X\) is called a \emph{global attractor}
for \(S(t)\) if
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item \(\mathfrak{A}\) is compact in \(X\);
    \item \(\mathfrak{A}\) is invariant under \(S(t)\), i.e., \(S(t)\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{A}\) for all
    \(t\ge0\);
    \item \(\mathfrak{A}\) attracts every bounded subset of \(X\), i.e., for every
    bounded set \(B\subset X\),
    \[
    \lim_{t\to+\infty}d_X\bigl(S(t)B,\mathfrak{A}\bigr)=0,
    \]
    where \(d_X(A,B)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\|a-b\|_X\) denotes the
    Hausdorff semi-distance.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:B0-bounded}
The absorbing ball \(\mathcal{B}_0\) is bounded in \(\mathcal{H}\). Consequently,
there exists \(R_1>R_0\) such that the closed ball
\(\mathcal{B}_1:=\{U\in\mathcal{H}:\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}}\le R_1\}\) contains
\(\mathcal{B}_0\) in its interior. Since \(F\) is locally Lipschitz, there exists
a constant \(L_1>0\), depending only on \(R_1\), such that
\[
\|F(U)-F(V)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\le L_1\|U-V\|_{\mathcal{H}},
\qquad
U,V\in\mathcal{B}_1.
\]
Moreover, \(F(\mathcal{B}_1)\) is bounded in \(\mathcal{H}\). We shall use these
facts repeatedly below.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:qstab}
Let \(\mathcal{H}\) be a Hilbert space, let \(\{S(t)\}_{t\ge0}\) be a continuous
semigroup on \(\mathcal{H}\), and let \(B\subset\mathcal{H}\) be a bounded set.
We say that \(S(t)\) is \emph{quasi-stable} on \(B\) if there exist \(T>0\),
\(q\in[0,1)\), and a relatively compact set \(\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{H}\)
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qstab-ineq}
\|S(T)U_0-S(T)V_0\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2
\le q^2\|U_0-V_0\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2
+\|K_T(U_0)-K_T(V_0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2,
\qquad\forall U_0,V_0\in B,
\end{equation}
for some map \(K_T:B\to\mathcal{K}\). Here \(\mathcal{K}\) is a relatively
compact subset of \(\mathcal{H}\) containing \(K_T(B)\).
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:asym-smooth}
Let \(X\) be a Banach space and let \(\{S(t)\}_{t\ge0}\) be a continuous
semigroup on \(X\). We say that \(S(t)\) is \emph{asymptotically smooth} if
the following two conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item \(S(t)\) possesses a bounded absorbing set \(B\subset X\), i.e.,
    for every bounded set \(B_0\subset X\) there exists \(t_0=t_0(B_0)\ge0\)
    such that
    \[
    S(t)B_0\subset B,\qquad \forall t\ge t_0;
    \]
    \item for every bounded set \(B_0\subset X\) such that \(S(t)B_0\subset B_0\)
    for all \(t\ge0\), the \(\omega\)-limit set \(\omega(B_0)\) is
    nonempty, compact, and invariant under \(S(t)\), and
    \[
    \lim_{t\to+\infty}d_X\bigl(S(t)B_0,\omega(B_0)\bigr)=0,
    \]
    where \(d_X(A,C)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{c\in C}\|a-c\|_X\) denotes the
    Hausdorff semi-distance.
\end{enumerate}

\label{thm:CL-asym}
Let \(\mathcal{H}\) be a Hilbert space and let \(\{S(t)\}_{t\ge0}\) be a
continuous semigroup on \(\mathcal{H}\) possessing a bounded absorbing set
\(B\). Suppose that \(S(t)\) is quasi-stable on \(B\) in the sense of
Definition \ref{def:qstab}. Then \(S(t)\) is asymptotically smooth on
\(\mathcal{H}\) in the sense of Definition \ref{def:asym-smooth}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{Exponential stability-precise}
Under Assumption \ref{assumg}, the linear semigroup
\(\{e^{-\mathcal{A}t}\}_{t\ge0}\) generated by \(-\mathcal{A}\), where
\(\mathcal{A}\) is the linear operator defined in \eqref{A}, is exponentially

\]

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{GP}
Let $T(t)$ be a bounded $C_0$-semigroup on a Hilbert space $H$ with generator
$-\mathcal{A}$. Then $T(t)$ is exponentially stable if and only if the
following conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item $i\mathbb{R}\subset\varrho(\mathcal{A})$, where $\varrho(\mathcal{A})$
    is the resolvent set of $\mathcal{A}$;
    \item $\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\|(i\lambda I-\mathcal{A})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H)}<{+\infty}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:GP-equivalent}
In the statement of Theorem \ref{GP}, condition (ii) is the standard uniform
boundedness of the resolvent on the imaginary axis. In the applications below
we shall verify the apparently weaker condition
\[
\limsup_{|\lambda|\to{+\infty}}\|(i\lambda I-\mathcal{A})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H)}<{+\infty}.
\]
Once condition (i) holds, the resolvent map
$\lambda\mapsto(i\lambda I-\mathcal{A})^{-1}$ is continuous on
$\mathbb{R}$; hence its boundedness for $|\lambda|$ sufficiently large,
together with its boundedness on every compact interval, implies the uniform
boundedness on the whole real line. Thus the two formulations are equivalent
under condition (i), and we may verify the limit superior version in practice.
In fact, the estimates obtained below yield the stronger uniform bound for all
$\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$, so the limit superior formulation is not needed.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:monotone}
Under Assumption \ref{assumg}, the operator $\mathcal{A}$ is monotone on
$\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$; that is,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monotone}
\operatorname{Re}\langle \mathcal{A} U,U\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\ge 0,
\qquad \forall\,U\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}).
\end{equation}
More precisely, for every $U=(u,\psi,\sigma,v,\phi,\eta)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monotone-explicit}
\operatorname{Re}\langle \mathcal{A} U,U\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
\ge
\gamma_1\|\psi\|^2+\frac{\lambda_1}{2}\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}^2
+\gamma_2\|\phi\|^2+\frac{\lambda_2}{2}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_2}}^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently, $-\mathcal{A}$ generates a $C_0$-semigroup of contractions
$\{e^{-\mathcal{A} t}\}_{t\ge0}$ on $\mathcal{H}$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $U=(u,\psi,\sigma,v,\phi,\eta)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$. By the definition

\begin{align*}

&=
\bar{\mu}_1\bigl(-\psi_{xx},u_{xx}\bigr)_\Omega
+\Bigl(\gamma_1\psi+\bar{\mu}_1u_{xxxx}
+\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\sigma_{xxxx}(s)\,ds,\psi\Bigr)_\Omega\\
&\quad
+\bigl(\sigma_s-\psi,\sigma\bigr)_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}
+\bar{\mu}_2\bigl(-\phi_x,v_x\bigr)_\Omega
\\
&\quad+\Bigl(\gamma_2\phi-\bar{\mu}_2v_{xx}
-\int_0^{+\infty} g_2(s)\eta_{xx}(s)\,ds,\phi\Bigr)_\Omega
+\bigl(\eta_s-\phi,\eta\bigr)_{\mathcal{M}_{g_2}}.
\end{align*}
Here we used that the first component of $\mathcal{A} U$ is $-\psi$ and the
fourth is $-\phi$, so that the corresponding contributions to the inner product
are $-\bar{\mu}_1(\psi_{xx},u_{xx})_\Omega$ and
$-\bar{\mu}_2(\phi_x,v_x)_\Omega$, respectively.

We now treat the elastic and memory terms separately.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Elastic terms.}
Using the boundary conditions $u=\psi=u_{xx}=\psi_{xx}=0$ at $x=0,\ell$, two
integrations by parts give
\[
\bar{\mu}_1\bigl(-\psi_{xx},u_{xx}\bigr)_\Omega
+\bar{\mu}_1\bigl(u_{xxxx},\psi\bigr)_\Omega
=
-\bar{\mu}_1(u_{xx},\psi_{xx})_\Omega
+\bar{\mu}_1(u_{xx},\psi_{xx})_\Omega.
\]
In the complexified setting the two terms are conjugate to each other, so their
sum is purely imaginary and therefore has zero real part. In particular, the
elastic terms do not contribute to
$\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{A} U,U\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$. Similarly,
\[
\bar{\mu}_2\bigl(-\phi_x,v_x\bigr)_\Omega
+\bar{\mu}_2\bigl(-v_{xx},\phi\bigr)_\Omega
=
-\bar{\mu}_2(v_x,\phi_x)_\Omega
+\bar{\mu}_2(v_x,\phi_x)_\Omega,
\]
whose sum is again purely imaginary and hence has zero real part.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Memory terms.}
For the first memory term, two integrations by parts yield, using the boundary
conditions $\sigma(0)=\sigma(\ell)=\sigma_{xx}(0)=\sigma_{xx}(\ell)=0$ and
$\psi(0)=\psi(\ell)=\psi_{xx}(0)=\psi_{xx}(\ell)=0$,
\[
\Bigl(\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\sigma_{xxxx}(s)\,ds,\psi\Bigr)_\Omega
=
\Bigl(\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\sigma_{xx}(s)\,ds,\psi_{xx}\Bigr)_\Omega,
\]
with no negative sign. On the other hand, the contribution of the third
component $\sigma_s-\psi$ to the inner product contains the term
\[
\bigl(-\psi,\sigma\bigr)_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}
=
-\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\bigl(\psi_{xx},\sigma_{xx}(s)\bigr)_\Omega\,ds.
\]
Therefore, the sum of the two off-diagonal memory terms is
\[
Z_1:=\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\bigl(\sigma_{xx}(s),\psi_{xx}\bigr)_\Omega\,ds
-\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\bigl(\psi_{xx},\sigma_{xx}(s)\bigr)_\Omega\,ds.
\]
Since the inner product is conjugate-linear in the second variable, we have
\[
(\psi_{xx},\sigma_{xx}(s))_\Omega
=
\overline{(\sigma_{xx}(s),\psi_{xx})_\Omega},
\]
and hence
\begin{align*}
Z_1
&=
\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\bigl(\sigma_{xx}(s),\psi_{xx}\bigr)_\Omega\,ds
-\overline{\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\bigl(\sigma_{xx}(s),\psi_{xx}\bigr)_\Omega\,ds}\\
&=
2i\operatorname{Im}\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\bigl(\sigma_{xx}(s),\psi_{xx}\bigr)_\Omega\,ds,
\end{align*}
which is purely imaginary and therefore does not contribute to the real part.
The same argument applies to the $\eta$-terms. Consequently, taking the real
part of $\langle \mathcal{A} U,U\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$ gives
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Re}\langle \mathcal{A} U,U\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
&=
\gamma_1\|\psi\|^2+\gamma_2\|\phi\|^2
+\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\sigma_s,\sigma\bigr)_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}
+\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\eta_s,\eta\bigr)_{\mathcal{M}_{g_2}}.
\end{align*}
Finally, we treat the memory terms. By Assumption \ref{assumg}, we have
$g_i(s)\le g_i(0)e^{-\lambda_i s}$ for almost every $s\ge0$, so that
$g_i({+\infty})=0$. Moreover, $\sigma(x,t,0)=u(x,t)-u(x,t)=0$ and
$\eta(x,t,0)=v(x,t)-v(x,t)=0$. Integrating by parts in $s$ and using these
facts, we obtain
\[
\bigl(\sigma_s,\sigma\bigr)_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}
=
\frac12\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\frac{d}{ds}\|\sigma_{xx}(s)\|^2\,ds
=
-\frac12\int_0^{+\infty} g_1'(s)\|\sigma_{xx}(s)\|^2\,ds,
\]
and the conclusion follows from $g_1'+\lambda_1g_1\le0$. The same argument
applies to $\eta$. Therefore \eqref{eq:monotone-explicit} holds, and since
$\gamma_i>0$ and $\lambda_i>0$, this implies \eqref{eq:monotone}. Finally,
since $\mathcal{A}$ is maximal monotone by \cite[Section~3]{Mukiawa2025}, the
L\"umer--Phillips theorem shows that $-\mathcal{A}$ generates a $C_0$-semigroup of
contractions.

By Lemma \ref{lem:monotone}, $-\mathcal{A}$ generates a $C_0$-semigroup of
contractions, so the Gearhart--Pr\"uss theorem (Theorem \ref{GP}) applies.

We first prove condition (i): $i\mathbb{R}\subset\varrho(\mathcal{A})$. Assume
for contradiction that there exists $\omega\in\mathbb{R}$ such that
$i\omega\notin\varrho(\mathcal{A})$. Since $\mathcal{A}$ is maximal monotone,
it is a closed operator, and for every $\lambda>0$ the operator
$\lambda I+\mathcal{A}$ is bijective. Since $-\mathcal{A}$ generates a
contraction semigroup, every point of $i\mathbb{R}\cap\sigma(\mathcal{A})$ is an
approximate eigenvalue; see, e.g., \cite[Chapter~2]{Pazy1983}. Hence
$i\omega\notin\varrho(\mathcal{A})$ implies
$i\omega\in\sigma_{\mathrm{ap}}(\mathcal{A})$. Therefore there exists a
sequence
$\{U_n\}=(u_n,\psi_n,\sigma_n,v_n,\phi_n,\eta_n)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$
with $\|U_n\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1$ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$, satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolvent_approx}
F_n:=i\omega U_n-\mathcal{A}U_n\to0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{H}.
\end{equation}
Writing $F_n=(f_n^1,f_n^2,f_n^3,f_n^4,f_n^5,f_n^6)$, the componentwise
convergence in \eqref{eq:resolvent_approx} reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:component_convergence}
\begin{cases}
f_n^1=i\omega u_n+\psi_n\to0 & \text{in }H_a^2(\Omega),\\
f_n^2=i\omega\psi_n-\gamma_1\psi_n-\bar{\mu}_1 u_{n,xxxx}
-\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}g_1(s)\sigma_{n,xxxx}(s)\,ds\to0
& \text{in }L^2(\Omega),\\
f_n^3=i\omega\sigma_n-\sigma_{n,s}+\psi_n\to0 & \text{in }\mathcal{M}_{g_1},\\
f_n^4=i\omega v_n+\phi_n\to0 & \text{in }H_0^1(\Omega),\\
f_n^5=i\omega\phi_n-\gamma_2\phi_n+\bar{\mu}_2 v_{n,xx}
+\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}g_2(s)\eta_{n,xx}(s)\,ds\to0
& \text{in }L^2(\Omega),\\
f_n^6=i\omega\eta_n-\eta_{n,s}+\phi_n\to0 & \text{in }\mathcal{M}_{g_2}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
From \eqref{eq:resolvent_approx}, Lemma \ref{lem:monotone}, and the
boundedness of $U_n$ in $\mathcal{H}$, we deduce
\begin{align}\label{eq:pq}
\gamma_1\|\psi_n\|^2+\frac{\lambda_1}{2}\|\sigma_n\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}^2
+\gamma_2\|\phi_n\|^2+\frac{\lambda_2}{2}\|\eta_n\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_2}}^2
&\le \operatorname{Re}\langle \mathcal{A} U_n,U_n\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\notag\\
&= -\operatorname{Re}\langle i\omega U_n-\mathcal{A}U_n,U_n\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\notag\\
&\le \|F_n\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|U_n\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to0.
\end{align}
Here we used that $\operatorname{Re}\langle i\omega U_n,U_n\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=0$.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{gj:pq}
\|\psi_n\|^2,\ \|\sigma_n\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}^2,\ \|\phi_n\|^2,\ \|\eta_n\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_2}}^2\to0.
\end{equation}
Since $\psi_n\to0$ and $\phi_n\to0$ in $L^2(\Omega)$ by \eqref{gj:pq}, the
damping terms $\gamma_1\psi_n$ and $\gamma_2\phi_n$ can be dropped in the
limit, and equation \eqref{eq:resolvent_approx} simplifies to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convergence_1}
\begin{cases}
f_n^1=i\omega u_n+\psi_n\to0 & \text{in }H_a^2(\Omega),\\
f_n^2=i\omega\psi_n-\bar{\mu}_1 u_{n,xxxx}
-\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}g_1(s)\sigma_{n,xxxx}(s)\,ds\to0
& \text{in }L^2(\Omega),\\
f_n^3=i\omega\sigma_n-\sigma_{n,s}+\psi_n\to0 & \text{in }\mathcal{M}_{g_1},\\
f_n^4=i\omega v_n+\phi_n\to0 & \text{in }H_0^1(\Omega),\\
f_n^5=i\omega\phi_n+\bar{\mu}_2 v_{n,xx}
+\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}g_2(s)\eta_{n,xx}(s)\,ds\to0
& \text{in }L^2(\Omega),\\
f_n^6=i\omega\eta_n-\eta_{n,s}+\phi_n\to0 & \text{in }\mathcal{M}_{g_2}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
We now split the proof into two cases.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Case 1: $\omega=0$.}
From \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_1$ and \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_4$, we obtain
\begin{align}\label{ssf_1}
\psi_n\to0\quad\text{in }H_a^2(\Omega),\qquad
\phi_n\to0\quad\text{in }H_0^1(\Omega).
\end{align}
By Poincar\'e's inequality,
\begin{align}\label{ssf_2}
\psi_n\to0\quad\text{in }L^2(\Omega),\qquad
\phi_n\to0\quad\text{in }L^2(\Omega).
\end{align}
From \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_3$ and \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_6$, together
with \eqref{ssf_2}, we derive
\begin{align}\label{ssf_3}
\sigma_{n,s}\to0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{M}_{g_1},\qquad
\eta_{n,s}\to0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{M}_{g_2}.
\end{align}
Taking the $L^2$-inner products of \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_2$ with $u_n$ and
\eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_5$ with $v_n$, respectively, and then taking real
parts, yields
\begin{align*}
\bar{\mu}_1\|u_{n,xx}\|^2+\bar{\mu}_2\|v_{n,x}\|^2
&=-\operatorname{Re}(f_n^2,u_n)-\operatorname{Re}(f_n^5,v_n)\\
&\quad-\operatorname{Re}\left(\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\sigma_{n,xx}(s)\,ds,u_{n,xx}\right)\\
&\quad-\operatorname{Re}\left(\int_0^{+\infty} g_2(s)\eta_{n,x}(s)\,ds,v_{n,x}\right).
\end{align*}
Since $\bar{\mu}_1\|u_{n,xx}\|^2+\bar{\mu}_2\|v_{n,x}\|^2
\le\|U_n\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1$, by Poincar\'e's inequality and H\"older's
inequality, there exists a positive constant $c$ such that
\begin{align*}
\bar{\mu}_1\|u_{n,xx}\|^2+\bar{\mu}_2\|v_{n,x}\|^2
&\le c\|f_n^2\|\|u_n\|+c\|f_n^5\|\|v_n\|\\
&\quad+\|u_{n,xx}\|\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\|\sigma_{n,xx}(s)\|\,ds
+\|v_{n,x}\|\int_0^{+\infty} g_2(s)\|\eta_{n,x}(s)\|\,ds\\
&\le c\|f_n^2\|+c\|f_n^5\|\\
&\quad+\left(\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\,ds\right)^{1/2}\|\sigma_n\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}
+\left(\int_0^{+\infty} g_2(s)\,ds\right)^{1/2}\|\eta_n\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_2}}.
\end{align*}
From \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_2$, \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_5$, and
\eqref{gj:pq}, we conclude
\begin{align}\label{ssf_4}
u_{n,xx}\to0,\quad v_{n,x}\to0\quad\text{in }L^2(\Omega).
\end{align}
Hence, from \eqref{gj:pq} and \eqref{ssf_4}, we obtain $\|U_n\|\to0$ in
$\mathcal{H}$, which contradicts $\|U_n\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1$ for all
$n\in\mathbb{N}$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Case 2: $\omega\ne0$.}
From \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_1$ and \eqref{gj:pq}, the relation
$i\omega u_n=f_n^1-\psi_n$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:u_n-small-L2}
u_n\to0\quad\text{in }L^2(\Omega).
\end{equation}
Similarly, from \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_4$ and \eqref{gj:pq}, the relation
$i\omega v_n=f_n^4-\phi_n$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v_n-small-L2}
v_n\to0\quad\text{in }L^2(\Omega).
\end{equation}
We prove the two remaining convergences in two steps.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: $\|u_{n,xx}\|\to0$.}
Taking the $L^2$-inner products of \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_2$ with $u_n$ and
\eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_1$ with $\psi_n$, respectively, and then taking real
parts, we obtain
\begin{align}\label{eq:case2-u-identity}
 \bar{\mu}_1\|u_{n,xx}\|^2
=
&-\operatorname{Re}(f_n^2,u_n)-\operatorname{Re}(f_n^1,\psi_n)
\notag\\
&+\|\psi_n\|^2
-\operatorname{Re}\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\bigl(\sigma_{n,xx}(s),u_{n,xx}\bigr)_\Omega\,ds.
\end{align}
Indeed, from \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_2$,
\[
(f_n^2,u_n)
=
i\omega(\psi_n,u_n)-\bar{\mu}_1(u_{n,xxxx},u_n)
-\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)(\sigma_{n,xxxx}(s),u_n)_\Omega\,ds,
\]
and two integrations by parts give
\[
(u_{n,xxxx},u_n)=\|u_{n,xx}\|^2,\qquad
(\sigma_{n,xxxx}(s),u_n)_\Omega=(\sigma_{n,xx}(s),u_{n,xx})_\Omega.
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{Re}(f_n^2,u_n)
=
\operatorname{Re}\bigl[i\omega(\psi_n,u_n)\bigr]
-\bar{\mu}_1\|u_{n,xx}\|^2
-\operatorname{Re}\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)(\sigma_{n,xx}(s),u_{n,xx})_\Omega\,ds.
\]
On the other hand, from \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_1$,
\[
(f_n^1,\psi_n)=i\omega(u_n,\psi_n)+\|\psi_n\|^2,
\]
so that
\[
\operatorname{Re}(f_n^1,\psi_n)
=
\operatorname{Re}\bigl[i\omega(u_n,\psi_n)\bigr]+\|\psi_n\|^2.
\]
Since the inner product is conjugate-linear in the second variable,
\[
\operatorname{Re}\bigl[i\omega(\psi_n,u_n)\bigr]
+
\operatorname{Re}\bigl[i\omega(u_n,\psi_n)\bigr]=0.
\]
Adding the two real parts therefore yields \eqref{eq:case2-u-identity}.
Now, by Poincar\'e's inequality, H\"older's inequality, and
$\|U_n\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1$, there exists a positive constant $c$ such that
\begin{align*}
\bar{\mu}_1\|u_{n,xx}\|^2
&\le c\|f_n^2\|\|u_n\|+c\|f_n^1\|\|\psi_n\|+\|\psi_n\|^2\\
&\quad+\|u_{n,xx}\|\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\|\sigma_{n,xx}(s)\|\,ds\\
&\le c\|f_n^2\|+c\|f_n^1\|+\|\psi_n\|^2
+\left(\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\,ds\right)^{1/2}\|\sigma_n\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}},
\end{align*}
where we used $\|u_n\|\le\|U_n\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1$. From
\eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_1$, \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_2$,
\eqref{gj:pq}, and \eqref{eq:u_n-small-L2}, we conclude
\begin{align}\label{ssf_5}
u_{n,xx}\to0\quad\text{in }L^2(\Omega).
\end{align}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: $\|v_{n,x}\|\to0$.}
Taking the $L^2$-inner products of \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_5$ with $v_n$ and
\eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_4$ with $\phi_n$, respectively, and then taking real
parts, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:case2-v-identity}
\bar{\mu}_2\|v_{n,x}\|^2
=
-\operatorname{Re}(f_n^5,v_n)-\operatorname{Re}(f_n^4,\phi_n)
+\|\phi_n\|^2
-\operatorname{Re}\int_0^{+\infty} g_2(s)\bigl(\eta_{n,x}(s),v_{n,x}\bigr)_\Omega\,ds.
\end{equation}
Indeed, from \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_5$,
\[
(f_n^5,v_n)
=
i\omega(\phi_n,v_n)+\bar{\mu}_2(v_{n,xx},v_n)
+\int_0^{+\infty} g_2(s)(\eta_{n,xx}(s),v_n)_\Omega\,ds,
\]
and two integrations by parts give
\[
(v_{n,xx},v_n)=-\|v_{n,x}\|^2,\qquad
(\eta_{n,xx}(s),v_n)_\Omega=-(\eta_{n,x}(s),v_{n,x})_\Omega.
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{Re}(f_n^5,v_n)
=
\operatorname{Re}\bigl[i\omega(\phi_n,v_n)\bigr]
-\bar{\mu}_2\|v_{n,x}\|^2
-\operatorname{Re}\int_0^{+\infty} g_2(s)(\eta_{n,x}(s),v_{n,x})_\Omega\,ds.
\]
On the other hand, from \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_4$,
\[
(f_n^4,\phi_n)=i\omega(v_n,\phi_n)+\|\phi_n\|^2,
\]
so that
\[
\operatorname{Re}(f_n^4,\phi_n)
=
\operatorname{Re}\bigl[i\omega(v_n,\phi_n)\bigr]+\|\phi_n\|^2.
\]
Since the inner product is conjugate-linear in the second variable,
\[
\operatorname{Re}\bigl[i\omega(\phi_n,v_n)\bigr]
+
\operatorname{Re}\bigl[i\omega(v_n,\phi_n)\bigr]=0.
\]
Adding the two real parts therefore yields \eqref{eq:case2-v-identity}.
By Poincar\'e's inequality, H\"older's inequality, and
$\|U_n\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1$, there exists a positive constant $c$ such that
\begin{align*}
\bar{\mu}_2\|v_{n,x}\|^2
&\le c\|f_n^5\|\|v_n\|+c\|f_n^4\|\|\phi_n\|+\|\phi_n\|^2+\|v_{n,x}\|\int_0^{+\infty} g_2(s)\|\eta_{n,x}(s)\|\,ds\\
&\le c\|f_n^5\|+c\|f_n^4\|+\|\phi_n\|^2
+\left(\int_0^{+\infty} g_2(s)\,ds\right)^{1/2}\|\eta_n\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_2}},
\end{align*}
where we used $\|v_n\|\le\|U_n\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1$. From
\eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_4$, \eqref{eq:convergence_1}$_5$,
\eqref{gj:pq}, and \eqref{eq:v_n-small-L2}, we conclude
\begin{align}\label{ssf_6}
v_{n,x}\to0\quad\text{in }L^2(\Omega).
\end{align}
Hence, from \eqref{gj:pq}, \eqref{ssf_5}, and \eqref{ssf_6}, we obtain
$\|U_n\|\to0$ in $\mathcal{H}$, which contradicts $\|U_n\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1$
for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$.

Next, we prove condition (ii), namely the uniform boundedness of the resolvent
operator $(i\lambda I-\mathcal{A})^{-1}$ for $|\lambda|$ sufficiently large.
Let $\Upsilon=(h^1,\ldots,h^6)\in\mathcal{H}$ be given, and let
$U=(u,\psi,\sigma,v,\phi,\eta)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$ satisfy the
resolvent equation
\begin{align}\label{eq:rq}
i\lambda U-\mathcal{A}U=\Upsilon,
\end{align}
which in components reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundedness}
\begin{cases}
i\lambda u+\psi=h^1,\\
i\lambda\psi-\gamma_1\psi-\bar{\mu}_1 u_{xxxx}
-\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}g_1(s)\sigma_{xxxx}(s)\,ds=h^2,\\
i\lambda\sigma-\sigma_s+\psi=h^3,\\
i\lambda v+\phi=h^4,\\
i\lambda\phi-\gamma_2\phi+\bar{\mu}_2 v_{xx}
+\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}g_2(s)\eta_{xx}(s)\,ds=h^5,\\
i\lambda\eta-\eta_s+\phi=h^6.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Our objective is to establish the existence of a constant $C>0$, independent
of $\lambda$, such that for every $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$ and every
$\Upsilon\in\mathcal{H}$, the solution $U$ of \eqref{eq:rq} satisfies
\begin{align}\label{bound}
\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}}\le C\|\Upsilon\|_{\mathcal{H}}.
\end{align}
Once \eqref{bound} is proved for all $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$, condition (ii) of
Theorem \ref{GP} follows immediately. In fact, the estimates below do not
require $|\lambda|$ to be large; they hold uniformly in $\lambda$.

\begin{lemma}\label{eq:hsx}
Under Assumption \ref{assumg}, there exists a constant $C>0$, independent of
$\lambda$, such that
\begin{align}\label{eq:hsx_estimate}
\|\psi\|^2,\ \|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}^2,\ \|\phi\|^2,\ \|\eta\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_2}}^2
\le C\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|\Upsilon\|_{\mathcal{H}}.
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma \ref{lem:monotone}, combined with \eqref{eq:rq}, we deduce
\begin{align*}
\gamma_1\|\psi\|^2+\frac{\lambda_1}{2}\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}^2
+\gamma_2\|\phi\|^2+\frac{\lambda_2}{2}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_2}}^2
&\le \operatorname{Re}\langle \mathcal{A} U,U\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\\
&=-\operatorname{Re}\langle i\lambda U-\mathcal{A}U,U\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\\
&=-\operatorname{Re}\langle\Upsilon,U\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
\le \|U\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|\Upsilon\|_{\mathcal{H}}.
\end{align*}
Thus \eqref{eq:hsx_estimate} follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{eq:u}
Under Assumption \ref{assumg}, there exists a constant $C>0$, independent of
$\lambda$, such that
\begin{align}\label{eq:u_estimate}
\bar{\mu}_1\|u_{xx}\|^2\le C\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|\Upsilon\|_{\mathcal{H}}.
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:boundedness}$_1$ we have $i\lambda u=h^1-\psi$. Taking the
$L^2$-inner product of \eqref{eq:boundedness}$_2$ with $u$ and using this
relation to replace $i\lambda u$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
(h^2,u)
&=
(i\lambda\psi-\gamma_1\psi-\bar{\mu}_1 u_{xxxx}
-\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\sigma_{xxxx}(s)\,ds,u)\\
&=
(\psi,\overline{i\lambda u})-\gamma_1(\psi,u)-\bar{\mu}_1\|u_{xx}\|^2
-\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)(\sigma_{xx}(s),u_{xx})_\Omega\,ds\\
&=
(\psi,\overline{h^1})-\|\psi\|^2-\gamma_1(\psi,u)-\bar{\mu}_1\|u_{xx}\|^2
-\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)(\sigma_{xx}(s),u_{xx})_\Omega\,ds.
\end{align*}
Here we used $\overline{i\lambda u}=\overline{h^1}-\overline{\psi}$, so that
$(\psi,\overline{i\lambda u})=(\psi,\overline{h^1})-\|\psi\|^2$. Taking real
parts gives
\begin{align*}
\bar{\mu}_1\|u_{xx}\|^2
&=
\operatorname{Re}(\psi,\overline{h^1})-\|\psi\|^2-\gamma_1\operatorname{Re}(\psi,u)
-\operatorname{Re}(h^2,u)\\
&\quad-\operatorname{Re}\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)(\sigma_{xx}(s),u_{xx})_\Omega\,ds.
\end{align*}
By the definition of the norm in $\mathcal{H}$,
\[
\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}}\le C\bigl(\|u_{xx}\|+\|\psi\|+\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:qstab}
Under Assumptions \ref{assumh} and \ref{assumg}, the solution semigroup
\(S(t)\) is quasi-stable on the absorbing ball \(\mathcal{B}_0\) in the sense
of Definition \ref{def:qstab}.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let \(U_0,V_0\in\mathcal{B}_0\) and set
\[
U(t):=S(t)U_0,\qquad V(t):=S(t)V_0,\qquad W(t):=U(t)-V(t),
\]
and
\[
\Phi(t):=F(U(t))-F(V(t)).
\]
Then \(W\) satisfies
\[
\partial_t W+\mathcal{A}W=\Phi(t),\qquad W(0)=U_0-V_0,
\]
in the mild sense. By the variation-of-constants formula for the linear
semigroup \(e^{-\mathcal{A} t}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Duhamel}
W(T)
=e^{-\mathcal{A}T}(U_0-V_0)
+\int_0^T e^{-\mathcal{A}(T-s)}\Phi(s)\,ds.
\end{equation}
Define the \emph{single-variable} nonlinear remainder
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KT-def}
K_T(U):=\int_0^T e^{-\mathcal{A}(T-s)}F(S(s)U)\,ds.
\end{equation}
Then
\[
K_T(U_0)-K_T(V_0)
=\int_0^T e^{-\mathcal{A}(T-s)}\Phi(s)\,ds,
\]
so that \eqref{eq:Duhamel} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W-split}
W(T)=e^{-\mathcal{A}T}(U_0-V_0)+K_T(U_0)-K_T(V_0).
\end{equation}

By Theorem \ref{Exponential stability-precise}, there exist \(\alpha\ge1\) and
\(\beta>0\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-decay}
\|e^{-\mathcal{A}\tau}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\le \alpha e^{-\beta\tau},
\qquad \forall \tau\ge0.
\end{equation}
Choose \(T>0\) sufficiently large so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T-choice}
q:=\alpha e^{-\beta T}<\frac12.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L-small}
\|e^{-\mathcal{A}T}(U_0-V_0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2
\le q^2\|U_0-V_0\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2.
\end{equation}

It remains to prove that \(K_T(\mathcal{B}_0)\) is relatively compact in
\(\mathcal{H}\). We split the proof into several steps.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: Uniform boundedness of the nonlinear orbits on
\([0,T]\).}
We first show that the orbits starting from \(\mathcal{B}_0\) remain uniformly
bounded on the finite time interval \([0,T]\). Let \(U_0\in\mathcal{B}_0\) and
\(U(t)=S(t)U_0\). Since \(\mathcal{B}_0\) is bounded and \(F\) is locally
Lipschitz, the standard local existence theory for the mild equation
\eqref{eq:mild-general} applies. Let \(R_1>R_0\) be as in
Remark \ref{rem:B0-bounded}, and let \(L_1\) be the corresponding Lipschitz
constant of \(F\) on \(\mathcal{B}_1\).

We claim that the solution \(U(t)\) remains in \(\mathcal{B}_1\) for all
\(0\le t\le T\), provided \(R_1\) is chosen sufficiently large. Indeed, as long
as \(\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\le R_1\), the mild formulation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}
&\le \|e^{-\mathcal{A}t}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\|U_0\|_{\mathcal{H}}
+\int_0^t \|e^{-\mathcal{A}(t-s)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}
\|F(U(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}}\,ds\\
&\le \alpha e^{-\beta t}R_0
+\alpha\int_0^t e^{-\beta(t-s)}
\bigl(L_1\|U(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|F(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplying by \(e^{\beta t}\), we obtain
\[
e^{\beta t}\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}
\le \alpha R_0
+\alpha\int_0^t e^{\beta s}
\bigl(L_1\|U(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|F(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)\,ds.
\]
By Gronwall's inequality,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gronwall}
\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}
\le
\Bigl(\alpha R_0 e^{-\beta t}
+\frac{\alpha\|F(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}}{\beta}
\bigl(1-e^{-\beta t}\bigr)\Bigr)
e^{\alpha L_1 t}
\le
C_1 e^{\alpha L_1 T},
\qquad
0\le t\le T,
\end{equation}
where
\[
C_1:=\alpha R_0+\frac{\alpha\|F(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}}}{\beta}.
\]
Now choose \(R_1\) so large that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R1-choice}
R_1>C_1 e^{\alpha L_1 T}.
\end{equation}
Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists \(t_*\in(0,T]\) such that
\(\|U(t_*)\|_{\mathcal{H}}=R_1\) and \(\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\le R_1\) for
\(0\le t\le t_*\). Then \eqref{eq:Gronwall} applies on \([0,t_*]\) and gives
\[
R_1=\|U(t_*)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\le C_1 e^{\alpha L_1 T}<R_1,
\]
a contradiction. Hence \(\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}<R_1\) for all \(0\le t\le T\),
and the solution remains in \(\mathcal{B}_1\). Setting
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R1}
R_*:=C_1 e^{\alpha L_1 T},
\end{equation}
we obtain
\[
\|S(t)U_0\|_{\mathcal{H}}\le R_*,
\qquad
U_0\in\mathcal{B}_0,\quad 0\le t\le T.
\]
Since \(F\) maps bounded sets into bounded sets, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-bound}
M_F:=\sup_{U\in\mathcal{B}_0}\sup_{0\le s\le T}\|F(S(s)U)\|_{\mathcal{H}}<+\infty.
\end{equation}
For notational simplicity, in the sequel we write \(M\) for \(M_F\). Moreover,
from the explicit form \eqref{F},
\[
F(U)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0\\
-k[u-v]^+ - h_1(u) + f_1\\
0\\
0\\
k[u-v]^+ - h_2(v) + f_2\\
0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
we see that \(F(U)\) has nonzero components only in the second and fifth
positions. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-subspace}
F(U)\in \{0\}\times L^2(\Omega)\times\{0\}\times\{0\}\times L^2(\Omega)\times\{0\}.
\end{equation}
In particular, the memory components of \(F(U)\) vanish identically.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: Splitting of \(K_T\).}
Fix \(\sigma\in(0,1]\) and split
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KT-split}
K_T(U)
=\underbrace{\int_0^{T-\sigma}e^{-\mathcal{A}(T-s)}F(S(s)U)\,ds}_{=:K_T^{(1)}(U)}
+\underbrace{\int_{T-\sigma}^T e^{-\mathcal{A}(T-s)}F(S(s)U)\,ds}_{=:K_T^{(2)}(U)}.
\end{equation}
The second term is small. Indeed, since \(e^{-\mathcal{A}t}\) is a
\(C_0\)-semigroup, the quantity
\[
C_0:=\sup_{0\le \tau\le 1}\|e^{-\mathcal{A}\tau}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}
\]
is finite. For \(0<\sigma\le 1\) and \(T-\sigma\le s\le T\), we have
\(0\le T-s\le\sigma\le1\), so that
\[
\|e^{-\mathcal{A}(T-s)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\le C_0.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K2-small}
\|K_T^{(2)}(U)\|_{\mathcal{H}}
\le \int_{T-\sigma}^T \|e^{-\mathcal{A}(T-s)}F(S(s)U)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\,ds
\le C_0 M \sigma.
\end{equation}
Thus, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), choosing \(\sigma>0\) so small that
\(C_0 M\sigma<\varepsilon\), we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K2-eps}
\sup_{U\in\mathcal{B}_0}\|K_T^{(2)}(U)\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
It remains to prove that \(K_T^{(1)}(\mathcal{B}_0)\) is relatively compact
in \(\mathcal{H}\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Representation of \(K_T^{(1)}\).}
Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K1-representation}
K_T^{(1)}(U)=e^{-\mathcal{A}\sigma}\Psi_\sigma(U),
\qquad
\Psi_\sigma(U):=\int_0^{T-\sigma}e^{-\mathcal{A}(T-s-\sigma)}F(S(s)U)\,ds.
\end{equation}
We decompose \(\Psi_\sigma(U)\) according to the state space:
\[
\Psi_\sigma(U)=
\bigl(\Psi_\sigma^u,\Psi_\sigma^\psi,\Psi_\sigma^\sigma,
\Psi_\sigma^v,\Psi_\sigma^\phi,\Psi_\sigma^\eta\bigr).
\]
Since \(F(S(s)U)\) has zero memory components by \eqref{eq:F-subspace}, the
memory components \(\Psi_\sigma^\sigma,\Psi_\sigma^\eta\) are generated by the
elastic velocities via the transport equations. The key point is that
the elastic components of \(\Psi_\sigma(U)\) gain regularity from the elliptic
part of \(\mathcal{A}\), while the memory components gain compactness from the
exponential decay of \(g_i\) and the compact embedding of \(H_a^2(\Omega)\)
into \(L^2(\Omega)\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 4: Regularity of the elastic components.}
Let us first consider the elastic part of \(\Psi_\sigma(U)\). We begin by
making the block structure of \(\mathcal{A}\) precise. Write
\[
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_e\oplus\mathcal{H}_m,
\qquad
\mathcal{H}_e=H_a^2(\Omega)\times L^2(\Omega)\times H_0^1(\Omega)\times L^2(\Omega),
\qquad
\mathcal{H}_m=\mathcal{M}_{g_1}\times\mathcal{M}_{g_2}.
\]
On \(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\), the operator \(\mathcal{A}\) admits the
block decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-block}
\mathcal{A}=
\begin{pmatrix}
\mathcal{A}_e & \mathcal{B}\\
\mathcal{C} & \mathcal{A}_m
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where
\[
\mathcal{A}_e=
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -I\\
\bar{\mu}_1 \partial_x^4 & \gamma_1 I
\end{pmatrix}
\oplus
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -I\\
-\bar{\mu}_2 \partial_x^2 & \gamma_2 I
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mathcal{A}_m=
\begin{pmatrix}
\partial_s & 0\\
0 & \partial_s
\end{pmatrix},
\]
the operator \(\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{H}_m\to\mathcal{H}_e\) is the bounded
operator that maps the memory variables into the elastic equations through the
convolutions
\[
(\sigma,\eta)\mapsto
\Bigl(B_1\sigma,B_2\eta\Bigr),
\]
with
\[
B_1\sigma:=\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\sigma_{xx}(s)\,ds\in L^2(\Omega),
\qquad
B_2\eta:=\int_0^{+\infty} g_2(s)\eta_x(s)\,ds\in L^2(\Omega),
\]
and the operator \(\mathcal{C}:\mathcal{H}_e\to\mathcal{H}_m\) is the
coupling induced by the transport equations, namely
\[
\mathcal{C}(u,\psi,v,\phi)=( -\psi, -\phi).
\]
Here the integrals defining \(B_1\) and \(B_2\) are understood as Bochner
integrals in \(L^2(\Omega)\); this is consistent with the definition of
\(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\), in which the fourth-order term is defined only
for the combined expression
\(\bar{\mu}_1 u+\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\sigma(s)\,ds\). The operator
\(\mathcal{B}\) is bounded from \(\mathcal{H}_m\) to \(\mathcal{H}_e\) because
of Assumption \ref{assumg}, while \(\mathcal{C}\) is clearly bounded from
\(\mathcal{H}_e\) to \(\mathcal{H}_m\). Note that both \(\mathcal{B}\) and
\(\mathcal{C}\) are nonzero: \(\mathcal{B}\) is precisely the feedback of the
memory variables into the elastic equations, whereas \(\mathcal{C}\) is the
coupling from the elastic velocities into the transport equations. The latter
coupling is not treated through the block decomposition below; instead, it is
handled separately in Step 5, where the memory components are represented
explicitly by solving the transport equations.

Using the block decomposition \eqref{eq:A-block}, the elastic component
\(\Xi(t)\) of \(e^{-\mathcal{A} t}\Upsilon\) with
\(\Upsilon=(\Upsilon_e,0)\) satisfies the Duhamel formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-Duhamel}
\Xi(t)
=
e^{-\mathcal{A}_e t}\Upsilon_e
+
\int_0^t
e^{-\mathcal{A}_e(t-\tau)}\mathcal{B}\,\Theta(\tau)\,d\tau,
\end{equation}
where \(\Theta(\tau)\) denotes the memory component of
\(e^{-\mathcal{A}\tau}\Upsilon\). This representation is obtained by applying
the variation-of-constants formula to the elastic equation
\[
\partial_t \Xi+\mathcal{A}_e\Xi=\mathcal{B}\Theta.
\]

With this notation, the elastic part of \(\Psi_\sigma(U)\) can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Psi-elastic}
\Psi_{\sigma,e}(U)
=\int_0^{T-\sigma} e^{-\mathcal{A}_e(T-s-\sigma)} F_e(S(s)U)\,ds
+\mathcal{R}_\sigma(U),
\end{equation}
where \(F_e\) denotes the second and fifth components of \(F\), and
\[
\mathcal{R}_\sigma(U)
=
\int_0^{T-\sigma}
e^{-\mathcal{A}_e(T-s-\sigma)}
\mathcal{B}\,\Theta(s)\,ds,
\]
with \(\Theta(s)\) the memory component of
\(e^{-\mathcal{A}(s-\tau)}F(S(\tau)U)\) for some \(\tau\in[0,T-\sigma]\). We
shall prove that \(\mathcal{R}_\sigma(\mathcal{B}_0)\) is relatively compact in
\(\mathcal{H}_e\).

The operator \(\mathcal{A}_e\) is sectorial with compact resolvent, and
\(-\mathcal{A}_e\) generates an analytic semigroup on \(\mathcal{H}_e\).
Consequently, the following smoothing estimates hold: for every \(t>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elastic-smoothing}
\|e^{-\mathcal{A}_e t} V\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_e^\theta)}
\le C_0 t^{-\theta} \|V\|_{\mathcal{H}_e},
\qquad 0<\theta<1,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elastic-smoothing-2}
\|e^{-\mathcal{A}_e t} V\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_e)}
\le C_1 t^{-1} \|V\|_{\mathcal{H}_e},
\qquad t>0,
\end{equation}
for some constants \(C_0,C_1>0\) depending only on \(\mathcal{A}_e\).

Since \(F_e(S(s)U)\in L^2(\Omega)\times L^2(\Omega)\), regarded as an element
of \(\mathcal{H}_e\) with only the second and fifth components nonzero, we have
the continuous embedding \(\mathcal{H}_e\hookrightarrow
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_e^{-1/2})\). More precisely, there exists a constant
\(C_2>0\) such that
\[
\|V\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_e^{-1/2})}\le C_2\|V\|_{\mathcal{H}_e},
\qquad \forall V\in\mathcal{H}_e.
\]
Therefore, using \eqref{eq:elastic-smoothing} with \(\theta=1/2\), we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elastic-smoothing-L2}
\|e^{-\mathcal{A}_e t} V\|_{\mathcal{H}_e}
\le C_0 C_2 t^{-1/2} \|V\|_{\mathcal{H}_e},
\qquad t>0,
\end{equation}
for every \(V\in\mathcal{H}_e\).

Using \eqref{eq:elastic-smoothing-L2} with \(t=T-s-\sigma\), and the uniform
boundedness \eqref{eq:M-bound} of \(F_e(S(s)U)\) in
\(\mathcal{H}_e\), we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Psi-elastic-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\Bigl\|\int_0^{T-\sigma} e^{-\mathcal{A}_e(T-s-\sigma)} F_e(S(s)U)\,ds\Bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}_e}
&\le C_0 C_2 \int_0^{T-\sigma} (T-s-\sigma)^{-1/2} \|F_e(S(s)U)\|_{\mathcal{H}_e}\,ds\\
&\le C_0 C_2 M \int_0^{T-\sigma} (T-s-\sigma)^{-1/2}\,ds
\le C,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(C\) depends only on \(T,\sigma,M\) and the constants in the smoothing
estimate. Moreover, applying \(e^{-\mathcal{A}_e\sigma}\) to the integral
term and using \eqref{eq:elastic-smoothing-2} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Psi-elastic-regularized}
\Bigl\|e^{-\mathcal{A}_e\sigma}
\int_0^{T-\sigma} e^{-\mathcal{A}_e(T-s-\sigma)} F_e(S(s)U)\,ds
\Bigr\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_e)}
\le C_1 \sigma^{-1} C.
\end{equation}
Thus
\[
\Bigl\{e^{-\mathcal{A}_e\sigma}
\int_0^{T-\sigma} e^{-\mathcal{A}_e(T-s-\sigma)} F_e(S(s)U)\,ds:
U\in\mathcal{B}_0\Bigr\}
\]
is bounded in \(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_e)\). Since \(\mathcal{A}_e\) has
compact resolvent, \(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_e)\) is compactly embedded in
\(\mathcal{H}_e\). Therefore this set is relatively compact in
\(\mathcal{H}_e\).

It remains to show that \(\mathcal{R}_\sigma(\mathcal{B}_0)\) is relatively
compact in \(\mathcal{H}_e\). We first prove that the coupling operators
\(B_1:\mathcal{M}_{g_1}\to L^2(\Omega)\) and
\(B_2:\mathcal{M}_{g_2}\to L^2(\Omega)\) are compact.

Consider \(B_1\). For \(R>0\), write
\[
B_1\sigma
=
\int_0^R g_1(s)\sigma_{xx}(s)\,ds
+
\int_R^{+\infty} g_1(s)\sigma_{xx}(s)\,ds
=:B_1^R\sigma+B_1^{\perp,R}\sigma.
\]
The tail is estimated by
\[
\|B_1^{\perp,R}\sigma\|_{L^2(\Omega)}
\le
\Bigl(\int_R^{+\infty} g_1(s)\,ds\Bigr)^{1/2}
\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}},
\]
which tends to zero as \(R\to{+\infty}\), uniformly in \(\sigma\), by Assumption
\ref{assumg}. For the truncated part, the map
\[
\sigma\mapsto B_1^R\sigma
=
\int_0^R g_1(s)\sigma_{xx}(s)\,ds
\]
is a Hilbert--Schmidt-type integral operator from
\(L^2_{g_1}(0,R;L^2(\Omega))\) to \(L^2(\Omega)\): indeed, its kernel is
\(g_1(s)I\), and
\[
\int_0^R\int_\Omega g_1(s)^2\,dx\,ds
\le
g_1(0)\int_0^R g_1(s)\,ds<{+\infty}.
\]
Hence \(B_1^R\) is compact. Since \(B_1^{\perp,R}\to0\) in operator norm as
\(R\to{+\infty}\), it follows that \(B_1\) is compact. The same argument applies
to \(B_2\). Therefore \(\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{H}_m\to\mathcal{H}_e\) is compact.

Since \(e^{-\mathcal{A}_e(T-s-\sigma)}\) is bounded and \(\Theta(\mathcal{B}_0)\)
is bounded in \(\mathcal{H}_m\) by \eqref{eq:M-bound}, the contribution
\(\mathcal{R}_\sigma(\mathcal{B}_0)\) is relatively compact in \(\mathcal{H}_e\).

Combining the two compactness facts, we conclude that
\[
\{e^{-\mathcal{A}_e\sigma}\Psi_{\sigma,e}(U):U\in\mathcal{B}_0\}
\]
is relatively compact in \(\mathcal{H}_e\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 5: Compactness of the memory components.}
Now we consider the memory components \(\Psi_\sigma^\sigma\) and
\(\Psi_\sigma^\eta\). We treat only \(\Psi_\sigma^\sigma\); the argument for
\(\Psi_\sigma^\eta\) is identical.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{5.1. Precise representation of the memory component.}
Recall that
\[
K_T(U)=\int_0^T e^{-\mathcal{A}(T-s)}F(S(s)U)\,ds,
\]
and that \(F(U)\) has zero memory components. Write
\[
e^{-\mathcal{A} t}\Upsilon
=\bigl(e^{-\mathcal{A} t}\Upsilon\bigr)_e
+\bigl(e^{-\mathcal{A} t}\Upsilon\bigr)_m,
\]
where the subscripts \(e\) and \(m\) denote the elastic and memory components,
respectively. Since \(F(S(s)U)\) has zero memory components, the memory
component of \(K_T(U)\) is generated solely by the elastic velocity through the
transport equations.

Using the block decomposition \eqref{eq:A-block} and the Duhamel
representation \eqref{eq:block-Duhamel}, for
\(\Upsilon=(\Upsilon_e,0)\in\mathcal{H}\) with \(\Upsilon_m=0\), the memory
component of \(e^{-\mathcal{A} t}\Upsilon\) solves
\[
\partial_t \Theta+\partial_s\Theta=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{tr}} \Xi(t),
\qquad \Theta(0,s)=0,
\]
where \(\Xi(t)\) is the elastic component of \(e^{-\mathcal{A} t}\Upsilon\) and
\(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{tr}}\) denotes the trace coupling induced by the
transport equations, namely
\[
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{tr}}\Xi(t)=(-\psi_e(t),-\phi_e(t)).
\]
More explicitly, if
\[
\Xi(t)=
\bigl(u_e(t),\psi_e(t),v_e(t),\phi_e(t)\bigr),
\]
then the memory component is
\[
\Theta(t,s)=
\bigl(\Theta^\sigma(t,s),\Theta^\eta(t,s)\bigr),
\]
where
\[
\partial_t\Theta^\sigma+\partial_s\Theta^\sigma=-\psi_e(t),
\qquad
\partial_t\Theta^\eta+\partial_s\Theta^\eta=-\phi_e(t),
\]
with zero initial and boundary conditions. The minus sign is consistent with
the fact that \(\sigma_t+\sigma_s-u_t=0\) and \(\eta_t+\eta_s-v_t=0\).

For the component \(\Psi_\sigma^\sigma\), we therefore have, for each fixed
source time \(\tau\in[0,T-\sigma]\),
\[
\Theta^\sigma(t,s;\tau)
=
-\int_{\max\{0,t-s\}}^{t}
\psi_e(r;\tau)\,dr,
\]
where
\[
\psi_e(r;\tau)
:=
\bigl[e^{-\mathcal{A}_e (r-\tau)}F_e(S(\tau)U)\bigr]_2,
\qquad r\ge\tau,
\]
and \(\psi_e(r;\tau)=0\) for \(r<\tau\). Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Psi-memory-precise}
\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)
=
-\int_0^{T-\sigma}
\int_{\max\{0,T-\sigma-\tau-s\}}^{T-\sigma-\tau}
\psi_e(r;\tau)
\,dr\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
Now change only the \emph{outer} time variable by setting
\(\theta=T-\sigma-\tau\). Then the inner time variable \(r\) is unchanged,
and the source term is evaluated at \(\tau=T-\sigma-\theta\). Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Psi-memory-precise-2}
\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)
=
-\int_0^{T-\sigma}
\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s\}}^{\theta}
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\,dr\,d\theta,
\end{equation}
with the \emph{two-variable} source
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi-src-def}
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)
:=
\bigl[e^{-\mathcal{A}_e (\theta-r)}F_e(S(T-\sigma-\theta)U)\bigr]_2,
\qquad 0\le r\le \theta\le T-\sigma.
\end{equation}
This is the precise form that we shall use. Note that
\(\psi_{\rm src}\) genuinely depends on both variables \(r\) and \(\theta\);
the inner variable \(r\) is not eliminated. The additional contribution of the
memory feedback \(\mathcal{B}\Theta\) to the elastic component has already been
handled in Step 4 through the term \(\mathcal{R}_\sigma(U)\).

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{5.2. Uniform boundedness of \(\psi_{\rm src}\).}
We first establish uniform bounds for \(\psi_{\rm src}\).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:psi-src-bound}
There exists a constant \(C_1>0\), independent of \(U\in\mathcal{B}_0\), such
that
\[
\sup_{0\le r<\theta\le T-\sigma}
(\theta-r)^{1/2}\|\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}
\le C_1,
\]
and
\[
\sup_{0\le \theta\le T-\sigma}\|\psi_{\rm src}(\theta,\theta)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le C_1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By definition,
\[
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)
=
\bigl[e^{-\mathcal{A}_e (\theta-r)}F_e(S(T-\sigma-\theta)U)\bigr]_2,
\qquad 0\le r\le \theta\le T-\sigma.
\]
Since \(U\in\mathcal{B}_0\), we have \(\|S(s)U\|_{\mathcal{H}}\le R_*\) for
\(0\le s\le T\), by \eqref{eq:R1}. Since \(F\) is locally Lipschitz and maps
bounded sets into bounded sets, there exists \(M>0\) such that
\[
\|F_e(S(s)U)\|_{\mathcal{H}_e}\le M,
\qquad 0\le s\le T.
\]
For \(r<\theta\), the analytic smoothing estimate \eqref{eq:elastic-smoothing}
with \(\theta=1/2\) yields
\[
\|e^{-\mathcal{A}_e (\theta-r)}F_e(S(T-\sigma-\theta)U)\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_e^{1/2})}
\le C_0 (\theta-r)^{-1/2}M.
\]
Since \(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_e^{1/2})\hookrightarrow H_a^2(\Omega)\) for the
second component, we obtain
\[
(\theta-r)^{1/2}\|\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}\le C_0 M.
\]
For \(r=\theta\), we have \(\psi_{\rm src}(\theta,\theta)=[F_e(S(T-\sigma-\theta)U)]_2\),
which is uniformly bounded in \(L^2(\Omega)\) by \(M\). This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{5.3. Uniform boundedness of \(\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)\) in
\(\mathcal{M}_{g_1}\).}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:memory-bound}
There exists a constant \(C_2>0\), independent of \(U\in\mathcal{B}_0\), such
that
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}\le C_2.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:Psi-memory-precise-2},
\[
\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)
=
-\int_0^{T-\sigma}
\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s\}}^{\theta}
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\,dr\,d\theta.
\]
Fix \(s\ge0\). We split the inner integral into the region
\(\theta-r\ge\delta\) and the region \(\theta-r<\delta\), where \(\delta>0\)
is a small parameter to be chosen. On the region \(\theta-r\ge\delta\),
Lemma \ref{lem:psi-src-bound} gives
\[
\|\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}\le C_1\delta^{-1/2},
\]
so the contribution of this region to
\(\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}\) is bounded by
\[
C_1\delta^{-1/2}\int_0^{T-\sigma}\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s\}}^\theta\,dr\,d\theta
\le C_1\delta^{-1/2}(T-\sigma)^2.
\]
On the region \(\theta-r<\delta\), we use the endpoint bound
\(\|\psi_{\rm src}(\theta,\theta)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le C_1\) and the
integrability of \((\theta-r)^{-1/2}\) to obtain
\[
\Bigl\|\int_0^{T-\sigma}\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s\}}^\theta
\mathbf 1_{\{\theta-r<\delta\}}\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\,dr\,d\theta\Bigr\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}
\le C_1(T-\sigma)\delta^{1/2}.
\]
Choosing \(\delta=(T-\sigma)^{-2}\) gives
\[
C_1\delta^{-1/2}(T-\sigma)^2=C_1(T-\sigma)^3,
\qquad
C_1(T-\sigma)\delta^{1/2}=C_1,
\]
so that
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)_{xx}\|
\le
C_1(1+(T-\sigma)^3)
\]
for all \(s\ge0\). By Poincar\'e's inequality in \(H_a^2(\Omega)\), there
exists a constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
\|w\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le c\|w_{xx}\|_{L^2(\Omega)},
\qquad
w\in H_a^2(\Omega).
\]
Hence
\[
\|w\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}^2
=
\|w\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2+\|w_{xx}\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
\le
(1+c^2)\|w_{xx}\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2,
\]
so that
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}
\le
c_a\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)_{xx}\|
\le
c_a C_1(1+(T-\sigma)^3),
\qquad
c_a:=\sqrt{1+c^2},
\]
for all \(s\ge0\). Consequently,
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}^2
=
\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)_{xx}\|^2\,ds
\le
C_1^2(1+(T-\sigma)^3)^2
\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\,ds.
\]
Since \(\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\,ds=\beta_1<{+\infty}\), the claim follows with
\[
C_2:=C_1(1+(T-\sigma)^3)\sqrt{\beta_1}.
\]
\end{proof}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{5.4. Tail smallness.}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:memory-tail}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(R>0\), depending only on
\(\varepsilon,T,\sigma,g_1\), such that
\[
\sup_{U\in\mathcal{B}_0}
\|\Psi_{\sigma,R}^{\sigma,\perp}(U)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}
<\varepsilon,
\]
where \(\Psi_{\sigma,R}^{\sigma,\perp}(U)\) is the restriction of
\(\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)\) to \(s>R\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From Lemma \ref{lem:memory-bound}, we have
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)_{xx}\|
\le C_1(1+(T-\sigma)^3)
\]
for all \(s\ge0\), and this bound is independent of \(s\). Using
\(g_1(s)\le g_1(0)e^{-\lambda_1 s}\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\Psi_{\sigma,R}^{\sigma,\perp}(U)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}^2
&=
\int_R^{+\infty} g_1(s)\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)_{xx}\|^2\,ds\\
&\le
C_1^2(1+(T-\sigma)^3)^2
\int_R^{+\infty} g_1(s)\,ds\\
&\le
C_1^2(1+(T-\sigma)^3)^2
\frac{g_1(0)}{\lambda_1}e^{-\lambda_1 R}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Choosing \(R\) sufficiently large so that the right-hand side is less than
\(\varepsilon^2\), the claim follows.
\end{proof}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{5.5. H\"older continuity in \(s\).}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:memory-equicont}
There exists a constant \(C_3>0\), independent of \(U\in\mathcal{B}_0\), such
that for all \(0\le s_1<s_2\),
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_1)-\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_2)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}
\le C_3|s_1-s_2|^{1/2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:Psi-memory-precise-2},
\[
\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)
=
-\int_0^{T-\sigma}
\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s\}}^{\theta}
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\,dr\,d\theta.
\]
For \(0\le s_1<s_2\), we have
\[
\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_1)-\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_2)
=
-\int_0^{T-\sigma}
\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s_1\}}^{\max\{0,\theta-s_2\}}
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\,dr\,d\theta.
\]
For fixed \(\theta\), the inner integral is over an interval of length at most
\(|s_1-s_2|\). Using Lemma \ref{lem:psi-src-bound}, we estimate
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Bigl\|\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s_1\}}^{\max\{0,\theta-s_2\}}
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\,dr\Bigr\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}\\
&\le
C_1\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s_1\}}^{\max\{0,\theta-s_2\}}
(\theta-r)^{-1/2}\,dr\\
&\le
C_1\bigl|(\theta-\max\{0,\theta-s_1\})^{1/2}
-(\theta-\max\{0,\theta-s_2\})^{1/2}\bigr|\\
&\le
C_1|s_1-s_2|^{1/2},
\end{aligned}
\]
where we used the elementary inequality
\(|a^{1/2}-b^{1/2}|\le|a-b|^{1/2}\) for \(a,b\ge0\), together with
\(|a-b|\le|s_1-s_2|\). Integrating over \(\theta\in[0,T-\sigma]\) gives
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_1)-\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_2)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}
\le
C_1(T-\sigma)|s_1-s_2|^{1/2}.
\]
This proves the lemma with \(C_3:=C_1(T-\sigma)\).
\end{proof}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{5.6. Compactness on the compact interval \([0,R]\).}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:memory-compact-interval}
For every \(R>0\), the set
\[
\{\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)|_{[0,R]}:U\in\mathcal{B}_0\}
\]
is relatively compact in \(L^2([0,R];H_a^2(\Omega))\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma \ref{lem:memory-equicont}, the family
\(\{\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)|_{[0,R]}:U\in\mathcal{B}_0\}\) is uniformly
H\"older continuous of order \(1/2\) with values in \(H_a^2(\Omega)\). By
Lemma \ref{lem:memory-bound}, it is uniformly bounded in
\(L^\infty([0,R];H_a^2(\Omega))\). Hence it is bounded in
\(H^{1/2}([0,R];H_a^2(\Omega))\). Since the embedding
\[
H^{1/2}([0,R];H_a^2(\Omega))\hookrightarrow L^2([0,R];H_a^2(\Omega))
\]
is compact (Rellich--Kondrachov), the set is relatively compact in
\(L^2([0,R];H_a^2(\Omega))\).
\end{proof}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{5.7. Overall compactness in \(\mathcal{M}_{g_1}\).}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:memory-compact}
The set
\[
\{\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U):U\in\mathcal{B}_0\}
\]
is relatively compact in \(\mathcal{M}_{g_1}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\varepsilon>0\). By Lemma \ref{lem:memory-tail}, choose \(R>0\) such
that
\[
\sup_{U\in\mathcal{B}_0}
\|\Psi_{\sigma,R}^{\sigma,\perp}(U)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}
<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
By Lemma \ref{lem:memory-compact-interval}, the set
\[
\{\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)|_{[0,R]}:U\in\mathcal{B}_0\}
\]
is relatively compact in \(L^2([0,R];H_a^2(\Omega))\). Hence there exist
finitely many functions \(V_1,\dots,V_N\in L^2([0,R];H_a^2(\Omega))\) such that
\[
\{\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)|_{[0,R]}:U\in\mathcal{B}_0\}
\subset
\bigcup_{j=1}^N B_{\varepsilon/(2\sqrt{g_1(0)})}(V_j),
\]
where the balls are taken in \(L^2([0,R];H_a^2(\Omega))\). Extend each \(V_j\)
by zero outside \([0,R]\), and regard it as an element of \(\mathcal{M}_{g_1}\).
For any \(U\in\mathcal{B}_0\), choose \(j\) such that
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)|_{[0,R]}-V_j\|_{L^2([0,R];H_a^2(\Omega))}
<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\sqrt{g_1(0)}}.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)-V_j\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}&\le
\|\Psi_{\sigma,R}^{\sigma}(U)-V_j\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}
+
\|\Psi_{\sigma,R}^{\sigma,\perp}(U)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}\\
&\le
\sqrt{g_1(0)}\,
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)|_{[0,R]}-V_j\|_{L^2([0,R];H_a^2(\Omega))}
+
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
<
\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\{\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U):U\in\mathcal{B}_0\}
\subset
\bigcup_{j=1}^N B_{\varepsilon}(V_j)
\]
in \(\mathcal{M}_{g_1}\). Hence the set is totally bounded, and since
\(\mathcal{M}_{g_1}\) is complete, it is relatively compact.
\end{proof}

The same argument applies to \(\Psi_\sigma^\eta\) in \(\mathcal{M}_{g_2}\).
Therefore the set
\[
\{\Psi_\sigma(U):U\in\mathcal{B}_0\}
\]
is relatively compact in \(\mathcal{H}\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 6: Relative compactness of \(K_T^{(1)}(\mathcal{B}_0)\).}
Combining Steps 4 and 5, we obtain that
\[
\{\Psi_\sigma(U):U\in\mathcal{B}_0\}
\]
is relatively compact in \(\mathcal{H}\). Since \(e^{-\mathcal{A}\sigma}\) is a
bounded linear operator on \(\mathcal{H}\), it maps relatively compact sets
into relatively compact sets. Therefore
\[
K_T^{(1)}(\mathcal{B}_0)=e^{-\mathcal{A}\sigma}\Psi_\sigma(\mathcal{B}_0)
\]
is relatively compact in \(\mathcal{H}\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 7: Relative compactness of \(K_T(\mathcal{B}_0)\).}
We have shown that for every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(\sigma>0\) such
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K2-uniform-small}
\sup_{U\in\mathcal{B}_0}\|K_T^{(2)}(U)\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
and \(K_T^{(1)}(\mathcal{B}_0)\) is relatively compact in \(\mathcal{H}\).
Therefore \(K_T(\mathcal{B}_0)\) is relatively compact in \(\mathcal{H}\).
Indeed, let \(\{U_n\}\subset\mathcal{B}_0\) be any sequence. Since
\(K_T^{(1)}(\mathcal{B}_0)\) is relatively compact, there is a subsequence
\(\{U_{n_k}\}\) such that \(K_T^{(1)}(U_{n_k})\) converges in \(\mathcal{H}\).
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|K_T(U_{n_k})-K_T(U_{n_l})\|_{\mathcal{H}}
&\le \|K_T^{(1)}(U_{n_k})-K_T^{(1)}(U_{n_l})\|_{\mathcal{H}}
+\|K_T^{(2)}(U_{n_k})\|_{\mathcal{H}}
+\|K_T^{(2)}(U_{n_l})\|_{\mathcal{H}}\\
&\le \varepsilon_{k,l}+2\varepsilon,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(\varepsilon_{k,l}\to0\) as \(k,l\to{+\infty}\). Since
\(\varepsilon>0\) is arbitrary, \(\{K_T(U_{n_k})\}\) is Cauchy in
\(\mathcal{H}\), hence converges. Thus \(K_T(\mathcal{B}_0)\) is relatively
compact.

Combining \eqref{eq:W-split}, \eqref{eq:L-small}, and the compactness of
\(K_T(\mathcal{B}_0)\), we obtain \eqref{eq:qstab-ineq} with \(q<1/2\). This
completes the proof.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:psi-src-bound}
There exists a constant \(C_1>0\), independent of \(U\in\mathcal{B}_0\), such
that
\[
\sup_{0\le r<\theta\le T-\sigma}
(\theta-r)^{1/2}\|\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}
\le C_1,
\]
and
\[
\sup_{0\le \theta\le T-\sigma}\|\psi_{\rm src}(\theta,\theta)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le C_1.
\]
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
By definition,
\[
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)
=
\bigl[e^{-\mathcal{A}_e (\theta-r)}F_e(S(T-\sigma-\theta)U)\bigr]_2,
\qquad 0\le r\le \theta\le T-\sigma.
\]
Since \(U\in\mathcal{B}_0\), we have \(\|S(s)U\|_{\mathcal{H}}\le R_*\) for
\(0\le s\le T\), by \eqref{eq:R1}. Since \(F\) is locally Lipschitz and maps
bounded sets into bounded sets, there exists \(M>0\) such that
\[
\|F_e(S(s)U)\|_{\mathcal{H}_e}\le M,
\qquad 0\le s\le T.
\]
For \(r<\theta\), the analytic smoothing estimate \eqref{eq:elastic-smoothing}
with \(\theta=1/2\) yields
\[
\|e^{-\mathcal{A}_e (\theta-r)}F_e(S(T-\sigma-\theta)U)\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_e^{1/2})}
\le C_0 (\theta-r)^{-1/2}M.
\]
Since \(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_e^{1/2})\hookrightarrow H_a^2(\Omega)\) for the
second component, we obtain
\[
(\theta-r)^{1/2}\|\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}\le C_0 M.
\]
For \(r=\theta\), we have \(\psi_{\rm src}(\theta,\theta)=[F_e(S(T-\sigma-\theta)U)]_2\),
which is uniformly bounded in \(L^2(\Omega)\) by \(M\). This proves the lemma.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:memory-bound}
There exists a constant \(C_2>0\), independent of \(U\in\mathcal{B}_0\), such
that
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}\le C_2.
\]
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
From \eqref{eq:Psi-memory-precise-2},
\[
\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)
=
-\int_0^{T-\sigma}
\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s\}}^{\theta}
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\,dr\,d\theta.
\]
Fix \(s\ge0\). We split the inner integral into the region
\(\theta-r\ge\delta\) and the region \(\theta-r<\delta\), where \(\delta>0\)
is a small parameter to be chosen. On the region \(\theta-r\ge\delta\),
Lemma \ref{lem:psi-src-bound} gives
\[
\|\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}\le C_1\delta^{-1/2},
\]
so the contribution of this region to
\(\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}\) is bounded by
\[
C_1\delta^{-1/2}\int_0^{T-\sigma}\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s\}}^\theta\,dr\,d\theta
\le C_1\delta^{-1/2}(T-\sigma)^2.
\]
On the region \(\theta-r<\delta\), we use the endpoint bound
\(\|\psi_{\rm src}(\theta,\theta)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le C_1\) and the
integrability of \((\theta-r)^{-1/2}\) to obtain
\[
\Bigl\|\int_0^{T-\sigma}\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s\}}^\theta
\mathbf 1_{\{\theta-r<\delta\}}\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\,dr\,d\theta\Bigr\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}
\le C_1(T-\sigma)\delta^{1/2}.
\]
Choosing \(\delta=(T-\sigma)^{-2}\) gives
\[
C_1\delta^{-1/2}(T-\sigma)^2=C_1(T-\sigma)^3,
\qquad
C_1(T-\sigma)\delta^{1/2}=C_1,
\]
so that
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)_{xx}\|
\le
C_1(1+(T-\sigma)^3)
\]
for all \(s\ge0\). By Poincar\'e's inequality in \(H_a^2(\Omega)\), there
exists a constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
\|w\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le c\|w_{xx}\|_{L^2(\Omega)},
\qquad
w\in H_a^2(\Omega).
\]
Hence
\[
\|w\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}^2
=
\|w\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2+\|w_{xx}\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
\le
(1+c^2)\|w_{xx}\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2,
\]
so that
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}
\le
c_a\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)_{xx}\|
\le
c_a C_1(1+(T-\sigma)^3),
\qquad
c_a:=\sqrt{1+c^2},
\]
for all \(s\ge0\). Consequently,
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}^2
=
\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)_{xx}\|^2\,ds
\le
C_1^2(1+(T-\sigma)^3)^2
\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\,ds.
\]
Since \(\int_0^{+\infty} g_1(s)\,ds=\beta_1<{+\infty}\), the claim follows with
\[
C_2:=C_1(1+(T-\sigma)^3)\sqrt{\beta_1}.
\]

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:memory-tail}
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(R>0\), depending only on
\(\varepsilon,T,\sigma,g_1\), such that
\[
\sup_{U\in\mathcal{B}_0}
\|\Psi_{\sigma,R}^{\sigma,\perp}(U)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}
<\varepsilon,
\]
where \(\Psi_{\sigma,R}^{\sigma,\perp}(U)\) is the restriction of
\(\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)\) to \(s>R\).
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
From Lemma \ref{lem:memory-bound}, we have
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)_{xx}\|
\le C_1(1+(T-\sigma)^3)
\]
for all \(s\ge0\), and this bound is independent of \(s\). Using
\(g_1(s)\le g_1(0)e^{-\lambda_1 s}\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\Psi_{\sigma,R}^{\sigma,\perp}(U)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{g_1}}^2
&=
\int_R^{+\infty} g_1(s)\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)_{xx}\|^2\,ds\\
&\le
C_1^2(1+(T-\sigma)^3)^2
\int_R^{+\infty} g_1(s)\,ds\\
&\le
C_1^2(1+(T-\sigma)^3)^2
\frac{g_1(0)}{\lambda_1}e^{-\lambda_1 R}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Choosing \(R\) sufficiently large so that the right-hand side is less than
\(\varepsilon^2\), the claim follows.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:memory-equicont}
There exists a constant \(C_3>0\), independent of \(U\in\mathcal{B}_0\), such
that for all \(0\le s_1<s_2\),
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_1)-\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_2)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}
\le C_3|s_1-s_2|^{1/2}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
From \eqref{eq:Psi-memory-precise-2},
\[
\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s)
=
-\int_0^{T-\sigma}
\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s\}}^{\theta}
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\,dr\,d\theta.
\]
For \(0\le s_1<s_2\), we have
\[
\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_1)-\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_2)
=
-\int_0^{T-\sigma}
\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s_1\}}^{\max\{0,\theta-s_2\}}
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\,dr\,d\theta.
\]
For fixed \(\theta\), the inner integral is over an interval of length at most
\(|s_1-s_2|\). Using Lemma \ref{lem:psi-src-bound}, we estimate
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Bigl\|\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s_1\}}^{\max\{0,\theta-s_2\}}
\psi_{\rm src}(r,\theta)\,dr\Bigr\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}\\
&\le
C_1\int_{\max\{0,\theta-s_1\}}^{\max\{0,\theta-s_2\}}
(\theta-r)^{-1/2}\,dr\\
&\le
C_1\bigl|(\theta-\max\{0,\theta-s_1\})^{1/2}
-(\theta-\max\{0,\theta-s_2\})^{1/2}\bigr|\\
&\le
C_1|s_1-s_2|^{1/2},
\end{aligned}
\]
where we used the elementary inequality
\(|a^{1/2}-b^{1/2}|\le|a-b|^{1/2}\) for \(a,b\ge0\), together with
\(|a-b|\le|s_1-s_2|\). Integrating over \(\theta\in[0,T-\sigma]\) gives
\[
\|\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_1)-\Psi_\sigma^\sigma(U)(s_2)\|_{H_a^2(\Omega)}
\le
C_1(T-\sigma)|s_1-s_2|^{1/2}.
\]
This proves the lemma with \(C_3:=C_1(T-\sigma)\).

\label{rem:correction}
The above proof addresses the point in \cite[Proposition~4.1]{Mukiawa2025}
that was discussed earlier. In that work, starting from the difference estimate
\[
\|U^1(t)-U^2(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2
\le c\|U_0^1-U_0^2\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2
+c_{\mathcal{B}}\int_0^t\bigl(\|u^1(s)-u^2(s)\|^2+\|v^1(s)-v^2(s)\|^2\bigr)\,ds,
\]
the non-decaying initial-difference term
\(c\|U_0^1-U_0^2\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2\) appears to be absorbed into an arbitrarily
small constant \(\epsilon\), a step that would seem to call for further
justification. Here, the exponential decay of the linear semigroup
\(e^{-\mathcal{A}T}\) provides the rigorous compression factor \(q<1\) in
\eqref{eq:L-small}, while the nonlinear remainder \(K_T\) is compact by the
smoothing of the elastic component and the exponential decay of the memory
kernels. Together, these ingredients yield a complete proof of
quasi-stability.
