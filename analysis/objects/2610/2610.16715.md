<!-- head-1: body_md -->
\emph{What is the worst-case online prefix discrepancy for immediate and irrevocable vector decisions, and how does this compare with the offline optimum?}\medskip
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:blackbox-main}
\Cref{alg:tree-routing} assigns every vector immediately to an
unused position and does not need to know the input total. Randomized
local signers use independent tapes, independent of the input.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \textbf{Terminal vector and count bounds.}
Suppose the input vectors have norm at most one in a fixed norm
$\lVert\cdot\rVert$. At a node receiving $x_1,\ldots,x_m$, suppose the
signer's proposed signs satisfy
\[
 \left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^m\varepsilon_i x_i\right\rVert\le V(m),
 \qquad
 \left|\sum_{i=1}^m\varepsilon_i\right|\le W(m).
\]
Whenever these inequalities hold at all internal nodes, every output
prefix satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \lVert\mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)\rVert
 \le\sum_{u\in P(k)}
 \left(\frac{V(m_u)+W(m_u)}2+\1\{m_u\text{ odd}\}\right).
 \label{eq:reduction-norm}
\end{equation}
If they hold for every local stream, this is a deterministic guarantee
against adaptive inputs. If, for each fixed length-$m$ stream, they
hold jointly with probability at least $1-p(m)$, then for every
oblivious input \eqref{eq:reduction-norm} holds simultaneously for all
prefixes with probability at least $1-\sum_u p(m_u)$.

\item \textbf{Subgaussian terminal sums.}
Suppose the inputs are in the Euclidean unit ball and the signer has
scale $\Sigma\ge1$ in the sense of \eqref{eq:terminal-mgf}, in dimension
$d+1$ and for every node capacity. Then, for every oblivious input
and $0<\delta<1$, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{res}_{\max}(v_\pi)
   \le C\Sigma\left(\log T+\sqrt{\log T\bigl(d+\log(1/\delta)\bigr)}\right),
 \label{eq:reduction-subgaussian}
\end{equation}
where $C$ is an absolute constant.
\end{enumerate}
Both parts require only the \emph{terminal} guarantee for each local
stream. For zero-sum inputs, the residuals are the ordinary prefix sums.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:one-path}
For every output prefix $k$, the boundary path and coefficients above
satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)=\sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}\mathrm{err}_u.
 \label{eq:one-path}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The cases $k=0,T$ are immediate. At a node $u\in P(k)$, write
$s=|I_u\cap[k]|$. If $s\le m_{u_L}$, the local prefix lies entirely in
the left child, and adding and subtracting
$(s/m_{u_L})\mathrm{tot}_{u_L}$ gives
\begin{align*}
    F_u(s)
    =F_{u_L}(s)+\frac{s}{m_{u_L}}\mathrm{tot}_{u_L}
      -\frac{s}{m_u}\mathrm{tot}_u =F_{u_L}(s)+\frac{s}{m_{u_L}}\mathrm{err}_u.
\end{align*}
Here $\alpha_{u,k}=s/m_{u_L}$, since
$(m_u-s)/m_{u_R}\ge1$. If instead $s=m_{u_L}+t$ with
$0\le t\le m_{u_R}$, the prefix contains the whole left child and the
first $t$ positions of the right child, so
\begin{align*}
    F_u(m_{u_L}+t)
    &=F_{u_R}(t)+\mathrm{tot}_{u_L}+\frac{t}{m_{u_R}}\mathrm{tot}_{u_R}
      -\frac{m_{u_L}+t}{m_u}\mathrm{tot}_u\\
    &=F_{u_R}(t)+\mathrm{err}_u
      +\frac{t}{m_{u_R}}
       \left(\mathrm{tot}_{u_R}-\frac{m_{u_R}}{m_u}\mathrm{tot}_u\right) =F_{u_R}(t)+\left(1-\frac{t}{m_{u_R}}\right)\mathrm{err}_u.
\end{align*}
In this case $\alpha_{u,k}=1-t/m_{u_R}$, since
$(m_{u_L}+t)/m_{u_L}\ge1$.
Starting with $F_{\rm root}(k)=\mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)$, each substitution
therefore adds the term $\alpha_{u,k}\mathrm{err}_u$ and leaves only the
residual in the child crossed by the prefix boundary. More explicitly,
when the recursion reaches a node $u$, it has established
\[
 \mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)
 =\sum_{\substack{v\in P(k)\\v\text{ a strict ancestor of }u}}
    \alpha_{v,k}\mathrm{err}_v
   +F_u\bigl(|I_u\cap[k]|\bigr).
\]
The formulas above preserve this identity as we descend to the unique
child that meets both the prefix and its complement. If the prefix
ends between the two children, then $s=m_{u_L}$ and the remaining
residual is $F_{u_L}(m_{u_L})=0$; a residual at a leaf is also zero.
Thus the recursion visits exactly the nodes of $P(k)$ and leaves no
residual term, proving \eqref{eq:one-path}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:quota-repair}
Let $u$ be an internal node of capacity $m$, receiving Euclidean
unit-ball vectors $x_1,\ldots,x_m$, and write $\mu=m^{-1}\sum_i x_i$.
Let $\varepsilon_i$ be the proposed signs and $\hat \varepsilon_i$ the quota-corrected
routing signs, where $+1$ means left, and put $q=\sum_i\varepsilon_i, Y=\sum_i\varepsilon_i(x_i-\mu)$ for succinctness. Then the local proportional error of \eqref{eq:local-error} has a
decomposition
\[
    \mathrm{err}_u=\frac12Y+R,
    \qquad \lVert R\rVert_2\le |q|+1.
\]
Under the subgaussian hypothesis of \cref{thm:blackbox-main},
for a fixed input, conditionally on the routing tapes at the strict
ancestors of $u$, for every unit vector $z\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and every
$\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$,
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E\!\left[e^{\lambda\left\langle z,Y\right\rangle/2}\mid\text{strict ancestors}\right]
 &\le e^{\Sigma^2\lambda^2/2},
 \label{eq:y-mgf}\\
 \mathbb E\!\left[e^{\lambda q}\mid\text{strict ancestors}\right]
 &\le e^{\Sigma^2\lambda^2}.
 \label{eq:q-mgf}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We first count the changes made by quota repair. Since the children
receive exactly their prescribed numbers of vectors, the corrected
signs satisfy $\sum_i\hat \varepsilon_i=\1\{m\text{ odd}\}$. Once a proposal is overridden, one
child is full, so every later override changes a proposal in the same
direction. Each change alters the signed count by two, and comparing
the proposed count $q$ with the corrected count $\1\{m\text{ odd}\}$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \text{number of overrides}=\frac12|q-\1\{m\text{ odd}\}|.
 \label{eq:override-count}
\end{equation}
\cref{fig:deterministic-quota-repair} illustrates this identity for even $m$ and $q=2$.
To translate this into a bound on the split error, we simply use 
$m_{u_L}/m=(1+\1\{m\text{ odd}\}/m)/2$ and $\mathrm{tot}_u=m\mu$ to write
\[
 \mathrm{err}_u
 =\frac12\left(\sum_i\hat \varepsilon_i x_i-\1\{m\text{ odd}\}\mu\right)
 =\frac12\sum_i\hat \varepsilon_i(x_i-\mu)
 =\frac12Y+\underbrace{\frac12\sum_i(\hat \varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_i)(x_i-\mu)}_{\coloneqq R},
\]
Each overridden sign contributes one vector
$\pm(x_i-\mu)$, whose norm is at most two because both $x_i$ and
$\mu$ lie in the unit ball. Together with \eqref{eq:override-count},
this yields $\lVert R\rVert_2\le |q-\1\{m\text{ odd}\}|\le |q|+1$.

We now turn to the conditional estimates, and fix the complete tapes of the strict
ancestors of $u$. The original input is fixed, and ancestor routing
never consults descendant signers, so these tapes determine the
entire stream reaching $u$, while its private tape remains independent
with its original distribution. We can therefore apply
\eqref{eq:terminal-mgf} to the augmented stream
$(x_i,1)/\sqrt2$, whose terminal signed sum is
$(\sum_i\varepsilon_i x_i,q)/\sqrt2$.
Taking $\theta=(\lambda/\sqrt2)(z,-\left\langle z,\mu\right\rangle)$ gives
\[
 \left\langle
   \frac{\lambda}{\sqrt2}(z,-\left\langle z,\mu\right\rangle),\,
   \frac1{\sqrt2}\left(\sum_i\varepsilon_i x_i,q\right)
 \right\rangle
 =\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\langle z,Y\right\rangle,
 \qquad
 \lVert \theta\rVert_2^2
 =\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\left(1+\left\langle z,\mu\right\rangle^2\right)
 \le\lambda^2,
\]
which substituted in \eqref{eq:terminal-mgf} proves \eqref{eq:y-mgf}.
Similarly, we may choose $\theta=(0,\ldots,0,\sqrt2\lambda)$, which has squared norm
$2\lambda^2$ and inner product $\lambda q$ with the augmented signed
sum, giving \eqref{eq:q-mgf}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
First, note that the same quota calculation applies in any norm. Moreover, with the notation of
\cref{lem:quota-repair}, each override changes the signed vector sum
by a vector of norm at most two. Hence \eqref{eq:override-count} and
the two terminal bounds assumed in part~\textup{(i)} of the statement give
\[
 \left\lVert\sum_i\hat \varepsilon_i x_i\right\rVert
 \le \left\lVert\sum_i\varepsilon_i x_i\right\rVert+|q-\1\{m\text{ odd}\}|
 \le V(m)+W(m)+\1\{m\text{ odd}\}.
\]
We now have
\begin{equation}
 \lVert\mathrm{err}_u\rVert = \left\lVert \frac12 \left(\sum_i\hat \varepsilon_i x_i-\frac{\1\{m\text{ odd}\}}{m}\mathrm{tot}_u\right)\right\rVert
 \le\frac{V(m)+W(m)}2+\1\{m\text{ odd}\},
 \label{eq:norm-tail-local}
\end{equation}
where the equality holds by definition of the exact proportional error $\mathrm{err}_u$ and the inequality by the fact that $\lVert\mathrm{tot}_u/m\rVert\le1$. Applying the triangle inequality to \cref{lem:one-path} now gives
\[
 \lVert\mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)\rVert
 \le\sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}\lVert\mathrm{err}_u\rVert
 \le\sum_{u\in P(k)}
 \left(\frac{V(m_u)+W(m_u)}2+\1\{m_u\text{ odd}\}\right),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:reduction-norm}. This argument holds for every
realized input and collection of proposed signs, so a deterministic
signer satisfying the terminal bounds on every stream also gives the
claimed guarantee against adaptive inputs.

For the probabilistic statement, fix an oblivious input and say that
a node fails if either of its two terminal bounds is violated.
Conditioning on its strict-ancestor tapes fixes its entire input
stream while leaving its own tape independent, just as in
\cref{lem:quota-repair}. The assumed fixed-stream guarantee thus bounds
its conditional failure probability by $p(m_u)$. Taking expectations
and then a union bound gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb{P}\!\left(\exists \text{ node }u\text{ fails}\right)
 &\le\sum_{u\text{ internal}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(\text{node }u\text{ fails}\right) =\sum_{u\text{ internal}}\mathbb E\!\left[
   \mathbb{P}\!\left(\text{node }u\text{ fails}
     \,\middle|\,\mathrm{anc}(u)\right)\right] \le\sum_{u\text{ internal}}p(m_u).
\end{align*}
Outside this failure event, all local bounds hold and the pathwise
argument above applies simultaneously to every prefix, which gives the first part of the theorem.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:path-composition}
Assume the subgaussian condition of \cref{thm:blackbox-main}, and
write $Y_u,q_u,R_u$ for the quantities in \cref{lem:quota-repair} at
node $u$. There is an absolute constant $C$ such that, for every
prefix $k$ and every $s>0$,
\begin{align}
 \mathbb{P}\!\left(
 \left\|\frac12\sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}Y_u\right\|_2
 >C\Sigma\sqrt{h(d+s)}\right)
 &\le e^{-s},
 \label{eq:path-vector-tail}\\
 \mathbb{P}\!\left(
 \sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}|q_u|
 >C\Sigma\sqrt{h(h+s)}\right)
 &\le e^{-s}.
 \label{eq:path-count-tail}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The statement is immediate when $P(k)$ is empty, so assume otherwise.
We first reduce the vector bound to a scalar tail estimate by choosing a
$1/2$-net of the Euclidean unit sphere with at most $e^{2d}$ points.
For every nonzero vector $x$, a net point within distance $1/2$ of
$x/\|x\|_2$ has inner product at least $\|x\|_2/2$ with $x$.
Consequently, a union bound over the net proves
\eqref{eq:path-vector-tail}, with $C=4$, as soon as we show that every
fixed unit vector $z$ satisfies
\[
 \mathbb{P}\!\left(
   \left\langle z,\frac12\sum_{v\in P(k)}\alpha_{v,k}Y_v\right\rangle
   >2\Sigma\sqrt{h(d+s)}
 \right)\le e^{-2d-s}.
\]
Indeed, a norm exceeding $4\Sigma\sqrt{h(d+s)}$ forces at least one
net direction to exceed the displayed threshold, and
$e^{2d}e^{-2d-s}=e^{-s}$. It thus remains to prove this directional
estimate.

To this end, let us fix $z$ and $\lambda>0$, and note that the path and its coefficients depend only
on the position tree and $k$, so they are deterministic. Let $u$ be
the deepest node of $P(k)$ so that every other node
$v\in P(k)\setminus\{u\}$ is a strict ancestor of $u$ (see
\cref{fig:path-conditioning}). Since $Y_v$ depends only on the tapes
at $v$ and its ancestors, conditioning on all strict-ancestor tapes
of $u$ fixes every term except the one at $u$. Its own tape remains
independent, and \eqref{eq:y-mgf}, applied with
$\lambda\alpha_{u,k}$, bounds its conditional exponential moment.
Pulling out the fixed terms and then taking expectations gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E\exp\!\left(
   \frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{v\in P(k)}\alpha_{v,k}\left\langle z,Y_v\right\rangle
 \right) &=\mathbb E\!\left[
   \exp\!\left(
    \frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{v\in P(k)\setminus\{u\}}
       \alpha_{v,k}\left\langle z,Y_v\right\rangle\right)
   \mathbb E\!\left[
     e^{\lambda\alpha_{u,k}\left\langle z,Y_u\right\rangle/2}
     \,\middle|\,\mathrm{anc}(u)
   \right]\right]\\
 &\le e^{\Sigma^2\lambda^2\alpha_{u,k}^2/2}
   \mathbb E\exp\!\left(
    \frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{v\in P(k)\setminus\{u\}}
       \alpha_{v,k}\left\langle z,Y_v\right\rangle\right).
\end{align*}
Thus averaging over the tape at $u$ removes its term from the sum,
at the cost of the deterministic factor
$e^{\Sigma^2\lambda^2\alpha_{u,k}^2/2}$. If any terms remain, the
parent of $u$ is now the deepest node, and the same calculation
applies with that parent in place of $u$. Continuing upward to the
root removes one term at a time and multiplies these factors, giving
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\!\left(
   \frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{v\in P(k)}\alpha_{v,k}\left\langle z,Y_v\right\rangle
 \right)
 \le\exp\!\left(
   \frac{\Sigma^2\lambda^2}{2}
   \sum_{v\in P(k)}\alpha_{v,k}^2\right)
 \le\exp\!\left(\frac{h\Sigma^2\lambda^2}{2}\right).
\]
The last inequality uses $0\le\alpha_{v,k}\le1$ and $|P(k)|\le h$.
Chernoff's bound with $\lambda=2\sqrt{d+s}/(\Sigma\sqrt h)$ now yields
\[
 \mathbb{P}\!\left(
   \left\langle z,\frac12\sum_{v\in P(k)}\alpha_{v,k}Y_v\right\rangle
   >2\Sigma\sqrt{h(d+s)}
 \right)\le e^{-2(d+s)}\le e^{-2d-s},
\]
which is precisely the directional estimate needed above.

For the count imbalances, we use the same conditioning argument,
now with \eqref{eq:q-mgf}. For $\lambda>0$, the inequality
$e^{\lambda|x|}\le e^{\lambda x}+e^{-\lambda x}$ gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E\!\left[
   e^{\lambda\alpha_{u,k}|q_u|}
   \,\middle|\,\mathrm{anc}(u)\right] \le\mathbb E\!\left[
   e^{\lambda\alpha_{u,k}q_u}+e^{-\lambda\alpha_{u,k}q_u}
   \,\middle|\,\mathrm{anc}(u)\right]
 \le 2e^{\Sigma^2\lambda^2\alpha_{u,k}^2}.
\end{align*}
Removing the nodes from deepest to root as before, and using
$2^{|P(k)|}\le e^h$ and $\sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}^2\le h$, we obtain $\mathbb E\exp\!\left(\lambda\sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}|q_u|\right)
 \le e^{h+h\Sigma^2\lambda^2}$. Markov's inequality with $\lambda=\sqrt{h+s}/(\Sigma\sqrt h)$ therefore
gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb{P}\!\left(
    \sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}|q_u|
    >2\Sigma\sqrt{h(h+s)}\right) \le
 \exp\!\left(h+h\Sigma^2\lambda^2
             -2\lambda\Sigma\sqrt{h(h+s)}\right)
 =e^{-s},
\end{align*}
which proves \eqref{eq:path-count-tail}.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For $T=1$ all residuals vanish, so assume $T\ge2$. Fix $k$ and use
\cref{lem:quota-repair,lem:one-path} to write
\[
 \mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)=\frac12\sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}Y_u
 +\sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}R_u.
\]
The first term is controlled by \eqref{eq:path-vector-tail}, while
$0\le\alpha_{u,k}\le1$ and $|P(k)|\le h$ give
\[
 \left\|\sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}R_u\right\|_2
 \le\sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}(|q_u|+1)
 \le h+\sum_{u\in P(k)}\alpha_{u,k}|q_u|.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:path-count-tail} controls the second term. Apply both
estimates with $s=\log(2(T+1)/\delta)$ and take a union bound over the
$T+1$ prefixes. The total failure probability is at most
$2(T+1)e^{-s}=\delta$, so with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{res}_{\max}(v_\pi)
 \le C\Sigma\left(\sqrt{h(d+s)}+\sqrt{h(h+s)}\right)+h.
 \label{eq:blackbox-explicit}
\end{equation}
Finally, $s \in O(h+\log(1/\delta))$ and $\Sigma\ge1$, so
\eqref{eq:blackbox-explicit} is at most
$C\Sigma\bigl(h+\sqrt{h(d+\log(1/\delta))}\bigr)$ after increasing the
absolute constant, yielding \eqref{eq:reduction-subgaussian}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:deterministic-main}
For every $T\ge2$, it holds that
\[
    \operatorname{OS}_{\det}(d,T) \in
    \begin{cases}
        O(\log T),&d=1,\\[2mm]
        O(\sqrt T),&d\ge2.
    \end{cases}
\]
These upper bounds hold against adaptive adversaries.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We use the following local rule at every node of
\cref{alg:tree-routing}, interpreting a left proposal as $+1$
and a right proposal as $-1$.
Fix a node $u$. For the local argument, write $L,R$ for the sums
already sent to its children and $r_L,r_R$ for their remaining
capacities; once the node is full, $L=\mathrm{tot}_{u_L}$ and
$R=\mathrm{tot}_{u_R}$. We maintain
\begin{equation}
    |L+r_Ly|+|R+r_Ry|
    \le
    |L+R+(r_L+r_R)y|+2
    \qquad(y\in[-1,1]).
    \label{eq:scalar-completion-invariant}
\end{equation}
Here $L+r_Ly$ and $R+r_Ry$ are the child totals if all remaining
positions receive the same value $y$. The inequality fails exactly
when one total exceeds $1$ and the other is below $-1$. Initially
$L=R=0$, so the two totals have the same sign and the invariant holds.

If one child is full, the local rule proposes the other, so the
meta-algorithm follows that proposal. Otherwise, propose a destination
according to
\[
    \begin{cases}
        \displaystyle
        \text{left if }\frac{L+1}{r_L}\le\frac{R+1}{r_R},
        \text{ right otherwise},
        & L+R+(r_L+r_R)v_t\ge0,\\[3mm]
        \displaystyle
        \text{left if }\frac{L-1}{r_L}\ge\frac{R-1}{r_R},
        \text{ right otherwise},
        & L+R+(r_L+r_R)v_t<0.
    \end{cases}
\]
Thus the hypothetical total determines which comparison the local
rule uses; the meta-algorithm updates the chosen sum and capacity
and continues from that child.
For preservation, suppose both children have space, the hypothetical
total using $v_t$ is nonnegative, and the rule chooses left.
A subsequent completion with $y$ would give child totals
$L+v_t+(r_L-1)y$ and $R+r_Ry$. 

There are only two possible
violations of the invariant: first, suppose the new left total exceeds $1$ and the right
total is below $-1$. The left total changed by $v_t-y\le2$, so its previous value
$L+r_Ly$ must exceed $-1$. Together with $R+r_Ry<-1$, this gives
\[
    \frac{L+1}{r_L}>-y>\frac{R+1}{r_R},
\]
contradicting the choice of the smaller ratio. Second, suppose the new left total is below $-1$ and the
right total exceeds $1$.
The old invariant at $y$ requires $L+r_Ly\ge-1$, so the assignment
must have decreased this total, meaning $v_t<y$. Therefore
\[
    L+r_Lv_t
    =
    \underbrace{L+v_t+(r_L-1)y}_{<-1}
    +
    \underbrace{(r_L-1)(v_t-y)}_{\le0}
    <-1.
\]
Applying the old invariant at $v_t$ now forces $R+r_Rv_t\le1$,
and hence $L+R+(r_L+r_R)v_t<0$, contradicting the assumed
nonnegative total. Choosing right is symmetric, and the
negative-total case follows by reversing all signs.

Forced routing also preserves the invariant. Indeed, if the left child
is full, assigning $v_t$ right and then filling its remaining
positions with $y$ gives the same totals as the previous
hypothetical completion with common value
$(v_t+(r_R-1)y)/r_R\in[-1,1]$.
The old invariant applies directly; the other case is symmetric.

At completion, $r_L=r_R=0$, so the invariant becomes
\[
    |\mathrm{tot}_{u_L}|+|\mathrm{tot}_{u_R}|\le|\mathrm{tot}_u|+2.
\]
Since nothing in the argument requires equal child capacities, the
rule applies directly to the balanced tree for every $T$.

To conclude, fix an output prefix $[1,k]$. Starting from the root,
split only the intervals in $P(k)$, as illustrated in \cref{fig:scalar-prefix-refinement}. Each split increases the sum of absolute interval totals by at most $2$. After these splits,
the prefix is a union of complete intervals, so the triangle inequality gives
\[
    \left|\sum_{t=1}^k v_{\pi(t)}\right|
    \le |\mathrm{tot}_{\mathrm{root}}|+2|P(k)|
    \le 2h.
\]
The last inequality uses the zero root total and the tree height.
The invariant holds for every arriving value, so the guarantee
also holds against adaptive adversaries.

At an internal node $u$, let $v_{u,i}$ be its $i$th arriving vector
and let $S_{u,i}$ be the signer's augmented signed sum after that
arrival, initially $S_{u,0}=0$. Choose
$\varepsilon_{u,i}\in\{-1,+1\}$ so that
\[
    \varepsilon_{u,i}\left\langle
        S_{u,i-1},\frac{(v_{u,i},1)}{\sqrt2}
    \right\rangle\le0,
\]
breaking ties in favor of $+1$, and update
$S_{u,i}=S_{u,i-1}+\varepsilon_{u,i}(v_{u,i},1)/\sqrt2$.
The update records the proposed sign even if the algorithm overrides
its routing request. Expanding the squared norm gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \|S_{u,i}\|_2^2
    =\|S_{u,i-1}\|_2^2
      +\frac{\|v_{u,i}\|_2^2+1}{2}
      +2\varepsilon_{u,i}\left\langle
          S_{u,i-1},\frac{(v_{u,i},1)}{\sqrt2}
       \right\rangle
    \le\|S_{u,i-1}\|_2^2+1\le i.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the vector and count imbalances obey the joint bound
\[
    \left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_u}\varepsilon_{u,i}v_{u,i}\right\|_2^2
    +\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_u}\varepsilon_{u,i}\right|^2
    =2\|S_{u,m_u}\|_2^2\le2m_u.
\]
The local estimate in the norm-and-count part of
\cref{thm:blackbox-main}, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, therefore gives
\[
    \|\mathrm{err}_u\|_2
    \le\frac12\left(
        \left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_u}\varepsilon_{u,i}v_{u,i}\right\|_2
        +\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_u}\varepsilon_{u,i}\right|
       \right)+\1\{m_u\text{ odd}\} \le\sqrt{m_u}+\1\{m_u\text{ odd}\}.
\]
The boundary-path conclusion of \cref{thm:blackbox-main} now yields
\[
    \|\mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)\|_2 \le\sum_{u\in P(k)}\|\mathrm{err}_u\|_2 \le(\sqrt2+1)\sqrt T

\]
Indeed, every internal node has children of capacity at most
$2m_u/3$, so a node at depth $\ell$ has capacity at most
$T(2/3)^\ell$, and $P(k)$ contains at most one node at each depth.
Every estimate holds for every local stream, which also proves
the adaptive guarantee.

\label{lem:deterministic-lower}
There exists an absolute constant $c>0$ such that, for every $T\ge2$
and $d\ge1$, every deterministic online ordering algorithm has a
fixed zero-sum unit-ball input satisfying
\[
 \mathrm{res}_{\max}(v_\pi)\ge

On the line, let us take $N=\lfloor T/2\rfloor$ and write
$p_i=p_{i,1}$ for the common positions in the construction above.
Let $c_0>0$ be the absolute constant in Schmidt's
theorem~\cite[Theorem~2]{Schmidt1972}. Applied to the points
$((i-1)/N,(p_i-1)/T)$, it gives $x,y\in[0,1]$ such that
\[
 \left|
 \left|\left\{
 i\le N:
 \frac{i-1}{N}<x,\ 
 \frac{p_i-1}{T}<y
 \right\}\right|
 -Nxy
 \right|
 \ge c_0\log N.
\]
Here the cardinality counts the points in $[0,x)\times[0,y)$,
while $Nxy$ is $N$ times the area of this rectangle. We now set $s=\lceil Nx\rceil$ and $k=\lceil Ty\rceil$, and note that rounding the upper sides to $s/N$ and $k/T$ preserves the point count because $(i-1)/N < x$ if and only if $i \le s$ and $(p_i-1)/T < y$ if and only if $p_i \le k$. 
In addition, this changes the area term by at most
\[
 0\le \frac{sk}{T}-Nxy
 =
 N\left(\frac{s}{N}-x\right)y
 +s\left(\frac{k}{T}-y\right)
 \le 1+\frac{N}{T}\le\frac32.
\]
The reverse triangle inequality therefore gives
\[
 \left|
 \lvert\{i\le s:p_i\le k\}\rvert-\frac{sk}{T}
 \right|
 \ge c_0\log N-\frac32
 \ge c_0\log N-2.
\]
On the input $I_s$, the completion identity
\eqref{eq:scalar-prefix-identity} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)
 =
 \frac{T}{T-s}
 \left(\lvert\{i\le s:p_i\le k\}\rvert-\frac{sk}{T}\right).
 \label{eq:scalar-lower-cost}
\end{equation}
Since $T/(T-s)\ge1$, this proves the logarithmic lower bound

$m=\lfloor T/4\rfloor$, and focus on the output prefix
$k=\lfloor T/2\rfloor$.
During the first $m$ arrivals, let $\xi_t=+1$ if the algorithm assigns
$v_t$ to one of the first $k$ positions, and $\xi_t=-1$ otherwise,
and write $U_t=\sum_{i=1}^t v_i, Z_t=\sum_{i=1}^t \xi_i v_i$ with $U_0=Z_0=0$.
We choose the vectors so that the difference $Z_t$ between the
two groups grows, while their total $U_t$ stays small enough that
the completion cannot cancel this difference. At time $t$, choose a unit vector $v_t$ in the plane spanned by
$e_1,e_2$ perpendicular to $Z_{t-1}$, reversing its orientation
if necessary so that $\left\langle U_{t-1},v_t\right\rangle\le0$.
This choice uses only previous assignments, and whichever value
of $\xi_t$ the algorithm then produces,
\[
 \lVert Z_t\rVert_2^2=\lVert Z_{t-1}\rVert_2^2+1,
 \qquad
 \lVert U_t\rVert_2^2\le\lVert U_{t-1}\rVert_2^2+1.
\]
Hence $\lVert Z_m\rVert_2=\sqrt m$ and $\lVert U_m\rVert_2\le\sqrt m$. We now complete the stream with $T-m$ copies of $-U_m/(T-m)$.
Among the initial vectors, those assigned to the first $k$
positions have sum $(U_m+Z_m)/2$ and number
$(m+\sum_{t=1}^m \xi_t)/2$, so
\eqref{eq:scalar-prefix-identity} gives
\[
 2\mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)
 =
 Z_m+\frac{T-2k+\sum_{t=1}^m \xi_t}{T-m}U_m.
\]
Since $T-2k\in\{0,1\}$ and
$\lvert\sum_{t=1}^m \xi_t\rvert\le m$, the triangle inequality yields
\[
 2\lVert \mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)\rVert_2
 \ge
 \left(1-\frac{m+1}{T-m}\right)\sqrt m
 \ge \frac{\sqrt m}{2}
 \ge \frac{\sqrt T}{4\sqrt2},
\]
where $(m+1)/(T-m)\le1/2$ and $m\ge T/8$ for $T\ge8$.
Choosing $c>0$ sufficiently small gives both bounds for large $T$;
the remaining bounded values follow from the one-cycle bound below
after further decreasing $c$. All vectors lie in the same fixed plane, and although the initial stream was described adaptively, for any deterministic algorithm its assignments can be simulated in advance, producing a fixed input with the claimed cost.

Let $d=1$ and $N=\lfloor T/2\rfloor$. The family \eqref{eq:scalar-family} becomes
\[
 I_s=\left(
 \underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{s\text{ copies}},
 \underbrace{-\frac{s}{T-s},\ldots,-\frac{s}{T-s}}_{T-s\text{ copies}}
 \right),
 \qquad s\in[N].
\]
Let us write $\mathrm{res}_{\max}(I_s)$ for the maximum residual of the algorithm's output on $I_s$, and let us fix a random tape so that the algorithm's decisions are deterministic, with $p_i$ being the output position assigned to the $i$th arriving copy of $1$ on $I_N$.
Using the same tape, its first $s$ assignments on $I_s$ are also $p_1,\ldots,p_s$, since both inputs begin with $s$ copies of $1$.
For $s\in[N]$, denote the discrepancy over output prefixes as
\[
 \Delta_s:=
 \max_{0\le k\le T}
 \left|
   \lvert\{i\le s:p_i\le k\}\rvert-\frac{sk}{T}
 \right|.
\]
By \eqref{eq:scalar-lower-cost}, we have
$\Delta_s=(T-s)/T \cdot \mathrm{res}_{\max}(I_s)\le\mathrm{res}_{\max}(I_s)$.
To apply Tijdeman--Wagner's theorem on an infinite sequence in $[0,1)$, we first append infinitely many zeros after $((p_i-1)/T)_{i=1}^N$.
By Tijdeman--Wagner's theorem~\cite[Theorem~1]{TW80}, there exists an absolute constant $c_0>0$ such that for every fixed tape,
\[
 \left|\left\{1\le s\le N:\Delta_s+\frac{1}{2}<c_0\log N\right\}\right|
 \le 2N^{5/6}.
\]
Here the term $1/2$ comes from restricting interval endpoints to multiples of $1/T$.
Now, for any $y\in[0,1)$, we round $y$ upwards to $\lceil Ty\rceil/T$, and, since all points lie on this grid, the intervals $[0,y)$ and $[0,\lceil Ty\rceil/T)$ contain the same points.
The term $sy$ changes by at most $s/T \leq 1/2$,
so the discrepancy over arbitrary intervals $[0,y)$ is at most $\Delta_s+1/2$.

Let $c_0$ be the constant in Tijdeman--Wagner's theorem and set
$c_1=\min\{c_0/4,1/26\}$. If $c_1\log T<1/2$, the input $I_1$
gives the desired bound with probability one. Otherwise,
$\log T\ge 13$, so $N>2^{12}$ and $\log N>1/2 \cdot \log T$, giving $c_1\log T+ 1/2 \le 2c_1\log T <c_0\log N$. This gives that, for any $s$ with $\mathrm{res}_{\max}(I_s)\le c_1\log N$,
\[
 \Delta_s+\frac{1}{2}
 \le \mathrm{res}_{\max}(I_s)+\frac{1}{2}
 \le c_1\log N+\frac{1}{2}
 <c_0\log N.
\]
Therefore for each random tape, there are at most
$2N^{5/6}$ indices with $\mathrm{res}_{\max}(I_s)\le c_1\log N$.
Averaging over the algorithm's random tape gives
\[
 \frac{1}{N}\sum_{s=1}^N
 \mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{res}_{\max}(I_s)>c_1\log N\right)
 \ge 1-2N^{-\frac{1}{6}}>\frac{1}{2},
\]
so there exists a fixed $I_s$ which has maximum residual greater than $c\log N$ with probability greater than $1/2$. Hence, some fixed
input $I_s$ satisfies
\[
 \mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{res}_{\max}(I_s)>c\log T\right)>\frac12,
\]
for $c = c_1/2$, which concludes the proof.

\label{lem:random-dimension-lower}
There exists an absolute constant $c>0$ such that the following holds.
Let $T\ge2$, $d\ge1$, and $0<\delta<1$.
If a randomized online ordering algorithm satisfies
\[
 \mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{res}_{\max}(v_\pi)\le \rho\right)\ge1-\delta
\]
on every fixed zero-sum unit-ball input, then $\rho\ge
 c(1-\delta)\sqrt{d_*\log(T/d_*)}$, where $d_*:=\min\{d,T\}$.

When $d>T/2$, the one-cycle input in
\cref{lem:deterministic-lower} has cost $\Omega(\sqrt{d_*})$
under every ordering and, since $\log(2+T/d_*) \in O(1)$, this proves
the claim for sufficiently small $c>0$.

Therefore we assume that $d\le T/2$ and let $N=\lfloor T/(2d)\rfloor$.
The expected fraction of inputs $I_1,\ldots,I_N$ on which the
algorithm succeeds is at least $1-\delta$, and so we fix
a tape that succeeds on a set $S\subseteq[N]$ with probability $p:=|S|/N\ge1-\delta$.
For its positions $p_{i,j}$, define for each $j=1,\ldots,d$ and $(x,y)\in[0,1]^2$,
\[
 D_j(x,y):=
 \left|\left\{
 i\in[N]:
 \frac{i-1}{N}<x,\ 
 \frac{p_{i,j}-1}{T}<y
 \right\}\right|-Nxy,
\]
and for fixed $s\in[N]$ and $0\le k\le T$, write $a_j:=D_j\left(s/N,k/T\right) =\lvert\{i\le s:p_{i,j}\le k\}\rvert-sk/T$. The identity in \eqref{eq:scalar-prefix-identity} can thus be rewritten as
\[
 \mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)
 =(a_j)_{j=1}^d
 +\frac{s}{T-ds}\left(\sum_{j=1}^d a_j\right)\mathbf1.
\]
Note that these two terms have nonnegative inner product since
\[
 \left\langle
 (a_j)_{j=1}^d,\,
 \frac{s}{T-ds}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^d a_\ell\right)\mathbf1
 \right\rangle
 =
 \frac{s}{T-ds}
 \left(\sum_{\ell=1}^d a_\ell\right)
 \left(\sum_{j=1}^d a_j\right)
 =
 \frac{s}{T-ds}\left(\sum_{j=1}^d a_j\right)^2
 \ge0,
\]
and therefore, for every $s\in S$
and $0\le k\le T$, success on $I_s$ implies
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^d D_j\left(\frac{s}{N},\frac{k}{T}\right)^2
 \le \|\mathrm{res}_k(v_\pi)\|_2^2
 \le \rho^2.
\]
We now define the set $E=\bigcup_{s\in S}((s-1)/N,s/N)$, so that each successful
stopping time contributes an interval of length $1/N$ and
$|E|=|S|/N=p$. We extend the discrepancy bound at successful
grid points to these intervals to show that
\begin{equation}
 \int_E\int_0^1\sum_{j=1}^d D_j(x,y)^2\,dy\,dx
 \le p\left(\rho+\frac{3\sqrt d}{2}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:dimension-success-integral}
\end{equation}
For any $1\le s\le N$, $1\le k\le T$, and
$(x,y)\in((s-1)/N,s/N)\times((k-1)/T,k/T)$, we have
\[
 \frac{i-1}{N}<x\iff i\le s,
 \qquad
 \frac{p_{i,j}-1}{T}<y\iff p_{i,j}\le k.
\]
Thus $(x,y)$ and the upper corner $(s/N,k/T)$ count exactly
the same points, so only the area term changes:
\[
 0\le D_j(x,y)-D_j\left(\frac{s}{N},\frac{k}{T}\right)
 =\frac{sk}{T}-Nxy =N\left(\frac{s}{N}-x\right)y
   +s\left(\frac{k}{T}-y\right)
 \le1+\frac{s}{T}\le\frac32.
\]
When $s\in S$, success bounds the discrepancy norm at the grid
corner by $\rho$, and the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \left(\sum_{j=1}^d D_j(x,y)^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le
 \left(\sum_{j=1}^d
 D_j\left(\frac{s}{N},\frac{k}{T}\right)^2\right)^{1/2}
 +\frac{3\sqrt d}{2}
 \le\rho+\frac{3\sqrt d}{2}.
\]
This holds almost everywhere on $E\times[0,1]$; squaring and
integrating over this set of area $p$ proves
\eqref{eq:dimension-success-integral}.


In the following claim, we prove a lower bound of the contribution of each $D_j$ to the integral \eqref{eq:dimension-success-integral}, i.e., a lower bound of $\int_E\int_0^1 D_j(x,y)^2\,dy\,dx$ for any each coordinate $j$. 
\begin{claim}\label{clm:restricted-roth}
Let $N\ge1$ and let $(u_i,v_i)\in[0,1]^2$ for $i=1,\ldots,N$.
For $(x,y)\in[0,1]^2$, define
\[
 D(x,y):=
 \left|\left\{i\in[N]:u_i<x,\ v_i<y\right\}\right|-Nxy.
\]
Then for every $E\subseteq[0,1]$ which is a union of intervals, with $p=|E|>0$, 
\[
 \int_E\int_0^1 D(x,y)^2\,dy\,dx
 \ge Cp^3\log(N+2),
\]
where $C>0$ is an absolute constant.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{clm:restricted-roth}]
We apply Roth's empty-rectangle argument~\cite{Roth54} on $E\times[0,1]$, letting $n=\lceil\log_2(2N)\rceil$ for succinctness.
First we construct two recursive partitions of $[0,1]$, one for each coordinate.
\begin{itemize}
    \item For the $x$-coordinate, start with $[0,1]$. Split each interval
    $I$ at a point $z$ into a left child $I_-$ and a right child
    $I_+$ such that
    \[
     |E\cap I_-|=|E\cap I_+|=\frac{|E\cap I|}{2}.
    \]
    Thus the split divides the length of the part of $E$ inside
    $I$ equally between the children; the children themselves
    need not have equal lengths. Such a point $z$ exists because
    the length of $E$ to its left varies continuously with $z$.
    Let $\mathcal I_a$ denote the collection of intervals after
    $a$ rounds of splitting. It contains $2^a$ intervals, each
    satisfying $|E\cap I|=p2^{-a}$.
    \item For the $y$-coordinate, repeatedly split each interval at its
    midpoint. Let $\mathcal J_b$ denote the collection after $b$
    rounds. It contains $2^b$ intervals, each of length $2^{-b}$.
    We construct both partitions through level $n+1$, so that
    every interval used below also has two children.
\end{itemize}
For each integer $a=0,\ldots,n$, consider all rectangles
$I\times J$ with $I\in\mathcal I_a$ and
$J\in\mathcal J_{n-a}$, which satisfy $|E\cap I|=p2^{-a}, |J|=2^{-(n-a)}$. There are $2^a2^{n-a}=2^n$ such rectangles, with disjoint
interiors, and, since the point set defining $D$ has $N$ points,
at most $N\le2^{n-1}$ rectangles contain a point in their
interior. We select exactly $2^{n-1}$ rectangles whose interiors
contain no points, which over all $a$, selects
$(n+1)2^{n-1}$ rectangles. Next, for each selected rectangle $I\times J$, let us write $I_-,I_+$
for the left and right children of $I$, and $J_-,J_+$ for
the lower and upper halves of $J$. We then define
\[
 h_{I,J}(x,y)=
 \begin{cases}
  +1,&(x,y)\in(I_-\times J_-)\cup(I_+\times J_+),\\
  -1,&(x,y)\in(I_-\times J_+)\cup(I_+\times J_-),\\
  0,&(x,y)\notin I\times J,
 \end{cases}
\]
with values on the boundaries that may be set to zero. 
Applying the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality to $D$
and $\sum_{I,J}h_{I,J}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roth-cauchy-schwarz}
    \int_E\int_0^1 D(x,y)^2\,dy\,dx
    \ge
    \frac{
    \left(\int_E\int_0^1
    D(x,y)\sum_{I,J}h_{I,J}(x,y)\,dy\,dx\right)^2
    }{
    \int_E\int_0^1
    \left(\sum_{I,J}h_{I,J}(x,y)\right)^2\,dy\,dx
    },
\end{equation}
where the sum is over all selected rectangles $(I,J)$. We will deal with the two terms denominator and numerator separately. First we compute the denominator: since the lengths of $E\cap I_-$ and $E\cap I_+$ are equal and so are $J_-$ and $J_+$, for every fixed $y$ and every fixed $x$, we have
\[
 \int_E h_{I,J}(x,y)\,dx=
 \int_0^1 h_{I,J}(x,y)\,dy=0, 
 \qquad
 \int_E\int_0^1 h_{I,J}(x,y)^2\,dy\,dx
 =|E\cap I|\,|J|
 =p2^{-n}.
\]
The functions $h_{I,J}$ associated with the selected rectangles
are pairwise orthogonal, since either the rectangles have disjoint
interiors, or the finer $x$-sign has integral zero over $E$
while the coarser sign is constant on that interval. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \int_E\int_0^1
 \left(\sum_{I,J}h_{I,J}(x,y)\right)^2\,dy\,dx
 =(n+1)2^{n-1}p2^{-n}
 =\frac{p(n+1)}{2}.
 \label{eq:roth-denominator}
\end{equation}
We next bound the numerator: for an empty rectangle, every point has at least one
coordinate outside the corresponding interval's interior.
Its counting indicator is constant almost everywhere in
that coordinate, so its integral against $h_{I,J}$ vanishes.
Consequently, only the term $-Nxy$ in $D$ contributes:
\[
 -\int_E\int_0^1 D(x,y)h_{I,J}(x,y)\,dy\,dx
 =
 N\int_{E\cap I}\int_J xyh_{I,J}(x,y)\,dy\,dx.
\]
To bound this expression, let $z$ be the splitting point of
$I$ and put $q=p2^{-a-1}$, the common length of
$E\cap I_-$ and $E\cap I_+$. Then
\begin{align*}
 \int_{E\cap I_+}x\,dx-\int_{E\cap I_-}x\,dx
 =
 \int_{E\cap I_+}(x-z)\,dx
 +\int_{E\cap I_-}(z-x)\,dx\ge 2\int_0^q t\,dt
 =q^2
 =\frac{p^2 2^{-2a}}{4}.
\end{align*}
The inequality holds because, among sets of length $q$
on either side of $z$, the interval adjacent to $z$
minimizes the integral of the distance from $z$.
For the two equal halves of $J$,
\[
 \int_{J_+}y\,dy-\int_{J_-}y\,dy=\frac{|J|^2}{4}.
\]
The definition of $h_{I,J}$ therefore gives
\begin{align*}
 -\int_E\int_0^1 D(x,y)h_{I,J}(x,y)\,dy\,dx
 &=
 N\left(
 \int_{E\cap I_+}x\,dx-\int_{E\cap I_-}x\,dx
 \right)
 \left(
 \int_{J_+}y\,dy-\int_{J_-}y\,dy
 \right)\\
 &\ge \frac{Np^2 2^{-2a}|J|^2}{16}
 =\frac{Np^2 2^{-2n}}{16}.
\end{align*}
Summing the preceding bounds and using
$2^n<4N$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 -\int_E\int_0^1
 D(x,y)\sum_{I,J}h_{I,J}(x,y)\,dy\,dx
 \ge
 (n+1)2^{n-1}\frac{Np^2 2^{-2n}}{16}=\frac{Np^2(n+1)}{32\cdot2^n}
 \ge\frac{p^2(n+1)}{128}.
\end{align*}
The integral is negative, so squaring gives
\begin{equation}
 \left(\int_E\int_0^1
 D(x,y)\sum_{I,J}h_{I,J}(x,y)\,dy\,dx\right)^2
 \ge \frac{p^4(n+1)^2}{128^2}.
 \label{eq:roth-numerator}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:roth-denominator} and
\eqref{eq:roth-numerator} into \eqref{eq:roth-cauchy-schwarz} gives, for an absolute constant $C>0$,
\[
 \int_E\int_0^1 D(x,y)^2\,dy\,dx
      
                                               
                                       
 \ge Cp^3(n+1)
 \ge Cp^3\log(N+2),
\]
as required.
\end{proof}
We are now ready to conclude the proof of \Cref{lem:random-dimension-lower}. First apply \cref{clm:restricted-roth} to the points defining $D_j$ and $E$, we have
\begin{equation}
 \int_E\int_0^1 D_j(x,y)^2\,dy\,dx
 \ge Cp^3\log(N+2).
 \label{eq:coordinate-discrepancy-lower}
\end{equation}
We then sum up \eqref{eq:coordinate-discrepancy-lower} over $j$ and apply \eqref{eq:dimension-success-integral} to get
\[
 p\left(\rho+\frac{3\sqrt d}{2}\right)^2
 \ge Cdp^3\log(N+2).
\]
The one-cycle input forces $\rho\ge\sqrt d/2$, since its cost
is at least $\sqrt d/2$ under every ordering and the success
probability is positive. Hence $\rho+3\sqrt d/2\le4\rho$.
Using $p\ge1-\delta$ and $N=\lfloor T/(2d)\rfloor$, we obtain
\[
 \rho\ge c_0p\sqrt{d\log\left(2+\frac{T}{d}\right)}
 \ge c_0(1-\delta)\sqrt{d\log\left(2+\frac{T}{d}\right)}
\]
for an absolute constant $c_0>0$.
Eventually, we may choose $c\le c_0$ sufficiently small to include the case
$d>T/2$ discussed at the very beginning, which will complete the proof.
