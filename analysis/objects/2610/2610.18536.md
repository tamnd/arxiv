<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:LuoSun}
Suppose $M^n (n\ge3)$ is a closed contact stationary Legendrian  submanifold of $\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}$ and
\begin{equation*}
|B|^2\leq\dfrac{(n-1)(n+2)}{n}+\dfrac{n^2+3n-2}{2n^2}|H|^2-\dfrac{(n-1)(n-2)|H|\sqrt{4n+|H|^2}}{2n^2}.
\end{equation*}
Then $M$ is either minimal or a Calabi product Legendrian immersion of a totally geodesic Legendrian immersion and a point.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Recently, Cui and Sun \cite{CuiSun} obtained a topological sphere theorem for Legendrian submanifolds in the unit sphere under the same pinching condition as in Theorem \ref{thm:LuoSun}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $F:M^n\to\mathbb S^{2n+1}\subset\mathbb C^{n+1}$ be a minimal Legendrian
immersion of a connected closed manifold, $n\ge3$. If $|B|^2\lec_n$
everywhere, then, up to a unitary transformation, $F$ parametrizes a
totally geodesic sphere or is a finite covering of the embedded image
\begin{equation}\tag{1.1}\label{eq:1.1}
 (\theta,x)\longmapsto
 \left(\frac{e^{in\theta}}{\sqrt{n+1}},
       \sqrt{\frac n{n+1}}e^{-i\theta}x\right),
 \qquad(\theta,x)\in\mathbb S^1\times\mathbb S^{n-1}.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbb S^1=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$. For odd $n$ the displayed map is
an embedding; for even $n$ it induces an embedding on the quotient by
$(\theta,x)\sim(\theta+\pi,-x)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:gram}
Every trace-free symmetric cubic on $\mathbb R^n$, $n\ge3$, satisfies
$0\le G\le\alpha Sg$. If $S>0$ and $G(e,e)=\alpha S$ for a unit vector
$e=e_1$, then
\[
 \sigma_{111}=a,\qquad \sigma_{1\beta\gamma}=-\frac{a}{n-1}\delta_{\beta\gamma},
 \qquad \sigma_{11\beta}=\sigma_{\beta\gamma\delta}=0,\qquad
 S=\frac{n+2}{n-1}a^2,
\]
where Greek indices are greater than one. In this equality case, the largest
Gram eigenvalue is simple. In particular, $S\le c_n$ implies
$\operatorname{Ric}\ge0$.

\label{lem:derivative}
For $t\ge\tau_n$, one has $\mathcal D_t\ge0$.

Write $T_{ijkl}=\sigma_{ijk;l}$, and set, within this proof,
\[
 E_{ijk}=\sigma_{iab}T_{abjk},\qquad
 \Gamma_i=\sigma_{abc}T_{abci},\qquad
 E^s_{ijk}=(E_{ijk}+E_{jik}+E_{kij})/3.
\]
The last two indices of $E$ are symmetric, and trace-freeness gives
$\sum_j E_{ijj}=0$ and $\sum_j E_{jij}=\Gamma_i$.
Differentiation of $G$ and expansion of the three summands in $E^s$ yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \nabla_iG_{jk}&=E_{jik}+E_{kij},\\
 \nabla_iG_{jk}\nabla_jG_{ik}&=|E|^2+3E_{ijk}E_{jik},\\
 |E^s|^2&=\tfrac13\bigl(|E|^2+2E_{ijk}E_{jik}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \nabla_iG_{jk}\nabla_jG_{ik}=-\tfrac12|E|^2+\tfrac92|E^s|^2,
 \qquad \operatorname{tr}E^s=\tfrac23\Gamma.
\]
For $(\delta\Gamma)_{ijk}=\delta_{ij}\Gamma_k+\delta_{ik}\Gamma_j
+\delta_{jk}\Gamma_i$,
\[
 |\delta\Gamma|^2=3(n+2)|\Gamma|^2,\qquad
 |E^s|^2=\left|E^s-\frac{2}{3(n+2)}\delta\Gamma\right|^2
             +\frac{4}{3(n+2)}|\Gamma|^2.
\]
Using $\nabla S=2\Gamma$ yields
\[
 \mathcal D_{\tau_n}
 =\tfrac12\bigl(\alpha S|T|^2-|E|^2\bigr)
  +\tfrac92\left|E^s-\frac{2}{3(n+2)}\delta\Gamma\right|^2
  +\frac{4}{n+2}|\Gamma|^2.
\]
Order the Gram eigenvalues as $g_1\ge\cdots\ge g_n\ge0$.
In a corresponding orthonormal eigenframe, the matrices $A_i/\sqrt{g_i}$
with $g_i>0$ are orthonormal; if $g_i=0$, then $A_i=0$.
For a symmetric matrix $U$, Bessel's inequality gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_i\langle A_i,U\rangle^2
 &=\sum_{g_i>0}g_i\left\langle\frac{A_i}{\sqrt{g_i}},U\right\rangle^2\\
 &\le g_1\sum_{g_i>0}\left\langle\frac{A_i}{\sqrt{g_i}},U\right\rangle^2
 \le g_1|U|^2\le\alpha S|U|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Apply this to $U_{ab}=T_{abjk}$ and sum over $j,k$ to obtain
$|E|^2\le\alpha S|T|^2$. Finally,
\[
 \mathcal D_t-\mathcal D_{\tau_n}=(t-\tau_n)\left(S|T|^2+\tfrac12|\nabla S|^2\right)\ge0.
\]

To estimate $Q$, take the completely symmetric part of the contracted product
$\sigma_{ija}\sigma_{kla}$:
\[
 \mathcal A_{ijkl}
 =\frac13(\sigma_{ija}\sigma_{kla}+\sigma_{ika}\sigma_{jla}
       +\sigma_{ila}\sigma_{jka}),\qquad K=h_4\mathcal A.
\]
The metric insertions are
\begin{align*}
 (\delta G)_{ijkl}
 &=\delta_{ij}G_{kl}+\delta_{ik}G_{jl}+\delta_{il}G_{jk}
   +\delta_{jk}G_{il}+\delta_{jl}G_{ik}+\delta_{kl}G_{ij},\\
 (\delta\delta)_{ijkl}
 &=\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}
   +\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}.
\end{align*}
For a symmetric two-tensor $H$ and a symmetric four-tensor $U$, complete
symmetry gives
\[
 \langle\delta H,U\rangle=6\langle H,\operatorname{tr}U\rangle,
 \qquad
 \langle\delta\delta,U\rangle=3\operatorname{tr}^2U,
\]
where $\operatorname{tr}^2U=\sum_{i,j}U_{iijj}$. Thus the metric insertions
are orthogonal to trace-free symmetric four-tensors. Taking traces gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}\mathcal A=\frac23G,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}(\delta G)=(n+4)G+g,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}(\delta\delta)=(n+2)g.
\]
It follows that the orthogonal trace part is
\[
 \mathcal A-K=\frac{2}{3(n+4)}\delta G
 -\frac{2}{3(n+4)(n+2)}\delta\delta.
\]
Its squared norm can be computed by pairing once with $\mathcal A$:
\[
 |\mathcal A-K|^2
 =\langle\mathcal A,\mathcal A-K\rangle
 =\frac{8}{3(n+4)}p_2-\frac{4}{3(n+2)(n+4)}.
\]
If $H_0=\sum_{i,j}\operatorname{tr}(A_iA_jA_iA_j)$, then
$Q=3p_2-2H_0$ and $|\mathcal A|^2=(p_2+2H_0)/3$. The first identity
follows from $\sum_iA_i^2=G$; the second follows by expanding the three
squared terms and six cross terms in $|\mathcal A|^2$. Subtracting the trace
part yields
\begin{equation}\tag{3.5}\label{eq:3.5}
 3|K|^2=\frac{4(n+2)}{n+4}p_2
       +\frac{4}{(n+2)(n+4)}-Q,
 \qquad K_{iabc}\sigma_{abc}=\frac{n+2}{n+4}V_i.
\end{equation}
For the second identity, first note that
$\mathcal A_{iabc}\sigma_{abc}=V_i$. In $\delta G$, exactly three metric
insertions survive the contraction, each contributing $V_i$.

For a unit vector $e$, put $A_e=\sigma(e,\cdot,\cdot)$ and
$T_e=\operatorname{Sym}(e^\flat\otimes\sigma)$, where the four placements
are averaged. The four diagonal terms and twelve cross terms in $|T_e|^2$
give
\[
 |T_e|^2=\frac14+\frac34|A_e|^2,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}T_e=\frac12A_e,\qquad
 h_4T_e=T_e-\frac{\delta A_e}{2(n+4)}.
\]
The same trace pairing then gives
\begin{align*}
 |h_4T_e|^2
 &=|T_e|^2-\frac{\langle\delta A_e,T_e\rangle}{2(n+4)}\\
 &=\frac14+\frac34|A_e|^2-\frac{3|A_e|^2}{2(n+4)}
 =\frac14+\frac{3(n+2)}{4(n+4)}G(e,e).
\end{align*}
When $V\ne0$, choose $e=V^\sharp/|V|$. Since
$\langle K,h_4T_e\rangle=(n+2)|V|/(n+4)$ and $G(e,e)\le\ell$,
Cauchy's inequality gives
\[
 \frac{(n+2)^2}{(n+4)^2}r
 =\langle K,h_4T_e\rangle^2
 \le |K|^2\left\{\frac14+\frac{3(n+2)}{4(n+4)}\ell\right\}.
\]
Eliminating $|K|^2$ by \eqref{eq:3.5} yields
\begin{equation}\tag{3.6}\label{eq:3.6}
 Q\le\frac{4(n+2)}{n+4}p_2
     +\frac{4}{(n+2)(n+4)}-k(\ell)r.
\end{equation}
For $V=0$ this follows from $|K|^2\ge0$.

Next, $\operatorname{tr}N=2V$ and $\langle N,\sigma\rangle=3p_2$ imply
\begin{equation}\tag{3.7}\label{eq:3.7}
 |N|^2=9p_2^2+\frac{12}{n+2}r+|W|^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the trace part of $N$ is $2\delta V/(n+2)$, with squared norm
$12r/(n+2)$, and $W\perp\sigma$. In a Gram eigenframe,
\[
 |N|^2=3p_3+6\sum_{i,j,a}g_ig_j\sigma_{ija}^2,
 \qquad
 \frac12\sum_a|[G,A_a]|^2
 =p_3-\sum_{i,j,a}g_ig_j\sigma_{ija}^2.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:2.6} becomes
\[
 \mathcal B_\tau=\frac{n}{c_n}p_2+\frac{\tau(n+1)-1}{c_n}
 -\tau Q-\frac32p_3+\frac16|N|^2-r.
\]
Substituting \eqref{eq:3.6} and \eqref{eq:3.7} gives \eqref{eq:3.3}; the
coefficient of $r$ is $\tau k(\ell)+2/(n+2)-1=z(\ell)$. Also $k$ is
decreasing and
\begin{equation}\tag{3.8}\label{eq:3.8}
 z(\ell)\ge z(\alpha)
 =\frac{n(n^2+2n+4)}{2(n+1)(n+2)(n+4)}>0.
\end{equation}

To bound $r$ from below, split the cubic relative to a largest-eigenvalue
direction. The quantity $2\ell-1$ arising in this split is positive precisely
in the range now under consideration, $\ell>1/2$. Choose a Gram eigenframe
with $g_1=\ell$ and put
\begin{gather*}
 m=n-1,\qquad \mu=\frac{1-\ell}{m},\qquad d=\ell-\mu,\\
 Z=G|_{e_1^\perp}-\mu I,\qquad \eta=|Z|^2,\qquad
 \mathcal K=\frac{m}{m-1}d^2-\eta=L-p_2.
\end{gather*}
Since $p_2\le\ell^2+(1-\ell)^2$,
\[
 \mathcal K\ge(2\ell-1)
 \left(1+\frac{2\ell-1}{n-2}\right)>0.
\]
Let $a_i=\sigma_{i11}$ and
$T_{i\beta\gamma}=\sigma_{i\beta\gamma}+a_i\delta_{\beta\gamma}/m$.
Each $T_i$ is trace-free on the transverse indices. Here
$|a_\bullet|^2=\sum_i a_i^2$ and
$|T|^2=\sum_{i,\beta,\gamma}T_{i\beta\gamma}^2$. Separating the entries
according to the number of indices equal to $1$ gives
\[
 S=2\ell-|a_\bullet|^2+\sum_{i,\beta,\gamma}\sigma_{i\beta\gamma}^2,
 \qquad
 |T|^2=\sum_{i,\beta,\gamma}\sigma_{i\beta\gamma}^2
       -\frac{|a_\bullet|^2}{m}.
\]
Since $S=1$ and $G$ is diagonal in the chosen frame, it follows that
\[
 2\ell-1=\frac{m-1}{m}|a_\bullet|^2-|T|^2,
 \qquad V_i=da_i+\langle T_i,Z\rangle.
\]
With $\rho=(m-1)/(md)$, the required square completion is
\[
 r-(2\ell-1)\mathcal K
 =\mathcal K\sum_i|T_i+\rho a_iZ|^2
  +\sum_i\langle T_i+\rho a_iZ,Z\rangle^2\ge0.
\]
Expanding the right-hand side,
$\mathcal K+\eta=md^2/(m-1)$ gives the cross coefficient
$2\rho(\mathcal K+\eta)=2d$ and the $|a_\bullet|^2$ coefficient
$\rho^2\eta(\mathcal K+\eta)=(m-1)\eta/m$.
Together with \eqref{eq:3.8}, this proves \eqref{eq:3.4}.

\[

 b_0=\frac An+C+d^2\left\{

 \tag{4.6}\label{eq:4.6}

 d_0=\frac{(n-2)(n+1)}{(n-1)(n+2)},\qquad
 c=\frac{3n(n-1)(n+2)(5n+14)}{8(n-2)(n+1)^2(n+4)},\qquad

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:axis}
Let $e$ be a smooth unit vector field on a Riemannian $n$-manifold,
and put $\xi=e^\flat$. Suppose the cubic tensor
\begin{equation}\tag{6.1}\label{eq:6.1}
 \sigma_{ijk}=\frac1{\sqrt n}
 \bigl((n+2)\xi_i\xi_j\xi_k
       -\xi_i g_{jk}-\xi_j g_{ik}-\xi_k g_{ij}\bigr)
\end{equation}
satisfies Codazzi, so that $\nabla\sigma$ is completely symmetric.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
At a point choose an orthonormal frame with $e_1=e$, and set
$h_{\beta l}=\langle\nabla_{e_l}e,e_\beta\rangle$ for $\beta>1$.
Since $e$ has unit length, differentiating \eqref{eq:6.1} gives
\begin{equation}\tag{6.2}\label{eq:6.2}
 T_{111l}=0,\qquad
 T_{11\beta l}=\frac{n+1}{\sqrt n}h_{\beta l},\qquad
 T_{1\beta\gamma l}=0,
 \quad T_{ijkl}=\sigma_{ijk;l}.
\end{equation}
Complete symmetry implies
$T_{11\beta1}=T_{111\beta}=0$ and
$T_{11\beta\gamma}=T_{1\beta\gamma1}=0$.
Thus every $h_{\beta l}$ vanishes, giving $\nabla e=0$.
Formula~\eqref{eq:6.1} then gives $\nabla\sigma=0$.

Choose $t=8/25$ for $n=3$ and $t=\tau_n$ for $n\ge4$; in either
case $t\ge\tau_n$. Lemma~\ref{lem:derivative},
Proposition~\ref{prop:sextic}, and \eqref{eq:2.8} make all three
terms in \eqref{eq:2.7} nonnegative. Their integrals vanish, so each
term vanishes pointwise. In particular,
\[
 (1-S/c_n)S^2=0.
\]
Connectedness gives $S\equiv0$ or $S\equivc_n$.

If $S=0$, the ambient Gauss formula shows that the real span of $F$
and $dF(TM)$ is a fixed $(n+1)$-plane. This plane is Lagrangian by
the Legendrian condition. The immersion into its great sphere is a
local isometry; compactness makes it a surjective covering, and simple
connectedness of $\mathbb S^n$ makes the covering one-sheeted. A unitary
transformation sends the plane to the standard real subspace.

Suppose henceforth that $S=c_n$. We also have $\mathcal B_t=0$.
The equality implication in Proposition~\ref{prop:sextic} gives
$\lambda_{\max}(G)=\alpha S$. Lemma~\ref{lem:gram} determines
the cubic, with independent components
\[
 \sigma_{111}=\frac{n-1}{\sqrt n},\qquad
 \sigma_{1\beta\gamma}=-\frac{\delta_{\beta\gamma}}{\sqrt n},
 \qquad \sigma_{11\beta}=\sigma_{\beta\gamma\delta}=0,
\]
after orienting its axis. In particular,
\[
 G=\operatorname{diag}(n-1,2/n,\ldots,2/n),\qquad
 V=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)(n+1)}{n\sqrt n}\,e^\flat.
\]
Since $n\ge3$, the one-form $V$ is nowhere zero, so
$e=V^\sharp/|V|$ defines a smooth global axis. Its cubic is exactly
\eqref{eq:6.1}. Lemma~\ref{lem:axis} now gives $\nabla e=0$ and
$\nabla\sigma=0$.

We identify tangent vectors with their images under $dF$. For $X\perp e$,
the ambient Gauss formula gives
\begin{equation}\tag{6.3}\label{eq:6.3}
 \begin{aligned}
 D_Ze&=-\frac1{\sqrt n}JZ+\sqrt n\,\xi(Z)Je-\xi(Z)F,\\
 D_ZX&=\nabla_ZX-
       \frac{\xi(Z)JX+\langle Z,X\rangle Je}{\sqrt n}
       -\langle Z,X\rangle F.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
On the universal cover, the parallel one-form $\xi$ is exact; write
$\mathrm d t=\xi$. Define
\begin{equation}\tag{6.4}\label{eq:6.4}
 q=e^{-i\sqrt n t}\frac{F-\sqrt n\,Je}{\sqrt{n+1}},\qquad
 \psi=e^{it/\sqrt n}\frac{\sqrt n F+Je}{\sqrt{n+1}}.
\end{equation}
For example,
$D_Z(F-\sqrt n\,Je)=i\sqrt n\,\xi(Z)(F-\sqrt n\,Je)$.
Differentiating both expressions therefore yields
\begin{equation}\tag{6.5}\label{eq:6.5}
 Dq=0,\qquad
 \mathrm d\psi(Z)=\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{n}}\,e^{it/\sqrt n}
             (Z-\xi(Z)e).
\end{equation}
For $X\perp e$, a second differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\tag{6.6}\label{eq:6.6}
 D_Z(\mathrm d\psi(X))
 =\mathrm d\psi(\nabla_ZX)-\frac{n+1}{n}\langle Z,X\rangle\psi.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the phase derivative cancels the term
$-\xi(Z)JX/\sqrt n$ in \eqref{eq:6.3}; the remaining normal terms
combine into a multiple of $\sqrt n F+Je$. These identities hold
for arbitrary $Z$.

Before multiplication by their phase factors, both
$F-\sqrt n\,Je$ and $\sqrt n F+Je$ have squared norm $n+1$.
The real and imaginary parts of their Hermitian inner product vanish, using
$\langle F,Je\rangle=\langle JF,e\rangle=0$.
Their Hermitian products with a transverse vector $X$ vanish as well.
Multiplication by complex phases preserves these zero products.
Thus $q$ and $\psi$ are unit vectors, and $q$ is Hermitian orthogonal
to $\psi$ and $\mathrm d\psi(TM)$.

By \eqref{eq:6.5}--\eqref{eq:6.6}, ambient derivatives of local fields spanning
$\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\psi,\mathrm d\psi(e^\perp)\}$ remain in this
span. It therefore defines a fixed real $n$-plane on the connected
universal cover. The Legendrian condition gives
\[
 \langle J\psi,\mathrm d\psi(X)\rangle=0,\qquad
 \langle J\mathrm d\psi(X),\mathrm d\psi(Y)\rangle=0
 \quad(X,Y\perp e).
\]
Hence this real plane is Lagrangian in the complex orthogonal
complement of $q$. A fixed unitary transformation sends $q$ to the
first coordinate vector and the plane to $\mathbb R^n\subset\mathbb C^n$.
After this transformation, we identify $\psi$ with its last $n$
coordinates, so that $\psi$ takes values in
$\mathbb S^{n-1}\subset\mathbb R^n$. Let $\widetilde F$ denote the lift of $F$ to
the universal cover. Solving \eqref{eq:6.4} gives
\[
 \widetilde F(p)=\left(
 \frac{e^{i\sqrt n\,t(p)}}{\sqrt{n+1}},
 \sqrt{\frac n{n+1}}e^{-it(p)/\sqrt n}\psi(p)\right).
\]
Hence the lifted immersion has image in \eqref{eq:1.1}, with parameters
$\theta=t(p)/\sqrt n$ and $x=\psi(p)$.

We determine the global identifications of the model. Its induced
metric is
\[
 n\,\mathrm d\theta^2+\frac n{n+1}g_{\mathbb S^{n-1}},
\]
so it is an immersion. Two parameters $(\theta,x)$ and $(\varphi,y)$
have the same image exactly when
\[
 e^{in(\varphi-\theta)}=1,\qquad
 y=e^{i(\varphi-\theta)}x.
\]
Since $x,y$ are real unit vectors, $\varphi-\theta\in\pi\mathbb Z$.
Modulo $2\pi$, the only extra identification is
$(\theta,x)\sim(\theta+\pi,-x)$ for even $n$.
The map on the stated compact quotient is injective, hence an
embedding. Call its connected image $C_n$. The original immersion
has image in $C_n$ and is a local diffeomorphism onto it. Its image
is open and, by compactness, closed, so it equals $C_n$.
A proper local diffeomorphism is a covering, and compactness makes
the covering finite.

Finally, the model is minimal Legendrian and attains the pinching value. For \eqref{eq:1.1}, take
$e=F_\theta/\sqrt n$ and unit transverse vectors
$e_\beta=(0,e^{-i\theta}v_\beta)$, where the $v_\beta$ are
orthonormal tangent vectors to $\mathbb S^{n-1}$. The contact form vanishes:
\[
 \langle JF,F_\theta\rangle=\frac n{n+1}-\frac n{n+1}=0,
 \qquad \langle JF,\mathrm d F(v)\rangle=0.
\]
Differentiation gives
\[
 \langle D_ee,Je\rangle=\frac{n-1}{\sqrt n},\qquad
 \langle D_ee_\beta,Je_\gamma\rangle
 =-\frac{\delta_{\beta\gamma}}{\sqrt n},
\]
while $\sigma_{11\beta}$ and $\sigma_{\beta\gamma\delta}$ vanish.
By symmetry, equivalently,
\[
 B(e,e)=\frac{n-1}{\sqrt n}Je,\qquad
 B(e,e_\beta)=-\frac1{\sqrt n}Je_\beta,\qquad
 B(e_\beta,e_\gamma)=-\frac{\delta_{\beta\gamma}}{\sqrt n}Je.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{tr} B=0,\qquad
 |B|^2=\frac{(n-1)^2+3(n-1)}n=c_n.
\]
