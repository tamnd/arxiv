<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thmhm}
		Let $R$ be a quasisyntomic ring (for instance a smooth algebra over a perfect field $k$ of
		characteristic $p$). There are complete, exhaustive, multiplicative $\mathbb{Z}$-indexed
		filtrations $F^\bullet_{\mathrm{BMS}}$ on the $p$-completions of $THH(R)$, $TC^-(R)$, $TP(R)$,
		and $TC(R)$, functorial in $R$ and constructed compatibly with the cyclotomic
		structure maps $\varphi$ and $\mathrm{can}$,  with
		\[
			\mathrm{gr}^nTHH(R;\mathbb{Z}_p)\simeq\mathrm{gr}^n_N\widehat\Delta_R\{n\}[2n],
			\qquad
			\mathrm{gr}^nTC^-(R;\mathbb{Z}_p)\simeq\mathcal N^{\ge n}\widehat\Delta_R\{n\}[2n],
		\]
		\[
			\mathrm{gr}^nTP(R;\mathbb{Z}_p)\simeq\widehat\Delta_R\{n\}[2n],
		\]
		where $\widehat\Delta_R\{n\}$ is the ($n$-th Breuil-Kisin twisted) Nygaard-completed
		prismatic complex of $R$, $\mathcal N^{\ge n}$ its Nygaard filtration, and $\mathrm{gr}^n_N$
		the associated graded.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thmazumaya}
		Let $Z$ be a commutative ring and let $A$ be an Azumaya $Z$-algebra. Then

		\smallskip
		\noindent{\rm(1)} there is an equivalence of underlying spectra
		\begin{equation}
			\Phi_A:THH(A)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }THH(Z),
		\end{equation}
		linear over the $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-ring $THH(Z)$, whose $\pi_0$ is the isomorphism
		$HH_0(A)=A/[A,A]\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{trd}\ }Z$ induced by the reduced trace. 

		\smallskip
		\noindent{\rm(2)} $\Phi_A$ is compatible with \'etale base change; 

		\smallskip
		\noindent{\rm(3)}  for every faithfully flat
		\'etale splitting cover $Z\to Z'$ trivializing $A$, there are induced equivalences (after this base change)   
$TR^r(A\otimes_ZZ';p)\simeq TR^r(Z';p)$ for all $r\ge1$,
		$TC^-(A\otimes_ZZ';\mathbb{Z}_p)\simeq
	TC^-(Z';\mathbb{Z}_p)$, $TP(A\otimes_ZZ';\mathbb{Z}_p)\simeq TP(Z';\mathbb{Z}_p)$,
 and
		$TC(A\otimes_ZZ';\mathbb{Z}_p)\simeq TC(Z';\mathbb{Z}_p)$, compatibly with the
		restriction, Frobenius, and Verschiebung operators of the $TR$-system, and globally on $A$  whenever $A$ admits a global splitting
		module, i.e., whenever the Brauer class $[A]\in\mathrm{Br}(Z)$
		vanishes.

All \'etale-local claims below about $TR^r$, $TC^-$, $TP$, and $TC$ (part {\rm(3)} of the theorem, and their analogues in Theorem
		\ref{thmderived}) 
are expressed through $\tau_P$, not through $\Phi_A$. 
 One should not assume that $\Phi_A$ is cyclotomic unless $A$ is globally split, i.e., $[A]=0\in\mathrm{Br}(Z)$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{deftransported}
	The \emph{transported noncommutative BMS filtration} 
(or \emph{transported filtration} for short)   
 on $THH(A;\mathbb{Z}_p)$, computed as a pullback
		of underlying $THH(Z;\mathbb{Z}_p)$-modules in $\mathrm{Mod}_{THH(Z;\mathbb{Z}_p)}$
		(equivalently, of underlying spectra, since the pullback diagram and its terms
		agree either way), is
		\begin{equation}
			F^nTHH(A;\mathbb{Z}_p):=THH(A;\mathbb{Z}_p)
			\times_{THH(Z;\mathbb{Z}_p)}F^n_{\mathrm{BMS}}THH(Z;\mathbb{Z}_p),
			\qquad n\ge 0.
		\end{equation}
		Here $F^\bullet_{\mathrm{BMS}}$ is the filtration of
		Bhatt-Morrow-Scholze \cite{bms} recalled in Theorem \ref{thmhm}. The pullback is
		formed along $\Phi_A$ on the left leg. It is not claimed that 
 this filtration is independent 
		of the chosen $\Phi_A$ up to a specified equivalence, see Proposition
		\ref{proptransported}. We call $F^\bullet THH(A;\mathbb{Z}_p)$ a filtered
		$THH(Z;\mathbb{Z}_p)$-module, not a multiplicative filtration on $THH(A)$:  unlike $THH(Z)$, for $A$ only associative, $THH(A)$ carries no canonical multiplication of its 
		compatible with $F^\bullet$. Similarly, after any faithfully flat \'etale splitting $Z\to Z'$,
		the cyclotomic Morita equivalence transports the BMS filtrations on
		$TC^-$, $TP$, and $TC$ from $Z'$ to $A\otimes_ZZ'$. These transported
		filtrations are global whenever $A$ admits a global splitting module.
		No global transport on $TC^-$, $TP$, or $TC$ is claimed in general.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{proptransported}
		The transported filtration of Definition \ref{deftransported} is complete and
		exhaustive, and consists of
		$F^\bullet_{\mathrm{BMS}}THH(Z;\mathbb{Z}_p)$-modules.  
It has graded pieces
		\begin{equation}
			\mathrm{gr}^nTHH(A;\mathbb{Z}_p)\;\simeq\;
			\mathrm{gr}_{\mathcal N}^n\widehat{\Delta}_Z\{n\}[2n].
		\end{equation}
 It is functorial for \'etale base change in the pair
		$(Z,A)$, compatibly transporting the chosen $\Phi_A$. 
It is a Brauer invariant in the
		following sense: if $A$ and $A'$ are Azumaya $Z$-algebras
		with $[A]=[A']\in\mathrm{Br}(Z)$, then some Morita equivalence between them
		identifies the two transported filtrations up to the same ambiguity.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Completeness, exhaustiveness, and the graded-piece formula follow from
		$F^\bullet_{\mathrm{BMS}}$ \cite{bms} together with parts (1)-(2) of
		Theorem \ref{thmazumaya}, as just described. Brauer invariance follows since a Morita
		equivalence $\mathrm{Perf}(A)\simeq\mathrm{Perf}(A')$ of $Z$-linear
		categories induces an equivalence on $THH$ commuting with the local trace
		equivalences, by Lemma \ref{lemmorita-pic}, up to the automorphism ambiguity of Lemma
		\ref{leminvtrivial}.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemkunneth}
		The natural map
		\begin{equation}
			THH(A)\otimes_{THH(Z)}THH(B)\longrightarrow THH(A\otimes_ZB)
		\end{equation}
		is an equivalence in $\mathrm{Mod}_{THH(Z)}$, compatibly with the $S^1$-action and the
		cyclotomic Frobenius $\varphi_p$ on each side, hence an equivalence of cyclotomic
		spectra once the $THH(Z)$-module structure is forgotten.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
This is the relative K\"unneth theorem for $THH$ of Blumberg-Mandell
		\cite{bm}, stated there in the category
		$\mathrm{Mod}_{THH(Z)}$ just described. 
Blumberg-Mandell note that, despite
		being ``well known,'' this statement did not previously have a complete proof in the
		literature, and supply one. We rely on their theorem for the  statement
		and do not prove it again. 
What follows is schematic motivation only, not a substitute
		proof. In particular, it does not establish the cyclotomic compatibility,
		which is part of Blumberg-Mandell  statement, not derived here from first
		principles. The equivalence is constructed from
		the two-sided bar construction $B_\bullet(A,Z,B)$, with $n$-simplices
		$A\otimes_{\mathbb S}Z^{\otimes_{\mathbb S}n}\otimes_{\mathbb S}B$, a
		simplicial $\mathbb E_1$-algebra in orthogonal spectra. Each term is a tensor product of algebras.
		The face maps multiply $Z$ into $A$ and into $B$ through the respective
		central maps. Hence these are algebra maps in the category of associative orthogonal-spectrum
		algebras. Note that only the centrality of $Z\to A$ and
		$Z\to B$ is used here (neither $A$ nor $B$ need to be commutative). 
 Its geometric realization,
		formed in this category of algebras,
		is $A\otimes_ZB$ as an $\mathbb E_1$-algebra. 
$THH$, applied at each levele, takes this
		simplicial algebra to a simplicial object in $\mathrm{Mod}_{THH(Z)}$ whose geometric
		realization is $THH(A\otimes_ZB)$, since $THH$ preserves sifted colimits of algebras
	\cite{bm}. This identifies $THH(A\otimes_ZB)$ with the bar resolution computing
		$THH(A)\otimes_{THH(Z)}THH(B)$,

		\begin{eqnarray*}
			THH(A\otimes_ZB)\simeq\bigl|THH(A)\otimes_{\mathbb S}
			THH(Z)^{\otimes_{\mathbb S}\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb S}THH(B)\bigr|
\\			
\simeq THH(A)\otimes_{THH(Z)}THH(B).
		\end{eqnarray*}

		It is not claimed that
 each individual simplicial structure map in this diagram is
		 an equivalence (it is not, in general) only that the colimit of the whole
		diagram computes the stated object, which is the content of the cited theorem.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lemkunnethhigher}
		Let $Z$ be a connective $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-ring and let $A,B$ be $\mathbb E_1$-algebras
		over $Z$ in the $\infty$-category of spectra, with $\otimes_Z$ the derived tensor
		product. Then the conclusion of Lemma \ref{lemkunneth} holds in this
		generality. The natural map
		\begin{equation}
			THH(A)\otimes_{THH(Z)}THH(B)\longrightarrow THH(A\otimes_ZB)
		\end{equation}
		is an equivalence of $THH(Z)$-modules, compatibly with the cyclotomic structure.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We list which facts are used and why each holds, and avoid deriving the
		cyclotomic upgrade from any claim that a Tate construction commutes with a
		colimit which is not true in general. 

		\smallskip
		\noindent\emph{Part 1, underlying spectra.} Form the two-sided bar construction
		$B_\bullet(A,Z,B)$ in the $\infty$-category of $\mathbb E_1$-algebras in spectra
		(over the sphere $\mathbb S$, exactly as in the proof of Lemma \ref{lemkunneth}).
		Its $n$-simplices are 
$A\otimes_{\mathbb S}Z^{\otimes_{\mathbb S}n}\otimes_{\mathbb S}B$
 (the internal tensor powers of $Z$ are taken over $\mathbb S$, not 
		over $Z$  
(if it were over $Z$, $Z^{\otimes_Zn}\simeq Z$ for every $n$ and the
		construction would trivialize)) 
 with face and degeneracy maps induced by the
		multiplication, and  
 unit of $Z$ acting on the two central factors via the given
		central maps $Z\to A$, $Z\to B$. 
Its geometric realization (a colimit indexed by
		$\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}$) is $A\otimes_ZB$, by the standard identification of a
		relative tensor product of algebras with the realization of its bar construction \cite{ha}.

		Apply $THH(-):\mathbb E_1\text{-}\mathrm{Alg}\to\mathrm{Sp}$, for the moment only as
		a functor to underlying spectra at each level.
  We use two separate facts: 
		{\rm(i)} $\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}$ is sifted \cite{htt},
		i.e., its diagonal $\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}$
		is cofinal, unrelated to filteredness; 
{\rm(ii)} the forgetful functor from
		$\mathbb E_1$-algebras in spectra to their underlying spectra creates sifted
		colimits \cite{ha}, since the free-algebra monad is built
		from tensor powers $(-)^{\otimes_{\mathbb S}n}$. 
For a sifted diagram 
$\{X_i\}$, cofinality of the diagonal gives 
$\operatorname{colim}_i(X_i\otimes_{\mathbb S}X_i)
\simeq(\operatorname{colim}_iX_i)\otimes_{\mathbb S}(\operatorname{colim}_iX_i)$. 
		Since $N^{\mathrm{cy}}_\bullet(-)$ (which computes $THH$)
 is an iterated tensor power of
		the algebra over $\mathbb S$ in each cyclic simplicial degree \cite{nik},
 $THH$ as a
		functor of the algebra preserves sifted colimits of underlying spectra by
		{\rm(ii)}. 
Combined with {\rm(i)}, $THH$ carries the realization of
		$B_\bullet(A,Z,B)$ (which is $A\otimes_ZB$)  to the realization,  
as underlying
		spectra, of $THH(B_\bullet(A,Z,B))$. On the other hand
		$THH(B_n(A,Z,B))=THH(A\otimes_{\mathbb S}Z^{\otimes_{\mathbb S}n}\otimes_{\mathbb
		S}B)$. The same bar-resolution identification as in Lemma \ref{lemkunneth}
		exhibits $|THH(B_\bullet(A,Z,B))|$, as underlying spectra, as
		$THH(A)\otimes_{THH(Z)}THH(B)$. Combining the two computations of the same
		realization gives a specific comparison map, and shows it is an equivalence of
		underlying spectra:
		\begin{equation}
			\alpha:\ THH(A)\otimes_{THH(Z)}THH(B)\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ THH(A\otimes_ZB)
			\quad\text{(as underlying spectra).}
		\end{equation}



		\smallskip
		\noindent\emph{Part 2, upgrading to cyclotomic spectra.}  
We do not try to show any Tate construction commutes with the colimit above. Instead, we observe that $\alpha$ is 
		already a well-defined map of cyclotomic spectra (equivalently, of
		$THH(Z)$-modules in $\mathrm{CycSp}$). Both sides are built functorially from
		$THH(-)$ applied to objects and structure maps of the bar construction. 
		$THH(-)$ belongs to $\mathrm{CycSp}$ by construction \cite{nik}. 
Thus,  every map entering
		the construction of $\alpha$ (face and degeneracy maps of the bar-simplicial
		object, the comparison map) is already a map of cyclotomic spectra. No 
		separate cyclotomic structure needs to be built on $\alpha$ after the fact. It
		remains only to check $\alpha$, now regarded as a map in $\mathrm{CycSp}$, is an
		equivalence there. For this we use that the forgetful functor
		$U:\mathrm{CycSp}\to\mathrm{Sp}$ is conservative (reflects equivalences)
		\cite{cmnn}. A map of cyclotomic spectra is an equivalence if and only if its
		underlying map of spectra is. This is not a deep fact requiring colimit-preservation
		of any Tate construction. It holds because the cyclotomic Frobenius is
		compatibility data on a map of underlying spectra (a path in a
		mapping space)  
instead of   additional data that could independently fail to be
		invertible. If $U(\alpha)$ is an equivalence, its inverse and the 
		homotopies transport the Frobenius-compatibility data along it automatically. Since
		$U(\alpha)$ is exactly the equivalence of underlying spectra established in Part 1,
		$\alpha$ is an equivalence of cyclotomic spectra. This is the 
 direct argument at the level of cyclotomic spectra. It bypasses the question of 
		whether $(-)^{tC_p}$ commutes with the colimit entirely. 
It is needed only that 
		equivalences in $\mathrm{CycSp}$ are detected on underlying spectra.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lemetale}
		Let $Z\to Z'$ be an \'etale map of commutative rings and $A$ an associative
		$Z$-algebra. Then the natural maps
		\begin{eqnarray}
			THH(Z)\otimes_ZZ'&\longrightarrow& THH(Z'),
\\
\nonumber
			THH(A)\otimes_ZZ'&\longrightarrow& THH(A\otimes_ZZ')
		\end{eqnarray}
		are equivalences.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The first map is an equivalence by \'etale base change for $THH$. For

		\cite{kn}.
		The second follows by combining the first with Lemma \ref{lemkunneth}
		\begin{align*}
			THH(A\otimes_ZZ')\simeq THH(A)\otimes_{THH(Z)}THH(Z')
			\simeq THH(A)\otimes_{THH(Z)}THH(Z)\otimes_ZZ'
\\
			\simeq THH(A)\otimes_ZZ'.
		\end{align*}

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lemmorita-pic}
		Let $C$ be a commutative ring.

		\smallskip
		\noindent{\rm(i)} For $P$ a finite projective faithful $C$-module, the
		$(\mathrm{End}_C(P),C)$-bimodule $P$ induces an equivalence
		$\mathrm{Perf}(\mathrm{End}_C(P))\simeq\mathrm{Perf}(C)$ and hence an
		equivalence of cyclotomic spectra
		\begin{equation}
			\tau_P:THH(\mathrm{End}_C(P))\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }THH(C),
		\end{equation}
		which on $\pi_0$ is induced by the trace
		$\mathrm{End}_C(P)/[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]\xrightarrow{\sim}C$.

		\smallskip
		\noindent{\rm(ii)} For $L$ an invertible $C$-module, conjugation by the
		$C$-linear autoequivalence $-\otimes_CL$ of $\mathrm{Perf}(C)$
 acts on $THH(C)$ by
		an automorphism which is homotopic to the identity via an explicitly
		constructed homotopy.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Part (i) is Morita invariance of $THH$ as an invariant of stable
		$\infty$-categories, refined to cyclotomic spectra \cite{bgt, nik, hss}.

		For (ii), the functor $-\otimes_CL$ corresponds, under the identification of
		$C$-linear endofunctors of $\mathrm{Perf}(C)$ with $C$-bimodules, to the
		 central bimodule $\mu^*L$, i.e., the pullback of $L$ along the
		multiplication $\mu:C\otimes C^{\mathrm{op}}\to C$. Centrality means that the
		left and right $C$-actions coincide. For central bimodules there is a
		canonical equivalence $THH(C;\mu^*M)\simeq M\otimes_CTHH(C)$, by the change
		of rings
		\begin{equation}
			\mu^*M\otimes^{\mathbb L}_{C\otimes C^{\mathrm{op}}}C
			\;\simeq\;
			M\otimes^{\mathbb L}_C\bigl(C\otimes^{\mathbb L}_{C\otimes
				C^{\mathrm{op}}}C\bigr).
		\end{equation}
		The automorphism of $THH(C)$ induced by conjugation by $-\otimes_CL$ is, by
		the cyclicity of the trace \cite{hss, HNS}, computed by transporting $L$ once
		around the trace and cancelling it against $L^\vee$. Under the displayed
		identification, it is the map induced by the composite
		$C\xrightarrow{\mathrm{coev}}L\otimes_CL^\vee\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ev}}C$. This
		composite computes the categorical dimension of the dualizable module $L$. 
The triangle identities alone only that say this dimension is some automorphism of
		$C$, not that it is the identity.
 What relates it to the identity is that $L$ is a 
		classical (degree-zero) rank-one projective $C$-module. $C$ is an ordinary
		commutative ring  instead of   a ring spectrum where a shift could contribute a
		nontrivial sign. 
 Locally on $\operatorname{Spec}C$ where $L\cong C$, coev and ev 
		become, under this trivialization,  the multiplication maps
		$C\to C\otimes_CC\to C$, whose composite is the identity. 
Since the dimension is
		locally the identity and $\operatorname{Spec}C$ is covered by such opens, it is the
		identity globally. Hence the conjugation action is
		homotopic to the identity. The last claim of (ii) follows since two
		Morita bimodules with the same endomorphism algebra differ by tensoring
		with an invertible module.

\label{lempi0azumaya}
	Let $Z$ be a connective $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-ring and $A$ a connective derived Azumaya
	$Z$-algebra. Then $\pi_0(A)$ is an ordinary Azumaya $\pi_0(Z)$-algebra, and the
	composite
	\[
		\pi_0(A)\longrightarrow\pi_0(A)/[\pi_0(A),\pi_0(A)]\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\pi_0(Z)
	\]
	given by the classical reduced trace of the Azumaya algebra $\pi_0(A)$ is independent
	of the choice, among \'etale-local presentations $A\otimes_ZZ'\simeq
	\mathrm{End}_{Z'}(P)$ equipped by Antieau-Gepner \cite{ag}, used to establish it. 
 We call this composite the reduced Morita trace of $A$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Fix such a presentation $A\otimes_ZZ'\simeq\mathrm{End}_{Z'}(P)$, $P$ perfect and
	faithful over $Z'$. Since $P$ is a perfect (dualizable) $Z'$-module, $\mathrm{End}_{Z'}(P)
	\simeq P\otimes_{Z'}P^\vee$, and dualizable objects, together with their duals and
	evaluation/coevaluation maps, are preserved by every symmetric monoidal functor
	\cite{ha}. Applying the base-change functor $-\otimes_{Z'}\kappa(x)$, which is
	symmetric monoidal, therefore gives
	$\mathrm{End}_{Z'}(P)\otimes_{Z'}\kappa(x)\simeq(P\otimes_{Z'}\kappa(x))\otimes_{\kappa(x)}
	(P\otimes_{Z'}\kappa(x))^\vee\simeq\mathrm{End}_{\kappa(x)}(P\otimes^L_{Z'}\kappa(x))$,
	which is the base-change identity used below. This requires only perfectness of $P$, not
	flatness or any special property of $\kappa(x)$.

	At a point $x\in\operatorname{Spec}\pi_0(Z')$ with residue field
	$\kappa(x)$, $P\otimes^L_{Z'}\kappa(x)$ is a perfect complex over a field, hence
	(non-canonically) a sum of shifts of $\kappa(x)$. Let $a(x)\le b(x)$ be the minimal and
	maximal degrees in which it is nonzero. If $a(x)<b(x)$, the graded endomorphism complex
	$\pi_*\operatorname{End}_{\kappa(x)}(P\otimes\kappa(x))$ has a nonzero class in degree
	$a(x)-b(x)<0$ (from a map out of the top degree into the bottom one), contradicting
	connectivity of $\operatorname{End}_Z(A)\otimes\kappa(x)\simeq
	\operatorname{End}_{\kappa(x)}(P\otimes\kappa(x))$, the identity just established. Thus, 
	$a(x)=b(x)$ at every point: $P$
	has Tor-amplitude $0$ at every point of $\operatorname{Spec}\pi_0(Z')$. For the passage
	from this pointwise statement to a global one (local constancy of $a(-)$ and the
	identification, on each locus where $a$ is constant, of $\Sigma^{-a_0}P$ with a finite
	projective module)  we cite the standard treatment of
	Tor-amplitude for perfect modules over a connective ring spectrum in
	\cite{ag} (Definition 2.11 and the surrounding results there, developed in exactly
	the generality used here). This is a derived reference in place of the earlier
	general \cite{sag}. On each open where $a$
	takes a fixed value $a_0$, $\Sigma^{-a_0}P$ is a perfect complex concentrated in degree $0$, hence a
	finite projective $\pi_0(Z')$-module there.

	For faithfulness, and for the componentwise construction of the shifted module, we
	make the following. Faithfulness of $P$ over $Z'$ means the
	functor $-\otimes_{Z'}P$ on $\mathrm{Mod}_{Z'}$ is conservative. 
 Equivalently (since
	$P$ is perfect) that $P$ generates $\mathrm{Mod}_{Z'}$ as a thick subcategory. 
	Equivalently, that $P\otimes_{Z'}\kappa(x)\not\simeq0$ for every point
	$x\in\operatorname{Spec}\pi_0(Z')$ (a perfect module vanishes on an open subscheme
	where all its residue fibers vanish, by Nakayama applied in each fiber).
 Since $a(-)$ is
	locally constant, it is constant on each connected component of
	$\operatorname{Spec}\pi_0(Z')$. But different components may carry different values of
	$a$. There is in general no single integer $a_0$ shifting $P$ to degree $0$
	everywhere at once. We therefore do not shift $P$ globally by a single
	suspension. Instead, let $\{U_i\}$ be the (open and closed) decomposition of
	$\operatorname{Spec}\pi_0(Z')$ into the loci where $a(-)$ takes each of its finitely
	many values $a_i$.
  $P$ is by assumption a perfect $Z'$-module, hence has bounded
	Tor-amplitude globally. I.e., there exist integers $m\le M$ (independent of $x$) with
	$P$ of Tor-amplitude $[m,M]$ \cite{ag}. Since $a(x)\in[m,M]$ for every $x$, the
	function $a(-)$ takes at most $M-m+1$ distinct values. Thus, $\{U_i\}$ is indexed by a
	finite set $i\in\{1,\ldots,M-m+1\}$ even though each individual $U_i$ may  be a
	union of infinitely many connected components of $\operatorname{Spec}\pi_0(Z')$. This
	finiteness of the index set (not of the components within each $U_i$) is what
	makes the direct sum defining $Q$ below a finite direct sum, hence a perfect
	module, and define
	\[
		Q\;:=\;\coprod_i\ \Sigma^{-a_i}\bigl(P|_{U_i}\bigr).
	\]
	I.e., $Q$ is the perfect $Z'$-module obtained from $P$ by applying the each component 
	shift $\Sigma^{-a_i}$ on each piece $U_i$ separately. This is well defined as a single
	global perfect $Z'$-module because the $U_i$ are clopen and hence
	$Z'$ splits as a product $\prod_i Z'_i$ compatibly with $P=\prod_iP|_{U_i}$.
  By construction $Q\otimes_{Z'}\kappa(x)$ is concentrated in degree $0$ for every $x$. Thus, 
	$Q$ is a finite projective $\pi_0(Z')$-module in the classical sense (via
	$\pi_0(Q)$), and $\mathrm{End}_{Z'}(Q)\simeq\mathrm{End}_{Z'}(P)$ since a shift by any
	locally constant integer does not change endomorphisms. On each $U_i$,
	$\mathrm{End}_{Z'}(\Sigma^{-a_i}P|_{U_i})\simeq\mathrm{End}_{Z'}(P|_{U_i})$ since 
	$\mathrm{End}$ is invariant under simultaneously shifting a module in both variables of
	$\mathrm{Hom}(-,-)$. Thus, the endomorphism algebras of $P$ and of $Q$ agree factor by
	factor over the decomposition $Z'=\prod_iZ'_i$, and hence agree globally. 

For faithfulness
	of $Q$: at each $x\in U_i$, $\pi_0(Q)\otimes_{\pi_0(Z')}\kappa(x)=\pi_{a_i}(P\otimes
	\kappa(x))\ne0$ by faithfulness of $P$. Thus,  $\pi_0(Q)$ is faithful over
	$\pi_0(Z')$ pointwise, i.e., its support is everything. Therefore, $\pi_0(Q)$ 
(not, in general, $\pi_0(P)$ , if $a(-)$ is non-constant)  is the finite, faithful,
	projective $\pi_0(Z')$-module with
	$\pi_0(A)\otimes_{\pi_0(Z)}\pi_0(Z')\simeq\operatorname{End}_{\pi_0(Z')}(\pi_0(Q))$,
	expressing $\pi_0(A)$ as Azumaya over $\pi_0(Z)$ \'etale-locally on
	$\operatorname{Spec}\pi_0(Z)$. To conclude Azumaya property globally from this, we
	use that each of the three defining conditions descends 
	through faithfully flat (in particular \'etale) base change $\pi_0(Z)\to\pi_0(Z')$.
	Finite projectivity of a module descends along fpqc covers (a classical fact, e.g., 
	\cite{sga1} or \cite{v}). Faithfulness descends since the 
	support of a module is a closed condition compatible with the faithfully flat surjection
	$\operatorname{Spec}\pi_0(Z')\to\operatorname{Spec}\pi_0(Z)$. Thus, a module with full
	support after such a base change already has full support before it. The
	endomorphism-algebra condition $A\otimes_ZA^{\mathrm{op}}\cong\mathrm{End}_Z(A)$
	descends since both sides are compatible with (flat) base change and agreement after
	a faithfully flat base change implies agreement before it (again fpqc descent, applied
	to the map comparing the two sides  instead of   to a single module). Azumaya property being
	an fpqc-local condition in this sense (which is how it is normally defined
	when not simply assumed classically, cf. \cite{grothbrauer, milne})  the \'etale-local
	statement  is what is required. 

For independence of the presentation: two local
	presentations, after passing to a common refinement of their covers, present the same
	$\pi_0(A)\otimes_{\pi_0(Z)}\pi_0(Z'')$ as $\operatorname{End}_{\pi_0(Z'')}$ of two
	faithful finite projective modules, which therefore differ by an invertible
	$\pi_0(Z'')$-module. By Lemma \ref{lemmorita-pic}\,(ii) (applied to the classical,
	degree-zero setting) the two induced reduced traces agree.

\label{thmderived}

\noindent\textit{(1) Underlying equivalence:} there is an equivalence of underlying spectra
\[
\Phi_A:THH(A)\xrightarrow{\sim}THH(Z),
 \]
linear over the $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-ring $THH(Z)$, which on $\pi_0$ recovers the reduced
Morita trace $\pi_0(A)/[\pi_0(A),\pi_0(A)]\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_0(Z)$, with the same

and Remark \ref{remscopenarrow}. 

\noindent\textit{(2) Base change compatibility} (as in Theorem
\ref{thmazumaya}{\rm(}2{\rm)}): once $\Phi_A$ is fixed as in part (1), its restriction
$\Phi_A\otimes_ZZ'$ along the \'etale base-change equivalences for $THH$ is a
valid choice of $\Phi_{A\otimes_ZZ'}$ for every \'etale $Z\to Z'$ simultaneously, coherently
under further base change, since restriction along a fixed equivalence is automatically
functorial (we do not claim this choice, or $\Phi_A$, is canonical).

\noindent\textit{(3) Conservation of cyclotomic invariants:} consequently,
for every faithfully flat \'etale splitting cover $Z\to Z'$ trivializing $A$, the local

TR^r(A\otimes_ZZ';p)\simeq TR^r(Z';p),
\quad

\quad 
TC(A\otimes_ZZ';\mathbb{Z}_p)\simeq TC(Z';\mathbb{Z}_p),
\end{align*}
compatibly with the restriction, Frobenius, and Verschiebung operators of the

<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
Connectivity of $A$ is not a technical convenience but an assumption the theorem
	needs. Without it, part (1) $\pi_0$ identification can fail. For $Z=H\pi_0(Z)$
	discrete and $P=Z\oplus\Sigma Z$, the algebra $A=\mathrm{End}_Z(P)$ is a
	(non-connective) derived Azumaya $Z$-algebra with $THH(A)\simeq THH(Z)$ by Morita
	invariance, yet $\pi_0(A)\cong\pi_0(Z)\times\pi_0(Z)$, thus 
	$\pi_0(A)/[\pi_0(A),\pi_0(A)]\not\cong\pi_0(Z)$. The failure of connectivity,  as opposed to just the $\pi_0$-count,  is transparent by computing $A$ in each degree. 
	Setting $P=Z\oplus\Sigma Z$ as a sum of the two evident summands,
	\begin{eqnarray*}
&A=\mathrm{Hom}_Z(P,P)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_Z(Z,Z)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}_Z(Z,\Sigma Z)
		\\ &\qquad \qquad \qquad  \oplus\mathrm{Hom}_Z(\Sigma Z,Z)
\oplus\mathrm{Hom}_Z(\Sigma Z,\Sigma Z)
		\simeq Z\oplus\Sigma Z\oplus\Sigma^{-1}Z\oplus Z,
	\end{eqnarray*}
	using $\mathrm{Hom}_Z(\Sigma Z,Z)\simeq\Sigma^{-1}\mathrm{Hom}_Z(Z,Z)\simeq\Sigma^{-1}Z$.
	In particular $\pi_{-1}(A)\cong\pi_0(Z)\ne0$ (this is exactly the
	$\mathrm{Hom}_Z(\Sigma Z,Z)$ summand, sitting in degree $-1$). $A$ has a nonzero
	homotopy group in a strictly negative degree. Thus, $A$ is  non-connective, not
	only failing the $\pi_0$-identification for some subtler reason. Connectivity of $A$ does not 
	 lead to a local splitting module $P$ to have trivial categorical dimension. 
	$P=\Sigma Z$ gives the connective algebra $\mathrm{End}_Z(P)\simeq Z$ even though $P$
	has categorical dimension $-1$. 
 Connectivity of $A=\mathrm{End}_Z(P)$ leads to 
	  that $P$ is concentrated in a single, locally
	constant degree. Thus, $P$ may be replaced by a shift of trivial categorical dimension
	without changing $\mathrm{End}_Z(P)$. It is this normalization, not connectivity of
	$A$ alone, that lets the trace argument proceed. Finally, connectivity
	of $A$ (hence of $THH(A)$) is what lets $TR^r$ in part (3) be recovered from the
	cyclotomic structure in the sense of \cite{nik}.

As in the proof of Theorem \ref{thmazumaya}, the algebra isomorphism
	$A\otimes_ZA^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathrm{End}_Z(A)$ defining derived
	Azumaya-ness exhibits $\mathrm{Perf}(A)$ as an invertible object of
	$\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{Perf}(Z)}(\mathrm{Cat}_\infty^{\mathrm{perf}})$ with inverse
	$\mathrm{Perf}(A^{\mathrm{op}})$ \cite{ag, toen}. Applying $THH$ (now Lemma
	\ref{lemkunnethhigher}, the $\infty$-categorical Kunneth equivalence proved above for
	$Z$ a connective $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-ring and $\otimes_Z$ derived,  instead of   the
	discrete Lemma \ref{lemkunneth}) to this isomorphism gives, as in the proof of
	Theorem \ref{thmazumaya},
	\[
		THH(A)\otimes_{THH(Z)}THH(A^{\mathrm{op}})\;\simeq\;THH(\mathrm{End}_Z(A))
		\;\xrightarrow[\ \sim\ ]{\ \tau_A\ }\;THH(Z),
	\]
	expressing $THH(A)$ as invertible in $\mathrm{CycSp}$, with inverse
	$THH(A^{\mathrm{op}})$. 

	Forgetting to underlying spectra: since $A$ and $A^{\mathrm{op}}$ are connective, so
	are $THH(A)$ and $THH(A^{\mathrm{op}})$, and $THH(Z)$ is a connective
	$\mathbb{E}_\infty$-ring. Lemma \ref{leminvtrivial} identifies the underlying
	$THH(Z)$-module $THH(A)$ with $\pi_0THH(A)=\pi_0(A)/[\pi_0(A),\pi_0(A)]$, provided
	$\pi_0(A)$ is Azumaya over $\pi_0(Z)$ (so that the \emph{reduced Morita trace} of
	Lemma \ref{lempi0azumaya} below trivializes this module). This supplies the
	underlying equivalence
	\[
		\Phi_A:THH(A)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }THH(Z),
		\qquad \pi_0\Phi_A=\text{reduced Morita trace}.
	\]
	As in the proof of Theorem \ref{thmazumaya}, choosing a faithfully flat
	\'etale splitting $Z\to Z'$ with $A\otimes_ZZ'\simeq\mathrm{End}_{Z'}(P)$
	\cite{ag}, the base-changed $\Phi_A\otimes_ZZ'$ and the local Morita trace
	$\tau_P$ both induce the reduced trace on $\pi_0$, but it is not possible 
to conclude they are
	equal. The same ambiguity analyzed in Lemma \ref{leminvtrivial} and in the proof of
	Theorem \ref{thmazumaya}. It is not claimed that $\Phi_A$  is
	\'etale-locally cyclotomic. Instead, $\tau_P$, cyclotomic, supplies
	the \'etale-local cyclotomic consequences of part (3). 
The same
	warning as in Theorem \ref{thmazumaya} applies to upgrading this to a single global
	cyclotomic equivalence when $A$ has no global splitting module. This is not resolved 
	in this paper. In particular, when $Z$ and $A$ are discrete this is Theorem
	\ref{thmazumaya}, of which the present theorem is the derived generalization.

	Part (2) follows exactly as in the corrected argument for Theorem
	\ref{thmazumaya}{\rm(}2{\rm)}: no independent reconstruction of $\Phi_{A\otimes_ZZ'}$
	is needed. The fixed $\Phi_A$ of part (1) restricts, along \'etale base change, to an
	equivalence $\Phi_A\otimes_ZZ':THH(A\otimes_ZZ')\to THH(Z')$ whose $\pi_0$ is the
	restriction of the reduced Morita trace (again because base change commutes with the
	natural transformation defining that trace). Thus, $\Phi_A\otimes_ZZ'$ satisfies part
	(1) requirements for $A\otimes_ZZ'$ by construction. The resulting system is
	coherent under further base change by associativity of $-\otimes_ZZ'$ alone.

	Part (3): \'etale-locally, $\tau_P$ is built entirely inside $\mathrm{CycSp}$, so
	$TC^-$, $TP$, $TC$ transport there, applied to $\tau_P$, as functors of the cyclotomic structure
	\cite{nik}. Since $A$ (hence $THH(A)$) is connective, the fixed points
	computing $TR^r$ are similarly recovered from this structure \cite{nik}. Thus, $TR^r$
	transports as well, \'etale-locally via $\tau_P$, and globally under the same  assumption as in
	Theorem \ref{thmazumaya}.
