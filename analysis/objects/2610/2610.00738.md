<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A proper, lower semicontinuous function $\Phi: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$ is said to have the \textbf{Kurdyka--\L ojasiewicz (KL) property} at a point $x^* \in \operatorname{dom} \partial \Phi$ if there exists a neighborhood $U$ of $x^*$, a constant $\eta \in [0, +\infty)$, and a continuous concave function $\varphi: [0, \eta) \to \mathbb{R}_+$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\varphi(0) = 0$, $\varphi$ is continuously differentiable on $(0, \eta)$ with $\varphi'(s) > 0$ for all $s \in (0, \eta)$,
    \item For all $x \in U \cap \{x \mid \Phi(x^*) < \Phi(x) < \Phi(x^*) + \eta\}$, the KL inequality holds:
    \begin{equation}
        \varphi'(\Phi(x) - \Phi(x^*)) \cdot \operatorname{dist}(0, \partial \Phi(x)) \ge 1,
    \end{equation}
    where $\partial \Phi(x)$ denotes the limiting subdifferential of $\Phi$, and $\operatorname{dist}(0, \partial \Phi(x)) \triangleq \inf \{\|v\| : v \in \partial \Phi(x)\}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
Let $\{x_k\}_{k \ge 0}$ be the parameter sequence generated by Q-MINO, and let $z_k = Q(x_k)$ denote the quantized state at iteration $k$. Under our assumptions (cf. $\S$3.1), the bundle drift matrix entry $\epsilon_{k,j} \triangleq \|z_j - z_k\|^2 / (\|z_k\|^2 + \epsilon_z)$ and the EMA surrogate gradient error satisfy:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2\right] \le (1-\beta) \sigma^2 + 2\delta^2 + \mathcal{O}\left(L^2 \sum_{j \in \mathcal{B}_k} \|x_k - x_j\|^2\right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}_k$ represents the set of bundle indices maintained at step $k$, $\beta \in (0, 1)$ is the EMA momentum coefficient, $\delta$ is the STE mismatch floor, and $L$ is the Lipschitz constant.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
For any iteration $k \ge 1$ and target alignment parameter $\rho \in (0, 1]$, the inner Frank-Wolfe subproblem search space $\mathcal{D}_k \triangleq \{\alpha \in \Delta^{M-1} \mid \alpha^\top H_k e_M \ge \rho \|\bar{g}_k\|^2\}$ is non-empty. Specifically, the fallback unit basis vector $e_M = (0, \dots, 0, 1)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^M$ satisfies $e_M^\top H_k e_M = \|\bar{g}_k\|^2 \ge \rho \|\bar{g}_k\|^2$, guaranteeing that the Frank-Wolfe sub-routine is strictly feasible at every step $k$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
Let $\eta_k \leq \frac{\rho}{2L}$. Under our assumptions (cf. $\S$3.1) and the inner loop feasibility lemma, the minimal-norm search direction $d_k = G_k \alpha_k^*$ constructed by Q-MINO with generalized composite update step $\Delta_k \triangleq \frac{1}{\eta_k}(x_k - x_{k+1}) = d_k + s_{k+1}$ yields the sufficient decrease condition:

\mathbb{E}[\Phi(x_{k+1}) \mid x_k] \le \Phi(x_k) - \frac{\eta_k}{4} \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}\Delta_k\Vert{}^2 \mid x_k\right] + \eta_k \Vert{}\bar{g}_k - d_k\Vert{}^2 + \eta_k (1-\beta)\sigma^2 + 2\eta_k \delta^2 + \mathcal{O}\left(\eta_k L^2 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}_k} \Vert{}x_k - x_i\Vert{}^2\right).
\end{equation}

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Let $\Phi(x) = f(x) + r(x)$ be a proper, lower semicontinuous function, and let $\mathcal{L}_k$ denote the augmented stochastic Lyapunov functional. Suppose the learning rate sequence satisfies $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \eta_k = \infty$ and $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \eta_k^2 < \infty$. Then, under the assumptions of $\S$3.1, the sequence $\{x_k\}_{k \ge 0}$ generated by Q-MINO satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bounded Trajectory Variance:} The expected total variation of the continuous parameter sequence is bounded up to the persistent STE distortion floor, i.e.,
    \begin{equation}
        \sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbb{E}\left[\|x_{k+1} - x_k\|^2\right] < \infty.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Asymptotic Neighborhood Convergence:} The sequence of iterates $\{x_k\}_{k \ge 0}$ converges in expectation to a compact error neighborhood $\mathcal{U}_\delta$ around the stationary set of $\Phi$, where the radius of $\mathcal{U}_\delta$ is explicitly scaled by the STE mismatch floor $\delta$, i.e., 
    \begin{equation}
        \lim_{k \to \infty} \mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{dist}(x_k, \mathcal{U}_\delta)\right] = 0.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
\textbf{Lemma 2 (Subgradient Tracking \& Quantized Drift Bound):} Recall $\bar{g}_k = \beta\bar{g}_{k-1} + (1-\beta)\hat{g}_k$ (step 6 in Algorithm 1) with EMA coefficient $\beta \in (0,1)$. Likewise, $\hat{g}_k = \text{STEGrad}(x_k, \xi_k)$ for the FP32 parameter iterate $x_k$ and mini-batch $\xi_k$ (step 5 in Algorithm 1). Note the following decomposition of $\hat{g}_k$,
\[
\hat{g}_k(x_k) = \nabla f(x_k) + e_k(x_k) + \xi_k,
\]
where $\nabla f(x_k)$ is the true smooth gradient, $e_k(x_k)$ is the deterministic quantization mismatch/bias bounded by $\Vert{}e_k(x_k)\Vert{} \le \delta$, and $\xi_k$ is the zero-mean stochastic sampling noise satisfying $\mathbb{E}[\xi_k \mid x_k] = 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[\Vert{}\xi_k\Vert{}^2 \mid x_k] \le \sigma^2$. 

Unrolling $\bar{g}_k$ over the optimization history yields the weighted linear combination:
\[
\bar{g}_k = (1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta^{k-i}\nabla f(x_i) + (1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta^{k-i}e_i(x_i) + (1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta^{k-i}\xi_i.
\]
With the geometric identity $(1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta^{k-i} = 1 - \beta^{k+1}$ and assuming a steady-state regime where the initial transient bias vanishes for sufficiently large $k$ ($k \to \infty$, hence $\beta^{k+1} \to 0$), we have $(1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta^{k-i} = 1$. Subtracting $\nabla f(x_k)$ from both sides isolates the tracking error into three explicit vectors:
\[
\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k) = \underbrace{(1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta^{k-i}\big(\nabla f(x_i) - \nabla f(x_k)\big)}_{A_k} + \underbrace{(1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta^{k-i}e_i(x_i)}_{B_k} + \underbrace{(1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta^{k-i}\xi_i}_{C_k}.
\]
Thus, $\|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\| \leq \|A_k\| + \|B_k\| + \|C_k\|$. Taking the conditional expectation of the squared norm, the zero-mean property and conditional independence of the mini-batch sampling noise ($\mathbb{E}[\xi_i \mid x_i] = 0$) ensure that all cross-terms between $C_k$ and the deterministic historical paths $A_k$ and $B_k$ vanish identically in expectation ($\mathbb{E}[\langle A_k, C_k \rangle] = 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[\langle B_k, C_k \rangle] = 0$). Thus, we obtain
\[
\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2 \mid x_k\right] = \mathbb{E}\left[\|A_k + B_k\|^2 \mid x_k\right] + \mathbb{E}\left[\|C_k\|^2 \mid x_k\right].
\]
We evaluate each term individually. Expanding the expected squared norm of the linear combination of independent noise vectors and evaluating the geometric series:
\[
\mathbb{E}\left[\|C_k\|^2 \mid x_k\right] = (1-\beta)^2 \sum_{i=0}^{k} \beta^{2(k-i)} \mathbb{E}\left[\|\xi_i\|^2 \mid x_i\right] \le (1-\beta)^2 \left( \frac{1 - \beta^{2(k+1)}}{1 - \beta^2} \right) \sigma^2 \le \frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}\sigma^2 \le (1-\beta)\sigma^2.
\]
Applying Young's inequality $\|A_k + B_k\|^2 \le 2\|B_k\|^2 + 2\|A_k\|^2$ separates the quantization bias from the parameter drift. For the quantization bias $B_k$, setting $w_i = (1-\beta)\beta^{k-i}$ (where $\sum_{i=0}^k w_i \le 1$) and applying Jensen's inequality for convex quadratic norms along with the uniform bound $\|e_i(x_i)\| \le \delta$:
\[
\|B_k\|^2 = \left\| \sum_{i=0}^{k} w_i e_i(x_i) \right\|^2 \le \left(\sum_{i=0}^{k} w_i\right) \sum_{i=0}^{k} w_i \|e_i(x_i)\|^2 \le \delta^2 \sum_{i=0}^{k} w_i \le \delta^2.
\]
For the parameter trajectory drift $A_k$, applying Jensen's inequality and the $L$-smoothness condition $\|\nabla f(x_i) - \nabla f(x_k)\| \le L \|x_k - x_i\|$:
\[
\|A_k\|^2 \le (1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta^{k-i}\|\nabla f(x_i) - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2 \le L^2 (1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta^{k-i}\|x_k - x_i\|^2.
\]
Partitioning the historical sum into indices within the active memory window $\mathcal{B}_k$ ($k - m \le i \le k$) and the historical tail ($i < k - m$), and assuming bounded iterate domain diameter $\sup_{k} \|x_k\| \le D$:
\[
(1-\beta)\sum_{i < k-m}\beta^{k-i}\|x_k - x_i\|^2 \le 4D^2 (1-\beta) \sum_{i < k-m} \beta^{k-i} = 4D^2 \beta^{m+1} = \mathcal{O}(\beta^m).
\]

Combining the variance bound, the quantization bias floor, and the active bundle drift yields
\[
\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2 \mid x_k\right] \le (1-\beta)\sigma^2 + 2\delta^2 + \mathcal{O}\left(L^2 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}_k} \|x_k - x_i\|^2\right).
\]

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
\textbf{Lemma 3 (Inner Loop Feasibility):} Q-MINO is constructed to ensure that $e_M$ is always strictly feasible in $D_k$ whenever $\rho \leq 1$, thus ensuring that $\mathcal{D}_k \neq \emptyset$ in the Frank-Wolfe sub-routine. Recall the definition of the inner loop search space at iteration $k$ (step 17 in Algorithm 1), 
\[
\mathcal{D}_k \triangleq \left\{ \alpha \in \Delta^{M-1} \;\middle|\; \alpha^\top H_k \alpha \ge \rho \|\bar{g}_k\|^2 \right\},
\]
where $\Delta^{M-1} = \left\{ \alpha \in \mathbb{R}^M\;\middle|\; \sum_{i=1}^M \alpha_i = 1, \; \alpha_i \ge 0 \right\}$ is the standard probability simplex, $H_k = V_k^\top V_k \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times M}$ is the Gram matrix of candidate direction vectors $V_k = [v_1, \dots, v_M]$, and $\rho \in (0,1]$ is the alignment parameter. To establish that $\mathcal{D}_k$ is non-empty, we construct an explicit feasible point $\alpha = e_M = (0, \dots, 0, 1)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^M$. The vector $e_M$ has non-negative components $(e_M)_i = 0 \ge 0$ for $i < M$ and $(e_M)_M = 1 \ge 0$, and satisfies $\sum_{i=1}^M (e_M)_i = 1$. Thus, $e_M \in \Delta^{M-1}$ (i.e., $e_M$ is always a simplex element). Evaluating the linear alignment form at $\alpha = e_M$ gives
\[
e_M^\top H_k e_M = (H_k)_{MM}.
\]
By construction of $H_k$ in Q-MINO, the $M$-th diagonal entry satisfies $(H_k)_{MM} = \Vert{}\bar{g}_k\Vert{}^2$ (since $H_k$ is a Gram matrix). Since $\rho \in (0, 1]$ and $\Vert{}\bar{g}_k\Vert{}^2 \ge 0$, we have
\[
e_M^\top H_k e_M = \Vert{}\bar{g}_k\Vert{}^2 \ge \rho \Vert{}\bar{g}_k\Vert{}^2,
\]
so $e_M \in \mathcal{D}_k$ and $\mathcal{D}_k$ is non-empty $\forall k \geq 1$.

\textbf{Lemma 4 (Sufficient Decrease / Descent Lemma):} Since $f$ is $L$-smooth, the parameter update  $x_{k+1} = x_k - \eta_k d_k$ satisfies
\[
f(x_{k+1}) \le f(x_k) - \eta_k \langle \nabla f(x_k), d_k \rangle + \frac{L\eta_k^2}{2} \|d_k\|^2.
\]
To relate the inner product $\langle \nabla f(x_k), d_k \rangle$ to the EMA gradient estimator $\bar{g}_k$, we decompose the true gradient as $\nabla f(x_k) = \bar{g}_k - e_k^{\text{track}}$, where $e_k^{\text{track}} \triangleq \bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)$ is the tracking error:
\[
\langle \nabla f(x_k), d_k \rangle = \langle \bar{g}_k, d_k \rangle - \langle e_k^{\text{track}}, d_k \rangle.
\]
By Lemma 3 and the construction of the Frank-Wolfe sub-routine, the optimal weight vector $\alpha_k^* \in \mathcal{D}_k$ defines a direction $d_k = G_k \alpha_k^*$ satisfying

\[
\langle \bar{g}_k, d_k \rangle \ge \rho \|\bar{g}_k\|^2,
\]

\[
\|d_k\|^2 \le \|G_k e_M\|^2 = \|\bar{g}_k\|^2.
\]
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz and Young's inequalities to the tracking error cross-term with (alignment) parameter $\rho > 0$, we obtain
\[
|\langle e_k^{\text{track}}, d_k \rangle| \le \|e_k^{\text{track}}\| \|d_k\| \le \frac{1}{2\rho} \|e_k^{\text{track}}\|^2 + \frac{\rho}{2} \|d_k\|^2.
\]
Substituting the alignment bound and error bound into the inner product decomposition yields
\[
\langle \nabla f(x_k), d_k \rangle \ge \rho \|\bar{g}_k\|^2 - \frac{\rho}{2} \|d_k\|^2 - \frac{1}{2\rho} \|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2.
\]
Using the norm bound $\|d_k\|^2 \le \|\bar{g}_k\|^2$, we simplify the first two terms:
\[
\rho \|\bar{g}_k\|^2 - \frac{\rho}{2} \|d_k\|^2 \ge \rho \|d_k\|^2 - \frac{\rho}{2} \|d_k\|^2 = \frac{\rho}{2} \|d_k\|^2.
\]
Inserting this bound back into the $L$-smoothness descent inequality gives:
\[
f(x_{k+1}) \le f(x_k) - \eta_k \left( \frac{\rho}{2} \|d_k\|^2 - \frac{1}{2\rho} \|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2 \right) + \frac{L\eta_k^2}{2} \|d_k\|^2.
\]
Regrouping terms by $\|d_k\|^2$:
\[
f(x_{k+1}) \le f(x_k) - \eta_k \left( \frac{\rho}{2} - \frac{L\eta_k}{2} \right) \|d_k\|^2 + \frac{\eta_k}{2\rho} \|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2.
\]
We need $\frac{\rho }{2}-\frac{L\eta _{k}}{2}>0$ for descent, which implies $\eta_k \leq \frac{\rho}{L}$, but we choose $\eta_k \leq \frac{\rho}{2L}$, which ensures $\frac{\rho}{2} - \frac{L\eta_k}{2} \geq \frac{\rho}{4}$. With $\eta_k \leq \frac{\rho}{2L}$, we have
\[
f(x_{k+1}) \le f(x_k) - \frac{\eta_k \rho}{4} \|d_k\|^2 + \frac{\eta_k}{2\rho} \|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2.
\]
Taking expectation on $x_k$ yields
\[
\mathbb{E}[f(x_{k+1}) \mid x_k] \le f(x_k) - \frac{\eta_k \rho}{4} \mathbb{E}[\Vert{}d_k\Vert{}^2 \mid x_k] + \frac{\eta_k}{2\rho} \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\Vert{}^2 \mid x_k\right].
\]
But recall that we derived in Lemma 2 that
\[
\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2 \mid x_k\right] \le (1-\beta)\sigma^2 + 2\delta^2 + \mathcal{O}\left(L^2 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}_k} \|x_k - x_i\|^2\right).
\]
Thus,
\[
\mathbb{E}[f(x_{k+1}) \mid x_k] \le f(x_k) - \frac{\eta_k \rho}{4} \mathbb{E}[\Vert{}d_k\Vert{}^2 \mid x_k] + \frac{\eta_k}{2\rho}[(1-\beta)\sigma^2 + 2\delta^2 + \mathcal{O}\left(L^2 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}_k} \|x_k - x_i\|^2\right)].
\]
We wish to find a descent lemma for the composite surrogate objective function $\Phi(x) = f(x) + r(x)$. By optimality of the proximal sub-problem, there exists a subgradient $s_{k+1} \in \partial r(x_{k+1})$ such that
\[
s_{k+1} = \frac{1}{\eta_k}(x_k - \eta_k d_k - x_{k+1}) \implies x_k - x_{k+1} = \eta_k (d_k + s_{k+1}).
\]
Since $r(x)$ is a convex regularizer, we have
\[
r(x_{k+1}) \le r(x_k) - \langle s_{k+1}, x_k - x_{k+1} \rangle,
\]
which implies $r(x_{k+1})-r(x_k) \leq -\langle s_{k+1}, x_k - x_{k+1} \rangle$. Define the generalized composite update step $\Delta_k \triangleq \frac{1}{\eta_k}(x_k - x_{k+1}) = d_k + s_{k+1}$. By decomposition of the composite objective,
\[
\Phi(x_{k+1}) - \Phi(x_k) = \left[ f(x_{k+1}) - f(x_k) \right] + \left[ r(x_{k+1}) - r(x_k) \right].
\]
We already derived $f(x_{k+1}) - f(x_k) \le -\eta_k \langle \nabla f(x_k), \Delta_k \rangle + \frac{L\eta_k^2}{2} \Vert{}\Delta_k\Vert{}^2$, so we then have
\[
\Phi(x_{k+1}) - \Phi(x_k) \le -\eta_k \langle \nabla f(x_k) + s_{k+1}, \Delta_k \rangle + \frac{L\eta_k^2}{2} \Vert{}\Delta_k\Vert{}^2,
\]
\[
\Phi(x_{k+1}) - \Phi(x_k) \le -\eta_k \left( 1 - \frac{L\eta_k}{2} \right) \|\Delta_k\|^2 - \eta_k \langle \nabla f(x_k) - d_k, \Delta_k \rangle.
\]
To incorporate the EMA gradient estimator $\bar{g}_k$, we decompose $\nabla f(x_k) - d_k = (\bar{g}_k - d_k) + (\nabla f(x_k) - \bar{g}_k)$:
\[
\langle \nabla f(x_k) - d_k, \Delta_k \rangle = \langle \bar{g}_k - d_k, \Delta_k \rangle - \langle \bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k), \Delta_k \rangle.
\]
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz and Young's inequalities ($ab \le a^2 + \frac{1}{4}b^2$) to both cross-terms with parameter $\frac{1}{4}$:

\begin{align*}
|\langle \bar{g}_k - d_k, \Delta_k \rangle| &\le \|\bar{g}_k - d_k\| \|\Delta_k\| \le \|\bar{g}_k - d_k\|^2 + \frac{1}{4} \|\Delta_k\|^2, \\
|\langle \bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k), \Delta_k \rangle| &\le \|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\| \|\Delta_k\| \le \|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2 + \frac{1}{4} \|\Delta_k\|^2.
\end{align*}

\noindent
Substituting these bounds back into the composite decrease inequality gives:
\[
\Phi(x_{k+1}) - \Phi(x_k) \le -\eta_k \left( 1 - \frac{L\eta_k}{2} - \frac{1}{2} \right) \|\Delta_k\|^2 + \eta_k \|\bar{g}_k - d_k\|^2 + \eta_k \|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2.
\]
Since $\eta_k \le \frac{\rho}{2L}$, we have $\frac{L\eta_k}{2} \leq \frac{\rho}{4}$. Thus, taking expectation yields
\[
\mathbb{E}[\Phi(x_{k+1}) \mid x_k] \le \Phi(x_k) - \frac{\eta_k \rho}{4} \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}\Delta_k\Vert{}^2 \mid x_k\right] + \eta_k \Vert{}\bar{g}_k - d_k\Vert{}^2 + \eta_k \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\Vert{}^2 \mid x_k\right].
\]
But recall from Lemma 2 that we derived $\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2 \mid x_k\right] \le (1-\beta)\sigma^2 + 2\delta^2 + \mathcal{O}\left(L^2 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}_k} \|x_k - x_i\|^2\right)$, which yields
\[
\mathbb{E}[\Phi(x_{k+1}) \mid x_k] \le \Phi(x_k) - \frac{\eta_k}{4} \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}\Delta_k\Vert{}^2 \mid x_k\right] + \eta_k \Vert{}\bar{g}_k - d_k\Vert{}^2 + \eta_k (1-\beta)\sigma^2 + 2\eta_k \delta^2 + \mathcal{O}\left(\eta_k L^2 \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}_k} \Vert{}x_k - x_i\Vert{}^2\right).
\]

\textbf{Theorem 5 (Asymptotic Convergence and Neighborhood Attainment under KL Property):} Define the candidate Lyapunov functional
\[
\mathcal{L}_k \triangleq \Phi(x_k) + \mu_k \mathcal{V}_k,
\]
where $\mathcal{V}_k \triangleq \|\bar{g}_k - d_k\|^2 + \|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2$ represents the realized variance and search direction tracking residual at step $k$, and $\mu_k \triangleq \frac{2\eta_k}{\beta}$ is the time-varying stabilization weight. By Lemma 4, for $\eta_k \le \frac{\rho}{2L}$, the composite expected step satisfies:
\[
\mathbb{E}[\Phi(x_{k+1}) \mid \mathcal{F}_k] \le \Phi(x_k) - \frac{\rho}{4\eta_k} \mathbb{E}\left[\|x_{k+1} - x_k\|^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_k\right] + \eta_k \mathcal{V}_k + \eta_k \delta^2.
\]
By the EMA error dynamics of Q-MINO, the variance tracking term obeys $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{V}_{k+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_k] \le (1-\beta)\mathcal{V}_k + C_1 \eta_k^2$, where $\beta \in [0,1)$. Taking conditional expectations of $\mathcal{L}_{k+1}$, since $\eta_{k+1} \le \eta_k$, we obtain
\[
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_k] \le \Phi(x_k) - \frac{\rho}{4\eta_k} \mathbb{E}\left[\|x_{k+1} - x_k\|^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_k\right] + \eta_k \mathcal{V}_k + \eta_k \delta^2 + \mu_k (1-\beta)\mathcal{V}_k + C_1 \mu_k \eta_k^2.
\]
Selecting the Lyapunov tracking coefficient $\mu_k \triangleq \frac{2\eta_k}{\beta}$, the historical momentum residual terms expand algebraically via $\mu_k(1-\beta)\mathcal{V}_k - \mu_k\mathcal{V}_k = -\beta\mu_k\mathcal{V}_k = -2\eta_k\mathcal{V}_k$. Grouping and canceling like terms yields the Lyapunov descent inequality
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_k] \le \mathcal{L}_k - \frac{\rho}{4\eta_k} \mathbb{E}\left[\|x_{k+1} - x_k\|^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_k\right] - \eta_k \mathcal{V}_k + \eta_k \delta^2 + C_2 \eta_k^3,
\end{equation}
where $C_2 \triangleq \frac{2 C_1}{\beta}$. Then we can obtain
\[
\frac{\rho }{4\eta _{k}}\mathbb{E}\left[\|{}x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|{}^{2}\mid \mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\le \mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1}\mid \mathcal{F}_{k}]-\eta _{k}\mathcal{V}_{k}+\eta _{k}\delta ^{2}+C_{2}\eta _{k}^{3},
\]
\[
\mathbb{E}\left[\|{}x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|{}^{2}\mid \mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\le \frac{4\eta _{k}}{\rho }\Big(\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1}\mid \mathcal{F}_{k}]\Big)+\frac{4\eta _{k}^{2}\delta ^{2}}{\rho }+\frac{4C_{2}\eta _{k}^{4}}{\rho }.
\]
Since $\eta_0 \geq \eta_k$ for all $k$, we obtain
\[
\mathbb{E}\left[\|{}x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|{}^{2}\mid \mathcal{F}_{k}\right] \leq \frac{4\eta_0}{\rho}(\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1}\mid \mathcal{F}_{k}]\Big) + \frac{4\delta^2}{\rho}\eta_k^2 + \frac{4C_2}{\rho}\eta_k^4.
\]
Applying the law of total expectation (\(\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[\cdot \mid \mathcal{F}_k]] = \mathbb{E}[\cdot]\)) and summing the inequality from $k=0$ up to $k=K$ yields
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|^2] \leq \frac{4\eta_0}{\rho}\sum_{k=0}^{K}(\mathcal{L}_k - \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1}|\mathcal{F}_k]) + \frac{4\delta^2}{\rho}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\eta_k^2+\frac{4C_2}{\rho}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\eta_k^4.
\]
Note that $\sum_{k=0}^{K}(\mathcal{L}_k - \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1}|\mathcal{F}_k]) = \sum_{k=0}^{K}(\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_k]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1}])$ by applying the law of total expectation and therefore this is a telescoping series, which implies $\sum_{k=0}^{K}(\mathcal{L}_k - \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1}|\mathcal{F}_k]) = \mathcal{L}_0 - \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{K+1}] \leq \mathcal{L}_0 - \Phi^*$. Thus, we have
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|^2] \leq \frac{4\eta_0(\mathcal{L}_0 - \Phi^*)}{\rho} + \frac{4\delta^2}{\rho}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\eta_k^2+\frac{4C_2}{\rho}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\eta_k^4.
\]
Taking $\text{lim}_{K \rightarrow \infty}$ yields
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|^2] \leq \frac{4\eta_0(\mathcal{L}_0 - \Phi^*)}{\rho} + \frac{4\delta^2}{\rho}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta_k^2+\frac{4C_2}{\rho}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta_k^4.
\]
Since $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \eta_k^2 < \infty$, and since \(\sum \eta_k^2\) converges, \(\eta_k \to 0\), which structurally guarantees that the higher-order sequence \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty \eta_k^4\) must also converge. Because every individual component on the right-hand side is strictly bounded, the infinite sum converges to a finite real value, and therefore
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|^2] < \infty.
\]
We last show asymptotic neighborhood convergence. By definition of the proximal update step $x_{k+1} = \operatorname{prox}_{\eta_k r}(x_k - \eta_k d_k)$, the first-order optimality condition states that there exists a subgradient $v_{k+1} \in \partial r(x_{k+1})$ such that
\[
0 = v_{k+1} + d_k + \frac{1}{\eta_k}(x_{k+1} - x_k).
\]
To bound the subdifferential of the full composite objective $\Phi(x_{k+1})$, define $w_{k+1} \triangleq \nabla f(x_{k+1}) + v_{k+1} \in \partial \Phi(x_{k+1})$. Substituting $v_{k+1}$ gives
\[
w_{k+1} = \nabla f(x_{k+1}) - d_k - \frac{1}{\eta_k}(x_{k+1} - x_k),
\]
which implies
\[
\|w_{k+1}\| \leq \|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-d_k\| + \frac{1}{\eta_k}\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|.
\]
Adding and subtracting $\nabla f(x_k)$ and $\bar{g}_k$, along with taking norms and applying the triangle inequality together with $L$-smoothness of $f$ yields
\[
\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{} \le \left(L + \frac{1}{\eta_k}\right) \Vert{}x_{k+1} - x_k\Vert{} + \Vert{}\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\Vert{} + \Vert{}\bar{g}_k - d_k\Vert{}.
\]
Applying the Young-type inequality $(a + b + c)^2 \le 3a^2 + 3b^2 + 3c^2$ yields
\[
\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{}^2 \le 3\left(L + \frac{1}{\eta_k}\right)^2 \Vert{}x_{k+1} - x_k\Vert{}^2 + 3\Vert{}\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\Vert{}^2 + 3\Vert{}\bar{g}_k - d_k\Vert{}^2.
\]
But recall $\mathcal{V}_k \triangleq \|\bar{g}_k - d_k\|^2 + \|\bar{g}_k - \nabla f(x_k)\|^2$, so we can simplify to
\[
\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{}^2 \le 3\left(L + \frac{1}{\eta_k}\right)^2 \Vert{}x_{k+1} - x_k\Vert{}^2 + 3\mathcal{V}_k.
\]
Taking expectation on $\mathcal{F}_k$ yields
\[
\mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{}^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_k\right] \le 3\left(L + \frac{1}{\eta_k}\right)^2 \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}x_{k+1} - x_k\Vert{}^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_k\right] + 3\mathcal{V}_k.
\]
Note $L + \frac{1}{\eta_k} = \frac{L\eta_k+1}{\eta_k} \leq \frac{L\eta_0+1}{\eta_k}$ since $\eta_k \leq \eta_0$. Let $C_L \triangleq L\eta_0 + 1$, so we obtain
\[
\mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{}^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_k\right] \le \frac{3 C_L^2}{\eta_k^2} \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}x_{k+1} - x_k\Vert{}^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_k\right] + 3\mathcal{V}_k.
\]
But recall from our learning rate dependent bound that $\mathbb{E}\left[\|{}x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|{}^{2}\mid \mathcal{F}_{k}\right] \leq \frac{4\eta_k}{\rho}(\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1}\mid \mathcal{F}_{k}]\Big) + \frac{4\delta^2}{\rho}\eta_k^2 + \frac{4C_2}{\rho}\eta_k^4$. Substituting this directly yields
\[
\mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{}^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_k\right] \le \frac{12 C_L^2}{\rho \eta_k} \Big(\mathcal{L}_k - \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_k]\Big) + \frac{12 C_L^2 \delta^2}{\rho} + \frac{12 C_L^2 C_2 \eta_k^2}{\rho} + 3\mathcal{V}_k,
\]
\[
\eta_k \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{}^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_k\right] \le \frac{12 C_L^2}{\rho} \Big(\mathcal{L}_k - \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_k]\Big) + \frac{12 C_L^2 \delta^2}{\rho} \eta_k + \frac{12 C_L^2 C_2}{\rho} \eta_k^3 + 3 \eta_k \mathcal{V}_k.
\]
Taking unconditioned total expectations $\mathbb{E}[\cdot]$ on both sides and via the law of total expectation we obtain
\[
\eta_k \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{}^2\right] \le \frac{12 C_L^2}{\rho} \left(\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_k] - \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1}]\right) + \frac{12 C_L^2 \delta^2}{\rho} \eta_k + \frac{12 C_L^2 C_2}{\rho} \eta_k^3 + 3 \eta_k \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{V}_k].
\]
Taking summation from $k = 0$ to $T-1$ and again leveraging that $\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\left(\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_k] - \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{k+1}]\right)$ is a telescoping series, we get
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{T-1} \eta_k \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{}^2\right] \le \frac{12 C_L^2}{\rho} \left(\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_0] - \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_T]\right) + \frac{12 C_L^2 \delta^2}{\rho} \sum_{k=0}^{T-1} \eta_k + \frac{12 C_L^2 C_2}{\rho} \sum_{k=0}^{T-1} \eta_k^3 + 3 \sum_{k=0}^{T-1} \eta_k \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{V}_k].
\]
We already established $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_T] \ge \Phi^* > -\infty$, so $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_0] - \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_T] \le \mathcal{L}_0 - \Phi^* < \infty$. Moreover, we can use our Lyapunov descent inequality and the fact that  $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \eta_k^2 < \infty$ implies the cubic sum is finite, i.e., $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \eta_k^3 < \infty$. Let $M \triangleq \frac{12 C_L^2}{\rho} (\mathcal{L}_0 - \Phi^*) + \frac{12 C_L^2 C_2}{\rho} \sum_{k=0}^\infty \eta_k^3 + 3 \sum_{k=0}^\infty \eta_k \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{V}_k] < \infty$. Thus, we have
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{T-1} \eta_k \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{}^2\right] \le M + \frac{12 C_L^2 \delta^2}{\rho} \sum_{k=0}^{T-1} \eta_k.
\]
Divide through by $S_T \triangleq \sum_{k=0}^{T-1} \eta_k$. Our learning rate condition requires $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \eta_k = \infty$, $S_T \to \infty$ as $T \to \infty$,
\[
\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{T-1} \eta_k \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{}^2\right]}{\sum_{k=0}^{T-1} \eta_k} \le \frac{M}{S_T} + \frac{12 C_L^2 \delta^2}{\rho}.
\]
As $T \to \infty$, the term $\frac{M}{S_T} \to 0$, which yields
\[
\limsup_{T \to \infty} \frac{\sum_{k=0}^{T-1} \eta_k \mathbb{E}\left[\Vert{}w_{k+1}\Vert{}^2\right]}{\sum_{k=0}^{T-1} \eta_k} \le \frac{12 C_L^2 \delta^2}{\rho}.
\]
By properties of weighted averages, this directly implies
\[
\liminf_{k \to \infty} \mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{dist}(0, \partial \Phi(x_k))^2\right] \le \frac{12 C_L^2 \delta^2}{\rho}
\]
We formalize our target stationary structural neighborhood as:
\[
\mathcal{U}_\delta \triangleq \left\{ x \in \operatorname{dom} \Phi \;\middle\vert{}\; \operatorname{dist}(0, \partial \Phi(x)) \le C_\delta \delta \right\},
\]
where $C_\delta \triangleq \sqrt{\frac{12 C_L^2}{\rho}}$. Because $\Phi$ is proper, lower-semicontinuous, and bounded from below, the restriction of our iterates to the bounded sub-level set $\Omega \triangleq \{x \in \mathbb{R}^d \mid \mathcal{L}_k \le \mathcal{L}_0\}$ ensures that $\Omega$ is a compact domain. By the KL property, there exists a concave, continuously differentiable desingularizing function $\phi(s) = c \cdot s^{1-\theta}$ for an exponent $\theta \in [0, 1)$ such that for all active parameter iterations remaining outside the neighborhood profile ($x_k \in \Omega \setminus \mathcal{U}_\delta$), the local geometry satisfies the functional gradient bounding inequality
\[
\phi'(\Phi(x_k) - \Phi^*) \cdot \operatorname{dist}(0, \partial \Phi(x_k)) \ge 1.
\]
Equivalently, this relation states that the subdifferential magnitude outside our neighborhood is uniformly lower bounded by the inverse slope of the desingularizing metric
\[
\operatorname{dist}(0, \partial \Phi(x_k)) \ge \frac{1}{\phi'(\Phi(x_k) - \Phi^*)}.
\]
Since $\mathcal{L}_k$ is a non-negative quasi-supermartingale (up to the summable noise $\eta_k^2 \delta^2 + C_2 \eta_k^3$), application of the Robbins–Siegmund theorem guarantees that the sequence $\{x_k\}$ remains within the compact sub-level set $\Omega$ almost surely, and the functional sequence $\mathcal{L}_k$ converges to a finite random variable. Recall that the total kinetic energy of the optimizer is bounded, i.e., $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbb{E}[\|x_{k+1} - x_k\|^2] < \infty$, which strictly dictates that $\lim_{k \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[\|x_{k+1} - x_k\|^2] = 0$. Because the distance function $\operatorname{dist}(\cdot, \mathcal{U}_\delta)$ is $1$-Lipschitz continuous, the step-to-step distance variation satisfies
\[
\left| \operatorname{dist}(x_{k+1}, \mathcal{U}_\delta) - \operatorname{dist}(x_k, \mathcal{U}_\delta) \right| \le \|x_{k+1} - x_k\|.
\]
Taking expectations reveals that the variation between consecutive distance profiles vanishes asymptotically ($\lim_{k \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[| \operatorname{dist}(x_{k+1}, \mathcal{U}_\delta) - \operatorname{dist}(x_k, \mathcal{U}_\delta) |] = 0$). Combining this vanishing variation property with our core subsequence tracking condition, $\liminf_{k \to \infty} \mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{dist}(0, \partial \Phi(x_k))^2\right] \le C_\delta^2 \delta^2$, 
\[
\lim_{k \to \infty} \mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{dist}(x_k, \mathcal{U}_\delta)\right] = 0.
\]
