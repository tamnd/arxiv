\label{theo: self duality}
    The pairing $\left\langle{\cdot}, {\cdot}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma}$ can be extended to a bounded bilinear form on $\operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_\times\left({\operatorname{div}_\Gamma, \Gamma}\right)$. Moreover, with respect to this pairing, the space $\operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_\times\left({\operatorname{div}_\Gamma, \Gamma}\right)$ is self-dual.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{theo: integration by parts}
    The Dirichlet trace operator $\gamma_D^{\text{s}}$ can be extended to a bounded linear and surjective operator from $\operatorname{\mathbf H}\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)$ into $\operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_\times\left({\operatorname{div}_\Gamma, \Gamma}\right)$. Moreover, it admits bounded linear right inverses. The following integration by parts formula holds:
    $$
    \int_{\Omega_{\text{s}}} \textbf{\textup{curl}}\, \boldsymbol{u} \cdot\boldsymbol{v} - \boldsymbol{u}\cdot\textbf{\textup{curl}}\, \boldsymbol{v} \,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x} = -\left\langle{\gamma_D^{\text{s}}\, \boldsymbol{u}}, {\gamma_D^{\text{s}}\, \boldsymbol{v}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma}\qquad\quad \forall \boldsymbol{u}, \boldsymbol{v} \in \operatorname{\mathbf H}\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right).
    $$
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{assum: resonant frequency}
    The angular frequency $\omega$ is assumed to lie outside the resonance sets of the source-free constant-coefficient and variable-coefficient Maxwell problems in $\Omega_{\text{s}}$. More precisely, we assume that:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), ref=\roman*]
        \item\label{assum: resonant frequency:const}
        The problem $\textbf{\textup{curl}}\, \mu_0^{-1}\textbf{\textup{curl}}\, \boldsymbol{u} - \omega^2 \varepsilon_0\, \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}$ in $\Omega_{\text{s}}$ subject to $\gamma_D^{\text{s}} \, \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}$ on $\Gamma$ admits only a trivial solution.
        \item\label{assum: resonant frequency:var}
        The problem $\textbf{\textup{curl}}\, \mu_{\text{s}}^{-1}\textbf{\textup{curl}}\, \boldsymbol{u} - \omega^2 \varepsilon_{\text{s}}\, \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}$ in $\Omega_{\text{s}}$ subject to $\gamma_D^{\text{s}} \, \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}$ on $\Gamma$ admits only a trivial solution.
    \end{enumerate}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem: well-defined of trace lifting operator}
    Assume that \Cref{assum: resonant frequency}(\ref{assum: resonant frequency:var}) holds. For each $\boldsymbol{\zeta} \in \operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)$, Problem \eqref{eq: trace lifting operator} admits a unique solution. Moreover, $\mathcal{E}$ defines a bounded linear operator.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Using a fixed bounded right inverse $\mathcal{R}$ of $\gamma_D^{\text{s}}$, we write $\mathcal{E}\, \boldsymbol{\zeta} = \mathcal{R}\, \boldsymbol{\zeta} + \operatorname{\mathbf U}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}$ with $\operatorname{\mathbf U}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}} \in \operatorname{\mathbf H}_0\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)$. Then Problem \eqref{eq: trace lifting operator} is equivalent to 
    $$
    a\left({\operatorname{\mathbf U}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}, \boldsymbol{v}}\right) = \ell_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\left({\boldsymbol{v}}\right) \qquad\quad \forall \boldsymbol{v} \in \operatorname{\mathbf H}_0\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right),
    $$
    where $\ell_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\left({\boldsymbol{v}}\right) := - a\left({\mathcal{R}\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}, \boldsymbol{v}}\right)$. Under \Cref{assum: resonant frequency}(\ref{assum: resonant frequency:var}), this problem admits a unique solution $\operatorname{\mathbf U}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}} \in \operatorname{\mathbf H}_0\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)$ that satisfies
    $$
    \left\lVert{\operatorname{\mathbf U}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}_0\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)}\le C \left\lVert{\ell_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}_0\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)^\prime},
    $$
    see \cite[Thm.~5.2]{Hiptmair2002}. By the continuity of $a$ and the boundedness of $\mathcal{R}$, we have 
    $$
    \left\lVert{\ell_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}_0\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)^\prime}\le C \left\lVert{\mathcal{R}\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)} \le C \left\lVert{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)},
    $$
    and hence $\left\lVert{\mathcal{E}\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)}\le C \left\lVert{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)}$ for all $\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in \operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)$. The proof is complete.
<!-- end proof-1 -->

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf H}\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)\times \operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)$ be the solution of Problem \eqref{eq: old formulation}. By testing \eqref{eq: old formulation} with $\operatorname{\mathbf W} \in \operatorname{\mathbf H}_0\left(\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}\right)$, we obtain
    $$
    a\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}, \operatorname{\mathbf W}}\right) = \boldsymbol{0} \qquad\quad \forall \operatorname{\mathbf W} \in \operatorname{\mathbf H}_0\left(\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}\right),
    $$
    which means that $\operatorname{\mathbf E} = \mathcal{E}\, \boldsymbol{\tau}$, where $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \gamma_D^{\text{s}} \operatorname{\mathbf E}$. Now, fix $\operatorname{\mathbf W}\in \operatorname{\mathbf H}\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)$, and denote $\boldsymbol{\xi} = \gamma_D^{\text{s}} \operatorname{\mathbf W}$. If $\mathcal{R}$ is an arbitrary right inverse of $\gamma_D^{\text{s}}$, then $\operatorname{\mathbf W}-\mathcal{R}\, \boldsymbol{\xi} \in \operatorname{\mathbf H}_0\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)$. It thus follows from \eqref{eq: trace lifting operator} and \eqref{eq: Steklov--Poincare operator} that $a\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}, \operatorname{\mathbf W}}\right) = a\left({\mathcal{E}\, \boldsymbol{\tau}, \mathcal{R}\, \boldsymbol{\xi}}\right) = \left\langle{\mathcal{S}\, \boldsymbol{\tau}}, {\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma}$. Substituting this into \eqref{eq: old formulation}, we obtain
    $$
    \left\{\begin{alignedat}{3}
         &\left\langle{\left({\mathcal{S} + \omega^2\varepsilon_0 \operatorname{V}_\kappa}\right)\boldsymbol{\tau}}, {\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &+&\  \left\langle{\left({-\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id} + \operatorname{K}_\kappa}\right)\boldsymbol{\uplambda}}, {\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &=&\  \ell_{1,\Gamma}\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right), \\
         &\left\langle{\left({\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id} + \operatorname{K}_\kappa}\right)\boldsymbol{\tau}}, {\overline{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &+&\ \mu_0\left\langle{\operatorname{V}_\kappa \boldsymbol{\uplambda}}, {\overline{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &=&\ \ell_2\left({\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right),
    \end{alignedat}\right.
    $$
    for all $\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in \operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)$. By varying $\operatorname{\mathbf W}$ in $\operatorname{\mathbf H}\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)$ and invoking the surjectivity of $\gamma_D^{\text{s}}$, we recover Problem \eqref{eq: new formulation}. Therefore, Problem \eqref{eq: new formulation} admits at least one solution $\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}}\right) = \left({\gamma_D^{\text{s}} \operatorname{\mathbf E}, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}}\right)$.

    Conversely, let $\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)\times \operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)$ be a solution of Problem \eqref{eq: new formulation}. From $\boldsymbol{\tau} \in \operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)$, one can uniquely determine $\mathcal{E}\, \boldsymbol{\tau} \in \operatorname{\mathbf H}\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)$ using \eqref{eq: trace lifting operator}. Consider a fixed $\boldsymbol{\xi} \in \operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)$. By substituting \eqref{eq: Steklov--Poincare operator} into \eqref{eq: new formulation}, we deduce that
    $$
    \left\{\begin{alignedat}{3}
        &a\left({\mathcal{E}\, \boldsymbol{\tau}, \mathcal{R}\, \boldsymbol{\xi}}\right) + \omega^2\varepsilon_0\left\langle{\operatorname{V}_{\kappa}\left({\gamma_D^{\text{s}}\, \mathcal{E}\, \boldsymbol{\tau}}\right)}, {\gamma_D^{\text{s}}\, \overline{\mathcal{R}\, \boldsymbol{\xi}}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &+&\  \left\langle{\left({-\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id} + \operatorname{K}_\kappa}\right)\boldsymbol{\uplambda}}, {\gamma_D^{\text{s}}\, \overline{\mathcal{R}\, \boldsymbol{\xi}}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &=&\  \ell_{1,\Gamma}\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right), \\
        &\left\langle{\left({\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id} + \operatorname{K}_\kappa}\right)\left({\gamma_D^{\text{s}}\, \mathcal{E}\, \boldsymbol{\tau}}\right)}, {\overline{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &+&\ \mu_0\left\langle{\operatorname{V}_\kappa \boldsymbol{\uplambda}}, {\overline{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &=&\ \ell_2\left({\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right),
    \end{alignedat}\right.
    $$
    for all $\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in \operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)$. Letting $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ vary in $\operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)$, we obtain a coupling formulation with the test space $\mathcal{R}\left({\operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_\times\left({\operatorname{div}_\Gamma, \Gamma}\right)}\right) \times \operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)$. Combining this with \eqref{eq: trace lifting operator}, and using \eqref{eq: direct sum}, we recover Problem \eqref{eq: old formulation} with $\operatorname{\mathbf E} = \mathcal{E}\, \boldsymbol{\tau}$ and $\operatorname{\mathbf W} = \mathcal{R}\, \boldsymbol{\xi} + \boldsymbol{v}$, where $\boldsymbol{v}\in \operatorname{\mathbf H}_0\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem: difference with Dobbelaere}
    Problem \eqref{eq: new formulation} has a form similar to that of \cite[Eq. (41)]{DZH+2015}, and both are posed on $\operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)\times \operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)$. However, the two formulations are conceptually different, as the corresponding unknowns have different meanings. Indeed, by \Cref{theo: continuous problems equivalence} and \cite[Lem.~6.1]{Hiptmair2003}, Problem \eqref{eq: new formulation} may be formally viewed as determining $\left({\gamma_D^{\text{s}} \operatorname{\mathbf E}, \gamma_N^0 \operatorname{\mathbf E}}\right)$. In contrast, \cite[Eq. (41)]{DZH+2015} finds $\left({-\gamma_D^{\text{s}} \operatorname{\mathbf E}, \mathbf{m}_0}\right)$, where $\mathbf{m}_0$ is not the Neumann trace itself, but an auxiliary variable entering the integral representation of $\operatorname{\mathbf E}_{\mid \, \Omega_0}$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem: properties of E_h and S_h}
    Assume that \Cref{assum: resonant frequency}(\ref{assum: resonant frequency:var}) is fulfilled. Then there exists $h_{\#}>0$ such that for all $h\in \left({0, h_{\#}}\right)$, the following results hold:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), ref=\roman*]
        \item Problem \eqref{eq: discrete trace lifting operator} is well-posed. Moreover, the operator $\mathcal{E}_h$ is uniformly bounded, i.e.,
        $$
        \left\lVert{\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}_h}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)}\le C \left\lVert{\boldsymbol{\zeta}_h}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)} \qquad\quad \forall \boldsymbol{\zeta}_h\in \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h.
        $$
        \item The definition \eqref{eq: discrete Steklov--Poincare operator} is independent of the choice of $\mathcal{R}_h$.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Take a uniformly bounded right inverse $\mathcal{R}_h$ of $\gamma_D^{\text{s}}$. By writing $\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}_h = \mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}_h + \operatorname{\mathbf U}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}, h}$ with $\operatorname{\mathbf U}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}, h}\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_{h,0}$, Problem \eqref{eq: discrete trace lifting operator} is equivalent to 
    $$
    a\left({\operatorname{\mathbf U}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}, h}, \boldsymbol{v}_h}\right) = \ell_{\boldsymbol{\zeta},h}\left({\boldsymbol{v}_h}\right) \qquad\quad \forall \boldsymbol{v}_h \in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_{h,0},
    $$
    where $\ell_{\boldsymbol{\zeta},h}\left({\boldsymbol{v}_h}\right) = -a\left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}_h, \boldsymbol{v}_h}\right)$. Under \Cref{assum: resonant frequency}(\ref{assum: resonant frequency:var}), this problem is well-posed \cite[Thm.~5.7]{Hiptmair2002}. This, together with the continuity of $a$ and the uniform boundedness of $\mathcal{R}_h$, implies that
    $$
    \left\lVert{\operatorname{\mathbf U}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}, h}}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)}\le C\left\lVert{\ell_{\boldsymbol{\zeta},h}}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf X}_{h,0}^\prime} \le C \left\lVert{\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}_h}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}\left({\textbf{\textup{curl}}, \Omega_{\text{s}}}\right)} \le C \left\lVert{\boldsymbol{\zeta}_h}\right\rVert_{\operatorname{\mathbf H}^{-1/2}_{\times}\left({\operatorname{div}_{\Gamma}, \Gamma}\right)},
    $$
    and consequently, $\mathcal{E}_h$ is uniformly bounded.

    The second part does not require $\mathcal{R}_h$ to be uniformly bounded. Let $\mathcal{R}_{1,h}$ and $\mathcal{R}_{2,h}$ denote two different discrete right inverses of $\gamma_D^{\text{s}}$. Since $\mathcal{R}_{1,h}\, \boldsymbol{\eta}_h - \mathcal{R}_{2,h}\, \boldsymbol{\eta}_h \in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_{h,0}$ for all $\boldsymbol{\eta}_h\in \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h$, \eqref{eq: discrete trace lifting operator} gives us $a\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}_h, \mathcal{R}_{1,h}\, \boldsymbol{\eta}_h - \mathcal{R}_{2,h}\, \boldsymbol{\eta}_h}\right) = 0$. Therefore, $a\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}_h, \mathcal{R}_{1,h}\, \boldsymbol{\eta}_h}\right) = a\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}_h, \mathcal{R}_{2,h}\, \boldsymbol{\eta}_h}\right)$, meaning that the definition of $\mathcal{S}_h$ does not depend on the choice of $\mathcal{R}_h$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{theo: discrete problems equivalence}
    Assume that \Cref{assum: resonant frequency} holds. Then there exists $h_{\#}>0$ such that for all $h\in \left({0, h_{\#}}\right)$, Problems \eqref{eq: discrete old formulation} and \eqref{eq: discrete new formulation} are equivalent.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Choose $h_{\#}>0$ sufficiently small so that Problems \eqref{eq: discrete old formulation} and \eqref{eq: discrete trace lifting operator} are well-posed for all $h\in \left({0, h_{\#}}\right)$. Denote by $\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h$ the solution of Problem \eqref{eq: discrete old formulation}, and let $\boldsymbol{\tau}_h = \gamma_D^{\text{s}} \operatorname{\mathbf E}_h$. Taking $\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h \in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_{h,0}$ in \eqref{eq: discrete old formulation} and using \eqref{eq: discrete trace lifting operator}, we obtain $\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h = \mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h$, and hence $\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h^{\mathcal{E}}$. Therefore, the trial space of Problem \eqref{eq: discrete old formulation} may be restricted to $\operatorname{\mathbf X}_h^{\mathcal{E}}\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h$. Next, consider an arbitrary discrete right inverse $\mathcal{R}_h$ of $\gamma_D^{\text{s}}$. Let $\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h$ be arbitrary, and denote $\boldsymbol{\xi}_h = \gamma_D^{\text{s}} \operatorname{\mathbf W}_h$.  Then $\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h$ admits the decomposition $\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h = \mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h + \left({\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h - \mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h}\right)$, where $\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h \in \mathcal{R}_h\left({\operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h}\right)$ and $ \operatorname{\mathbf W}_h - \mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h \in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_{h,0}$. Together with \eqref{eq: discrete trace lifting operator}, we see that it is sufficient to test Problem \eqref{eq: discrete old formulation} with pairs in $\mathcal{R}_h \left({\operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h}\right) \times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h$. Hence, Problem \eqref{eq: discrete old formulation} is equivalent to the following reduced formulation: Find $\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h^{\mathcal{E}}\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h$ such that
    $$
    \left\{\begin{alignedat}{3}
        &a\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h}\right) + \omega^2\varepsilon_0\left\langle{\operatorname{V}_{\kappa}\left({\gamma_D^{\text{s}}\, \mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h}\right)}, {\gamma_D^{\text{s}}\,  \overline{\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &+&\  \left\langle{\left({-\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id} + \operatorname{K}_\kappa}\right)\boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}, {\gamma_D^{\text{s}}\, \overline{\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &=&\  \ell_{1,\Gamma}\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h}\right), \\
        &\left\langle{\left({\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id} + \operatorname{K}_\kappa}\right)\left({\gamma_D^{\text{s}}\, \mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h}\right)}, {\overline{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &+&\ \mu_0\left\langle{\operatorname{V}_\kappa \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}, {\overline{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}}\right\rangle_{\times, \Gamma} \ &=&\ \ell_2\left({\boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right),
    \end{alignedat}\right.
    $$
    for all $\left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)\in \mathcal{R}_h\left({\operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h}\right)\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h$. Using \eqref{eq: discrete Steklov--Poincare operator}, this becomes Problem \eqref{eq: discrete new formulation}. Conversely, let $\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h$ be the solution of Problem \eqref{eq: discrete new formulation}. Then by \eqref{eq: discrete trace lifting operator} and \eqref{eq: discrete Steklov--Poincare operator}, the pair $\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)$ satisfies the reduced formulation above, and therefore also Problem \eqref{eq: discrete old formulation}.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{theo: discrete inf-sup condition}
    Under \Cref{assum: resonant frequency}, there exists a constant $C>0$ such that
    $$
    \sup_{\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right) \in \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus \left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}} \dfrac{\left\vert{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathrm{R}}\left({\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right)}\right\vert}{\left\lVert{\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Gamma, \Gamma}} \ge C\ \left\lVert{\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Gamma, \Gamma} \qquad\quad \forall \left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right) \in \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h,
    $$
    for all $h\in \left({0, h_{\#}}\right)$, where $h_{\#}>0$ is sufficiently small.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By \eqref{eq: discrete old inf-sup} and the inclusion $\operatorname{\mathbf X}_h^{\mathcal{E}}\subset\operatorname{\mathbf X}_h$, there exists $h_{\#}>0$ such that for all $h\in \left({0, h_{\#}}\right)$, the following inequality holds:
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq: discrete inf-sup condition 1}
        C \le \inf_{\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h^{\mathcal{E}}\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus\left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}} \sup_{\left({\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus\left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}} \dfrac{\left\vert{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{C}}\left({\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right)}\right\vert}{\left\lVert{\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma} \left\lVert{\left({\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma}}.
    \end{equation}
    From the proof of \Cref{theo: discrete problems equivalence}, every $\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h \in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h^{\mathcal{E}}$ can be written as $\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h =\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h$, where $\boldsymbol{\tau}_h = \gamma_D^{\text{s}} \operatorname{\mathbf E}_h$. Consider a uniformly bounded discrete right inverse $\mathcal{R}_h$ of $\gamma_D^{\text{s}}$. Then for any $\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h$, if $\boldsymbol{\xi}_h = \gamma_D^{\text{s}}\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h$, then $\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h - \mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_{h,0}$. By using \eqref{eq: discrete trace lifting operator}, we hence obtain
    $$
    a\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h, \operatorname{\mathbf W}_h - \mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h}\right) = a\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \operatorname{\mathbf W}_h - \mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h}\right) = 0,
    $$
    which leads to
    $$
    \boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{C}}\left({\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right)=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{C}}\left({\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right).
    $$
    By using the uniform boundedness of $\mathcal{R}_h$ and the continuity of $\gamma_D^{\text{s}}$, we have
    $$
    \left\lVert{\left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma} \le C \left\lVert{\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Gamma, \Gamma} \le C\left\lVert{\left({\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma} \qquad\quad \forall \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h\in \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h.
    $$
    It follows that
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq: discrete inf-sup condition 2}
        \sup_{\left({\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus\left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}} \dfrac{\left\vert{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{C}}\left({\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right)}\right\vert}{\left\lVert{\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma} \left\lVert{\left({\operatorname{\mathbf W}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma}} \le C \sup_{\left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)\in \mathcal{R}_h\left({\operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h}\right)\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus\left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}} \dfrac{\left\vert{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{C}}\left({\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right)}\right\vert}{\left\lVert{\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma} \left\lVert{\left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma}},
    \end{equation}
    for all $\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h^{\mathcal{E}}\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus \left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}$. From the proof of \Cref{theo: discrete problems equivalence}, we have
    $$
    \boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{C}}\left({\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right) = \boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{R}}\left({\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right) \qquad\quad \forall \left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right) \in \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h.
    $$
    Moreover, the maps $\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right) \mapsto \left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)$ and $\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right) \mapsto \left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)$ from $\operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h$ into $\operatorname{\mathbf X}_h^{\mathcal{E}}\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h$ and $\mathcal{R}_h\left({\operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h}\right)\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h$, respectively, are bijective. Together with the continuity of $\gamma_D^{\text{s}}$, we finally obtain
    \begin{align*}
        &\inf_{\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h^{\mathcal{E}}\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus\left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}} \sup_{\left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)\in \mathcal{R}_h\left({\operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h}\right)\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus\left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}} \dfrac{\left\vert{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{C}}\left({\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right)}\right\vert}{\left\lVert{\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma} \left\lVert{\left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma}} \\
        &\qquad = \inf_{\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus\left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}} \sup_{\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus\left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}} \dfrac{\left\vert{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{R}}\left({\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right)}\right\vert}{\left\lVert{\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma} \left\lVert{\left({\mathcal{R}_h\, \boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{\text{s}}, \Gamma}} \\
        &\qquad \le C \inf_{\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus\left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}} \sup_{\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus\left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}} \dfrac{\left\vert{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{R}}\left({\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right), \left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right)}\right\vert}{\left\lVert{\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Gamma, \Gamma} \left\lVert{\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}_h, \boldsymbol{\varphi}_h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Gamma, \Gamma}}.
    \end{align*}
    By combining \eqref{eq: discrete inf-sup condition 1}, the inequality obtained by taking the infimum over $\left({\operatorname{\mathbf E}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)\in \operatorname{\mathbf X}_h^{\mathcal{E}}\times \operatorname{\mathbf Y}_h \setminus \left\{\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{0}\right\}$ in \eqref{eq: discrete inf-sup condition 2}, and the last estimate, we finish the proof.

\label{rem: other preconditioners}
    As discussed above, the particular choice of $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}$ and $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}$ in \eqref{eq: sesquilinear forms for preconditioners} is motivated by \cite{DZH+2015, BC2007}. <!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem: advantage of the method}
    The proof of \Cref{theo: discrete problems equivalence} shows that if $\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)$ solves Problem \eqref{eq: discrete new formulation}, then $\left({\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h, \boldsymbol{\uplambda}_h}\right)$ solves Problem \eqref{eq: discrete old formulation}. At the algebraic level, this corresponds to first solving \eqref{eq: matrix equation}, and then recovering $\mathcal{E}_h\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_h$ from \eqref{eq: coordinate representation}--\eqref{eq: matrix trace lifting equation}. The main computational burden lies in \eqref{eq: matrix equation}, since it contains dense BEM blocks, whereas \eqref{eq: matrix trace lifting equation} is a sparse FEM system. Available FEM solvers, including direct solvers \cite{DD1997, ADLK2001} and preconditioned iterative methods \cite{Hiptmair1999, GP2003, QDA+2026}, can be used both to apply the Schur complement $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{sc}}$ and to solve \eqref{eq: matrix trace lifting equation}. Therefore, preconditioning of \eqref{eq: matrix equation} provides a practical strategy to solve the original problem.
