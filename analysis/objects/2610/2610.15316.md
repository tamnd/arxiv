\label{thm:main-non-vanishing}
Let $\ell\in\{3,4,6\}$, and fix an odd prime power
$q\equiv-1\pmod\ell$. For the family \eqref{intro:family},
\begin{equation}\label{intro:non-vanishing}
 \liminf_{\substack{g\to\infty\\\ell-1\mid g}}
 \frac{\#\{\chi\in\mathcal C_\ell(g):L_q(1/2,\chi)\ne0\}}
      {|\mathcal C_\ell(g)|}
 \ge\frac{A_{\ell,q,G}}{2 e^{e^{65}+3}}
 \ge\frac{1}{2 e^{e^{65}+3}}
 > e^{-e^{66}}.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:family}
The parameterization \eqref{eq:family} is one-to-one onto the primitive
order-four characters satisfying \eqref{eq:conductor-condition} and
having conductor degree $2m$.  For these characters,
\[
 \operatorname{cond}(\chi_F)=FF^\sigma,\qquad
 \chi_{F^\sigma}=\overline{\chi_F}.
\]

$g=3m-3$, and the completed $L$-polynomial has degree $2m-2$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
A base-field prime admits a local character of exact order four only
when its degree is even.  It then splits as $P=\pi\pi^\sigma$ over
$\mathbb F_{q^2}$, and the two choices $\pi$ and $\pi^\sigma$ give the two
local characters of exact order four.  Condition
\eqref{eq:conductor-condition} excludes local characters of order two
at ramified primes.  The Chinese remainder theorem now gives the claimed bijection; see
\cite[Lemma~3.6]{Lin}.

of $\mathbb F_{q^2}[T]$ is one, since $q-1$ divides
$(q^{2d}-1)/4$.  Thus these characters are even.  Each finite ramified
prime has ramification index four and infinity is unramified.
Riemann--Hurwitz gives $2g-2=-8+3(2m)$, hence $g=3m-3$.

degree minus two.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:count}
For every $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
 |\mathcal C_4(g)|=c_q q^{2m}+O_{q,\varepsilon}(q^{(1+\varepsilon)m}),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cq}
 c_q=
 \prod_{\deg P\ \text{odd}}(1-|P|^{-2})
 \prod_{\deg P\ \text{even}}
 (1+2|P|^{-1})(1-|P|^{-1})^2>0.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cq-lower}
 c_q\ge\zeta_q(2)^{-3}=(1-q^{-1})^3.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Write $\mu_q$ and $\mu_Q$ for the M\"obius functions over the two
fields. As in \cite[proof of Corollary~1.1]{Hong}, insert
\[
 1_{(F,F^\sigma)=1}=\sum_{\substack{D\in\mathcal A_q\\D\mid F}}\mu_q(D),
 \qquad \mu_Q^2(F)=\sum_{A^2\mid F}\mu_Q(A).
\]
For $|u|<q^{-2}$, the counting series, including $F=1$, is therefore
\begin{align}\label{eq:B}
 \sum_{\substack{F\in\mathcal A_{q^2}\text{ square-free}\\(F,F^\sigma)=1}} u^{\deg F}
 &=\sum_{D\in\mathcal A_q}\mu_q(D)u^{\deg D}
   \sum_{\substack{A,G\in\mathcal A_{q^2}\\(AG,D)=1}}
       \mu_Q(A)u^{2\deg A+\deg G}\notag\\
 &=\frac{\zeta_Q(u)}{\zeta_Q(u^2)}
   \prod_P\left(1-u^{\deg P}
       \prod_{\pi\mid P}(1+u^{\deg\pi})^{-1}\right)\notag\\
 &=\zeta_Q(u)
   \prod_{\deg P\text{ odd}}(1-u^{\deg P})
   \prod_{\deg P\text{ even}}
       (1+2u^{\deg P/2})(1-u^{\deg P/2})^2.
\end{align}
Here and below, $\pi$ denotes a monic prime over $\mathbb F_Q$.
The product multiplying $\zeta_Q(u)$ converges normally for
$|u|<q^{-1}$, since each local factor is $1+O(|u|^{\deg P})$.
Its value at $u=q^{-2}$ is $c_q$. Perron's formula and a shift to
$|u|=q^{-1-\varepsilon}$, with $0<\varepsilon<1$, give
\eqref{eq:count}. Finally, at each even-degree prime,
\[
 (1+2|P|^{-1})(1-|P|^{-1})^2\ge(1-|P|^{-2})^3;
\]
the same lower bound holds for the odd-degree factor. Multiplication
over $P$ gives \eqref{eq:cq-lower}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:orthog}
Let $A,B\in\mathcal A_q$ be monic and suppose
$\deg\operatorname{rad}(AB)\le m$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orthog}
 q^{-2m}\sum_{\substack{R\in\mathcal A_{q^2}\\\deg R=m}}
 \chi_R(A)\overline{\chi_R(B)}
 =\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{\phi_Q(\operatorname{rad}(AB))}{|\operatorname{rad}(AB)|_2},
       &AB^3\text{ is a fourth power},\\[2mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The radical and Euler function on the right may be evaluated after
factorization over $\mathbb F_{q^2}$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By quartic reciprocity, the summand is the character
$\left(\frac{R}{AB^3}\right)_4$.  It is induced modulo $\operatorname{rad}(AB)$, and the degree
condition makes monic degree-$m$ polynomials equidistributed over its
residue classes.  The induced character is principal exactly when
all the exponents in $AB^3$ are divisible by four.  Its principal
average is the proportion of invertible classes; otherwise its
average is zero.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:model-bounds}
If $Z=\sum_Pc_PX_P$ and $V=\sum_P|c_P|^2$, then, for $t\ge0$ and

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:restricted-gauss}
Let $5/4<\alpha<3/2$ and $|v|=Q^{-\alpha}$. On closed
subintervals of this range, for every $\epsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:needed-gauss}
 \widetilde\Psi_Q(r,v;h)\ll_{q,\epsilon,\alpha}
 |rh|_2^\epsilon |r|_2^{3/4-\alpha/2}.
\end{equation}
The implied constant is uniform in $r,h$ and in the argument of $v$.
The series has a holomorphic continuation throughout
$|v|<Q^{-5/4}$.

but not $r$. In the notation of \cite[Section~5]{DFL25}, set
\[
 \psi_r^{(i)}(r,E,v)=\frac1{1-Q^4v^4}
 \sum_{\substack{F\in\mathcal A_{q^2}\\(F,r)=1,\ E\mid F\\
                    \deg F\equiv i\ (4)}}G_Q(r,F)v^{\deg F}.
\]
Finite inclusion--exclusion gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restricted-inclusion}
 \widetilde\Psi_Q(r,v;h)=(1-Q^4v^4)
 \sum_{E\mid a}\mu_Q(E)\sum_{i=0}^3\psi_r^{(i)}(r,E,v).
\end{equation}
Theorem~5.3 and Corollary~5.4 of \cite{DFL25}, with base field $Q$
and order four, apply since $E$ is square-free and $(E,r)=1$.
More explicitly, write
$r=r_1r_2^2r_3^3r_4^4$, with $r_1,r_2,r_3$ square-free and pairwise
coprime, as required by the cited theorem. Its second case applies

$4\le6/(3-2\alpha)$. The exponent of $|E|_2$ becomes
$\ell(3/4-\alpha/2)-1=2-2\alpha$, while the exponent of $|r|_2$

\[
 \psi_r^{(i)}(r,E,v)
 \ll |Er|_2^\epsilon |E|_2^{2-2\alpha}
                   |r|_2^{3/4-\alpha/2}.
\]
The divisor sum is bounded by
\[
 |r|_2^\epsilon\sum_{E\mid a}|E|_2^{2-2\alpha+\epsilon}
 =|r|_2^\epsilon\prod_{\pi\mid a}
                (1+|\pi|_2^{2-2\alpha+\epsilon}).
\]
Choose the preliminary $\epsilon$ smaller than $2\alpha-2$ on
the chosen closed interval. Each factor is then at most two, and
$2^{\omega(a)}\ll_{Q,\epsilon'}|a|_2^{\epsilon'}$ for every
$\epsilon'>0$: separate the finitely many primes of bounded degree
and use $2\le|\pi|_2^{\epsilon'}$ for the others.

sign is one because
$Q\equiv1\pmod8$; their form is $1-Q^{3\deg\pi}v^{4\deg\pi}$.
The global factor has zeros at $|v|=Q^{-5/4}$ and the local factors
at $|v|=Q^{-3/4}$. The decomposition and \eqref{eq:restricted-inclusion} therefore give
holomorphy for $|v|<Q^{-5/4}$, with uniform bounds on compact
subannuli.

\label{lem:GZshort}
Uniformly in $0\le j\le J$, for sufficiently large $m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GZshort}
 \log|L_q(1/2,\chi)|\le
 \operatorname{Re}\sum_{r=0}^j\mathcal P_{r,N_j}(\chi)+\theta_j^{-1}+2.

The logarithmic-derivative argument of
\cite[Lemma~3.1]{DFL21}, before its prime-power terms are separated,
gives, with conductor degree $2m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-upper}
 \log|L_q(1/2,\chi)|\le
 \operatorname{Re}\sum_{\deg f\le N}
 \frac{\Lambda(f)\chi(f)}{\deg f\sqrt{|f|}}
 e^{-\deg f/N}(1-\deg f/N)+\frac{2m}{N}.
\end{equation}
The logarithmic-derivative argument uses the shift $1/(N\log q)$
and the fact that the reciprocal zeros of the completed $L$-polynomial
have modulus $q^{1/2}$; the zero contribution is then nonpositive. For the
even characters considered here, the factor $1-q^{-s}$ is retained.
The terms of exponent at least three have absolute sum at most
\[
 \sum_P\sum_{v\ge3}\frac1{v|P|^{v/2}}
 \le\frac{-\log(1-q^{-1/2})}{3(1-q^{-1/2})}<1.
\]

formula and the modulus $q^{1/2}$ of its reciprocal zeros imply
$|\sum_{\deg P=d}\chi(P)^2|\ll_q mq^{d/2}/d$.

$O_q(mq^{-B/2}/(B+1))$. For $d\le B$, replacing
$e^{-2d/N}(1-2d/N)$ by one costs $O(B/N)$, using
$\#\{P:\deg P=d\}\le q^d/d$. Hence, for $N\ge2B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GZsquare-shortening}
 \frac12\sum_{\deg P\le N/2}
 \frac{e^{-2\deg P/N}(1-2\deg P/N)\chi(P)^2}{|P|}
 =Q_B(\chi)+O_q\left(\frac BN+\frac{mq^{-B/2}}{B+1}\right).
\end{equation}
This error tends uniformly to zero for $N=N_j$.
Also $2m/N_j=\theta_j^{-1}+o(1)$ uniformly, since
$m\theta_0^2\to\infty$. Combining these facts proves the lemma.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{sextic:parameterization}
The map $F\mapsto\chi_F$ is a bijection onto the primitive order-six
characters of conductor degree $2m$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{sextic:conductor}
 \operatorname{cond}(\chi^2)=\operatorname{cond}(\chi^3)=\operatorname{cond}(\chi).
\end{equation}
These characters are even, their conductor is $FF^\sigma$, and their
completed $L$-polynomial has degree $2m-2$.  The associated cyclic
sextic extension has genus $g=5m-5$.
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
A base-field prime supports a local character of exact order six
precisely when its degree is even.  It then splits as
$P=\pi\pi^\sigma$ over $\mathbb F_Q$, and the two choices $\pi,\pi^\sigma$
give its two local characters of exact order six.  Condition
\eqref{sextic:conductor} excludes local order two and local order three
at ramified primes.  Independent local choices give the parameterization,
including $(F,F^\sigma)=1$; see \cite[Lemma~3.6]{Lin}.
For $a\in\mathbb F_q^\times$, its sextic symbol is one since $q-1$ divides
$(Q^{\deg\pi}-1)/6$.  Thus the characters are even and infinity is
unramified.  Riemann--Hurwitz gives
$2g-2=-12+5(2m)$, whereas the degree of each completed $L$-polynomial
is its conductor degree minus two.

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{sextic:restricted-gauss}

 D\le s_nN_n+L,\qquad
 3D\le\frac{3m}{4}+3L<0.750611m<m.
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{cubic:finite-average} applies after the indicator is
bounded by $\sum_{u=n}^J|\mathcal P^{(3)}_{n,N_u}/K_n|^{2s_n}$.

For the preceding blocks, the common prime-square term cancels,
and the total variance of the difference of prime weights is at
most four. The argument for \eqref{eq:GZmatched} bounds their
product of expectations by $e^3$. In block $n$, the two prime sums
have variance at most two, and the moment and exponential bounds
give
\[
 \mathbb E\left\{|Z|^{2s_n}|E_{\ell_n}(-W/2)|^2\right\}
 \le4e^4(16s_n)^{s_n}.
\]
Each subsequent block contributes at most $4e^2<e^4$. Consequently,
\begin{align}\label{cubic:single-block}
 q^{-2m}\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal B_n^{(3)}}
        |L_q(1/2,\chi)M_{3,G}(\chi)|
 \le{}&e^{20}(J-n+1)e^{\theta_{n-1}^{-1}+4(J-n)}
          \left(\frac{16s_n}{K_n^2}\right)^{s_n}.
\end{align}
This is the same bound as \eqref{eq:GZbadblock}.
Writing $t=J-n$, the inequalities
$\theta_n=\theta_Je^{-t}$ and $s_n\ge1/(9\theta_n)$ give
\[
 \theta_{n-1}^{-1}+4t+s_n\log\frac{16s_n}{K_n^2}
 \le\frac{e+(\log2)/9-(64+t)/18}{\theta_n}+4t
 \le-\frac{0.7}{\theta_n}-t.
\]
Here $e+(\log2)/9-64/18<-0.7602$.
Summing \eqref{cubic:single-block} over $n\ge1$ yields the first
term in \eqref{cubic:exceptional-bound}.

For the initial block, take $s_0=\lceil K_0\rceil$.
Since $K_0=(\log m)^{3/2}$ and $3s_0N_0=o(m)$,
finite orthogonality and the bound $V_0\le3\log m$ imply
\begin{equation}\label{cubic:initial-count}
 q^{-2m}|\mathcal B_0^{(3)}|
 \le4(J+1)\left(\frac{12s_0\log m}{K_0^2}\right)^{s_0}
 \le e^{-c(\log m)^{3/2}\log\log m}
\end{equation}
for some absolute $c>0$.
The polynomial inside the modulus square representing $|M_{3,G}|^4$
has degree at most $2L$. Since $6L<m$, another application of
\eqref{cubic:finite-average} gives
\begin{equation}\label{cubic:mollifier-fourth}
 q^{-2m}\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal C_3(g)}|M_{3,G}(\chi)|^4
 \le4^{J+1}e^{48\log m+32J}\ll m^{50}.
\end{equation}
H\"older's inequality, \eqref{cubic:fourth}, and
\eqref{cubic:initial-count}--\eqref{cubic:mollifier-fourth} now give
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{\chi\in\mathcal B_0^{(3)}}|L_q(1/2,\chi)M_{3,G}(\chi)|
 &\le |\mathcal B_0^{(3)}|^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal C_3(g)}|L_q(1/2,\chi)|^4\right)^{1/4}
 \left(\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal C_3(g)}|M_{3,G}(\chi)|^4\right)^{1/4}\\
 &\ll_{q,A}q^{2m}m^{-A}.
\end{align*}
This proves \eqref{cubic:exceptional-bound}.

\label{cubic:positive}
For every fixed odd prime power $q\equiv2\pmod3$,
\begin{equation}\label{cubic:positive-bound}
 \liminf_{\substack{g\to\infty\\2\mid g}}
 \frac{\#\{\chi\in\mathcal C_3(g):L_q(1/2,\chi)\ne0\}}
      {|\mathcal C_3(g)|}
 \ge\frac{A_{3,q,G}}{2 e^{e^{65}+3}}
 \ge\frac1{2 e^{e^{65}+3}}
 > e^{-e^{66}}.
\end{equation}
