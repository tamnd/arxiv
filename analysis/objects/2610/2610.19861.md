<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop: relax max principle}
    Suppose $\boldsymbol{x}$ is a stationary point of $\bar F$ with $\bar F(\boldsymbol{x})>0$, and let $\mathbf w$ attain the minimum in \eqref{eq:relaxed-objective}.
     
    Suppose also that $k$ is ETP.
     
    Then $\Delta\leq0$, every distinct particle location is active, with positive total weight, and, for all $j=1,\dots,N$,
     
    \begin{equation*}
        \Delta'(x_j)=0,
        \qquad
        \Delta''(x_j)<0.
    \end{equation*}
     
    Further, all particles lie in the convex hull of $\operatorname{supp}(P^*)$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop: runstablecollisionpoints}
    Under the assumptions of Prop. \ref{prop: relax max principle}, a collision is unstable under the unweighted particle gradient flow.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Following ideas used for collision singularities in Prony systems \citep{batenkov2013geometrysingularitiespronymapping} and exponential least-squares approximation \citep{1f9723a1-a04e-3d21-9c1b-61527608e570}, a pair of colliding kernel functions can be replaced by a divided-difference basis.
 
This remains non-degenerate at the collision and converges to a kernel derivative.
 
Smoothness of an ETP kernel gives the required continuity of $\Delta''$; Prop.~\ref{prop: relax max principle} then implies that separating the particles is unstable.
<!-- end proof-1 -->

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm: relaxbound}
Suppose the kernel is ETP and the problem is well specified with $P^*$ supported on $K$ points.
 
If $N\geq K$, then $\bar F$ has no spurious local minima, and every spurious stationary point is unstable under the unweighted particle gradient flow.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
First consider a nonzero local minimum.
 
By Prop.~\ref{prop: posweightrelaxed}, all particle locations are distinct and every optimal weight is nonzero.
 
Hence stationarity forces a double zero of $\Delta$ at each of the $N$ particle locations.
 
Since $\Delta$ is a nonzero combination of at most $N+K$ distinct kernel sections, extended total positivity implies that it has at most $N+K-1$ zeros counting multiplicity.
 
Thus $2N\leq N+K-1$, which requires $N\leq K-1$.
 
Therefore, when $N\geq K$, every local minimum must satisfy $\bar F=0$.

Now consider a spurious stationary point.
 
If all particle locations are distinct, Prop.~\ref{prop: relax max principle} implies that every location is active, so the same zero-counting argument again gives a contradiction when $N\geq K$.
 
Hence every spurious stationary point must contain a collision, which is unstable by Prop.~\ref{prop: runstablecollisionpoints}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm: relaxepsbound}
Let $D>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$.
 
There exists a finite threshold $N_\varepsilon(D)$ such that, uniformly over all target distributions with compact support of diameter at most $D$, whenever $N>N_\varepsilon(D)$ every local minimum of $\bar F$ satisfies
 
\begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{MMD}(P,P^*)<\varepsilon.
\end{equation*}
 
Moreover, every stationary point with $\operatorname{MMD}(P,P^*)\geq\varepsilon$ is unstable under the unweighted particle gradient flow.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
This result is also proved via a zero counting argument.
 
First, the maximum principle (cf. Prop.~\ref{prop: relax max principle}) confines the active particles to the convex hull of the target support.
 
Meanwhile, compactness and analyticity give an upper bound on the number of distinct active locations that a stationary witness with $\operatorname{MMD}(P,P^*)\geq\varepsilon$ can support, depending only on $D$ and $\varepsilon$.
 
At a nonzero local minimum, all $N$ particle locations must be distinct (cf. Prop.~\ref{prop: posweightrelaxed}), yielding a contradiction once $N$ exceeds this bound.
 
Meanwhile, for a general stationary point, exceeding the bound forces a collision, which must be unstable (cf. Prop.~\ref{prop: runstablecollisionpoints}).

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop: unstablecollisionpoints}
Under the assumptions of Prop.~\ref{prop: simp max principle}, every collision is unstable under the unweighted particle gradient flow.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Separate two colliding particles by a small distance $r=x_j-x_i$.
 
Under the unweighted particle flow,
 
$
    \dot r
    =
    -\bigl(\Delta'(x_j)-\Delta'(x_i)\bigr)
    =
    -\Delta''(\vartheta)r
$
 
for some $\vartheta\in(x_i,x_j)$ by the mean value theorem.
 
Since $\Delta''<0$ in a neighbourhood of the collision, $\dot r$ has the same sign as $r$.
 
Thus the collision is unstable.

    Perturb the locations to
     
    $x_i - \frac{w_j}{w_i + w_j}\varepsilon$ and $x_j + \frac{w_i}{w_i + w_j}\varepsilon$.

<!-- proposition-9: body_md -->
\label{prop: MMDFourierSplit}
Let $P,P^*$ be probability measures on $\mathbb R^d$, let $\rho=P-P^*$, and suppose $k$ is continuous, translation invariant, and has positive spectral density.
 
Then, writing $\Delta=\mu_P-\mu_{P^*}$, for every $\nu>0$ it holds that
 
\begin{align*}
    \operatorname{MMD}^2(P^*,P)
    \leq&
    \frac{\|\Delta\|_1^2}{(2\pi)^d}
    \int_{|\omega|\leq\nu}\frac{1}{\hat k(\omega)}\,\mathrm d\omega\\
    &+
    \frac{\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^2}{(2\pi)^d}
    \int_{|\omega|>\nu}\hat k(\omega)\,\mathrm d\omega.
\end{align*}
 
In particular, for the one-dimensional Gaussian kernel with $2\sigma^2=1$,
 
\begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{MMD}^2(P,P^*)
    \leq
    \tfrac{\|\Delta\|_1^2}{\pi}\operatorname{erfi}\left(\tfrac{\nu}{2}\right)
    +
    4\operatorname{erfc}\left(\tfrac{\nu}{2}\right).
\end{equation*}
where $\operatorname{erfi}(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_0^z e^{t^2}\,\mathrm dt$, $\operatorname{erfc}(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_z^\infty e^{-t^2}\,\mathrm dt$ denote the imaginary error function and complementary error function, respectively.

<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{prop: Lcbound}
Suppose $P^*$ has compact support of diameter at most $D$, and $(\mathbf w,\boldsymbol{x})$ is a stationary point with $F(\mathbf w,\boldsymbol{x})>0$.
 
Then
 
\begin{align*}
    \|\Delta\|_1
    \leq&
    2(\sqrt{2}+D)c
    +16c\sqrt{-\log c}\\
    &+
    6\sqrt{\pi}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{-\log c})
    +
    4\sqrt{\pi}c\sqrt{1-2\log c}.
\end{align*}
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Split the real line into regions near the target support, intermediate regions, and Gaussian tails.
 
The maximum principle controls the witness directly on the central region; the tails are bounded using Gaussian decay.
 
The main difficulty is to remove any dependence on how far the extreme particles lie from the target.
 
We do this using the stationarity equations at the extreme particles to obtain probability-mass bounds, after which suitable choices of the splitting points yield the stated uniform estimate.

A vector space $\mathcal{H}$ is called a Hilbert space if it has an inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_\mathcal{H}$ and is complete.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
For a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ of real-valued functions on $\mathcal{X}$, a function $k: \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a reproducing kernel of $\mathcal{H}$ if
     
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $k(x, \cdot) \in \mathcal{H}$ for all $x \in \mathcal{X}$;
         
        \item $\langle f, k(x, \cdot) \rangle_\mathcal{H} = f(x)$ for all $f \in \mathcal{H}$ and all $x \in \mathcal{X}$.
         
    \end{enumerate}
     
    In this case, $\mathcal{H}$ is called a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS).

<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
A function $f(x,y)$ of two real variables, $x$ ranging over $X$ and $y$ ranging over $Y$, is said to be totally positive of order $k$ ($TP_k$) if for all $x_1 < \ldots < x_m, y_1 < \ldots < y_m (x_i \in X, y_j \in Y)$, and all $1 \le m \le k$,
     
    \begin{equation}
        f \begin{pmatrix} x_1, \ldots, x_m\\y_1, \ldots, y_m \end{pmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} f(x_1, y_1) & \ldots &f(x_1, y_m)\\ \vdots && \vdots\\ f(x_m, y_1) & \ldots & f(x_m, y_m) \end{vmatrix} \ge 0.
    \end{equation}
     
    Additionally, if all indicated derivatives exist, and $X, Y$ represent open intervals on the line, then for $1 \le n \le k$,
     
    \begin{equation}
        \label{TPnonstrict}
        \begin{vmatrix} f(x, y) & \ldots &\frac{\partial^{n-1}}{\partial y^{n-1}}f(x, y)\\ \vdots && \vdots\\ \frac{\partial^{n-1}}{\partial x^{n-1}}f(x, y) & \ldots & \frac{\partial^{2n-2}}{\partial x^{n-1} \partial y^{n-1}}f(x, y) \end{vmatrix} \ge 0.
    \end{equation}

<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{def: Variation deimishing}
     
    Let $V(g)$ denote the number of variations of sign of a function $g(x)$ as $x$ traverses the real line from left to right.
     
    Let $h(x)$ be given by the absolutely convergent integral
     
    \begin{equation}
        \label{convergenttpintegral}
        h(x) = \int f(x, w)g(w)d\sigma(w),
    \end{equation}
     
    where $f(x, w)$ is $TP_k$, $\sigma(w)$ is a non-negative $\sigma$-finite measure and $V(g) \le k-1$.
     
    Suppose also that $h(x)$ is $k$ times continuously differentiable, the interchange of integral and derivative is permissible in \eqref{convergenttpintegral}, and \eqref{TPnonstrict} holds strictly.
     
    Suppose $h \not\equiv 0$.
     
    Let $Z(h)$ denote the number of zeros of $h(x)$, counting multiplicities.
     
    Then $Z(h) \le V(g)$.

<!-- proposition-15: body_md -->
\label{prop: jenzerocount}
     
    Let $R > 0$.
     
    Let $\delta  > 1$.
     
    Let $\Omega$ be an open set that contains the closure of a disc $D_{\delta R}$ and suppose that $f$ is holomorphic in $\Omega$ with $f(0) \neq 0$.
     
    Let $\mathbf{n}_f(r)$ denote the number of zeros (counting multiplicity) of $f$ in the disc of radius $r$.
     
    Assume that $\vert f(x + iy) \vert \le A_fe^{B_fy^2}$.
     
    Then
     
    \begin{equation*}
        \mathbf{n}_{f} (R) \le \frac{\log\bigl(\frac{A_f}{\vert f(0) \vert}\bigr) + \frac{1}{2}B_f\delta^2R^2}{\log(\delta)}.
    \end{equation*}

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
By Jensen's formula \citep[e.g., pg. 137][]{stein2010complex}, we have that
     
    \begin{align*}
        {\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{0}^{2\pi} \log \vert f(\delta Re^{i\theta}) \vert d\theta - \log \vert f(0) \vert} &= \int_{0}^{\delta R} \frac{{\mathbf{n}_{f}}(r)}{r}dr \ge \int_{R}^{\delta R} \frac{{\mathbf{n}_{f}}(r)}{r} dr \ge  {\mathbf{n}_{f}} (R) \log(\delta).
    \end{align*}
     
    By the assumed bound, we have $\vert f(\delta Re^{i\theta})\vert \le A_fe^{B_f\delta^2 R^2\sin^2(\theta)}$.
     
    Thus, integrating and substituting into the previous bound, it follows that
     
    \begin{align*}
        \mathbf{n}_{f} (R) &\le \frac{\log\bigl(\frac{A_f}{\vert f(0) \vert}\bigr) + \frac{1}{2}B_f\delta^2R^2}{\log(\delta)}. \qedhere
    \end{align*}

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We begin by defining the reduced measure of $P$ by merging particles at the same location, and removing locations with zero total weight.
     
    Write $\vartheta_1,\ldots,\vartheta_A$ for its distinct locations and $\varsigma_1,\ldots,\varsigma_A$ for their nonzero total weights.
     
    By the stationarity conditions, we have
     
    \begin{equation*}
        \Delta(\vartheta_j)=0, \qquad \Delta'(\vartheta_j)=0, \qquad j=1,\ldots,A.
    \end{equation*}
     
    To apply the variation diminishing property, write
     
    \begin{equation*}
        \Delta(x)=\int k(t,x)g(t)\,\mathrm d\sigma(t), \qquad g=\frac{\mathrm d(P-P^*)}{\mathrm d\sigma}, \qquad \sigma=|P|+P^*.
    \end{equation*}
     
    Since the positive part of $g$ is supported on at most $A$ distinct locations, we must have $V(g)\leq 2A$.
     
    Moreover, since $\bar F(\boldsymbol{x})>0$, the witness $\Delta$ is not identically zero.
     
    The variation diminishing property (cf. Def.~\ref{def: Variation deimishing}) therefore gives $Z(\Delta)\leq V(g)$, where zeros are counted with multiplicity.
     
    On the other hand, the stationarity conditions imply that each $\vartheta_j$ is a zero of multiplicity at least two.
     
    Consequently,
     
    \begin{equation*}
        2A\leq Z(\Delta)\leq V(g)\leq 2A.
    \end{equation*}

        \end{align*}
         

        \item $[L,L+\delta_L]$:

        \begin{equation*}
            \int_L^{L+\delta_L}[\Delta]_+\leq c\delta_L.
        \end{equation*}

        \begin{align*}
