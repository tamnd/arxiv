\label{ex:two-component-gluing}
    Take $\mathcal N=\{1,3\}.$
    Then
    $$
    \mathcal A
    =
    \mathbb Z[X]/\bigl((X-1)\Phi_3(X)\bigr)
    \hookrightarrow
    \mathcal O
    =
    \mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z[\zeta_3].
    $$
    Writing $\beta=m+n\zeta_3$, a pair
    $(a,\beta)\in\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z[\zeta_3]$ lies in the image of
    $\mathcal A$ precisely when
    $$
    a\equiv m+n\pmod 3.
    $$
    In particular, if $a=0$, so that the element is supported only on the cyclotomic component corresponding to $n=3$, then
    $
    m+n\equiv0\pmod3.
    $
    Equivalently, the possible values of $\beta$ form the sublattice
    $
    (1-\zeta_3)\mathbb Z[\zeta_3]
    \subset \mathbb Z[\zeta_3],
    $
    which has index $3$, since
    $
    \mathbb Z[\zeta_3]\big/
    (1-\zeta_3)\mathbb Z[\zeta_3]
    \simeq \mathbb F_3.
    $
    The point of the example is that forcing one cyclotomic component to
    vanish imposes a nontrivial restriction on the remaining component.
    Indeed, it forces it into a proper sublattice.  This gluing condition is illustrated in \cref{fig:two-component-gluing}.

    $$

    [\mathcal O_S:\mathcal A\cap K_S]

<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The first identity follows by computing the determinant of the evaluation map
    $
    \mathbb Z[X]/(F)
    \to
    \bigoplus_{n\in\mathcal N}\mathbb Z[\zeta_n].
    $
    With respect to the monomial basis on the left and the power basis of each
    cyclotomic ring on the right, its determinant is, up to sign,
    $
    \prod_{\{m,n\}\subset\mathcal N}
    \operatorname{Res}(\Phi_m,\Phi_n).
    $
    Taking absolute values therefore gives
    $
    [\mathcal O:\mathcal A]=I_{\mathrm{all}}.
    $
    Now fix $S\subset\mathcal N$ and set
    $$
    F_{S^c}=\prod_{n\notin S}\Phi_n,
    \qquad
    \mathcal A_S=\mathbb Z[X]\Big/\Bigl(\prod_{m\in S}\Phi_m\Bigr),
    $$
    viewing $\mathcal A_S$ as an order in $K_S$ via evaluation.
    An element of $\mathcal A$ lies in $K_S$ precisely when its polynomial
    representative vanishes at every $\zeta_n$ with $n\notin S$, or
    equivalently is divisible by $F_{S^c}$.  Hence
    $
    \mathcal A\cap K_S=F_{S^c}\mathcal A_S.
    $
    By the first identity, we have 
    $
    [\mathcal O_S:\mathcal A_S]=I_{\mathrm{internal}}(S).
    $
    For a number field $K$, write $N_{K/\mathbb Q}$ for its field norm.
    Multiplication by $F_{S^c}$ on the $m$-th component has absolute 
    determinant
    $$
    \left|N_{K_m/\mathbb Q}\bigl(F_{S^c}(\zeta_m)\bigr)\right|
    =
    \prod_{n\notin S}
    \left|N_{K_m/\mathbb Q}\bigl(\Phi_n(\zeta_m)\bigr)\right|
    =
    \prod_{n\notin S}
    \left|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_m,\Phi_n)\right|,
    $$
    so taking the product over $m\in S$ gives
    $
    [\mathcal A_S:F_{S^c}\mathcal A_S]
    =I_{\mathrm{cross}}(S).
    $
    Multiplicativity of index now gives the result.

\label{lem:support-exponent-comparison}
    Suppose that $I/C \ll\log C$ and $|y_1|\ll\log C.$ 
    Then there exists an absolute constant $K>0$ such that, for every nonempty
    $S\subseteq\mathcal N$,

    $$
    \mathcal L(S)
    \le
    r\mathscr A(S)+KC\log d.
    $$

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Starting from \eqref{eq:finite-support}, we compare each term with the
    corresponding term in $r\mathscr A(S)$. First, Stirling's formula gives, uniformly for $1\le s\le d$,
    $$
    \log v_s-\frac{s}{d}\log v_d
    \le
    \frac{s}{2}\log\frac ds+O(\log d).
    $$
    Since
    $$
    s=rc_S+a\varepsilon_S,
    \qquad
    d=Cr+a,
    \qquad
    0\le a<C,
    $$
    we obtain
    $$
    \frac{s}{2}\log\frac ds
    =
    r\frac{c_S}{2}\log\frac C{c_S}
    +O(C\log d).
    $$
    Next,
    $$
    \frac{s}{d}
    =
    \frac{rc_S+a\varepsilon_S}{Cr+a}
    =
    \frac{c_S}{C}
    +O\!\left(\frac1r\right).
    $$
    Therefore, using the assumed bound $I/C\ll\log C$, we get 
    $$
    \frac{s}{d}rI
    =
    r\frac{c_S}{C}I
    +O(I)
    =
    r\frac{c_S}{C}I
    +O(C\log d).
    $$
    The term involving $\operatorname{covol}(L_S)$ requires no approximation. For the componentwise scaling term, recall that
    $
    \widetilde y_n=y_n-\eta$ and 
    $
    \eta=\frac{ay_1}{d}.
    $
    Using
    $
    s
    =
    \sum_{n\in S}
    \bigl(rc_n+a\mathbf1_{\{n=1\}}\bigr)
    =
    rc_S+a\varepsilon_S,
    $
    we obtain
    \begin{align*}
        \sum_{n\in S}
        \bigl(rc_n+a\mathbf1_{{n=1}}\bigr)\widetilde y_n
        -
        r\sum_{n\in S}c_ny_n
        =
        a\varepsilon_Sy_1-\eta s
        =
        ay_1
        \left(
            \varepsilon_S-\frac{s}{d}
        \right).
    \end{align*}
    Since $0\le s/d\le1$ and $\varepsilon_S\in\{0,1\}$, we have 
    $
    \left|
    \varepsilon_S-\frac{s}{d}
    \right|
    \le1.
    $
    Thus
    $$
    \left|
    \sum_{n\in S}
    \bigl(rc_n+a\mathbf1_{\{n=1\}}\bigr)\widetilde y_n
    -
    r\sum_{n\in S}c_ny_n
    \right|
    \le
    a|y_1|.
    $$
    The assumption $|y_1|\ll\log C$ and $a<C$ implies that 
    $$
    \sum_{n\in S}
    \bigl(rc_n+a\mathbf1_{\{n=1\}}\bigr)\widetilde y_n
    =
    r\sum_{n\in S}c_ny_n
    +O(C\log d),
    $$
    uniformly in $S$. Substituting these estimates into \eqref{eq:finite-support} gives
    $$
    \mathcal L(S)
    \le
    r
    \left[
        \frac{c_S}{2}\log\frac C{c_S}
        +\frac{c_S}{C}I
        -I_{\mathrm{inc}}(S)
        -\sum_{n\in S}c_ny_n
    \right]
    +KC\log d
    $$
    for some absolute constant $K>0$, uniformly in $S$.

    $1\in\mathcal N$ and $2\notin\mathcal N$, and put

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For a support $S$ of real dimension $s$, replacing the unit ball by
    $B_\rho$ changes $\mathcal L(S)$ by
    $
    s\log\rho.
    $
    Since $s\le d$, \eqref{eq:rho-range} gives
    $
    |s\log\rho|
    \ll
    1+d\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{r}}.
    $
    Since
    $
    d=Cr+a
    \le C(r+1),
    $
    we obtain
    $
    |s\log\rho|
    \ll
    1+C\sqrt{r\log d}.
    $
    The admissibility condition 
    $
    r\gg C^3\log d
    $
    gives 
    $$
    \frac{
        C\sqrt{r\log d}
    }{r}
    =
    C\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{r}}
    \ll
    \frac1{\sqrt C}
    \to 0.
    $$
    Thus the change in $\mathcal L(S)$ is $o(r)$, uniformly in $S$.
    By \cref{lem:fixed-support}, for all sufficiently large $d$, the Haar
    expected number of vectors in $B_\rho$ with any fixed nonempty proper
    support is therefore, say, at most
    $
    e^{-\delta r/2}.
    $

    It remains only to count the possible supports.  Since
    $
    c_n=\varphi(n)\ge1
    $
    for every $n\in\mathcal N$, we have 
    $
    |\mathcal N|
    \le
    \sum_{n\in\mathcal N}c_n
    =
    C.
    $
    Hence the number of supports is at most
    $
    2^{|\mathcal N|}
    \le
    2^C.
    $

    \frac Cr
    \ll
    \frac{1}{C^2\log d}
    \to 0
    $$
    gives 
    $
    C\log2=o(r).
    $
    Summing the estimates over all supports gives
    $
    2^C e^{-\delta r/2}
    \le

\label{lem:entropy-tensorization}
    For every $S\subseteq X$ we have 
    $$
    \frac{c(S)}2\log\frac{C}{c(S)}
    \le
    \sum_{j=1}^J
    \sum_{u\in X_{-j}}
    c_{-j}(u)\,
    \frac{c_j(S_{j,u})}{2}
    \log\frac{C_j}{c_j(S_{j,u})},
    $$
    where the summand is interpreted as $0$ when
    $S_{j,u}=\varnothing$.

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let
    $
    \mu_j(x_j)=\frac{c_j(x_j)}{C_j}
    $
    be the probability measure on $X_j$ and let
    $
    \mu=\bigotimes_{j=1}^J\mu_j
    $
    be the product measure on $X$. By the product structure we have 
    $$
    \mu(x)=\prod_{j=1}^J\frac{c_j(x_j)}{C_j} = \frac{c(x)}{C},
    \qquad
    \mu(S)=\frac{c(S)}{C}.
    $$
    The entropy tensorization inequality
    \cite[Eq.~(1.2)]{CaputoMenzTetali2015}, applied to $f=1_S$, is
    $$
    \operatorname{Ent}_\mu(1_S)
    \le
    \sum_{j=1}^J
    \mu\!\left[\operatorname{Ent}_{\mu_j}(1_S)\right].
    $$
    Since
    $
    \operatorname{Ent}_\mu(1_S)
    =
    \mu(S)\log(1/\mu(S)),
    $
    the left-hand side becomes
    $$
    \frac C2\,\operatorname{Ent}_\mu(1_S)
    =
    \frac{c(S)}{2}\log\frac{C}{c(S)}.
    $$
    For $u\in X_{-j}$ we have 
    $$
    \operatorname{Ent}_{\mu_j}(1_{S_{j,u}})
    =
    \frac{c_j(S_{j,u})}{C_j}
    \log\frac{C_j}{c_j(S_{j,u})},
    \qquad
    \mu_{-j}(u)
    =
    \frac{c_{-j}(u)}{C/C_j}.
    $$
    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mu\!\left[\operatorname{Ent}_{\mu_j}(1_S)\right]
        =
        \sum_{u\in X_{-j}}
        \mu_{-j}(u)\,
        \operatorname{Ent}_{\mu_j}(1_{S_{j,u}}) 
        =
        \frac1C
        \sum_{u\in X_{-j}}
        c_{-j}(u)c_j(S_{j,u})
        \log\frac{C_j}{c_j(S_{j,u})}.
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Multiplying the tensorization inequality by $C/2$ gives the claim.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:product-support}
    For $1\le j\le J$, let $X_j$ be finite weighted graphs with positive
    integer vertex weights $c_j$, edge weight functions $w_j$, total
    weights $C_j$, and potentials $v_j$ satisfying
    $
    \sum_{x_j\in X_j}c_j(x_j)v_j(x_j)=0.
    $
    Suppose that, for some $\delta>0$,
    $
    \mathcal E_{X_j}(U)\le-\delta
    $
    for every $j$ and every nonempty proper $U\subsetneq X_j$.

    Equip
    $
    X=\prod_{j=1}^JX_j
    $
    with the vertex weights
    $
    c(x)=\prod_{j=1}^Jc_j(x_j)
    $
    and potential
    $
    v(x)=\sum_{j=1}^Jv_j(x_j).
    $
    Give $X$ the product weighted graph in which two vertices are adjacent if they
    differ in exactly one coordinate, say the $j$-th, and such an edge has
    weight
    $$
    \left(\prod_{i\ne j}c_i(x_i)\right)
    w_j(x_j,x_j').
    $$
    Then $v$ has weighted mean zero and
    $
    \mathcal E_X(S)\le-\delta
    $
    for every nonempty proper $S\subsetneq X$.

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We have 
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \sum_{x\in X}c(x)v(x)
&=
\sum_{j=1}^J
\left(\prod_{i\ne j}C_i\right)
\sum_{x_j\in X_j}c_j(x_j)v_j(x_j)
=0,
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    so the product potential has weighted mean zero.

    Now fix $S\subseteq X$. \cref{lem:entropy-tensorization} gives 
    $$
    \frac{c(S)}2\log\frac{C}{c(S)}
    \le
    \sum_{j=1}^J
    \sum_{u\in X_{-j}}
    c_{-j}(u)
    \frac{c_j(S_{j,u})}{2}
    \log\frac{C_j}{c_j(S_{j,u})}.
    $$
    By the definition of the product graph, the boundary term decomposes as
    $$
    W_X(S,S^c)
    =
    \sum_{j=1}^J
    \sum_{u\in X_{-j}}
    c_{-j}(u)
    W_j(S_{j,u},S_{j,u}^c).
    $$
    Likewise we have 
    $$
    \sum_{x\in S}c(x)v(x)
    =
    \sum_{j=1}^J
    \sum_{u\in X_{-j}}
    c_{-j}(u)
    \sum_{x_j\in S_{j,u}}
    c_j(x_j)v_j(x_j).
    $$
    Combining these three relations gives
    $$
    \mathcal E_X(S)
    \le
    \sum_{j=1}^J
    \sum_{u\in X_{-j}}
    c_{-j}(u)\,
    \mathcal E_{X_j}(S_{j,u}).
    $$
    If $S_{j,u}=\varnothing$, then
    $\mathcal E_{X_j}(S_{j,u})=0$.  The same is true when
    $S_{j,u}=X_j$, by the zero-mean condition on $v_j$.
    Every nonempty proper fiber, on the other hand, satisfies
    $
    \mathcal E_{X_j}(S_{j,u})\le-\delta.
    $
    Suppose now that $S$ is nonempty and proper.  Choose
    $x\in S$ and $x'\notin S$, and replace the coordinates of $x$ by
    those of $x'$ one at a time.  At some step one passes from $S$ to
    $S^c$.  The corresponding fiber $S_{j,u}$ is therefore nonempty and

Set
    $
    \mu(B):=\frac{\rho(B)}{P}.
    $
    Then
    $$
    \mu(B)
    =
    \prod_{p\in B}\frac1p
    \prod_{p\in\mathcal P\setminus B}\frac{p-1}{p},
    $$
    so $\mu$ is obtained by including each $p\in\mathcal P$
    independently with probability $1/p$.
    Let $f=\mathbf1_{\mathcal U}$.  Entropy tensorization gives
    $$
    \operatorname{Ent}_\mu(f)
    \le
    \sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
    \mathbb E_{\mu_{-p}}\!\left[\operatorname{Ent}_{\mu_p}(f)\right].
    $$

    $B\subseteq\mathcal P\setminus\{p\}$.  Varying whether $p$ is
    included gives the cube edge
    $
    B\longleftrightarrow B\cup\{p\}.
    $

    has the same value at its two endpoints and hence contributes zero
    entropy.  If it does cross the boundary, then $f$ takes the values
    $0$ and $1$. The endpoint at which
    $f=1$ has probability either $1/p$ or $(p-1)/p$
    and in either case its entropy is at most
    $
    (\log p)/p.
    $ 

    The probability of the choice $B$ outside the $p$-coordinate is
    $$
    \mu_{-p}(B)
    =
    \prod_{p'\in B}\frac1{p'}
    \prod_{p'\in\mathcal P\setminus(B\cup\{p\})}\frac{p'-1}{p'}.
    $$

    \operatorname{Ent}_\mu(f)
    \le
    \sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P,\,
            B\subseteq\mathcal P\setminus\{p\}\\
            B\leftrightarrow B\cup\{p\}

    \mu_{-p}(B)\frac{\log p}{p}.
    $$
    But

    P\,\mu_{-p}(B)\frac{\log p}{p}

    \rho(B\cup\{p\})\log p,

    $

    \le
    E_{\mathcal P}(\mathcal U).

    $   P\operatorname{Ent}_\mu(\mathbf1_{\mathcal U})
    =
    u(\mathcal U)\log\frac{P}{u(\mathcal U)}

    proves the claim.

    \mathbb E_{\mathrm{Br}}

    $$

    \end{equation*}

    E=E_{A_T}

    \log\det A_T
    \le

    $$

    $$
    \frac{\operatorname{vol}(E_{A_T})}{v_d}
    =
    (\det A_T)^{-1/2}
    \gg
    \exp\left(\frac{qT}{4}\right) =     \left(
        \frac{M\gamma}{K_0}
    \right)^{q/(4\gamma)}.
    $$
    Note that 
    $$
    \frac{q}{4\gamma}
    =
    \frac{
        2q(d+2)
    }{
        d(2q+d)
    }
    =
    1-O(r^{-1}),
    $$
    where we used $q\asymp dr$.  Moreover,
    $
    \log(M\gamma)
    =
    O(\log d)
    =
    o(r),
    $
    where the last estimate follows from the admissibility assumption $r \gg (\log d)^4 $. It follows that
    $
    (M\gamma/K_0)^{q/(4\gamma)}
    \gg
    M\gamma.
    $

    $
    \gamma\asymp q,
    $

        \operatorname{vol}(E)
        \gg

        \label{eq:ellipsoid-volume}

    \le
    C(r+1)

    $

    preserving packing density.  This proves the proposition.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
For a $\tau$-invariant ball of normalized volume $V$ (Euclidean volume
$Vv_d$), Siegel's formula gives an expected count $V$ of vectors with full support, and
this count is a multiple of $M$. The estimate for vectors of proper support then
shows that a lattice-free ball exists at $V=M-o(1)$, provided
$Me^{-cr}=o(1)$. This yields density $(M-o(1))2^{-d}$.  In the present
construction $M=d^{o(1)}$, so direct orbit counting alone does not give
$d\log\log d\,2^{-d}$.
