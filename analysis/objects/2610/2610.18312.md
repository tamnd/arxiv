<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:basic}
Let $\xi$ be an environment, let $m\le n$, and let $A\subseteq H_{m}$ be
infinite and not bounded from below. Then $\mathbb{P}^{\xi}(\zeta_{m,n}(A)=\emptyset)=0$
and, for every integer $k\ge0$ and every $b\in H_{m}$,
\[
  \mathbb{P}^{\xi}\bigl(\max\zeta_{m,n}(H_{m}^{b})\le b-2k\bigr)
  \ \le\ \bigl(1-p_{c}^{\,n-m}\bigr)^{k}.
\]
In particular, $\mathbb{E}^{\xi}|\max\zeta_{m,n}(H_{m}^{b})|<\infty$ and $\mathbb{E}^{\xi}|r_{n}|<\infty$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For $x\in H_{m}$, let $D(x):=\{(x+i,m+i):1\le i\le n-m\}$ be the ascending
diagonal issued from $(x,m)$. Diagonals issued from distinct points are disjoint and each is
entirely open with probability at least $p_{c}^{\,n-m}$ because
$p_{\xi_{i}}\gep_{c}$. If $x\in A$ and $D(x)$ is open then
$x+(n-m)\in\zeta_{m,n}(A)$.

Since $A$ is infinite and not bounded from below, we may pick
$x_{0}>x_{1}>\dots$ in $A$. The diagonals $D(x_{0}),D(x_{1}),\dots$ being
disjoint, the corresponding events are conditionally independent given $\xi$, so
that the probability that none of $D(x_{0}),\dots,D(x_{j-1})$ is open is at most
$(1-p_{c}^{\,n-m})^{j}$. Letting $j\to\infty$ shows that
$\zeta_{m,n}(A)\ne\emptyset$ almost surely.

The same argument applied to the $k$ points $b,b-2,\dots,b-2(k-1)$ of
$H_{m}^{b}$ gives that if $D(b-2i)$ is open for some
$0\le i\le k-1$, then $b-2i+(n-m)\in\zeta_{m,n}(H_{m}^{b})$, thus
\[
  \max\zeta_{m,n}(H_{m}^{b})\ \ge\ b-2(k-1)+(n-m)\ >\ b-2k ,
\]
so that the event $\{\max\zeta_{m,n}(H_{m}^{b})\le b-2k\}$ forces all $k$
diagonals to be closed.

Finally, $\max\zeta_{m,n}(H_{m}^{b})\le b+(n-m)$, since the $x$-coordinate increases
by at most $1$ at each step, and the above bound gives an exponential tail on
the left, thus $\mathbb{E}^{\xi}|\max\zeta_{m,n}(H_{m}^{b})|<\infty$, uniformly in $\xi$.

Write $q:=1-\delta$ for the density of good lines and see $a_{n}$ as a
function of $q$. The edge $r_{n}$ depends on the environment only through
$(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})$, so for $w\in\{0,1\}^{n}$ we write
$\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[r_{n}]=\mathbb{E}^{w}[r_{n}]$ whenever $\xi$ agrees with $w$ on the first $n$
lines. Let $\mu_{q}$ be the law of a single line, $\mu_{q}(0)=q$ and
$\mu_{q}(1)=1-q$, so that
\[
  a_{n}=\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\ \prod_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{q}(w_{i})\ \mathbb{E}^{w}[r_{n}].
\]
This is a polynomial in $q$. Indeed, the sum is finite, and the coefficients
$\mathbb{E}^{w}[r_{n}]$ are finite by Lemma~\ref{lem:basic} and do not depend on $q$.
Since $\mathrm{d}\mu_{q}(0)/\mathrm{d}q=1$ and
$\mathrm{d}\mu_{q}(1)/\mathrm{d}q=-1$, the product rule gives
\[
  \frac{\mathrm{d}a_{n}}{\mathrm{d}q}
  =\sum_{m=1}^{n}\ \sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{n}}
    \varepsilon(w_{m})\prod_{i\ne m}\mu_{q}(w_{i})\ \mathbb{E}^{w}[r_{n}],
  \qquad \varepsilon(0)=1,\ \varepsilon(1)=-1 .
\]
For fixed $m$, the weight $\prod_{i\ne m}\mu_{q}(w_{i})$ does not depend on
$w_{m}$. It only involves the status of the lines other than $H_{m}$. Pairing
each environment $w$ with the one obtained by changing the status of $H_{m}$,
and using $\varepsilon(0)=1$, $\varepsilon(1)=-1$, the inner sum becomes
\[
  \sum_{v}\ \prod_{i\ne m}\mu_{q}(v_{i})
  \Bigl(\mathbb{E}^{\xi^{m,G}}[r_{n}]-\mathbb{E}^{\xi^{m,B}}[r_{n}]\Bigr),
\]
where $v$ runs over the $2^{n-1}$ environments of the lines
$H_{i}$, $i\le n$, $i\ne m$, and $\xi$ is any environment agreeing with $v$ off

exactly $\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{m}r_{n}]$. Notice that we recover Russo's formula. Moreover, Lemma~\ref{lem:line} gives
\[
  \frac{\mathrm{d}a_{n}}{\mathrm{d}q}
  =\sum_{m=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\Delta_{m}r_{n}\bigr]
  \ \ge\ 2\,(p_{G}-p_{c})\,n
  \qquad\text{for every } q\in[0,1].

speed vanishes, $\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{p_{c}}[r_{n}]/n=0$,
by the classical result of Durrett and Griffeath~\cite{DurrettGriffeath1983}. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:speeds} this limit is also an infimum, so that
$\mathbb{E}_{p_{c}}[r_{n}]\ge0$ for every $n\ge1$. Integrating therefore gives
$a_{n}\ge2(p_{G}-p_{c})\,q\,n$ for every $n\ge1$, and taking the infimum over $n$

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The factor 2 in the lower bound of $\alpha(\delta)$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:strict} may be surprising since $\alpha(\delta) \leq 1$, but it is in fact not a contradiction. since for $p_{B}>p_{c}$ percolation holds even at $\delta=1$.

probability $p^{1+J\kappa_{n}}$, that is, the model of
Theorem~\ref{thm:HilarioSaSanchisTeixeira2026} with $J\kappa_{n}$ in place of
$\zeta_{n}$. After choosing the selection $(X_{k})_{k\ge0}$, we prove two
dominations. First, conditionally on the selection $(X_{k})_{k\ge0}$, which
determines the scheme, and on the environment $\xi$, under which the occupation
variables are independent, the probability that the renormalized model has an
infinite open path starting at $(0,0)$ is at least the probability that the
intermediate model has one. Second, the intermediate model dominates the model
of Theorem~\ref{thm:HilarioSaSanchisTeixeira2026} as soon as
$J\kappa_{n}\le\zeta_{n}$ for defects $\zeta_{n}$ as in that theorem, since then
$p^{1+J\kappa_{n}}\ge p^{1+\zeta_{n}}$; that theorem then concludes.

The second domination requires the $\kappa_{n}$ to be i.i.d.\ with a geometric
tail, whereas the slabs on which the transport boxes are likely to be crossed,
called favorable below, only form a $1$-dependent sequence; the selection
therefore discards some of them, using
\cite[Theorem~0.0]{LiggettSchonmannStacey1997}. The first domination meets two
obstacles. An edge depends on its two endpoints, through the corridors, and on
the neighboring edges, through the overlaps of Lemma~\ref{lem:regions}; the
Harris--FKG inequality removes the first dependence and
\cite[Corollary~1.4]{LiggettSchonmannStacey1997} dominates the second by an
independent field. Finally, Theorem~\ref{thm:HilarioSaSanchisTeixeira2026} has no
vertex variables, so the price $\varepsilon^{2\kappa_{n}}$ of a corridor is
charged to the two edges issued from it, at the cost $\varepsilon^{4\kappa_{n}}$
each, which $J$ converts into $p^{J\kappa_{n}}$.

The probability space is assumed to be rich enough to carry the auxiliary
independent random variables constructed below.

\cite[Corollary~1.4, Lemma~1.0]{LiggettSchonmannStacey1997}, there are i.i.d.\ Bernoulli

$\mathcal{G}$ such that $\psi_{e}\le\widetilde{Z}_{e}$.

since $\varepsilon\le1/2$, and the inequality is trivial if $\kappa=0$. Hence, conditionally on $\sigma(\xi,(X_{k})_{k\ge0})$, one can construct,
independently of $(\psi_{e})_{e}$ and for each $v$ at floor $n$, two
independent Bernoulli variables of parameter $\varepsilon^{4\kappa_{n}}$, one
for each edge issued from $v$, bounded above by $Y_{v}$. We call these
variables $(\chi_{e})_{e}$; they are independent conditionally on
$\sigma(\xi,(X_{k})_{k\ge0})$, as the $Y_{v}$ are.

path of $\mathbb{V}$ starting at $(0,0)$ whose edges satisfy $Y_{a(e)}=1$ and

edge of the path, so $Y_v=1$ at each of its vertices, hence $Z_{e}=\widetilde{Z}_{e}=1$ at each of

$\mathcal{O}$. Conditionally on $\sigma(\xi,(X_{k})_{k\ge0})$, the variables
$\chi_{e}\psi_{e}$ are independent Bernoulli of parameter

floor of $a(e)$. Hence $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\mid\sigma(\xi,(X_{k})_{k\ge0}))$ is at
least the probability that the intermediate model has an infinite open path
starting at $(0,0)$.

\emph{Second domination.}
Since the $\kappa_{n}$ are i.i.d.\ with $\mathbb{P}(\kappa_{n}=k)=(1-\theta^{J})\theta^{Jk}$,
\[
  \mathbb{P}(J\kappa_{n}\ge j)\ =\ \theta^{J\lceil j/J\rceil}\ \le\ \theta^{j},
  \qquad j\ge0 ,
\]
so $(\kappa_{n})_{n\ge0}$ can be coupled
with i.i.d.\ variables
$(\zeta_{n})_{n\ge0}$ of law $(1-\theta)\theta^{k}$ so that
$J\kappa_{n}\le\zeta_{n}$. Then $p^{1+J\kappa_{n}}\ge p^{1+\zeta_{n}}$ and, the
existence of an infinite open path being an increasing event, the probability
that the intermediate model has one starting at $(0,0)$ is at least its
probability under $\mathbb{P}^{\zeta}_{p}$, which is the second domination. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:HilarioSaSanchisTeixeira2026} the latter is positive for almost
every $\zeta$, so $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\mid\sigma(\xi,(X_{k})_{k\ge0}))>0$ almost surely, and
$\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O})>0$.
