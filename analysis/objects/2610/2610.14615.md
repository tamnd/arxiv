<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:intro-sequential}
A distribution has a small next-block pseudoentropy gap if and only if
it admits an efficient stateless sequential sampler with small KL error.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:intro-state-elimination}
A sequential sampler with $k$ bits of state can be approximated in KL-divergence by a stateless sequential sampler. The circuit-size overhead is $2^{k}$ and polynomial in the other parameters, including token alphabet size.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:intro-insertional}
A distribution has a small insertional pseudoentropy gap if and only if
it admits an efficient stateless insertional sampler with small KL error.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
Assuming one-way functions exist, there is a PRG whose distribution can be sampled by a stateless insertional sampler.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
Assuming the existence of very hard-to-invert one-way functions, there is an efficiently sampleable distribution that cannot be sampled efficiently in a stateless insertional manner.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
For a discrete random variable $X$ with finite support, we define the Shannon entropy of $X$ to be $H(X) \coloneqq - \operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x \sim X} [ \log_2 \Pr(X = x) ]$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
For $p \in [0, 1]$, define the  binary entropy function $h(p) = - p \log_2 p - (1-p) \log_2 (1-p)$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $P,Q$ be distributions over a finite set $\Omega$. If $P$ is absolutely
continuous with respect to $Q$, meaning that $P(a)>0$ implies $Q(a)>0$, then the
KL divergence from $P$ to $Q$ is
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)
\coloneqq
\sum_{a\in\Omega} P(a)\log \frac{P(a)}{Q(a)}.
\]
If $P$ is not absolutely continuous with respect to $Q$, we define
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)=+\infty$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- fact-1: body_md -->
\label{fact:kl-nonnegative}
For any two distributions $P,Q$ over a finite set $\Omega$,
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)\geq 0$.
<!-- end fact-1 -->
<!-- fact-2: body_md -->
\label{fact:pinsker}
For finite distributions $P,Q$,
\[
\Delta(P,Q)\leq\sqrt{(\ln2)D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P ~\|~ Q \right) /2}.
\]
<!-- end fact-2 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:markov-chain}
A sequence of random variables $Z_1,\ldots,Z_n$ over finite sets is a Markov
chain, denoted $Z_1-Z_2-\cdots-Z_n$
if for every $i\in\{2,\ldots,n-1\}$,
\[
\Pr[Z_{i+1}=z_{i+1}\mid Z_1=z_1,\ldots,Z_i=z_i]
=
\Pr[Z_{i+1}=z_{i+1}\mid Z_i=z_i]
\]
whenever the conditioning event has positive probability.

Equivalently, for every $i\in\{2,\ldots,n-1\}$,
$Z_{i+1}$ is conditionally independent of $(Z_1,\ldots,Z_{i-1})$ given $Z_i$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- fact-3: body_md -->
\label{fact:kl-chain-rule}
Let $P_{XY}$ and $Q_{XY}$ be distributions over the same finite set
$\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y$. Then
\[
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P_{XY} ~\|~ Q_{XY} \right) 
=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P_X ~\|~ Q_X \right) 
+\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_X}D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P_{Y\mid X=x} ~\|~ Q_{Y\mid X=x} \right) .
\]
Conditional distributions on events of probability zero may be chosen
arbitrarily, and the expectation sums only over $x$ with $P_X(x)>0$.
The identity allows infinite divergence.
<!-- end fact-3 -->
<!-- fact-4: body_md -->
\label{fact:kl-chain-rule-markov}
Let $n\geq1$ and let
$X_0,\ldots,X_n$ and $Y_0,\ldots,Y_n$ be Markov chains, where for each
$i$ the variables $X_i,Y_i$ take values in the same finite set, which may
depend on $i$. If they have the same initial distribution, $X_0\equiv Y_0$,
then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kl-chain-rule-markov}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (X_0,\ldots,X_n) ~\|~ (Y_0,\ldots,Y_n) \right) 
=\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim X_{i-1}}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_i\mid_{X_{i-1}=x} ~\|~ Y_i\mid_{Y_{i-1}=x} \right) .
\end{equation}
Both transitions are conditioned on the same value $x$, sampled from
$X_{i-1}$, with the same conventions for zero-probability events as above.
<!-- end fact-4 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Applying \Cref{fact:kl-chain-rule} to the first coordinate gives
\begin{align*}
&D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (X_0,\ldots,X_n) ~\|~ (Y_0,\ldots,Y_n) \right) \\
&\quad=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_0 ~\|~ Y_0 \right) 
+\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x_0\sim X_0}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (X_1,\ldots,X_n)\mid_{X_0=x_0} ~\|~ (Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)\mid_{Y_0=x_0} \right) .
\end{align*}
Here $D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_0 ~\|~ Y_0 \right) =0$ because $X_0\equiv Y_0$.
For $n=1$, this already gives the claim. For $i\in[n]$, write
$X_{<i}=(X_0,\ldots,X_{i-1})$ and $Y_{<i}=(Y_0,\ldots,Y_{i-1})$.
For $n\geq2$, applying the same fact inside the expectation separates
the next coordinate:
\begin{align*}
&D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (X_0,\ldots,X_n) ~\|~ (Y_0,\ldots,Y_n) \right) \\
&\quad=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_0 ~\|~ Y_0 \right) 
+\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x_0\sim X_0}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_1\mid_{X_0=x_0} ~\|~ Y_1\mid_{Y_0=x_0} \right) \\
&\qquad+\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{(x_0,x_1)\sim(X_0,X_1)}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (X_2,\ldots,X_n)\mid_{X_0=x_0,\,X_1=x_1} ~\|~ (Y_2,\ldots,Y_n)\mid_{Y_0=x_0,\,Y_1=x_1} \right) \\
&\quad=\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x_{<i}\sim X_{<i}}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_i\mid_{X_{<i}=x_{<i}} ~\|~ Y_i\mid_{Y_{<i}=x_{<i}} \right) .
\end{align*}
The Markov property gives
\begin{align*}
X_i\mid_{X_{<i}=x_{<i}}
&\equiv X_i\mid_{X_{i-1}=x_{i-1}},\\
Y_i\mid_{Y_{<i}=x_{<i}}
&\equiv Y_i\mid_{Y_{i-1}=x_{i-1}}.
\end{align*}
Consequently, tuples $x_{<i}$ with the same last value $x_{i-1}=x$
give the same KL divergence. Their weights in the expectation add up to
$\Pr[X_{i-1}=x]$:
\begin{align*}
&\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x_{<i}\sim X_{<i}}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_i\mid_{X_{<i}=x_{<i}} ~\|~ Y_i\mid_{Y_{<i}=x_{<i}} \right) \\
&\quad=\sum_x
\left(\sum_{\substack{x_{<i}:\,x_{i-1}=x}}
\Pr[X_{<i}=x_{<i}]\right)
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_i\mid_{X_{i-1}=x} ~\|~ Y_i\mid_{Y_{i-1}=x} \right) \\
&\quad=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim X_{i-1}}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_i\mid_{X_{i-1}=x} ~\|~ Y_i\mid_{Y_{i-1}=x} \right) .
\end{align*}
Substituting these equalities into the expansion proves
\eqref{eq:kl-chain-rule-markov}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- fact-5: body_md -->
\label{fact:DPI}
Let $P_X,Q_X$ be distributions on a finite set $\mathcal X$.
Let $P_Y,Q_Y$ be obtained by applying the same randomized map from
$\mathcal X$ to a finite set $\mathcal Y$. Then
$D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P_Y ~\|~ Q_Y \right) \leqD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P_X ~\|~ Q_X \right) $.
<!-- end fact-5 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:laplace-estimator}
    Let $P$ be a distribution over a finite domain $\Omega$ of size $|\Omega|=K$. For $N>0$, let $\bar{x} = x^{(1)}, \dots x^{(N)}$ be i.i.d. samples according to $P$. The Laplace estimator is defined (w.r.t. $\bar{x}$) for any $\omega \in \Omega$ as 
  \begin{equation}\label{eqn:laplace-estimator}
      \hat{P}_N(\omega) = \frac{1}{N + K} \left(1 + \sum_{j=1}^N \mathds{1}(x^{(j)}=\omega)\right) 
  \end{equation}
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- claim-1: body_md -->
\label{claim:bound-expected-kl-laplace}
Let $P,Q$ be distributions over a finite domain $\Omega$ of size $K\geq1$,
and let $N\geq1$ be an integer. Let $\hat P_N$ be the Laplace estimate
from $N$ independent samples of $P$. Then
\[
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Q ~\|~ \hat P_N \right) 
\leqD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Q ~\|~ P \right) +\log\frac{N+K}{N+1}
\leqD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Q ~\|~ P \right) +\frac{(K-1)\log e}{N+1},
\]
where the expectation is over the samples and logarithms have base two.
<!-- end claim-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $Q$ is not absolutely continuous with respect to $P$, the right-hand
side is infinite and the claim holds. Otherwise, fix $x\in\mathsf{supp}(Q)$
and put $p=P(x)>0$. Its sample count $V_x$ has distribution
$\operatorname{Bin}(N,p)$, and $\hat P_N(x)=(V_x+1)/(N+K)$. Hence
\begin{align*}
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{P(x)}{\hat P_N(x)}\right]
&=p(N+K)\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac1{V_x+1}\right]\\
&=\frac{N+K}{N+1}\bigl(1-(1-p)^{N+1}\bigr)
\leq\frac{N+K}{N+1}.
\end{align*}
The second equality follows by expanding the binomial expectation and
using $\binom Nv/(v+1)=\binom{N+1}{v+1}/(N+1)$.
Expanding KL divergence and applying Jensen's inequality now gives
\begin{align*}
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Q ~\|~ \hat P_N \right) 
&=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Q ~\|~ P \right) 
  +\sum_{x\in\mathsf{supp}(Q)}Q(x)\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\log\frac{P(x)}{\hat P_N(x)}\\
&\leqD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Q ~\|~ P \right) 
  +\sum_{x\in\mathsf{supp}(Q)}Q(x)\log\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{P(x)}{\hat P_N(x)}\right]\\
&\leqD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Q ~\|~ P \right) +\log\frac{N+K}{N+1}.
\end{align*}
Finally, $\log(1+y)\leq y\log e$ for $y\geq0$ gives the second bound.

<!-- fact-6: body_md -->
Let $P$ be any distribution over a finite domain of size $K$. For any constant $c \geq 0$, for any $N = O(K^c)$ samples sampled from $P$, there exists a Boolean circuit $C$ depending only on the $N$ sample values and the domain size $K$ with $
\tilde{O}(N + K)$ two-input gates such that $C$ on $\lambda=\lceil(\log_2 N)^2\rceil
+\lceil\log_2(N+K)\rceil+1$
independent uniform random input wires samples a distribution that is $2^{-\log^2 N}$ KL-distance within the Laplace estimator define above.
<!-- end fact-6 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Fix any $N$ samples $x_1, \ldots x_N \in [K]$.
    
    Given $\lambda \coloneqq \lceil \log^2 N \rceil + \lceil \log_2 (N+ K) \rceil + 1$ input wires uniform randomly distributed, let $U$ be uniformly distributed over $[0,\ldots, 2^\lambda - 1]$,  compute $J \coloneqq 1 + \lfloor (N+K) \cdot U \cdot 2^{-\lambda} \rfloor \in [N+K]$.
    If $J \leq K$ output the $J$-th symbol. 
    If $J > K$, output the symbol $x_{J-K}$.
    

    The total Boolean circuit complexity is $\tilde{O}(N+K)$.
    Multiplying $U$ with $N+K$ takes $O(\lambda \cdot \log(N+K))$ gates, dividing by $2^{\lambda}$ is throwing away the lower $\lambda$ bits using zero gates, and adding $1$ takes $\log (N+K)$ gates.
    Encode $J$ by the $\lceil\log_2(N+K)\rceil$-bit representation of $J-1$. First convert this encoding into $N+K$ wires, exactly one of which is one, using $O(N+K)$ gates. Combine the wires corresponding to each symbol using $N$ two-input OR gates in total, and encode the resulting $K$ symbol-indicator wires in binary using $O(K)$ additional gates. Thus this conversion costs $O(N+K)$ gates.
   
    We now bound the KL-divergence.
    Let $\widetilde P(\sigma)=\Pr[C(U)=\sigma]$ for $\sigma \in [K]$.
    Let $\widehat P$
    be the Laplace estimate of the samples $x_1, \ldots x_N$. 
    Each index $j\in[N+K]$ satisfies
    \[
    \Pr[J=j]\leq\frac{1}{N+K}+\frac{1}{2^\lambda}.
    \]
    Summing over the $1+ \abs{\{i \in [N]:x_i= \sigma\}}$ indices that output
    symbol $\sigma$, we obtain
    \[
    \widetilde P(\sigma)
    \leq
    \left(1+\frac{N+K}{2^\lambda}\right)\widehat P(\sigma)
    \leq
    (1+2^{-\log^2 N-1})\widehat P(\sigma).
    \]
    Therefore, opening the definition of KL divergence,
    \begin{align*}
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\widetilde P\Vert\widehat P)
    &=\sum_{\sigma\in[K]}\widetilde P(\sigma)
      \log_2\frac{\widetilde P(\sigma)}{\widehat P(\sigma)}\\
    &\leq\sum_{\sigma\in[K]}\widetilde P(\sigma)
      \log_2(1+2^{-\log^2 N-1})\\
    &\leq\sum_{\sigma\in[K]}\widetilde P(\sigma)\,2^{-\log^2 N}\\
    &=2^{-\log^2 N},
    \end{align*}
    where we used $\log_2(1+t)\leq t\log_2 e\leq2t$ for any $t > 0$.

\label{thm:sorting-networks}
For every pair of integers $N,w\geq1$, there is a sorting network with
$O(N\log^2(2N))$ compare-and-swap operations that sorts any $N$

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:batched-table-lookup}
Let $M,K,L\geq1$ be integers with $2\leq K\leq M$.
There exists a Boolean circuit depending only on $M,K,L$ that,
on binary-encoded inputs $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_M)\in[K]^M$ and
$(J_1,\ldots,J_L)\in[M]^L$, outputs
$(a_{J_1},\ldots,a_{J_L})$, using
$O((M+L)\log^3(2(M+L)))=\widetilde O(M+L)$ two-input gates.
The circuit construction is deterministic.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Set $R=M+L$. A record is a tuple $(d,\tau,q,v)$ whose coordinates
are a table address $d$, a type bit $\tau$ ($0$ for a table entry,
$1$ for a query), a query index $q$, and a value $v$.
Unused coordinates are set to zero. Form the $R$ records
\[
(j,0,0,a_j)\quad(j\in[M]),
\qquad
(J_q,1,q,0)\quad(q\in[L]).
\]
For $h=\lceil\log(R+1)\rceil$, represent $(d,\tau,q,v)$ by the
$4h$-bit integer
\[
d\,2^{3h}+\tau\,2^{2h}+q\,2^h+v.
\]
Since $d,q,v\leq R<2^h$, numerical order on these encodings agrees
with lexicographic order on the tuples, and $4h=O(\log(2R))$.

Apply \Cref{thm:sorting-networks} to sort these records
lexicographically. Thus, for each $q\in[L]$, the query record
$(J_q,1,q,0)$ occurs after $(J_q,0,0,a_{J_q})$, with no other
table, i.e. type bit $0$, between them.
Write the sorted records as $(d_r,\tau_r,q_r,v_r)_{r=1}^R$,
and compute
\[
c_0=0,\qquad
c_r=
\begin{cases}
v_r,&\tau_r=0,\\
c_{r-1},&\tau_r=1.
\end{cases}
\]
For a query record with $q_r=q$, we have $c_r=a_{J_q}$.
This computation uses $O(R\log K)$ gates.

Finally, sort the triples $(1-\tau_r,q_r,c_r)$ lexicographically,
using the integer encodings $(1-\tau_r)2^{2h}+q_r2^h+c_r$.
The first $L$ triples correspond to queries in increasing order
of $q$, so their third coordinates give $(a_{J_1},\ldots,a_{J_L})$.
Each of the two sorting networks uses $O(R\log^3(2R))$ gates,
giving the claimed size bound.
All operations are fixed by $M,K,L$, so the circuit construction
is deterministic.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:batched-laplace-sampling}
Let $N,K,\lambda\geq1$. There exists a Boolean circuit depending only
on $N,K,\lambda$, with $\widetilde O(\lambda(N+K))$ two-input gates,
that takes binary-encoded samples $x_1,\ldots,x_N\in[K]$ as inputs
and uses $N\lambda$ independent uniform random bits. For every fixed
input, its $N$ outputs are independent with a common distribution
$\widetilde P$ satisfying
\[
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
        \widetilde P^{\otimes N}
        \,\middle\|\,
        \widehat P^{\otimes N}
    \right)
    \leq 2N(N+K)\,2^{-\lambda}. 
\]
Here, $\widehat P$ is the Laplace estimate of the input samples,
and $\widetilde O$ hides polylogarithmic factors in $N$ and $K$.

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For $K=1$, output $N$ copies of the sole symbol.
Henceforth assume $K\geq2$.
Write $T=N+K$, and form the table $a=(1,\ldots,K,x_1,\ldots,x_N)\in[K]^T$.
For each $i\in[N]$, use a separate block of $\lambda$
random bits to generate
$U_i$ uniformly in $\{0,\ldots,2^\lambda-1\}$, and compute
$J_i=1+\left\lfloor\frac{TU_i}{2^\lambda}\right\rfloor$.
Computing all indices requires
$O(N\lambda\log(2T))$ two-input gates.
By Corollary~\ref{cor:batched-table-lookup}, computing
$(a_{J_1},\ldots,a_{J_N})$ requires
$O(T\log^3(2T))$ additional gates.
The total size is therefore
$\widetilde O(\lambda T)$.

For every fixed input, the outputs are independent, since each equals
$a_{J_i}$ and the indices are independent.
Moreover, for every $j\in[T]$, $\Pr[J_i=j]\leq \frac1T+2^{-\lambda}$.

    \leq (1+T2^{-\lambda})\widehat P(\sigma)$.

    \widetilde P^{\otimes N}
    \,\middle\|\,
    \widehat P^{\otimes N}
\right)
&=N D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\widetilde P\|\widehat P)\\
&\leq N\log_2(1+T2^{-\lambda})\\
&\leq 2NT\,2^{-\lambda},
\end{align*}
using the fact that $\log_2(1+t)\leq 2t$ for $t > 0$.

\label{cor:batched-laplace-sampling-inputs}

There is a Boolean circuit depending only on $N,K,\lambda$, of size

\]
that takes binary-encoded samples $x_1,\ldots,x_N\in[K]$ and

of the selected samples:
\[
\widehat P(\sigma)
=\frac{1+\sum_{t=1}^N b_t\mathbf1[x_t=\sigma]}{H+K}
\quad(\sigma\in[K]).
\]
Thus $\widehat P$ is uniform when $H=0$.

Using $N\lambda$ independent
uniform random bits, the Boolean circuit outputs $N$ symbols that, conditional on these
inputs, are independent with a common distribution $\widetilde P$.
This distribution depends only on the selected samples, in their
original order.
Then, for every

\[
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Q ~\|~ \widetilde P \right) 
\leqD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Q ~\|~ \widehat P \right) +\log_2\frac1{1-\eta}.
\]
The circuit construction is deterministic, and $\widetilde O$ hides
polylogarithmic factors in $N$ and $K$.

\]

<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{def:one-way-functions-circuits}
Let $f=(f_\lambda)_{\lambda\geq1}$ be a function family with
$f_\lambda:\{0,1\}^\lambda\to\{0,1\}^\lambda$, where $f_\lambda(x)$
is computable from $(1^\lambda,x)$ in deterministic polynomial time.
For functions $T:\mathbb{N}\to[1,\infty)$ and $\varepsilon:\mathbb{N}\to[0,1]$, we say
that $f$ is $(T,\varepsilon)$-one-way against circuits if, for all
sufficiently large $\lambda$, every randomized circuit $A_\lambda$ of
size at most $T(\lambda)$ satisfies
\[
\Pr_{X\gets U_\lambda,R}\left[
A_\lambda(f_\lambda(X);R)\in f_\lambda^{-1}(f_\lambda(X))
\right]\leq\varepsilon(\lambda).
\]
Here $R$ denotes independent uniform random coins for $A_\lambda$.
The circuit may depend arbitrarily on $\lambda$, and success means
finding any preimage; an output $\bot$ counts as failure.

We call $f$ a \emph{one-way function against polynomial-size circuits}
if it is $(\lambda^c,\lambda^{-c})$-one-way for every constant $c>0$.
For a size bound $T$, we call $f$ \emph{$T$-secure} if it is
$(T,1/T)$-one-way.

<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def:pseudoentropy-nonuniform}
Let $X$ be a random variable over a finite set. We say that $X$ has
$(T,\varepsilon)$ nonuniform pseudoentropy at least $k$ if there exists a
random variable $Y$ over the same set with
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $H(Y)\geq k$ and
    \item $X$ and $Y$ are $(T,\varepsilon)$ nonuniformly indistinguishable.
\end{enumerate}
Here indistinguishability
means that every Boolean circuit of size at most $T$ has distinguishing
advantage at most $\varepsilon$ in absolute value.

If $X=X(n)$, we say that $X$ has nonuniform pseudoentropy at least $k=k(n)$
if, for every constant $c>0$, it has $(n^c,n^{-c})$ nonuniform pseudoentropy
at least $k(n)-n^{-c}$ for all sufficiently large $n$.

<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{def:conditional-pseudoentropy-nonuniform}
Let $(X,B)$ take values in the finite set $\mathcal X\times\mathcal B$.
We say that $B$ has
$(T,\varepsilon)$ conditional pseudoentropy at least $k$ given $X$ if there
exists a random variable $Z$ over $\mathcal B$, jointly distributed with $X$,
such that
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $H(Z\mid X)\geq k$ and
    \item $(X,B)$ and $(X,Z)$ are $(T,\varepsilon)$ nonuniformly indistinguishable.
\end{enumerate}
The conditional pseudoentropy is the supremum of such $k$. The
$(T,\varepsilon)$ conditional pseudoentropy gap of $B$ given $X$ is this
supremum minus $H(B\mid X)$.
The asymptotic definition uses the same quantifiers and entropy slack as
\Cref{def:pseudoentropy-nonuniform}.

\label{thm:formal-characterizing-pseudoentropy-nonuniform}
There exist a universal constant $c_1\in(0,1]$ and a polynomial $p_0$ with
nonnegative coefficients and constant term at least one such that the following
holds. Let $(X,B)$ take
values in $\{0,1\}^m\times[q]$, with $m\geq0$ and $q\geq2$, and let
$t\geq1$, $\delta,g\geq0$, and $0<\epsilon,\eta,\sigma<1$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $B$ has $(t,\epsilon)$ conditional pseudoentropy gap at least
    $\delta$ given $X$, then every randomized circuit
    $S:\{0,1\}^m\to[q]$ of size at most
    \[
    \frac{\min\{t^{c_1},\,\sigma/\epsilon\}}
         {p_0(m,q,1/\eta,\log(1/\sigma))}
    \]
    satisfies $D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (X,B) ~\|~ (X,S(X)) \right) >\delta-\sigma-\eta$.
    \item If the $(t,\eta)$ conditional pseudoentropy gap of $B$ given
    $X$ is at most $g$, then there exists a randomized circuit
    $S:\{0,1\}^m\to[q]$ of size at most $p_0(t,m,q,1/\eta)$ such that
    \[D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (X,B) ~\|~ (X,S(X)) \right) \leq g+2\eta.\]
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The first part combines~\cite[Theorem~3.8 and Lemma~3.6]{vadhan-zheng},
with respective KL-divergence losses $\sigma$ and $\eta$. Choosing $c_1$
sufficiently small and $p_0$ sufficiently large gives the stated circuit
sizes. For $m=0$, add a fixed conditioning bit; for $\delta=0$, the first
part follows from nonnegativity of KL-divergence.

For the second part, suppose that every sampler of size at most
$p_0(t,m,q,1/\eta)$ has KL-divergence greater than $g+2\eta$. For sufficiently
large $p_0$, the same results give a $(t,\eta)$ conditional pseudoentropy
gap at least $g+\eta$, contradicting its upper bound $g$. One polynomial
$p_0$ can be chosen large enough for both parts, absorbing constant shifts
of its arguments.

Here $Y_{<I}$ determines $I$. Since $H(Y_I\mid X)=H(Y)/n$, the

Each $C_j$ contributes at most $B$ gates, with $O(n\ell)$ more for its

&>\frac{\delta-\sigma}{n}-\eta.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The equality conditions on the common uniform index $I$.

Under the hypotheses of the second part of

most $H(Y)+\delta-\epsilon$. We use this bound in the second part of
\Cref{thm:formal-characterizing-pseudoentropy-nonuniform} to obtain a
sampler $S$ that receives $(i,y_{<i})$ and samples $Y_I$ given $X$ up to
KL-divergence $\delta/n$.
Fixing its index input to $i$ gives the circuit $C_i$: on input
$y_{<i}$, it outputs $S(i,y_{<i})$.

By \Cref{def:nbp} and $H(Y_I\mid X)=H(Y)/n$, the
$(t,\epsilon)$ conditional pseudoentropy gap of $Y_I$ given $X$ is at
most $(\delta-\epsilon)/n$. The target KL-divergence $\delta/n$ leaves
$\epsilon/n$ for the theorem's additive loss $2\eta$. We therefore set
$\eta'=\epsilon/(2n)$. Since $\eta'\leq\epsilon$, every distribution satisfying
$(t,\eta')$-indistinguishability also satisfies
$(t,\epsilon)$-indistinguishability, so the same upper bound on the gap
holds at error $\eta'$.

apply that theorem with $g=(\delta-\epsilon)/n$ and $\eta'$ in

$p_0(t,m,q,2n/\epsilon)$ such that
\[
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (X,Y_I) ~\|~ (X,S(X)) \right) 
\leq \frac{\delta-\epsilon}{n}+2\eta'
=\frac{\delta}{n}.
\]

For each $i\in[n]$, define $C_i(y_{<i})=S(i,y_{<i})$ by hardwiring
the index input to $i$. This does not increase the circuit size.
Using fresh independent coins at every step, the circuits $C_i$ form a
stateless sequential sampler. Since $m=O(nq)$, their size is bounded by
a fixed polynomial in $t,n,q,1/\epsilon$. Enlarge the polynomial $p$
from the preceding lemma to dominate this bound, retaining nonnegative
coefficients and constant term at least one. This is compatible with
that lemma because enlarging $p$ only strengthens its size hypothesis.
Thus $B\leq p(t,n,2^\ell,1/\epsilon)$.

uniform index $I$. Conditioning on this index gives
\[
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{i\gets[n]}D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (Y_{<i},Y_i) ~\|~ (Y_{<i},C_i(Y_{<i})) \right) 
=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (X,Y_I) ~\|~ (X,S(X)) \right) 
\leq\frac{\delta}{n}.
\]

Since the sampler is stateless and uses fresh coins at each step,
$\Pr[C(1^n)=y]=\prod_i\Pr[C_i(y_{<i})=y_i]$.

&=\sum_{i=1}^nD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (Y_{<i},Y_i) ~\|~ (Y_{<i},C_i(Y_{<i})) \right)

\end{align*}

For each $i$, marginalizing over $y_{>i}$ identifies its contribution as

<!-- definition-12: body_md -->
\label{def:k-state-sequential-sampler}
A sequential sampler with $k$ bits of state and size $B$ for $n$ blocks over alphabet $\mathcal X$
is a sequence $C=(C_1,\ldots,C_n)$ of randomized circuits,
each of size at most $B$, with state space $\mathcal S=\{0,1\}^k$. The variables
$(X_1,S_1),\ldots,(X_n,S_n)$ are generated using independent
random coins for each circuit:
\[
(X_1,S_1)\gets C_1(1^n),
\]
and for $i\ge 2$,
\[
(X_i,S_i)\gets C_i(1^n,X_{<i},S_{i-1}).
\]
The sampler $C$ outputs $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$; the states
$S_1,\ldots,S_n$ are not part of its output.

\label{lem:ssa-markov-chain}
Let $(X_1,S_1),\ldots,(X_n,S_n)$ be generated by a sequential
sampler with private state. For $i\in[n]$, define
$A_i\coloneqq(X_{\leq i},S_i)$. Then $A_1-A_2-\cdots-A_n$ is a
Markov chain.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Fix $i\in\{2,\ldots,n\}$ and $a_1,\ldots,a_{i-1}$ such that
$\Pr[A_1=a_1,\ldots,A_{i-1}=a_{i-1}]>0$. Write
$a_{i-1}=(x_{<i},s_{i-1})$. For every $x_i\in\mathcal X$ and $s_i\in\mathcal S$,
put $a_i=(x_{\leq i},s_i)$. Since $C_i$ uses fresh random coins,
\begin{align*}
&\Pr[A_i=a_i\mid A_1=a_1,\ldots,A_{i-1}=a_{i-1}]\\
&\quad=\Pr[C_i(1^n,x_{<i},s_{i-1})=(x_i,s_i)]\\
&\quad=\Pr[A_i=a_i\mid A_{i-1}=a_{i-1}].
\end{align*}
If the prefix in $a_i$ does not extend $x_{<i}$, both conditional
probabilities are zero. This proves the Markov property.

<!-- theorem-9: body_md -->
\label{thm:state-elimination-KL}
Let $n\geq1$ and $k,\ell\geq0$ be integers, and let $0<\delta\leq1$. Let
$C=(C_1,\ldots,C_n)$ be a sequential sampler with $k$ bits of private
state and size at most $B$ per circuit, over alphabet
$\mathcal X=\{0,1\}^\ell$, and let $\mathcal S=\{0,1\}^k$.
There is a stateless sequential sampler $D=(D_1,\ldots,D_n)$
such that
$D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( C(1^n) ~\|~ D(1^n) \right) \leq\delta
$
and every $D_i$ has Boolean circuit size at most
\[
O\left(
\frac{n^3|\mathcal X||\mathcal S|}{\delta}
\left[B+\log^3\!\left(\frac{n|\mathcal X||\mathcal S|}{\delta}\right)\right]
\right).
\]
The circuit construction is deterministic; all sampling and estimation

Write $X=C(1^n)$. Let $D_1$ run $C_1(1^n)$
and return the first component of its output. Thus $D_1(1^n)\equiv X_1$,
proving the case $n=1$. Assume $n\geq2$ and set
\[
m=\left\lceil\frac{4n^2|\mathcal X||\mathcal S|}{\delta}\right\rceil.
\]
We use the circuit $\mathsf{Sample}$ from
\Cref{cor:batched-laplace-sampling-inputs} with
\[
N=m,\qquad K=|\mathcal S|,\qquad
\lambda=\left\lceil\log\bigl(2m(m+|\mathcal S|)\bigr)\right\rceil,
\]
identifying $[|\mathcal S|]$ with $\mathcal S$. For a list $L$ of at most $m$ states,
$\mathsf{Sample}(L)$ receives states $v_1,\ldots,v_m\in\mathcal S$ and
selection bits $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ such that
$L=(v_t:b_t=1)$, in increasing order of $t$.
Write $\widehat\pi^L$ for its Laplace estimate and
$\widetilde\pi^L$ for the common distribution of the $m$ independent
outputs. Since $\eta=(m+|\mathcal S|)2^{-\lambda}\leq1/(2m)$, the corollary gives,
for every distribution $P$ over $\mathcal S$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stateful-laplace-rounding}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P ~\|~ \widetilde\pi^L \right) 
\leqD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P ~\|~ \widehat\pi^L \right) +\frac1m,
\end{equation}
where $\log(1/(1-1/(2m)))\leq1/m$ follows from
$(1-1/(2m))^m\geq1/2$.

In the algorithm below, a list $L_j$ formed from sampled pairs
$(Z_t,T_t)_{t=1}^m$ is represented by the states
$(T_t)_{t=1}^m$ and selection bits
$(\mathbf1[Z_t=x_j])_{t=1}^m$.
Each list preserves repetitions and sample order, and may be empty.
All lists are computed inside $D_i$; no state is passed between
the circuits $D_1,\ldots,D_n$.

For $i\geq2$, the circuit $D_i(1^n,x_{<i})$ runs the following algorithm,
using fresh independent random bits in every call:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Draw $m$ pairs from $C_1(1^n)$. Form a list $L_1$ of states, consisting of the second components
of those symbol-state pairs whose first component equals $x_1$.
\item For $j=2,\ldots,i-1$, run $\mathsf{Sample}(L_{j-1})$ to obtain
$m$ states $s_1,\ldots,s_m$. For each $t\in[m]$, run
$C_j(1^n,x_{<j},s_t)$. Form a list $L_j$ from the second components of those symbol-state
pairs whose first component equals $x_j$.
\item Run $\mathsf{Sample}(L_{i-1})$, take its first output $s$, and return the
first component of $C_i(1^n,x_{<i},s)$.
\end{enumerate}

For
$\Pr[X_{\leq j}=x_{\leq j}]>0$, let
\[
\pi_j^{x_{\leq j}}(s)
=\Pr[S_j=s\mid X_{\leq j}=x_{\leq j}]
\]
denote the true state posterior distribution. In the following,
$[\cdot]_1$ denotes the first output component. Let
\[
\varepsilon_j
=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x_{\leq j}\sim X_{\leq j}}\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{L_j\mid x_{\leq j}}
  D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( \pi_j^{x_{\leq j}} ~\|~ \widetilde\pi^{L_j} \right) ,

where the expectation $\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{L_j\mid x_{\leq j}}$ averages only over the algorithm's
random bits on input $x_{\leq j}$, independent of $X$.
By the chain rule of KL (\Cref{fact:kl-chain-rule}),
\begin{align*}
&D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( C(1^n) ~\|~ D(1^n) \right) \\
&\quad=\sum_{i=2}^n\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x_{<i}\sim X_{<i}}D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_i\mid_{X_{<i}=x_{<i}} ~\|~ D_i(1^n,x_{<i}) \right) \\

  D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_i\mid_{X_{<i}=x_{<i}} ~\|~ \bigl[C_i(1^n,x_{<i},[\mathsf{Sample}(L_{i-1})]_1)\bigr]_1 \right) ,
\end{align*}
where the last inequality follows from the convexity of KL divergence, as $D_i$ constructs $L_{i-1}$ using fresh randomness.
Conditioned on $X_{<i}=x_{<i}$, by \Cref{lem:ssa-markov-chain}, $X_i$ is the first component of $C_i$ when given as input the prefix $x_{<i}$ together with a state distributed according to $\pi^{x_{<i}}_{i-1}$. Thus, for any fixed list of states $L$, by DPI (\cref{fact:DPI}),
\[
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X_i\mid_{X_{<i}=x_{<i}} ~\|~ \bigl[C_i(1^n,x_{<i},[\mathsf{Sample}(L)]_1)\bigr]_1 \right) 
\leqD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( \pi_{i-1}^{x_{<i}} ~\|~ \widetilde\pi^L \right) ,
\]
which together with the previous bound gives us
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-bound-stateful}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( C(1^n) ~\|~ D(1^n) \right) \leq\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon_j.
\end{equation}

For $2\leq j\leq n-1$, fix $x_{<j}$ with
$\Pr[X_{<j}=x_{<j}]>0$ and a realization $L$ of the computed list $L_{j-1}$. Set
\begin{align*}
P_j(x,s)&=\Pr[X_j=x,S_j=s\mid X_{<j}=x_{<j}],\\
Q_j(x,s)&=\Pr\bigl[
C_j(1^n,x_{<j},[\mathsf{Sample}(L)]_1)=(x,s)
\bigr].
\end{align*}
By
\Cref{lem:ssa-markov-chain}, $P_j$ and $Q_j$ result from running
$C_j(1^n,x_{<j},\cdot)$ on $\pi_{j-1}^{x_{<j}}$ and
$\widetilde\pi^L$, respectively. Thus, by DPI
(\Cref{fact:DPI}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-bound-joint-stateful-dpi}

\end{equation}

Step~2 produces $m$ i.i.d. pairs $(Z_t,T_t)_{t=1}^m$ from $Q_j$, and forms the next list of states $L_j=(T_t : Z_t=x_j)$.
Let $\widehat Q_j$ denote the joint Laplace estimate from these pairs.

\[

The corresponding conditional Laplace estimate is exactly that of $L_j$:
\[

\qquad N_x=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}N_{x,s}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:stateful-laplace-rounding}, for every realization of $L_j$,

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:state-distribution-sample-lower-bound}
Let $k\geq1$ and $N\geq0$ be integers, and let $\mathcal S=\{0,1\}^k$. Suppose that a
randomized algorithm receives at most $N$ independent samples from an unknown
distribution $P$ over $\mathcal S$ and outputs a vector of probabilities
$\hat\pi$ over $\mathcal S$, with no subsequent access to the sampling oracle.
The algorithm receives no other information about $P$.
Then there is a distribution $P$ for which
\[
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P ~\|~ \hat\pi \right) \bigr]
\geq 1-\frac{2N}{\abs{\mathcal S}},
\]
where the expectation is over the samples and the algorithm's internal
randomness. Consequently, if
$\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P ~\|~ \hat\pi \right) ]\leq1/4$ for every $P$, then
$N\geq3\abs{\mathcal S}/8=3\cdot2^{k-3}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
We partition the states into pairs and choose one state from each pair
using a hidden bit. Each sample reveals only the bit for its pair;
every pair that is never sampled contributes to the expected KL error.

Let $m=2^{k-1}$ and identify $\mathcal S$ with $[m]\times\{0,1\}$. For each
$\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_m)\in\{0,1\}^m$, define
\[
P_\theta(a,b)=
\begin{cases}
1/m,&b=\theta_a,\\
0,&b\neq\theta_a.
\end{cases}
\]
Choose $\Theta$ uniformly from $\{0,1\}^m$. Conditional on $\Theta$, a sample
from $P_\Theta$ is generated by choosing $A$ uniformly from $[m]$,
independently of $\Theta$, and returning $(A,\Theta_A)$.
We may generate $N$ independent samples in advance and let the algorithm
read as many as it requests, ignoring any unused samples.

Let $W$ consist of these $N$ samples and the algorithm's independent
random coins. Fix $W$, so that the output $\hat\pi$ is fixed, and let
$U\subseteq[m]$ contain the first coordinates absent from the samples.
For each $a\notin U$, the samples determine $\Theta_a$; conditional on $W$,
the bits $(\Theta_a)_{a\in U}$ remain independent and uniform.
Consequently, the distribution $\bar P=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[P_\Theta\mid W]$ satisfies
\[
\bar P(a,b)=
\begin{cases}
1/m,&a\notin U\text{ and }b=\Theta_a,\\
0,&a\notin U\text{ and }b\neq\Theta_a,\\
1/(2m),&a\in U.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular,
\[
H(\bar P)
=\frac{m-|U|}{m}\log m+\frac{|U|}{m}\log(2m)
=\log m+\frac{|U|}{m},
\qquad H(P_\theta)=\log m.
\]
Expanding KL divergence gives
\begin{align*}
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P_\Theta ~\|~ \hat\pi \right) \mid W\bigr]
&=-\log m-\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\bar P(s)\log\hat\pi(s)\\
&=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( \bar P ~\|~ \hat\pi \right) +H(\bar P)-\log m
\geq\frac{|U|}{m}.
\end{align*}
At most $N$ pairs appear among the samples, so $|U|\geq m-N$. Therefore
\[
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P_\Theta ~\|~ \hat\pi \right) \bigr]
\geq\frac{\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}|U|}{m}
\geq1-\frac Nm
=1-\frac{2N}{|\mathcal S|}.
\]
Averaging over the uniform choice of $\Theta$ implies that some fixed
$\theta$ satisfies the same lower bound, with expectation only over its
samples and the algorithm's coins. If this expectation is at most $1/4$

\label{def:statelessInsertionalSampler}
    Let $n,\ell\geq1$ and let $L$ be a distribution over $(\{0,1\}^\ell)^n$. An insertional sampler $C = (C_{1},\dots,C_{n})$ of $L$ is a sequence of randomized (possibly unbounded) circuits, where
    \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
        \item $C(1^n) \coloneqq C_{n}(C_{n-1}(\cdots(C_1(\emptyset)))) \equiv L$, and
        \item for every $i \in [n]$ and $x \in (\{0,1\}^\ell)^{i-1}$, $C_{i}(x)$ is a distribution over strings in $(\{0,1\}^\ell)^i$ for which $x$ is a subsequence.
    \end{itemize}

\label{def:insertionalDecomposition}
    Let $n,\ell\geq1$ and let $L$ be a distribution over $(\{0,1\}^\ell)^n$. An insertional decomposition $(L_{0},\dots,L_{n})$ of $L$ is a sequence of jointly distributed random variables, where
    \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]

        \item $L_{n} \equiv L$, and

    \end{itemize}

<!-- definition-15: body_md -->
\label{def:insertionalPseudoentropy}
    Let $n,\ell\geq1$ and let $L$ be distributed over $(\{0,1\}^\ell)^n$. Let $T\geq1$, $k\geq0$, and
    $0<\varepsilon<1$; these quantities may depend on a security
    parameter $\lambda$.
    We say that $L$ has $(T,\varepsilon)$ insertional pseudoentropy at
    least $k$ if, for every insertional decomposition $(L_0,\ldots,L_n)$
    of $L$, with $I$ uniform on $[n]$ independently of the decomposition,
    there exists a random variable $Z$, jointly distributed with
    $(I,L_0,\ldots,L_n)$ and satisfying $Z\in(\{0,1\}^\ell)^i$ whenever $I=i$,
    such that
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item
        $H(Z\mid I,L_{I-1})-H(L_I\mid I,L_{I-1})
        \geq\frac{k-H(L)}{n}$,
        and
        \item $(I,L_I,L_{I-1})$ and $(I,Z,L_{I-1})$ are
        $(T,\varepsilon)$ nonuniformly indistinguishable.
    \end{enumerate}
    The insertional pseudoentropy is the supremum of the values $k$
    satisfying this definition.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:insertional-nbpe-comparison}
Both definitions measure the increase in conditional entropy that can
remain computationally undetectable. In next-block pseudoentropy, the
entropy chain rule makes the subtraction of the actual conditional
entropy implicit. For $Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)$ with
$Y_i\in\{0,1\}^\ell$, $\ell\geq1$, and $I$ uniform on $[n]$
independently of $Y$, the length of $Y_{<I}$ determines $I$, so
\[
nH(Y_I\mid Y_{<I})
=\sum_{i=1}^nH(Y_i\mid Y_{<i})=H(Y).
\]
Consequently, the condition $H(Z\mid Y_{<I})\geq k/n$ in
\Cref{def:nbp} is equivalent to
\[
H(Z\mid Y_{<I})-H(Y_I\mid Y_{<I})
\geq\frac{k-H(Y)}{n}.
\]
This is the same entropy-gap form as in
\Cref{def:insertionalPseudoentropy}.
For an insertional decomposition, however, the entropy chain rule gives
\begin{align*}
nH(L_I\mid I,L_{I-1})
&=\sum_{i=1}^nH(L_i\mid L_{i-1})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n\bigl(H(L_i)-H(L_{i-1})+H(L_{i-1}\mid L_i)\bigr)\\
&=H(L)+\sum_{i=1}^nH(L_{i-1}\mid L_i).
\end{align*}
In next-block pseudoentropy, a prefix determines all prior information, whereas an in insertional pseudoentropy, a string given by $L_{i}$ does not necessarily determine its preceding string $L_{i-1}$. 
For example, for even $i$, if $L_i$ is $01$ repeated $i/2$ times, then there are $i$ distinct possibilities for $L_{i-1}$ and a random choice of $L_{i-1}$ among them would result in $H(L_{i-1} \mid L_i) = \log i$.
Therefore, the sum $\sum_{i \in [n]} H(L_{i-1} \mid L_i)$ could be as large as $\log (n!)$. Subtracting the actual conditional
entropy in \Cref{def:insertionalPseudoentropy} ensures that the gap
$k-H(L)$ does not count randomness in the choice of insertion order.
The quantification over every insertional decomposition accounts for
the sampler's freedom to choose this order.
This is implicitly the case for next-block pseudoentropy since left-to-right generation
has a single fixed prefix decomposition.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:insertional-common-distribution}
Equivalently, every insertional decomposition $(L_0,\ldots,L_n)$
admits random variables $Z_i\in(\{0,1\}^\ell)^i$, jointly distributed with the
decomposition and independently of $I$, satisfying
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n\bigl(H(Z_i\mid L_{i-1})-H(L_i\mid L_{i-1})\bigr)
\geq k-H(L)
\]
and making $(I,Z_I,L_{I-1})$ and $(I,L_I,L_{I-1})$
$(T,\varepsilon)$ nonuniformly indistinguishable.
The family is chosen once for all distinguishing circuits.

Given $Z$, draw each $Z_i$ conditionally on the decomposition using the
distribution of $Z$ given $I=i$ and $L_{i-1}$, with fresh randomness
independent of $I$. On zero-probability conditioning events, choose any
distribution on $(\{0,1\}^\ell)^i$. Conversely, given such a family, take $Z=Z_I$
with $I$ independent of the decomposition and the family.
These constructions preserve the joint distributions
being compared and give
\begin{align*}
H(Z\mid I,L_{I-1})&=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nH(Z_i\mid L_{i-1}),\\
H(L_I\mid I,L_{I-1})&=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nH(L_i\mid L_{i-1}).
\end{align*}
Thus both formulations have the same entropy gap, circuit size, and
distinguishing error.

<!-- theorem-10: body_md -->
\label{thm:insertional-characterization}
There exist a universal constant $c_0\in(0,1]$ and a polynomial $p$ with
nonnegative coefficients and constant term at least one such that the
following holds. Let $n,\ell\geq1$ and let $L$ take values in
$(\{0,1\}^\ell)^n$. Let $t\geq1$,
$0<\epsilon,\eta,\sigma<1$, and $\delta\geq\epsilon$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $L$ has $(t,\epsilon)$ insertional pseudoentropy at least
    $H(L)+\delta$, then, for every $B\geq1$ satisfying
    \[
    B\leq\frac{\min\{t^{c_0},\,\sigma/(n\epsilon)\}}
                   {p(n,2^\ell,1/\eta,\log(n/\sigma))},
    \]
    every size-$B$ stateless insertional sampler $C$ over $\{0,1\}^\ell$ satisfies
    \[
    D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^n) \right) >\delta-\sigma-n\eta.
    \]
    \item If the $(t,\epsilon)$ insertional
    pseudoentropy of $L$ is at most $H(L)+\delta-\epsilon$, then there
    exists a size-$B$ stateless insertional sampler $C$ over $\{0,1\}^\ell$ with
    \[
    B\leq p(t,n,2^\ell,1/\epsilon),
    \qquad D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^n) \right) \leq\delta.
    \]
\end{enumerate}

If $\delta<\epsilon$ and some $B\geq1$ satisfies the size bound in item~1, then $p\geq1$ forces $\sigma\geq n\epsilon>\delta$, so the claimed lower bound is negative and the inequality follows from nonnegativity of KL-divergence. If no such $B$ exists, item~1 is vacuous.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:subexp-insertion-actions}
Let $i,\ell\geq1$ and $w\in(\{0,1\}^\ell)^{i-1}$. The set
\[
\mathcal E_i(w)=\{z\in(\{0,1\}^\ell)^i:
w\text{ is a subsequence of }z\}
\]
has exactly $K_i=i(2^\ell-1)+1$ elements and admits a bijection
$\mathsf{Ext}_i(w,\cdot):[K_i]\to\mathcal E_i(w)$.
Given $(i,\ell,w,k)$, one can compute $\mathsf{Ext}_i(w,k)$ in
$\operatorname{poly}(i,2^\ell)$ time. Given $(i,\ell,w,z)$ with
$z\in\mathcal E_i(w)$, one can compute the unique $k\in[K_i]$ satisfying
$\mathsf{Ext}_i(w,k)=z$ in the same time bound.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
We count each resulting string once by choosing the rightmost position
at which its inserted symbol can be placed. For $a\in\{0,1\}^\ell$ and
$j\in[i]$, write
\[
\mathsf{Ins}(w,a,j)=(w_1,\ldots,w_{j-1},a,w_j,\ldots,w_{i-1}).
\]
For $a,b\in\{0,1\}^\ell$ and $1\leq j<k\leq i$, comparison of coordinates
$j,\ldots,k$ gives
\[
\mathsf{Ins}(w,a,j)=\mathsf{Ins}(w,b,k)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a=w_j=\cdots=w_{k-1}=b.
\]
Retain all pairs $(a,j)$ with $j=i$, and those with $j<i$ and $a\ne w_j$.
By the displayed equivalence, these are exactly the rightmost
representations, one for each output. Hence
\[
|\mathcal E_i(w)|=2^\ell+(i-1)(2^\ell-1)=i(2^\ell-1)+1.
\]
Order the retained pairs by increasing $j$, then lexicographically by $a$.
Define $\mathsf{Ext}_i(w,k)$ by inserting the symbol at the position
specified by the $k$-th pair. To invert this map given $w$ and $z$,
enumerate the retained pairs and return the index of the unique pair
whose insertion produces $z$. Both algorithms examine at most
$i2^\ell$ pairs and use $O(i\ell)$ time per pair, giving the claimed
$\operatorname{poly}(i,2^\ell)$ bound.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:insertional-path-kl}
Let $n,\ell\geq1$ and let $L$ be distributed over $(\{0,1\}^\ell)^n$.
For every sequence $C=(C_1,\ldots,C_n)$ of valid insertion circuits,
\[
\inf_{(L_0,\ldots,L_n)}
\sum_{i=1}^nD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (L_{i-1},L_i) ~\|~ (L_{i-1},C_i(L_{i-1})) \right) 
=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^n) \right) ,
\]
where the infimum is over insertional decompositions of $L$.
Moreover, if $D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^n) \right) <\infty$, there exists an insertional
decomposition $(L_0,\ldots,L_n)$ of $L$ that forms a Markov chain and
satisfies
\[
\sum_{i=1}^nD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (L_{i-1},L_i) ~\|~ (L_{i-1},C_i(L_{i-1})) \right) 
=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^n) \right) .
\]
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Let $(L_0,\ldots,L_n)$ be any insertional decomposition of $L$,
and $X_0=\emptyset$, $X_i\gets C_i(X_{i-1})$ with fresh independent
coins, so $X_n\equiv C(1^n)$.
Then,
\begin{align*}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^n) \right) 
&=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L_n ~\|~ X_n \right) \\
&\leqD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (L_{n-1},L_n) ~\|~ (X_{n-1},X_n) \right) \\
&=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L_{n-1} ~\|~ X_{n-1} \right) 
+\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim L_{n-1}}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L_n\mid_{L_{n-1}=x} ~\|~ C_n(x) \right) \\
&\leq
\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim L_{i-1}}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L_i\mid_{L_{i-1}=x} ~\|~ C_i(x) \right) \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (L_{i-1},L_i) ~\|~ (L_{i-1},C_i(L_{i-1})) \right) .
\end{align*}
The first inequality is \Cref{fact:DPI} applied to the map that keeps
the second coordinate. The next equality is \Cref{fact:kl-chain-rule}:
given $X_{n-1}=x$, the distribution of $X_n$ is $C_n(x)$.
Repeating these two steps down to $i=1$ gives the sum, since
$D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L_0 ~\|~ X_0 \right) =0$.
The last equality applies the same chain rule to two pairs with the
same first-coordinate distribution $L_{i-1}$.
Since the decomposition was arbitrary, the final KL divergence is at
most the infimum in the statement.

Write $Q(x_0,\ldots,x_n)=\Pr[X_0=x_0,\ldots,X_n=x_n]$ and
$Q_n(z)=\Pr[X_n=z]$.
For the reverse inequality, assume $D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ Q_n \right) <\infty$ and define
\[
M(x_0,\ldots,x_n)=
\begin{cases}
Q(x_0,\ldots,x_n)\dfrac{\Pr[L=x_n]}{Q_n(x_n)},&Q_n(x_n)>0,\\
0,&Q_n(x_n)=0.
\end{cases}
\]
For $Q_n(z)>0$,
\begin{align*}
\sum_{x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}}M(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1},z)
&=\frac{\Pr[L=z]}{Q_n(z)}
\sum_{x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}}Q(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1},z)\\
&=\Pr[L=z].
\end{align*}
Both sides are zero otherwise, since $\mathsf{supp}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{supp}(Q_n)$.
Thus $(M_0,\ldots,M_n)\sim M$ satisfies $M_n\equiv L$.
Since $\mathsf{supp}(M)\subseteq\mathsf{supp}(Q)$, $M_0=\emptyset$ and every step
inserts one symbol.

To compute the conditional probabilities, for $i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}$ set
\[
h_i(w)=\sum_{z:\,Q_n(z)>0}
\Pr[X_n=z\mid X_i=w]\frac{\Pr[L=z]}{Q_n(z)}
\qquad(w\in\mathsf{supp}(X_i)),
\]
and set $h_i(w)=0$ otherwise. The Markov property of $(X_0,\ldots,X_n)$
gives
\begin{align*}
&\Pr[M_0=x_0,\ldots,M_i=x_i]\\
&\quad=\sum_{z:\,Q_n(z)>0}
\Pr[X_0=x_0,\ldots,X_i=x_i,X_n=z]
\frac{\Pr[L=z]}{Q_n(z)}\\
&\quad=\Pr[X_0=x_0,\ldots,X_i=x_i]h_i(x_i).
\end{align*}
For $i\in[n]$ and every tuple with
$\Pr[M_0=x_0,\ldots,M_{i-1}=x_{i-1}]>0$, division gives
\begin{align*}
&\Pr[M_i=z\mid M_0=x_0,\ldots,M_{i-1}=x_{i-1}]\\
&\quad=\Pr[C_i(x_{i-1})=z]\frac{h_i(z)}{h_{i-1}(x_{i-1})}\\
&\quad=\Pr[M_i=z\mid M_{i-1}=x_{i-1}].
\end{align*}
The ratio depends on the preceding tuple only through $x_{i-1}$, so the
last equality holds and $(M_0,\ldots,M_n)$ is a Markov insertional
decomposition of $L$.
By \Cref{fact:kl-chain-rule-markov},
\begin{align*}
&\sum_{i=1}^n
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (M_{i-1},M_i) ~\|~ (M_{i-1},C_i(M_{i-1})) \right) \\
&\quad=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( M ~\|~ Q \right) \\
&\quad=\sum_{(x_0,\ldots,x_n)\in\mathsf{supp}(M)}
M(x_0,\ldots,x_n)\log\frac{\Pr[L=x_n]}{Q_n(x_n)}\\
&\quad=\sum_{z\in\mathsf{supp}(L)}\Pr[L=z]\log\frac{\Pr[L=z]}{Q_n(z)}\\
&\quad=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^n) \right) .
\end{align*}
This decomposition attains the infimum.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lemma:insertional-PE-implies-conditional-hardness}
Under the hypotheses and size condition of the first part of
\Cref{thm:insertional-characterization}, every insertional decomposition
$(L_0,\ldots,L_n)$ of $L$ and every size-$B$ stateless insertional
sampler $C$ over $\{0,1\}^\ell$ satisfy
\[
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{i\gets[n]}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (L_{i-1},L_i) ~\|~ (L_{i-1},C_i(L_{i-1})) \right) 
>\frac{\delta-\sigma}{n}-\eta.
\]
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Fix an insertional decomposition $(L_0,\ldots,L_n)$ and a sampler $C$, and put
\[
d_i=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (L_{i-1},L_i) ~\|~ (L_{i-1},C_i(L_{i-1})) \right) ,
\qquad d=\sum_{i=1}^n d_i.
\]
If $d=\infty$, the claim follows, so assume $d<\infty$.
By \Cref{def:insertionalPseudoentropy} and \Cref{rem:insertional-common-distribution},
choose a family $Z_i\in(\{0,1\}^\ell)^i$ such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n H(Z_i\mid L_{i-1})
\geq\sum_{i=1}^n H(L_i\mid L_{i-1})+\delta
\]
and $(I,Z_I,L_{I-1})$ and $(I,L_I,L_{I-1})$ are
$(t,\epsilon)$ nonuniformly indistinguishable, where $I$ is uniform on
$[n]$ and independent of the family and decomposition.
We estimate the sampler's output probabilities and test their negative
logarithms. The expected negative logarithm will be at least $H(Z_i\mid L_{i-1})$
on $Z_i$ and at most $H(L_i\mid L_{i-1})+d_i+\eta/4$ on $L_i$.
Indistinguishability compares their averages over the uniform index $I$.

Let $\mathcal E_i(w)$ and $K_i=i(2^\ell-1)+1$ be as in
\Cref{lem:subexp-insertion-actions}, and set
\[
q=n2^\ell,\qquad M=\lceil32q/\eta\rceil,\qquad
\theta=2^{-\lceil\log(32/\eta)\rceil},\qquad
\Lambda=n\ell+\log(1/\theta).
\]
On input $(i,z,w)$, draw $M$ samples from $C_i(w)$ with independent
coins, also independent of the decomposition and auxiliary family.
Let $N_z$ count the samples equal to $z$. Define
\[
\widehat P_w(z)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{N_z+1}{M+K_i},&z\in\mathcal E_i(w),\\
0,&z\notin\mathcal E_i(w),
\end{cases}
\qquad
R_w(z)=(1-\theta)\widehat P_w(z)+\frac{\theta}{2^{i\ell}}.
\]
Since $\sum_{z\in\mathcal E_i(w)}N_z=M$, both $\widehat P_w$ and $R_w$
are probability distributions. The uniform term is needed because $Z_i$
need not belong to $\mathcal E_i(L_{i-1})$. For every length-$i$ string $z$,
\[
\frac{\theta}{2^{i\ell}}\leq R_w(z)\leq1,
\qquad 0\leq-\log R_w(z)\leq\Lambda.
\]

Set $b=\lceil\log(16\Lambda/\eta)\rceil$ and $\rho=2^{-b}$.
The circuit $D$ accepts with probability
\[
\rho\left\lfloor\frac{-\log R_w(z)}{\Lambda\rho}\right\rfloor.
\]
For each $i$, store the displayed floor for the $M+1$ valid-extension
counts $N_z=0,\ldots,M$ and for the single invalid-extension case.
This gives $n(M+2)$ entries, each an integer in $\{0,\ldots,2^b\}$
stored using $b+1$ bits. Draw a fresh uniform $b$-bit integer and accept
exactly when it is smaller than the stored integer.
The circuit contains $M$ copies of each $C_j$; it selects the count from
the branch $j=i$, whose copies receive $w$; the other branches receive
fixed strings of the required lengths. Membership in
$\mathcal E_i(w)$ is tested by deleting each position of $z$ and comparing
the resulting string with $w$. Selection, counting, these comparisons,
and table lookup require $\operatorname{poly}(n,M,\ell,b)$ additional gates.
Consequently, for a universal polynomial $A$,
\[
|D|\leq nMB+\operatorname{poly}(n,M,\ell,b)
\leq B A(n,2^\ell,1/\eta),
\]
where the last inequality uses $B\geq1$ and the choices of $M,b$.
Choose the polynomial $p$ in \Cref{thm:insertional-characterization}
large enough that
$p(n,2^\ell,1/\eta,\log(n/\sigma))\geq\max\{A(n,2^\ell,1/\eta),2\Lambda\}$.
The assumed size bound and $c_0\leq1$ give
\[
|D|\leq BA(n,2^\ell,1/\eta)\leq t^{c_0}\leq t,
\qquad n\Lambda\epsilon\leq\sigma/2,
\qquad \Lambda\rho\leq\eta/16.
\]

We first bound the expected negative logarithm on $L_i$.
For $w\in\mathsf{supp}(L_{i-1})$, apply
\Cref{claim:bound-expected-kl-laplace} on $\mathcal E_i(w)$ with
$P=C_i(w)$, $Q=L_i\mid_{L_{i-1}=w}$, and $M$ samples. Since
$R_w(z)\geq(1-\theta)\widehat P_w(z)$,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L_i\mid_{L_{i-1}=w} ~\|~ R_w \right) 
&\leq\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L_i\mid_{L_{i-1}=w} ~\|~ \widehat P_w \right) 
-\log(1-\theta)\\
&\leqD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L_i\mid_{L_{i-1}=w} ~\|~ C_i(w) \right) 
+\log\frac{M+K_i}{M+1}-\log(1-\theta).
\end{align*}
Here expectations are over the $M$ samples. Since $K_i\leq q$ and
$\theta\leq\eta/32$, the parameter choices give
\[
\log\frac{M+K_i}{M+1}-\log(1-\theta)
<\eta/4.
\]
Drawing the samples independently of $L_i$ conditional on $L_{i-1}$ and
applying \Cref{fact:kl-chain-rule} to $d_i$ gives
\begin{align*}
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[-\log R_{L_{i-1}}(L_i)]
&=H(L_i\mid L_{i-1})
+\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{w\sim L_{i-1}}\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L_i\mid_{L_{i-1}=w} ~\|~ R_w \right) \\
&\leq H(L_i\mid L_{i-1})+d_i+\eta/4.
\end{align*}

The same samples are independent of $Z_i$ conditional on $L_{i-1}$.
For every $w\in\mathsf{supp}(L_{i-1})$ and every value of $R_w$,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim Z_i\mid_{L_{i-1}=w}}[-\log R_w(z)]
&=H(Z_i\mid L_{i-1}=w)
+D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Z_i\mid_{L_{i-1}=w} ~\|~ R_w \right) \\
&\geq H(Z_i\mid L_{i-1}=w).
\end{align*}
For $V_i=L_i$ or $V_i=Z_i$, the rounding gives
\[
\left|\Lambda\Pr[D(i,V_i,L_{i-1})=1]
-\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[-\log R_{L_{i-1}}(V_i)]\right|\leq\Lambda\rho.
\]
Here the expectations include the fresh samples drawn by $D$.
Since $|D|\leq t$, indistinguishability gives
\[
\left|\sum_i\Pr[D(i,Z_i,L_{i-1})=1]
-\sum_i\Pr[D(i,L_i,L_{i-1})=1]\right|\leq n\epsilon.
\]
Combining these inequalities,
\begin{align*}
\sum_i H(L_i\mid L_{i-1})+\delta
&\leq\sum_i H(Z_i\mid L_{i-1})\\
&\leq\sum_i\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[-\log R_{L_{i-1}}(Z_i)]\\
&\leq\Lambda\sum_i\Pr[D(i,Z_i,L_{i-1})=1]+n\Lambda\rho\\
&\leq\Lambda\sum_i\Pr[D(i,L_i,L_{i-1})=1]
+n\Lambda\epsilon+n\Lambda\rho\\
&\leq\sum_i\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[-\log R_{L_{i-1}}(L_i)]
+n\Lambda\epsilon+2n\Lambda\rho\\
&\leq\sum_i H(L_i\mid L_{i-1})+d+n\eta/4
+n\Lambda\epsilon+2n\Lambda\rho\\
&\leq\sum_i H(L_i\mid L_{i-1})+d+\sigma/2+3n\eta/8.
\end{align*}
Subtracting $\sum_iH(L_i\mid L_{i-1})$ from both sides gives

\label{prop:insertionally-samplable-prg}
Let $G:\{0,1\}^t\to\{0,1\}^{2t^2}$ be a polynomial-time
pseudorandom generator secure against nonuniform polynomial-size
circuits. There exists a polynomial-time pseudorandom generator
$H:\{0,1\}^{3t^2}\to\{0,1\}^{4t^2}$ whose output distribution
admits an exact polynomial-size stateless insertional bit sampler.
<!-- end theorem-11 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
The sampler first generates bits from which it can recover a seed.
After using this seed, it inserts additional random bits so that the
final string conceals which bits were originally generated.

Define $H$ by the following process, with its seed consisting of
the $3t^2$ independent uniform bits used below.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Append $t^2$ independent uniform bits, obtaining
    $X=X_1\cdots X_{t^2}$. Partition $X$ into $t$ consecutive
    groups of $t$ bits. Define $s_j$ as the parity of the bits in group $j$,
    and put $s=s_1\cdots s_t$. 

    \item For $a=1,\ldots,2t^2$, read $X$ from the first $t^2$
    positions, recompute $s$, and append $G(s)_a$.
    (Following this process, the resulting string is $X\Vert G(s)$.)

    \item For $i=1,\ldots,t^2$, sample fresh independent uniform
    bits $b,r$. Insert $b$ immediately before $X_i$ if $r=0$,
    and immediately after it otherwise.
    Denote the resulting ordered pair by $Z_i$, and put
    $Z=Z_1\Vert\cdots\Vert Z_{t^2}$.
    The final output is $Z\Vert G(s)$.
\end{enumerate}

The process uses $t^2$ random bits in item~1 and $2t^2$ in item~3,
and outputs $4t^2$ bits. It is stateless: throughout item~2, $X$
occupies the first $t^2$ positions. After processing $i-1$ bits in
item~3, the current string is
$$
Z_1\Vert\cdots\Vert Z_{i-1}\Vert
X_i\cdots X_{t^2}\Vert G(s),
$$
so $X_i$ occupies position $2i-1$.
The current length determines the phase and index. Every transition
therefore uses only the current string and fresh coins, and has
polynomial-size circuits.

It remains to prove pseudorandomness. The pairs $Z_i$ are independent
and uniform over $\{00,01,10,11\}$, so $Z$ is uniform.
Conditioned on any $Z=z$, the original bits $X_i$ remain independent:
an equal pair determines its original bit, whereas an unequal pair
leaves its original bit uniform. Hence, if a group contains an
unequal pair, its parity is uniform given $Z=z$.

The probability that all $t$ pairs of a given group are equal is
$2^{-t}$. Except on an event of probability at most $t2^{-t}$,
every group therefore has an unequal pair, making all seed bits
independent and uniform given $Z$. Consequently,
$$
\Delta\bigl((Z,s),(U_{2t^2},U_t)\bigr)\leq t2^{-t},
$$
where the uniforms on the right are independent.
Applying $(z,s)\mapsto z\Vert G(s)$ shows that $H(U_{3t^2})$
is statistically close to $U_{2t^2}\Vert G(U_t)$.
By security of $G$, the latter is computationally indistinguishable
from $U_{4t^2}$, proving security of $H$.

There exists a distribution $L$ over $\{0,1\}^{2n}$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Every stateless sequential sampler of size $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(n)$ satisfies $D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^{2n}) \right) >\log(n)$, and
    \item There is a $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(n)$-size insertional sampler that samples $L$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
We set $L$ to be the distribution $L=(f(X),X)$ where $X$ is uniform over $\{0,1\}^{n}$. 

We now prove the first item. Suppose towards contradiction that for some constant $c>0$ and infinitely many
$n$, a size-$n^c$ stateless sequential sampler $C$
satisfies $D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^{2n}) \right) \leq\log n$. We show that this invalidates security of the one-way function.
Given $y=f(x)$ the inverter $I$ runs $C$ sequentially on prefix $y$ and outputs the $n$-bit suffix generated by $C$.

For $X\gets U_n$, put $Y=f(X)$ and write $(V,W)\gets C(1^{2n})$,
where $V,W\in\{0,1\}^n$. Since $C$ is stateless, $W\mid_{V=y}$ is
distributed as $I(y)$ for every $y\in\mathsf{supp}(V)$. The assumed finite
KL-divergence ensures that $\mathsf{supp}(Y)\subseteq\mathsf{supp}(V)$.
Applying the KL chain rule (\Cref{fact:kl-chain-rule}) to both pairs gives
\begin{align*}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (Y,X) ~\|~ (Y,I(Y)) \right) 
&=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{y\gets Y}\left[D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( X\mid_{Y=y} ~\|~ I(y) \right) \right]\\
&=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^{2n}) \right) -D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Y ~\|~ V \right) 
\leq\log n,
\end{align*}
where the inequality uses nonnegativity of KL-divergence.

Let $p=\Pr[f(I(Y))=Y]$ be the inverter's success probability.
The test $T(y,x')=\mathds{1}[f(x')=y]$ maps $(Y,X)$ to the constant
$1$ and $(Y,I(Y))$ to a Bernoulli random variable with parameter $p$.
Thus data processing (\Cref{fact:DPI}) gives
\begin{align*}
\log(1/p)
&=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( \operatorname{Ber}(1) ~\|~ \operatorname{Ber}(p) \right) \\
&\leqD_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (Y,X) ~\|~ (Y,I(Y)) \right) \leq\log n.
\end{align*}
Hence the polynomial-size inverter $I$ succeeds with probability
$p\geq1/n$ for infinitely many $n$, contradicting one-wayness.

We now show the second item by describing an insertional sampler for $L$:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item For the first $n$ steps, append independent uniform bits,
    obtaining $x\gets U_{n}$.
    \item At step $n+j$, for $j\in[n]$, read $x$ from the input, compute $f(x)$ and output $(j, f(x)_{j})$ (i.e., place the $j$-th bit of $f(x)$ at index $j$.
\end{enumerate}
That this insertional sampler samples $L$ exactly and is efficient is immediate by construction.

<!-- theorem-12: body_md -->
\label{thm:sm-insertional-separation}
Let $(f_\lambda)_{\lambda\geq1}$ be a length-preserving one-way
function family with $2^{\gamma\lambda^\alpha}$ security against
circuits, for constants $\gamma>0$ and $1/2<\alpha<1$.
For $t\geq2$, let $\ell=2t+\lceil\log(2t)\rceil$.
There exists a distribution $L=L_t$ over $(\{0,1\}^\ell)^{2t}$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
	 \item For every constant $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $t$,
	every stateless insertional sampler $C$ of size at most $2^{ct}$
	satisfies $D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^{2t}) \right) \geq\gamma t^{2\alpha}-2t$.
    \item There is a $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(t)$-size randomized circuit that samples $L$ exactly.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-12 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
We first define $L$. Let $q=2^t$, fix distinct
$\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_{2t}\in\mathbb F_q$, and identify
$\mathbb F_q^t$ with $\{0,1\}^{t^2}$. For $x,r,y\in\mathbb F_q^t$, put
$P_{x,r}(z)=\sum_{j<t}r_jz^j+\sum_{j<t}x_jz^{t+j}$ and
$Q_y(z)=\sum_{j<t}y_jz^j$.
The distribution $L$ is sampled as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Sample independent uniform $X,R\gets\mathbb F_q^t$ and
    compute $Y=f_{t^2}(X)$.
    \item Output $((i,P_{X,R}(\beta_i),Q_Y(\beta_i)))_{i=1}^{2t}$,
    treating each triple as one $\ell$-bit token.
\end{enumerate}
The evaluations of $P_{X,R}$ form a ramp secret-sharing of $X$:
any fixed $t$ shares are uniform and independent of $X$, since the
Vandermonde matrix acting on $R$ is invertible, while all $2t$ shares
recover $X,R$ by interpolation. Intermediate numbers of shares may
reveal partial information about $X$. In contrast, any $t$ evaluations
of $Q_Y$ determine $Y$. Thus we can sample any fixed $t$ tokens given
only the image, but completing them to a valid output reveals a preimage.

Observe that the description of the distribution defines a circuit for sampling from it. This sampler runs in $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(t)$ time, proving the second item. Its alphabet is $[2t]\times\mathbb F_q^2$, which has size $2t\,2^{2t}$.




We now prove the first item. Suppose towards contradiction that for
some constant $c>0$ and infinitely many $t$, a stateless insertional
sampler $C$ of size at most $2^{ct}$ has KL error
$d:=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L ~\|~ C(1^{2t}) \right) $ smaller than $\gamma t^{2\alpha}-2t$.
We show that this invalidates security of the one-way function.

Let $\mathcal S=\mathsf{supp}(L)$ and observe that membership in $\mathcal S$ can be efficiently tested by interpolating to derive $X$ and then checking whether the third entries in the output are consistent with $f(X)$.
We now describe the inverter. Let $b=\lceil\log\binom{2t}{t}\rceil\leq2t$.
Given $y=f_{t^2}(x)$, the inverter $I$ proceeds as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Choose a $t$-element subset $J\subseteq[2t]$ by reducing
    a uniform $b$-bit integer modulo $\binom{2t}{t}$ and taking the
    corresponding subset in lexicographic order.
    \item Sample independent uniform $u_j\gets\mathbb F_q$ for $j\in J$.
    Set $W_t=((j,u_j,Q_y(\beta_j)))_{j\in J}$ in increasing tag order.
    \item For $i=t+1,\ldots,2t$, sample $W_i\gets C_i(W_{i-1})$
    using fresh coins.
    \item If $W_{2t}\in\mathcal S$, output the preimage recovered by interpolating the entries that encode $x$; otherwise output $\bot$.
\end{enumerate}
On acceptance, the preserved $t$ evaluations of $Q_y$ agree with
those of $Q_{f_{t^2}(x')}$ for the recovered $x'$. Both polynomials
have degree less than $t$, so $f_{t^2}(x')=y$.
The inverter has size $2^{O(t)}$: it makes $t$ calls to $C$ and
otherwise uses $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(t)$ computation.

It remains to lower-bound its success probability. Below, the strings
$W_t,\ldots,W_{2t}$ are generated on a random challenge
$Y=f_{t^2}(X)$ with $X\gets U_{t^2}$.
By \Cref{lem:insertional-path-kl}, there is a Markov insertional
decomposition $(L_0,\ldots,L_{2t})$ of $L$ such that, writing
$d_i=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (L_{i-1},L_i) ~\|~ (L_{i-1},C_i(L_{i-1})) \right) $,
we have $\sum_{i=1}^{2t}d_i=d$.

For a fixed set $J$ of $t$ tags, write $L|_J$ for the restriction
of $L$ to those tags. The secret-sharing property implies that
$W_t$, conditioned on choosing $J$, is distributed as $L|_J$.
Moreover, each $J$ is chosen with probability at least $2^{-b}$.
If $w$ has tag set $J$, the event $L_t=w$ implies $L|_J=w$.
Consequently, for every $w\in\mathsf{supp}(L_t)$,
\[
\Pr[L_t=w]\leq\Pr[L|_J=w]\leq2^b\Pr[W_t=w],
\]
and hence $D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L_t ~\|~ W_t \right) \leq b$. This comparison allows the
indices in $L_t$ to depend on the final string; it uses secrecy only
for each fixed $J$.

Since $C$ is stateless, the inverter continues from $W_t$ using only
the current string. The KL chain rule (\Cref{fact:kl-chain-rule}) and
nonnegativity of the $d_i$ therefore give
\begin{equation*}
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (L_t,\ldots,L_{2t}) ~\|~ (W_t,\ldots,W_{2t}) \right) 
=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( L_t ~\|~ W_t \right) +\sum_{i=t+1}^{2t}d_i\leq b+d.
\end{equation*}
Let $p=\Pr[W_{2t}\in\mathcal S]$ be the inverter's success probability.
The support test accepts $L_{2t}$ with probability one and $W_{2t}$
with probability $p$. Thus data processing (\Cref{fact:DPI}) gives
\begin{equation*}
\log(1/p)
=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( \operatorname{Ber}(1) ~\|~ \operatorname{Ber}(p) \right) 
\leq b+d<\gamma t^{2\alpha}.
\end{equation*}
Hence $p>2^{-\gamma t^{2\alpha}}$. Since $2\alpha>1$, the inverter's
size $2^{O(t)}$ is at most $2^{\gamma t^{2\alpha}}$ for all sufficiently
large $t$. This contradicts the assumed security at input length
$\lambda=t^2$ for infinitely many $t$, proving the second item.

Let $(f_n)_{n\geq1}$ be a length-preserving one-way function family
secure against polynomial-size circuits. For $X\gets U_n$, let
$L=(f_n(X),X)$, viewed as $2n$ binary tokens.
For every sufficiently large constant $c$ and all sufficiently large $n$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The $(n^c,n^{-c})$ insertional pseudoentropy gap of $L$
    is at most $1/n$.
    \item The $(n^c,n^{-c})$ next-block pseudoentropy gap of $L$

\end{enumerate}

\Cref{thm:insertional-vs-sequential}, and let $(L_0,\ldots,L_{2n})$
be its intermediate strings. Write $A_i(w)$ for the possible successors
of $w$: for $i\leq n$, these are $w0,w1$; for $i>n$, there is only
the prescribed insertion of an image bit. A circuit of size $T_0(n)$
checks whether $z\in A_i(w)$, for some polynomial $T_0$.

&\leq h(a)+\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}[\log|A_I(L_{I-1})|]+2na\\
&\leq h(\varepsilon)+\tfrac12+2n\varepsilon.
\end{align*}

sampler of size $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(n^c,2n,2,n^c)=\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(n)$ and KL error at most

\label{cor:insertional-vs-ordinary-pseudoentropy}
Let $(f_\lambda)_{\lambda\geq1}$ be a length-preserving one-way
function family with $2^{\gamma\lambda^\alpha}$ security against
circuits, for constants $\gamma>0$ and $1/2<\alpha<1$.
For $t\geq2$, let $\ell=2t+\lceil\log(2t)\rceil$.
There is a distribution $L=L_t$ over $(\{0,1\}^\ell)^{2t}$, samplable
in $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(t)$ time, such that for every sufficiently large constant $c$
and all sufficiently large $t$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The ordinary $(t^c,t^{-c})$ pseudoentropy gap of $L$
    is at most $1/t$.
    \item The $(t^c,t^{-c})$ insertional pseudoentropy gap of $L$
    is at least $\tfrac\gamma2 t^{2\alpha}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-5 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
Use the distribution constructed in \Cref{thm:sm-insertional-separation}.
Every output determines the independent uniform $X,R$, so $L$ is
uniform on its support $\mathcal S$ of size $2^{2t^2}$ and $H(L)=2t^2$.
Let $T_0(t)$ be a polynomial bound on the size of the support test in
that proof. Fix $c\geq4$ large enough that $T_0(t)\leq t^c$ for all
sufficiently large $t$, and set $\varepsilon=t^{-c}$.
If $V$ is $(t^c,\varepsilon)$-indistinguishable from $L$,
then $a=\Pr[V\notin\mathcal S]\leq\varepsilon$.
Conditioning on membership in $\mathcal S$ gives
\[
H(V)\leq h(a)+(1-a)2t^2+a(2t\ell)
\leq H(L)+h(\varepsilon)+2t\ell\varepsilon,
\]
so the ordinary pseudoentropy gap is at most
$h(\varepsilon)+2t\ell\varepsilon=O(t^{2-c})\leq1/t$ for all
sufficiently large $t$, proving the first item.

For the second item, suppose towards contradiction that for some
$c>0$ and infinitely many $t$, the $(t^c,t^{-c})$ insertional
pseudoentropy gap is less than $\tfrac\gamma2 t^{2\alpha}$.
Set $\delta=\tfrac\gamma2 t^{2\alpha}+t^{-c}$, so the insertional
pseudoentropy is at most $H(L)+\delta-t^{-c}$.
Part 2 of \Cref{thm:insertional-characterization} gives a stateless
insertional sampler of size
$\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(t^c,2t,2^\ell,t^c)=2^{O(t)}$ and KL error at most
$\delta$. Since $2\alpha>1$, for all sufficiently large $t$ we have
$\delta<\gamma t^{2\alpha}-2t$, contradicting
\Cref{thm:sm-insertional-separation}.

\label{cor:stateless-general-consequence}
Let $(W,Y)$ be jointly distributed and indexed by $n$. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item $W$ has polynomial length, and $Y$ consists of $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(n)$ items over a $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(n)$-size alphabet.
    \item $Y$ has a noticeable nonuniform next-block pseudoentropy
    gap given $W$.
\end{enumerate}
Then no sequential sampler $C$ with $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(n)$-size circuits and
$O(\log n)$ private bits satisfies
\[
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( (W,Y) ~\|~ (W,C(W)) \right) =\mathsf{negl}(n),
\]
even given $W$ and the entire visible prefix at every step.
<!-- end corollary-6 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
Suppose towards contradiction that such a sampler $C$ exists, and
write $k=O(\log n)$ for its state length. Let $N\geq1$ be the number
of tokens of $Y$, each of length $\ell$. Put
$P=(W,Y)$, $Q=(W,C(W))$, and $e=D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P ~\|~ Q \right) =\mathsf{negl}(n)$.
Choose $d>0$ so that the pseudoentropy gap is at least $n^{-d}$
for all sufficiently large $n$, and set $\rho=n^{-d}/8$.
We remove the private state, control the resulting error, and then
apply the stateless characterization.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Remove the private state.}
    Apply \Cref{thm:state-elimination-KL} with accuracy $\rho$.
    Its construction allows visible input $W$: use the same $w$ in
    every simulated invocation of $C$, and average the KL bound over
    $W$. The resulting stateless sampler $D$ has polynomial size,
    since $2^k,2^\ell,1/\rho,N,|W|$ are polynomially bounded.
    Writing $R=(W,D(W))$, we have $D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Q ~\|~ R \right) \leq\rho$.

    \item \emph{Compare with the target distribution.}
    Both $C$ and $D$ use at most $r=\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(n)$ fair random bits
    over the entire run. Thus every positive conditional output
    probability, given $W=w$, is at least $2^{-r}$.
    Finite KL-divergence gives
    $\mathsf{supp}(P)\subseteq\mathsf{supp}(Q)\subseteq\mathsf{supp}(R)$.
    On $\mathsf{supp}(Q)$, put
    $F(w,y)=\log(\Pr[C(w)=y]/\Pr[D(w)=y])$, and set $F=0$ elsewhere.
    Then $|F|\leq r$.
    By \Cref{fact:pinsker}, $\nu:=\Delta(P,Q)=\mathsf{negl}(n)$.
    Since all three distributions have the same $W$ marginal,
    \begin{align*}
    D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P ~\|~ R \right) 
    &=e+D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( Q ~\|~ R \right) +\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_P F-\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_Q F\\
    &\leq e+\rho+2r\nu
    =\rho+\mathsf{negl}(n)\leq2\rho
    \end{align*}
    for all sufficiently large $n$.

    \item \emph{Apply the pseudoentropy lower bound.}
    Part 1 of \Cref{thm:sequential-characterization} allows visible
    input $W$: replace $(I,Y_{<I})$ by $(W,I,Y_{<I})$, condition the
    entropies on $W$, and include $|W|$ in the polynomial size bound.
    Set $\delta=4\rho$, $\sigma=\rho$, $\eta=\rho/N$,
    $t=n^c$, and $\epsilon=n^{-c}$.
    The denominator in the size bound is polynomial in $n$
    independently of $c$, while the numerator is
    $\min\{n^{cc_0},\rho n^c/N\}$.
    Choose $c$ large enough to cover the size of $D$ and ensure
    $n^{-c}\leq\rho$. The pseudoentropy hypothesis then gives
    $(n^c,n^{-c})$ next-block pseudoentropy given $W$
    at least $H(Y\mid W)+8\rho-n^{-c}\geq H(Y\mid W)+4\rho$.
    The theorem yields $D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( P ~\|~ R \right) >4\rho-\rho-N(\rho/N)=2\rho$,
    contradicting the preceding bound.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- corollary-7: body_md -->
\label{cor:no-stateless-prg}
Let $G:\{0,1\}^{k(n)}\to\{0,1\}^n$ be a pseudorandom generator secure against
nonuniform polynomial-size circuits. No sequential bit sampler $C$ with $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(n)$-size circuits and $O(\log n)$ private bits satisfies
\[
D_{\mathsf{KL}} \left( G(U_{k(n)}) ~\|~ C(1^n) \right) =\mathsf{negl}(n).
\]
The same holds for any apriori fixed ordering, rather than just left-to-right.

<!-- corollary-8: body_md -->
\label{thm:no-stateless-pke}
Let a polynomial-time public-key encryption scheme have security
parameter $n$. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item IND-CPA security against nonuniform polynomial-size circuits
    and negligible correctness error.
    \item Ciphertexts consist of a fixed polynomially bounded number
    of $O(\log n)$-bit tokens.
\end{enumerate}
For any two distinct equal-length messages of polynomial length, their
ciphertext distributions cannot both be sampled with negligible
KL-divergence by sequential samplers with $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(n)$-size circuits and
$O(\log n)$ private bits.
The public key, message, and visible prefix are available at every step;
divergence is measured jointly with the public key.

<!-- corollary-9: body_md -->
\label{cor:no-stateless-steganography}
Let a public-key steganography scheme run in polynomial time, with
security parameter $n$. Fix two distinct messages of polynomial length.
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Negligible decoding error; both encodings consist of the same
    fixed polynomially bounded number of $O(\log n)$-bit tokens.
    \item Both encodings are computationally indistinguishable from the
    same cover distribution, jointly with the public key, against
    nonuniform polynomial-size circuits.
\end{enumerate}
The two encoding distributions cannot both be sampled with negligible
KL-divergence by sequential samplers with $\operatorname{\mathsf{poly}}(n)$-size circuits and
$O(\log n)$ private bits.
The public key, message, and visible prefix are available at every step;
divergence is measured jointly with the public key.

<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
For the PRG, we describe it for left-to-right ordering, but the same argument holds for any fixed known ordering up to applying permutation. Put $Y=G(U_{k(n)})$. For uniform $I\in[n]$ and an
independent uniform bit $B$, the distributions $(I,Y_{<I},Y_I)$ and
$(I,Y_{<I},B)$ are computationally indistinguishable: replacing $Y$
by $U_n$ on both sides makes them identical. Thus $Y$ has next-bit
pseudoentropy $n$, whereas $H(Y)\leq k(n)<n$.
\Cref{cor:stateless-general-consequence} applies with constant visible input.

For the remaining claims, let $P_b=(W,Y^b)$ be the two encoding
distributions, including their common public key or setup $W$.
Write $N$ for the number of tokens and $u=2^\ell$ for the alphabet size,
where $\ell$ is the token length.
They are computationally indistinguishable and satisfy
$\Delta(P_0,P_1)\geq1-\mathsf{negl}(n)$.
For encryption and steganography, correctness gives the distance bound:
appending the secret key preserves distance, since it is independent of
the encoding given the public key. Steganographic security gives
indistinguishability through the common cover. 

Suppose towards contradiction that both samplers exist. We distinguish
$P_0$ from $P_1$ in three steps.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Remove the private state.}
    Put $\alpha=1/(100N)$. Apply \Cref{thm:state-elimination-KL}
    with KL error $\alpha^2/4$, keeping $W$ visible.
    By \Cref{fact:pinsker} and the assumed negligible sampling error,
    the resulting polynomial-size stateless samplers $D^b$ satisfy
    $\Delta(P_b,R_b)\leq\alpha$, where $R_b=(W,D^b(W))$.
    Hence $\Delta(R_0,R_1)\geq1/3$ for all sufficiently large $n$.

    \item \emph{Find a differing transition.}
    Replacing the circuits of $D^0$ by those of $D^1$ one at a time
    gives an index $i$ such that, for $X=(W,D^0(W)_{<i})$ and
    $A_b\gets D_i^b(X)$ with fresh coins,
    $d:=\Delta((X,A_0),(X,A_1))\geq1/(3N)$.
    Each hybrid uses the message-$0$ prefix and a common message-$1$
    suffix, which cannot increase distance.
    Yet every polynomial-size test distinguishes these pairs with
    advantage at most $4\alpha+\mathsf{negl}(n)$: replacing the
    $R_0$ prefix by the $R_1$ prefix through $P_0,P_1$ before invoking
    $D_i^1$ costs
    $2\alpha+\mathsf{negl}(n)$, and comparing the two honest prefixes
    including token $i$ costs the same amount.

    \item \emph{Test the transition probabilities.}
    On input $(x,a)$, draw $M=\lceil576uN^2\rceil$ samples from each
    $D_i^b(x)$, and accept if $a$ occurs more often in the first batch.
    Write $p_b$ for the true token distribution and $\widehat p_b$
    for its empirical distribution. By Cauchy--Schwarz,
    $\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat p_b-p_b\|_1\leq\sqrt{u/M}\leq1/(24N)$.
    Choosing the larger empirical probability loses at most
    $\|\widehat p_0-p_0\|_1+\|\widehat p_1-p_1\|_1$ relative to
    the optimal token test. Averaging over $X$, our test therefore has
    advantage at least $d-1/(12N)\geq1/(4N)$, contradicting the
    preceding upper bound.
\end{enumerate}
The index $i$ is nonuniform advice, and all circuits have polynomial size.
The test's random coins can be fixed by averaging.

The transition-testing argument also gives an alternative proof of the
PRG claim. Take $W$ constant, $N=n$, $\ell=1$, and $u=2$, with
$P_0=G(U_{k(n)})$ and $P_1=U_n$. These distributions are computationally
indistinguishable, while the support-size bound gives
\[
\Delta(P_0,P_1)\geq1-2^{k(n)-n}\geq1/2.
\]
The sampler for $P_1$ draws independent uniform bits exactly, so a
hypothesized sampler for $P_0$ supplies both samplers needed above.
With $\alpha=1/(100n)$, the first step gives
$\Delta(R_0,R_1)\geq1/2-2\alpha\geq1/3$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
The remaining two steps, applied to this pair of distributions, yield
the same contradiction. As above, any fixed known ordering is handled
by permuting the coordinates.
