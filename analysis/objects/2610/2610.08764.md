\label{thm:intro}
Consider the plant \eqref{eq:plant} with $\lambda\in W^{1,\infty}(0,1)$ under the feedback laws
\begin{align}
U_1(t)=\varepsilon\int_0^1e^{-\theta y}\,u(y,t)\,\mathrm d y,\qquad
U_2(t)=\int_0^1h(y)\,u(y,t)\,\mathrm d y,\label{eq:intro-laws}
\end{align}
with the gain
\begin{align}
h(y)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{a+\tilde\kappa_n}{\tilde\chi_n'(0)}\,\tilde\chi_n(y),\label{eq:intro-gain}
\end{align}
where $\tilde\mu_n$, $n\ge1$, are the eigenvalues, with eigenfunctions $\tilde\chi_n$, of
\begin{align}
-\tilde\chi''''-(\lambda\tilde\chi')'-\varepsilon\big(\tilde\chi'''(0)+\lambda(0)\tilde\chi'(0)\big)e^{-\theta x}=\tilde\mu\,\tilde\chi,\qquad
\tilde\chi(0)=\tilde\chi''(0)=\tilde\chi(1)=\tilde\chi''(1)=0,\label{eq:intro-eig}
\end{align}
and $(\tilde\kappa_n)$ is the bounded solution of
\begin{align}
\tilde\kappa_m=-a\sum_{n\ne m}\frac{a+\tilde\kappa_n}{\tilde\mu_n-\tilde\mu_m+a},\qquad m\ge1.\label{eq:intro-kappa}
\end{align}
There exist a discrete set $\mathscr T_\lambda\subset(0,\infty)$ and a positive function $\varepsilon_1$ on $(0,\infty)\setminus\mathscr T_\lambda$ such that, for all $\theta\notin\mathscr T_\lambda$, $0<|\varepsilon|<\varepsilon_1(\theta)$, and $a>\max\{0,\max_n\operatorname{Re}\tilde\mu_n\}$ with $a\notin\{\tilde\mu_i-\tilde\mu_j:\ i,j\ge1\}$, every $\tilde\mu_n$ is algebraically simple, $\tilde\chi_n'(0)\ne0$, the bounded solution $(\tilde\kappa_n)$ of \eqref{eq:intro-kappa} exists and is unique, the series \eqref{eq:intro-gain} converges in $L^2(0,1)$ to a real-valued function, and, for every $\omega\in\big(0,\,a-\max_n\operatorname{Re}\tilde\mu_n\big)$, there exist $\rho_0,C,\epsilon^*>0$ such that, for every $u_0\inL^2(0,1)$ with $\|u_0\|\le\rho_0$, the closed-loop system \eqref{eq:plant}, \eqref{eq:intro-laws}, with $h$ in $U_2$ defined by \eqref{eq:intro-gain} or replaced by any $\hat h\inL^2(0,1)$ satisfying $\|\hat h-h\|\le\epsilon^*$, has a unique mild solution $u\in C([0,\infty);L^2(0,1))\cap L^2_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);H^2(0,1))$ satisfying
\begin{align}
\|u(t)\|\le C\,e^{-\omega t}\,\|u_0\|,\qquad t\ge0.\label{eq:intro-decay}
\end{align}

\label{thm:control}
Let $\lambda\in W^{1,\infty}(0,1)$ and $T_c>0$. For the linearization of \eqref{eq:plant} with the two boundary inputs $U_1=u(0,\cdot)$ and $U_2=u_{xx}(0,\cdot)$, started from $u(\cdot,0)=0$, the set of terminal states \eqref{eq:reachable} reached by controls $(U_1,U_2)\in C_c^\infty((0,T_c);\mathbb R^2)$ is dense in $L^2(0,1)$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By duality, it suffices to show that an adjoint solution with both boundary observations identically zero must have zero initial state.
Let $\mathcal R(T_c)$ be the linear space of terminal states in
\eqref{eq:reachable}. Take $z_0\in\mathcal R(T_c)^\perp$ and set
$z(t)=e^{tA_{\lambda}}z_0$. Self-adjointness and \eqref{eq:reachable} give
\begin{align}
0=\langle u(T_c),z_0\rangle
&=\;\int_0^{T_c}\big[U_1(s)\langle B_1,z(T_c-s)\rangle
+U_2(s)\langle B_2,z(T_c-s)\rangle\big]\,\mathrm d s.\label{eq:boundary-linear-proof}
\end{align}
For $t>0$, parabolic smoothing gives $z(t)\in D(A_{\lambda})$, so both boundary
observations are well defined and continuous. Since the controls are
arbitrary, \eqref{eq:b1}--\eqref{eq:b2} imply
\begin{align}
z_x(0,t)&=\;z^{[3]}(0,t)=0,\qquad 0<t<T_c.\label{eq:obs}
\end{align}

To recover the traces of each spectral projection from \eqref{eq:obs},
we first bound their growth with the eigenvalue. The trace inequality
\cite[Sec.~5.5, Thm.~1]{Evans} and the elliptic estimate give,
for $\phi\in D(A_{\lambda})$,
\begin{align}
|\phi'(0)|+|\phi^{[3]}(0)|&\le\;C_\lambda\|\phi\|_{H^4},\label{eq:control-trace}\\
\|\phi\|_{H^4}&\le\;C_\lambda\big(\|A_{\lambda}\phi\|+\|\phi\|\big),
\label{eq:control-elliptic}
\end{align}
with $C_\lambda$ depending only on $\|\lambda\|_{W^{1,\infty}}$.
For the second estimate, the sine expansion gives
$\|\phi\|_{H^4}\le C\|A_0\phi\|$. Writing $A_0=A_{\lambda}-P$ and
using \eqref{eq:relbound} with $\epsilon=1/2$ absorbs the perturbation
term and proves \eqref{eq:control-elliptic}.
Applying these estimates to $P_\mu z_0$ yields, after enlarging $C_\lambda$,
\begin{align}
|(P_\mu z_0)'(0)|+|(P_\mu z_0)^{[3]}(0)|&\le\;C_\lambda(1+|\mu|)\|P_\mu z_0\|.\label{eq:app-b-display-016}
\end{align}

Now expand $z(t)=\sum_\mu e^{\mu t}P_\mu z_0$ over the distinct
eigenvalues. Repeated eigenvalues are grouped in a single projection,
so no simplicity assumption is needed. Equation~\eqref{eq:obs} becomes
\begin{align}
\sum_\mu e^{\mu t}(P_\mu z_0)'(0)=0,\qquad
\sum_\mu e^{\mu t}(P_\mu z_0)^{[3]}(0)=0,
\quad 0<t<T_c.\label{eq:boundary-series}
\end{align}
The coefficient bound \eqref{eq:app-b-display-016} and quartic spectral
decay \eqref{eq:mu-asymp} give locally uniform convergence for
$\operatorname{Re}t>0$. These series are therefore analytic, and their
vanishing on $(0,T_c)$ extends to all $t>0$.
Dividing by $e^{\mu^\star t}$ for the largest eigenvalue $\mu^\star$
and letting $t\to\infty$ isolates the two traces of $P_{\mu^\star}z_0$.
Dominated convergence shows that both vanish. Removing this term and
repeating gives $(P_\mu z_0)'(0)=(P_\mu z_0)^{[3]}(0)=0$ for every $\mu$.

Each $\phi=P_\mu z_0$ thus has four zero Cauchy data at the left endpoint:
the hinged conditions give $\phi(0)=\phi''(0)=0$, and the two observations
give $\phi'(0)=\phi^{[3]}(0)=0$.
Writing $y=(\phi,\phi',\phi'',\phi^{[3]})^{\mathsf T}$ turns its
eigenvalue equation into
\begin{align}
y_1'&=\;y_2,\qquad y_2'=y_3,\qquad
y_3'=y_4-\lambda y_2,\qquad y_4'=-\mu y_1,
\qquad y(0)=0.\label{eq:app-b-display-002}
\end{align}
Uniqueness for this linear system with $L^\infty$ coefficients gives
$y\equiv0$. Hence every spectral projection $P_\mu z_0$ vanishes,
so $z_0=0$ and $\overline{\mathcal R(T_c)}=L^2(0,1)$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:plant-spectral}
Let $\lambda\in W^{1,\infty}(0,1)$, set $N_1:=\max\{3,\lceil2\|\lambda\|_{L^\infty}/\pi^2\rceil\}$ and $s_n(x):=\sqrt2\sin(n\pi x)$, and normalize $\varphi_n$ by $\langle \varphi_n,s_n\rangle>0$ for $n\ge N_1$. Then, the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For every eigenvalue $\mu$ of $A_{\lambda}$, the eigenspace $E_\mu$ has dimension at most two.
\item For $\phi \in E_\mu$, the linear map $\phi\mapsto\big(\phi'(0),\phi^{[3]}(0)\big)\in\mathbb R^2$ is injective.
In particular, for a double eigenvalue with eigenfunctions $\varphi_j,\varphi_k$ forming a basis of $E_\mu$, the matrix
$\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_j&\alpha_k\\\beta_j&\beta_k\end{pmatrix}$ is nonsingular.
\item With constants depending only on $\|\lambda\|_{W^{1,\infty}}$,
\begin{gather}
\|\varphi_n-s_n\|_{H^s(0,1)}\le C\,n^{s-1}\quad(0\le s\le4),\qquad n\ge N_1,\\
\|\varphi_n^{(j)}-s_n^{(j)}\|_{L^\infty}\le C\,n^{j-1/2}\quad(j=0,1,2,3),\qquad n\ge N_1 ,
\end{gather}
and consequently $\beta_n=\sqrt2\,n\pi+O(n^{1/2})$, $\alpha_n=-\sqrt2(n\pi)^3+O(n^{5/2})$. In particular, after enlarging $N_1$, there are $c_0,C_0>0$ and $N_1$, depending only on $\|\lambda\|_{W^{1,\infty}}$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-bounds}
|\beta_n|\le C_0 n,\quad |\alpha_n|\le C_0n^3\ \ (n\ge1),\qquad c_0n\le|\beta_n|,\quad c_0n^3\le|\alpha_n|\ \ (n\ge N_1).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We prove (i) and (ii) together. For $\phi\in E_\mu$, the hinged conditions give
$\phi(0)=\phi''(0)=0$. The ODE uniqueness argument in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:control} shows that the remaining Cauchy data
$\phi'(0)$ and $\phi^{[3]}(0)$ uniquely determine $\phi$. Hence
$\dim E_\mu\le2$, proving (i), and the trace map is injective, proving (ii).

We next prove (iii). Here $C>0$ may vary but depends only on $\|\lambda\|_{W^{1,\infty}(0,1)}$, not on mode indices.

Use the decomposition $A_{\lambda}=A_0+P$ from
\eqref{eq:A0P}. Expanding $P\phi=-(\lambda\phi')'$ and applying
H\"older's inequality gives
\begin{align}
\|P\phi\|&\le\;C\|\lambda\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|\phi\|_{H^2},\label{eq:app-d-display-003}
\end{align}

We will need to bound the difference between eigenvalues.
For $n\ge N_1$ and $m\ne n$, set $p=\min\{m,n\}$ and
$q=\max\{m,n\}$. The min--max lower bound
$\mu_p\ge-p^4\pi^4-\|\lambda\|_{L^\infty}p^2\pi^2$ holds for every $p$.
Combining it with the upper bound in \eqref{eq:mu-asymp} at $q\ge N_1$
and using the eigenvalue ordering gives
\begin{align}
|\mu_m-\mu_n|
&\ge\;\pi^4|m^4-n^4|-\|\lambda\|_{L^\infty}\pi^2(m^2+n^2).\label{eq:app-d-display-080}
\end{align}
Using $n\ge N_1\ge 2\|\lambda\|_{L^\infty}/\pi^2$, we obtain, for $m\ne n$,
\begin{align}
|m^2-n^2|&\ge2n-1\ge2\|\lambda\|_{L^\infty}/\pi^2.\label{eq:app-d-display-001}
\end{align}
Factoring $|m^4-n^4|=|m^2-n^2|(m^2+n^2)$ therefore gives
\begin{align}
|\mu_m-\mu_n|&\ge\;\tfrac12\pi^4|m^4-n^4|,
\qquad n\ge N_1,\quad m\ne n.\label{eq:gap}
\end{align}
Taking the minimum over $m\ne n$ and using
$n^4-(n-1)^4\ge2n^3$ for $n\ge3$ gives
$\mathfrak g_n\ge\pi^4n^3$. In particular, $\mu_n$ is simple for $n\ge N_1$.

Since $A_0$ has eigenpairs $(-n^4\pi^4,s_n)$,
\eqref{eq:mu-asymp} and \eqref{eq:app-d-display-003} give the residual bound
\begin{align}
\|(A_{\lambda}-\mu_n)s_n\|
&\le\;\|Ps_n\|+|\mu_n+n^4\pi^4|
\le Cn^2.\label{eq:app-d-display-004}
\end{align}
Expand $s_n=\sum_k\langle s_n,\varphi_k\rangle\varphi_k$ in the orthonormal
plant eigenbasis. Applying $A_{\lambda}-\mu_n$ removes its component along
$\varphi_n$, and orthogonality with the spectral gap gives
\begin{align}
(A_{\lambda}-\mu_n)s_n&=\;\sum_{k\ne n}(\mu_k-\mu_n)
\langle s_n,\varphi_k\rangle\varphi_k,\label{eq:sine-residual-expansion}\\
\|(A_{\lambda}-\mu_n)s_n\|^2
&=\;\sum_{k\ne n}|\mu_k-\mu_n|^2|\langle s_n,\varphi_k\rangle|^2
\ge\;\mathfrak g_n^2\sum_{k\ne n}|\langle s_n,\varphi_k\rangle|^2.
\label{eq:sine-residual-gap}
\end{align}
The sum on the right is the squared norm of the component of $s_n$
orthogonal to $\varphi_n$. Taking square roots, using the residual bound
\eqref{eq:app-d-display-004} and $\mathfrak g_n\ge\pi^4n^3$, gives
\begin{align}
\|s_n-\langle s_n,\varphi_n\rangle\varphi_n\|
&\le\;\frac{\|(A_{\lambda}-\mu_n)s_n\|}{\mathfrak g_n}
\le\frac{Cn^2}{\pi^4n^3}\le Cn^{-1}.
\label{eq:sine-projection-error}
\end{align}
Since $\|s_n\|=1$, orthogonality also gives
\begin{align}
|\langle s_n,\varphi_n\rangle|^2
&=\;1-\|s_n-\langle s_n,\varphi_n\rangle\varphi_n\|^2
\ge1-C^2n^{-2}.
\label{eq:app-d-display-006}
\end{align}
The chosen sign of $\varphi_n$ therefore gives
\begin{align}
\|\varphi_n-s_n\|&\le\;Cn^{-1}.\label{eq:app-d-display-007}
\end{align}

To estimate the derivatives, set $v=\varphi_n-s_n$. The eigenvalue equations for $\varphi_n$ and $s_n$ are
\begin{align}
(A_0+P)\varphi_n=\mu_n\varphi_n,\qquad
A_0s_n=-n^4\pi^4s_n.\label{eq:eigenvalue-comparison}
\end{align}
Subtracting the second equation from the first and substituting
$\varphi_n=v+s_n$ gives
\begin{align}
A_0v&=\;\mu_nv+(\mu_n+n^4\pi^4)s_n-P\varphi_n.\label{eq:app-d-display-008}
\end{align}
Estimates \eqref{eq:control-elliptic}, \eqref{eq:mu-asymp}, and \eqref{eq:app-d-display-003}, with interpolation and $\|\varphi_n\|=1$, give
\begin{align}
\|\varphi_n\|_{H^4}
&\le\;C\big(\|A_{\lambda}\varphi_n\|+\|\varphi_n\|\big)
=C(1+|\mu_n|)\le Cn^4,\label{eq:eigenfunction-H4-bound}\\
\|\varphi_n\|_{H^2}
&\le\;C\|\varphi_n\|^{1/2}\|\varphi_n\|_{H^4}^{1/2}
\le Cn^2,\label{eq:eigenfunction-H2-bound}\\
\|P\varphi_n\|
&\le\;C\|\lambda\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|\varphi_n\|_{H^2}
\le Cn^2.\label{eq:app-d-display-009}
\end{align}
Combining these bounds with \eqref{eq:app-d-display-007} gives
\begin{align}
\|A_0v\|&\le\;Cn^4n^{-1}+Cn^2\le Cn^3,
\qquad \|v\|_{H^4}\le C(\|A_0v\|+\|v\|)\le Cn^3.\label{eq:app-d-display-010}
\end{align}
Interpolation gives $\|v\|_{H^s}\le Cn^{s-1}$ for $0\le s\le4$.
To obtain pointwise bounds, we apply the one-dimensional
Gagliardo--Nirenberg inequality \cite[p.~233, Eq.~(42)]{Brezis2011}
to $v^{(j)}$. Each derivative $v^{(j)}$, $0\le j\le3$, either vanishes
at both endpoints or has zero mean. Thus Poincar\'e's inequality gives
$\|v^{(j)}\|\le C\|v^{(j+1)}\|$, allowing the additional $L^2$ term
in the Gagliardo--Nirenberg inequality to be absorbed into the product below:
\begin{align}
\|v^{(j)}\|_{L^\infty}
&\le\;C\|v^{(j)}\|^{1/2}\|v^{(j+1)}\|^{1/2}
\le Cn^{j-1/2},\qquad 0\le j\le3.\label{eq:app-d-display-012}
\end{align}

It remains to bound the boundary traces. The trace coefficients can be written as
\begin{align}
\beta_n=s_n'(0)+v'(0),\qquad
\alpha_n=s_n'''(0)+v'''(0)
+\lambda(0)\big(s_n'(0)+v'(0)\big).\label{eq:app-d-display-013}
\end{align}
Since
\begin{align}
s_n'(0)&=\;\sqrt2\,n\pi,
\qquad s_n'''(0)=-\sqrt2(n\pi)^3,\label{eq:app-d-display-014}
\end{align}
\eqref{eq:app-d-display-012} gives the high-mode bounds in
\eqref{eq:trace-bounds}. For the finitely many remaining modes, the upper
bounds follow from the elliptic estimate and the trace inequality after
enlarging the constant. This completes (iii).

Then the following hold.

(\mathcal A'_{\lambda}-z)^{-1}f&=\;(A_{\lambda}-z)^{-1}f+\frac{\varepsilon\langle g,(A_{\lambda}-z)^{-1}f\rangle}{\Xi(z)}\,\rho(z),\qquad f\inL^2(0,1) .\label{eq:resolvent}
\end{align}
The plant resolvent is compact, and the correction has rank at most one.
Hence $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}$ has compact resolvent.
\item The adjoint has domain $D(\mathcal A'_{\lambda}{}^*)=D(A_{\lambda})$ and, for
$\phi\in D(A_{\lambda})$, acts as
\begin{align}
\mathcal A'_{\lambda}{}^*\phi&=\;A_{\lambda}\phi-\varepsilon\,\phi^{[3]}(0)\,g.\label{eq:app-d-display-016}
\end{align}
\item $z\in\sigma(\mathcal A'_{\lambda})$ if and only if one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $z\notin\sigma(A_{\lambda})$ and $\Xi(z)=0$.
\item $z\in\sigma(A_{\lambda})$ and $E_z\cap g^\perp\ne\{0\}$.
\item $z\in\sigma(A_{\lambda})$ and $\Xi$ is regular at $z$. For $\varepsilon\ne0$,
this is equivalent to $\sum_{\mu_n=z}g_n\alpha_n=0$.
\end{enumerate}
Here $E_z$ is the eigenspace of $A_{\lambda}$ at $z$, and $g^\perp$ consists
of the functions in $L^2(0,1)$ orthogonal to $g$.
\item Assume $\varepsilon\ne0$. Let $\mu'$ be real, with $\mu'\notin\sigma(A_{\lambda})$ and $\Xi(\mu')=0$.
Then $\mu'$ is algebraically simple if and only if $\Xi'(\mu')\ne0$.
Moreover, when $\Xi'(\mu')\ne0$, the eigenvectors $\chi_{\mu'}$ and $\tilde\chi_{\mu'}$
of the pre-compensated operator $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}$ and its adjoint $(\mathcal A'_{\lambda})^*$,
respectively, associated with $\mu'$ and normalized by
$\langle \chi_{\mu'},\tilde\chi_{\mu'}\rangle=1$, are given by
\begin{align}
\chi_{\mu'}&:=\;\rho(\mu'),\qquad
\tilde\chi_{\mu'}:=-\frac{\varepsilon}{\Xi'(\mu')}(A_{\lambda}-\mu')^{-1}g.
\label{eq:app-d-display-019}
\end{align}
The effect of the curvature input on this mode is given by the coefficient
\begin{align}
b'_{\mu'}&:=\;\langle B_2,\tilde\chi_{\mu'}\rangle=-\tilde\chi_{\mu'}'(0)
=\frac{\varepsilon}{\Xi'(\mu')}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{g_n\beta_n}{\mu_n-\mu'}.
\label{eq:bprime-moved}
\end{align}
\item Assume $\varepsilon\ne0$. Let $\mu$ be a double eigenvalue of $A_{\lambda}$ with orthonormal eigenfunctions $\varphi_j,\varphi_k$, and define
\begin{align}
\mathsf r_\mu&:=\;g_j\alpha_j+g_k\alpha_k.\label{eq:app-d-display-021}
\end{align}
If $\mathsf r_\mu\ne0$, then $\mu$ is an algebraically simple eigenvalue of $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}$ with
\begin{align}
\chi_\mu&=\;g_k\varphi_j-g_j\varphi_k,\qquad \tilde\chi_\mu=\frac{\alpha_k\varphi_j-\alpha_j\varphi_k}{\mathsf r_\mu},\qquad b'_\mu=-\frac{\beta_j\alpha_k-\beta_k\alpha_j}{\mathsf r_\mu}\ne0 .\label{eq:app-d-display-083}
\end{align}
If $\mathsf r_\mu=0$, then $\mu$ has algebraic multiplicity at least two.
\end{enumerate}

We first prove (i). We add a boundary lifting to the plant resolvent
to enforce the pre-feedback condition. Fix $z\notin\sigma(A_{\lambda})$.
The lifting $\rho(z)$ is the $H^4$ solution of the boundary-value problem
\begin{align}
-\rho(z)''''-(\lambda\rho(z)')'&=\;z\rho(z),\qquad
\rho(z)(0)=\;1,\qquad \rho(z)(1)=\rho(z)''(0)=\rho(z)''(1)=0.
\label{eq:boundary-lifting-homogeneous}
\end{align}
Since $z\notin\sigma(A_{\lambda})$, this problem has a unique solution.
Multiplying it by a scalar changes only its left displacement.

Lemma~\ref{lem:ibp} gives
$(\mu_n-z)\langle \rho(z),\varphi_n\rangle=\alpha_n$, yielding the series in
\eqref{eq:secular-function}. The series converges in $L^2$, while its
boundary traces are supplied by the boundary-value problem.

For $\phi\in D(\mathcal A'_{\lambda})$, Lemma~\ref{lem:ibp} with test function $\varphi_n$,
the boundary conditions $\phi''(0)=0$ and
$\phi(0)=\varepsilon\langle g,\phi\rangle$, and
$\varphi_n^{[3]}(0)=\alpha_n$ give
\begin{align}
\langle \mathcal A'_{\lambda}\phi,\varphi_n\rangle&=\;\mu_n\langle \phi,\varphi_n\rangle
-\varepsilon\alpha_n\langle g,\phi\rangle,\qquad n\ge1.
\label{eq:Aprime-modal}
\end{align}

For the equation $(\mathcal A'_{\lambda}-z)\psi=f$, set $p=\langle g,\psi\rangle$.
The modal identity \eqref{eq:Aprime-modal} gives
\begin{align}
(\mu_n-z)\psi_n-\varepsilon\alpha_np&=\;f_n.\label{eq:app-d-display-023}
\end{align}
Solving in the plant basis and then applying the observation yields
\begin{align}
\psi&=\;(A_{\lambda}-z)^{-1}f+\varepsilon p\rho(z),
\qquad p\Xi(z)=\langle g,(A_{\lambda}-z)^{-1}f\rangle.\label{eq:app-d-display-024}
\end{align}
This proves \eqref{eq:resolvent} when $\Xi(z)\ne0$. Conversely,
\eqref{eq:boundary-lifting-homogeneous} gives the homogeneous boundary
traces, while $\psi(0)=\varepsilon p=\varepsilon\langle g,\psi\rangle$,
so the formula satisfies the domain conditions.

The eigenvalue and trace bounds \eqref{eq:mu-asymp} and
\eqref{eq:trace-bounds} give $\|\rho(z)\|\to0$ as real $z\to+\infty$,
so $\Xi(z)\to1$. Thus the resolvent formula applies for sufficiently
large real $z$.
The resolvent is compact as the sum of a compact operator and a rank-one
operator. Its boundedness and full domain also show that $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}$ is closed.

To verify density of the domain, approximate an arbitrary $f\inL^2(0,1)$ by
$\phi_0\in D(A_{\lambda})$ with error at most $\delta$. Choose a smooth boundary
lifting $\psi_\delta$ supported near zero such that
\begin{align}
\psi_\delta(0)&=\;1,\qquad \psi_\delta''(0)=0,
\qquad \psi_\delta(1)=\psi_\delta''(1)=0,
\qquad \|\psi_\delta\|\le\delta.\label{eq:app-d-display-026}
\end{align}
Such a lifting is obtained by rescaling a fixed smooth bump. Set
\begin{align}
\phi&=\;\phi_0+c\psi_\delta,
\qquad
c=\frac{\varepsilon\langle g,\phi_0\rangle}{1-\varepsilon\langle g,\psi_\delta\rangle}.
\label{eq:app-d-display-027}
\end{align}
For small $\delta$, the denominator stays away from zero and $c$ remains
bounded. Thus $\phi\in D(\mathcal A'_{\lambda})$ and
$\|f-\phi\|\le\delta+|c|\delta\to0$, proving density of $D(\mathcal A'_{\lambda})$
and completing (i).

We next prove (ii), identifying the adjoint and its domain. For $\phi\in D(\mathcal A'_{\lambda})$ and $\zeta\in D(A_{\lambda})$, the boundary identity gives
\begin{align}
\langle \mathcal A'_{\lambda}\phi,\zeta\rangle
&=\;\langle \phi,A_{\lambda}\zeta\rangle
 -\varepsilon\langle g,\phi\rangle\sum_n\alpha_n\zeta_n.\label{eq:app-d-display-029}
\end{align}
The eigenfunction expansion of $\zeta$ converges in the graph norm and
hence in $H^4$, giving $\sum_n\alpha_n\zeta_n=\zeta^{[3]}(0)$.
The series also converges absolutely, since the trace coefficients grow
like $n^3$ and $(n^4\zeta_n)_n\in\ell^2$. This proves the adjoint
formula and the inclusion $D(A_{\lambda})\subseteq D(\mathcal A'_{\lambda}{}^*)$.
For the reverse domain inclusion, take the adjoint
of \eqref{eq:resolvent}:
\begin{align}
\big((\mathcal A'_{\lambda}-z)^{-1}\big)^*
&=\;(A_{\lambda}-\bar z)^{-1}
 +\frac{\varepsilon}{\overline{\Xi(z)}}
  (A_{\lambda}-\bar z)^{-1}g\,\langle \cdot,\rho(z)\rangle.\label{eq:app-d-display-031}
\end{align}
Its range is contained in $D(A_{\lambda})$ and equals $D(\mathcal A'_{\lambda}{}^*)$.
This proves (ii).

We next prove (iii). If $\varepsilon=0$, then $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}=A_{\lambda}$ and
$\Xi\equiv1$, so case (c) gives exactly the plant spectrum.
Assume henceforth that $\varepsilon\ne0$. Since $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}$ has compact
resolvent, it suffices to characterize its eigenvalues.
Set $f=0$ in \eqref{eq:app-d-display-023} and recall that
$p=\langle g,\psi\rangle$.

For $z\notin\sigma(A_{\lambda})$, \eqref{eq:app-d-display-024} gives
$\psi=\varepsilon p\rho(z)$ and $p\Xi(z)=0$, so a nonzero eigenvector
requires $\Xi(z)=0$. Conversely, since $\rho(z)(0)=1$, the condition
$\Xi(z)=0$ is precisely $1=\varepsilon\langle g,\rho(z)\rangle$.
Thus $\rho(z)$ satisfies the pre-feedback boundary condition and is an
eigenfunction of $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}$. This proves case (a).

At a plant eigenvalue, every nonzero $\psi\in E_z\cap g^\perp$
satisfies the pre-feedback condition and remains an eigenfunction,
proving case (b). By Lemma~\ref{lem:plant-spectral}(i), the plant
eigenvalue is either simple or double. At a double eigenvalue,
$E_z\cap g^\perp$ is necessarily nontrivial. At a simple eigenvalue
$z=\mu_n$, $g_n=0$ also gives case (b).

It remains to consider a simple plant eigenvalue with $g_n\ne0$.
The $n$th modal equation gives $\varepsilon\alpha_np=0$.
If $\alpha_n\ne0$, then $p=0$, all coefficients except $\psi_n$
vanish, and $p=g_n\psi_n$ forces $\psi=0$.
If $\alpha_n=0$, the coefficient along $\varphi_n$ remains free.
For $m\ne n$, the modal equations give
$\psi_m=\varepsilon\alpha_mp/(\mu_m-\mu_n)$, and $\psi_n$ can be
chosen to satisfy $p=\langle g,\psi\rangle$ for any nonzero $p$.
Thus a nonzero eigenvector exists precisely when $\alpha_n=0$,
which is the pole-cancellation condition $g_n\alpha_n=0$ in case (c).
This exhausts the possibilities and proves (iii).

We next prove (iv). At a real root $\mu'$ outside the plant spectrum, the right eigenvector
is proportional to $\rho(\mu')$. Substituting the adjoint formula from
part (ii) into
$(\mathcal A'_{\lambda})^*\tilde\chi_{\mu'}=\mu'\tilde\chi_{\mu'}$ gives
\begin{align}
(A_{\lambda}-\mu')\tilde\chi_{\mu'}
&=\;\varepsilon\tilde\chi_{\mu'}^{[3]}(0)g,\label{eq:app-d-display-035}
\end{align}
so an unnormalized adjoint eigenvector is
\begin{align}
\zeta&:=\;(A_{\lambda}-\mu')^{-1}g
=\sum_n\frac{g_n}{\mu_n-\mu'}\varphi_n.
\label{eq:app-d-display-017}
\end{align}
The consistency condition is precisely $\Xi(\mu')=0$, since
\begin{align}
[(A_{\lambda}-\mu')^{-1}g]^{[3]}(0)
&=\;\sum_n\frac{g_n\alpha_n}{\mu_n-\mu'}
=\varepsilon^{-1}.\label{eq:app-d-display-036}
\end{align}
The pairing of these two eigenvectors is
\begin{align}
\langle \rho(\mu'),(A_{\lambda}-\mu')^{-1}g\rangle
&=\;\sum_n\frac{g_n\alpha_n}{(\mu_n-\mu')^2}
=-\varepsilon^{-1}\Xi'(\mu').\label{eq:app-d-display-037}
\end{align}
By \eqref{eq:resolvent}, the resolvent has a simple pole with a rank-one
spectral projection exactly when this pairing is nonzero. Normalization
and the derivative trace give \eqref{eq:app-d-display-019}--\eqref{eq:bprime-moved},
proving (iv).

Finally, we prove (v). Suppose first that $\mathsf r_\mu\ne0$. The vector $\chi_\mu\in E_\mu\cap g^\perp$ remains an eigenvector by
part (iii). Expanding \eqref{eq:secular-function} at $\mu$ gives
\begin{align}
\Xi(z)&=\;-\frac{\varepsilon\mathsf r_\mu}{\mu-z}+O(1),
\qquad \Xi(z)^{-1}=O(\mu-z).\label{eq:app-d-display-038}
\end{align}
Writing $\hat\alpha=\alpha_j\varphi_j+\alpha_k\varphi_k$, the spectral
projection obtained from \eqref{eq:resolvent} is
\begin{align}
&P_\mu-\mathsf r_\mu^{-1}
 (P_\mu\hat\alpha)\langle P_\mu g,\cdot\rangle.\label{eq:app-d-display-040}
\end{align}
This projection has rank one, as its trace is $2-1$. Thus the retained
eigenvalue is algebraically simple and has the eigenvectors stated in
(v). Its curvature coefficient is nonzero by the injectivity of the boundary-trace map in
Lemma~\ref{lem:plant-spectral}(ii). When $\mathsf r_\mu=0$, the pole in $\Xi$ is
canceled and the eigenvalue has algebraic multiplicity at least two.
This completes (v).

\label{lem:high}
Let $\lambda\in W^{1,\infty}$, $g\inL^2(0,1)$, $\varepsilon\in\mathbb R$. There exist an index $N_0$ and a sequence $\vartheta_n\to0$, both depending only on $\|\lambda\|_{W^{1,\infty}}$, $\|g\|$, $|\varepsilon|$ and the tails $\sum_{m\ge N}|g_m|^2$, such that for every $n\ge N_0$ the following holds: the disk $\{|z-\mu_n|\le\mathfrak g_n/2\}$ contains exactly one eigenvalue $\mu_n'$ of $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}$; it is real and algebraically simple; and there are eigenvectors $\chi_n$ of $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}$ and $\tilde\chi_n$ of $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}{}^*$, normalized by $\langle \chi_n,\tilde\chi_n\rangle=1$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:high-est}
\begin{gathered}
|\mu_n'-\mu_n|\le 2|\varepsilon|\,|g_n|\,|\alpha_n|,\qquad \|\chi_n-\varphi_n\|\le C|\varepsilon|\,|g_n|,\\
b_n':=\langle B_2,\tilde\chi_n\rangle=-\beta_n(1+\vartheta_n),\qquad\vartheta_n\to0 .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Moreover $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}$ has no eigenvalue with $|z|\ge|\mu_{N_0}|+\mathfrak g_{N_0}/2$ outside these disks, and only finitely many eigenvalues elsewhere.

The gap bound \eqref{eq:gap}, established in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:plant-spectral}(iii), and its trace estimates give simplicity
and nonzero curvature traces for high plant modes. We locate the perturbed eigenvalues,
estimate their eigenvectors and traces, and establish spectral separation.

We first locate the eigenvalue in each high-mode disk.
Fix $n\ge N_1$, set $\mathcal D_n:=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z-\mu_n|\le\mathfrak g_n/2\}$, and separate the $n$th pole from the secular function:
\begin{align}
R_n(z)&:=\;\sum_{m\ne n}\frac{g_m\alpha_m}{\mu_m-z},
\qquad
(\mu_n-z)\Xi(z)
=(\mu_n-z)-\varepsilon g_n\alpha_n
 -\varepsilon(\mu_n-z)R_n(z).\label{eq:app-d-display-041}
\end{align}
On $\mathcal D_n$, $|\mu_m-z|\ge\tfrac12|\mu_m-\mu_n|$ for $m\ne n$.
Set $\bar R_n:=\sup_{z\in\mathcal D_n}|R_n(z)|$.
The trace and gap estimates, splitting the sum at $m=n/2$, give
\begin{align}
\bar R_n&\le\;C\sum_{m\ne n}\frac{|g_m|m^3}{|m^4-n^4|}
\le C\left(\sum_{m\ge n/2}|g_m|^2\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_{m\ne n}\frac{m^6}{(m^4-n^4)^2}\right)^{1/2}
 +C\|g\|n^{-1/2}.\label{eq:Rbar}
\end{align}
The square sum is uniformly bounded, with terms comparable to
$(16(m-n)^2)^{-1}$ near $m=n$. Thus the high-mode contribution vanishes
with the $\ell^2$ tail. For $m<n/2$, the bound
$|m^4-n^4|\ge15n^4/16$ gives the remaining $O(\|g\|n^{-1/2})$ term,
so $\bar R_n\to0$. Also, $g_n\to0$ and the cubic gap give
\begin{align}
|\varepsilon g_n\alpha_n|
&\le\;C|\varepsilon||g_n|n^3\le\mathfrak g_n/8\label{eq:app-d-display-043}
\end{align}
for sufficiently large $n$. Choose $N_0$ so that
\begin{align}
|\varepsilon|\bar R_n&\le\;\tfrac14,
\qquad |\varepsilon g_n\alpha_n|\le\mathfrak g_n/8,
\qquad n\ge N_0.\label{eq:app-d-display-044}
\end{align}
On the boundary of the disk, these estimates give
\begin{align}
|\varepsilon g_n\alpha_n+\varepsilon(\mu_n-z)R_n(z)|
&\le\;\mathfrak g_n/4<|\mu_n-z|.\label{eq:app-d-display-045}
\end{align}
Rouch\'e's theorem \cite[p.~70]{Naimark} therefore gives exactly one zero of
$(\mu_n-z)\Xi(z)$ in the disk, counted with multiplicity. This zero is real
by conjugation symmetry. If it differs from $\mu_n$, it satisfies
\begin{align}
\mu_n-\mu_n'
&=\;\frac{\varepsilon g_n\alpha_n}{1-\varepsilon R_n(\mu_n')},\label{eq:app-d-display-046}
\end{align}
which gives the eigenvalue-displacement estimate in \eqref{eq:high-est}.
The multiplicity count also makes the zero simple. For a moved eigenvalue,
$\mu_n-\mu_n'\ne0$, so $\Xi'(\mu_n')\ne0$ and
Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum}(iv) gives algebraic simplicity.

We next estimate the eigenvectors and curvature coefficient when the eigenvalue is retained.
If $g_n\alpha_n=0$, the high-mode trace bound gives $g_n=0$. The zero is
then the retained eigenvalue $\mu_n$, with $\chi_n=\varphi_n$.
The adjoint eigenvector, normalized by its $n$th coefficient, is
\begin{align}
\tilde\chi_n&=\;\varphi_n+
\frac{\varepsilon\alpha_n}{1-\varepsilon R_n(\mu_n)}
\sum_{m\ne n}\frac{g_m}{\mu_m-\mu_n}\varphi_m.\label{eq:app-d-display-048}
\end{align}
Splitting this sum at $m=n/2$ and applying \eqref{eq:gap} gives
\begin{align}
\|\tilde\chi_n-\varphi_n\|
&\le\;C|\varepsilon|
\left[\left(\sum_{m\ge n/2}|g_m|^2\right)^{1/2}
 +\frac{\|g\|}{n}\right]\longrightarrow0.\label{eq:app-d-display-049}
\end{align}
The $L^2$ estimate does not control the boundary derivative.
Taking the trace in \eqref{eq:app-d-display-048} and using
$|\beta_m|\le Cm$ gives
\begin{align}
b_n'&=\;-\beta_n(1+O(\eta_n)),
\qquad
\eta_n:=n^2\sum_{m\ne n}\frac{|g_m|m}{|m^4-n^4|}.\label{eq:app-d-display-050}
\end{align}
The same low/high splitting shows $\eta_n\to0$. These estimates prove
the asserted bounds for the retained eigenvalue.

For a moved eigenvalue, we obtain the same estimates from the explicit eigenvector formulas.
For $g_n\alpha_n\ne0$, normalize the right eigenvector from
Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum}(iv) to have $n$th coefficient one,
and denote it by $\chi_n$. Then
\begin{align}
\chi_n&=\;\varphi_n+\sum_{m\ne n}
 \frac{\alpha_m(\mu_n-\mu_n')}
      {\alpha_n(\mu_m-\mu_n')}\varphi_m,\notag\\
\|\chi_n-\varphi_n\|^2
&\le4\varepsilon^2g_n^2
 \sum_{m\ne n}\frac{\alpha_m^2}{(\mu_m-\mu_n')^2}
\le C\varepsilon^2g_n^2.\label{eq:chi-hi}
\end{align}
Here we used the displacement bound, the bounded sum in \eqref{eq:Rbar}, and
\begin{align}
\frac{|\alpha_m|}{|\alpha_n|}&\le\;C(m/n)^3,
\qquad |\mu_m-\mu_n'|\ge\tfrac12|\mu_m-\mu_n|.\label{eq:app-d-display-051}
\end{align}
Write the adjoint eigenvector as
$\tilde\chi_n=\varpi_n\sum_m\frac{g_m}{\mu_m-\mu_n'}\varphi_m$,
where $\varpi_n$ enforces biorthogonality. The corresponding curvature
coefficient $b_n'$ therefore uses a different scaling from
$b'_{\mu_n'}$ in \eqref{eq:bprime-moved}.
Multiplying the numerator and denominator of its trace formula by
$(\mu_n-\mu_n')/g_n$ gives
\begin{align}
\frac{b_n'}{-\beta_n}
&=\;\frac{1+\vartheta_n^{(1)}}{1+\vartheta_n^{(2)}},\label{eq:bratio}
\end{align}
where, with $R_n=R_n(\mu_n')$,
\begin{align}
\vartheta_n^{(1)}
&=\;\frac{\varepsilon\alpha_n}{(1-\varepsilon R_n)\beta_n}
 \sum_{m\ne n}\frac{g_m\beta_m}{\mu_m-\mu_n'},\notag\\
\vartheta_n^{(2)}
&=\;\frac{\varepsilon^2g_n\alpha_n}{(1-\varepsilon R_n)^2}
 \sum_{m\ne n}\frac{g_m\alpha_m}{(\mu_m-\mu_n')^2}.\label{eq:app-d-display-053}
\end{align}
Using $\eta_n$ from \eqref{eq:app-d-display-050} and the splitting used in \eqref{eq:Rbar} gives
\begin{align}
|\vartheta_n^{(1)}|&\le\;C\eta_n\longrightarrow0,\notag\\
|\vartheta_n^{(2)}|
&\le\;C\varepsilon^2|g_n|n^3
 \sum_{m\ne n}\frac{|g_m|m^3}{(m^4-n^4)^2}
\le C\varepsilon^2|g_n|\|g\|\longrightarrow0.\label{eq:app-d-display-054}
\end{align}
Together with the retained-mode estimate \eqref{eq:app-d-display-050}, this gives the sequence $\vartheta_n$ in
\eqref{eq:high-est}.

We now exclude further eigenvalues outside the high-mode disks.
Choose large circles that stay at least $\mathfrak g_n/2$ from each
plant eigenvalue. On such a circle $|z|=R$, split the sum in
\eqref{eq:secular-function} at a fixed index $N$ to obtain
\begin{align}
|\Xi(z)-1|&\le\;|\varepsilon|\sum_{m\le N}
 \frac{|g_m\alpha_m|}{R-|\mu_m|}
 +C|\varepsilon|\left(\sum_{m>N}|g_m|^2\right)^{1/2}.\label{eq:app-d-display-055}
\end{align}
Cauchy--Schwarz and the preceding sum bound give the second term.
Choosing $N$ large, then $R$ large, gives
$\sup_{|z|=R}|\Xi(z)-1|\to0$ along these circles.
Apply Rouch\'e's theorem \cite[p.~70]{Naimark} to the analytic function
$z\mapsto\Xi(z)\prod_{|\mu_m|\le R}(\mu_m-z)$.

Its zeros encode the moved and retained eigenvalues, with algebraic
multiplicities determined by the resolvent formula \eqref{eq:resolvent}
and the moved and retained eigenvalue analysis in
Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum} and its proof. The total count equals the plant count. With one eigenvalue per high-mode disk, this proves the final assertion.

Finally, we record the spectral separation and uniformity needed below.
Since $g_n\to0$ and $|\alpha_n|\le Cn^3$, the displacement bound
\eqref{eq:high-est} gives
\begin{align}
|\mu_n'-\mu_n|&=\;o(n^3),\qquad n\to\infty.
\label{eq:prefeedback-shift-small}
\end{align}
Combining this with the plant gap \eqref{eq:gap}, for distinct
sufficiently large indices we obtain
\begin{align}
|\mu_i'-\mu_j'|
&\ge\;|\mu_i-\mu_j|-|\mu_i'-\mu_i|-|\mu_j'-\mu_j|
\ge\tfrac14\pi^4|i^4-j^4|.
\label{eq:prefeedback-high-gap}
\end{align}
After enlarging $N_0$, this holds for all distinct $i,j\ge N_0$.
For $|\varepsilon|\le1$, replacing $|\varepsilon|$ by $1$ in these
estimates gives a common $N_0$, uniform convergence of $\vartheta_n$,
and uniform displacement bounds and spectral separation.

\label{thm:kernel}
Let $(a,\varepsilon,\theta)\in\mathscr U_\lambda$, let $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}$ be the pre-compensated operator \eqref{eq:Aprime} and $B_2$ the curvature control vector. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item There exist a unique sequence $(K_n)_{n\ge1}$ with $(K_nb_n'n^{-1/2-\epsilon})\in\ell^2$ for all $\epsilon>0$ and a bounded operator $T$ on $L^2(0,1)$ such that, with $h:=\sum_nK_n\overline{\tilde\chi_n}$ (so that $\langle \chi_n,h\rangle=K_n$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fequiv}
\begin{gathered}
T(\mathcal A'_{\lambda}+B_2\langle h,\cdot\rangle)=(\mathcal A'_{\lambda}-a)T\quad\text{in }\mathcal L(\mathcal H^{1+s},\mathcal H^{-3+s})\ \text{ for }|s|<\tfrac32,\\
TB_2=B_2\quad\text{in }\mathcal H^{-3/2-\epsilon}\ \text{ for every }\epsilon>0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Moreover $\sum_nn^{1-2\epsilon}|K_n|^2<\infty$ for every $\epsilon>0$, so that $h\inL^2(0,1)$, and $h\in H^{1/2-\epsilon}(0,1)$ for every $\epsilon\in(0,\tfrac12]$. Also $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}+B_2\langle h,\cdot\rangle$ generates a $C^0$ semigroup on $\mathcal H^r$ for $r\in(-9/2,5/2)$.

\item \emph{(Invertibility.)} $T$ is an isomorphism of $\mathcal H^r$ for every $r\in(-9/2,5/2)$. In particular $T=I-K$ is boundedly invertible on $L^2(0,1)$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transformation-bounds}
c_1=\|I-K\|_{\mathcal L(L^2)}<\infty,\qquad c_2=\|(I-K)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal L(L^2)}<\infty .

&=-2\nu_0\,V(w)-\sum_{n\ge1}\gamma_n|w_n|^2+2\operatorname{Re}\langle f,w\rangle_{\mathcal H},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\eqref{eq:dissipation-weights} and \eqref{eq:dissipation-coercivity}.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:target-decay}
V(w(t))\le e^{-2\nu t}V(w_0),\qquad \|w(t)\|\le\sqrt{M/m}\;e^{-\nu t}\|w_0\|,\qquad t\ge0 ,
\end{equation}

Theorem~\ref{thm:generic}(ii) supplies the Riesz basis and quartic spectral growth,
while Lemma~\ref{lem:high} shows that sufficiently high eigenvalues are
real and negative. We use these properties to prove (i), then establish
the forced energy identity in (ii) and the decay and forcing estimates in (iii).

We first prove (i). In the Riesz coordinates of Theorem~\ref{thm:generic}(ii),
$J^{-1}(\mathcal A'_{\lambda}-a)J=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_n'-a)$, and the semigroup is
obtained by shifting \eqref{eq:app-d-display-066}. By \eqref{eq:nu},
$\operatorname{Re}(\mu_n'-a)\le-\nu$. Since only finitely many eigenvalues
are nonreal, Lemma~\ref{lem:high} also gives
$|\arg(a-\mu_n')|\le\pi/2-\delta'$ for some $\delta'>0$.
The diagonal semigroup therefore extends analytically to a sector.

To establish existence and regularity in (i), fix a finite interval
$[0,\tau]$ and let
$f\in L^2(0,\tau;\mathcal H^{-2})$. The variation-of-constants formula
gives, in each coordinate,
\begin{align}
w_n(t)&=\;e^{(\mu_n'-a)t}w_{0,n}
 +\int_0^t e^{(\mu_n'-a)(t-s)}f_n(s)\,\mathrm d s.\label{eq:app-f-display-005}
\end{align}
Taking the real part of the coordinate equation after multiplication by
$\overline{w_n}$, applying Young's inequality, and integrating gives
\begin{align}
\int_0^\tau(a-\operatorname{Re}\mu_n')|w_n|^2\,\mathrm d t
&\le\;|w_{0,n}|^2+
\int_0^\tau\frac{|f_n|^2}{a-\operatorname{Re}\mu_n'}\,\mathrm d t.\label{eq:app-f-display-006}
\end{align}
Theorem~\ref{thm:generic}(ii) and the positive margin \eqref{eq:nu} give
$cn^4\le a-\operatorname{Re}\mu_n'\le Cn^4$. Summing therefore gives
$w\in L^2(0,\tau;\mathcal H^2)$ and
$w_t\in L^2(0,\tau;\mathcal H^{-2})$.
Retaining the terminal energy in the same scalar estimate and summing
only over $n>N$ gives
\begin{align}
\sup_{0\le t\le\tau}\sum_{n>N}|w_n(t)|^2
&\le\;\sum_{n>N}|w_{0,n}|^2
+\int_0^\tau\sum_{n>N}\frac{|f_n(s)|^2}{a-\operatorname{Re}\mu_n'}\,\mathrm d s
\longrightarrow0\quad(N\to\infty).\label{eq:target-continuous-tail}
\end{align}
Thus the finite modal sums converge in $C([0,\tau];L^2(0,1))$ to the mild
solution. Uniqueness follows from \eqref{eq:app-f-display-005}.
Since $\tau$ is arbitrary, this proves (i).

We next prove (ii). Each coordinate is absolutely continuous and satisfies, almost everywhere,
\begin{align}
\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm d t}|w_n|^2
&=\;2\operatorname{Re}\big((\mu_n'-a)|w_n|^2+f_n\bar w_n\big).\label{eq:app-f-display-008}
\end{align}
By \eqref{eq:app-f-display-006}, the summed dissipation is integrable.
Moreover, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{align}
\sum_n|f_n\bar w_n|
&\le\;\|f\|_{\mathcal H^{-2}}\|w\|_{\mathcal H^2}
\in L^1(0,\tau).\label{eq:app-f-display-010}
\end{align}

their derivatives converge in $L^1(0,\tau)$. Thus $V(w)$ is absolutely
continuous and satisfies \eqref{eq:Vdot}.

The weight bounds and the choice of $c_\gamma$ were established in
Section~\ref{ssec:uniform}. Together with \eqref{eq:Vdot}, they complete (ii).

\eqref{eq:nu} give $V(w(t))\le e^{-2\nu t}V(w_0)$.
The norm equivalence \eqref{eq:riesz-const} yields the second estimate
in \eqref{eq:target-decay}. For general $f$, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz

\begin{align}
2\operatorname{Re}\langle f,w\rangle_{\mathcal H}
&\le\;\delta\sum_n\gamma_n|w_n|^2
 +\delta^{-1}\sum_n\frac{|f_n|^2}{\gamma_n},
\qquad \delta>0.\label{eq:app-f-display-014}
\end{align}

Substituting into \eqref{eq:Vdot} for $0<\delta<1$ gives
\eqref{eq:target-forced}. Its norm bound follows from
$\gamma_n\ge c_\gamma n^4$, completing (iii).

We next use the initial-state bound to close the energy estimate.
By \eqref{eq:transformation-bounds}, \eqref{eq:riesz-const}, and the
definition of $\rho_0$ in \eqref{eq:basin},
\begin{align}
V(0)&\le\;Mc_1^2\|u_0\|^2\le r_0.\label{eq:app-j-display-008}
\end{align}
For $V\le r_0$, \eqref{eq:basin} gives
$C_*V^{4/5}\le\bar\nu-\omega$, hence
$2\bar\nu-C_*V^{4/5}\ge\bar\nu+\omega>2\omega$.
Scalar comparison applied to \eqref{eq:app-j-display-005} therefore
preserves $V\le r_0$. Along the solution,
\begin{align}
\dot V&\le\;-2\omega V-(1-\varrho)\Sigma.\label{eq:app-j-display-007}
\end{align}
Multiplying by $e^{2\omega t}$ and integrating gives
\begin{align}
e^{2\omega t}V(t)
+(1-\varrho)\int_0^t e^{2\omega s}\Sigma(w(s))\,\mathrm d s
&\le\;V(0).\label{eq:app-j-display-010}
\end{align}
In particular, $V(t)\le e^{-2\omega t}V(0)$, so
\begin{align}
\|u(t)\|
&\le\;c_2m^{-1/2}e^{-\omega t}V(0)^{1/2}
\le c_1c_2\sqrt{M/m}\,e^{-\omega t}\|u_0\|.\label{eq:app-j-display-009}
\end{align}
This bound and the blow-up alternative in Proposition~\ref{prop:wp}
imply global existence and prove \eqref{eq:nominal-est}.

Finally, discarding the nonnegative terminal energy in
\eqref{eq:app-j-display-010} and letting $t\to\infty$ gives
\eqref{eq:nominal-H2}, using \eqref{eq:app-j-display-008}.
The physical $H^2$ estimate stated after the theorem follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:smoothing}(ii), the embedding established in
Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:target-lin}(ii):
\begin{align}
\|u\|_{H^2}
&\le\;C\|T^{-1}\|_{\mathcal L(\mathcal H^2)}
 (\Sigma/c_\gamma)^{1/2}.\label{eq:app-j-display-011}
\end{align}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tolerance}
\epsilon^*:=\frac{\sqrt{\varkappa_e\,m\,(\bar\nu-\omega)}}{c_2\,\Lambda},\qquad
r_0:=\Big(\frac{\bar\nu-\omega}{C_*}\Big)^{5/4},\qquad \rho_0:=\frac{\sqrt{r_0/M}}{c_1},\qquad 
C_*:=\tfrac58\Big(\tfrac{8\varkappa_{\rm N}}{3}\Big)^{-3/5}\Big(\frac{2C_{\rm N}c_2^{3/2}}{m^{3/4}c_\gamma^{3/8}}\Big)^{8/5}.
\end{equation}
Let $\hat h=\hat{\mathcal G}(\lambda,a,\varepsilon,\theta)$ be the implemented gain, with $\|\hat h-h\|\le\epsilon^*$. \begin{equation}\label{eq:main-est}
\|u(t)\|\le c_1c_2\sqrt{M/m}\;e^{-\omega t}\,\|u_0\|,\qquad t\ge0,
\end{equation}
and

(1-\varkappa_e-\varkappa_{\rm N})\int_0^\infty e^{2\omega t}\,\Sigma(Tu(t))\,dt\le Mc_1^2\|u_0\|^2 .
\end{equation}

\label{lem:gamma}
Let $(a,\varepsilon,\theta)\in\mathscr U_\lambda$ and let $\Gamma$ be the map \eqref{eq:Gamma} relating the modal inner product of the eigenbasis of $\mathcal A'_{\lambda}$ to the $L^2$ inner product. \begin{align}
\Gamma(\mathcal H^s)&\subset\;\{v\in H^s(0,1):v(0)=v(1)=0\}\label{eq:app-h-display-001}
\end{align}
 for $\frac12<s\le4$, in particular $\Gamma(\mathcal H^1)\subset H^1_0(0,1)$. On $\mathscr U_{B,d}$ the norms $\|\Gamma\|_{\mathcal L(\mathcal H^s,H^s)}$ are bounded by $C(B,d)$.

<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
The coordinate maps give the $L^2$ bounds directly. Indeed,
\begin{align}
\Gamma J&=\;\tilde J,
\qquad \Gamma=\tilde JJ^{-1},
\qquad \tilde J=(J^{-1})^*.\label{eq:app-h-display-002}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{align}
\|\Gamma\|&\le\;\|J^{-1}\|^2=M,
\qquad \|\Gamma^{-1}\|\le\|J\|^2=m^{-1}.\label{eq:app-h-display-003}
\end{align}

its adjoint. The following constants depend on $\lambda$ and
$(a,\varepsilon,\theta)$ for a fixed design and are uniform on
$\mathscr U_{B,d}$ as indicated.
For $\phi\in D(\mathcal A'_{\lambda})$, the equation and boundary condition give
\begin{align}
\phi''''&=\;-\mathcal A'_{\lambda}\phi-(\lambda\phi')',
\qquad |\phi(0)|=|\varepsilon\langle g_\theta,\phi\rangle|
\le C(B)\|\phi\|.\label{eq:app-g-display-030}
\end{align}
The elliptic estimate, with interpolation to absorb the $H^2$ term, gives
\begin{align}
\|\phi\|_{H^4}&\le\;C(B)(\|\mathcal A'_{\lambda}\phi\|+\|\phi\|).\label{eq:app-g-display-031}
\end{align}
For the adjoint, the additional trace term is absorbed using
\begin{align}
|\psi^{[3]}(0)|&\le\;\delta\|\psi\|_{H^4}
 +C_{\delta,B}\|\psi\|,\label{eq:app-g-display-032}
\end{align}
with $\delta$ small. This is uniform because
$|\varepsilon|\|g_\theta\|$ is bounded on the class. Hence both operators
have uniform equivalence of graph and $H^4$ norms. For $\phi\in\mathcal H^4$,

\begin{align}
\Gamma\phi&=\;\sum_n\phi_n\tilde\chi_n,
\qquad \sum_n\bar\mu_n'\phi_n\tilde\chi_n

converge in $L^2(0,1)$ by the spectral growth and Riesz bounds.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:wp}
Consider the Kuramoto--Sivashinsky plant \eqref{eq:plant} with the pre-feedback \eqref{eq:U1} and the perturbed curvature feedback \eqref{eq:U2hat}, that is $U_2=\langle \hat h,u\rangle$. For every $u_0\inL^2(0,1)$ and every $\hat h\inL^2(0,1)$ there is $\tau>0$, depending only on $\|u_0\|$, $\|\hat h-h\|$ and the design, and a unique solution of the closed loop on $[0,\tau)$ in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:solution}. Any two solutions coincide on their common interval of existence. If $[0,\tau^*)$ is the maximal interval of existence and $\tau^*<\infty$, then $\|u(t)\|\to\infty$ as $t\uparrow\tau^*$. Moreover $t\mapsto V(Tu(t))$ is absolutely continuous on compact subsets of $[0,\tau^*)$ and satisfies \eqref{eq:Vdot} with $f$ as in \eqref{eq:target-full}.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
We first prove local existence and uniqueness by contraction in target coordinates. Fix
$0<\nu_0<\nu$ and use the weighted norm
$\|f\|_{\mathcal H^{-2}_\gamma}$ defined in \eqref{eq:target-forced}. Equivalence follows from
$cn^4\le\gamma_n\le Cn^4$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:target-lin} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:generic}(ii). Write $w=Tu$, $u=T^{-1}w$, $e=\hat h-h$, and work in
\begin{align}
X_\tau&=\;C([0,\tau];\mathcal H^0)
       \cap L^2(0,\tau;\mathcal H^2),
\qquad
\|w\|_{X_\tau}
=\sup_{0\le t\le\tau}\|w(t)\|_{\mathcal H^0}
 +\left(\int_0^\tau\Sigma(w(t))\,\mathrm d t\right)^{1/2}.\label{eq:app-i-display-003}
\end{align}
Define $\Phi(w)$ as the mild solution of the linear target system with
initial condition $Tu_0$ and forcing $f(w)=B_2\langle e,u\rangle-T(uu_x)$ from \eqref{eq:target-full}.
A fixed point gives a solution in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:solution}.
Integrating the identity of Proposition~\ref{prop:target-lin}(ii) with
\eqref{eq:app-f-display-014} at $\delta=1/2$ gives
\begin{align}
\|\Phi(w)\|_{X_\tau}
&\le\;3\|Tu_0\|_{\mathcal H^0}
 +4\|f(w)\|_{L^2(0,\tau;\mathcal H^{-2}_\gamma)}.\label{eq:app-i-display-009}
\end{align}

In the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:forcing}, replace $w$ in the second slot
by an arbitrary $v\in\mathcal H^2$. Lemma~\ref{lem:smoothing}(ii) and
Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma} control $\Gamma T^\sharp v$ in $H^1_0(0,1)$.
Using $\|v\|_{\mathcal H^1}\le c_\gamma^{-1/2}\Sigma(v)^{1/2}$ from
\eqref{eq:dissipation-constants} and taking the supremum over
$\Sigma(v)\le1$ gives the first bound below. Spectral interpolation
and the $L^2$ norm equivalence give the second:
\begin{align}
\|T(uu_x)\|_{\mathcal H^{-2}_\gamma}
&\le\;C\|u\|^{3/2}\|u\|_{\mathcal H^1}^{1/2}
\le C\|u\|^{7/4}\|u\|_{\mathcal H^2}^{1/4}.\label{eq:nlH2}
\end{align}
H\"older's inequality in time and boundedness of $T^{-1}$ on
$\mathcal H^0$ and $\mathcal H^2$, from Lemma~\ref{lem:smoothing}(ii), give
\begin{align}
\|T(uu_x)\|_{L^2(0,\tau;\mathcal H^{-2}_\gamma)}
&\le\;C\tau^{3/8}\|w\|_{X_\tau}^2.\label{eq:app-i-display-006}
\end{align}
For the boundary forcing, \eqref{eq:Lambda} gives
$\|B_2\|_{\mathcal H^{-2}_\gamma}=\Lambda$, so
\begin{align}
\|B_2\langle e,u\rangle\|_{L^2(0,\tau;\mathcal H^{-2}_\gamma)}
&\le\;C\Lambda\|e\|\tau^{1/2}\|w\|_{X_\tau}.\label{eq:app-i-display-007}
\end{align}
For differences, use
$uu_x-vv_x=\tfrac12\partial_x((u-v)(u+v))$.
The same product estimates and the linear estimate with zero initial data give
\begin{align}
\|\Phi(w)-\Phi(z)\|_{X_\tau}
&\le\;C\big[\tau^{3/8}(\|w\|_{X_\tau}+\|z\|_{X_\tau})
 +\Lambda\|e\|\tau^{1/2}\big]\|w-z\|_{X_\tau}.
\label{eq:local-contraction}
\end{align}

Choose $R=6\|Tu_0\|_{\mathcal H^0}+1$. Estimates
\eqref{eq:app-i-display-009}, \eqref{eq:app-i-display-006}, and
\eqref{eq:app-i-display-007} show that $\Phi$ maps the closed radius-$R$
ball of $X_\tau$ into itself for sufficiently small $\tau$.
Estimate \eqref{eq:local-contraction} makes it a strict contraction.
Banach's fixed-point theorem \cite[Thm.~5.7]{Brezis2011} gives a unique
fixed point. The choice of $\tau$ depends only on the stated data and
the design. Applying the same difference estimate on short intervals
gives uniqueness on overlapping intervals.

We next prove continuation and the blow-up alternative. Restarting the local construction gives a maximal
interval of existence. The existence time is uniform when the initial
norm is bounded. If the solution remained bounded along times approaching
a finite $\tau^*$, restarting at one of those times would extend it
beyond $\tau^*$, proving the blow-up alternative.

Finally, we prove the Lyapunov identity. The forcing estimates place $f$ in
$L^2(0,\tau;\mathcal H^{-2})$ on every compact subinterval of existence.
Proposition~\ref{prop:target-lin}(ii) therefore gives absolute continuity
of $V(Tu)$ and identity \eqref{eq:Vdot}.
