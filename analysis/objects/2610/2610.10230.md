\label{thm:prorepresentability}
Suppose $m\in S$. There exist a finitely generated positively
graded $\mathbb C$-algebra $B_0$ and an explicit deformation family of
$(X,V(\chi^m))$ whose family algebra $\mathcal A_0$ is free over $B_0$.
Its completion is formally universal for
$\operatorname{Def}_m^{(X,V(\chi^m))}$.

The irreducible components of the reduced universal base
$(\operatorname{Spec}\widehat B_0)_{\mathrm{red}}$ are in one-to-one correspondence
with maximal lattice-friendly Minkowski decompositions of $P_m$.
A maximal decomposition $\mathcal D$ into $k>0$ nontrivial summands
gives a component of dimension $k$.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
For $m\notin-S$, there is an explicit finitely generated positively graded
$\mathbb C$-algebra $B$, with parameter degrees in $\mathbb Z_{>0}m$,
and a finitely generated $B$-algebra $\mathcal A$ that is free as a
$B$-module and has special fibre $A$. The completion of this family
at the origin is formally universal for $\operatorname{Def}_m^X$.

The irreducible components of the reduced universal equivariant
base $(\operatorname{Spec}\widehat B)_{\mathrm{red}}$ are in one-to-one
correspondence with maximal lattice-friendly Minkowski
decompositions of $P_m$.
For a maximal decomposition $\mathcal D$ into $k>0$ nontrivial
summands, the corresponding component has dimension $k-1$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:usual-miniversality}
If $\operatorname{Def}^X$ admits a hull, then a corresponding miniversal formal
family can be chosen torus-equivariantly so that its restriction to

Let $B$ be a finitely generated $\mathbb C$-algebra with an augmentation

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:free}
The algebra $\mathcal A_0$ is free over $B_0[U]$, with basis
$\{N_b:b\in\partial_{\mathbb N m}S\}$, and $\mathcal A_0/(U)$ is
free over $B_0$ with basis the images of these monomials.
Consequently,
\[
 (\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A_0,V(U))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec} B_0
\]
is a flat family of $(X,V(\chi^m))$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Division expresses every residue class as a $B_0$-linear
combination of standard monomials, proving spanning. For linear
independence, suppose a finite sum $\sum_{N\in\mathcal N}r_NN$,
with $r_N\in B_0$, belongs to $(G_1,\ldots,G_r)$. If some
$r_N\ne0$, this is a nonzero polynomial with a standard leading
monomial. The Gröbner-basis property would make that monomial
divisible by some $\operatorname{LM}(G_\alpha)=\operatorname{LM}(g_\alpha)$, a contradiction.
Thus all $r_N$ vanish.
The standard monomials are $U^qN_b$, with
$b\in\partial_{\mathbb N m}S$ and $q\ge0$, so this basis gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adaptedfree}
 \mathcal A_0=\bigoplus_{b\in\partial_{\mathbb N m}S}B_0[U]N_b,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_0/(U)=\bigoplus_{b\in\partial_{\mathbb N m}S}B_0\overline{N_b},
\end{equation}
where the bars denote images in the quotient by $U$.
Both algebras are therefore $B_0$-flat. At the origin, the equations
$G_\alpha$ specialize to $g_\alpha$ and $U$ maps to $\chi^m$,
so their special fibres are $A$ and $A/(\chi^m)$, respectively.

 s_1=(8,0),\qquad s_2=(5,1),\qquad
 s_3=(1,5),\qquad s_4=(0,8).

Use the presentation $P=\mathbb C[U,z_0,z_1,z_3,z_4]\twoheadrightarrow A$

primitive. In the basis dual to $((3,-1),m)$, the primitive ray
generators of $\sigma$ are $(-1,2)$ and $(3,2)$.

$[-\tfrac12,\tfrac32]$.

above, first compare total $\ell$-weights. For equal weights,
compare the weights
contributed by the variables other than $U$, namely $(8,6,0,6,8)$ on

degrees in $z_0,z_1,z_3,z_4$. If the degrees are equal as well, use

$z_0>z_1>z_3>z_4>U$.

\begin{aligned}
 g_1&=z_1^2-Uz_0,&g_2&=z_3^2-Uz_4,\\
 g_3&=z_0z_3-U^2z_1,&g_4&=z_1z_4-U^2z_3,\\

\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first monomial of each $g_\alpha$ is its leading monomial.

 z_3g_1-z_1g_5=-Ug_3.

 =\{(8n,0),(0,8n),(8n+5,1),(1,8n+5):n\ge0\}.

       \cup\{z_1z_0^n,z_3z_4^n:n\ge0\}.

\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
 \alpha&d_\alpha&\eta_{\mathbb Z,\alpha}&b_\alpha&W_\alpha\\ \hline

 p_5=p_1p_3=p_2p_4,\qquad p_6=p_3p_4.

 p_1p_3

\end{aligned}

\begin{aligned}
 t_{5,1}&=t_{1,1}+t_{3,1},
 &t_{5,1}&=t_{2,1}+t_{4,1},\\
 t_{5,2}&=t_{3,2}+t_{1,1}t_{3,1},
 &t_{5,2}&=t_{4,2}+t_{2,1}t_{4,1},\\
 t_{5,3}&=t_{1,1}t_{3,2},
 &t_{5,3}&=t_{2,1}t_{4,2}.
\end{aligned}

The identity $p_6=p_3p_4$ similarly gives

\begin{aligned}
 t_{6,1}&=t_{3,1}+t_{4,1},\\
 t_{6,2}&=t_{3,2}+t_{3,1}t_{4,1}+t_{4,2},\\
 t_{6,3}&=t_{3,2}t_{4,1}+t_{3,1}t_{4,2},\\
 t_{6,4}&=t_{3,2}t_{4,2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The ideal $J_{\mathrm{coeff}}$ is generated by the differences

\label{def:pair}
Let $m\in\mathbb L$ be primitive. Let $\operatorname{Art}_m$ be the
category of local Artinian $\mathbb L$-graded $\mathbb C$-algebras
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bases}
 R=\mathbb C\oplus\bigoplus_{j\ge1}R_{jm},\qquad
 \mathfrak m_R=\bigoplus_{j\ge1}R_{jm},
\end{equation}
with graded local $\mathbb C$-algebra homomorphisms.
The functor $\operatorname{Def}_m^X:\operatorname{Art}_m\to\mathrm{Sets}$
assigns to $R$ the isomorphism classes of pairs $(A_R,\iota_R)$,
where $A_R$ is a finitely generated flat commutative $\mathbb L$-graded
$R$-algebra
and $\iota_R:A_R/\mathfrak m_RA_R\xrightarrow{\sim}A$ is a
graded $\mathbb C$-algebra isomorphism. An isomorphism
$(A_R,\iota_R)\to(A'_R,\iota'_R)$ is a graded $R$-algebra
isomorphism $\varphi:A_R\to A'_R$ satisfying
$\iota'_R\circ\overline\varphi=\iota_R$, where
$\overline\varphi:A_R/\mathfrak m_RA_R\to
A'_R/\mathfrak m_RA'_R$ is the map induced by $\varphi$ on the
special fibres.

If $m\in S$, the functor
$\operatorname{Def}_m^{(X,V(\chi^m))}:\operatorname{Art}_m\to\mathrm{Sets}$
assigns to $R$ the isomorphism classes of triples
$(A_R,I_R,\iota_R)$, where $(A_R,\iota_R)$ is as above and
$I_R\subset A_R$ is a homogeneous ideal such that $A_R/I_R$
is $R$-flat and
$\iota_R\bigl((I_R+\mathfrak m_RA_R)/\mathfrak m_RA_R\bigr)=(\chi^m)$.
Isomorphisms additionally satisfy $\varphi(I_R)=I'_R$.

For a base map $R\to R'$, both functors act by the base change
$A_R\mapsto A_R\otimes_RR'$, with the induced special-fibre
identification; for pairs, the ideal becomes
$I_R(A_R\otimes_RR')$. We call these the torus-equivariant
deformation functors on $\operatorname{Art}_m$.

\label{lem:basis}
Let $R$ be a local Artinian ring and $M$ a flat $R$-module.
Any lifts to $M$ of a basis of $M/\mathfrak m_RM$ form an
$R$-basis of $M$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:divisor-lifts}
For every $0\ne s\in S$, let $I_R\subset A_R$ be a homogeneous
ideal such that $A_R/I_R$ is $R$-flat and $I_R$ maps onto
$(\chi^s)$ under the special-fibre map $A_R\to A$.
Then $I_R=(f)$ for a unique homogeneous element $f\in(A_R)_s$
mapping to $\chi^s$. Conversely, every such lift $f$ generates
an ideal with these properties. This correspondence commutes
with base change.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $A_R$ and $A_R/I_R$ are $R$-flat, the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow I_R\longrightarrow A_R\longrightarrow A_R/I_R
 \longrightarrow0
\]
shows that $I_R$ is $R$-flat. Moreover, the sequence remains exact
after reduction modulo $\mathfrak m_R$. Thus $I_R/\mathfrak m_RI_R$ identifies
with $(\chi^s)\subset A$, and we can choose a homogeneous
lift $f\in I_R$ of $\chi^s$.
Multiplication $A_R\to I_R$ by $f$ is an isomorphism after reduction.
Nilpotence of $\mathfrak m_R$ gives surjectivity; flatness of $I_R$
then makes its kernel have zero reduction, hence zero. Thus
$I_R=(f)$ and $f$ is regular.

For uniqueness, we first show that the degree-zero subalgebra
$(A_R)_0$ consists only of scalars, so $(A_R)_0=\mathbb C$.
Choose homogeneous lifts $e_b$ of $\chi^b$ for $b\in S$, with
$e_0=1$. They form an $R$-basis by Lemma~\ref{lem:basis}, so
\[
 (A_R)_0=\bigoplus_{b\in S}R_{-b}e_b=\mathbb C\cdot1.
\]
Indeed, $R_{-b}\ne0$ requires $b=-jm$ for some $j\ge0$;
since $0\ne m\in S$ and $S\cap(-S)=\{0\}$, this is possible
only for $b=0$. Now let $g\in(I_R)_s$ be another lift of $\chi^s$.
Write $g=af$ using $I_R=(f)$. Taking the degree-$s$ component
gives $g=a_0f$, where $a_0\in(A_R)_0=\mathbb C$. Reduction to the
special fibre gives $\chi^s=a_0\chi^s$, hence $a_0=1$ and $g=f$.

Conversely, let $f$ be any homogeneous lift of $\chi^s$ and
choose homogeneous lifts $e_b$ of $\chi^b$ for
$b\in\partial_{\mathbb N s}S$. By \eqref{eq:boundarydecomp}, the
elements $f^qe_b$, $q\ge0$, lift a basis of $A$ and are an
$R$-basis by Lemma~\ref{lem:basis}. Hence $A_R$ is free over
$R[f]$, and $A_R/(f)$ is $R$-free with basis the images of the
$e_b$. Uniqueness of the generator proves compatibility with base change.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:marked-pair}\label{prop:versal}
The completion $\widehat\pi_0$ of the pair family constructed in
Section~\ref{sec:finite} induces a natural isomorphism
$h_{\widehat B_0}\simeq\operatorname{Def}_m^{(X,V(\chi^m))}$; thus
$\widehat B_0$ prorepresents this functor.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Consider a pair deformation $(A_R,I_R)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:divisor-lifts} determines a unique homogeneous
generator $U_R$ of $I_R$ lifting $\chi^m$. Every other generator
degree $s_i$ is indecomposable in $S$ and differs from $m$, so
$s_i-jm\notin S$ for all $j\ge1$.
Two homogeneous lifts of $\chi^{s_i}$ differ
by an element of $(\mathfrak m_RA_R)_{s_i}$.
For every $p\ge1$, equation~\eqref{eq:flatgraded} gives
\[
 \bigl(\mathfrak m_R^pA_R/\mathfrak m_R^{p+1}A_R\bigr)_{s_i}
 \simeq\bigoplus_{j\ge1}
 (\mathfrak m_R^p/\mathfrak m_R^{p+1})_{jm}
 \otimes_\mathbb C A_{s_i-jm}=0.
\]
Since $\mathfrak m_R$ is nilpotent, this implies
$(\mathfrak m_RA_R)_{s_i}=0$. Hence the homogeneous lift
$\widetilde x_i$ is unique.

The elements $N_s(\widetilde x,U_R)$ lift the monomial basis of
$A$ and form an $R$-basis by Lemma~\ref{lem:basis}. In particular,
the chosen elements generate $A_R$. Expanding the central relations
in this basis gives uniquely
\[
 g_\alpha(\widetilde x,U_R)=
 \sum_{j=1}^{\eta_{\mathbb Z,\alpha}}r_{\alpha,j}
 N_{d_\alpha-jm}(\widetilde x,U_R),
 \qquad r_{\alpha,j}\in R_{jm}.
\]
Consequently the lifted equations $G_\alpha$ of \eqref{eq:G}
vanish after setting $t_{\alpha,j}=r_{\alpha,j}$.
Recall that $R_{\alpha\beta}$ is the remainder obtained in
Section~\ref{sec:finite} by dividing the $S$-polynomial
$S_{\alpha\beta}$ by the ordered list $(G_1,\ldots,G_r)$.
The division identity expresses $S_{\alpha\beta}-R_{\alpha\beta}$
as a sum of multiples of the $G_\gamma$. Since the $S$-polynomial
and every $G_\gamma$ vanish in $A_R$, so does its remainder.
Since this remainder is a linear combination of standard monomials
whose images form an $R$-basis, each of its coefficients is zero.
Thus the assignment $t_{\alpha,j}\mapsto r_{\alpha,j}$ annihilates
$J_{\mathrm{coeff}}$ and these values
define a graded map $B_0\to R$ and an isomorphism of pair
deformations $\mathcal A_0\otimes_{B_0}R\xrightarrow{\sim}A_R$
sending $U$ to $U_R$.
Conversely, every such map gives a pair deformation by the
flatness in \eqref{eq:adaptedfree}. An isomorphism of pairs preserves
$U_R$ and all the other
uniquely determined generator lifts, hence all the coefficients
$r_{\alpha,j}$. The classifying map is unique.

The algebraic family already classifies pair deformations over
every allowed Artinian base $R$. We use the completion
$\widehat B_0$ to express this universal property over a complete
local parameter algebra, as required for prorepresentability.
Indeed, every graded map $B_0\to R$ sends $\mathfrak m_0$ into
$\mathfrak m_R$. If $\mathfrak m_R^N=0$, it factors through
$B_0/\mathfrak m_0^N$ and extends uniquely to a continuous local
graded map $\widehat B_0\to R$. Restriction to $B_0$ gives the
inverse correspondence, and pullback of $\widehat\pi_0$ gives
the same pair deformation. Under a graded base map $R\to R'$,
the coefficients $r_{\alpha,j}$ in the displayed expressions for
$g_\alpha(\widetilde x,U_R)$ map to their images in $R'$.
The standard monomials remain a basis after base change, so these
are precisely the coefficients determining the classifying map
of the pulled-back pair. Hence the correspondence is natural in
$R$, giving the bijection
$h_{\widehat B_0}(R)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Def}_m^{(X,V(\chi^m))}(R)$.\qedhere

\label{cor:ordinary-pair}
If $\operatorname{Def}^{(X,V(\chi^m))}$ admits a hull with ring

Put $Y=(X,V(\chi^m))$. We first check equivariant lifting;
the same argument applies to $Y=X$.
Fix equivariant lifting data across a small graded extension
$R'\twoheadrightarrow R$ with kernel $E$.
Formal smoothness of the hull transformation
$h_{H_Y}\to\operatorname{Def}^Y$ supplies a lift of the prescribed base map
$H_Y\to R$. The lifts $H_Y\to R'$ form an affine space:
their differences are precisely the linear maps
$\mathfrak m_{H_Y}/\mathfrak m_{H_Y}^2\to E$.
Linear reductivity gives a fixed point for the induced
$\mathbb T$-action, hence an equivariant lift.
Keeping the identification over $R$ fixed, its difference from
the prescribed deformation lies in $(E\otimes_\mathbb C T_Y^1)^{\mathbb T}$.
The equivariant Kodaira--Spencer isomorphism supplies an invariant
correction cancelling this difference \cite{Rim}.
The comparison isomorphisms extending the fixed identification
form a torsor under $E\otimes_\mathbb C T_Y^0$, so linear reductivity
also supplies an equivariant isomorphism.
Here $T_Y^1$ and $T_Y^0$ denote the tangent and
infinitesimal automorphism spaces of the deformation problem for $Y$; for pairs, $T_Y^0$ consists
of derivations preserving the divisor ideal. All these modules
are rational $\mathbb T$-modules.

Every graded map $H_Y\to R$, with $R\in\operatorname{Art}_m$,
factors through $H_{Y,m}$. Thus the restricted family is formally
versal for $\operatorname{Def}_m^Y$. Since $J_Y\subset(t_1,\ldots,t_N)^2$,
its Kodaira--Spencer map is an isomorphism onto
$\bigoplus_{j\ge1}T_Y^1(-jm)$, so it is miniversal.

Universality in Proposition~\ref{prop:marked-pair} gives a graded
classifying map $D\to C$. Equivariant versality gives compatible
classifying maps $C\to D/\mathfrak m_D^n$, hence a map $C\to D$.
Both induce tangent-space isomorphisms, so their composites are
automorphisms of complete Noetherian local rings.
Consequently the bases are isomorphic, and the classifying
isomorphisms identify their formal families.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:latticefriendly}
A rational Minkowski decomposition
\[
 \mathcal D:\qquad P=Q_1+\cdots+Q_k,\qquad
 \operatorname{tail}(Q_i)=\kappa,
\]
is \emph{lattice-friendly} if, for every $c\in\kappa^\vee\cap M$, all but at most one of the minimizing faces
\[
 \operatorname{face}(Q_i,c)
 =\{v\in Q_i:\langle c,v\rangle=\min_{w\in Q_i}\langle c,w\rangle\}
\]
contain a point of $N$. This is \cite[Definition 10.6]{ACFpoly}. Equivalently, for each vertex decomposition $v=v_1+\cdots+v_k$ induced by a minimizing linear form, at most one $v_i$ is outside $N$: one obtains the vertex condition by choosing a form in a full-dimensional normal cone, and the face condition by refining a minimizing face to a vertex.

Decompositions are considered up to permutation and integral translations
of the summands whose sum is zero. A summand $n+\kappa$ with $n\in N$
is \emph{trivial}. We always remove such a summand when another summand
$Q_j$ is present, replacing $(n+\kappa)+Q_j$ by $n+Q_j$.
A refinement is \emph{proper} if, after removing trivial summands,
it replaces at least one summand by two or more nontrivial summands.
A decomposition is \emph{maximal} if it admits no proper
lattice-friendly refinement.

Ilten--Vollmert \cite{IltenVollmert}
constructed homogeneous deformations of toric varieties in arbitrary
degree from admissible Minkowski decompositions. Their vertex
condition \cite[Definition 2.2]{IltenVollmert} agrees with the
lattice-friendly condition used here.

Put $\Lambda=\mathbb C[\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k]$. Substitute for
$t_{\beta,j}$ the coefficient of $U^{\eta_{\mathbb Z,\beta}-j}$ in
$\prod_i(U-\lambda_i)^{n_{i\beta}}$. We verify Buchberger's

\[
 \widetilde G_\beta
 =x^{\boldsymbol\alpha_\beta}
  -\prod_i(U-\lambda_i)^{n_{i\beta}}W_\beta.
\]
Apply the division procedure of Section~\ref{sec:finite} to a monomial
$x^{\boldsymbol a}$, treating $U$ as part of the coefficient ring
$\Lambda[U]$. At each step, replace a factor
$x^{\boldsymbol\alpha_\beta}$ by
$\prod_i(U-\lambda_i)^{n_{i\beta}}W_\beta$.
The monomial in the $x$-variables strictly decreases in the order
induced by Section~\ref{sec:finite}, so the process terminates at the
unique standard monomial $W_d$, where $d=\sum_j a_jc_j$.
Write $W_d=x^{\boldsymbol b}$. It represents
$\chi^{(d,\eta_{\mathbb Z}(d))}$, so equality of degrees gives
$d=\sum_j b_jc_j$ and
$\eta_{\mathbb Z}(d)=\sum_j b_j\eta_{\mathbb Z}(c_j)$.
For each $i$, subadditivity gives
$\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(d)\le\sum_j b_j\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(c_j)$.
The sum of these inequalities is an equality, since
$\eta_{\mathbb Z}=\sum_i\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}$. Hence each is an equality:
\[
 \sum_j b_j\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(c_j)=\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(d)
 \qquad\text{for every }i.
\]
The same identity for $W_\beta$ shows that a division step using
$\widetilde G_\beta$ decreases the sum of the $\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(c_j)$
over the factors of the current monomial by $n_{i\beta}$,
while multiplying its coefficient by $(U-\lambda_i)^{n_{i\beta}}$.
Thus the remainder of $x^{\boldsymbol a}$ is
\[
 \prod_i(U-\lambda_i)^{
       \sum_j a_j\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(c_j)-\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(d)}W_d,
\]
independently of the choices made during division.
Each $S$-polynomial compares the two first division steps
obtained by cancelling the same least common multiple with
two different equations. The resulting expressions have the same
remainder by the displayed formula, so every $S$-polynomial has
zero remainder.
Buchberger's criterion therefore shows that the
$\widetilde G_\beta$ form a monic Gr\"obner basis over
$\Lambda[U]$, with standard monomials $W_d$.

To check the equations of $B_0$, recall that each remainder

by subtracting multiples of the $G_\beta$.
It therefore belongs to $(G_1,\ldots,G_r)$ and is a linear
combination of standard monomials.
After replacing the parameters by their prescribed polynomials

The pullback and its quotient by $(U)$ are free by
\eqref{eq:adaptedfree}, proving flatness of the pair.
Applying this division procedure to $W_cW_d$ gives
\eqref{eq:decomposition-family}.

\label{prop:recover}

Then every closed point of $\operatorname{Spec} B_0$ is obtained from a

Fix a closed point $q\in\operatorname{Spec} B_0$. This amounts to choosing complex
values $t_{\beta,j}(q)$ satisfying the equations defining $B_0$.
Substitute $t_{\beta,j}(q)$ for each parameter $t_{\beta,j}$ in the
family equations. In particular,
\[
 p_\beta(U)=U^{\eta_{\mathbb Z,\beta}}
 +\sum_{j=1}^{\eta_{\mathbb Z,\beta}}
 t_{\beta,j}(q)U^{\eta_{\mathbb Z,\beta}-j}\in\mathbb C[U],
\]
and the fibre over $q$ has multiplication
\[
 e_ce_d=p_{c,d}(U)e_{c+d}.
\]
Recall that $\beta$ indexes the defining equations from
Section~\ref{sec:finite}, whereas $c,d\in C$ index basis elements
$e_c,e_d$.
Throughout this proof, $p_\beta$ and $p_{c,d}$ denote the polynomials
after this substitution. Their coefficients are now complex numbers, while $U$ remains
an indeterminate. Their leading coefficient is still $1$, so
their degrees are unchanged.
We will recover one Minkowski summand for each distinct root
of these polynomials and use that root as its parameter value.

First, we prove that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-sets}
 \bigcup_{c,d\in C}\{a\in\mathbb C:p_{c,d}(a)=0\}
 =\bigcup_{\beta=1}^{r}\{a\in\mathbb C:p_\beta(a)=0\}.
\end{equation}
To compute $p_{c,d}$, apply the division procedure of
Section~\ref{sec:finite} to $W_cW_d$, treating $U$ as part of the
coefficient ring $\mathbb C[U]$. By \eqref{eq:finiteeta}, each step replaces
a factor $\operatorname{LM}(g_\beta)$ by $p_\beta(U)W_\beta$. The monomial in the
other variables strictly decreases, and the remainder is
$p_{c,d}(U)W_{c+d}$. Since each step contributes a factor $p_\beta(U)$,
$p_{c,d}$ is a product of the $p_\beta$.

Conversely, fix a defining equation $G_\beta=0$. Since
$\operatorname{LM}(g_\beta)=\prod_jx_j^{\alpha_{\beta,j}}$, $x_j=e_{c_j}$ and
$W_\beta=e_{c_\beta}$ in the fibre algebra, this equation reads
\[
 \prod_j e_{c_j}^{\alpha_{\beta,j}}=p_\beta(U)e_{c_\beta}.
\]
Compute the same product by multiplying its factors one at a time
using \eqref{eq:F}. Each multiplication contributes a polynomial
$p_{c,d}(U)$, and the final basis element is $e_{c_\beta}$ because
$c_\beta=\sum_j\alpha_{\beta,j}c_j$. As the $e_c$ form a basis over
$\mathbb C[U]$, the coefficient obtained in this way must equal $p_\beta(U)$.
Thus every $p_\beta$ is a product of the $p_{c,d}$, and the
multiplication polynomials recover all original parameters
$t_{\beta,j}(q)$ as the coefficients of the $p_\beta$.
The two product statements prove \eqref{eq:root-sets}.
This common set is finite, since there are finitely many $p_\beta$,
and nonempty because some $p_\beta$ has positive degree.
Denote the distinct roots by $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ and write
\[
 \eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(c,d)=\operatorname{ord}_{a_i}p_{c,d},\qquad
 p_{c,d}(U)=\prod_i(U-a_i)^{\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(c,d)}.
\]
These multiplicities are nonnegative integers whose sum is
$\deg p_{c,d}=\eta_{\mathbb Z}(c,d)$.

Next, we seek integer-valued functions $g_i:C\to\mathbb Z$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplicity-difference}
 \eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(c,d)=g_i(c)+g_i(d)-g_i(c+d)
 \qquad(c,d\in C).
\end{equation}
To construct them, put $K=\mathbb C(U)$, where every $p_{c,d}$ is invertible.
Multiplication by $e_c$ is injective: it sends each basis element
$e_d$ to a nonzero multiple of $e_{c+d}$. We may therefore invert
all $e_c$. Since $C-C=M$, every degree of the resulting algebra
contains a unit $e_c/e_d$. The multiplication rule shows that
any two such fractions of the same degree are proportional over $K$,
so each homogeneous component is one-dimensional. Choose units
in the degrees of a lattice basis of $M$. Their products and
inverses give Laurent monomials $x^c$ for $c\in M$, satisfying
$x^cx^d=x^{c+d}$. Each $e_c$ is a nonzero scalar
multiple of $x^c$, and comparison with \eqref{eq:F} gives
\[
 e_c=f_c(U)x^c,\qquad
 p_{c,d}(U)=\frac{f_c(U)f_d(U)}{f_{c+d}(U)},
 \qquad f_c(U)\in K^\times.
\]
Set $g_i(c)=\operatorname{ord}_{a_i}f_c$. Taking orders at $a_i$
in the quotient formula proves \eqref{eq:multiplicity-difference},
since $\operatorname{ord}_{a_i}p_{c,d}=\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(c,d)$.
In particular, the $g_i$ are subadditive because these multiplicities
are nonnegative. Put $L(c)=\sum_i g_i(c)-\eta_{\mathbb Z}(c)$.
Since $\sum_i\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}(c,d)=\eta_{\mathbb Z}(c,d)$, summing
\eqref{eq:multiplicity-difference} over $i$ gives
$L(c+d)=L(c)+L(d)$. As $C-C=M$, the rule
$L(c-d)=L(c)-L(d)$ extends $L$ uniquely to an integral linear form
on $M$. Replace $g_1$ by $g_1-L$, leaving the other $g_i$ unchanged
and retaining the same notation. Additivity of $L$ ensures that
\eqref{eq:multiplicity-difference} is unchanged, while now
$\sum_i g_i=\eta_{\mathbb Z}$.

We now turn the $g_i$ into support functions of polyhedra.
Choose an integer $D\ge2$ such that $Dv\in N$ for every vertex
$v$ of $P$. This removes rounding on $DC$, since
$\eta_{\mathbb Z}(Dc)=\eta(Dc)=D\eta(c)$. Put
\[
 \eta_i(c)=D^{-1}g_i(Dc)\qquad(c\in C),
 \qquad\sum_i\eta_i(c)=\eta(c).
\]
On a full-dimensional normal cone $\nu$ of $P$, the function
$\eta$ is linear. Hence, for $c,d\in\nu\cap M$,
\[
 \sum_i\bigl(g_i(Dc)+g_i(Dd)-g_i(D(c+d))\bigr)=0.
\]
Every summand is nonnegative by subadditivity, so each is zero.
Consequently $g_i(D\,\cdot)$ is additive
on $\nu\cap M$. This monoid generates $M$ as a group:
for $w\in\operatorname{int}(\nu)\cap M$ and $a\in M$,
write $a=(a+nw)-nw$ with both terms in $\nu\cap M$ for $n\gg0$.
Thus $D^{-1}g_i(D\,\cdot)$ extends uniquely to a linear form
$\ell_{i,\nu}$ taking values in $D^{-1}\mathbb Z$ on $M$.

On intersections of normal cones, the linear forms agree on
lattice points, since they extend the same function
$D^{-1}g_i(D\,\cdot)$. These lattice points span the intersection,
so the forms agree everywhere on it. They therefore
glue to a continuous piecewise linear function $\eta_i$ on
$\kappa^\vee$. Subadditivity and $\sum_i\eta_i=\eta$ hold
on lattice points; scaling and continuity extend them first
to rational points and then to all of $\kappa^\vee$.

Write $\ell_{i,\nu}(c)=-\langle c,v_{i,\nu}\rangle$ with
$v_{i,\nu}\in D^{-1}N$. Each linear piece is bounded above
by $\eta_i$ everywhere: for $y\in\operatorname{int}(\nu)$,
$c\in\kappa^\vee$ and sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$,
we have $y-\varepsilon c\in\nu$, and
\[
 \eta_i(y)\le\eta_i(y-\varepsilon c)+\varepsilon\eta_i(c)
 =\eta_i(y)-\varepsilon\ell_{i,\nu}(c)+\varepsilon\eta_i(c).
\]
Hence $\eta_i=\max_\nu\ell_{i,\nu}$. Define
\[
 Q_i=\{v\in N_{\mathbb R}:\langle c,v\rangle\ge-\eta_i(c)
                         \text{ for all }c\in\kappa^\vee\}.
\]
Then
\[
 Q_i=\operatorname{conv}\{v_{i,\nu}:\nu\text{ a full-dimensional
 normal cone of }P\}+\kappa.
\]
Thus $Q_i$ has tail $\kappa$, its vertices lie in $D^{-1}N$, and
$\eta_i(c)=-\min_{v\in Q_i}\langle c,v\rangle$.
Since support functions add under Minkowski sums,
$\sum_i\eta_i=\eta$ gives $P=\sum_iQ_i$.

We first show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounding-recovery}
 \lceil\eta_i(c)\rceil=g_i(c)
 \qquad(c\in C,\ 1\le i\le k).
\end{equation}
Subadditivity gives $g_i(Dc)\le Dg_i(c)$, hence
$g_i(c)\ge D^{-1}g_i(Dc)=\eta_i(c)$. Together with
$\sum_i g_i=\eta_{\mathbb Z}$ and $\sum_i\eta_i=\eta$, this yields
\[
 0\le g_i(c)-\eta_i(c),\qquad
 \sum_i\bigl(g_i(c)-\eta_i(c)\bigr)
       =\lceil\eta(c)\rceil-\eta(c)<1.
\]
Since $g_i(c)$ is integral, these inequalities prove
\eqref{eq:rounding-recovery}; in particular, $g_i=\eta_{i,\mathbb Z}$.

We now show that the decomposition is lattice-friendly.
Put $\epsilon(c)=\lceil\eta(c)\rceil-\eta(c)\in[0,1)$.
We first prove that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonintegral-count}
 \#\{i:\eta_i(c)\notin\mathbb Z\}
 =\epsilon(c)+\epsilon((D-1)c)<2
 \qquad(c\in C).
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:rounding-recovery} and $\sum_i g_i=\eta_{\mathbb Z}$,
$\epsilon(c)$ is the sum of the rounding errors
$\lceil\eta_i(c)\rceil-\eta_i(c)$.
Since $D\eta_i(c)$ is integral and
$\eta_i((D-1)c)=(D-1)\eta_i(c)$, the two errors at $c$ and
$(D-1)c$ sum to $\lceil\eta_i(c)\rceil-\lfloor\eta_i(c)\rfloor$.
This is $0$ when $\eta_i(c)$ is integral and $1$ otherwise.
Summing over $i$ proves the equality in \eqref{eq:nonintegral-count};
the strict inequality follows because both values of $\epsilon$
are less than $1$. Thus at most one $\eta_i(c)$ is nonintegral
for each $c\in C$.

We verify the equivalent vertex condition of
Definition~\ref{def:latticefriendly}. Fix a full-dimensional normal
cone $\nu$ of $P$, and let $v_{i,\nu}$ be the corresponding
vertices of the $Q_i$. Suppose that $v_{i,\nu}$ and $v_{j,\nu}$,
with $i\ne j$, are both nonintegral. We will find a single
$c\in\operatorname{int}(\nu)\cap M$ for which both support values
$\eta_i(c)$ and $\eta_j(c)$ are nonintegral.
Since $Dv_{i,\nu},Dv_{j,\nu}\in N$, the pairings define homomorphisms
\[
 M/DM\longrightarrow\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z,\qquad
 [c]\longmapsto\langle c,v_{i,\nu}\rangle\bmod\mathbb Z
\]
and likewise for $j$. Nonintegrality of the vertices makes both
maps nonzero, so their kernels are proper subgroups.
Two proper subgroups cannot cover a group: if one contains the
other, their union is proper; otherwise, the sum of an element
from each set difference lies outside both.
Choose a residue class outside both kernels and a representative
$c_0\in M$. For $w\in\operatorname{int}(\nu)\cap M$ and $n\gg0$,
$c=c_0+nDw$ lies in $\operatorname{int}(\nu)$ and represents the
same class. Hence both
$\eta_i(c)=-\langle c,v_{i,\nu}\rangle$ and
$\eta_j(c)=-\langle c,v_{j,\nu}\rangle$ are nonintegral,
contradicting \eqref{eq:nonintegral-count}.
Thus every induced vertex decomposition has at most one
nonintegral summand, so the decomposition is lattice-friendly
by Definition~\ref{def:latticefriendly}.
Moreover, \eqref{eq:rounding-recovery} shows that
\eqref{eq:decomposition-family}, with $\lambda_i=a_i$, has exactly
the multiplicities used in the factorization of $p_{c,d}$ above.
It therefore recovers all multiplication polynomials. Hence the
parameters of the family associated with
$\mathcal D:P=Q_1+\cdots+Q_k$, evaluated at $\lambda_i=a_i$,
equal $t_{\beta,j}(q)$. Therefore
$\psi_{\mathcal D}(a_1,\ldots,a_k)=q$, as required.

Finally, we prove uniqueness and exclude trivial summands.
For pairwise distinct parameter values, polynomial factorization
determines the multiplicity functions root by root, up to permutation.
If $g_i$ and $g'_i$ give the same multiplicities, their
difference is additive and extends to an integral linear form
on $M=C-C$, so their summands differ by integral translations.
These translations sum to zero because the support functions
sum to $\eta$. A zero multiplicity function makes $g_i$ integral
linear, hence $\eta_i=g_i$ and $Q_i=n_i+\kappa$ for some $n_i\in N$.
Conversely, such a trivial summand has zero multiplicities.
Each chosen root $a_i$ occurs in some $p_{c,d}$, so its multiplicity
function is nonzero. Thus all recovered summands are nontrivial.

For a lattice-friendly decomposition $\mathcal D$ into $k$ nontrivial

Assume first that $B_0\ne\mathbb C$. We first prove that each image
$\mathcal C^0_{\mathcal D}$ is closed and irreducible of the stated
dimension. Fix $\mathcal D:P=Q_1+\cdots+Q_k$ and recall that
$B_{0,\mathcal D}=\operatorname{im}\psi_{\mathcal D}^*$ is a
subring of $\Lambda=\mathbb C[\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k]$.
For each nontrivial $Q_i$, some $n_{i\beta}$ is positive, by the
last argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:recover}. The polynomial
\[
 p_\beta(U)=\prod_{j=1}^k(U-\lambda_j)^{n_{j\beta}}
 \in B_{0,\mathcal D}[U]
\]
is monic and vanishes at $U=\lambda_i$. Thus every $\lambda_i$
is integral over $B_{0,\mathcal D}$, so $\Lambda$ is finite over
this ring. Consequently $\psi_{\mathcal D}$ is finite and
surjective onto $\operatorname{Spec} B_{0,\mathcal D}$, a closed subscheme of
$\operatorname{Spec} B_0$. Since $B_{0,\mathcal D}$ is a domain and its integral
extension $\Lambda$ has dimension $k$, this proves the assertion
about $\mathcal C^0_{\mathcal D}$.

Next, we show that these images form a finite cover of the reduced
base. The fixed division procedure expresses each $p_{c,d}$ as a
product of the $p_\beta$. Taking multiplicities therefore shows
that the vector $(n_{i\beta})_\beta$ determines the entire
multiplicity function of $Q_i$, hence $Q_i$ up to integral
translation by Proposition~\ref{prop:recover}. These vectors are
nonzero, have nonnegative integer entries, and satisfy
\[
 \sum_i n_{i\beta}=\eta_{\mathbb Z,\beta},\qquad
 k\le\sum_\beta\eta_{\mathbb Z,\beta}.
\]
Only finitely many collections of such vectors are possible.
Their decompositions are determined up to the equivalence of
Definition~\ref{def:latticefriendly}, since the translations sum
to zero when both decompositions sum to $P$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:recover} places every closed point of
$\operatorname{Spec} B_0$ in one of these images. Their union is closed, so
these finitely many closed irreducible images cover the reduced base.

We now prove the containment criterion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:component-refinement}
 \mathcal C^0_{\mathcal D}\subseteq\mathcal C^0_{\mathcal E}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal E\text{ refines }\mathcal D.
\end{equation}
Write $\mathcal E:P=R_1+\cdots+R_s$, with parameters
$\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_s$. If $\mathcal E$ refines $\mathcal D$,
then, after the allowed integral translations, there is a
partition into nonempty blocks $J_1,\ldots,J_k$ such that
\[
 Q_i=\sum_{j\in J_i}R_j,\qquad
 n^{\mathcal D}_{i\beta}
 =\sum_{j\in J_i}n^{\mathcal E}_{j\beta}.
\]
The second equality follows because lattice-friendliness makes
the rounded support functions additive within each block.
Setting $\mu_j=\lambda_i$ for $j\in J_i$ in the family for
$\mathcal E$ therefore gives exactly the polynomials $p_\beta$
for $\mathcal D$, proving the required containment.

Conversely, assume the containment in
\eqref{eq:component-refinement}. Choose pairwise distinct
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k$ and write
\[
 q=\psi_{\mathcal D}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)
  =\psi_{\mathcal E}(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_s).
\]
Every parameter value on either side occurs as a root of some
$p_\beta$, since every summand is nontrivial. Equality of these
polynomials therefore shows that their distinct root values
coincide. Thus $J_i=\{j:\mu_j=\lambda_i\}$ form a partition into
nonempty blocks. Comparing multiplicities at $\lambda_i$ gives
$n^{\mathcal D}_{i\beta}=\sum_{j\in J_i}n^{\mathcal E}_{j\beta}$.
Grouping the $R_j$ by these blocks preserves lattice-friendliness,
and Proposition~\ref{prop:recover} identifies each grouped sum with
$Q_i$ up to integral translation. The translations sum to zero
because both decompositions sum to $P$. Hence $\mathcal E$ refines
$\mathcal D$, proving \eqref{eq:component-refinement}.
A proper refinement increases the number of nontrivial summands
and therefore the dimension of the image. Together with the
finite cover above, \eqref{eq:component-refinement} shows that
maximal decompositions correspond bijectively to the irreducible
components of the reduced base.

We next show that completion preserves these components.
For each maximal decomposition $\mathcal D$, put
$I_{\mathcal D}=\ker\psi_{\mathcal D}^*$, so
$B_0/I_{\mathcal D}=B_{0,\mathcal D}$.
Exactness of completion \cite[Tag 00MB]{Stacks} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completed-component}
 \widehat B_0/I_{\mathcal D}\widehat B_0
 \simeq\widehat B_{0,\mathcal D}.
\end{equation}
The degree and maximal-ideal filtrations of the finitely generated
positively graded domain $B_{0,\mathcal D}$ are cofinal.
Its completion consists of formal sums of homogeneous elements,
and the product of two such nonzero sums has a nonzero lowest-degree
term. Hence $\widehat B_{0,\mathcal D}$ is a domain, and
$B_{0,\mathcal D}\to\widehat B_{0,\mathcal D}$ is injective.
By \eqref{eq:completed-component}, the extended ideals
$I_{\mathcal D}\widehat B_0$ are therefore prime and contract to
$I_{\mathcal D}$; in particular, they remain distinct and none
contains another. Their intersection is nilpotent: the intersection
of the $I_{\mathcal D}$ is the nilradical of the Noetherian ring
$B_0$, and exactness of completion preserves this finite intersection.
Thus the extended ideals are exactly the minimal primes of
$\widehat B_0$, proving the component correspondence after completion.
Their dimensions are unchanged, and completing
\eqref{eq:pair-component-family} gives their families.

Finally, suppose $B_0=\mathbb C$. All positive-degree parameters then
vanish. If some $\eta_{\mathbb Z,\beta}>0$, the one-summand family of
Proposition~\ref{prop:decomposition-family} would send
$t_{\beta,1}$ to the nonzero polynomial
$-\eta_{\mathbb Z,\beta}\lambda$, a contradiction.
Thus all $\eta_{\mathbb Z,\beta}=0$, and division gives $p_{c,d}=1$
for every $c,d$. In any lattice-friendly decomposition, the
nonnegative multiplicities of the summands therefore sum to zero,
so each is zero. By the last argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:recover}, every summand is trivial.
Only the trivial decomposition remains, and both bases are single points.

 p_1=p_2=U-\lambda_0,\qquad

\label{lem:truncationcohomology}
For every $j\ge1$, restriction of homogeneous Harrison cochains induces
isomorphisms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truncationcohomology}
 T_{X_\tau}^i(-jm)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }T_{X_\sigma}^i(-jm),
 \qquad i=1,2.
\end{equation}
These are the tangent and obstruction maps associated to $\mathcal R$.

<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
The case $\tau=\sigma$ is immediate. Assume $\tau\ne\sigma$
and fix $j\ge1$. We first compare the quasilinear complexes
for $\sigma$ and $\tau$. We then identify this
comparison with the restriction of Harrison cochains and check
its compatibility with lifting deformations.

For a ray $\rho$ of either cone, let $v_\rho\in\mathbb L^*$ denote
its primitive lattice generator. For $\gamma=\sigma,\tau$ and
$F\preceq\gamma$, put
\[
 K_{\gamma,F}^{jm}
 =\{a\in S_\gamma\setminus\{0\}:
   \langle a,v_\rho\rangle<j\langle m,v_\rho\rangle
   \text{ for every ray }\rho\preceq F\}.
\]
For $F=0$, this is $S_\gamma\setminus\{0\}$.
Let $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{ql}}(K,\mathbb C)$ denote the functions
$f:K\to\mathbb C$ satisfying $f(a+b)=f(a)+f(b)$ whenever
$a,b,a+b\in K$. By \cite[Theorem~5.3]{AltmannSletsjoe},
which applies to arbitrary rational polyhedral cones,
$T_{X_\gamma}^i(-jm)$ is the $i$th cohomology, for $i=1,2$, of
\[
 C_\gamma^p(jm)=
 \bigoplus_{\substack{F\preceq\gamma\\\dim F=p}}
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{ql}}(K_{\gamma,F}^{jm},\mathbb C),
\]
with the signed face-restriction differentials.

We will show that restriction induces an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quasilinear-restriction}
 C_\tau^\bullet(jm)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }C_\sigma^\bullet(jm).
\end{equation}
A nonzero face has $K_{\gamma,F}^{jm}=\varnothing$ unless
$m$ is positive on all its
rays. Such faces are common to $\sigma$ and $\tau$: every new
or truncated nonzero face of $\tau$ contains a ray in $[m=0]$,
whereas a face of $\sigma$ not retained by the cut has a ray
on which $m$ is negative.
For each contributing common face $F$, including $F=0$, choose
a nonzero lattice vector
$w\in\mathbb L\cap\operatorname{relint}(\sigma^\vee\cap F^\perp)$.
The face $F$ is proper in $\sigma$, so this is possible.
Then $w$ vanishes on $F$ and is positive on every other ray
of $\sigma$. Consequently, for $a\in K_{\tau,F}^{jm}$,
we have $a+nw\in K_{\sigma,F}^{jm}$ for $n\gg0$,
and every positive multiple of $w$ lies in $K_{\sigma,F}^{jm}$.
Given a quasilinear function $f$ on $K_{\sigma,F}^{jm}$, define
\[
 \widetilde f(a)=f(a+nw)-nf(w),\qquad n\gg0.
\]
The equality $f(a+(n+1)w)=f(a+nw)+f(w)$ shows that this
does not depend on the sufficiently large choice of $n$.
It agrees with $f$ on $K_{\sigma,F}^{jm}$, and any quasilinear
extension must satisfy this formula. For $a,b,a+b\in K_{\tau,F}^{jm}$,
choose $n$ sufficiently large that all shifted arguments below belong
to $K_{\sigma,F}^{jm}$. Using the same $w$, quasilinearity of $f$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde f(a)+\widetilde f(b)
 &=f(a+nw)+f(b+nw)-2nf(w)\\
 &=f(a+b+2nw)-2nf(w)\\
 &=\widetilde f(a+b).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the last equality uses the defining formula with shift $2n$.
Thus restriction is an isomorphism on every contributing face.
With matching face orientations, these maps commute with the
differentials, proving \eqref{eq:quasilinear-restriction}.

We next show that the induced cohomology maps are obtained by
restricting Harrison cochains on $A_\tau$ to inputs in the
subalgebra $A_\sigma$. For a $q$-cochain $h$ of degree $-jm$,
this is the map $h|_{A_\sigma^{\otimes q}}$, whose values lie
in $A_\sigma$ by \eqref{eq:truncationdownward}.
This identification will let us compare the obstruction classes.
Put $S_\gamma^+=S_\gamma\setminus\{0\}$ and let
$\mathcal H_\gamma^q$ be the normalized Harrison cochains of
degree $-jm$ on $A_\gamma$. Writing their monomial coefficients as
\[
 h(\chi^{a_1},\ldots,\chi^{a_q})
 =f(a_1,\ldots,a_q)\chi^{a_1+\cdots+a_q-jm}
\]
identifies them with scalar Harrison cochains on $(S_\gamma^+)^q$
subject to
\[
 f(a_1,\ldots,a_q)=0
 \quad\text{if}\quad a_1+\cdots+a_q-jm\notin S_\gamma.
\]
This is the relative complex of
\cite[Section~2.2 and Theorem~2.3]{AltmannSletsjoe}.

To relate these cochains to the quasilinear complex, let
$\mathcal C^q(K)$ denote scalar Harrison cochains on
$q$-tuples in $K$ whose sum is in $K$, and form the double complex
\[
 D_\gamma^{p,q}
 =\bigoplus_{\substack{F\preceq\gamma\\\dim F=p}}
    \mathcal C^q(K_{\gamma,F}^{jm}),
 \qquad p\ge0,\quad q\ge1.
\]
Its vertical differential $\delta$ is the inhomogeneous Harrison
differential, and its horizontal differential $d$ is signed
face restriction; the total differential is $d+(-1)^p\delta$.
The coefficient inclusion
$\alpha_\gamma:\mathcal H_\gamma^\bullet\to
\operatorname{Tot}D_\gamma$ places $f$ in $D_\gamma^{0,q}$
and zero elsewhere. Its horizontal differential vanishes:
a tuple whose sum lies in $K_{\gamma,\rho}^{jm}$ for a ray
$\rho$ satisfies the displayed vanishing condition.
The augmented horizontal rows are exact by
\cite[Lemma~3.2]{AltmannSletsjoe}, so $\alpha_\gamma$ is a
quasi-isomorphism.

Restriction of tuple functions on each common face defines
$r:D_\tau\to D_\sigma$; the remaining face summands are zero.
It commutes with $\delta$ and $d$, since both differentials
use only addition of inputs and restriction to faces.
Restricting inputs gives the cochain map $\operatorname{res}$
in the commutative square
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
 \mathcal H_\tau^\bullet&
 \xrightarrow{\alpha_\tau}&\operatorname{Tot}D_\tau\\
 {\scriptstyle\operatorname{res}}\downarrow&&\downarrow{\scriptstyle r}\\
 \mathcal H_\sigma^\bullet&
 \xrightarrow{\alpha_\sigma}&\operatorname{Tot}D_\sigma.
\end{array}
\]
Indeed, both composites restrict the same coefficient function
to $(S_\sigma^+)^q$ in the zero-face column.

Taking vertical cohomology gives
$E_1^{p,q}=H_\delta^q(D_\gamma^{p,\bullet})$.
Its $q=1$ row is $C_\gamma^\bullet(jm)$, and the induced map
is precisely the quasilinear restriction already considered.
Moreover, $E_1^{0,q}=E_1^{1,q}=0$ for $q\ge2$ by
\cite[Corollary~4.4]{AltmannSletsjoe}.
In total degrees $2$ and $3$, these vanishings leave only the
$q=1$ row. Its inclusion therefore induces
\[
 H^i(C_\gamma^\bullet(jm))
 \xrightarrow{\sim}H^{i+1}(\operatorname{Tot}D_\gamma),
 \qquad i=1,2,
\]
as in \cite[Theorem~5.3]{AltmannSletsjoe}.
These inclusions commute with $r$, so the maps
$T_{X_\tau}^i(-jm)\to T_{X_\sigma}^i(-jm)$, $i=1,2$,
induced by \eqref{eq:quasilinear-restriction} agree with those
induced by restricting Harrison cochains. This proves
\eqref{eq:truncationcohomology}.

It remains to check that these are the maps used in lifting
deformations. Let $R'\twoheadrightarrow R$ be a small graded extension
with kernel $E$. Lift the multiplication of $A_{\tau,R}$ to a
homogeneous commutative unital operation on
$\bigoplus_{s\in S_\tau}R'e_s$, keeping $e_0=1$ as the unit.
By \eqref{eq:truncationdownward}, its restriction preserves
$\bigoplus_{s\in S_\sigma}R'e_s$, and its associator restricts
to the associator of this restricted operation.
Here the associator measures the failure of the chosen operation
to be associative.
Since $\mathfrak m_{R'}E=0$, these associators represent the
obstruction classes in $(T_{X_\tau}^2\otimes_\mathbb C E)_0$ and
$(T_{X_\sigma}^2\otimes_\mathbb C E)_0$, respectively.
Likewise, differences of associative liftings and the
coboundaries arising from changes of basis restrict.
Thus the maps
\[
 (T_{X_\tau}^i\otimes_\mathbb C E)_0
 \longrightarrow (T_{X_\sigma}^i\otimes_\mathbb C E)_0,
 \qquad i=1,2,
\]
carry obstruction classes for $i=2$ and difference classes for
$i=1$ to those of the restricted deformations.

\label{thm:truncation}

We first prove formal smoothness: a prescribed lifting of the
restricted deformation comes from a lifting of the original
deformation.
Let $R'\twoheadrightarrow R$ be a small graded extension with
kernel $E$. Suppose we are given $A_{\tau,R}$ and a lifting
$A_{\sigma,R'}$ with a fixed identification
$A_{\sigma,R'}\otimes_{R'}R\simeq\mathcal R(A_{\tau,R})$.
We must find a lifting of $A_{\tau,R}$ whose restriction is
isomorphic to $A_{\sigma,R'}$ compatibly with this identification.

The obstruction to lifting $A_{\tau,R}$ over $R'$ lies in
$(T_{X_\tau}^2\otimes_\mathbb C E)_0$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:truncationcohomology}, its image in
$(T_{X_\sigma}^2\otimes_\mathbb C E)_0$ is the obstruction to lifting
$\mathcal R(A_{\tau,R})$ over $R'$.
This image is zero, since the given $A_{\sigma,R'}$ already
provides such a lifting.
Injectivity of this map therefore makes the original obstruction
vanish, so a lifting $A_{\tau,R'}$ exists by the standard lifting theorem
\cite[Theorem~10.1]{HartshorneDef}.
Here total degree includes the grading of $E$: a cochain of
degree $-jm$ with coefficient in $E_{jm}$ has total degree zero.
Since the Harrison differential preserves total degree, the
cochain used to cancel the obstruction may be replaced by its
degree-zero part. Thus the lifting can be chosen graded.

This lifting need not yet have the prescribed restriction.
We therefore compare $\mathcal R(A_{\tau,R'})$ with
$A_{\sigma,R'}$, keeping their identifications over $R$ fixed.
By the same lifting theorem, their difference is a class in
$(T_{X_\sigma}^1\otimes_\mathbb C E)_0$, and such classes describe
all changes of a lifting up to isomorphism respecting its reduction.
The isomorphism
$(T_{X_\tau}^1\otimes_\mathbb C E)_0\xrightarrow{\sim}
(T_{X_\sigma}^1\otimes_\mathbb C E)_0$
induced by Lemma~\ref{lem:truncationcohomology} lifts this difference
to $(T_{X_\tau}^1\otimes_\mathbb C E)_0$.
Changing $A_{\tau,R'}$ by that class gives the required restriction,
by the compatibility proved in the lemma. This proves formal smoothness.

Formal smoothness gives surjectivity of $\mathcal R$ over every
Artinian base, by induction over small extensions. It remains
to prove injectivity. If $P_m$ is an integral translate of its
tail cone, its rounded support function is integral linear.
Thus all $\eta_{\mathbb Z,\beta}$ vanish and $B_0=B=\mathbb C$.
The source is then the point functor by
Corollary~\ref{cor:universal-X}, and surjectivity makes the
target the point functor as well.

In the remaining case, we first show that a $\sigma$-deformation
has no nonidentity equivariant automorphisms preserving its
special fibre. This will make the identifications over $R$
agree in the final lifting argument. We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truncation-derivations}
 \operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb C[S_\sigma])_{-jm}=0
 \qquad\text{for all }j\ge1.
\end{equation}

A derivation of $\mathbb C[S_\sigma]$ of degree $-jm$ extends to the
torus as $\chi^{-jm}\partial_n$, where
$\partial_n(\chi^s)=\langle s,n\rangle\chi^s$ and
$n\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb L,\mathbb C)$.
For each primitive ray vector $v$ with $\langle m,v\rangle>0$,
regularity on $S_\sigma\cap v^\perp$ forces $n\in\mathbb C v$.
Indeed, subtracting $jm$ gives negative pairing with $v$,
so $n$ must vanish on this facet of the semigroup.
There is at least one such ray because $m\notin-S$;
two such rays force $n=0$. If there is only one, choose
$s\in S_\sigma$ with $\langle s,v\rangle=1$: primitiveness
provides a lattice element of height one, and adding a sufficiently
large lattice element in the relative interior of
$\sigma^\vee\cap v^\perp$ puts it in $S_\sigma$.
If $n\ne0$, regularity on this monomial requires
$1-j\langle m,v\rangle\ge0$, hence
$j=\langle m,v\rangle=1$.
In that case $P_m$ has the single vertex $v$ and tail cone
$\sigma\cap[m=0]$, so it is the excluded integral translate.
This proves \eqref{eq:truncation-derivations}.
An equivariant automorphism preserving the special fibre is
the identity modulo the base maximal ideal. Its first nonzero
term in the filtration by powers of that ideal would be a
nonzero element of
$(\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb C[S_\sigma])\otimes_\mathbb C
\mathfrak m_R^a/\mathfrak m_R^{a+1})_0$ for some $a\ge1$.
Every coefficient has degree $jm$ with $j\ge1$, so
\eqref{eq:truncation-derivations} rules this out.

We can now prove injectivity by induction over small extensions.
Suppose two $\tau$-deformations over
$R'\twoheadrightarrow R$ have isomorphic restrictions.
Inductively their reductions over $R$ are isomorphic; fix such
an identification. Its restriction agrees with the reduction
of the given isomorphism, since the two differ by an equivariant
automorphism of the common $\sigma$-deformation, which must be
the identity.
The difference between the two liftings is consequently a class
in $(T_{X_\tau}^1\otimes_\mathbb C E)_0$ mapping to zero for $\sigma$.
Injectivity in Lemma~\ref{lem:truncationcohomology} makes this
class zero, so the liftings are isomorphic. Induction proves
injectivity of $\mathcal R$, and hence the assertion.

 q_1&=Z+av,&q_2&=Z,\\
 q_3&=Z^2+bZv+cv^2,
 &q_4&=Z^2+(a+b)Zv+(c+ab)v^2,\\
 q_5&=q_1q_3=q_2q_4,&q_6&=q_3q_4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the equivariant family over $B$ has equations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-sign-sigma}
\begin{aligned}
 z_1^2&=q_1z_0,&z_3^2&=q_2z_4,\\
 z_0z_3&=q_3z_1,&z_1z_4&=q_4z_3,\\
 z_1z_3&=q_5,&z_0z_4&=q_6.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The standard monomials $Z^qv^kW$, with $q,k\ge0$ and
$W\in\mathcal W$, form a free $B$-basis.
Since $Q_\beta(Uv,v)=v^{\eta_{\mathbb Z,\beta}}p_\beta(U)$,
this is the restriction under $Z=Uv$ of the family for
$\tau=\sigma\cap[m\ge0]$, where $U=\chi^m$; its completion
is universal by Theorem~\ref{thm:truncation}.
Setting $v=1$ recovers exactly the normalized family of
Example~\ref{ex:eighth-components} over the same base $B$.
