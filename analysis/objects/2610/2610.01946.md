<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For each induced subgraph $F$ of $G$, choose a positive integer $d_F$
as in Lemma~\ref{lem:clique-product}, and let $s$ be the least common
multiple of these integers. For each $F$, partition $[s]$ into $s/d_F$
blocks of size $d_F$ and color the corresponding copies of
$F\boxtimes K_{d_F}$ with disjoint palettes, using
$(s/d_F)\cdot d_F\cdot\chi_f(F)=s\chi_f(F)$ colors in total. Also by Lemma~\ref{lem:clique-product},
$\chi(F\boxtimes K_s)\ge\chi_f(F\boxtimes K_s)=s\chi_f(F)$, and hence
$\chi(F\boxtimes K_s)=s\chi_f(F)$ for every induced subgraph $F$ of $G$.

Now $\chi(G\boxtimes K_s)=s\chi_f(G)\ge7sk$.
By Lemma~\ref{thm:local-ordinary-extraction}, $G\boxtimes K_s$ has an
$sk$-connected subgraph $J'$ with
$\chi(J')\ge s\chi_f(G)-6sk$.
Let $J$ be the subgraph of $G$ induced by the vertices $v$ for which
$C_v$ contains a vertex of $J'$. Since $J'$ has more than $sk$ vertices
and each $C_v$ has only $s$ vertices, $J$ has more than $k$ vertices.
For any $X\subseteq V(J)$ with $|X|<k$, delete from $J'$ all copies of
vertices in $X$. Fewer than $sk$ vertices are deleted, so the remaining
graph is connected. Replacing each remaining copy by its original
vertex produces a connected subgraph spanning $J\setminus X$.
Hence $J\setminus X$ is connected, and $J$ is $k$-connected.
Finally, $J'\subseteq J\boxtimes K_s$ and the identity above applied to
$J$ imply
$\chi_f(J)=\chi(J\boxtimes K_s)/s\ge\chi(J')/s\ge\chi_f(G)-6k$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:local-cut-tests}
Let $G$ be an $n$-vertex $k$-connected graph with $\alpha(G)\le s$,
where $s$ is a positive integer and $k$ is sufficiently large.
There is a family $\mathcal M$ of at most $(n+1)^s$ matchings, each of
order $\lfloor k/8\rfloor$, such that for every $Z\subseteq V(G)$
satisfying the conditions
\begin{enumerate}
\item every vertex has at least $k/2$ neighbors in $V(G)\setminus Z$, and
\item each matching in $\mathcal M$ has fewer than $k/32$ endpoints in $Z$,
\end{enumerate}
we have that $G\setminus Z$ is $\lfloor k/32\rfloor$-connected.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For each $S\subseteq V(G)$ with $|S|\le s$ such that $N_G[S]$ and its
complement both have order at least $k/3$, fix a matching $M_S$ of order
$\lfloor k/8\rfloor$ between $N_G[S]$ and $V(G)\setminus N_G[S]$.
If no such matching existed, the endpoints of a maximum crossing
matching would separate the two nonempty sides after fewer than $k/4$
vertices were deleted, contrary to $k$-connectivity.
Let $\mathcal M$ be the family of these matchings. Thus we have
\[
 |\mathcal M|\le\sum_{i=0}^{s}\binom{n}{i}
 \le\sum_{i=0}^{s}n^i\le(n+1)^s.
\]

Fix $Z$ satisfying (1) and (2).
By (1), $G\setminus Z$ has minimum degree at least $k/2$, and hence
more than $\lfloor k/32\rfloor$ vertices.
Suppose that deleting $X\subseteq V(G)\setminus Z$ of order less than $k/32$ leaves two
nonempty sides $Y_1,Y_2$ with no edges between them.
Every vertex of either side has more than $k/3$ neighbors in that side.
Choose a maximal independent set $S$ of $G[Y_1]$.
Then $|S|\le s$, $Y_1\subseteq N_G[S]$ by maximality, and
$Y_2\cap N_G[S]=\varnothing$.
Since $|Y_1|,|Y_2|>k/3$, we have $M_S\in\mathcal M$.
Every edge of $M_S$ meets $Z\cup X$, since otherwise it would join
$Y_1$ to $Y_2$.
But (2) and $|X|<k/32$ imply that $Z\cup X$ contains fewer than $k/16$
of its endpoints. This is too few to meet $\lfloor k/8\rfloor$ disjoint
edges, a contradiction.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->

We repeatedly choose an independent set $I$ of $G$ of order $\left\lceil n/r\right\rceil$
consisting of undeleted vertices, together with an unused index $i$
satisfying $|I\cap A_i|\ge |I|/4$.
At each step, we record $I_i=I$ as one of the independent sets required
by the lemma and delete the vertices of $I\cap A_i$.
The process continues until no further deletion is possible.
Let $H$ be the induced subgraph of $G$ remaining at this point, and let
$K\subseteq[N]$ be the set of unused indices.
Each deletion removes at least $n/(4r)$ vertices, so the process uses
at most $4r\le N/8$ indices in total.
As every vertex of $G$ belongs to at least $N/2$ of the sets $A_i$,
at least $N/2-N/8=3N/8>N/4$ of these sets have indices in $K$.
Suppose that $H$ contains an independent set $I$ of order $\left\lceil n/r\right\rceil$.
Then $\sum_{i\in K}|I\cap A_i|\ge N|I|/4$.
Since $|K|\le N$, some $i\in K$ satisfies $|I\cap A_i|\ge |I|/4$.
This would allow another deletion, contrary to the termination of the
process. Hence $\alpha(H)\le\left\lceil n/r\right\rceil-1<n/r$.

Let $k=32C_{\ref{thm:knit}}t(2^{s+4}-1)$.

\begin{claim}\label{clm:thinning-families}
There are a family $\mathcal H$ of induced subgraphs of $H$ and a family
$\mathcal C$ of vertex subsets of $H$ such that
$H=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal H}F$, $|\mathcal H|\le n+1$, and $|\mathcal C|\le n$.
Every $F\in\mathcal H$ has at most $2k$ vertices or is $k$-connected,
and $|C|<k$ for every $C\in\mathcal C$.
Moreover, every subgraph $J$ of $H$ such that $J\setminus C$ is
connected for every $C\in\mathcal C$ is contained in some $F\in\mathcal H$.
\end{claim}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal H_0=\{H\}$ and $\mathcal C_0=\varnothing$.
For $i=1,2,\ldots$, if $\mathcal H_{i-1}$ contains a subgraph of
order greater than $2k$ that is not $k$-connected, choose one such
subgraph $F$.
For this subgraph $F$, let $C$ be a vertex separator with $|C|<k$,
and let $Y_1,Y_2$ be nonempty sets partitioning $V(F)\setminus C$,
with no edges between them.
Let $F_1=F\setminus Y_2$ and $F_2=F\setminus Y_1$.
The family $\mathcal H_i$ is obtained from $\mathcal H_{i-1}$ by
replacing $F$ with $F_1,F_2$, and $\mathcal C_i$ is obtained from
$\mathcal C_{i-1}$ by adding $C$.
The process continues until no such subgraph $F$ exists.

We now show that at most $n$ replacements can occur.
For $i\ge0$, let
\[
 \tau(\mathcal H_i)=\sum_{J\in\mathcal H_i}\max\{\mathsf v(J)-k,0\}.
\]
Then $\tau(\mathcal H_0)\le n$.
Suppose that $\mathcal H_i$ is obtained from $\mathcal H_{i-1}$ by
replacing $F$ with $F_1,F_2$, labeled so that
$\mathsf v(F_1)\le\mathsf v(F_2)$.
Since $\mathsf v(F_1)+\mathsf v(F_2)=\mathsf v(F)+|C|$, we have
$\mathsf v(F_2)\ge\mathsf v(F)/2>k$.
It follows that
\[
 \tau(\mathcal H_{i-1})-\tau(\mathcal H_i)
 \ge\mathsf v(F_1)-|C|-\max\{\mathsf v(F_1)-k,0\}
 =\min\{\mathsf v(F_1),k\}-|C|\ge1.
\]
The last inequality uses $\mathsf v(F_1)>|C|$ and $k>|C|$.
Since $\tau(\mathcal H_i)$ is nonnegative, the process terminates
after at most $n$ replacements.

Suppose that the process terminates after $\ell$ replacements, and let
$\mathcal H=\mathcal H_\ell$ and $\mathcal C=\mathcal C_\ell$.
Each replacement records at most one separator and increases the number
of subgraphs by at most one, so $|\mathcal C|\le n$ and
$|\mathcal H|\le n+1$.
Each replacement preserves the union of the subgraphs, so
$H=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal H}F$.
By the stopping condition, every $F\in \mathcal H$ has at most $2k$
vertices or is $k$-connected.

Suppose that $J$ is a subgraph of $H$ such that $J\setminus C$ is
connected for every $C\in\mathcal C$. To show that there exists some graph in $\mathcal H$ containing $J$,
it suffices to show that some member of $\mathcal H_i$ contains $J$
for every $i=0,\ldots,\ell$. We prove this by induction on $i$.
The case $i=0$ follows from $\mathcal H_0=\{H\}$.
Suppose that $F\in\mathcal H_{i-1}$ contains $J$.
If $F\in\mathcal H_i$, we are done.
Otherwise, $\mathcal H_i$ contains $F_1=F\setminus Y_2$ and
$F_2=F\setminus Y_1$, where $C,Y_1,Y_2$ are the sets chosen at step $i$.
The vertices of $J\setminus C$ lie entirely in one of $Y_1,Y_2$,
since $J\setminus C$ is connected and there are no edges between these sets.
Since $V(F_1)\cap V(F_2)=C$, one of $F_1,F_2$ contains $J$, as desired.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal H,\mathcal C$ be the families from
Claim~\ref{clm:thinning-families}.
Since $\chi_f(H)\le r$, let $w(I)$ be fractional coloring weights
on $H$ of total weight $r$, adding any missing weight to the empty set.
For each $i\in K$, independently choose an independent set $I_i$ of $H$
with $\operatorname{\mathbb P}(I_i=I)=w(I)/r$.
Let $R=V(H)\setminus\bigcup_{i\in K}(I_i\cap A_i)$.
For every $i\in K$ and $v\in V(H)\cap A_i$,
$\operatorname{\mathbb P}(v\in I_i)=\sum_{I\ni v}w(I)/r\ge1/r$.
As the greedy deletion process used at most $N/8$ indices,
each vertex of $H$ still belongs to at least $N/2-N/8=3N/8>N/4$
of the sets $A_i$ with indices in $K$.
By the definition of $R$, $v\in R$ if and only if $v\notin I_i$
for every $i\in K$ with $v\in A_i$.
There are more than $N/4$ such indices, and for each of them
$\operatorname{\mathbb P}(v\notin I_i)\le1-1/r$.
Thus,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uncovered-probability}
 \operatorname{\mathbb P}(v\in R)
 =\prod_{i\in K,\,A_i\ni v}\operatorname{\mathbb P}(v\notin I_i)
 \le\left(1-\frac1r\right)^{N/4}\le e^{-\frac{N}{4r}}.
\end{equation}

For $i\in[N]\setminus K$, let $I_i$ be the independent set recorded
for index $i$ during the greedy deletions.
Then $G\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^N(I_i\cap A_i)=H[R]$.
If the independent sets $\{I_i\}_{i\in K}$ can be chosen so that
$\chi_f(H[R])\le r/2$, then the lemma holds.
We may therefore assume that $\chi_f(H[R])>r/2$ for every choice of
$\{I_i\}_{i\in K}$.

\begin{claim}\label{clm:thinning-core}
There is a fixed $k$-connected induced subgraph $J$ of $H$ such that
$\operatorname{\mathbb E}[\chi_f(J[R])]>r/4$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal X=\mathcal X_1\cup\mathcal X_2\cup\mathcal X_3$, where
the three families of random variables are defined by
\[
 \mathcal X_1=\{|C\cap R|\}_{C\in\mathcal C},\quad
 \mathcal X_2=\{|V(F)\cap R|\}_{F\in\mathcal H,\,\mathsf v(F)\le2k},\quad
 \mathcal X_3=\{\chi_f(F[R])\}_{F\in\mathcal H,\,\mathsf v(F)>2k}.
\]
Since $N\ge\beta(s+1)r$ and $4e^{-\beta/4}\le4e^{-8}<1$,
by the choice of $\beta$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uncovered-expectation}
 2ke^{-\frac{N}{4r}}
 <2^{2s+9}C_{\ref{thm:knit}}r e^{-\frac{\beta(s+1)}4}
 =2^9C_{\ref{thm:knit}}r e^{-\frac\beta4}
   \bigl(4e^{-\frac\beta4}\bigr)^s
 \le2^9C_{\ref{thm:knit}}r e^{-\frac\beta4}\le r/64.
\end{equation}
For every $X\in\mathcal X_1\cup\mathcal X_2$,
by Inequalities~\eqref{eq:uncovered-probability} and
\eqref{eq:uncovered-expectation} and linearity of expectation,
$\operatorname{\mathbb E}[X]\le2ke^{-N/(4r)}\le r/64$.

Every $X\in\mathcal X$ depends on the same $|K|\le N$ independent
choices and changes by at most $2n/r$ when one $I_i$ is replaced.
Indeed, such a replacement may add a subset of the original independent
set to $R$ and remove a subset of the new independent set from $R$.
Both subsets are independent and have order less than $n/r$,
so every $X\in\mathcal X_1\cup\mathcal X_2$ changes by at most $2n/r$.
Deleting vertices cannot increase $\chi_f$, while adding an independent
set increases $\chi_f$ by at most one.
Comparing in both directions, every $X\in\mathcal X_3$ changes by at
most one, which is at most $2n/r$ for sufficiently large $t$.

Since $s+1\le2^s$ and both $\beta$ and $t$ are integers, we have
$N\le2\beta t$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-differences}, using
$r\ge t/\ln t$ and $n=t(\ln t)^5$, for every $X\in\mathcal X$ we have
\[
 \operatorname{\mathbb P}\left(|X-\operatorname{\mathbb E}[X]|\ge\frac r{64}\right)
 \le2\exp\left(-\frac{r^4}{2^{13}Nn^2}\right)
 \le2\exp\left(-\frac{t}{2^{14}\beta(\ln t)^{14}}\right)
 \le2e^{-\sqrt t}.
\]
By Claim~\ref{clm:thinning-families}, $|\mathcal X|\le2n+1$.
By the union bound, we have
\[
 \operatorname{\mathbb P}\left(\max_{X\in\mathcal X}|X-\operatorname{\mathbb E}[X]|\ge\frac r{64}\right)
 \le2(2n+1)e^{-\sqrt t}<1.
\]
Thus the independent sets $\{I_i\}_{i\in K}$ can be chosen so that
$|X-\operatorname{\mathbb E}[X]|<r/64$ for every $X\in\mathcal X$.
Together with Inequality~\eqref{eq:uncovered-expectation}, it follows
that this choice satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-mean-comparison}
 \begin{gathered}
  |C\cap R|<\frac r{32}\quad(C\in\mathcal C),\qquad
  |V(F)\cap R|<\frac r{32}\quad(F\in\mathcal H,\ \mathsf v(F)\le2k),\\
  \operatorname{\mathbb E}[\chi_f(F[R])]>\chi_f(F[R])-\frac r{64}
  \quad(F\in\mathcal H,\ \mathsf v(F)>2k).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}

By our assumption, $\chi_f(H[R])>r/2$.
For large $t$, $r>128$, so
$7\left\lceil r/32\right\rceil<7r/16<r/2$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:fractional-extraction}, $H[R]$ contains a
$\left\lceil r/32\right\rceil$-connected subgraph $F$ with
\[
 \chi_f(F)\ge\chi_f(H[R])-6\left\lceil r/32\right\rceil
 >\frac r2-6\left(\frac r{32}+1\right)
 =\frac{5r}{16}-6>\frac r4+\frac r{64}.
\]
Since $V(F)\subseteq R$, by Inequalities~\eqref{eq:core-mean-comparison}
we have $|C\cap V(F)|\le|C\cap R|<r/32$ for every $C\in\mathcal C$.
The graph $F$ is $\left\lceil r/32\right\rceil$-connected, so $F\setminus C$ is
connected for every $C\in\mathcal C$.
By Claim~\ref{clm:thinning-families}, some $J\in\mathcal H$ contains $F$.
If $\mathsf v(J)\le2k$, then by Inequalities~\eqref{eq:core-mean-comparison},
$\chi_f(F)\le\mathsf v(F)\le|V(J)\cap R|<r/32$, a contradiction.
Thus $\mathsf v(J)>2k$, and $J$ is $k$-connected by
Claim~\ref{clm:thinning-families}.
Moreover, by Inequalities~\eqref{eq:core-mean-comparison}, we have
$\operatorname{\mathbb E}[\chi_f(J[R])]>\chi_f(J[R])-\frac r{64}
\ge\chi_f(F)-\frac r{64}>\frac r4$.
\end{proof}

Let $J$ be the fixed graph from Claim~\ref{clm:thinning-core}, and let
$p=\left\lceil r/32\right\rceil$ and $q=\binom{2^{s+4}}2$.
Recall that $k=32C_{\ref{thm:knit}}t(2^{s+4}-1)$.
By the definitions of $r$ and $q$, we have $k=2C_{\ref{thm:knit}}rq$.

\begin{claim}\label{clm:thinning-models}
There is a set $X\subseteq V(J)$ such that $J[X]$ contains $q$
pairwise disjoint $K_{2p}$ models, every vertex of $J$ has at least
$k/2$ neighbors in $V(J)\setminus X$, and $J\setminus X$ is
$k/32$-connected.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
For each $i\in K$ and $j\in[q]$, independently choose an independent
set $I_i^j$ of $H$, with $\operatorname{\mathbb P}(I_i^j=I)=w(I)/r$
for every independent set $I$ of $H$.
All these choices are independent of the sets $\{I_i\}_{i\in K}$.
For $j\in[q]$, let
\[
 R_j=V(J)\setminus\bigcup_{i\in K}(I_i^j\cap A_i),\qquad
 S_j=R_j\setminus\bigcup_{h\ne j}R_h,\qquad
 \text{and}\quad X=\bigcup_{j=1}^qR_j.
\]
The sets $S_1,\ldots,S_q$ are pairwise disjoint and contained in $X$.
Each $R_j$ has the same distribution as $R\cap V(J)$, so by
Claim~\ref{clm:thinning-core}, $\operatorname{\mathbb E}[\chi_f(J[R_j])]>r/4$.

By Inequalities~\eqref{eq:uncovered-probability} and
\eqref{eq:uncovered-expectation} and the union bound,
using $k=2C_{\ref{thm:knit}}rq$ and $C_{\ref{thm:knit}}\ge1$,
for every $v\in V(J)$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uncovered-union-probability}
 \operatorname{\mathbb P}(v\in X)
 \le\sum_{h=1}^q\operatorname{\mathbb P}(v\in R_h)
 \le qe^{-\frac{N}{4r}}
 \le\frac{qr}{2^7k}=\frac1{2^8C_{\ref{thm:knit}}}\le\frac1{64}.
\end{equation}
Since $R_h$ is independent of $R_j$ for $h\ne j$,
by the definition of $S_j$ and Inequality~\eqref{eq:uncovered-union-probability},
for every $j\in[q]$ and $v\in R_j$ we have
\[
 \operatorname{\mathbb P}(v\in S_j\mid R_j)
 =1-\operatorname{\mathbb P}\left(v\in\bigcup_{h\ne j}R_h\mid R_j\right)
 \ge1-\sum_{h\ne j}\operatorname{\mathbb P}(v\in R_h)
 \ge1-qe^{-\frac{N}{4r}}\ge\frac{63}{64}.
\]
For each $j\in[q]$, conditional on $R_j$, each vertex $v\in R_j$
has a nonnegative weight $x_v$ such that
$\sum_{v\in R_j}x_v=\chi_f(J[R_j])$ and
$\sum_{v\in I}x_v\le1$ for every independent set $I$ of $J[R_j]$.
Such weights exist by the dual characterization of $\chi_f$.
Since every independent set of $J[S_j]$ is also independent in $J[R_j]$,
by the dual characterization we have
$\chi_f(J[S_j])\ge\sum_{v\in S_j}x_v$.
Thus, by linearity of expectation, we have
\[
 \operatorname{\mathbb E}[\chi_f(J[S_j])\mid R_j]
 \ge\sum_{v\in R_j}x_v\operatorname{\mathbb P}(v\in S_j\mid R_j)
 \ge\frac{63}{64}\chi_f(J[R_j]).
\]
Since $R_j$ has the same distribution as $R\cap V(J)$ and
$\operatorname{\mathbb E}[\chi_f(J[R])]\le r$, by the law of total expectation we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disjoint-mean}
 \operatorname{\mathbb E}[\chi_f(J[S_j])]
 \ge\frac{63}{64}\operatorname{\mathbb E}[\chi_f(J[R])]
 \ge\operatorname{\mathbb E}[\chi_f(J[R])]-\frac r{64}>\frac r4-\frac r{64}
 =\frac{15r}{64}.
\end{equation}

Since $\alpha(J)<n/r\le(\ln t)^6$ and $\mathsf v(J)\le n = t(\ln t)^5$,
there is a fixed family $\mathcal M$ of matchings in $J$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-cut-tests}, each of order $\lfloor k/8\rfloor$, such that
\[
 |\mathcal M|\le(\mathsf v(J)+1)^{\alpha(J)}
 \le\exp\bigl((\ln t)^6\ln(n+1)\bigr)
 \le\exp(4(\ln t)^7).
\]
The last inequality holds for sufficiently large $t$, since
 $\ln(n+1)\le\ln2+\ln t+5\ln\ln t\le4\ln t$.

Let $\mathcal Y=\mathcal Y_1\cup\mathcal Y_2\cup\mathcal Y_3$, where
the three families of random variables are defined by
\[
 \mathcal Y_1=\{|N_J(v)\cap X|\}_{v\in V(J)},\quad
 \mathcal Y_2=\{|V(M)\cap X|\}_{M\in\mathcal M},\quad
 \mathcal Y_3=\{\chi_f(J[S_j])\}_{j\in[q]}.
\]
Every $Y\in\mathcal Y$ depends on the same $q|K|\le qN$ independent
choices. As in the proof of Claim~\ref{clm:thinning-core}, replacing
one $I_i^h$ changes every $Y\in\mathcal Y$ by at most $2n/r$,
since the vertex sets added to or removed from $X$ and each $S_j$
are independent and have order less than $n/r$.
Since $2^{s+4}=32t/r\le32\ln t$, we have $q<512(\ln t)^2$.
Together with $N\le2\beta t$, this gives $qN\le t(\ln t)^3$
for sufficiently large $t$, uniformly over the allowed values of $s$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-differences}, for every $Y\in\mathcal Y$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-concentration}
 \operatorname{\mathbb P}\left(|Y-\operatorname{\mathbb E}[Y]|\ge\frac r{64}\right)
 \le2\exp\left(-\frac{r^4}{2^{13}qNn^2}\right)
 \le2\exp\left(-\frac{t}{2^{13}(\ln t)^{17}}\right)
 \le2e^{-\sqrt t}.
\end{equation}
Since $|\mathcal Y|\le n+\exp(4(\ln t)^7)+q$, by
Inequality~\eqref{eq:local-concentration} and the union bound, we have
\[
 \operatorname{\mathbb P}\left(\max_{Y\in\mathcal Y}|Y-\operatorname{\mathbb E}[Y]|\ge\frac r{64}\right)
 \le2\bigl(n+\exp(4(\ln t)^7)+q\bigr)e^{-\sqrt t}<1.
\]
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Thus the independent sets $\{I_i^j\}_{i\in K,\,j\in[q]}$ can be chosen
so that $|Y-\operatorname{\mathbb E}[Y]|<r/64$ for every $Y\in\mathcal Y$.
For this choice, we have the following bounds.
\begin{itemize}
 \item Since $J$ is $k$-connected, $|N_J(v)|\ge k\ge r$ for every
 $v\in V(J)$. By Inequality~\eqref{eq:uncovered-union-probability} and
 linearity of expectation, $\operatorname{\mathbb E}[|N_J(v)\cap X|]\le|N_J(v)|/64$,
 so $|N_J(v)\cap X|<\frac{|N_J(v)|+r}{64}\le\frac{|N_J(v)|}{32}$.
 \item For every $M\in\mathcal M$, we have
 $|V(M)|=2\lfloor k/8\rfloor\le k/4$.
 Similarly, $\operatorname{\mathbb E}[|V(M)\cap X|]=\sum_{v\in V(M)}\operatorname{\mathbb P}(v\in X)
 \le\frac{|V(M)|}{64}\le\frac{k}{256}\le\frac{k}{64}$,
 so $|V(M)\cap X|<\frac{k+r}{64}\le\frac k{32}$.
 \item By Inequality~\eqref{eq:disjoint-mean}, for every $j\in[q]$ we have
 $\chi_f(J[S_j])>\frac{15r}{64}-\frac r{64}=\frac{7r}{32}>4p$ for large $t$.
\end{itemize}

By the first two bounds and Lemma~\ref{lem:local-cut-tests},
with $X$ playing the role of $Z$, $J\setminus X$ is $k/32$-connected.
Indeed, $|N_J(v)\setminus X|>\frac{31}{32}|N_J(v)|\ge\frac{31k}{32}>\frac k2$
for every $v\in V(J)$.
By the third bound and Theorem~\ref{thm:local-fractional},
each $J[S_j]$ contains a $K_{2p}$ model.
Since $S_1,\ldots,S_q$ are pairwise disjoint subsets of $X$,
these $q$ models are pairwise disjoint and lie in $J[X]$.
\end{proof}

Let $X$ and the $q$ models be as in Claim~\ref{clm:thinning-models}.
Since $2^{s+4}=32t/r\le32\ln t<t$ and $p=\left\lceil t/2^{s+4}\right\rceil$, we
partition $[t]$ into nonempty blocks $T_1,\ldots,T_{2^{s+4}}$,
each of order at most $p$.
There are exactly $q=\binom{2^{s+4}}2$ unordered pairs of distinct blocks,
and we associate the $q$ models bijectively with these pairs.
For each pair $\{T_i,T_j\}$ with $i<j$ and each $\ell\in T_i\cup T_j$,
we select a branch set $B_\ell^{(i,j)}$ of the associated model,
with the $|T_i\cup T_j|\le2p$ selected branch sets all distinct.
There are $t(2^{s+4}-1)=rq/16$ selected branch sets in total.

Since every vertex of $J$ has at least $k/2>rq/16$ neighbors in
$J\setminus X$, we can choose distinct vertices
$y_\ell^{(i,j)}\in V(J)\setminus X$, each with a neighbor in $B_\ell^{(i,j)}$.
By Claim~\ref{clm:thinning-models}, $J\setminus X$ is $k/32$-connected.
Since $k/32=C_{\ref{thm:knit}}rq/16$, by Lemma~\ref{thm:knit},
$J\setminus X$ is $(rq/16)$-knit and contains pairwise disjoint
connected subgraphs $D_1,\ldots,D_t$ such that $D_\ell$ contains
$y_\ell^{(i,j)}$ for every pair $\{T_i,T_j\}$ with $\ell\in T_i\cup T_j$.

For $\ell\in[t]$, let
\[
 F_\ell=J\left[V(D_\ell)\cup\bigcup_{i<j,\,\ell\in T_i\cup T_j}V(B_\ell^{(i,j)})\right].
\]
Every $B_\ell^{(i,j)}$ has an edge to $y_\ell^{(i,j)}\in V(D_\ell)$,
so $F_\ell$ is connected.
The subgraphs $F_1,\ldots,F_t$ are pairwise disjoint, since the
$B_\ell^{(i,j)}$ are pairwise disjoint within $X$ and the $D_\ell$
are pairwise disjoint outside $X$.
For any distinct labels $\ell,\ell'\in[t]$, there are $i<j$ such that
$\ell,\ell'\in T_i\cup T_j$.
The branch sets $B_\ell^{(i,j)}$ and $B_{\ell'}^{(i,j)}$ are adjacent
in the model associated with $\{T_i,T_j\}$, so $F_\ell$ and
$F_{\ell'}$ are adjacent.
Thus $F_1,\ldots,F_t$ form a $K_t$ model in $G$, a contradiction.

Hence the independent sets $\{I_i\}_{i\in K}$ can be chosen so that
$\chi_f(H[R])\le r/2$.
Together with the independent sets recorded during the greedy deletions,
they satisfy the required bound.

 \tau(\mathcal H_i)=\sum_{J\in\mathcal H_i}\max\{\mathsf v(J)-k,0\}.
\]
Then $\tau(\mathcal H_0)\le n$.
Suppose that $\mathcal H_i$ is obtained from $\mathcal H_{i-1}$ by
replacing $F$ with $F_1,F_2$, labeled so that
$\mathsf v(F_1)\le\mathsf v(F_2)$.
Since $\mathsf v(F_1)+\mathsf v(F_2)=\mathsf v(F)+|C|$, we have
$\mathsf v(F_2)\ge\mathsf v(F)/2>k$.
It follows that
\[
 \tau(\mathcal H_{i-1})-\tau(\mathcal H_i)
 \ge\mathsf v(F_1)-|C|-\max\{\mathsf v(F_1)-k,0\}
 =\min\{\mathsf v(F_1),k\}-|C|\ge1.
\]
The last inequality uses $\mathsf v(F_1)>|C|$ and $k>|C|$.
Since $\tau(\mathcal H_i)$ is nonnegative, the process terminates
after at most $n$ replacements.

Suppose that the process terminates after $\ell$ replacements, and let
$\mathcal H=\mathcal H_\ell$ and $\mathcal C=\mathcal C_\ell$.
Each replacement records at most one separator and increases the number
of subgraphs by at most one, so $|\mathcal C|\le n$ and
$|\mathcal H|\le n+1$.
Each replacement preserves the union of the subgraphs, so
$H=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal H}F$.
By the stopping condition, every $F\in \mathcal H$ has at most $2k$
vertices or is $k$-connected.

Suppose that $J$ is a subgraph of $H$ such that $J\setminus C$ is
connected for every $C\in\mathcal C$. To show that there exists some graph in $\mathcal H$ containing $J$,
it suffices to show that some member of $\mathcal H_i$ contains $J$
for every $i=0,\ldots,\ell$. We prove this by induction on $i$.
The case $i=0$ follows from $\mathcal H_0=\{H\}$.
Suppose that $F\in\mathcal H_{i-1}$ contains $J$.
If $F\in\mathcal H_i$, we are done.
Otherwise, $\mathcal H_i$ contains $F_1=F\setminus Y_2$ and
$F_2=F\setminus Y_1$, where $C,Y_1,Y_2$ are the sets chosen at step $i$.
The vertices of $J\setminus C$ lie entirely in one of $Y_1,Y_2$,
since $J\setminus C$ is connected and there are no edges between these sets.
Since $V(F_1)\cap V(F_2)=C$, one of $F_1,F_2$ contains $J$, as desired.

Let $k\ge1$ be an integer. If $L$ is a bad list assignment on $G$
with $|L|\ge4k+1$, then $G$ has an induced $k$-connected subgraph $H$
with a bad sublist $L_H\subseteq L$ satisfying $|L_H|\ge|L|-4k$.

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{cor:many-pieces}
For integers $k\ge t\ge T_{\ref{thm:local-list}}$, every $K_t$-minor-free graph $G$
with a bad list assignment $L$ satisfying
$|L|\ge8C_{\ref{lem:deletion}}C_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}k$ contains
$\left\lceil \ln t\right\rceil+1$ disjoint induced $k$-connected subgraphs,
each on at most $C_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}^2t(\ln t)^3$ vertices.
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Choose a maximal family $\mathcal H$ of disjoint induced $k$-connected
subgraphs, each of order at most $C_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}^2t(\ln t)^3$.
Suppose that $|\mathcal H|\le\left\lceil \ln t\right\rceil$, and let
$X=\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal H}V(H)$. Then
$|X|\le2C_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}^2t(\ln t)^4\le t(\ln t)^5$.
Since $C_{\ref{lem:deletion}}(t+1)\le2C_{\ref{lem:deletion}}k\le|L|/2$,
by Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion} with $Y=\varnothing$, the graph
$G\setminus X$ has a bad sublist $L'\subseteq L$ with $|L'|\ge|L|/2$.
By Observation~\ref{obs:bad-density}, it has an induced subgraph of
density at least $|L'|/2\ge|L|/4\geC_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}k$.
By Lemma~\ref{thm:small-connected}, this subgraph contains another
induced $k$-connected subgraph of the required order, contradicting
the maximality of $\mathcal H$.

<!-- lemma-14: body_md -->
\label{lem:menger}
For disjoint vertex sets $A,B$ in a graph, the maximum number of
vertex-disjoint $(A,B)$-paths equals the minimum size of a set
separating $A$ from $B$.
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- lemma-15: body_md -->
\label{lem:double-fan}
Let $A,B$ be disjoint vertex sets in a graph $G$, with $A\ne\varnothing$.
If $G$ is $2|A|$-connected and $|B|\ge2|A|$, then $G$ contains
a double fan from $A$ to $B$.
<!-- end lemma-15 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
For each $a\in A$, add a copy $a'$ with the same neighbors as $a$.
Let $A'$ be the set of copies. The resulting graph is still
$2|A|$-connected. By Lemma~\ref{lem:menger}, it contains $2|A|$ disjoint
$(A\cup A',B)$-paths, one with an end at each vertex of $A\cup A'$.
Replace each end $a'$ by $a$. The resulting paths form the required double fan.
<!-- end proof-11 -->
<!-- lemma-16: body_md -->
\label{lem:join-fans}
Let $r\ge1$ be an integer, and let $A,B,C$ be pairwise disjoint vertex sets
in a graph $G$.
Let $\mathcal P$ be a double fan from $A$ to $C$ avoiding $B$.
Let $\mathcal Q$ be a family of $2r$ disjoint $(B,C)$-paths in $G\setminus A$.
The subgraph formed by the paths in $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal Q$ contains
$|A|+r$ disjoint $(A\cup B,C)$-paths.
Each vertex of $A$ is an end of exactly one of these paths.
The other $r$ paths have distinct ends among the ends of $\mathcal Q$ in $B$.
<!-- end lemma-16 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
If $A=\varnothing$, any $r$ paths of $\mathcal Q$ suffice. Assume $A\ne\varnothing$
and work in the subgraph formed by $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal Q$.
Pair the $2r$ ends of $\mathcal Q$ in $B$. For each pair, add a new vertex
adjacent to both ends, and let $D$ be the set of these $r$ new vertices.
The extended paths form a double fan from $D$ to $C$ avoiding $A$.
By the cited redundancy version of Menger's theorem, there are
$|A|+r$ disjoint $(A\cup D,C)$-paths, one starting at each vertex of $A\cup D$.
Delete the end in $D$ from each path starting there. The shortened paths
have distinct ends among the ends of $\mathcal Q$ in $B$.
Every vertex of $B$ has degree at most one in the original subgraph,
so none is an internal vertex of the resulting paths.
<!-- end proof-12 -->
<!-- lemma-17: body_md -->
\label{lem:short-fan}
Let $\ell\ge1$ be an integer, and let $A,B$ be disjoint vertex sets
in a graph $G$. Let $\mathcal P=\{P_i\}_{i\in[\ell]}$ be a family of $\ell$
disjoint $(A,B)$-paths with minimum total length among all such families.
Then the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For $i\in[\ell]$ and every $v\in V(G)\setminus V(\mathcal P)$,
$|N_G(v)\cap V(P_i)|\le3$.
\item $G[V(\mathcal P)\setminus(A\cup B)]$ is $(2\ell-1)$-degenerate.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-17 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:short-double-fan}
The conclusions of Lemma~\ref{lem:short-fan} also hold for a double fan
from $A$ to $B$ of minimum total length, with $\ell=2|A|\ge1$.
Split each vertex of $A$ into two copies with the same neighbors outside
$A$, one for each path. We obtain $\ell$ disjoint paths, and minimum
total length is preserved. A shorter family of $\ell$ disjoint paths
from the copies to $B$ would form a shorter double fan after replacing
the copies by the original vertices, a contradiction. The graph induced
by the internal vertices and the number of neighbors of each outside
vertex on each path are unchanged.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:linkage}
Let $G$ be a graph, let $k\ge0$ be an integer, and let
$\mathcal S=\{(s_i,t_i)\}_{i\in[k]}$ be a set of pairs of vertices
of $G$ such that $s_i\ne s_j$, $t_i\ne t_j$ and $s_i\ne t_j$
whenever $i\ne j$.
An \emph{$\mathcal S$-linkage} in $G$ is a family
$\mathcal P=\{P_i\}_{i\in[k]}$ of pairwise vertex-disjoint paths in $G$
such that $P_i$ has ends $s_i$ and $t_i$ for every $i\in[k]$.
If $s_i=t_i$, the path $P_i$ consists of the single vertex $s_i$.
For $k=0$, both $\mathcal S$ and $\mathcal P$ are empty.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:linked}
For an integer $k\ge1$, a graph $G$ is \emph{$k$-linked} if
$\mathsf{v}(G)\ge2k$ and,
for every set $\mathcal S=\{(s_i,t_i)\}_{i\in[k]}$ of pairs of vertices of $G$
with $s_i\ne s_j$, $t_i\ne t_j$ and $s_i\ne t_j$ whenever $i\ne j$,
$G$ contains an $\mathcal S$-linkage.

For every integer $k\ge1$, every $10k$-connected graph is $k$-linked.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:model}\label{def:rooted-model}
For graphs $H$ and $G$, an \emph{$H$ model in $G$} is a collection
$\mathcal M=\{B_v:v\in V(H)\}$ of pairwise vertex-disjoint nonempty
connected subgraphs of $G$ such that, for every $uv\in E(H)$,
some edge of $G$ has one end in $V(B_u)$ and the other in $V(B_v)$.
The subgraphs $B_v$ are the \emph{branch sets} of $\mathcal M$.
Let $R\subseteq V(G)$ have size $\mathsf{v}(H)$.
The model $\mathcal M$ is \emph{rooted at $R$} if
$|V(B_v)\cap R|=1$ for every $v\in V(H)$.

\label{lem:rooted-minor}
Let $p\ge1$ be an integer. If a $p$-connected graph $G$ contains
a $K_{2p}$ minor, then for every set $R\subseteq V(G)$ of size $p$,
$G$ contains a $K_p$ model rooted at $R$.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:woven}
Let $p\ge1$ and $q\ge0$ be integers. A graph $G$ with at least
$p+2q$ vertices is \emph{$(p,q)$-woven} if the following holds.
For every three sets $R=\{r_1,\ldots,r_p\}$,
$S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_q\}$ and $T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_q\}$ in $V(G)$
with $|R|=p$, $|S|=|T|=q$ and $s_i\ne t_j$ whenever $i\ne j$,
there are a $K_p$ model $\mathcal M$ in $G$ rooted at $R$ and a
$\{(s_i,t_i)\}_{i\in[q]}$-linkage $\mathcal P$ in $G$ such that
\[
 V(\mathcal M)\cap V(\mathcal P)=R\cap(S\cup T).
\]

<!-- lemma-21: body_md -->
\label{lem:reroute}
Let $p,q\ge1$ be integers, and let $G$ be a graph.
Let $S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_q\}$ and $T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_q\}$ be disjoint
sets of $q$ vertices in $G$, and let $\mathcal P$ be an
$\{(s_i,t_i)\}_{i\in[q]}$-linkage in $G$.
Suppose that $H$ is a $(p,q)$-woven subgraph of $G$ and
$R=\{r_1,\ldots,r_p\}\subseteq V(H)\cap(S\cup T)$ has size $p$.
Then there are an $\{(s_i,t_i)\}_{i\in[q]}$-linkage $\mathcal P'$ in $G$
and a $K_p$ model $\mathcal M$ in $H$ rooted at $R$ such that
$V(\mathcal P')\cap V(\mathcal M)=R$ and $V(\mathcal P')\subseteq V(H)\cup V(\mathcal P)$.

<!-- lemma-22: body_md -->
\label{lem:separation}
There is an absolute constant $C=C_{\ref{lem:separation}}$ such that,
for every integer $t\ge T_{\ref{thm:local-list}}$, every bad list assignment $L$ on a
$K_t$-minor-free graph $G$ with $|L|\ge2Ct$
is $Ct$-separable in $G$.
<!-- end lemma-22 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
\phantomsection\label{par:separation-constants}
We take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separation-parameters}
 N_0=\left\lceil 100C_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}^2C_{\ref{lem:bipartite}}C_{\ref{thm:routing}}\right\rceil,\quad
 C=100C_{\ref{lem:deletion}}N_0^2.
\end{equation}
With $N_0$ fixed, increase the threshold $T_{\ref{thm:local-list}}$ from
Lemma~\ref{thm:local-list} so that $\ln t\ge16N_0^2$
for every $t\ge T_{\ref{thm:local-list}}$.

Let $k=Ct$, and suppose that $L$ is not
$k$-separable in $G$. By hypothesis, $|L|\ge2k$.
Since $N_0\ge1000$ and $C_{\ref{lem:deletion}}\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separation-list-budget}
 k=100C_{\ref{lem:deletion}}N_0^2t\ge16N_0t.
\end{equation}

Let $p=\left\lceil \sqrt{\ln(2t)}\right\rceil$ and $q=\left\lceil t/p\right\rceil$.
Since $t\ge3$, we have $p\le\min\{2\ln t,t\}$, $q\le t$ and
$t\le pq<t+p\le2t$.

For $r=0,\ldots,p$, we recursively construct a tuple
\[
 (H_r,L_r,\mathcal M_r,\mathcal T_r^1,\mathcal T_r^2,U_r)
\]
satisfying the following conditions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(C\arabic*),ref=(C\arabic*),leftmargin=3em]
\item\label{inv:carrier}
$H_r$ is an induced $N_0t$-connected subgraph of $G$ with a bad
sublist $L_r\subseteq L$ satisfying $|L_r|\ge|L|-k/2$.
\item\label{inv:tangent}
$\mathcal M_r=(B_r^1,\ldots,B_r^{rq})$ is a $K_{rq}$ model in $G$.
For each $i\in[rq]$, $H_r$ and $B_r^i$ have exactly one common vertex, denoted by $w_r^i$.
Let $W_r=\{w_r^1,\ldots,w_r^{rq}\}$; for $r=0$, the model and $W_0$ are empty.
\item\label{inv:paths}
$\mathcal T_r^1$ and $\mathcal T_r^2$ are path families in $G$ avoiding $H_r$.
The paths in each family are pairwise disjoint, and
$|\mathcal T_r^1|+|\mathcal T_r^2|\le16tr$.
\item\label{inv:size}
$U_r\subseteq V(G)$ satisfies $|U_r|\le t+rN_0t(\ln t)^4$,
$W_r\subseteq U_r$ and $V(\mathcal M_r)\subseteq U_r\cup V(\mathcal T_r^1\cup\mathcal T_r^2)$.
\item\label{inv:degenerate}
$G[V(\mathcal T_r^1\cup\mathcal T_r^2)\setminus U_r]$ is $100t$-degenerate.
\item\label{inv:neighbors}
Every vertex outside $U_r\cup V(\mathcal T_r^1\cup\mathcal T_r^2)$ has at most three neighbors on
each path in $\mathcal T_r^1\cup\mathcal T_r^2$ and at most $12t$ neighbors in $V(\mathcal T_r^1\cup\mathcal T_r^2)$.
\end{enumerate}

By Inequality~\eqref{eq:separation-list-budget}, $|L|\ge2k\ge4N_0t+1$.
By Lemma~\ref{thm:bad-connected} (with $N_0t$ in place of $k$), there are an
induced $N_0t$-connected subgraph $H_0$ and a bad sublist
$L_0\subseteq L$ with $|L_0|\ge|L|-4N_0t\ge|L|-k/2$.
Let $\mathcal M_0$ be the empty model, let $U_0=W_0=\varnothing$, and let $\mathcal T_0^1=\mathcal T_0^2=\varnothing$.
Then~\ref{inv:carrier}--\ref{inv:neighbors} hold.

Let $0\le r<p$, and suppose that the tuple at stage $r$
has been constructed and satisfies~\ref{inv:carrier}--\ref{inv:neighbors}.
We construct the tuple at stage $r+1$ in six steps
(see Fig.~\ref{fig:separation-step} for an illustration).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1.} Reserving subgraphs and paths to extend $\mathcal M_r$ to $\mathcal M_{r+1}$.\par\nobreak
\medskip\nobreak
To form $\mathcal M_{r+1}$, we enlarge the existing $rq$ branch sets and add $q$ new branch sets.
Since $|L_r|\ge3k/2>2t\ge|W_r|$, color $W_r$ with distinct colors
and delete it, removing the colors used on neighbors in $W_r$.
The resulting bad sublist on $H_r\setminus W_r$ has size at least $|L_r|-rq$.
By $|L|\ge2k$, \ref{inv:carrier} and Inequality~\eqref{eq:separation-list-budget}, its size is at least
\[
 |L_r|-rq\ge\frac{3k}{2}-2t\ge k
 \ge8C_{\ref{lem:deletion}}C_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}N_0t.
\]
Since $p\le\left\lceil \ln t\right\rceil$, Lemma~\ref{cor:many-pieces} (with $N_0t$ in place of $k$) gives pairwise disjoint induced $N_0t$-connected subgraphs $J_1,\ldots,J_p,D$ of $H_r\setminus W_r$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separation-small-pieces}
 \max\{\mathsf v(D),\mathsf v(J_1),\ldots,\mathsf v(J_p)\}
 \leC_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}^2t(\ln t)^3.
\end{equation}
Since $\mathsf v(J_i)>N_0t\ge2q$, choose a set $R_i$ of $2q$ vertices in $J_i$ for each $i\in[p]$.
Let $R=W_r\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^pR_i$, so $|R|=rq+2pq\le6t$.
Let
\[
 G'=G\setminus\bigl((V(\mathcal M_r)\setminus W_r)\cup V(\mathcal T_r^1\cup\mathcal T_r^2)\bigr).
\]
By~\ref{inv:tangent} and~\ref{inv:paths}, $H_r\subseteq G'$.
Recall that $D$ is $N_0t$-connected, so $\mathsf v(D)>N_0t\ge2|R|$.
Also, by~\ref{inv:carrier}, $\kappa(H_r)\ge N_0t\ge2|R|$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:double-fan} in $H_r$ with $A=R$ and $B=V(D)$, there is a double fan from $R$ to $V(D)$ in $G'$.
Choose such a double fan $\mathcal P_1$ with minimum total length in $G'$.
We have $|\mathcal P_1|=2|R|=2rq+4pq\le12t$.

The subgraphs $J_1,\ldots,J_p$ will supply parts of the new branch sets and the enlarged old branch sets.
The graph $D$ and the paths in $\mathcal P_1$ are reserved for joining the parts of these branches.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2.} Constructing $H_{r+1}$ and its bad sublist $L_{r+1}$.\par\nobreak
\medskip\nobreak
Let
\[
 X=U_r\cup V(D)\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^pV(J_i),\quad
 Y=V(\mathcal T_r^1\cup\mathcal T_r^2\cup\mathcal P_1)\setminus X.
\]
We verify the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion} with $d=100t$.

Recall that $\mathcal P_1$ has minimum total length in $G'$,
$|\mathcal P_1|\le12t$, and $R\cup V(D)\subseteq X$.
By Remark~\ref{rem:short-double-fan},
$G[V(\mathcal P_1)\setminus X]$ is $(24t-1)$-degenerate.
The paths in $\mathcal P_1$ avoid $V(\mathcal T_r^1\cup\mathcal T_r^2)$.
By~\ref{inv:neighbors}, every vertex of $V(\mathcal P_1)\setminus X$
has at most $12t$ neighbors on these old paths.
Delete the vertices of $V(\mathcal P_1)\setminus X$ first in a degeneracy
ordering; each has at most $36t-1$ remaining neighbors in $G[Y]$.
The remaining graph is $100t$-degenerate by~\ref{inv:degenerate},
since $U_r\subseteq X$. Thus $G[Y]$ is $100t$-degenerate.

By~\ref{inv:size} at stage $r$,
$V(\mathcal M_r)\cup V(\mathcal T_r^1\cup\mathcal T_r^2)\subseteq X\cup Y$,
so every vertex outside $X\cup Y$ belongs to $G'$.
By~\ref{inv:neighbors} at stage $r$ and
Remark~\ref{rem:short-double-fan} in $G'$,
every vertex outside $X\cup Y$ has at most $12t+3|\mathcal P_1|\le48t<100t$
neighbors in $Y$.

Note that $p+1\le3\ln t$, $r+1\le p\le2\ln t$ and
$N_0\ge3C_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}^2$.
By~\ref{inv:size} at stage $r$ and Inequality~\eqref{eq:separation-small-pieces}, we have
\[
 |X|\le t+rN_0t(\ln t)^4+(p+1)C_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}^2t(\ln t)^3
 \le t+(r+1)N_0t(\ln t)^4\le t+pN_0t(\ln t)^4.
\]
By the choice of $T_{\ref{thm:local-list}}$, $p\le2\sqrt{\ln t}$ and
$N_0p\le(\ln t)/2$, so $|X|\le t+\tfrac12t(\ln t)^5\le t(\ln t)^5$.
By Inequality~\eqref{eq:separation-list-budget},
\[
 C_{\ref{lem:deletion}}(101t+1)
 \le102C_{\ref{lem:deletion}}t
 \le2C_{\ref{lem:deletion}}N_0t\le\frac k4<|L|.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion} (with $d=100t$),
$G\setminus(X\cup Y)$ has a bad sublist of $L$ of size at least
$|L|-k/4\ge7k/4\ge4N_0t+1$.
By Lemma~\ref{thm:bad-connected} (with $N_0t$ in place of $k$) and
Inequality~\eqref{eq:separation-list-budget},
$G\setminus(X\cup Y)$ has an induced $N_0t$-connected subgraph $H_{r+1}$
with a bad sublist $L_{r+1}\subseteq L$ satisfying
\[
 |L_{r+1}|\ge|L|-\frac k4-4N_0t\ge|L|-\frac k2.
\]

The subgraph $H_{r+1}$ and the bad sublist $L_{r+1}$ satisfy~\ref{inv:carrier}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3.} Joining $R$ and $H_{r+1}$ through $D$.\par\nobreak
\medskip\nobreak
Suppose that $|V(H_r)\cap V(H_{r+1})|\le N_0t$.
Since $|L_{r+1}|\ge3k/2>N_0t$, color and delete this intersection
as in Section~\ref{subsec:bad-lists-connectivity}.
The remaining bad sublist on $H_{r+1}\setminus V(H_r)$ has size at least
\[
 |L_{r+1}|-|V(H_r)\cap V(H_{r+1})|\ge|L|-\frac k2-N_0t\ge|L|-k.
\]
By~\ref{inv:carrier} at stage $r$, $H_r$ also has a bad sublist of $L$
of size at least $|L|-k$.
The subgraphs $H_r$ and $H_{r+1}\setminus V(H_r)$ are disjoint,
so $L$ is $k$-separable in $G$, a contradiction.
Hence $|V(H_r)\cap V(H_{r+1})|>N_0t$.

The disjoint sets $V(D)$ and $V(H_r)\cap V(H_{r+1})$ lie in $H_r\setminus R$ and each has more than $N_0t\ge2(r+1)q$ vertices.
Recall from Step 1 that $|R|\le6t$.
By~\ref{inv:carrier} at stage $r$ and the choice of $N_0$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:separation-parameters},
\[
 \kappa(H_r\setminus R)\ge\kappa(H_r)-|R|\ge(N_0-6)t\ge2(r+1)q.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:menger}, $H_r\setminus R$ contains $2(r+1)q$ disjoint $(V(H_r)\cap V(H_{r+1}),V(D))$-paths.
Since $H_r\subseteq G'$, we choose $\mathcal P_2$ with minimum total length among all families of $2(r+1)q$ disjoint $(V(H_{r+1}),V(D))$-paths in $G'\setminus R$.

Since $H_{r+1}\subseteq G\setminus(X\cup Y)$, the double fan $\mathcal P_1$ avoids $H_{r+1}$.
The paths in $\mathcal P_2$ avoid $R$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:join-fans} (applied to the sets $R,V(H_{r+1}),V(D)$, with $(r+1)q$ in place of the parameter $r$), the subgraph formed by $\mathcal P_1$ and $\mathcal P_2$ contains a family $\mathcal Q_0$ of
$|R|+(r+1)q=(2r+1)q+2pq\le8t$ disjoint $(R\cup V(H_{r+1}),V(D))$-paths.
Each vertex of $R$ is an end of one path in $\mathcal Q_0$.
The remaining $(r+1)q$ paths in $\mathcal Q_0$ have distinct ends $w_{r+1}^1,\ldots,w_{r+1}^{(r+1)q}$ in $V(\mathcal P_2)\cap V(H_{r+1})$.
Let $W_{r+1}=\{w_{r+1}^1,\ldots,w_{r+1}^{(r+1)q}\}$.
No path in $\mathcal Q_0$ has an internal vertex in $R\cup V(H_{r+1})\cup V(D)$.
Let $\mathcal S$ be the set of pairs of ends of the paths in $\mathcal Q_0$,
with the first entry in $R\cup W_{r+1}$ and the second in $V(D)$.
Then $\mathcal Q_0$ is an $\mathcal S$-linkage.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 4.} Extending $\mathcal M_r$ to $\mathcal M_{r+1}$.\par\nobreak
\medskip\nobreak
We first construct a $K_{2q}$ model in each $J_i$ rooted at $R_i$.
Since $q\le t$, we have $\ln(2q)\le\ln(2t)\le p^2$.
Hence
\[
 2q\sqrt{\ln(2q)}+|R|+(r+1)q\le2pq+8t\le12t.
\]
By Equation~\eqref{eq:separation-parameters}, $\kappa(J_i)\ge N_0t>12C_{\ref{thm:routing}}t$ for every $i\in[p]$.
By Lemma~\ref{thm:routing}, each $J_i$ is $(2q,|R|+(r+1)q)$-woven.
Starting with $\mathcal Q_0$, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:reroute} in $G'$ successively with subgraphs $J_1,\ldots,J_p$.
At step $i$, use root set $R_i$ and input linkage $\mathcal Q_{i-1}$ to obtain a $K_{2q}$ model $\mathcal N_i$ in $J_i$ rooted at $R_i$ and an $\mathcal S$-linkage $\mathcal Q_i$ such that
\[
 V(\mathcal Q_i)\cap V(\mathcal N_i)=R_i,\qquad V(\mathcal Q_i)\subseteq V(\mathcal Q_{i-1})\cup V(J_i).
\]
For each $i\in[p]$, later applications add path vertices only in subgraphs $J_s$ with $s>i$, all disjoint from $\mathcal N_i$.
Since every vertex of $R_i$ remains a path end, $V(\mathcal Q_p)\cap V(\mathcal N_i)=R_i$.

By the inclusion above,
\[
 V(\mathcal Q_p)\subseteq V(\mathcal Q_0)\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^pV(J_i)\subseteq V(\mathcal Q_0)\cup X.
\]
Each $J_i$ is disjoint from $H_{r+1}$, $D$ and $\mathcal M_r$.
Thus no added path vertex lies in $H_{r+1}$, $D$ or $\mathcal M_r$.
Since $\mathcal Q_p$ is an $\mathcal S$-linkage, it meets $H_{r+1}$ only at $W_{r+1}$ and $\mathcal M_r$ only at $W_r$, as does $\mathcal Q_0$ in Step 3.
No path in $\mathcal Q_p$ has an internal vertex in $D$.
For each $z\in R\cup W_{r+1}$, let $Q_z$ be the unique path in $\mathcal Q_p$ with end $z$.

For each $i\in[p]$, label $q$ vertices of $R_i$ as $v_i^1,\ldots,v_i^q$.
From the $pq\ge rq$ vertices of $\bigcup_{i=1}^p(R_i\setminus\{v_i^1,\ldots,v_i^q\})$, choose distinct vertices $u_1,\ldots,u_{rq}$.
For $i\in[rq]$ and $j\in[q]$, let
\[
 F_i=\{w_r^i,u_i,w_{r+1}^i\},\qquad
 F_{rq+j}=\{v_h^j:h\in[p]\}\cup\{w_{r+1}^{rq+j}\}.
\]
The sets $F_1,\ldots,F_{(r+1)q}$ are pairwise disjoint and contain $3rq+(p+1)q\le4pq\le8t$ vertices in total.
Their corresponding ends in $D$ are distinct, since the paths in $\mathcal Q_p$ are disjoint.
By Equation~\eqref{eq:separation-parameters} and $C_{\ref{thm:routing}}\geC_{\ref{thm:knit}}$, we have $\kappa(D)\ge N_0t>8C_{\ref{thm:knit}}t$.
By Lemma~\ref{thm:knit}, there are pairwise disjoint connected subgraphs $D_1,\ldots,D_{(r+1)q}$ of $D$ such that, for each $i\in[(r+1)q]$, $D_i$ contains the ends in $D$ of the paths $Q_z$ with $z\in F_i$ (see Fig.~\ref{fig:separation-step} for an illustration).

For $i\in[rq]$, form $B_{r+1}^i$ from $B_r^i$ by adding $D_i$, the paths $Q_z$ for $z\in F_i$, and the branch set of $\mathcal N_s$ containing $u_i$, where $s$ is the unique index with $u_i\in R_s$.
For $j\in[q]$, form $B_{r+1}^{rq+j}$ from $D_{rq+j}$ by adding the paths $Q_z$ for $z\in F_{rq+j}$ and, for each $i\in[p]$, the branch set of $\mathcal N_i$ containing $v_i^j$.
The paths in $\mathcal Q_p$ are disjoint, meet each $\mathcal N_i$ only at $R_i$, and have no internal vertex in $D$.
The subgraphs $D_1,\ldots,D_{(r+1)q}$ are disjoint.
Hence the branch sets of $\mathcal M_{r+1}$ are connected and pairwise disjoint.

Adjacencies between the enlarged old branch sets are inherited from $\mathcal M_r$.
For $i\in[rq]$, let $s$ be the index with $u_i\in R_s$.
In $\mathcal N_s$, the branch set containing $u_i$ is adjacent to the branch set containing $v_s^j$ for every $j\in[q]$.
Thus $B_{r+1}^i$ is adjacent to every new branch set.
Any two new branch sets are adjacent within $\mathcal N_1$.
Therefore $\mathcal M_{r+1}=(B_{r+1}^1,\ldots,B_{r+1}^{(r+1)q})$ is a $K_{(r+1)q}$ model.
Since $\mathcal Q_p$ meets $H_{r+1}$ only at $W_{r+1}$, each $B_{r+1}^i$ meets $H_{r+1}$ only at $w_{r+1}^i$.
Thus~\ref{inv:tangent} holds.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 5.} Constructing $\mathcal T_{r+1}^1$ and $\mathcal T_{r+1}^2$.\par\nobreak
\medskip\nobreak
Recall that $\mathcal P_1$ is the double fan from $R$ to $V(D)$ in $G'$
defined in Step 1, and $\mathcal P_2$ is the family of $2(r+1)q$ disjoint
$(V(H_{r+1}),V(D))$-paths in $G'\setminus R$ defined in Step 3.
Let
\[
 \mathcal T_{r+1}^1=\mathcal T_r^1\cup\{P\setminus R:P\in\mathcal P_1\},\quad
 \mathcal T_{r+1}^2=\mathcal T_r^2\cup\{P\setminus V(H_{r+1}):P\in\mathcal P_2\}.
\]
The paths in $\mathcal P_1\cup\mathcal P_2$ lie in $G'$ and avoid $V(\mathcal T_r^1\cup\mathcal T_r^2)$.
Thus the paths in each of $\mathcal T_{r+1}^1,\mathcal T_{r+1}^2$ are pairwise disjoint.
By the construction in Step 2, $H_{r+1}$ is disjoint from
$V(\mathcal T_r^1\cup\mathcal T_r^2\cup\mathcal P_1)$.
Each path in $\mathcal P_2$ meets $H_{r+1}$ only at the end deleted when defining $\mathcal T_{r+1}^2$.
Hence both $\mathcal T_{r+1}^1$ and $\mathcal T_{r+1}^2$ avoid $H_{r+1}$.
By~\ref{inv:paths} at stage $r$, $|\mathcal T_{r+1}^1|+|\mathcal T_{r+1}^2|\le16tr+12t+4t=16t(r+1)$.
This proves~\ref{inv:paths}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 6.} Constructing $U_{r+1}$.\par\nobreak
\medskip\nobreak
For each $i\in[2]$, let $Z_i$ be the set of vertices outside
$X\cup V(\mathcal T_{r+1}^1\cup\mathcal T_{r+1}^2)$ that have a neighbor on at least $2t$
distinct paths in $\mathcal T_{r+1}^i$.
Let
\[
 U_{r+1}=X\cup Z_1\cup Z_2\cup\bigl(V(\mathcal P_2)\cap V(H_{r+1})\bigr).
\]
We next verify that~\ref{inv:size}--\ref{inv:neighbors} hold.

For each $i\in[2]$, consider the bipartite minor of $G$ with one part
formed by contracting each path in $\mathcal T_{r+1}^i$ to a single vertex
and the other part $Z_i$.
Every vertex of $Z_i$ has degree at least $2t$ in this minor.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:bipartite}, $|Z_i|\leC_{\ref{lem:bipartite}}(\ln t)|\mathcal T_{r+1}^i|$.
Thus
\[
  |U_{r+1}\setminus X|\le2(r+1)q+|Z_1|+|Z_2|\le4t+16C_{\ref{lem:bipartite}}t(\ln t)(r+1).
\]
By Inequality~\eqref{eq:separation-small-pieces}, Equation~\eqref{eq:separation-parameters},
\ref{inv:size} at stage $r$, and the inequalities $p+1\le3\ln t$ and $r+1\le p\le2\ln t$,
\begin{align*}
 |U_{r+1}|\le {}& |U_r|+(p+1)C_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}^2t(\ln t)^3+4t
              +16C_{\ref{lem:bipartite}}t(\ln t)(r+1)\\
 \le {}& t+rN_0t(\ln t)^4+(3C_{\ref{thm:small-connected}}^2+4+32C_{\ref{lem:bipartite}})t(\ln t)^4
 \le t+(r+1)N_0t(\ln t)^4.
\end{align*}
Recall that, in the construction of $\mathcal M_{r+1}$ in Step 4, we use the old model
$\mathcal M_r$, the subgraphs $D_1,\ldots,D_{(r+1)q}$ of $D$, branch sets of
$\mathcal N_i$ in $J_i$ for $i\in[p]$, and paths in $\mathcal Q_p$.
Here $V(D)\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^pV(J_i)\subseteq X$ and
$V(\mathcal Q_p)\subseteq V(\mathcal Q_0)\cup X\subseteq V(\mathcal P_1\cup\mathcal P_2)\cup X$.
The ends removed from $\mathcal P_1$ and $\mathcal P_2$ in Step 5 all lie in $U_{r+1}$.
Thus, by~\ref{inv:size} at stage $r$ and the definitions of $\mathcal T_{r+1}^1,\mathcal T_{r+1}^2$, we have
\[
 V(\mathcal M_{r+1})\subseteq V(\mathcal M_r)\cup X\cup V(\mathcal P_1\cup\mathcal P_2)
 \subseteq U_{r+1}\cup V(\mathcal T_{r+1}^1\cup\mathcal T_{r+1}^2).
\]
Also, $W_{r+1}\subseteq V(\mathcal P_2)\cap V(H_{r+1})\subseteq U_{r+1}$, so~\ref{inv:size} holds.

Outside $U_{r+1}$, the new path families consist of the vertices in
$Y\setminus U_{r+1}$ and those of $\mathcal P_2$ outside $U_{r+1}\cup Y$.
All ends of $\mathcal P_2$ lie in $U_{r+1}$, and $|\mathcal P_2|\le4t$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:short-fan}(2) in $G'\setminus R$, the graph induced
by $V(\mathcal P_2)\setminus(U_{r+1}\cup Y)$ is $(8t-1)$-degenerate.
Every vertex of $V(\mathcal P_2)\setminus(U_{r+1}\cup Y)$ lies outside $X\cup Y$
and hence has at most $48t$ neighbors in $Y$ by Step 2.
Delete the new vertices first, each with at most $56t-1$ remaining neighbors.
The remaining graph is $G[Y\setminus U_{r+1}]$, which is
$100t$-degenerate by Step 2. This proves~\ref{inv:degenerate}.

Let $v$ be any vertex outside $U_{r+1}\cup V(\mathcal T_{r+1}^1\cup\mathcal T_{r+1}^2)$.
Then $v\in V(G')\setminus R$ and $v$ lies on no path in $\mathcal P_1\cup\mathcal P_2$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:short-fan}(1), Remark~\ref{rem:short-double-fan}, and~\ref{inv:neighbors}
at stage $r$, $v$ has at most three neighbors on each path in $\mathcal T_{r+1}^1\cup\mathcal T_{r+1}^2$.
Since $Z_1\cup Z_2\subseteq U_{r+1}$, we have $v\notin Z_1\cup Z_2$.
By the definitions of $Z_1,Z_2$, the vertex $v$ has neighbors on at most
$2t-1$ paths in $\mathcal T_{r+1}^1$ and at most $2t-1$ paths in $\mathcal T_{r+1}^2$.
Thus $v$ has at most $3(2t-1)+3(2t-1)<12t$ neighbors in
$V(\mathcal T_{r+1}^1\cup\mathcal T_{r+1}^2)$, so~\ref{inv:neighbors} holds.

This completes the construction of the tuple at stage $r+1$
satisfying~\ref{inv:carrier}--\ref{inv:neighbors}.

\smallskip
Repeating the six steps for $r=0,\ldots,p-1$, we obtain a $K_{pq}$ model $\mathcal M_p$.
Since $pq\ge t$, $G$ contains a $K_t$ minor, contrary to the hypothesis.
Hence $L$ is $k$-separable in $G$.

<!-- lemma-23: body_md -->
\label{thm:recursive}
There are absolute positive integers $N,C=C_{\ref{thm:recursive}}$ such that,
for all integers $a\ge a_0$ and $0\le b\le a$, every
$N2^{a-b}3^b$-connected graph $G$ with a bad list assignment $L$ satisfying
$|L|\ge C\bigl(3^a+2^{a-b}3^b\bigr)$
is $(2^{a-b}3^b,\,3\cdot2^{a-b}3^b)$-woven.
<!-- end lemma-23 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Let $t\ge3^{a_0}$ be an integer, and choose an integer $a$ such that
$t\le3^a<3t$. Then $a\ge a_0$.
We take $C=3(4N+3C_{\ref{thm:recursive}})$.
Suppose that a $K_t$-minor-free graph $G$ has a bad list assignment $L$
with $|L|=Ct$. Since $3^a<3t$,
\[
 |L|-4N3^a\ge Ct-4N3^a>3C_{\ref{thm:recursive}}3^a.
\]
In particular, $|L|\ge4N3^a+1$.
By Lemma~\ref{thm:bad-connected}, $G$ has an induced
$N3^a$-connected subgraph $H$ with a bad sublist $L_H\subseteq L$
satisfying $|L_H|\ge|L|-4N3^a\ge2C_{\ref{thm:recursive}}3^a$.
By Lemma~\ref{thm:recursive} with $b=a$, $H$ is $(3^a,3^{a+1})$-woven.
Thus $H$ has a $K_{3^a}$ minor and hence a $K_t$ minor, a contradiction.

For $2\le t<3^{a_0}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:density} below and greedy list coloring imply
\[
 \operatorname{ch}(G)\le2C_{\ref{thm:density}}t\sqrt{\ln t}+1
 \le\bigl(2C_{\ref{thm:density}}\sqrt{a_0\ln3}+1\bigr)t.
\]
Enlarging $C$ to a sufficiently large integer if necessary, every
$K_t$-minor-free graph is
$Ct$-choosable for every integer $t\ge2$.

<!-- claim-4: body_md -->
\label{clm:disjoint-roots}
It suffices to consider the case where $R\cap(S\cup T)=\varnothing$.

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
For each $r_i\in R\cap(S\cup T)$, choose a distinct neighbor $r'_i$
outside $R\cup S\cup T$. This is possible since
$|R\cup S\cup T|+p-1\le8p-1<\delta(G)$.
Let $r'_i=r_i$ for $r_i\notin S\cup T$, and let $R'=\{r'_1,\ldots,r'_p\}$.
Then $R'\cap(S\cup T)=\varnothing$.
Suppose that the required conclusion holds for $R'$.
Let $\mathcal M$ be the resulting $R'$-rooted $K_p$ model and $\mathcal Q$ the linkage
disjoint from $\mathcal M$. For each $i$ with $r'_i\ne r_i$, add $r_i$ and
the edge $r_ir'_i$ to the branch set containing $r'_i$.
All added vertices belong to $S\cup T$ and hence were avoided by $\mathcal M$.
The resulting branch sets therefore form an $R$-rooted $K_p$ model
whose intersection with $\mathcal Q$ is exactly $R\cap(S\cup T)$.
