<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{entropy_thm}
Let $\Phi_1,\dots,\Phi_m$ be single-mode Gaussian channels in the canonical form~\eqref{canonical}. For every state $\rho$ of $m$ modes with finite mean energy, possibly entangled and non-Gaussian,
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
S\bigl((\Phi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\Phi_m)(\rho)\bigr) \geq \sum_{j = 1}^m\varepsilon_j\bigl(2\langle x_j^2\rangle_\rho,2\langle p_j^2\rangle_\rho\bigr) \, .
\label{entropy}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Here $\varepsilon_j$ is the function $\varepsilon$ defined in~\eqref{eps}, evaluated with the parameters $k_j,y_j,\xi_j$ of the $j^{\,\text{th}}$ channel.
Equality holds for every product of squeezed vacua $\gamma_{u_j}$. Furthermore, for all $z_j,w_j>0$ such that $z_jw_j \geq 1$, the bound is tight under the corresponding upper bounds on the second moments:
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\sum_{j = 1}^m\varepsilon_j(z_j,w_j) = \min\Big\{ &S\bigl((\Phi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\Phi_m)(\rho)\bigr):\\
&2\langle x_j^2\rangle_\rho \leq z_j,\ 2\langle p_j^2\rangle_\rho \leq w_j\ \,\forall\, j\Big\}\, .
\label{entropy_tightness}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
The minimum can be attained with all the moment constraints saturated.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Write $z_j = 2\operatorname{Tr}[\rho x_j^2]$ and $w_j = 2\operatorname{Tr}[\rho p_j^2]$. We first obtain lower bounds that are affine in these moments. The two auxiliary ingredients used in the proof, Proposition~\ref{epr_prop} and Lemma~\ref{witness_lemma}, are established in Appendix~\ref{entropy_app}. For each channel, choose a squeezed vacuum $\gamma_{u_j}$, with $u_j\neq1/\xi_j$, independently of the actual input $\rho$. Lemma~\ref{witness_lemma} constructs a dilation by a symplectic unitary on the input mode $A_j$ and two auxiliary modes $B_j,E_j$, initially in vacuum. After a local squeezing of the receiver $A_j$, the resulting unitary $\widetilde V_{j,u_j}$ sends $\gamma_{u_j}\otimes\lvert0\rangle\!\langle0\rvert_{B_jE_j}$ to a two-mode squeezed vacuum on $A_jB_j$ and vacuum on $E_j$. Apply the product of these unitaries to $\rho_A$ and the auxiliary vacuum, then trace out $E = E_1\ldots E_m$:
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\Omega_{AB} \coloneqq \operatorname{Tr}_E\Bigg[&\left(\bigotimes\nolimits_j\widetilde V_{j,u_j}\right)\big(\rho_A\otimes\lvert0\rangle\!\langle0\rvert_{BE}\big)\left(\bigotimes\nolimits_j\widetilde V_{j,u_j}\right)^\dag\Bigg] \, .
\label{entropy_dilation}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Here $A = A_1\ldots A_m$, $B = B_1\ldots B_m$, and $|0\rangle_{BE}$ is the joint vacuum of the $2m$ auxiliary modes. The state $\Omega_{AB}$ has finite energy, but in general will be mixed and potentially non-Gaussian.
                                  
Its marginal on $A$ is unitarily equivalent to $(\Phi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\Phi_m)(\rho)$, so it has the same entropy.

We apply the mixed-state form of the many-pair entropy inequality, Proposition~\ref{epr_prop}, to $\Omega_{AB}$. Lemma~\ref{witness_lemma} chooses the coefficients of each quadratic observable so that its expectation depends on only one input quadrature moment. Substituting its identity~\eqref{pullback} into~\eqref{epr}, for $u_j<1/\xi_j$ on every mode, gives
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
&S\big((\Phi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\Phi_m)(\rho)\big)\\
&\quad\geq\sum_{j = 1}^m\left[h_j(u_j,1/u_j) + \left.\frac{d}{dv}h_j(v,1/v)\right|_{v = u_j}(z_j - u_j)\right] \, .
\label{main_tangent}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Here $h_j$ is the function $h$ defined in~\eqref{h}, evaluated with the parameters $k_j,y_j,\xi_j$ of the $j^{\,\text{th}}$ channel.
Each summand is the tangent to the squeezed-vacuum output entropy as a function of its position second moment. On any mode we may instead choose $u_j>1/\xi_j$, obtaining the tangent to $w\mapsto h_j(1/w,w)$ at $w = 1/u_j$, evaluated at $w_j$. The many-pair inequality is essential here: separate bounds on the marginal output entropies would not give this lower bound on their joint entropy.

We can now choose $u_j$ so that the affine corrections vanish. If $z_j<1/\xi_j$, take $u_j = z_j$; if $w_j<\xi_j$, take $u_j = 1/w_j$. These cases are mutually exclusive because $z_jw_j \geq 1$, and the resulting summand is exactly $\varepsilon_j(z_j,w_j)$. Otherwise both $z_j\geq1/\xi_j$ and $w_j\geq\xi_j$, so take $u_j\to1/\xi_j$. The seed entropy tends to $S_{\min,j}$ and its derivative tends to zero, giving the same conclusion. This derivative limit also holds when $k_j + y_j = 1$: the output symplectic eigenvalue minus one vanishes quadratically in $u_j - 1/\xi_j$, which compensates for the logarithmic divergence of the entropy derivative. Taking these scalar limits mode by mode proves~\eqref{entropy}.

For a product of squeezed vacua the interval in~\eqref{eps} collapses to a single point on each mode, so equality holds in~\eqref{entropy}. To prove~\eqref{entropy_tightness}, every feasible state satisfies
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
S\big((\Phi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\Phi_m)(\rho)\big) &\geq \sum_j\varepsilon_j\big(2\langle x_j^2\rangle_\rho,2\langle p_j^2\rangle_\rho\big)\\
&\geq \sum_j\varepsilon_j(z_j,w_j),
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
where the second inequality uses that each $\varepsilon_j$ is nonincreasing in both arguments. Let $\hat u_j$ be the minimiser in~\eqref{eps} for the $j^{\,\text{th}}$ channel. The product $\bigotimes_j\gamma_{\hat u_j}$ attains this value and obeys the constraints. Moreover, displacing its $j^{\,\text{th}}$ mode to have first moments $\sqrt{(z_j - \hat u_j)/2}$ and $\sqrt{(w_j - 1/\hat u_j)/2}$ makes both constraints equalities, while leaving every output entropy unchanged.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{holevo_thm}
Let $\Phi_1,\dots,\Phi_m$ be single-mode Gaussian channels in the canonical form~\eqref{canonical}. For every ensemble $(p_i,\rho_i)_i$ of states of $m$ modes with finite mean energy, possibly entangled and non-Gaussian, whose average has second moments $z_j = 2\langle x_j^2\rangle$ and $w_j = 2\langle p_j^2\rangle$,
\begin{equation}
\chi\big(\Phi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\Phi_m;(p_i,\rho_i)_i\big) \leq \sum_{j = 1}^m\big[h_j(z_j,w_j) - \varepsilon_j(z_j,w_j)\big].
\label{holevo}
\end{equation}
Here $h_j$ and $\varepsilon_j$ are the functions defined in~\eqref{h} and~\eqref{eps}, respectively, evaluated with the parameters $k_j,y_j,\xi_j$ of $\Phi_j$. For all admissible $(z_j,w_j)$, equality is attained by a product of Gaussian ensembles with these second moments.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Apply Theorem~\ref{entropy_thm} to every signal state in the ensemble. Since $\varepsilon_j$ is convex and second moments are affine in the state, Jensen's inequality bounds the average signal output entropy from below by $\sum_j\varepsilon_j(z_j,w_j)$. Subtracting this from the upper bound on the average output entropy proves~\eqref{holevo}. The product of the Gaussian displacement ensembles constructed after~\eqref{eps} attains equality, because its average covariance matrix is diagonal and every displaced signal has output entropy $\sum_j\varepsilon_j(z_j,w_j)$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{capacity_cor}
For arbitrary single-mode Gaussian channels $\Phi_1,\dots,\Phi_m$,
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\chi(\Phi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\Phi_m,E)
=
\max_{\substack{N_j\geq0\\ \sum_jN_j\leq E}}
\sum_{j=1}^m \chi_{\mathrm{G}}(\Phi_j,N_j).
\label{waterfilling}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Here $\chi_{\mathrm{G}}(\Phi,N)$ is obtained by restricting the optimisation in
Eq.~\eqref{channel_holevo} to Gaussian signal ensembles. For each $\Phi$,
the function $N\mapsto\chi_{\mathrm{G}}(\Phi,N)$ is concave. In particular:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item for every single-mode Gaussian channel $\Phi$,
\[
\chi(\Phi^{\otimes n},nN)=n\,\chi_{\mathrm{G}}(\Phi,N),
\]
and hence
\[
C(\Phi,N)=\chi_{\mathrm{G}}(\Phi,N)
\]
at every input energy;

\item for any two finite products $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ of single-mode Gaussian channels,
\[
\chi(\Phi\otimes\Psi,E)
=
\max_{\substack{E_1,E_2\geq0\\E_1+E_2\leq E}}
\big[\chi(\Phi,E_1)+\chi(\Psi,E_2)\big];
\]

\item the classical capacity of
$\Phi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\Phi_m$ at total energy $E$ per use is given by
the water-filling formula~\eqref{waterfilling}.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{memory_cor}
If $Y_xY_p=Y_pY_x$, the $n$-use channel is equivalent, through a passive transformation that preserves the total photon number, to a product of single-mode channels with noise $\operatorname{diag}(y_{x,l},y_{p,l})$, where $y_{x,l}$ and $y_{p,l}$ are the eigenvalues of $Y_x$ and $Y_p$ on a common eigenvector. The capacity of this block, when independently repeated with energy $E$ per block, is given by Eq.~\eqref{waterfilling} over these normal modes.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{radius_prop}
Fix a single-mode Gaussian channel in the canonical form~\eqref{canonical} and $N>0$. Let $\sigma_*$ be the centred Gaussian average output of an optimal ensemble at energy $N$, and let $\lambda = \partial_N C(\Phi,N)$. Then, for every $n$-mode input $\rho$ with finite energy,
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
D\Bigl(\Phi^{\otimes n}(\rho)\Big\|\sigma_*^{\otimes n}\Bigr) \leq  nC(\Phi,N) + \lambda\big(\operatorname{Tr}[\rho H_n] - nN\big).
\label{radius}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
In particular, the supremum of the left-hand side over $\operatorname{Tr}[\rho H_n] \leq  nN$ equals $nC(\Phi,N)$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{strong_sc_thm}
For every single-mode Gaussian channel $\Phi$, every $E>0$, and every $R>C(\Phi,E)$, there exist $a>0$ and $n_0$ such that every $M_n$-message code with $\ln M_n \geq nR$, whose codewords are all supported on the span of $n$-mode Fock states with total photon number at most $\lfloor nE\rfloor$, satisfies

\end{aligned}\end{equation}
The encodings may be non-Gaussian and entangled across all channel uses, and the decoder may be collective. The vanishing-error capacity under this cutoff equals $C(\Phi,E)$. At $E = 0$, all inputs are vacuum and $p_{\rm succ}^{(n)} = 1/M_n$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{epr_prop}
Let $A = A_1\ldots A_m$ and $B = B_1\ldots B_m$ be two collections of modes, with annihilation operators $a_j,b_j$. For each $j$, let $\nu_j,t_j,s_j>0$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\frac{t_js_j(2\nu_j + 1)}{\sqrt{\nu_j(\nu_j + 1)}} - t_j^2 - s_j^2 = \ln(1 + 1/\nu_j) \, .
\label{epr_normalisation}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Then every state $\rho_{AB}$ with finite mean energy satisfies
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
S(\rho_A) \geq \sum_{j = 1}^m\bigg\{&g(\nu_j) + \left(s_j\sqrt{\nu_j + 1} - t_j\sqrt{\nu_j}\right)^2\\
&- \operatorname{Tr}\Big[\rho_{AB}\big(t_ja_j^\dag - s_jb_j\big)\big(t_ja_j - s_jb_j^\dag\big)\Big]\bigg\}\, .
\label{epr}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Equality holds for $\rho_{AB} = \bigotimes_j \lvert\Psi_{\nu_j}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{\nu_j}\rvert$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For pure states this is the cited inequality, after changing its squeezing parameter to $\sqrt{\nu_j/(\nu_j + 1)}$ and rescaling the two coefficients. Indeed, positivity of the left-hand side of~\eqref{epr_normalisation} is equivalent to $\sqrt{\nu_j/(\nu_j + 1)}<t_j/s_j<\sqrt{(\nu_j + 1)/\nu_j}$. Any ratio in this interval can be normalised by a common positive rescaling, and~\eqref{epr_normalisation} makes the entropy prefactor equal to one. The two possible mode orders in the original statement are included because exchanging the modes and coefficients changes~\eqref{ordered_epr} only by a constant, which cancels against its reference expectation.

For a mixed state, apply the pure-state inequality to its spectral decomposition and average, using entropy concavity and linearity of the quadratic expectations. Every eigenvector of nonzero weight has finite energy. Moreover, $(t_ja_j^\dag - s_jb_j)(t_ja_j - s_jb_j^\dag)\leq2t_j^2a_j^\dag a_j + 2s_j^2(b_j^\dag b_j + \mathds{1})$ as quadratic forms, so all expectations are finite and the countable averages are legitimate. The stated equality follows from the reference expectations above.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{witness_lemma}
Let $\Phi$ be as in~\eqref{canonical}, fix $u>0$ with $u\neq1/\xi$, and let $2\nu + 1$ be the symplectic eigenvalue of the covariance matrix of $\Phi(\gamma_u)$, so that $g(\nu) = h(u,1/u)$. There exist a symplectic unitary $V$ on three modes $ABE$ and a squeezing unitary $U_r$ on $A$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item $V$ is a Stinespring dilation of $\Phi$, meaning that
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\Phi(\rho) &= \operatorname{Tr}_{BE}\Big[V\big(\rho_A\otimes\lvert0\rangle\!\langle0\rvert_{BE}\big)V^\dag\Big]\, ;
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
and
\item defining $\widetilde V_u \coloneqq (U_r\otimes\mathds{1}_{BE})V$, it holds that
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\widetilde{V}_u\big(\gamma_u\otimes\lvert0\rangle\!\langle0\rvert_{BE}\big)\widetilde V_u^\dag &= \lvert\Psi_\nu\rangle\!\langle\Psi_\nu\rvert_{AB}\otimes \lvert0\rangle\!\langle0\rvert_E\, .
\label{witness_reference}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Here $A$ labels both the input and the receiver. There also exist $t,s>0$ satisfying~\eqref{epr_normalisation} with parameter $\nu$, for which every input $\rho$ with finite second moments obeys
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\operatorname{Tr}\!\Big[&\widetilde{V}_u \big(\rho_A\!\otimes\lvert0\rangle\!\langle0\rvert_{BE}\big)\widetilde{V}_u^\dag\big(ta^\dag \!-\! sb\big)\big(ta \!-\! sb^\dag\big)\Big] - \left(s\sqrt{\nu \!+\!1} \!- t\sqrt\nu\right)^2\\
&= \begin{cases}
-\left.\dfrac{d}{dv}h(v,1/v)\right|_{v = u}\big(2\operatorname{Tr}[\rho x^2] - u\big),&u<1/\xi,\\[2mm]
-\left.\dfrac{d}{dw}h(1/w,w)\right|_{w = 1/u}\big(2\operatorname{Tr}[\rho p^2] - 1/u\big),&u>1/\xi.
\end{cases}
\label{pullback}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
The annihilation operators $a,b$ refer to output modes $A,B$. All choices depend only on the channel and $u$, independently of $\rho$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We order the quadratures as
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
R_{ABE} = (x_A,p_A,x_B,p_B,x_E,p_E)^{\mathsf T} ,
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
and write $\omega = \left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $\Omega_3 = \omega^{\oplus3}$, and $\sigma_z = \operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. The unitary $V$ corresponds to a real symplectic matrix $T$, defined by the relation $V^\dag R_{ABE}V = TR_{ABE}$. Following~\cite[Sec.~5.3]{BUCCO}, we see that, in order for $V$ to be a Stinespring dilation of $\Phi$, we may choose the first two rows, corresponding to system $A$, as follows in terms of the $X$ and $Y$ matrices of the channel:
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
T &= \begin{pmatrix}
X&\cos(\theta)\,\sqrt Y&-\sin(\theta)\,\sqrt Y\,\sigma_z \\
J & K & L \\
*&*&*
\end{pmatrix},
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
where the angle $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$ is chosen so that\footnote{Note that the definition after Eq.~(5.48) in~\cite{BUCCO} has a typo; it should feature $\cos(2\theta_j)$ instead of $\sin(2\theta_j)$.}
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\cos(2\theta) &= \frac{1-\det X}{\sqrt{\det Y}} = \frac{1 - \varsigma k}{y}\, .
\label{witness_dilation_matrix}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}

Complete positivity ensures that this choice of $\theta$ is possible. We are not interested, for now, in the last two ($E$) rows of $T$; however, we need to choose the blocks $J$, $K$, $L$ so that~\eqref{witness_reference} is satisfied.

Let $U_r$ be represented by the symplectic matrix
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
S_r \coloneqq \operatorname{diag}(r,r^{-1}),\qquad r \coloneqq \left(\frac{k/u + y\xi}{ku + y/\xi}\right)^{1/4} \, ,
\label{witness_squeezing}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
in the sense that $U_r^\dag R_A U_r = S_r R_A$. Then, $S_r(XV_uX^{\mathsf T} + Y)S_r^{\mathsf T} = (2\nu + 1)\mathds{1}_2$. Moreover, $(2\nu + 1)^2 = k^2 + y^2 + ky(\xi u + 1/(\xi u))>(k + y)^2\geq1$, so $\nu>0$. Since the symplectic matrix representing $\widetilde{V}_u$ is $(S_r\oplus\mathds{1}_4)T$, we thus need to impose that
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
&(S_r\oplus\mathds{1}_4)T(V_u\oplus\mathds{1}_4)T^{\mathsf T}(S_r\oplus\mathds{1}_4) \\
&\quad = \begin{pmatrix}
(2\nu + 1)\mathds{1}_2&2\sqrt{\nu(\nu + 1)}\,\sigma_z&0\\
2\sqrt{\nu(\nu + 1)}\,\sigma_z&(2\nu + 1)\mathds{1}_2&0\\
0&0&\mathds{1}_2
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{witness_reference_covariance}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Its first two rows, multiplied on the right by $(S_r^{-1}\oplus\mathds{1}_4)T^{-\mathsf T}\Omega_3$, give the identity
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
&\begin{pmatrix}S_r&0&0\end{pmatrix}T(V_u\oplus\mathds{1}_4)\Omega_3\\
&\ = (2\nu \!+\! 1)\omega\begin{pmatrix}S_r&0&0\end{pmatrix}T + 2\sqrt{\nu(\nu \!+\! 1)}\,\sigma_z\omega\begin{pmatrix}J&K&L\end{pmatrix},
\label{witness_row_elimination}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
where we used $T^{-\mathsf T} = -\Omega_3T\Omega_3$ and $S_r^{-1}\omega = \omega S_r$. Since $(\sigma_z\omega)^2 = \mathds{1}_2$, this shows that
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
&\begin{pmatrix}J&K&L\end{pmatrix}\\
&\, = \frac{\sigma_z}{2\sqrt{\nu(\nu \!+\! 1)}}\Big[ (2\nu \!+\! 1) S_r\begin{pmatrix} X & \cos(\theta) \sqrt{Y} & -\sin(\theta) \sqrt{Y} \sigma_z\end{pmatrix} \\
&\, \quad + \omega S_r\begin{pmatrix} X & \cos(\theta) \sqrt{Y} & -\sin(\theta) \sqrt{Y} \sigma_z\end{pmatrix} (V_u\oplus\mathds{1}_4)\Omega_3\Big].
\label{witness_environmental_row}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}

To check that this choice actually permits the desired completion, note that the first two rows of $(S_r\oplus\mathds{1}_4)T$ have symplectic products $\omega$ and give covariance matrix $(2\nu + 1)\mathds{1}_2$ on the input $\gamma_u\otimes\lvert0\rangle\!\langle0\rvert_{BE}$. Together with $(V_u\oplus\mathds{1}_4)\Omega_3(V_u\oplus\mathds{1}_4)=\Omega_3$, these identities determine the cross terms when substituting~\eqref{witness_environmental_row}. In the symplectic product of the two-term expression in square brackets with its transpose, the two square terms give $(2\nu + 1)^2\omega$ and $\omega$, while each mixed term gives $-(2\nu + 1)^2\omega$. Hence
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\begin{pmatrix}J&K&L\end{pmatrix}\Omega_3\begin{pmatrix}J&K&L\end{pmatrix}^{\mathsf T} &= \frac{1 - (2\nu + 1)^2}{4\nu(\nu + 1)}\,\sigma_z\omega\sigma_z = \omega \, .
\label{block_identities_T_1}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
For the covariance matrix products, the mixed terms before multiplication by the coefficient $2\nu + 1$ are both $-\mathds{1}_2$. The same substitution therefore gives the other three identities:
\begin{align}
\begin{pmatrix}S_r&0&0\end{pmatrix}T\Omega_3\begin{pmatrix}J&K&L\end{pmatrix}^{\mathsf T} &= 0, \label{block_identities_T_2}\\
\begin{pmatrix}J&K&L\end{pmatrix}(V_u\oplus\mathds{1}_4)\begin{pmatrix}J&K&L\end{pmatrix}^{\mathsf T} &= (2\nu + 1)\mathds{1}_2, \label{block_identities_T_3}\\
\begin{pmatrix}S_r&0&0\end{pmatrix}T(V_u\oplus\mathds{1}_4)\begin{pmatrix}J&K&L\end{pmatrix}^{\mathsf T} &= 2\sqrt{\nu(\nu + 1)}\,\sigma_z \, .
\label{block_identities_T_4}
\end{align}
Due to~\eqref{block_identities_T_1}--\eqref{block_identities_T_2}, the first four rows of $T$, corresponding to modes $A$ and $B$, span a symplectic subspace. Hence, choosing a canonical pair in its symplectic complement completes $T$ to a symplectic matrix. Note that we still have some freedom in choosing $T$, as we can multiply the sub-matrix obtained by collecting the last two ($E$) rows by a local symplectic matrix on the third mode.

Due to~\eqref{block_identities_T_3}--\eqref{block_identities_T_4}, the two matrices in~\eqref{witness_reference_covariance} coincide in the $4\times 4$ top left entries, i.e.\ in the whole $AB$ block. Since this is the covariance matrix of a pure Gaussian state, $AB$ is uncorrelated with $E$. The full output is pure as well, because the input is pure and $T$ is symplectic, so the state of $E$ is also pure. By Williamson's theorem, we can transform it into the vacuum by applying a symplectic unitary on $E$ (without changing the first four rows). This establishes~\eqref{witness_reference_covariance}.

The first two columns of~\eqref{witness_environmental_row} give
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
J &= \frac{\sigma_z\big((2\nu + 1)S_rX + \omega S_rXV_u\omega\big)}{2\sqrt{\nu(\nu + 1)}},
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Since $k>0$ and $r>0$, the two diagonal entries of $S_rX = \sqrt{k}\operatorname{diag}(r,\varsigma/r)$ are nonzero. We can therefore multiply by its inverse to obtain
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\sigma_zJ(S_rX)^{-1} &= \frac{(2\nu + 1)\mathds{1}_2 - \varsigma\operatorname{diag}\big((r^2u)^{-1},r^2u\big)}{2\sqrt{\nu(\nu + 1)}} \, .
\label{witness_input_block}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Since $r^4u^2 - 1 = y(\xi u^2 - \xi^{-1})/(ku + y/\xi)$, the two positive numbers $r^2u$ and $(r^2u)^{-1}$ are reciprocal and unequal. Exactly one diagonal entry of the last matrix belongs to $\left(\sqrt{\nu/(\nu + 1)},\sqrt{(\nu + 1)/\nu}\right)$: the second for $u<1/\xi$, and the first for $u>1/\xi$. Choose $t/s$ to equal that entry. Then
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
tS_rX - s\sigma_z J\ \propto\ \begin{cases}
\operatorname{diag}(1,0),&u<1/\xi,\\
\operatorname{diag}(0,1),&u>1/\xi,
\end{cases}
\label{witness_one_quadrature}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
The proportionality factor is nonzero: the two diagonal entries in~\eqref{witness_input_block} are different, so choosing $t/s$ to equal one of them makes exactly one diagonal entry of $t\mathds{1}_2 - s\sigma_zJ(S_rX)^{-1}$ vanish, and multiplying by the invertible diagonal matrix $S_rX$ preserves this property. The strict interval makes the left-hand side of~\eqref{epr_normalisation} positive before normalisation, so a common positive rescaling of $t,s$ imposes that equation.

To evaluate the quadratic observable, we write its two quadrature combinations in terms of the input quadratures. The definition of $T$ gives
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\widetilde V_u^\dag\begin{pmatrix}tx_A - sx_B\\tp_A + sp_B\end{pmatrix}\widetilde V_u &= (tS_rX - s\sigma_zJ)R_A\\
&\quad + \big(t\cos(\theta)S_r\sqrt Y - s\sigma_zK\big)R_B\\
&\quad - \big(t\sin(\theta)S_r\sqrt Y\sigma_z + s\sigma_zL\big)R_E \, .
\label{witness_quadratures}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
On the input $\rho_A\otimes\lvert0\rangle\!\langle0\rvert_{BE}$, each cross term between an input quadrature on $A$ and an auxiliary quadrature on either $B$ or $E$ has zero expectation, because the vacuum first moments vanish. This remains true when $\rho$ is not centred. The terms involving only $B,E$ are independent of $\rho$, as is the scalar term in~\eqref{ordered_epr}; they therefore cancel when subtracting the expectation obtained with input $\gamma_u$. By~\eqref{witness_reference}, that expectation is $\left(s\sqrt{\nu + 1}-t\sqrt\nu\right)^2$, and~\eqref{witness_one_quadrature} now gives
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\operatorname{Tr}\!\Big[&\widetilde{V}_u\big(\rho_A\!\otimes\lvert0\rangle\!\langle0\rvert_{BE}\big)\widetilde{V}_u^\dag\big(ta^\dag\!-\!sb\big)\big(ta\!-\!sb^\dag\big)\Big] - \left(s\sqrt{\nu\!+\!1}\!-t\sqrt\nu\right)^2\\
&= \begin{cases}
c\big(2\operatorname{Tr}[\rho x^2] - u\big),&u<1/\xi,\\[2mm]
c\big(2\operatorname{Tr}[\rho p^2] - 1/u\big),&u>1/\xi.
\end{cases}
\label{witness_reference_subtraction}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Here $c>0$ is one quarter of the square of the nonzero entry of $tS_rX - s\sigma_zJ$, and is independent of $\rho$.

For $u<1/\xi$, keep $V,U_r,t,s$ fixed and apply the mixed-state one-pair case of Proposition~\ref{epr_prop} to the $AB$ marginal obtained from $\gamma_v$. Its receiver entropy is $h(v,1/v)$, so
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
h(v,1/v) \geq h(u,1/u) - c(v - u),\qquad v>0 \, ,
\label{witness_tangency}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
with equality at $v = u$. Differentiability at this interior point therefore gives $c = -\left.\frac{d}{dv}h(v,1/v)\right|_{v = u}$. For $u>1/\xi$, the same argument with the input $\gamma_{1/w}$ gives the difference $c\big(2\operatorname{Tr}[\rho p^2] - 1/u\big)$ and identifies $c = -\left.\frac{d}{dw}h(1/w,w)\right|_{w = 1/u}$. This establishes~\eqref{pullback} and therefore completes the proof.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{memory_sc_prop}
Fix finite memory strength $s$, thermal occupancy $T \geq 0$, and $0<\eta<1$ in the chain model of Ref.~\cite{LPM2009}. For every $E>0$ and $R>C_{\rm mem}(E)$, codes of rate at least $R$ whose inputs are supported on the subspace spanned by Fock states with total photon number at most $\lfloor nE\rfloor$ have exponentially vanishing success along the growing sequence of blocks. The cutoff capacity equals $C_{\rm mem}(E)$. Under a mean-energy constraint and average error $0 \leq \epsilon<1$, Eq.~\eqref{memoryepsilon} holds.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The passive normal-mode transformation preserves total photon number. It reduces each block to $\bigotimes_{j = 1}^n\Phi_{\theta_j}$, with $\theta_j = \pi j/(n + 1)$. These channels need not be identical. Each has a dilation on three modes: prepare a two-mode squeezed vacuum purifying the thermal environment, squeeze the environmental input by $s\cos\theta$, and couple it to the signal with a beam splitter of transmissivity $\eta$. If $r_T = \tfrac12\operatorname{arcosh}(2T + 1)$, the dilation matrices satisfy
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\sup_{\theta\in[0,\pi]}\max\{\|S_\theta\|_\infty,\|S_\theta^{-1}\|_\infty\} \leq  e^{|s|+r_T}.
\label{memorydilation}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
This remains valid at $T = 0$, with an unused vacuum purification mode.

All estimates in Appendix~\ref{energyfilter_app} are consequently uniform in $n$ and in the normal-mode parameters. To see this explicitly, the Husimi moment argument uses only a common upper bound on the operator norms of the block-diagonal symplectic matrices and their inverses. A common smoothing covariance matrix and the radial input identity therefore give a single function $\ell(s',L)$ for the whole product, allowing arbitrary correlated inputs. Here $s'>0$ is the moment parameter, distinct from the memory strength. The small-subset quadratic estimate uses the same uniform coefficient operator norm. The image projectors and transformed input Hamiltonians still commute site by site, and the polynomial degree, subset counting, and receiver Fock dimensions are unchanged. Thus the approximation error and projective-norm bounds have constants independent of the block length.

Let
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
C_n(E_1) = \frac1n\max_{\sum_jN_j \leq  nE_1} \sum_{j = 1}^n C(\Phi_{\theta_j},N_j).
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Corollary~\ref{capacity_cor} bounds the Holevo information of every auxiliary ensemble selected by the positive filter by $nC_n(E_1)$. As proved above, $C_n(E_1)\to C_{\rm mem}(E_1)$. The limiting function is finite, concave, and continuous for $E_1>0$. Choose $E_1>E$ with $C_{\rm mem}(E_1)<R$ and apply the same filter inequality and contradiction as in Proposition~\ref{strong_sc_thm}, replacing $C(\Phi,E_1)$ by $C_n(E_1)$. Its convergence and the uniform approximation constants give one positive exponential decay rate for all sufficiently large blocks. This establishes a converse for growing blocks, without replacing them by repetitions of a fixed block.

For cutoff achievability, take a bounded normal-mode allocation with strict energy slack, as in the preceding achievability proof. The average input is a product of centred Gaussian states whose covariance matrices range over a compact set. Their photon-number generating functions are finite on a common neighbourhood of zero, with uniformly bounded second derivatives of their logarithms. Their first derivatives are their mean photon numbers. A Chernoff bound therefore makes the average weight above $nE$ exponentially small. Random coding can simultaneously make this weight and the decoding error vanish. Projecting each codeword onto the cutoff and replacing its discarded weight by vacuum changes average success by a vanishing amount. Letting the energy slack tend to zero proves cutoff achievability at $C_{\rm mem}(E)$.

Finally, Proposition~\ref{transfer_prop} applies to an arbitrary block channel, so the uniform cutoff converse gives the upper bound in Eq.~\eqref{memoryepsilon}. Diluting the reliable codes at energy $N'<N/(1 - \epsilon)$ with vacuum gives the lower bound. Continuity permits $N'\uparrow N/(1 - \epsilon)$. At $N = 0$ all inputs are vacuum and both sides vanish; at $\epsilon = 0$ the preceding capacity result applies.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{transfer_prop}
For a fixed channel $\Phi$, let $B_n(M,E)$ be the supremum of the decoding success probability for $M$-message codes whose inputs are supported on the span of $n$-mode Fock states with total photon number at most $\lfloor nE\rfloor$. Every code with average energy at most $nN$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
p_{\rm succ} \leq \min\!\left\{1, \left(\sqrt{B_n(M,E)} + \sqrt{\delta_n}\right)^2\right\},
\label{transfer}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
where $\delta_n = \min\{1,nN/(\lfloor nE\rfloor + 1)\}$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We prove the bound without dephasing the codewords. Put $\Pi = \mathds{1}_{H_n \leq \lfloor nE\rfloor}$ and $Q = \mathds{1} - \Pi$, and let $\{\Lambda_m\}_{m = 1}^M$ be the decoder POVM. Apply the adjoint channel to its effects, defining $T_m = (\Phi^{\otimes n})^\dag(\Lambda_m)$. Then $0 \leq  T_m \leq \mathds{1}$ and $\sum_mT_m = \mathds{1}$. Define
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
p_L = \frac1M\sum_m\operatorname{Tr}\big[T_m\Pi\rho_m\Pi\big],\qquad p_H = \frac1M\sum_m\operatorname{Tr}\big[T_m Q\rho_m Q\big].
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Cauchy--Schwarz for the Hilbert--Schmidt inner product, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz over messages, bounds the cross term by $\sqrt{p_Lp_H}$. Hence
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
p_{\rm succ} \leq  p_L + p_H + 2\sqrt{p_Lp_H} = \left(\sqrt{p_L} + \sqrt{p_H}\right)^2.
\label{sectorbound}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
In particular, $p_H \leq \operatorname{Tr}\big[Q\bar\rho\big] \leq \delta_n$ by the energy constraint. Replace each input by the normalised state
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\rho'_m = \Pi\rho_m\Pi + \operatorname{Tr}[Q\rho_m]|0\rangle\langle0|^{\otimes n}.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
These form a valid code supported on $\operatorname{ran}\Pi$. Its success with the same decoder is at least $p_L$, so $p_L \leq  B_n(M,E)$. Equation~\eqref{transfer} follows. The argument allows arbitrary entangled inputs and coherences between the two sectors.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{sc_filter_lemma}
Fix $E<E_1$. Let $0 \le  G \le \mathds{1}$ be a finite-rank operator on the joint Stinespring output such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G V^{\otimes n}P_{n,E} = V^{\otimes n}P_{n,E}$;
\item $\operatorname{ran}G\subseteq V^{\otimes n}\operatorname{ran}P_{n,E_1}$.
\end{enumerate}
Suppose $A$ has finite local support, $\left\lVertA - G\right\rVert_\infty \le \epsilon$, and $\left\lVertA\right\rVert_\pi \le \Gamma$. For an $M$-message photon-number-cutoff code of success $p>0$, put $z = \epsilon/\sqrt p$. If $z<1/4$, then
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
n C(\Phi,E_1) \ge {}&(1 - 4z)\ln M + 2\ln p - 2\ln\Gamma + 2\ln(1 - z)\\
&-(1 + 2z)h_2\left(\frac{2z}{1 + 2z}\right).
\label{sc_filtercoding}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
A receiver-side Naimark isometry makes the decoder effects orthogonal projections $Q_m$. Conjugate $G$ and $A$, and compose the channel isometry, with that local isometry; their bounds and input-energy interpretation are unchanged. Let $\lambda_m = \left\lVertGQ_mG\right\rVert_\infty$. The identity action of $G$ on the original input image implies that $\lambda_m$ is at least the success of the original $m$th codeword, including for mixed codewords. For $\lambda_m>0$, choose a normalised top eigenvector $\lvert\psi_m\rangle$ of $GQ_mG$. It belongs to $\operatorname{ran}G$ and therefore corresponds to a genuine input supported on $\operatorname{ran}P_{n,E_1}$. Also $\lambda_m \le 1$. Set $Z = \sum_m\lambda_m \ge  Mp$ and $q_m = \lambda_m/Z$; zero terms are omitted. Writing $H(X)_q = -\sum_mq_m\ln q_m$ for the label entropy, we have $H(X)_q \ge - \ln\max_mq_m \ge \ln M + \ln p$.

On two classical-label copies and the dilated output define
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\lvert\psi\rangle& = \sum_m\sqrt{q_m}\lvertm\rangle_X\lvertm\rangle_{X'}\lvert\psi_m\rangle,\\
\lvert\theta\rangle& = \sum_m\frac{\sqrt{q_m}}{\lambda_m} \lvertm\rangle_X\lvertm\rangle_{X'}Q_mG\lvert\psi_m\rangle.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
The eigenvector equations give $G\lvert\theta\rangle = \lvert\psi\rangle$ and $\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert^2 = \sum_mq_m/\lambda_m \le 1/p$. Consequently $\left\lVertA\lvert\theta\rangle - \lvert\psi\rangle\right\rVert \le  z$ and $v \coloneqq \left\lVertA\lvert\theta\rangle\right\rVert \ge 1 - z$. The normalised vector $\lvert\varphi\rangle = A\lvert\theta\rangle/v$ has trace distance (with the factor $1/2$) at most $2z$ from $\lvert\psi\rangle$.

Orthogonality of the decoder projectors implies
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\theta_{XX'F} \le \mathds{1}_X\otimes\theta_{X'F}, \qquad\operatorname{Tr}\big[\theta_{X'F}\big] = \left\lVert\theta\right\rVert^2 \le 1/p.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
For a decomposition $A = \sum_lc_lX_l\otimes Y_l$ with local contractions, $c_l \ge 0$, and $\sum_lc_l \le \Gamma$ (or arbitrarily close), apply vector Cauchy--Schwarz. For each contraction $X_l$ and positive $T$, one has $\operatorname{Tr}_B\big[(X_l\otimes \mathds{1})T(X_l^\dag\otimes \mathds{1})\big] \leq \operatorname{Tr}_B[T]$. Together these give
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\varphi_{XX'F} \le \mathds{1}_X\otimes\mu_{X'F},\qquad \operatorname{Tr}\big[\mu_{X'F}\big] \le \frac{\Gamma^2}{p v^2}.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
More explicitly, one may take $\mu = v^{-2}\Gamma\sum_lc_l(\mathds{1}_{X'}\otimes Y_l) \theta_{X'F}(\mathds{1}_{X'}\otimes Y_l^\dag)$. Operator domination bounds the max-relative entropy, and hence the relative entropy, against $\mathds{1}_X\otimes\mu/\operatorname{Tr}\big[\mu\big]$. Positivity of the marginal relative entropy then gives $S(X|X'F)_\varphi \ge - \ln\operatorname{Tr}\big[\mu\big]$. With $S(A|B) = S(AB) - S(B)$, pure-state conditional-entropy duality therefore yields
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
S(X|B)_\varphi \le 2\ln\Gamma - \ln p - 2\ln v.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
The conditional-entropy continuity bound with finite $X$~\cite{Winter2016} gives
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
S(X|B)_\psi \le  S(X|B)_\varphi + 4z\ln M + (1 + 2z)h_2\left(\frac{2z}{1 + 2z}\right).
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
The $XB$ marginal of $\psi$ is a genuine channel ensemble with all inputs supported on $\operatorname{ran}P_{n,E_1}$. Subtract its conditional entropy from $H(X)_q$ and use \eqref{sc_holevo}, $v \ge 1 - z$, to obtain \eqref{sc_filtercoding}. The approximant has finite receiver and environment support. Conditional entropy continuity depends only on $\dim X = M$; the genuine channel ensemble also has finite energy. No finite-dimensional assumption on the full bosonic output is needed.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{sc_moment_lemma}
For a fixed $d$-mode symplectic unitary $W$, there is $s_0>0$ depending only on $W$, and a finite function $\ell_W(s,L)$ for every $L \ge 0$ and $0<s<s_0$, such that
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\sup_{\rho:\,\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq\operatorname{ran}\mathds{1}_{\{H_{dn}^{\rm phys}\leq nL\}}} \operatorname{Tr}\big[W^{\otimes n}\rho W^{\dag\otimes n}e^{sH_{dn}^{\rm phys}}\big] \le  e^{n\ell_W(s,L)}.
\label{sc_moment}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
The function is independent of $n$ and of correlations in $\rho$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let $S$ be the symplectic matrix of $W$, $\kappa_S = \left\lVertS\right\rVert_\infty^2$, and choose $c \ge (\kappa_S - 1)/2$. Put $A = c\mathds{1} + (\mathds{1} - SS^{\mathsf{T}})/2 \ge 0$ and $a_S = \left\lVertA\right\rVert_\infty$. Let $Q_\rho$ denote the heterodyne (Husimi) probability distribution of $\rho$. Its real outcome vector $Z$ is normalised so that the vacuum has covariance matrix $\mathds{1}$, and
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\mathbb{E}_{Q_\rho}e^{u|Z|^2/2} = \operatorname{Tr}\big[\rho(1 - u)^{-(H_{dn}^{\rm phys} + dn)}\big],\qquad 0<u<1.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Adding independent centred Gaussian noise with covariance matrix $c\mathds{1}$ to the output Husimi distribution gives the distribution of $S^{\oplus n}Z + \zeta$, where $\zeta$ is independent Gaussian with covariance matrix $A^{\oplus n}$. This characteristic-function identity is valid for arbitrary, possibly non-Gaussian, inputs. Set
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
u = 1 - e^{-s},\qquad v = \frac{u}{1 + cu},\qquad t = \frac{v\kappa_S}{1 - va_S}.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
For sufficiently small $s>0$, $va_S<1$ and $t<1$. Conditional Gaussian integration and $S^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathds{1} - vA)^{-1}S \le \kappa_S(1 - va_S)^{-1}\mathds{1}$ give
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\mathbb{E} e^{v|S^{\oplus n}Z + \zeta|^2/2} \leq (1 - va_S)^{-dn}\mathbb{E}_{Q_\rho}e^{t|Z|^2/2}.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Undoing the classical smoothing contributes $(1 - cv)^{dn}$, while converting the output Husimi moment into the photon moment contributes $e^{-dns}$. The resulting bound is
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\ell_W(s,L)& = -ds + d\ln(1 - cv) - d\ln(1 - va_S)\\
&\quad - (L + d)\ln(1 - t).
\label{sc_ell}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
The remaining input Husimi moment is at most $(1 - t)^{-n(L + d)}$ by the radial identity and the input cutoff. All uses of positivity concern Husimi distributions, not Wigner functions. The nonnegative integrals may initially be read in the extended sense; the finite input bound proves their integrability.

\emph{Choice of output and spectral cutoffs.} Choose a sufficiently small positive moment parameter $s$ for both $U$ and $U^\dag$. By Lemma~\ref{sc_moment_lemma}, choose $F_0$ so large that $sF_0>\ell_U(s,E_1)$. Let

\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
G_n = \Pi^{(n)}f_n(K^{(n)}/n).
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
This is a finite-rank positive contraction with the exact identity and energy-support properties in the proposition. It is not required to be a projection or a Gaussian operator. Since the range of $G_n$ is contained in the image of $P_{n,E_1}$, the direct moment bound gives $\left\lVert(\mathds{1} - P)G_n\right\rVert_\infty^2 \leq  e^{-n[sF_0 - \ell_U(s,E_1)]}$. Using self-adjointness and $\left\lVertG_n - PG_nP\right\rVert_\infty \leq 2\left\lVert(\mathds{1} - P)G_n\right\rVert_\infty$ yields
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\left\lVertG_n - PG_nP\right\rVert_\infty \le 2e^{-n[sF_0 - \ell_U(s,E_1)]/2}.
\label{sc_directtail}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}

\emph{Image polynomial.} Write $\Delta_j = \mathds{1} - \Pi_j$ and $L_n = \sum_j\Delta_j$. A NOR polynomial approximates one when all $\Delta_j$ vanish and zero otherwise. The approximation of~\cite[Theorem~4.5]{Sherstov2020}, used in~\cite[Lemma~3 and Fact~2]{BeigiTomamichel2026}, applies to commuting projections on arbitrary Hilbert spaces: it gives a real polynomial $p_n$ of degree at most $D_0$ with
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
p_n(0)& = 1,\qquad\max_{j\in\{0,\ldots,n\}}|p_n(j)| \le 1,\\
\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}|p_n(j)|& \le \epsilon_0 \coloneqq  e^{-cD_0^2/n}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
for a universal $c>0$ and $D_0 \ge 3$, as holds for all sufficiently large $n$. Its Newton expansion is
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
p_n(L_n) = \sum_{s' = 0}^{D_0}a_{s'}\sum_{|S'| = s'}\prod_{j\in S'}\Delta_j, \qquad |a_{s'}| \le 2^{s'}.
\label{sc_newton}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
In particular $\left\lVertp_n(L_n) - \Pi^{(n)}\right\rVert_\infty \le \epsilon_0$.

\emph{The approximant and its operator-norm error.} Set
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
A_n = P p_n(L_n)q_n(K^{(n)}/n)P.
\label{sc_approximant}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
It has finite local output support. Outside $[0,T]$, $|q_n(x)| \le (1 + 2x/T)^{D_1} \le  e^{2D_1x/T}$ for $x \ge 0$. Thus \eqref{sc_reverse} gives
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\left\lVertq_n(K^{(n)}/n)\mathds{1}_{\{K^{(n)}>nT\}}P\right\rVert_\infty \le \epsilon_2 \coloneqq  e^{-n[sT - \ell_{U^\dag}(s,2F_0) - 4t_1]/2}.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Choose $t_1<1/4$. The exponent is then strictly positive. On the complementary spectral interval $q_n$ is bounded by one and differs from $f_n$ by at most $\epsilon_1$. All functions of $K^{(n)}$ commute with $p_n(L_n)$ and $\Pi^{(n)}$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\left\lVertA_n - G_n\right\rVert_\infty \le 2e^{-n[sF_0 - \ell_U(s,E_1)]/2} + \epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2 + \epsilon_0(1 + \epsilon_2).
\label{sc_error}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
For fixed positive $t_0,t_1$ this is exponentially small.

\emph{Projective-norm estimate.} It remains to show that the exponent of $\left\lVertA_n\right\rVert_\pi$ can be arbitrarily small; using a local Fock dimension proportional to $n$ at every site would not suffice. We instead use the total photon number on each small subset. There is a constant $A_U$ such that, on any subset $J$ of $v$ sites,
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in J}K_j\right)^r P^{\rm phys}_{J, \le  L}\right\rVert_\infty \le [A_U(L + dv + 2r + 2)]^r.
\label{sc_quadratic}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
To justify this, write $\sum_{j\in J}K_j = \sum_{j\in J}c_j^\dag c_j$ where $P^{\rm phys}_{J, \le  L}$ is the total photon-number cutoff on these sites and the $c_j$ are fixed Bogoliubov linear combinations of the physical modes. The coefficient matrix is block diagonal with a fixed squared operator norm $q_U$, so the column $\mathcal{C}_J = (c_j)_{j\in J}$ obeys $\left\lVert\mathcal{C}_J P^{\rm phys}_{J, \le  L}\right\rVert_\infty^2 \le  q_U(2L + dv)$. Each $c_j$ changes the photon number by at most one. The adjoint row on vectors of photon number at most $L + 1$ therefore has norm at most $\sqrt{q_U(2L + dv + 4)}$, giving $\left\lVert(\sum_{j\in J}K_j)P^{\rm phys}_{J, \le  L}\right\rVert_\infty \le  q_U(2L + dv + 2)$. One may take $q_U \leq \kappa_S = \|S\|_\infty^2$, since conversion between quadratures and the doubled annihilation--creation vector is unitary. Iterating, with at most two additional photons per power, proves \eqref{sc_quadratic} with $A_U = 2\kappa_S$. The same largest singular value applies to $S^{-1}$, because $S$ is symplectic.

Expand $q_n(x) = \sum_{r = 0}^{D_1}b_rx^r$ and use the multinomial expansion of $(K^{(n)})^r$. Each resulting term, together with a term of \eqref{sc_newton}, has the form
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\frac{r!}{n^r\prod_jm_j!}\, \left(\prod_jK_j^{m_j}\right)\left(\prod_{j\in S'}\Delta_j\right), \qquad\sum_jm_j = r.
\label{sc_localterm}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
It is supported on at most $D_0 + D_1$ sites. All its factors commute and are nonnegative. For $r = 0$ it is a contraction. For $r \geq 1$, positivity of the terms in the multinomial expansion gives the joint spectral inequality
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\frac{r!}{\prod_jm_j!}\prod_jK_j^{m_j} \leq \left(\sum_{j:m_j>0}K_j\right)^r.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Since the $\Delta$ product is a commuting contraction, \eqref{sc_quadratic} bounds the norm of every compressed term \eqref{sc_localterm} by $\beta_0^r$, with a constant $\beta_0$ depending on $U,F_0,d$ but independent of $n,r,m,S'$, provided $(D_0 + D_1)/n \le 1$. Here the physical total cutoff on the supporting subset is at most $2nF_0$; one may take $\beta_0 = A_U(2F_0 + d + 4)$. All unbounded-operator comparisons are understood as quadratic-form comparisons, followed by finite photon-number compression. The $\Delta_j$ need not preserve physical photon number: their commutation with the $K_j$ supplies the positive-operator comparison.

For clarity, the compression by $P$ does not preserve small support, but its cost across $B:F$ is only polynomial. On a supporting subset $J$ use
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
P_{B, \le  L} = \sum_{a = 0}^L P_{B_J, = a}\otimes P_{B_{J^c}, \le  L - a}, \qquad L = \lfloor nF_0\rfloor,
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
and the analogous environment identity on both sides of the local term. This produces at most $(L + 1)^4$ terms. The receiver subset has dimension at most $\binom{L + v}{v}$, where $v = |J|$. The elementary finite-dimensional inequality $\left\lVertZ\right\rVert_\pi \le (\dim B_J)^2\left\lVertZ\right\rVert_\infty$ bounds each term; its remaining factors are local projections of norm at most one. Therefore the projective-norm cost per compressed local term is at most
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
(L + 1)^4\binom{L + D_0 + D_1}{D_0 + D_1}^{\!2}\beta_0^r.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
There are $\binom{n + r - 1}{r} \le \binom{n + D_1}{D_1}$ multinomial index tuples. The coefficient sum of \eqref{sc_binomial}, expanded in $x$, is at most $(1 + 2/T)^{D_1} \le 3^{D_1}$. Finally, $\sum_{s' = 0}^{D_0}2^{s'}\binom{n}{s'} \le 2^{D_0}e^{nh_2(D_0/n)}$ when $D_0 \le  n/2$. These estimates give
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\left\lVertA_n\right\rVert_\pi \le {}&(L + 1)^4 \binom{L + D_0 + D_1}{D_0 + D_1}^{\!2}\binom{n + D_1}{D_1}\\
&\times(3\max\{1,\beta_0\})^{D_1}2^{D_0}e^{nh_2(D_0/n)}.
\label{sc_projectivenorm}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Put $t = t_0 + t_1$ and recall the thermal entropy function $g$ defined in Eq.~\eqref{thermal_entropy}. It follows that
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}&\frac1n\ln\left\lVertA_n\right\rVert_\pi\\
\le {}&2t g(F_0/t) + h_2(t_0) + t_0\ln2\\
&+(1 + t_1)h_2\left(\frac{t_1}{1 + t_1}\right) + t_1\ln(3\max\{1,\beta_0\}).
\label{sc_smallrate}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
The right-hand side tends to zero as $t_0,t_1\downarrow0$. Choose them positive and small enough that it is less than $\eta$. Equation~\eqref{sc_error} still has a strictly positive exponential rate. Absorb fixed prefactors by decreasing that rate to obtain \eqref{sc_approx}.

Use real heterodyne outcomes $Z\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}$, normalised so that a coherent amplitude $\alpha$ corresponds to $(\sqrt2\,\mathrm{Re}\,\alpha,\sqrt2\,\mathrm{Im}\,\alpha)$. A Gaussian input with covariance matrix $V$ has heterodyne covariance matrix $(V + \mathds{1})/2$, consistently with $\hbar = 1$. For an arbitrary state, the radial Husimi identity is
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\mathbb{E}_{Q_\rho}e^{u|Z|^2/2} = \operatorname{Tr}\big[\rho(1 - u)^{-(H_n + n)}\big],\qquad 0 \leq  u<1.
\label{husimiradial}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
It follows by integrating the coherent-state Fock expansion; angular integration removes off-diagonal terms, and each radial integral is a gamma integral. Thus the right-hand side is at most $(1 - u)^{-n(E + 1)}$ under the input cutoff. No independence between input modes is assumed.

Temporarily smooth the output Husimi distribution by adding a centred classical Gaussian of covariance matrix $c\mathds{1}_{2n}$, with $c = (v - 1)/2$ and $v \geq \max\{1,k - y_p\}$. Its characteristic function is that of the output Wigner distribution multiplied by $e^{-v|\zeta|^2/4}$. The Gaussian channel action shows that this smoothed distribution is exactly the law of
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
Z_v&=X^{\oplus n}Z+G,\\
\operatorname{Cov}(G)&=A_v^{\oplus n},\\
A_v&=\frac{Y + (v - k)\mathds{1}}{2}\geq0 \, ,
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
where $Z$ has the input Husimi distribution and $G$ is independent. This identity is valid for all input states, including entangled ones.

Put $t = q_s/(1 + cq_s)$. Gaussian integration over the smoothing noise gives, for $\sigma = \Phi^{\otimes n}(\rho)$,
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\operatorname{Tr}\big[\sigma e^{sH_n}\big] = e^{-ns}(1 + cq_s)^{-n} \mathbb{E} e^{t|Z_v|^2/2}.
\label{smoothingidentity}
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Integration over $G$, conditional on $Z$, gives
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\mathbb{E}[e^{t|Z_v|^2/2}\mid Z] & = \det(\mathds{1} - tA_v)^{-n/2}\\
&\quad\times\exp\!\left[\frac t2 Z^{\mathsf{T}} \bigl(X^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathds{1} - tA_v)^{-1}X\bigr)^{\oplus n}Z\right].
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
Since $XX^{\mathsf{T}} = k\mathds{1}$, its quadratic form is at most $u_s|Z|^2/2$. Indeed, $t k/[1 - t\lambda_{\max}(A_v)] = u_s<1$. Apply Eq.~\eqref{husimiradial}. Substitution into Eq.~\eqref{smoothingidentity} cancels the auxiliary smoothing parameter, because
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\det(\mathds{1} - tA_v) = \frac{(1 - a_+q_s)(1 - a_-q_s)}{(1 + cq_s)^2}.
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
The result is Eq.~\eqref{mgfuniform}. These nonnegative integrals can first be interpreted in the extended sense; the finite upper bound proves their integrability. Finally, direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\hspace{0pt}
\partial_sL_\Phi(0,E) = \frac{a_++a_-}{2} + k(E + 1) - 1 = N_{\rm out}(E).
\end{aligned}\end{equation}
