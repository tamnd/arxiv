<!-- theorema-1: body_md -->
The automorphism group of $(\mathbb{R}^8,\circ)$ is $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{O})\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle\cong PSU(3)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_2$, where $\sigma$ is an anti-automorphism of $\mathcal{O}$ fixing $C$. For every $c\in C$ with $\arg c\notin 60^\circ\mathbb{Z}$ the stabilizer of $c$ is $T\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle$, where $T$ is a two-dimensional torus that rotates $V_1,V_2,V_3$ by the angles $2\alpha$, $\beta-\alpha$, $\alpha+\beta$; the filled Julia set $K_c$ of $z\mapsto z\diamond z+c$ is invariant under $(T\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle)\times\{\pm\mathrm{id}\}$, and exactly $18$ of the $56$ coordinate sections of $K_c$ admit a continuous symmetry of algebraic origin: those containing a root plane. On the Weyl walls $\arg c\in60^\circ\mathbb{Z}$ the stabilizer grows to $U(2)$ and new revolutions appear, about the wall itself.

<!-- theoremb-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{M}_0$ be the Mandelbrot set with seed $0$ of a symmetric composition algebra and let $\Delta=4\mathrm{n}(c)^3-\mathrm{n}(c,c\diamond c)^2$. For $\Delta>0$, $c\in\mathcal{M}_0$ if and only if the complex number $\zeta$ with $|\zeta|^2=\mathrm{n}(c)$ and $2\operatorname{Re}\zeta^3=\mathrm{n}(c,c\diamond c)$ belongs to the tricorn $\mathcal{T}$; for $\Delta<0$ the same holds with $\zeta$ in the split-complex numbers and the \emph{hyperbolic tricorn}. For the para-octonions and the compact Okubo algebra $\Delta\geq0$, the escape times coincide, and $\mathcal{M}_0=G_2\cdot\mathcal{T}$, respectively $\mathcal{M}_0=PSU(3)\cdot\mathcal{T}$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:colonna}
The name ``pseudo-octonions'' is sometimes used in the fractal literature for a hyperspherical ``multiplication'' of $\mathbb{R}^8$ that is not a bilinear product; it has nothing to do with Okubo's pseudo-octonion algebra, which is the object of this work.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:torus}
For every angle $\theta$ the maps $\varphi_{a,b}$ are automorphisms of the Petersson algebra \eqref{eq:petersson}. They fix $C$ pointwise and rotate the oriented planes $(j,k)$, $(l,il)$, $(jl,kl)$ by the angles
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angles}
2\alpha,\qquad \beta-\alpha,\qquad \alpha+\beta ,
\end{equation}
and they form a two-dimensional torus $T$ containing $\tau_\theta=\varphi_{1,u_\theta}$. The map $\sigma$ is an anti-automorphism of every Petersson algebra, of the para-octonions and of the octonions, i.e., $\sigma(x\diamond y)=\sigma y\diamond\sigma x$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For a unit quaternion $a$ the map $p+ql\mapsto apa^{-1}+(aqa^{-1})l$ is an automorphism of the octonions, and for a unit quaternion $b$ so is $p+ql\mapsto p+(bq)l$: both claims follow at once from \eqref{eq:cd}, since $|a|=|b|=1$. Then $\varphi_{a,b}$ is the composition of the first map with the second one for $b\bar a$, and it is an automorphism of $\mathbb{O}$. Automorphisms of $\mathbb{O}$ commute with the conjugation and, since $a,b,u_\theta$ lie in the commutative field $C$, the maps $\varphi_{a,b}$ commute with $\tau_\theta=\varphi_{1,u_\theta}$; hence they preserve \eqref{eq:petersson}. The angles follow from $aj\bar a=a^2j$, $(b\bar a)l=e^{i(\beta-\alpha)}l$ and $(bj\bar a)l=(e^{i(\alpha+\beta)}j)l$. For the second claim, let $g=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,1,-1,1,-1,1,-1)$ be the automorphism $x\mapsto jxj^{-1}$ of $\mathbb{O}$ extended as above, which fixes the quaternion subalgebra spanned by $1,j,l,jl$. Then $\sigma=g\circ\overline{\,\cdot\,}$, and $g\tau_\theta g=\tau_\theta^{-1}$ since $jujj^{-1}=\bar u$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma}
\sigma(x*y)=g\big(\overline{\tau(\bar x)\,\tau^{-1}(\bar y)}\big)=g\big(\tau^{-1}(y)\,\tau(x)\big)=\tau(gy)\,\tau^{-1}(gx)=\sigma y*\sigma x ,
\end{equation}
because $gy=\overline{\sigma y}$. The same computation with $\tau=\mathrm{id}$ gives the para-octonions, and without the outer conjugations the octonions.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The anti-automorphism $\sigma$ is not an automorphism of the Okubo algebra: otherwise the product would be commutative. It fixes the plane $C$ pointwise and, as we shall see in Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, it represents the non-identity component of the automorphism group of the symmetrized product; in Okubo's matrix model of the algebra \cite{Okubo95} this component contains the transposition of traceless matrices, which realizes the outer automorphism of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ preserving the symmetric $d$-tensor.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:circ}
The \emph{symmetrized product} of $\diamond$ is $x\circ y=\tfrac12(x\diamond y+y\diamond x)$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:framework}
Let $g$ be an automorphism of $(\mathbb{R}^8,\circ)$ with $g(c)=c$. Then $f\circ g=g\circ f$ and $gK_c=K_c$; if $g$ is orthogonal, the escape times of $z$ and $gz$ with respect to a Euclidean bailout coincide. Moreover, for every algebra $f(-z)=f(z)$, hence $-K_c=K_c$ with equal escape times, and in every three-dimensional section through $0$ a mirror symmetry holds if and only if the half-turn about the normal axis of the mirror holds.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The first claim is immediate from $Q\circ g=g\circ Q$ and $g(c)=c$. For the second, $(-z)\diamond(-z)=z\diamond z$, so that the orbits of $z$ and $-z$ coincide from the first step on. In a section through $0$ the point reflection is the restriction of $-\mathrm{id}$, and the composition of $-\mathrm{id}$ with the half-turn about an axis is the mirror in the plane orthogonal to that axis.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:onestep}
Let $S\subset\mathbb{R}^8$ be a linear subspace and $\rho$ an isometry of $S$. If there is $h\in G_c$ with $Q(\rho z)=h\,Q(z)$ for all $z\in S$, then $\rho(K_c\cap S)=K_c\cap S$, with equal escape times.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since $h(c)=c$, $f(\rho z)=h\,f(z)$, and $h$ preserves $K_c$ and the escape times.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:nonalgebraic}
We shall never claim that a Julia set has ``no other symmetry''. Excluding symmetries that do not come from the algebra is a rigidity problem of a different nature; our statements concern symmetries of algebraic origin, and the absence of further symmetries is only tested numerically (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}).

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:O6}
Let $\diamond$ be the product of $\mathbb{O}(\gamma)$ or of its para-Hurwitz algebra, and let $g$ be a linear map of $\mathbb{R}^8$ fixing $1$ and preserving the norm \eqref{eq:norm}. Then $g(z\diamond z)=g(z)\diamond g(z)$. In particular $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{R}^8,\circ)$ contains the orthogonal group $O(7)$ of the imaginary part and, for the compact algebras and $c\in C$, the symmetry group of $K_c$ contains $O(6)\times\{\pm\mathrm{id}\}$, with $O(6)$ acting on $V_1\oplus V_2\oplus V_3$. A purely imaginary $z$ satisfies $z\diamond z=-\mathrm{n}(z)$, so that the section of $K_c$ by the imaginary hyperplane is a union of level sets of the norm, i.e., of spheres, and every orbit lies in the three-dimensional space spanned by $1$, $\operatorname{Im} z_0$ and $\operatorname{Im} c$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
A norm-preserving map fixing $1$ preserves the polar form, hence the real part $\operatorname{Re} z=\tfrac12\mathrm{n}(z,1)$, and commutes with the conjugation. The remaining claims follow from $z^2=-\mathrm{n}(z)$ for $\operatorname{Re} z=0$ and from the formulas above by induction.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:A}
Let $\mathcal{O}$ be the compact Okubo algebra and $\circ$ its symmetrized product.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{R}^8,\circ)=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{O})\sqcup\sigma\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{O})\cong PSU(3)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_2$.
\item For $c\in C$ regular, $\operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{Aut}(\circ)}(c)=T\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle$; for $c\in C\setminus\{0\}$ on a wall, the identity component of the stabilizer is $U(2)$; for every $c\in\mathbb{R}^8$ the stabilizer contains a maximal torus $T_c$, conjugate to $T$, which is the whole identity component when $c$ is regular.
\item For $c\in C$ regular, the filled Julia set $K_c$ is invariant under $(T\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle)\times\{\pm\mathrm{id}\}$, with equal escape times. A coordinate three-section $S$ through $0$ is mapped into itself by a one-parameter subgroup of $T$ acting non-trivially on $S$ if and only if $S$ contains one of the root planes $V_1,V_2,V_3$; exactly $18$ of the $56$ coordinate sections have this property, and each of them is a body of revolution about its third axis.
\item If $c\neq0$ lies on the wall $\ell_m$, the sections $\langle\ell_m,\ell_m^\perp,v\rangle$ with $v\in V_{k(m)}$, where $k(0)=1$, $k(60)=2$, $k(120)=3$, are bodies of revolution about $\ell_m$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
(1) By Proposition~\ref{prop:torus}, $\sigma$ is an automorphism of $\circ$, and so is every automorphism of $\mathcal{O}$. Every derivation of $*$ is a derivation of $\circ$, and the derivations of $*$ form a Lie algebra isomorphic to $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, of dimension $8$. The linear system that defines the derivations of $\circ$ has rank $56$ over the finite field $\mathbb{F}_p$, $p=2147483579$, into which the structure constants, lying in $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,\sqrt3]$, can be reduced; since the rank can only decrease by reduction, the derivations of $\circ$ form a space of dimension at most $8$, hence $\operatorname{Der}(\circ)=\operatorname{Der}(*)$ and the identity components of the two automorphism groups coincide, $\operatorname{Aut}(\circ)^0=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{O})$, which is connected \cite{Elduque15}. Now let $g\in\operatorname{Aut}(\circ)$. Conjugation by $g$ normalizes $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{O})$ and induces an automorphism of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, which is inner up to the complex conjugation. If it is inner, say induced by $h\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{O})$, then $h^{-1}g$ commutes with the action of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ on $\mathbb{R}^8$, which is the adjoint action and is absolutely irreducible; hence $h^{-1}g=\lambda\,\mathrm{id}$, and $\lambda(x\circ y)=\lambda^2(x\circ y)$ forces $\lambda=1$. Since $\sigma\notin\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{O})$, the same argument shows that conjugation by $\sigma$ is outer, and if $g$ induces an outer automorphism then $\sigma^{-1}g$ induces an inner one and lies in $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{O})$.

(2) The stabilizer of $c$ in the compact connected group $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{O})$ is the centralizer of the corresponding element of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$: it is connected, it is the maximal torus $T$ when $c$ is regular, and it is $S(U(2)\times U(1))\cong U(2)$ when two eigenvalues coincide, i.e., on the walls \cite{BroeckerDieck,Knapp}. Since $\sigma$ fixes $C$, the stabilizer in $\operatorname{Aut}(\circ)$ is $T\sqcup\sigma T$. Every element of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is conjugate into the Cartan subalgebra \cite[Thm.~4.34]{Knapp}, which proves the last claim.

(3) The first claim follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:framework}, since $T$ and $\sigma$ are orthogonal. Let $D$ be a non-zero element of the Lie algebra of $T$; by \eqref{eq:angles} it rotates $V_1,V_2,V_3$ with angular speeds $w_1,w_2,w_3=w_1+w_2$, so that at most one of them vanishes. A coordinate section is $D$-invariant only if, for each $k$ with $w_k\neq0$, it contains both axes of $V_k$ or none of them. If $S$ contains no root plane, all its axes lie in $C$ or in root planes with $w_k=0$, and $D$ acts trivially on $S$. If $S\supset V_k$ and $e$ is the third axis, then either $e\in C$, and the whole torus preserves $S$, or $e\in V_m$, $m\neq k$, and the circle $w_m=0$ preserves $S$ and rotates $V_k$. The count is $3\cdot6=18$.

(4) For the wall $\ell_0=\mathbb{R}$ the stabilizer is explicit. The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:torus} shows that $\varphi_{a,b}(p+ql)=apa^{-1}+(bqa^{-1})l$ is an automorphism of $\mathbb{O}$ for all unit quaternions $a,b$, and it commutes with $\tau_\theta=\varphi_{1,u_\theta}$ as soon as $b$ commutes with $u_\theta$, i.e., $b\in C$. Hence the maps $\varphi_{a,b}$ with $a\in Sp(1)$ and $b\in C$ form a four-dimensional group of automorphisms of every Petersson algebra fixing $\mathbb{R}$ pointwise, which is the group $U(2)$ of part (2), and its subgroup $\{\varphi_{a,1}\}$ acts on $\langle i,j,k\rangle=\ell_0^\perp\oplus V_1$ as $SO(3)$, by $p\mapsto apa^{-1}$. For $v\in V_1$ the conjugations by $e^{wt}$, with $w$ the unit imaginary quaternion orthogonal to $i$ and $v$, rotate the plane $\langle i,v\rangle$ and preserve the section $\langle1,i,v\rangle$, which is therefore a body of revolution about $\ell_0$. For $m=60$ and $m=120$ we use the Weyl group of $PSU(3)$ with respect to $T$, which acts on $C$ by the dihedral group of the walls and permutes the root planes: an element mapping $\ell_0$ to $\ell_m$ conjugates the stabilizers and maps $V_1$ to a root plane $V_{k(m)}$. Which of $V_2,V_3$ corresponds to which wall depends on orientations, and it was determined by computing the stabilizers explicitly (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
Part~(1) answers a question left open in the first version of this work, where the Okubo algebra was described as ``the algebra of broken revolutions'' and the stabilizer of $c$ was claimed to act on the coordinate sections only through half-turns. Both statements were wrong: the stabilizer of a regular $c$ is a two-dimensional torus, which acts by rotations on the three root planes, and the revolutions are displaced, not broken. The comparison with the octonions is dimensional: $O(6)$, of dimension $15$, against $T^2$, of dimension $2$, and $U(2)$, of dimension $4$, on the walls.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:B}
Let $(S,\diamond,\mathrm{n})$ be a real symmetric composition algebra and $c\in S$ with $\Delta\neq0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\Delta>0$, then $\mathrm{n}(c)>0$, there is $\zeta\in\mathbb{C}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta}
|\zeta|^2=\mathrm{n}(c),\qquad 2\operatorname{Re}\zeta^3=\mathrm{n}(c,c\diamond c),
\end{equation}
unique up to the dihedral group generated by $\zeta\mapsto e^{2\pi i/3}\zeta$ and $\zeta\mapsto\bar\zeta$, and the linear map $c\mapsto\zeta$, $c\diamond c\mapsto\bar\zeta^{\,2}$ is an isometric isomorphism of $P_c$ onto $(\mathbb{C},\bar x\bar y)$. In particular $c\in\mathcal{M}_0$ if and only if $\zeta\in\mathcal{T}$.
\item If $\Delta<0$, the same holds with a split-complex $\zeta=x+ye$ satisfying $x^2-y^2=\mathrm{n}(c)$ and $2\operatorname{Re}\zeta^3=\mathrm{n}(c,c\diamond c)$, and with $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{hyp}}$ in place of $\mathcal{T}$. In the light-cone coordinates $u=x+y$, $v=x-y$ the hyperbolic tricorn map is $(u,v)\mapsto(v^2+\zeta_u,\,u^2+\zeta_v)$, and $\zeta\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{hyp}}$ if and only if $0$ has a bounded orbit under both real quartic maps $t\mapsto(t^2+\zeta_v)^2+\zeta_u$ and $t\mapsto(t^2+\zeta_u)^2+\zeta_v$.
\item If $\mathrm{n}$ is positive definite, then $\Delta\geq0$ for every $c$ and, with a bailout on the norm, the escape time of $c$ equals the Mandelbar escape time of $\zeta$. For the para-octonions and for the compact Okubo algebra,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:saturation}
\mathcal{M}_0=G\cdot\mathcal{T},\qquad G=G_2,\ \text{resp.}\ G=PSU(3),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}$ is placed in the plane $C$, and the image of the invariant map $c\mapsto(\mathrm{n}(c),K)$ is the region $K^2\leq4\mathrm{n}^3$, whose boundary corresponds to the Weyl walls.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
(1) If $\Delta>0$ then $\mathrm{n}(c)^3>K^2/4\geq0$. Put $r=\mathrm{n}(c)^{1/2}$ and $\zeta=re^{i\phi}$ with $\cos3\phi=K/2r^3\in(-1,1)$. In $(\mathbb{C},\bar x\bar y)$ we have $\zeta\diamond\zeta=\bar\zeta^{\,2}$, $\zeta\diamond\bar\zeta^{\,2}=\bar\zeta^{\,2}\diamond\zeta=|\zeta|^2\zeta$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta4}
\bar\zeta^{\,2}\diamond\bar\zeta^{\,2}=\zeta^4=(\zeta^3+\bar\zeta^3)\,\zeta-|\zeta|^2\bar\zeta^{\,2}=K\zeta-\mathrm{n}(c)\,\bar\zeta^{\,2},
\end{equation}
which are the structure constants \eqref{eq:plane}; $\zeta$ and $\bar\zeta^{\,2}$ are independent because $\zeta^3\notin\mathbb{R}$. The map is an isometry, since $\mathrm{n}(c\diamond c)=\mathrm{n}(c)^2=|\zeta|^4$ and $\mathrm{n}(c,c\diamond c)=K=2\operatorname{Re}(\zeta\,\zeta^2)$. The admissible $\zeta$ form one orbit of the dihedral group, under which the Mandelbar dynamics is equivariant, since $\overline{\omega w}^{\,2}+\omega\zeta=\omega(\bar w^{\,2}+\zeta)$.
(2) With $y^2=x^2-\mathrm{n}(c)$ the second condition reads $8x^3-6\mathrm{n}(c)x=K$, which always has a root with $x^2\geq\mathrm{n}(c)$: any real root if $\mathrm{n}(c)\leq0$, and $x=\mathrm{n}(c)^{1/2}\cosh t$ with $2\mathrm{n}(c)^{3/2}\cosh3t=|K|$ otherwise. The computation \eqref{eq:zeta4} holds verbatim in the commutative and associative algebra of split-complex numbers. In light-cone coordinates the conjugation exchanges $u$ and $v$ and the square acts coordinatewise, which gives the formula; the even and odd terms of the orbit are orbits of the two quartic maps.
(3) If $\mathrm{n}$ is definite, its restriction to $P_c$ cannot be isometric to the split plane, so $\Delta\geq0$; the isometry gives the equality of the escape times. For the para-octonions, an automorphism in $G_2$ maps $\operatorname{Im} c$ to $|\operatorname{Im} c|\,i$, hence $c$ into $C$; for the Okubo algebra, every element is conjugate into the Cartan plane $C$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}). On $C$ the product is $\bar x\bar y$ by \eqref{eq:onC}, so $\mathcal{M}_0\cap C=\mathcal{T}$, and on $C$ one has $\mathrm{n}=r^2$ and $K=2r^3\cos3\phi$, which gives the image and its boundary $\sin3\phi=0$.

\label{rem:bailout}
In a split algebra the Euclidean bailout of the renderer is not an escape certificate, since orbits may remain bounded while their Euclidean norm is large. Theorem~\ref{thm:B} is a statement on bounded orbits, which is independent of the choice of the norm, while the rendered pictures depend on the bailout.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rem:transverse}
In the split Okubo algebra the invariant plane $P_c$ can be \emph{transversally unstable}: a double-precision iteration in $\mathbb{R}^8$ leaves $P_c$ by rounding, the distance from the plane grows exponentially, from $10^{-16}$ to $10^{-1}$ in about $180$ iterations, and the orbit escapes although the exact orbit is bounded. Among $1500$ random constants, $18$ escape times computed in $\mathbb{R}^8$ differ from those of the planar model, and all $18$ agree with the planar model when the iteration in $\mathbb{R}^8$ is repeated with $80$ significant digits. In a generic plane $(1,u)$ the double-precision iteration loses 902 of the 14731 points of $\mathcal{M}_0$ found by the planar model (Figure~\ref{fig:split}); no such loss occurs for the compact algebras. The rendered split Mandelbrot sets (Plate~F06 (Figure~\ref{pl:F06})) are therefore approximations of $\mathcal{M}_0$ from inside.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:sandwich}
In a symmetric composition algebra the sandwich map satisfies $(z\diamond a)\diamond z+c=\mathrm{n}(z)\,a+c$ and the cubic map $(z\diamond z)\diamond z+c=\mathrm{n}(z)\,z+c$. The norms $t_n=\mathrm{n}(z_n)$ of a sandwich orbit obey the real recursion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sandwich}
t_{n+1}=\mathrm{n}(a)\,t_n^2+\mathrm{n}(a,c)\,t_n+\mathrm{n}(c),
\end{equation}
which is affinely conjugate to $s\mapsto s^2+c_{\mathrm{eff}}$ with $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathrm{n}(a)\mathrm{n}(c)+\tfrac12\mathrm{n}(a,c)-\tfrac14\mathrm{n}(a,c)^2$; in the compact case the filled set is a ball, or a spherical shell.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The first two identities are \eqref{eq:symmetric-intro}. Taking norms of $z_{n+1}=t_na+c$ gives \eqref{eq:sandwich}, and the change of variable $s=\mathrm{n}(a)t+\tfrac12\mathrm{n}(a,c)$ gives the conjugation.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:bilateral}
Let $(S,\diamond,\mathrm{n})$ be a symmetric composition algebra, $a,b\in S$ and $e=b\diamond a$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bilateral}
(a\diamond z)\diamond(z\diamond b)=\mathrm{n}(z,e)\,z-\mathrm{n}(z)\,e\qquad\text{for all }z\in S .
\end{equation}
The product $x\star y=(a\diamond x)\diamond(y\diamond b)$ satisfies $e\star y=y\star e=\mathrm{n}(a)\mathrm{n}(b)\,y$, so that for $\mathrm{n}(a)\mathrm{n}(b)=1$ it is a Hurwitz algebra with unit $e$. If $\mathrm{n}$ is positive definite and $Q$ is an orthogonal map with $Qe=|e|\,1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bilateral-conj}
|e|\,Q\big((a\diamond z)\diamond(z\diamond b)+c\big)=v^2+|e|\,Qc,\qquad v=|e|\,Qz,
\end{equation}
where $v^2$ is the octonionic square. In particular every bilateral filled Julia set over the para-octonions or the Okubo algebra is similar to an octonionic filled Julia set, every orbit lies in $\operatorname{span}(z_0,e,c)$, and Kaplansky's trick $(1\diamond x)\diamond(y\diamond1)=xy$ is the special case $a=b=1$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The linearization of $(x\diamond y)\diamond x=\mathrm{n}(x)y$ reads $(x\diamond y)\diamond w+(w\diamond y)\diamond x=\mathrm{n}(x,w)\,y$. With $x=a$, $y=z$, $w=z\diamond b$, and since $(z\diamond b)\diamond z=\mathrm{n}(z)b$, we obtain $(a\diamond z)\diamond(z\diamond b)=\mathrm{n}(a,z\diamond b)\,z-\mathrm{n}(z)\,b\diamond a$; the associativity of the norm, $\mathrm{n}(x\diamond y,w)=\mathrm{n}(x,y\diamond w)$ \cite{KMRT,Elduque97}, gives $\mathrm{n}(a,z\diamond b)=\mathrm{n}(z,b\diamond a)$. Next, $a\diamond e=a\diamond(b\diamond a)=\mathrm{n}(a)b$, so $e\star y=\mathrm{n}(a)\,b\diamond(y\diamond b)=\mathrm{n}(a)\mathrm{n}(b)y$, and similarly on the right. Finally, with $v=|e|Qz$ one has $\mathrm{n}(z,e)=2|e|\operatorname{Re}(Qz)$ and $\mathrm{n}(z)=\mathrm{n}(Qz)$, and \eqref{eq:bilateral-conj} follows from $v^2=2\operatorname{Re}(v)\,v-\mathrm{n}(v)$. For $a=b=1$ one has $e=1$ both in the para-octonions and in the Okubo algebra, and \eqref{eq:bilateral} gives $2\operatorname{Re}(z)z-\mathrm{n}(z)=z^2$.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:theta90}
Let $\theta=90^\circ$. For $z=p+ql$ with $p,q\in\mathbb{H}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square90}
z*z=\bar p^{\,2}+|q|^2-2\,\big(i\,q\,\operatorname{Im} p\big)\,l .
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every unit quaternion $u$ the isometry $g_u(p+ql)=p+(uq)l$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twisted}
Q(g_uz)=g_{iui^{-1}}\,Q(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^8 ,
\end{equation}
and for $c\in C$ the map $f(z)=z*z+c$ satisfies $f\circ g_u=g_{iui^{-1}}\circ f$. For $u\in C$ the map $g_u=\varphi_{1,u}$ is an automorphism of the torus $T$; for $u=e^{j\varphi}$ or $u=e^{k\varphi}$ it is twisted, $f\circ g_u=g_u^{-1}\circ f$. Hence, for $c\in C$, the group $G_c$ of Section~\ref{sec:framework} contains the four-dimensional group generated by $T$ and $\{g_u\}$, and $K_c$ is invariant under it, with equal escape times. In particular:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every section of $K_c$ contained in $\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{H} l$ is the same ball, since there $z*z=x_0^2+|q|^2$;
\item the subspace $W=\langle1,i\rangle\oplus\langle l,kl\rangle$ is closed under squaring, $z*z=\bar p^{\,2}+|q|^2+2\operatorname{Im}(p)\,\bar q\,l$ for $p\in\langle1,i\rangle$, $q\in\langle1,k\rangle$, and $K_c\cap W$ is invariant under the rotations $R_\varphi=g_{e^{k\varphi}}$ of the plane $\langle l,kl\rangle$;
\item the section $\langle i,j,l\rangle$ of $K_c$ is a body of revolution about $l$, since for $z=x_1i+x_2j+x_4l$ one has $z*z=(x_4^2-x_1^2-x_2^2)+2x_4\,(x_1\,l-x_2\,kl)$, and a rotation of $(x_1,x_2)$ by $t$ rotates the square by $g_{e^{-kt}}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:onestep}).
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
With $u_\theta=i$ we have $\tau(\bar z)=\bar p-(iq)l$ and $\tau^{-1}(\bar z)=\bar p+(iq)l$, and \eqref{eq:cd} gives $\bar p^{\,2}-\overline{iq}\,(-iq)=\bar p^{\,2}+|q|^2$ for the first component and $iq\,\bar p-iq\,p=-2\,iq\operatorname{Im} p$ for the second one, which is \eqref{eq:square90}. Replacing $q$ by $uq$ leaves $|q|^2$ unchanged and multiplies $-2\,iq\operatorname{Im} p$ on the left by $iui^{-1}$, which is \eqref{eq:twisted}; the maps $g_u$ fix $C$ pointwise. For $u\in C$, $iui^{-1}=u$; for $u=e^{j\varphi}$ or $e^{k\varphi}$, $iui^{-1}=\bar u$ and $g_{\bar u}=g_u^{-1}$. The group generated by the $g_u$ and by $T=\{\varphi_{a,b}\}$ has dimension $4$: the $g_u$ form a copy of $Sp(1)$, which meets $T$ in the circle $u\in C$. For (1), $p=x_0$ is real and $\operatorname{Im} p=0$. For (2), $iqi=-\bar q$ for $q\in\langle1,k\rangle$. For (3), apply Lemma~\ref{lem:onestep} with $h=g_{e^{-kt}}$.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{atl:prop:equivariance}
Let $\diamond$ be the product of $\mathbb{O}(\gamma)$ or of its para-Hurwitz algebra, and let $g$ be a linear map of $\mathbb{R}^8$ fixing $1$ and preserving the norm \eqref{atl:eq:norm}. Then $g(z\diamond z)=g(z)\diamond g(z)$. In particular, if $g(c)=c$, the map $z\mapsto z\diamond z+c$ commutes with $g$, its filled Julia set is $g$-invariant, and for the compact algebras and $c\in\mathbb{C}$ the symmetry group contains $O(6)$ acting on the span of $j,k,l,il,jl,kl$.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Since $\mathbb{O}(\gamma)$ is quadratic, $z^2=2\operatorname{Re}(z)z-\mathrm{n}(z)$ and $\overline{z^2}=2\operatorname{Re}(z)\bar z-\mathrm{n}(z)$. A norm-preserving map fixing $1$ preserves the polar form, hence the real part $\operatorname{Re} z=\tfrac12\mathrm{n}(z,1)$, and commutes with the conjugation. The claim follows at once.

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{atl:prop:imaginary}
In $\mathbb{O}(\gamma)$ and in its para-Hurwitz algebra, a purely imaginary $z$ satisfies $z\diamond z=-\mathrm{n}(z)$. Hence on the hyperplane $\operatorname{Re} z_0=0$ the first iterate $z_1=c-\mathrm{n}(z_0)$ depends only on $\mathrm{n}(z_0)$, and the filled Julia set meets that hyperplane in a union of level sets of the norm: balls for the compact algebras, regions between hyperboloids for the split ones. In the Okubo algebra the identity fails.
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
If $\operatorname{Re} z=0$ then $\bar z=-z$ and $z^2=-z\bar z=-\mathrm{n}(z)$; the para-Hurwitz square is $\overline{z^2}=-\mathrm{n}(z)$ as well.

<!-- proposition-9: body_md -->
\label{atl:prop:span}
In $\mathbb{O}(\gamma)$ and in the para-Hurwitz algebras, every orbit of $z\mapsto z\diamond z+c$ lies in the three-dimensional space spanned by $1$, $\operatorname{Im} z_0$ and $\operatorname{Im} c$; every orbit of the Mandelbrot map with seed $0$ lies in the plane of $1$ and $c$. In the symmetric composition algebras the Mandelbrot orbit with seed $0$ lies in the plane spanned by $c$ and $c\diamond c$.
<!-- end proposition-9 -->

The first two claims follow from $z^2=2\operatorname{Re}(z)z-\mathrm{n}(z)$ by induction. For the third, \eqref{atl:eq:symmetric} gives $c*(c*c)=(c*c)*c=\mathrm{n}(c)c$ and $(c*c)*(c*c)=\mathrm{n}(c,c*c)\,c-\mathrm{n}(c)\,c*c$, where $\mathrm{n}(x,y)=\mathrm{n}(x+y)-\mathrm{n}(x)-\mathrm{n}(y)$ is the polar form; so the span of $c$ and $c*c$ is invariant.

<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{atl:prop:okubocubic}
In the para-octonions $\mathrm{n}(c,c\bullet c)=2c_0(c_0^2-3|\operatorname{Im} c|^2)$ depends only on $c_0$ and $|\operatorname{Im} c|$. In the Petersson algebra of angle $\theta$, for $c=c_0+ql$ with $c_0\in\mathbb{R}$ and $q\in\mathbb{H}$,
\begin{equation}\label{atl:eq:okubocubic}
c*c=c_0^2-\bar q\,u_\theta^2\,q-2\cos\theta\;c_0\,q\,l ,\qquad \mathrm{n}(c,c*c)=2c_0\big(c_0^2-(\cos2\theta+2\cos\theta)\,|q|^2\big),
\end{equation}
which depends only on $c_0$ and $|q|$; but for general $c$ the Okubo cubic form is not a function of $c_0$ and $|\operatorname{Im} c|$. Neither $q\,u_\theta^2\,q$ nor $\bar q\,\bar u_\theta^{\,2}\,q$ may replace the middle term of \eqref{atl:eq:okubocubic}.
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
For the para-octonions $c\bullet c=\overline{c^2}=2c_0\bar c-\mathrm{n}(c)$, and $\mathrm{n}(c,\bar c)=2(c_0^2-|\operatorname{Im} c|^2)$ gives the formula. For $c=c_0+ql$ one has $\tau(\bar c)=c_0-(uq)l$ and $\tau^{-1}(\bar c)=c_0-(\bar uq)l$; the product \eqref{atl:eq:cd} with $\gamma_3=-1$ gives $c_0^2-\overline{(\bar uq)}(uq)-c_0(\bar u+u)\,q\,l$, where the first factor of the middle term is the \emph{conjugate} of $\bar uq$; since $\overline{\bar u q}=\bar q u$ this is \eqref{atl:eq:okubocubic}. The polar form with $c$ uses $\operatorname{Re}(\bar q u^2 q)=\operatorname{Re}(u^2)|q|^2$. The last claim follows from the fact that the orbits of $PSU(3)$, cut out by $\mathrm{n}(c)$ and $K$, are smaller than those of $G_2$, cut out by $c_0$ and $|\operatorname{Im} c|$; it is visible in the plates F03 and F11.

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
\label{atl:rem:minus}
In every algebra $(-z)\diamond(-z)=z\diamond z$, so that every even map (the quadratic map, the bilateral and the sandwich maps, $z^p+c$ with $p$ even) has the symmetry $z\mapsto-z$, and its filled Julia set is centrally symmetric, with equal escape times. In a section through the origin the composition of $-\mathrm{id}$ with the half-turn about an axis is the mirror in the plane orthogonal to that axis: mirrors and half-turns come in pairs, and many of the finite symmetries of the tables are of this kind. Shapes with a single tip can appear only in sections that do not pass through the origin. The same conclusion holds for an odd map without additive constant, such as $c\sin z$, since $f(-z)=-f(z)$ maps the orbit of $z$ to the opposite of the orbit of $z$; and the logistic map $c\diamond(z-z\diamond z)$ is even about its critical point $\tfrac12$, since $z-z\diamond z=\tfrac14-w\diamond w$ for $z=\tfrac12+w$, so that its filled set is centrally symmetric about $\tfrac12$. The cubic map $(z\diamond z)\diamond z+c$ has none of these symmetries, and neither have the Mandelbrot sets, whose variable is the constant.

\label{atl:prop:symmetric}
In a symmetric composition algebra the sandwich map satisfies $(z*a)*z+c=\mathrm{n}(z)\,a+c$ and the cubic map $(z*z)*z+c=\mathrm{n}(z)\,z+c$. The norms $t_n=\mathrm{n}(z_n)$ of a sandwich orbit obey the real quadratic recursion

\end{equation}

<!-- proposition-13: body_md -->
\label{atl:prop:kaplansky}
In the para-octonions and in the Okubo algebra $(1*x)*(y*1)=xy$, the octonionic product. \label{atl:rem:dual}

<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
\label{atl:rem:mappings}
The four-dimensional mappings of the atlas (Part~M) are the transformations of HyperFractal Studio, which act on the first four coordinates $1,i,j,k$ of the eight-dimensional sample and leave $l,il,jl,kl$ untouched. A mapping that depends on the coordinate $k$ (``$W$'') is therefore the identity on sections with $x_3=0$; this explains why some mappings are invisible in some quarters. Since the mappings act on $1,i,j,k$ only, they also break, artificially, the symmetries of the root planes $V_2=\langle l,il\rangle$ and $V_3=\langle jl,kl\rangle$ of the Okubo algebra: the plates of Part~M do not respect the algebra, and they are not measured.
