<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{DEF:Hypersurface Gamma-elliptique}
    Let $(E,\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>)$ be a real Euclidean vector space and $\Gamma \subset E$ a non-empty open cone with strictly convex closure. We say that $H \subset E$ is a \textit{smooth $\Gamma$-elliptic convex hypersurface} of $E$ if
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $H$ is a non-empty connected closed smooth hypersurface of $E$.
        \item For all $A \in H$, the unit normal vector $\mathrm{N}_H(A)$ at $A$ lies in $\Gamma^\lor$.
        \item For all $A \in H$, the second fundamental form $\2_H(A)(\hat{A},\hat{A}) = -\left<d\mathrm{N}_H(A)\hat{A},\hat{A}\right>$ is non-negative on $T_AH$.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Notice that the second point is equivalent to saying that if $\varphi : E \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a linear form whose kernel is $T_AH$, then all the $\varphi(P)$ for $P \in \overline{\Gamma}\backslash\{0\}$ are positive (up to replacing $\varphi$ by $-\varphi$). In particular, this condition doesn't depend on the choice of the scalar product. Similarly, the third point is equivalent to saying that when $B \in H$ tends toward $A$, $\varphi(B) \geqslant \mathrm{o}(\|B - A\|^2)$, which is also independent from the scalar product.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
If $\mathrm{N}$ is a unit normal vector of $H$ at some point $A$, so is $-\mathrm{N}$. Since $\Gamma$ is open and non-empty, $\Gamma^\lor$ has a strictly convex closure so $\mathrm{N}$ and $-\mathrm{N}$ can't both lie in this cone. Therefore, the first point in Definition \ref{DEF:Hypersurface Gamma-elliptique} ensures a way to choose canonically the normal unit vector. In particular, the second fundamental form is also well-defined globally and $H$ must be orientable.
<!-- end remark-2 -->

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{LEM:TFI construction F_P}
    Let $A \in E$, $t \in \mathbb{R}$ and $P \in V$ such that $A - tP \in H$ and $\left<\mathrm{N}_H(A - tP),P\right> > 0$. There is a unique continuous germ of functions
    $$
    F_{A,t,P} : U_1 \times U_2 \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}
    $$
    with $U_1$ an open neighbourhood of $A$ in $E$ and $U_2$ an open neighbourhood of $P$ in $V$ such that $F_{A,t,P}(A,P) = t$ and for all $(B,Q) \in U_1 \times U_2$, $B - F_{A,t,P}(B,Q)Q \in H$. Moreover, $F_{A,t,P}$ is smooth and its gradient with respect to $B \in U_1$ is given at the origin by
    $$
    \nabla_BF_{A,t,P}(A,P) = \frac{\mathrm{N}_H(A - tP)}{\left<\mathrm{N}_H(A - tP),P\right>}.
    $$
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
It simply follows from the implicit function theorem. Since $H$ is a smooth hypersurface, there is a smooth function $G$ defined in an open neighbourhood $\tilde{U}$ of $A - tP$ in $E$ with real values such that $\nabla G(A - tP) \neq 0$ and $\tilde{U} \cap H = \{B \in \tilde{U} \mid G(B) = 0\}$. Moreover, up to multiplying $G$ by a non-zero constant, we may assume that $\nabla G(A - tP) = \mathrm{N}_H(A - tP)$ is the unit normal vector in $\Gamma^\lor$.

By assumption, $\left<\nabla G(A - tP),P\right> = \left<\mathrm{N}_H(A - tP),P\right> > 0$. Let $U_1 = \tilde{U} + tP$, which is an open neighbourhood of $A \in E$ and let the germ of smooth functions,
$$
\Psi : 
\left\{
\begin{array}{ccc}
    \displaystyle (E,A) \times (E,P) \times (\mathbb{R},t) & \longrightarrow & \displaystyle (\mathbb{R},0)\\
    \displaystyle (B,Q,s) & \longmapsto & \displaystyle G(B - sQ)
\end{array}
\right.
.
$$
We have $\Psi(A,P,t) = G(A - tP) = 0$ and $\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial s}(A,P,t) = \left<\nabla G(A - tP),-P\right> < 0$. The desired result follows directly from the implicit function theorem applied to $\Psi$. The gradient with respect to the first variable is given by
$$
\nabla_BF_{A,t,P}(A,P) = -\frac{\nabla_B\Psi(A,P,t)}{\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial s}(A,P,t)} = \frac{\mathrm{N}_H(A - tP)}{\left<\mathrm{N}_H(A - tP),P\right>}.
$$
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{LEM:Unicité F_I}
    If $r > 0$ and $F_I,\tilde{F}_I : B(A_0,r) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ are continuous functions with $F_I(A_0) = \tilde{F}_I(A_0) = 0$ and they both satisfy \eqref{EQ:Définition F_I}, then $F_I = \tilde{F}_I$. Moreover, $F_I$ is smooth and for all $A \in B(A_0,r)$,
    $$
    \nabla F_I(A) = \frac{\mathrm{N}_H(A - F_I(A)I)}{\left<\mathrm{N}_H(A - F_I(A)I),I\right>}
    $$
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $A \in B(0,r)$. For all $B \in B(A_0,r)$ close enough to $A$, $B - F_I(B)I \in H$ and by continuity of $F_I$ at $A$ and Lemma \ref{LEM:TFI construction F_P}, we must have $F_I(B) = F_{A,I,F_I(A)}(B,I)$. In particular, $F_I$ is smooth near $A$ and
$$
\nabla F_I(A) = \frac{\mathrm{N}_H(A - F_I(A)I)}{\left<\mathrm{N}_H(A - F_I(A)I),I\right>}.
$$

Symmetrically, $\tilde{F}_I$ is smooth near $A$ and
$$
\nabla\tilde{F}_I(A) = \frac{\mathrm{N}_H(A - \tilde{F}_I(A)I)}{\left<\mathrm{N}_H(A - \tilde{F}_I(A)I),I\right>}.
$$
This shows that both $F_I$ and $\tilde{F}_I$ satisfy the same first order differential equation. Since moreover $F_I(A_0) = \tilde{F}_I(A_0) = 0$ and $B(A_0,r)$ is connected, we must have $\tilde{F}_I = F_I$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{PRO:F_I bien définie}
    We have $r_{\sup} = +\infty$ and $F_I$ is defined on the whole $E$. Moreover, for all $(A,t)$ such that $A - tI \in H$, we have $t = F_I(A)$. In other words, each affine line of direction $I$ crosses $H$ exactly once.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
First of all, by Lemma \ref{LEM:TFI construction F_P} applied at $A_0$, we must have $r_{\sup} > 0$ with $F_I = F_{A_0,I,0}(\cdot,I)$ near $A_0$. Assume from now on that $r_{\sup} < +\infty$.

By the uniqueness result of Lemma \ref{LEM:Unicité F_I}, $F_I$ must in particular be defined on the whole $B(A_0,r_{\sup})$. By Lemma \ref{LEM:Unicité F_I} and \eqref{EQ:c(I)}, for all $A \in B(A_0,r_{\sup})$,
$$
\left\|\nabla F_I(A)\right\| = \left\|\frac{\mathrm{N}_H(A - F_I(A)I)}{\left<\mathrm{N}_H(A - F_I(A)I),I\right>}\right\| \leqslant \frac{\left\|\mathrm{N}_H(A - F_I(A)I)\right\|}{c(I)} = \frac{1}{c(I)}.
$$
This shows that $F_I$ is globally Lipschitz so it extends to a continuous function on $\overline{B}(A_0,r_{\sup})$. Moreover, since $H$ is closed in $E$ by Definition \ref{DEF:Hypersurface Gamma-elliptique}, each $A - F_I(A)I$ is in $H$ even when $A$ is in the sphere $S(A_0,r_{\sup})$.

By applying Lemma \ref{LEM:TFI construction F_P} at each point of $S(A_0,r_{\sup})$ and by gluing the functions we obtain thanks to Lemma \ref{LEM:Unicité F_I}, $F_I$ can be extended in an open neighbourhood $U$ of $\overline{B}(A_0,r_{\sup})$. It is a standard result that $U$ must contain an open ball of centre $A_0$ and radius $r > r_{\sup}$. It contradicts the maximality of $r_{\sup}$ hence $r_{\sup} = +\infty$. As above, $F_I$ is well-defined on the whole $E$.

We have left to show that for all $A$ and $t$ with $A - tI \in H$, $t = F_I(A)$. Let
$$
\tilde{H} = \{A \in H \mid F_I(A) = 0\}.
$$
Clearly, $\tilde{H}$ is a closed sub-set of $H$ and it is non-empty since $A_0 \in \tilde{H}$. By Lemma \ref{LEM:TFI construction F_P}, $\tilde{H}$ is also open in $H$. Recall that by Definition \ref{DEF:Hypersurface Gamma-elliptique}, $H$ is connected hence $\tilde{H} = H$.

Therefore, if $(A,t) \in E \times \mathbb{R}$ is such that $A - tI \in H$, we have $F_I(A - tI) = 0$. By Lemma \ref{LEM:Unicité F_I}, for all $B \in E$, $\left<\nabla F_I(B),I\right> = 1$. We deduce that
$$
F_I(A) = F_I(A - tI) + t = t.
$$
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
A consequence of Proposition \ref{PRO:F_I bien définie} is that $F_I$ is fully characterised by the equality $A - F_I(A)I \in H$ for all $A$. In particular, $F_I$ is independent of the choice of $A_0$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{LEM:F_Q avec Q au bord}
    Let $Q \in \partial\Gamma\backslash\{0\}$ and $A \in V$.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item If there is $t \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $A - tQ \in H$, then

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->

B - F_{A,Q,t}(B,P)P \in H.
$$
By Proposition \ref{PRO:F_I bien définie}, if moreover $P \in \Gamma$, we must have $F_{A,Q,t}(B,P) = F_P(B)$. By continuity of $F_{A,Q,t}(A,\cdot)$ at $Q$,
$$
\lim_{P \rightarrow Q, P \in \Gamma} F_P(A) = t,
$$
hence the first point of the lemma. Moreover, for all $(B,P) \in E \times \partial\Gamma$ close enough to $(A,Q)$, $B - F_{A,Q,t}(B,P)P \in H$ hence the openness of this property.

We then show the contraposition of the second point. Assume that
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:limsup > -infty}
    \limsup_{P \rightarrow Q, P \in \Gamma} F_P(A) > -\infty.
\end{equation}
Let $A_0 \in H$. Since $\mathrm{N}_H(A_0) \in \Gamma^\lor$ and $\left\|\mathrm{N}_H(A_0)\right\| = 1$, there is a constant $\varepsilon > 0$ such that for all $P \in \Gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne inférieure ps}
    \left<\mathrm{N}_H(A_0),P\right> \geqslant \varepsilon\left\|P\right\|.
\end{equation}
This $\varepsilon$ is the analogue of $c(I)$ for $\mathrm{N}_H(A_0)$. By concavity of $F_P$ (Proposition \ref{PRO:F_I concave}), we have
\begin{align*}
    F_P(A) & \geqslant \left<\nabla F_P(A_0),A\right> + F_P(A_0)\\
    & = \frac{\left<\mathrm{N}_H(A_0),A\right>}{\left<\mathrm{N}_H(A_0),P\right>} \textrm{ by Lemma \ref{LEM:Unicité F_I}},\\
    & \geqslant -\frac{\left|\left<\mathrm{N}_H(A_0),A\right>\right|}{\varepsilon\left\|P\right\|} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Borne inférieure ps}}.
\end{align*}
We deduce that
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:limsup < +infty}
    \limsup_{P \rightarrow Q, P \in \Gamma} F_P(A) < +\infty,
\end{equation}
since $Q \neq 0$.

By \eqref{EQ:limsup > -infty} and \eqref{EQ:limsup < +infty}, we can construct a converging sequence $P_m \rightarrow Q$ such that $(F_{P_m}(A))$ converges in $\mathbb{R}$. Let $t$ be the limit. By closedness of $H$, $A - tQ \in H$, which proves the second point of the lemma.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{PRO:Caractérisation Omega_H}
    For all $A \in E$, $A \in \Omega_H$ if and only if for all $Q \in \partial\Gamma\backslash\{0\}$, the point 1 in Lemma \ref{LEM:F_Q avec Q au bord} holds.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
If for some $Q \in \partial\Gamma\backslash\{0\}$, the point 2 holds, then by this lemma, $F_P(A) \rightarrow -\infty$ when $P \rightarrow Q$. In particular, $A \in H^-$ so all the $F_P(A)$ are non-positive thus for all $P \in \Gamma$, $A - F_P(A)P \in (A + \Gamma) \cap H$ and
$$
\left\|A - F_P(A)P\right\| \geqslant -F_P(A)\left\|P\right\| - \left\|A\right\| \underset{P \rightarrow Q}{\longrightarrow} +\infty.
$$
We deduce that $A \notin \Omega_H$.

Else, there is a constant $R > 0$ such that for all $P \in \Gamma$, if $x + P \in H$, then $\left\|x + P\right\| \leqslant R$. In this case, for all $P$,
$$
-F_P(A) \leqslant \frac{\left\|F_P(A)P\right\|}{\left\|P\right\|} \leqslant \frac{\left\|A - F_P(A)P\right\| + \left\|A\right\|}{\left\|P\right\|} \leqslant \frac{R + \left\|A\right\|}{\left\|P\right\|}.
$$
It shows that $F_P(A)$ is bounded from above when $P$ approaches any $Q \in \partial\Gamma\backslash\{0\}$. In particular, the point 2 of Lemma \ref{LEM:F_Q avec Q au bord} never holds, which proves the proposition.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{COR:Propriétés Omega_H}
    $\Omega_H$ is open in $E$, convex and it contains $H^+$.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
If $A \in H^+$, then for all $P$, $F_P(x) \geqslant 0$ so $x + P \notin H$. We deduce that $(A + \Gamma) \cap H = \emptyset$ is bounded so $H^+ \subset \Omega_H$.

For the openness, let $A \in \Omega_H$. By Proposition \ref{PRO:Caractérisation Omega_H}, for all $Q \in \partial\Gamma\backslash\{0\}$, the couple $(A,Q)$ verifies the point 1 of Lemma \ref{LEM:F_Q avec Q au bord}. Moreover, this property is open so there is a neighbourhood $U_Q \times W_Q$ of $(A,Q)$ in $E \times \partial\Gamma\backslash\{0\}$ such that each $(B,P) \in U_Q \times W_Q$ also verify the point 1 of Lemma \ref{LEM:F_Q avec Q au bord}.

Let $K = \{Q \in \partial\Gamma \mid \left\|Q\right\| = 1\}$. $K$ is compact and is covered by $(W_Q)_{Q \in K}$ so there is a finite sub-set $J$ of $K$ such that
$$
K \subset \bigcup_{j \in J} W_j.
$$
Let $U = \bigcap_{j \in J} U_j$, which is an open neighbourhood of $A \in E$.

For all $B \in U$ and all $Q \in \partial\Gamma\backslash\{0\}$, there is $j \in J$ such that $\frac{Q}{\left\|Q\right\|} \in W_j$. Since $B \in U_j$, there is a $t \in \mathbb{R}$ such that
$$
B - \frac{t}{\left\|Q\right\|}Q \in H.
$$
It is true for all $Q$ so $B \in \Omega_H$ by Proposition \ref{PRO:Caractérisation Omega_H} hence the openness of $\Omega_H$.

Finally, to see that $\Omega_H$ is convex, let $(A,B) \in \Omega_H^2$ and $Q \in \partial\Gamma\backslash\{0\}$. By Proposition \ref{PRO:Caractérisation Omega_H}, there are real numbers $(s,t)$ such that
$$
A - sQ \in H, \quad B - tQ \in H.
$$
By Corollary \ref{COR:H^+ convexe}, $H^+$ is convex so
$$
\frac{A + B}{2} - \frac{s + t}{2}Q \in H^+.
$$
Since $H^+ \subset \Omega_H$, there is a real number $r$ such that
$$
\frac{A + B}{2} - \left(\frac{s + t}{2} + r\right)Q \in H.
$$
It holds for any $Q$ so by Proposition \ref{PRO:Caractérisation Omega_H}, $\frac{A + B}{2} \in \Omega_H$ hence the convexity of $\Omega_H$.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{PRO:Guan}
    There is $\kappa > 0$ that only depends on $\delta$ and $R$ such that for all $A \in D \cap H^-$,
    $$
    -\left<\nabla F(A),A - A_0\right> \geqslant \kappa\left<\nabla F(A),I\right>, \quad \textrm{or} \quad \inf_{P \in \Gamma, \left\|P\right\| = 1} \left<\nabla F(A),P\right> \geqslant \kappa\left<\nabla F(A),I\right>.

    $$

    $$

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Assume without loss of generality that
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Contradiction inégalité 1}
    -\left<\nabla F(A),A - A_0\right> \leqslant \frac{\delta}{2}\left<\nabla F(A),I\right>,
\end{equation}
or else the first inequality holds with $\kappa = \frac{\delta}{2}$.

The assumption that $A \in H^-$ (and $F$ is $\Gamma$-elliptic) implies that $F(A) \leqslant 0$ so
\begin{align}
    F\!\left(A_0 - \frac{\delta}{2}v\right) & \leqslant F(A) + \left<\nabla F(A),A_0 - \frac{\delta}{2}I - A\right> \textrm{ by concavity of $F$}, \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant -\left<\nabla F(A),A - A_0\right> - \frac{\delta}{2}\left<\nabla F(A),I\right> \textrm{ because } F(A) \leqslant 0, \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant 0 \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Contradiction inégalité 1}}. \label{EQ:A_0 - delta/2 I dans H^-}
\end{align}
We deduce that $A_0 - \frac{\delta}{2}I \in H^-$. In particular, $A_0 - \delta I \in H^-\backslash H$.

Let $P \in \Gamma$ with $\left\|P\right\| = 1$ and let
$$
B = A_0 - \delta I - F_P(A_0 - \delta I)P \in H.
$$
Since $A_0 - \delta I \in H^-\backslash H$, we must have $F_P(A_0 - \delta I) < 0$.

By concavity of $F$ and because $A \in H^-$,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Inégalité gradient}
    0 = F(B) \leqslant F(A) + \left<\nabla F(A),B - A\right> \leqslant \left<\nabla F(A),B - A\right>.
\end{equation}

Therefore,
\begin{align*}
    \left<\nabla F(A),P\right> & = \left<\nabla F(A),\frac{B - A_0 + \delta I}{-F_P(A_0 - \delta I)}\right>\\
    & \geqslant \left<\nabla F(A),\frac{A - A_0 + \delta I}{-F_P(A_0 - \delta I)}\right> \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Inégalité gradient}},\\
    & \geqslant \left<\nabla F(A),\frac{\frac{\delta}{2}I}{-F_P(A_0 - \delta I)}\right> \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Contradiction inégalité 1}},\\
    & \geqslant \frac{\delta}{2R}\left<\nabla F(A),I\right> \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Définition R}}.
\end{align*}

For the last inequality, choose $P = I$ in the definition of $B$. We have in any case
\begin{align*}
    -\left<\nabla F(A),A - A_0\right> & \geqslant \left<\nabla F(A),A_0 - B\right> \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Inégalité gradient}},\\
    & = \left<\nabla F(A),\delta I + F_I(A_0 - \delta I)I\right>\\
    & \geqslant -R\left<\nabla F(A),I\right> \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Définition delta et R}}.
\end{align*}

<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{DEF:Presque spectrale}
    We say that ${\boldsymbol{H}}$ is \textit{almost spectral} with tuple $\underline{M}$ if it satisfies the following property.
    
    For all $(x_0,A_0) \in {\boldsymbol{H}}$ with $\left\|A_0\right\| \geqslant M_1$, we can write locally ${\boldsymbol{H}}$ around $(x_0,A_0)$ as
    $$
    {\boldsymbol{H}} = \{(x,A) \in \mathrm{End}_S(TX,g) \mid f(\lambda(B(x) + A)) + G(x,A) = 0\},
    $$
    where
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $(\mathbb{R}_+^*)^n \subset \Gamma \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is an $\mathfrak{S}_n$-invariant open cone and
        $$
        f : \Gamma \longrightarrow \mathbb{R},
        $$
        is a smooth $\mathfrak{S}_n$-invariant symmetric $\mathbb{R}_+^n$-elliptic concave function.
        \item $B : (X,x_0) \longrightarrow \mathrm{End}_S(TX,g)$ is a smooth germ of sections such that $\lambda(B(x_0) + A_0) \in \Gamma$.
        \item We have
        $$
        \lim_{t \rightarrow 0^+} f(t\lambda(B(x_0) + A_0)) < f(\lambda(B(x_0) + A_0)) < \lim_{t \rightarrow +\infty} f(t\lambda(B(x_0) + A_0)).
        $$
        \item $G : (\mathrm{End}_S(TX,g),(x_0,A_0)) \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a smooth germ of functions such that $G(x_0,A_0) = -f(\lambda(B(x_0) + A_0))$.
        \item Let $\mathcal{F} = \left<\nabla f(\lambda(B(x_0) + A_0)),\lambda(\mathrm{Id})\right> = \sum_{k = 1}^n \frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_k}(\lambda(B(x_0) + A_0))$. We have the bounds
        \begin{equation}\label{EQ:Hypothèse bornes B}
            \left\|B(x_0)\right\| \leqslant M_2, \qquad \left\|\nabla B(x_0)\right\| \leqslant M_3,
        \end{equation}
        \begin{equation}\label{EQ:Hypothèse nabla G}
            \left\|\nabla_xG(x_0,A_0)\right\| \leqslant M_4\mathcal{F}, \qquad \left\|\nabla_AG(x_0,A_0)\right\| \leqslant \frac{M_5}{\left\|A_0\right\|}\mathcal{F},
        \end{equation}
        \begin{equation}\label{EQ:Hypothèse encadrement nabla_AG}
            -\left(1 - \frac{1}{M_6}\right)\nabla(f \circ \lambda)(B(x_0) + A_0) \leqslant \nabla_AG(x_0,A_0) \leqslant M_7\nabla(f \circ \lambda)(B(x_0) + A_0),
        \end{equation}
        \item For all $(\hat{x},\hat{A}) \in T_{(x_0,A_0)}{\boldsymbol{H}}$,
        \begin{equation}\label{EQ:Hypothèse nabla2G}
            -\nabla^2G(x_0,A_0)((\hat{x},\hat{A}),(\hat{x},\hat{A})) \geqslant -M_8\mathcal{F}\left\|A_0\right\|\left\|\hat{x}\right\|^2 + \frac{M_9}{2}d^2(f \circ \lambda)(B(x_0) + A_0)(\hat{A},\hat{A}).
        \end{equation}
        \item $M_7 + M_9 \leqslant 1$.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
When we can choose $f$ and $B$ globally and independently of the point $(x_0,A_0)$ and when $G = 0$, we recover the standard frame of Caffarelli, Nirenberg and Spruck \cite{CNS}, for which the \textit{a priori} estimates are well-known \cite{Li}\cite{Urbas}\cite{Guan}\cite{Szekelyhidi}. Roughly speaking, Definition \ref{DEF:Presque spectrale} requires that the non-spectral part represented by $G$ is controlled by the spectral part.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{LEM:Conséquences hypothèse}
    If ${\boldsymbol{H}}$ is almost spectral with tuple $\underline{M}$, there is a tuple $\underline{M}' = (M_{10},M_{11},M_{12},M_{13},M_{14},M_{15})$ that only depends on $\underline{M}$ and $n$ such that for all $(x_0,A_0) \in {\boldsymbol{H}}$ with $\left\|A_0\right\| \geqslant M_{10}$ which is diagonal in the basis $\frac{\partial}{\partial x_1}(x_0),\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_n}(x_0)$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \geqslant \ldots \geqslant \lambda_n$ and $\lambda_1 > \lambda_n$,
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $F_{,nn}(x_0,A_0) \geqslant \frac{1}{M_{11}}$,
        \item $\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{,ii}(x_0,A_0)\lambda_i \geqslant -M_{12}$,
        \item For all $k$, $\left|F_{k,}(x_0,A_0)\right| \leqslant M_{13}$,
        \item For all $(\hat{x},\hat{A}) \in T_{(x_0,A_0)}{\boldsymbol{H}}$,
        $$
        -d^2F(x_0,A_0)((\hat{x},\hat{A}),(\hat{x},\hat{A})) \geqslant -M_{14}\left\|A_0\right\| + \frac{1}{\lambda_1 - \lambda_n}\sum_{i,j = 1}^n (F_{,ij}(x_0,A_0) - M_{15}\delta_{ij}F_{,11}(x_0,A_0))\hat{A}_{1i}\hat{A}_{1j}.
        $$
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
By a density argument, we may assume without loss of generality that $B(x_0) + A_0$ has pairwise distinct eigenvalues $\lambda_1 < \ldots < \lambda_n$.

Let $M_{10} = \max\{M_1,2nM_5\}$. In particular, if $\left\|A_0\right\| \geqslant M_{10}$, then $\left\|A_0\right\| \geqslant M_1$ so the properties in Definition \ref{DEF:Presque spectrale} apply. Let $f$, $B$ and $G$ given by this definition. Recall that $\mathcal{F} = \sum_{i = 1}^n \frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_i}(\lambda(B(x_0) + A_0))$ is positive by ellipticity of $f$. Since ${\boldsymbol{H}}$ is locally given defined by the equation $f(\lambda(B(x) + A)) + G(x,A) = 0$, we have, for all $(\hat{x},\hat{A}) \in T_{(x_0,A_0)}\mathrm{End}_S(T_{x_0}X,g)$,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Vecteur normal en fonction de f et G}
    L\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}(x_0,A_0),(\hat{x},\hat{A})\right> = d(f \circ \lambda)(dB(x_0)\hat{x} + \hat{A}) + dG(x_0,A_0)(\hat{x},\hat{A}).
\end{equation}
If moreover, $(\hat{x},\hat{A}) \in T_{(x_0,A_0)}{\boldsymbol{H}}$,
\begin{align}\label{EQ:II en fonction de f et G}
\begin{split}
    L\,\2_{\boldsymbol{H}}(x_0,A_0)((\hat{x},\hat{A}),(\hat{x},\hat{A})) & = -d^2(f \circ \lambda)(x_0,B_0 + A_0)((dB(x_0)\hat{x} + \hat{A}),(dB(x_0)\hat{x} + \hat{A}))\\
    & - d(f \circ \lambda)(x_0,B_0 + A_0)d^2B(x_0)(\hat{x},\hat{x}) - d^2G(x_0,A_0)((\hat{x},\hat{A}),(\hat{x},\hat{A})).
\end{split}
\end{align}
where $L \in \mathbb{R}$ is a normalisation constant. Similarly, by Proposition \ref{PRO:Différentielles F_I}, for all $(\hat{x},\hat{A})$,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Vecteur normal en fonction de F}
    \frac{\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}(x_0,A_0),(\hat{x},\hat{A})\right>}{\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}(x_0,A_0),(0,\mathrm{Id})\right>} = dF(x_0,A_0)(\hat{x},\hat{A}),
\end{equation}
and if $(\hat{x},\hat{A})$ is tangent to ${\boldsymbol{H}}$,
\begin{align}\label{EQ:II en fonction de F}
    \frac{\2_{\boldsymbol{H}}(x_0,A_0)((\hat{x},\hat{A}),(\hat{x},\hat{A}))}{\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}(x_0,A_0),(0,\mathrm{Id})\right>} = -d^2F(x_0,A_0)((\hat{x},\hat{A}),(\hat{x},\hat{A})).
\end{align}

\noindent\textbf{Step 1:} Lower bound on $L$.

From now on, we always evaluate functions at $(x_0,A_0)$ so we omit to write it. Assume without loss of generality that $\left\|A_0\right\| \geqslant M_{10} \geqslant 2nM_5$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Conséquence A_0 grande}
    \frac{M_5}{\left\|A_0\right\|} \leqslant \frac{1}{2n} \leqslant \frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}.
\end{equation}

Applying \eqref{EQ:Vecteur normal en fonction de f et G} with $\hat{x} = 0$ and $\hat{A} = \mathrm{Id}$, we obtain that
\begin{align}
    L\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}},(0,\mathrm{Id})\right> & = \mathcal{F} + d_AG\,\mathrm{Id} \geqslant \mathcal{F} - \left\|\nabla_AG\right\|\left\|\mathrm{Id}\right\| \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant \left(1 - \frac{M_5\sqrt{n}}{\left\|A_0\right\|}\right)\mathcal{F} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Hypothèse nabla G}} \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant \frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Conséquence A_0 grande}}. \label{EQ:L fois PS > F/2}.
\end{align}
By positivity of $\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}$, $\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}},(0,\mathrm{Id})\right> \geqslant \left\|\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}\right\| = 1$. We deduce that $L$ is positive and
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne inférieure L}
    L \geqslant \frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F} > 0.
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Step 2:} Proof of point 3.

We have
\begin{align}
    L\left\|\pi_{T_{x_0}X}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}\right\|^2 & = L\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}},(\pi_{T_{x_0}X}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}},0)\right> \nonumber\\
    & = d(f \circ \lambda)dB\,(\pi_{T_{x_0}X}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}) + d_xG\,(\pi_{T_{x_0}X}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Vecteur normal en fonction de f et G}}, \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant \left\|\nabla(f \circ \lambda)\right\|\left\|\nabla B\right\|\left\|\pi_{T_{x_0}X}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}\right\| + M_4\mathcal{F}\left\|\pi_{T_{x_0}X}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}\right\| \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Hypothèse nabla G}}, \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant (M_3 + M_4)\mathcal{F}\left\|\pi_{T_{x_0}X}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}\right\| \textrm{ by ellipticity of $f$ and \eqref{EQ:Hypothèse bornes B}}, \nonumber\\
    L\left\|\pi_{T_{x_0}X}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}\right\| & \leqslant (M_3 + M_4)\mathcal{F}. \label{EQ:L norme x}
\end{align}

Therefore, by \eqref{EQ:Vecteur normal en fonction de F}, \eqref{EQ:L fois PS > F/2} and \eqref{EQ:L norme x},
$$
\left\|\nabla_xF\right\| = \frac{\left\|\pi_{T_{x_0}X}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}\right\|}{\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}},(0,\mathrm{Id})\right>} \leqslant 2(M_3 + M_4).
$$

\noindent\textbf{Step 3:} Upper bound on $L$.

On the one hand, we have
\begin{align*}
    L\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}},(0,\mathrm{Id})\right> & = \mathcal{F} + d_AG\,\mathrm{Id} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Vecteur normal en fonction de f et G}},\\
    & \leqslant \mathcal{F} + \left\|\nabla_AG\right\|\left\|\mathrm{Id}\right\|\\
    & \leqslant \left(1 + \frac{M_5\sqrt{n}}{\left\|A_0\right\|}\right)\mathcal{F} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Hypothèse nabla G}},\\
    & \leqslant \frac{3}{2}\mathcal{F} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Conséquence A_0 grande}},
\end{align*}
and on the other hand,
\begin{align}
    \left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}},(0,\mathrm{Id})\right> & \geqslant \left\|\pi_{\mathrm{End}_S(T_{x_0}X,g)}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}\right\| \textrm{ by ellipticity}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant 1 - \left\|\pi_{T_{x_0}X}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}\right\| \nonumber\\
    & = 1 - \left\|\nabla_xF\right\|\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}},(0,\mathrm{Id})\right> \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Vecteur normal en fonction de F}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant 1 - 2(M_3 + M_4)\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}},(0,\mathrm{Id})\right>. \label{EQ:Borne inférieure NN contre Id}
\end{align}
Hence,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne supérieure L}
    L \leqslant \frac{3\mathcal{F}}{2\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}},(0,\mathrm{Id})\right>} \leqslant \frac{3(2(M_3 + M_4) + 1)}{2}\mathcal{F}.
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Step 4:} Proof of point 1.

Recall that we work in an orthonormal basis $\frac{\partial}{\partial x_1},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_n}$ where $B(x_0) + A_0$ is diagonal with pairwise distinct eigenvalues. By \eqref{EQ:Dérivée première f o lambda}, in this basis,
$$
\nabla(f \circ \lambda) =
\begin{pmatrix}
    f_1 & 0 & \hdots & 0\\
    0 & f_2 & \hdots & 0\\
    \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
    0 & 0 & \hdots & f_n
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Moreover, it has decreasing eigenvalues,
$$
f_1 \leqslant \cdots \leqslant f_n.
$$

In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne inférieure Fnn}
    \hat{x}_n\nabla(f \circ \lambda)\hat{x}_n = f_n \geqslant \frac{1}{n}\mathcal{F}.
\end{equation}
We deduce that
\begin{align*}
    F_{,nn} & = \hat{x}_n^*\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{End}_S(T_{x_0}X,g)}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}}{\left<\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}},(0,\mathrm{Id})\right>}\hat{x}_n \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Vecteur normal en fonction de F}},\\
    & \geqslant \hat{x}_n^*\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{End}_S(T_{x_0}X,g)}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}}{\left\|\pi_{\mathrm{End}_S(T_{x_0}X,g)}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}}\right\|\left\|\mathrm{Id}\right\|}\hat{x}_n\\
    & \geqslant \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\hat{x}_n^*(\pi_{\mathrm{End}_S(T_{x_0}X,g)}\mathrm{N}_{\boldsymbol{H}})\hat{x}_n\\
    & = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}L}(\hat{x}_n^*\nabla(f \circ \lambda)\hat{x}_n + \hat{x}_n^*\nabla_AG\hat{x}_n) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Vecteur normal en fonction de f et G}},\\
    & \geqslant \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}L}\left(\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{F} - \frac{M_5}{\left\|A\right\|_0}\mathcal{F}\right) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Borne inférieure Fnn} and \eqref{EQ:Hypothèse nabla G}},\\
    & \geqslant \frac{1}{2n^{3/2}L}\mathcal{F} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Conséquence A_0 grande}},\\
    & \geqslant \frac{1}{3n^{3/2}(2(M_3 + M_4) + 1)} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Borne supérieure L}}.
\end{align*}

\textbf{Step 5:} Proof of point 2.

Let the real function
$$
a : 
\left\{
\begin{array}{ccc}
    \displaystyle \mathbb{R}_+^* & \longrightarrow & \displaystyle \mathbb{R}_+\\
    \displaystyle t & \longmapsto & \displaystyle f(t\lambda(B(x_0) + A_0))

By assumption on $f$, $\lim_{t \rightarrow 0} a(t) < a(1) < \lim_{t \rightarrow +\infty}$ and $a$ is concave. In particular, $a$ must be non-decreasing near $1$ so $a'(1) \geqslant 0$ \textit{i.e.}

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
The proof is the same as the one of Tosatti and Weinkove \cite[Lemma 3.3]{Tosatti_Weinkove} except that we do not have a lower bound on the Laplacian of $u$ so we must introduce another non-positive linear elliptic operator that depends on ${\boldsymbol{H}}_0$,
$$
\square u(x) = \left<\nabla^2u(x),\mathrm{N}_0(x)\right>.
$$
Since $H_{0,x} \subset H_x^-$, we also have $H_x \subset H_{0,x}^+$ hence for all $A \in H_x$, $\left<\mathrm{N}_0(x),A\right> \geqslant a(x)$.

In particular, for $A = \nabla^2u(x)$, we obtain that
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne inférieure square phi}
    \square u \geqslant a(x) \geqslant -m,
\end{equation}
where $m = -\min_{x \in X} a(x)$. Notice that inequality \eqref{EQ:Borne inférieure square phi} proves that $m$ must be non-negative.

From now on, we only use \eqref{EQ:Borne inférieure square phi} to obtain a bound on $\left\|u\right\|_{L^1}$. In particular, we don't require any more that $u$ solves \eqref{EQ:Equation}. Let $G : X^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$ be the Green function associated to $-\square$. Recall that for all $y$,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Moyenne G = 0}
    \int_X G(x,y) \, d\mu_g(x) = 0,
\end{equation}
due to the fact that $\ker(\square) = \mathbb{R} 1$.

Unlike the Laplacian operator, $\square$ needs not be self-adjoint so we introduce a generator $k$ of $\ker(\square^*)$. In particular, this function satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:square*k = 0}
    \int_X k(x)\square u(x) \, d\mu_g(x) = \int_X \square^*k(x)u(x) \, d\mu_g(x) = 0.
\end{equation}
$\square$ depends only on $X$ and ${\boldsymbol{H}}_0$, thus $G$ and $k$ depend only on $X$, $g$ and ${\boldsymbol{H}}_0$.

Then, assume without loss of generality that $k$ takes at least one positive value, say at a point $x_0$. We show that $k$ is positive. First of all, if $k$ takes a negative value, say at a point $x_1 \in X$, we can easily build a positive function $f$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:f orthogonale à k}
    \int_X f(x)k(x) \, d\mu_g(x) = 0.
\end{equation}
For this, start from $f = 1$ and add a bump near $x_0$ if the integral of $k$ is negative, or a bump near $x_1$ else. With a well-adjusted bump, we obtain \eqref{EQ:f orthogonale à k}. This integral means that $f \in \ker(\square^*)^\bot = \mathrm{im}(\square)$ so there is a function $g$ such that $\square g = f > 0$. It contradicts the existence of a maximum for $g$.

It shows that $k \geqslant 0$ (and we still have $k(x_0) > 0$). If $k$ vanishes on $X$, there is a positive radius $r$ such that $k$ is positive on the geodesic ball $B$ of centre $x_0$ and radius $r$ but vanishes at a point of $\partial B$. Since $k|_B$ is a solution to a uniform elliptic equation, by the Harnack inequality, there is a constant $C_1$ that only depends on $B$, $X$ and $\square^*$ such that
$$
\sup_B k \leqslant C_1\inf_B k.
$$
However, $\inf_B k = 0$, which contradicts the fact that $k(x_0) > 0$. Thus, $k > 0$ on $X$.

Let $x_1 \in X$ be a point where $u$ reaches its maximum $0$. We have
\begin{align*}
    0 & = u(x_1)\\
    & = \frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_g(X)}\int_X u(x) d\mu_g(x) - \int_X G(x,x_1)\square u(x) \, d\mu_g(x)\\
    & \leqslant -\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_g(X)}\left\|u\right\|_{L^1} - \int_X G(x,x_1)\left(m + \square u(x)\right) \, d\mu_g(x) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Moyenne G = 0}},\\
    & \leqslant -\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_g(X)}\left\|u\right\|_{L^1} + \int_X \frac{\max_{X^2} (-G)}{\min_X k}k(x)\left(m + \square u(x)\right) \, d\mu_g(x) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Borne inférieure square phi}},\\
    & = -\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_g(X)}\left\|u\right\|_{L^1} + m\frac{\max_{X^2} (-G)}{\min k}\left\|k\right\|_{L^1} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:square*k = 0}}.
\end{align*}
We deduce that
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne norme L1}
    \left\|u\right\|_{L^1} \leqslant C_2,
\end{equation}
with $C_2$ that depends only on $X$, $g$ and ${\boldsymbol{H}}_0$.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
The proof relies on the Alexandroff--Bakelman--Pucci maximum principle as used by Blocki \cite{Blocki} in the case of the complex Monge--Ampère equation and by Székeleyhidi \cite{Szekelyhidi} for more general complex Hessian equations.

Then, let $x_0 \in X$ be a point where $u$ reaches its minimum $\min_X u = -\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}$. When $r > 0$, we denote by $B_r$ the Euclidean ball of centre $0$ and radius $r$ in $T_{x_0}X$. Let $r > 0$ small enough so
$$
\exp_{g,x_0} : B_r \longrightarrow X
$$
is a diffeomorphism on its image. Up to reducing $r$, we may assume that there are positive constants $C_1$, $C_2$ and $C_3$ such that for all $x \in B_r$ and $x \in \exp_{g,x_0}(B_r)$,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne dexp}
    \left\|d\exp_{g,x_0}(x)\right\| \leqslant C_1,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne dexp-1}
    \left\|d\exp_{g,x_0}^{-1}(x)\right\| \leqslant C_2,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne d^2exp-1}
    \left\|d^2\exp_{g,x_0}^{-1}(x)\right\| \leqslant C_3.
\end{equation}
We used the operator norms. By compactness of $X$, we can choose the triple $(r,C_1,C_2,C_3)$ uniformly with respect to $x_0$. In other words, they only depend on $X$ and $g$.

Let the real function of $B_r$ defined by
$$
\psi : x \longmapsto u(\exp_{g,x_0}^{-1}(x)) + \epsilon\left\|x\right\|^2.
$$
where
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Définition epsilon}
    \epsilon = \frac{\delta}{2C_2^2 + \frac{C_3}{2} + 2rC_3}.
\end{equation}

We introduce
$$
P = \left\{a \in B_r \mid \left\|d\psi(a)\right\| < \frac{\epsilon}{2} \textrm{ and } \forall b \in B_r, \psi(b) \geqslant \psi(a) + d\psi(a)(b - a)\right\},
$$
By \cite[Proposition 11]{Szekelyhidi}, since $u$ reaches its minimum at $x_0$, there is a constant $c_4$ that only depends on $n$, $r$, and $\epsilon$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne inférieure intégrale sur P}
    \int_P \det(d^2\psi(a)) \, da \geqslant c_4
\end{equation}

Let $(x,\hat{x}) \in T\exp_{g,x_0}(B_r)$, $a = \exp_{g,x_0}^{-1}(x) \in B_r$ and $\hat{a} = d\exp_{g,x_0}^{-1}(x)\hat{x}$. We can compute that
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Hessienne phi}
    d^2u(x)(\hat{x},\hat{x}) = d^2\psi(a)(\hat{a},\hat{a}) + d\psi(a)d^2\exp_{g,x_0}^{-1}(x)(\hat{x},\hat{x}) - 2\epsilon\left\|\hat{a}\right\|^2 - 2\epsilon\left<a,d^2\exp_{g,x_0}^{-1}(x)\hat{x}\right>.
\end{equation}
If $a \in P$, then
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Positivité d^2psi et borne dpsi}
    d^2\psi(a)(\hat{a},\hat{a}) \geqslant 0, \qquad \left\|d\psi(a)\right\| < \frac{\epsilon}{2},
\end{equation}
We deduce from \eqref{EQ:Hessienne phi} and \eqref{EQ:Positivité d^2psi et borne dpsi} that
\begin{align*}
    d^2u(x)(\hat{x},\hat{x}) & > -\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left\|d^2\exp_{g,x_0}^{-1}(x)(\hat{x},\hat{x})\right\| - 2\epsilon\left\|d\exp_{g,x_0}^{-1}(x)\hat{x}\right\|^2 - 2\epsilon\left\|a\right\|\left\|d^2\exp_{g,p_0}^{-1}(x)(\hat{x},\hat{x})\right\|\\
    & \geqslant -\frac{\epsilon}{2}C_3\left\|\hat{x}\right\|^2 - 2\epsilon C_2^2\left\|\hat{x}\right\|^2 - 2\epsilon rC_3\left\|\hat{x}\right\|^2 \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Borne dexp-1} and \eqref{EQ:Borne d^2exp-1}},\\
    & = -\delta\left\|\hat{x}\right\|^2 \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Définition epsilon}}.
\end{align*}

We deduce that $\nabla^2u(x) > -\delta\mathrm{Id}$ when $x \in \exp_{g,x_0}(P)$. Since $u$ is a solution of \eqref{EQ:Equation}, by \eqref{EQ:Définition delta et R},
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne Hessienne dans P}
    \left\|\nabla^2u(x)\right\| \leqslant R,
\end{equation}
still when $x \in \exp_{g,x_0}(P)$.

Therefore,
\begin{align}
    d^2\psi(a)(\hat{a},\hat{a}) & = d^2u(x)(\hat{x},\hat{x}) - d\psi(a)d^2\exp_{g,x_0}^{-1}(x)(\hat{x},\hat{x}) + 2\epsilon\left\|\hat{a}\right\|^2 + 2\epsilon\left<a,d^2\exp_{g,x_0}^{-1}(x)\hat{x}\right> \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Hessienne phi}}, \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant \left\|\nabla^2u(x)\right\|\left\|\hat{x}\right\|^2 + \frac{\epsilon}{2}C_3\left\|\hat{x}\right\|^2 + 2\epsilon\left\|\hat{a}\right\|^2 + 2\epsilon rC_3\left\|\hat{x}\right\|^2 \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Borne dexp-1}, \eqref{EQ:Borne d^2exp-1} and \eqref{EQ:Positivité d^2psi et borne dpsi}}, \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant C_1^2R\hat{a}^2 + \frac{\epsilon}{2}C_1^2C_3\left\|\hat{a}\right\|^2 + 2\epsilon\left\|\hat{a}\right\|^2 + 2\epsilon rC_1^2C_3\left\|\hat{a}\right\|^2 \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Borne dexp} and \eqref{EQ:Borne Hessienne dans P}}, \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant C_5\hat{a}^2, \label{EQ:Borne d^2psi dans P}
\end{align}
with $C_5 = C_1^2R + \frac{\epsilon}{2}C_1^2C_3 + 2\epsilon + 2\epsilon rC_1^2C_3$ that only depends on $X$, $g$, $\delta$ and $R$.

Finally, notice that for all $a \in P$,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:psi(a) bornée par psi(0)}
    -\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} = \psi(0) \geqslant \psi(a) - \frac{\epsilon r}{2},
\end{equation}
hence
\begin{align*}
    \left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} & \leqslant \frac{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}}{c_4}\int_P \det(d^2\psi(a)) \, da \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Borne inférieure intégrale sur P}},\\
    & \leqslant \frac{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}}{c_4}\int_P C_5^n \, da \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Borne d^2psi dans P}},\\
    & \leqslant \frac{C_5^n\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}}{c_4}\int_P \frac{-\psi(a) + \frac{\epsilon r}{2}}{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}} \, dx \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:psi(a) bornée par psi(0)}},\\
    & \leqslant \frac{C_5^nC_2^n}{c_4}\int_{\exp_{g,x_0}(P)} -u(x) - \epsilon\left\|\exp^{-1}(x)\right\|^2 + \frac{\epsilon r}{2} \, d\mu_g(x) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Borne dexp-1}},\\
    & \leqslant \frac{C_5^nC_2^n}{c_4}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{L^1} + \frac{\epsilon r}{2}\mathrm{Vol}_g(X)\right).
\end{align*}

The proof is based on the introduction of an auxiliary function and the fact that this function must have a maximum by compactness of $X$. We prove it in five steps.\\

\noindent\textbf{Step 1:} Introducing the function $f$ and its maximum.

Let
$$
f : x \longmapsto \left\|\nabla u(x)\right\| + \frac{1}{2}(u(x) + A)^2,
$$
where
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Estimée C1 définition A}
    A = \left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + \sqrt{M_{11}(nB_1 + 1)}
\end{equation}

Usually, when such an auxiliary function is introduced to show a $\mathcal{C}^1$-estimate, the function in $u$ (here, $\frac{1}{2}(u(x) + A)^2$) is a decreasing function. See for example \cite{Blocki}\cite{Zhang}. Here, we make it increasing so the gradient of $u$ will be an eigenvector of its Hessian associated with a negative eigenvalue with large absolute value. We then use the first point of Lemma \ref{LEM:Conséquences hypothèse} which, roughly speaking, says that we have uniform ellipticity of the equation in the direction of the smallest eigenvalue of the Hessian.

Notice that $A \geqslant \left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + 1$ so for all $x$, $1 \leqslant u(x) + A \leqslant A$.

Let $x_0$ be a point where $f$ reaches its maximum. We have
\begin{align}
    \left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} & = \max_{x \in X} f(x) - \frac{1}{2}(u(x) + A)^2 \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant f(x_0) \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant \left\|\nabla u(x_0)\right\| + \frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + \sqrt{M_{11}(nB_1 + 1)}\right)^2. \label{EQ:Norme C1 en fonction de la valeur en x_0}
\end{align}
We deduce that it is enough to bound $\frac{\left\|\nabla u(x_0)\right\|}{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + 1}$.\\

\noindent\textbf{Step 2:} Computing the first differential of $f$ and showing that the gradient is an eigenvector of the Hessian matrix.

If $\nabla u(x_0) = 0$, we are finished. Else, $f$ is smooth in an open neighbourhood of $x_0$ and locally around this point
$$
f(x) = \left(\sum_{k = 1}^n u_k(x)^2\right)^{1/2} + \frac{1}{2}(u(x) + A)^2,
$$
thus for all $i$,
\begin{align}
    f_i(x) & = \left(\sum_{k = 1}^n u_k(x)^2\right)^{-1/2}\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{ki}(x)u_k(x) + (u(x) + A)u_i(x) \label{EQ:f_i estimées C1}\\
    & = \frac{1}{\left\|\nabla u(x_0)\right\|}\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{ki}(x_0)u_k(x_0) + (u(x) + A)u_i(x_0) \textrm{ at } x = x_0, \nonumber\\
    & = 0 \textrm{ because $f$ is maximal at } x_0. \label{EQ:f_i = 0 estimées C1}
\end{align}

Let $\lambda_1 \geqslant \cdots \geqslant \lambda_n$ be the eigenvalues of $\nabla^2u(x_0)$. Equality \eqref{EQ:f_i = 0 estimées C1} exactly tells that $\nabla u(x_0)$ is an eigenvector of $\nabla^2u(x_0)$. We may choose the local coordinates so there is an index $r$ such that
$$
u_i(x_0) = \delta_{ir}\left\|\nabla u(x_0)\right\|, \qquad u_{ij}(x_0) = \delta_{ij}\lambda_i.
$$
Moreover, \eqref{EQ:f_i = 0 estimées C1} at $i = r$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:lambda_r}
    \lambda_r = -(u(x) + A)\left\|\nabla u(x_0)\right\| < 0.
\end{equation}

If $\left\|\nabla u(x_0)\right\| \leqslant M_{10}$, we are finished. Else, $\lambda_r \leqslant -M_{10}$ so $\left\|\nabla^2u(x_0)\right\| \geqslant M_{10}$ and Lemma \ref{LEM:Conséquences hypothèse} applies at $(x_0,\nabla^2u(x_0))$.

\noindent\textbf{Step 3:} Computing the second differential of $f$.

From now on, when we evaluate an expression at $x = x_0$, we omit to write it to lighten the expressions. From \eqref{EQ:f_i estimées C1}, we compute that

\begin{align*}
    f_{ij} & = \left(\sum_{k = 1}^n u_k(x)^2\right)^{-1/2}\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{kij}(x)u_k(x) + \left(\sum_{k = 1}^n u_k(x)^2\right)^{-1/2}\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{ki}(x)u_{kj}(x)\\
    & -\left(\sum_{k = 1}^n u_k(x)^2\right)^{-3/2}\left(\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{ki}(x)u_k(x)\right)\left(\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{kj}(x)u_k(x)\right)\\
    & + (u(x) + A)u_{ij}(x) + u_i(x)u_j(x)\\
    & = u_{rij} + \frac{\delta_{ij}}{u_r}\lambda_i^2 - \frac{\delta_{ir}\delta_{jr}}{u_r}\lambda_r^2 + \delta_{ij}(u + A)\lambda_i + \delta_{ir}\delta_{jr}u_r^2 \textrm{ at } x = x_0,\\
    & = u_{rij} + \frac{\delta_{ij}}{u_r}\lambda_i^2 - \delta_{ir}\delta_{jr}(u + A)^2u_r\\
    & + \delta_{ij}(u + A)\lambda_i + \delta_{ir}\delta_{jr}u_r^2 \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:lambda_r}}.
\end{align*}

Since $f$ reaches a maximum at $x_0$, its Hessian matrix at this point is negative. By ellipticity, the matrix $(F_{,ij})$ (at $(x_0,\nabla^2u(x_0))$) is positive definite. In particular, we deduce that
\begin{align}
    0 & \geqslant \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}f_{ij} \nonumber\\
    & = \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_{rij} + (u + A)\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{ii}\lambda_i \label{EQ:Estimée C1 terme 1}\\
    & + \frac{1}{u_r}\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{ii}\lambda_i^2 \label{EQ:Estimée C1 terme 2}\\
    & - F_{rr}(u + A)^2u_r + F_{rr}u_r^2. \label{EQ:Estimée C1 terme 3}
\end{align}

Finally, we bound from below each line of the above equation in order to bound $u_r = \left\|\nabla u\right\|$.\\

\noindent\textbf{Step 4:} Bounding \eqref{EQ:Estimée C1 terme 1}, \eqref{EQ:Estimée C1 terme 2} and \eqref{EQ:Estimée C1 terme 3}.

For the first term, we have
\begin{align}
    & \quad\ \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_{rij} + (u + A)\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{ii}\lambda_i\\
    & \geqslant \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_{rij} - M_{12}(u + A) \textrm{ by point 2 of Lemma \ref{LEM:Conséquences hypothèse}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_{ijr} - nB_1u_r - M_{12}(u + A) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Commutativité dérivées} and \eqref{EQ:nabla F borné}}, \nonumber\\
    & = -F_{r,} - nB_1u_r - M_{12}(u + A) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Dérivée F = 0 localement}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -M_{13} - nB_1u_r - M_{12}(u + A) \textrm{ by point 3 of Lemma \ref{LEM:Conséquences hypothèse}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -nB_1u_r - M_{12}A - M_{13}. \label{EQ:Borne estimée C1 terme 1}
\end{align}

The second term is very positive, especially when $u_r$ becomes large so it will give us the final bound on $u_r$.
\begin{align}
    \frac{1}{u_r}\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{ii}\lambda_i^2 & \geqslant \frac{1}{u_r}F_{nn}\lambda_n^2 \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant \frac{1}{M_{11}u_r}\lambda_r^2 \textrm{ by point 1 of Lemma \ref{LEM:Conséquences hypothèse} and the fact that } \lambda_n \leqslant \lambda_r \leqslant 0, \nonumber\\
    & = \frac{1}{M_{11}}(u + A)^2u_r \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:lambda_r}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant \frac{1}{M_{11}}(-\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + A)^2u_r. \label{EQ:Borne estimée C1 terme 2}
\end{align}

The third term can easily be bounded as
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne estimée C1 terme 3}
    -F_{rr}(u + A)^2u_r + F_{rr}u_r^2 \geqslant (u_r - A^2)F_{rr}u_r
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Step 5:} Conclusion.

Finally, by \eqref{EQ:Borne estimée C1 terme 1}, \eqref{EQ:Borne estimée C1 terme 2} and \eqref{EQ:Borne estimée C1 terme 3},
\begin{align*}
    0 & \geqslant \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}f_{ij}\\
    & \geqslant -nB_1u_r - M_{12}A - M_{13} + \frac{1}{M_{11}}(-\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + A)^2u_r + (u_r - A^2)F_{rr}u_r\\
    & = u_r - M_{12}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + \sqrt{M_{11}(nB_1 + 1)}\right) - M_{13}\\
    & + \left(u_r - \left(\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + \sqrt{M_{11}(nB_1 + 1)}\right)^2\right)F_{rr}u_r \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Estimée C1 définition A}}.
\end{align*}
We easily deduce that if $\frac{u_r}{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + 1}$ is large enough, both lines in the above equation will be positive, leading to a contradiction. We conclude by using the fact that $\left\|\nabla u\right\| = u_r$ and \eqref{EQ:Norme C1 en fonction de la valeur en x_0}.

<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
The proof is once again based on studying what happens when a suitable auxiliary function reaches its maximum. The introduced auxiliary function and the method used are the same as that used in \cite{Chou_Wang}\cite{Hou_Ma_Wu}\cite{Tosatti_Weinkove} for example. It is also a simplified version of Székelyhidi's proof \cite{Szekelyhidi}, where we don't have to deal with torsion.

During all the proof, we denote by $C_1,C_2,C_3,\ldots$ constants that depend only on $X$, $g$, ${\boldsymbol{H}}_0$, $\delta$, $R$, $M_{10}$, $M_{11}$, $M_{12}$, $M_{13}$, $M_{14}$ and $M_{15}$. We prove the inequality in ten steps.\\

\noindent\textbf{Step 1:} Bounding the lowest eigenvalue of the Hessian matrix.

For all $x \in X$, let $\lambda_1(x) \geqslant \cdots \geqslant \lambda_n(x)$ be the eigenvalues of $\nabla^2u(x)$. Using the same notations as in the proof of Proposition \ref{PRO:Estimées L1}, we have for all $x$,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne inférieure laplacien H_0}
    \left<\nabla^2u(x),\mathrm{N}_0(x)\right> \geqslant -m,
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{N}_0$ and $m$ only depend on $X$ and ${\boldsymbol{H}}_0$. Let $c_1$ and $C_2$ be constants such that for all $x$, $c_1\mathrm{Id} \leqslant \mathrm{N}_0(x) \leqslant C_2\mathrm{Id}$. If $\lambda_n(x) \leqslant 0$, \eqref{EQ:Borne inférieure laplacien H_0} implies that
$$
-m \leqslant \left<\nabla^2u(x),\mathrm{N}_0(x)\right> \leqslant (n - 1)C_2\lambda_1(x) + c_1\lambda_n(x),
$$
hence
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Norme en fonction de lambda_1}
    \left\|\nabla^2u(x)\right\| \leqslant \sqrt{n}\max\{\lambda_1(x),-\lambda_n(x)\} \leqslant \sqrt{n}\max\left\{\lambda_1(x),\frac{m + (n - 1)C_2\lambda_1(x)}{c_1}\right\}.
\end{equation}
It means that it is enough to control $\lambda_1$ from above in order to control the norm. Let $\lambda_{\max} = \max_{x \in X} \lambda_1(x)$.\\

\noindent\textbf{Step 2:} Introducing the function $f$ and its maximum.

Let
$$
f : x \longmapsto \ln(\lambda_1(x)) + \phi\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u(x)\right\|^2\right) - \psi(u(x)),
$$
with the convention that $f(x) = -\infty$ if $\lambda_1(x) \leqslant 0$, where
$$
\phi(s) = -\frac{1}{A_1}\ln\left(1 - \frac{s}{K}\right), \qquad \psi(t) = -\frac{A_2}{2\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}}t^2 + A_2t
$$
with $K = \left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}^2 + 1$ and $A_1,A_2 \geqslant 2$ are constants to be determined later. Notice that when $0 \leqslant s \leqslant \frac{1}{2}K$ and $-\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} \leqslant t \leqslant 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Bornes phi}
    0 \leqslant \phi \leqslant \ln(2), \qquad \frac{1}{A_1K} \leqslant \phi' \leqslant \frac{2}{A_1K} \leqslant \frac{1}{K}, \qquad \phi'' = A_1(\phi')^2,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Bornes psi}
    -\frac{3}{2}A_2\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} \leqslant \psi \leqslant 0, \qquad A_2 \leqslant \psi' \leqslant 2A_2, \qquad -\psi'' \geqslant \frac{1}{4A_2\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}}(\psi')^2.
\end{equation}

$f$ is continuous as a function with values in $\mathbb{R} \cup \{-\infty\}$ so it admits a maximum. Let $x_0$ be a point where $f$ reaches its maximum. We have
\begin{align}
    \lambda_{\max} & \leqslant \max_{x \in X} \exp\left(f(x) - \phi\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u(x)\right\|^2\right) + \psi(u(x))\right) \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant \exp(f(x_0)) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Bornes phi} and \eqref{EQ:Bornes psi}}, \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant \lambda_1(x_0)^+\exp\left(-\phi\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u(x_0)\right\|^2\right) + \psi(u(x_0))\right) \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant 2\exp\left(\frac{3}{2}A_2\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}\right)\lambda_1(x_0)^+ \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Bornes phi} and \eqref{EQ:Bornes psi}}, \label{EQ:lambda_max en fonction de lambda_1}
\end{align}
so it is enough to bound $\frac{\lambda_1(x_0)^+}{K}$ to deduce the wanted inequality.\\

\noindent\textbf{Step 3:} Computing the first differential of $f$.

Assume without loss of generality that $\lambda_1(x_0) \geqslant K > 0$ and $\lambda_1(x_0) \geqslant M_{10}$ so the assumptions on ${\boldsymbol{H}}$ apply at $x_0$. Assume also that $\lambda_n(x_0) < 0$. Indeed, if $\lambda_n(x_0) \geqslant 0$, then $\nabla^2u(x_0)$ is positive and can immediately be bounded by $R$ since $0$ is a $\mathcal{C}$-sub-solution. Consider a local orthonormal frame around $x_0$ in which $\nabla^2u(x_0)$ is diagonal with $u_{ii}(x_0) = \lambda_i(x_0)$. For all $x$ near $x_0$, $u_{11}(x) \leqslant \lambda_1(x)$ with equality at $x_0$ so the function
$$
\tilde{f} : x \longmapsto \ln(u_{11}(x)) + \phi\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u(x)\right\|^2\right) - \psi(u(x))
$$
is well-defined near $x_0$ and reaches a maximum at this point. Moreover, $\tilde{f}$ is smooth on its domain and for all $i$,
\begin{align}
    \tilde{f}_i(x) & = \frac{u_{11i}(x)}{u_{11}(x)} + \phi'\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u(x)\right\|^2\right)\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{ki}(x)u_k(x) - \psi'(u(x))u_i(x) \label{EQ:f_i estimées C2}\\
    & = \frac{u_{11i}(x_0)}{\lambda_1(x_0)} + \phi'\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u(x)\right\|^2\right)\lambda_i(x_0)u_i(x_0) - \psi'(u(x_0))u_i(x_0) \textrm{ at } x = x_0, \nonumber\\
    & = 0 \textrm{ because $\tilde{f}$ is maximal at } x_0. \label{EQ:f_i = 0 estimées C2}
\end{align}

\noindent\textbf{Step 4:} Computing the second differential of $\tilde{f}$.

From now on, when we evaluate an expression at $x = x_0$, we omit to write it to lighten the expressions. Similarly, we write $\psi'$ instead of $\psi'(u(x_0))$ for example. From \eqref{EQ:f_i estimées C2}, we compute that
\begin{align*}
    \tilde{f}_{ij} & = \frac{u_{11ij}(x)}{u_{11}(x)} - \frac{u_{11i}(x)u_{11j}(x)}{u_{11}(x)^2} + \phi'\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u(x)\right\|^2\right)\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{kij}(x)u_k(x)\\
    & + \phi'\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u(x)\right\|^2\right)\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{ki}(x)u_{kj}(x) + \phi''\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u(x)\right\|^2\right)\left(\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{ki}(x)u_k(x)\right)\left(\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{kj}(x)u_k(x)\right)\\
    & - \psi'(u(x))u_{ij}(x) - \psi''(u(x))u_i(x)u_j(x)\\
    & = \frac{u_{11ij}}{\lambda_1} - \frac{u_{11i}u_{11j}}{\lambda_1^2} + \phi'\sum_{k = 1}^n u_{kij}u_k + \delta_{ij}\phi'\lambda_i^2 + \phi''\lambda_i\lambda_ju_iu_j - \delta_{ij}\psi'\lambda_i - \psi''u_iu_j \textrm{ at } x = x_0.
\end{align*}
Since $\tilde{f}$ reaches a maximum at $x_0$, its Hessian matrix at this point is negative. By ellipticity, the matrix $(F_{,ij})$ (at $(x_0,\nabla^2u(x_0))$) is positive definite. In particular, we deduce that
\begin{align}
    0 & \geqslant \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\tilde{f}_{ij} \nonumber\\
    & = \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\frac{u_{11ij}}{\lambda_1} - \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\frac{u_{11i}u_{11j}}{\lambda_1^2} + \phi'\sum_{i,j,k = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_{kij}u_k \label{EQ:Terme à borner 1}\\
    & + \phi'\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{,ii}\lambda_i^2 + \phi''\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\lambda_i\lambda_ju_iu_j - \psi'\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{,ii}\lambda_i - \psi''\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_iu_j.
\end{align}

\noindent\textbf{Step 5:} Bounding \eqref{EQ:Terme à borner 1}.

First,
\begin{align}
    \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_{11ij} & \geqslant -nB_2\left\|\nabla^2u\right\| + \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_{ij11} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Commutativité dérivées}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -(nB_1 + M_{14})\left\|\nabla^2u\right\| + \frac{1}{\lambda_1 - \lambda_n}\sum_{i,j = 1}^n (F_{,ij} - M_{15}F_{,11})u_{i11}u_{j11}\textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Borne inférieure d_AF localement}} \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_3\lambda_1 + \frac{1}{\lambda_1 - \lambda_n}\sum_{i,j = 1}^n (F_{,ij} - M_{15}F_{,11})u_{i11}u_{j11} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Norme C1 en fonction de la valeur en x_0}}. \label{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 1}
\end{align}

Then,
\begin{align}
    \left|u_{i11}u_{j11} - u_{11i}u_{11j}\right| & \leqslant \left|u_{i11}u_{j11} - u_{11i}u_{j11}\right| + \left|u_{11i}u_{j11} - u_{11i}u_{11j}\right| \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant B_1\sqrt{K}(\left|u_{j11}\right| + \left|u_{11j}\right|) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Commutativité dérivées}}, \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant B_1\sqrt{K}(2\left|u_{11j}\right| + B_1\sqrt{K}) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Commutativité dérivées}}, \nonumber\\
    & = B_1^2K - 2B_1\sqrt{K}\lambda_1\left|\phi'\lambda_ju_j - \psi'u_j\right| \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:f_i = 0 estimées C2}}, \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant B_1^2K + 2B_1\lambda_1^2 + 4B_1A_2K \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Bornes phi} and \eqref{EQ:Bornes psi}}, \nonumber\\
    & \leqslant C_4\lambda_1\left(\lambda_1 + A_2\right) \textrm{ because } \lambda_1 \geqslant K. \label{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 2}
\end{align}

Therefore,
\begin{align}
    \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\frac{u_{11ij}}{\lambda_1} & \geqslant -C_3 + \frac{1}{\lambda_1(\lambda_1 - \lambda_n)}\sum_{i,j = 1}^n (F_{,ij} - M_{15}F_{,11})u_{i11}u_{j11} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 1}} \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_3 - C_5\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1 - \lambda_n} + \frac{1}{\lambda_1(\lambda_1 - \lambda_n)}\sum_{i,j = 1}^n (F_{,ij} - M_{15}F_{,11})u_{11i}u_{11j} \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 2} and \eqref{EQ:nabla F borné}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_3 - C_5\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} - M_{15}F_{,11}\left(\sum_{i = 1}^n \frac{u_{11i}}{\lambda_1}\right)^2 + \left(1 - \frac{\left|\lambda_n\right|}{\lambda_1}\right)\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\frac{u_{11i}u_{11j}}{\lambda_1^2}. \label{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 3}
\end{align}

We then bound the third term in \eqref{EQ:Terme à borner 1}.
\begin{align}
    \phi'\sum_{i,j,k = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_{kij}u_k & \geqslant -nB_1\phi'K + \phi'\sum_{i,j,k = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_{ijk}u_k \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Commutativité dérivées} and \eqref{EQ:nabla F borné}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -nB_1 + \phi'\sum_{k = 1}^n F_{k,}u_k \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Bornes phi} and \eqref{EQ:Dérivée F = 0 localement}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -nB_1 - \frac{nM_{13}}{\sqrt{K}} \textrm{ by point 3 of Lemma \ref{LEM:Conséquences hypothèse} and \eqref{EQ:Bornes phi}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_6 \textrm{ because } K \geqslant 1. \label{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 4}
\end{align}

Finally, combining \eqref{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 3} and \eqref{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 4}, we obtain that
\begin{align}
    & \quad\ \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\frac{u_{11ij}}{\lambda_1} - \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\frac{u_{11i}u_{11j}}{\lambda_1^2} + \phi'\sum_{i,j,k = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_{kij}u_k \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_3 - C_5\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} - M_{15}F_{,11}\left(\sum_{i = 1}^n \frac{u_{11i}}{\lambda_1}\right)^2 + \left(1 - \frac{\left|\lambda_n\right|}{\lambda_1}\right)\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\frac{u_{11i}u_{11j}}{\lambda_1^2} - \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\frac{u_{11i}u_{11j}}{\lambda_1^2} - C_6 \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} - M_{15}F_{,11}\left(\sum_{i = 1}^n \frac{u_{11i}}{\lambda_1}\right)^2 - \frac{\left|\lambda_n\right|}{\lambda_1}\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\frac{u_{11i}u_{11j}}{\lambda_1^2} \nonumber\\
    & = -C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} - M_{15}F_{,11}\left(\sum_{i = 1}^n \phi'\lambda_iu_i - \psi'u_i\right)^2 - \frac{\left|\lambda_n\right|}{\lambda_1}\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}(\phi'\lambda_iu_i - \psi'u_i)(\phi'\lambda_ju_j - \psi'u_j) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:f_i = 0 estimées C2}}. \label{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 5}
\end{align}

We then do a case disjunction as in \cite{Szekelyhidi}. Let $\kappa$ given by Proposition \ref{PRO:Guan} and $\varepsilon$ a positive constant that we determine later. It will play the role of $\delta$ in \cite{Szekelyhidi}. We will choose first $A_1$ in function of $\varepsilon$, then $A_2$ independently from $A_1$ and $\varepsilon$ and finally, we will choose $\varepsilon$ in function of $A_2$.\\

\noindent\textbf{Step 6:} In this step, we assume either that $F_{,11} \geqslant \frac{\varepsilon^2}{M_{11}}$ or $\left|\lambda_n\right| \geqslant \varepsilon\lambda_1$. We show a bound on $\lambda_1$.

If $F_{,11} \geqslant \frac{\varepsilon^2}{M_{11}}$,
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Ellipticité uniforme}
    \sum_{i = 1}^n F_{,ii}\lambda_i^2 \geqslant F_{,11}\lambda_1^2 \geqslant \frac{\varepsilon^2}{M_{11}}\lambda_1^2.
\end{equation}
If $\left|\lambda_n\right| \geqslant \varepsilon\lambda_1$, then by point 1 of Lemma \ref{LEM:Conséquences hypothèse},
$$
\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{,ii}\lambda_i^2 \geqslant F_{,nn}\lambda_n^2 \geqslant \frac{\varepsilon^2}{M_{11}}\lambda_1^2.
$$
In any case, \eqref{EQ:Ellipticité uniforme} holds.

Using \eqref{EQ:nabla F borné} and the fact that $\frac{\left|\lambda_n\right|}{\lambda_1}$ is bounded (by \eqref{EQ:Norme en fonction de lambda_1}), \eqref{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 5} becomes
\begin{align}
    & \quad\ \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\frac{u_{11ij}}{\lambda_1} - \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\frac{u_{11i}u_{11j}}{\lambda_1^2} + \phi'\sum_{i,j,k = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_{kij}u_k \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} - C_9\sum_{i = 1}^n (\phi'\lambda_iu_i - \psi'u_i)^2 \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} - \frac{C_{10}}{A_1^2K}\lambda_1^2 - C_{11}A_2^2K \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Bornes phi} and \eqref{EQ:Bornes psi}}. \label{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 6}
\end{align}

Therefore,
\begin{align}
    0 & \geqslant \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\tilde{f}_{ij} \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} - \frac{C_{10}}{A_1^2K}\lambda_1^2 - C_{11}A_2^2K \nonumber\\
    & + \phi'\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{,ii}\lambda_i^2 + \phi''\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\lambda_i\lambda_ju_iu_j - \psi'\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{,ii}\lambda_i - \psi''\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_iu_j \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 6}} \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} - \frac{C_{10}}{A_1^2K}\lambda_1^2 - C_{11}A_2^2K + \frac{1}{A_1K}\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{,ii}\lambda_i^2 - 2A_2R \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Bornes phi}, \eqref{EQ:Bornes psi} and Proposition \ref{PRO:Guan}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} - \frac{C_{10}}{A_1^2K}\lambda_1^2 - C_{11}A_2^2K + \frac{\varepsilon^2}{A_1M_{11}K}\lambda_1^2 - 2A_2R \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Ellipticité uniforme}}, \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant - C_{12}A_2^2K + \left(\frac{\varepsilon^2}{A_1M_{11}K} - \frac{C_{10}}{A_1^2K}\right)\lambda_1^2. \label{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 7}
\end{align}
We choose
$$
A_1 = \frac{2C_{10}M_{11}}{\varepsilon^2},
$$
and we deduce from \eqref{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 7} that
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Borne finale lambda_1 cas 1}
    \lambda_1 \leqslant \frac{\sqrt{2C_{10}C_{12}}A_2M_{11}}{\varepsilon^2}K.
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Step 7:} From now on, we assume that $F_{,11} < \frac{\varepsilon^2}{M_{11}}$ and $\left|\lambda_n\right| \leqslant \varepsilon\lambda_1$ because else, \eqref{EQ:Borne finale lambda_1 cas 1} holds. We similarly show a bound on $\lambda_1$.

We choose $\varepsilon$ small enough so $\frac{\varepsilon^2}{M_{11}} \leqslant \kappa$. In particular, $F_{,11} < \kappa$ so by Proposition \ref{PRO:Guan}, we have
\begin{equation}\label{EQ:Guan}
    -\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{,ii}\lambda_i \geqslant \kappa.
\end{equation}

Therefore,
\begin{align}
    0 & \geqslant \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\tilde{f}_{ij} \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} - M_{15}F_{,11}\left(\sum_{i = 1}^n \phi'\lambda_iu_i - \psi'u_i\right)^2 - \frac{\left|\lambda_n\right|}{\lambda_1}\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}(\phi'\lambda_iu_i - \psi'u_i)(\phi'\lambda_ju_j - \psi'u_j) \nonumber\\
    & + \phi'\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{,ii}\lambda_i^2 + \phi''\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\lambda_i\lambda_ju_iu_j - \psi'\sum_{i = 1}^n F_{,ii}\lambda_i - \psi''\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_iu_j \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} - C_{13}F_{,11}(\phi')^2K\lambda_1 - C_{14}F_{,11}(\psi')^2K - \varepsilon\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}(\phi'\lambda_iu_i - \psi'u_i)(\phi'\lambda_ju_j - \psi'u_j) \nonumber\\
    & + F_{,11}\phi'\lambda_1^2 + \phi''\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\lambda_i\lambda_ju_iu_j + \kappa\psi' - \psi''\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_iu_j \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Bornes phi}, \eqref{EQ:Bornes psi} and \eqref{EQ:Guan}}, \nonumber\\
    & = -C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} + \kappa\psi' + F_{,11}\left(\phi'\lambda_1^2 - C_{13}(\phi')^2K\lambda_1 - C_{14}(\psi')^2K\right) \nonumber\\
    & + \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_iu_j\left((\phi'' - \varepsilon(\phi')^2)\lambda_i\lambda_j + \varepsilon\phi'\psi'(\lambda_i + \lambda_j) + (-\psi'' - \varepsilon(\psi')^2)\right) \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant -C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} + \kappa A_2 \label{EQ:Terme à borner 2}\\
    & + F_{,11}\left(\frac{1}{A_1K}\lambda_1^2 - \frac{4C_{13}A_1^2}{K}\lambda_1 - 4C_{14}A_2^2K\right) \label{EQ:Terme à borner 3}\\
    & + \sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}u_iu_j\left((\phi'' - \varepsilon(\phi')^2)\lambda_i\lambda_j + \varepsilon\phi'\psi'(\lambda_i + \lambda_j) + (-\psi'' - \varepsilon(\psi')^2)\right) \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Bornes phi} and \eqref{EQ:Bornes psi}}. \label{EQ:Terme à borner 4}
\end{align}

\noindent\textbf{Step 8:} Bounding \eqref{EQ:Terme à borner 2} and \eqref{EQ:Terme à borner 3}.

We choose
$$
A_2 = \frac{2C_7}{\kappa},
$$
so
$$
-C_7\frac{\lambda_1 + A_2}{\lambda_1} + \kappa A_2 = C_7\left(1 - \frac{2C_7}{\kappa\lambda_1}\right),
$$
hence the positivity of \eqref{EQ:Terme à borner 2} as long as $\lambda_1 > \frac{2C_7}{\kappa}$, which we may assume without loss of generality.

Similarly, we easily see that \eqref{EQ:Terme à borner 3} is non-negative if $\frac{\lambda_1}{K}$ is large enough (in function of $A_1$ and $A_2$).

\noindent\textbf{Step 9:} Bounding \eqref{EQ:Terme à borner 4}.

For all indices $i$ and $j$, we have
\begin{align}
    & \quad\ (\phi'' - \varepsilon(\phi')^2)\lambda_i\lambda_j + \varepsilon\phi'\psi'(\lambda_i + \lambda_j) + (-\psi'' - \varepsilon(\psi')^2) \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant (A_1 - 1)\varepsilon(\phi')^2\lambda_i\lambda_j + \varepsilon\phi'\psi'(\lambda_i + \lambda_j) + \left(\frac{1}{4A_2\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}} - \varepsilon\right)(\psi')^2 \textrm{ by \eqref{EQ:Bornes phi} and \eqref{EQ:Bornes psi}}, \nonumber\\
    & = (A_1 - 1)\left(\phi'\lambda_i + \frac{\varepsilon\psi'}{A_1 - 1}\right)\left(\phi'\lambda_j + \frac{\varepsilon\psi'}{A_1 - 1}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{4A_2\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0}} - \varepsilon - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{A_1 - 1}\right)(\psi')^2 \nonumber\\
    & \geqslant (A_1 - 1)\left(\phi'\lambda_i + \frac{\varepsilon\psi'}{A_1 - 1}\right)\left(\phi'\lambda_j + \frac{\varepsilon\psi'}{A_1 - 1}\right), \label{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 8}
\end{align}
as long as $\varepsilon$ is small enough. Notice that $\varepsilon$ depends linearly $\frac{1}{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + 1}$, thus $A_1$ depends quadratically in $\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + 1$ but this quadratic term can be absorbed in \eqref{EQ:lambda_max en fonction de lambda_1} in the exponential so we will eventually obtain a bound on $\frac{\lambda_{\max}}{K}$ which is exponential in $\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^0} + 1$.

Since the matrix $(F_{,ij}u_iu_j)$ is positive, we deduce from \eqref{EQ:Inégalité estimées C2 8} that \eqref{EQ:Terme à borner 4} is non-negative.\\

\noindent\textbf{Step 10:} Conclusion.

We showed in steps 7, 8 and 9 that \eqref{EQ:Terme à borner 2} is positive and \eqref{EQ:Terme à borner 3} and \eqref{EQ:Terme à borner 4} are non-negative if $\frac{\lambda_1}{K}$ is large enough, which contradicts the non-negativity of $\sum_{i,j = 1}^n F_{,ij}\tilde{f}_{ij}$. We deduce the desired bound by contradiction, and we conclude with \eqref{EQ:Norme en fonction de lambda_1} and \eqref{EQ:lambda_max en fonction de lambda_1}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{THE:Unicité}
    Let ${\boldsymbol{H}}^1$ and ${\boldsymbol{H}}^2$ be smooth $\mathrm{End}_S^{++}(TX,g)$-elliptic convex hypersurfaces of $\mathrm{End}_S(TX,g)$ such that ${\boldsymbol{H}}^1 \subset ({\boldsymbol{H}}^2)^-$. Let $u_1$ and $u_2$ be smooth functions that satisfy for each $i \in \{1,2\}$,
    $$
    \forall x \in X, \quad \nabla^2u_i(x) \in H_x^i.
    $$

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Assume up to a translation that $u_1 = 0$ and set $u = u_2$. Let
$$
{\boldsymbol{H}}_0 = \{(x,A) \in \mathrm{End}_S(TX,g) \mid \left<\mathrm{N}_{H_x^1}(0),A\right> = 0\}.
$$
It is a family of $\mathrm{End}_S^{++}(TX,g)$-elliptic convex hypersurfaces as in Sub-section \ref{SEC:Estimées C0}. Let $\square$ be the associated linear elliptic operator. We know (see the proof of Proposition \ref{PRO:Estimées L1}) that $\ker(\square) = \mathbb{R} 1$ and $\ker(\square) = \mathbb{R} k$ for some smooth positive function $k : X \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$.

Moreover, for all $x$, $\nabla^2u(x) \in H_x^2 \subset (H_x^1)^+ \subset H_{0,x}^+$ so $\square u(x) \geqslant 0$ where $\mathrm{N}_{0,x}$ is the normal vector to $H_{0,x}$. We deduce that
$$
0 \leqslant \int_X \square u(x)k(x) \, d\mu_g(x) = \int_X u(x)\square^*k(x) \, d\mu_g(x) = 0.
$$
Therefore, the inequality must be an equality so $\square u = 0$ \textit{i.e.} $u$ is constant.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{LEM:Borne K}
    If $(u,K)$ is a solution to $(E_{t,c})$, then
    $$
    \min_{x \in X} F^b(x,0) \leqslant t\min_{x \in X} F^c(x,0) \leqslant K \leqslant t\max_{x \in X} F^c(x,0) \leqslant \max_{x \in X} F^a(x,0).
    $$
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Let $x_0$ be a point where $u$ reaches its maximum. In particular, $\nabla^2u(x_0) \leqslant 0$ so by ellipticity, $F^c(x_0,\nabla^2u(x_0)) \leqslant F^c(x_0,0)$ hence
$$
K = F^c(x_0,\nabla^2u(x_0)) - (1 - t)F^c(x_0,0) \leqslant tF^c(x_0,0) \leqslant t\max_{x \in X} F^c(x,0).
$$
Moreover, since ${\boldsymbol{H}}^c \subset ({\boldsymbol{H}}^a)^+$, $F^c \leqslant F^a$ and since for all $x$, $(x,0) \in ({\boldsymbol{H}}^a)^+$, $F^a(x,0) \geqslant 0$ hence
$$
t\max_{x \in X} F^c(x,0) \leqslant \max_{x \in X} F^a(x,0).
$$
We get the other inequality similarly.
