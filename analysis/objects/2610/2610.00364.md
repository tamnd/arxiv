<!-- defn--1: body_md -->
Let ${\mathscr A}$ be an algebra over a field $\Bbbk$. The left ${\mathscr A}$-module $M$ is
  \emph{amenable} if for every $\epsilon>0$ and every finite
  $S\le{\mathscr A}$ there is a finite $F\le M$ with
  \[\dim((F+S F)/F)<\epsilon\dim F.
  \]
  (Here and throughout, ``finite'' means ``finite-dimensional'' when applied to vector spaces. The strict inclusion `$<$' prevents $F=0$.)
<!-- end defn--1 -->
<!-- maindefn-1: body_md -->
Let $M$ be a left ${\mathscr A}$-module. It is \emph{extensively amenable} if the symmetric algebra $\operatorname{Sym}(M)$ is amenable when viewed as a $\operatorname{Sym}(M)\#{\mathscr A}$-module.

<!-- mainprop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:locally-finite}
Every locally finite ${\mathscr A}$-module (namely, a module in which every finite subspace is contained in a finite submodule) is extensively amenable.
<!-- end mainprop-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For given finite $S\le M\wr{\mathscr A}$, choose a finite ${\mathscr A}$-submodule $V\le M$
containing all variables in $S$'s polynomial coefficients. Set \(q=\dim V\),
and bound the degrees of those coefficients by \(d\). The ${\mathscr A}$-action
preserves polynomial degree. For
\(E_n=\operatorname{Sym}^{\le n}(V)\), the expanded space is contained in
\(\operatorname{Sym}^{\le n+d}(V)\), so its expansion ratio is at most
\[
  \left.\binom{n+d+q}{q}\right/\binom{n+q}{q}\to 1.\qedhere
\]
<!-- end proof-1 -->

<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:tensor}
Let \({\mathscr H}\) be a cocommutative Hopf algebra, let $U,V$ be left ${\mathscr H}$-modules, and give \(U\otimes V\) the
diagonal \({\mathscr H}\)-action. If \(F\le {\mathscr H}\) is a finite subcoalgebra
containing \(1\), then for every nonzero finite \(E\le U\otimes V\)
there is a nonzero finite \(V_0\le V\) such that
\[
 \frac{\dim FV_0}{\dim V_0}\le \frac{\dim FE}{\dim E}.
\]
Consequently, if \(U\otimes V\) contains an amenable \({\mathscr H}\)-submodule,
then \(V\) is amenable.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Put \(E^+=FE\) and choose a finite subspace \(W\le V\) containing all second tensor coefficients of \(E\) and \(E^+\).  For \(Z=E,E^+\) put \(r_Z(L)=\dim(Z\cap(U\otimes L))\). The functions \(r_Z\) are monotone and supermodular on the subspace lattice of $W$. For \(t>0\), let \(C_t(Z)\)
be the largest maximizer of \(r_Z(L)-t\dim L\), which exists by
\cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Lemma~1.2}. These are also maximizers over
all finite subspaces of \(V\): replacing \(L\) by \(L\cap W\) leaves its
rank unchanged and cannot increase its dimension.

First suppose that \(F\) has a complete subcoalgebra flag
\(0=F_0<\cdots<F_s=F\). We claim that \(F C_t(E)\le C_t(E^+)\).
Write \(C=C_t(E)\), \(C^+=C_t(E^+)\), and suppose inductively that
\(F_{i-1}C\le C^+\). For \(a\in F_i\setminus F_{i-1}\), there is
a group-like \(g\in F_i\), by the codimension-one argument in
\cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Proposition~1.8}, with
\(\Delta(a)-g\otimes a\in F_i\otimes F_{i-1}\). Set \(K=\{c\in C:ac\in C^+\}\), let \(q\colon V\to V/C^+\) be the quotient map, and put \(\bar a\colon C\to V/C^+\), \(\bar a(c)=q(ac)\).
Write \(\delta_a\) for the diagonal action of \(a\) on \(U\otimes V\),
and \(g_U\) for the action of \(g\) on \(U\).
The relation for \(\Delta(a)\) and the inductive inclusion
\(F_{i-1}C\le C^+\) give
\[
 (\mathrm{id}_U\otimes q)\delta_a(z)=(g_U\otimes\bar a)(z)
 \qquad(z\in U\otimes C).
\]
Since \(g_U\) is invertible with inverse \(S(g)_U\)
and \(\ker\bar a=K\), exactness of the tensor product gives
\[
 \ker(g_U\otimes\bar a)=U\otimes K.
\]
Moreover, \(\delta_a(E)\le E^+\), because \(a\in F\), and the same
triangular relation gives
\(\delta_a(U\otimes C)\le U\otimes(C^++aC)\).
Thus the restriction of \(\delta_a\) to \(E\cap(U\otimes C)\), followed
by quotienting by \(E^+\cap(U\otimes C^+)\), has kernel exactly
\(E\cap(U\otimes K)\). It therefore induces an injection
\[
 \frac{E\cap(U\otimes C)}{E\cap(U\otimes K)}\to
 \frac{E^+\cap(U\otimes(C^++aC))}{E^+\cap(U\otimes C^+)}.
\]
Maximality of \(C\) bounds the dimension on the left below by
\(t(\dim C-\dim K)\); rank-nullity identifies this with
\(t(\dim(C^++aC)-\dim C^+)\). Hence \(C^++aC\) is also a maximizer
for \(E^+\), and the choice of the largest maximizer gives \(aC\le C^+\).

The density/coarea identity \cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Lemma~1.2} now gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty\dim C_t(E)\,dt=\dim E,\qquad
 \int_0^\infty\dim F C_t(E)\,dt\le\dim E^+.
\]
Some nonzero \(C_t(E)\) has the asserted ratio.

For the general case, split a subcoalgebra flag after a finite field extension and descend the ratio, using \cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Lemmas~1.6 and~1.7}.

<!-- cor-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:all-modules}
Let \({\mathscr A}\) be a cocommutative Hopf algebra. The regular module
\({}_{\mathscr A}{\mathscr A}\) is amenable if and only if every nonzero left \({\mathscr A}\)-module
is amenable.
<!-- end cor-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The reverse implication follows by taking the regular module.
Conversely, suppose that \({\mathscr A}\) is amenable and let \(M\ne0\).
The linear isomorphism
\[
 T\colon{\mathscr A}\otimes M\to{\mathscr A}\otimes M,\qquad
 a\otimes m\mapsto\sum a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}m,
\]
with inverse \(a\otimes m\mapsto\sum a_{(1)}\otimes S(a_{(2)})m\),
intertwines the action on the first factor with the diagonal action.
For any fixed \(0\ne m\in M\), the diagonal module therefore contains
the amenable submodule \(T({\mathscr A}\otimes\Bbbk m)\simeq{}_{\mathscr A}{\mathscr A}\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor} makes \(M\) amenable.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:induction}
Let \({\mathscr K}\le{\mathscr B}\) be cocommutative Hopf algebras. Suppose there is
a linear section \(\sigma\colon Q\to{\mathscr B}\) of \(q\) for which
\[
 \Theta\colon Q\otimes {\mathscr K}\to {\mathscr B},\qquad c\otimes k\mapsto\sigma(c)k
\]
is a right \({\mathscr K}\)-module and left \(Q\)-comodule isomorphism, where the
source coaction is \(\Delta_Q\otimes\mathrm{id}\).
If \(Q\) is amenable over \({\mathscr B}\) and \(V\) is amenable over \({\mathscr K}\),
then \({\mathscr B}\otimes_{\mathscr K} V\) is amenable over \({\mathscr B}\).
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let \(F\le {\mathscr B}\) be a finite subcoalgebra containing \(1\), and fix
\(0<\delta\). By \cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Theorem~1.1}, choose a
nonzero finite subcoalgebra \(C\le Q\) with
\(\dim FC<(1+\delta)\dim C\), and put \(D=FC\).
Colinearity and the module-coalgebra identity give
\[
 \rho(F\sigma(C))\le D\otimes {\mathscr B}.
\]
Applying \(\mathrm{id}_Q\otimes\Theta^{-1}\) and using $Q$-colinearity of $\Theta$, we obtain
\[(\Delta_Q\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr K})\Theta^{-1}(F\sigma(C))\le D\otimes Q\otimes{\mathscr K},\]
and applying \(\Theta\circ(\mathrm{id}_Q\otimes\varepsilon_Q\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr K})\) yields
\(F\sigma(C)\le\Theta(D\otimes {\mathscr K})\).
Consequently there is a finite \(T\le{\mathscr K}\), containing \(1\),
such that \(F\sigma(C)\le\sigma(D)T\).
Choose a finite \(E\le V\) with
\(\dim TE<(1+\delta)\dim E\).
The induced vector-space identification \({\mathscr B}\otimes_{\mathscr K} V\simeq Q\otimes V\)
then gives, for \(W=C\otimes E\),
\[
 FW\le D\otimes TE,\qquad
 \frac{\dim FW}{\dim W}<(1+\delta)^2.
\]
Let \(\delta\) tend to zero.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:ext=>amen}
Every nonzero extensively amenable ${\mathscr A}$-module is amenable.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $M$ be a nonzero extensively amenable ${\mathscr A}$-module and let
\[
 d\colon \operatorname{Sym}(M)\to \operatorname{Sym}(M)\otimes M,\qquad d(m)=1\otimes m
\]
be the universal derivation. It is ${\mathscr A}$-linear for the diagonal action
and by the Leibniz identity satisfies
\[d(m_1\cdots m_k)=\sum_{i=1}^k m_1\cdots \widehat{m_i}\cdots m_k\otimes m_i.\]

Fix a finite \(F\le{\mathscr A}\) containing \(1\), a nonzero \(m\in M\),
and \(\eta>0\). Extensive amenability gives a finite
\(E\le \operatorname{Sym}(M)\) such that
\[
 Z=FE+F(mE)\quad\text{satisfies}\quad \dim Z<(1+\eta)\dim E.
\]
Put \(E'=E+mE\) and \(V=d(E')\). The map
\[
 E\to V\oplus V,\qquad
 u\mapsto\bigl(d(u),d(mu)\bigr)
\]
is injective: if both coordinates vanish, the Leibniz identity gives
\(u\otimes m=0\), hence \(u=0\). Thus \(\dim E\le2\dim V\).
Since \(d\) is ${\mathscr A}$-linear, \(d(FE')=FV\), so \(d\) induces a
surjection \(FE'/E'\twoheadrightarrow FV/V\). As \(FE'=Z\) and
\(E\le E'\), we have
\[
 \dim FV-\dim V
 \le \dim FE'-\dim E'
 <\eta \dim E\le2\eta\dim V.
\]
Hence \(\operatorname{Sym}(M)\otimes M\) is amenable. Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor} extracts
F{\o}lner spaces in \(M\).
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:amen=>reg}
Every module over an amenable cocommutative Hopf algebra is extensively
amenable. In particular, its regular module is extensively amenable.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The commutative Hopf algebra \(\operatorname{Sym}(M)\) is amenable
\cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Theorem~2.4}. The split cleft extension
\[
 \Bbbk\longrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}(M)\longrightarrow M\wr{\mathscr A}
       \longrightarrow {\mathscr A}\longrightarrow\Bbbk
\]
therefore makes \(M\wr{\mathscr A}\) amenable by \cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Theorem~2.5}.
Its nonzero module \(\operatorname{Sym}(M)\) is amenable by
Corollary~\ref{cor:all-modules}.

<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:exact}
For every exact sequence of ${\mathscr A}$-modules
\[
 0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow0,
\]
$N$ is extensively amenable if and only if both $M$ and $P$ are extensively amenable.
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Suppose first that $N$ is extensively amenable. Give \(N\) the two-step filtration $0\le M\le N$, giving $M$ weight zero and \(N/M\simeq P\) in degree $1$. Then $\operatorname{gr} N\simeq M\oplus P$, and the induced multiplicative filtrations satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{gr}\operatorname{Sym}(N)\simeq\operatorname{Sym}(M\oplus P),\qquad
 \operatorname{gr}(N\wr{\mathscr A})\simeq\operatorname{Sym}(M\oplus P)\mathbin{\#}{\mathscr A}.
\]
The preceding observation proves that $M\oplus P$ is extensively amenable.
The two projections of \(M\oplus P\) induce surjective counital
module-coalgebra maps to \(\operatorname{Sym}(M)\) and \(\operatorname{Sym}(P)\). The quotient theorem
\cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Theorem~1.9}, with the actions factored through \(M\wr{\mathscr A}\)
and \(P\wr{\mathscr A}\), proves both conclusions.

Conversely, take \({\mathscr B}=N\wr{\mathscr A}\), \({\mathscr K}=M\wr{\mathscr A}\), and
\(Q={\mathscr B}/{\mathscr B}\ker\varepsilon_{\mathscr K}\simeq\operatorname{Sym}(P)\), with quotient map \(q\) and coaction
\(\rho=(q\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta_{\mathscr B}\).
A vector-space section \(P\to N\) induces an algebra and coalgebra
section \(\sigma\colon\operatorname{Sym}(P)\to\operatorname{Sym}(N)\le {\mathscr B}\). Multiplication gives
\[
 \Theta\colon\operatorname{Sym}(P)\otimes {\mathscr K}\xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ } {\mathscr B},\qquad
 p\otimes k\mapsto\sigma(p)k.
\]
It is a right \({\mathscr K}\)-module isomorphism by the polynomial normal form,
and is colinear because
\(\rho(\sigma(p))=\sum p_{(1)}\otimes\sigma(p_{(2)})\) and
\(q(bk)=q(b)\varepsilon(k)\).
The \({\mathscr B}\)-action on \(\operatorname{Sym}(P)\) factors through \(P\wr{\mathscr A}\), so \({}_{\mathscr B}(\operatorname{Sym}(P))\) is amenable.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:induction} to the amenable \({\mathscr K}\)-module \(\operatorname{Sym}(M)\).
The action map
\[
 {\mathscr B}\otimes_{\mathscr K}\operatorname{Sym}(M)\to\operatorname{Sym}(N),\qquad
 (r\mathbin{\#} a)\otimes s\mapsto r(a\cdot s),
\]
identifies these modules, proving that $N$ is extensively amenable.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- cor-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:unions}
Extensive amenability passes to submodules and quotients, is closed
under extensions and direct sums, and is local on directed unions
of ${\mathscr A}$-submodules.
<!-- end cor-2 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The first assertions and the finite direct-sum assertion follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}. If \(M=\bigcup_iM_i\) is directed and every
\(M_i\) is extensively amenable, the coefficients of any finite subspace of \(M\wr{\mathscr A}\) lie in some \(\operatorname{Sym}(M_i)\); a F{\o}lner space there works in
\(\operatorname{Sym}(M)\). The converse follows by submodule permanence. Arbitrary
direct sums are directed unions of finite ones.

<!-- thm-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:group}
For every field \(\Bbbk\) and every \(G\)-set \(X\), the permutation
\(\Bbbk[G]\)-module \(\Bbbk[X]\) is extensively amenable if and only if
\(G\curvearrowright X\) is extensively amenable.
<!-- end thm-4 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Set \(L={\mathbb Z}^{(X)}\) and $R=\Bbbk[L]$, and write $u_x$ for the standard generators of $L$ and $z_x=u_x-1$. Let \(\varpi=\ker(\varepsilon\colon R\to\Bbbk)\) be the augmentation ideal, and filter \(R\) by \(\varpi^n\). The classical description of the augmentation filtration of a free abelian group gives
\[
 \bigcap_{n\ge0}\varpi^n=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{gr}_\varpi R=\operatorname{Sym}_\Bbbk(\Bbbk\otimes_{\mathbb Z} L)\simeq\operatorname{Sym}(\Bbbk[X]),\]
see~\cite{passi:gr}*{Chapter~VI and Corollary~VIII.8.2}.
The isomorphism sends \(x\) to the initial form of \(u_x-1\), so is
\(G\)-equivariant. Since \(G\) preserves \(\varpi\), the compatible
filtration \((R\mathbin{\#}\Bbbk[G])_n=\varpi^n\mathbin{\#}\Bbbk[G]\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradedkG}
  \operatorname{gr}(R\mathbin{\#}\Bbbk[G])\simeq\operatorname{Sym}(\Bbbk[X])\mathbin{\#}\Bbbk[G].
\end{equation}

  Here \(R\mathbin{\#}\Bbbk[G]\simeq\Bbbk[L\rtimes G]\), and \(R\)
is the permutation module of the affine action \(L\rtimes G\curvearrowright L\).
By the preceding criterion and the permutation-module equivalence
\cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{\S3}, it suffices to prove that
\(\operatorname{Sym}(\Bbbk[X])\) is amenable under \(\operatorname{Sym}(\Bbbk[X])\mathbin{\#}\Bbbk[G]\)
if and only if \(R\) is amenable under \(R\mathbin{\#}\Bbbk[G]\).

  Suppose first that \(\operatorname{Sym}(\Bbbk[X])\) is amenable under $\Bbbk[X]\wr\Bbbk[G]$. The $G$-equivariant map
  \[\operatorname{Sym}(\Bbbk[X])\to R,\qquad x\mapsto z_x\]
  identifies the symmetric algebra with the polynomial subalgebra $\Bbbk[z_x:x\in X]$; these are also the canonical lifts of the generators in $\operatorname{gr}_\varpi R\simeq\operatorname{Sym}(\Bbbk[X])$. If $E$ is a F\o lner space in $\operatorname{Sym}(\Bbbk[X])$, let $\widetilde E$ be its image in $R$. For $g\in G$ the expansion ratios of $E$ and $\widetilde E$ agree, while $\widetilde E+u_x\widetilde E=\widetilde E+z_x\widetilde E$ shows that multiplication by $u_x$ has the same boundary as multiplication by $z_x$.

For any finite-dimensional subspace \(W\) and invertible linear operators
\(a,b\) on its ambient space, put
\(b_W(a)=\dim(W+aW)-\dim W\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator-boundary}
 b_W(ab)\le b_W(a)+b_W(b),\qquad b_W(a^{-1})=b_W(a).
\end{equation}
The first inequality follows by enlarging \(W+abW\) to \(W+aW+abW\); the additional dimension beyond $W+aW$ is at most $\dim((aW+abW)/aW)=\dim((W+bW)/W)=b_W(b)$; the second follows by applying \(a\) to \(W+a^{-1}W\).
Given a finite subset of \(L\rtimes G\), express its elements as words in
finitely many \(u_x^{\pm1}\) and elements of \(G\). Choose \(E\)
simultaneously F\o lner for the corresponding variables \(x\) and group
operators, with error small compared with the sum of the word lengths.
By \eqref{eq:operator-boundary} and
\[
 \dim\Bigl(W+\sum_j a_jW\Bigr)-\dim W\le\sum_j b_W(a_j),
\]
the image \(\widetilde E\) is F\o lner for the prescribed finite subset.
Thus \(R\) is amenable under \(R\mathbin{\#}\Bbbk[G]\), so the affine action
is amenable and \(G\curvearrowright X\) is extensively amenable.

  Conversely, suppose that $R$ is amenable under $R\mathbin{\#}\Bbbk[G]$. The separated-filtration argument preceding Theorem~\ref{thm:exact} proves that amenability is preserved on the associated graded, so \(\Bbbk[X]\) is extensively amenable by~\eqref{eq:gradedkG}.

<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:pointed}
Let \({\mathscr H}\) be a nonnegatively graded Hopf superalgebra, with parity
given by degree modulo two and cocommutative degree-zero part.
For compatibly nonnegatively graded objects and degree-preserving
maps, the conclusions of \cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Theorems~1.1 and~1.9}
(coalgebraic rounding and module-coalgebra quotient permanence),
and Lemmas~\ref{lem:tensor} and~\ref{lem:induction} hold in the
superalgebra convention. In the quantitative statements the finite
testing subcoalgebras and input subspaces are assumed homogeneous,
and the resulting subcoalgebras or subspaces may be chosen homogeneous.
The amenability conclusions hold for arbitrary finite testing spaces.
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Every finite subspace of a graded coalgebra is contained in a finite
graded subcoalgebra: take a finite subcoalgebra containing it and then
its homogeneous hull. Indeed, if \(p_n\) denotes projection to degree
\(n\), then \(\Delta(p_nc)=\sum_{i+j=n}(p_i\otimes p_j)\Delta(c)\).
Thus arbitrary finite testing spaces may be enlarged to homogeneous
subcoalgebras containing \(1\); the filtered-space observation preceding
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact} then supplies homogeneous F\o lner spaces.

Complete homogeneous subcoalgebra flags exist by coalgebra duality: let
\(0\ne F=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}F_n\le{\mathscr H}\) be a finite graded subcoalgebra,
put \(\Omega=\overline{\Bbbk}\), and consider the ordinary convolution
algebra \(R=(\Omega\otimes F)^*\), with product
\[
 (fg)(c)=\sum f(c_{(1)})g(c_{(2)}).
\]
Give \(R\) the grading \(R_n=(\Omega\otimes F_n)^*\), extending
functionals by zero on the other degrees. Then
\(R_iR_j\le R_{i+j}\), so \(R_{>0}\) is a nilpotent two-sided ideal.
Moreover, \(R/R_{>0}\simeq R_0\) is commutative, since \(F_0\) is
cocommutative. Thus, writing \(J_0\) for the radical of \(R_0\), we have
\[
 J:=\operatorname{rad}R=J_0\oplus R_{>0},
 \qquad R/J\simeq\Omega^r
\]
for some \(r\). In particular, \(J\) is homogeneous and nilpotent,
while \(R/J\) is concentrated in degree zero.

For every \(m\ge0\), the radical layer
\(J^m/J^{m+1}\) is a graded bimodule over \(R/J\). Its left and right
actions commute and preserve each degree; equivalently, it is a graded
module over
\[
 (R/J)\otimes_\Omega(R/J)^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\Omega^{r^2}.
\]
This algebra is split semisimple and concentrated in degree zero, so
each layer is a direct sum of homogeneous one-dimensional sub-bimodules. Refining each radical layer by these
summands and taking inverse images gives a complete flag of homogeneous
two-sided ideals of \(R\). Their ordinary annihilators give, in reverse
order, a complete homogeneous subcoalgebra flag in \(\Omega\otimes F\).
Indeed, if \(I\) is a two-sided ideal and \(c\in I^\perp\), then
\(\Delta(c)\) annihilates \(I\otimes R+R\otimes I\), whence
\(\Delta(c)\in I^\perp\otimes I^\perp\).

Finally, a homogeneous basis adapted to the
flag involves only finitely many coefficients in \(\Omega\). They
generate a finite extension of \(\Bbbk\), over which the entire
homogeneous subcoalgebra flag is defined.

For a homogeneous codimension-one step \(F'\subset F'+\Bbbk a\), write
\[
 \Delta(a)-a\otimes g\in F'\otimes(F'+\Bbbk a),\qquad
 \Delta(a)-h\otimes a\in(F'+\Bbbk a)\otimes F',
\]
where \(g,h\) are even group-like elements. The super module-coalgebra
identity is
\[
 \Delta(ac)=
 \sum(-1)^{|a_{(2)}||c_{(1)}|}
       a_{(1)}c_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}c_{(2)}.
\]
More explicitly, let \(C\) be a finite graded subcoalgebra of an
\({\mathscr H}\)-module coalgebra, not necessarily \({\mathscr H}\)-stable, and let
\(D\le(F'+\Bbbk a)C\) be a graded subcoalgebra. Put
\(V=D+F'C\) and \(\phi:C\to(F'+\Bbbk a)C/V\), \(c\mapsto ac+V\),
as in \cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Proposition~1.8}.
On underlying vector spaces, using ordinary tensor products of linear
operators, the first triangular relation gives
\((\phi\otimes g)\Delta(c)=0\) on \(\ker\phi\); the second gives
\((h\Pi^{|a|}\otimes\phi)\Delta(c)=0\), where
\(\Pi(c)=(-1)^{|c|}c\). Since \(g\) and \(h\Pi^{|a|}\) are
invertible on the ambient module, their restrictions to \(C\) are injective,
and the two tensor-kernel arguments are unchanged.
Their intersection shows that \(\ker\phi\) is a graded subcoalgebra.
The dimension inequalities in \cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Proposition~1.8}
therefore telescope unchanged along the homogeneous flag.
To remove the splitting assumption, extend scalars to a finite
splitting field. The tensor-amplification and base-change arguments of
\cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Lemmas~1.4--1.5} apply with a purely even
field-extension factor and graded subspaces, so preserve semistability
on the lattice of graded subcoalgebras. Apply the split transfer
inequality to scalar extensions of the original graded data and descend
it by preservation of dimensions and intersections.
The density and comparison arguments of
\cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Lemmas~1.2--1.3 and the proof of Theorem~1.1}
then give rounding on the lattice of graded subcoalgebras over the
original field; no descent of the rounded subcoalgebra is needed.

Likewise, in Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor}, the leading action on
\(U\otimes V\) is \(h\Pi_U^{|a|}\otimes a\); its first factor is
invertible. Use graded subspaces in the density argument, and descend
the extracted subspace's expansion ratio by
\cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Lemma~1.7}; its coefficient filtration
preserves homogeneity. This ratio descent is used only for subspaces,
not for subcoalgebras.
For an even counital coalgebra quotient \(q\colon C\to Q\), the map
\((\mathrm{id}\otimes q)\Delta_C\colon C\to C\otimes Q\) remains an injective
\({\mathscr H}\)-module map with the super diagonal action, proving quotient
permanence. Finally, the normal-basis proof uses only rounding,
colinearity and dimensions, and is unchanged with graded spaces.

For a locally finite module, \(\bigwedge V\), with \(V\) a sufficiently

For an exact sequence, the weight filtration used in

Exterior normal form gives the right-\({\mathscr K}\) isomorphism
\(\Theta(p\otimes k)=\sigma(p)k\), and

 (q\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta_{\mathscr B}(\sigma(p))
   =\sum p_{(1)}\otimes\sigma(p_{(2)}).
\]
Thus \(\Theta\) is also a left-\(Q\) comodule map.
The super version of Lemma~\ref{lem:induction} proves ascent
exactly as before. The direct-sum and directed-union arguments

If ${\mathscr A}$ is amenable, put \({\mathscr B}=M\wr_\wedge{\mathscr A}\) and \({\mathscr K}=\bigwedge M\).
The algebra \({\mathscr K}\) is locally finite-dimensional and therefore amenable.
The quotient \({\mathscr B}\otimes_{\mathscr K}\Bbbk={\mathscr A}\), with its regular action, is amenable.
The Hopf section \({\mathscr A}\to {\mathscr B}\) gives the right-\({\mathscr K}\) normal basis
\({\mathscr A}\otimes {\mathscr K}\to {\mathscr B}\); bijectivity follows from the smash-product
relations and the bijective antipode of ${\mathscr A}$.
The super induction lemma, applied to \(V={\mathscr K}\), makes \({\mathscr B}\) amenable.
Its quotient module coalgebra
\({\mathscr B}\otimes_{\mathscr A}\Bbbk\simeq\bigwedge M\) is amenable by
Lemma~\ref{lem:pointed}.

It remains to pass from $\wedge$-extensive amenability of $M$, for \(M\ne0\), to ordinary

\(0\ne m\in M\), a finite subspace \(F\le{\mathscr A}\) containing \(1\), and

\(\dim(FE+F(mE))<(1+\eta)\dim E\), and put

 E\to W\oplus W,\qquad

<!-- thm-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:comparison-perm}
For every field \(\Bbbk\) and every \(G\)-set \(X\),
\[
 \Bbbk[X]\text{ is $\wedge$-extensively amenable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 G\curvearrowright X\text{ is extensively amenable}.
\]
<!-- end thm-6 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Put \(L=(\mathbf Z/2)^{(X)}\), identified with \(\mathcal P_f(X)\) by support.
By~\cite{juschenko-mattebon-monod-delasalle:extensive}*{Theorem~1.3}, the action \(G\curvearrowright X\) is extensively amenable if and only if the affine action \(L\rtimes G\curvearrowright L\) is amenable.

Suppose first that this affine action is amenable. Choose an order on \(X\)
and the resulting wedge basis
\[
(e_B)_{B\in\mathcal P_f(X)}
\]
of \(\bigwedge \Bbbk[X]\). A finite testing space \(S\) in
\(\bigwedge \Bbbk[X]\# \Bbbk[G]\) is contained in the span of finitely many elements
\(r_i\# g_i\), where each \(r_i\) is an exterior monomial. Write
\(T_i=\operatorname{supp}(r_i)\in L\), and put \(h_i=(T_i,g_i)\in L\rtimes G\).

Choose a finite F\o lner set \(F\subseteq L\) for the finitely many
\(h_i\), and set
\[
E=\langle e_B:B\in F\rangle_\Bbbk.
\]
For \(B\in F\), the vector \((r_i\# g_i)e_B\) is either \(0\), or a
nonzero scalar multiple of \(e_{T_i\triangle g_iB}=e_{h_iB}\); 
indeed, it is nonzero precisely when \(T_i\cap g_iB=\varnothing\). Hence
\[
E+S E
\le
\left\langle e_B:B\in F\cup\bigcup_i h_iF\right\rangle_\Bbbk.
\]
Choosing \(F\) sufficiently F\o lner therefore makes \(E\) F\o lner for
the original testing space. Thus \(\Bbbk[X]\) is \(\bigwedge\)-extensively
amenable.

Conversely, suppose that \(\Bbbk[X]\) is \(\bigwedge\)-extensively amenable. For
\(x\in X\), define
\[
\varepsilon_x\colon\bigwedge \Bbbk[X]\to\bigwedge \Bbbk[X],
\qquad
\varepsilon_x(v)=x\wedge v,
\]
let \(p_1\colon\bigwedge \Bbbk[X]\to \Bbbk[X]\) be projection onto degree one,
and let \(x^*\colon \Bbbk[X]\to \Bbbk\) be the coordinate functional of \(x\).
Define contractions by
\[
 \iota_x=((x^*\circ p_1)\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta,
\]
and put
\[
c_x=\varepsilon_x+\iota_x.
\]
On the wedge basis, \(c_x(e_B)=\pm e_{B\triangle\{x\}}\), so \(c_x\) is a signed permutation whose underlying permutation is the toggle
\(B\mapsto B\triangle\{x\}\). Likewise \(G\) acts by signed permutations, with
underlying permutation \(B\mapsto gB\). Let
\(\Gamma\le\operatorname{GL}(\bigwedge \Bbbk[X])\) be the group generated by
all \(c_x\) and the operators induced by \(G\). Its underlying permutation
action on \(\mathcal P_f(X)\) has the same image as the affine action
\(L\rtimes G\curvearrowright L\).

Let \(T\subseteq X\) and \(S\subseteq G\) be finite. By Lemma~\ref{lem:pointed}, there is a nonzero finite graded subcoalgebra \(C\leq\bigwedge \Bbbk[X]\) which is arbitrarily F\o lner for multiplication by the \(x\in T\) and for the \(g\in S\). Since
\(C\) is a subcoalgebra, every coefficient operator of its coproduct preserves
\(C\); in particular,
\[
\iota_x C\le C.
\]
Consequently
\[
c_xC\le C+xC,
\]
so the same \(C\) is arbitrarily F\o lner for the signed permutation operators
\(c_x\), \(x\in T\), and for \(g\in S\). The boundary estimates
\eqref{eq:operator-boundary}, applied to words in these operators, show that
this monomial representation of \(\Gamma\) is amenable.

Gromov's ordering argument for linear actions
\cite{gromov:linear}*{\S3.6} applies unchanged to monomial actions:
the nonzero scalar coefficients do not affect the leading coordinates.
Therefore amenability of this monomial representation implies amenability of
its underlying permutation action on \(\mathcal P_f(X)\). Since this action
has the same image as \(L\rtimes G\curvearrowright L\), the affine action
is amenable. By \cite{juschenko-mattebon-monod-delasalle:extensive}*{Theorem~1.3},
\(G\curvearrowright X\) is extensively amenable.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:char-two}
If ${\mathscr A}$ is a cocommutative Hopf algebra in characteristic $2$, then every extensively amenable ${\mathscr A}$-module is $\wedge$-extensively amenable. If ${\mathscr A}$ is generated as an algebra by its primitive elements, the converse also holds.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
The quotient
\(\operatorname{Sym}(M)\to\operatorname{Sym}(M)/(m^2:m\in M)=\bigwedge M\)
is an ${\mathscr A}$-equivariant Hopf map: in characteristic two,
cocommutativity makes the ideal generated by squares ${\mathscr A}$-stable.
The module-coalgebra quotient theorem
\cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Theorem~1.9} gives the first implication.

For the converse put \(R=\operatorname{Sym}(M)\), let \(R^{[2]}\) be the subalgebra
generated by squares, and put \({\mathscr B}=R\mathbin{\#}{\mathscr A}\),
\({\mathscr K}=R^{[2]}\mathbin{\#}{\mathscr A}\).
Primitives act as derivations and annihilate every square, so ${\mathscr A}$
acts on \(R^{[2]}\) through its counit. The commutative Hopf algebra
\(R^{[2]}\) is amenable by \cite{bartholdi:hopfamen}*{Theorem~2.4};
since the \({\mathscr K}\)-action factors through its regular action,
it is an amenable \({\mathscr K}\)-module. Moreover \({\mathscr B}\otimes_{\mathscr K}\Bbbk=\bigwedge M\),
which is amenable by hypothesis.
Choose a basis of \(M\). Sending a wedge basis monomial to its
squarefree polynomial gives a coalgebra section; separating even
exponents from squarefree exponents gives the colinear right-\({\mathscr K}\)
normal basis. Lemma~\ref{lem:induction} applied to \(V=R^{[2]}\)
proves amenability of \({\mathscr B}\otimes_{\mathscr K} R^{[2]}\simeq R\).
