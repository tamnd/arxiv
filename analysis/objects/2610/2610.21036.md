<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{a-main-general}
Every proper $k$-edge-coloring of a multigraph $G$ can be refined to a rich-neighbor edge-coloring with at most $3k$
colors. In particular, $\chi'_{\mathrm{rn}}(G)\le3\chi'(G)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{a-main-bipartite}
For all positive integers $m$ and $n$,
\(
\chi'_{\mathrm{rn}}(K_{m,n})=m+n-1,
\)
except that $\chi'_{\mathrm{rn}}(K_{2,2})=4$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{a-main-complete}
For every integer $n\ge3$,
\(
\chi'_{\mathrm{rn}}(K_n)=
\begin{cases}
2n-2,&n\in\{4,6\},\\
2n-3,&n\notin\{4,6\}.
\end{cases}
\)
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{a-main-structure}
For every integer $n\ge7$, the rich-edge graph of every optimal rich-neighbor edge-coloring of $K_n$ is a star forest with at most one isolated vertex and at least two edges in each nontrivial component. Moreover,
\[
s(n)=
\begin{cases}
1,&n\text{ odd},\\
2,&n\equiv0\pmod4,\\
3,&n\equiv2\pmod4.
\end{cases}
\]
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{a-local-lower}
If an edge joining $u$ and $v$ is rich in a proper edge-coloring of a multigraph $G$, then the coloring uses at least $d_G(u)+d_G(v)-\mu_G(u,v)$ colors. For a simple graph, this bound is $d_G(u)+d_G(v)-1$. In particular, every nonempty simple graph $G$ with $\delta(G)\ge2$ satisfies $\chi'_{\mathrm{rn}}(G)\ge2\delta(G)-1$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\varphi$ be the coloring, with color set $\mathcal C$. By properness and~\eqref{a-palette-rich-multigraph},
\[
|\mathcal C|
\ge |\Phi(u)\cup\Phi(v)|
= d_G(u)+d_G(v)-\mu_G(u,v).
\]
If $G$ is simple, then $\mu_G(u,v)=1$. If, in addition, $G$ is nonempty and $\delta(G)\ge2$, then every edge is non-isolated, so every rich-neighbor edge-coloring contains a rich edge. Applying the bound to such an edge gives $\chi'_{\mathrm{rn}}(G)\ge2\delta(G)-1$. \hfill $\Box$
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{a-lem-cover}
Let $G$ be a multigraph, and let $F\subseteq G$ be a simple forest each of whose components contains at least two edges.
Then $F$ is total edge-dominating if and only if $V(G)\setminus V(F)$ is independent.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $F$ is total edge-dominating, then every edge of $G$ has an endpoint in $V(F)$, so $V(G)\setminus V(F)$ is independent.
Conversely, suppose that $V(G)\setminus V(F)$ is independent. Every edge in $E(G)\setminus E(F)$ has an endpoint in $V(F)$ and hence is adjacent to an edge of $F$. Every edge of $F$ also has a neighbor in $F$, since its component contains at least two edges. \hfill $\Box$
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{a-lem-star-removal}
Every connected simple graph $G$ of order at least three contains a large star $S$, not necessarily induced, such that
$G-V(S)$ has at most one nontrivial component.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{a-thm-almost-star}
Every connected multigraph $G$ of order at least three contains a simple almost-star forest $F$ such that $V(G)\setminus V(F)$ is independent. Consequently, $F$ is total edge-dominating.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
A forest in the support graph of $G$ can be realized as an isomorphic simple forest in $G$ by choosing one edge from each
corresponding parallel class. Since $G$ and its support graph have the same independent sets, we may assume that $G$ is
simple. We induct on $|V(G)|$. If $|V(G)|=3$, take a spanning two-edge star.

For $|V(G)|\ge4$, Lemma~\ref{a-lem-star-removal} gives a large star $S\cong K_{1,t}$ such that $G-V(S)$ has at most one
nontrivial component. Let $c$ be its center and $x_1,\ldots,x_t$ its leaves. Let $I$ be the set of isolated vertices of $G-V(S)$, and let $H$ be its nontrivial component, if one exists. If $H$ does not exist, take $F=S$. If $|V(H)|\ge3$, let $F_H$ be given by induction and take $F=S\cup F_H$. In both cases, $F$ is an almost-star forest and its uncovered vertex set is independent, since the vertices of $I$ are isolated in $G-V(S)$.

It remains to consider $H\cong K_2$, with edge $uv$. Since $G$ is connected, we may assume that $u$ has a neighbor in $S$. <!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{a-lem-degenerate}
Let $G$ be a multigraph, let $F\subseteq G$ be an almost-star forest, and let $\psi$ be a proper edge-coloring of $G$. Then each graph $B_i$ defined in~\eqref{a-eq-mono-conflict} is $2$-degenerate and hence $3$-colorable.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Fix $i$ and write $B=B_i$. Suppose that $B$ has a nonempty subgraph $H$ with $\delta(H)\ge3$. Put $n=|V(H)|$ and $M=\{e\in M_i\colon\, \widehat e\in V(H)\}$. Since $M$ is a matching, its edges have $2n$ distinct endpoints, and each edge of $F$ witnesses at most one edge of $H$.

Let $P$ be the $P_4$-component of $F$, or the empty graph if no such component exists. Let $E_P$ be the set of edges of $H$ having a witness in $P$, and choose one such witness for each edge of $E_P$. Put $E^*=E(H)\setminus E_P$, $p=|E_P|$, and $m=|E^*|$. Let $U=V(P)\cap V_G(M)$ and $t=|U|$. The chosen witnesses for $E_P$ are distinct edges of $P[U]$. Since $P[U]$ is a forest on $t$ vertices,
\begin{equation}\label{a-eq-path-count}
p\le |E(P[U])|\le
\begin{cases}
0, & \text{if }t=0,\\
t-1, & \text{if }t\ge1.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
For each edge of $E^*$, choose a witness in a star component of $F$. Let $\mathcal S$ be the set of star components containing a chosen witness, and put $s=|\mathcal S|$.

Suppose that an edge $e\in M$ meets neither the center of a star in $\mathcal S$ nor an internal vertex of $P$. Each endpoint of $e$ then lies on at most one chosen witness: a star leaf or an endpoint of $P$ lies on only one edge of $F$, whereas a vertex outside $V(F)$ or the center of a star outside $\mathcal S$ lies on no chosen witness. Thus $d_H(\widehat e)\le2$, a contradiction. Every edge of $M$ therefore meets a center of a star in $\mathcal S$ or an internal vertex of $P$. Since $M$ is a matching and $P$ has at most two internal vertices,
\begin{equation}\label{a-eq-centers}
s\ge n-2.
\end{equation}
If $t=0$, no edge of $M$ meets $P$, so the same argument gives
\begin{equation}\label{a-eq-centers-no-path}
s\ge n.
\end{equation}

The $m$ chosen star witnesses are distinct and have distinct leaf endpoints, since distinct edges of a simple star have distinct leaves and the components of $F$ are vertex-disjoint. These $m$ leaves, the $s$ star centers, and the $t$ vertices
of $U$ are pairwise distinct endpoints of edges in $M$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{a-eq-endpoint-count}
t+s+m\le2n.
\end{equation}
If $t=0$, equations~\eqref{a-eq-centers-no-path} and~\eqref{a-eq-endpoint-count} give $|E(H)|=m\le n$. If $t\ge1$, equations~\eqref{a-eq-path-count}, \eqref{a-eq-centers}, and~\eqref{a-eq-endpoint-count} give
\[
|E(H)|=m+p
\le 2n-s-t+(t-1)
=2n-s-1
\le n+1.
\]
However, $H$ is simple and $\delta(H)\ge3$, so $n\ge4$ and $|E(H)|\ge3n/2>n+1$, a contradiction. Thus $B$ is $2$-degenerate. \hfill $\Box$

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{c-structure}
Let $n\ge4$, and let $\varphi:E(K_n)\to\mathcal C$ be a proper edge-coloring with $|\mathcal C|=2n-3$. Let $R$ be the spanning subgraph formed by all rich edges. Then $R$ is a star forest. If $\varphi$ is a rich-neighbor edge-coloring, then $R$ has no isolated edges and at most one isolated vertex.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For every rich edge $uv$, equation~\eqref{a-palette-rich} and $|\Phi(u)|=|\Phi(v)|=n-1$ give
\begin{equation}\label{c-complement} 
\Phi(u)\cup\Phi(v)=\mathcal C,
\qquad
\Phi(u)\cap\Phi(v)=\{\varphi(uv)\}.
\end{equation}
Suppose that $uv$ and $vw$ are distinct edges of $R$. Put $\alpha=\varphi(uv)$, $\beta=\varphi(vw)$, and $M=\mathcal C\setminus\Phi(v)$. By~\eqref{c-complement},
\(
\Phi(u)=M\cup\{\alpha\}, \Phi(w)=M\cup\{\beta\}.
\)
Since $\alpha\ne\beta$, we have $\Phi(u)\cap\Phi(w)=M$, whose size is $n-2\ge2$. Thus $uw$ is not rich, so $R$ contains no triangle. Suppose now that $wz\in E(R)$ for some $z\notin\{u,v,w\}$, and put $\gamma=\varphi(wz)$. Properness gives
$\gamma\in\Phi(w)\setminus\{\beta\}=M$, and
\eqref{c-complement} yields
\(
\Phi(z)=(\Phi(v)\setminus\{\beta\})\cup\{\gamma\}.
\)
Hence $\Phi(u)\cap\Phi(z)=\{\alpha,\gamma\}$. The color of $uz$ must belong to this intersection, but $\alpha$ is already used on $uv$ and $\gamma$ on $wz$, contradicting properness. Thus $R$ contains neither a triangle nor a path of length three, so every nontrivial component of $R$ is a star.

If $\varphi$ is a rich-neighbor edge-coloring, no component of $R$ consists of a single edge, since that edge would have no rich neighbor. Nor can $R$ have two isolated vertices, since the edge joining them in $K_n$ would have no rich neighbor. \hfill $\Box$

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{c-component-lower}
Let $n\ge4$ be even, and let $\varphi$ be a rich-neighbor edge-coloring of $K_n$ with $2n-3$ colors. Its rich-edge graph has at least two nontrivial components. If $n\equiv2\pmod4$, then it has at least three nontrivial components.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal C$ be the color set, let $\Phi(v)$ be the set of colors on edges incident with $v$, and let $R$ be the rich-edge graph. By Lemma~\ref{c-structure}, every nontrivial component of $R$ is a star with at least two edges, and $R$ has at most one isolated vertex. Also, $R$ contains an edge by the rich-neighbor condition. By~\eqref{c-complement}, if $uv$ is rich, then every edge incident with $v$ other than $uv$ has its color outside $\Phi(u)$.

Suppose that $R$ has exactly one nontrivial component, with center $a$. All but at most one vertex of $K_n-a$ are leaves of this star. Hence every edge of $K_n-a$ has its color in $\mathcal C\setminus\Phi(a)$, which has size $n-2$. Since $n-1$ is odd, each color class in $K_n-a$ has at most $(n-2)/2$ edges. Thus these colors cover at most $(n-2)^2/2<\binom{n-1}{2}$ edges, a contradiction. Therefore, $R$ has at least two nontrivial components.

Now suppose that $n\equiv2\pmod4$ and that $R$ has exactly two nontrivial components. Write $n=2r$, where $r\ge3$ is odd. Let $a,b$ be the centers of the two stars, and let $A,B$ be their leaf sets, respectively. Put $p=|A|$ and $q=|B|$, so $p,q\ge2$. Let $s\in\{0,1\}$ be the number of isolated vertices of $R$. Then $p+q=2r-2-s$. Let $t=|\Phi(a)\cap\Phi(b)|$ and put $T=\mathcal C\setminus(\Phi(a)\cup\Phi(b))$. Since $|\Phi(a)|=|\Phi(b)|=2r-1$, we have $|T|=t-1$. Every edge joining $A$ to $B$ has its color in $T$. The edges from $a$ to $B$ and from $b$ to $A$ have their colors in $\Phi(a)\setminus\Phi(b)$ and $\Phi(b)\setminus\Phi(a)$, respectively. Properness therefore gives $t-1\ge\max\{p,q\}$ and $2r-1-t\ge\max\{p,q\}$. Adding these inequalities yields $\max\{p,q\}\le r-1$. Together with $p+q=2r-2-s$, this implies, after interchanging the stars if necessary, that $q=r-1$, $p=r-1-s$ and $t=r$. Put $Q=\Phi(b)\setminus\Phi(a)$. Then $|Q|=|T|=r-1$ and $\mathcal C\setminus\Phi(a)=T\cup Q$.

At each vertex of $A$, the $r-1$ edges to $B$ use all colors in $T$. Every other incident edge, except the edge to $a$, must therefore have its color in $Q$. If $s=0$, let $J=K_n[A\cup\{b\}]$. If $s=1$, let $x$ be the isolated vertex of $R$ and let $J=K_n[A\cup\{b,x\}]-bx$. In both cases, $J$ has $r$ vertices and $\binom r2-s$ edges, all colored from $Q$.

Since $r$ is odd, each color class in $J$ has at most $(r-1)/2$ edges. Thus the $r-1$ colors in $Q$ cover at most $(r-1)^2/2$ edges. However,
\[
|E(J)|-\frac{(r-1)^2}{2}
=\binom r2-s-\frac{(r-1)^2}{2}
=\frac{p-s}{2}>0,
\]
since $p\ge2$ and $s\le1$. This contradiction proves the second assertion. \hfill $\Box$

To each of the other $h-1$ perfect classes, add one edge from $E(a,A\setminus A')$ and one from $E(b,B\setminus B')$, using every such edge once. This is possible since $|A\setminus A'|=|B\setminus B'|=h-1$. Each class remains a matching and now covers every vertex of $C\cup\{a,b\}$.

\emph{The color sets $\mathcal S_A$ and $\mathcal S_B$.}
Choose $a_*\in A$ and $b_*\in B$. By Lemma~\ref{c-rainbow}, choose one-factorizations of $K_{A\cup\{b\}}$ and $K_{B\cup\{a\}}$ with palettes $\mathcal S_A$ and $\mathcal S_B$, respectively, admitting rainbow matchings $M_A$ and $M_B$ of size $h-1$ that miss exactly $\{b,a_*\}$ and $\{a,b_*\}$. Choose partitions
\(
A=U_A\cup V_A\cup\{a_*\},
B=U_B\cup V_B\cup\{b_*\},
\)
where $|U_A|=|V_A|=|U_B|=|V_B|=h-1$, such that each edge of $M_A$ joins $U_A$ to $V_A$ and each edge of $M_B$ joins $U_B$ to $V_B$.

For each $uv\in M_A$, with $u\in U_A$ and $v\in V_A$, replace $uv$ in its $\mathcal S_A$-class by $cu$ and $xv$. Since $M_A$ is rainbow, each class is modified at most once and remains a matching. There are $(k-1)-(h-1)=h$ unmodified classes. Add the edges of $E(c,C_0)$ to these classes, one edge per class. Every $\mathcal S_A$-class now covers every vertex of $A\cup\{b,c\}$. Apply the analogous replacements to $M_B$, and add the edges of $E(c,C_1)$ to the $h$ unmodified $\mathcal S_B$-classes, one edge per class. Every $\mathcal S_B$-class then covers every vertex of $B\cup\{a,c\}$. Reserve the removed edges of $M_A$ and $M_B$ for $\mathcal P_{AB}$.

\emph{The remaining color classes.}
Apply Lemma~\ref{c-switched-bipartite} with $(u,v)=(c,x)$, $A_1=V_A$, and $B_1=U_B$. Use $k$ colors of $\mathcal P_{AB}$ for the resulting one-factorization of the graph with edge set
\[
\begin{aligned}
E(A,B)\cup\{cx\}
&\cup E(c,V_A\cup(B\setminus U_B))\cup E(x,(A\setminus V_A)\cup U_B).
\end{aligned}
\]
Assign the remaining color of $\mathcal P_{AB}$ to
\(
M_A\cup M_B\cup\{ca_*,xb_*\}.
\)
This is a perfect matching on $A\cup B\cup\{c,x\}$ and contains no edge of the preceding graph. Thus every vertex of $A\cup B\cup\{c,x\}$ sees every color in $\mathcal P_{AB}$. Finally, set
\(
\varphi(ac)=\varphi(bx)=\alpha,
\varphi(bc)=\varphi(ax)=\beta.
\)

\emph{Verification.}
Every color class is a matching. The edges of $M_A$ and $M_B$ have been removed from their original classes and assigned to $\mathcal P_{AB}$. The assignments at $c$ and $x$ partition their edges to $A$ as follows:
\[
\begin{aligned}
E(c,A)
 &=E(c,V_A\cup\{a_*\})\cup E(c,U_A),\\
E(x,A)
 &=E(x,U_A\cup\{a_*\})\cup E(x,V_A),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the first set in each row uses $\mathcal P_{AB}$ and the second uses $\mathcal S_A$. The corresponding partitions for $B$ use $\mathcal P_{AB}$ and $\mathcal S_B$. The edges of $E(c,C_0)$ and $E(c,C_1)$ use $\mathcal S_A$ and $\mathcal S_B$, respectively. Among the six edges on $\{a,b,c,x\}$, the edge $ab$ uses $\mathcal S_C$, the edge $cx$ uses $\mathcal P_{AB}$, and the other four use $\mathcal O$. Together with the first step, these assignments partition $E(K_{3k+2})$.

Each center has the palette in Table~\ref{c-tab-palettes}. Every vertex of $A$ sees all colors in $\mathcal P_{AB}\cup\mathcal P_{AC}\cup\mathcal S_A$ on edges other than its star edge. These $3k$ colors form its entire non-star palette. The same argument applies to $B$ and $C$, so all palette equalities in Table~\ref{c-tab-palettes} hold. Lemma~\ref{c-three-palettes} completes the proof. \hfill $\Box$

Write $k=2h$, where $h\ge3$ is odd. Use the vertex partition in Lemma~\ref{c-three-palettes} with $|A|=|B|=k-2$ and $|C|=k$. Choose disjoint color sets with the sizes listed for Construction II in Table~\ref{c-tab-families}, and put $\mathcal O=\{\alpha,\beta\}$. These sets contain $6k-3$ colors in total. Partition $A=A_0\cup A_1$ and $B=B_0\cup B_1$, where each part has size $h-1$. Write $C=\{v_0,\ldots,v_{k-1}\}$, and put $C_0=\{v_{2j}:0\le j<h\}$ and $C_1=\{v_{2j+1}:0\le j<h\}$, with subscripts on the vertices $v_i$ taken modulo $k$.

\emph{The color sets $\mathcal P_{AB}$,
$\mathcal S_A$, and $\mathcal S_B$.} Apply Lemma~\ref{c-switched-bipartite} with $(u,v)=(c,x)$ and the subsets $A_1,B_1$. Use $\mathcal P_{AB}$ for the resulting one-factorization of the graph with edge set
\begin{equation}\label{c-c-pab}
E(A,B)\cup E(c,A_1\cup B_0)
\cup E(x,A_0\cup B_1)\cup\{cx\}.
\end{equation}

Take a one-factorization of $K_{A\cup\{b,w\}}$, where $w$ is an auxiliary vertex, and assign its $k-1$ factors the colors in $\mathcal S_A$. In each factor, replace the edge $wu$ by $cu$ if $u\in A_0$, by $xu$ if $u\in A_1$, and by $bx$ if $u=b$. Exactly $h-1$ classes now cover $c$. Add the $h$ edges of $E(c,C_0)$ to the remaining classes, one edge per class. Each class remains a matching and covers $A\cup\{b,c\}$. The resulting classes partition $E(K_A)\cup E(b,A)\cup E(c,A_0) \cup E(x,A_1)\cup\{bx\}\cup E(c,C_0)$.

Similarly, use $\mathcal S_B$ for a one-factorization of $K_{B\cup\{a,w\}}$. Replace $wu$ by $cu$ if $u\in B_1$, by $xu$ if $u\in B_0$, and by $ax$ if $u=a$. Add the edges of $E(c,C_1)$ to the $h$ classes missing $c$, one edge per class. Each resulting class is a matching covering $B\cup\{a,c\}$, and these classes partition $E(K_B)\cup E(a,B)\cup E(c,B_1) \cup E(x,B_0)\cup\{ax\}\cup E(c,C_1)$.

\emph{The color sets $\mathcal P_{AC}$, $\mathcal P_{BC}$, and $\mathcal O$.} Choose $u_A\in A$ and $u_B\in B$, and set $\varphi(ac)=\varphi(bu_B)=\alpha$ and $\varphi(bc)=\varphi(au_A)=\beta$. Both color classes are matchings. Order $A\cup\{b,x\}$ as $\ell_0=b,\ell_1=x,\ell_2,\ldots,\ell_{k-1}$. The matchings $M_t=\{\ell_i v_{t+i}:0\le i<k\}$, where $0\le t<k$, form a one-factorization of $K_{A\cup\{b,x\},C}$. Assign them the colors in $\mathcal P_{AC}$. For each even $t$, replace $bv_t,xv_{t+1}$ by $bu_t^B,v_tv_{t+1}$, where the $u_t^B$ are distinct vertices of $B\setminus\{u_B\}$. These choices are possible since $k-3=2h-3\ge h$. Each class remains a matching covering $A\cup C\cup\{b\}$. Their edge sets partition $E(A,C)\cup E(b,C_1)\cup E(x,C_0) \cup\{bu_t^B:0\le t<k,\ t\text{ even}\}\cup D_0$, where $D_0=\{v_{2i}v_{2i+1}:0\le i<h\}$.

For $\mathcal P_{BC}$, use the corresponding cyclic one-factorization of $K_{B\cup\{a,x\},C}$, with $a,x$ as the first two vertices. For each odd $t$, replace $av_t,xv_{t+1}$ by $au_t^A,v_tv_{t+1}$, using distinct vertices $u_t^A\in A\setminus\{u_A\}$. Each class remains a matching covering $B\cup C\cup\{a\}$. Their edge sets partition $E(B,C)\cup E(a,C_0)\cup E(x,C_1) \cup\{au_t^A:0\le t<k,\ t\text{ odd}\}\cup D_1$, where $D_1=\{v_{2i+1}v_{2i+2}:0\le i<h\}$.

\emph{The color set $\mathcal S_C$.} Since $h$ is odd, for each $j\in\{0,1\}$, decompose $K_{C_j}$ into near-perfect matchings $N_0^j,\ldots,N_{h-1}^j$, where $N_i^j$ misses $v_{2i+j}$. Use $h$ colors of $\mathcal S_C$ for the matchings $N_i^0\cup N_i^1\cup\{bv_{2i},av_{2i+1}\}$, where $0\le i<h$. Each covers $C\cup\{a,b\}$.

The remaining uncolored edges within $C$ form $K_{C_0,C_1}-D_0-D_1$. They decompose into the perfect matchings $F_d=\{v_{2i}v_{2(i+d)+1}:0\le i<h\}$, where $1\le d\le h-2$. Indeed, $D_0$ and $D_1$ correspond to cyclic shifts $0$ and $-1$ modulo $h$, respectively, so these are precisely the remaining shifts. Add $ab$ to $F_1$. To each of the other $h-3$ factors, add one unused edge of $E(a,A)$ and one unused edge of $E(b,B)$, using every such edge once. Each of these stars has exactly $(k-2)-1-h=h-3$ unused edges, so these additions are possible. Assign the remaining $h-2$ colors of $\mathcal S_C$ to the augmented factors. When $h=3$, only $F_1$ occurs and no star edges remain. Every $\mathcal S_C$-class is a matching covering $C\cup\{a,b\}$.

\emph{Verification.}
The listed edge sets partition $E(K_{3k})$. The edges inside $C$ are partitioned by $D_0,D_1$ and the restrictions of the $\mathcal S_C$-classes to $C$. The edges from $c,x$ to $A,B$ are partitioned by \eqref{c-c-pab} and the two auxiliary-vertex constructions, while $E(c,C)$ is split between $\mathcal S_A$ and $\mathcal S_B$. The edges of $E(a,A)$ are divided among $\mathcal O,\mathcal P_{BC},\mathcal S_C$ in numbers $1,h,h-3$, respectively. The same counts hold for $E(b,B)$, with $\mathcal P_{AC}$ in place of $\mathcal P_{BC}$. On $\{a,b,c,x\}$, the edge $ab$ uses $\mathcal S_C$, the edges $ac,bc$ use $\mathcal O$, the edges $ax,bx$ use $\mathcal S_B,\mathcal S_A$, respectively, and $cx$ uses $\mathcal P_{AB}$. Every color class is a matching.

The center palettes are those in Table~\ref{c-tab-palettes}. Every leaf receives all colors in its prescribed non-star palette on non-star edges. Each such palette has $3k-2$ colors, equal to the number of incident non-star edges, so the palette equalities in the table hold. Lemma~\ref{c-three-palettes} completes the proof. \hfill $\Box$

Let $n\ge7$. By Theorem~\ref{a-main-complete}, every optimal rich-neighbor edge-coloring of $K_n$ uses $2n-3$ colors. Lemma~\ref{c-structure} therefore gives the asserted structure of its full rich-edge graph. Every such coloring contains a rich edge, so $s(n)\ge1$. Lemma~\ref{c-component-lower} gives $s(n)\ge2$ for even $n$ and $s(n)\ge3$ when $n\equiv2\pmod4$.

For the upper bounds, Lemma~\ref{c-one-star} gives a coloring with a spanning rich star when $n$ is odd. Proposition~\ref{c-two-stars} gives a coloring with two vertex-disjoint rich stars covering all vertices when $n\equiv0\pmod4$. All these colorings use $2n-3$ colors and hence are optimal.

In each case, every nontrivial component of the full rich-edge graph meets a prescribed star and therefore contains that star, since at most one vertex is uncovered.
