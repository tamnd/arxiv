The chromatic number of the $(n-1)$-dimensional associahedron is at most $6\cdot \log_2 n + 18$.

<!-- head-1: body_md -->
Given the above scheme, all that remains to prove is that a single rotation always changes the color of the tree.

Consider a rotation that transforms a tree $T$ to $T'$. We assume the rotation is on two nodes $x$ and $y$ where $y$ is the right child of $x$ in $T$ and $x$ becomes the left child of $y$ in $T'$; the reverse case is symmetric. We consider 16 cases based on whether $x$ and $y$ are each left or right heavy before and after the rotation. These are illustrated in Figure~\ref{f1}. These are split into four groups, those where the left color changes, those where the heavy child color changes, and finally those cases that are impossible.

\begin{description}
    \item[\textcolor{olive}{Cases II-V, VIII, IX, XII-XV (Left color changes)}] In these cases the number of left heavy nodes, $|L(T)|$ changes by 1 or 2, and thus $c_\ell(T)$ which is $|L(T)| \bmod 3$ changes.
    \item[\textcolor{teal}{Cases I and XVI (Heavy child color changes)}] For I (XVI is symmetric): Here the increase in $\sum_{x \in H} \log_2 s(x)$ is $\log_2 (|A|+|B|+1) - \log_2 |B| = \log_2 \frac{|A|+|B|+1}{|B|}$. 
    As $|A|>|B|$, $\log \frac{|A|+|B|+1}{|B|}\geq \log_2 \frac{|B|+|B|+1}{|B|}>1$. Also, $\log_2 \frac{|A|+|B|+1}{|B|}\leq \log_2 n$. Thus $\sum_{x \in H} \log_2 s(x)$ changes by at least 1 and at most $\log_2 n$, which causes $c_h(T) = \left\lceil  \sum_{x \in H} \log_2 s(x) \right\rceil \bmod \lceil \log_2n+1 \rceil$ to change.
    \item[\textcolor{purple}{Case X}] The key point of this case is that the heavy paths do not change, with the one exception that $x$ and $y$, which are adjacent on the same heavy path, have their positions change; $x$ remains right heavy and $y$ remains left heavy. Either $x$ before (right heavy) and $y$ after (left heavy) are at even depth on their path or $y$ before (left heavy) and $x$ after (right heavy) are at even depth; in either case the number of left heavy nodes of even path depth changes by one and thus the path color changes.
    \item[\textcolor{orange}{Cases VI, VII and XI (Impossible)}] These are impossible. For VI (XI is symmetric) the following contradictions occur:
    \begin{itemize}[topsep=0pt]
        \item $|A|>|B|+|C|+1>|B|$ and
        \item $|A|\leq |B|$
    \end{itemize}
    For VII:
      \begin{itemize}[topsep=0pt]
        \item $|A|>|B|+|C|+1>|C|$ and
        \item $|C|\geq |A|+|B|+1>|A|$
    \end{itemize}  
\end{description}
