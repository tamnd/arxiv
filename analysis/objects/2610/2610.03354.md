\label{ss3excGrad}

The method of \cite{LSh} applies to a~${\mathbb  Z}$-graded ${\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus_{i\geq-d}\ {\mathfrak{g}}_i$ such
that
\begin{equation}\label{cond3exc}
\begin{minipage}[l]{14cm}
$\bullet$ the grading is symmetric, of depth $d$, without gaps;\\
$\bullet$ ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$ generates ${\mathfrak{g}}_-$ \textup{(}the Weisfeiler
property\textup{)};\\
$\bullet$ the ${\mathfrak{g}}_0$-action on each ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-k}$, where $k>0$, is irreducible.
\end{minipage}
\end{equation}
Every ${\mathbb  Z}$-grading of ${\mathfrak{g}}$ is given by an element $h$ of a~maximal toral subalgebra
such that $\alpha(h)\in{\mathbb  Z}$ for every root $\alpha$, the degree of ${\mathfrak{g}}_\alpha$
being~$\alpha(h)$. Since all the root spaces are $1$-dimensional, and ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-k}$
consists of root vectors only for $k>0$, every ${\mathfrak{g}}_0$-submodule of ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-k}$ is
spanned by root vectors, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{irr3exc}
\text{${\mathfrak{g}}_{-k}$ is ${\mathfrak{g}}_0$-irreducible}\iff\text{the set of roots of ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-k}$
is connected under adding roots of degree $0$,}
\end{equation}
which turns the classification of the gradings satisfying \eqref{cond3exc} into
a~finite check on the root system. Its result is as follows.

 \begin{Statement}[The gradings to which the method applies: $2+5+8$]\label{StGr3exc} Up to isomorphism, the gradings of the three
exceptions \eqref{3exc} satisfying \eqref{cond3exc} are exactly the ones listed
in tables \eqref{tabOspGr}--\eqref{tabAbGr}: two for ${\mathfrak{osp}}_a(4|2)$, five for
${\mathfrak{ag}}(2)$, eight for ${\mathfrak{ab}}(3)$; all of them are of depth~$\leq3$. If depth $4$ is
allowed, exactly one more grading appears for ${\mathfrak{ag}}(2)$ and one for ${\mathfrak{ab}}(3)$,
namely
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
{\mathfrak{ag}}(2):&1|1,\ 2|2,\ 1|1,\ 2|2,\ \mathbf{5|2},\ 2|2,\ 1|1,\ 2|2,\ 1|1,\\
{\mathfrak{ab}}(3):&3|0,\ 0|2,\ 3|0,\ 0|6,\ \mathbf{12|0},\ 0|6,\ 3|0,\ 0|2,\ 3|0

\textup{(}the superdimensions of the graded components, the $0$th one in
boldface\textup{)}; there are no gradings of depth~$\geq5$. The grading elements $h$

\@startsection{subsection}{2}{0pt} 
    {-1ex \@plus -.4ex \@minus -.2ex} 
    {-.7em plus .3em} 
    {\normalfont\normalsize\bfseries\boldmath}{Depth $1$ occurs only twice, and it is the Tits--Kantor--Koecher
grading.}\label{ssTKK} The Lie superalgebra ${\mathfrak{osp}}_a(4|2)$ has exactly one grading of
depth $1$, with ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$ of $\sdim=(2|2)$, and ${\mathfrak{ab}}(3)$ has exactly one, with ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$
of $\sdim=(6|4)$, whereas ${\mathfrak{ag}}(2)$ has \textbf{none}. This matches the
Tits--Kantor--Koecher picture: the ${\mathbb  Z}$-gradings of depth $1$ correspond to
Jordan superalgebras, see \cite{K77, Kap}, and ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$ is, respectively, the
$4$-dimensional Jordan superalgebra $D_t$ of the $1$-parameter family (whence the
parameter $a$) and the $10$-dimensional Kac Jordan superalgebra $K_{10}$, whose even part
is the Jordan algebra of a~non-degenerate quadratic form on ${\mathbb  C}^{1+5}$ and whose odd
part is the spinor module $\spin_5$ of ${\mathfrak{o}}(5)={\mathfrak{sp}}(4)$, i.e., the tautological
${\mathfrak{sp}}(4)$-module, $\dim\spin_5=4$, --- exactly what the root data of
table \eqref{tabAbGr} give. For ${\mathfrak{ag}}(2)$, the corresponding Kaplansky superalgebra $K_3$,
$\sdim K_3=(1|2)$, is not unital and, accordingly, no grading of depth $1$ exists; the
shallowest grading of ${\mathfrak{ag}}(2)$ is the contact one, $N=1$ in table \eqref{tabAgGr}.

Consequently, of the three exceptions the complete answer given in \cite{LSh} for
depth-$1$ ambients covers only ${\mathfrak{osp}}_a(4|2)$ and ${\mathfrak{ab}}(3)$; for ${\mathfrak{ag}}(2)$ one has
to work in depth $2$ from the start.

\@startsection{subsection}{2}{0pt} 
    {-1ex \@plus -.4ex \@minus -.2ex} 
    {-.7em plus .3em} 
    {\normalfont\normalsize\bfseries\boldmath}{The uniform contact family.}\label{ssContactFamily} Each of the three exceptions has
a~contact grading, with ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-2}={\mathbb  C}$ even and ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$ purely odd:
\begin{equation}\label{contactfamily3exc}
{\mathfrak{osp}}_a(4|2)\subset {\mathfrak{k}}(1|4),\qquad {\mathfrak{ag}}(2)\subset{\mathfrak{k}}(1|7),\qquad
{\mathfrak{ab}}(3)\subset{\mathfrak{k}}(1|8),
\end{equation}
with ${\mathfrak{g}}_0={\mathfrak{c}}({\mathfrak{o}}(4))$, ${\mathfrak{c}}({\mathfrak{g}}(2))$, ${\mathfrak{c}}({\mathfrak{o}}(7))$ and
${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}=\Pi(\id_{{\mathfrak{o}}(4)})$, $\Pi(\pi_1)$, $\Pi(\spin_7)$, respectively; the
inclusions ${\mathfrak{o}}(4)={\mathfrak{o}}(4)$, ${\mathfrak{g}}(2)\subset{\mathfrak{o}}(7)$ and ${\mathfrak{o}}(7)\subset{\mathfrak{o}}(8)$ (the spinor
embedding) are the reason why ${\mathfrak{g}}_0\subseteq{\mathfrak{k}}(1|n)_0$ in each case. In the
first case, ${\mathfrak{g}}_0={\mathfrak{k}}(1|4)_0$ and ${\mathfrak{g}}_{\leq0}={\mathfrak{k}}(1|4)_{\leq0}$.

This answers a~question raised in \cite{LSh}, where Poletaeva's embedding \cite{Po1} of
${\mathfrak{osp}}_a(4|2)$ into a~stringy Lie superalgebra is called unclear: the grading $N=2$ of
table \eqref{tabOspGr} embeds ${\mathfrak{osp}}_a(4|2)$ into ${\mathfrak{k}}(1|4)$, and hence into the stringy
${\mathfrak{k}}^L(1|4)$, and it does so uniformly in $a$; the parameter appears there as the mixing
parameter of the two copies of $\id_{{\mathfrak{o}}(4)}$ inside
${\mathfrak{k}}(1|4)_1=\Span(K_{t\xi_i})\oplus\Lambda^3(\xi)$. That the Cartan prolong of the
negative part of this grading is the whole ${\mathfrak{k}}(1|4)$ is also shown in \cite{EKT}.

 \begin{Remark}[How these gradings sit
among the parabolic ones]\label{RParab3exc} \textup{1)} Up to isomorphism, ${\mathfrak{osp}}_a(4|2)$
has six parabolic subalgebras, see \cite{EKT}, i.e., six ${\mathbb  Z}$-gradings defined by the
marked nodes of its Dynkin diagrams. The grading $N=1$ of table \eqref{tabOspGr} is the
grading of depth~$1$ among them, with ${\mathfrak{g}}_0\simeq{\mathfrak{gl}}(2|1)$, and the grading $N=2$ is
the contact one, with ${\mathfrak{g}}_0={\mathfrak{c}}({\mathfrak{o}}(4))$. Each of the other four gradings defined by parabolic subalgebras violates the last of the
conditions \eqref{cond3exc} already at ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$. In two of them, the grading of depth
$4$ given by the Borel subalgebra and the grading of depth $3$ with ${\mathfrak{g}}_0={\mathfrak{gl}}(2)\oplus{\mathbb  C}$,
the Lie algebra ${\mathfrak{g}}_0$ is purely even, whereas $\sdim{\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}=(2|1)$, resp.~$(1|2)$; so
the even and the odd parts of ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$ are two ${\mathfrak{g}}_0$-submodules. 

In the grading of
depth $3$ given by the other Borel subalgebra, ${\mathfrak{g}}_0={\mathfrak{t}}$ and $\sdim{\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}=(0|3)$, so
every root line of ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$ is a~submodule. In the grading of depth $2$ with
${\mathfrak{g}}_0\simeq{\mathfrak{gl}}(1|1)\oplus{\mathbb  C}$, of $\sdim(3|2)$, the even part of ${\mathfrak{g}}_0$ is ${\mathfrak{t}}$, and
the odd one is spanned by the root vectors of two opposite isotropic roots $\pm\beta$;
since $2\beta$ is not a~root and $\gamma+2\beta$ is a~root for no root $\gamma$ of
${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$, the four roots of ${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$, of $\sdim(2|2)$, split under adding $\pm\beta$
into chains of length $\leq2$, and by the criterion \eqref{irr3exc} the module
${\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}$ is reducible.

\textup{2)} For ${\mathfrak{ag}}(2)$ and ${\mathfrak{ab}}(3)$, which have $19$ and $55$ parabolic subalgebras,
the Cartan prolong of ${\mathfrak{g}}_-$ is ${\mathfrak{g}}$ itself for every parabolic except the following
ones, see \cite{KLl}: for ${\mathfrak{ag}}(2)$, the two contact gradings, i.e., the gradings $N=1$
and $N=2$ of table \eqref{tabAgGr}, with the ambients ${\mathfrak{k}}(1|7)$ and ${\mathfrak{k}}(5|4)$; for
${\mathfrak{ab}}(3)$, the grading of depth $1$ and the two contact ones, i.e., the gradings $N=1$,
$N=2$ and $N=3$ of table \eqref{tabAbGr}, with the ambients $({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1},{\mathfrak{g}}_0)_*$,
${\mathfrak{k}}(1|8)$ and ${\mathfrak{k}}(7|4)$. For ${\mathfrak{osp}}_a(4|2)$, the picture is the opposite one: the
Cartan prolong of ${\mathfrak{g}}_-$ is larger than ${\mathfrak{g}}$ for five of its six parabolic subalgebras,
and equal to ${\mathfrak{g}}$ only for the grading of depth $3$ with $\sdim{\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}=(0|3)$, see
\cite{EKT}; for the gradings of table \eqref{tabOspGr} it is ${\mathfrak{vect}}(2|2)$ for $N=1$ and
${\mathfrak{k}}(1|4)$ for $N=2$.
\end{Remark}
