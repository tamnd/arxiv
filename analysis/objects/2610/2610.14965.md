<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:coverage}
Under the stated assumptions, all risk and paired-cost intervals used at
all visited looks and generations simultaneously cover their respective
population quantities with probability at least $1-\alpha_s$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For a fixed generation, condition on its incoming history and sampled
population. Each risk count is binomial and each paired-cost sequence is
i.i.d. and bounded. Each interval therefore has conditional noncoverage at
most $\varepsilon$. A union bound over the predeclared looks, candidates,
and unordered pairs, followed by averaging over the incoming history and
summing over at most $G_{\max}$ generations, gives $Q\varepsilon=\alpha_s$.
Stopping a race at a visited look selects a subset of these simultaneous
statements; it does not introduce an unadjusted optional-stopping claim.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:update}
On the simultaneous coverage event, for every visited acyclic look,
\begin{align}
\|\widehat{\bar z}_g-\bar z_g^*\|_2&\le E_z, \ \ 
\|\widehat S_g-S_g^*\|_2\le E_S,\label{eq:stat-bounds}\\
\|\widehat m_{g+1}-m_{g+1}^*\|_2&\le\sigma_g\|M_g\|_2 E_z,
 \ \ \|\widehat s_{g+1}-s_{g+1}^*\|_2\le h,\label{eq:mean-path-bounds}\\
\|\widehat M_{g+1}-M_{g+1}^*\|_2
&\le\|M_g\|_2\left[\frac{c_1}{2}(2\|\widehat s_{g+1}\|_2+h)h
+\frac{c_\mu}{2}E_S\right],\label{eq:matrix-bound}\\
\left|\log\frac{\widehat\sigma_{g+1}}{\sigma_{g+1}^*}\right|
&\le \frac{c_s}{d_\sigma\chi_n}h.\label{eq:scale-bound}
\end{align}
Vector norms are Euclidean and matrix norms are spectral.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The true ordering is an admissible extension. Monotonicity of the extended
weights gives $a_i^*\in[a_i^-,a_i^+]$, hence
$|\widehat a_i-a_i^*|\le b_i$. The triangle inequality and
$\|z_i z_i^{\mathsf T}\|_2=\|z_i\|_2^2$ prove
\eqref{eq:stat-bounds}. Substitution into the mean and path recursions
proves~\eqref{eq:mean-path-bounds}. Writing
$e=\widehat s_{g+1}-s_{g+1}^*$ gives
\[
\|\widehat s_{g+1}\widehat s_{g+1}^{\mathsf T}
-s_{g+1}^*s_{g+1}^{*\mathsf T}\|_2
\le 2\|\widehat s_{g+1}\|_2\|e\|_2+\|e\|_2^2.
\]
The matrix recursion and submultiplicativity imply
\eqref{eq:matrix-bound}. Taking the difference of the two log-scale
updates and applying the reverse triangle inequality gives
\eqref{eq:scale-bound}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:s5-discrepancy}
	On the simultaneous coverage event, consider the chosen-order
	and true-order updates from the same incoming search state
	and with the same scenario and nonmonotone-memory history.
	
	(i) If $E_z>0$, the line search is skipped and the bounds of
	Proposition~\ref{prop:update} apply directly.
	
	(ii) If $E_z=0$, then $E_S=0$, and the chosen- and true-order
	recombination directions coincide. Under the prescribed
	deterministic line-search rule, both updates evaluate the same
	trial points and select the same multiplier. Consequently,
	\[
	\widehat m_{g+1}^{\mathrm{ls}}=m_{g+1}^{\mathrm{ls},*},
	\qquad
	\widehat s_{g+1}=s_{g+1}^*,
	\qquad
	\widehat M_{g+1}=M_{g+1}^*,
	\qquad
	\widehat\sigma_{g+1}=\sigma_{g+1}^*.
	\]
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Part (i) follows from the line-search activation rule and
	Proposition~\ref{prop:update}. For part (ii), each term
	$b_i\|z_i\|_2$ in the definition of $E_z$ is nonnegative.
	Thus $E_z=0$ implies $b_i\|z_i\|_2=0$ for every $i$,
	and consequently $E_S=0$. Proposition~\ref{prop:update}
	then gives equality of the standard recombination direction,
	evolution path, transformation matrix, and global scale.
	With a common incoming history and scenario prefix, the
	deterministic line-search construction evaluates the same
	points and selects the same multiplier under both orderings.
	The refined means therefore coincide.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:validation}
	If the frozen controller has true failure probability $p>\delta$ under
	the test law and the $N$ test scenarios are i.i.d. and independent of all
	selection data, then
	$\mathbb P\{U_N(F_N;\alpha_v)\le\delta\}\le\alpha_v$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Condition on the frozen controller. Exact one-sided binomial coverage gives
	$\mathbb P\{p>U_N(F_N;\alpha_v)\}\le\alpha_v$. When $p>\delta$, the
	acceptance event is contained in that noncoverage event. The same statement
	holds after averaging over any independent training and development
	selection procedure.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:computational-complexity}
	Consider the proposed framework under the computational
	model specified above. Assume that the robot state and
	control dimensions are fixed, and that predicted and executed
	robot transitions, including obstacle and route processing,
	require at most
	$\mathcal{O}(N_o+N_\Gamma+1)$ arithmetic operations.
	
	Assume further that the \texttt{MA-ES} transformation-matrix
	update is evaluated using conventional dense matrix
	operations, that pairwise racing statistics are recomputed
	directly at each look, and that the comparison graph is
	processed using a cubic-time transitive-closure algorithm.
	Let $C_{\mathrm{CP}}$ denote an upper bound on the
	arithmetic work required to compute one numerical
	Clopper--Pearson confidence endpoint using the
	prescribed numerical tolerance and quantile algorithm.
	
	Then the worst-case arithmetic complexity of the offline
	training procedure is bounded by
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:total-computational-complexity}
		\begin{aligned}
			\mathcal{O}\Bigl(
			&E_{\max}\bigl[TRBH(N_o+N_\Gamma+1)+n\bigr]+G_{\max}\Bigl[
			\lambda n^2+n^3\\
			&\quad+L_{\mathrm{look}}
			\bigl(\lambda^2K_{\max}+\lambda^3+n^3
			+\lambda C_{\mathrm{CP}}\bigr)+m_{\mathrm{nm}}\lambda
			\log(m_{\mathrm{nm}}\lambda+1)
			\Bigr]\Bigr).
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	In particular, the arithmetic complexity of one online
	\texttt{MPPI} control update is
	\begin{equation}
		\label{eq:mppi-online-complexity}
		\mathcal{O}\bigl(RBH(N_o+N_\Gamma+1)\bigr).
	\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
At each control instant, \texttt{MPPI} performs $R$
	sampling/update passes, each propagating $B$ predicted
	trajectories over $H$ steps. Each predicted transition
	and its associated planning-score evaluation require at
	most $\mathcal{O}(N_o+N_\Gamma+1)$ operations.
	Gaussian perturbation generation, importance-weight
	computation, and nominal-command updating require
	$\mathcal{O}(RBH)$ additional operations because the
	control dimension is fixed. This establishes
	\eqref{eq:mppi-online-complexity}.
	
	A complete closed-loop trial contains at most $T$
	control instants. The executed-state propagation,
	collision checking, and outer-cost evaluation are
	absorbed into the per-control-instant bound. Hence,
	one complete trial requires at most
	$\mathcal{O}(TRBH(N_o+N_\Gamma+1))$ operations.
	By the hard training-budget assumption, the total
	robotic-simulation cost is bounded by
	$\mathcal{O}(E_{\max}TRBH(N_o+N_\Gamma+1))$.
	
	Within each generation, drawing and transforming
	$\lambda$ Gaussian mutation vectors requires
	$\mathcal{O}(\lambda n^2)$ operations. Forming the
	weighted second-moment matrix requires at most
	$\mathcal{O}(\lambda n^2)$ additional operations.
	The standard \texttt{MA-ES} mean, path, matrix, and
	scale recursions are given in
	\eqref{eq:mean}--\eqref{eq:scale}.
	Evaluating the transformation-matrix recursion
	\eqref{eq:matrix} through a conventional dense
	matrix--matrix multiplication requires
	$\mathcal{O}(n^3)$ operations.
	The remaining state updates are absorbed into
	these bounds. Thus, the standard per-generation
	adaptation cost is bounded by
	$\mathcal{O}(\lambda n^2+n^3)$.
	
	At each racing look, directly recomputing all
	pairwise cost-difference statistics using at most
	$K_{\max}$ observations per candidate requires
	$\mathcal{O}(\lambda^2K_{\max})$ operations.
	Computing the transitive closure of the comparison
	graph requires $\mathcal{O}(\lambda^3)$ operations.
	The admissible rank intervals, chosen ordering,
	and update-discrepancy bounds can be computed within
	these bounds, apart from the spectral-norm calculation.
	
	The spectral norm of the current transformation matrix,
	which enters the update-discrepancy bounds, can be
	computed by a standard singular-value decomposition
	in $\mathcal{O}(n^3)$ operations.
	Computing the risk-confidence endpoints for the
	$\lambda$ candidates requires at most
	$\mathcal{O}(\lambda C_{\mathrm{CP}})$ operations.
	Consequently, the racing overhead per generation is
	bounded by
	\[
	\mathcal{O}\left(
	L_{\mathrm{look}}
	\left[
	\lambda^2K_{\max}+\lambda^3+n^3
	+\lambda C_{\mathrm{CP}}
	\right]
	\right).
	\]
	
	The nonmonotone reference defined in
	\eqref{eq:ls-memory}--\eqref{eq:ls-min-med-max}
	contains at most $m_{\mathrm{nm}}\lambda$
	stored population-cost values. Computing the
	required minimum, median, and maximum by sorting
	requires at most
	\[
	\mathcal{O}\left(
	m_{\mathrm{nm}}\lambda
	\log(m_{\mathrm{nm}}\lambda+1)
	\right)
	\]
	operations per generation.
	
	Each evaluated line-search point defined by
	\eqref{eq:ls-trial-point} requires
	$\mathcal{O}(n)$ additional arithmetic operations
	to form its latent coordinates and transform them
	to admissible controller parameters.
	
	By the evaluation accounting in
	\eqref{eq:ls-training-budget}, and since each
	line-search point is evaluated on $K_g\geq1$
	complete scenarios,
	\[
	\sum_{g=0}^{G-1}Q_g
	\leq
	\sum_{g=0}^{G-1}Q_gK_g
	\leq E_{\max}.
	\]
	Therefore, the total line-search point-construction
	overhead is bounded by $\mathcal{O}(E_{\max}n)$.
	The corresponding closed-loop evaluations are
	already counted in the robotic-simulation cost.
	
	Finally, summing the adaptation, racing, and
	nonmonotone-memory overhead over at most
	$G_{\max}$ generations and adding the total
	simulation and line-search point-construction costs
	establishes \eqref{eq:total-computational-complexity}.
