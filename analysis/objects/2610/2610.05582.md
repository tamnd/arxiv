<!-- maintheorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
  Consistently with $\textsf{MA}$ + $2^{\omega} = \omega_{2}$ there is a family of
  $2^{\omega_{1}}$-many coherent Aronszajn trees
  which pairwise contain no club-isomorphic subtrees.
  Consequently,
  $\textsf{MA}$ + $2^{\omega} = \omega_{2}$ is consistent with the existence of
  $2^{\omega_{1}}$-many Countryman lines which are pairwise far.
<!-- end maintheorem-1 -->
<!-- fact-1: body_md -->
Let $T$ be suitable. Then $T$ is an Aronszajn tree, every uncountable
  subset of $T$ contains an uncountable antichain, and $T$ remains
  suitable in any $\omega_{1}$-preserving forcing extension.
<!-- end fact-1 -->
<!-- fact-2: body_md -->
Let $T$ be suitable. The natural lexicographic ordering
  of $T$, $(T,<_{\operatorname{lex}})$, is a Countryman line.
<!-- end fact-2 -->
<!-- fact-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:club-iso-embedding}
  Suppose that $T$ and $S$ are lexicographically ordered
  Hausdorff Aronszajn tree. If there is
  a $<_{\operatorname{lex}}$-increasing or $<_{\operatorname{lex}}$-decreasing map from
  an uncountable subset of $T$ into $S$, then some
  subtree of $T$ club-embeds into $S$.
<!-- end fact-3 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:basis}
  If $\mathcal{T}$ is a family of suitable trees such that for all distinct
  $T,S \in \mathcal{T}$, $T$ and $S$ have no club-isomorphic subtrees,
  then $\{(T,<_{\operatorname{lex}}),(T,<_{\operatorname{lex}}^{*}) : T \in \mathcal{T}\}$
  is a family of pairwise far
  Countryman lines, and thus any basis for the Countryman lines
  has size at least $2|\mathcal{T}|$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a family of $\omega_{1}$-trees. By $\Xi(\mathcal{T})$ we mean
  that $\Xi(T_{0},\dots,T_{n-1})$ holds whenever $T_{0},\dots,T_{n-1} \in \mathcal{T}$
  are distinct. $\Xi^{+}(\mathcal{T})$ is defined analogously.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{df:safe}
  A forcing poset $\mathbb {P}$ is called
  \emph{$\mathcal{T}$-dangerous} if for some club $C$ and
  binary relation $R$ such that
  $\operatorname{dom}(R) \subseteq \mathbb {P}$ and $\operatorname{ran}(R)$ is an uncountable
  member of $\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{T})$,
  whenever $(p,a),(p',a') \in R$ and $p$ is compatible
  with $p'$, $a$ does not conflict with $a'$ on $C$.
  We say that $\mathbb {P}$ is $\mathcal{T}$-safe if it is not $\mathcal{T}$-dangerous.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:safe-preserves}
  Assume $\Xi(\mathcal{T})$. If $\mathbb {P}$ is any $\mathcal{T}$-safe
  forcing, then $\mathbb {P} \Vdash \Xi(\mathcal{T})$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Suppose the conclusion fails. We will show that $\mathbb {P}$ is not $\mathcal{T}$-safe.
  Since $\mathbb {P} \not\Vdash \Xi(\mathcal{T})$, for
  some $p \in \mathbb {P}$, names $\mathring{A}$ and $\mathring{C}$, and distinct $T_{0},\dots,
  T_{n-1} \in \mathcal{T}$, we have that
  $p$ forces $\mathring{A} \subseteq T_{0} \otimes \dots \otimes T_{n-1}$
  is uncountable, $\mathring{C}$ is a club,
  and that no two elements of $\mathring{A}$ conflict on $\mathring{C}$. Since $\mathbb {P}$ is
  ccc, we may assume that $\mathring{C}$ is a club in the ground model,
  call it $C$ (conflicting in a coarser club implies conflicting in the
  finer).

  For each $\xi < \omega_{1}$, one finds $p_{\xi} \le p$ and
  $a_{\xi} \in T_{0}\otimes \dots \otimes T_{n-1}$ of height no less than
  $\xi$, such that $p_{\xi}\Vdash a_{\xi} \in \mathring{A}$. Let
  $R := \{(p_{\xi},a_{\xi}) : \xi < \omega_{1}\}$. It should be clear
  that $\operatorname{ran}(R)$ is an uncountable subset of
  $T_{0}\otimes \dots \otimes T_{n-1}$, and that whenever $p_{\xi}$ and $p_{\eta}$
  are compatible, $a_{\xi}$ and $a_{\eta}$ do not conflict on $C$.
  So $R$ witnesses that $\mathbb {P}$ is not $\mathcal{T}$-safe.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rmk:converse-if-ccc}
  Note that if we assume that $\mathbb {P}$ is ccc, then the converse also holds.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:psi+-safe}
  Assume $\Xi^{+}(\mathcal{T})$. Every ccc forcing
  is $\mathcal{T}$-safe.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:safe-iterations}
  Assume $\Xi(\mathcal{T})$.
  Being $\mathcal{T}$-safe is preserved under finite support iterations.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The proof is by induction on the length of the iteration.
  The limit case follows from the induction hypothesis exactly as in
  the proof for the preservation of the ccc. We prove the successor step.
  
  Suppose $\mathbb {P}$ is $\mathcal{T}$-safe and that $\mathring{\mathbb{Q}}$ is a $\mathbb {P}$-name such that
  $\mathbb {P} \Vdash$ ``$\mathring{\mathbb{Q}}$ is $\mathcal{T}$-safe''. Let
  $C$ be a club, and $R$ be such that $\operatorname{dom}(R) \subseteq \mathbb {P}*\mathring{\mathbb{Q}}$
  and $\operatorname{ran}(R)$ is an uncountable member of $\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{T})$.
  Let $\langle((p_{\xi},\mathring{q}_{\xi}),a_{\xi} ) : \xi < \omega_{1}\rangle$
  enumerate a subset of $R$ satisfying that the $a_{\xi}$ are all distinct.
  We need to show that there are $\xi,\eta < \omega_{1}$ such that
  $(p_{\xi},\mathring{q}_{\xi})$ and $(p_{\eta},\mathring{q}_{\eta})$ are compatible,
  and $a_{\xi}$ and $a_{\eta}$ are conflicting on $C$.

  Let
  $\mathring{\Gamma} := \{(\check{\xi},p_{\xi}) : \xi < \omega_{1}\}$. Then
  the fact that $\mathbb {P}$ is ccc implies that $\mathbb {P} \not\Vdash$ ``$\mathring{\Gamma}$
  is countable''. Thus for some  generic $G$ for $\mathbb {P}$,
  $\Gamma := \mathring{\Gamma}[G]$ is

    $\Delta(a_{\xi,j}(i'),a_{\xi,j'}(i')) < \xi$.

    case.
    \item[$\blacktriangleright$]

    thus they conflict on $C$, so $\nu_{C}(\alpha_{0})
    \neq \nu_{C}(\alpha_{1})$ and we are done.
  \end{itemize}

  our family of $\omega_{1}$ many dense sets which will give us
  our uncountable subfamily of $A$ of pairwise conflicting elements.

  for each $\gamma < \omega_{1}$,
  $\{q \in \mathbb{Q} : (\exists a \in q)(\operatorname{ht}(a) > \gamma)\}$ is dense. But this
  is not necessarily the case.

  For each $\alpha < \omega_{1}$ let
  $\mathbb{Q}^{\alpha} := \{q \in \mathbb{Q} : (\forall a \in q)(\alpha \le \operatorname{ht}(a))\}$.
  Because $\mathbb{Q}^{\alpha} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}$, it should be clear that $\mathbb{Q}^{\alpha}$
  is $\mathcal{T}$-safe. Thus to finish the proof of the theorem
  is enough to show that there is $\alpha < \omega_{1}$ such that
  for all $\gamma < \omega_{1}$,
  $\{q \in \mathbb{Q}^{\alpha} : (\exists a \in q)(\operatorname{ht}(a) > \gamma)\}$

    \item $\beta_{\xi} \le \min\{\operatorname{ht}(a) : a \in q_{\xi}\},
    \max \{\operatorname{ht}(a) : a \in q_{\xi}\} < \beta_{\eta}$.
    \item For no $a \in A$ with $\beta_{\eta} \le \operatorname{ht}(a)$,
    $q_{\xi} \cup \{a\} \in \mathbb{Q}$. 
  \end{itemize}
  Then $\{q_{\xi} : \xi < \omega_{1}\}$ is an uncountable antichain
  of $\mathbb{Q}$, a contradiction since we proved that $\mathbb{Q}$ is $\mathcal{T}$-safe,
  and thus ccc.

  \{x^{\frown}\langlen\rangle : x \in T^{t}_{\alpha-1}, n < \omega\}$.

  $T^{t^{\frown}\langle0\rangle}$ and $T^{t^{\frown}\langle1\rangle}$
  for $t \in 2^{\alpha}$ with limit $\alpha$.

  Let $1 \le n < \omega$ be such that $X_{\alpha} =
  (t_{\alpha,0},\dots,t_{\alpha,n-1},c_{\alpha},A_{\alpha}) \in
  (2^{\alpha})^{n}\times [\alpha]^{\le\omega}\times
    [\bigotimes_{i <n} \mathbb{S}\!\upharpoonright\! \alpha]^{\le\omega}$.
  For each $t \in 2^{\alpha}$, fix  ${T'}^{t}$
  some uniformly coherent
  subtree of $\mathbb{S} \!\upharpoonright\! (\alpha+1)$ extending $T^{t}$. This exists
  by \zcref{lem:fund-ext}. For each $t \in 2^{\alpha}$ not in
  $\{t_{\alpha,0},\dots,t_{\alpha,n-1}\}$ define
  $T^{t^{\frown}\langle0\rangle}$ and $T^{t^{\frown}\langle1\rangle}$ both be equal
  to ${T'}^{t}$.  
  If any of the following conditions fail, do the
  same for all the $t \in \{t_{\alpha,0},\dots,t_{\alpha,n-1}\}$.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item For all $i < j < n$, $t_{\alpha,i} \neq t_{\alpha,j}$.
    \item $c_{\alpha}$ is unbounded in $\alpha$.
    \item $A_{\alpha} \subseteq \bigotimes_{i}T^{t_{\alpha,i}}$ is such that
    no two elements of $A_{\alpha}$ conflict on $c_{\alpha}$, and is maximal
    with respect to this property.
  \end{itemize}
  Otherwise, apply \zcref{lem:hard-ext}
  to $T^{t_{\alpha,0}},\dots,T^{t_{\alpha,n-1}}$, using $A = A_{\alpha}$
  and $C = c_{\alpha}$,
  to obtain $b_{0},\dots,
  b_{n-1}$. Then, for all $i < n$ define
  both $T^{t_{\alpha,i}^{\frown}\langle0\rangle}$ and
  $T^{t_{\alpha,i}^{\frown}\langle1\rangle}$ as the result of adjoining
  $b_{i}$ to $T^{t_{\alpha,i}}$. This finishes the construction
  of our family.

  Let $\mathcal{T} = \{T^{t} : t \in 2^{\omega_{1}}\}$. We claim
  that $\Xi(\mathcal{T})$ holds. For this fix $1 \le n < \omega$
  and $t_{0},\dots,t_{n-1}$ distinct elements of $2^{\omega_{1}}$.
  Also fix $C$ a club and $A \subseteq \bigotimes_{i < n}T^{t_{i}}$
  be such that no two elements of $A$ conflict on $C$.
  Extending $A$ if necessary, we may assume that $A$ is maximal
  with this property. We now show that $A$ is countable.

  Let $\gamma < \omega_{1}$ be such that for all $i < j < n$,
  $t_{i}\!\upharpoonright\! \gamma \neq t_{j}\!\upharpoonright\! \gamma$. One easily finds
  a club $D \subseteq C$ such that for all $\alpha \in D$,
  $\alpha$ is limit of elements in $C$ and $A \!\upharpoonright\! \alpha$ is a maximal
  subset of $\bigotimes_{i < n}T^{t_{i}}\!\upharpoonright\! \alpha$ having
  no two elements conflicting on $C \cap \alpha$. We may
  also assume that $\gamma < \min(D)$. Using the
  diamond property, let $\alpha \in D$ be such that
  $C \cap \alpha = c_{\alpha}$, $t_{i}\!\upharpoonright\! \alpha = t_{\alpha,i}$
  for all $i < n$, and $A \!\upharpoonright\! \alpha = A_{\alpha}$. Let
  $f : n \to 2$ be such that $t_{i}\!\upharpoonright\!(\alpha+1) = t_{\alpha,i}^{\frown}
  \langlef(i)\rangle$.

  We claim that $A = A_{\alpha}$. If not, there is $a \in A$ of
  height at least $\alpha$. The maximality implies that in fact
  $A$ must have an element of height exactly $\alpha$,
  that is, an element $(b_{0}',\dots,b_{n-1}')$ where
  each $b_{i}'$ is an element of $T^{t_{\alpha,i}^{\frown}\langlef(i)\rangle}$
  of height $\alpha$. But 
  by the choice of $b_{0},\dots,b_{n-1}$ in
  the $\alpha$-th step of the construction, this implies that
  $(b_{0}',\dots,b_{n-1}')$ conflicts on $C$
  with an element of $A_{\alpha} \subseteq A$.
  So we conclude that $A = A_{\alpha}$.

The result is trivial if $\mathcal{T}$ is empty. Fix $1 \le n < \omega$,
  distinct $T^{0},\dots,T^{n-1} \in \mathcal{T}$, a club $C$,
  and uncountable
  $A \subseteq T^{0}\otimes \cdots \otimes T^{n-1} \otimes U$. We need to
  show that there are $a,b \in A$ conflicting on $C$.
  By $\Xi^{+}(\mathcal{T})$ and the fact that $U$ is suitable, going to an uncountable
  subset we may assume that the elements of $\{(a(0),\dots,a(n-1)) : a \in A\}$
  are pairwise conflicting on $C$ and that $\{a(n) : a \in A\}$ is an antichain.

  Let $\{a_{\xi} : \xi < \omega_{1}\}$
  enumerate an uncountable subset of $A$ such that
  for each $\xi < \omega_{1}$, $\xi \le \operatorname{ht}(a_{\xi})$.
  For each $\xi < \omega_{1}$, let $b_{\xi} := (a_{\xi}(0)\!\upharpoonright\! \xi,\dots,
  a_{\xi}(n)\!\upharpoonright\! \xi)$. Because for each $i < n$, $T^{i}$ is special,
  we find stationary $\Gamma \subseteq C$ such that for all
  $\xi < \eta$ and for all $i < n$, $b_{\xi}(i)$ is incomparable
  with $b_{\eta}(i)$. Now because $U$ has no stationary antichains,
  we find $\xi < \eta$ in $\Gamma$ such that
  $b_{\xi}(n) \le_{T} b_{\eta}(n)$. Observe then
  that for all $i < n$,
  $\Delta(a_{\xi}(i),a_{\eta}(i)) < \xi \le \Delta(a_{\xi}(n),a_{\eta}(n))$.
  Since $\xi \in C$, we conclude that $a_{\xi}$ and $a_{\eta}$ conflict on $C$.
