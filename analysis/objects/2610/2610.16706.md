<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:overview}
Let $S$ be a noetherian algebraic space and let $\mathscr X \to S$ be an algebraic stack of finite type with qcqs diagonal. Then there are only finitely many open substacks $\mathscr U \subseteq \mathscr X$ for which $\mathscr U \to S$ is universally closed. In particular, there are only finitely many open substacks of $\mathscr X$ with a universally closed adequate moduli space.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
For reductive group acting on complex varieties, finiteness of the opens admitting proper good quotients follows from Bia{\l}ynicki-Birula's result \cite{BB98}; see \Cref{lem:finite-proper-good-quotients}. However, even for locally reductive stacks, an \'{e}tale reduction to quotient charts does not by itself suffice: the existence of a moduli space need not pass along an \'{e}tale morphism. Our approach here is different.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:closure-stratification}
Let $S$ be a noetherian scheme and let $\mathscr X \to S$ be an algebraic stack of finite type with qcqs diagonal. Let $p: X \to \mathscr X$ be an atlas, where $X$ is a scheme of finite type over $S$. Then there exist a finite stratification $X=\sqcup_i X_i$ by integral locally closed subschemes, and closed substacks
\[
\mathscr Z_i \subseteq \mathscr X \times_S X_i
\]
such that $\mathscr Z_i \to X_i$ is flat and of finite presentation,
and for every point $x: \operatorname{Spec} K \to X_i$
\[
(\mathscr Z_i)_x=\mathrm{Im_{sch}}(p_x) \subseteq \mathscr X_K,
\]
where $p_x: \operatorname{Spec} K \to \mathscr X_K$ is the morphism induced by $p \circ x$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $R:=X \times_{\mathscr X} X$ with projections $s,t: R \rightrightarrows X$. Consider
the Cartesian diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
R \ar[rr,"{(s,t)}"] \ar[d,"t"'] & & X \times_S X \ar[d,"{p \times \mathrm{id}_X}"] \\
X \ar[rr,"{(p,\mathrm{id}_X)}"'] & & \mathscr X \times_S X.
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The hypothesis on diagonal implies that $(p,\mathrm{id}_X)$ is quasi-compact and that $R$ is qcqs. Since $t$ is smooth, it is of finite presentation. Choose an \'{e}tale surjection\footnote{If $\mathscr X$ has affine diagonal, then $R$ is a scheme of finite presentation over $X$, and we need not pass to $V$.} $V \twoheadrightarrow R$ with $V$ a scheme of finite presentation over $X$, and regard
\[
a: V \to R \xrightarrow{(s,t)} X \times_S X
\]
as a morphism over the second $X$ factor. By \cite[\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0H41}{Tag 0H41}]{sp}, there is a finite stratification of $X$ over which formation of the scheme-theoretic image of $a$ commutes with arbitrary base change. Refining this stratification using generic flatness \cite[\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/052A}{Tag 052A}]{sp} and noetherian induction, we obtain finitely many integral locally closed strata $X_i$ such that
\[
V_i:=\mathrm{Im_{sch}} \bigl( V \times_X X_i \to X \times_S X_i \bigr)
\]
is flat over $X_i$. Compatibility with arbitrary base change
is preserved under these refinements. Let $\mathscr Z_i \subseteq \mathscr X \times_S X_i$ be the scheme-theoretic image
of
\[
(p|_{X_i},\mathrm{id}_{X_i}): X_i \to \mathscr X \times_S X_i.
\]
Scheme-theoretic images are unchanged by pre-composing with
a smooth surjection and, for quasi-compact morphisms, commute
with flat base change
\cite[\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CMJ}{Tag 0CMJ} and \href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CMK}{Tag 0CMK}]{sp}. Hence
\[
\mathscr Z_i \times_{\mathscr X \times_S X_i}(X \times_S X_i)=V_i.
\]
Therefore, $\mathscr Z_i \to X_i$ is flat by smooth descent.
It is of finite type and quasi-separated over the noetherian
scheme $X_i$, hence of finite presentation. The same comparison then shows that formation of $\mathscr Z_i$ commutes with arbitrary base change over $X_i$. For any point $x \in X_i(K)$, the fibre $(\mathscr Z_i)_x$ is the scheme-theoretic image of $p_x: \operatorname{Spec} K \to \mathscr X_K$. In particular, by \cite[\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CML}{Tag 0CML}]{sp} it follows that $|(\mathscr Z_i)_x|=\overline{\{p_x\}}$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Choose an \'{e}tale surjection $S' \twoheadrightarrow S$ with $S'$ a noetherian scheme. Pullback preserves universal closedness and distinguishes open substacks, so we may and do assume that $S$ is a scheme.

Choose an atlas $p: X \to \mathscr X$ with $X$ a scheme of finite type over $S$. Fix a finite stratification $X=\sqcup_i X_i$ and a family of closed substacks $\mathscr Z_i \subseteq \mathscr X \times_S X_i$ as in \Cref{lem:closure-stratification}. Let $\mathscr U \subseteq \mathscr X$ be an open substack such that $\mathscr U \to S$ is universally closed. For each $i$, let $\mathscr W_i:=\mathscr Z_i \times_{\mathscr X} \mathscr U$ and consider the commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathscr W_i \ar[r] \ar[d,hook] \ar[ddd,bend right=4.5em,"q_i"'] & \mathscr U \ar[dd,hook] \ar[ddd,bend left=4.5em,"\text{univ. closed}"] \\
\mathscr Z_i \ar[d,hook] & \\
\mathscr X \times_S X_i \ar[r] \ar[d] & \mathscr X \ar[d] \\
X_i \ar[r] & S
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The projection $q_i: \mathscr W_i \to X_i$ factors as the closed immersion $\mathscr W_i \hookrightarrow \mathscr U \times_S X_i$ followed by the base change of $\mathscr U \to S$, so it is universally closed. It is also flat and locally of finite presentation, being the restriction of $\mathscr Z_i\to X_i$ to an open substack. Hence $\operatorname{im}(q_i)$ is open and closed in the integral scheme $X_i$, and is therefore either empty or all of $X_i$.

To conclude, we claim that $\operatorname{im}(q_i)=p^{-1}(\mathscr U) \cap X_i$ and hence $p^{-1}(\mathscr U)$ is a union of strata. Indeed, for any point $x: \operatorname{Spec} K \to X_i$, let $p_x \in |\mathscr X_K|$ be the point represented by the morphism $p \circ x$. By \Cref{lem:closure-stratification}, we have
\[
(\mathscr W_i)_x \neq \varnothing \Leftrightarrow \overline{\{p_x\}} \cap \mathscr U_K \neq \varnothing \Leftrightarrow p_x \in \mathscr U_K,
\]
and the last equivalence follows because $\mathscr U_K$ is open in $\mathscr X_K$. The last condition is equivalent to $x \in p^{-1}(\mathscr U)$, proving the claim.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:finite-proper-good-quotients}
Let $G$ be a reductive group over $\mathbb{C}$, and let $X$ be a complex variety with a $G$-action. Then there are only finitely many $G$-invariant open subsets $U \subseteq X$ that admits a proper good quotient $q_U: U \to Q_U$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By \cite[Thm. I]{BB98}, there are only finitely many $G$-invariant open subsets admitting separated good quotients
that are maximal with respect to saturated inclusion. It suffices to show that every open subset admitting a proper good quotient is maximal in this sense. 

Let $U \subseteq V \subseteq X$ be $G$-invariant open subsets with good quotients
\[
q_U: U \to Q_U \text{ and } q_V: V \to Q_V,
\]
such that $Q_U$ is proper, $Q_V$ is separated, and $U$ is saturated with respect to $q_V$, meaning that
\[
U=q_V^{-1}\bigl(q_V(U)\bigr).
\]
Then the restriction of $q_V$ to $U$ identifies $Q_U$ with the open subspace $q_V(U) \subseteq Q_V$. Thus the induced morphism $Q_U \to Q_V$ is an open immersion. It is also proper, since $Q_U$ is proper and $Q_V$ is separated. Its image is therefore both open and closed. Since $Q_V$ is irreducible, this image is all of $Q_V$, and saturation gives $U=V$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
However, the na\"{i}ve generalization from quotient stacks to general stacks seems not to work, even if the stack is locally reductive: the existence of an \'{e}tale morphism from a stack to a stack with an adequate moduli space (which may even be proper) does not guarantee that the former admits an adequate moduli space. Even a representable open immersion gives a counterexample:
\[
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_a \simeq [(\mathbb A^2 \setminus \{0\})/\operatorname{SL}_2] \hookrightarrow [\mathbb A^2/\operatorname{SL}_2].
\]
<!-- end remark-2 -->
