<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Part \ref{geo:prod} follows from the description of $Q^{\times}$ and its ends given above. Indeed, $Q^{\times}\setminus Q_{0}$ is obtained by removing the level $t=0$ from each end $U_{i}\cong F_{i}\times[0,\infty)$, and the local groups of $F_{i}\times(0,\infty)$ are those of $F_{i}$.
        
        For part \ref{geo:retract} we construct an explicit deformation retraction of $\mathbb{H}^{3}$ onto $C$. Put $\Sigma\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(\partial Q_{0})$ and $P\coloneqq\pi^{-1}\bigl(\bigsqcup_{i}U_{i}\bigr)$. These are closed subsets of $\mathbb{H}^{3}$ and since $Q^{\times}$ is the union of $Q_{0}$ and the ends $U_{i}$, which meet $Q_{0}$ along $\partial Q_{0}$, we have $\mathbb{H}^{3}=C\cup P$ and $C\cap P=\Sigma$. Each end $U_{i}\cong F_{i}\times[0,\infty)$ retracts onto $F_{i}\times\{0\}$ by shrinking the interval, and we lift this to $P$ while keeping $C$ fixed. In order to do this we will need a diffeomorphism $P\cong\Sigma\times[0,\infty)$ which maps $\Sigma$ to $\Sigma\times\{0\}$, and we obtain such a diffeomorphism by lifting the product structure of the ends.
        
        Fix an end $U_{i}$ and a point $x_{0}\in F_{i}$ with trivial local group, and write $P_{i}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(U_{i})$ and $\Sigma_{i}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(F_{i})$. Since the restriction of an orbifold covering over a suborbifold is again an orbifold covering, both $\pi|_{P_{i}}$ and $\pi|_{\Sigma_{i}}\times\mathrm{id}$ are orbifold coverings of $U_{i}=F_{i}\times[0,\infty)$, with the same fibre over $x_{0}$. Every element of $\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(U_{i})$ is represented by a loop in $F_{i}\times\{0\}$, and for both coverings the lifts of such a loop are its lifts to $\Sigma_{i}=\Sigma_{i}\times\{0\}$ under $\pi|_{\Sigma_{i}}$. The two monodromy actions on the fibre therefore agree, and since a possibly disconnected orbifold covering is determined up to equivalence by its monodromy action \cite[Thm.~2.2(ii)]{BoileauMaillotPorti2003}, we obtain a diffeomorphism $\Phi_{i}\colon P_{i}\to\Sigma_{i}\times[0,\infty)$ over $U_{i}$. Moreover, $\Phi_{i}$ maps $\Sigma_{i}$ onto $\Sigma_{i}\times\{0\}$, since these are the parts lying over $F_{i}\times\{0\}$. Taking the disjoint union over the ends, we obtain a diffeomorphism $\Phi\colon P\to\Sigma\times[0,\infty)$ which satisfies $\Phi(\Sigma)=\Sigma\times\{0\}$.
        
        We can now write down the retraction. For $u\in[0,1]$ let $\theta_{u}\colon P\to P$ correspond under $\Phi$ to the map $(a,t)\mapsto(a,(1-u)t)$, which shrinks the interval factor. Then $\theta_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{P}$ and the image of $\theta_{1}$ is $\Sigma$. Now using the decomposition $\mathbb{H}^{3}=C\cup P$, we can extend this to all of $\mathbb{H}^{3}$ by defining $
        \Theta_{u}\colon\mathbb{H}^{3}\to\mathbb{H}^{3}$ to be the identity on $C$ and $\theta_{u}$ on $P$. Notice that these two definitions agree on the intersection $C\cap P=\Sigma$ because every $\theta_{u}$ fixes $\Sigma$ pointwise. Then since $C\times[0,1]$ and $P\times[0,1]$ are closed sets covering $\mathbb{H}^{3}\times[0,1]$, the pasting lemma implies that the map $\Theta\colon\mathbb{H}^{3}\times[0,1]\to \mathbb{H}^{3}$ given by $(x,u)\mapsto \Theta_{u}(x)$ is continuous. By construction $\Theta$ is the required deformation retraction of $\mathbb{H}^{3}$ onto $C$, and in particular $C$ is contractible.
        
        Finally, $C$ is $\Gamma$-invariant, and $\pi|_{C}\colon C\to Q_{0}$ is an orbifold covering whose fibres are $\Gamma$-orbits. Thus $C/\Gamma=Q_{0}$, and since $C$ is simply connected, $\pi|_{C}$ is the universal orbifold covering by \S\ref{subsec:orbifolds}.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:peripheral}

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->

Further, the group $\Gamma$ acts transitively on the fibres of $\pi$, so it permutes these components transitively.
        
        Now fix a component $Y\subseteq \pi^{-1}(F_{i})$. Then by \S\ref{subsec:orbifolds} the restriction $\pi|_{Y}\colon Y\to F_{i}$ is an orbifold covering, and there is a commutative square
        \[
            \begin{tikzcd}
                Y \arrow[r, "\pi|_{Y}"] \arrow[d, hook] & F_{i} \arrow[d, hook] \\
                \mathbb{H}^{3} \arrow[r, "\pi"] & \mathbb{H}^{3}/\Gamma,
            \end{tikzcd}    
        \]

Then $\Gamma_{y}$ is the stabiliser of $y$ in $S_{i}$, and it is also the local group at $\pi(y)$ by \S\ref{subsec:orbifolds}. In this way the homeomorphism $Y/S_{i}\to F_{i}$ identifies local groups, and hence it is an isomorphism of orbifolds.

Theorems \ref{thm:homologyG} and \ref{thm:homologyT} now give
        \[
            \operatorname{H}_{i}(G,\mathbb{Z})\cong\begin{cases}
                \mathbb{Z} & i=0,\\
                \mathbb{Z}/3\oplus \mathbb{Z}/3 & i=1,\\
                0 & i=2, \\
                \mathbb{Z} & i=3,\\
                0 & i\ge 4,
            \end{cases}
            \qquad
            \operatorname{H}_{i}(T,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\oplus\mathbb{Z}/3\oplus\mathbb{Z}/4 \quad(i\ge3\ \text{odd}).
        \]
        Here $\operatorname{H}_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})$ vanishes because $\det J\ne0$, while $\operatorname{H}_{3}(G,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}$ comes from the last term of the resolution of Theorem \ref{thm:Gres}, whose differential vanishes after applying the augmentation map. In odd degrees the homology of $T$ is the sum of the homology of its cyclic subgroups $\langle f\rangle$, $\langle g\rangle$ and $\langle W\rangle$, which carry all of its torsion by Lemma \ref{lem:torsion-class}. This is the family treated by Ellis and Williams \cite[\S5]{ellistri}, and Remark \ref{rem:comparison} compares the resolution constructed in \cite{ellistri} with ours.

\label{ex:detzero}
        For $(r,s)=(2,3)$ the Farey word is $W=fg^{-1}fgf^{-1}g$, and its Fox derivatives are
        \[
            W_{f}=1+fg^{-1}-fg^{-1}fgf^{-1}, \qquad W_{g}=fg^{-1}f+fg^{-1}fgf^{-1}-fg^{-1}.
        \]
        In this case we take $(l,m,n)=(6,3,2)$. Then $c_{1}=c_{2}=\tfrac16+\tfrac13+\tfrac12-1=0$, and both vertex links are the Euclidean turnover $S^{2}(6,3,2)$, which is one of the three turnovers of Lemma \ref{lem:euclidean-links}. Here $\eta=3$ and $\nu=1$, and
        \[
          \det J = lm-n(l\sigma_{g} + m\sigma_{f}) = 18-2\cdot 9 = 0 .
        \]
        This example therefore falls under case (c) of Proposition \ref{prop:Gab}, which by Lemma \ref{lem:detJk2} can only occur under the present hypotheses.
    
        Since $\nu=1$, the abelianisation $G^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong\mathbb{Z}$ is torsion-free. The class $\bar c$ generates a subgroup of index $\operatorname{lcm}(l,m)=6$, and hence $T^{\mathrm{ab}}=G^{\mathrm{ab}}/\langle\bar c\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/6$. The homomorphism $\Psi$ of Lemma \ref{lem:Psi} sends $f$, $g$ and $c$ to $1$, $2$ and $6$ respectively, and it generates $\operatorname{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}$. Theorem \ref{thm:homologyG} now gives $\operatorname{H}_{i}(G,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}$ for $0\le i\le3$ and $\operatorname{H}_{i}(G,\mathbb{Z})=0$ for $i\ge4$. In particular $\operatorname{H}_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})$ is non-zero, and by Theorem \ref{thm:central}(b) the map $\alpha$ identifies it with $\operatorname{H}_{2}(T,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}$. The situation is reversed in Example \ref{ex:half}, where $\operatorname{H}_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})=0$ and $\xi$ maps $\operatorname{H}_{2}(T,\mathbb{Z})$ isomorphically onto $\langle c\rangle$. Finally, Theorem \ref{thm:homologyT} gives $\operatorname{H}_{i}(T,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/6\oplus\mathbb{Z}/3\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2$ for odd $i\ge3$.
