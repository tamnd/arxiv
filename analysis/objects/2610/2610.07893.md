<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
For $R_{\max}\geq R_{\min}>0$ and $0<\alpha<\pi/2$, replacing $\mathcal F_1$ by $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_1$ in \eqref{eq:exact} gives exactly
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal C_{\mathrm{safe}}={}&\mathbb{B}(A_1,R_{\min})\\
 &\cap\mathbb{B}(A_1+R_{\min}e(\theta_1-\alpha),R_{\min})\\
 &\cap\mathbb{B}(A_1+R_{\min}e(\theta_1+\alpha),R_{\min}).
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:three}
\end{equation}
Consequently $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{safe}}\subseteq\mathcal C_{\mathrm{recv}}$.

<!-- ieeeproof-1: body_md -->
Write $A_2=A_1+u e_1+v n_1$ and $h_\omega=u\cos\omega+v\sin\omega$. For a sector point at radius $r$,
\begin{equation}
 \left\lVertA_2-G\right\rVert^2=r^2+u^2+v^2-2r h_\omega.
 \label{eq:distance}
\end{equation}
For $0\leq r\leq R_{\min}$, this expression is convex in $r$; its maximum occurs at an endpoint. The endpoints require $u^2+v^2\leq R_{\min}^2$ and $u^2+v^2\leq2R_{\min}h_\omega$. For $r\geq R_{\min}$, the reception inequality reduces to $u^2+v^2\leq2r h_\omega$. The endpoint condition already ensures $h_\omega\geq0$, so larger $r$ cannot tighten it.

Necessity of the two angular-endpoint conditions implies $u\geq0$. For such $u$, $\cos\omega\geq\cos\alpha$ and $v\sin\omega\geq-|v|\sin\alpha$, with equality at one endpoint. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \min_{|\omega|\leq\alpha}h_\omega
 =u\cos\alpha-|v|\sin\alpha.
\end{equation}
Checking the sector apex and the two points at $r=R_{\min}$, $\omega=\pm\alpha$, is thus necessary and sufficient. These are precisely the three disks in \eqref{eq:three}. Enlarging the source set can only shrink its intersection of reception disks.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
Assume exact geometry, bounded bearing errors, accurate unobstructed motion with $v_{\mathrm{m}}>0$, reliable feedback and terminal service, and $0<r_{\mathrm{near}}\leq\rho$. If the planning polygon contains the true source and the fallback lattice spacing $h$ satisfies $h\sqrt2/2\leq r_{\mathrm{near}}$, the specified policy satisfies $S_\pi(q;\xi)=1$ for every $q\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{safe}}$ and $\xi\in\Xi$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- ieeeproof-2: body_md -->
The reception and admissibility tests guarantee usable feedback during normal sensing. Every normal wedge contains $G$, so clipping preserves it in both the angular region and the bounded planning polygon. A stop by \eqref{eq:stop} is successful by \eqref{eq:certificate}; a close-range stop is successful since $r_{\mathrm{near}}\leq\rho$. Otherwise normal sensing ends after at most eight additional readings. The planning rectangle is bounded and contains $G$. Rounding and padding retain a nearest lattice point to $G$, so the finite scan reaches within $h\sqrt2/2$ of $G$, triggering close-range completion. All moves and readings are finite, hence so is completion time.
