$$U_{p^n}U'_{p^n}=I_{p^n},\hspace{3pt} V_{p^n}V'_{p^n}=I_{p^n}\hspace{3pt}\text{ and } \hspace{3pt}D_{p^n}=U_{p^n}B_{p^n}V_{p^n}.$$

For $n\leq 3$, the claim holds trivially. For $n>3$, the proofs of the 
equalities of the corresponding matrix entries             
in each of these matrix relations
                                             
involve at most summation of some geometric series. For example,
consider the identity $D_{p^n}=U_{p^n}B_{p^n}V_{p^n}$.
                                                                        
                                                                                                        
                                                 
                      
                              
                                   
                                 
                                
                              
    
             
                                     
             
         
                                                                                                  
                       
                                      
                         
                                                                              
         
             
                                               
             
                                          
             
                                          
                                                                            
                                                                                    
                                                                                                                                                                         
       
 It is equivalent to $D_{p^n}V'_{p^n}=U_{p^n}B_{p^n}$, assuming  $V_{p^n}V'_{p^n}=I_{p^n}$. Now  from \eqref{6:48AM} and \eqref{v'},  we see that for $i,j\in\{0,\hdots,n\}$, the $(i,j)$-th entry of $D_{p^n}V'_{p^n}$ is given by
\begin{equation*}\begin{tabular}{ l|c|c|c| }
\multicolumn{1}{r}{}
 & \multicolumn{1}{c}{$j=0$}
 &  \multicolumn{1}{c}{$0<j<n$}
 & \multicolumn{1}{c}{$j=n$}\\
                               
\cline{2-4}
      
$i=0$&1 &$\begin{matrix}-1\quad&0\quad&\hdots\quad&0\quad\end{matrix}$&$-p^{n-2}(p^2-1)$\\
\cline{2-4}
       
$0<i<n-1$ & 0& $\left\{\begin{array}{cl}
p&\text{if  $i=j$,}\\
-1\hspace{8pt}&\text{if  $i=j-1$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}    
      \end{array}\right.$&$-p^{n-i-2}(p^2-1)$\\
       
\cline{2-4}
      
$i=n-1$ & 0 & $\begin{matrix}p^{n-1}\quad&0\quad&\hdots\quad&0\quad\end{matrix}$&$-p^{2n-3}$\\
      
\cline{2-4}
$i=n$ &0 & $0$&$p^{n-1}(p^2-1$)\\
\cline{2-4}
\end{tabular}\hspace{8pt}.\end{equation*}
                       
If we consider the case $0<i<n-1$ and $0<j<n$, then from
$(\ref{u})$ and $(\ref{equ1})$ it follows that the product of $U_{p^n}(p^i,\hspace{1.3pt}\hspace{1.3pt}\underline{\hspace{5pt}}\hspace{1.3pt})$, i.e. the $i$-th row of $U_{p^n}$
and $B_{p^n}(\hspace{1.3pt}\underline{\hspace{5pt}}\hspace{1.3pt}\hspace{1.3pt},p^j)$, i.e. the $j$-th column of $B_{p^n}$ is \begin{align*}-\sum_{k=i+1}^np^{k-i-1}\hspace{2pt}B_{p^n}(p^k,p^j)&=\sum_{k=i+1}^np^{k-i-1}(p\hspace{1pt}\delta_{|k-j|\hspace{1pt},\hspace{1pt}1}-(p^2+1)\hspace{1pt}\delta_{k,\hspace{1pt}j})\\
&=\sum_{k=\max\{i+1,\hspace{1pt}j-1\}}^{j+1}p^{k-i-1}(p\hspace{1pt}\delta_{|k-j|\hspace{1pt},\hspace{1pt}1}-(p^2+1)\hspace{1pt}\delta_{k,\hspace{1pt}j})\\
&=\begin{cases}
                  
\hspace{8pt}0&\text{ if $j<i$,}\\
\hspace{8pt}p&\text{ if $j=i$,}\\
-1&\text{ if $j=i+1$,}\\
\hspace{8pt}0&\text{ if $j>i+1$,}
\end{cases}
                   
\end{align*}
where $\delta$ is the Kronecker delta function. So, the claim holds in this case.

Again, for $i=n-1$ and $0<j<n$, we have
                                                                                  
\begin{align*}
U_{p^n}(p^{n-1},\hspace{1.3pt}\hspace{1.3pt}\underline{\hspace{5pt}}\hspace{1.3pt})\cdot &B_{p^n}(\hspace{1.3pt}\underline{\hspace{5pt}}\hspace{1.3pt}\hspace{1.3pt},p^j)\\
&=
-\sum_{k=1}^np^{n-k}\cdot\frac{p^{2(k-1)}-1}{p^2-1}\cdot\hspace{2pt}B_{p^n}(p^k,p^j)\\
                                                                                                                                                                          
&=\sum_{k=\max\{2,\hspace{1pt}j-1\}}^{j+1}p^{n-k}\cdot\frac{p^{2(k-1)}-1}{p^2-1}\cdot\left(p\hspace{1pt}\delta_{|k-j|\hspace{1pt},\hspace{1pt}1}-(p^2+1)\hspace{1pt}\delta_{k,\hspace{1pt}j}\right)\\
&=\begin{cases}
                  
p^{n-1}&\text{ if $j=1$,}\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{align*}
                                                                
Thus, the claim also holds in this case.
