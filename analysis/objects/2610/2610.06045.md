<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
For $\alpha=0$, the weighted divergence reduces to the classical divergence $\operatorname{div} f$ \cite{Arnold}. 
Hence, classical results on volumetric dissipativity and contractivity based on the sign of $\operatorname{div} f$ are recovered as special cases of the present framework.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{Def:def1}
System \eqref{eq:sys} is called $w^\alpha$-volumetrically conservative if for any measurable domain $D \Subset M$ and all $t\ge0$ one has
$\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D))=\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)$ (for continuous $D_{w^\alpha}f$ this is equivalent to $D_{w^\alpha}f\equiv 0$ on $M$).
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{Def:def2}
System \eqref{eq:sys} is $w^\alpha$-volumetrically dissipative if for any measurable domain $D\Subset M$ and all $t\ge0$ one has
$\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D))\le \Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)$ 
(for continuous $D_{w^\alpha}f$ this is equivalent to the inequality $D_{w^\alpha}f(x)\le 0$ for all $x\in M$).
If the condition is restricted to domains within an open positively invariant region $U$, we speak of regional $w^\alpha$-volumetric dissipativity.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{Def:def3}
System \eqref{eq:sys} is called asymptotically $w^\alpha$-volumetrically contracting if, for every measurable $D\Subset M$,
$\lim_{t\to\infty}\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D))=0$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{th:wrate_alpha_comp}
Let $U\subseteq M$ be an open positively invariant domain, $\lambda>0$, and suppose
$D_{w^\alpha}f(x) \le -\lambda$ for all $x\in U$.
Then for every measurable $D\Subset U$ the estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{ineq:dissipat}
\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D)) \le e^{-\lambda t}\,\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D),\qquad t\ge0,
\end{equation}
holds. If $U=M$, then the system is asymptotically $w^\alpha$-contracting at the stated volume rate for all $D\Subset M$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Differently from the notion of dissipativity in the sense of Levinson \cite{Levinson}, which only requires the existence of absorbing sets, the proposed approach provides a quantitative measure of dissipation. 
Indeed, if the condition $D_{w^\alpha}f(x) \le -\lambda < 0$ holds for all $x$ in a positively invariant domain $U\subset M$, then by Theorem~\ref{th:wrate_alpha_comp} the weighted volume of any initial domain $D\subset U$ satisfies the explicit exponential estimate \eqref{ineq:dissipat}. 
This is a quantitative estimate for phase volume, distinct from the existence of an absorbing set. It does not by itself estimate convergence times of individual trajectories.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Consider an initial measurable domain $D\Subset U$. The flow $\varphi_t$ is forward-complete. Define the function of time
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:V_D}
V(t): = \Omega_{w^\alpha}(D(t)) = \int_{D(t)} w(x)^\alpha \, dV(x),
\end{equation}
where $D(t) = \varphi_t(D)$. At $t=0$, the value of $V(0)$ is finite since $\overline D$ is compact and $w^\alpha$ is continuous and positive there, where here and throughout $\overline{D}$ denotes the closure of the domain $D$.
Using \eqref{eq:Vwalpha_ps}--\eqref{eq:Dwalpha} and the assumptions of the theorem, there exists a constant $\lambda > 0$ such that
$D_{w^\alpha}f(x) \le -\lambda$ for all $x \in U$.
The domain $U$ is positively invariant, i.e. $D(t) \subset U$ for all $t \ge 0$. Hence, for every point $x \in D(t)$ we have $D_{w^\alpha}f(x) \le -\lambda$. Multiplying this inequality by the positive function $w^\alpha$, we obtain the pointwise estimate of the integrand:
\begin{equation*}
D_{w^\alpha}f(x) \, w(x)^\alpha \le -\lambda \, w(x)^\alpha, ~~ x \in D(t) .
\end{equation*}

Integrating this inequality over $D(t)$ and using \eqref{eq:Vwalpha_ps}, \eqref{eq:deriv_alpha} and \eqref{eq:V_D}, we obtain
$\dot V(t) \le -\lambda \int_{D(t)} w^\alpha \, dV = -\lambda V(t)$.
Thus, the function $V(t)$ satisfies the differential inequality
$\dot V(t) + \lambda V(t) \le 0$, $t \ge 0$.
Multiplying by the integrating factor $e^{\lambda t}$, we get
$\frac{d}{dt}\bigl( V(t) e^{\lambda t} \bigr)
= e^{\lambda t} \bigl( \dot V(t) + \lambda V(t) \bigr) \le 0$.
Consequently, the product $V(t) e^{\lambda t}$ is nonincreasing in $t$. In particular, for any $t \ge 0$ we have
$V(t) e^{\lambda t} \le V(0) e^{0} = V(0)$.
Dividing by $e^{\lambda t} > 0$, we arrive at the required exponential estimate
\begin{equation*}
\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D)) = V(t) \le V(0) e^{-\lambda t} = \Omega_{w^\alpha}(D) e^{-\lambda t}.
\end{equation*}

If $U=M$, the same estimate holds for each $D\Subset M$ and all $t\ge0$. Passing to the limit gives
$\lim_{t\to\infty} \Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D)) = 0$.
This means that the system is asymptotically $w^\alpha$-contracting. Theorem \ref{th:wrate_alpha_comp} is proved.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{th:wrate_alpha_exp}
If in an open positively invariant domain $U$ we have $D_{w^\alpha}f(x) \ge \mu$ for some $\mu>0$, then for every measurable $D\Subset U$ one gets 
$\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D)) \ge e^{\mu t}\,\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)$, $t\ge0$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Analogous to the proof of Theorem \ref{th:wrate_alpha_comp}, using \eqref{eq:deriv_alpha} and $D_{w^\alpha}f\ge \mu$ we obtain $\dot V(t) \ge \mu V(t)$, which yields the lower estimate.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:3.4_3.5}
Consider the forward-complete system on $M=\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}$
\begin{equation}\label{ex:sys_first}
\dot r=r(1-r),\qquad \dot\theta=1,
\end{equation}
which has an attracting limit cycle at $r=1$. The Euclidean divergence in the polar coordinate basis is
$\operatorname{div}f=r^{-1}\partial_r(r\dot r)+\partial_\theta\dot\theta=2-3r$. It is positive for $r<2/3$ and negative for $r>2/3$. Thus its sign varies on the annulus $1/2\le r\le3/2$, although it is strictly negative in a sufficiently small neighbourhood of the cycle.

Choose $w(r)=e^{16(r-1)^2}$ and $\alpha=1$. Then
$D_wf=2-3r-32r(r-1)^2$.

The compact annulus $K=\{1/2\le r\le3/2\}$ is positively invariant and contained in the positively invariant open annulus $U=\{0.49<r<1.51\}$. On $U$ and hence on $K$ the maximum of $D_wf$ is approximately $-0.93$, reached at $r\approx0.95$ (the interior critical point of $D_wf$). In particular $D_wf\le-1/2$ on $U$. Hence Theorem~\ref{th:wrate_alpha_comp} yields
$\Omega_w(\varphi_t(D))\le e^{-t/2}\Omega_w(D)$ for every measurable $D\subset K$.
For $\alpha=-1$, the expression $D_{w^{-1}}f=2-3r+32r(r-1)^2$ changes sign on $K$, so neither uniform-sign theorem applies there. The example concerns weighted volume. The pointwise attraction of the cycle follows separately from the radial equation.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{th:wwandering_alpha}
Assume that the system is $w^\alpha$-volumetrically dissipative ($D_{w^\alpha}f\le0$ almost everywhere) and, moreover, the set of points where $D_{w^\alpha}f(x)<0$ is dense in $M$.
Then there exist no relatively compact Jordan-measurable invariant (i.e. $\varphi_t(A)=A$ for all $t$) sets $A$ with $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(A)>0$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The weighted volume $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)$ itself serves as a Lyapunov functional whose properties are completely determined by the sign of $D_{w^\alpha}f$. Thanks to the parameter $\alpha$, this functional can be tuned and adapted to the geometry of the flow without solving additional equations.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $A\subset M$ be an arbitrary relatively compact, Jordan-measurable and flow-invariant set (i.e. $\varphi_t(A)=A$ for all $t\ge0$). We show that the assumptions of the theorem imply $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(A)=0$.

By invariance of $A$, the weighted volume $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(A))$ is constant and equal to $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(A)$. Differentiating this equality at $t=0$ using \eqref{eq:deriv_alpha}, we get
\begin{equation*}
0 = \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} \Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(A)) = \int_A D_{w^\alpha}f(x)\, w(x)^\alpha\, dV(x).
\end{equation*}
By the assumption of the theorem, $D_{w^\alpha}f(x)\le 0$ for almost all $x\in M$, and the weight $w^\alpha$ is strictly positive. Hence the integrand $D_{w^\alpha}f \cdot w^\alpha$ is almost everywhere non-positive, and its integral over $A$ is zero. This is possible only if $D_{w^\alpha}f(x)=0$ almost everywhere on $A$. By continuity of $D_{w^\alpha}f$, the equality holds on the open set $U:=\operatorname{int}(A)$: if it failed at an interior point, it would fail on a neighbourhood of positive measure within $A$.

On the other hand, the set $\{x\in M : D_{w^\alpha}f(x)<0\}$ is dense in $M$. If $U$ were nonempty, it would contain at least one point where $D_{w^\alpha}f<0$, contradicting the above. Therefore, $\operatorname{int}(A)=\varnothing$.

For a Jordan-measurable set, the boundary has Lebesgue measure zero, so its volume equals the volume of its interior: $\operatorname{vol}(A)=\operatorname{vol}(\operatorname{int}(A))=0$. Then
\begin{equation*}
\Omega_{w^\alpha}(A) = \int_A w(x)^\alpha\, dV(x)
\le \bigl(\sup_{x\in A} w(x)^\alpha\bigr) \operatorname{vol}(A) = 0.
\end{equation*}
Since $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(A)\ge0$, we get $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(A)=0$. 
Theorem \ref{th:wwandering_alpha} is proved.
<!-- end proof-3 -->

\label{ex:th2_alpha}
Consider system \eqref{ex:sys_first} on $M=\{(r,\theta):r>1/4\}$ and the smooth positive weight $w(r)=e^{3r^2}/r^3$.
We have $\ln w = 3r^2-3\ln r$, $\nabla\ln w = (6r-3/r) \partial_r$, where $\partial_r$ is the unit radial vector in polar coordinates on the plane. 
Compute $f^{\top} \nabla\ln w = 3(2r^2-1)(1-r)$.
Then
$D_{w^\alpha}f = 2-3r + 3\alpha(2r^2-1)(1-r)$.
For $\alpha=1$ we get $D_w f = -1+6r^2(1-r)$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{th:wLyap_alpha}
If the system is both $w^\alpha$-volumetrically dissipative and asymptotically $w^\alpha$-contracting, then the set functional
$F_{w^\alpha}(D)=\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)$ has the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D))$ is monotonically nonincreasing and tends to zero as $t\to\infty$;
\item the level sets $\{D: F_{w^\alpha}(D)\le c\}$ are invariant in the sense that from ${F}_{w^\alpha}(D)\le c$ it follows that $F_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D))\le c$ for all $t\ge0$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By definition of $w^\alpha$-volumetric dissipativity, for any measurable domain $D\Subset M$ and all $t\ge0$ we have
$\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D)) \le \Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)$.
This means that the functional $F_{w^\alpha}(D)=\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)$ is monotonically nonincreasing along the flow.
By definition of asymptotic $w^\alpha$-contractivity,
$\lim_{t\to\infty}\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D)) = 0$,
i.e. $F_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D))\to 0$ as $t\to\infty$. The first property is proved.

Now suppose $F_{w^\alpha}(D)\le c$, i.e. $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)\le c$. By monotonic nonincrease,
$\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D)) \le \Omega_{w^\alpha}(D) \le c$ for all $t\ge0$.
Hence $\varphi_t(D)$ remains in the level set $\{G : F_{w^\alpha}(G)\le c\}$, proving the second property.
This proves the stated set-functional properties.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{th:wattractor_alpha}
Suppose the system is asymptotically $w^\alpha$-contracting and has a compact positively invariant absorbing set $B$.
Thus $\varphi_t(B)\subseteq B$ for $t\ge0$, and each bounded set $D$ is mapped into $B$ after a sufficiently large time. Then
$\mathcal A=\bigcap_{t\ge0}\varphi_t(B)$
is a global attractor with $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\mathcal A)=0$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Consider the family of sets $\{\varphi_t(B)\}_{t\ge0}$. 
By positive invariance of $B$ ($\varphi_t(B) \subseteq B$ for all $t\ge0$) and the semigroup property of the forward flow $\varphi_{t+s}=\varphi_t\circ\varphi_s$, this family is decreasing: for any $t_2\ge t_1\ge0$ we have $\varphi_{t_2}(B) = \varphi_{t_1}(\varphi_{t_2-t_1}(B)) \subseteq \varphi_{t_1}(B)$. 

Define $\mathcal{A} = \bigcap_{t\ge0} \varphi_t(B)$.
As an intersection of nested nonempty compact sets, $\mathcal{A}$ is nonempty and compact. Continuity of the finite-dimensional flow, compactness and the absorbing property imply that $\mathcal{A}$ is invariant and attracts every bounded set, hence is a global attractor (see \cite{Temam}).

By construction $\mathcal{A}\subseteq \varphi_t(B)$ for any $t\ge0$. Monotonicity of the integral with respect to the positive measure $w^\alpha\,dV$ gives
$\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\mathcal{A}) \le \Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(B))$ for all $t\ge0$.
Choose a relatively compact open domain $G$ containing the compact set $B$. Asymptotic $w^\alpha$-contractivity gives $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(G))\to0$, and monotonicity of the measure yields $0\le\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(B))\le\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(G))\to0$.
Passing to the limit $t\to\infty$ in the inequality, we get
$\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\mathcal{A}) \le \lim_{t\to\infty} \Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(B)) = 0$.

Since $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\mathcal{A})$ is an integral of a positive function, it is nonnegative. Hence $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\mathcal{A}) = 0$. 
Theorem \ref{th:wattractor_alpha} is proved.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{th:wnonaut_alpha}
Consider a $T$-periodic nonautonomous system $\dot x=f(t,x)$, locally Lipschitz in $x$, whose time-$T$ map $P=\varphi_{0,T}$ is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism onto its image. Assume that $P$ admits a compact positively invariant set $B$ absorbing every bounded subset of $M$. Suppose that for some $\kappa\in(0,1)$,
\[
\Omega_{w^\alpha}(P(D))\le\kappa\,\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)
\]
for every relatively compact measurable domain $D\Subset M$. Then the global attractor $\mathcal A=\bigcap_{k\ge0}P^k(B)$ has zero $w^\alpha$-volume.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The sets $P^k(B)$ are nested nonempty compact sets. Their intersection $\mathcal A$ is nonempty, compact and invariant. Compactness and the absorbing property imply that it attracts every bounded set. Choose a relatively compact open domain $G\supset B$. Iteration of the assumed inequality gives
\[
\Omega_{w^\alpha}(P^k(G))\le\kappa^k\Omega_{w^\alpha}(G) \to 0.
\]
Since $\mathcal A\subset P^k(B)\subset P^k(G)$ for every $k$, positivity of the measure gives $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\mathcal A)=0$.

<!-- example-4: body_md -->
Consider the $2$-periodic polar system
\[
\dot r=a(t)r-r^3,\qquad \dot\theta=1,
\]
where $a(t)=\alpha_1>0$ for $t\in[0,1)$ and $a(t)=\alpha_2>0$ for $t\in[1,2)$, extended periodically. The solutions are understood in the Carath\'eodory sense at switching times. Put
\begin{align*}
A&=\alpha_1+\alpha_2>0,\\
C&=2\int_0^2\exp\!\left(2\int_0^s a(u)\,du\right)ds>0.
\end{align*}
Solving the radial equation gives the time-$2$ map
\begin{align*}
P(r,\theta)=(R(r),\theta+2),~~~
R(r)=\frac{r e^A}{\sqrt{1+Cr^2}}.
    
                                   
\end{align*}
The unique positive fixed radius is
$\rho_*^2=(e^{2A}-1)/C$, with $R'(\rho_*)=e^{-2A}<1$.
Choose a sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$ satisfying
$\varepsilon<\min\{\rho_*/2,\sqrt{\alpha_1},\sqrt{\alpha_2}\}$ and work on the forward-invariant phase space $M=\{r>\varepsilon\}$. The compact annulus
$B=\{\rho_*/2\le r\le3\rho_*/2\}$ is positively invariant for $P$ and absorbs every bounded subset of $M$ under iteration.

For $w(r)=r^{-3}$ and $\alpha=1$, the weighted polar area element is $r^{-2}dr\,d\theta$, and the ratio of weighted image area to weighted initial area at radius $r$ is
\[
g(r)=\frac{r^2 R'(r)}{R(r)^2}
=\frac{e^{-A}}{\sqrt{1+Cr^2}}\le e^{-A}<1.
\]
The change-of-variables formula therefore gives
$\Omega_w(P(D))\le e^{-A}\Omega_w(D)$ for every measurable $D\Subset M$, and, in particular, for $D=B$. The invariant core $\bigcap_{k\ge0}P^k(B)=\{r=\rho_*\}$ is a zero-$w$-area global attractor for the discrete-time system on $M$.

For the inverse weight $w^{-1}=r^3$, the corresponding ratio is
$R(r)^4R'(r)/r^4=e^{5A}(1+Cr^2)^{-7/2}$, which exceeds $1$ near $r=0$ (and for points of $M$ sufficiently close to $\varepsilon$ if $\varepsilon$ is chosen small enough). Thus the same global contraction bound does not hold for this inverse measure. The invariant circle does not become a repeller: only its measured volume behaviour changes.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
Classical Lyapunov stability \cite{Lyapunov} studies the behavior of individual trajectories and relies on the existence of a Lyapunov function decreasing along solutions. In contrast, the approach developed in this paper introduces an integral criterion in which the weighted volume $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)$ serves as a new Lyapunov functional whose monotonicity is determined by the sign of $D_{w^\alpha}f$. This controls weighted volumes of transported domains and provides explicit exponential integral estimates, not automatic pointwise stability. The flexibility in choosing the parameter $\alpha$ and the weight $w$ makes the method applicable to systems for which the construction of a classical pointwise Lyapunov function is difficult, and allows one to detect dissipative properties hidden from standard analysis.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{th:wNmain_alpha}
Assume that $N$ is invariant and that there exists a positively invariant tubular neighbourhood $U\supset N$ and a constant $\lambda>0$ such that for $x\in U\setminus N$ the transverse dissipativity condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wcond_alpha}
D_{w^\alpha}f(x) + m\,\frac{f(x)^{\top}\nabla\rho_N(x)}{\rho_N(x)} \le -\lambda
\end{equation}
holds. Then for every measurable initial domain $D\Subset U$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:est_exp_N}
\mathcal{V}_{N,w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D)) \le e^{-\lambda t}\,\mathcal{V}_{N,w^\alpha}(D), ~~ t\ge0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Define the function of time
\begin{equation*}
V(t): = \mathcal{V}_{N,w^\alpha}(D(t)) = \int_{D(t)} \rho_N(x)^m \, w(x)^\alpha \, dV(x).
\end{equation*}
The distance $\rho_N$ is smooth on $U\setminus N$ and locally Lipschitz on $U$. Since $m\ge1$, $N$ has zero ambient Riemannian volume. The transport identity for the locally Lipschitz weight $\rho_N^m w^\alpha$ thus holds by differentiating off $N$ and using dominated convergence on compact flow tubes. Applying the transport formula for the moving domain $D(t) = \varphi_t(D)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proof_main_rho}
\begin{array}{lll}
\frac{dV}{dt} = \int_{D(t)} \bigl( \mathcal{L}_f (\rho_N^m w^\alpha) + (\rho_N^m w^\alpha) \operatorname{div} f \bigr) dV.
\end{array}
\end{equation}
We compute the Lie derivative of the integrand in \eqref{eq:proof_main_rho}:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{array}{lll}
\mathcal{L}_f (\rho_N^m) = m \rho_N^{m-1} \mathcal{L}_f (\rho_N) = m \rho_N^{m-1} f^{\top} \nabla\rho_N,
\\
\mathcal{L}_f (w^\alpha) = \alpha w^{\alpha-1} \mathcal{L}_f (w) = \alpha w^\alpha f^{\top} \nabla\ln w.
\end{array}
\end{equation*}
Hence,
$\mathcal{L}_f (\rho_N^m w^\alpha) = \rho_N^m \,\alpha w^\alpha f^{\top} \nabla\ln w + m \rho_N^{m-1} w^\alpha f^{\top} \nabla\rho_N$.
Adding the term $(\rho_N^m w^\alpha) \operatorname{div} f$, we obtain the total rate of change of the integral:
$
\frac{dV}{dt} = \int_{D(t)} \rho_N^m w^\alpha \Bigl( \operatorname{div} f + \alpha f^{\top} \nabla\ln w + m \frac{f^{\top} \nabla\rho_N}{\rho_N} \Bigr) dV$.

Using the definition \eqref{eq:Dwalpha} of the weighted divergence of order $\alpha$, we get
\begin{equation*}
\frac{dV}{dt} = \int_{D(t)} \rho_N^m w^\alpha \Bigl( D_{w^\alpha}f + m \frac{f^{\top} \nabla\rho_N}{\rho_N} \Bigr) dV.
\end{equation*}

By the hypothesis of the theorem, in the positively invariant tubular neighbourhood $U$, inequality \eqref{eq:wcond_alpha} holds for $x \in U \setminus N$.
Since $D \subset U$ and $U$ is positively invariant, $D(t) \subset U$ for all $t \ge 0$. For almost every point of $D(t)$ (except possibly a set of measure zero where $\rho_N=0$), the estimate holds. Substituting it under the integral sign, we have
\begin{equation*}
\frac{dV}{dt} \le -\lambda \int_{D(t)} \rho_N^m w^\alpha \, dV = -\lambda V(t).
\end{equation*}

Thus $V(t)$ satisfies the differential inequality $\dot V(t) + \lambda V(t) \le 0$. Multiplying by the integrating factor $e^{\lambda t}$, we get
$\frac{d}{dt}\bigl( V(t) e^{\lambda t} \bigr) \le 0$.
Integrating from $0$ to $t$, we find $V(t) e^{\lambda t} \le V(0)$, or equivalently,
$\mathcal{V}_{N,w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D)) = V(t) \le e^{-\lambda t} V(0) = e^{-\lambda t} \, \mathcal{V}_{N,w^\alpha}(D)$.
Theorem \ref{th:wNmain_alpha} is proved.

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{th:geom_equiv_alpha}
The following statements hold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D) = \operatorname{vol}_{\tilde g_\alpha}(D)$.
    \item[(b)] The system is $w^\alpha$-volumetrically conservative in $g$ $\Leftrightarrow$ $\widetilde{\operatorname{div}}_\alpha f\equiv0$.
    \item[(c)] The system is $w^\alpha$-volumetrically dissipative in $g$ $\Leftrightarrow$ $\widetilde{\operatorname{div}}_\alpha f\le 0$.
    \item[(d)] Asymptotic $w^\alpha$-contractivity in $g$ is equivalent to asymptotic volumetric contractivity in $\tilde g_\alpha$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Consider the conformal metric \eqref{eq:conf_metric_alpha}. 
Its volume form is \eqref{eq:form_metric}.
Hence, for any measurable domain $D$,
$\operatorname{vol}_{\tilde g_\alpha}(D) = \int_D \omega_{\tilde g_\alpha} = \int_D w^\alpha \omega_g = \Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)$,
which proves (a).

The divergence of $f$ in $\tilde g_\alpha$ is expressed via the divergence in $g$ and the conformal factor as in \eqref{eq:div_new_metric}.
From \eqref{eq:Dwalpha} we have $\widetilde{\operatorname{div}}_\alpha f = D_{w^\alpha}f$.

Using \eqref{eq:deriv_alpha}, the rate of change of the weighted volume is
$\frac{d}{dt}\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D(t)) = \int_{D(t)} D_{w^\alpha}f \; w^\alpha\, dV_g$.
But $w^\alpha\, dV_g = \omega_{\tilde g_\alpha}$ and $D_{w^\alpha}f = \widetilde{\operatorname{div}}_\alpha f$, so
$\frac{d}{dt}\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D(t)) = \int_{D(t)} \widetilde{\operatorname{div}}_\alpha f \; \omega_{\tilde g_\alpha}$.
Exactly the same formula holds for the ordinary Riemannian volume in $\tilde g_\alpha$:
\begin{equation*}
\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{vol}_{\tilde g_\alpha}(D(t)) = \int_{D(t)} \widetilde{\operatorname{div}}_\alpha f \; \omega_{\tilde g_\alpha} .
\end{equation*}
Thus the functions $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D(t))$ and $\operatorname{vol}_{\tilde g_\alpha}(D(t))$ have the same derivative and coincide at $t=0$. Hence they are equal for all $t\ge0$.

We now prove the equivalences in (b)--(d).

The system is $w^\alpha$-volumetrically conservative in $g$ iff for every $D\Subset M$, $\frac{d}{dt}\Omega_{w^\alpha}(D(t))\equiv0$. Since $w^\alpha>0$ and $D$ is arbitrary, this is equivalent to $D_{w^\alpha}f\equiv0$. As $D_{w^\alpha}f = \widetilde{\operatorname{div}}_\alpha f$, this is equivalent to $\widetilde{\operatorname{div}}_\alpha f\equiv0$, i.e. volumetric conservativity in $\tilde g_\alpha$.

Similarly, $w^\alpha$-volumetric dissipativity means $\Omega_{w^\alpha}(\varphi_t(D))\le \Omega_{w^\alpha}(D)$ for all $D$ and $t\ge0$. In the smooth case this is equivalent to $D_{w^\alpha}f\le0$, which, since $D_{w^\alpha}f = \widetilde{\operatorname{div}}_\alpha f$, becomes $\widetilde{\operatorname{div}}_\alpha f\le0$, exactly the dissipativity condition in $\tilde g_\alpha$.

Asymptotic $w^\alpha$-contractivity in $g$ means that the weighted volume of $\varphi_t(D)$ tends to zero for every $D\Subset M$. Since weighted volume in $g$ equals ordinary volume in $\tilde g_\alpha$ at each time, the same convergence holds in the conformal metric. The equivalence is established.
Theorem \ref{th:geom_equiv_alpha} is proved.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{sec:family} 
Consider the family of $w^\alpha$-volumes and their duality.
The introduced family $\Omega_{w^\alpha}$ for different $\alpha$ possesses a certain symmetry. From \eqref{eq:Dwalpha} we have
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:duality}
D_{w^\alpha}f + D_{w^{-\alpha}}f = 2\operatorname{div} f .
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\operatorname{div} f \equiv 0$ (the system preserves ordinary volume), then $D_{w^{-\alpha}}f = - D_{w^\alpha}f$. 
If ordinary divergence vanishes and one weighted divergence is uniformly negative on a common invariant region, the opposite weight has uniformly positive divergence there.
More generally, for any $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}$, $\beta\neq0$, we have
\begin{equation*}
D_{w^\alpha}f = \frac{\alpha}{\beta} D_{w^\beta}f + \left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right)\operatorname{div} f,
\end{equation*}
which expresses an affine dependence on $\alpha$ (interpolation only when $\alpha/\beta\in[0,1]$). This identity permits checking the desired sign for different $\alpha$. The existence of a suitable exponent requires the relevant pointwise inequalities to have a common solution.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
The identity for inverse weights must not be interpreted as a change in dynamical attraction or repulsion.
Changing $\alpha$ changes the reference measure but does not change the vector field, invariant sets, or their dynamical attraction and repulsion. Equation~\eqref{eq:duality} only relates the corresponding local weighted divergences. Without information about $\operatorname{div}f$, it does not imply opposite volume behaviour.
<!-- end remark-6 -->

Return to the system with a limit cycle \eqref{ex:sys_first} and the weight $w=e^{16(r-1)^2}$. For the given neighbourhood $U_{1/2}=\{|r-1| \leq \frac{1}{2}\}$, the expression $D_{w^\alpha}f = -1-3u - 32\alpha(1+u)u^2$ (where $u=r-1$ and $u \in [-0.5,0.5]$) shows that increasing $\alpha$ lowers the weighted divergence at every $r\ne1$, whereas decreasing it raises the weighted divergence there. These statements concern the chosen weighted-volume estimate and do not alter dynamical stability.

<!-- theorem-9: body_md -->
\label{th:main_orient}
Let $O\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open and let $f\in C^1(O;\mathbb R^n)$ generate a forward-complete flow on $O$. Let $\psi\in C^2(O)$ and let $\Sigma=\{x\in O:\psi(x)=0\}$ be a smooth compact invariant hypersurface, with $\nabla\psi\neq0$ on $\Sigma$. Set $w=\mathcal L_f\psi$. Suppose there is a tubular neighbourhood $U\Subset O$ of $\Sigma$ and constants $c_1,c_2,\lambda,\mu>0$, with $\mu<\lambda$, such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $w^{-1}(0)\cap U=\Sigma$, $\nabla w$ is transverse to $\Sigma$, and
$c_1\operatorname{dist}(x,\Sigma)\le|w(x)|\le c_2\operatorname{dist}(x,\Sigma)$ on $U$;
\item[(b)] $\operatorname{sgn}(w)\operatorname{div}(wf)\le-\lambda|w|$ on $U\setminus\Sigma$;
\item[(c)] $\lambda+\operatorname{div}f\ge\mu$ on $U$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\Sigma$ is locally exponentially asymptotically stable. More precisely, there exists a positively invariant neighbourhood $U_0\Subset U$ of $\Sigma$ such that every $x_0\in U_0$ satisfies
\[
\operatorname{dist}(\varphi_t(x_0),\Sigma)
\le \frac{c_2}{c_1}e^{-\mu t}\operatorname{dist}(x_0,\Sigma).
\]
Moreover, for every measurable $D\Subset U_0$,
\[
\int_{\varphi_t(D)}|w|\,dV
\le e^{-\lambda t}\int_D|w|\,dV,\qquad t\ge0.
\]
<!-- end theorem-9 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
For a trajectory in $U$, let $v(t)=|w(\varphi_t(x_0))|$. On $U\setminus\Sigma$, the identity
$\mathcal L_fw=\operatorname{div}(wf)-w\operatorname{div}f$
and assumptions (b),(c) give
\[
\dot v\le-\bigl(\lambda+\operatorname{div}f\bigr)v\le-\mu v.
\]
On $\Sigma$, $w=0$ and invariance ensures that $v$ remains zero, so the same inequality holds along all trajectories in $U$. Since $\Sigma$ is compact, choose $h>0$ so that the closed $h$-tube about $\Sigma$ is contained in $U$. Fix $0<\delta<c_1h$ and put
$U_0:=\{x\in O:\operatorname{dist}(x,\Sigma)<h,\ |w(x)|<\delta\}$.
By (a), its closure lies in $U$, because $|w|\le\delta$ implies $\operatorname{dist}(x,\Sigma)\le\delta/c_1<h$ within the $h$-tube. The inequality $v(t)\le e^{-\mu t}v(0)$ holds up to any possible first exit from $U_0$. Such an exit cannot occur through $|w|=\delta$ (since $v$ decreases) or through $\operatorname{dist}(x,\Sigma)=h$ (where (a) gives $|w|\ge c_1h>\delta$). Thus $U_0$ is positively invariant, and the two bounds in (a) imply the distance estimate.

For $D\Subset U_0$, put $W(t)=\int_{\varphi_t(D)}|w|\,dV$.
The transport formula applies to the locally Lipschitz weight $|w|$. Since $w^{-1}(0)\cap U=\Sigma$ has zero ambient volume,
\begin{align*}
\dot W(t)
=\int_{\varphi_t(D)}\operatorname{sgn}(w)
     \operatorname{div}(wf)\,dV
\le-\lambda W(t).
\end{align*}
Gronwall's inequality completes the proof.
