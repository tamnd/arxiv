<!-- maintheorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:gwzero}
Let \(T\) be a Galton--Watson tree with offspring law \(Z\), conditioned on
survival, and assume that \(Z<\infty\) almost surely. If
\(\mathbb E Z=\infty\), then for the random interchange process
\[
        \beta_c^{\mathrm{loop}}(T)
        =
        \beta_c^{\mathrm{link}}(T)
        =
        0
\]
almost surely.
More precisely, there exists \(\beta_0>0\), depending only
on the offspring law, such that for every fixed
\(\beta\in(0,\beta_0)\),
\[
        \mathbb P_{T,\beta}(|L_o|=\infty)>0
\]
for
\(\mathbf P_Z(\,\cdot\mid T\text{ survives})\)-almost every realisation
\(T\).

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:multi}
Let \(T\) be an unconditioned Galton--Watson tree with offspring law \(Z\),
where \(Z<\infty\) almost surely, and run the random interchange process at a
fixed parameter \(\beta>0\). Let
\(C^{(1)}(o)\) be the number of outward one-link exits of the multi-link loop
\(\gamma_o\). If
\[
        \mathbb E_\beta^{\mathrm{ann}}[C^{(1)}(o)]>1,
\]
then the full loop containing \((o,0)\) has infinite vertex support with
positive annealed probability:
\[
        \mathbb P_\beta^{\mathrm{ann}}(|L_o|=\infty)>0.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
If the multi-link loop \(\gamma_o\) has infinite vertex support with positive
\(\mathbb P_\beta^{\mathrm{ann}}\)-probability, Lemma~\ref{lem:addunilinks} implies that
the full loop is infinite with positive annealed probability. We may therefore
assume that \(\gamma_o\) is finite almost surely. Its finite vertex support,
local finiteness of the tree, and finiteness of the Poisson counts leave only
finitely many outward one-link exits. In particular, \(C^{(1)}(o)<\infty\)
almost surely.

Start from \(\gamma_o\) and explore its outward one-link exits. Trace each
new multi-link loop and reveal the incident information needed to list its
exits, withholding the descendant environment beyond each exit until that
exit is processed. Contracting the multi-link components of the underlying
tree leaves a forest. Thus every outward uni-link enters a previously
unvisited component, and distinct exits lead to disjoint descendant
subtrees.

When an exit enters a child \(x\), its parent edge carries only one cross
and is absent from the multi-link graph. Since \(T\) is a tree, the full-tree
component \(M_x\) is contained in the descendant subtree below \(x\).
The entering uni-link is excluded from the offspring count.
Let \(\mathcal E_n\) record the information revealed while processing
generations \(0,\ldots,n-1\), including the entry edges and times of the
unprocessed generation-\(n\) frontier. Conditional on this history, the
descendant environments at that frontier are independent copies of the
unconditioned Galton--Watson tree with its link processes: no offspring
variable or descendant link process beyond a frontier entrance has yet
been exposed. Each entry time is \(\mathcal E_n\)-measurable. Shifting by
that time preserves the conditional descendant Poisson law, so each fresh
exit count has the law of \(C^{(1)}(o)\), independently of the other counts.

The generation sizes therefore form a Galton--Watson process with offspring
\(C^{(1)}(o)\). To include a possibly infinite offspring mean, put
\(g(s)=\mathbb E_\beta^{\mathrm{ann}}[s^{C^{(1)}(o)}]\).
Since \(g'(1-)>1\), there is \(s<1\) with \(g(s)<s\). Continuity gives a fixed
point below one, and the extinction probabilities obtained by iterating
\(g\) from zero increase to the smallest fixed point. This branching
exploration consequently survives with positive probability.

Suppose now that the full root orbit were finite. Every outward uni-link
it crosses must be crossed back, because removing its edge disconnects the
tree. The unique cross on that edge makes the returning excursion resume
immediately after the same cross time. Contracting these returning excursions
in the finite full orbit gives the entire restricted multi-link loop in
the current component, so the full orbit crosses every outward exit of
that loop. Apply the same argument in each component reached through such
an exit. Inductively, all vertices of the branching exploration would lie
in the finite full-orbit support. On survival the exploration reaches
infinitely many distinct components, which is a contradiction. Thus, on
survival, the full root loop has infinite vertex support.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:small}
There are constants \(\delta_0>0\), \(a>0\), and \(p>0\) such that the
following holds. Let \(\chi\) be a deterministic permutation of a set \(H\)
with \(|H|=2K\). Suppose that the number of points of \(H\) lying in

\(j\in H\) which does not lie in a \(\chi\)-cycle of length one or two,
\[
        \mathbb P\bigl(|C_{\tau\chi}(j)|\ge aK\bigr)
        \ge p-o_K(1).
\]

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:finreturn}
Fix a vertex \(x\) of \(T\). Let
\(H_x\) be the set of multi-link endpoints at \(x\). Say that an endpoint
\(h\in H_x\) has finite first return if the directed multi-link orbit reached
immediately after crossing \(h\) contains a directed interval on the circle of
\(x\). The first such interval determines the return endpoint at \(x\). Let
\(D_x\) be the event that some endpoint in \(H_x\) has no finite first return.
Then
\[
\begin{split}
&\widehat{\mathbb P}_{T,\beta}\bigl(
        \gamma_x^U\text{ has infinite vertex support}
        \bigm|\mathcal R_x\bigr)>0 \hspace{5 pt}\text{almost surely on }
\bigl\{\mathbb P_{T,\beta}(D_x\mid\mathcal R_x)>0\bigr\}.
\end{split}
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The set \(H_x\) is finite almost surely, because \(T\) is locally finite and
each incident edge carries finitely many crosses. For \(h\in H_x\), let
\(I_h\) be the cyclic interval on the circle of \(x\) immediately preceding
\(h\) and containing no other endpoint of \(H_x\). The endpoint times are
distinct almost surely, so \(|I_h|>0\). If \(h\) has no finite first return and
\(U\in I_h\), then the directed multi-link orbit from
\((x,U)\) contains the interval \(I_h\), crosses \(h\), and then follows the
orbit reached immediately after \(h\). If the resulting loop had finite vertex
support, then local finiteness would give only finitely many directed intervals
in that support. The successor orbit would then be periodic. Since the
successor map is bijective and \(I_h\) is the predecessor of the state reached
after crossing \(h\), this periodic orbit would have to return to an interval
on the circle of \(x\), contradicting the choice of \(h\). Define
\[
        V_x
        =
        \max\{|I_h|:h\in H_x\text{ has no finite first return}\},
\]
with the maximum of the empty set equal to zero. Conditional on the link
configuration, the probability over \(U\) of an infinite multi-link loop is at
least \(V_x\), and \(V_x>0\) exactly on \(D_x\). Therefore
\[
        \widehat{\mathbb P}_{T,\beta}\bigl(
        \gamma_x^U\text{ has infinite vertex support}\bigm|\mathcal R_x\bigr)
        \ge
        \mathbb E_{T,\beta}[V_x\mid\mathcal R_x],
\]
and the right-hand side is strictly positive almost surely on
\(\{\mathbb P_{T,\beta}(D_x\mid\mathcal R_x)>0\}\).
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:addunilinks}
For \(\mathbb P_{T,\beta}\)-almost every link configuration and every \(s\in[0,1]\) not
equal to a link time incident to \(o\), if \(\gamma_o^s\) has infinite vertex
support, then the full loop through \((o,s)\) has infinite vertex support.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Compare the full directed orbit with \(\gamma_o^s\). Whenever a uni-link sends it
into the component not containing \(o\) after that edge is removed, an infinite child-side orbit proves the claim.
If the child-side orbit has finite vertex support, local finiteness makes its state space finite. The tree provides
no other edge back; without a return through the same uni-link, the entered state would have both an internal
predecessor in a finite directed cycle and the incoming predecessor, contradicting bijectivity. After the return,
the full orbit resumes \(\gamma_o^s\). By local finiteness, only finitely many uni-link endpoints lie between
successive multi-link endpoints of the restricted orbit. Hence either
some excursion has infinite support, or induction along those endpoints shows
that the full orbit reaches all of them after finite excursions; in both cases
its vertex support is infinite.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:endpt}
The map \(\chi:T_x\to T_x\) is a permutation. If \(b=|B_x|\), then
\[
        \#\operatorname{cycles}(\chi)\le b+1.
\]
Moreover, conditional on \(\mathcal G_x\), \(\tau\) is uniformly distributed over the
fixed-point-free involutions of \(T_x\). More precisely, for every bounded
function \(F\) of a matching,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau-uniform}
        \mathbb E[F(\tau)\mid\mathcal G_x]
        =
        \frac{1}{(2K_x-1)!!}
        \sum_{\vartheta\in\mathcal M(T_x)}F(\vartheta)
        \qquad\text{almost surely},
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal M(T_x)\) is the set of fixed-point-free involutions of
\(T_x\), with the empty matching and \((-1)!!=1\) understood when \(K_x=0\).
The map \(\tau\chi\) takes each tagged return endpoint to the next
one along the loop.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
If \(H_x=\varnothing\), all assertions are immediate from
the empty-set conventions in the statement.  We henceforth assume that
\(H_x\ne\varnothing\).
The map \(\psi\) is a permutation of the finite set \(H_x\). Hence the
\(\psi\)-orbit of any \(t\in T_x\) is a finite cycle containing \(t\), and so
returns to \(T_x\). The induced first-return map is therefore a permutation of
\(T_x\).

Since \(\sigma\) is one cycle and \(\bar\rho_{B_x}\) is supported on the \(b\)
non-tagged endpoints, \(\bar\rho_{B_x}\) can be written as a product of at most
\(b\) transpositions. Multiplication by a transposition changes the number of
cycles of a permutation by at most one, so \(\psi=\bar\rho_{B_x}\sigma\) has at
most \(b+1\) cycles. First return to \(T_x\) creates one cycle for each
\(\psi\)-cycle meeting \(T_x\), and therefore cannot increase this number.

Conditional on \(\mathcal F_x\), the hidden tagged cross times are independent
and uniform. After their unordered set \(Q_x\) has been revealed, every
assignment of its \(2K_x\) points to the two slots of each of the \(K_x\) labelled
tagged children has the same conditional density. Every
\(\vartheta\in\mathcal M(T_x)\) has the same number \(2^{K_x}K_x!\) of such labelled
assignments. Hence \eqref{eq:tau-uniform} holds. Since
\(\chi\) is \(\mathcal G_x\)-measurable, this also gives the required
conditional independence from \(\chi\).

Starting from a tagged return endpoint, \(\chi\) follows the circle
at \(x\) and all non-tagged first-return excursions until the next tagged
departure endpoint. The excursion through that tagged child then returns
at the endpoint specified by \(\tau\). Thus \(\tau\chi\) gives the next
tagged return endpoint.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->

Let \(T\) be a locally finite rooted tree with root \(o\), and for a child

For a fixed rooted tree \(S\), write
\[
        p_S(\beta)
        =
        \mathbb P_{S,\beta}(|L_o|=\infty).
\]
Thus \(p_S(\beta)\) is a quenched probability. We prove both implications at a
fixed parameter \(\beta>0\).

First suppose that \(p_T(\beta)>0\). For a child \(y\), let \(I_y\) be the set
of times \(s\in[0,1]\) for which the directed loop through \((y,s)\) in the
isolated subtree \(T_y\) is infinite. By circular time-shift invariance,
\[
        p_{T_y}(\beta)
        =
        \mathbb E_{T_y,\beta}|I_y| .
\]
The root has finitely many incident edges and, at fixed \(\beta\), finitely
many incident crosses almost surely. Hence the root circle is split into
finitely many directed intervals. A directed orbit with infinite vertex support
cannot visit the same directed interval twice, because a repeated interval
would make the successor orbit periodic and therefore supported on finitely many
vertices. Thus an infinite loop can visit the root circle only finitely many
times. On the event that the root loop in \(T\) is infinite, let \(a\) be the
last root-circle interval in the forward orbit from \((o,0)\). The terminal
endpoint of \(a\) is a cross on some root-child edge \(\{o,y\}\), say at time
\(s\), and after crossing it the orbit never returns to the root.

Passing to the isolated subtree \(T_y\) deletes every cross
on the root-child edge, not only the cross at time \(s\).  The forward tail
after the crossing at \(s\) encounters no further endpoint of that edge on the
\(y\)-circle, since such an encounter would send the orbit back to the root.
Deleting all root-child crosses therefore leaves this forward tail unchanged;
it can only enlarge its first interval backwards across \(s\).  The isolated
loop through \((y,s)\) consequently contains the same infinite forward tail,
and hence \(s\in I_y\). Thus the root-child Poisson process has
hit \(I_y\) for some child \(y\). Conditional on the child-subtree
configurations, the root-child crossing times are independent absolutely
continuous Poisson points. If \(|I_y|=0\) for every child, this hit event has
conditional probability zero. Since \(p_T(\beta)>0\) and the root has only
finitely many children, there is a child \(y\) with
\(\mathbb P_{T_y,\beta}(|I_y|>0)>0\), hence \(p_{T_y}(\beta)>0\). Therefore
\(B_\beta^{\mathrm{loop}}(T_y)\) holds.

Conversely suppose that \(p_{T_y}(\beta)>0\) for some child \(y\). For this child,
let \(I_y\) be the set of times giving an infinite isolated \(T_y\)-loop. Then
\(\mathbb E_{T_y,\beta}|I_y|=p_{T_y}(\beta)>0\). On \(\{|I_y|>0\}\), require at the root that, among all
edges incident to \(o\), the only cross is on \(\{o,y\}\), and its time lies in
\(I_y\) but is not one of the finitely many child-subtree endpoint times on the
circle of \(y\). Its conditional probability is
\(\beta e^{-\beta\deg(o)}|I_y|\), which is positive whenever \(|I_y|>0\).
On this event, the loop from
\((o,0)\) hits the unique cross to \(y\). Splitting an interval of an infinite
isolated \(T_y\)-loop at the crossing time cannot make the forward orbit from
the outgoing side finite; otherwise the original isolated directed orbit would
return to the same interval and be finite. Hence the full loop has infinite
vertex support. Thus \(p_T(\beta)>0\), so
\(B_\beta^{\mathrm{loop}}(T)\) holds.

\label{lem:direct-orbit}
Fix \(\chi\), a perfect matching \(\tau\), and a starting side \(j\).
During the initial exploration, the left sides inspected
by Algorithm~R are distinct elements of the cycle
\(C_{\tau\chi}(j)\).  If the initial exploration ends by a strong
closure at exposure \(m\), then
\[
        |C_{\tau\chi}(j)|\geq m+1 .
\]
The only way in which this exploration can end without a strong closure
is a loop closure of the initial red corner.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->

Label the corner between \(s\) and \(\chi(s)\) by \(s\).
Orientation-reversing gluing of the sides \(s\) and \(\tau(s)\)
identifies the corner labelled \(s\) with the corner labelled
\(\chi^{-1}\tau(s)\).  Consequently two labelled corners become the
same vertex of the fully glued diagram exactly when their labels lie in
the same orbit of
\[
        \chi^{-1}\tau=(\tau\chi)^{-1}.
\]
A permutation and its inverse have the same orbits, differing only in
cyclic orientation.  Hence the corner labels identified at any one vertex
form exactly one orbit of \(\tau\chi\).

We now follow the active corner locally.  Suppose that the active
boundary contains the consecutive three-side segment
\[
        u\longrightarrow \ell\longrightarrow r
\]
at the red corner, and that the chosen partner \(z\) lies in the segment
\[
        p\longrightarrow z\longrightarrow q.
\]
After gluing \(\ell\) to \(z\) and deleting the paired sides, the two
segments are replaced by
\[
        u\longrightarrow q,
        \qquad
        p\longrightarrow r.
\]
In the non-overlapping case the active corner continues at the second
segment, with new left side
\[
        p=\chi_i^{-1}(z)=\chi_i^{-1}\tau(\ell),
\]
where \(\chi_i\) is the current boundary permutation.  This is the
next reverse-vertex step.  The splice deletes the exposed sides from
the current cyclic orders.  Hence, inductively, whenever the red corner
has a canonical continuation during the initial exploration, its new left
side \(\ell_{i+1}\) is the first still-unexposed element encountered after
\(\ell_i\) along the original \((\tau\chi)^{-1}\)-orbit.  If \(z=r\), then
\(\chi_i^{-1}\tau(\ell)=\ell\), so the move is precisely a strong
closure.

It remains to check the possible overlaps in this local description.
If \(z\) is the predecessor of \(\ell\), the adjacent weak splice gives
the same first-surviving reverse-orbit update.  If \(z\) is a boundary
\(1\)-cycle, the first reverse step lands at the deleted side \(z\);
the next surviving step continues the active corner unless the active
boundary is also a \(1\)-cycle, in which case the move is exactly a
loop closure of the initial red corner.  If \(z\) belongs to the
boundary \(2\)-cycle
\((z,w)\), the next surviving label is \(w\), the side inserted at the
active corner by the splice.  Finally, if the active boundary is a
\(2\)-cycle, pairing \(\ell\) to its other side is the strong closure,
and every external partner is covered by the preceding cases.  These
cases exhaust the degeneracies of the two-segment description.

It follows that, throughout the initial exploration, the
inspected sequence \(\ell_0=j,\ell_1,\ldots\) advances through the original
reverse orbit without repetition.  At a strong closure at exposure
\(m\), the \(m+1\) sides
\(\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_m\) are therefore distinct members of
\(C_{\tau\chi}(j)\).  The overlap analysis also proves the final
assertion.

\label{lem:direct-short}
Fix \(0<b<1/2\).  There are constants \(C_b,D_b<\infty\) such that, for
all sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{
   \max_{0\leq i\leq bN}R_i>D_b\delta N
 \right\}=o_N(1)
 \tag{20}\label{eq:direct-short-mass}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{
   \sum_{0\leq i<bN}\1_{\{P_i\leq2\}}
       >C_b\sqrt{\delta}\,N
 \right\}
 \leq C_b\sqrt{\delta}+o_N(1).
 \tag{21}\label{eq:direct-short-time}
\]
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Proposition~13 of
\cite{budzinski_curien_petri_2019_universality}, applied with
half-perimeter \(N/2\), gives the stochastic dominations
\[
 \sup_i L_i\preccurlyeq L_0+X_N,\qquad
 \sup_i B_i\preccurlyeq B_0+L_0+X_N,\qquad
 \sum_i\1_{\{P_i=2\}}\preccurlyeq B_0+L_0+X_N,
 \tag{22}\label{eq:direct-bcp-control}
\]
where \(\preccurlyeq\) denotes stochastic domination and
\(X_N=O_{\mathbb P}(\log N)\), uniformly in the initial configuration.
If simultaneously
\(\sup_iL_i\le L_0+t\) and
\(\sup_iB_i\le B_0+L_0+t\), then
\[
 \sup_iR_i
 \le (L_0+t)+2(B_0+L_0+t)
 =3L_0+2B_0+3t
 \le3\delta N+3t .
\]
The two separate stochastic dominations in
\eqref{eq:direct-bcp-control} and a union bound therefore give
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{
   \max_{0\le i\le bN}R_i>3\delta N+3t
 \right\}
 \le 2\,\mathbb P\{X_N>t\}.
\]
Taking \(t=\delta N/3\) and using
\(X_N=O_{\mathbb P}(\log N)\) proves
\eqref{eq:direct-short-mass}, for instance with \(D_b=4\).

It remains to control the times at which the active boundary is a
\(1\)-cycle.  Equation~(11) of
\cite{budzinski_curien_petri_2019_universality}, translated from
half-perimeter \(N/2\), is
\[
 \mathbb E\left[
   \1_{\{P_i=1\}}+L_{i+1}-L_i
   \,\middle|\,\mathcal F_i
 \right]
 \leq \frac{2}{D_i}
      +\frac{2B_i\1_{\{P_i=1\}}}{D_i}.
 \tag{23}\label{eq:direct-one-cycle}
\]
Let \(m=\lceil bN\rceil\) and
\(Y_1=\sum_{i<m}\1_{\{P_i=1\}}\).
Summing \eqref{eq:direct-one-cycle}, using \(D_i\geq(1-2b)N-1\),
and telescoping the \(L_i\)'s gives
\[
 \mathbb E Y_1
 \leq L_0+C_b+
 C_b\,\mathbb E\left[
       Y_1\,\frac{\max_{i<m}B_i}{N}
 \right].
 \tag{24}\label{eq:direct-absorb}
\]
Let
\[
        E_N:=\left\{\max_{0\leq i\leq bN}R_i\leq D_b\delta N\right\}.
\]
On \(E_N\), the last term is at most
\(C_bD_b\delta\,\mathbb E[Y_1;E_N]\).  On \(E_N^c\), the
deterministic bounds \(Y_1\leq\lceil bN\rceil\) and \(B_i\leq N/2\), together with
\eqref{eq:direct-short-mass}, contribute \(o_N(N)\).  Choosing
\(\delta\) so that
\(C_bD_b\delta<1/2\), we may absorb the restricted term in
\eqref{eq:direct-absorb}; hence
\[
        \mathbb E Y_1\leq C_b\delta N+o_N(N).
\]
Markov's inequality yields
\[
        \mathbb P\{Y_1>C_b\sqrt{\delta}\,N\}
        \leq C_b\sqrt{\delta}+o_N(1).
\]
Combining this with the last estimate in
\eqref{eq:direct-bcp-control} proves
\eqref{eq:direct-short-time}.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:direct-swallow}
Fix \(0<c<1/2\). There are constants \(C_c<\infty\) and
\(\delta_c>0\) such that the following holds for
\(0<\delta\leq\delta_c\). Let \(\tau_{\mathrm{str}}\) and
\(\tau_{\mathrm{loop}}\) be the indices of the exposures causing the
respective strong and loop closures of the initial corner, with a missing
type set to infinity. A closure caused by \(z_i\) thus has index \(i\).
Then
\[
 \mathbb P\{\tau_{\mathrm{loop}}<\tau_{\mathrm{str}},\
            \tau_{\mathrm{loop}}\leq cN\}
 \leq C_c\delta+o_N(1).
 \tag{25}\label{eq:direct-swallow}
\]
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Choose \(D=D_c\) as in \eqref{eq:direct-short-mass}, and put
\[
 \tau_D:=\inf\{i:R_i>D\delta N\},\qquad
 \nu:=\tau_D\wedge(\tau_{\mathrm{str}}+1)\wedge(\tau_{\mathrm{loop}}+1).
\]
The event \(\{i<\nu\}\) means that the short-boundary mass has not exceeded
its threshold by time \(i\), and no closure occurred at an exposure before \(i\).
It is \(\mathcal F_i\)-measurable, so \(\nu\) is a stopping time for this
filtration. The added ones retain the possibility of a closure at the
unrevealed exposure \(i\).
Set
\[
 m:=\lfloor cN\rfloor+1,\qquad
 V(p):=r^p,\qquad W_i:=V(P_i)\1_{\{i<\nu\}},
\]
where \(r\in(0,1)\) will be fixed below, and consider \(i<m\).

Condition on \(\mathcal F_i\), on the event \(\{i<\nu\}\), and put \(p=P_i\).
For a partner choice \(z\), let \(W_{i+1}(z)\) be the resulting value,
set to zero if that exposure causes either closure or makes
\(R_{i+1}>D\delta N\). Uniformity gives
\(\mathbb E[W_{i+1}\mid\mathcal F_i]=D_i^{-1}\sum_z W_{i+1}(z)\).
If \(z\) lies on a different non-short cycle of perimeter \(s\geq3\),
then \(P_{i+1}=p+s-2\geq p+1\), so this class contributes at most
\(rV(p)\) to this conditional expectation. At most \(D\delta N\) choices lie on different
short cycles, each with \(W_{i+1}(z)\leq r^{-1}V(p)\). Among non-stopping
choices on the active boundary, at most two give each resulting perimeter
\(q\geq1\). These latter two classes contribute at most, respectively,
\[
 \frac{D\delta N}{D_i}\,r^{-1}V(p),\qquad
 \frac{2}{D_i}\sum_{q\geq1}r^q\leq\frac{C_c}{N}.
\]
Since \(D_i\geq(1-2c)N-1\), first fix \(r<1\), and then take \(\delta>0\)
sufficiently small. The three bounds give constants \(\rho<1\) and
\(C_c<\infty\) such that
\[
 \mathbb E[W_{i+1}\mid\mathcal F_i]
 \leq \rho W_i+\frac{C_c}{N}\1_{\{i<\nu\}}.
 \tag{26}\label{eq:direct-drift}
\]
On \(\{i\geq\nu\}\), both \(W_i\) and \(W_{i+1}\) vanish, so the inequality
holds there as well. Taking expectations and iterating gives
\[
 \mathbb E W_i
 \leq \rho^i\mathbb E W_0+\frac{C_c}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\rho^k
 \leq \rho^i+\frac{C_c}{N(1-\rho)}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sum_{i<m}\mathbb E W_i
 \leq \frac{1}{1-\rho}+\frac{C_cm}{N(1-\rho)}
 \leq C_c.
\]
Since \(\1_{\{P_i=1,\ i<\nu\}}\leq r^{-1}W_i\), this proves
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{i<m}\1_{\{P_i=1,\ i<\nu\}}\leq C_c.
 \tag{27}\label{eq:direct-one-occupation}
\]

At any such pre-exposure state, a loop closure requires a partner on
another \(1\)-cycle. Its conditional probability is at most
\(D\delta N/D_i\leq C_c\delta\). Summing these adapted hazards and using
\eqref{eq:direct-one-occupation} yields
\[
 \mathbb P\{\tau_{\mathrm{loop}}<\tau_{\mathrm{str}},\
             \tau_{\mathrm{loop}}\leq cN,\
             \tau_{\mathrm{loop}}<\tau_D\}\leq C_c\delta.
\]
The sum includes every integer exposure at most \(cN\). Adding
\(\mathbb P\{\tau_D\leq cN\}=o_N(1)\) from
\eqref{eq:direct-short-mass} proves \eqref{eq:direct-swallow}.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:direct-clock}
Fix
\[
        0<a_1<a_2<b<1/2
\]
and set
\[
        q:=\sqrt{1-2a_1}-\sqrt{1-2a_2}>0.
\]
For all sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{
   |C_{\tau\chi}(j)|\geq \lceil a_1N\rceil
 \right\}
 \geq q-C_b\sqrt{\delta}-C_b\delta-o_N(1).
 \tag{28}\label{eq:direct-clock-bound}
\]
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Enlarge \(\mathcal F_i\) by the auxiliary Bernoulli variables, if
any, from exposures strictly before \(i\); this preserves the uniform
unexplored matching.
At exposure \(i\), before the partner is revealed, put
\[
        h_i:=\frac1{D_i}.
\]
If \(P_i\geq2\), the right side of the active corner is a specified
eligible partner.  We may therefore reveal the uniform partner in two
stages: first sample a Bernoulli variable \(\xi_i\) with parameter
\(h_i\); on \(\{\xi_i=1\}\), choose that specified side, and on
\(\{\xi_i=0\}\), choose uniformly among the remaining eligible sides.
If \(P_i=1\), adjoin an auxiliary Bernoulli variable with the same
parameter, independently of the actual partner choice.  In this
construction the sequence \((\xi_i)\) is independent, because its
conditional success probabilities \(h_i\) are deterministic.  A
success is an actual strong closure whenever \(P_i\geq3\).  The
possible discrepancies occur only at times for which \(P_i\leq2\).

Let
\[
        \Theta:=\inf\{i:\xi_i=1\}.
\]
For every fixed \(t<b\),
\[
 \mathbb P\{\Theta\geq tN\}
   =\prod_{0\leq i<tN}\left(1-\frac1{N-2i-1}\right)
   =\sqrt{1-2t}+o_N(1).
 \tag{29}\label{eq:direct-survival}
\]
The last equality follows by taking logarithms: the sum of the
squared hazards is \(O(N^{-1})\), while the first-order sum is a
Riemann sum for
\(\int_0^t(1-2s)^{-1}\,ds\).
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb P\{a_1N\leq\Theta<a_2N\}=q+o_N(1).
 \tag{30}\label{eq:direct-window}
\]

Let
\[
        A_b:=\sum_{0\leq i<bN}\1_{\{P_i\leq2\}},
        \qquad
        \eta:=C_b\sqrt{\delta},
\]
where \(C_b\) is increased if necessary so that
\eqref{eq:direct-short-time} gives
\[
        \mathbb P\{A_b>\eta N\}
        \leq C_b\sqrt{\delta}+o_N(1).
\]
To obtain \eqref{eq:direct-bad-coin}, consider only the first
\(\lfloor\eta N\rfloor\) indices \(i<bN\) for which \(P_i\leq2\).
Whether exposure \(i\) is counted is known before its auxiliary variable is
sampled. Before time \(bN\), each auxiliary Bernoulli variable has success
probability at most \(C_b/N\), so the conditional union bound over these
counted times is at most
\(C_b\eta\). On \(\{A_b\leq\eta N\}\), they include every active short time.
Adding the preceding exceptional probability gives
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{
   \xi_i=1\text{ for some \(i<bN\) with \(P_i\leq2\)}
 \right\}
 \leq C_b\sqrt{\delta}+o_N(1).
 \tag{31}\label{eq:direct-bad-coin}
\]
This use of the truncated count is valid although the set
\(\{i:P_i\leq2\}\) is random, since its indicator is known before the
variable \(\xi_i\) is sampled.

On the event in \eqref{eq:direct-window}, outside the event in
\eqref{eq:direct-bad-coin}, the first auxiliary success gives a strong
closure unless \(\tau_{\mathrm{loop}}<\Theta\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:direct-swallow}, with \(c=b\), bounds the latter
possibility by \(C_b\delta+o_N(1)\).  Thus
Lemma~\ref{lem:direct-orbit}, \eqref{eq:direct-window}, and
\eqref{eq:direct-bad-coin} give
\[
 \mathbb P\left\{
   |C_{\tau\chi}(j)|\geq \lceil a_1N\rceil
 \right\}
 \geq q-C_b\sqrt{\delta}-C_b\delta-o_N(1),
\]
because a closure at the integer time
\(\Theta\geq a_1N\) exposes at least
\(\lceil a_1N\rceil\) distinct orbit points.  This is
\eqref{eq:direct-clock-bound}.
