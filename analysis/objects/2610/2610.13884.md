<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:deterministic_limit}
Let $H=WQ\widetilde A-I$. If $H$ is invertible, then, as
$t\to\infty$, the process $\mathbf Z_t$ converges
almost surely to the deterministic limit
\begin{equation} \label{deter_limit}
\mbox{${\mathbf Z}$}^*=-C(WQ\widetilde A-I)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The reinforcement assumptions imply
$\sum_j|\widetilde A_{ji}|\leq1$ for every $i$.
Hence, in the induced matrix $1$-norm,
\[
\|WQ\widetilde A\|_1
\leq
\|W\|_1\|Q\|_1\|\widetilde A\|_1
\leq1.
\]
Every eigenvalue $\zeta$ of $WQ\widetilde A$ therefore
satisfies $|\zeta|\leq1$. Since
$H=WQ\widetilde A-I$ is invertible, $\zeta\ne1$,
and consequently $\operatorname{Re}(\zeta-1)<0$.
Thus all eigenvalues of $H$ have strictly negative
real parts. The associated ODE
\[
\dot z=zH+C
\]
has the unique globally asymptotically stable equilibrium
$-CH^{-1}$. In \eqref{eq:main_recur_vec}, the state
variables and martingale differences are bounded,
the step sizes are $1/(t+1)$, and the remainder tends
to zero almost surely. Lemma~\ref{lem:sas} therefore
gives the claimed convergence.
The same argument applies when the recursion is
initialized at any fixed finite time.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:invertibility_1}
    Assume that, for every critical vertex $i$,
$\mu_{ji}\ne0$ for every $j\in N(i)$.
 Then $H$ is invertible if either
of the following conditions holds:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item there exists a mixed-sampling vertex and either $\mathcal G$ contains an odd cycle or two mixed-sampling vertices are connected by a directed path of odd length;
        \item there exists a subcritical vertex and either $\mathcal G$ contains an odd cycle or two subcritical vertices are connected by a directed path of odd length.
    \end{enumerate}
Consequently, by Theorem~\ref{thm:deterministic_limit}, \[ \mbox{${{\mathbf Z}}$}_t \xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}} -CH^{-1}. \]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Suppose, for contradiction, that $H$ is singular. Since $H$ is a real matrix, there exists a nonzero real row vector $v=(v_1,\ldots,v_N) $  such that $vH=0$, i.e. $v=vWQ\widetilde A.$ Hence, for each $k\in\mbox{${\mathcal V}$}$, 
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:nullvector} 
    v_k = \sum_{r\in\mbox{${\mathcal V}$}} (2q_r-1)\mu_{rk} \sum_{j=1}^{N} w_{jr}v_j . 
    \end{equation} 
    Choose $l\in\mbox{${\mathcal V}$}$ such that $|v_l|=\max_{i\in\mbox{${\mathcal V}$}}|v_i|,$ and define $\widetilde v=\frac{v}{|v_l|}.$ Then 
    \[ |\widetilde v_l|=1, \qquad |\widetilde v_i|\le 1, \quad i\in\mbox{${\mathcal V}$} . \]
    Applying \eqref{eq:nullvector} with $k=l$ gives
    \[ 1 = |\widetilde v_l| = \left| \sum_{r\in\mbox{${\mathcal V}$}} (2q_r-1)\mu_{rl} \sum_{j=1}^{N} w_{jr}\widetilde v_j \right|. \]
    Using the triangle inequality, $1 \le \sum_{r\in\mbox{${\mathcal V}$}} |2q_r-1|\,|\mu_{rl}| \sum_{j=1}^{N} w_{jr}|\widetilde v_j|.$
    Since $W$ is column-stochastic and $|\widetilde v_j|\le 1$ for all $j$, 
    \[ 1 \le \sum_{r\in\mbox{${\mathcal V}$}} |2q_r-1|\,|\mu_{rl}| \le \sum_{r\in\mbox{${\mathcal V}$}}|\mu_{rl}| \le 1, 
    \] where the last inequality follows from \eqref{reinforcement_assumption}. Therefore equality must hold throughout. Consequently,
    \begin{enumerate}
    \item every in-neighbour $r$ of $l$ satisfies $|2q_r-1|=1$, i.e. $r$ is not mixed-sampling;
    \item the vertex $l$ is critical, i.e. $\sum_{r\in\mbox{${\mathcal V}$}}|\mu_{rl}|=1$; 
    \item $\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{jr}|\widetilde v_j|=1$ for every $r\to l$. 
    \end{enumerate}
    Since the weights in each column of $W$ are nonnegative and sum to one, the third condition implies that $|\widetilde v_j|=1$ for every $j$ with $w_{jr}>0$ and every $r\to l$. Hence 
    \[ |\widetilde v_j|=1, \qquad j\in N_l^{(2)}. \]
    Repeating the same argument at any vertex $x$ satisfying $|\widetilde v_x|=1$, we obtain inductively that 
    \[ |\widetilde v_j|=1, \qquad j\in N_l^{(2m)}, \quad m\ge 1. \]
    Moreover, for every $m\ge0$, 
    \begin{itemize} 
    \item every vertex in $N_l^{(2m+1)}$ is not \textit{mixed-sampling},
    \item every vertex in $N_l^{(2m)}$ is \textit{critical}.
    \end{itemize}
    Thus, the existence of a nonzero null vector forces all vertices in odd layers to satisfy $q_j\in\{0,1\}$ and all vertices in even layers to be critical.

    We now derive a contradiction. Suppose condition {\rm (i)} holds. First assume that $\mathcal G$ contains an odd cycle and that there exists a vertex $r^*$ with $|2q_{r^*}-1|<1$.  Since $\mathcal G$ is strongly connected, there exists a directed path from $r^*$ to $l$. Combining this path with the odd cycle, if necessary, yields a directed path from $r^*$ to $l$ of odd length. Hence $r^*\in N_l^{(2m+1)}$ for some $m\ge0$, contradicting the fact that every vertex in an odd layer must satisfy $|2q_j-1|=1$.

    Now, suppose there exist two mixed-sampling vertices $r^*$ and $r'$ connected by a directed path of odd length, i.e.  $r^*\rightsquigarrow r'$ (or $r'\rightsquigarrow r^*$) via a path of odd length. Then relative to $l$, at least one of the vertices $r^*$ and $r'$ must belong to an odd layer, giving the same contradiction. 

Next assume that condition {\rm (ii)} holds. The argument is analogous. If $\mathcal G$ contains an odd cycle and there exists a subcritical vertex $v$, then there exists an even-length directed walk from $v$ to $l$. Thus $v\in N_l^{(2m)}$ for some $m\ge0$, contradicting the fact that every even-layer vertex is critical.
    
Similarly, if two subcritical vertices are connected by a directed path of odd length, then relative to $l$ at least one of them must belong to an even layer, again contradicting the requirement that every even-layer vertex be critical.  Therefore $H$ is invertible. The convergence statement follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:deterministic_limit}.

=

=
vW
=
u$.

$\tilde J=\sum_{U\in\mathcal B}|U|^{-1}\mathbf{1}_U^\top\mathbf{1}_U$. Hence,

=

=

=

\frac{\sigma_Ur_{UV}}{|V|}\mathbf{1}_U^\top\mathbf{1}_V.

+
\sum_{i \in D_1} \mu_{ij} = 1
\end{equation*}

\begin{equation*}

\sum_{i \in D_1} v_i \mu_{ij}=1.
\end{equation*}

    \item $v_i=1$ for $i\in P_1$,
    \item $v_i=-1$ for $i\in D_1$.

Define $D_t=\mbox{${{\mathbf Z}}$}_t(I-\tilde J)$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle1}, it is
enough to show that $D_t\to{\bf 0}$ almost surely. We do this in two steps:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $\|D_t\|^2$ admits an almost sure limit.
\item $\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[\|D_t\|^2]\to0$.
\end{enumerate}
Together, these two statements imply $D_t\to{\bf 0}$ almost surely. From \eqref{recur_cycle}, in vector form, and using
\(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal D}A=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal P}\), we get
\[
\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[\mbox{${{\mathbf Z}}$}_{t+1}\mid\mbox{${\mathcal F}$}_t]
=
\mbox{${{\mathbf Z}}$}_t
\left[
\left(1-\frac{\bar m^{in}}{T_{t+1}}\right)I
+
\frac{\bar m^{in}}{T_{t+1}}QA
\right]
+
\frac{\bar m^{in}}{T_{t+1}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal P}.
\]
Since $\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal P}(I-\tilde J)=0$ and
$QA(I-\tilde J)=(I-\tilde J)QA$, we get
\[
\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[D_{t+1}\mid\mbox{${\mathcal F}$}_t]
=
D_tK_t,
\qquad
K_t:=I-\frac{\bar m^{in}}{T_{t+1}}(I-QA).
\]

\noindent\textit{Proof of (i).}
Write $D_{t+1}=D_tK_t+\Delta D_{t+1}$, where $\Delta D_{t+1}$ is a martingale
difference. Since the urn increments are bounded and $T_{t+1}$ grows linearly,
there exists a constant $C>0$ such that $\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[\|\Delta D_{t+1}\|^2\mid\mbox{${\mathcal F}$}_t]\leq \frac{C}{T_{t+1}^2}$. Therefore,
\[
\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[\|D_{t+1}\|^2\mid\mbox{${\mathcal F}$}_t]
=
\|D_tK_t\|^2+\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[\|\Delta D_{t+1}\|^2\mid\mbox{${\mathcal F}$}_t].
\]
Since  \(AA^\top=I\), \(QA\) is orthogonal. Using this, we get
\[
I-K_tK_t^\top
=
\frac{\bar m^{in}}{T_{t+1}}
\left(1-\frac{\bar m^{in}}{T_{t+1}}\right)
\{2I-QA-A^\top Q\}
=
\frac{\bar m^{in}}{T_{t+1}}
\left(1-\frac{\bar m^{in}}{T_{t+1}}\right)
(I-QA)(I-A^\top Q).
\]
Thus $I-K_tK_t^\top$ is positive semidefinite, and hence $\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[\|D_{t+1}\|^2\mid\mbox{${\mathcal F}$}_t]
\leq
\|D_t\|^2+\frac{C}{T_{t+1}^2}$.
Since $\sum_tT_{t+1}^{-2}<\infty$, $\|D_t\|^2$ is an almost supermartingale,
and hence admits an almost sure limit.

From the preceding conditional expectation recursion, $\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[D_{t+1}] =

\[

\left(1-\frac{\bar m^{in}}{T_{i+1}}\nu_k\right)
\longrightarrow0.

Therefore $\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[D_t]\to{\bf 0}$. We now consider the variance.
Let $G_k:=\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[\operatorname{Var}(\mbox{${\mathbb I}$}_{k+1}\mid\mbox{${\mathcal F}$}_k)]$.
The entries of $G_k$ are uniformly bounded. Applying the

\[
\operatorname{Var}(D_{t+1}) = K_t^\top\operatorname{Var}(D_t)K_t
+ \frac{(\bar m^{in})^2}{T_{t+1}^2}

\]
where an empty product is understood to be the identity matrix. Using the diagonalization of $I-QA$, we have
\[

\]

because $(I-\tilde J)Pe_s^\top=0$, the $s$-th row and the $s$-th column of this middle
matrix are zero. Hence only indices with non-zero eigenvalues contribute.
For $p,q\neq s$, Euler's product estimate gives

\leq

\sum_{k=1}^{t}
\frac{1}{k^{2-(\operatorname{Re}(\nu_p)+\operatorname{Re}(\nu_q))}}.

\mathcal O(t^{-1}),&

\mathcal O((\log t)/t),&

\mathcal O\!\left(t^{-(\operatorname{Re}(\nu_p)+\operatorname{Re}(\nu_q))}\right),&

=
\|\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[D_t]\|^2+\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Var}(D_t))
\longrightarrow0.
\]

By (i), $\|D_t\|^2$ has an almost sure limit. Since $D_t$ is bounded and
$\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[\|D_t\|^2]\to0$, bounded convergence implies that this almost sure limit
is zero. Hence $D_t\to{\bf 0}$ almost surely.

Finally, $D_t=\mbox{${{\mathbf Z}}$}_t(I-\tilde J)\to{\bf 0}$ means that each coordinate
synchronizes with its block average. Combining this with Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle1} we get that as $t \to \infty$, for every $i\in P_1\cup D_2$, $Z_t(i) \xrightarrow{a.s.} \xi_\infty$ and for every $i\in P_2\cup D_1$,
$Z_t(i)\xrightarrow{a.s.} 1-\xi_\infty$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:sas}
A recursion for a sequence $\{x_t\}_{t\geq0}$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$ of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:recursion}
x_{t+1}
=
x_t+a_t\bigl(h(x_t)+M_{t+1}+\epsilon_{t+1}\bigr),
\qquad t\geq0,
\end{equation}
is called a \emph{stochastic approximation scheme} if it satisfies the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)]
The \emph{map} $h\colon\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d$ is Lipschitz.

\item[(2)]
The \emph{step sizes} $\{a_t\}_{t\geq0}$ satisfy $a_t\in(0,\infty)$ for all $t\geq0$, $\sum_{t\geq0}a_t=\infty$, and $\sum_{t\geq0}a_t^2<\infty$.

\item[(3)]
The sequence $\{M_t\}_{t\geq1}$ is a martingale
difference sequence with respect to $\{\mbox{${\mathcal F}$}_t\}_{t\geq0}$ and, for
some constant $K>0$,
\[
\mbox{${\mathbb E}$}[\|M_{t+1}\|^2\mid\mbox{${\mathcal F}$}_t]
\leq K(1+\|x_t\|^2)
\qquad \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}
\]

\item[(4)]
The \emph{solution} $\{x_t\}_{t\geq0}$ satisfies $\sup_{t\geq0}\|x_t\|<\infty$ $\mathbb{P}$-a.s.

\item[(5)]
The \emph{errors} $\{\epsilon_t\}_{t\geq1}$ are adapted to the filtration $\{\mbox{${\mathcal F}$}_t\}_{t\geq0}$, are bounded, and satisfy $\epsilon_t\to0$ $\mathbb{P}$-a.s.
\end{enumerate}

\label{lem:sas}
Suppose that conditions~{\rm(1)--(5)} in Definition~\ref{def:sas} hold. Furthermore, suppose that the set of limit points of the solutions to the ODE $y'(u)=h(y(u))$, $u\geq0$, with $y(0)\in\mathbb{R}^d$ coincides with the set of zeroes of $h$, and that these zeroes are isolated. \label{aux:duflo_martingale_estimate}
Let $(M_n)$ be a square-integrable martingale with increasing process $\langle M\rangle_n$, and set $\langle M\rangle_\infty:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle M\rangle_n$. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item

\item

$M_n/\langle M\rangle_n\xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}0$. More precisely, for every $\gamma>0$, on $\{\langle M\rangle_\infty=\infty\}$,

M_n
=

\langle M\rangle_n^{1/2}
(\log\langle M\rangle_n)^{(1+\gamma)/2}
\right)
\qquad\text{a.s.}
\]
\end{itemize}

<!-- theorem-15: body_md -->
\label{aux:MP_compact_LIL}
Consider the stochastic approximation algorithm
\[
Z_{n+1}
=
Z_n+\gamma_n\bigl[h(Z_n)+r_{n+1}\bigr]
+\sigma_n\xi_{n+1},
\]
where $Z_n\in\mbox{${\mathbb R}$}^d$, $\gamma_n>0$, and $\sigma_n>0$. Set $v_n:=\gamma_n/\sigma_n^2$ and $s_n:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_k$. Assume the following conditions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[{\bf (1)}]
There exists a deterministic point $z_\star\in\mbox{${\mathbb R}$}^d$ such that $Z_n\to z_\star$ almost surely.

\item[{\bf (2)}]
The function $h$ is differentiable at $z_\star$, and its Jacobian matrix $H:=Dh(z_\star)$ satisfies
\[
\max\{\mathrm{Re}(\mu):\mu\in\mathrm{Sp}(H)\}=-L<0.
\]

\item[{\bf (3)}]
We have $\sigma_n\to0$, $\gamma_n\to0$, $s_n\to\infty$, $\gamma_n/\gamma_{n+1}=1+O(\gamma_n)$, and there exists $\rho_{\mathrm{MP}}\in[0,2L)$ such that
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac1{\gamma_n}
\left(1-\frac{v_{n-1}}{v_n}\right)
=
\rho_{\mathrm{MP}}.
\]

\item[{\bf (4)}]
There exist a positive definite matrix $\Gamma$ and independent $\mathcal N(0,\Gamma)$ random vectors $(\eta_k)_{k\geq1}$ such that
\[
\left\|
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi_{k+1}
-
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k+1}
\right\|_2
=
o\!\left(
\sqrt{\frac{\log s_n}{\gamma_n}}
\right)
\qquad\text{a.s.}
\]

\item[{\bf (5)}]
The remainder satisfies $r_{n+1}=r_{n+1}^{(1)}+o(\|Z_n-z_\star\|_2)$ a.s. with $\|r_{n+1}^{(1)}\|_2=O(v_n^{-1/2})$ a.s.
\end{enumerate}

Define
\[
\Sigma
:=
\int_0^\infty
\exp\!\left((H+\tfrac{\rho_{\mathrm{MP}}}{2}I)u\right)^\top
\Gamma
\exp\!\left((H+\tfrac{\rho_{\mathrm{MP}}}{2}I)u\right)
\,du.
\]
Then, with probability one, the sequence
$\left\{\sqrt{v_n/(2\log s_n)}\,(Z_n-z_\star)\right\}_{n\geq1}$
is relatively compact in $\mbox{${\mathbb R}$}^d$, and its set of limit points is the ellipsoid
$\{x\in\mbox{${\mathbb R}$}^d:x\Sigma^{-1}x^\top\leq1\}$.
