<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:extremal}
Let $f\inLC(X)$.
As $t\to\infty$, all weak-$*$ accumulation points of $m_{tf}$ are
$f$-extremal measures, and $P(tf)-t\beta(f)$ decreases to the limit
$h_{\max}(f)$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The result seems to have first appeared in the unpublished manuscript
\cite{conzeguivarch}; see \cite[Thm.~1]{morris} for a proof in a more
general (countable alphabet) setting.
The first part of the lemma appears in \cite{coelho}, and published
versions appear in \cite[Prop.~29 (i)]{clt} and \cite[Lem.~14]{jetds}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- mainthm-1: body_md -->
Let $(X,\sigma)$ be a topologically mixing subshift of finite type, let
$f:X\to\mathbb R$ be locally constant, and for $t\in\mathbb R$ let $m_{tf}$ denote the
unique equilibrium measure for $tf$.
Then $m_{tf}$ converges in the weak-$*$ topology as $t\to\infty$.
<!-- end mainthm-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:formula}
For all $t\in\mathbb R$, the number $z(t):=e^{-P(tf)}$ is a simple zero of
$\Delta(\cdot,t,0)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formula}
m_{tf}(g)\,z(t)\,\partial_z\Delta(z(t),t,0)=\partial_u\Delta(z(t),t,0)\,.
\end{equation}
Moreover, the functions $t\mapsto z(t)$ and $t\mapsto m_{tf}(g)$ are real
analytic on $\mathbb R$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For $t,u\in\mathbb R$, set $z(t,u):=e^{-P(tf+ug)}$.
We have $\Delta(z,t,u)=\prod_{i=1}^N(1-z\lambda_i)$, where
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N$ are the eigenvalues of $B_{t,u}$, repeated
according to algebraic multiplicity.
Since $e^{P(tf+ug)}$ is a simple eigenvalue, it follows that $z(t,u)$ is
a simple zero of $\Delta(\cdot,t,u)$.
The coefficients of the polynomial $\Delta(\cdot,t,u)$ are real analytic
functions of $(t,u)$, and $z(t,u)$ depends continuously on $(t,u)$, so
$(t,u)\mapsto z(t,u)$ is real analytic by the implicit function theorem.
Differentiating the identity $\Delta(z(t,u),t,u)=0$ with respect to $u$
gives
$\partial_z\Delta(z(t,u),t,u)\,\partial_u z(t,u)+\partial_u\Delta(z(t,u),t,u)=0$.
Now $\partial_uP(tf+ug)|_{u=0}=m_{tf}(g)$ (see e.g.~\cite[p.~133]{ruellebook}),
so $\partial_uz(t,0)=-m_{tf}(g)\,z(t)$, and (\ref{eq:formula}) follows.
In particular, $m_{tf}(g)=-\partial_uz(t,0)/z(t)$ is real analytic in $t$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:bounded}
Let $y:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be continuous and bounded.
Suppose there exist $D\in\mathbb N$ and $q_0,\ldots,q_D\in\mathcal E$, not all
identically zero, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algrel}
\sum_{j=0}^D q_j(t)\,y(t)^j=0 \quad\text{for all } t\in\mathbb R\,.
\end{equation}
Then $\lim_{t\to\infty}y(t)$ exists.
If, moreover, $q_0,\ldots,q_D\in\mathcal E_\mathbb Q$, then $\lim_{t\to\infty}y(t)$ is an
algebraic number of degree at most $D$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Collecting terms with equal exponents, and discarding those exponents for
which all coefficients vanish, we may write
\begin{equation*}
q_j(t)=\sum_{k=1}^K b_{j,k}\,e^{\gamma_kt}\qquad\text{for } 0\le j\le D\,,
\end{equation*}
where $\gamma_1>\gamma_2>\cdots>\gamma_K$ and, for all $1\le k\le K$, the
coefficients $b_{0,k},\ldots,b_{D,k}$ are not all zero; here $K\ge1$,
since the $q_j$ are not all identically zero.
The polynomial
\begin{equation*}
Q(\xi):=\sum_{j=0}^D b_{j,1}\,\xi^j
\end{equation*}
is therefore non-zero, and $e^{-\gamma_1t}q_j(t)\to b_{j,1}$ as
$t\to\infty$, for $0\le j\le D$.
If $t_i\to\infty$ and $y(t_i)$ converges to some $y^*\in\mathbb R$, then multiplying
(\ref{eq:algrel}) by $e^{-\gamma_1t}$, setting $t=t_i$, and letting
$i\to\infty$, gives
\begin{equation*}
0=\lim_{i\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^D e^{-\gamma_1t_i}q_j(t_i)\,y(t_i)^j
=\sum_{j=0}^D b_{j,1}\,(y^*)^j=Q(y^*)\,.
\end{equation*}
Since $y$ is bounded, at least one such $y^*$ exists, so $Q$ is not
constant, and its set of zeros is finite.
Set $\underline y:=\liminf_{t\to\infty}y(t)$ and
$\overline y:=\limsup_{t\to\infty}y(t)$.
By continuity of $y$, all points of the interval
$[\underline y,\overline y]$ are of the form $\lim_{i\to\infty}y(t_i)$
for some sequence $t_i\to\infty$, so this interval is contained in the
finite set of zeros of $Q$.
Therefore $\underline y=\overline y$, so $\lim_{t\to\infty}y(t)$ exists,
and it is a zero of $Q$.
If $q_0,\ldots,q_D\in\mathcal E_\mathbb Q$ then the above representations can be obtained
by collecting terms in representations with rational coefficients, so the
$b_{j,k}$ are rational.
In this case $Q$ is a non-zero polynomial of degree at most $D$ with
rational coefficients, so its zero $\lim_{t\to\infty}y(t)$ is an algebraic
number of degree at most $D$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Since $LC(X)$ is dense in $C(X)$, it suffices to show that the limit of
$m_{tf}(g)$ as $t\to\infty$ exists for all $g\inLC(X)$.
Fix $g\inLC(X)$, let $B$, $N$, $\Delta$ and $c_n$ be as in
Section~\ref{sec:formula}, and for $t\in\mathbb R$ set
\begin{equation*}
y(t):=m_{tf}(g) \quad\text{and}\quad z(t):=e^{-P(tf)}\,.
\end{equation*}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:formula}, $y$ and $z$ are real analytic, and
$|y(t)|\le\|g\|_\infty$ for all $t\in\mathbb R$.

The rest of the proof uses only elementary algebra.
Let $\mathcal A$ denote the ring of real analytic functions $\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$.
If $a,b\in\mathcal A$ satisfy $ab=0$ and $a\ne0$, then $b$ vanishes outside
the zero set of $a$, which is discrete, so $b=0$ by continuity; in other
words, $\mathcal A$ is an integral domain.
Consequently (see \cite[\S7.5, Thm.~15]{dummitfoote})
$\mathcal A$ is contained in a field $\mathcal K$, its field of fractions,
whose elements are the quotients $a/b$ with $a,b\in\mathcal A$ and $b\ne0$,
added and multiplied according to the usual rules for fractions, and with
$a/b=a'/b'$ if and only if $ab'=a'b$.
(Thus $a/b$ may be regarded as the function $t\mapsto a(t)/b(t)$, defined
outside the discrete zero set of $b$.)
Since $\mathcal E$ is closed under addition and multiplication, the quotients
$p/q$ with $p,q\in\mathcal E$ and $q\ne0$ form a subfield $F$ of $\mathcal K$; in
particular, $\mathcal K$ is a vector space over $F$.

By (\ref{eq:coeffs}), the formulae
\begin{equation*}
\Phi(\xi):=1+\sum_{n=1}^Nc_n(\cdot,0)\,\xi^n\,,\quad
\Psi_1(\xi):=\sum_{n=1}^Nn\,c_n(\cdot,0)\,\xi^n\,,\quad
\Psi_2(\xi):=\sum_{n=1}^N\partial_uc_n(\cdot,0)\,\xi^n
\end{equation*}
define polynomials with coefficients in $\mathcal E$, and hence in $F$.
Evaluating them at $\xi=z$ gives the elements of $\mathcal A$ defined, for
$t\in\mathbb R$, by
\begin{equation*}
\Phi(z)(t)=\Delta(z(t),t,0)\,,\quad
\Psi_1(z)(t)=z(t)\,\partial_z\Delta(z(t),t,0)\,,\quad
\Psi_2(z)(t)=\partial_u\Delta(z(t),t,0)\,,
\end{equation*}
so Lemma~\ref{lem:formula} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zy}
\Phi(z)=0 \quad\text{and}\quad y\,\Psi_1(z)=\Psi_2(z)\,,
\end{equation}
where $\Psi_1(z)$ has no zeros, since $z(t)$ is a positive simple zero of
$\Delta(\cdot,t,0)$ for all $t\in\mathbb R$.

We now show that the $N+1$ elements $1,y,\ldots,y^N$ lie in a vector
space over $F$ of dimension at most $N$, and are therefore linearly
dependent over $F$.
Let $d$ be the degree of $\Phi$, i.e.~the largest $n$ such that
$c_n(\cdot,0)$ is not identically zero; such $n$ exists, and $1\le d\le N$,
because $\Phi(z)=0$ while the constant term of $\Phi$ is $1$.
Let $V$ denote the vector space over $F$ spanned by $1,z,\ldots,z^{d-1}$,
i.e.~the set of elements $p_0+p_1z+\cdots+p_{d-1}z^{d-1}$ of $\mathcal K$
with $p_0,\ldots,p_{d-1}\in F$; its dimension is at most $d$, and hence at
most $N$.
Since $c_d(\cdot,0)$ is a non-zero element of the field $F$, the identity
$\Phi(z)=0$ can be rearranged as
\begin{equation*}
z^d=-c_d(\cdot,0)^{-1}\bigl(1+c_1(\cdot,0)\,z+\cdots+c_{d-1}(\cdot,0)\,z^{d-1}\bigr)\,,
\end{equation*}
so $z^d\in V$.
It follows that $zv\in V$ for all $v\in V$, and hence, by induction, that
$z^k\in V$ for all $k\ge0$, so $V$ is closed under multiplication.
Moreover, if $w\in V$ is non-zero then $v\mapsto wv$ defines an $F$-linear
map from $V$ to $V$ which is injective, since $\mathcal K$ is a field, and
therefore surjective, since $V$ is finite-dimensional; choosing $v\in V$
with $wv=1$ shows that $1/w\in V$.
The elements $\Psi_1(z)$ and $\Psi_2(z)$ belong to $V$, being polynomial
expressions in $z$ with coefficients in $F$, and $\Psi_1(z)\ne0$, so the
second identity in (\ref{eq:zy}) shows that $y=\Psi_2(z)/\Psi_1(z)$
belongs to $V$.
As $V$ is closed under multiplication, the $N+1$ elements $1,y,\ldots,y^N$
belong to $V$, so they are linearly dependent over $F$: there exist
$p_0,\ldots,p_N\in F$, not all zero, such that $\sum_{j=0}^Np_jy^j=0$.
Multiplying by the product of the denominators of the $p_j$ gives
$q_0,\ldots,q_N\in\mathcal E$, not all identically zero, such that
\begin{equation*}
\sum_{j=0}^Nq_j(t)\,y(t)^j=0 \quad\text{for all } t\in\mathbb R\,,
\end{equation*}
since $\sum_{j=0}^Nq_jy^j$ belongs to $\mathcal A$.
This is (\ref{eq:algrel}) with $D=N$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded} implies
that $\lim_{t\to\infty}y(t)$ exists.
(In the language of field theory, $V=F(z)$, and $z$ and $y$ are algebraic
over $F$ of degree at most $N$; cf.~\cite[\S13.2, Prop.~12]{dummitfoote}.)

Let $(X,\sigma)$ be a topologically mixing subshift of finite type, let
$f:X\to\mathbb R$ be locally constant, and for $t\in\mathbb R$ let $m_{tf}$ denote the
unique equilibrium measure for $tf$.
Then $\lim_{t\to\infty}m_{tf}(C)$ is an algebraic number for all
cylinders $C\subseteq X$.
Moreover, if $r\ge2$ is such that $f$ is constant on all cylinders of
length $r$, and $C\subseteq X$ is a cylinder of length at most $r$, then
the degree of $\lim_{t\to\infty}m_{tf}(C)$ is at most the number of
cylinders of length $r-1$.

Let $C\subseteq X$ be a cylinder, let $g$ be the indicator function of
$C$, and let $r\ge2$ be such that $f$ is constant on all cylinders of
length $r$ and $C$ has length at most $r$; such $r$ exists since $f$ is
locally constant.
Then $g$ is also constant on all cylinders of length $r$, so we may take
$B$, $N$, $\Delta$ and $c_n$ as in Section~\ref{sec:formula}, with $N$ the
number of cylinders of length $r-1$.
After passing to the higher block presentation, $g$ still takes values in
$\{0,1\}$, so the coefficients of the polynomials $\Phi$, $\Psi_1$ and
$\Psi_2$ in the proof of the Theorem belong to $\mathcal E_\mathbb Q$, by
(\ref{eq:coeffsQ}).
Repeating that proof with $\mathcal E_\mathbb Q$ in place of $\mathcal E$, and the field of
fractions of $\mathcal E_\mathbb Q$ in place of $F$, gives $q_0,\ldots,q_N\in\mathcal E_\mathbb Q$, not
all identically zero, satisfying (\ref{eq:algrel}) with $D=N$ for
$y(t)=m_{tf}(C)$.
The second part of Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded} then shows that
$\lim_{t\to\infty}m_{tf}(C)$ is an algebraic number of degree at most $N$.
