\label{prop:no-continuity}
Let $\mathcal{X} = \mathbb{Z}$ and, for $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$, let $P_{\theta}^{}$ be defined by
\[
  4P_{\theta}^{} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(-1\vert0\right)}{(-1\vert0)}{(-1\vert0)}{(-1\vert0)}} = 4P_{\theta}^{} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(1\vert0\right)}{(1\vert0)}{(1\vert0)}{(1\vert0)}}
  = 2 P_{\theta}^{} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(0\vert0\right)}{(0\vert0)}{(0\vert0)}{(0\vert0)}} = 1,
\]
and, for every $x \neq 0$,
\[
  P_{\theta}^{} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(x + \mathrm{sgn}(x)\vertx\right)}{(x + \mathrm{sgn}(x)\vertx)}{(x + \mathrm{sgn}(x)\vertx)}{(x + \mathrm{sgn}(x)\vertx)}}
  = 1 - \frac{1}{1 + |\theta| x^{2}}
  = 1 - P_{\theta}^{} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(0\vertx\right)}{(0\vertx)}{(0\vertx)}{(0\vertx)}}.
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item If $\theta = 0$, the chain is irreducible, aperiodic, positive recurrent and uniformly ergodic, and admits a unique invariant distribution.
  \item If $\theta \neq 0$, the chain escapes to $+\infty$ or to $-\infty$ with positive probability, and no invariant probability distribution exists.
\end{itemize}

For $\theta = 0$, the chain is supported on $\{-1,0,1\}$, where it is irreducible and aperiodic.
In addition, Doeblin's condition holds since, for any $x$,
\[
  2 P_{0}^{} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(\cdot\vertx\right)}{(\cdot\vertx)}{(\cdot\vertx)}{(\cdot\vertx)}} \geq \delta_{0}(\cdot),
\]
which proves the first claim by~\cite[Theorem~16.0.2]{MeynTweedie2009}.
If $\theta \neq 0$, the probability
\[
  \mathbf{P}\left(\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\ X_n \neq 0 \mid X_0 = 1\right)

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:local-versus-global}
From a theoretical perspective, \eqref{eq:estimator-pointwise} and \eqref{eq:estimator-global} define the same estimator.
From a computational perspective, however, the two representations differ significantly.
The formulation given in \eqref{eq:estimator-pointwise} is \emph{local}, since it only requires access to the transition probabilities $P^{k} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(\cdot\verty\right)}{(\cdot\verty)}{(\cdot\verty)}{(\cdot\verty)}}$ for the states $y$ that are actually visited by the observations $(X_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n}$.
In contrast, the formulation \eqref{eq:estimator-global} is \emph{global}, as computing $\widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,k}$ requires access to the entire transition matrix $P^{k}$.
This distinction becomes particularly relevant when the state space is very large, or only implicitly specified, as noted in the introduction.
In such settings, storing or even accessing the full transition matrix $P^{k}$ may be computationally prohibitive.
As a consequence, the global approach may become impractical or impossible to implement, while the local formulation remains feasible since it only relies on the transition probabilities associated with the visited states.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:data-partition3}
Under Conditions~\ref{ass:regularity1} and~\ref{ass:regularity2}, if $\widehat{\theta}_{m}$ satisfies the general assumptions above with $g(m) \sim m^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:def:optimalsplit}
  \alpha^{*}
= \operatorname*{argmin}_{\alpha \in \left]0,1\right[}
     \mathbf{MSE}_{\infty}\left(\widehat{\pi}_{\widehat{\theta}_{m},\,n-m}^{\,k}\right)
= \frac{\sqrt{r_{2}}}
          {\sqrt{r_{1}}+\sqrt{r_{2}}}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, for any split of the form $m \sim \alpha^{*} n$,
\[
  \mathbf{MSE}_{\infty}\left(\widehat{\pi}_{\widehat{\theta}_{m},\,n-m}^{\,k}\right)
  < \mathbf{MSE}_{\infty}\left(\widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0}\right),
\]
if and only if $\sqrt{r_{1}}+\sqrt{r_{2}} < 1$.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rem:additional-knowledge-corollary}
From a practical viewpoint, one does not need to use the exact optimal split ratio $\alpha^{*}$ to guarantee that $\widehat{\pi}_{\widehat{\theta}_{m},\,n-m}^{\,k}$ improves upon $\widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0}$.
Indeed, any split of the form $m \sim \alpha n$ will yield a strictly smaller asymptotic MSE, provided that
\[
  \alpha \in \left]\psi^{-}(r_{1},r_{2}),
  \psi^{+}(r_{1},r_{2})\right[,
\]
where the expressions for the boundaries $\psi^{\pm}(r_{1},r_{2})$ are provided by \eqref{eq:def:interval:alpha} in Appendix~\ref{app:proof-data-partition3}.
Conversely, any split sequence where $m$ and $n-m$ are not proportional asymptotically (i.e.\ $m = o(n)$ or $n-m = o(n)$) will lead to an estimator converging at a slower rate than the standard parametric rate, making such splitting strategies irrelevant.

<!-- proposition-16: body_md -->
\label{prop:birth-death-law}
For $\theta, \varepsilon \in \, ]0,1[$, $P_{\theta, \varepsilon}$ is irreducible, aperiodic and reversible.
Moreover, if $\theta < 0.5$, the invariant distribution $\pi_{\theta, \varepsilon}$ is given by
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \pi_{\theta, \varepsilon}(0) &= \frac{1-\rho}{2\left(1-\rho^{L}\right)}, \\
  \forall 1 \leq x \leq L-1, \quad \pi_{\theta, \varepsilon}(x)
    &= \pi_{\theta, \varepsilon}(0)(1+\rho)\rho^{x-1}, \\
  \pi_{\theta, \varepsilon}(L) &= \pi_{\theta, \varepsilon}(0)\rho^{L-1},
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\rho = \theta/(1-\theta)$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:span-empirical}
Let $F$ be the vector subspace of~$\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}$ composed of the finite support vectors of~$\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}$, and for $n \in \mathbb{N}^{*}$, let
\[
  E_n = \left\{ \widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(A\right)}{(A)}{(A)}{(A)}} \,:\, A \in \mathcal{X}^{n},\
  \mathbf{P}\left((X_1, \dots, X_n) = A\right) > 0 \right\},
\]
where, for $A = (a_1, \dots, a_n) \in \mathcal{X}^{n}$, $\widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(A\right)}{(A)}{(A)}{(A)}} = n^{-1} \sum_{i = 1}^{n} \delta_{a_i}$ denotes the empirical measure of the sample $A$.
Then $\mathbf{span}(E_n) = F$.

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For $x \in \mathcal{X}$, let $e_x = (\delta_{x}(y))_{y \in \mathcal{X}}$.
Since every empirical measure has finite support, $E_n \subset F$, hence $\mathbf{span}(E_n) \subset F$, and it suffices to show that $e_x \in \mathbf{span}(E_n)$ for every $x \in \mathcal{X}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Independent and identically distributed observations.}\\
  Let $x \in \mathcal{X}$.
  We have
\[
  \mathbf{P}\left((X_1, \dots, X_n) = (x, \dots, x)\right) = \pi(x)^{n} > 0,
\]
and $\widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left((x, \dots, x)\right)}{((x, \dots, x))}{((x, \dots, x))}{((x, \dots, x))}} = e_x$.
Therefore, $\mathbf{span}(E_n) = F$.
\item \textit{Stationary Markov chain.}\\
  Since $P^{}$ is irreducible and positive recurrent, $\pi(x) > 0$ for every $x \in \mathcal{X}$, so that, the chain being stationary, any sequence $(x_1, \dots, x_n) \in \mathcal{X}^{n}$ such that $P^{} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(x_{i+1}\vertx_i\right)}{(x_{i+1}\vertx_i)}{(x_{i+1}\vertx_i)}{(x_{i+1}\vertx_i)}} > 0$ for all $1 \leq i \leq n-1$ satisfies $\mathbf{P}((X_1, \dots, X_n) = (x_1, \dots, x_n)) > 0$.
  Let $(x_1, \dots, x_{n+1}) \in \mathcal{X}^{n+1}$ be a chain of length $n+1$ such that for all $1 \leq i \leq n$, $P^{} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(x_{i+1}\vertx_i\right)}{(x_{i+1}\vertx_i)}{(x_{i+1}\vertx_i)}{(x_{i+1}\vertx_i)}} > 0$.
  We define $C = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ and $C' = (x_2, \dots, x_{n+1})$, which both belong to the set of samples defining $E_n$.
  We have
\[
  \widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(C\right)}{(C)}{(C)}{(C)}} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^{n} e_{x_i}
  \quad \text{and} \quad
  \widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(C'\right)}{(C')}{(C')}{(C')}} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 2}^{n+1} e_{x_i},
\]
hence
\[
  n \widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(C\right)}{(C)}{(C)}{(C)}} - n \widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(C'\right)}{(C')}{(C')}{(C')}} = e_{x_1} - e_{x_{n+1}}.
\]
Since $P^{}$ is irreducible and aperiodic, $P^{n+1}$ is irreducible.
Hence, for all $x, y \in \mathcal{X}$ with $x \neq y$, there exists a path $x = w_1, \dots, w_q = y$ of length $q \geq 2$ such that for all $1 \leq i \leq q-1$, $P^{n+1} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(w_{i+1}\vertw_i\right)}{(w_{i+1}\vertw_i)}{(w_{i+1}\vertw_i)}{(w_{i+1}\vertw_i)}} > 0$.
Thus, for each $1 \leq i \leq q-1$, there exists a chain $(z_1^i, \dots, z_{n+1}^i) \in \mathcal{X}^{n+1}$ of length $n+1$ such that $z_1^i = w_i$, $z_{n+1}^i = w_{i+1}$, and for all $1 \leq j \leq n$, $P^{} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(z_{j+1}^i\vertz_j^i\right)}{(z_{j+1}^i\vertz_j^i)}{(z_{j+1}^i\vertz_j^i)}{(z_{j+1}^i\vertz_j^i)}} > 0$.
Setting $C_i = (z_1^i, \dots, z_n^i)$ and $C'_i = (z_2^i, \dots, z_{n+1}^i)$, the previous identity yields
\[
  n \widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(C_i\right)}{(C_i)}{(C_i)}{(C_i)}} - n \widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(C'_i\right)}{(C'_i)}{(C'_i)}{(C'_i)}}
  = e_{z_1^i} - e_{z_{n+1}^i} = e_{w_i} - e_{w_{i+1}}.
\]
Summing these differences gives
\[
  \sum_{i = 1}^{q-1}\left[ n \widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(C_i\right)}{(C_i)}{(C_i)}{(C_i)}} - n \widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left(C'_i\right)}{(C'_i)}{(C'_i)}{(C'_i)}} \right]
  = \sum_{i = 1}^{q-1} \left(e_{w_i} - e_{w_{i+1}}\right)
  = e_{w_1} - e_{w_q}
  = \delta_{x} - \delta_{y}.
\]
Therefore, for all $x, y \in \mathcal{X}$ with $x \neq y$, $e_x - e_y \in \mathbf{span}(E_n)$.

On the other hand, for any $x \in \mathcal{X}$, consider a path $(x, x_2, \dots, x_n) \in \mathcal{X}^{n}$ such that $\mathbf{P}((X_1, \dots, X_n) = (x, x_2, \dots, x_n)) > 0$.
We write
\[
\begin{aligned}
  n \widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left((x, x_2, \dots, x_n)\right)}{((x, x_2, \dots, x_n))}{((x, x_2, \dots, x_n))}{((x, x_2, \dots, x_n))}}
  &= e_x + \sum_{i = 2}^{n} e_{x_i}
   = e_x + \sum_{i = 2}^{n} \left[ e_x + (e_{x_i} - e_x) \right] \\
  &= n e_x + \sum_{i = 2}^{n} (e_{x_i} - e_x).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $n \widehat{\pi}_{n}^{\,0} 
  {\mathchoice{\!\left((x, x_2, \dots, x_n)\right)}{((x, x_2, \dots, x_n))}{((x, x_2, \dots, x_n))}{((x, x_2, \dots, x_n))}} \in \mathbf{span}(E_n)$ and $\sum_{i = 2}^{n} (e_{x_i} - e_x) \in \mathbf{span}(E_n)$, it follows that $e_x \in \mathbf{span}(E_n)$ for all $x \in \mathcal{X}$.
The vector space spanned by $(e_x)_{x \in \mathcal{X}}$ is precisely $F$, consequently, $F = \mathbf{span}(E_n)$.
\end{itemize}
