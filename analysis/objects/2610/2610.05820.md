        f^{v_j,j} &= \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ K \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}, \\
        f^{\varrho \theta, j} &= \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho v_j \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}\, \theta^{\mathrm{num}, j},\\
        v_i^{\mathrm{num},j} &= \frac{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho v_i \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}},
        \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    where $\theta^{\mathrm{num},j}$ is any consistent discretization of $\theta$, is total-energy-conservative (TEC).
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Note that the condition to derive a TEC flux for $v_i^{\mathrm{num},j}$ in the $x_1$-direction decouples into
  \begin{equation}
  \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho v_1 \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \} v_3^{\mathrm{num}} = \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho v_3 \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \} v_1^{\mathrm{num}},
  \end{equation}
  which can be satisfied in many different ways, e.g., by $v_i^{\mathrm{num},j} = \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho v_i \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \} / \varrho^{\mathrm{num}}$, where $\varrho^{\mathrm{num}}$ is any consistent discretization of the density.
  We adopt the simplest choice $\varrho^{\mathrm{num}} = \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}$, since we found no reason to introduce a more expensive mean value.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The scheme~\eqref{eq:tec_fluxes} uses a different momentum discretization from the energy-conservative scheme proposed by Ricardo et al.~\cite{ricardo2024thermodynamicconsistencystructurepreservationsummation}:
here the skew-symmetric vorticity term is discretized by means of nonconservative split forms~\cite{artiano2026affordable}, and the scheme of Ricardo et al.\ is recovered when the vorticity term $ \left ( \nabla \times \boldsymbol{V} \right ) \times \boldsymbol{V}$ is discretized pointwise (i.e., evaluated at the right interface as $\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_i}{2} \left\llbracket \boldsymbol{g}  \right\rrbracket_{i+1/2}$) and the potential temperature numerical flux is the central one.
The freedom to choose the potential-temperature numerical flux in the present generalization, in contrast to the work of~\cite{ricardo2024thermodynamicconsistencystructurepreservationsummation}, plays a key role when deriving a method that conserves the entropy- as well as the total energy and guarantees preservation of isothermal and isentropic background states, as shown later in Section~\ref{sec:well_balanced}.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{th:es}
A numerical flux for the compressible Euler equations in vector-invariant form~\eqref{eq:vector_invariant_form_2d} satisfying
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
f^{\varrho\theta,j} &= f^{\varrho,j}\,\theta^{\mathrm{num}, j}, \\
\theta^{\mathrm{num},j} &= \frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}} - \frac{\theta^{\mathrm{min}}}{2\,\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}}\left\llbracket \theta  \right\rrbracket\,\operatorname{sign}\!\left(f^{\varrho,j}\right),
\label{eq:es_flux}
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $f^{\varrho,j}$ is any consistent numerical flux of the density, is entropy-stable (ES). Moreover, $\theta^{\mathrm{num},j} \in~[\theta_{\min},\theta_{\max}]$ with $\theta^{\max}_{i+1/2} = \max(\theta_i,\theta_{i+1})$ and $\theta^{\mathrm{min}}_{i+1/2} = \min(\theta_i,\theta_{i+1})$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The entropy stability condition~\eqref{eq:ec_condition} becomes
\begin{equation}
\left\llbracket \log \left (1/\theta \right )  \right\rrbracket f^{\varrho,j} - \left\llbracket 1/\theta  \right\rrbracket\, f^{\varrho \theta,j} \leq 0.
\end{equation}
Substituting the entropy-stable flux~\eqref{eq:es_flux} and using $\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}} = \left\llbracket 1/\theta  \right\rrbracket/\left\llbracket \log(1/\theta)  \right\rrbracket$, we obtain
\begin{multline}
f^{\varrho, j} \left ( \left\llbracket \log(1/\theta)  \right\rrbracket - \left\llbracket 1/\theta  \right\rrbracket\frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}} + \left\llbracket 1/\theta  \right\rrbracket\frac{\theta^{\mathrm{min}}}{2\,\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}}\left\llbracket \theta  \right\rrbracket\,\operatorname{sign}\!\left(f^{\varrho,j}\right) \right )
\\
=
- \frac{\theta^{\mathrm{min}}}{2\,\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}}\frac{\left\llbracket \theta  \right\rrbracket^2}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{geo}}^2} | f^{\varrho,j} | \leq 0,
\end{multline}
where we used
\begin{equation}
\left\llbracket \frac{1}{\theta}  \right\rrbracket = - \frac{\left\llbracket \theta  \right\rrbracket}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}^2_{\mathrm{geo}}}.
\end{equation}
Now we show that the numerical flux is bounded by the minimum and maximum values at the interface.
First, we notice that the inverse of the logarithmic mean of $1/\theta$ is bounded by the harmonic and geometric means~\cite{MeansGenerated}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:log_bounds}
\frac{2 \theta^{\mathrm{min}} \theta^{\mathrm{max}}}{\theta^{\mathrm{max}} + \theta^{\mathrm{min}}} \leq \frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/ \theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}} \leq \sqrt{\theta^{\mathrm{min}} \theta^{\mathrm{max}}}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, we can rewrite $\theta^{\mathrm{num}} = \frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}} - S$, where $S = \frac{\theta^{\mathrm{min}}}{2 \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}} \left\llbracket \theta  \right\rrbracket \mathrm{sign}(f^{\varrho,j})$ and therefore
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}} - |S| \leq \theta^{\mathrm{num}} \leq \frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}} + |S|,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
|S| = \frac{\theta^{\mathrm{min}} ( \theta^{\mathrm{max}} - \theta^{\mathrm{min}})}{\theta^{\mathrm{max}} + \theta^{\mathrm{min}}} \geq 0.
\end{equation}
For the lower bound, using~\eqref{eq:log_bounds} we obtain
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}} - |S| \geq \frac{2 \theta^{\mathrm{min}} \theta^{\mathrm{max}}}{\theta^{\mathrm{max}} + \theta^{\mathrm{min}}} - \frac{\theta^{\mathrm{min}} ( \theta^{\mathrm{max}} - \theta^{\mathrm{min}})}{\theta^{\mathrm{max}} + \theta^{\mathrm{min}}} = \theta^{\mathrm{min}}.
\end{equation}
For the upper bound, we have
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}} + |S| \leq \sqrt{\theta^{\mathrm{max}} \theta^{\mathrm{min}}} + \frac{\theta^{\mathrm{min}} ( \theta^{\mathrm{max}} - \theta^{\mathrm{min}})}{\theta^{\mathrm{max}} + \theta^{\mathrm{min}}}.
\end{equation}
We have to prove the following
\begin{equation}
\sqrt{\theta^{\mathrm{max}} \theta^{\mathrm{min}}} +  \frac{\theta^{\mathrm{min}} ( \theta^{\mathrm{max}} - \theta^{\mathrm{min}})}{\theta^{\mathrm{max}} + \theta^{\mathrm{min}}} \leq \theta^{\mathrm{max}},
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to proving the following
\begin{equation}
\sqrt{a_{i+1} a_i} \leq \frac{a_{i+1}^2 + a_i^2}{a_{i+1} + a_i} = 2\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ a \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \} - \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ a \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{har}},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ a \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{har}} = 2 \frac{a_i a_{i+1}}{a_{i+1}+a_i}
\end{equation}
is the harmonic mean.
Therefore, the proof boils down to the following inequality
\begin{equation}
\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ a \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{geo}} \leq 2 \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ a \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \} - \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ a \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{har}},
\end{equation}
which is satisfied by the strictly increasing monotonicity of the mean values generated by the integral~\cite{MeansGenerated}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
The simpler numerical flux for the potential temperature $\theta$
    \begin{equation}
        \theta^{\mathrm{num}} = \frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}} - \frac{\left\llbracket \theta  \right\rrbracket}{2}\operatorname{sign}\!\left(f^{\varrho,j}\right)
    \end{equation}
    is also entropy-stable, but it does not guarantee that $\theta^{\mathrm{num}}$ remains bounded in the interval $[\theta^{\mathrm{min}}, \theta^{\mathrm{max}}]$.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{th:positivity}
  Assume that all $\boldsymbol{u}_i^n \in G$.
  Consider an explicit Euler discretization of the FV scheme~\eqref{eq:fv_nonconservative_form} with flux $f^{\varrho \theta} = f^{\varrho} \theta^{\mathrm{num}}$, where $f^{\varrho} = \left (\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \} -\frac{\left\llbracket \varrho  \right\rrbracket}{2} \mathrm{sign}(v^{\mathrm{num}}) \right ) v^{\mathrm{num}}$, $v^{\mathrm{num}}$ is a consistent interface velocity, and $\theta^{\mathrm{num}} \in [\theta^{\min},\theta^{\max}]$ with $\theta^{\min} = \min(\theta_i,\theta_{i+1})$, and $\theta^{\max} = \max(\theta_i,\theta_{i+1})$.
  Then, the resulting scheme preserves the positivity of $\varrho\theta$, and hence of the pressure, under the Courant--Friedrichs--Lewy (CFL) condition
  \begin{equation}
      \Delta t < \frac{\Delta x}{2 \lambda}, \qquad
      \lambda \geq \max\left( v^{\mathrm{num},+}_{i+1/2}\,\frac{\theta^{\mathrm{max}}_{i+1/2}}{\theta_i},\;
      |v^{\mathrm{num},-}_{i-1/2}|\,\frac{\theta^{\mathrm{max}}_{i-1/2}}{\theta_i} \right),
      \label{eq:positivity_cfl}
  \end{equation}
  where $v^{\mathrm{num},+} = \max(v^{\mathrm{num}}, 0)$ and $v^{\mathrm{num},-} = \min(v^{\mathrm{num}}, 0)$ denote the positive and negative parts of the interface velocity.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The explicit Euler step of the first-order FV scheme for $\varrho\theta$ in the
$x$-direction reads
\begin{equation}
(\varrho\theta)_i^{n+1}
= (\varrho\theta)_i^{n}
- \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\left( f^{\varrho\theta}_{i+1/2} - f^{\varrho\theta}_{i-1/2}\right).
\end{equation}
With the upwind density flux
$f^{\varrho}_{i+1/2} = v^{\mathrm{num},+}_{i+1/2}\varrho_i + v^{\mathrm{num},-}_{i+1/2}\varrho_{i+1}$, the update can be split into three contributions
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(\varrho\theta)_i^{n+1}
= \varrho_i\theta_i
&- \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\,\varrho_i
\left( v^{\mathrm{num},+}_{i+1/2}\,\theta^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}
- v^{\mathrm{num},-}_{i-1/2}\,\theta^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\right)\\
&- \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}
\left( v^{\mathrm{num},-}_{i+1/2}\,\varrho_{i+1}\,\theta^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}
- v^{\mathrm{num},+}_{i-1/2}\,\varrho_{i-1}\,\theta^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}\right),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where we dropped the superscript $\mathrm{num}$ on the velocity for simplicity.
Collecting the different contributions yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(\varrho\theta)_i^{n+1}
= \underbrace{\varrho_i\theta_i
\left( 1 - \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}
\left[ v^{\mathrm{num},+}_{i+1/2}\,\frac{\theta^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}}{\theta_i}
+ |v^{\mathrm{num},-}_{i-1/2}|\,\frac{\theta^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}}{\theta_i}\right]\right)}_{(\mathrm I)}
&+ \underbrace{\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\,|v^{\mathrm{num},-}_{i+1/2}|\,\varrho_{i+1}\,\theta^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}}_{(\mathrm{II})}\\
&+ \underbrace{\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\,v^{\mathrm{num},+}_{i-1/2}\,\varrho_{i-1}\,\theta^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}}_{(\mathrm{III})}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We show that the first term is strictly positive and that the remaining two terms are non-negative.

The second and third terms are products of non-negative factors. Indeed, the velocity parts satisfy $|v^{\mathrm{num},-}_{i+1/2}| \ge 0$ and $v^{\mathrm{num},+}_{i-1/2}\ge 0$, the densities $\varrho_{i\pm 1} > 0$ by assumption, while
$\theta^{\mathrm{num}}_{i\pm 1/2}\in[\theta^{\min},\theta^{\max}]$ holds by construction.

The first term is the product of $\varrho_i\theta_i > 0$ with the factor in parentheses, which is non-negative under the CFL condition~\eqref{eq:positivity_cfl}:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}
\left( v^{\mathrm{num},+}_{i+1/2}\,\frac{\theta^{\mathrm{num}}_{i+1/2}}{\theta_i}
+ |v^{\mathrm{num},-}_{i-1/2}|\frac{\theta^{\mathrm{num}}_{i-1/2}}{\theta_i}\right)
\le \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}2\lambda < 1.
\end{equation}
Since $p = p_0(R \varrho\theta / p_0)^\gamma$, the positivity of $\varrho\theta$ implies $p_i^{n+1} > 0$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lemma:wb_temperature}
    A numerical flux preserves the hydrostatic balance~\eqref{eq:hydrostatic_balance} prescribed by a constant background temperature $T$ if
    \begin{equation}
    \theta^{\mathrm{num}} = \frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}}.
    \label{eq:wb_temperature_flux}
    \end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For a constant temperature, $T = \theta\,\Pi = \text{const}$ and thus $\theta = T/\Pi$, so we can integrate the hydrostatic balance exactly:
\begin{equation}
c_p \theta \partial_x \Pi = - \partial_x \phi,
\end{equation}
which, upon substituting $\Pi = T/\theta$, can be written as
\begin{equation}
-\frac{1}{\theta} \partial_x \theta = - \frac{1}{c_p T} \partial_x \phi.
\end{equation}
A solution must be of the form
\begin{equation}
  -\log(\theta) + \log(\theta_0) = \frac{1}{c_p T}\left (-\phi + \phi_0 \right ),
\end{equation}
which implies that, for any two points, we have
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:condition_isothermal}
    c_p T \left\llbracket \log \theta  \right\rrbracket = \left\llbracket \phi  \right\rrbracket.
\end{equation}
The condition~\eqref{eq:wb_vector_invariant} at the isothermal background state, using~\eqref{eq:condition_isothermal}, reduces to
\begin{equation}
c_p T \theta^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket \frac{1}{\theta}  \right\rrbracket + c_p T \left\llbracket \log \theta  \right\rrbracket = 0,
\end{equation}
which is satisfied if $\theta^{\mathrm{num}} = \frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}}$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma:wb_theta}
    A numerical flux preserves the hydrostatic balance~\eqref{eq:hydrostatic_balance} prescribed by a constant background potential temperature $\theta$ if
    \begin{equation}
    \theta^{\mathrm{num}} = \text{any consistent two-point function of $\theta_\pm$.}
    \label{eq:wb_theta_flux}
    \end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For a constant potential temperature, the hydrostatic balance law~\eqref{eq:hydrostatic_balance} has the closed-form solution
  \begin{equation}
  \Pi = \Pi_0 - \frac{\phi}{c_p \theta}.
  \end{equation}
  Thus, substituting in~\eqref{eq:wb_vector_invariant} and using the consistency of the numerical flux $\theta^{\mathrm{num}}$, we obtain
  \begin{equation}
  \left\llbracket \Pi  \right\rrbracket = - \frac{1}{c_p \theta}\left\llbracket \phi  \right\rrbracket.
  \end{equation}
  The condition becomes
  \begin{equation}
  c_p \theta \left\llbracket \Pi  \right\rrbracket -c_p \theta \left\llbracket \Pi  \right\rrbracket = 0,
  \end{equation}
  which proves the claim.

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{th:es_wb}
The numerical flux for the compressible Euler equations in vector-invariant form~\eqref{eq:vector_invariant_form_2d} given by
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
\label{eq:es_tec_wb_fluxes}
    f^{\varrho,j} &= \left (\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \} - \frac{\left\llbracket \varrho  \right\rrbracket}{2} \mathrm{sign}(V^{\mathrm{num},j}) \right ) V^{\mathrm{num},j}, \\
      f^{v_i,j} & = \delta_{ij} \left ( \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ K \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \} - \frac{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ c \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}}{2 \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}} \left\llbracket \varrho v_j  \right\rrbracket \right ) - \frac{\Bigl | \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ v_j \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \} \Bigr|}{2 \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}} \left\llbracket \varrho v_i  \right\rrbracket, \\
    f^{\varrho \theta, j} &= f^{\varrho,j} \left ( \theta^{\mathrm{num}} - \frac{\theta^{\mathrm{min}}}{2 \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}} \left\llbracket \theta  \right\rrbracket \mathrm{sign}(V^{\mathrm{num},j}) \right ), \\
    v_i^{\mathrm{num}} &= \frac{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho v_i \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \varrho \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}},\\
    V^{\mathrm{num},j} &= \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ v_j \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \} - c_p\theta^{\mathrm{num}}\frac{\left\llbracket \Pi  \right\rrbracket}{2 \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ c \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}} - \frac{\left\llbracket \phi  \right\rrbracket}{2\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ c \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}},\\
    \theta^{\mathrm{num}} &= \frac{1}{\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ 1/\theta \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}_{\mathrm{log}}},
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $c = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$ is the speed of sound, is entropy-stable and well-balanced for the isothermal and isentropic background states.

The numerical flux for the compressible Euler equations in conservative form~\eqref{eq:euler_conservative} given by
\begin{equation}

<!-- theorem-10: body_md -->
\label{th:dgsem_curvilinear}
Consider the semidiscretization~\eqref{eq:semi_high_order} with the volume and surface terms~\eqref{volume_terms_curvilinear} and~\eqref{surface_terms_curvilinear}.
If the volume fluxes $\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{vol}},\boldsymbol{H}^{\mathrm{vol}}$ are as in Theorem~\ref{th:ec_tec_wb} and the surface fluxes $\boldsymbol{f}^{\mathrm{num}},\boldsymbol{H}^{\mathrm{num}}$ as in Theorem~\ref{th:es_wb}, $\boldsymbol{M}$ and $\boldsymbol{R}^{T}\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{N}^{j}\boldsymbol{R}$ are diagonal and the metric identities~\eqref{metric_identities} hold discretely, then the vector-invariant semidiscretization is entropy-stable and well-balanced on general curvilinear meshes with slip wall or periodic boundary conditions.
<!-- end theorem-10 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The result is a specialization of Theorems~6.1 and~6.6 of~\cite{artiano2026affordable} to the vector-invariant fluxes of Theorems~\ref{th:ec_tec_wb} and~\ref{th:es_wb}.
  The well-balanced property follows from Lemmas~\ref{lemma:wb_temperature} and~\ref{lemma:wb_theta}.
  The slip wall boundary conditions are imposed through the interface entropy-stable flux~\eqref{eq:es_flux_normal}, evaluated at the outward-pointing face normal $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}$, using a reflected exterior state
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mirrored_state}
    \boldsymbol{u}_b = \left ( \varrho_-,\; \boldsymbol{V}_- - 2\left (\boldsymbol{V}_-\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\right )\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}},\;
      (\varrho\theta)_- \right ), \qquad \hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}} = \frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}}{\left\lVert \hat{\boldsymbol{n}} \right\rVert}.
\end{equation}
We now derive the condition for entropy stability at the boundary.
For the compressible Euler equations in conservative form, this condition has already been derived by Gassner et al.~\cite{gassner2018br1} (see also~\cite{hindenlang2020wall,svard2021boundary}, where slip wall boundary conditions are proved to be entropy stable when imposed weakly through the numerical flux).
Here, we show that this result extends also to slip wall boundary conditions for the compressible Euler equations in vector-invariant form.
We contract the semidiscretization~\eqref{eq:semi_high_order} with $\boldsymbol{\omega}^{T} \boldsymbol{M}$.
The SBP property~\eqref{eq:sbp_property}, the metric identities~\eqref{metric_identities} and the entropy conservation of the volume fluxes then
yield the curvilinear counterpart of Eq.~(178) in~\cite{artiano2026affordable}, which reads
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:entropy_rate_element}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\boldsymbol{M}_{ii}\,J_i\,U(\boldsymbol{u}_i)
      = \sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}
          \left ( \boldsymbol{R}^{T}\boldsymbol{B}\,\boldsymbol{N}^{j}\boldsymbol{R} \right )_{i,k}
          \tilde\psi^{\,j}_{k}
      - \boldsymbol{\omega}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\boldsymbol{R}^{T}\boldsymbol{B}\left ( \tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\,\mathrm{num},j}
        + \tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\,\mathrm{num}}
          \left\llbracket \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}^{\,j}  \right\rrbracket \right ),
\end{equation}
with $\tilde\psi^{\,j} = \hat{\boldsymbol{n}}^{\,j}\cdot(\psi^1, \psi^2)$ the contravariant entropy
potential.
The entropy rate of the element then reduces to
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:entropy_rate_faces}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\boldsymbol{M}_{ii}\,J_i\,U(\boldsymbol{u}_i)
      = -\boldsymbol{1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\boldsymbol{R}^{T}\boldsymbol{B}\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}^{j}\,,
    \qquad
    \tilde{F}^{j} := \boldsymbol{\omega}_{-} \cdot \left (
        \tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\,\mathrm{num},j}
      + \frac{1}{2}\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\,\mathrm{num}}\,
        \left\llbracket \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}^{\,j}  \right\rrbracket \right ) - \tilde\psi^{\,j}_{-}\,,
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}^{j}$ collects the values of $\tilde{F}^{j}$ at the face nodes, $\boldsymbol{u}_{-}$ denotes the value of the element itself, and $\boldsymbol{u}_{+}$ denotes the value of the neighboring element or the prescribed boundary state at the boundary of the domain.
For a face normal $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}$, we write $\tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}\circ(\boldsymbol{g}^1,\boldsymbol{g}^2)$ and $\tilde{\psi}(\boldsymbol{u},\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}) =\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot(\psi^1,\psi^2)$; thus, at each face node we have
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:one_sided_face_term}
    \tilde{F}^{j} = \tilde{F}(\boldsymbol{u}_{-}, \boldsymbol{u}_{+}, \hat{\boldsymbol{n}}) := \boldsymbol{\omega}_{-} \cdot \left (
        \boldsymbol{f}^{\,\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{-}, \boldsymbol{u}_{+}, \hat{\boldsymbol{n}})
      + \frac{1}{2}\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\,\mathrm{num}}\,\left\llbracket \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}  \right\rrbracket \right ) - \tilde\psi ( \boldsymbol{u}_-, \hat{\boldsymbol{n}})\,,
\end{equation}
where, with a slight abuse of notation, $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}} = \pm\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}^{\,j}$ on $\xi_j = \pm 1$ is the outward normal of the element and the face quantities satisfy
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:telescoping_property}
    \boldsymbol{f}^{\,\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{+}, \boldsymbol{u}_{-}, -\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}) = -\boldsymbol{f}^{\,\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{-}, \boldsymbol{u}_{+}, \hat{\boldsymbol{n}}).
\end{equation}
Note that $\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\,\mathrm{num}}$ depends only on the two states, while the normal enters the nonconservative term through $\left\llbracket \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}  \right\rrbracket$, so both the jump and the normal change sign and $\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\mathrm{num}} \left\llbracket \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}  \right\rrbracket$ is invariant under a change of the normal direction.
At an interface, we have therefore
\begin{multline}
    \tilde{F}(\boldsymbol{u}_{+}, \boldsymbol{u}_{-}, -\hat{\boldsymbol{n}})
      = \boldsymbol{\omega}_{+} \cdot \left ( \boldsymbol{f}^{\,\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{+}, \boldsymbol{u}_{-}, -\hat{\boldsymbol{n}})
      + \frac{1}{2}\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\,\mathrm{num}}\,\left\llbracket \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}  \right\rrbracket \right ) - \tilde\psi(\boldsymbol{u}_{+}, -\hat{\boldsymbol{n}})
      \\
      = \boldsymbol{\omega}_{+} \cdot \left ( -\boldsymbol{f}^{\,\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{-}, \boldsymbol{u}_{+}, \hat{\boldsymbol{n}})
      + \frac{1}{2}\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\,\mathrm{num}}\,\left\llbracket \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}  \right\rrbracket \right ) + \tilde\psi_{+}\,.
\end{multline}
The two contributions of the interface then sum up to
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:interface_pairing}
    \begin{aligned}
    \tilde{F}(\boldsymbol{u}_{-}, \boldsymbol{u}_{+}, \hat{\boldsymbol{n}}) + \tilde{F}(\boldsymbol{u}_{+}, \boldsymbol{u}_{-}, -\hat{\boldsymbol{n}})
      &= \left ( \boldsymbol{\omega}_{-} - \boldsymbol{\omega}_{+} \right ) \cdot \tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\,\mathrm{num}}
      + \frac{1}{2} \left ( \boldsymbol{\omega}_{-} + \boldsymbol{\omega}_{+} \right ) \cdot \tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\,\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}  \right\rrbracket
      - \tilde\psi_{-} + \tilde\psi_{+} \\
      &= - \left ( \left\llbracket \boldsymbol{\omega}  \right\rrbracket\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\,\mathrm{num}}
        - \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \boldsymbol{\omega} \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\,\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}  \right\rrbracket
        - \left\llbracket \tilde\psi  \right\rrbracket \right ).
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Summing up the contributions of all elements, the entropy rate reads
\begin{multline}
    \label{eq:entropy_rate_global}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} t}
      \sum_{\mathrm{elements}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\boldsymbol{M}_{ii}\,J_i\,U(\boldsymbol{u}_i)
      \\= \sum_{\substack{\mathrm{interior}\\ \mathrm{interfaces}}}\sum_{l}\boldsymbol{B}_{ll}
        \left [
          \left\llbracket \boldsymbol{\omega}  \right\rrbracket\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\mathrm{num}}
          - \left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \boldsymbol{\omega} \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\mathrm{num}}\left\llbracket \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}  \right\rrbracket -\left\llbracket \tilde\psi  \right\rrbracket
        \right ]_l
      - \sum_{\substack{\mathrm{boundary}\\ \mathrm{interfaces}}}\sum_{l}\boldsymbol{B}_{ll}\,\tilde{F}_{l}\,.
\end{multline}
Here, the sum over $l$ runs over all quadrature nodes on the interface.
The sum in square brackets is nonpositive by the entropy stability condition~\eqref{eq:TadmorDef_form3} and positivity of the weights $\boldsymbol{B}_{ll}$.
At a boundary interface, the exterior state is the prescribed $\boldsymbol{u}_b$ and the condition for entropy stability reads
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:wall_boundary_term}
    \tilde{F}_b = \boldsymbol{\omega}_{-} \cdot \left (
        \boldsymbol{f}^{\,\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{-}, \boldsymbol{u}_{b}, \hat{\boldsymbol{n}})
      + \frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{H}^{\,\mathrm{num}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{-}, \boldsymbol{u}_{b})\,
        \left\llbracket \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}  \right\rrbracket \right ) - \tilde\psi_{-} \geq 0
\end{equation}
at every boundary quadrature node. Note that condition~\eqref{eq:wall_boundary_term} generalizes the one derived in~\cite{gassner2018br1,svard2021boundary} by including the nonconservative contribution.

We now insert the reflection state~\eqref{eq:mirrored_state}.
Considering that $\tilde{\psi}_- = 0$ and that the only nonzero components of the entropy variables~\eqref{eq:ec_entropy_variables} are the density and potential-temperature ones, only $f^{\varrho,\mathrm{num}}$ and $f^{\varrho\theta,\mathrm{num}}$ enter the boundary term~\eqref{eq:wall_boundary_term}.
For the boundary state $\boldsymbol{u}_b$ in~\eqref{eq:mirrored_state}, $\left\{\mskip-5mu \left \{ \boldsymbol{V} \right \}\mskip-5mu \right \} \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{n}} = 0$ and $\left\llbracket \Pi  \right\rrbracket = \left\llbracket \phi  \right\rrbracket = 0$, so $f^{\varrho,\mathrm{num}} = f^{\varrho\theta,\mathrm{num}} = 0$ and the boundary term vanishes.
Therefore, $\tilde{F}_b = 0$ for any choice of the momentum flux; the slip wall does not contribute to the entropy rate, and the semidiscretization is entropy stable up to the boundary.

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
We emphasize that the semidiscretization of Theorem~\ref{th:dgsem_curvilinear} ensures entropy stability (and total energy conservation in the volume kernel) at the cost of a single nonlinear mean value, i.e., the inverse of the logarithmic mean.
In contrast, the schemes presented in~\cite{WARUSZEWSKI2022111507,artiano2025structurepreservinghighordermethodscompressible} require the evaluation of two nonlinear mean values.
Moreover, this scheme also preserves locally and globally two background states, while the schemes derived in~\cite{WARUSZEWSKI2022111507,artiano2025structurepreservinghighordermethodscompressible} can preserve only one given background state.
Furthermore, given the particular form of the nonconservative terms, which is antisymmetric, the computational cost of the flux-differencing DGSEM implementation can also be reduced for the nonconservative terms by computing only the upper or lower triangular part of the derivative operator~\cite{ranocha2023efficient, artiano2026affordable} (see also the implementation in our reproducibility repository~\cite{artiano2026vectorinvariantRepro}).
