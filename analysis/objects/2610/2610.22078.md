\label{thm:main-counterexample}
The ring $R$ in \eqref{eq:main-ring} is commutative, coherent,
local, nonnoetherian, and of Krull dimension zero.
There is a countably generated PGF $R$-module $B$ such that, for the injective
$R^{\mathrm{op}}$-module $I=D(R)$, one has
$\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(I,B^{\mathbb N})\ne0$ for every $n\ge1$.
Consequently, $\operatorname{Gfd}_R B^{\mathbb N}=\infty$.
There are a countably generated free $R$-module $Q$ and an
endomorphism $\delta:Q\to Q$ such that
$B=\ker\delta=\operatorname{im}\delta$, $\delta^2=0$, and the periodic complex
$(Q,\delta)$, with $Q$ in every degree and differential $\delta$,
remains exact after tensoring with every injective $R^{\mathrm{op}}$-module.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:character-Tor}\label{lem:product-into-bidual}
Let $T$ be an $R^{\mathrm{op}}$-module, $M$ an $R$-module, and $q\ge0$.
Then there are natural isomorphisms
$\operatorname{Tor}_q^R(T,M)^+\cong\operatorname{Ext}^q_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}(T,M^+)
\cong\operatorname{Ext}_R^q(M,T^+)$.
A pure monomorphism $N\to M$ induces a monomorphism
$\operatorname{Tor}_q^R(T,N)\to\operatorname{Tor}_q^R(T,M)$.
For $q\ge1$, the class
$\mathcal A_q(T)=\{M:\operatorname{Tor}_q^R(T,M)=0\}$ is closed under pure
submodules, pure quotients, direct summands, and direct limits.
For every family $(M_i)$ of $R$-modules, there is a pure
monomorphism $\prod_iM_i\to(\bigoplus_iM_i)^{++}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The isomorphisms follow from \cite[p.~241]{Lambek}, applied to $R$ and $R^{\mathrm{op}}$.
If $0\to N\to M\to D\to0$ is pure, its character dual splits
(see \cite[Proposition 9.1]{Stenstrom}). Thus the dual of
$\operatorname{Tor}_q^R(T,N)\to\operatorname{Tor}_q^R(T,M)$ is a split epimorphism,
so this Tor map is a monomorphism. The splitting also gives
$M^+\cong D^+\oplus N^+$, so the Tor--Ext isomorphism gives
closure under pure submodules and pure quotients.
Closure under direct summands follows from additivity of Tor.
Closure under direct limits follows because Tor commutes with
direct limits. For the pure embedding, see the proof of
$(ii)\Rightarrow(iv)$ in \cite[Theorem 4.4]{MP}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:explicit-ring}
The ring properties asserted in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-counterexample} hold for $R$.
It is free over every $R_N$ for $N\ge0$ and over every
$A_0\otimes A_j$ for $j\ge1$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The monomials obtained by choosing at most one of $x_j,y_j$ for
each $j$, with only finitely many indices used, form a $k$-basis
of $R$. Those using only indices greater than $N$ form an
$R_N$-basis of $R$. For $j\ge1$, those using neither index $0$
nor index $j$ form an $A_0\otimes A_j$-basis.
The monomial $k$-basis of $R$ is countably infinite, so $|R|=|k|+\aleph_0$.
Every finite matrix over $R$ has its entries in some $R_N$.
Since $R_N$ is finite-dimensional, the kernel of this matrix over $R_N$ is
finitely generated. Flat base change identifies the kernel over $R$
with the scalar extension of that kernel. Therefore every finite matrix over $R$ has finitely
generated kernel, and $R$ is coherent (see \cite{Chase,GT}).

Let $\mathfrak m$ be the augmentation ideal of $R$.
Every element of $\mathfrak m$ is nilpotent in some $R_N$, whereas
an element with nonzero constant coefficient is a unit in a
subring $R_N$ containing it. Hence $\mathfrak m$ is the unique maximal ideal and
the nilradical. It follows that $R$ has Krull dimension zero.
The classes of $x_j,y_j$ are linearly independent in
$\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$. Thus $\mathfrak m$ is not finitely
generated, and $R$ is not noetherian.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:finite-complete}
The periodic complex $(U_j,\Delta_j)$ is exact and remains exact
after tensoring with every injective $\Lambda_j^{\mathrm{op}}$-module.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Define a $k$-linear map $h:F_0\to F_0$ by
$h(e_n)=0$, $h(x_0e_n)=e_{2n}$, and
$h(y_0e_n)=e_{2n+1}$.
Then $dh+hd=1$, and $h\otimes1$ commutes with $1\otimes t_j$.
Consequently,
$$
 \Delta_j\begin{pmatrix}h\otimes1&0\\0&-h\otimes1\end{pmatrix}
 +\begin{pmatrix}h\otimes1&0\\0&-h\otimes1\end{pmatrix}\Delta_j=1.
$$
Thus $(U_j,\Delta_j)$ is exact.

Let $\epsilon_j,a_{x_j},a_{y_j}$ be the basis of $D(A_j)$
dual to $1,x_j,y_j$.
The differential on $D(A_j)\otimes_{A_j}V_j$ sends
$a_{x_j}f_n$ to $\epsilon_jf_{2n}$,
$a_{y_j}f_n$ to $\epsilon_jf_{2n+1}$, and
$\epsilon_jf_n$ to zero.
It has a $k$-linear contraction $h'$ defined by
$h'(\epsilon_jf_{2n})=a_{x_j}f_n$,
$h'(\epsilon_jf_{2n+1})=a_{y_j}f_n$, and
$h'(a_{x_j}f_n)=h'(a_{y_j}f_n)=0$.
Since $\Lambda_j$ is finite-dimensional, one has
$$
 D(\Lambda_j)\otimes_{\Lambda_j}U_j
 \cong\bigl((D(A_0)\otimes_{A_0}F_0)
       \otimes(D(A_j)\otimes_{A_j}V_j)\bigr)^{\oplus2}.
$$
Under this identification, the differential $1\otimes\Delta_j$
has the same block matrix, with the maps induced by $d$ and $t_j$.
The map $1\otimes h'$ contracts the differential induced by $t_j$
and commutes with the differential induced by $d$.
Hence
$$
 (1\otimes\Delta_j)
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\otimes h'\\1\otimes h'&0\end{pmatrix}
 +\begin{pmatrix}0&1\otimes h'\\1\otimes h'&0\end{pmatrix}
 (1\otimes\Delta_j)=1.
$$
This proves exactness after tensoring with $D(\Lambda_j)$.

Finally, every injective $\Lambda_j^{\mathrm{op}}$-module $J$ is a direct
summand of a direct sum of copies of $D(\Lambda_j)$.
Indeed, $a\mapsto[b\mapsto ab]$ embeds $J$ into
$\operatorname{Hom}_k(\Lambda_j,J)\congD(\Lambda_j)\otimes J$.
The embedding splits by injectivity. A vector-space basis of $J$
expresses the latter module as a direct sum of copies of
$D(\Lambda_j)$. Tensor products preserve direct sums and summands.
Thus $(U_j,\Delta_j)$ remains exact after tensoring with every injective $J$.
Note that the contractions need only be $k$-linear.

We first prove that $\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(I,\prod_jB_j)\ne0$ for every $n\ge1$.

$$

 \end{aligned}

 \cdots\xrightarrow{\partial}W\xrightarrow{\partial}W
       \xrightarrow{\overline\partial}P\longrightarrow0.
$$

\label{lem:detecting-dimensions}

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:coordinate}
Let $T$ be an $R^{\mathrm{op}}$-module and $n\ge1$. If $(M_i)_{i\in I}$ is a
family of $R$-modules and
$\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(T,\prod_{i\in I}M_i)\ne0$, then there is
$X\subseteq I$ with $|X|\le\rho$ such that
$\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(T,\prod_{i\in X}M_i)\ne0$.
Consequently, $\mathcal A_n(T)$ is closed under arbitrary direct products
if and only if it is closed under direct products with at most $\rho$
factors.

Let $R$ be a right coherent ring, and let $(G_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family in
$\mathcal{GF}(R)$. Then $\prod_{i\in I}G_i$ is Gorenstein flat if and only
if every direct product over a subset of the index set with at most
$\rho$ factors is Gorenstein flat. In particular, if $R$ is countable, $\mathcal{GF}(R)$ is closed under
countable direct products if and only if it is closed under arbitrary direct products.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Fix a free resolution $F_\bullet$ of $T$ and bases of its terms.
Set $P=\prod_iM_i$. Choose a cycle representing a nonzero class,
and write it as $z=\sum_{j=1}^m e_j\otimes a_j$, where the $e_j$
are distinct basis vectors of $F_n$ and $a_j\in P$.

For each finite subset of the basis of $F_{n+1}$, write the images
of its elements using $e_1,\ldots,e_m$ and the additional basis
vectors which occur. A preimage of $z$ involving only the basis
vectors in this subset exists
exactly when a finite system of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-matrix}
 \varphi(\mathbf a):\quad
 \exists\mathbf y\ (A\mathbf y=\mathbf a\ \text{and}\ B\mathbf y=0)
\end{equation}
has a solution, where $A$ and $B$ are finite matrices over $R$
and $\mathbf a$ is the column vector with entries $a_1,\ldots,a_m$.
The second block imposes zero coefficients on all
additional basis vectors. Every preimage of $z$ in
$F_{n+1}\otimes_RP$ involves only finitely many basis vectors of
$F_{n+1}$, so these systems characterize the boundaries whose
coefficients vanish outside $e_1,\ldots,e_m$.

Only the sizes and entries of $A$ and $B$ occur in
\eqref{eq:boundary-matrix}; the names of additional basis vectors
do not occur. There are at most $\rho$ distinct such systems.
Denote their set by $\Phi$, including the condition
$\mathbf a=0$ corresponding to the empty subset of the basis of $F_{n+1}$.
A fixed finite system is solvable in a direct product
if and only if it is solvable in every coordinate.

Since $z$ is not a boundary, every $\varphi\in\Phi$ fails in $P$.
For each $\varphi$, choose a coordinate $i_\varphi$ where it fails,
and put $X=\{i_\varphi:\varphi\in\Phi\}$.
Then $|X|\le\rho$. Coordinate projection sends $z$ to a cycle
$z_X$ in $F_n\otimes_R\prod_{i\in X}M_i$.
Every system in $\Phi$ still fails at its selected coordinate.
Hence $z_X$ is not a boundary. This proves the assertion, and the
criterion for closure under direct products follows immediately.
For the assertion about Gorenstein flat modules, apply this result
in degree one and use \eqref{eq:detecting-test}, since direct products
over subsets of the index set are direct summands.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:stationary-intersection}
\label{prop:stationary-criterion}
For every $\lambda$, one has
$\bigcap_{\mu\ge\lambda}H_{j_{\mu\lambda}}(E_U)
=H_{j_\lambda}(E_U)=H_{u j_\lambda}(E_U)$.

The following conditions are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The module $L$ is $\mathcal{GF}(R)$-Mittag--Leffler.
\item The module $L$ is $E_U$-stationary.
\item For every $\lambda$, there is $\mu\ge\lambda$ such that
$H_{j_{\mu\lambda}}(E_U)=H_{u j_\lambda}(E_U)$.
\end{enumerate}
Condition~(3) says that every map $L_\mu\to E_U$, restricted
to $L_\lambda$, extends to $F_T$. Here $\mu$ depends on $\lambda$,
but not on the map being extended.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $h:L_\lambda\to E_U$ belong to each image in the intersection.
Introduce variables $x_a$ with values in $E_U$, one for every
$a\in L$. Require $x_{a+b}=x_a+x_b$ for all $a,b\in L$,
$x_{ar}=x_a r$ for all $a\in L$ and $r\in R$, and
$x_a=h(a)$ for all $a\in L_\lambda$. Every finite subsystem involves finitely many
elements of $L$. They lie in some finitely generated
$L_\mu\supseteq L_\lambda$, and an extension of $h$ to $L_\mu$
solves that subsystem. Since $E_U$ is pure-injective, algebraic
compactness supplies a solution of the entire system;
see \cite[Proposition 9.2]{Stenstrom}. It defines an extension $L\to E_U$.
This proves the first equality. Since $E_U\in T^\perp$,
\eqref{eq:detecting-presentation} implies that
$\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}(F_T,E_U)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,E_U)$ is surjective. This proves the second
equality.

By the first assertion, the intersection of
the images is $H_{u j_\lambda}(E_U)$. The images stabilize if and
only if this intersection is attained at some stage. Thus the
conditions~(2) and~(3) are equivalent.

We use tensor--Hom adjunction. For a map $f:M\to N$
of $R^{\mathrm{op}}$-modules and an $R$-module $G$, the image of
$\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}(f,G^+)$ is the annihilator of $\ker(f\otimes_RG)$ under
$\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}(M,G^+)\cong(M\otimes_RG)^+$.
Indeed, a character vanishing on the kernel defines a character
on the image and extends to $N\otimes_RG$, because
$\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ is injective. Characters separate points
of quotient groups, so equality of these annihilators is equivalent
to equality of the kernels.

Assume condition~(1). Apply the criterion
\eqref{eq:relative-ML-stages} to $L$ with $Q=C_U$. The equal kernels give
$H_{j_{\mu\lambda}}(E_U)=H_{j_\lambda}(E_U)$, proving condition~(3).

Conversely, assume condition~(3). The extension property holds
with the same $\mu$ when $E_U$ is replaced by any direct product
of copies of $E_U$, since maps into a direct product are determined
by their coordinate maps. It also holds for every direct summand of such a direct product,
by projecting an extension to the summand. As shown above, every
$G^+$ with $G\in\mathcal{GF}(R)$ is a direct summand of a direct product of
copies of $E_U$. Hence the condition holds for every such $G^+$.
Since $G^+\in T^\perp$, one has
$H_{u j_\lambda}(G^+)=H_{j_\lambda}(G^+)$.
The annihilator observation gives
$\ker(j_\lambda\otimes_RG)=\ker(j_{\mu\lambda}\otimes_RG)$
for every such $G$. Thus \eqref{eq:relative-ML-stages} gives
condition~(1).

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:canonical-bidual}
Let $R$ be a right coherent ring. Then the following conditions are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The class $\mathcal{GF}(R)$ is closed under direct products.
\item The module $C_U^\rho$ is Gorenstein flat.
\item The module $C_U^{++}$ is Gorenstein flat.
\item $\operatorname{Gfd}_RC_U^{++}<\infty$.
\item The module $E_U^{++}$ is Gorenstein injective.
\item $\operatorname{Gid}_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}E_U^{++}<\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
We prove $(4)\Rightarrow(1)$. Choose $d<\infty$ with
$\operatorname{Gfd}_RC_U^{++}\le d$. We first show that every direct product of
Gorenstein flat modules has Gorenstein flat dimension at most $d$.
Otherwise, there is a nonzero
$\operatorname{Tor}_{d+1}^R(T,\prod_{i\in X}G_i)$ with $|X|\le\rho$,
by Lemma~\ref{lem:detecting-dimensions} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:coordinate}.
Choose a free resolution of $T$ and a cycle representing a nonzero
class in its tensor product with $\prod_{i\in X}G_i$.
For each $i\in X$, choose a pure Gorenstein flat submodule
$H_i\subseteq G_i$ of cardinality at most $\rho$ containing the
finitely many coefficients in that coordinate, by
\cite[Lemma 2.25(a)]{GT} and Lemma~\ref{lem:character-Tor}.
Since the terms of the resolution are free, the inclusion
$\prod_{i\in X}H_i\subseteq\prod_{i\in X}G_i$ induces a
monomorphism in each degree. Hence the same expression defines a
cycle after tensoring the resolution with $\prod_{i\in X}H_i$.
It is not a boundary, since its image in the resolution tensored with
$\prod_{i\in X}G_i$ is not a boundary.

Since $|X|\le\rho$, $D=\bigoplus_{i\in X}H_i$ is isomorphic
to a direct summand of $C_U$.
The module $\prod_{i\in X}H_i$ embeds
purely into $D^{++}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:product-into-bidual},
and hence into $C_U^{++}$.
Thus $\operatorname{Gfd}_R(\prod_{i\in X}H_i)\le d$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:detecting-dimensions}, a contradiction.
This proves that finite Gorenstein flat dimension of $C_U^{++}$ bounds the
Gorenstein flat dimensions of all direct products of modules in $\mathcal{GF}(R)$.
These direct products are Gorenstein flat by
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-product-dimensions}.

For $(2)\Rightarrow(1)$, suppose that $C_U^\rho$ is Gorenstein flat.
If $\mathcal{GF}(R)$ were not closed under direct products, the coordinate
projection and the choice of pure submodules used for
$(4)\Rightarrow(1)$, applied in degree one, would give a direct product $\prod_{i\in X}H_i$ with
$|X|\le\rho$ and $\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(T,\prod_{i\in X}H_i)\ne0$.
Each $H_i$ is isomorphic to a direct summand of $C_U$, so
$\prod_{i\in X}H_i$ is isomorphic to a direct summand of $C_U^X$,
and hence of $C_U^\rho$, a contradiction.

Assume condition~(1). Then $C_U^\rho\in\mathcal{GF}(R)$, proving
condition~(2).
Together with the already established closure under pure
submodules and direct limits, closure under direct products makes
$\mathcal{GF}(R)$ definable.
Definable classes are closed under character biduals
(see \cite[Corollary 4.6]{MP}). Hence condition~(3) holds, and it implies condition~(4).
Finally, $(C_U^{++})^+=E_U^{++}$, so
$(3)\Longleftrightarrow(5)$ and $(4)\Longleftrightarrow(6)$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:detecting-dimensions}.

Conditions~(1) and~(2) are equivalent by

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:countable-intersections}
The modules $N_n$ form a decreasing sequence with $N_n\prec F$
for every $n$. Its intersection $N=\bigcap_nN_n$ has infinite Gorenstein
flat dimension. Therefore $(\mathcal{GF}(R),\prec)$ has finite character
and L\"owenheim--Skolem number $\rho=|k|+\aleph_0$, but does not admit intersections.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Since $F/N_n\cong M/M_n\cong B^n$, closure under finite direct products and the
resolving property imply $N_n\prec F$. Moreover,
$N=\ker\pi$. For every $q\geq1$, the exact sequence
$0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow0$
gives $\operatorname{Tor}_q^R(I,N)\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{q+1}^R(I,M)\ne0$.
Thus $\operatorname{Gfd}_RN=\infty$. Finite character and the upper bound $\rho$
for the L\"owenheim--Skolem number follow from
\cite[Theorem 2.11]{ST} and \cite[Theorem 10.28]{GT}, respectively.
For the lower bound, apply the L\"owenheim--Skolem property to
$\{1\}\subseteq R\in\mathcal{GF}(R)$. Every submodule of $R$ containing
$1$ equals $R$, so the L\"owenheim--Skolem number is at least
$|R|=\rho$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:tail-equivalence}
\label{prop:large-cofinality}
The following conditions are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The class $\mathcal{GF}(R)$ is closed under arbitrary direct products.
\item For every limit ordinal $\lambda$, every family
$(G_\beta)_{\beta<\lambda}$ in $\mathcal{GF}(R)$, and every epimorphism
$\pi:F\to P$ from a free module, $L_\alpha\prec F$ for all
$\alpha<\lambda$ implies
$\bigcap_{\alpha<\lambda}L_\alpha\prec F$.
\item The implication in condition~(2) holds whenever
$\lambda$ is an infinite cardinal with $\lambda\leq\rho$.
\end{enumerate}

In the notation above, if $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)>\rho$ and
$L_\alpha\prec F$ for every $\alpha<\lambda$, then
$\bigcap_{\alpha<\lambda}L_\alpha\prec F$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
If $\mathcal{GF}(R)$ is closed under direct products, then $F/\ker\pi\cong P$
is Gorenstein flat. Thus $\ker\pi\prec F$, proving condition~(2).
Condition~(2) implies condition~(3).

For $(3)\Rightarrow(1)$, we adapt the argument in the proof of
$(1)\Rightarrow(2)$ in \cite[Lemma 2.8]{BET}.
Suppose that $\mathcal{GF}(R)$ is not closed under direct products.
There is a least cardinal
$\lambda\leq\rho$ such that a direct product of $\lambda$ Gorenstein flat
modules is not Gorenstein flat, by Theorem~\ref{thm:coordinate}. Since finite direct products are Gorenstein flat, $\lambda$
is infinite. Choose such a direct product and an epimorphism from a free module as above.
Minimality gives $F/L_\alpha\in\mathcal{GF}(R)$ for every $\alpha<\lambda$.
Then $L_\alpha\prec F$ by the resolving property.
Condition~(3) would give $\ker\pi\prec F$, and hence
$P\in\mathcal{GF}(R)$, a contradiction.

For the final assertion, assume that $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)>\rho$
and $L_\alpha\prec F$ for every $\alpha<\lambda$.
Each direct product $\prod_{\beta<\alpha}G_\beta$, with $\alpha<\lambda$,
belongs to $\mathcal{GF}(R)$. Every subset $X\subseteq\lambda$
of cardinality at most $\rho$ is bounded in $\lambda$. Thus
$\prod_{\beta\in X}G_\beta$ is a direct summand of one of these
direct products and is Gorenstein flat.
We have $P\in\mathcal{GF}(R)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:coordinate},
and hence $\ker\pi\prec F$.

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
\label{rmk:cofinality-restriction}
For the ring $R$ in \eqref{eq:main-ring}, condition~(2) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:tail-equivalence} fails at $\lambda=\omega$,
by Corollary~\ref{cor:countable-intersections}.
The implication in condition~(2) holds whenever
$\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)>\rho=|k|+\aleph_0$,
by the final assertion of Proposition~\ref{prop:large-cofinality}. Hence the cofinality restriction
cannot be omitted. If instead every $L_\alpha$ is pure in $F$, then
each direct product $\prod_{\beta<\alpha}G_\beta$, with $\alpha<\lambda$,
is flat and every $G_\beta$ is flat.
Thus $P$ is flat by Chase's theorem (see \cite{Chase}),
and $\ker\pi$ is pure in $F$.
The argument using Chase's theorem concerns the submodules
$L_\alpha=\pi^{-1}(P_\alpha)$ and does not assert closure under arbitrary
intersections of pure submodules.
