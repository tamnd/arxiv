\label{def:weighted_dlra}
The WDLRA of the matrix differential equation \eqref{eq:matODE} is defined as the rank-$r$ matrix
$\mathcal{Z}(t) \in \mathcal{M}_r$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted_dlra_dr}

=

\end{equation}
with initial condition
\(
\mathcal{Z}(0)=\mathcal{Z}_0\in\mathcal{M}_r,

Given the BUG-MLFEM solution $\hat w_h^{\mathfrak{n}}$, with

=
w_L^{\mathfrak{n}}
=
w_S^{\mathfrak{n}}

\eqref{eq:Kvar32}--\eqref{eq:Svar32} admits a unique solution
\begin{equation}\label{wkls}
    (w_K^{\mathfrak{n}+1},\,w_L^{\mathfrak{n}+1},\,w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1})
\in
V_1^{\mathfrak{n}}
\times
V_2^{\mathfrak{n}}
\times
V_0^{\mathfrak{n}+1}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the matrix system in \Cref{alg:KLS-Steps} admits a unique
solution
\begin{equation}
(K^{\mathfrak{n}+1},\,L^{\mathfrak{n}+1},\,S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times r}\times \mathbb{R}^{n\times r}\times \mathbb{R}^{r\times r}.
\end{equation}
(ii) The BUG-MLFEM solution satisfies the mass-lumped

\leq
\|\hat w_h^{\mathfrak{n}}\|_h.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
(i) We present the proof for the $S$-step in detail; the $K$- and $L$-steps follow by the same reasoning. Since \eqref{eq:Svar32} is a finite-dimensional linear system, existence is equivalent to uniqueness. Thus, it suffices to prove uniqueness.

Suppose that $w_{S,1}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}$ and $w_{S,2}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}$ are two solutions of \eqref{eq:Svar32} corresponding to the same data $\hat w_h^\mathfrak{n}$. Define
\(
\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}
:=w_{S,1}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}-w_{S,2}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}
\in V_0^{\mathfrak{n}+1}.
\)
Subtracting the two equations gives
\[
\bigl(D_t\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1},w_0\bigr)_h
+A(\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1},w_0)
=\alpha\bigl(\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1},w_0\bigr)_h,
\quad
\forall\,w_0\in V_0^{\mathfrak{n}+1}.
\]
Since both solutions share the same previous-time data, 
\(
\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}}=0,
\)
and hence
\[
D_t\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}
=\frac{\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}}{\Delta t}.
\]
Taking $w_0=\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}$ yields
\[
\frac{1}{\Delta t}
\|\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_h^2
+\|\nabla\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
=
\alpha\|\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_h^2,
\]
where we have used
\(
A(v,v)=\|\nabla v\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2.
\)
Therefore,
\[
\left(\frac{1}{\Delta t}-\alpha\right)
\|\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_h^2
+\|\nabla\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
=0.
\]
Since $\Delta t>0$ and $\alpha\le0$, we have
\(
\frac{1}{\Delta t}-\alpha>0.
\)
Both terms in the above identity are therefore nonnegative, which implies
\(
\|\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_h=0.
\)
Hence,
\(
\delta w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}=0,
\)
and consequently
\[
w_{S,1}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}=w_{S,2}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}.
\]
Thus, the $S$-step solution is unique.

The existence and uniqueness of the solution
$(K^{\mathfrak{n}+1},L^{\mathfrak{n}+1},S^{\mathfrak{n}+1})$
in \Cref{alg:KLS-Steps} then follow from those of \eqref{wkls} and \Cref{lem:mateqfem}.

(ii) Choose $w_0 = w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}$ in \eqref{eq:Svar32} to obtain
\[
  \bigl(D_t w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}, w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\bigr)_h
  + \|\nabla w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
  = \alpha\|w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_h^2.
\]
Applying the polarization identity
\(
  (a-b,a)
  = \frac{1}{2}
  \left(
    \|a\|^2-\|b\|^2+\|a-b\|^2
  \right)
\)
with $a=w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}$ and $b=w_S^\mathfrak{n}$, and multiplying by $2\Delta t$, gives
\[
  \|w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_h^2
  -\|w_S^\mathfrak{n}\|_h^2
  +\|w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}-w_S^\mathfrak{n}\|_h^2
  +2\Delta t
  \|\nabla w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
  =
  2\Delta t\,\alpha
  \|w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_h^2.
\]
Since $\Delta t>0$ and $\alpha\leq0$, the right-hand side is nonpositive, while the third and fourth terms on the left-hand side are nonnegative. Hence,
\[
  \|w_S^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\|_h^2
  \leq
  \|w_S^\mathfrak{n}\|_h^2.
\]
By \Cref{def:lows}, this is equivalent to \eqref{l2stab}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:psd}
Let $A_x$ and $A_y$ denote the stiffness matrices associated with the
one-dimensional periodic finite element discretizations in the
$x$- and $y$-directions, respectively. Then, the following results hold.
\begin{enumerate}
    \par\hangindent 40\p@[(i)] $A_x$ and $A_y$ are positive semidefinite, namely,
    \[
        A_x\succeq0,
        \qquad
        A_y\succeq0.
    \]
    
    \par\hangindent 40\p@[(ii)]
    Let $\widehat{A}_x$ and $\widehat{A}_y$ denote the
    $(2k+1)$-banded parts of $A_x$ and $A_y$, respectively, obtained by
    retaining only the entries within the $(2k+1)$ banded diagonals.
    Then, $\widehat{A}_x$ and $\widehat{A}_y$ are also positive semidefinite, namely,
    \[
        \widehat{A}_x\succeq0,
        \qquad
        \widehat{A}_y\succeq0.
    \]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
It suffices to prove the assertions for $A_x$ and $\widehat{A}_x$;
the arguments for $A_y$ and $\widehat{A}_y$ are analogous.

{(i)}
Let $v=(v_1,\ldots,v_m)^\top\in\mathbb{R}^m$ be arbitrary, and let
$v_h$ be the corresponding finite element function,
\(
    v_h(x)=\sum_{j=1}^m v_j\phi_j(x),
\)
where $\{\phi_j\}_{j=1}^m$ denotes the finite element basis.
By the definition of the stiffness matrix,
\begin{equation}
    v^\top A_x v
    =
    \sum_{i=1}^{M}
    \int_{I_i^x}
    \left(v_h'(x)\right)^2\,dx
    \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Hence,
\[
    A_x\succeq0.
\]

{(ii)}
For each element $I_i^x$, let
\[
    K^{(i)}
    =
    \left[
        \int_{I_i^x}
        \phi_{(i-1)k+p}'(x)
        \phi_{(i-1)k+q}'(x)\,dx
    \right]_{p,q=1}^{k+1}
    \in\mathbb{R}^{(k+1)\times(k+1)}
\]
denote the corresponding local stiffness matrix. For any
$z=(z_1,\ldots,z_{k+1})^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    z^\top K^{(i)}z
    &=
    \int_{I_i^x}
    \left(
        \sum_{p=1}^{k+1}
        z_p\phi_{(i-1)k+p}'(x)
    \right)^2\,dx
    \ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus,
\[
    K^{(i)}\succeq0,
    \qquad i=1,\ldots,M.
\]

For $i=1,\ldots,M-1$, the standard finite element assembly gives
\[
    R_i^\top K^{(i)}R_i,
\]
where $R_i \in \mathbb{R}^{(k+1)\times m}$ denotes the local-to-global restriction matrix.

For the last element $I_M^x$, the periodic identification of the
endpoints causes the last local degree of freedom to coincide with
the first global degree of freedom. Consequently, the corresponding
entries coupling these two sets of degrees of freedom appear in the
corner blocks of $A_x$ and lie outside the $(2k+1)$ banded diagonals.
Removing these entries amounts to replacing $K^{(M)}$ by
\[
    \widehat{K}^{(M)}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        K^{(M)}_{1:k,1:k} & 0\\
        0 & K^{(M)}_{k+1,k+1}
    \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since $K^{(M)}\succeq0$, its principal submatrix satisfies
\[
    K^{(M)}_{1:k,1:k}\succeq0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
    K^{(M)}_{k+1,k+1}
    =
    \int_{I_M^x}
    \left|\phi_m'(x)\right|^2\,dx
    \ge0.
\]
Therefore,
\[
    \widehat{K}^{(M)}\succeq0.
\]

It follows that the $(2k+1)$-banded part of $A_x$ admits the
decomposition
\[
    \widehat{A}_x
    =
    \sum_{i=1}^{M-1}
    R_i^\top K^{(i)}R_i
    +
    R_M^\top\widehat{K}^{(M)}R_M,
\]
where $R_M$ denotes the local-to-global restriction matrix without
the periodic wrap-around contribution.

Therefore, for any $v\in\mathbb{R}^m$,
\begin{align}
    v^\top\widehat{A}_xv
    &=
    \sum_{i=1}^{M-1}
    (R_iv)^\top K^{(i)}(R_iv)
    +
    (R_Mv)^\top
    \widehat{K}^{(M)}
    (R_Mv)
    \ge0.
\end{align}
Hence,
\[
    \widehat{A}_x\succeq0.
\]
The proof is complete.

(i) Recall that
\[
    A=-\Delta t\,L_x
    =\Delta t\,M_x^{-1}A_x
    \qquad\text{or}\qquad
    A=-\Delta t\,L_y
    =\Delta t\,M_y^{-1}A_y.
\]
Let $\widehat{A}_x$ and $\widehat{A}_y$ be the matrices defined in
\Cref{lem:psd}. Then, for the matrix $\widehat{A}$ defined in
\eqref{eq:A-periodic-structure}, we have
\[
    \widehat{A}
    =\Delta t\,M_x^{-1}\widehat{A}_x
    \qquad\text{or}\qquad
    \widehat{A}
    =\Delta t\,M_y^{-1}\widehat{A}_y.
\]
Since $M_x\succ0$ and $\widehat{A}_x\succeq0$ by
\Cref{lem:psd},
\[
    M_x^{-1}\widehat{A}_x
    \sim
    M_x^{-1/2}\widehat{A}_xM_x^{-1/2}
    \succeq0.
\]
Hence, all eigenvalues of $\widehat{A}$ are real and nonnegative.
The same conclusion follows when
\[
    \widehat{A}
    =\Delta t\,M_y^{-1}\widehat{A}_y.
\]

Moreover, since $\alpha\leq0$ and $A_y\succeq0$ by
\Cref{lem:psd}, we have
\[
    B
    =(1-\Delta t\,\alpha)I_r
      +\Delta t\,(V^\mathfrak{n})^\top A_yV^\mathfrak{n}
    \succ0.
\]
Therefore, all eigenvalues $\lambda_j$ of $B$ satisfy
\[
    \lambda_j>0,
    \qquad j=1,\ldots,r.
\]

By \eqref{eq:shifted-periodic-decomp}, each $\widehat{A}_j$ is a
positive shift of $\widehat{A}$, namely,
\[
    \widehat{A}_j=\widehat{A}+\lambda_j I.
\]
Thus, if $\mu$ is any eigenvalue of $\widehat{A}$, then
$\mu\geq0$, and the corresponding eigenvalue of $\widehat{A}_j$ is
\[
    \mu+\lambda_j>0.
\]
Hence, $\widehat{A}_j$ is nonsingular for every $j=1,\ldots,r$.

Finally, by \eqref{eq:shifted-periodic-decomp},
\[
    \widehat{A}_j+\widehat{U}\widehat{V}^{\top}
    =A+\lambda_j I.
\]
Since $M_x\succ0$ and $A_x\succeq0$ by \Cref{lem:psd},
\[
    M_x^{-1}A_x
    \sim
    M_x^{-1/2}A_xM_x^{-1/2}
    \succeq0.
\]
Therefore, all eigenvalues of $A$ are real and nonnegative. The same
conclusion follows from
\[
    A=\Delta t\,M_y^{-1}A_y
\]
using $M_y\succ0$ and $A_y\succeq0$. Consequently, all eigenvalues of
\[
    A+\lambda_j I
    =\widehat{A}_j+\widehat{U}\widehat{V}^{\top}
\]
are strictly positive, since $\lambda_j>0$. Hence,
$\widehat{A}_j+\widehat{U}\widehat{V}^{\top}$ is nonsingular for every
$j=1,\ldots,r$.

(ii)
By (i), both $\widehat{A}_j$ and
$\widehat{A}_j+\widehat{U}\widehat{V}^{\top}$ are nonsingular. Since
$\widehat{A}_j$ is nonsingular,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\det\left(
    \widehat{A}_j+\widehat{U}\widehat{V}^{\top}
\right)
&=
            
                   
            
                                
                                       
             
           
\det(\widehat{A}_j)
\det\left(
    I_m+\widehat{A}_j^{-1}
    \widehat{U}\widehat{V}^{\top}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
By Sylvester's determinant theorem,
\[
\det(I_m+XY)=\det(I_2+YX),
\]
with
\[
X=\widehat{A}_j^{-1}\widehat{U}
\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times2},
\qquad
Y=\widehat{V}^{\top}
\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times m}.
\]
Hence,
\[
\det\left(
    I_m+\widehat{A}_j^{-1}
    \widehat{U}\widehat{V}^{\top}
\right)
=
\det\left(
    I_2+\widehat{V}^{\top}
    \widehat{A}_j^{-1}\widehat{U}
\right).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\det\left(
    \widehat{A}_j+\widehat{U}\widehat{V}^{\top}
\right)
=
\det(\widehat{A}_j)
\det\left(
    I_2+\widehat{V}^{\top}
    \widehat{A}_j^{-1}\widehat{U}
\right).
\]
By (i), the determinants of
$\widehat{A}_j$ and
$\widehat{A}_j+\widehat{U}\widehat{V}^{\top}$ are both nonzero.
Therefore,
\[
    \det\left(
        I_2+\widehat{V}^{\top}
        \widehat{A}_j^{-1}\widehat{U}
    \right)\neq0.
\]
Thus, the conclusion (ii) holds.

For matrix $\widehat{A}_j+\widehat{U}\widehat{V}^{\top}$ in \eqref{eq:shifted-periodic-decomp},  it holds the Sherman--Morrison--Woodbury \cite{woodbury1950inverting} identity,

        \widehat{A}_j+\widehat{U}\widehat{V}^{\top}

    \label{eq:woodbury-identity}
\end{equation}

\label{ex:augbug}

Following \cite{ceruti2024robust}, we assess the accuracy, computational
efficiency, and rank adaptivity of the augmented BUG integrator
\Cref{alg:AugBUG-Steps} for a stiff, forced heat equation on the two-dimensional
torus $\Omega=[-\pi,\pi)^2$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stiff_heat}
  \partial_t u(x,y,t)
  = \frac{1}{2}\left(
      \partial_{xx}u(x,y,t)+\partial_{yy}u(x,y,t)
    \right)+g(x,y),
\end{equation}
subject to periodic boundary conditions in both spatial directions. The
time-independent forcing term and the initial condition are, respectively, given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stiff_forcing}
  g(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{11}10^{-(j-1)}
  \exp\!\bigl(-j(x^2+y^2)\bigr)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stiff_ic}
  u(x,y,0)=\sin(x)\sin(y).
\end{equation}

We first examine spatial convergence. We fix the time step at
$\Delta t=10^{-5}$ and set the initial rank to $r^0=10$. For polynomial
degrees $k=1,2,3$, we refine the spatial mesh and measure the $L^2(\Omega)$
error at $T=1$. Since the exact solution is unavailable, we
use the FR-MLFEM solution on a fine mesh with $N=160$ as the reference
solution. The results are presented in \Cref{fig:stiff_heat_space}.
Both FR-MLFEM and augmented BUG-MLFEM (ABUG-MLFEM) exhibit the optimal spatial convergence
rate $k+1$. Moreover, their error curves are nearly indistinguishable,
suggesting that the low-rank approximation
introduces no discernible error beyond that associated with spatial
discretization.

We then investigate temporal convergence. We fix $N=80$, $k=2$, and
$r^0=10$, and successively halve the time step from
$\Delta t=10^{-2}$ to $\Delta t=6.25\times10^{-4}$. The $L^2(\Omega)$
error is evaluated at $T=1$. As shown in the left panel of
\Cref{fig:stiff_heat_time_rank}, both FR-MLFEM and ABUG-MLFEM
exhibit first-order convergence in time. Their errors are nearly identical
for all tested time steps, indicating that the augmented BUG integrator
preserves the temporal accuracy of the underlying time discretization in
these experiments.

Finally, we examine the rank evolution of ABUG-MLFEM for
polynomial degrees $k=1,2,3$ up to $T=2.5$, using $\Delta t=10^{-2}$
and truncation tolerance $\vartheta=10^{-12}$. The rank histories are shown
in the right panel of \Cref{fig:stiff_heat_time_rank}. Each term in
\eqref{eq:stiff_forcing} is separable, since
\(
  \exp\!\bigl(-j(x^2+y^2)\bigr)
  =\exp(-jx^2)\exp(-jy^2).
\)
Consequently, the forcing term has separation rank at most $11$.
The geometrically decreasing coefficients $10^{-(j-1)}$ produce modes
of progressively smaller amplitude. As these modes contribute to the
evolving solution, the adaptive integrator increases the rank in discrete
steps, from $8$ to $9$ and then to $10$. The rank subsequently remains
at $10$ over the simulated interval, with the remaining modes not retained
under the prescribed truncation tolerance. The rank histories are similar
for all three polynomial degrees, although the times at which the rank
increases differ: the $\mathbb{P}^2$ computation reaches rank $10$ at
approximately $t=0.2$, whereas the $\mathbb{P}^1$ and $\mathbb{P}^3$
computations reach this rank at approximately $t=0.6$. This behavior
suggests that the adaptive rank is primarily determined by the effective
low-rank structure of the evolving solution, while the spatial
discretization can affect the timing of rank enrichment.
