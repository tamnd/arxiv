<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{introthm:nonunique-Z-embeddings}
The two canonical unital embeddings of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$ into the universal unital free product \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra $\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}\ast_{\mathbb C}\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$ are not approximately unitarily equivalent.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{introthm:asymptotic-uniqueness}
Let $(A,\tau)$ be a tracial, selfless \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra, that is, a simple, unital, monotracial, non-elementary \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra with strict comparison by its trace.
Then any two unital embeddings $\varphi,\psi\colon\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}\to A$ are strongly
asymptotically unitarily equivalent, that is, there exists a
norm-continuous path $(u_t)_{t\geq0}\subseteq\mathcal U(A)$,
such that  $u_0=1_A$, and
\[
    \lim_{t\to\infty}
    \|u_t\varphi(z)u_t^*-\psi(z)\|=0
    \qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal Z.
\]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-probability space $(A,\rho)$ consists of  a unital \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra $A$ and a GNS-faithful state $\rho$ on $A$, i.e., the GNS-representation of $\rho$ is faithful.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- notation-1: body_md -->
The following \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-probability spaces will often appear in the article, and hence we fix their notation, and use it without further mention,
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
        \item $(C(\mathbb T),\tau_{\ensuremath{\mathrm{Leb}}})$ is the \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-probability space generated by one Haar unitary, i.e., $\tau_{\ensuremath{\mathrm{Leb}}}$ is the trace induced by integration against the Lebesgue measure;
        \item $(\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}},\tau_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}})$ is the Jiang--Su algebra, which was introduced in \cite{JS99}, equipped with its trace, whose uniqueness is proved in \cite[Theorem~2.9]{JS99};
        \item $(\ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}_r(G),\tau_G)$ is the reduced \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra of $G$, for a discrete group $G$, and is equipped with the vector state of $\delta_{1_G}\in\ell^2(G)$.
    \end{itemize}
<!-- end notation-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Let $(A_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family of \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebras, and let
$\omega$ be an ultrafilter on $I$. Consider the \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra
\[
    \prod_{i\in I}A_i
    :=
    \big\{
        (a_i)_{i\in I}
        \,\mid \,
        a_i\in A_i,\ 
        \sup_{i\in I}\|a_i\|<\infty
    \big\},
\]
with coordinatewise operations and the supremum norm.
The {(norm) ultraproduct} of $(A_i)_{i\in I}$ with
respect to $\omega$ is the quotient \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra 
\[
    \prod_{\omega}A_i
    :=
    \frac{\prod_{i\in I}A_i}{\{
        (a_i)_{i\in I}
        \,\mid \,
        \lim_{i\to\omega}\|a_i\|=0
    \}}.
\]
We denote the class of a bounded family $(a_i)_{i\in I}$
by $[(a_i)_i]_\omega$. Its norm satisfies
\[
    \bigl\|[(a_i)_i]_\omega\bigr\|
    =
    \lim_{i\to\omega}\|a_i\|.
\]
If each $A_i$ is unital, then $\prod_\omega A_i$ is
unital, with unit $[(1_{A_i})_i]_\omega$.

When $A_i=A$ for every $i\in I$, we write $A_\omega$
and call it the {(norm) ultrapower} of $A$.
We identify $A$ with its diagonal copy in $A_\omega$
via the diagonal embedding
\[
    A\longrightarrow A_\omega,
    \qquad
    a\longmapsto[(a)_{i\in I}]_\omega.
\]
    If $\rho$ is a state on $A$, we denote by $\rho_\omega$ the state on $A_\omega$ given by
    \[
    \rho_{\omega}([(a_i)_i]_\omega)=\lim_{i\to\omega}\rho(a_i).
    \]
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
A \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-probability space $(A,\rho)$ is \emph{selfless} if
$A\not\cong\mathbb C$ and there exist a \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-probability space $(C,\kappa)$ with $C\not\cong\mathbb C$, an ultrafilter $\omega$, and an embedding
\[
    \sigma\colon (A,\rho)\ast(C,\kappa)
    \longrightarrow (A_\omega,\rho_\omega)
\]
whose restriction to the first factor is the diagonal embedding.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Let $A$ be a \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra, and let $\ensuremath{\mathcal{K}}$ denote the compact operators on a separable, infinite-dimensional Hilbert space.
For $a,b\in(A\otimes\mathcal K)_+$,
write $a\precsim b$ if there exists a sequence $(x_n)_n\subset A\otimes\mathcal K$
such that $x_n^*bx_n\to a$ in norm, and write $a\sim b$ if both
$a\precsim b$ and $b\precsim a$ occur at the same time. The \emph{Cuntz semigroup} of $A$ is defined as
\[
    \ensuremath{\mathrm{Cu}}(A):=(A\otimes\mathcal K)_+/{\sim}.
\]
ordered by $[a]\leq[b]$ whenever $a\precsim b$, with addition
$[a]+[b]=[a\oplus b]$. A $\ast$-homomorphism $\varphi\colon A\to B$
induces the map
$\ensuremath{\mathrm{Cu}}(\varphi)([a])=[(\varphi\otimes\ensuremath{\mathrm{id}}_{\mathcal K})(a)]$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
Let $A$ be a \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra with stable rank one. Then, $\ensuremath{\mathrm{Cu}}(A)$ has cancellation of projections, i.e., for any $[a],[b],[p]\in\ensuremath{\mathrm{Cu}}(A)$, where $p$ is a projection, one has that $[a]+[p] \leq [b]+[p]$ implies $[a]\leq[b]$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:strict-comparison}
A \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra $A$ has {strict comparison of
positive elements} if, whenever
$a,b\in(A\otimes\mathcal K)_+$ are such that $a$ is contained in the ideal generated by $b$ in $A\otimes\ensuremath{\mathcal{K}}$ and that $d_\tau(a)<d_\tau(b)$ for every $\tau\in\mathrm{QT}_c(A)$
with $d_\tau(b)=1$, we have $a\precsim b$.

By \cite[Propositions~4.2 and~6.2]{ERS11}, this is
equivalent to almost unperforation of $\mathrm{Cu}(A)$,
that is,
\[
    (n+1)x\leq ny
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    x\leq y
\]
for all $x,y\in\mathrm{Cu}(A)$ and $n\geq1$.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:strict-comparison-full-elements}
Let $A$ be a unital \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra with strict comparison,
and let $a,b\in A_+$, with $b$ full.
If $d_\tau(a)<d_\tau(b)$ for every $\tau\in\mathrm{QT}_1(A)$, then $a\precsim b$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $b$ is full, $a$ belongs to the ideal generated
by $b$, and there exists $m\geq1$ such that $[1_A]\leq m[b]$.
Fix $\rho\in\mathrm{QT}_c(A)$ with $d_\rho(b)=1$.
Since $b\precsim 1_A$, we have
\[
    1=d_\rho(b)\leq\rho(1_A)
    =d_\rho(1_A)\leq m\,d_\rho(b)=m.
\]
Thus
\[
    \tau=\frac{\rho}{\rho(1_A)}
    \in\mathrm{QT}_1(A).
\]
By assumption,
\[
    d_\rho(a)
    =
    \rho(1_A)d_\tau(a)
    <
    \rho(1_A)d_\tau(b)
    =
    d_\rho(b).
\]
Strict comparison now gives $a\precsim b$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:equivariant-zero}
Let $N$ be a power of a prime and let $1\leq r<N$.
Every continuous map
$f\colon\mathcal U(N)\to\mathbb C^r$ satisfying
\[
    f(\lambda v)=\lambda f(v)
    \quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\mathbb T,\
    v\in\mathcal U(N)
\]
vanishes at some point.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For $1\leq k\leq n$, let $V_k(\mathbb C^n)$ denote the
space of ordered orthonormal $k$-tuples in $\mathbb C^n$,
equipped with the subspace topology and the circle action
\[
    \lambda(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_k)
       =(\lambda\xi_1,\ldots,\lambda\xi_k).
\]
Identifying a unitary matrix with its ordered columns gives
a $\mathbb T$-equivariant homeomorphism
$\mathcal U(M_N)\cong V_N(\mathbb C^N)$.
Likewise, $V_1(\mathbb C^r)=S^{2r-1}$, where
$S^{2r-1}$ is the unit sphere of $\mathbb C^r$.
Suppose that $f$ never vanishes.
Then
\[
  V_N(\mathbb C^N)\longrightarrow V_1(\mathbb C^r),\quad  v\longmapsto\frac{f(v)}{\|f(v)\|}
\]
defines a continuous $\mathbb T$-equivariant map.
Since $N$ is a prime power and $N>r$, this contradicts
\cite[Theorem~3.10(i)]{PP13}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- notation-2: body_md -->
\label{not:two-embeddings}
    For each $N>0$, define
unital embeddings by
\[
  \sigma_N\colon M_N\to C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N),M_N),\quad   \sigma_N(a)([v])=a,  
\]
and 
\[
\lambda_N\colon M_N\to C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N),M_N),\quad \lambda_N(a)([v])=v^*av.
\]
Note that the second map does not depend on the representative of
$[v]$.
<!-- end notation-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:obstruction-lemma}
Let $N$ be a power of a prime.
For any nonzero elements $a,b\in (M_N)_+$, with $b$ non-invertible, and $x\in  C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N),M_N)$, one has
\[
    \|\sigma_N(a)-x\lambda_N(b)x^*\| \
       \geq\|a\|.                                                    
\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $a,b$ and $x$ be as in the statement.
Choose a unit vector $\xi\in\mathbb C^N$ with
$a\xi=\|a\|\xi$, which exists by the spectral theorem.
Let $r<N$ be the rank of $b$, and identify $\mathrm{ran}(b)$ with $\mathbb C^r$.  
Define the map
\[
   f\colon \ensuremath{\mathcal{U}}(N) \to \mathbb C^r,\quad  f(v)=b^{1/2}v\,x([v])^*\xi
\]
and note that it is continuous, and satisfies $f(\lambda v)=\lambda f(v)$ for all $\lambda\in\mathbb T$ and $v\in \ensuremath{\mathcal{U}}(N)$. Hence, by Lemma~\ref{lem:equivariant-zero}, there exists $v_0\in\mathrm \ensuremath{\mathcal{U}}(N)$ with $f(v_0)=0$.
Note that this implies the following identity,
\[
\langle x([v_0])v_0^*bv_0x([v_0])^*\xi,\xi \rangle = \|f(v_0)\|^2 = 0.
\] 
Now, we use this identity to deduce the claimed norm estimate, 
\begin{align*}
   \|\sigma_N(a)-x\lambda_N(b)x^*\|
&=\sup_{[v]\in \ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N)}
  \|a-x([v])v^*bvx([v])^*\|\\
&\geq  \|a-x([v_0])v_0^*bv_0x([v_0])^*\|\\
&\geq \|(a-x([v_0])v_0^*bv_0x([v_0])^*)\xi\|\\
&= \|a\|.
\end{align*}
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:matrix-orbit-gap}
Let $N$ be a power of a prime.
For every nonzero, self-adjoint element $a\in M_N$ and $w\in\mathcal U(C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N),M_N))$, we have
\[
    \|w\sigma_N(a)w^*-\lambda_N(a)\|
       =\max\operatorname{sp}(a)-\min\operatorname{sp}(a).
\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The assertion is immediate when $a$ is a multiple of the identity matrix.
Since both embeddings are unital, subtracting
$\min\operatorname{sp}(a)1_N$ reduces us to the case where
$a$ is positive, nonzero, and non-invertible. By Lemma \ref{lem:obstruction-lemma}, 
\[
\|w\sigma_N(a)w^*-\lambda_N(a)\|\geq\|a\|.
\]
The reverse inequality follows because both positive
elements lie between $0$ and $\|a\|1$. This proves the statement.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:non-unique-embeddings}
    Let $A\not\cong\mathbb C$ be a unital \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra that embeds unitally into $\prod_{\omega}M_{N_n}$, where $({N_n})_n$ is a sequence of powers of prime numbers.
    Let $\iota_0,\iota_1$ denote the canonical unital embeddings into the universal unital free product $A\ast_{\mathbb C} A$.
    Then,
    \[
    \|w\iota_0(h)w^*-\iota_1(h)\|=1
    \]
    for all   $w\in\ensuremath{\mathcal{U}}(A\ast_{\mathbb C}A)$ and all positive, non-invertible, norm one elements $h\in A$.

    In particular, $\iota_0,\iota_1$ are not approximately unitarily equivalent.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We identify $A$ with its image in $\prod_{\omega}M_{N_n}$.
    Let $\sigma,\lambda\colon A \to \prod_{\omega}C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N_n),M_{N_n})$ be the unital $\ast$-homomorphisms induced by the sequences of maps $(\sigma_{N_n})_n$ and $(\lambda_{N_n})_n$ defined in Notation \ref{not:two-embeddings}.
    Note that, for any positive, non-invertible, norm one $h\in A$,
\[
    \max\operatorname{sp}(h)-\min\operatorname{sp}(h)
       =\|h\|+\|1-h\|-1=1.
\]
Consequently, lifting $h$ to a sequence of positive, norm one elements $(h_n)_n$ in $\prod_{n=1}^\infty M_{N_n}$, we have that 
\[
    \lim_{n\to\omega}
    \left(
       \max\operatorname{sp}(h_n)
       -\min\operatorname{sp}(h_n)
    \right)=1.
\]
Consider now any unitary $u\in \prod_{\omega}C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N_n),M_{N_n})$, and choose a representative sequence
$(u_n)_n$ with $u_n\in\mathcal U(C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N_n),M_{N_n}))$.
It follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:matrix-orbit-gap} that 
\begin{align*}
    \|u\sigma(h)u^*-\lambda(h)\|
      &=
      \lim_{n\to\omega}
      \|u_n\sigma_{N_n}(h_n)u_n^*
                 -\lambda_{N_n}(h_n)\| \\
    &=\lim_{n\to\omega}\left(
       \max\operatorname{sp}(h_n)
       -\min\operatorname{sp}(h_n)
    \right)\\
    &=1.
\end{align*}
Use the universal property of $A\ast_{\mathbb C}A$ to obtain a unital $\ast$-homomorphism
\[
q\colon A\ast_{\mathbb C}A\to \prod_{\omega}C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N_n),M_{N_n})
\]
with $q\circ \iota_0=\sigma$ and
$q\circ \iota_1=\lambda$.
For every $w\in\mathcal U(A\ast_{\mathbb C}A)$,
\[
    \|w\iota_0(h)w^*-\iota_1(h)\|
       \geq
    \|q(w)\varphi(h)q(w)^*-\psi(h)\|=1.
\]
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:obstruction-Cuntz}
Let $(N_n)_n$ be a sequence of powers of primes such that $\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$ embeds unitally into $\prod_{\omega}M_{N_n}$ for some free ultrafilter $\omega$. 
Let $\sigma,\lambda\colon \ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}} \to \prod_{\omega}C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N_n),M_{N_n})$ be the unital $\ast$-homomorphisms induced by the sequences of maps $(\sigma_{N_n})_n$ and $(\lambda_{N_n})_n$ defined in Notation \ref{not:two-embeddings}.
Then every \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-subalgebra $B$ in the inclusion
\[
    C^*(\sigma(\mathcal Z),\lambda(\mathcal Z))
    \subseteq B\subseteq \prod_{\omega}C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N_n),M_{N_n})
\]
fails strict comparison of positive elements.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Choose a positive contraction $h\in \ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$ with
$\operatorname{sp}(h)=[0,1]$, and pairwise orthogonal positive contractions
$f_1,f_2,f_3\in C([0,1])$ of norm one.
Then, use continuous functional calculus to define 
\[
    a_j=f_j(h)\in \ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}},
    \qquad j=1,2,3.
\]
These are pairwise orthogonal positive contractions
of norm one.
Now, since $\tau_{\mathcal Z}$ is faithful,
\[
    d_{\tau_{\mathcal Z}}(a_j)>0
    \qquad\text{for }j=1,2,3.
\]
After relabelling, we may assume that
\[
    d_{\tau_{\mathcal Z}}(a_1)
    \leq d_{\tau_{\mathcal Z}}(a_j)
    \qquad\text{for }j=2,3.
\]
Set $ a=a_1$, and $ b=a_2+a_3$.
Then $a,b\in \ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$ are positive contractions of norm one,
$ab=0$, and orthogonality gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
    d_{\tau_{\mathcal Z}}(a)
    <
    d_{\tau_{\mathcal Z}}(a_2)
    +d_{\tau_{\mathcal Z}}(a_3)
    =d_{\tau_{\mathcal Z}}(b).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

Let $\tau$ be any normalized $2$-quasitrace on $B$.
Since $\mathcal Z$ is exact,
\cite[Theorem~5.11]{Haa14} implies that
$\tau\circ\sigma$ and $\tau\circ\lambda$ are tracial
states on $\mathcal Z$.
By uniqueness of its trace,
\[
    \tau\circ\sigma
    =\tau_{\mathcal Z}
    =\tau\circ\lambda.
\]
It follows that 
that
\begin{equation*}
    d_\tau(\sigma(a))
    =
    d_{\tau_{\mathcal Z}}(a)
    <
    d_{\tau_{\mathcal Z}}(b)
    =
    d_\tau(\lambda(b)).
\end{equation*}

We show that nevertheless
$\sigma(a)$ is not Cuntz subequivalent to $\lambda(b)$, even inside the larger \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra $\prod_{\omega}C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N_n),M_{N_n})$.
Lift $a$ and $b$ to sequences of positive contractions $(c_n)_n$ and $(d_n)_n$, respectively, which we may choose so that they satisfy $c_nd_n=0$. The last condition is justified by taking a lift $(h_n)_n$ of $h$, and $c_n=f_1(h_n)$, $d_n=(f_2+f_3)(h_n)$.
Since $\sigma$ is isometric,
\[
    \lim_{n\to\omega}\|c_n\|=\lim_{n\to\omega}\|\sigma_{N_n}(c_n)\|=\|\sigma(a)\|=1.
\]
Note that, whenever $c_n\neq0$, the relation $c_nd_n=0$ implies
that $d_n$ is a non-invertible matrix of size $N_n$. Moreover, if either $c_n=0$ or $d_n=0$, the estimate we want to show below is immediate. 

Fix $x=[(x_n)_n]_\omega\in \prod_{\omega}C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N_n),M_{N_n})$.
Applying Lemma \ref{lem:obstruction-lemma}, we obtain that
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \|\sigma(a)-x\lambda(b)x^*\|
    &=
    \lim_{n\to\omega}
    \|\sigma_{N_n}(c_n)
        -x_n\lambda_{N_n}(d_n)x_n^*\|\\
    &\geq
    \lim_{n\to\omega}\|c_n\|\\
    &=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence, $\sigma(a)$ is not Cuntz subequivalent to $\lambda(b)$ in $\prod_{\omega}C(\ensuremath{\mathcal{PU}}(N_n),M_{N_n})$, and therefore also inside $B$.
Since \(b\neq0\), simplicity of \(\mathcal Z\) and unitality of \(\lambda\) imply that \(\lambda(b)\) is full in \(B\). If \(B\) had strict comparison, Lemma~\ref{lem:strict-comparison-full-elements} would give \(\sigma(a)\precsim\lambda(b)\), contradicting the preceding conclusion.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:Cu-asymptotic-uniqueness}
Let $C$ be a separable, unital \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra of stable rank one,
and suppose that $K_1(J)=0$ for every closed two-sided ideal
$J\subseteq C$.
Then any two unital
$\ast$-homomorphisms $\varphi,\psi\colon\mathcal Z\to C$ that satisfy $\ensuremath{\mathrm{Cu}}(\varphi)=\ensuremath{\mathrm{Cu}}(\psi)$ are
strongly asymptotically unitarily equivalent.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Put $j:=\mathrm{Cu}(\varphi)=\mathrm{Cu}(\psi)$ and define
\[
    E:=\{f\in C([0,1],C):
    f(0)\in\varphi(\mathcal Z),\ 
    f(1)\in\psi(\mathcal Z)\}.
\]
Observe that $E$ is isomorphic to the pullback \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra $C([0,1],C) \oplus_{C\oplus C}(\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}\oplus\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}})$ along the maps $\ensuremath{\mathrm{ev}}_0\oplus\ensuremath{\mathrm{ev}}_1$ and $\varphi\oplus\psi$.
Each fibre has stable rank one and vanishing $K_1$ for every
closed two-sided ideal. Note also that $E$ is a continuous field over $[0,1]$ with fibres isomorphic to $C$ for $0<t<1$, and to $\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$ at the endpoints. Hence, both $E$ and $C([0,1],C)$ have
stable rank one by \cite[Theorems 1.9 and 1.11]{ABPP14}.

By \cite[Corollaries 2.7 and 3.5]{APS11}, there is a natural identification
\[
    \mathrm{Cu}(E)\cong
    \left\{
    (x_0,x_1,f)\in
    \mathrm{Cu}(\mathcal Z)^2
    \times\operatorname{Lsc}([0,1],\mathrm{Cu}(C)):
    f(0)=j(x_0),\ f(1)=j(x_1)
    \right\}.
\]
Under this identification, define
\[
    \Lambda\colon\mathrm{Cu}(\mathcal Z)\to\mathrm{Cu}(E),
    \qquad
    \Lambda(x)=(x,x,[s\mapsto j(x)]).
\]
This is a morphism in the category of abstract Cuntz semigroups. Indeed, zero, addition,
order, and increasing suprema are computed coordinatewise.
The constant-function map
$\mathrm{Cu}(C)\to\operatorname{Lsc}([0,1],\mathrm{Cu}(C))$
is induced by the inclusion $C\to C([0,1],C)$, and therefore
preserves compact containment. Thus, if $x'\ll x$, each
coordinate of $\Lambda(x')$ is compactly contained in the
corresponding coordinate of $\Lambda(x)$. Given an increasing
sequence of triples whose supremum dominates $\Lambda(x)$,
one may choose a common index at which all three coordinates
dominate those of $\Lambda(x')$. This proves that
$\Lambda(x')\ll\Lambda(x)$.
Moreover, $\Lambda([1_{\mathcal Z}])=[1_E]$.

By \cite[Theorems 1.0.1 and 3.2.2(i)]{Rob12}, there exists a
$\ast$-homomorphism $\Theta\colon\mathcal Z\to E$ inducing
$\Lambda$. Put $p:=\Theta(1_{\mathcal Z})$. Since
$[p]=[1_E]$ and
\[
    [p]+[1_E-p]=[1_E]=[p],
\]
cancellation of projections gives $[1_E-p]=0$, and hence
$p=1_E$. Thus $\Theta$ is unital.
Set $\theta_s:=\operatorname{ev}_s\circ\Theta$, and observe that
\[
    \mathrm{Cu}(\theta_s)=j,\quad
    \theta_0=\varphi\circ\alpha_0,\quad
    \theta_1=\psi\circ\alpha_1,
\]
for all $s\in[0,1]$, where $\alpha_0,\alpha_1\colon\mathcal Z\to\mathcal Z$ are some unital endomorphisms.

If we view $\Theta$ as a homomorphism into $C([0,1],C)$, and using \cite[Corollary 2.7]{APS11}, we see that its Cuntz-semigroup map agrees
with that of the constant homomorphism
$z\mapsto[s\mapsto\theta_0(z)]$.
Applying \cite[Theorems 1.0.1 and 3.2.2(i)]{Rob12} in
$C([0,1],C)$, we obtain norm-continuous unitary paths
$(u_{n,s})_{s\in[0,1]}\subseteq\mathcal U(C)$, for $n\geq0$, such that
\[
    \sup_{s\in[0,1]}
    \|u_{n,s}\theta_0(z)u_{n,s}^*-\theta_s(z)\|
    \xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
\]
for all $z\in\mathcal Z$.
Put $v_{n,s}:=u_{n,s}u_{n,0}^*$. Then $v_{n,0}=1_C$ and
\begin{align*}
    e_n(z)&:=\sup_{s\in[0,1]}
    \|v_{n,s}\theta_0(z)v_{n,s}^*-\theta_s(z)\| \\
    &\leq \sup_{s\in[0,1]} (
    \|u_{n,s}(u_{n,0}^*\theta_0(z)u_{n,0}-\theta_0(z))u_{n,s}^*\| + \|u_{n,s}\theta_0(z)u_{n,s}^*-\theta_s(z)\|)\\
    &= 
    \|u_{n,0}^*\theta_0(z)u_{n,0}-\theta_0(z)\| + \sup_{s\in[0,1]}\|u_{n,s}\theta_0(z)u_{n,s}^*-\theta_s(z)\|
\end{align*}
and thus $e_n(z) \to0$ as $n\to\infty$ for all $z\in\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$.
Define $c_{n,s}:=v_{n,s}^*v_{n+1,s}$, and note that  
\begin{align*}
    \|[c_{n,s},\theta_0(z)]\|
    &=\|v_{n,s}
        \bigl(c_{n,s}\theta_0(z)-\theta_0(z)c_{n,s}\bigr)
        v_{n+1,s}^*\|\\
    &=\bigl\|
        v_{n,s}v_{n,s}^*v_{n+1,s}\theta_0(z)v_{n+1,s}^*
        -v_{n,s}\theta_0(z)v_{n,s}^*
        v_{n+1,s}v_{n+1,s}^*
      \bigr\|\\
    &=\|v_{n+1,s}\theta_0(z)v_{n+1,s}^*
        -v_{n,s}\theta_0(z)v_{n,s}^*\|\\
    &=\bigl\|
        \bigl(v_{n+1,s}\theta_0(z)v_{n+1,s}^*-\theta_s(z)\bigr)
        +\bigl(\theta_s(z)-v_{n,s}\theta_0(z)v_{n,s}^*\bigr)
      \bigr\|\\
    &\leq
        \|v_{n+1,s}\theta_0(z)v_{n+1,s}^*-\theta_s(z)\|
        +\|v_{n,s}\theta_0(z)v_{n,s}^*-\theta_s(z)\|\\
    &\leq e_{n+1}(z)+e_n(z)
\end{align*}
for all $s\in[0,1]$, $n\geq0$, and $z\in\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$.
Since $v_{n,0}=1_C$ for every $n$, we have that
\[
    c_{n,0}=1_C,\qquad
    c_{n,1}=v_{n,1}^*v_{n+1,1}.
\]
Thus, setting, for each $n\geq1$, $s\in[0,1]$,
\[
    r_{n+s}:=v_{n,1}c_{n,s}
\]
we have defined a continuous unitary path on $[n,n+1]$ with endpoints
\begin{align*}
    r_n
    &=v_{n,1}c_{n,0}=v_{n,1},\\
    r_{n+1}
    &=v_{n,1}c_{n,1}
      =v_{n,1}v_{n,1}^*v_{n+1,1}
      =v_{n+1,1}.
\end{align*}
The consecutive paths therefore agree at their common endpoints.
Together with $r_t:=v_{1,t}$ for $t\in[0,1]$, they define a
norm-continuous unitary path $(r_t)_{t\geq0}$ with $r_0=1_C$.
Now, for every $n\geq1$, $s\in[0,1]$, and $z\in\mathcal Z$,
\begin{align*}
    \|r_{n+s}\theta_0(z)r_{n+s}^*-\theta_1(z)\| &=\|v_{n,1}c_{n,s}\theta_0(z)c_{n,s}^*v_{n,1}^*
        -\theta_1(z)\|\\
    &\leq
    \|v_{n,1}
        \bigl(c_{n,s}\theta_0(z)c_{n,s}^*-\theta_0(z)\bigr)
        v_{n,1}^*\|\\
    & \quad +
    \|v_{n,1}\theta_0(z)v_{n,1}^*-\theta_1(z)\|\\
    &=
    \|[c_{n,s},\theta_0(z)]\|
    +\|v_{n,1}\theta_0(z)v_{n,1}^*-\theta_1(z)\|\\
    &\leq
    \bigl(e_n(z)+e_{n+1}(z)\bigr)+e_n(z)\\
    &=2e_n(z)+e_{n+1}(z).
\end{align*}
Since the bound is independent of $s$, we obtain
\[
    \sup_{s\in[0,1]}
    \|r_{n+s}\theta_0(z)r_{n+s}^*-\theta_1(z)\|
    \leq2e_n(z)+e_{n+1}(z)
    \xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.
\]
Thus $\theta_0$ and $\theta_1$ are strongly asymptotically
unitarily equivalent.

Finally, since $\mathcal Z$ is strongly self-absorbing and
$K_1$-injective (in fact, all strongly self-absorbing \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebras have this property by \cite{Win11}), \cite[Theorem~2.2]{DW09} gives norm-continuous
paths $(w_{i,t})_{t\geq0}\subseteq\mathcal U(\mathcal Z)$,
for $i=0,1$, such that $w_{i,0}=1_{\mathcal Z}$ and
\[
    \lim_{t\to\infty}
    \|w_{i,t}\alpha_i(z)w_{i,t}^*-z\|=0
\]
for all $z\in\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$.
Define, for $t\ge0$,
\[
    a_t:=\varphi(w_{0,t}^*),
    \qquad
    b_t:=\psi(w_{1,t}).
\]
These are norm-continuous unitary paths in $C$ with
$a_0=b_0=1_C$. Using
$\theta_0=\varphi\circ\alpha_0$ and
$\theta_1=\psi\circ\alpha_1$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
    \|a_t\varphi(z)a_t^*-\theta_0(z)\|
    &=\|\varphi(w_{0,t}^*zw_{0,t}-\alpha_0(z))\|\\
    &=\|w_{0,t}^*zw_{0,t}-\alpha_0(z)\|\\
    &=\|z-w_{0,t}\alpha_0(z)w_{0,t}^*\|
    \xrightarrow{t\to\infty}0,
\end{align*}
and
\begin{align*}
    \|b_t\theta_1(z)b_t^*-\psi(z)\|
    &=\|\psi(w_{1,t}\alpha_1(z)w_{1,t}^*-z)\|\\
    &=\|w_{1,t}\alpha_1(z)w_{1,t}^*-z\|
    \xrightarrow{t\to\infty}0
\end{align*}
for every $z\in\mathcal Z$.
The path $(b_tr_ta_t)_{t\geq0}$ implements the required
strong asymptotic unitary equivalence between $\varphi$ and
$\psi$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:free-product-asymptotic-uniqueness}
Let $A$ be a unital \ensuremath{\mathrm{C}^\ast}-algebra with a faithful tracial
state $\tau$. Suppose that, for some ultrafilter $\omega$
on a set $I$, there exists a unital embedding
\[
    \sigma\colon (A,\tau)*(\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}},\tau_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}})
    \longrightarrow A_\omega
\]
whose restriction to the first free factor is the diagonal
embedding of $A$.
Then any two unital embeddings $\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}\to A$ are strongly
asymptotically unitarily equivalent.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $\varphi,\psi\colon\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}\to A$ be unital embeddings, and put
\[
    D:=(\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}},\tau_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}})*(\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}},\tau_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}}),
    \qquad
    E:=(A,\tau)*(\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}},\tau_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}}).
\]
Write $\iota_1,\iota_2\colon\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}\to D$ for the canonical
embeddings and $\kappa\colon\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}\to E$ for the second
free factor embedding.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:free-Z-path}, there exists a norm-continuous path
$(q_t)_{t\geq0}\subseteq\mathcal U(D)$ such that $q_0=1_D$ and
\[
    \lim_{t\to\infty}
    \|q_t\iota_1(z)q_t^*-\iota_2(z)\|=0
    \qquad (z\in\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}).
\]

Let $\alpha\colon\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}\to A$ be a unital embedding.
Since $\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$ has a unique tracial state,
$\tau\circ\alpha=\tau_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}}$.
By \cite[Theorem~1.3]{BD01}, we have a unital embedding
$j_\alpha\colon D\to E$ satisfying
\[
    j_\alpha\circ\iota_1=\alpha,
    \qquad
    j_\alpha\circ\iota_2=\kappa.
\]
For $t\geq0$, define
\[
    p_t^\alpha:=j_\psi(q_t)^*j_\alpha(q_t).
\]
This is a norm-continuous unitary path with $p_0^\alpha=1_E$.
For every $z\in\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$, we have
\begin{align*}
    &\|p_t^\alpha\alpha(z)(p_t^\alpha)^*-\psi(z)\|\\
    &\quad=
    \|j_\alpha(q_t)\alpha(z)j_\alpha(q_t)^*
      -j_\psi(q_t)\psi(z)j_\psi(q_t)^*\|\\
    &\quad\leq
    \|j_\alpha(q_t)\alpha(z)j_\alpha(q_t)^*-\kappa(z)\|
    +\|\kappa(z)-j_\psi(q_t)\psi(z)j_\psi(q_t)^*\|\\
    &\quad=
    \|j_\alpha(q_t\iota_1(z)q_t^*-\iota_2(z))\|
    +\|j_\psi(\iota_2(z)-q_t\iota_1(z)q_t^*)\|\\
    &\quad=
    2\|q_t\iota_1(z)q_t^*-\iota_2(z)\|.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:free-product-path-convergence}
    \|p_t^\alpha\alpha(z)(p_t^\alpha)^*-\psi(z)\|
    \leq 2\|q_t\iota_1(z)q_t^*-\iota_2(z)\|
    \xrightarrow{t\to\infty}0.
\end{equation}
The bound is independent of $\alpha$.

Choose increasing nonempty finite subsets $F_n$ of the
unit ball of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$ whose union is dense,
and put $\varepsilon_n:=2^{-n}$.
For each $n\geq1$, choose $T_n>0$ such that
\[
    \sup_{t\geq T_n}\max_{z\in F_n}
    \|q_t\iota_1(z)q_t^*-\iota_2(z)\|
    <\frac{\varepsilon_n}{8}.
\]

We next find a finite subset $G_n\supseteq F_n$ of the unit
ball of $\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}$ and $0<\delta_n<\varepsilon_n/4$ such that
every unital embedding $\alpha\colon\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}\to A$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:free-product-initial-control}
    \max_{z\in G_n}\|\alpha(z)-\psi(z)\|<\delta_n
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \sup_{0\leq t\leq T_n}
    \|j_\alpha(q_t)-j_\psi(q_t)\|
    <\frac{\varepsilon_n}{8}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, compactness of $\{q_t:0\leq t\leq T_n\}$ allows
us to choose finitely many elements $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ in
the unital $\ast$-algebra generated by the two free factors
such that
\[
    \min_{1\leq k\leq m}\|q_t-b_k\|
    <\frac{\varepsilon_n}{32}
    \qquad (0\leq t\leq T_n).
\]
Each $b_k$ is a $\ast$-polynomial in finitely many elements
from those factors.
The maps $j_\alpha$ and $j_\psi$ agree on the second factor.
Continuity of these finitely many polynomials therefore
gives $G_n$ and $\delta_n$ such that
\[
    \max_{z\in G_n}\|\alpha(z)-\psi(z)\|<\delta_n
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \max_{1\leq k\leq m}
    \|j_\alpha(b_k)-j_\psi(b_k)\|
    <\frac{\varepsilon_n}{16}.
\]
Here we include in $G_n$ the first-factor coefficients
appearing in the polynomials, rescaled into the unit ball,
and decrease $\delta_n$ as necessary.
By contractivity,
\begin{align*}
    \sup_{0\leq t\leq T_n}
    \|j_\alpha(q_t)-j_\psi(q_t)\|
    &\leq
    \frac{\varepsilon_n}{16}
    +\max_{1\leq k\leq m}
    \|j_\alpha(b_k)-j_\psi(b_k)\|\\
    &<\frac{\varepsilon_n}{8},
\end{align*}
proving \eqref{eq:free-product-initial-control}.
Suppose now that
\[
    \max_{z\in G_n}\|\alpha(z)-\psi(z)\|<\delta_n.
\]
Since
\[
    \|p_t^\alpha-1_E\|
    =
    \|j_\psi(q_t)^*
      (j_\alpha(q_t)-j_\psi(q_t))\|
    =
    \|j_\alpha(q_t)-j_\psi(q_t)\|,
\]
we obtain
\begin{align*}
    &\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_n}\max_{z\in F_n}
    \|p_t^\alpha\alpha(z)(p_t^\alpha)^*-\psi(z)\|\\
    &\quad\leq
    \sup_{0\leq t\leq T_n}\max_{z\in F_n}
    \|p_t^\alpha\alpha(z)(p_t^\alpha)^*-\alpha(z)\|
    +\max_{z\in F_n}\|\alpha(z)-\psi(z)\|\\
    &\quad\leq
    2\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_n}\|p_t^\alpha-1_E\|
    +\max_{z\in F_n}\|\alpha(z)-\psi(z)\|\\
    &\quad<
    \frac{\varepsilon_n}{4}+\delta_n
    <\frac{\varepsilon_n}{2}.
\end{align*}
For $t\geq T_n$, \eqref{eq:free-product-path-convergence}
and the choice of $T_n$ give
\[
    \sup_{t\geq T_n}\max_{z\in F_n}
    \|p_t^\alpha\alpha(z)(p_t^\alpha)^*-\psi(z)\|
    <\frac{\varepsilon_n}{4}.
\]
Combining these estimates, we have proved that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:free-product-path-control}
    \sup_{t\geq0}\max_{z\in F_n}
    \|p_t^\alpha\alpha(z)(p_t^\alpha)^*-\psi(z)\|
    <\frac{\varepsilon_n}{2}
\end{equation}
whenever
$\max_{z\in G_n}\|\alpha(z)-\psi(z)\|<\delta_n$.

We now construct the implementing path in $A$.
By \eqref{eq:free-product-path-convergence}, choose $S_0>0$
such that
\[
    \max_{z\in G_1}
    \|p_{S_0}^{\varphi}\varphi(z)
       (p_{S_0}^{\varphi})^*-\psi(z)\|<\delta_1.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer-unitary-path} to
$(p_{S_0s}^{\varphi})_{s\in[0,1]}$, with
$F=\{1_{\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}}\}$, $H=G_1$, $\varepsilon=1$, and
$\eta=\delta_1$.
We obtain a norm-continuous path
$(u_t)_{t\in[0,1]}\subseteq\mathcal U(A)$ such that $u_0=1_A$
and
\[
    \max_{z\in G_1}\|u_1\varphi(z)u_1^*-\psi(z)\|<\delta_1.
\]

Suppose inductively that the path has been defined on
$[0,n]$ and satisfies
\[
    \max_{z\in G_n}\|u_n\varphi(z)u_n^*-\psi(z)\|<\delta_n.
\]
Put $\alpha_n:=\operatorname{Ad}(u_n)\circ\varphi$.
By \eqref{eq:free-product-path-convergence}, choose
$S_n\geq T_n$ large enough that
\[
    \max_{z\in G_{n+1}}
    \|p_{S_n}^{\alpha_n}\alpha_n(z)
       (p_{S_n}^{\alpha_n})^*-\psi(z)\|<\delta_{n+1}.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:free-product-path-control}, apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer-unitary-path} to
$(p_{S_ns}^{\alpha_n})_{s\in[0,1]}$, with the embeddings
$\alpha_n,\psi$, the sets $F_n,G_{n+1}$, and the tolerances
$\varepsilon_n,\delta_{n+1}$.
We obtain a norm-continuous path
$(w_{n,s})_{s\in[0,1]}\subseteq\mathcal U(A)$ with
$w_{n,0}=1_A$,
\[
    \sup_{s\in[0,1]}\max_{z\in F_n}
    \|w_{n,s}\alpha_n(z)w_{n,s}^*-\psi(z)\|
    <\varepsilon_n,
\]
and
\[
    \max_{z\in G_{n+1}}
    \|w_{n,1}\alpha_n(z)w_{n,1}^*-\psi(z)\|
    <\delta_{n+1}.
\]
Extend the path by setting
\[
    u_{n+s}:=w_{n,s}u_n
    \qquad (0\leq s\leq1).
\]
Since $w_{n,0}=1_A$, this extension agrees with the
previous path at $n$.
Moreover,
\[
    u_{n+s}\varphi(z)u_{n+s}^*
    =
    w_{n,s}u_n\varphi(z)u_n^*w_{n,s}^*
    =
    w_{n,s}\alpha_n(z)w_{n,s}^*.
\]
Thus
\[
    \sup_{t\in[n,n+1]}\max_{z\in F_n}
    \|u_t\varphi(z)u_t^*-\psi(z)\|<\varepsilon_n,
\]
and the endpoint satisfies
\[
    \max_{z\in G_{n+1}}
    \|u_{n+1}\varphi(z)u_{n+1}^*-\psi(z)\|<\delta_{n+1},
\]
which is the next induction hypothesis.

The resulting path $(u_t)_{t\geq0}$ is norm continuous
and starts at $1_A$.
Since the sets $F_n$ are increasing with dense union in
the unit ball and $\varepsilon_n\to0$, contractivity gives
\[
    \lim_{t\to\infty}\|u_t\varphi(z)u_t^*-\psi(z)\|=0
    \qquad (z\in\ensuremath{\mathcal{Z}}).
\]
This proves the proposition.
