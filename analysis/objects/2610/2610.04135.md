<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:fundamental_lower_bound} Fix $s>0$, $\delta\in(0,1/2)$, and $u\in[\delta,1-\delta]$. Suppose that $L,p_{\max}>1$. Then, for all sufficiently large $n$, the minimax rate is lower bounded as
\[
    \mathcal R_{n,t}
    \gtrsim
    \begin{cases}
        t^{-s},
        & t\leq\log n, \\[0.5em]
        \Big[t\left\{
            1+\log\left(\frac{n}{t^{s+1/2}}\right)
        \right\}\Big]^{-s/2},
        & \log n<t<n^{\frac{2}{2s+1}}, \\[0.5em]
        n^{-\frac{s}{2s+1}},
        & t\geq n^{\frac{2}{2s+1}}.
    \end{cases}
\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Existing minimax theory for binomial mixtures has primarily focused on estimation of the mixing distribution under Wasserstein-$1$ loss and weak structural assumptions \citep{tian2017learning,vinayak2019maximum}. This is a distinct problem from the one studied here; our target is the pointwise density value $p(u)$, our parameter space imposes $s$-H\"older smoothness, and our loss is the pointwise absolute error. Accordingly, we emphasize that their results should not be viewed as special cases of ours, nor vice versa.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Nonetheless, it is worth comparing our intermediate $t$ rate with $(t\log n)^{-s/2}$, which closely resembles the minimax rate that \cite{vinayak2019maximum} obtain in their intermediate regime, $(t\log n)^{-1/2}$, but with smoothness incorporated. Note that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lnt}
\frac{(t\ell_{n,t})^{-s/2}}{(t\log n)^{-s/2}}
=
\left(\frac{\log n}{\ell_{n,t}}\right)^{s/2}, \qquad \ell_{n,t} \equiv 1+\log\!\left(\frac{n}{t^{s+1/2}}\right).
\end{equation} 
In our intermediate regime, we have $1<\ell_{n,t}\leq 1+\log n$. Hence, if $\ell_{n,t}\gtrsim\log n$, then $\ell_{n,t}\asymp\log n$.                                                            
In this case, therefore, the minimax rate is $(t\log n)^{-s/2}$ up to constants.                                                                                                           

If instead $\ell_{n,t}=o(\log n)$, the minimax rate is slower than $(t\log n)^{-s/2}$ by the diverging factor $(\log n/\ell_{n,t})^{s/2}$. In particular, when $t\asymp n^{2/(2s+1)}$ with $t<n^{2/(2s+1)}$, we have $\ell_{n,t}\asymp1$, and hence
\[
    (t\ell_{n,t})^{-s/2}
    \asymp t^{-s/2}
    \asymp n^{-s/(2s+1)}.
\]
Thus the intermediate rate is already of the same order as the
large $t$ rate in this constant-factor neighborhood of the
transition, whereas $(t\log n)^{-s/2}$ is too small by a factor
of order $(\log n)^{s/2}$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We refer the readers to \cite{Derriennic1981}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The proof proceeds in two parts, first some preliminary calculations and then bounding the actual quantity of interest.

\paragraph{Preliminary calculations.} Write $2u-1 = \cos\theta$. Since $u\in[\delta,1-\delta]$, we have $\theta\in[\theta_\delta,\pi-\theta_\delta]$ for some $\theta_\delta>0$ depending only on $\delta$. As $k \to \infty$, Theorem 8.21.8 of \cite{szego75} yields
\[
    P_k(\cos\theta) = \left(\frac{2}{\pi k\sin\theta}\right)^{1/2} \cos\left(\left(k+\frac12\right)\theta-\frac{\pi}{4}\right) + O_\delta(k^{-3/2}),
\]
uniformly for $\theta\in[\theta_\delta,\pi-\theta_\delta]$. As a result, for the shifted Legendre polynomial $\phi_k$, we have
\[
    \phi_k(u) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi\sin\theta}}
    \cos\left(\left(k+\frac12\right)\theta-\frac{\pi}{4}\right) + O_\delta(k^{-1}) \equiv \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi\sin\theta}}\cos\left(a_k\right) + O_\delta(k^{-1}),
\]
uniformly for $\theta\in[\theta_\delta,\pi-\theta_\delta]$ as $k\to\infty$. 
If we define $R_k(\theta)$ to be the exact difference
\[
    R_k(\theta) \equiv \phi_k(u) - \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi\sin\theta}}\cos(a_k),
\]
then the asymptotic expansion implies that there exist $k_\delta\geq1$ and $A_\delta<\infty$ such that
\[
    \sup_{\theta\in[\theta_\delta,\pi-\theta_\delta]}
    |R_k(\theta)|
    \leq \frac{A_\delta}{k}, \qquad k\geq k_\delta.
\]
For each of the finitely many integers $0\leq k<k_\delta$, the function $R_k$ is continuous on the compact interval $[\theta_\delta,\pi-\theta_\delta]$. Therefore,
\[
    C_{\delta,0} \equiv \max\left\{2A_\delta,\,\max_{0\leq k<k_\delta}(k+1)\sup_{\theta\in[\theta_\delta,\pi-\theta_\delta]}|R_k(\theta)|\right\}
    <\infty.
\]
Since $k^{-1}\leq2(k+1)^{-1}$ for $k\geq1$, it follows for every $k\in\mathbb N_0$ that
\[
    \phi_k(u) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi\sin\theta}}\cos(a_k)
    +R_k(\theta), \qquad |R_k(\theta)|
    \leq \frac{C_{\delta,0}}{k+1},
\]
uniformly over $\theta \in [\theta_\delta,\pi-\theta_\delta]$. 
                       
    
                            
                                              
                
    
Consequently, for every $a\in\mathbb N_0$ and $w\geq1$,
\[
    \sum_{k\inJ(a,w)}R_k(\theta)^2
    \leq C_\delta\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)^{-2}
    \leq C_\delta.
\]

Next, fix $a\in\mathbb N_0$ and $w\geq1$. By the identity $2\cos^2(a_k)=1+\cos(2a_k)$, we have
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{k\inJ(a,w)}\cos^2(a_k)
    &=
    \frac{w}{2}
    +
    \frac12
    \sum_{k=a+1}^{a+w}\cos(2a_k)\\
    &=
    \frac{w}{2}
    +
    \frac12
    \sum_{k=a+1}^{a+w}
    \cos\left(
        2k\theta+\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}
    \right)\\
    &=
    \frac{w}{2}
    +
    \frac12\operatorname{Re}
    \left\{
        e^{i(\theta-\pi/2)}
        \sum_{k=a+1}^{a+w}e^{2ik\theta}
    \right\},
\end{align*}
where
\begin{align*}
    \left|
        \sum_{k=a+1}^{a+w}e^{2ik\theta}
    \right|
    =
    \left|
        e^{2i(a+1)\theta}
        \sum_{j=0}^{w-1}e^{2ij\theta}
    \right| =
    \left|
        e^{2i(a+1)\theta}
        \frac{1-e^{2iw\theta}}{1-e^{2i\theta}}
    \right| \leq
    \frac{2}{|1-e^{2i\theta}|}
    =
    \frac{1}{|\sin\theta|}.
\end{align*}
It follows that
\[
    \left|
        \sum_{k\inJ(a,w)}
        \cos^2(a_k)
        -
        \frac{w}{2}
    \right|
    \leq
    \frac{1}{2|\sin\theta|}.
\]

\paragraph{Bounding the quantity of interest.} We now bound the quantity of interest
$\sum_{k\inJ(a,w)}\phi_k(u)^2$. For the upper bound,
$(x+y)^2\leq2x^2+2y^2$ yields
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{k\inJ(a,w)}\phi_k(u)^2
    &\leq
    \frac{8}{\pi\sin\theta}
    \sum_{k\inJ(a,w)}\cos^2(a_k)
    +
    2\sum_{k\inJ(a,w)}R_k(\theta)^2\\
    &\leq
    \frac{8}{\pi\sin\theta}
    \left(
        \frac{w}{2}
        +
        \frac{1}{2\sin\theta}
    \right)
    +
    C_\delta\\
    &=
    \frac{4w}{\pi\sin\theta}
    +
    \frac{4}{\pi\sin^2\theta}
    +
    C_\delta \leq C_\delta w,
\end{align*}
where the last inequality follows from
$\sin\theta\geq2\sqrt{\delta(1-\delta)}$ and $w\geq1$.

For the lower bound, applying $(x+y)^2\geq x^2/2-y^2$ gives
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{k\inJ(a,w)}\phi_k(u)^2
    &\geq
    \frac{2}{\pi\sin\theta}
    \sum_{k\inJ(a,w)}\cos^2(a_k)
    -
    \sum_{k\inJ(a,w)}R_k(\theta)^2\\
    &\geq
    \frac{2}{\pi\sin\theta}
    \left(
        \frac{w}{2}
        -
        \frac{1}{2\sin\theta}
    \right)
    -
    C_\delta\\
    &=
    \frac{w}{\pi\sin\theta}
    -
    \frac{1}{\pi\sin^2\theta}
    -
    C_\delta\\
    &\geq
    \frac{w}{\pi}
    -
    C_\delta,
\end{align*}
where the last inequality follows from $\sin\theta\leq1$ for the first term and from
$\sin\theta\geq2\sqrt{\delta(1-\delta)}$ to absorb
$1/(\pi\sin^2\theta)$ into $C_\delta$.

If we choose
$
    W_0(\delta)
    \geq
    \max\left\{
        1,
        \left\lceil2\pi C_\delta\right\rceil
    \right\},
$
then, for every $w\geq W_0(\delta)$,
\[
    \frac{w}{\pi}-C_\delta
    \geq
    \frac{w}{2\pi}.
\]
Combining this with the upper bound gives
\[

The result is trivial when $\ell=0$, so suppose that $1\le \ell\le m$. Note that
    \[
        \log\frac{\lambda_{t,m}^{-1}}{\lambda_{t,m-\ell}^{-1}} = \sum_{j=m-\ell}^{m-1}\log\left(\frac{t+j+2}{t-j}\right) 
        = \sum_{j=m-\ell}^{m-1}\log\left(1+\frac{2j+2}{t-j}\right).
    \]
    Since $(2j+2)/(t-j) \geq 0$ for every $j \in \{m-\ell,\ldots,m-1\}$, by 
    \[
        \frac{x}{1+x}\leq \log(1+x) \leq x,
        \qquad x \geq 0,
    \]
    with $x = (2j+2)/(t-j)$, we have 
    \[
        \frac{2j+2}{t+j+2}
        \leq \log\left(1+\frac{2j+2}{t-j}\right)
        \leq \frac{2j+2}{t-j}.
    \]
    We further claim that
    \[
        \frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}\frac{2j+2}{t}
        \leq \log\left(1+\frac{2j+2}{t-j}\right)
        \leq \frac{1}{1-\rho}\frac{2j+2}{t}.
    \]

    \[
        \frac{2j+2}{t-j} \leq \frac{2j+2}{(1-\rho)t}.
    \]

    since $j+1\le m\le\rho t$,
    \[
        x = \frac{2(j+1)}{t-j}
        \leq \frac{2\rho t}{(1-\rho)t}
        = \frac{2\rho}{1-\rho}.
    \]
    Consequently,
    \[
        \frac{x}{1+x}
        \geq \frac{x}{1+2\rho/(1-\rho)}
        = \frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}x 
        \geq \frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}\frac{2j+2}{t},
    \]

    \[
    \sum_{j=m-\ell}^{m-1}(j+1) = \frac{\ell(2m-\ell+1)}{2},

    \quad q\in[0,1],
\]
for $c>0$ sufficiently small.

Let $q_{i,m,\pm}$ denote the probability mass functions of $X_i$
under these alternatives. Then
\[
    \sum_{i=1}^n\chi^2(q_{i,m,+},q_{i,m,-})
    \lesssim
    (\Delta_m^{\mathrm{het}})^2
    \left\langle
        g_{J_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{het}}},
        \mathtt{BD}_{\mathbf t}g_{J_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{het}}}
    \right\rangle_{L_2}
    \lesssim
    (\Delta_m^{\mathrm{het}})^2\frac{\mathcal I_m}{m}
    \lesssim c^2,
\]
where the first inequality follows by applying the $\chi^2$
calculation in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:stat_lower_bound} to each observation,
the second from Lemma~\ref{lem:heterogeneous_operator_bound},
and the last from the definition of $\Delta_m^{\mathrm{het}}$.
By tensorization,
\[
    \chi^2\left(
        \bigotimes_{i=1}^n q_{i,m,+},
        \bigotimes_{i=1}^n q_{i,m,-}
    \right)
    \leq
    \exp\left\{
        \sum_{i=1}^n\chi^2(q_{i,m,+},q_{i,m,-})
    \right\}-1
    \lesssim c^2,
\]
for $c>0$ sufficiently small.                                                                                                                       

Since $g_{J_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{het}}}(u)=1$, the alternatives
differ at $u$ by $2\Delta_m^{\mathrm{het}}$. Fixing $c>0$
sufficiently small and applying Le Cam's two-point lemma
therefore gives
\[
    \mathcal R_{n,\mathbf t}
    \gtrsim
    \Delta_m^{\mathrm{het}}
    \asymp
    \min\left\{
        m^{-s},
        \sqrt{\frac{m}{\mathcal I_m}}
    \right\}.
\]
Maximizing over $m\in\mathcal M_{\mathbf t}$ gives the desired bound.

Suppose, for notational simplicity, that the trial parameters are sorted in decreasing order. 

\paragraph{Lower bound.} By the eigenvalue
formula and $\log(1+x)\geq x/(1+x)$ for $x\geq0$,
\[
    -\log\lambda_{t,m}
    \geq
    \sum_{k=1}^m\frac{2k}{t+k+1}
    \geq \frac{m^2}{3t},
    \qquad 1\leq m\leq t.
\]
Since $\lambda_{t,m}=0$ for $m>t$, the subsequent upper bound $\lambda_{t,m} \leq \exp\{-m^2/(3t)\}$ holds for every integer $m \geq 1$. Hence, for some fixed $c>0$, we have
\[
    \mathcal I_m
    \leq
    \sum_{i=1}^n\exp\{-cm^2(i/n)^{1/\beta}\}
    \leq
    n\int_0^\infty\exp(-cm^2x^{1/\beta})\,d x
    \lesssim nm^{-2\beta},
\]
where the second inequality follows because the integrand is nonincreasing, and the last by substituting $x=m^{-2\beta}y$. Thus, we have for every $m\in\mathcal M_{\mathbf t}$ that
\[
    m^{-s}+\sqrt{\frac{m}{\mathcal I_m}}
    \gtrsim m^{-s}+n^{-1/2}m^{\beta+1/2}
    \geq n^{-s/(2s+2\beta+1)}.
\]
The last inequality follows from the first term when $m\leq n^{1/(2s+2\beta+1)}$ and from the second otherwise.

\paragraph{Upper bound.} Choose $m\asymp n^{1/(2s+2\beta+1)}$. Since $T\asymp n^{1/\beta}$, this degree belongs to
$\mathcal M_{\mathbf t}$ for sufficiently large $n$. 
The condition $t_i\gtrsim(n/i)^{1/\beta}$ therefore implies that at least a constant multiple of $nm^{-2\beta}$ observations satisfy $t_i\geq m^2/\rho$ for sufficiently large $n$. For these observations, $m\leq m^2\leq\rho t_i$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse_eig_ratio} gives
\[
    \lambda_{t_i,m}
    \geq
    \exp\left(
        -\frac{2}{1-\rho}\frac{m^2}{t_i}
    \right)
    \geq
    \exp\left(-\frac{2\rho}{1-\rho}\right)
    \gtrsim1.
\]
It follows that 
\[
    m^{-s}+\sqrt{\frac{m}{\mathcal I_m}} \lesssim m^{-s}+n^{-1/2}m^{\beta+1/2}\asymp n^{-s/(2s+2\beta+1)}, 
\]
since $\mathcal I_m\gtrsim nm^{-2\beta}$ at this degree.
