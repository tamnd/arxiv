<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:frac:anchor}
    A job arriving to an empty system becomes the \emph{anchor}.  The anchor
    keeps its role until it completes.  When the anchor completes, the job
    with the largest \emph{original} size among those present, if any,
    becomes the new anchor; ties are broken by arrival order.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:frac:policy}
Let the anchor be as in Definition~\ref{def:frac:anchor}.
If at most \(n\) jobs are present, every job is served at rate one.  If more
than \(n\) jobs are present and the anchor has original size \(z\), then: 
\begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\roman{enumi}}{\roman{enumi}}\renewcommand{\textup{(\roman{enumi})}}{\textup{(\roman{enumi})}}
    \item the anchor is served at rate \(\gamma(z)\);
    \item the \(n-1\) non-anchor jobs with the smallest remaining sizes are
          each served at rate one;
    \item the non-anchor job with the nth smallest remaining size is served
          at rate \(1-\gamma(z)\).
\end{enumerate}
All other jobs receive rate zero.  Ties in the non-anchor SRPT order are
broken by arrival order.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:frac:optimality}
Suppose \(n\ge2\), \((n-1)/n\le\rho<1\), and the job-size distribution has a
regularly varying tail with tail index \(\alpha>1\).  For the capacity
margin constructed in Section~\ref{sec:frac:combining},
\[
    \lim_{t\to\infty}
    \frac{\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[T^{\text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace}>t\right]}{\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[S>\delta t\right]}
    =1,
    \qquad\text{where}\quad \delta:=n(1-\rho).
\]
Consequently, \text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace is strongly tail optimal.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:frac:anchor-occupancy}
For every \(a>0\),
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[Z(0)>a\right]
    \le \frac{4r^{>a}}{\delta}.
    \label{eq:frac:anchor-occupancy}
\end{equation}
where \(r^{>a}\) is the part of the resource requirement contributed by
jobs larger than \(a\), defined in~\eqref{eq:r_x}.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let \(W_a(s)\) be the total remaining work in jobs with original
size exceeding \(a\) at time \(s\); this work enters the system at mean
rate \(r^{>a}\), and the events \(\{W_a(0)>0\}\) and \(\{Z(0)>a\}\) are
identical.

Consider a period during which \(W_a>0\).  It starts when a
job larger than \(a\) arrives while no such job is present, and it ends
when \(W_a\) returns to zero; Figure~\ref{fig:frac:large-work} illustrates
one such period.
The job that starts the period joins the non-anchor
side, and the current anchor, if any, has original size at most \(a\).
The current anchor is served at rate one when at most \(n\) jobs are
present, and at rate \(\gamma\ge\delta/2\) otherwise,
by~\eqref{eq:frac:condition-margin-shape}, where \(\delta\le1\), so the
current anchor completes within \(2a/\delta\) time units.  From
then on, whenever the anchor completes while \(W_a>0\), the largest job
present is larger than \(a\) and becomes the new anchor.  Hence, after an
initial delay of at most \(2a/\delta\), the anchor is a job larger than
\(a\) for the rest of the period, and \(W_a\) decreases at rate at least
\(\delta/2\).  Other jobs larger than \(a\) may also be served on the
non-anchor side, which only makes \(W_a\) decrease faster.  Let \(B\) be
the length of the period where \(W_a>0\), and let \(W\) be the
total work of jobs larger than \(a\) that arrive during this period, including the
job that starts it.  Then \(W>a\), and
\[
    B\le\frac{2a}{\delta}+\frac{2W}{\delta}
    <\frac{4W}{\delta}.
\]
Summing over the successive $W_a>0$ periods in \([0,T]\) and letting
\(T\to\infty\), the long-run fraction of time with \(W_a>0\) is at most
\(4/\delta\) times the long-run rate at which work of jobs larger than
\(a\) arrives, which is \(r^{>a}\).  Since we consider the system in steady
state, this long-run fraction of time equals
\(\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[W_a(0)>0\right]\), which proves \eqref{eq:frac:anchor-occupancy}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:frac:recovery}
Under condition~\eqref{eq:frac:condition-cap}, for all sufficiently large
\(t\), the stationary recovery debt is finite and satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[D(0)>0\right]
    \le \frac{8r^{>\hat{A}_t}}{\delta}.
    \label{eq:frac:recovery-probability}
\end{equation}
Moreover, if \(D(s)=0\), then the capacity bound \(\mu_{\mathrm{na}}\)
over every backward interval ending at \(s\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \int_u^s\mu_{\mathrm{na}}(v)\,dv
    \ge c_t(s-u)
    \qquad\text{for every \(u\le s\)}.
    \label{eq:frac:backward-capacity}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Lemma~\ref{lem:frac:anchor-occupancy} at \(a=\hat{A}_t\), together with
\(\hat{A}_t\to\infty\) and \(r^{>a}\to0\) as \(a\to\infty\), gives
\(\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[B(0)=1\right]<1/2\) for all sufficiently large \(t\).  This is what makes the debt finite and \(D(s)<\infty\) almost surely.  This is the Loynes
construction~\citep{loynes1962stability}, and \(D\) is a stationary
functional of the stationary sample path.

We now compare the time the debt spends rising with the time it spends
falling.  Over any interval \([0,T]\), the debt rises at rate one during
bad time and falls at rate one during good time while positive, so
\[
    D(T)-D(0)
    =\int_0^T B(v)\,dv
     -\int_0^T\mathbf 1\{B(v)=0,\ D(v)>0\}\,dv.
\]
Since \(D(T)\ge0\), the time spent falling is at most the time spent
rising plus \(D(0)\).  Now divide by \(T\) and let \(T\to\infty\).  The two
integrands are bounded stationary processes, so the ergodic theorem gives
their time averages, and \(D(0)/T\to0\) because \(D(0)<\infty\).  Hence we
have that 
\[
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[B(0)=0,\ D(0)>0\right]
    \le\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[B(0)=1\right].
\]
Adding \(\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[B(0)=1,\ D(0)>0\right]\le\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[B(0)=1\right]\) to both sides and applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:frac:anchor-occupancy} gives
\[
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[D(0)>0\right]
        \le 2\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[B(0)=1\right]
    \le \frac{8r^{>\hat{A}_t}}{\delta}.
\]

It remains to connect recovery debt with capacity.  By the definition of
the benchmark rate and the monotonicity of \(\varepsilon\),
\[
    c_t-\mu_{\mathrm{na}}(v)
    =m_t-\varepsilon(Z(v))
    \le
    \begin{cases}
       -m_t, & Z(v)\le \hat{A}_t,\\
        m_t, & Z(v)>\hat{A}_t.
    \end{cases}
\]
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
    c_t-\mu_{\mathrm{na}}(v)
    \le m_t\bigl(2B(v)-1\bigr).
    \label{eq:frac:pointwise-capacity}
\end{equation}
If \(D(s)=0\), definition~\eqref{eq:frac:recovery-debt} says that
\(\int_u^s(2B(v)-1)\,dv\le0\) for every \(u\le s\).  Integrating both sides of~\eqref{eq:frac:pointwise-capacity} over \((u,s]\) when $D(s)=0$ yields
\[
    c_t(s-u)-\int_u^s\mu_{\mathrm{na}}(v)\,dv\le0,
\]
which is exactly~\eqref{eq:frac:backward-capacity}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:frac:work-comparison}
On \(\{D(s)=0\}\),
\begin{equation}
    W^{\mathrm{rel}}(s)
    \le (n+1)\ell_t+U(s).
    \label{eq:frac:work-comparison}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Relevant work can enter in two ways. First, an arrival increases it by at most
\(S_i\wedge \ell_t\); an arrival to an empty system becomes the anchor
and adds nothing.  Second, a non-anchor job can enter the relevant set when its
remaining size crosses \(\ell_t\). Such a crossing can occur only while
fewer than \(n\) relevant non-anchor jobs are present; otherwise all
available non-anchor capacity is assigned to jobs whose remaining sizes are
already at most \(\ell_t\).  Immediately after a crossing, therefore,
\[
    W^{\mathrm{rel}}\le n\ell_t.
\]

The desired bound is immediate when
\(W^{\mathrm{rel}}(s)\le(n+1)\ell_t\).  Otherwise, trace backward to the
start \(u\) of the current excursion above \(n\ell_t\).  An $\ell_t$ threshold down-crossing cannot start this excursion,
so it starts with an arrival and contains at most \((n+1)\ell_t\) work
immediately afterward.  Throughout the rest of the excursion, more than
\(n\) relevant non-anchor jobs are present.  They use all of the available
non-anchor capacity, which is at least \(\mu_{\mathrm{na}}(v)\)
by~\eqref{eq:frac:mu-na}, and hence
\[
    W^{\mathrm{rel}}(s)
    \le (n+1)\ell_t+\mathcal A(u,s]
              -\int_u^s\mu_{\mathrm{na}}(v)\,dv.
\]
Because \(D(s)=0\), Lemma~\ref{lem:frac:recovery} bounds the integral below
by \(c_t(s-u)\).  Taking the supremum over possible excursion starting times
gives~\eqref{eq:frac:work-comparison}.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:frac:tagged-work-bound}
Suppose a tagged job of size \(x\le \ell_t\) arrives at time zero.  Then  
\begin{align}
&\left\{T_x^{\text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace}>t,\
          \sup_{0\le s\le t}Z(s)\le \hat{A}_t\right\}
\notag\\
&\quad\subseteq
\left\{
    \frac{2}{\delta}x
    +\frac{W^{\mathrm{rel}}(0-)}{c_t}
    +\frac{\mathcal A(0,t]}{c_t}
    >t
\right\}.
\label{eq:frac:tagged-work-bound}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let \(R(s)\) be the tagged job's remaining size at time \(s\).  Roughly
speaking, the non-anchor work that can run ahead of the tagged job must be
completed at rate at least \(c_t\); the only exception is when the tagged
job itself is being served, which happens at rate at least \(\delta/2\).
It remains to identify the work that can run ahead of the tagged job.
Under SRPT, a non-anchor job that is behind the tagged job never overtakes
it, so this work consists of two groups of jobs: the non-anchor jobs
present at time \(0-\) with remaining size at most \(\ell_t\), which are
exactly the jobs counted in \(W^{\mathrm{rel}}(0-)\), and the jobs arriving
after time zero with size at most \(\ell_t\).

To see this more precisely, let \(L(s)\) be the total remaining work at
time \(s\) of the jobs in the above two groups, and define the potential
\begin{equation}
    G(s):=\frac{2}{\delta}R(s)+\frac{1}{c_t}L(s).
    \label{eq:frac:tagged-potential}
\end{equation}
We verify three properties of \(G\).

\emph{Initial value.}
At time zero, \(L(0)=W^{\mathrm{rel}}(0-)\), so
\begin{equation}
    G(0)=\frac{2}{\delta}x+\frac{W^{\mathrm{rel}}(0-)}{c_t}.
    \label{eq:frac:credit-initial}
\end{equation}

\emph{Jumps.}
\(L\) increases only when a job of size \(S_i\le\ell_t\) arrives, and then
by exactly \(S_i\). Hence the total
increase of \(G\) over \((0,t]\) is at most
\[
    \frac{1}{c_t}\sum_{i:\,0<\tau_i\le t,\ S_i\le\ell_t}S_i
    \le\frac{\mathcal A(0,t]}{c_t}.
\]

\emph{Drift.}
Between arrivals, \(G\) decreases at rate at least one while the tagged
job is present.  Let \(q(s)\) be the tagged job's service rate.  There are
three cases.  First, if the tagged job is the anchor, then it is served at
rate one or at rate \(\gamma(x)\ge\delta/2\)
by~\eqref{eq:frac:condition-margin-shape}, so the first term of \(G\)
alone decreases at rate at least one.  Second, if the tagged job is a
non-anchor job with \(q(s)=1\), the same holds.  Third, suppose the tagged
job is a non-anchor job with \(q(s)<1\).  Then more than \(n\) jobs are
present and the tagged job is at least the \(n\)th in the non-anchor SRPT
order, so the jobs ahead of it receive all the non-anchor capacity that
the tagged job does not.  Writing \(p(s)\) for their total service rate,
\[
    p(s)+q(s)=n-\gamma\bigl(Z(s)\bigr)\ge\mu_{\mathrm{na}}(s)
\]
by~\eqref{eq:frac:mu-na}.  On the event on the left side
of~\eqref{eq:frac:tagged-work-bound}, \(Z(s)\le\hat{A}_t\), so, by the
monotonicity of \(\varepsilon\),
\[
    \mu_{\mathrm{na}}(s)
    \ge r+\varepsilon(\hat{A}_t)
    =c_t+m_t
    >c_t.
\]
We claim that every job ahead of the tagged job belongs to one of the two
groups counted in \(L\), so that \(L\) decreases at rate at least \(p(s)\) between arrivals.  Indeed, let
\(j\) be a non-anchor job at time \(s\) that belongs to neither group.
Since the anchor role is permanent, \(j\) was not the anchor at time
\(0-\); hence either \(j\) was present at time \(0-\) with
\(R_j(0-)>\ell_t\), or \(j\) arrived at some time \(\tau_j\in(0,s]\) with
\(S_j>\ell_t\).  In both cases, at the corresponding time
\(s_0\in\{0,\tau_j\}\) we have \(R_j(s_0)>\ell_t\ge x\ge R(s_0)\), so
\(j\) was behind the tagged job.  Both jobs are non-anchor jobs throughout
\([s_0,s]\), and the non-anchor service rates are nonincreasing along the
SRPT order, so \(R_j-R\) is nondecreasing as long as it is positive.
Hence \(R_j(s)>R(s)\), and \(j\) is still behind the tagged job at time
\(s\), which proves the claim.

Finally, the high-load assumption of Theorem~\ref{thm:frac:optimality}
gives \(r\ge n-1\ge1\) and \(\delta\le1\), so \(c_t>r\ge1>\delta/2\), that
is, \(2/\delta>1/c_t\): A unit of the tagged job's own service reduces
\(G\) at least as much as a unit of service to the jobs ahead of it.  Hence
we have that
\[
    -\frac{d}{ds}G(s)
    \ge \frac{2}{\delta}q(s)+\frac{1}{c_t}p(s)
    \ge \frac{p(s)+q(s)}{c_t}
    \ge \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{na}}(s)}{c_t}
    \ge1.
\]

If the tagged job is still present at time \(t\), then \(G(t)>0\).
Integrating the drift and adding the jumps, using
\eqref{eq:frac:credit-initial}, gives
\[
    0<G(t)
    \le
    \frac{2}{\delta}x
    +\frac{W^{\mathrm{rel}}(0-)}{c_t}
        +\frac{\mathcal A(0,t]}{c_t}-t,
\]
which proves the event inclusion.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:frac:concentration}
Assume the following condition:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[(S\wedge\ell_t)^2\right]}{\ell_t}
    =o(m_t)=o\bigl(\varepsilon(\hat{A}_t)\bigr).
    \label{eq:frac:condition-small-moment}
\end{equation}
For all sufficiently
large \(t\),
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[U(0-)>m_t t/4\right]
    &\le \exp\left\{-\frac{m_t t}{8\ell_t}\right\},
    \label{eq:frac:backlog-concentration}\\
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[\mathcal A(0,t]-r_{\ell_t}t>m_t t/4\right]
    &\le \exp\left\{-\frac{m_t t}{8\ell_t}\right\}.
    \label{eq:frac:arrival-concentration}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let \(Y=S\wedge\ell_t\).  The capped input \(\mathcal A(0,s]\) is a
compound Poisson process with rate \(\lambda\) and jumps distributed as
\(Y\), so for every \(\theta>0\),
\[
    \mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[e^{\theta\mathcal A(0,s]}\right]
    =\exp\bigl\{\lambda s\bigl(\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[e^{\theta Y}\right]-1\bigr)\bigr\}.
\]
Its mean is \(\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[\mathcal A(0,s]\right]=\lambda\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[Y\right]s=r_{\ell_t}s\).  Let
\[
    X(s):=\mathcal A(0,s]-r_{\ell_t}s,
    \qquad s\ge0,
\]
be the capped input with its mean removed.  Then \(X(0)=0\), and
\begin{equation}
    \log\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[e^{\theta X(s)}\right]
    =\lambda s\bigl(\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[e^{\theta Y}\right]-1\bigr)-\theta r_{\ell_t}s.
    \label{eq:frac:capped-mgf}
\end{equation}
Set \(\theta=1/\ell_t\).  Since \(0\le\theta Y\le1\), the elementary inequality
\(e^y\le1+y+(e/2)y^2\) gives 
\[
    \lambda s\bigl(\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[e^{\theta Y}\right]-1\bigr)
    \le\theta r_{\ell_t}s+\frac{e}{2}\lambda s\theta^2\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[Y^2\right],
\] 
and hence
\[
    \log\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[e^{\theta X(s)}\right]
        \le \frac{e\lambda s}{2}\theta^2\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[Y^2\right].
\]
Condition~\eqref{eq:frac:condition-small-moment} says
\(\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[Y^2\right]/\ell_t=o(\varepsilon(\hat{A}_t))=o(m_t)\).  Thus, since $\frac{e \lambda}{2}$ is a constant independent of $t$, for all large \(t\), we have
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[e^{\theta X(s)}\right]
    \le \exp\left\{ \frac{e\lambda s}{2}\theta^2\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[Y^2\right] \right\} = \exp\left\{ \frac{e\lambda}{2} s\theta \frac{\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[Y^2\right]}{\ell_t} \right\} \le \exp\left\{\frac{m_t\theta s}{8}\right\}
    \qquad\text{for every \(s\ge0\)}.
        \label{eq:frac:uniform-mgf}
\end{equation}

Markov's inequality and~\eqref{eq:frac:uniform-mgf} yield
\[
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[X(t)>m_t t/4\right]
    \le \exp\{-\theta m_t t/4\}\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[e^{\theta X(t)}\right]
    \le \exp\left\{-\frac{m_t t}{8\ell_t}\right\},
\]
which proves~\eqref{eq:frac:arrival-concentration}.

For the stationary initial backlog, recall from
\eqref{eq:frac:comparison-workload} that
\(U(0-)=\sup_{u\le0}\{\mathcal A(u,0]-c_t(0-u)\}\).  The marked Poisson
process is stationary and reversible in time, so the capped input over
\((u,0]\) has the same law as that over \((0,-u]\).  Hence we have that
\begin{equation}
    U(0-)
    \stackrel{\mathrm d}{=}
    \sup_{s\ge0}\{\mathcal A(0,s]-c_ts\}
    =\sup_{s\ge0}\{X(s)-(c_t-r_{\ell_t})s\},
    \label{eq:frac:backlog-supremum}
\end{equation}
where the last equality uses the definition of \(X\).
Define
\[
    M(s):=\exp\{\theta[X(s)-(c_t-r_{\ell_t})s]\}.
\]
For \(0\le u\le v\), write
\(M(v)=M(u)\exp\{\theta[X(v)-X(u)]-\theta(c_t-r_{\ell_t})(v-u)\}\).
Conditionally on the path of \(X\) up to time \(u\), the factor \(M(u)\)
is fixed, and the increment \(X(v)-X(u)\) is independent of that path
and distributed as \(X(v-u)\).  Hence we have that
\begin{align*}
    \mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[M(v)\mid X(w),\,w\le u\right]
    &=M(u)\,\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[e^{\theta X(v-u)}\right]\,e^{-\theta(c_t-r_{\ell_t})(v-u)}\\
    &\le M(u)\,e^{\theta m_t(v-u)/8}\,e^{-\theta(c_t-r_{\ell_t})(v-u)}
    &&\text{by~\eqref{eq:frac:uniform-mgf}}\\
    &\le M(u)
        &&\text{by \(c_t-r_{\ell_t}\ge m_t\)}.
\end{align*}
Hence \(M\) is a nonnegative supermartingale with \(M(0)=1\).  Doob's
maximal inequality states that a nonnegative supermartingale exceeds a
level \(a>0\) at some time in \([0,T]\) with probability at most
\(\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[M(0)\right]/a\) \citep{durrett2019probability}.  Fix \(z>0\) and
\(T>0\).  Since \(e^{\theta y}\) is increasing in \(y\), the process
\(X(s)-(c_t-r_{\ell_t})s\) exceeds \(z\) at some time in \([0,T]\)
exactly when \(M\) exceeds \(e^{\theta z}\) there.  Hence we have that
\begin{align*}
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sup_{0\le s\le T}\{X(s)-(c_t-r_{\ell_t})s\}>z\right]
    &=\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sup_{0\le s\le T}M(s)>e^{\theta z}\right]\\
    &\le \mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[M(0)\right]\,e^{-\theta z}
    &&\text{by Doob's maximal inequality}\\
    &=e^{-\theta z}
        &&\text{by \(M(0)=1\)}.
\end{align*}
As \(T\to\infty\), the events on the left increase to
\(\{\sup_{s\ge0}\{X(s)-(c_t-r_{\ell_t})s\}>z\}\), so the same bound
holds for that event.  By \eqref{eq:frac:backlog-supremum}, for every
\(z>0\),
\[
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[U(0-)>z\right]
    \le e^{-\theta z}.
\]
Taking \(z=m_t t/4\) gives the stronger exponent \(-m_t t/(4\ell_t)\), and therefore
also proves~\eqref{eq:frac:backlog-concentration}.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:frac:collision}
Suppose \eqref{eq:frac:condition-completion} and
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-large-threshold} hold.
For all sufficiently large \(t\) and every
\(\ell_t<x\le h_t\),
\begin{equation}

<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let \(C_0(x)\) be the event that a job with original size larger than
\(\sqrt x\) is present at time \(0-\), and let \(C_1(x)\) be the event that
a job larger than \(x\) arrives during \((0,2\sqrt x/\delta]\).  Since
\(x>\ell_t\to\infty\), we may assume \(x>\sqrt x\).  On
\(C_0(x)^c\cap C_1(x)^c\), no job larger than \(x\) is present at time
\(0-\) or arrives before the current anchor completes, and ties with jobs
of size exactly \(x\) are broken in favor of the tagged job by arrival
order.  The path described before \eqref{eq:frac:condition-completion} therefore applies: the tagged
job becomes the anchor within \(2\sqrt x/\delta\) time units and completes
within a further \(x/\gamma(\ell_t)\).  Since \(x\le h_t\le t\) and by
condition~\eqref{eq:frac:condition-completion},
\[
    \frac{2\sqrt x}{\delta}+\frac{x}{\gamma(\ell_t)} \le \frac{2\sqrt h_t}{\delta}+\frac{h_t}{\gamma(\ell_t)}
        \le\frac{2\sqrt t}{\delta}+\frac{h_t}{\gamma(\ell_t)}
    \le t,
\]
which shows
\[
    \{T_x^{\text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace}>t\}\subseteq C_0(x)\cup C_1(x).
\]

By PASTA and Lemma~\ref{lem:frac:anchor-occupancy} at threshold
\(a=\sqrt x\), the probability \(\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[C_0(x)\right]\) is at most
\(4r^{>\sqrt x}/\delta\).  Arrivals larger than \(x\) form a Poisson
process with rate \(\lambda\overline F(x)\), so \(\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[C_1(x)\right]\) is at most \((2\sqrt x/\delta)\lambda\overline F(x)\).  A union bound proves~\eqref{eq:frac:collision-bound}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:frac:large}
Under
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-completion},
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-large-threshold}, and
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-collision},
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[T_{x}^{\text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace}>t,\ \ell_t<x\le h_t\right]
    =o\bigl(\overline F(\delta t)\bigr).
    \label{eq:frac:large-contribution}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Fix \(x>\ell_t\).  Since \(x\ge\sqrt x\), we have
\(\overline F(x)\le\overline F(\sqrt x)\), and
\[
    \lambda\sqrt x\,\overline F(\sqrt x)
    \le\lambda\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[S\mathbf 1\{S>\sqrt x\}\right]
    =r^{>\sqrt x}.
\]
Hence the second term of~\eqref{eq:frac:collision-bound} is at most
\(2r^{>\sqrt x}/\delta\), and because \(r^{>y}\) is nonincreasing in \(y\),
\[
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[T_x^{\text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace}>t\right]
    \le\frac{6r^{>\sqrt x}}{\delta}
    \le\frac{6r^{>\sqrt{\ell_t}}}{\delta}
        \qquad\text{for all \(\ell_t<x\le h_t\)}.
\]
Conditioning on the tagged size and integrating over \(\ell_t<x\le h_t\)
multiplies this uniform bound by at most \(\overline F(\ell_t)\), so
\[
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[T_{x}^{\text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace}>t,\ \ell_t<x\le h_t\right]
        \le\frac{6}{\delta}\,r^{>\sqrt{\ell_t}}\,\overline F(\ell_t),
\]
which is \(o(\overline F(\delta t))\) by~\eqref{eq:frac:condition-collision}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:frac:explicit}
Suppose \(n\ge2\), \((n-1)/n\le\rho<1\), and the job-size distribution has a
regularly varying tail with tail index \(\alpha>1\).  Let \text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace use
the capacity margin
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon(z):=\frac{\delta}{2+\log(1+z)}.
    \label{eq:frac:explicit-margin}
\end{equation}
Then
\[
    \lim_{t\to\infty}
    \frac{\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[T^{\text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace}>t\right]}{\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[S>\delta t\right]}
    =1.
\]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
The three bounds of the roadmap were proved under requirements on the
capacity margin and on the thresholds \(\hat{A}_t\), \(\ell_t\), and
\(h_t\), which we first collect.
\begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\roman{enumi}}{\roman{enumi}}\renewcommand{\textup{(\roman{enumi})}}{\textup{(\roman{enumi})}}
    \item The capacity margin: \(0<\varepsilon(z)\le\delta/2\),
          \(\varepsilon\) is nonincreasing, and \(\varepsilon(z)\to0\) as
          \(z\to\infty\); these are \eqref{eq:frac:margin-basic} and
          \eqref{eq:frac:condition-margin-shape}.
    \item The anchor threshold: \(\hat{A}_t\to\infty\) and
          \(\ell_t\ll\hat{A}_t\), which is \eqref{eq:frac:condition-cap}, and
          \(r^{>\hat{A}_t}+t\overline F(\hat{A}_t)=o(\overline F(\delta t))\), which is
          \eqref{eq:frac:condition-anchor}.
    \item The small/large cutoff: \(\ell_t\to\infty\), which is
          \eqref{eq:frac:condition-large-threshold};
          \(\varepsilon(\hat{A}_t)t/(\ell_t\log t)\to\infty\), which is
          \eqref{eq:frac:condition-small-exponent};
          \(\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[(S\wedge\ell_t)^2\right]/\ell_t=o(\varepsilon(\hat{A}_t))\), which is
          \eqref{eq:frac:condition-small-moment}; and
          \(r^{>\sqrt{\ell_t}}\,\overline F(\ell_t)=o(\overline F(\delta t))\), which is
          \eqref{eq:frac:condition-collision}.
    \item The large/huge cutoff:
          \(\ell_t<h_t\le\gamma(\ell_t)(t-2\sqrt t/\delta)\), which is
          \eqref{eq:frac:condition-completion}, and \(h_t/(\delta t)\to1\),
          which is \eqref{eq:frac:condition-huge-boundary}.
\end{enumerate}

It is straightforward that the capacity margin \eqref{eq:frac:explicit-margin}  satisfies (i).  For the thresholds we take
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_t:=\exp\{(\log t)^2\},
    \qquad
    \ell_t:=\frac{t}{(\log t)^6},
    \qquad
    h_t:=\gamma(\ell_t)\Bigl(t-\frac{2\sqrt t}{\delta}\Bigr)=(\delta - \varepsilon(z))\Bigl(t-\frac{2\sqrt t}{\delta}\Bigr).
    \label{eq:frac:explicit-thresholds}
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:frac:rv-verification} in
Appendix~\ref{app:frac:rv} verifies that these choices satisfy (ii)--(iv).  


With all requirements in place, split the tagged job by size as in
\eqref{eq:frac:three-way-split}:
\begin{align*}
    \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[T^{\text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace}>t\right]
    &\le \mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[T_{x}^{\text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace}>t,\ x\le\ell_t\right]\\
    &\quad+\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[T_{x}^{\text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace}>t,\ \ell_t<x\le h_t\right]
       +\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[x>h_t\right].
\end{align*}
Propositions~\ref{prop:frac:small} and \ref{prop:frac:large} make the
first two terms \(o(\overline F(\delta t))\), and \eqref{eq:frac:huge-tail}
makes the last term \((1+o(1))\overline F(\delta t)\).  Hence we have that
\[
    \limsup_{t\to\infty}
    \frac{\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[T^{\text{DynaSPLIT}\xspace}>t\right]}{\mathrm{\mathbb{P}}\left[S>\delta t\right]}
        \le1.
\]
This matches the super-server lower bound~\eqref{eq:lower bound super server}, and completes the proof of strong tail optimality.

There is a constant \(C<\infty\) such that, for all sufficiently large

    r^{>y}\le Cy\overline F(y).
    \label{eq:frac:rv-bounds}
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:frac:rv-verification}
Let \(\varepsilon\) be given by \eqref{eq:frac:explicit-margin} and
\(\hat{A}_t\), \(\ell_t\), \(h_t\) by \eqref{eq:frac:explicit-thresholds}.
Then conditions
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-cap},
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-anchor},
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-large-threshold},
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-small-exponent},
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-small-moment},
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-collision},
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-completion}, and
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-huge-boundary} hold.

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Throughout, choose \(\eta\in(0,\alpha-1)\) and write
\(\beta:=\alpha-\eta>1\).  The margin at the anchor threshold is
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon(\hat{A}_t)
    =\frac{\delta}{2+\log\bigl(1+e^{(\log t)^2}\bigr)}
    =\Theta\bigl((\log t)^{-2}\bigr).
    \label{eq:frac:explicit-cap-margin}
\end{equation}

\emph{Conditions \eqref{eq:frac:condition-cap},
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-large-threshold}, and
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-small-exponent}.}
Clearly \(\hat{A}_t\to\infty\) and \(\ell_t\to\infty\), and
\(\ell_t/\hat{A}_t\to0\) because \(\hat{A}_t=t^{\log t}\) grows faster than
every fixed power of \(t\).  By~\eqref{eq:frac:explicit-cap-margin},
\[
    \frac{\varepsilon(\hat{A}_t)t}{\ell_t\log t}
        =\Theta\bigl((\log t)^3\bigr)
    \longrightarrow\infty.
\]

\emph{Conditions \eqref{eq:frac:condition-completion} and
\eqref{eq:frac:condition-huge-boundary}.}
Because \(\ell_t/t\to0\) and \(\gamma(\ell_t)\to\delta>0\), we have
\(\ell_t<h_t\) eventually, and \(h_t\) satisfies the upper inequality
in~\eqref{eq:frac:condition-completion} with equality.  Moreover,
\[
    \frac{h_t}{\delta t}
    =\frac{\gamma(\ell_t)}{\delta}\Bigl(1-\frac{2}{\delta\sqrt t}\Bigr)
    \longrightarrow1.
\]

\emph{Condition \eqref{eq:frac:condition-anchor}.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:frac:rv-bounds} gives \(r^{>\hat{A}_t}\le C\hat{A}_t\overline F(\hat{A}_t)\),
and the upper bound in~\eqref{eq:frac:potter} with \(x=\delta t\) and
\(y=\hat{A}_t\) gives \(\overline F(\hat{A}_t)/\overline F(\delta t)\le C(\hat{A}_t/t)^{-\beta}\).
Hence we have that
\begin{align*}
    \frac{r^{>\hat{A}_t}+t\overline F(\hat{A}_t)}{\overline F(\delta t)}
    &\le C(\hat{A}_t+t)\left(\frac{\hat{A}_t}{t}\right)^{-\beta}\\
    &=C\left(
        t^\beta\hat{A}_t^{1-\beta}
        +t^{\beta+1}\hat{A}_t^{-\beta}
      \right).
\end{align*}
Because \(\hat{A}_t=t^{\log t}\) grows faster than every fixed power of
\(t\), both terms converge to zero.

\emph{Condition \eqref{eq:frac:condition-small-moment}.}
Without loss of generality take \(\beta<2\), which is possible because
\(\eta\) may be increased toward \(\alpha-1\).  The tail-integral identity
for the capped second moment gives
\[
    \mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[(S\wedge\ell_t)^2\right]
    =2\int_0^{\ell_t}x\overline F(x)\,dx.
\]
Using \(\overline F(x)\le Cx^{-\beta}\) for \(x\ge x_\eta\) and
\(\overline F\le1\) below, the integral is at most
\(C+C\ell_t^{2-\beta}\), and therefore
\[
    \frac{\mathrm{\SFbf E}\left[(S\wedge\ell_t)^2\right]}{\ell_t}
    \le C\ell_t^{1-\beta}+\frac{C}{\ell_t}
    =O\bigl(\ell_t^{1-\beta}\bigr).
\]
The right side decays polynomially in \(t\), because
\(\ell_t=t/(\log t)^6\) and \(\beta>1\), whereas
\eqref{eq:frac:explicit-cap-margin} says that \(\varepsilon(\hat{A}_t)\)
decays only logarithmically.

\emph{Condition \eqref{eq:frac:condition-collision}.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:frac:rv-bounds} gives
\(r^{>\sqrt{\ell_t}}\le C\sqrt{\ell_t}\,\overline F(\sqrt{\ell_t})\).
Since \(\ell_t<\delta t\) for all large \(t\), the lower bound
in~\eqref{eq:frac:potter} with \(x=\ell_t\) and \(y=\delta t\) gives
\[
    \frac{\overline F(\ell_t)}{\overline F(\delta t)}
    \le C\left(\frac{\delta t}{\ell_t}\right)^{\alpha+\eta}
    =C\delta^{\alpha+\eta}(\log t)^{6(\alpha+\eta)}.
\]
Combining this with the polynomial bound
\(\overline F(\sqrt{\ell_t})\le C\ell_t^{-\beta/2}\) yields
\[
    \frac{r^{>\sqrt{\ell_t}}\,\overline F(\ell_t)}{\overline F(\delta t)}
    \le C\ell_t^{(1-\beta)/2}(\log t)^{6(\alpha+\eta)}
    \le Ct^{(1-\beta)/2}(\log t)^{3(\beta-1)+6(\alpha+\eta)}
    \longrightarrow0,
\]
because \(\beta>1\).  The only essential fact in this calculation is
\(\alpha>1\): it makes the negative power of \(t\) dominate the logarithmic
factor.
