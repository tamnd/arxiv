<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{rankus}
Let $A=(a_{ij})\in \mathbb{K}^{N\times N},$ where $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}.$ If 
$$
2|a_{ii}|>\sum_{j\ne i}|a_{ij}|, \qquad i\in [1:N] 
$$
then ${\rm rank}(A)\geq \tfrac{N}{2}.$
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
Suppose, to the contrary, that $r:=\mathrm{rank}(A)<\tfrac{N}{2}$. The assumption implies $a_{ii}\neq 0$ for all $i$, so $A\neq 0$ and $r\geq 1$. Among all $r\times r$ submatrices of $A$, choose one, say $A_{I,J}$, with maximal $|\det A_{I,J}|$. Since $\mathrm{rank}(A)=r$, we have $\det A_{I,J}\neq 0$.  In particular, the columns $a_j$, $j\in J$, of $A$  form a basis of the column space of $A$. Hence, for every $k\in J':=[1:N]\setminus J$, there exist scalars $c_j^k\in \mathbb{K}$, $j\in J$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{kombin}
a_k=\sum_{j\in J}c_j^ka_j .
\end{equation}
We claim that $|c_j^k|\leq 1$ for all $j\in J$ and $k\in J'$. Indeed, fix such $j$ and $k$ and put $J_j^k := (J \cup \{k\}) \setminus \{j\}$.  By \eqref{kombin} and multilinearity of the determinant, $|\det A_{I,J_j^k}| = |c_j^k| \cdot |\det A_{I,J}|$. The maximality of $|\det A_{I,J}|$ implies
$$
|\det A_{I,J}|\geq |\det A_{I,J_j^k}|=|c_j^k||\det A_{I,J}|.
$$
Since $\det A_{I,J}\neq 0$, this gives $|c_j^k|\leq 1$, as required. Now let $k \in J'$. By \eqref{kombin}  and the above observation, we get
$$
|a_{kk}|\leq \sum_{j\in J}|c_j^k||a_{kj}|\leq \sum_{j\in J}|a_{kj}|.
$$
Since $k \notin J$, this and the lemma's assumption imply
$$
\sum_{\substack{j\in J'\\ j\neq k}}|a_{kj}|=\sum_{j\ne k}|a_{kj}|-\sum_{j\in J}|a_{kj}|<2|a_{kk}|-|a_{kk}|=|a_{kk}|.
$$
Thus the submatrix $A_{J',J'}$ is strictly diagonally dominant and hence, by Theorem~\ref{domina}, nonsingular. It follows that
$$
N-r=|J'|\leq \mathrm{rank}(A)=r.
$$
Hence $\tfrac{N}{2}\leq r$,  a contradiction.\\

So it is enough to consider $0<\varepsilon < \frac13. $ Since $\lambda(V)=\lambda(V,\ell_{\infty}^N)$  there exists   a projection $P:\ell_\infty^N\to V$ such that
$$
\|P\|= 1+\varepsilon=\lambda(V).
$$
By Theorem~\ref{base}, there exist pairwise disjoint nonempty sets
$A_1,\ldots,A_{N-2}\subset[1:N]$ and indices $k_i\in A_i$ such that
$$
\sum_{j\in A_i}|p_{k_i j}|\geq1-\varepsilon,
\qquad i\in[1:N-2].
$$
Let $p_j$ denote the $j$-th column of $P$ and set
$$
q_i:=\sum_{j\in A_i}{\rm sgn}(p_{k_i j})p_j.
$$
Since each $p_j$ belongs to $V$, we have $q_i\in V$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{qdiag}
(q_i)_{k_i}
=
\sum_{j\in A_i}{\rm sgn}(p_{k_i j})p_{k_i j}
=
\sum_{j\in A_i}|p_{k_i j}|
\geq1-\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
The disjointness of the sets $A_i$ implies that, for every
$l\in[1:N]$,
\begin{align}
\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}|(q_i)_l|
\leq
\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}\sum_{j\in A_i}|p_{lj}| 
\leq
\sum_{j=1}^N|p_{lj}|
\leq1+\varepsilon.
\label{qineq}
\end{align}
We first show that $q_1,\ldots,q_{N-2}$ are linearly independent.
Suppose that
$\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}a_iq_i=0$
and choose $m$ such that $|a_m|=\max_i|a_i|$. 
Then, using \eqref{qdiag} and \eqref{qineq}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
0 &= \Big| \sum_{i=1}^{N-2}a_i(q_i)_{k_m} \Big|
\geq |a_m||(q_m)_{k_m}|- \sum_{i\neq m}|a_i||(q_i)_{k_m}|
\geq |a_m|\Big( |(q_m)_{k_m}|- \sum_{i\neq m}|(q_i)_{k_m}|\Big)\\
&=|a_m| \Big( 2|(q_m)_{k_m}| - \sum_{i=1}^{N-2}|(q_i)_{k_m}| \Big)
\geq |a_m|\bigl(2(1-\varepsilon)-(1+\varepsilon)\bigr)
=|a_m|(1-3\varepsilon).
\end{align*}
Since $\varepsilon<\frac13$ we get $|a_m|=0,$ and consequently $a_i=0$ for all $i\in[1,N-2].$

$$
T:V\longrightarrow\ell_\infty^{N-2},
\qquad
T\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}a_iq_i\Big)
=
(a_1,\ldots,a_{N-2}).
$$

\begin{align*}
\|T^{-1}a\|_\infty
&= \Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}a_iq_i\Big\|_\infty
\leq \|a\|_\infty \max_{l\in[1:N]}\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}|(q_i)_l|
\leq
(1+\varepsilon)\|a\|_\infty.
\end{align*}
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{Tinverse}
\|T^{-1}\|\leq1+\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
On the other hand, choose $m$ such that $|a_m|=\|a\|_\infty$. Using
\eqref{qdiag} and \eqref{qineq} once again, we get
\begin{align*}
\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}a_iq_i\Big\|_\infty
\geq \Big| \sum_{i=1}^{N-2}a_i(q_i)_{k_m} \Big|
\geq |a_m| \Big( 2|(q_m)_{k_m}|- \sum_{i=1}^{N-2}|(q_i)_{k_m}| \Big)
\geq (1-3\varepsilon)\|a\|_\infty.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{Tnorm}
\|T\|\leq\frac1{1-3\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{Tinverse} and \eqref{Tnorm}, we obtain
$$
d(V,\ell_\infty^{N-2})
\leq
\|T\|\,\|T^{-1}\|
\leq
\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-3\varepsilon}=\frac{\lambda(V)}{4-3\lambda(V)}
$$
 as required.

$A_{3+j}=\{2j+5,2j+6\},$ $
k_{3+j}=2j+5.$
Since $e_6\in W_n$ and $e_{2j+5}+e_{2j+6}\in W_n,$
and $P$ is a projection onto $W_n$, we have
$$
Pe_6=e_6\qquad \textrm{and} \qquad P(e_{2j+5}+e_{2j+6})
=
e_{2j+5}+e_{2j+6}.
$$
In particular,
$$
p_{66}=1\qquad \textrm{and} \qquad
p_{2j+5,\,2j+5}+p_{2j+5,\,2j+6}=1.
$$
Hence
$\sum_{l\in A_3}|p_{k_3l}|=1$
and, for every $j\in[1:n-3]$,
$$
\sum_{l\in A_{3+j}}|p_{k_{3+j}l}|
=
|p_{2j+5,\,2j+5}|+|p_{2j+5,\,2j+6}|
\geq1.
$$
Thus all the sets $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ satisfy the required estimate,
which completes the proof.\\

The spaces $W_n$ show that the geometric property used above
and the column-combining condition are genuinely different.
The former fails for these spaces, whereas \eqref{colcomb} holds
for every projection onto $W_n$ of norm at most $4/3$.

So far, we have not found a counterexample to \eqref{colcomb}
among projections of norm less than $4/3$, in any codimension.
It is therefore natural to ask whether this condition holds
for every such projection onto a subspace of $\ell_\infty^N$.
By the argument of Theorem~\ref{Bazur}, this would yield the
corresponding Banach--Mazur estimate without any restriction
on the codimension. Proposition~\ref{counterhigher} shows,
however, that a general proof would require more than a direct
extension of the geometric argument used here.
