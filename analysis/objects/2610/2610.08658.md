Let $\mathscr Q$ be a given source term. A nonnegative function $f\in L^\infty((0,\infty);L^1(\mathcal O))$ is called a \emph{renormalized solution} of
\[
\begin{cases}
\partial_t f+v_1\partial_x f=\mathscr Q &\text{in }(0,\infty)\times\mathcal O,\\
f|_{t=0}=f_0,
\end{cases}
\]
with boundary condition \textbf{(In)} or \textbf{(Max)} if there exist trace functions $f_l,f_r$ satisfying \eqref{eq/ inflow} or \eqref{eq/ maxwell} almost everywhere, respectively, such that for every $t>0$, $\varphi\in\mathcal D([0,t]\times\overline{\mathcal O})$, and $\beta\in\mathfrak B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ green}
\begin{aligned}

\label{thm1}
Assume that $f_0$ satisfies \eqref{eq/ data}. Consider either the inflow condition \textbf{(In)} with \eqref{eq/ in data}, or the Maxwell condition \textbf{(Max)} with $\alpha_l,\alpha_r>0$. Then there exists a nonnegative function
\[
f\in  C([0,\infty);L^1(\mathcal O,\langle v \rangle^2 \mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v)) 
\]
with traces satisfying the prescribed boundary condition almost everywhere and such that for every $\beta\in\mathfrak B$:
\[
\begin{cases}
\partial_t\beta(f)+v_1\partial_x\beta(f) = \beta'(f)\rho_f(M[f]-f ) &\text{in }\mathcal D'((0,\infty)\times\mathcal O),\\
\beta(f)|_{t=0}=\beta(f_0).
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, $f$ is a global renormalized solution of \eqref{eq/ bgk}. It also has the following properties. 
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{(In)}] The solution satisfies the Boltzmann entropy inequality \eqref{eq/ ent in}. Moreover, for every $t\ge0$ and $\psi\in\{1,v_1,v_2,v_3,|v|^2\}$,
\[

\]
\item[\textbf{(Max)}] The total mass is conserved:
\[
\iint_{\mathcal O}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v=\iint_{\mathcal O}f_0\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v,\quad t\ge0.
\]
The solution satisfies the relative entropy inequality \eqref{eq/ ent max}, including the collision dissipation and the Darroz\`es--Guiraud boundary dissipation.
\end{enumerate}
Both entropy inequalities hold for every $t\ge0$. These properties are proved in Proposition \ref{prop/ bal}.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem/ j bounds}
Let $f\ge0$ be a weak solution of \eqref{eq/ lambda} on $[0,T]$, satisfying either \textbf{(In)} or \textbf{(Max)}, and assume
\[
f\in C([0,T];L^1(\mathcal O)) \cap L^\infty(0,T; L^1(\mathcal O,\langle v\rangle^2\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v) )
\]
In case \textbf{(Max)}, set
\[
K(t):=\iint_{\mathcal O}xv_1f(t,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v, \quad c_{\mathfrak M,1}:= \int_{v_1<0}v_1^2\mathfrak M(v)\,\textnormal{d}v>0.
\]
Then, for every $t\in[0,T]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ J est}
\frac{c_{\mathfrak M,1}}2 \int_0^t(\alpha_lJ_l+\alpha_rJ_r )\,\mathrm{d}s \le K(0)-K(t) +\int_0^t\iint_{\mathcal O}v_1^2f\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s.
\end{equation}
For either boundary condition, the traces satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ tr wt}
\int_0^T\int_{\mathbb R^3} \langle v\rangle^2(f_l+f_r)|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t<\infty.
\end{equation}
More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ bdy E}
\alpha_l \int_0^T\int_{v_1<0} \langle v\rangle^2 f_l|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t +\alpha_r \int_0^T\int_{v_1>0} \langle v\rangle^2 f_rv_1\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t \le C_T,
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_l=\alpha_r=1$ in case \textbf{(In)}. The constant $C_T$ depends only on the bulk second-moment bound and the prescribed boundary data; in the Maxwell case it may also depend on $\alpha_l,\alpha_r$ and $\mathfrak M$. It is independent of $\|\lambda\|_{L^\infty}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We use a standard multiplier argument; see \cite{AM94,Mis00,Mis00T,Mis10}. Let $0\le\chi_R\le1$ be a family of smooth radial velocity cutoffs such that $\chi_R\uparrow1$ as $R\to\infty$. The bound \eqref{eq/ Q wt} justifies the passage to the limit in all volume terms arising from the velocity truncation.

Consider first \textbf{(Max)}. Testing the Green formula with $xv_1\chi_R(v)$ and passing to the limit $R\to\infty$, using the momentum cancellation in \eqref{eq/ M mom}, gives, initially for almost every $t\in(0,T)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ xv1}
K(t)-K(0) +\frac12\int_0^t\int_{\mathbb R^3} v_1^2(f_l+f_r)\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s = \int_0^t\iint_{\mathcal O}v_1^2f\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s.
\end{equation}
We also note that $K\in C([0,T])$. Indeed, for every $s,t\in[0,T]$ and $R>0$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq/ v1 cont}
\begin{aligned}
|K(t)-K(s)| &\le \frac12\iint_{\mathcal O}|v_1|\,|f(t)-f(s)|\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \\
&\le \frac{R}{2}\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^1(\mathcal O)} +\frac1R\sup_{0\le\tau\le T} \iint_{\mathcal O}|v|^2f(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Fixing $R\gg 1$ large and then using the continuity of $f$ in $L^1(\mathcal O)$ yields the claimed continuity. Since the remaining terms in \eqref{eq/ xv1} are absolutely continuous in time, the identity extends to every $t\in[0,T]$.

The Maxwell boundary condition yields
\begin{align*}
\frac{c_{\mathfrak M,1}}2 \int_0^t(\alpha_lJ_l+\alpha_rJ_r)\,\mathrm{d}s ={}& K(0)-K(t) +\int_0^t\iint_{\mathcal O}v_1^2f\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s\\
&- \left(1-\frac{\alpha_l}{2}\right) \int_0^t\int_{v_1<0}v_1^2f_l\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s - \left(1-\frac{\alpha_r}{2}\right) \int_0^t\int_{v_1>0}v_1^2f_r\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s.
\end{align*}
Discarding the last two nonpositive terms proves \eqref{eq/ J est}.

We next test with $\langle v\rangle^2\chi_R(v)$. In case \textbf{(In)}, the collision contribution vanishes in the limit due to \eqref{eq/ M mom}, and the Green formula gives
\begin{align*}
&\iint_{\mathcal O}\langle v\rangle^2f(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v +\int_0^t\int_{v_1<0} \langle v\rangle^2f_l|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s +\int_0^t\int_{v_1>0} \langle v\rangle^2f_rv_1\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad = \iint_{\mathcal O}\langle v\rangle^2f_0\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v +\int_0^t\int_{v_1>0} \langle v\rangle^2g_lv_1\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s +\int_0^t\int_{v_1<0} \langle v\rangle^2g_r|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s.
\end{align*}
The right-hand side is finite by \eqref{eq/ in data}.

For \textbf{(Max)}, the specular part preserves the radial weight $\langle v\rangle^2$. Set
\[
c_{\mathfrak M,2} := \int_{v_1>0}\langle v\rangle^2\mathfrak M(v)v_1\,\textnormal{d}v.
\]
The same velocity-truncation argument gives
\begin{align*}
&\iint_{\mathcal O}\langle v\rangle^2f(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v +\alpha_l\int_0^t\int_{v_1<0} \langle v\rangle^2f_l|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s +\alpha_r\int_0^t\int_{v_1>0} \langle v\rangle^2f_rv_1\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad = \iint_{\mathcal O}\langle v\rangle^2f_0\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v +c_{\mathfrak M,2} \int_0^t(\alpha_lJ_l+\alpha_rJ_r)\,\mathrm{d}s.
\end{align*}
Together with \eqref{eq/ J est}, this proves \eqref{eq/ bdy E}. Since $\alpha_l,\alpha_r>0$, the Maxwell boundary condition also controls the corresponding incoming weighted fluxes through the reflected outgoing part and the Maxwellian contribution. This gives \eqref{eq/ tr wt}. The inflow case follows directly from the above energy balance and \eqref{eq/ in data}. This completes the proof.

Under the assumptions of Lemma \ref{lem/ j bounds}, the macroscopic moments satisfy

\partial_t\rho+\partial_xj_1=0, \quad \partial_tj_1+\partial_xe_1=0 \quad\text{in }\mathcal D'((0,T)\times I).

Moreover, define

Then, for every $\varphi\in\mathcal D((0,T)\times\overline I)$,

\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \int_0^T \Big[ \varphi(t,\frac12)j_{f_r,1}(t) -\varphi(t,-\frac12)j_{f_l,1}(t) \Big]\,\mathrm{d}t, &\text{in case \textbf{(In)}},\\[8pt]

We first derive the continuity equation together with its boundary contribution. Let $\chi_R\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$ be a radial cutoff such that $\mathbf 1_{\{|v|\le R\}}\le\chi_R\le\mathbf 1_{\{|v|\le2R\}}$. For $\varphi\in\mathcal D((0,T)\times\overline I)$, use $\varphi_R(t,x,v):=\varphi(t,x)\chi_R(v)$ as a test function in the Green formula for \eqref{eq/ lambda}. Since $\varphi$ is compactly supported in time, the initial and terminal terms vanish, and we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ chir}
\begin{aligned}
&\int_0^T \int_{\mathbb R^3} v_1f_r(t,v)\varphi(t,\frac12)\chi_R(v)\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t - \int_0^T \int_{\mathbb R^3} v_1f_l(t,v)\varphi(t,-\frac12)\chi_R(v)\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t\\
&= \int_0^T \iint_{\mathcal O} f(\partial_t\varphi+v_1\partial_x\varphi)\chi_R \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t + \int_0^T \iint_{\mathcal O} \lambda(\rho)(M[f]-f)\varphi\chi_R \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

We next pass to the limit $R\to\infty$. The boundary terms are well defined since \eqref{eq/ tr wt} implies
\[
\int_0^T|j_{f_r,1}(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t \le \int_0^T\int_{\mathbb R^3}|v_1|f_r\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t<\infty \quad \text{and} \quad \int_0^T|j_{f_l,1}(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t \le \int_0^T\int_{\mathbb R^3}|v_1|f_l\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t<\infty.
\]
Hence the boundary integrals in \eqref{eq/ chir} converge by the dominated convergence theorem.

For the bulk transport term, we use $|v_1|\le\langle v\rangle$ together with the finite second moment of $f$. Thus
\[
\int_0^T\iint_{\mathcal O} f(\partial_t\varphi+v_1\partial_x\varphi)\chi_R \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t \longrightarrow  \int_0^T\int_I (\rho\partial_t\varphi+j_1\partial_x\varphi ) \,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.
\]

It remains to consider the collision term. The bound \eqref{eq/ Q wt} provides an integrable majorant. Hence the dominated convergence theorem, together with the mass cancellation in \eqref{eq/ M mom}, gives
\[
\int_0^T\iint_{\mathcal O} \lambda(\rho)(M[f]-f)\varphi\chi_R \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t \longrightarrow  \int_0^T\int_I \lambda(\rho)\varphi \left( \int_{\mathbb R^3}(M[f]-f)\,\textnormal{d}v \right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t =0.
\]
Passing to the limit in \eqref{eq/ chir}, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ mass green}
\int_0^T \varphi(t,\frac12)j_{f_r,1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t - \int_0^T \varphi(t,-\frac12)j_{f_l,1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t = \int_0^T\int_I (\rho\partial_t\varphi+j_1\partial_x\varphi ) \,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.
\end{equation}
In particular, taking $\varphi\in\mathcal D((0,T)\times I)$ gives
\[
\partial_t\rho+\partial_xj_1=0 \quad\text{in }\mathcal D'((0,T)\times I).
\]
Equation \eqref{eq/ mass green} also proves \eqref{eq/ mass bdy} in case \textbf{(In)}.

For the Maxwell boundary condition, the net mass flux vanishes at each endpoint. We verify this at $x=\frac12$. For almost every $t\in(0,T)$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
j_{f_r,1}(t) &= \int_{v_1>0}v_1f_r(t,v)\,\textnormal{d}v + \int_{v_1<0}v_1f_r(t,v)\,\textnormal{d}v\\
&= J_r(t) + \int_{v_1<0} v_1 \left[ (1-\alpha_r)f_r(t,\mathscr V v) +\alpha_r\mathfrak M(v)J_r(t) \right] \textnormal{d}v.
\end{aligned}
\]
Under the change of variables $v\mapsto\mathscr V v$,
\[
\int_{v_1<0} v_1f_r(t,\mathscr V v)\,\textnormal{d}v = -\int_{v_1>0}v_1f_r(t,v)\,\textnormal{d}v = -J_r(t),
\]
while the normalization \eqref{eq/ M norm} gives
\[
\int_{v_1<0}v_1\mathfrak M(v)\,\textnormal{d}v=-1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
j_{f_r,1}(t) = J_r(t) -(1-\alpha_r)J_r(t) -\alpha_rJ_r(t) =0.
\]
The same computation at $x=-\frac12$ gives $j_{f_l,1}(t)=0$. Substituting these identities into \eqref{eq/ mass green} proves \eqref{eq/ mass bdy} for \textbf{(Max)}.




We finally derive the normal momentum equation. Let now $\varphi\in\mathcal D((0,T)\times I)$ and use $\psi_R(t,x,v) := \varphi(t,x)v_1\chi_R(v)$ as a test function. Since $\varphi$ is compactly supported in the interior of $I$, the boundary terms vanish. The Green formula yields
\[
0 = \int_0^T\iint_{\mathcal O} f \Big( v_1\partial_t\varphi +v_1^2\partial_x\varphi \Big) \chi_R \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t + \int_0^T\iint_{\mathcal O} \lambda(\rho)(M[f]-f) v_1\varphi\chi_R \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t.
\]
The first integral converges as $R\to\infty$ to
\[
\int_0^T\int_I ( j_1\partial_t\varphi +e_1\partial_x\varphi  )\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,
\]
since the second velocity moment is integrable.

For the collision term, \eqref{eq/ Q wt} provides an integrable majorant, while the momentum cancellation in \eqref{eq/ M mom} gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^3}v_1(M[f]-f)\,\textnormal{d}v=0.
\]
Hence
\[
\int_0^T \iint_{\mathcal O} \lambda(\rho)(M[f]-f) v_1\varphi\chi_R \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t \longrightarrow  0,
\]
and this yields
\[
\int_0^T\int_I ( j_1\partial_t\varphi +e_1\partial_x\varphi  )\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=0
\]
for every $\varphi\in\mathcal D((0,T)\times I)$. This is equivalent to
\[
\partial_tj_1+\partial_xe_1=0 \quad\text{in }\mathcal D'((0,T)\times I).
\]
This completes the proof.

We regularize only in time. Choose $0\le\zeta\in C_c^\infty((-2,-1))$, $\int_{\mathbb R}\zeta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=1$, and, for $0<\varepsilon<\min\{1,\frac T2\}$, set $\zeta_\varepsilon(t):= \frac1\varepsilon\zeta\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\right)$. \rho_\varepsilon(t) :=\int_0^T\rho(s)\zeta_\varepsilon(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

and analogously $j_\varepsilon$ and $e_{1\varepsilon}$. Since $\operatorname{supp}\zeta_\varepsilon(t-\cdot) \subset(t+\varepsilon,t+2\varepsilon) \subset(0,T)$ for $t\in J_\varepsilon$, the balance laws \eqref{eq/ mom} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ mol cont}
\forall t\in J_\varepsilon, \quad \partial_t\rho_\varepsilon(t) +\partial_xj_{\varepsilon1}(t)=0, \quad \partial_tj_{\varepsilon1}(t)+\partial_xe_{1\varepsilon}(t)=0 \quad\text{in }\mathcal D'(I).
\end{equation}
The temporal smoothing and \eqref{eq/ mol cont} then imply $j_{\varepsilon1},e_{1\varepsilon} \in C^\infty(J_\varepsilon;W^{1,1}(I))$. 

We then define
\[
\mathscr A_\varepsilon(t,x) := \int_{-\frac12}^x\rho_\varepsilon(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y - \left(x+\frac12\right) \int_I\rho_\varepsilon(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y \quad \text{and} \quad \mathscr B_\varepsilon(t) := \int_I\mathscr A_\varepsilon(t,x)j_{\varepsilon1}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

\begin{equation}\label{eq/ A bdy}
\mathscr A_\varepsilon(t,-\frac12) = \mathscr A_\varepsilon(t,\frac12) =0.

On the other hand, because $j_{\varepsilon1}(t)\in W^{1,1}(I)$, the following computations are justified:

\int_I j_{\varepsilon1}(t,x)\partial_x\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x =\int_I\partial_t\rho_\varepsilon(t,x)\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x +j_{\varepsilon1}(t,\frac12)\psi(\frac12)-j_{\varepsilon1}(t,-\frac12)\psi(-\frac12),

\int_0^t\int_I (\rho_\varepsilon e_{1\varepsilon}-j_{\varepsilon1}^2 )\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s &= \mathscr B_\varepsilon(t)-\mathscr B_\varepsilon(0) - \int_0^t j_{\varepsilon1}(s,-\frac12) \left(\int_Ij_{\varepsilon1}(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right) \mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad- \int_0^t \left( j_{\varepsilon1}(s,\frac12)-j_{\varepsilon1}(s,-\frac12)  \right) \left( \int_I \left(x+\frac12\right)j_{\varepsilon1}(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}x \right)\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad+ \int_0^t m_\varepsilon(s) \left(\int_Ie_{1\varepsilon}(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right) \mathrm{d}s.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

It remains to control the boundary values of $j_{\varepsilon1}$. Convolving the Green formula in time and using the absolutely continuous representative of $j_{\varepsilon1}(t)$, we have
\[
j_{\varepsilon1}(t,-\frac12) = \int_{\mathbb R^3}v_1f_{l\varepsilon}(t,v)\,\textnormal{d}v, \quad j_{\varepsilon1}(t,\frac12) = \int_{\mathbb R^3}v_1f_{r\varepsilon}(t,v)\,\textnormal{d}v,
\]
where $f_{l\varepsilon}:=f_l*_t\zeta_\varepsilon$, $f_{r\varepsilon}:=f_r*_t\zeta_\varepsilon$. Since convolution with $\zeta_\varepsilon$ is $L^1$-contractive in time, \eqref{eq/ tr wt} yields
\[
\int_0^{T-2\varepsilon} \left( |j_{\varepsilon1}(s,-\frac12)| +|j_{\varepsilon1}(s,\frac12)|  \right) \mathrm{d}s \le C_T.
\]
The bulk moment bounds also give
\[
\sup_{0<\varepsilon<\min\{1,\frac T2\}} \left( \|m_\varepsilon\|_{L^\infty(J_\varepsilon)} + \|j_{\varepsilon1}\|_{L^\infty(J_\varepsilon;L^1(I))} + \|e_{1\varepsilon}\|_{L^\infty(J_\varepsilon;L^1(I))} \right) \le C_T.
\]
Applying these estimates to \eqref{eq/ B in} yields
\[
\int_0^t\int_I (\rho_\varepsilon e_{1\varepsilon}-j_{\varepsilon1}^2 )\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s \le \mathscr B_\varepsilon(t)-\mathscr B_\varepsilon(0)+C_T.
\]
The convergence argument used in the Maxwell case now gives \eqref{eq/ B prelim} for \textbf{(In)}.

Finally, from \eqref{eq/ scra}, $|\mathscr A(t,x)| \le \|\rho(t)\|_{L^1(I)}$, and thus
\[
|\mathscr B(t)| \le \|\rho(t)\|_{L^1(I)} \|j_1(t)\|_{L^1(I)} \le C_T.
\]
Letting $t\uparrow T$ in \eqref{eq/ B prelim}, using the continuity of $\mathscr B$ and the monotone convergence of the nonnegative integral on the left-hand side, proves \eqref{eq/ useful}. This completes the proof.

Write $\overline v:=(v_2,v_3)$, $\overline u:=(u_2,u_3)$, and introduce the transverse pressure
\[
\overline P := \int_{\mathbb R^3} |\overline v-\overline u|^2f\,\textnormal{d}v.
\]
The definition of the temperature gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ tr press}
3\rho\theta = \int_{\mathbb R^3}|v-u|^2f\,\textnormal{d}v = \overline P + \int_{\mathbb R^3}(v_1-u_1)^2f\,\textnormal{d}v = \overline P + \frac{\rho e_1-j_1^2}{\rho}.
\end{equation}
All quotients are interpreted as zero on the vacuum set.

Since the local Maxwellian is isotropic around $u$, we also have
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^3} |\overline v|^2(M[f]-f )\,\textnormal{d}v = 2\rho\theta-\overline P.
\]
Combining this identity with \eqref{eq/ tr press} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ tr Q}
\int_{\mathbb R^3} |\overline v|^2(M[f]-f )\,\textnormal{d}v = \frac{\rho e_1-j_1^2}{\rho} -\rho\theta.
\end{equation}

Set
\[
\overline E(t) := \iint_{\mathcal O}|\overline v|^2f(t,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \quad \text{and} \quad \overline F(t) := \int_{\mathbb R^3} |\overline v|^2v_1 (f_r(t,v)-f_l(t,v) )\,\textnormal{d}v.
\]
We remark that \eqref{eq/ tr wt} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ tr flux}
\int_0^T|\overline F(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t \le C_T,
\end{equation}
with a constant independent of $\|\lambda\|_{L^\infty}$; moreover, the bound \eqref{eq/ Q wt} shows that
\begin{equation*}
    \int_0^T \iint_{\mathcal O} \lambda(\rho) |\overline v|^2 |M[f] - f| \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t \le \|\lambda\|_{L^\infty} C_T.
\end{equation*}
These show that, after an approximation procedure, $|\overline v|^2$ is admissible as a test function in the Green formula of \eqref{eq/ lambda}. In particular, it is deduced that $\overline E(t)$ is absolutely continuous on $[0,T]$ with the identity

\begin{equation}\label{eq/ tr E}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\overline E(t) &= - \overline F(t) + \iint_{\mathcal O} \lambda(\rho) |\overline v|^2 (M[f] - f) \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v  \\
&= - \overline F(t) + \int_I \lambda(\rho) \left( \frac{\rho e_1-j_1^2}{\rho} -\rho\theta \right) \mathrm{d}x,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation} 
where the last equality is due to \eqref{eq/ tr Q}. Consequently, we find
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ lambda rho}
\int_0^T\int_I \lambda(\rho)\rho\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t = \int_0^T\int_I \frac{\lambda(\rho)}{\rho} (\rho e_1-j_1^2 )\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t + \overline E(0)-\overline E(T) -\int_0^T\overline F(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.
\end{equation}
Since $\lambda(\rho)\le\rho$ and $\rho e_1-j_1^2\ge0$,
\[
\frac{\lambda(\rho)}{\rho} (\rho e_1-j_1^2 ) \le \rho e_1-j_1^2.
\]
Using also $\overline E(T)\ge0$, \eqref{eq/ tr flux}, and Proposition \ref{prop/ bony}, we conclude from \eqref{eq/ lambda rho} that
\[
\int_0^T\int_I \lambda(\rho)\rho\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t \le \int_0^T\int_I (\rho e_1-j_1^2 )\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t +C_T \le C_T.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq/ therm} and completes the proof.

\label{lem/ every}
Let $f_\varepsilon$ be the entropy-admissible solution of \eqref{eq/ regbgk} corresponding to $f_{\varepsilon0}$. Then, for every $T>0$, there exists $C_T>0$, independent of $\varepsilon$, such that the following estimates hold.
\begin{enumerate}

\item[(i)] The bulk energy and entropy satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ bdd}
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\, \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{0<t<T} \iint_{\mathcal O} f_\varepsilon(\langle v\rangle^2+|\log f_\varepsilon| ) \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \le C_T.
\end{equation}

\item[(ii)] The entropy production satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ reg diss}
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\int_0^T\iint_{\mathcal O} &\lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_{f_\varepsilon}) (f_\varepsilon-M[f_\varepsilon] ) (\log f_\varepsilon-\log M[f_\varepsilon] ) \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t \le C_T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\item[(iii)] In case \textbf{(In)}, the outgoing traces satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ out bdd}
\begin{aligned}
&\sup_{\varepsilon>0} \int_0^T\int_{v_1<0} f_{\varepsilon l} (\langle v\rangle^2+|\log f_{\varepsilon l}| ) |v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t \le C_T,\\
&\sup_{\varepsilon>0} \int_0^T\int_{v_1>0} f_{\varepsilon r} (\langle v\rangle^2+|\log f_{\varepsilon r}| ) v_1\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t \le C_T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\item[(iv)] In case \textbf{(Max)}, define
\[
J_{\varepsilon l}(t) := \int_{v_1<0}f_{\varepsilon l}(t,v)|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v, \quad J_{\varepsilon r}(t) := \int_{v_1>0}f_{\varepsilon r}(t,v)v_1\,\textnormal{d}v,
\]
and let $h(z)=z\log z$. Define the Darroz\`es--Guiraud information terms by
\[
\mathscr E_{\varepsilon l}(t) := \int_{v_1<0} h(\frac{f_{\varepsilon l}}{\mathfrak M})\mathfrak M|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v -h(J_{\varepsilon l}(t)),\quad \mathscr E_{\varepsilon r}(t) := \int_{v_1>0} h(\frac{f_{\varepsilon r}}{\mathfrak M})\mathfrak M v_1\,\textnormal{d}v -h(J_{\varepsilon r}(t)).
\] 
When the corresponding outgoing flux vanishes, the information is defined to be zero. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ darr bdd}
\int_0^T ( \alpha_l\mathscr E_{\varepsilon l}(t) +\alpha_r\mathscr E_{\varepsilon r}(t)  )\,\mathrm{d}t \le C_T.
\end{equation}

\end{enumerate}

We first consider the inflow boundary condition \textbf{(In)}.

By the collision-invariant identities in \eqref{eq/ M mom}, the collision operator does not contribute to the mass or energy balance. Testing the equation with a standard radial truncation of $\langle v\rangle^2=1+|v|^2$ and passing to the limit in the truncation parameter, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ E diff}
\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\iint_{\mathcal O} f_\varepsilon\langle v\rangle^2\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v = -\int_{\mathbb R^3} \langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon r}v_1\,\textnormal{d}v +\int_{\mathbb R^3} \langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon l}v_1\,\textnormal{d}v.
\end{equation}
Using the inflow boundary condition, we decompose the two boundary terms as
\[
\begin{aligned}
-\int_{\mathbb R^3} \langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon r}v_1\,\textnormal{d}v &= \int_{v_1<0} \langle v\rangle^2g_r|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v - \int_{v_1>0} \langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon r}v_1\,\textnormal{d}v,\\
\int_{\mathbb R^3} \langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon l}v_1\,\textnormal{d}v &= \int_{v_1>0} \langle v\rangle^2g_lv_1\,\textnormal{d}v - \int_{v_1<0} \langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon l}|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v.
\end{aligned}
\]
Integrating \eqref{eq/ E diff} over $(0,t)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ E in}
\begin{aligned}
&\iint_{\mathcal O} f_\varepsilon(t)\langle v\rangle^2\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v +\int_0^t\int_{v_1>0} \langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon r}v_1\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s +\int_0^t\int_{v_1<0} \langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon l}|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s \\
&\quad= \iint_{\mathcal O} f_{\varepsilon0}\langle v\rangle^2\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v +\int_0^t\int_{v_1>0} \langle v\rangle^2g_lv_1\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s +\int_0^t\int_{v_1<0} \langle v\rangle^2g_r|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is bounded uniformly in $\varepsilon$ by \eqref{eq/ init bdd} and \eqref{eq/ in data}. In particular, \eqref{eq/ E in} yields both the uniform bulk second-moment bound and the outgoing energy-flux bound.

We next consider the entropy. Formally multiplying \eqref{eq/ regbgk} by $1+\log f_\varepsilon$, we use
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^3}(M[f_\varepsilon]-f_\varepsilon)\,\textnormal{d}v=0 \quad \text{and} \quad \int_{\mathbb R^3} \log M[f_\varepsilon]\, (M[f_\varepsilon]-f_\varepsilon)\,\textnormal{d}v=0,
\]
where the latter follows since $\log M[f_\varepsilon]$ is a linear combination of $1$, $v$, and $|v|^2$. Hence
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^3} (1+\log f_\varepsilon) \lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_{f_\varepsilon}) (M[f_\varepsilon]-f_\varepsilon)\,\textnormal{d}v = -\int_{\mathbb R^3} \lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_{f_\varepsilon}) (f_\varepsilon-M[f_\varepsilon] ) (\log f_\varepsilon-\log M[f_\varepsilon] )\,\textnormal{d}v.
\]
The entropy-admissibility of the approximations then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ H in}
\begin{aligned}
&\iint_{\mathcal O} f_\varepsilon(t)\log f_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v +\int_0^t\int_{v_1>0} f_{\varepsilon r}\log f_{\varepsilon r}\,v_1\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s +\int_0^t\int_{v_1<0} f_{\varepsilon l}\log f_{\varepsilon l}\,|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s \\
&\quad+ \int_0^t\iint_{\mathcal O} \lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_{f_\varepsilon}) (f_\varepsilon-M[f_\varepsilon] ) (\log f_\varepsilon-\log M[f_\varepsilon] ) \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s \\
&\quad \quad\le \iint_{\mathcal O} f_{\varepsilon0}\log f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v +\int_0^t\int_{v_1>0} g_l\log g_l\,v_1\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s +\int_0^t\int_{v_1<0} g_r\log g_r\,|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

To control the negative parts of the entropy, let $\log^-z:=\max\{0,-\log z\}$. For every $a>0$ and $z\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ H neg}
z \log^-z \le \frac{a}{2}\langle v\rangle^2 z +e^{-1} e^{-\frac a2 \langle v\rangle^2}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, this follows by applying $r\log^-r\le e^{-1}$ to $r=z e^{\frac a2\langle v\rangle^2}$. Since $\int_{\mathbb R^3} e^{-\frac a2\langle v\rangle^2}(1 + |v_1|)\,\textnormal{d}v<\infty$,   the bulk and outgoing negative entropy terms are controlled by \eqref{eq/ E in}. The incoming negative entropy terms are controlled in the same way using \eqref{eq/ in data}. Thus \eqref{eq/ H in}, together with the assumptions on $f_{\varepsilon0}$ and the inflow data, yields
\begin{align*}
&\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{0<t<T} \iint_{\mathcal O} f_\varepsilon(t)|\log f_\varepsilon(t)|\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v + \int_0^T\int_{v_1>0} f_{\varepsilon r}|\log f_{\varepsilon r}|v_1\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t + \int_0^T\int_{v_1<0} f_{\varepsilon l}|\log f_{\varepsilon l}||v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad+ \int_0^T\iint_{\mathcal O} \lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_{f_\varepsilon}) (f_\varepsilon-M[f_\varepsilon] ) (\log f_\varepsilon-\log M[f_\varepsilon] ) \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t \le C_T.
\end{align*}
Together with \eqref{eq/ E in}, this proves \eqref{eq/ bdd}, \eqref{eq/ reg diss}, and \eqref{eq/ out bdd} in case \textbf{(In)}.

We now turn to the Maxwell boundary condition \textbf{(Max)}. Let $h(z):=z\log z$ and introduce the relative entropy with respect to the fixed boundary Maxwellian $\mathfrak M$,
\[
\mathscr H_{\mathfrak M}[f_\varepsilon] := \iint_{\mathcal O} h(\frac{f_\varepsilon}{\mathfrak M})\mathfrak M\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v.
\]
At the formal level, multiplication of \eqref{eq/ regbgk} by $h'(\frac{f_\varepsilon}{\mathfrak M})$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ test hprime}
\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\mathscr H_{\mathfrak M}[f_\varepsilon] = -\int_{\mathbb R^3} h(\frac{f_{\varepsilon r}}{\mathfrak M})\mathfrak M v_1\,\textnormal{d}v +\int_{\mathbb R^3} h(\frac{f_{\varepsilon l}}{\mathfrak M})\mathfrak M v_1\,\textnormal{d}v + \iint_{\mathcal O} h'(\frac{f_\varepsilon}{\mathfrak M}) \lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_{f_\varepsilon}) (M[f_\varepsilon]-f_\varepsilon ) \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v.
\end{equation}
The entropy-admissibility assumption means that the integrated form of the resulting inequality is valid for the approximate solutions.

We first identify the bulk dissipation term. Since $h'(z)=1+\log z$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ H diss}
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R^3} h'(\frac{f_\varepsilon}{\mathfrak M}) (M[f_\varepsilon]-f_\varepsilon )\,\textnormal{d}v &= \int_{\mathbb R^3} \left( 1+\log f_\varepsilon-\log\mathfrak M \right) (M[f_\varepsilon]-f_\varepsilon )\,\textnormal{d}v  \\
&= \int_{\mathbb R^3} \log f_\varepsilon (M[f_\varepsilon]-f_\varepsilon )\,\textnormal{d}v \\
&= \int_{\mathbb R^3} ( \log f_\varepsilon-\log M[f_\varepsilon]  ) (M[f_\varepsilon]-f_\varepsilon )\,\textnormal{d}v. 
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Both cancellations follow from \eqref{eq/ M mom}, since $\log\mathfrak M$ and $\log M[f_\varepsilon]$ are linear combinations of the collision invariants.

We next estimate the boundary entropy flux. We give the details at $x=-\frac12$; the computation at $x=\frac12$ is analogous. We use the Darroz\`es--Guiraud argument for the Maxwell boundary condition; see \cite[Lemma 2.2]{Mis10}. Since $\mathfrak M$ is centered, $\mathfrak M(\mathscr V v)=\mathfrak M(v)$, and the normalization \eqref{eq/ M norm} gives
\[
\int_{v_1>0}\mathfrak M(v)v_1\,\textnormal{d}v = \int_{v_1<0}\mathfrak M(v)|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v =1.
\]
Using the Maxwell boundary condition for $v_1>0$, we write
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ darr first}
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R^3} h\left(\frac{f_{\varepsilon l}}{\mathfrak M}\right) \mathfrak M v_1\,\textnormal{d}v &= \int_{v_1>0} h\left( \frac{ (1-\alpha_l)f_{\varepsilon l}(\mathscr V v) +\alpha_l\mathfrak M(v)J_{\varepsilon l}(t) }{\mathfrak M(v)} \right) \mathfrak M(v)v_1\,\textnormal{d}v  \\
&\quad+ \int_{v_1<0} h\left(\frac{f_{\varepsilon l}}{\mathfrak M}\right) \mathfrak M v_1\,\textnormal{d}v. 
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since $h$ is convex, we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
h\left( (1-\alpha_l) \frac{f_{\varepsilon l}(\mathscr V v)}{\mathfrak M(v)} +\alpha_lJ_{\varepsilon l}(t) \right) &\le (1-\alpha_l) h\left( \frac{f_{\varepsilon l}(\mathscr V v)}{\mathfrak M(v)} \right) + \alpha_lh(J_{\varepsilon l}(t)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Substituting this into \eqref{eq/ darr first} yields
\begin{align*}
\int_{\mathbb R^3} h\left(\frac{f_{\varepsilon l}}{\mathfrak M}\right) \mathfrak M v_1\,\textnormal{d}v &\le (1-\alpha_l) \int_{v_1>0} h\left( \frac{f_{\varepsilon l}(\mathscr V v)}{\mathfrak M(v)} \right) \mathfrak M(v)v_1\,\textnormal{d}v\\
&\quad+ \alpha_lh(J_{\varepsilon l}(t)) \int_{v_1>0}\mathfrak M(v)v_1\,\textnormal{d}v + \int_{v_1<0} h\left(\frac{f_{\varepsilon l}}{\mathfrak M}\right) \mathfrak M v_1\,\textnormal{d}v.
\end{align*}
Under the change of variables $v\mapsto\mathscr V v$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_{v_1>0} h\left( \frac{f_{\varepsilon l}(\mathscr V v)}{\mathfrak M(v)} \right) \mathfrak M(v)v_1\,\textnormal{d}v = \int_{v_1<0} h\left( \frac{f_{\varepsilon l}(v)}{\mathfrak M(v)} \right) \mathfrak M(v)|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v.
\end{aligned}
\]
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ darr}
\int_{\mathbb R^3} h\left(\frac{f_{\varepsilon l}}{\mathfrak M}\right) \mathfrak M v_1\,\textnormal{d}v \le -\alpha_l \left[ \int_{v_1<0} h\left(\frac{f_{\varepsilon l}}{\mathfrak M}\right) \mathfrak M|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v - h(J_{\varepsilon l}(t)) \right] = -\alpha_l\mathscr E_{\varepsilon l}(t).
\end{equation}

Let us also verify explicitly that $\mathscr E_{\varepsilon l}(t)\ge0$. If $J_{\varepsilon l}(t)>0$, then
\[
\mathscr E_{\varepsilon l}(t) = \int_{v_1<0} f_{\varepsilon l} \log\frac{f_{\varepsilon l}} {J_{\varepsilon l}(t)\mathfrak M} |v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v = J_{\varepsilon l}(t) \int_{v_1<0} \frac{f_{\varepsilon l}} {J_{\varepsilon l}(t)\mathfrak M} \log \frac{f_{\varepsilon l}} {J_{\varepsilon l}(t)\mathfrak M} \mathfrak M|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v.
\]
Since $\mathfrak M|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v$ is a probability measure on $\{v_1<0\}$ and
\[
\int_{v_1<0} \frac{f_{\varepsilon l}} {J_{\varepsilon l}(t)\mathfrak M} \mathfrak M|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v =1,
\]
Jensen's inequality gives $\mathscr E_{\varepsilon l}(t) \ge J_{\varepsilon l}(t)h(1) =0$. When $J_{\varepsilon l}(t)=0$, we set $\mathscr E_{\varepsilon l}(t)=0$. The same argument at $x=\frac12$ gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^3} h(\frac{f_{\varepsilon r}}{\mathfrak M})\mathfrak M v_1\,\textnormal{d}v \ge \alpha_r\mathscr E_{\varepsilon r}(t) \ge0.
\]
Notice that the sign is reversed at the right endpoint due to the orientation of the boundary flux.

Combining \eqref{eq/ test hprime}, \eqref{eq/ H diss}, and \eqref{eq/ darr}, together with the analogous estimate at $x=\frac12$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ H max}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathscr H_{\mathfrak M}[f_\varepsilon(t)] +\int_0^t ( \alpha_l\mathscr E_{\varepsilon l}(s) +\alpha_r\mathscr E_{\varepsilon r}(s)  )\,\mathrm{d}s \\
&\quad + \int_0^t\iint_{\mathcal O} \lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_{f_\varepsilon}) (f_\varepsilon-M[f_\varepsilon] ) (\log f_\varepsilon-\log M[f_\varepsilon] ) \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}s \\
&\quad \quad\le \mathscr H_{\mathfrak M}[f_{\varepsilon0}].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We now extract the second-moment estimate from the relative entropy. Write
\[
\mathfrak M(v)=A e^{-b|v|^2}, \quad A,b>0.
\]
For $0<a<b$, Young's inequality $z\log z\ge yz-e^{y-1}$ for $z\ge0$, $y\in\mathbb R$, applied with $z=\frac{f_\varepsilon}{\mathfrak M}$, $y=a|v|^2$, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
h(\frac{f_\varepsilon}{\mathfrak M})\mathfrak M \ge a|v|^2f_\varepsilon - e^{-1}\mathfrak M e^{a|v|^2} = a|v|^2f_\varepsilon - A e^{-1}e^{-(b-a)|v|^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ H coerc}
\mathscr H_{\mathfrak M}[f_\varepsilon(t)] \ge a\iint_{\mathcal O}|v|^2f_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v -C_{\mathfrak M,a},
\end{equation}
where $C_{\mathfrak M,a}<\infty$ is independent of $\varepsilon$ and $t$.

The initial relative entropy is uniformly bounded from above. Indeed, since $\log\mathfrak M(v)=\log A-b|v|^2$, we have
\[
\mathscr H_{\mathfrak M}[f_{\varepsilon0}] = \iint_{\mathcal O} f_{\varepsilon0}\log f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v -(\log A) \iint_{\mathcal O}f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v + b\iint_{\mathcal O}|v|^2f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v,
\]
which is bounded uniformly in $\varepsilon$ by the assumptions on the approximate initial data. The Maxwell boundary condition preserves the total mass, and hence
\[
\iint_{\mathcal O}f_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v = \iint_{\mathcal O}f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v
\]
for almost every $t>0$. In particular, the mass is bounded uniformly in $\varepsilon$. Combining this fact with \eqref{eq/ H max} and \eqref{eq/ H coerc} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ E max}
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\, \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{0<t<T} \iint_{\mathcal O} \langle v\rangle^2f_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \le C_T.
\end{equation}

We next recover the absolute entropy. Since
\[
\mathscr H_{\mathfrak M}[f_\varepsilon] = \iint_{\mathcal O}f_\varepsilon\log f_\varepsilon\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v - \iint_{\mathcal O}f_\varepsilon\log\mathfrak M\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \quad \text{and} \quad \log\mathfrak M=\log A-b|v|^2,
\]
the upper bound furnished by \eqref{eq/ H max}, together with mass conservation, gives a uniform upper bound on the signed entropy
\[
\iint_{\mathcal O}f_\varepsilon\log f_\varepsilon\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v.
\]
On the other hand, \eqref{eq/ H neg} and \eqref{eq/ E max} yield
\[
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\, \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{0<t<T} \iint_{\mathcal O} f_\varepsilon(t)\log^-f_\varepsilon(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \le C_T.
\]
It follows that
\[

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem/ dlVP}
Let $(f_{\varepsilon0})$ be a nonnegative sequence satisfying \eqref{eq/ init E}. Then there exists $\Phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}

\item[(i)] $\Phi\in C^\infty([0,\infty))$ and $\Phi(0)=\Phi'(0)=0$. 

\item[(ii)] $\Phi$ is convex and $\Phi'$ is increasing and concave. Moreover, for every $z>0$,
\begin{align}
&\frac{\Phi(z)}{z} \le \Phi'(z) \le 2\frac{\Phi(z)}{z}, \label{eq/ Phi p}\\
&0\le \Phi''(z) \le \min\left\{ \frac{\Phi'(z)}{z}, \frac{C}{z} \right\} \quad\text{for some }C>0, \label{eq/ Phi pp}\\
&\lim_{z\to\infty}\frac{\Phi(z)}{z} =\infty. \label{eq/ Phi sup}
\end{align}

\item[(iii)] The initial data satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ init tight}
\sup_{\varepsilon>0} \iint_{\mathcal O} \Phi(\langle v\rangle^2)f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \le1.
\end{equation}

\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Since \eqref{eq/ init E} assumes strong convergence in $L^1(\mathcal O,\langle v\rangle^2\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v)$, the corresponding energy tails are uniformly small. More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ init tail}
\lim_{R\to\infty} \sup_{\varepsilon>0} \iint_{I\times\{|v|>R\}} \langle v\rangle^2 f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v =0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, let $\eta>0$. By \eqref{eq/ init E}, for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ along the fixed sequence,
\[
\iint_{\mathcal O} \langle v\rangle^2|f_{\varepsilon0}-f_0|\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v <\frac{\eta}{2}.
\]
Since $\langle v\rangle^2f_0\in L^1(\mathcal O)$, we may choose $R>0$ large enough that
\[
\iint_{I\times\{|v|>R\}} \langle v\rangle^2f_0\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v <\frac{\eta}{2}.
\]
Hence, for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ along the fixed sequence,
\[
\iint_{I\times\{|v|>R\}} \langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v <\eta.
\]
The finitely many remaining elements are treated by increasing $R$ once more. This proves \eqref{eq/ init tail}.

Set
\[
M_0:= \sup_{\varepsilon>0} \iint_{\mathcal O}\langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v<\infty.
\]
We construct $\Phi'$ as a series. For $k\ge1$, let
\[
\phi_k(z):=\frac{z}{a_k+z},
\]
where the positive sequence $(a_k)$ will be chosen recursively. Using \eqref{eq/ init tail}, choose $R_k$ large enough that
\[
\sup_{\varepsilon>0} \iint_{I\times\{|v|>R_k\}} \langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v <\frac1{2^{k+1}}.
\]
We then choose $a_k$ sufficiently large such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ doubling}
a_1\ge2,\quad a_{k+1}\ge2a_k, \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{\langle R_k\rangle^2}{a_k}M_0<\frac1{2^{k+1}}.
\end{equation}
This yields, for every $\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ phik}
\iint_{\mathcal O} \langle v\rangle^2 \phi_k(\langle v\rangle^2) f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \le \frac{\langle R_k\rangle^2}{a_k} \iint_{\mathcal O}\langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v + \iint_{I\times\{|v|>R_k\}} \langle v\rangle^2f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v <\frac1{2^{k}}.
\end{equation}

Define
\[
\Phi'(z):=\sum_{k\ge1}\phi_k(z), \quad \Phi(z):=\int_0^z\Phi'(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.
\]
The doubling condition on $(a_k)$ guarantees convergence of this series and all its derivatives uniformly on compact subsets of $[0,\infty)$. Indeed, for $m\ge1$, $|\phi_k^{(m)}(z)| \le \frac{m!}{a_k^m}$, while on every bounded interval $0\le z\le L$, $0\le\phi_k(z)\le\frac{L}{a_k}$. Hence $\Phi\in C^\infty([0,\infty))$.

Since each $\phi_k$ is increasing and concave, $\Phi'$ has the same properties. Also, for any fixed $N$,
\[
\liminf_{z\to\infty}\Phi'(z) \ge \lim_{z\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^N\phi_k(z) =N,
\]
and hence $\Phi'(z)\xrightarrow[z\to\infty]{}\infty$. Together with the comparison between $\frac{\Phi(z)}z$ and $\Phi'(z)$ proved below, this gives the superlinearity needed for velocity tightness.

By monotone convergence and \eqref{eq/ phik}, we estimate
\[
\iint_{\mathcal O} \Phi(\langle v\rangle^2)f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \le \iint_{\mathcal O} \langle v\rangle^2\Phi'(\langle v\rangle^2) f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v = \sum_{k\ge1} \iint_{\mathcal O} \langle v\rangle^2\phi_k(\langle v\rangle^2) f_{\varepsilon0}\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \le1.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq/ init tight}.

The monotonicity of $\Phi'$ and the identity $\Phi(0)=0$ give $\Phi(z)\le z\Phi'(z)$. Since $\Phi'$ is concave and $\Phi'(0)=0$,
\[
\frac{\Phi'(s)}{s} \ge \frac{\Phi'(z)}{z}, \quad 0<s<z.
\]
Consequently, we obtain
\[
\Phi(z) = \int_0^z\Phi'(s)\,\mathrm{d}s \ge \frac{\Phi'(z)}{z} \int_0^z s\,\mathrm{d}s = \frac12z\Phi'(z),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq/ Phi p}. In particular, \eqref{eq/ Phi p} and $\Phi'(z)\to\infty$ imply \eqref{eq/ Phi sup}.

It remains to prove the second estimate in \eqref{eq/ Phi pp}. Since
\[
\Phi''(z) = \sum_{k\ge1} \frac{a_k}{(a_k+z)^2},
\]
we split
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ split sum}
\Phi''(z) \le \frac1{z^2} \sum_{\{k:a_k\le z\}}a_k + \sum_{\{k:a_k>z\}}\frac1{a_k}.
\end{equation}
If $0<z<a_1$, the first sum is empty and
\[
\Phi''(z) \le \sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{a_k} \le \frac2{a_1} \le \frac2z.
\]
If $z\ge a_1$, let $k_*:=\max\{k:a_k\le z\}$. The doubling property (recalling \eqref{eq/ doubling}) gives
\[
\sum_{k\le k_*}a_k\le2z, \quad \sum_{k>k_*}\frac1{a_k}\le\frac2z.
\]
Inserting these bounds into \eqref{eq/ split sum} yields $\Phi''(z)\le\frac4z$. Finally, the concavity of $\Phi'$ and $\Phi'(0)=0$ imply
\[
\Phi''(z)\le\frac{\Phi'(z)}{z}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq/ Phi pp}. This completes the proof.

\label{prop/ super}

We first perform the computation formally with $\Phi(\langle v\rangle^2)$ as a test function. The justification by truncation is given at the end of the proof. We obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ direct}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t} \iint_{\mathcal O} \Phi(\langle v\rangle^2)f_\varepsilon\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v &= \iint_{\mathcal O} \lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_{f_\varepsilon}) \Phi(\langle v\rangle^2) (M[f_\varepsilon]-f_\varepsilon ) \,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\\

\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\medskip \noindent $\bullet$ (The bulk collision term) It suffices to consider $\rho_{f_\varepsilon}>0$, since the estimate is immediate on the vacuum set. Set
\[
X_\varepsilon := \int_{\mathbb R^3} \langle v\rangle^2 \frac{M[f_\varepsilon]}{\rho_{f_\varepsilon}}\,\textnormal{d}v = 1+|u_{f_\varepsilon}|^2+3\theta_{f_\varepsilon}.
\]
By Jensen's inequality, applied to the probability measure $\frac{f_\varepsilon}{\rho_{f_\varepsilon}}\,\textnormal{d}v$,
\[
\frac1{\rho_{f_\varepsilon}} \int_{\mathbb R^3} \Phi(\langle v\rangle^2)f_\varepsilon\,\textnormal{d}v \ge \Phi\left( \frac1{\rho_{f_\varepsilon}} \int_{\mathbb R^3}\langle v\rangle^2f_\varepsilon\,\textnormal{d}v \right) = \Phi(X_\varepsilon),
\]
where the last identity follows from the definitions of $u_{f_\varepsilon}$ and $\theta_{f_\varepsilon}$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ inter}
\int_{\mathbb R^3} \Phi(\langle v\rangle^2) (M[f_\varepsilon]-f_\varepsilon )\,\textnormal{d}v \le \rho_{f_\varepsilon} \left[ \int_{\mathbb R^3} \Phi(\langle v\rangle^2) \frac{M[f_\varepsilon]}{\rho_{f_\varepsilon}}\,\textnormal{d}v - \Phi(X_\varepsilon) \right]. 
\end{equation}

Let $h(z):=z\log z$. By \eqref{eq/ Phi pp}, after enlarging the constant $C$ if necessary,
\[
\Phi''(z)\le\frac{C}{z}=Ch''(z),
\]
and hence $\Phi-Ch$ is concave. Thus, for $y,X>0$,
\[
\Phi(y)-\Phi(X)-\Phi'(X)(y-X) \le C\left( h(y)-h(X)-h'(X)(y-X) \right).
\]
Writing $r=\frac yX$, we compute
\[
h(y)-h(X)-h'(X)(y-X) = X(r\log r-r+1 ).
\]
Since $\log r\le r-1$,
\[
r\log r-r+1 \le r(r-1)-r+1 = (r-1)^2,
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ log gap}
h(y)-h(X)-h'(X)(y-X) \le \frac{(y-X)^2}{X}.
\end{equation}

Applying \eqref{eq/ log gap} with $y=\langle v\rangle^2$ and $X=X_\varepsilon$, and using
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^3} (\langle v\rangle^2-X_\varepsilon) \frac{M[f_\varepsilon]}{\rho_{f_\varepsilon}}\,\textnormal{d}v =0,
\]
we obtain
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^3} \Phi(\langle v\rangle^2) \frac{M[f_\varepsilon]}{\rho_{f_\varepsilon}}\,\textnormal{d}v -\Phi(X_\varepsilon) \le \frac{C}{X_\varepsilon} \int_{\mathbb R^3} (\langle v\rangle^2-X_\varepsilon)^2 \frac{M[f_\varepsilon]}{\rho_{f_\varepsilon}}\,\textnormal{d}v.

To compute the last integral, we use the change of variables $w=v-u_{f_\varepsilon}$. Since $X_\varepsilon = 1+|u_{f_\varepsilon}|^2+3\theta_{f_\varepsilon}$, we have
\[

Expanding the square, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\int_{\mathbb R^3} (\langle v\rangle^2-X_\varepsilon)^2M[f_\varepsilon]\,\textnormal{d}v &= 4\int_{\mathbb R^3} (u_{f_\varepsilon}\cdot w)^2M[f_\varepsilon]\,\textnormal{d}v + \int_{\mathbb R^3} (|w|^2-3\theta_{f_\varepsilon} )^2 M[f_\varepsilon]\,\textnormal{d}v\\

\end{align*}
The last integral vanishes by oddness. For the first term, the standard Gaussian identities

For the second term, we use

\[

\end{align*}

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem/ max L1}
Along a further subsequence, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ ae}
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{f_\varepsilon} &\longrightarrow \rho_f &&\text{a.e. in }(0,T)\times I,\\
\lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_{f_\varepsilon}) &\longrightarrow \rho_f &&\text{a.e. in }(0,T)\times I,\\
u_{f_\varepsilon} &\longrightarrow  u_f &&\text{a.e. on }\{\rho_f>0\},\\
\theta_{f_\varepsilon} &\longrightarrow \theta_f &&\text{a.e. on }\{\rho_f>0\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{align}
\lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_{f_\varepsilon}) &\longrightarrow \rho_f \quad \text{strongly in }L^1((0,T)\times I), \label{eq/ rate str}\\
M[f_\varepsilon] &\longrightarrow  M[f] \quad \text{strongly in } L^1((0,T)\times\mathcal O, \langle v\rangle^2\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v). \label{eq/ M str}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Set
\[
\rho_\varepsilon:=\rho_{f_\varepsilon}, \quad j_\varepsilon:=\int_{\mathbb R^3}vf_\varepsilon\,\textnormal{d}v, \quad e_\varepsilon:=\int_{\mathbb R^3}|v|^2f_\varepsilon\,\textnormal{d}v,
\]
and define $\rho$, $j$, and $e$ analogously from $f$.

The convergence \eqref{eq/ avg}, combined with the second-moment bounds, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ rho j strong}
\rho_\varepsilon\to\rho, \quad j_\varepsilon\to j \quad\text{strongly in }L^1((0,T)\times I).
\end{equation}
Indeed, after introducing a radial velocity cutoff, the tails are controlled by
\[
\int_{|v|>R}f_\varepsilon\,\textnormal{d}v \le \frac1{R^2} \int_{\mathbb R^3}|v|^2f_\varepsilon\,\textnormal{d}v, \quad \text{and} \quad \int_{|v|>R}|v|f_\varepsilon\,\textnormal{d}v \le \frac1R \int_{\mathbb R^3}|v|^2f_\varepsilon\,\textnormal{d}v,
\]
and the same estimates hold for $f$. Furthermore, \eqref{eq/ mom2 conv} and the first convergence in \eqref{eq/ rho j strong} yield
\[
e_\varepsilon \to  e \quad\text{strongly in }L^1((0,T)\times I).
\]

After passing to a further subsequence, all three convergences hold almost everywhere. On $\{\rho>0\}$,
\[
u_{f_\varepsilon} = \frac{j_\varepsilon}{\rho_\varepsilon} \longrightarrow  \frac{j}{\rho} = u_f.
\]

To treat the temperature, define
\[
q_\varepsilon :=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{|j_\varepsilon|^2}{\rho_\varepsilon}, &\rho_\varepsilon>0,\\[6pt]
0,&\rho_\varepsilon=0.
\end{cases}
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ q energy}
0\le q_\varepsilon\le e_\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
On $\{\rho>0\}$,
\[
q_\varepsilon \longrightarrow  \frac{|j|^2}{\rho}.
\]
On $\{\rho=0\}$, we have $f=0$ for almost every velocity and hence $e=0$. Since $e_\varepsilon\to e$ almost everywhere after the above extraction, \eqref{eq/ q energy} gives $q_\varepsilon\to0$ there. Thus $q_\varepsilon$ converges almost everywhere to
\[
q:=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{|j|^2}{\rho}, &\rho>0,\\[6pt]
0,&\rho=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Since $(e_\varepsilon)$ is strongly convergent in $L^1$, it is uniformly integrable. The bound \eqref{eq/ q energy} and Vitali's theorem then give
\[
q_\varepsilon \longrightarrow  q \quad\text{strongly in }L^1((0,T)\times I).
\]
Using $3\rho_\varepsilon\theta_{f_\varepsilon} = e_\varepsilon-q_\varepsilon$ and $3\rho\theta_f = e-q$, we obtain
\[
\rho_\varepsilon\theta_{f_\varepsilon} \longrightarrow  \rho\theta_f \quad\text{strongly in }L^1((0,T)\times I).
\]
In particular,
\[
\theta_{f_\varepsilon}\longrightarrow \theta_f \quad\text{a.e. on }\{\rho>0\}.
\]
This proves the macroscopic convergences in \eqref{eq/ ae}.

Since $\lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_\varepsilon) = \frac{\rho_\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon\rho_\varepsilon}$, the almost everywhere convergence $\rho_\varepsilon\to\rho$ gives
\[
\lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_\varepsilon)\longrightarrow \rho \quad\text{a.e. in }(0,T)\times I.
\]
Moreover,
\[
0\le\lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_\varepsilon)\le\rho_\varepsilon.
\]
The strong $L^1$ convergence of $\rho_\varepsilon$ implies uniform integrability, and hence Vitali's theorem gives \eqref{eq/ rate str}.

It remains to prove \eqref{eq/ M str}. Since $f(t,x,\cdot)$ is an integrable function, $\rho>0$ implies $\theta_f>0$: otherwise $f$ would be supported at the single velocity $u_f$. On $\{\rho>0\}$, the almost everywhere convergence of $\rho_\varepsilon$, $u_{f_\varepsilon}$, and $\theta_{f_\varepsilon}$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ novac ae}
M[f_\varepsilon](t,x,v) \longrightarrow  M[f](t,x,v)
\end{equation}
for almost every $(t,x,v)$ with $\rho(t,x)>0$.

Let
\[
\mathcal V_0:=\{(t,x)\in(0,T)\times I:\rho(t,x)=0\}.
\]
Since $M[f]=0$ on $\mathcal V_0\times\mathbb R^3$,
\[
\iiint_{\mathcal V_0\times\mathbb R^3} |M[f_\varepsilon]-M[f]|\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v = \iiint_{\mathcal V_0\times\mathbb R^3} M[f_\varepsilon]\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v = \iint_{\mathcal V_0}\rho_\varepsilon\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x \longrightarrow 0.
\]
After a further extraction,
\[
M[f_\varepsilon]\longrightarrow 0=M[f] \quad\text{a.e. on }\mathcal V_0\times\mathbb R^3.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq/ novac ae}, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ max ae}
M[f_\varepsilon]\longrightarrow  M[f] \quad\text{a.e. in }(0,T)\times\mathcal O.
\end{equation}

Finally, the Maxwellian moment identities and Lemma \ref{lem/ mom2} give
\begin{align*}
\iiint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal O} \langle v\rangle^2M[f_\varepsilon]\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v &= \iiint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal O} \langle v\rangle^2f_\varepsilon\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \\
&\longrightarrow  \iiint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal O} \langle v\rangle^2f\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v = \iiint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal O} \langle v\rangle^2M[f]\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v.
\end{align*}
Applying Scheff\'e's lemma with respect to the measure $\langle v\rangle^2\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v$ and using \eqref{eq/ max ae}, we obtain \eqref{eq/ M str}. This completes the proof.

\label{lem/ Q weak}
For every fixed $\delta=\delta_j$, the family $(Q_{\varepsilon,\delta})_\varepsilon$ defined in \eqref{eq/ Q reg} is weakly compact in $L^1_{\rm loc}([0,T]\times\overline{\mathcal O})$.

On $\{M[f_\varepsilon]>Rf_\varepsilon\}$, we find
\[
\log\frac{M[f_\varepsilon]}{f_\varepsilon}\ge\log R,
\]
and hence

\iiint_{K_L} |Q_{\varepsilon,\delta}| \mathbf 1_{\{M[f_\varepsilon]>Rf_\varepsilon\}} \,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v \le \frac1{\log R} \int_0^T\mathscr D_\varepsilon[f_\varepsilon]\,\mathrm{d}t \le \frac{C_T}{\log R}.

\begin{equation}\label{eq/ Q UI}

\end{equation}

We first choose $R$ large in \eqref{eq/ Q UI}, then $N$ large, and finally $|A|$ small. This proves the local uniform integrability of $(Q_{\varepsilon,\delta})_\varepsilon$, and the Dunford--Pettis theorem gives the claim. This completes the proof.

\[

z\log\frac{z}{\mathfrak M(v)}+\frac{\mathfrak M(v)}e\ge0
\]
is convex in $z$. At the right boundary, put $F_r:=\frac{f_r}{\mathfrak M}$ and recall that $\mathrm d\mu_+=\mathfrak M v_1\mathbf 1_{\{v_1>0\}}\,\textnormal{d}v$. The weak convergence of $F_{\varepsilon r}$ and $J_{\varepsilon r}$ on $\mathcal G_k$, together with the joint convexity of $h_{\rm rel}$ in \eqref{eq/ DG rel}, gives
\[
\int_{\mathcal G_k}\widehat\chi(s)\mathscr E_r(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\mathcal G_k}\int_{\mathbb R^3_+}\widehat\chi(s)h_{\rm rel}(F_r|J_r)\,\mathrm d\mu_+\,\mathrm{d}s \le\liminf_{\varepsilon\to0}\int_{\mathcal G_k}\widehat\chi(s)\mathscr E_{\varepsilon r}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.
\]
The same argument applies at the left boundary. Since the information terms are nonnegative and $\mathcal G_k$ increases to $(0,T)$ up to a null set, monotone convergence yields
\[
\int_0^T\widehat\chi(s)\bigl(\alpha_l\mathscr E_l(s)+\alpha_r\mathscr E_r(s)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s \le\liminf_{\varepsilon\to0}\int_0^T\widehat\chi(s)\bigl(\alpha_l\mathscr E_{\varepsilon l}(s)+\alpha_r\mathscr E_{\varepsilon r}(s)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s.
\]
We may now integrate \eqref{eq/ H max} against $\chi(t)$ and pass to the limit. Combining the bulk, collision, and boundary lower semicontinuity estimates gives
\[
\int_0^T\chi(t)\mathscr H_{\mathfrak M}[f(t)]\,\mathrm{d}t +\int_0^T\widehat\chi(s)\bigl(\mathscr D[f](s)+\alpha_l\mathscr E_l(s)+\alpha_r\mathscr E_r(s)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s\le\mathscr H_{\mathfrak M}[f_0]\int_0^T\chi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.
\]
The terminal time argument used above proves \eqref{eq/ ent max} for every $t\ge0$.

In case \textbf{(In)}, summing \eqref{eq/ bal in} for $\psi=1$ and $\psi=|v|^2$, and discarding the nonnegative outgoing flux, gives a bound on the total mass and energy that is uniform in time, by \eqref{eq/ in data}. In case \textbf{(Max)}, \eqref{eq/ H coerc} and \eqref{eq/ ent max} give
\[
a\iint_{\mathcal O}|v|^2f(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\le\mathscr H_{\mathfrak M}[f(t)]+C_{\mathfrak M,a}\le\mathscr H_{\mathfrak M}[f_0]+C_{\mathfrak M,a},
\]
with $a>0$ chosen as in \eqref{eq/ H coerc}. Together with mass conservation, this proves the asserted global bound.

Finally, Lemma \ref{lem/ max L1} and Fatou's lemma applied to \eqref{eq/ therm reg} give
\[
\int_0^T\int_I\rho_f^2\theta_f\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\le\liminf_{\varepsilon\to0}\int_0^T\int_I\lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_{f_\varepsilon})\rho_{f_\varepsilon}\theta_{f_\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\le C_T,
\]
where $\rho_f^2\theta_f$ is defined to be zero on $\{\rho_f=0\}$. This completes the proof.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The Maxwell boundary condition describes exchange of momentum and energy with the boundary; thus these quantities are not generally conserved. The convergence proved here directly yields mass conservation and the relative entropy inequality \eqref{eq/ ent max}. An identity for the full momentum or energy flux at the Maxwell boundary would additionally require ruling out concentration in time of the unrenormalized boundary fluxes; biting weak convergence alone does not justify such an identity. Similarly, a local energy balance would require control of the third velocity moment in the transport flux, which is not assumed here.

Write $X=L^1(\mathcal O,\langle v\rangle^2\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v)$. We first recall a linear transport estimate. If $h$ solves $\partial_t h+v_1\partial_xh=q$ with zero inflow or with \textbf{(Max)}, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq/ lin tr}
\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|h(t)\|_X+\sum_{b\in\{l,r\}}\int_0^T\int_{\mathbb R^3}\langle v\rangle^2|h_b|\,|v_1|\,\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t\le C_T\left(\|h(0)\|_X+\int_0^T\|q(t)\|_X\,\mathrm{d}t\right).
\end{equation}
For zero inflow this follows from the characteristic formula. For \textbf{(Max)}, the positive linear solution is constructed by iterating the incoming trace, starting with zero incoming trace. The required bound follows by testing with
\[
W(x,v)=\langle v\rangle^2+c_{\mathfrak M}\left(\frac12+x\,\operatorname{sgn}v_1\right),\quad c_{\mathfrak M}=\int_{v_1>0}|v|^2\mathfrak M(v)v_1\,\textnormal{d}v.
\]
Indeed, $W$ is equivalent to $\langle v\rangle^2$, $|v_1\partial_xW|\le c_{\mathfrak M}W$, and the outgoing weight minus the reflected average of the incoming weight is $\alpha_b|v|^2+(1-\alpha_b)c_{\mathfrak M}\ge0$ at boundary $b$. This gives the bulk bound by Gr\"onwall's inequality. The tests $xv_1$ and $\langle v\rangle^2$, as in Lemma \ref{lem/ j bounds}, control the boundary fluxes; the additional source terms are bounded by $C\int_0^T\|q\|_X\,\mathrm{d}t$. These bounds justify the monotone construction. Positivity gives \eqref{eq/ lin tr} for signed data by comparison. The characteristic formula and approximation of the data give continuity in $X$. Prescribed inflow is included through its free transport solution.

For $F\ge0$, write $j_F=\int_{\mathbb R^3}vF\,\textnormal{d}v$ and $E_F=\int_{\mathbb R^3}|v|^2F\,\textnormal{d}v$, and set
\[
\mathcal U=\left\{(r,j,E)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R:E>\frac{|j|^2}{r}\right\}.
\]
Choose $\chi_n\in C_c^\infty(\mathcal U)$ with $0\le\chi_n\le1$, equal to one on each compact subset of $\mathcal U$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Extend it by zero outside $\mathcal U$, and define
\[
a_n[F]=\lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_F)\chi_n(\rho_F,j_F,E_F),\quad Q_n(F)=a_n[F](M[F]-F).
\]
The moment vector belongs to $\mathcal U$ wherever $\rho_F>0$, since a nonzero integrable velocity density has positive temperature. On the support of $\chi_n$, the density and temperature are bounded away from zero and all macroscopic parameters are bounded. Smooth dependence of the Gaussian on these parameters shows that $Q_n$ is globally Lipschitz on the nonnegative cone of $X$; for the loss term, use also the bound on the local weighted mass $\rho_F+E_F$ on this support. Moreover, $Q_n(F)+\frac1\varepsilon F\ge0$. The Duhamel fixed point argument, using \eqref{eq/ lin tr}, thus gives a global nonnegative solution $f_n\in C([0,T];X)$ of
\[
\partial_t f_n+v_1\partial_xf_n=Q_n(f_n),\quad f_n(0)=f_{\varepsilon0},
\]
with the prescribed boundary condition. We write $a_n=a_n[f_n]$.

The cutoff is independent of $v$ and multiplies the entire collision operator. Consequently, its moments against $1,v,|v|^2$ vanish, and its entropy production is $a_n(f_n-M[f_n])(\log f_n-\log M[f_n])\ge0$. Approximation of the initial and incoming data, followed by convex regularizations of $z\log z$ in the Green formula, justifies the entropy inequalities computed in Lemma \ref{lem/ every}. That proof and the multiplier argument of Lemma \ref{lem/ j bounds} give the bulk energy, absolute entropy, and boundary bounds uniformly in $n$.

Choose $\Phi$ by Lemma \ref{lem/ dlVP} and Corollary \ref{cor/ in Phi}, applied to the fixed initial datum and, in case \textbf{(In)}, the incoming data. The algebraic estimate \eqref{eq/ Phi Q} gives
\[
\int_0^T\iint_{\mathcal O}\Phi(\langle v\rangle^2)Q_n(f_n)\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t\le C\int_0^T\int_I a_n\rho_{f_n}\theta_{f_n}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\le\frac{C}{3\varepsilon}\int_0^T\iint_{\mathcal O}|v|^2f_n\,\mathrm{d}x\textnormal{d}v\mathrm{d}t.
\]
The boundary calculation and truncation in Proposition \ref{prop/ super} yield its weighted energy estimate uniformly in $n$, with a constant $C_{\varepsilon,T}$. Thus this construction uses only the bounded rate to propagate the energy weight.

Extract $f_n\rightharpoonup f$ in $L^1((0,T)\times\mathcal O)$. Since
\[
\int_0^T\|Q_n(f_n)\|_X\,\mathrm{d}t\le\frac2\varepsilon\int_0^T\|f_n\|_X\,\mathrm{d}t,
\]
velocity averaging applied to $\beta_\delta(f_n)$, followed by the entropy truncation estimate \eqref{eq/ beta err}, gives compactness of the bounded velocity moments; see \cite[Section III, Theorem 6]{DLM91}. The energy tails supplied by $\Phi$ then give strong convergence of $(\rho_{f_n},j_{f_n},E_{f_n})$ in $L^1((0,T)\times I)$, exactly as in Lemma \ref{lem/ mom2}. The argument of Lemma \ref{lem/ max L1} gives $M[f_n]\to M[f]$ in $L^1(0,T;X)$. After extraction,
\[
a_n\to a:=\lambda_\varepsilon(\rho_f)\quad\text{a.e.},\quad G_n:=a_nM[f_n]\to G:=aM[f]\quad\text{in }L^1(0,T;X).
\]
On $\{\rho_f>0\}$, the cutoff is eventually one along the converging moment vectors; on $\{\rho_f=0\}$, use $0\le a_n\le\rho_{f_n}$. The gain convergence follows from $a_n\le\frac1\varepsilon$.

To identify the full solution and its boundary traces, let $F$ solve the linear problem $\partial_tF+v_1\partial_xF+aF=G$ with the same initial and boundary data. Its existence follows from the linear construction above and the boundedness of $a$. Set $H_n=G_n-G-(a_n-a)F$. Dominated convergence gives $H_n\to0$ in $L^1(0,T;X)$, while
\[
\partial_t(f_n-F)+v_1\partial_x(f_n-F)=H_n-a_n(f_n-F),\quad (f_n-F)(0)=0.
\]
Estimate \eqref{eq/ lin tr} and Gr\"onwall's inequality imply $f_n\to F$ in $C([0,T];X)$ and convergence of both traces in $L^1((0,T)\times\mathbb R^3,\langle v\rangle^2|v_1|\,\mathrm{d}t\textnormal{d}v)$. Hence $F=f$, and the limit satisfies \eqref{eq/ regbgk} and the exact boundary condition. The collision term converges in $L^1(0,T;X)$, which also justifies the moment identities and the transport renormalizations.

Finally, lower semicontinuity gives \eqref{eq/ H in} or \eqref{eq/ H max}. The weighted strong convergence controls the negative entropy parts through \eqref{eq/ H neg}; Fatou's lemma applies to the nonnegative bulk production and to the Darroz\`es--Guiraud integrand \eqref{eq/ DG rel}. The outgoing fluxes converge strongly as a consequence of the trace convergence. Continuity in $X$ and lower semicontinuity of the entropy extend the inequalities to every terminal time. A diagonal extraction in $T$ gives the global solution $f_\varepsilon$. This completes the proof.
