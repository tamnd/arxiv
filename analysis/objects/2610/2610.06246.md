<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
These definitions of (global) rigidity are related to the generic ones as follows.
    Let $\ell:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ be the bar-lengths of a generic framework $(G,\rho')$ with edge set $E$.
    Let $\mathcal{S}_d$ be the set of all realisations~$\rho$ of $G$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$
    up to congruence such that $\|\rho(u)-\rho(v)\|=\ell(uv)$ for all $uv\in E$.
    It is a well-known fact that $G$ is generically (globally) $d$-rigid if and only
    if $\mathcal{S}_d$ is finite ($|\mathcal{S}_d|=1$);
    see, e.g., \cite{GortlerHealyThurston2007} for more details.
    The definitions of rigidity and global rigidity for frameworks with unit-length bars are therefore a natural analogue of generic rigidity and global rigidity: now, we simply substitute $\ell$ with a map that assigns 1 to each edge.

\label{rem:relationship}
    Every $d$-sphere graph is a $d$-unit-distance graph.
    Moreover, every penny graph is a matchstick graph and every matchstick graph is a $2$-unit-distance graph. The same is true for frameworks.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:d_sphere_unique_realisation}
    Let $G$ be a $d$-tree. If there are three vertices that have common neighbours inducing $K_d$,
    then $G$ is not a $d$-unit-distance graph.
    If $G$ is a $d$-sphere/matchstick/$d$-unit-distance graph,
    then $G$ is globally $d$-sphere/matchstick/$d$-unit-distance rigid;
    the matchstick case holds for $d=2$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since there are only two unit-distance realisations of $K_{d+1}$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$
    up to direct isometries, no three distinct vertices can 
    have common neighbours inducing $K_d$ in a $d$-unit-distance realisation,
    as it is required to be injective.
    
    Hence, suppose that no three vertices have common neighbours inducing $K_d$
    and $G$ is a $d$-sphere/matchstick/$d$-unit-distance $d$-tree.
    We proceed by induction to prove the latter implication in the statement.
    The statement is clearly true for $K_{d+1}$.
    Now suppose the statement holds for all graphs on fewer vertices than $G$,
    and suppose that $G$ is a $d$-sphere/matchstick/$d$-unit-distance graph.
    Let $v$ be a degree $d$ vertex of $G$ such that $G'=G -v$ is a $d$-tree and $N_G(v)$ be the set of $d$ neighbours of $v$ in $G$.
    By the definition of $d$-tree, $N_G(v)$ is the vertex set of a complete graph $K_d$, which is contained in a $K_{d+1}$ subgraph of $G'$. 
    By our assumption, this implies that there is exactly one vertex $u\neq v$ such that $N_G(v)\subseteq N_G(u)$.
    Since $G$ is a $d$-sphere/matchstick/$d$-unit-distance graph, so is $G'$. Therefore,
    by the inductive hypothesis, there is a unique $d$-sphere/matchstick/$d$-unit-distance framework $(G',\rho')$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$ up to isometries.
    
    Since there are two unit-distance realisations of $K_{d+1}$ in~$\mathbb{R}^d$ up to direct isometries,
    there are exactly two points $A_1, A_2$ such that $\|A_i-\rho'(w)\|=1$ for all $w\in N_G(v)$.
    One of them is $\rho'(u)$, say $A_2$.
    Therefore, the realisation $\rho$ of $G$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$ defined by letting $\rho(v)=A_1$ and $\rho(w)=\rho'(w)$ for $w\in V(G')$ is the unique realisation of $G$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$ up to isometries.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:pum-rigid-iff-all-rigid}
    A penny/matchstick/$2$-unit-distance graph $G$ is \textbf{p}-rigid/\textbf{m}-rigid/\textbf{u}-rigid\ if and only if
    every penny/matchstick/$2$-unit-distance realisation of $G$ is rigid.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Suppose that a $2$-unit-distance framework $(G, \rho)$ has a flex $t \mapsto m_t$, $t\in [0,1]$.
    Then $m_t$ is injective for all $t$ in some interval $[0,\varepsilon)$ and so $G$ is not \textbf{u}-rigid.
    If $\rho$ is matchstick, then $(G,m_t)$ is planar for all $t$ in some interval $[0,\varepsilon)$ and so $G$ is not \textbf{m}-rigid.
    Finally, suppose that $\rho$ is a penny realisation.
    For $uv\notin E$, let $\varepsilon_{uv}$ be the smallest~$t$ such that $\|m_t(u)-m_t(v)\|=1$,
    or $\varepsilon_{uv}=1$ if no such $t$ exists.
    Since $m_0=\rho$ is a penny realisation, $\varepsilon_{uv}>0$ for all $uv\not\in E$.
    Since there are only finitely many non-edges, $\varepsilon=\min\left\lbrace \varepsilon_{uv}\,\textbf{\upshape\textbar}\,uv\notin E \right\rbrace$ is positive
    and hence all $m_t$ for $t\in[0,\varepsilon)$ are penny realisations of $G$, i.e., $(G, \rho)$ is not \textbf{p}-rigid.

    The other implication follows from the fact that
    a framework can have only finitely many non-congruent rigid frameworks equivalent to it.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:pennyouterplanar}
    Let $G=(V,E)$ be an outerplanar penny/matchstick/$2$-unit-distance graph with $|V|\geq3$.
    Then every penny/matchstick/2-unit-distance framework $(G,\rho)$ is independent.
    Furthermore,
    the following are equivalent:
    \begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
        \item\label{it:pennyouterplanar:prigid} $G$ is \textbf{p}-rigid/\textbf{m}-rigid/\textbf{u}-rigid;
        \item\label{it:pennyouterplanar:gprigid} $G$ is globally \textbf{p}-rigid/\textbf{m}-rigid/\textbf{u}-rigid;
        \item\label{it:pennyouterplanar:maxout} $G$ is a maximal outerplanar graph;
        \item\label{it:pennyouterplanar:edgecount} $|E|=2|V|-3$;
        \item\label{it:pennyouterplanar:2tree} $G$ is a 2-tree.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We prove the result for penny graphs. The same proof applies to matchstick and 2-unit-distance graphs. We start by proving the independence part of the result by induction on $|V|$.
    The statement is clearly true for $|V|=3$, since the complete graph $K_3$ has only one realisation, and triangles are independent.
    Now suppose that all outerplanar penny graphs on at most $n-1$ vertices have only independent penny frameworks, and let $|V|=n$.
    Choose a penny framework $(G,\rho)$.
    If $G$ is not 2-connected,
    it has a cut vertex.
    It follows that $(G,\rho)$ is formed from gluing two outerplanar penny frameworks at a vertex, and thus $(G,\rho)$ is independent by the induction hypothesis.
    Suppose instead that $G$ is 2-connected.
    Then $G$ has a (unique) Hamiltonian cycle, any chord of which is a cut set.
    If $G$ has no chords then $(G,\rho)$ is independent as any framework of a cycle that is not restricted to a line is independent.
    If $G$ has a chord, then $G$ is formed from gluing two outerplanar penny frameworks at an edge,
    and hence $(G,\rho)$ is independent by the induction hypothesis.

    We now prove the second part of the statement.
    It is well-known that \ref{it:pennyouterplanar:maxout}, \ref{it:pennyouterplanar:edgecount} and \ref{it:pennyouterplanar:2tree} are equivalent.
    Let $(G,\rho)$ be a penny realisation.
    Since it is independent,
    it is also regular, i.e., the rank of $R(G,\rho)$ is the maximum possible among all $(G,\rho')$.
    By the main result of~\cite{AsimowRoth1978},
    $(G,\rho)$ is rigid if and only if $|E|=2|V|-3$.
    Hence, \ref{it:pennyouterplanar:edgecount} and \ref{it:pennyouterplanar:prigid} are equivalent
    by \Cref{lem:pum-rigid-iff-all-rigid}.
    It is immediate that \ref{it:pennyouterplanar:gprigid} implies \ref{it:pennyouterplanar:prigid}.
    Finally \ref{it:pennyouterplanar:2tree} implies \ref{it:pennyouterplanar:gprigid} by \Cref{lem:d_sphere_unique_realisation}.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a graph.
    A colouring of edges $\delta\colon  E\rightarrow \{\text{\text{blue}{}, \text{red}{}}\}$
    is called a \emph{NAC-colouring},
    if it is surjective and for every cycle in~$G$,
    either all edges have the same colour, or
    there are at least two edges of each colour.
    A NAC-colouring is called \emph{Cartesian} if no pair of vertices is connected by a red path and blue path simultaneously.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a graph.
    Let $\tikz[scale=0.225,baseline={(0,0)}]{\draw[](0, 0)--(1, 0)--(1, 1)--(0, 1)--cycle;\draw[](0, 1)--(1, 0);}\,(G)$ be the 2-dimensional abstract simplicial complex
    obtained from $V\cup E$ by adding $\{u,v\}$ whenever $u,v\in V$ lie in an induced 4-cycle
    and $\{u,v,w\}$ whenever $u,v,w\in V$ lie in a 3-cycle or an induced 4-cycle.
    Let $\rho$ be a realisation of $G$ such that every 3-cycle is mapped to a non-degenerate triangle and every 4-cycle is mapped to
    a non-degenerate parallelogram.\footnote{By ``degenerate'' we mean all joints being collinear.}
    If $\tikz[scale=0.225,baseline={(0,0)}]{\draw[](0, 0)--(1, 0)--(1, 1)--(0, 1)--cycle;\draw[](0, 1)--(1, 0);}\,(G)$ is simply connected, the framework $(G, \rho)$ is called a \emph{TP-framework}.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:GraseggerLegersky2024}
    Let $(G,\rho)$ be a TP-framework.
    The following statements are equivalent:
    \begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
        \item $(G,\rho)$ is rigid,
        \item $(G,\rho)$ is infinitesimally rigid,
        \item $G$ has no Cartesian NAC-colouring.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:allFaces3or4cycles}
    Let $G$ be a graph that has a penny/matchstick realisation $\rho$ such that all bounded faces are 3- or 4-cycles. 
    Then the following statements are equivalent:
    \begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
        \item\label{it:pennyRigid} $G$ is \textbf{p}-rigid/\textbf{m}-rigid,
        \item\label{it:2unitDistRigid} $G$ is \textbf{u}-rigid,
        \item\label{it:contRigid} $(G,\rho)$ is rigid,
        \item\label{it:infRigid} $(G,\rho)$ is infinitesimally rigid,
        \item\label{it:noNAC} $G$ has no Cartesian NAC-colouring.
    \end{enumerate}
    In particular, if $G$ is a penny graph,
    then $G$ is \textbf{p}-rigid\, if and only if it is \textbf{m}-rigid.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The equivalence of \ref{it:contRigid}--\ref{it:noNAC} follows from \Cref{thm:GraseggerLegersky2024} once we show
    that $(G,\rho)$ is a TP-framework.
    Indeed, since $\rho$ is a penny/matchstick-realisation, it is injective and all 3-cycles are equilateral, hence non-degenerate, triangles;
    each 4-cycle is a rhombus with no coincident vertices, and so a non-degenerate parallelogram. 
    Since $\rho$ is planar and all bounded faces are triangles or
    rhombi by the assumption, $\tikz[scale=0.225,baseline={(0,0)}]{\draw[](0, 0)--(1, 0)--(1, 1)--(0, 1)--cycle;\draw[](0, 1)--(1, 0);}\,(G)$ is simply connected. 

    By the argument, any penny/matchstick/$2$-unit-distance framework $(G,\rho)$ is a TP-framework.
    Since \ref{it:noNAC} is independent of $\rho$,
    either all penny/matchstick/$2$-unit-distance realisations of $G$ are rigid,
    or none of them is by \Cref{thm:GraseggerLegersky2024}.
    Therefore, \ref{it:pennyRigid}, \ref{it:2unitDistRigid} and \ref{it:noNAC} are equivalent
    by \Cref{lem:pum-rigid-iff-all-rigid}.
