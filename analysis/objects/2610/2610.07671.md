In addition to our definition of the area, there is another popular definition (commonly used in algebraic combinatorics) that counts only the number of full squares between the Dyck path and the center line $x=y$. We can easily convert between these two definitions by noting that the only non-square areas are the triangles next to the center line, which have total area $\frac{n}{2}$. Subtracting this amount from the area given by our definition yields the area given by the other definition.

<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
The vector $ \vec{y} = (y_{0},\ldots, y_{s-1}) $ is called the \emph{type} of the path $ P $.
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:type_count}
Let $\vec{y}$ be a type with distances $d_{0}, \ldots, d_{s}$. Then the map $P \mapsto (P_{0}, \ldots, P_{s})$ is a bijection between the set of paths $P \in D^s_{n+1}$ of type $\vec{y}$ and the product $D^s_{d_0} \times \cdots \times D^s_{d_s}$, where each subpath $P_{l}$ is identified with an $(sd_l, d_l)$-Dyck path by translation. In particular, the number of paths of type $\vec{y}$ is
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    F^s_{d_0} F^s_{d_1} \cdots F^s_{d_s}
\end{split}\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The subpath $P_{0}$ runs from $(0,0)$ to $(sy_{0}, y_{0})$ while staying weakly beneath $L_{0}$, so it is itself an $(sd_0, d_0)$-Dyck path. For $1 \leq l \leq s$, after its last touchpoint on $L_{l-1}$ the path lies strictly beneath $L_{l-1}$, and hence (since it moves between lattice points) weakly beneath $L_{l}$. Consequently, $P_{l}$ runs from $Z_{l-1} + (1,0)$ to $Z_{l}$, both points of $L_{l}$, while staying weakly beneath $L_{l}$; translating its starting point to the origin identifies $P_{l}$ with an $(sd_l, d_l)$-Dyck path.

Conversely, concatenating any tuple of such paths, with the forced step $(1,0)$ inserted between consecutive subpaths and the final step $(0,1)$ appended, produces a path in $D^s_{n+1}$ of type $\vec{y}$, and the two constructions are mutually inverse. Since the subpaths may be chosen independently, the product formula follows.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:weighted_type_sum}
Let $(c_d)_{d \geq 0}$ be a sequence with generating function $C(z) = \sum_{d=0}^{\infty} c_d z^d$. Then, for each $0 \leq l \leq s$,
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \sum_{d_{0}+\cdots+d_{s}=n} \left(\prod_{\substack{0 \leq i \leq s\\i \neq l}} F^s_{d_i}\right) c_{d_l} = [C(z) F(z)^{s}]_{n}
\end{split}\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Swapping the coordinates $d_l$ and $d_s$ permutes the terms of the sum, so we may assume $l = s$. The sum then factors as the convolution
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \sum_{0 \leq d_s \leq n} c_{d_s} \sum_{d_{0}+\cdots+d_{s-1}=n-d_s} \prod_{0 \leq i \leq s-1} F^s_{d_i} = \sum_{0 \leq d_s \leq n} c_{d_s} \cdot [F(z)^{s}]_{n-d_s} = [C(z) F(z)^{s}]_{n}
\end{split}\end{equation}
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:recursive}
For all $n \geq 0$ and $s \geq 1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recursive}
  A^s_{n+1} =\frac{s}{2}[F(z)^{s+1}]_{n} + \frac{s(s+1)}{2}[zF^{\prime}(z)F(z)^{s}]_{n}+ (s+1)[A(z)F(z)^{s}]_{n}
\end{equation}

If we directly plug in the formulas for $zF'(z)$ and $\frac{1}{F(z)}$ into Equation \ref{eq:functional_equation} and simplify, we end up with a lengthy triple-sum formula for $A^s_n$, closely resembling one derived by Mansour and Ramírez for a related problem (\cite{mansour2021enumerations}, Theorem 3.3). Fortunately, the transformations below will allow us to do substantially better.

Banderier and Gittenberger use the kernel method to find generating functions for the total area below directed lattice paths (\cite{banderier2006area}, Theorem 5), and Section 6 of their paper shows how paths lying below a line of rational slope, such as $(sn,n)$-Dyck paths, can be recast as directed lattice paths. <!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:exact_area} For all $n \geq 0, s \geq 1$,
    \begin{equation}\begin{split}\label{eq:total_area}
        A^s_n = \frac{1}{2} \left(\sum_{k=0}^{n} {(s+1)k \choose k} {(s+1)(n-k) \choose n-k} - {(s+1)n+1 \choose n}\right)
    \end{split}\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We extract the coefficient of $z^n$ from each term of Equation \ref{eq:functional_transformed2}. The first term gives the sum in Equation \ref{eq:total_area} directly:
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    [G(z)^2]_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} {(s+1)k \choose k} {(s+1)(n-k) \choose n-k}
\end{split}\end{equation}
For the second term, Equation \ref{eq:absorption} rewrites $F^s_k = \frac{1}{sk+1} {(s+1)k \choose k} = \frac{1}{(s+1)k+1} {(s+1)k+1 \choose k}$, which puts the coefficient in the form of convolution Identity 5.62 from \cite{knuth1989concrete}:
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    [F(z) G(z)]_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} F^s_k {(s+1)(n-k) \choose n-k} = {(s+1)n+1 \choose n}
\end{split}\end{equation}

Subtracting these two coefficients and halving yields Equation \ref{eq:total_area}, completing the proof.
<!-- end proof-3 -->

\begin{equation}\begin{split}
A^s_n = \frac{1}{2} \left(\sum_{k=0}^{n} {2k \choose k} {2(n-k) \choose n-k} - {2n+1 \choose n}\right) = \frac{1}{2} \left(4^n - {2n+1 \choose n}\right)
\end{split}\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:stanica_bounds} Let $s, n \in \mathbb{N}\xspace$, with $s \geq 1$ and $n \geq 1$. Then, 
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \sqrt{\frac{s+1}{s}} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \cdot e^{-\frac{1}{8n}} < {(s+1)n \choose n} < \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \sqrt{\frac{s+1}{s}} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n
\end{split}\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:inner_sum_upper_bound} Let $s, n \in \mathbb{N}\xspace$, with $s \geq 1$ and $n \geq 2$. Then, 
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \sum_{k=0}^{n} {(s+1)k \choose k} {(s+1)(n-k) \choose n-k} < \frac{1}{2 \pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left(\pi - \frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\right) + 2 {(s+1)n \choose n}
\end{split}\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Applying Lemma \ref{lem:stanica_bounds} to Equation \ref{eq:inner_sum_rewritten} yields
\begin{equation}\begin{split}\label{eq:initial_upper_bound}
    \frac{1}{2 \pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{k(n-k)}} + 2 {(s+1)n \choose n}
\end{split}\end{equation}

We now bound the remaining sum using convexity. Note that for any $p, q > 0$, the function $k^{-p} (n-k)^{-q}$ is convex on $(0,n)$: its logarithm $-p \ln{k} - q \ln{(n-k)}$ has second derivative $\frac{p}{k^2} + \frac{q}{(n-k)^2} > 0$, and exponentiating preserves convexity by the composition rule in \cite[Section 3.2.4]{boyd2004convex}. In particular, the summand $f(k) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{k(n-k)}}$ is convex in $k$ on $(0,n)$. This lets us apply the Hermite-Hadamard inequality (see \cite{niculescu2006convex}), which states that any function $g$ that is convex on $[a,b]$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\begin{split}\label{eq:hermite_hadamard}
    g\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right) \leq \frac{1}{b-a} \int_{a}^{b} g(x) dx \leq \frac{g(a) + g(b)}{2}
\end{split}\end{equation}

Applying the left half of Equation \ref{eq:hermite_hadamard} to $f$ on each interval $\left[k - \frac{1}{2}, k + \frac{1}{2}\right]$, and summing over $1 \leq k \leq n-1$, yields
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{k(n-k)}} \leq \int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-\frac{1}{2}} \frac{1}{\sqrt{k(n-k)}} dk
\end{split}\end{equation}

For any $0 < t \leq \frac{n}{2}$, this integral evaluates to
\begin{equation}\begin{split}\label{eq:arcsin_integral}
    \int_{t}^{n-t} \frac{1}{\sqrt{k(n-k)}} dk = 2\arcsin\left(\sqrt{1 - \frac{t}{n}}\right) - 2\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\right) = \pi - 4\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\right)
\end{split}\end{equation}
where the second equality uses the identity $\arcsin\left(\sqrt{x}\right) + \arcsin\left(\sqrt{1-x}\right) = \frac{\pi}{2}$. Taking $t = \frac{1}{2}$ and applying the Taylor series lower bound $\arcsin(x) \geq x$ gives
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{k(n-k)}} \leq \pi - 4\arcsin\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}\right) \leq \pi - \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{n}} < \pi - \frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}
\end{split}\end{equation}

Plugging this back into Equation \ref{eq:initial_upper_bound} completes the proof.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
While we could also simplify the ${(s+1)n \choose n}$ term in Lemma \ref{lem:inner_sum_upper_bound}, we will leave it unsimplified for the time being. This is because we will be able to combine it with similar terms when applying the bound to the formula for the total area.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:inner_sum_lower_bound} Let $s, n \in \mathbb{N}\xspace$, with $s \geq 1$ and $n \geq 2$. Then, 
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \sum_{k=0}^{n} {(s+1)k \choose k} {(s+1)(n-k) \choose n-k} > \frac{1}{2 \pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left(\pi - \frac{5}{\sqrt{n}}\right) + 2 {(s+1)n \choose n}
\end{split}\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We proceed similarly to the proof for the upper bound, this time applying the lower bound from Lemma \ref{lem:stanica_bounds}. Combining the two resulting exponential factors via $\frac{1}{8k} + \frac{1}{8(n-k)} = \frac{n}{8k(n-k)}$, and then applying the standard bound $1 - x < e^{-x}$, yields the initial lower bound
\begin{equation}\begin{split}\label{eq:initial_lower_bound}
    \frac{1}{2 \pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k(n-k)}} - \frac{n}{8} \cdot \frac{1}{(k(n-k))^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right) + 2 {(s+1)n \choose n}
\end{split}\end{equation}

We will now find a lower bound for the summation in Equation \ref{eq:initial_lower_bound}, bounding its two sums separately. For the first sum, recall from the proof of Lemma \ref{lem:inner_sum_upper_bound} that $\frac{1}{\sqrt{k(n-k)}}$ is convex in $k$ on $(0,n)$. Applying the right half of the Hermite-Hadamard inequality (Equation \ref{eq:hermite_hadamard}) on each interval $[k, k+1]$, and summing over $1 \leq k \leq n-2$, gives
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{k(n-k)}} \geq \int_{1}^{n-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{k(n-k)}} dk + \frac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}} \geq \\
    \pi - 4\arcsin\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right) + \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \geq \pi - \left(\pi\sqrt{2} - 1\right) \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
\end{split}\end{equation}
where the integral was evaluated in Equation \ref{eq:arcsin_integral} (with $t = 1$). The final step uses the bound $\arcsin(x) \leq \frac{\pi \sqrt{2}}{4} x$ for $0 \leq x \leq \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, which holds because $\arcsin$ is convex and so lies below its chord on $\left[0, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right]$.

For the second sum, the summand $\frac{1}{(k(n-k))^{\frac{3}{2}}}$ is again of the form $k^{-p} (n-k)^{-q}$, hence convex on $(0,n)$. Applying the left half of the Hermite-Hadamard inequality on each interval $\left[k - \frac{1}{2}, k + \frac{1}{2}\right]$, and summing over $1 \leq k \leq n-1$, gives
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{n}{8} \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{1}{(k(n-k))^{\frac{3}{2}}} \leq \frac{n}{8} \int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-\frac{1}{2}} \frac{1}{(k(n-k))^{\frac{3}{2}}} dk = \frac{n-1}{n\sqrt{2n-1}} \leq \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
\end{split}\end{equation}
where the integral was evaluated using the antiderivative $\frac{2(2k-n)}{n^2 \sqrt{k(n-k)}}$, and the final step uses $2n-1 \geq n$.

Combining these two bounds yields
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k(n-k)}} - \frac{n}{8} \cdot \frac{1}{(k(n-k))^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right) \geq \pi - \pi\sqrt{2} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} > \pi - \frac{5}{\sqrt{n}}
\end{split}\end{equation}
since $\pi\sqrt{2} < 5$. Plugging this back into Equation \ref{eq:initial_lower_bound} completes the proof.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:inner_sum_asymp} Let $s,n \in \mathbb{N}\xspace$, with $s \geq 1$. Then, as $n$ grows large,
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \sum_{k=0}^{n} {(s+1)k \choose k} {(s+1)(n-k) \choose n-k} \sim \frac{1}{2} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n
\end{split}\end{equation}

\end{equation*}
for general $m$. Setting $m = s+1$, this is precisely the statement of Corollary \ref{cor:inner_sum_asymp}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:total_area_bounds} Let $s, n \in \mathbb{N}\xspace$, with $s \geq 1$ and $n \geq 2$. Then, 
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{1}{4 \pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left(\pi - \frac{5}{\sqrt{n}}\right) < A^s_n < \frac{1}{4\pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left( \pi + \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \right)
\end{split}\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We can apply Lemmas \ref{lem:inner_sum_lower_bound} and \ref{lem:inner_sum_upper_bound} to the area equation, which gives
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{1}{4 \pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left(\pi - \frac{5}{\sqrt{n}}\right) + {(s+1)n \choose n} - \frac{1}{2} {(s+1)n+1 \choose n} < \\
    A^s_n < \frac{1}{4 \pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left(\pi - \frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\right) + {(s+1)n \choose n} - \frac{1}{2} {(s+1)n+1 \choose n}
\end{split}\end{equation}

To help simplify the remaining binomial coefficients, we can note that $1 < \frac{(s+1)n+1}{sn+1} < 2$ for $n \geq 1$, so by Equation \ref{eq:absorption},
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    0 < {(s+1)n \choose n} - \frac{1}{2} {(s+1)n+1 \choose n} < \frac{1}{2} {(s+1)n \choose n}
\end{split}\end{equation}

Applying this gives
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{1}{4 \pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left(\pi - \frac{5}{\sqrt{n}}\right) < A^s_n < \frac{1}{4 \pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left(\pi - \frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\right) + \frac{1}{2} {(s+1)n \choose n}
\end{split}\end{equation}

This completes the proof for the lower bound, but we still need to simplify the upper bound. Applying the bounds from Lemma \ref{lem:stanica_bounds} to eliminate the ${(s+1)n \choose n}$ term yields
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    A^s_n < \frac{1}{4 \pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left(\pi - \frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\right) + \frac{1}{2 \sqrt{2\pi}} \sqrt{\frac{s+1}{s}} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} = \\
    \frac{1}{4\pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left( \pi + \left(-2 + \sqrt{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{s}{s+1}}\right) \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \right)
\end{split}\end{equation}

We would like to simplify the $\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ coefficient. We can note that $\sqrt{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{s}{s+1}} < \sqrt{2\pi} < 3$, so we can obtain the upper bound
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    A^s_n < \frac{1}{4\pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left( \pi + \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \right)
\end{split}\end{equation}

This completes the proof.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:average_area_bounds} Let $s, n \in \mathbb{N}\xspace$, with $s \geq 1$ and $n \geq 2$. Then, 
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}} \sqrt{\frac{s+1}{s}} \left(\pi \sqrt{n} - 5\right) (sn+1) < \frac{A^s_n}{F^s_n} < \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}} \sqrt{\frac{s+1}{s}} \left(\pi \sqrt{n} + 2\right) (sn+1)
\end{split}\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We begin with the lower bound. Taking the lower bound on the total area from Theorem \ref{thm:total_area_bounds} and dividing by $F^s_n = \frac{1}{sn+1} {(s+1)n \choose n}$ gives
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{A^s_n}{F^s_n} > \frac{\frac{1}{4 \pi} \frac{s+1}{s} \left(\frac{(s+1)^{s+1}}{s^s}\right)^n \left(\pi - \frac{5}{\sqrt{n}}\right)}{\frac{1}{sn+1} {(s+1)n \choose n}}
\end{split}\end{equation}

Applying the upper bound from Lemma \ref{lem:stanica_bounds} to the binomial coefficient in the denominator yields the desired lower bound
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{A^s_n}{F^s_n} > \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}} \sqrt{\frac{s+1}{s}} \left(\pi \sqrt{n} - 5\right) (sn+1)
\end{split}\end{equation}

For the upper bound, dividing by $F^s_n$ instead requires the lower bound from Lemma \ref{lem:stanica_bounds}, which introduces a factor of $e^{\frac{1}{8n}}$. Dividing the upper bound of Theorem \ref{thm:total_area_bounds} by $F^s_n$ and applying Lemma \ref{lem:stanica_bounds} thus gives
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{A^s_n}{F^s_n} < \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}} \sqrt{\frac{s+1}{s}} \left(\pi \sqrt{n} + 1\right) (sn+1) \cdot e^{\frac{1}{8n}}
\end{split}\end{equation}

Finally, since $e^{-x} > 1 - x$, we have $e^{\frac{1}{8n}} < \frac{1}{1 - \frac{1}{8n}} = 1 + \frac{1}{8n-1} \leq 1 + \frac{1}{\pi \sqrt{n} + 1}$, where the last step uses $8n - 1 \geq \pi \sqrt{n} + 1$ for $n \geq 1$. Hence $\left(\pi \sqrt{n} + 1\right) e^{\frac{1}{8n}} < \pi \sqrt{n} + 2$, which yields the desired upper bound and completes the proof.

\label{lem:average_area_bounds_s} Let $s, n \in \mathbb{N}\xspace$, with $s \geq 1$ and $n \geq 0$. Then,
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{1}{2} (sn+1) \left(\frac{s}{s+1}\right)^n \left(Q(n)+1\right) - \frac{1}{2} ((s+1)n+1) \leq \\
    \frac{A^s_n}{F^s_n} \leq \frac{1}{2} (sn+1) \left(\frac{s+1}{s}\right)^n \left(Q(n)+1\right) - \frac{1}{2} ((s+1)n+1)
\end{split}\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Take Equation \ref{eq:total_area_bounds_s} and divide by $F^s_n$ to obtain
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{\frac{1}{2} s^n \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{n^k}{k!}}{\frac{1}{sn+1} {(s+1)n \choose n}} - \frac{1}{2} ((s+1)n+1) \leq \frac{A^s_n}{F^s_n} \leq \frac{\frac{1}{2} (s+1)^n \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{n^k}{k!}}{\frac{1}{sn+1} {(s+1)n \choose n}} - \frac{1}{2} ((s+1)n+1)
\end{split}\end{equation}

We then apply Equation \ref{eq:binomial_bounds_s} to the remaining binomial coefficients. Since these appear in denominators, the upper bound in Equation \ref{eq:binomial_bounds_s} produces the lower bound below, and vice versa. This gives
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{1}{2} (sn+1) \left(\frac{s}{s+1}\right)^n \cdot \frac{n!}{n^n} \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{n^k}{k!} - \frac{1}{2} ((s+1)n+1) \leq \\
    \frac{A^s_n}{F^s_n} \leq \frac{1}{2} (sn+1) \left(\frac{s+1}{s}\right)^n \cdot \frac{n!}{n^n} \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{n^k}{k!} - \frac{1}{2} ((s+1)n+1)
\end{split}\end{equation}

Applying Equation \ref{eq:q_identity} completes the proof.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- corollary-5: body_md -->
\label{cor:average_s_asymp} Let $s, n \in \mathbb{N}\xspace$, with $n \geq 1$. Then, as $s$ grows large,
\begin{equation}\begin{split}
    \frac{A^s_n}{F^s_n} \sim \frac{1}{2} sn \cdot Q(n)
\end{split}\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-5 -->
<!-- remark-11: body_md -->
\label{rem:urn_ev}
By the birthday interpretation of $Q(n)$, Corollary \ref{cor:average_s_asymp} states that, for large $s$, the average area is approximately $\frac{sn}{2}$ times the expected number of draws made before the first repetition. There is likely some kind of combinatorial explanation for this connection, but it is not immediately clear to us.
