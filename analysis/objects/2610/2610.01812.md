<!-- problem-1: body_md -->
\label{prob:gap-convexity}
The input consists of a function \(p\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)\) and two positive
rational thresholds \(\gamma_+\) and \(\gamma_-\). It is promised that
exactly one of the following alternatives holds:
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \textsc{Yes:}\quad&
    \kappa_\Omega(p)\geq\gamma_+,\\
    \textsc{No:}\quad&
    \kappa_\Omega(p)\leq-\gamma_-.
\end{aligned}
\]
The task is to decide which alternative holds.
<!-- end problem-1 -->
<!-- problem-2: body_md -->
\label{prob:signomial-convexity}
Given a signomial \(f\), consider the following two variants:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] \emph{Constrained:} Decide whether \(f\) is convex on \(X\).
\item[\textup{(ii)}] \emph{Unconstrained:} Decide whether \(f\)
is convex on \(\mathbb{R}_{++}^n\).
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end problem-2 -->

Given a signomial \(f\), consider the following two variants:

\item[\textup{(ii)}] \emph{Unconstrained:} Decide whether

\end{enumerate}

<!-- problem-4: body_md -->
\label{prob:log-log-convexity}
Given a signomial \(f\), under the promise that \(f>0\)
throughout the relevant domain, consider the following two variants:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] \emph{Constrained:} Decide whether
\(y\mapsto\log f(e^y)\) is convex on \(Y\).
\item[\textup{(ii)}] \emph{Unconstrained:} Decide whether
\(y\mapsto\log f(e^y)\) is convex on \(\mathbb{R}^n\).
\end{enumerate}

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:original-coordinate-hardness}
Both the constrained and unconstrained  
  \hyperref[prob:signomial-convexity]{ 
    \textsc{Signomial-Convexity}}{} problems are strongly NP-hard.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For the constrained problem, let \(p:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}\) be
an instance of \hyperref[prob:cbgc]{\textsc{CBGC}}{} and define
$X=[1,2]^n$,
$T(x)=2x-3\mathbf{1}$,
$f(x)=p(T(x))$.
The affine map \(T\) sends \(X\) bijectively onto \([-1,1]^n\), and
$\nabla^2 f(x)=4\nabla^2p(T(x))$.
Consequently, \(f\) is convex on \(X\) if and only if \(p\) is convex
on \([-1,1]^n\). Moreover, \(f\) is a polynomial and hence a signomial
on \(\mathbb{R}_{++}^n\).

For the unconstrained problem, let
\(p:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}\) be an instance of \hyperref[prob:gqgc]{\textsc{GQGC}}{} and define
$f(u,v)=p(u-v)$,
$(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2n}$.
If \(p\) is convex on \(\mathbb{R}^n\), then \(f\) is convex as its
composition with a linear map. Conversely, suppose that \(f\) is
convex on \(\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2n}\). For any \(x,d\in\mathbb{R}^n\),
choose \(u,v\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^n\) such that \(u-v=x\). 
      
                                            
                
                                  
   
                     
         
Let $z=(d^\top,0)^\top$, then
$z^\top
\nabla^2f(u,v)
z
=
d^\top\nabla^2p(x)d
\geq0$.
Since \(x\) and \(d\) are arbitrary, \(p\) is convex on
\(\mathbb{R}^n\). Thus, \(f\) is convex on
\(\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2n}\) if and only if \(p\) is convex on
\(\mathbb{R}^n\).

Both transformations are computable in polynomial time, preserve
fixed degree, and produce coefficients of polynomial encoding length.
Strong NP-hardness therefore follows.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:exponential-curvature-approximation}
Let \(p:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}\) be a polynomial,
and let \(q_h=p\circ\Phi_h\). Then \(q_h\) is the exponential form of a
signomial and, for every rational \(0<h\leq1/2\),
\[
    \sup_{y\in[-1,1]^n}
    \left\|
        \nabla^2q_h(y)-\nabla^2p(y)
    \right\|_2
    \leq C_{\mathrm{err}}(p)h,
\]
where \(C_{\mathrm{err}}(p)=L_p+6H_p+2G_p\).
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:compact-log-coordinate}
The constrained  
  \hyperref[prob:log-coordinate-convexity]{ 
    \textsc{Log-Coordinate-Convexity}}{} problem is strongly NP-hard.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let \(p\) be an instance of \hyperref[prob:cbgc]{\textsc{CBGC}}{} on \(Y=[-1,1]^n\), with curvature
margins \(\gamma_+\) and \(\gamma_-\).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:exponential-curvature-approximation}, we can choose a positive
rational \(h\), with polynomial encoding length, and construct a
signomial \(q_h(y)=f_h(e^y)\) such that
    $\sup_{y\in Y}
    \|\nabla^2q_h(y)-\nabla^2p(y)\|_2
    \leq E$,
    $E\leq\frac12\min\{\gamma_+,\gamma_-\}$.
By the gap-preserving argument of
Section~\ref{subsec:gap-convexity}, \(q_h\) is convex on \(Y\) in the positive case and nonconvex on \(Y\) in the negative case. Since
\(q_h(y)=f_h(e^y)\), the signomial \(f_h\) is log-coordinate convex on
\(Y\) exactly in the positive case.

The construction of \(f_h\) is computable in polynomial time and preserves
the required coefficient and exponent encoding bounds. Thus, an algorithm for the constrained version of
\textsc{Log-Coordinate-Convexity} would solve \hyperref[prob:cbgc]{\textsc{CBGC}}{}. Since \hyperref[prob:cbgc]{\textsc{CBGC}}{} is
strongly NP-hard and the reduction preserves the required encoding
bounds, the result follows.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:compact-log-log}
The constrained  
  \hyperref[prob:log-log-convexity]{ 
    \textsc{Log-Log-Convexity}}{} problem is strongly NP-hard.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let \(p\) be an instance of \hyperref[prob:cbgc]{\textsc{CBGC}}{} on \(Y=[-1,1]^n\). As in the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:compact-log-coordinate}, choose a positive rational
\(h\) and construct \(q_h(y)=f_h(e^y)\). The gap-preserving
approximation retains inverse-polynomial margins
\(\gamma_+,\gamma_->0\), so
$\kappa_Y(q_h)\geq\gamma_+$ in the positive case,
$\kappa_Y(q_h)\leq-\gamma_-$ in the negative case.

Choose rational bounds \(B_0,B_1\geq1\) such that
\(|q_h(y)|\leq B_0\) and
\(\|\nabla q_h(y)\|_2\leq B_1\) for every \(y\in Y\), and take a
positive rational
\(\varepsilon\leq
\min\{1/(2B_0),\gamma_+/(4B_1^2)\}\).
Define \(g_h(y)=1+\varepsilon q_h(y)\). Then \(g_h\geq1/2\) on
\(Y\), and
$M_{g_h}=\varepsilon g_h\nabla^2q_h
-\varepsilon^2\nabla q_h\nabla q_h^\top$.

In the positive case, Weyl's inequality gives, for every \(y\in Y\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \lambda_{\min}\!\left(M_{g_h}(y)\right)
    \geq
    \varepsilon g_h(y)
    \lambda_{\min}\!\left(\nabla^2q_h(y)\right)
    -
    \varepsilon^2\|\nabla q_h(y)\|_2^2 \geq
    \varepsilon
    \left(
        \frac{\gamma_+}{2}-\varepsilon B_1^2
    \right)
    \geq
    \frac{\varepsilon\gamma_+}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence \(M_{g_h}(y)\succeq0\) throughout \(Y\), and therefore
\(\log g_h\) is convex on \(Y\).

In the negative case, there exists \(\hat y\in Y\) such that
\(\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^2q_h(\hat y))\leq-\gamma_-\). Since the
rank-one term in \(M_{g_h}\) is negative semidefinite,
\[
    \lambda_{\min}\!\left(M_{g_h}(\hat y)\right)
    \leq
    \varepsilon g_h(\hat y)
    \lambda_{\min}\!\left(\nabla^2q_h(\hat y)\right)
    \leq
    -\varepsilon g_h(\hat y)\gamma_-
    <0.
\]
Thus, \(\log g_h\) is not convex on \(Y\).

The exponential-approximation bounds allow \(B_0\), \(B_1\), and
\(\varepsilon\) to be chosen with polynomial encoding length. Since
\(q_h\) is the exponential form of a signomial, so is
\(g_h=1+\varepsilon q_h\). The corresponding signomial can therefore
be constructed in polynomial time while preserving the required
encoding bounds. This gives a reduction from \hyperref[prob:cbgc]{\textsc{CBGC}}{}, and strong
NP-hardness follows.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:auxiliary-exponential}
The function \(\Psi_L\) is positive, globally convex, and globally
log-convex. Writing \(R=\|y\|_\infty\), and letting
\(M_{\Psi_L}\) denote the log-curvature matrix of \(\Psi_L\), the
smallest eigenvalues of its Hessian and log-curvature matrix satisfy
$\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^2\Psi_L(y))
\geq2e^{(L-1)R}$,
$\lambda_{\min}(M_{\Psi_L}(y))\geq4e^{2LR}$.
Moreover, there
exist polynomials \(P_1(n,L)\) and \(P_2(n,L)\) such that
\(\Psi_L(y)\), \(\|\nabla\Psi_L(y)\|_2\), and
\(\|\nabla^2\Psi_L(y)\|_2\) are bounded above by
\(P_1(n,L)e^{(L+1)R}\), while
$\|M_{\Psi_L}(y)\|_2 \leq P_2(n,L)e^{2(L+1)R}$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:global-log-coordinate}
The unconstrained  
  \hyperref[prob:log-coordinate-convexity]{ 
    \textsc{Log-Coordinate-Convexity}}{} problem is strongly NP-hard.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Consider \(Q_L=q+\delta_L\Psi_L\). In the negative alternative,
$\kappa_{[-\frac12,\frac12]^n}(q)\leq-\gamma$.
On \([-\frac12,\frac12]^n\), Lemma~\ref{lem:auxiliary-exponential} gives
$\|\nabla^2\Psi_L\|_2
\leq P_1(n,L)e^{(L+1)/2}$.
We therefore choose \(\delta_L>0\) such that
$\delta_LP_1(n,L)e^{(L+1)/2}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}$.
By the curvature perturbation bound,
$\kappa_{[-\frac12,\frac12]^n}(Q_L)
\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}$,
so \(Q_L\) remains nonconvex.

In the positive alternative,
$\kappa_{[-1,1]^n}(q)\geq\gamma$.
Since \(\Psi_L\) is convex, \(Q_L\) is convex on the unit box. For
\(R=\|y\|_\infty\geq1\), the exponent bound \(D\leq2\) gives
$\|\nabla^2q(y)\|_2\leq C_2e^{DR}$
for a polynomially bounded constant \(C_2\). Lemma~\ref{lem:auxiliary-exponential} and Weyl's inequality then give
\[
\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\nabla^2Q_L(y)\right)
\geq
2\delta_Le^{(L-1)R}-C_2e^{DR}.
\]
The ratio of the auxiliary curvature term to the adverse curvature term is
\(2\delta_L e^{(L-1)R}/(C_2e^{DR})
=(2\delta_L/C_2)e^{(L-1-D)R}\).
                                                                
    
                                            
       
                           
                   
    
When \(L-1>D\), this ratio increases with \(R\). Hence it suffices to make the preceding lower bound nonnegative at \(R=1\).
                           
                                         
                                                                                                        
The quantitative bounds show that an integer \(L=O(\log n)\) and a positive rational \(\delta_L\) of inverse-polynomial magnitude can simultaneously preserve the negative alternative and ensure
$\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\nabla^2Q_L(y)\right)\geq0$
whenever $\|y\|_\infty\geq1$.

Thus, \(Q_L\) is convex on \(\mathbb{R}^n\) in the positive alternative and remains nonconvex in the negative alternative. Since \(Q_L\) is the exponential form of a signomial and \(L\) and \(\delta_L\) have polynomial encoding length, this gives a polynomial-time reduction from \hyperref[prob:gqgc]{\textsc{GQGC}}{}. Strong NP-hardness follows.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:global-log-log-convexity-hardness}
The unconstrained  
  \hyperref[prob:log-log-convexity]{ 
    \textsc{Log-Log-Convexity}}{} problem is strongly NP-hard.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Starting from the same exponential polynomial \(q\), choose
\(\varepsilon>0\) as in the compact log-log reduction and define
$g_0=1+\varepsilon q$.
The choice of \(\varepsilon\) ensures that \(g_0\ge\frac12\) on the unit box in both alternatives. In the positive alternative, \(g_0\) is log-convex there; in the negative alternative, it retains a strict
\(M_{g_0}\)-curvature witness in \([-\frac12,\frac12]^n\). We augment
\(g_0\) by setting
$G_L=g_0+\delta_L\Psi_L$.

Unlike the Hessian, \(M_g=g\nabla^2g-\nabla g\nabla g^\top\) is
nonlinear under addition. For twice continuously differentiable
functions \(u\) and \(v\), define
$\mathcal{C}(u,v)
=
u\nabla^2v+v\nabla^2u
-\nabla u\nabla v^\top
-\nabla v\nabla u^\top$.
A direct expansion gives
$M_{G_L}
=
M_{g_0}
+\delta_L\mathcal{C}(g_0,\Psi_L)
+\delta_L^2M_{\Psi_L}$.
For a fixed \(L\), we first choose \(\delta_L>0\) sufficiently small
that the last two terms preserve the negative eigenvalue of
\(M_{g_0}\) at the interior witness. Hence, \(G_L\) remains non-log-convex
in the negative alternative.
In the positive alternative, both \(g_0\) and \(\Psi_L\) are positive
and log-convex on the unit box. Since positive sums of log-convex
functions are log-convex, \(G_L\) is log-convex there.

Now let \(R=\|y\|_\infty\geq1\). The growth bounds for \(q\), together
with Lemma~\ref{lem:auxiliary-exponential}, give polynomially bounded
constants \(A_0\) and \(A_1\) such that
$\|M_{g_0}(y)\|_2\leq A_0e^{2DR}$,
$\|\mathcal{C}(g_0,\Psi_L)(y)\|_2
\leq A_1e^{(D+L+1)R}$.
Using the lower bound on \(M_{\Psi_L}\), we obtain
\[
\lambda_{\min}\!\left(M_{G_L}(y)\right)
\geq
4\delta_L^2e^{2LR}
-A_0e^{2DR}
-\delta_LA_1e^{(D+L+1)R}.
\]
When \(L>D+1\), the positive term grows faster than both potentially
negative terms. It therefore suffices to make this lower bound
nonnegative at \(R=1\).
The quantitative estimates show that an integer \(L=O(\log n)\) and a positive rational \(\delta_L\) of inverse-polynomial magnitude can be chosen to satisfy this condition while preserving the interior negative-curvature witness.

It remains to establish global positivity in both alternatives. For some polynomially bounded
constant \(C_0\), we have
$G_L(y)

Now suppose the positive alternative holds. On the unit box,
\(g_0\) and \(\Psi_L\) are positive and log-convex,
so their positive sum \(G_L=g_0+\delta_L\Psi_L\) is log-convex there.

For \(R\ge1\), the bounds above and
Lemma~\ref{lem:auxiliary-exponential} give
\[
    M_{G_L}(y)
    \succeq
    \left(
        4\delta_L^2e^{2LR}
        -
        \delta_LK_Le^{(D+L+1)R}
        -
        K_Me^{2DR}
    \right)I_n.
\]

We first compare the positive auxiliary term with the cross term.
At \(R=1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{
        4\delta_L^2e^{2L}
    }{
        \delta_LK_Le^{D+L+1}
    }
    =
    \frac{4\delta_Le^{L-D-1}}{K_L} 
    \ge
    \frac{\nu}{2P_LK_Le^{D+1}}
    \left(\frac{3e}{5}\right)^L 
    >
    \frac{\nu}{42P_LK_L}
    \left(\frac85\right)^L
    \ge2,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(D\le2\), \(e^3<21\), and the last step uses the
cross-term domination condition.

Similarly, comparing the positive auxiliary term with
\(M_{g_0}\) gives, at \(R=1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{
        4\delta_L^2e^{2L}
    }{
        K_Me^{2D}
    }
    \ge
    \frac{\nu^2}{16P_L^2K_Me^{2D}}
    \left(\frac{3e}{5}\right)^{2L} 
    >
    \frac{\nu^2}{880P_L^2K_M}
    \left(\frac{64}{25}\right)^L
    \ge2,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(D\le2\), \(e^4<55\), and the last step uses the
source-curvature domination condition.

Thus, at \(R=1\), the positive auxiliary term is at least twice each
negative contribution. Moreover, \(L\ge4\) and \(D\le2\) imply
\(2L>D+L+1\) and \(2L>2D\), so both ratios increase with \(R\).
Therefore, for every \(R\ge1\),
\[
    4\delta_L^2e^{2LR}
    \ge
    2\delta_LK_Le^{(D+L+1)R},
    \qquad
    4\delta_L^2e^{2LR}
    \ge
    2K_Me^{2DR}.
\]
It follows that \(M_{G_L}(y)\succeq0\) throughout the exterior region.
Combined with log-convexity on the unit box and the global positivity
proved above, \(G_L\) is globally log-convex in the positive
alternative.

Thus, \(G_L\) is positive on \(\mathbb{R}^n\) in both alternatives. In
the positive alternative, \(\log G_L\) is convex on
\(\mathbb{R}^n\); in the negative alternative, the preserved interior
witness shows that \(\log G_L\) is not convex. Since \(G_L\) is the
exponential form of a signomial, the corresponding signomial satisfies
the positivity promise in both alternatives and distinguishes the two
cases of the unconstrained  
  \hyperref[prob:log-log-convexity]{ 
    \textsc{Log-Log-Convexity}}{} problem.

Finally, \(L=O(\log n)\), while
\(\varepsilon\), \(\nu\), and \(\delta_L\) have polynomially bounded
rational data, and only \(4n^2\) auxiliary terms are introduced.
Hence the construction preserves the encoding bounds and strong
NP-hardness.
