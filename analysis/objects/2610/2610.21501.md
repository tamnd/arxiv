<!-- conjecture-1: body_md -->
(Ferroni--Jochemko--Schr\"oter; \cite[Conjecture~6.3]{FerroniJochemkoSchroter2022})
All positroids are Ehrhart positive.
<!-- end conjecture-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Every paving positroid is Ehrhart positive. More precisely, if $M$ is a paving positroid and $d=\dim P(M)$, then
$$\operatorname{ehr}_M(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_jt^j, \qquad c_j>0\quad\text{for }0\leq j\leq d.$$
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:geometry}
Let $M$ be a connected rank-$r$ paving positroid on a cyclically ordered
set $E$ of size $n$, where $2\leq r\leq n-2$. Let
$H_1,\ldots,H_\ell$ be its hyperplanes of size at least $r$. Then
\[
 P(M)=\{x\in\Delta(r,n):x(H_i)\leq r-1
                                  \text{ for }1\leq i\leq\ell\}.
\]
Each $H_i$ is an interval in the same cyclic order, and
\[
 |H_i\cap H_j|\leq r-2\qquad(1\leq i<j\leq\ell).
\]
The regions $x(H_i)>r-1$ inside $\Delta(r,n)$ are pairwise disjoint.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
If $r_M(A)\leq r-2$ and $A$ were dependent, a basis of $A$ together
with one additional element would contain a circuit of size at most
$r_M(A)+1<r$. Hence such an $A$ is independent, and its rank inequality
follows from $x_e\leq1$. A set of rank $r-1$ is contained in a
hyperplane. A hyperplane of size $r-1$ gives another consequence of
the coordinate bounds, whereas the remaining hyperplanes are precisely
$H_1,\ldots,H_\ell$. The inequality for a spanning set follows from
nonnegativity and $x(E)=r$. The rank description therefore gives the
displayed description of $P(M)$.

Every $(r-1)$-set is independent and its closure is its unique
containing hyperplane. Distinct hyperplanes therefore intersect
in at most $r-2$ elements.

For each $H_i$, the restriction is $U_{r-1,|H_i|}$ and the contraction
is $U_{1,n-|H_i|}$. Connectedness implies $n-|H_i|\geq2$: a singleton
complement would be a coloop. The face $x(H_i)=r-1$ is the product of
the two uniform base polytopes and has dimension
$(|H_i|-1)+(n-|H_i|-1)=n-2$.
It is therefore a facet. The cyclic interval description of $P(M)$
must include an inequality supporting this facet. Its outward normal,
modulo the normal to $x(E)=r$, gives
\[
 \mathbf1_{H_i}=a\mathbf1_J+b\mathbf1_E
 \qquad\text{with }a>0
\]
for a proper nonempty cyclic interval $J$. The two values on the
right are $b$ and $a+b$; they must be $0$ and $1$ in that order.
Thus $a=1$, $b=0$, and $H_i=J$.

Finally, for distinct $H_i,H_j$ and $x\in\Delta(r,n)$,
\[
 x(H_i)+x(H_j)
 =x(H_i\cup H_j)+x(H_i\cap H_j)
 \leq r+|H_i\cap H_j|\leq2r-2.
\]
The two sums cannot both exceed $r-1$. 
The same disjoint cap geometry appears in the paving Ehrhart formulas of
\cite[Proposition~5.10]{HanelyEtAl2023}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:four-element}
Let $M$ be the column matroid of
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1&1&1&0\\
  0&0&1&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The ordered $2\times2$ minors are all $1$ except the zero minor on
$\{1,2\}$. Thus $M$ is a loopless rank-two positroid, hence paving,
with bases all two-element subsets except $\{1,2\}$. The circuits
$\{1,2\}$ and $\{1,3,4\}$ force every element into the same
direct summand, so $M$ is connected. Its only dependent hyperplane is
$H=\{1,2\}$, so \cref{lem:geometry} gives
\[
 P(M)=\{x\in\Delta(2,4):x_1+x_2\leq1\}.
\]

Take $e=4$, with nonroot coordinates $1,2,3$ in this order.
For integers $t\geq1$ and $0\leq a\leq t$, set $s=x_1+x_2$.
The equations $x_4=a$ and $x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4=2t$ give
$x_3=2t-a-s$. The bound $0\leq x_3\leq t$ and the hyperplane
constraint give $t-a\leq s\leq t$.
For each such $s$, there are $s+1$ choices for $(x_1,x_2)$, and
their upper bounds follow from $s\leq t$. Hence
\[
 F_e(a,t)=\sum_{s=t-a}^{t}(s+1)
        =(a+1)(t+1)-\frac{a(a+1)}2.
\]

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:root-summation}
For $G(t,X)=\sum_{s=0}^{d}h_s(t)X^s$, one has
\[
 \sum_{s=0}^{d}h_s(t)\binom{t+s+1}{s+1}
       =G(t,1)+t\int_0^1\Gamma_v(G)\,dv.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Differentiation with respect to $v$ gives the formal identity
\[
 \frac{\partial}{\partial v}(1-\lambda(1-v))^t
       =t\lambda(1-\lambda(1-v))^{t-1}.
\]
Integrating coefficientwise over $0\leq v\leq1$ yields
\[
 \frac1{1-\lambda}
 +t\int_0^1
       \frac{(1-\lambda(1-v))^{t-1}}{(1-\lambda)^{t+1}}\,dv
 =\frac{(1-\lambda)^{-t-1}-1}{\lambda}.
\]
The numerator on the right has zero constant term, so the quotient
is a formal power series. Its coefficient of $\lambda^s$ is
$\binom{t+s+1}{s+1}$. On the left this coefficient is
$1+t\int_0^1\Gamma_v(X^s)\,dv$.
Multiplication by $h_s(t)$ and summation prove the formula.
All extracted coefficients are polynomials, so the identity also
includes $t=0$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:resolvent}
For $0\leq u<v\leq B$ and an integer $s\geq0$, the integral kernel
of $D^{s+1}$ satisfies
\[
 \frac{K_{D^{s+1}}(u,v)}t=[\lambda^s]R_\lambda(u,v),
 \qquad
 R_\lambda(u,v)=
 \frac{\exp\!\left(t\lambda\int_u^v
                         \frac{dw}{1-\lambda\varphi(w)}\right)}
      {(1-\lambda\varphi(u))(1-\lambda\varphi(v))}.
\]
The assigned values of $\varphi$ at $u$ and $v$ occur in the two
denominator factors, independently of the integral in the numerator.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The identity has zero integral kernel. Composition with $D$ gives,
for every integer $j\geq0$ and $u<v$,
\[
 K_{D^{j+1}}(u,v)=\varphi(u)K_{D^j}(u,v)
       +t\varphi(v)^j+t\int_u^v K_{D^j}(w,v)\,dw.
\]
The middle term integrates the evaluation term of $D^j$; the last
comes from reversing two integrations of a bounded piecewise continuous
integrand on a finite triangle. This recurrence fixes the kernel at every $u<v$,
including assigned endpoint values.

Fix a nonzero real $t$. Induction in the recurrence gives
$|K_{D^j}(u,v)|\leq j|t|(1+|t|B)^{j-1}$ for $j\geq1$, uniformly
on $0\leq u<v\leq B$. Hence, for real
$0<|\lambda|<(1+|t|B)^{-1}$,
$\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda^jK_{D^j}(u,v)$ converges uniformly.
Multiplying the recurrence by $\lambda^{j+1}$ and summing over
$j\geq0$ shows that this series, divided by $\lambda t$, satisfies
\[
 R_\lambda(u,v)=
 \frac{1}{(1-\lambda\varphi(u))(1-\lambda\varphi(v))}
 +\frac{\lambda t}{1-\lambda\varphi(u)}
                  \int_u^v R_\lambda(w,v)\,dw.
\]
For fixed $v$, the continuous function
$Y(u)=\int_u^v R_\lambda(w,v)\,dw$ satisfies, on each open partition
interval,
\[
 Y'(u)+\frac{\lambda t}{1-\lambda\varphi(u)}Y(u)
   =-\frac{1}{(1-\lambda\varphi(u))(1-\lambda\varphi(v))},
 \qquad Y(v)=0.
\]
The integrating factor
$\exp(\int_0^u\lambda t/(1-\lambda\varphi(w))\,dw)$ gives
\[
 \frac{1}{1-\lambda\varphi(v)}+\lambda tY(u)
 =\frac{\exp(\lambda t\int_u^v
                     dw/(1-\lambda\varphi(w)))}
        {1-\lambda\varphi(v)}.
\]
Continuity of $Y$ joins the solutions at partition points.
Substitution into the integral equation gives the stated $R_\lambda$,
with the assigned endpoint values. Comparing coefficients of
$\lambda^{s+1}$ proves the identity for nonzero real $t$.
Each coefficient identity is polynomial in $t$, so it holds for
formal $t$, including $t=0$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A function $m:\mathbb R\longrightarrow[0,1]$ is called
\emph{period-uniform} if it has period one, is affine on the open
intervals of a finite partition of each period, and satisfies
$\int_0^1 f(m(u))\,du=\int_0^1 f(v)\,dv$
for every continuous function $f$ on $[0,1]$. Values at the partition
points may be assigned independently. On locally bounded functions
which are piecewise continuous with
finitely many pieces on each compact interval and vanish sufficiently
far to the right, define
\[
 (\mathcal E g)(u)=m(u)g(u)+t\int_u^\infty g(v)\,dv,
 \qquad (Sg)(u)=g(u+1).
\]
The integral is finite because $g$ vanishes to the right.
The operator for one bounded coordinate is
$\mathcal A=\mathcal E+(I-\mathcal E)S$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:coordinate-kernels}
For every integer $q\geq1$ and $u<v$ with $v-u\notin\mathbb Z$, we have 
\[
 \frac{K_{\mathcal A^q}(u,v)}t
 =\sum_{h=1}^{q}c_{q,h}(u,v)t^{h-1},
 \qquad c_{q,h}(u,v)\geq0\quad(1\leq h\leq q).
\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Terms without integrated jumps evaluate at integer translates of $u$,
so do not contribute at a noninteger displacement. Fix a term with
$h$ integrated jumps and $j$ unit jumps, where $1\leq h\leq q$.
It is $t^h$ times an integral over $(0,1)^h$ with integrand
$\omega(x_1,\ldots,x_h)g(u+j+\sum_{i=1}^{h}x_i)$, where
$\omega\in[0,1]$ is the product of frozen weights.

For $h\geq2$, the final-point equation gives
$x_h=v-u-j-\sum_{i=1}^{h-1}x_i$. The remaining variables range over
the polytope $\Delta_{h,j}(u,v)$ defined by $0<x_i<1$ for
$1\leq i\leq h-1$ and
$0<v-u-j-\sum_{i=1}^{h-1}x_i<1$.
The change from $x_h$ to $v$ has derivative one, giving the kernel
contribution
\[
 t^h\int_{\Delta_{h,j}(u,v)}
 \omega\!\left(x_1,\ldots,x_{h-1},
                  v-u-j-\sum_{i=1}^{h-1}x_i\right)
 \,dx_1\cdots dx_{h-1}.
\]
For $h=1$, it is $t\omega(v-u-j)$ when $0<v-u-j<1$, and zero
otherwise. Assigned weights at the initial, final, and frozen
intermediate points are retained.

Each intermediate point is affine in the remaining variables.
Constant points retain their assigned $m$-values; at nonconstant
points, the affine expression for $m$ changes only on finitely
many proper affine hyperplanes. The integrands are bounded and
piecewise polynomial on polytopes, hence Riemann integrable, also
as successive integrals.

Each contribution is $t^h$ times a nonnegative quantity. Summing
over terms with $h$ integrated jumps defines $c_{q,h}(u,v)$;
division by $t$ proves the formula.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:kernel-compatibility}
Consider finite evaluation-and-integral expansions formed from
$\mathcal E,S,\mathcal A$, including restrictions of coordinate blocks by
linear inequalities on their jumps. Allow also evaluation terms
$a(t,u)g(u+j)$, where $j$ is a nonnegative integer and $a$ is a
polynomial in $t$ whose coefficients are piecewise polynomial in
$u$, with finitely many pieces on each compact interval and
specified values at partition points.

Suppose that two representations of each factor have the same
coefficient of every integer-translate evaluation at every
starting point, and have the same integral kernel at every
noninteger displacement. Their finite compositions then have the
same evaluation coefficients and the same integral kernels at
every noninteger total displacement.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
All calculations are coefficientwise in $t$ and involve finitely many
powers. Fix $u$ and $g$ in the operator domain, and choose $B>u$ with $g=0$ on
$[B,\infty)$. All jumps are nonnegative, so a contributing term
has total jump at most $B-u$. This bounds every integrated jump
and truncates all integrals in a finite composition, including
those defining $\mathcal E$. Intermediate outputs also vanish sufficiently
far to the right.

The jump variables now range over finite polytopes. Arguments of
profiles and evaluation coefficients are affine: nonconstant arguments
meet partition points on finitely many proper affine hyperplanes,
and constant arguments retain their specified values. Coefficient
integrands are therefore piecewise polynomial on finite polyhedral
partitions. Multiplying by $g$ at the final point gives bounded
piecewise continuous integrands. This remains true after fixing any
subset of variables, with assigned values retained at constant arguments.
Thus the multiple integrals and all their sections are Riemann
integrable, allowing successive integrations to be reversed. At fixed
initial and final points, the same reasoning applies after eliminating
the last integrated jump.

It suffices to consider two factors, written as
\[
 (Tg)(u)=\sum_{i=0}^{I}a_i(t,u)g(u+i)
                  +\int_u^\infty K_T(u,z)g(z)\,dz
\]
and
\[
 (Ug)(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}b_j(t,u)g(u+j)
                  +\int_u^\infty K_U(u,z)g(z)\,dz.
\]
Kernels are set to zero outside their forward support.
The evaluation part of $TU$ is
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{I}\sum_{j=0}^{J}
       a_i(t,u)b_j(t,u+i)g(u+i+j).
\]
An evaluation before an integral shifts its starting point to $u+i$;
an evaluation after an integral uses $v=z+j$, with derivative one.
Two integrals range over $u\leq z\leq v$. Thus a representative of
the composed kernel is
\begin{align*}
K_{TU}(u,v)=\sum_{i=0}^{I}a_i(t,u)K_U(u+i,v)+\sum_{j=0}^{J}K_T(u,v-j)b_j(t,v-j)+\int_u^vK_T(u,z)K_U(z,v)\,dz.
\end{align*}
This formula also represents the grouped jump expansion at noninteger
total displacement. If both groups contain integrated jumps, replace
their last integrated jumps by $z$ and $v$, respectively. Both changes
have derivative one; reversing the remaining finite Riemann integrals
gives the last term. Changes of kernel convention at integer block
displacements affect only finitely many intermediate $z$ and leave
this integral unchanged. If just one group contains an integrated jump,
the first or second sum results. Its displacement is noninteger because
the other group's displacement is integral. Terms without integration
give the displayed evaluation part.

Fix $u<v$ with $v-u\notin\mathbb Z$. Each supported kernel in the two
sums has noninteger displacement, since $i,j$ are integers. Thus
the sums agree for the two representations, with evaluation
coefficients taken at their specified points. In the integral, the
integrands agree except possibly where $z-u$ or $v-z$ is an integer.
There are finitely many such points in $[u,v]$, so the integrals agree.
Evaluation coefficients agree pointwise as well. Induction proves the
claim for finite compositions.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:transfer}
Let $r\geq1$, $b\geq1$, $0\leq j\leq r-1$, and
$0\leq\delta\leq b-1$ be integers. For the complementary profile just constructed,
\[
 \Gamma_{w,\alpha,\beta}
       \bigl(f_b((r-j)t-\delta,X)\bigr)
 =\frac{K_{S^j\mathcal E^{b-\delta}(\mathcal E-I)^\delta}(0,r-L)}t.
\]
Moreover, for $t\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}$ and $a\in\{0,\ldots,t\}$, we have 
\[
 [z^{rt-a}]\frac{z^{jt+\delta}}{(1-z)^b}
       =\binom{(r-j)t-a-\delta+b-1}{b-1}.
\]
The right side is interpreted as a binomial polynomial, including
when the remaining exponent is negative.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For $T=(r-j)t-\delta$, the coefficient formula above is
$[\lambda^{b-1}](1-\lambda)^\delta R_\lambda(j,r-L)$.
Since $\delta\leq b-1$, every exponent $b-\delta+\ell$ below
is at least one. The binomial expansion and \cref{lem:resolvent} therefore give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{K_{\mathcal E^{b-\delta}(\mathcal E-I)^\delta}(j,r-L)}t
 &=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\delta}(-1)^{\delta-\ell}\binom{\delta}{\ell}
       \frac{K_{\mathcal E^{b-\delta+\ell}}(j,r-L)}t\\
 &=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\delta}(-1)^{\delta-\ell}\binom{\delta}{\ell}
       [\lambda^{b-\delta+\ell-1}]R_\lambda(j,r-L)\\
 &=[\lambda^{b-1}](1-\lambda)^\delta R_\lambda(j,r-L).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The shift $S^j$ changes the starting point from $0$ to $j$, which
proves the kernel identity.

The remaining counting exponent is $N=(r-j)t-a-\delta$.
For $N\geq0$, extracting the coefficient of $(1-z)^{-b}$ gives
the stated binomial coefficient. For $N<0$,
$N\geq(r-j-1)t-\delta\geq-\delta\geq-(b-1)$, so
$0\leq N+b-1<b-1$. Both the binomial polynomial and the
extracted coefficient are then zero, including at small positive $t$.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:upper-discrete}
The block generating polynomial satisfies
\[
 B_{p,k}(t,z)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^j\binom pj
                   \frac{z^{j(t+1)}}{(1-z)^p}
       -\frac{z^{kt+1}}{1-z}
                   G_{p,k}\!\left(t,-\frac z{1-z}\right).
\]
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Through degree $kt$, the first sum counts bounded compositions:
terms with at least $k$ coordinates exceeding $t$ begin in degree
$k(t+1)>kt$. For $v\geq0$, its coefficient in degree $kt+1+v$ is
$F_{p,k}(-v-1,t)$. The binomial expansion of $F_{p,k}$ gives
\[
 F_{p,k}(-v-1,t)=\sum_{s=0}^{p-1}h_s(t)(-1)^s\binom vs,
 \qquad G_{p,k}(t,X)=\sum_{s=0}^{p-1}h_s(t)X^s.
\]
Here $\binom{-v-1+s}{s}=(-1)^s\binom vs$, so the tail is
\[
 \sum_{v=0}^{\infty}F_{p,k}(-v-1,t)z^v
 =\sum_{s=0}^{p-1}h_s(t)(-1)^s\frac{z^s}{(1-z)^{s+1}}
 =\frac1{1-z}G_{p,k}\!\left(t,-\frac z{1-z}\right).
\]
The upper binomial arguments in the tail are at least $p-j\geq1$
(the minimum occurs at $v=0$, $a=-1$), including at small dilations.
Subtracting the tail proves the identity.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:upper-operator}
The integral kernel of $\mathcal B_{p,k}$ at noninteger block jumps and
its integer evaluation terms agree with the expansion of $\mathcal A^p$
restricted to block jumps at most $k$, with the additional evaluation term
$Q_{p,k}(t,m(u))g(u+k)$, where
\[
 Q_{p,k}(t,x)=(1-x)G_{p,k}(t,1-x)-\binom pk(1-x)^kx^{p-k}.
\]
Every coefficient in $t$ of $Q_{p,k}(t,x)$ is nonnegative for
$0\leq x\leq1$. A restriction retains eligible zero and unit steps
and imposes the block inequality on each integration domain.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
\emph{Kernel cancellation and evaluations below $k$.}
Below jump $k$, the last term of $\mathcal B_{p,k}$ vanishes, and its first
sum contains exactly the terms of $\mathcal A^p$ that can reach the jump.
This includes the evaluation weights at $0,\ldots,k-1$.

For cancellation above $k$, the coefficient formula for $f_p$ gives,
for any polynomial parameter $T$,
\[
 (I-\mathcal E)f_p(T,I-\mathcal E)
 =[\lambda^{p-1}](1-\lambda)^{-T-1}
                  (I-\mathcal E)(I-\lambda\mathcal E)^{-1}.
\]
The integral kernel of the expression on the right, divided by
$t$, is $-[\lambda^{p-1}](1-\lambda)^{-T}R_\lambda(u,v)$.
Indeed, the kernel of $(I-\lambda\mathcal E)^{-1}$ is
$\lambda tR_\lambda$, whereas that of
$\mathcal E(I-\lambda\mathcal E)^{-1}$ is $tR_\lambda$.

Suppose $v-u>k$. For $0\leq j\leq k-1$, the kernel of
$S^j\mathcal E^{p-j}(I-\mathcal E)^j$, divided by $t$, is
\[
 [\lambda^{p-1}](-1)^j(1-\lambda)^jR_\lambda(u+j,v).
\]
The arcs $[u+j,v]$ and $[u+k,v]$ differ by $k-j$ full periods, so
$R_\lambda(u+j,v)=(1-\lambda)^{-(k-j)t}R_\lambda(u+k,v)$.
The $j$-th term of the first sum therefore has kernel divided by $t$ equal to
$\binom pj[\lambda^{p-1}](-1)^j
(1-\lambda)^{j-(k-j)t}R_\lambda(u+k,v)$.
The $j$-th term of $S^k(I-\mathcal E)G_{p,k}(t,I-\mathcal E)$
has the negative of this expression, by the preceding kernel identity
with $T=(k-j)t-j$. Thus the kernel above $k$ is zero.

\smallskip\noindent
\emph{The evaluation at jump $k$.}
The first sum has shifts smaller than $k$, so the evaluation weight
at $k$ is the multiplication part of the last term,
$(1-m(u))G_{p,k}(t,1-m(u))$. In $\mathcal A^p$, exactly $k$ unit steps and
$p-k$ zero steps give the old weight
$\binom pk(1-m(u))^km(u)^{p-k}$, since periodicity makes all endpoint
values equal to $m(u)$. Their difference is $Q_{p,k}(t,m(u))$.
Neither $\mathcal B_{p,k}$ nor the restricted expansion of $\mathcal A^p$
has evaluation terms above $k$.
\cref{lem:kernel-compatibility} permits composition with external
coordinate operators at noninteger total displacement.

\smallskip\noindent
\emph{Coefficients of the correction.}
The subtracted evaluation weight has degree zero in $t$, so only
the constant coefficient requires a comparison. Set $s=1-x$ with
$0<s<1$. In \cref{lem:uniform-circle}, choose $w(u)=\varepsilon$,
$\alpha=s$, and $\beta=0$, where $0<\varepsilon<1$.
The total jump is $k-\varepsilon$; the complementary profile has
endpoints $x,1$ and
$\int_0^{1-\varepsilon}g(m(u))\,du
=(1-\varepsilon)\int_0^1g(y)\,dy$.
The operator $\mathcal A$ below depends on this profile.
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the finite moments defining $\Gamma$
tend to zero, while the endpoints remain fixed. Consequently,
\[
 sG_{p,k}(t,s)
   =s\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
                        \frac{K_{\mathcal A^p}(0,k-\varepsilon)}t.
\]
The coefficients on the right are nonnegative, so all positive-degree
coefficients of $sG_{p,k}(t,s)$ are nonnegative.

For the constant coefficient, the contributing paths before division
by $t$ have one continuous step and $k-1$ unit steps.
The continuous jump must be $1-\varepsilon$. If this step has
index $i$, all unit steps must occur before it, since a later unit
step has weight $1-1=0$. There are $\binom{i-1}{k-1}$ choices for
these earlier unit steps. Every zero step before the continuous step has weight $x=1-s$,
every earlier unit step has weight $s$, and every zero step after
it has weight $1$. Including the factor $s$ therefore gives
\[
 [t^0]\bigl(sG_{p,k}(t,s)\bigr)
   =s^k\sum_{i=k}^{p}\binom{i-1}{k-1}(1-s)^{i-k}.
\]
This counts length-$p$ binary strings with at least $k$ ones,
weighted by $s$ for a one and $1-s$ for a zero: $i$ is the position
of the $k$-th one, and the suffix has total weight
$(s+(1-s))^{p-i}=1$. Grouping by the number of ones gives
\[
 [t^0]\bigl(sG_{p,k}(t,s)\bigr)
       =\sum_{j=k}^{p}\binom pj s^j(1-s)^{p-j}.
\]
Subtracting the term with exactly $k$ ones yields
\[
 [t^0]Q_{p,k}(t,x)
       =\sum_{j=k+1}^{p}\binom pj(1-x)^jx^{p-j}\geq0,
\]
with an empty sum when $k=p$. This proves all coefficients are
nonnegative for $0<x<1$; polynomial continuity gives $x=0,1$.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:rooted}
The rooted count $F_e(a,t)$ is represented on its integer triangle by

 Q_{p,r-1}(t,m(u))g(u+r-1),&e\notin H_i,\quad p=|H_i|,\\

 \end{cases}

to $\Gamma_{w,\alpha,\beta}(G_e)$, preserving nonnegativity.

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:faces}
Let $M$ be connected on $\{e,1,\ldots,q\}$, where $q\geq1$,
and let $f=1$.
There are nested sets
\[
 \{e,f\}=S_1\subset S_2\subset\cdots\subset S_q=E,
 \qquad |S_h|=h+1,
\]
and, for each $h=1,\ldots,q$, a coordinate face of $P(M)$ whose
unfixed coordinates are $S_h$ and form a connected matroid base
polytope. For every $0<v<1$,
the slice $x_e=v$ of each such face has a nonempty relatively open
part with all other unfixed coordinates in $(0,1)$, of dimension $h-1$.
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
\emph{Coordinate faces and connected minors.}
Choose a circuit containing $e,f$ and extend the independent set
obtained by removing $f$ to a basis $B$. This basis avoids $f$,
and the chosen circuit is the unique circuit in $B\cup\{f\}$.
Thus $ef$ is an edge of the connected fundamental graph.
Starting with this edge and successively adding adjacent vertices
gives the sets $S_h$ with connected induced subgraphs.

The coordinate face
\[
 x_b=1\quad(b\in B\setminus S_h),\qquad
 x_g=0\quad(g\in(E\setminus B)\setminus S_h)
\]
contains $\mathbf1_B$. Removing its fixed coordinates identifies
it with $P(N_h)$, where $N_h=(M/(B\setminus S_h))|S_h$.
For $b\in B\cap S_h$ and $g\in S_h\setminus B$,
\begin{align*}
(B\cap S_h)\setminus\{b\}\cup\{g\}\text{ is a basis of }N_h\quad \Longleftrightarrow\quad B\setminus\{b\}\cup\{g\}\text{ is a basis of }M.
\end{align*}
Indeed, the contraction basis correspondence
\cite[Corollary~3.1.8]{Oxley2011} gives the equivalence before
deletion. The basis $B\cap S_h$ survives deletion, preserving
rank, and the exchanged set contains no deleted element.
The fundamental graph of $N_h$ is therefore the connected induced
graph on $S_h$. Hence $N_h$ is connected, $\dim P(N_h)=h$,
and its affine hull is $x(S_h)=r(N_h)$.

\smallskip\noindent
\emph{Root slices.}
Let $\bar x$ be the average of all basis indicators of $N_h$.
A supporting functional maximal at $\bar x$ is maximal at every
basis indicator, hence constant on $P(N_h)$; thus $\bar x$ is
in its relative interior. Since $N_h$ is connected and has at least two elements, it has
neither loops nor coloops; every coordinate of $\bar x$ lies in $(0,1)$.
Set $v_0=\bar x_e$ and choose bases $D_0,D_1$ with
$e\notin D_0$ and $e\in D_1$. For $0<v<1$, set
\[
 x(v)=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{v}{v_0}\bar x+
       \left(1-\dfrac{v}{v_0}\right)\mathbf1_{D_0},
                                      &0<v<v_0,\\[3pt]
 \bar x,                             &v=v_0,\\[3pt]
 \dfrac{1-v}{1-v_0}\bar x+
       \dfrac{v-v_0}{1-v_0}\mathbf1_{D_1},
                                      &v_0<v<1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then $x_e(v)=v$. The positive coefficient of $\bar x$ keeps
$x(v)$ in the relative interior with all coordinates in $(0,1)$.
Since $x_e$ is nonconstant on the $h$-dimensional affine hull,
its level set has dimension $h-1$. A relative neighborhood of
$x(v)$ in that level set remains in the polytope and in $(0,1)^{S_h}$.
Restoring the fixed coordinates gives the required root slice;
for $h=1$ it is a point.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:all-degrees}
Let $M$ be the connected paving positroid in \cref{lem:rooted}. For every $0<v<1$, each coefficient of
$\Gamma_v(G_e)$ in degrees $0,\ldots,q-1$ is positive.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
\emph{Allowed paths and integer prefixes.}
Use \eqref{eq:root-profile} and fix $1\leq h\leq q$. \cref{lem:faces} gives a coordinate face with $h$ unfixed
nonroot coordinates, including $f=1$. Write their jumps in order
as $y_1,\ldots,y_h$, and let $j$ count the frozen unit jumps.
Their root slice satisfies
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{h}y_k=r-v-j,\qquad
 0<y_k<1\quad(1\leq k\leq h).
\]
Choose the integration term at these coordinates and the zero or
unit evaluation at every fixed coordinate. All points of this
slice satisfy the hyperplane inequalities, so this path term is
allowed there. Connectedness of the minor makes the displayed
sum its only affine equation.

The first step is continuous. Every frozen step therefore starts
at an integer plus a nonempty prefix $\sum_{k=1}^{a}y_k$.
For $a=h$ this is $r-v-j\notin\mathbb Z$. For $1\leq a<h$,
increasing a prefix coordinate and decreasing one outside it shows
that the prefix is nonconstant on the fixed-sum affine hull.
Since it lies in $(0,a)$, the conditions that it be integral form
finitely many proper affine hyperplanes. For $h=1$ there are
no proper nonempty prefixes.

\smallskip\noindent
\emph{A positive integral for $h\geq2$.}
These hyperplanes cannot cover the relatively open slice: a line
with direction parallel to none of them meets each at most once,
so a short segment in the slice contains a point outside them all.
At this point every frozen step starts at a noninteger, and both
weights $m(u)$ and $1-m(u)$ are positive.

Eliminate the last continuous coordinate by
$y_h=r-v-j-\sum_{k=1}^{h-1}y_k$. Its coefficient in the total-jump
equation is $1$, so changing to the final point has derivative $1$.
Let $\Omega\subseteq(0,1)^{h-1}$ be the allowed domain of the
remaining integration variables and $\omega$ the product of the
frozen weights after elimination. Projection onto these variables
is an affine bijection from the fixed-sum affine hull to
$\mathbb R^{h-1}$, so the relatively open root slice projects to an open
subset of $\Omega$. Only these continuous coordinates are integrated;
the evaluation factors fix the remaining coordinates.

A sufficiently small closed box
$Q=\prod_{k=1}^{h-1}[a_k,b_k]$ about the chosen point lies in
$\Omega$, has $a_k<b_k$, and avoids the exceptional hyperplanes.
The frozen weights are continuous and positive on $Q$, so
$\omega\geq\eta>0$ there. Hence
\[
 \int_{\Omega}\omega(y_1,\ldots,y_{h-1})
                         \,dy_1\cdots dy_{h-1}
 \geq\eta\prod_{k=1}^{h-1}(b_k-a_k)>0.
\]
With no frozen steps the product is $1$. Thus the selected term
has a positive coefficient of $t^h$ in the allowed kernel.

\smallskip\noindent
\emph{One continuous step and the coefficients.}
For $h=1$, the continuous step is $f=1$ and
$y_1=r-v-j\in(0,1)$ forces $j=r-1$, $y_1=1-v$.
Every later starting point has fractional part $1-v$.
The $q-r$ frozen zero jumps have weight $v$ and the $r-1$
frozen unit jumps have weight $1-v$, giving the positive
coefficient $v^{q-r}(1-v)^{r-1}$ of $t$.

For each $h$, division by $t$ gives a positive contribution in
degree $h-1$. All other allowed terms and boundary corrections
have nonnegative coefficients by \cref{prop:kernel-positive}. Therefore
\[
 [t^{h-1}]\Gamma_v(G_e)>0\qquad(1\leq h\leq q).
\]
This completes the proof.

<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
The empty-ground-set and singleton cases have point base polytopes
and Ehrhart polynomial $1$. Suppose $M$ is connected with at least
two elements. In rank one or corank one it is uniform, so
\cite[Theorem~1.2]{Ferroni2021} applies. Otherwise
$2\leq r\leq n-2$ and $q=n-1$.
For $1\leq j\leq q$, \cref{prop:all-degrees} makes
$[t^{j-1}]\Gamma_v(G_e)$ positive on $(0,1)$.
This coefficient is polynomial in $v$ and hence continuous;
it has a positive lower bound on some closed interval in $(0,1)$,
so its integral is positive. \cref{prop:closing},
including nonnegativity of the local term, gives
$[t^j]\operatorname{ehr}_M(t)>0$. Ehrhart's theorem gives $\operatorname{ehr}_M(0)=1$,
and connectedness gives $\dim P(M)=q$.

For general $M$, its connected components $M_1,\ldots,M_c$ are
positroids in the induced orders, being restrictions
\cite[Proposition~3.5]{ARW2016}. Every circuit of $M_i$ is a
circuit of $M$, of size at least $r(M)\geq r(M_i)$; hence each
$M_i$ is paving. Their Cartesian product decomposition gives
\[
 \operatorname{ehr}_M(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{c}\operatorname{ehr}_{M_i}(t),
 \qquad \dim P(M)=\sum_{i=1}^{c}\dim P(M_i).
\]
Each factor has positive coefficients throughout its degree range.
Every integer between zero and the sum of these degrees is a sum
of integers from the respective ranges. Its coefficient in the
product therefore contains a positive product, and all remaining
summands are nonnegative.
