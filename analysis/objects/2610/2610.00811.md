Note that hyperbolicity of the system follows directly from symmetrizability, which is proven in the next section.
The eigenvalue structure is simple. We have, for example, in 1D two positive and two negative simple eigenvalues
and  in 2D a double zero eigenvalue, a double positive and negative  as well as  a simple positive and negative  eigenvalue,

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Comparing the hyperbolic main part of our system to the one of the  linear Grad moment system 	\cite{struchtrup2003regularization, sarna2020convergence}, we observe, that they are equivalent in 1D, but not the same in higher dimensions. 
In contrast to the linear Grad approach the present approach is not a full expansion in terms of Hermite polynomials to the corresponding order. Instead, by neglecting the term $\epsilon (I-P)(v g)$, we have  neglected terms in the Hermite expansion. 
In particular, this results in missing terms in equations (\ref{relax4}) and (\ref{relax5}) in higher dimensions.
In the following we show analytically and numerically,  that the system is still appropriate as a relaxation system for the incompressible Navier-Stokes limit, for appropriately small values of the scaling parameter $\epsilon$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
An IMEX RK method is said to be of type A, if the matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{s \times s}$ is invertible, or equivalently $a_{ii} \neq 0$, $i = 1,\dots,s$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
An IMEX RK method is said to be of type CK, if the matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{s \times s}$ can be written as
	\begin{align*}A=\left(
		\begin{matrix}
			0 & 0 \\ a & \hat{A}
		\end{matrix}\right)
	\end{align*}
	with $a = (a_{21},\dots,a_{s1})^T \in \mathbb{R}^{(s-1)}$ and the submatrix $\hat{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{(s-1) \times (s-1)}$ invertible. If additionally $a = 0$, the scheme is said to be of type ARS.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
An IMEX RK method is called implicitly stiffly accurate (ISA), if the corresponding DIRK method is stiffly accurate, which means that
	\begin{align*}
		a_{si} = w_i, \qquad i = 1,\dots,s.
	\end{align*}
	If additionally the explicit method satisfies
	\begin{align}
		\tilde{a}_{si} = \tilde{w}_i, \qquad i = 1,\dots,s,
	\end{align}
	the IMEX RK method is called globally stiffly accurate (GSA).
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:5_1}
	If the IMEX method is of type A (of type CK/ARS) and satisfies the GSA property (and the initial velocity field is divergence free), then in the fluid dynamic limit $\varepsilon \rightarrow 0$ the IMEX RK scheme \eqref{InternalStages} becomes a consistent discretization for the incompressible Navier Stokes equations.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
To establish the result, we closely follow the framework developed in \cite[Thm 4.4 and Thm 4.5]{2ndPaper}. 

The moment reduction mechanism ensures that as $\epsilon$ vanishes, higher-order kinetic moments are systematically slaved to the macroscopic variables through the dominant relaxation terms, yielding the correct macroscopic limit equations.

<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
\label{rem:ap_properties}
When interpreting Theorem~\ref{thm:5_1}, it is essential to maintain a strict conceptual distinction between:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item the \emph{structural stability} of the continuous and semi-discrete systems,
    \item the \emph{formal continuous limit} as $\epsilon \to 0$, and
    \item the \emph{time-semidiscrete AP property} ensuring consistency at the discrete level.
\end{enumerate}
Furthermore, while the scheme achieves \emph{high-order} accuracy, assertions regarding uniform numerical stability are strictly bounded by the established CFL and dissipation constraints, avoiding generalized claims of unconditional uniform stability.

<!-- remark-10: body_md -->
On the one hand, the CPU time for the BGK simulation \cite{TKR} obviously is proportional to the number $N_v$ of velocity discretization points. 
Moreover, at a fixed spatial discretization and fixed final time $t_{final}$, the computational cost for this conventional CFL-limited solver is inversely proportional to $\epsilon$ due to the restrictive CFL condition \eqref{CFL}.
On the other hand, the computation of the relaxation model, using the above-described AP scheme, is independent of $\epsilon$ and obviously does not depend on $N_v$.

For the comparison of the CPU times, we have performed simulations of the BGK equation and the relaxation model for $\tau= 0.01$, using a total number of $1000$ spatial grid points and a final time $t_{final} = 0.01$. 
For the conventional BGK model at $\epsilon=1$, we considered a CFL number of $0.1$, which corresponds to a time step $\Delta t = 10^{-5}$, taking $N_v = 20$ velocity points. The same time discretization has been used for the relaxation system for comparison.
As shown in Table \ref{tab1}, for these parameters the CPU times for the relaxation system and the BGK equation are essentially the same (101 s vs. 110 s).

However, for smaller values of $\epsilon$, the computation times for the BGK equation become increasingly longer. 
According to Table \ref{tab1}, decreasing $\epsilon$ from $1$ to $0.1$ increases the BGK computation time from 110 seconds to 19 minutes (a factor of roughly 10.4). Decreasing $\epsilon$ further to $0.05$ results in a CPU time of 38.8 minutes (a factor of roughly 21.2). 
This behavior perfectly reflects the expected inverse proportionality $O(1/\epsilon)$.
The same linear increase in cost would apply if the velocity discretization were changed from $N_v=20$ to $N_v=200$.

Ultimately, for small values of $\epsilon$ or large values of $N_v$, the proposed AP scheme leads to a considerable speedup for the relaxation model compared to the conventional CFL-limited solver \cite{TKR}. We emphasize that this speedup claim applies specifically to the standard explicit discretization; no timing comparison is made here with implicit AP BGK solvers such as \cite{2ndPaper}. 
The simulations were performed on macOS 26.5 with an Apple M5 Pro processor and 48 GB of memory.
