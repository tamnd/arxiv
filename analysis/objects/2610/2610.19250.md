\label{thm:core_certificate}
Let $M$ and $b$ satisfy~\eqref{eq:bounds}, let $(z,u)$ be a planned ROM trajectory--input pair with values in $\mathcal Z\times\mathcal U$ on $[t_0,t_1]$, and let $x$ solve the FOM~\eqref{eq:fom} on $[t_0,t_1]$ under the same input $u$ and any disturbance with $\|d(t)\|\leq\bar d$, with the errors $e_\parallel,e_\perp$ defined by~\eqref{eq:error_definitions}. Let $\rho$ solve the comparison system~\eqref{eq:rho_dynamics} with $\rho_\parallel(t_0)\geq\|e_\parallel(t_0)\|$ and $\rho_\perp(t_0)\geq\|e_\perp(t_0)\|$, and suppose that $\rho<\bar\rho$ on $[t_0,t_1]$. Then $\|e_\parallel\|\leq\rho_\parallel$ and $\|e_\perp\|\leq\rho_\perp$ on $[t_0,t_1]$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Throughout, we omit the time argument and the $(z,u)$-dependence. We first show that, for $(e_\parallel,e_\perp)\in\mathcal E_{\bar\rho}$,
\begin{equation}
    D^+\!\begin{bmatrix}\|e_\parallel\|\\\|e_\perp\|\end{bmatrix}
    \leq M\begin{bmatrix}\|e_\parallel\|\\\|e_\perp\|\end{bmatrix}+b,
    \label{eq:core_bound_split}
\end{equation}
with $D^+$ acting componentwise. For $e_\parallel\neq0$, \eqref{eq:exact_eparallel} and the upper Dini derivative of the Euclidean norm give
\begin{align*}
D^+\|e_\parallel\|
&=\frac{e_\parallel^\top}{\|e_\parallel\|}
\bigl[g_\parallel(e_\parallel,e_\perp)+Pd\bigr]\\
&\leq
\frac{e_\parallel^\top}{\|e_\parallel\|}
\bigl[g_\parallel(e_\parallel,0)-g_\parallel(0,0)\bigr] +
\|g_\parallel(0,0)\|\\
&\quad+
\|g_\parallel(e_\parallel,e_\perp)-g_\parallel(e_\parallel,0)\|+\|Pd\|\\
&\leq
\mu_\parallel\|e_\parallel\|
+\gamma_{\parallel\perp}\|e_\perp\|
+b_\parallel,
\end{align*}
where the first inequality follows from telescoping $g_\parallel(e_\parallel,e_\perp)$ around $(e_\parallel,0)$ and $(0,0)$ and from Cauchy--Schwarz (except the first term, which keeps its sign) and the second from~\eqref{eq:bounds} and $\|d\|\leq\bar d$.
The case $e_\parallel=0$ follows from $D^+\|e_\parallel\|\leq\|\dot e_\parallel\|$. A similar argument applied to~\eqref{eq:exact_eperp} gives the second row of~\eqref{eq:core_bound_split}. 
Since $M$ is Metzler, the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:rho_dynamics} is quasimonotone. Hence, the comparison theorem for systems of differential inequalities~\cite[Cor.~1.7.1]{lakshmikantham1969differential} gives $\|e_\parallel\|\leq\rho_\parallel$ and $\|e_\perp\|\leq\rho_\perp$ as long as $(e_\parallel,e_\perp)\in\mathcal E_{\bar\rho}$, which $Pe_\perp=0$ from~\eqref{eq:exact_state_decomposition}, these bounds, and $\rho<\bar\rho$ ensure on $[t_0,t_1]$ by a continuation argument.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:radius}
Let $M(z,u,\rho)$ and $b(z,u,\rho)$ be continuous, locally Lipschitz and componentwise nondecreasing in $\rho$, and satisfy~\eqref{eq:bounds} on $\mathcal E_\rho$ for every $\rho\geq0$. Then Theorem~\ref{thm:core_certificate} holds for $\dot\rho=M(z,u,\rho)\rho+b(z,u,\rho)$ without the condition $\rho<\bar\rho$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $v:=(\|e_\parallel\|,\|e_\perp\|)$. Since $(e_\parallel,e_\perp)\in\mathcal E_v$, the derivation of~\eqref{eq:core_bound_split} with the bounds evaluated at $\rho=v$ gives $D^+v\leq G(t,v):=M(z,u,v)v+b(z,u,v)$ at every $t$. For $0\leq w\leq w'$ with $w_i=w_i'$, the monotonicity of $M$ and $b$ and $\gamma\geq0$ give $G_i(t,w)\leq G_i(t,w')$, so $G$ is quasimonotone nondecreasing and locally Lipschitz in $w$, and the comparison theorem~\cite[Cor.~1.7.1]{lakshmikantham1969differential} yields $v\leq\rho$ on $[t_0,t_1]$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:M}
While Theorem~\ref{thm:core_certificate} applies to general matrices $M$ constructed according to~\eqref{eq:bounds}, the stability of the growth of the radii depends on whether $M$ admits a Hurwitz constant bound. For $M(z,u)\leq \bar M$ and $b(z,u)\leq\bar b$ on $\mathcal Z\times\mathcal U$, $\bar M$ is Hurwitz iff $\bar\mu_\parallel,\bar\mu_\perp<0$ and $\bar\mu_\parallel\bar\mu_\perp>\bar\gamma_{\parallel\perp}\bar\gamma_{\perp\parallel}$. When $\bar M$ is Hurwitz, $\rho$ is ultimately bounded by $-\bar M^{-1}\bar b$. Negative $\bar\mu_\parallel,\bar\mu_\perp$ require stable error dynamics and a suitable norm or change of coordinates (footnote~\ref{fn:norm}).
As we see in the numerical example (Sec.~\ref{sec:burgers}), 
CoRE-MPC can also be applied when $M$ is not Hurwitz, since re-centering on the measurement can reset $\rho_\parallel$ at every update. The carried transverse radius then stays bounded if, over one sampling interval, its own contraction outweighs the growth it receives through the coupling (thus, a negative $\mu_\perp$ is necessary but not sufficient). 
For invariant manifolds~\cite{haller2016ssm}, $Q(\zeta)F(\Phi(\zeta),0)=0$, the autonomous flow never leaves the manifold, so $\gamma_{\perp\parallel}(z,0)=0$ and $M(z,0)$ is upper triangular. Thus, $M(z,0)$ is Hurwitz iff $\mu_\parallel,\mu_\perp<0$. This is the setting of RN-ROMPC~\cite{alora2023rompc}. In contrast, for general POD projections (Section~\ref{sec:burgers}), the general structure of $M$ proposed in CoRE-MPC is required.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:core_terminal}
There exist a closed terminal set
$\mathcal X_f\subset\mathcal Z\times[0,\bar\rho)$ and a continuous terminal law
$\kappa_f:\mathcal X_f\to\mathcal U$ such that, for every $\chi=(z,\rho)\in\mathcal X_f$,
\begin{align}
&\widehat h_j(z,\rho)\leq0,
\quad j=1,\ldots,n_h,
\label{eq:terminal_constraints}\\
&\mathcal X_f\text{ is positively invariant under }
  \dot\chi=f_{\rm C}(\chi,\kappa_f(\chi)).
\label{eq:terminal_invariance}
\end{align}
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:core_mpc}
Let $M$ and $b$ satisfy~\eqref{eq:bounds}, let $c_j^\parallel,c_j^\perp$ satisfy~\eqref{eq:tightening_constants}, let Assumption~\ref{ass:core_terminal} hold,
let $\sigma_0\geq\|e_\perp(0)\|$, and suppose that the CoRE-MPC problem~\eqref{eq:core_mpc_ocp} is feasible at $t_0=0$. Then, under
Algorithm~\ref{alg:core}, \eqref{eq:core_mpc_ocp} is feasible at every
$t_k$ and the closed-loop FOM satisfies
\begin{equation}
    u(t)\in\mathcal U,\qquad h(x(t))\leq0,\qquad t\geq0.
    \label{eq:fom_constraint_guarantee}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The argument is that of~\cite[Thm.~1]{alora2023rompc}, with the coupled
comparison system~\eqref{eq:rho_dynamics} in place of two one-way coupled scalar
tubes. \emph{Constraint satisfaction:}
If~\eqref{eq:core_mpc_ocp} is feasible at $t_k$ with
$\sigma_k\geq\|e_\perp(t_k)\|$, then the initial-tube constraint~\eqref{eq:core_mpc_initial} gives
$(e_\parallel(t_k),e_\perp(t_k))\in\mathcal E_{\rho_k^\star(0)}$, the domain constraint~\eqref{eq:core_mpc_domain} keeps the
certificate valid, and Theorem~\ref{thm:core_certificate} gives
$(e_\parallel(t_k+\tau),e_\perp(t_k+\tau))\in\mathcal E_{\rho_k^\star(\tau)}$ for $\tau\in[0,\delta]$.
The tightened constraints~\eqref{eq:core_mpc_tightening} then imply
$h(x)\leq0$ there by~\eqref{eq:affine_tightening}, and the input constraint in~\eqref{eq:core_mpc_domain} gives $u\in\mathcal U$. \emph{Recursive feasibility:} The same
containment at $\tau=\delta$ is the initial
condition~\eqref{eq:core_mpc_initial} for the shifted plan
$(\chi_k^\star(\cdot+\delta),u_k^\star(\cdot+\delta))$ at $t_{k+1}$, and
appending $\kappa_f$ on $[T-\delta,T]$ keeps $\chi$ in $\mathcal X_f$
by invariance~\eqref{eq:terminal_invariance}. Induction over $k$ completes the proof.

<!-- head-2: body_md -->
We consider the oscillator chain from~\cite{jain2022invariant} with $40$ masses connected by nonlinear springs and linear dampers, resulting in $N=80$ states (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}(a)). A single actuator with the spatial shape of the first linear mode drives the chain, and a bounded force disturbance acts on the last mass. Every displacement is constrained, $|q_i|\leq q_{\max}$, and the reference requests a first-mode peak one third above this limit, so the constraint is active. The ROM has $r=2$ states on a spectral submanifold~\cite{haller2016ssm}, which is invariant for the autonomous FOM, so the assumption of RN-ROMPC holds. The horizon is $2$~s and the sampling time $100$~ms. The bounds of CoRE-MPC and RN-ROMPC are obtained from samples on the same domain. Those of CoRE-MPC include the covering margins of footnote~\ref{fn:cover}, so~\eqref{eq:bounds} holds on all of $\mathcal Z\times\mathcal U\times\mathcal E_{\bar\rho}$. CoRE-MPC further imposes the terminal set~\eqref{eq:core_mpc_terminal}. The augmented terminal region uses a continuous-time, two-radius adaptation of the construction in~\cite[Prop.~6]{kohler2021adaptive}. Its numerical definition and its admissibility and invariance certificate, \eqref{eq:terminal_constraints} and~\eqref{eq:terminal_invariance}, are provided with the accompanying code. We test $48$ initial conditions for $10$~s each, $16$ of which were generated to stress the constraint handling of the controllers.
<!-- end head-2 -->
<!-- head-3: body_md -->
Fig.~\ref{fig:chain} shows the case with the largest nominal violation and the statistics over all cases. Nominal ROM-MPC violates the displacement limit in $23$ of $48$ cases, by up to $4\%$ of $q_{\max}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}(b)). In contrast, all robust controllers satisfy the constraints in all cases, and the realized errors remain inside the predicted tubes throughout, in accordance with Theorem~\ref{thm:core_certificate}. The excess closed-loop cost over FOM-MPC (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}(c)) increases with the conservatism of the bounds, from $35\%$ for CoRE-MPC to $49\%$ for Constant CoRE and $149\%$ for RN-ROMPC. Constant CoRE and RN-ROMPC replace the state- and input-dependent bounds by constants, so their tubes grow at the worst-case rate along the entire plan. Consequently, RN-ROMPC requires a large conservative backoff from the constraints, while CoRE-MPC can operate close to them (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}(b)). Linear ROMPC is infeasible in all $48$ cases, since bounding the nonlinear spring force as a disturbance is too conservative. In computation (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}(d)), CoRE-MPC solves $61$ times faster than FOM-MPC at $2.9$ times the solve time of nominal ROM-MPC, with a 95th percentile of $10.6$~ms against the $100$~ms sampling time. Constant CoRE and RN-ROMPC, which also propagate two radii but omit the terminal set and use simpler bounds, solve in about $80\%$ of the time of CoRE-MPC. Thus, even where the assumption of RN-ROMPC holds, CoRE-MPC incurs less than a quarter of its excess closed-loop cost at comparable computation.

<!-- head-4: body_md -->
We consider the viscous Burgers equation on the unit interval with viscosity $\nu=0.06$, discretized on $128$ nodes and driven by three Gaussian actuators (Fig.~\ref{fig:burgers}(a)). A six-mode POD--Galerkin ROM~\cite{kunisch1999burgers} captures $99.998\%$ of the snapshot energy, and its approximation manifold is not invariant. The magnitude of the field is bounded by $w_{\max}$ at every node, while the reference peaks $54\%$ above this limit. The horizon is $0.3$~s and the sampling time $100$~ms. A bounded, spatially localized sinusoidal disturbance acts on the field, and its $24$ equally spaced phases define the $24$ cases, each run for $8$~s from rest. The FOM dynamics are quadratic in the state and the lift is linear, so the Jacobian blocks and residuals are polynomial in $(z,u,e)$ and the norm inequalities give closed-form upper bounds satisfying~\eqref{eq:bounds} on $\mathcal E_\rho$ for every $\rho\geq0$ instead of on a fixed $\mathcal E_{\bar\rho}$, affine in $\rho$ with nonnegative coefficients. The comparison system~\eqref{eq:rho_dynamics} becomes $\dot\rho=M(z,u,\rho)\rho+b(z,u,\rho)$, to which Theorem~\ref{thm:core_certificate} extends by Corollary~\ref{cor:radius}.
<!-- end head-4 -->
<!-- head-5: body_md -->
Fig.~\ref{fig:burgers} shows the case with the largest nominal violation and the solve times. Despite the small discarded snapshot energy, nominal ROM-MPC violates the limit in all $24$ cases, by up to $0.9\%$ of $w_{\max}$ and for most of the run (Fig.~\ref{fig:burgers}(b)). CoRE-MPC and FOM-MPC complete all cases without violation and converge to a similar steady state at about $0.90\,w_{\max}$. Along the reference, both couplings and the transverse residual are nonzero, so the coupled structure of~\eqref{eq:rho_dynamics} is required, and $M$ is not Hurwitz. CoRE-MPC nevertheless remains feasible in every case: with the free initialization~\eqref{eq:core_mpc_initial}, the optimizer selects $\rho_\parallel(0)=0$ at every update after the first, so the tangential radius restarts, and the carried transverse radius stays bounded (the gain from $\sigma_k$ to $\sigma_{k+1}$ is at most $0.73$ over all intervals, cf. Remark~\ref{rem:M}). The certificate is valid and tight: every tube contains the realized errors, which reach $63\%$ of the transverse and $98\%$ of the tangential radius within the applied intervals over all cases (Fig.~\ref{fig:burgers}(c)), and along the evaluated trajectories the coupling bounds exceed the exact Jacobian block norms by factors of at most $1.15$ and $1.38$. CoRE-MPC incurs $27\%$ higher closed-loop cost than FOM-MPC and solves $13$ times faster, at $1.5$ times the solve time of nominal ROM-MPC, with a 95th percentile of $12.3$~ms against the $100$~ms sampling time (Fig.~\ref{fig:burgers}(d)).

We consider the cable-driven diamond robot of~\cite{goury2018mor}, simulated in SOFA~\cite{faure2012sofa} with $9{,}768$ states, $1{,}628$ nodes, and four cable forces between $0$ and $3$~N. The task is to track an elliptical tip reference that extends beyond a wall (Fig.~\ref{fig:fem}(a)), so it cannot be tracked exactly on its upper arc, while every node must stay clear of the wall. The ROM has $r=2$ states on a spectral submanifold of the FEM~\cite{haller2016ssm}, whose lifting reconstructs all nodes. The horizon is $60$~ms and the sampling time $20$~ms. Each controller runs the task for $3.8$~s without disturbance. Since the FOM is too large to sample the block Jacobian, an input-dependent $M$ and a state- and input-dependent $b$ are fitted to the prediction errors of ten earlier rollouts on the same robot and wall with nearby elliptical references (Section~\ref{sec:core_cert}), and the bounds of RN-ROMPC and Constant CoRE are fitted to the same data, so all three are empirical. The weighting of the error norm is optimized on the residuals of the calibration rollouts to minimize the wall tightening over all nodes (footnote~\ref{fn:norm}). Only the nodes with the largest predicted wall envelope enter the optimization, and every plan is checked against all $1{,}628$ nodes, which CoRE-MPC never violates.
<!-- end head-6 -->
<!-- head-7: body_md -->
The clearance in Fig.~\ref{fig:fem}(c) summarizes the outcome: nominal ROM-MPC penetrates the wall by $13$~mm, whereas the three robust controllers keep every node clear, with minimum clearances of $3.7$~mm for CoRE-MPC, $5.4$~mm for Constant CoRE, and $0.5$~mm for RN-ROMPC. RN-ROMPC~\cite{alora2023rompc}, with its constant upper-triangular $M$, backs away from the wall along the upper arc yet comes closest to it. Averaged over matched prediction stages with identical initial radii, the wall tightening of RN-ROMPC is $2.3$ times that of CoRE-MPC ($15.3$ against $6.6$~mm). CoRE-MPC, whose $M$ and $b$ follow the plan, tracks with $29\%$ less error than Constant CoRE and $38\%$ less than RN-ROMPC (Fig.~\ref{fig:fem}(b)), and the lower error of nominal ROM-MPC is obtained on a trajectory that penetrates the wall. In computation (Fig.~\ref{fig:fem}(d)), all four controllers solve in under $2$~ms at the median, CoRE-MPC in $0.9$~ms against $0.55$~ms for nominal ROM-MPC and never above $5$~ms, whereas two RN-ROMPC updates exceed the $20$~ms period. Full-order MPC is excluded, since one FEM horizon prediction alone takes $0.56$~s, or $28$ sampling periods.
