<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:renewal-model}
 Let
$\{N_{n,j}\}_{j\geq1}$ be independent, identically distributed positive
integer-valued random variables, independent of the codeword
and the BSC noise.
                                                     
                                                     
For every integer $k\geq 0$, let $S_{n,k}$ denote the cumulative
length of the first $k$ $N_{n,j}$'s with $S_{n,0}=0$, and let $K_n$
be the first index at which the cumulative length reaches or
exceeds~$n$, i.e.,                          
\begin{align*}
 S_{n,k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}N_{n,j}
\mbox{ and }
 K_n=\min\{k\geq 1:S_{n,k}\geq n\}.
\end{align*}
  The codeword is divided into $K_n$ fragments,
where $1\leq K_n\leq n$, and the length $L_{n,j}$ of the
$j$th fragment, for $j\in[K_n]\triangleq\{1,\ldots,K_n\}$, is
\begin{align*}
 L_{n,j}&=
 \begin{cases}
 N_{n,j}, & 1\leq j<K_n,\\
 n-S_{n,K_n-1}, & j=K_n.
 \end{cases}
\end{align*}
In particular, the last fragment is truncated if
$S_{n,K_n}>n$, and $\sum_{j=1}^{K_n}L_{n,j}=n$.
The breaks occur after bits $S_{n,j}$ for
$j\in[K_n-1]$.
Write $\mathbf{T}=(T_1,\ldots,T_{n-1})$ for the \emph{cut pattern},
where $T_i=1$ when a break occurs after bit $i$ and $T_i=0$
otherwise.
For each $j\in[K_n]$, the $j$th fragment occupies positions
$S_{n,j-1}+1,\ldots,\min\{S_{n,j},n\}$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:nsc}
The \emph{noisy shuffling channel} (NSC) is the noisy TPC in which
every sampled length equals the same deterministic positive
integer~$\ell_n$, that is, $N_{n,j}=\ell_n$ for every~$j$, where
$\ell_n$ divides~$n$. The codeword is then divided into
$K_n=n/\ell_n$ consecutive fragments of length~$\ell_n$, no fragment
is truncated, and the decoder observes the unordered multiset $\mathcal{Y}$ of
the noisy fragments. Codes, rates, and capacity for the NSC are
defined as for the noisy TPC, with codeword lengths~$n$ divisible
by~$\ell_n$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Other than the NSC (Definition~\ref{def:nsc}), the noisy TPC includes several additional special cases obtained by
specifying the distribution from which each fragment length is sampled. Geometric sampled lengths provide the fragmentation
rule of the torn paper channel
in~\cite{shomorony2021torn} (termed \emph{independent-cut} torn paper channel herein), where each boundary between
consecutive bits is cut independently with probability
$q_n$. 
Together with the BSC noise, this yields the
independent-cut torn paper channel with substitution errors.
Another special case, that is similar to~\cite{barlev}, is obtained by sampling successive
lengths independently and uniformly from
$\{a_n,a_n+1,\ldots,b_n\}$, where
$1\leq a_n\leq b_n$ are integers.  
These cases differ in their fragment-length distributions,
while retaining the same BSC substitution errors and an unordered multiset of noisy fragments as the decoder observation.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{remark:assumptions}
                                                                                         
Let $\ell_n=\mathbb{E}[N_{n,1}]$ and assume that there
is a fixed constant $\alpha\in(0,\infty)$ such that
\begin{align}
\ell_n&=\mathbb{E}[N_{n,1}],
&
\frac{\log n}{\ell_n}
&\longrightarrow\alpha\in(0,\infty),
&
\mathbb{E}[N_{n,1}^2]&=\Theta((\log n)^2)
\qquad \text{when}\ n\to\infty.
\label{eq:renewal-assumptions}
\end{align}
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:renewal-capacity}
Consider the noisy TPC with substitution probability
$p\in(0,1/2)$, and let $\kappa=1-h_2(p)$, where $h_2(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)$ is the binary entropy
function.
Under Assumption~\ref{remark:assumptions}, we have that
\begin{equation}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 \sup_{P_{\mathbf{X}}}I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})
 \le\limsup_{n\to\infty}r_n(\kappa).
 \label{eq:renewal-converse}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:capacity-limit}
Under the assumptions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:renewal-capacity},
if $\lim_{n\to\infty}r_n(\kappa)$ exists, then
the capacity of the noisy TPC is $C=\lim_{n\to\infty}r_n(\kappa)$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fano's inequality implies that every rate achievable
with vanishing average decoding error as $n\to\infty$
is at most the limiting upper bound on mutual
information per codeword bit. 
Therefore, Theorem~\ref{thm:renewal-capacity} implies that
\begin{align*}
C
&\leq
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
\sup_{P_{\mathbf{X}}}I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})
\overset{\eqref{eq:renewal-converse}}{\leq}
\lim_{n\to\infty}r_n(\kappa).
\end{align*}
For the converse inequality, the random-coding
argument in~\cite[Section~III-A,
eqs.~(45)--(47)]{ravi2024recovering}
establishes achievability
of every nonnegative rate strictly below
$\lim_{n\to\infty}r_n(\kappa)$.
Combining the two inequalities proves the claim.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:geometric}
Consider the independent-cut torn paper channel with substitution errors, with
break probability~$q_n$ satisfying
$q_n\log n\to\alpha\in(0,\infty)$ and substitution probability
$p\in(0,1/2)$. Let $C(\alpha,p)$ denote its capacity.
Theorem~\ref{thm:renewal-capacity}, together with the achievable
rate in~\cite[Theorem~3]{ravi2024recovering}, implies that
\begin{equation}
 C(\alpha,p)=\kappa e^{-\alpha/\kappa}.
 \label{eq:geometric-capacity}
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:nsc}
Consider the NSC with substitution probability $p\in(0,1/2)$. Under Assumption~\ref{remark:assumptions}, we have
\begin{equation}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sup_{P_{\mathbf{X}}}I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})\leq(1-h_2(p)-\alpha)^+.
 \label{eq:nsc-converse}
\end{equation}
Together with the achievable rate of~\cite{shomorony2019capacity},
it follows that the capacity of the NSC is $(1-h_2(p)-\alpha)^+$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{definition:Csh}
For integers $m\geq1$ and $k\geq1$, let $\mathsf{C}_p(m,k)$ be the
supremum of $I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})$ over all joint distributions of
the ordered tuple $\mathbf{X}=(\mathbf{X}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{X}_m)$, where
$\mathbf{X}_j\in\{0,1\}^k$ for every $j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$, and where $\mathcal{Y}$ is the unordered multiset obtained by applying
the BSC to these strings with independent substitution noise across all $mk$ bits.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The quantity $\mathsf{C}_p(m,k)$ is the largest mutual information between the
input and the output of the NSC (Definition~\ref{def:nsc}) with $m$
fragments of length~$k$.
The capacity of the NSC is instead an asymptotic quantity per codeword
bit: it concerns $\mathsf{C}_p(K_n,\ell_n)/n$ as $n\to\infty$, with $\ell_n=\Theta(\log n)$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:nsc} evaluates it.
In contrast, the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:renewal-capacity} need not follow this scaling on fragment length, so we need bounds on $\mathsf{C}_p(m,k)$ that
hold for every $m$ and $k$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:uniform}
Fix $\epsilon>0$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$, and define
\begin{equation*}
 b=\frac{1-\delta}{\kappa+\epsilon},
 \qquad\lambda=\kappa b\in(0,1).
\end{equation*}
There is a constant $c_2=c_2(p,\epsilon,\delta)$ such that
for every $m,L\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf{C}_p(m,L)
 \le m(\kappa L-\lambda\log m)^+
       +c_2m+2b\,m^{1-\delta}\log(m+1).
 \label{eq:uniform-shuffling}
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:sets}
Let $\mathbf{T}=(T_1,\ldots,T_{n-1})$ be the cut pattern from Definition~\ref{def:renewal-model}.
Write $\mathbf{t}=(t_1,\ldots,t_{n-1})\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}$
for a fixed possible value of $\mathbf{T}$.
Fix $\mathbf{t}$ with $\Pr(\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})>0$, and let
$m_L$ be the
number of fragments of length $L$ and
$\mathcal{L}=\{L\in[n]:m_L>0\}$ as defined in Lemma~\ref{lem:partition-counts} in Appendix~\ref{app:bernoulli}.
Then, for every distribution of $\mathbf{X}$,
\begin{align}
I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y}\mid\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
&\leq\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\mathsf{C}_p(m_L,L).
\label{eq:set-information}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For each $L\in\mathcal{L}$, let $\mathcal{Y}_L$ be the multiset of
noisy fragments of length $L$. List the distinct
lengths as $L_1<\cdots<L_{|\mathcal{L}|}$.
Separating $\mathcal{Y}$ by fragment length provides the tuple
$(\mathcal{Y}_L)_{L\in\mathcal{L}}$, and combining its entries
recovers~$\mathcal{Y}$. Therefore, 
\begin{align}
I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y}\mid\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
&=H((\mathcal{Y}_L)_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\mid\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
-H((\mathcal{Y}_L)_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\mid\mathbf{X},\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
\notag\\
&\overset{(a)}{=}
H((\mathcal{Y}_L)_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\mid\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
-\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}H(\mathcal{Y}_L\mid\mathbf{X},\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
\notag\\
&\overset{(b)}{=}
\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{L}|}
H(\mathcal{Y}_{L_i}\mid
\mathcal{Y}_{L_1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{L_{i-1}},\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
-\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{L}|}
H(\mathcal{Y}_{L_i}\mid\mathbf{X},\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
\notag\\
&\overset{(c)}{\leq}
\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{L}|}
\left[
H(\mathcal{Y}_{L_i}\mid\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
-H(\mathcal{Y}_{L_i}\mid\mathbf{X},\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
\right]
\notag\\
&=\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}
I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y}_L\mid\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
\overset{(d)}{\leq}
\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\mathsf{C}_p(m_L,L),
\notag
\end{align}
where $(a)$ follows from the conditional independence
of the multisets $\mathcal{Y}_L$: once the codeword and cut
locations are fixed, each $\mathcal{Y}_L$ depends only on
the BSC noise affecting the fragments of length $L$.
Fragments of different lengths occupy disjoint bit
positions, so their independent noise makes the
conditional entropies add exactly.
Equality~$(b)$ follows from the entropy chain rule.
Inequality~$(c)$ removes the conditioning
on the earlier multisets, which can only increase
each entropy.
Inequality~$(d)$ follows from
Definition~\ref{definition:Csh}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:partition}
Suppose there exists a constant~$c_1>0$ such that
$\Pr(K_n\leq c_1n/\log n)\to1$ as $n\to\infty$. 
Then
\begin{align}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
\sup_{P_{\mathbf{X}}}I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})
&\leq
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
\mathbb{E}\left[
\sum_{j=1}^{K_n}(\kappa L_{n,j}-\log n)^+
\right].
\label{eq:partition-converse}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Suppose the decoder knows
the exact cut locations $\mathbf{T}$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:sets}, in addition to $\mathcal{Y}$.
Then
\begin{align}
I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})
&\leq I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y},\mathbf{T})
=I(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{T})+I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y}\mid\mathbf{T})
\overset{(\dagger)}{=}
I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y}\mid\mathbf{T}),
\label{eq:reveal-cuts}
\end{align}
where $(\dagger)$ follows from the
independence of $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{T}$.

Fix $\epsilon>0$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$, and use the
constants $b,\lambda,c_2$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform}.
We first consider fixed cut locations
$\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t}$ with $K_n\leq c_1n/\log n$
(in calculations conditional on $\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t}$,
$K_n$ and $L_{n,j}$ refer to  fixed values
determined by $\mathbf{t}$), for which we have,
\begin{align}
I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y}\mid\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
&\overset{\eqref{eq:set-information}}{\leq}
\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\mathsf{C}_p(m_L,L)
\notag\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:uniform-shuffling}}{\leq}
\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L(\kappa L-\lambda\log m_L)^+
+c_2K_n
+2b\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L^{1-\delta}\log(m_L+1)
\notag\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:counts-power}}{\leq}
\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L(\kappa L-\lambda\log m_L)^+
+c_2K_n
+2b\log(n+1)(2n)^{\delta/2}K_n^{1-\delta}
\notag\\
&\overset{(\dagger)}{\leq}
\sum_{j=1}^{K_n}(\kappa L_{n,j}-\lambda\log n)^+
+\lambda\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L\log\frac{n}{m_L}
+c_2K_n
+2b\log(n+1)(2n)^{\delta/2}K_n^{1-\delta}
\notag\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:counts-entropy}}{\leq}
\sum_{j=1}^{K_n}(\kappa L_{n,j}-\lambda\log n)^+
+(c_2+\lambda\log e)K_n
+2\lambda K_n\log\frac{n}{K_n}
+2b\log(n+1)(2n)^{\delta/2}K_n^{1-\delta},
\label{eq:replace-length-logarithms}
\end{align}
where $(\dagger)$ follows since
$\kappa L-\lambda\log m_L
=\kappa L-\lambda\log n+\lambda\log(n/m_L)$
and $(a+d)^+\leq a^++d$ for $d\geq0$.

We next bound the last three terms in~\eqref{eq:replace-length-logarithms},
divided by~$n$, one at a time.
Since $K_n/n\leq c_1/\log n<1/e$ for a sufficiently large~$n$,
we have,
\begin{align*}
\frac{(c_2+\lambda\log e)K_n}{n}
\leq\frac{c_1(c_2+\lambda\log e)}{\log n}.
\end{align*}
For the second term, we cannot bound
$\log(n/K_n)$ by itself; instead we bound the product
$K_n\log(n/K_n)$.
The function $x\mapsto x\log(n/x)$ is nondecreasing on $(0,n/e]$.
Thus, we have
\begin{align*}
\frac{2\lambda K_n\log(n/K_n)}{n}
\leq\frac{2\lambda}{n}\cdot\frac{c_1n}{\log n}
\log\left(\frac{n}{c_1n/\log n}\right)
=\frac{2\lambda c_1\log(\log n/c_1)}{\log n}.
\end{align*}
For the third term, we use $K_n^{1-\delta}\leq(c_1n/\log n)^{1-\delta}$,
which holds since $1-\delta>0$:
\begin{align*}
\frac{2b\log(n+1)(2n)^{\delta/2}K_n^{1-\delta}}{n}
\leq\frac{2b\log(n+1)\,2^{\delta/2}n^{\delta/2}\,c_1^{1-\delta}n^{1-\delta}}
{n(\log n)^{1-\delta}}
=\frac{2^{1+\delta/2}bc_1^{1-\delta}\log(n+1)}
{n^{\delta/2}(\log n)^{1-\delta}}.
\end{align*}
Adding the three bounds shows that the last three terms
in~\eqref{eq:replace-length-logarithms}, divided by~$n$, are at most
\begin{align}
\frac{c_1(c_2+\lambda\log e)}{\log n}
+\frac{2\lambda c_1\log(\log n/c_1)}{\log n}
+\frac{2^{1+\delta/2}bc_1^{1-\delta}\log(n+1)}
{n^{\delta/2}(\log n)^{1-\delta}}
=:e_n.
\notag
\end{align}
Each of the three terms defining $e_n$ tends to
zero as $n\to\infty$, so $e_n\to0$ as $n\to\infty$.


Since~$\lambda\in(0,1)$ (see Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform}), it follows that
\begin{align}
I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y}\mid\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
&\overset{\eqref{eq:replace-length-logarithms}}{\leq}
\sum_{j=1}^{K_n}(\kappa L_{n,j}-\lambda\log n)^+
+ne_n
\notag\\
&\overset{(\dagger)}{\leq}
\sum_{j=1}^{K_n}(\kappa L_{n,j}-\log n)^+
+(1-\lambda)K_n\log n+ne_n
\notag\\
&\leq
\sum_{j=1}^{K_n}(\kappa L_{n,j}-\log n)^+
+(1-\lambda)c_1n+ne_n,
\label{eq:partition-small-fragment-number}
\end{align}
where $(\dagger)$ follows since
$\kappa L_{n,j}-\lambda\log n
=\kappa L_{n,j}-\log n+(1-\lambda)\log n$ and since~$(a+d)^+\le a^++d$ for every real~$a$ and every~$d\geq0$; applying this with~$d=(1-\lambda)\log n$ to each of the~$K_n$ terms and summing over~$j$ produces the term~$(1-\lambda)K_n\log n$.




We now average over all cut locations to get the final bound for
$I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})$.
\begin{align}
\frac1n I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})
&\overset{\eqref{eq:reveal-cuts}}{\leq}
\frac1n I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y}\mid\mathbf{T})\overset{(\dagger)}{=}
\frac1n\sum_{\mathbf{t}}
\Pr(\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y}\mid\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t})
\notag\\

\frac1n\sum_{\mathbf{t}}
\Pr(\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t},\,K_n\leq c_1n/\log n)

\notag\\
&\quad+
\frac1n\sum_{\mathbf{t}}
\Pr(\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t},\,K_n>c_1n/\log n)
I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y}\mid\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t}),
\label{eq:split-cut-patterns}
\end{align}
where $(\dagger)$ follows from the definition of conditional
mutual information, and $(\dagger\dagger)$ follows from the law of

For the remaining cut patterns, we use the trivial bound

Given $\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{t}$, the number of fragments $K_n$ and the lengths

\notag\\

\label{eq:partition-final-finite}
\end{align}

$\Pr(K_n\leq c_1n/\log n)\leq1$.

\left\{

\frac{

\notag\\

Inequality~$(c)$ follows from~\eqref{eq:convergence of mean}.

-\frac12\left(\frac{c_1}{\alpha}-1\right)

\right]
\notag\\

\notag\\
&\overset{(\dagger)}{\leq}
r_n(\kappa)\left(

\right)

We first verify that the independent-cut torn paper channel
satisfies the fragment-length assumptions in
Assumption~\ref{remark:assumptions}.
Ravi, Vahid, and Shomorony describe the original torn paper
channel in~\cite[Section~I]{ravi2024recovering} as the special
case in which the fragment lengths are geometrically distributed: every possible cut
between two consecutive bits occurs independently with probability
$q_n$.
So, we have
$\Pr(N_{n,1}=j)=q_n(1-q_n)^{j-1},\ j\geq1,
$
and
$\ell_n=\mathbb{E}[N_{n,1}]=\frac1{q_n},\mathbb{E}[N_{n,1}^2]=\frac{2-q_n}{q_n^2}.$
The assumption $q_n\log n\to\alpha\in(0,\infty)$ provides
$\frac{\log n}{\ell_n}=q_n\log n\longrightarrow\alpha$ when $n\to\infty$.
Moreover, $q_n\log n\to\alpha$ when $n\to\infty$ implies
$q_n^{-1}=\Theta(\log n)$, and hence
$\mathbb{E}[N_{n,1}^2]=\frac{2-q_n}{q_n^2}=\Theta((\log n)^2)$ when $n\to\infty$.
Thus, the independent-cut torn paper channel satisfies
\eqref{eq:renewal-assumptions}.

Next, we prove an explicit bound for $r_n(\kappa)$.
\begin{align}
r_n(\kappa)
&\overset{\eqref{eq:reward-rate}}{=}
q_n\mathbb{E}[(\kappa N_{n,1}-\log n)^+]
=\kappa q_n\mathbb{E}\left[\left(N_{n,1}-\frac{\log n}{\kappa}\right)^+\right]
\overset{(\dagger)}{=}
\kappa(1-q_n)^{\log n/\kappa}
\notag\\
&=
\kappa\exp\Bigl(\frac{q_n\log n}{\kappa}\cdot\frac{\ln(1-q_n)}{q_n}\Bigr)
\overset{(\dagger\dagger)}{\longrightarrow}
\kappa e^{-\alpha/\kappa},\qquad n\to\infty,
\label{eq:geometric-reward}
\end{align}
where, for simplicity, we assume that $\log n/\kappa$ is an integer.
Equality~$(\dagger)$ follows from the memoryless property of the
geometric distribution.
The limit~$(\dagger\dagger)$ follows from $q_n\log n\to\alpha$ and
$\ln(1-q_n)/q_n\to-1$, where $q_n\to0$ since $q_n\log n\to\alpha$.
If $\log n/\kappa$ is not an integer, let $a$ be the integer with
$a\leq\log n/\kappa<a+1$.
Since $\mathbb{E}[(N_{n,1}-x)^+]$ is nonincreasing in $x$, the same
argument shows that $\kappa(1-q_n)^{a+1}\leq r_n(\kappa)\leq\kappa(1-q_n)^{a}$,
and both bounds tend to $\kappa e^{-\alpha/\kappa}$ since
$(1-q_n)^{\pm1}\to1$ when $n\to\infty$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:renewal-capacity} and Fano's inequality, as in the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:capacity-limit}, therefore provide
\begin{align*}
C(\alpha,p)
&\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}r_n(\kappa)
\overset{\eqref{eq:geometric-reward}}{=}
\kappa e^{-\alpha/\kappa}.
\end{align*}
On the other hand, the achievable
rate in~\cite[Theorem~3]{ravi2024recovering} provides
$
C(\alpha,p)
\geq
\kappa e^{-\alpha/\kappa}.
$
Combining the upper and lower bounds yields
$
C(\alpha,p)
=
\kappa e^{-\alpha/\kappa},
$
which proves~\eqref{eq:geometric-capacity}.

\label{remark:distribution before bsc}
In what follows, we treat each fixed tuple of equal-length binary strings, that is, each possible value of the strings before the BSC, as a probability distribution on single strings, namely its empirical distribution.
Accordingly, the distribution of strings before the BSC considered above will be taken to be the empirical distribution of a fixed tuple, rather than the distribution of a random tuple.
For a fixed tuple of binary strings, the empirical
distribution is fixed: the probability assigned to
each string is its number of occurrences divided
by the number of entries in the tuple.
Selecting one entry uniformly therefore provides one
string with this distribution, and applying the BSC
to the selected string provides the distribution $\widetilde{P}$.
We prove the approximation for any fixed distribution
$P$, so that it can later be applied to the empirical
distribution of every fixed tuple. 
Different tuples
may provide different empirical distributions, but the
same finite collection must provide an approximation
for all of them.
This empirical distribution is defined formally in Definition~\ref{def:fixed-fragment-output} and is used in this way in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:short}.

We first verify that the required choice of $s$ exists.
Since $p\in(0,1/2)$,
the quantity $\log((1-p)/p)$ is positive and finite, so

To calculate $\tau_k$, fix $\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^k$ and let $Z$ denote the number

       W_k(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}) =\Pr(|Z-kp|>ks).

 \tau_k=\Pr(|Z-kp|>ks)\leq 2e^{-2ks^2}.

To bound the variance at a fixed noisy string $\mathbf{y}$, we
first compute the expectation of $\hat{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})$.
Each auxiliary sampled string $\mathbf{X}_j$ has distribution $P$,
so averaging $W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)$ over its
possible values provides
\begin{align}\label{eq:expectation of Wk circ}
 \mathbb{E}[W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)]
 &=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 P(\mathbf{x})W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})
 =\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y}).
\end{align}
By the definition of $\hat{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})$ and linearity
of expectation,
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb{E}[\hat{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})]
 =\mathbb{E}\left[\frac1{J_k}\sum_{j=1}^{J_k}
 W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)\right] 
 =\frac1{J_k}\sum_{j=1}^{J_k}
 \mathbb{E}[W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)] 
 =\frac1{J_k}\sum_{j=1}^{J_k}\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})
 =\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y}).
\end{align*}
We next compute the variance of $\hat{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})$. 
Each
$W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)$ depends only on $\mathbf{X}_j$.
Since $\mathbf{X}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{J_k}$ are independent and
identically distributed, the random variables $W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)$, $j\in[J_k]$ are also independent
and identically distributed. 
For distinct indices $i\neq j$, independence therefore provides
\begin{align}\label{eq:distinct indice expectation}
 \mathbb{E}&\left[
 \bigl(W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_i)-\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})\bigr)
 \bigl(W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)-\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})\bigr)
 \right]\notag\\ &=
 \mathbb{E}\left[W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_i)-\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})\right]
 \mathbb{E}\left[W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)-\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})\right]
 =0.
\end{align}
Thus, we have
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Var}(\hat{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y}))
 &=\frac1{J_k^2}\mathbb{E}\left[

\label{lem:fixed-mixture-approximation}
For any real-valued function $f$ on $\{0,1\}^k$, define
$\|f\|_1=\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}|f(\mathbf{y})|$.
Then, for every distribution $P$ on $\{0,1\}^k$, we have

 v_k=\min\left\{1,\,
 2e^{-2ks^2}+2^{-k\epsilon/4-1}\right\}.
 \label{eq:tv-sequence}
\end{equation}
The sequence $(v_k)$ with $s$ chosen as in Lemma~\ref{lem:truncated-bsc} depends only on $p,\epsilon$ and tends
to zero as $k\to\infty$.

We first bound the expected absolute difference at a fixed
$\mathbf{y}$. Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to
$|\hat{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})-\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})|$ provides
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb{E}\left[
 |\hat{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})-\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})|
 \right]
 &\leq
 \sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[
 (\hat{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})-\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y}))^2
 \right]\mathbb{E}[1^2]} =\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\hat{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y}))}
 \overset{\eqref{eq:variance of p hat circ}}\leq
 \sqrt{\frac{2^{k(\kappa+\epsilon/2)}}{J_k}}
 \sqrt{2^{-k}\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})}.
\end{align*}
Summing this bound over all possible noisy strings provides
\begin{align}
 \mathbb{E}\left[\|\hat{P}^\circ-\widetilde{P}^\circ\|_1\right]
 &=\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \mathbb{E}\left[
 |\hat{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})-\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})|
 \right] \leq
 \sqrt{\frac{2^{k(\kappa+\epsilon/2)}}{J_k}}
 \sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \sqrt{2^{-k}\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})}\notag\\
 &\overset{(\dagger)}\leq
 \sqrt{\frac{2^{k(\kappa+\epsilon/2)}}{J_k}}\sqrt{
 \left(\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}2^{-k}\right)
 \left(\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})\right)}
 \notag\\
 &\overset{(\dagger\dagger)}\leq
 \sqrt{\frac{2^{k(\kappa+\epsilon/2)}}{J_k}}
 \sqrt{1-\tau_k} \overset{(\dagger\dagger\dagger)}\leq
 \sqrt{\frac{2^{k(\kappa+\epsilon/2)}}{2^{k(\kappa+\epsilon)}}}
 =2^{-k\epsilon/4}.
 \label{eq:mixture-l1}
\end{align}
Here, $(\dagger)$ follows from applying Cauchy--Schwarz to the
two finite lists
$\{\sqrt{2^{-k}}\}_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}$ and
$\{\sqrt{\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})}\}_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}$;
$(\dagger\dagger)$ follows from
$\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}2^{-k}=1$ and $\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})\overset{\eqref{eq:expectation of Wk circ}}=\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}\mathbb{E}[W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)]\overset{\eqref{eq:tau_k}}=1-\tau_k$;
$(\dagger\dagger\dagger)$ follows from $1-\tau_k\leq1$ and
$J_k\geq2^{k(\kappa+\epsilon)}$ in Construction~\ref{con:sparse-family}.

For every binary string $\mathbf{x}$, the difference
$W_k(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})-W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})$ is
nonnegative for every $\mathbf{y}$, and its sum over all possible
noisy strings equals $\tau_k$. Hence,
$\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})-\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})\geq0$:
\begin{align}
 \|\widetilde{P}-\widetilde{P}^\circ\|_1
 &=\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \bigl(\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})-\widetilde{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})\bigr) \notag\\
 &=\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^k}P(\mathbf{x})
 \bigl(W_k(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})
       -W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})\bigr) \notag\\
 &=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^k}P(\mathbf{x})
 \sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \bigl(W_k(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})
       -W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})\bigr) \notag\\
 &\overset{\eqref{eq:tau_k}}=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^k}P(\mathbf{x})\tau_k
 =\tau_k.
\label{eq:l1 norm between mu and mu circ}
\end{align}


For every 
$\mathbf{X}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{J_k}$, each difference
$W_k(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)-W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)$
is also nonnegative and sums to $\tau_k$ over $\mathbf{y}$.
Consequently,
\begin{align}
 \|\hat{P}-\hat{P}^\circ\|_1
 &=\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \bigl(\hat{P}(\mathbf{y})-\hat{P}^\circ(\mathbf{y})\bigr) \notag\\
 &=\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \frac1{J_k}\sum_{j=1}^{J_k}
 \bigl(W_k(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)
       -W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)\bigr) \notag\\
 &=\frac1{J_k}\sum_{j=1}^{J_k}
 \sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \bigl(W_k(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)
       -W_k^\circ(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X}_j)\bigr) \notag\\
 &\overset{\eqref{eq:tau_k}}=\frac1{J_k}\sum_{j=1}^{J_k}\tau_k
 =\tau_k.
\label{eq:l1 norm between P and P circ}
\end{align}


We now bound the difference between the full distributions of noisy strings
$\hat{P}$ and $\widetilde{P}$ by writing
$\hat{P}-\widetilde{P}=(\hat{P}-\hat{P}^\circ)
+(\hat{P}^\circ-\widetilde{P}^\circ)+(\widetilde{P}^\circ-\widetilde{P})$
and applying the triangle inequality.
\begin{align}
 \mathbb{E}\left[\|\hat{P}-\widetilde{P}\|_1\right]
 &\leq
 \mathbb{E}\left[\|\hat{P}-\hat{P}^\circ\|_1\right]
 +\mathbb{E}\left[\|\hat{P}^\circ-\widetilde{P}^\circ\|_1\right]
 +\|\widetilde{P}^\circ-\widetilde{P}\|_1 \notag\\
 &\overset{\eqref{eq:l1 norm between mu and mu circ},~\eqref{eq:l1 norm between P and P circ}}=\tau_k+
 \mathbb{E}\left[\|\hat{P}^\circ-\widetilde{P}^\circ\|_1\right]+\tau_k \notag\\
 &\overset{~\eqref{eq:mixture-l1}}\leq2\tau_k+2^{-k\epsilon/4},
 \label{eq:full-mixture-l1}
\end{align}


Equation~\eqref{eq:full-mixture-l1} bounds the expected
approximation error $\|\hat{P}-\widetilde{P}\|_1$, where the expectation
is over the auxiliary random list
$\mathbf{X}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{J_k}$.
Thus, the error averaged over
all sampled lists is at most $2\tau_k+2^{-k\epsilon/4}$, so  at
least one fixed list produces a distribution $Q\in\mathcal{V}_k$
satisfying
$\|Q-\widetilde{P}\|_1\leq2\tau_k+2^{-k\epsilon/4}$.

Dividing this bound by two provides
\begin{align*}
 \frac12\|\widetilde{P}-Q\|_1
 &\leq \frac12\left(2\tau_k+2^{-k\epsilon/4}\right)=\tau_k+2^{-k\epsilon/4-1}\leq 2e^{-2ks^2}+2^{-k\epsilon/4-1},
\end{align*}
where the last inequality uses
$\tau_k\leq2e^{-2ks^2}$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:truncated-bsc}.

We also have a second bound that holds for any two probability
distributions.
To verify this directly, nonnegativity provides
$|\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})-Q(\mathbf{y})|
\leq\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})+Q(\mathbf{y})$ for every possible noisy string $\mathbf{y}$.
\begin{align*}
 \frac12\|\widetilde{P}-Q\|_1
 &=\frac12\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 |\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})-Q(\mathbf{y})|\leq\frac12\left(
 \sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})
 +\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}Q(\mathbf{y})\right)
 =\frac12(1+1)=1.
\end{align*}
Both upper bounds hold simultaneously, so we may take the
smaller one:
\begin{align*}
 \frac12\|\widetilde{P}-Q\|_1
 &\leq\min\left\{1,\,
 2e^{-2ks^2}+2^{-k\epsilon/4-1}\right\}
 =v_k.
\end{align*}


The choice of $s$ uses only $p$ and $\epsilon$, so $v_k$
does not depend on the distribution $P$. Moreover,
$s>0$ and $\epsilon>0$ remain fixed as $k$ increases.
Thus, both $e^{-2ks^2}$ and $2^{-k\epsilon/4}$ decrease
exponentially, and consequently $v_k\to0$ exponentially.
The same vanishing error bound therefore applies to every
distribution $P$ on the binary strings before the BSC.

We convert the approximation bound from
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-mixture-approximation} into a bound on
$D(\widetilde{P}\|Q)$. Choose the fixed distribution $Q\in\mathcal{V}_k$
provided by Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-mixture-approximation}, and let
$d=\frac12\|\widetilde{P}-Q\|_1$. Then $d\leq v_k$, and
\begin{align}\label{eq:l1 norm equals 2d}
 \sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 |\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})-Q(\mathbf{y})|
 &=2d.
\end{align}
This controls the absolute differences between the probabilities
assigned to noisy strings. 

Then, we establish a lower bound on $Q(\mathbf{y})$. Each of
the $k$ factors in $W_k(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})$ is either $p$
or $1-p$. Since $p<1/2$, both $p$ and~$1-p$ are at least $p$,
so $W_k(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x})\geq p^k$ for every pair of binary
strings. By Construction~\ref{con:sparse-family}, $Q=Q_{\mathbf{x}}$ is an
equal-weight linear combination associated with some fixed list
$\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{J_k})$. Hence, for every $\mathbf{y}$,
\begin{align*}
 Q(\mathbf{y})
 &=\frac1{J_k}\sum_{j=1}^{J_k}
 W_k(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_j)
 \geq\frac1{J_k}\sum_{j=1}^{J_k}p^k
 =p^k.
\end{align*}
Also, $Q(\mathbf{y})\leq1$, as $Q$ is a probability
distribution. Therefore,
\begin{align}\label{eq: abstract value of log qy less than klog1p}
 0\leq-\log Q(\mathbf{y})
 &\leq-\log p^k
 =k\log(1/p),
\end{align}
and thus $|\log Q(\mathbf{y})|\leq k\log(1/p)$.

We next bound the difference between the entropies of
$Q$ and $\widetilde{P}$. Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-continuity} in Appendix~\ref{app:bernoulli} yields
\begin{align}\label{eq:hq minus hpwk}
 |H(Q)-H(\widetilde{P})|
 &\leq h_2(d)+d\log(2^k)
 =h_2(d)+kd.
\end{align}

Finally, we have
\begin{align}
 D(\widetilde{P}\|Q)
 &=\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})
 \log\frac{\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})}{Q(\mathbf{y})}
 \notag\\
 &=-H(\widetilde{P})
 -\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})\log Q(\mathbf{y})
 \notag\\
 &=-H(\widetilde{P})
 -\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 Q(\mathbf{y})\log Q(\mathbf{y})
 +\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \bigl(Q(\mathbf{y})-\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})\bigr)\log Q(\mathbf{y})
 \notag\\
 &=H(Q)-H(\widetilde{P})
 +\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 \bigl(Q(\mathbf{y})-\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})\bigr)\log Q(\mathbf{y})
 \notag\\
 &\leq |H(Q)-H(\widetilde{P})|
 +\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 |Q(\mathbf{y})-\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})|\,|\log Q(\mathbf{y})|
 \notag\\
 &\overset{\eqref{eq: abstract value of log qy less than klog1p},~\eqref{eq:hq minus hpwk}}\leq h_2(d)+kd
 +k\log(1/p)\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k}
 |Q(\mathbf{y})-\widetilde{P}(\mathbf{y})|
 \notag\\
 &\overset{\eqref{eq:l1 norm equals 2d}}=h_2(d)+kd+2kd\log(1/p).
 \label{eq:reverse-kl}
\end{align}

independent of the distribution $P$, such that
$D(\widetilde{P}\|Q)\leq a_k$ for the selected $Q$
and $a_k\to0$ as $k\to\infty$. 
Since a convergent sequence is bounded, $A_0=\sup_{k\geq1}a_k$ is then finite.

To construct a bound that applies to every $P$, recall
that~\eqref{eq:reverse-kl} bounds $D(\widetilde{P}\|Q)$ in terms
of the distance $d$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-mixture-approximation} provides
$0\leq d=\frac12\|\widetilde{P}-Q\|_1\leq v_k$. 
We define
\begin{align*}
 a_k
 &=\max_{0\leq t\leq v_k}
 \left\{h_2(t)+kt+2kt\log(1/p)\right\}.
\end{align*}
The maximum exists since $h_2(t)+kt+2kt\log(1/p)$ is continuous in $t$ and $[0,v_k]$ is a closed interval.
Thus, $D(\widetilde{P}\|Q)\leq a_k.$

Now, we turn to prove that $\lim_{k\to\infty}a_k=0$. 
For every $t\in[0,v_k]$, we have
\begin{align*}
 kt\leq kv_k, \qquad
 2kt\log(1/p)\leq2kv_k\log(1/p).
\end{align*}
 Adding these bounds and then taking the maximum over $t\in[0,v_k]$
provides
\begin{align}\label{eq:ak less than three terms}
 a_k
 &\leq kv_k+2kv_k\log(1/p)
 +\max_{0\leq t\leq v_k}h_2(t).
\end{align}
To bound the first two terms $kv_k,2kv_k\log(1/p)$ in~\eqref{eq:ak less than three terms}, recall that \[
0\leq kv_k\overset{\eqref{eq:tv-sequence}}{\leq}2ke^{-2ks^2}+k\,2^{-k\epsilon/4-1}\longrightarrow0\qquad(k\to\infty),
\]
where  $s>0$ and $\epsilon>0$ are fixed as in Lemma~\ref{lem:truncated-bsc} and $\log(1/p)$ is a constant.
Therefore,
$\lim_{k\to\infty}(kv_k+2kv_k\log(1/p))=0.$
To bound the last term $\max_{0\leq t\leq v_k}h_2(t)$ in~\eqref{eq:ak less than three terms},
recall that $v_k\to0$ as $k\to\infty$, so $v_k\leq1/2$
for all sufficiently large $k$. 
Since $h_2$ is increasing on $[0,1/2]$,
$
 \max_{0\leq t\leq v_k} h_2(t)=h_2(v_k)
$
for all sufficiently large $k$. 
Therefore,
\[
 \lim_{k\to\infty}\max_{0\leq t\leq v_k} h_2(t)
 =
 \lim_{k\to\infty}h_2(v_k)
 =
 h_2(0)
 =
 0,
\]
where the convergence follows from the continuity of $h_2$ at zero.
Thus, we have $\lim_{k\to\infty}a_k=0$.
Since $a_k\to0$ as $k\to\infty$, the sequence $(a_k)$
is bounded. 
Thus, let $A_0=\sup_{k\geq1}a_k<\infty$, and
for every $k\geq1$ and every distribution $P$,
 $Q$ satisfies
$D(\widetilde{P}\|Q)\leq a_k\leq A_0$.

\label{def:fixed-fragment-output}
Fix positive integers $m,k$ and a tuple
$\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{x}_m)$, where
$\mathbf{x}_j\in\{0,1\}^k$ for every $j\in[m]$.
From here through the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:short}, $\mathbf{x}$ denotes such a tuple of $m$ strings.
For each $\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^k$, define
$m_{\mathbf{a}}=|\{j\in[m]:\mathbf{x}_j=\mathbf{a}\}|$ and
$P_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{a})=m_{\mathbf{a}}/m$.
The distribution obtained by selecting an index uniformly
and applying the BSC to its string is
\begin{align}
\widetilde{P}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})
&=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m W_k(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_j),
\qquad \mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^k.
\label{eq:empirical-noisy-law}
\end{align}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The first joint distribution, denoted by $\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}$,
is obtained by applying independent BSC noise to the
$m$ fixed strings and then arranging the noisy strings
according to an independent uniform permutation of
$[m]$. 
For every
$(\mathbf{y}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{y}_m)\in(\{0,1\}^k)^m$,
\begin{align*}
\Lambda_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{y}_m)
&=\frac1{m!}
\sum_{\pi\text{ a permutation of }[m]}
\prod_{j=1}^m W_k(\mathbf{y}_j\mid\mathbf{x}_{\pi(j)}).
\end{align*}
Here $\pi(j)$ is the original index assigned to
position $j$. For a fixed permutation, independence
of the BSC noise provides the product of probabilities,
and the factor $1/m!$ averages over all permutations.
Every original index is used exactly once, even when
different indices carry identical strings. The resulting
tuple contains only the noisy strings, without their
original indices.
    \item The second joint distribution, denoted by
$\widetilde{P}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\otimes m}$, is obtained by independently
selecting an original index uniformly for each of the
$m$ positions and applying fresh independent BSC noise
to the selected string.
Indices may therefore be selected
repeatedly or omitted. The noisy strings are independent,
each with distribution $\widetilde{P}_{\mathbf{x}}$, and
\begin{align*}
\widetilde{P}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\otimes m}(\mathbf{y}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{y}_m)
&=\prod_{j=1}^m\widetilde{P}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}_j),
\qquad \mathbf{y}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{y}_m\in\{0,1\}^k.
\end{align*}
\end{itemize}

=m\ln m-m+1
\geq m\ln m-m.
\end{align*}

{m^{\sum_{\mathbf{a}:m_{\mathbf{a}}>0}m_{\mathbf{a}}}}

\end{align}
Here, $(\dagger)$ uses $m!\geq(m/e)^m$ and

-\log(e^{-m})=m\log e. \qedhere

addition modulo two, and let $\mathcal{Y}$ be
the unordered multiset of these $m$ noisy strings. Then
\begin{align}
\mathbf{Y}_j[1:k]
&=\mathbf{X}_j[1:k]\oplus\mathbf{Z}_j[1:k],
\qquad
\mathbf{Y}_j[k+1:L]
=\mathbf{X}_j[k+1:L]\oplus\mathbf{Z}_j[k+1:L].
\label{eq:y=x+z}
\end{align}

\begin{align*}
\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{pre}}
&=(\mathbf{Z}_1[1:k],\ldots,\mathbf{Z}_m[1:k]),
\qquad
\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{suf}}
=(\mathbf{Z}_1[k+1:L],\ldots,\mathbf{Z}_m[k+1:L]).
\end{align*}

$\mathbf{Y}_j[1:k]$, $j\in[m]$ in lexicographic order, i.e. from smallest to largest

after fixing the quantities used for sorting.

of $\mathbf{X}$, the prefix noise, and the fresh sorting randomness.

$\Pi=\pi$ with positive joint probability. For every
$\mathbf{z}\in\{0,1\}^{m(L-k)}$ and with \(\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{z})\) being the number of ones in \(\mathbf{z}\),
\begin{align}

\Pi\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{suf}}=\mathbf{z}
\mid\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{x},\,
\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{pre}}=\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{pre}},\,
\Pi=\pi\right) \notag
&=
\Pr\left(
\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{suf}}=\pi^{-1}\mathbf{z}
\mid\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{x},\,
\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{pre}}=\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{pre}},\,
\Pi=\pi\right) \notag\\

\Pr\left(\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{suf}}=\pi^{-1}\mathbf{z}\right)
=p^{\operatorname{wt}(\pi^{-1}\mathbf{z})}
(1-p)^{m(L-k)-\operatorname{wt}(\pi^{-1}\mathbf{z})} \notag\\

p^{\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{z})}(1-p)^{m(L-k)-\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{z})}.
\label{eq:permuted-noise}

Step $(\dagger)$ uses the independence of
$\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{suf}}$ from
$(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{pre}},\Pi)$.
Step $(\dagger\dagger)$ uses
$\operatorname{wt}(\pi^{-1}\mathbf{z})=\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{z})$, since reordering
preserves the number of bits equal to one.
The final expression is the probability of $\mathbf{z}$
under independent Bernoulli$(p)$ noise and does not
depend on the conditioning values. Hence $\Pi\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{suf}}$ consists of independent Bernoulli$(p)$ bits and is independent of $(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{pre}},\Pi)$.
The tuples $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ in

\mathbf{Y}_{\Pi(i)}[k+1:L]

\mathbf{X}_{\Pi(i)}[k+1:L]
\oplus\mathbf{Z}_{\Pi(i)}[k+1:L],
\qquad i\in[m].
\end{align*}

I(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{B};\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A})
-I(\mathbf{B};\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A},\mathbf{X})\leq
I(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{B};\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A})\\
&\overset{(\dagger)}{=}
I(\mathbf{B};\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A})
+I(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\overset{\text{Lemma}~\ref{lem:sorted-suffix-noise}}{=}
I(\mathbf{B};\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A})\\
&=H(\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A})-H(\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\overset{\eqref{eq:suffix-noise-entropy}}{=}
H(\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A})-m(L-k)h_2(p)\\
&\overset{(\dagger\dagger)}{\leq}
m(L-k)-m(L-k)h_2(p)
=m\kappa(L-k),
\end{align*}
 where $(\dagger)$ follows from the chain rule, the equality marked by Lemma~\ref{lem:sorted-suffix-noise} holds since~\eqref{eq:suffix-noise-entropy} implies $I(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})=H(\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})-H(\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})=0$,
and  $(\dagger\dagger)$ follows from
$H(\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A})\leq m(L-k)$, since
$\mathbf{D}$ contains $m(L-k)$ bits.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:suffix}
For every integer $m\geq1$ and integers $0\leq k\leq L$,
\begin{align}
\mathsf{C}_p(m,L)&\leq\mathsf{C}_p(m,k)+m\kappa(L-k).
\label{eq:suffix-bound}
\end{align}
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
We first verify the boundary cases. If $k=L$, the
inequality reduces to $\mathsf{C}_p(m,L)\leq\mathsf{C}_p(m,L)$.
If $k=0$, all prefixes are empty and $\mathsf{C}_p(m,0)=0$.
To prove $\mathsf{C}_p(m,L)\leq mL\kappa$ in this case,
fix any joint distribution of
$\mathbf{X}=(\mathbf{X}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{X}_m)$, with
$\mathbf{X}_j\in\{0,1\}^L$ for every $j\in[m]$,
and reveal the original
string indices to the decoder. Let $\mathbf{Y}$
be the length-$mL$ vector obtained by concatenating
the noisy strings in their original order.
This vector determines $\mathcal{Y}$, so
$I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})\leq I(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{Y})$.
Moreover, $H(\mathbf{Y})\leq mL$, and the independent
BSC errors provide $H(\mathbf{Y}\mid\mathbf{X})=mLh_2(p)$.
Hence,
\begin{align*}
I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})
&\leq I(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{Y})
=H(\mathbf{Y})-H(\mathbf{Y}\mid\mathbf{X})
\leq mL-mLh_2(p)=mL\kappa.
\end{align*}
Taking the supremum over all joint distributions
provides $\mathsf{C}_p(m,L)\leq mL\kappa
=\mathsf{C}_p(m,0)+m\kappa(L-0)$.

For $0<k<L$, fix any joint distribution of $\mathbf{X}$
and apply Construction~\ref{con:suffix-sorting}.
The pair $(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{D})$ determines $\mathcal{Y}$ by
joining corresponding prefixes and suffixes and
forgetting the order of the resulting noisy strings. Thus,
\begin{align}
I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})
&\leq I(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{A},\mathbf{D})
=I(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{A})+I(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A}) \overset{\eqref{eq:sorted-prefix-information}}{\leq}
\mathsf{C}_p(m,k)+I(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{A})
\overset{\eqref{eq:sorted-suffix-information}}{\leq}
\mathsf{C}_p(m,k)+m\kappa(L-k).
\label{eq:suffix-information-proof}
\end{align}
Taking the supremum over all joint distributions
of $\mathbf{X}$ in~\eqref{eq:suffix-information-proof}
proves~\eqref{eq:suffix-bound}.

<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
To choose a prefix length to which the short-multiset bound in Proposition~\ref{prop:short} applies,
set~$k=\min\{L, \lfloor  b\log m\rfloor\}$ and then we have
\begin{equation*}
 \left\lceil2^{k(\kappa+\epsilon)}\right\rceil
 \le2m^{1-\delta},
 \qquad
 k\left\lceil2^{k(\kappa+\epsilon)}\right\rceil
 \le2b\,m^{1-\delta}\log(m+1).
\end{equation*}
The first inequality follows from $k(\kappa+\epsilon)\le b(\kappa+\epsilon)\log m=(1-\delta)\log m$, which shows $1\leq2^{k(\kappa+\epsilon)}\leq m^{1-\delta}$, and from $\lceil x\rceil\leq2x$ for $x\geq1$; the second inequality then follows from $k\leq b\log m\leq b\log(m+1)$. 
Combining
Proposition~\ref{prop:short} with Lemma~\ref{lem:suffix} provides
\begin{align*}
\mathsf{C}_p(m,L)
&\overset{\text{Lemma}~\ref{lem:suffix}}{\leq}
\mathsf{C}_p(m,k)+m\kappa(L-k)\\
&\overset{}{\leq}
k\left\lceil2^{k(\kappa+\epsilon)}\right\rceil
+Am+m\kappa(L-k)\\
&\overset{}{\leq}
2b\,m^{1-\delta}\log(m+1)+Am+m\kappa(L-k)\\
&\overset{(\dagger)}{\mathrel{\leq}}
m(\kappa L-\lambda\log m)^+
+(A+\kappa)m+2b\,m^{1-\delta}\log(m+1).
\end{align*}
where $(\dagger)$ follows from
$L-k\leq(L-b\log m)^++1$ and $\lambda=\kappa b$. 
Indeed, if $L\leq\lfloor b\log m\rfloor$, then $k=L$ and $L-k=0$. Otherwise, $k=\lfloor b\log m\rfloor>b\log m-1$ and $L\geq\lfloor b\log m\rfloor+1>b\log m$, so $L-k<(L-b\log m)+1=(L-b\log m)^++1$. Multiplying by $m\kappa$ shows $m\kappa(L-k)\leq m(\kappa L-\lambda\log m)^++\kappa m$. Hence~\eqref{eq:uniform-shuffling} holds with $c_2=A+\kappa$, which depends only on $p$ and $\epsilon$.

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
By Definition~\ref{def:nsc}, the codeword of the NSC consists of
$K_n=n/\ell_n$ fragments of length~$\ell_n$, so we write its input as
$\mathbf{X}=(\mathbf{X}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{K_n})$, where
$\mathbf{X}_j\in\{0,1\}^{\ell_n}$ is the $j$th fragment. The output $\mathcal{Y}$
is the unordered multiset of the $K_n$ noisy fragments. This is exactly
the setting of Definition~\ref{definition:Csh}, and therefore
$\sup_{P_{\mathbf{X}}}I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})=\mathsf{C}_p(K_n,\ell_n)$.

Fix $\epsilon>0$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$, and let $b$, $\lambda$, and $c_2$
be as in Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform}; in particular,
$\lambda=(1-h_2(p))(1-\delta)/(1-h_2(p)+\epsilon)$. Applying
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform} with $m=K_n$ and $L=\ell_n$, and dividing
by $n=K_n\ell_n$, yields
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{n}\sup_{P_{\mathbf{X}}}I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})
=\frac{\mathsf{C}_p(K_n,\ell_n)}{n}
\leq\Bigl(1-h_2(p)-\lambda\frac{\log K_n}{\ell_n}\Bigr)^+
+\frac{c_2}{\ell_n}
+\frac{2bK_n^{-\delta}\log(K_n+1)}{\ell_n}.
\label{eq:nsc-bound}
\end{equation}
By Assumption~\ref{remark:assumptions}, $\log n/\ell_n\to\alpha\in(0,\infty)$,
so $\ell_n\to\infty$ and $K_n=n/\ell_n\to\infty$ as $n\to\infty$.
Moreover, $\log K_n/\ell_n=\log n/\ell_n-\log\ell_n/\ell_n\to\alpha$.
Hence, as $n\to\infty$, the first term on the right-hand side
of~\eqref{eq:nsc-bound} tends to $(1-h_2(p)-\lambda\alpha)^+$, and the
second and third terms tend to zero, which shows that
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sup_{P_{\mathbf{X}}}I(\mathbf{X};\mathcal{Y})
\leq\bigl(1-h_2(p)-\lambda\alpha\bigr)^+.
\]
This bound holds for every $\epsilon>0$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$, and
$\lambda\to1$ as $\epsilon,\delta\to0$, which
proves~\eqref{eq:nsc-converse}.

Fano's inequality, as in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:capacity-limit}, shows that every achievable rate is
at most $(1-h_2(p)-\alpha)^+$. 
Conversely, the index-based scheme
of~\cite{shomorony2019capacity} achieves every rate below
$1-h_2(p)-\alpha$ when this value is positive. Hence the capacity of
the NSC is $(1-h_2(p)-\alpha)^+$.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:partition-counts}
Fix integers $n\geq2$ and $1\leq K\leq n$, and partition
$n$ into $K$ positive integer lengths. Let $m_L$ be the
number of fragments of length $L$ and
$\mathcal{L}=\{L\in[n]:m_L>0\}$.
Thus, we have $\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L=K$ and
$\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}Lm_L=n$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \renewcommand{(\Alph{enumi})}{(\Alph{enumi})}
    \renewcommand{(\Alph{enumi})}{(\Alph{enumi})}
\item\label{lemma:A}  We have $|\mathcal{L}|(|\mathcal{L}|+1)/2\leq n$, and hence $|\mathcal{L}|\leq\sqrt{2n}$.
\item\label{lemma:B} 
\begin{align}
\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L\log\frac{n}{m_L}
&\leq 2K\log\frac{n}{K}+K\log e.
\label{eq:counts-entropy}
\end{align}
\item\label{lemma:C} For every fixed $\delta\in(0,1)$,
\begin{align}
\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L^{1-\delta}\log(m_L+1)
&\leq\log(n+1)(2n)^{\delta/2}K^{1-\delta}.
\label{eq:counts-power}
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
List the distinct fragment lengths in increasing order
as $L_1<\cdots<L_{|\mathcal{L}|}$. 
Since the lengths are distinct
positive integers, $L_j\geq j$ for every
$j\in\{1,\ldots,|\mathcal{L}|\}$. 
Moreover, each length occurs
at least once, so $m_L\geq1$ for every $L\in\mathcal{L}$.
Therefore,
\begin{align*}
n=\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}Lm_L
&\geq\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{L}|}L_j
\geq\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{L}|}j
=\frac{|\mathcal{L}|(|\mathcal{L}|+1)}{2}. \qedhere
\end{align*}
<!-- end proof-17 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Choose one of the $K$ fragments uniformly and let $V$
be its length. 
Then $\Pr(V=L)=m_L/K$ for every
$L\in\mathcal{L}$, and
$\mathbb{E}[V]=\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}Lm_L/K=n/K$.
Using $\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L=K$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L\log\frac{n}{m_L}
&=\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L
\left(\log\frac{n}{K}+\log\frac{K}{m_L}\right)=K\log\frac{n}{K}+KH(V)
\overset{(\dagger)}{\leq}
2K\log\frac{n}{K}+K\log e.
\end{align*}
Here $(\dagger)$ follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:integer-entropy} in Appendix~\ref{app:bernoulli}, applied to the
positive integer random variable $V$ with mean $n/K$.
<!-- end proof-18 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$. Since $m_L\leq n$ for every
$L\in\mathcal{L}$, we have
\begin{align*}
\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L^{1-\delta}\log(m_L+1)
&\leq\log(n+1)\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L^{1-\delta}
\overset{(\dagger)}{\leq}
\log(n+1)|\mathcal{L}|^\delta K^{1-\delta}\overset{\text{Lemma}~\ref{lem:partition-counts}. \ref{lemma:A}}{\leq}
\log(n+1)(2n)^{\delta/2}K^{1-\delta}.
\end{align*}
Here $(\dagger)$ follows from the concavity of
$x^{1-\delta}$ and $\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L=K$, which provide

\frac1{|\mathcal{L}|}\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L^{1-\delta}
&\leq
\left(\frac1{|\mathcal{L}|}\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}m_L\right)^{1-\delta}
=\left(\frac{K}{|\mathcal{L}|}\right)^{1-\delta}. \qedhere
\end{align*}

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:integer-entropy}
Let $V$ be a random variable taking only finitely many
positive integer values and let
$\mu=\mathbb{E}[V]\in[1,\infty)$ denote its mean.
Then $H(V)\leq\log\mu+\log e$.
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
If $\mu=1$, then $V=1$ with probability one, so
$H(V)=0$. 
For $\mu>1$, let $P_V$ denote the distribution of $V$
and introduce the geometric distribution
\begin{align*}
Q(j)&=\frac1{\mu}\left(1-\frac1{\mu}\right)^{j-1},
\qquad j=1,2,\ldots.
\end{align*}
We choose $Q$ to have the same mean $\mu$ as $V$.

Since $V$ takes only finitely many values, the sum
defining $D(P_V\|Q)$ contains finitely many nonzero
terms. Moreover, $Q(j)>0$ for every positive integer
$j$, so each nonzero term is finite. Expanding the
logarithm and using nonnegativity of relative entropy
provides
\begin{align*}
0\leq D(P_V\|Q)
&=\sum_{j:P_V(j)>0}P_V(j)
\log\frac{P_V(j)}{Q(j)}
=-H(V)-\mathbb{E}[\log Q(V)].
\end{align*}
Rearranging therefore yields
$H(V)\leq-\mathbb{E}[\log Q(V)]$.

To evaluate this upper bound, the definition of $Q$
provides
$\log Q(V)=-\log\mu+(V-1)\log(1-1/\mu)$.
Since $\mathbb{E}[V-1]=\mu-1$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
H(V)
&\leq-\mathbb{E}[\log Q(V)]
=\log\mu-\mathbb{E}[V-1]\log(1-1/\mu)\\
&=\log\mu-(\mu-1)\log(1-1/\mu)
=\log\mu+(\mu-1)\log\left(1+\frac1{\mu-1}\right)\\
&\overset{(\dagger)}{\leq}\log\mu+\log e.
\end{align*}
Here $(\dagger)$ follows from
$\log(1+x)\leq x\log e$ for $x\geq0$, applied with
$x=1/(\mu-1)$.

<!-- lemma-14: body_md -->
\label{lem:bernoulli}
Let $k$ be a positive integer and let $p\in(0,1)$. Let $Z$ be
Binomial$(k,p)$, that is, the sum of $k$ independent
Bernoulli$(p)$ bits.
For $s>0$ with $p+s<1$,
$\Pr(Z\ge k(p+s))\le e^{-2ks^2}$.
If also $p-s>0$, then
$\Pr(|Z-kp|>ks)\le2e^{-2ks^2}$.
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Write $Z=\sum_{i=1}^{k} X_i$, where the $X_i$ are independent
Bernoulli$(p)$ random variables. Since $0\leq X_i\leq1$ and
$\mathbb{E}[Z]=kp$, Hoeffding's inequality~\cite{Hoeffding1963} provides,
for every $t>0$,
\[
 \Pr(Z-kp\geq t)\leq e^{-2t^2/k},
 \qquad
 \Pr(Z-kp\leq-t)\leq e^{-2t^2/k}.
\]
Taking $t=ks$ proves the upper-tail bound. Adding the
upper- and lower-tail bounds provides
$\Pr(|Z-kp|>ks)\leq2e^{-2ks^2}$.\qedhere
