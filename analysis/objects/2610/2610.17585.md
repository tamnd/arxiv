Let $\Omega=\mathbb{R}^3$ and $\bar{\rho}$ be a constant. Suppose that the initial data $(\rho_0,\,u_0,\,w_0)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{1.6}
\frac{3}{4}\bar{\rho}\leq \rho_0\leq\frac{5}{4}\bar{\rho},\,\, \rho_0-\bar{\rho}\in D^{1,2}\cap D^{1,q},\quad 3<q<6,\,\, G(\rho_0)\in L^1,\,\, u_0\in H^2,\,\, w_0\in H^2.
\end{equation}
Let $\alpha,\,\beta$ and $\gamma$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{1.7}
\alpha>1,\quad 0\leq\beta< \alpha,\quad \gamma>1,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{1.8}
\alpha>\displaystyle\!max \left\{\frac{\gamma+1}{2},\,\gamma-1\right\}.
\end{equation}
Then there exists a positive constant
$$\Lambda_0=\Lambda_0\left(A,\,\mu_1,\,\lambda_1,\,\mu_2,\,\lambda_2,\,\bar{\xi},\,\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma,\,\|G(\rho_0)\|_{L^1},\,\|\nabla \rho_0\|_{L^2},\,\|\nabla \rho_0\|_{L^q},\,\|u_0\|_{H^2},\,\|w_0\|_{H^2}\right)$$
such that if
\begin{equation}\label{1.9}
\bar{\rho}\geq \Lambda_0,
\end{equation}
then the problem (\ref{1.1})--(\ref{1.5}) admits a unique global solution $(\rho,u,w)$ in $\mathbb{R}^3\times(0,\infty)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{1.10}
\frac{2}{3}\bar{\rho}\leq \rho_0\leq\frac{4}{3}\bar{\rho}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&\rho\in C([0,\infty);D^{1,2}\cap D^{1,q}),\quad \rho_t\in C([0,\infty);L^q),\\
&\nabla u\in C([0,\infty);H^1)\cap L^2((0,\infty);W^{1,q}),\\
&u_t\in L^\infty((0,\infty);L^2)\cap L^2((0,\infty);H^1),\\
& w\in C([0,\infty);H^2)\cap L^2((0,\infty);W^{2,q}),\\
&w_t\in L^\infty((0,\infty);L^2)\cap L^2((0,\infty);H^1).\label{1.11}
\end{aligned}\right.
\end{equation}

<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
If $\bar{\xi}=0$, the system (\ref{1.1}) will reduce to Navier-Stokes system with density-dependent viscosities. This result is consistent with the results obtained by \cite{HLZ} to the Navier-Stokes equations. To the best of our knowledge, this work presents the first global existence result for large strong solutions to the 3D micropolar equations without small initial data assumption, a requirement that has been essential in all existing results \cite{CXZ2015,SXZ2025,Z2023}.

In addition to the same $\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma$ requirements of the Navier-Stokes equations in \cite{HLZ}, we also require $0\leq\beta<\alpha$ in (\ref{1.7}), which play a crucial role to the micropolar model, see details in  (\ref{3.17}), (\ref{3.18}), (\ref{3.31}), (\ref{3.57}) and (\ref{3.64}).

<!-- rem-3: body_md -->
Compared with the assumption $\alpha>1, 0\leq \beta \leq \frac{\alpha+1}{2}$ of inhomogeneous incompressible micropolar equations with density-dependent viscosities in \cite{LQ2025}, the assumption (\ref{1.7}) of Theorem \ref{th1} implies that $\beta$ has a wider range in our research work due to the fact $\frac{\alpha+1}{2}<\alpha$.

<!-- rem-4: body_md -->
The form of the micropolar equations with density-dependent viscosities differs fundamentally from that of the constant-viscosity model. The equation (4.16) of \cite{Eringen1966} implies that the momentum equation of micropolar system should have the term $\mathrm{curl}\mathrm{curl} u$, see detail of $(\ref{1.1})_2$. The micropolar equations of \cite{LQ2025} may be incorrect where they missed the rotation term.

\label{re5}
We can prove that Theorem \ref{th1} also holds for the 3D compressible system (\ref{1.1}) with $\lambda_1(\rho)$ and $\lambda_2(\rho)$ are nonnegative constants, which consistent with the research work of Navier-Stokes equations in \cite{ZZ2014}, where they only requiring initial shear viscosity to be large. And this result improves the study of \cite{HLZ} to some extent, for they requiring initial shear and bulk viscosities all to be large.
<!-- end rem-5 -->
<!-- rem-6: body_md -->
As described in \cite{HLZ}, Xin and Zhu obtained the global regular solutions of 3D compressible Navier-Stokes equations if $\alpha>2\gamma-1$. In this paper, we assume that $\alpha>\displaystyle\!max \left\{\frac{\gamma+1}{2},\,\gamma-1\right\}$. It is obvious that $2\gamma-1>\frac{\gamma+1}{2}$ and $2\gamma-1>\gamma-1$ due to $\gamma>1$. The problem of \cite{HLZ} allows vacuum. However, the present of vacuum will bring  essential difficulties to the proof of global existence of large solutions of 3D compressible micropolar system which will be addressed in our future work.

\label{lem2.1}
{\rm (Local existence)}
Assume that $(\rho_0, u_0, w_0)$ satisfies (\ref{1.6}), then there exist a small time $T_0 >0$ and a unique strong solution $(\rho, u, w)$ to the problem (\ref{1.1})--(\ref{1.5}) in $\mathbb{R}^3 \times (0, T_0]$ satisfying \begin{equation}\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&\rho\in C([0,T_0];D^{1,2}\cap D^{1,q}),\quad \rho_t\in C([0,T_0];L^q),\\
&\nabla u\in C([0,T_0];H^1)\cap L^2([0,T_0];W^{1,q}),\\
&\sqrt{\rho}u_t\in L^\infty([0,T_0];L^2),\quad u_t\in L^2([0,T_0];H^1),\\
& w\in C([0,T_0];H^2)\cap L^2([0,T_0];W^{2,q}),\\
&\sqrt{\rho}w_t\in L^\infty([0,T_0];L^2),\quad w_t\in L^2([0,T_0];H^1).\label{2.1}
\end{aligned}\right.
\end{equation}

\label{lem2.2}
{\rm (Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality)}
For $r\in(6/5,3)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{2.2}
p \in \left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{aligned}
 &[2,\frac{3r}{3-r}],\\
& \left[2,\infty\right),\\
& \left[2,\infty\right],
\end{aligned}
\begin{aligned}
&\quad{ if}\,\,r\in(\frac{6}{5},3),\\
&\quad{ if}\,\,r=3,\\
&\quad{ if}\,\,r\in(3,\infty],
\end{aligned}\end{matrix}\right.\end{equation}
 then there exists some generic positive constant $C$ that may depend on $p$ and $r$ such that for any  $f\in \left\{f|f\in L^2,\,\nabla f\in L^r\right\}$,
\begin{equation}\|f\|_{L^p} \leq C(p,r) \|f\|_{L^2}^{\alpha} \|\nabla f\|_{L^r}^{1-\alpha},\quad\frac{\alpha}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{3}\right)(1-\alpha)=\frac{1}{p}.\label{2.2}
\end{equation}

$$N_i=N_i(A,\,\mu_1,\,\lambda_1,\,\mu_2,\,\lambda_2,\,\bar{\xi},\,\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma,\,\|G(\rho_0)\|_{L^1},\,\|\nabla \rho_0\|_{L^2},\,\|\nabla \rho_0\|_{L^q},\,\|\nabla u_0\|_{H^1},\,\|w_0\|_{H^2})$$

$$\Lambda_0=\Lambda_0(A,\,\mu_1,\,\lambda_1,\,\mu_2,\,\lambda_2,\,\bar{\xi},\,\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma,\,\|G(\rho_0)\|_{L^1},\,\|\nabla \rho_0\|_{L^2},\,\|\nabla \rho_0\|_{L^q},\,\|u_0\|_{H^2},\,\|w_0\|_{H^2})$$
such that if $(\rho,\,u,\,w)$ is a smooth solution to the problem (\ref{1.1})-(\ref{1.5}) on $\mathbb{R}^3\times(0,T]$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{3.3}
\begin{aligned}
&\varepsilon_{\rho,1}(T)\leq \frac{\bar{\rho}}{2},\quad
\varepsilon_{\rho,2}(T)\leq 2N_1{\bar{\rho}}^b,\quad
\varepsilon_{\rho,3}(T)\leq 2N_2{\bar{\rho}}^b,\\
&\varepsilon_{u,w,1}(T)\leq 2^{\alpha+2}N_3{\bar{\rho}}^\alpha,\quad
\varepsilon_{u,w,2}(T)\leq 3N_4{\bar{\rho}}^{2\alpha-1},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
the following estimates hold:
\begin{equation}\label{3.4}
\begin{aligned}
&\varepsilon_{\rho,1}(T)\leq \frac{\bar{\rho}}{4},\quad
\varepsilon_{\rho,2}(T)\leq N_1{\bar{\rho}}^b,\quad
\varepsilon_{\rho,3}(T)\leq N_2{\bar{\rho}}^b,\\
&\varepsilon_{u,w,1}(T)\leq 2^{\alpha+1}N_3{\bar{\rho}}^\alpha,\quad
\varepsilon_{u,w,2}(T)\leq 2N_4{\bar{\rho}}^{2\alpha-1},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
provided that
$$\bar{\rho}\geq \Lambda_0,$$
with $\Lambda_0$ defined in (\ref{3.67}).
