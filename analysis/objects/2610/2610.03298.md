<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:kervaire-existence}
The exotic Kervaire nine-sphere $\Sigma_K^9$ admits a smooth Riemannian metric of strictly positive sectional curvature.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:classification}
A homotopy nine-sphere admits a smooth Riemannian metric of positive sectional curvature if and only if its real Dirac index vanishes. More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classification}
\mathcal P_9=\ker\alpha=\{0,K,C_\eta,K+C_\eta\}.
\end{equation}
The other four smooth types admit no smooth metric of nonnegative sectional curvature.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:nonnegative-obstruction}
A homotopy nine-sphere with nonzero real Dirac index admits no smooth metric with $\sec\ge0$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For a homotopy nine-sphere $\Sigma$, the vanishing of $H^2(\Sigma;\mathbb Z/2)$ gives $w_2(T\Sigma)=0$, so $\Sigma$ is spin. Its spin structure is unique since $H^1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z/2)=0$. The real index
\[
\alpha(\Sigma)\in KO_9\cong\mathbb Z/2
\]
is independent of the Riemannian metric. If it is nonzero, the Dirac operator for every metric has nonzero kernel \cite{Hitchin,CS}. In dimension $1\pmod8$, the index takes values in $\mathbb Z/2$.

Suppose $\sec_g\ge0$. Choose a nonzero harmonic spinor $\psi$. The Schr\"odinger--Lichnerowicz formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SL}
0=\int_\Sigma\left(|\nabla\psi|^2+\frac{\operatorname{Scal}_g}{4}|\psi|^2\right)\,dV_g.
\end{equation}
Since $\operatorname{Scal}_g\ge0$, both terms vanish. Hence $\psi$ is parallel; its nonzero norm is constant, and $\operatorname{Scal}_g\equiv0$. For any orthonormal frame,
\[
\operatorname{Scal}_g=2\sum_{i<j}\sec_g(e_i,e_j).
\]
A sum of nonnegative summands can vanish only if all summands vanish. Extending any chosen two-plane to an orthonormal frame therefore shows that every sectional curvature is zero. The metric is flat. A closed simply connected flat manifold would have to be isometric to its Euclidean universal cover, which is noncompact. This contradiction proves the proposition.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:topology}
There is a smooth map $\beta:S^8\to S^3$, given explicitly in \cref{sec:clutching}, which satisfies \eqref{eq:betaeq} for \eqref{eq:representation}. The map $J_\beta$ is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, and its double-disk manifold is diffeomorphic to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kdefinition}
D_N^9\cup_{J_\beta}D_S^9
\cong
\partial\bigl(D(TS^5)\mathbin{\mathrm{plumb}} D(TS^5)\bigr)=\Sigma_K^9.
\end{equation}
The latter is the nonstandard Kervaire nine-sphere.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:amap}
The map $a:\mathbb H\to S^3$ is smooth, equals $-1$ on $|x|\le1/2$, and equals $1$ on $|x|\ge1$. It satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aeq}
a(qx)=q a(x)q^{-1},\qquad q\in S^3.
\end{equation}
Its one-point compactification $\bar a:S^4\to S^3$ represents the suspended Hopf element.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For $x\ne0$, the quaternion $xi\overline x/|x|^2$ is purely imaginary of norm one, so \eqref{eq:amap} has norm one. The apparent division by $|x|^2$ causes no singularity, since the full expression is the constant $-1$ on an entire neighborhood of the origin. It is constant $1$ outside the unit ball, so it also extends smoothly across the compactification point. Norm preservation and $(qx)i\overline{qx}=q(xi\overline x)q^{-1}$ prove \eqref{eq:aeq}.

Write $x=r\omega$ with $\omega\in S^3$. Then
\[
a(r\omega)=\cos\vartheta(r)+\sin\vartheta(r)\,\omega i\omega^{-1}.
\]
The angular map is the Hopf fibration $S^3\to S^2$, and the radial variable joins its two constant suspension ends. The constant end neighborhoods are a smooth reparameterization of the suspension. Reversing the suspension coordinate does not change the nonzero element of $\pi_4(S^3)\cong\mathbb Z/2$. This is the characteristic class of $\operatorname{SU}(3)\to S^5$ described in \cite[p.~5]{Puttmann}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:explicitbeta}
The map \eqref{eq:explicitbeta} is smooth and satisfies \eqref{eq:betaeq}. It is identically $1$ whenever $|t|\ge1/3$. Its reentrance map $J_\beta$ is a diffeomorphism; in \eqref{eq:stereo} it is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Psi}
\Psi(x,y)=(C(x,y)x,C(x,y)y).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Smoothness at the north pole follows because $C=1$ near infinity. Equation \eqref{eq:aeq} gives $C(qx,qy)=qC(x,y)q^{-1}$, and stereographic projection commutes with the left action, proving equivariance. If $t\ge1/3$, then $R^2\ge2$ and at least one variable has norm at least one. If $t\le-1/3$, then $R^2\le1/2$ and at least one variable has norm at most $1/2$. This proves the asserted constant regions.

Equivariance with $q=\beta(z)$ gives $\beta(J_\beta(z))=\beta(z)$. Hence $J_\beta^{-1}=J_{\beta^{-1}}$. The map is the identity near a pole, so its local orientation sign, which is constant on the connected sphere, is positive. It fixes $t$ and left-multiplies both quaternionic coordinates; \eqref{eq:Psi} follows.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:commutator}
With composition read from right to left,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exactcommutator}
G^{-1}\circ F^{-1}\circ G\circ F=\Psi.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Put $p=a(x)$, $q=a(y)$, $k=pqp^{-1}$, and $C=kq^{-1}$. Equivariance gives
\[
(G\circ F)(x,y)=(a(py)x,py)=(kx,py).
\]
Since $a(kx)=kpk^{-1}$,
\[
(F^{-1}\circ G\circ F)(x,y)
=(kx,kp^{-1}k^{-1}py)=(kx,Cy).
\]
Also $a(Cy)=CqC^{-1}$, so the final application of $G^{-1}$ gives
\[
((CqC^{-1})^{-1}kx,Cy)=(Cx,Cy),
\]
where $C^{-1}k=q$ was used. This is \eqref{eq:Psi}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:hopfcomparison}
Let $\mathcal D=\operatorname{Diff}_\partial(D^6)$ and let $\Gamma_j:\pi_j\mathcal D\to\Theta_{j+7}$ be the disk-family Gromoll map. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qgromoll}
\Gamma_2\mathcal Q_{\mathcal D}(x)=\eta\,\Gamma_1(x)
\quad\text{in }\Theta_9,\qquad x\in\pi_1\mathcal D.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Put $Y=\operatorname{Top}(6)/O(6)$ and let $s:Y\to\operatorname{Top}/O$ be stabilization. We use the delooped smoothing-section comparison, rather than choosing an arbitrary equivalence of the underlying loop spaces. For the disk of dimension six this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deloopMorlet}
B\mathcal D\simeq\Omega^6_0Y;
\end{equation}
Here the subscript denotes the component of the constant map; fixing a boundary collar instead of fixing the boundary pointwise gives the same homotopy type. This is Morlet's equivalence in its classifying-space form \cite[Essay V, Theorem 3.4]{KS}, also recalled in \cite[p.~2, before Corollary C]{KK}; the smoothing-section construction and its stabilization compatibility are described in \cite[Theorems 1.2--1.3]{Burghelea}. We choose the equivalence induced by that construction. Composing the canonical equivalence $\mathcal D\simeq\Omega B\mathcal D$ with its loop gives the Morlet comparison used below. Thus the universal-bundle boundary and the delooping are part of the same comparison, not independently chosen identifications. Combining that boundary map, this equivalence, and sixfold adjunction gives isomorphisms
\[
\mu_j:\pi_j\mathcal D\longrightarrow\pi_{j+7}Y,
\qquad j=1,2.
\]
The definition of $\mathcal Q_{\mathcal D}$ now gives the commutative diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
\pi_1\mathcal D&\xrightarrow{\ \mu_1\ }&\pi_8Y&\xrightarrow{\ s_*\ }&\pi_8(\operatorname{Top}/O)\\[3pt]
\mathcal Q_{\mathcal D}\,\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\,{\circ\eta_8}&&\big\downarrow\,{\circ\eta_8}\\[3pt]
\pi_2\mathcal D&\xrightarrow{\ \mu_2\ }&\pi_9Y&\xrightarrow{\ s_*\ }&\pi_9(\operatorname{Top}/O).
\end{array}
\]
For the left square, a representative $S^2\to B\mathcal D$ of $\partial_{\mathcal D}^{-1}x$ becomes, after the chosen equivalence and adjunction, a map $S^8\to Y$. Precomposing the former with $\eta_2$ precomposes the latter with $\Sigma^6\eta_2=\eta_8$. The right square is naturality of stabilization.

For precision, write $\mathfrak m:B\mathcal D\to\Omega^6_0Y$ for this chosen smoothing-section equivalence and $\operatorname{adj}_6$ for sixfold adjunction. Then the maps just used are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison-composite}
\begin{split}
\mu_j&=\operatorname{adj}_6\circ\mathfrak m_*\circ\partial_{\mathcal D}^{-1},\\
\Gamma_j&=s_*\circ\operatorname{adj}_6\circ\mathfrak m_*\circ\partial_{\mathcal D}^{-1},
\qquad j=1,2.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
In the second line the target is identified with $\Theta_{j+7}$. In particular, the equivalence used to compute the Hopf operation and the one used to identify the disk-family sphere are the same smoothing comparison. The arrows and their compatibilities are summarized by
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
\pi_j\mathcal D&\xrightarrow{\ \partial_{\mathcal D}^{-1}\ }&\pi_{j+1}B\mathcal D
&\xrightarrow{\ \mathfrak m_*\ }&\pi_{j+1}\Omega^6_0Y\\
&&&&\big\downarrow\operatorname{adj}_6\\
\Theta_{j+7}&\xleftarrow{\ \cong\ }&\pi_{j+7}(\operatorname{Top}/O)
&\xleftarrow{\ s_*\ }&\pi_{j+7}Y.
\end{array}
\]
The universal-bundle boundary supplies the first arrow, the smoothing-section construction supplies the second, and adjunction turns precomposition by $\eta_2$ into precomposition by $\Sigma^6\eta_2$. Stabilization commutes with that precomposition. The identification of the composite with $\Gamma_j$, rather than merely an abstract group isomorphism, is the stabilization compatibility discussed next.

Under $\Theta_{j+7}\cong\pi_{j+7}(\operatorname{Top}/O)$, the composite $s_*\mu_j$ is $\Gamma_j$ for $j=1,2$. This is the stabilization compatibility of the smoothing-section and disk-family constructions; see \cite[Theorem 1.3]{Burghelea} and the Morlet--Gromoll stabilization diagram in \cite[Section 2.4]{Wang}. To compare conventions, the latter reference uses $\mathrm{PL}/O$. The stable smoothing fibration
\[
\mathrm{PL}/O\longrightarrow\operatorname{Top}/O\longrightarrow\operatorname{Top}/\mathrm{PL},
\qquad \operatorname{Top}/\mathrm{PL}\simeq K(\mathbb Z/2,3),
\]
gives natural isomorphisms from $\pi_k(\mathrm{PL}/O)$ to $\pi_k(\operatorname{Top}/O)$ for $k=8,9$ \cite{KS}. These isomorphisms commute with precomposition by $\eta_8$. The comparison with the cited diagram uses the natural maps from the unstable smoothing spaces to these stable spaces; no arbitrary identification of unstable $\mathrm{PL}$ and topological smoothing spaces is needed.

Finally, the compatibility with the Bredon pairing in \cite[Theorem 1.5]{Burghelea} identifies this precomposition operation with $\eta\,\Gamma_1(x)$. The final equality is obtained in the smoothing group $\pi_9(\operatorname{Top}/O)\cong\Theta_9$ before any passage to $\operatorname{coker}J_9$. It therefore retains the $bP_{10}$ component. Reading the diagram proves \eqref{eq:Qgromoll}.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:gauge-localization}
Let $P\to S^4$ be the left quaternionic Hopf bundle, let $\mathcal H$ be its smooth gauge group, and let $\mathcal H_*$ consist of gauge transformations equal to $1$ on a chosen fiber over $x_0$. Choose a closed four-ball $U\subset S^4$ with $x_0$ in the interior of its complementary ball. Gauge transformations supported in $\operatorname{int}U$ induce an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localgauge}
\pi_1\operatorname{Map}_*(S^4,S^3)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_1\mathcal H
\cong\pi_5S^3=\mathbb Z/2.
\end{equation}
A smooth based loop that is constant on a parameter collar can be homotoped to such a supported loop, through based loops with a common constant parameter collar. The corresponding gauge diffeomorphisms give an isotopy of the suspended attaching maps, relative to the two polar neighborhoods.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Evaluation on the chosen fiber is a locally trivial fibration of gauge groups. Since $\pi_2S^3=\pi_1S^3=0$, its homotopy exact sequence gives $\pi_1\mathcal H_*\cong\pi_1\mathcal H$.

Put $V=\overline{S^4\setminus U}$. A trivialization on $V$ identifies restriction of based gauge transformations with the map in the sequence
\[
\mathcal H_U\longrightarrow\mathcal H_*
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Map}_*((V,x_0),(S^3,1)).
\]
One may first work with continuous sections of the adjoint bundle. In particular, the inclusion of transformations supported in $\operatorname{int}U$ induces the claimed isomorphism on $\pi_1$.

The homotopies may be taken relative to the base point of the parameter circle. Precompose every loop and every intermediate loop with one fixed smooth reparameterization that is constant near that base point. Relative smoothing then makes the family smooth while retaining a common constant parameter collar. Such a family gives an isotopy of the suspended gauge diffeomorphisms: every slice is a gauge diffeomorphism, and constancy on the parameter collar makes every slice the identity on fixed polar neighborhoods. Finally, adjunction gives $\pi_1\operatorname{Map}_*(S^4,S^3)=\pi_5S^3$.

\emph{Common collar and uniform estimates.}
Pull back the positive-side boundary coordinates by $\phi$ and use normal collars. On $(-2a,2a)\times X$, with $X$ the common compact boundary, the glued continuous metric is
\[
dt^2+h(t),\qquad h(0^-)=h(0^+)=h_0.
\]
The parameter increases from $M_-$ to $M_+$. The two families are smooth up to $t=0$ from their respective sides. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gluejump}
A=h'(0^-),\quad B=h'(0^+),\quad D=A-B=2(\mathrm{II}_-+\phi^*\mathrm{II}_+)\ge0.
\end{equation}
All tensor comparisons below use $\bar g=dt^2+h_0$ and the fixed collar identification. On finitely many coordinate charts covering $X$, smoothness on the two closed half-collars gives uniform bounds for every derivative used below. In particular, for $|s|\le2\delta$, separately on each side,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gluejets}
\begin{split}
\|h(s)-h_0\|_{C^2(X)}&\le C\delta,\\
\|h'(s)-A\|_{C^1(X)}&\le C\delta\quad(s<0),\\
\|h'(s)-B\|_{C^1(X)}&\le C\delta\quad(s>0),\qquad
\|h''(s)\|_{C^0(X)}\le C.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Constants denoted by $C$ may increase, but depend only on the fixed metrics and collar, not on $\delta$. The notation $O(\delta)$ for a tensor means a bound $C\delta$ in its $\bar g$-norm, uniformly in the indicated collar. The width $a$ is fixed first and may be chosen inside any prescribed seam neighborhood.

\emph{The kernel and the distributional derivative.}
Choose the smooth even probability density
\[
\rho(v)=c_\rho\exp\bigl(-1/(1-v^2)\bigr)\quad(|v|<1),\qquad
\rho(v)=0\quad(|v|\ge1),
\]
where $c_\rho>0$ normalizes its integral. It is nonincreasing for $v\ge0$. For $0<\delta<a/4$, define, on $|t|<a$,
\[
\rho_\delta(t)=\delta^{-1}\rho(t/\delta),\qquad
\langle T\rangle_\delta(t)=\int_{-1}^1\rho(v)T(t-\delta v)\,dv,
\qquad h_\delta=\langle h\rangle_\delta.
\]
Averages use the fixed product identification of the tangent spaces; no metric is used to raise indices before averaging. Positivity of $h_\delta$ follows from positivity of the metrics being averaged. The family $h_\delta$ is smooth in both variables. Indeed, normal convolution smooths each piecewise smooth family, and all tangential derivatives commute with the integral.

Let $S=h'$ off the seam and let $h''_{\mathrm{reg}}$ denote the ordinary second derivative on the two sides. Continuity of $h$ and \eqref{eq:gluejump} give the distributional identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gluedistribution}
h_\delta'=\langle S\rangle_\delta,\qquad
h_\delta''=\langle h''_{\mathrm{reg}}\rangle_\delta-\rho_\delta(t)D.
\end{equation}
For $|t|\le\delta$, set
\[
\lambda(t)=\int_{t/\delta}^1\rho(v)\,dv,
\]
the mass sampling the negative side. The monotonicity and symmetry of $\rho$ imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernelmass}
\lambda(1-\lambda)\le\delta\rho_\delta(t).
\end{equation}
For example, if $u=t/\delta\ge0$, then
$\lambda\le(1-u)\rho(u)\le\rho(u)$; if $u\le0$, the same argument gives $1-\lambda\le\rho(u)$. This also proves the inequality at the endpoints, where both sides vanish. Equations~\eqref{eq:gluejets} give, uniformly on this strip,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:glueaveragejets}
h_\delta=h_0+O(\delta)\ \text{in }C^2(X),\qquad
h_\delta'=\lambda A+(1-\lambda)B+O(\delta)\ \text{in }C^1(X).
\end{equation}

\emph{Curvature identities.}
For a smooth family $h$, put $S=h'$. In the curvature-operator convention of \cref{sec:connection}, the metric $dt^2+h$ satisfies
\begin{align}
\mathcal R(X\wedge Y,X\wedge Y)
&=\mathcal R_h(X\wedge Y,X\wedge Y)
-\tfrac14\bigl(S(X,X)S(Y,Y)-S(X,Y)^2\bigr),\label{eq:glueGauss}\\
\mathcal R(\partial_t\wedge Z,\partial_t\wedge W)
&=-\tfrac12h''(Z,W)+\tfrac14(Sh^{-1}S)(Z,W),\label{eq:glueRiccati}\\
\mathcal R(\partial_t\wedge X,Y\wedge Z)
&=\tfrac12\bigl((\nabla^h_ZS)(X,Y)-(\nabla^h_YS)(X,Z)\bigr).
\label{eq:glueCodazzi}
\end{align}
Here all capital-letter vectors are tangent to $X$. These formulas follow directly from
$\Gamma^t_{ij}=-S_{ij}/2$,
$\Gamma^i_{tj}=h^{ik}S_{kj}/2$, and
$\Gamma^i_{jk}=\Gamma^i_{jk}(h)$ in the coordinate curvature formula \eqref{eq:coordinateR}. Alternatively, they are the Gauss, normal-curvature, and Codazzi formulas for the slices. We compare their covariant components, so the possibly large term in \eqref{eq:gluedistribution} is never multiplied by an approximate inverse metric.

Let $\mathcal R_\delta$ be the curvature tensor of $dt^2+h_\delta$, and let $\overline{\mathcal R}_\delta=\langle\mathcal R\rangle_\delta$ average the ordinary curvature tensors of the original metrics off the seam. The value at the seam itself is immaterial to this integral.

\emph{The tangential block.}
For a symmetric bilinear form $T$ define
$\Delta_T(X,Y)=T(X,X)T(Y,Y)-T(X,Y)^2$.
For $\widehat S=\lambda A+(1-\lambda)B$, the exact quadratic identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gluedetvariance}
\lambda\Delta_A+(1-\lambda)\Delta_B-\Delta_{\widehat S}
=\lambda(1-\lambda)\Delta_D.
\end{equation}
The intrinsic curvature tensor is a smooth function of a metric and its first two tangential derivatives on uniformly positive metrics. Thus \eqref{eq:gluejets}--\eqref{eq:glueaveragejets} imply
$\mathcal R_{h_\delta}-\langle\mathcal R_{h}\rangle_\delta=O(\delta)$.
Replacing $S(t-\delta v)$ by $A$ or $B$ in the quadratic terms also has error $O(\delta)$. It follows from \eqref{eq:glueGauss} and \eqref{eq:gluedetvariance} that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gluetangent}
(\mathcal R_\delta-\overline{\mathcal R}_\delta)(X\wedge Y,X\wedge Y)
=\tfrac14\lambda(1-\lambda)\Delta_D(X,Y)
+O(\delta)|X\wedge Y|_{h_0}^2.
\end{equation}
The remainder is a uniformly bounded curvature-type tensor times $\delta$, which gives the stated area bound even for dependent or nearly dependent $X,Y$. Since $D\ge0$, its restriction to any two-plane has nonnegative determinant; hence $\Delta_D\ge0$.

\emph{The normal and cross blocks.}
Keeping the second-derivative average in \eqref{eq:gluedistribution} exact, use
$h_\delta^{-1}=h_0^{-1}+O(\delta)$ and the same estimate for every sampled $h^{-1}$. The quadratic identity
\[
\widehat S h_0^{-1}\widehat S
-\lambda A h_0^{-1}A-(1-\lambda)B h_0^{-1}B
=-\lambda(1-\lambda)D h_0^{-1}D
\]
and \eqref{eq:glueRiccati} yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gluenormal}
\begin{split}
(\mathcal R_\delta-\overline{\mathcal R}_\delta)
(\partial_t\wedge Z,\partial_t\wedge Z)
={}&\tfrac12\rho_\delta(t)D(Z,Z)\\
&-\tfrac14\lambda(1-\lambda)(Dh_0^{-1}D)(Z,Z)
+O(\delta)|Z|_{h_0}^2.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Let $L=\sup_X\|h_0^{-1}D\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\infty$. The spectral theorem gives
$Dh_0^{-1}D\le LD$. By \eqref{eq:kernelmass}, the two displayed leading terms in \eqref{eq:gluenormal} have sum at least
\[
\rho_\delta(t)(1/2-\delta L/4)D(Z,Z)\ge0
\]
when $\delta L\le2$. This remains true in every kernel direction of $D$.

For the cross block, \eqref{eq:glueCodazzi} is linear in the first tangential derivatives of $S$. Tangential differentiation commutes with averaging. The Christoffel symbols of $h_\delta$ and of every sampled $h$ differ from those of $h_0$ by $O(\delta)$, while $S$ and its tangential derivatives are uniformly bounded. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gluecross}
\bigl|(\mathcal R_\delta-\overline{\mathcal R}_\delta)
(\partial_t\wedge Z,\eta)\bigr|
\le C\delta|Z|_{h_0}|\eta|_{h_0},\qquad \eta\in\Lambda^2TX.
\end{equation}
In particular there is no unbounded cross-block error at the seam.

\emph{All sectional curvatures and localization.}
Every decomposable bivector is of the form
\[
\xi=(b\partial_t+X)\wedge(c\partial_t+Y)
=X\wedge Y+\partial_t\wedge(bY-cX).
\]
Its tangential component is decomposable. Combining \eqref{eq:gluetangent}--\eqref{eq:gluecross}, and using $2uv\le u^2+v^2$ for the cross error, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:glueallplanes}
\mathcal R_\delta(\xi,\xi)
\ge\overline{\mathcal R}_\delta(\xi,\xi)-C\delta|\xi|_{\bar g}^2
\qquad(|t|\le\delta).
\end{equation}
This argument uses positivity on decomposable bivectors only; no positive-curvature-operator hypothesis has been imposed on either original metric.

Compactness and the strict hypotheses give a number $\gamma>0$ with
$\sec(g_\pm)\ge\kappa+\gamma$. Applying this bound to the same decomposable $\xi$ in every sampled tangent space gives
\[
\overline{\mathcal R}_\delta(\xi,\xi)
\ge(\kappa+\gamma)\langle|\xi|_{dt^2+h}^2\rangle_\delta.
\]
On the crossing strip, the averaged squared norm differs from
$|\xi|_{dt^2+h_\delta}^2$ by at most $C\delta|\xi|_{\bar g}^2$, by \eqref{eq:gluejets}. These norms are uniformly equivalent. Equation~\eqref{eq:glueallplanes} consequently gives
$\sec(dt^2+h_\delta)\ge\kappa+\gamma/2$ there after decreasing $\delta$.
For $\delta\le|t|<a$, the convolution samples only one smooth side. The one-sided smooth derivative bounds give uniform $C^2$ convergence to the original metric on this region, and hence $\sec>\kappa$ there as well.

Finally choose a fixed smooth cutoff $\chi(t)$ equal to one for $|t|\le a/2$ and zero for $|t|\ge3a/4$, and replace $h$ by
\[
\widetilde h_\delta=h+\chi(t)(h_\delta-h).
\]
This is smooth across zero, positive definite, and equals $h$ outside the chosen collar. On the support of $d\chi$ the original family is smooth and $h_\delta-h\to0$ in $C^2$; the cutoff and its derivatives are fixed before $\delta$ is chosen. The same strict curvature margin therefore survives this final modification. This proves the lemma with the prescribed attaching map and support.
