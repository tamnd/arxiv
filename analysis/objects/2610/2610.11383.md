<!-- theorema-1: body_md -->
\label{main thm}
   Let $\mathbb{F}$ be a finite rank non-abelian free group and let $\phi\in \operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})$.
   The free-by-cyclic group $\mathbb{G} \cong \mathbb{F} \rtimes_\phi \mathbb{Z}$ is finite index rigid if and only if $\phi$ has infinite order in $\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})$.
<!-- end theorema-1 -->
<!-- theorema-2: body_md -->
\label{linearly growing are fir}
    Let $\mathbb{F}$ be a finite rank non-abelian free group. 
    Let $\phi\in \operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})$ be a polynomially growing outer automorphism with $\deg(\phi) \geq 1$.
    Then the free-by-cyclic group $\mathbb{G} \cong \mathbb{F} \rtimes_\phi \mathbb{Z}$  is finite index rigid.
<!-- end theorema-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{reduction to normal}
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $G$ is FIR.
    \item Every finite index subgroup of $G$ is FIR.
    \item Every finite index subgroup $H\le G$ has a finite index subgroup $K\le H$ that is nFIR.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The implications $(1)\implies (2)\implies (3)$ are clear.
To prove $(3)\implies (1)$, let $H_1,H_2\le G$ be finite index subgroups, and let $f:H_1\to H_2$ be an isomorphism. 
By the assumption there exists a finite index subgroup $K_2\le H_2$ which is nFIR.
Let $K_1=f^{-1}(K_2)$.
Then $[H_1:K_1]=[H_2:K_2]$.
Let $N\le K_1\cap K_2$ be a normal finite index subgroup of $G$. Then $N\lhd K_2$, and $f(N)\lhd K_2$ (because $N\lhd K_1$).
Since $K_2$ is nFIR, $[K_2:N]=[K_2:f(N)]=[K_1:N]$. 
Finally, using $$[G:H_1][H_1:K_1][K_1:N]=[G:N]=[G:H_2][H_2:K_2][K_2:N]$$ we conclude that $[G:H_1]=[G:H_2]$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a set of groups closed under taking finite index subgroups. A \emph{multiplicative function} $\xi\colon\mathcal{G}\to \mathbb{R}$ is a 
function that satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\xi$ is a group invariant, i.e. if $G_1\simeq G_2$ then $\xi(G_1)=\xi(G_2)$.
    \item $\xi$ is proportional to index, i.e. if $H\le G$ has finite index then $\xi(H)=[G:H]\xi(G)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The two conditions in the above definition imply that if $H_1,H_2$ are abstractly commensurable, then $\xi(H_1)$ is determined by $\xi(H_2)$. Thus, $\xi$ can be defined independently on abstract commensurability classes.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{fir to multiplicative}
    Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a set of groups closed under taking finite index subgroups. There exists a multiplicative function $\xi\colon \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$ such that $\xi(G)>0$ for every finite index rigid group.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $G$ is finite index rigid then any group commensurable to $G$ is finite index rigid.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a set of groups closed under taking finite index subgroups. Let $G$ be a group acting on a set $X$ with finitely many orbits and stabilizers in $\mathcal{G}$. Let $\xi\colon \mathcal{G}\to \mathbb{R}$ be a multiplicative function.
Define
$$\chi_\xi(G\curvearrowright X) = \sum_{\overline{x}\in X/G} \xi(G_{\overline{x}}), $$ where $G_{\overline{x}}$ denotes the stabilizer in $G$ of a representative of $\overline{x}$ in $X$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{sum of indices}
    Let $G\curvearrowright  X$ transitively and let $H\le G$ be a finite index subgroup.  Let $x_1,\dots,x_k\in X$ be $H$-orbit representatives with stabilizers $H_{x_i}$ (resp. $G_{x_i}$) in $H$ (resp. $G$), then $\sum _{i=1}^k[G_{x_i}:H_{x_i}]=[G:H].$
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Fix $x_0\in X$, and fix $g_i\in G$ such that $g_i x_0=x_i$.
    Fix  
    $g_{i1},\dots,g_{id_i}$ 
    representatives of right $H_{x_i}$-cosets in $G_{x_i}$ (where $d_i=[G_{x_i}:H_{x_i}]$).
    Let us show that the set $\{g_{ij}g_i\mid 1\le i\le k,1\le j\le d_i\}$, which has size $\sum_{i=1}^k d_i$ is a set of right $H$-coset representatives in $G$.

    
    For all $g\in G$, there exists a unique $i$ and some $h\in H$ such that $gx_0=hx_i=gg_i^{-1}x_i$, from which we get $h^{-1} g g_i^{-1} \in G_{x_i}$. There exists a unique $g_{ij}$ such that $h^{-1} g g_i^{-1}=h'g_{ij}$ for $h'\in H_{x_i}$.
    Therefore, $g=hh'g_{ij}g_i$ is contained in the right $H$-coset $Hg_{ij}g_i$.

    Suppose $Hg_{ij}g_i = Hg_{kl}g_k$.   
     Since $g_{ij}g_i x_0 = x_i$ and $g_{kl}g_k x_0 = x_k$, we get $H x_i=Hg_{ij}g_i x_0 = Hg_{kl}g_k x_0=H x_k$. which implies  $i=k$,  $g_i=g_k$
    and $Hg_{ij}=Hg_{il}$. 
Thus $g_{ij}g_{il}^{-1} \in H$ and also by definition $g_{ij}g_{il}^{-1} \in G_{x_i}$. Therefore, $g_{ij}g_{il}^{-1} \in H_{x_i} = H \cap G_{x_i}$ which implies $H_{x_i}g_{ij}=H_{x_i}g_{il}$ and hence $j=l$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:gen Euler char}
Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a set of groups closed under taking finite index subgroups, and let $\xi:\mathcal{G}\to \mathbb{R}$ be a multiplicative function. Let $G$ be a group acting on a set $X$ with finitely many orbits and stabilizers in $\mathcal{G}$.
If $H\le G$ has finite index, then 
$\chi_\xi(H\curvearrowright X)=[G:H]\chi_\xi(G\curvearrowright X)$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $v\in X/G$ and let $v_1,\dots,v_k\in X/H$ be the elements that project to $v$ under the quotient map $X/H\to X/G$. Then by \Cref{sum of indices} and multiplicativity of the function $\xi$, we get
    $$[G:H]\xi(G_v)=\sum _{i=1}^k[G_{v}:H_{v_i}]\xi(G_v)=\sum _{i=1}^k\xi(H_{v_i}).$$
    Summing over all vertices of $X/G$ gives the desired equality.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{canonical tree}
    Let $\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{F} \rtimes_\phi \mathbb{Z} = \left< \mathbb{F},t  \mid  tgt^{-1} =\Phi(g) \right>$ where $\phi$ is a UPG outer automorphism of degree $d$ and $\Phi \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})$ represents $\phi$.
    
    Then there exists a tree $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{T}_\mathbb{G}$ and a minimal action $\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright \mathbb{T}$ such that:
    \begin{enumerate}[label = (T\arabic*)]
        \item\label{T: vertex groups} 
        Vertex groups are polynomial free-by-cyclic \footnote{Here \emph{free-by-cyclic} includes degenerate cases, such as $\mathbb{Z}=1\rtimes \mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}^2=\mathbb{Z}\rtimes \mathbb{Z}$, which are clearly polynomially growing of degree $0$.} groups of degree at most $d-1$, with at least one vertex group of degree equal to $d-1$. 
        \item \label{T: finite index}
        If $H\le \mathbb{G}$ has finite index, then $\mathbb{T}_H$ is $H$-equivariantly isomorphic to $\mathbb{T}_G$.
        \item\label{T: commensurability invariance} If $\eta\colon H\to H'\le \mathbb{G}$ is an isomorphism between finite index subgroups $H,H'\le \mathbb{G}$, then there is an $\eta$-equivariant isomorphism $H\curvearrowright \mathbb{T}_\mathbb{G}\to H'\curvearrowright \mathbb{T}_\mathbb{G}$. 
    \end{enumerate}
    If $d=1$, then moreover
    \begin{enumerate}[label = (T\arabic*)]\setcounter{enumi}{3}
        \item\label{T: edge groups linear} Edge groups are maximal $\mathbb{Z}^2$ subgroups of $\mathbb{G}$.
        \item\label{T: vertex groups linear} The tree has a bipartition $V(\mathbb{T}) = V_0 \sqcup V_1$. The stabilizers of vertices in $V_0$ are of the form $F \times \left< yt \right>$ where $F\le \mathbb{F}$ is non-abelian and $y\in \mathbb{F}$; and those of vertices in $V_1$ are isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}^2$. 
        \item\label{T: cylinders linear} Every vertex in $V_1$ has finite valence.
    \end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
First consider the limiting tree $\mathbb{F}\curvearrowright T_\phi$ given by 
\cite[Theorem 13.2]{cohen1995verysmall} when $d=1$ 
and \cite{BFH:TitsII} when $d \ge 2$ (also see \cite[Theorem 6.1]{andrewguerchhorbezwade} for details).
The action $\mathbb{F}\curvearrowright T_\phi$ has the following properties:
\begin{itemize}
    \item edge stabilizers of $\mathbb{F} \curvearrowright T_\phi$ are maximal infinite cyclic if $d=1$ and trivial if $d\ge 2$,
    \item vertex stabilizers of $\mathbb{F} \curvearrowright T_\phi$ are either maximal $\phi$-invariant subgroups $F$ of $\mathbb{F}$ of rank at least 2 such that $\deg(\phi|_F) < \deg(\phi)$, or trivial when $d \ge 2$, 
    \item there exists at least one vertex group $F$ as above with $\deg(\phi|_F) = d-1$.
\end{itemize}


The tree $T_\phi$ is $\phi$-invariant, i.e. there exists a $\Phi$-equivariant graph automorphism $f\colon T_\phi\to T_\phi$. 
The action $\mathbb{F}\curvearrowright T_\phi$ can be extended to an action $\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright T_\phi$ by setting $t \cdot x = f(x)$ (see \cite[Lemma 3.1]{JPPoly}). 
The action $\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright T_\phi$ has the following properties (see \cite[Lemma 6.7, 6.8]{andrewguerchhorbezwade}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item the edge stabilizers of $\mathbb{G} \curvearrowright T_\phi$ are maximal $\mathbb{Z}^2$ subgroups if $d=1$, and maximal cyclic if $d\ge 2$,
    \item vertex stabilizers of $\mathbb{G} \curvearrowright T_\phi$  are either maximal polynomial free-by-cyclic subgroups of the form $F \rtimes_\psi \mathbb{Z}$ for a free group $F$ of rank at least 2 and $\deg(\psi) < \deg(\phi)$, or $\mathbb{Z}$ when $d \geq 2$, 
                                                                                    
    \item at least one vertex group is of the form $F \rtimes_\psi \mathbb{Z}$ with $\deg(\psi) = d-1$. 
\end{itemize}  

Finally, let $\mathbb{T}$ be the collapsed tree of cylinders of $T_\phi$ \`a la Guirardel-Levitt \cite{guirardel2011trees} with respect to the collection $\mathcal{E}$ of maximal abelian subgroups and the admissible relation of commensurability \cite[Definition 6.13]{andrewguerchhorbezwade}.

Property \ref{T: vertex groups}, \ref{T: finite index} follow from \cite[Theorem 6.15, Proposition 6.18]{andrewguerchhorbezwade}, respectively.

Property \ref{T: commensurability invariance} is as follows: With respect to the action $\rho$ of $\mathbb{G}$ on the limiting tree, $T_\phi$ is a slender JSJ tree relative to free-by-cyclic subgroups of lower degrees and with $\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{Z}^2$ (depending on $d$) edge stabilizers by \cite[Proposition 6.12]{andrewguerchhorbezwade}. Therefore, the same is true for the restriction $\rho|_H$ and $\rho|_{H'}\circ \eta$. It follows from Property \ref{T: finite index} and \cite[Corollary 4.10]{guirardel2011trees} that an $\eta$-equivariant isomorphism $\mathbb{T}\to \mathbb{T}$ exists.

Property \ref{T: edge groups linear} and \ref{T: vertex groups linear} follow from \cite[Lemma 6.16]{andrewguerchhorbezwade} for UPG $\phi$ (see also \cite[Proposition 5.2.2]{andrew2022free}). 


Property \ref{T: cylinders linear} is as follows: By maximality of edge stabilizers \ref{T: edge groups linear}, the stabilizer of each $v\in V_1$ is equal to the stabilizer of each of its incident edges. This shows that the valence of $v$ in $\mathbb{T}$ is equal to the valence of the corresponding vertex $\bar v$ in the quotient graph $\mathbb{T}/\mathbb{G}$. Since $\mathbb{G}$ is finitely generated, and $\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright \mathbb{T}$ is minimal, the graph $\mathbb{T}/\mathbb{G}$ is finite. In particular, $\bar v$ has finite valence, and so does $v$.

    \begin{equation}\label{eq1}
        d=[\mathbb{G}:\hat G]=[G_i:\hat G_i] u_i.
    \end{equation}

    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq2}[G_i:\hat G_i]=[Z_i:\hat Z_i][F_i:\hat F_i]

\label{prop:fir linear}
        Let $\mathbb{F}$ be a finite rank non-abelian free group.

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
By \Cref{reduction to UPG and normal}, it suffices to show that $\mathbb{G}$ is normal finite index rigid when $\phi$ is a linear UPG outer automorphism.
    Let $H$ be a normal finite index subgroup of $\mathbb{G}$. 
    Define a non-negative real-valued symmetric matrix $\mathbf{B}^\mathbb{G}$ indexed by $V(\Gamma(\mathbb{G}))^2$ to be
    \[\mathbf{B}^\mathbb{G}_{u,v} = \begin{cases}
        \sum_{e} \alpha(e) &u\ne v\\
        2\sum_{e} \alpha(e) &u= v
    \end{cases} \]
    where the sum runs over all edges $e$ with endpoints $u,v$. Similarly, one can define a matrix $\mathbf{B}^H$ using $\Gamma(H)$. However, there is some symmetry in $\mathbf{B}^H$ and we will define a new matrix $\mathbf{H}^H$ obtained from $\mathbf{B}^H$ by an averaging process. 
    
    Let $\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(H))$ be the group of automorphisms of $\Gamma(H)$ preserving the weights.
    Consider $O(H) = V(\Gamma(H))/\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(H))$ and denote by $|a|$ the number of vertices in an orbit $a$. The symmetry in $B^H$ is as follows (see \cite[Theorem 2]{yuwang}): for two orbits $a, b \in O(H)$, the interaction of two vertices $p \in a$ and $q \in a$ with the orbit $b$ is exactly the same in the sense that 
    \[ \sum_{s \in b} \mathbf{B}^H_{p,s} = \sum_{s \in b} \mathbf{B}^H_{q,s}.\]
    
    Motivated by this symmetry, define a non-negative real-valued symmetric matrix $\mathbf{H}^H$ indexed by $O(H)^2$ to be
\[\mathbf{H}^H_{a,b}=\frac{1}{|a||b|}\sum_{u\in a} \sum_{v\in b} \mathbf{B}^H_{u,v} \]

    Let $p\colon \Gamma(H)\to \Gamma(\mathbb{G})$ be the quotient map. 
    For $a \in O(H)$ and $u \in V(\Gamma(\mathbb{G}))$, define \[\mathbf{X}_{a,u} ^H= \begin{cases} |p^{-1}(u)|/|a| & p^{-1}(u) \subseteq a\\ 0 &p^{-1}(u) \nsubseteq a\end{cases} \quad \text{ and }\quad \mathbf{W}^H_{a,u} =\begin{cases} 1 &p^{-1}(u) \subseteq a\\ 0 &p^{-1}(u) \nsubseteq a\end{cases},  \]
    where $\mathbf{X}^H$ captures normalized sizes of fibers under the map $p$ and $\mathbf{W}^H$ records orbit membership. 
    Then, as in the proof of  \cite[Theorem 3]{yuwang}, the equation from \Cref{alpha of cover} that captures the information of the quotient map $p$, can be converted into the matrix equation 
        \[\mathbf{X}^H   \mathbf{B}^\mathbb{G}=[\mathbb{G}:H] \, \mathbf{H}^H  \mathbf{W}^H.\]

    Now let $H,H'$ be normal finite index subgroups of $\mathbb{G}$, and let $\eta\colon H\to H'$ be an isomorphism.
    By \Cref{isomorphic covers}, there is a weight-preserving graph isomorphism $\bar \Theta\colon \Gamma(H)\to \Gamma(H')$.
    Therefore, $\mathbf{H}^H$ and $\mathbf{H}^{H'}$ are the same up to re-indexing given by $\bar \Theta$. In other words, $\mathbf{H}^H$ is independent of the quotient maps $p$. However, note that both $\mathbf{X}^H$ and $\mathbf{W}^H$ depend on $p$.  
    As in the proof of \cite[Theorem 4]{yuwang}, by manipulating the two equations
  \begin{align*}
       \mathbf{X}^H   \mathbf{B}^\mathbb{G} & =[\mathbb{G}:H] \, \mathbf{H}^H\mathbf{W}^H \\ 
       \mathbf{X}^{H'}   \mathbf{B}^\mathbb{G} & =[\mathbb{G}:H'] \, \mathbf{H}^{H'} \mathbf{W}^{H'}
   \end{align*} 
    and an application of Perron--Frobenius theorem imply that $[\mathbb{G}:H]=[\mathbb{G}:H']$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop: multiplicative function for free-by-cyclic}
Let $d\ge 1$, and let $\mathcal{G}_d$ be the collection of all polynomial free-by-cyclic groups of degree $d$. There exists a multiplicative function $\xi_d \colon \mathcal{G}_d \to (0,\infty)$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
We prove the proposition by induction on $d$.
Every polynomial free-by-cyclic group $\mathbb{G}$ with $\deg(\mathbb{G}) =1$ is finite index rigid by \Cref{prop:fir linear} and hence, by \Cref{fir to multiplicative}, there exists a multiplicative function $\xi_1\colon \mathcal{G}_1\to (0,\infty)$.

Suppose for every $1 \leq i \leq d$ we have a multiplicative function $\xi_i\colon \mathcal{G}_i\to (0,\infty)$. 
Let $\mathcal{G}_0$ be the collection of all polynomial free-by-cyclic groups of degree $0$ (including degenerate free-by-cyclic groups such as $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}^2$).
Let $\xi_0 \colon \mathcal{G}_0\to [0,\infty)$ be the constant $0$ function and let $\xi \colon \bigcup_{i=0}^d\mathcal{G}_i\to [0,\infty)$ be defined by $\xi = \bigcup _{i=0}^d\xi_i$. 

Let $\mathbb{G}$ be a polynomial free-by-cyclic group with $\deg(\mathbb{G}) = d+1>1$. 
Let $\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright \mathbb{T}_\mathbb{G}$ be the canonical tree given by \Cref{canonical tree}. 
By \ref{T: vertex groups}, all vertex groups are in $\bigcup_{i=0}^d\mathcal{G}_i$ therefore $\xi(G_v)$ is defined for every vertex group $G_v$.
Moreover, at least one vertex group $G_{v_0}$ is contained in $\mathcal{G}_d$ which implies $\xi(G_{v_0})>0$. 
Therefore,
\[\chi_\xi(\mathbb{G} \curvearrowright \mathbb{T}_\mathbb{G}) = \sum_{v \in \mathbb{T}/\mathbb{G}} \xi(G_v) >0.\]

We define $\xi_{d+1}(\mathbb{G}):= \chi_\xi(\mathbb{G} \curvearrowright \mathbb{T}_\mathbb{G}) >0$. Since $\mathbb{T}_\mathbb{G}$ is canonical for $\mathbb{G}$ (in the sense of \ref{T: commensurability invariance}), $\xi_{d+1}(\mathbb{G})$ is an isomorphism invariant.  
Moreover, by \ref{T: finite index} if $H$ is a finite index subgroup of $\mathbb{G}$, then $H \curvearrowright \mathbb{T}_H$ is equivariantly isomorphic to $H\curvearrowright \mathbb{T}_\mathbb{G}$. By \Cref{cor:gen Euler char}, $$\xi_{d+1}(H) = \chi_\xi(H\curvearrowright \mathbb{T}_H) = \chi_\xi(H\curvearrowright \mathbb{T}_\mathbb{G}) = [\mathbb{G}:H]\chi_\xi(\mathbb{G} \curvearrowright \mathbb{T}_\mathbb{G}) = [\mathbb{G}:H]\xi_{d+1}(\mathbb{G}).$$ 
Thus, $\xi_{d+1}$ is a non-zero multiplicative function on $\mathcal{G}_{d+1}$.
