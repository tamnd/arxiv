<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:coarse-lift-residual}
Let $\mathbf{P}^c$ be the entropic plan induced by any finite coarse potentials, $\lambda_c=\max_J|\log(\sum_I P^c_{IJ}/b_J)|$ its largest log column-mass error, $R_Y=\max_J\max_{j\in J}\|y_j-y_J^c\|$ the largest target-cell radius, and $\theta=2\operatorname{diam}(\mathbf{X})R_Y/\varepsilon$. The plan $\mathbf{P}$ induced by the two lifts satisfies
\begin{equation}
\|\mathbf{P}\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{a}\|_1
\le (e^{\lambda_c}-1)+e^{\lambda_c}(e^\theta-1-\theta).
\label{eq:coarse-lift-bound}
\end{equation}
With the clouds exchanged, the same bound holds for the column residual $\|\mathbf{P}^\top\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{b}\|_1$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:rule}
Fix the potentials and $\delta_{\mathrm{rel}}>0$. Write $a_I=\sum_{i\in I}a_i$, $b_J=\sum_{j\in J}b_j$, and define
\begin{equation}
m_{IJ}=\max_{i\in I,j\in J}\frac{f_i+g_j-C_{ij}}{\varepsilon},
\qquad
\rho_{IJ}=\log\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{rel}}}{\max(a_I,b_J)}.
\label{eq:exacttest}
\end{equation}
If $m_{IJ}\le\rho_{IJ}$, then $\sum_{j\in J}P_{ij}\le\delta_{\mathrm{rel}}a_i$ for every $i\in I$, and $\sum_{i\in I}P_{ij}\le\delta_{\mathrm{rel}}b_j$ for every $j\in J$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:mask-error}
Let $\nu_i$ be the fraction of row~$i$'s plan mass omitted by the mask, with positive retained mass. Then \textup{(i)}~the sparse and full row updates differ by $|f_i^{+,\,\mathcal{A}}-f_i^+|=\varepsilon\log\frac{1}{1-\nu_i}$, and \textup{(ii)}~the barycentric projection over the admitted columns satisfies $\|T_\varepsilon^{\mathcal{A}}(x_i)-T_\varepsilon(x_i)\|\le\nu_i\,\operatorname{diam}(\mathbf{Y})$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
We benchmark 32 problems in $d{=}3$ with uniform weights, 16 on clustered clouds of 32 Gaussian clusters and 16 on heterogeneous clouds that mix a compact Gaussian, a thin needle and a 1\% uniform background. Both families span $n=m$ from $2^{19}$ to $2^{23}$ and $\varepsilon$ from 0.001 to 0.02. This range of $\varepsilon$ puts the blur $\sqrt\varepsilon$ between 0.032 and 0.14, or 3--14\% of the side of the unit cube of the clustered clouds. All methods receive the same coordinates and the full squared-Euclidean cost, GeomLoss always runs its multiscale solver, and the target is $\tau=0.005$. Appendix~\ref{app:benchmarks} gives the data, the protocol, the endpoint of every run and a sweep of GeomLoss's scaling and cluster scale.

Fix $J$ and $z=z_j$, and put $\ell_I=2(x^c_I-x_i)^\top z/\varepsilon$ and $F(s)=(\sum_I\pi_{I\mid J}e^{s\ell_I})^{-1}$, so that $\phi_{iJ}(z)=F(1)$. Exact centroids are convex combinations of points, so $|\ell_I|\le2\operatorname{diam}(\mathbf{X})R_Y/\varepsilon=\theta$. With $\mu_s$ and $v_s$ the mean and variance of $\ell_I$ under probabilities proportional to $\pi_{I\mid J}e^{s\ell_I}$, we have $F'(s)=-F(s)\mu_s$ and $F''(s)=F(s)(\mu_s^2-v_s)$. Since $\mu_s^2+v_s\le\theta^2$ and $F(s)\le e^{s\theta}$ on $[0,1]$, $|F''(s)|\le e^{s\theta}\theta^2$, and
\[
|F(1)-1-F'(0)|\le\int_0^1(1-s)e^{s\theta}\theta^2\,ds=e^\theta-1-\theta.
\]
The cell average of $F'(0)=-2(u_J-x_i)^\top z/\varepsilon$ vanishes because $y^c_J$ is the exact mass-weighted centroid, so $|\sum_{j\in J}(b_j/b_J)\phi_{iJ}(z_j)-1|\le e^\theta-1-\theta$. Substituting into \Cref{eq:lift-identity} with $\sum_Jw_{iJ}=1$, $c_J^{-1}\le e^{\lambda_c}$ and $|c_J^{-1}-1|\le e^{\lambda_c}-1$ gives, for every row,

\le(e^{\lambda_c}-1)+e^{\lambda_c}(e^\theta-1-\theta),
\]
and multiplying by $a_i$ and summing proves \Cref{eq:coarse-lift-bound}. The column bound exchanges the roles of the two clouds.

\label{prop:coarse-barycentric}
For $T_i=\sum_jp_{ij}y_j$ and $\widetilde T_i=\sum_Jw_{iJ}y_J^c$,
\begin{equation}
\|T_i-\widetilde T_i\|
\le \sum_Jp_{iJ}r_J
+\operatorname{diam}(\mathbf{Y}^c)\,
\operatorname{TV}_i.
\label{eq:coarse-barycentric-error}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $G_i=2a_i(x_i-T_i)$ and $\widetilde G_i=2a_i(x_i-\widetilde T_i)$,
\begin{equation}
\|G-\widetilde G\|_F
\le 2\left[\sum_i a_i^2
\left(\sum_Jp_{iJ}r_J+
\operatorname{diam}(\mathbf{Y}^c)\operatorname{TV}_i
\right)^2\right]^{1/2}.
\label{eq:coarse-gradient-error}
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Insert the intermediate barycentre $T_i^c=\sum_Jp_{iJ}y_J^c$. The within-cell term satisfies
\[
\|T_i-T_i^c\|
=\left\|\sum_Jp_{iJ}(\bar y_{iJ}-y_J^c)\right\|
\le\sum_Jp_{iJ}r_J.
\]
The differences $p_{iJ}-w_{iJ}$ sum to zero, so their positive and negative parts both have total mass $\operatorname{TV}_i$. If $\operatorname{TV}_i=0$, the two centroid averages coincide. Otherwise
\[
T_i^c-\widetilde T_i
=\operatorname{TV}_i\left(\sum_J\frac{(p_{iJ}-w_{iJ})_+}{\operatorname{TV}_i}y_J^c
        -\sum_J\frac{(w_{iJ}-p_{iJ})_+}{\operatorname{TV}_i}y_J^c\right).
\]
The two sums in parentheses are convex combinations of the same centroid set and their distance is at most its diameter. This proves $\|T_i^c-\widetilde T_i\| \le\operatorname{diam}(\mathbf{Y}^c)\operatorname{TV}_i$. The first claim follows by another triangle inequality. Multiplying row $i$ by $2a_i$, squaring, and summing proves \Cref{eq:coarse-gradient-error}.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:coarse-direction}
Let $G=\nabla\mathcal L(\mathbf{X})\ne0$, let $H\ne0$ be a supplied direction, and write $c=\langle G,H\rangle/(\|G\|_F\|H\|_F)$. Suppose the gradient of $\mathcal L$ is $\beta$-Lipschitz, with $\beta>0$, on the segment from $\mathbf{X}$ to $\mathbf{X}^+=\mathbf{X}-\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}H/\|H\|_F$. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(\mathbf{X}^+)\le\mathcal L(\mathbf{X})
-\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}\|G\|_F c+\frac{\beta\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}^2}{2}.
\label{eq:inexact-descent}
\end{equation}
If $c>0$ and $0<\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}<2\|G\|_F c/\beta$, the objective decreases. A sufficient condition for $c>0$ is $\|H-G\|_F\le\delta\|G\|_F$ with $0\le\delta<1$, which implies $c\ge\sqrt{1-\delta^2}$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $v=H/\|H\|_F$. The descent lemma for a $\beta$-smooth function gives
\[
\mathcal L(\mathbf{X}-\eta_{\mathrm{opt}} v)
\le\mathcal L(\mathbf{X})-\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}\langle G,v\rangle
+\frac{\beta\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}^2}{2}.
\]
Since $\langle G,v\rangle=\|G\|_F c$, this is \Cref{eq:inexact-descent}, and the stated step-size condition makes its right-hand side strictly smaller than $\mathcal L(\mathbf{X})$.

For the relative-error claim, set $u=G/\|G\|_F$, $h=H/\|G\|_F$, and $r=\|h\|_F>0$. If $c=\langle u,h\rangle/r$, then $\|h-u\|_F^2=r^2+1-2rc\le\delta^2$, and therefore
\[
c\ge\frac{r^2+1-\delta^2}{2r}
=\frac12\left(r+\frac{1-\delta^2}{r}\right)
\ge\sqrt{1-\delta^2},
\]
where the last step is the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality.

Write the plan at the current potentials, with no convergence assumed, as
\begin{equation}
P_{ij}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})=a_i\,b_j\exp\!\big((f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\varepsilon\big).
\label{eq:planapp}
\end{equation}
Every exponent in the tile is at most $m_{IJ}$, so $P_{ij}\le a_ib_je^{m_{IJ}}$ for $i\in I$, $j\in J$, and summing over the opposite block gives
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j'\in J}P_{ij'}\le a_i\,b_J\,e^{m_{IJ}},
\qquad
\sum_{i'\in I}P_{i'j}\le b_j\,a_I\,e^{m_{IJ}}.
\label{eq:tilebound}
\end{equation}
Since $e^{\rho_{IJ}}=\delta_{\mathrm{rel}}/\max(a_I,b_J)$ and both block masses are at most $\max(a_I,b_J)$, the hypothesis $m_{IJ}\le\rho_{IJ}$ bounds the two right-hand sides by $\delta_{\mathrm{rel}}a_i$ and $\delta_{\mathrm{rel}}b_j$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:io}
Let a neighbour block record occupy $\sigma=\Theta(B(d+1))$ scalars. Suppose one source block's queries and accumulators stay resident in fast memory of size $M\ge c_0\sigma$ for a fixed constant $c_0$, costs are computed online, CSR indices are read once per traversal, and caches are cold at the start of each half-step. If $L_f$ and $L_g$ count neighbour-record misses along the CSR and its transpose, one iteration evaluating both directions moves
\begin{equation}
Q_{\mathrm{iter}}=
\Theta\!\big((n+m)(d+1)+|\mathcal{A}|+\sigma[L_f+L_g]\big)
\label{eq:io}
\end{equation}
scalars between slow and fast memory. Each miss count lies between the number of active neighbour blocks in its traversal and $|\mathcal{A}|$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We count the $\hat\mathbf{f}$ half-step, and the $\hat\mathbf{g}$ half-step is the same count on the transposed index. Each of the $N_B$ source blocks moves its queries $\mathbf{X}_I$ and writes back $\hat\mathbf{f}_I$ once, $\Theta(B(d+1))$ scalars, because both stay resident for the whole row. Over both directions this is $\Theta((n+m)(d+1))$. The traversal reads $\mathrm{crow}$ once and each entry of $\mathrm{col}$ once, $\Theta(N_B+|\mathcal{A}|)$ scalars, and $N_B\le n$ is absorbed by the first term. An empty row reads no neighbour record. Its repair is a dense reduction, counted with the dense updates in \Cref{eq:forward-operation-count}. Visiting an admitted pair needs $\mathbf{Y}_J$, $\hat\mathbf{g}_J$ and $\boldsymbol{\delta}_J$, $\sigma=\Theta(B(d+1))$ scalars, and moves nothing when the record is already resident. The $L_f$ visits that miss contribute $\Theta(\sigma L_f)$. Summing gives \Cref{eq:io}. Each half-step starts cold, so every active neighbour block misses at least once. At most one miss is charged per admitted pair, so $L_f$ lies between the number of active neighbour blocks and $|\mathcal{A}|$, and so does $L_g$.
