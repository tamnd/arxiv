<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:caputo}
For $\gamma>0$ and $g\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}[0,\infty)$, the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order $\gamma$ is
\begin{equation}
  I_t^\gamma g(t) = \frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_0^t (t-\tau)^{\gamma-1} g(\tau)\,d\tau, \qquad t>0.
\end{equation}
For $0<\alpha<1$ and $f$ absolutely continuous, the Caputo fractional derivative of order $\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integral}
    D_t^\alpha f(t) = I_t^{1-\alpha} f'(t) 
    = \frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)} \int_0^t (t-\tau)^{-\alpha} f'(\tau)\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
We extend this definition to the endpoint $\alpha=1$ by the usual convention $D_t^1 f := f'$, recovering the ordinary derivative; this value is not covered by the integral formula \eqref{eq:integral} (which requires $1-\alpha>0$) but is the natural limiting case and is the one used whenever $\beta=1$ below.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:LTcaputo}
                                                                                                   
Let $0<\alpha\le1$ and let $f$ be of exponential order with $f'\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}[0,\infty)$. 
Then, for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$ and with $s^\alpha$ denoting the principal branch,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LTcaputo0}
    \mathcal{L}\{D_t^\alpha f\}(s) = s^\alpha \widehat{f}(s) - s^{\alpha-1}f(0). 
\end{equation}
In particular, if $f(0)=0$ then $\mathcal{L}\{D_t^\alpha f\}(s)=s^\alpha \widehat{f}(s)$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:branch}
Let $0<\gamma\le1$. If $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$, then $\operatorname{Re}(s^\gamma)>0$, for the principal branch of $s^\gamma$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$, the point $s$ lies strictly in the right half of the complex plane, so we may write it in polar form as 
$s=r\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}$ with modulus $r>0$ and argument 
$\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$. 
Recall that the argument is restricted to this open interval precisely because $s$ has positive real part. 
We use the principal branch of the power function, defined on this same right half-plane by
$s^\gamma := r^\gamma \,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\gamma\theta}$, 
i.e.\ it raises the modulus to the power $\gamma$ and multiplies the argument by $\gamma$.

Since $0<\gamma\le1$ and $\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, multiplying through by $\gamma$ gives $\gamma\theta\in(-\gamma\pi/2,\gamma\pi/2)\subseteq(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$: 
the rescaled angle $\gamma\theta$ still lies strictly between $-\pi/2$ and $\pi/2$,
and in particular it can never reach $\pm\pi/2$. 
This is exactly the range on which the cosine function is strictly positive, so $\cos(\gamma\theta)>0$.

Finally, taking the real part of $s^\gamma=r^\gamma\, \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\gamma\theta}=r^\gamma\big(\cos(\gamma\theta)+ \mathrm{i}\sin(\gamma\theta)\big)$ gives $\operatorname{Re}(s^\gamma) = r^\gamma\,\cos(\gamma\theta)$. 
Since $r^\gamma>0$ (as $r>0$) and $\cos(\gamma\theta)>0$ by the previous step, their product is positive, i.e.\ $\operatorname{Re}(s^\gamma)>0$, as claimed. 
We note that the assumption $\gamma\le1$ is essential here: for $\gamma>1$ the angle $\gamma\theta$ could exceed $\pi/2$ in absolute value even when $\theta$ is close to $\pm\pi/2$, making $\cos(\gamma\theta)$ negative and the conclusion false.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:regularity}
Throughout Sections~\ref{sec:line}--\ref{sec:graph}, we assume that the solutions under consideration exist and admit a Laplace transform in $t$, for every fixed $x$: 
for solutions posed on a domain $x>0$, this transform is assumed bounded as $x\to\infty$, for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$; for solutions posed on a domain $x<0$, it is assumed bounded as $x\to-\infty$, for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$.
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:exterior}
Let $u$ solve \eqref{eq:fracheat} for $x>0$, $t>0$, with quiescent data $u(x,0)=0$ for $x>0$ and $u(0,0)=0$, subject to Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity}. 
Then
\begin{equation} \label{eq:TBCext}
   \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\bigg|_{x=0} 
   = -\frac{1}{\kappa\,\Gamma(1-\beta/2)}\int_0^t \frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{\beta/2}}\,\frac{\partial u(0,\tau)}{\partial \tau}\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Applying the Laplace transform in $t$ to~\eqref{eq:fracheat} and using Lemma~\ref{lem:LTcaputo} with $\alpha=\beta$ and $u(x,0)=0$,
\begin{equation}
      s^\beta\,\widehat{u}(x,s) = \kappa^2\, \widehat{u}''(x,s), \qquad x>0,
\end{equation}
whose general solution is 
\begin{equation*}
    \widehat{u}(x,s) = A(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{xs^{\beta/2}/\kappa} 
    + B(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{-x s^{\beta/2}/\kappa}.
\end{equation*}
The characteristic roots of this constant-coefficient ODE are $\pm\sqrt{s^\beta}/\kappa$; since $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$ implies, by Lemma~\ref{lem:branch}, that $s^\beta$ itself lies in the right half-plane, its principal square root coincides with $s^{\beta/2}$, justifying the ansatz. 
 
By Lemma~\ref{lem:branch} (with $\gamma=\beta/2$), $\operatorname{Re}(s^{\beta/2})>0$ for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$, so $\mathrm{e}^{xs^{\beta/2}/\kappa}\to\infty$ as $x\to\infty$; Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity} forces $A(s)=0$, hence
\begin{equation*}
   \widehat{u}(x,s) = \widehat{u}(0,s)\, \mathrm{e}^{-x s^{\beta/2}/\kappa}.
\end{equation*}
Differentiating with respect to $x$ (justified by the interchange fact recorded in Section~\ref{sec:prelim}) and evaluating at $x=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LTflux}
     \widehat{u}'(0,s) = -\frac{s^{\beta/2}}{\kappa}\,\widehat{u}(0,s). 
\end{equation}
Applying the inverse Laplace transform to~\eqref{eq:LTflux}, 
and using Lemma~\ref{lem:LTcaputo} with $\alpha=\beta/2$ (noting $u(0,0)=0$), the multiplier $s^{\beta/2}$ maps to a Caputo fractional derivative of order $\beta/2$ in the time domain, 
yielding~\eqref{eq:TBCext}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:integrable}
Since $0<\beta/2\le1/2<1$, the kernel $(t-\tau)^{-\beta/2}$ is integrable on $[0,t]$ for every finite $t$: 
\begin{equation*}
    \int_0^t (t-\tau)^{-\beta/2}\,d\tau 
    = t^{1-\beta/2}/(1-\beta/2)<\infty.
\end{equation*}
The operator in~\eqref{eq:TBCext} is thus a genuine (weakly singular but well-defined) convolution acting on any $u(0,\cdot)\in C^1[0,T]$, for any fixed $T>0$; no regularity beyond what is already needed to form $\partial_\tau u(0,\tau)$ pointwise is required for the integral to make sense.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:uniqueness}
Suppose $u_1,u_2$ both solve~\eqref{eq:fracheat} for $x>0$ with $u_1(x,0)=u_2(x,0)=0$ and the same boundary trace $u_1(0,t)=u_2(0,t)=g(t)$, both subject to Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity}. Then $u_1\equiv u_2$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $u_1,u_2$ solve the problem of Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior} with the same boundary trace $g(t):=u_1(0,t)=u_2(0,t)$, and set $w:=u_1-u_2$.
By linearity, $w$ solves~\eqref{eq:fracheat} for $x>0$ with quiescent initial data $w(x,0)=0$, and its Laplace transform satisfies the same ODE as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unique-ode}
    s^\beta\,\widehat{w}(x,s) = \kappa^2\,\widehat{w}''(x,s), \qquad x>0, 
\end{equation}
with general solution $\widehat{w}(x,s) = A(s)\mathrm{e}^{xs^{\beta/2}/\kappa} + B(s)\mathrm{e}^{-xs^{\beta/2}/\kappa}$. 
The boundedness argument of that proof forces $A(s)=0$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unique-gen}
    \widehat{w}(x,s) = B(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{-x s^{\beta/2}/\kappa}. 
\end{equation}
The boundary traces cancel,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unique-bc}
     \widehat{w}(0,s) = \widehat{u}_1(0,s) - \widehat{u}_2(0,s) 
      = \widehat{g}(s) - \widehat{g}(s) = 0, 
\end{equation}
and evaluating~\eqref{eq:unique-gen} at $x=0$ gives $B(s)=\widehat{w}(0,s)=0$ by~\eqref{eq:unique-bc}. 
Hence, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unique-vanish}
    \widehat{w}(x,s) \equiv 0 \qquad \text{for every}\quad x>0,\ \operatorname{Re}(s)>0. 
\end{equation}
By Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity}, $w(x,\cdot)$ is of exponential order for each fixed $x$, and it is continuous in $t$ as a difference of classical solutions with continuous data; Lerch's uniqueness theorem~\cite{Doetsch1974} then upgrades~\eqref{eq:unique-vanish} to
\begin{equation*}
     w(x,t) = 0 \qquad \text{for every}\quad x>0,\ t\ge0,
\end{equation*}
i.e.\ $u_1\equiv u_2$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:interior}
Under the mirrored hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior} on the half-line $x<0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TBCint}
    \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\bigg|_{x=0} = +\frac{1}{\kappa\,\Gamma(1-\beta/2)}
    \int_0^t \frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{\beta/2}}\,\frac{\partial u(0,\tau)}{\partial \tau}\,d\tau. 
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Define $v(x,t):=u(-x,t)$ for $x>0$.
Since~\eqref{eq:fracheat} involves only $\partial_x^2$ and $D_t^\beta$, both unaffected by $x\mapsto-x$, $v$ solves~\eqref{eq:fracheat} for $x>0$ with
\begin{equation*}
    v(x,0) = u(-x,0) = 0, \qquad v(0,0) = u(0,0) = 0,
\end{equation*}
and $v$ bounded as $x\to\infty$ (transferred from the boundedness of $u$ as $x\to-\infty$ in Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity}). Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior} therefore applies to $v$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TBCint-vproof}
     \frac{\partial v}{\partial x}(0,t)
     = -\frac{1}{\kappa\,\Gamma(1-\beta/2)}\int_0^t \frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{\beta/2}}\,\frac{\partial v(0,\tau)}{\partial \tau}\,d\tau 
     = -\frac{1}{\kappa}\,D_t^{\beta/2}v(0,t). 
\end{equation}
By the chain rule,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TBCint-chain}
    \partial_x v(x,t) = -\partial_x u(-x,t) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \partial_x v(0,t) = -\partial_x u(0,t), 
\end{equation}
while $v(0,t)=u(-0,t)=u(0,t)$ identically, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TBCint-caputo}
D_t^{\beta/2}v(0,t) = D_t^{\beta/2}u(0,t). 
\end{equation}
Substituting~\eqref{eq:TBCint-chain} and~\eqref{eq:TBCint-caputo} into~\eqref{eq:TBCint-vproof} yields
\begin{equation*}
    -\partial_x u(0,t) = -\frac{1}{\kappa}\,D_t^{\beta/2}u(0,t) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \partial_x u(0,t) = \frac{1}{\kappa}\,D_t^{\beta/2}u(0,t).
\end{equation*}
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:vertex}
Under~\eqref{eq:fracheatgraph}--\eqref{eq:vertexconds}, with $u_1(0,\cdot)$ admitting a Laplace transform for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$, $u_1(0,0)=0$, and Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity} applied to $u_2,u_3$ on $b_{2,3}$,
we have the fractional vertex TBC
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertexTBC}
    \frac{\partial u_1}{\partial x}(0,t) = -\frac{\kappa_2+\kappa_3}{\kappa_1^2\,\Gamma(1-\beta/2)}\int_0^t \frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{\beta/2}}\,\frac{\partial u_1}{\partial \tau}(0,\tau)\,d\tau. 
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Repeating the argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior} on $b_2$, $b_3$, with $\kappa$ replaced by $\kappa_{2,3}$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:branchflux}
     \frac{\partial \widehat{u}_{2,3}}{\partial x}(0,s) = -\frac{s^{\beta/2}}{\kappa_{2,3}}\,\widehat{u}_{2,3}(0,s) = -\frac{s^{\beta/2}}{\kappa_{2,3}}\,\widehat{u}_1(0,s), 
\end{equation}
the second equality using the continuity condition in~\eqref{eq:vertexconds}. Substituting~\eqref{eq:branchflux} into the 
Laplace-transformed Kirchhoff condition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertexLT}
\begin{split}
\kappa_1^2\,\frac{\partial\widehat{u}_1}{\partial x}(0,s) 
&= \kappa_2^2\left(-\frac{s^{\beta/2}}{\kappa_2}\right)\widehat{u}_1(0,s) + \kappa_3^2\left(-\frac{s^{\beta/2}}{\kappa_3}\right)\widehat{u}_1(0,s) \\
&= -(\kappa_2+\kappa_3)\,s^{\beta/2}\,\widehat{u}_1(0,s). 
\end{split}
\end{equation}
                                                                                                                 
By Lemma~\ref{lem:LTcaputo} with $\alpha=\beta/2$, $\mathcal{L}\{D_t^{\beta/2}u_1(0,\cdot)\}(s) = s^{\beta/2}\widehat{u}_1(0,s) - s^{\beta/2-1}u_1(0,0)$; 
since we have assumed $u_1(0,0)=0$, this reduces to $\mathcal{L}\{D_t^{\beta/2}u_1(0,\cdot)\}(s) = s^{\beta/2}\widehat{u}_1(0,s)$, the same clean form obtained for the exterior problem in Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior}. 
Applying the inverse Laplace transform to \eqref{eq:vertexLT} in the form
\begin{equation*}
    \partial_x\widehat{u}_1(0,s) = -\big[(\kappa_2+\kappa_3)/\kappa_1^2\big]s^{\beta/2}\widehat{u}_1(0,s)
\end{equation*}
    therefore gives the TBC~\eqref{eq:vertexTBC}.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:invariance}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:vertex}, the vertex operator~\eqref{eq:vertexTBC} coincides with the single-bond exterior relation~\eqref{eq:TBCext1} above (Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior} with $\kappa=\kappa_1$), for every $\beta\in(0,1]$, if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sumrule2}
    \kappa_1 = \kappa_2 + \kappa_3. 
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Both~\eqref{eq:vertexTBC} and~\eqref{eq:TBCext1} are built from the same non-local kernel $D_t^{\beta/2}u_1(0,\cdot)$ (the latter, like the former, under the standing hypothesis $u_1(0,0)=0$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:vertex}, needed to invert $s^{\beta/2}\widehat{u}_1(0,s)$ cleanly by Lemma~\ref{lem:LTcaputo}); the fractional order $\beta$ enters both sides only through this common factor, which is untouched by the Kirchhoff algebra of Theorem~\ref{thm:vertex}, since that algebra acts solely on the $\kappa_j$-dependent prefactors. 
The two expressions therefore coincide, for every $t$ and every admissible boundary trace, if and only if their scalar prefactors agree: 
$-(\kappa_2+\kappa_3)/\kappa_1^2 = -1/\kappa_1 \iff \kappa_1=\kappa_2+\kappa_3$ (multiplying both sides by $-\kappa_1^2<0$). Since this equivalence does not involve $\beta$, it holds for every $\beta\in(0,1]$.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:nbond}
Consider an $n$-bond star graph with one incoming bond $b_1$ and $n-1$ outgoing bonds $b_2,\ldots,b_n$, each of diffusivity $\kappa_j$, sharing the common order $\beta$, 
and governed by~\eqref{eq:fracheatgraph} with the $n$-bond Kirchhoff condition 
$\kappa_1^2\partial_x u_1(0,t) = \sum_{j=2}^n \kappa_j^2\,\partial_x u_j(0,t)$. 
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nbondTBC}
  \frac{\partial u_1}{\partial x}(0,t) 
  = -\frac{\sum_{j=2}^n\kappa_j}{\kappa_1^2\,\Gamma(1-\beta/2)}\int_0^t\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{\beta/2}}\frac{\partial u_1}{\partial\tau}(0,\tau)\,d\tau, 
\end{equation}
which, under the standing hypothesis $u_1(0,0)=0$, coincides with the single-bond exterior relation~\eqref{eq:TBCext1} for every $\beta\in(0,1]$ if and only if $\kappa_1=\sum_{j=2}^n\kappa_j$.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Repeating~\eqref{eq:branchflux} on every outgoing bond,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nbond-branch}
\partial_x\widehat{u}_j(0,s) = -\frac{s^{\beta/2}}{\kappa_j}\,\widehat{u}_1(0,s), \qquad j=2,\ldots,n. 
\end{equation}
Substituting~\eqref{eq:nbond-branch} into the $n$-bond Kirchhoff condition $\kappa_1^2\partial_x u_1(0,t) = \sum_{j=2}^n \kappa_j^2\,\partial_x u_j(0,t)$, in Laplace-transformed form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nbond-LT}
  \kappa_1^2\,\frac{\partial\widehat{u}_1}{\partial x}(0,s) 
  = \sum_{j=2}^n \kappa_j^2\left(-\frac{s^{\beta/2}}{\kappa_j}\right)\widehat{u}_1(0,s) 
  = -\Big(\sum_{j=2}^n\kappa_j\Big)\,s^{\beta/2}\,\widehat{u}_1(0,s), 
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $\partial_x\widehat{u}_1(0,s) = -\big[(\sum_{j=2}^n\kappa_j)/\kappa_1^2\big]s^{\beta/2}\widehat{u}_1(0,s)$. 
By Lemma~\ref{lem:LTcaputo} with $u_1(0,0)=0$, $\mathcal{L}\{D_t^{\beta/2}u_1(0,\cdot)\}(s)=s^{\beta/2}\widehat{u}_1(0,s)$, 
so inverting the Laplace transform in~\eqref{eq:nbond-LT} gives~\eqref{eq:nbondTBC}.

Comparing prefactors with~\eqref{eq:TBCext1},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nbond-prefactor}
    -\frac{\sum_{j=2}^n\kappa_j}{\kappa_1^2} = -\frac{1}{\kappa_1} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \kappa_1 = \sum_{j=2}^n\kappa_j, 
\end{equation}
by the same algebra as in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}, with $\kappa_2+\kappa_3$ replaced by $\sum_{j=2}^n\kappa_j$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:wellposed}
Let $f_1^I\in L^1(-\infty,0)$ be bounded (e.g.\ compactly supported, or decaying faster than any polynomial, or merely $L^1$ and bounded, such as a Gaussian).
The coupled problem consisting of~\eqref{eq:fracheatgraph} on $b_1$, $b_2$, $b_3$ with $u_1(x,0)=f_1^I(x)$, $u_2(x,0)=u_3(x,0)=0$, and the vertex conditions~\eqref{eq:vertexconds}, has at most one solution satisfying Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity}.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Combining bond~1's own equation with its initial datum, the Laplace transform of~\eqref{eq:fracheatgraph} on $b_1$ reads, by Lemma~\ref{lem:LTcaputo} with the inhomogeneous term retained,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wp-ode}
   \widehat{u}_1''(x,s) - \lambda(s)^2\,\widehat{u}_1(x,s) = h(x,s), \qquad x<0, 
\end{equation}
where we abbreviate
\begin{equation*}
   \lambda(s) := \frac{s^{\beta/2}}{\kappa_1}, \qquad 
   h(x,s) := -\frac{s^{\beta-1}}{\kappa_1^2}\,f_1^I(x),
\end{equation*}
subject to the Robin-type condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wp-robin}
     \widehat{u}_1'(0,s) = -\frac{\kappa_2+\kappa_3}{\kappa_1^2}\,s^{\beta/2}\,\widehat{u}_1(0,s) 
\end{equation}
from~\eqref{eq:vertexLT}, together with boundedness as $x\to-\infty$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:branch}, $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda(s))>0$ for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$.

Since $f_1^I\in L^1(-\infty,0)$, 
$h(\cdot,s)=-\kappa_1^{-2}s^{\beta-1}f_1^I(\cdot)\in L^1(-\infty,0)$ for every fixed $s$ with $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$, and a particular solution of~\eqref{eq:wp-ode}, 
bounded as $x\to-\infty$, is furnished by the standard Green's function for $y''-\lambda^2y=h$ on the whole line,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wp-particular}
   \widehat{u}_{1,\mathrm{part}}(x,s) = -\frac{1}{2\lambda(s)}
   \int_{-\infty}^0 \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(s)|x-\xi|}\,h(\xi,s)\,d\xi. 
\end{equation}
The general solution of~\eqref{eq:wp-ode} bounded as $x\to-\infty$ is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wp-general}
    \widehat{u}_1(x,s) = \widehat{u}_{1,\mathrm{part}}(x,s) + C(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{\lambda(s)x}, 
\end{equation}
where the second homogeneous mode, $\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(s)x}$, has already been discarded: since $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda(s))>0$, $\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(s)x}\to\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$, whereas $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda(s)x}\to0$, mirroring the boundedness argument of Corollary~\ref{thm:interior}. 
The constant $C(s)$ remains to be fixed by the Robin condition~\eqref{eq:wp-robin}.

Next, differentiating~\eqref{eq:wp-general} and evaluating both $\widehat{u}_1$ and $\widehat{u}_1'$ at $x=0$,
\begin{equation*}
   \widehat{u}_1(0,s) = \widehat{u}_{1,\mathrm{part}}(0,s) + C(s), \qquad
   \widehat{u}_1'(0,s) = \widehat{u}_{1,\mathrm{part}}'(0,s) + C(s)\,\lambda(s).
\end{equation*}
Substituting into~\eqref{eq:wp-robin} and collecting the terms in $C(s)$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:wp-coeffeq}
    C(s)\biggl[\lambda(s) + \frac{\kappa_2+\kappa_3}{\kappa_1^2}\,s^{\beta/2}\biggr] 
   = -\frac{\kappa_2+\kappa_3}{\kappa_1^2}\,s^{\beta/2}\,\widehat{u}_{1,\mathrm{part}}(0,s) - \widehat{u}_{1,\mathrm{part}}'(0,s).
\end{equation}
Using $\lambda(s)=s^{\beta/2}/\kappa_1$, the bracket on the left simplifies to
\begin{equation} \label{eq:wp-coeff}
    \lambda(s) + \frac{\kappa_2+\kappa_3}{\kappa_1^2}\,s^{\beta/2} = \frac{s^{\beta/2}}{\kappa_1} + \frac{(\kappa_2+\kappa_3)\,s^{\beta/2}}{\kappa_1^2} = \frac{s^{\beta/2}\,(\kappa_1+\kappa_2+\kappa_3)}{\kappa_1^2},
\end{equation}
which is nonzero for every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$, since $\kappa_1$, $\kappa_2$, $\kappa_3>0$ and $s^{\beta/2}\neq0$ there.
Equation~\eqref{eq:wp-coeffeq} therefore determines $C(s)$ uniquely, and with it, via~\eqref{eq:wp-general}, $\widehat{u}_1(x,s)$ for every $x<0$ and every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$.

If $u_1$, $\tilde{u}_1$ both solve the coupled problem, their Laplace transforms consequently agree for every $x<0$ and every such $s$; 
Lerch's uniqueness theorem \cite{Doetsch1974} then gives $u_1\equiv\tilde{u}_1$, and~\eqref{eq:branchflux} propagates this identity to $u_2\equiv\tilde{u}_2$, $u_3\equiv\tilde{u}_3$.
