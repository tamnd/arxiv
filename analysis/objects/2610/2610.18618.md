<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:framed-congruence}
Let $\mathcal L$ be an oriented link with integer framing vector $\vec\tau$, and let 
$\vec\nu$ be a nonempty partition tuple with total length $\ell(\vec\nu)=r$.  For every $n\ge2$ and every root $\alpha$ with $\operatorname{ord}(\alpha^2)=n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:framed-congruence}
 H^{\vec\tau}_{n\vec\nu}-n^{r-1}\Psi_nH^{\vec\tau}_{\vec\nu}
 \in(q-\alpha)^2\mathcal O_\alpha[a^{\pm1}].

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:standard-LMOV}
Let $f^{\vec\tau}_{\vec A}(q,a)$ be the reformulated colored invariants determined by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:standard-reformulated}
 \log Z^{\vec\tau}(x)
 =\sum_{d\ge1}\frac1d\sum_{\vec A\ne\vec\varnothing}
 f^{\vec\tau}_{\vec A}(q^d,a^d)
 \prod_{c=1}^{L}s_{A^c}\bigl((x^c)^d\bigr).
\end{equation}
For partitions $A,B$ of the same size define, in the present normalization,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MAB}
 M_{AB}(q)=\sum_{\mu\vdash |A|}
 \frac{\chi_A(\mu)\chi_B(\mu)}{\mathfrak z_\mu}
 \prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\{\mu_i\}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fMP}
 f^{\vec\tau}_{\vec A}(q,a)
 =\sum_{\vec B}P^{\vec\tau}_{\vec B}(q,a)
 \prod_{c=1}^{L}M_{A^cB^c}(q).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $\vec\tau=0$, the coefficients $P^0_{\vec B}$ are exactly the standard LMOV reformulated invariants of \cite{LP} under the variable convention $q_{\mathrm{LP}}=q^{-2}$ and $t_{\mathrm{LP}}=a^{-2}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The M\"obius operator in \eqref{eq:PLog} is inverse to the multiple-cover expansion \eqref{eq:standard-reformulated}.  Hence, before dividing the alphabet,
\[
 \operatorname{PLog}Z^{\vec\tau}(x)
 =\sum_{\vec A\ne\vec\varnothing}f^{\vec\tau}_{\vec A}(q,a)s_{\vec A}(x).
\]
By Frobenius' formula and \eqref{eq:divided-alphabet}, componentwise,
\[
 s_A[Y]
 =\sum_{\mu\vdash |A|}
 \frac{\chi_A(\mu)}{\mathfrak z_\mu\{\mu\}}p_\mu(y)
 =\sum_B (M(q)^{-1})_{AB}s_B(y).
\]
Indeed the character table is orthogonal with weight $\mathfrak z_\mu^{-1}$, so the matrices obtained by weighting the $\mu$-column by $\{\mu\}$ and by $\{\mu\}^{-1}$ are inverse.  Therefore the Schur coefficient invariant after the divided-alphabet substitution is $M(q)^{-1}f$, or equivalently $f=M(q)P$.  Taking the tensor product over the link components gives \eqref{eq:fMP}.  This is the standard LMOV change of basis in \cite{LP}.  More explicitly, with $q_{\mathrm{LP}}=q^{-2}$ one has

so the matrix \eqref{eq:MAB} is exactly the Liu--Peng matrix \cite{LP} in the present variables (with $t_{\mathrm{LP}}=a^{-2}$).

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:cumulant-contraction}
Let $\mathcal L$ be a finite labeled framed link.

\medskip
\noindent
{\rm (i)}
If the labels can be divided into two nonempty groups which are
separated by an embedded $2$-sphere, then
\[
\operatorname{Cum}(\mathcal L)=0.
\]

\medskip
\noindent
{\rm (ii)}
Suppose a local change involves two labels $i$ and $j$, and assume
that for every sublink retaining both labels the corresponding moment
changes by
\[
\Delta M(T)
=
c\,M(T_{i\sim j}),
\qquad
i,j\in T,
\]
where $T_{i\sim j}$ denotes the label set obtained by merging $i$ and
$j$ into a single label.  Assume also that
$
\Delta M(T)=0
$
whenever $T$ omits $i$ or $j$.  Then
\[
\Delta\operatorname{Cum}(\mathcal L)
=
c\,\operatorname{Cum}(\mathcal L_{i\sim j}).
\]
The same conclusion holds termwise for any finite linear combination
of merge operations.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For part~{\rm (i)}, suppose the label set decomposes as
\[
\{1,..,r\}=I\sqcup J,
\qquad
I,J\neq\varnothing,
\]
and the two groups are separated by a $2$-sphere.

Introduce commuting nilpotent variables
$
u_1,\ldots,u_r,
\quad
(u_i^2=0),
$
and form the moment generating polynomial
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathcal L}(u)
=
\sum_{T\subseteq[r]}
M(T)\prod_{i\in T}u_i,
\qquad
M(\varnothing)=1.
\]
The coefficient of
$
u_1\cdots u_r
$
in
$
\log\mathcal M_{\mathcal L}(u)
$
is precisely $\operatorname{Cum}(\mathcal L)$.

Since the two groups are split, every moment factors:
\[
M(T)
=
M(T\cap I)\,M(T\cap J).
\]
Hence we have
$
\mathcal M_{\mathcal L}(u)
=
\mathcal M_{\mathcal L_I}(u_I)
\mathcal M_{\mathcal L_J}(u_J),
$
and therefore
$
\log\mathcal M_{\mathcal L}
=
\log\mathcal M_{\mathcal L_I}
+
\log\mathcal M_{\mathcal L_J}.
$
Neither summand contains a monomial involving variables from both
$I$ and $J$.  Thus the coefficient of
$
u_1\cdots u_r
$
is zero which implies
$
\operatorname{Cum}(\mathcal L)=0.
$

For part~{\rm (ii)}, it is convenient to use the explicit
set-partition formula.  In
\[
\operatorname{Cum}(\mathcal L)
=
\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_r}
(-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!
\prod_{B\in\pi}M(B),
\]
a set partition $\pi$ contributes to the change only when $i$ and
$j$ belong to the same block.  Indeed, if they lie in different
blocks, each of those blocks omits one of the two labels, and by
hypothesis its moment does not change.

Suppose $i,j\in B_0\in\pi$.  Since there is only one block containing
both labels,
\[
\Delta
\left(
\prod_{B\in\pi}M(B)
\right)
=
c\,
M((B_0)_{i\sim j})
\prod_{\substack{B\in\pi\\B\neq B_0}}
M(B).
\]
Contracting $i$ and $j$ inside $B_0$ gives a bijection
\[
\left\{
\pi\in\Pi_r:
i,j\text{ lie in the same block}
\right\}
\longleftrightarrow
\Pi_{r-1},
\]
and this bijection preserves the number of blocks.  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\operatorname{Cum}(\mathcal L)
&=
c
\sum_{\pi'\in\Pi_{r-1}}
(-1)^{|\pi'|-1}(|\pi'|-1)!
\prod_{B\in\pi'}M(B)
\\
&=
c\,\operatorname{Cum}(\mathcal L_{i\sim j}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Linearity gives the final assertion.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:gcd}
Let $\mathcal L$ have an arbitrary integer framing vector $\vec\tau$, and let $\vec\lambda$ be a nonempty cycle tuple with $r$ total parts and part-gcd $D=D_{\vec\lambda}$.  For the signed $q$-framed partition function,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gcd}
 \check F^{\vec\tau}_{\vec\lambda}\in
 \frac{\{\vec\lambda\}^2}{\{D\}^2}\mathbb Z[v,a^{\pm1}],
 \qquad
 H^{\vec\tau}_{\vec\lambda}\in\mathbb Z[v,a^{\pm1}].
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
List the $r$ cycle parts of $\vec\lambda$ as $n_1,\ldots,n_r$, retaining their original link-component assignments, and put $D=\gcd(n_1,\ldots,n_r)$.
Using Lemma~\ref{lem:framed-parallel}.  The framing correction contributed by all labeled cycles is the common Laurent monomial
\[
 u_{\vec\lambda,\vec\tau}
 =(-a^{-1})^{\sum_{j=1}^r\tau_{c(j)}n_j},
\]
where $c(j)$ is the original component carrying the $j$th cycle.  Since every labeled cycle occurs exactly once in every set-partition term, this factor is independent of the block decomposition and is therefore irrelevant to divisibility.

Fix $i$.  Expand only the normalized color $B_{n_i}$ by the integral Newton representative
\[
 b_{n_i}=z\sum_{\nu=0}^{n_i-1}\beta_{i,\nu},
\]
where each $\beta_{i,\nu}$ is an integral Hecke word whose annular closure is one ordinary framed component.  By linearity of the cumulant and Proposition~\ref{prop:anchor}, applied with one ordinary label and the remaining $r-1$ normalized cycle labels,
\[
 \operatorname{Cum}\!\left(\operatorname{cl}(\beta_{i,\nu});B_{n_j},\ j\ne i\right)
 \in\left(\prod_{j\ne i}\{n_j\}^2\right)z^{-1}\mathbb Z[v,a^{\pm1}].
\]
The explicit factor $z$ in $b_{n_i}$ cancels the knot pole $z^{-1}$.  Hence, for every $i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gcd-step-one}
 \check F^{\vec\tau}_{\vec\lambda}
 \in\left(\prod_{j\ne i}\{n_j\}^2\right)\mathbb Z[v,a^{\pm1}].
\end{equation}

Now, set $A_j(v)=\{n_j\}^2\in\mathbb Z[v]$ and $T(v)=\prod_jA_j(v)=\{\vec\lambda\}^2$.  The divisors in \eqref{eq:gcd-step-one} are $G_i=T/A_i$.  Since every $G_i$ is monic, divisibility by all $G_i$ implies divisibility by their monic least common multiple, denoted by $\operatorname{lcm}_i(G_i)$.  For an irreducible polynomial $\phi(v)\in\mathbb Q[v]$, write $a_j=\operatorname{ord}_\phi A_j$.  Then
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_\phi\operatorname{lcm}_i(G_i)
 =\max_i\left(\sum_j a_j-a_i\right)
 =\sum_j a_j-\min_i a_i.
\]
Thus, we obtain
\[
 \operatorname{lcm}_i(G_i)
 =\frac{\prod_jA_j}{\gcd(A_1,\ldots,A_r)}.
\]
Now $\{m\}=q^{-m}(q^{2m}-1)$ and
$\gcd(q^{2m}-1,q^{2n}-1)=q^{2\gcd(m,n)}-1$.  Up to Laurent monomial units this gives
$\gcd(\{m\},\{n\})=\{\gcd(m,n)\}$.  Since the squares $\{m\}^2$ are monic polynomials in $v$, it follows that
\[
 \gcd\bigl(\{n_1\}^2,\ldots,\{n_r\}^2\bigr)=\{D\}^2.
\]
Consequently, we have
\[
 \operatorname{lcm}_{1\le i\le r}\prod_{j\ne i}\{n_j\}^2
 =\frac{\{\vec\lambda\}^2}{\{D\}^2}.
\]
Therefore, it proves the first assertion in \eqref{eq:gcd}.

By definition,
\(
 H^{\vec\tau}_{\vec\lambda}
 =\frac{\{D\}^2\check F^{\vec\tau}_{\vec\lambda}}{\{\vec\lambda\}^2},
\)
so the preceding divisibility immediately gives
$H^{\vec\tau}_{\vec\lambda}\in\mathbb Z[v,a^{\pm1}]$.

\label{lem:root-framing}
If $\operatorname{ord}(\alpha^2)=n$ and $\varepsilon_n=(-1)^{n-1}$, then for every integer $\tau$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-framing}
 \check Z_n(K;\tau)
 \equiv a^{n\tau}\varepsilon_n^{\tau}\check Z_n(K;0)
 \pmod{(q-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
This lemma can be regarded as a generalization of the prime-cycle framing congruence in \cite{CLPZ}, but the proof is the same.  The case $\tau=0$ is immediate.  For $\tau\ne0$, character inversion and the
standard hook-character identity give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hook-frame}
 \check Z_n(K;\tau)
 =a^{n\tau}\frac{\{n\}}{\{n\tau\}}
 \sum_{\mu\vdash n}\frac{\check Z^0_\mu(K)}{\mathfrak z_\mu}
 \prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\frac{\{n\tau\mu_i\}}{\{\mu_i\}}.
\end{equation}
If $\mu\ne(n)$, then $\ell(\mu)\ge2$ and every part satisfies $0<\mu_i<n$.
Hence $\{\mu_i\}(\alpha)\ne0$, while $\{n\tau\mu_i\}(\alpha)=0$, so the
corresponding term has order at least two at $q=\alpha$.

For $\mu=(n)$ the coefficient is
\[
 R_{n,\tau}(q)=\frac1n\frac{\{n^2\tau\}}{\{n\tau\}}.
\]
Putting $x=q^{n\tau}$ gives
\[
 R_{n,\tau}(q)=\frac1n\frac{x^n-x^{-n}}{x-x^{-1}}.
\]
At $q=\alpha$, $x=\alpha^{n\tau}=\pm1$, and
$R_{n,\tau}(\alpha)=\varepsilon_n^\tau$.  The Laurent polynomial
$(x^n-x^{-n})/(x-x^{-1})$ is invariant under $x\mapsto x^{-1}$; hence its first
derivative vanishes at the fixed points $x=\pm1$.  Therefore
$R_{n,\tau}(q)=\varepsilon_n^\tau+O((q-\alpha)^2)$, proving the lemma.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:smoothing-framing}
Let $c$ be a crossing of sign $\sigma(c)\in\{\pm1\}$ in an oriented framed diagram,
and give the oriented smoothing the inherited ribbon framing.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $c$ is a mixed crossing between distinct components $K_i,K_j$ with framings
$\tau_i,\tau_j$, the smoothing merges them into $K_{ij}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-smoothing-framing}
 \tau(K_{ij})=\tau_i+\tau_j+2\operatorname{lk}(K_i,K_j)-\sigma(c),
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-smoothing-parity}
 \tau(K_{ij})\equiv\tau_i+\tau_j+1\pmod2.
\end{equation}
\item If $c$ is a self-crossing of $K$ and the smoothing splits it into $K',K''$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-smoothing-framing}
 \tau(K)=\tau(K')+\tau(K'')+2\operatorname{lk}(K',K'')+\sigma(c),
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-smoothing-parity}
 \tau(K)\equiv\tau(K')+\tau(K'')+1\pmod2.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Thus every oriented smoothing changes the parity of the sum of the individual framings by
exactly one.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
For blackboard framings the formulas follow by comparing self-writhes.  In the mixed case,
after smoothing, every remaining crossing between $K_i$ and $K_j$ becomes a self-crossing
of $K_{ij}$; their signed sum is
$2\operatorname{lk}(K_i,K_j)-\sigma(c)$.  In the self-crossing case, after smoothing,
the crossings between $K'$ and $K''$ have signed sum $2\operatorname{lk}(K',K'')$,
and restoring the removed crossing contributes $\sigma(c)$.  For arbitrary ribbon
framings, the additional full twists add under merging and split additively under
separation.  Reduction modulo two gives the parity statements.

\label{prop:uniform-knot}
If $\operatorname{ord}(\alpha^2)=n$, then every framed knot $(K,\tau)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-knot}
 \check Z_n(K;\tau)
 \equiv\varepsilon_n^\tau\Psi_n\check Z_1(K;\tau)
 \pmod{(q-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Let $D_+,D_-,D_0$ be a self-crossing triple, with $D_0=K'\cup K''$ carrying the
inherited framings.  The self-crossing identity \eqref{eq:self-main}, together with
Lemmas~\ref{lem:exterior-transport} and \ref{lem:regular-closure}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:knot-upper-recurrence}
 \check Z_n(D_+)-\check Z_n(D_-)
 \equiv\varepsilon_n\check Z_{(n,n)}(D_0)
 \pmod{(q-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
By the moment--cumulant formula and Theorem~\ref{thm:gcd},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:knot-upper-factorization}
 \check Z_{(n,n)}(D_0)
 \equiv\check Z_n(K';\tau')\check Z_n(K'';\tau'')
 \pmod{(q-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
For the fundamental cycle, the HOMFLY--PT skein relation gives the exact identity
\[
 \check Z_1(D_+)-\check Z_1(D_-)=\check Z_{(1,1)}(D_0).
\]
Again Theorem~\ref{thm:gcd} gives
$\check Z_{(1,1)}(D_0)-\check Z_1(K';\tau')\check Z_1(K'';\tau'')\in z^2\mathbb Z[v,a^{\pm1}]$.
Applying $\Psi_n$ and using $\Psi_n(z^2)=\{n\}^2$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:knot-lower-factorization}
 \Psi_n\check Z_{(1,1)}(D_0)
 \equiv
 \Psi_n\check Z_1(K';\tau')\,\Psi_n\check Z_1(K'';\tau'')
 \pmod{(q-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
If the induction hypothesis is applied to $K'$ and $K''$, the leading term in
\eqref{eq:knot-upper-recurrence} acquires the sign
$\varepsilon_n^{1+\tau'+\tau''}$.  By
\eqref{eq:self-smoothing-parity}, this equals $\varepsilon_n^{\tau(D)}$.  Thus both sides of
\eqref{eq:uniform-knot} satisfy the same crossing-change recursion.  Switching crossings
to a descending diagram reduces to a framed unknot, where the claim follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:root-framing} and
$\check Z_n(U;0)=a^n-a^{-n}=\Psi_n\check Z_1(U;0)$.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:equal-cycle}
Let $\mathcal L$ be a framed link with $r\ge1$ labeled components, each carrying $B_n$, and put
$\tau(\mathcal L)=\sum_i\tau(K_i)$.  If $\operatorname{ord}(\alpha^2)=n$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equal-cycle}
 H^{\mathrm{ord}}_{(n^r)}(\mathcal L)
 \equiv n^{r-1}\varepsilon_n^{\tau(\mathcal L)}
 \Psi_nH^{\mathrm{ord}}_{(1^r)}(\mathcal L)
 \pmod{(q-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
For $r=1$ this is Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-knot}.  Suppose $r>1$ and choose a mixed
crossing.  The mixed identity \eqref{eq:mixed-main} has leading term
$\varepsilon_n n\{n\}^2$ times the contracted label and remainder divisible by $\{n\}^4$.
Inside the $r$-label cumulant, the remaining $r-2$ normalized cycle labels contribute
$\{n\}^{2(r-2)}$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:anchor}; hence the total unnormalized error is
divisible by $\{n\}^{2r}$.  Dividing by the connected normalization
$\{n\}^{2r-2}$ leaves a second-order error and gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equal-cycle-recurrence}
 H^{\mathrm{ord}}_{(n^r)}(\mathcal L_+)-H^{\mathrm{ord}}_{(n^r)}(\mathcal L_-)
 \equiv\varepsilon_n n\,H^{\mathrm{ord}}_{(n^{r-1})}(\mathcal L_0)
 \pmod{(q-\alpha)^2}.
\end{equation}
For the fundamental cycle, contraction of connected cumulants gives exactly
\[
 H^{\mathrm{ord}}_{(1^r)}(\mathcal L_+)-H^{\mathrm{ord}}_{(1^r)}(\mathcal L_-)
 =H^{\mathrm{ord}}_{(1^{r-1})}(\mathcal L_0).
\]
Apply the induction hypothesis to $\mathcal L_0$.  By
\eqref{eq:mixed-smoothing-parity},
$\tau(\mathcal L_0)\equiv\tau(\mathcal L)+1\pmod2$, so the extra local factor $\varepsilon_n$ in
\eqref{eq:equal-cycle-recurrence} changes $\varepsilon_n^{\tau(\mathcal L_0)}$ into
$\varepsilon_n^{\tau(\mathcal L)}$.  Hence the difference between the two sides of
\eqref{eq:equal-cycle} is invariant modulo $(q-\alpha)^2$ under mixed crossing changes.
Switch mixed crossings until the labeled link splits into two nonempty groups; both
connected cumulants then vanish by Lemma~\ref{lem:cumulant-contraction}.  The result follows.

\label{lem:frobenius-lift}

\[
 \Psi_p(q)=q^p,\qquad
 \Psi_p(a)=a^p,\qquad
 \Psi_p\bigl(p_m(x^c)\bigr)=p_{pm}(x^c)
\]
extends continuously to $A_p$ and satisfies

 \Psi_p(F)\equiv F^p\pmod{pA_p}\qquad(F\in A_p).
\end{equation}

\label{lem:dwork}
Fix a prime $p$. Let $B$ be a commutative torsion-free $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$-algebra, and
let $\varphi_p:B\to B$ be an endomorphism satisfying
$\varphi_p(b)\equiv b^p\pmod{pB}$ for every $b\in B$.
Put $A=B[[u_1,\ldots,u_L]]$ and $I=(u_1,\ldots,u_L)$.
Extend $\varphi_p$ continuously to $A$ by
\[
 \psi_p\left(\sum_{\mathbf d}b_{\mathbf d}\mathbf u^{\mathbf d}\right)
       =\sum_{\mathbf d}\varphi_p(b_{\mathbf d})
                                      \mathbf u^{p\mathbf d}.
\]
For $Z\in1+I$, put
\[
 D_p(Z)=\log Z-\frac1p\psi_p(\log Z).
\]
Then $D_p(Z)\in A$. If $\{\varphi_k\}_{k\ge1}$ is a compatible
system of Adams operations on $B$, extended to $\psi_k$ by
$u_c\mapsto u_c^k$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dwork-pairing}
 \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\mu(k)}k\psi_k(\log Z)
  =\sum_{\substack{k\ge1\\(k,p)=1}}\frac{\mu(k)}k
                                      \psi_k\bigl(D_p(Z)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The sums are interpreted coefficientwise in total $\mathbf u$-degree.
<!-- end lemma-15 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
The Frobenius congruence gives $\psi_p(Z)\equiv Z^p\pmod{pA}$.
Both terms are units with constant term one. Consequently, there
is $K\in I$ with
\[
 \frac{Z^p}{\psi_p(Z)}=1+pK.
\]
Here each positive-degree coefficient of the quotient minus one
is divisible by $p$, and its constant coefficient is zero; this
gives $K\in I$ coefficientwise.
Using continuity and $\psi_p(\log Z)=\log\psi_p(Z)$,
\[
 D_p(Z)=\frac1p\log(1+pK)
       =\sum_{j\ge1}(-1)^{j-1}\frac{p^{j-1}}jK^j.
\]
Since $\operatorname{ord}_p(j)\le j-1$, every coefficient belongs to
$\mathbb Z_{(p)}$. The series converges $I$-adically because $K\in I$.
Thus $D_p(Z)\in A$.

For \eqref{eq:dwork-pairing}, terms with $p^2\mid k$ vanish
because $\mu(k)=0$. Every nonzero M\"obius term whose index is
divisible by $p$ is therefore paired uniquely with the term of
index $k$, where $(k,p)=1$. Since $\mu(pk)=-\mu(k)$ and
$\psi_{pk}=\psi_k\psi_p$,
\begin{align*}
 &\frac{\mu(k)}k\psi_k(\log Z)

 &\qquad=
 \frac{\mu(k)}k\psi_k
             \left(\log Z-\frac1p\psi_p(\log Z)\right).
\end{align*}
Summing over $(k,p)=1$ gives the identity. This rearrangement is
legitimate: $\psi_k$ multiplies positive total degree by $k$, so
only finitely many terms contribute to a fixed multidegree.

\label{lem:higman-square}
The actual element $h_{\mathsf{H}}(b_n)$ belongs to $\{n\}^2\mathsf{H}$.
<!-- end lemma-20 -->
<!-- proof-30: body_md -->
On a generic simple block, the Higman map is
$s_\lambda\operatorname{Tr}_\lambda$. The
Murnaghan--Nakayama rule \cite[Chapter~I, Section~7]{Macdonald}
says that the $n$-cycle character vanishes off hooks and is
$(-1)^j$ on $\eta_j$. Since $b_n$ closes to $\{n\}P_n$,
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\eta_j}(b_n)=\{n\}(-1)^j,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}_\lambda(b_n)=0\quad
 (\lambda\text{ not a hook}).
\]
Writing $p_j$ for the generic central hook projectors gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central-higman}
 z^n h_{\mathsf{H}}(b_n)
 =\{n\}^2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^jc_jp_j.
\end{equation}

An individual $p_j$ may have a simple pole. The projector
formula
\[
 p_\lambda=s_\lambda^{-1}
       \sum_{w\in S_n}\operatorname{Tr}_\lambda(T_w)T_{w^{-1}}
\]
has integral character coefficients. A hook of size $n$
has exactly one hook length $n$; a nonhook has none.
Thus $\{n\}p_j$ is regular, and the nonhook projectors
are regular. The total hook projector is regular as well.

Let $\omega=\alpha^n\in\{1,-1\}$. Successive ratios give
$c_{j+1}(\alpha)/c_j(\alpha)=-1$. The primitive product
identity gives
\[
 c_0(\alpha)
 =\alpha^{n(n-1)}\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}(1-\alpha^{-2r})
 =\varepsilon_n n.
\]
Here $\alpha^n=-1$ if $n$ is even, while $n-1$ is even if
$n$ is odd. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hook-coefficient-values}
 c_j(\alpha)=\varepsilon_n n(-1)^j.
\end{equation}
We may therefore write
\[
 \sum_j(-1)^jc_jp_j
 =\varepsilon_n n\sum_jp_j
  +\sum_j\bigl((-1)^jc_j-\varepsilon_n n\bigr)p_j.
\]
The first term is regular. Each coefficient in the second
sum is divisible by $\{n\}$, so each summand is a regular
multiple of $\{n\}p_j$. The bracket in
\eqref{eq:central-higman} is regular, and $z$ is a unit.
This proves the square factor.

 \mathcal E_B^-=\sum_xu_x\otimes_Bv_x
                   \in \mathsf{H}\otimes_B\mathsf{H}
\]
therefore satisfies $h\mathcal E_B^-=\mathcal E_B^-h$
for every $h\in \mathsf{H}$. To see this directly, use the two
expansions in \eqref{eq:self-relative-duality}:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h\mathcal E_B^-
 &=\sum_{x,y}u_y\mathsf E_B^-(v_yhu_x)\otimes_Bv_x\\
 &=\sum_yu_y\otimes_B
                \left(\sum_x\mathsf E_B^-(v_yhu_x)v_x\right)\\
 &=\sum_yu_y\otimes_Bv_yh=\mathcal E_B^-h.
 \end{aligned}
\]

For $c\in B_L$, block transport and commutation with
$\theta(\Delta_k)$ give $c_RW_k=W_kc_L$.
The map
\[
 \mathsf{H}\otimes_{B_L}\mathsf{H}\longrightarrow M_k,
 \qquad u\otimes v\longmapsto u_RW_kv_L
\]
is consequently balanced, and sends $\mathcal E_{B_L}^-$
to $\Xi_k$. The Casimir identity proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-full-packet-intertwining}
 h_R\Xi_k=\Xi_kh_L\qquad(h\in \mathsf{H}).
\end{equation}
On the other hand, \eqref{eq:self-pointwise-intertwining} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-proper-packet-intertwining}
 c_L\Xi_k=\Xi_kc_R\qquad(c\in B_R).
\end{equation}
For $c\in B_R$ and $h\in \mathsf{H}$, cyclicity in $M_k/[A,M_k]$
and these two identities imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [(c_Lh_L-h_Lc_L)\Xi_k]
 &=[h_L\Xi_kc_L-h_L\Xi_kc_R]\\
 &=[h_Lc_R\Xi_k-h_L\Xi_kc_R]\\
 &=[c_Rh_L\Xi_k-h_L\Xi_kc_R]=0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last expression is an $A$-commutator. Thus the map
factors through $\mathsf{H}/[B_R,\mathsf{H}]$, proving
\eqref{eq:self-transfer-map}. Interchanging the packets proves
the right-handed statement.

We prove the divisibility assertions on the full
$A$-bimodule $\mathsf{G}$. For any symmetric trace on $\mathsf{H}$, let
$e_i,e_i^*$ be dual bases and put
$\Omega=\sum_i e_i\otimes e_i^*$. Casimir duality gives
$\Omega(1\otimes x)=(x\otimes1)\Omega$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-tensor-higman}
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{i,j}e_i^*be_j\otimes e_j^*e_i
 &=\sum_i(e_i^*b\otimes1)\Omega(1\otimes e_i)\\
 &=\sum_i(e_i^*be_i\otimes1)\Omega\\
 &=(h_{\mathsf{H}}(b)\otimes1)\Omega.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This is an identity in the actual tensor algebra.

Apply it to the transported trace $t_{\mathsf{H}}^-$ and write
$\Omega_{\mathsf{H}}^-=\theta(\Omega_{\mathsf{H}})$. The tensor-product trace
on $A$ has transported dual bases
$(e_i)_L(e_j)_R$ and $(e_i^*)_L(e_j^*)_R$. Block transport
therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-full-higman}
 \begin{aligned}
 h_A^-(b_LC)
 &=\sum_{i,j}(e_i^*)_L(e_j^*)_R\,b_LC(e_i)_L(e_j)_R\\
 &=\sum_{i,j}(e_i^*be_j)_L(e_j^*e_i)_R\,C\\
 &=(h_{\mathsf{H}}^-(b)\otimes1)\Omega_{\mathsf{H}}^-C.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:transported-higman-square} proves that this belongs to
$\{n\}^2\mathsf{G}$.

The traces on $B_k\subset A$ are compatible. Transporting
the right coset decomposition $w=xu$ gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h_{B_k}^-N_{B_k}^{A,-}(y)
 &=\sum_{u\in W(B_k)}\sum_{x\in\mathcal D_k}
   \theta(T_{u^{-1}})\theta(T_{x^{-1}})
             y\theta(T_x)\theta(T_u)\\
 &=\sum_{w\in W(A)}\theta(T_{w^{-1}})y\theta(T_w)
  =h_A^-(y).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Every factor of $B_k$ has size $k$ or $n-k$, strictly
less than $n$. Its transported Schur elements are therefore
units. The module $\mathsf{G}$ is free over $R_\alpha$. Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:higman} to \eqref{eq:self-full-higman}
proves \eqref{eq:self-norm-square}.

Finally, apply $\theta$ to the shuffle formula and move
the trailing shuffle around the relative cocenter:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-pairing}
 \begin{aligned}
 [b_LC\theta(R_k)]
 &=\sum_{x\in\mathcal D_k}
   [b_LC\theta(T_x)\theta(\Delta_k)\theta(T_{x^{-1}})]\\
 &=\sum_{x\in\mathcal D_k}
   [\theta(T_{x^{-1}})b_LC\theta(T_x)\theta(\Delta_k)]\\
 &=[N_{B_k}^{A,-}(b_LC)\theta(\Delta_k)].
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The cyclic step is valid because $\theta(T_{x^{-1}})\in A$.
Since $\theta$ preserves $B_k$, the element
$\theta(\Delta_k)$ commutes with $B_k$. Thus, for
$x\in B_k$ and $Y\in \mathsf{G}$,
\[
 [x,Y]\theta(\Delta_k)=[x,Y\theta(\Delta_k)]\in[A,\mathsf{G}].
\]
Substituting \eqref{eq:self-norm-square} into
\eqref{eq:self-pairing} proves
\eqref{eq:self-proper-divisibility}.

The map $S_k$ also records the one-packet norm transfer.
Expand the shuffle formula with
$x=x_L\oplus x_R$:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [b_LC\theta(R_k)]
 =\sum_{x_L\in\mathcal D_L}\sum_{x_R\in\mathcal D_R}
 \bigl[&b_LC\theta(T_{x_L,L})\theta(T_{x_R,R})\theta(\Delta_k)\\
      &\hspace{1em}\cdot\theta(T_{x_L^{-1},L})
                         \theta(T_{x_R^{-1},R})\bigr].
 \end{aligned}
\]
For each $x_R\in\mathcal D_R$, write

After summing over $x_L$, its contribution is

 &=[v_Rb_Lu_L\Xi_k]
  =[b_Lu_Lv_R\Xi_k]\\
 &=[b_Lu_L\Xi_kv_L]
  =[v_Lb_Lu_L\Xi_k],
 \end{aligned}

where the middle step uses

Summing over $x_R$ gives

$[N_{B_R}^{\mathsf{H},-}(b)]=\{n\}^2[y_R]$ in $\mathsf{H}/[B_R,\mathsf{H}]$.

By \eqref{eq:inverse-grid} and Lemma~\ref{lem:self-proper},
every proper component of $b_L(C-C^{-1})$ belongs to
$[A,\mathsf{G}]+\{n\}^2\mathsf{G}$. The remaining full-matching term returns
to $A$: indeed,
\[
 C\theta(R_n)=(-1)^n\Omega_{\mathsf{H}}^-,
\]
because packet exchange preserves the symmetric tensor
$\Omega_{\mathsf{H}}^-$. Hence the remaining self remainder is
represented by
\[
 \varepsilon_n\bigl(z^nb_L\Omega_{\mathsf{H}}^- -b_Lb_R\bigr).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:self-full}, its class, apart from
$\varepsilon_n$, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-remaining}
 \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\delta_jf_j\otimes f_j
 -\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^jf_j\right)^{\otimes2}.
\end{equation}

At the root, $\delta_j(\alpha)=n$ for every $j$.
One may see this either from \eqref{eq:transported-hook-values}
or from
\[
 \frac{\delta_{j+1}}{\delta_j}
 =-q^n\frac{\{j+1\}}{\{n-j-1\}},\qquad
 \delta_0(\alpha)
 =\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}(1-\alpha^{-2r})=n.
\]
Furthermore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-delta-sum}
 \sum_j\delta_j-n^2\in\{n\}^2R_\alpha.
\end{equation}
The value at $\alpha$ is zero. The logarithmic derivatives
satisfy
\[
 \sum_j\frac{q\delta_j'}{\delta_j}
 =-\frac12\sum_j\kappa_j
   +2\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}(n-r)L_r,
\]
which vanishes at $\alpha$ by \eqref{eq:derivative-cancel}.
Since every $\delta_j(\alpha)=n$, the ordinary derivative
of $\sum_j\delta_j-n^2$ also vanishes, proving
\eqref{eq:self-delta-sum}.

Insert $f_j=(-1)^jf_0+\{n\}r_j$ into
\eqref{eq:self-remaining} and put
$r_* =\sum_j(-1)^jr_j$. The result is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left(\sum_j\delta_j-n^2\right)f_0\otimes f_0\\
 &\quad+\{n\}\sum_j(-1)^j(\delta_j-n)
                      (f_0\otimes r_j+r_j\otimes f_0)\\
 &\quad+\{n\}^2
       \left(\sum_j\delta_jr_j\otimes r_j-r_*\otimes r_*\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first coefficient belongs to $\{n\}^2R_\alpha$,
and $\delta_j-n\in\{n\}R_\alpha$. Every term is therefore
divisible by $\{n\}^2$ in
$\overline{\mathsf{H}}\otimes\overline{\mathsf{H}}$, and hence in its image
in $\mathsf{G}/[A,\mathsf{G}]$. This proves the self identity.

\label{rem:composite}
No primality assumption occurs in Theorem~\ref{thm:relative-hecke}.
The cyclotomic inputs are $\{j\}(\alpha)\ne0$ for
$0<j<n$ when $\operatorname{ord}(\alpha^2)=n$, and the primitive product
identity for $\alpha^2$. The local theorem therefore applies
to composite packet sizes as well. For composite $n$, the
passage from local divisibility by $\{n\}^2$ at an exact
squared-order-$n$ root to a global congruence modulo $U_n(v)$
also requires the roots of proper-divisor squared order.
Those roots are treated separately in the global argument.
