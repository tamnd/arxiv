<!-- defn-1: body_md -->
\label{def:iterated-cotangent-integral}
Let
\( a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_r,b_0\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z,\)
and let
\(\gamma:\theta_{a_0}\rightsquigarrow\theta_{b_0}\)
be a path whose interior lies in \(\Theta_N^\circ\), equipped with
tangential base points at its endpoints.
The associated \textit{iterated
cotangent integral} is
\[
                                           
 C_\gamma^{\operatorname{reg}} (a_0;\,a_1,\ldots,a_r;\,b_0)
 :=
 \operatorname{reg}\int_\gamma
 \eta_{a_1}^{\cot}\cdots\eta_{a_r}^{\cot}.
\]
<!-- end defn-1 -->
<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
Bachmann~\cite[Section~4]{BachmannShiftedLogSine2026}
defines iterated cotangent integrals using the forms
\(d\theta\) and \(\cot\theta\,d\theta\) on \([0,\pi/6]\).
In Remark~4.5 of loc.~cit., he also observes that the upper endpoint can
be replaced by \(\pi/n\), subject to endpoint convergence
conditions. Our definition uses the shifted forms
\(\eta_a^{\cot}\), with paths between cyclotomic angles and
tangential regularization. The common form is
\(\eta_0^{\cot}=\cot\theta\,d\theta\); the form \(d\theta\)
is not included among our defining forms.
<!-- end rem-1 -->
<!-- eg-1: body_md -->
Suppose that \(\gamma\) is represented by a real interval from \(\theta_{a_0}\) to \(\theta_{b_0}\), with no puncture in its interior, and that \(a_1\neq a_0,b_0\). Using real lifts of the angular coordinates, we obtain
\[
 C_\gamma^{\operatorname{reg}}(a_0;\,a_1;\,b_0)
 =
 \int_{\theta_{a_0}}^{\theta_{b_0}}
 \cot(\theta-\theta_{a_1})\,d\theta
 =
 \log\left|
 \frac{\sin(\theta_{b_0}-\theta_{a_1})}
      {\sin(\theta_{a_0}-\theta_{a_1})}
 \right|.
\]
No regularization is needed in this case. For example, at level \(N=4\), the real interval \(\gamma:\pi/4\rightsquigarrow\pi/2\) gives
\[
 C_\gamma^{\operatorname{reg}}(1;\,0;\,2)
 =
 \log\frac{\sin(\pi/2)}{\sin(\pi/4)}
 =
 \frac12\log 2.
\]
<!-- end eg-1 -->
<!-- eg-2: body_md -->
Take \(N=4\), and let \(\gamma_{\mathrm{re}}:0\rightsquigarrow\pi/4\) be the increasing real interval with the tangent vectors specified above. The form \(\eta_0^{\cot}=\cot\theta\,d\theta\) has a pole at the initial endpoint. Since its normalized local parameter is \(\theta\), we have
\[
 \int_\varepsilon^{\pi/4}\cot\theta\,d\theta
 =
 \log\sin(\pi/4)-\log\sin\varepsilon
 =
 -\log\varepsilon-\frac12\log 2+O(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Taking the constant term therefore gives
\[
 C_{\gamma_{\mathrm{re}}}^{\operatorname{reg}}(0;\,0;\,1)
 =
 -\frac12\log 2.
\]
This value uses the specified initial tangent vector; changing its scale changes the regularized value.

<!-- eg-3: body_md -->
Again take \(N=4\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{re}}:0\rightsquigarrow\pi/4\). Since
\[
 \eta_2^{\cot}=-\tan\theta\,d\theta,
 \qquad
 \eta_0^{\cot}=\cot\theta\,d\theta,
\]
our convention for the order of integration gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_{\gamma_{\mathrm{re}}}^{\operatorname{reg}}(0;\,2,0;\,1)
 &=
 \int_{0<t_1<t_2<\pi/4}
 (-\tan t_1)\cot t_2\,dt_1\,dt_2 
     
 =
 \int_0^{\pi/4}\log(\cos t)\cot t\,dt \\
 &=
 \frac14\int_0^{1/2}\frac{\log(1-u)}{u}\,du 
     
 =
 -\frac14\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac12\right)
 =
 -\frac{\pi^2}{48}+\frac18\log^2 2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here we used \(u=\sin^2t\) and the classical dilogarithm
\(\operatorname{Li}_2(x)=\sum_{n\geq1}x^n/n^2\).
The iterated integral converges without regularization: although
\(\eta_0^{\cot}\) has a pole at \(0\), the inner integral
\(\log(\cos t)\) vanishes to order two there.

\label{thm:algebraic-realization-cotangent-kernel}
For every \(a\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), one has
\[
 \Phi^*\kappa_a=-2\,\eta_a^{\cot}
\]
on \(\Theta_N^\circ\).
Let
\(
 \gamma:\theta_{a_0}\rightsquigarrow\theta_{b_0}
\)
be a path equipped with tangential base points, and put
\[
 \gamma_v:=\Phi\circ\gamma:
 v_{a_0}\rightsquigarrow v_{b_0}.
\]
At the endpoints, \(\gamma_v\) is equipped with the tangential base points
obtained by applying \(d\Phi\) to those of \(\gamma\).  Then
\[
 \operatorname{reg}\int_{\gamma_v}
 \kappa_{a_1}\cdots\kappa_{a_r}
 =
 (-2)^r
 C_\gamma^{\operatorname{reg}}
 (a_0;\,a_1,\ldots,a_r;\,b_0).
\]
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Suppose first that \(v_a\neq\infty\).  From
\[
 v=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\delta_N}\tan\theta,
 \qquad
 v_a=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\delta_N}\tan\theta_a,
 \qquad
 \lambda_N=-\delta_N^2,
\]
we obtain
\[
 1+\lambda_Nv^2
 =
 1+\tan^2\theta
 =
 \frac{1}{\cos^2\theta}
\]
and
\[
 v-v_a
 =
 \frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\delta_N}
 \bigl(\tan\theta-\tan\theta_a\bigr).
\]
Since
\[
 \tan\theta-\tan\theta_a
 =
 \frac{\sin(\theta-\theta_a)}
 {\cos\theta\cos\theta_a},
\]
it follows that
\[
 \frac{1+\lambda_Nv^2}{(v-v_a)^2}
 =
 \lambda_N
 \frac{\cos^2\theta_a}
 {\sin^2(\theta-\theta_a)}.
\]
The factor \(\lambda_N\cos^2\theta_a\) is independent of \(\theta\).
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \kappa_a
 =
 d\log
 \frac{1+\lambda_Nv^2}{(v-v_a)^2} 
 =
 -2\,d\log\sin(\theta-\theta_a) 
 =
 -2\cot(\theta-\theta_a)\,d\theta
 =
 -2\,\eta_a^{\cot}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Now suppose that \(v_a=\infty\).  Then \(N\) is even and
\(\theta_a=\pi/2\pmod{\pi}\).  Since
\[
 1+\lambda_Nv^2
 =
 1+\tan^2\theta
 =
 \sec^2\theta,
\]
we have
\[
 \kappa_a
 =
 d\log(\sec^2\theta)
 =
 2\tan\theta\,d\theta.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
 \cot\left(\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
 =
 -\tan\theta.
\]
Hence
\[
 \kappa_a
 =
 -2\cot(\theta-\theta_a)\,d\theta
 =
 -2\,\eta_a^{\cot}
\]
also in this case.

The identity for iterated integrals follows from the pullback formula,
multilinearity, and compatibility of tangential regularization with the
change of variables.

\label{def:cotangent-punctured-line}
The \textit{cotangent punctured line of level \(N\)} is the open curve
\[
 X_N^{\cot}
 :=
 \mathbb P^1_F\setminus\operatorname{Supp}(B_N^{\cot}).
\]
Its boundary consists of the \(F\)-rational cotangent points \(v_a\) and
the degree-two Cayley boundary divisor \(D_N^{\mathrm{Cay}}\).

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:cotangent-forms-over-F}
For every \(a\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), the form \(\kappa_a\) is an
\(F\)-rational logarithmic differential form on \(X_N^{\cot}\).
More precisely, if
\[
 f_a(v)=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{1+\lambda_Nv^2}{(v-v_a)^2},
   & v_a\neq\infty,\\[8pt]
 1+\lambda_Nv^2,
   & v_a=\infty,
 \end{cases}
\]
then
\[
 \kappa_a=d\log f_a(v)
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{div}(f_a)
 =
 D_N^{\mathrm{Cay}}-2[v_a].
\]
Thus \(\kappa_a\) has residue \(1\) at each of the two geometric Cayley
boundary points and residue \(-2\) at \(v_a\).
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Both \(v_a\) and \(\lambda_N\) belong to \(F\), so
\(
 f_a(v)\in F(v)^\times.
\)
Its zero divisor is \(D_N^{\mathrm{Cay}}\), while its pole divisor is
\(2[v_a]\), including the case \(v_a=\infty\).  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{div}(f_a)
 =
 D_N^{\mathrm{Cay}}-2[v_a].
\]
The asserted properties follow by taking the logarithmic differential.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- rem-2: body_md -->
The fact that \(X_N^{\cot}\) and the forms \(\kappa_a\) are defined over \(F\) does not, by itself, imply that every period of \(X_N^{\cot}\) is a mixed Tate period over \(F\).  The nonsplit divisor \(D_N^{\mathrm{Cay}}\) may contribute an Artin--Tate direction. However it splits into its two Cayley boundary points after extending scalars from \(F\) to \(E\). The descent construction studied below select particular families of periods for which a mixed Tate interpretation over a suitable ring of integers of \(F\) can be established.

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:cotangent-cyclotomic-isomorphism}
The Cayley transformation
\[
 \Psi(v):=\frac{1+\delta_Nv}{1-\delta_Nv},
 \qquad
 \Psi^{-1}(z)=\frac{z-1}{\delta_N(z+1)},
\]
induces an isomorphism
\[
 \Psi:(X_N^{\cot})_E
 \xrightarrow{\sim}X_{N,E}^{\mathrm{cyc}}.
\]
For every \(a\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), one has
\[
 \kappa_a
 =
 \Psi^*(\omega_0-2\omega_{\xi_a})
 =
 -2\Psi^*h_a.
\]
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The transformation \(\Psi\) sends
\[
 v_a\longmapsto\xi_a,
 \qquad
 -\delta_N^{-1}\longmapsto0,
 \qquad
 \delta_N^{-1}\longmapsto\infty.
\]
When \(v_a=\infty\), its image is \(-1=\xi_a\). Thus \(\Psi\) identifies the boundary divisors and induces the stated isomorphism.

Suppose first that \(v_a\neq\infty\). Since \(\lambda_N=-\delta_N^2\), substitution gives
\[
 1+\lambda_Nv^2=\frac{4z}{(z+1)^2},
 \qquad
 v-v_a
 =
 \frac{2(z-\xi_a)}
 {\delta_N(z+1)(\xi_a+1)}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \frac{1+\lambda_Nv^2}{(v-v_a)^2}
 =
 \delta_N^2(\xi_a+1)^2
 \frac{z}{(z-\xi_a)^2}.
\]
Taking logarithmic differentials yields
\[
 \kappa_a
 =
 \Psi^*\bigl(d\log z-2d\log(z-\xi_a)\bigr).
\]
If \(v_a=\infty\), then \(\xi_a=-1\), and
\[
 \kappa_a
 =
 d\log(1+\lambda_Nv^2)
 =
 \Psi^*\left(d\log\frac{4z}{(z+1)^2}\right).
\]
This gives the same formula.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- rem-3: body_md -->
The composition of \(\Phi\) from \eqref{eq:theta-to-v-map} with \(\Psi\) is
\[
 (\Psi\circ\Phi)(\theta)=e^{2\sqrt{-1}\theta}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 (\Psi\circ\Phi)^*h_a=\eta_a^{\cot}.
\]
Thus the analytic cotangent differential form corresponds to the cyclotomic logarithmic form \(h_a\).

With the paths and tangential data specified above, one has

 ={}&
 \sum_{\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_r\in\{0,1\}}

Equivalently,
\[

 I_{\gamma_z}\bigl(
 \xi(a_1,\varepsilon_1),\ldots,\xi(a_r,\varepsilon_r)
 \bigr).

Every iterated integral on the right has length \(r\).

\label{thm:cyclotomic-endpoint-formula}
Let \(m,n\in\mathbb Z\) represent \(a_0,b_0\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\),
respectively. For the reference path \(\gamma_{m,n}\), with the
tangential data specified above, one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &C_{\gamma_{m,n}}^{\operatorname{reg}}
 (a_0;\,a_1,\ldots,a_r;\,b_0)
           
 =
 \sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^j
 \mathcal H_m^{\mathrm{fr}}(a_j,\ldots,a_1)
 \mathcal H_n^{\mathrm{fr}}(a_{j+1},\ldots,a_r).
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular,
\[
 C_{\gamma_{m,n}}^{\operatorname{reg}}
 (a_0;\,a_1,\ldots,a_r;\,b_0)
 \in\mathcal Z_{N,r}.
\]
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The pullback identity \(e^*h_a=\eta_a^{\cot}\), together with the
transported tangential data, gives
\[
 C_{\gamma_{m,n}}^{\operatorname{reg}}
 (a_0;\,a_1,\ldots,a_r;\,b_0)
 =
 \operatorname{reg}\int_{\rho_{m,n}}h_{a_1}\cdots h_{a_r}.
\]
Apply Chen's composition formula to
\(\rho_{m,n}=p_m^{-1}*p_n\). Path reversal yields
\[
 \operatorname{reg}\int_{p_m^{-1}}h_{a_1}\cdots h_{a_j}
 =
 (-1)^j\mathcal H_m^{\mathrm{fr}}(a_j,\ldots,a_1),
\]
which proves the endpoint formula.

By the preceding subsection,
\[
 \mathcal H_m(a_1,\ldots,a_s)\in\mathcal Z_{N,s}.
\]
Moreover, for the standard branches,
\[
 \operatorname{Li}^{\shuffle}_1(\zeta_N)
 -
 \operatorname{Li}^{\shuffle}_1(\zeta_N^{-1})
 =
 \frac{N-2}{N}\pi\sqrt{-1}.
\]
Since \(N\geq3\), this implies
\(\pi\sqrt{-1}\in\mathcal Z_{N,1}\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:framed-radial-h-integral} and the shuffle product
therefore give
\[
 \mathcal H_m^{\mathrm{fr}}(a_1,\ldots,a_s)
 \in\mathcal Z_{N,s}.
\]
Each product in the endpoint formula has total weight \(r\).
A further application of the shuffle product proves the assertion.

The integers \(m\) and \(n\) record the chosen lifts of the endpoint
labels and hence are part of the path data.  Simultaneously replacing
\((m,n)\) by \((m+Nk,n+Nk)\) does not change the tangential homotopy

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:cotangent-difference-kernels}
Let \(\Phi\) denote the angular-to-algebraic coordinate map defined
in (\ref{eq:theta-to-v-map}). Then
\[
 \kappa^{\mathrm{diff}}_{a,b}=-\frac12(\kappa_a-\kappa_b),
 \qquad
 \Phi^*\kappa^{\mathrm{diff}}_{a,b}=\eta_{a,b}^{\mathrm{diff}}.
\]
If \(a\neq b\), the only poles of \(\kappa^{\mathrm{diff}}_{a,b}\) are \(v_a\)
and \(v_b\), with residues \(1\) and \(-1\), respectively.
In particular, \(\kappa^{\mathrm{diff}}_{a,b}\) is regular at both geometric
points of the quadratic boundary divisor.
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
In homogeneous coordinates, the definition of \(\kappa_a\) can
be written uniformly as
\[
 \kappa_a
 =
 d\log\frac{X_1^2-\delta_N^2X_0^2}{L_a(X_0,X_1)^2}.
\]
This includes \(v_a=\infty\), since then \(L_a=X_1\).
Consequently,

 =
 -2d\log\frac{L_a}{L_b}
 =
 -2\kappa^{\mathrm{diff}}_{a,b}.
\]
Applying \(\Phi^*\kappa_a=-2\eta_a^{\cot}\) proves the pullback
identity. The statements about poles and residues follow from
\[
 \operatorname{div}\left(\frac{L_a}{L_b}\right)
 =

\label{def:cotangent-difference-integral}
Let
\[
 a_0,b_0,a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_r,b_r\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z,
\]
and let
\(\gamma:\theta_{a_0}\rightsquigarrow\theta_{b_0}\)
be an admissible path with prescribed tangential endpoint data.
\textit{Iterated cotangent-difference integral} is defined by
\[
 C_\gamma^{\mathrm{diff},\operatorname{reg}}
 \left(
  a_0;\,(a_1,b_1),\ldots,(a_r,b_r);\,b_0
 \right)
 :=
 \operatorname{reg}\int_\gamma
 \eta_{a_1,b_1}^{\mathrm{diff}}\cdots
 \eta_{a_r,b_r}^{\mathrm{diff}}.
\]
Its weight is the length \(r\). The empty integral has value \(1\).
<!-- end defn-4 -->
<!-- eg-8: body_md -->
Assume first that the four points
\[
 v_{a_0},\quad v_{b_0},\quad v_{a_1},\quad v_{b_1}
\]
are finite and that the integral converges without regularization. Since
\[
 \eta_{a_1,b_1}^{\mathrm{diff}}
 =
 d\log
 \frac{\sin(\theta-\theta_{a_1})}
      {\sin(\theta-\theta_{b_1})},
\]
we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
&C_\gamma^{\mathrm{diff}}
 \bigl(a_0;\,(a_1,b_1);\,b_0\bigr)   
        
=
 \log_\gamma
 \frac{
  \sin(\theta_{b_0}-\theta_{a_1})
  \sin(\theta_{a_0}-\theta_{b_1})
 }{
  \sin(\theta_{b_0}-\theta_{b_1})
  \sin(\theta_{a_0}-\theta_{a_1})
 }.
\end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently, in the \(v\)-coordinate,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&C_\gamma^{\mathrm{diff}}
 \bigl(a_0;\,(a_1,b_1);\,b_0\bigr)   
        
=
 \log_{\gamma_v}
 \frac{
  (v_{b_0}-v_{a_1})(v_{a_0}-v_{b_1})
 }{
  (v_{b_0}-v_{b_1})(v_{a_0}-v_{a_1})
 }.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus a length-one cotangent-difference integral is a logarithm of an
\(F\)-rational cross-ratio. If some of the points coincide or are at
infinity, the same formula is interpreted using homogeneous coordinates
and tangential regularization.

\label{prop:cotangent-difference-good-reduction}
The marked curve
\[
 \bigl(\mathbb P^1_F,(v_a)_{a\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z}\bigr)
\]
admits a model over \(\mathcal O_{F,S}\) whose marked sections
are pairwise disjoint.
<!-- end prop-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Choose three distinct labels \(c_0,c_1,c_\infty\), and let
\[
 t:\mathbb P^1_F\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb P^1_F
\]
be the unique projective coordinate satisfying
\[
 t(v_{c_0})=0,
 \qquad
 t(v_{c_1})=1,
 \qquad
 t(v_{c_\infty})=\infty.
\]
Write \(t_a=t(v_a)\). By invariance of cross-ratios under the
Cayley transformation, for \(a\neq c_\infty\),
\[
 t_a
 =
 \frac{
  (\xi_a-\xi_{c_0})(\xi_{c_1}-\xi_{c_\infty})
 }{
  (\xi_a-\xi_{c_\infty})(\xi_{c_1}-\xi_{c_0})
 }.
\]
Every nonzero difference of two \(N\)-th roots of unity is a unit
after inverting \(N\). Hence
\[
 t_a\in\mathcal O_{F,S}^{\times}
 \qquad
 (a\neq c_0,c_\infty).
\]
Similarly, for distinct \(a,b\neq c_\infty\),
\[
 t_a-t_b
 =
 \frac{
  (\xi_{c_1}-\xi_{c_\infty})
  (\xi_{c_0}-\xi_{c_\infty})(\xi_a-\xi_b)
 }{
  (\xi_{c_1}-\xi_{c_0})
  (\xi_a-\xi_{c_\infty})(\xi_b-\xi_{c_\infty})
 }
 \in\mathcal O_{F,S}^{\times}.
\]
These expressions lie in \(F\), since \(t\) and the points \(v_a\)
are defined over \(F\). Their unit property over the cyclotomic
extension therefore implies the asserted unit property over \(F\).

Consequently, the finite points \(t_a\) define pairwise disjoint
sections of \(\mathbb A^1_{\mathcal O_{F,S}}\), also disjoint
from the section at infinity. They give the required model.

 0=i_0<i_1<\cdots<i_k<i_{k+1}=r+1,

\begin{aligned}
&\Delta_{\mathcal M}
 I^{\mathfrak m}
 (\beta_0;\kappa_{d_1}^\circ|\cdots|

    \beta^{(h)};

    \kappa_{d_{i_{h+1}-1}}^\circ;
    \beta^{(h+1)}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:motdiff-goncharov-coproduct}
\end{equation}

 \mathcal H_N^{\mathrm{diff},\mathfrak m}(\mathcal B_N)
 \subseteq
 \mathcal H_N^{\mathrm{diff},\mathfrak m}(\mathcal B_N)
 \otimes_{\mathbb Q}
 \mathcal H_N^{\mathrm{diff},\mathfrak m}(\mathcal B_N).
 \label{eq:motdiff-coproduct-closure}
\end{equation}

\(\mathcal H_N^{\mathrm{diff},\mathfrak m}(\mathcal B_N)\)
is a connected graded Hopf subalgebra of
\(\mathcal A^{\mathcal M}_{F,S}\).

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
For the Tate-framed classes just defined,
\eqref{eq:motdiff-goncharov-coproduct} is Goncharov's cut formula
\cite[Theorems~1.2 and~3.3]{Goncharov2005}.
The fixed tangential base points are used in every factor.
The assertion with an endpoint at infinity is understood in
the local coordinate \(q_{c_\infty}=1/t\), with its tangent
transported under the coordinate change.

Put
\(H=\mathcal H_N^{\mathrm{diff},\mathfrak m}(\mathcal B_N)\).
Each retained letter \(\kappa_d^\circ\) is a difference form.
Every complementary block is a word in the same forms, and its
endpoints belong to \(\mathcal B_N\). Thus every tensor factor on
the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:motdiff-goncharov-coproduct} belongs to \(H\).
Multilinearity and \eqref{eq:motdiff-signed-expansion} give the

is multiplicative, \eqref{eq:motdiff-coproduct-closure} follows.

The counit restricts to \(H\), so \(H\) is a connected graded
sub-bialgebra. 
For a positive-degree homogeneous element \(x\in H\), write

 =x\otimes1+1\otimes x+\sum_\nu x'_\nu\otimes x''_\nu,
\]

\[
 \mathsf S(x)=-x-\sum_\nu\mathsf S(x'_\nu)x''_\nu.
\]
Induction on degree, starting with \(\mathsf S(1)=1\), gives \(\mathsf S(H)\subseteq H\). This proves the Hopf subalgebra assertion.

<!-- prop-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:cyclotomic-cross-ratio-s-unit}
The cross-ratio \(R(p,q;a,b)\) belongs to
\(\mathcal O_{F,S}^{\times}\). More precisely, it is a real
cyclotomic \(S\)-unit.
<!-- end prop-7 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The cross-ratio belongs to \(E\), and its sine expression shows that
it is real. Hence it belongs to \(F=E^+\).

Every nonzero difference of \(N\)-th roots of unity is a unit after
inverting the primes above \(N\). Indeed,
\[
 \xi_c-\xi_d=\xi_d(\zeta_N^{c-d}-1),
\]
and the prime divisors of \(1-\zeta_N^{c-d}\) lie above primes
dividing \(N\). Thus \(R(p,q;a,b)\) is an \(S\)-unit in \(E\).
Since it belongs to \(F\), it lies in
\(\mathcal O_{F,S}^{\times}\). Its expression as a quotient of
cyclotomic differences gives the final assertion.

With upper endpoint \(T<1\), successive integration and
\[
 \frac{d}{dT}\operatorname{Li}_r(\pm T)
 =
 \frac{\operatorname{Li}_{r-1}(\pm T)}{T}
\]
identify the two iterated integrals with
\(-\operatorname{Li}_r(T)\) and

 \operatorname{Li}_r(1)=\zeta(r),

 =

\]
Absolute convergence follows from the same calculation: on
\(0<t<1\), each constituent differential form has constant sign, so the
integral of the absolute value is the absolute value of the
corresponding convergent integral.
