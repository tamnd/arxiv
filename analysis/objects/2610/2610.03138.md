<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Let $H,K$ be two subgroups of a finite group $G$ and choose a set of representative $S \subset G$ for the double coset $K \backslash G/H$.
    For any representation $\rho$ of $H$ and any $s \in S$ let $\rho^s \colon sHs^{-1} \to V$ defined by $\rho^s(shs^{-1})= \rho(h)$. Then
    \[ \operatorname{Res}^G_K \operatorname{Ind}_H^G \rho \cong \bigoplus_{s \in S} \operatorname{Ind}_{K \cap sHs^{-1}}^K \operatorname{Res}^{sHs^{-1}}_{K \cap sHs^{-1}} \rho^s. \]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
The irreducible representations of $G$ over an algebraically closed field are exactly the representations
    \[ \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes_\varphi H_\chi}^G \chi \otimes \rho \]
    where $\chi$ runs over a set of representatives of $H$-conjugate irreducible representations of $K$ and $\rho$ run over the irreducible representations of $H_\chi$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let us compute 
    \[ \langle \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes H_\chi}^G \chi \otimes \rho , \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes H_{\chi'}}^G \chi' \otimes \rho' \rangle = \langle  \chi \otimes \rho , \operatorname{Res}_{K \rtimes H_\chi}^G \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes H_{\chi'}}^G \chi' \otimes \rho' \rangle \]
    Using the Mackey subgroup theorem we have 
    \[\operatorname{Res}_{K \rtimes H_\chi}^G \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes H_{\chi'}}^G \chi' \otimes \rho' \cong \bigoplus _{s \in S} \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes ( H_\chi \cap sH_{\chi'}s^{-1})}^{K \rtimes  H_\chi } 
    \operatorname{Res}^{K \rtimes sH_{\chi'}s^{-1}}_{K \rtimes ( H_\chi \cap sH_{\chi'}s^{-1})} (\chi' \otimes \rho')^s, \]
    where $S$ is a set of representative of $H_{\chi'}\backslash H /H_\chi$ and the subgroup $K$ is fixed by $s$. Therefore,
    \begin{align*}
        \langle \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes H_\chi}^G \chi \otimes \rho , \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes H_{\chi'}}^G \chi' \otimes \rho' \rangle &= \sum_{s\in S} \langle \operatorname{Res}_{K \rtimes ( H_\chi \cap sH_{\chi'}s^{-1})}^{K \rtimes  H_\chi }  \chi \otimes \rho , \operatorname{Res}^{K \rtimes sH_{\chi'}s^{-1}}_{K \rtimes ( H_\chi \cap sH_{\chi'}s^{-1})} (\chi' \otimes \rho')^s \rangle  \\
        &= \sum_{s\in S} \langle \chi, (\chi')^s \rangle \langle \operatorname{Res}_{H_\chi \cap sH_{\chi'}s^{-1}}^{H_\chi } \rho , \operatorname{Res}^{sH_{\chi'}s^{-1}}_{H_\chi \cap sH_{\chi'}s^{-1}} (\rho')^s \rangle
    \end{align*}
    that is zero if $\chi$ and $\chi'$ are not $H$-conjugate.
    Otherwise, $\chi = (\chi')^s$ and $\operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes H_{\chi'}}^G \chi' \otimes \rho' = \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes H_{\chi}}^G \chi \otimes (\rho')^s$ so we can assume $\chi'=\chi$
    We finally have
    \[\langle \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes H_\chi}^G \chi \otimes \rho , \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes H_{\chi}}^G \chi \otimes \rho' \rangle = \langle \chi, \chi \rangle \langle \rho, \rho' \rangle.\]
    In particular the representations $ \operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes_\varphi H_\chi}^G \chi \otimes \rho $ are irreducible and pairwise non-isomorphic.

    Moreover, let $V$ be any irreducible representation of $G$ then $\operatorname{Res}^G_K V$ contains an irreducible representation $\chi$ and $H_\chi$ act on the $\chi$-isotypical component of $V$.
    This action contains an irreducible representation $\rho$ of $H_\chi$ and its easy to check that $\operatorname{Res}^G_{K\rtimes H_\chi} V$ contains the representation $\chi \otimes \rho$.
    In particular, $V$ contains the subrepresentation $\operatorname{Ind}_{K \rtimes H_\chi}^G \chi \otimes \rho $.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{representationsbn}
    The irreducible representations of $B_n$ are parametrized by pairs of partitions $(\pi, \lambda)$ such that $\lvert \pi \rvert + \lvert \lambda \rvert = n$:
    \[ V_{\pi,\lambda}= \operatorname{Ind}_{B_{k}\times B_{n-k}}^{B_n} V_\pi \boxtimes \tilde{V}_\lambda.\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
This is a particular case of the previous theorem. The stabilizer in $S_n$ of the $\mathbb{Z}_2^n$-representation $1^k \boxtimes (-1)^{n-k}$ is $S_k \times S_{n-k}$ and its irreducible representations are exactly tensor product of two Specht modules.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
The \textit{Frobenius characteristic map} in type $B_n$ is 
    \[ \operatorname{ch}(\chi) = \frac{1}{2^n n!}\sum_{\sigma \in B_n} \operatorname{Tr}(\chi(\sigma)) p_{\sigma^+}[X+Y]p_{\sigma^-}[X-Y]\]
    where $\sigma^+$ is the partition of associated with positive cycle of $\sigma$ and $\sigma^-$ associated to negative cycles.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
We have:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $\operatorname{ch} (\operatorname{Ind}_{B_k \times B_{n-k}}^{B_n} \rho \boxtimes \chi )= \operatorname{ch}(\rho) \operatorname{ch}(\chi)$,
        \item $\operatorname{ch}(V_{\pi,\lambda})=s_\pi(X)s_\lambda(Y)$,
        \item $\operatorname{ch} (\operatorname{Ind}_{S_n}^{B_n} \chi) = \operatorname{ch}(\chi)[X+Y]$.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
\begin{enumerate}
        \item We use the formula for the induced character
        \[ \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Ind}_{B_k \times B_{n-k}}^{B_n} \rho \boxtimes \chi)(\sigma) = \frac{1}{2^n k! n-k!} \sum_{\substack{\tau \in B_n \\ \tau^{-1} \sigma \tau \in B_k \times B_{n-k}} } \operatorname{Tr}((\rho \boxtimes \chi)(\tau^{-1} \sigma \tau)) \]
        and so
        \[\operatorname{ch} (\operatorname{Ind}_{B_k \times B_{n-k}}^{B_n} \rho \boxtimes \chi )= \frac{1}{2^{2n} n! k! n-k!} \sum_{\substack{\sigma, \tau \in B_n \\ \tau^{-1} \sigma \tau \in B_k \times B_{n-k}}} \operatorname{Tr}((\rho \boxtimes \chi)(\tau^{-1} \sigma \tau)) p_{\sigma^+}[X+Y]p_{\sigma^-}[X-Y]. \]
        Notice that $\sigma$ and $\tau^{-1} \sigma \tau$ have the same signed cycle type because are conjugate and the formula simplifies to
        \[\operatorname{ch} (\operatorname{Ind}_{B_k \times B_{n-k}}^{B_n} \rho \boxtimes \chi )= \frac{1}{2^{n} k! n-k!} \sum_{ \sigma \in B_k \times B_{n-k}} \operatorname{Tr}((\rho \boxtimes \chi)( \sigma )) p_{\sigma^+}[X+Y]p_{\sigma^-}[X-Y]= \operatorname{ch}(\rho) \operatorname{ch}(\chi).\]
        \item By definition of $V_{\pi,\lambda}$ and the first assertion, it is enough to prove that $\operatorname{ch}(V_\pi)=s_\pi(X)$ and $\operatorname{ch}( \tilde{V}_\lambda ) =s_\pi(Y)$.
        We split the signed permutations of $B_n$ accordingly the unsigned underlying permutation of $S_n$:
        \[\operatorname{ch}(V_\pi)= \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \operatorname{Tr} (\operatorname{Specht}_\pi (\sigma)) \frac{1}{2^n}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\sigma)} 2^{\sigma_i-1} (p_{\sigma_i}[X+Y] + p_{\sigma_i}[X-Y]) = \operatorname{ch}(\operatorname{Specht}_\pi)(X) = s_\pi(X). \]
        The same proof holds for $\tilde{V}_\lambda$ with the difference that the character depends on the signed cycle type. Each negative cycle multiply the $S_n$-character by $-1$:
        \[\operatorname{ch}(\tilde{V}_\lambda)= \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \operatorname{Tr} (\operatorname{Specht}_\lambda (\sigma)) \frac{1}{2^n}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\sigma)} 2^{\sigma_i-1} (p_{\sigma_i}[X+Y] - p_{\sigma_i}[X-Y]) = \operatorname{ch}(\operatorname{Specht}_\lambda)(Y) = s_\lambda(Y). \]
        \item We apply the formula for the induced character
        \[ \operatorname{ch} (\operatorname{Ind}_{S_n}^{B_n} \chi) = \frac{1}{2^n (n!)^2} \sum_{\substack{\sigma, \tau \in B_n \\ \tau^{-1} \sigma \tau \in S_n}} \operatorname{Tr}(\chi(\tau^{-1} \sigma \tau)) p_{\sigma^+}[X+Y] p_{\sigma^-}[X-Y]\]
        and the permutations $\sigma$ and $\tau^{-1} \sigma \tau \in S_n$ have the same cycle type consisting only in positive cycles.
        We obtain
        \[\operatorname{ch} (\operatorname{Ind}_{S_n}^{B_n} \chi) = \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{ \sigma  \in S_n} \operatorname{Tr}(\chi( \sigma )) p_{\sigma}[X+Y] = \operatorname{ch} (\chi)[X+Y]. \qedhere\]
    \end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Recall that, for any $n$, there is a distinct $1$-dimensional representation $\operatorname{sgn}_n^B \colon B_n \to \mathbb{C}^{\times}$, called the \textit{sign} representation, corresponding to the $2$-partition $(\emptyset,(1^n))$. Through the Frobenius characteristic construction introduced above, tensoring with $\operatorname{sgn}_n^B$ in degree $n$ gives an involution $\omega_B \colon \Lambda^{(2)} \to \Lambda^{(2)}$. 

Moreover, there is the $1$-dimensional representation obtained as the composition of the projection $B_n \to S_n$ with the $\operatorname{sgn}$ representation of $S_n$, corresponding thus to the $2$-partition $((1^n),\emptyset)$. As above, tensoring with such a representation gives an involution which we denote by $\omega \colon \Lambda^{(2)} \to \Lambda^{(2)}$.
Notice that, when restricted to the subrings $\Lambda(X),\Lambda(Y)$, the map $\omega$ corresponds to the corresponding canonical involution $\omega$ in the ring of symmetric functions.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A $\lambda$-ring $R$ is a commutative $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra with homomorphisms $\psi_d \colon R \to R$ for any $d \geq 1$ such that $\psi_{d'}(\psi_d(r))=\psi_{dd'}(r)$ for every $d,d'\in \mathbb{N}_{>0}$ and $r \in R$. The morphisms $\psi_d$ are called  Adams operations. 

A morphism $f\colon R \to R'$ of $\lambda$-rings is a morphism of $\mathbb{Q}$-algebras commuting with the Adams operations.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
The ring $\mathbb{Q}(t)$ is a $\lambda$-ring , with Adams operations $\psi_d(f(t))=f(t^d)$. Notice that we have $$ \psi_d(t^n)=t^{nd} .$$

The ring $\Lambda(X)$ is a $\lambda$-ring, with Adams operation $\psi_d(f(x_1,\dots,x_n,\dots))=f(x_1^d,\dots,x_n^d,\dots,)$. We remark that we have $$\psi_d(p_n)=p_{nd} .$$

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:Ind_repr_wr0}
    For every representation $V$ of $S_k$ and $W$ of $B_j$ we have
    \begin{align}
        & \operatorname{ch}( \operatorname{Ind}_{B_j \wr S_k}^{B_{jk}} W^{\boxtimes k} \otimes V) = \operatorname{ch}(V)[\operatorname{ch}(W)].
    \end{align}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Since the wanted equality is linear in the entry $V$, it is enough to show this for any basis of the class functions of $S_k$. In particular, it is enough to show that $ \operatorname{ch}( \operatorname{Ind}_{B_j \wr S_k}^{B_{jk}} \chi_W^{k} \otimes 1_{C_{\lambda}})=\operatorname{ch}(1_{C_{\lambda}})[\operatorname{ch}(W)]$ for every $\lambda$ partition of $k$, where $C_{\lambda}$ is the corresponding conjugacy class of $S_{k}$ and $1_{C_{\lambda}}$ its characteristic function. Recall that $\operatorname{ch}(1_{C_{\lambda}})=p_{\lambda}/z_{\lambda}$. Moreover, if $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r)$, we have $$p_{\lambda}[\operatorname{ch}(W)]=\prod_{i=1}^r p_{\lambda_i}[\operatorname{ch}(W)] .$$

Notice that, for any two representations $W_1,W_2$ of $B_j$ and any $d \in \mathbb{N}_{>0}$, we have that the following equality of class functions \begin{equation}(\chi_{W_1} \oplus \chi_{W_2})^{d} \otimes 1_{C_{(d)}}=\chi_{W_1}^d \otimes 1_{C_{(d)}} \oplus \chi_{W_2}^d \otimes 1_{C_{(d)}} .\label{linearityplethysm}\end{equation}

Eq.(\ref{linearityplethysm}) above is not true in general, replacing $1_{C_{(d)}}$ with another class function. For the function $1_{C_{(d)}}$ this comes from the fact that a cycle of length $d$ acts transitively on the set $\{1,\dots,d\}$.

From \Cref{lem:ind_B_j_S_k} and the linearity of the plethysm $p_d[-]$, we deduce that for any representation $W$ of $B_j$ and any $d$, we have $$\operatorname{ch}( \operatorname{Ind}_{B_j \wr S_d}^{B_{jd}} \chi_W^{k} \otimes z_{(d)}1_{C_{(d)}})=p_d[\operatorname{ch}(W)] .$$

Notice now that we have $$1_{C_{\lambda}}=\dfrac{z_{(\lambda_1)} \cdots z_{(\lambda_r)}}{z_{\lambda}} \operatorname{Ind}_{S_{\lambda_1} \times \cdots \times S_{\lambda_r}}^{S_k}(1_{C_{(\lambda_1)}} \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes 1_{C_{(\lambda_r)}}) .$$
We have therefore \begin{align*}&\operatorname{Ind}_{B_j \wr S_k}^{B_{jk}} \chi_W^{k} \otimes 1_{C_{\lambda}}=\\ &=\dfrac{z_{(\lambda_1)} \cdots z_{(\lambda_r)}}{z_{\lambda}}\operatorname{Ind}_{B_j \wr S_k}^{B_{jk}} \operatorname{Ind}^{B_j \wr S_k}_{(B_j \wr S_{\lambda_1}) \times \cdots (B_j \wr \times S_{\lambda_r})}(\chi_W^{\lambda_1} \otimes 1_{C_{(\lambda_1)}} \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes \chi_W^{\lambda_r} \otimes 1_{C_{(\lambda_r)}} )=\\ &=\dfrac{z_{(\lambda_1)} \cdots z_{(\lambda_r)}}{z_{\lambda}} \operatorname{Ind}^{B_{jk}}_{B_{j \lambda_1} \times \cdots \times B_{j \lambda_r}} \operatorname{Ind}^{B_{j \lambda_1} \times \cdots \times B_{j \lambda_r}}_{(B_j \wr S_{\lambda_1}) \times \cdots (B_j \wr \times S_{\lambda_r})}(\chi_W^{\lambda_1} \otimes 1_{C_{(\lambda_1)}} \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes \chi_W^{\lambda_r} \otimes 1_{C_{(\lambda_r)}} )=\\ &=\prod_{i=1}^r \operatorname{ch}(\operatorname{Ind}^{B_{j\lambda_i}}_{B_j \times S_{\lambda_i}}(\chi_W^{ \lambda_i} \otimes 1_{C_{(\lambda_i)}}))=\dfrac{p_{\lambda}[\operatorname{ch}(W)]}{z_{\lambda}} . \qedhere
\end{align*}

$$

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{compositionalformulaA}
The coefficient of $t^n$ in the power series $H(G(t))$ is given as follows.

\begin{equation}
    \sum_{\lambda \in \mathcal{P}_n}m_{l(\lambda)}\left(\operatorname{Res}_{\Lambda(|\operatorname{supp}(\lambda)|)}^{\Lambda_{l(\lambda)}}(H_{l(\lambda)})[\prod_{j \in \operatorname{supp}(\lambda)} G_j]\right).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
For each $m \in \mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}$, write $H_m=\displaystyle \sum_{\mu \in  \mathcal{P}_m}H_{\mu}z_{\mu}^{-1}p_{\mu}$. If $\mu=(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_{l(\mu)})$, we have $$p_{\mu}[G(t)]=\prod_{i=1}^{l(\mu)}\left(\sum_{a_i \geq 1}p_{\mu_i}[G_{a_i}]t^{a_i\mu_i}\right) .$$

Let $A_n=\{(\mu,a=(a_1,\dots,a_{l(\mu)}) \in \mathcal{P} \times \mathbb{N}^{l(\mu)} \ | \ \sum_i \mu_i a_i=n\}$. The coefficient of $t^n$ in $H(G(t))$ is thus given by $$\sum_{(\mu,a) \in A_n}H_{\mu}z_{\mu}^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{l(\mu)}p_{\mu_i}[G_{a_i}] .$$

We have a map $f\colon A_n \to \mathcal{P}_n$ defined as follows. The element $f((\mu,a))$ is the partition $\lambda \in \mathcal{P}_n$ such that $\operatorname{supp}(\lambda)=\{a_1,\dots,a_{l(\mu)}\}$ and $m_{\lambda,a_j}=\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{h \text{ s.t.}\\ a_h=a_j}}\mu_h$. Notice that $l(f(\mu,a))=|\mu|$.

Fix now $\lambda \in \mathcal{P}_n$. We compute now $$\sum_{\substack{(\mu,a) \in A_n\\ f(\mu,a)=\lambda}}H_{\mu}z_{\mu}^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{l(\mu)}p_{\mu_i}[G_{a_i}] .$$
 Let $g \colon \{(\mu,a) \in A_n \ | \ f(\mu,a)=\lambda\} \to \mathcal{P}_{l(\lambda)}$ be the function $g(\mu,a)=\mu$. Put $k=m(\lambda)$, let $\operatorname{supp}(\lambda)=(b_1,\dots,b_k)$. For any $\mu \in \mathcal{P}_{l(\lambda)}$, we defined the following map $$\psi \colon g^{-1}(\mu) \to \{(\mu^1,\dots,\mu^k) \in \mathcal{P}_{m_{\lambda,b_1}} \times \cdots \times \mathcal{P}_{m_{\lambda,b_k}} \ | \ \mu^1 \cup \cdots  \cup \mu^k=\mu\} $$  as follows. Given an element $(\mu,a) \in g^{-1}(\mu)$ we associate to it the multipartition $\psi(\mu,a)=(\psi(\mu,a)^1,\dots,
\psi(\mu,a)^k)$ where $\psi(\mu,a)^j$ is the partition whose entries are given by the integers $\mu_h$ with $a_h=b_j$. Notice that, if $\psi(\mu,a)=(\mu^1,\dots,\mu^k)$, we have $$\prod_{i=1}^{l(\mu)}p_{\mu_i}[G_{a_i}]=\prod_{j=1}^k p_{\mu^j}[G_{b_j}] .$$

 In particular, we have that \begin{align}&\sum_{\substack{(\mu,a) \in A_n\\ f(\mu,a)=\lambda}}H_{\mu}z_{\mu}^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{l(\mu)}p_{\mu_i}[G_{a_i}]= \nonumber \\ &=\sum_{\mu \in \mathcal{P}_{l(\lambda)}}H_{\mu}z_{\mu}^{-1} \sum_{\substack{(\mu^1,\dots,\mu^k) \in \mathcal{P}_{m_{\lambda,b_1}} \times \cdots \times \mathcal{P}_{m_{\lambda,b_k}} \\ \mu^1 \cup \cdots  \cup \mu^k=\mu}}|\psi^{-1}(\mu,a)| \prod_{j=1}^k p_{\mu^j}[G_{b_j}] .\label{rewriting1}\end{align}

Assume that $\operatorname{supp}(\mu)=\{c_1,\dots,c_t\}$. We have that \begin{equation}
    |\psi^{-1}(\mu^1,\dots,\mu^k)|=\prod_{i=1}^t \dfrac{(m_{\mu,c_i})!}{\prod_{j=1}^k (m_{\mu^j,c_i})! }=\dfrac{z_{\mu}}{z_{\mu^1} \cdots z_{\mu^k}}.
\end{equation}

From eq.(\ref{rewriting1}) and eq.(\ref{restriction}), we deduce $$\sum_{\substack{(\mu,a) \in A_n\\ f(\mu,a)=\lambda}}H_{\mu}z_{\mu}^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{l(\mu)}p_{\mu_i}[G_{a_i}]=m_{l(\lambda)}\left(\operatorname{Res}_{\Lambda(|\operatorname{supp}(\lambda)|)}^{\Lambda_{l(\lambda)}}(H_{l(\lambda)})[\prod_{j \in \operatorname{supp}(\lambda)} G_j]\right)$$ and thus the Theorem.

    \end{align*}
    where in the second equality we used \Cref{lemma:sum_of_signs_vanishes} and the only term that does not vanish is associated to $\min S = \emptyset$ that implies $S= \emptyset$.
