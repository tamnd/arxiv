<!-- conjecture-1: body_md -->
\label{conjecture}
Let \(K\) be a number field, and \(C/K\) be a nice curve of genus at least \(2\). Fix an integer $n\geq 2$. Then
\(
C(L)_{\mathrm{new}}=\varnothing
\)
for \(100\%\) of \(L/K\) of degree $n$, ordered by their absolute
discriminants.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:Deric}
    Let $K$ be a number field, \(C/K\) a smooth projective geometrically integral curve, and
let \(n\geq 2\). Suppose that \(C\) has infinitely many primitive
closed points of degree \(n\) and that
\[
g(C)>(2n-1)^2.
\]
Then there is a smooth projective geometrically integral curve \(Y/K\)
with \(Y(K)\) infinite, and an indecomposable morphism
\(\psi:C\to Y\) of degree \(n\), such that all but finitely many
primitive degree \(n\) points of \(C\) are of the form
\(\psi^*(y)\) for some \(y\in Y(K)\).
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $n\geq2$, and let $\psi:C\to E$ be a finite morphism of degree
$n$ from a smooth projective absolutely irreducible curve $C/\mathbb{Q}$ of
genus $g$ to an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$. Suppose that
$g>n^2+1$
and either $B_\psi=\varnothing$, or the action of
$\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ on $B_\psi(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ contains a derangement. For $X\geq2$, let
\[
\mathcal D_{C,n}(X)
=
\left\{
|\Delta_L|\leq X:
[L:\mathbb{Q}]=n,\ L/\mathbb{Q}\text{ primitive}, 
C(L)_{\mathrm{new}}\neq\varnothing\right\}.
\]
Then the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $B_\psi=\varnothing$, then
$\#\mathcal D_{C,n}(X)\ll_\psi X^{1/2}$.
\item If $B_\psi\neq\varnothing$, let $L_\psi/\mathbb{Q}$ be the finite
Galois extension cut out by the action of $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ on
$B_\psi(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$. Write
$
G_\psi=\operatorname{Gal}(L_\psi/\mathbb{Q})$
and
$$
 \alpha_\psi
 :=
 \frac{
 \#\{\sigma\in G_\psi:B_\psi(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^\sigma=\varnothing\}
 }{\#G_\psi}.$$
Then $0<\alpha_\psi<1$ and
 $\#\mathcal D_{C,n}(X)
 \ll_\psi
 \frac{X}{(\log X)^{\alpha_\psi}}$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:field density}
Under the hypotheses and notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the
following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $n\in\{2,3,5\}$, the degree $n$ fields $L/\mathbb{Q}$ for
which
$C(L)_{\mathrm{new}}\neq\varnothing$
have density $0$ among all degree $n$ fields ordered by absolute
discriminant.

\item If $n=4$, the primitive quartic fields $L/\mathbb{Q}$ for which
$C(L)_{\mathrm{new}}\neq~\varnothing$
have density $0$ both among all quartic fields and among the primitive
quartic fields, in each case ordered by absolute discriminant.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:disc}
On an open set on which $\mathcal A$ has basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$, form
the determinant
\[
 \det\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal A/\mathcal{O}_E}(e_i e_j)\bigr)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}.
\]
A change of basis multiplies this determinant by the square of a unit,
so these local generators define a locally principal ideal sheaf
$\mathfrak{d}_\psi\subset\mathcal{O}_E$, called the \emph{discriminant ideal} of
$\psi$. Let $\eta$ be the generic point of $E$. The trace pairing
on $\mathcal A_\eta=k(C)$ is the field trace pairing for
$k(C)/k(E)$. Since this extension is separable, the pairing is
nondegenerate, so its determinant is nonzero. Thus
$(\mathfrak{d}_\psi)_\eta\neq0$. As $E$ is integral, the local generators of
$\mathfrak{d}_\psi$ are non-zero divisors. Hence $\mathfrak{d}_\psi$ is an invertible
ideal sheaf and determines an effective divisor
\begin{equation} \label{def:disc-divisor}
 D_\psi
 =
 \sum_{b\in E^{(1)}}m_b[b],
 \qquad
 m_b=\operatorname{ord}_b(\mathfrak{d}_\psi),
\end{equation}
where $E^{(1)}$ denotes the set of closed points of $E$. We call
$D_\psi$ the \emph{discriminant divisor} of $\psi$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:disc-ramification}
For every $b\in E(\bar k)$, one has
\[
 m_b
 =
 \sum_{\substack{P\in C(\bar k)\\\psi(P)=b}}(e_P-1).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \deg D_\psi
 =
 2g(C)-2-n\bigl(2g(E)-2\bigr).
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathfrak{D}_\psi$ denote the different divisor of $\psi$; see
\cite[\S7.4.2, pp.~289--291]{Liu}. Since $\operatorname{char}(k)=0$,
the cover is tamely ramified and the coefficient of $\mathfrak{D}_\psi$ at a
point $P$ is $e_P-1$. The local discriminant--different formula,
applied over each closed point $b\in E$ to the finite extension of
$\mathcal O_{E,b}$ to the semilocal Dedekind domain
$(\psi_*\mathcal O_C)_b$, gives
\[
 D_\psi=\psi_*\mathfrak{D}_\psi;
\]
see \cite[Chapter~III, Section~2]{Neu} and
\cite[Tags~0BVH and~0C17]{Stacks}. Applying the same identity after base
change to $\bar k$, where all residue degrees are $1$, yields the
formula for $m_b$. Taking degrees and applying the Riemann--Hurwitz
formula gives the final assertion.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:odd}
The \emph{odd locus} of the discriminant divisor is the reduced closed
subscheme
\[
 B_\psi
 :=
 \coprod_{\substack{b\in E^{(1)}\\m_b\text{ odd}}}
 \operatorname{Spec} k(b)
 \hookrightarrow E.
\]
Since $E$ is regular of dimension $1$, the reduced closed subscheme
$B_\psi$ determines the reduced effective divisor
\[
 \sum_{\substack{b\in E^{(1)}\\m_b\text{ odd}}}[b].
\]
By abuse of notation, we also write $B_\psi$ for this divisor.
We refer to either of them as the \emph{odd part} of $D_\psi$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:connected-odd-locus}
Suppose that $E$ is an elliptic curve and that $B_\psi$ is nonempty
and connected over $k$. Since $B_\psi$ is finite and reduced, this
means that it consists of a single closed point of $E$, not necessarily
a $k$-rational point. Equivalently, the action of $G_k$ on
$B_\psi(\bar k)$ is transitive. Moreover, $\deg B_\psi$ is even and
hence at least $2$. Jordan's Theorem therefore shows that this action
contains a derangement (see \cite{Ser03}). In
particular, the Galois-theoretic hypothesis in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is
automatic in this case.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:KS-dictionary}
In the notation above, take $k=\mathbb{Q}$ and $E=\mathbb{P}^1$, so that $\psi:C\to\mathbb{P}^1$. Choose an affine coordinate $T$ on
$\mathbb{P}^1$, with corresponding point at infinity $\infty$. Let
$Y\in\mathbb{Q}(C)$ satisfy $\mathbb{Q}(C)=\mathbb{Q}(T)(Y)$, and choose
$F(T,Y)\in\mathbb{Q}[T,Y]$, irreducible of degree $n$ in $Y$,
such that $F(T,Y)=0$ in $\mathbb{Q}(C)$. Write
\[
 \Delta_Y(F)=g(T)h(T)^2,
\]
where $g,h\in\mathbb{Q}[T]$ and $g$ is squarefree. Write $\operatorname{Gal}(g)$ for
the Galois group of the splitting field of $g$, acting on its roots. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the finite geometric points of $B_\psi$ are exactly the roots
of $g$;
\item $\infty\in B_\psi(\mathbb{Q})$ if and only if $\deg g$ is odd;
\item the conditions that $\deg g$ be even and that the action of
$\operatorname{Gal}(g)$ on the roots of $g$ contain a derangement are equivalent
to the condition that the action of $G_\mathbb{Q}$ on
$B_\psi(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ contain a derangement.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $a_n(T)$ be the leading coefficient of $F$ as a polynomial in
$Y$. Over $K=\mathbb{Q}(T)$, the monic minimal polynomial of $Y$ is
$a_n^{-1}F$. Consequently, the discriminant of the $K$-basis
$\boldsymbol\beta=(1,Y,\ldots,Y^{n-1})$ is
\[
 \operatorname{disc}(\boldsymbol\beta)
 =
 a_n(T)^{-2n+2}\Delta_Y(F).
\]

Fix a place $b$ of $\mathbb{P}^1$, possibly $b=\infty$, and let
$A_b=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1,b}$. The finite flat $A_b$-algebra
$(\psi_*\mathcal{O}_C)_b$ has an $A_b$-basis
$\mathbf e=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$. After tensoring with $K$, this is a
$K$-basis of $\mathbb{Q}(C)$. Thus, although $\boldsymbol\beta$ need not be
an $A_b$-basis, there is some $M\in\mathrm{GL}_n(K)$ such that
$\boldsymbol\beta=\mathbf eM$. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{disc}(\boldsymbol\beta)
 =
 \det(M)^2\operatorname{disc}(\mathbf e).
\]
The valuation of $\operatorname{disc}(\mathbf e)$ is the coefficient
$m_b$ of the discriminant divisor. Since both the exponent $2n-2$ of
$a_n$ and the exponent of $\det(M)$ are even, we obtain
\[
 m_b\equiv\operatorname{ord}_b\Delta_Y(F)\pmod2.
\]

As $\Delta_Y(F)=gh^2$ and $g$ is squarefree, its valuation at a
finite geometric point $\alpha$ is odd precisely when $g(\alpha)=0$,
proving (1). At infinity,
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_\infty\Delta_Y(F)
 \equiv
 \operatorname{ord}_\infty g
 \equiv
 -\deg g
 \pmod2,
\]
which proves (2).

If $\infty\in B_\psi(\mathbb{Q})$, then it is fixed by every Galois element.
If $\infty\notin B_\psi(\mathbb{Q})$, then the Galois set
$B_\psi(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ is exactly the root set of $g$. This proves (3).
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:double-cover}
Assume that $\deg\psi=2$. There
is a class
$\delta\in k(E)^\times/k(E)^{\times2}$
such that
$k(C)=k(E)(\sqrt\delta)$.
Choose a representative and write
\[
 \operatorname{div}(\delta)=\sum_Q m_Q[Q].
\]
At a closed point $Q$, choose a uniformiser $t$ and write
$\delta=ut^{m_Q}$, with $u$ a unit. The quadratic extension is
ramified at $Q$ if and only if $m_Q$ is odd. Since the ramification is
tame, Lemma~\ref{lem:disc-ramification} gives
$$
 D_\psi
 =
 B_\psi
 =
 \sum\limits_{m_Q\text{ odd}}[Q].$$
Thus, for a double cover, the odd part of the discriminant divisor is
precisely the branch divisor. This description is unchanged when
$\delta$ is multiplied by a square; in particular, odd poles as well as
odd zeros must be included.

For $R\in E(k)\setminus\operatorname{supp}(\operatorname{div}(\delta))$, the fibre is
\[
 C_R\simeq\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(k[w]/(w^2-\delta(R))\bigr).
\]
It is split when $\delta(R)$ is a square in $k$, and otherwise is the
spectrum of the quadratic field $k(\sqrt{\delta(R)})$.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:galois-odd}
Suppose that $\psi$ is geometrically Galois of odd degree $n$, for
instance a Kummer cover $w^\ell=f$ of odd prime degree $\ell$. Above a
point $b\in E(\bar k)$ the Galois group permutes the fibre transitively,
so all points of the fibre have the same ramification index $e$, which
divides $n$ and is therefore odd. Hence
$m_b=(n/e)(e-1)$ is even for every $b$, and $B_\psi=\varnothing$.
Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(1) applies to every geometrically Galois cover
of odd degree $n$ with $g(C)>n^2+1$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:avoid}
Let $N\geq1$, let $U=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$ and 
\(
 \mathcal B\to U
\)
be a nonempty finite \'etale scheme, and write $B=\mathcal B_\mathbb{Q}$ for
its generic fibre and $\mathcal B_p$ for the special fibre at a prime $p\nmid N$. 

Let $L/\mathbb{Q}$ be the finite Galois extension cut out by
the action of $G_\mathbb{Q}$ on $B(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$, and write
$G_B=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})$. Suppose that the action of $G_B$ on $B(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ contains a
derangement. Then
\[
 \alpha_B
 :=
 \frac{\#\{\sigma\in G_B:B(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^\sigma=\varnothing\}}{\#G_B}
\]
lies in $(0,1)$, and there is a Frobenian, hence regular, set
$\mathcal P_B$ of primes $p\nmid N$, of density $\alpha_B$, such
that
\(
 \mathcal B_p(\mathbb{F}_p)=\varnothing
\)
for every $p\in\mathcal P_B$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since
$\mathcal B\to U$ is finite \'etale, the action of $G_\mathbb{Q}$ on
$B(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ factors through the
\'etale fundamental group of $U$. In particular, $L/\mathbb{Q}$ is unramified
at every prime $p\nmid N$.
Let
\[
 \mathcal D_B
 :=
 \{\sigma\in G_B:B(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^\sigma=\varnothing\}.
\]
By hypothesis, $\mathcal D_B$ is nonempty. It is stable under
conjugation, since
\[
 B(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^{\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}}
 =
 \tau\bigl(B(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^\sigma\bigr)
\]
for all $\sigma,\tau\in G_B$. Moreover, since $\mathcal B\to U$ is
finite \'etale and $U$ is connected, the rank of $\mathcal B$ over
$U$ is constant, so the generic fibre $B$ is nonempty. Hence the
identity does not belong to $\mathcal D_B$, and
$0<\alpha_B<1$.

Let $\mathcal P_B$ be the set of primes $p\nmid N$ whose arithmetic
Frobenius conjugacy class in $G_B$ is contained in $\mathcal D_B$. By
Chebotar{\"e}v's Density Theorem~\cite[p.~34]{Chebotarev}, it has Frobenian (and hence regular) density
\(\frac{\#\mathcal D_B}{\#G_B}=\alpha_B.\)

For $p\nmid N$, choose a place of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ above $p$. Since
$\mathcal B\to U$ is finite \'etale, reduction at this place identifies
$B(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ with $\mathcal B_p(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$. Under this
identification, arithmetic Frobenius on the special fibre corresponds,
up to conjugacy, to a Frobenius element, $\operatorname{Frob}_p\in G_B$.
For $p\in\mathcal P_B$, the corresponding element $\operatorname{Frob}_p$ is a derangement. Since the
rational points of the special fibre are precisely its geometric points
fixed by arithmetic Frobenius,
\[
 \mathcal B_p(\mathbb{F}_p)
 =

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:spread}
There is a finite set of primes $\Sigma$ such that, for
$U=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}[1/\Sigma]$, there are smooth proper models $\mathcal C/U$
and $\mathcal E/U$ with geometrically integral fibres and generic fibres
$C$ and $E$, respectively, and a finite locally free morphism
\[
 \Psi:\mathcal C\to\mathcal E
\]
of degree $n$ extending $\psi$. Its discriminant ideal
defines an effective Cartier divisor $D_\Psi$ on $\mathcal E$ with
generic fibre $D_\psi$. Let $\mathcal Z_j$ be the reduced closure in
$\mathcal E$ of the closed point $b_j$. After enlarging $\Sigma$,
\[
 D_\Psi=\sum_jm_j\mathcal Z_j,
\]
so that $D_\Psi$ has no vertical components, each $\mathcal Z_j\to U$
is finite \'etale, and the $\mathcal Z_j$ are pairwise disjoint. In
particular,
\[
 \mathcal B_\psi
 :=
 \coprod_{\substack{j\\m_j\text{ odd}}}\mathcal Z_j
\]
is a closed subscheme of $\mathcal E$ that is finite \'etale over
$U$, with generic fibre $B_\psi$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
By the spreading out theorem \cite[Theorem~3.2.1]{Poo}, after replacing $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$ by a nonempty open subscheme $U=\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[1/\Sigma],$ the curves $C$ and $E$ admit models $\mathcal C$ and $\mathcal E$ over $U$ by part~(i), which may be taken smooth, proper with geometrically integral fibres by part~(ii). By parts~(iii) and~(iv), respectively, $\psi$ extends to a morphism \[ \Psi:\mathcal C\longrightarrow\mathcal E \] which may be taken finite and flat. Hence $\Psi$ is finite locally free of degree $n$: its degree is locally constant on the integral scheme $\mathcal E$, and its generic fibre has degree $n$. Note that as $U $ is open in $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$, it follows that $\Sigma$ is a finite set of primes.

The local discriminant determinants differ on overlaps by squares of
units, so they define a locally principal ideal sheaf
$\mathfrak d_\Psi$. The trace pairing commutes with base change, so
its restriction to the generic fibre is
$\mathfrak d_\psi\neq0$. Since $\mathcal E$ is integral, the local
generators are non-zero divisors. Thus $\mathfrak d_\Psi$ is
invertible and defines an effective Cartier divisor $D_\Psi$, whose
generic fibre is $D_\psi$.
Thus, we may write \[ D_\Psi=\sum_j m_j\mathcal Z_j+V, \] where $V$ is supported on finitely many vertical components. After enlarging $\Sigma$ by the finitely many primes supporting $V$, we therefore have $D_\Psi=\sum_j m_j\mathcal Z_j$.

For each $j$, the generic fibre of $\mathcal Z_j\to U$ is $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Q}(b_j)\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Q}$, which is finite \'etale. By \cite[Theorem~3.2.1(iv)]{Poo}, after shrinking $U$ further, each $\mathcal Z_j\to U$ is finite \'etale. Moreover, for $i\neq j$, the intersection $\mathcal Z_i\cap\mathcal Z_j$ has empty generic fibre. Since it is proper over $U$, its image in $U$ is closed and does not contain the generic point, hence is finite. Removing these images makes the $\mathcal Z_j$ pairwise disjoint. Since only finitely many components occur, one common shrinking gives all the assertions.

With the notation above, suppose that there exists
$P\in C(\overline{\mathbb Q})$ such that $\psi(P)=R$ and

Since $[\mathbb Q(P):\mathbb Q]=n$, this homomorphism

$C_R\simeq\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q(P)$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:bc}
Let $\Psi:\mathcal C\to\mathcal E$ be finite locally free, and let
$\mathcal A:=\Psi_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal C},$
with discriminant ideal $\mathfrak d_\Psi\subset\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}$. For a section $s:U\to\mathcal E$, let
$\mathcal A_s:=s^*\mathcal A$. Then
\[
 \mathfrak d(\mathcal A_s/\mathcal O_U)
 =
 s^{-1}\mathfrak d_\Psi\cdot\mathcal O_U.
\]
If the generic fibre of $\mathcal A_s$ is \'etale, then the ideal on the
right is nonzero and
\[
 \operatorname{div}_U\bigl(\mathfrak d(\mathcal A_s/\mathcal O_U)\bigr)
 =
 s^*D_\Psi.
\]
In particular, for $p\notin\Sigma$,
\[
 v_p\bigl(\mathfrak d(\mathcal A_s/\mathcal O_U)\bigr)
 =
 i_{s(p)}\bigl(s(U),D_\Psi\bigr),
\]
where $i_{s(p)}$ denotes the local intersection multiplicity at
$s(p)\in\mathcal E$; see~\cite[Definition~9.1.1]{Liu}.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The assertion is local on $\mathcal E$ and $U$. Work on an affine
open over which $\mathcal A$ is free with basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$.
After base change, the elements $e_1\otimes1,\ldots,e_n\otimes1$ form a
basis of $\mathcal A_s$. Trace commutes with base change, so the trace
matrix
\[
 \bigl(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal A/\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}}(e_i e_j)\bigr)_{i,j}
\]
is carried to the trace matrix of $\mathcal A_s$. The same is therefore
true of its determinant, proving the identity of discriminant ideals.

If the generic fibre of $\mathcal A_s$ is \'etale, its discriminant is
nonzero. Hence $s^{-1}\mathfrak d_\Psi\cdot\mathcal O_U$ is nonzero,
so the pullback $s^*D_\Psi$ is defined. Since the ideal sheaf of
$s^*D_\Psi$ is $s^{-1}\mathfrak d_\Psi\cdot\mathcal O_U$, the first
assertion gives
\[
\operatorname{div}_U\bigl(\mathfrak d(\mathcal A_s/\mathcal O_U)\bigr)
=s^*D_\Psi.
\]

For $p\notin\Sigma$, let $f$ be a local equation for $D_\Psi$ at
$s(p)$. Let $s^\#: s^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal E}\to \mathcal O_U$ be the morphism induced by $s$.
Then $s^\#(f)$ is a local equation for $s^*D_\Psi$ at
$p$, and hence
$
\operatorname{ord}_p(s^*D_\Psi)
=v_p\bigl(s^\#(f)\bigr).
$
Let $x=s(p)$. Since $D_\Psi$ is an effective Cartier divisor with
local equation $f$ at $x$, we have
\[
\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}(-D_\Psi)_x=(f)
\subseteq \mathcal O_{\mathcal E,x}.
\]
Moreover,
$\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}(-s(U))_x$ is the ideal of the section
$s(U)$ at $x$. Hence, by the definition of local intersection
multiplicity \cite[Definition~9.1.1]{Liu},
\[
i_x(s(U),D_\Psi)
=
\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{\mathcal E,x}}
\frac{\mathcal O_{\mathcal E,x}}
{\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}(-s(U))_x+(f)}.
\]
Since $s:U\to s(U)$ is an isomorphism, we have
$
{\mathcal O_{\mathcal E,x}}/
{\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}(-s(U))_x}
\simeq \mathcal O_{U,p},
$
and under this identification the image of $f$ is $s^\#(f)$.
Therefore
\[
i_{s(p)}(s(U),D_\Psi)
=
\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{U,p}}
\bigl(\mathcal O_{U,p}/(s^\#f)\bigr)
=
v_p(s^\#f).
\]
Thus
$
v_p\bigl(\mathfrak d(\mathcal A_s/\mathcal O_U)\bigr)
=i_{s(p)}\bigl(s(U),D_\Psi\bigr).
$

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:parity}
Let $\psi:C\to E$ satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
There exist a finite set $\Sigma$ of rational primes and a finite set
$\mathcal F_{\mathrm{exc}}$ of $\mathbb{Q}$-isomorphism classes of degree $n$
fields with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $B_\psi=\varnothing$, then every primitive degree $n$ field
$L/\mathbb{Q}$ whose isomorphism class does not belong to
$\mathcal F_{\mathrm{exc}}$, and for which
$C(L)_{\mathrm{new}}\neq\varnothing$, satisfies
\[
 v_p(\Delta_L)\equiv0\pmod2
 \qquad\text{for every }p\notin\Sigma.
\]
\item If $B_\psi\neq\varnothing$, let $L_\psi/\mathbb{Q}$ be the finite
Galois extension cut out by the action of $G_\mathbb{Q}$ on
$B_\psi(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$. Write
$G_\psi=\operatorname{Gal}(L_\psi/\mathbb{Q})$ and
\[
 \alpha_\psi
 :=
 \frac{\#\{\sigma\in G_\psi:B_\psi(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^\sigma=\varnothing\}}
      {\#G_\psi}.
\]
Then $0<\alpha_\psi<1$, and there is a regular set
$\mathcal S_0\subseteq\mathcal P_{\mathrm{all}}\setminus\Sigma$ of
density $\alpha_\psi$ such that every primitive degree $n$ field
$L/\mathbb{Q}$ whose isomorphism class does not belong to
$\mathcal F_{\mathrm{exc}}$, and for which
$C(L)_{\mathrm{new}}\neq\varnothing$, satisfies
\[
 v_p(\Delta_L)\equiv0\pmod2
 \qquad\text{for every }p\in\mathcal S_0.
\]
\end{enumerate}
The same conclusions hold, with the same proof, for a finite morphism $\psi:C\to Y$ of degree $n$ to a smooth projective geometrically integral curve $Y/\mathbb{Q}$ of genus $g_0$, provided that the hypothesis on $B_\psi$ is kept and the genus hypothesis is replaced by $g(C)-1>n(n+g_0-1)$.

Consider the set

We start by applying Theorem~\ref{thm:ST} with $g_0=1$, $\nu=n$, and

$\Xi_n(\psi)$ is finite. Let $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{exc}}$ be the

Lemma~\ref{lem:avoid}, with $N=\prod_{p\in\Sigma}p$, to the finite

 \end{cases}

$\mathbb{Q}(R)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(P)=L$ and $L/\mathbb{Q}$ is primitive, either
$\mathbb{Q}(R)=\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{Q}(R)=L$. The second possibility would imply

 A_R

$
 D_\Psi=\sum_i m_i\mathcal Z_i

$$
 \mathfrak d(A_R)

 v_p\bigl(\mathfrak d(A_R)\bigr)

 s_R(p)

\label{rem:general-target}
The proof uses only the fact that $E$ is a smooth projective geometrically
integral curve and its genus. More generally, let
$\psi:C\to Y$ be a finite morphism of degree $n$ to a curve of genus
$g_0$, and assume that either $B_\psi=\varnothing$, or that the Galois action on $B_\psi(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ contains a derangement. The conclusions of Theorems~\ref{thm:main}
and~\ref{thm:field density} hold, with the same definition of the odd locus, under the
numerical hypothesis
\[
 g(C)-1>n(n+g_0-1).
\]
For $Y=\mathbb{P}^1$, this becomes $g(C)>n^2-n+1$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:KS-dictionary} recovers the projective line
condition of \cite[Theorem~9]{KS}. For $g_0=1$, it is precisely the
hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
The same bound occurs in Castelnuovo--Severi: if $g(C)>n^2+1$, then
any two degree $n$ maps from $C$ to elliptic curves factor through a
common map of degree greater than $1$ \cite[Theorem~3.11.3]{Sti}; for
prime $n$ they therefore differ by an isomorphism of their targets.
This observation is not used in the proofs.
If $g(Y)\geq 2$, then $Y(\mathbb Q)$ is finite by Faltings'
Theorem. Assuming that $\Xi_n(\psi)$ is finite, all but finitely many primitive degree $n$ points of $C$ lie above $Y(\mathbb{Q})$. Since $\psi$ is finite, there are therefore only
finitely many such points, making our density question vacuous in this
case.

\label{lem:qqext-local-masses}
For $p>3$, one has
\[
 M_4(p)=1+p^{-1}+2p^{-2}+p^{-3},
\]
and the contribution from the algebras having even discriminant
exponent is
\[
 M_4^{\mathrm{even}}(p)=1+2p^{-2}.
\]
For $p>5$, one has
\[
 M_5(p)=1+p^{-1}+2p^{-2}+2p^{-3}+p^{-4},
\]
and the contribution from the algebras having even discriminant
exponent is
\[
 M_5^{\mathrm{even}}(p)=1+2p^{-2}+p^{-4}.
\]

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
In each case $p>n$, so every algebra under consideration is tame.
Let $e(\tau):=n-\#\{\text{cycles of }\tau\}$, be the index of $\tau$ (where we count fixed points
as cycles). By \eqref{eq:discA}, this is its discriminant exponent. We now apply
Kedlaya's tame mass formula
\cite[Proposition~5.3 and Corollary~5.4]{Kedlaya} to $S_n$. It
assigns the mass
$p^{-e(\tau)}$ to each conjugacy class for which $\tau^p$ is
conjugate to $\tau$. Every class has this property, since all cycle
lengths are at most $n<p$.

A direct way to see this is as follows. There are $n!/\#C_{S_n}(\tau)$ choices for an
inertia element in its conjugacy class and, for each choice,
$\#C_{S_n}(\tau)$ Frobenius elements satisfying
$\phi\tau\phi^{-1}=\tau^p$. Dividing the number of labelled pairs
by $n!$ is equivalent, by the orbit-stabiliser theorem, to weighting each
isomorphism class by the reciprocal of its automorphism group.
Thus the contribution is exactly $p^{-e(\tau)}$.

The cycle types and their indices are given in the following tables.
\[
\begin{array}{c@{\qquad}ccccc}
\toprule
n=4&1^4&2\,1^2&3\,1&2^2&4\\
\midrule
e(\tau)&0&1&2&2&3\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
\[
\begin{array}{c@{\qquad}ccccccc}
\toprule
n=5&1^5&2\,1^3&2^2\,1&3\,1^2&4\,1&3\,2&5\\
\midrule
e(\tau)&0&1&2&2&3&3&4\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
Summing up over all conjugacy classes gives the values for $M_n(p)$, while summing over the even $e(\tau)$ gives $M_n^{\mathrm{even}}(p)$.

\label{thm:qqext-quintic-parity}

density.  Then
\[A_{5,\mathcal S}(X):=
 \#\left\{L/\mathbb{Q}:
 \begin{array}{l}
 [L:\mathbb{Q}]=5,\quad |\Delta_L|\leq X,\\
 v_p(\Delta_L)\equiv0\pmod2

 \end{array}

\mathcal{E}_p
=
\left\{
A/\mathbb{Q}_p \text{ \'etale of degree } 5 :
v_p\bigl(\operatorname{Disc}(A/\mathbb{Q}_p)\bigr)
\equiv 0 \pmod{2}
\right\}.

Because $p>5$, every such algebra is tamely ramified, and thus whether the exponent is even depends only on the splitting type
$\bigl((e_j,f_j)\bigr)_j$ by \eqref{eq:discA}. Let $\mathcal{T}_p^{\mathrm{even}}$ denote the set of splitting types giving $v_p(\operatorname{Disc} (A/\mathbb{Q}_p))$ even. Hence

\mathcal E_p
=

\Sigma_p(\lambda),

 \boldsymbol\lambda=(\lambda_p)_{p\in T}
 \in\prod_{p\in T}\mathcal T_p^{\mathrm{even}},

and let
\[
 N(X;\boldsymbol\lambda)
 :=\#\left\{L/\mathbb{Q}:
 \begin{array}{l}
 [L:\mathbb{Q}]=5,\quad |\Delta_L|\leq X,\\
 L\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_p\in\Sigma_p(\lambda_p)
       \text{ for every }p\in T
 \end{array}
 \right\}.

 c_p(\lambda_p)

 N_{5,T}(X)

 \begin{array}{l}
 [L:\mathbb{Q}]=5,\quad |\Delta_L|\leq X,\\
 v_p(\Delta_L)\equiv0\pmod2
       \text{ for every }p\in T
 \end{array}
 \right\}.

therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qqext-quintic-fixed}

\end{equation}

$
 A_{5,\mathcal S}(X)\leq N_{5,T}(X),

\[

\label{thm:qqext-quartic-parity}
Let $\mathcal S$ be a regular set of rational primes of positive

\[A_{4,\mathcal S}(X):=

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
As above, we may delete the primes $p\leq 4$ from $\mathcal S$.
Let $T\subseteq\mathcal S$ be finite.  At every $p\in T$, prescribe
that the degree $4$ \'etale $\mathbb{Q}_p$-algebra have even discriminant
exponent, and impose no local condition elsewhere.  This is an
acceptable collection of local specifications in the sense of
\cite[Theorem~2]{BSWglobal}. 

This theorem counts $S_4$-quartic
fields, rather than arbitrary quartic orders or all quartic fields.
The fields are weighted by
$\#\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}}(L)^{-1}$, which is $1$ for an
$S_4$-quartic (see \cite[p.~2]{BSWglobal}). Denote by
\[
N_{G,T}(X)
:=
\#\left\{
L/\mathbb{Q}:
\begin{array}{l}
[L:\mathbb{Q}]=4,\quad |\Delta_L|\leq X,
\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{L}/\mathbb{Q})\simeq G,\\
v_p(\Delta_L)\equiv0\pmod{2}\text{ for every }p\in T
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]
Theorem~2 in \cite{BSWglobal} and Lemma~\ref{lem:qqext-local-masses} then give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qqext-quartic-fixed}
 N_{S_4,T}(X)
 =\left(c_{S_4}\prod_{p\in T}q_4(p)+o_T(1)\right)X,
\end{equation}
where $c_{S_4}>0$ is the leading constant for unrestricted
$S_4$-quartic fields.
The unrestricted and restricted finite local factors are
$(1-p^{-1})M_4(p)$ and
$(1-p^{-1})M_4^{\mathrm{even}}(p)$, respectively. Their ratio is
\[
 \frac{(1-p^{-1})M_4^{\mathrm{even}}(p)}
      {(1-p^{-1})M_4(p)}
 =q_4(p).
\]

A quartic field is primitive (i.e. it has no nontrivial
proper subfield) if and only if the Galois group of its normal closure
is $S_4$ or $A_4$.  Indeed, by classical group theory, the transitive subgroups of $S_4$ are
$S_4,A_4,D_4,V_4$, and $C_4$, and only $S_4$ and $A_4$ act
primitively in their natural degree $4$ actions. By
\cite[Theorem~1.4]{BSTTTZ}, we get $N_{A_4}(X):=N_{A_4,\emptyset}(X)=o(X)$. 

For every fixed finite
$T\subseteq\mathcal S$, we have
$
 A_{4,\mathcal S}(X)
 \leq N_{S_4,T}(X)+N_{A_4}(X).
$
It follows from \eqref{eq:qqext-quartic-fixed} that
\[
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{A_{4,\mathcal S}(X)}X
 \leq c_{S_4}\prod_{p\in T}q_4(p).
\]
As before, Lemma~\ref{lem:q4q5} gives
$A_{4,\mathcal S}(X)=o(X)$.

With no local conditions, \cite[Theorem~2]{BSWglobal} gives
$N_{S_4,\varnothing}(X)\sim c_{S_4}X$, where $c_{S_4}>0$.
Both the family of all quartic fields and the family of primitive
quartic fields contain these fields. Since
$A_{4,\mathcal S}(X)=o(X)$, both asserted density statements follow.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:fixed-points}
Let $N>4$ and let $\operatorname{Fix}(w_N)$ denote the fixed locus of $w_N$
on $X_0(N)$. 
Then $(E,\ker \alpha)\mapsto j(E)$ is a $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$-equivariant bijection
\[
  \operatorname{Fix}(w_N)(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\;\to\;
  \begin{cases}
    S_{-4N}, & N\not\equiv 3\pmod 4,\\[2pt]
    S_{-N}\sqcup S_{-4N}, & N\equiv 3\pmod 4 .
  \end{cases}
\]
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
By \cite[Proposition~3 and p.~454]{Ogg}, $w_N$ fixes no cusp for
$N>4$, and its noncuspidal fixed points are precisely the pairs
$
(E,\ker\alpha),
$
where $\alpha\in\operatorname{\text{End}}_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(E)$ has cyclic kernel of
order $N$ and satisfies
$
\alpha^2=[-N].
$

Let $\mathcal O=\operatorname{\text{End}}_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(E)$ and let
$
m=[\mathcal O:\mathbb Z[\alpha]].
$
Since $\ker\alpha$ is cyclic, $\alpha$ is primitive in $\mathcal O$:
otherwise, if $\alpha=r\gamma$ with $r>1$, then
$E[r]\subseteq\ker\alpha$, contradicting cyclicity. Choose an integral
basis $1,\omega$ of $\mathcal O$ and write
\[
\alpha=a+m\omega.
\]
The primitivity of $\alpha$ gives $\gcd(a,m)=1$, while
$\operatorname{\text{Tr}}(\alpha)=0$ gives
\[
2a+m\operatorname{\text{Tr}}(\omega)=0.
\]
Hence $m\mid 2$. Since
$
\Delta(\mathbb Z[\alpha])=-4N
       =m^2\Delta(\mathcal O),
$
we obtain
\[
\Delta(\mathcal O)=
\begin{cases}
-4N,&m=1,\\
-N,&m=2.
\end{cases}
\]
The second case can occur only when $N\equiv3\pmod4$: indeed, if
$m=2$, then $\gcd(a,2)=1$, so the trace of $\omega$ is odd and
$\Delta(\mathcal O)\equiv1\pmod4$.

Conversely, if $\mathcal O=\mathcal O_{-4N}$, or if
$N\equiv3\pmod4$ and $\mathcal O=\mathcal O_{-N}$, then
$\sqrt{-N}\in\mathcal O$ has norm $N$ and cyclic kernel, and the
corresponding point of $X_0(N)$ is fixed by $w_N$. Moreover, the
elements of $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-N})$ having trace $0$ and norm $N$
are exactly $\pm\sqrt{-N}$, which have the same kernel. Thus $\ker \alpha$ is
uniquely determined by $E$, and $(E,\ker \alpha)\mapsto j(E)$ gives the stated
bijection.

Finally, this map is defined over $\mathbb Q$, since $j(E^\sigma)=j(E)^\sigma$  for all $\sigma\in G_{\mathbb Q}$. Therefore, the bijection is $G_{\mathbb Q}$-equivariant.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:AL-derangement}
Let $N>4$ and let
$\psi_N\colon X_0(N)\to X_0^{+}(N):=X_0(N)/\langle w_N\rangle$ be the
quotient by the Atkin--Lehner involution.  Let $B_N:=B_{\psi_N}$ be the odd
part of the discriminant divisor of $\psi_N$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item if $N\not\equiv 3\pmod 4$, the action of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ on
        $B_N(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ contains a derangement if and only if
        $h(-4N)>1$;
  \item if $N\equiv 3\pmod 4$, the action of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ on
        $B_N(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ contains a derangement if and only if
        $h(-N)>1$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Since $\psi_N$ is the quotient by an involution in characteristic zero,
it is ramified precisely at the fixed points of $w_N$, each with ramification index $2$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:disc-ramification} each branch point occurs with coefficient $1$ in the discriminant divisor.  Consequently $\psi_N$ induces a
$G_{\mathbb{Q}}$-equivariant bijection
$\operatorname{Fix}(w_N)(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\to B_N(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$,
and Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-points} therefore identifies $B_N(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ with
$S_{-4N}$, respectively $S_{-N}\sqcup S_{-4N}$, as $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$-sets.
 
 By the theory of complex multiplication (e.g. \cite[Corollary 10.20]{Cox}), we get that
$\#S_D=h(D)$. Moreover, the field generated by the
elements of $S_D$ over $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-N})$ is the ring class field $H_D$, and
$\operatorname{Gal}(H_D/K)\simeq\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{O}_D)$ acts
simply transitively on $S_D$; see for example \cite[Corollary 11.37]{Cox}.  In particular the
action of $G_K$ on $S_D$ factors through $\operatorname{Gal}(H_D/K)$, and
{every} non-trivial element of $\operatorname{Gal}(H_D/K)$ acts on
$S_D$ without fixed points.
 
Suppose first that $N\not\equiv 3\pmod4$, so
$B_N(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq S_{-4N}$.  If $h(-4N)=1$, this is a
$G_{\mathbb{Q}}$-stable singleton, so no derangement exists.  If
$h(-4N)>1$, choose $\tau\ne 1$ in $\operatorname{Gal}(H_{-4N}/K)$ and lift it
to $\tilde\tau\in G_K$; then $\tilde\tau$ is a
derangement.
 
Suppose now that $N\equiv 3\pmod 4$, so
$B_N(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq S_{-N}\sqcup S_{-4N}$.  If $h(-N)=1$ then
$S_{-N}$ is a $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$-stable singleton and no derangement exists.
Conversely, suppose that $h(-N)>1$.  The corresponding orders satisfy
$\mathcal O_{-4N}\subseteq\mathcal O_{-N}$ with index $2$, so $
  H_{-N}\subseteq H_{-4N}
$
(e.g. \cite[Exercise~9.19]{Cox}). Restriction gives a surjection
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(H_{-4N}/K)
\twoheadrightarrow
\operatorname{Gal}(H_{-N}/K).
\]
Choose
$
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(H_{-4N}/K)
$
whose restriction to $H_{-N}$ is nonidentity.  Then $\tau$ is
itself nonidentity, so it fixes no point of $S_{-4N}$.
Its restriction to $H_{-N}$ is also nonidentity, and therefore it
fixes no point of $S_{-N}$.
Choose a lift
$
\widetilde\tau\in G_K
$
once again to get the desired derangement.

\label{rmk:37-43}
As noted above, $X_0(37)$ is both hyperelliptic and bielliptic, with
