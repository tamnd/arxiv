<!-- colorprop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:app-color-main}
For \(g\geq0\), \(n\geq1\) and \(2g-2+n>0\), the function \(N^{(+\cdots+)}_{g,n}\) is a polynomial in the \(b_i^2\), \(\widehat E_0\) and finitely many \(Z_m\). With the weights
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{wt}(b_i)=1,\qquad \operatorname{wt}(Z_m)=2m,\qquad \operatorname{wt}(\widehat E_0)=-2,
 \label{eq:app-color-weight}
\end{equation}
all its monomials have weight at most \(2g-2\), and
\begin{equation}
 \displaystyle
 V^q_{g,n}(b_1,\ldots,b_n)=
 2^{-n}\,[N^{(+\cdots+)}_{g,n}]_{2g-2}
       \left(\frac{b_1}{2},\ldots,\frac{b_n}{2}\right).
 \label{eq:app-color-identity}
\end{equation}
This is an identity over \(\mathbb Q[\widehat E_0,Z_1,Z_2,\ldots]\), so in particular it holds after specializing \(Z_m=\zeta_{q^2}(2m)\), and also at \(\widehat E_0=0\).

<!-- colorlemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:app-color-basis}
For every stable pair, \(\omega^{+}_{g,n}\) has a unique finite expansion
\begin{equation}
 \omega^{+}_{g,n}(\mathbf z)
   =\sum_{\mathbf k\geq0}\mathcal C_{g,n;\mathbf k}(\widehat E_0,\mathbf Z)
                        \prod_{i=1}^n \Xi_{k_i}(z_i),
 \qquad
 \Xi_k(z)=d_z\!\left[(z\partial_z)^{2k}\frac{z}{1-z}\right],
 \label{eq:app-color-basis}
\end{equation}
where the \(\mathcal C_{g,n;\mathbf k}\) are polynomials and \(\deg_{\widehat E_0} \mathcal C_{g,n;\mathbf k}\leq d\). Consequently
\begin{equation}
 N^{(+\cdots+)}_{g,n}(\mathbf b)
 =\sum_{\mathbf k}\mathcal C_{g,n;\mathbf k}\prod_i b_i^{2k_i}.
 \label{eq:app-color-poly}
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
At a fixed recursion step, only finitely many Taylor coefficients of the exponential in~\eqref{eq:app-color-kernel} can contribute to the residue, so by induction we get rational external differentials with coefficients in \(\mathbb Q[\widehat E_0,\mathbf Z]\). In each external variable, their only possible pole is at \(1\), and their scalar coefficient is \(O(z_i^{-2})\) at infinity and regular at zero. These properties follow from the external denominators in \(K\) and \(B\), and they are preserved by taking the residue at \(1\).

We also need the anti-invariance in \emph{each} external variable,
\begin{equation}
 \iota_i^*\omega^{+}_{g,n}=-\omega^{+}_{g,n}.
 \label{eq:app-color-anti}
\end{equation}
For the first variable, this follows from \(\iota_1^*K=-K\), which can be checked directly in~\eqref{eq:app-color-kernel}. For another variable in a stable child, we use induction. If that variable lies in a \(B\) factor instead, the two ordered splittings containing that factor combine as
\begin{align*}
 B(z,z_i)\widetilde\omega(\iota z,J')+B(\iota z,z_i)\widetilde\omega(z,J')
  =\big[B(\iota z,z_i)-B(z,z_i)\big]\widetilde\omega(z,J').
\end{align*}
Here \(\widetilde\omega\) is the stable child, and we have used its anti-invariance in the first variable. Since \((\iota\times\iota)^*B=B\), the inversion of \(z_i\) exchanges the two Bergman factors, which gives the required minus sign. The only case in which both factors are unstable is \((0,3)\), whose direct residue is
\begin{equation}
 \omega^{+}_{0,3}=8\widehat E_0\prod_{i=1}^3\frac{dz_i}{(1-z_i)^2},
 \label{eq:app-color-03}
\end{equation}
which has the same property, while the other initial case, \((1,1)\), has only the first external variable. This proves~\eqref{eq:app-color-anti} directly in each variable, without using the permutation symmetry of the restricted recursion.

The differentials \(\Xi_k\) are anti-invariant and satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \Xi_k(z)=\sum_{b\geq1}b^{2k+1}z^{b-1}dz,\qquad
 \Xi_k(z)\sim(2k+1)!\frac{dz}{(z-1)^{2k+2}}\quad(z\to1).
 \label{eq:app-color-xi}
\end{equation}
Conversely, for a rational one-form with the properties above, anti-invariance forces the highest pole order at \(1\) to be even, since the local derivative of \(\iota\) there is \(-1\). We can then subtract the appropriate multiple of \(\Xi_k\) to reduce that order, and repeat, until we are left with a holomorphic one-form on the sphere, which must vanish. This proves both spanning and independence. Applying the argument in each variable gives~\eqref{eq:app-color-basis}, and~\eqref{eq:app-color-xi} then gives~\eqref{eq:app-color-poly}.

Finally, every fully expanded recursion term contains exactly \(d\) kernels, since a quadratic bracket has total child complexity \(d-1\), with \(B\) assigned complexity zero. Each kernel is linear in \(\widehat E_0\), which proves the claimed bound on the degree in the gap.

<!-- colorlemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:app-color-scaling}
Let \(\widehat\omega^\beta_{g,n}\) denote the pullback of \(\omega^{+}_{g,n}\) under~\eqref{eq:app-color-scaling}, in all external variables. Then
\begin{equation}
 \beta^{\,d}\widehat\omega^\beta_{g,n}
      =\omega^\mathrm{loc}_{g,n}+O(\beta^2),\qquad d=2g-2+n.
 \label{eq:app-color-correlator-scaling}
\end{equation}
In fact, the left-hand side is a formal power series in \(\beta^2\).
<!-- end colorlemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Residues are invariant under \(z=z_\beta(w)\), viewed as an invertible local coordinate over the field of Laurent series in \(\beta\), since \(z_\beta(0)=1\) and \(z_\beta'(0)=2\beta\neq0\). So~\eqref{eq:app-color-recursion} becomes the single-residue recursion in \(w\), with the exact pulled-back data \(B_\beta,K_\beta\) and the involution \(w\mapsto-w\), and the original recursive order of the residues is preserved.

Next, we justify extracting the coefficients in \(\beta\) before evaluating each residue. For this, we need a bound on the principal part (i.e., the negative powers of the local coordinate) that is uniform in the coefficient being extracted. The coordinate satisfies
\begin{equation}
 z_\beta(w)-1=2\beta w\,[\mathfrak r_\beta(w)+\beta w],
 \label{eq:app-color-coordinate-unit}
\end{equation}
where the last factor has constant term one, and is therefore invertible as a formal power series. Consequently, a fixed pole of order \(\ell\) at \(z=1\) pulls back to a Laurent series with pole order at most \(\ell\) in \(w\), independently of the order in \(\beta\). Lemma~\ref{lem:app-color-basis} supplies finite pole orders for every stable child, and when two child variables are set to \(w\) and \(-w\), their orders add but remain finite. The unstable child \(B_\beta(w,-w)\) has a fixed double pole, including the pullback of \(d(-w)\), while for the other Bergman factors the external variables are held as parameters. In addition,~\eqref{eq:app-color-K-exact} bounds the pole of the kernel in the residue variable by a fixed simple pole, and the factor \(\mathcal U_\beta\) contains only nonnegative powers of \(w\), so it preserves this bound.

We now induct on \(d\), with \(B_\beta=B_\mathrm{loc}+O(\beta^2)\) as the complexity-zero datum. Assuming the claimed normalized expansions for the children, the root integrand, multiplied by \(\beta^d\) and with the external differentials suppressed, belongs to \(w^{-\ell_{\max}}\mathbb A[[w,\beta^2]]\) for some finite \(\ell_{\max}\). Here \(\mathbb A\) is a coefficient algebra containing the formal parameters and the external variables, localized as necessary in those external variables, and its dependence on \(\widehat E_0\) remains polynomial. Since the bound on \(\ell_{\max}\) that we have just established is independent of the power of \(\beta\), taking the residue and extracting a parameter coefficient are commuting algebraic operations for such an integrand \(I_\beta(w)\,dw\),
\begin{equation}
 [\beta^{2j}]\mathop{\rm Res}_{w=0}I_\beta(w)\,dw
 = [\beta^{2j}w^{-1}]I_\beta(w)
 =\mathop{\rm Res}_{w=0}[\beta^{2j}]I_\beta(w)\,dw,
 \qquad j\geq0.
 \label{eq:app-color-residue-exchange}
\end{equation}
So the exchange is justified coefficient by coefficient in the formal series.

The genus-reducing child has complexity \(d-1\), and the two children in a product have total complexity \(d-1\). Since the root kernel contributes \(\beta^{-1}\), we get the singularity bound \(\beta^{-d}\). By~\eqref{eq:app-color-residue-exchange}, the coefficient of \(\beta^{-d}\) is the residue of the leading child coefficients and \(K_\mathrm{loc}\), which obey the local recursion, with the same explicit minus sign, differential pullbacks and ordered splittings. The recursive initial data agree as well, so uniqueness proves~\eqref{eq:app-color-correlator-scaling}. All corrections occur in even powers of \(\beta\), and the formal identity also covers specializations of the parameters for which the leading coefficient vanishes.
