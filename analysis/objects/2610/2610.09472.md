\label{comp:hi:thm:input}
Under \eqref{comp:hi:eq:positive}--\eqref{comp:hi:eq:informative}, there is a unique
vector $j:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \langle q,j\rangle\le F_c(q),\qquad
 \langle p,j\rangle=\phi_c.
 \label{comp:hi:eq:dual}
\end{equation}
Let $d(q)=F_c(q)-\langle q,j\rangle$ and
$\mathcal C=\{q:d(q)=0\}$.  The contact set $\mathcal C$ is compact
and contained in the interior of the probability simplex.  At every
contact,
\begin{equation}
 \log\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}+cm(q,x)=j(x)
 \qquad(x\in\mathcal X).
 \label{comp:hi:eq:contact}
\end{equation}
The complete optimizer fiber at the prescribed rate pair is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal M_{\boldsymbol r}=\bigg\{\mu:\ &\operatorname{supp}\mu\subseteq\mathcal C,
 \quad\int q\,d\mu=p,\\
 &\int H(q)\,d\mu=a_0\bigg\}.
 \end{split}
 \label{comp:hi:eq:fiber}
\end{equation}
Its elements are probability measures.  They also satisfy
$\int H(qW)\,d\mu=r_\Sigma$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 v(q)&=c^2\sum_xq(x)\operatorname{Var}_{W(\cdot|x)}h(q,Y),\\
 \tau_-^2&=\min_{\mu\in\mathcal M_{\boldsymbol r}}\int v\,d\mu,\\
 \tau_+^2&=\max_{\mu\in\mathcal M_{\boldsymbol r}}\int v\,d\mu.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{comp:hi:eq:variance-range}
\end{equation}
Then $0<\tau_-\le\tau_+<\infty$, and all intermediate variances are
attainable by finite auxiliaries preserving $\boldsymbol r$.

The scalar image assertion has the following uniform form.  Suppose $p_n\to p$, $\mathcal X_n\subseteq T_n(p_n)$, and
output budgets $b_n$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \frac{\log|\mathcal X_n|}{n}\to a_0,\qquad
 \frac{b_n}{n}\to r_\Sigma,\\
 s_n=\frac{cb_n-\log|\mathcal X_n|
        -n\langle j+\log p,p_n\rangle}{\sqrt n}\to s.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{comp:hi:eq:input-scaling}
\end{equation}
For every $\mathcal D_n$ with $\log|\mathcal D_n|\le b_n$,
\begin{equation}
 \limsup_n\frac{1}{|\mathcal X_n|}
      \sum_{x^n\in\mathcal X_n}W^n(\mathcal D_n|x^n)
 \le f_{\boldsymbol r}(s),
 \label{comp:hi:eq:scalar-image}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 f_{\boldsymbol r}(s)=\sup_{\tau\in[\tau_-,\tau_+]}\Phi(s/\tau)
 =\begin{cases}\Phi(s/\tau_-),&s\ge0,\\
                 \Phi(s/\tau_+),&s<0.
   \end{cases}
 \label{comp:hi:eq:scalar-envelope}
\end{equation}
The bound is uniform when the three first-order errors in
\eqref{comp:hi:eq:input-scaling} vanish uniformly and $s_n$ lies in a fixed
bounded interval.  Separate bounded second-order offsets for the input
and output cardinalities are not required.

Suppose $A-C-D$, and put
\begin{equation}
 t_S(b)=-\log P_{D|C}(d|c),\qquad

 \le e^{-\gamma}.
 \label{comp:hi:eq:ancillary-spectrum}
\end{equation}
The same inequality holds after restricting $A^n$ to any nonempty

 \label{comp:hi:eq:weighted-spectrum}

 s_{0,n}&=\frac{cb_{0,n}-\log|\mathcal A_n|
                 -n\langle j+\log p,p_n\rangle}{\sqrt n},\\
 s_{S,n}^{\rm a}&=\frac{b_{S,n}-n\langle m_S,p_n\rangle}{\sqrt n},\\
 s_{S,n}^{\rm w}&=\frac{cb_{S,n}-\log|\mathcal A_n|

 \end{aligned}
 \label{comp:hi:eq:vector-thresholds}

 \le \sup_{\mu\in\mathcal M_{\boldsymbol r}}

 \label{comp:hi:eq:vector-image}
\end{equation}
Here and below the displayed probability formula applies directly when

marginals it denotes the associated closed coefficient bound, obtained
by arbitrarily small positive threshold shifts.  The error is uniform
on bounded threshold windows and vanishing first-order neighborhoods.

\label{new:dense}
Let $s$ be the number of image rows and put $\Delta_n=\delta_n+|\mathcal X||\mathcal U|\log(n+1)$. For every $\gamma>0$,
\begin{align}
 \Pr\{J_{j,n}\le b_j\ \forall j,\ \mathcal E_n(t)\}
 &\le P_{\rm im}^*(b,t),\label{new:dense-lower}\\
 P_{\rm im}^*(b,t)
 &\le\Pr\{J_{j,n}\le b_j+\Delta_n+2\gamma\ \forall j,\ \mathcal E_n(t)\}
       +2s e^{-\gamma}.\label{new:dense-upper}
\end{align}
There is no independence assumption between $U$ and any complement $C_j$, and channel transitions may vanish.

For compatible $Q_n\to Q$, let $\xi_Q(u,x,b)$ be the vector consisting of all $j_{j,Q}(b,u)$ and all $d_\ell(u,x,b)$. Set
\begin{equation}
 m(Q)=\mathbb E_{QW}\xi_Q(U,X,B),\qquad
 \Gamma(Q)=\sum_{u,x}Q(u,x)\operatorname{Cov}_{W(\cdot|x)}\xi_Q(u,x,B).
 \label{eq:marked-covariance}
\end{equation}
The image-coordinate means are $H_Q(V_j|C_j,U)$. Suppose all retained marginal variances in $\Gamma(Q)$ are positive. If $\delta_n=o(\sqrt n)$ and
\[
 (b_n,t_n)=nm(Q_n)+\sqrt nL+o(\sqrt n),
\]
then $P_{\rm im}^*(b_n,t_n)\to\Phi_{\Gamma(Q)}(L)$. Singular joint covariance is permitted. If instead $\limsup\delta_n/\sqrt n\le\beta$, the corresponding lower and upper limiting bounds are $\Phi_{\Gamma(Q)}(L)$ and $\Phi_{\Gamma(Q)}(L+\beta e_{\rm im})$, where $e_{\rm im}$ is one on image coordinates and zero on distortion coordinates.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Under the product input every member of $\mathcal T_n$ has probability $e^{-nH_Q(X|U)}$. Hence the conditioning event $\{X^n\in\mathcal A_n\}$ has probability $\pi\ge e^{-\Delta_n}$. For every output projection $O$, marginalization preserves
\[
 \frac{P_O^{\mathcal A}}{R_O}\le e^{\Delta_n}.
\]
Also $P\{\log(P/R)<-\gamma\}\le e^{-\gamma}$ for probability laws $P\ll R$. The exact conditional identity for row $j$ is
\begin{equation}
 J_{j,n}=-\log P^{\mathcal A}(V_j^n\mid C_j^n)
       +\log\frac{P_{V_j^nC_j^n}^{\mathcal A}}{R_{V_j^nC_j^n}}
       -\log\frac{P_{C_j^n}^{\mathcal A}}{R_{C_j^n}}.
 \label{eq:projected-identity}
\end{equation}
For each $c_j^n$, an admissible projection contains at most $e^{b_j}$ values. Its mass where $-\log P^{\mathcal A}(V_j^n|C_j^n)>b_j+\gamma$ is at most $e^{-\gamma}$. Averaging over $C_j^n$ and excluding the lower tail of the final likelihood ratio in \eqref{eq:projected-identity} proves \eqref{new:dense-upper}. \begin{equation}

 \label{v5:lp-dual}
\end{equation}

 \beta\ge\sup_s\sum_x\bigl(p(x)q_s(x)-\alpha_x\bigr)_+.

\end{equation}

\begin{equation}

 \label{v5:lp-rate-bracket}
\end{equation}

\label{comp:hb:lem:cell}
For every source code and every positive integer $K$,
\begin{equation}
 P_{\mathrm c}\le s_{\mathcal T}(K)+\frac{M(K-1)}{N}.
 \label{comp:hb:eq:cell-upper}
\end{equation}

 \label{comp:hb:eq:cell-lower}
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
For message $m$, intersect its source cell with $\mathcal B(a_m)$ and call the resulting set $\mathcal A_m$. The informed decoding rules for that message form a channel code on $\mathcal A_m$. Write $s_m(x)$ for the probability that every informed decoder is correct when $x\in\mathcal A_m$ is transmitted. If $|\mathcal A_m|\ge K$, retain the $K$ words with the largest values of $s_m(x)$. The selection uses the actual joint success probabilities, before any likelihood thresholds are imposed.

For receiver $j$, use the product reference

 \label{v5:quadratic-entropy}
\end{align}
The same inequality holds conditionally on any finite variable by averaging. The source in this statement need not be Gaussian, memoryless, centered, or a product law after conditioning.

This is the Gaussian-reference projection form of the entropic Brascamp--Lieb inequality \cite{blinfo,blfr}. A proof of the needed form also identifies its exact matrix condition. Whiten $Z$ to reference covariance $I$, and put
$u_i=S^{1/2}e_i/\sqrt{S_{ii}}$. Each $u_i$ is a unit vector and
$\sum_i\lambda_i u_iu_i^{\mathsf T}\preceq I$ by \eqref{v5:bl-condition}.

For a smooth positive density relative to standard Gaussian measure, the relative score of a projection $u_i^{\mathsf T}Z$ is the conditional expectation of $u_i^{\mathsf T}$ times the full relative score. Conditional Jensen therefore gives
\[
 \sum_i\lambda_i I_\gamma(P_i)
 \le\mathbb E\left[\rho(Z)^{\mathsf T}
       \Bigl(\sum_i\lambda_i u_iu_i^{\mathsf T}\Bigr)\rho(Z)\right]
 \le I_\gamma(P),
\]
where $I_\gamma$ is relative Fisher information and $\rho$ is the relative score. The Ornstein--Uhlenbeck semigroup commutes with these normalized projections. Integrating its identity
$-\frac{d}{dt}D(P_t\|\gamma)=I_\gamma(P_t)$ from zero to infinity proves \eqref{v5:relative-bl}. For a general finite-relative-entropy law, first smooth for a positive time and truncate its density, then pass to the limit by lower semicontinuity and convergence of the entropy dissipation integral. Equivalently this is the standard finite-entropy extension of the same semigroup identity. For blocks replace $u_i^{\mathsf T}$ by $u_i^{\mathsf T}\otimes I_n$; the matrix inequality and proof are unchanged.

Expand both sides of \eqref{v5:relative-bl} against their Gaussian reference densities. Their quadratic difference is $\tfrac12 z^{\mathsf T}\Lambda z$, and $\operatorname{tr}\Lambda S=k-\sum_i\lambda_i$. Rearrangement gives \eqref{v5:quadratic-entropy}. Applying it to each conditional law and averaging proves the conditional assertion. These extensions are used below only for restrictions of a Gaussian source to positive-probability measurable sets, whose relative entropies and second moments are finite.

\label{v5:polynomial-window}
The event that every entry of the joint Gram matrix of $(B,U)$ differs from its target by at most $c_1n^{-b}$ has target conditional probability at least $n^{-c_2}$. The constants are uniform under the preceding assumptions. If a Gaussian reference conditional law has bounded regression and covariance eigenvalues between $cn^{-a}$ and $C$, then on that event the log ratio $\log(dQ_{U^n|B}/dR_{U^n|B})$ differs from
\[
 nD(Q_{U|B}\|R_{U|B}\mid Q_B)
\]
by at most $O(n^{1+a-b})$. In particular, the reference hit probability is at least
\begin{equation}
 n^{-c_3}\exp[-nD(Q_{U|B}\|R_{U|B}\mid Q_B)]
 \label{v5:window-hit}
\end{equation}
when $b>a+2$.

Let $\Pi_B$ be the orthogonal projection onto the columns of $B$, and put $K_{B,n}=B^{\mathsf T}B/n$. The matrices
\[
 H=B^{\mathsf T}E/n,\qquad
 T=E^{\mathsf T}(I-\Pi_B)E/n
\]
are independent. The vectorization of $H$ is centered Gaussian with covariance $C_n\otimes K_{B,n}/n$. All its eigenvalues lie between $c'n^{-1-a}$ and $C'/n$. Its density throughout an entrywise box of width $n^{-b}$ about zero is bounded below by a power of $n$: the quadratic exponent is $O(n^{1+a-2b})=o(1)$ and its normalization determinant is polynomial. Integrating over the fixed-dimensional box gives $\Pr\{\|H\|_{\max}\le n^{-b}\}\ge n^{-c_4}$.

Whiten the orthogonal component. For an $(n-d)$-by-$q$ matrix $G$ with iid standard Gaussian entries, $T$ has the law
$C_n^{1/2}(G^{\mathsf T}G/n)C_n^{1/2}$. The density of $V=G^{\mathsf T}G/n$ is
\[
 f(V)=\frac{n^{q(n-d)/2}(\det V)^{(n-d-q-1)/2}}
 {2^{q(n-d)/2}\Gamma_q((n-d)/2)}e^{-n\operatorname{tr}V/2},\quad V\succ0,
\]
with $\Gamma_q(t)=\pi^{q(q-1)/4}\prod_{j=1}^q\Gamma(t-(j-1)/2)$. Stirling's formula bounds $f(I)$ above and below by powers of $n$. Its log-gradient at $I$ is bounded, and its log-Hessian on a fixed neighborhood of $I$ is $O(n)$. On a box of width $n^{-b}$ it therefore retains a polynomial lower bound. The box volume is polynomial, so
$\Pr\{\|V-I\|_{\max}\le n^{-b}\}\ge n^{-c_5}$. This argument is independent of the small eigenvalues of $C_n$. Since $\|C_n\|$ is bounded, the resulting $T$ differs from $C_n$ by $O(n^{-b})$.

The projected component contributes
$E^{\mathsf T}\Pi_BE/n=H^{\mathsf T}K_{B,n}^{-1}H=O(n^{-2b})$. Expanding the joint Gram using the bounded regression $L$ shows that the two independent events just constructed imply the asserted joint window. The base-covariance discrepancy costs $O(n^{-b-2})$. This proves its target probability bound.

The log ratio of two Gaussian conditional densities is a quadratic function of the entries of the joint Gram matrix, multiplied by $n$, together with a deterministic normalization. Inverse covariance norms are $O(n^a)$ and regression norms are bounded, so all its quadratic coefficients are $O(n^a)$. At the target Gram its value is exactly the displayed conditional divergence. The error on the window is $O(n^{1+a-b})$, including the smaller base error. Change of measure and the target probability lower bound prove \eqref{v5:window-hit}.

\emph{Observable design parameters.}
Let $s=\|x^n\|^2/n$ be the observed source power. With the physical channel in \eqref{v5:wz-model} fixed, its conditional variance under a Gaussian input of variance $s$ is
\[
 v(s)=\left(s^{-1}+\frac{(1-c)^2}{v(1-c)}\right)^{-1}.
\]
For a bounded allocation $t$, choose target distortion $D_t=D-t/\sqrt n$, auxiliary variance
$q(s,t)=v(s)D_t/[v(s)-D_t]$, and the conditional-mean reconstruction for this design law. Cover $x^n$ by an auxiliary word satisfying, to accuracy $O(n^{-4})$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\langle x^n,u^n-x^n\rangle}{n}=0,\qquad
 \frac{\|u^n-x^n\|^2}{n}=q(s,t).
 \label{v5:wz-gram}
\end{equation}
Use source-power bins of width $n^{-6}$ and choose design parameters at their representatives. Replacing the actual $s$ by its representative changes the displayed Gram targets and information means by $O(n^{-6})$ and $O(n^{-5})$, respectively. These bins and a finite allocation tag cost $O(\log n)$ message nats. All covering lists and bin labels for all these tags are fixed in advance.

\emph{Covering and binning.}
For each design, generate auxiliary words independently from the Gaussian marginal, optionally conditioned on its thin norm annulus. The annulus has polynomial probability and therefore changes its density by at most a polynomial factor. Lemma~\ref{v5:polynomial-window}, here on a compact positive definite covariance family, shows that a fixed source word has a Gram hit with probability at least
$n^{-C}e^{-nI(X;U)}$. Take $e^{nI(X;U)}$ times a sufficiently large polynomial number of candidates and choose the first hit. Its covering failure is polynomially small. Assign independent random bin labels only after generating the list. The label and design header are the transmitted message.

The decoder applies a threshold to $\imath(U^n;Y^n)$ within the bin. For an independent candidate and fixed $y^n$,
$\mathbb E\exp\imath(U^n;y^n)=1$ under the unconditioned Gaussian marginal, and is at most polynomial under the annulus law. Conditional on a first hit, earlier candidates have the candidate law conditioned on rejection. Its density is increased by at most $1/(1-p_{\rm hit})\le2$ for large $n$, uniformly on the design window; later candidates remain fresh. Given the source and the selected word, the physical side-information noise is independent of these candidates. Thus the usual union bound over candidate bin collisions remains valid, with only a polynomial factor. On \eqref{v5:wz-gram},
$\imath(X^n;U^n)=nI(X;U)+O(1)$. Choosing the threshold and list overhead consistently proves that the decoding and distortion success probability is at least
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\left\{\sum_{i=1}^n J_i\le\log M-C\log(n+1),\quad
                         \sum_{i=1}^n e_i^2\le nD\right\}-o(1).
 \label{v5:wz-marked-ach}
\end{equation}
This is an ensemble average; one deterministic code attains it after all lists and labels are fixed. Conditional independence is used before any conditioning on terminal success.

\emph{Source and residual fluctuations.}
The design means in \eqref{v5:wz-marked-ach} are
$nR(s,D_t)$ and $nD_t$, up to $O(1)$ window errors. Since $v'(P)=c^2$,
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt n[R(s,D_t)-R]
       =\frac{c}{2P}\sqrt n(s-P)+\frac{t}{2D}+o(1).
 \label{v5:wz-source-shift}
\end{equation}
The first term converges to $G_s\sim\mathcal N(0,c^2/2)$.

For the residual covariance it suffices to use the limiting design. Given $(x^n,u^n)$, put $Q_i=u_i-x_i$, $w=v(1-c)$, and $e_{0,i}=dcx_i-(1-d)Q_i$. Centering each mark over $N_i$ gives
\begin{align}
 J_i-\mathbb E[J_i\mid x_i,Q_i]
  &=-\frac{cx_i+Q_i}{v+q}N_i+\frac{N_i^2-w}{2(v+q)},\label{v5:wz-noise-J}\\
 e_i^2-\mathbb E[e_i^2\mid x_i,Q_i]
  &=-2d e_{0,i}N_i+d^2(N_i^2-w).\label{v5:wz-noise-D}
\end{align}
The linear and centered quadratic Gaussian terms are uncorrelated. From the Gram constraints,
\begin{align*}
 n^{-1}\sum_i(cx_i+Q_i)^2&=cv+q+o(1),\\
 n^{-1}\sum_i e_{0,i}^2&=vd[1-d(1-c)]+o(1),\\
 n^{-1}\sum_i(cx_i+Q_i)e_{0,i}&=-dv(1-c)+o(1).
\end{align*}
Using $\operatorname{Var}(N_i^2)=2w^2$ gives exactly \eqref{v5:wz-Sigma}.

To establish the conditional Gaussian limit uniformly over the Gram-constrained pairs, project the noise onto the at most two-dimensional span of $x^n$ and $u^n-x^n$. The two normalized linear marks are exactly jointly Gaussian. The centered squared norm on the orthogonal complement is an independent chi-square fluctuation, while the contribution of the at most two projected squared coordinates, divided by $\sqrt n$, vanishes in probability. This proves the joint Gaussian limit uniformly over the Gram-constrained source--auxiliary pairs and bounded design windows. Together with \eqref{v5:wz-source-shift}, \eqref{v5:wz-marked-ach} tends to the rectangle in \eqref{v5:wz-profile} for each permitted allocation.

\emph{Selection and inversion.}
The covariance is positive definite because
\[
 \det\Sigma=2D^2d(1-d)(1-c)^2(1+c)>0.
\]
For each finite $\ell$, the rectangle probability
$\Phi_\Sigma(\ell-t/(2D),t)$ tends to zero as $t\to\pm\infty$, and is positive for finite $t$. Its maximum is attained. Maximizers stay bounded locally in $\ell$, so the envelope is continuous; fixing a maximizing $t$ shows that it is strictly increasing in $\ell$. Its limits are zero and one. On a bounded source-fluctuation window, a finite grid of allocations approximates the envelope uniformly. Select the best grid point from the observed source-power bin. Increasing the window and refining the grid after taking $n\to\infty$ proves achievability of $F_{\rm G}$; a diagonal sequence and a vanishing favorable rate adjustment give \eqref{v5:wz-upper}. Dominated convergence proves continuity and the endpoint limits of $F_{\rm G}$, and pointwise strict monotonicity gives a unique inverse. Finally, giving $Y^n$ also to the encoder yields the conditional Gaussian rate-distortion lower coefficient $Q^{-1}(\varepsilon)/\sqrt2$ \cite{conditionalrd,rd}, proving \eqref{v5:wz-interval}.
