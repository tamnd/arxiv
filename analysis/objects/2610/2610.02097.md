<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:clifford-em-not-z2}
Consider the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ toric code on an $L_x\times L_y$ square lattice with periodic boundary conditions, with the system size sufficiently large compared with the locality range.
Any locality-preserving Clifford realization $U_{em}$ of the $e$-$m$ exchange satisfies
\begin{align}
U_{em}^2 \not\propto I .
\end{align}
In particular, there is no order-two Clifford realization of the $e$-$m$ exchange.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:clifford-em-not-z2-diagonal}
Consider the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ toric code on a square lattice $\Lambda$ with periodic boundary conditions, with the system size sufficiently large compared with the locality range.
If a locality-preserving Clifford unitary $U$ induces diagonal exchanges of vertex and plaquette stabilizers
\begin{align*}
UA_vU^\dagger=B_{p(v)},
\qquad
UB_{p(v)}U^\dagger=A_v
\end{align*}
for every $v\in V(\Lambda)$, then $U^2\not\propto I$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:diagonal-exchange-string-dressing}
Let $U$ satisfy the exchange relations in Theorem~\ref{thm:clifford-em-not-z2-diagonal}.
Let $\alpha_e$ be an open Pauli-$Z$ string connecting vertices $v_1,v_2$, and let $\alpha_m$ be a nearby Pauli-$X$ string connecting the paired plaquettes $p(v_1),p(v_2)$.
Choose a disk-shaped neighborhood $R_{\alpha_e}$ of the strings, wide enough to contain the support of $U\alpha_eU^\dagger$.
Then
\begin{align}
U\alpha_eU^\dagger\propto\alpha_m S_{R_{\alpha_e}},
\label{eq:diagonal-local-string-dressing}
\end{align}
where $S_{R_{\alpha_e}}$ has a unique decomposition into vertex and plaquette stabilizers in $R_{\alpha_e}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $U$ is Clifford, $U\alpha_eU^\dagger$ is a Pauli operator.
It creates the same pair of magnetic excitations as $\alpha_m$, so $U\alpha_eU^\dagger\alpha_m$ commutes with every stabilizer.
This operator is supported in a disk-topology region $R_{\alpha_e}$ and hence is proportional to a product of stabilizers in $R_{\alpha_e}$ whose decomposition is unique.
The global redundancy relations give alternative decompositions, but these involve stabilizer generators outside $R_{\alpha_e}$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Suppose, for contradiction, that $U^2\propto I$.
After absorbing an overall phase into $U$, we may assume $U^2=I$.
We choose three vertex--plaquette pairs
\begin{align}
h_1=(v_1,p_1),\qquad h_2=(v_2,p_2),\qquad h_3=(v_3,p_3),
\qquad p_j=p(v_j),
\label{eq:diagonal-three-pairs}
\end{align}
in the L-shaped arrangement shown in Fig.\,\ref{fig:physical-picture-geometry}, with separations much larger than the locality range of $U$.
Let $\alpha_e$ and $\alpha_m$ be electric and magnetic strings connecting $h_1$ to $h_2$, and let $\beta_e$ and $\beta_m$ connect $h_2$ to $h_3$.
We choose their paths so that $\alpha_m$ and $\beta_e$ cross once, whereas $\alpha_e$ and $\beta_m$ are disjoint.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\makebox[\textwidth][c]{ 
\includegraphics[scale=1.4]{figures/fig-physical-picture-geometry.pdf}}
\caption{String geometry used in the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:clifford-em-not-z2-diagonal}.
Blue electric strings lie on the direct lattice and red magnetic strings lie on the dual lattice.}
\label{fig:physical-picture-geometry}
\end{figure}

By Lemma \ref{lem:diagonal-exchange-string-dressing}, $U\alpha_eU^\dagger$ is the magnetic string $\alpha_m$ decorated by a local product of stabilizers.
Here, we separate the factors $A_{v_2}$ and $B_{p_2}$, associated with $h_2=(v_2,p_2)$, from the remaining stabilizers.
\begin{align}
U\alpha_eU^\dagger
\propto\alpha_m A_{v_2}^{x}B_{p_2}^{y}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_e},
\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{F}_2,
\label{eq:diagonal-alpha-image}
\end{align}
where $\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_e}$ contains neither $A_{v_2}$ nor $B_{p_2}$.
These factors $A_{v_2}$ and $B_{p_2}$ determine how the stabilizer decoration contributes to the commutation relations with the $\beta$ strings.

Applying $U$ once more gives
\begin{align}
\alpha_e
\propto
(U\alpha_mU^\dagger)B_{p_2}^{x}A_{v_2}^{y}
(U\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_e}U^\dagger).
\label{eq:diagonal-alpha-twice}
\end{align}
Since $U$ exchanges the stabilizers pairwise, $U\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_e}U^\dagger$ also contains neither $A_{v_2}$ nor $B_{p_2}$.
Hence, the vertex and plaquette stabilizers at $h_2=(v_2,p_2)$ have their exponents interchanged. $A_{v_2}^{x}B_{p_2}^{y} \rightarrow B_{p_2}^{x}A_{v_2}^{y}$.
The same reasoning applies to the $\beta$ strings, and we obtain
\begin{align}
U\alpha_eU^\dagger
&\propto\alpha_m A_{v_2}^{x}B_{p_2}^{y}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_e},
\nonumber\\
U\alpha_mU^\dagger
&\propto\alpha_e A_{v_2}^{y}B_{p_2}^{x}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_m},
\nonumber\\
U\beta_eU^\dagger
&\propto\beta_m A_{v_2}^{z}B_{p_2}^{w}\widetilde{S}_{\beta_e},
\nonumber\\
U\beta_mU^\dagger
&\propto\beta_e A_{v_2}^{w}B_{p_2}^{z}\widetilde{S}_{\beta_m},
\qquad x,y,z,w\in\mathbb{F}_2.
\label{eq:physical-picture-string-images}
\end{align}
Each tilded operator is a product of stabilizers near its corresponding string and contains neither $A_{v_2}$ nor $B_{p_2}$.
Overall Pauli phases will not affect the argument.

For Pauli operators $P,Q$, define the commutation relation by
\begin{align}
PQ=(-1)^{\langle P,Q\rangle}QP,
\qquad \langle P,Q\rangle\in\mathbb{F}_2.
\label{eq:diagonal-pauli-pairing}
\end{align}
Since $\alpha_m$ and $\beta_e$ cross once, we have
\begin{align}
1
&=\langle\alpha_m,\beta_e\rangle
=\langle U\alpha_mU^\dagger,U\beta_eU^\dagger\rangle
\nonumber\\
&=\left\langle
\alpha_e A_{v_2}^{y}B_{p_2}^{x},
\beta_m A_{v_2}^{z}B_{p_2}^{w}
\right\rangle
\nonumber\\
&=z\langle\alpha_e,A_{v_2}\rangle
+x\langle B_{p_2},\beta_m\rangle
=x+z.
\label{eq:diagonal-commutation-calculation}
\end{align}
In the second line we dropped the tilded stabilizers as they do not contain $A_{v_2}$ or $B_{p_2}$.
In the third line we used that $\alpha_e$ and $\beta_m$ are disjoint, while $\alpha_e$ anticommutes with $A_{v_2}$ and $\beta_m$ anticommutes with $B_{p_2}$.
Thus
\begin{align}
x+z=1\pmod{2}.
\label{eq:physical-picture-crossing}
\end{align}

We now derive another constraint by closing the strings into contractible loops.
The product $\alpha_e\beta_e$ is an electric string connecting $v_1$ and $v_3$ (Fig.\,\ref{fig:no-go-string-geometry}(a)).
Multiplying the transformations in Eq.\,\eqref{eq:physical-picture-string-images}, we find
\begin{align}
U(\alpha_e\beta_e)U^\dagger
&\propto\alpha_m\beta_m A_{v_2}^{a}B_{p_2}^{b}
\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_e\beta_e},
\nonumber\\
a&=x+z,\qquad b=y+w\pmod{2},
\label{eq:diagonal-joined-string-image}
\end{align}
where $\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_e\beta_e}=\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_e}\widetilde{S}_{\beta_e}$ contains neither middle generator.

Choose an electric string $\gamma_e$ from $v_3$ back to $v_1$, following the distant sides of the rectangle in Fig.\,\ref{fig:no-go-string-geometry}(b).
Here, $\alpha_e\beta_e\gamma_e$ forms a closed loop surrounding a rectangular region $M$, and can be expressed as a product of plaquettes on $M$
\begin{align}
\alpha_e\beta_e\gamma_e=\prod_{q\in M}B_q.
\label{eq:diagonal-electric-loop}
\end{align}
The region $M$ lies to the left of $\beta_e$, whereas $p_2$ lies immediately to the upper right of $v_2$. Hence, 
\begin{align}
p_2\notin M.
\end{align}
Using the exchange relations of stabilizers, we have
\begin{align}
U(\alpha_e\beta_e\gamma_e)U^\dagger
=\prod_{v\in V_M}A_v,
\qquad
V_M:=\{v\in V(\Lambda):p(v)\in M\}.
\label{eq:diagonal-electric-loop-image}
\end{align}
Since $p(v)$ is the plaquette immediately to the upper right of $v$, the vertex region $V_M$ is obtained from the plaquette region $M$ by shifting it to the lower left.
This direction is opposite to the vertex-to-plaquette pairing $A_v\mapsto B_{p(v)}$, which shifts the stabilizer label to the upper right.
In particular, since $p_2\notin M$, we have
\begin{align}
v_2\notin V_M.
\end{align}

\centering
\raisebox{-18.0pt}[234.59846pt][18.0pt]{ 
\includegraphics[width=491.43622pt,height=252.59846pt]{figures/fig-no-go-string-geometry.pdf}}
\caption{String geometry used in the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:clifford-em-not-z2-diagonal}.
Blue direct-lattice strings are Pauli-$Z$ operators and red dual-lattice strings are Pauli-$X$ operators; arrows give the orientations used for the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ argument.
(a) $\alpha_m$ and $\beta_e$ cross once, while $\alpha_e$ and $\beta_m$ are disjoint.
Dashed closing strings form (b) an electric loop enclosing the plaquette set $M$ with $p_2\notin M$, and (c) a magnetic loop enclosing the vertex set $N$ with $v_2\notin N$.}
\label{fig:no-go-string-geometry}
\end{figure}


Likewise, choose a nearby magnetic closing string $\gamma_m$ from $p_3$ to $p_1$, as in Fig.\,\ref{fig:no-go-string-geometry}(c).
The loop $\alpha_m\beta_m\gamma_m$ bounds a simply connected vertex region $N$, and thus
\begin{align}
\alpha_m\beta_m\gamma_m=\prod_{v\in N}A_v.
\label{eq:diagonal-magnetic-loop}
\end{align}
Here, $N$ and $V_M$ do not coincide.
Since $p(v)$ lies immediately to the upper right of $v$, $V_M$ is shifted to the lower left relative to $N$.
In particular, the lower boundary of $N$ is $\alpha_m$, which lies above $v_2$, and hence
\begin{align}
v_2\notin N.
\end{align}

The closing string $\gamma_e$ can be chosen sufficiently far from the middle pair $(v_2,p_2)$.
Hence, Lemma \ref{lem:diagonal-exchange-string-dressing} gives
\begin{align}
U\gamma_eU^\dagger\propto\gamma_m\widetilde{S}_{\gamma_e},
\label{eq:diagonal-closing-string-image}
\end{align}
where $\widetilde{S}_{\gamma_e}$ contains neither $A_{v_2}$ nor $B_{p_2}$.

We can now compute the transformed electric loop in a second way, by multiplying the three transformed segments.
Combining Eqs.\,\eqref{eq:diagonal-joined-string-image}, \eqref{eq:diagonal-electric-loop-image}, and \eqref{eq:diagonal-closing-string-image}, we obtain
\begin{align}
\alpha_m\beta_m\gamma_m
A_{v_2}^{a}B_{p_2}^{b}
\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_e\beta_e}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma_e}
\propto\prod_{v\in V_M}A_v.
\label{eq:diagonal-loop-comparison}
\end{align}
Substituting Eq.\,\eqref{eq:diagonal-magnetic-loop} gives an equality between stabilizer products,
\begin{align}
\left(\prod_{v\in N}A_v\right)
A_{v_2}^{a}B_{p_2}^{b}
\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_e\beta_e}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma_e}
\propto\prod_{v\in V_M}A_v.
\label{eq:diagonal-stabilizer-comparison}
\end{align}
Neither $N$ nor $V_M$ contains $v_2$, and the tilded products contain no $A_{v_2}$.
The only possible occurrence of this generator is therefore the explicit factor $A_{v_2}^{a}$ on the left-hand side.
Comparing its coefficient in the two disk-supported decompositions yields
\begin{align}
a=x+z=0\pmod{2},
\label{eq:physical-picture-loop}
\end{align}
in contradiction with Eq.\,\eqref{eq:physical-picture-crossing}.
This completes the proof.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:z2-em-exchanging-symmetry}
A locality-preserving Clifford unitary $U$ is called an $e$-$m$ exchanging symmetry if there exists a constant $R$, independent of the system size, such that for every vertex $v$ and plaquette $p$,
\begin{align}
\begin{aligned}
U A_v U^\dagger
&=
\prod_{q\in \mathcal{P}(v)} B_q,
\\
U B_p U^\dagger
&=
\prod_{w\in \mathcal{V}(p)} A_w,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:general-em-exchange}
\end{align}
where every plaquette in $\mathcal{P}(v)$ lies within distance $R$ from $v$, and every vertex in $\mathcal{V}(p)$ lies within distance $R$ from $p$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:local-stabilizer-basis-change}

Let $F:C_2\longrightarrow C_2$ be a locality-preserving and locally invertible $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear map satisfying $F\mathbf{1}_2=\mathbf{1}_2$.
Then there exists a locality-preserving CSS Clifford unitary $V_F$ such that
\begin{align}
\begin{aligned}
V_F A_v V_F^\dagger &= A_v,
\\
V_F B(b)V_F^\dagger &= B(Fb)
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:local-stabilizer-basis-change}
\end{align}
for every vertex $v$ and every $b\in C_2$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We first show that the deviation of $F$ from the identity can be written as
\begin{align}
L:= F-I=HG_2
\end{align}
for some locality-preserving linear map $H:C_1\to C_2$.

Since $F\mathbf{1}_2=\mathbf{1}_2$, we have
\begin{align}
L\mathbf{1}_2=(F-I)\mathbf{1}_2=0.
\label{eq:L-even-parity}
\end{align}
For each plaquette $p$, let $\ell_p$ denote the transpose of the $p$-th row of $L$.
Then, Eq.\,\eqref{eq:L-even-parity} implies
\begin{align}
\ell_p^t\mathbf{1}_2=0,
\end{align}
so $\operatorname{supp}(\ell_p)$ contains an even number of plaquettes.
Then, we can pair plaquettes in $\operatorname{supp}(\ell_p)$ as $(q_i,q_i')$.
For each pair, one may choose some simple sequence of adjacent plaquettes
\begin{align}
q_i=p_0,\ p_1,\ \ldots,\ p_k=q_i'
\end{align}
within the neighborhood of $p$.
Let $s_i$ be the edge vector supported on the edges shared by plaquettes in this sequence, as shown in Fig.\,\ref{fig:local-basis-change-path}.
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\raisebox{-0.0pt}[54.42168pt][0.0pt]{ 
\includegraphics[width=491.43622pt,height=54.42168pt]{figures/fig-local-basis-change-path.pdf}}
\caption{The edge vector $s_i$ (left) and the resulting plaquette vector $G_2^t s_i$ (right).}
\label{fig:local-basis-change-path}
\end{figure}
Each intermediate plaquette $p_j$ contains two selected edges from the sequence, while each endpoint plaquette contains only one.
Since we work over $\mathbb{F}_2$, the intermediate contributions cancel, and hence
\begin{align}
G_2^t s_i=q_i+q_i'.
\end{align}
Setting $h_p:=\sum_i s_i\in C_1$, we obtain
\begin{align}
G_2^t h_p
=\sum_i(q_i+q_i')
=\ell_p.
\end{align}
Taking $h_p^t$ as the $p$-th row of a locality-preserving map $H:C_1\to C_2$, we therefore have
\begin{align}
L=HG_2.
\label{eq:L-HG2}
\end{align}

We now lift the plaquette-label transformation $F$ to a locality-preserving invertible transformation on the physical edge labels.
Define
\begin{align}
T:=I+G_2H,
\qquad
T:C_1\longrightarrow C_1.
\end{align}
Then, $T$ reproduces the action of $F$ on plaquette stabilizers.
\begin{align}
TG_2
&=(I+G_2H)G_2 \nonumber\\
&=G_2+G_2HG_2 \nonumber\\
&=G_2+G_2(F-I)
=G_2F.
\end{align}
                                                                                                                                                                      
At the same time, $T$ leaves the vertex stabilizers invariant.
Namely, using the commutativity condition $G_2^tG_1=0$, we find
\begin{align}
T^tG_1
=(I+H^tG_2^t)G_1
=G_1.
\end{align}
Here, $T$ is invertible.
Indeed, the inverse is
\begin{align}
T^{-1}=I+G_2F^{-1}H.
\end{align}
To verify this, we use $HG_2=F-I$ and compute
\begin{align}
&(I+G_2H)(I+G_2F^{-1}H) \nonumber\\
&\qquad
=I+G_2H+G_2F^{-1}H+G_2HG_2F^{-1}H \nonumber\\
&\qquad
=I+G_2\!\left[
H+F^{-1}H+(F-I)F^{-1}H
\right]
=I.
\end{align}
Since $G_2$, $H$, and $F^{-1}$ are all locality preserving, both $T$ and $T^{-1}$ are locality preserving.

We now extend $T$ to a transformation of the full Pauli group.
Up to an overall phase, an edge Pauli operator can be labeled by a pair $(z,x)^t\in C_1\oplus C_1$, corresponding to
\begin{align}
Z(z)X(x).
\end{align}
Since the $Z$-type labels transform as $z\mapsto Tz$, preservation of the Pauli commutation relations requires the $X$-type labels to transform as $x\mapsto (T^{-1})^t x$.
We therefore define
\begin{align}
S:=
\begin{pmatrix}
T&0\\
0&(T^{-1})^t
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
This transformation is symplectic and hence can be implemented by some Clifford unitary, which we denote by $V_F$.
Since $T$ and $T^{-1}$ are locality preserving,  both $S$ and $S^{-1}$ are locality preserving, and thus $V_F$ defines a locality-preserving CSS Clifford transformation.
Finally, one can verify
\begin{align}
V_F B_p V_F^\dagger
&= Z(TG_2p) \nonumber\\
&= Z(G_2Fp)
= B(Fp)
\end{align}
and
\begin{align}
V_F A_v V_F^\dagger
&= X\bigl((T^{-1})^tG_1v\bigr)\\
&= X\bigl(G_1v\bigr) = A_v.
\end{align}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:order-2n}
The order of $U_{em}$ is $2N$ up to an overall phase.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
It follows from Eq.\,\eqref{eq:anyon_permutation_sym} that
\begin{align}
U_{em}^2A_v^{(1)}U_{em}^{-2}=A_v^{(1)},\qquad
U_{em}^2B_p^{(1)}U_{em}^{-2}=B_p^{(1)}.
\end{align}
Now take the horizontal edge $h=h_{0,0}$.
The conjugation action of $U_{em}^2$ on $X_h$ is (see Fig.\,\ref{fig:u_action_qd})
\begin{align}
U_{em}^2X_hU_{em}^{-2}
=
X_h\,A_{(0,0)}^{(1)}
\bigl(A_{(0,-1)}^{(1)}\bigr)^{-1}.
\end{align}
Since $U_{em}^2$ fixes every $A_v^{(1)}$, repeated application gives
\begin{align}
U_{em}^{2k}X_hU_{em}^{-2k}
=
X_h
\Bigl(
A_{(0,0)}^{(1)}
\bigl(A_{(0,-1)}^{(1)}\bigr)^{-1}
\Bigr)^k.
\end{align}
The stabilizer factor
$A_{(0,0)}^{(1)}\bigl(A_{(0,-1)}^{(1)}\bigr)^{-1}$
has order $N$.
Hence the conjugation action of $U_{em}^2$ has order at least $N$.
The same calculation for $Z_h$, $X_v$, and $Z_v$ shows that $U_{em}^{2N}$ acts trivially on the full Pauli algebra, and hence
\begin{align}
U_{em}^{2N}\propto I.
\end{align}
Moreover, the action on $X_h$ remains nontrivial for $0<k<N$, so no smaller even power can be proportional to the identity, while odd powers exchange vertex and plaquette stabilizers.
Therefore $U_{em}$ has order $2N$ up to an overall phase.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:ZN-em-exchanging-symmetry}
We use the vertex--plaquette pairing $p(v)$ introduced in Section~2.2 to identify vertex and plaquette indices.
For finitely supported exponent column vectors $a$ and $b$ over $\mathbb{Z}_N$, define
$\mathsf{A}(a):=\prod_v A_v^{(a_v)}$ and
$\mathsf{B}(b):=\prod_p B_p^{(-b_p)}$.
The minus sign in the definition of $\mathsf{B}(b)$ is chosen so that the diagonal $e$-$m$ exchange corresponds to the identity map on the exponent labels.

A locality-preserving Clifford unitary $U$ is called an $e$-$m$ exchanging symmetry if there exist locality-preserving, locally invertible $\mathbb{Z}_N$-linear maps $F$ and $G$ such that
\begin{align}
U\mathsf{A}(a)U^\dagger=\mathsf{B}(Fa),
\qquad
U\mathsf{B}(b)U^\dagger=\mathsf{A}(Gb),
\label{eq:ZN-general-stabilizer-exchange}
\end{align}
for all $a$ and $b$, with $F\mathbf{1}=G\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}$.
Here, local invertibility means that $F^{-1}$ and $G^{-1}$ are also locality preserving.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Suppose, for contradiction, that $U_{em}^2\propto I$.
We first use Lemma \ref{lem:ZN-local-stabilizer-basis-change} to reduce the stabilizer action to an exact exchange of diagonal pairs.
With $F$ as in Eq.\,\eqref{eq:ZN-general-stabilizer-exchange}, define
\begin{align}
\widehat{U}:=V_F^\dagger U_{em}V_F.
\end{align}
Since $V_F$ fixes the vertex stabilizers and sends $\mathsf{B}(b)$ to $\mathsf{B}(Fb)$, we obtain
$\widehat{U}\mathsf{A}(a)\widehat{U}^\dagger=\mathsf{B}(a)$.
Applying $\widehat{U}$ once more and using $\widehat{U}^2\propto I$ gives the reverse exchange.
Thus,
\begin{align}
\widehat{U}A_v^{(1)}\widehat{U}^\dagger
=B_{p(v)}^{(-1)},
\qquad
\widehat{U}B_{p(v)}^{(-1)}\widehat{U}^\dagger
=A_v^{(1)}.
\label{eq:ZN-normalized-stabilizer-exchange}
\end{align}
After absorbing an overall phase, we may assume $\widehat{U}^2=I$.

We use the same L-shaped string arrangement as in Fig.\,\ref{fig:no-go-string-geometry}, with separations much larger than the locality range of $\widehat{U}$.
Orient $\alpha_e$ from $v_1$ to $v_2$ and $\beta_e$ from $v_2$ to $v_3$, with $e^{-1}$ at each initial endpoint and $e$ at each final endpoint.
Choose $\alpha_m$ and $\beta_m$ on the nearby dual paths with the same endpoint syndromes as $\widehat{U}\alpha_e\widehat{U}^\dagger$ and $\widehat{U}\beta_e\widehat{U}^\dagger$, respectively.
Since $\alpha_m$ and $\beta_e$ cross once, whereas $\alpha_e$ and $\beta_m$ are disjoint,
\begin{align}
\langle\alpha_m,\beta_e\rangle=\pm1,
\qquad
\langle\alpha_e,\beta_m\rangle=0.
\label{eq:ZN-alpha-m-beta-e}
\end{align}

The local stabilizer decomposition in Lemma \ref{lem:diagonal-exchange-string-dressing} extends to $\mathbb{Z}_N$ by the same contractible-region argument.
Separating the factors at the middle pair $h_2=(v_2,p_2)$ from the remaining stabilizers, we obtain
\begin{align}
\widehat{U}\alpha_e\widehat{U}^\dagger
&\propto\alpha_m A_{v_2}^{(x)}B_{p_2}^{(-y)}
\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_e},
\nonumber\\
\widehat{U}\alpha_m\widehat{U}^\dagger
&\propto\alpha_e A_{v_2}^{(-y)}B_{p_2}^{(x)}
\widetilde{S}_{\alpha_m},
\nonumber\\
\widehat{U}\beta_e\widehat{U}^\dagger
&\propto\beta_m A_{v_2}^{(z)}B_{p_2}^{(-w)}
\widetilde{S}_{\beta_e},
\qquad x,y,z,w\in\mathbb{Z}_N.
\label{eq:ZN-string-images}
\end{align}
The second relation follows by applying $\widehat{U}$ once more to the first and using $\widehat{U}^2=I$ together with Eq.\,\eqref{eq:ZN-normalized-stabilizer-exchange}.
Each tilded operator is a product of stabilizers near its corresponding string and contains neither $A_{v_2}^{(1)}$ nor $B_{p_2}^{(1)}$.

As in the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ proof, preservation of the commutation exponent gives
\begin{align}
\langle\alpha_m,\beta_e\rangle
&=
\langle\widehat{U}\alpha_m\widehat{U}^\dagger,
\widehat{U}\beta_e\widehat{U}^\dagger\rangle
\nonumber\\
&=
z\langle\alpha_e,A_{v_2}^{(1)}\rangle
+x\langle B_{p_2}^{(1)},\beta_m\rangle.
\end{align}
The tilded operators make no contribution because they exclude the middle generators and are supported far from the remaining endpoints.
Both endpoint commutation exponents on the right are $\pm1$, since the endpoints have unit charge.
For even $N$, reduction modulo two is well defined and removes these orientation signs.
Together with Eq.\,\eqref{eq:ZN-alpha-m-beta-e}, this gives
\begin{align}
x+z=1\pmod{2}.
\label{eq:ZN-commutation-condition}
\end{align}

We now derive another constraint by closing the strings into contractible loops.
Choose the oriented closing strings $\gamma_e$ and $\gamma_m$ as in Fig.\,\ref{fig:no-go-string-geometry}(b,c), sufficiently far from $h_2$ that
$\widehat{U}\gamma_e\widehat{U}^\dagger\propto\gamma_m\widetilde{S}_{\gamma_e}$,
where $\widetilde{S}_{\gamma_e}$ contains neither middle generator.
The electric loop is a product of oriented plaquette stabilizers in $M$, with $p_2\notin M$.
Its image is therefore a product of vertex stabilizers with no factor at $v_2$, since each plaquette maps to the vertex immediately to its lower left.
The bare magnetic loop also encloses no $v_2$.

We can now compute the transformed electric loop in a second way, by multiplying the three transformed segments.
The first and third relations in Eq.\,\eqref{eq:ZN-string-images} give a factor $A_{v_2}^{(x+z)}$, while neither the bare magnetic loop nor the tilded products contributes at $v_2$.
All decompositions are taken in a common contractible region smaller than the torus, so global redundancy cannot change a local exponent.
Comparing the exponent at $v_2$ in the two disk-supported decompositions yields
\begin{align}
x+z=0\pmod{N}.
\label{eq:ZN-loop-condition}
\end{align}
Because $N$ is even, this contradicts Eq.\,\eqref{eq:ZN-commutation-condition}.
Hence $U_{em}^2\not\propto I$.

We follow the construction of Lemma \ref{lem:local-stabilizer-basis-change}, retaining the oriented incidence numbers that disappear over $\mathbb{F}_2$.
All modules in this proof have coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}_N$.
Let $C_0:=\bigoplus_{v\in V(\Lambda)}\mathbb{Z}_Nv$, $C_1:=\bigoplus_{e\in E(\Lambda)}\mathbb{Z}_Ne$, $C_2:=\bigoplus_{p\in F(\Lambda)}\mathbb{Z}_Np$ be the vertex, edge, and plaquette modules, and let
\begin{align}
G_1:C_0\longrightarrow C_1,
\qquad
G_2:C_2\longrightarrow C_1
\end{align}
be the oriented vertex and plaquette generator matrices.
Write $G_2(e,p)\in\{0,\pm1\}$ for the coefficient of the oriented edge $e$ in the boundary of $p$.
The all-ones plaquette vector is
\begin{align}
\mathbf{1}:=\sum_{p\in F(\Lambda)}p
\in C_2\cong\mathbb{Z}_N^{|F(\Lambda)|\times1}.
\label{eq:ZN-all-ones-plaquette-vector}
\end{align}
Set $L:=F-I$.
The condition $F\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}$ implies
\begin{align}
L\mathbf{1}=0.
\label{eq:ZN-L-augmentation}
\end{align}
Let $\ell_p\in C_2^*$ be the $p$-th row of $L$.
Eq.\,\eqref{eq:ZN-L-augmentation} states that
\begin{align}
\sum_q\ell_p(q)=\ell_p\mathbf{1}=0
\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{Z}_N.
\label{eq:ZN-row-coefficient-sum}
\end{align}
Let $R$ be the locality range of $F$, and let $P_R(p)$ be the set of plaquettes reachable from $p$ by at most $R$ edge-sharing steps.
Then $\operatorname{supp}(\ell_p)\subset P_R(p)$.
For each $q\in P_R(p)\setminus\{p\}$, choose a simple path of plaquettes
\begin{align}
p=p_0,p_1,\ldots,p_k=q,
\qquad
k\leq R,
\end{align}
and denote the edge shared by $p_{j-1}$ and $p_j$ by $e_j$.
Let $e^*\in C_1^*$ denote the row vector dual to the edge basis element $e\in C_1$.
Similarly, let $p^*,q^*\in C_2^*$ denote the row vectors dual to the plaquette basis elements $p,q\in C_2$.
Define
\begin{align}
s_q:=\sum_{j=1}^kG_2(e_j,p_j)e_j^*
\in C_1^*.
\label{eq:ZN-signed-path-vector}
\end{align}
The two plaquettes incident to $e_j$ have opposite incidence numbers.
\begin{align}
G_2(e_j,p_{j-1})=-G_2(e_j,p_j).
\label{eq:ZN-opposite-incidence}
\end{align}
At an intermediate plaquette $p_j$, the two contributions to $s_qG_2$ cancel because
\begin{align}
G_2(e_j,p_j)^2
+G_2(e_{j+1},p_{j+1})G_2(e_{j+1},p_j)
=1-1=0.
\end{align}
The coefficients at $p_0=p$ and $p_k=q$ are $-1$ and $+1$, respectively, while every plaquette outside the path has coefficient zero.
Hence
\begin{align}
s_qG_2=q^*-p^*.
\label{eq:ZN-path-boundary}
\end{align}
Eq.\,\eqref{eq:ZN-row-coefficient-sum} permits the decomposition
\begin{align}
\ell_p
=\sum_{q\in P_R(p)\setminus\{p\}}\ell_p(q)(q^*-p^*).
\end{align}
Set
\begin{align}
h_p:=\sum_{q\in P_R(p)\setminus\{p\}}\ell_p(q)s_q.
\end{align}
Eq.\,\eqref{eq:ZN-path-boundary} implies
\begin{align}
h_pG_2
=\sum_{q\in P_R(p)\setminus\{p\}}\ell_p(q)(q^*-p^*)
=\ell_p.
\end{align}
Every edge in $\operatorname{supp}(h_p)$ lies within distance $R$ of $p$.
The vectors $h_p$ can therefore be assembled as the rows of a locality-preserving map $H:C_1\to C_2$, and they obey
\begin{align}
L=HG_2.
\label{eq:ZN-L-HG2}
\end{align}

Define the transformation of edge labels
\begin{align}
T:=I+G_2H.
\end{align}
Eq.\,\eqref{eq:ZN-L-HG2} implies
\begin{align}
TG_2
=G_2+G_2HG_2
=G_2(I+L)
=G_2F.
\end{align}
The CSS relation $G_2^tG_1=0$ also implies
\begin{align}
T^tG_1
=(I+H^tG_2^t)G_1
=G_1.
\end{align}
Since $T$ is invertible, the last identity is equivalent to
\begin{align}
(T^{-1})^tG_1=G_1.
\label{eq:ZN-T-relations}
\end{align}
The inverse contains the minus sign that is invisible over $\mathbb{F}_2$.
\begin{align}
T^{-1}=I-G_2F^{-1}H.
\label{eq:ZN-T-inverse}
\end{align}
To verify this identity, use $HG_2=F-I$ and compute
\begin{align}
(I+G_2H)(I-G_2F^{-1}H)
&=I+G_2\bigl(H-F^{-1}H-HG_2F^{-1}H\bigr)
\nonumber\\

\end{align}
Thus $T$ and $T^{-1}$ are both locality preserving.
