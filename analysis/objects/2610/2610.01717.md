<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Every weakly bell-shaped subprobability measure on $\mathbb{R}$ is the hitting distribution for a possibly intransitive shift-invariant diffusion in $\mathbb{R} \times [0, \infty)$.

Generators of intransitive shift-invariant diffusions are given by the same formal expressions, but their domains can be different. A two-way interface between reflecting layers, as in Section~\ref{sec:amcm}, corresponds to matching of values and normal derivatives. A one-way interface above a reflected layer, as in the exponential layer in Section~\ref{sec:pff}, corresponds to a one-sided Neumann condition at its upper boundary, together with matching of values from the layer above. An interface above a layer with a deterministic downward motion, as in the Gaussian layer also in Section~\ref{sec:pff}, corresponds to matching the values only; no Neumann condition is imposed there.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and the results of~\cite{k26} strongly suggest the following characterisation: $f$ is a weakly bell-shaped distribution if and only if it is the (nontrivial) hitting distribution of an intransitive shift-invariant diffusion in $\mathbb{R} \times [0, \infty)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} proves the direct implication, while Theorem~1.1 in~\cite{k26} gives the converse for transitive shift-invariant diffusions. The complete converse result can be approached as follows.

It is intuitively clear that every intransitive diffusion can be obtained by stacking a transitive layer atop a family of intransitive layers with one-way interfaces, in a way similar to the construction carried out in Section~\ref{sec:pff}, but possibly much more complicated. The results of~\cite{k26} imply that each such layer, crossed from its top boundary to the bottom one, contributes a Pólya frequency function to the overall hitting distribution. Finally, the convolution with a Pólya frequency function preserves the class of bell-shaped distributions, and this class is also closed by (nontrivial) vague limits.

We do not pursue this direction here: a rigorous version of the above argument would take us too far from the main scope of this note.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:amcm}
Every nonzero $\mathscr{A}\mspace{-4mu}\mathscr{M}\mspace{-2mu}\mathhyphen\mspace{-1mu}\mathscr{C}\mspace{-4mu}\mathscr{M}$ subprobability measure is the hitting distribution of a regular transitive shift-invariant diffusion in $\mathbb{R} \times [0, R)$, where $R \in (0, \infty]$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->

Equation~\eqref{eq:hat:check} also follows directly from the construction carried out in Section~3.3 of~\cite{k26}, at least when $\hat a(\{0\}) = 0$. With $\check R = 1$, $\check a(dy) = 0 dy$ and $\check b(y) = 0$, we then have $\check \psi(\xi) = \tfrac{1}{2}$, and Eq.~(3.7) in~\cite{k26} gives~\eqref{eq:hat:check}. <!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:pff}
Every probability measure which is a Pólya frequency function is the hitting distribution of an intransitive irregular shift-invariant diffusion in $\mathbb{R} \times [0, R]$, where $R \in (0, \infty)$, started at the top boundary $\mathbb{R} \times \{R\}$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By Corollary~1.9 in~\cite{ks22}, $f$ is the convolution of an $\mathscr{A}\mspace{-4mu}\mathscr{M}\mspace{-2mu}\mathhyphen\mspace{-1mu}\mathscr{C}\mspace{-4mu}\mathscr{M}$ measure $g$ and an integrable Pólya frequency function $h$. Since $f$ has mass at most $1$, both factors have a finite positive mass, and we may normalise both of them so that $h(\mathbb{R}) = 1$ and $g(\mathbb{R}) \leqslant 1$.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:amcm} $g$ is the hitting distribution of a regular transitive shift-invariant diffusion in $\mathbb{R} \times [0, R_f)$, started at $(0, 1)$; here $R_f \in (0, \infty]$. Proposition~\ref{prop:pff} states that $h$ is the hitting distribution of an intransitive shift-invariant diffusion in $\mathbb{R} \times [0, R_g]$, started at $(x, R_g)$; with $R_g \in (0, \infty)$ and $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

We now stack the $\mathscr{A}\mspace{-4mu}\mathscr{M}\mspace{-2mu}\mathhyphen\mspace{-1mu}\mathscr{C}\mspace{-4mu}\mathscr{M}$ layer corresponding to $g$ atop the Pólya frequency function layer corresponding to $h$, with a one-way interface that $Y_t$ can only cross downwards. As in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:pff}, we obtain an intransitive shift-invariant diffusion in $\mathbb{R} \times [0, R_g + R_f)$, whose hitting distribution when started at $(x, R_g + 1)$ is the convolution $g * h = f$.

Finally, if $R_f$ is finite, we perform a nonlinear change of variables from $[0, R_g + R_f)$ to $[0, \infty)$ to obtain an intransitive shift-invariant diffusion in $\mathbb{R} \times [0, \infty)$ with the same hitting distribution $f$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
