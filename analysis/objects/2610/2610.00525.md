<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:cosys:expansion:informal}
Every direction-wise lossless decorated cubical complex satisfying suitable lossless expansion and imbalance assumptions is a robust small-set level coboundary expander.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
Every sufficiently small cochain $S:V_r\to\mathbb{F}_2$ in a direction-wise lossless cubical complex satisfying the imbalance assumptions admits cochains $L_t$, each supported in $\operatorname{Lk}(t)$, such that
\[
\bigcup_t\operatorname{supp}(L_t)=\operatorname{supp}(S),
\qquad
\left\lVert\delta S\right\rVert\ge\sum_t\left\lVert\delta L_t\right\rVert-C_{\varepsilon,k}\left\lVertS\right\rVert,
\]
where $C_{\varepsilon,k}\to 0$ as the loss parameters vanish.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:arbitrary-rank-expansion}
Let $X$ be a $k$-dimensional lossless square complex, with parameters $\left(D_i, D_i',c_i,\varepsilon_i\right)$. If for every $s<t$, $\varepsilon_tD_s' \leq \sum \varepsilon_i$ then there exist  constants $c,a$ independent of $\left\lvertX(0)\right\rvert$ such that $X$ is a robust $\left(c, a-\varepsilon\right)$ small-set coboundary expander, for $\varepsilon = O_k\left(\left(\sum\varepsilon_i\right)^{\Omega_k\left(1\right)}\right)$
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A simplicial complex $Y$ consists of a vertex set $Y(0)$ and a downward-closed collection of nonempty subsets of $Y(0)$. For $i\geq 0$, let
\[
Y(i)=\{\sigma\in Y:|\sigma|=i+1\}
\]
be the set of $i$-dimensional faces.\footnote{We adjoin the empty face, of dimension $-1$, and set $Y(-1)=\{\varnothing\}$.} In particular, $Y(1)$ is the edge set of the underlying graph $(Y(0),Y(1))$. For $i\geq 0$, let
\[
\operatorname{Cl}_Y(i)=
\left\{\sigma\in\binom{Y(0)}{i+1}:\binom{\sigma}{2}\subseteq Y(1)\right\}.
\]
Thus $\operatorname{Cl}_Y(i)$ is the collection of $(i+1)$-vertex cliques in this graph, and $Y(i)\subseteq\operatorname{Cl}_Y(i)$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A simplicial complex $Y$ is $k$-decorated if $Y(0)=V_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_k$ and every edge has endpoints in distinct blocks. Consequently, every face contains at most one vertex from each block.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
A graph $G$ is $k$-cube-decorated if its vertex set is
partitioned into $2^k$ blocks $V_I$, indexed by
$I\subseteq[k]$, and every edge joins blocks $V_I,V_J$
with $\{I,J\}\in E(Q_k)$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
A decorated $k$-cube complex $X$ is a $k$-cube-decorated
graph $G=(X(0),X(1))$ with sets
$X(i)\subseteq\operatorname{Cu}_G(i)$, $0\leq i\leq k$,
closed under subcubes, meaning that every $j$-subcube of
a cube in $X(i)$ belongs to $X(j)$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
Let $1\leq r\leq k$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ has \emph{$r$-joint completion} if, for every $1\leq t\leq r$, every vertex together with $t$ incident edges in \emph{distinct} directions is contained in exactly one member of $X(t)$.
\item $X$ has \emph{$r$-path completion} if, for every $1\leq t\leq r$, every path of $t$ edges in \emph{distinct} directions is contained in exactly one member of $X(t)$.
\item $X$ has \emph{$r$-rainbow-tree completion} if, for every $1\leq t\leq r$, every \emph{rainbow} tree in $X(1)$ with $t$ edges is contained in exactly one member of $X(t)$.
\end{enumerate}
In each case, uniqueness is among the cubes in $X(t)$, and not with respect to $\operatorname{Cu}_G(t)$.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:completion-equivalence}
For every decorated $k$-cube complex and $1\leq r\leq k$, the completion properties are equivalent.
\[
\begin{aligned}
r\text{-joint completion}
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r\text{-rainbow-tree completion}
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r\text{-path completion}.
\end{aligned}
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We prove that the three properties imply one another cyclically. Both nontrivial implications use induction on the number $t\leq r$ of edges, with $t=1$ immediate.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Joint $\Rightarrow$ rainbow.}
Let $T$ be a rainbow tree with $t\geq2$ edges, and delete a leaf $u$ adjacent to $v$. By induction, $T\setminus\left\{u\right\}$ has a unique completion $Q'\in X(t-1)$. Complete the $t$-joint formed by $uv$ and the edges of $Q'$ at $v$ to $Q\in X(t)$. Its face through $v$ in the directions of $Q'$ equals $Q'$ by joint completion, so $T\subseteq Q$. Any member of $X(t)$ containing $T$ contains $Q'$ by induction, hence this joint, and therefore equals $Q$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Rainbow $\Rightarrow$ path.}
Every rainbow path is a rainbow tree.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Path $\Rightarrow$ joint.}
For a $t$-joint centered at $v$, remove an edge $e$ and complete the remaining joint to $Q'\in X(t-1)$ by induction. Append $e$ to a rainbow path of length $t-1$ in $Q'$ ending at $v$, and complete it to $Q\in X(t)$. Its face through $v$ in the directions of $Q'$ equals $Q'$ by path completion, so $Q$ contains the joint. Any member of $X(t)$ containing the joint contains $Q'$ by induction, hence this path, and therefore equals $Q$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
A decorated $(k,r)$-cubical complex is a decorated $k$-cube complex satisfying any of the equivalent $r$-completion properties. We subsequently omit the word decorated. A $(k,2)$-cubical complex is called a \emph{$k$-dimensional square complex}.
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
An embedded cube $Q\in \operatorname{Cu}_X(i)$ need not be in $X(i)$. For example, when $A=B=\Gamma$, $\operatorname{Cu}_{X_{A,\Gamma,B}}(2)$ contains $|\Gamma|^4$ four-cycles with one vertex in each block, whereas only the $|\Gamma|^3$ squares $Q(g,a,b)$ are in $X_{A,\Gamma,B}(2)$. Thus our definition does not impose a ``flag'' condition requiring every embedded cube to be declared.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
One may also impose stronger uniqueness properties: whenever $x\in V_I$ and $y\in V_J$ are joined by a rainbow path of length $d=|I\mathbin{\triangle}J|$, they belong to a unique declared $d$-cube. This requires uniqueness from the endpoints alone. The LPS-based cubical complexes constructed in \cite[Lemma~3.3 and Theorem~3.5]{HLMRZ25} satisfy this condition, but left--right Cayley complexes need not, as depicted in \cref{fig:left-right-weak-strong}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{chain:complex}
For every $(k,2)$-cubical complex $X$ and $1\leq s\leq k-1$,
\[
\partial_s\partial_{s+1}=0,
\qquad
\delta_s\delta_{s-1}=0.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix $x\in V_{s+1}$ and $z\in V_{s-1}$. The $(z,x)$-entry of $\partial_s\partial_{s+1}$ counts, modulo two, the vertices $y\in V_s$ satisfying $z\lessdot y\lessdot x$. For each such $y$, the unique square completing along this path has another middle vertex
\[
y'=z 
    \if\relax\detokenize{y}\relax
        \wedge
    \else
        \mathbin{\overset{y}{\wedge}} 
    \fi
x.
\]
Observe that completing $z\lessdot y'\lessdot x$ gives the same square, i.e. $z  
    \if\relax\detokenize{y'}\relax
        \wedge
    \else
        \mathbin{\overset{y'}{\wedge}} 
    \fi
 x = y$, so $y\mapsto y'$ is a fixed-point-free involution, since $\operatorname{type}(y) \neq \operatorname{type}(y')$ . Therefore the number of vertices $y\in V_s$ satisfying $z\lessdot y\lessdot x$ is even, proving $\partial_s\partial_{s+1}=0$. Taking transposes yields $\delta_s\delta_{s-1}=0$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:hamming-vs-norm}
For every $f:X(0)\to\mathbb{F}_2$,
\[
\frac{|\operatorname{supp} f|}{D_{[k]}}
\leq \left\lVertf\right\rVert
\leq |\operatorname{supp} f|.
\]
Thus the weighted norm and Hamming weight are equivalent up to a factor depending only on the degrees.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:reversal}
For $0\leq s\leq k$, the identity on functions identifies $C_s(X)$ with $C^{k-s}(X^{\mathrm{rev}})$. Under this identification,
\[
\partial_s^X=\delta_{k-s}^{X^{\mathrm{rev}}}
\qquad(1\leq s\leq k).
\]
Moreover, $D_i^{\mathrm{rev}}=D_i'$ and $(D_i')^{\mathrm{rev}}=D_i$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By definition, $V_s(X)=V_{k-s}(X^{\mathrm{rev}})$, and $x\lessdot_X y$ if and only if $y\lessdot_{X^{\mathrm{rev}}}x$. Thus the corresponding boundary and coboundary maps sum over the same neighbors. Reversal also exchanges the two sides of every directional graph, giving the degree identities.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def:augmented-link}
Let $v\in V_I$. For $J\subseteq[k]\setminus I$, define
\[
\operatorname{Lk}_J(v)=\{x\in V_{I\cup J}:v\leq x\}.
\]
The link of $v$ is $\operatorname{Lk}(v)=\{x\in X(0):v\leq x\}$, with levels
\[
\operatorname{Lk}_s(v)=\bigsqcup_{\substack{J\subseteq[k]\setminus I\\|J|=s+1}}\operatorname{Lk}_J(v),
\qquad -1\leq s\leq k-|I|-1.
\]
In particular, $\operatorname{Lk}_{-1}(v)=\operatorname{Lk}_{\emptyset}(v)=\left\{v\right\}$.
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:link-chain-complex}
If $X$ is a $(k,2)$-cubical complex, then the restricted link maps satisfy $\partial^2=0$ and $\delta^2=0$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The involution of \cref{chain:complex} preserves the link.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
\label{def:minimality}
For $1\leq r\leq k$, a cochain $f\in C^r(X)$ is \emph{minimal} if
\[
\left\lVertf\right\rVert\leq\left\lVertf+\delta_{r-1}h\right\rVert
\qquad\text{for every }h\in C^{r-1}(X).
\]
It is \emph{locally minimal} if, for every $0\leq s\leq r-1$, every $v\in V_s$, and every $h\in C^{r-s-2}(\operatorname{Lk}(v))$,
\[
\left\lVertf\right\rVert\leq\left\lVertf+\delta_{r-1}(\iota_vh)\right\rVert.
\]
All cochains in \(C^0(X)\) are minimal.
<!-- end definition-8 -->
<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
\label{def:small-set-coboundary-expansion}
For $0\leq r\leq k-1$, a cubical complex $X$ is an $(\alpha,\beta)$-small-set coboundary expander at level $r$ if every locally minimal $f\in C^r(X)$ satisfying $\left\lVertf\right\rVert\leq\alpha$ also satisfies $\left\lVert\delta_rf\right\rVert\geq\beta\left\lVertf\right\rVert$.
<!-- end definition-9 -->
<!-- definition-10: body_md -->
Let $H=(L,R,E)$ be a $(D,D')$-biregular bigraph. For $c,\varepsilon\in\left(0,1\right)$, we say that $H$ is \emph{one-sided $(c,\varepsilon)$-lossless} from $L$ to $R$ if every $A\subseteq L$ with $|A|\leq c|L|$ satisfies
\[
|\Gamma(A)|\geq(1-\varepsilon)D|A|.
\]
<!-- end definition-10 -->
<!-- definition-11: body_md -->
\label{def:lossless-cubical:complex}
A cubical complex $X$ is \emph{lossless} with parameters $(D_i,D_i',c_i,\varepsilon_i)_{i=1}^k$ if, for every $I\subseteq[k]$ and $i\notin I$, the directional graph $X_{I,i}$ is one-sided $(c_i,\varepsilon_i)$-lossless from $V_I$ to $V_{I\cup\{i\}}$. It is \emph{two-sided lossless} if $X^{\mathrm{rev}}$ is also lossless, with $D_i,D_i'$ interchanged and the same $c_i,\varepsilon_i$.
<!-- end definition-11 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:lossless-estimates}
Let $H=(L,R,E)$ be a one-sided $(c,\varepsilon)$-lossless $(D,D')$-biregular bigraph.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{lossless:excess} \textnormal{\textbf{Excess bound.}} If $A\subseteq L$ satisfies $|A|\leq c|L|$, then
\[
\sum_{y\in R}(\deg_A(y)-1)_+
=D|A|-|\Gamma(A)|
\leq\varepsilon D|A|.
\]
\item\label{lossless:edge} \textnormal{\textbf{Lossless edge bound.}} If $A\subseteq L$ satisfies $|A|\leq c|L|$, then, for every $B\subseteq R$,
\[
e(A,B)\leq |B|+\varepsilon D|A|.
\]
\item\label{lossless:large:degree} \textnormal{\textbf{Large-degree bound.}} For $B\subseteq R$, suppose that at most $c|L|$ vertices $x\in L$ satisfy $\deg_B(x)>\varepsilon D$. Then
\[
\sum_{x\in L}\left(\frac{\deg_B(x)}D-\varepsilon\right)_+
\leq\frac{|B|}D.
\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
\noindent\textbf{Excess bound (\ref*{lossless:excess}).}
Fix $A\subseteq L$ with $|A|\leq c|L|$. Every vertex in $\Gamma(A)$ has at least one neighbor in $A$, and vertices outside $\Gamma(A)$ have none. Consequently, by regularity and lossless expansion,
\[
\sum_{y\in R}(\deg_A(y)-1)_+
=\sum_{y\in\Gamma(A)}(\deg_A(y)-1)
=D|A|-|\Gamma(A)|
\leq\varepsilon D|A|.
\]

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Lossless edge bound (\ref*{lossless:edge}).}
Fix $A\subseteq L$ with $|A|\leq c|L|$ and $B\subseteq R$. At each vertex of $B\cap\Gamma(A)$, count one incident edge from $A$ separately from the remaining edges. This gives
\[
e(A,B)
=|B\cap\Gamma(A)|+\sum_{y\in B}(\deg_A(y)-1)_+
\leq |B|+\sum_{y\in R}(\deg_A(y)-1)_+
\leq |B|+\varepsilon D|A|.
\]
The last inequality follows from part~(\ref{lossless:excess}).

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Large-degree bound (\ref*{lossless:large:degree}).}
Fix $B\subseteq R$ as in the statement, and put $A=\{x\in L:\deg_B(x)>\varepsilon D\}$, so $|A|\leq c|L|$. The summand $(\deg_B(x)/D-\varepsilon)_+$ is positive precisely when $x\in A$. Therefore,
\[
\sum_{x\in L}\left(\frac{\deg_B(x)}D-\varepsilon\right)_+
=\sum_{x\in A}\left(\frac{\deg_B(x)}D-\varepsilon\right)
=\frac{e(A,B)}D-\varepsilon|A|.
\]
The bound $|A|\leq c|L|$ allows us to apply part~(\ref{lossless:edge}), obtaining
\[
e(A,B)\leq |B|+\varepsilon D|A|.
\]
Substituting this bound into the preceding identity gives the required upper bound $|B|/D$.

<!-- claim-1: body_md -->
\label{clm:good-interval}
For every $x_I\in G$, the interval $[v,x_I]$\footnote{As usual
for a poset, $[v,x_I]=\left\{y:v\le y\le x_I\right\}$.}
satisfies the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] For every $J\subseteq I$, there is a unique vertex
$x_J\in[v,x_I]\cap\operatorname{Lk}_J(v)$.
\item[(2)] For every $J,K\subseteq I$, we have $x_J\le x_K$
if and only if $J\subseteq K$. In particular, the interval is
an $|I|$-dimensional Boolean cube.
\item[(3)] Every four-cycle in this interval is a square in $X(2)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end claim-1 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
\noindent\emph{(1)}
Fix $J\subseteq I$ and an ordering $\pi$ of $I$ that lists $J$ first i.e. $\pi|_{[|J|]}$ is an ordering of $J$.
By \cref{cor:ordered-endpoint-surjective}, there is a path $P$
of order $\pi$ from $v$ to $x_I$.
Let $x_J$ be its vertex after $|J|$ steps.
Any $y\in[v,x_I]\cap\operatorname{Lk}_J(v)$ lies on a monotone path from $v$
to $x_I$. The first $\left\lvertJ\right\rvert$ directions in this path are $J$. Applying the square-switch argument of
\cref{lem:ordered-path-switching} separately before and after $y$
gives a path of order $\pi$ through $y$.
Since $x_I\in G$, this path must be $P$, so $y=x_J$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{(2)}
Suppose $J\subseteq K\subseteq I$. Choose an ordering of $I$
that lists first $J$, then $K\setminus J$, and finally $I\setminus K$.
By \cref{cor:ordered-endpoint-surjective}, there is a path of this order
from $v$ to $x_I$. The vertices reached after $|J|$ and $|K|$ steps
have types $J$ and $K$, respectively. Both lie in $[v,x_I]$, so
by (1) they are $x_J$ and $x_K$. The part of the path from $x_J$
to $x_K$ gives $x_J\le x_K$.
Conversely, if $x_J\le x_K$, an upward path from $x_J$ to $x_K$
can only add directions to the type, so $J\subseteq K$.
By (1), the vertices of the interval are precisely
$\left\{x_J:J\subseteq I\right\}$. The equivalence just proved identifies
their order with inclusion of subsets of $I$, giving an
$|I|$-dimensional Boolean cube.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{(3)}
By (2), every four-cycle in $[v,x_I]$ is a two-dimensional face
of the Boolean cube. Thus, for some $J\subseteq I$ and distinct
$j_1,j_2\in I\setminus J$, its vertices can be labeled
\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=x_{J\cup\left\{j_1,j_2\right\}},
& b&=x_{J\cup\left\{j_1\right\}},\\
b'&=x_{J\cup\left\{j_2\right\}},
& c&=x_J.
\end{aligned}
\]
Complete the path $c\lessdot b\lessdot a$ to a square in $X(2)$,
and let $b''$ be its fourth vertex.
The path through $b$ uses directions $j_1,j_2$, so the other path
$c\lessdot b''\lessdot a$ uses directions $j_2,j_1$.
Hence $b''$ has type $J\cup\left\{j_2\right\}$, the same as $b'$.
Also, $v\le c\lessdot b''\lessdot a\le x_I$, so
$b''\in[v,x_I]$. By the uniqueness in (1), $b''=b'$, and hence
$\left\{a,b,b',c\right\}\in X(2)$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:poset-homomorphism}
The map $\varphi:G\to\mathsf M$ is a type-preserving poset isomorphism
between $G$ and its image.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We need to show that $\varphi$ preserves types, is injective on $G$,
and preserves the order in both directions.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{(1) Type preservation.}
If $x_I\in G$ has type $I$, then $\varphi(x_I)$ contains exactly
one vertex from each part indexed by an element of $I$ and no other vertices.
Thus $\varphi(x_I)$ also has type $I$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{(2) Injectivity on $G$.}
Suppose that two distinct vertices have the same image.
By (1), they have the same type $I$; write them as $x_I,x_I'\in G$.
For every $J\subseteq I$, let $x_J$ and $x_J'$ be the unique
type-$J$ vertices in $[v,x_I]$ and $[v,x_I']$, respectively,
given by \cref{clm:good-interval}.
Choose $J$ of minimum cardinality for which $x_J\ne x_J'$.
The two intervals agree at $v$ and, since the images are equal,
at every singleton type, so $|J|\ge2$.
Choose distinct $j,j'\in J$ and put
\[
\begin{aligned}
b&\mathrel{:=} x_{J\setminus\left\{j\right\}}
           =x_{J\setminus\left\{j\right\}}',\\
b'&\mathrel{:=} x_{J\setminus\left\{j'\right\}}
           =x_{J\setminus\left\{j'\right\}}',\\
c&\mathrel{:=} x_{J\setminus\left\{j,j'\right\}}
           =x_{J\setminus\left\{j,j'\right\}}',
\end{aligned}
\]
where the equalities follow from the choice of $J$.
By \cref{clm:good-interval}, both $\left\{x_J;b,b';c\right\}$ and
$\left\{x_J';b,b';c\right\}$ are squares in $X(2)$.
They complete the same joint formed by the edges $cb$ and $cb'$,
so uniqueness of square completion gives $x_J=x_J'$, a contradiction.
Thus $\varphi$ is injective on $G$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{(3) Order preservation in both directions.}
If $x_J\le x_I$, every level-one vertex below $x_J$ also lies
below $x_I$, so $\varphi(x_J)\subseteq\varphi(x_I)$.
Conversely, suppose $\varphi(x_J)\subseteq\varphi(x_I)$.
By (1), $J\subseteq I$.
Let $x_J'\in[v,x_I]$ be the unique type-$J$ vertex given by
\cref{clm:good-interval}. Since $G$ is downward closed, $x_J'\in G$.
By the forward implication and (1), both $\varphi(x_J)$ and
$\varphi(x_J')$ are contained in $\varphi(x_I)$ and have type $J$.
Since $\varphi(x_I)$ contains at most one vertex in each part,
these two faces are equal. By (2), $x_J=x_J'\le x_I$.
Therefore $\varphi$ is a type-preserving poset isomorphism onto
its image, as depicted in \cref{fig:good-cube-embedding}.

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
We start by propagating the norm assumption on the top part of the link to
all its parts.  Fix $I\subseteq[k]$, $j\in I$, and put
$J\mathrel{:=} I\setminus\left\{j\right\}$.  Choose an ordering of $I$ whose first
$|J|$ directions are the elements of $J$.  By
\cref{cor:ordered-endpoint-surjective},
\[
    \operatorname{Lk}_I(v)=\Gamma_j\left(\operatorname{Lk}_J(v)\right).
\]
Since every vertex has $D_j$ upward neighbours in direction $j$,
\[
    \left\lvert\operatorname{Lk}_I(v)\right\rvert
    \le D_j\left\lvert\operatorname{Lk}_J(v)\right\rvert.
\]
Dividing by $D_I=D_J\,D_j$, we get that
$\left\lVert\operatorname{Lk}_I(v)\right\rVert\le\left\lVert\operatorname{Lk}_J(v)\right\rVert$.  Iterating from $I=[k]$ down to an
arbitrary type gives
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:top-fullness-propagates}
    \left\lVert\operatorname{Lk}_I(v)\right\rVert
    \ge
    \left\lVert\operatorname{Lk}_{[k]}(v)\right\rVert
    \ge 1-\eta.
\end{equation}

For $I\subseteq[k]$, let $B_I$ and $G_I$ be the bad and good vertices of
type $I$, respectively, and set
\[
    \widehat G_I\mathrel{:=}\varphi(G_I)\subseteq\mathsf M(I),
    \qquad
    \widehat B_I\mathrel{:=}\mathsf M(I)\setminus\widehat G_I.
\]
The exact endpoint count in \eqref{eq:endpoint-excess} gives
\[
    \left\lVertB_I\right\rVert\le\left\lVert\mu_I\right\rVert
    =
    1-\left\lVert\operatorname{Lk}_I(v)\right\rVert
    \le \eta.
\]
The map $\varphi$ is type preserving, so it preserves the norm on $G_I$.
Thus $\left\lVertG_I\right\rVert=\left\lVert\varphi(G_I)\right\rVert$, and since
$\left\lVert\mathbf 1_{\mathsf M(I)}\right\rVert=1$, we also have 
\begin{equation*}
    \left\lVert\widehat B_I\right\rVert
    =
    1-\left\lVert\widehat{G}_I\right\rVert 
    =
    \left(1-\left\lVert\mathbf 1_{\operatorname{Lk}_I(v)}\right\rVert\right)+\left\lVertB_I\right\rVert 
    =
    1-\left\lVert\operatorname{Lk}_I(v)\right\rVert+\left\lVertB_I\right\rVert
    \le 2\eta.
\end{equation*}
For $-1\le s\le k-1$, let $B_s,G_s,\widehat B_s,\widehat G_s$ be the
corresponding unions over all types of size $s+1$. Thus
$B_s,G_s\subseteq\operatorname{Lk}_s(v)$ and $\widehat B_s,\widehat G_s\subseteq\mathsf M(s)$,
and we obtain
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:bad-level-bounds}
    \begin{aligned}
    \left\lVertB_s\right\rVert
    \le
    \binom{k}{s+1}\eta,
    \qquad
    \left\lVert\widehat B_s\right\rVert
    \le
    2\binom{k}{s+1}\eta.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Let $\widehat h_{r-1}:\mathsf M(r-1)\to\mathbb{F}_2$. Define the pushforward
$\widehat f_r:\mathsf M(r)\to\mathbb{F}_2$ of $f_r$ and the pullback
$h_{r-1}:\operatorname{Lk}_{r-1}(v)\to\mathbb{F}_2$ of $\widehat h_{r-1}$ by
\[
    \widehat f_r(y)
    \mathrel{:=}
    \begin{cases}
        f_r\left(\varphi^{-1}(y)\right), & y\in\widehat G_r, \\
        0, & y\in\widehat B_r.
    \end{cases}, 
    \qquad
    h_{r-1}(x)
    \mathrel{:=}
    \begin{cases}
        \widehat h_{r-1}\left(\varphi(x)\right), & x\in G_{r-1}, \\
        0, & x\in B_{r-1}.
    \end{cases}
\]
We now compare $\operatorname{dist}_{\mathsf M}\widehat f_r$ with $\operatorname{dist}_{\operatorname{Lk}(v)}(f_r)$. If $x\in G_r$, then all children of $x$
in $\operatorname{Lk}(v)$ are good, and
\cref{clm:good-interval,lem:poset-homomorphism} show that $\varphi$ bijects
them with the children of $\varphi(x)$.  Thus, for $x\in G$ and for $\widehat{\delta}$ the coboundary map on $\mathsf M$,
\[
    \widehat{\delta}\widehat h_{r-1}\left(\varphi(x)\right)
    =
    \delta h_{r-1}(x).
\]
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
    \left\lVert\widehat f_r+\widehat{\delta}\widehat h_{r-1}\right\rVert
    &\ge
    \left\lVert\left.
        \left(\widehat f_r+\widehat{\delta}\widehat h_{r-1}\right)
        \right|_{\widehat G_r}\right\rVert \\
    &=
    \left\lVert\left.
        \left(f_r+\delta h_{r-1}\right)
        \right|_{G_r}\right\rVert \\
    &\ge
    \left\lVertf_r+\delta h_{r-1}\right\rVert-\left\lVertB_r\right\rVert \\
    &\ge
    \operatorname{dist}(f_r)-\left\lVertB_r\right\rVert.
\end{align*}
Taking the minimum over $\widehat h_{r-1}$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:formal-approximate-minimality}
    \operatorname{dist}_{\mathsf M}(\widehat f_r)
    \ge
    \operatorname{dist}(f_r)-\left\lVertB_r\right\rVert.
\end{equation}
Therefore \cref{thm:lmm-multipartite} implies
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:formal-coboundary-lower-bound}
    \left\lVert\widehat{\delta}\widehat f_r\right\rVert
    \ge
    m_{k,r}\left(\operatorname{dist}(f_r)-\left\lVertB_r\right\rVert\right).
\end{equation}

Likewise, if $x\in G_{r+1}$, then its children in $\operatorname{Lk}(v)$ are good and
correspond under $\varphi$ to the children of $\varphi(x)$, so
\[
    \widehat{\delta}\widehat f_r\left(\varphi(x)\right)
    =
    \delta f_r(x).
\]
Hence,
\[
\left\lVert\delta f_r\right\rVert
    \ge
    \left\lVert\left.\left(\delta f_r\right)\right|_{G_{r+1}}\right\rVert 
    =
    \left\lVert\left.\left(\widehat{\delta}\widehat f_r\right)
        \right|_{\widehat G_{r+1}}\right\rVert 
    \geq
    \left\lVert\widehat{\delta}\widehat f_r\right\rVert
        -\left\lVert\widehat B_{r+1}\right\rVert 
\] 
Combining this with \eqref{eq:formal-coboundary-lower-bound} and then using
\eqref{eq:bad-level-bounds}, we get
\begin{align*}
    \left\lVert\delta f_r\right\rVert
    &\ge
    m_{k,r}\operatorname{dist}(f_r)
        -m_{k,r}\left\lVertB_r\right\rVert
        -\left\lVert\widehat B_{r+1}\right\rVert \\
    &\ge
    m_{k,r}\operatorname{dist}(f_r)
        -\left(
            m_{k,r}\binom{k}{r+1}
            +2\binom{k}{r+2}
        \right)\eta \\
    &=
    m_{k,r}\operatorname{dist}(f_r)-O_k(\eta),
\end{align*}
as claimed.

\label{ltg}

Notice that
\[
H_r
=
\sum_{t\in \operatorname{Ter}}H_r(t)
    +
    \sum_{a\in H_r}\operatorname{E}(a)\left\{a\right\}.
\]
where the sum is over \(\mathbb{F}_2\),  
because every
\(a\in H_r\) 
is counted $\left\lvert\operatorname{Ter}(a)\right\rvert$ times in the first argument, and $\operatorname{E}(a)=\left\lvert\operatorname{Ter}(a)\right\rvert-1$ in the second argument.

We first deal with the problem that a vertex $a \in H_r$ may have several terminals.

\ge

-
\sum_{a\in H_r}\operatorname{E}(a)\left\lVert\delta\left\{a\right\}\right\rVert

\left\lVert\sum_{t\in \operatorname{Ter}}\delta H_r(t)\right\rVert
    -
    (k-r)\left\lVert\operatorname{E}_r\right\rVert,
    \label{ltg:first:error}
\end{align}
where the first inequality is the triangle inequality,
and the equality follows from
$\left\lVert\delta\left\{a\right\}\right\rVert=(k-r)\left\lVerta\right\rVert$ for $a\in H_r$.

We now deal with the problem that neighborhoods of vertices in $H_r$ may overlap. 
For \(b\in H_{r+1}\) and a terminal $t \in \operatorname{Ter}$, we say $t$ \emph{contributes} to $b$, if $b\in\delta H_r(t)$,
i.e., if $b$ belongs to the coboundary of the local piece $H_r(t)$. 
Let

    n_b
    \mathrel{:=}
    \left\lvert\left\{t\in \operatorname{Ter} :b\in\delta H_r(t)\right\}\right\rvert.
\]
Thus \(n_b\) is the number of terminal-cluster coboundaries that contain
\(b\). 
Notice that
\[
    n_b\le\left\lvert\operatorname{Ter}(b)\right\rvert,
\]
and equality may not hold, because it is possible that $b$ is connected through heavy vertices to a terminal $t$, but $b$ cancels out in $\delta H_r(t)$.

In this terminology,
\[
\sum_{t\in \operatorname{Ter}}\left\lVert\delta H_r(t)\right\rVert
=
\sum_{b \in H_{r+1}} n_b \left\lVertb\right\rVert, \quad \text{and} \quad
\left\lVert\sum_{t\in \operatorname{Ter}}\delta H_r(t)\right\rVert
=
\sum_{b \in H_{r+1}} (n_b \bmod 2) \left\lVertb\right\rVert.
\]
Hence,
\begin{align*}
\sum_{t\in \operatorname{Ter}}\left\lVert\delta H_r(t)\right\rVert
-
\left\lVert\sum_{t\in \operatorname{Ter}}\delta H_r(t)\right\rVert
&=
\sum_{b \in H_{r+1}}(n_b- (n_b \bmod 2)) \left\lVertb\right\rVert 
\leq \sum_{b \in H_{r+1}} 2(n_b-1)_+\left\lVertb\right\rVert.
\end{align*}
where we used the inequality $n-(n \mod 2) \leq 2(n-1)_+$ which holds for every $n\in \mathbb{N}$. Now, substituting $n_b \le \left\lvert\operatorname{Ter}(b)\right\rvert =\operatorname{E}(b)+1$ and rearranging, we get
\begin{equation}

    \le
    \left\lVert\sum_{t\in \operatorname{Ter}}\delta H_r(t)\right\rVert
    +
    2\sum_{b\in H_{r+1}} \operatorname{E}(b)\left\lVertb\right\rVert
    =
    \left\lVert\sum_{t\in \operatorname{Ter}}\delta H_r(t)\right\rVert
    +
    2\left\lVert\operatorname{E}_{r+1}\right\rVert.
    \label{ltg:second:error}
\end{equation}
Combining \cref{ltg:first:error,ltg:second:error} proves the proposition.

\item Vertices in $\operatorname{Ch}(a)$ that have the same type as $b^\star$,
\item Vertices in $\operatorname{Ch}(a)$ that have a different type than $b^\star$, such that $b

\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal-subtraction}
|\operatorname{Ter}(b)\setminus\operatorname{Ter}(b^\star)| 
 \leq (|\operatorname{Ter}(b)|-1) + 
\mathbf{1}_{b \in \operatorname{SameType}_{a,b^\star}}
+ 
\mathbf{1}_{b \in \operatorname{LightSquare}_{a,b^\star}}.
\end{equation}

$|\operatorname{Ter}(b)\setminus \operatorname{Ter}(b^\star)|

    Fix a terminal vertex $t\in V_I$ and put $i=\left\lvertI\right\rvert$. Viewed as a
    cochain on $\operatorname{Lk}(t)$, the cluster $H_r(t)$ lies at link level $r-i-1$.
    This cochain is minimal in the link. Indeed, a link coboundary extends to an ambient coboundary of a cochain supported in $\operatorname{Lk}(t)$. Since $H_r(t)\subseteq H_r$, any such correction decreasing the norm of $H_r(t)$ would also decrease the norm of $H_r$, contradicting local minimality.
    Applying \cref{thm:link-expansion-local} at this level and
    converting link norms to ambient norms by the factor $D_I^{-1}$ gives
    \[
        \left\lVert\delta H_r(t)\right\rVert
        \geq
        m_{k-i,r-i-1}\left\lVertH_r(t)\right\rVert-D_I^{-1}O_k\left(\varepsilon_\star\right) \geq m_{k,r-1}\left\lVertH_r(t)\right\rVert-D_I^{-1}O_k\left(\varepsilon_\star\right).
    \]
    We now show that the additive term is negligible compared with the mass of
    the terminal cluster. The heavy mass propagates, and \cref{cor:heavy-mass-prop} invoked with $H(t)$ instead of $H$ gives
    \begin{equation}
        \left\lVertH_r(t)\right\rVert
        \geq
        \frac{i!}{r!}
        \varepsilon_\star^{\nicefrac{1}{2^i}-\nicefrac{1}{2^r}}\left\lVertt\right\rVert.
                                                 
    \end{equation}
    As $\left\lVertt\right\rVert=D_I^{-1}$, the error term in $D_I^{-1}O_k\left(\varepsilon_\star\right)$ is bounded by
    \[
        D_I^{-1}O_k\left(\varepsilon_\star\right)
        \leq
        O_k\left(\varepsilon_\star^{\,1-\nicefrac{1}{2^i}+\nicefrac{1}{2^r}}\right)\left\lVertH_r(t)\right\rVert
        \leq
        O_k\left(\varepsilon_\star^{\,\nicefrac{1}{2^r}}\right)\left\lVertH_r(t)\right\rVert.
    \]
    Hence, we can replace the additive error by a multiplicative error and obtain
    \[
        \left\lVert\delta H_r(t)\right\rVert\geq \left(m_{k,r-1}-O_k\left(\varepsilon_\star^{\nicefrac{1}{2^r}}\right)\right)\left\lVertH_r(t)\right\rVert.
    \]
    Summing over all terminals yields
    \[
        \sum_{t\in\operatorname{Ter}}\left\lVert\delta H_r(t)\right\rVert
        \geq
        \left(m_{k,r-1}-O_k\left(\varepsilon_\star^{\nicefrac{1}{2^r}}\right)\right)
        \sum_{t\in\operatorname{Ter}}\left\lVertH_r(t)\right\rVert
        \geq
        \left(m_{k,r-1}-O_k\left(\varepsilon_\star^{\nicefrac{1}{2^r}}\right)\right)
        \left\lVertH_r\right\rVert,
    \]
    where the last inequality follows because every $x\in H_r$ belongs to
    the cluster of at least one terminal. Plugging this into
    \cref{eq:ltg-prop-2} concludes the proof.
<!-- end proof-18 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
With $c$ as in \eqref{eq:c}, \cref{prop:small-set-cosystolic-expansion}
implies that, at every level $0\leq r<k$, every locally minimal cochain
$H_r$ of norm at most $c\left\lVertV_r\right\rVert$ expands by a factor of at least
$m_{k,r-1}-O_k\left(\varepsilon_\star^{\Omega_k(1)}\right)$. Choosing $a = \min_{r}m_{k,r-1}$ concludes the proof.

<!-- definition-13: body_md -->
\label{def:ltc}
    For $s>0$, a classical linear code $\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_2^n$ is $s$-locally testable if it has a parity-check matrix $H\in\mathbb{F}_2^{m\times n}$ ($\ker(H)=\mathcal{C}$) with rows and columns of weight bounded by a constant such that for every word $w\in\mathbb{F}_2^n$,
    \[\left\lVertHw\right\rVert\geq s\cdot\operatorname{dist}(w,\mathcal{C})\]
    where, recall,  $\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathcal{C})=\min_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\left\lVertx-c\right\rVert$.
<!-- end definition-13 -->
<!-- definition-14: body_md -->
A quantum CSS code $\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}_X,\mathcal{C}_Z)$ is specified by two classical linear
codes $\mathcal{C}_X,\mathcal{C}_Z\leq\mathbb{F}_2^n$ satisfying
$\mathcal{C}_X^\perp\leq\mathcal{C}_Z$ (and thus also $\mathcal{C}_Z^\perp\leq\mathcal{C}_X$).
<!-- end definition-14 -->
<!-- definition-15: body_md -->
A quantum CSS code $(\mathcal{C}_X,\mathcal{C}_Z)$ is $s$-locally testable if both $\mathcal{C}_X$ and $\mathcal{C}_Z$ are.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{lem:flipping-algo-local-minimality}
    For an input cochain $f$, \Cref{def:flipping-algo} stops after at most $D_{[k]}\cdot\left\lVertf\right\rVert$ iterations and returns a locally-minimal cochain.
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
By the stopping condition, no correction supported in a bottom-vertex link decreases the output's norm. Every vertex $v$ has a bottom ancestor $u\in V_0$, with $\operatorname{Lk}(v)\subseteq\operatorname{Lk}(u)$. Thus every correction allowed by \cref{def:minimality} is also supported in a bottom-vertex link, so the output is locally minimal.

Every cochain norm is an integer multiple of $\alpha\coloneq D_{[k]}^{-1}$, since $D_I$ divides $D_{[k]}$ for every $I\subseteq[k]$. Each iteration strictly decreases the norm, hence decreases it by at least $\alpha$. Thus the algorithm stops after at most $\tfrac1\alpha\left\lVertf\right\rVert=D_{[k]}\left\lVertf\right\rVert$ iterations for an input cochain $f$.

<!-- theorem-9: body_md -->
\label{cor:qltc}
Let $X$ be a $(4,2)$-cubical complex. Assume $X$ and $X^{\mathrm{rev}}$ satisfy the assumptions in \cref{prop:small-set-cosystolic-expansion} with $\varepsilon_\star$ sufficiently small\footnote{$\varepsilon_\star$ needs to be small so that the inequality in \cref{prop:small-set-cosystolic-expansion} is meaningful.} and with stretch/shrinkage factors  $\mu_1,\mu_2\geq 4$, and $
\mu_3,\mu_4\leq\tfrac14$.

Then $\mathcal{C}(\ker{\delta^t_1},\ker{\delta_2}) $ is a quantum error-correcting code with constant rate, relative distance, and local testability soundness.
<!-- end theorem-9 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Every vertex in $V_3$ and in $V_1$ has at most a constant number of neighbors in $V_2$. Specifically, every row of $H_Z=\delta_2$ has weight at most $3\max_{i\in[4]}D_i'$, and every row of $H_X=\delta_1^t$ has weight at most $3\max_{i\in[4]}D_i$. Thus the checks have constant weight, at most $3\max_{i\in[4]}\{D_i,D_i'\}$. Similarly, every vertex in $V_2$ has at most $\sum_{i=1}^k D_i+D_i'$ neighbors in both $V_1,V_3$, meaning $H_Z,H_X$ have constant weight columns, or equivalently, every qubit participates in a constant number of checks.

    For the rate, by biregularity, $\left\lvertV_{I\cup\{i\}}\right\rvert=\mu_i\left\lvertV_I\right\rvert$ for $i\notin I$, so $\left\lvertV_I\right\rvert=\bigl(\prod_{i\in I}\mu_i\bigr)\left\lvertV_\emptyset\right\rvert$, as illustrated in \cref{fig:boolean-cube-rate}.
    To bound the rate, count qubits and checks
    \[
        \rho \geq  1 - \frac{\left\lvertV_1\right\rvert + \left\lvertV_3\right\rvert}{\left\lvertV_2\right\rvert} > 0.1.
    \]
    To bound both the local testability soundness and the relative distance, we apply the Greedy Flipping Algorithm, \cref{def:flipping-algo}, using both \cref{lem:flipping-algo-local-minimality} and \cref{prop:small-set-cosystolic-expansion}, to show the parameters are bounded below by positive constants. We give the full arguments for both parameters considering the $Z$-codes, $\mathcal{C}_Z=\ker(\delta_2)$ and $\mathcal{C}_Z/\mathcal{C}_X^\perp=\ker(\delta_2)/\operatorname{im}(\delta_1)$, respectively. The same arguments applied to the reversed complex give the local testability soundness and relative distance of the $X$-codes $\mathcal{C}_X=\ker(\delta^{\mathrm{rev}}_2)=\ker(\delta_1^t)$ and $\mathcal{C}_X/\mathcal{C}_Z^\perp=\ker(\delta^{\mathrm{rev}}_2)/\operatorname{im}(\delta^{\mathrm{rev}}_1)=\ker(\delta_1^t)/\operatorname{im}(\delta_2^t)$.

    For distance, let $f\in\ker(\delta_2)\setminus\operatorname{im}(\delta_1)$ represent a nonzero element in $\mathcal{C}_Z/\mathcal{C}_X^\perp$. Run the greedy flipping algorithm on it. Its output is $f'=f+\sum_{i=1}^r\delta_1(h_i)$, with $\left\lVertf'\right\rVert\leq\left\lVertf\right\rVert$, and is locally minimal by \cref{lem:flipping-algo-local-minimality}. Since $f'-f\in\operatorname{im}(\delta_1)=\mathcal{C}_X^\perp$, the cochains $f$ and $f'$ represent the same nonzero element, so $f'\neq0$. As $\delta_2(f')=0$, \cref{prop:small-set-cosystolic-expansion} gives $\left\lVertf\right\rVert\geq\left\lVertf'\right\rVert\geq c\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert$.

    For the local testability of $\mathcal{C}_Z=\ker(\delta_2)$, look at its syndrome layer $V_3$. Let $w\in C^2(X)$ be some word (not necessarily a codeword in the kernel of $\delta_2$). By adding the closest codeword to it with no change to its syndrome we may assume its closest codeword is $0$. We wish to show that its norm is bounded above by a constant multiple of the norm of its syndrome. Let $f_{\mathrm{syn}}\coloneq \delta_2(w)$ be its syndrome. Run the greedy flipping algorithm on $f_{\mathrm{syn}}$ and obtain $f_{\mathrm{syn}}'=\delta_2(w)+\sum_{i=1}^r\delta_2(h_i)$ which has at most the same norm and is locally minimal. As $C^2(X)\to C^3(X)\to C^4(X)$ is a cochain complex, $\delta_3(f_{\mathrm{syn}}')=\delta_3(\delta_2(w+\sum_{i=1}^r h_i))=0$. By \cref{prop:small-set-cosystolic-expansion} either $f_{\mathrm{syn}}'=0$ or it has large norm $\left\lVertf_{\mathrm{syn}}'\right\rVert\geq c\left\lVertV_3\right\rVert$. Divide into these two cases:

    \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\left\lVertf_{\mathrm{syn}}'\right\rVert\geq c\left\lVertV_3\right\rVert$. The algorithm never increases the norm, so $\left\lVertf_{\mathrm{syn}}\right\rVert\geq\left\lVertf_{\mathrm{syn}}'\right\rVert$. Also, $\operatorname{dist}(w,\mathcal{C}_Z)\leq\left\lVertw\right\rVert\leq\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert$. By biregularity, $\beta\coloneq\left\lVertV_3\right\rVert/\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert$ is a positive constant depending only on the degree parameters. Thus,
    \[
        \left\lVertf_{\mathrm{syn}}\right\rVert
        \geq \left\lVertf_{\mathrm{syn}}'\right\rVert
        \geq c\left\lVertV_3\right\rVert
        =c\beta\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert
        \geq c\beta\cdot\operatorname{dist}(w,\mathcal{C}_Z).
    \]
    This gives the desired soundness bound with $s=c\beta>0$ in this case.
    \item $f_{\mathrm{syn}}'=0$. Set $u=\sum_{i=1}^r h_i$. Since $\delta_2(w+u)=f_{\mathrm{syn}}'=0$, the corrected word $w+u$ belongs to $\mathcal{C}_Z$. Each change $h_i\in C^2(X)$ is supported on $\operatorname{Lk}_1(v)$ for some $v\in V_0$. This link level has six blocks, each of ambient norm at most $1$; hence $\left\lVerth_i\right\rVert\leq 6$. By \cref{lem:flipping-algo-local-minimality}, $r\leq D_{[k]}\cdot\left\lVertf_{\mathrm{syn}}\right\rVert$. Therefore,
    \[
        \operatorname{dist}(w,\mathcal{C}_Z)
        \leq \left\lVertu\right\rVert
        \leq \sum_{i=1}^r\left\lVerth_i\right\rVert
        \leq 6r
        \leq {6D_{[k]}}\left\lVertf_{\mathrm{syn}}\right\rVert.
    \]
    Rearranging gives $\left\lVertf_{\mathrm{syn}}\right\rVert\geq s\cdot\operatorname{dist}(w,\mathcal{C}_Z)$ with the constant $s=\frac{1}{6D_{[k]}}>0$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Together, the two cases give soundness $s_Z=\min\{c\beta,\frac{1}{6D_{[k]}}\}>0$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
Let $X$ be a $(4,2)$-cubical complex satisfying the assumptions for \cref{cor:qltc}.

    Denote by $c,\alpha$ the constants in the hypothesis and the conclusion of \cref{prop:small-set-cosystolic-expansion}, respectively. Let $e\in C^2(X)$ be a cochain close to $\ker(\delta_2)$ i.e. $\operatorname{dist}(e,\ker(\delta_2))\leq\min\{\frac{c}{2}\left\lVertV_3\right\rVert,\frac{c}{2}\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert-12,\frac{\alpha}{2}(\frac{c}{2}\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert-12)\}$. Then running \cref{alg:lin-time-flipping-algo} on $\delta_2(e)\in C^3(X)$ takes linear time in $\left\lVert\delta_2(e)\right\rVert$ (and thus linear in the number of qubits $\left\lvertV_2\right\rvert$) and outputs a change $\sum_{i=1}^r h_i$ such that $e+\sum_{i=1}^r h_i\in\ker(\delta_2)$ and there is a boundary $x\in\operatorname{im}(\delta_1)$ such that $e+\sum_{i=1}^r h_i+x$ is the closest cochain in $\ker(\delta_2)$ to $e$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Let $w\in \ker(\delta_2)$ be a closest one to $e$. We may replace $e$ with $e+w$ without loss of generality, by linearity and by $w$ not changing the syndrome. Then $0$ is a closest word in $\ker(\delta_2)$ to $e$ and we may use the norm as the relevant quantity to be zeroed-out by an equivalent word in $\mathcal{C}_Z/\mathcal{C}_X^\perp$ in order to show correctness. 
    
    Throughout this proof we will use notations to talk about the errors and syndromes after several iterations. Denote by $ e'_0\coloneq e$ the initial error and by $e_i'=e+\sum_{j=1}^ig_j$ the error after $i$ iterations of the while loop (line \ref{alg:linear-flipping line:while}), meagning $g_j$ is the sum of the changes $h_\ell$ whose cobaoundary were added during the $j$'th iteration. Denote by $e_i$ the minimal cochain in the class of $e'_i$ , i.e. $e_i\coloneq e'_i+\delta_1(x_i)$ for $x_i$ which minimizes its norm. Clearly $\left\lVerte_i\right\rVert\leq\left\lVerte'_i\right\rVert$. Denote the syndrome at that time by $f_i\coloneq\delta_2(e_i)=
    \delta_2(e'_i)$. The $e_i$-s are helpful for analysis, but the algorithm does not try to compute them at any point. It only accumulates the $e'_i$-s. 

    First we show the algorithm's linear time. Each iteration of the while loop (line \ref{alg:linear-flipping line:while}) is bounded by a constant number of steps, as the number of cochains in $C^1(\operatorname{Lk}(v))$ is bounded by a constant regardless of the vertex $v\in V_0$. We need to show the algorithm stops after linear number of iterations. $Q$ is initialized to have linear number of items in $\left\lVertf_0\right\rVert$, say at most $K_1\left\lVertf_0\right\rVert$. In each iteration either a vertex is taken out or the norm of the syndrome is reduced. In the latter case the norm is reduced by at least $D_{[k]}^{-1}$, a constant, and $Q$ is enlarged by at most another constant $K_2=\sum_{\substack{i_1\neq j_1\in[k]\\ i_2\neq j_2\in[k]}}D_{i_1}D_{j_1}D'_{i_2}D'_{j_2}$. Thus, in a linear number of steps (at most $(K_1+D_{[k]}K_2)\left\lVertf_0\right\rVert$) we have either an empty $Q$ or a zero $f$, each of which stops the while loop and ends the algorithm.   
    
    Next we show that the syndrome at the end is $0$. Let $t$ be the number of iterations made before the algorithm stopped.
    The syndrome is never increased by the algorithm $\left\lVertf_t\right\rVert\leq\left\lVertf_0\right\rVert$. Additionally, by \cref{eq:norm-propagation}, $\left\lVertf_0\right\rVert\leq2\left\lVerte'_0\right\rVert$ and by the proposition's hypothesis $\left\lVerte'_0\right\rVert\leq \frac{c}{2}\left\lVertV_3\right\rVert$. We will show $f_t$ must be locally minimal, which implies by \cref{prop:small-set-cosystolic-expansion} that it expands $0=\left\lVert\delta_3(\delta_2(e_t))\right\rVert=\left\lVert\delta_3(f_t)\right\rVert\geq\alpha\left\lVertf_t\right\rVert$ showing $f_t=0$. To see $f_t$ is locally minimal note the algorithm, before each iteration at line \ref{alg:linear-flipping line:while}, conserves the invariant: for every cochain $h\in C^2(X)$ that can reduce the syndrome $\left\lVertf_i+\delta_2(h)\right\rVert<\left\lVertf_i\right\rVert$ and is supported inside a single link $C^1(\operatorname{Lk}(v_h))$ for $v_h\in V_0$, the vertex $v_h$ is in $Q$.\footnote{There can be more than one such vertex -- for each such cochain assign one arbitrarily} This invariant clearly holds just before the first iteration, by the initialization. Assume the invariant holds before the $i$'th iteration. During this iteration the algorithm takes out one $v_i\in Q$ and examines all the cochains in $V_2$ supported inside its link. $f_i$ is the syndrome at the end of this iteration. Let $h'\in C^2(X)$ be a cochain that reduces $f_i$'s norm and that is supported on a single link $\operatorname{Lk}(v_{h'})$. Either $\delta_2(h')$ overlaps with the coboundary $\delta_2(h)$ of one of the cochains $h$ that were added to the overall change in line \ref{alg:linear-flipping line:add-change} during this iteration, or it does not overlap with any of them (including the case of having none during this iteration). If it does, then $v_{h'}\in\operatorname{Rts}(\operatorname{supp}(\delta_2(h)))$ and $v_{h'}$ was added to $Q$ in line \ref{alg:linear-flipping line:add-roots} during the iteration. Otherwise, its coboundary does not overlap with any bit that was changed from $f_{i-1}$ to $f_i$ and hence must decrease the norm of $f_{i-1}$ as well. In this case $v_{h'}\in Q$ before this iteration and $v_{h'}\neq v_i$ so it was not taken out from $Q$, showing it to still be in $Q$ after the iteration. 
    
    We will use this now to show the error at the end is also $0$. By regularity and the normalization of the norm, $\left\lVertf_i\right\rVert\leq2\left\lVerte_i\right\rVert$. On the other hand, for $\left\lVerte_i\right\rVert\leq c\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert$ we get by \cref{prop:small-set-cosystolic-expansion} $\left\lVerte_i\right\rVert\leq \frac1\alpha\left\lVertf_i\right\rVert$ for a constant $\alpha$. By induction we'll see the condition is satisfied for all $i$, showing the final error to be $0$. The induction will show and use something stronger: that $\left\lVerte_i\right\rVert\leq \frac{c}{2}\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert-\beta$ for the constant  $\beta\coloneq 12$ bounding the norm of all vertices in $V_2$ in a single link ($1$ for each monotone path of directions from $V_0$ to $V_2$, i.e. for each ordered pair $i\neq j\in[4]$), which is the maximal change in the error in a single iteration. The induction basis is given by the hypothesis of the proposition. For the step, by the induction hypothesis $\left\lVerte_{i-1}\right\rVert\leq \frac{c}{2}\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert-\beta$ and the fact that at most $\beta$ bits are changed from $e_{i-1}$ to $e_{i-1}+g$ for $g\in C^2(X)$ supported in a single link, which is equivalent to the minimal $e_i$, we get that $\left\lVerte_i\right\rVert\leq\left\lVerte_{i-1}+g\right\rVert\leq \frac{c}{2}\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert$. Thus by \cref{prop:small-set-cosystolic-expansion} we have $\left\lVerte_i\right\rVert \leq \frac1\alpha\left\lVertf_i\right\rVert \leq \frac1\alpha\left\lVertf_0\right\rVert \leq \frac2\alpha\left\lVerte_0\right\rVert \leq \frac2\alpha\frac{\alpha}{2}(\frac{c}{2}\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert-\beta)=\frac{c}{2}\left\lVertV_2\right\rVert-\beta$ where the second inequality is by the norm of the syndrome never increasing in iterations, and the last inequality is by the proposition's hypothesis.
