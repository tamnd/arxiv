<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:knapsack}
Define $\mathcal{P}_{ij}$ as in \eqref{eq:path}. Under \Cref{asm:improve}, problem~\eqref{eq:obj} is equivalent to
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:rcspp}
            \max_{p \in \mathcal{P}_{1, T+1}}\quad
        \sum_{(i, j) \in p} \boldsymbol R_{ij} \quad \mathrm{s.t.}~
        \sum_{(i, j) \in p} \boldsymbol{S}_{ij} \leq \epsilon.
    \end{equation}
where  $\boldsymbol R \in \mathbb{R}^{(T + 1) \times (T + 1)}$ and $\boldsymbol S \in \mathbb{R}^{(T + 1) \times (T+1)}$ denote the reward and safety cost matrices,
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
    \boldsymbol R_{ij}&\coloneqq
            \frac{j - i}{T} \cdot \mathbb{E}[V(\pi_i)],\\
    \boldsymbol S_{ij}&\coloneqq
    \begin{cases}
    \mathbb{P}\left( V(\pi_j) < V(\pi_i) \right), & \text{if } j \leq T
     \\
    0, & \text{if } j = T + 1.
    \end{cases}
    \end{aligned}
    \]

<!-- assumption-3: body_md -->
\label{ass:ito}
Let $\mu(\cdot): [0, 1] \to \mathbb R_+$ and $\sigma(\cdot): [0, 1] \to \mathbb R_+$ be two deterministic smooth functions, and $V_0$ be a constant. We assume that
    \begin{equation*}
        \begin{aligned}
        V(\pi_t)&=V_0+\frac{1}{T}\int_0^t \mu\left(\frac \tau T\right)\mathrm{d}\tau + \frac{1}{T}\int_0^t \sigma\left(\frac{\tau}{T}\right)\mathrm{d} W_\tau,
        \end{aligned}
    \end{equation*}
where $\{W_\tau\}_{\tau\geq0}$ is a standard Brownian motion.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:continuous}
Under \Cref{ass:ito}, as $T \to \infty$, Problem~\eqref{eq:obj} admits the following continuous leading-order approximation:
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:continuous}
        \begin{aligned}
        \min_{h_T(\cdot)}\quad&\int_0^1 \mu(u) h_T(u) \mathrm{d} u\\
        \mathrm{s.t.}
        \quad &
        \int_0^1
        \frac{
        \Phi\!\left(
        -\mu(u)
        \sqrt{h_T(u)} / \sigma(u)
        \right)
        }{
        h_T(u)
        }
        \mathrm{d} u
        \le
        \frac{\epsilon}{T}.
        \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:update-interval}
Define the following constants and functions,
    $$
    L_T = \log\left( \frac{T}{\epsilon} \right),
    \quad
    I(u) = \frac{\mu(u)^2}{2 \sigma(u)^2}.
    $$
Under the same assumptions as \Cref{lem:continuous}, there is
    $$
    h_T^\star(u) = \frac{1}{I(u)}\Big[ L_T-\frac32\log L_T+O(1) \Big].
    $$

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:risk}
Under the same assumptions and notations as in \Cref{thm:update-interval}, let
    $$
    A(u) = \frac{\mu(u)}{I(u)},
    \quad
    B = \int_{0}^1A(u) \mathrm{d} u.

Then, there is
    $$
    \mathbb{P}\left\{ V(\pi_{t_{k+1}}) < V(\pi_{t_k}) \right\} = \Theta \left( \frac{\epsilon L_T}{TB}

    \right),

where $u_k \coloneqq t_{k} / T$ denotes the normalized update times.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The deterministic meta-policy class is finite, and its no-update member is feasible, so a maximizer exists. We show how to transform any feasible meta-policy into a newest-policy schedule without decreasing its objective or increasing its risk.

First, remove backward switches. Suppose a schedule switches from candidate $i$ to candidate $j<i$, and its next switch, if any, is from $j$ to $\ell$. We delete the switch to $j$ and retain $i$ until that next switch. On the intervening periods, the expected value weakly increases because $\nu_i\geq\nu_j$. For every realized candidate-value vector,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-triangle}
\mathbbm 1\{V_\ell<V_i\}
\leq \mathbbm 1\{V_j<V_i\}+\mathbbm 1\{V_\ell<V_j\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, if both events on the right fail, then $V_\ell\geq V_j\geq V_i$. Thus the new direct transition has risk no larger than the sum of the two removed risks. If there is no later switch, deleting the last switch removes a nonnegative risk. A resulting transition from $i$ to itself is a hold and has zero risk. Each operation removes at least one switch, so repetition terminates with a schedule whose deployed candidate indices are strictly increasing.

Write these indices as $1=j_0<j_1<\cdots<j_K$, and let $s_k\geq j_k$ be their original deployment times. We now advance the deployment of candidate $j_k$ to time $j_k$, processing the switches in increasing order. The preceding switch has already been advanced to $j_{k-1}<j_k$, and the next switch remains later than $s_k\geq j_k$, so this change preserves the order and availability constraints. It replaces the preceding, lower-index candidate by $j_k$ on additional periods, weakly increasing expected value. The ordered pairs of candidates compared at actual switches do not change, so neither does the total risk. The final schedule either retains its incumbent or deploys the candidate trained at the current time. Applying this transformation to a maximizer proves the claim.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Given a newest-policy schedule, form the path $P=((t_0,t_1),\ldots,(t_K,t_{K+1}))$ from $1$ to $T+1$. On the edge $(t_k,t_{k+1})$, we deploy $\pi_{t_k}$ for exactly $h_k$ periods. Therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-path-identities}
J_T(P)=\frac1T\sum_{k=0}^K h_k\nu_{t_k}
      =\sum_{(i,j)\in P}\boldsymbol{R}_{ij},
\qquad
S_T(P)=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}p_{t_k,t_{k+1}}
      =\sum_{(i,j)\in P}\boldsymbol{S}_{ij}.
\end{equation}
The second equality follows from linearity of expectation applied to the unsafe-update indicators.

Conversely, every source--sink path in the time-ordered graph has strictly increasing vertices and defines a valid newest-policy schedule by retaining its current vertex's candidate until the next vertex. The terminal edge retains the last candidate through period $T$. The two identities in \eqref{eq:app-path-identities} again hold. Thus paths and newest-policy schedules have the same rewards and risks, and \Cref{lem:ss} establishes equivalence with \eqref{eq:obj}. The direct path $(1,T+1)$ represents no updates. For a zero-length subpath, we use $\mathcal P_{ii}=\{\varnothing\}$, with zero reward and zero resource consumption.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:app-dp}
With $B_\Delta=\lfloor\epsilon/\Delta\rfloor$ and $c_{ij}=\lceil\widehat{\boldsymbol{S}}_{ij}/\Delta\rceil$, where $c_{i,T+1}=0$, \Cref{alg:main} maximizes the estimated objective over paths satisfying $\sum_{(i,j)\in P}c_{ij}\leq B_\Delta$. Given the edge estimates, its time and memory requirements are
\[
O\!\left(T^2(B_\Delta+1)\right),
\qquad
O\!\left(T^2+T(B_\Delta+1)\right),
\]
respectively, when the edge matrices and backtracking information are stored.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We prove by induction in $j$ that $F(j,b)$ is the largest estimated reward of a path from $1$ to $j$ with rounded cost at most $b$. The initialization $F(1,b)=0$ gives the assertion at the source. A path reaching $j>1$ has a unique last edge $(i,j)$, where $i<j$. Removing that edge leaves a path with available budget $b-c_{ij}$. By the induction hypothesis, its reward is at most $F(i,b-c_{ij})$. Conversely, a path attaining a finite predecessor state followed by $(i,j)$ is a valid path to $j$ with the prescribed budget. Taking the maximum over predecessors gives the Bellman recursion in the main paper. Induction proves the state interpretation, and backtracking from $(T+1,B_\Delta)$ recovers an optimizer.

There are $(T+1)(B_\Delta+1)$ states, with at most $T$ predecessor comparisons per state. The state values and predecessor pointers require $O(T(B_\Delta+1))$ memory, and the edge matrices require $O(T^2)$ memory. Forming all pairwise empirical risks from $N$ value trajectories separately costs $O(NT^2)$ time. The source--sink edge has zero cost, so the rounded problem remains feasible even when $B_\Delta=0$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:app-grid-error}
Suppose, on an event $\mathcal A$, that
\[
\max_i|\widehat\nu_i-\nu_i|\leq r,
\qquad
\max_{i<j\leq T}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{S}}_{ij}-p_{ij}|\leq s.
\]
Let $\widehat P_\Delta$ solve the rounded empirical problem within $\mathcal M_{T,\bar K}$, and put
\[
g_\Delta=\epsilon-\Delta\lfloor\epsilon/\Delta\rfloor,
\qquad
b_\Delta=\bar K(s+\Delta)+g_\Delta.
\]
On $\mathcal A$, we have $S_T(\widehat P_\Delta)\leq\epsilon+\bar K s$. If $b_\Delta\leq\epsilon$, then
\begin{align}
\label{eq:app-grid-sandwich}
J^\star_{T,\bar K}(\epsilon-b_\Delta)-r
&\leq\widehat J_T(\widehat P_\Delta)
\leq J^\star_{T,\bar K}(\epsilon+\bar K s)+r,\\
\label{eq:app-grid-oracle}
J_T(\widehat P_\Delta)
&\geq J^\star_{T,\bar K}(\epsilon-b_\Delta)-2r.
\end{align}
In particular, for $\omega_\epsilon(b)=J^\star_{T,\bar K}(\epsilon)-J^\star_{T,\bar K}(\epsilon-b)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-grid-modulus}
J^\star_{T,\bar K}(\epsilon)-J_T(\widehat P_\Delta)
\leq 2r+\omega_\epsilon(b_\Delta).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For every schedule, its holding fractions are nonnegative and sum to one. Hence, simultaneously over all paths,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-reward-uniform}
|\widehat J_T(P)-J_T(P)|
\leq \sum_{(i,j)\in P}\frac{j-i}{T}|\widehat\nu_i-\nu_i|
\leq r.
\end{equation}
There is no factor $\bar K$ in this reward bound. Rounded feasibility implies
\[
S_T(\widehat P_\Delta)
\leq\sum_{e\in\widehat P_\Delta}\widehat{\boldsymbol{S}}_e+\bar K s
\leq\Delta B_\Delta+\bar K s
\leq\epsilon+\bar K s.
\]
For a comparator $P$ with $S_T(P)\leq\epsilon-b_\Delta$, upward rounding on its actual transitions gives
\[
\Delta\sum_{e\in P}c_e
\leq S_T(P)+\bar K s+\bar K\Delta
\leq\epsilon-g_\Delta=\Delta B_\Delta.
\]
The terminal edge contributes no rounding error. Thus every such population comparator is feasible for the rounded empirical problem. Empirical optimality and \eqref{eq:app-reward-uniform} give the left inequality in \eqref{eq:app-grid-sandwich} and \eqref{eq:app-grid-oracle}. The risk bound for the selected path gives the right inequality. Subtracting \eqref{eq:app-grid-oracle} from the population frontier at $\epsilon$ proves \eqref{eq:app-grid-modulus}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:app-labeling}
\Cref{alg:app-labeling} returns an exact maximizer of the unrounded problem for its chosen edge quantities. If $A_j$ labels are generated at node $j$ before pruning and $L_j$ survive, an implementation using sorting requires
\[
O\!\left(T^2+\sum_{j=2}^{T+1}A_j\log(A_j+1)\right)
\]
time and $O(T^2+\sum_jL_j)$ persistent storage, in addition to temporary storage for the labels being processed. The numbers of labels can be exponential in the size of a general resource-constrained acyclic path instance.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Any suffix from a node adds the same resource and reward to two labels at that node. Extending the dominating label therefore yields no larger resource and no smaller reward. Discarding a dominated prefix cannot remove the sole representative of a better feasible complete path. Nonnegative resources likewise justify discarding an over-budget prefix. Induction over the node order now shows that every feasible prefix is represented or dominated by a retained label. At the sink, selecting the largest reward is therefore exact.

Generating the incoming labels costs $O(A_j)$ operations. After sorting by resource, we keep a label only when its reward exceeds the best reward at a no-larger resource; equal-resource ties are resolved by retaining the largest reward. This costs $O(A_j\log(A_j+1))$ time. The stated storage follows from the retained labels and their pointers. To see the worst-case obstruction, consider a chain of binary-choice gadgets with resource and reward increments both equal to $2^{-k}$ for taking choice $k$, and zero for skipping it. Under a sufficiently large budget, every subset generates a different pair $(s,s)$. None dominates another, so there are exponentially many nondominated labels.

<!-- assumption-4: body_md -->
\label{ass:app-historical}
The vectors $(V_1^{(n)},\ldots,V_T^{(n)})$, $n\geq1$, are independent and identically distributed copies of the future candidate-value vector and are independent of that future vector. Every coordinate lies in $[0,1]$. Dependence between coordinates within a vector is unrestricted.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:app-concentration}
For $N$ independent $[0,1]$ variables with common mean $v$ and average $\bar X$, and $N$ independent Bernoulli variables with mean $p$ and average $\bar B$, we have, for $\ell>0$ and $a=\ell/N$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-concentration}
\mathbb P\!\left(|\bar X-v|>\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{2N}}\right)\leq2e^{-\ell},
\qquad
\mathbb P\!\left(|\bar B-p|>\sqrt{2pa}+\frac{2a}{3}\right)\leq2e^{-\ell}.
\end{equation}
The function $U_a(x)=\min\{1,x+\sqrt{2ax}+4a\}$ is nondecreasing on $[0,1]$. Whenever $|x-p|\leq\sqrt{2pa}+2a/3$, it satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-U-sandwich}
p\leq U_a(x)\leq p+2\sqrt{2pa}+7a.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For a variable $X\in[0,1]$, the second derivative of $k(\lambda)=\log\mathbb E[e^{\lambda X}]$ is its variance under exponential tilting, which is at most $1/4$. Integrating twice gives $\log\mathbb E[e^{\lambda(X-v)}]\leq\lambda^2/8$. Independence and exponential Markov bounds, optimized over positive and negative $\lambda$, give the first inequality in \eqref{eq:app-concentration}.

For a Bernoulli variable $B$, we have
\[
\log\mathbb E[e^{\lambda(B-p)}]
\leq p(e^\lambda-1-\lambda)
\leq\frac{p\lambda^2}{2(1-|\lambda|/3)},\qquad |\lambda|<3.
\]
The last bound follows by expanding the exponential and using $k!\geq2\cdot3^{k-2}$ for $k\geq2$; the negative-argument bound follows as well. For $p>0$, optimization with $\lambda=t/(p+t/3)$ yields
\[
\mathbb P(|\bar B-p|>t)\leq2\exp\!\left(-\frac{Nt^2}{2(p+t/3)}\right).
\]
At $t=\sqrt{2pa}+2a/3$, direct expansion gives $t^2\geq2a(p+t/3)$, proving the second inequality. If $p=0$, the assertion is immediate.

The map $U_a$ is nondecreasing. Solving $p-x\leq\sqrt{2pa}+2a/3$ as a quadratic inequality in $\sqrt p$ gives
\[
p\leq\left(\sqrt{a/2}+\sqrt{x+7a/6}\right)^2
=x+\frac{5a}{3}+\sqrt{2ax+\frac{7a^2}{3}}
\leq x+\sqrt{2ax}+4a.
\]
Clipping at one preserves the lower bound. Also, $x\leq p+\sqrt{2pa}+2a/3\leq(\sqrt p+\sqrt a)^2$, and hence
\[
U_a(x)-p\leq(x-p)+\sqrt{2ax}+4a
\leq2\sqrt{2pa}+(2/3+\sqrt2+4)a
\leq2\sqrt{2pa}+7a.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:app-U-sandwich}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:app-anytime}
Under \Cref{ass:app-historical}, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-anytime-budget}
b_N=2\sqrt{2\bar K\epsilon a_N}+7\bar K a_N+\bar K\Delta+g_\Delta.
\end{equation}
With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for every integer $N\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-anytime-guarantee}
S_T(\widehat P_N)\leq\epsilon,
\qquad
J_T(\widehat P_N)\geq J^\star_{T,\bar K}(\epsilon-b_N)-2r_N
\quad\text{whenever }b_N\leq\epsilon.
\end{equation}
The no-update schedule is feasible for every historical dataset. The same guarantees apply at any almost surely finite, data-dependent stopping sample size. Including the randomness of the historical data and the independent future trajectory, the probability of at least one unsafe future update is at most $\epsilon+\delta$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
At sample size $N$, apply \Cref{lem:app-concentration} to all $T$ candidate coordinates and all $M$ actual edge indicators. A union bound with \eqref{eq:app-anytime-costs} gives failure probability at most $\delta_N$. Since $\sum_{N\geq1}\delta_N=\delta$, a second union bound gives an event $\mathcal A_\infty$ of probability at least $1-\delta$ on which, for every $N$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-all-N-event}
\max_i|\widehat\nu_{i,N}-\nu_i|\leq r_N,
\qquad
p_e\leq U_{e,N}\leq p_e+2\sqrt{2p_ea_N}+7a_N
\quad(e\text{ an actual edge}).
\end{equation}
The estimates at different sample sizes need not be independent.

On this event, every rounded-feasible path satisfies
\[
S_T(P)\leq\sum_{e\in P}U_{e,N}
\leq\Delta\sum_{e\in P}\left\lceil U_{e,N}/\Delta\right\rceil
\leq\Delta B_\Delta\leq\epsilon.
\]
This is simultaneous over paths and therefore holds for $\widehat P_N$. For a comparator with at most $\bar K$ updates and true risk $S$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-certification-cost}
\sum_{e\in P}U_{e,N}
\leq S+2\sqrt{2a_N}\sum_{e\in P,\ e\text{ actual}}\sqrt{p_e}+7\bar K a_N
\leq S+2\sqrt{2\bar K S a_N}+7\bar K a_N.
\end{equation}
If $S\leq\epsilon-b_N$, substituting $S\leq\epsilon$ in the square root and adding at most $\bar K\Delta$ for rounding proves empirical feasibility of the comparator. The terminal edge has zero cost throughout. The uniform reward bound \eqref{eq:app-reward-uniform}, with $r=r_N$, then gives
\[
J_T(\widehat P_N)\geq\widehat J_T(\widehat P_N)-r_N
\geq\widehat J_T(P^\star)-r_N
\geq J_T(P^\star)-2r_N,
\]
where $P^\star$ optimizes the reduced-budget population problem. This proves the oracle inequality. All statements hold on the same event for all $N$, so evaluating them at a stopping sample size introduces no additional failure probability.

Conditional on historical data in $\mathcal A_\infty$, the selected schedule is fixed relative to the future trajectory. The union bound for its unsafe events gives a conditional probability at most $S_T(\widehat P_N)\leq\epsilon$. On the complementary event, we bound this probability by one. Averaging yields at most $\epsilon+\delta$. The direct terminal edge proves feasibility on every dataset.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:app-evaluation}
Fix $N$. Suppose \Cref{ass:app-historical} holds for the true values, and evaluation estimates $\widetilde V_i^{(n)}$ satisfy an event
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-eval-event}
\mathcal A_\eta=\left\{\max_{n\leq N,\,i\leq T}|\widetilde V_i^{(n)}-V_i^{(n)}|\leq\eta\right\},
\qquad \mathbb P(\mathcal A_\eta)\geq1-\delta_{\mathrm{eval}},
\end{equation}
for a specified deterministic $\eta\geq0$. Use the conservative labels
\[
C_{ij}^{(n)}=\mathbf1\{\widetilde V_j^{(n)}-\widetilde V_i^{(n)}\leq2\eta\}
\]
in the empirical upper confidence costs. In this proposition set
\[
\ell_N=\log\frac{2(2M+T)}{\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}},\qquad
a_N=\ell_N/N,\qquad r_N=\sqrt{\ell_N/(2N)},\qquad
\zeta_T(\eta)=\max_{i<j\leq T}\mathbb P(0\leq V_j-V_i\leq4\eta).
\]
With
\[
b_{N,\eta}=\bar K\zeta_T(\eta)
+2\sqrt{2\bar K\{\epsilon+\bar K\zeta_T(\eta)\}a_N}
+7\bar K a_N+\bar K\Delta+g_\Delta,
\]
the selected schedule satisfies, with probability at least $1-\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}-\delta_{\mathrm{eval}}$,
\[
S_T(\widehat P)\leq\epsilon,
\qquad
J_T(\widehat P)\geq J^\star_{T,\bar K}(\epsilon-b_{N,\eta})-2(r_N+\eta)
\quad\text{if }b_{N,\eta}\leq\epsilon.
\]
No independence between the evaluation errors is required beyond \eqref{eq:app-eval-event}.

For each actual edge, define the true labels $B_{ij}^{(n)}=\mathbf1\{V_j^{(n)}-V_i^{(n)}<0\}$ and $D_{ij}^{(n)}=\mathbf1\{V_j^{(n)}-V_i^{(n)}\leq4\eta\}$. On $\mathcal A_\eta$, the deterministic inequalities

\label{eq:app-label-brackets}
B_{ij}^{(n)}\leq C_{ij}^{(n)}\leq D_{ij}^{(n)}
\end{equation}
hold. Their true means satisfy $p_{ij}\leq p^+_{ij}\leq p_{ij}+\zeta_T(\eta)$. Apply \Cref{lem:app-concentration} to the $2M$ collections of true $B$ and $D$ labels and the $T$ true value coordinates. These collections are independent across historical replications even when the estimated labels share evaluation data. A union bound produces an event of probability at least $1-\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}$.

p_e\leq U_{a_N}(\bar B_e)\leq U_{a_N}(\bar C_e)
\leq U_{a_N}(\bar D_e)
\leq p_e^++2\sqrt{2p_e^+a_N}+7a_N.
\]
Thus the implemented costs upper-bound every population risk. For a comparator of true risk $S$, summing the upper bounds yields
\[
\sum_{e\in P}U_{a_N}(\bar C_e)
\leq S+\bar K\zeta_T(\eta)
+2\sqrt{2\bar K\{S+\bar K\zeta_T(\eta)\}a_N}+7\bar K a_N.
\]
The definition of $b_{N,\eta}$ and upward rounding therefore make every comparator at budget $\epsilon-b_{N,\eta}$ empirically feasible. Each estimated empirical value mean is within $r_N+\eta$ of its population mean, so the reward comparison in \Cref{thm:app-anytime} gives the oracle bound. A union bound over the two events completes the proof.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:app-normal}
Let $c_0=(2\sqrt\pi)^{-1}$. The functions $Q$ and $f$ are strictly decreasing and strictly convex on $(0,\infty)$. As $x\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:app-normal-tail}
Q(x)&=c_0e^{-x}x^{-1/2}\{1+O(x^{-1})\},\\
f(x)&=c_0e^{-x}x^{-3/2}\{1+O(x^{-1})\},\qquad -f'(x)=c_0e^{-x}x^{-3/2}\{1+O(x^{-1})\}.\nonumber
\end{align}
Consequently $f(x)/(-f'(x))\to1$ and $f''(x)/(-f'(x))\to1$. Moreover, $-f'$ decreases continuously from $+\infty$ to zero.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
For the standard normal density $\phi$, integration by parts gives
$$
1-\Phi(z)=\frac{\phi(z)}z-\int_z^\infty\frac{\phi(y)}{y^2}\,\mathrm{d} y,
\qquad
\frac{z\phi(z)}{1+z^2}\leq1-\Phi(z)\leq\frac{\phi(z)}z.
$$
Substituting $z=\sqrt{2x}$ proves the expansion for $Q$. Direct differentiation yields
$$
Q'(x)=-c_0e^{-x}x^{-1/2},\qquad Q''(x)=c_0e^{-x}\left(x^{-1/2}+\frac12x^{-3/2}\right),
$$
$$
f'(x)=\frac{Q'(x)}x-\frac{Q(x)}{x^2}<0,
\qquad
f''(x)=\frac{Q''(x)}x-\frac{2Q'(x)}{x^2}+\frac{2Q(x)}{x^3}>0.
$$
These identities prove the remaining expansions and ratios. Since $Q(x)\to1/2$ as $x\downarrow0$, we have $-f'(x)\to\infty$ there; \eqref{eq:app-normal-tail} gives its limit zero at infinity. Strict convexity gives the asserted monotonicity.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:app-intervals}
For an interval $[s,s+h]\subseteq[0,T]$, define
$$
M_{s,h}=\int_s^{s+h}\mu(v/T)\,\mathrm{d} v,
\qquad
Q^{\mathrm{var}}_{s,h}=\int_s^{s+h}\sigma(v/T)^2\,\mathrm{d} v,
\qquad
\mathcal I_{s,h}=\frac{M_{s,h}^2}{2Q^{\mathrm{var}}_{s,h}}.
$$
Its exact unsafe-update probability is $q_T(s,h)=Q(\mathcal I_{s,h})$. Uniformly in admissible $s,h$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-local-expansions}
M_{s,h}=\mu(s/T)h+O(h^2/T),\qquad Q^{\mathrm{var}}_{s,h}=\sigma(s/T)^2h+O(h^2/T),\qquad \mathcal I_{s,h}=I(s/T)h+O(h^2/T).
\end{equation}
Also,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-global-exponent}
\mathcal I_{s,h}\leq\int_s^{s+h}I(v/T)\,\mathrm{d} v\leq h\max_{v\in[s,s+h]}I(v/T).
\end{equation}
For fixed $0<a<d<\infty$ and $aL\leq h\leq dL$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-relative-local}
q_T(s,h)=q(s/T,h)\{1+O(L^2/T)\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Under \Cref{ass:ito}, the interval increment is Gaussian with mean $M_{s,h}/T$ and variance $Q^{\mathrm{var}}_{s,h}/T^2$, proving the exact probability formula. Bounded first derivatives give the first two expansions in \eqref{eq:app-local-expansions}. Since $Q^{\mathrm{var}}_{s,h}\geq\sigma_-^2h$, division gives the exponent expansion. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$$
M_{s,h}^2\leq\left(\int_s^{s+h}\sigma(v/T)^2\,\mathrm{d} v\right)\left(\int_s^{s+h}\frac{\mu(v/T)^2}{\sigma(v/T)^2}\,\mathrm{d} v\right),
$$
which proves \eqref{eq:app-global-exponent}. Finally, on $h\asymp L$, the two exponent arguments differ by $O(L^2/T)=o(1)$ and both tend to infinity. By \Cref{lem:app-normal}, $|(\log Q)'|$ is bounded on these arguments. Applying the mean value theorem to $\log Q$ proves \eqref{eq:app-relative-local}.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:app-competitive}
There is a feasible integer schedule with regret $O(L/T)$. Every integer schedule satisfying $\mathcal R_T(P)\leq C_1L/T$, for a fixed $C_1$, obeys
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-mesh}
\sum_{k=0}^{K}h_k^2=O(TL),\qquad \max_k h_k=O(\sqrt{TL}),\qquad \max_k h_k/T\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Let $I_{\min}=\min_{u\in[0,1]}I(u)>0$ and take full integer holds of length $\lceil L/I_{\min}\rceil$, followed by a terminal remainder. By \Cref{lem:app-intervals}, the exponent of every actual update is at least $L-O(L^2/T)$. The normal-tail upper bound gives probability at most $CL^{-1/2}e^{-L}$ per update. There are at most $CT/L$ updates, so their total risk is at most $C\epsilon_T/L^{3/2}\leq\epsilon_T$ for sufficiently large $T$. Bounded drift and holding lengths $O(L)$ give regret $O(L/T)$.

For any integer schedule, positivity of the drift gives $\mathbb E[V_T(s+j)-V_T(s)]\geq\mu_-j/T$. Hence
$$
\mathcal R_T(P)\geq\frac{\mu_-}{2T^2}\sum_{k=0}^{K}h_k(h_k-1).
$$
Since $\sum_kh_k=T$, the assumed regret bound yields $\sum_kh_k^2=O(TL)$. The maximum is at most the square root of this sum, which proves the remaining statements.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
We solve \eqref{eq:app-allocation-x} for $b\in[\epsilon_T/2,\epsilon_T]$ and then take $b=\epsilon_T$. \textcolor{black}{This argument concerns the functional problem itself and does not use the optimized comparison \eqref{eq:app-leading-value}.}

\paragraph{(I) Pointwise minimization.} The Lagrangian is
$$
\mathcal L(x,\lambda)=\int_0^1\big\{A(u)x(u)+\lambda I(u)f(x(u))\big\}\,\mathrm{d} u-\lambda\frac bT.
$$
For a fixed $\lambda>0$, the integrand contains no terms coupling different values of $u$, so we minimize it separately at each $u$. By \Cref{lem:app-normal}, it is strictly convex in $x$ and diverges as $x\downarrow0$ or $x\uparrow\infty$. Its unique minimizer $x_\lambda(u)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-stationarity}
A(u)+\lambda I(u)f'(x_\lambda(u))=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
-f'(x_\lambda(u))=\frac{\mu(u)}{\lambda I(u)^2}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{(II) Binding constraint.} Since $-f'$ is a strictly decreasing bijection from $(0,\infty)$ onto $(0,\infty)$, the solution $x_\lambda(u)$ is continuous in $u$, strictly increasing in $\lambda$, and tends uniformly to zero or infinity as $\lambda$ tends to zero or infinity, respectively. The coefficient ratios are bounded above and away from zero. It follows that $\int_0^1I(u)f(x_\lambda(u))\,\mathrm{d} u$ decreases continuously from infinity to zero. There is therefore a unique $\lambda_b>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-binding-budget}
\int_0^1I(u)f(x_{\lambda_b}(u))\,\mathrm{d} u=\frac bT.
\end{equation}
Write $x_b=x_{\lambda_b}$. For every feasible $x$, pointwise optimality gives
$$
A(u)x(u)+\lambda_bI(u)f(x(u))\geq A(u)x_b(u)+\lambda_bI(u)f(x_b(u)).
$$
Integrating this inequality and using feasibility and \eqref{eq:app-binding-budget} yields $\int_0^1Ax\geq\int_0^1Ax_b$. Strict convexity gives uniqueness up to null sets. Thus $h_b=x_b/I$ is the continuous representative of the optimizer of \eqref{eq:app-Fc}.

\paragraph{(III) Multiplier and waiting-time rates.} As $b/T\to0$, the binding equation forces $\lambda_b\to\infty$, and hence $x_b(u)\to\infty$ uniformly. Substituting \eqref{eq:app-stationarity} into \eqref{eq:app-binding-budget} gives
\begin{align}
\label{eq:app-multiplier}
\frac bT
&=\int_0^1 I(u)\big[-f'(x_b(u))\big]\frac{f(x_b(u))}{-f'(x_b(u))}\,\mathrm{d} u\\
&=\frac1{\lambda_b}\int_0^1A(u)\frac{f(x_b(u))}{-f'(x_b(u))}\,\mathrm{d} u
=\frac B{\lambda_b}\{1+o(1)\}.\nonumber
\end{align}
Consequently $\lambda_b\sim BT/b$ and $\log\lambda_b=L_b+O(1)$, where $L_b=\log(T/b)=L+O(1)$. Taking logarithms in \eqref{eq:app-stationarity} and applying \eqref{eq:app-normal-tail} yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-log-root}
x_b(u)+\frac32\log x_b(u)
=\log\lambda_b+\log\!\left(\frac{c_0I(u)^2}{\mu(u)}\right)+O(x_b(u)^{-1})
=L_b+O(1).
\end{equation}
The error is uniform in $u$. First, this identity gives $x_b(u)=L_b+O(\log L_b)$: for large $x_b$, it bounds $x_b$ above by $L_b+C$, and substitution into its logarithm gives the corresponding lower bound. Therefore
$$
\log x_b(u)=\log L_b+O(\log L_b/L_b).
$$
Substituting this back into \eqref{eq:app-log-root} proves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-optimizer-expansion}
x_b(u)=L_b-\frac32\log L_b+O(1),\qquad
h_b(u)=\frac1{I(u)}\left[L_b-\frac32\log L_b+O(1)\right].
\end{equation}
Taking $b=\epsilon_T$ proves \Cref{thm:update-interval}. In particular, $h_b\asymp L$ uniformly, and integration gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-Fc-expansion}
F_T^{\mathrm c}(b)=\int_0^1A(u)x_b(u)\,\mathrm{d} u
=B\left[L_b-\frac32\log L_b\right]+O(1).
\end{equation}
For later use, differentiate the logarithm of \eqref{eq:app-stationarity}:
$$
-\frac{f''(x_b(u))}{-f'(x_b(u))}x_b'(u)
=\frac{\mu'(u)}{\mu(u)}-2\frac{I'(u)}{I(u)}.
$$
By \Cref{lem:app-normal} and the coefficient bounds, $x_b'=O(1)$ and $h_b'=O(L)$, uniformly for $b\in[\epsilon_T/2,\epsilon_T]$.

Consider a full interval generated by $h_b$, for $b\in[\epsilon_T/2,\epsilon_T]$. Its length is $h_b(u_k)$ for the continuous construction, or $\lceil h_b(u_k)\rceil$ for its integer realization. We first evaluate the local probability before rounding. By \eqref{eq:app-stationarity} and \eqref{eq:app-multiplier},
\begin{align}
\label{eq:app-local-risk-order}
q(u,h_b(u))
&=Q(x_b(u))=x_b(u)f(x_b(u))\\
&=\frac{x_b(u)\mu(u)}{\lambda_bI(u)^2}\frac{f(x_b(u))}{-f'(x_b(u))}
=\Theta\!\left(\frac{\epsilon_T L}{TB}\frac{\mu(u)}{I(u)^2}\right).\nonumber
\end{align}
Here $x_b\asymp L$, $\lambda_b\asymp TB/b$, $b\asymp\epsilon_T$, and the ratio $f/(-f')$ is bounded above and away from zero for large $T$.

Write an integer length as $j=h_b(u)+d$ with $0\leq d<1$. Its local exponent changes from $x_b(u)$ to $x_b(u)+I(u)d$. The normal-tail formula gives
$$
\frac{Q(x_b(u)+I(u)d)}{Q(x_b(u))}
=e^{-I(u)d}\left(\frac{x_b(u)}{x_b(u)+I(u)d}\right)^{1/2}\{1+O(L^{-1})\}.
$$
This ratio is bounded above and away from zero uniformly in $u,d$. Since both full interval lengths are of order $L$, \eqref{eq:app-relative-local} then proves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-per-update-risk}
\mathbb P\big\{V_T(t_{k+1})<V_T(t_k)\big\}
=\Theta\!\left(\frac{\epsilon_T L}{TB}\frac{\mu(u_k)}{I(u_k)^2}\right),\qquad k=0,\ldots,K-1.
\end{equation}
Reversing the index shift gives the main paper's policy-value notation for integer updates. The feasible integer schedule used in the regret bound below is the rounded construction with $b=\epsilon_T/2$, so it satisfies the same risk order.

<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Let $z_L=L-\tfrac32\log L$ and $D=\int_0^1I(u)\,\mathrm{d} u$. We first match a lower bound over feasible integer schedules with a feasible construction. We then establish the leading count for an optimal integer schedule.

\paragraph{(I) Lower bound by weighted Jensen's inequality.} By \Cref{lem:app-competitive}, we only need to consider schedules with regret at most $C_1L/T$, for a sufficiently large fixed $C_1$. For each risk-bearing hold, define
$$
\mu_k^- =\min_{v\in[t_k,t_{k+1}]}\mu(v/T),\qquad
I_k^+ =\max_{v\in[t_k,t_{k+1}]}I(v/T),\qquad
x_k=I_k^+h_k,\qquad
w_k=\frac{h_k}{T}\frac{\mu_k^-}{I_k^+}.
$$
Put $W_T=\sum_{k<K}w_k$ and $Z_T=\sum_{k<K}w_kx_k$. Coefficient smoothness and the mesh bound imply
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-weight-total}
W_T=B+O\!\left(\frac{\sum_kh_k^2}{T^2}+\frac{h_K}{T}\right)
=B+O\!\left(\sqrt{L/T}\right).
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\mu_k^-/I_k^+$ differs from $A(u)$ by at most $Ch_k/T$ within its interval, and the omitted terminal interval has width $h_K/T$. In particular, $W_T$ is positive and bounded away from zero for large $T$.

The exact Gaussian exponent bound \eqref{eq:app-global-exponent} gives $q_T(t_k,h_k)\geq Q(x_k)$. Let $I_- =\min I>0$ and $d_0=I_-^2/\mu_+$. Then
\begin{align}
\label{eq:app-weighted-risk}
S_T(P)
&\geq\sum_{k<K}Q(x_k)
=T\sum_{k<K}\frac{h_k}{T}I_k^+f(x_k)\\
&=T\sum_{k<K}w_k\frac{(I_k^+)^2}{\mu_k^-}f(x_k)
\geq d_0T W_T f\!\left(\frac{Z_T}{W_T}\right).\nonumber
\end{align}
The last inequality uses convexity of $f$ and Jensen's inequality with weights $w_k/W_T$. For a feasible schedule, it follows that
$$
f\!\left(\frac{Z_T}{W_T}\right)\leq\frac{\epsilon_T}{d_0TW_T}.
$$
Let $y_T$ solve $f(y_T)=\epsilon_T/(d_0TW_T)$. The solution is unique because $f$ decreases from infinity to zero. Since $W_T$ stays between fixed positive bounds, \eqref{eq:app-normal-tail} gives
$$
y_T+\frac32\log y_T=L+O(1),\qquad y_T=L-\frac32\log L+O(1).
$$
Monotonicity of $f$ therefore yields $Z_T\geq W_T\{z_L+O(1)\}=Bz_L+O(1)$; here \eqref{eq:app-weight-total} gives $(W_T-B)L=o(1)$.

Within an integer hold, the mean increment is at least $\mu_k^-j/T$. Including the terminal cost, which is nonnegative, we obtain
$$
2T\mathcal R_T(P)
\geq\frac1T\sum_{k<K}\mu_k^-h_k(h_k-1)
\geq Z_T-\mu_+.
$$
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-global-lower}
2T\mathcal R_T^\star(\epsilon_T)\geq B\left[L-\frac32\log L\right]-O(1).
\end{equation}
Schedules outside the competitive class have a larger regret than this bound for sufficiently large $C_1$.

\paragraph{(II) A feasible integer upper bound.} Take $b=\epsilon_T/2$ and use the continuum optimizer $h_b$ from \Cref{app:update-interval-proof}. Starting at $t_0=0$, propose the integer length
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-clock-construction}
j_k=\lceil h_b(t_k/T)\rceil,\qquad t_{k+1}=t_k+j_k\quad\text{when }t_k+j_k<T.
\end{equation}
When the proposed endpoint is at least $T$, retain the incumbent to $T$. Thus every full hold has length $\Theta(L)$, and the terminal hold is at most $CL$. Denote this schedule by $\widehat P_T$ and its step function by $H_T$.

We verify feasibility directly. Since $h_b'=O(L)$ and every hold has width $O(L/T)$ in normalized time, on each full interval
$$
H_T(u)=\lceil h_b(u_k)\rceil\geq h_b(u)-CL^2/T.
$$
The rate $r(u,h)$ decreases in $h$, and for $h\asymp L$ its logarithmic derivative in $h$ is bounded:
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial h}\log r(u,h)=I(u)\frac{Q'(I(u)h)}{Q(I(u)h)}-\frac1h=O(1).
$$
It follows that $r(u,H_T(u))\leq r(u,h_b(u))\{1+O(L^2/T)\}$ on the full intervals. Using \eqref{eq:app-risk-integral} and the binding budget of $h_b$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-attaining-risk}
S_T(\widehat P_T)
\leq T\{1+O(L^2/T)\}\int_0^{u_K}r(u,h_b(u))\,\mathrm{d} u
\leq b\{1+O(L^2/T)\}\leq\epsilon_T
\end{equation}
for sufficiently large $T$. The fixed half-budget leaves room for both the local approximation and rounding errors.

For the objective, every hold, including the terminal remainder, satisfies
$$
H_T(u)\leq h_b(u)+1+CL^2/T.
$$
The schedule has regret $O(L/T)$ because its holds are $O(L)$, so \eqref{eq:app-regret-expansions} applies. Hence
\begin{align}
\label{eq:app-integer-upper}
2T\mathcal R_T(\widehat P_T)
&=\int_0^1\mu(u)H_T(u)\,\mathrm{d} u+O(1)\\
&\leq F_T^{\mathrm c}(\epsilon_T/2)+O(1)
=B\left[L-\frac32\log L\right]+O(1).\nonumber
\end{align}
The last equality uses \eqref{eq:app-Fc-expansion} and $\log(T/b)=L+\log2$. Combining \eqref{eq:app-global-lower} and \eqref{eq:app-integer-upper} proves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-regret-result}
\mathcal R_T^\star(\epsilon_T)=\frac B{2T}\left[L_T-\frac32\log L_T\right]+O(T^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Together with \eqref{eq:app-Fc-expansion} at $b=\epsilon_T$, this also proves \eqref{eq:app-leading-value} and completes the optimized continuum reduction in \Cref{lem:continuous}. The constructed schedule satisfies $0\leq\mathcal R_T(\widehat P_T)-\mathcal R_T^\star(\epsilon_T)=O(T^{-1})$ and, on every risk-bearing interval,
$$
I(u_k)h_k=L-\frac32\log L+O(1).
$$

\paragraph{(III) Counting the constructed schedule.} For a full hold of \eqref{eq:app-clock-construction}, \eqref{eq:app-optimizer-expansion} gives $h_k=L\{1+o(1)\}/I(u_k)$ uniformly. Therefore
$$
\frac L T K(\widehat P_T)
=\sum_{k<K}\frac{h_k}{T}\frac L{h_k}
=\sum_{k<K}\frac{h_k}{T}I(u_k)\{1+o(1)\}
\longrightarrow\int_0^1I(u)\,\mathrm{d} u=D.
$$
The last step is a Riemann sum, since the mesh is $O(L/T)$ and the terminal interval has vanishing normalized length.

\paragraph{(IV) Counting an optimal schedule.} Let $P_T^\star$ be any optimal integer schedule. The upper bound and \Cref{lem:app-competitive} give its mesh bound. By \eqref{eq:app-regret-expansions} and \eqref{eq:app-regret-result},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-optimal-scaled-cost}
\frac1{TL}\sum_{k=0}^{K_T^\star}\mu(u_k)h_k^2\longrightarrow B.
\end{equation}
Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$ and call a risk-bearing interval short if $I(u_k)h_k\leq(1-\delta)L$. Such an interval has length at most $CL$. By \eqref{eq:app-local-expansions}, its exact Gaussian exponent is at most $(1-\delta)L+O(L^2/T)$. The normal-tail lower bound therefore gives risk at least $cL^{-1/2}e^{-(1-\delta)L}$. Feasibility bounds the number $N_{\mathrm{short}}$ of these intervals by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-short-count}
N_{\mathrm{short}}\leq C\epsilon_T\sqrt L\,e^{(1-\delta)L}
=CT\sqrt L\,e^{-\delta L}=o(T/L).
\end{equation}
Their total calendar fraction is $o(1)$ because each is at most $CL$. The terminal fraction is also $o(1)$ by \eqref{eq:app-mesh}.

Let $G_\delta$ contain the remaining risk-bearing intervals and put $z_k=I(u_k)h_k/L$. Then $z_k>1-\delta$ on $G_\delta$, and
$$
\sum_{k\in G_\delta}\frac{h_k}{T}A(u_k)\longrightarrow B,
\qquad
\sum_{k\in G_\delta}\frac{h_k}{T}I(u_k)\longrightarrow D.
$$
Both statements follow from the mesh bound, smoothness, and the negligible calendar fractions just removed. Using nonnegativity of the omitted costs in \eqref{eq:app-optimal-scaled-cost}, we get
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-excess-length}
\sum_{k\in G_\delta}\frac{h_k}{T}A(u_k)\big\{z_k-(1-\delta)\big\}\leq\delta B+o(1).
\end{equation}
For $\eta>0$, let $W_{\mathrm{long}}$ be the calendar fraction of intervals in $G_\delta$ with $z_k>1+\eta$. Since $A_-:=\min A>0$, \eqref{eq:app-excess-length} implies
$$
\limsup_{T\to\infty}W_{\mathrm{long}}\leq\frac{\delta B}{A_-(\eta+\delta)}.
$$
We can now bound the count from both sides. From $z_k>1-\delta$ and \eqref{eq:app-short-count},
$$
\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{LK_T^\star}{T}
=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\sum_{k\in G_\delta}\frac{h_k}{T}\frac{I(u_k)}{z_k}
\leq\frac D{1-\delta}.
$$
For the lower bound, retain only $k\in G_\delta$ with $z_k\leq1+\eta$ and use $I_+:=\max I$:
$$
\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{LK_T^\star}{T}
\geq\frac1{1+\eta}\left\{D-\frac{I_+\delta B}{A_-(\eta+\delta)}\right\}.
$$
Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ and then $\eta\downarrow0$ makes the two bounds equal to $D$. Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-count-result}
K_T^\star\sim\frac{T}{L_T}\int_0^1I(u)\,\mathrm{d} u,
\end{equation}
which proves the count assertion in \Cref{cor:regret} for an optimal integer schedule, not just for the upper-bound construction.
