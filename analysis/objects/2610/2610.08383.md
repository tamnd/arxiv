\label{main}
	Let $d\geq5$, and, for $\delta>0$, set $X^{(\delta)}=(X^{(\delta)}_t)_{t\geq0}$, where 
	\begin{equation}\label{xd}
		X^{(\delta)}_t=\delta^{\frac{2-\alpha}{4}}X_{\delta^{-1}t},\, t\geq0.
	\end{equation}
	               
	Then for a.e.~realization of the environment
	\begin{equation}
		X^{(\delta)}\Rightarrow
		\big(B_{t^{1-\frac{\alpha}2}}\big)_{t\geq0} \text{ as } \delta\to0,
	\end{equation}
    in the finite dimensional sense, 
	where 
	$(B_t)_{t\geq0}$ is Brownian motion with diffusion coefficient $1/\mu$, with $\mu\in(0,\infty)$ to be specified below ---
	see~\eqref{c15}.

	criteria for such kinds of convergence, which, we expect, would mostly involve standard arguments.

\label{version}
	Given $H$, $(Y_t)_{t\geq0}$ is a Markov jump process starting at the origin, with jump chain $(\mathcal{X}_\ell)_{\ell\geq0}$, and instantaneous jump rates at $(x,t)$ given by $\rho^x_t:=\gamma (W^x_{t^\gamma})^{-\alpha}$, $t\geq0$, $x\in{\mathbb Z}^d$.
<!-- end lema-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
This is a straightforward argument, which we outline for completeness. 

That $(Y_t)$ inherits the Markovian character and jump chain from $(X_t)$ is quite clear. 
 
It remains to characterize the jump times of $(Y_t)$, which we will relate to those of $(X_t)$, as follows. The latter may be obtained,
via  a standard Harris graphical construction, from the family $\{ {\mathcal P}^x,\,x\in{\mathbb Z}^d\}$ of independent Poisson point processes
on $[0,\infty)$ with intensity function $\lambda^x_\cdot$, respectively. 

It is now enough to check that for each $x\in{\mathbb Z}^d$, ${\mathcal P}^x$ is mapped, by the transformation $t\to t^\gamma$, to $\tilde{\mathcal P}^x$, a  Poisson point processes
on $[0,\infty)$ with intensity function $\gamma (W^x_{t^\gamma})^{-\alpha}$, $t\geq0$.

That such a transformation maps a Poisson point process to a Poisson point process is quite clear, so it is enough to obtain 
the expected value of the occupation random variables of $\tilde{\mathcal P}^x$, namely, ${\mathbb E}(\#(r,s))$, $0\leq r<s<\infty$, where
\begin{equation}\label{occ}

We conclude by resorting to an obvious change of variables in the  integral to find that it equals $\int_{r}^{s} \rho^x_t\,dt$.

\label{taufin}
	It is perhaps quite clear that $\hat\tau_n$ is almost surely finite for every $n$, but let us give next a brief argument. 
	
	It is enough to show that jump times are all finite almost surely. Let us fix $t\geq0$ and $x\in{\mathbb Z}^d$. By Markovianess, given that $Y_t=x$, the probability that  the next jump time is infinite is given by 
	\begin{equation}\label{f1}
		{\mathbb E}\Big[\exp\big\{-\gamma\int_{t}^\infty (W^x_{s^\gamma})^{-\alpha}ds\big\}\Big].
	\end{equation}
	
	It suffices to show that the above  integral diverges in probability. Given $L>1$, we have that
	\begin{equation}\label{f2}
		\int_{t}^\infty (W^x_{s^\gamma})^{-\alpha}ds\geq\int_{t+L}^{t+2L} (W^x_{s^\gamma})^{-\alpha}ds\geq (M_{t,L})^{-\alpha} L,
	\end{equation}
	where $M_{t,L}=\max\big\{W^x_s:\,(t+L)^\gamma\leq s\leq (t+2L)^\gamma\big\}$.
	It is enough then to argue that  $M_{t,L},\,t>0,\,L>1$, are tight.
	We have that
	\begin{equation}\label{f3}
		M_{t,L}\leq1+(t+L)^{-\gamma/2}
		\max_{0\leq s\leq (t+2L)^\gamma}\big|S^x_{N^x_s}\big|.
	\end{equation}
	
	Kolmogorov's Maximal Inequality implies that the probability that the latter term on the right hand side exceeds an arbitrary $K>0$ is bounded above by 
	$2^\gamma K^{-2}$, and tightness follows.

Let ${\mathcal G}_t=\sigma(Y_s,\eta^x_s;\,s\geq0,\,x\in{\mathbb Z}^d)$, $t\geq0$, be the (joint) filtration generated by $(Y_t)$ and $(\eta^x_t)$, and set ${\cal F}_n={\mathcal G}_{\hat\tau_n}$, $n\geq0$.   We have that
\begin{equation}\label{ts1}
	\hat\tau_n\geq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} {\mathcal E}_i\geq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \1_{\{{\mathcal E}_i>1\}}=:B_{n-1},\,n\geq1,
\end{equation}
where ${\mathcal E}_i$ denotes the time elapsed since $\hat\tau_i$ until the next jump of $(Y_t)$. 
Notice that ${\mathcal E}_i\in{\cal F}_n$ if $i<n$.
By a standard argument using an exponential Chebyshev inequality, it is enough to show that there exists $\zeta<1$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{lapb}
	{\mathbb E}(e^{- B_n})\leq\zeta^n,\,n\geq1.
\end{equation}


We next argue that~\eqref{lapb} follows from the following claim:
\begin{equation}\label{zeta}
	p:=\inf_{i,t\geq0,,x\in{\mathbb Z}^d}{\mathbb P}\big({\mathcal E}_i>1\big|\hat\tau_i=t,{\mathcal Y}_i=y\big)>0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, let us write
\begin{equation}\label{lapb1}
	{\mathbb E}(e^{- B_n}|{\cal F}_n)=e^{- B_{n-1}} {\mathbb E}(e^{-\1_{\{{\mathcal E}_n>1\}}}|{\cal F}_n)=e^{- B_{n-1}} \big[1-(1-e^{-1}){\mathbb P}({\mathcal E}_n>1|{\cal F}_n)\big],
\end{equation}
$n\geq0$ (where $B_{-1}:=0$).
 
By Markovianess and spatial homogeneity,  the expression in square brackets is bounded above by $1-(1-e^{-1})p=:\zeta<1$. Applying this bound to the right hand side of~\eqref{lapb1}, and iterating, we establish~\eqref{lapb}.

In order to check~\eqref{lapb1}, let us first notice that, again by Markovianess and spatial homogeneity, 
\begin{equation}\label{ts2}
	p=\inf_{t\geq0}\,{\mathbb E}\Big[\exp\big\{-\gamma\int_{t}^{t+1} (W^0_{s^\gamma})^{-\alpha}ds\big\}\Big].
\end{equation}

If $t<1$, then the latter expectation is bounded  below by
\begin{equation}\label{ts3}
	{\mathbb E}\big[e^{-\gamma'(m+1)^{-\alpha}}\big] 
\end{equation}
where $\gamma'=\gamma\int_{1}^{2} s^{\alpha/(2-\alpha)}\,ds=2^\gamma-1$, and $m=\min_{0\leq s\leq2^\gamma}|S^0_{N_s}|$; it readily follows that the expression in~\eqref{ts3} is a (strictly) positive constant.

If $t\geq1$, we 
find that the expectation on the right hand side of~\eqref{ts2} is bounded  below by
\begin{equation}\label{ts4}
	{\mathbb E}\big[e^{-\gamma \,m_t^{-\alpha}}\big],
\end{equation}
where $m_t=\min\big\{W^0_s,\,t^\gamma\leq s\leq(t+1)^\gamma\big\}$, and to find an uniformly positive lower bound for~\eqref{ts4}, it is enough to argue that $\tilde m_t$ converges in distribution as $t\to\infty$ to a nontrivial (positive) random variable.
We have that
\begin{equation}\label{ts5}
	t^{-\gamma/2}\min_{t^\gamma\leq s\leq (t+1)^\gamma}\big|S^0_{N^0_s}\big|
	\leq m_{t}
	\leq t^{-\gamma/2}+t^{-\gamma/2}\,\big|S^0_{N^0_{t^\gamma}}\big|.
\end{equation}
The left hand side of~\eqref{ts5} is bounded below by 
\begin{equation}\label{ts6}
	t^{-\gamma/2}\,\big|S^0_{N^0_{t^\gamma}}\big| -t^{-\gamma/2}\max_{t^\gamma\leq s\leq (t+1)^\gamma}\big|S^0_{N^0_s}-S^0_{N^0_{t^\gamma}}\big|,
\end{equation}
 
and the CLT and Kolmogorov's Maximal Inequality imply that $m_{t}$ converges in distribution as $t\to\infty$ to $|{\mathcal N}|^{-\alpha}$, 
where ${\mathcal N}$ is a standard Gaussian random variable.

We write
\begin{equation}\label{se1}
	\hat\kappa_n=\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa_n}{\mathcal E}^n_j,
\end{equation} 	
where ${\mathcal E}^n_1$ is the duration of the visit by $(Y_t)$ to ${\mathcal X}_{\tau_n}$ starting at ${\mathcal T}^n_0:=\hat\tau_n$, and, inductively, for $j\geq2$,
${\mathcal E}^n_j$  is the duration of the visit by $(Y_t)$ to ${\mathcal X}_{\tau_n+j-1}$ starting at ${\mathcal T}^n_{j-1}:=\hat\tau_n+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}{\mathcal E}^n_i$. 

By~\eqref{kapadis}, we may assume that $\kappa_n\leq n^\delta$ for $\delta\in\big(\frac2{d-2},1\big)$ fixed.
We may also assume, by Lemma~\ref{lg}, that $\tau_n>an$. 

Let us fix $c\in(0,1-\delta)$. By Markovianess and spatial homogeneity, 
\begin{align}\label{se2}
	{\mathbb P}\big({\mathcal E}^n_1>t_0^c\big|\hat\tau_n={\mathcal T}^n_{0}=t_0,Y_{t_0}=y\big) &={\mathbb E}\Big[\exp\big\{-\gamma\int_{t_0}^{t_0+t_0^c} (W^0_{s^\gamma})^{-\alpha}ds\big\}\Big]
	\nonumber \\ &\leq {\mathbb E}\big(e^{-\gamma t_0^c(M_{t_0})^{-\alpha}}\big),
\end{align}
where 
$M_t=\max\big\{W^0_s:\,t^\gamma\leq s\leq (t+t_0^{c+\delta})^\gamma\big\}$, $t\geq0$.

Let us set $c'=c/(2+\alpha)$. Given $t_0>an$, we have that
\begin{align}\label{se3}
	{\mathbb P}\big(M_{t_0}>t_0^{c'}\big)&\leq{\mathbb P}\Big(\max_{t_0^\gamma\leq s\leq (t_0+t_0^{c+\delta})^\gamma}|S^0_{N^0_s}|>t_0^{\gamma/2} t_0^{c'}-1\Big)
	\nonumber \\ &= {\mathbb P}\Big(\max_{N^0_{t_0^\gamma}\leq k\leq N^0_{(t_0+t_0^{c+\delta})^\gamma}}|S^0_{k}| >t_0^{\gamma/2} t_0^{c'}-1\Big).
\end{align}
Resorting to standard large deviation bounds for $(N^0_\cdot)$, it is enough to bound 
\begin{equation}\label{se4}
	{\mathbb P}\Big(\max_{\frac12t_0^\gamma\leq k\leq \frac32t_0^\gamma}|S^0_{k}|>t_0^{\gamma/2} t_0^{c'}-1\Big)\leq e^{-c''t_0^{2c'}},
\end{equation}
where $c''>0$ is a constant, and the latter inequality follows from the union bound and  Bernstein Inequality. It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{se5}
	{\mathbb P}\big({\mathcal E}^n_1>t_0^c\big|{\mathcal T}^n_{0}=t_0,Y_{t_0}=y\big)\leq e^{-\gamma t_0^{c-\alpha c'}}+e^{-c''t_0^{2c'}}\leq 2e^{-\gamma''t_0^{2c'}},
\end{equation}
where $\gamma''=\gamma\wedge c''$. 

Proceeding inductively, we may repeat the latter approach to find that, for $1\leq j<\lfloor n^\delta\rfloor$,
we have that, for $t_0\leq t_j\leq t_0+jt_0^c$,
\begin{align}\label{se6}

	\nonumber \\ 
    &\leq {\mathbb E}\big(e^{-\gamma t_0^c(M_{t_j})^{-\alpha}}\big),
\end{align}
and the bound on the right hand side of~\eqref{se5} follows for the left hand side of~\eqref{se6} with a similar argument as above.

It follows that outside of an event of probability bounded above by $2n^\delta e^{-c''n^{2ac'}}$ (on account of the above
estimates on  ${\mathcal E}^n_j$, $1\leq j\leq n^\delta$), combined with the estimates of the probability of $\{\kappa_n>n^\delta\}$, from~\eqref{kapadis},
and of  $\{\tau_n\leq an\}$, from Lemma~\ref{lg}, we have that, outside of an event with summable probability,
\begin{equation}\label{se7}
	\hat\kappa_n\leq n^{\delta+c},
\end{equation} 	
and the result follows, using again Lemma~\ref{lg}, for any choice $\delta'\in(\delta+c,1)$.
