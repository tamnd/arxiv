\label{thm:CLPW} Let $g$ and $n$ be nonnegative integers such that $2g+n\geq 3$. Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The motivic Euler characteristic $\chi_c(M_{g,n})$ is polynomial in $\mathsf L$;
    \item The compactly supported cohomology of $M_{g,n}$ is of Tate type;
    \item $g=0$ or $3g+2n<25$.
\end{enumerate}

\label{smaller Eulers}
                                                                    
 The equivariant motivic Euler characteristic $\chi_c^{S_n}(\overline M_{0,n})$ is always a polynomial in $\mathsf L$ and can be computed                                                            
 for instance using the Getzler--Kapranov formula.

\label{thm:Mbargn}
We compute $\chi_c^{S_n}(\overline M_{g,n})$ for $g=4$ and $4\leq n\leq 7$, $g=5$ and $n\leq 3$, and $g=6$ and $n\leq 1$. In these cases, $\chi^{S_n}_c(\overline M_{g,n})$ is a polynomial in $\mathsf L$.

If $X$ is smooth and proper, then each Galois representation $H^i_c(X,\mathbf Q_{\ell})$ is pure of weight $i$, and hence $\chi_c^{S_n}(X)$ determines each  $H^i_c(X,\mathbf Q_{\ell})$ as an element of $\operatorname{Gal}^{S_n}_\mathbf Q$ up to semisimplification. Therefore, Theorem \ref{thm:Mbargn} determines the Betti numbers of $\overline M_{g,n}$ in the relevant cases. On the other hand, Theorem \ref{thm:Mgn} does not determine the Betti numbers of $M_{g,n}$ in those cases. See Remark \ref{rem:categorify} for a discussion of the extra information needed to compute the cohomology of $M_{g,n}$.

<!-- rem-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:categorify}
 The spectral sequence induced by the stratification by topological type and the weight spectral sequence for the normal crossings pair $(\overline M_{g,n}, \partial M_{g,n})$ lift the above observation about the relationship between Euler characteristics of $\overline M_{g,n}$ and $M_{g,n}$ to a relationship between cohomology groups. It is much more difficult to compute cohomology than Euler characteristics because computing cohomology involves studying all of the arrows of the spectral sequences, rather than just the entries. Recently, Bergström and Wennink have implemented the weight spectral sequence in the Sagemath package \texttt{admcycles} \cite{admcycles} and used it to compute the cohomology of $M_5$ \cite{BergstromWennink}.

<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:Young}
Let $m_\lambda$ be the monomial symmetric polynomial associated to the partition $\lambda$. Then 
\[ \chi_c^{S_n}(X) = \sum_{\lambda \vdash n} m_\lambda \otimes \chi_c(X/S_\lambda).\]
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $h_\lambda$ be the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial associated to the partition $\lambda$. Since the Schur polynomials are an orthonormal basis with respect to $\langle-,-\rangle$, Schur's lemma implies that the inner product satisfies 
$$ \langle [V], [W] \rangle = \dim \mathrm{Hom}_{S_n}(V,W)$$
for $V,W \in \mathrm{Rep}(S_n)$. It then follows by Frobenius reciprocity that 
\[ \langle h_\lambda, [W] \rangle = \dim \left(W^{S_\lambda}\right),\]
since $h_\lambda = [\operatorname{Ind}_{S_\lambda}^{S_n} \mathbf Q]$, where $\mathbf Q$ denotes the trivial one-dimensional representation of $S_\lambda$.  
    Hence $\langle h_\lambda, [H^i_c(X,\mathbf Q_\ell)]\rangle = \dim H^i_c(X/S_\lambda,\mathbf Q_\ell)$ and $\langle h_\lambda,\chi_c^{S_n}(X) \rangle = \chi_c(X/S_\lambda)$. The result follows from this fact, and that $m_\lambda$ and $h_\lambda$ are dual bases of $\Lambda_n$ with respect to $\langle-,-\rangle$  \cite[Section I.4]{macdonald}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:specializations}
    Suppose that $X$ is such that $\chi_c(X) = f(\mathsf L)$ is a polynomial in $\mathsf L$. Then
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item The Euler characteristic $e(X)$ is given by $f(1)$.
        \item Let $p$ be a prime, and $t$ a positive integer. If $X$ is defined over $\mathbf Z_{(p)}$ and has good reduction mod $p$, then  the number $\# X(\mathbf F_{p^t})$ of $\mathbf F_{p^t}$-points of $X$ is given by $f(p^t)$.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
There is a ring homomorphism $K_0(\operatorname{Gal}_\mathbf Q) \to \mathbf Z$ defined by sending an $\ell$-adic Galois representation to its dimension. This homomorphism maps $\mathsf L$ to $1$, and it also maps $\chi_c(X)$ to the compactly supported Euler characteristic of $X$, by definition. This coincides with the ordinary Euler characeristic by \cite{laumon}.

    Let $\operatorname{Gal}'_\mathbf Q$ denote the category of $\ell$-adic Galois representations unramified at $p$. There is a similar homomorphism $K_0(\operatorname{Gal}'_\mathbf Q) \to \mathbf Z$ which sends an $\ell$-adic Galois representation $V$ to $\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p^t} |\, V)$. It maps $\mathsf L$ to $p^t$, and sends $\chi_c(X)$ to the number of $\mathbf F_{p^t}$-points, using the Grothendieck--Lefschetz trace formula and in addition the good reduction hypothesis to identify \'etale cohomology over $\overline\mathbf Q$ and $\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For any pair $(g,n)$ in the statement, $\chi^{S_n}_c(\overline M_{g',n'})$ has been computed for all $g',n'$ such that $0<2g'-2+n'\leq 2g-2+n$ and $g'\leq g$ by Theorem \ref{thm:Mbargn} and previous calculations of several authors, which can be found at \cite{CohomologyModuliSpaces}. Applying the Getzler--Kapranov method yields the result.
<!-- end proof-3 -->

Let $X$ be an algebraic variety, $\mathbb V$ an $\ell$-adic sheaf on $X$ which is mixed of weights $\leq w$. Then $H^k_c(X,\mathbb V)$ is mixed of weights $\leq k+w$.
<!-- end thm-5 -->
<!-- cor-1: body_md -->
\label{Deligne corollary}
The compactly supported cohomology $H^\bullet_c(M_g,\mathbb V_\lambda)$ is of weights $\leq 6g-6+|\lambda|$. In particular, if $\chi_c(M_g,\mathbb V_\lambda)$ is a polynomial in $\mathsf L$, then it is of degree at most $3g-3+\tfrac 1 2 |\lambda|$.
<!-- end cor-1 -->
<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:H0}
	We have $H^0(M_g,\mathbb V_\lambda)=0$ unless $\lambda=0$.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
It is classical that the mapping class group surjects to $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbf Z)$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- thm-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:Hain} Let $g \geq 3$. Then $H^1(M_g,\mathbb V_{111})\cong \mathsf L^2$. If $\lambda \neq (1,1,1)$, then $H^1(M_g,\mathbb V_{\lambda})=0$.
<!-- end thm-6 -->
<!-- cor-2: body_md -->
\label{weight corollary}
    Let $g \geq 3$ and $\lambda \neq 0$.                                                                 
    \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $\lambda = (1,1,1)$, then $\chi_c(M_g,\mathbb V_\lambda)$                       
    has highest weight contribution               
    $-\mathsf L^{3g-2}$. 
    \item If $\lambda \neq (1,1,1)$, then $\chi_c(M_g,\mathbb V_\lambda)$ has contribution of weight at most $6g-8+ |\lambda|$.                                                   
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end cor-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The result follows from Corollary \ref{Deligne corollary}, Proposition \ref{prop:H0}, Theorem \ref{thm:Hain}, and Poincar\'e duality.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{weight proposition}
Fix $(g,n)$ with $g \geq 3$. The following pieces of data determine $\chi_c^{S_n}(\overline M_{g,n})$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\chi_c^{S_n}(\partial  M_{g,n})=\chi_c^{S_n}(\overline M_{g,n})-\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{g,n})$;
    \item $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{g,n'})$ for all $n'<n$;
    \item for every partition $\lambda$ with $|\lambda|=n$, all contributions to $\chi_c(M_g,\mathbb V_{\lambda})$ of weights $w$ such that $3g-3+n \leq w \leq 6g-8+n$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- rem-7: body_md -->
The statement is formulated for $g \geq 3$ only because for $g \leq 2$, $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{g,n})$ is computable for every $n$; see \cite{GetzlerGenus0} for genus $0$, \cite{getzler-resolving} for genus $1$ and \cite{PetersenA2} together with \cite{Petersendelliptic} for genus $2$. For a few more details on the genus $2$ computation, see \cite[Section 4]{BergstromFaber}.
<!-- end rem-7 -->
<!-- rem-8: body_md -->
Note that the range of weights of the contributions to $\chi_c(M_g,\mathbb V_{\lambda})$, for $\lambda$ with $|\lambda|=n$, that are needed in the assumption of in Proposition~\ref{weight proposition}, is equal to $3g-4$  and thus does not depend on $n$.
<!-- end rem-8 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Since $\overline M_{g,n}$ is smooth and proper, if we know all the contributions of weight greater or equal to $\dim \overline M_{g,n}=3g-3+n$, then we can determine all contributions of smaller weight by Poincar\'e duality. Since we know $\chi_c^{S_n}(\partial  M_{g,n})$, we only need to determine the contributions to $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{g,n})$ of weight greater or equal to $3g-3+n$.

As described in Section~\ref{subsec: local systems}, $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{g,n})$ can be computed from $\chi_c(M_g,\mathbb V_{\lambda})$ for all $\lambda$ such that $|\lambda| \leq n$. From knowing $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{g,n'})$ for all $n'<n$ we can compute $\chi_c(M_g,\mathbb V_{\lambda})$ for all  $|\lambda| < n$. When writing $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{g,n})$ in terms of $\chi_c(M_g,\mathbb V_{\lambda})$ for $|\lambda| \leq n$, then all contributions with $|\lambda|=n$ appear without Tate twist (in fact with coefficient $(-1)^{|\lambda|}$ to $s_{\lambda'}$, where $\lambda'$ denotes the conjugate of $\lambda$, see Section~\ref{subsec: local systems}).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  
The result now follows from Corollary~\ref{Deligne corollary}, Proposition~\ref{prop:H0} and Corollary~\ref{weight corollary} (where we use part $(2)$ in the case of $n=3$).
<!-- end proof-6 -->

Note first that $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{1,1},\mathbb V_a)$ is known for any $a \geq 0$ by \cite{getzler-resolving} and hence that we can compute $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{1,n})$ for any $n \geq 1$. So, if we fix $n \leq 7$ and use the results mentioned in Remark~\ref{smaller Eulers} and the Getzler-Kapranov method in Section~\ref{sec:GetzlerKapranov}, then if we know $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{4,n'})$ for $n'<n$, we can compute $\chi_c^{S_n}(\partial  M_{4,n})$. Already from earlier, we know $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{4,n})$ for $n \leq 3$, see \cite{BergstromFaberPayne}. 

By Theorem~\ref{thm:CLPW} we know that $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{4,n})$ is Tate for $n \leq 6$. This means that $\chi_c(M_4,\mathbb V_\lambda)=\sum_i a_{i,\lambda} \mathsf L^i$ for some integers $a_{i,\lambda}$ for all $|\lambda| \leq 6$. By, Proposition~\ref{weight proposition} it follows that, for a fixed $n$, if we know $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{4,n'})$ for $n'<n$ then we only need to determine $a_{i+\lceil (9+n)/2 \rceil,\lambda}$ for $i=0,1,2,3$ and all $\lambda$ such that $|\lambda|=n$ to be able to compute $\chi_c^{S_n}(M_{4,n})$. 

The $S_n$-equivariant integer valued Euler characteristic of $M_{4,n}$ is known for all $n$ (see \cite{Gorsky}), and hence $\sum_i a_{i,\lambda}$ is known for all $\lambda$ such that $|\lambda| \leq 6$. Furthermore, $\sum_i a_{i,\lambda} q^i$ is known from counts of points over finite fields for $q=2,3,4$ for all $\lambda$ such that $|\lambda| \leq 6$, see \cite{BergstromFaberPayne} for $q=2,3$ and from personal communication with Carel Faber for $q=4$.

Since the boundary divisors will consist of contributions from the cohomology of $\overline M_{3,9}/S_2$, $\overline M_{4,1+j} \times \overline M_{0,8-j}$ for $0 \leq j \leq 5$, $\overline M_{4-i,1+j} \times \overline M_{i,8-j}$ for $1 \leq i \leq 2$ and $0\leq j \leq 7$, we know by Remark~\ref{smaller Eulers} and the previous computations, that the cohomology of $\widetilde{\partial M}_{4,7}$ will be of Tate type. 
From this we conclude that the cohomology of $\overline M_{4,7}$ is of Tate type.
