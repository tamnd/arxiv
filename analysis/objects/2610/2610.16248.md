<!-- head-1: body_md -->
We write \(\operatorname{Law}(X)\) for the law of a random variable \(X\).
For probability densities \(\nu\) and \(\pi\), write the total variation distance as
\(\operatorname{TV}(\nu,\pi):=\sup_A\lvert\nu(A)-\pi(A)\rvert\).  When \(\nu\ll\pi\), the
order-\(r\) R\'enyi divergence for \(r>1\) is
\(D_r(\nu\Vert\pi):=\frac{1}{r-1}\log\int
(\frac{\nu(x)}{\pi(x)})^r\pi(x)\,\mathrm{d} x\).  In
particular, the $\chi^2$ divergence is
\(\chi^2(\nu\Vert\pi):=\int(\frac{\nu(x)}{\pi(x)}-1)^2\pi(x)\,\mathrm{d} x
=\mathrm{e}^{D_2(\nu\Vert\pi)}-1\), and
\(\operatorname{TV}(\nu,\pi)\le\tfrac12\sqrt{\chi^2(\nu\Vert\pi)}\).  We call \(\nu\)
a constant-\(D_2\) warm start of \(\pi\) if
\(D_2(\nu\Vert\pi)=O(1)\). The notations \(\widetilde O(\cdot), \widetilde\Theta(\cdot)\) suppress polylogarithmic factors in the displayed problem parameters. 
\([a]_+:=\max\{a,0\}\).

<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:target}
We assume that the target density is
\(\pi(x)\propto\mathrm{e}^{-V(x)}\), where the potential
\(V:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\) is \(\alpha\)-strongly convex and \(\beta\)-smooth,

\tfrac{\beta}{2}\lVertx-x'\rVert^2\) for all \(x,x'\in\mathbb R^d\).  We denote the condition
number by \(\kappa:=\beta/\alpha\).

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let \(\varphi_\eta:=\mathcal N(0,\eta I_d)\), so that \(\Pi_\eta(x,y)=\pi(x)\varphi_\eta(y-x)\).  For every \(f\),
\begin{align*}
    \mathop{\textstyle\int} f(x,2x-y)\,\pi(x)\varphi_\eta(y-x)\,\mathrm{d} x\,\mathrm{d} y
    =\mathop{\textstyle\int} f(x,y')\,\pi(x)\varphi_\eta(x-y')\,\mathrm{d} x\,\mathrm{d} y'
    =\mathop{\textstyle\int} f\,\mathrm{d}\Pi_\eta.
\end{align*}
Hence the reflection preserves \(\Pi_\eta\).  Now fix \(y\) and let
\(\overline M_y(x,x')=(1-\rho)M_y(x,x')+\rho q_y(x')\) be the mixture distribution of the accelerated and RGO branches.  Since \(M_y\) preserves \(q_y\),
\begin{align*}
  \mathop{\textstyle\int} q_y(x)M_y(x,x')\,\mathrm{d} x = q_y(x')
  \implies
  \mathop{\textstyle\int}[\mathop{\textstyle\int} f(x',y)\overline M_y(x,x')\,\mathrm{d} x']q_y(x)\,\mathrm{d} x
 =\mathop{\textstyle\int} f(x',y)q_y(x')\,\mathrm{d} x'.
\end{align*}
Thus the second update preserves the conditional law \(q_y\) for every \(y\).  Composing it with the reflection preserves \(\Pi_\eta\), whose position marginal is \(\pi\).

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{flux:lem:conditional-geometry}
For every \(y\),
\[
 \frac1{1+s}I\preceq\Sigma_y\preceq\frac1{1+a}I,
 \qquad
 \frac a{1+a}I\preceq I-\Sigma_y\preceq\frac s{1+s}I.
\]
If \(\Pi_\eta^Y(y)\propto \mathrm{e}^{-U_\eta(y)}\), then
\[
 \nabla U_\eta(y)=\eta^{-1}\{y-\mathbb E[X\mid Y=y]\},\qquad
 \nabla^2U_\eta(y)=\eta^{-1}(I-\Sigma_y),
\]
and hence
\(\alpha(1+a)^{-1}I\preceq\nabla^2U_\eta
\preceq\beta(1+s)^{-1}I\).
Moreover, when \(s<1\),
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E g_y(W)=-\bar w_y,\qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(W,g_y(W))=I-\Sigma_y,\qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(g_y(W))\preceq\frac{s}{1-s}(I-\Sigma_y).
 \label{flux:eq:conditional-identities}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The normalized conditional potential has curvature between
\((1+a)I\) and \((1+s)I\).  Brascamp--Lieb gives the upper covariance
bound.  The information inequality gives
\(\operatorname{Cov}(W)\succeq\{\mathbb E\nabla^2[-\log q_y^w(W)]\}^{-1}
\succeq(1+s)^{-1}I\), proving the remaining covariance bounds.
Differentiating the conditional normalizer gives the two identities for
\(U_\eta\).

For \cref{flux:eq:conditional-identities}, use
\(\nabla\log q_y^w(w)=-w-g_y(w)\).  Integration by parts first gives
\(\mathbb E(W+g_y(W))=0\) and
\(\Sigma_y+\operatorname{Cov}(W,g_y(W))=I\).  Applying it once more to \(g_y\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E\nabla g_y(W)=I-\Sigma_y+\operatorname{Cov}(g_y(W)).
\]
The conditional law has Poincar\'e constant at most one, while
\(0\preceq\nabla g_y\preceq sI\).  Therefore
\(\operatorname{Cov}(g_y(W))\preceq s\mathbb E\nabla g_y(W)\); rearrangement proves the last
claim.  These standard Brascamp--Lieb and integration-by-parts facts are
reviewed, for example, by \citet{chewi2023log}.  The extension from smooth
potentials and test functions to the regularity in \cref{ass:target} is
collected in \cref{flux:app:regularity}.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{flux:prop:fixed-validity}
For every fixed \((y,\widetilde w)\), the preceding process is
non-explosive and preserves \(q_y^w\otimes\gamma_d\).  If
\(\mathsf S_t^{y,\widetilde w}\) is its transition operator and
\((Jf)(w,p)=f(w,-p)\), then
\[
 (\mathsf S_t^{y,\widetilde w})^*=J\mathsf S_t^{y,\widetilde w}J.
\]
It is a strongly continuous contraction semigroup on
\(L^2(q_y^w\otimes\gamma_d)\).
Consequently, inserting an independent Gaussian momentum, running for a
fixed time, and discarding momentum gives a reversible position kernel for
\(q_y^w\).  The conclusion remains true after averaging over any reference
law independent of the incoming \((W,P)\) conditional on \(Y=y\).

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The calculation follows the usual skew-balance argument for event-driven
kinetic samplers
\citep{vanetti2017piecewise,bierkens2020boomerang,michel2020forward,
monmarche2022exact}; we give the semigroup step because our reference
motion also contains Langevin noise.

The proof has three steps.  First, a trajectory with no event can be
reversed after flipping momentum; its no-event probability supplies exactly
the likelihood correction caused by the residual force.  Second, the
Rayleigh update balances the stationary probability flow through a single
collision.  Finally, we sum these identities over the number and times of
the collisions.  This proves reversal of the complete process as well as
invariance.

\emph{Non-explosion.}
With
\(\mathcal V=1+\{\lVertw+b_{\rm ref}\rVert^2+\lVertp\rVert^2\}/2\), the full
generator obeys
\(\mathcal L\mathcal V\le C_{\widetilde w}(1+\mathcal V)\), and the event
rate is at most \(C_{\widetilde w}(1+\mathcal V)\), where
\(C_{\widetilde w}\) is a finite constant that may depend on the fixed
reference.  Indeed, a jump changes \(\mathcal V\) by
\((R^2-z^2)/2\), \(\mathbb E R^2=2\), and
\(\lVerth(w)\rVert\le s\lVertw-\widetilde w\rVert\).
A stopped Dynkin formula and Gronwall bound both
\(\mathbb E\mathcal V(W_t,P_t)\) and the
expected event count on every finite interval.  The recursive
integrated-hazard construction is therefore well defined and
non-explosive.

\emph{Reversal between and at events.}
Let \(Z_t^0=(W_t^0,P_t^0)\) be the affine Langevin diffusion without
events, let \(\lambda(w,p)=[p^\mathrm{T} h(w)]_+\), and define its killed
semigroup---that is, its transition weighted by the probability of seeing
no event---by
\[
 \mathsf D_tf(z)=\mathbb E_z\!\left[
 \mathrm{e}^{-\int_0^t\lambda(Z_u^0)\,\mathrm{d} u}f(Z_t^0)\right].
\]
The affine diffusion is invariant and momentum-reversible for the Gaussian
density
\(\mu_{\rm ref}\propto
\exp\{-\lVertw+b_{\rm ref}\rVert^2/2-\lVertp\rVert^2/2\}\).
For the desired density \(\mu=q_y^w\otimes\gamma_d\), the ratio
\(\varrho=\mathrm{d}\mu/\mathrm{d}\mu_{\rm ref}\) satisfies
\(\nabla\log\varrho=-h\).  Since the position
path is absolutely continuous,
\[
 \frac{\varrho(W_t^0)}{\varrho(W_0^0)}
 =\exp\left\{-\int_0^tP_u^{0\mathrm{T}}h(W_u^0)\,\mathrm{d} u\right\}
 =\exp\left\{-\int_0^t(\lambda-\lambda\circ J)(Z_u^0)\,\mathrm{d} u\right\}.
\]
Stationary path reversal for the affine diffusion therefore gives
\(\mathsf D_t^*=J\mathsf D_tJ\) first for bounded nonnegative functions.
Taking one test function equal to one shows \(\mu\mathsf D_t\le\mu\), and Jensen's inequality
extends the identity to \(L^2(\mu)\).

Let \(\mathcal G\) be the gain kernel: it applies one collision, weighted by
the rate at which that collision occurs.  At fixed \(w\), write the incoming momentum as
\(p=v+zn\), where \(v\perp n\) and \(z>0\).  Apart from a common constant,
the stationary marked flux is
\[
 q_y^w(w)\lVerth(w)\rVert\varphi_{d-1}(v)
 z \mathrm{e}^{-z^2/2}r \mathrm{e}^{-r^2/2}\,
 \mathrm{d} w\,\mathrm{d} v\,\mathrm{d} z\,\mathrm{d} r,\qquad z,r>0.
\]
Here \(\varphi_{d-1}\) is the standard Gaussian density on the subspace
orthogonal to \(n\).
It is unchanged by \((v,z,r)\mapsto(-v,r,z)\), which turns a marked
transition into the momentum reversal of its time-reversed transition.
Thus \(\mathcal G^*=J\mathcal GJ\) in the corresponding kernel sense.

The contribution of paths with exactly \(m\) events is the time-simplex
integral of
\(\mathsf D_{t_0}\mathcal G\mathsf D_{t_1}\cdots
\mathcal G\mathsf D_{t_m}\).
Reversing the simplex coordinates and using the preceding two identities
shows that its adjoint is obtained by conjugating with \(J\).  Tonelli
justifies this calculation for nonnegative tests, and non-explosion makes
the sum over \(m\) conservative, meaning that it retains total probability
one.  Summing proves
\((\mathsf S_t^{y,\widetilde w})^*=J\mathsf S_t^{y,\widetilde w}J\);
taking one test
function equal to one proves invariance.  Jensen gives \(L^2\)
contractivity, while stochastic continuity and density of bounded
continuous compactly supported functions give strong continuity.

Finally, if \(\iota f(w,p)=f(w)\) and
\(\mathsf M u(w)=\int u(w,p)\,\gamma_d(\mathrm{d} p)\), then
\(\mathsf M=\iota^*\), \(J\iota=\iota\), and \(\mathsf M J=\mathsf M\).
Thus inserting a Gaussian momentum and then discarding it are adjoint
operations.  Suppressing the fixed superscripts \((y,\widetilde w)\),
\((\mathsf M\mathsf S_t\iota)^*=\mathsf M\mathsf S_t\iota\).
Conditioning on an
independent reference and applying Fubini proves the last assertion.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{persistent:lem:fixed-invariance}
For every fixed \(y,e\), \cref{eq:persistent-generator} defines a
non-explosive Markov process.  If \(S_t^{y,e}\) denotes its transition
operator and \(\mathsf R(w,p)=(w,-p)\), then

 \qquad (S_t^{y,e})^*=\mathsf R S_t^{y,e}\mathsf R .
\]

measurable reference law depending on \(y\), provided the reference is
conditionally independent of the incoming \((W,P)\).

<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Put \(a=b_y(e)\), \(r(w)=b_y(w)-a\), and
\[
 \mathsf J(w,p)=(w,\operatorname{Ref}_{r(w)}p),
 \qquad s(w,p)=p^\mathrm{T} r(w).
\]
Between events the process follows the harmonic flow
\[
 \phi_u(w,p)=\bigl(-a+(w+a)\cos u+p\sin u,
                    -(w+a)\sin u+p\cos u\bigr).
\]
Both this flow and \(\mathsf J\) preserve
\(H(w,p)=\{\lVertw+a\rVert^2+\lVertp\rVert^2\}/2\).  Thus every set
\(K_R=\{H\le R\}\) is invariant.  Lipschitz continuity of \(b_y\) bounds
the event rate \(\lambda=[s]_+\) on \(K_R\) by
\[
 \lVertp\rVert\,\lVertb_y(w)-b_y(e)\rVert
 \le\beta\eta\sqrt{2R}\{\sqrt{2R}+\lVerte+a\rVert\}
 =:\Lambda_R.
\]
Thinning a homogeneous Poisson process of rate \(\Lambda_R\) therefore
constructs the process from every point of \(K_R\).  Since every initial
state lies in some \(K_R\), this proves well-posedness and non-explosion.

We prove skew balance directly on paths.  The idea is to pair every path with
its time reversal after flipping momentum: collision factors agree one by
one, while the change in target density exactly cancels the change in the
survival probability.  Write \(z=(w,p)\), set
\[
 \Psi(w)=V(y+\sqrt\eta w)-a^\mathrm{T} w,
 \qquad \nu=q_y^w\otimes\gamma_d,
\]
so that the Lebesgue density of \(\nu\) is, up to normalization,
\(\varrho(z)=\exp\{-H(z)-\Psi(w)\}\).  Along a flight,
\(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} u}\Psi(w_u)=s(z_u)\), while a collision changes neither
\(H\) nor \(\Psi\).  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf R\phi_u\mathsf R=\phi_{-u},\qquad
 \mathsf J\mathsf R=\mathsf R\mathsf J,\qquad
 s\circ\mathsf J=s\circ\mathsf R=-s.
 \label{persistent:eq:path-reversal-identities}
\end{equation}

Fix \(t>0\).  For
\(\boldsymbol t=(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\) in the ordered simplex
\(\Delta_n(t)=\{0<t_1<\cdots<t_n<t\}\), let
\(z_u=z_u(z_0,\boldsymbol t)\) be the deterministic skeleton obtained by
following \(\phi\) and inserting a collision \(\mathsf J\) at every
\(t_i\).  On \(K_R\), the iterated-hazard expansion of the transition
operator is
\begin{equation}
 S_t^{y,e}F(z_0)
 =\sum_{n\ge0}\int_{\Delta_n(t)}
   \exp\!\left\{-\int_0^t\lambda(z_u)\,\mathrm{d} u\right\}
   \prod_{i=1}^n\lambda(z_{t_i-})F(z_t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol t .
 \label{persistent:eq:ordered-event-expansion}
\end{equation}
The bound by \(\Lambda_R\) makes this series absolutely convergent.

Pair this skeleton with the one starting at \(\mathsf Rz_t\) and having
ordered collision times
\((t-t_n,\ldots,t-t_1)\).  By
\cref{persistent:eq:path-reversal-identities}, it is the momentum-reversed
time reversal of the original skeleton and ends at \(\mathsf Rz_0\).  The
map
\[
 (z_0,t_1,\ldots,t_n)
 \longmapsto(\mathsf Rz_t,t-t_n,\ldots,t-t_1)
\]
preserves Lebesgue measure.  Indeed, \(\phi_u\) and \(\mathsf R\) preserve
volume, while \(\mathsf J\) does so by applying, for every fixed \(w\), an
orthogonal change of variables in \(p\).  This argument does not require
continuity of the reflection direction at \(r(w)=0\), where the event rate
vanishes.

If \(z^-\) is a forward pre-collision state, the corresponding reverse
pre-collision state is \(\mathsf R\mathsf Jz^-\); hence
\[
 \lambda(\mathsf R\mathsf Jz^-)=\lambda(z^-).
\]
Thus the collision-rate products in
\cref{persistent:eq:ordered-event-expansion} agree.  The reverse survival
hazard is \(\int_0^t[-s(z_u)]_+\,\mathrm{d} u\), and
\[
 \Psi(w_t)-\Psi(w_0)
 =\int_0^t s(z_u)\,\mathrm{d} u
 =\int_0^t\bigl\{[s(z_u)]_+-[-s(z_u)]_+\bigr\}\,\mathrm{d} u.
\]
Since \(H(z_t)=H(z_0)\) and \(\varrho(\mathsf Rz)=\varrho(z)\), this is
exactly the survival-weight identity
\begin{equation}
 \varrho(z_0)\exp\!\left\{-\int_0^t[s(z_u)]_+\,\mathrm{d} u\right\}
 =\varrho(\mathsf Rz_t)
  \exp\!\left\{-\int_0^t[-s(z_u)]_+\,\mathrm{d} u\right\}.
 \label{persistent:eq:path-weight-balance}
\end{equation}

Let \(\nu_R\) be \(\nu\) conditioned on \(K_R\).  Pairing paths term by
term in \cref{persistent:eq:ordered-event-expansion} using
\cref{persistent:eq:path-weight-balance} gives, for bounded measurable
\(F,G\),
\[
 \int F S_t^{y,e}G\,\mathrm{d}\nu_R
 =\int (\mathsf R G)S_t^{y,e}(\mathsf R F)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_R
 =\int (\mathsf R S_t^{y,e}\mathsf R F)G\,\mathrm{d}\nu_R.
\]
Letting \(R\to\infty\) gives the same identity under \(\nu\).  Taking
\(F=1\) proves invariance.  Jensen's inequality then makes
\(S_t^{y,e}\) a contraction on \(L^2(\nu)\), and density of bounded
functions extends the identity to \(L^2(\nu)\), proving
\((S_t^{y,e})^*=\mathsf R S_t^{y,e}\mathsf R\).

Finally, the recursive hazard construction is jointly Borel in
\((y,e,z_0)\): the flow, rate, integrated hazard, and reflection are Borel,
and successive event times are obtained by taking level-hitting times of
the integrated hazard.  Non-explosion gives the measurable pointwise
limit.  Consequently, conditioning on an incoming-state-independent
reference and applying Fubini passes both invariance and the adjoint
identity to every measurable reference mixture.

\label{persistent:lem:affine-control}

\(\mathsf Q\mathsf U\mathsf P f=L_f+e_f\), where
\(\mathbb E[e_f\mid Y_+]=0\).  With

<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Conditionally on \(Y_+=y\), we have
\(Y_-=y+2\sqrt\eta W\).  Set
\(e_f=\mathsf Q\mathsf U\mathsf Pf-L_f\).  The choice of \(a_f(y)\)
makes both \(\mathbb E[e_f\mid Y_+=y]\) and
\(\mathbb E[\nabla_We_f\mid Y_+=y]\) zero.
The conditional position law is one-strongly log-concave in \(W\), so
conditional Poincar\'e gives the first inequality.

For the second, let
\(b_y(w)=\sqrt\eta\,\nabla V(y+\sqrt\eta w)\).  Weak integration by parts
under the conditional density gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(W,b_y(W)\mid y)=D_y,\qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(b_y(W)\mid y)\preceq
 \frac{\beta\eta}{1-\beta\eta}D_y.
\]
Conditional Poincar\'e applied to linear functions gives
\(\Sigma_y\preceq I\), while the Stein identity
\(\mathbb E[\nabla b_y]=D_y+\operatorname{Cov}(b_y)\) and
\(0\preceq\nabla b_y\preceq\beta\eta I\) give
\(0\preceq D_y\preceq\beta\eta I\).
The second relation follows by applying conditional Poincar\'e to \(b_y\)
and using
\((\nabla b_y)^2\preceq(\beta\eta)\nabla b_y\).
These are standard Brascamp--Lieb/Stein calculations
\citep{chewi2023log}; for a \(C^{1,1}\) potential they follow by
mollification and cutoff.

Differentiating \(\mathsf Tf\) weakly and inserting
\(\mathsf Q\mathsf U\mathsf Pf=L_f+e_f\) yields
\[
 \nabla(I-\mathsf T)f
 =\nabla f-a_f+2D_ya_f+\eta^{-1/2}
   \operatorname{Cov}(e_f,b_y(W)\mid Y_+=y).
\]
Take its inner product with \(\nabla f+a_f\) and average over \(y\).
Using \(a_f=\mathsf T\nabla f\), exact rearrangement gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal R_f+4\mathcal D_f
 ={}&\langle\nabla(I-\mathsf T)f,\nabla f+a_f\rangle
 -2\mathbb E\!\left[a_f^\mathrm{T} D_{Y_+}(\nabla f-a_f)\right]\\
 &-\eta^{-1/2}\mathbb E\!\left[
   \operatorname{Cov}(e_f,b_{Y_+}(W)\mid Y_+)^\mathrm{T}(\nabla f+a_f)\right].
\end{align*}
Cauchy--Schwarz, the two conditional covariance bounds above, and the first
inequality control the right-hand side by
\[
 2\lVert\nabla(I-\mathsf T)f\rVert\lVert\nabla f\rVert
 +C\sqrt{\beta\eta\,\mathcal R_f\mathcal D_f}
 +C\sqrt{\beta\eta\,\mathcal R_f
                 (\beta\eta\,\mathcal R_f+\mathcal D_f)}.
\]
For sufficiently small \(\beta\eta\), Young's inequality absorbs the last
two terms and proves \cref{persistent:eq:affine-control}.

\toprule

\midrule

algorithmic output law costs another \(\widetilde O(K^{1/2}d^{1/4})\).  Total-

\label{lem:warm-removal-hypercontractivity}

 \lVertS_uf\rVert_{1+(1+\alpha u)^2(p-1)}\le\lVertf\rVert_p.

 D_2(\nu S_{u_1}\cdots S_{u_m}\Vert\pi)

\(A_u^*f(y)=\mathbb E[f(X)\mid Y=y]\), which maps density ratios on \(\pi\) to

\(A_ug(x)=\mathbb E[g(Y)\mid X=x]\), and one Gibbs row has density operator
\(S_u=A_uA_u^*\).  Put \(c_u=1+\alpha u\).  The two-measure heat-flow

entire exact path, including its observations, and let
\(\mathbb P_\pi^{(m)}\) be the law of the same path started at stationarity.
Because their transition kernels agree,
\[
 \frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb P_{\nu_0}^{(m)}}
      {\mathrm{d}\mathbb P_\pi^{(m)}}
 =\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_0}{\mathrm{d}\pi}(X_0),
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_{\nu_0}^{(m)}(E)
 \le\mathrm{e}^{B/2}\mathbb P_\pi^{(m)}(E)^{1/2}
\]
for every path event \(E\), where the second inequality is
Cauchy--Schwarz.  Under the stationary path, strong log-concavity and the
independent Gaussian observation give exponential tails for
\(\lVertY-x_*\rVert\).  Because the conditional mode solver converges
geometrically and its stopping test is observable, a threshold with
stationary failure probability at most
\(\mathrm{e}^{-B}\delta^2/(4m^2)\) increases its iteration limit only
logarithmically.  A union bound followed by the displayed path inequality
makes the probability of any cap failure at most \(\delta\).  This bound is
for the whole adaptive path, so no independence between successive centers
is used, and its threshold is determined entirely by the displayed problem
parameters.

<!-- lemma-25: body_md -->
\label{lem:warm-removal-coarse-compiler}
Let \(\nu_m^\dagger=\nu_0S_u^m\) be the ideal coarse endpoint with \(m\) as in
\cref{eq:warm-removal-row-count}.  Using either posterior interface in
\cref{prop:warm-removal-posteriors}, there is an algorithmic endpoint
\(\widehat\nu_m\) such that
\(\operatorname{TV}(\widehat\nu_m,\nu_m^\dagger)\le\delta\).  Its number of oracle
queries is deterministically bounded, up to logarithmic factors, by
\(mC_{\rm reg}(K_u,d)\) or \(mC_{\rm HB}(K_u,d)\), respectively.
<!-- end lemma-25 -->
<!-- proof-42: body_md -->
The row-wise coupling has the following form as long as the two paths have
not yet disagreed:
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
 X_{j-1}^\dagger
 &\xrightarrow{\;Y_j^\dagger=X_{j-1}^\dagger+\sqrt uG_j\;}
 &Y_j^\dagger
 &\xrightarrow{\;q_{Y_j^\dagger}\;}
 &X_j^\dagger,\\[0.25em]
 \widehat X_{j-1}
 &\xrightarrow{\;\widehat Y_j=\widehat X_{j-1}+\sqrt uG_j\;}
 &\widehat Y_j
 &\xrightarrow{\;\widehat P_{j,\widehat Y_j}\;}
 &\widehat X_j.
\end{array}
\]
The shared Gaussian \(G_j\) makes
\(Y_j^\dagger=\widehat Y_j\) whenever
\(X_{j-1}^\dagger=\widehat X_{j-1}\).  At that common observation, we use a
maximal coupling of the two conditional draws.

At row \(j\), apply \cref{prop:warm-removal-posteriors} with total error
budget \(\delta/m\); this budget includes the posterior approximation and
all of its mode and work caps.  Couple the exact and approximate
conditional draws maximally whenever the two paths have agreed so far.  To
avoid conditioning on the agreement event, first generate the entire ideal coarse
path and let \(e_j(y)=\operatorname{TV}(\widehat P_{j,y},q_y)\).  The probability
that the first mismatch occurs in row \(j\) is at most
\(\mathbb E[1_{\text{no earlier mismatch}}e_j(Y_j)]\), which is bounded by
the unconditional exact-path average
\(\mathbb E e_j(Y_j)\le\delta/m\).  Summing over the rows gives total mismatch at
most \(\delta\).  The simultaneous choice of all random-center caps is
justified by \cref{lem:warm-removal-random-centers}.

This is ordinary total-variation telescoping; a Hellinger chain rule is not
needed because the requested accuracy affects both posterior interfaces
only logarithmically.  Crucially, every call is work-capped.  Thus the
query bound remains valid after the first coupling disagreement, even
though the later algorithmic centers no longer have the exact center law.
The ideal coarse endpoint \(\nu_m^\dagger\), rather than the algorithmic endpoint,
is the law to which \cref{eq:warm-removal-d2-conversion} is applied.

\label{lem:warm-removal-localization}
Starting from \(x_0\), accelerated optimization followed by one explicit
Gaussian draw produces a law \(q_0\) such that
\[
 D_2(q_0\Vert\pi)\le1+\frac d2\log(2\kappa)
\]
using \(\widetilde O(Q_{\rm loc})\) oracle queries.

<!-- proof-44: body_md -->
Run accelerated gradient descent until the observable certificate
\(\lVert\nabla V(x_{\rm ref})\rVert\le\alpha/\sqrt\beta\) holds.  The standard
strongly-convex accelerated rate and
\(V(x_0)-V(x_*)\le\beta\lVertx_0-x_*\rVert^2/2\) give the stated
\(Q_{\rm loc}\) bound.  Strong convexity then implies
\(\sqrt\beta\lVertx_{\rm ref}-x_*\rVert\le1\).

Draw \(q_0=\mathcal N(x_{\rm ref},(2\beta)^{-1}I_d)\).  Strong convexity bounds
the target normalizer by
\(Z\le\mathrm{e}^{-V(x_*)}(2\pi/\alpha)^{d/2}\), while smoothness gives
\(V(x)\le V(x_*)+\beta\lVertx-x_*\rVert^2/2\).  Completing the square yields
\[
 \left\|\frac{\mathrm{d} q_0}{\mathrm{d}\pi}\right\|_\infty
 \le\mathrm{e}(2\kappa)^{d/2}.
\]
An \(L^\infty\) density-ratio bound by \(M\) implies
\(D_2\le\log M\), proving the result.
