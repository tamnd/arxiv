<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
There exist constants $C>0$ and $k_0\in\mathbb N$ such that, for
every integer $k\ge k_0$, there are real functions
\[
 0\not\equiv u_k\in C^2(\overline{B_3})\cap H^1_0(B_3),
 \qquad V_k\in L^\infty(B_3)
\]
satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-result}
 \Delta u_k=V_ku_k\text{ in }B_3,\qquad
 u_k|_{\partial B_3}=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_0u_k=k,\qquad \|V_k\|_\infty\le Ck^{3/2}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, there is a nonzero homogeneous harmonic polynomial
$\mathcal P_k$ of degree $k$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-expansion}
 D^j(u_k-\mathcal P_k)(x)=O_k(|x|^{k+2-j}),
 \qquad j=0,1,2,\qquad x\to0.
\end{equation}
Both $u_k$ and $V_k$ are smooth in $B_3\setminus\{0\}$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\rm  We have two remarks below. 

\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] The construction extends directly to higher dimensions. 
For any $n\ge 4$, consider the domain $B_3\times B_{n-3}(0,1)$, where $B_{n-3}(0,1)$ 
is the unit ball in dimension $n-3$. Let $v_1$ be the first Dirichlet eigenfunction of $B_{n-3}(0,1)$, with corresponding eigenvalue $\lambda_1$. 
Then $u_k\times v_1$ satisfies 
$$\Delta u_k\times v_1 = (V_k-\lambda_1) u_k\times v_1.$$
The potential $V_k-\lambda_1$ is bounded in norm by a constant times $k^{3/2}$, while $u_k\times v_1$ 
has vanishing order $k$ at the origin. Moreover, one can extend the example to spheres by 
using a conformal mapping and an odd extension.  

\item[2.] Theorem \ref{thm:main} together with part 1 of this remark, 
and Kenig~\cite{Kenig2006} (see also Bakri~\cite{Bakri2013}) imply that in all dimensions larger than two, 
the solutions to the Schr\"indger equation with Dirichlet boundary condition or conditions as in \eqref{eq:normalized-local-ucp}, 
the vanishing order satisfies 
 \begin{equation*}
  \operatorname{ord}_p u \leq  C(1+\|V\|_\infty^{2/3}),\qquad p\in B_1,
\end{equation*}
which meanwhile is best possible in general. 
\end{itemize}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
For every fixed nonnegative integer $s$, there exists $C_s>0$ such that
for every $\omega\in\mathbb{S}^2$, 
\begin{equation}
 |\nabla_{\mathbb S^2}^{s} W_d^i(\omega)|\le C_s d^sW_d^i(\omega),
 \qquad
 |\nabla_{\mathbb S^2}^{s} Z_d^i(\omega)|\le C_s d^sW_d^i(\omega).
 \label{ep-zonal-derivatives}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We prove both derivative estimates.  Write
$H=1-(a_i\cdot\omega)^2+\lambda_d^{-2}$, so $W_d^i=H^{-1/4}$.
One has $H\ge\lambda_d^{-2}$, $|\nabla H|\le2\sqrt H$, and
$|\nabla^jH|\le C_j$ for $j\ge2$.  In particular
$|\nabla\log W_d^i|\le C d$.  Thus $H$ is comparable to its central
value on balls of radius $c/d$.  On rescaling such a ball by $d$, every
derivative of $H/H(\omega_0)$ is bounded: for order one use
$d^{-1}H(\omega_0)^{-1/2}\le C$, and for order $j\ge2$ use
$d^{-j}H(\omega_0)^{-1}\le C_j$.  The chain rule for $H^{-1/4}$ proves
the first estimate in \eqref{ep-zonal-derivatives}.

For the second, fix $0<c<1/8$ and consider the solid extension
\[
 Y_d^i(x)=|x|^d Z_d^i(x/|x|),
\]
which is a harmonic polynomial.   In the ball $B(\omega_0,c/d)$, its radial factor
$|x|^d$ is bounded above and below by constants depending only on $c$. Thus,
\eqref{ep-zonal-size} gives
$$|Y_d^i(x)|\le C W_d^i(x/|x|)\le CW_d^i(\omega_0).$$ 
Interior derivative estimates for harmonic functions give 
\[
 |D^sY_d^i(\omega_0)|\le C_s d^s\fint_{B(\omega_0,c/d)}|Y_d^i(x)|\,dx \le C_sd^sW_d^i(\omega_0).
\]
The restriction chain rule expresses $\nabla_{\mathbb S^2}^sZ_d^i$
as a sum of $D^jY_d^i$, $0\le j\le s$, with bounded smooth tensor
coefficients on the sphere. Since $d\ge1$, the same bound proves the
second estimate in \eqref{ep-zonal-derivatives}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{ep-directions-lemma}
There is a number $\delta>0$, independent of $k,L$ and the number of
patches, such that one can choose four vectors
$t_{j,1},\ldots,t_{j,4}\in\mathbb S^2\cap x_j^\perp$ at each center
with the following property.  Directions in one patch, or in two
patches joined by an edge, satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \min\{|t_{j,\alpha}-t_{i,\beta}|,
          |t_{j,\alpha}+t_{i,\beta}|\}\ge\delta
 \label{ep-direction-separation}
\end{equation}
unless $(j,\alpha)=(i,\beta)$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Order the finitely many vertices arbitrarily and choose the four
directions at a vertex successively.  On the unit circle in $x_j^\perp$,
the set within Euclidean distance $\delta$ of either of two antipodal
unit vectors has arc length at most $C\delta$, uniformly in the vector
and in the plane of the circle.  To check this, if such a set is nonempty
and $\delta<1/10$, the projection of the vector onto that plane has
length at least $1-C\delta^2$; the elementary chord estimate on the
circle then bounds its angular extent by $C\delta$.  If the set is empty
there is nothing to estimate.  At any choice there are at most $4D+3$
previously chosen forbidden directions.  Choose $\delta$ so small that
$C(4D+3)\delta<2\pi$.  A permitted point remains on the circle.  The
induction proves the assertion.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{ep-plane-defect}
On $3/4\le|x|\le9/4$,
\begin{equation}
 |\Delta(\Psi Q)|\le C k^{3/2}\Psi.
 \label{ep-plane-defect-bound}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Write $C_{j\alpha}(x)=\cos(\mu_jt_{j,\alpha}\cdot(x-x_j))$
and $S_{j\alpha}(x)=\sin(\mu_jt_{j,\alpha}\cdot(x-x_j))$.
Since $\Psi'/\Psi=\mu$, the product rule gives
\begin{align*}
 \Psi^{-1}\Delta(\Psi Q)
 =\frac14\sum_{j,\alpha}\Big[&
 \big((\mu^2-\mu_j^2)\theta_j+\Delta\theta_j
       +2\mu\partial_r\theta_j
       +(\mu'+2\mu/r)\theta_j\big)C_{j\alpha}\\
 &-2\mu_j\big(\nabla\theta_j\cdot t_{j,\alpha}
        +\mu\theta_jt_{j,\alpha}\cdot\omega\big)S_{j\alpha}\Big].
\end{align*}
On $\operatorname{supp}\theta_j$, the estimates already proved yield
\[
 |\mu^2-\mu_j^2|\le Ck^2a,\quad

 \quad |t_{j,\alpha}\cdot\omega|\le Ca.
\]
Using also $|\mu'|+2\mu/r\le Ck$ and bounded overlap, we obtain
\[
 \Psi^{-1}|\Delta(\Psi Q)|
 \le C(k^2a+k/a+a^{-2}+k)\le Ck^{3/2}.
\]

 &\le Ck^{3/2}+C\mu\frac{|\chi'|(1+W_k)}{T}
       +C\frac{(|\chi''|+2|\chi'|/r)(1+W_k)}{T}\\
 &\le Ck^{3/2}+C\mu\sqrt k+C(k+\sqrt k)\le Ck^{3/2}.
\end{align*}

$f=(1-\chi_+)p+\chi_+v$. Here $\Delta v=0$ as well; the two
cutoff terms change sign, giving the same bound.

For the remaining derivative bounds use
\eqref{ep-zonal-derivatives}, \eqref{ep-plane-derivatives},
\eqref{ep-radial-estimates} and \eqref{ep-spatial-bridge-bounds}.
Each cutoff derivative costs at most $C\sqrt k$ in the weighted first
derivative and at most $Ck$ in the weighted second derivative.
These are bounded respectively by $C\mu$ and $C\mu^2$.
In each bridge the zonal degree is precisely the local value of $m$,
so its angular derivatives have the same scale $\mu=d/r$.
Direct differentiation gives $|\nabla E|\le C\mu E$; differentiating
$q=f/E$ proves the last bound in \eqref{ep-local-first-second}.
Finally, $|\nabla\mu|/\mu^2\le Ck/L^2\to0$.  Thus $\mu$ is uniformly
comparable on the stated microscopic balls.  Integrating
$|\nabla\log E|\le C\mu$ along line segments gives comparability of $E$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{Classification-zonal-profiles} 
Every normalized zonal profile admits a subsequence
converging locally uniformly to a nonzero real analytic function of one
of the following forms:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a sum of three nonzero cosines with unit frequencies;
\item a single nonzero cosine with unit frequency;
\item a nonzero multiple of a translated radial function $J_0$, 
which is a Bessel function as in \eqref{leg-bessel-definition}. 
\end{enumerate}

For $i=1,2,3$, put
\[
 \theta_i(\omega)=\arccos(a_i\cdot\omega),\qquad
 \theta_{i,j}=\theta_i(\omega_j).
\]
We identify each moving tangent plane with $\mathbb R^2$ by the chosen
orthonormal frame.  On $|z|\le R$, the exponential map satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expansion-moving-frame}
 \exp_{\omega_j}(z/d_j)=\omega_j+\frac{z}{d_j}+O_R(d_j^{-2}).
\end{equation}

\emph{Case 1: $\omega_*$ is not a coordinate pole.}
All $\theta_{i,j}$ stay in a compact subinterval of $(0,\pi)$.  Taylor
expansion of $\arccos$ in \eqref{eq:expansion-moving-frame} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angle-expansion-regular}
 \theta_i(\exp_{\omega_j}(z/d_j))
 =\theta_{i,j}-\frac{a_i\cdot z}{d_j\sin\theta_{i,j}}
       +O_R(d_j^{-2}).
\end{equation}
Since $z\perp\omega_j$, the vector
\[
 u_{i,j}=-\frac{a_i-(a_i\cdot\omega_j)\omega_j}
                  {\sin\theta_{i,j}}
 \in T_{\omega_j}\mathbb S^2
\]
has unit length and the linear term in
\eqref{eq:angle-expansion-regular} is $u_{i,j}\cdot z/d_j$.
The classical bulk asymptotic (see \cite[10.01, p.~312]{Olver1974}) is
\begin{equation}\label{leg-bulk-asymptotic}
 P_d(\cos\alpha)=
 \left(\frac{2}{\pi d\sin\alpha}\right)^{1/2}
 \cos\left((d+\tfrac12)\alpha-\tfrac\pi4\right)
 +O_\delta(d^{-3/2})
\end{equation}
uniformly for $\delta\le\alpha\le\pi-\delta$.  After taking a common
subsequence, the vectors $u_{i,j}$ and the three phases converge.  Since
\[
 W_{d_j}(\omega_j)\longrightarrow
       \sum_{\ell=1}^3(\sin\theta_{\ell,*})^{-1/2},
\]
equations \eqref{eq:angle-expansion-regular} and
\eqref{leg-bulk-asymptotic} yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-zonal-limit}
 F_j(z)\longrightarrow
 \sum_{i=1}^3 B_i\cos(u_i\cdot z+\varphi_i),\qquad
 B_i=\frac{\sqrt{2/\pi}\,(\sin\theta_{i,*})^{-1/2}}
 {\sum_{\ell=1}^3(\sin\theta_{\ell,*})^{-1/2}}>0.
\end{equation}
The convergence is uniform on bounded sets.

The limit in \eqref{eq:regular-zonal-limit} cannot vanish identically.
Indeed, the three tangent projections of $a_1,a_2,a_3$ cannot all be
collinear; otherwise the coordinate vectors would lie in the
two-dimensional span of $\omega_*$ and one tangent vector.  Hence at
least one unoriented frequency $\{u_i,-u_i\}$ occurs only once.  For
completeness, distinct exponentials are linearly independent: choose
$e$ so that the numbers $\xi_j\cdot e$ are distinct and differentiate
$\sum_jc_je^{i\xi_j\cdot te}$ at $t=0$.  The resulting Vandermonde
matrix has nonzero determinant.  Thus the coefficient at the unique
frequency cannot be cancelled.

\emph{Case 2: $\omega_*$ is a coordinate pole.}
Suppose first that $\omega_*=a_i$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pole-weight-dominance}
 W_{d_j}^i(\omega_j)\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 \frac{W_{d_j}^i(\omega_j)}{W_{d_j}(\omega_j)}\longrightarrow1,
\end{equation}
whereas the other two weights remain bounded.  The bound
\eqref{ep-zonal-size} therefore shows that the other two normalized
zonal terms tend to zero locally uniformly.  A negative pole is reduced
to this case by the parity identity $P_d(-t)=(-1)^dP_d(t)$ and a
subsequence of fixed parity.

There are two subcases.  If $d_j\theta_{i,j}$ is bounded, use normal
coordinates at the pole and pass to a subsequence such that
\[
 d_j\exp_{a_i}^{-1}(\omega_j)\longrightarrow p\in\mathbb R^2.
\]
Smooth dependence of normal coordinates, together with
\eqref{eq:expansion-moving-frame}, gives after a harmless fixed rotation
of $z$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pole-coordinate-limit}
 d_j\theta_i(\exp_{\omega_j}(z/d_j))\longrightarrow|p+z|
\end{equation}
locally uniformly.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pole-weight-limit}
 \frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{d_j}}}{W_{d_j}(\omega_j)}
 \longrightarrow(1+|p|^2)^{1/4}.
\end{equation}
The polar limit \eqref{leg-polar-limit} now gives
\begin{equation}\label{leg-zonal-bessel-profile}
 F_j(z)\longrightarrow
 (1+|p|^2)^{1/4}J_0(|p+z|),
\end{equation}
which is analytic and nonzero.  Notice that only the value of the weight
at the center is used in \eqref{eq:pole-weight-limit}; the angular
weight itself need not become constant on the rescaled ball.

It remains to consider $d_j\theta_{i,j}\to\infty$.  
Suppose $i=1$. Since \(\theta_{1,*} = 0\) in this case, the Liouville--Green approximation must account for angles approaching an endpoint. We therefore use the following stronger Liouville--Green approximation: 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
	P_{d_{j}}(\cos \beta)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi d_{j} \sin \beta }}\cos\left(d_{j}\beta+\frac{1}{2}\beta-\frac{1}{4}\pi\right)+s_{d_{j}}(\beta)
\end{equation}
where \(\beta \in (0,\pi)\),  
and the error term satisfies the estimate 
\begin{equation}
	|s_{d_{j}}(\beta)| \leq \frac{3}{4}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{(d_{j}\sin\beta)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the last estimate follows from the formula in \cite{hale2014fast}: 
\begin{align*}
P_{n}(\cos \beta)&=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi \sin \beta }}\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+\frac{3}{2})}
\cos\left((n+\frac{1}{2})\beta-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+\rho(\beta)\\
&=\left[\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi n\sin \beta }}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi \sin \beta }}\left(\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+\frac{3}{2})}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}}\right)\right]\cos\left((n+\frac{1}{2})\beta-\frac \pi4\right)+\rho(\beta), 
\end{align*}
where \(\rho(\beta)\) satisfies the estimate 
\begin{equation}
	| \rho(\beta)| \leq \frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+\frac{3}{2})}
	\frac{1}{(n+\frac{3}{2})\sin^{\frac{3}{2}}\beta}, 
\end{equation}
and 
$$\left|\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+\frac{3}{2})}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}}\right|\le \frac{1}{2n^{3/2}}.$$


Set 
\begin{equation}
u_{i,j}=\frac{a_{i}-a_{i}\cdot \omega_{j} \omega_{j}}{\sin\theta_{i,j}}
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:angle-expansion-regular}, we have 
\begin{equation}
	Z_{d_{j}}^{1}\left(\exp_{\omega_{j}}(\frac{z}{d_{j}})\right)=\sqrt{\frac{2d_{j}+1}{\pi d_{j}\sin(\theta_{1,j} - \frac{u_{1,j} \cdot z}{d_{j}} +O(\frac{1}{d_{j}^{2}}))}}\cos\left(u_{1,j}\cdot z + \varphi_{1,j}+O(d_{j}^{-1})\right)+o(d_{j}^{-1}). 
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
	W_{d_{j}}^{1}(\omega_{j})=(\sin^{2}\theta_{1,j}+\lambda_{d_{j}}^{-2})^{-\frac{1}{4}}
	=\left({\sin\theta_{1,j}}\right)^{-1/2}\left(1+(\lambda_{d_j}\sin\theta_{1,j})^{-2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}},
\end{equation}
we conclude that 
\begin{align*}
 \lim\limits_{j \to \infty} \frac{Z_{d_{j}}^{1}(\exp_{\omega_{j}}(\frac{z}{d_{j}}))}{W_{d_{j}}(\omega_{j})}&=
	  \lim\limits_{j \to \infty} \sqrt{\frac{2d_{j}\sin \theta_{1,j}}{\pi d_{j}\sin(\theta_{1,j} - \frac{u_{1,j} \cdot z}{d_{j}} +O(\frac{1}{d_{j}^{2}}))}}\cos(u_{1,j}\cdot z + \varphi_{1,j}+O(d_{j}^{-1}))\\
&
 =\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cos(u_{1} \cdot z + \varphi_{1}). 
 \end{align*}
Moreover, from \eqref{eq:error}, the above convergence is uniform on every bounded set in \(z\). 
The proposition follows.

To prove \eqref{eq:pure-zonal-ball-density}, write $x_0=r_0\omega_0$
and use the exact change of variables
\[
 x=x_0+\frac{r_0}{d}z=r_0(\omega_0+z/d),\qquad
 \Omega_{d,\omega_0}(z)=
       \frac{\omega_0+z/d}{|\omega_0+z/d|}.
\]
The ball becomes $B(0,c)$, and $r_0$ disappears from its angular
profile. Define
\[
 A_{d,\omega_0}(z)
   =\frac{Z_d(\Omega_{d,\omega_0}(z))}{W_d(\omega_0)},\qquad
 R_{d,\omega_0}(z)
   =\frac{W_d(\Omega_{d,\omega_0}(z))}{W_d(\omega_0)}.
\]
By \eqref{ep-zonal-derivatives},
$|\nabla_{\mathbb S^2}\log W_d|\le Cd$. Since
$d_{\mathbb S^2}(\Omega_{d,\omega_0}(z),\omega_0)\le C_c/d$
for $|z|\le c$ and $d\ge2c$, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pure-zonal-weight-ratio}
 C_c^{-1}\le R_{d,\omega_0}(z)\le C_c,
 \qquad
 \zeta_d(x_0+(r_0/d)z)
       =\frac{A_{d,\omega_0}(z)}{R_{d,\omega_0}(z)}.
\end{equation}
Only comparability is asserted; the weight ratio need not tend to one
in the polar regime.

$d_j\to\infty$, $t_j\downarrow0$, and centers $r_j\omega_j$ such that
the corresponding bad sets occupy more than an $\varepsilon$ fraction
of $B(0,c)$. Proposition~\ref{Classification-zonal-profiles}, together
with the full Euclidean-ball rescaling following its proof, gives a
subsequence for which
\[
 A_{d_j,\omega_j}\longrightarrow A
 \quad\hbox{uniformly on }\overline{B(0,c)},
\]
where $A$ is a nonzero real analytic function. One can apply the
profile classification on a larger fixed ball to obtain the stated
uniform convergence on the closed ball. In particular,
\eqref{eq:pure-zonal-weight-ratio} gives, for every $\delta>0$ and all
sufficiently large $j$,
\[
 \left\{z\in B(0,c):
       |\zeta_{d_j}(r_j\omega_j+(r_j/d_j)z)|<t_j\right\}
 \subset\{|A_{d_j,\omega_j}|<C_ct_j\}
 \subset\{|A|<\delta\}.
\]
Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ makes the measure on the right tend to
zero, since a nonzero real analytic function has a zero set of measure
zero. This is a contradiction. The Jacobian is the constant
$(r_j/d_j)^3$, so relative volumes are unchanged. The argument places
no lower bound on $r_j$ and applies, in particular, when $r_j\to0$.
This proves \eqref{eq:pure-zonal-ball-density}.

We first describe limits along an arbitrary sequence $k_j\to\infty$,
admissible $L_j$ and centers $y_j$.  Passing to subsequences, the centers
converge in the closure of $U_0$ and their radial unit vectors converge to
$\omega_*$.  Write $r_j=|y_j|$ and $\mu_j^*=\mu(y_j)$ and use the exact Euclidean
rescaling
\[
 x=y_j+z/\mu_j^*,\qquad z\in\mathbb R^3.
\]
All estimates below hold locally uniformly in $z$.

For the plane field, only a uniformly bounded number of patches can
meet any fixed rescaled ball.  In fact, for all large $j$ their centers
lie within $3a$ of $y_j$, where $a=k^{-1/2}$ by \eqref{scale-a}; packing bounds their number independently of
the rescaled radius.  Relabel these patches and pass to a subsequence.
Their coefficients converge to numbers $c_i\ge0$ with $\sum_i c_i=1$.
They become constant on the rescaled balls because
\begin{equation}
 \frac{a^{-1}}{\mu_j^*}
      \le C\frac{\sqrt{k_j}}{L_j}\longrightarrow0.
 \label{ep-partition-freezes}
\end{equation}
Their frequency ratios tend to one by
\eqref{ep-frequency-freezing}; their phases have convergent subsequences
modulo $2\pi$.  Thus the plane field converges to
\begin{equation}
 P(z)=\sum_i\frac{c_i}{4}\sum_{\alpha=1}^4
           \cos(v_{i,\alpha}\cdot z+\phi_{i,\alpha}),
 \label{ep-plane-limit}
\end{equation}
where all $v_{i,\alpha}$ are unit vectors in $\omega_*^\perp$.
Any two relevant patch centers are at distance at most $6a$ and hence
are adjacent in the direction graph.  Consequently all the unoriented
frequencies in \eqref{ep-plane-limit} with $c_i>0$ are mutually
$\delta$-separated.  Since some $c_i$ is positive, at least four distinct
unoriented frequencies survive, with nonzero coefficients.  In
particular, $P$ is nonzero.

Near a bridge, including sequences approaching its endpoints, write
the local formula uniformly as
\[
 \mathcal A(x)=\alpha(r)Z_d(\omega)+b(r)Q(x),\qquad
 T(x)=\alpha(r)W_d(\omega)+b(r),\qquad \alpha+b=1.
\]
Here $d=k$ or $d=L$ is fixed on that bridge and on a neighborhood of
either endpoint; extend its coefficient by zero or one as appropriate.
Then $\mu=d/r$ in the entire local representation.  Let
$T_j=T(y_j)$ and $W_j=W_d(y_j/|y_j|)$.  The estimates
\eqref{ep-spatial-bridge-bounds}, applied with the angular argument
held fixed, imply
\begin{equation}
 \frac{|\alpha(r)-\alpha(r_j)|W_j+|b(r)-b(r_j)|}{T_j}
 \le C_R\frac{\sqrt{k_j}}{\mu_j^*}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{if }|r-r_j|\le R/\mu_j^*.
 \label{ep-weighted-coefficients-freeze}
\end{equation}
To see this, put
$G_j(s)=\alpha(s)W_j+b(s)$, with $W_j$ fixed. The weighted
cutoff estimate gives
\[
 |\alpha'(s)|W_j+|b'(s)|\le C\sqrt{k_j}G_j(s),\qquad
 |(\log G_j)'(s)|\le C\sqrt{k_j}.
\]
Since $G_j(r_j)=T_j$, for $\delta=|r-r_j|\le R/\mu_j^*$ we have
\begin{align*}
 G_j(s)&\le T_j e^{C\sqrt{k_j}|s-r_j|},\\
 \frac{|\alpha(r)-\alpha(r_j)|W_j+|b(r)-b(r_j)|}{T_j}
 &\le C\sqrt{k_j}\delta e^{C\sqrt{k_j}\delta}\\
 &\le CR\frac{\sqrt{k_j}}{\mu_j^*}
          e^{CR\sqrt{k_j}/\mu_j^*}\le C_Rk_j^{-1/4}\longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}
This estimate also applies when a coefficient vanishes at the center.
Only $W_j$ is held fixed; the angular weight itself need not converge
to a constant on the rescaled ball.

Define the two center weights
\[
 A_j=\frac{\alpha(r_j)W_j}{T_j},\qquad
 B_j=\frac{b(r_j)}{T_j};\qquad A_j+B_j=1.
\]
After taking a subsequence, $A_j\to A$ and $B_j\to B$.
The bounds on $W_d$ show that its ratio to $W_j$ stays bounded above
and below on each rescaled ball.  This uses comparability, not
convergence to a constant.  Combining this fact with
\eqref{ep-weighted-coefficients-freeze} and the zonal limits described
above shows that
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F_j(z):=
   \frac{ {\mathcal A}(y_j+z/\mu_j^*)}{T_j}
       \longrightarrow A Z_*(z)+B P(z)
 \label{ep-combined-profile}
\end{equation}
locally uniformly.  Here $Z_*$ is a nonzero zonal limiting profile (see Proposition \ref{Classification-zonal-profiles}),
extended constantly in the radial coordinate.  The error from freezing
$\alpha$ tends to zero even when $\alpha(r_j)=0$, because it is
estimated as a difference as in
\eqref{ep-weighted-coefficients-freeze}, rather than as a ratio.

Every limit in \eqref{ep-combined-profile} is nonzero and real analytic.
If $B=0$, it is the nonzero profile $Z_*$.  If $A=0$, it is the
nonzero profile $P$.  If both are positive and $Z_*$ is a sum of at most
three cosines (i.e., $Z_*$ is of one of the first two types in Proposition~\ref{Classification-zonal-profiles}), those cosines can affect at most three unoriented
frequency classes of $P$.  An active patch in $P$ supplies four
classes, all distinct from the classes supplied by every other active
patch.  At least one therefore remains uncancelled.  If $Z_*$ is a
nonzero multiple of a translated $J_0$ (i.e., $Z_*$ is of the third type in Proposition~\ref{Classification-zonal-profiles}), its Fourier transform on the
tangent plane contains a nonzero continuous measure on the unit circle:
this follows directly from the derived integral identity \eqref{leg-bessel-circle}, written as
\[
 J_0(|z-z_0|)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb S^1}
                      e^{i\xi\cdot(z-z_0)}\,d\sigma(\xi).
\]
The Fourier transform of a finite sum of plane waves is supported on
finitely many points, so it cannot cancel this continuous measure.
This treats the polar case as well.

For centers staying outside the bridges, the same argument reduces
either to the plane limit \eqref{ep-plane-limit} or to a pure zonal
limit.  A microscopic ball cannot meet both bridges for large $j$.
Thus every sequence of normalized profiles admits a subsequence
converging locally uniformly to a nonzero real analytic function.

The preceding argument gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F_j(z)=
   \frac{ {\mathcal A}(y_j+z/\mu_j^*)}{T_j}
       \longrightarrow A Z_*(z)+B P(z), 
\end{equation}
where the limit is a nonzero real analytic function. 
If \eqref{ep-full-ball-density} were false, the argument in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lemma-inner-small} would yield a contradiction, since a nonzero real analytic function
cannot vanish on a set of positive measure. This proves the lemma.

choices are independent of $k$ and $L$; increasing $k_0$ ensures that
both $k$ and $L\ge k^{3/4}$ exceed the required pure-zonal degree
threshold and that all sampling-ball inclusions used above hold.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:weighted-correction}
  Suppose that for a fixed $c>0$,
$a(x)=c/\mu(x)<|x|/2$. 
Let $D\subset\Omega\setminus\{0\}$ be open and for some $\nu\in (0,1)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-domain}
 |D\cap B_{a(x)}(x)|\le\nu|B_{a(x)}(x)|, 
 \qquad x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}.
\end{equation}
If $g\in L^\infty(\Omega)$ has compact support in $\Omega$ and
$|g|\le HE$ almost everywhere on $D$, then the solution
\[
 -\Delta w=g\quad\text{in }D,\qquad w\in H^1_0(D),
\]
satisfies, provided $\nu\le\nu_*(c,C_0)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-correction}
 |w(x)|\le C\nu^{2/3}H\frac{E(x)}{\mu(x)^2}
 \quad\text{for almost every }x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}.
\end{equation}
Here $C$ depends only on $c,C_0$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Extend $g$ by zero outside $\Omega$. Sobolev's inequality gives
\[
 \left|\int_Dg\phi\right|
 \le\|g\|_{6/5}\|\phi\|_6
 \le C\|g\|_{6/5}\|\nabla\phi\|_2.
\]
The Riesz representation theorem on $H^1_0(D)$ proves existence
and uniqueness. First solve the same problem on
$D_j=D\cap\{|x|>j^{-1}\}$, and denote its zero extension by $w_j$.
For $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$, $\phi\ge0$, test the equation with
$\beta_\varepsilon'(w_j)\phi$, where
$\beta_\varepsilon(t)=\sqrt{t^2+\varepsilon^2}-\varepsilon$.
This test function belongs to $H^1_0(D_j)$ because
$\beta_\varepsilon'(0)=0$. Since $\beta_\varepsilon''\ge0$,
\[
 \int\beta_\varepsilon'(w_j)\nabla w_j\cdot\nabla\phi
 \le\int_{D_j}|g|\phi.
\]
Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-extension-kato}
 -\Delta|w_j|\le |g|\mathbf1_{D_j}
 \quad\text{in }\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^3).
\end{equation}
This inequality accounts for the boundary of $D_j$; no global
Poisson equation for the zero extension is asserted.

Let $\Gamma(x)=(4\pi|x|)^{-1}$. Comparing \eqref{eq:zero-extension-kato}
with the Newton potential, using the positive part of their difference
as test function, gives
\[
 |w_j|\le\Gamma*(|g|\mathbf1_{D_j})\le\Gamma*|g|.
\]
The last function is bounded. Since $E/\mu^2$ has a positive
minimum on the compact set
$\{x\in\overline\Omega:|x|\ge j^{-1}\}$, it follows that
\[
 T_j=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}}
          \frac{\mu(x)^2|w_j(x)|}{E(x)}<\infty.
\]
For $x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}$,   the submean inequality gives  
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-newton-average}
 |w_j(x)|\le\fint_{B_{a(x)}(x)}|w_j(y)|\,dy
      +C\int_{D_j\cap B_{a(x)}(x)}\frac{|g(y)|}{|x-y|}\,dy.
\end{equation}
Note that for every measurable $S\subset B_a(x)$, rearrangement gives
\[
 \int_S|x-y|^{-1}\,dy\le C|S|^{2/3}.
\]
The second term of \eqref{eq:local-newton-average} is therefore at
most $CHE(x)a(x)^2\nu^{2/3}$, by \eqref{eq:comparable-emu-1}, \eqref{eq:sparse-domain} and $|g|\le HE$. 
Similarly, the first is at most
$C\nu T_jE(x)/\mu(x)^2$, because $w_j=0$ off $D_j$.
Only points of $\Omega$ enter these two estimates, even when
$B_a(x)$ crosses $\partial\Omega$. Thus
\[
 T_j\le C\nu T_j+C\nu^{2/3}H.
\]
Choose $\nu_*>0$ so that $C\nu_*\le1/2$ and absorb the first term.
This proves \eqref{eq:weighted-correction} for $w_j$, uniformly in $j$.

The increasing spaces $H^1_0(D_j)$ have dense union in $H^1_0(D)$.
In the Dirichlet inner product, $w_j$ is the orthogonal projection
of $w$ onto $H^1_0(D_j)$. Hence $w_j\to w$ in $H^1_0(\Omega)$ and
in $L^6(\Omega)$. An almost-everywhere convergent subsequence
transfers the estimate to $w$.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:ball-smoothing}
For a sufficiently small fixed $c_0$ in
\eqref{eq:interior-smoothing-radius}, the functions
$F=\mathcal S f^*$ on $\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $f^*$ on
$\Omega_{\mathrm{out}}=\{x:3-2\delta<|x|<3\}$ agree on their overlap.
They define a function $u$ smooth in $\overline\Omega\setminus\{0\}$,
with $u|_{\partial\Omega}=0$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothed-proximity}
 |u-f|\le\tau E/8\quad\text{in }\Omega\setminus\{0\},
 \qquad u=f^*\quad\text{on }\mathcal U.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-bound}
 |\Delta u|
 \le C\tau^{-1}(H_0+L^2+H\sigma^{-2})|u|
 \le CH_0|u|
 \quad\text{in }\Omega\setminus\{0\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Reduce $c_0$ so that $E,\mu$ vary by factors at most $2$ on
each sampling ball, using \eqref{eq:global-wavelength} and the
local comparability established in \eqref{eq:comparable-emu}.
To quantify the variation of $q$, let $C_q$ be a constant independent
of $k,L$ such that $|\nabla q|\le C_q\mu$, as provided by
\eqref{eq:global-wavelength}. For $x\in\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}$ and
$y\in B_{\ell(x)}(x)$, the segment $x+t(y-x)$, $0\le t\le1$,
lies in $B_{\ell(x)}(x)\subset\Omega\setminus\{0\}$, and
$\mu(x+t(y-x))\le2\mu(x)$. Hence
\[
 |q(y)-q(x)|
 \le |y-x|\int_0^1|\nabla q(x+t(y-x))|\,dt
 \le 2C_q\mu(x)|y-x|
 \le 2C_q\sigma=2C_qc_0\tau.
\]
Choose also $2C_qc_0\le1/4$, so that this difference is at most
$\tau/4$ uniformly in $x,k,L$.
Equations \eqref{eq:small-correction} and
$|\nabla f|\le C\mu E$ (see  \eqref{eq:global-wavelength}) imply
\[
 |\mathcal Sf-f|\le C\sigma E,\qquad
 |\mathcal Sw|\le\tau E/50.
\]
Choose also $Cc_0\le1/16$. Then
$|F-f|\le(1/16+1/50)\tau E<\tau E/8$.
For $x\in\mathcal U\cap\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}$, we have
$|q(x)|<\tau/2$. Thus every $y\in B_{\ell(x)}(x)$ satisfies
\[
 |q(y)|\le |q(x)|+|q(y)-q(x)|
 <\frac{\tau}{2}+\frac{\tau}{4}
 =\frac{3\tau}{4}<\tau.
\]
Together with $B_{\ell(x)}(x)\subset\Omega\setminus\{0\}$,
this proves $B_{\ell(x)}(x)\subset D$. Since $f^*$ is harmonic
there and $\varphi$ is radial, $F(x)=f^*(x)$.

For a point in $\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}\cap\Omega_{\mathrm{out}}$,
one has $\delta<3-|x|<2\delta$. If $|y-x|<\ell(x)<\delta/2$, then
\[
 \delta/2<3-|y|<5\delta/2.
\]
Thus the sampling ball lies in $A_{4\delta}\subset D$, and again
$F=f^*$. Equality on this open overlap makes the two definitions
of $u$ consistent and smooth, without a transition cutoff.
Equation \eqref{eq:boundary-harmonic-regularity} gives smoothness
up to $\partial\Omega$ and zero boundary values.
On $\Omega_{\mathrm{out}}\subset\mathcal U$ we have $u=f^*$ and
$|u-f|=|w|\le\tau E/100$, proving \eqref{eq:smoothed-proximity}.

On $\mathcal U$, the function $u=f^*$ is harmonic. On its complement,
\[
 |u|\ge |f|-|u-f|\ge3\tau E/8.
\]
This complement lies in $\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}$. There,
\eqref{eq:smoothed-correction} and
\eqref{eq:smoothed-approximation} give
\[
 |\Delta u|\le C(H_0+L^2+H\sigma^{-2})E\le C\tau^{-1} (H_0+L^2+H\sigma^{-2})|u|, 
\]
which proves the first inequality in \eqref{eq:quotient-bound}.
Finally $L^2\asymp KH_0$ and $H=H_0+\tau L^2$; since
$\nu,\tau,K,\sigma$ are fixed independently of $k$, the coefficient
is at most $CH_0$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:core-order}
The function $u$ extends to a $C^2$ function at zero and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-expansion}
 D^j(u-\mathcal P_k)(x)=O_k(|x|^{k+2-j}),
 \qquad j=0,1,2,\quad x\to0.
\end{equation}
In particular, $\Delta u(0)=0$ and $\operatorname{ord}_0u=k$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
In the core, $f=\mathcal P_k=r^kZ_k$, $E=r^kW_k$, and $\mu=k/r$.
For every fixed $k$, $W_k$ is bounded on $\mathbb S^2$.
Thus \eqref{eq:smoothed-correction} gives
\[
 |D^j\mathcal Sw(x)|
 \le C_k r^k(r/k)^{2-j}=O_k(r^{k+2-j}),
 \qquad j=0,1,2.
\]
On a sufficiently small ball, radial averaging gives
$\mathcal Sf=f=\mathcal P_k$. Since $u=\mathcal S(f+w)$ there,
this proves \eqref{eq:core-expansion}. For $k\ge1$, the second
derivatives of the remainder tend to zero; its value and first
derivatives extend consistently to zero as well. Hence $u$ has
a $C^2$ extension, and harmonicity of $\mathcal P_k$ gives
$\Delta u(0)=0$.

Choose $\omega_0\in\mathbb S^2$ with $\mathcal P_k(\omega_0)\ne0$.
For small $r$ the expansion yields
\[
 2^{-k-1}|\mathcal P_k(\omega_0)|r^k
 \le |u((r/2)\omega_0)|
 \le\sup_{B_r}|u|\le C_kr^k.
\]
By \eqref{label-vanishing}, these inequalities imply $\operatorname{ord}_0u=k$.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $u_k=u$ be the function of Proposition~\ref{prop:ball-smoothing},
with the extension in Proposition~\ref{prop:core-order}, and set
\[
 V_k(x)=
 \begin{cases}
  \Delta u_k(x)/u_k(x),&u_k(x)\ne0,\\
  0,&u_k(x)=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:quotient-bound} gives
$\|V_k\|_\infty\le Ck^{3/2}$.
Every zero of $u_k$ in $\Omega\setminus\{0\}$ belongs to $\mathcal U$,
on which $u_k$ is harmonic. Consequently $V_k$ is identically zero on $\mathcal U$ and is
smooth throughout $\Omega\setminus\{0\}$, using the quotient on the
complement of its zero set. At zero, $u_k(0)=\Delta u_k(0)=0$.
We therefore have, pointwise and distributionally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-equation}
 \Delta u_k=V_ku_k\quad\text{in }B_3.
\end{equation}
The boundary construction and the core expansion show
$u_k\in C^2(\overline{B_3})$, $u_k|_{\partial B_3}=0$, and hence
$u_k\in H^1_0(B_3)$.
Finally, Proposition~\ref{prop:core-order} proves
\eqref{eq:main-expansion}, nontriviality, and $\operatorname{ord}_0u_k=k$.
