<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
 At least $508$ pairwise nondiffeomorphic exotic smooth fifteen-spheres admit smooth Riemannian metrics with strictly positive sectional curvature. More precisely, the construction gives $1015$ distinct nonstandard oriented diffeomorphism classes carrying such metrics.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:topology}
For every integer $m$, there is a smooth map $\beta_m:S^{14}\to S^3$ satisfying \eqref{eq:equivariance} such that the homotopy sphere
\[
 \Sigma_m=D^{15}_+\cup_{J_{\beta_m}}D^{15}_-
\]
has Eells--Kuiper invariant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu-main}
 \mu(\Sigma_m)=\frac{15m}{1016}\quad\text{in }\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z.
\end{equation}
The orientation is induced by the spin filling constructed below, and $\mu$ is normalized by \eqref{eq:mu-definition}. The choice at $m=0$ can be made so that $\Sigma_0$ is the standard sphere. The classes $\Sigma_m$, $0\leq m\leq1015$, are pairwise distinct as oriented smooth manifolds; those with $1\leq m\leq508$ are exotic and pairwise nondiffeomorphic even after orientation is forgotten.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:input}
For the data $(U,\tau,\alpha)$ above, there are smooth metrics $g_+$ and $g_-$ on two standard $n$-disks with $\sec_{g_\pm}>0$ such that, on their boundary,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compatible-boundaries}
 h_+=(J_\alpha^\tau)^*h_-,\qquad
 \mathrm{II}_++(J_\alpha^\tau)^*\mathrm{II}_->0.
\end{equation}
The closed manifold $D^n_+\cup_{J_\alpha^\tau}D^n_-$ therefore admits a smooth Riemannian metric with strictly positive sectional curvature.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:connection-input}
Assume $\sec_{g_0}\geq\kappa>0$. If a smooth function $\varphi:B_0\to\mathbb R$ and a real number $\Lambda$ satisfy
\[
 -\operatorname{Hess}_{g_0}\varphi\geq\Lambda g_0,
 \qquad \Lambda>M_0^2+2M_1^2/\kappa,
\]
then $G_{r_\varepsilon}$ has strictly positive sectional curvature for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, where
\[
 r_\varepsilon^2=\varepsilon e^{\varepsilon\varphi}.
\]
The allowed smallness of $\varepsilon$ depends on the fixed base, connection, and function.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:gauge-family}
For each $k\in180\mathbb Z$, there is a based smooth family $f:S^3\to\mathcal G_2$ whose evaluation at a chosen Hopf frame has degree $k$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The relevant portions of the homotopy exact sequences of \eqref{eq:gauge-fibrations} are
\[
 \pi_3\mathcal G_1\longrightarrow\pi_3(S^3)
 \longrightarrow\pi_6(S^3),
 \qquad
 \pi_3\mathcal G_2\longrightarrow\pi_3\mathcal G_1
 \longrightarrow\pi_{10}(S^3).
\]
The right-hand terms are $\pi_2(\Omega^4S^3)=\pi_6(S^3)$ and $\pi_2(\Omega^8S^3)=\pi_{10}(S^3)$. The classical groups are $\pi_6(S^3)\cong\mathbb Z/12$ and $\pi_{10}(S^3)\cong\mathbb Z/15$; see \cite[Section~1.a and Example~3.6]{CG}. Let $\iota$ generate $\pi_3(S^3)\cong\mathbb Z$. The first obstruction homomorphism kills $12\iota$, so exactness gives a class $a\in\pi_3\mathcal G_1$ evaluating to $12\iota$. The second obstruction homomorphism kills $15a$, so there is a class $b\in\pi_3\mathcal G_2$ restricting to $15a$. Evaluation therefore sends $b$ to $180\iota$.

For $k=180m$, represent the class $mb$ by a based family. Precompose with a smooth degree-one map of the parameter sphere that collapses a neighborhood of its basepoint, and then use the relative smoothing just described. The result is jointly smooth and the identity on that neighborhood. Neither obstruction group is being asserted to be the image of its obstruction map; only its exponent is used.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:bundle}
For every $k\in180\mathbb Z$, there is a quaternionic line bundle $\xi_k\to B$ whose restriction to every $\mathbb H\mathrm P^2$-slice is isomorphic to $\gamma_2$ and whose second Chern class is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2}
 e_k=c_2(\xi_k)=x+ky.
\end{equation}
Its sphere bundle $E_k=S(\xi_k)$ is an $S^{11}$-bundle over the second factor $S^4$, with a transition family $f$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge-family}.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Decompose $S^4=D^4_+\cup_{S^3}D^4_-$. Glue two fixed copies of the Hopf principal bundle over $\mathbb H\mathrm P^2\times D^4_\pm$ by the family $f$, retaining their standard Hopf coordinates: $f$ maps the southern ($-$) coordinates to the northern ($+$) coordinates. The associated quaternionic line bundle is $\xi_k$. Its restriction to a projective slice is the tautological bundle. On $\{b_0\}\times S^4$, the clutching map is the evaluation of $f$ at a frame above $b_0$, and consequently has second Chern number $k$ with the chosen orientation. These two restrictions determine \eqref{eq:c2}. The total space of the sphere bundle over each projective slice is $S(\gamma_2)=S^{11}$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:homotopy}
The manifold $\widehat\Sigma_k$ is a homotopy fifteen-sphere.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $A_1=S^{11}\times D^4$, $A_2=D^{12}\times S^3$, and $C=S^{11}\times S^3$ be the pieces and their common boundary. Their homology groups are free, and the only nonzero reduced groups are
\[
 \widetilde H_{11}(A_1)=\mathbb Z,\qquad
 \widetilde H_3(A_2)=\mathbb Z,\qquad
 \widetilde H_j(C)=\mathbb Z\quad(j=3,11,14).
\]
Here $\widetilde H$ denotes reduced homology. The Mayer--Vietoris map
\[
 H_j(C)\longrightarrow H_j(A_1)\oplus H_j(A_2)
\]
is an isomorphism for $j=3,11$. In degree three this follows from the unchanged parameter coordinate. In degree eleven it follows because $f_p$ is orientation preserving: a path from the parameter basepoint to $p$ connects it to the identity through diffeomorphisms. The top segment is
\[
 0\longrightarrow H_{15}(\widehat\Sigma_k)
 \longrightarrow H_{14}(C)\longrightarrow0.
\]
All the other segments give vanishing reduced homology below degree fifteen. Thus $\widehat\Sigma_k$ has the integral homology of $S^{15}$.

Both pieces and $C$ are simply connected, so the van Kampen theorem gives $\pi_1(\widehat\Sigma_k)=0$. Successive applications of the Hurewicz theorem show that the first possible nonzero homotopy group is in degree fifteen. A representative of its generator gives a map from $S^{15}$ inducing an isomorphism on all homology groups. The homological form of Whitehead's theorem for simply connected CW complexes then gives a homotopy equivalence.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:twisted-sphere}
The map $J_{\widehat\beta_k}$ is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of $S^{14}$, and there is an oriented disk presentation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disk-presentation}
 \widehat\Sigma_k\cong D^{15}_+\cup_{J_{\widehat\beta_k}}D^{15}_-.
\end{equation}
The orientations of the abstract disks are pulled back from the surgery boundary.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For any conjugation-equivariant map $\beta$, equivariance with $q=\beta(z)$ gives $\beta(J_\beta(z))=\beta(z)$. Hence $J_\beta^{-1}=J_{\beta^{-1}}$. In our case the map is the identity on the nonempty open set $|a|>r_0$, so it preserves orientation.

The relevant equatorial decomposition is
\[
 S^{14}=(S^{11}\times D^3)\cup_{S^{11}\times S^2}(D^{12}\times S^2).
\]
On its first piece, \eqref{eq:beta} identifies the twist with $(w,p)\mapsto(f_p(w),p)$; on its second piece it is the identity. The radial rescaling by $r_0$ only changes the parametrization.

We give the disk-cutting construction of \cite[Lemma~3.3]{DHZ} explicitly in the present dimensions. Put $Q_0=D^3$, $I=[-1,1]$, and identify the parameter disk with the rounded product $Q_0\times I$. Choose the transition family to be supported in the interior of the top face $Q_0\times\{1\}$ of its boundary. Write $g_p=f_{(p,1)}$ there; this is the family previously parameterized by $D^3$, and $g_p=\mathrm{id}$ near $\partial Q_0$.

Let $I_+=[0,1]$, $I_-=[-1,0]$, and define the two boundary three-disks
\[
 H_+=(Q_0\times\{1\})\cup(\partial Q_0\times I_+),\qquad
 H_-=(Q_0\times\{-1\})\cup(\partial Q_0\times I_-).
\]
The cut pieces of \eqref{eq:surgery-sphere} are
\[
 Y_\pm=(S^{11}\times Q_0\times I_\pm)\cup(D^{12}\times H_\pm).
\]
They have common boundary
\[
 C_0=(S^{11}\times Q_0)\cup(D^{12}\times\partial Q_0)
     =\partial(D^{12}\times Q_0).
\]
With the identity transition, these are the standard two halves of
$\partial(D^{12}\times Q_0\times I)$ and are $15$-disks after rounding corners. The lower piece already has that identity transition.

On the upper cylindrical part change coordinates by
\[
 (w,p,s)\longmapsto(g_p^{-1}(w),p,s),
\]
and use the identity on $D^{12}\times H_+$. On the top attaching face the first map cancels $g_p$; on the side attaching face it is the identity because $g_p=\mathrm{id}$ near $\partial Q_0$. Thus the two maps agree along the attachment and give a diffeomorphism from $Y_+$ to the untwisted standard half. This proves that both $Y_\pm$ are standard disks without extending any individual $g_p$ over $D^{12}$.

On $C_0$, the lower sphere coordinate equals $g_p$ applied to the upper coordinate. To compare with the earlier unit-sphere model, its product region is parameterized by
\[
 (p,u)\longmapsto
 \bigl(r_0p,\sqrt{1-r_0^2|p|^2}\,u\bigr),
 \qquad p\in D^3,\quad u\in S^{11}.
\]
The map $J_{\widehat\beta_k}$ defined using \eqref{eq:beta} sends this point to the one with coordinates $(p,g_p(u))$. The complementary $D^{12}\times S^2$ region is fixed. Consequently the boundary transition is exactly $J_{\widehat\beta_k}$. Pulling back orientations from $\partial Z_k$ gives the claimed oriented presentation; the induced boundary orientations are opposite even though the transition preserves the common ambient orientation on $S^{14}$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:capped-fiber}
The capped fiber is a smoothly embedded manifold $F\subset Z_k$ diffeomorphic, without a specified orientation, to $\mathbb H\mathrm P^3$. Its normal bundle is trivial of rank four. Under the restriction of $x$ to $F$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p-hp3}
 p(TF)=1-4x+12x^2-8x^3.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
In right quaternionic coordinates, a vector of the tautological disk bundle over $\mathbb H\mathrm P^2$ can be written $v=wa$, where $|w|=1$ and $|a|\leq1$. The map
\[
 ([w],wa)\longmapsto[w,\bar a]\in\mathbb H\mathrm P^3
\]
is well defined: replacing $w$ by $wq$ replaces $a$ by $q^{-1}a$, and hence multiplies all projective coordinates on the right by $q$. Write the coordinates of $\mathbb H\mathrm P^3$ as $[z,t]$, with $z\in\mathbb H^3$ and $t\in\mathbb H$. The image is the region $|z|\geq|t|$, namely the complement of the open affine unit $12$-ball. The inverse on this region is
\[
 [z,t]\longmapsto\left([z],\frac{z\bar t}{|z|^2}\right).
\]
It is unchanged by simultaneous right multiplication of $z,t$ by a nonzero quaternion and is smooth because $z\ne0$ on that region. This proves the diffeomorphism of manifolds with boundary, not only a set identification. On $|a|=1$, the affine coordinate is $w(\bar a)^{-1}=wa=v$. Therefore the boundary map of the complementary standard ball is exactly the sphere parameterization fixed for the surgery. Capping by the handle core gives $F\cong\mathbb H\mathrm P^3$; compare \cite[Section~3, p.~28]{CG}.

The normal coordinates along the disk-bundle fiber are the four base coordinates in the fixed southern chart. For a sufficiently small base disk $D^4_\delta$, a neighborhood of the capped fiber is
\[
 \bigl(D(\gamma_2)\cup_{\mathrm{id}}D^{12}\bigr)\times D^4_\delta
 \cong\mathbb H\mathrm P^3\times D^4_\delta.
\]
The product framing and product corner smoothing give this identification on both sides of the attachment. Hence the normal bundle of $F$ is $F\times\mathbb R^4$.

The inclusion $D(\gamma_2)\subset F$ attaches only a $12$-cell and induces an isomorphism in degree four. Consequently the extended classes restrict as $x|_F=c_2(\gamma_3)$ and $y|_F=0$. The standard projective-space formula, writing $x=c_2(\gamma_r)$ on $\mathbb H\mathrm P^r$, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p-hpn}
 p(T\mathbb H\mathrm P^r)=\frac{(1-x)^{2r+2}}{1-4x}.
\end{equation}
It follows from the stable tangent-bundle identity in \cite[proof of Theorem~3.1, p.~31]{CG}, where the positive generator is written $-c_2(\gamma)$. Expanding at $r=3$ gives \eqref{eq:p-hp3}. The possible reversal of the orientation of $F$ does not alter this identity of characteristic classes.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:pontryagin}
The first three Pontryagin classes of $Z_k$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pontryagin}
 \begin{aligned}
 p_1(TZ_k)&=-4x-2ky,\\
 p_2(TZ_k)&=12x^2+6kxy,\\
 p_3(TZ_k)&=-8x^3-18kx^2y.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Moreover, $Z_k$ is spin.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For a quaternionic line with second Chern class $e$, the underlying real bundle has $p_1=-2e$ and $p_2=e^2$. On $B$, formula \eqref{eq:p-hpn} gives
\[
 p(TB)=1-2x+7x^2,
\]
because $TS^4$ is stably trivial. The splitting $TZ_k|_B=TB\oplus\xi_k$ and $e=x+ky$ therefore give
\[
 p_1|_B=-4x-2ky,\quad
 p_2|_B=12x^2+6kxy,\quad
 p_3|_B=-18kx^2y.
\]
The restriction isomorphisms determine $p_1,p_2$ globally. They determine only the $x^2y$ coefficient of $p_3$. On the capped fiber the normal bundle is trivial, so Lemma~\ref{lem:capped-fiber} determines the remaining coefficient to be $-8$. Finally $H^2(Z_k;\mathbb Z/2)=0$, and hence its oriented tangent bundle has vanishing second Stiefel--Whitney class.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:numbers}
The signature is $\operatorname{sign}(Z_k)=0$, and its relative Pontryagin numbers are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numbers}
 \langle p_1^4\rangle=256k,\quad
 \langle p_1^2p_2\rangle=96k,\quad
 \langle p_2^2\rangle=0,\quad
 \langle p_1p_3\rangle=56k.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The classes $ux,uy$ form an integral basis of $H^8(Z_k,\partial Z_k)$: their images are $x^2+kxy,xy$. Using the Thom identity, the intersection form in this basis is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \langle (x+ky)x^2,[B]\rangle & \langle (x+ky)xy,[B]\rangle\\
 \langle (x+ky)xy,[B]\rangle & \langle (x+ky)y^2,[B]\rangle
 \end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}k&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant is $-1$, so its signature is zero. Formula \eqref{eq:thom} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:top-products}
 \langle x^4\rangle=\langle(x-ky)x^2,[B]\rangle=-k,
 \qquad
 \langle x^3y\rangle=\langle(x-ky)xy,[B]\rangle=1.
\end{equation}
All products containing $y^2$ vanish. Substitution of \eqref{eq:pontryagin} into \eqref{eq:top-products} gives \eqref{eq:numbers}. In particular, the $-8x^3$ term of $p_3$ contributes to the last number and cannot be omitted.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:mu}
For the orientation determined by $Z_k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu-k}
 \mu(\widehat\Sigma_k)=\frac{k}{12192}\pmod{\mathbb Z}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
The signature vanishes. By \eqref{eq:N} and Proposition~\ref{prop:numbers},
\[
 \langle N_4(TZ_k),[Z_k,\partial Z_k]\rangle
 =\frac{153(256k)-360(96k)+192(56k)}{187269120}
 =\frac{k}{12192}.
\]
This is \eqref{eq:mu-k}.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Choose $k=180m$ and set $\Sigma_m=\widehat\Sigma_{180m}$ and $\beta_m=\widehat\beta_{180m}$, using \eqref{eq:beta}. Proposition~\ref{prop:twisted-sphere} gives the required presentation, and Proposition~\ref{prop:mu} gives
\[
 \mu(\Sigma_m)=\frac{180m}{12192}=\frac{15m}{1016}\pmod{\mathbb Z}.
\]
Since $\gcd(15,1016)=1$, these values are distinct for $0\leq m\leq1015$. For $m=0$ take the identity gauge family; its twist is the identity and its sphere is standard. Each of the remaining $1015$ values is nonzero, so each corresponding sphere is exotic.

An unoriented diffeomorphism between two connected oriented manifolds either preserves or reverses orientation. Thus a diffeomorphism $\Sigma_i\cong\Sigma_j$ would imply $i\equiv j$ or $i\equiv-j\pmod{1016}$. For $1\leq i<j\leq508$, neither congruence is possible. This proves the asserted unoriented count. No converse to the invariant test is used.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:signature-test}
The exoticness of the first example can also be seen without the Dirac index. If $\widehat\Sigma_{180}$ were standard, capping its filling by $D^{16}$ would give a closed oriented manifold $M$ with signature zero and the same four numbers in \eqref{eq:numbers}. Formula \eqref{eq:L} would then force
\[
 381p_4[M]=2632\cdot180,\qquad
 p_4[M]=\frac{157920}{127}\notin\mathbb Z,
\]
contradicting integrality of Pontryagin numbers. The Eells--Kuiper invariant is needed above to distinguish the larger family, not just to detect this one exotic sphere.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
For each integer $m$, Theorem~\ref{thm:topology} supplies a smooth conjugation-equivariant $\beta_m$ for the orthogonal representation \eqref{eq:rho}. Its real dimension is $3+3\cdot4=15$. Theorem~\ref{thm:input} therefore gives a smooth positively curved metric on $\Sigma_m$. The distinctness and exoticness conclusions for $1\leq m\leq1015$, and the unoriented count for $1\leq m\leq508$, are precisely those of Theorem~\ref{thm:topology}.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The construction does not assert positive sectional curvature on every smooth fifteen-sphere. The invariant distinguishes the displayed family, but it does not classify all smooth structures or determine which others admit the required equivariant presentation. Extending the count requires additional topological constructions or a classification of their images, rather than a change in the infinitesimal bound \eqref{eq:action-bound}.
