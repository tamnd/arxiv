<!-- question-1: body_md -->
Does the bigraded BV algebra structure persist when the
Poisson structure is non-unimodular?
<!-- end question-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{mainthm1.1}
Let $\Sigma$ be a finite-dimensional bigraded vector
space over $k$, and let
$A=\mathcal{O}(\Sigma)$ be equipped with a Poisson
bivector $\pi$ of degree $(0,-2)$.
Let $\gamma$ be the standard mixed volume form on $A$.
If the modular derivation determined by $\gamma$ is diagonalizable over $k$,
then $\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)$ admits a
canonical Batalin--Vilkovisky algebra structure whose operator
has cohomological degree $(0,-1)$ and generates
the induced Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{mainthm}
Let $A=(\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n}),\pi)$ be an
admissible quadratic Poisson algebra,
and let
$A^!=(\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m}),\pi^!)$
be its Koszul dual Poisson algebra.
Equip $A$ and $A^!$ with the mixed volume forms
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma=dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_m
 \wedge y_1^*\wedge\cdots\wedge y_n^*,\quad
\gamma^!=\xi_1^*\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi_m^*
 \wedge d\eta_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d\eta_n.
\end{aligned}
$$
Assume that the modular derivation of $A$ determined by $\gamma$ is diagonalizable over $k$.
Then the Koszul correspondence induces an isomorphism
$$
    \mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
    \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}
    \mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^!)
$$
of Batalin--Vilkovisky algebras, with the BV operators
determined by $\gamma$ and $\gamma^!$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
Let $k^{m||n}=\mathrm{Span}_k\{x_1,\cdots, x_m, y_1,\cdots, y_n\}$
with
$|x_i|=(0,0)$ and $|y_j|=(1,0)$.
The coordinate degrees thus give the relations
$$
x_i x_a=x_a x_i,\qquad
x_i y_j=y_j x_i,\qquad
y_j y_b=-y_b y_j.
$$
Thus, as an ordinary associative algebra,
$\mathcal O(k^{m||n})=k[x_1,\ldots,x_m]\otimes
\Lambda(y_1,\ldots,y_n)$.

For the second space, let $k^{n\wedge m}=\mathrm{Span}_k\{\xi_1,\cdots,\xi_m,\eta_1,\cdots,\eta_n\}$, with $|\xi_i|=(0,-1)$ and $|\eta_j|=(-1,-1)$.
The commutation relations are
$$
\xi_i\xi_a=-\xi_a\xi_i,\qquad
\xi_i\eta_j=-\eta_j\xi_i,\qquad
\eta_j\eta_b=\eta_b\eta_j.
$$
With these degrees, both function algebras are bigraded commutative and form a Koszul dual pair \cite[Theorem~2.6]{CY}.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $A$ be a bigraded commutative algebra. A bivector field $\pi$ satisfying $[\pi,\pi]=0$ defines a \textit{Poisson algebra} $(A,\pi)$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Let $A$ be a bigraded algebra. A \textit{derivation} of $A$ with values in an $A$-module $M$ is a $k$-linear map $\upsilon: A \to M$ satisfying
$$
\upsilon(ab) = \upsilon(a)b + (-1)^{p(a)p(\upsilon)+w(a)w(\upsilon)}a\upsilon(b) \quad \text{for all homogeneous } a,b \in A,
$$
and $\upsilon(c)=0$ for every $c \in k$. 
We denote the set of such derivations by $\overline{\mathfrak X}(A,M)$, abbreviated to $\overline{\mathfrak X}(A)$ or $\operatorname{Der}(A)$ when $M=A$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Suppose $A$ is a bigraded Poisson algebra. A \textit{Poisson derivation} of $A$ is a derivation $\upsilon \in \mathrm{Der}(A)$ satisfying $$\upsilon (\{f,g\})=\{\upsilon (f),g\}+(-1)^{p(f)p(\upsilon)+w(f)w(\upsilon)}\{f,\upsilon (g)\}$$
    for any homogeneous elements $f,g\in A$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Suppose $A$ is a bigraded commutative algebra.
For $p\ge 0$, the $A$-module of
$p$-th polyvectors, denoted by

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{diagonalizable}
Suppose that $\overline{\upsilon}$ is diagonalizable
on $A$ over $k$.
Then the inclusions of the zero-eigenvalue subcomplexes
are homotopy equivalences. In particular,
$$
\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A;\upsilon)
\cong
\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet}
\bigl(\widetilde{\mathrm{CP}}^0_{\bullet,\bullet}
(A;\upsilon)\bigr),\quad
\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
\cong
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet}
\bigl(\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}_0(A)\bigr).
$$
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
To obtain the eigenspace decompositions, take the eigencomponents of a finite homogeneous generating set of $A$. Their span $W\subset A$ is a finite-dimensional homogeneous $\overline\upsilon$-invariant subspace, because $\overline\upsilon$ is diagonalizable and preserves the bigrading. The subspace $W$ still generates $A$.

Restriction to $W$ gives an injective equivariant map
$$
\operatorname{Der}_k(A)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Hom}_k(W,A),
\qquad
D\longmapsto D|_W,
$$
where the action on the target is
$T\mapsto\overline\upsilon\circ T
-T\circ\overline\upsilon|_W$.
Choose an eigenbasis $e_1,\ldots,e_N$ of $W$, with $\overline\upsilon(e_i)=\lambda_i e_i$. Evaluation on this basis identifies the action on $\operatorname{Hom}_k(W,A)$ with $\bigoplus_{i=1}^N(\overline\upsilon-\lambda_i)$ on $A^{\oplus N}$, which is diagonalizable.

Every invariant subspace of a diagonalizable operator
is a direct sum of eigenspaces: the finitely many
eigencomponents of any vector are recovered by
polynomial spectral projections. The same argument
applies to invariant quotients. Consequently,
$[\overline\upsilon,-]$ is diagonalizable on
$\operatorname{Der}_k(A)$, and the shifted action
$[\upsilon,-]$ is diagonalizable on $\mathfrak X^1(A)$.
Since this action is a derivation of the wedge product,
tensor products and invariant quotients give
diagonalizability on
$\mathfrak X^r(A)=\operatorname{Sym}_A^r\mathfrak X^1(A)$
for every $r\geq1$. Together with the hypothesis on
$\mathfrak X^0(A)=A$, this yields
$$
\mathrm{CP}^{s,t}(A)
=
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in k}\mathrm{CP}^{s,t}_\lambda(A).
$$
Transporting this decomposition along the
eigenvalue-preserving isomorphism $D_\gamma$, we obtain
$$
\widetilde{\mathrm{CP}}_{s,t}(A;\upsilon)
=
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in k}
\widetilde{\mathrm{CP}}^\lambda_{s,t}(A;\upsilon).
$$
Both decompositions are algebraic direct sums.

Let $\iota_0$ and $p_0$ denote the inclusion of the
zero-eigenvalue chain subcomplex and the projection
onto it. By \eqref{homotopy},
\begin{equation}\label{homotopyid}
\left.
(\partial_\upsilon\tilde d+\tilde d\partial_\upsilon)
\right|_{\widetilde{\mathrm{CP}}^\lambda_{\bullet,\bullet}
(A;\upsilon)}
=
-\lambda\,\mathrm{id}.
\end{equation}
Define an operator $h$ of homological degree $(0,1)$ by
$$
h\big|_{\widetilde{\mathrm{CP}}^\lambda_{\bullet,\bullet}
(A;\upsilon)}
=
\begin{cases}
-\lambda^{-1}\tilde d,&\lambda\neq0,\\
0,&\lambda=0.
\end{cases}
$$
This operator is well defined because $\tilde d$
preserves eigenspaces and every element has only finitely many nonzero eigencomponents.
Equation~\eqref{homotopyid} gives
$$
\partial_\upsilon h+h\partial_\upsilon
=
\mathrm{id}-\iota_0p_0,
\qquad
p_0\iota_0=\mathrm{id}.
$$
Thus $\iota_0$ is a homotopy equivalence.

For cochains, let $F$ denote the isomorphism of complexes
constructed in Theorem~\ref{horiso}, and let $i_0$ and $q_0$ denote
the zero-eigenvalue inclusion and projection.
Then $H=F^{-1}hF$ has cohomological degree $(0,-1)$
and satisfies
$$
\delta^\pi H+H\delta^\pi
=
F^{-1}(\mathrm{id}-\iota_0p_0)F
=
\mathrm{id}-i_0q_0,
\qquad
q_0i_0=\mathrm{id}.
$$
Hence $i_0$ is also a homotopy equivalence.
The asserted homology and cohomology isomorphisms follow.

\label{diffcalculus}
Let $\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet}$ and $\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet}$ 
be bigraded vector spaces.
A \textit{differential calculus} is a sextuple
$$
(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},
\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet}, \cup, \iota, [-,-], d)
$$
satisfying the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] $(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\cup,[-,-])$ is a 
bigraded Gerstenhaber
algebra.
\begin{itemize}
\item $(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\cup)$ is a bigraded commutative algebra;
\item $(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},[-,-])$ is a Lie algebra of cohomological degree $(0,-1)$,
corresponding to actual bracket degree $(0,1)$ on polyvectors;
\item the bracket satisfies the bigraded Leibniz rule
$$
[P,Q\cup R]
=[P,Q]\cup R
+(-1)^{|P||Q|+w(Q)}\,Q\cup[P,R],
$$
for homogeneous elements $P, Q, R$;
\end{itemize}


\item[(2)] $\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet}$ is a bigraded (left) 
module over 
$(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet}, \cup)$ via the map
$$
\iota: \mathrm{H}^{i,j}\otimes\mathrm{H}_{s,t}\to 
\mathrm{H}_{s-i,t-j},\;f\otimes p\mapsto \iota_fp,
$$
for any $f\in \mathrm{H}^{i,j}$ and $p \in \mathrm{H}_{s,t}$;
                
\item[(3)] There is a map $d: \mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet}\to \mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet+1}$ 
satisfying $d^2=0$.
With $L_f:=[\iota_f,d]=\iota_fd-(-1)^{w(f)}d\iota_f$, the Cartan identity for homogeneous $f,g\in\mathrm H^{\bullet,\bullet}$ is
\begin{align}\label{calculusformula}
\iota_{[f,g]}=[L_f,\iota_g] :={L}_{f}\iota_g-(-1)^{p(g)p(f)
+w(g)(w(f)+1)}\iota_g {L}_f.
\end{align}       
\end{enumerate}

\label{thm:bv-duality}

$(\mathrm H^{\bullet,\bullet},\mathrm H_{\bullet,\bullet},

be a differential calculus with duality, and write
$\mathrm{PD}(a)=\iota_a\gamma$. Then

$$

=(-1)^{w(a)}
\bigl(\Delta(a\cup b)-\Delta(a)\cup b\bigr)
-a\cup\Delta(b).
$$

=(-1)^{w(a)}

\partial_{\xi_i}\wedge\partial_{\xi_j}
+
\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq n}
b_{st}\eta_s\eta_t\,
\partial_{\eta_s}\wedge\partial_{\eta_t}
-
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{s=1}^{n}
c_{is}\xi_i\eta_s\,
\partial_{\xi_i}\wedge\partial_{\eta_s}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\pi^!$ is diagonal with respect to
$\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m,\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_n$.
Every choice of $a_{ij},b_{st},c_{is}\in k$ gives a Poisson structure. Conversely, every Poisson structure diagonal in the specified dual coordinates is obtained in this way.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{modulardual}
Let $A=\mathcal O(k^{m||n})$ and
$A^!=\mathcal O(k^{n\wedge m})$ be equipped with
Koszul-dual quadratic Poisson structures $\pi$ and $\pi^!$
of degree $(0,-2)$, respectively.
Let $\upsilon$ and $\upsilon^!$ be their modular vector
fields with respect to the mixed volume forms
$\gamma$ and $\gamma^!$ chosen above.
Then the correspondence \eqref{corres} sends
$\upsilon$ to $\upsilon^!$.
Moreover, the derivation of $A$ corresponding to $\upsilon$ is diagonalizable over $k$ if and only if the derivation of $A^!$ corresponding to $\upsilon^!$ is.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $\Psi$ denote the isomorphism of polyvector algebras
given by \eqref{corres}, and let $\Phi$ be the corresponding
isomorphism of mixed differential forms, determined by
$$x_i\longmapsto\partial_{\xi_i},\quad
dx_i\longmapsto\xi_i^*,\quad
\partial_{y_j}\longmapsto\eta_j,\quad
y_j^*\longmapsto d\eta_j.$$
By \cite[Proposition~3.19]{CY}, these maps satisfy
$\Phi\tilde d=\tilde d\Phi$ and $
\Phi\tilde\iota_P=\tilde\iota_{\Psi(P)}\Phi.$
Moreover, the chosen volume forms satisfy
$\Phi(\gamma)=\gamma^!$.
Writing $D_\gamma(P)=\tilde\iota_P\gamma$ and
$D_{\gamma^!}(Q)=\tilde\iota_Q\gamma^!$, we therefore obtain
$\Phi D_\gamma=D_{\gamma^!}\Psi$.
It follows that
$$\Psi\mathrm{Div}_{\gamma}
=\Psi D_\gamma^{-1}\tilde dD_\gamma
=D_{\gamma^!}^{-1}\Phi\tilde dD_\gamma
=D_{\gamma^!}^{-1}\tilde dD_{\gamma^!}\Psi
=\mathrm{Div}_{\gamma^!}\Psi.$$
Since $\Psi(\pi)=\pi^!$, this gives
$\Psi(\upsilon)
=-\Psi\mathrm{Div}_{\gamma}(\pi)
=-\mathrm{Div}_{\gamma^!}(\pi^!)
=\upsilon^!.$


Let $M_x$ and $M_y$ denote the matrices of
$\overline\upsilon$ on
$\operatorname{span}_k\{x_1,\ldots,x_m\}$ and
$\operatorname{span}_k\{y_1,\ldots,y_n\}$, respectively.
On the spaces of linear generators, the modular derivations of $A$ and $A^!$ have matrices
$$
\begin{pmatrix} M_x&0\\0&M_y\end{pmatrix}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\begin{pmatrix} M_x^{\mathsf T}&0\\0&-M_y^{\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix},
$$
respectively. These matrices are diagonalizable over $k$ simultaneously.

For either algebra, diagonalizability on the space of
linear generators is equivalent to diagonalizability
on the whole algebra. Indeed, a homogeneous eigenbasis
of generators yields a monomial eigenbasis by the
Leibniz rule. Conversely, the restriction of a
diagonalizable operator to an invariant subspace is
diagonalizable.
Hence the derivation of $A$ associated with $\upsilon$ is
diagonalizable if and only if the derivation of
$A^!$ associated with $\upsilon^!$ is.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{comdiagram}
    Let $A=(\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n}),\pi)$ be a quadratic Poisson algebra with $|\pi|=(0,-2)$, and let $A^!=(\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m}),\pi^!)$ be its Koszul dual Poisson algebra.
    Their modular vector fields are denoted by $\upsilon$ and $\upsilon^!$, respectively.
    Then the following diagram commutes:
\begin{equation}\label{diagPoisson}
\xymatrixcolsep{4pc}
\xymatrix{
\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
\ar[r]^-{\cong}
\ar[d]_{\cong}
&
\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{-n-\bullet,m-\bullet}
(A;\upsilon)
\ar[d]_{\cong}
\\
\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^!)
\ar[r]^-{\cong}
&
\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{-n-\bullet,m-\bullet}
(A^!;\upsilon^!).
}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Recall the polyvector and mixed form isomorphisms $\Psi$ and $\Phi$ from the proof of Proposition~\ref{modulardual}:
\begin{align}\label{correspondence}
\Psi:&\quad x_i\mapsto\partial_{\xi_i},\quad
 y_j\mapsto\partial_{\eta_j},\quad
 \partial_{x_i}\mapsto\xi_i,\quad\partial_{y_j}\mapsto\eta_j,\\
\Phi:&\quad x_i\mapsto\partial_{\xi_i},\quad
 y_j^*\mapsto d\eta_j,\quad dx_i\mapsto\xi_i^*,\quad
 \partial_{y_j}\mapsto\eta_j.\label{correspondence2}
\end{align}
They preserve degrees. The identities
$\Psi[\pi,P]=[\pi^!,\Psi(P)]$ and
$\Phi\tilde\iota_P=\tilde\iota_{\Psi(P)}\Phi$, together
with $\Phi\tilde d=\tilde d\Phi$ and
$\Psi(\upsilon)=\upsilon^!$, give
$$
\Psi\delta^\pi=\delta^{\pi^!}\Psi,\qquad
\Phi\partial_\upsilon=\partial_{\upsilon^!}\Phi.
$$
Thus $\Psi$ and $\Phi$ induce the left and right vertical
isomorphisms in \eqref{diagPoisson}, respectively.
The horizontal maps are induced by volume contraction,
as in Theorem~\ref{horiso}. Finally, the identity
$$
\Phi D_\gamma=D_{\gamma^!}\Psi,
$$
proved in Proposition~\ref{modulardual}, shows that the square commutes on representatives and hence on (co)homology. This argument applies without a diagonalizability hypothesis.

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{modulardual}, the modular
derivation of $A^!$ is also diagonalizable.

By \cite[Proposition~3.19]{CY}, the maps $\Psi$ and
$\Phi$ satisfy
$\Psi(P\wedge Q)=\Psi(P)\wedge\Psi(Q)$,
$\Psi[P,Q]=[\Psi(P),\Psi(Q)]$,
and
$\Phi\tilde\iota_P=\tilde\iota_{\Psi(P)}\Phi$,
$\Phi\tilde d=\tilde d\Phi$.
Theorem~\ref{comdiagram} shows that these maps

to (co)homology.

Moreover, $\Psi(\upsilon)=\upsilon^!$ gives
$\Phi L_\upsilon=L_{\upsilon^!}\Phi$.
Thus $\Phi$ preserves eigenspaces and intertwines the

Consequently,
$\Phi_*\tilde d=\tilde d\Phi_*$,
where $\tilde d$ on twisted Poisson homology denotes
the transported operator defined after
Theorem~\ref{diagonalizable}.

Finally, $\Phi(\gamma)=\gamma^!$, so $\Phi_*$
preserves the fundamental classes. This proves
compatibility with all the calculus operations
and with the duality.

<!-- example-3: body_md -->
\label{ex:nonunimodular-22}
Consider $A=\mathcal O(k^{2||2})$, with coordinates
$x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2$, and let
$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi=
x_1x_2\partial_{x_1}\wedge\partial_{x_2}
-y_1y_2\partial_{y_1}\wedge\partial_{y_2}
+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}
x_i y_j\partial_{x_i}\wedge\partial_{y_j}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The bivector $\pi$ is diagonal and quadratic of degree $(0,-2)$. It is a linear combination of polyvector products of coordinate Euler derivations, which commute pairwise. Hence $[\pi,\pi]=0$.

For the mixed volume form
$\gamma=dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge y_1^*\wedge y_2^*$,
direct mixed contraction gives
$$
\upsilon=-\mathrm{Div}_{\gamma}(\pi)
=-x_1\partial_{x_1}
-3x_2\partial_{x_2}
-3y_1\partial_{y_1}
-y_2\partial_{y_2}.
$$
In particular,
$\upsilon
(x_1^a x_2^b y_1^{\varepsilon_1}y_2^{\varepsilon_2})=
-(a+3b+3\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2)
x_1^a x_2^b y_1^{\varepsilon_1}y_2^{\varepsilon_2}$,
where $\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2\in\{0,1\}$.
Thus the modular derivation is nonzero and diagonalizable. The structure $\pi$ is therefore non-unimodular, and Theorem~\ref{mainthm1.1} gives a BV structure on $\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)$.

The coordinate Euler fields commute with $\pi$ and $\upsilon$.
Since
$\mathrm{Div}_\gamma(x_i\partial_{x_i})=1$ and
$\mathrm{Div}_\gamma(y_j\partial_{y_j})=-1$,
$\Delta[x_i\partial_{x_i}]=-[1]$,
$\Delta[y_j\partial_{y_j}]=[1]$.
Since the Euler fields commute, \eqref{eq:second-order} shows that $\Delta$ restricts to a graded derivation on the subalgebra generated by their classes.
In particular,
\begin{align*}
\Delta[x_1x_2\partial_{x_1}\wedge\partial_{x_2}]
&=[x_1\partial_{x_1}]-[x_2\partial_{x_2}],\\
\Delta[y_1y_2\partial_{y_1}\wedge\partial_{y_2}]
&=[y_1\partial_{y_1}]-[y_2\partial_{y_2}],\\
\Delta[x_i y_j\partial_{x_i}\wedge\partial_{y_j}]
&=-[x_i\partial_{x_i}]-[y_j\partial_{y_j}].
\end{align*}
To see that $[\upsilon]\ne0$, assign polynomial degree one
to each coordinate. Since $\pi$ has quadratic coefficients,
the coefficients of $[f,\pi]$ have no terms of degree less
than two for any $f\in A$. 
Thus $\upsilon$, a nonzero vector field with linear
coefficients, is not of the form $[f,\pi]$ for any $f\in A$.
Since the Poisson differential increases polyvector length
by one, this excludes all possible coboundaries for $\upsilon$.
By Remark~\ref{rem:modular-class-bv}, we therefore obtain
$\Delta[\pi]=[\upsilon]\ne0$.

On $A^!=\mathcal O(k^{2\wedge2})$, the dual Poisson bivector  is
$$
\pi^!=\xi_1\xi_2\partial_{\xi_1}\wedge\partial_{\xi_2}
-\eta_1\eta_2\partial_{\eta_1}\wedge\partial_{\eta_2}
-\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\xi_i\eta_j
\partial_{\xi_i}\wedge\partial_{\eta_j}.
$$
For $\gamma^!=\xi_1^*\wedge\xi_2^*\wedge d\eta_1\wedge d\eta_2$,
$\upsilon^!=-\xi_1\partial_{\xi_1}-3\xi_2\partial_{\xi_2}
+3\eta_1\partial_{\eta_1}+\eta_2\partial_{\eta_2}$.
The dual modular derivation is again nonzero and diagonalizable.
Under the correspondence
$x_i\partial_{x_i}\mapsto\xi_i\partial_{\xi_i}$,
$y_j\partial_{y_j}\mapsto-\eta_j\partial_{\eta_j}$,
Theorem~\ref{mainthm} identifies the BV operators.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:homogeneous-scope}
On $\mathcal O(k^{m||n})$, suppose that the coefficients
of $\pi$ are homogeneous of polynomial degree $d$.
Then its modular derivation shifts polynomial degree by
$d-2$. If $d>2$, the lowest nonzero homogeneous term of
an eigenvector rules out a nonzero eigenvalue; if $d<2$,
the highest nonzero term does so. Thus, when $d\ne2$,
all eigenvalues are zero, and diagonalizability forces
$\upsilon=0$. 
Among Poisson structures homogeneous in polynomial degree, the non-unimodular case of Theorem~\ref{mainthm1.1} is therefore quadratic. The theorem also applies to inhomogeneous Poisson structures of degree $(0,-2)$.
