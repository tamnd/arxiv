<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{le:wxBODY}
 Consider problem~\eqref{bilevelProblem}. Then for any $\Delta \hat y \in \mathbb{Z}^{n_2}$, the set
\[
T(\Delta \hat y) = \left\{ (x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^n \colon Ax  + B(y  + \Delta \hat y) \ge f,~   \ell \le  y  +   \Delta \hat y  \le u,~ q(y+\Delta \hat{y})< q(y)
\right\}
\]
 does not contain any bilevel-feasible solution.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Due to $q(y+\Delta \hat{y})< q(y)$ we have that by moving from $y$ to $y + \Delta\hat{y}$ the follower objective function value is improved. Moreover, due to $Ax  + B(y  + \Delta \hat y) \ge f$ and $\ell \le  y  +   \Delta \hat y  \le u$ we have that the point $(x,y+\Delta \hat{y})$ is feasible for the follower problem. Hence, any $(x,y)$ in $T(\Delta \hat y)$ cannot be bilevel-feasible, as $(x,y+\Delta \hat{y})$ is a better solution for the follower problem compared to $(x,y)$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:Xdelta}
 Consider problem~\eqref{bilevelProblem}. Then for any $\Delta \hat y \in \mathbb{Z}^{n_2}$ with $R\Delta   \hat y \neq \mathbf{0}$ or $g^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 \Delta\hat{y} < 0$, the extended set $T^+(\Delta \hat y)$ defined as 
\begin{align*}
T^+(\Delta \hat y) = \left\{ (x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^n\colon Ax  + B (y  + \Delta \hat y) \ge f-\mathbf{1},~   \ell-\mathbf{1} \le  y  +   \Delta \hat y  \le u+ \mathbf{1},~ q(y+\Delta \hat{y})\leq q(y) \right\}
\end{align*}
is a polyhedron, even for a non-convex quadratic function $q$, and does not contain any bilevel-feasible solution in its interior $\operatorname{int}(T^+(\Delta \hat y))$, for which $\operatorname{int}(T^+(\Delta \hat y))\cap \mathbb{Z}^{n}=T(\Delta \hat y)\cap \mathbb{Z}^{n}$ holds.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We first observe that the constraint $q(y+\Delta \hat{y}) \leq q(y)$ can be reformulated as
 \begin{align}\label{eq:reformulation}
 q(y+\Delta \hat{y}) - q(y) = 
 & (y +\Delta \hat y)^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 R(y +\Delta \hat y) + g^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 (y +\Delta \hat y) - y^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 Ry - g^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 y = \\
 & \quad 2{\Delta   \hat y}^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 R y + \Delta   \hat y^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 R\Delta   \hat y + g^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 \Delta \hat    y \le 0, \nonumber
 \end{align}  
 which is linear in $y$, thus, the set $T^+(\Delta \hat y)$ is indeed a polyhedron. Furthermore, if $ R\Delta  \hat y \neq \mathbf{0}$ and thus also ${\Delta   \hat y}^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 R \neq 0$ holds, it follows that this constraint does not vanish in the definition of $T^+(\Delta\hat{y})$.
 On the other hand, if $R\Delta   \hat y = \mathbf{0}$ and $g^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 \Delta\hat{y} < 0$ holds, then this constraint is trivially strictly satisfied.

 Thus, to be in the interior of $T^+(\Delta\hat{y})$, for any integer solution $(x,y)$  is equivalent to satisfying
  \begin{equation*}
    Ax + B (y + \Delta\hat{y}) > f - \mathbf{1}, 
    \qquad 
    \ell - \mathbf{1} < y+\Delta\hat{y} < u + \mathbf{1}, 
    ~\text{and}\qquad 
    q(y+\Delta \hat{y})< q(y).
  \end{equation*}
 Due to $A \in \mathbb{Z}^{m_2 \times n_1}$, $B \in \mathbb{Z}^{m_2 \times n_2}$,  $f \in \mathbb{Z}^{m_2 }$, $\ell,u\in\mathbb{Z}^{n_2}$ and $x\in \mathbb Z^{n_1}$,$y\in \mathbb Z^{n_2}$ the
  first two conditions are equivalent to $Ax +B (y + \Delta\hat{y}) \ge f$
  and $\ell \le y+\Delta\hat{y} \le u$.
  As a consequence, indeed $\operatorname{int}(T^+(\Delta \hat y))\cap \mathbb{Z}^{n} = T(\Delta \hat y)\cap \mathbb{Z}^{n}$ holds.
  Thus, the claim follows from Theorem~\ref{le:wxBODY}, as any bilevel-feasible solution must be integer.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:existence}
 Consider problem~\eqref{bilevelProblem} and a bilevel-infeasible solution $(x^*,y^*)$ of it. Then, there exists a $\Delta\hat{y}  \in \mathbb{Z}^{n_2}$ with $ R\Delta   \hat y \neq \mathbf{0}$ or $g^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 \Delta\hat{y} < 0$  such that $(x^*,y^*) \in \operatorname{int}(T^+(\Delta \hat y))$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\hat y \in \arg \min \left\{
q(y) :    A x^*+ B y \geq f,~ \ell \le y \le u,~ y \in \mathbb{Z}^{n_2} 
                                    
                        
                                         
\right\}$ be an optimal solution to the follower problem for $x^*$.
Define $\Delta \hat{y}=\hat y - y^*$.
Since $(x^*,y^*)$ is bilevel-infeasible, we have that $q(y^*+\Delta \hat{y})< q(y^*)$, which implies that $R\Delta   \hat y \neq \mathbf{0}$ or $g^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 \Delta\hat{y} < 0$ with the computations done in~\eqref{eq:reformulation}. Moreover, we have that $Ax^*  + B(y^*  + \Delta \hat y) \ge f$ and $\ell \le  y^*  +   \Delta \hat y  \le u$ by definition of $\hat y$ and $\Delta \hat y$. Thus, $(x^*,y^*) \in T(\Delta \hat y)$ and consequently $(x^*,y^*) \in \operatorname{int} (T^+(\Delta \hat y))$ by  Corollary~\ref{cor:Xdelta}, as any bilevel-infeasible solution is integer.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:separation}
Let $(x^*,y^*)$ be a bilevel-infeasible solution that is an extreme point of $\mathcal P$. Let $\Delta \hat y \in \mathbb{Z}^{n_2}$ with $R\Delta   \hat y \neq \mathbf{0}$ or $g^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 \Delta\hat{y} < 0$ be such that $(x^*,y^*) \in \operatorname{int} (T^+(\Delta \hat y))$. Then, there is a valid inequality for $\mathrm{conv}\big(\bigcup_{k \in \mathcal K} (\mathcal P \cap \mathcal D_k(\Delta \hat y))\big)$ that is violated by $(x^*,y^*)$, and it can be obtained by solving \eqref{eq:CGLP}.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
First, note that a $\Delta \hat y$ with  $(x^*,y^*) \in \operatorname{int} (T^+(\Delta \hat y))$ as claimed exists by Theorem~\ref{thm:existence}. Then,
since $(x^*,y^*) \in \operatorname{int} (T^+(\Delta \hat y))$, the solution $(x^*,y^*)$ strictly satisfies each of the defining inequalities of $T^+(\Delta \hat y)$.  Equivalently, $(x^*,y^*)$ does not satisfy any inequality that defines a disjunct $\mathcal D_k(\Delta \hat y)$.

Assume, for contradiction, that no inequality separating $(x^*,y^*)$ from $\mathrm{conv}\big(\bigcup_{k \in \mathcal K} (\mathcal P \cap \mathcal D_k(\Delta \hat y))\big)$ exists. Then $(x^*,y^*)$ lies in this convex hull, i.e.,
\[
(x^*,y^*) = \sum_{k \in \mathcal K} \lambda_k (x^k,y^k),
\]
for some $\lambda_k \ge 0$ with $\sum_{k \in \mathcal K} \lambda_k = 1$ and $(x^k,y^k) \in \mathcal P \cap \mathcal D_k(\Delta \hat y)$ whenever $\lambda_k > 0$. Since $(x^*,y^*)$ satisfies no single disjunct, we have $(x^k,y^k) \neq (x^*,y^*)$ for every $k$ with $\lambda_k>0$, and thus at least two of the $\lambda_k$ must be strictly positive (otherwise $(x^*,y^*) = (x^k,y^k)$ for the unique $k$ with $\lambda_k=1$, a contradiction). Hence $(x^*,y^*)$ is a proper convex combination of two or more points of $\mathcal P$, contradicting that $(x^*,y^*)$ is an extreme point of $\mathcal P$.

Thus, a separating inequality exists and the LP~\eqref{primalcglp} is infeasible for the given $(x^*,y^*)$. By LP duality, its dual \eqref{eq:CGLP} is then unbounded once the normalization constraint is dropped, and with the normalization constraint added it attains a finite optimal solution with a strictly positive objective function value, which yields the coefficients of the desired DC\xspace.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:convergence}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:bounded}, the B\&C\xspace described above solves problem~\eqref{bilevelProblem} to optimality in a finite number of iterations.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Suppose the B\&C\xspace terminates but returns a solution $(x^*,y^*)$ that is bilevel-infeasible. This is not possible, because by Theorem~\ref{thm:existence}, an improving direction $\Delta \hat y$ with $(x^*,y^*) \in \operatorname{int} (T^+(\Delta \hat y))$ exists, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:separation} \eqref{eq:CGLP} then produces a DC\xspace violated by $(x^*,y^*)$, so $(x^*,y^*)$ would have been cut off rather than returned as the final optimal solution.

Suppose the B\&C\xspace terminates with a bilevel-feasible solution that is not optimal. This is not possible either, as by Corollary~\ref{cor:Xdelta} and the definition of our disjunctions, every DC\xspace added throughout the B\&C\xspace preserves all bilevel-feasible solutions because of~\eqref{eq:fsubsetD}.
Hence no DC\xspace ever removes a bilevel-feasible solution from the relaxation of any node, and in particular an optimal solution of problem~\eqref{bilevelProblem} remains feasible for the relaxation of some active (i.e., non-fathomed) node until it is found or an equally good incumbent has already been recorded.

Finally, suppose the B\&C\xspace does not terminate.  By Assumption~\ref{as:bounded}, $\overline{\mbox{SLR\xspace}}$\xspace is a bounded polyhedron and thus the SLR\xspace has a bounded feasible region, so branching on bounded integer variables produces a tree of finite depth. Whenever an integer solution which is not bilevel-feasible is encountered as solution at a  
node, Theorem~\ref{thm:separation} guarantees that the added DC\xspace is violated by the integer solution that generated it, thereby removing that solution from this node relaxation. Since the bounded node relaxation contains only finitely many integer solutions, the processing of each node terminates after finitely many separation rounds. Finite depth of the tree together with finitely many DC separation rounds per node in the tree contradicts non-termination.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemma:generalRedundancy}
For any $\Delta \hat y \in \mathbb{Z}^{n_2}$ and for each $k \in \mathcal{K}$, the disjunct $\mathcal{D}_k(\Delta \hat y)$ is redundant
\begin{itemize}
    \item if $\mathcal{D}_k(\Delta \hat y) \cap \mathcal{P} = \emptyset$ (LP-feasibility), or    
    \item if $\mathcal{D}_k(\Delta \hat y) \cap \mathcal{P} \cap \mathbb{Z}^{n} = \emptyset$ (IP-feasibility), or
    \item if $\mathcal{D}_k(\Delta \hat y) \cap \mathcal{P} \cap \mathcal{F} = \emptyset$ 
    (bilevel-feasibility).
\end{itemize}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma:unifiedRedundancy}
Let $x^-,x^+ \in \mathbb{Z}^{n_1}$ and $y^-,y^+ \in \mathbb{Z}^{n_2}$ be the current lower and upper bounds on the leader and follower variables within one node of the B\&C\xspace tree, respectively. 
Let $\Delta \hat y \in \mathbb{Z}^{n_2}$.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \label{lemma:unifiedRedundancy:gen}
Let $k \in \mathcal{K}$ and recall from Section~\ref{sec:CGLP} that the disjunct $\mathcal D_k(\Delta \hat y)$ can be written as $G_k x + H_k y \ge s_k$ for the corresponding $G_k$, $H_k$ and $s_k$ given in Table~\ref{tab:disjunction_mapping}. 
 If
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{n_1} \max\{ G_{kj} x^-_j, G_{kj} x^+_j\} + \sum_{j=1}^{n_2} \max\{ H_{kj} y^-_j, H_{kj} y^+_j\} \le s_k -1
\]
holds, then the disjunct $\mathcal D_k(\Delta \hat y)$ is redundant.

\item \label{lemma:unifiedRedundancy:bounds}
For any $j\in \{1,\dots,n_2\} $, if $\Delta \hat y_j > \ell_j - y_j^- -1$ holds, then the lower-bound disjunct $\mathcal{D}^\ell_j(\Delta \hat y)$ is redundant and if $\Delta \hat y_j < u_j - y_j^+ +1$ holds, then the upper-bound disjunct $\mathcal{D}^u_j(\Delta \hat y)$ is redundant.

\item \label{lemma:unifiedRedundancy:linking}
For any $i \in \{1, \dots, m_2\}$, if
\[ \sum_{j=1}^{n_1} \min\{ A_{ij} x^-_{j}, A_{ij} x^+_{j}\}  + \sum_{j=1}^{n_2} \min\{ B_{ij} y^-_j, B_{ij} y^+_j\} \ge f_i - B_i \Delta \hat y \]
holds, then the lower-level disjunct $\mathcal{D}_i(\Delta \hat y)$ is redundant.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The first part follows from the results of \cite{Gaar-et-al:2024}. 
    
    The second part on the lower and upper-bound disjuncts for $j\in \{1,\dots,n_2\}$ is a direct consequence of the first part with $k = j$ with using the fact that $(G_k,H_k,s_k) = (\mathbf{0}, -e_j^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
, -\ell_j + \Delta \hat y_j + 1)$ for $\mathcal D_j^\ell(\Delta \hat y)$ and $(\mathbf{0}, e_j^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
,  u_j - \Delta \hat y_j + 1)$ for $\mathcal D_j^u(\Delta \hat y)$. 

    Finally, the third part on the lower-level disjuncts for $i \in \{1, \dots, m_2\}$ follows from the first part with $k=i$ with $(G_k,H_k,s_k) = (-A_i, -B_i, -f_i + B_i \Delta \hat y + 1)$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lemma:RO}
Let $\mathrm{UB}$ be the objective-function value of the best-known feasible solution for problem~\eqref{bilevelProblem}. 
Let $\Delta \hat y \in \mathbb{Z}^{n_2}$ and let $k \in \mathcal{K}$ such that $\mathcal P \cap \mathcal D_{k}(\Delta \hat y) \cap \mathbb{Z}^n \neq \emptyset$.
Let $\bar U_{k}$ be the optimal objective-function value of
\[
\min\{c^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 x + d^ 
{\mathpalette\@transpose{}} 
 y : (x,y) \in \mathcal P \cap \mathcal D_{k}(\Delta \hat y) \cap \mathbb{Z}^n\}.
\]
If $\bar U_{k} \ge \mathrm{UB}$, then the disjunct $\mathcal D_{k}(\Delta \hat y)$ is redundant.
