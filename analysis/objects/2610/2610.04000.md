<!-- theorema-1: body_md -->
Let $d\geq3$ and let $\mathcal F$ be a fixed scale-invariant family of
$k$-point configurations in $\mathbb{R}^d$ with a rank-regular fixed-rank scale
atlas.  Almost surely the trace of Brownian motion contains a member of
$\mathcal F$ if
\[
 r_I>(d-2)\,\# I
 \qquad\text{for every nonempty }I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}.
\]
If this inequality fails for some nonempty $I$, including at equality, then
almost surely the trace contains no member of $\mathcal F$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{Lemma:dimBrownie}
For every deterministic Borel set $E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$,
\[
\dim_H(E\cap B)=\dim_H E
\qquad\text{almost surely}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We may assume $E\ne\varnothing$.  Put
$T_E:=W^{-1}(E)=\{t\geq0:W(t)\in E\}$.  Hawkes' preimage theorem
\cite{Hawkes}, in the form \cite[Eq.~(1.9)]{KX}, applied to planar
Brownian motion, the isotropic $2$-stable process in $\mathbb{R}^2$, gives
\[
 \dim_HT_E=\tfrac12\dim_HE
 \qquad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Kaufman's uniform
dimension-doubling theorem \cite{Kaufman}, \cite[Thm.~9.28]{PM} gives an
event $\Omega_K$ of probability one on which $\dim_HW(A)=2\dim_HA$ for every
$A\subseteq[0,1]$ simultaneously.  Applying it to the shifted path on each
interval $[m,m+1]$, intersecting the countably many events and using
\eqref{eq:countablestability}, we obtain $\dim_HW(A)=2\dim_HA$ for every
$A\subseteq[0,\infty)$ on one probability-one event.  Since
$W(T_E)=E\cap B$, taking $A=T_E$ proves the formula.  The simultaneous
quantifier is essential, because $T_E$ depends on the sampled path.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:intersection}
Let $\tau$ be an almost surely finite stopping time and let $K$ be an
$\mathcal F_\tau$-measurable random compact subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$.  If
$q\geq0$ and $\dim_H K>q$ almost surely, then there is a stopping time
$\sigma$ with $\mathbb P(\tau<\sigma<\infty)=1$ such that
\[
K':=K\cap W([\tau,\sigma])
\]
is an $\mathcal F_\sigma$-measurable random compact set with
$\dim_H K'>q$ almost surely.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since the filtration is complete, we may modify $\tau$ and $K$ on the null
event $\{\tau=\infty\}$ and assume $\tau<\infty$ everywhere.  Put
$\widetilde W(t)=W(\tau+t)-W(\tau)$, a planar Brownian motion independent
of $\mathcal F_\tau$ by the strong Markov property.

\emph{Conditioning.}  For a compact set $L$ and a continuous path $\gamma$
put
\[
 D(L,\gamma):=\sup_{m\ge1}\dim_H\bigl(L\cap\gamma([0,m])\bigr)
 =\dim_H\bigl(L\cap\gamma([0,\infty))\bigr),
\]
the equality by \eqref{eq:countablestability}.  This is Borel in
$(L,\gamma)$: finite path ranges are compact, intersection is a Borel
operation on the hyperspace, and Hausdorff dimension is a Borel function
there \cite[Thm.~2.1(b)]{MattilaMauldin}.  Hence
$h(L):=\mathbb P\{D(L,\widetilde W)\ne\dim_HL\}$ is a Borel function of
$L$, and Lemma~\ref{Lemma:dimBrownie} says that $h(L)=0$ for every compact
$L$.  Since $K-W(\tau)$ is $\mathcal F_\tau$-measurable and
$\widetilde W$ is independent of $\mathcal F_\tau$,
\[
 \mathbb P\bigl(
  D(K-W(\tau),\widetilde W)\ne\dim_H(K-W(\tau))
 \bigm|\mathcal F_\tau\bigr)
 =h\bigl(K-W(\tau)\bigr)=0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
As $(K\cap W([\tau,\infty)))-W(\tau)=(K-W(\tau))\cap\widetilde W([0,\infty))$
and Hausdorff dimension is translation invariant, this says that
$\dim_H(K\cap W([\tau,\infty)))=\dim_HK>q$ almost surely.

\emph{Stopping.}  For $m\ge1$ put $R_m:=K\cap W([\tau,\tau+m])$.  These
are increasing $\mathcal F_{\tau+m}$-measurable random compact sets (the
range over $[\tau,\tau+m]$ is determined by $\tau$ and the path stopped at
$\tau+m$) with union $K\cap W([\tau,\infty))$, so by
\eqref{eq:countablestability},
$\sup_m\dim_HR_m=\dim_H(K\cap W([\tau,\infty)))>q$ almost surely, and
hence $\dim_HR_m>q$ for some finite $m$; this is where the strict
inequality $\dim_HK>q$ is used.  Let
$N_0:=\min\{m\ge1:\dim_HR_m>q\}$, with $\min\varnothing=\infty$.  The
event $A:=\{N_0=\infty\}$ is null, hence lies in $\mathcal F_0$ by
completeness.  Put $N:=N_0$ on $A^c$, $N:=1$ on $A$, $\sigma:=\tau+N$ and
$K':=R_N$.  Then $\sigma<\infty$ everywhere and $\dim_HK'>q$ outside $A$.
For $m\ge1$, $\{N_0\le m\}=\{\dim_HR_m>q\}\in\mathcal F_{\tau+m}$ and
$A\in\mathcal F_0\subseteq\mathcal F_{\tau+m}$, so
$\{N\le m\}=(\{N_0\le m\}\cap A^c)\cup A\in\mathcal F_{\tau+m}$ and
$\{N=m\}=\{N\le m\}\setminus\{N\le m-1\}\in\mathcal F_{\tau+m}$.  Hence
\[
 \{\sigma\le t\}
 =\bigcup_{m\ge1}\bigl(\{N=m\}\cap\{\tau+m\le t\}\bigr)
 \in\mathcal F_t ,
\]
so $\sigma$ is a stopping time, and for every open $U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$,
\[
 \{K'\cap U\ne\varnothing,\ \sigma\le t\}
 =\bigcup_{m\ge1}
 \{N=m,\ \tau+m\le t,\ R_m\cap U\ne\varnothing\}
 \in\mathcal F_t ,
\]
so $K'$ is $\mathcal F_\sigma$-measurable.  Finally $\sigma>\tau$ because
$N\ge1$.

\label{thm:radial}
Let $C$ be a deterministic Borel radial set, let
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k>0$, and put $\lambda_0=1$.  Then almost surely
\[
X(C;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)
:=\bigl\{x\in C:\lambda_jx\in B\text{ for }0\leq j\leq k\bigr\}
\]
has Hausdorff dimension $\dim_H C$.  If moreover the radial projection
$\pi|_C:C\to S^1$, $\pi(x)=x/|x|$, is bilipschitz, then the set of
directions $\pi(X(C;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k))$ also has Hausdorff
dimension $\dim_H C$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
If $\dim_HC=0$ the claim follows from
$X(C;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)\subseteq C$.  Let $\dim_HC>0$, fix
$0\le q<\dim_HC$ and choose $q<s<\dim_HC$.  Frostman's lemma
\cite{Falconer} gives a nonzero finite Borel measure $\nu$
carried by $C$ with $\nu(B(x,r))\le Cr^s$, and by inner regularity some
compact $K_0\subseteq C$ has $\nu(K_0)>0$; the ball estimate for
$\nu|_{K_0}$ and the mass distribution principle \cite[Chapter~4]{Falconer}
give $\dim_HK_0\ge s>q$.  Put
$\tau_0=0$, $\mu_1=1$ and $\mu_{j+1}=\lambda_j$ for $1\le j\le k$.  Given
a compact $\mathcal F_{\tau_{j-1}}$-measurable $K_{j-1}\subseteq C$ of
dimension greater than $q$, where $1\le j\le k+1$, apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:intersection} after time $\tau_{j-1}$ to $\mu_jK_{j-1}$,
which is an $\mathcal F_{\tau_{j-1}}$-measurable random compact set of the
same dimension because multiplication by $\mu_j$ is bilipschitz.  This
gives a finite stopping time $\tau_j>\tau_{j-1}$ for which
\[

   \bigl(\mu_jK_{j-1}\cap W([\tau_{j-1},\tau_j])\bigr)
\]
is a compact subset of $K_{j-1}$ of dimension greater than $q$.  If
$x\in K_{k+1}$ then $\mu_jx\in B$ for $1\le j\le k+1$, that is,
$\lambda_jx\in B$ for $0\le j\le k$.  Hence
$K_{k+1}\subseteq X(C;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)$ and

$q<\dim_HC$ and using $X(C;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)\subseteq C$ gives
equality.  The last assertion follows from bilipschitz invariance of
Hausdorff dimension, since $\pi|_X$ is a restriction of $\pi|_C$.

\label{theo:bilip}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:fibre}
Let $s,t\geq0$, let $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^m\times\mathbb{R}^n$ be Borel, and for
$y\in\mathbb{R}^n$ write $A_y:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^m:(x,y)\in A\}$.  Suppose there is a Borel
set $Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ with $\mathcal H^s(Y)>0$ such that $A_y\ne\varnothing$
and $\dim_HA_y\geq t$ for every $y\in Y$.  Then $\dim_HA\geq s+t$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
If $s=0$, pick $y\in Y$: $A$ contains the isometric copy
$A_y\times\{y\}$, so $\dim_HA\geq t$.  If $t=0$, the Lipschitz projection
to the second coordinate maps $A$ onto a set containing $Y$, so
$\dim_HA\geq s$.  Let $s,t>0$ and fix $0<s'<s$, $0<t'<t$.  Frostman's
lemma \cite{Falconer} gives a nonzero finite Borel measure $\nu$
carried by $Y$ with $\nu(B(y,r))\le Cr^{s'}$ for all $y$ and $r$.  Suppose
$\mathcal H^{s'+t'}(A)=0$.  Given $\delta,\eta>0$, cover $A$ by balls
$B_i\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+n}$ of radii $r_i<\delta$ with
$\sum_ir_i^{s'+t'}<\eta$.  For each $y$ the nonempty sections $(B_i)_y$
cover $A_y$ and have diameter at most $2r_i$, so
\[
 \int\mathcal H^{t'}_{2\delta}(A_y)\,d\nu(y)
 \le2^{t'}\sum_ir_i^{t'}\nu(\pi_2(B_i))
 \le C'\sum_ir_i^{s'+t'}<C'\eta ,
\]
where the left-hand side is an upper integral; the pointwise Borel majorant
$2^{t'}\sum_ir_i^{t'}\mathbf 1_{\pi_2(B_i)}(y)$ is all that is needed.
Letting $\eta\downarrow0$ and then $\delta\downarrow0$ gives
$\mathcal H^{t'}(A_y)=0$ for $\nu$-almost every $y$, contradicting
$\dim_HA_y\ge t>t'$ on $Y$.  Hence $\dim_HA\geq s'+t'$; let
$s'\uparrow s$ and $t'\uparrow t$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:planarAdim}
Almost surely, simultaneously for every $n\geq2$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal A_n$ is locally of full dimension:
$\dim_H(\mathcal A_n\cap O)=4$ for every nonempty open set
$O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^4$; in particular, $\mathcal A_n$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}^4$;
\item for Lebesgue-almost every $\vec d\in\mathbb{R}^2$, the fibre
$(\mathcal A_n)_{\vec d}$ is locally of full dimension:
$\dim_H\bigl((\mathcal A_n)_{\vec d}\cap U\bigr)=2$ for every nonempty
open set $U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal U$ and $\mathcal V$ be the countable bases of open disks with
rational centres and positive rational radii in translation and gap space.
Fix $n\geq2$ and $U\in\mathcal U$.  For each fixed $\vec d\ne0$,
Corollary~\ref{cor:planartranslates} applied to the deterministic set
$\{0,\vec d,\ldots,(n-1)\vec d\}$ gives
$\mathbb P\bigl(\dim_H((\mathcal A_n)_{\vec d}\cap U)=2\bigr)=1$.  By the
measurability discussion above, Tonelli's theorem gives, for every $R>0$,
\[
 \mathbb E\Bigl[
 \operatorname{Leb}\bigl\{\vec d\in B(0,R):
 \dim_H((\mathcal A_n)_{\vec d}\cap U)<2\bigr\}
 \Bigr]
 =\int_{B(0,R)}
 \mathbb P\bigl(
 \dim_H((\mathcal A_n)_{\vec d}\cap U)<2
 \bigr)\,d\vec d=0 ,
\]
the point $\vec d=0$ being Lebesgue null.  The nonnegative random variable
inside the expectation is therefore zero almost surely.  Intersecting over
$R\in\mathbb{N}$, $n\geq2$ and $U\in\mathcal U$ gives one probability-one event on
which $\dim_H((\mathcal A_n)_{\vec d}\cap U)=2$ for Lebesgue-almost every
$\vec d$, simultaneously for all $n$ and $U$; since every nonempty open set
contains some $U\in\mathcal U$, this proves (2).

For (1), fix $U\in\mathcal U$ and $V\in\mathcal V$.  Almost every
$\vec d\in V$ has a fibre of dimension two inside $U$, so by inner
regularity of Lebesgue measure there is a compact $Y\subset V$ of positive
Lebesgue measure consisting of such vectors.  Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre} with
$s=t=2$, applied to $\mathcal A_n\cap(U\times V)$, gives
$\dim_H(\mathcal A_n\cap(U\times V))\geq4$, and the reverse inequality is
trivial.  Every nonempty open subset of $\mathbb{R}^4$ contains such a product
$U\times V$.

\label{prop:countable}
For every fixed countable set $F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$, almost surely the
translation set
\[
T_F:=\{a\in\mathbb{R}^2:a+F\subseteq B\}
\]
is dense in $\mathbb{R}^2$.  In particular, the trace contains a translate of $F$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
If $F=\varnothing$ then $T_F=\mathbb{R}^2$.  Otherwise enumerate
$F=\{x_1,x_2,\ldots\}$, with repetitions if $F$ is finite.  Fix a
nonempty open set $V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$, a closed disk $K_0\subset V$ of positive
radius, and put $\tau_0=0$.  Given an $\mathcal F_{\tau_{n-1}}$-measurable
compact $K_{n-1}$ with $\dim_HK_{n-1}>1$, apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:intersection} with $q=1$ after $\tau_{n-1}$ to
$x_n+K_{n-1}$: it gives a finite stopping time $\tau_n>\tau_{n-1}$ such
that
\[
K_n:=\bigl((x_n+K_{n-1})
       \cap W([\tau_{n-1},\tau_n])\bigr)-x_n
\]
is an $\mathcal F_{\tau_n}$-measurable compact subset of $K_{n-1}$ with
$\dim_HK_n>1$.  All countably many stages succeed on one probability-one
event, on which $K_0\supseteq K_1\supseteq\cdots$ are nonempty compact
sets, so $\bigcap_nK_n\ne\varnothing$.  Any $a$ in this intersection
satisfies $a+x_n\in W([\tau_{n-1},\tau_n])\subseteq B$ for every $n$, so
$a\in T_F\cap V$.  Intersecting over a countable base of open sets $V$
proves density.  The intersection of the nested compact sets $K_n$ is
nonempty, but its Hausdorff dimension may be zero; this is why the argument
gives density rather than a dimension bound.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The finite time intervals are what make the compactness argument work: the
nested sets $T_{F_N}=\{a:a+\{x_1,\ldots,x_N\}\subseteq B\}$ need not be
compact, because $B$ is not closed.  Indeed, $B$ is dense by neighbourhood
recurrence \cite[Thm.~3.20]{PM}, while every fixed point is polar at positive
times \cite[Cor.~2.26]{PM}, so Tonelli's theorem gives
$\mathbb E\bigl[\operatorname{Leb}(B\cap[-R,R]^2)\bigr]
=\int_{[-R,R]^2}\mathbb P(x\in B)\,dx=0$ for every $R$; hence
$\operatorname{Leb}(B)=0$ almost surely, and a dense closed subset of
$\mathbb{R}^2$ would be all of $\mathbb{R}^2$.  Each $K_n$ above, in contrast, is defined
through the range over a finite time interval and is compact.

\label{lem:single}

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:master}
Fix $k$ and $a>0$, and let $V_1,\dots,V_k$ be fixed bounded sets such that
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(V_i,0)\ge a,
 \qquad \operatorname{dist}(V_i,V_l)\ge a\quad(i\ne l).
\]
For $x_i\in V_i$, let $H_x$ be the event that all balls $B(x_i,\varepsilon)$ are
hit.  Then, uniformly for $0<\varepsilon<a/10$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onecopyhit}
 \mathbb P(H_x)\asymp\varepsilon^{qk},
\end{equation}
and for any second choice $y_i\in V_i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairedhit}
 \mathbb P(H_x\cap H_y)
 \lesssim
 \varepsilon^{qk}\prod_{i=1}^k g_\varepsilon(x_i,y_i).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
All constants depend only on $d,k,a$ and the sets $V_1,\ldots,V_k$.  If
$u\in V_i$, $v\in V_l$ with $i\ne l$, and $z\in\overline{B(u,\varepsilon)}$, then
$|z-v|\ge a-\varepsilon$, so \eqref{eq:ballformula} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:differentlabeltransition}
 \mathbb P_z\bigl(B\cap\overline{B(v,\varepsilon)}\ne\varnothing\bigr)
 \le \left(\frac{\varepsilon}{a-\varepsilon}\right)^q
 \le C\varepsilon^q,
\end{equation}
also for $z=0$.  Conversely, boundedness of the $V_i$ and
\eqref{eq:ballformula} with radius $\varepsilon/4$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:differentlabeltransitionlower}
 \mathbb P_z\bigl(B\cap\overline{B(v,\varepsilon/4)}\ne\varnothing\bigr)
 \ge c\varepsilon^q
\end{equation}
for $z=0$ and for $z$ within $\varepsilon/4$ of a centre carrying a different
label.  Both estimates are uniform in the centres.

\emph{Lower bound in \eqref{eq:onecopyhit}.}  Put $\tau_0=0$ and
\[
 \tau_i:=\inf\{t\ge\tau_{i-1}:
 W(t)\in\overline{B(x_i,\varepsilon/4)}\},
 \qquad 1\le i\le k,
\]
with all later times infinite once one of them is.  By the strong Markov
property and \eqref{eq:differentlabeltransitionlower}, each of the $k$
transitions has conditional probability at least $c\varepsilon^q$, so
$\mathbb P(\tau_k<\infty)\ge c^k\varepsilon^{qk}$; and $\{\tau_k<\infty\}\subseteq H_x$
because the closed balls of radius $\varepsilon/4$ lie inside the open balls of
radius $\varepsilon$.

\emph{Upper bound in \eqref{eq:onecopyhit}.}  On $H_x$ all the closed balls
$\overline{B(x_i,\varepsilon)}$ are hit; they are pairwise disjoint, so their
first-hit order has no ties.  Fix an order and drop the requirement that
later balls are not hit earlier.  Iterating
\eqref{eq:differentlabeltransition} with the strong Markov property bounds
the resulting sequential hitting event by $C^k\varepsilon^{qk}$; summing over the
at most $k!$ orders proves the upper bound.

\emph{Proof of \eqref{eq:pairedhit}.}  Put
\[
 R:=\{i:|x_i-y_i|>2\varepsilon\},\qquad s:=\# R.
\]
For $i\notin R$ we have $g_\varepsilon(x_i,y_i)\ge3^{-q}$, so we may drop the
second target ball $B(y_i,\varepsilon)$ for such labels at the cost of a constant.
The $k+s$ remaining closed targets are pairwise disjoint; fix their
first-hit order.  A transition from the origin to a target, or between
different labels, is bounded by $C\varepsilon^q$ as above.  For an equal-label pair
with $|x_i-y_i|>2\varepsilon$ and $z\in\overline{B(x_i,\varepsilon)}$,
\eqref{eq:ballformula} gives
\[
 \mathbb P_z\bigl(B\cap\overline{B(y_i,\varepsilon)}\ne\varnothing\bigr)
 \le \left(\frac{\varepsilon}{|x_i-y_i|-\varepsilon}\right)^q
 \le 3^q g_\varepsilon(x_i,y_i),
\]
and the same holds with $x_i,y_i$ interchanged.  Suppose the two
equal-label targets are consecutive exactly for the labels in
$J\subseteq R$, and put $c=\#J$.  Among the $k+s$ transitions (counting the
initial one from the origin) there are $c$ equal-label transitions and
$k+s-c$ others, so this order contributes at most
$C\varepsilon^{q(k+s-c)}\prod_{i\in J}g_\varepsilon(x_i,y_i)$.  Since all centres lie in a
fixed bounded set, $g_\varepsilon(x_i,y_i)\ge c_0\varepsilon^q$ for $i\in R\setminus J$,
and hence
\[
 \varepsilon^{q(k+s-c)}\prod_{i\in J}g_\varepsilon(x_i,y_i)
 =\varepsilon^{qk}\prod_{i\in J}g_\varepsilon(x_i,y_i)
   \prod_{i\in R\setminus J}\varepsilon^q
 \le C\varepsilon^{qk}\prod_{i\in R}g_\varepsilon(x_i,y_i).
\]
Summing over the at most $(2k)!$ orders and restoring the factors with
$i\notin R$, each bounded below by $3^{-q}$, proves \eqref{eq:pairedhit}.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:family}
Let $U\subset\mathbb{R}^D$ be open, let
\[
 \Phi=(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_k):U\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Conf}_k(\mathbb{R}^d)
\]
be $C^1$, and let $K\subset U$ be a compact parameter cube with nonempty
interior.  We call $(K,\Phi)$ a \emph{normalised rank chart} if there is
$a>0$ such that \ref{N1}--\ref{N2} hold, and a \emph{normalised
positive-excess chart} if \ref{N3} holds as well.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\normalfont(N\arabic*)]
\item\label{N1} \emph{Geometry and labels:} for all $\omega,\omega'\in K$,
\[
 a\le |\phi_i(\omega)|\le a^{-1},\qquad
 |\phi_i(\omega)-\phi_l(\omega')|\ge a\quad(i\ne l),
\]
and the image of each single label has diameter at most $a/10$.
\item\label{N2} \emph{Effective parametrisation:} $\Phi$ is an embedding on
a neighbourhood of $K$, every $\phi_i$ is a submersion, and every submap
$\Phi_I=(\phi_i)_{i\in I}$ has constant rank $r_I$ there.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:measurebox}, if $\pi$ is a permutation,
$I_j=\{\pi(1),\dots,\pi(j)\}$, $0<t_1\le\cdots\le t_k\le1$ and
$\omega\in K$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flagbox}
\operatorname{Leb}\bigl\{\omega'\in K:
 |\phi_{\pi(j)}(\omega')-\phi_{\pi(j)}(\omega)|\le t_j,
 \ 1\le j\le k\bigr\}
 \lesssim\prod_{j=1}^k t_j^{\,r_{I_j}-r_{I_{j-1}}},
 \qquad r_\varnothing=0,
\end{equation}
with a constant depending only on the chart and on the maps $\phi_i$ on a
fixed neighbourhood of $K$, uniform in $\omega$, in the radii $t_j$ and in
the finitely many permutations $\pi$.
\item\label{N3} \emph{Proper subconfigurations:}
\[
 \Delta_I:=r_I-q\,\# I>0
 \qquad(\varnothing\ne I\subsetneq\{1,\dots,k\}).
\]
\end{enumerate}
Since $\Phi$ is an embedding, $r_{\{1,\dots,k\}}=D$: this is the number of
nonredundant parameters of a placed copy, with translation, rotation and
dilation counted whenever they are free.  At resolution $\varepsilon$ a
$D$-dimensional parameter set has about $\varepsilon^{-D}$ cells, while hitting
the $k$ target balls costs a factor $\varepsilon^{qk}$; this gives the
\emph{full excess}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Delta}
 \Delta:=D-k(d-2)=D-qk.
\end{equation}

\label{def:atlas}
A nonempty scale-invariant family $\mathcal F$ of unordered $k$-point
configurations
has a \emph{rank-regular scale atlas} if it is covered, after local
labelling, by countably many $C^1$ embeddings
\[

parameter can be refined to a normalised rank chart.  Dilation is one of the

from second countability and a compact exhaustion, not from quotienting out
the scale.  The atlas is \emph{fixed-rank} if all its charts have the same
$D$ and, after the corresponding permutations of local labels, the same
ranks $r_I$; the collection of inequalities over all $I$ is unchanged by
such permutations.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:measurebox}
Let $U\subset\mathbb{R}^D$ be open, let $K\subset U$ be compact, let
$f_1,\dots,f_k:U\to\mathbb{R}^d$ be $C^1$, and fix a permutation $\pi$.  Suppose
every nested map $F_j=(f_{\pi(1)},\dots,f_{\pi(j)})$ has constant rank
$\rho_j$ on a neighbourhood of $K$.  Then, uniformly in the centres $y_j$
and in $0<t_1\le\cdots\le t_k\le1$,
\[
 \operatorname{Leb}\{x\in K:|f_{\pi(j)}(x)-y_j|\le t_j,
 \ 1\le j\le k\}
 \lesssim\prod_{j=1}^k t_j^{\rho_j-\rho_{j-1}},
 \qquad \rho_0=0,
\]
with a constant depending only on $K$, on the maps $f_i$ on a fixed
neighbourhood of $K$, and on $\pi$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Put $a_j=\rho_j-\rho_{j-1}\ge0$ and fix $x_0\in K$.  The row spaces
$\mathcal R_j:=\operatorname{row}DF_j(x_0)\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^D)^*$,
$\mathcal R_0=\{0\}$, form a flag
$\mathcal R_0\subseteq\mathcal R_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal R_k$
with $\dim\mathcal R_j=\rho_j$, and $\mathcal R_j$ is spanned by
$\mathcal R_{j-1}$ and the rows of $Df_{\pi(j)}(x_0)$.  Select $a_j$
linear combinations of the latter rows whose classes form a basis of
$\mathcal R_j/\mathcal R_{j-1}$; equivalently, choose a linear map
$L_j:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^{a_j}$ such that the rows of $D(L_jf_{\pi(j)})(x_0)$
complement $\mathcal R_{j-1}$ inside $\mathcal R_j$ (this block is omitted
when $a_j=0$).  All selected rows form a basis of $\mathcal R_k$; complete
it to a basis of $(\mathbb{R}^D)^*$ by the rows of a linear map
$h:\mathbb{R}^D\to\mathbb{R}^{D-\rho_k}$.  Since $\sum_ja_j+(D-\rho_k)=D$, the map
\[
 G=(L_1f_{\pi(1)},\ldots,L_kf_{\pi(k)},h):U\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^D
\]
has invertible derivative at $x_0$, and the inverse function theorem gives
a precompact neighbourhood $P_{x_0}$ on which $G$ is one-to-one and
$|\det DG|$ is bounded below by a positive constant.  Let
\[
 E:=\{x\in K:|f_{\pi(j)}(x)-y_j|\le t_j,\ 1\le j\le k\}.
\]
For $x\in E\cap P_{x_0}$ we have
$|L_jf_{\pi(j)}(x)-L_jy_j|\le\|L_j\|t_j$, so $G(E\cap P_{x_0})$ is
contained in $\prod_{j}B_{\mathbb{R}^{a_j}}(L_jy_j,\|L_j\|t_j)\times H_{x_0}$,
where $H_{x_0}$ is a fixed bounded set containing the possible
$h$-coordinates.  The change of variables formula gives
$\operatorname{Leb}(E\cap P_{x_0})\le C_{x_0}\prod_jt_j^{a_j}$, with a
constant independent of the centres (translating the balls does not change
their volumes) and of the radii.  Since the ranks are constant on a
neighbourhood of $K$, compactness provides finitely many patches $P_{x_0}$
covering $K$; summing their estimates proves the lemma.  The constant-rank
hypothesis is what produces the nondegenerate coordinate map $G$; a
Lipschitz bound alone would give no lower Jacobian control.  The ordering
of the radii is not used here; it matters in the flag decomposition below.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:resonance}
Let $(K,\Phi)$ satisfy \ref{N1}--\ref{N3} and suppose $\Delta>0$.  Then,
for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, uniformly for $\omega\in\Lambda_\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resonancesum}
 \sum_{\omega'\in\Lambda_\varepsilon}Q_\varepsilon(\omega,\omega')
 \lesssim\varepsilon^{-\Delta}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Choose a compact neighbourhood $K^+\subset U$ of $K$ on which all submap
ranks remain constant; for small $\varepsilon$ every $C\varepsilon$-thickening used
below lies in $K^+$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:measurebox} applied to $K^+$ gives
the flag estimate for the thickened cells.  An $\varepsilon$-separated net has at
most $C\varepsilon^{-D}\operatorname{Leb}(A^{C\varepsilon})$ points in a set $A$, since
the balls of radius $\varepsilon/3$ about those points are disjoint and lie in
$A^{\varepsilon/3}$; thickening a dyadic cell enlarges each flag tolerance by only
$O(\varepsilon)$, which is absorbed at the lowest dyadic scale.

Decompose dyadically the $k$ quantities
$|\phi_i(\omega')-\phi_i(\omega)|+\varepsilon$, and choose $L$ with
$2^{-L}\asymp\varepsilon$.  On each dyadic cell, order the labels by a permutation
$\pi$ so that
\[
 |\phi_{\pi(j)}(\omega')-\phi_{\pi(j)}(\omega)|+\varepsilon
 \asymp 2^{-n_j},
 \qquad L\ge n_1\ge\cdots\ge n_k\ge0 ;
\]
for small $\varepsilon$, \ref{N1} keeps these same-label distances below $1$, so
the radii lie in the range of \eqref{eq:flagbox}.  Put
$I_j=\{\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(j)\}$, $a_j=r_{I_j}-r_{I_{j-1}}$, $n_{k+1}=0$
and $g_j=n_j-n_{j+1}\ge0$.  The net count contributes $\varepsilon^{-D}$, the
kernels contribute $\varepsilon^{qk}2^{q\sum_j n_j}$, and \eqref{eq:flagbox}
contributes $2^{-\sum_j n_ja_j}$.  Abel summation gives
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^k n_j(a_j-q)
 =\sum_{j=1}^k g_j\sum_{h=1}^j(a_h-q)
 =\sum_{j=1}^k g_j\Delta_{I_j},
\]
so, for a fixed permutation, the sum over dyadic cells is at most
\[
 \varepsilon^{qk-D}
 \sum_{g_1,\dots,g_k\ge0}
 2^{-\sum_{j=1}^k g_j\Delta_{I_j}},
\]
where extending the gaps to all nonnegative integers only enlarges the sum.
Every exponent is positive, by \ref{N3} for a proper prefix and by
$\Delta>0$ for the last one, so the product of geometric series is bounded,
and $\varepsilon^{qk-D}=\varepsilon^{-\Delta}$.  Summing over the finitely many
permutations proves \eqref{eq:resonancesum}.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:scaleergodic}
For every $\lambda>1$, the map
\[
 (S_\lambda W)(t):=\lambda^{-1}W(\lambda^2t)
\]
is measure preserving and ergodic on one-sided Wiener space.  Hence every
event determined by the trace through a property invariant under spatial
dilation has probability $0$ or $1$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
$S_\lambda$ is measure preserving by Brownian scaling \cite[Lem.~1.7]{PM}.
The Lamperti transform $Y(s)=e^{-s/2}W(e^s)$, $s\in\mathbb{R}$ (see also
\cite[Rem.~1.10]{PM}), is a measurable shift-equivariant isomorphism onto
its image, with inverse $W(t)=t^{1/2}Y(\log t)$ for $t>0$ and $W(0)=0$; it
conjugates $S_\lambda$ to translation by $2\log\lambda$.  The process $Y$
is stationary Gaussian with covariance
$\mathbb E(Y(s)Y(t)^*)=e^{-|t-s|/2}I_d$.  For two finite vectors of sampled
values of $Y$ whose sampling times are separated by $h$, every
cross-covariance tends to zero as $h\to\infty$; after repeated time
coordinates are removed, the covariance matrices converge to a
block-diagonal positive-definite matrix, and the Gaussian densities converge
in $L^1$ to the product density.  Thus cylinder events are mixing.  The
cylinder algebra generates the Borel sigma-field of path space and is dense
for the metric $d(A,C)=\mathbb P(A\mathbin{\triangle}C)$; since
translation is measure preserving, approximating either event changes the
mixing identity uniformly in the shift, so the identity extends to all
measurable events.  Hence the shift, and with it $S_\lambda$, is mixing and
therefore ergodic.  Finally, the trace of $S_\lambda W$ is
$\lambda^{-1}B$, so a dilation-invariant property of the trace defines an
$S_\lambda$-invariant event.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:productcapacity}
Let $E\subset(\mathbb{R}^d)^s$ be Borel.  Let the initial vector
$(W^1(0),\dots,W^s(0))$ have a law absolutely continuous with respect to
Lebesgue measure and, conditionally on it, let
$W^i(t)=W^i(0)+\widetilde W^i(t)$, where $\widetilde W^1,\dots,\widetilde W^s$
are independent standard Brownian motions, independent of the initial
vector.  If $\operatorname{Cap}_s(E)=0$, then
\[
 \mathbb P\{(W^1(t_1),\dots,W^s(t_s))\in E
 \text{ for some }t_1,\dots,t_s>0\}=0.
\]
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Kill the $s$ Brownian motions at independent exponential times of mean one,
and let $h(x_1,\ldots,x_s)$ be the probability, starting from
$(x_1,\ldots,x_s)$, that the killed paths satisfy
$(W^1(t_1),\ldots,W^s(t_s))\in E$ for some $t_1,\ldots,t_s>0$.
Exponentially killed Brownian motion is a transient symmetric Borel right
process, self-dual with respect to Lebesgue measure, and satisfies the
standing hypotheses of Fitzsimmons and Salisbury \cite{FS}.  Let
$U^if(x):=\mathbb E_x\int_0^{\zeta_i}f(W^i(t))\,dt$ and let $\nu_iU^i$ be
the measure $A\mapsto\int U^i\mathbf1_A\,d\nu_i$.  If $\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_s$
are probability measures with $\nu_iU^i\le\operatorname{Leb}$ in the order
of measures, that is, $\nu_iU^i$ has a Lebesgue density at most one, then
\cite[Lem.~3.2(a), Thm.~4.16(i) and proof of Thm.~5.1]{FS} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FShitting}
 \int h(x_1,\ldots,x_s)\,d\nu_1(x_1)\cdots d\nu_s(x_s)
 \le2^{3s}\operatorname{Cap}_s(E);
\end{equation}
indeed, the symmetrised kernels in \cite[(4.3)]{FS} all equal
$\mathcal K_s$ because killed Brownian motion is symmetric, so their energy
is the one defining $\operatorname{Cap}_s$.  This is the only input from
multiparameter Markov-process theory.

Let $Q_i$ be half-open unit cubes and let $\nu_i$ be Lebesgue measure
restricted to $Q_i$.  The Green-density identity gives
\[
 \frac{d(\nu_iU^i)}{d\operatorname{Leb}}(y)
 =\int_{Q_i}u_1(x-y)\,dx
 \le\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}u_1(x-y)\,dx=1,
\]
so the $\nu_i$ are admissible in \eqref{eq:FShitting}, and
$\operatorname{Cap}_s(E)=0$ gives $\int_{Q_1\times\cdots\times Q_s}h=0$.
Products of half-open integer unit cubes partition $(\mathbb{R}^d)^s$, so $h=0$
Lebesgue-almost everywhere; integrating against the density of the initial
vector proves the assertion for the killed processes.  Finally, for
$T<\infty$ the killing times all exceed $T$ with probability $e^{-sT}$,
independently of the paths, so a positive hitting probability for the
unkilled processes with $(t_1,\ldots,t_s)\in(0,T]^s$ would give one for the
killed processes.  Take the union over $T\in\mathbb{N}$.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:polar}
Let $M\subset(\mathbb{R}^d)^s$ be a countable union of compact sets $M_n$, each
contained in a precompact coordinate patch of a $C^1$ embedded manifold of
dimension at most $qs$.  Then $\operatorname{Cap}_s(M)=0$; consequently, $M$
is not hit under any initial law covered by
Lemma~\ref{lem:productcapacity}.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
On a bounded set, the substitution $t=|z|^2v$ in the definition of $u_1$,
with $v$ restricted to a compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$, gives
$u_1(z)\gtrsim|z|^{-q}$; since $|x_i-y_i|\le|x-y|$, it follows that
$\mathcal K_s(x,y)\gtrsim|x-y|^{-qs}$.  Consider one patch of dimension
$r\le qs$.  It is covered by $N\le C\rho^{-r}$ balls of radius $\rho$;
assigning every point to the first ball containing it gives a Borel
partition into $N$ sets of diameter at most $2\rho$.  For a probability
measure $\mu$ on the patch, $(\mu\times\mu)\{|x-y|\le2\rho\}$ is at least
the sum of the squared masses of the cells, hence at least
$1/N\gtrsim\rho^r$ by Cauchy--Schwarz, and the layer-cake formula gives
\[
 \iint |x-y|^{-qs}\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y)
 \gtrsim\int_0^1\rho^{r-qs-1}\,d\rho=\infty
 \qquad(r\le qs).
\]
Thus every probability measure on one patch has infinite
$\mathcal K_s$-energy.  Disjointify $M=\bigcup_nM_n$ into Borel sets
$F_n\subset M_n$; a probability measure on $M$ gives positive mass to some
$F_n$, its normalised restriction to $F_n$ has infinite energy, and
positivity of the kernel makes the energy of the original measure infinite.
Hence $\operatorname{Cap}_s(M)=0$.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:onepathcapacity}
Let $M\subset\operatorname{Conf}_s(\mathbb{R}^d)$ be a countable union of compact
$C^1$ coordinate patches of dimension at most $qs$.  Almost surely there
are no distinct positive times $t_1,\dots,t_s$ such that
$(W(t_1),\dots,W(t_s))\in M$.
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Distinct spatial coordinates force the hitting times to be distinct.  Cover
the tuples of distinct positive times by countably many products
$J_1\times\cdots\times J_s$ of open intervals with rational endpoints and
pairwise disjoint closures, cover each $J_i$ by countably many compact
intervals $[a_i,b_i]\subset J_i$ whose interiors cover $J_i$, and exhaust
each $(a_i,b_i)$ by the intervals $[a_i+\delta,b_i-\delta]$; it suffices to
treat one resulting product.

A \emph{Brownian bridge} from $x$ at time $a$ to $y$ at time $b$ is the
conditional law of the path on $[a,b]$ given these endpoint values; with
$T=b-a$ and $\widehat W$ a standard Brownian motion started at the origin,
it is the law of
\[
 X(t)=x+\frac{t-a}{T}(y-x)+\widehat W(t-a)
      -\frac{t-a}{T}\widehat W(T),
 \qquad a\le t\le b,
\]
see \cite[Ex.~1.5(a),(b) and Ex.~2.11]{PM}.  By the Markov property and
the independence of increments over disjoint intervals, conditionally on
all values $W(a_i)$ and $W(b_i)$ the path pieces on the intervals
$[a_i,b_i]$ are independent Brownian bridges.  Write
$p_t(x,y)=(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\{-|x-y|^2/(2t)\}$ and choose
$0<\eta<\delta<T/2$.  The value $Z$ of the bridge at time $a+\eta$ has
density
\[
 z\longmapsto\frac{p_\eta(x,z)\,p_{T-\eta}(z,y)}{p_T(x,y)},
\]
and, conditionally on $Z=z$, the law of the bridge on $[a+\eta,b-\delta]$
is absolutely continuous with respect to that of Brownian motion started
at $z$, with Radon--Nikodym derivative
\[
 \frac{p_\delta(W(b-\delta),y)}{p_{T-\eta}(z,y)}
\]
on the corresponding sigma-field.  All denominators are strictly positive.
The interior interval is needed because the bridge is pinned at time $b$,
where it is singular with respect to Brownian motion, while the initial
time $a+\eta$ gives the starting vector a Lebesgue density, as
Lemma~\ref{lem:productcapacity} requires; every time tested in
$[a+\delta,b-\delta]$ is at least $\delta-\eta>0$ after the comparison
Brownian motion starts, so hitting is tested at positive times.  Thus, on
the smaller compact intervals, the joint
starting vector has a Lebesgue density and, conditionally on it, the
independent bridge pieces are absolutely continuous with respect to
independent Brownian motions at strictly positive times, the joint
Radon--Nikodym derivative being the product of the displayed finite positive
factors.  Every null event for independent Brownian motions is therefore
null for the bridge pieces, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:productcapacity} and
\ref{lem:polar} make the hitting probability zero.  Integrating over the
endpoint data, exhausting the intervals from within and taking the countable
union proves the claim.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:obstruction}
Let $\mathcal F$ be covered by a countable rank-regular $C^1$ atlas as in
Definition~\ref{def:atlas}, without any assumption of scale invariance.  If
on one of its charts a nonempty submap $\Psi_I$ has rank
\[
 r_I\le(d-2)\,\# I,
\]
then almost surely the trace contains no configuration from that chart.  In
particular, if every chart has such a subconfiguration, almost surely the
trace contains no member of $\mathcal F$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
By the constant-rank theorem, the image of $\Psi_I$ is locally a countable
union of $r_I$-dimensional $C^1$ submanifolds.  On the part where none of
its coordinates is the origin, Lemma~\ref{lem:onepathcapacity} rules out a
hit.

It remains to treat configurations containing the starting point.  Since
points are polar, the trace visits the origin only at time $0$, and by
distinctness at most one label $j\in I$ can be at the origin.  Note that
$\# I\ge2$, since $r_{\{i\}}=d>d-2$.  As $\psi_j$ is a submersion,
$Z_j=\{\omega:\psi_j(\omega)=0\}$ is a $C^1$ submanifold.  With

\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A,C)
 =\operatorname{rank}A+\operatorname{rank}(C|_{\ker A}),
\]
together with $\ker A=TZ_j$, gives

 \operatorname{rank}
 D\!\left(\Psi_{I\setminus\{j\}}\big|_{Z_j}\right)=r_I-d
\]
at every point of $Z_j$.  By the constant-rank theorem and a compact
exhaustion, the image of $\Psi_{I\setminus\{j\}}|_{Z_j}$ is a countable

\[
 r_I-d\le q\,\# I-d=q(\# I-1)-2<q(\# I-1),
\]
so the remaining positive-time coordinates are polar by
Lemma~\ref{lem:onepathcapacity}.  If the anchored label does not belong to
$I$, the first paragraph applies to $\Psi_I$ directly.  A countable atlas
and compact exhaustion complete the proof.

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{thm:trichotomy}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a scale-invariant family in $\mathbb{R}^d$, $d\ge3$, with a
rank-regular fixed-rank scale atlas of effective dimension $D$, and put
$\Delta_I=r_I-(d-2)\,\# I$ and $\Delta=D-k(d-2)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\normalfont(\alph*)]
\item If $\Delta>0$ and every proper nonempty subconfiguration has positive
excess, almost surely the trace of Brownian motion contains a member of
$\mathcal F$.
\item If $\Delta<0$, almost surely it contains no member of $\mathcal F$.
\item If $\Delta=0$, almost surely it contains no member of $\mathcal F$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
If $\Delta>0$, choose a nonanchored parameter and a sufficiently small
normalised positive-excess compact chart around it; nonanchored parameters
exist because every coordinate map is a submersion, so each zero fibre
$\psi_i^{-1}(0)$ is a codimension-$d$ submanifold of Lebesgue measure zero,
and finitely many of them cannot cover a nonempty open chart.
Theorem~\ref{thm:localtrichotomy} gives a copy with positive probability.
The event that the trace contains some member of $\mathcal F$ is invariant
under spatial dilation, since the scaled path has trace $\lambda^{-1}B$ and
$\mathcal F$ is scale invariant; by Lemma~\ref{lem:scaleergodic} its
probability is zero or one, hence one.

If $\Delta\le0$, the full-label submap has nonpositive excess on every
chart.  Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruction} to each chart, which
covers the strict and the critical inequality alike as well as
configurations through the starting point, and use countability of the
atlas.

\label{lem:atlasfromchart}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a nonempty scale-invariant family of $k$-point
configurations covered, after local labelling, by countably many $C^1$
embeddings $\Psi_\nu:U_\nu\to\operatorname{Conf}_k(\mathbb{R}^d)$ with $U_\nu$
open, such that every coordinate of $\Psi_\nu$ is a submersion and every
submap $\Psi_{\nu,I}$ has constant rank $r_I$ on $U_\nu$, with the same
$D_\nu=D$ and the same ranks $r_I$ for all $\nu$ after relabelling.  Then
$\mathcal F$ has a rank-regular fixed-rank scale atlas.
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
Each $U_\nu$ is second countable, hence exhausted by countably many
compact cubes, and the ranks are constant on a neighbourhood of each of
them; so the $\Psi_\nu$ form a countable rank-regular atlas, fixed-rank by
assumption.  It remains to refine a nonanchored parameter
$\omega_0\in U_\nu$ to a normalised rank chart.  The $k$ points of
$\Psi_\nu(\omega_0)$ are distinct and nonzero, so by continuity a
sufficiently small compact cube $K\ni\omega_0$ has labelled images lying
in pairwise disjoint balls away from the origin, each image of diameter at
most $a/10$ for a suitable $a>0$; this is \ref{N1}, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:Timpliesreg} gives \ref{N2}.

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:APnondeg}
The progression family has a rank-regular fixed-rank scale atlas, with
\[
 r_I=
 \begin{cases}
 d,&\# I=1,\\
 2d,&\# I\ge2,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \Delta_I=
 \begin{cases}
 2,&\# I=1,\\
 2d-(d-2)\,\# I,&\# I\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
The differential of the term with index $i$ is $A_i(u,v)=u+(i-1)v$, which
is surjective.  For $i_1\ne i_2$, the map
$(u,v)\mapsto\bigl(u+(i_1-1)v,u+(i_2-1)v\bigr)$ is invertible, since the
difference of its components gives $(i_2-i_1)v$ and then either component
gives $u$; so every submap with at least two terms has rank $2d$.  The full
ordered map is an embedding, because its first two coordinates recover
$(a,g)$ continuously, each coordinate is a submersion, because translation
is free, and dilation acts on parameters by $(a,g)\mapsto(\lambda a,\lambda g)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:atlasfromchart} gives the atlas.  An unordered progression
has the two local labellings $(a,g)$ and $(a+(k-1)g,-g)$; this finite
redundancy changes neither dimensions nor ranks.

<!-- corollary-7: body_md -->
\label{cor:APall}
For $d\ge3$, the trace of Brownian motion in $\mathbb{R}^d$ almost surely contains
an arithmetic progression of length $\lceil2d/(d-2)\rceil-1$ and almost
surely contains none of length $\lceil2d/(d-2)\rceil$.  The maximal length
is thus
\[
 5\quad(d=3),\qquad
 3\quad(d=4,5),\qquad
 2\quad(d\ge6).
\]
<!-- end corollary-7 -->
<!-- proof-28: body_md -->
The largest integer strictly smaller than $2d/(d-2)$ is
$\lceil2d/(d-2)\rceil-1$.  Corollary~\ref{cor:subcriterion} gives a
progression of this length, and Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruction} excludes
the next length, also when its excess is exactly zero; a longer progression
contains one of the excluded length.  Substituting $d=3,4,5$ and $d\ge6$
gives the displayed values.
<!-- end proof-28 -->
<!-- corollary-8: body_md -->
\label{cor:homothetic}
For $d\ge3$ and every fixed set $F\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ of $k\ge2$ distinct points,
the trace of Brownian motion almost surely contains a positive homothetic
copy of $F$ if
\[
 k<\frac{d+1}{d-2},
\]
and almost surely contains none if $k\ge(d+1)/(d-2)$.
<!-- end corollary-8 -->
<!-- proof-29: body_md -->
The full ordered map is an embedding: two labelled coordinates
$x_i=a+tf_i$ and $x_j=a+tf_j$ give
\[
 t=\frac{\langle x_i-x_j,f_i-f_j\rangle}{|f_i-f_j|^2},
 \qquad a=x_i-tf_i,
\]
where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the Euclidean inner product.  Each
coordinate is a submersion because translation is free, the submap ranks
computed above are constant, and dilation acts on parameters by
$(a,t)\mapsto(\lambda a,\lambda t)$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:atlasfromchart} gives
a rank-regular fixed-rank scale atlas, and Corollary~\ref{cor:subcriterion}
gives the conclusion.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:distance}
Almost surely the distance set $\mathcal D(B):=\{|x-y|:x,y\in B\}$ equals
$[0,\infty)$.  Indeed, $W(0)=0$, $t\mapsto|W(t)|$ is continuous, and
$|W(t)|\to\infty$ by transience \cite[Thm.~3.20]{PM}, so the intermediate
value theorem gives $\{|W(t)-W(0)|:t\ge0\}=[0,\infty)$.  This says nothing
about prescribed gap vectors.

<!-- lemma-14: body_md -->
\label{lem:simrank}
For a singleton, $r_{\{i\}}=d$.  If $\# I\ge2$ and
$e_I=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{f_i:i\in I\}$, then the corresponding submap
of the nonredundant similarity family has rank
\[
 r_I=p-\binom{d-e_I}{2}
 =d+1+\tfrac12e_I(2d-e_I-1).
\]
In particular, $r_{\{1,\dots,k\}}=D_m$.
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- proof-30: body_md -->
The kernel of $\widetilde A_I$ consists of the triples with
$(s\,\mathrm{Id}+X)(f_i-f_l)=0$ for all $i,l\in I$.  For a nonzero vector
$v$ in the $e_I$-dimensional direction space of the affine span,
$0=\langle(s\,\mathrm{Id}+X)v,v\rangle=s|v|^2$ because $X$ is
antisymmetric, so $s=0$.  Then $X$ vanishes on the direction space, whose
orthogonal complement is invariant by skew-symmetry, and the free block
there is an arbitrary element of $\mathfrak{so}(d-e_I)$; finally
$u=-Xf_i$ for any $i\in I$, independently of $i$ because $X$ kills all
differences $f_i-f_l$.  Hence $\dim\ker\widetilde A_I=\binom{d-e_I}{2}$.
Let $K_F\subseteq K_I$ be the kernels for the full configuration and for
$I$, and let $T$ be a linear complement of $K_F$ in the unreduced tangent
space; $T$ supplies the nonredundant parameter directions.  Since
$K_I=K_F\oplus(K_I\cap T)$,
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{rank}(D\Phi_I|_T)
 &=\dim T-\dim(K_I\cap T)\\
 &=(p-\dim K_F)-(\dim K_I-\dim K_F)
 =p-\binom{d-e_I}{2}.
\end{align*}
A singleton has rank $d$ because translation is free.
<!-- end proof-30 -->
<!-- theorem-9: body_md -->
\label{thm:similar}
Fix $d\ge3$ and a finite set $F\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ of $k\ge2$ distinct points with
affine dimension $m\ge1$, and put $D_m=d+1+\frac12m(2d-m-1)$.  The trace
of Brownian motion almost surely contains a similar copy of $F$ if
\[
 k<\frac{D_m}{d-2},
\]
and almost surely contains none if $k\ge D_m/(d-2)$.
<!-- end theorem-9 -->
<!-- proof-31: body_md -->
If the full excess $D_m-(d-2)k$ is positive, every subconfiguration has
positive excess by the computation above, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:subcriterion} gives occurrence.  If it is nonpositive,
Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruction} gives absence, including at equality.
<!-- end proof-31 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:corners}
In $\mathbb{R}^3$, the right isosceles corner $\{0,e_1,e_2\}$ has affine dimension
$2$, full rank $7$ and excess $7-3=4$, and every pair has rank $6$ and
excess $6-2=4$; Theorem~\ref{thm:similar} therefore gives a translated,
rotated and uniformly scaled copy on the trace.  The same computation
applies to every fixed noncollinear triangle: an aperture and a side-length
ratio may be prescribed in advance, and the theorem supplies some
orientation of that shape.  The quantifier is for each fixed shape; it does
not assert simultaneous realisation of an uncountable family of shapes.
Prescribing absolute side lengths removes dilation and is not covered by
this scale-invariant theorem.
