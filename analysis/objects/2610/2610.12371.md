A point $x \in \mathbb{X}$ is \emph{in-bounds} if it satisfies one of the following conditions:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] $L_{\Phi,f}(x) < f'(x) < U_{\Phi,f}(x)$,
\item[(b)] $f'(x) = f(x)$.

A point $x \in \mathbb{X}$ is \emph{correctly classified} if $C_{\Phi,f}(x) = C_{\Phi,f'}(x)$. Equivalently, $x$ is correctly classified if all points in $\mathrm{St}(x)$ are marked as good.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
When a point $x \in \mathbb{X}$ is visited during logarithmic-scaling quantization, the quantization process terminates, and upon termination, $x$ is in-bounds.
\label{lemma:pipeline-quantization}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Logarithmic-scaling quantization is an iterative procedure that repeatedly applies linear-scaling quantization with increasing precision levels $p_x$. At each iteration, if $L_{\Phi,f}(x) < f'(x) < U_{\Phi,f}(x)$, the process terminates. Otherwise, the precision $p_x$ is incremented and quantization continues.

If this termination condition is not satisfied after a fixed number of iterations, the process is truncated and $f'(x) \gets f(x)$. In either case, the procedure terminates, and upon termination, $x$ is in-bounds.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
Assume that $C_{\Phi,f}$ is nondegenerate. Let $x \in \mathbb{X}$ be a point visited during iterative star correction. Then the correction process for $x$ terminates, and upon termination, $x$ is correctly classified.
\label{lemma:pipeline-point-correction}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
When $x$ is visited during star correction, the process proceeds through a sequence of iterations. In each iteration, termination occurs only if no points in $\mathrm{St}(x)$ are marked as bad. Consider an iteration in which some points are still marked as bad.

By the nondegeneracy assumption, if a set of points $\{y_i\} \subseteq \mathrm{St}(x)$ is marked as bad, then at least one such $y_i$ satisfies $f(y_i) \neq f'(y_i)$. This implies that $p_{y_i} \leq P$, since otherwise $y_i$ would be stored losslessly.

Among all points marked as bad, let $y_j$ be one with minimal precision $p_{y_j}$. Then $p_{y_j} \leq P$, and during this iteration, its precision is incremented: $p_{y_j} \gets p_{y_j} + 1$. Therefore, at each iteration, the minimum precision among bad points strictly increases. Since $p_{y_j}$ is bounded above by $P$, only finitely many such increments are possible.

Consequently, after a finite number of iterations, all points in $\mathrm{St}(x)$ will have precision exceeding $P$ and hence will be stored losslessly, so no points remain marked as bad. This implies that star correction terminates.

Finally, since termination occurs only when no points in $\mathrm{St}(x)$ are marked as bad, it follows by assumption that $x$ is correctly classified upon termination.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
Assume that $C_{\Phi,f}$ is nondegenerate. Suppose that at the beginning of pass $t$, every incorrectly classified point belongs to $D_t$. Then, at the beginning of pass $t+1$, every incorrectly classified point belongs to $D_{t+1}$.
\label{lemma:pipeline-all-dirty}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
During pass $t$, a point $x$ is added to $D_{t+1}$ if either of the following conditions holds:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
\item[(a)] $f'(x)$ is updated when a neighbor $y$ undergoes star correction,
\item[(b)] $x$ has a neighbor $y$ whose value $f'(y)$ is updated at some point during the pass.
\end{itemize}

Let $x \in \mathbb{X}$. We show that if neither (a) nor (b) holds, then $\mathrm{St}(x)$ is correctly classified at the end of pass $t$. We consider two cases.

\underline{Case 1: $x \notin D_t$.} In this case, $x$ does not undergo star correction during pass $t$. The value $f'(x)$ can only change either when $x$ itself is corrected or when a neighbor undergoes star correction. Since $x \notin D_t$, the former does not occur. Because (a) is false, the latter does not affect $f'(x)$ either. Hence, $f'(x)$ remains unchanged throughout the pass. 

Moreover, since (b) is false, none of the neighbors of $x$ experience a change in value. Therefore, the values of $f'$ over $\mathrm{St}(x)$ remain unchanged during the pass. Because $x$ was not marked dirty at the start of the pass, it is correctly classified initially. Since the values on $\mathrm{St}(x)$ do not change, $x$ remains correctly classified at the end of the pass.

\underline{Case 2: $x \in D_t$.} In this case, $x$ undergoes star correction during pass $t$. By \cref{lemma:pipeline-point-correction}, this ensures that $\mathrm{St}(x)$ is correctly classified immediately after correction. Since both (a) and (b) are false, no values in $\mathrm{St}(x)$ change for the remainder of the pass. Hence, $\mathrm{St}(x)$ remains correctly classified at the end of the pass.

In both cases, we conclude that if $x \notin D_{t+1}$, then $x$ is correctly classified at the start of pass $t+1$. Therefore, every incorrectly classified point must belong to $D_{t+1}$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
Each $x \in \mathbb{X}$ can be marked as dirty during only finitely many passes.
\label{lemma:pipeline-finite-passes}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
During pass $t$, a vertex $x \in \mathbb{X}$ is added to $D_{t+1}$ only if there exists some $y \in \mathrm{St}(x)$ whose value is updated. By reasoning analogous to that in the proof of \cref{lemma:pipeline-point-correction}, each such $y \in \mathrm{St}(x)$ can undergo only finitely many value updates across all passes.

Since $\mathrm{St}(x)$ contains finitely many vertices, it follows that $x$ can be marked as dirty only finitely many times.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
If $C_{\Phi,f}$ is nondegenerate, then the main pipeline terminates. Upon termination, every $x \in \mathbb{X}$ is correctly classified and in-bounds.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The first step of the main pipeline is initial quantization. By \cref{lemma:pipeline-quantization}, this step terminates, and upon completion, every $x \in \mathbb{X}$ is in-bounds.

We then proceed to iterative star correction. During the first pass, every vertex $x \in \mathbb{X}$ belongs to $D_1$, so all incorrectly classified points are marked as dirty. By repeatedly applying \cref{lemma:pipeline-all-dirty}, it follows that at the start of each pass $t > 1$, every incorrectly classified point is contained in $D_t$. If, for some pass $T$, we have $|D_T| = 0$, then iterative star correction terminates. By \cref{lemma:pipeline-finite-passes}, each $x \in \mathbb{X}$ can belong to only finitely many sets $D_t$. Therefore, for sufficiently large $t$, we must have $|D_t| = 0$, and star correction terminates. At that point, since no points are marked as dirty, \cref{lemma:pipeline-all-dirty} implies that all points are correctly classified.

It remains to show that all points remain in-bounds. Fix $x \in \mathbb{X}$. After initial quantization, $x$ is in-bounds. During star correction, the value of $f'(x)$ may change only if logarithmic-scaling quantization is applied to $x$. By definition, this procedure preserves the bounds. Hence, $x$ remains in-bounds throughout star correction, and therefore is in-bounds upon termination.

Finally, the remaining step of the pipeline is lossless compression, which does not modify the values of $f'$. This completes the proof.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
{\emph{FeatureZ}} correctly preserves critical points.
\label{lemma:descriptors-critical-points}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By construction, $x$ is marked as bad if and only if there exists some $y \in \mathrm{Lk}(x)$ that is marked as bad. A vertex $y$ is marked as bad if and only if 
\[
\bigl(f(x) < f(y)\bigr) \;\oplus\; \bigl(f'(x) < f'(y)\bigr).
\]
If $f(x) = f'(x)$ and $f(y) = f'(y)$, then this xor ($\oplus$) condition is false. Hence, whenever $y$ is marked as bad, it must be that either $f(x) \neq f'(x)$ or $f(y) \neq f'(y)$. Therefore, $C_{\Phi,f}$ is nondegenerate.

Now suppose that some $x \in \mathbb{X}$ has an incorrect criticality type in $f'$. Then there exists a vertex $y \in \mathrm{Lk}(x)$ whose classification relative to $x$ is inconsistent between $f$ and $f'$; that is, $y \in \mathrm{Lk}^+(x)$ in one field and $y \in \mathrm{Lk}^-(x)$ in the other. By construction, such a $y$ is marked as bad. Consequently, if no points in $\mathrm{St}(x)$ are marked as bad, then no such inconsistency exists, and $x$ must have the correct criticality type in $f'$.

Thus, if every $x \in \mathbb{X}$ is correctly classified, then every $x$ has the same criticality type in $f'$ as in $f$, which is equivalent to preserving all critical points.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
{\emph{FeatureZ}} correctly preserves Morse--Smale segmentation.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We first show that $C_{\Phi,f}$ is nondegenerate. Suppose that some $y \in \mathrm{St}(x)$ is marked as bad. By construction, this occurs if at least one of the following holds:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
\item[(i)] $x$ is inconsistently classified as a minimum or maximum between $f$ and $f'$,
\item[(ii)] the largest vertex in $\mathrm{Lk}^+(x)$ differs between $f$ and $f'$,
\item[(iii)] the smallest vertex in $\mathrm{Lk}^-(x)$ differs between $f$ and $f'$.
\end{itemize}

We show that in each case there exists a bad $y \in \mathrm{St}(x)$ such that $f(y) \neq f'(y)$. Case (i) follows as in the proof of \cref{lemma:descriptors-critical-points}. For case (ii), let $y$ and $y'$ denote the largest vertices in $\mathrm{Lk}^+(x)$ with respect to $f$ and $f'$, respectively, with $y \neq y'$. Then $f(y) > f(y')$ while $f'(y) < f'(y')$. This reversal is only possible if either $f(y) \neq f'(y)$ or $f(y') \neq f'(y')$. Case (iii) is analogous. Therefore, $C_{\Phi,f}$ is nondegenerate.

We now show that if all points are correctly classified, then the Morse--Smale segmentation is preserved. It suffices to show that all forward and backward integral lines are identical in $f$ and $f'$. We represent an integral line as a sequence of vertices in $\mathbb{X}$. Let $a_1 a_2 \cdots a_N$ and $b_1 b_2 \cdots b_M$ be integral lines of $f$ and $f'$, respectively, with $a_1 = b_1$. We show that these sequences coincide.

Let $Q = \min(N,M)$. We prove by induction that $a_i = b_i$ for all $i \leq Q$. The base case $i=1$ is immediate. Assume $a_k = b_k$ for some $k < Q$. Since $k < Q \leq N$, the vertex $a_k$ is not a maximum in $f$, so $a_{k+1}$ is the largest vertex in $\mathrm{Lk}^+(a_k)$ with respect to $f$. Similarly, $b_{k+1}$ is the largest vertex in $\mathrm{Lk}^+(b_k)$ with respect to $f'$. Because $a_k$ is correctly classified, these largest neighbors agree in $f$ and $f'$. Since $a_k = b_k$, it follows that $a_{k+1} = b_{k+1}$.

Thus, $a_i = b_i$ for all $i \leq Q$. Without loss of generality, assume $Q = N$. Then $a_Q$ is a maximum in $f$. Since $a_Q = b_Q$ and $a_Q$ is correctly classified, $b_Q$ is also a maximum in $f'$, implying $M = N$. Hence, the two integral lines coincide.

Therefore, all forward integral lines agree between $f$ and $f'$. By an analogous argument, all backward integral lines also agree. This establishes that the Morse--Smale segmentation is preserved.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
{\emph{FeatureZ}} correctly preserves local order.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
As with critical points, by construction a vertex $x$ is marked as bad if and only if there exists some $y \in \mathrm{Lk}(x)$ that is marked as bad. A vertex $y \in \mathrm{Lk}(x)$ is marked as bad if and only if
\[
\bigl(f(x) < f(y)\bigr) \;\oplus\; \bigl(f'(x) < f'(y)\bigr),
\]
where $\oplus$ denotes exclusive or (xor).

If $f(x) = f'(x)$ and $f(y) = f'(y)$, then the two inequalities agree, and hence the above condition is false. Therefore, no such $y$ is marked as bad, and it follows that $C_{\Phi,f}$ is nondegenerate.

We now verify that all ``less-than'' relationships are preserved. Suppose, for contradiction, that some such relationship is not preserved. Then there exist adjacent vertices $x,y \in \mathbb{X}$ such that $f(x) < f(y)$ but $f'(x) > f'(y)$. Equivalently, $y \in \mathrm{Lk}^+(x)$ with respect to $f$, while $y \in \mathrm{Lk}^-(x)$ with respect to $f'$. In this situation, the xor  condition above holds, so $y$ is marked as bad. 

Consequently, if no vertices are marked as bad, all ``less-than'' relationships are preserved.

{\emph{FeatureZ}} correctly preserves isocontours. In particular, let $\sigma \in \mathbb{X}$ be a simplex and let $c$ be an isovalue whose isocontour is preserved. If every $x \in \mathbb{X}$ is in-bounds, then $f^{-1}(c)$ intersects $\sigma$ if and only if $(f')^{-1}(c)$ intersects $\sigma$.
\label{lemma:descriptors-isocontours}
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Since isocontour preservation does not involve star correction, it is not necessary to establish nondegeneracy of $C_{\Phi,f}$.

Let $\{x_i\}$ denote the vertices of $\sigma$. In the PL setting, whether $f^{-1}(c)$ intersects $\sigma$ is determined by whether each $f(x_i)$ is greater than, less than, or equal to $c$.

Fix a vertex $x_i$. If $f(x_i) > c$, then by construction $L_{\Phi,f}(x_i) \geq c$. Since $x_i$ is in-bounds, it follows that $f'(x_i) > L_{\Phi,f}(x_i) \geq c$. Similarly, if $f(x_i) < c$, then $U_{\Phi,f}(x_i) \leq c$, and thus $f'(x_i) < U_{\Phi,f}(x_i) \leq c$. Finally, if $f(x_i) = c$, then by construction $f'(x_i) = f(x_i) = c$.

Therefore, for each vertex $x_i$, the relative ordering of $f(x_i)$ with respect to $c$ agrees with that of $f'(x_i)$. Since this holds for all vertices of $\sigma$, it follows that $f^{-1}(c)$ intersects $\sigma$ if and only if $(f')^{-1}(c)$ intersects $\sigma$.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
{\emph{FeatureZ}} correctly preserves quantiles.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Quantile bounds are preserved by first computing the quantile values and then applying the same isocontour construction procedure using those quantile thresholds. Under this approach, preservation is defined pointwise: for a given quantile level, each point is constrained relative to the corresponding quantile value according to its position in the ground truth distribution—points above the quantile are bounded from below by the quantile value, and points below the quantile are bounded from above by it.

Since quantile preservation does not involve star correction, it is not necessary to establish the nondegeneracy of $C_{\Phi,f}$.

Let $|\mathbb{X}| = n$ and fix $m < n$. Let $s$ be a quantile value selected by {\emph{FeatureZ}} that separates the $m$ smallest points from the $n-m$ largest points, so that
\[
\bigl|f^{-1}(-\infty,s)\bigr| = m.
\]
We claim that
\[
f^{-1}(-\infty,s) = (f')^{-1}(-\infty,s).
\]

First, suppose that $x \in f^{-1}(-\infty,s)$. Then $f(x) < s$ and $U(x) \le s$, which implies that $f'(x) < s$. Hence $x \in (f')^{-1}(-\infty,s)$. 
Conversely, suppose that $x \notin f^{-1}(-\infty,s)$. Then $f(x) \ge s$ and $L(x) \ge s$, which implies that $f'(x) \ge s$. Hence $x \notin (f')^{-1}(-\infty,s)$.

Therefore,
\[
f^{-1}(-\infty,s) = (f')^{-1}(-\infty,s).
\]
It follows that the threshold $s$ separates the $m$ smallest points from the $n-m$ largest points for both $f$ and $f'$. Thus, quantiles are preserved.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
Let $(a,b)$ be an edge of $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$. Then the boundary of $\pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}$ is composed of contours with isovalues $f(a)$ and $f(b)$.
\label{corollary:contour-boundary}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->

Let $p$ be the path traveling from $x$ to $y$ along the line segment that connects them. Let $u$ and $v$ respectively be the points at which $p$ leaves $\pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(a,b)$ and enters $\pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(c,d)$. These points are illustrated in \cref{fig:adjacent-increasing}. Then

\[
f(x) \leq f(u) = f(b) \leq f(v) = f(c) < f(y) < f(d)
\]

\label{lemma:adjacent-increasing}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Since $f$ is PL, then $f$ will strictly increase along $p$ from $f(x)$ to $f(y)$. By assumption, $f(y) < f(d)$.

Notice that the path $p$ connects $x$ to $y$ without taking a function value above $f(d)$. Since $\pi(y)$ descends from $d$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$, then by \cref{lemma:combine-paths-descendant}, $\pi(x)$ also must descend from $d$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$ and $x$ lies in the same connected component of $f^{-1}(-\infty,f(d))$ as $y$ and $c$. Since $\pi(x)$ also descends from $b$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$, this implies that either $b$ descends from $d$ or $d$ descends from $b$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$. 

If $b$ descends from $d$, then because $a$ is the child of $b$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$, it must hold that $f(a) < f(b) < f(d)$. Likewise, since $c$ is the child of $d$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$, and $c$ also lies the same connected component of $f^{-1}(-\infty,f(d))$ as $b$, it would need to follow that $c$ lies between $b$ and $d$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$, or $b = c$, forcing $f(a) < f(b) \leq f(c) < f(d)$. Using similar reasoning, if it held that $d$ was instead a descendant of $a$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$, then it would instead hold that $f(c) < f(d) \leq f(a) < f(b)$.

By \cref{corollary:contour-boundary}, either $f(u) = f(a)$ or $f(u) = f(b)$, so $f(a) \leq f(u)$. We also know that $f(u) < f(y)$ because $f$ increases on $p$. And by assumption, $f(y) < f(d)$. Chaining these inequalities yields $f(a) \leq f(u) < f(y) < f(d)$, so $f(a) < f(d)$. This forces $f(a) < f(b) \leq f(c) < f(d)$.

By \cref{corollary:contour-boundary}, either $f(v) = (c)$ or $f(v) = f(d)$. Since $v$ comes before $y$ on $p$, it follows that $f(v) < f(y)$. Since $f(y) < f(d)$, this implies that $f(v) = f(c)$. Thus, $f(c) < f(y)$. And therefore, we have that $f(x) < f(b) \leq f(c) = f(v) < f(y)$.

We now show that $f(u) = f(b)$. We check two cases for $f(a)$. 

\underline{case 1: $f(a) < f(x)$:} In this case, no contour with isovalue $f(a)$ can intersect $p$, forcing $f(u) = f(b)$. 

\underline{Case 2: $f(a) > f(x)$:} since $f(x) < f(b) < f(y)$, there must be some point $w$ on $p$ such that $f(u) = f(a) < f(w) < f(b) < f(y)$. There is a clear path from $x$ to $w$ that does not take a function value above $f(b)$ (namely, along the segment connecting $x$ to $y$). From \cref{lemma:combine-paths-descendant}, this implies that $\pi_{\mathbb{M}(f)}(w)$ is a descendant of $b$ that lies in the same connected component of $f^{-1}(-\infty,f(b))$ as $x$ (and thus $a$). Since $a$ is a child of $b$, if $b$ was not the lowest ancestor of $\pi(w)$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$ that is also a node in $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$, then $\pi(w)$ would also be a descendant of $a$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$. However, this is impossible since $f(a) < f(w)$. Thus, $w \in \pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(a,b)$. Since $x \in \pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(a,b)$, $x$ and $w$ must be on the same side of $u$, meaning that $f(u) = f(b)$.

Combining inequalities gives the result.

Let $\sigma \in \mathbb{X}$ be a simplex and $s \in \mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$ be a saddle. \label{lemma:contours-are-preserved}

<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
We check cases based on the dimension of $\sigma$.

\underline{Case 1: $\sigma$ is a $0$-simplex:} In this case, $\sigma$ is a vertex of $\mathbb{X}$. Since $f$ is injective on the vertices of $\mathbb{X}$, this implies that $\sigma = s$. By assumption, $f'(s) = f(s)$.

Without loss of generality, suppose that $x \in \pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(a,b)$ and $y \in \pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(c,d)$. Let $p$, $u$, and $v$ be as in the statement of \cref{lemma:adjacent-increasing}. By \cref{lemma:adjacent-increasing}, $f(x) < f(u) = f(b) \leq f(v) = f(c) < f(y)$. Let $w$ be the point along $p$ that intersects the contour with isovalue $f(s)$. Since $x \in \pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(a,b)$, the path $p$ must intersect $u$ (the boundary of that region) prior to intersecting $w$ (which is itself a region boundary). Thus, $f(u) = f(b) \leq f(s)$. Similarly, $f(s) \leq f(c)$.

From our bounds, $f'(x) < b$. Since $f(y) > c$, it also holds that $f'(y) > c$. Thus, there exists some point $w'$ on the segment between $x$ and $y$ such that $f'(x) < b \leq f'(w') = f(s) \leq c < f'(y)$.

\underline{Case 3: $\sigma$ is a $2$-simplex or $3$-simplex:} Since $f$ is PL, the contour with isovalue $f(s)$ must intersect one of the $0$ or $1$ dimensional faces of $\sigma$. Appealing to cases 1 and 2, that contour must thus intersect the same face in $f'$, and thus it will also intersect $\sigma$.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
Suppose that $s$ is a merge saddle in $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$ and $x \in \mathbb{X}$ is a vertex where $\pi_{\mathbb{M}(f)}(x)$ descends from $s$ in $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$. Then there is a path $p'$ from $s$ to $x$ that never takes a function value (in $f'$) above $f'(s)$ (except for the endpoint $s$).
\label{lemma:sublevel-sets-are-preserved}
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Let $p$ be a path from $s$ to $x$ that never takes a function value (in $f$) above $f(s)$ except for at the endpoint $s$. We know that such a path exists becase $\pi_{\mathbb{M}(f)}(x)$ descends from $s$. Let $\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \ldots, \sigma_k$ be the (not necessearily distinct) simplices that $p$ passes through, ordered in terms of when $p$ intersects them (so $\sigma_1 = p$ and $\sigma_k = x$). Order the $\sigma_i$ so that $\sigma_i$ is either a face or co-face of $\sigma_{i+1}$. For each $\sigma_i$, let $y_i$ be the vertex of $\sigma_i$ with the lowest function value (in $f$). Then it must follow that $f(y_i) < f(s)$ for all $i$, except for $y_1$ where $f(y_1) = f(s)$. Since each $\sigma_i$ is a face or co-face of $\sigma_{i+1}$, the $y_i$ are all adjacent vertices of $\mathbb{X}$.

Let $p'$ be the path formed by connecting the $y_i$ in order by line segments. Since each $y_i$ is adjacent to $y_{i+1}$, the segment of $p$ between $y_i$ and $y_{i+1}$ will take values of $f$ that interpolate linearly between $f(y_i)$ and $f(y_{i+1})$. Since each $f(y_i) \leq f(s)$, this implies that the entire  path $p$ takes a function value (in $f$) below $f(s)$ except for the endpoint $s$. Thus, each $\pi_{\mathbb{M}(f)}(y_i)$ is a descendant of $s$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$. Therefore, each $y_i$ belongs to some edge $(a_i,b_i)$ of $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$ with $f(b_i) \leq f(s)$. 

From the error bounds, for each $y_i$ except $y_1$, we either have $f'(y_i) < f(b_i) \leq f(s)$ or $f'(y_i) \leq f(y_i) < f(s)$. In either case, $f'(y_i) < f(s)$. And also, $f'(y_1) = f(y_1) = f(s)$. Thus, the path $p'$ will also never take a function value (in $f'$) above $f'(s) = f(s)$, except for at the intial point $y_1$ where $f(y_1) = f'(s) = f(s)$.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
Let $x, y \in \mathbb{X}$ be two points. Let $s$ be the least common ancestor of $\pi(x)$ and $\pi(y)$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$. Suppose that $s$ is also a node in $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$. Suppose that $x$ and $y$ lie in different connected components of the sub-level set $f^{-1}(-\infty,f(s))$. Then $x$ and $y$ lie in different connected components of the sub-level set $(f')^{-1}(-\infty,f'(s))$.
\label{lemma:sublevel-set-complements-are-preserved}
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Let $S_1$ and $S_2$ be the two connected components of $f^{-1}(-\infty,f(s)$ that contain $x$ and $y$. Let $E_1$ be the set of edges that descend from $s$ in $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$ whose lower endpoints lie in $S_1$. Clearly, if $e \in E_1$ and $z \in \pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(e)$, then $z \in S_1$. It is also easy to show that any point in $S_1$ will belong to at least one edge in $E_1$. Thus, $S_1 = \bigcup_{e \in E_1} \pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(e)$.

Let $p$ be a path from $x$ to $y$. The path $p$ must leave $S_1$ at some point. It will cross the boundary of $S_1$ at some point $q$ in some simplex $\sigma$. Clearly, $f(q) \geq f(s)$. Since $S_1 = \bigcup_{e \in E_1} \pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(e)$, every point in the boundary of $S_1$ is part of the boundary of some $\pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(e)$ for some $e \in E_1$. Thus, there exists some $e \in E_1$ where $q$ lies on the boundary of $\pi^{-1}_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(e)$. By \cref{lemma:contours-are-preserved}, the path $p$ must cross a point $q'$ with $f(q') > f(s) = f'(s)$. Since this holds for all paths between $x$ and $y$, it  follows that $x$ and $y$ lie in different connected commponents of $(f')^{-1}(-\infty,f'(s))$.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
Let $s$ be a split saddle in $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$ that pairs with minimum $m$. Then $s$ is a vertex of $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f')$ that pairs with minimum $m$.
\label{lemma:saddles-preserved-persistence}
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Let $x$ and $y$ be nodes of $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$ that descend from $s$ yet are in different connected components of $f^{-1}(-\infty,f(s))$. By \cref{lemma:sublevel-set-complements-are-preserved}, these two points are in different connected components of the sub-level set $(f')^{-1}(-\infty,f(s))$. However, by \cref{lemma:sublevel-sets-are-preserved}, they lie in the same connected component of $(f')^{-1}(-\infty,f(s)]$. This implies that a merge event happens at $s$ in $f'$, so $s$ is a vertex of $\mathbb{M}(f')$.

We now show that $s$ pairs with minimum $m$ in $f'$. Let $S'_1$ and $S'_2$ be the two connected components of $(f')^{-1}(-\infty,f(s))$ incident to $s$. Let $S_1$ and $S_2$ be the corresponding sets for $f$. We show that the lowest point in each $S'_i$ is the same as in the $S_i$. Because $f$ and $f'$ are piecewise-linear, all the minima in their merge trees are vertices of $\mathbb{X}$, and so the lowest points in each set will be vertices of $\mathbb{X}$. Let $m_1$ be the lowest point in $S_1$. Then $m_1$ is a vertex of $\mathbb{X}$. From \cref{lemma:sublevel-sets-are-preserved}, a vertex $z$ of $\mathbb{X}$ satisfies $z \in S'_1$ if and only if $z \in S_1$. Thus, $m_1 \in S'_1$. Let $z$ be a vertex of $S_1$ with $z \not = m_1$. Then $z$ belongs to some edge $(a,b)$ of $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$ that is a descendant of $s$. Notice that $f(a) \geq f(m_1)$ and $f(z) > f(m_1)$. If $f(z) > f(a)$, then from the error bounds, $f'(z) > f(a) \geq f(m_1)$. If $f(z) \leq f(a)$, then from the bounds $f'(z) \geq f(z) > f(m_1)$. Thus, each vertex in $S_1$ other than $m_1$ will have a value in $f'$ greater than $f(m_1) = f'(m_1)$. So $m_1$ is the lowest point in $S_1'$. Similarly, $m_2$ will be the lowest point in $S_2'$.

Between the two lowest points in $S_1$ and $S_2$, $s$ will pair (in $f$) with whichever has a higher function value. Similarly, between the two lowest points in $S'_1$ and $S'_2$, $s$ will pair (in $f'$) with whichever has a higher function value. Since the lowest points in $S_1$ and $S_2$ are the same as in $S'_1$ and $S'_2$ (namely, $m_1$ and $m_2$). Since $m_1$ and $m_2$ are minima in $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$, by construction $f'(m_1) = f(m_1)$ and $f'(m_2) = f(m_2)$. Thus, $s$ will pair with the same point in $f$ and $f'$. Call it $m$

Since $f'(s) = f(s)$ and $f'(m) = f(m)$, the pair $(s,m)$ will have the same persistence in both $f$ and $f'$, namely, $f(s) - f(m)$. And therefore, the pair $(s,m)$ will have a persistence above $\varepsilon$ in $f'$ and both will be vertices of $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f')$.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
Let $m_1, m_2$ be local minima of $f$ with $f(m_1) > f(m_2)$. Suppose that $m_1$ has persistence $\varepsilon$. Let $p:[0,1] \rightarrow \mathbb{X}$ be a path from $m_1$ to $m_2$. Then
\[
\max_{t \in [0,1]} (f \circ p)(t) - f(m_1) \geq \varepsilon.
\]
\label{lemma:persistence-bound}
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
Let $s$ be the least common ancestor of $m_1$ and $m_2$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$, as illustrated in \cref{fig:persistence-bound}. For any threshold $k \leq f(s)$, nodes $m_1$ and $m_2$ lie in distinct connected components of $f^{-1}(-\infty,k)$. Therefore, any path $p$ from $m_1$ to $m_2$ must pass through some point $q$ with $f(q) \geq f(s)$. Hence,
\[
\max_{t \in [0,1]} (f \circ p)(t) \geq f(q) \geq f(s).
\]

Let $S$ be the connected component of $f^{-1}(-\infty,f(s)]$ that contains $s$. 
                                                                                                                
Since $f(m_1) > f(m_2)$, $m_1$ is not the global minimum of $S$. Thus, $m_1$ is paired with some saddle $s_1 \in S$ satisfying $f(s_1) \leq f(s)$.

Combining these inequalities, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\max_{t \in [0,1]} (f \circ p)(t) - f(m_1)
&\geq f(s) - f(m_1) \\
&\geq f(s_1) - f(m_1) \\
&= \varepsilon.
\end{align*}

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
Suppose that $m$ is a minimum of $f'$ and is not a node of $\mathcal{T}_\varepsilon(f)$. Then $m$ has persistence at most $\varepsilon + 2\xi$ in $f'$.
\label{lemma:false-positive-persistence-bound}
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Let $m_1$ be the lowest descendant of $\pi(m)$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$, and let $m_2$ be the lowest descendant of $m$ in $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$. Then
\[
f(m) \geq f(m_1) \geq f(m_2).
\]
Let $s$ be the least common ancestor of $m_1$ and $m_2$ in $\mathbb{M}(f)$ (see \cref{fig:false-positive-persistence-bounds}).

Let $p_1 : [0,1] \rightarrow \mathbb{X}$ be a path from $m_1$ to $m_2$ passing through $s$ that never exceeds the value $f(s)$. Such a path exists because $m_1$ and $m_2$ lie in the same connected component of $f^{-1}(-\infty,f(s)]$. Similarly, let $p_2 : [0,1] \rightarrow \mathbb{X}$ be a path from $m$ to $m_1$ that never exceeds $f(m)$. Concatenating $p_2$ with $p_1$ yields a path $p$ from $m$ to $m_2$, whose maximum function value is $\max\{f(m), f(s)\}$.

Let $s'$ be the parent of $\pi_{\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)}(m)$ in $\mathbb{M}_\varepsilon(f)$. Then $m$ and $m_2$ lie in the same connected component of $f^{-1}(-\infty,f(s')]$, and $m_2$ is the minimum of this component. In $\mathcal{T}_\varepsilon(f)$, $m_2$ lies on some edge $(a,b)$ with $f(a) \geq f(m_2)$. By the $\varepsilon$-boundedness of $f'$, it follows that either $f'(m) > f(a) \geq f(m_2)$ or $f'(m) \geq f(m) > f(m_2)$. Since $f'(m_2) = f(m_2)$, we conclude that $f'(m) > f'(m_2)$.

Moreover, by \cref{lemma:saddles-preserved-persistence}, $m_2$ is a minimum of $f'$. We now consider two cases.

\underline{Case 1: $m_1 \neq m_2$.} In this case, $p$ attains a maximum value of $f(s)$ in $f$, and hence at most $f(s) + \xi$ in $f'$. Also, $f'(m) \geq f(m) - \xi$. Since $p$ connects the minimum $m$ to the lower minimum $m_2$, \cref{lemma:persistence-bound} implies that the persistence of $m$ in $f'$ is at most
\[
f(s) + \xi - (f(m) - \xi) = f(s) - f(m) + 2\xi.
\]

\underline{Case 2: $m_1 = m_2$.} In this case, $s = m_2$, and the path $p$ decreases monotonically from $m$ to $m_2$, never exceeding $f(m)$. Thus, in $f'$, the maximum value along $p$ is at most $f(m) + \xi$, and $f'(m) \geq f(m) - \xi$. Applying \cref{lemma:persistence-bound}, the persistence of $m$ in $f'$ is at most
\[
(f(m) + \xi) - (f(m) - \xi) = 2\xi.
\]

Finally, since $s$ is removed during persistence simplification, the persistence of the pair $(m_1,s)$ is at most $\varepsilon$, i.e., $f(s) - f(m_1) \leq \varepsilon$. Combining the above bounds yields that the persistence of $m$ in $f'$ is at most
\[
f(s) - f(m) + 2\xi \leq f(s) - f(m_1) + 2\xi \leq \varepsilon + 2\xi.
\]

This completes the proof.

Since preservation of the simplified contour tree does not involve star correction, it is not necessary to establish nondegeneracy of $C_{\Phi,f}$.

We construct $f'$ to be $(\varepsilon - 2\xi)$-bounded.
