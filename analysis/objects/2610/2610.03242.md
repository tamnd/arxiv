<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:prelim-system-node}
A system node on $(X,U,Y)$ is an operator
\[
\mathcal S
=
\begin{bmatrix}
A\&B\\
C\&D
\end{bmatrix}
:
\operatorname{dom}(A\&B)\subset X\times U
\rightarrow
X\times Y
\]
with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $A\&B$ is closed;
\item $C\&D$ is bounded on $\operatorname{dom}(A\&B)$ equipped with the graph norm of
      $A\&B$;
\item for all $u\in U$, there exists $x\in X$ such that
      $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\u\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{dom}(A\&B)$;
\item the main operator
\[
Ax=A\&B\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\0\end{smallmatrix}\right),
\qquad
\operatorname{dom}(A)
=
\left\{\,x\in X\;\middle|\;\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{dom}(A\&B)\,\right\},
\]
generates a strongly continuous semigroup
$\mathcal T=(\mathcal T(t))_{t\geq0}$ on $X$.
\end{enumerate}

\label{def:prelim-finite-cost-detectability}
The system node is called \emph{finite-cost detectable} if every
infinite-time generalized trajectory satisfying
$u\in L^2(0,\infty;U)$ and
$y\in L^2(0,\infty;Y)$
also satisfies $x(t)\to0$ as $t\to\infty$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The fixed parameter $\alpha>0$ introduced above is a normalization
parameter for the Newton factor and should not be interpreted as a step
size or regularization parameter. For a fixed current feedback, the associated cost operator
$P_{k+1}$ is independent of $\alpha$; the parameter enters only when
selecting the next Newton factor from the non-unique linearized Lur'e
factorization. Different choices of $\alpha$ may therefore lead to
different intermediate feedbacks and cost-operator iterates, while the limiting
optimal value operator is independent of the fixed normalization parameter
under the assumptions of the convergence theorem. In finite dimensions, for a fixed current feedback, the
resolvent-normalized update converges to the classical
zero-state-normalized Newton--Kleinman update as $\alpha\to\infty$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:nk-decrement}
Let $k\geq1$ and let $\Sigma_k$ be the system node associated with the
stabilizing feedback obtained after $k$ Newton steps.  Denote by
\[
\Theta_k:\operatorname{dom}(A_k)\to U
\]
the zero-input observation operator of the correction output
$\vartheta_k=v_{k-1}-v_k$.  Then
\begin{equation}
P_k-P_{k+1}
=
\mathcal C_{\Theta,k,\infty}^*
\mathcal C_{\Theta,k,\infty}
\geq0.
\label{eq:nk-decrement}
\end{equation}
In particular, $P_{k+1}\leq P_k$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Apply \eqref{eq:nk-lure-residual-factorization} from Newton step $k-1$ and
set the new auxiliary input $v_k$ equal to zero. Along classical zero-input
trajectories of $\Sigma_k$ this gives
\[
\tfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\langle P_kx(t),x(t)\rangle
+\|z_k(t)\|^2+\|\vartheta_k(t)\|^2=0.
\]
Integration over $[0,T]$ and density give the corresponding identity for
every initial state. Strong stability of $\mathcal T_k$ allows
$T\to\infty$, so that
\[
P_k
=
\mathcal C_{k,\infty}^*\mathcal C_{k,\infty}
+
\mathcal C_{\Theta,k,\infty}^*\mathcal C_{\Theta,k,\infty}.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:nk-feedback-value} proves \eqref{eq:nk-decrement}.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:nk-port-polarization}
Let $k\geq0$ and suppose that $\Sigma_k$ is stabilizing.  Let
$\Delta_{k+1}$ and $E_{k+1}$ be defined by
\eqref{eq:nk-factor-data}.  Then
\[
\Delta_{k+1}\in\mathcal L(\operatorname{dom}(A_k),U),
\qquad
E_{k+1}=E_{k+1}^*\in\mathcal L(U),
\]
where $\operatorname{dom}(A_k)$ carries its graph norm.  The operator
$\Gamma_{k+1}\&\Lambda_{k+1}$ defined by
\eqref{eq:nk-factor} belongs to
\[
\mathcal L(\operatorname{dom}(A_k\&B_k),U)
\]
and satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak b_k\!\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\w\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)
={}&
\left\langle
(\Gamma_{k+1}\&\Lambda_{k+1})\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right),w
\right\rangle
\\
&+
\left\langle
v,(\Gamma_{k+1}\&\Lambda_{k+1})\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\w\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\right\rangle
-\langle v,w\rangle
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nk-polarized-newton}
\end{equation}
for all
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\w\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{dom}(A_k\&B_k)$.
It is the unique factor in
$\mathcal L(\operatorname{dom}(A_k\&B_k),U)$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:nk-polarized-newton} for which
\[
(\Gamma_{k+1}\&\Lambda_{k+1})\iota_k:U\to U
\]
is self-adjoint.  In particular,
\eqref{eq:nk-linearized-lure} holds and
\begin{equation}
(\Gamma_{k+1}\&\Lambda_{k+1})\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
=
\Delta_{k+1}x,
\qquad x\in\operatorname{dom}(A_k).
\label{eq:nk-factor-zero-input}
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For $x\in\operatorname{dom}(A)$, the map
$w\mapsto\mathfrak b_k(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\iota_kw)$ is a bounded functional on
$U$, and hence defines $\Delta_{k+1}x$ by the Riesz representation theorem.
The boundedness of $\iota_k$ gives
$\Delta_{k+1}\in\mathcal L(\operatorname{dom}(A),U)$, where $\operatorname{dom}(A)$ carries its graph
norm.  Likewise, the restriction
$(v,w)\mapsto\mathfrak b_k(\iota_kv,\iota_kw)$ is a bounded Hermitian form
on $U$ and therefore defines a bounded self-adjoint operator $E_{k+1}$.

Write $\iota_kv=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x_v\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.  Every
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{dom}(A\&B)$ decomposes as
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x-x_v\\0\end{smallmatrix}\right)+\iota_kv$.  Applying this decomposition to
both arguments of $\mathfrak b_k$ and using
\eqref{eq:nk-form-kernel-zero} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak b_k\!\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\w\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)
={}&
\langle\Delta_{k+1}(x-x_v),w\rangle
+\langle v,\Delta_{k+1}(\xi-x_w)\rangle
+\langle E_{k+1}v,w\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\]
This is exactly \eqref{eq:nk-polarized-newton} with the factor
\eqref{eq:nk-factor}. On the diagonal it gives
\eqref{eq:nk-linearized-lure}, and $v=0$ gives
\eqref{eq:nk-factor-zero-input}.

If two factors satisfy \eqref{eq:nk-polarized-newton}, their difference
vanishes on all $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $x\in\operatorname{dom}(A)$ and hence has the form $Kv$ with
$K\in\mathcal L(U)$.  The polarized identity implies $K^*=-K$, whereas the
normalization that $(\Gamma_{k+1}\&\Lambda_{k+1})\iota_k$ be self-adjoint forces $K=0$.

\label{lem:nk-delta-admissible}
Let $\Delta_{k+1}$ be the zero-input part of the Newton factor constructed
in \eqref{eq:nk-factor-data}.  Then
$\Delta_{k+1}:\operatorname{dom}(A_k)\to U$
is infinite-time admissible for $\mathcal T_k$.  If
$\mathcal B_{k,T}$, $\mathcal C_{k,T}$, and $\mathcal D_{k,T}$ denote the
finite-time maps of $\Sigma_k$, then the finite-time
observation maps of $\Delta_{k+1}$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_{\Delta,k+1,T}
=
\mathcal B_{k,T}^*P_{k+1}\mathcal T_k(T)
+\mathcal D_{k,T}^*\mathcal C_{k,T},
\qquad T>0.
\label{eq:nk-delta-green-map}
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $x\in\operatorname{dom}(A)$ and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathcal E$. Since
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)-\iota_kv$ has zero input component,
\eqref{eq:nk-form-kernel-zero} gives
\[
\langle\Delta_{k+1}x,v\rangle
=
\mathfrak b_k\!\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right).
\]
Hence, by \eqref{eq:nk-polarized-form},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\langle\Delta_{k+1}x,v\rangle
={}&
\langle PAx,\xi\rangle
+\left\langle Px, A\&B\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\rangle
+\left\langle Zx,Z\&W\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nk-adjoint-correction}
\end{equation}
Let $h\in C_c^\infty((0,T);U)$ and let $(\xi,h,\zeta)$ be the zero-state
classical trajectory of $\Sigma_k$.  Its existence follows from
\cite[Thm.~4.3.9]{Staffans2005}.  Applying
\eqref{eq:nk-adjoint-correction} to $\mathcal T_k(t)x$ and
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi(t)\\h(t)\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and integrating over $[0,T]$, yields
\[
\int_0^T
\langle\Delta_{k+1}\mathcal T_k(t)x,h(t)\rangle\,dt
=
\left\langle
\bigl(\mathcal B_{k,T}^*P\mathcal T_k(T)
+\mathcal D_{k,T}^*\mathcal C_{k,T}\bigr)x,h
\right\rangle_{L^2}.
\]
Density gives \eqref{eq:nk-delta-green-map}.  Since $\Sigma_k$ is
infinite-time well posed and $\mathcal T_k$ is bounded, the right-hand side
of
\[
\|\mathcal C_{\Delta,k+1,T}\|
\leq
\|\mathcal B_{k,T}\|\,\|P\|\,\|\mathcal T_k(T)\|
+\|\mathcal D_{k,T}\|\,\|\mathcal C_{k,T}\|
\]
is bounded uniformly in $T$.  Thus $\Delta_{k+1}$ is infinite-time
admissible.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:nk-newton-symbol}
Let $k\geq0$, suppose that $\Sigma_k$ is stabilizing, and let
$\Gamma_{k+1}\&\Lambda_{k+1}$ be the normalized Newton factor from
Lemma~\ref{lem:nk-port-polarization}.  Then, for all
$\lambda\in\mathbb C_+$ and $v\in U$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
2\operatorname{Re}\langle \mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)v,v\rangle
={}&
\|v\|^2
+
2\operatorname{Re}\lambda\,
\left\langle
P_{k+1}(\lambda-A_{k,-1})^{-1}B_kv,
(\lambda-A_{k,-1})^{-1}B_kv
\right\rangle
\\
&+
\left\|
(Z_k\&W_k)
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}(\lambda-A_{k,-1})^{-1}B_kv\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\right\|^2 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nk-frequency-green}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Re}\langle \mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)v,v\rangle
\geq\tfrac12\|v\|^2,
\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb C_+,
\label{eq:nk-strict-accretivity}
\end{equation}
and $\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)$ is invertible with
\begin{equation}
\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb C_+}
\|\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)^{-1}\|
\leq2.
\label{eq:nk-Q-Hinf}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
\lambda\mapsto\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)^{-1}
\]
belongs to $H^\infty(\mathbb C_+;\mathcal L(U))$, and
\begin{equation}
\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)-\mathbf N_{k+1}(\mu)
=
\Delta_{k+1}
\bigl(
(\lambda-A_{k,-1})^{-1}
-
(\mu-A_{k,-1})^{-1}
\bigr)B_k.
\label{eq:nk-port-resolvent-identity}
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The element
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}(\lambda-A_{-1})^{-1}Bv\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ satisfies
\[
A\&B
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}(\lambda-A_{-1})^{-1}Bv\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)
=
\lambda(\lambda-A_{-1})^{-1}Bv.
\]
Substitution into \eqref{eq:nk-linearized-lure} gives
\eqref{eq:nk-frequency-green}.  Since $P\geq0$,
\eqref{eq:nk-strict-accretivity} follows. By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
\eqref{eq:nk-strict-accretivity} implies
\[
\|\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)v\|
\geq \tfrac12\|v\|,
\qquad v\in U.
\]
Hence $\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)$ is injective and has closed range.
The same accretivity estimate holds for
$\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)^*$, so that
$\ker \mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)^*=\{0\}$. Consequently,
$\operatorname{ran}\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)$ is dense in $U$.
It is therefore equal to $U$, and
\[
\|\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)^{-1}\|\leq2.
\]
This proves invertibility and \eqref{eq:nk-Q-Hinf}.  The map
$\lambda\mapsto\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb C_+$
by the system-node resolvent representation. Since it is invertible
throughout $\mathbb C_+$, the holomorphic inverse theorem shows that
$\lambda\mapsto\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)^{-1}$ is holomorphic.
Together with \eqref{eq:nk-Q-Hinf}, this gives
$\mathbf N_{k+1}^{-1}\in H^\infty(\mathbb C_+;\mathcal L(U))$.
Finally, subtracting the two resolvent lifts in the definition of
$\mathbf N_{k+1}$ and using \eqref{eq:nk-factor-zero-input} gives
\eqref{eq:nk-port-resolvent-identity}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:nk-flow-invertible}
Let $k\geq0$ and suppose that $\Sigma_k$ is stabilizing.  Let
$\Gamma_{k+1}\&\Lambda_{k+1}$ be the normalized Newton factor from
Lemma~\ref{lem:nk-port-polarization}.  Then the port relation
\[
v_{k+1}
=
(\Gamma_{k+1}\&\Lambda_{k+1})
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\v_k\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\]
is flow invertible.  Its flow inverse is an infinite-time well-posed system
node with external input $v_{k+1}$, and its zero-input semigroup
$\mathcal T_{k+1}$ is strongly stable. Hence setting $v_{k+1}=0$ defines
the next stabilizing feedback, with associated system node $\Sigma_{k+1}$.

Its generator $A_{k+1}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(\lambda-A_{k+1})^{-1}
={}&
(\lambda-A_k)^{-1}
\\
&-
(\lambda-A_{k,-1})^{-1}B_k
\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)^{-1}
\Delta_{k+1}(\lambda-A_k)^{-1},
\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb C_+.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nk-closed-resolvent}
\end{equation}
Moreover, the zero-input observation operator
$\Theta_{k+1}:\operatorname{dom}(A_{k+1})\to U$
of the correction output
$\vartheta_{k+1}=v_k-v_{k+1}$ is infinite-time admissible for
$\mathcal T_{k+1}$.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The proof proceeds in three steps: we first construct the candidate
closed-loop semigroup, then prove its strong stability, and finally identify
it with the flow inverse of the Newton port.

Let $\mathfrak Q_{k+1}$ be the bounded causal time-invariant operator
on $L^2(0,\infty;U)$ whose symbol is $\mathbf N_{k+1}^{-1}$; its existence and
$\|\mathfrak Q_{k+1}\|\leq2$ follow from the multiplier theorem recalled in
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} and Theorem~\ref{thm:nk-newton-symbol}.

For $\lambda\in\mathbb C_+$ denote the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:nk-closed-resolvent} by $M_\lambda$.  The usual resolvent identity,
combined with \eqref{eq:nk-port-resolvent-identity} and the identity for
inverses, gives
\begin{equation}
M_\lambda-M_\mu
=(\mu-\lambda)M_\lambda M_\mu,
\qquad \lambda,\mu\in\mathbb C_+.
\label{eq:nk-pseudo-resolvent}
\end{equation}
For $x\in X$, put
\[
r_x:=
-\mathfrak Q_{k+1}\mathcal C_{\Delta,k+1,\infty}x
\in L^2(0,\infty;U)
\]
and define
\[
\mathcal T_{k+1}(t)x
:=
\mathcal T_k(t)x
+
\mathcal B_{k,t}(r_x|_{[0,t]}).
\]
Since $\Sigma_k$ is infinite-time well posed,
$\mathfrak Q_{k+1}$ is bounded, and
$\Delta_{k+1}$ is infinite-time admissible, this family is uniformly
bounded in $t\geq0$. Moreover,
$t\mapsto\mathcal B_{k,t}(r_x|_{[0,t]})$ is the zero-state trajectory
of $\Sigma_k$ driven by $r_x$ and is therefore continuous in $X$.
Hence $\mathcal T_{k+1}$ is a strongly continuous uniformly bounded
family with $\mathcal T_{k+1}(0)=I$. The Laplace
formulas for admissible control and observation operators
\cite[Prop.~2.3.1 and Secs.~4.3--4.4]{TucsnakWeiss2009} give
\begin{equation}
\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}\mathcal T_{k+1}(t)x\,dt
=M_\lambda x,
\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb C_+.
\label{eq:nk-laplace-M}
\end{equation}
For $\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb C_+$, $\lambda\neq\mu$, the double Laplace
transform of $(t,s)\mapsto\mathcal T_{k+1}(t+s)x$ is
\[
\frac{M_\lambda-M_\mu}{\mu-\lambda}x
=
M_\lambda M_\mu x
\]
by \eqref{eq:nk-pseudo-resolvent}. On the other hand, using
\eqref{eq:nk-laplace-M} first in the $s$-variable and then in the
$t$-variable gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
e^{-\lambda t-\mu s}
\mathcal T_{k+1}(t)\mathcal T_{k+1}(s)x\,ds\,dt
\\
&\qquad=
\int_0^\infty
e^{-\lambda t}\mathcal T_{k+1}(t)
\left(
\int_0^\infty e^{-\mu s}\mathcal T_{k+1}(s)x\,ds
\right)\,dt
\\
&\qquad=
\int_0^\infty
e^{-\lambda t}\mathcal T_{k+1}(t)M_\mu x\,dt
=
M_\lambda M_\mu x.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the two functions
\[
(t,s)\mapsto\mathcal T_{k+1}(t+s)x
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(t,s)\mapsto\mathcal T_{k+1}(t)\mathcal T_{k+1}(s)x
\]
have the same double Laplace transform. After pairing their difference
with an arbitrary element of $X$, scalar uniqueness of the double
Laplace transform gives equality almost everywhere in $(t,s)$.
Since both functions are strongly continuous, the equality holds for
all $t,s\geq0$. Hence
\[
\mathcal T_{k+1}(t+s)
=
\mathcal T_{k+1}(t)\mathcal T_{k+1}(s),
\qquad t,s\geq0.
\]
Hence
$\mathcal T_{k+1}$ is a bounded $C_0$-semigroup, and
\eqref{eq:nk-laplace-M} yields \eqref{eq:nk-closed-resolvent} for its
generator $A_{k+1}$.

The new semigroup is strongly stable. Indeed, by the definition above,
$\mathcal T_{k+1}(t)x=\mathcal T_k(t)x+
\mathcal B_{k,t}(r_x|_{[0,t]})$.  For $t>S$, the concatenation identity gives
\[
\mathcal B_{k,t}(r_x|_{[0,t]})
=
\mathcal T_k(t-S)\mathcal B_{k,S}(r_x|_{[0,S]})
+
\mathcal B_{k,t-S}(r_x(S+\cdot)|_{[0,t-S]}).
\]
The first term tends to zero for fixed $S$ by strong stability of
$\mathcal T_k$, whereas the second is bounded by
$\sup_T\|\mathcal B_{k,T}\|\,\|r_x\|_{L^2(S,\infty;U)}$.  Letting first
$t\to\infty$ and then $S\to\infty$ proves
$\mathcal T_{k+1}(t)x\to0$.

It remains to identify this semigroup with the feedback relation generated by the Newton step.  Since
$\Gamma_{k+1}\&\Lambda_{k+1}\in\mathcal L(\operatorname{dom}(A\&B),U)$, appending it as an artificial
output to the current state equation gives a system node.  At the normalization point $\alpha$, the resolvent map in Staffans'
flow-inversion criterion is solved, for $f\in X$ and $g\in U$, by
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_k&=-\mathbf N_{k+1}(\alpha)^{-1}
\bigl(g+\Delta_{k+1}(\alpha-A)^{-1}f\bigr),\\
x&=(\alpha-A)^{-1}f
+(\alpha-A_{-1})^{-1}Bv_k.
\end{aligned}
\]
It is therefore bijective, and the upper-left block of its inverse is
$M_{11}=(\alpha-A_{k+1})^{-1}$.
Thus $M_{11}$ is injective with dense range and
$-M_{11}^{-1}=A_{k+1}-\alpha I$
generates a $C_0$-semigroup.  Staffans' flow-inversion criterion
\cite[Thm.~6.6.11]{Staffans2005} now shows that the auxiliary node is flow
invertible and that its flow inverse is a system node.

We next identify its finite-time maps.  First consider classical
trajectories of the flow inverse with compatible smooth data.  By the
trajectory-interchange theorem
\cite[Thm.~6.6.15]{Staffans2005}, interchanging $v_k$ and $v_{k+1}$
produces a classical trajectory of the auxiliary node.  The Laplace-domain
representation of system-node trajectories therefore gives
\[
\widehat v_{k+1}(\lambda)
=
\Delta_{k+1}(\lambda-A)^{-1}x_0
+
\mathbf N_{k+1}(\lambda)\widehat v_k(\lambda).
\]
Since $\mathbf N_{k+1}^{-1}$ is the symbol of
$\mathfrak Q_{k+1}$, causality implies on every finite interval
\[
v_k
=
\mathfrak Q_{k+1,T}
\bigl(v_{k+1}-\mathcal C_{\Delta,k+1,T}x_0\bigr).
\]
Substitution into the finite-time maps of $\Sigma_k$ gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}
x(T)\\ z\\ v_k
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathcal T_k(T)-\mathcal B_{k,T}\mathfrak Q_{k+1,T}
\mathcal C_{\Delta,k+1,T}
&
\mathcal B_{k,T}\mathfrak Q_{k+1,T}
\\
\mathcal C_{k,T}-\mathcal D_{k,T}\mathfrak Q_{k+1,T}
\mathcal C_{\Delta,k+1,T}
&
\mathcal D_{k,T}\mathfrak Q_{k+1,T}
\\
-\mathfrak Q_{k+1,T}\mathcal C_{\Delta,k+1,T}
&
\mathfrak Q_{k+1,T}
\end{bmatrix}
\left(\begin{matrix}x_0\\v_{k+1}\end{matrix}\right).
\label{eq:nk-inverse-state-maps}
\end{equation}

Compatible classical data are dense in the generalized-trajectory topology;
see \cite[Secs.~4.6--4.7]{Staffans2005}.  Since every block in
\eqref{eq:nk-inverse-state-maps} is bounded, the identity extends by density
to arbitrary initial states in $X$ and inputs in $L^2(0,T;U)$.  Thus these
are precisely the finite-time maps of the flow inverse.

Moreover, their norms are bounded uniformly in $T$, because $\Sigma_k$ is
infinite-time well posed, $\Delta_{k+1}$ is infinite-time admissible, and
\[
\|\mathfrak Q_{k+1,T}\|
\leq
\|\mathfrak Q_{k+1}\|
\leq2.
\]
Hence the flow inverse is infinite-time well posed.  For zero new input,
\[
v_k
=
-\mathfrak Q_{k+1}
\mathcal C_{\Delta,k+1,\infty}x_0.
\]
Since $\vartheta_{k+1}=v_k-v_{k+1}$, this proves infinite-time
admissibility of the zero-input correction observation
$\Theta_{k+1}$. Together with the strong stability proved above, this shows

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:nk-monotone-convergence}
Let the resolvent-normalized iteration be initialized by a stabilizing
feedback. Then
\begin{equation}
P_\star\leq P_{k+1}\leq P_k\leq P_1,
\qquad k\geq1.
\label{eq:nk-order}
\end{equation}
There exists a bounded nonnegative self-adjoint operator
$P_\infty\in\mathcal L(X)$ such that
\begin{equation}
P_kx\rightarrow P_\infty x,
\qquad x\in X.
\label{eq:nk-strong-limit}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For $k\geq1$, $P_k$ is the cost operator of the zero-input trajectory of
$\Sigma_{k-1}$.  This trajectory is admissible for the original optimal
control problem, and hence $P_\star\leq P_k$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:nk-decrement}
gives $P_k-P_{k+1}\geq0$, proving \eqref{eq:nk-order}.

For each $x\in X$, the sequence $\langle P_kx,x\rangle$ is decreasing and
bounded from below. Define
\[
q_\infty(x)
=
\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle P_kx,x\rangle.
\]
The quadratic-form identities satisfied by
$\langle P_k\,\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ pass to the pointwise limit, and
\[
0\leq q_\infty(x)\leq\langle P_1x,x\rangle
\leq\|P_1\|\,\|x\|^2.
\]
Hence polarization yields a bounded nonnegative self-adjoint operator
$P_\infty\in\mathcal L(X)$ satisfying
$q_\infty(x)=\langle P_\infty x,x\rangle$ and
$P_\star\leq P_\infty\leq P_k$. Put $D_k=P_k-P_\infty$. Then $D_k\geq0$
and
\[
\|D_kx\|^2
\leq
\|P_1-P_\infty\|\,\langle D_kx,x\rangle
\rightarrow0,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:nk-strong-limit}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:nk-cayley-ports}
Let $\alpha>0$ be the fixed normalization parameter of the
resolvent-normalized iteration, with $\alpha\in\rho(A)$, where
$A$ is the main operator of the original system node.  Then the map
\[
(\xi,u)
\mapsto
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\u\end{smallmatrix}\right),
\qquad
x=
\sqrt{2\alpha}\,(\alpha-A)^{-1}\xi
+
(\alpha-A_{-1})^{-1}Bu,
\]
is a bounded isomorphism from $X\times U$ onto
$\operatorname{dom}(A\&B)$ equipped with its graph norm, with inverse given by
\eqref{eq:nk-cayley-coordinates}.

For every $k\geq0$, the auxiliary input $v_k$, pulled back to the
original system-node domain, is bounded with respect to the graph norm.  Consequently there exist
unique operators
$F_k\in\mathcal L(X,U)$,
$H_k\in\mathcal L(U)$,
such that
\begin{equation}
v_k=F_k\xi+H_ku.
\label{eq:nk-cayley-port}
\end{equation}
If $H_k$ is invertible, then the resolvent lift
\[
\iota_kv
=
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}(\alpha-A_{k,-1})^{-1}B_kv\\v\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\]
corresponds to the fixed Cayley coordinates
$(0,H_k^{-1}v)$, and hence
\begin{equation}
(\Gamma_{k+1}\&\Lambda_{k+1})\iota_k
=
H_{k+1}H_k^{-1}.
\label{eq:nk-cayley-lift}
\end{equation}
By the normalization of the Newton factor, this operator is self-adjoint.

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->

=
\alpha x-\sqrt{2\alpha}\,\xi\in X,

so \eqref{eq:prelim-node-domain} shows that

For $k=0$, boundedness of the auxiliary-input coordinate with respect to

Assume $\dim U<\infty$, and let $F_k$ and $H_k$ be the operators from
Lemma~\ref{lem:nk-cayley-ports}.  Then, for all $\xi\in X$ and $u\in U$,

(F_{k+1}-F_k)\xi+(H_{k+1}-H_k)u

\end{equation}

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
We first control the input blocks $H_k$, then the corresponding Cayley
feedbacks, and finally combine the two increment estimates.

Setting $\xi=0$ in \eqref{eq:nk-cayley-newton} gives
\begin{equation}
\widehat B^*P_{k+1}\widehat B+\widehat D^*\widehat D
=
H_{k+1}^*H_{k+1}
-(H_{k+1}-H_k)^*(H_{k+1}-H_k).
\label{eq:nk-H-difference}
\end{equation}
Since $P_{k+1}\geq0$ and $\widehat D^*\widehat D\geq I_U$,
$H_{k+1}^*H_{k+1}\geq I_U$.  Finite dimensionality of $U$ therefore implies
that $H_{k+1}$ is invertible and $\|H_{k+1}^{-1}\|\leq1$.

For $k\geq1$, $H_k$ is invertible by the preceding estimate. Set
\[
R_k:=H_{k+1}H_k^{-1}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:nk-cayley-lift} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:nk-port-polarization}, $R_k$ is self-adjoint.
Taking the $U$--$U$ part of the first identity in
\eqref{eq:nk-cayley-newton} therefore yields
\[
\widehat B^*P_{k+1}\widehat B+\widehat D^*\widehat D
=
H_k^*(2R_k-I)H_k.
\]
By monotonicity of $P_k$ and \eqref{eq:nk-H-difference} from the preceding
Newton step,
\[
I_U
\leq
\widehat B^*P_{k+1}\widehat B+\widehat D^*\widehat D
\leq
\widehat B^*P_k\widehat B+\widehat D^*\widehat D
\leq H_k^*H_k.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\tfrac12I_U
\leq
R_k
\leq I_U,
\qquad k\geq1.
\label{eq:nk-H-ratio}
\end{equation}
Since
\[
H_{k+1}^*H_{k+1}
=
H_k^*R_k^2H_k
\leq H_k^*H_k,
\]
the sequence $H_k^*H_k$ is decreasing and, since $U$ is finite dimensional,
converges in operator norm. Moreover,
\[
I-R_k^2
=
H_k^{-*}
\bigl(H_k^*H_k-H_{k+1}^*H_{k+1}\bigr)
H_k^{-1}
\rightarrow0,
\]
where we also use $\|H_k^{-1}\|\leq1$. By
\eqref{eq:nk-H-ratio},
\[
I-R_k
=
(I-R_k^2)(I+R_k)^{-1},
\]
and hence $R_k\to I$ in operator norm. Since $(H_k)$ is bounded,
\[
H_{k+1}-H_k=(R_k-I)H_k,
\]
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\|H_{k+1}-H_k\|\rightarrow0.
\label{eq:nk-H-increments}
\end{equation}
For $k\geq1$ put
$\widehat K_k=-H_k^{-1}F_k$.
The relation $v_k=0$ is then
$u=\widehat K_k\xi$.  Feedback evaluation, expressed through
\eqref{eq:nk-cayley-form}, gives
$d_{P_{k+1}}(\xi,\widehat K_k\xi)=0$.  Since the performance output contains
the physical input, this implies
\begin{equation}
\|\widehat K_k\xi\|^2
\leq
\|\widehat C\xi+\widehat D\widehat K_k\xi\|^2
\leq
\langle P_{k+1}\xi,\xi\rangle
\leq
\langle P_1\xi,\xi\rangle,
\label{eq:nk-K-bound}
\end{equation}
so
\[
\|\widehat K_k\|\leq\|P_1\|^{1/2},
\qquad k\geq1.
\]
For the feedback defined by $v_{k+1}=0$, one has
$u=\widehat K_{k+1}\xi$, and the correction output is
$H_k(\widehat K_{k+1}-\widehat K_k)\xi$.
Put
\[
D_{k+1}=P_{k+1}-P_{k+2}\geq0
\]
and
\[
x_{k+1}^+
=
\widehat A\xi+\widehat B\widehat K_{k+1}\xi.
\]
Applying the Cayley transformation to the decrement Lyapunov equation
\eqref{eq:nk-decrement-lyap} yields
\[
\langle D_{k+1}x_{k+1}^+,x_{k+1}^+\rangle
-
\langle D_{k+1}\xi,\xi\rangle
+
\|H_k(\widehat K_{k+1}-\widehat K_k)\xi\|^2
=
0.
\]
Since $D_{k+1}\geq0$, it follows that
\[
\|H_k(\widehat K_{k+1}-\widehat K_k)\xi\|^2
\leq
\langle(P_{k+1}-P_{k+2})\xi,\xi\rangle.
\]
The right-hand side tends to zero by
Theorem~\ref{thm:nk-monotone-convergence}. Finally,
\[
(F_{k+1}-F_k)\xi+(H_{k+1}-H_k)u
=
(H_{k+1}-H_k)(u-\widehat K_{k+1}\xi)
+H_k(\widehat K_k-\widehat K_{k+1})\xi,
\]
and \eqref{eq:nk-port-increments} follows from
\eqref{eq:nk-H-increments}, \eqref{eq:nk-K-bound}, and the preceding
estimate.

<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:nk-monotone-convergence},
$P_kx\to P_\infty x$ for all $x\in X$, and $P_\star\leq P_\infty$.

Let $(x,u,y)$ be any infinite-time generalized trajectory of the original

Section~\ref{subsec:prelim-system-nodes} gives the same inequality for
generalized trajectories: the states converge in $C([0,T];X)$ and the

2\nu
\langle\varepsilon(\varphi),\varepsilon(v)\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}

The input projection of $\operatorname{dom}(A\&B)$ is all of $U$. In fact, for every
$u\in U$, the variational problem
\[
a(q_u,\varphi)
=
\langle Gu,\gamma\varphi\rangle_{L^2(\Gamma_{\rm N})},
\qquad
\varphi\in H^1_{\Gamma_{\rm D}}(\Omega;\mathbb C^2),
\]
has a unique solution by Lax--Milgram; equivalently, see
\cite[Thm.~5.4 and Prop.~5.3]{GudoshnikovKrizek2025}.
The corresponding pair with $p=0$ belongs to $\operatorname{dom}(A\&B)$ and has input
component $u$.

The output map of $\mathcal S_{\rm el}$,
\[
C\&D\left(\begin{matrix}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q\\p\end{smallmatrix}\right)\\u\end{matrix}\right)
=
2G^*\gamma q,
\]
is bounded already with respect to the state norm, by the trace theorem
\cite[Thm.~7.39]{AdamsFournier2003} and Korn's inequality. It remains
to verify generation of the main operator $A$.

For $x=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q\\p\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{dom}(A)$, Green's formula
\eqref{eq:num-green} and the homogeneous boundary condition give
\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle Ax,x\rangle_X
=
-\eta
\|\rho^{-1}\gamma p\|_{L^2(\Gamma_{\rm N})}^2
\leq0.
\]
For $\alpha>0$, eliminating $p=\rho(\alpha q-f)$ from
\[
(\alpha I-A)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q\\p\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}f\\g\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\]
gives
\begin{multline*}
a(q,\varphi)
+
\rho\alpha^2\langle q,\varphi\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}
+
\eta\alpha
\langle\gamma q,\gamma\varphi\rangle_{L^2(\Gamma_{\rm N})}
\\
=
\rho\alpha\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}
+
\langle g,\varphi\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}
+
\eta\langle\gamma f,\gamma\varphi\rangle_{L^2(\Gamma_{\rm N})}.
\end{multline*}
Lax--Milgram yields a unique $q$. Green's formula then recovers the
boundary condition, so that $\operatorname{ran}(\alpha I-A)=X$. Hence $A$ is maximal
dissipative, and the Lumer--Phillips theorem
\cite[Thm.~II.3.15]{EngelNagel2000} shows that $A$ generates a
contraction semigroup. Together with the properties established above,
this proves that $\mathcal S_{\rm el}$ is a system node.

We next consider the system node with the same state-control operator
$A\&B$ and the collocated boundary-velocity output $C_{\rm v}\&D_{\rm v}$.
The latter is bounded with respect to the graph norm of $A\&B$: the first
state component of
\[
A\&B\left(\begin{matrix}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q\\p\end{smallmatrix}\right)\\u\end{matrix}\right)
\]
is $\rho^{-1}p\in H^1_{\Gamma_{\rm D}}(\Omega;\mathbb C^2)$, and the
trace theorem \cite[Thm.~7.39]{AdamsFournier2003} gives the required
bound. Moreover, Green's formula yields
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Re}
\left\langle
(A\&B)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\u\end{smallmatrix}\right),x
\right\rangle_X
=
-\eta
\|\rho^{-1}\gamma p\|_{L^2(\Gamma_{\rm N})}^2
+
\operatorname{Re}
\left\langle
u,
(C_{\rm v}\&D_{\rm v})\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\u\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\right\rangle_U .
\label{eq:num-velocity-passivity}
\end{equation}
Thus this auxiliary system node is impedance passive. By the
infinite-time feedback result for impedance-passive system nodes
\cite[Thm.~2.5]{HastirPaunonen2025}, the feedback
\[
K_0\&L_0=-\kappa(C_{\rm v}\&D_{\rm v})
\]
is admissible and the corresponding system node with auxiliary input
$v_0$ is infinite-time well posed.

Its main operator is
\begin{align*}
A_0\left(\begin{matrix}q\\p\end{matrix}\right)
&=
\left(\begin{matrix}\rho^{-1}p\\\operatorname{div}\sigma(q)\end{matrix}\right),\\
\operatorname{dom}(A_0)
&=
\left\{\,
\left(\begin{matrix}q\\p\end{matrix}\right)\in X
\;\middle|\;
\begin{array}{l}
p\in H^1_{\Gamma_{\rm D}}(\Omega;\mathbb C^2),\\
\sigma(q)\in H(\operatorname{div},\Omega;\mathbb C^{2\times2}),\\
\gamma_n\sigma(q)
+
\rho^{-1}\eta\,\gamma p
+
\kappa\rho^{-1}GG^*\gamma p
=0
\;\text{ on }\Gamma_{\rm N}
\end{array}
\,\right\}.
\end{align*}
Setting $v_0=0$ in \eqref{eq:num-velocity-passivity} gives
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Re}\langle A_0x,x\rangle_X
=
-\eta
\|\rho^{-1}\gamma p\|_{L^2(\Gamma_{\rm N})}^2
-
\kappa
\left\|
C_{\rm v}\&D_{\rm v}
\left(\begin{matrix}x\\-\kappa(C_{\rm v}\&D_{\rm v})\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\end{matrix}\right)
\right\|_U^2.
\label{eq:num-feedback-dissipation}
\end{equation}
By definition of $C_{\rm v}\&D_{\rm v}$,
\[
(C_{\rm v}\&D_{\rm v})
\left(\begin{matrix}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q\\p\end{smallmatrix}\right)\\u\end{matrix}\right)
=
\rho^{-1}G^*\gamma p.
\]
Hence, for $x\in\operatorname{dom}(A_0)$, the feedback relation
\[
u=-\kappa
(C_{\rm v}\&D_{\rm v})
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\u\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\]
and \eqref{eq:num-velocity-passivity} give
\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle A_0x,x\rangle_X
=
-\eta
\|\rho^{-1}\gamma p\|_{L^2(\Gamma_{\rm N})}^2
-
\kappa
\|\rho^{-1}G^*\gamma p\|_U^2.
\]
In particular, the semigroup generated by $A_0$ is contractive.

We show that it is strongly stable. First, $A_0$ has compact resolvent.
Writing
$D_0=\eta I+\kappa GG^*$
on $L^2(\Gamma_{\rm N};\mathbb C^2)$, the resolvent equation
\[
(\alpha I-A_0)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q\\p\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}f\\g\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\]
reduces to
\begin{multline}
a(q,\varphi)
+
\rho\alpha^2\langle q,\varphi\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}
+
\alpha
\langle D_0\gamma q,\gamma\varphi\rangle_{L^2(\Gamma_{\rm N})}
\\
=
\rho\alpha\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}
+
\langle g,\varphi\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}
+
\langle D_0\gamma f,\gamma\varphi\rangle_{L^2(\Gamma_{\rm N})}.
\label{eq:num-feedback-resolvent}
\end{multline}
For a bounded sequence $(f_n,g_n)$ in $X$, the compact embedding
\[
H^1_{\Gamma_{\rm D}}(\Omega;\mathbb C^2)
\hookrightarrow
L^2(\Omega;\mathbb C^2)
\]
and the compactness of the trace embedding into
$L^2(\Gamma_{\rm N};\mathbb C^2)$, together with the continuity of
\[
L^2(\Omega;\mathbb C^2)
\hookrightarrow
\bigl(H^1_{\Gamma_{\rm D}}(\Omega;\mathbb C^2)\bigr)',
\]
show that the right-hand sides of
\eqref{eq:num-feedback-resolvent} are precompact in
$\bigl(H^1_{\Gamma_{\rm D}}(\Omega;\mathbb C^2)\bigr)'$; see
\cite[Thm.~6.3 and Thm.~7.39]{AdamsFournier2003}.
Coercivity then yields convergence of $q_n$ in
$H^1_{\Gamma_{\rm D}}(\Omega;\mathbb C^2)$, and
$p_n=\rho(\alpha q_n-f_n)$
converges in $L^2(\Omega;\mathbb C^2)$. Hence
$(\alpha I-A_0)^{-1}$ is compact.

Suppose now that
\[
A_0\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q\\p\end{smallmatrix}\right)=i\omega\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q\\p\end{smallmatrix}\right).
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:num-feedback-dissipation} gives
$\gamma p=0$ on $\Gamma_{\rm N}$.
If $\omega=0$, then $p=0$, and testing the equilibrium equation with
$q$ gives $a(q,q)=0$. Korn's inequality therefore yields $q=0$.

Let $\omega\neq0$. The first component of the eigenvalue equation gives
$p=i\rho\omega q$,
and hence
$\gamma q=0$ on $\Gamma_{\rm N}$.
The boundary condition for $A_0$ then also gives
$\gamma_n\sigma(q)=0$
on $\Gamma_{\rm N}$,
whereas the second component yields
\[
-\operatorname{div}\sigma(q)=\rho\omega^2q.
\]
Thus both Cauchy data of $q$ vanish on a nonempty straight segment of
$\Gamma_{\rm N}$. Extension by zero across this segment, Holmgren
uniqueness for the constant-coefficient Lam\'e system, and analytic
continuation imply $q=0$; compare
\cite[Sec.~1]{DiaoLiuWang2020}. Consequently $p=0$ as well.

We have therefore proved
$\ker(i\omega I-A_0)=\{0\}$,
$\omega\in\mathbb R$.
Since $A_0$ has compact resolvent, every spectral point of $A_0$ is an
eigenvalue; see
\cite[Cor.~IV.1.19]{EngelNagel2000}. Hence
\[
\sigma(A_0)\cap i\mathbb R=\varnothing.
\]
The semigroup generated by $A_0$ is contractive and therefore bounded.
Since $\sigma(A_0^*)=\overline{\sigma(A_0)}$, the
Arendt--Batty--Lyubich--V{\~u} theorem
\cite[Thm.~V.2.21]{EngelNagel2000}
now yields strong stability. In particular,
$0\in\rho(A_0)$.

It remains to pass from the auxiliary velocity output to the position
output of $\mathcal S_{\rm el}$. Put
\[
C_{\rm pos}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q\\p\end{smallmatrix}\right)=G^*\gamma q.
\]
The first component of $A_0$ gives
\[
C_{\rm pos}A_0x
=
(C_{\rm v}\&D_{\rm v})
\left(\begin{matrix}x\\(K_0\&L_0)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\end{matrix}\right),
\qquad
x\in\operatorname{dom}(A_0).
\]
Since $D_{\rm v}=0$, this reduces to
$C_{\rm pos}A_0x=\rho^{-1}G^*\gamma p$,
and therefore
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm pos}
=
C_{{\rm v},0}A_0^{-1},
\label{eq:num-feedback-position-velocity}
\end{equation}
where $C_{{\rm v},0}$ denotes the observation operator of the feedback-transformed
auxiliary system. Since $C_{{\rm v},0}$ is infinite-time admissible,
\eqref{eq:num-feedback-position-velocity} and $0\in\rho(A_0)$ imply
infinite-time admissibility of $C_{\rm pos}$.

Finally, let $\mathbf G_{\rm v}$ and $\mathbf G_{\rm pos}$ denote the
zero-state transfer functions of the feedback-transformed system with outputs
$C_{{\rm v},0}$ and $C_{\rm pos}$, respectively. The first resolvent
component gives
\[
\mathbf G_{\rm v}(\lambda)
=
\lambda\mathbf G_{\rm pos}(\lambda),
\qquad
\lambda\in\mathbb C_+.
\]
Infinite-time well-posedness of the auxiliary system node yields
\[
\mathbf G_{\rm v}
\in
H^\infty(\mathbb C_+;\mathcal L(U)).
\]
On the other hand, $0\in\rho(A_0)$ implies that
$\mathbf G_{\rm pos}$ extends holomorphically to a neighbourhood of the
origin. Hence the preceding identity implies
\[
\mathbf G_{\rm pos}
\in
H^\infty(\mathbb C_+;\mathcal L(U)).
\]
Thus the zero-state input-output operator associated with $C_{\rm pos}$
is bounded and defined on $L^2(0,\infty;U)$. Together with the infinite-time
input-state bound of the auxiliary system node and the infinite-time
admissibility of $C_{\rm pos}$, this proves infinite-time well-posedness
of the feedback-transformed system with output $2C_{\rm pos}=2G^*\gamma q$.
