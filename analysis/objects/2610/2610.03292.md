\label{thm:spectral-index}
Assume that \(A_u\) has simple \(K\)-invariant spectrum for every \(u\in(H^1(M))^K\). Then every connected component of \(\overline{\mathcal{N}_K}\) intersects \(\mathcal{T}_K\) in at most one point.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:shifted-schrodinger}
For every \(u\in(H^1(M))^K\) and all sufficiently large \(c>0\), equation \eqref{eq:shifted-schrodinger} has a unique weak solution \(v\in(H^1(M))^K\) for every \(f\in(L^2(M))^K\). Moreover, the solution satisfies \(\|v\|_{H^1(M)}\leq2\|f\|_{L^2(M)}\).
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix \(u\in(H^1(M))^K\). Splitting the integral \(\int_M|u|^{p-2}v^2 dM\) over the sets where \(|u|^{p-2}\leq R\) and \(|u|^{p-2}>R\), and applying H\"older's inequality and the continuous embedding of \((H^1(M))^K\) into \(L^p(M)\), we obtain
\[
\int_M|u|^{p-2}v^2 dM\leq R\|v\|_{L^2(M)}^2+C\left(\int_{\{|u|^{p-2}>R\}}|u|^p dM\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\|v\|_{H^1(M)}^2
\]
for every \(R>0\) and \(v\in(H^1(M))^K\), where \(C>0\) is independent of \(R\) and \(v\).
 Since \(u\in L^p(M)\), the coefficient of \(\|v\|_{H^1(M)}^2\) tends to zero as \(R\to\infty\). Choose \(R\) so that this coefficient is at most \(1/2\). For every \(c\geq R+1\), it follows that
\[
B_u(v,v)\geq\frac12\int_M|\nabla v|^2 dM+\left(c-R-\frac12\right)\int_Mv^2 dM\geq\frac12\|v\|_{H^1(M)}^2.
\]
Thus \(B_u\) is coercive. It is also continuous by the Cauchy--Schwarz and H\"older inequalities and the assumed embedding.

For \(f\in(L^2(M))^K\), the functional \(w\mapsto\int_Mfw dM\) is bounded on \((H^1(M))^K\). The Lax--Milgram theorem therefore gives a unique \(v\in(H^1(M))^K\) satisfying \(B_u(v,w)=\int_Mfw dM\) for every \(w\in(H^1(M))^K\). Taking \(w=v\), we obtain
\[
\frac12\|v\|_{H^1(M)}^2\leq B_u(v,v)=\int_Mfv dM\leq\|f\|_{L^2(M)}\|v\|_{H^1(M)},
\]
which gives the asserted estimate.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:resolvent-properties}
The operator \((A_u+c)^{-1}:(L^2(M))^K\to(L^2(M))^K\) is compact, self-adjoint, positive and injective.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-schrodinger}, \((A_u+c)^{-1}\) maps bounded subsets of \((L^2(M))^K\) into bounded subsets of \((H^1(M))^K\). The Rellich--Kondrachov theorem therefore implies its compactness as an operator on \((L^2(M))^K\).

For \(f,h\in(L^2(M))^K\), the symmetry of \(B_u\) and \eqref{eq:Tu-weak} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle(A_u+c)^{-1}f,h\rangle_{L^2(M)}
=B_u((A_u+c)^{-1}f,(A_u+c)^{-1}h)=\langle f,(A_u+c)^{-1}h\rangle_{L^2(M)},
\end{aligned}
\]
so \((A_u+c)^{-1}\) is self-adjoint. Moreover, taking \(w=(A_u+c)^{-1}f\) in \eqref{eq:Tu-weak} and using coercivity, we obtain
\[
\langle(A_u+c)^{-1}f,f\rangle_{L^2(M)}=B_u((A_u+c)^{-1}f,(A_u+c)^{-1}f)\geq\frac12\|(A_u+c)^{-1}f\|_{H^1(M)}^2\geq0.
\]
Thus the operator is positive. Finally, if \((A_u+c)^{-1}f=0\), then \(v=0\) solves \eqref{eq:shifted-schrodinger}, so \(f=0\). Thus \((A_u+c)^{-1}\) is injective.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:shift-independence}
The numbers \(\mu_j(u)\) defined by \eqref{eq:Au-eigenvalues} are independent of the choice of \(c\).
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let \(\widetilde c\) be another sufficiently large shift as in Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-schrodinger}, and let \(\widetilde\alpha_j(u)\) denote the nonzero eigenvalues of \((A_u+\widetilde c)^{-1}\), ordered decreasingly and counted with their \(K\)-invariant multiplicities. Interchanging the two shifts if necessary, we may assume that \(\widetilde c\geq c\).

We first show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eigeq}
\mathbb{V}_{(A_u+c)^{-1}}^K(\alpha_j(u))=\mathbb{V}_{(A_u+\widetilde c)^{-1}}^K\bigl((\alpha_j(u)^{-1}+\widetilde c-c)^{-1}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Since \(\alpha_j(u)^{-1}+\widetilde c-c\neq0\), the weak formulation of \eqref{eq:shifted-schrodinger} and uniqueness in Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-schrodinger} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
(A_u+c)^{-1}v=\alpha_j(u)v
&\Longleftrightarrow (A_u+c)v=\alpha_j(u)^{-1}v\\
&\Longleftrightarrow (A_u+\widetilde c)v=(\alpha_j(u)^{-1}+\widetilde c-c)v\\
&\Longleftrightarrow (A_u+\widetilde c)^{-1}v=(\alpha_j(u)^{-1}+\widetilde c-c)^{-1}v
\end{aligned}
\]
for \(v\in(H^1(M))^K\). This proves \eqref{eq:eigeq}.
Therefore
\[
\sigma_K((A_u+\widetilde c)^{-1})\setminus\{0\}=\bigl\{(\alpha_j(u)^{-1}+\widetilde c-c)^{-1}:\alpha_j(u)\in\sigma_K((A_u+c)^{-1})\setminus\{0\}\bigr\}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:eigeq}, the eigenvalues \(\alpha_j(u)\) and \((\alpha_j(u)^{-1}+\widetilde c-c)^{-1}\) have the same \(K\)-invariant multiplicity. Moreover, since \(t\mapsto(t^{-1}+\widetilde c-c)^{-1}\) is strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\), the numbers \((\alpha_j(u)^{-1}+\widetilde c-c)^{-1}\) are ordered decreasingly as \(j\) increases. Hence \(\widetilde\alpha_j(u)=(\alpha_j(u)^{-1}+\widetilde c-c)^{-1}\) for every \(j\geq0\), and therefore \( \widetilde\alpha_j(u)^{-1}-\widetilde c=\alpha_j(u)^{-1}-c. \)
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:eigenvalue-continuity}
For every \(j\geq0\), the map \((H^1(M))^K\ni u\longmapsto\mu_j(u)\in\mathbb{R}\) is continuous.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let \(u_n\to u\) in \((H^1(M))^K\). We first choose a common shift \(c>0\) for which the inverse operators \((A_{u_n}+c)^{-1}\) converge to \((A_u+c)^{-1}\) in operator norm on \((L^2(M))^K\).

Choose \(c>0\) as in Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-schrodinger} for \(u\), so that \(B_u(v,v)\geq\frac12\|v\|_{H^1(M)}^2\). H\"older's inequality and the assumed continuous embedding of \((H^1(M))^K\) into \(L^p(M)\) give a constant \(C>0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bilinear-form-difference}
|B_{u_n}(v,w)-B_u(v,w)|
\leq C\bigl\||u_n|^{p-2}-|u|^{p-2}\bigr\|_{L^{p/(p-2)}(M)}\|v\|_{H^1(M)}\|w\|_{H^1(M)}
\end{equation}
for every \(v,w\in(H^1(M))^K\).
Since \(u_n\to u\) in \((H^1(M))^K\), the same embedding gives \(u_n\to u\) in \(L^p(M)\), and hence \(|u_n|^{p-2}\to|u|^{p-2}\) in \(L^{p/(p-2)}(M)\).
Applying \eqref{eq:bilinear-form-difference} with \(w=v\), we therefore obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-coercivity}
B_{u_n}(v,v)\geq\frac14\|v\|_{H^1(M)}^2
\end{equation}
for every \(v\in(H^1(M))^K\) and all sufficiently large \(n\). The Lax--Milgram theorem thus defines \((A_{u_n}+c)^{-1}\) with this same shift \(c\).

Fix \(f\in(L^2(M))^K\). By \eqref{eq:Tu-weak},
\[
B_{u_n}((A_{u_n}+c)^{-1}f,w)=\int_Mfw dM=B_u((A_u+c)^{-1}f,w)
\]
for every \(w\in(H^1(M))^K\). From this and using linearity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolvent-difference-identity}
B_{u_n}\bigl((A_{u_n}+c)^{-1}f-(A_u+c)^{-1}f,w\bigr)=B_u((A_u+c)^{-1}f,w)-B_{u_n}((A_u+c)^{-1}f,w)
\end{equation}
for every \(w\in(H^1(M))^K\). Choosing \(w=(A_{u_n}+c)^{-1}f-(A_u+c)^{-1}f\) in \eqref{eq:resolvent-difference-identity} and applying \eqref{eq:uniform-coercivity}, \eqref{eq:bilinear-form-difference}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-schrodinger}, we obtain
\[
\bigl\|(A_{u_n}+c)^{-1}f-(A_u+c)^{-1}f\bigr\|_{H^1(M)}
\leq 8C\bigl\||u_n|^{p-2}-|u|^{p-2}\bigr\|_{L^{p/(p-2)}(M)}\|f\|_{L^2(M)}.
\]
Since \(\|v\|_{L^2(M)}\leq\|v\|_{H^1(M)}\), the same bound holds with the \(L^2(M)\)-norm on the left. Taking the supremum over \(f\in(L^2(M))^K\) with \(\|f\|_{L^2(M)}\leq1\), and using \(|u_n|^{p-2}\to|u|^{p-2}\) in \(L^{p/(p-2)}(M)\), proves that \((A_{u_n}+c)^{-1}\to(A_u+c)^{-1}\) in operator norm on \((L^2(M))^K\).

Hence, by the max--min principle, the ordered eigenvalues of these compact positive self-adjoint operators satisfy \(\alpha_j(u_n)\to\alpha_j(u)\) for every \(j\geq0\). Since \(\alpha_j(u)>0\), \eqref{eq:Au-eigenvalues} and Lemma~\ref{lem:shift-independence} yield
\(
\mu_j(u_n)=\alpha_j(u_n)^{-1}-c\longrightarrow\alpha_j(u)^{-1}-c=\mu_j(u).
\)
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:index-continuity}
The index
\(
    \mathcal{I}:\overline{\mathcal{N}_K}\to\mathbb N_0
\)
is continuous.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let \((u_n,\mu_{j_n}(u_n))\to(u,\mu_j(u))\) in \(\overline{\mathcal{N}_K}\). We will show that \(\mathcal{I}(u_n,\mu_{j_n}(u_n))=j_n\to j=\mathcal{I}(u,\mu_j(u))\). It suffices to prove that \(j_n=j\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

By the ordering and simplicity of the eigenvalues, \(\mu_j(u)<\mu_{j+1}(u)\). Since \(\mu_{j_n}(u_n)\to\mu_j(u)\) and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:eigenvalue-continuity}, \(\mu_{j+1}(u_n)\to\mu_{j+1}(u)\), we obtain \(\mu_{j_n}(u_n)<\mu_{j+1}(u_n)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). The ordering of the eigenvalues therefore gives \(j_n\leq j\).

If \(j\geq1\), the same argument applied to \(\mu_{j-1}(u)<\mu_j(u)\) gives \(\mu_{j-1}(u_n)<\mu_{j_n}(u_n)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), hence \(j_n\geq j\). For \(j=0\), this lower bound holds because \(j_n\in\mathbb N_0\). Thus \(j_n=j\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), proving the continuity of \(\mathcal{I}\).
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:index-trivial}, every point of \(\overline{\mathcal{N}_K}\cap\mathcal{T}_K\) has the form \((0,\mu_j)\) for some \(j\geq0\). Since \(\mathcal{I}\) is constant on connected components and \(\mathcal{I}(0,\mu_j)=j\), a component containing both \((0,\mu_j)\) and \((0,\mu_k)\) must satisfy \(j=k\). Thus the two points coincide.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:invariant-integral-identities}
For every \(f\in L^1(\Omega_{a,b})^K\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit-integration}
\int_{\Omega_{a,b}}f dM=\int_a^b\widetilde f(t)\rho(t)dt.
\end{equation}
If \(v,w\in C^\infty(M)^K\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-h1-reduction}
\int_{\Omega_{a,b}}\bigl(|\nabla v|^2+v^2\bigr)dM=\int_a^b\bigl(|\widetilde v'(t)|^2+|\widetilde v(t)|^2\bigr)\rho(t)dt
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-gradient-reduction}
\int_{\Omega_{a,b}}\langle\nabla v,\nabla w\rangle dM=\int_a^b\widetilde v'(t)\widetilde w'(t)\rho(t)dt.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The metric decomposition \(dt^2+g_t\) and the definition of \(\rho\) give \eqref{eq:orbit-integration}.

Let \(v\in C^\infty(M)^K\). Since \(v\) is constant on the orbits, its gradient is normal to them. The normal space to \(K(\gamma(t))\) is spanned by \(\gamma'(t)\), so \(\nabla v(\gamma(t))=a(t)\gamma'(t)\) for some function \(a:(0,l)\to\mathbb{R}\). Taking the inner product with \(\gamma'(t)\) and using \(|\gamma'(t)|=1\), we obtain
\(
a(t)=\langle\nabla v(\gamma(t)),\gamma'(t)\rangle=\widetilde v'(t),
\)
where the last equality follows from the chain rule. Thus \(\nabla v(\gamma(t))=\widetilde v'(t)\gamma'(t)\), and consequently \(|\nabla v(\gamma(t))|^2=|\widetilde v'(t)|^2\).   Applying the same argument to \(w\in C^\infty(M)^K\), we also obtain \(\langle\nabla v(\gamma(t)),\nabla w(\gamma(t))\rangle=\widetilde v'(t)\widetilde w'(t)\).

Formula \eqref{eq:orbit-integration}, applied to \(|\nabla v|^2+v^2\) and to \(\langle\nabla v,\nabla w\rangle\), now gives \eqref{eq:local-h1-reduction} and \eqref{eq:local-gradient-reduction}, respectively.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:invariant-h1-reduction}
If \(v\in(H^1(M))^K\), then \(\widetilde v\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,l)\).
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Let \(v\in(H^1(M))^K\). Choose \(v_n\in C^\infty(M)^K\) such that \(v_n\to v\) in \(H^1(M)\). For every \(0<a<b<l\), applying \eqref{eq:local-h1-reduction} to \(v_n-v_m\) gives
\[
\|\widetilde v_n-\widetilde v_m\|_{H^1(a,b)}^2\leq\frac{1}{\min_{t\in[a,b]}\rho(t)}\|v_n-v_m\|_{H^1(M)}^2.
\]
Thus \((\widetilde v_n)\) converges in \(H^1(a,b)\). By the continuous embedding of \(H^1(a,b)\) into \(C([a,b])\), it also converges uniformly on \([a,b]\). We may therefore define \(\widetilde v(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde v_n(t)\) for every \(t\in(0,l)\). On each \([a,b]\subset(0,l)\), uniform convergence implies convergence in \(L^2(a,b)\), so uniqueness of the limit shows that \(\widetilde v\) agrees almost everywhere with the \(H^1(a,b)\)-limit. Consequently, \(\widetilde v_n\to\widetilde v\) in \(H^1(a,b)\) for every \(0<a<b<l\), and therefore \(\widetilde v\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,l)\).

Define \(\widehat v:M_{\mathrm{princ}}\to\mathbb{R}\) by \(\widehat v(k\gamma(t))=\widetilde v(t)\). Since \(\gamma\) meets each principal orbit exactly once, this function is well-defined and \(K\)-invariant. To prove that it represents \(v\), we first verify equality almost everywhere on each \(\Omega_{a,b}\). By \eqref{eq:orbit-integration},
\[
\|v_n-\widehat v\|_{L^2(\Omega_{a,b})}^2=\int_a^b|\widetilde v_n(t)-\widetilde v(t)|^2\rho(t)dt\leq\left(\max_{t\in[a,b]}\rho(t)\right)\|\widetilde v_n-\widetilde v\|_{L^2(a,b)}^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since \(v_n\to v\) in \(L^2(\Omega_{a,b})\), uniqueness of the limit gives \(\widehat v=v\) almost everywhere on \(\Omega_{a,b}\).

Now let \(E=\{x\in M_{\mathrm{princ}}:\widehat v(x)\neq v(x)\}\). The equality \(\widehat v=v\) almost everywhere on \(\Omega_{a,b}\) implies that \(E\cap\Omega_{a,b}\) has measure zero for every \(0<a<b<l\). Since \(M_{\mathrm{princ}}=\Omega_{l/3,2l/3}\cup\Omega_{l/4,3l/4}\cup\cdots\), we have \(E=(E\cap\Omega_{l/3,2l/3})\cup(E\cap\Omega_{l/4,3l/4})\cup\cdots\). Thus \(E\) has measure zero, being a countable union of sets of measure zero. Hence \(\widehat v\) is a \(K\)-invariant representative of \(v\) on \(M_{\mathrm{princ}}\), with \(\widetilde v\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,l)\).
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:invariant-eigenfunction-reduction}
Let \(u\in(H^1(M))^K\) and \(v\in\mathbb{V}_{A_u}^K(\mu_j(u))\). Then the corresponding continuous function \(\widetilde v\) belongs to \(C^2(0,l)\) and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced-schrodinger-derivative}
\bigl(\rho(t)\widetilde v'(t)\bigr)'=-\rho(t)\bigl(\mu_j(u)+|\widetilde u(t)|^{p-2}\bigr)\widetilde v(t)
\end{equation}
for every \(t\in(0,l)\).
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Fix \(0<a<b<l\). We first extend \eqref{eq:local-gradient-reduction} to \(v\in(H^1(M))^K\). Let \(v_n\in C^\infty(M)^K\) be such that \(v_n\to v\) in \(H^1(M)\). As shown in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant-h1-reduction}, we also have \(\widetilde v_n\to\widetilde v\) in \(H^1(a,b)\). By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and the boundedness of \(\rho\) on \([a,b]\), passing to the limit in \eqref{eq:local-gradient-reduction} applied to \(v_n\) and \(w\in C^\infty(M)^K\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sobolev-gradient-reduction}
\int_{\Omega_{a,b}}\langle\nabla v,\nabla w\rangle dM=\int_a^b\widetilde v'(t)\widetilde w'(t)\rho(t)dt
\end{equation}
for every \(w\in C^\infty(M)^K\).

Now let \(u\in(H^1(M))^K\) and \(v\in\mathbb{V}_{A_u}^K(\mu_j(u))\). We show that \(\widetilde v\) satisfies \eqref{eq:reduced-schrodinger-derivative} in the sense of distributions on \((a,b)\). Since \(v\) is an eigenfunction of \(A_u\) corresponding to \(\mu_j(u)\), it satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-eigenfunction}
\int_M\langle\nabla v,\nabla w\rangle dM-\int_M|u|^{p-2}vw dM=\mu_j(u)\int_Mvw dM
\end{equation}
for every \(w\in(H^1(M))^K\).

Let \(\widetilde w\in C_c^\infty(a,b)\), and define \(w\in C^\infty(M)^K\) by \(w(k\gamma(t))=\widetilde w(t)\) on \(\Omega_{a,b}\) and \(w=0\) outside \(\Omega_{a,b}\). Both \(w\) and \(\nabla w\) vanish outside \(\Omega_{a,b}\), so all integrals in \eqref{eq:weak-eigenfunction} can be taken over \(\Omega_{a,b}\). Applying \eqref{eq:orbit-integration} and \eqref{eq:sobolev-gradient-reduction} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced-schrodinger-weak}
\int_a^b\rho(t)\widetilde v'(t)\widetilde w'(t)dt=\int_a^b\rho(t)\bigl(\mu_j(u)+|\widetilde u(t)|^{p-2}\bigr)\widetilde v(t)\widetilde w(t)dt.
\end{equation}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant-h1-reduction}, \(\widetilde v\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,l)\), so \(\rho\widetilde v'\in L^2(a,b)\). Since \eqref{eq:reduced-schrodinger-weak} holds for every \(\widetilde w\in C_c^\infty(a,b)\), the distributional derivative of \(\rho\widetilde v'\) equals \(-\rho(\mu_j(u)+|\widetilde u|^{p-2})\widetilde v\). This function is continuous, so \(\rho\widetilde v'\) has a \(C^1\) representative on \((a,b)\). Since \(\rho\) is smooth and strictly positive, it follows that \(\widetilde v\in C^2(a,b)\), and the equation holds classically there. As \(0<a<b<l\) was arbitrary, \(\widetilde v\in C^2(0,l)\), and \eqref{eq:reduced-schrodinger-derivative} holds for every \(t\in(0,l)\).

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:schrodinger-simplicity}
Let \(M\) be a compact connected Riemannian manifold and let \(K\) be a compact Lie group acting isometrically on \(M\). Assume that \(M/K\) is homeomorphic to a closed interval and that \((H^1(M))^K\) is continuously embedded in \(L^p(M)\). Then, for every \(u\in(H^1(M))^K\), every \(K\)-invariant eigenvalue of \(A_u=-\Delta-|u|^{p-2}\) is simple.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Fix \(u\in(H^1(M))^K\) and let \(v_1,v_2\in\mathbb{V}_{A_u}^K(\mu_j(u))\setminus\{0\}\). We show that \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) are linearly dependent. On \(M_{\mathrm{princ}}\), we use their continuous \(K\)-invariant representatives given by Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant-h1-reduction}. By Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant-eigenfunction-reduction}, the corresponding functions \(\widetilde v_1,\widetilde v_2\) belong to \(C^2(0,l)\) and satisfy \eqref{eq:reduced-schrodinger-derivative}.
Define their Wronskian by
\(
W(t)=\widetilde v_1(t)\widetilde v_2'(t)-\widetilde v_1'(t)\widetilde v_2(t).
\)
Equation \eqref{eq:reduced-schrodinger-derivative} gives
\(
(\rho W)'=\widetilde v_1(\rho\widetilde v_2')'-\widetilde v_2(\rho\widetilde v_1')'=0.
\)
Consequently, there exists \(C\in\mathbb{R}\) such that
\(\rho(t)W(t)=C\)
for every \(t\in(0,l)\).

To show that \(C=0\), let \(f\in C^\infty(M)^K\). Then \(fv_1,fv_2\in(H^1(M))^K\). Using \(fv_2\) in the weak equation \(A_uv_1=\mu_j(u)v_1\) and \(fv_1\) in the weak equation \(A_uv_2=\mu_j(u)v_2\), and subtracting, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wronskian-weak-identity}
0=\int_M\langle\nabla v_1,\nabla(fv_2)\rangle dM-\int_M\langle\nabla v_2,\nabla(fv_1)\rangle dM.
\end{equation}

Now choose \(f\) such that \(f=1\) near \(O_-\) and \(f=0\) near \(O_+\). By the product rule, the difference of the integrands in \eqref{eq:wronskian-weak-identity} is \(\langle v_2\nabla v_1-v_1\nabla v_2,\nabla f\rangle\), which vanishes near both nonprincipal orbits. By Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant-eigenfunction-reduction}, \(\widetilde v_1,\widetilde v_2\in C^2(0,l)\). Reasoning as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant-integral-identities}, we obtain \(\nabla v_i(\gamma(t))=\widetilde v_i'(t)\gamma'(t)\) for \(i=1,2\).
Thus \eqref{eq:orbit-integration} gives
\[
0=\int_0^l\bigl(\widetilde v_2(t)\widetilde v_1'(t)-\widetilde v_1(t)\widetilde v_2'(t)\bigr)\widetilde f'(t)\rho(t)dt=-\int_0^l\widetilde f'(t)\rho(t)W(t)dt.
\]
Since \(\rho(t)W(t)=C\), while \(\widetilde f=1\) near \(0\) and \(\widetilde f=0\) near \(l\), we obtain
\(
0=-C\int_0^l\widetilde f'(t)dt=C.
\)
Thus \(C=0\), and positivity of \(\rho\) implies that \(W=0\) on \((0,l)\).
Therefore \(\widetilde v_1=c\widetilde v_2\) on \((0,l)\) for some \(c\in\mathbb{R}\). Since \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) are constant on each orbit, it follows that \(v_1=cv_2\) on \(M_{\mathrm{princ}}\). Its complement consists of the two nonprincipal orbits and has Riemannian measure zero, so \(v_1=cv_2\) almost everywhere on \(M\). Hence~\(\dim\mathbb{V}_{A_u}^K(\mu_j(u))=1\).

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:sign-changing}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:schrodinger-simplicity}, let \((u,\mu_j(u))\in\mathcal{N}_K\). If \(j=0\), then \(u\) is either positive or negative almost everywhere on \(M\). If \(j\geq1\), then \(u\) changes sign on \(M\).

Consequently, if \(\mathcal C\) is a connected component of \(\overline{\mathcal{N}_K}\) containing \((0,\mu_j)\), then every nontrivial solution in \(\mathcal C\) has one sign when \(j=0\), and changes sign when \(j\geq1\).
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Fix \((u,\mu_j(u))\in\mathcal{N}_K\). Then \(u\) is a nonzero eigenfunction of \(A_u\) corresponding to \(\mu_j(u)\). To determine its sign, we first construct an eigenfunction corresponding to \(\mu_0(u)\) that is positive almost everywhere on \(M\).

By the standard variational characterization,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-eigenvalue-characterization}
\mu_0(u)=\min_{v\in(H^1(M))^K\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_M\left(|\nabla v|^2-|u|^{p-2}v^2\right)dM}{\int_Mv^2dM},
\end{equation}
and the minimizers are precisely the nonzero eigenfunctions corresponding to \(\mu_0(u)\). Choose such an eigenfunction \(v_0\). By \cite[Proposition~3.49]{Aubin1982}, we have \(|v_0|\in H^1(M)\) and \(\left|\nabla|v_0|\right|=|\nabla v_0|\) almost everywhere. Moreover, \(|v_0|\) is \(K\)-invariant. Thus \(|v_0|\) attains the same minimum in \eqref{eq:first-eigenvalue-characterization} and also belongs to \(\mathbb{V}_{A_u}^K(\mu_0(u))\). Replacing \(v_0\) by \(|v_0|\), we may assume that \(v_0\geq0\) almost everywhere.

We next show that \(v_0>0\) almost everywhere. By Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant-eigenfunction-reduction}, \(\widetilde v_0\in C^2(0,l)\) satisfies \eqref{eq:reduced-schrodinger-derivative} with eigenvalue \(\mu_0(u)\). Its continuity and the inequality \(v_0\geq0\) almost everywhere imply that \(\widetilde v_0\geq0\) on \((0,l)\). If \(\widetilde v_0(t_0)=0\) for some \(t_0\in(0,l)\), then \(t_0\) is a minimum, so \(\widetilde v_0'(t_0)=0\). Uniqueness for the homogeneous linear equation \eqref{eq:reduced-schrodinger-derivative} with initial conditions \(\widetilde v_0(t_0)=\widetilde v_0'(t_0)=0\) implies that \(\widetilde v_0\equiv0\) on \((0,l)\). The corresponding representative of \(v_0\) would therefore vanish on \(M_{\mathrm{princ}}\). As its complement has measure zero, this contradicts \(v_0\neq0\) in \(H^1(M)\). Hence \(\widetilde v_0>0\) on \((0,l)\), which gives \(v_0>0\) almost everywhere on \(M\).

If \(j=0\), then \(u\) and \(v_0\) correspond to the same eigenvalue \(\mu_0(u)\). By Theorem~\ref{thm:schrodinger-simplicity}, \(u\) is a nonzero multiple of \(v_0\), so \(u\) is either positive or negative almost everywhere.

If \(j\geq1\), then \(\mu_j(u)>\mu_0(u)\). Using \(v_0\) in the weak eigenvalue equation for \(u\), and \(u\) in that for \(v_0\), gives
\[
\mu_j(u)\int_Muv_0 dM
=\int_M\bigl(\langle\nabla u,\nabla v_0\rangle-|u|^{p-2}uv_0\bigr)dM
=\mu_0(u)\int_Muv_0 dM.
\]
Thus \(\int_Muv_0 dM=0\). Since \(u\neq0\) and \(v_0>0\) almost everywhere, this equality excludes both \(u\geq0\) and \(u\leq0\) almost everywhere. Hence \(u\) changes sign.

Finally, let \(\mathcal C\) be a connected component of \(\overline{\mathcal{N}_K}\) containing \((0,\mu_j)\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:index-continuity}, \(\mathcal{I}\) is constant on \(\mathcal C\), with value \(\mathcal{I}(0,\mu_j)=j\). Consequently, every nontrivial solution in \(\mathcal C\) has the form \((w,\mu_j(w))\). Applying the result just proved to \(w\), we conclude that it is either positive or negative almost everywhere if \(j=0\), and changes sign if \(j\geq1\).

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:HV}
If \(n-d_K\leq2\), then \((H^1(M))^K\) is compactly embedded in \(L^q(M)\) for every \(1<q<\infty\). If \(n-d_K>2\), then this embedding is compact for every \(1<q<2(n-d_K)/(n-d_K-2)\).

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:unbounded-continuum}
Assume that \(M/K\) is homeomorphic to a closed interval and, if \(n-d_K>2\), that \(p<2(n-d_K)/(n-d_K-2)\). Then, for every \(\mu_j\in\sigma_K(-\Delta)\), \(j\geq0\), the point \((0,\mu_j)\in\mathcal{T}_K\) is a global bifurcation point. The component \(\mathcal{C}_K(\mu_j)\) is unbounded in \((H^1(M))^K\times\mathbb{R}\) and meets \(\mathcal{T}_K\) only at \((0,\mu_j)\). These components are pairwise disjoint. Moreover, every nontrivial solution in \(\mathcal{C}_K(\mu_j)\) is either positive or negative almost everywhere on \(M\) if \(j=0\), and changes sign if \(j\geq1\).
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
By Theorem~\ref{thm:HV}, the embedding of \((H^1(M))^K\) into \(L^p(M)\) is compact and therefore continuous. Theorem~\ref{thm:schrodinger-simplicity} implies that \(A_u\) has simple \(K\)-invariant spectrum for every \(u\in(H^1(M))^K\). In particular, every \(\mu_j\in\sigma_K(-\Delta)\) is simple. The Rabinowitz global bifurcation theorem from \cite{Rabinowitz1971} therefore shows that \((0,\mu_j)\in\mathcal{T}_K\) is a global bifurcation point: its component \(\mathcal{C}_K(\mu_j)\) is either unbounded or meets \(\mathcal{T}_K\) at another point.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-index}, the only point of \(\mathcal{T}_K\) in \(\mathcal{C}_K(\mu_j)\) is \((0,\mu_j)\), so the component is unbounded. Components corresponding to different eigenvalues are therefore distinct and hence disjoint. Finally, Proposition~\ref{prop:sign-changing} gives the stated conclusions about the signs of their nontrivial solutions.
<!-- end proof-12 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The two possibilities in the Rabinowitz alternative are not mutually exclusive: an unbounded component may also meet the trivial branch at another eigenvalue. Theorem~\ref{thm:unbounded-continuum} excludes this possibility, since \(\mathcal{C}_K(\mu_j)\cap\mathcal{T}_K=\{(0,\mu_j)\}\).

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:positive-dimensional-orbits}
Let \(G\) be a compact Lie group, and let \(K,H\subset G\) be connected closed subgroups. For the action of \(K\) on \(G/H\), one has \(d_K>0\) if and only if \((G/H)^K=\varnothing\), or equivalently, if no conjugate of \(K\) is contained in \(H\).
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Since \(K\) is connected, every orbit is connected and therefore has dimension zero if and only if it consists of a single point. Thus \(d_K=0\) if and only if the action has a fixed point.
A point \(gH\in G/H\) is fixed by \(K\) if and only if \(kgH=gH\) for every \(k\in K\), or equivalently \(g^{-1}Kg\subset H\). Hence a fixed point exists precisely when some conjugate of \(K\) is contained in \(H\).

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:orbit-spaces-interval}
Let \(G\) be a compact connected semisimple Lie group, and let \(K,H\subset G\) be connected closed subgroups. Assume that the action of \(K\times H\) on \(G\), given by \((k,h)\cdot g=kgh^{-1}\), has cohomogeneity one. Then \((G/H)/K\) is homeomorphic to \([0,1]\).
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
By the structure theorem for compact cohomogeneity one actions, see \cite[Section~6.3]{AlexandrinoBettiol2015}, the orbit space \(G/(K\times H)\) is homeomorphic either to a circle or to a closed interval. Suppose that it is homeomorphic to \(S^1\), and let \(\pi:G\to S^1\) be the corresponding orbit projection.

Since \(G\) is path connected and every orbit of \(K\times H\) is connected, it follows from \cite[Corollary~II.6.3]{Bredon} that the induced homomorphism \(\pi_*\colon\pi_1(G,g)\to\pi_1(S^1,\pi(g))\) is surjective. However, \(\pi_1(G,g)\) is finite by Weyl's theorem, see \cite[Theorem~4.69]{KnappLie}, whereas \(\pi_1(S^1,\pi(g))\) is isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z\). This is a contradiction. Thus \(G/(K\times H)\), and hence \((G/H)/K\), is homeomorphic to a closed interval.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{lem:maximal-symmetric-subgroup}
Let \(G\) be a compact connected simple Lie group, and let \(H\subset G\) be a proper connected symmetric subgroup. If \(L\subset G\) is a connected closed subgroup containing \(H\), then either \(L=H\) or \(L=G\).
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:symmetric-positive-dimensional-orbits}
Let \(G\) be a compact connected simple Lie group, and let \(H,K\subset G\) be proper connected symmetric subgroups. For the \(K\)-action on \(G/H\), one has \(d_K>0\) if and only if \(H\) and \(K\) are not conjugate in \(G\).

By Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-dimensional-orbits}, \(d_K=0\) if and only if \(gKg^{-1}\subset H\) for some \(g\in G\). Since \(gKg^{-1}\) is a proper connected symmetric subgroup and \(H\) is connected and proper, Lemma~\ref{lem:maximal-symmetric-subgroup} shows that this inclusion holds only when \(gKg^{-1}=H\). Thus \(d_K=0\) if and only if \(H\) and \(K\) are conjugate.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Let \(H,K\subset G\) satisfy the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetric-positive-dimensional-orbits}, and suppose that they are not conjugate. If the \(K\)-action on \(G/H\) has cohomogeneity one, then \(d_K>0\) and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit-spaces-interval}, its orbit space is a closed interval. <!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:geometric-separation}
Assume that \(G\) acts transitively on \(M\), and let \(K,L\subset G\) be closed subgroups satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-point-separation}
\forall g\in G\colon (H^1(M))^K\cap(H^1(M))^{gLg^{-1}}=(H^1(M))^G.
\end{equation}
If \(\mu>0\) and \((0,\mu)\) is a bifurcation point in both \((H^1(M))^K\times\mathbb{R}\) and \((H^1(M))^L\times\mathbb{R}\), then the corresponding bifurcations are \(G\)-geometrically distinct.

If, in addition, every nontrivial solution in at least one of the components \(\mathcal{C}_K(\mu)\) and \(\mathcal{C}_L(\mu)\) changes sign, then their nontrivial parts are \(G\)-geometrically distinct and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disjointcont}
\bigl(\mathcal{C}_K(\mu)\setminus\mathcal{T}_K\bigr)\cap\bigl(\mathcal{C}_L(\mu)\setminus\mathcal{T}_L\bigr)=\varnothing.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Let \((u,\lambda)\) and \((v,\lambda)\) be nontrivial \(L\)- and \(K\)-invariant solutions, respectively. Suppose that they are \(G\)-geometrically equivalent, so \(v=gu\) for some \(g\in G\). Since \(u\) is \(L\)-invariant, \(v\) is \(gLg^{-1}\)-invariant. By \eqref{eq:fixed-point-separation},
\(
v\in(H^1(M))^K\cap(H^1(M))^{gLg^{-1}}=(H^1(M))^G.
\)
Transitivity of \(G\) implies that \(v\) is constant, and therefore \(u=g^{-1}v\) is constant as well. Writing \(u\equiv c\neq0\) in \eqref{eq:main}, we obtain \(\lambda=-|c|^{p-2}<0\).

Thus no nontrivial \(K\)-invariant solution can be \(G\)-geometrically equivalent to a nontrivial \(L\)-invariant solution at a positive parameter. Since \(\mu>0\), we may choose a neighborhood of \((0,\mu)\) in which every point has positive parameter. This proves that the bifurcations are \(G\)-geometrically distinct.

For the second assertion, any two \(G\)-geometrically equivalent nontrivial solutions in the respective components would be constant, contradicting the sign-changing assumption. Thus their nontrivial parts are \(G\)-geometrically distinct and, in particular, disjoint.
<!-- end proof-16 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
At \(\mu=0\), the constant solutions \(u\equiv c\neq0\), with \(\lambda=-|c|^{p-2}\), converge to \((0,0)\) as \(c\to0\). Since these solutions are \(G\)-invariant, they belong to both \(\mathcal{N}_K\) and \(\mathcal{N}_L\). Thus every neighborhood of \((0,0)\) contains common nontrivial solutions, so the corresponding bifurcations are not \(G\)-geometrically distinct.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:multiplicity-block-actions}
Let \(m\geq4\), and let \(M\) be \(S^{m-1}\), \(\mathbb{C} P^{m-1}\), or \(\mathbb{H} P^{m-1}\), endowed with its standard metric. Let \(G=SO(m)\), \(SU(m)\), or \(Sp(m)\), respectively. Consider the Yamabe-type equation at the critical exponent
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-yamabe}
-\Delta u-\lambda u=|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u,
\end{equation}
where \(n=\dim M\). For every \(j\geq1\), equation \eqref{eq:critical-yamabe} admits at least \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor-1\) unbounded continua bifurcating from \((0,\mu_j)\). If \(M=\mathbb{C} P^{m-1}\) or \(M=\mathbb{H} P^{m-1}\) and \(j\) is even, there are at least \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) such continua. 
Every nontrivial solution belonging to these continua changes sign, and their nontrivial parts are pairwise \(G\)-geometrically distinct and disjoint.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- lemma-14: body_md -->
\label{lem:intersection-block-actions}
Let \(m\geq4\), and call an integer \(a\) admissible if \(2\leq a\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor\). Consider the following actions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(M=S^{m-1}\), \(G=SO(m)\), and \(K_a=SO(a)\times SO(m-a)\);

\item \(M=\mathbb{C} P^{m-1}\), \(G=SU(m)\), and \(K_a=S(U(a)\times U(m-a))\);

\item \(M=\mathbb{H} P^{m-1}\), \(G=Sp(m)\), and \(K_a=Sp(a)\times Sp(m-a)\).
\end{enumerate}
In each case, the subgroups \(K_a\), with \(a\) admissible, satisfy \eqref{eq:fixed-point-separation} pairwise.

In the complex case, the same remains true after adjoining \(SO(m)\) to this family. In the quaternionic case, it remains true after adjoining \(U(m)\), where \(U(m)\subset Sp(m)\) is the subgroup of quaternionic unitary matrices with complex entries.
<!-- end lemma-14 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
For the sphere action with \(m=4\), there is only one admissible block subgroup, so there is nothing to prove. In all remaining cases, \(G\) is simple and all the subgroups under consideration are proper connected symmetric subgroups; see for example \cite[Theorem~B(i)]{Kollross}.

To establish \eqref{eq:fixed-point-separation}, it suffices to prove that \(\overline{\langle K,gLg^{-1}\rangle}=G\) for every pair of distinct subgroups \(K,L\) in each family and every \(g\in G\), since \((H^1(M))^K\cap(H^1(M))^{gLg^{-1}}=(H^1(M))^{\overline{\langle K,gLg^{-1}\rangle}}\). Since \(G\) is simple, Lemma~\ref{lem:maximal-symmetric-subgroup} applies to the subgroups under consideration. We first use it to show that \(\overline{\langle K,gLg^{-1}\rangle}=G\) whenever \(K\) and \(L\) are nonconjugate, and then verify pairwise nonconjugacy within each family.

Suppose that \(K\) and \(L\) are nonconjugate, and fix \(g\in G\). Since \(K\) and \(L\) are connected, \(\overline{\langle K,gLg^{-1}\rangle}\) is a connected closed subgroup containing \(K\). Since \(G\) is simple and \(K\) is a proper connected symmetric subgroup, Lemma~\ref{lem:maximal-symmetric-subgroup} shows that \(\overline{\langle K,gLg^{-1}\rangle}\) equals either \(K\) or \(G\). In the first case, \(gLg^{-1}\subset K\). Applying the same lemma to the symmetric subgroup \(gLg^{-1}\) and the proper connected closed subgroup \(K\), we obtain \(K=gLg^{-1}\), contrary to nonconjugacy. Therefore \(\overline{\langle K,gLg^{-1}\rangle}=G\).

It remains to verify pairwise nonconjugacy within each family. Since \(\dim K_a\) strictly decreases as \(a\) increases in the admissible range, distinct block subgroups are not conjugate. Moreover,
\[
\dim K_a-\dim SO(m)=\frac{(m-2a)^2+m-2}{2}>0
\]
in the complex case, and
\[
\dim K_a-\dim U(m)=(m-2a)^2+m>0
\]
in the quaternionic case. Thus the additional subgroup is not conjugate to any of the corresponding block subgroups. This proves pairwise nonconjugacy and hence \eqref{eq:fixed-point-separation}.
<!-- end proof-17 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
We apply Theorem~\ref{thm:unbounded-continuum} to each subgroup in Lemma~\ref{lem:intersection-block-actions}, and then use Proposition~\ref{prop:geometric-separation} to distinguish the resulting components.

We first verify the geometric assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:unbounded-continuum}. Except for the sphere case \(m=4\), the actions in Lemma~\ref{lem:intersection-block-actions} have cohomogeneity one by \cite[Theorem~B(i)]{Kollross}. For \(m=4\), the principal orbits of \(SO(2)\times SO(2)\) on \(S^3\) are products of two circles, so this action also has cohomogeneity one. In every case, \(G\) is compact, connected, and semisimple, and both the acting subgroup and the isotropy subgroup defining \(M=G/H\) are connected. Hence Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit-spaces-interval} shows that all these actions have interval orbit space.

To apply Theorem~\ref{thm:unbounded-continuum} at the critical exponent, we next show that \(d_K>0\) for every subgroup \(K\) listed in Lemma~\ref{lem:intersection-block-actions}. For the sphere actions, suppose that \((x,y)\in S^{m-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^a\oplus\mathbb{R}^{m-a}\) is fixed by \(K_a=SO(a)\times SO(m-a)\). Then \(Ax=x\) for every \(A\in SO(a)\) and \(By=y\) for every \(B\in SO(m-a)\). Since \(a,m-a\geq2\), the only vector fixed by every rotation in either factor is the zero vector. Hence \(x=y=0\), contradicting \(|x|^2+|y|^2=1\). Thus the action has no fixed points and, since \(K_a\) is connected, Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-dimensional-orbits} gives \(d_{K_a}>0\).

For the projective actions, write \(M=G/H\), where \(G=SU(m)\) and \(H=S(U(1)\times U(m-1))\) in the complex case, while \(G=Sp(m)\) and \(H=Sp(1)\times Sp(m-1)\) in the quaternionic case. In both cases, \(H\) is a proper connected symmetric subgroup of \(G\); see \cite[Theorem~B(i)]{Kollross}. As established in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:intersection-block-actions}, every acting subgroup \(K\) is also proper, connected, and symmetric. Since \(G\) is simple, Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetric-positive-dimensional-orbits} reduces the condition \(d_K>0\) to nonconjugacy of \(K\) and \(H\). This follows from \(\dim K<\dim H\).

Thus, in every case, \(d_K>0\), so the critical exponent \(p=2n/(n-2)\), where \(n=\dim M\), satisfies the exponent condition in Theorem~\ref{thm:unbounded-continuum}. By (E1)--(E3), the positive \(K_a\)-invariant eigenvalues are precisely \(\mu_j\), \(j\geq1\), for every admissible \(a\). Applying the theorem at each of these eigenvalues gives an unbounded component \(\mathcal{C}_{K_a}(\mu_j)\) bifurcating from \((0,\mu_j)\), all of whose nontrivial solutions change sign. In the projective cases, (E2) and (E3) also allow us to apply the theorem to the additional subgroups at every \(\mu_{2r}\), \(r\geq1\). We obtain components \(\mathcal{C}_{SO(m)}(\mu_{2r})\) in the complex case and \(\mathcal{C}_{U(m)}(\mu_{2r})\) in the quaternionic case, with the same properties.

It remains to distinguish and count the components obtained at each \((0,\mu_j)\). The corresponding subgroups satisfy \eqref{eq:fixed-point-separation} pairwise by Lemma~\ref{lem:intersection-block-actions}. Since every nontrivial solution in these components changes sign, Proposition~\ref{prop:geometric-separation} shows that their nontrivial parts are pairwise \(G\)-geometrically distinct and disjoint. There are \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor-1\) admissible block subgroups. In each projective case, the additional subgroup increases this number to \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) when \(j\) is even.

The conclusion of Lemma~\ref{lem:intersection-block-actions} remains valid if \(K_1\) is included among the block subgroups. In each of the three cases, however, \(K_1\) has fixed points, so \(d_{K_1}=0\), and the embedding of \((H^1(M))^{K_1}\) into \(L^{2n/(n-2)}(M)\) is not compact. Thus the compactness argument used in Theorem~\ref{thm:multiplicity-block-actions} does not apply to \(K_1\). For equation \eqref{eq:main} with \(2<p<2n/(n-2)\), the ordinary compact Sobolev embedding allows \(K_1\) to be included. The same argument therefore gives multiplicity bounds larger by one than those established for the critical exponent in Theorem~\ref{thm:multiplicity-block-actions}.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
The same argument also applies to equation \eqref{eq:main} with
supercritical exponents. A subgroup \(K\) considered above gives
an unbounded continuum at each positive \(K\)-invariant eigenvalue
whenever \(n-d_K\leq2\), or whenever
\(2<p<2(n-d_K)/(n-d_K-2)\) if \(n-d_K>2\).
Counting the subgroups satisfying this condition at a common
eigenvalue gives a lower bound on the number of geometrically
distinct continua bifurcating from that eigenvalue.
For the critical equation, it suffices to know that \(d_K>0\);
for supercritical exponents, the admissible range depends on
the value of \(d_K\). These values are computed in
Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-orbit-dimensions} in the Appendix.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
Since the equation is \(G\)-invariant, one might also try to prove unboundedness using equivariant degree. Such an argument was used in \cite{Stefaniak2026}, where subcritical growth provides compactness on the full Sobolev space and allows the use of a torus action and its weights. Even in the subcritical case, however, unboundedness of a component in the full solution space does not imply unboundedness of the components obtained in prescribed fixed-point spaces. Thus an ambient unboundedness result alone would not give the multiplicity of geometrically distinct continua established here.

For critical and supercritical exponents, there is a further difficulty. Compactness is recovered by restricting to \((H^1(M))^K\), which is generally not preserved by \(G\). This space carries an action of \(N_G(K)/K\), where \(N_G(K)\) is the normalizer of \(K\) in \(G\), but this quotient may be finite; see \cite[Proposition~3.10 and Corollary~3.11]{Marzantowicz2019} for orthogonal block subgroups. The argument based on torus weights therefore does not transfer directly to these spaces.

Let \(\mathfrak{g}\), \(\mathfrak{h}\), and \(\mathfrak{l}\) denote the Lie algebras of \(G\), \(H\), and \(L\), respectively, and suppose that \(\mathfrak{h}\neq\mathfrak{l}\). We will show that \(\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{g}\). Since a connected Lie subgroup is determined by its Lie algebra, this will prove the lemma.

Consider the Cartan decomposition \(\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{p}\) associated with the symmetric pair \((\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})\). We have \([\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{p}\); see \cite[Section~5.7]{Gurarie}. Thus \(\mathfrak{p}\), equipped with the action \(\rho(X)Y=[X,Y]\) for \(X\in\mathfrak{h}\) and \(Y\in\mathfrak{p}\), is a representation of \(\mathfrak{h}\). Since \(G\) is compact and \(\mathfrak{g}\) is simple, this representation is irreducible by case~I of \cite[Theorem~VIII.5.3]{Helgason}.

Consider \(\mathfrak{l}\cap\mathfrak{p}\). It is nonzero: choose \(Z\in\mathfrak{l}\setminus\mathfrak{h}\) and write \(Z=X+Y\), where \(X\in\mathfrak{h}\) and \(Y\in\mathfrak{p}\setminus\{0\}\). Since \(\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{l}\), we have \(Y=Z-X\in\mathfrak{l}\cap\mathfrak{p}\). This subspace is also invariant under \(\rho\): if \(X\in\mathfrak{h}\) and \(Y\in\mathfrak{l}\cap\mathfrak{p}\), then \([X,Y]\in\mathfrak{l}\), since \(\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{l}\), and \([X,Y]\in\mathfrak{p}\), since \([\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{p}\). Irreducibility therefore gives \(\mathfrak{l}\cap\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}\). Hence \(\mathfrak{l}\) contains both \(\mathfrak{h}\) and \(\mathfrak{p}\), so \(\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{g}\).

<!-- lemma-15: body_md -->
\label{lem:minimal-orbit-dimensions}
The following cohomogeneity one actions have the indicated smallest orbit dimensions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For the action of \(K=SO(a)\times SO(m-a)\) on \(S^{m-1}\), where \(2\leq a\leq m-2\), one has \(d_K=\min\{a-1,m-a-1\}\).

\item\label{item:complex-block-orbits} For the action of \(K_a=S(U(a)\times U(m-a))\subset SU(m)\) on \(\mathbb{C} P^{m-1}\), where \(m\geq2\) and \(1\leq a\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), one has \(d_{K_a}=2(a-1)\).

\item\label{item:quaternionic-block-orbits} For the action of \(K_a=Sp(a)\times Sp(m-a)\subset Sp(m)\) on \(\mathbb{H} P^{m-1}\), where \(m\geq2\) and \(1\leq a\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), one has \(d_{K_a}=4(a-1)\).

\item For the action of \(SO(m)\subset SU(m)\) on \(\mathbb{C} P^{m-1}\), where \(m\geq4\), one has \(d_{SO(m)}=m-1\).

\item For the action of \(U(m)\subset Sp(m)\) on \(\mathbb{H} P^{m-1}\), where \(m\geq4\), one has \(d_{U(m)}=2m-2\).

\item For the action of \(K=SO(m-1)\) on the oriented real Grassmann manifold \(\mathrm{Gr}_k^+(\mathbb{R}^m)\), induced by its action on \(\mathbb{R}^m\) fixing the last coordinate, where \(2\leq k\leq m-2\), one has \(d_K=\min\{k(m-k-1),(k-1)(m-k)\}\).

\item For the diagonal action of \(K=\{(A,A):A\in SO(m)\}\) on \(S^{m-1}\times S^{m-1}\), where \(m\geq3\), one has \(d_K=m-1\).
\end{enumerate}
All orbits are infinite except when \(a=1\) in cases~\ref{item:complex-block-orbits} and~\ref{item:quaternionic-block-orbits}.
<!-- end lemma-15 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
In each case, the smallest orbit is one of the two nonprincipal orbits.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Let \(x\in S^{a-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^a\) and \(y\in S^{m-a-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m-a}\). The two nonprincipal orbits are \(K(x,0)=S^{a-1}\times\{0\}\) and \(K(0,y)=\{0\}\times S^{m-a-1}\). Their dimensions are \(a-1\) and \(m-a-1\), respectively, so \(d_K=\min\{a-1,m-a-1\}\).

\item Write a point of \(\mathbb{C} P^{m-1}\) as \([x,y]\), where \((x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^a\oplus\mathbb{C}^{m-a}\). The two nonprincipal orbits are \(\{[x,0]:x\in\mathbb{C}^a\setminus\{0\}\}\) and \(\{[0,y]:y\in\mathbb{C}^{m-a}\setminus\{0\}\}\). They are diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb{C} P^{a-1}\) and \(\mathbb{C} P^{m-a-1}\), respectively, and hence have dimensions \(2(a-1)\) and \(2(m-a-1)\). Since \(a\leq m-a\), it follows that \(d_{K_a}=2(a-1)\).

\item The quaternionic case follows in the same way, with \(\mathbb{C}\) replaced by \(\mathbb{H}\). The two nonprincipal orbits are diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb{H} P^{a-1}\) and \(\mathbb{H} P^{m-a-1}\), and hence have dimensions \(4(a-1)\) and \(4(m-a-1)\). Since \(a\leq m-a\), it follows that \(d_{K_a}=4(a-1)\).

\item By \cite[Table~F]{GroWilZil}, the two nonprincipal orbits of the \(SO(m)\)-action on \(\mathbb{C} P^{m-1}\) are \(SO(m)/(SO(2)\times SO(m-2))\) and \(SO(m)/O(m-1)\). Their dimensions are \(2m-4\) and \(m-1\), respectively. For \(m\geq4\), this gives \(d_{SO(m)}=m-1\).

\item By \cite[Table~F]{GroWilZil}, the two nonprincipal orbits of the \(U(m)\)-action on \(\mathbb{H} P^{m-1}\) are \(U(m)/(SU(2)\times U(m-2))\) and \(U(m)/(U(1)\times U(m-1))\). Their dimensions are \(4m-7\) and \(2m-2\), respectively. For \(m\geq4\), this gives \(d_{U(m)}=2m-2\).

\item The group \(K=SO(m-1)\) fixes \(e_m\) and preserves \(e_m^\perp\). One nonprincipal orbit consists of the oriented \(k\)-planes contained in \(e_m^\perp\). It is diffeomorphic to \(\mathrm{Gr}_k^+(\mathbb{R}^{m-1})\) and has dimension \(k(m-k-1)\).

The other nonprincipal orbit consists of the oriented \(k\)-planes containing \(e_m\). Intersecting such a plane with \(e_m^\perp\) identifies this orbit with \(\mathrm{Gr}_{k-1}^+(\mathbb{R}^{m-1})\), where the orientation of the intersection is determined using \(e_m\). Its dimension is \((k-1)(m-k)\). Therefore \(d_K=\min\{k(m-k-1),(k-1)(m-k)\}\).

\item Two pairs \((x,y)\) and \((x',y')\) belong to the same diagonal orbit if and only if \(\langle x,y\rangle=\langle x',y'\rangle\). Thus the map \(f\colon(S^{m-1}\times S^{m-1})/K\to[-1,1]\), defined by \(f(K(x,y))=\langle x,y\rangle\), identifies the orbit space with \([-1,1]\). The nonprincipal orbits corresponding to the endpoints are \(\{(x,x):x\in S^{m-1}\}\) and \(\{(x,-x):x\in S^{m-1}\}\). Both are diffeomorphic to \(S^{m-1}\), so \(d_K=m-1\).
\end{enumerate}

Since the acting groups are compact, all their orbits are infinite if and only if \(d_K>0\). The remaining assertions follow from the formulas above.
