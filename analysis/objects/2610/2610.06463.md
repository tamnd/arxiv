<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{D-02}
\rm
A \textit{weak almost contact metric structure} on a differentiable connected manifold $M^{2n+1}$ is a set $(\varphi,Q,\xi$, $\eta,g)$, where $g$ is a Riemannian metric on $M$, $\varphi$ is a skew-symmetric (1,1)-tensor of rank $2n$, $\xi$ is a unit (Reeb) vector field, $\eta$ is a 1-form such that ${\eta}({\xi})=1$, satisfying
\begin{align}\label{2.2}
 & g({\varphi} X,{\varphi} Y)= g(X,Q\,Y)
 -{\eta}(X)\,{\eta}(Y)
                                  
 \qquad (X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_M),
\end{align}
and $Q:=-{\varphi}^2 + {\eta}\otimes {\xi}$ is a self-adjoint (1,1)-tensor field
                          
(and $Q=I$ for almost contact metric manifolds).
A~\textit{weak contact metric $($w.c.m.$)$ structure} is a weak almost contact metric structure satisfying
\begin{align}\label{2.2b}
                                         
                             
 d\eta(X,Y)=g(X,\varphi Y).
                               
\end{align}
                                                                                 
                                                                   
A w.c.m. structure is called a \textit{weak $K$-contact structure} if the
               
vector field ${\xi}$ is Killing,~i.e., ${\cal L}_{\xi}\,g=0$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{D-002}\rm
An $n$-dimensional distribution $D$ on a contact manifold $(M^{2n+1},\eta)$ is called a \textit{Legendrian distribution} if $d\eta(X,Y)=0$ for all vectors $X,Y$ in $D$. When the distribution $D$ is involutive, it defines a \textit{Legendrian foliation} on $(M^{2n+1},\eta)$.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          
Two Legendrian distributions $D$ and $D'$, or, foliations ${\cal F}$ and ${\cal F}'$,
of a w.c.m. manifold $M^{2n+1}(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)$
are said to be \textit{conjugate} if $\varphi(D')=D$,
or $\varphi(T{\cal F}')=T{\cal F}$, respectively,
and the tangent bundle splits as the orthogonal sum
$TM=D\oplus D'\oplus\{\xi\}$. Conjugate distributions or foliations form a pair known as a \textit{bi-Legendrian structure} on $M^{2n+1}$.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
On a w.c.m. manifold, the tensor $h$ and its conjugate $(1,1)$-tensor $h^*$ satisfy, see \cite{rov-119},
\begin{align}
\label{E-31}
 h-h^* & =  \nabla_{\xi}\,\varphi ,\\
 \label{E-31A}
 h\varphi+\varphi h &= -\tfrac12 \mathcal{L}_\xi\,Q
 ,\\
 \label{E-31B}
 h Q - Q h &= \tfrac12\,[\varphi, \mathcal{L}_\xi\,Q], \\
\label{E-30}
 Q\,\nabla \xi &= -\varphi(Q + h^*).
                                     
\end{align}
                                              
By conditions, \eqref{E-31A} and \eqref{E-31B}, $h$ anti-commutes with $\varphi$ and commutes with $Q$. The~same is true for $h^*$.
Since $Q$ is a self-adjoint positive definite (1,1)-tensor, the tensor $h$ is also self-adjoint, that is, $h=h^*$. Hence, \eqref{E-31} gives
$\nabla_{\xi}\,\varphi=0$.
By \eqref{E-30} and \eqref{P-6}, we have
               
 $\nabla \xi= - \varphi - \varphi\, Q^{-1} h^*$.
             
From this, $h=h^*$ we obtain \eqref{E-xi-n}.
Using \eqref{E-xi-n}, we get $\nabla_{\xi}\,{Q}=0$:
\begin{align*}
 0 = (\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\,{Q})X
 = [\xi, QX] - Q[\xi, X]
 = (\nabla_{\xi}\,{Q})X +Q\nabla_X\,\xi
 -\nabla_{QX}\,\xi
  = (\nabla_{\xi}\,{Q})X.
\end{align*}
If $h X=\lambda X$, then using $h\varphi = - \varphi h$
(by assumptions and \eqref{E-31A}),
we get $h \varphi X=-\lambda \varphi X$.
Thus, if $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $h$, then $-\lambda$ is also an eigenvalue of $h$; hence, ${\rm trace}\,h=0$.
By the above and Proposition~\ref{thm6.2},
a w.c.m. manifold is weak K-contact if and only if \eqref{E-xi-nK} is true.

$(i)$ From the $(\kappa,\mu)$-nullity condition
\eqref{E-k-mu-nullity}, we get
 $QR_{\,\xi, X}\, \xi=\kappa\,Q\varphi^2X-\mu\, QhX$
 and  $\varphi R_{\,\xi,\,\varphi X}\,\xi =
-\kappa\,Q\,\varphi^2 X -\mu\,QhX$.
Combining these two equations, we acquire
\begin{align}\label{E-31a}
 QR_{\,\xi, X}\,\xi
 -\varphi R_{\,\xi,\,\varphi X}\,\xi
 = 2\kappa\,Q\varphi^2 X.
\end{align}
Comparing \eqref{E-31a} with \eqref{E-37}
where \eqref{Eq-2assump} is used, we get
$h^2X =(1-\kappa)\big\{Q^2X -\eta(X) \xi\big\}$,
hence
                             
$\widetilde h^{\,2}X=(1-\kappa)X\ (X\in\ker\eta)$.
             

Since $\widetilde h=Q^{-1}h$ is self-adjoint,
$\widetilde h^{\,2}$ is non-negative definite; hence
$\kappa\le 1$ holds. Since $\widetilde{h}\,\xi=0$ is true, we have $\ker\widetilde h=\{\xi\}$ and $\ker\eta={\cal D}_+ \oplus {\cal D}_-$,
where ${\cal D}_\pm+$ are the eigen-distributions for the eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt{1-\kappa}$.
                        
                
Using $\varphi h = -h \varphi$ and $\varphi Q = Q \varphi$, we obtain
