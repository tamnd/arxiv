\label{prop:radial-band-cue}
	Let $Q$ satisfy the regular analytic assumptions of
	Section~\ref{sec:theorem}. We fix $M$ and introduce  $\varphi$ as the normalized
	exterior conformal map of $D$.  In a fixed collar of $\partial D$ where
	$\varphi$ extends conformally, we set $\Theta(z)=\arg\varphi(z)$ and
	extend $\Theta$ measurably elsewhere.
	Let us label the points of $Z^{\mathrm{eq}}_{N,M}$ exchangeably and set
	$\theta_a=\Theta(z_a)$. Then, as $N\to\infty$, the joint law of the labelled
	angles converges in total variation
	to the CUE eigenangle density of Haar-distributed unitary matrices

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:polynomial}
	For every distinct $Y$,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-space}
		\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_Y^\perp=q_Y\operatorname{Poly}_{<M},
		\qquad q_Y(z)=\prod_{i=1}^N(z-y_i).
	\end{equation}
	Let $G_Y$ be the $M\times M$ Gram matrix of the ordered basis
	$q_Y,q_Yz,\ldots,q_Yz^{M-1}$ in $L^2(\lambda_N)$.  Conditional on $Y$,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-density}
		\mathbb P(dZ\mid Y)=
		\frac{|V(Z)|^2\prod_{a=1}^M|q_Y(z_a)|^2d\lambda_N(z_a)}
		{M!\det G_Y}.
	\end{equation}
	For $M=1$, this becomes
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-particle}
		\mathbb P(dz\mid Y)=
		\frac{|q_Y(z)|^2e^{-2NQ(z)}dA(z)}
		{\int_{\mathbb C}|q_Y(w)|^2e^{-2NQ(w)}dA(w)}.
	\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
A polynomial in $F$, where $F=\operatorname{Poly}_{<N+M}$, is orthogonal to every $K_F(\cdot,y_i)$ precisely when
	it vanishes at every $y_i$.  Polynomial division gives
	\eqref{eq:q-space}, and for an arbitrary basis
	$f_0,\ldots,f_{M-1}$, the projection-DPP density reads
	$|\det[f_{j-1}(z_a)]|^2/(M!\det[\langle f_i,f_j\rangle])$.
	Upon taking $f_j=q_Yz^j$, we obtain exactly \eqref{eq:conditional-density}, and its rank-one
	case reduces to \eqref{eq:one-particle}.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:band-harmonic-measure}
	Suppose $Q$ satisfies the regular analytic assumptions of
	Section~\ref{sec:theorem}, and let $M=M_N\geq1$ satisfy $M/N\to0$.
	Then, $\mu^{\mathrm{eq}}_{N,M}$ converges weakly to $\omega_D$.
	If also $M\to\infty$, the empirical measure
	$\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{eq}}_{N,M}$ converges weakly to $\omega_D$
	in probability.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let us write $\nu_{j,N}=|\phi_{j,N}|^2d\lambda_N$ and set $t_j=j/N$.
	The uniform $L^2$ orbital expansion of
	\cite[Theorem~3.2]{HedenmalmWennman}, together with its leading
	coefficient \cite[Theorem~1.5]{HedenmalmWennman}, applies for
	$t_j$ in a fixed neighborhood of $1$.  The $L^2$ error gives an $o(1)$
	error for bounded test functions,
	uniformly in $t_j$.  In the exterior conformal coordinate for
	$D_{t_j}$, the leading weighted density is a Gaussian ridge of
	width $O(N^{-1/2})$ about the unit circle.  Uniform radial Laplace
	integration with the explicit leading coefficient gives
	$d\theta/(2\pi)$, while the mass outside a fixed boundary collar
	is negligible.  Thus,
	for every bounded continuous $f$,
	\[
		\sup_{N\leq j<N+M}\left|
		\int f\,d\nu_{j,N}-\int f\,d\omega_{D_{t_j}}
		\right|\longrightarrow0.
	\]
	Here $\sup_{N\leq j<N+M}|t_j-1|\leq M/N\to0$.  The smooth
	droplet flow makes $\omega_{D_{t_j}}\Rightarrow\omega_D$
	uniformly over this range.  Averaging $\nu_{j,N}$ and using
	\eqref{eq:equilibrium-band-mean} proves the mean-measure assertion.

	For real bounded $f$, the rank-$M$ projection-DPP variance identity
	gives
	\[
		\operatorname{Var}\left(\int f\,d\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{eq}}_{N,M}\right)
		\leq\frac{\|f\|_\infty^2}{M}.
	\]
	As $M\to\infty$, Chebyshev's inequality shows that each sampled average
	concentrates around its mean.  Since the mean converges to the
	harmonic-measure average, the empirical measure converges to $\omega_D$
	in probability.  Here weak convergence in probability means that
	$\int f\,d\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{eq}}_{N,M}\to\int f\,d\omega_D$ in
	probability for each bounded continuous $f$.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:quenched}
	Let $Q$ satisfy the preceding regular analytic assumptions, and let
	$M=M_N$ with
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesoscopic-regime}
		\frac{M_N}{\log N}\longrightarrow\infty,
		\qquad \frac{M_N}{N}\longrightarrow0.
	\end{equation}
	By $Y=\{y_1,\ldots,y_N\}$ we denote the eigenvalues of the rank-$N$
	normal-matrix ensemble with weight $e^{-2NQ}dA$. With $Y$ held
	fixed, let $Z=\{z_1,\ldots,z_M\}$ be the $M$ added eigenvalues of
	quenched growth, that is, the $\beta=2$ Coulomb gas in the field of the
	frozen eigenvalues,
	\[
		\mathbb P(dZ\mid Y)\ \propto\
		\prod_{a<b}|z_a-z_b|^2
		\prod_{a=1}^{M}\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{N}|z_a-y_i|^2\Bigr)
		e^{-2NQ(z_a)}\,dA(z_a),
	\]
	as in \eqref{eq:conditional-density}.  Then the empirical measure
	$\widehat\mu_Z=M^{-1}\sum_{a=1}^{M}\delta_{z_a}$ converges weakly to
	$\omega_D$, conditionally on $Y$, outside a set of frozen spectra whose
	$\mathbb P_N^{\mathrm{old}}$-probability tends to zero.  More precisely, for
	every bounded continuous $f\colon\mathbb C\to\mathbb R$ and every
	$\eta,\varepsilon>0$,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:quenched-convergence}
		\mathbb P_N^{\mathrm{old}}\biggl\{
		\mathbb P\biggl(
		\Bigl|\frac1M\sum_{a=1}^{M}f(z_a)
		-\int_{\partial D}f\,d\omega_D\Bigr|>\eta
		\ \Bigm|\ Y\biggr)>\varepsilon\biggr\}
		\longrightarrow0.
	\end{equation}
	In particular, the conditional one-point density of the added
	eigenvalues, divided by $M$, converges weakly to $\omega_D$ in
	$\mathbb P_N^{\mathrm{old}}$-probability, and so does its average over the
	frozen spectrum.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Conditionally on $Y$, the added eigenvalues are $M$ unit charges of a
	two-dimensional $\beta=2$ Coulomb gas in the external potential
	$2NQ(z)-2\sum_{i}\log|z-y_i|$.  In the mean-field approximation this

	therefore see a conductor with equilibrium charge distribution
	$\omega_D$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:quenched} proves this harmonic-measure law

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:faber-fluctuations}
	Let $\Pi_Y^\perp$ be the conditional projection, with range
	$q_Y\operatorname{Poly}_{<M}$ in $L^2(\lambda_N)$, and suppose the sampling region is
	contained in $\{|z|\le R\}$.  For every polynomial $p$ of degree
	$d<M$,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:polynomial-variance-bound}
		\operatorname{Var}\!\left[
			\frac1M\sum_{z\in Z}p(z)\,\middle|\,Y
			\right]
		\le \frac{d}{M^2}\|p\|_{L^\infty(|z|\le R)}^2.
	\end{equation}
	Consequently, for $M>6$,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:faber-fluctuation-bound}
		\mathbb E\!\left[
			\sum_{n=1}^6\frac1{n^2}
			\left|\int F_n\,d\left(
			\widehat\mu_Z-\mathbb E^Y_{N,M}[\widehat\mu_Z]
			\right)\right|^2\,\middle|\,Y
			\right]
		\le\frac1{M^2}\sum_{n=1}^6
		\frac{\|F_n\|_{L^\infty(|z|\le R)}^2}{n}.
	\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Choose an orthonormal basis $\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_M$ of
	$q_Y\operatorname{Poly}_{<M}$ so that the first $M-d$ elements span
	$q_Y\operatorname{Poly}_{<M-d}$.  Multiplying any of these first elements by
	$p$ gives a polynomial still in $q_Y\operatorname{Poly}_{<M}$, so
	\[
		(1-\Pi_Y^\perp)(p\psi_j)=0,\qquad j\le M-d.
	\]
	Only the last $d$ basis elements therefore contribute to the
	conditional variance.  The variance formula for a projection
	process gives
	\[
		\operatorname{Var}\!\left[
			\frac1M\sum_{z\in Z}p(z)\,\middle|\,Y
			\right]
		=\frac1{M^2}\sum_{j=M-d+1}^{M}
		\|(1-\Pi_Y^\perp)(p\psi_j)\|_{L^2(\lambda_N)}^2.
	\]
	An orthogonal projection cannot increase the norm, and
	$\|\psi_j\|_{L^2(\lambda_N)}=1$.  Each summand is thus at most
	$\|p\|_{L^\infty(|z|\le R)}^2$, proving
	\eqref{eq:polynomial-variance-bound}.
	Applying this bound to $F_n$, which has degree $n$, and summing
	with weights $1/n^2$ gives \eqref{eq:faber-fluctuation-bound}.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:screening}
	Let $Y$ be distinct, and let $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_N\in\operatorname{Ran}P_Y$
	satisfy $\ell_i(y_j)=\delta_{ij}$.  Let $p$ be a polynomial of degree $d$
	and assume $\operatorname{Poly}_{<N+M+d}\subset L^2(\lambda_N)$, as holds for the
	bounded hard-wall sampling region.  Then
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:screening}
		M\!\int p\,d\bigl(\mathbb E^Y_{N,M}[\widehat\mu_Z]-\mu^{\mathrm{eq}}_{N,M}\bigr)
		=-\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^Np(y_i)-\mathbb E_N^{\mathrm{old}}\sum_{i=1}^Np(y_i)\Bigr)
		+\mathcal R_p(Y),
	\end{equation}
	where
	\[
		\mathcal R_p(Y)=\sum_{i=1}^N\bigl[(1-\Pi_F)(p\,\ell_i)\bigr](y_i),
	\]
	and $(1-\Pi_F)(p\,\ell_i)$ lies in the span of
	$\phi_{N+M,N},\ldots,\phi_{N+M+d-1,N}$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
A polynomial in $\operatorname{Ran}P_Y$ that vanishes on $Y$ must be zero.
	Evaluation is therefore invertible on this $N$-dimensional space, so the
	interpolation polynomials $\ell_i$ exist.
	For $f\in F$, the polynomial $f-\sum_if(y_i)\ell_i$
	lies in $F$ and vanishes on $Y$, hence is orthogonal to
	$\operatorname{Ran}P_Y$.  Thus $P_Yg=\sum_i\langle g,K_F(\cdot,y_i)\rangle\ell_i$
	for $g\in L^2(\lambda_N)$, and
	\[
		\operatorname{Tr}(pP_Y)=\sum_i\langle p\,\ell_i,K_F(\cdot,y_i)\rangle
		=\sum_i\bigl(\Pi_F(p\,\ell_i)\bigr)(y_i)
		=\sum_ip(y_i)-\mathcal R_p(Y).
	\]
	Since $\operatorname{Tr}(p\,\Pi_H)=\mathbb E_N^{\mathrm{old}}\sum_ip(y_i)$, integrating $p$
	against \eqref{eq:vacancy-decomposition} gives \eqref{eq:screening}.
	Finally, $p\,\ell_i\in\operatorname{Poly}_{<N+M+d}$.

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $u_{k,N}=\phi_{N+k,N}e^{-NQ}$, $0\le k<M$, which are
	orthonormal in $L^2(dA)$.  Choose a fixed collar $U$ of $\partial D$
	on which the exterior maps $\varphi_t$ and the holomorphic boundary
	functions $\mathcal Q_t$ extend for $t$ near $1$.  Locally, with a branch
	of the logarithm, we put
	\[
		\mathcal F_t=t\log\varphi_t+\mathcal Q_t.
	\]
	On the exterior, $\operatorname{Re}\mathcal F_t=\widehat Q_t$ by the obstacle identity
	in Section~\ref{sec:proof}. Inside the collar it denotes the harmonic
	continuation of that exterior solution.  With $U_t$ and $C_t$ as in
	Section~\ref{sec:proof}, the weighted growth law
	\eqref{eq:rule-a-drift} gives, for exterior $z$,
	\[
		\partial_t U_t(z)=\int\log|z-w|\,d\omega_{D_t}(w)
		=\log r_t+\log|\varphi_t(z)|.
	\]
	The contact identity gives $C_t'=-\log r_t$, as in the derivation
	of \eqref{eq:norm-derivative}.  Hence,
	$\partial_t\operatorname{Re}\mathcal F_t=\log|\varphi_t|$.  Holomorphicity and the
	normalization at infinity then give, locally on $U$,
	\[
		\partial_t \mathcal F_t=\log\varphi_t.
	\]

	The uniform planar orthogonal-polynomial expansion
	\cite[Theorems~1.3 and 3.2]{HedenmalmWennman} has leading term
	\[
		u_{k,N}(z)=N^{1/4}\sqrt{\varphi_{t_k}'(z)}\,
		\mathcal B_{0,t_k}(z)\exp\{N(\mathcal F_{t_k}(z)-Q(z))\}
		\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr),
		\qquad t_k=1+k/N,
	\]
	uniformly in a shrinking boundary collar, for each fixed $k$.
	The leading factor $\mathcal B_{0,t}$ is nonzero and varies smoothly with $t$
	\cite[Theorem~1.5]{HedenmalmWennman}.  The identity
	$\partial_t \mathcal F_t=\log\varphi_t$ and smoothness of the flow give
	$N(\mathcal F_{t_k}-\mathcal F_1)=k\log\varphi_1+O(N^{-1})$ uniformly there.
	The $L^2$ expansion and the normal Gaussian localization give, with
	$\varphi=\varphi_1$ and a smooth cutoff $\chi$ supported in $U$ and
	equal to one near $\partial D$,
	\[
		\|u_{k,N}-\chi\varphi^k u_{0,N}\|_{L^2(dA)}\longrightarrow0.
	\]
	The orbitals have asymptotically all their mass where
	$|\varphi|=1+o(1)$.  Since $M$ is fixed, replacing $\varphi^k$ by
	$e^{ik\Theta}$ gives
	\[
		\|u_{k,N}-v_{k,N}\|_{L^2(dA)}\longrightarrow0,
		\qquad v_{k,N}=\chi u_{0,N}e^{ik\Theta},\quad 0\le k<M.
	\]

	Let $m_N(\theta)d\theta$ be the conformal-angle marginal of
	$|\chi u_{0,N}|^2dA$.  The leading coefficient in
	\cite[Theorem~1.5]{HedenmalmWennman} has squared modulus
	$\pi^{-1/2}\sqrt{\Delta Q}$ on $\partial D$.  At a boundary point
	$p$, with signed normal coordinate $x_\perp$,
	$Q-\operatorname{Re}\mathcal F_1=2\Delta Q(p)x_\perp^2+O(x_\perp^3)$.
	Thus, uniformly in $p$ for bounded $\sqrt N x_\perp$,
	\[
		|u_{0,N}(p+x_\perp\mathbf n_p)|^2dA
		=(1+o(1))\frac{\sqrt N}{\pi^{3/2}}
		|\varphi'(p)|\sqrt{\Delta Q(p)}
		e^{-4N\Delta Q(p)x_\perp^2}\,dx_\perp\,d\ell(p).
	\]
	The $L^2$ expansion controls the integrated tails outside this normal
	scale.  Integration in $x_\perp$ cancels the factor $\sqrt{\Delta Q(p)}$.
	Using $|\varphi'(p)|\,d\ell(p)=d\theta$ we obtain
	\[
		\left\|m_N-\frac1{2\pi}\right\|_{L^1([0,2\pi])}
		\longrightarrow0.
	\]

	The band density relative to $dA^{\otimes M}$ is
	$M!^{-1}|\det[u_{k,N}(z_a)]_{k=0}^{M-1}{}_{a=1}^{M}|^2$.
	By multilinearity and the $L^2$ comparison of adjacent orbitals,
	replacing $u_{k,N}$ by $v_{k,N}$ in the determinant changes the
	normalized wave function by $o(1)$ in $L^2(dA^{\otimes M})$.
	The corresponding densities therefore differ by $o(1)$ in $L^1$.
	After integrating normal coordinates, the latter angular density is
	\[
		\frac1{M!}
		\left|\det[e^{ik\theta_a}]_{k=0}^{M-1}{}_{a=1}^{M}\right|^2
		\prod_{a=1}^M m_N(\theta_a).
	\]
	The angular marginal limit and the Vandermonde determinant formula
	show $L^1$, hence total-variation, convergence to
	\eqref{eq:radial-band-cue}.\qedhere
