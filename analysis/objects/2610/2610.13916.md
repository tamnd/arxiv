\label{prop:ipm-comparison-relaxation}
Let \(P\) be a Markov kernel with invariant probability measure \(\pi\), and let \(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_1,\mathcal{F}_2\) be nonempty classes of bounded measurable real-valued functions on \(\mathcal{X}\). The corresponding IPMs are allowed to take the value \(+\infty\).

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}]
If \(\mathcal{F}_1\subseteq\Lambda\mathcal{F}_2+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}\) for some \(\Lambda>0\), then
\begin{equation}
d_{\mathcal{F}_1}(\mu,\nu)
\leq \Lambda d_{\mathcal{F}_2}(\mu,\nu),
\qquad \mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}).
\label{eq:ipm-comparison-scaled}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}d_{\mathcal{F}_2}\bigl(P^n(x,\cdot),\pi\bigr)
\leq C\theta^n,
\qquad n \ge 0,
\]
for some \(0\leq C<\infty\) and \(\theta\in[0,1)\), then
\begin{equation}
\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}d_{\mathcal{F}_1}\bigl(P^n(x,\cdot),\pi\bigr)
\leq \Lambda C\theta^n,
\qquad n \ge 0.
\label{eq:weaker-ipm-uniform-convergence}
\end{equation}

\item[\textup{(ii)}]
For every \(n \ge 0\),
\begin{equation}
\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})}d_{\mathcal{F}}(\mu P^n,\pi)
= \sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}d_{\mathcal{F}}\bigl(P^n(x,\cdot),\pi\bigr).
\label{eq:ipm-uniform-initial-distributions}
\end{equation}

\item[\textup{(iii)}]
Suppose that
\[
R_{\mathcal{F}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}d_{\mathcal{F}}(\delta_x,\pi)<\infty
\]
and, for some \(\theta\in[0,1)\),
\begin{equation}
d_{\mathcal{F}}(\mu P,\pi)
\leq\theta d_{\mathcal{F}}(\mu,\pi),
\qquad \mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}).
\label{eq:ipm-one-step-toward-invariant}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}d_{\mathcal{F}}\bigl(P^n(x,\cdot),\pi\bigr)
\leq R_{\mathcal{F}}\theta^n,
\qquad n \ge 0.
\label{eq:ipm-contraction-uniform-relaxation}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For \textup{(i)}, every \(f\in\mathcal{F}_1\) can be written as \(f=\Lambda g+c\mathbf{1}\), with \(g\in\mathcal{F}_2\) and \(c\in\mathbb{R}\). Since probability measures have equal total mass,
\begin{equation}
\left| (\mu-\nu)(f) \right|
=\Lambda\left| (\mu-\nu)(g) \right|
\leq\Lambda d_{\mathcal{F}_2}(\mu,\nu).
\end{equation}
Taking the supremum over \(f\in\mathcal{F}_1\) proves \eqref{eq:ipm-comparison-scaled}, from which \eqref{eq:weaker-ipm-uniform-convergence} follows immediately. For \textup{(ii)}, fix \(\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})\) and \(f\in\mathcal{F}\). By the definition of \(\mu P^n\),
\begin{equation}
\left| (\mu P^n-\pi)(f) \right|
= \left| \int \bigl(P^n(x,\cdot)-\pi\bigr)(f)\,\mu(dx) \right|
\leq \sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}d_{\mathcal{F}}\bigl(P^n(x,\cdot),\pi\bigr).
\end{equation}
Taking the supremum over \(f\) and then over \(\mu\) gives one inequality. The reverse inequality follows by choosing \(\mu=\delta_x\). Finally, iterating \eqref{eq:ipm-one-step-toward-invariant} from \(\delta_x\) gives
\begin{equation}
d_{\mathcal{F}}\bigl(P^n(x,\cdot),\pi\bigr)
\leq\theta^n d_{\mathcal{F}}(\delta_x,\pi)
\leq R_{\mathcal{F}}\theta^n.
\end{equation}
Taking the supremum over \(x\) proves \textup{(iii)}.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The qualification preceding~\eqref{eq:d-b0-sup} is essential. For a general Banach space of measurable functions, a probability measure need not define a continuous functional on \(\mathcal{B}_0\); in that case, the supremum in~\eqref{eq:d-b0-sup} may be infinite. The natural domain of this IPM is therefore
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}_0}(\mathcal{X})
:= \left\{ \mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}): f\mapsto\mu(f) \text{ defines an element of }\mathcal{B}_0^\ast \right\}.
\end{equation}
For \(\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}_0}(\mathcal{X})\),
\begin{equation}
d_{\mathcal{B}_0}(\mu,\nu)
= \| (\mu-\nu)|_{\mathcal{B}_0} \|_{\mathcal{B}_0^\ast}
<\infty.
\end{equation}
A simple sufficient condition ensuring \(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}_0}(\mathcal{X})=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})\) is the continuous embedding
\begin{equation}
\| f \|_\infty
\leq C_{\mathcal{B}}\| f \|_{\mathcal{B}},
\qquad f\in\mathcal{B}_0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, this gives \(| \mu(f) |\leq C_{\mathcal{B}}\| f \|_{\mathcal{B}}\) for every probability measure \(\mu\).

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
By Proposition~\ref{prop:ipm-comparison-relaxation}\textup{(i)}, the inclusion \(\mathcal{F}_1\subseteq\Lambda\mathcal{F}_2+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}\) transfers uniform geometric convergence toward \(\pi\) from \(d_{\mathcal{F}_2}\) to \(d_{\mathcal{F}_1}\). Importantly, this comparison alone does not transfer one-step contraction: even if \(d_{\mathcal{F}_2}\) contracts with factor \(\rho<1\) as in~\eqref{eq:dual-ipm-contraction}, it gives only
\begin{equation}
d_{\mathcal{F}_1}(\mu P,\nu P)
\leq \Lambda \rho d_{\mathcal{F}_2}(\mu,\nu),
\end{equation}
whose right-hand side is expressed in the stronger IPM.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Condition~\eqref{eq:spectral-gap-def} automatically implies \(\mathrm{Fix}(P)=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{1}\}\). Indeed, if \(Pf=f\), then \(g:=f-\pi(f)\mathbf{1}\in L_0^2(\pi)\) satisfies \(Pg=g\), and hence
\begin{equation}
\| g \|_\pi
=\| P_0g \|_\pi
\leq \rho\| g \|_\pi.
\end{equation}
Since \(\rho<1\), it follows that \(g=0\). Consequently, in the notation introduced above,
\begin{equation}
\Pi f=\pi(f)\mathbf{1},
\qquad \mathcal{B}_0=L_0^2(\pi).
\end{equation}

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
Throughout the paper, the term \emph{relaxation} is used in the statistical-physics sense: a system displaced from equilibrium \emph{relaxes} back to it, and the relaxation time is the characteristic timescale of that return. For Markov chains, the term is standard~\citep[Chapter~12]{levin2017markov} for describing the action of \( P \) on centered functions \( f \), as in~\eqref{eq:banach-space-contraction}: any fluctuation around \( \pi(f) \) is damped at a geometrical rate. We reserve \emph{contraction} for the one-step estimate itself and \emph{relaxation} for the resulting geometric decay over \( n \) steps.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
We say Markov operator \( P \) is \( \pi \)-\emph{reversible} if it is self-adjoint on \( L^2(\pi) \), that is, \( \langle f, Pg \rangle_\pi = \langle Pf, g \rangle_\pi \) for \( f,g \in L^2(\pi) \). In such case, the operator \( P_0 \) is also self-adjoint, so \( \sigma(P_0) \subset [-1,1] \) and \( \vert\!\vert\!\vert P_0 \vert\!\vert\!\vert = \sup\{ | \lambda | : \lambda \in \sigma(P_0) \} \); hence \( 1 - \rho \) is the classical absolute spectral gap.
Without reversibility, \( P_0 \) need not be normal and its spectrum may contain non-real points. Moreover, \( \vert\!\vert\!\vert P_0 \vert\!\vert\!\vert \) is only bounded below by the spectral radius \( r(P_0) = \lim_{n} \vert\!\vert\!\vert P_0^n \vert\!\vert\!\vert^{1/n} \), so \( 1 - \rho \) is a possibly smaller operator-norm gap. We keep the name, following~\citet{fan2021hoeffding}.
Complex scalars play no further role: our arguments use only operator norms, never the spectrum, and for a bounded operator \( A \) on a real Hilbert space it holds that \( \vert\!\vert\!\vert A_\mathbb{C} \vert\!\vert\!\vert = \vert\!\vert\!\vert A \vert\!\vert\!\vert \). We refer to~\citet[Chapter~12]{levin2017markov} for the classical reversible setting and its relation to the relaxation time.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
It may be tempting to interpret the spectral gap as contraction in the IPM dual to \(L_0^2(\pi)\). This IPM, however, has a restricted domain: if \(\mu,\nu\ll\pi\) have \(L^2(\pi)\)-densities, then
\begin{equation}
d_{2,\pi}(\mu,\nu)
:=\sup_{\substack{f\in L_0^2(\pi)\\ \| f \|_\pi\leq1}} \left| \mu(f)-\nu(f) \right|
= \left\| \frac{d\mu}{d\pi}-\frac{d\nu}{d\pi} \right\|_\pi.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
d_{2,\pi}(\mu,\pi)^2
= \int\left(\frac{d\mu}{d\pi}-1\right)^2d\pi
=: \chi^2(\mu\Vert\pi),
\end{equation}
is the so-called \( \chi^2 \)-divergence. This geometry does not generally allow to  include Dirac measures and is therefore unsuitable for measuring convergence from arbitrary initializations. For this reason, the \( L^2(\pi) \) spectral gap enters our analysis only as a decay bound
\begin{equation}
\| P^n g \|_\pi
\leq \rho^n\| g \|_\pi,
\qquad g\in L_0^2(\pi),
\end{equation}
and not as a contraction in the \( L^2(\pi) \)-induced geometry on measures.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:gap-transfer-on-bochner}
Let \(\mathcal{H}\) be a separable Hilbert space, and let \(P_{\mathcal{H}}:L^2(\pi;\mathcal{H})\to L^2(\pi;\mathcal{H})\) denote the Markov operator defined by~\eqref{eq:markov-operator}, with the integral understood in the Bochner sense. Then \(P_{\mathcal{H}}\) leaves \(L_0^2(\pi;\mathcal{H})\) invariant, and
\[
\vert\!\vert\!\vert P_{\mathcal{H}} \vert\!\vert\!\vert_{L_0^2(\pi;\mathcal{H})}
= \vert\!\vert\!\vert P_0 \vert\!\vert\!\vert_{L_0^2(\pi)}.
\]
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By \(\pi\)-invariance, for any \( g \in L_0^2(\pi; \mathcal{H}) \), it holds that \( \pi(P_\mathcal{H} g) = \pi(g) \), so \(L_0^2(\pi;\mathcal{H})\) is invariant under \(P_{\mathcal{H}}\). Using the canonical identification \( L_0^2(\pi;\mathcal{H}) \simeq L_0^2(\pi) \otimes\mathcal{H} \), the restriction of \(P_{\mathcal{H}}\) to \(L_0^2(\pi;\mathcal{H})\) is \( P_0\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}} \). Hence, by the standard tensor-product norm identity,
\begin{equation}
\vert\!\vert\!\vert P_{\mathcal{H}} \vert\!\vert\!\vert_{L_0^2(\pi;\mathcal{H})}
= \vert\!\vert\!\vert P_0\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}} \vert\!\vert\!\vert_{L_0^2(\pi;\mathcal{H})}
= \vert\!\vert\!\vert P_0 \vert\!\vert\!\vert_{L_0^2(\pi)},
\end{equation}
as claimed.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:mmd-affine-transformation}
Let \(\mathcal{H},\widetilde\mathcal{H}\) be separable Hilbert spaces, let \(\phi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{H}\) be measurable, and define
\[
\widetilde\phi(x):=A\phi(x)+b,
\]
where \(A:\mathcal{H}\to\widetilde\mathcal{H}\) is a bounded linear map and \(b\in\widetilde\mathcal{H}\). For any \(\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})\) with respect to which \(\phi\) is Bochner integrable,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MMD}_{\widetilde\phi}(\mu,\nu)
\leq \| A \|_{\mathcal{H}\to\widetilde\mathcal{H}}\, \mathrm{MMD}_\phi(\mu,\nu).
\label{eq:mmd-affine-comparison}
\end{equation}
In particular, translations of the feature map leave MMD unchanged.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Bounded linear operators commute with Bochner integration, so
\begin{equation}
m_{\widetilde\phi}(\mu)=A m_\phi(\mu)+b.
\end{equation}
Subtracting the corresponding identity for \(\nu\) and taking norms proves~\eqref{eq:mmd-affine-comparison}. Taking \(A=I_\mathcal{H}\) gives translation invariance.

<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:uniform-mmd-ergodicity}
There exist constants \( 0 \le C < \infty  \) and \( \theta \in [0, 1) \), such that
\begin{equation}
\sup_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \mathrm{MMD}_\phi(P^n(x, \cdot), \pi) \leq C \theta^n,
\quad n \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Throughout, we fix constants \(C\) and \(\theta\) satisfying this
assumption and write
\begin{equation}
\tau_\theta :=  C / (1-\theta).
\end{equation}

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:mmd-occupation-measure-whp}
Let \((X_t)_{t \ge 0}\) be a stationary Markov process with invariant measure \( \pi \), and assume that \(P\) admits an absolute \(L^2(\pi)\) spectral gap \(1 - \rho > 0\). 
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-mmd-ergodicity} hold with constants \(C < \infty\) and \( \theta \in [0, 1) \). 
Then, for every \(T\ge 1\) and every \(\delta\in(0,1)\), with probability at least \(1-\delta\),
\[
\mathrm{MMD}_\phi(\widehat \mu_T,\pi)
\leq 2 \| \phi \|_\pi \sqrt{\frac{\tau_\mathrm{rel}  \log(4/\delta)}{T}}
+ \frac{12 \tau_\theta \sqrt{\tau_\mathrm{rel}} \log(4/\delta)}{T},
\]
where \( \tau_\mathrm{rel} \) is defined in~\eqref{eq:tau-rel-def}.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
Recall that \( \| \phi \|_\pi = \left( \int \| \phi(x) \|_\mathcal{H}^2 \, \pi(dx) \right)^{1/2} \).
The leading term can be sharpened by replacing \(\| \phi \|_\pi\) with the centered quantity \(\| \phi-\pi\phi \|_\pi\). Indeed, the proof directly uses \(\| \phi-\pi\phi \|_\pi\leq\| \phi \|_\pi\) as a simplification. This sharpened version is invariant under translations of the feature map \(\phi \mapsto \phi+h\), as is the MMD itself. We retain \(\| \phi \|_\pi\) in the statement for simplicity. This separation will be crucial later for deriving fast rates for statistical learning context in  Section~\ref{sec:stats-learning}.
<!-- end remark-7 -->
<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
If the feature map is uniformly bounded as in~\eqref{eq:bounded-feature}, and the one-step contraction~\eqref{eq:mmd-one-step-contraction} holds, then Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-mmd-ergodicity} holds with \(C=2\kappa\) by~\eqref{eq:mmd-one-step-contraction-kappa}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:mmd-occupation-measure-whp} gives, for every \(T\ge1\) and \(\delta\in(0,1)\), with probability at least \(1-\delta\),
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MMD}_\phi(\widehat\mu_T,\pi)
\le 2\| \phi \|_\pi\sqrt{\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}\log(4/\delta)}{T}}
+\frac{24\kappa \sqrt{\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}}\,\log(4/\delta)}{(1-\theta) T}.
\end{equation}
Thus, under~\eqref{eq:mmd-one-step-contraction}, the uniform bound \(\kappa\) enters only the lower-order term, of order \(T^{-1}\), while the leading fluctuation term is governed by the norm \(\| \phi \|_\pi\) rather than by the worst-case bound. Then, the leading term in the bound depends on the average size of the feature map under \(\pi\), and the uniform bound controls only the non-asymptotic correction.
<!-- end remark-8 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Set \(g:=\phi-\pi\phi\). Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-mmd-ergodicity} gives a solution \(u:=\sum_{n\geq0}P^ng\) of \(u-Pu=g\), with \(\| u \|_\infty\leq\tau_\theta\). Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{mart_dec}
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}g(X_t)
= M_T+u(X_0)-u(X_T),
\qquad M_T
:=\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(u(X_t)-Pu(X_{t-1})\bigr)
\end{equation}
is a martingale decomposition with uniformly bounded increments and boundary term. For the conditional variance function \(V_u:=P\| u \|_\mathcal{H}^2-\| Pu \|_\mathcal{H}^2\), the spectral gap gives
\begin{equation}
\pi(V_u)
\leq\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}\| \phi-\pi\phi \|_\pi^2.
\end{equation}
We first apply the stationary Bernstein inequality to \(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}V_u(X_t)\), then the localized Pinelis--Bernstein inequality to \(M_T\). Dividing by \(T\) and adding the boundary contribution gives the stated bound. The full proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:occupation-stationary}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:mmd-occupation-measure-whp-nonstationary}
Let \((X_t)_{t\geq 0}\) be a Markov process with initial distribution \(X_0 \sim \nu\) and invariant measure \(\pi\). Let \(p\in(1,\infty]\), assume that \(\nu \ll \pi\) and that \( d\nu/d\pi \in L^p(\pi) \). Set \( q := p / (p-1) \). Assume that \(P\) admits an absolute \(L^2(\pi)\) spectral gap \(1 - \rho > 0\). Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-mmd-ergodicity} holds with constants \( C < \infty \) and \( \theta \in [0,1) \).

\medskip \noindent
For \(t_0 \geq 0\) and \(T \geq 1\), define the occupation measure after a burn-in period of length \(t_0\) by
\[
\widehat\mu_{T,t_0}
:= \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=t_0}^{t_0+T-1}\delta_{X_t}.
\]
Then, for every \(\delta\in(0,1)\), with probability at least \(1-\delta\),
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MMD}_\phi(\widehat\mu_{T,t_0},\pi)
\leq 2\| \phi \|_\pi \sqrt{\frac{\tau_\mathrm{rel}\log(4/\delta)}{T}}
+ \frac{ 12\tau_\theta \sqrt{ \tau_\mathrm{rel} \, q\log(4/\delta) \log(4D/\delta) } }{T},
\label{eq:mmd-nonstationary-bound}
\end{equation}
where \( D = D(\nu, t_0, p) := \left\|  d(\nu P^{t_0}) / d\pi  \right\|_{L^p(\pi)} \) and \( \tau_\mathrm{rel} \) is defined in~\eqref{eq:tau-rel-def}.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We use the same Poisson solution and martingale decomposition on the block \(X_{t_0},\ldots,X_{t_0+T}\). The only change is to apply the non-stationary Bernstein inequality of Theorem~\ref{thm:jiang-bernstein-nonstationary} to \(V_u-\pi(V_u)\), with initial distribution \(\nu P^{t_0}\). This introduces \(q\) and \(D\) into the deviation bound for the predictable quadratic variation, while its stationary mean \(T\pi(V_u)\) is unchanged. The localized Pinelis--Bernstein inequality and the boundary estimate then yield~\eqref{eq:mmd-nonstationary-bound}. The full proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:occupation-nonstationary}.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:no-gap}
Let \((X_t)_{t\geq0}\) be a Markov chain with transition kernel \(P\), invariant measure \(\pi\), and initial distribution \(X_0\sim\nu\), where \(\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})\) is arbitrary.
                                                                                                                                         
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-mmd-ergodicity} holds with constants \(C<\infty\) and \(\theta\in[0,1)\).
                                    
Then, for every \(T\geq1\) and every \(\delta\in(0,1)\), with probability at least \(1-\delta\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:no-gap}
\mathrm{MMD}_\phi(\widehat\mu_T,\pi)
\leq \tau_\theta\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\delta)}{T}}
+\frac{5 \log(2/\delta)}{T}\right).
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The proof uses the same Poisson equation and martingale decomposition as the main theorem, with the deterministic bound \(\langle M \rangle_T \leq T \tau_\theta^2\) replacing the spectral gap estimate. The details are given in Appendix~\ref{app:occupation-nogap}.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:mmd-occupation-weak-as}
Under the conditions of Corollary~\ref{cor:no-gap},
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MMD}_\phi(\widehat\mu_T,\pi)\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{a.s.}.
\end{equation}
If, in addition, \(\mathcal{X}\) is compact and the induced kernel \(k(x,y):=\langle\phi(x),\phi(y)\rangle_\mathcal{H}\) is continuous and characteristic, then
\begin{equation}
\widehat\mu_T\rightharpoonup^\star\pi
\quad\text{a.s.}.
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For \(T\geq2\), let \(r(T)\) be the right-hand side of the bound in Corollary~\ref{cor:no-gap} with \(\delta=T^{-2}\). Then \(r(T)\to0\) and
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\mathrm{MMD}_\phi(\widehat\mu_T,  \pi)>r(T)\right\}
\leq T^{-2}.
\label{eq:t-2-bound}
\end{equation}
By the Borel--Cantelli lemma, almost surely,
\(\mathrm{MMD}_\phi(\widehat\mu_T,\pi)\leq r(T)\) for all sufficiently large \(T\), proving the first claim. Under the additional assumptions, \(\mathrm{MMD}_\phi\) metrizes weak convergence on \(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})\), which gives the second claim; see, e.g.,~\citet{simon2023metrizing}.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
Take \( \mathcal{X} = [0, 1] \) and the deterministic map \( T_q(x) = q x \) for \( q \in (0, 1) \). The unique invariant (and therefore unique ergodic) measure is \( \pi = \delta_0 \). Note that \( \| P^n(x, \cdot) - \pi \|_\mathrm{TV} = 1  \) for any \( x \in (0, 1] \). Now, consider a Gaussian kernel which is characteristic on \( \mathcal{X} \) and \( \phi: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{H} \) is known to be Lipschitz, so that
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MMD}_\phi(P^n(x, \cdot), \pi)
= \|  \phi(q^n x) - \phi(0)  \|_\mathcal{H}
\leq \mathrm{Lip}(\phi) |  q^n x - 0  |
= \mathrm{Lip}(\phi) | x | q^n
\leq \mathrm{Lip}(\phi) q^n.
\end{equation}
Hence Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-mmd-ergodicity} holds with \(C=\mathrm{Lip}(\phi)\) and \(\theta=q\). More generally, Lipschitz regularity is not essential. If \(\phi\) is \(\beta\)-Hölder for some \(\beta\in(0,1]\), namely
\begin{equation}
\| \phi(x)-\phi(y) \|_\mathcal{H}
\leq L_{\phi,\beta}| x-y |^{\beta},
\qquad x,y\in[0,1],
\end{equation}
then Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-mmd-ergodicity} holds with
\(C=L_{\phi,\beta}\) and \(\theta=q^\beta\). For example, the canonical feature map of the Laplace kernel is \(1/2\)-H{\"o}lder.

<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
A basic class of examples for which condition~\eqref{eq:w1-pointwise-contraction} holds is given by iterated random functions~\citep{diaconis1999iterated}
\begin{equation}
X_{n+1}=F(X_n,\xi_{n+1}),
\end{equation}
where \( (\xi_n)_{n\geq1} \) is a sequence of i.i.d.\ random innovations, independent of \(X_0\). If, for some \( \theta<1 \),
\begin{equation}
d\bigl(F(x,\xi_1),F(y,\xi_1)\bigr)
\leq \theta d(x,y),
\qquad x,y\in\mathcal{X},
\label{eq:w1-coupling}
\end{equation}
almost surely, then the synchronous coupling immediately yields the one-step \(W_1\)-contraction as in~\eqref{eq:w1-pointwise-contraction}. More generally, contraction in Wasserstein-type distances have been studied extensively; see, for example, \citet{hairer2011asymptotic,butkovsky2014subgeometric,
kloeckner2015contraction,durmus2016subgeometric} and the references therein.

<!-- example-2: body_md -->
Let \(\mathcal{X}=[0,1]\) and consider the autoregressive process
\begin{equation}
X_{n+1}=\Lambda X_n+\xi_{n+1},
\qquad \Lambda=\frac13,
\end{equation}
where the innovations \((\xi_n)_{n\geq1}\) are i.i.d., independent of \(X_0\), and take the values \(\{ 0, 2/3 \}\) with probability \(1/2\) each. The unique invariant measure is the \emph{Cantor distribution},
\begin{equation}
\pi
=\operatorname{Law}\!\left( \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2B_j}{3^j} \right),
\end{equation}
where \((B_j)_{j\geq1}\) are i.i.d. Bernoulli random variables with parameter \(1/2\). Dobrushin condition~\eqref{eq:w1-pointwise-contraction} holds by synchronous coupling argument in~\eqref{eq:w1-coupling}. Indeed,
\begin{equation}
| \Lambda x+\xi_1-\Lambda y-\xi_1 |
=\Lambda | x-y |,
\qquad x,y \in [0, 1].
\end{equation}
Since \(\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{X})=1\), the \(W_1\) control criterion~\eqref{eq:w1-control} yields, for every \(L_\phi\)-Lipschitz feature map \(\phi:[0,1]\to\mathcal{H}\),
\begin{equation}
\sup_{x\in[0,1]}
\mathrm{MMD}_\phi\bigl(P^n(x,\cdot),\pi\bigr)
\leq L_\phi\,\Lambda^n,
\qquad n \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Thus Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-mmd-ergodicity} holds with \(C=L_\phi\) and \(\theta=\Lambda\).

\medskip
Convergence in total variation nevertheless fails. Indeed, \(\pi\) is atomless and supported on the middle-thirds Cantor set; in particular, it is singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. For every \(x\in[0,1]\) and finite \(n\), the measure \(P^n(x,\cdot)\) has finite support and is therefore mutually singular with \(\pi\). Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\| P^n(x,\cdot)-\pi \|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1,
\qquad x\in[0,1], \; n \ge 0.
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
By~\eqref{eq:dual-ipm-contraction},
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MMD}_\alpha(\mu P,\pi)
\leq\rho_\alpha\mathrm{MMD}_\alpha(\mu,\pi).
\end{equation}
Moreover, the centered pointwise realization gives
\begin{equation}
\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{MMD}_\alpha(\delta_x,\pi)

\leq\kappa_\alpha.

Proposition~\ref{prop:ipm-comparison-relaxation}\textup{(ii)--(iii)} therefore proves the first claim.

&=\| \upsilon \|_{-1}^2

<!-- remark-10: body_md -->
The map
\begin{equation}
J_\alpha:\mathcal{E}_{-\alpha}\to\mathcal{G},
\qquad J_\alpha h:=\overline L_\phi^{\alpha/2}h,
\end{equation}
initially defined on \(\mathcal{G}\), extends to an isometric isomorphism. Indeed,
if \(h=\sum_{j\geq1}h_j\overline e_j\), then
\begin{equation}
\| J_\alpha h \|_\pi^2
=\sum_{j\geq1}\overline\lambda_j^\alpha h_j^2
=\| h \|_{-\alpha}^2.
\end{equation}
Under this identification, the contraction condition is equivalently written as
\begin{equation}
\big\|  \overline L_\phi^{\alpha/2} P^\ast \overline L_\phi^{-\alpha/2}  \big\|_{\mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{G}}
\leq\rho_\alpha.
\label{eq:conjugated-hilbert-scale-gap}
\end{equation}
The conjugated operator is initially defined on \(\mathrm{Ran}(\overline L_\phi^{\alpha/2})\subseteq\mathcal{G}\), and the condition means that it admits a bounded extension to \(\mathcal{G}\).
<!-- end remark-10 -->
<!-- remark-11: body_md -->
As \(\alpha\downarrow0\), the spaces \(\mathcal{E}_\alpha\) formally approach \(\mathcal{G}\). More precisely, for every \(h\in\mathcal{G}\),
\begin{equation}
\| h \|_{-\alpha}\longrightarrow\| h \|_\pi.
\end{equation}
This convergence is pointwise, but not uniform over the \(L^2(\pi)\)-unit ball. Indeed, if \(\overline L_\phi\) has infinite rank, then \(\overline\lambda_j\to0\), and for every fixed \(\alpha>0\),
\begin{equation}
\| \overline e_j \|_{-\alpha}
=\overline\lambda_j^{\alpha/2}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad \| \overline e_j \|_\pi=1.
\end{equation}
Consequently, contraction in \(\mathcal{E}_{-\alpha}\) for a fixed \(\alpha>0\), however small, does not by itself imply an absolute spectral gap on \(L_0^2(\pi)\). If \(\overline L_\phi\) is injective on \(L_0^2(\pi)\) and the contraction condition holds along a sequence \(\alpha_n\downarrow0\) with \(\sup_n\rho_{\alpha_n}<1\), then passage to the limit recovers an absolute \(L^2(\pi)\)-spectral gap. In particular, when \(\mathcal{G}=L_0^2(\pi)\), the contraction condition in Assumption~\ref{ass:hilbert-scale-gap}\textup{(ii)} at \(\alpha=0\) is precisely the usual absolute \(L^2(\pi)\)-spectral-gap condition.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:commuting-hilbert-scale}
Suppose that \(P\) admits an absolute \(L^2(\pi)\) spectral gap \( 1 - \rho > 0 \) and commutes with the centered feature operator:
\[
P\overline L_\phi=\overline L_\phi P
\qquad\text{on }L_0^2(\pi).
\]
Fix \(\alpha\geq0\) such that Assumption~\ref{ass:hilbert-scale-gap}~\textup{(i)} holds, and suppose that \(P\) preserves the corresponding fixed pointwise realization of \(\mathcal{E}_\alpha\). Then Assumption~\ref{ass:hilbert-scale-gap} holds with
\begin{equation}
\rho_\alpha
:=\vert\!\vert\!\vert P \vert\!\vert\!\vert_{\mathcal{E}_\alpha}
=\vert\!\vert\!\vert P|_{\mathcal{G}} \vert\!\vert\!\vert_{\mathcal{G}}
\leq\rho.
\label{eq:commuting-scale-contraction}
\end{equation}
In particular, if \(\mathcal{G}=L_0^2(\pi)\), then \(\rho_\alpha=\rho\).
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Commutation implies that \(P\) preserves
\(\mathcal{G}=\overline{\mathrm{Ran}(\overline L_\phi)}\) and, by functional calculus, commutes with \(\overline L_\phi^{\alpha/2}\) on \(\mathcal{G}\). Every \(f\in\mathcal{E}_\alpha\) has a unique representation \(f=\overline L_\phi^{\alpha/2}g\), with \(g\in\mathcal{G}\), and \(\| f \|_\alpha=\| g \|_\pi\). Consequently,
\begin{equation}
Pf=\overline L_\phi^{\alpha/2}Pg,
\qquad
\| Pf \|_\alpha=\| Pg \|_\pi
\leq\rho\| g \|_\pi
=\rho\| f \|_\alpha.
\end{equation}
Since \(g\mapsto\overline L_\phi^{\alpha/2}g\) is an isometric isomorphism from \(\mathcal{G}\) onto \(\mathcal{E}_\alpha\), taking the supremum over its unit ball proves \eqref{eq:commuting-scale-contraction}. Together with the assumed pointwise preservation and bounded realization, this verifies Assumption~\ref{ass:hilbert-scale-gap}.

At a conceptual level, the Hilbert scale above is part of a broader line of work connecting MMD and \(L^2\)-type geometries through operator regularization. The \emph{de-regularized maximum mean discrepancy} (DrMMD) interpolates between MMD and the \(\chi^2\)-divergence \citep{chen2025regularized}, while the regularized Stein variational gradient flow interpolates between the RKHS-based Stein flow and the \(L^2\)-based Wasserstein flow \citep{he2025regularized}. \label{thm:pinelis-bernstein}
\label{thm:pinelis-bernstein-hilbert}
Fix \(T\geq1\), and suppose that there exists \(R<\infty\) such that
\[
\| d_t \|_\mathcal{H}\leq R \;\text{a.s.},
\qquad t=1,\ldots,T.
\]
Then, for every \(V\geq0\) and every \(\delta\in(0,1)\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:localized-pinelis-bernstein}
\mathbb{P}\left\{ \| M_T \|_\mathcal{H}
> \sqrt{2V\log(2/\delta)} + \frac{2R\log(2/\delta)}{3},
\; \langle M\rangle_T\leq V \right\}

\end{equation}

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
For \(t=1,\ldots,T\), set
\begin{equation}
a_t:=\mathbb{E}_{t-1}\| d_t \|_\mathcal{H}^2,
\qquad Q_t:=\sum_{s=1}^t a_s,
\qquad Q_0:=0.
\end{equation}
Define the predictably truncated increments \( \widetilde d_t:=d_t\mathbf{1}_{\{Q_t\leq V\}} \). Since \(Q_t\) is \(\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\)-measurable,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_{t-1}\widetilde d_t
= \mathbf{1}_{\{Q_t\leq V\}}\mathbb{E}_{t-1}d_t
=0.
\end{equation}
Thus, \( \widetilde M_s:=\sum_{t=1}^s\widetilde d_t \) for  \( s=0,\ldots,T \) is a martingale. Moreover, \(\| \widetilde d_t \|_\mathcal{H}\leq R\), and, since \((Q_t)_t\) is nondecreasing,
\begin{equation}
\langle\widetilde M\rangle_T
= \sum_{t=1}^T \mathbf{1}_{\{Q_t\leq V\}}a_t
\leq V
\; \text{a.s.}.
\end{equation}
The standard Pinelis--Bernstein inequality therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{P}\left\{ \| \widetilde M_T \|_\mathcal{H} > \sqrt{2V\log(2/\delta)} +\frac{2R\log(2/\delta)}{3} \right\}
\leq\delta.
\end{equation}
On the event
\begin{equation}
\{\langle M\rangle_T\leq V\}
= \{Q_T\leq V\},
\end{equation}
we have \(Q_t\leq V\) for every \(t=1,\ldots,T\), and hence \(\widetilde M_T=M_T\). This proves the claim.

Fix \( \lambda > 0 \). By the standard argument, for any \( w \in \mathcal{H} \) and the corresponding function \( f_w(x) := \langle w, \phi(x) \rangle_\mathcal{H} \), it holds that
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}(f_w, f_\star)
:= \big\| f_w - f_\star \big\|_\pi^2
= \big\| \Sigma^{1/2}(w-w_\star) \big\|_\mathcal{H}^2.
\end{equation}
Since the model is well specified, \(h=\Sigma w_\star\). Moreover, using the empirical normal equation \( \widehat\Sigma_\lambda \widehat w_\lambda=\widehat h \), we have
\begin{align}
\widehat \Sigma_\lambda (\widehat w_\lambda-w_\star)
= \widehat h-\widehat\Sigma_\lambda w_\star
= \widehat h-\widehat\Sigma w_\star-\lambda w_\star,
\end{align}
so that
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^{1/2} (\widehat w_\lambda-w_\star)
= \Sigma^{1/2} \widehat \Sigma_\lambda^{-1} \Sigma_\lambda^{1/2} \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} \big( \widehat h-\widehat\Sigma w_\star-\lambda w_\star \big).
\end{equation}
Hence by the triangle inequality,
\begin{equation}
\big\| \Sigma^{1/2}(\widehat w_\lambda-w_\star) \big\|_\mathcal{H}
\leq
\underbrace{
\big\|  \Sigma^{1/2}\widehat\Sigma_\lambda^{-1} \Sigma_\lambda^{1/2}  \big\|
}_{\mathcal{T}_\text{stability}}
\Big(
\underbrace{
\big\| \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} (\widehat h-\widehat \Sigma w_\star) \big\|_\mathcal{H}
}_{\mathcal{T}_\text{stochastic}}
+
\underbrace{
\lambda \big\| \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2}w_\star \big\|_\mathcal{H}
}_{\mathcal{T}_\text{reg}}
\Big).
\label{eq:error-decom-well}
\end{equation}
We proceed by bounding each term separately.

\paragraph{Deterministic bounds.}
From spectral properties of the involved operators, the following bounds hold:
\begin{equation}
\big\| \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} \big\| \leq \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
\quad \text{and} \quad
\big\|  \Sigma^{1/2} \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2}  \big\| \leq 1.
\label{eq:det-op-bound}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Regularization bias.}
By Assumption~\ref{ass:learning-problem}~(i),
\begin{align}
\mathcal{T}_\text{reg}
:= \lambda\big\| \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2}w_\star \big\|_\mathcal{H}
= \lambda \big\|  \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} \Sigma^{r-1/2}g_\star  \big\|_\mathcal{H}
\leq \lambda^r G_\star,
\qquad \frac12 \leq r \leq 1,
\label{eq:reg-term}
\end{align}
by the spectral theorem.

\paragraph{Stability term.} Introduce the preconditioned covariance fluctuation
\begin{equation}
\Delta_\lambda
:= \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} \big(\widehat \Sigma - \Sigma\big) \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\widehat \Sigma_\lambda
= \widehat \Sigma + \lambda I
= \Sigma_\lambda + \big( \widehat \Sigma - \Sigma \big)
= \Sigma_\lambda^{1/2} (I+\Delta_\lambda) \Sigma_\lambda^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Therefore, on the event \(\| \Delta_\lambda \|<1\), the operator
\(I+\Delta_\lambda\) is invertible and
\begin{equation}
\widehat\Sigma_\lambda^{-1}
= \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} (I+\Delta_\lambda)^{-1} \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
Substituting this identity into \(\mathcal{T}_\text{stability}\) and applying the bound from Equation~\eqref{eq:det-op-bound}, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stab-bound}
\mathcal{T}_\text{stability}
:= \big\|  \Sigma^{1/2}\widehat\Sigma_\lambda^{-1}\Sigma_\lambda^{1/2}  \big\|
\leq \big\| \Sigma^{1/2}\Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} \big\| \cdot \big\| (I+\Delta_\lambda)^{-1} \big\|
\leq \frac{1}{1-\| \Delta_\lambda \|}.
\end{equation}
In particular, on the event \(\| \Delta_\lambda \| \leq 1/2\), we get \( \mathcal{T}_\text{stability} \leq 2 \).

\medskip
To bound the \( \| \Delta_\lambda \| \) term, define the following preconditioned feature map \( \Phi_\lambda: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{L}_2(\mathcal{H}) \),
\begin{equation}
\Phi_\lambda(x)
:= \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} \left(\phi(x) \otimes \phi(x)\right) \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2}
= \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} \, \Phi(x) \, \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2},
\quad x \in \mathcal{X}.
\end{equation}
An important observation which will be used later: by self-adjointness of \( \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} \) we can write \( \Phi_\lambda \) as
\begin{equation}
\Phi_\lambda(x) = \phi_\lambda(x) \otimes \phi_\lambda(x),
\quad \text{where} \; \phi_\lambda(x) := \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} \phi(x).
\label{eq:phi-lambda-def}
\end{equation}
In particular, \( \|  \Phi_\lambda(x)  \|_{\mathcal{L}_2(\mathcal{H})} = \| \phi_\lambda(x) \|_\mathcal{H}^2 \).

\medskip \noindent
With \( \widehat \Sigma = \widehat \mu_T(\Phi) \) and \( \Sigma = \pi(\Phi) \), by linearity we get
\begin{equation}
\|  \Delta_\lambda  \|
\leq \|  \Delta_\lambda  \|_{\mathcal{L}_2(\mathcal{H})}
= \mathrm{MMD}_{\Phi_\lambda}(\widehat \mu_T, \pi).
\end{equation}
This allows us to apply Theorem~\ref{thm:mmd-occupation-measure-whp} to obtain a high-probability upper bound on this term.

The identity above is the key observation behind the learning result. It shows that the stability of the empirical inverse problem is governed directly by the convergence of the occupation measure of the process \( \widehat \mu_T \) to the invariant measure \( \pi \) in a certain kernel-induced geometry \( \mathrm{MMD}_{\Phi_\lambda} \).
\end{remark}

\mathrm{MMD}_{\Phi_\lambda}\bigl(P^n(x,\cdot),\pi\bigr)

\qquad n \ge 0.
\label{eq:phi-lambda-contraction}

\begin{equation}
\| \Phi_\lambda \|_\infty
\leq \frac{1}{\lambda} \| \Phi \|_\infty
\leq \frac{\kappa^2}{\lambda}.
\label{eq:phi-lambda-inf-norm}

\begin{align}

:= \int \|  \Phi_\lambda(x)  \|_{\mathcal{L}_2(\mathcal{H})}^2 \, \pi(dx)
\leq \| \Phi_\lambda \|_{\infty} \cdot \int \|  \Phi_\lambda(x)  \|_{\mathcal{L}_2(\mathcal{H})} \, \pi(dx).
\label{eq:xi1-l2-bound}

\begin{equation}

=: \mathcal{N}(\lambda).
\label{eq:phi-lambda-pre-l2-norm}
\end{equation}
Putting~\eqref{eq:phi-lambda-inf-norm} and~\eqref{eq:phi-lambda-pre-l2-norm} into~\eqref{eq:xi1-l2-bound},
\begin{equation}
\| \Phi_\lambda \|_{\pi}^2 \leq \frac{\kappa^2 \mathcal{N}(\lambda)}{\lambda}.
\label{eq:phi-lambda-l2-norm}
\end{equation}

\medskip \noindent
Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:mmd-occupation-measure-whp} with~\eqref{eq:phi-lambda-contraction} and~\eqref{eq:phi-lambda-l2-norm} yields, for any \(\delta\in(0,1)\), with probability at least \(1-\delta\),

\label{eq:delta-lambda-bound}

where \( B_\alpha:=\kappa^{2(1-\alpha)}\kappa_\alpha \).

\paragraph{Stochastic error.} To control the term \( \mathcal{T}_\text{stochastic} \), consider the following observable \( \xi: \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{H} \),
\begin{equation}
\xi(x, x') := \left(x' - f_\star(x)\right) \phi_\lambda(x) \in \mathcal{H},
\qquad x, x' \in \mathcal{X}.
\end{equation}
Observe, that
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \xi(x_t, x_{t+1}) = \Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2} \big(\widehat h - \widehat \Sigma w_\star\big)
\quad \text{and} \quad
\int \xi(x, x') \, \pi(dx) P(x, dx') = 0.
\end{equation}
Now, consider the stochastic process \( (d_t)_{t \ge 1} \) given by
\begin{equation}
d_{t}
:= \xi(X_{t-1}, X_{t})
= \left(X_{t} - f_\star(X_{t-1})\right) \phi_\lambda(X_{t-1}),
\qquad t\geq1.
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}_t
:= \sigma\left((X_0, X_1), \ldots (X_{t-1}, X_{t})\right)
= \sigma(X_0,\ldots,X_t),
\qquad t\geq0.
\end{equation}
Then \(d_t\) is \(\mathcal{F}_t\)-measurable and, by Markovianity,
\begin{align}
\mathbb{E} \left[ d_{t} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1} \right]
&= \mathbb{E} \left[ \left(X_{t} - f_\star(X_{t-1})\right) \phi_\lambda(X_{t-1}) \mid X_{t-1} \right] \\
&= \underbrace{\left( \mathbb{E}[ X_{t} \mid X_{t-1} ] - f_\star(X_{t-1}) \right)}_{= 0} \phi_\lambda(X_{t-1}).
\label{eq:dt-martingale-exp}
\end{align}
Therefore, \( (d_t)_{t \ge 1} \) is a martingale difference sequence with respect to \( (\mathcal{F}_t)_t \) and \( M_T := \sum_{t=1}^T d_t \) is a martingale. Moreover, by measurability,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_{t-1}\| d_t \|_\mathcal{H}^2
= \| \phi_\lambda(X_{t-1}) \|_\mathcal{H}^2 \cdot \mathbb{E}\left[ | X_t-f_\star(X_{t-1}) |^2 \mid X_{t-1} \right].
\end{equation}
Hence, under Assumption~\ref{ass:markov-chain}\textup{(iii)},
\begin{equation}
\langle M \rangle_T := \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbb{E}_{t-1} \| d_{t} \|_\mathcal{H}^2
\leq \sigma^2 \cdot \sum_{t=1}^{T} \|  \phi_\lambda(X_{t-1})  \|_\mathcal{H}^2
= \sigma^2 \cdot \sum_{s=0}^{T-1} \|  \phi_\lambda(X_s)  \|_\mathcal{H}^2.
\label{eq:m-t-pre-bound}
\end{equation}
Define a measurable bounded real-valued observable \( g_\lambda(x) :=  \|  \phi_\lambda(x)  \|_\mathcal{H}^2 = \| \Phi_\lambda(x) \|_{\mathcal{L}_2(\mathcal{H})} \). Its stationary variance, by~\eqref{eq:phi-lambda-l2-norm}, satisfies
\begin{align}
v_\lambda
:= \pi\left( \big(g_\lambda-\pi(g_\lambda)\big)^2 \right)
\leq \pi \big(g_\lambda^2\big)
= \| \Phi_\lambda \|_\pi^2
\leq \frac{\kappa^2 \mathcal{N}(\lambda)}{\lambda}.
\label{eq:g-lambda-variance}
\end{align}
Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:jiang-bernstein-stationary} to the centered observable \(g_\lambda-\pi(g_\lambda)\), which is bounded and measurable, gives, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{P}\left\{ \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}g_\lambda(X_t) - T\mathcal{N}(\lambda) > \varepsilon \right\}
\leq \exp\left\{ -\frac{\varepsilon^2} {2\left( \alpha_1(\rho) T v_\lambda + \frac{5\kappa^2}{(1-\rho)\lambda}\varepsilon \right)} \right\}.
\label{eq:g-lambda-bernstein}
\end{equation}
Consequently, letting \(L_\delta:=\log(4/\delta)\), with probability at
least \(1-\delta/2\),
\begin{align}
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}g_\lambda(X_t)
\leq T\mathcal{N}(\lambda)
+ \sqrt{2\alpha_1(\rho) T v_\lambda L_\delta}
+ \frac{10\kappa^2L_\delta}{(1-\rho)\lambda}
\leq T\mathcal{N}(\lambda)
+ 2\kappa \sqrt{ \frac{T\mathcal{N}(\lambda)L_\delta} {(1-\rho)\lambda} }
+ \frac{10\kappa^2L_\delta} {(1-\rho)\lambda},
\label{eq:g-lambda-sum-bound}
\end{align}
where in the second inequality we used~\eqref{eq:g-lambda-variance} and \(1+\rho\leq2\). Putting~\eqref{eq:g-lambda-sum-bound} back into Equation~\eqref{eq:m-t-pre-bound}, we obtain
\begin{align}
\langle M\rangle_T
\leq T\sigma^2\mathcal{N}(\lambda)
+ 2\sigma^2\kappa \sqrt{ \frac{T\mathcal{N}(\lambda)L_\delta}{(1-\rho)\lambda} }
+ \frac{10\sigma^2\kappa^2L_\delta} {(1-\rho)\lambda}
=: V_T
\label{eq:t3-quad-var-bound}
\end{align}
with probability at least \(1-\delta/2\). We now apply Theorem~\ref{thm:pinelis-bernstein-hilbert} with
\begin{equation}
R_\lambda:=\frac{\kappa M}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.
\end{equation}
Combining the two estimates and using \(\sqrt{a+b+c}\leq\sqrt a+\sqrt b+\sqrt c\), we obtain, with probability at least \(1-\delta\),
\begin{align}
\mathcal{T}_\text{stochastic}
&= \frac{1}{T}\| M_T \|_\mathcal{H} \\
&\leq \frac{\sqrt{2V_T L_\delta}}{T}
+ \frac{2 R_\lambda L_\delta}{3T} \\
&\leq \sqrt{ \frac{2\sigma^2\mathcal{N}(\lambda)L_\delta}{T} }
+ 2\sigma \left( \frac{\kappa^2\mathcal{N}(\lambda)}{(1-\rho)\lambda} \right)^{1/4} \frac{L_\delta^{3/4}}{T^{3/4}}
+ \frac{2\sqrt{5}\,\sigma\kappa L_\delta} {\sqrt{(1-\rho)\lambda}\,T}
+ \frac{2\kappa M L_\delta} {3\sqrt{\lambda}\,T}.
\label{eq:t3-bound}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Putting the bounds together.}
We now collect the estimates obtained above. Apply \eqref{eq:delta-lambda-bound} and \eqref{eq:t3-bound} with $\delta/2$ in place of $\delta$: each then holds with probability at least $1-\delta/2$, and in both the confidence factor becomes \( L_\delta := \log(8/\delta) \). By~\eqref{eq:delta-lambda-bound}, a sufficient condition for \(\| \Delta_\lambda \|_{\mathcal{L}_2(\mathcal{H})}\leq1/2\) is
\begin{equation}
T\geq
\max\left\{
\frac{64 \kappa^2 \tau_{\mathrm{rel}} \mathcal{N}(\lambda) L_\delta}{\lambda},
\,
\frac{48B_\alpha\sqrt{\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}} L_\delta}
{(1-\rho_\alpha)\lambda}
\right\}.
\end{equation}
On this event \( \mathcal{T}_\text{stability} \le 2 \) by \eqref{eq:stab-bound}. Substituting \eqref{eq:reg-term} and \eqref{eq:t3-bound} into \eqref{eq:error-decom-well} and taking a union bound over the two events, we obtain, with probability at least \( 1-\delta \),
\begin{align}
\big\| \widehat f_\lambda-f_\star \big\|_\pi
\leq\;&
2\lambda^rG_\star
+ 2\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2 \mathcal{N}(\lambda) L_\delta}{T}} \notag \\
&+ 4\sigma \left( \frac{\kappa^2 \tau_\mathrm{rel} \, \mathcal{N}(\lambda)}{\lambda} \right)^{1/4} \frac{L_\delta^{3/4}}{T^{3/4}}
+ \frac{4\sqrt{5 \tau_\mathrm{rel}}\,\sigma\kappa L_\delta}{\sqrt{\lambda}\,T}
+ \frac{4\kappa M L_\delta}{3\sqrt{\lambda}\,T}
\label{eq:error-bound-final-app}

as claimed.

T^{-3/4} \left( \frac{\mathcal{N}(\lambda)}{\lambda} \right)^{1/4}

in the term~\eqref{eq:t3-bound} is natural for the specific proof strategy but is unlikely to be sharp. In particular, if \(\mathcal{N}(\lambda)\lesssim \lambda^{-p}\), the correction is of order \(T^{-3/4}\lambda^{-(p+1)/4}\). Asymptotically, this term is still dominated by
\begin{equation}
\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{N}(\lambda)}{T}}
\end{equation}
under \( T \lambda^{1-p} \to \infty \) regularization schedule, which coincides with the expected scale from classical i.i.d. analysis.

By Assumption~\ref{ass:learning-problem}\textup{(ii)}, the effective dimension satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{N}(\lambda)\lesssim \lambda^{-p},
\qquad p=\frac{1}{b}\in(0,1).
\label{eq:effective-dimension-polynomial}
\end{equation}
Therefore, up to multiplicative constants and logarithmic factors, the bound in~\eqref{eq:error-bound-final} becomes
\begin{equation}
\big\| \widehat f_\lambda-f_\star \big\|_\pi
\lesssim \lambda^r
+ \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda^pT}}
+ \frac{1}{\lambda^{(p+1)/4}T^{3/4}}
+ \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}T}.
\label{eq:error-bound-rate}
\end{equation}
The first two terms are the usual regularization bias and stochastic error. Balancing them gives
\begin{equation}
\lambda^r
\asymp \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda^pT}},
\qquad \text{so that} \qquad
\lambda_T^r
\asymp T^{-\frac{r}{2r+p}}.
\label{eq:lambda-rate-proof}
\end{equation}
We now need to check that the two Bernstein correction terms in~\eqref{eq:error-bound-rate} are of no larger order. First,
\begin{align}
\frac{1}{\lambda_T^{(p+1)/4}T^{3/4}}
= T^{-\frac34+\frac{p+1}{4(2r+p)}}
= T^{-\frac{6r+2p-1}{4(2r+p)}}.
\end{align}
Since \(r\geq1/2\) and \(p>0\), we have \( (6r+2p-1)/4 \geq r, \) or equivalently \(2r+2p\geq1\). Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{\lambda_T^{(p+1)/4}T^{3/4}}
\lesssim T^{-\frac{r}{2r+p}},
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_T}T} = T^{-1+\frac{1}{2(2r+p)}}.
\end{equation}
Since \(r+p\geq1/2\),
\begin{equation}
-1+\frac{1}{2(2r+p)}
\leq -\frac{r}{2r+p},
\implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_T}T}
\lesssim T^{-\frac{r}{2r+p}}.
\end{equation}
Thus all the terms in~\eqref{eq:error-bound-rate} are of order at most \(T^{-r/(2r+p)}\).

\medskip
It remains to verify the stability condition. By
Assumption~\ref{ass:learning-problem}\textup{(ii)} and Equation~\eqref{eq:delta-lambda-bound}, up to multiplicative constants and logarithmic factors,
\begin{equation}
\| \Delta_\lambda \|
\lesssim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda^{p+1}T}}
+ \frac{1}{\lambda T}.
\label{eq:delta-rate-proof}
\end{equation}
For \( \lambda_T\asymp T^{-1/(2r+p)} \) we have
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_T^{p+1}T}}
= T^{-\frac{2r-1}{2(2r+p)}},
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
\frac{1}{\lambda_T T}
= T^{-1+\frac{1}{2r+p}}.
\end{equation}
Both terms converge to zero for \(r>1/2\). At the endpoint \(r=1/2\), the first term is of constant order, and the multiplicative constant in the choice of \(\lambda_T\) can be taken sufficiently large so that \(\| \Delta_{\lambda_T} \|\leq1/2\).

\medskip
Consequently, on the stability event,
\begin{equation}
\big\| \widehat f_{\lambda_T}-f_\star \big\|_\pi
\lesssim T^{-\frac{r}{2r+p}},
\end{equation}
and squaring both sides yields
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}\big(\widehat f_{\lambda_T},f_\star\big)
\lesssim T^{-\frac{2r}{2r+p}},
\end{equation}
which proves the claim.

\end{equation}

The map \(m\mapsto\ell m\) is injective. Thus \eqref{eq:fourier-markov-action} and orthogonality give
\begin{equation}
\| Pf \|_\pi^2
=\sum_{m\neq0}| \widehat\upsilon(m) |^2
| \widehat f(m) |^2,
\qquad f\in L_0^2(\pi),
\end{equation}
which proves the norm identity in \eqref{eq:fourier-l2-contraction}.

\medskip \noindent
For each \(m\neq0\), one has \(| \widehat\upsilon(m) |<1\). Indeed, equality would force the unit-modulus random variable \(e^{-2\pi i m\cdot\xi_1}\) to be constant almost surely. The noise would then be supported on a level set of a nonconstant torus character, which has Lebesgue measure zero, contradicting absolute continuity. The Riemann--Lebesgue lemma gives
\(\widehat\upsilon(m)\to0\) as \(| m |\to\infty\).
Combining this decay with the strict inequality for each of the finitely many remaining modes yields \(\rho<1\).

\medskip \noindent
For a centered trigonometric polynomial, the same calculation in the weighted norm gives
\begin{align}
\| Pf \|_\alpha^2
&=\sum_{m\neq0}
a_{\ell m}^{-\alpha}
| \widehat\upsilon(m) |^2
| \widehat f(m) |^2
\leq R_\alpha^2
\sum_{m\neq0}a_m^{-\alpha}| \widehat f(m) |^2
=R_\alpha^2\| f \|_\alpha^2.
\end{align}
Testing on individual Fourier modes gives the reverse inequality for the operator norm. The same norm is obtained on the real space by using the corresponding sine and cosine modes. The estimate extends by density to \(\mathcal{E}_\alpha\). By \eqref{eq:fourier-uniform-embedding}, convergence in this space implies uniform convergence, so the extension agrees with the pointwise Markov action. The MMD bound follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:mmd-alpha-bound} with \(\rho_\alpha=R_\alpha\).

<!-- remark-16: body_md -->
\label{rem:drift-noise}
More generally, on an additive state space \( \mathcal{X} \), consider
\begin{equation}
X_{t+1}=F(X_t)+\xi_{t+1},
\end{equation}
where \(F:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}\) is measurable and \((\xi_t)_{t\geq1}\) are i.i.d. noise variables. Define the deterministic Koopman operator \(U_F\) and the noise operator \(Q_\xi\) by
\begin{equation}
(U_Ff)(x):=f(F(x)),
\qquad (Q_\xi f)(x):=\mathbb{E}[f(x+\xi_1)].
\end{equation}
Whenever the expectations are well defined,
\begin{equation}
Pf(x)
=\mathbb{E}[f(F(x)+\xi_1)]
=(U_FQ_\xi f)(x),
\end{equation}
so \(P=U_FQ_\xi\). Suppose that both operators preserve the chosen centered space \(\mathcal{E}_\alpha\) and act boundedly on it. Then
\begin{equation}
\vert\!\vert\!\vert P \vert\!\vert\!\vert_{\mathcal{E}_\alpha}
\leq \rho_{F,\alpha}\rho_{\xi,\alpha},
\qquad \rho_{F,\alpha}
:=\vert\!\vert\!\vert U_F \vert\!\vert\!\vert_{\mathcal{E}_\alpha},
\quad \rho_{\xi,\alpha}
:=\vert\!\vert\!\vert Q_\xi \vert\!\vert\!\vert_{\mathcal{E}_\alpha}.
\end{equation}
These are substantive requirements: invariance of \(\pi\) under \(P\) alone need not imply that the two factors separately preserve the centered space. Moreover, boundedness of \(U_F\) in a smooth function space requires appropriate regularity of \(F\). By duality, the same bounds hold for the push-forward action on \(\mathcal{E}_{-\alpha}\). Together with the bounded pointwise realization, a product strictly below one implies Assumption~\ref{ass:hilbert-scale-gap}.

\medskip
For the noisy expanding map in Example~\ref{ex:noisy-expanding-map}, \( \rho_{F,\alpha}=\ell^{s\alpha} \),  \( \rho_{\xi,\alpha}=e^{-2\pi^2\eta^2} \), and the product bound is sharp. The deterministic factor measures the amplification of frequencies, while the noise factor measures their attenuation.  This also clarifies the relation with Example~\ref{ex:noisy-irrational-rotation}. There the deterministic map is the translation \(x\mapsto x+\omega\), which plays the role of \(\ell=1\). Thus the deterministic Koopman operator is an isometry on the translation-invariant power scale, and the amplification factor is absent. For a general noise density \(\upsilon\), the contraction factor is \( \sup_{m\neq0}| \widehat\upsilon(m) | \), which is precisely the Fourier multiplier appearing in the noisy rotation example.
