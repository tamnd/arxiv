<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
    Let \(\Sigma^n\) be a minimal, closed embedded submanifold of
    the sphere \(\mathbb S^{n+m},\) with \(n \ge 1\) and \(m \ge 1.\)
    Fix \(0 < R < \pi/2\) such that the tube \(T_R(\Sigma)\)
    does not self-intersect. The mean exit time from \(T_R(\Sigma)\)
    admits:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The lower bound
        \[
        E_R(p) \ge \frac{R - r(p)}{m\sin^m R} \sin^{m-1} \left(
        \sqrt{\frac2{\pi(n+1)}} \frac{m-1}m r(p) 
        \right) \frac{1 - \cos^{n+1} r(p)}{(n+1) \cos^n r(p)},
        \]
        where the right-hand side approaches infinity as \(n\) tends to infinity
        if \(R, r, m\) are fixed.
        \item Let \(c\) be the maximum principal curvature, and let
        \(A = \min_{\Sigma} (1-c\tan R) > 0.\)
        Assume the norm of the second fundamental form \(\mathcal B\) satisfies
        \begin{equation}
            \label{eq:bound-B-iiff}
            \|\mathcal B\|^2 \le \frac{Aq\cos^{n+1} R}{\tan^2 R}
        \end{equation}
        in all \(\Sigma.\) Then we have the upper bound
        \[ E_R(p) \le \frac1{m\cos^n RP_{\Sigma}(R)}
        \log\left(\frac{\cos r(p)}{\cos R}\right), \]
        where \(P_{\Sigma}(R) = 1-\tan^{2} R \max_{\Sigma}\|\mathcal B\|^2 /
        (Aq\cos^{n+1} R).\)
        The right-hand side approaches zero as \(m\) tends to infinity if \(R, r, n\)
        and a bound on \(\|\mathcal B\|^2\) are fixed.
    \end{itemize}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The definition of \(A\) is made possible by
    Lemma 8.20 of \cite{gray90:_tubes}, which ensures that
    \(c\tan R < 1\) whenever \(R\) is small enough so that
    the tube does not self-intersect (which, as we will see, amounts to the
    condition that \(R\) is less than the \emph{minimal focal distance} of
    the embedding), and the sectional curvature
    of the ambient manifold satisfies \(\sec \ge 1.\)
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Minimal, closed submanifolds of the sphere
    with bounded second fundamental form have
    been extensively studied. In \cite{an-min92:rigiditytheorem}, it was
    shown that the bound \(\|\mathcal B\|^2 \le (2/3)n\) implies that the submanifold
    is totally geodesic, with the exception of Veronese surfaces in
    \(\mathbb S^4.\) However, for large codimension, our condition
    \eqref{eq:bound-B-iiff} is not tight, and so the result
    may apply to submanifolds that fall outside of this classification.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:geodesic-embeddings}
    Let \(\mathbb S^n \hookrightarrow \mathbb S^{n+m}\)
    be a totally geodesic embedding of spheres, with \(m \ge 1.\)
    The mean exit time is the radial
    function \(E_R(p) = f_R(r(p))\) given by:
    \[ f_R(r) = \int_r^R \frac{\int_0^u \sin^{m-1} s \cos^n s\, ds}
    {\sin^{m-1} u \cos^n u}\, du,\]
    which satisfies \(f_R''(r) + f_R'(r) [(m-1) \cot r - n \tan r] = -1,\)
    with \(f_R(\pm R) = 0\) and \(f_R'(0) = 0.\)
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
If we rescale to the sphere \(\mathbb S^{n+m}(\rho)\) of radius \(\rho\)
    (sectional curvature \(\frac1{\rho^2}\)), then the expression
    for the mean exit time must be rescaled somehow. Notice that it
    would be valid (after replacing \(R,\ r\)  by \(R/\rho,\ r/\rho\)
    respectively) for
    a Brownian motion with variance \(\rho^2\) per unit of time. Thus,
    if \(\sigma^2\) is the variance per unit of time,
    the function for the mean exit time becomes:
    \[ f_{R;\rho,\sigma^2}(r) = \frac{\rho^2}{\sigma^2}
    \int_{r/\rho}^{R/\rho} \frac{\int_0^u \sin^{m-1} s \cos^n s\, ds}
    {\sin^{m-1} u \cos^n u}\, du. \]
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:volumes}
    Let \(\Sigma^n \hookrightarrow \mathbb S^{n+m}\) be a minimal, closed
    embedding (\(m, n \ge 1\)). Let
    \(\mathbb S^n \hookrightarrow \mathbb S^{n+m}\) be a totally
    geodesic embedding. Let \(0 < r < R.\)
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The tube volumes are related by:
    \[
    \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T_R(\Sigma))}{\operatorname{Vol}(T_r(\Sigma))} \le
    \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T_R(\mathbb S^n))}{\operatorname{Vol}(T_r(\mathbb S^n))}. \]
    As a consequence:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:volume-inequality}
    \begin{aligned}
    \operatorname{Vol}(T_R(\Sigma))
    &\le \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T_R(\mathbb S^n))}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb S^n)} \operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma) \\
    &= \frac{2\pi^{m/2}}{\Gamma(m/2)} \left(\int_0^R \sin^{m-1} s \cos^n s\, ds \right) \operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma).
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    \item
    Let \(\|\mathcal B\|^2,\  A\) and \(P_{\Sigma}(R)\) be as in \autoref{thm:main},
    and assume the bound \eqref{eq:bound-B-iiff} on the second
    fundamental form. The tube volumes satisfy:
    \[
    \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T_R(\Sigma))}{\operatorname{Vol}(T_r(\Sigma))}
    \ge
    \left( \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T_R(\mathbb S^n))}{\operatorname{Vol}(T_r(\mathbb S^n))}
    \right)^{P_{\Sigma}(R)}.
    \]
    \end{itemize}

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
There currently exist estimations for the volume of a closed,
    minimal embedded
    submanifold \(\Sigma^n \hookrightarrow \mathbb S^{n+m}.\)
    One of them is, simply, \(\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma) \ge \operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb S^n).\)
    A more refined one from \cite{chengli81:heat-equations-minimal},
    in the case where \(\Sigma\) is of maximal dimension (which means that
    \(\Sigma\) is not contained in a hyperplane of \(\mathbb R^{n+m+1}\)) is
    \[ \operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma) > \left( 1 + \frac{2m+1}{B_n} \right) \operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb S^n), \]
    where \(B_n\) is a constant that depends only on \(n.\)
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
Consider a
    tube of radius \(R\) around a closed embedded submanifold \(\Sigma^n\)
    in a manifold \(M^{n+m}\) of positive sectional curvature
    (\(\sec_M > 0\)). According
    to theorem 8.4 of \cite{gray90:_tubes}, the volume of the tube
    in \(M\) is bounded by:
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:weyl-inequality}
    \operatorname{Vol}(T_R(\Sigma)) \le \frac{2\pi^{m/2} R^m}{m\Gamma(m/2)}
    \left(\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma) + \sum_{c=1}^{\lfloor{m/2}\rfloor}
    \frac{k_{2c}(\mathcal R^\Sigma - \mathcal R^M)R^{2c}}{(m+2)\cdots (m+2c)} \right),
    \end{equation}
    where the \(k_{2c}\) terms are known as \emph{integrated mean curvatures}.
    According to Corollary 9.25 of the same book, \(\operatorname{Vol}(T_R(\Sigma))\) is
    smaller than the analogue expression with \(\mathcal R^M = 0,\) i.e., when
    \(\Sigma\) is embedded in \(\mathbb R^{n+m}\)
    (which implies \eqref{eq:weyl-inequality} is an
    equality, called \emph{Weyl's tube formula}).
    There is also
    a formula for the volume of the tube in \(M\) as a power expansion:
    \[ \operatorname{Vol}(T_R(\Sigma)) = \frac{2\pi^{m/2} R^m}{m\Gamma(m/2)}
    \int_\Sigma \left(1 + O(R^2) \right)\, d\Sigma. \]
    (See Theorem 9.23 of the same book.)
    
    Our inequality \eqref{eq:volume-inequality} is a specialization to the
    spherical case. While of a similar spirit, it does without the summation
    or series expansion.

\label{thm:minus-infty}
  \(\lim_{r\to0} \kappa_i(r) = -\infty\) for \(i = n+2, \dots, n+m.\)

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Our proof of this already-known fact
  rests on Fermi coordinates, a generalization of normal
  coordinates which is explained in detail in
  \cite[ch.~2]{gray90:_tubes}. A system of Fermi coordinates
  \(x^1, \dots, x^{n+m}\) for \(T_R(\Sigma)\)
  around \(p \in \Sigma\) can be defined when we have
  a coordinate system \(y^1, \dots, y^n\) in an open set \(U \subseteq \Sigma\)
  around \(p,\) and orthonormal sections \(E_{1}, \dots, E_{m}\) of
  \(\mathcal T\Sigma^\perp.\) An arbitrary point of \(\mathcal T U^\perp\)
  can be written as \(\sum_{j = 1}^m t^j E_j(q)\) for a certain
  \(q \in U.\) The associated Fermi coordinate system on
  \(T_R(U) = \{\exp_{\mathcal T\Sigma^\perp}(q, v)\ |\ (q, v) \in \mathcal T U^\perp\ |\ \|v\| \le R\}\)
  is given by:
  \begin{align*}
      x^i \left( \exp_{\mathcal T \Sigma^\perp}
      \sum_{j = 1}^m t^{j}E_{j}(q) \right) &= y^i(q), && i = 1, \dots, n; \\
      x^{n+i} \left( \exp_{\mathcal T \Sigma^\perp}
      \sum_{j = 1}^m t^{j}E_{j}(q)\right) &= t^i, && i = 1, \dots, m.
  \end{align*}
  Note that the coordinate fields
  \(\partial_1, \dots, \partial_n\) are tangent to \(\Sigma\) at \(p,\)
  whereas \(\partial_{n+1}, \dots,\allowbreak \partial_{n+m} \) are orthogonal to
  \(\Sigma\) at \(p.\)
  
  Let's take a Fermi coordinate system \(x^1, \dots, x^{n+m}\)
  around \(p = \gamma(0) \in \Sigma\) that also satisfies 
  \(\partial_{n+1}|_{\gamma(r)} = \gamma'(r) = N_{\gamma(r)} \)
  and \(\partial_j|_{\gamma(0)} = F_j\) for \(j=1, \dots, n.\)
  Take one of the eigenvectors
  \(F \in \{F_{n+2}, \dots, F_{n+m} \}\)
  with associated eigenvalue \(\kappa.\)
  We will show that \(\lim_{r\to0} r\kappa(r) = -1,\) from
  which the lemma follows. Writing \(F = F^i \partial_i\)
  (using the Einstein summation convention), we have:
  \begin{align*}
      r\kappa(r)
      &= r\langle S F, F\rangle_{\gamma(r)} \\
      &= r\langle -\nabla_{F} N, F \rangle_{\gamma(r)} \\
      &= -r\langle F^j \nabla_{\partial_j} N, F \rangle_{\gamma(r)} \\
      &= -r\langle F^j (\nabla_{N} \partial j + [\partial_j, N]), F \rangle_{\gamma(r)} \\
      &= -rF^j \langle \nabla_{\partial_{n+1}} \partial_j, F \rangle_{\gamma(r)}
          -r\langle F^j [\partial_j, N], F \rangle_{\gamma(r)}.
      \end{align*}
    The first term tends to zero as \(r \to 0.\)
    The Lie Brackets in the second term are calculated
    in Lemma 2.8 of \cite{gray90:_tubes}.
    They are \([\partial_j, N] = 0\)
    for \(j = 1, \dots, n+1,\) whereas their value for
    \(j = n+2, \dots, n+m\) is:
    \[ [\partial_j, N] = \frac{1}{r}\partial_j - \frac{\partial_j r}{r} N. \]
    Thus:
    \begin{align*}
    \lim_{r \to 0} r\kappa(r)
    &= \lim_{r\to0} -r\langle F^j [\partial_j, N], F \rangle_{\gamma(r)} \\
    &= \lim_{r\to0} r\left\langle - \sum_{j=n+2}^{n+m} \frac{F^j\partial_j}{r}, F \right\rangle_{\gamma(r)} \\
    &= \lim_{r \to 0} -\left\langle \sum_{j=n+2}^{n+m} F^j \partial_j, F \right\rangle_{\gamma(r)} \\
    &= \lim_{r \to 0} -\left\langle \sum_{j=1}^{n+m} F^j \partial_j, F \right\rangle_{\gamma(r)} \\
    &= \lim_{r\to0} -\langle F, F \rangle_{\gamma(r)} \\
    &= -1.
    \end{align*}
    The fourth equality requires some explanation. First,
    \(\langle \partial_{n+1}, F \rangle_{\gamma(r)} =
    \langle N, F \rangle_{\gamma(r)} = 0.\)
    Second, for \(j = 1, \dots, n,\) we have that
    \(\partial_j|_{\gamma(0)} = F_j|_{\gamma(0)}.\) Hence,
    \(\lim_{r\to0}\langle \partial_j, F \rangle_{\gamma(r)} \allowbreak=
    \lim_{r\to0} \langle F_j, F \rangle_{\gamma(r)} = 0.\)
    Lastly, each of the \(F^j\) are bounded because \(\|F\| = 1.\)
