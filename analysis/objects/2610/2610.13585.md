<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
We'll say that a function $\phi$ from some domain $X$ to reals is lower semicomputable iff there is a computable
function $f$ from $X,\mathbb{N}$ to rationals, such that $f(x,n+1)\geq f(x,n)$ and $\phi(x)=\lim_n f(x,n)$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A (possibly partial) function $\mu$ from strings to non-negative reals is called a (probability) \textit{measure} iff $\mu(\epsilon)=1$ and for any string $a$ and bit $v$,
when $\mu(av)$ is defined, then $\mu(a\bar{v})$ is also defined and $\mu(a)=\mu(a0)+\mu(a1)$.
If  $\mu(a)\geq\mu(a0)+\mu(a1)$, $\mu$ is called a \textit{semimeasure}.
The ratio between  measures $\mu$ and $\lambda$ we denote with $\dot\mu$ and call a \textit{martingale}, .
The interpretation is that of a gambler's betting strategy, 
where, having bet on the first $|a|$ bits of a sequence extending $a$,
 a bet is placed on the value of the next bit by
dividing the \textit{capital} $\dot\mu(a)$ between
two outcomes, $a0,a1$.  
For outcome $av$, the capital put on $a\bar v$ is lost, and the capital put on $av$ is doubled.
The martingale \textit{wins} on sequence $\alpha$ if 
the capital over the sequence of bets is unbounded,  
$\sup_n \dot\mu(\alpha{1:n}) = \infty$. 

If $\mu$ is a semimeasure, $\dot\mu$ is called a \textit{supermartingale}.
We will look at \textit{partial computable martingales} where $\mu$ is partial computable,
and at  \textit{lower semicomputable supermartingales} where $\mu$ is a lower semicomputable semimeasure.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $\pi$ be a (possibly partial) function
from strings to natural numbers such that 
if $\pi(a)$ is defined then it is also defined for all prefixes of $a$,
and $\pi(a)\neq\pi(a')$, for any prefix $a'$ of $a$.
Such $\pi$ is called a \textit{scan rule}, and we interpret the output as the position of bit to be scanned next.
For a string $s$ consisting of $n$ bits, we'll call the finite assignment 

$(\pi(\epsilon),s[1]),(\pi(s[1]),s[2]),\dots,(\pi(s[1:n-1]),s[n])$ \textit{$\pi$'s finite assignment for $s$} and denote it with $\mathtt{fa}_\pi(s)$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
A \textit{betting strategy} is a pair of scan rule and measure.
Let $A=(\pi,\mu)$ be a betting strategy.
We can view martingales as betting strategies where the scan rule is in-order, $\pi(a)=|a|+1$.
When the scan rule is not restricted to be in order, to highlight this, we say that the betting strategy is \textit{nonmonotonic}.
We will be interested in betting strategies where $\pi$ is not necessarily in-order, and both $\pi$ and  $\mu$ are total computable, and we'll call these strategies \textit{total computable nonmonotonic betting strategies}.

For strategy $A$, we'll write $\mathtt{fa}_A$ and mean $\mathtt{fa}_\pi$. Instead of writing $\mathtt{pos}(\mathtt{fa}_A(s))$ we write $\mathtt{pos}_A(s)$.

Let $\alpha,\sigma$ be sequences such that for all $n$, $\alpha$ is consistent
with $\mathtt{fa}_A(\sigma[1:n])$.
We'll say that $A$ has \textit{capital} 
$\dot\mu(\sigma[1:n])=\frac{\mu(\sigma[1:n])}{\lambda(\tilde{\mathtt{fa}_A(\sigma[1:n]))}}=2^n\mu(\sigma[1:n])$
when betting on $\alpha$ after $n$ bets, and
 wins on $\alpha$ if $\sup_n \dot\mu(\sigma_{1:n}) = \infty$. 

We will be defining strategies recursively.
If a strategy is defined on a string $x$, but not on $x0,x1$, we'll say that $x$ is a \textit{leaf} of the strategy. 
Defining a bet for a leaf $x$ consists of defining the scan rule for $x$, and measure for the two new
leafs $x0,x1$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
We'll say that a sequence is \textit{partially computably random} if no partial computable martingale wins on the sequence.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
We'll say that a sequence is \textit{Kolmogorov-Loveland random} if no total computable nonmonotonic betting strategy wins on the sequence.
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
We'll say that a sequence is \textit{Martin-L\"of random} if no lower semicomputable supermartingale wins on the sequence. 
A lower semicomputable supermartingale is universal if it  multiplicatively dominates all other lower semicomputable supermartingales.
We pick some universal lower semicomputable supermartingale  and denote it with $u$.
<!-- end definition-7 -->
<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
We'll call a map from basic to clopen sets of binary sequences an \textit{encoding} if it preserves uniform Lebesgue measure and the disjointedness relation, that is, for any string $a$,  $\lambda(E(\tilde{a}))=\lambda(\tilde{a})$ and $E(\tilde{a})=E(\tilde{a0})\sqcup E(\tilde{a1})$.
For an encoding $E$ and sequence $\alpha$, instead of writing $\bigcap_n E(\tilde\alpha[1:n])$ we'll  just write $E(\alpha)$.
<!-- end definition-8 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm_no_E_for_PCR}
There is no computable encoding $E$ such that for every non-MLR sequence $\alpha$ the sequences in $E(\alpha)$ are non-PCR.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Suppose there is such an $E$.
We'll construct a lower semicomputable semimeasure $\gamma$ so that $\dot\gamma$ wins on some sequence $\alpha$ such that $E(\alpha)$ contains a PCR sequence.
Let $\mu_1,\mu_2,\dots$ be a computable enumeration of partial computable measures.
Let $\hat \mu$ denote the largest capital achieved on a string so far, $\hat \mu (s) = \sup_{\ell\leq |s|} \dot\mu (s[1:\ell])$.

To construct $\gamma$, we first construct two "helper" functions $F,Q$.
$F$ is a partial computable function whose input is
a finite sequence of strings $x=s_1,\dots,s_n$ and outputs a string pair $(a,b)$
where $a$ will have high capital achieved by $\gamma$,
 and $b$  has bounded capital achieved by the first $n$ partial computable martingales
 and is a subset of$E(\tilde a)$.
$Q$ is a partial computable function from finite sequences of strings to rationals that computes bounds on
achieved capital by partial computable martingales.
The strings in the pair $F(x[1:i+1])$ extend the strings in the pair $F(x[1:i])$ and we'll construct
a pair of sequences $(\alpha,\beta)$ such that $\beta\in E(\alpha)$ and $\gamma$ wins on $\alpha$,
whereas every partial computable martingale achieves only bounded capital on $\beta$.

For empty sequence of strings, $\xi$, we define $F(\xi)=(\epsilon,\epsilon)$ and $Q(\xi)=0$.
Let $x$ be a finite sequence of strings, $x=s_1,\dots,s_n$,
and let $x'$ be $x$ extended by string $s_{n+1}$,  
$x'=s_1,\dots,s_n,s_{n+1}=x,s_{n+1}$.
If $F(x)$ or $Q(x)$ is undefined then $F(x')$ and $Q(x')$ are both undefined (for any $s_{n+1}$).
Suppose both $F(x)$ and $Q(x)$ are defined.
Let $(a,b)=F(x)$.
For $i\leq n$, denote with $q_i=Q(x[1:i])$. 
Denote with $C_n(b)$ the weighted sum of achieved capitals
by the first $n$ partial computable martingales,
 $C_n(b) = \sum_{i\leq n} q_i \hat \mu_i(b)$.
If for $F(x),Q(x)$ the condition
\begin{equation}
C_n(b)< 2\text{ and }\tilde{b}\subseteq E(\tilde a). 
\label{CA}
\end{equation}
is not satisfied, $F(x'),Q(x')$ are undefined (for any $s_{n+1}$).

We'll now define how $F(x')$ and $Q(x')$ are computed, when $F(x),Q(x)$ are defined and condition \eqref{CA} is satisfied. 
Based on the string $s_{n+1}$, we'll make an additional assumption $H(x')$.
\begin{numcases}{H(x') = }
s_{n+1}=\epsilon, &for all  $b'\succeq b$, if $C_n(b') < 2$, $\mu_{n+1}(b')$ is defined\label{allb} \\
s_{n+1}\neq\epsilon, &$\mu_{n+1}$ is not defined on $b'=bs_{n+1}$ and $C_n(b') < 2$\label{existsb}
\end{numcases}

If $s_{n+1}\neq\epsilon$,
we set $q_{n+1} = Q(x') = 0$. 
Let $b' = bs_{n+1}$. By assumption $H(x')$, $C_{n+1}(b')<2$.
We then compute every measure $\mu_i, i\leq n$ for which $q_i>0$ on all continuations of $b'$ until we find
some $b''$ such that there is a continuation of $a$, $a'$ that is $2|s_{n+1}|+2$ bits longer than $a$ and
$C_{n+1}(b'')\leq C_{n+1}(b')$ and $\tilde{b''}\subseteq E(a')$. If we find such $b''$, we set $F(x')=(a',b'')$.

If $s_{n+1}=\epsilon$, 
we define $\frac{1}{q_{n+1}}=2\frac{ \hat c_{n+1}(b)}{(2-C_n(b))}$ and set $Q(x')=q_{n+1}$.
We have $C_{n+1}(b)=C_n(b) + \frac{2-C_n(b)}{2} < 2$.
We then compute every measure $\mu_i, i\leq n+1$ for which $q_i>0$ on all continuations of $b'$ until we find
some $b''$ such that there is an $a'$ that extends $a$ by $2$ bits and
$C_{n+1}(b')\leq C_{n+1}(b)$ and $\tilde{b'}\subseteq E(a')$. If we find such $b'$, we set $F(x')=(a',b')$.

\begin{claim}\label{cl_H}
If all of the assumptions $H(s_1), H(x[1:2]),\dots, H(x),H(x')$ are true, for some string $s_{n+1}$ assumption $H(x')$ is true.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
There either exists some $b'\succ b$ such that $\mu_{n+1}$ is undefined and $C_n(b')<2$ (case \eqref{existsb}) or
not (case \eqref{allb}). Therefore, for some string $s_{n+1}$ assumption $H(x')$ is true.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}\label{cl_v}
If all of the assumptions $H(s_1), H(x[1:2]),\dots, H(x),H(x')$ are true,
then for any $b'\succeq b$, such that $C_{n+1}(b')<2$ there is some bit $v$
such that $C_{n+1}(b'v)<2$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Let $I$ be the subset of indices $i\leq n+1$ such that $s_i\neq\epsilon$ (or, equivalently, $q_i>0$).
Order $I$ in increasing order, $I=\{i_1,\dots,i_z\}$, and denote
$(a_1,b_1)=F(x'[1:i_1]),\dots,(a_z,b_z)=F(x'[1:i_z])$.
We have that $b_1\prec\dots\prec b_z\preceq b\preceq b'$.
Since $C_{i_1-1}$ is always zero, condition $H(x[1:i_1])$ implies that $\mu_{i_1}$ is defined on all continuations of $b_1$,
and in particular on $b'0$ and $b'1$. Then $C_{i_1}(b'0)$ and $C_{i_1}(b'1)$ are both defined.
Since $C_{i_1}(b')\leq C_{n+1}(b') < 2$  and  $\frac{C_{i_1}(b'0) + C_{i_1}(b'1)}{2}=C_{i_1}(b')$,
we have that for at least one $v$, $C_{i_1}(b'v)<2$.
But then, by $H(x[1:i_2])$ , $\mu_{i_2}$ is defined on $b'v$, and by definition of measure it is also defined on 
$b'\bar{v}$. Again, for at least one $v$, $C_{i_2}(b'v)<2$.
And so on...
We have that $C_{n+1}(b'v)$ is defined for both values of $v$, and for at least one $C_{n+1}(b'v)<2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}\label{cl_indstep}
If all of the assumptions $H(s_1), H(x[1:2]),\dots, H(x),H(x')$ are true, 
then both $F(x')$ and $Q(x')$ are defined,
and satisfy condition \eqref{CA}.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
In case $s_{n+1}$ is a nonempty string, $Q(x')=0$ and 
we have $C_{n+1}(bs_{n+1})<2$.
In the other case, $s_{n+1}$ is an empty string, $Q(x')>0$ and $C_{n+1}(b)<2$.
In both cases $Q(x')$ is defined.
Denote $b'=bs_{n+1}$.
In both cases $b\preceq b'$  and $C_{n+1}(b')<2$.
Let $\ell$ be the smallest number such that for every string $s$ of length $\ell$,
and any $a'\succ a$ with $|a'|-|a|=2|s_{n+1}|+2$, we have that $\tilde{s}$ is either a subset of $E(a')$ or disjoint from it.
By repeated use of claim \ref{cl_v} we can find $b''\succeq b'$ of length $\ell$
such that $C_{n+1}(b'')<2$.
We have that $\tilde{b''}$ is a subset of $E(a')$ for some
$a'\succ a$ with $|a'|-|a|=2|s_{n+1}|+2$. The pair $(a',b'')$ satisfies condition \eqref{CA}.
The value $F(x')$ is the first such pair that is found.
\end{proof}

For $F(\xi),Q(\xi)$, condition \eqref{CA} is satisfied.
By claims \ref{cl_H}, \ref{cl_indstep} and induction, 
there is an infinite sequence of strings $\chi = s_1,s_2,\dots$ such that
for all $n$, $H(\chi[1:n])$ is true and $F(\chi[1:n]), Q(\chi[1:n])$ are defined and satisfy \eqref{CA}.
We'll say that such $\chi$ is \textit{good}.
Denote $(a_n,b_n)=F(\chi[1:n])$.
Let the pair $(\alpha,\beta)$ denote the binary sequences such that for all $n$, 
$a_n\prec \alpha$ and $b_n\prec \beta$.
We'll say that the pair $(\alpha,\beta)$ is \textit{good}.
\begin{claim}
If $(\alpha,\beta)$  is a good pair, $\beta$ is PCR and $\beta\in E(\alpha)$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Clearly, $\beta\in E(\alpha)$.
The $i$-th partially computable measure $\mu_i$ is either undefined on $\beta$, or
is less than $C_i(\beta)/q_i$ which is less than $2/q_i$, and $\dot\mu_i$ does not win on $\beta$.
Therefore no partially computable martingale wins on $\beta$.
\end{proof}

All that remains to show is that $\alpha$ is non-MLR.
We define a function that assigns rational weights to sequences of strings. 
$w(s_1,\dots,s_n) = \prod_{i\leq n} 2^{-2|s_i|-1}$.
Let $G(a)$ denote the set of sequences of strings such that $s_1,\dots,s_n,s_{n+1}$ is in the set iff
$a'\prec a \preceq a''$ where $(a',b')=F(s_1,\dots,s_n)$ and $(a'',b'')=F(s_1,\dots,s_n,s_{n+1})$.
We define $\gamma(a)=\sum_{x\in G(a)} w(x)$.

\begin{claim}
$\gamma$ is a lower semicomputable semimeasure.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Since $F$ is partially computable, $G(a)$ is computably enumerable and $\gamma$ is lower semicomputable.
We still need to show that for all $a$, $\gamma(a) \geq \gamma(a0) + \gamma(a1)$.
For any sequence of strings $x$, summing weights over all single-string continuations of $x$,
we have $$\sum_{s\in\{0,1\}^*} w(x,s)=w(x)\sum_{s\in\{0,1\}^*} 2^{-2|s|-1}=w(x)\sum_{\ell\geq 0} 2^\ell2^{-2\ell-1}=w(x)$$.

Let $x\in G(a)$ and $(a',b')=F(x[1:|x|-1])$, $(a'',b'')=F(x)$.
We split $G(a)$ into two sets, $G^\prec(a)$ and $G^=(a)$.
If $a'\prec a \prec a''$, then $x\in G^\prec(a)$.
On the other hand,  if $a'\prec a = a''$, then $x\in G^=(a)$.
For $x\in G^\prec(a)$, either $a'\prec a0 \preceq a''$ or $a'\prec a1 \preceq a''$ (but not both),
so either $x\in G(a0)$ or $x\in G(a1)$.
For $x\in G^=(a)$, $x$ is neither in $G(a0)$ nor in $G(a1)$.
Let $x\in G^=(a)$ and $s$ any string. 
Suppose  $F(x,s)$ is defined and denote $(a^*,b^*)=F(x,s)$. 
Then again, either 
$a''\prec a0 \preceq a^*$ or $a''\prec a1 \preceq a^*$ (but not both),
so either $x,s\in G(a0)$ or $x,s\in G(a1)$.
We have that $G(a0)$ and $G(a1)$ are disjoint,
and $\gamma(a0) + \gamma(a1) = \sum_{x\in G^\prec(a)} w(x) + \sum_{x\in G^=(a)}\sum_{s\in\{0,1\}^*, F(x,s)\text{is defined} } w(x,s)$.
This is less than the sum
$\sum_{x\in G^\prec(a)} w(x) + \sum_{x\in G^=(a)}\sum_{s\in\{0,1\}^*} w(x,s)$.
This  is equal to
$\sum_{x\in G^\prec(a)} w(x) + \sum_{x\in G^=(a)} w(x) = \sum_{x\in G(a)} w(x) = \gamma(a)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}
If $(\alpha,\beta)$  is a good pair, $\alpha$ is non-MLR.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
We have that $|a_n|=\sum_{i\leq n} 2|s_i|+2$, and $\gamma(a) \geq w(\chi[1:n])= \prod_{i\leq n} 2^{-2|s_i|-1}$.
Then $\gamma(a_n)/\lambda(a_n)\geq2^n$, and $\dot\gamma$ wins on $\alpha$.
\end{proof}

We have shown that $\alpha$ is non-MLR, and $E(\alpha)$ contains $\beta$ that is PCR, a contradiction.

<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{KLR=_EMLR}
There is a computable encoding $E$ and two total computable nonmonotonic betting strategies $A,B$ such that for every non-MLR sequence $\sigma$ and every $\rho$ in $E(\sigma)$, $A$ or $B$ win on $\rho$.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
We first define the encoding $E$.
\begin{definition}[Encoding]
For string $s$:\\ 
Function $v$ computes the integer value of $s$, $v(s):=2^0\cdot s[1] +\dots+ 2^{|s|-1}s[|s|]$\\
Function $p$ assigns a unique position to each $s$, $p(s):=2^{|s|}+v(s)$.\\
Function $e$ is $p$'s finite assignment for $s$, that is, $e(s):=\mathtt{fa}_p(s)$.\\
The encoder, $E$, is the (clopen) set of sequences consistent with the finite assignment for $s$, $E(\tilde s)=\tilde{e}(s)$.
\end{definition}
\begin{claim}\label{E_is_encoding}
Encoder preserves uniform Lebesgue measure and disjointedness relation 
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
For any string $s$, $\lambda(E(\tilde s))=\lambda(\tilde e(s))=2^{-|e(s)|}=2^{-|s|}=\lambda(s)$.
For all strings $s_1,s_2$,
if $\tilde s_1$ is disjoint from $\tilde s_2$, then there is some 
string $s$ and bit $v$ such that $sv\preceq s_1$ and $s\bar v\preceq s_2$.
But then $e(s_1)$ assigns $v$  and $e(s_2)$ assigns $\bar v$ to position $p(s)$, and $e(s_1),e(s_2)$ are inconsistent.
Therefore  $E(\tilde s_1),E(\tilde s_2)$  are disjoint.
\end{proof}

We will define the betting strategies $A,B$ in stages.
A stage starts with a pair of strings $a,b$, 
their finite assignments $\mathtt{fa}_A(a), \mathtt{fa}_B(b)$, 
and measures $\mu_A(a),\mu_B(b)$.
This stage will define the strategies $A,B$ on extensions of $a,b$.
As the stage computes $A,B$, for some of the pairs of extensions, the stage will stop, and the pair will exit the stage.
On such pairs, we start the next stage, and so on, defining $A,B$ entirely.

\begin{definition}[Stage]
A stage starts with a pair of strings
for which the finite assignments 
and measures of strategies $A,B$
have already been defined.
We require that the measures are non-zero and that strings are leafs of $A,B$ such that their finite assignments are equivalent, and even more, equivalent to a string.
Denote the input pair with $(a,b)$ and string $c\equiv\mathtt{fa}_A(a)\equiv\mathtt{fa}_B(b)$ .


Stage iteratively defines strategies $A,B$ on leafs extending $a,b$.
The capitals  on strings $a,b$ will determine how the bets are defined.
The strategy with the smaller capital will at first place bets on positions in the same order as the position function $p$, we'll call this the \textit{forward} strategy and label it with $F$.  
The strategy with the larger capital will at first skip the first position and place bets on other positions determined by $p$ and later return to the first position. 
We'll call this the \textit{rewind} strategy and label it with $R$.
So if $\dot\mu_A\leq\dot\mu_B$, then $F=A$, $R=B$, and $F,R$ will be defined on continuations of $f,r = a,b$.
In the other case, $F=B$, $R=A$, and $F,R$ will be defined on continuations of $f,r = b,a$.
We set the threshold $T$ for the capital that needs to be achieved
$T=\frac{3}{2}c_R(r)$.

We'll define the measure functions $\mu_F$, $\mu_R$ as sums of two measure functions. 
One, $\eta$, will assign measure evenly, this ensures that the strings always have non-zero measure assigned to them.
The other that we will define, $\nu$, will increase (or decrease) capital on some of the strings.
We set $\eta_F(\epsilon)=\nu_F(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_F(f)$ and
$\eta_R(\epsilon)=\nu_R(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_R(r)$.
We have
$\mu_F(f)=\eta_F(f)+\nu_F(f)$, $\mu_R(r)=\eta_R(r)+\nu_R(r)$.

Since the encoding's position function $p$, 
as the input strings become longer, chooses positions in increasing order, 
there exists a string $s$ such that $E(\tilde s)$ still contains $\tilde{c}$, and $p(s)>|c|$.
 We will computably enumerate a set of strings $M$, that are continuations of this $s$ and on which the universal lower semicomputable semimeasure $u$
 achieves capital larger than $H=\frac{2T}{ \lambda(s)\dot\nu_F(f)}$.
 More precisely, starting with empty $M$,
 we dovetail the computation of $u$ on all inputs, until finding $v\succ s$ such that
 $\dot u(v)\geq H$.
 If $\tilde v$ is already a subset of $\tilde M$, nothing new gets  added to $M$,
 otherwise, we will
 add one by one strings $s_1,\dots,s_n$ 
 such that $\tilde{s_1},\dots,\tilde{s_n}$ are disjoint and
 contain all of the sequences in $\tilde M\setminus\tilde{v}$.
 
 When we add a string $s'$ to $M$,
 we define finitely many new bets 
 in an \textit{iteration} of the stage .
Let $M'$ denote the strings that have been added in previous iterations.
The bets are defined only for the leafs of $F,R$ that don't extend strings that have exited the stage
after some previous iteration.
We denote these leafs $F^-,R^-$.
We define bets on strings that extend $F^-,R^-$ in three consecutive phases.
 \\
 \\ 
Phase F)\\
 For every $f^-\in F^-$, every leaf of $F$, $f'$, that extends $f^-$,
 if $\mathtt{fa}_F(f')$ is consistent, but doesn't refine $e(s')$,
 we define $\pi_F(f')$ to be the smallest position in $\mathtt{pos}(e(s'))$ on which $\mathtt{fa}_F(f')$ doesn't have assigned value.
 The mass is split evenly, $\nu_F(f'0)=\nu_F(f'1)=\frac{1}{2}\nu_F(f')$.
 We keep doing this until for every leaf $f'$ of $F$ that extends some $f^-\in F^-$,
 $\mathtt{fa}_F(f')$ is inconsistent with $e(s')$, or refines it.
\\
\\
Phase R)\\
For every $r^-\in R^-$, every leaf of $R$, $r'$, that extends $r^-$,
 if $\mathtt{fa}_R(r')$ is consistent, but doesn't refine $e(s')$,
 we define $\pi_R(r')$ to be the smallest position larger than $p(s)$ in $\mathtt{pos}(e(s'))$ on which $\mathtt{fa}_F(r')$ doesn't have assigned value, if there is such a position.
 In this case, the mass is split evenly, $\nu_R(r'0)=\nu_R(b'1)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_R(r')$.
 In the other case, $p(s)$ is the only position in $\mathtt{pos}(e(s'))$ on which $\mathtt{fa}_F(r')$ doesn't have assigned value.
 We define $\pi_R(r')=p(s)$. Let $v=s'[|s|+1]$, and set $\nu_R(r'v)=\nu_R(r')$, $\nu_R(r'\bar{v})=0$. 
 We repeat defining bets like this until for every leaf $r'$ of $R$ that extends some $r^-\in R^-$ we have that 
 $\mathtt{fa}_R(r')$ is inconsistent from $e(s')$, or refines it.
\\

                                                              
Let $F',R'$ denote the leafs extending $F^-,R^-$ after phases F), R), whose finite assignments are inconsistent
with $e(s')$, and $F^+,R^+$ the ones that refine $e(s')$. Let $Z$ be the strings in $R^-$ that have measure $\nu_R$ zero, and denote the set of sequences consistent 
 with their finite assignments with $\tilde Z = \bigsqcup_{z\in Z} \tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_R(z)$.
 \\
 \\
Phase C)\\
 Let $g$ be the maximal position in finite assignments of leafs of both strategies.
Let $r^+$ be a leaf in $R^+$,
Let $G$ be a subset of $[1,g]$ on which  $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^+)$ does not assign a value.
For each string $x$ of length $|G|$, we define $\pi_R$ so that $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^+x)$ assigns values to all positions in $[1,g]$. The mass $\nu_R$ is distributed evenly,
$\dot\nu_R(r^+x)=\dot\nu_R(r^+)$.
 Let $f^+$ be a leaf in $F^+$.
 Like before, let $G$ be a subset of $[1,g]$ on which  $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^+)$ does not assign a value,
 For each string $x$ of length $|G|$, we define $\pi_F$ so that $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^+x)$ assigns values to all positions in $[1,g]$.
 This is the same as what we did in this phase for $R$ when defining $\pi_R$.
 However, the measure $\nu_F$ is not always distributed evenly.
 
 In case $\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f')$ intersects $\tilde Z$ we assign measure the following way.
 If $\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f'x)\subseteq \tilde Z$ then $\nu_F(f'x)=\frac{2^{-|x|}}{\lambda(\tilde Z|\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f'))}\nu_F(f')$, and if not
 then $\nu_F(f'x)=0$.
 In case $\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f')$ is disjoint from $\tilde Z$, the measure $\nu_F$ is distributed evenly among continuations of $f'$.
\\
 
 Let $F^\circ,R^\circ$ be the leafs extending strings in $F^+,R^+$, after phase C).
 The strings in $F^\circ,R^\circ$ exit the stage, $s'$ is added to $M'$, and the stage then continues with the 
 adding of strings to $M$.
\end{definition}

In the remainder of the proof, we use the notation from the above definition, and
in addition to the notation used for an arbitrary iteration,
for $i$-th iteration we denote the input string with $s_i$,
$M_{i-1}=\{s_1,\dots,s_{i-1}\}$  is the set strings that were enumerated into $M$ in the first $i-1$ iterations,
$F^-_{i-1},R^-_{i-1}$  the leafs in $F,R$, before any of the bets for $s_i$ were defined, that do not extend some string that exited stage after the first $i-1$ iterations,
$F'_i,R'_i,F^+_i,R^+_i$ the leafs extending $F^-_{i-1},R^-_{i-1}$  after phases F) R) are finished, and
with $F^\circ_i,R^\circ_i$ the leafs that exit the stage.

From the definition of stage we have $M_0=\{\}, F^-_0=\{f\}, R^-_0=\{r\}$, 
and $\tilde F^+_{i} \sqcup \tilde F'_{i}=\tilde F^-_{i-1}$, $\tilde R^+_{i} \sqcup \tilde R'_{i}=\tilde R^-_{i-1}$,
$\tilde F^\circ_i=\tilde F^+_i$, $\tilde R^\circ_i=\tilde R^+_i$,
$F^-_i=F'_i$, $R^-_i=R'_i$, $M_i=M_{i-1}\cup\{s_i\}$.

\begin{claim}\label{cl_F}
Let $f^-\in F^-$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), ref=\theclaim(\arabic*)]
\item If $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^-)$ inconsistent with $e(s')$ then $F'|f^-=\{f^-\}$.\label{cl_F:f-inc_e}

\item If $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^-)$ consistent with $e(s')$, then for 
every $f'\in F'|f^-$ there is some
string $x$, and bit $v$ such that $s\preceq xv\preceq s'$  and \\ 
$\mathtt{fa}_F(f')=\mathtt{fa}_F(f),e(x\bar v)[|s|+1:|x\bar v|]$ and 
$\dot\nu_F(f')=\dot\nu_F(f)$.\label{cl_F:f-con_e_f'}

\item If $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^-)$ consistent with $e(s')$, then there is exactly one 
$f^+\in F^+|f^-$, and $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^+)=\mathtt{fa}_F(f),e(s')[|s|+1,|s'|]$ and 
$\dot\nu_F(f^+)=\dot\nu_F(f)$. \label{cl_F:f-con_e_f+}
 
                        
                                                                 
                                                                                          

\item If $\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f^+)$ intersects $\tilde Z$ then for all
$f^\circ\in F^\circ|f^+$,
if, for some $r^-\in Z$, $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^\circ)$ refines $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$
then $\dot\nu_F(f^\circ)=\dot\nu_F(f^-)/\lambda(\tilde Z| \tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f^+))$
else $\nu_F(f^\circ)=0$. \label{cl_F:f-con_e_Z_fo}
\end{enumerate}
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
By definition of phase F), if $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^-)$ is inconsistent with $e(s')$, then no new bets are defined for $f^-$, and
$f^-$ does not exit the stage, therefore \ref{cl_F:f-inc_e}.

For $i=0$, $F^-_i=\{f\}$, and we have that for every $f^-\in F^-_i$, $f^-$ fulfills the condition that
there is some string $y\succeq s$  such that $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^-)$ is 
equal to $\mathtt{fa}_F(f),e(y)[|s|+1:|y|]$ and $\dot\nu_F(f^-)=\dot\nu_F(f)$, to see this set $y=s$.
Assume that the same is true for some $i>0$.
Let $f^-\in F^-_i$.
If $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^-)$ is inconsistent with $e(s_{i+1})$, then by \ref{cl_F:f-inc_e},
$F'_i|f^-=\{f^-\}$, and the condition is true for all $f'\in F'_i|f^-$.
If $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^-)$ is consistent with $e(s_{i+1})$, then for some $y\preceq s_{i+1}$
$\mathtt{fa}_F(f^-)=\mathtt{fa}_F(f),e(y)[|s|+1:|y|]$ and $\dot\nu_F(f^-)=\dot\nu_F(f)$.
Let $\ell=|s_{i+1}|-|y|$, and let $v_1, \dots, v_\ell$ be the sequence of bits that extend $y$ to $s_{i+1}$,
that is, $s_{i+1}=y v_1 \dots v_{\ell}$
By definition of phase F),
$F'_{i+1}|f^-=\{f^-\bar v_1,f^-v_1\bar v_2,\dots, f^- v_1\dots v_{\ell-1}\bar v_{\ell} \}$,
and for every $f'\in F'|f^-$, there is some $n\leq \ell$, $f'=f^- v_1\dots v_{n-1}\bar v_{n} $ 
and $\mathtt{fa}_F(f' )$ is 
equivalent to $\mathtt{fa}_F(f),e(yv_1\dots v_{n-1}\bar v_{n})[|s|+1:|y|+n]$ and $\dot\nu_F(f')=\dot\nu_F(f)$.
Since $F'_{i+1}=\bigsqcup_{f^-\in F^-_i} F'_{i+1}|f^-$, and $F^-_{i+1}=F'_{i+1}$,
we have that for every $f^-\in F^-_{i+1}$, $f^-$ fulfills the condition, and \ref{cl_F:f-con_e_f'} follows by induction.

By \ref{cl_F:f-con_e_f'} for $f^-\in F^-$
there is some string $y\succeq s$  such that $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^-)$ is 
equal to $\mathtt{fa}_F(f),e(y)[|s|+1:|y|]$ and $\dot\nu_F(f^-)=\dot\nu_F(f)$.
If $f^-$ is consistent with $e(s')$ then $y\preceq s'$. 
Let $w$ denote the extension of $y$ to $s'$, that is, $yw=s'$.
By definition of phase F), $f^+=f^-w$, and
$\mathtt{fa}_F(f^+)=\mathtt{fa}_F(f),e(yw)[|s|+1:|yw|]$ and 
$\dot\nu_F(f^+)=\dot\nu_F(f)$, proving \ref{cl_F:f-con_e_f+}.

From definition of phase C), 
$f^\circ\in F^\circ$ assigns to all positions up to $g$,
the largest position assigned by a leaf of either strategy, before phase C) started.
Then for any $r^-\in R^-$, $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^\circ)$ either refines $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$, or is disjoint from it.
Therefore if $\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(F^\circ)\subseteq \tilde Z$, there is some $r^-\in Z$, such that
$\mathtt{fa}_F(f^\circ)$ refines $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$.
The value for $\nu_F(f^\circ)$ in
\ref{cl_F:f-con_e_Z_fo} then follows from definition of phase C).

\end{proof}

\begin{claim}\label{cl_R}
Let $r^-\in R^-$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), ref=\theclaim(\arabic*)]
\item If $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ inconsistent with $e(s')$ then $R'|r^-=\{r^-\}$.\label{cl_R_inc_e}

\item If $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ consistent with $e(s')$ and $p(s)\in\mathtt{pos}_R(r^-)$
then for every $r'\in R'|r^-$, $\dot\nu_R(r')=\dot\nu_R(r^-)$.
For $r^+\in R^+|r^-$, $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^+)$ refines $e(s')$ and $\dot\nu_R(r^+)=\dot\nu_R(r^-)$.
For every $r^\circ\in R^\circ|r^+$, $\dot\nu_R(r^\circ)=\dot\nu_R(r^-)$. \label{cl_R_con_e_p}

\item If $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ consistent with $e(s')$ and $p(s)\not\in\mathtt{pos}_R(r^-)$,
then for every $r^+\in R^+|r^-$, $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^+)$ refines $e(s')$
and $\dot\nu_R(r^+)=2\dot\nu_R(r^-)$, 
and for all $r^\circ\in R^\circ|r^+$ $\dot\nu_R(r^\circ)=\dot\nu_R(r^+)$. \label{cl_R_con_e_nop_r+}

\item If $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ consistent with $e(s')$ and $p(s)\not\in\mathtt{pos}_R(r^-)$,
there is exactly one string $z$ in $R'|r^-$
such that $\mathtt{pos}(e(s'))\subseteq \mathtt{pos}_R(z)$. $\mathtt{fa}_R(z)$ assigns negation of $s'[|s|+1]$
to position $p(s)$ and $\nu_R(z)=0$.
For all other $r'\in R'|r^-$,
$\mathtt{fa}_R(r')$ does not assign a value to $p(s)$, and $\dot\nu_R(r')=\dot\nu_R(r^-)$.  \label{cl_R_con_e_nop_r'}
\end{enumerate}
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
By definition of phase R), if $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ is inconsistent with $e(s')$, then no new bets are defined for $r^-$, and
$r^-$ does not exit the stage, therefore \ref{cl_R_inc_e}.

By definition of phase R), $\dot\nu_R$ changes only in the bet on position $p(s)$, 
if $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ already assigns to $p(s)$, $\dot\nu_R$  remains the same for all strings that extend it.
In phase R) new bets are defined for strings that have finite assignment consistent with $e(s')$,
until all of the positions in $\mathtt{pos}(e(s'))$ are assigned to, therefore $\mathtt{pos}(e(s')) \subseteq \mathtt{pos}_R(r^+)$
and
$\mathtt{fa}_R(r^+)$ is consistent with $e(s')$ implies $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^+)$ refines $e(s')$, proving \ref{cl_R_con_e_p}.

On the other hand, if $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ does not assign a value to $p(s)$, then there is
a single string $x$ that extends $r^-$ such that $\mathtt{fa}_R(x)$ is consistent with $e(s')$ 
and assigns to all positions in $\mathtt{pos}(e(s'))$ except $p(s)$.
Let $v$ be the value that $e(s')$ assigns to position $p(s)$, that is, $v=s'[|s|+1]$.
A new bet is defined for $x$ on position $p(s)$, 
then $\{xv\}=R^+|r^-$, $\mathtt{fa}_R(xv)$ refines $e(s')$ and $\dot\nu_R(xv)=2\dot\nu_R(r^-)$,
and $\mathtt{fa}_R(x\bar v)$ inconsistent with $e(s')$ and $\nu_R(x\bar v)=0$,
proving \ref{cl_R_con_e_nop_r'},\ref{cl_R_con_e_nop_r+}.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}\label{cl_R-}
For every $r^-\in R^-$ either $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$
doesn't assign to $p(s)$ and has $\dot\nu_R(r^-)=\dot\nu_R(r)$
or $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ assigns to $p(s)$ and has $\nu_R(r^-)=0$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
For $i=0$, we have that $R^-_i=\{r\}$ and for every $r^-\in R^-_i$ either $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$
doesn't assign to $p(s)$ and has $\dot\nu_R(r^-)=\dot\nu_R(r)$
or $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ assigns to $p(s)$ and has $\nu_R(r^-)=0$.
Assume this is true for some $i>0$.
If for $r^-\in R^-_i$ $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ assigns to $p(s)$ and has $\nu_R(r^-)=0$,
then for any string $r'$ that extends $r^-$, and in particular
all $r'\in R'|r^-$, $\mathtt{fa}_R(r')$ assigns to $p(s)$ and  $\nu_R(r^-)=0$.
On the other hand, if for $r^-\in R^-_i$ $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ 
doesn't assign to $p(s)$ and has $\dot\nu_R(r^-)=\dot\nu_R(r)$,
in case $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ is inconsistent with $e(s')$, by claim \ref{cl_R_inc_e}, the same is true 
for all $R'|r^-$.
In case $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ is consistent with $e(s')$, by claim \ref{cl_R_con_e_nop_r'},
for every $r'\in R'|r^-$
either $\mathtt{fa}_R(r')$
doesn't assign to $p(s)$ and has $\dot\nu_R(r')=\dot\nu_R(r)$
or $\mathtt{fa}_R(r')$ assigns to $p(s)$ and has $\nu_R(r')=0$.
Since $R'_{i+1}=\bigsqcup_{r^-\in R^-_i} R'|r^-$, and $R^-_{i+1}=R'_{i+1}$.
we have that 
the statement is true for $i+1$, and by induction for arbitrary iteration.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem_Z|f+}
For $f^+\in F^+$, $\lambda(\tilde Z|\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f^+))\leq \frac{\dot\nu_F(f)}{T}$
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Note that by claim \ref{cl_R}, the only case when a string is assigned $\nu_R$ zero is in case
\ref{cl_R_con_e_nop_r'}.
Therefore, if $r'\in Z$ then for some previous iteration $i$
there is an $r^-_i,r'_i\in R^-_i,R'_i$, $r^-_i\preceq r'_i\preceq r'$, $\nu_R(r^-_i)>\nu_R(r_i)=0$,
and
$\mathtt{fa}_R(r_i)$ assigns $\bar v$ to  $p(s)$, where $v$ is the $|s|+1$-st bit of $s_i$ 
and
we have $\mathtt{pos}(e(s_i))\subseteq \mathtt{pos}_R(r_i)\subseteq \mathtt{pos}_R(r')$.
Let $Z_i$ denote the subset of $Z$ such that for $r'\in Z_i$, there is
$r^-_i,r^+_i\in R^-_i,R^+_i$, $r^-_i\preceq r^+_i\preceq r'$, $\nu_R(r^-_i)>\nu_R(r_i)=0$.
Suppose we are in the $n$-th iteration. We have $Z=\bigsqcup_{i<n} Z_i$.
Then
$\lambda(\tilde Z\cap\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f^+))=\sum_{i<n}\lambda(\tilde Z_i\cap\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f^+))$.

By claim \ref{cl_F:f-con_e_f+} $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^+)$ is equivalent to $\mathtt{fa}_F(f),e(s')[|s|+1:|s'|]$,  and for all $i<n$ it is inconsistent with $e(s_i)$,  and $\mathtt{pos}_F(f^+)=\mathtt{pos}(e(s'))\cup [1,|c|]$.
Let $r'\in Z_i$  such that $\mathtt{fa}_R(r'),\mathtt{fa}_F(f^+)$ consistent, and let $v$ be the $|s|+1$-st bit of $s_i$.
We have that $|s|+1$-st bit of $s'$ is $\bar v$, and since encoding's position function $p$ assigns a unique position to each string,
$|\mathtt{pos}(e(s_i))\setminus \mathtt{pos}(e(s_n))|=|s_i|-|s|-1$.
Denote with  $h_i$ the last $|s_i|-|s|-1$ assignments of $e(s_i)$, that is,
$h_i=e(s_i)[|s|+2:|s_i|]$. We have that $\mathtt{pos}(h_i)$ is disjoint from $\mathtt{pos}_F(f^+)$ and every $r'\in Z_i$ refines
$h_i$. Therefore
$\lambda(\tilde Z\cap\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f^+))\leq\sum_{i<n}\lambda(\tilde h_i)\lambda(\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f^+))=2\frac{\lambda(\tilde M_{n-1})}{\lambda(s)} \lambda(\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(f^+))$.
The result follows from $\lambda(\tilde M_{n-1})\leq \lambda(\tilde M)\leq \frac{1}{H}=\frac{\lambda(s)\dot\nu_F(f)}{2T}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem_FR_exit_seqs}
$\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(F^\circ) =\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_R(R^\circ)= E(\tilde s')\cap \tilde c$, and
for every $f^\circ \in F^\circ$ there is a $r^\circ \in R^\circ$ and
string $c'\succ c$
such that $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^\circ)\equiv\mathtt{fa}_R(r^\circ)\equiv c'$,
and $\max(\dot\mu_F(f^\circ),\dot\mu_R(r^\circ))\geq T$,
$\min(\dot\mu_F(f^\circ),\dot\mu_R(r^\circ))\geq 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We have $\tilde F^-=\tilde F^+ \sqcup \tilde F'$,
every $f'\in F'$ has $\mathtt{fa}_F(f')$ inconsistent with $e(s')$,
$\tilde F^+ = \tilde F^\circ$ implies $\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F (F^+)=\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F (F^\circ)$
therefore
$\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F (F^\circ) = \tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F (F^-)\cap E(\tilde s')$. 
Similarly, 
$\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_R (R^\circ) = \tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_R (R^-)\cap E(\tilde s')$.
For first iteration,
$\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F (F_1^\circ) = \tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F (F_0^-)\cap E(s_1)=\tilde c \cap E(s_1)=\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_R (R_0^-)\cap E(\tilde s_1) = \tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_R (R_1^\circ)$.
Assume that for all iterations $i\leq n$ we have  $\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(F_i^\circ) =\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_R(R_i^\circ)= E(\tilde s_i)\cap \tilde c$.
We have $\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(F^-_n)=\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(R^-_n)=\tilde c \setminus \sqcup_{i\leq n}E(\tilde s_i)$.
Since $ \sqcup_{i\leq n} E(\tilde s_i)$ is disjoint from $E(\tilde s_{n+1})$, we have
$\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(F_{n+1}^\circ) =\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_R(R_{n+1}^\circ)= E(\tilde s_{n+1})\cap \tilde c$.
By induction, we have that for arbitrary iteration, $\tilde F^\circ = \tilde R^\circ = \tilde c \cap E(s')$.

Since for any $f^\circ\in F^\circ,r^\circ\in R^\circ$, $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^\circ),\mathtt{fa}_R(r^\circ)$ assign to positions in $[1,g]$, $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^\circ),\mathtt{fa}_R(r^\circ)$ are either inconsistent or equivalent.
Then it cannot be that for some $f^\circ\in F^\circ$ there is no $r^\circ\in R^\circ$ with an equivalent finite assignment,
because then $\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(F^\circ) \neq \tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_R (R^\circ)$.

It remains to show that for each pair $f^\circ\in F^\circ,r^\circ\in R^\circ$
where $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^\circ)\equiv\mathtt{fa}_R(r^\circ)$ we have
 $\max(\dot\nu_F(f^\circ),\dot\nu_R(r^\circ))\geq T$.
 
By claim \ref{cl_F:f-con_e_f'}, for all $f^-\in F^-$, $\dot\nu_F(f^-)=\dot\nu_F(f)$.
Let $f^\circ\in F^\circ, r^\circ\in R^\circ$ and $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^\circ)\equiv \mathtt{fa}_R(r^\circ)$.
Then there is $f^-\in F^-, f^+\in F^+|f^-,r^-\in R^-$ 
such that $f^\circ\in F^\circ|f^+, r^\circ\in R^\circ|r^-$
and $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^-),\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ both consistent with $e(s')$.
If $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ does not assign to $p(s)$, then by claim \ref{cl_R-},
$\dot\nu_R(r^-)=\dot\nu_R(r)$, and by claim \ref{cl_R_con_e_nop_r+},
$\dot\nu_R(r^\circ)=2\dot\nu_R(r)=T$.
On the other hand, if $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ does  assign to $p(s)$, then by claim \ref{cl_R-},
$\nu_R(r^-)=0$. Since for arbitrary $i$, $F'_i=F^-_i$, by claim \ref{cl_F:f-con_e_f'}, $\dot\nu_F(f^-)=\dot\nu_F(f)$ and by claim \ref{cl_F:f-con_e_Z_fo},
$\dot\nu_F(f^\circ)=\dot\nu_F(f)/\lambda(\tilde Z |\mathtt{fa}_F(f^+))$.
This is by lemma \ref{lem_Z|f+} larger than $T$.
Therefore $\max(\dot\nu_F(f^\circ),\dot\nu_R(r^\circ))\geq T$.
We have that $\dot\eta_F(f^\circ)=\dot\eta_F(f)$, $\dot\eta_R(r^\circ)=\dot\eta_R(r)$.
Since $\mu$ is a sum of $\eta$ and $\nu$, and the stage requires that  $\mu_F(f),\mu_R(r)$ are larger than zero,
the result follows.
\end{proof}

If $F=A,R=B$
let $C_i$ denote the set of all pairs of strings $(a', b')\in F^\circ_i \times R^\circ_i$
such that $\mathtt{fa}_A(a'),\mathtt{fa}_B(b')$ are equivalent to a same string.
If $F=B,R=A$, then $C_i$ are pairs $(a', b')\in R^\circ_i \times F^\circ_i$ that are equivalent to a same string.
For a pair in $C_i$ we say that the pair exits the stage in $i$-th iteration.
Let $C$ be the set of pairs that exit the stage in some iteration, $C=\bigcup_{i}C_i$, 
and let $\tilde C$ be the computably enumerable open set of 
sequences consistent with finite assignments of strings that exit the stage,
$\tilde C = \bigsqcup_{(a', b')\in C}\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_A(a')$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop_stage}
Let $(a,b)$ be a pair of strings on which a stage is run.
Let $c$ be the string equivalent to $\mathtt{fa}_A(a),\mathtt{fa}_B(b)$.
$\tilde C$ contains all sequences that extend $c$ and are contained in an encoding of a non-MLR sequence.
For each $(a',b')\in C$, $\max(\dot\mu_A(a'),\dot\mu_B(b'))\geq\frac{3}{2}\max(\dot\mu_A(a),\dot\mu_B(b))$,
$\min(\dot\mu_A(a'),\dot\mu_B(b'))\geq 0$,
and there is some string $c'\succeq c$ such that $\mathtt{fa}_A(a')\equiv\mathtt{fa}_B(b')\equiv c'$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let  $\sigma$ be a non-MLR sequence that extends $s$.
Let $\rho$ be a sequence that extends $c$ and is contained in $E(\sigma)$.
Since $\sigma$ is non-MLR, it must have a prefix $s'$ that extends $s$,
and $\dot u(s')\geq H$. Then $s'\in M$.
We have
$\tilde C =\bigcup_{i} \bigsqcup_{(a', b')\in C_i}\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_A(a') $.
By lemma \ref{lem_FR_exit_seqs}, 
$\tilde C =\bigcup_{i}\tilde c\cap E(s_i)$.
This is equal to $\tilde c\cap \bigcup_{s'\in M}E(\tilde s')$, and $\tilde C$ contains $\rho$.
The rest of the result follows from that same lemma.
\end{proof}

We recursively define entire $A,B$.
\begin{definition}
We set $\mu_A(\epsilon)=\mu_B(\epsilon)=1$
and $\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_A(\epsilon)=\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_B(\epsilon)=\tilde{\epsilon}$
and run a stage on $(\epsilon,\epsilon)$.
For a pair of strings that exits any running stage,
we run another stage on the pair.
\end{definition}

Suppose that some $\rho\in E(\sigma)$ is consistent with finite assignments of only finitely many pairs that have exited some stages.
For the last  such pair, the stage is run on the pair, and $\rho$ is consistent with the pair's finite assignments,

there is an infinite sequence of pairs 
$(\epsilon,\epsilon),(a_1,b_1),\dots$
such that for all $n$
$(a_n,b_n)$ exits the stage run on $(a_{n-1},b_{n-1})$ and 
$\rho$ is consistent with their finite assignments.
By proposition \ref{prop_stage},
$\max(\dot\mu_A(a_n),\dot\mu_B(b_n))\geq\frac{3}{2}^n$.
Then either $\sup_n(\dot\mu_A(a_n))=\infty$, or $\sup_n(\dot\mu_B(b_n))=\infty$ (or both),

A stage starts with a pair of strings
for which the finite assignments 
and measures of strategies $A,B$
have already been defined.
We require that the measures are non-zero and that strings are leafs of $A,B$ such that their finite assignments are equivalent, and even more, equivalent to a string.
Denote the input pair with $(a,b)$ and string $c\equiv\mathtt{fa}_A(a)\equiv\mathtt{fa}_B(b)$ .


Stage iteratively defines strategies $A,B$ on leafs extending $a,b$.
The capitals  on strings $a,b$ will determine how the bets are defined.
The strategy with the smaller capital will at first place bets on positions in the same order as the position function $p$, we'll call this the \textit{forward} strategy and label it with $F$.  
The strategy with the larger capital will at first skip the first position and place bets on other positions determined by $p$ and later return to the first position. 
We'll call this the \textit{rewind} strategy and label it with $R$.
So if $\dot\mu_A\leq\dot\mu_B$, then $F=A$, $R=B$, and $F,R$ will be defined on continuations of $f,r = a,b$.
In the other case, $F=B$, $R=A$, and $F,R$ will be defined on continuations of $f,r = b,a$.
We set the threshold $T$ for the capital that needs to be achieved
$T=\frac{3}{2}c_R(r)$.

We'll define the measure functions $\mu_F$, $\mu_R$ as sums of two measure functions. 
One, $\eta$, will assign measure evenly, this ensures that the strings always have non-zero measure assigned to them.
The other that we will define, $\nu$, will increase (or decrease) capital on some of the strings.
We set $\eta_F(\epsilon)=\nu_F(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_F(f)$ and
$\eta_R(\epsilon)=\nu_R(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_R(r)$.
We have
$\mu_F(f)=\eta_F(f)+\nu_F(f)$, $\mu_R(r)=\eta_R(r)+\nu_R(r)$.

Since the encoding's position function $p$, 
as the input strings become longer, chooses positions in increasing order, 
there exists a string $s$ such that $E(\tilde s)$ still contains $\tilde{c}$, and $p(s)>|c|$.
 We will computably enumerate a set of strings $M$, that are continuations of this $s$ and on which the universal lower semicomputable semimeasure $u$
 achieves capital larger than $H=\frac{2T}{ \lambda(s)\dot\nu_F(f)}$.
 More precisely, starting with empty $M$,
 we dovetail the computation of $u$ on all inputs, until finding $v\succ s$ such that
 $\dot u(v)\geq H$.
 If $\tilde v$ is already a subset of $\tilde M$, nothing new gets  added to $M$,
 otherwise, we will
 add one by one strings $s_1,\dots,s_n$ 
 such that $\tilde{s_1},\dots,\tilde{s_n}$ are disjoint and
 contain all of the sequences in $\tilde M\setminus\tilde{v}$.
 
 When we add a string $s'$ to $M$,
 we define finitely many new bets 
 in an \textit{iteration} of the stage .
Let $M'$ denote the strings that have been added in previous iterations.
The bets are defined only for the leafs of $F,R$ that don't extend strings that have exited the stage
after some previous iteration.

We define bets on strings that extend $F^-,R^-$ in three consecutive phases.

because then $\tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_F(F^\circ) \neq \tilde{\mathtt{fa}}_R (R^\circ)$.

It remains to show that for each pair $f^\circ\in F^\circ,r^\circ\in R^\circ$
where $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^\circ)\equiv\mathtt{fa}_R(r^\circ)$ we have
 $\max(\dot\nu_F(f^\circ),\dot\nu_R(r^\circ))\geq T$.
 
By claim \ref{cl_F:f-con_e_f'}, for all $f^-\in F^-$, $\dot\nu_F(f^-)=\dot\nu_F(f)$.
Let $f^\circ\in F^\circ, r^\circ\in R^\circ$ and $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^\circ)\equiv \mathtt{fa}_R(r^\circ)$.
Then there is $f^-\in F^-, f^+\in F^+|f^-,r^-\in R^-$ 
such that $f^\circ\in F^\circ|f^+, r^\circ\in R^\circ|r^-$
and $\mathtt{fa}_F(f^-),\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ both consistent with $e(s')$.
If $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ does not assign to $p(s)$, then by claim \ref{cl_R-},
$\dot\nu_R(r^-)=\dot\nu_R(r)$, and by claim \ref{cl_R_con_e_nop_r+},
$\dot\nu_R(r^\circ)=2\dot\nu_R(r)=T$.
On the other hand, if $\mathtt{fa}_R(r^-)$ does  assign to $p(s)$, then by claim \ref{cl_R-},
$\nu_R(r^-)=0$. Since for arbitrary $i$, $F'_i=F^-_i$, by claim \ref{cl_F:f-con_e_f'}, $\dot\nu_F(f^-)=\dot\nu_F(f)$ and by claim \ref{cl_F:f-con_e_Z_fo},
$\dot\nu_F(f^\circ)=\dot\nu_F(f)/\lambda(\tilde Z |\mathtt{fa}_F(f^+))$.
This is by lemma \ref{lem_Z|f+} larger than $T$.
Therefore $\max(\dot\nu_F(f^\circ),\dot\nu_R(r^\circ))\geq T$.
We have that $\dot\eta_F(f^\circ)=\dot\eta_F(f)$, $\dot\eta_R(r^\circ)=\dot\eta_R(r)$.
Since $\mu$ is a sum of $\eta$ and $\nu$, and the stage requires that  $\mu_F(f),\mu_R(r)$ are larger than zero,
the result follows.

\label{prop_stage}
Let $(a,b)$ be a pair of strings on which a stage is run.
Let $c$ be the string equivalent to $\mathtt{fa}_A(a),\mathtt{fa}_B(b)$.
$\tilde C$ contains all sequences that extend $c$ and are contained in an encoding of a non-MLR sequence.
For each $(a',b')\in C$, $\max(\dot\mu_A(a'),\dot\mu_B(b'))\geq\frac{3}{2}\max(\dot\mu_A(a),\dot\mu_B(b))$,
$\min(\dot\mu_A(a'),\dot\mu_B(b'))\geq 0$,
and there is some string $c'\succeq c$ such that $\mathtt{fa}_A(a')\equiv\mathtt{fa}_B(b')\equiv c'$.
