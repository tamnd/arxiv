<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main_SRB}
    The strong Ramanujan bound holds for all $q$ and all~$\mathfrak{p}^n$. In other words, for any prime power $q$, prime ideal $\mathfrak p \trianglelefteq \mathbb{F}_q[T]$ and $n \geqslant 1$, we have
    \[
        \deg \operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}^n_{\mathfrak{p}} \, | \, \operatorname{S}_{k,l}) \leqslant  \frac{nd(k-(q+1))}{2} \qquad \text{for all }k,\,l \in \mathbb Z.
    \]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
For fixed $k$ and $l$, whether the strong Ramanujan bound is sharp on $\operatorname{S}_{k,l}$ depends only on the parity of $\deg \mathfrak{p}^n$.
    Furthermore, for every $\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}$, the strong Ramanujan bound is sharp in a set of weights with positive density.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
The number of isomorphism classes of rank 2 Drinfeld modules over $\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}$ whose Frobenius polynomial $X^2-aX+b\wp^n$ has $a_0=\alpha$ and $b=\beta$ is $\equiv 1 \pmod p$. The number of those whose top $j$ coefficients $a_N,\dots,a_{N-j+1}$ and $b$ are prescribed is divisible by $q$ provided $j^2<nd$; for $nd$ odd, it is divisible by an explicit higher power $q^{\mu}$. More general sets of prescribed coefficients are also allowed.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rmk:class-numbers}
    The numbers $\# \operatorname{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}(a,b)$ are given by class numbers of orders in quadratic extensions of~$K$ or quaternion algebras over~$K$. For instance, in the ordinary case, $\# \operatorname{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}(a,b)$ is the Hurwitz class number of the order $A[\pi]$ in the imaginary quadratic extension $K(\pi) = K[X]/(X^2-aX+b\wp^n)$, cf.~\cite[Appendix~A]{devries_traces}.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:domain}
    Let $\Bbbk$ be a field extension of~$\mathbb{F}_q$ of degree~$m$. Let $f$ be a function on isomorphism classes of Drinfeld modules over $\Bbbk$ taking values in an $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space $V$. Then 
    \[
    \sum_{[\varphi]} f[\varphi] = \sum_{(g,\Delta) \in \Bbbk \times \Bbbk^\times} f[\varphi^{(g,\Delta)}].
    \]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By definition, each Drinfeld module $\varphi$ is of the form $\varphi^{(g,\Delta)}$ for some $(g,\Delta) \in \Bbbk \times \Bbbk^\times$. Consider the $\Bbbk^\times$-action on such pairs given by $\lambda \star (g,\Delta) = (\lambda^{q-1}g,\lambda^{q^2-1}\Delta)$. Then $[\varphi^{(g,\Delta)}] = [\varphi^{(g',\Delta')}]$ if and only if $(g,\Delta)$ lies in the $\star$-orbit of $(g',\Delta')$. Therefore
    \[
    \sum_{(g,\Delta) \in \Bbbk \times \Bbbk^\times} f[\varphi^{(g,\Delta)}] = \sum_{[\varphi]} \# \text{Orb}_\star(\varphi) f[\varphi].
    \]
    By the Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem, we have 
    \[
    \# \text{Orb}_\star(\varphi) = \frac{\#\Bbbk^\times}{\# \text{Stab}_\star(\varphi)} = \frac{q^m-1}{\# \operatorname{Aut}(\varphi)}.
    \]
    If $\#\operatorname{Aut}(\varphi) = q-1$, then $\#\text{Orb}_\star(\varphi) = 1 + q + \ldots + q^{m-1} \equiv_p 1$. If $\#\operatorname{Aut}(\varphi) = q^2-1$, then $m$ is even and again $\#\text{Orb}_\star(\varphi) = 1 + q^2 + \ldots + q^{m -2} \equiv_p 1$. This finishes the proof.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:mass}
Let $\Bbbk = \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}$. For all $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb Z_{\geqslant 0}^{N+1}$ and $t \in \mathbb Z$,
\[
 \mu(\mathbf{v},t) = \sum_{a,b} \#\operatorname{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}(a,b)\, \prod_{i=0}^N a_i^{v_i} b^t
= \sum_{(g,\Delta) \in \Bbbk \times \Bbbk^\times} b(g,\Delta)^t \prod_{i=0}^N a_i(g,\Delta)^{v_i}
\quad \text{in } \mathbb{F}_q.
\]
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $f$ be any function on $A \times \mathbb{F}_{q}^\times$ with values in an abelian group. By definition of $\operatorname{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}(a,b)$ (see \cite[Def.~2.21]{devries_traces}), we have
\[
\sum_{a,b} \#\operatorname{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}(a,b)f(a,b) = \sum_{[\varphi]} f(a_\varphi,b_\varphi).
\]
Now apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:domain} with $V = \mathbb{F}_q$ and $f[\varphi] = f(a_\varphi,b_\varphi) = b_\varphi^t\prod_{i=0}^N a_{i,\varphi}^{v_i} $.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:motive-basis} Let $\Bbbk = \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}$ and $\varphi = \varphi^{(g,\Delta)}$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $M(\varphi)$ is free of rank~$2$ over $\Bbbk[T]$ with basis $\{1, \tau\}$;
    \item With respect to the above basis, we have
\[
\tau_M \begin{pmatrix} z \\ w \end{pmatrix}
= F \cdot \sigma\!\begin{pmatrix} z \\ w \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
F := \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \Delta^{-1}(T - \vartheta) \\ 1 & -\Delta^{-1} g\end{pmatrix} \in
\mathrm{M}_2(\Bbbk[T]);
\]
\item $\pi_\varphi = \tau_M^{nd}$ is a $\Bbbk[T]$-linear map acting on $M(\varphi)$ with matrix
\[
\Phi := F\,\sigma(F)\,\sigma^2(F)\cdots \sigma^{nd-1}(F);
\]
\item Set $a := \operatorname{Tr}(\Phi)$ and $D := \det(\Phi)$.
                      
Then $a, D \in \mathbb{F}_q[T]$;
\item $a = a(g,\Delta)$ and $D = b(g,\Delta)\wp^n$ with $b(g,\Delta) = (-1)^{nd}\, \operatorname{\operatorname{N}}_{\Bbbk/\mathbb{F}_q}(\Delta)^{-1} \in \mathbb{F}_q^\times$;
\item $c_\varphi(X) = X^2 - aX + D$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
1. This is standard, see e.g. \cite[Lem.~3.4.4]{papikian}. 

2. Since $T \cdot 1 = \varphi_T = \vartheta + g\tau + \Delta\tau^2$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tausq}
\tau^2 = \Delta^{-1}(T-\vartheta)\cdot 1 - \Delta^{-1}g \cdot \tau,
\end{equation}
which yields the expression for~$F$. 

3. Iterating the equality $\tau_M = F\sigma$, we find that $\tau_M^i = F \sigma(F) \cdots \sigma^{i-1}(F)\sigma^i$ for all $i \geqslant 1$. Since $\sigma^{nd} = \text{id}$, we get the claimed expression for $\pi$.

4. A priori, we have $a(g,\Delta), D \in \Bbbk[T]$. Since $\sigma^{nd}(F) = F$, we have
$\sigma(\Phi) = \sigma(F)\cdots\sigma^{nd-1}(F)\,F = F^{-1}\,\Phi\, F$ over $\Bbbk(T)$.
                                                                                   
Hence $\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi)$ and $\det(\Phi)$ are
$\sigma$-invariant, i.e., lie in $\mathbb{F}_q[T]$.

5. It is a standard fact that $c_\varphi(X)$ is the characteristic polynomial of~$\pi_{\varphi}$ acting on~$M(\varphi)$, which gives $a = a(g,\Delta)$. Moreover, $b(g,\Delta) \wp^n = \det \Phi$, which can be computed as 
\[
\det \Phi = \prod_{i=0}^{nd-1}\sigma^i(\det F) = \prod_{i=0}^{nd-1}
\bigl(-\Delta^{-q^i}(T-\vartheta^{q^i})\bigr) = (-1)^{nd} \operatorname{\operatorname{N}}_{\Bbbk/\mathbb{F}_q}(\Delta)^{-1}
\prod_{i=0}^{nd-1}(T - \vartheta^{q^i}).
\]
The $d$ roots of $\wp(T)$ over $\Bbbk$ are $\vartheta, \vartheta^q, \dots, \vartheta^{q^{d-1}}$, and the list $i = 0, \dots, nd-1$ runs through them exactly $n$ times each; hence the product is $\wp(T)^n$.

6. This follows from points 4 and 5.

Since $b(g,\Delta)$ only depends on~$\Delta$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:motive-basis}.5, we will simply denote it by~$b(\Delta)$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Call a subset $S \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/m$ \emph{disconnected} if $i \in S \implies i + 1 \notin S$.
                                                                                                                                                                                      
For $S$ disconnected, put $\mathrm{Cov}(S) := \bigcup_{i \in S}\{i-1, i\}$ and $U(S) := (\mathbb{Z}/m) \setminus
\mathrm{Cov}(S)$ ($U$ for ``uncovered"), so that $\#U(S) = m - 2\#S$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:a-expr}
For $(g,\Delta) \in \Bbbk \times \Bbbk^\times$, where $\Bbbk \cong \mathbb{F}_{q^m}$, we have
\[
a(g,\Delta) = \sum_{\substack{S \subset \mathbb{Z}/m \\ \text{disconnected}}} \ \prod_{i \in S} u_i
\prod_{j \in U(S)} w_j .
\]
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $M_i := \sigma^i(F)$. By writing out explicitly the diagonal entries of the product $\prod M_i$, we obtain
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(M_0 M_1 \cdots M_{m-1}) = \sum_{s\: \mathbb{Z}/m \to \{1,2\}} \ \prod_{i \in \mathbb{Z}/m}
(M_i)_{s(i),\, s(i+1)} .
\]
               
    
                                                                                                                                   
    
                                                                  
Note that $(M_i)_{1,1} = 0$ for all $i$. Thus if $s\: \mathbb{Z}/m \to \{1,2\}$ is such that $s(i) = s(i+1) = 1$ for some~$i$, then the corresponding term is zero. It follows that if the term corresponding to $s$ is nonzero, then $S := s^{-1}(1) \subset \mathbb{Z}/m$ is disconnected. 
                                            
The identifications $(M_i)_{1,2} = u_i$, $(M_i)_{2,1} = 1$, and
$(M_i)_{2,2} = w_i$ now give the claimed expression.

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:rewrite}
    For the Drinfeld module $\varphi^{(g,\Delta)}$ over $\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:term}
a(g,\Delta) = \sum_{\substack{S \subset \mathbb{Z}/nd \\ \text{disconnected}}}  \prod_{i \in S} (T - \vartheta^{q^i})
\ \cdot\ (-1)^{\#U(S)}\cdot g^{\sum_{j \in U(S)} q^j}\cdot
\Delta^{-\sum_{i \in S} q^i - \sum_{j \in U(S)} q^j}.
\end{equation}
In particular, for $h \geqslant 0$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coeff}
a_h(g,\Delta) = \sum_{\substack{S \subset \mathbb{Z}/nd \text{ disconnected}\\ \#S \geqslant h}} c_{h}(S) \cdot g^{\sum_{j \in U(S)} q^j} \cdot \Delta^{-\sum_{i \in S}q^i - \sum_{j \in U(S)}q^j},
\end{equation}
where $c_h(S) \in \mathbb{F}_{q^{nd}}$ is the coefficient of $T^h$ in $(-1)^{\#U(S)}\prod_{i \in S}(T-\vartheta^{q^i})$.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Only the condition $\#S \geqslant h$ in \eqref{eq:coeff} needs to be justified. For this, simply note that the summand corresponding to $S$ in \eqref{eq:term} has degree~$\#S$, so that $\#S < h \implies c_h(S) = 0$.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Since a disconnected subset of $\mathbb{Z}/m$ has cardinality at most $\lfloor m/2 \rfloor$, we recover the well-known fact that $\deg a_\varphi \leqslant m/2$ for $\varphi$ over $\mathbb{F}_{q^m}$ (the Riemann hypothesis for rank~2 Drinfeld modules).

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:digitsum}
    Suppose $E > 0$ and $q^m - 1 \mid E$. Then $s_q(E) \geqslant m(q-1)$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Suppose first that $0 < E < q^m$. Then by hypothesis $E = q^m-1$, and so $s_q(E) = m(q-1)$. If $E \geqslant q^m$, we may write $E = q^mF + G$ with $F \geqslant 1$ and $0 \leqslant G < q^m$. Then $E' := F + G > 0$ is divisible by $q^m-1$ and $s_q(E) = s_q(q^mF) + s_q(G) = s_q(F) + s_q(G) \geqslant s_q(E')$, so we are done by induction.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $\mathbf{v}$ be as in the statement of the theorem. By Corollary~\ref{cor:mass} and Lemma~\ref{lem:motive-basis}.5, we may write
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:mu(v,t)}
     \mu(\mathbf{v},t) = \sum_{(g,\Delta) \in \Bbbk \times \Bbbk^\times} (-1)^{ndt}\Delta^{-t\frac{q^{nd}-1}{q-1}} \prod_{i=0}^N a_i(g,\Delta)^{v_i}.
    \end{equation}
    By Corollary~\ref{cor:rewrite}, we have
    \[
    a_i(g,\Delta) = \sum_{\substack{S \subset \mathbb{Z}/nd \text{ disconnected} \\ \#S \geqslant i}} c_{i}(S,\Delta) \, g^{E(S)},
    \]
    for some $c_{i}(S,\Delta) \in \Bbbk$, where we let $E(S) := \sum_{j \in U(S)} q^j$. Note that $\# U(S) = nd - 2\#S \leqslant nd - 2i$ for all~$S$ appearing in the sum, so in particular $s_q(E(S)) \leqslant nd - 2i$. 
    
    Expanding the product in \eqref{eq:mu(v,t)}, we obtain
    \[
    \mu(\mathbf{v},t) = \sum_{\Delta \in \Bbbk^\times} \sum_{\mathbf{S}} c(\mathbf{S},\Delta,t) \sum_{g \in \Bbbk}g^{E(\mathbf{S})}, 
    \]
    where $\mathbf{S}$ ranges over tuples $(\mathbf{S}_i)_{i=0}^{N}$ of tuples $\mathbf{S}_i = (S_{i,j})_{j=1}^{v_i}$ of disconnected subsets satisfying $\#S_{i,j} \geqslant i$ for all~$j$. Here $E(\mathbf{S}) := \sum_{i=0}^{N} \sum_{j =1}^{v_i} E(S_{i,j})$, and the $c(\mathbf{S},\Delta,t)$ are constants not depending on~$g$.
    
                                    
        
                                                                                                                                              
        
                                                                                                                                              
    
    For such $E = E(\mathbf{S})$, we have
    \[
    s_q(E) \leqslant \sum_{i=0}^N \sum_{j=1}^{v_i} s_q(E(S_{i,j})) \leqslant \sum_{i=0}^N v_i(nd-2i).
    \]
    By assumption, we have $2 \sum_{i=0}^N iv_i > nd \left( \sum_{i=0}^N v_i - q + 1 \right)$, and hence $s_q(E) < nd(q-1)$. In particular, by Lemma~\ref{lem:digitsum}, we find that either $E = 0$ or $q^{nd}-1$ does not divide $E$. In either case, Lemma~\ref{lem:orth} gives $\sum_{g \in \Bbbk} g^E = 0$,
    and hence $ \mu(\mathbf{v},t) = 0$.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Let $k$ and~$m$ be positive integers. Let $\mathbf{S} = (S_1,\ldots,S_k)$ be a $k$-tuple of disconnected subsets of~$\mathbb Z/m$. Define
    \[
        E(\mathbf{S}) := \sum_{\alpha = 1}^k \sum_{j \in U(S_\alpha)} q^j, \qquad C(\mathbf{S},t) := t\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1} + \sum_{\alpha=1}^k \left( \sum_{i \in S_\alpha} q^i + \sum_{j \in U(S_\alpha)} q^j \right).
    \]
    Then $\mathbf{S}$ is called \emph{$t$-admissible} if $E(\mathbf{S}) > 0$ and $E(\mathbf{S}) \equiv C(\mathbf{S},t) \equiv 0 \pmod{q^m-1}$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:exact}
For all $k\ge0$ and $t\in\mathbb{Z}$,
\[
W_{k,t}\;=\;(-1)^{nd(k+t)}\sum_{\mathbf{S}}\ \prod_{\alpha=1}^{k}\prod_{i\in S_\alpha}\bigl(T-\vartheta^{q^i}\bigr),
\]
the sum being over $t$-admissible $k$-tuples $\mathbf{S}=(S_1,\dots,S_k)$ of disconnected subsets of $\mathbb{Z}/nd$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
This follows by substituting \eqref{eq:term} into the definition of~$W_{k,t}$, expanding $a^k$, and summing over $(g,\Delta) \in \Bbbk \times \Bbbk^\times$.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rmk:W-for-deg1}
    If $d=1$, then $\vartheta \in \mathbb{F}_q$ and hence $T-\vartheta^{q^i} = \wp$ for all~$i$. In this case, Prop.~\ref{prop:exact} reduces to
    \[
    W_{k,t} = (-1)^{n(k+t)} \sum_{\mathbf{S}} \wp^{\# \mathbf{S}},
    \]
    where $\mathbf{S}$ runs over $t$-admissible $k$-tuples of disconnected subsets of $\mathbb{Z}/n$, and $\#\mathbf{S} := \sum_{\alpha=1}^k \# S_\alpha$.

    In particular, there are polynomials $f_{k,l,n} \in \mathbb{F}_p[X] \subset A[X]$ such that for each prime~$\mathfrak{p}$ of degree~1, $\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^n \, | \, \operatorname{S}_{k,l}) = f_{k,l,n}(\wp)$.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
If $nd=1$, the only disconnected subset is the empty set. In this case Proposition~\ref{prop:exact} says that $W_{k,t} = 0$ unless $k > 0$ and $k \equiv t \equiv 0 \pmod{q-1}$, in which case $W_{k,t}=1$. This gives another way to prove the closed-form formula for degree one primes \cite[Thm.~3.13]{devries_traces}.

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rmk:ram-sharp}
    The strong Ramanujan bound is truly a level~1 phenomenon. The eigenvalue form of the usual Ramanujan bound for $A = \mathbb{F}_q[T]$ is the inequality
    \[
    -v_{\tilde{\infty}}(\lambda) \leqslant \frac{d(k-2)}{2},
    \]
    where $\lambda$ is a Hecke eigenvalue on $\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\Gamma)$ and $v_{\tilde{\infty}}$ is any valuation on~$K(\lambda)$ extending~$v_{\infty}$. If $\Gamma = \Gamma_0(\mathfrak{p})$, then there is a Drinfeld modular form $E_{\mathfrak{p}} \in \operatorname{S}_{2,1}(\Gamma)$ such that $\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{l}}E_{\mathfrak{p}} = E_{\mathfrak{p}}$ for all $\mathfrak{l} \neq \mathfrak{p}$ (see e.g.~\cite[\textsection 5.3.1]{BVdV-structure}). Thus $E_{\mathfrak{p}}$ violates the strong Ramanujan bound, and in fact for any $n \geqslant 0$ this gives a Hecke eigenform $E_{\mathfrak{p}}^{p^n} \in \operatorname{S}_{2p^n,p^n}(\Gamma)$ with $\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{l}}$-eigenvalue $\mathfrak{l}^{p^n-1}$ (here $\mathfrak{l}$ is identified with its monic generator). Hence the usual Ramanujan bound is attained in infinitely many weights.
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
The trace form of the strong Ramanujan bound is weaker than the eigenvalue form, and may be satisfied trivially because of $p$-fold repetition of eigenvalues. In particular, this paper does not settle Conjecture~\cite[Conj.~5.24]{devries_traces} even when $v=v_\infty$.
    
    This issue rears its head when considering higher levels, as shown in \cite{hattori_constancy} for level $\Gamma_1(\mathfrak{n}\wp^r)$ with $\mathfrak{n} \trianglelefteq A$ nonzero, $\mathfrak{p} \nmid \mathfrak{n}$, and $r \geqslant 2$. Similarly, it follows from the trace formula that traces of Hecke operators vanish identically at full level $\Gamma(\mathfrak{n})$ whenever $\mathfrak{n} \neq A$: if $\varphi$ admits a full level $\mathfrak{n}$-structure, then it admits $\#\operatorname{GL}_2(A/\mathfrak{n})$ such structures, and this number is divisible by~$q$. 
    
    At level $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_q[T])$ with $q$ odd, however, we expect that Hecke eigenvalues are never repeated in a given weight and type. If this is true, the trace form of the strong Ramanujan bound is equivalent to the eigenvalue form (this is well known). On the other hand, the results of this paper do not imply the strong Ramanujan bound on the repeated Hecke eigenvalues in characteristic~2, of which there are known to be infinitely many \cite[Sec.~3]{devries_traces}. As such, it would be valuable to have a more conceptual proof that does not rely on the trace formula.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:sharpness-criterion}
     Let $k+2 \equiv_{q-1} 2l$. The strong Ramanujan bound for $\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^n$ acting on $\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}$ is sharp if and only if 
    \[
    \begin{cases}
        2q \mid k-(q-1) \text{ and } \nu_{k,l} \text{ is odd} & \text{if } nd \text{ is odd}; \\
        q \mid k-(q-1) \text{ and } p \nmid N_{k,l} & \text{if } nd \text{ is even},
    \end{cases}
    \]
    where $N_{k,l} = \# \{ 0 \leqslant c \leqslant (k-(q-1))/q \ | \ c \equiv_{q+1} \nu_{k,l} - l \}$. In the first case, the top coefficient in the trace is $(-1)^{l-1}$. In the second case, the top coefficient is $N_{k,l}$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
It follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:sharpness-criterion} that the strong Ramanujan bound for any fixed $\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}$ and~$l$ is sharp in infinitely many weights.
    
    If $q$ is even, the two conditions coincide, and are both equivalent to the binomial coefficient $\binom{k-R(k)/2}{R(k)/2}$ being odd, where $R(k) = k-(q-1)$ (as should be the case by \cite[Thm.~3.26]{devries_traces}). In this case the weight determines the type since $k+2 \equiv_{q-1} 2l$ has a unique solution, and 
    the density of weights $k$ for which the strong Ramanujan bound is attained is $1/2q$.

    If $q$ is odd, the congruence $k+2 \equiv_{q-1}2l$ ensures that $k$ is even. Then for a fixed~$l$, the density of weights congruent to~$2l$ in which the strong Ramanujan bound is attained is $\delta$, with
    \[
    \delta = \begin{cases}
        1/2q & \text{if } nd \text{ is odd};\\
        (p-1)/pq & \text{if } nd \text{ is even.}
    \end{cases}
    \]
    Moreover, if $q$ is odd, there are zero or two solutions to the equation $k+2 \equiv_{q-1} 2l$, namely $l_1$ and $l_2 = l_1 + (q-1)/2$, and if $nd$ is odd, the strong Ramanujan bound is attained on at most one of the spaces $\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l_1}$ and $\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l_2}$.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:dual-bound}
    Let $\deg \mathfrak{p} = 1$ and $n \geqslant 1$. Fix $l \in \{1,\ldots,q-1\}$ and let $k+2 \equiv_{q-1} 2l$ and $k+2 > 2l$. Then 
    \[
    v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^n \, | \, \operatorname{S}_{k+2,l})) \geqslant n(l-1),
    \]
    and equality holds if and only if $p \nmid \binom{k-(l-1)}{l-1}$.
    In particular, whether equality holds depends only on $k \pmod{q}$.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- remark-9: body_md -->

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor:smallest-slope}
    Suppose $\deg \mathfrak{p} = 1$. Fix $l \in \{1,\ldots,q-1\}$ and suppose $\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l} \neq 0$. Then the smallest $\mathfrak{p}$-adic slope of $\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}$ on $\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}$ is $l-1$ if and only if $p \nmid \binom{k-(l-1)}{l-1}$. If slope~$l-1$ occurs, it does so with multiplicity~1. If it does not, then the smallest slope is at least $\alpha := l-1 + (q-1)/2$, and $\alpha$ occurs with multiplicity~0 or~2.
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Since the ring automorphisms of $A$ act transitively on primes of degree~1, we may assume $\wp = T$. By \cite[Prop.~6.2]{band-val_slopes} we know that in type~$l$, the smallest $\mathbf{U}_T$-slope at level $\Gamma_0(T)$ is $l-1$, and it occurs with multiplicity $\leqslant 1$. Since each $\mathbf{T}_{T}$-slope occurs as a $\mathbf{U}_T$-slope, with $\mathbf{U}_T$-multiplicity at least the $\mathbf{T}_{T}$-multiplicity (and equal multiplicities if the slope is not $k/2$), we know that the smallest slope of $\mathbf{T}_{T}$ is not repeated $p$~times and can hence be read off from the traces, cf.~\cite[Prop.~4.2]{devries_newton}. Therefore Theorem~\ref{thm:dual-bound} determines when this slope occurs.

    Now suppose slope $l-1$ does not occur. In the notation of \cite[Sec.~6]{band-val_slopes} (but with our normalization), the characteristic polynomial of~$\mathbf{U}_T$ is then
    \[
    X^n + \ell_1 X^{n-1} + \ell_2 X^{n-2} + \ldots + \ell_{n-r}X^r,
    \]
    and $v_T(\ell_i) \geqslant i(l-1) + i(i-1)(q-1)/2$. Moreover, our assumption implies (by \emph{loc.~cit.}) that $v_T(\ell_1) \geqslant l-1 + (q-1)$. Considering the Newton polygon, we see that the smallest $\mathbf{U}_T$-slope that can occur is $\alpha$, which happens if and only if $v_T(\ell_2) = 2\alpha$, in which case the slope has multiplicity~2. The multiplicity of $\mathbf{T}_{T}$-slope $\alpha$ is then also 0 or~2, since under our assumptions $\alpha \neq k/2$: indeed, $\alpha = k/2$ would force $k+2=2l+q-1$, in which case $\dim \operatorname{S}_{k+2,l} > 0$ implies $l = 1$. But on $\operatorname{S}_{q+1,1} = \langle h \rangle$ the smallest slope is known to be $0 = l-1$.
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- remark-10: body_md -->
After proving Corollary~\ref{cor:smallest-slope}, we noted that it is also a consequence of \cite[Prop.~6.2]{band-val_slopes} and \cite[Thm.~3.13]{devries_traces}. However, the proof presented here is different and has some advantages: it actually gives the coefficient of the lowest $\mathfrak{p}$-adic term in the trace of $\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^n$ for any $n \geqslant 1$, and it is possible that the method can be adapted to treat primes of higher degree.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $\mathbf{S} = (S_1,\ldots,S_k)$ be a $k$-tuple of disconnected subsets of $\mathbb Z/m$. Define its \emph{coverage function} $f_{\mathbf{S}}$ as follows:
    \[
    f_{\mathbf{S}} \: \mathbb Z/m \longrightarrow \mathbb Z_{\geqslant 0}, \qquad i \longmapsto \# \{\alpha \in \{1,\ldots,k\} \ | \ i \in S_{\alpha} \}.
    \]
    If $f\: \mathbb Z/m \to \mathbb Z$ is any function, we define
    \[
    \omega_k(f) := \# \{ \mathbf{S} = (S_1,\ldots,S_k) \ | \ \text{each } S_\alpha \subset \mathbb Z/m \text{ is disconnected and } f_{\mathbf{S}} = f \}.
    \]
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-11: body_md -->
The fact that each $S_i$ is disconnected implies that $f_{\mathbf{S}}(i) + f_{\mathbf{S}}(i+1) \leqslant k$ for all~$i\in \mathbb Z/m$. In particular, $\omega_k(f) = 0$ if $f$ does not satisfy this property. Of course, the same holds if $f(i) < 0$ for some~$i$.
    Thus, $\omega_k$ has finite support as a function on $\{ f \: \mathbb{Z}/m \to \mathbb{Z}\}$.
<!-- end remark-11 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:tr-a^k}
    We have
    \[
    a^k = \sum_f \omega_k(f) x_k^f, \qquad x_k^f := \prod_{j \in \mathbb Z/m} u_j^{f(j)} w_j^{k-f(j)-f(j+1)},
    \]
    where the sum is over all functions $f\: \mathbb{Z}/m \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The expression from Prop.~\ref{prop:a-expr} holds also over $\mathbb{Z}[u_i,w_i]$. Raising it to the $k$-th power yields the expression
    \[
    a^k = \sum_{\mathbf{S}} \prod_{\alpha = 1}^{k} \prod_{i \in S_\alpha} u_i \prod_{j \in U(S_\alpha)} w_j.
    \]
    In the summand corresponding to $\mathbf{S}$, each $u_i$ occurs with multiplicity $f_{\mathbf{S}}(i)$ and each $w_j$ with multiplicity $k - f_{\mathbf{S}}(j) - f_{\mathbf{S}}(j+1)$. Grouping the tuples with the same coverage function gives the claimed expression.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:tr-sym-phi}
    Keeping the previous notation, we have
    \[
    \sum_{0 \leqslant j \leqslant k/2} (-1)^j \binom{k-j}{j} a^{k-2j} D^{j} = \sum_f \prod_{j \in \mathbb{Z}/m} \binom{k-f(j+1)}{f(j)} x_k^f,
    \]
    where the sum is over all functions $f\: \mathbb{Z}/m \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}$ such that $f(i) + f(i+1) \leqslant k$ for all~$i$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Write $(X-\pi)(X-\bar{\pi}) = X^2 - aX + D$ and let $h_k(x,y) = \sum_{i=0}^k x^iy^{k-i}$. Then by \cite[Lem.~3.3]{devries_traces}, the left-hand side equals $h_k(\pi,\bar{\pi})$. On the other hand, $h_k(\pi,\bar{\pi}) = \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Sym}^k\Phi)$; this will give the right-hand side, as follows. We have $\operatorname{Sym}^k \Phi = \prod_{i=0}^{nd-1}\operatorname{Sym}^k F_i$. Denote by $X, Y$ the standard basis of $M := \mathbb{Z}^2$, so $F_i$ sends $X \mapsto Y$ and $Y \mapsto u_iX + w_iY$. In the basis $\{X^jY^{k-j} \ | \ 0 \leqslant j \leqslant k \}$ of $\operatorname{Sym}^k M$, this gives
    \[
    \operatorname{Sym}^k F_i (X^jY^{k-j}) = Y^j (u_iX+w_iY)^{k-j} = \sum_{j'=0}^k \binom{k-j}{j'}u_i^{j'}w_i^{k-j-j'}X^{j'}Y^{k-j'}.
    \]
    Just like in the proof of Prop.~\ref{prop:a-expr}, we obtain
    \[
    \operatorname{Tr} (\operatorname{Sym}^k \Phi) = \operatorname{Tr} \bigg{(} \prod_{i \in \mathbb{Z}/m} \operatorname{Sym}^k F_i\bigg{)} = \sum_f \prod_{j \in \mathbb{Z}/m} \binom{k-f(j+1)}{f(j)} x_k^f,
    \]
    the sum being over all functions $f\: \mathbb{Z}/m \to \{0,1,\ldots,k\}$. If $f(j) + f(j+1) > k$, then the binomial coefficient will be zero, which completes the proof.
<!-- end proof-11 -->
<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:walk-identity}
    Let $k \geqslant 1$ and let $f\: \mathbb{Z}/m \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}$ satisfy $f(j) + f(j+1) \leqslant k$ for all~$j$. Then
    \[
    \sum_{j \geqslant 0} (-1)^{(m+1)j} \binom{k-j}{j} \omega_{k-2j}(f-j) = \prod_{j \in \mathbb{Z}/m} \binom{k - f(j+1)}{f(j)}.
    \]
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
We have $\det \Phi = \prod \det F_i = (-1)^{m} \prod u_i$. Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:tr-a^k}, this gives
    \[
    a^{k-2j}D^{j} = (-1)^{mj} \sum_f \omega_{k-2j}(f) x_k^{f+j}.
    \]
    Plugging this into Lemma~\ref{lem:tr-sym-phi} gives
    \[
    \sum_f \sum_{0 \leqslant j \leqslant k/2} (-1)^{(m+1)j} \binom{k-j}{j} \omega_{k-2j}(f)x_k^{f+j} = \sum_f \prod_{j \in \mathbb{Z}/m} \binom{k - f(j+1)}{f(j)} x_k^f,
    \]
    both sums being over functions $f\: \mathbb{Z}/m \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}$ such that $f(i) + f(i+1) \leqslant k$.
    We can view this as an identity in $\mathbb{Z}[u_i,w_i]$, in which case the map $f \mapsto x_k^f$ is injective; comparing coefficients completes the proof.

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:W-top-coeff}
    Suppose $ndR(k)$ is even. Then we have
    \[
    [T^{ndR(k)/2}]W_{k,t} = (-1)^{nd(k+t)} \sum_{R(k)\text{-balanced }f\in \mathcal{A}_t } \omega_k(f),
    \]
    where 
    \[
    \mathcal{A}_t = \left\{ f \: \mathbb{Z}/nd \to \mathbb{Z} \ \Bigg{|} \ \sum_{j=0}^{nd-1} f(j)q^j \equiv_{q^{nd}-1} -t \frac{q^{nd}-1}{q-1} \right\}.
    \]
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
We first rewrite Prop.~\ref{prop:exact} in terms of coverage functions. For $f\: \mathbb{Z}/nd \to \mathbb{Z}$, write $x_j := k - f(j) - f(j+1)$. If $f = f_{\mathbf{S}}$, one checks that $x_j = \# \{ 1 \leqslant \alpha \leqslant k \ |\ j \in U(S_\alpha)\}$. Thus, we define
    \[
    E(f) := \sum_{j=0}^{nd-1} x_j q^j, \qquad C(f,t) := t \frac{q^{nd}-1}{q-1} + \sum_{j=0}^{nd-1} (f(j)+x_j)q^j,
    \]
    and call $f$ $t$-admissible if $E(f) > 0$ and $E(f) \equiv_{q^{nd}-1} C(f,t) \equiv_{q^{nd}-1} 0$. Note that $E(\mathbf{S}) = E(f_{\mathbf{S}})$ and $C(\mathbf{S},t) = C(f_{\mathbf{S}},t)$. Thus Prop.~\ref{prop:exact} becomes
    \[
    W_{k,t} = (-1)^{nd(k+t)} \sum_{f t\text{-admissible}} \omega_k(f) \prod_{i \in \mathbb{Z}/nd} (T - \vartheta^{q^i})^{f(i)}.
    \]
    So it suffices to show that the $t$-admissible $f$ with $\sum f(i) = ndR(k)/2$ are precisely the $R(k)$-balanced $f \in \mathcal A_t$.

    If $\sum f(i) = ndR(k)/2$, then $\sum x_j = ndk - 2\sum f(i) = nd(q-1)$. On the other hand,
    \[
    \sum x_j \geqslant s_q(E(f)) \geqslant nd(q-1),
    \]
    the second inequality being Lemma~\ref{lem:digitsum}. Thus the first inequality is an equality. But $s_q(\sum x_i q^i) = \sum x_i$ if and only if $x_i \leqslant q-1$ for all~$i$, and since the sum equals $nd(q-1)$ we must have $x_i = q-1$ for all~$i$. Hence $f(j) + f(j+1) = k - (q-1)$ for all~$j$, i.e., $f$ is $R(k)$-balanced. If $f$ is moreover $t$-admissible, then clearly $f \in \mathcal A_t$. 

    Conversely, if $f$ is $R(k)$-balanced then $x_j = q-1$ for all~$j$, so $E(f) > 0$ and $q^{nd}-1 \mid E(f)$. If moreover $f \in \mathcal A_t$ then also $q^{nd}-1 \mid C(f,t)$, so $f$ is $t$-admissible, as we wanted.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:B-is-indicator}
    Let $B\: \mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0} \to \mathbb{F}_p$ be the function $B(m) := \binom{m + q - 1}{q-1}$. Then $B = \mathds{1}_{q \mid m}$ is the indicator function of~$q\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
We use Lucas's Theorem. If $q = p^e$, the first $e$ base-$p$ digits of $q-1$ are all $p-1$, so $B(m) = 1$ if and only if the first $e$ base-$p$ digits of $m + q - 1$ are also~$p-1$, and $B(m) = 0$ otherwise. So $B(m) = 1 \iff m+q-1 \equiv_q q-1 \iff q \mid m$.
<!-- end proof-15 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
We use the equation
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-and-W}
        \operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^n \, | \, \operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}) = \sum_{0 \leqslant j \leqslant k/2} (-1)^j \binom{k-j}{j} W_{k-2j,j+l-k-1} \wp^{nj},
    \end{equation}
    which is just the Hecke trace formula from the introduction: the term $j = k/2$, if it occurs, is zero by \cite[Lemma~3.4]{devries_traces}. This gives
    \[
    [T^{ndR(k)/2}]\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^n \, | \, \operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}) = \sum_{0 \leqslant j \leqslant k/2} (-1)^j \binom{k-j}{j} [T^{ndR(k-2j)/2}]W_{k-2j,j+l-k-1}.
    \]
    Now plug in the expression from Prop.~\ref{prop:W-top-coeff}. Note that the map $f \mapsto f + i$ defines a bijection
    \[
    \{ (r-2i)\text{-balanced maps } \mathbb{Z}/m \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0} \} \longrightarrow \{ r\text{-balanced maps } \mathbb{Z}/m \to \mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant i} \}.
    \]
    Moreover, $f \in \mathcal A_t$ if and only if $f + i \in \mathcal A_{t-i}$. Thus we obtain
    \begin{align*}
    [T^{ndR(k)/2}]\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^n \, | \, \operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}) &= (-1)^{nd(l-1)} \sum_{R(k)\text{-balanced }f \in \mathcal A_{1-l}} \sum_{j \geqslant 0} (-1)^{j(nd+1)} \binom{k-j}{j} \omega_{k-2j}(f - j) \\
    &= (-1)^{nd(l-1)} \sum_{R(k)\text{-balanced }f \in \mathcal A_{1-l}} \prod_{j \in \mathbb{Z}/nd} \binom{k-f(j+1)}{f(j)},
    \end{align*}
    the last equality being Prop.~\ref{prop:walk-identity}. If $f$ is $R(k)$-balanced then $k - f(j+1) = f(j) + q - 1$, so the binomial coefficient equals $\binom{f(j) + q - 1}{q-1} = B(f(j))$.

    To conclude, first suppose $nd$ is odd. If $R(k)$ is odd, the bound $ndR(k)/2$ is not an integer and hence not attained; moreover, $2q \nmid R(k)$, so the theorem holds. Hence we may assume that $R(k)$ is even. The constant function $R(k)/2$ lies in $\mathcal{A}_{1-l}$ if and only if $R(k)/2 \equiv_{q-1} l-1$. This is equivalent to $\nu_{k,l}$ being odd,
                                                              
    so by Lemma~\ref{lem:balanced} we obtain
    \[
    [T^{ndR(k)/2}]\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^n \, | \, \operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}) = \begin{cases}
        (-1)^{l-1} B(R(k)/2) & \text{if } \nu_{k,l} \equiv_2 1; \\
        0 & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
    \]
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:B-is-indicator}, we get the claimed criterion.
    
    Next, suppose $nd$ is even and let $0 \leqslant c \leqslant R(k)$. Then $f^{(c)}$ lies in $\mathcal A_{1-l}$ if and only if 
    \[
    qR(k) - (q-1)c \equiv_{q^2-1} (q+1)(l-1),
    \]
    which is equivalent to $c \equiv_{q+1} l - \nu_{k,l}$. 
                                                                                                                                                
    So in this case, again by Lemma~\ref{lem:balanced},
    \[
    [T^{ndR(k)/2}]\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^n \, | \, \operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}) = \sum_{c \in C} (B(c)B(R(k)-c))^{nd/2},
    \]
    where $C = \{ 0 \leqslant c \leqslant R(k) \ | \ c \equiv_{q+1} l - \nu_{k,l} \}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:B-is-indicator}, $B(c) = B(R(k)-c) = 1$ if and only if $q$ divides both $c$ and~$R(k)$. Therefore the top coefficient equals $\# \{ 0 \leqslant c' \leqslant R(k)/q \ | \ qc' \equiv_{q+1} l - \nu_{k,l}\}$, that is, $N_{k,l}$.
<!-- end proof-16 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
We use the expression of Prop.~\ref{prop:W-top-coeff}. Since $nd$ is odd, the bound can only be attained if $R(k)$ is even. Write $k = q-1+2s$ with $s \geqslant 0$. We observed that the only $R(k)$-balanced map is then the constant function $s$, and this lies in $\mathcal{A}_t$ if and only if $s+t \equiv_{q-1} 0$. In this case, the top coefficient of $W_{k,t}$ is $\omega_k(s)$.
    
    If $s=0$, clearly $\omega_k(s) = 1$, so $W_{q-1,0} = 1$ attains the bound. It remains to show that $\omega_k(s)$ is divisible by~$p$ for all~$s \geqslant 1$. Prop.~\ref{prop:walk-identity} gives
    \begin{align*}
    \omega_k(s)  &= \binom{k-s}{s}^{nd} - \sum_{j=1}^s \binom{k-j}{j} \omega_{k-2j}(s-j) \\
    &= B(s)^{nd} - B(s) - \sum_{j=1}^{s-1} \binom{k-j}{j} \omega_{k-2j}(s-j).
    \end{align*}
    Since $B(s)$ is either 0 or 1, this always vanishes mod~$p$ by induction on~$s$.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:digitsum_v2}
    Let $s \in \{1,\ldots,q-1\}$. If $E > 0$ and $E \equiv s \frac{q^m-1}{q-1} \pmod{q^m-1}$, then $s_q(E) \geqslant ms$. In particular, if $E = \sum_{i \geqslant 0} b_i q^i$ with $b_i \geqslant 0$, let $c_j := \displaystyle{\sum_{i \equiv_m \,j}} b_i$. Then $\sum b_i \geqslant ms$, and equality holds if and only if $c_j = s$ for all~$j \in \mathbb{Z}/m$.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
The proof is similar to that of Lemma~\ref{lem:digitsum}, so we only give a sketch. Clearly $\sum b_i \geqslant s_q(E)$, with equality if and only if $b_i \leqslant q-1$ for all~$i$. Replacing $E$ by the sum of its base-$q$ blocks of length~$m$ can only decrease the digit sums and eventually ends up in $(0,q^m-1]$. In that case $E = s\frac{q^m-1}{q-1}$ with digit sum $ms$. Equality holds if and only if no carries occurred in this procedure, which is equivalent to $c_j = s$ for all~$j$.

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:dual-bound-W}
    Suppose $\deg \mathfrak{p} = 1$. Fix $t \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $k \geqslant 0$. Let $s \in \{0,\ldots,q-2\}$ denote the representative of $-t \pmod{q-1}$. Then $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(W_{k,t}) \geqslant ns$, and equality holds if and only if $k > 2s$, $k \equiv_{q-1} 2s$, and $p \nmid \omega_k(f)$ for the constant function $f = s\: \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{Z}$.
<!-- end proposition-7 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
Since $W_{k,t} = 0$ unless $k+2t \equiv_{q-1} 0$, we may assume $k \equiv_{q-1} 2s$. First assume $s > 0$.
    
    By Remark~\ref{rmk:W-for-deg1}, it suffices to show that if $\mathbf{S}$ is a $t$-admissible tuple of disconnected subsets of $\mathbb Z/n$, then $\# \mathbf{S} \geqslant ns$. Let $\mathbf{S}$ be such a tuple. By $t$-admissibility, we have
    \[
    \sum_{\alpha = 1}^k \sum_{i \in S_\alpha} q^i \equiv -t \frac{q^n-1}{q-1} \pmod{q^n-1} \equiv s \frac{q^n-1}{q-1} \pmod{q^n-1}.
    \]
                                                                                   
    Thus, writing $B := \sum_{\alpha = 1}^k \sum_{i \in S_{\alpha}} q^i = \sum_{i = 0}^{n-1} f_{\mathbf{S}}(i)q^i$, Lemma~\ref{lem:digitsum_v2} gives $s_q(B) \geqslant ns$. But then also $\# \mathbf{S}= \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} f_{\mathbf{S}}(i) \geqslant s_q(B) \geqslant ns$, as desired. Lemma~\ref{lem:digitsum_v2} also says that we have equality if and only if $f_{\mathbf{S}}(i) = s$ for all~$i$, i.e., $f_{\mathbf{S}}$ is the constant function~$s$, and one checks that this function is $t$-admissible whenever $k > 2s$ and $k \equiv_{q-1} 2s$.
                                                                                          
    In this case,
    \[
    [\wp^{ns}] W_{k,t} = (-1)^{n(k+t)}\omega_k(s).
    \]
    Finally, if $s=0$ the inequality is trivial, and the equality is easily seen to hold for each positive multiple~$k$ of~$q-1$ by Remark~\ref{rmk:W-for-deg1}. This agrees with the lemma because $\omega_k(0)=1$.

<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
We now deduce the statement about the traces from Equation~\eqref{eq:trace-and-W}.
    We may assume $k+2 \equiv_{q-1} 2l$. Denoting by $[c]_{q-1}$ the representative in $\{0,\ldots,q-2\}$ of~$c \in \mathbb Z$, the $j$-term has valuation at least $nj + n[l-1-j]_{q-1} \geqslant n[l-1]_{q-1} = n(l-1)$; here the inequality is an equality for $0 \leqslant j \leqslant l-1$. This proves the claimed inequality. 
    The question of equality remains. Since we assume $k > 2(l-1)$, the above yields
    \[
    [\wp^{n(l-1)}] \operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^n \, | \, \operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}) = (-1)^{n(l-1)}\sum_{0 \leqslant j \leqslant l-1} (-1)^{(n+1)j} \binom{k-j}{j} \omega_{k-2j}(l-1-j).
    \]
    By Prop.~\ref{prop:walk-identity}, this simplifies to
    \[
    (-1)^{n(l-1)} \binom{k-(l-1)}{l-1}^n.
    \]
    By Lucas's Theorem, this vanishes if and only if $\binom{a_0}{l-1} \equiv_p 0$, where $a_0$ denotes the $q^0$-digit of $k-(l-1)$; in particular, it depends only on $k \pmod{q}$.

<!-- remark-12: body_md -->
\label{rmk:kneser}
    The notions introduced in this section have graph-theoretic interpretations. For any $k \geqslant 1$, denote by $D_k$ the \emph{disjointness graph} on the set $\{1,\ldots,k\}$: this is the graph with vertices given by subsets of $\{1,\ldots,k\}$ and edges $(V_i,V_j)$ if and only if $V_i \cap V_j = \emptyset$. In particular, the only vertex with a loop is the empty set.

    A \emph{closed walk of length~$m$} in a graph $G$ is a map $V\: \mathbb{Z}/m \to G$ such that $(V(i),V(i+1))$ is an edge for all~$i$. Now observe that a closed walk of length~$m$ in $D_1$ is the same as (the coverage function of) a disconnected subset $S \subset \mathbb{Z}/m$: the function $V\: \mathbb{Z}/m \to \{ \emptyset, \{1\}\} \xrightarrow{\sim} \{0,1\}$ satisfies the characterizing property $V(i) = 1 \implies V(i+1) = 0$.

    More generally, $V\: \mathbb{Z}/m \to D_k$ is a closed walk if and only if $V(i) \cap V(i+1) = \emptyset$ for all~$i$, so the $k$-tuple $\mathbf{S} = (S_\alpha)$ with $S_{\alpha} = \{ i \in \mathbb{Z}/m \ | \ \alpha \in V(i)\}$ consists of disconnected subsets. Thus, for arbitrary $f\: \mathbb{Z}/m \to \mathbb{Z}$, we have the interpretation $\omega_k(f) = \# \Omega_k(f)$, where
    \[
    \Omega_k(f) := \{ \text{closed walks }V\text{ of length }m\text{ in }D_k\text{ such that }\#V(i) = f(i) \text{ for all }i\}. 
    \]
                                                                                                                                                                       
    If $f = s$ is constant, then this counts closed walks in the Kneser graph $K(k,s)$, defined as the induced subgraph of $D_k$ with vertices the $s$-element subsets. In particular, if $m$ is odd and $f$ is $r$-balanced, $\omega_k(f)$ counts closed walks in $K(k,r/2)$. In this case statements such as Prop.~\ref{prop:W-sharpness} and Prop.~\ref{prop:walk-identity} acquire graph-theoretic content: the former states that the number of closed walks of any odd length~$m$ in $K(2s+q-1,s)$ is divisible by~$p$ for every $s \geqslant 1$. This can also be proved using the eigenvalues of the adjacency matrices of Kneser graphs \cite[Thm.~9.4.3]{alg-graph-thy}.
                                                                                       
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       
    
    It would be interesting to explore to what extent this connection can be used in the study of Drinfeld modular forms and/or graph theory.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:determine}
    Let $f\: \mathbb{F}_{q}^{N+1} \times \mathbb{F}_q^\times \to \mathbb{F}_q$ be a function. Then $f$ is determined by the values $\sum_{a,b}f(a,b)a_0^{v_0}a_1^{v_1}\cdots a_N^{v_N}b^t$ with $0 \leqslant v_i \leqslant q-1$ and $0 \leqslant t \leqslant q-2$.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Each function $f\: \mathbb{F}_q \to \mathbb{F}_q$ is represented by a polynomial $f(\alpha)$, so $\{\text{functions }\mathbb{F}_q \to \mathbb{F}_q\} \cong \mathbb{F}_q[\alpha]/(\alpha^q-\alpha)$, which has $\mathbb{F}_q$-basis $\{1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{q-1}\}$. Similarly, $\{\text{functions }\mathbb{F}_q^\times \to \mathbb{F}_q\}$ has basis $1,b,\ldots,b^{q-2}$. Hence functions on the product $\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N+1} \times \mathbb{F}_q^\times$ have a corresponding product basis $\{a_0^{v_0}\cdots a_N^{v_N}b^t \ | \ 0 \leqslant v_i \leqslant q-1, \, 0 \leqslant t \leqslant q-2 \}$.

    Next, the bilinear pairing $(f,g) \mapsto \sum_{a,b}f(a,b)g(a,b) \in \mathbb{F}_q$ is non-degenerate, as can be seen by pairing against indicator functions. Therefore a function is zero if it pairs to zero with all elements in the given basis, which yields our claim.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{thm:bottom-uniformity}
    For all $(\alpha,\beta) \in \mathbb{F}_q \times \mathbb{F}_q^\times$, we have
    \[
    \# \{ [\varphi] / \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^n} \ | \ a_{0,\varphi} = \alpha, \ b_\varphi = \beta\} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}.
    \]
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Let $f(\alpha,\beta) := \sum_{a_0 = \alpha} \# \operatorname{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}(a,\beta)$. We aim to show that $f(\alpha,\beta) \equiv_p 1$ for all $\alpha,\beta$. For $0 \leqslant k \leqslant q-1$ and $0 \leqslant t \leqslant q-2$, we have
    \[
    \sum_{\alpha,\beta} f(\alpha,\beta)\alpha^k\beta^t
                                                                                        
    = \sum_{a,b} \# \operatorname{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}(a,b)a_0^kb^t = \mu((k,0,\ldots,0),t) = \delta_{k,q-1}\delta_{t,0},
    \]
    the last equality being Lemma~\ref{lem:low_moments}. For the constant function $\mathds{1}(\alpha,\beta) = 1$ we compute
    \[
    \sum_{\alpha,\beta}\mathds{1}(\alpha,\beta)\alpha^k\beta^t = \sum_{\alpha}\alpha^k \sum_\beta \beta^t = \delta_{k,q-1}\delta_{t,0},
    \]
    and hence $f = \mathds{1}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:determine}.

<!-- corollary-5: body_md -->
Let $\mathfrak{p}^n$ and $(\alpha,\beta) \in \mathbb{F}_q \times \mathbb{F}_q^\times$ be arbitrary. Then there exists a rank~2 Drinfeld module $\psi$ over $\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}$ with $a_{0,\psi} = \alpha$ and $b_\psi = \beta$. Let $\pi$ be a root of $X^2 - a_\psi X + b_\psi\wp^n$ and suppose\footnote{This condition says we are not in (the exceptional) case 4 of Yu's classification \cite[Prop.~5]{yu_isogs}.} $[K(\pi) : K] = 2$. Then the Hurwitz class number $H(A[\pi])$ is not divisible by~$p$. In particular, $H(\mathcal O_{K(\pi)})$ is not divisible by~$p$, where $\mathcal O_{K(\pi)}$ denotes the maximal order in~$K(\pi)$.

The fact that $\psi$ exists is immediate from Theorem~\ref{thm:bottom-uniformity}. If $\pi \notin K$, we have $\#\operatorname{Iso}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}(a,b) \equiv_p H(A[\pi])$, where $H$ denotes the Hurwitz class number defined in \cite[Sec.~6]{gek_frobdist}. The last statement holds since if $H(A[\pi]) \not \equiv_p 0$, then $H(A[\pi]) \equiv_p H(K(\pi)) c_\pi$ with $c_\pi$ a power of~2, see \cite[Lem.~A.1]{devries_traces}.

\label{prop:coeff-is-pol}
    Fix $\Delta \in \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}^\times$. Then for each $0 \leqslant j \leqslant N$, the map $g \mapsto a_j(g,\Delta) \in \mathbb{F}_q$ is a polynomial function of $g \in \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}$ over~$\mathbb{F}_q$ of degree at most~$nd-2j$ in $nd$ variables.

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{thm:g-count-divisibility}
    Fix $S \subseteq \{0,\ldots,N\}$. Then for any  $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = (\alpha_i)_{i \in S} \in \mathbb{F}_q^S$ and $\beta \in \mathbb{F}_q^\times$, we have
    \[
    d(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) \equiv 0 \pmod{q^{\mu(S)}}.
    \]
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
We will prove that $d'(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\Delta) \equiv 0 \pmod{q^{\mu(S)}}$ for all~$\Delta \in \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^n}^\times$, where \[
                                                                                                                   
    d'(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\Delta) := \# D'(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\Delta) := \#\{ g \in \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^n} \ | \ a_i(g,\Delta) = \alpha_i \ \text{for all } i \in S \}.
    \]
    This suffices, since then
    $d(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) = \sum_{b(\Delta) = \beta} d'(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\Delta) \equiv 0 \pmod{q^{\mu(S)}}$.
    
    The case $S = \emptyset$ is trivial, since then $d'(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\Delta) = q^{nd}$ for all~$\Delta$ and $\mu(S) = nd$. The case $S = \{ nd/2 \}$ occurs only if~$nd$ is even. By Prop.~\ref{prop:coeff-is-pol}, the map $g \mapsto a_N(g,\Delta)$ is then a constant $\lambda \in \mathbb{F}_q$. Thus $d'(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\Delta)$ is either 0 (if $\lambda \neq \alpha_N$) or $q^{nd}$ (if $\lambda = \alpha_N$), so in either case the statement holds.
    
    Next suppose $S' \neq \emptyset$. If $nd/2 \in S$, then by the above argument, we either have $d'(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\Delta) = 0$ or $d'(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\Delta) = d'(\boldsymbol{\alpha}',\Delta)$, where $\boldsymbol{\alpha}' = (\alpha_i)_{i \in S'}$. In the first case we are done, so we may assume $S = S'$. Then
    \[
    d'(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\Delta) = d'(\boldsymbol{\alpha}',\Delta) = \# \{\mathbf{x} \in (\mathbb{F}_q)^{nd} \ | \ f_j(\mathbf{x}) - \alpha_j = 0 \text{ for all } j \in S' \},
    \]
    where $f_j \in \mathbb{F}_q[X_1,\ldots,X_{nd}]$ is the polynomial representing $g \mapsto a_j(g,\Delta)$ (see Prop.~\ref{prop:coeff-is-pol}).

    Since $\deg f_j \leqslant nd-2j$, the congruence now follows immediately from the Ax--Katz theorem \cite{wan_ax-katz}.

<!-- theorem-9: body_md -->
\label{thm:top-invisibility}
    Fix $S \subseteq \{0,\ldots,N\}$. Then for any  $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = (\alpha_i)_{i \in S} \in \mathbb{F}_q^S$ and $\beta \in \mathbb{F}_q^\times$,
    \[
    h(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) \equiv \begin{cases}
        0 \pmod{q^{\mu(S)}} & \text{if } nd \text{ is odd}; \\
        0 \pmod{q^{\min\{1,\mu(S)\}}} & \text{if } nd \text{ is even.}
    \end{cases}
    \]
<!-- end theorem-9 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
Suppose $nd$ is odd. By the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:domain}, the number of pairs $(g,\Delta)$ such that $[\varphi^{(g,\Delta)}] = [\varphi]$ is $(q^m-1)/(q-1)$ for each Drinfeld module $\varphi$ over $\mathbb{F}_{q^m}$. Hence we obtain
    \[
    d(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) = (1 + q + \ldots + q^{nd-1}) h(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta),
    \]
    so in particular $d(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta)$ and $h(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta)$ have the same $p$-adic valuation. Hence this case follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:g-count-divisibility}.

    Now assume $nd$ is even. Let
    \[
    h_0(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) := \# H_0(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) := \#\{[\varphi] \in H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) \ | \ \# \operatorname{Aut}(\varphi) = q^2-1\}.
    \]
    Then we have
    \[
    d(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) = \frac{q^{nd}-1}{q^2-1} h_0(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) + \frac{q^{nd}-1}{q-1} (h(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) - h_0(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta)).
    \]
    Since $d(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) \equiv_{q^{\mu(S)}} 0$, after rewriting the right-hand side we obtain
    \[
    (1+q^2 + \ldots + q^{nd-2}) (h(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) + q(h(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta) -h_0(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta))) \equiv_{q^{\mu(S)}} 0.
    \]
    In particular, if $\mu(S) > 0$ then $h(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\beta)$ is divisible by~$q$, as claimed.
<!-- end proof-26 -->
<!-- remark-14: body_md -->
In the case $n=1$ and $S = \{N\}$, the power of $q$ occurring in Theorem~\ref{thm:top-invisibility} is optimal, as evidenced by Equation~\eqref{eq:gekeler_counts}. 
    
    In general, the theorem is meaningful whenever $\mu(S) > 0$. In the case of the top coefficients $S = \{ N, N-1,\ldots, N-j\}$, we have $\mu(S) > 0$ as long as $j(j+1)<nd$ if $nd$ is even and $(j+1)^2 < nd$ if $nd$ is odd.
