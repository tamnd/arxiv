<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:stability}
For every $k\ge2$ there is a constant $c_k>0$ such that every $S\in\mathcal A_k$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stability}
 c_k\delta(S)\le\Delta(S)\le r_k\delta(S).
\end{equation}
The constants are independent of the cardinality of $S$ and of the choice of
isotropic measure supported in $S$. For fixed $k$, the linear power is optimal.
There are admissible sets with $\delta(S)\downarrow0$ and
$\Delta(S)$ comparable to $\delta(S)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:arithmetic}
Fix $k\ge2$, and let $c_k$ be as in \cref{thm:stability}.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item If $N=mk$, then $L_{N,k}=a_k$. The minimizing matrices are exactly the
 matrices that, after a simultaneous permutation of rows and columns, consist
 of $k$ diagonal blocks equal to the $m\times m$ all-ones matrix.
 \item If $N\ge k$ and $k\nmid N$, then
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:arithmetic-lower}
  L_{N,k}-a_k\ge\frac{c_k}{\sqrt{kN}}.
 \end{equation}
 \item If $k\nmid N$ and $N\ge2k^2$, then
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:arithmetic-upper}
  L_{N,k}-a_k\le\frac{2\sqrt{k}\,r_k}{\sqrt N}
                  \le\frac{2k}{\sqrt N}.
 \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, along integers not divisible by $k$,
$L_{N,k}-a_k\asymp_k N^{-1/2}$ as $N\to\infty$.

\label{thm:planar}

\end{equation}
In particular,
$\lim_{m\to\infty}\sqrt{2m+1}(L_{2m+1,2}-a_2)=1/(2\sqrt\pi)$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:finite}
Let $u_i\in S^{k-1}$ and $d_i>0$, for $1\le i\le N$, satisfy
$\sum_i d_i u_i u_i^{\mathsf T}=I_k$. If
$M=\max_i\langle g,u_i\rangle$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-dom}
 \mathbb P(M\le t)\le\Phi(t)^k\qquad(t\in\mathbb R).
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\mathbb E M=a_k$ if and only if the distinct values among the $u_i$ form
an orthonormal basis. In that case, the weights on each basis vector sum to $1$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For nonnegative integrable functions $f_i:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)$, the geometric
Brascamp--Lieb inequality \cite{Ball89,Barthe98reverse} states that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BL}
 \int_{\mathbb R^k}\prod_i f_i(\langle x,u_i\rangle)^{d_i}\,dx
 \le\prod_i\left(\int_\mathbb R f_i(s)\,ds\right)^{d_i}.
\end{equation}
Choose $f_i(s)=e^{-s^2/2}\mathbf 1_{(-\infty,t]}(s)$. Isotropy and
$\sum_i d_i=k$ turn the left side into
$(2\pi)^{k/2}\mathbb P(M\le t)$ and the right side into
$(2\pi)^{k/2}\Phi(t)^k$. This proves \eqref{eq:finite-dom}.

Let $F$ be the distribution function of $M$. Integrability and the layer-cake
identity give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-integral}
 \mathbb E M-a_k=\int_\mathbb R\bigl(\Phi(t)^k-F(t)\bigr)\,dt.
\end{equation}
The integrand is nonnegative and right-continuous. If the expectations are
equal, it vanishes everywhere. A strictly positive value, by right-continuity,
would give a positive integral over an interval to its right. Thus equality of
expectations implies equality of the two distributions.

Let $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ be the distinct directions. For $j\ne l$ write
$\rho_{jl}=\langle v_j,v_l\rangle<1$ and $A_j(t)=\{\langle g,v_j\rangle>t\}$.
If $-1<\rho_{jl}<1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-tail}
 \mathbb P(A_j(t)\cap A_l(t))
 \le\overline\Phi\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+\rho_{jl}}}\,t\right)
 =o(\overline\Phi(t))\qquad(t\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Indeed the intersection implies that the sum of the two normal variables is
larger than $2t$, while its variance is $2(1+\rho_{jl})$; the final comparison
follows from $\overline\Phi(t)\sim e^{-t^2/2}/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,t)$. If
$\rho_{jl}=-1$, the intersection is empty for $t\ge0$. Bonferroni's inequalities
and finiteness of $n$ now give
\[
 \mathbb P(M>t)=n\overline\Phi(t)(1+o(1)).
\]
Equality of laws gives the same expression with $n$ replaced by $k$, because
$1-\Phi(t)^k\sim k\overline\Phi(t)$. Hence $n=k$.

Group the weights on each distinct vector into $D_j>0$, and let $V$ have
columns $\sqrt{D_j}v_j$. Then $VV^{\mathsf T}=I_k$. Since $V$ is square,
$V^{\mathsf T}V=I_k$ as well. Its diagonal entries imply $D_j=1$, and its
off-diagonal entries imply that the $v_j$ are orthogonal. Conversely, an
orthonormal basis gives $k$ independent standard normal coordinates, and
repetitions do not change their maximum.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:compact-equality}
For every $S\in\mathcal A_k$, \eqref{eq:dom-intro} holds. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality}
 \Delta(S)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S=Q\mathcal B_k
 \text{ for some }Q\in O(k).
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $\mu$ witnesses admissibility, then $\mu/k$ is a probability measure and
$I_k/k$ is the barycenter of $uu^{\mathsf T}$ under this measure. The image
of $S$ is compact, so its convex hull contains this barycenter. The symmetric
trace-one matrices lie in an affine space of dimension $k(k+1)/2-1$.
Carath\'eodory's theorem therefore supplies vectors $u_1,\ldots,u_s\in S$,
where $s\le k(k+1)/2$, and positive weights $d_i$ such that
$\sum_i d_i u_i u_i^{\mathsf T}=I_k$. Apply \cref{prop:finite} to
$T=\{u_1,\ldots,u_s\}$ and use $h_S\ge h_T$.

If $w(S)=a_k$, then $a_k\le w(T)\le w(S)$, so $T$ is an orthonormal basis.
If $u\in S\setminus T$, then
$\langle u,u\rangle=1>\max_{v\in T}\langle u,v\rangle$. By continuity the strict inequality
$h_S(x)>h_T(x)$ holds on an open neighborhood of $u$. This neighborhood has
positive Gaussian measure, and $h_S\ge h_T$ everywhere, contradicting
$w(S)=w(T)$. Thus $S=T$. The converse is immediate.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:compactness}
The class $\mathcal A_k$ is compact in the Hausdorff metric. The functions $\delta$
and $w$ are continuous on it, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lipschitz}
 |w(S)-w(T)|\le r_k d_H(S,T).
\end{equation}
For every $\eta>0$ such that
$\{S\in\mathcal A_k:\delta(S)\ge\eta\}$ is nonempty, the minimum of $\Delta$
on this set is strictly positive.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The space of nonempty compact subsets of the compact sphere is compact in
$d_H$. To see that $\mathcal A_k$ is closed, suppose $S_n\to S$ with isotropic
measures $\mu_n$ supported in $S_n$. All these measures have mass $k$.
After passing to a subsequence they converge weakly to a measure $\mu$ of
mass $k$. Since
\[
 \int\operatorname{dist}(u,S)\,d\mu_n(u)\le k d_H(S_n,S)\longrightarrow0,
\]
weak convergence shows that $\mu$ is supported in $S$. The coordinate
functions $u\mapsto u_i u_j$ are continuous, so their integrals retain
\eqref{eq:isotropy}. Thus $S\in\mathcal A_k$.

For every $x$, the definition of $d_H$ implies
$|h_S(x)-h_T(x)|\le\lVert x\rVert d_H(S,T)$. Taking expectations proves
\eqref{eq:Lipschitz}. The triangle inequality also gives
$|\delta(S)-\delta(T)|\le d_H(S,T)$. The final assertion follows from
compactness, continuity, and \cref{prop:compact-equality}.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:masses}
Suppose $S\in\mathcal A_k$ and $d_H(S,\mathcal B_k)\le\varepsilon\le1/2$.
Then the sets
\[
 S_j=S\cap\{u:\lVert u-e_j\rVert\le\varepsilon\},\qquad 1\le j\le k,
\]
are nonempty, pairwise disjoint compact sets with union $S$. For every
isotropic measure $\mu$ supported in $S$, their masses $D_j=\mu(S_j)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass-bound}
 1-k\varepsilon^2\le D_j\le1+2\varepsilon^2,
 \qquad |D_j-1|\le k\varepsilon^2.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The covering and nonemptiness follow from the two parts of the Hausdorff
bound. The balls are disjoint because distinct basis vectors are at distance
$\sqrt2>2\varepsilon$. For $u\in S_j$,
$\langle u,e_j\rangle=1-\lVert u-e_j\rVert^2/2\ge1-\varepsilon^2/2$. For
$u\in S_l$ with $l\ne j$, $|\langle u,e_j\rangle|\le\varepsilon$. Applying the
$j$th diagonal entry of \eqref{eq:isotropy} gives
\[
 D_j(1-\varepsilon^2/2)^2\le1\le D_j+k\varepsilon^2.
\]
For $0\le\varepsilon\le1/2$, one has
$(1-\varepsilon^2/2)^{-2}\le1+2\varepsilon^2$. The two bounds and $k\ge2$
give \eqref{eq:mass-bound}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:centers}
Under the assumptions of \cref{lem:masses}, suppose also that
$k\varepsilon^2\le1/2$. Then $D_j>0$, and the centers
\[
 m_j=\frac1{D_j}\int_{S_j}u\,d\mu(u)
\]
admit an orthonormal basis $q_1,\ldots,q_k$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:center-bound}
 \max_j\lVert m_j-q_j\rVert\le2k\varepsilon^2.
\end{equation}
Furthermore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cluster-radius}
 \sup_{u\in S_j}\lVert u-q_j\rVert\le2\varepsilon+2k\varepsilon^2.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Positivity follows from \eqref{eq:mass-bound}. Define
\[
 C=\sum_j\int_{S_j}(u-m_j)(u-m_j)^{\mathsf T}\,d\mu(u),\qquad
 A=[\sqrt{D_1}m_1\ \cdots\ \sqrt{D_k}m_k].
\]
The variance decomposition gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance}
 AA^{\mathsf T}=I_k-C,\qquad C\succeq0,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr} C\le\sum_j\int_{S_j}\lVert u-e_j\rVert^2\,d\mu(u)
          \le k\varepsilon^2.
\end{equation}
The trace inequality uses the minimizing property of the mean for squared
distance. In particular, $\lVert C\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le1/2$ and $A$ is invertible.
Its polar decomposition has the form
$A=(I_k-C)^{1/2}Q$ with $Q\in O(k)$. Since
$1-\sqrt{1-s}\le s$ for $0\le s\le1$, we obtain
\[
 \lVert A-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\lVert C\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le k\varepsilon^2.
\]
Let $q_j$ be the columns of $Q$. Using $\lVert m_j\rVert\le1$ and
$|1-\sqrt{D_j}|\le|1-D_j|$, we find
\[
 \lVert m_j-q_j\rVert
 \le\lVert \sqrt{D_j}m_j-q_j\rVert+|1-\sqrt{D_j}|\lVert m_j\rVert
 \le2k\varepsilon^2.
\]
Finally, $\lVert m_j-e_j\rVert\le\varepsilon$ because $m_j$ is a barycenter of
points in the ball around $e_j$. The triangle inequality proves
\eqref{eq:cluster-radius}.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:local}
For each $k\ge2$ there are $\eta_k>0$ and $\beta_k>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local}
 \Delta(S)\ge\frac{\beta_k}{2}\delta(S)
 \qquad\text{if }S\in\mathcal A_k\text{ and }\delta(S)\le\eta_k.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Put $\varepsilon=\delta(S)$. If $\varepsilon=0$ there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise rotate a minimizing basis to $\mathcal B_k$, and choose any isotropic
measure supported in $S$. Set
\[
 K_k=\sqrt{4+(k-1)/16},\qquad
 \varepsilon_{0,k}=\min\left\{\frac18,\frac1{2k},
                                \sqrt{\frac3{32kK_k}}\right\}.
\]
For the moment suppose $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_{0,k}$, and use the
clusters, centers, and orthonormal basis from \cref{lem:masses,lem:centers}.
Since $2k\varepsilon^2\le\varepsilon$, \eqref{eq:cluster-radius} gives
$\lVert u-q_j\rVert\le3\varepsilon$ for $u\in S_j$. This is smaller than
$\sqrt2/2$. Hence every point of $S_j$ is closer to $q_j$ than to any other
$q_l$. Nonemptiness of all clusters also implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:new-basis-distance}
 d_H(S,\{q_1,\ldots,q_k\})
 =\max_j\max_{u\in S_j}\lVert u-q_j\rVert\ge\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
The last inequality uses minimality of the original distance $\delta(S)$.
Choose $j$ and $u\in S_j$ with $\lVert u-q_j\rVert\ge\varepsilon$.

Decompose $u-m_j=t+bq_j$, where $t\perp q_j$. Since $u$ and $q_j$ are unit,
\[
 \langle u-q_j,q_j\rangle=-\tfrac12\lVert u-q_j\rVert^2.
\]
Consequently, with $B_k=2k+9/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent}
 |b|\le B_k\varepsilon^2,\qquad
 \lVert t\rVert\ge\varepsilon-(2k+B_k)\varepsilon^2.
\end{equation}
For the first estimate use $\lVert u-q_j\rVert\le3\varepsilon$ and
\eqref{eq:center-bound}; for the second use
$\lVert u-q_j\rVert\le\lVert t\rVert+|b|+\lVert m_j-q_j\rVert$.

Define $H(x)=\max_l\langle x,m_l\rangle$. Since each $m_l\in\operatorname{conv} S$,
$h_S(x)\ge H(x)$ for every $x$. Consider the Gaussian event
\begin{equation}\label{eq:box}
 E_j=\{1\le\langle g,q_j\rangle\le2,\quad
           |\langle g,q_l\rangle|\le1/4\text{ for }l\ne j\}.
\end{equation}
On $E_j$, $\lVert g\rVert\le K_k$ and, for $l\ne j$,
\[
 \langle g,m_j-m_l\rangle\ge\frac34-4kK_k\varepsilon^2\ge\frac38.
\]
Thus $H(g)=\langle g,m_j\rangle$ on $E_j$. We have both $h_S(g)\ge H(g)$ and
$h_S(g)\ge\langle g,u\rangle$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain-pointwise}
 h_S(g)-H(g)\ge\mathbf 1_{E_j}(g)(\langle g,u-m_j\rangle)_+.
\end{equation}

Let $Z\sim N(0,I_{k-1})$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
 \beta_k=\mathbb P(1\le g_1\le2)
  \min_{\substack{v\in\mathbb R^{k-1}\\\lVert v\rVert=1}}
  \mathbb E\left[(\langle Z,v\rangle)_+\mathbf 1_{[-1/4,1/4]^{k-1}}(Z)\right].
\end{equation}
The expectation is continuous in $v$. For every unit $v$, the linear
functional $z\mapsto\langle z,v\rangle$ is positive on an open subset of the cube,
where the Gaussian density is positive. Compactness of the unit sphere
therefore shows that $\beta_k>0$.

The coordinates of $g$ in the $q$-basis are independent standard normals.
Since $t\perp q_j$, the definition of $\beta_k$ and
$(x+y)_+\ge x_+-|y|$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain-expectation}
 \mathbb E\left[\mathbf 1_{E_j}(\langle g,u-m_j\rangle)_+\right]
 \ge\beta_k\lVert t\rVert-2|b|.
\end{equation}
Here the term $2|b|$ bounds $|b\langle g,q_j\rangle|$ on $E_j$; the estimate also
holds when $t=0$. On the other hand, \eqref{eq:center-bound} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:baseline-centers}
 \mathbb E H(g)\ge a_k-2kr_k\varepsilon^2.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:tangent} and
\eqref{eq:gain-pointwise}--\eqref{eq:baseline-centers}, we obtain
\[
 \Delta(S)\ge\beta_k\varepsilon-A_k\varepsilon^2,
 \qquad A_k=2kr_k+\beta_k(2k+B_k)+2B_k.
\]
Taking $\eta_k=\min\{\varepsilon_{0,k},\beta_k/(2A_k)\}$ proves
\eqref{eq:local}.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:sharpness}
For every $k\ge2$ there is a family $S_\theta\in\mathcal A_k$, $\theta\downarrow0$,
such that $\delta(S_\theta)\asymp\theta$ and
$\Delta(S_\theta)\asymp_k\theta$. In particular, no estimate
$\delta(S)\le C\Delta(S)^p$, with fixed $C$ and $p>1$, holds for all
admissible sets in any neighborhood of the orthonormal bases.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $R_\theta$ rotate the $(e_1,e_2)$-plane by $\theta$ and fix its orthogonal
complement, and put $S_\theta=\mathcal B_k\cup R_\theta\mathcal B_k$.
Assign weight $1/2$ to each vector of the two bases, adding weights at repeated
points. The resulting measure is isotropic. The midpoint basis gives
$\delta(S_\theta)\le2\sin(\theta/4)$.

For the reverse bound, take $0<\theta\le1/4$ and any basis at Hausdorff
distance $d<1/4$ from $S_\theta$. The points $e_1$ and $R_\theta e_1$ must
be assigned to the same basis vector. If they were within $d$ of distinct
basis vectors, the distance between those vectors would be at most
$2d+2\sin(\theta/2)<\sqrt2$, a contradiction. Hence
$d\ge\lVert e_1-R_\theta e_1\rVert/2=\sin(\theta/2)$. If $d\ge1/4$, this
lower bound holds as well. Therefore
\[
 \sin(\theta/2)\le\delta(S_\theta)\le2\sin(\theta/4).
\]
The already proved two-sided estimate gives the claimed order for $\Delta$.
If $\delta\le C\Delta^p$ with $p>1$, the upper estimate
$\Delta\le r_k\delta$ would imply
$1\le Cr_k^p\delta^{p-1}$ along this family, which is impossible.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:construction}
Suppose $N=mk+r$, $1\le r\le k-1$, and $m\ge2k$. There is a frame in
$\mathcal F_{N,k}$ whose set of directions $S$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:construction-bound}
 d_H(S,\mathcal B_k)\le\frac{2\sqrt{k}}{\sqrt N},\qquad
 w(S)-a_k\le\frac{2\sqrt{k}\,r_k}{\sqrt N}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let $p=k-r$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:construction-parameters}
 P=\left\lfloor\frac{m+1}{2p}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 \sin^2\theta=\frac1{2kP},\qquad 0<\theta<\frac\pi2.
\end{equation}
Here $P\ge1$. Start with $m$ copies of $e_l$ for $1\le l\le p$, and
$m+1$ copies of $e_j$ for $p<j\le k$. For each pair $(j,l)$ with
$j>p$ and $l\le p$, replace $2P$ of the copies assigned to $j$ by $P$
copies of each of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:construction-vectors}
 v_{jl}^+=\cos\theta\,e_j+\sin\theta\,e_l,
 \qquad v_{jl}^-=\cos\theta\,e_j-\sin\theta\,e_l.
\end{equation}
Keep the remaining $m+1-2Pp$ copies of $e_j$. This number is nonnegative,
and the total number of vectors remains $pm+r(m+1)=N$. All vectors are unit.

The sum of the outer products for one group of $2P$ vectors is
\[
 P\bigl(v_{jl}^+(v_{jl}^+)^{\mathsf T}
       +v_{jl}^-(v_{jl}^-)^{\mathsf T}\bigr)
 =2P\cos^2\theta\,e_je_j^{\mathsf T}
   +2P\sin^2\theta\,e_le_l^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Compared with $2P$ copies of $e_j$, this transfers
$2P\sin^2\theta=1/k$ from the $j$th diagonal to the $l$th diagonal and
creates no off-diagonal entries. Each low-count axis receives $r/k$, and
each high-count axis loses $p/k$. The resulting diagonal entries are all
$m+r/k=N/k$. Hence the constructed family belongs to $\mathcal F_{N,k}$.

Every vector assigned to axis $j$ lies within $2\sin(\theta/2)$ of $e_j$,
and each axis has at least one assigned vector. Thus the same quantity bounds
both directed Hausdorff distances. Since $\theta\in(0,\pi/2)$,
\[
 2\sin(\theta/2)\le\sqrt2\sin\theta=\frac1{\sqrt{kP}}.
\]
The inequality $\lfloor x\rfloor\ge x/2$ for $x\ge1$ gives
$P\ge(m+1)/(4p)$. Therefore
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt{kP}}
 \le2\sqrt{\frac{p}{k(m+1)}}
 \le\frac2{\sqrt{m+1}}
 \le\frac{2\sqrt{k}}{\sqrt N},
\]
where the last step uses $N<k(m+1)$. The width estimate follows from
\eqref{eq:Lipschitz} and $w(\mathcal B_k)=a_k$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:blocks}
Let $(u_i,d_i)_{i=1}^N$ be a finite isotropic family in $\mathbb R^k$, let
$R_{ii'}=\langle u_i,u_{i'}\rangle$, and put
$\Delta=\mathbb E\max_i\langle g,u_i\rangle-a_k$. If $\Delta\le c_k/2$, there are an
orthonormal basis $q_1,\ldots,q_k$ and a partition into nonempty classes
$I_1,\ldots,I_k$ such that
\begin{align}
 \max_{i\in I_j}\lVert u_i-q_j\rVert&\le\frac{\Delta}{c_k},
                                                        \label{eq:block-directions}\\
 \left|\sum_{i\in I_j}d_i-1\right|&\le\frac{k\Delta^2}{c_k^2},
                                                        \label{eq:block-masses}\\
 0\le1-R_{ii'}&\le\frac{2\Delta^2}{c_k^2}
          &&(i,i'\in I_j),                              \label{eq:block-within}\\
 |R_{ii'}|&\le\frac{2\Delta}{c_k}
          &&(i\in I_j,\ i'\in I_l,\ j\ne l).           \label{eq:block-between}
\end{align}
If in addition $N=mk$ and all $d_i=1/m$, then $|I_j|=m$ for every $j$
whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-threshold}
 \Delta<\min\left\{\frac{c_k}{2},\frac{c_k}{\sqrt{km}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Choose a basis realizing $\delta(S)$, where $S=\{u_i\}$.
\Cref{thm:stability} gives $\delta(S)\le\Delta/c_k\le1/2$.
Partition indices according to the clusters around that basis.
\Cref{lem:masses} proves \eqref{eq:block-directions} and
\eqref{eq:block-masses}. Within one cluster,
$\lVert u_i-u_{i'}\rVert\le2\Delta/c_k$, so
$1-R_{ii'}=\lVert u_i-u_{i'}\rVert^2/2$ proves \eqref{eq:block-within}.
For different clusters, orthogonality and the unit norms imply
\[
 |\langle u_i,u_{i'}\rangle|
 =|\langle u_i-q_j,u_{i'}\rangle+\langle q_j,u_{i'}-q_l\rangle|
 \le\frac{2\Delta}{c_k},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:block-between}. In the equal-weight case,
\eqref{eq:block-masses} gives
$\bigl||I_j|-m\bigr|\le mk\Delta^2/c_k^2<1$ under
\eqref{eq:block-threshold}. This is an integer, and hence equals zero.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:planar-expansion}
Suppose a finite set of unit vectors consists of two nonempty clusters with
angles $x_i$ and $\pi/2+y_i$, where $|x_i|,|y_i|\le\epsilon$ and
$0<\epsilon\le1/8$. Write $x_-,x_+$ and $y_-,y_+$ for the extreme
angles of the two clusters. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perimeter-expansion}
 \begin{split}
 P(\operatorname{conv} S)=2\sqrt2
 &+(x_+-x_-)+(y_+-y_-)\\
 &+\frac1{\sqrt2}(y_-+y_+-x_--x_+)+O(\epsilon^2).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The error constant is independent of the number of vectors and their
multiplicities.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Remove repeated points and order the remaining angles around the circle.
Each point is a vertex of the convex hull. A gap $v$ between consecutive
angles contributes the chord $2\sin(v/2)$ to the perimeter. Within the
first cluster the gaps sum to $x_+-x_-$, and their cubic powers sum to at
most $(x_+-x_-)^3$. Thus the total chord length within that cluster is
$x_+-x_-+O(\epsilon^3)$. The same argument applies to the second cluster.
The two remaining gaps are
$\pi/2+y_--x_+$ and $3\pi/2+x_--y_+$. Taylor expansion of their chord
lengths gives the constant $2\sqrt2$ and the linear term displayed in
\eqref{eq:perimeter-expansion}, with error $O(\epsilon^2)$. The calculation
also applies when a cluster consists of a single point.

We first prove a lower bound for a minimizing frame with odd size $N$.
\Cref{thm:arithmetic} gives an upper bound $O(N^{-1/2})$ for its excess.
\Cref{thm:stability} therefore places its directions within $O(N^{-1/2})$
of a basis. Apply \cref{lem:centers} and use its orthonormal basis as the
coordinate basis. The resulting cluster angles $x_i,y_i$ obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:planar-small}
 \begin{gathered}
 \max_i(|x_i|,|y_i|)=O(N^{-1/2}),\quad n_1,n_2=N/2+O(1),\\
 \bar x=O(N^{-1}),\qquad \bar y=O(N^{-1}).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All constants here are uniform over minimizing frames. The angle means
follow from \eqref{eq:center-bound}, since the averages of $\sin x_i$
and $\sin y_i$ are $O(N^{-1})$ and $|s-\sin s|=O(|s|^3)$.
The count estimate follows from \cref{lem:masses}.

Let the centered, scaled angles in cluster $j$ range over $[-A_j,B_j]$,
and let $V_j$ be their empirical variance. Thus in the first cluster
the scaled angles are $\sqrt N(x_i-\bar x)$, and likewise in the second.
Their means are zero, so $A_j,B_j\ge0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:range-variance}
 V_j\le A_jB_j.
\end{equation}
Indeed $(z+A_j)(B_j-z)\ge0$ on this interval, and its empirical mean is
$A_jB_j-V_j$.

The diagonal tight-frame equation is
$\sum_{i=1}^{n_1}\cos(2x_i)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_2}\cos(2y_i)$.
Using \eqref{eq:planar-small} and $\cos(2s)=1-2s^2+O(s^4)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:planar-defect}
 n_1-n_2=\frac{2n_1}{N}V_1-\frac{2n_2}{N}V_2+O(N^{-1})
         =V_1-V_2+O(N^{-1}).
\end{equation}
The integer $n_1-n_2$ is odd. Hence $|V_1-V_2|\ge1-O(N^{-1})$.

Put $\kappa_\pm=1\pm1/\sqrt2$. By
\cref{lem:planar-expansion}, replacing angle means by zero changes the
perimeter by only $O(N^{-1})$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:planar-objective}
 \begin{gathered}
 \sqrt N\,(w(S)-a_2)=\frac{J}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}+O(N^{-1/2}),\\
 J=\kappa_+A_1+\kappa_-B_1+\kappa_-A_2+\kappa_+B_2.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Since $\kappa_-\kappa_+=1/2$, weighted arithmetic--geometric mean and
\eqref{eq:range-variance} imply
\[
 J\ge\sqrt2(\sqrt{V_1}+\sqrt{V_2})
   \ge\sqrt{2|V_1-V_2|}\ge\sqrt2-O(N^{-1}).
\]
Thus $L_{N,2}-a_2\ge1/(2\sqrt{\pi N})-O(N^{-1})$.

For the upper bound write $N=2m+1$, $n=m+1$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:planar-construction}
 p=\frac{2-\sqrt2}{4},\quad A=\lfloor pn+1/2\rfloor,\quad B=n-A,
 \quad d=\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{N}{4AB}}.
\end{equation}
These expressions are well-defined for all sufficiently large $m$. Define
\[
 x=-\tfrac12\arg(A+B e^{2\mathrm i d}),\qquad y=x+d,
\]
using the argument near zero. Since
$|A+B e^{2\mathrm i d}|^2=n^2-4AB\sin^2d=m^2$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:planar-exact}
 A e^{2\mathrm i x}+B e^{2\mathrm i y}=m.
\end{equation}
Take $m$ copies of $e_1$. Add $A$ copies of $(-\sin x,\cos x)$
and $B$ copies of $(-\sin y,\cos y)$. Equation \eqref{eq:planar-exact} says
that their doubled-angle sum vanishes. They form an exactly tight frame.

Since $A/n=p+O(N^{-1})$ and $p(1-p)=1/8$, expansion of
\eqref{eq:planar-construction} yields
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \sqrt N\,d=2\sqrt2+O(N^{-1}),\\
 \sqrt N\,x=-(1+\sqrt2)+O(N^{-1}),\quad
 \sqrt N\,y=\sqrt2-1+O(N^{-1}).
 \end{gathered}
\]
For the last two identities use
$\arg(A+B e^{2\mathrm i d})=2(B/n)d+O(d^3)$.
The first cluster has zero range, while the second has the scaled endpoints
$-(1+\sqrt2)$ and $\sqrt2-1$ up to $O(N^{-1})$.
Their contribution to $J$ is
$\kappa_-(1+\sqrt2)+\kappa_+(\sqrt2-1)=\sqrt2$.
\Cref{lem:planar-expansion} gives the matching upper bound and proves
\eqref{eq:planar-main}.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:planar-rigidity}
Suppose $N=2m+1\to\infty$ and a sequence of tight frames satisfies
\[
 w(S_N)-a_2=\frac1{2\sqrt{\pi N}}+o(N^{-1/2}).

<!-- proposition-7: body_md -->
\label{prop:relaxation}
The relaxation has a positive attained minimum and

preserve its mean and second moments. Restricting the support to these

relaxation is feasible.
