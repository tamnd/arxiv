<!-- theorem--1: body_md -->
Let $\mathbb{K}$ be a field, let $Q$ be a nonempty quiver, and let
$C=\mathbb{K} Q$ be its path coalgebra with dual algebra $R=C^*$.
The following conditions are equivalent:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Every vertex of $Q$ has access to a branching vertex.
\item $R$ has no nonzero uniform right ideals.
\item The injective hull $E(R_R)$ is superdecomposable.
\item $C$ has no nonzero hollow factor modules as a left
      $R$-module under the canonical action.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem--1 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
Let $R = \mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{N}}/I$ be the quotient of the ring of all sequences in $\mathbb{K}$ by the ideal $I = \mathbb{K}^{(\mathbb{N})}$ of all sequences with finitely many nonzero entries. Given any nonzero element $a = (a_n) + I$ with $\operatorname{supp}((a_n)) = \{n \in \mathbb{N} : a_n \neq 0 \}$, we can write $\operatorname{supp}((a_n)) = X \cup Y$ into a disjoint union of infinite sets. Let $e_X$ be the idempotent of $R$ given by $(e_X)_n = 1$ if and only if $n \in X$ and zero otherwise. Then $ae_X R$ and $ae_Y R$ are both nonzero such that $ae_X R \cap ae_Y R = 0$. Thus, $E(R_R)$ is superdecomposable.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- example-2: body_md -->
\label{powerseries}
Let $R = \mathbb{K} \langle x,y\rangle$ be the free algebra in two variables over a field $\mathbb{K}$. If $I$ is a nonzero right ideal of $R$ and $f$ is a nonzero element of $I$, then $fxR$ and $fyR$ are two nonzero right ideals contained in $I$ with zero intersection. Hence, no nonzero right ideal of $R$ is uniform and its injective hull $E(R_R)$ is superdecomposable. More generally, the same argument holds for 
$D=\mathbb{K}\langle\langle x,y\rangle\rangle$, the free power series ring in $x$ and $y$.
<!-- end example-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
Let $M$ be a nonzero semi-injective coretractable module with endomorphism ring $S=\mathrm{End}_R (M)$. If $N<M$ and $M/N$  is hollow, then $\operatorname{Ann}_S(N)$ is a nonzero uniform right ideal of $S$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{E(S) superdecomposable iff no hollow factors}
Let $M$ be a nonzero semi-injective coretractable module with endomorphism ring $S=\mathrm{End}_R(M)$. Then $E(S_S)$ is superdecomposable if and only if $M$ has no nonzero hollow factor modules.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
($\Rightarrow$) If $M/N$ is a nonzero hollow factor, then $\operatorname{Ann}_S(N)$ is a nonzero uniform right ideal. So $E(S_S)$ is not superdecomposable by the equivalence given at the beginning of Section 1. 

($\Leftarrow$) Suppose that $M$ has no nonzero hollow factor modules and that $I$ is a nonzero uniform right ideal of $S$. Let $0 \neq f \in I$. Then the right ideal $fS \subseteq I$ is also uniform. Since $M$ is semi-injective, we have $\operatorname{Ann}_S (\ker f) = fS$. 

We claim that $M/\ker f$ is hollow. Otherwise, there exist proper submodules $A,B$ of $M$ both containing $\ker f$ such that $A+B = M$. Since $M$ is coretractable, the sets $\operatorname{Hom}_R (M/A,M)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M/B,M)$ are nonzero and hence $\operatorname{Ann}_S (A)$ and $\operatorname{Ann}_S (B)$ are nonzero right ideals contained in $\operatorname{Ann}_S (\ker f) = fS$. Since $\operatorname{Ann}_S (A) \cap \operatorname{Ann}_S (B) = \operatorname{Ann}_S (A+B) = \operatorname{Ann}_S (M) = 0$, this contradicts the uniformity of $fS$. Thus, $M/\ker f$ is a nonzero hollow factor module, contradicting the hypothesis. Hence $S$ has no nonzero uniform right ideals, so $E(S_S)$ is superdecomposable.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:coalgebra-hollow}
Let $C$ be a nonzero coalgebra over $\mathbb{K}$. Then $E(C^*_{C^*})$ is superdecomposable if and only if $C$ has no nonzero hollow factor modules as a left $C^*$-module, equivalently, no nonzero hollow factors as a right $C$-comodule.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{Finite and Totally Ordered Path Sets}
Let $Q = (Q_0, Q_1, s, t)$ be a nonempty quiver, $\mathbb{K}$ be a field, and $R$ be the dual of the path coalgebra of $Q$. Let $e \in Q_0$. 
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $S_e$ is finite, then $\pi_e R$ contains a nonzero minimal right ideal.
\item If $S_e$ is totally ordered with respect to the prefix order, then $\pi_e R$ contains a nonzero uniserial Noetherian right ideal.
\end{enumerate}
Analogous statements hold for $T_e$ and the left ideal $R\pi_e$ with the suffix order.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
(1) Consider first the case $S_e = \{e\}$. Then for any path $q$,
 
\[(\pi_e f)(q) = \sum_{uv=q} \pi_e(u)f(v) = \begin{cases}f(e) & \text{ if } q = e\\ 0 & \text{ else}\end{cases}\]
 
because if $q = ev$, then $s(q) = e$ and hence $q \in S_e = \{e\}$. Hence, $\pi_e f = \lambda \pi_e$ for $\lambda = f(e)$, and $\pi_e R = \pi_e\mathbb{K}$ is one-dimensional and therefore minimal. In case $S_e$ is finite, choose a path $p \in S_e$ of maximal length. Then $S_{t(p)} = \{t(p)\}$ and $\pi_e R \supseteq \pi_e \pi_p R = \pi_p \mathbb{K}$ is minimal. 

(2) If $S_e$ is finite, then the result follows from (1). Suppose that $S_e$ is an infinite set which is totally ordered with respect to the prefix order. Hence, for any $i \geq 0$, there exists precisely one path $p_i \in S_e$ of length $i$. Clearly, $p_0 = e$ and there exists a sequence $\{a_i\}_{i\geq 1} \in Q_{1}^{\mathbb{N}}$ such that $p_i = a_1\cdots a_i$, i.e. $S_e = \{e\} \cup \{p_i : i\geq 1\}$. Set
 
\[u_{i,i} := t(p_i), \quad u_{k,m} = a_{k+1}\cdots a_m, \quad \forall m > k.\]
 
Then for any $m \geq k$ one has $p_m = p_k u_{k,m}$. 

Let $0 \neq f \in \pi_e R$ and let $n$ be minimal with $f(p_{n})\ne 0$. Set $\lambda = f(p_{n})^{-1}$. Define $g \in R$ to vanish outside the set $\{u_{n,m}: m \ge n\}$, and on these paths by recursion on the length:
 
\[g(u_{n,n}) = \lambda, \qquad g(u_{n,m}) = -\lambda\sum_{k = n + 1}^m f(p_k)g(u_{k,m}) \quad (m > n).\]
 
We claim that $fg = \pi_{p_n}$, because
 
\[(fg)(p_n) = \sum_{uv=p_n} f(u)g(v) = f(p_n)g(u_{n,n}) = 1,\]
 
since $f(p_k) = 0$ for $k < n$ because of the minimality of $n$. Moreover, for any $m>n$, 
 
\[ (fg)(p_m) = \sum_{k=n}^{m} f(p_k)g(u_{k,m}) = \lambda^{-1}g(u_{n,m}) + \sum_{k=n+1}^m f(p_k)g(u_{k,m}) = 0. \]
 
It is also clear that $(fg)(p_k) = 0$ for $k < n$ by the minimality of $n$. Thus, $fg = \pi_{p_n}$ and  $\pi_{p_n}R \subseteq fR$. On the other hand, $f \in \pi_{p_n}R$ because $\pi_{p_n}R$ consists of functions that are supported on paths which have $p_n$ as a prefix and $f$ is such a function by the assumption. Hence, $fR = \pi_{p_n}R$. 

Let $J$ be a nonzero right subideal of $\pi_e R$. From the preceding paragraph, for each nonzero $f\in J$, there exists $i \ge 0$ such that $fR = \pi_{p_i}R$. Let $k = \min \{i : \text{there exists } f\in J \text{ with } f(p_i)\ne 0 \}$. Then $J = \pi_{p_k}R$ and each nonzero subideal of $\pi_e R$ is of the form $\pi_{p_i}R$ and hence $\pi_e R$ is uniserial and Noetherian since a strictly ascending chain of these ideals would give a strictly decreasing sequence of their indices.
<!-- end proof-2 -->

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{Access to a Branching Vertex}
Let $\mathbb{K}$ be a field, $Q$ be a quiver, and $C$ and $R$ be its path coalgebra and convolution algebra, respectively. Suppose that $f$ is a nonzero element in $R$ and $p$ is a path of minimal length in the support of $f$ so that $f(\alpha) = 0$ for every path $\alpha$ such that $|\alpha| < |p|$. If $t(p)$ is a branching vertex, then the right ideal $I = fR$ is not uniform.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $v = t(p)$. By hypothesis, there are distinct arrows $a,b$ whose source is $v$. Consider the right ideals
 
\[U = f \pi_a  R, \qquad V = f \pi_b  R\]
 
contained in $I$, which are both nonzero since $(f \pi_a)(pa) = f(p) \neq 0$ and $(f \pi_b)(pb) = f(p)\neq 0$.

We claim that $U \cap V = 0$. Suppose that this is not true and that
 
\[0 \neq f \pi_a  g = f \pi_b  h\]
 
for some $g, h \in R$. Since $f \pi_a  g$ is nonzero, there exists a path $u$ for which
 
\[(f \pi_a  g)(u) = \sum_{u_1 au_2 = u}f(u_1)g(u_2) \neq 0.\]
 
So there exists a decomposition $u = u_1 ar$ with $f(u_1)g(r) \neq 0$ (otherwise the above sum would be zero); in particular, the set of paths $\rho$ with $s(\rho) = t(a)$ and $g(\rho)\neq 0$ is nonempty. Among all such paths $\rho$, choose $r$ of minimal length. Similarly, from $f \pi_b  h \neq 0$ we deduce that the set of paths $\sigma$ with $s(\sigma) = t(b)$ and $h(\sigma)\neq 0$ is nonempty, and we choose $s$ of minimal length among such paths. Without loss of generality (interchanging the pairs $(a,g)$ and $(b,h)$ if necessary), we may assume
 
\[|r| \leq |s|.\]

We evaluate $f \pi_a  g$ and $f \pi_b  h$ at $par$. Let
 
\[(f \pi_a  g)(par) = \sum_{\alpha a\gamma = par}f(\alpha)g(\gamma).\]
 
For every decomposition in the above sum, exactly one of the following holds:
 
\[|\alpha| < |p|, \qquad |\alpha| = |p|, \qquad |\alpha| > |p|.\]
 
In the first case $f(\alpha) = 0$, in the third $g(\gamma) = 0$, and in the second case we necessarily have $(\alpha,\gamma) = (p,r)$. Hence 
 
\[(f \pi_a  g)(par) = f(p)g(r) \neq 0.\]
 
On the other hand, when calculating $(f \pi_b   h)(par)$, then
 
\[(f \pi_b  h)(par) = \sum_{\alpha b\gamma = par}f(\alpha)h(\gamma).\]
 
If  $|\alpha| < |p|$, then $\alpha$ is a prefix of $p$ and $f(\alpha)=0$ by the minimality of $p$.
If  $|\alpha| > |p|$, then $pa$ is a prefix of $\alpha$. Thus, $\alpha b \gamma = par$ yields $|\gamma| < |r| \le |s|$ and hence $h(\gamma)=0$ by minimality of  $s$. The only remaining case where $|\alpha| = |p|$ is impossible because the arrow immediately following $p$ in $par$ is $a \neq b$. Therefore,
 
\[(f \pi_b  h)(par) = 0,\]
 
contradicting $f \pi_a  g = f \pi_b  h$. Therefore, $U\cap V = 0$. Hence,  $I$ contains two nonzero right ideals with zero intersection and it is not uniform.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{Main Theorem}
Let $\mathbb{K}$ be a field, $Q$ a nonempty quiver, and $C$ and $R$ be its path coalgebra and convolution algebra, respectively. The following statements are equivalent:
 
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every vertex of $Q$ has access to a branching vertex.
\item $R$ has no nonzero uniform right ideals.
\item $E(R_R)$ is superdecomposable.
\item $C$ has no nonzero hollow factor modules as a left
$R$-module.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
$(1) \Rightarrow (2)$ Let $I$ be a nonzero right ideal of $R$. Take $0 \neq f \in I$ and let $p$ be a path of minimal length such that $f(p) \neq 0$. By assumption, there exists a path $q$ such that $s(q) = t(p)$ for which $t(q)$ emits at least two distinct arrows. The functional 
 
\[\overline{f} = f\pi_q \in I,\]
 
satisfies $\overline{f}(pq) = f(p) \ne 0$ and has the path $pq$ as a minimal length element in its support, since $p$ is minimal and any element in its support must have $q$ as a suffix. Since $t(pq)$ is a branching vertex, then $\overline{f}R$ is not uniform by Lemma~\ref{Access to a Branching Vertex} which, in turn, implies $I$ is not uniform.

(2) $\Rightarrow$ (1) Suppose that $Q$ has a vertex $e$ which does not have access to a branching vertex. Then the set $S_e$ is totally ordered by the prefix order, because every vertex reachable from $e$ emits at most one arrow. Since minimal and uniserial ideals are uniform, in each case, it follows from Lemma~\ref{Finite and Totally Ordered Path Sets} that $R$ contains a nonzero uniform right ideal.

The equivalence of (2) and (3) is established at the beginning of Section~\ref{sec:superdecomposable modules}, while the equivalence of (3) and (4) follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:coalgebra-hollow}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->

Consider the quiver
\[
Q: \xymatrix{
1 \ar[r]^z & 2 \ar@(ul,ur)[]^x \ar@(dl,dr)[]_y
}
\qquad \qquad 
Q^{op}: \xymatrix{
1  & 2 \ar[l]_z \ar@(ul,ur)[]^x \ar@(dl,dr)[]_y
}
\]
and let $C$ be its path coalgebra over a field $\mathbb{K}$ and let $R=C^*$ be the complete path algebra of $Q$. By Theorem \ref{Main Theorem},  $E(R_R)$ is superdecomposable. We can identify $R$ with the generalized upper triangular matrix ring
$$R \simeq \left(\begin{array}{cc} \mathbb{K} & D \\ 0 & D\end{array}\right), \qquad \mbox{ with } D=\mathbb{K}\langle\langle x,y\rangle\rangle.$$
Note that $R$ has a minimal left ideal. Hence $E({_RR})$ is not superdecomposable. Alternatively, one could consider $R^{op}$ as the complete path algebra of the quiver $Q^{op}$, obtained from $Q$ by reversing arrows, which does not satisfy   Theorem \ref{Main Theorem}.

<!-- example-4: body_md -->
Consider the quiver
\[
Q: \xymatrix{
1 \ar[r] &
2 \ar[r] &
3 \ar[r] &
4.
}
\]
Then $Q$ does not satisfy Theorem \ref{Main Theorem}. Adding to each arrow in $Q$ an arrow in the opposite direction, yields
\[
\overline{Q}:\xymatrix{
1 \ar@/^/[r] &
2 \ar@/^/[l] \ar@/^/[r] &
3 \ar@/^/[l] \ar@/^/[r] &
4 \ar@/^/[l]
}
\]
which satisfies the condition of Theorem \ref{Main Theorem}. More generally, let  $Q=(Q_0,Q_1,s,t)$ be any nonempty quiver. Create a new quiver  $\overline{Q}=(\overline{Q}_0,\overline{Q}_1,\overline{s},\overline{t})$, by adding to $Q_1$ a copy of each arrow in reverse direction, i.e. set $\overline{Q}_0=Q_0$, $\overline{Q}_1 = Q_1 \cup Q_1^{op}$, with $Q_1^{op}=\{ a^{op} : a\in Q_1\}$, $\overline{s}(a^{op})=t(a)$ and $\overline{t}(a^{op})=s(a)$, for any $a\in Q_1$, while $\overline{s}_{\mid_{Q_1}}=s$ and $\overline{t}_{\mid_{Q_1}}=t$. If $Q$ is a non-empty, connected  quiver $Q$, then  $\overline{Q}$ satisfies Theorem \ref{Main Theorem}, provided $Q$ has at least one arrow and is not the quiver $\xymatrix{ 1 \ar[r] &2 }$.
