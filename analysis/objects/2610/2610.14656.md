<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:A1}
At each iterate the $k$-th and $(k{+}1)$-th largest entries of
$\boldsymbol{b}+\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}$ are distinct, so the active tail is unique. This holds with
probability one for continuous loss distributions and can be enforced by an
$\varepsilon$-perturbation otherwise.
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:ccecp}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:A1}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:ccecp} terminates after
finitely many iterations --- at most the number of distinct tail sets,
$\binom{S}{k}$ --- and returns a global optimum of \eqref{eq:ratio}.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The feasible set of the full homogenised problem \eqref{eq:homfull} is the intersection of finitely many half-spaces \eqref{eq:homcut}, one per tail set. Algorithm~\ref{alg:ccecp} is Kelley's cutting-plane method
\cite{Kelley1960} on this polyhedron: at each step it solves a relaxation using a subset of the cuts, so its optimal value upper-bounds the true optimum. Let $\mathcal{T}^{j}$ be the active tail at the recovered $\boldsymbol{x}^{j+1}=\boldsymbol{y}^{j+1}/t^{j+1}$. If $\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{b}+\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}^{j+1})=\mathcal{T}^{j}$, the cut just added is the supporting hyperplane that is tight at $\boldsymbol{x}^{j+1}$, so \eqref{eq:support} holds with equality and $\Delta\operatorname{\mathsf{CVaR}}_\alpha(\boldsymbol{x}^{j+1}) = (\bar{\boldsymbol{c}}^{j})^\top\boldsymbol{x}^{j+1}+C^{j} \le 1/t^{j+1}$: the point is feasible for \eqref{eq:ratio} and, maximising the objective over a relaxation, globally optimal. Otherwise the newly revealed tail $\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{b}+\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}^{j+1})$ yields a cut violated by the current iterate (its CVaR exceeds the relaxation bound), so it differs from every previous cut and strictly shrinks the relaxation. Each non-terminating step thus consumes a distinct tail set; with $\binom{S}{k}$ tail sets of size $k$, termination is finite.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rmk:edge}
Three conditions underlie the homogenisation. 
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Positive denominator:} The substitution $t = 1/\Delta\operatorname{\mathsf{CVaR}}_\alpha(\boldsymbol{x})$ requires 
  \begin{equation}
  \Delta\operatorname{\mathsf{CVaR}}_\alpha(\boldsymbol{x})>0,
  \end{equation} 
  which holds for any allocation that adds tail
risk. The only excluded point is the riskless direction
$\Delta\operatorname{\mathsf{CVaR}}_\alpha(\boldsymbol{x})=0$, where the ratio is undefined. In that degenerate case the homogenised master \eqref{eq:homfull} is unbounded along $t\to\infty$,
which the solver flags directly --- it is a property of the instance, not a failure of the method, and signals that the reward can be earned at zero
marginal tail risk. 

\item \emph{Integer tail:} When $(1-\alpha)S$
is fractional, the boundary scenario carries a fractional Value-at-Risk weight; the cut then acquires the extra $z$-term of the general Rockafellar--Uryasev
representation \cite{Fabian2008}, and ECP applies unchanged with that cut.

\item \emph{A minimum $\boldsymbol{x}$ is required when $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\neq0$:} For a
\emph{strictly} concave numerator the box must exclude the origin, $\underline{\boldsymbol{x}}>\boldsymbol0$; otherwise the supremum of \eqref{eq:ratio} is approached as $\boldsymbol{x}\to\boldsymbol0$ and is not attained. The reason is the
degenerate cell of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. Near the origin the active tail is the base tail, whose intercept vanishes, $C(\mathcal{T}_0)=0$, so there $\Delta\operatorname{\mathsf{CVaR}}_\alpha$ is not merely linear but positively homogeneous; along a
ray $\boldsymbol{x}=\theta\boldsymbol{d}$ the ratio is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \frac{f(\theta\boldsymbol{d})}{\Delta\operatorname{\mathsf{CVaR}}_\alpha(\theta\boldsymbol{d})}
  \;=\; \frac{\boldsymbol{r}^\top\boldsymbol{d} - \tfrac12\theta\,\boldsymbol{d}^\top\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{d}}
             {\bar{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\mathcal{T}_0}^\top\boldsymbol{d}},
  \label{eq:raydegen}
\end{equation}
which is \emph{strictly decreasing} in $\theta$ whenever $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{d}\neq0$: the
reward and the marginal risk both scale linearly, while the quadratic penalty
is second order, so shrinking the allocation always improves the ratio. In
homogenised terms, $C(\mathcal{T}_0)=0$ means the base cut $\bar{\boldsymbol{c}}(0)^\top\boldsymbol{y}\le1$
never bounds $t$, so $t\to\infty$, the perspective term
$\boldsymbol{y}^\top\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{y}/(2t)$ vanishes and the master degenerates to its $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=0$
linear case. The implementation therefore rejects
$\underline{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol0$ for nonzero $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ rather than reporting a tolerance-dependent point for a supremum that is not attained. Any strictly positive floor $\underline{\boldsymbol{x}}>\boldsymbol0$ bounds $\theta$ away from zero and restores attainment; the experiments of Section~\ref{sec:qpexp} use $\underline{x}=0.05$. The linear case is unaffected, since \eqref{eq:raydegen} is then constant in $\theta$.
\end{enumerate}
