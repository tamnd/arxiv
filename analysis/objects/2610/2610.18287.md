<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:totof} 
Fix $k\geq1$ and let $B=[k]\times\mathbb Q$, where $[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}$.
Then $C_i\coloneqq \{i\}\times\mathbb Q$ carries a strict rational order $<_i$;
the relation $\sim$ says that two elements belong to the same part.
Formally, 
\begin{alignat*}{2}
 \mathfrak D_k=(B;<_1,\ldots,<_k,\sim),\qquad & 
 (i,a)<_j(i',b)\ & &\Longleftrightarrow\ i=i'=j\text{ and }a<b \\
&  (i,a) \sim (i',b) \ & &\Longleftrightarrow\ i=i' 
\end{alignat*} 
A structure is called \emph{finite-on-top-of-temporal} if it is a first-order expansion 
of $\mathfrak D_k$ with a finite relational signature.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- example-3: body_md -->
A structure $\mathfrak B$ is finite if and only if $f_{\mathfrak B}(n)$ is eventually $0$.
<!-- end example-3 -->
<!-- example-4: body_md -->
A famous result of Cameron~\cite{Cameron5} states that for any countably infinite structure $\mathfrak B$, we have 
$f_{\mathfrak B}(n) = 1$ for all $n \in {\mathbb N}$ if and only if $\mathfrak B$ is isomorphic to a temporal structure.
<!-- end example-4 -->
<!-- example-5: body_md -->
By the theorem of Ryll-Nardzewski, Engeler, and Svenonius (see, e.g.,~\cite{Hodges}) a countable structure $\mathfrak B$ is 
$\omega$-categorical\@gobble{A relational structure $\mathfrak B$ with finite signature is called \emph{$\omega$-categorical} if its first-order theory, i.e., the set of all first-order sentences that hold in $\mathfrak B$, has only one countable model up to isomorphism.} if and only if 
$f_{\mathfrak B}(n)$ is finite for all $n \in {\mathbb N}$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:products}
    Let $f\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathfrak B)$ be $s$-ary, and let $L^1,\dots,L^s$ be finite CSAC tuber systems of $\mathfrak A$. Then the tuber system $H\coloneqq f(L^1,\dots,L^s)$, defined for each constraint $\phi\in\Phi$ as \[H_\phi \coloneqq f(L^1_\phi,\dots,L^s_\phi) = \left\{ f(a^1,\dots,a^s) \mid a^j \in L_\phi^j, j\in[s]\right\},\]is finite and CSAC.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $L^1,\dots,L^s$ be finite CSAC tuber systems of $\mathfrak A$. We define the system $P$ by \[P_\phi \coloneqq \left\{ \left((a^1_i,\dots,a^s_i)\right)_{i\in V_\phi}\mid (a_i^j)_{i\in V_\phi} \in L_\phi^j, j\in[s]\right\}\]for every $\phi\in\Phi$. Applying $f$ coordinatewise to every tuple of $P_\phi$ yields $H_\phi$. Since, for arbitrary $\phi\in\Phi$, each $a^j\in L^j_\phi$ satisfies $\phi$, and $f$ is a polymorphism of $\mathfrak B$ by assumption, we get that also each $f(a^1,\dots,a^s)$ satisfies $\phi$. By construction, $P$ and $H$ are finite tuber systems.
    For every $x\in V_\phi$, we have \[ \operatorname{pr}_x(P_\phi) = \prod_{j=1}^s \operatorname{pr}_x(L^j_\phi) = \prod_{j=1}^s L_x^j = P_x,\]where $\prod$ denotes the Cartesian product of sets, and the middle equality holds since each $L^j$ is CSAC and hence AC. Similarly, \[\operatorname{pr}_x(H_\phi) = \left\{f(a^1_x,\dots,a_x^s) \mid a^j \in L_\phi^j, j\in[s] \right\} = f(\operatorname{pr}_x(P_\phi)) = f(P_x) = H_x,\]so it follows that both $P$ and $H$ are AC. 
    To prove CSAC of $H$, fix an arbitrary constraint $\phi\in\Phi$ and a tuple $a\in P_\phi$. We write $a_x$ for the tuple $(a_x^1,\dots,a_x^s)$. Note that for every $j\in[s]$, we have $a^j = (a_x^j)_{x\in V_\phi} \in L_\phi^j$. Choose for each $j$ an AC-certificate $K^j\leq L^j$ with $K_\phi^j = \{a^j\}$. As before, we define the tuber system $Q\coloneqq \prod_{j=1}^s K^j$, and $N\coloneqq f(Q)$. By the same argument as for $P$ and $H$, we get that $N$ and $Q$ are AC. Since $Q\leq P$ and $N \leq H$, and $Q_\phi = \{a\}$ and $N_\phi = \{f(a)\}$ by construction, it follows that each tuple of every constraint list of $P$ and $H$ has an AC-certificate. Thus, $P$ and $H$ are CSAC.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:certificates}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item\label{cert:colours} Each $D_x$, $x\in A$, is a non-empty subalgebra of $\mathfrak E$.
        \item The tuber system $W\coloneqq \{D_x\mid x\in A\}\cup\{Q_{\psi_{xy}}\mid{\psi_{xy}}\in\Psi\}$ is SAC.
        \item\label{cert:prescribed} For every $\psi_{xy}\in\Psi$ and every $(a,b)\in Q_{\psi_{xy}}$, there is a finite CSAC tuber system $L\leq T$ with $\pi(L_x)=\{a\}$ and $\pi(L_y)=\{b\}$.
    \end{enumerate}

To prove (i), note that the projection $\pi\colon(B;g_1,g_2)\to([k];\widetilde g_1,\widetilde g_2)$ is a homomorphism, and observe that $D_x = \pi(T_x)$, where $T_x$ is the closure of the set $S_x$ under $g_1,g_2$ in $B$. Since $\widetilde g_1, \widetilde g_2$ are the only operations of $\mathfrak E$ and $S_x$ is non-empty, it follows that $D_x$ is a non-empty subalgebra of $\mathfrak E$.

For (ii), we first show that the tuber system $T$ is non-empty and AC. We define for every $\{g_1,g_2\}$-term $t$ with arity $q_t$ the tuber system $L^t$ via \[ L^t_\theta \coloneqq \{t^\mathfrak B (u_1,\dots,u_{q_t}) \mid u_1,\dots,u_{q_t}\in S_\theta\},\] for each $\theta\in\Phi\cup\Psi$, and where $t^\mathfrak B$ denotes the operation defined by $t$ in $\mathfrak B$. Since $S$ is non-empty, also $L^t$ is non-empty for every $\{g_1,g_2\}$-term $t$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:products}, each $L^t$ is also finite and CSAC. For every $\theta\in\Phi\cup\Psi$, we have that $T_\theta = \cup_t L_\theta^t$. For $\theta\in\Phi\cup\Psi$, we get $\operatorname{pr}_x (T_\theta) = \operatorname{pr}_x(\cup_t L_\theta^t) = \cup_t L_x^t = T_x$, so $T$ is AC, and non-empty, since $S_\theta \subseteq T_\theta$ for each $\theta$. In particular, for every $\psi_{xy}\in\Psi$, we have $\operatorname{pr}_1(Q_{\psi_{xy}}) = \pi(\operatorname{pr}_1(T_{\psi_{xy}})) = \pi(T_x) = D_x$, and $\operatorname{pr}_2(Q_{\psi_{xy}})=D_y$. Since $L^t$ is CSAC, there is an AC tuber subsystem $K^t\leq L^t$ with $K_x^t=\{b\}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:products}, the tuber system $L\coloneqq t^\mathfrak B(L^1,\dots,L^q)$ is a finite CSAC subsystem of $T$, which by construction satisfies $L_{\psi_{xy}} = \{u\}$, and thus also $\pi(L_x)=\{a\}$ and $\pi(L_y)=\{b\}$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:monochromatic}
Let $\mathfrak A$ be an instance of $\operatorname{CSP}(\mathfrak B)$ which the algorithm from Section~\ref{sec:algorithm} accepts, and let $S$ be the non-empty tuber system on which the algorithm terminates. Then there is a map $c\colon A\to[k]$ as well as a finite non-empty CSAC tuber system $U$ of $\mathfrak A$ such that for every $\phi\in\Phi$, \begin{equation}\label{eq:monochromatic}
    U_\phi \subseteq \phi^\mathfrak B \cap \prod_{j=1}^{s_\phi} C_{c(x_j)}
\end{equation}holds. A tuber system satisfying property~(\ref{eq:monochromatic}) is called \emph{$c$-colour-determined}.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $T$, $D$, $Q$ and $W$ be defined as for Lemma~\ref{lem:certificates}. For each $\psi_{xy}\in\Psi$ and each $(a,b)\in Q_{\psi_{xy}}$, we choose a CSAC tuber system $L^{xy,ab}\leq T$ such that $\pi(L^{xy,ab}_x)=\{a\}$ and $\pi(L^{xy,ab}_y)=\{b\}$. Define $P_x \coloneqq \prod_{l=1}^s L_x^l$, and define $\Lambda\coloneqq \{(x,u)\mid x\in A, u\in P_x\}$ and $r_{(x,u)}^l=\pi(u_l)$.

Fix an arbitrary $x\in A$ and $a\in D_x$. Since \(Q_{\psi_{xx}}=\{(b,b)\mid b\in D_x\}\), we have \((a,a)\in Q_{\psi_{xx}}\). By construction of the algorithm, there exists a CSAC tuber system $L^l$ with \(\pi(L_x^l)=\{a\}\).

Take an arbitrary $u\in P_x = \prod_{j=1}^s L_x^j$. It follows that $u_l\in L_x^l$, and thus $r_{(x,u)}^l = \pi(u_l) = a$. Importantly, since $l$ does not depend on $u$, we know that also $r_{(x,u')}^l = a$ holds for all $u'\neq u$. Since $r^l\in R$ by definition, we know that $a \in \operatorname{pr}_{(x,u)}(R)$. Since the choice of $a\in D_x$ was arbitrary, we get $D_x \subseteq \operatorname{pr}_{(x,u)}(R)$, proving the claim.

Additionally, we consider the projection for pairs of distinct coordinates. For $i=(x,u)$ and $j=(y,v)$, define $\operatorname{pr}_{ij}(R) = \{(r_i,r_j)\mid r\in R\}$. We claim that for $x\neq y$, we have $Q_{\psi_{xy}} \subseteq \operatorname{pr}_{ij}(R)$, while for $x=y$ and $u\neq v$, we get $\{(a,a)\mid a\in D_x\}\subseteq \operatorname{pr}_{ij}(R)$. Let $x\neq y$, and consider arbitrary $(a,b)\in Q_{\psi_{xy}}$. Select a CSAC tuber system $L^l$ such that $\pi(L_x^l)=\{a\}$ and $\pi(L_y^l)=\{b\}$. Since $u\in P_x$ and $v\in P_y$, we have $u_l\in L_x^l$ and $v_l\in L_y^l$, and so the same tuple $r^l\in R$ satisfies both $r_i^l = \pi(u_l)=a$ and $r_j^l=\pi(v_l)=b$, i.e., $(a,b)\in\operatorname{pr}_{ij}(R)$. Since the choice of $(a,b)\in Q_{\psi_{xy}}$ was arbitrary, we have $Q_{\psi_{xy}} \subseteq \operatorname{pr}_{ij}(R)$. For $i=(x,u)$ and $j=(x,v)$ with $u\neq v$, choose an arbitrary $a\in D_x$, and select a CSAC tuber system $L^l$ such that $\pi(L^l_x)=\{a\}$. Since now both $u_l,v_l\in L_x^l$, we get $r_i^l = \pi(u_l) = a = \pi (v_l) = r_j^l$, proving that $(a,a)\in\operatorname{pr}_{ij}(R)$ for each $a\in D_x$.

We now construct an instance $\mathfrak H$ of $\operatorname{CSP}([k];R, (D_x)_{x\in A}, (\Delta_{D_x})_{x\in A})$, where $\Delta_{D_x}\coloneqq \{(a,a)\mid a\in D_x\}$. Note that all relations of this template are invariant under $\widetilde g_1, \widetilde g_2$, i.e., the template has 3-4 weak near-unanimity polymorphisms. The instance $\mathfrak H$ is defined as follows:\begin{itemize}
    \item The variable set of $\mathfrak H$ is $\Lambda$. Fix an enumeration $v_1,\dots,v_\lambda\in\Lambda$, where $\lambda = |\Lambda|$.
    \item For each variable  $(x,u)\in\Lambda$, add a constraint $D_x\left((x,u)\right)$.
    \item For each pair of variables $(x,u),(x,w)\in\Lambda$, add a constraint $\Delta_{D_x}\left((x,u),(x,w)\right)$.
    \item Add one $R$-constraint on all variables of $\Lambda$, i.e., $R(v_1,\dots,v_\lambda)$.
\end{itemize}
The instance $\mathfrak H$ is AC, which follows directly from our previously proved claims. 

To see that $\mathfrak H$ is also cycle consistent, note that we can use SAC of the tuber system $W$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:certificates} (ii). The only constraint of $\mathfrak H$ not having a corresponding set in $W$ is $R$, but since $Q_{\psi_{xy}}\subseteq \operatorname{pr}_{ij}(R)$ and all $Q_{\psi_{xy}}$ are sets in $W$, all steps possible in $W$ are possible in $R$. Fix a closed constraint walk in $\mathfrak H$ starting at $v_0=(x_0,u_0)$ and a value $a\in D_{x_0}$. Choose an AC tuber system $W'\leq W$ with $W'_{x_0}=\{a\}$ and $W'_{\psi_{xy}}\subseteq Q_{\psi_{xy}}$ for each $\psi_{xy}\in\Psi$, the existence of which is given by SAC of $W$. We set the values $d_i$ for each variable $v_i=(x_i,u_i)$ of the constraint walk recursively, beginning with $d_0\coloneqq a$. If for $v_{i+1}$ we have $x_{i+1}=x_i$, we set $d_{i+1}\coloneqq d_i$. This works for each possible constraint $\theta_{i+1}$ between $v_i$ and $v_{i+1}$: if $\theta_{i+1}$ is either of the form $D_{x_i}$ or $\Delta_{D_{x_i}}$, it follows from the definitions. If $\theta_{i+1}$ is $R$, this follows since we have shown before that $\{(a,a)\mid a\in D_x\}\subseteq \operatorname{pr}_{(x,u)(x,v)}(R)$. If, on the other hand, for $v_{i+1}$ we have $x_{i+1}\neq x_i$, then the constraint $\theta_{i+1}$ has to be $R$, and we set $d_{i+1}$ such that $(d_i,d_{i+1})\in W'_{\psi_{x_ix_{i+1}}}$. This choice is compatible with $R$ since $Q_{\psi_{xy}}\subseteq \operatorname{pr}_{ij}(R)$. At the end of the constraint walk, we get $d_m\in W'_{x_m}=W'_{x_0}=\{a\}$, proving cycle consistency of $\mathfrak H$.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:external-cycle}, the instance $\mathfrak H$ has a solution, i.e., a map $p\colon\Lambda\to[k]$ preserving all constraint relations. By construction of $\mathfrak H$, we have $p(x,u) = p(x,u')$ for all $(x,u),(x,u')\in\Lambda$. We define $c$ as this constant value for each $x$, i.e., $c(x)\coloneqq p(x,u)$. Since the relation $R$ is generated by the $|\Lambda|$-tuples $r^1,\dots,r^s$, there is an $s$-ary $\{g_1,g_2\}$-term $t$ such that, evaluated in $\mathfrak E$, we have $t^\mathfrak E(r^1,\dots,r^s) = (p((x,u)))_{(x,u)\in\Lambda}$. We evaluate the term $t$ in $\mathfrak B$ and define $L'\coloneqq t^\mathfrak B(L^1,\dots,L^s)$. Since $t^\mathfrak B\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathfrak B)$, the tuber system $L'$ is finite and CSAC by Lemma~\ref{lem:products}. Also, for each $x\in A$ and every $b\in L'_x$, there is $u=(u_1,\dots,u_s)\in P_x$ such that $b=t^\mathfrak B(u_1,\dots,u_s)$. Since \[\pi(b) = \pi(t^\mathfrak B(u_1,\dots,u_s)) = t^\mathfrak E(r^1_{(x,u)},\dots,r^s_{(x,u)})=p(x,u)=c(x),\]we get $L'_x\subseteq C_{c(x)}$. Since $L'\leq T$, we also get $L'_\phi\subseteq \phi^\mathfrak B$ for all $\phi\in\Phi$. Defining $U$ as the restriction of $L'$ to $\Phi$ and $A$ (i.e., removing the sets $L'_\psi$ for all $\psi\in\Psi$) gives the required CSAC tuber system $U$ of $\mathfrak A$, finishing the proof.

\label{thm:product-normal}
    Let $d\geq 2$, and let $\mathfrak P$ be a first-order expansion of $\mathfrak Q^{(d)}$. Let $S\subseteq[d]$ be such that \begin{itemize}

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Let $k_1,\dots,k_n\in\mathbb N$, and let $m=\sum_i k_i$. A function $f\colon B^m\to B$ is \emph{$(k_1,\dots,k_n)$-block totally symmetric} if the value $f(\bar a_1,\dots, \bar a_n)$ depends only on the sets $\left(\{(\bar a_i)_j\mid j\in[k_i]\} \right)_{i\in[n]}$. 
	An $m$-tuple $t$ is a \emph{$(k_1,\dots,k_n)$-palette tuple} if every value appearing somewhere in $t$ fills some block entirely, i.e., if for all $i\in[n]$ and $j\in[k_i]$, there is $l\in[n]$ such that, for $c\coloneqq t_{k_1+\cdots+k_{i-1}+j}$, the $l$-th block of $t$ is $(c,\dots,c)$.
	A function $f\colon B^m\to B$ is \emph{$(k_1,\dots,k_n)$-palette totally symmetric} if it is $(k_1,\dots,k_n)$-block totally symmetric on all $(k_1,\dots,k_n)$-palette tuples from $B^m$.
	A function $f\colon B^m \to B$ is called a \emph{$(k_1,\dots,k_n)$-palette polymorphism} of $\mathfrak B$ if, for each relation symbol $R$ and each $(k_1,\dots,k_n)$-palette tuple $(\bar a_1,\dots,\bar a_m) \in (R^\mathfrak{B})^m$, we have $f(\bar a_1,\dots,\bar a_m) \in R^\mathfrak B$. We denote the set of all $(k_1,\dots,k_n)$-palette polymorphisms of $\mathfrak B$ by $(k_1,\dots,k_n)$-$\operatorname{Pol}(\mathfrak B)$.

so $L$ is a finite non-empty CSAC tuber system. For every

Since $t$ survives and $\operatorname{Pair}(t)$ holds, the outer
fixed-point equation implies
$\neg\operatorname{Reject}(\mathcal C[D_*\lor P_t])$.
Its remaining constraint lists form a finite non-empty CSAC subsystem
$K\leq L$ with $K_{\psi_{xy}}=\{a\}$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fp-pair-certificates}
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{for every $x,y\in A$ and $a\in L_{\psi_{xy}}$, there is a finite}\\
 \text{non-empty CSAC subsystem $K\leq L$ with $K_{\psi_{xy}}=\{a\}$.}
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
With the variable lists read from the diagonal constraints, this is
exactly the certificate property used in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:monochromatic}. That proof applies to $L$ and
yields a finite non-empty colour-determined CSAC tuber system.
Lemma~\ref{lem:temporal} then gives a homomorphism $\mathfrak A\to\mathfrak B$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:local_closure}
   Let $\mathfrak B$ be finite-on-top-of-temporal, let $i\in[k]$, and let $t\colon C_i^2\to C_i$. Then $t\in\overline{\{f|_{C_i}\mid f\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathfrak B)\}}$ if and only if there exists a binary $f\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathfrak B) $ and an embedding $ e\colon C_i\hookrightarrow C_i$ such that $f|_{C_i}=e\circ t$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Suppose first that $f \in \operatorname{Pol}(\mathfrak B)$ is such that $f|_{C_i^2}=e\circ t$, and let
$F\subseteq C_i^2$ be finite. The map sending $e(a)$ to $a$
for $a\in t(F)$ is a finite partial automorphism of $(C_i;<_i)$.
Extend it to an automorphism of this order, and act as the identity
on the other colours. The resulting map $\alpha$ is an automorphism
of $\mathfrak D_k$, hence of $\mathfrak B$. Thus $\alpha\circ f$ agrees
with $t$ on $F$, proving that $t \in \overline{\{f|_{C_i}\mid f\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathfrak B)\}}$.

Conversely, suppose $t\in\overline{\{f|_{C_i}\mid f\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathfrak B)\}}$.
Take the \emph{elementary diagram} of $\mathfrak B$, using constants $c_a$ for
$a\in B$, i.e., the set of all first-order sentences that hold in the expansion of $\mathfrak B$ with the additional constants $c_a$ for $a \in B$. 
Then further 
expand the signature by fresh constant symbols $z_{(a,b)}$, one for every $(a,b)\in B^2$.
For every basic $r$-ary relation $R$ and all
$(a_1,\ldots,a_r),(b_1,\ldots,b_r)\in R^{\mathfrak B}$, add
$R(z_{(a_1,b_1)},\ldots,z_{(a_r,b_r)})$.
Also add $z_{(a,b)}=c_{t(a,b)}$ for all $a,b\in C_i$. Let $T$ be the resulting set of first-order sentences. 
Every finite subset of $T$ is satisfiable in $\mathfrak B$:
interpret the $c_a$ by $a$ and the $z_{(a,b)}$ using a global
polymorphism whose restriction agrees with $t$ at the finitely many
specified pairs. A standard model theory argument using Compactness and downward L\"owenheim--Skolem
therefore give a countable model $\mathfrak M$ of $T$ (see, e.g.,~\cite{Hodges}).
Its reduct to the signature of $\mathfrak B$ is isomorphic to $\mathfrak B$ by
$\omega$-categoricity of $\mathfrak B$. Fix such an isomorphism $h$, and define
$f(a,b)=h(z_{(a,b)}^{\mathfrak M})$ and
$e(a)=h(c_a^{\mathfrak M})$ for $a\in C_i$.
The relation-preservation sentences make $f$ a polymorphism of $\mathfrak B$.
The elementary diagram makes $e$ an increasing embedding into $C_i$,
and the added equalities give $f|_{C_i^2}=e\circ t$.

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:pp-construction_transfer}
    Let $\mathfrak B$ and $\mathfrak C$ be structures with finite relational signatures such that $\mathfrak B$ is pp-constructible from $\mathfrak C$. If $\operatorname{CSP}(\mathfrak C)$ is expressible in $\mathrm{FPC}$, then $\operatorname{CSP}(\mathfrak B)$ is expressible in $\mathrm{FPC}$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:fp-inexpr}
    Let $\mathfrak B$ be finite-on-top-of-temporal which does not satisfy one of the conditions
    in Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic}. Then $\mathfrak B$ pp-constructs $\mathfrak X$ or some finite affine structure, and $\operatorname{CSP}(\mathfrak B)$ is inexpressible in $\mathrm{FPC}$.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Assume first that $\mathfrak B$ does not satisfy condition~(\ref{cond:temporal}) for $i\in[k]$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:local_closure}, we get that $\mathscr C_i\coloneqq\overline{\{f|_{C_i}\mid f\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathfrak B)\}}$ contains no operation from $\mathcal T$. Note that $(C_i;<_i) \cong (\mathbb Q;<)$, and that every automorphism of $(C_i;<_i)$ can be extended to an automorphism of $\mathfrak B$. Hence, 
    after appropriately identifying $C_i$ with ${\mathbb Q}$ we obtain
    $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Q;<)\subseteq\mathscr C_i$,                                                                                                                
    and since $\mathscr C_i$ is locally closed, $\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb Q;\operatorname{Inv}(\mathscr C_i))=\mathscr C_i$. Since for each $\mathfrak t \in \mathcal{T}$ we have $\mathfrak t\notin\mathscr{C}_i$, there is some $R_\mathfrak{t}\in\operatorname{Inv}(\mathscr{C}_i)$ such that $R_\mathfrak t$ is not preserved by $\mathfrak t$. We take $\mathfrak G \coloneqq (\mathbb Q;<,(R_{\mathfrak t})_{\mathfrak t\in\mathcal T})$. This $\mathfrak G$ is a temporal structure with finite signature and no polymorphism from $\mathcal T$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:temporal_descriptive}, it pp-constructs $\mathfrak X$ or $(\{0,1\};\operatorname{1IN3})$, and $\operatorname{CSP}(\mathfrak G)$ is inexpressible in $\mathrm{FPC}$. 
    For every relation $R$ of $\mathfrak G$, the relation $R^{(i)} \coloneqq \{((i,a_1),\ldots,(i,a_r))\mid(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\in R\}$ is preserved by all polymorphisms of $\mathfrak B$ and hence is pp-definable in $\mathfrak B$. We obtain a pp-construction of $\mathfrak G$ in $\mathfrak B$. Proposition~\ref{prop:pp-construction_transfer} implies that $\operatorname{CSP}(\mathfrak B)$ is inexpressible in $\mathrm{FPC}$. By transitivity of pp-constructions, $\mathfrak B$ pp-constructs either $\mathfrak X$ or $(\{0,1\};\operatorname{1IN3})$.
    In the former case, we are done.
    In the latter case, by \cite[Theorem~3.2.2]{Book}, it also constructs a finite affine structure.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     

    Assume now that $\mathfrak B$ does not satisfy condition~(\ref{cond:colour}), i.e., there are no $g_1,g_2\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathfrak B)$ such that $\widetilde g_1, \widetilde g_2$ are 3-4 weak near-unanimity operations. We write $\mathscr{C}=\{\widetilde f\mid f\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathfrak B)\}$. Since $\sim$ is a relation of $\mathfrak B$ and thus taking induced operations commutes with composition, $\mathscr C$ is a clone over $[k]$ which does not contain 3-4 weak near-unanimity operations. Write $\mathscr{H}$ for the set of ternary and quaternary operations on $[k]$ which are not in $\mathscr{C}$, i.e., $\mathscr{H}\cap\mathscr{C}=\varnothing$. For each $h\in \mathscr{H}$, choose $R_h\in\operatorname{Inv}(\mathscr{C})$ not preserved by $h$, and take $\mathfrak C \coloneqq ([k]; (R_h)_{h\in\mathscr{H}})$. By the choice of all $R_h$ from $\operatorname{Inv}(\mathscr{C})$, all ternary and quaternary polymorphisms of $\mathfrak C$ are precisely the $3$- and $4$-ary operations in $\mathscr{C}$. By Theorem 2.8 from \cite{3-4-wnu} (also see~\cite[Theorem 15.62]{GHUA}), we know that $\mathfrak C$ pp-constructs a finite affine structure and, by Theorem 10 of \cite{AtseriasBulatovDawar}, $\operatorname{CSP}(\mathfrak C)$ is inexpressible in $\mathrm{FPC}$. For every relation $R$ of $\mathfrak C$, the relation $\hat R \coloneqq \{(b_1,\dots,b_r)\mid (\pi(b_1),\dots,\pi(b_r))\in R \}$ is preserved by all polymorphisms of $\mathfrak B$, and so has a pp-definition. This yields a pp-construction of $\mathfrak C$ in $\mathfrak B$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:pp-construction_transfer} again gives the inexpressibility of $\operatorname{CSP}(\mathfrak B)$ in $\mathrm{FPC}$, and by transitivity of pp-constructions, also $\mathfrak B$ pp-constructs a finite affine structure.

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We prove the equivalence of the four items by showing $(4)\to (3) \to (1) \to (2) \to (4)$.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:fp-upper}, if $\mathfrak B$ is finite-on-top-of-temporal satisfying the conditions of (4), then $\operatorname{CSP}(\mathfrak B)$ is definable in $\mathrm{FP}^{*}$, which is item (3). By definition, $\mathrm{FP}^{*} \subseteq \mathrm{FPC}$, so (3) implies (1). The implication (1) to (2) follows by contraposition and transitivity of pp-constructions, since neither $\mathfrak X$ nor any finite affine structure have a CSP expressible in $\mathrm{FPC}$ (\cite{RydvalDescr}, \cite{AtseriasBulatovDawar}), and the implication from (2) to (4) follow by contraposition of Proposition~\ref{prop:fp-inexpr}.
