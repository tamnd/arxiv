<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
(see \cite{AS})  \label{thm:1.1}
 For $r \geq 1,$ and $v = (a_0, a_1, . . . , a_r), w = (b_0, b_1, . . . , b_r) \in M_{1,r+1}(R)$, the Suslin matrix $S_r(v, w)$ has the following property: 
 $$S_r(v,w)S_r(w,v)^T=\langle v,w\rangle I_{2^r}=S_r(w,v)^T S_r(v,w).$$
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:1.2}
                                                                                        
 $\mathcal{S}_r$ is symmetric for all $r$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We prove this result by induction on $r$. For $r=1$, $\mathcal{S}_1=\begin{pmatrix}
   1 & 1\\
  1 & 1                                                                                                                                                                                                                                \end{pmatrix},$ which is clearly a symmetric matrix.
  Further we assume that the result is true for $r=k-1$, i.e., $\mathcal{S}_{k-1}^T=\mathcal{S}_{k-1}$ and we prove the result for $r=k$. By definition,
  \begin{align*}
   \mathcal{S}_k^T &=                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   \begin{pmatrix}
   I_{2^{k-1}} & \mathcal{S}_{k-1}\\
  \mathcal{S}_{k-1} & I_{2^{k-1}}                                                                                                                                                                                                                          \end{pmatrix}^T 
  =                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   \begin{pmatrix}
   I_{2^{k-1}} & \mathcal{S}_{k-1}^T\\
  \mathcal{S}_{k-1}^T & I_{2^{k-1}}                                                                                                                                                                                                                          \end{pmatrix}
  =                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   \begin{pmatrix}
   I_{2^{k-1}} & \mathcal{S}_{k-1}\\
  \mathcal{S}_{k-1} & I_{2^{k-1}}                                                                                                                                                                                                                          \end{pmatrix} 
  =\mathcal{S}_k.
  \end{align*}
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
$\mathcal{S}_r$ is nilpotent of index $2$ whenever $r$ is odd and it is an involution whenever $r$ is even.
                                                                                                                                                                      
                                                                                                               
\label{thm:1.4}
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By construction, each row and each column of $\mathcal{S}_r$ has $r+1$ ones, which implies $\mathcal{S}_r\neq {\bf{0}}.$ 
 By Theorem $\ref{thm:1.1}$, $\mathcal{S}_r\mathcal{S}_r^T=\langle {\bf{1}},{\bf{1}}\rangle I_{2^r}$. Using the fact that $\mathcal{S}_r$ is symmetric from Lemma \ref{lem:1.2}, we write, $\mathcal{S}_r\mathcal{S}_r=\langle {\bf{1}},{\bf{1}}\rangle I_{2^r}$
  i.e., $\mathcal{S}_r^2=\langle {\bf{1}},{\bf{1}}\rangle I_{2^r}.$ 
 If $r$ is odd, then $\langle {\bf{1}},{\bf{1}}\rangle=0$ and if $r$ is even, then $\langle {\bf{1}},{\bf{1}}\rangle=1.$ Hence,
 $$\mathcal{S}^2_r =\begin{cases} \mbox{ }0, & \mbox{if }r \mbox{ } is~odd,  \\ \mbox{ }1, & \mbox{if } r \mbox{ }is~even.  
         \end{cases}
$$

It follows that $\mathcal{S}_r$ is nilpotent of index $2$ when $r$ is odd and $\mathcal{S}_r$ is an involution matrix whenever $r$ is even.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
\label{rem2}
 As $\mathcal{S}_r^2=I_{2^r}$ whenever $r$ is even it follows that any two distinct rows of $\mathcal{S}_r$ are orthogonal (i.e., their dot product is zero) over $\mathbb{F}_2$.
<!-- end rem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:1.3}
 For $r$ even, if $R_j$ is the $j$-th row of $\mathcal{S}_r$ and $e_j$ is the $j$-th standard basis vector in $\mathbb{F}_2^{\,r+1}$ then we have : $$\displaystyle\sum_{i \in \operatorname{supp}(R_j)} R_i = e_j.$$
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $x \in \mathbb{F}_2^{\,r+1}$ be such that
$
\displaystyle\sum_{i \in \operatorname{supp}(R_j)} R_i = x.
$
Then the $k^{th}$ coordinate of $x$ is
$\displaystyle
x_k = \sum_{i \in \operatorname{supp}(R_j)} (R_i)_k,
$ where $(R_i)_k$ denotes the $k^{th}$ coordinate of row $R_i.$ 
Since $\mathcal{S}_r$ is symmetric for all $r$ (see Lemma \ref{lem:1.2}), we have
$
(R_i)_k = (R_k)_i.
$ Now we make the following cases.\\
\noindent{Case 1:} $k = j$.

\noindent It follows that 
$
\displaystyle x_j = \sum_{i \in \operatorname{supp}(R_j)} (R_j)_i.
$
Since $(R_j)_i = 1,$ exactly for $i \in \operatorname{supp}(R_j)$. As each row of 
$\mathcal{S}_r$ has weight $r+1$ and $r$ is even we get
$\displaystyle
x_j = \sum_{i \in \operatorname{supp}(R_j)} (R_j)_i 
= r+1 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}.
$ As $x_j\in \mathbb{F}_2,~x_j=1.$

\noindent {Case 2:} $k \neq j$.

When $k \neq j$, only those indices $i$ such that 
$
i \in \operatorname{supp}(R_j) 
 ~\text{and}~
k \in \operatorname{supp}(R_i)
$ contribute in a nontrivial manner to the sum $\displaystyle
x_k = \sum_{i \in \operatorname{supp}(R_j)} (R_i)_k
$.
Symmetry of $\mathcal{S}_r$ implies that
$
k \in \operatorname{supp}(R_i)
~ \text{if and only if} ~
i \in \operatorname{supp}(R_k).
$ 
Therefore, $x_k = \left| \operatorname{supp}(R_j) \cap \operatorname{supp}(R_k) \right|.$
From Theorem \ref{thm:1.4} and Remark \ref{rem2}, distinct rows of $\mathcal{S}_r$ have even dot product for even $r$. Therefore, $\left| \operatorname{supp}(R_j) \cap \operatorname{supp}(R_k) \right|$ is even.  Hence, $x_k \equiv 0 \pmod{2},$ so $x_k = 0$ as $x_k\in \mathbb{F}_2.$ 

Thus, $x = e_j.$ This completes the proof.

<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:1.3}
Let $\mathcal{S}_r=\begin{pmatrix}
A\\B                                                                                \end{pmatrix},$                                                                               
where $A$ and $B$ are block sub-matrices of $\mathcal{S}_r$ of size $(2^{r-1}\times 2^r)$ each. Then for $r$ odd, each row of block $B$ is a linear combination of exactly $r$ rows of block $A$ and vice versa.
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
From the construction of special particular case of Suslin matrix, $\mathcal{S}_r=\begin{pmatrix}
                  I_{2^{r-1}} & \mathcal{S}_{r-1}\\
                  \mathcal{S}_{r-1} & I_{2^{r-1}}
                 \end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}
A\\B                                                                                \end{pmatrix}$, where $A=\begin{pmatrix}I_{2^{r-1}} & \mathcal{S}_{r-1} \end{pmatrix}$ and $B=\begin{pmatrix} \mathcal{S}_{r-1} & I_{2^{r-1}} \end{pmatrix}$.


For $1\leq i \leq 2^{r-1}$, the $i^{th}$ row vector of $A$ is of the type $R'_i=(e_i,R_i)$ and that of $B$ is of the type $R''_i=(R_i,e_i)$, where $e_i$ is $i^{th}$ row of the identity matrix $I_{2^{r-1}}$ and $R_i$ is the $i^{th}$ row of $\mathcal{S}_{r-1}.$ 



                                                                                                           
                                      
For each $j$, $1\leq j \leq 2^{r-1}$, the $j^{th}$ row of $\mathcal{S}_{r-1}$ can be written as $\displaystyle R_j=\sum_{i\in Supp(R_j)} e_i$. From Lemma \ref{lem:1.3}, if $r$ is odd then $j^{th}$ row of $I_{2^{r-1}}$ can be written as  $\displaystyle e_j=\sum_{i\in Supp(R_j)} R_i.$ Therefore, $j^{th}$ row of $B,$  $\displaystyle R''_j=(R_j,e_j)=\left (\sum_{i\in Supp(R_j)} e_i~,~ \sum_{i\in Supp(R_j)} R_i \right ).$ This implies that the $j^{th}$ row of $B$ is a sum of as many rows of $A$ as the size of $Supp(R_j)$. 
As $\mathcal{S}_{r-1}$ has $r$ ones in each row, size of $Supp(R_j)$ is always $r$ for all $j.$
        
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    Hence, each row of block $B$ is linear combination of exactly $r$ rows of block $A$.
Similarly we can prove that each row of block $A$ is linear combination of exactly $r$ rows of block $B$.
        
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   
   
        
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          
               
                 
                
                
             
              
  
                                                                                                                                                               This completes the proof.

<!-- thm-4: body_md -->
(Minimum Hamming distance of the Suslin-Rao Code)\label{thm:2.1}\\
 The minimum Hamming distance of Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ is: \\                                                       
 $$d_{min}(\mathcal{SRC}_r) =\begin{cases} \mbox{ }r+1, & \mbox{if }r \mbox{ } odd,  \\ \mbox{ }r & \mbox{if } r \mbox{ }even.  
         \end{cases}
$$
<!-- end thm-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We prove this result in two parts. In first part, we prove $d_{min}(\mathcal{SRC}_r)=r+1$ for odd $r$ and in second part we prove $d_{min}(\mathcal{SRC}_r)=r$ for even $r.$  
         
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  
 
 \noindent{Case I:} $r$ is odd.\\
 We prove the result by induction.  When $r=1$, the generator matrix for $\mathcal{SRC}_1$ is $G_1=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$ Codewords generated from $G_1$ are $\{11,00\}.$ Clearly the minimum Hamming distance of $\mathcal{SRC}_1$ is $d_{min}(\mathcal{SRC}_1)=2$. Thus, the result is true for $r=1.$ We now assume that the result is true for an odd integer $r=k>1$ i.e., $d_{min}(\mathcal{SRC}_k)=k+1$ and we prove $d_{min}(\mathcal{SRC}_{k+2})=k+3.$
 
 
         
                                                                                                                  
                       
                              
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    

                                                                                                                                                    

                                                 
                                                                                                                                                                                                                  

                                                               

                          

  
                         
                        
                     
             
                       
                                    
  
   

From the construction of Suslin-Rao code, generator matrix of $\mathcal{SRC}_{k+2}$ is given by $G_{k+2}= A_{k+2} = [I_{2^{k+1}} \mid \mathcal{S}_{k+1}].$ Therefore, codewords of $\mathcal{SRC}_{k+2}$ are of the form,  
                                                          
$c =xG_{k+2}=x[I_{2^{k+1}} \mid \mathcal{S}_{k+1}]=(x,\ x \mathcal{S}_{k+1}) ~\text{for } x \in \mathbb{F}_2^{2^{k+1}},\ x \neq 0.
$

Let us fix some nonzero $x$. Then, $wt(c) = wt(x) + wt(x S_{k+1}).$ We now make cases on $wt(x)$. \\
\noindent {Case 1: } We assume $x=e_i$, for some $i$ i.e., $wt(x) = 1$.\\
Then $x S_{k+1}$ is the $i$-th row of $S_{k+1}$. As each row of $S_{k+1}$ has weight $k + 2$, 
$
wt(c) = 1 + (k + 2) = k + 3.
$ 
So, at least one codeword in $\mathcal{SRC}_{k+2}$ has weight $k + 3$.
                                                                                                                                
        
                                           
  
                                       
                       
                                               
     \\
{Case 2: ${wt}(x) \geq 2.$}\\
                                
                                      
                                                            
        
           
              
                                
                            
                      
               
        
                                   
  
                         
                
             
             
                      
                                                                
  
                                                    
Write $x=(x_1,x_2)\in \mathbb{F}_2^{2^k} \times \mathbb{F}_2^{2^k}.$ Therefore, 
$
x \mathcal{S}_{k+1} = (x_1,\ x_2)
\begin{pmatrix}
I_{2^k} & \mathcal{S}_k \\
\mathcal{S}_k & I_{2^k}
\end{pmatrix}
= \left(x_1 + x_2 \mathcal{S}_k,\ x_1 \mathcal{S}_k + x_2\right)
.$
    
  
                                            
  

                                                                                     
\noindent We now make the following subcases:\\
\noindent{$(a)$} Both $x_1$ and $x_2$ are non-zero i.e., $x_1\neq 0$ and $x_2\neq 0.$
                                                       
We claim that at least one of $x_1 + x_2 \mathcal{S}_k$ and $x_1 \mathcal{S}_k + x_2$ is nonzero. If both are zero i.e., $x_1 + x_2 \mathcal{S}_k=0$ and $x_1 \mathcal{S}_k + x_2=0$ then from first equation we get $x_1=x_2 \mathcal{S}_k$. From the second equation we get $(x_2 \mathcal{S}_k)\mathcal{S}_k+x_2=0$. It follows that, $x_2(\mathcal{S}_k^2+I)=0$. Since $k$ is odd, $\mathcal{S}_k^2=0$. Hence, $x_2=0$, a contradiction. 
Note that, each of the vectors $x_1 + x_2 \mathcal{S}_k$ and $x_1 \mathcal{S}_k + x_2$ is a codeword in $\mathcal{SRC}_k$. As $d_{min}(\mathcal{SRC}_k)=k+1,$ every non-zero codeword in $\mathcal{SRC}_k$ has weight at least $k + 1$. Hence, $
wt(x_1 + x_2 \mathcal{S}_k) \geq k + 1~{or}~wt(x_1 S_k + x_2) \geq k + 1.
$
This implies,
$$
wt(x S_{k+1}) \geq k + 1
\text{ and therefore, }wt(c) \geq 2 + (k + 1) = k + 3.
$$
                                                              

\noindent(b) Exactly one of $x_1$ and $x_2$ is zero. Let us say $x_1=0$ and $x_2\neq 0$.\\
Clearly,  
$
x S_{k+1} = (x_2 S_k,\ x_2) \text{ which implies } wt(x S_{k+1}) = wt(x_2 S_k) + wt(x_2).$
As $x_2 \ne 0$ and $\mathcal{SRC}_k$ has minimum weight $k + 1$, we have
$
wt(x_2 S_k) + wt(x_2) > k + 1.$ This implies 
$
wt(c) = wt(x) + {wt}(x S_{k+1}) > 2 + (k + 1) = k + 3
.$                  
                                                                

Hence, for all $x$ with ${wt}(x) > 1$, we have:
$wt(c) \geq k + 3.$ 
                                            
                                             
        
                                                                                
                                                                                          
             
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      
                                                                                          
                                
   
As $d_{min}=\displaystyle\min_{c\in C} wt(c)$ (see \cite{RR}), it is clear that minimum Hamming distance $d_{min}(\mathcal{SRC}_{k+2})=k+3$. Hence, $d_{\min}(\mathcal{SRC}_r) = r+1$ for all odd $r.$                                            
\vspace{2mm}

\noindent{Case II: $r$ is even.}\\
                                             
Consider the Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_{r}$ with generator matrix $G_{r}.$ By Construction  
$$G_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}                                                                                                                             I_{2^{r-1}}~|~\mathcal{S}_{r-1}                                                                                                                        \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}
I_{2^{r-1}}~|~\begin{pmatrix}                                                                                                                                  I_{2^{r-2}} & \mathcal{S}_{r-2}\\                                                                                                                        \mathcal{S}_{r-2} & I_{2^{r-2}}                                                                                                                                  \end{pmatrix}
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}
I_{2^{r-1}} ~|~ \begin{pmatrix}                                                                                                                                  A\\                                                                                                                        B                                                                                                                                  \end{pmatrix}
\end{pmatrix}.$$
The block submatrix $A=\begin{pmatrix} I_{2^{r-2}} & \mathcal{S}_{r-2} \end{pmatrix}$ of $\mathcal{S}_{r-1}$ is a generator $G_{r-1}$ of Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_{r-1}$.
                                                                                                                                                                                                 
As $r-1$ is odd, $d_{min}(\mathcal{SRC}_{r-1})=r$ and from Theorem \ref{thm:1.3}, every row vector of block submatrix $B$ is a linear combination of rows of $A=G_{r-1}$.  Thus, codewords generated from  $\begin{pmatrix}
I_{2^{r-1}} &|& \begin{pmatrix}                                                                                                                                  A\\                                                                                                                        B                                                                                                                                  \end{pmatrix}
\end{pmatrix}$ can not have minimum Hamming weight less than $r$.                                                                                                                                                            
Now we check whether $\mathcal{SRC}_{r}$ has a codeword of Hamming weight equal to $r$.


        
                                                                                                                                                
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      
                                                                                                                                                                                  
   
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          
From Theorem \ref{thm:1.3} we know, each row of $B$ is linear combination of $r-1$ rows of $A.$ Lets say row vectors $\{R_{A,1},\dots,R_{A,r-1}\}$ of matrix $A$ generates row vector $R_{B,i}$ of matrix $B$. Addition of these rows together contributes a zero vector. When we add corresponding rows of the matrix $G_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}                                                                                                                                  I_{2^{r-1}} &|& \mathcal{S}_{r-1}                                                                                                                        \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}
I_{2^{r-1}} &|& \begin{pmatrix}                                                                                                                                  A\\                                                                                                                        B                                                                                                                                  \end{pmatrix}
\end{pmatrix},$ i.e., row vectors $\{G_1,\dots,G_{r-1}\}$ and $G_i$ we get a row vector of weight exactly $r$ ($r$ rows of first block of $G_{r}$ i.e., identity matrix contributes $r$ ones and second block contributes zero in this weight). This is the codeword in $\mathcal{SRC}_{r}$ with weight $r$. Therefore, $d_{min}(\mathcal{SRC}_{r})=\displaystyle\min_{c\in \mathcal{SRC}_r} wt(c)=r.$                                             
This completes the calculation of minimum distance of $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ for $r$ even.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
For $r$ odd, the Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ is self-dual.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
From the construction of Suslin-Rao codes discussed in Section \ref{sec:construction}, generator matrix of the code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ is $G_r=A=\begin{pmatrix} I_{2^{r-1}} & S_{r-1}\end{pmatrix}$  and the corresponding parity check matrix is $H_r=D^T=\begin{pmatrix}S_{r-1} & I_{2^{r-1}}                  \end{pmatrix}$.  $G_r$ and $H_r$ are both $2^{r-1}\times 2^r$ matrices with full row rank. Therefore, $dim(G_r)=dim(H_r)=2^{r-1}.$ This is equal to $\frac{n}{2}$ as, $n=2^r.$  

From (\cite{Huff}) and (\cite{FN}), a linear code $C$ is self-orthogonal if and only if $GG^T=0$, where $G$ is generator matrix of $C$. From Theorem \ref{thm:1.1} and Theorem \ref{thm:1.4}, for odd $r$, $\mathcal{S}_r^2=0$. It follows that, $AA^T=G_rG_r^T=0$. Therefore, all codewords in $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ are  orthogonal to each other.  

                                                                                                                      

Now we prove that the sets of codewords spanned by $G_r$ and $H_r$ are equal. For that we look into the recursive construction of special particular case of Suslin matrices, \\
$\mathcal{S}_r=\begin{pmatrix}A\\B \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}
I_{2^{r-1}} & \mathcal{S}_{r-1}\\
\mathcal{S}_{r-1} & I_{2^{r-1}}
\end{pmatrix}$ and 
$\mathcal{S}_r^T=\begin{pmatrix}
C & D                                                                                \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}
I_{2^{r-1}} & \mathcal{S}_{r-1}\\
\mathcal{S}_{r-1} & I_{2^{r-1}}
\end{pmatrix}.$\\
Clearly,                                                                                                        
the check matrix $H_r=D^T$, of code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ is equal to block submatrix $B$ of $\mathcal{S}_r$.
By Theorem \ref{thm:1.3}, each row of matrix $H_r$ is a linear combination of rows of $G_r$ and vice versa whenever $r$ is odd. Hence both matrices spans the same set of codewords. Thus, $\langle G_r \rangle=\langle H_r\rangle$. This proves that for odd $r,$ Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ is a self-dual code.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
The Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ satisfy the Singleton bound.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
It is well known that any linear code with parameters $[n,k,d]$ satisfy the Singleton bound $d \leq n-k+1$ (see \cite{RR}).
 It gives upper bound on minimum distance $d$.
                                                                      
 We claim that, the minimum distance $d$ of Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ satisfy this bound. 
 
 By construction, Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ has $n=2^r$, $k=2^{r-1}$ and $d=r$ if $r$ is even and $d=r+1$ if $r$ is odd. Therefore, $n-k+1=2^r-2^{r-1}+1=
  2^{r-1}+1.$ It is easy to see that, in both cases i.e., for odd and even $r,$  $d\leq 2^{r-1}+1.$  Therefore, $d\leq n-k+1$ is always true for Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ for all $r$. 
This proves that Suslin-Rao code satisfy the Singleton bound.

<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
The Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ satisfy the Griesmer bound.
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
It is well known from (\cite{Griesmer}) that, every $[n,k,d]$ linear code satisfy Griesmer bound i.e., $\displaystyle n\geq \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \lceil\frac{d}{2^i}\rceil.$ We claim that, the length of the Suslin-Rao code also satisfy this bound. 
  By construction, Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ has $n=2^r$, $k=2^{r-1}$ and $d=r$ if $r$ is even and $d=r+1$ if $r$ is odd. 
Therefore, when $r$ is even, 
\begin{align*}
 \displaystyle \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \lceil\frac{d}{2^i}\rceil &= \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \lceil\frac{r}{2^i}\rceil
 = \lceil\frac{r}{1}\rceil+\lceil\frac{r}{2^1}\rceil+\dots+\lceil\frac{r}{2^{k-1}}\rceil. 
\end{align*}

Here, if $2^i\leq r,$ then $\lceil\frac{r}{2^i}\rceil\leq \frac{r}{2^i}+1$ and if $2^i> r,$ then $\lceil\frac{r}{2^i}\rceil=1$. Let us assume $t$ is the largest integer power index such that $2^t\leq r$ i.e., $t\leq \lfloor log_2(r)\rfloor$ and then we split the right-hand sum over at $t$. 
Therefore,  
\begin{align*}
 \displaystyle \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \lceil\frac{d}{2^i}\rceil 
 &=\sum_{i=0}^{t} \lceil\frac{r}{2^i}\rceil+\sum_{i=t+1}^{k-1} \lceil\frac{r}{2^i}\rceil
 =\sum_{i=0}^{t} \lceil\frac{r}{2^i}\rceil+\sum_{i=t+1}^{k-1} 1\\
 &\leq \left(r+\frac{r}{2}+\dots+\frac{r}{2^t}+(t+1) \right )+(k-1-t)\\
 &\leq r\left(1+\frac{1}{2}+\dots+\frac{1}{2^t} \right )+(t+1)+(k-1-t)\\
 &< 2r+k=2r+2^{r-1}.
 \end{align*}
Clearly, for all even $r>2$, $\displaystyle n=2^r> 2r+2^{r-1}\geq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \lceil\frac{d}{2^i}\rceil$. 
For $r=2$, the parameters of $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ are given by $n = 2^2 = 4$, $k = 2^{2-1} = 2$, and $d = 2$. Evaluating the exact Griesmer sum yields:
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \left\lceil \frac{d}{2^i} \right\rceil &= \sum_{i=0}^{1} \left\lceil \frac{2}{2^i} \right\rceil
= \left\lceil \frac{2}{1} \right\rceil + \left\lceil \frac{2}{2} \right\rceil= 2 + 1 = 3.
\end{align*}
Since $n = 4 > 3$, the Griesmer bound holds true for $r=2$.
Using similar arguments, when $r$ is odd we obtain $\displaystyle n>2(r+1)+2^{r-1}\geq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \lceil\frac{d}{2^i}\rceil$ for all $r>3.$ 
One can easily check Griesmer bound holds, by evaluating the exact Griesmer sum for cases $r=1$ and $3$. 
Hence, the Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ satisfy the Griesmer bound.

<!-- prop-4: body_md -->
The Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ satisfy the sphere packing bound.
<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
From (\cite{RR}), sphere packing bound for $[n,k,d]$ linear code over $\mathbb{F}_2$ is given by $\displaystyle 2^k \sum_{i=0}^t {n \choose i}\leq 2^n$, where $t=\lfloor \frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor.$ 
 First we find $t$, if $d=r$ then $t=\lfloor \frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor$ and if $d=r+1$ then $t=\lfloor \frac{r}{2}\rfloor.$ In both cases $t\leq \frac{r}{2}$. For Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r,$ $n=2^r$, $k=2^{r-1},$ thus it is enough to prove that $\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^t {2^r \choose i}\leq 2^{2^{r-1}}.$
 As ${n \choose i} \leq n^i,$ for all integers $i>0$, we have 
 \begin{align*}
  \displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^t{2^r \choose i}\leq \sum_{i=0}^t(2^r)^i&=\frac{(2^r)^{t+1}-1}{2^r-1}\\
  &< (2^r)^{t+1}\\
  &\leq{2^r}^{(\frac{r}{2}+1)}\\
  &<2^{2^{r-1}}~\text{for all~}r\geq 6. 
 \end{align*}
The remaining cases i.e., $r=1$ to $5$ can be verified manually. Hence, the Suslin-Rao code $\mathcal{SRC}_r$ satisfy the sphere packing bound.

<!-- prop-5: body_md -->
\label{pro:5}
 If $C$ is a binary linear  code of length $n$ and contains the all one vector as a codeword, then the weight distribution of $C$ is symmetric i.e., $A_w=A_{n-w}$ for all $0\leq w\leq n$.
<!-- end prop-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Define mapping $\phi$ from $C$ to $C$ as $\phi(c)=c+{\bf 1}.$ As ${\bf 1}\in C$ and $C$ is linear, $\phi$ is a bijection from $C$ to itself. Moreover, $wt(\phi(c))=n-wt(c).$ Thus, $\phi$ induces a bijection between codewords of weight $w$ and those of weight $n-w$. Hence, $A_w=A_{n-w}$ for all $0\leq w\leq n$.
