<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{theointro:main theorem}
 Let $P\subset M_\mathbb{R}$ be a lattice polytope with facet presentation \eqref{eq:facet presentation}. Assume that the following subspace concentration inequalities hold: for any subspace $E\subset N_\mathbb{R}$,
 \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:SCC for lattice polytopes}
   \sum_{u_\rho\in E} \: \mathrm{Vol}_M(F^\rho) \leq \frac{\dim(E)}{n} \: \sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)} \: \mathrm{Vol}_M(F^\rho).
 \end{equation}
Then,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:BG for lattice polytopes}
 \sum_{\tau\in\Sigma(2)}\mathcal{A}_\tau \: \mathrm{Vol}_M(F^\tau) \geq 0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{theointro:Fano case}
 Let $P\subset M_\mathbb{R}$ be a smooth reflexive lattice polytope with facets $(F^\rho)_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}$ and primitive inward pointing facet normals $(u_\rho)_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}$. Assume that the following subspace concentration inequalities hold: for any subspace $E\subset N_\mathbb{R}$,
 \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:SCC for reflexive polytopes}
   \sum_{u_\rho\in E} \: \mathrm{Vol}_M(F^\rho) \leq \dim(E) \:  \mathrm{Vol}_M(P).
 \end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:BG for reflexive polytopes}
 \sum_{\tau\in\Sigma(2)}\, \mathrm{Vol}_M(F^\tau) \geq \frac{(n-1)^2}{2}\,\mathrm{Vol}_M(P).
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:intersection numbers}
 Let $\rho\in\Sigma'(1)$ and $\tau'\in\Sigma'(2)$. Then the following holds.
 \begin{enumerate}
  \item Either $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$ and then
  \[
   D_\rho\cdot (D')^{n-1}=(n-1)!\,\mathrm{Vol}_M(F^\rho),
  \]
or $\rho\notin\Sigma(1)$ and then
\[
D_\rho\cdot (D')^{n-1}=0.
\]
\item Either there exists $\tau\in\Sigma(2)$ with $\tau'\subset \tau$ and then
\[
   [V(\tau')]\cdot (D')^{n-2}=(n-2)!\,\mathrm{Vol}_M(F^\tau),
  \]
  or
  \[
[V(\tau')]\cdot (D')^{n-2}=0.
   \]
 \end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The results are certainly well known, but we recall the proof for the sake of completeness. By the push--pull formula, we have
 \[
   D_\rho\cdot (D')^{n-1}=f_*D_\rho\cdot D^{n-1}
 \]
and
  \[
    [V(\tau')]\cdot (D')^{n-2}=f_* [V(\tau')]\cdot D^{n-2}.
 \]
 Using the properties of pushforward of cycles (see the discussion at the end of \cite[Section 5.1]{Ful93}), we find that either $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$ and then $f_*D_\rho=D_\rho\subset X$, or $f_* D_\rho=0$. Similarly for $\tau'$, either there exists $\tau\in\Sigma(2)$ with $\tau'\subset \tau$ and then $f_*[V(\tau')]=[V(\tau)]$ or $f_*[V(\tau')]=0$. To conclude, we use the fact that from \cite[Chapter 5]{Ful93}, the intersection theory on $X$ yields
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:intersection D rho}
\mathrm{Vol}_M(F^\rho)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\, D_\rho\cdot D^{n-1}
\end{equation}
and for $\tau\in\Sigma(2)$,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:intersection D tau}
\mathrm{Vol}_M(F^\tau)= \frac{1}{(n-2)!}\, [V(\tau)]\cdot D^{n-2}.
\end{equation}
Note that for Equations \eqref{eq:intersection D rho} and \eqref{eq:intersection D tau}, the arguments from \cite[Section 5.3]{Ful93} extend to the normal setting.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:equiv semistable}
 The tangent sheaf $\mathcal{T}_{X'}$ is slope semistable with respect to $L$ if and only if the SCI \eqref{eq:SCC for lattice polytopes} holds for any subspace $E\subset N_\mathbb{R}$, where $P\subset M_\mathbb{R}$ is the lattice polytope associated to $(X,D)$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By Proposition \ref{prop:intersection numbers}, equation \eqref{eq:intersection D rho} and equation \eqref{eq:total chern class},
 \begin{equation*}
  \begin{array}{ccc}
  \displaystyle  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\,\mathrm{Vol}_M(F^\rho) &  = &\displaystyle \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\,  D_\rho\cdot D^{n-1}\\
  &  = &\displaystyle \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma'(1)}\,  D_\rho\cdot D^{n-1}\\
     & = &\displaystyle  \frac{1}{n!}\,c_1(\mathcal{T}_{X'})\cdot D^{n-1}\\
  & = & \displaystyle\frac{\mu_L(\mathcal{T}_{X'})}{(n-1)!}.
  \end{array}
 \end{equation*}
On the over hand, by Klyachko's work \cite{Kly90}, as the tangeant bundle $\mathcal{T}_{X'}$ is torus equivariant, it corresponds to a family of filtrations $(T^\rho(j))_{j\in\mathbb{Z}, \rho\in\Sigma'(1)}$ for the vector space $N_\mathbb{C}=N\otimes_\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{C}$. From Perling \cite{Per04}, any saturated coherent equivariant subsheaf $\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{T}_{X'}$ corresponds to a subfamily of filtrations of the form $(E\cap T^\rho(j))_{j\in\mathbb{Z}, \rho\in\Sigma(1)}$, for some vector subspace $E\subset N_\mathbb{C}$ (see e.g. \cite[Lemma 2.15]{NaTip} for the saturation property). Then, from \cite[Section 2]{HNS19}, the slope of $\mathcal{E}$ with $0<\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})$ is precisely given by
\[
 \mu_L(\mathcal{E})=\frac{1}{\dim(E)}\sum_{\substack{\rho\in\Sigma'(1)\\ u_\rho\in E} } \: D_\rho\cdot L^{n-1}.
\]
By Proposition \ref{prop:intersection numbers} again, this equals
\[
 \mu_L(\mathcal{E})=\frac{(n-1)!}{\dim(E)}\sum_{ u_\rho\in E } \:\mathrm{Vol}_M(F^\rho).
\]
Hence, the SCI \eqref{eq:SCC for lattice polytopes} are satisfied precisely when $\mathcal{T}_{X'}$ is slope semistable with respect to \textit{equivariant} subsheaves. This in turn implies semistability \cite{Koo11}. Indeed, as $D$ is nef, there exists by \cite[Section 2.6]{GrebKebPet} a unique Jordan--H\"older filtration for $\mathcal{T}_{X'}$, that is a filtration by coherent subsheaves
\[
 0=\mathcal{E}_0\subsetneq \mathcal{E}_1\subsetneq \mathcal{E}_1\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq \mathcal{E}_d=\mathcal{T}_{X'}
\]
where each quotient $\mathcal{Q}_i=\mathcal{E}_{i}/\mathcal{E}_{i-1}$ is torsion--free, slope semistable, and where the sequence of slopes $\mu_D(\mathcal{Q}_i)$ is strictly decreasing. From uniqueness, we deduce that it is equivariant under the torus action. Hence,  semistability, which is characterized by having a single term $\mathcal{E}_1=\mathcal{T}_{X'}$ in the Jordan--H\"older filtration, is implied by its equivariant version. This concludes the proof.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{theo:BG}
 Let $\mathcal{E}$ be a torsion-free slope semistable sheaf on $(X',D')$. Then
 \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:BG inequality}
  \Delta(\mathcal{E})\cdot (D')^{n-2}\geq 0,
 \end{equation}
where $\Delta(\mathcal{E})=2\,\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})\,c_2(\mathcal{E})-(\mathrm{rank}(E)-1)\,c_1^2(\mathcal{E})$ is the discriminant of $\mathcal{E}$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:2 cones}
  The resolution $f : X'\to X$ may be chosen such that for any $\tau\in\Sigma(2)$,  $\tau$ is exactly the union of $\mathrm{mult}(\tau)$ cones in $\Sigma'(2)$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Note that the resolution $f : X' \to X$ is obtained by successive star subdivisions, see the proofs of \cite[Theorem 11.1.9]{CLS} and \cite[Proposition 11.1.7]{CLS}. The first subdivisions, \cite[Proposition 11.1.7]{CLS}, provide a simplicial model for $X$. By construction, see \cite[Proposition 11.1.6]{CLS}, the star subdivisions involved in the simplicialization may add $2$-dimensional cones to $\Sigma$, but will not subdivide the elements in $\Sigma(2)$. Then, the smooth model is obtained by successive subdivisions of cones $\sigma$ with $\mathrm{mult}(\sigma)>1$, cf proof of  \cite[Theorem 11.1.9]{CLS}. This process can be done by decreasing induction on $\dim(\sigma)$ by \cite[Proposition 11.1.8(d)]{CLS}. When $\dim(\sigma) >2$, the star subdivision involved will not affect the elements in $\Sigma(2)$. Hence, only the end of the process, where $\tau\in\Sigma(2)$ has $\mathrm{mult}(\tau)>1$, will modify the cones in $\Sigma(2)$. In that case, by the proof of \cite[Theorem 11.1.9]{CLS}, and \cite[Proposition 11.1.8(b)]{CLS}, we see that $\tau$ is subdivided in $\mathrm{mult}(\tau)$ cones, which concludes the proof of the lemma.
