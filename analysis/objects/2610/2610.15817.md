<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
The category of $G$-objects in $\mathcal{C}$, denoted by $G$-$\mathcal{C}$, the category of functors.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
Given any subgroup $H$ of $G$, define the $H$-fixed point functor, denoted by $(-)^H$, as the composite 
    $$G\text{-}\mathcal{C} \xrightarrow[]{\operatorname{restrict}} H\text{-}\mathcal{C}  \xrightarrow[]{\operatorname{lim}}  \mathcal{C} .$$
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{cellular}
    The $H$-fixed point functors $(-)^H$ are said to be cellular, or satisfy the cellularity conditions, if for any subgroup $H$ of $G$ the follow holds: 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item the functor $(-)^H$ preserves pushouts of diagrams where one arrow is of the form 
    $$id_{G/L} \otimes f : G/L \otimes X \rightarrow G/L \otimes Y$$
    for some subgroup $L$ of $G$ and $f$ a generating cofibration of $\mathcal{C}$
    \item the functor $(-)^H$ preserves filtered colimits of diagrams in $G$-$\mathcal{C}$
    \item for any subgroup $L$ of $G$ and object $X$ in $\mathcal{C}$, the induced map 
    $$(G/H)^L \otimes X \rightarrow (G/H \otimes X)^L$$
    is an isomorphism in $\mathcal{C}$
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{t2.1}(Thereom 3.17 in \cite{stephan},  Theorem 1.8, Chapter 3 in \cite{MM02})
    If $\mathcal{C}$ is a cofibrantly generated model category, with a generating set of cofibrations denoted $I$ and a generating set of acyclic cofibrations dentored $J$, such that $G$-$\mathcal{C}$ has cellular fixed point functors, then $G$-$\mathcal{C}$ is a cofibrantly generated model category with generating cofibrations
    $$I_G = \{G/H \otimes i \}_{i \in I, H \leq G}, $$
    generating acyclic cofibrations
    $$J_G = \{G/H \otimes j\}_{j \in J, H \leq G},$$ 
    and weak equivalences are maps of $G$-objects which are weak equivalences in $\mathcal{C}$ after passage to $H$ fixed points for all $H \leq G$.

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We begin by showing the third cellularity condition.
Given a subgroup \(L \leq G\) and \(A \in \mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a\) we want to show that the induced map 
\[\left(\coprod_{({G/L})^H} A \right) \rightarrow  \left(\coprod_{G/L} A\right)^H, \]
is an isomorphism.
Note that the coproduct in \(\mathsf{dgAlg}_R^a\) is the usual tensor product of algebras. 
So the action of \(H\) on \(\otimes_{G/L} A\) is given by permuting the copies of \(A\), since each \(A\) itself has trivial \(G\)-action.
Therefore, this action is determined precisely by the action of \(H\) on \(G/L\). 
The desired isomorphism follows.

To prove 1. we use condition 3.
Consider the pushout in \(G\)-\(\mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a\)
\[\begin{tikzcd}
 \otimes_{G/L} R  \arrow[d, "\otimes_{G/L} i"'] \arrow[rr, "f"] &  & A \arrow[d] \\
\otimes_{G/L} T_n(x)\arrow[rr]  &  & D,
\end{tikzcd}\]
where \(D = A \otimes \otimes_{G/L} T_n(x)\) 
and \(i\) is a generating cofibration as described earlier. 
Note that \(D\) is given by concatenating \(G/L\)-many copies tensor copies of \(T_n(x)\) to \(A\). 
This can be thought of a equivariantly gluing ``\(n\)-disks'' to the \(n\)-th level of \(A\) and their boundaries attached at level \(n-1\) of \(A\).
Now we apply the fixed point functor \((-)^H\) to the upper horizontal arrow and left vertical arrow and then take the pushout, so we get the diagram below 
\[\begin{tikzcd}
 (\otimes_{G/L} R)^H  \arrow[d, "(\otimes_{G/L} i)^H"'] \arrow[rr, "f^H"] &  & A^H \arrow[d] \\
(\otimes_{G/L} T_n(x))^H\arrow[rr]  &  & D^H,
\end{tikzcd}\]
but using condition 3. the diagram becomes 
\[\begin{tikzcd}
 \otimes_{(G/L)^H} R  \arrow[d, "\otimes_{(G/L)^H} i"'] \arrow[rr, "f^H"] &  & A^H \arrow[d] \\
\otimes_{(G/L)^H} T_n(x)\arrow[rr]  &  & A^H \otimes (\otimes_{(G/L)^H} T_n(x)). 
\end{tikzcd}\]
So the resulting pushout is given by concatenating to \(A_n^H\) the appropriate copy of \(T_n(x)\) indexed by \((G/L)^H\). 

If we inspect the \(H\)-fixed points of \(D\) we find that they coincide with pushout above because \(D^H\) is only concerned with the copies of \(T_n(x)\) indexed by \((G/H)^L\) and glues them equivariantly to the \(H\)-fixed points \(A\) at level \(n.\)

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
(Theorem A in \cite{Maru}) \label{thm:maru}
Let $\left(\mathcal{M}, \operatorname{Cof}_{\mathcal{M}}, \operatorname{Fib}_{\mathcal{M}}, \mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{M}}\right)$ be a combinatorial model category, and let $\mathcal{C}$ be a locally presentable category. Suppose that we have an adjunction
$$\begin{tikzcd} 
{\mathcal{C}}  \arrow[rr, "F"', shift left=-2] & & {\mathcal{M}},  \arrow[ll, "F'"',shift right=2 ]
\end{tikzcd}$$
and that the following properties are satisfied:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item  every morphism $f \in F^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Fib}_{\mathcal{M}} \cap \mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{M}}\right)$ has a fibrant replacement $f' \in F^{-1}\left(\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{M}}\right)$,
    \item for every object $X$ such that $FX$ is fibrant, there is a factorization of the diagonal morphism $X \xrightarrow{w} P X \xrightarrow{p} X \times X$ with $w \in F^{-1}\left(\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{M}}\right)$ and $p \in F^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Fib}_{\mathcal{M}}\right)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then, there exists a combinatorial model structure on $\mathcal{C}$ in which an object $X$ is fibrant if and only if $R X$ is fibrant in $\mathcal{M}$, and a morphism $f$ between fibrant objects is a weak equivalence (resp. fibration) if and only if $R f$ is so in $\mathcal{M}$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Condition 1: 
Suppose we have $f: X \rightarrow Y$ in $G$-$\mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a$ satisfying the condition above, then we want to show that 
$$\begin{tikzcd}
    X  \arrow[rr, "f"]  \arrow[d]& & Y\arrow[d]\\
    X'  \arrow[rr, "f'"] && Y',
\end{tikzcd}$$
there exists $f': X' \rightarrow Y'$ such that $(X')^H$ and $(Y')^H$ are fibrant and $(f')^H$ is a quasi-isomorphism. 
Consider the diagram below above when the $(-)^H$ is applied to $f$:
$$\begin{tikzcd}
    X^H  \arrow[rr, "f^H"]  \arrow[d]& & Y^H\arrow[d]\\
    \tilde{X}  \arrow[rr, dashed] && \tilde{Y},
\end{tikzcd}$$
where the vertical arrows are fibrant replacements in $\mathsf{dgAlg}_k$. Note that $f^H$ is assumed to be a trivial fibration.
Our goal is to construct the dashed arrow in such a way that it is a quasi-isomorphism in $\mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a.$
Recall that all objects in $\mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a$ are fibrant in the model structure prescribed so the diagram prior collapses to just the top arrow. 

A potential candidate for $f' $ is $G/H \otimes f^H$ which given the previous discussion translates to $\sqcup f^H$. Unraveling the symbols we find, 
$$\sqcup f^H: \sqcup X^H \rightarrow \sqcup Y^H,$$
so now the goal is to show that $(\sqcup f^H)^H$ is a quasi-isomorphism of dg algebras.
We know that $f^H$ is a quasi-isomorphism since it is a trivial fibration so $(\sqcup f^H)^H$ will also be a trivial fibration, since it is coproduct, indexed by cosets of $H$, of trivial fibrations.
Therefore $f'$ in the inverse image of $\mathcal{W}_{\mathsf{dgAlg}_k}$ under $(-)^H$, and hence the fibrant replacement of $f$.

Condition 2: Given any $X \in G$-$\mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a$, consider the diagram below:
$$\begin{tikzcd} 
{X}  \arrow[dr, "w"', shift left=-2] \arrow[rr, "\text{diagonal}"] & & {X \times X}  \\
& P(X) \arrow[ur, "p"', shift left=-2] 
\end{tikzcd}$$
where,
\begin{enumerate} 
\item our candidate for the path object $P(X)$ is $\operatorname{Hom}_R(C,X)$ where $C$ is the dg coalgebra as follows
$$0 \leftarrow C_0 = R[b,b_e] \leftarrow C_1 = R[e] \leftarrow C_2 =0 \cdots,$$
with the only nonzero differential being $e \mapsto b_e$. 
Notice that by construction this dg coalgebra $C$ is contractible and using the point $b$ we can define an augmentation map from $P(X)$ to the ground ring $R$ as follows: 
$$a: P(X)\rightarrow R,$$
$$p_b \mapsto 1_R,$$
where $p_b$ is the constant path at the point $b$.
The differential for the dg algebra $P(X)$ will be denoted $d$ and is defined as 
$$d \varphi = \varphi \circ \partial_C + \partial_X \circ \varphi,$$
with $\partial_C$ and $\partial_X$ being the differentials of $C$ and $X$, respectively. 
Additionally the multiplication of this dg algebra is given by the convolution, so given $\varphi$ and $\psi$ in $P(X)$ then 
$$\varphi \star \psi := \mu_X (\varphi \otimes \psi) \Delta_C,$$
where $\mu_X$ is the product in $X$ and $\Delta_C$ is the product in $C$.
\item our candidate for the map $w$ is as follows: 
$$w: X \rightarrow P(X),$$
$$x \mapsto w_x: C \rightarrow X,$$
and 
$$e \mapsto 0 ,\quad b_e \mapsto 0 \quad b \mapsto x,$$
\item our candidate for $p$ is an evaluation map as follows
$$p : P(X) \rightarrow X\times X,$$
$$ \varphi \mapsto (\varphi(b_e), \varphi(b)).$$
\end{enumerate}
If $X$ is taken to be a $G$-$\mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a$, then $P(X)$ as defined also inherits a $G$-action defined as 
$$g \cdot \varphi = g \varphi(c),$$
for all $\varphi \in P(X)$, $c\in C$, and $g \in G$.
Additionally $(X \times X, \partial_{X \times X})$, is also a $G$-$\mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a$ with the action performed component-wise

We start by checking we have the necessary structure and properties for the map $p$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $p$ is a $G$-map: 
    Given $\varphi \in P(X)$ and $g \in G$, we have
    $$g\cdot p(\varphi) = g (\varphi(b), \varphi(b_e)) = (g(\varphi(b), g \varphi(b_e)) = p(g \varphi).$$
    \item $p$ is a map of dg algebras: 
    Given $\varphi, \psi \in P(X)$, we have
    \begin{align*}p(\varphi \star \psi) = & p(\mu_X(\varphi \otimes \psi)\Delta_C) \\
    =&  (\mu_X(\varphi \otimes \psi)\Delta_C (b),\mu_X(\varphi \otimes \psi)\Delta_C (b_e)) \\
    = &(\varphi(b) \psi(b) , \varphi(b_e) \psi(b_e)),
    \end{align*}
    while 
       \begin{align*} \mu_{X \times X} (p(\varphi)  p (\psi) ) = & \mu_{X \times X} ((\varphi(b),\varphi(b_e), (\psi(b), \psi(b_e)) 
       =  (\varphi(b) \psi(b), \varphi(b_e) \psi(b_e)).
    \end{align*}
    \item $p$ is a chain map: Given $\varphi \in P(X)$, we have 
    \begin{align*}
        p \circ d \varphi = & (d \varphi (b), d \varphi (b_e)) \\
        = & ( \varphi \partial_C (b) + \partial_X \varphi(b), \varphi \partial_C (b_e) + \partial_X \varphi(b_e))\\
        = & (\partial_x \varphi (b) \partial_X \varphi(b_e))\\
        =& \partial_{X\times X} (\varphi(b), \varphi(b_e))\\
        =& \partial_{X\times X} \cdot p \varphi.
    \end{align*}
    \item $p$ is a surjective map on fixed points: 
    Given $(x,y) \in X\times X$ we can define a map $\theta \in P(X)$ as a map
    $\theta: C \rightarrow X$
    where $b \mapsto a$, $b_e \mapsto b $, and $ e \mapsto 0.$
    Clearly, $\theta$ is a linear map, hence in $P(X)$, and $p(\theta) = (x,y)$, so $p$ is surjective.
    Now suppose $H$ is a subgroup of $G$ and consider $(x,y) \in X^H \times X^H$. 
    Then, for any $h \in H$, we have $hx= x$ and $hy= y$.
    The goal is to show that the associated $\theta$ is in $P(X)^H$:
    \begin{align*}
        h(\theta(b)) & = h(x)= x = \theta(b),\\
        h(\theta(b_e)) & = h(y)= y = \theta(b_e),\\
        h(\theta(e)) & = h(0)= 0 = \theta(e).\\
    \end{align*}
    Hence $h \theta = \theta$.
\end{enumerate}
Now we check we have the necessary structure and properties for the map $w$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $w$ is a $G$-map:
    Given $x \in X$ and $g \in G$, then we have
    $$g \cdot w (x)(e) = g\cdot 0  = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad  w(gx)(e) = 0 , $$
   and similarly for $b_e$.
      $$g \cdot w (x)(b) = g\cdot x  = gx \quad \text{and} \quad  w(gx)(b) = gx $$
    \item $w$ is a map of dg algebras:
     Given $x, y \in X$, then we have the map
     $$w \circ \mu_X(x,y) = w(xy)= w_{xy}.$$
     So for $e \in C$ we have 
     $$w \circ \mu_X(x,y)(e) =  w_{xy}(e) = 0,$$
     on the other hand 
     \begin{align*}
    (w(x) \star w(y)) (e) = &  (w_x \star w_y)(e) \\
    =& \mu_X(w_x \otimes w_y)\Delta_C(e)\\
    =& \mu_X(w_x \otimes w_y)(e \otimes e)\\
    =& \mu_X(w_x \otimes w_y)(e \otimes e)\\
    =& \mu_X(w_x(e) \otimes w_y(e))\\
    =& \mu_X(0 \otimes 0) = 0 .
     \end{align*}
    A similar argument holds for $b_e$. 
    For $b \in C$, we have 
     $$w \circ \mu_X(x,y)(b) =  w_{xy}(b) = xy,$$
     on the other hand,
     \begin{align*}
    (w(x) \star w(y)) (b) = &  (w_x \star w_y)(b) \\
    =& \mu_X(w_x \otimes w_y)\Delta_C(b)\\
    =& \mu_X(w_x \otimes w_y)(b \otimes b)\\
    =& \mu_X(w_x \otimes w_y)(b \otimes b)\\
    =& \mu_X(w_x(b) \otimes w_y(b))\\
    =& \mu_X(x \otimes y) = xy.
     \end{align*}
    Hence $w$ is a map of dg algebras. 
    \item $w$ is a chain map: 
     Given $x \in X$, we have 
     \begin{align*}
     d \circ w(x) (b) = &  w_x \partial_C(b) + \partial_X w_x(b)\\
       =&  w_x (0) + \partial_X (x) = \partial_X (x),
     \end{align*}
     and on the other hand we have 
      \begin{align*}
     w_x \circ \partial_x(x) (b) = & w_{\partial_X (x)}(b)
    = \partial_X (x).
     \end{align*}
    Similarly for $b_e$ we get 
     \begin{align*}
     d \circ w(x) (b_e) = &  w_x \partial_C(b_e) + \partial_X w_x(b_e)\\
       =&  w_x (0) + \partial_X (0) = 0, 
     \end{align*}
     and on the other hand we have 
      \begin{align*}
     w_x \circ \partial_x(x) (b_e) = & w_{\partial_X (x)}(b_e) =  0.
     \end{align*}
     For $e$ we have
       \begin{align*}
     d \circ w(x) (e) = &  w_x \partial_C(e) + \partial_X w_x(e)\\
       =&  w_x (b_e) + \partial_X (0)\\
       =&  w_x(b_e) + 0  = 0 +0 = 0,
     \end{align*}
     and on the other hand we have 
      \begin{align*}
     w_x \circ \partial_x(x) (e) = & w_{\partial_X (x)}(e) =  0.
     \end{align*}
    \item $w$ is a quasi-isomorphism:
    Recall that $C$ is a finite dimensional dg coalgebra which is also contractible, hence the linear dual of $C$, denoted $C^*$, is a finite dimensional dg algebra. 
    Additionally notice that $$P(X) = \operatorname{Hom}_R(C, X) = C^* \otimes X,$$
    and the last dg algebra in this list of equalities is quasi-isomorphic to $X$ via the map $w$.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          

\end{enumerate}

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
We begin by showing that $(G\text{-}\operatorname{Cobar}, G\text{-}\operatorname{Bar})$ form a Quillen adjunction.
    To do this we will show that $G\text{-}\operatorname{Bar}$ preserves fibrations and acyclic fibrations so consider $\varphi$ and $\tilde{\varphi}$ respectively in $G\text{-}\mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a$.
    Namely, we will show that $G\text{-}\operatorname{Bar}(\varphi)$ is a fibration and $G\text{-}\operatorname{Bar}(\tilde{\varphi})$ is an acyclic fibration in $G\text{-}\mathsf{dgCoalg}.$
    By definition of the fixed point model structure on $G\text{-} \mathsf{dgAlg}_k$, $\varphi$ is (acyclic)  fibration if for every subgroup $H$ of $G$ the induced morphism $\varphi^H$ is a (acyclic) fibration in $\mathsf{dgAlg}_k$.
    The functor $\operatorname{Bar}$ is a right Quillen functor, so it preserves (acyclic) fibrations hence $\operatorname{Bar}(\varphi^H)$ is a fibration in $\mathsf{dgCoalg}^c_k$. 
    Lastly, we need to show that $G/H \otimes \operatorname{Bar}(\varphi^H)$ is a (acyclic) fibration in $G\text{-}\mathsf{dgCoalg}_k^c$, and for this to be true we need $(G/H \otimes \operatorname{Bar}(\varphi^H))^H$ to be a (acyclic) fibration in $\mathsf{dgCoalg}_k^c$.
   This is clear since $G/H\otimes-$ induces the trivial action and $\operatorname{Bar}(\varphi^H)$ is a (acyclic) fibration. 
   With the appropriate modifications indicated a similar argument holds for $\tilde{\varphi}$, so we conclude that $(G\text{-}\operatorname{Cobar}, G\text{-}\operatorname{Bar})$ form a Quillen adjunction.

   Next, we show that the derived unit and counit of $(G\text{-}\operatorname{Cobar}, G\text{-}\operatorname{Bar})$ are weak equivalences in the corresponding categories. 
   Let $D \in G\text{-}\mathsf{dgCoalg}_k^c$ be a cofibrant object and consider the unit 
   $$\eta:  G\text{-}\mathsf{dgCoalg}_k^c \xrightarrow[]{G\text{-}\operatorname{Cobar}}   G\text{-}\mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a \xrightarrow[]{G\text{-}\operatorname{Bar}}  G\text{-}\mathsf{dgCoalg}_k^c ,$$
   where on the component of $D$ we have 
   $$\eta_D: D \mapsto (G\text{-}\operatorname{Cobar}(D))^f\mapsto G\text{-}\operatorname{Bar}( (G\text{-}\operatorname{Cobar}(D))^f) ,$$
   and where $(-)^f$ denotes the fibrant replacement. 
   We want to show that $\eta_D$ is a $\operatorname{Cobar}$-quasi-isomorphism on fixed points and to better understand the functors at play we unravel the symbols in the previous line;
      $$\eta_D: D \mapsto (G/e \otimes \operatorname{Cobar}(D^e))^f\mapsto G/e \otimes \operatorname{Bar}( ( (G/e \otimes \operatorname{Cobar}(D^e))^f)^e) .$$
  Recall that all objects are fibrant in $G\text{-}\mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a$. 
                                                                                                                                   
  Having reduced to considering $G/e \otimes \operatorname{Bar}( ( (G/e \otimes \operatorname{Cobar}(D^e)))^e)$, we wish to show when it is $\operatorname{Cobar}$-quasi-isomorphic to $D.$
  Inspecting further we that we are taking fixed points with respect to the trivial action, namely the one induced by $G/e \otimes -,$ hence we can reduce the target object of $\eta_D$ to
   $G/e \otimes \operatorname{Bar}(\operatorname{Cobar}(D^e))$. 
   At this juncture we can utilize the fact that $(\operatorname{Cobar}, \operatorname{Bar})$ form a Quillen equivalence so the unit is well behaved, so we get that the target object becomes $G/e \otimes (D^e)$. 
   It becomes clearer now that $D$ is $\operatorname{Cobar}$-quasi-isomorphic to $G/e \otimes (D^e)$, since  $G/e \otimes -$ induces the trivial action at which point we are comparing the homology of the fixed points of $D$, the homology of the domain of $\eta_D$, with the homology of the fixed points of $D$, the homology of the codomain of $\eta_D$. 

   To show that the counit is a weak equivalence (quasi-isomorphism) in $G\text{-}\mathsf{dgAlg}_k^a$ we can apply the same reasoning as with the counit.
