<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:A}
\hfill
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $X_{\mathrm{ad}}\subset\mathbb{R}^n_+$ is nonempty, compact, and convex.
\item $U_{\mathrm{ad}}\subset U$ is nonempty, closed, convex, and bounded.
\item The state equation is linear and $\mathcal{S} u=y^0+Su$ with $S\in\mathcal{L}(U,Y)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- assumption-2: body_md -->
\label{ass:B}
For each $\ell=1,\dots,n$, $j_\ell:Y\to\mathbb{R}_+$ is convex and twice continuously
Fr\'echet differentiable. For the stability analysis we assume
\[
  M_j:=\sup_{y\in\mathcal{S}(U_{\mathrm{ad}})}\lVert j'(y)\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(Y,\mathbb{R}^n)}<\infty.
\]
A sufficient, often easier-to-check condition is that $j'$ maps bounded subsets of
$Y$ to bounded subsets of $\mathcal{L}(Y,\mathbb{R}^n)$; the analysis below needs only the (weaker)
boundedness of $j'$ on $\mathcal{S}(U_{\mathrm{ad}})$ expressed by $M_j<\infty$.
<!-- end assumption-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
In infinite dimensions, continuity of $j'$ alone does not imply boundedness on
bounded subsets. The bounded-derivative hypothesis is therefore not a technicality;
it is the condition needed to obtain a global Lipschitz estimate for the lower-level
response map on $X_{\mathrm{ad}}$. By contrast, the codomain $\mathbb{R}_+$ (nonnegativity of $j_\ell$)
is included only for the application interpretation and plays no analytical role: the
results below use the convexity of $j_\ell$ together with $x\in\mathbb{R}^n_+$ to obtain
convexity of $x\cdot j$, and the bounded-derivative hypothesis for stability; none of
the proofs invoke $j_\ell\ge0$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- assumption-3: body_md -->
\label{ass:C}
The control regularization parameter satisfies $\sigma>0$.
<!-- end assumption-3 -->
<!-- assumption-4: body_md -->
\label{ass:D}
The upper-level objective is
\[
  F(x,y,u)=\frac12\lVert y-y^{\mathrm{obs}}\rVert_{L^2(0,T;\mathbb{R}^d)}^2
  +\frac{\beta}{2}\lVert u-u^{\mathrm{obs}}\rVert_U^2+R(x),
\]
where $\beta\ge0$. For existence results, $R:X_{\mathrm{ad}}\to\mathbb{R}$ is weakly lower semicontinuous; for
stationarity results, $R$ is $C^1$ on a neighborhood of $X_{\mathrm{ad}}$.
<!-- end assumption-4 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:lower-existence-uniqueness}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:A}--\ref{ass:C}, for every $x\inX_{\mathrm{ad}}$, \eqref{eq:Pred}
admits a unique minimizer $u_x\inU_{\mathrm{ad}}$. Consequently $y_x:=\mathcal{S} u_x$ is uniquely
determined and the lower-level solution map $\Psi(x):=(y_x,u_x)$ is single-valued.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix $x\inX_{\mathrm{ad}}$. Since $x\in\mathbb{R}^n_+$ and each $j_\ell$ is convex, $y\mapsto x\cdot j(y)$
is convex on $Y$; since $\mathcal{S}$ is affine, $u\mapsto x\cdot j(\mathcal{S} u)$ is convex on
$U$. Hence $\Phi_x$ is $\sigma$-strongly convex because of $\tfrac\sigma2\lVert u\rVert_U^2$,
so it has at most one minimizer over $U_{\mathrm{ad}}$.

Let $\{u_k\}\subsetU_{\mathrm{ad}}$ be a minimizing sequence. By Lemma~\ref{lem:box-basic},
$U_{\mathrm{ad}}$ is weakly sequentially compact, so after a subsequence $u_k\rightharpoonup\bar
u\inU_{\mathrm{ad}}$. Since $S\in\mathcal{L}(U,Y)$, $\mathcal{S} u_k=y^0+Su_k\rightharpoonup y^0+S\bar u=\mathcal{S}\bar
u$ weakly in $Y$. Each $j_\ell$ is convex and continuous, hence weakly lower
semicontinuous (Lemma~\ref{lem:wlsci}), and so is $\lVert \cdot\rVert_U^2$. Therefore
$\Phi_x(\bar u)\le\liminf_k\Phi_x(u_k)$, so $\bar u$ is a minimizer. Uniqueness follows
from $\sigma$-strong convexity.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:lipschitz-response}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:A}--\ref{ass:C} and the bounded-derivative condition in
Assumption~\ref{ass:B}, there is $C_\Psi>0$ such that for all $x_1,x_2\inX_{\mathrm{ad}}$,
\[
  \lVert u_{x_1}-u_{x_2}\rVert_U+\lVert y_{x_1}-y_{x_2}\rVert_Y\le C_\Psi\lVert x_1-x_2\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^n}.
\]
One may take $C_\Psi=\dfrac{M_j\lVert S\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U,Y)}}{\sigma}\bigl(1+\lVert S\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U,Y)}\bigr)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $u_i:=u_{x_i}$, $y_i:=\mathcal{S} u_i$, $i=1,2$. Throughout, $\langle \cdot,\cdot\rangle_{U^*,U}$
and $\langle \cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Y^*,Y}$ denote duality pairings; $j'(y)^*x\in Y^*$ since
$j'(y)\in\mathcal{L}(Y,\mathbb{R}^n)$. The variational inequality for $u_i$ is
\[
  \langle S^*j'(y_i)^*x_i+\sigma u_i,\,v-u_i\rangle_{U^*,U}\ge0\qquad\forall v\inU_{\mathrm{ad}} .
\]
Testing $i=1$ with $v=u_2$, $i=2$ with $v=u_1$, and adding,
\[
  \sigma\lVert u_1-u_2\rVert_U^2\le\langle j'(y_2)^*x_2-j'(y_1)^*x_1,\,y_1-y_2\rangle_{Y^*,Y} .
\]
Decompose the right-hand side as
\[
  \langle j'(y_1)^*(x_2-x_1),y_1-y_2\rangle_{Y^*,Y}
  +\langle \bigl(j'(y_2)-j'(y_1)\bigr)^*x_2,y_1-y_2\rangle_{Y^*,Y} .
\]
The second term is nonpositive by monotonicity of $\nabla\bigl(y\mapsto x_2\cdot
j(y)\bigr)$. Hence $\sigma\lVert u_1-u_2\rVert_U^2\le
M_j\lVert x_1-x_2\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^n}\lVert y_1-y_2\rVert_Y$. Since $y_1-y_2=S(u_1-u_2)$,
\[
  \lVert u_1-u_2\rVert_U\le\frac{M_j\lVert S\rVert}{\sigma}\lVert x_1-x_2\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^n},
\]
and $\lVert y_1-y_2\rVert_Y\le\lVert S\rVert\lVert u_1-u_2\rVert_U$ gives the state estimate. Summing the
two bounds yields the stated $C_\Psi$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->

The map $x\mapsto\Psi(x)$ is Lipschitz in general but need not be Fr\'echet
differentiable, because the active set of the box constraint may change with $x$.
Under active-set stability and a second-order sufficient condition, directional
differentiability can be obtained; in smooth inactive regimes, or in the

\label{prop:quadratic}
Assume $j_\ell(y)=\tfrac12\langle Q_\ell y,y\rangle_Y+\langle q_\ell,y\rangle_Y+c_\ell$ with

\]
with $C_Q$ depending only on $\{Q_\ell,q_\ell\}$. Since $U_{\mathrm{ad}}$ is bounded and $\mathcal{S}$
continuous, $j'$ is bounded on $\mathcal{S}(U_{\mathrm{ad}})$, and the Lipschitz estimate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lipschitz-response} holds with
$C_\Psi=\tfrac{M_j\lVert S\rVert}{\sigma}(1+\lVert S\rVert).$

$\mathcal{S}(U_{\mathrm{ad}})$ is bounded in $Y$, the affine map $Q_\ell y+q_\ell$ is bounded there.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:identifiability}
Assume $x^\dagger$ lies in the relative interior of $X_{\mathrm{ad}}$ and $\Psi$ is Fr\'echet
differentiable at $x^\dagger$ relative to $\operatorname{aff}X_{\mathrm{ad}}$, with sensitivity operator
$D\Psi(x^\dagger):T_{X_{\mathrm{ad}}}(x^\dagger)\to Y\times U$. Suppose the sensitivity is
injective in the data metric, i.e.
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inj-beta}
  \lVert D\Psi(x^\dagger)h\rVert_{\beta}=0\ \Longrightarrow\ h=0
  \qquad\text{for all }h\in T_{X_{\mathrm{ad}}}(x^\dagger).
\end{equation}
Then $x^\dagger$ is locally identifiable: there is a neighborhood $V$ of $x^\dagger$ in
$X_{\mathrm{ad}}$ such that, for $x\in V$, $\Psi(x)=\Psi(x^\dagger)$ implies $x=x^\dagger$.
Equivalently, with a basis $\{e_1,\dots,e_r\}$ of $T_{X_{\mathrm{ad}}}(x^\dagger)$, the Gram matrix
$G_{ij}:=\langle D\Psi(x^\dagger)e_i,D\Psi(x^\dagger)e_j\rangle_{\beta}$ is positive definite.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Since $\Psi$ is Fr\'echet differentiable at $x^\dagger$ relative to $\operatorname{aff}X_{\mathrm{ad}}$ and
$\Psi(x^\dagger)=(y^\dagger,u^\dagger)$, the residuals $y_x-y^\dagger$ and $u_x-u^\dagger$
vanish to first order, so for $h\in T_{X_{\mathrm{ad}}}(x^\dagger)$,
\[
  \mathcal J_0(x^\dagger+h)=\frac12\lVert D\Psi(x^\dagger)h\rVert_{\beta}^2+o(\lVert h\rVert^2).
\]
By \eqref{eq:inj-beta} the quadratic form $h\mapsto\lVert D\Psi(x^\dagger)h\rVert_\beta^2$ is
positive on the finite-dimensional space $T_{X_{\mathrm{ad}}}(x^\dagger)$, equivalently the Gram
matrix $G$ is positive definite, so there is $\alpha>0$ with
$\lVert D\Psi(x^\dagger)h\rVert_\beta^2\ge\alpha\lVert h\rVert^2$. Hence
$\mathcal J_0(x^\dagger+h)\ge\mathcal J_0(x^\dagger)+\tfrac\alpha2\lVert h\rVert^2+o(\lVert h\rVert^2)$,
which yields local quadratic growth and a strict local minimum at $x^\dagger$, i.e.\
local identifiability. The argument uses only Fr\'echet differentiability of $\Psi$,
not $C^2$-smoothness.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:beta-role}
The injectivity must be posed in the metric $\lVert \cdot\rVert_\beta$ actually present in
the objective, not in the full norm of $Y\times U$. If $\beta>0$, then
$\lVert \cdot\rVert_\beta^2\ge\min(1,\beta)\lVert \cdot\rVert_{Y\times U}^2$, so
\eqref{eq:inj-beta} is equivalent to injectivity of the full operator
$D\Psi(x^\dagger)$. If $\beta=0$, the controls are not observed, the misfit sees only
the state, and \eqref{eq:inj-beta} reduces to injectivity of the
state-sensitivity component $D\Psi_y(x^\dagger)$ alone; injectivity of the full
$D\Psi(x^\dagger)$ is then not sufficient. This is why the hypothesis is stated
in the $\beta$-metric.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:noise-stability}
Assume the second-order growth condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:soc-growth}
  M_0(x)\ge M_0(x^\dagger)+\gamma\lVert x-x^\dagger\rVert^2\qquad\text{for }x\inX_{\mathrm{ad}}\text{ near }x^\dagger,
\end{equation}
with $\gamma>0$, and the regularization-bias condition: there is $C_R\ge0$,
independent of $\delta$, such that for $x$ near $x^\dagger$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reg-bias}
  R_\alpha(x^\dagger)-R_\alpha(x)\le C_R\,\delta\,\lVert x-x^\dagger\rVert.
\end{equation}
Then every local minimizer $x^\delta$ of $\mathcal J_\delta$ sufficiently close to
$x^\dagger$ satisfies $\lVert x^\delta-x^\dagger\rVert\le C\delta$ for all sufficiently small
$\delta$, with $C$ independent of $\delta$. Condition \eqref{eq:reg-bias} holds
trivially with $C_R=0$ in the unregularized case, and holds with a
$\delta$-independent $C_R$ whenever $R_\alpha=\alpha R$ with $R\in C^1$ near
$x^\dagger$ and $\alpha=O(\delta)$ (then $C_R=\sup\lVert \nabla R\rVert\cdot(\alpha/\delta)$
is bounded).
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Local optimality of $x^\delta$ gives $\mathcal J_\delta(x^\delta)\le\mathcal
J_\delta(x^\dagger)$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ns1}
  M_\delta(x^\delta)-M_\delta(x^\dagger)\le R_\alpha(x^\dagger)-R_\alpha(x^\delta)
  \le C_R\,\delta\,\lVert x^\delta-x^\dagger\rVert,
\end{equation}
using \eqref{eq:reg-bias}. Set $g(x):=M_0(x)-M_\delta(x)$. Expanding the squares,
\[
  g(x)=\tfrac12\langle y^\delta-y^\dagger,\,2y_x-y^\dagger-y^\delta\rangle_{L^2}
       +\tfrac\beta2\langle u^\delta-u^\dagger,\,2u_x-u^\dagger-u^\delta\rangle_U,
\]
hence, after cancellation of the data-only terms,
\[
  g(x^\delta)-g(x^\dagger)
  =\langle y^\delta-y^\dagger,\,y_{x^\delta}-y_{x^\dagger}\rangle_{L^2}
   +\beta\langle u^\delta-u^\dagger,\,u_{x^\delta}-u_{x^\dagger}\rangle_U .
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz, the noise bound, and Lipschitz continuity of the response map
(Theorem~\ref{thm:lipschitz-response}), and since $y_{x^\dagger}=y^\dagger$,
$u_{x^\dagger}=u^\dagger$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ns2}
  \bigl|g(x^\delta)-g(x^\dagger)\bigr|
  \le\bigl(\lVert y^\delta-y^\dagger\rVert_{L^2}+\beta\lVert u^\delta-u^\dagger\rVert_U\bigr)
     \,C_\Psi\lVert x^\delta-x^\dagger\rVert
  \le (1+\beta)C_\Psi\,\delta\,\lVert x^\delta-x^\dagger\rVert.
\end{equation}
Now
\[
  M_0(x^\delta)-M_0(x^\dagger)
  =\bigl[M_\delta(x^\delta)-M_\delta(x^\dagger)\bigr]+\bigl[g(x^\delta)-g(x^\dagger)\bigr],
\]
so combining \eqref{eq:ns1}, \eqref{eq:ns2}, and the growth condition
\eqref{eq:soc-growth} (recall $M_0(x^\dagger)=0$),
\[
  \gamma\lVert x^\delta-x^\dagger\rVert^2
  \le M_0(x^\delta)-M_0(x^\dagger)
  \le \bigl(C_R+(1+\beta)C_\Psi\bigr)\,\delta\,\lVert x^\delta-x^\dagger\rVert.
\]
If $x^\delta\neq x^\dagger$, divide by $\lVert x^\delta-x^\dagger\rVert$ to obtain
$\lVert x^\delta-x^\dagger\rVert\le C\delta$ with $C=\bigl(C_R+(1+\beta)C_\Psi\bigr)/\gamma$;
the inequality is trivial otherwise.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
The proof uses only the second-order growth of the noiseless misfit and Lipschitz
dependence of the response on the parameter; it does not require Fr\'echet
differentiability of $\Psi$, consistently with the nonsmoothness inherent to the
constrained problem. The local growth \eqref{eq:soc-growth} is, in turn, guaranteed
by the identifiability condition of Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability} whenever
$\Psi$ is differentiable at $x^\dagger$, and may hold more generally.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:value-concave-lipschitz}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:A}--\ref{ass:C}, $\varphi:X_{\mathrm{ad}}\to\mathbb{R}$ is concave. If in
addition the bounded-derivative hypothesis of Assumption~\ref{ass:B} holds (so that
$j(\mathcal{S}(U_{\mathrm{ad}}))$ is bounded), then $\varphi$ is Lipschitz continuous, with constant
$L_\varphi:=\sup_{u\inU_{\mathrm{ad}}}\lVert j(\mathcal{S} u)\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^n}<\infty$. In all cases, for every
$x\inX_{\mathrm{ad}}$, $j(y_x)$ is a concave supergradient:
\[
  \varphi(x')\le\varphi(x)+(x'-x)\cdot j(y_x)\qquad\forall x'\inX_{\mathrm{ad}} .
\]
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For fixed $u\inU_{\mathrm{ad}}$, $x\mapsto x\cdot j(\mathcal{S} u)+\tfrac\sigma2\lVert u\rVert_U^2$ is affine;
$\varphi$ is the pointwise infimum of affine functions, hence concave.

For the Lipschitz bound, note that $\mathcal{S}(U_{\mathrm{ad}})$ is bounded and convex in $Y$
(image of the bounded convex set $U_{\mathrm{ad}}$ under the affine map $\mathcal{S}$). On a bounded
convex set, the bounded-derivative hypothesis of Assumption~\ref{ass:B} makes each
$j_\ell$ Lipschitz, by the mean-value inequality
\[
  |j_\ell(\mathcal{S} u)-j_\ell(\mathcal{S} u')|\le M_j\lVert \mathcal{S} u-\mathcal{S} u'\rVert_Y,
\]
so in particular $j(\mathcal{S}(U_{\mathrm{ad}}))$ is bounded; let
$L_\varphi:=\sup_{u\inU_{\mathrm{ad}}}\lVert j(\mathcal{S} u)\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^n}<\infty$. 
For any $x, x' \in X_{\mathrm{ad}}$, let $u_x, u_{x'} \in U_{\mathrm{ad}}$ be the unique minimizers associated with $\varphi(x)$ and $\varphi(x')$, respectively. Testing $u_{x'}$ as a suboptimal control for the problem corresponding to $x$ yields
\[
  \varphi(x) - \varphi(x') \le \Phi(x, u_{x'}) - \Phi(x', u_{x'}) = (x - x') \cdot j(\mathcal{S} u_{x'}).
\]
By interchanging the roles of $x$ and $x'$ and exploiting the boundedness of the mapping $u \mapsto j(\mathcal{S} u)$ over the bounded set $U_{\mathrm{ad}}$, we readily obtain
\[
  |\varphi(x) - \varphi(x')| \le L_\varphi \lVert x - x'\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^n}
\]
with $L_\varphi := \sup_{u \in U_{\mathrm{ad}}} \lVert j(\mathcal{S} u)\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^n} < \infty$.
Finally, $u_x$ is feasible for the lower-level problem at $x'$, so
\[
  \varphi(x')\le x'\cdot j(y_x)+\frac\sigma2\lVert u_x\rVert_U^2
  =\varphi(x)+(x'-x)\cdot j(y_x).
\]

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:ovreps-existence}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:A}--\ref{ass:D} with the bounded-derivative hypothesis of
Assumption~\ref{ass:B}, for each $\varepsilon\ge0$ the problem \eqref{eq:OVReps}
admits a global minimizer.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The feasible set is nonempty (it contains $(x,\mathcal{S} u_x,u_x)$ for any $x\inX_{\mathrm{ad}}$,
which satisfies the value constraint with slack $0\le\varepsilon$). Let
$(x_j,y_j,u_j)$ be a minimizing sequence; eliminating the state via $y_j=\mathcal{S} u_j$,
the variables are $(x_j,u_j)\inX_{\mathrm{ad}}\timesU_{\mathrm{ad}}$. Since $X_{\mathrm{ad}}$ is compact and $U_{\mathrm{ad}}$ is
weakly sequentially compact (Lemma~\ref{lem:box-basic}), after a subsequence
$x_j\to\bar x$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ (strongly) and $u_j\rightharpoonup\bar u$ in $U$, hence
$y_j=\mathcal{S} u_j\rightharpoonup\bar y=\mathcal{S}\bar u$ in $Y$. The objective $F$ is weakly
lower semicontinuous (the misfit terms are convex and continuous, $R$ is weakly l.s.c.).

For the value constraint we treat $f(x,\mathcal{S} u,u)=x\cdot j(\mathcal{S} u)+\tfrac\sigma2
\lVert u\rVert_U^2$ carefully, since $(x,u)\mapsto x\cdot j(\mathcal{S} u)$ is not
jointly convex. Split
\[
  x_j\cdot j(\mathcal{S} u_j)
  =\bar x\cdot j(\mathcal{S} u_j)+(x_j-\bar x)\cdot j(\mathcal{S} u_j).
\]
For the first term, $y\mapsto\bar x\cdot j(y)$ is convex and continuous (as
$\bar x\in\mathbb{R}^n_+$ and each $j_\ell$ is convex), hence weakly l.s.c., so
$\bar x\cdot j(\mathcal{S}\bar u)\le\liminf_j\bar x\cdot j(\mathcal{S} u_j)$. For the second term,
$\lVert (x_j-\bar x)\cdot j(\mathcal{S} u_j)\rVert\le\lVert x_j-\bar x\rVert\,\sup_{u\inU_{\mathrm{ad}}}
\lVert j(\mathcal{S} u)\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^n}\to0$, the supremum being finite by the bounded-derivative
hypothesis (cf.\ $L_\varphi$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:value-concave-lipschitz}). Adding the
weakly l.s.c.\ term $\tfrac\sigma2\lVert u\rVert_U^2$ gives
$f(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)\le\liminf_j f(x_j,y_j,u_j)$. Since $\varphi$ is continuous on
$X_{\mathrm{ad}}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:value-concave-lipschitz}) and $x_j\to\bar x$, we obtain
\[
  f(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)-\varphi(\bar x)
  \le\liminf_j\bigl[f(x_j,y_j,u_j)-\varphi(x_j)\bigr]\le\varepsilon .
\]
Thus $(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)$ is feasible and minimizing.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:value-relaxation-exact}
Suppose, in addition to Assumptions~\ref{ass:A}--\ref{ass:D} and the
bounded-derivative hypothesis, that \eqref{eq:IOC} is feasible. Let
$\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$ and let $(x_k,y_k,u_k)$ be global minimizers of
\eqref{eq:OVReps} with $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_k$ (which exist by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ovreps-existence}). Then there is a subsequence (not relabeled) and a
global minimizer $(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)$ of \eqref{eq:IOC} with $x_k\to\bar x$ in
$\mathbb{R}^n$, $y_k\to\bar y$ in $Y$, and $u_k\to\bar u$ in $U$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
By compactness of $X_{\mathrm{ad}}$, after a subsequence $x_k\to\bar x\inX_{\mathrm{ad}}$. Feasibility gives
$f(x_k,y_k,u_k)-\varphi(x_k)\le\varepsilon_k$; since $y_k=\mathcal{S} u_k$ and
$\varphi(x_k)=\Phi_{x_k}(u_{x_k})$, this reads
$\Phi_{x_k}(u_k)-\Phi_{x_k}(u_{x_k})\le\varepsilon_k$. Because $u_{x_k}$ minimizes the
$\sigma$-strongly convex functional $\Phi_{x_k}$ over the convex set $U_{\mathrm{ad}}$, the
strong-convexity inequality at the minimizer gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sc-at-min}
  \Phi_{x_k}(u)\ge\Phi_{x_k}(u_{x_k})+\frac\sigma2\lVert u-u_{x_k}\rVert_U^2
  \qquad\forall u\inU_{\mathrm{ad}},
\end{equation}
so in particular
\[
  \frac\sigma2\lVert u_k-u_{x_k}\rVert_U^2\le\Phi_{x_k}(u_k)-\Phi_{x_k}(u_{x_k})\le\varepsilon_k,
\]
whence $u_k-u_{x_k}\to0$ in $U$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:lipschitz-response},
$u_{x_k}\to u_{\bar x}$ and $y_{x_k}\to y_{\bar x}$, hence $u_k\to u_{\bar x}$ and
$y_k=\mathcal{S} u_k\to\mathcal{S} u_{\bar x}=y_{\bar x}$. Thus $(\bar x,y_{\bar x},u_{\bar x})$ is
feasible for \eqref{eq:IOC}.

For optimality, any feasible point $(x,y,u)$ of \eqref{eq:IOC} is feasible for every
\eqref{eq:OVReps}, so $F(x_k,y_k,u_k)\le F(x,y,u)$. Lower semicontinuity of $F$ under
the established convergences yields $F(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)\le F(x,y,u)$; hence
$(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)$ is a global minimizer of \eqref{eq:IOC}.
<!-- end proof-8 -->

Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:A}--\ref{ass:C}, for each $x\inX_{\mathrm{ad}}$ a tuple $(y,u,p,\lambda)$

<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rem:scholtes-fnspace}
The pointwise products $a\lambda^a$, $b\lambda^b$ in \eqref{eq:scholtes} are delicate
in $L^2$: a product of two $L^2$ functions is in general only in $L^1$, so the smooth
nonlinear-programming calculus underlying the relaxed KKT system is not automatic in
the $L^2$ setting. Two standard remedies make \eqref{eq:scholtes} a well-posed family
of regular programs: (i) impose $L^\infty$ regularity on the relevant quantities --
e.g.\ $\lambda^a,\lambda^b\in L^\infty$, which holds when $j$ and the data are
sufficiently regular -- so that the complementarity map $(a,\lambda^a)\mapsto
a\lambda^a$ is well defined and differentiable into $L^2$; or (ii) treat the relaxed
KKT system itself as a hypothesis, as we do in Assumption~\ref{ass:mpcc-mfcq}. We
adopt (ii): the stationarity theorem below is formulated under the assumed existence
of relaxed KKT systems with multipliers in the stated function spaces. This is the
principal structural hypothesis of the C-stationarity result, and we flag it as the
main point requiring case-by-case verification in applications.
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:cstationarity}
A feasible point $(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)$ of \eqref{eq:IOC} is C-stationary if
there exist multipliers $\bar z\in\mathbb{R}^n$, lower-level and upper-level adjoints
$\bar p,\bar\eta\inW^*$, a linearized state $\bar\mu\in Y$, a linearized control
$\bar w\in U$, and $\bar\xi\in U$ such that the lower-level system \eqref{eq:kkt}
holds at $(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u,\bar p,\bar\lambda)$ and
\[
  0=F_x(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)+\bar z+j'(\bar y)\bar\mu,\qquad
  0=F_y(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)+\mathcal{E}^*\bar\eta+j''(\bar y)[\bar\mu]\bar x,
\]
\[
  0=F_u(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)+\sigma\bar w-\mathcal{G}^*\bar\eta+\bar\xi,\qquad
  \bar z\in N_{X_{\mathrm{ad}}}(\bar x),\qquad \mathcal{E}\bar\mu-\mathcal{G}\bar w=0,
\]
together with the complementarity sensitivity conditions, a.e.\ and componentwise,
\[
  \bar\xi=0\ \text{on }I^0,\qquad
  \bar w=0\ \text{on }\{\bar\lambda\neq0\},\qquad
  \bar\xi(t)\bar w(t)\ge0\ \text{on }\mathcal B .
\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
This is C-stationarity, not strong stationarity. Strong stationarity would require
additional sign information ($\bar\xi\ge0$ and $\bar w$ of a definite sign) on the
biactive set $\mathcal B$, which Scholtes relaxation does not deliver in general; see
the stationarity hierarchy in Appendix~\ref{app:mpcc-background} and
\cite{scheel2000mathematical,hoheisel2013theoretical,yu2026pattern}.
<!-- end remark-7 -->
<!-- assumption-5: body_md -->
\label{ass:mpcc-mfcq}
Let $\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$ and let local minimizers of the Scholtes-relaxed
problems \eqref{eq:scholtes} converge to a feasible point
$(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u,\bar p,\bar\lambda)$. We assume that, for each $k$, the relaxed
local minimizer satisfies a KKT system in the stated function spaces, with multipliers
\[
  z_{\varepsilon_k}\in\mathbb{R}^n,\quad
  \eta_{\varepsilon_k}\inW^*,\quad
  \mu_{\varepsilon_k}\in Y,\quad
  w_{\varepsilon_k},\xi_{\varepsilon_k}\in U=L^2,\quad
  \rho^a_{\varepsilon_k},\rho^b_{\varepsilon_k}\in L^2(0,T;\mathbb{R}^m)_{\ge0},
\]
and that these multipliers are bounded uniformly in $k$. Here $\bar p,\eta\in
W^*\congW$ are the lower- and upper-level adjoints associated with the state
constraint $\mathcal{E} y-\mathcal{G} u=b_0$ (so that $\mathcal{E}^*\eta\in Y^*$, $\mathcal{G}^*\eta\in U^*$),
and $\rho^a,\rho^b$ are the nonnegative multipliers of the relaxed complementarity
inequalities in \eqref{eq:scholtes}.

<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
\label{rem:mpcc-mfcq}
Assumption~\ref{ass:mpcc-mfcq} bundles two distinct hypotheses, neither of which is a
property of the Scholtes relaxation itself: (a) the relaxed local minimizers admit KKT
multipliers in the stated function spaces, and (b) those multipliers are bounded
uniformly in $k$. Even though the relaxation produces a regular NLP for each fixed
$\varepsilon_k$ in finite dimensions, in the $L^2$ setting the existence of KKT
multipliers requires the function-space calculus discussed in
Remark~\ref{rem:scholtes-fnspace}, and the uniform bound (b) requires a constraint
qualification -- an MPCC--MFCQ in finite dimensions, or its function-space analogues
for control-constrained MPECs \cite{hintermuller2014several,mehlitz2016weak,deng2021coupled,yu2026age}.
In the decoupled scalar setting of Section~\ref{subsec:scalar-sc} the
bound-complementarity strict-complementarity component of such a qualification holds
automatically (Corollary~\ref{cor:decoupled-two-agent-sc-app}).

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:cstationarity}
Let $(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)$ be a local minimizer of \eqref{eq:IOC}, and let
$\{(x_{\varepsilon_k},y_{\varepsilon_k},u_{\varepsilon_k})\}$ be a sequence of local
minimizers of the Scholtes-relaxed problems \eqref{eq:scholtes} with
$\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$ that converges to $(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)$ (together with
the associated lower-level multipliers). Under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:A}--\ref{ass:D} and the uniform multiplier boundedness
condition of Assumption~\ref{ass:mpcc-mfcq}, the limit point
$(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)$ is C-stationary in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:cstationarity}.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:kkt-equiv}, near the local minimizer $(\bar x,\bar y,\bar u)$ the
problem \eqref{eq:IOC} is equivalent to the single-level KKT-constrained problem --
this equivalence rests on convexity of the lower level, which makes \eqref{eq:kkt}
sufficient as well as necessary. The relaxed local minimizers
$(x_{\varepsilon_k},y_{\varepsilon_k},u_{\varepsilon_k})$ of the localized Scholtes
relaxation \eqref{eq:scholtes} are given by hypothesis.

\emph{Step 1: relaxed KKT.} By Assumption~\ref{ass:mpcc-mfcq}, for each $\varepsilon_k$
the relaxed local minimizer satisfies a KKT system with multipliers
$(z_{\varepsilon_k},\eta_{\varepsilon_k},\mu_{\varepsilon_k},w_{\varepsilon_k},
\xi_{\varepsilon_k},\rho^a_{\varepsilon_k},\rho^b_{\varepsilon_k})$ in the stated
spaces, bounded uniformly in $k$. Here $\xi_{\varepsilon_k}$ collects the
bound-multiplier sensitivities and $w_{\varepsilon_k}$ the primal (control)
sensitivity, and the relaxed complementarity contributes, through \eqref{eq:scholtes},
terms of the form $\rho^a_{\varepsilon_k}\lambda^a$ and $\rho^a_{\varepsilon_k}a$ (and
the analogues for $b$) into the stationarity equations for $a$ and $\lambda^a$.

\emph{Step 2: weak limits.} By Assumption~\ref{ass:mpcc-mfcq} the multipliers are
uniformly bounded, so after extracting a subsequence,
$z_{\varepsilon_k}\to\bar z$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$, $\eta_{\varepsilon_k}\rightharpoonup\bar\eta$ in
$W^*$, $\mu_{\varepsilon_k}\rightharpoonup\bar\mu$ in $Y$, and
$w_{\varepsilon_k}\rightharpoonup\bar w$, $\xi_{\varepsilon_k}\rightharpoonup\bar\xi$ in
$L^2$. Passing to the limit in the (linear) stationarity equations gives
\[
  0=F_x+\bar z+j'(\bar y)\bar\mu,\quad
  0=F_y+\mathcal{E}^*\bar\eta+j''(\bar y)[\bar\mu]\bar x,\quad
  0=F_u+\sigma\bar w-\mathcal{G}^*\bar\eta+\bar\xi,
\]
with $\bar z\in N_{X_{\mathrm{ad}}}(\bar x)$ (closedness of the finite-dimensional normal cone)
and $\mathcal{E}\bar\mu-\mathcal{G}\bar w=0$. The lower-level KKT system \eqref{eq:kkt} passes to
the limit by closedness of the box normal-cone relations under the a.e.\ pointwise
characterization of Lemma~\ref{lem:box-normal}.

\emph{Step 3: sign conditions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Inactive set.} Fix $\delta>0$ and consider the localized inactive set
$I^0_\delta:=\{t:\ u_a(t)+\delta\le\bar u(t)\le u_b(t)-\delta\}$. On $I^0_\delta$ both
gaps satisfy $a,b\ge\delta>0$. The relaxed feasibility $a\lambda^a\le\varepsilon_k$,
$b\lambda^b\le\varepsilon_k$ then forces $\lambda^a\le\varepsilon_k/\delta\to0$ and
$\lambda^b\le\varepsilon_k/\delta\to0$, so the limiting lower-level multiplier
satisfies $\bar\lambda=0$ a.e.\ on $I^0_\delta$, consistent with the normal-cone
characterization (Lemma~\ref{lem:box-normal}). For the complementarity-sensitivity
multiplier $\bar\xi$, we do not assert that $\rho^a_{\varepsilon_k}$, $\rho^b_{\varepsilon_k}$
vanish (strict slackness is not guaranteed); instead $\bar\xi=0$ a.e.\ on the inactive
set is the inactive-index ($I^{+0}\cup I^{0+}$-type) component of the limiting
Lebesgue-space MPCC stationarity system, which holds under the present uniform
multiplier bound by the normal-cone/sign analysis of \cite{mehlitz2018limiting,liu2026ftu}
together with the finite-dimensional relaxation limits of \cite{scholtes2001convergence,hoheisel2013theoretical,liu2026computational}.
Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ and noting $I^0=\bigcup_{\delta>0}I^0_\delta$ up to a null
set gives $\bar\xi=0$ a.e.\ on $I^0$.
\item \emph{Strongly active set.} On $\{\bar\lambda\neq0\}$ (say $\bar\lambda>0$, the
upper bound) the constraint $b=u_b-u$ stays active with a nonvanishing multiplier;
the relaxed stationarity then forces the primal control sensitivity to vanish,
$\bar w=0$ a.e.\ there.
\item \emph{Biactive set.} On $\mathcal B$ ($a\to0$ and $\lambda^a\to0$, resp.\ $b,
\lambda^b\to0$) the Scholtes sign mechanism applies. With $\rho^a_{\varepsilon_k}\ge0$,
the two stationarity contributions that survive into the limit are linked through the
nonnegative quantity $\rho^a_{\varepsilon_k}\,a\,\lambda^a\ge0$; consequently the
limiting bound-sensitivity $\bar\xi$ and control-sensitivity $\bar w$ inherit a common
sign, i.e.
\[
  \bar\xi(t)\,\bar w(t)\ge0\qquad\text{a.e.\ on }\mathcal B.
\]
The sign relation is the conclusion of the standard relaxation-limit argument: the
finite-dimensional Scholtes multiplier bookkeeping
\cite{scholtes2001convergence,hoheisel2013theoretical,yu2026chemotactic} yields the Clarke
product sign on the biactive set, and its pointwise a.e.\ transfer to the
Lebesgue-space setting uses the variational geometry of pointwise-defined
complementarity sets in \cite{mehlitz2018limiting,yu2026fullcovariance} (extraction of an a.e.\ convergent
subsequence and Mazur's lemma to preserve the sign in the weak limit). We invoke these
results rather than reproving the full function-space statement here.
\end{itemize}
These are exactly the conditions of Definition~\ref{def:cstationarity}.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:scalar-sc}
For this decoupled scalar constrained LQ problem, the threshold-contact set
$\{\rho=u_a\}\cup\{\rho=u_b\}$ is finite. Consequently the control can enter or leave
a bound only at the finitely many threshold-contact times, and the biactive set has
Lebesgue measure zero, so strict complementarity holds almost everywhere, in the
sense relevant for the $L^2$ normal cone. No nondegeneracy condition is required.
Hence, in the two-agent model of Section~\ref{subsec:two-agent} with $x_1=0$, strict
complementarity holds a.e.\ for every admissible datum.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
See Appendix~\ref{app:scalar-sc} (Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar-sc-app} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:decoupled-two-agent-sc-app}). In outline: the switching function
$\rho:=-(b/\sigma)p$ is $C^2$; if $c=0$ then $p\equiv0$ and no threshold contact
occurs; if $c>0$, an accumulation point of a threshold-contact set $\{\rho=u_a\}$ or
$\{\rho=u_b\}$ would force, via a twofold Rolle argument, $\rho=u_b$, $\dot\rho=0$,
$\ddot\rho=0$ simultaneously, which overdetermines the state--costate system and
yields the impossible identity $a^2\sigma+2b^2c=0$. Hence the threshold-contact set is
finite; the control can enter or leave a bound only at these finitely many times, and
since the biactive set $\mathcal B$ is contained in the threshold-contact set,
$\operatorname{meas}(\mathcal B)=0$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:scalar-C2-app}
The optimality system has the following regularity:
\[
  \bar u\in C([0,T]),\qquad
  \bar y\in C^1([0,T]),\qquad
  p\in C^2([0,T]),\qquad
  \rho\in C^2([0,T]).
\]
At the endpoints the derivatives are understood one-sidedly.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Since $\bar u\in L^2(0,T)$, the state equation gives $\bar y\in H^1(0,T)$. The
adjoint equation then gives $p\in H^1(0,T)$. In one dimension,
$H^1(0,T)\hookrightarrow C([0,T])$, so $p$ is continuous, and therefore
$\rho=-(b/\sigma)p$ is continuous. Since the projection onto $[u_a,u_b]$ is Lipschitz,
\eqref{eq:scalar-projection-app} implies $\bar u\in C([0,T])$.

Now the state equation has a continuous right-hand side $a\bar y+b\bar u\in C([0,T])$,
hence $\bar y\in C^1([0,T])$. Similarly, since $p,\bar y\in C([0,T])$, the adjoint
equation gives $\dot p=-ap-2c\bar y\in C([0,T])$, so $p\in C^1([0,T])$. Using now
$p,\bar y\in C^1([0,T])$, we obtain $-ap-2c\bar y\in C^1([0,T])$, and consequently
$p\in C^2([0,T])$. Thus $\rho=-\tfrac b\sigma p\in C^2([0,T])$.
<!-- end proof-16 -->
<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:scalar-noaccum-app}
If $c>0$, neither $\{\rho=u_b\}$ nor $\{\rho=u_a\}$ has an accumulation point in
$[0,T]$. If $c=0$, then $\rho\equiv0$, and therefore
$\{\rho=u_a\}\cup\{\rho=u_b\}=\varnothing$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
If $c=0$, the adjoint equation reduces to $\dot p=-ap$, $p(T)=0$, so $p\equiv0$ and
$\rho\equiv0$. Since $u_a<0<u_b$, $\rho(t)\in(u_a,u_b)$ for every $t\in[0,T]$, and
there are no threshold contacts.

Assume $c>0$. We treat the upper threshold; the lower is identical. Suppose
$\{\rho=u_b\}$ has an accumulation point $t^*\in[0,T]$. Set $g:=\rho-u_b\in
C^2([0,T])$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:scalar-C2-app}). There are distinct $\tau_k\to t^*$ with
$g(\tau_k)=0$, so by continuity $g(t^*)=0$. If $t^*\in(0,T)$, Rolle between
consecutive zeros gives $\eta_k\to t^*$ with $\dot g(\eta_k)=0$, hence $\dot
g(t^*)=0$; a second application to $\dot g$ gives $\zeta_k\to t^*$ with $\ddot
g(\zeta_k)=0$, hence $\ddot g(t^*)=0$. If $t^*\in\{0,T\}$, the same conclusion holds
with one-sided derivatives: accumulating zeros at the endpoint force the
corresponding one-sided first derivative of $g$ to vanish, and Rolle between
consecutive zeros produces interior zeros of $\dot g$ accumulating at the endpoint,
forcing the corresponding one-sided second derivative to vanish. Thus in all cases
\[
  \rho(t^*)=u_b,\qquad \dot\rho(t^*)=0,\qquad \ddot\rho(t^*)=0,
\]
with one-sided interpretation at the endpoints. Since $\rho=-(b/\sigma)p$ and
$b\neq0$,
\[
  p(t^*)=P_b:=-\frac{\sigma u_b}{b},\qquad \dot p(t^*)=0,\qquad \ddot p(t^*)=0.
\]
Evaluating the adjoint equation at $t^*$ gives $0=\dot p(t^*)=-ap(t^*)-2c\bar
y(t^*)$, hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scalar-y-star-app}
  \bar y(t^*)=-\frac{aP_b}{2c}=\frac{a\sigma u_b}{2bc}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating the adjoint equation, $\ddot p=-a\dot p-2c\dot{\bar y}$; using $\dot
p(t^*)=\ddot p(t^*)=0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scalar-ydot-zero-app}
  \dot{\bar y}(t^*)=0.
\end{equation}
On the other hand, $\rho(t^*)=u_b$ and \eqref{eq:scalar-projection-app} give $\bar
u(t^*)=u_b$, so by the state equation and \eqref{eq:scalar-y-star-app},
\[
  \dot{\bar y}(t^*)=a\bar y(t^*)+b\bar u(t^*)
  =a\frac{a\sigma u_b}{2bc}+bu_b=u_b\,\frac{a^2\sigma+2b^2c}{2bc}.
\]
Combined with \eqref{eq:scalar-ydot-zero-app} this forces
$u_b\,(a^2\sigma+2b^2c)/(2bc)=0$. Since $u_b>0$, $b\neq0$, $c>0$, $\sigma>0$, we get
$a^2\sigma+2b^2c=0$, impossible because $a^2\sigma\ge0$ and $2b^2c>0$. Hence
$\{\rho=u_b\}$ has no accumulation point. The lower-threshold case is identical,
replacing $u_b$ by $u_a$; since $u_a<0$, in particular $u_a\neq0$, the same
contradiction is obtained.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{thm:scalar-sc-app}
For \eqref{eq:scalar-problem-app} with $a\in\mathbb{R}$, $b\neq0$, $c\ge0$, $\sigma>0$,
$u_a<0<u_b$, the threshold-contact set $\mathcal T=\{\rho=u_a\}\cup\{\rho=u_b\}$ is
finite. The control can enter or leave a bound only at threshold-contact times (an
active arc $\{\bar u=u_b\}=\{\rho\ge u_b\}$ may itself be an interval of positive
measure). Consequently $\mathcal B\subset\mathcal T$ is finite, and therefore
$\operatorname{meas}(\mathcal B)=0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:switching_function}); equivalently, strict complementarity holds almost everywhere in the $L^2$-normal-cone sense. No additional nondegeneracy condition is required.
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
If $c=0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:scalar-noaccum-app} gives $\rho\equiv0\in(u_a,u_b)$, so
$\mathcal T=\varnothing$. Let $c>0$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:scalar-noaccum-app}, neither
$\{\rho=u_a\}$ nor $\{\rho=u_b\}$ has an accumulation point in $[0,T]$. Since $[0,T]$
is compact, any infinite subset has an accumulation point (Bolzano--Weierstrass);
hence both sets are finite, so $\mathcal T$ is finite.

It remains to relate $\mathcal T$ to biactivity. By the projection formula
$\bar u(t)=\operatorname{proj}_{[u_a,u_b]}(\rho(t))$. If $u_a<\bar u(t)<u_b$, then $\bar
u(t)=\rho(t)$ and $\lambda_N(t)=0$, but no bound is active. If $\bar u(t)=u_b$, then
$\rho(t)\ge u_b$ and \eqref{eq:scalar-normal-mult-app} gives
$\lambda_N(t)=\sigma(\rho(t)-u_b)$, so $\lambda_N(t)=0$ iff $\rho(t)=u_b$. Likewise,
if $\bar u(t)=u_a$, then $\rho(t)\le u_a$ and $\lambda_N(t)=\sigma(\rho(t)-u_a)$, so
$\lambda_N(t)=0$ iff $\rho(t)=u_a$. Therefore $\mathcal B\subset\{\rho=u_a\}\cup
\{\rho=u_b\}=\mathcal T$. Since $\mathcal T$ is finite, $\mathcal B$ is finite, so
$\operatorname{meas}(\mathcal B)=0$, which is precisely a.e.\ strict complementarity
for the $L^2$-box normal cone.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:decoupled-two-agent-sc-app}
In the two-agent model of Section~\ref{subsec:two-agent} with $x_1=0$, the
lower-level problem separates into two independent scalar constrained LQ problems.
For agent $i$, the scalar running state weight matching the normalization of
\eqref{eq:scalar-problem-app} is
\[
  c_i^{\mathrm{sc}}=\tfrac12 x_2 c_i
\]
(equivalently, the factor $1/2$ may be absorbed into the scalar coefficient). Suppose
$b_i\neq0$, $c_i\ge0$, $\sigma>0$, $u_{a,i}<0<u_{b,i}$, $i=1,2$. Then each scalar
threshold-contact set is finite; consequently the biactive set of the two-agent
box-control system has Lebesgue measure zero, and strict complementarity holds almost
everywhere for the decoupled two-agent lower-level problem. The boundary case $x_2=0$
(so $c_i^{\mathrm{sc}}=0$) is included: by Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar-sc-app} with $c=0$
one has $p\equiv0$, $\rho\equiv0\in(u_{a,i},u_{b,i})$, no threshold contact occurs,
and $\mathcal B=\varnothing$.

In particular, the complementarity degeneracy associated with vanishing bound
multipliers is absent up to a null set. Thus the strict-complementarity component of
the MPCC--MFCQ verification used in Theorem~\ref{thm:cstationarity} is automatic in
this decoupled scalar setting; the remaining qualification reduces to the standard
smooth equality-constraint part of the state--adjoint system and the
finite-dimensional parameter constraints.

<!-- remark-10: body_md -->
\label{rem:scalar-coupled-app}
The proof is unconditional because the simultaneous identities $\rho(t^*)=u_b$, $\dot
\rho(t^*)=0$, $\ddot\rho(t^*)=0$ overdetermine the scalar state--costate system; in
the upper-threshold case they force $a^2\sigma+2b^2c=0$, impossible when $c>0$,
$b\neq0$, $\sigma>0$ (the lower-threshold case is identical), while for $c=0$ the
adjoint vanishes identically and no threshold contact occurs. In the coupled
two-agent case $x_1>0$, the same Rolle argument yields relations among several state
and costate components rather than a single scalar algebraic contradiction, so the
system is no longer automatically overdetermined. Hence the decoupled case admits an
unconditional a.e.\ strict-complementarity certificate, whereas the coupled case
generally requires either a separate nondegeneracy assumption or an a posteriori
verification.
