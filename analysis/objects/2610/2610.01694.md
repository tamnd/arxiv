<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:mainintro}
Fix a finite quiver $Q$, a complete non-Archimedean field $K$, a real
stability parameter $\theta\in\mathbb R^{Q_0}$, a family
$f=(f_a)_{a\in Q_1}$ of weak ramps, and a finite-dimensional representation
$E$ of $Q$ over $K$.
Choose nonzero determinant vectors in the spaces $\det E_i$, and let $S_E$
be the resulting extended HKKP potential.  Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E\text{ is $\theta$-polystable}
 &\Longrightarrow E\text{ admits a split HKKP harmonic norm for }(\theta,f)\\
 &\Longrightarrow E\text{ is $\theta$-semistable}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, if $E$ is $\theta$-polystable, $S_E$ admits a global minimizer
on $\mathcal N(E)$, the complete space of all norms on $E$.
Furthermore, if $\theta(E)=0$ and $h\in\mathcal N(E)$ globally minimizes
$S_E$, there is a split global minimizer $k\in\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}(E)$ with
the same arrow singular profiles (the ordered lists of logarithmic singular
values of the arrow maps, as defined in~ 
  \hyperref[eq:extendedsingularvalues]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:extendedsingularvalues}}}})
and the same potential value as $h$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:euclideanbuilding}
Let $(A,W)$ be model-apartment data as in
\cite[Definition~1.9]{RTW}: $A$ is a Euclidean affine space with underlying
vector space $A_{\mathrm{vect}}$, the vectorial part
$W_0\leq O(A_{\mathrm{vect}})$ of $W$ is a finite reflection group, and
there exist a special point $x\in A$ and a possibly nondiscrete translation
subgroup $T$ such that $W=W_xT$, where
$W_x=\{w\in W\mid w(x)=x\}$ is the stabilizer of $x$.
A \emph{Euclidean building} $X$ modeled on $(A,W)$ is a space
with an apartment atlas satisfying axioms \textup{(EB1)}--\textup{(EB5)} of
that definition.
In particular, charts are stable under precomposition by $W$, any two points
lie in a common apartment, chart changes on apartment intersections are
restrictions of elements of $W$, and any two Weyl sectors contain subsectors
in a common apartment.  For every apartment $A'$ and every $y\in A'$, axiom
\textup{(EB5)} also supplies a distance-nonincreasing retraction
$r_{y,A'}:X\to A'$ such that
\[
 r_{y,A'}|_{A'}=\operatorname{id}_{A'},\qquad
 r_{y,A'}^{-1}(y)=\{y\}.
\]
The induced geodesic metric makes the chart images, called
\emph{apartments}, isometric copies of $A$. Thus a building is
assembled by gluing copies of its model apartment, which provide its local
coordinates.  In this paper, an \emph{extended Euclidean building} means a metric product
$X_{\mathrm{ess}}\times\mathbb R^m$ on whose Euclidean factor $W_0$ acts trivially;
$X_{\mathrm{ess}}$ is its \emph{essential}, or \emph{reduced}, building;
compare \cite[Remark~1.23]{RTW}.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:normsapartments}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space. A
\emph{non-Archimedean norm} on $V$ is a function
$h:V\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}$ such that $h(v)=0$ if and only if $v=0$ and, for
all $a\in K$ and $v,w\in V$,
\[
 h(av)=|a|h(v),\qquad h(v+w)\leq\max\{h(v),h(w)\}.
\]
A basis
$\mathbf e=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ is \emph{$h$-orthogonal} if
\[
 h\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^n a_je_j\right)
   =\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|a_j|h(e_j)
 \qquad\text{for all }a_1,\ldots,a_n\in K.
\]
An $h$-orthogonal basis $\mathbf e$ is \emph{$h$-orthonormal} if $h(e_j)=1$ for every
$j$.  The norm $h$ is \emph{split}, or \emph{diagonalizable}, if it
admits an orthogonal basis.  We write $\mathcal N(V)$ for all norms on $V$ and
$\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}(V)\subseteq\mathcal N(V)$ for the split norms.  For a fixed basis
$\mathbf e$, its \emph{apartment} is
\[
 \mathcal A_{\mathbf e}
 :=\{h_{\mathbf e,\mathbf w}\mid \mathbf w\in\mathbb R^n\}
 \subseteq\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}(V),
\]
where
\[
 h_{\mathbf e,\mathbf w}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^n a_je_j\right)
 :=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}e^{w_j}|a_j|.
\]
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
When $V=\{0\}$, the empty basis is orthogonal by convention,
$\mathcal N(V)=\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}(V)$ consists of the unique norm $h_\varnothing(0)=0$, and
the preceding coordinate formula is interpreted as this unique norm. We set
$d_\infty=d_2=0$, identify $\det V$ with $K$, choose determinant vector $1$
of norm one, and interpret empty suprema, metric sums, and maxima as zero.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:canonicalgeodesics}
For $h,k\in\mathcal N(V)$, the \emph{canonical geodesic} from $h$ to $k$ is
the unique constant-speed $d_2$-geodesic
$[h,k]:[0,1]\to\mathcal N(V)$.  If $h$ and $k$ are split, choose a common
apartment and write
$h=h_{\mathbf e,\mathbf u}$ and $k=h_{\mathbf e,\mathbf w}$.  Then
\[
 [h,k]_t=h_{\mathbf e,(1-t)\mathbf u+t\mathbf w},
 \qquad 0\leq t\leq1.
\]
This formula is independent of the chosen common apartment by uniqueness.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:adapted}
A subspace of a finite-dimensional split normed space over $K$ is split
\cite[Lemma~1.13]{BE}. The corresponding quotient, with its quotient norm, is
also split. Every prescribed flag of subspaces admits an adapted
orthogonal basis. A basis is \emph{adapted} to a flag if every member of
the flag is spanned by a subset of that basis.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Fix an orthogonal basis $(b_1,\ldots,b_n)$ of $V$. If
$v=\sum_jc_jb_j\ne0$, choose $p$ such that
$h(v)=|c_p|h(b_p)$. The basis obtained by replacing $b_p$ with $v$ is
orthogonal. Indeed, equip the replacement vector $v$ with its actual weight
$h(v)=|c_p|h(b_p)$. Both the resulting change-of-coordinate map between the
weighted max-normed coordinate spaces and its inverse are non-expansive because
$|c_j|h(b_j)\leq |c_p|h(b_p)$ for every $j$. Hence
\[
 V=Kv\mathbin{\widehat\oplus}V',
 \qquad V'=\operatorname{span}\{b_j\mid j\ne p\},
\]
is an orthogonal direct sum, meaning
$h(av+v')=\max\{|a|h(v),h(v')\}$ for $a\in K$ and $v'\in V'$.

Discard repetitions from the prescribed flag and argue by induction on
$\dim V$. Choose $v$ in its smallest nonzero member. Every later member
$F$ contains $v$, and the displayed decomposition gives
\[
 F=Kv\mathbin{\widehat\oplus}(F\cap V').
\]
The intersections with $V'$ form a flag in the lower-dimensional split
space $V'$. Applying the induction hypothesis there and adjoining $v$
produces an orthogonal basis adapted to the original flag. The assertion
for one subspace is the special case of the flag $\{0\}\subseteq W\subseteq V$.
For this flag, the images in $V/W$ of the complementary adapted basis
vectors form an orthogonal basis for the quotient norm.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:metrizations}
For a representation $E=(E_i,\rho(a))$ of $Q$, a \emph{norm on $E$} is a family
$h=(h_i)_{i\in Q_0}$ with $h_i\in\mathcal N(E_i)$ for every vertex $i$.  It is
\emph{split} if every $h_i$ is split, and it is a \emph{lattice
norm} if every $h_i$ admits an orthonormal basis.  Two norms
$h=(h_i)_i$ and $k=(k_i)_i$ are \emph{simultaneously homothetic} if there
exists a single positive real number $c$ such that
$k_i(v)=c\,h_i(v)$ for every $i\in Q_0$ and every $v\in E_i$; again
we denote the simultaneous-homothety class of $h$ by $[h]$.  We write
\[
\mathcal N(E)=\prod_{i\in Q_0}\mathcal N(E_i),\qquad
\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}(E)=\prod_{i\in Q_0}\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}(E_i),
\]
for the spaces of all norms and split norms, respectively.
The subspace $\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}(E)\subseteq\mathcal N(E)$ is called the \emph{split
locus}.  We write $\overline{\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}}(E)$ and $\overline{\mathcal N}(E)$ for the
respective quotients by simultaneous homothety and call
$\overline{\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}}(E)$ the \emph{quotient split locus}.

<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:gradedflagobject}
For a split normed representation $(E,\rho,h)$, define
\[
 \mathsf{GF}_{Q,K}(E,\rho,h)
 :=\left(
   (\operatorname{gr}_{h_i}E_i)_{i\in Q_0},
   (\mathcal F^-(\rho(a)),\mathcal F^+(\rho(a)))_{a\in Q_1}
 \right)\in\mathcal C_\star(Q).
\]
For a non-expansive intertwiner $g$, write
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  g=(g_i)&:(E,\rho,h)\longrightarrow(E',\rho',h'),\\
  \mathsf{GF}_{Q,K}(g)&=(\operatorname{gr} g_i)_{i\in Q_0},
  \qquad \operatorname{gr} g_i:\operatorname{gr}_{h_i}E_i\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_{h_i'}E_i'.
 \end{aligned}
\]
By \cite[Lemma~6.15]{HKKP}, the maps $\operatorname{gr} g_i$ preserve every incident filtration. This defines a functor
\[
 \mathsf{GF}_{Q,K}:\mathcal C^{\mathrm{sp}}(Q)\longrightarrow\mathcal C_\star(Q),
\]
and $\mathsf{GF}_{Q,K}(E,\rho,h)$ is the \emph{graded-flag object} of $(E,\rho,h)$.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:coreinterpretation}
Under HKKP's standing spherical-completeness hypothesis every
finite-dimensional norm is split \cite[Proposition~6.9]{HKKP}, and the functor
$\mathsf{GF}_{Q,K}$ is the one they denote by $\operatorname{Core}$.  There are no residual
arrow maps between the graded vertex spaces: each $\rho(a)$ is recorded by
its two singular filtrations.  The arrow maps need not be non-expansive;
non-expansiveness is required only of morphisms in $\mathcal C(Q)$.

For an arbitrary, possibly nonsplit norm, the same graded quotients can be
formed, but their total residual dimension is at most the original dimension,
with equality exactly for split norms.  Indeed, linearly independent
homogeneous initial forms lift to an orthogonal family; their number is at
most the original dimension, with equality exactly when the lifts form an
orthogonal basis.  Thus nonsplit norms can lose dimension under graded
reduction.  We reserve \emph{HKKP harmonic} for split
norms and use \emph{variationally harmonic} for minimizers in $\mathcal N(E)$. 
Split attainment is what connects these notions.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:monomialcertificate}
Let $B:(L^n,q_u)\to(L^m,q_w)$ be linear over a non-Archimedean field $L$. There are weighted isometries $C_s,C_t$ of the
source and target, respectively, such that
\[
 B C_s=C_tD,
\]
where $D$ has at most one nonzero entry in each row and each column, and has
exactly $\operatorname{rk} B$ nonzero entries. In the coordinates defined by $C_s,C_t$,
both singular filtrations are coordinate filtrations. Each nonzero entry
$d$ in position $(i,j)$ has singular value $\ln|d|+w_i-u_j$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
If $B=0$, take $C_s$ and $C_t$ to be the identity maps and $D=0$.
Otherwise, choose a nonzero entry attaining the maximum in~ 
  \hyperref[eq:weightedmatrixnorm]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:weightedmatrixnorm}}}}, in position $(i_0,j_0)$. For $j\ne j_0$, replace the source basis vector $e_j$ by
\[
 e_j-\frac{B_{i_0j}}{B_{i_0j_0}}e_{j_0}.
\]
Maximality gives $|B_{i_0j}/B_{i_0j_0}|e^{u_{j_0}}\leq e^{u_j}$, so this change and its inverse are non-expansive. Next, replace the target basis vector $f_{i_0}$ by
\[
 f_{i_0}+\sum_{i\ne i_0}\frac{B_{ij_0}}{B_{i_0j_0}}f_i.
\]
Again, maximality gives
$|B_{ij_0}/B_{i_0j_0}|e^{w_i}\leq e^{w_{i_0}}$ for every $i\ne i_0$, so
the displayed target-basis change and its inverse are non-expansive. Thus both
basis changes are weighted isometries. The remaining source and target
coordinate spans inherit the restricted weighted max norms, and the pivot row and column have
no other nonzero entries. The identical argument therefore applies
inductively to the complementary block, terminating when that block is zero.
There are at most $\min(m,n)$ pivots. On the resulting monomial matrix the
assertions about the singular filtrations follow directly from their
definitions: a source coordinate of singular value $\sigma$ belongs to
$\mathcal F^-(B)_{\leq\beta}$ exactly when $\sigma\leq\beta$, and the
corresponding target coordinate belongs to $\mathcal F^+(B)_{\geq\beta}$
exactly when $\sigma\geq\beta$. Kernel coordinates belong to every step of
the source filtration, and target coordinates outside the image belong to
none of the target steps. Equal singular values are grouped together.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:allnormprofile}
The functions $s_j$ in~

\label{lem:allnormconvexity}
Let $T:V\to W$ have rank $r$.  Each function $s_j(T;-,-)$ is convex along
canonical $d_2$-geodesics in $\mathcal N(V)\times\mathcal N(W)$.  Consequently, if

\]
is geodesically convex. If $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ is increasing and convex, then
$\sum_{j=1}^rf(\sigma_j(T;-,-))$ is geodesically convex.

and each fixed $0\ne\omega\in\det V$, the function
$h\mapsto\ln h_{\det}(\omega)$ is affine along canonical geodesics.  In particular, the continuous extension
$S_E:\mathcal N(E)\to\mathbb R$ is geodesically convex.

<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
First suppose that the endpoints are split and choose a common product
apartment, with source weights $u$ and target weights $w$. In its fixed
bases let $T_{I,J}$ denote a $j\times j$ minor of the matrix of $T$. The
weighted exterior operator norm gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partialminorformula}
 s_j(T;q_u,q_w)=
 \max_{\substack{|I|=|J|=j\\ \det T_{I,J}\ne0}}
 \left(\ln|\det T_{I,J}|+\sum_{i\in I}w_i-\sum_{k\in J}u_k\right).
\end{equation}
This is a finite maximum of affine functions, hence is convex along the
joining segment. With
$c_{r+1}=0$,
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^r c_j\sigma_j
 =\sum_{j=1}^r(c_j-c_{j+1})s_j,
\]
so the asserted linear combinations are convex.

Let $x_j,z_j$ be the decreasing singular-value lists at the endpoints, let
$y_j$ be the list at parameter $t$, and put
$w_j=(1-t)x_j+tz_j$.  Convexity of the partial sums gives
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^q y_j\leq\sum_{j=1}^q w_j,
 \qquad 1\leq q\leq r.
\]
Thus $y$ is weakly submajorized by $w$.  The majorization inequality for
increasing convex functions \cite[Chapter~3, C.1.b, p.~92]{MOA}, followed by
scalar convexity, gives, as in \cite[Lemma~6.27]{HKKP},
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^r f(y_j)
 \leq\sum_{j=1}^r f(w_j)
 \leq(1-t)\sum_{j=1}^r f(x_j)+t\sum_{j=1}^r f(z_j).
\]
The full determinant logarithms on $V$ and $W$ are affine in the apartment
coordinates.

For arbitrary endpoints $x_0,x_1$, choose split

 d_2\bigl(\gamma_n(t),\gamma(t)\bigr)
 \leq(1-t)d_2(x_0^{(n)},x_0)+t\,d_2(x_1^{(n)},x_1).
\]
Hence $\gamma_n(t)\to\gamma(t)$ for every $t$.
\hyperref[lem:allnormprofile]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:allnormprofile}} permits passage to the limit in the
convexity inequalities; continuity of determinant norms does the same for
the affine identity.  Summing proves convexity of $S_E$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:universalcoercivity}
Fix a finite quiver $Q$, a real weight $\theta=(\theta_i)_{i\in Q_0}$,
a weak ramp $f_a$ for each $a\in Q_1$, and a complete non-Archimedean
field $K$, possibly trivially valued. Let $E=((E_i)_i,\rho)$ be a

  \hyperref[eq:HKKPpotential]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:HKKPpotential}}}}, extended to $\mathcal N(E)$ by
\hyperref[lem:allnormprofile]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:allnormprofile}}. Since $\theta(E)=0$, it descends to
$\overline{\mathcal N}(E)$.

There exist constants $c>0$ and $C_0\in\mathbb R$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniformlinearcoercivity}
 S_E(h)\geq c\,\bar d_\infty([h],[h^0])-C_0
 \qquad\text{for every }h\in\mathcal N(E).
\end{equation}
Consequently, every sublevel of $S_E$ on $\overline{\mathcal N}(E)$ is bounded
for $\bar d_\infty$, equivalently for $\bar d_2$. The same holds for its
intersection with $\overline{\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}}(E)$.

Put $d_i=\dim E_i$ and $N=N_E$. If $N\leq1$, the

At least one arrow is nonzero: otherwise every line at every nonzero

$\theta_i>0$ at all nonzero vertices, contrary to $\theta(E)=0$.

Let
\[
 \mathscr D=\left\{e\in\mathbb Z^{Q_0}:
 0\leq e_i\leq d_i\text{ for every }i,\quad
 0<\sum_i e_i<N\right\},\qquad

\]
Stability excludes all vectors in $\mathscr B$ as subrepresentations.
Obtain $\varepsilon\in(0,1]$ from
\hyperref[lem:uniformforbiddenblock]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:uniformforbiddenblock}}.

For split $h$, choose common vertexwise orthogonal bases with $h^0$.
Since $h^0$ takes nonzero values in $|K^\times|$, scalar rescaling makes

For a nonzero arrow with matrix entries $c^a_{qp}$,

 \end{aligned}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:coercivitydeterminantbound}

\]

singular value equals $H$ and all other terms have their usual lower

\]

 \left|\bar d_\infty([h^{(n)}],[h^0])

 \leq d_\infty(h^{(n)},h)\longrightarrow0.
\]

comparison gives bounded sublevels.

\label{lem:variationaldirectsum}
If $h$ and $k$ minimize $S_E$ and $S_F$, respectively, then the vertexwise

Let $g=(g_i)\in\mathcal N(E\oplus F)$.  Write
$g|_E=(g_i|_{E_i})_i\in\mathcal N(E)$ for the restricted norm.  Under the
canonical identification $(E_i\oplus F_i)/E_i\simeq F_i$, write
$\bar g=g/E=(\bar g_i)_i\in\mathcal N(F)$ for the quotient norm on
$F\simeq(E\oplus F)/E$, where
$\bar g_i(f):=\inf_{e\in E_i}g_i(e,f)$ for $f\in F_i$.
In general $\bar g$ is not the restriction $g|_F$. Write
$A=\rho_E(a)$, $B=\rho_F(a)$, and $C=A\oplus B$ for a fixed arrow.

First suppose that the source and target norms of $g$ are split.
\hyperref[lem:adapted]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:adapted}} supplies orthogonal bases adapted to the relevant
copies of $E$, without changing the norms. In these bases the matrix of $C$

$F\simeq(E\oplus F)/E$, respectively. If
$r_A=\operatorname{rk} A$, $r_B=\operatorname{rk} B$, and $s_0=0$, combining maximizing minors in the

\[
 s_{p+q}(C;g)\geq s_p(A;g|_E)+s_q(B;\bar g),
 \qquad 0\leq p\leq r_A,\quad0\leq q\leq r_B.
\]
For each $j$, the sum of the $j$ largest entries in the union of the
singular-value vectors of $A$ and $B$ is the maximum of the right-hand side
over $p+q=j$. Hence that union is weakly submajorized by the singular-value
vector of $C$. These vectors have the same length because
$\operatorname{rk} C=r_A+r_B$. Since $f_a$ is increasing and convex,
\cite[Chapter~3, C.1.b, p.~92]{MOA} yields, with the quotient norm
$\bar g$ on $F\simeq(E\oplus F)/E$,
\[
 \sum_jf_a(\sigma_j(C;g))\geq
 \sum_jf_a(\sigma_j(A;g|_E))
 +\sum_jf_a(\sigma_j(B;\bar g)).
\]

$\bar g^{(n)}:=g^{(n)}/E\in\mathcal N(F)$, using
$F\simeq(E\oplus F)/E$. For each
$g^{(n)}$, \hyperref[lem:adapted]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:adapted}} supplies the adapted bases used above.
Restriction and quotient are non-expansive for $d_\infty$: inequalities
$e^{-\delta}g\leq g'\leq e^\delta g$ remain true after restriction and
after taking infima over quotient cosets. The following estimates give
convergence in $\mathcal N(E)$ and $\mathcal N(F)$, respectively, where
$F\simeq(E\oplus F)/E$ in the quotient term:
\[
 d_\infty(g^{(n)}|_E,g|_E)
 \leq d_\infty(g^{(n)},g)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 d_\infty(\bar g^{(n)},\bar g)
 \leq d_\infty(g^{(n)},g)\longrightarrow0.
\]
By \hyperref[lem:allnormprofile]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:allnormprofile}}, the arrow inequality therefore passes
to arbitrary $g$. Determinant norms are multiplicative in the restriction--quotient
sequence \cite[Corollary~2.10]{BE}; consequently, with $\bar g$ the quotient
norm on $F\simeq(E\oplus F)/E$,
\[
 S_{E\oplus F}(g)\geq S_E(g|_E)+S_F(\bar g).
\]

$h^{(n)}\oplus k^{(n)}$, every arrow is block diagonal between orthogonal
sums, so its singular-value multiset is the sorted union of the two summand
multisets and
\[
 S_{E\oplus F}(h^{(n)}\oplus k^{(n)})
 =S_E(h^{(n)})+S_F(k^{(n)}).
\]
The orthogonal sums converge to $h\oplus k$; continuity therefore gives the
same equality for the possibly nonsplit norms $h,k$. Thus, again viewing
$\bar g$ as the quotient norm on $F\simeq(E\oplus F)/E$,
\[
 S_{E\oplus F}(g)
 \geq S_E(g|_E)+S_F(\bar g)
 \geq S_E(h)+S_F(k)
 =S_{E\oplus F}(h\oplus k),
\]
which proves the claim.

\label{thm:universalvariational}
Fix a finite quiver $Q$, a complete non-Archimedean field $K$,
$\theta\in\mathbb R^{Q_0}$, weak ramps $f=(f_a)_{a\in Q_1}$, and a
finite-dimensional representation $E$ of $Q$ over $K$.  For fixed nonzero

 &\Longrightarrow E\text{ admits a variationally harmonic norm for }(\theta,f)\\
 &\Longrightarrow E\text{ is $\theta$-semistable}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the zero representation, the unique norm minimizes the constant
potential, so the assertions are immediate. Assume henceforth that $E\ne0$.

\hyperref[lem:quotientcompletion]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:quotientcompletion}}. If $E$ is $\theta$-stable,

continuous, and convex, so Ba\v{c}\'ak's minimizer theorem
\cite[Lemma~2.2.19, p.~42]{Bacak} gives a global minimizer. If $E$ is
$\theta$-polystable, apply this argument to its $\theta$-stable summands and use
\hyperref[lem:variationaldirectsum]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:variationaldirectsum}}; a simultaneous rescaling places the
result in $\mathscr S_E$. Continuity and geodesic convexity show, for every
representation $E$ with $\theta(E)=0$, that its set of minimizers is closed and convex whenever it
is nonempty.

Simultaneous rescaling forces $\theta(E)=0$.
Let $U\subseteq E$ be a subrepresentation, choose vertexwise complements
$E_i=U_i\oplus B_i$, and choose bases of the two summands. Let $q_{i,t}$
be the split norm assigning weight $e^{-t}$ to the chosen basis of $U_i$
and weight $1$ to that of $B_i$, for $t\geq0$. In these adapted bases
every arrow has block matrix
\[
 \rho(a)=\begin{pmatrix}A_a&C_a\\0&D_a\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, for every nonzero arrow,
$\sigma_{a,1}(q_t)\leq\sigma_{a,1}(q_0)$. Since the singular values are
decreasing and $f_a$ is increasing, its contribution is bounded by
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{r_a}f_a(\sigma_{a,j}(q_t))
 \leq r_af_a(\sigma_{a,1}(q_0)),
 \qquad r_a=\operatorname{rk}\rho(a),
\]
uniformly in $t$; zero arrows contribute zero. Moreover, the product of
the basis weights gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shrinkingdeterminant}
 \ell_{E_i}(q_{i,t})=\ell_{E_i}(q_{i,0})-t\dim U_i.
\end{equation}
Thus, for a constant $C_0$ independent of $t$,
\[
 S_E(q_t)\leq C_0+t\theta(U).
\]
If $\theta(U)<0$, this contradicts $S_E(q_t)\geq S_E(h)$ for large $t$.
The comparison ray need not start at $h$, so the argument also applies
when $h$ is nonsplit. Therefore $E$ is $\theta$-semistable.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:splitfirstvariation}
Let $k\in\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}(E)$, put $C=\mathsf{GF}_{Q,K}(E,\rho,k)$, and let $\mathcal G$
be a finite exhaustive separated increasing filtration of its vertex
spaces, compatible with their gradings, with jumps
$\beta_1<\cdots<\beta_m$. We call $\mathcal G$ the \emph{direction filtration},
distinguishing it from the incident singular filtrations of $C$.
Lift homogeneous bases adapted to $\mathcal G$
to orthogonal bases over $K$ and multiply the norm of a vector of direction
weight $\beta$ by $e^{t\beta}$.  The resulting apartment geodesic $k_t$
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:splitfirstvariation}
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0+}S_E(k_t)
 =-\sum_{j=1}^m\beta_j\,
       \theta^\star\!\left(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal G}_{\beta_j}C\right).
\end{equation}
Direction subobjects carry the incident filtrations by intersection,
and successive quotients carry the induced quotient filtrations.

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
The orthogonal lifting is justified in
\hyperref[rem:coreinterpretation]{Remark~\ref*{rem:coreinterpretation}}. For any finite filtered graded
space $(H,\mathcal G)$, put
\[
 D_{\mathcal G}(H)=\sum_\beta\beta\dim\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal G}_\beta H.
\]
For graded subspaces $H_1\subseteq H_2\subseteq H$, use the intersected
filtration on $H_2$ and the induced quotient filtration
\[
 \mathcal G_{\leq\beta}(H_2/H_1)
 =\bigl((H_2\cap\mathcal G_{\leq\beta}H)+H_1\bigr)/H_1.
\]
The corresponding formula with $<\beta$ defines the strict step.
Thus $D_{\mathcal G}$ is additive for the induced
restriction--quotient filtrations.

Fix a nonzero arrow map $T=\rho(a):V\to W$ and write
$\overline V=\operatorname{gr}_kV$, $\overline W=\operatorname{gr}_kW$. Let $\mathcal G_s$ and
$\mathcal G_t$ denote the direction filtrations on $\overline V$ and
$\overline W$, respectively. List the distinct finite singular values of
$T$ as $\lambda_1>\cdots>\lambda_q$. Let $n_l$ be the
cumulative multiplicity through $\lambda_l$, and set
\[
 A_l=\mathcal F^-(T)_{<\lambda_l},\qquad
 B_l=\mathcal F^+(T)_{\geq\lambda_l}.
\]
Both $\overline V/A_l$ and $B_l$ have dimension $n_l$.
By  
  \hyperref[eq:partialminorformula]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:partialminorformula}}}}, $s_j(T;k_t)$ is a finite maximum
of affine functions.  Its right derivative is the largest slope of an
active minor, namely one attaining the maximum at $t=0$.

At $j=n_l$, the leading exterior map has rank one.  Indeed, in the
monomial form of \hyperref[lem:monomialcertificate]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:monomialcertificate}}, the unique
largest exterior monomial uses the first $n_l$ entries: this index is a
boundary between distinct singular-value groups.  After scaling the
target exterior norm by $e^{-s_{n_l}(T;k)}$, its graded map has source
factor $\det(\overline V/A_l)^\vee$ and target factor $\det B_l$.
This scaling is a real regrading: for the target exterior norm $p$ and
$s=s_{n_l}(T;k)$, its component at degree $\alpha$ is
\[
 \{e^{-s}p\leq e^\alpha\}/\{e^{-s}p<e^\alpha\}
 =\{p\leq e^{\alpha+s}\}/\{p<e^{\alpha+s}\}.
\]
Thus the target grading shifts by $-s$, while its direction filtration
and Pl\"ucker support are unchanged.  This normalization is valid for
every real $s$ and requires no scalar in $K$.
This description is invariant under weighted isometries; at $n_q=\operatorname{rk} T$
it follows also from the rank-one full exterior map.

The active minors are therefore products of the nonzero Pl\"ucker
coordinates of these factors.  The largest target weight sum is
$D_{\mathcal G_t}(B_l)$; the smallest source weight sum is
$D_{\mathcal G_s}(\overline V/A_l)$.  To justify the first assertion,
consider any subspace $B$ in a coordinate space with direction weights
$\beta_j$.  A basis of $B$ adapted to the intersected filtration has
weights $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_{\dim B}$; each basis vector has
coordinates only in steps of weight at most its $\gamma_j$.  Hence every
nonzero Pl\"ucker coordinate of $B$ has weight at most
$\sum_j\gamma_j=D_{\mathcal G}(B)$.  An echelon choice of basis in the
successive coordinate blocks gives a nonzero coordinate attaining this
weight.  For the source factor, the nonzero Pl\"ucker coordinates of
$\det(\overline V/A_l)^\vee$ are indexed by complements of the nonzero
Pl\"ucker coordinates of $A_l$.  Their smallest source weight is therefore
\[
 D_{\mathcal G_s}(\overline V)-D_{\mathcal G_s}(A_l)
 =D_{\mathcal G_s}(\overline V/A_l),
\]
by additivity for the induced quotient filtration.  Since the leading
exterior map has rank one, each nonzero source coordinate can be paired
with each nonzero target coordinate.  Taking the largest active slope
therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partialsumfirstvariation}
 s'_{n_l}(0+)=D_{\mathcal G_t}(B_l)
                  -D_{\mathcal G_s}(\overline V/A_l).
\end{equation}

Put $c_l=f_a'(\lambda_l)$, $c_{q+1}=0$.  Differentiating
$\sigma_j=s_j-s_{j-1}$ and grouping equal singular values gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left(\sum_jf_a(\sigma_j(t))\right)'_{0+}
 &=\sum_l(c_l-c_{l+1})s'_{n_l}(0+)\\
 &=\sum_\lambda f_a'(\lambda)
 \bigl(D_{\mathcal G_t}(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal F^+}_\lambda\overline W)-D_{\mathcal G_s}(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal F^-}_\lambda\overline V)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second equality follows by telescoping
 
  \hyperref[eq:partialsumfirstvariation]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:partialsumfirstvariation}}}}.  Only finite singular jumps
contribute; a zero arrow contributes an empty sum.
For clarity, the interchange of the incident and direction filtrations
can be seen on a single graded vertex space $H$.  At a finite jump
$\lambda$ of an incident filtration $\mathcal F$, put
\[
 (F_0,F_1)=
 \begin{cases}
  (\mathcal F_{\leq\lambda}H,\mathcal F_{<\lambda}H),
       &\text{if $\mathcal F$ is increasing},\\
  (\mathcal F_{\geq\lambda}H,\mathcal F_{>\lambda}H),
       &\text{if $\mathcal F$ is decreasing}.
 \end{cases}
\]
On $F_0/F_1$, the direction steps are the induced quotient steps
defined above.  On $\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal G}_\beta H$, the incident steps
corresponding to $F_j$, $j=0,1$, are
\[
 \bigl((F_j\cap\mathcal G_{\leq\beta}H)
                  +\mathcal G_{<\beta}H\bigr)
                  /\mathcal G_{<\beta}H.
\]
Consequently both orders of taking graded pieces have the common
quotient
\[
 \operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal G}_\beta(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal F}_\lambda H)
 \simeq
 \frac{F_0\cap\mathcal G_{\leq\beta}H}
 {(F_1\cap\mathcal G_{\leq\beta}H)
                    +(F_0\cap\mathcal G_{<\beta}H)}
 \simeq
 \operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal F}_\lambda(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal G}_\beta H).
\]
Their dimensions therefore agree for the source increasing and target
decreasing singular filtrations.  Adding the determinant derivative
$-\sum_i\theta_iD_{\mathcal G_i}(\operatorname{gr}_{k_i}E_i)$ and using
 
  \hyperref[eq:coredegree]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:coredegree}}}} proves  
  \hyperref[eq:splitfirstvariation]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:splitfirstvariation}}}}.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:splitcriterionallfields}
Fix a finite quiver $Q$, a complete non-Archimedean field $K$,
$\theta\in\mathbb R^{Q_0}$, weak ramps $(f_a)_{a\in Q_1}$, and a
finite-dimensional representation $E$ over $K$.  Choose
$0\ne\omega_i\in\det E_i$ and let $S_E$ be the resulting potential.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item A split norm is HKKP harmonic if and only if it globally minimizes
$S_E$ on the split locus; equivalently, it minimizes its continuous
extension on all norms.  The split harmonic locus is geodesically convex.
\item If $E$ admits a split harmonic norm, then $E$ is
$\theta$-semistable.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
For part~(i), let $C=\mathsf{GF}_{Q,K}(E,\rho,k)$ be semistable.
Since $\theta^\star(C)=0$, summation by parts in
 
  \hyperref[eq:splitfirstvariation]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:splitfirstvariation}}}} gives
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0+}S_E(k_t)
 =\sum_{j<m}(\beta_{j+1}-\beta_j)
        \theta^\star(\mathcal G_{\leq\beta_j}C)\geq0.
\]
Every split competitor lies with $k$ in a common product apartment;
its joining segment has a direction filtration given by the coordinate
slopes.  Convexity and the nonnegative initial derivative prove minimality
on the split locus.  Density and continuity extend it to all norms.

Conversely, minimality on the split locus forces $\theta(E)=0$ by
simultaneous scaling in both directions.  Thus $\theta^\star(C)=0$ by
\hyperref[lem:coretotaldegree]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:coretotaldegree}}.  For a strict subobject $U\subseteq C$,
the direction with weight $-1$ on $U$ and $0$ on $C/U$ has derivative
$\theta^\star(U)$ by the lemma.  Minimality makes it nonnegative, proving
semistability of $C$.  Minimization on all norms implies minimization on
the split locus, so both equivalences follow.
Split endpoints have a split joining segment; convexity makes the
segment between minimizers a segment of minimizers.  Part~(i) is therefore
independent of \hyperref[thm:universalvariational]{Theorem~\ref*{thm:universalvariational}}.

For part~(ii), part~(i) makes a split harmonic norm a global minimizer
on all norms, and the converse implication of
\hyperref[thm:universalvariational]{Theorem~\ref*{thm:universalvariational}} gives $\theta$-semistability.

<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
\label{def:realgradedmodules}
The \emph{real-graded residue field} of $K$ is
\[
 \mathcal R=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb R}\mathcal R_\alpha,
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_\alpha=
 \{x\in K\mid |x|\leq e^\alpha\}/
 \{x\in K\mid |x|<e^\alpha\}.
\]
Multiplication is induced by multiplication in $K$. Thus
$\mathcal R_0=\widetilde K$, and every nonzero homogeneous element is invertible:
the initial form of $x^{-1}$ is the inverse of the initial form of
$x\ne0$. Here \emph{graded field} refers to the invertibility of nonzero homogeneous
elements, not of arbitrary nonzero elements of the underlying ring.
The component $\mathcal R_\alpha$ is zero if $\alpha\notin\Gamma_K$ and
one-dimensional over $\widetilde K$ otherwise.

For a norm $p$ on a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space $V$, put
\[
 M_p(V)=\operatorname{gr}_p^{\mathbb R}V
 :=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb R}
 \{v\in V\mid p(v)\leq e^\alpha\}/
 \{v\in V\mid p(v)<e^\alpha\}.
\]
Scalar multiplication makes this a graded $\mathcal R$-module. A homogeneous
$\mathcal R$-linear map of degree $\eta$ sends the component of degree $\alpha$
to that of degree $\alpha+\eta$; its space is denoted
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal R}^\eta$. A graded module is \emph{graded free of finite
rank} if it has a finite homogeneous basis, and $\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R}$ denotes the number
of basis vectors. All submodules and module morphisms below are graded,
with morphisms of degree zero unless a degree is specified.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lem:realgradedlinearalgebra}
Graded submodules and quotients of a finite-rank graded free $\mathcal R$-module
are graded free, and every graded submodule has a homogeneous complement.
Graded rank is additive in exact sequences and is zero only for the zero
module. If $p$ is a split norm on a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space
$V$, then $M_p(V)$ has graded rank $\dim_KV$, and every
homogeneous basis of $M_p(V)$ lifts to a $p$-orthogonal basis of $V$.

If $p,q$ are split norms on finite-dimensional $K$-vector spaces $V,W$,
respectively, then, for every
$\eta\in\mathbb R$, taking initial forms gives an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradedhomidentification}
 \bigl(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R}_{\|\cdot\|_{p\to q}}
          \operatorname{Hom}_K(V,W)\bigr)_\eta
 \simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal R}^\eta(M_p(V),M_q(W)).
\end{equation}
These identifications respect addition and composition in the specified
degrees.
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Homogeneous Gaussian elimination works because every nonzero homogeneous
coefficient is invertible. More explicitly, a nonzero homogeneous vector
in a graded submodule has an invertible coefficient in some homogeneous
basis coordinate. Use that coordinate as a pivot, subtract homogeneous
multiples to clear it, and continue in the remaining coordinates. Induction
on the ambient rank gives a homogeneous basis of the submodule extending
to one of the whole module. It also proves the quotient and rank assertions.

The initial forms of a $p$-orthogonal basis form a homogeneous $\mathcal R$-basis
of $M_p(V)$, by the maximum formula for $p$. Conversely, lift a homogeneous
basis to vectors in $V$ of the corresponding norms. In any nonzero linear
combination of the lifts, let $e^\alpha$ be the largest norm of its terms.
The initial forms of those terms in degree $\alpha$ have nonzero sum by
graded linear independence. The combination therefore has norm exactly
$e^\alpha$. This proves orthogonality and linear independence; there are
$\dim_KV$ lifts, so they form a basis of $V$.

If $V$ or $W$ is zero, both Hom spaces are zero. Otherwise choose
orthogonal bases of $V,W$ with logarithmic weights $a_j,c_k$.
For a matrix $(t_{kj})$, its operator norm is
$\max_{k,j}|t_{kj}|e^{c_k-a_j}$. A matrix of norm at most $e^\eta$
therefore has degree-$\eta$ initial coefficient $(k,j)$ in
$\mathcal R_{\eta+a_j-c_k}$. These are exactly the
coefficients of a degree-$\eta$ graded map. Every such finite matrix lifts
coefficientwise to $K$, and its initial form is zero exactly when its
operator norm is strictly less than $e^\eta$. Matrix addition and
multiplication induce the asserted compatibility with addition and
composition.

\label{def:radiuslabelledreduction}
Let $\mathscr Q$ be a finite quiver, let $X$ be a finite-dimensional
representation of $\mathscr Q$, with arrow maps
\[
 \rho_X(e):X_{s(e)}\longrightarrow X_{t(e)},
 \qquad e\in\mathscr Q_1,
\]
and fix a label function
\[
 b:\mathscr Q_1\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
 \qquad e\longmapsto b_e.
\]
A norm $p=(p_v)_{v\in\mathscr Q_0}$ of $X$ is \emph{$b$-bounded} if,
for all $e\in\mathscr Q_1$ and $x\in X_{s(e)}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:labelledbound}
 p_{t(e)}(\rho_X(e)x)\leq e^{b_e}p_{s(e)}(x).
\end{equation}
The label $b_e$ is logarithmic; the corresponding operator-norm bound is
$e^{b_e}$.
Let $\mathcal A_b$ be the category whose objects consist of finite-rank
graded free $\mathcal R$-modules $Y_v$ at the vertices and homogeneous maps
$Y_{s(e)}\to Y_{t(e)}$ of degree $b_e$ at the arrows. Morphisms are
degree-zero vertexwise maps intertwining all arrows.
If $p$ is split and $b$-bounded, its \emph{radius-labeled graded reduction}
$\operatorname{gr}_p^bX\in\mathcal A_b$ has vertex modules $M_{p_v}(X_v)$ and arrow maps
\[
 \operatorname{gr}_p^{b_e}(\rho_X(e)):
 (M_{p_{s(e)}}(X_{s(e)}))_\alpha\longrightarrow
 (M_{p_{t(e)}}(X_{t(e)}))_{\alpha+b_e},
 \qquad \alpha\in\mathbb R,
\]
induced by $\rho_X(e)$ on the displayed quotients. The bound
 
  \hyperref[eq:labelledbound]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:labelledbound}}}} makes these maps well-defined, including on
the strict sublevel quotients. The grading retains every real degree; no
scalar identifications between different radii are chosen.

Fix $\kappa=(\kappa_v)_{v\in\mathscr Q_0}\in\mathbb R^{\mathscr Q_0}$ with
\[
 \kappa(X):=\sum_{v\in\mathscr Q_0}\kappa_v\dim X_v=0.
\]
Our sign convention for $X$ is that it is
\emph{$\kappa$-semistable} when
\[
 \kappa(U):=\sum_{v\in\mathscr Q_0}\kappa_v\dim U_v\leq0
\]
for every subrepresentation $U\subseteq X$. For $Y\in\mathcal A_b$, put
\[
 d(Y):=\sum_{v\in\mathscr Q_0}\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R}Y_v,
 \qquad \kappa(Y):=\sum_{v\in\mathscr Q_0}\kappa_v\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R}Y_v.
\]
An object $Y$ with $\kappa(Y)=0$ is $\kappa$-semistable if
$\kappa(U)\leq0$ for every graded subrepresentation $U\subseteq Y$.

<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
\label{def:gradedslope}
For a nonzero object $Y\in\mathcal A_b$, its \emph{slope} and
\emph{maximal slope} are
\[
 \mu(Y):=\frac{\kappa(Y)}{d(Y)},\qquad
 \mu_{\max}(Y):=
 \max_{\{0\}\ne U\subseteq Y}\mu(U).
\]
The maximum exists: the vertex graded ranks of $U$ are bounded integers,
so only finitely many slopes occur. We call $Y$ slope-semistable if every
nonzero subobject has slope at most $\mu(Y)$. When $\kappa(Y)=0$, this is
exactly $\kappa$-semistability. The zero object is $\kappa$-semistable; no
slope is assigned to it.

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{lem:maximalslope}
Every nonzero $Y\in\mathcal A_b$ has a unique largest subobject $A(Y)$
of slope $\mu_{\max}(Y)$. It is slope-semistable, every nonzero quotient
of $A(Y)$ has slope at least $\mu_{\max}(Y)$, and every nonzero subobject
of $Y/A(Y)$ has strictly smaller slope.
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Put $\mu_0=\mu_{\max}(Y)$ and choose a subobject $A$ of slope $\mu_0$
having largest total rank. For a subobject $U\subseteq Y$, put
$\nu(U)=\kappa(U)-\mu_0d(U)$, including $\nu(\{0\})=0$.
Then $\nu(U)\leq0$. If $C$ also has slope $\mu_0$, additivity gives
\[
 \nu(A+C)=\nu(A)+\nu(C)-\nu(A\cap C)
          =-\nu(A\cap C)\geq0.
\]
Hence $A+C$ has slope $\mu_0$. Maximality of $d(A)$ forces $A+C=A$,
so $C\subseteq A$. Thus $A=A(Y)$ is unique and largest, and its
slope-semistability follows from the definition of $\mu_0$.
For a nonzero quotient $I$ of $A$, additivity and the slope bound for its
kernel give $\kappa(I)\geq\mu_0d(I)$.

If a nonzero subobject of $Y/A$ had slope at least $\mu_0$, its inverse
image in $Y$ would have slope at least $\mu_0$ and rank strictly larger
than $d(A)$. A strictly larger slope contradicts maximality of $\mu_0$;
equality contradicts the choice of $A$. This proves the strict quotient
bound.

\label{lem:radiuslangton}
Suppose that $X$ admits a split $b$-bounded norm.  Then $X$ is
$\kappa$-semistable if and only if it admits a split $b$-bounded norm $p$
for which $\operatorname{gr}_p^bX$ is $\kappa$-semistable.

<!-- proof-17: body_md -->

subrepresentation.  \hyperref[lem:adapted]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:adapted}} makes the restricted norm on

\[
 \kappa(U)=\kappa(\operatorname{gr}_{p|_U}^bU)\leq0.
\]

Conversely, suppose that $X$ is $\kappa$-semistable and choose a split

arbitrary real labels, of the modification used in
\cite[Proposition~7.4]{HKKP}.
We first adjust the orthogonal lifts by a shear so that rescaling at the
residual radius produces a nonsplit reversed extension. We then compare
maximal-slope subobjects to prove termination.

 \rho_X(e)=

 \end{pmatrix}.

 \begin{gathered}

 \end{gathered}

Each block has operator norm at most $e^{b_e}$.  Since $A_0$ is a graded

 \end{aligned}

Thus $\gamma\in C^1$.

 \begin{aligned}
 D:C^0&\longrightarrow C^1,\\
 c&\longmapsto
 \bigl(d_ec_{s(e)}-c_{t(e)}a_e\bigr)_{e\in\mathscr Q_1}.
 \end{aligned}

Give $C^0$ its operator-norm filtration and the $e$-summand of $C^1$ the
filtration defined by $e^{-b_e}\|\cdot\|$, retaining all real degrees.
In the lifted homogeneous bases,
a matrix unit from an $A$-basis vector of grade $\alpha$ to a $B$-basis
vector of grade $\beta$ has degree $\beta-\alpha$ in $C^0$, and degree
$\beta-\alpha-b_e$ in the $e$-summand of $C^1$. Consequently,
by

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R} C^0)_\eta
 &\simeq\bigoplus_v\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal R}^\eta((A_0)_v,(B_0)_v),\\
 (\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R} C^1)_\eta
 &\simeq\bigoplus_e\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal R}^{\eta+b_e}
                ((A_0)_{s(e)},(B_0)_{t(e)}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
In particular, degree $-\delta$ in the first line is the space of
degree-zero vertexwise maps $A_0\to T_\delta B_0$, while degree
$-\delta$ in the second is the space of degree-$b_e$ arrowwise maps
to $T_\delta B_0$. If $\bar a_e,\bar d_e$ denote the arrow maps of $A_0,B_0$,
then
\[
 (\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R}_{-\delta}D)(\bar c)_e
 =\bar d_e\bar c_{s(e)}-\bar c_{t(e)}\bar a_e.
\]
Hence
\[
 \ker(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R}_{-\delta}D)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_b}(A_0,T_\delta B_0).
\]
Grade translation preserves slopes. A nonzero morphism
$A_0\to T_\delta B_0$ would have an image that is both a nonzero quotient
of $A_0$, of slope at least $\mu_0$, and a nonzero subobject of
$T_\delta B_0$, of slope strictly less than $\mu_0$. Thus
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_b}(A_0,T_\delta B_0)=\{0\}.
\]

The block bounds give $\|D(c)\|_{1,b}\leq\|c\|_0$. If this inequality were
strict for some nonzero $c$, put $\delta=-\ln\|c\|_0$. Since $C^0$ is
split, $c$ has a nonzero initial form
$\bar c\in(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R} C^0)_{-\delta}$, while strictness would give
$(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R}_{-\delta}D)(\bar c)=0$, contradicting the preceding vanishing. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coboundaryisometry}
 \|D(c)\|_{1,b}=\|c\|_0
 \qquad\text{for all }c\in C^0.
\end{equation}

By  
  \hyperref[eq:coboundaryisometry]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:coboundaryisometry}}}}, $D$ is an isometric embedding.  The
chosen orthogonal bases make $C^0$ and $C^1$ split, and
\hyperref[lem:adapted]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:adapted}} gives an orthogonal complement to
$\operatorname{im}D$ in $C^1$.  Projection along this complement, followed
by $D^{-1}$, gives a $K$-linear map
\[
 P:C^1\longrightarrow C^0,
 \qquad \|P\|\leq1,
 \qquad PD=\operatorname{id}_{C^0}.
\]

Define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q:C^0&\longrightarrow C^1,\\
 c&\longmapsto
 \bigl(c_{t(e)}\beta_ec_{s(e)}\bigr)_{e\in\mathscr Q_1}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since $\max_{e\in\mathscr Q_1} e^{-b_e}\|\beta_e\|\leq1$, one has
\[
 \|Q(c)\|_{1,b}\leq\|c\|_0^2
\]
and
\[
 \|Q(c)-Q(c')\|_{1,b}
 \leq
 \max\{\|c\|_0,\|c'\|_0\}\,\|c-c'\|_0.
\]
It follows that the map
\[
 \Phi:C^0\longrightarrow C^0,
 \qquad c\longmapsto-P\gamma+PQ(c),
\]
preserves the complete ball
\[
 \mathbb B_q=\{c\in C^0\mid\|c\|_0\leq q\}
\]
and is $q$-Lipschitz there, with $q<1$.  The Banach fixed-point theorem
therefore gives a unique fixed point $c\in\mathbb B_q$.

For each vertex define the automorphism
\[
 S_v:A_v\oplus B_v\longrightarrow A_v\oplus B_v,
 \qquad
 S_v=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \operatorname{id}_{A_v}&0\\
  c_v&\operatorname{id}_{B_v}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since $\|c_v\|<1$, both $S_v$ and $S_v^{-1}$ are isometries.  In the new
orthogonal decompositions,
\[
 S_{t(e)}^{-1}\rho_X(e)S_{s(e)}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
  a_e+\beta_ec_{s(e)}&\beta_e\\
  z_e&d_e-c_{t(e)}\beta_e
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
where
\[
 z_e=\gamma_e+D(c)_e-c_{t(e)}\beta_ec_{s(e)}.
\]
Since $PD=\operatorname{id}_{C^0}$, the fixed-point equation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residualslice}
 Pz=P\gamma+PD(c)-PQ(c)=P\gamma+c-PQ(c)=0.
\end{equation}
Moreover
\[
 \|z\|_{1,b}
 \leq\max\{\|\gamma\|_{1,b},\|D(c)\|_{1,b},\|Q(c)\|_{1,b}\}
 \leq\max\{q,q,q^2\}=q.
\]
If $z=0$, the subspaces $S_v(A_v\oplus\{0\})$ form a $K$-subrepresentation of
$X$ of weight $\kappa(A_0)>0$, contradicting semistability. Hence
\[
 0<r:=\|z\|_{1,b}\leq q<1.
\]

Put $\lambda=-\ln r>0$, write
$p_{A_v}=p_v|_{A_v}$ and $p_{B_v}=p_v|_{B_v}$, and, for each
$v\in\mathscr Q_0$, define a new split norm by
\[
 p'_v\bigl(S_v(x,y)\bigr)
 =\max\{p_{A_v}(x),r^{-1}p_{B_v}(y)\},
 \qquad\text{for all }(x,y)\in A_v\oplus B_v.
\]
Put
\[
 a'_e=a_e+\beta_ec_{s(e)},\qquad
 d'_e=d_e-c_{t(e)}\beta_e.
\]
Because $\|c\|_0<1$, the perturbations $\beta_ec_{s(e)}$ and
$c_{t(e)}\beta_e$ have normalized norm strictly less than one. With respect
to $p'$,
\[
 e^{-b_e}\|a'_e\|_{p_{A_{s(e)}}\to p_{A_{t(e)}}}\leq1,\qquad
 e^{-b_e}\|d'_e\|_{p_{B_{s(e)}}\to p_{B_{t(e)}}}\leq1,
\]
\[
 e^{-b_e}\|\beta_e\|_{r^{-1}p_{B_{s(e)}}\to p_{A_{t(e)}}}\leq r<1,
 \qquad
 e^{-b_e}\|z_e\|_{p_{A_{s(e)}}\to r^{-1}p_{B_{t(e)}}}\leq1.
\]
Thus $p'$ is $b$-bounded. Scaling each $B_v$-norm by
$r^{-1}=e^\lambda$ replaces $B_0$ by $T_\lambda B_0$. The $\beta_e$-blocks
have zero degree-$b_e$ reduction, the diagonal reductions remain
$\bar a_e$ and the canonically reindexed $\bar d_e$, and at least one
$\bar z_e$ is nonzero because the
finite weighted maximum defining $r$ is attained. Write
$\bar z=(\bar z_e)_e\in(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R} C^1)_{-\lambda}$. By the preceding
Hom identifications, these are exactly the degree-$b_e$ maps from $A_0$
to $T_\lambda B_0$. Hence, relative to
$A_0\oplus T_\lambda B_0$,
\[
 \operatorname{gr}_{p'}^b\rho_X(e)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \bar a_e&0\\
  \bar z_e&\bar d_e
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
and $Y':=\operatorname{gr}_{p'}^bX$ fits into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reversedradiusextension}
 0\longrightarrow T_\lambda B_0
 \longrightarrow Y'
 \longrightarrow A_0
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

Both $D$ and $P$ preserve their norm filtrations, so they induce
degree-zero maps of the real-graded modules. Functoriality of
associated grading carries $PD=\operatorname{id}$ to the same identity on
the associated graded modules. At degree $-\lambda=\ln r$, write these maps as
\[
 \overline D:(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R} C^0)_{-\lambda}\longrightarrow(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R} C^1)_{-\lambda},
 \qquad
 \overline P:(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R} C^1)_{-\lambda}\longrightarrow(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathbb R} C^0)_{-\lambda},
\]
with
\[
 \overline P\,\overline D=\operatorname{id},
 \qquad
 \overline P(\bar z)=0
\]
by $PD=\operatorname{id}$ and  
  \hyperref[eq:residualslice]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:residualslice}}}}. Here $\overline P$ is a
map of the graded Hom spaces; it need not intertwine quiver arrows.
A splitting
of  
  \hyperref[eq:reversedradiusextension]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:reversedradiusextension}}}} would be the graph of a homogeneous
vertexwise map $\overline h:A_0\to T_\lambda B_0$. The intertwining
equations for that graph are
\[
 \bar z+\overline D(\overline h)=0.
\]
Applying $\overline P$ gives $\overline h=0$, hence $\bar z=0$,
contradicting the choice of $r$. Therefore the extension is nonsplit.

We now prove termination by an elementary slope comparison. Write
$B'=T_\lambda B_0$. For a nonzero subobject $C\subseteq Y'$, let
$C_B=C\cap B'$ and let $I$ be the image of $C$ in $A_0$. The exact sequence
$0\to C_B\to C\to I\to0$ gives
\[
 \kappa(C)-\mu_0d(C)
 =\bigl(\kappa(C_B)-\mu_0d(C_B)\bigr)
  +\bigl(\kappa(I)-\mu_0d(I)\bigr)\leq0.
\]
The first summand is strictly negative if $C_B\ne\{0\}$; the second is
nonpositive because $I\subseteq A_0$. Consequently
$\mu_{\max}(Y')\leq\mu_0$. If equality holds, apply this calculation to
$C=A(Y')$. It forces $C_B=\{0\}$, so $C$ injects into $A_0$ and
$d(C)\leq d(A_0)$. Equality of these ranks would make the image all of
$A_0$: the quotient would have rank zero. The inverse of $C\to A_0$,
followed by $C\subseteq Y'$, would then split
 
  \hyperref[eq:reversedradiusextension]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:reversedradiusextension}}}}, a contradiction. Hence either
\[
 \mu_{\max}(Y')<\mu_{\max}(Y),\qquad\text{or}\qquad
 \mu_{\max}(Y')=\mu_{\max}(Y)\ \text{and}\ d(A(Y'))<d(A(Y)).
\]
Thus the pair $(\mu_{\max}(Y),d(A(Y)))$ strictly decreases in lexicographic
order at every nonterminal modification. Its possible values form a
finite set: the vertex graded-rank vector of $A(Y)$ lies in
\[
 \prod_{v\in\mathscr Q_0}\{0,1,\ldots,\dim X_v\},
\]
and both its slope and total rank depend only on that nonzero vector,
not on the real grades. This remains a finite set for arbitrary real
weights $\kappa_v$. Iteration therefore
terminates at a split $b$-bounded norm whose radius-labeled graded
reduction is $\kappa$-semistable.

\label{lem:labelleddetminimum}
Let $p$ be a split $b$-bounded norm for which $\operatorname{gr}_p^bX$ is

 J_\kappa(q)\geq J_\kappa(p)
\]

<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
If $X$ is zero, the assertion is immediate. First suppose that $q$ is
split. At each vertex choose a basis orthogonal for both $p_v$ and $q_v$
\cite[Proposition~1.14]{BE}, and write
\[
 q_v(e_{v,j})=e^{\lambda_{v,j}}p_v(e_{v,j}).
\]
In $p$-orthogonal coordinates, the degree-$b_e$ associated-graded map
retains precisely the entries whose normalized weighted size is one.
Thus, if a matrix coefficient contributes to that residue map, it attains
the arrow bound:
\[
 |(\rho_X(e))_{kj}|p_{t(e)}(e_{t(e),k})
 =e^{b_e}p_{s(e)}(e_{s(e),j}).
\]
Orthogonality and feasibility at $q$ give the corresponding weak
inequality after inserting $e^{\lambda_{t(e),k}}$ and
$e^{\lambda_{s(e),j}}$; cancellation yields
$\lambda_{t(e),k}\leq\lambda_{s(e),j}$. Consequently, for every
$u\in\mathbb R$, the graded $\mathcal R$-spans of the initial basis vectors with
$\lambda_{v,j}\leq u$ form a graded subrepresentation $F_{\leq u}$ of
$\operatorname{gr}_p^bX$. Their vertex graded ranks count precisely those selected basis
vectors. Since determinant weights add in the chosen product apartment,
\[
 J_\kappa(q)-J_\kappa(p)
 =\sum_{v,j}\kappa_v\lambda_{v,j}.
\]
If $\eta_1<\cdots<\eta_m$ are the distinct $\lambda_{v,j}$, then
$\kappa(X)=0$ gives the finite-sum identity
\[
 \sum_{v,j}\kappa_v\lambda_{v,j}
 =-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(\eta_{k+1}-\eta_k)
       \kappa(F_{\leq\eta_k}).
\]
Equivalently, the integrand below is zero outside the finite range of the
$\lambda_{v,j}$ and
\[
 J_\kappa(q)-J_\kappa(p)
 =-\int_\mathbb R\kappa(F_{\leq u})\,du\geq0.
\]

Now let $q$ be arbitrary. Recall that an extension of valued fields is
\emph{immediate} if it induces isomorphisms of value groups and residue
fields, and that a valued field is \emph{maximally complete} if it has no
proper immediate valued-field extension. For valued fields, maximal
completeness is equivalent to spherical completeness
\cite[Section~5, remark following Theorem~1]{Poonen}.
Choose a maximally complete
immediate extension $L/K$. Such an extension exists and is complete
\cite[Corollary~6 and Proposition~6(1)]{Poonen}; it is spherically complete,
and every finite-dimensional norm over $L$ is split
\cite[Proposition~6.9]{HKKP}.

Immediacy identifies the real-graded residue fields
$\mathcal R_K\simeq\mathcal R_L$: for $z\in L^\times$, choose
$a\in K^\times$ with $|a|=|z|$ and $u\in K^\circ$ whose residue equals
that of $z/a$; then $|z-au|<|z|$, so $au$ represents the same homogeneous
class as $z$. For every vertex, inclusion induces a
grade-preserving isomorphism
\[
 M_{p_v}(X_v)\xrightarrow{\sim}
 M_{(p_v)_L}(X_v\otimes_KL).
\]
Indeed, a $p_v$-orthogonal $K$-basis remains orthogonal with the same
weights over $L$ \cite[Proposition~1.25(iv)]{BE}, and its initial forms
give homogeneous bases on both sides over the identified graded residue
fields. The inclusions commute with the arrow maps and their
degree-$b_e$ reductions. Thus $\operatorname{gr}_{p_L}^bX_L$ identifies with
$\operatorname{gr}_p^bX$, and remains $\kappa$-semistable.

Feasibility also passes to scalar extension. For every decomposition
$w=\sum_j a_jx_j$, with $a_j\in L$ and $x_j\in X_{s(e)}$, the
projective scalar-extension norms satisfy
\[
 (q_{t(e)})_L(\rho_X(e)_Lw)
 \leq \max_j |a_j|q_{t(e)}(\rho_X(e)x_j)
 \leq e^{b_e}\max_j |a_j|q_{s(e)}(x_j).
\]
Taking the infimum over all such decompositions proves the required
$b$-bound for $q_L$; the same applies to $p_L$.
Both $p_L$ and $q_L$ are split, so the first part of the proof over $L$
gives
\[
 J_{\kappa,L}(q_L)\geq J_{\kappa,L}(p_L),
\]
where the determinant vectors are transported from $K$.
Determinant norms commute with scalar extension for all norms over a
non-Archimedean field \cite[Corollary~2.10]{BE}. Therefore
\[
 J_{\kappa,L}(q_L)=J_\kappa(q),\qquad
 J_{\kappa,L}(p_L)=J_\kappa(p),
\]
which proves the assertion.

<!-- lemma-15: body_md -->
\label{lem:supportinghinges}
Fix a finite quiver $Q$, a complete non-Archimedean field $K$,
a finite-dimensional representation $E=(E_i,\rho(a))$ of $Q$ over $K$, a
real stability parameter $\theta=(\theta_i)_{i\in Q_0}$, a weak ramp $f_a$
for each $a\in Q_1$, and nonzero determinant vectors in the spaces
$\det E_i$.  Let $S_E$ be the potential in~ 
  \hyperref[eq:HKKPpotential]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:HKKPpotential}}}}
determined by these data, and let $h\in\mathcal N(E)$ be a global minimizer of
$S_E$. For every arrow $a$, put $r_a=\operatorname{rk}\rho(a)$. If $r_a>0$, let
$d_{a,1}<\cdots<d_{a,m_a}$ be the distinct values among
$\sigma_{a,j}(h)$. There exist positive coefficients $\mu_{a,\nu}$ such
that the convex piecewise-linear function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hingeexpansion}
 g_a(x)=\sum_{\nu=1}^{m_a}\mu_{a,\nu}(x-d_{a,\nu})_+
\end{equation}
satisfies
\[
 g'_{a,-}(d_{a,\nu})<f_a'(d_{a,\nu})<g'_{a,+}(d_{a,\nu})
 \qquad(1\leq\nu\leq m_a),
\]
where $g'_{a,-}$ and $g'_{a,+}$ denote the left and right derivatives.
Call such functions \emph{strict supporting hinges} for $f_a$ at the
singular profile of $h$.
If $r_a=0$, take $m_a=0$ and $g_a=0$.
For any such choice, the norm $h$ minimizes the function
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 G_E:\mathcal N(E)&\longrightarrow\mathbb R,\\
 k&\longmapsto\sum_{a\in Q_1}\sum_{j=1}^{r_a}
 g_a(\sigma_{a,j}(k))
 -\sum_{i\in Q_0}\theta_i\ell_{E_i}(k_i).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Every global minimizer $k$ of $G_E$
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hingeprofileequality}
 \sigma_{a,j}(k)=\sigma_{a,j}(h)
 \qquad\text{for all }a\in Q_1,\ 1\leq j\leq r_a,
\end{equation}
and $S_E(k)=S_E(h)$.

<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Put $y_{a,j}=f_a'(\sigma_{a,j}(h))$ and define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L_h:\mathcal N(E)&\longrightarrow\mathbb R,\\
 k&\longmapsto\sum_{a,j}y_{a,j}\sigma_{a,j}(k)
 -\sum_i\theta_i\ell_{E_i}(k_i).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For each arrow, $y_{a,1}\geq\cdots\geq y_{a,r_a}>0$.  With
$y_{a,r_a+1}=0$ and
$s_{a,j}=\sigma_{a,1}+\cdots+\sigma_{a,j}$,
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{r_a}y_{a,j}\sigma_{a,j}
 =\sum_{j=1}^{r_a}(y_{a,j}-y_{a,j+1})s_{a,j}.
\]
By \hyperref[lem:allnormconvexity]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:allnormconvexity}}, the functions $s_{a,j}$ are
geodesically convex on the completed norm space; the vertex determinant
terms are affine, so $L_h$ is geodesically convex.

We claim that $h$ minimizes $L_h$.  Otherwise choose $k$ with
$L_h(k)<L_h(h)$ and let $h_t$ be the canonical geodesic from $h$ to $k$.
Convexity gives
\[
 L_h(h_t)-L_h(h)\leq t\bigl(L_h(k)-L_h(h)\bigr).
\]
The geodesic has $d_2(h_t,h)=O(t)$, hence also
$d_\infty(h_t,h)=O(t)$ by~ 
  \hyperref[eq:d2dinftycomparison]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:d2dinftycomparison}}}}.
 
  \hyperref[eq:profilelipschitz]{Equation~\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:profilelipschitz}}}}, applied to successive partial sums,
therefore gives
\[
 |\sigma_{a,j}(h_t)-\sigma_{a,j}(h)|=O(t)
\]
for every $a,j$, while each determinant coordinate is affine in $t$.
Taylor's formula for the $C^1$ ramps, summed over the finite set of singular
values, now gives
\[
 S_E(h_t)-S_E(h)=L_h(h_t)-L_h(h)+o(t),
\]
contradicting the minimality of $h$.

To prove existence of strict supporting hinges, fix an arrow $a$ with
$r_a>0$ and put $u_{a,\nu}=f_a'(d_{a,\nu})$. Since $f_a'$ is positive
and strictly increasing, we may choose real numbers
\[
 0=c_{a,0}<u_{a,1}<c_{a,1}<u_{a,2}<\cdots
 <c_{a,m_a-1}<u_{a,m_a}<c_{a,m_a}
\]
and put $\mu_{a,\nu}=c_{a,\nu}-c_{a,\nu-1}$. The resulting function
in~ 
  \hyperref[eq:hingeexpansion]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:hingeexpansion}}}} has
\[
 g'_{a,-}(d_{a,\nu})=c_{a,\nu-1},\qquad
 g'_{a,+}(d_{a,\nu})=c_{a,\nu},
\]
so it satisfies the required strict inequalities.

Now fix any choice of strict supporting hinges. For every arrow with
$r_a>0$, put $u_{a,\nu}=f_a'(d_{a,\nu})$. Convexity and the strict
left and right slope inequalities give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stricthingesupport}
 g_a(x)\geq g_a(d_{a,\nu})
 +u_{a,\nu}(x-d_{a,\nu}),
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if $x=d_{a,\nu}$.
For an arrow $a$ with $r_a=0$, the singular profile is empty, and we take
$g_a=0$.

Summing~ 
  \hyperref[eq:stricthingesupport]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:stricthingesupport}}}} over all singular values gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GsupportL}
 G_E(k)-G_E(h)=L_h(k)-L_h(h)+D_h(k),
 \qquad D_h(k)\geq0,
\end{equation}
where $D_h(k)=0$ precisely when~ 
  \hyperref[eq:hingeprofileequality]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:hingeprofileequality}}}} holds.
Thus $h$ minimizes $G_E$.  Equality at the minimum forces both terms on the
right of~ 
  \hyperref[eq:GsupportL]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:GsupportL}}}} to vanish.  The arrow singular profiles and
the $\theta$-weighted determinant sum are therefore unchanged, which gives
$S_E(k)=S_E(h)$.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:splitattainment}
Fix a finite quiver $Q$, a complete non-Archimedean field $K$,
a finite-dimensional representation $E=(E_i,\rho(a))$ of $Q$ over $K$,
a real stability parameter $\theta=(\theta_i)_{i\in Q_0}$ satisfying
$\theta(E)=0$, weak ramps $f=(f_a)_{a\in Q_1}$, and nonzero determinant vectors
in $\det E_i$. Let $S_E$ be the resulting potential. If $h\in\mathcal N(E)$ is a
global minimizer of $S_E$, then
there is $k\in\mathcal N^{\mathrm{diag}}(E)$ such that
\[
 \sigma_{a,j}(k)=\sigma_{a,j}(h)
 \quad\text{for all }a\in Q_1,\ 1\leq j\leq\operatorname{rk}\rho(a),
 \qquad S_E(k)=S_E(h).
\]
Consequently, for these data,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E\text{ is $\theta$-polystable}
 &\Longrightarrow E\text{ admits a split HKKP harmonic norm for }(\theta,f)\\
 &\Longrightarrow E\text{ is $\theta$-semistable}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \hyperref[quote:hkkpquestion624]{HKKP Question~6.24} has an affirmative
answer over every complete non-Archimedean field.

Choose strict supporting hinges as in
\hyperref[lem:supportinghinges]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:supportinghinges}}, and form a new quiver $\widehat Q$.  Its
vertices are the original vertices $i\in Q_0$ together with one auxiliary
vertex $u=(a,\nu)$ for every hinge in~ 
  \hyperref[eq:hingeexpansion]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:hingeexpansion}}}}, and
\[
 \widehat E_u=E_{s(a)}.
\]
From $u=(a,\nu)$ draw precisely two arrows
\[
 \widehat E_u\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{id}\ }E_{s(a)},
 \qquad
 \widehat E_u\xrightarrow{\ \rho(a)\ }E_{t(a)},
\]
with logarithmic radius bounds $0$ and $d_{a,\nu}$, respectively; write $b$ for this
label function on $\widehat Q_1$.  Thus a $b$-bounded norm $p$ satisfies
\[
 p_u\geq p_{s(a)},
 \qquad p_u\geq e^{-d_{a,\nu}}p_{t(a)}\circ\rho(a).
\]
Every arrow of $\widehat Q$ goes from an auxiliary vertex to an original
vertex.

Assign the following determinant weights to the vertices:
\begin{align*}
 \kappa_u&=\mu_{a,\nu},\\
 \kappa_i&=-\theta_i-
 \sum_{\substack{a\in Q_1,\ 1\leq\nu\leq m_a\\s(a)=i}}\mu_{a,\nu}.
\end{align*}
They are balanced:
\[
 \sum_{v\in\widehat Q_0}\kappa_v\dim\widehat E_v
 =-\theta(E)=0.
\]
Transport the determinant vector of $E_{s(a)}$ to each auxiliary copy and
define
\[
 J:\prod_{v\in\widehat Q_0}\mathcal N(\widehat E_v)\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
 \qquad
 p\longmapsto\sum_{v\in\widehat Q_0}
 \kappa_v\ell_{\widehat E_v}(p_v).
\]

For fixed $(a,\nu)$, the least feasible auxiliary norm is given by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 q_{a,\nu}:\prod_{i\in Q_0}\mathcal N(E_i)&\longrightarrow
 \mathcal N(E_{s(a)}),\\
 p^0=(p_i)_i&\longmapsto p_{s(a)}\vee
 e^{-d_{a,\nu}}\rho(a)^*p_{t(a)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The determinant hinge identity gives the exact elimination formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:auxiliaryelimination}
 J\bigl(p^0,(q_{a,\nu}(p^0))_{a,\nu}\bigr)=G_E(p^0).
\end{equation}
For a feasible norm $p$, write $p^0=(p_i)_{i\in Q_0}$ for its
original-vertex part. Every auxiliary norm $p_u$ dominates the
corresponding least one.
Pointwise domination passes to the projective tensor norm and then to its
quotient on the determinant line, so determinant norms are monotone. Since
all $\mu_{a,\nu}$ are positive, this monotonicity and
\hyperref[lem:supportinghinges]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:supportinghinges}} give
\[
 J(p)\geq G_E(p^0)\geq G_E(h).
\]
Hence
\[
 \widehat h:=\bigl(h,(q_{a,\nu}(h))_{a,\nu}\bigr)
\]
is a global minimizer of $J$ on the full $b$-bounded locus.

We next prove that the fixed representation $\widehat E$ is
$\kappa$-semistable.  Let $U\subseteq\widehat E$ be a subrepresentation.
Choose bases adapted to every $U_v$, put arbitrary split coordinate norms on
the original vertices, and make each auxiliary-source norm sufficiently
large that both of its outgoing bounds hold.  This gives a split feasible
norm adapted to $U$. Relative to the decompositions
$\widehat E_v=U_v\oplus C_v$, every arrow has the block form
\[
 \rho_{\widehat E}(e)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  a_e&\beta_e'\\
  0&d_e'
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Multiply the norm on every $U_v$ by $e^{-t}$ and leave the complementary
coordinate norm fixed. The diagonal block norms stay fixed and the
upper-right block norm is multiplied by $e^{-t}$. More precisely, writing
$p_{U_v}$ and $p_{C_v}$ for the initial summand norms,
\[
 \|\beta_e'\|_{p_{C_{s(e)}}\to e^{-t}p_{U_{t(e)}}}
 =e^{-t}\|\beta_e'\|_{p_{C_{s(e)}}\to p_{U_{t(e)}}}.
\]
The real factor rescales the target norm; every $K$-linear arrow map
stays fixed. Thus all bounds persist. Since the
determinant logarithm at $v$ changes by $-t\dim U_v$, along this ray
\[
 J(p_t)=J(p_0)-t\kappa(U).
\]
The lower bound for $J$ therefore forces $\kappa(U)\leq0$.

The preceding construction, applied with $U=\{0\}$, also supplies an initial
split $b$-bounded norm on $\widehat E$.
\hyperref[lem:radiuslangton]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:radiuslangton}} now supplies a split $b$-bounded
norm $p$ for which $\operatorname{gr}_p^b\widehat E$ is $\kappa$-semistable.  By
\hyperref[lem:labelleddetminimum]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:labelleddetminimum}}, $p$ minimizes $J$ on the full
$b$-bounded locus.  Thus $J(p)\leq J(\widehat h)$, while the preceding global lower
bound gives the reverse inequality; hence
\[
 J(p)=J(\widehat h)=G_E(h).
\]
Let $k=(p_i)_{i\in Q_0}$ be the original-vertex part of $p$; it is split
because $p$ is split. The preceding inequalities give directly
\[
 G_E(h)\leq G_E(k)\leq J(p)=G_E(h).
\]
Thus $G_E(k)=G_E(h)$, and \hyperref[lem:supportinghinges]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:supportinghinges}} gives equality of all
arrow singular profiles and $S_E(k)=S_E(h)$.

If $E$ is $\theta$-polystable,
\hyperref[thm:universalvariational]{Theorem~\ref*{thm:universalvariational}} supplies a minimizer in the
completed norm space.  The
first assertion supplies a split minimizer, and
\hyperref[cor:splitcriterionallfields]{Corollary~\ref*{cor:splitcriterionallfields}} identifies it with an HKKP
harmonic norm. The second displayed implication is the final assertion of
\hyperref[cor:splitcriterionallfields]{Corollary~\ref*{cor:splitcriterionallfields}}.

<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
For an arbitrary norm $h=(h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3)$, define norms on $W$ and
determinant logarithms by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p_i(v)&=h_0(\rho_i(v)),&
 d_i&=\ln(p_i)_{\det}(\omega),\\
 \ell_i&=\ln(h_i)_{\det}(\omega),&
 D&=\ln(h_0)_{\det}(\Omega).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For each pair $i<k$, the corresponding direct-sum norm dominates $h_0$
under the isomorphism $W\oplus W\to E_0$ induced by $\rho_i,\rho_k$.
Determinant monotonicity and the direct-sum determinant identity, using
the preceding wedge identity, therefore give
\[
 D\leq d_i+d_k\qquad(1\leq i<k\leq3).
\]
The determinant identities used here hold for arbitrary non-Archimedean
norms \cite[Corollary~2.10]{BE}. Moreover,
$\sigma_{i,1}+\sigma_{i,2}=d_i-\ell_i$.
This follows in adapted orthogonal coordinates for split norms, and
extends to all norms by density and continuity, including continuity
of restriction. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d4multiplicitypotential}
 \begin{aligned}
 S_E(h)&=\sum_{i=1}^3\sum_{j=1}^2
             (e^{\sigma_{i,j}}-\sigma_{i,j})+R(h_0),\\
 R(h_0)&=\sum_i d_i-\tfrac32D
        =\tfrac12\sum_{i<k}(d_i+d_k-D)\geq0.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since $e^\sigma-\sigma\geq1$, this proves $S_E\geq6$.
At $H(\nu)$, each arrow is an isometric embedding with a non-expansive
left inverse given by one of the central coordinate projections. The
same holds on every exterior power, so its singular values are zero.
Also $p_i=\nu$ and $D=2\ln\nu_{\det}(\omega)$, giving $R=0$ and $S_E=6$.

To identify the equality locus, we use determinant rigidity: if
$r\leq s$ are norms on a finite-dimensional space and their determinant
norms agree, then $r=s$. For any nonzero vector $v$, the quotient norms
satisfy $r/Kv\leq s/Kv$. The restriction--quotient determinant identity
\cite[Corollary~2.10]{BE} therefore shows that $r(v)<s(v)$ would make
the determinant norm of $r$ strictly smaller than that of $s$.

If $S_E(h)=6$, every scalar term in
 
  \hyperref[eq:d4multiplicitypotential]{\textup{\tagform@{\ref*{eq:d4multiplicitypotential}}}} equals $1$, and every pairwise
determinant gap vanishes. Thus
\[
 \sigma_{i,j}=0,\qquad d_1=d_2=d_3=\tfrac12D.
\]
The norm $p_1\oplus p_2$ dominates $h_0$ and has the same determinant,
so rigidity gives $h_0=p_1\oplus p_2$. Consequently
$p_3=\max\{p_1,p_2\}$ pointwise. Since $p_3$ dominates each of $p_1,p_2$
and all three determinants agree, rigidity gives
$p_1=p_2=p_3=\nu$. Finally,
$\sigma_{i,1}=\ln\|\mathrm{id}:(W,h_i)\to(W,\nu)\|=0$
gives $\nu\leq h_i$, while the full singular sum gives equality of
their determinants. Rigidity yields $h_i=\nu$.

If $\nu$ is split, so is $H(\nu)$. Conversely, if its central norm were
split, its restriction to the first image plane would be split by
\hyperref[lem:adapted]{Lemma~\ref*{lem:adapted}}, and that restriction is $\nu$.
