<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:exp-fiber}
The following are equivalent: \textup{(a)} $d_Z\ge3$; \textup{(b)} no column has $\beta=0$ with
$\alpha\ne0$, and no two columns share the same nonzero $\beta$ with different labels $\alpha$. A support satisfying \textup{(b)}
is called \emph{multiplicity-free}.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
A $Z$ error of weight at most two is determined by its support columns. For such $v$, the syndrome
$Sv$ and label $Kv$ are the sums of at most two columns, and $d_Z\le2$ means some such string
satisfies $Sv=0$ with $Kv\ne0$. The only candidates are
\[
v=e_j:\ (Sv,Kv)=(\beta_j,\alpha_j);
\qquad
v=e_i+e_j:\ (Sv,Kv)=(\beta_i+\beta_j,\ \alpha_i+\alpha_j).
\]
So a weight-one $Z$-logical is a column with zero syndrome but nonzero label, and a weight-two
$Z$-logical is a pair of columns sharing a syndrome but not a label. Condition \textup{(b)} forbids

\label{prop:exp-uniqueness}
Up to a permutation of coordinates, the only projective $8$-divisible $[48,6]$ code is FG48, the

The $48$ columns of $\mathcal C_2$ occupy $48$ of the $63$ points of $\operatorname{PG}(5,2)$, missing $15$.
Each hyperplane $\mathcal H_a$ has $31$ points, so it meets the column set in at least
$31-15=16$ points and at most $31$. By~\eqref{eq:exp-weight-section} the nonzero codeword
$c_a$ has weight $48$ minus this section size, and $8$-divisibility forces the section size to
be a multiple of $8$. In the range $[16,31]$ the only multiples of $8$ are $16$ and $24$,
giving codeword weights $32$ and $24$.

intersection with the $48$-point column set has size $m$. Then
\[

\]

Definition~\ref{def:exp-flatcomplement} attains it
(Calderbank--Kantor Example~SU1~\cite[\S7]{calderbankKantor1986}), and among

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:exp-affine-invariance}
An \emph{invertible affine map} on $\mathbb{F}_2^m$ is a map
$L\colon\mathbb{F}_2^m\to\mathbb{F}_2^m$ of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-affine-map}
L(x)=Ax+b,
\end{equation}
where $A\in\operatorname{GL}(m,2)$ and $b\in\mathbb{F}_2^m$. For a set
$E\subseteq\mathbb{F}_2^m$, write $L(E)=\{L(x):x\in E\}$ for its direct image.
Boolean functions $c,c'\colon\mathbb{F}_2^m\to\mathbb{F}_2$ are \emph{affine-equivalent}
when $c'=c\circ L^{-1}$ for such a map $L$. The induced transformation satisfies
\begin{equation*}
{\rm supp}(c')=L({\rm supp}(c)),\qquad |c'|=|c|,\qquad \deg c'=\deg c.
\end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
For $y\in\mathbb{F}_2^m$, $c'(y)=1$ if and only if $c(L^{-1}(y))=1$.
Hence ${\rm supp}(c')=\{L(x):c(x)=1\}=L({\rm supp}(c))$, and bijectivity gives the weight
identity. Substitution of affine linear forms gives
$\deg(c\circ L^{-1})\le\deg c$, and substituting into $c=c'\circ L$ gives the
opposite inequality.

\label{cor:exp-ch-codes}
Let $G=[K;S]$ be the generator matrix for the codes $\mathcal C_2\subseteq\mathcal C_1$. Then $G$ satisfies the nine
CH conditions if and only if
\begin{enumerate}
\par\hangindent 40\p@[(i)] $|a\wedge b|$ is even for all $a,b\in\mathcal C_1$,
\par\hangindent 40\p@[(ii)] $|a\wedge b\wedge u|$ is even for all $a,b\in\mathcal C_1$ and
$u\in\mathcal C_2$, and
\par\hangindent 40\p@[(iii)] $|K_1\wedge K_2\wedge K_3|$ is odd.
\end{enumerate}
In that case every codeword of $\mathcal C_1$ has even weight, by~(i) with $a=b$, and
the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\par\hangindent 40\p@[(a)] For $x\in\mathbb{F}_2^3$, $y\in\mathbb{F}_2^{\widetilde s}$, and
$\{i,i',i''\}=\{1,2,3\}$,
\begin{equation*}
w=\sum_{h=1}^{3}x_hK_h+\sum_{l=1}^{\widetilde s}y_lS_l
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|w\wedge K_{i'}\wedge K_{i''}|\equiv x_i\pmod2.
\end{equation*}
\par\hangindent 40\p@[(b)] If $n$ is even, then the matrix $[K;S;\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}]$, with the
all-ones row appended as a stabilizer row, satisfies the nine CH conditions.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
By Corollary~\ref{cor:exp-xorweight}, the parities of $|a\wedge b|$ and $|a\wedge b\wedge u|$ are additive in each argument, so (i)
and~(ii) hold for all codewords exactly when they hold for rows. On rows, $R\wedge R=R$ reduces a
repeated row, (i) becomes K1, K2, KS1, S1, and S2, (ii) becomes KS2, KS3, and S3, and (iii) is
K3.

$\sum_hx_h|K_h\wedge K_{i'}\wedge K_{i''}|$ plus the overlap of
$\sum_ly_lS_l\in\mathcal C_2$ with $K_{i'}\wedge K_{i''}$. The terms with $h=i',i''$ are even
by~(i), the stabilizer term is even by~(ii), and~(iii) makes the term with $h=i$ equal to $x_i$

For part~(b), the codes become $\mathcal C_1\cup(\mathbf1+\mathcal C_1)$ and

$\mathbf1$. Each equals $n$ or an overlap $|a\wedge b|$ with $a,b\in\mathcal C_1$, which is even
by hypothesis or by~(i). Condition~(iii) is unchanged.

and $v\notin\mathcal P$. Applying the lemma proves the claimed reduction.

\label{cor:exp-hyperplane-reduction}
Let $G=[K;S]$ satisfy the nine CH conditions with $k=3$, $d_Z\ge3$, and
$\mathcal C_2$ projective, at stabilizer rank $s$. If its syndrome set $\mathcal P$ lies in an
affine hyperplane $H$ of $\mathbb{F}_2^s$, then $G$ reduces to a generator matrix of stabilizer rank at most
$s-1$ and length at most $n$, with $k=3$, all nine CH conditions, $d_Z\ge3$, and $\mathcal C_2$ projective.
The length stays $n$ when $\mathcal P\subsetneq H$ and becomes $n-1$ when $\mathcal P=H$.

<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
Since $\operatorname{span}\mathcal P=\mathbb{F}_2^s$, write
$H=\{\beta:a\cdot\beta=1\}$ with $a\ne0$. For $v\in H$ and $\beta\in H$,
$a\cdot(\beta+v)=0$, so $\beta+v\notin H$ and $\Delta_v=\emptyset$.
Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:exp-projection}(i) holds for any $\delta\in\mathbb{F}_2^3$, the coefficient vector
in the logical-row additions. The projection $\pi$ of~\eqref{eq:exp-levelred-kernel} is
injective on $H$: equal images of distinct points would require their difference to be $v$.

\phantomsection\label{case:exp-hyperplane-proper}
If $\mathcal P\subsetneq H$, choose $v\in H\setminus\mathcal P$ and $\delta=0$.
Both hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:exp-projection} hold, and the column operation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-hyperplane-proper-map}
\binom{\kappa(\beta)}{\beta}\longmapsto
\binom{\kappa(\beta)}{\pi(\beta)}.
\end{equation}
Injectivity on $H$ and $\mathcal P\cap\ker\pi=\emptyset$ give distinct nonzero
syndromes. No puncturing is needed, so $n'=n$, $s'=s-1$, and $\mathcal C_2'$ is projective.

\phantomsection\label{case:exp-hyperplane-full}
If $\mathcal P=H$, choose $v\in H$ and $\delta=\kappa(v)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:exp-projection} now applies with a supported $v$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-rank6-hyperplane-reduction}
\binom{\kappa(\beta)}{\beta}\longmapsto
\binom{\kappa(\beta)+\beta_m\kappa(v)}{\pi(\beta)},\qquad\beta\in H.
\end{equation}
The column at $v$ becomes $(\kappa(v)+\kappa(v);0)=0$. Since $|H|=2^{s-1}$ and $\pi|_H$ is injective, $\pi(H)=\mathbb{F}_2^{s-1}$.
Puncturing the zero column therefore leaves the syndrome set
$\pi(H\setminus\{v\})=\mathbb{F}_2^{s-1}\setminus\{0\}$, with $n'=n-1$ and $s'=s-1$.
The zero column contributes nothing to the row ranks, and $\mathcal C_2'$ is projective.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 1. Restrict the stabilizer ranks.}\quad
Suppose a generator matrix $G=[K;S]$ of length at most $38$ satisfies the nine CH
conditions with $k=3$ and $d_Z\ge3$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:exp-projred} makes $\mathcal C_2$ projective. We then apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:exp-levelred} repeatedly to lower the stabilizer rank of $S$.
For $n\le38$ and $s\ge9$, the counting inequality of Lemma~\ref{lem:exp-levelred} holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-floor-ranks}
2^s-1>n+\Bigl\lfloor\tbinom n2\big/2\Bigr\rfloor.
\end{equation}

Each reduction strictly decreases rank, so we obtain $S$ with $s\le8$.
Since Proposition~\ref{prop:exp-lb} excludes $s\le5$, only $s\in\{6,7,8\}$ remain.

For these ranks, Theorem~\ref{thm:exp-spectrum} expresses each candidate support as
$\mathcal P={\rm supp}(c)\setminus\{0\}$ for $c\in\operatorname{RM}(s-4,s)$,
with length $n=|c|-c(0)$.

By Appendix~\ref{app:exp-affine-equivalence}, it suffices to test one polynomial $c$
from each affine-equivalence class in $\operatorname{RM}(s-4,s)$, together with its translated supports
$\mathcal P_a$ of~\eqref{eq:exp-floor-origin}. Each step below checks the rank of $\mathcal P_a$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 2. Exclude ranks $6$ and $7$.}\quad
For each rank, we first determine the possible weights of $c$ and, at each weight, the
affine-equivalence classes of $c$. The flat and hyperplane cases are then excluded, and
Algorithm~\ref{alg:exp-search} is applied to the remaining candidate supports $\mathcal P_a$.

At $s=6$, the Dickson forms~\eqref{eq:exp-dickson-general} in
Appendix~\ref{app:exp-quadratic-normal-forms} classify $c\in\operatorname{RM}(2,6)$.
Table~\ref{tab:exp-spectrum} lists the possible weights $16,24,28,32,36$ when $n\le38$.
Weight $16$ gives $n\in\{15,16\}$ and an affine $4$-flat, which
Lemma~\ref{lem:exp-flatobstruction} excludes. The other weights give six affine-equivalence
classes of $c$, counted in the $s=6$ rows of Table~\ref{tab:exp-floorcells}: the degree-one class
$c=y_6$ and five quadratic classes.

The degree-one representative $c(y)=y_6$ has support $H=\{y:y_6=1\}$ and weight $32$.
If $a\notin H$, then $\mathcal P_a=H+a$ is a full hyperplane not containing zero, so
Corollary~\ref{cor:exp-hyperplane-reduction}, through the
\hyperref[case:exp-hyperplane-full]{reduction}~\eqref{eq:exp-rank6-hyperplane-reduction},
lowers rank $6$ to $5$.
If $a\in H$, then $\mathcal P_a=(H+a)\setminus\{0\}$ lies in a hyperplane through zero and
has actual rank $5$ by the \hyperref[case:exp-hyperplane-origin]{span bound} of
Appendix~\ref{app:exp-flat-supports}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:exp-lb} excludes the resulting generator matrices of rank $5$.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:exp-search} finds no CH triple on the supports $\mathcal P_a$ of
the five quadratic classes, as recorded in the $s=6$ rows of Table~\ref{tab:exp-floorcells}.
Hence rank $6$ is excluded for $n\le38$.

At $s=7$, the candidates are $c\in\operatorname{RM}(3,7)$ with $|c|\le n+1<40$, and
Table~\ref{tab:exp-spectrum} lists their possible weights. The code $\operatorname{RM}(3,7)$ has minimum weight
$16$, which gives an affine $4$-flat that Lemma~\ref{lem:exp-flatobstruction}
excludes. The weights $24$, $28$, and $36$ are associated with the candidates counted in the $s=7$ rows of
Table~\ref{tab:exp-floorcells}.

Weight $32$ needs one more distinction, because the classification of
Kasami--Tokura--Azumi at this weight covers only the polynomials that are not divisible by an
affine function. A polynomial $c$ is \emph{divisible by an
affine function} $\ell(\beta)=a\cdot\beta+b$, with $a\ne0$, when $c=\ell c'$ for some
polynomial $c'$. Then $c(\beta)=1$ forces $\ell(\beta)=1$, so ${\rm supp}(c)$ lies in the affine
hyperplane $H=\{\beta:\ell(\beta)=1\}$. Table~\ref{tab:exp-s7-w32} sorts the weight-$32$
polynomials $c$ by degree and by this divisibility. No weight-$32$ polynomial has degree one,
since a nonconstant affine function on $\mathbb{F}_2^7$ has weight $64$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 3. Reduce candidates at rank $8$.}\quad
We first exclude candidates at rank $8$ that reduce to a rank already excluded
in Steps~1--2. If $|c|\le30$, then $n\le30$ by~\eqref{eq:exp-puncture}, and the counting
hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:exp-levelred} holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-rank8-small}
n+\Bigl\lfloor\tbinom n2\big/2\Bigr\rfloor\le247<2^8-1.
\end{equation}
Hence every candidate with $|c|\le30$ reduces to a rank excluded in Steps~1--2.

By~\eqref{eq:exp-affine-constant}, $|c|$ is even. Together with $|c|\le n+1\le39$, only
weights in the interval $32\le|c|<40$ remain.

To exclude lower-degree polynomials, suppose $\deg c<4$, so $c\in\operatorname{RM}(3,8)$.
The code $\operatorname{RM}(3,8)$ has minimum weight $32$, and
Kasami--Tokura~\cite[Thm.~1]{kasamiTokura1970} exclude weights strictly between $32$ and $40$.
Thus $32\le|c|<40$ forces $|c|=32$, whose support is an affine $5$-flat.

Every translated support $\mathcal P_a$ remains inside an affine $5$-flat.
The \hyperref[case:exp-hyperplane-origin]{span bound} of
Appendix~\ref{app:exp-flat-supports} therefore gives actual rank at most $6$, excluded in
Steps~1--2. Only degree $4$ and weights $32,34,36,38$ remain at rank $8$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 4. Exclude degree-$4$ cases at rank $8$.}\quad
Steps~4--7 exclude the remaining degree-$4$ polynomials by expressing them in
six-variable polynomials and enumerating the possibilities.

Theorem~1 of Kasami--Tokura--Azumi~\cite{kasamiTokuraAzumi1976} gives an affine
$6$-flat in $\mathbb{F}_2^8$ on which $c$ vanishes. Choose affine coordinates $(u,y_7,y_8)$ with
$u=(y_1,\ldots,y_6)\in\mathbb{F}_2^6$ so that this flat is given by $y_7=y_8=0$.
Since $c$ vanishes on the flat, $c(u,0,0)=0$ for every $u$, so no monomial of $c$ uses only the
variables $u$. Grouping the remaining monomials by whether they contain $y_7$, $y_8$, or both
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-rank8-decomposition}
c(u,y_7,y_8)=y_7f(u)+y_8g(u)+y_7y_8h(u).
\end{equation}
Since $\deg c=4$, the polynomials $f$ and $g$ have degree at most $3$, and $h$ has
degree at most $2$. Setting $(y_7,y_8)$ to each of its four values in~\eqref{eq:exp-rank8-decomposition}
gives
\begin{equation*}
c(u,0,0)=0,\quad c(u,1,0)=f(u),\quad c(u,0,1)=g(u),\quad
c(u,1,1)=f(u)+g(u)+h(u).
\end{equation*}
The weight $|c|$ counts the points $(u,y_7,y_8)$ of $\mathbb{F}_2^8$ at which $c=1$.
Grouping these $256$ points by the value of $(y_7,y_8)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-rank8-weight-sum}
|c|=|f|+|g|+|f+g+h|.
\end{equation}
The candidate supports still require the origin recovery~\eqref{eq:exp-floor-origin}
and rank checks of Appendix~\ref{app:exp-affine-equivalence}.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 5. Exclude vanishing components and bound their weights.}\quad
To exclude the cases $f=0$, $g=0$, or $f+g+h=0$, we examine the hyperplane that
contains ${\rm supp}(c)$ in each case:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-rank8-vanishing-components}
\begin{aligned}
f=0:\quad &c=y_8(g+y_7h),
&&{\rm supp}(c)\subseteq\{y_8=1\},\\
g=0:\quad &c=y_7(f+y_8h),
&&{\rm supp}(c)\subseteq\{y_7=1\},\\
f+g+h=0:\quad &c=y_7(1+y_8)f+y_8(1+y_7)g,
&&{\rm supp}(c)\subseteq\{y_7+y_8=1\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Denote any of the hyperplanes in~\eqref{eq:exp-rank8-vanishing-components} by $H$.
For each $a\in\mathbb{F}_2^8$, Equation~\eqref{eq:exp-floor-origin} gives
$\mathcal P_a\subseteq H+a$. If $0\in H+a$, the
\hyperref[case:exp-hyperplane-origin]{span bound in Appendix~\ref{app:exp-flat-supports}}
gives actual rank at most $7$.
Otherwise $n\le38<|H+a|=128$ makes $\mathcal P_a$ a proper subset, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:exp-hyperplane-reduction} lowers rank $8$ to $7$.
The exclusion of rank $7$ in Step~2 therefore forces all three polynomials
$f$, $g$, and $f+g+h$ to be nonzero for every remaining candidate at rank $8$.

To avoid enumerating equivalent permutations of the nonzero components, order their
weights as $0<|f|\le|g|\le|f+g+h|$.
An invertible linear change of $(y_7,y_8)$ permits this ordering:
$\operatorname{GL}(2,2)$ permutes the three nonzero values of $(y_7,y_8)$ and hence the
three restrictions $f$, $g$, and $f+g+h$. With this ordering,
\eqref{eq:exp-rank8-weight-sum} gives $|c|\ge|f|+2|g|$, so $|c|<40$ bounds the two smaller
weights.

The bound $|f|+2|g|<40$ leaves only a few weight pairs, which we now list. Both $f$
and $g$ are nonzero polynomials of degree at most $3$ in $6$ variables, that is, nonzero codewords
of $\operatorname{RM}(3,6)$. The code $\operatorname{RM}(3,6)$ has minimum weight $8$, and its only weights below $16$
are $8$, $12$, and $14$~\cite[Thm.~1]{kasamiTokura1970}. If $|g|\ge16$, then
$|f|+2|g|\ge8+32=40$. If $|g|\le14$, the condition $|f|+2|g|<40$ with $|f|\le|g|$ leaves exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-quadpairs}
(|f|,|g|)\in\{(8,8),(8,12),(12,12),(8,14)\},
\end{equation}
because the pairs $(12,14)$ and $(14,14)$ give $40$ and $42$. The four pairs are the rows
of Table~\ref{tab:exp-orbitcover}.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 6. Enumerate the remaining polynomials.}\quad
Fix $f$ to be the Kasami--Tokura normal form~\cite[Thm.~1]{kasamiTokura1970}
listed in Table~\ref{tab:exp-orbitcover}. Subsequent changes of the six coordinates must
preserve this chosen $f$. For an invertible affine map $L$ of $\mathbb{F}_2^6$, that is, $L\in\operatorname{AGL}(6,2)$, with
$f\circ L^{-1}=f$, the substitution in~\eqref{eq:exp-rank8-decomposition} acts on the
remaining polynomials by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-rank8-polynomial-transport}
(g,h)\longmapsto(g\circ L^{-1},h\circ L^{-1}).
\end{equation}
These maps $L$ form the subgroup of $\operatorname{AGL}(6,2)$ that fixes $f$, and the orbit of $g$ under them is
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{Orb}_f(g)=\{\,g\circ L^{-1}:L\in\operatorname{AGL}(6,2),\ f\circ L^{-1}=f\,\}.
\end{equation*}
Choose one representative $g$ from each orbit.

For each representative pair $(f,g)$ and each candidate weight $|c|$,
enumerate every $h$ of degree at most $2$ satisfying the weight constraint
from~\eqref{eq:exp-rank8-weight-sum}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-rank8-h-weight}
|f+g+h|=|c|-|f|-|g|.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:exp-affine-invariance} shows that the substitution
\eqref{eq:exp-rank8-polynomial-transport} preserves the degree of $h$ and the weights of
$f$, $g$, and $f+g+h$. The substitution therefore preserves the weight
constraint~\eqref{eq:exp-rank8-h-weight} and the total weight $|c|$.

A computation with \texttt{nauty}~\cite{mckayPracticalGraphIsomorphism2014} lists one
representative $g$ of each orbit for every row of Table~\ref{tab:exp-orbitcover}. Three checks
show that no polynomial $g$ is missed. First, the listed orbits are pairwise disjoint. Second,
every $g$ in a listed orbit has the weight of its row. Third, for each row the orbit sizes sum to
the number of all $g$ of that weight, given in the third column. Hence every remaining candidate
$c$ at rank $8$ is affine-equivalent to $y_7f+y_8g+y_7y_8h$ with $f$ from
Table~\ref{tab:exp-orbitcover}, $g$ a listed representative, and $h$ one of the enumerated
polynomials.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Step 7. Exclude the remaining candidates at rank $8$.}\quad
It remains to exclude logical labels for the polynomials enumerated in Step~6.
