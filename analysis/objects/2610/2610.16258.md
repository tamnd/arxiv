<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Every elementary graph $G$ satisfies $
\operatorname{\chi_{\ell}}(G)=\chi(G)=\omega(G).
$
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:hall}
Let $P$ be a list assignment on a finite set $W$. One can choose colours $c(w)\in P(w)$,
$w\in W$, that are pairwise distinct if and only if no nonempty subset of $W$ is deficient.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:hall}
If a graph with vertex set $W$ has no $P$-colouring, then some nonempty $S\subseteq W$ is
deficient.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:konig}
Let $F$ be an $X \text{---} Y$ bipartite graph and let $J$ be its complement.  If $\nu$ is the maximum size of a matching in $F$, then
$\omega(J)=|X|+|Y|-\nu$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $X$ and $Y$ are cliques, a set $K\subseteq X\cup Y$ is a clique of $J$ if and only if it
contains no edge of $F$, that is, if and only if $(X\cup Y)\setminus K$ meets every edge of $F$
(is a \emph{vertex cover} of $F$). Hence $\omega(J)=|X|+|Y|-\tau$, where $\tau$ is the minimum size
of a vertex cover of $F$. By K\H{o}nig's theorem \cite{Kon31}, $\tau=\nu$ for bipartite graphs.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:KXYM}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be disjoint finite sets and let $M$ be a \emph{matching between $X$ and $Y$}.
We write $K(X,Y)-M$ 
for the graph with vertex set $X\cup Y$ in which two distinct vertices are adjacent unless they
form a pair of $M$. 
We call the vertices covered by $M$ \emph{matched} and the others \emph{free}, and we use
the notation
\begin{gather*}
X_0=\{x\in X:\ x\text{ is matched}\},\qquad Y_0=\{y\in Y:\ y\text{ is matched}\},\\
U=X\setminus X_0,\qquad V=Y\setminus Y_0,\qquad r=|U|,\qquad s=|V|,
\end{gather*}
so that $|X_0|=|Y_0|=\mu$, $|X|=\mu+r$ and $|Y|=\mu+s$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:deficiency}
Let $J=K(X,Y)-M$, 
and let $R$ be a list
assignment on $X\cup Y$ with $|R(x)|\ge|X|\ \ (x\in X)\qquad\text{and}\qquad |R(y)|\ge|Y|\ \ (y\in Y).$ 
If $J$ has no $R$-colouring, then there is a set $T$ of free vertices, $T\subseteq U\cup V$,
that meets both $U$ and $V$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witness}
|R(T)|\le|T|+\mu-1 .
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We use induction on $\mu$. By Halls' theorem, there is a nonempty $W\subseteq X\cup Y$
with $|R(W)|\le|W|-1$. Such a $W$ meets both $X$ and $Y$: if, say, $W\subseteq X$, then any
$x\in W$ gives $|R(W)|\ge|R(x)|\ge|X|\ge|W|$. We have the following cases. 

\emph{Case 1: $W$ contains no pair of $M$.}
Then $W$ contains at most one vertex from each pair of $M$, so at most $\mu$ matched vertices.
Put $T=W\cap(U\cup V)$. Then $|W|\le|T|+\mu$ and
\[|R(T)|\le|R(W)|\le|W|-1\le|T|+\mu-1 .\]
We now show that $T$ meets both $U$ and $V$. If $T\cap U=\varnothing$, then $W$ consists of
at most $\mu$ matched vertices together with vertices of $V$, so $|W|\le\mu+s=|Y|$. Since $W$ meets
$Y$, we have $|R(W)|\ge|Y|\ge|W|$, a contradiction. Symmetrically, $T\cap V\ne\varnothing$.
When $\mu=0$ there are no pairs at all, and the base case holds. 

\emph{Case 2: $W$ contains both vertices $x,y$ of some pair of $M$.}
Since $R(x)\cup R(y)\subseteq R(W)$,
\[
|R(x)\cap R(y)|\ \ge\ |R(x)|+|R(y)|-|R(W)|\ \ge\ |X|+|Y|-(|W|-1)\ \ge\ 1 .
\]
Choose a colour $c\in R(x)\cap R(y)$. The graph $J'=J-\{x,y\}$ is $K(X\setminus x,Y\setminus y)-(M\setminus\{xy\})$.
It has $\mu-1$ missing pairs and the same free sets $U$ and $V$. Assign it the lists
$R'(w)=R(w)\setminus\{c\}$, which have at least $|X|-1=|X\setminus x|$ colours on $X\setminus x$ and at least
$|Y|-1$ colours on $Y\setminus y$. If $J'$ had an $R'$-colouring, giving $x$ and $y$ the colour $c$ would
extend it to an $R$-colouring of $J$ ($x$ and $y$ are non-adjacent, and no other vertex uses $c$).
So $J'$ has none, and by induction there is $T\subseteq U\cup V$ meeting both $U$ and $V$ with
$|R'(T)|\le|T|+(\mu-1)-1$. Restoring $c$ adds at most one colour: $|R(T)|\le|R'(T)|+1\le|T|+\mu-1$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:deficiency}
In the following special cases of  Lemma~\ref{lem:deficiency}, $J$ has an $R$-colouring:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item $U=\varnothing$ or $V=\varnothing$;
\item every vertex of $U$, or every vertex of $V$, has a list of at least $\omega(J)=\mu+r+s$ colours.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
A set $T$ as in the lemma meets both $U$ and $V$, which is impossible in case (a). In case (b),
$T$ contains a vertex $w$ with $|R(w)|\ge\mu+r+s\ge\mu+|T|>|T|+\mu-1\ge|R(T)|\ge|R(w)|$, which is
absurd.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:augment}
Let $xy$ be a flat edge of a graph $G_0$, and let $B$ be a cobipartite graph, disjoint from
$G_0$, whose vertex set is partitioned into two nonempty cliques $X$ and $Y$ with at least one
edge between $X$ and $Y$. To \emph{augment} $xy$ by $B$: delete $x$ and $y$, add $B$, and make
$X$ complete to $N(x)\setminus\{y\}$ and $Y$ complete to $N(y)\setminus\{x\}$. We call $B$, or the
pair $(X,Y)$, the \emph{augment} replacing $xy$, and $X,Y$ its two \emph{sides}.
If $x_1y_1,\dots,x_hy_h$ are pairwise vertex-disjoint flat edges of $G_0$, augmenting each of them
(by pairwise disjoint augments) gives a well-defined graph: after one augmentation the remaining
edges $x_iy_i$ are still flat, and the operations commute \cite[\S1]{MR99}. Any graph so obtained,
including $G_0$ itself ($h=0$), is an \emph{augmentation} of $G_0$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:MR}
A graph is elementary if and only if it is an augmentation of the line graph of a bipartite
multigraph.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:anatomy}
Let $G$ be an augmentation of $L(H)$ for a bipartite multigraph $H$. The representation can be
chosen so that every augmented flat edge $e_Xe_Y$ of $L(H)$ consists of two non-parallel edges
$e_X=p_X c$ and $e_Y=cp_Y$ of $H$ whose common endpoint $c$ has degree $2$ in $H$. Moreover, for
every augment $J=(X,Y)$ of the representation:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item $X$ is complete to $N_X$ and $Y$ is complete to $N_Y$: every vertex of $X$ has exactly the
      neighbours $N_X$ outside $J$, and every vertex of $Y$ exactly $N_Y$;
\item $X\cupN_X$ and $Y\cupN_Y$ are cliques of $G$;
\item $N_X\capN_Y=\varnothing$, i.e.\ no vertex outside $J$ has neighbours in both $X$ and $Y$.
      Consequently, every clique of $G$ that meets both $X$ and $Y$ is contained in $V(J)$.
\end{enumerate}
Statements \textup{(a)--(c)} remain true if the set of edges between $X$ and $Y$ is replaced by
any other nonempty set of $X$--$Y$ edges.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $ee'$ be a flat edge of $L(H)$.  Then, $e$ and $e'$ share an endpoint. Suppose that they are
not parallel, say $e=pc$ and $e'=cp'$ with $p\ne p'$. A third edge at $c$ would be adjacent to
both $e$ and $e'$, and $pp'$ is not an edge of $H$ because $p$ and $p'$, both neighbours of $c$, lie
in the same part of the bipartition. Hence $ee'$ is flat if and only if $c$ has degree~$2$.
Another possibility is that $e,e'$ are parallel edges joining $p$ and $c$. Any other edge at $p$ or at
$c$ is adjacent to both, so $ee'$ is flat if and only if $p$ and $c$ both have degree $2$, that is,
if and only if $\{e,e'\}$ is a  component of $H$. In that case replace this component by a
path $p\,c\,p'$ with a new vertex $p'$: the line graph is unchanged. 
Fix an augmented flat edge $e_Xe_Y$ with $e_X=p_X c$, $e_Y=cp_Y$ and $\deg c=2$, and its augment
$J=(X,Y)$. In $L(H)$, the neighbours of $e_X$ other than $e_Y$ are exactly the other edges of $H$
at $p_X$; they form a clique $A_X$ (part of the star of $p_X$), and $A_X\cup\{e_X\}$ is a clique.
Define $A_Y$ at $p_Y$ likewise. Then $A_X\cap A_Y=\varnothing$, since an edge in both would join
$p_X$ to $p_Y$. \par 
Since augmentations commute, we may perform the augmentation of $e_Xe_Y$ last. Let $gg'$ be
another augmented flat edge. It has at most one end in $A_X$: neither end is $e_X$ (the flat edges
are vertex-disjoint), and if both $g,g'\in A_X$ then $g,g',e_X$ would be a triangle through $gg'$.
Consequently, after all the other augmentations have been performed, every vertex of $A_X$ is
either still present or has been replaced by \emph{one side} of an augment. By
Definition~\ref{def:augment}, that side is a clique complete to every neighbour of the replaced
vertex other than its partner in its own flat edge, and the partner lies outside $A_X\cup\{e_X\}$;
so the side is complete to $e_X$ and to (what has become of) the rest of $A_X$. Thus, immediately
before $e_Xe_Y$ is augmented, the neighbours of $e_X$ other than $e_Y$ form a clique; by the rule in
Definition~\ref{def:augment}, $X$ is made complete to exactly this set, which is therefore $N_X$.
Since $X$ is a clique complete to the clique $N_X$, this proves (a) and (b) for $X$, and
symmetrically for $Y$.
For (c), a vertex of $N_X$ is either an unaugmented vertex of $A_X$ or lies in the side replacing a
vertex of $A_X$; likewise for $N_Y$; and a side of an augment replaces a single vertex. Since
$A_X\cap A_Y=\varnothing$, we get $N_X\capN_Y=\varnothing$. A clique containing $x\in X$ and
$y\in Y$ cannot contain a vertex outside $J$, as such a vertex would lie in $N_X\capN_Y$.
Nothing in the above argument used the edges between $X$ and $Y$, hence any nonempty set of edges between $X,Y$ works.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:saturation}
Let $J=(X,Y)$ be an incomplete augment in the representation of $G$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item the non-adjacent pairs between $X$ and $Y$ form a matching $M$ with $\mu=|M|\ge1$; thus
      $J=K(X,Y)-M$, and we use the notation $X_0,Y_0,U,V,r,s$ of Definition~\ref{def:KXYM}.
\item $\omega(J)=|X|+|Y|-\mu=k$, i.e.\ $k=\mu+r+s$.
\item $N_X\ne\varnothing$ and $N_Y\ne\varnothing$.
\item $r\ge1$, $s\ge1$, $|N_X|\le s$ and $|N_Y|\le r$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
(a) Let $F$ be the bipartite graph with parts $X,Y$ whose edges are the non-adjacent $X$--$Y$
pairs of $J$. $F$ has at least one edge because $J$ is incomplete. Let $M$ be a maximum matching of
$F$ and $\mu=|M|\ge1$. Let $G^+$ be obtained from $G$ by adding, as edges, all pairs in
$E(F)\setminus M$. The augment $J^+=(X,Y)$ of $G^+$ is also cobipartite with at least one edge between
$X$ and $Y$. Hence $G^+$ is an augmentation of $L(H)$ along the same flat edges and is elementary by
Theorem~\ref{thm:MR}. Note that its clique number is at most $k$. This is because a clique of $G^+$ that is not a clique
of $G$ contains an added pair. Thus, it meets both $X$ and $Y$, and therefore lies inside $J^+$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:anatomy}(c). 
Moreover, $\omega(J^+)=|X|+|Y|-\mu=\omega(J)\le k$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:konig}, applied to $J^+$ (whose
non-adjacent $X$--$Y$ pairs are exactly the $\mu$ pairs of $M$) and to $J$ (where the maximum
size of a matching in $F$ is $\mu$).  Lemma~\ref{lem:mincex}(ii), then implies that no edge was added to $G$. Thus, $E(F)=M$.
(b)
As above, have $\omega(J)=|X|+|Y|-\mu\le k$.
If the inequality were strict, add a single pair of $M$ as an edge. The non-adjacent $X$--$Y$
pairs then form the matching $M$ minus one pair, so the clique number of the augment rises by
exactly one and stays at most $k$. As in the case (a), 
the new graph is elementary with clique number at
most $k$, contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:mincex}(ii). Hence $|X|+|Y|-\mu=k$, which is
$k=\mu+r+s$ by Definition~\ref{def:KXYM}.
(c) Suppose $N_X=\varnothing$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:mincex}(i), the graph $G-V(J)$ has an
$L$-colouring $f$ (if $G=J$, take $f$ empty). Let $F_Y=f(N_Y)$ and define lists on $J$ by
$R(x)=L(x)$ for $x\in X$ and $R(y)=L(y)\setminusF_Y$ for $y\in Y$. Since $Y\cupN_Y$ is a clique
(Lemma~\ref{lem:anatomy}(b)), $|Y|+|N_Y|\le k$, so $|R(y)|\ge k-|N_Y|\ge|Y|$; and $|R(x)|=k\ge|X|$.
This implies $J$ admits an $R$-colouring $g$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:deficiency}.
Then $f\cup g$ is an $L$-colouring of $G$.
This contradicts the
choice of $L$. Hence $N_X\ne\varnothing$, and $N_Y\ne\varnothing$ by symmetry.
(d) By (b) and (c), $X\cupN_X$ is a clique with $|X|+|N_X|\le k$ and $N_X\ne\varnothing$, so
$|X|\le k-1$ and therefore $s=k-|X|\ge1$ and $|N_X|\le k-|X|=s$. Symmetrically $r\ge1$ and
$|N_Y|\le r$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:noincomplete}
Every augment in the representation of $G$ is complete.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Suppose that $J=(X,Y)$ is an incomplete augment. Throughout the proof we use the notation and
the conclusions of Lemma~\ref{lem:saturation}: $J=K(X,Y)-M$ with $|M|=\mu\ge1$,
$X=X_0\cup U$ and $Y=Y_0\cup V$ with $|U|=r\ge1$ and $|V|=s\ge1$, $k=\mu+r+s$, and the exterior
neighbourhoods $N_X,N_Y$ with $|N_X|\le s$ and $|N_Y|\le r$. Recall from Lemma~\ref{lem:anatomy}
that $N_X$ and $N_Y$ are disjoint, that $X\cupN_X$ and $Y\cupN_Y$ are cliques, and that the pairs
of $M$ are the only non-adjacent pairs inside $X\cup Y$.
Our proof proceeds in three phases. In the first phase, we fix a side of $J$ and choose a specific subset with least surplus of colours. We choose a specific vertex and and copy its list to 
vertices in $X_0$ and try to colour $G-Y_0$ and then extend this to whole of $J$.  
\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Fix a side and a set $S$.}\par\nopagebreak\smallskip
For a nonempty set $S$ of vertices write $\sigma(S)=|L(S)|-|S|$ for its surplus with respect to the
original lists $L$, and let $m_X=\min\{\sigma(S):\ \varnothing\ne S\subseteq U\}$, and 
$m_Y=\min\{\sigma(S):\ \varnothing\ne S\subseteq V\}$. Due to symmetry, we may assume without loss of generality that,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:side}
m_X+r\ \le\ m_Y+s .
\end{equation}
Fix a nonempty $S\subseteq U$ with $\sigma(S)=m_X$, and fix a vertex $z\in S$. We note two
consequences of this choice. First, since $|L(S)|\ge|L(z)|=k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sbig}
|S|\ \ge\ k-m_X .
\end{equation}
Second, for every nonempty $I\subseteq S$ we have $\sigma(I)\ge m_X=\sigma(S)$ and $L(I)\subseteq L(S)$,
so $|L(S)|-|L(I)|\le|S|-|I|$, that is,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Smin}
|L(S)\setminus L(I)|\ \le\ |S\setminus I|\qquad\text{for every nonempty }I\subseteq S .
\end{equation}
In words, \eqref{eq:Smin} says that the vertices of $S\setminus I$ contribute at most one new
colour each beyond $L(I)$.
\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Colouring $G-Y_0$ with the list of $z$ copied onto $X_0$.}\par\nopagebreak\smallskip
Let $G'=G-Y_0$, a proper induced subgraph of $G$ because $\mu\ge1$, and give it the lists
\[
L'(v)=\begin{cases} L(z), & v\in X_0,\\ L(v), & v\notin X_0.\end{cases}
\]
Every list $L'(v)$ has $k$ colours, so Lemma~\ref{lem:mincex}(i) provides an $L'$-colouring $f$ of
$G'$. The sets $N_X$ and $N_Y$ lie outside $J$, and hence in $G'$. Set
\[
F_X=f(N_X),\qquad F_Y=f(N_Y),\qquad\text{then } |F_X|\le s,\ |F_Y|\le r .
\]
Now define the \emph{residual lists} on $V(J)$, using the \emph{original} lists,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
R(x)=L(x)\setminusF_X\ \ (x\in X),\qquad R(y)=L(y)\setminusF_Y\ \ (y\in Y).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rsizes}
|R(x)|\ \ge\ k-s\ =\ |X|\quad(x\in X),\qquad |R(y)|\ \ge\ k-r\ =\ |Y|\quad(y\in Y).
\end{equation}
The following two observations will be useful (see Figure~\ref{fig:reduced}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(B\arabic*)},leftmargin=3em]
\item\label{B1} \emph{If $J$ has an $R$-colouring $g$, then $G$ has an $L$-colouring.}\\
Define $c=g$ on $V(J)$ and $c=f$ on $V(G)\setminus V(J)$. Lists are respected, because
$R\subseteq L$ on $J$ and $L'=L$ off $X_0\subseteq V(J)$. Edges inside $J$ are properly coloured
by $g$, edges outside $J$ by $f$. An edge from $x\in X$ to a vertex $w\notin V(J)$ has
$w\inN_X$, so $f(w)\inF_X$ while $g(x)\notinF_X$; similarly for $Y$ and $F_Y$.
\item\label{B2} \emph{$Q=X_0\cup U\cup V$ is a clique of $G'$ with $|Q|=\mu+r+s=k$; moreover
$f(x)\in R(z)$ for every $x\in X_0$, and $f(w)\in R(w)$ for every $w\in U\cup V$.}\\ 
Indeed $Q\subseteq V(G')$, and every non-adjacent pair inside $X\cup Y$ is a pair of $M$, which
has a vertex in the deleted set $Y_0$; so $Q$ is a clique. Let $x\in X_0$. Then $f(x)\in L'(x)=L(z)$,
and $x$ is adjacent in $G'$ to all of $N_X$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:anatomy}(a)), so $f(x)\notinF_X$; hence
$f(x)\in L(z)\setminusF_X=R(z)$. A vertex $w\in U$ has $f(w)\in L(w)$ and is adjacent to all of $N_X$, so
$f(w)\in R(w)$; a vertex $w\in V$ is adjacent to all of $N_Y$, so likewise $f(w)\in R(w)$.
\end{enumerate}

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Extend the colouring $f$ to $J$.}\par\nopagebreak\smallskip
We claim that $J$ has an $R$-colouring. Suppose not. By~\eqref{eq:Rsizes} the Deficiency
Lemma~\ref{lem:deficiency} applies to $J=K(X,Y)-M$ with the lists $R$. This gives a set
$T\subseteq U\cup V$ with
\[
T_U:=T\cap U\ne\varnothing,\qquad T_V:=T\cap V\ne\varnothing,\qquad |R(T)|\le|T|+\mu-1 .
\]
The following holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(C\arabic*)},leftmargin=3em]
\item\label{C1} \emph{$T$ has nonempty intersection with $S$.}\\ 
For $y\in T_V$ we have $L(y)\subseteq R(y)\cupF_Y$, hence $L(T_V)\subseteq R(T)\cupF_Y$. Using the
definition of $m_Y$ (note $T_V\ne\varnothing$), followed by $|F_Y|\le r$ and the bound on $|R(T)|$ we have,
\[
m_Y+|T_V|\ \le\ |L(T_V)|\ \le\ |R(T)|+|F_Y|\ \le\ |T_U|+|T_V|+\mu-1+r .
\]
Since $\mu+r=k-s$, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TUlower}
|T_U|\ \ge\ m_Y+s-k+1 .
\end{equation}
If $T\cap S=\varnothing$, then $T_U\subseteq U\setminus S$, and by \eqref{eq:Sbig} and \eqref{eq:side}
\[
|T_U|\ \le\ r-|S|\ \le\ m_X+r-k\ \le\ m_Y+s-k ,
\]
contradicting \eqref{eq:TUlower}. Hence $T\cap S\ne\varnothing$.
\item\label{C2} \emph{The set $T'=T\cup S$ satisfies $|R(T')|\le|T'|+\mu-1$.}\\ 
Put $I=T\cap S$. This is nonempty by \ref{C1}. All vertices of $S$ lie in $X$, so by
\eqref{eq:residual} the same set $F_X$ is removed from each of their lists, and therefore
\[
R(S)\setminus R(I)=\bigl(L(S)\setminus L(I)\bigr)\setminusF_X .
\qquad\text{Hence}\qquad
|R(S)\setminus R(I)|\le|S\setminus I|=|S\setminus T|
\]
by \eqref{eq:Smin}. Since $R(I)\subseteq R(T)$, the new colours contributed by $S$ satisfy
$R(T')\setminus R(T)=R(S)\setminus R(T)\subseteq R(S)\setminus R(I)$. Hence
\[
|R(T')|\ \le\ |R(T)|+|S\setminus T|\ \le\ |T|+\mu-1+|S\setminus T|\ =\ |T'|+\mu-1 .
\]
\end{enumerate}
Note that $X_0$ and $T'$ are disjoint subsets of the clique $Q$ of \ref{B2}, so $f$ takes $\mu+|T'|$
pairwise distinct values on $X_0\cup T'$. By \ref{B2} all of them lie in $R(z)\cup R(T')=R(T')$,
because $z\in S\subseteq T'$. Hence $|R(T')|\ge\mu+|T'|$, contradicting \ref{C2}.
This means that  $J$ must admit an $R$-colouring, and by observation \ref{B1} the graph $G$ has an $L$-colouring. This contradicts the choice of $(G,L)$ and proves the proposition.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:linegraph}
Suppose that every augment in the representation of $G$ is complete. Let $H'$ be obtained from
$H$ by replacing, for every augmented flat edge $e_Xe_Y$ with augment $(X,Y)$, the edge
$e_X=p_X c$ by $|X|$ parallel copies and the edge $e_Y=cp_Y$ by $|Y|$ parallel copies. Then $H'$ is
a bipartite multigraph, $G\cong L(H')$, and $\Delta(H')\le\omega(G)$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Distinct augmented flat edges are vertex-disjoint in $L(H)$, so they involve distinct edges of
$H$ and the replacements do not interfere. $H'$ has the same vertex set and bipartition as $H$,
so it is bipartite. Define $\varphi\colon V(G)\to E(H')$ by sending every vertex of $G$ that is an
unaugmented edge of $H$ to itself, the $|X|$ vertices of each side $X$ bijectively to the copies
of $e_X$, and the $|Y|$ vertices of each side $Y$ bijectively to the copies of $e_Y$. It is not difficult to verify that this is a
bijection, and  that it preserves adjacency and non-adjacency. That is, $\varphi$ defines an
isomorphism $G\to L(H')$.
Finally, the edges of $H'$ at any vertex form a clique of $L(H')\cong G$, so $\Delta(H')\le\omega(G)$.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let $G$, $L$ and $k$ be as fixed before. By Proposition~\ref{prop:noincomplete}
every augment of $G$ is complete, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:linegraph} $G\cong L(H')$ for a bipartite
multigraph $H'$ with $\Delta(H')\le\omega(G)\le k$. 
Galvin's theorem now gives an $L$-colouring of $G$. This contradicts the choice of $L$. Hence no counterexample exists, and
every elementary graph with clique number at most $k$ is $k$-choosable proving the claim and  Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
