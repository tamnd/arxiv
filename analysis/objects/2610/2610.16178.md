<!-- deff-1: body_md -->
\label{z1}
		A ring $R$ is called $Z$-Noetherian if every ideal $I\subseteq Z(R)$ is finitely generated.
<!-- end deff-1 -->
<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
When $Z(R)=Nil(R)$, the notions of $Z$-Noetherian and Nil$_{*}$-Noetherian ring same. Obviously, $Nil(R)\subseteq Z(R)$ but $Z(R)\nsubseteq Nil(R)$ (in general) for this case $Z$-Noetherian ring is different class of ring which lies between Noetherian and  Nil$_{*}$-Noetherian ring class. For more details, we illustrate an example of ring which is $Nil_{*}$-Noetherian ring but not a $Z$-Noetherian as follows:
<!-- end rem-1 -->
<!-- eg-1: body_md -->
\label{z2}
		Let $R=\prod\limits_{i\in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z}_{2}$. Then $Z(R)=\{(a_i)\in R \mid \text{there exists} i \text{ such that} a_i=0\}$, and Nil$(R)=(0)$. Thus $R$ is a Nil$_{*}$-Noetherian ring. Let $I=\{(a_1,a_2,\dots)\in R \mid a_i=0  ~for ~all ~but ~finitely ~many~  i\}$. Then $I$ is an ideal of $R$ with $I\subseteq Z(R)$, but $I$ is not finitely generated. Hence $R$ is not a $Z$-Noetherian ring.
<!-- end eg-1 -->
<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
Let $R$ be a $Z$-Noetherian ring and let $I$ be a nil ideal of $R$. Then $R/I$ is $Z$-Noetherian.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\varphi : R \longrightarrow R/I$ be the natural ring homomorphism defined by $\varphi(a)=a+I$. Let $J$ be an ideal of $R/I$ such that $J \subseteq Z(R/I)$. Set
		$J'=\varphi^{-1}(J)$. Then $J'$ is an ideal of $R$. We claim that $J' \subseteq Z(R)$. Indeed, let $a\in J'$. Then $a+I\in J\subseteq Z(R/I)$, so there exists $b+I\in R\setminus I$ such that $(a+I)(b+I)=I$. Hence $ab\in I$.
		Since $I$ is a nil ideal, there exists $n\ge 1$ such that
		$(ab)^n=0$. Since $b\notin I$, and hence $b^n\neq 0$ for any $n\in\mathbb{N}$. Therefore $a^n
		\in  Z(R)$, which implies that $a\in Z(R)$. Thus $J'\subseteq Z(R)$. Since $R$ is a $Z$-Noetherian, $J'$ is finitely generated. Finally, since
		$J=\varphi(J')$, it follows that $J$ is finitely generated.
		Therefore $R/I$ is $Z$--Noetherian.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{n}
		Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring, and let $I$ be an ideal of $R$ such that $I \subseteq Z(R)$. Then $\operatorname{Ann}_R(I) \neq (0)$.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
\noindent
		Suppose $R$ is Noetherian, the set of zero divisors of $R$ is the union of the associated primes of the $R$-module $R$: $Z(R) = \bigcup_{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Ass}_R(R)} \mathfrak{p}$, by \cite[Theorem 4.7]{fm69}. Also $\operatorname{Ass}_R(R)$ is finite, and each $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Ass}_R(R)$ \[
		\mathfrak{p} = \operatorname{Ann}(m) = \{ r \in R : rm = 0 \}, \quad m \in R,\ m \neq 0\]
		Since $I\subseteq Z(R)$, we have $I\subseteq \bigcup_{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Ass}_R(R)} \mathfrak{p}$,  by \cite[Proposition 1.11]{fm69}, $I\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ for some $\mathfrak{p}\in \operatorname{Ass}_R(R)$. Since $I\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ so
		$am = 0$ for all $a \in I$. Hence $m\in \operatorname{Ann}_R(I)$ and $m \neq 0$, so $\operatorname{Ann}_R(I) \neq(0) $.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{j1}
		Let $R$ be a commutative ring. The following are equivalent:
		\begin{enumerate}
			\item $R$ is $Z$-Noetherian.
			
			\item Every ideal $I\subseteq Z(R)$ is finitely generated.
			
			\item The set of ideals contained in $Z(R)$ satisfies the ascending chain condition.
			
			\item Every nonempty set of ideals contained in $Z(R)$ has a maximal element.
		\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
\noindent
		$(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)$ follows directly from the definition.\\
		$(2)\Rightarrow(3)$. Let $I_1 \subseteq I_2 \subseteq I_3 \subseteq \cdots$ be an ascending chain of ideals of $R$ such each $I_{i\in\mathbf{N}}$ contained in $Z(R)$. Set $I=\bigcup\limits_{j\ge 1} I_j$. Clearly $I\subseteq Z(R)$ and $I$ is an ideal of $R$. By assumption, $I$ is finitely generated, say $I=(a_1,a_2,\dots,a_m)$. Each generator $a_r$ belongs to some $I_{N_r}$. Let $N=\max\{N_1,\dots,N_m\}$.
		Then $a_1,\dots,a_m\in I_N$, hence $I\subseteq I_N$.
		Since always $I_N\subseteq I$, we obtain $I=I_N$. Therefore the chain stabilizes, and the ascending chain condition holds.\\
		                     
		                                                                                                                                                               
		$(3)\Rightarrow(4)$: Assume that ACC holds for ideals contained in $Z(R)$. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a nonempty family of ideals with each $I\in\mathcal{F}$ satisfying $I\subseteq Z(R)$. Suppose that $\mathcal{F}$ has no maximal element.Then for every $I_j\in\mathcal{F}$ there exist $I_{j+1}\in\mathcal{F}$ with $I_j \subsetneq I_{j+1}$. Then we can construct a strictly increasing chain $I_1 \subsetneq I_2 \subsetneq I_3 \subsetneq \cdots$ with each $I_j\in\mathcal{F}$, contradicting ACC. Hence $\mathcal{F}$ must have a maximal element.\\
		$(4)\Rightarrow(3)$: 
		Assume that every nonempty family of ideals contained in $Z(R)$ has a maximal element. If ACC failed, then there would exist a strictly increasing chain $I_1 \subsetneq I_2 \subsetneq I_3 \subsetneq \cdots$ of ideals contained in $Z(R)$. Then the family
		$\{ I_n \mid n\in\mathbb{N} \}$ would have no maximal element, contradicting (4). Therefore ACC holds for ideals contained in $Z(R)$.\\
		$(3)\Rightarrow(2)$: Assume that ACC holds for ideals contained in $Z(R)$. Let $I\subseteq Z(R)$ be an ideal. Suppose that $I$ is not finitely generated. Then we can choose elements $a_1,a_2,a_3,\dots \in I$ such that $(a_1)\subsetneq (a_1,a_2)\subsetneq (a_1,a_2,a_3)\subsetneq \cdots$. This gives a strictly ascending chain of ideals contained in $Z(R)$, contradicting ACC. Therefore $I$ must be finitely generated.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{j2}
		Let $R$ be a $Z$ Noetherian ring, and let $I$ be an ideal of $R$ such that $I\subseteq Z(R)$. If $I$ is an irreducible ideal of $R$, then $I$ is a primary ideal.
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
\noindent
		To prove that $I$ is primary, we must show that if $ab \in I$ and $b \notin I$, then there exists an integer $n \geq 1$ such that $a^n \in I$. Consider the colon ideal $(I : a^n) = \{\, r \in R \mid ra^{n}\in I \,\}$. Since $R$ is a $Z$-Noetherian ring, any ascending chain of ideals contained in $Z(R)$ stabilizes, by Theorem \ref{j1}. We divide the proof into two cases.
		\medskip
		
		\noindent\textbf{Case 1:} $(I : a^n) \subseteq Z(R)$ for all $n \geq 1$. Consider the ascending chain $(I : a) \subseteq (I : a^2) \subseteq (I : a^3) \subseteq \cdots$. By assumption, all these ideals are contained in $Z(R)$, so the $Z$-Noetherian property implies that the chain stabilizes, by Theorem \ref{j1}. Hence, there exists $N \geq 1$ such that $(I : a^N) = (I : a^{N+1})$. We claim that $I = (I + (a^n)) \cap (I + (b))$. The inclusion $I \subseteq (I + (a^n)) \cap (I + (b))$ is clear. Conversely, let $x \in (I + (a^n)) \cap (I + (b))$. Then $x = i_1 + r a^n= i_2 + s b$ for some $i_1, i_2 \in I$ and $r, s \in R$. Multiplying by $a$, we get $ax = a i_1 + r a^{n+1} = a i_2 + s ab$. Since $i_1, i_2 \in I$ and $ab \in I$, the right-hand side lies in $I$. Hence $ax \in I$. Thus $a i_1 + r a^{n+1} \in I$. Since $a i_1 \in I$, it follows that $r a^{n+1} \in I$, so $r \in (I : a^{n+1}) = (I : a^n)$. Hence $ra^n \in I$, and therefore $x = i_1 + r a^n \in I$. Thus $(I + (a^n)) \cap (I + (b)) \subseteq I$, and equality holds. Since $I$ is irreducible, it follows that either $I = I + (a^n)$ or $I = I + (b)$. As $b \notin I$, we must have $I = I + (a^n)$, which implies $a^n \in I$, as desired.
		\medskip
		
		\noindent \textbf{Case 2:} $(I : a^n) \nsubseteq Z(R)$ for some $n \geq 1$. Then there exists $y \in (I : a^n)$ such that $y \notin Z(R)$. Hence $y$ is a regular element. Since $y \in (I : a^n)$, we have $y a^n \in I$. Consider the chain $(I : y) \subseteq (I : y^2) \subseteq (I : y^3) \subseteq \cdots$. Let $z \in (I : y^k)$. Then $z y^k \in I \subseteq Z(R)$. Since $y$ is regular, so is $y^k$, and hence $z$ must be a zero-divisor. Thus $(I : y^k) \subseteq Z(R)$ for all $k \geq 1$. By the $Z$-Noetherian property, the chain stabilizes, by Theorem \ref{j1}. So there exists $l\geq 1$ such that $(I : y^l) = (I : y^{l+1})$. We claim that $I = (I + (y^l)) \cap (I + (a^n))$. Let $x \in (I + (y^l)) \cap (I + (a^n))$. Then $x = j_1 + u y^l= j_2 + v a^n$ for some $j_1, j_2 \in I$ and $u, v \in R$. Multiplying by $y$, we get $yx = y j_1 + u y^{l+1} = y j_2 + v y a^n$. Since $y a^n \in I$ and $j_2 \in I$, the right-hand side lies in $I$, so $yx \in I$. Thus $y j_1 + u y^{l+1} \in I$. Since $y j_1 \in I$, we get $u y^{l+1} \in I$, hence $u \in (I : y^{l+1}) = (I : y^l)$. Therefore $u y^l \in I$, and so $x = j_1 + u y^l\in I$. Thus $(I + (y^M)) \cap (I + (a^n)) = I$. Since $y$ is regular, $y^l$ is also regular, so $y^M \notin I$ (because $I \subseteq Z(R)$). Hence $I \neq I + (y^l)$. Since $I$ is irreducible, it follows that $I = I + (a^n)$, so $a^n \in I$. Therefore, in both cases, $a^n \in I$ for some $n \geq 1$, and hence $I$ is primary.

\label{sp}
		\textbf{(Existence of primary decomposition in $Z$-Noetherian rings)}
		Let $R$ be a $Z$-Noetherian ring such that $Z(R)$ is an ideal of $R$. Then every proper ideal $I$ of $R$ contained in $Z(R)$ can be expressed as a finite intersection of primary ideals.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
\noindent
		Let $E= \{\,I \subseteq Z(R) \mid I~is~not~ a~ finite~ intersection~of~ primary~ ideals \,\}$. 
		We claim that $E = \emptyset$. Suppose, to the contrary, that $E \neq \emptyset$. Since $R$ is a $Z$-Noetherian ring, by Theorem~\ref{j1}, $E$ contains a maximal element with respect to inclusion. Let $I\in E$ be such a maximal element. Clearly, $I$ is not a primary ideal. By Lemma~\ref{j2}, $I$ is not irreducible. Hence there exist proper ideals $K$ and $L$ of $R$ such that $I = K \cap L$, with $I \subsetneq K$ and $I \subsetneq L$. Set $K_1 = K \cap Z(R)$ and $L_1= L \cap Z(R)$. Since $I \subseteq Z(R)$, $K_1 \cap L_1 = (K \cap Z(R)) \cap (L \cap Z(R)) = (K \cap L) \cap Z(R) = I \cap Z(R) = I$, so $I = K_1 \cap L_1$, with $K_1, L_1 \subseteq Z(R)$. We claim that $K_1, L_1 \notin E$. Suppose, for instance, that $K_1 \in E$. Since $I \subsetneq K_1$ and $I$ is maximal in $E$, it follows that $K_1 = I$, a contradiction. Similarly, $L_1 \notin E$. Then  $L_1$ and $K_1$ is a finite intersection of primary ideals. Since $I = K_1 \cap L_1$, it follows that $I$ is also a finite intersection of primary ideals, which contradicts the fact that $I \in E$. Therefore, $E = \emptyset$. Hence, every ideal $I \subseteq Z(R)$ can be written as a finite intersection of primary ideals.

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
\noindent
		Assume first that $R$ is $Z$--Noetherian. Then every ideal $I$ of $R$ with $I\subseteq Z(R)$ is finitely generated. Hence, every prime ideal $P\subseteq Z(R)$ is finitely generated. Conversely, suppose that every prime ideal $P$ of $R$ with $P\subseteq Z(R)$ is finitely generated. Assume that $R$ is not $Z$--Noetherian. Let $\mathcal{F}=\{ I\subseteq Z(R)\mid I ~is ~non ~finitely ~generated ~ideal ~of ~R\}$. Then $\mathcal{F}\neq\varnothing$. By Zorn's Lemma, $\mathcal{F}$ has a maximal element $P$.
		Since $R$ is a ZD--ring, the set $Z_R(R/P)$ is a finite union of prime ideals of $R$, say $Z_R(R/P)=\bigcup_{i=1}^n P_i$, where each $P_i$ is a prime ideal of $R$.
		We show that $P\subseteq Z_R(R/P)$. Let $p\in P$. Since $p\in Z(R)$, there exists $r\in R\setminus\{0\}$ such that $pr=0$. If $r\in P$, then $r+P=0$ in $R/P$. Otherwise, $r+P\neq 0$ in $R/P$, and in either case we have $p(r+P)=0$ in $R/P$. Hence $p\in Z_R(R/P)$, and therefore
		$P\subseteq Z_R(R/P)$. Thus $P\subseteq P_i$ for some $i$. By the maximality of $P$ in $\mathcal{F}$, we must have $P=P_i$. Hence $P$ is a prime ideal of $R$ contained in $Z(R)$. By hypothesis, $P$ is finitely generated, which contradicts the choice of $P$. Therefore $R$ is $Z$--Noetherian.

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
\noindent
		First, note that $J = \bigcap_{n=1}^\infty I^n \subseteq I$, and hence $IJ \subseteq I^2 \subseteq I \subseteq Z(R)$. Thus $B = IJ \subseteq Z(R)$. Since $B \subseteq Z(R)$ and $R$ is $Z$-Noetherian, by Theorem 2.9, the ideal $B$ admits a primary decomposition: $B = B_1 \cap B_2 \cap \dots \cap B_k$, where each $B_i$ is $P_i$-primary and $P_i = \text{rad}(B_i)$. Fix $i$ ($1 \le i \le k$). Observe that $IJ = B \subseteq B_i$. As $B_i$ is $P_i$-primary, either $J \subseteq B_i$ or $I \subseteq P_i$.
		
		\begin{itemize}
			\item \textbf{Case 1: $I \subseteq P_i$.} Since $\text{rad}(I) \subseteq Z(R)$ and $R$ is $Z$-Noetherian, the ideal $I$ is contained in $Z(R)$ and is therefore finitely generated. Write $I =(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_l )$. For each generator $x_j \in I$, since $I \subseteq P_i = \text{rad}(B_i)$, there exists an integer $n_j \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $x_j^{n_j} \in B_i$. 			\item \textbf{Case 2: $I \not\subseteq P_i$.} There exists an element $a \in I \setminus P_i$. Since $aJ \subseteq IJ = B \subseteq B_i$
			and $B_i$ is $P_i$-primary, by the definition of primary ideals, we have $J \subseteq B_i$.
		\end{itemize}
		Combining both cases, we conclude that $J \subseteq B_i$ for all $1 \le i \le k$. Hence, $J \subseteq \bigcap_{i=1}^k B_i = B = IJ$. Since it is trivially true that $IJ \subseteq J$, we obtain the equality $J = IJ$. Note that $J$ is finitely generated because $J \subseteq Z(R)$ and $R$ is a $Z$-Noetherian ring. Finally, \cite[Corollary 2.5]{fm69}, there exists an element $r\in R$ with $r\cong 1(mod (I))$ such that $rJ = 0$. This completes the proof.

Let $J$ be an ideal of $R[x]$ such that $J \subseteq Z(R[x])$. We show that $J$ is finitely generated.
		For each $i \ge 0$, define $L_i$ to be the set consisting of $0$ and the leading coefficients of all polynomials in $J$ of degree $i$. Then each $L_i$ is an ideal of $R$. Moreover, since $f(x) \in J$ implies $x f(x) \in J$, we obtain an ascending chain
		\begin{center}
			$
			L_0 \subseteq L_1 \subseteq L_2 \subseteq \cdots.
			$
		\end{center}
		Let $L = \bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty} L_i$. Then $L$ is an ideal of $R$.

		Let $a \in L$, $a \neq 0$. Then $a$ is the leading coefficient of some $f(x) \in J$. Since $J \subseteq Z(R[x])$, $f(x)$ is a zero divisor in $R[x]$. By Lemma \ref{am1}, there exists $0 \neq c \in R$ such that $c f(x) = 0$. Hence $c a = 0$, so $a \in Z(R)$. Thus $L \subseteq Z(R)$.
		Since $R$ is $Z$-Noetherian and $L \subseteq Z(R)$, the ideal $L$ is finitely generated.
		Since $L = \bigcup L_i \subseteq Z(R)$, it follows that each $L_i \subseteq Z(R)$. Since $L$ is finitely generated and $L = \bigcup L_i$, all generators lie in some $L_m$, hence: $L = L_m$.
		Hence the chain stabilizes. Then there exists $m$ such that
		$L_m = L_{m+1} = \cdots = L$.
		Each $L_i \subseteq Z(R)$, so each $L_i$ is finitely generated.
		For each $i = 0,1,\dots,m-1$,  construct the  generators. Suppose
		$L_i = (a_{i1}, a_{i2}, \dots, a_{ik_i})$, and choose $f_{ij}(x) \in J$ of degree $i$ with leading coefficient $a_{ij}$.
		Similarly, let $L_m = (a_{m1}, a_{m2}, \dots, a_{mk_m})$, 
		and choose $f_{mj}(x) \in J$ of degree $m$ with leading coefficient $a_{mj}$. Let $J'$ be the ideal generated by all $f_{ij}(x) ,~ 0 \leq i \leq m,\quad 1 \leq j \leq k_i$. Then $J' \subseteq J$.
		
		\noindent Now we prove that $J = J'$ by induction on the degree $d$ that every $f(x) \in J$ lies in $J'$.
		
		\noindent\textit{\textbf{Case 1: $d \ge m$.}}  
		Let $a$ be the leading coefficient of $f(x)$. Then $a \in L_d = L_m$, so $a = \sum_{j=1}^{k_m} r_j a_{mj}$. Define $g(x) = \sum_{j=1}^{k_m} r_j x^{d-m} f_{mj}(x)$. Then $g(x) \in J'$ and $\deg g = d$, and its leading coefficient is $a$. Hence $f(x) - g(x) \in J$ has degree $< d$.
