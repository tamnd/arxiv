\label{thm:mechanism}
Let $F\colon\mathbb P^2\to\mathbb P^2$ be a holomorphic endomorphism of algebraic degree
$d\ge2$ satisfying hypothesis
\hyperref[hyp:H]{(H)}.
Suppose that the fast direction is history-dependent.
Then the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $\lambda_2\ge2\log d$, then
$\dim_{\mathrm H}\mu_F=2\log d/\lambda_2$.
\item If $\log d<\lambda_2<2\log d$, then
$\dim_{\mathrm H}\mu_F\ge1+(2\log d-\lambda_2)/\lambda_1$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, if $\lambda_2>\log d$, then
$\dim_{\mathrm H}\mu_F-\mathcal D_{\mathrm{P}}(F)>0$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:holomorphic-leaves}
There are constants $r_*>0$ and $C_*<\infty$ such that, for every
$\hat x\in\hat K$, the ball $B(x_0,r_*)$ admits holomorphic
coordinates $(u,v)$ whose $v$-fibers are the local strong unstable
leaves associated with the inverse history of $\hat x$.
On a fixed smaller neighborhood, the coordinate maps and their
inverses have $C^2$ norms bounded by $C_*$.
The coordinates can be chosen continuously on each member of a
finite open cover of $\hat K$, and coordinates associated with
the same inverse history define the same foliation on overlaps.

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
After passing to a fixed iterate,
the domination estimate \eqref{eq:uniform-domination} gives uniform
contraction of slopes in the fast cones. This contraction persists
on complex neighborhoods whose radius is independent of the
history and the backward iterate. The inverse branches map these
neighborhoods into the corresponding backward neighborhoods.
Starting with a constant line field in the fast cone at a backward
iterate and transporting it forward along the prescribed history
gives holomorphic line fields converging locally uniformly,
with error at most $C\rho^n$ after $n$ graph transform steps,
where $C>0$ and $0<\rho<1$ are independent of the history.
The limit is independent of the initial field, depends continuously
on the history, and has value $E^f(\hat x)$ at $x_0$.
Its integral curves are the strong unstable leaves by uniqueness
of the graph transform.

For the fixed inverse history, write the limiting field in suitable
linear coordinates $(z,w)$ as $\operatorname{span}(1,\sigma(z,w))$,
where $\sigma$ is its holomorphic slope function.
Solving $dw/dz=\sigma(z,w)$ with initial values on a transverse
complex disk gives the required holomorphic flow box.
Uniform cone bounds and Cauchy estimates give uniform $C^2$ bounds
for the coordinates and their inverses after a fixed shrinking.
Choose the initial coordinates and transverse disks continuously
on each member of a finite open cover of $\hat K$.
Continuous dependence of the flow then gives continuous choices
of leaf coordinates, while uniqueness of the limiting line field
makes the foliations agree on overlaps for the same history.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:conditional-mass}

Let $\nu$ be a Borel probability measure on $\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^n$,
let $\zeta$ be its projection to the second factor, and write
$\nu=\int\nu_v\,d\zeta(v)$ 
for the disintegration of $\nu$ with respect to $\zeta$, so that $\nu_v$ is the conditional probability on
$\mathbb R^m\times\{v\}$, identified with a measure on $\mathbb R^m$.
Suppose $\underline d_{\nu_v}(y)\ge\alpha\ge0$ for
$\nu$-almost every $(y,v) \in \mathbb R^m \times \mathbb R^n$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item
If $\nu$ is exact dimensional of dimension $D$, then
$\overline d_\zeta(v)\le D-\alpha$ for $\zeta$-almost every $v$.

\item
Suppose $\underline d_\zeta(v)\ge\beta\ge0$ for
$\zeta$-almost every $v$.
Fix $a,b>0$ and translation vectors $h_f\in\mathbb R^m$ and
$h_t\in\mathbb R^n$, and let
$\mathcal R_k$ be the product partition $\mathcal D_{\lfloor ak\rfloor}^{h_f}
\times\mathcal D_{\lfloor bk\rfloor}^{h_t}$.
Then
$$
 \liminf_{k\to\infty}
 \frac{\log\nu(\mathcal R_k(x))}{-k\log2}
 \ge a\alpha+b\beta
 \qquad\text{for $\nu$-almost every $x$,}
$$
where $\mathcal R_k(x)$ is the element in the partition $\mathcal R_k$ containing $x$.
\end{enumerate}
Both (i) and (ii) remain valid under a bi-Lipschitz change
of local product coordinates preserving the fiber foliation,
with the product partition in (ii) pulled back under
the coordinate map.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix $\varepsilon>0$.
Since $\underline d_{\nu_v}(y)\ge\alpha\ge0$, there are measurable
sets $J_N$ increasing to full $\nu$-measure such that
$\nu_v(B(y,r))\le r^{\alpha-\varepsilon}$ whenever
$(y,v)\in J_N$ and $0<r<1/N$.
Suppose a Borel set $E$ has fiber sections of diameter at most
$r_f<1/(2N)$ and its projection to $\mathbb R^n$ is contained in a
ball $B_t$.
On each fiber meeting $J_N\cap E$, a ball of radius $2r_f$
centered at a point of that intersection contains the fiber
section of $E$.
Applying
$\nu_v(B(y,2r_f))\leq (2r_f)^{\alpha-\varepsilon}$
on each such fiber and integrating over $v\in B_t$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-mass-estimate}
 \nu(J_N\cap E)
 \le C_\varepsilon r_f^{\alpha-\varepsilon}\zeta(B_t).
\end{equation}

\smallskip

For (i), the differentiation theorem for finite Borel measures
on Euclidean space gives
$\nu(J_N\cap B(x,r))/\nu(B(x,r))\to1$
as $r\to0$
for $\nu$-almost every $x\in J_N$.
Thus $\nu(B(x,r))\le2\nu(J_N\cap B(x,r))$ for
$\nu$-almost every $x=(y,v)\in J_N$ and all sufficiently
small $r$.
Applying \eqref{eq:conditional-mass-estimate}
with $E=B(x,r)$, $r_f=2r$, and $B_t=B(v,r)$ yields
$\nu(B(x,r))\le C_\varepsilon
r^{\alpha-\varepsilon}\zeta(B(v,r))$.
Exact dimensionality gives
$\nu(B(x,r))\ge r^{D+\varepsilon}$ for all sufficiently
small $r$, so
$\zeta(B(v,r))\ge C_\varepsilon^{-1}
r^{D-\alpha+2\varepsilon}$.
Hence $\overline d_\zeta(v)\le D-\alpha+2\varepsilon$
for $\nu$-almost every $(y,v)\in J_N$.
Taking the union over $N$, this inequality holds for
$\nu$-almost every $(y,v)$, hence for $\zeta$-almost every $v$,
since it depends only on $v$.
Letting $\varepsilon\to0$ along a countable sequence proves (i).

\smallskip

For (ii), let $\mathcal F_k:=\sigma(\mathcal R_k)$ be the
$\sigma$-algebra generated by the elements of $\mathcal R_k$.
These $\sigma$-algebras increase and together generate the Borel
$\sigma$-algebra. L\'evy's upward theorem
\cite[Section 14.2]{Williams1991} gives
$\mathbb E_\nu(\mathbf1_{J_N}\mid\mathcal F_k)\to\mathbf1_{J_N}$
$\nu$-almost everywhere, and therefore
$\nu(J_N\cap\mathcal R_k(x))/\nu(\mathcal R_k(x))\to1$
for $\nu$-almost every $x\in J_N$.
Thus
$\nu(\mathcal R_k(x))\le2\nu(J_N\cap\mathcal R_k(x))$
for $\nu$-almost every $x=(y,v)\in J_N$ and all sufficiently
large $k$.
Apply \eqref{eq:conditional-mass-estimate} with
$E=\mathcal R_k(x)$,
$r_f=C2^{-ak}$,
and $B_t=B(v,C2^{-bk})$, where $C$ depends
only on $m$ and $n$.
The assumption $\underline d_\zeta(v)\ge\beta$ gives
$\zeta(B_t)\le C_\varepsilon2^{-bk(\beta-\varepsilon)}$
for all sufficiently large $k$, depending on $v$.
Thus
$\nu(\mathcal R_k(x))
\le C_\varepsilon
2^{-k(a(\alpha-\varepsilon)+b(\beta-\varepsilon))}$.
Taking logarithms, then the lower limit, and finally the
union over $N$ and $\varepsilon\to0$ proves (ii).

\smallskip

For local product coordinates given by a bi-Lipschitz map $\Phi$
preserving the fiber foliation, apply the preceding argument
to the pushforward $\Phi_*\nu$.
The coordinate map and its restrictions to fibers preserve
local dimensions, and the partition elements in the original coordinates
are $\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal R_k(\Phi(x)))$.

\label{prop:dimension}
Under \hyperref[hyp:H]{(H)}, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item
The equilibrium measure $\mu$ is exact dimensional, with
$\dim_{\mathrm H}\mu=h_1/\lambda_1+(2L-h_1)/\lambda_2$.

\item

\item

$(p_s)_*\mu_U$ is exact dimensional with dimension
$(2L-h_1)/\lambda_2$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, we have
$\dim_{\mathrm H}\mu-\mathcal D_{\mathrm{P}}(F)=(L-h_1)(\lambda_2^{-1}-\lambda_1^{-1})$

We first establish an atomicity dichotomy. The remaining two steps
use standard arguments to exclude the Dirac case under history
dependence and to select the required pair of histories.

\smallskip

\emph{Step 1: Atomicity dichotomy.}
We show that either $\eta_x$ is Dirac for $\mu$-almost every $x$,
or $\eta_x$ is non-atomic for $\mu$-almost every $x$.

Set $A_x:=\mathbb P(DF_x)$.
Since $DF$ is non-singular on $K$, each $A_x$ is a projective
automorphism.
The equal weights of the inverse branches and equivariance
of the fast direction give
$$
 \eta_y=\frac1q\sum_{Fx=y}(A_x)_*\eta_x,
$$
where the sum runs over the $q$ preimages of $y$ in $K$.
Let $m(x)$ be the largest atom mass of $\eta_x$, with
$m(x)=0$ when $\eta_x$ is non-atomic, and define the transfer
operator 
on bounded measurable functions
by
$$
 (P\varphi)(y):=\frac1q\sum_{Fx=y}\varphi(x).
$$
The preceding identity gives $m\le Pm$.
Since $\int P\varphi\,d\mu=\int\varphi\,d\mu$,
we have $m=Pm$ almost everywhere.
Jensen's inequality gives $(Pm)^2\le P(m^2)$, and these
two functions have the same integral.
Equality therefore holds almost everywhere, so the values
of $m$ agree on the preimages of almost every $y$.
Their common value is $(Pm)(y)=m(y)$.
Thus $m(x)=m(Fx)$ almost everywhere, and ergodicity makes
$m$ constant almost everywhere. If this constant is zero, then $\eta_x$
is non-atomic almost everywhere.

Suppose instead
that $m>0$, and let $\mathcal A_x$ be the
set of atoms of $\eta_x$ having mass $m$.
For $\ell\in\mathcal A_y$, the identity
$$
 m=\frac1q\sum_{Fx=y}\eta_x(\{A_x^{-1}\ell\})
$$
forces every summand to equal $m$.
Hence $A_x^{-1}\mathcal A_{Fx}\subseteq\mathcal A_x$
almost everywhere
and therefore,
 for almost every $x$ and every $n\geq1$, we have
$\mathcal A_{F^n x}
 \subset \mathbb P(DF_x^n)\mathcal A_x$.
The support of $\eta_x$ consists of fast directions arising from
inverse histories over $x$. By \eqref{eq:uniform-domination} and compactness these
directions are uniformly transverse to $E^s(x)$.
Fix $\ell_x\in\mathcal A_x$.
In the splitting $\ell_x\oplus E^s(x)$, the other lines
of $\mathcal A_x$ have uniformly bounded slopes,
which contract under the projective action by
\eqref{eq:uniform-domination}.
Consequently, we have
$\operatorname{diam}
 \bigl(\mathbb P(DF_x^n)\mathcal A_x\bigr)
 \le C_0\tau^n$,
for some $C_0$ independent of $x$ and $n$.
Thus, setting $D(x):=\operatorname{diam}\mathcal A_x$, we have
$$
 \int D\,d\mu=\int D\circ F^n\,d\mu\le C_0\tau^n.
$$
Letting $n\to\infty$ gives $D=0$ almost everywhere.
Thus $\mathcal A_x=\{\ell(x)\}$ for a measurable line field
satisfying $A_x\ell(x)=\ell(Fx)$ almost everywhere.
The event $\{E^f(\hat x)=\ell(x_0)\}$ is invariant and has measure
$m>0$.
By ergodicity,
its $\hat\mu$-measure is therefore one,
so
$\eta_x$ is Dirac almost everywhere. This proves the dichotomy.

\emph{Step 2: Excluding the Dirac case.}

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:inverse-reduction}
Fix two integers $m,\ell\ge1$
and three real numbers 
$0<\kappa<1$, $R_*\ge1$, and $c_0>0$.
There exists $\delta_0>0$ such that, for every $0<\delta<\delta_0$
and all sufficiently large $n$, the following holds.
Let $\omega$ be a $2^{-n}$-grid probability and let
$A,B\subset[-R_*,R_*]^m$ lie on the same grid.
Suppose that
$\omega(A)\ge1/2$
and
$2\le|B|\le2^{(1-\kappa)n}$.
For each $a\in A$, 
choose a subset $B_a\subset B$ such that
$|B_a|\ge |B|^{1-\delta}$.
If
$$
 \left|\bigcup_{a\in A}(a+B_a)\right|
 \le |B|^{1+\delta},
$$
then there is $E\subset A$ with
$\omega(E)\ge2^{-c_0n}$ such that every $x\in E$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:unique descendant}  on at least $\kappa n/2$ scales in
$\{0,\ldots,n-\ell\}$.

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
In this proof, the constants implicit in $O(\cdot)$
may depend
on the fixed parameters but are independent of $n$.
Set
$$
 N_m:=8\cdot2^m
 \qquad \text{and} \qquad
 \xi_T:=T^{-1}\log_2(2mT+2).
$$
Choose an integer $T$, the number of consecutive dyadic
scales in each regularization block, so large that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-length-choice}
T>4\ell(N_m+1)
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
\frac1T+\xi_T<\frac{c_0}{3}.
\end{equation}
The remaining choices are made in the order $\rho$, $\delta_0$,
and then $n$, which is taken sufficiently large.

\smallskip

\noindent {\bf Step 1: Reduction to an entropy problem.}
This step is essentially the same as in Wu's proof: the mass
decomposition, asymmetric Balog--Szemer\'edi--Gowers reduction, and regularization argument
carry over directly to $\mathbb R^m$, with only the bookkeeping changes
indicated in Steps 1.1--1.4.

\smallskip
\noindent
\emph{Step 1.1: Mass decomposition
(see \cite[(4.17)--(4.18)]{Wu2025}).}
We begin exactly as in \cite{Wu2025}, 
grouping points of $A$ according to
their $\omega$-masses. The only change is the number of grid points:
a bounded $2^{-n}$-grid in $\mathbb R^m$ contains
$O_{m,R_*}(2^{mn})$ points. Hence the points of mass less than
$2^{-n(m+1/T)}$ have total mass $O_{m,R_*}(2^{-n/T})$.

Partition the remaining points according to which interval
$(2^{-(r+1)n/T},\,2^{-rn/T}]$,
$r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$,
contains their point mass $\omega(\{a\})$. At most $mT+2$
such intervals occur, and the point masses within each resulting
group vary by a factor at most $2^{n/T}$. Thus, for all
sufficiently large $n$, one such group $A_0\subset A$ satisfies
$$
 \omega(A_0)\ge c_{m,R_*,T}>0
\quad \text{and}
\quad
 2^{-n/T}
 \le
 \frac{\omega(\{a\})}{\omega(\{a'\})}
 \le
 2^{n/T},
 \quad \text{for every } a,a'\in A_0.
$$

\smallskip
\noindent
\emph{Step 1.2: Asymmetric Balog--Szemer\'edi--Gowers theorem
(see \cite[(4.19)--(4.23)]{Wu2025}).}
This step is unchanged, since
\cite[Theorem 4.8]{Wu2025} holds in arbitrary additive groups.
Apply it, with the first set equal to $B$ and the second to $A_0$,
to
$$
 \mathcal G
 :=
 \{(b,a):a\in A_0,\ b\in B_a\}
 \subset B\times A_0.
$$
Set $M:=2^{(m+2)n}$. Then we have
$|B|\le M|A_0|$ and 
 $ |\mathcal G|\ge M^{-\delta}|B||A_0|$,
and the set of restricted sums has cardinality at most
$M^\delta|B|$. Therefore there are
$A'\subset A_0$ and $B'\subset B$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bsg-output}
 |A'|\ge2^{-\tau n}|A_0|,
 \qquad
 |B'|\ge2^{-\tau n}|B|,
 \qquad \text{and} \qquad
 |A'+B'|\le2^{\tau n}|B'|,
\end{equation}
where $\tau=\tau(\delta)\to0$ as $\delta\to0$.

Wu works inside $[0,1]$. To apply the regularization in Step 1.3, replace $A'$ by its intersection with a level-zero dyadic cube containing the largest number of points of $A'$.
The resulting bounded loss can be absorbed into $\tau$.
Translating the sets and $\omega$ by the negative of the
lower left corner of this cube, we may assume that the cube is
$[0,1)^m$ and that the translated dyadic partitions are
$\mathcal D_j^0$.

\smallskip
\noindent
\emph{Step 1.3: Regularization
(see \cite[Lemma 4.9 and (4.24)--(4.28)]{Wu2025}).}
No dimensional modification is required here:
\cite[Lemma 4.9]{Wu2025} is already stated in $\mathbb R^d$.
Apply it to the uniform probability on $A'$ through
$s:=\lfloor n/T\rfloor$ blocks. We obtain
$A''\subset A'$ with
$$
 \frac{|A''|}{|A'|}
 \ge
 (2mT+2)^{-s}
 \ge
 2^{-n\xi_T}.
$$
Let $\sigma_A$ and $\sigma_B$ be the uniform probabilities on
$A''$ and $B'$, respectively.
The regularization gives numbers
$\sigma_r\in[0,m]$, $r=0,\ldots,s-1$, such that whenever
$Q$ is a level-$(r+1)T$ cube with $\sigma_A(Q)>0$ and
$\widehat Q$ is its level-$rT$ parent,
$$
 2^{-\sigma_rT-1}
 \le
 \frac{\sigma_A(Q)}{\sigma_A(\widehat Q)}
 \le
 2^{-\sigma_rT}.
$$
In particular, cubes intersecting $A''$ two block levels beneath
the same ancestor have masses within a factor four.

\smallskip
\noindent
\emph{Step 1.4: The convolution entropy bound
(see
the end of Wu's reduction after \cite[(4.28)]{Wu2025}).}
Let $\sigma_A \ast \sigma_B$ 
denote the convolution  of 
$\sigma_A$ and $\sigma_B$, i.e., the 
push-forward of
$\sigma_A\otimes\sigma_B$ under the addition map
$(x,y)\mapsto x+y$.
Since $\sigma_A\ast\sigma_B$ is supported on
$A''+B'\subset A'+B'$,
\eqref{eq:bsg-output} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convolution-bound}
 H(\sigma_A\ast\sigma_B,\mathcal D_n)
 \le
 \log_2|A'+B'|
 \le
 H(\sigma_B,\mathcal D_n)+\tau n.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
we have
$H(\sigma_B,\mathcal D_n)
 =
 \log_2|B'|
 \le
 (1-\kappa)n$.
Thus, in $\mathbb R^m$, we have the same entropy bounds used in \cite[(4.28)--(4.29)]{Wu2025}.

\smallskip
\noindent {\bf Step 2: From small entropy growth to point concentration.}
This is the main multidimensional modification of Wu's argument.
Hochman's inverse theorem now produces arbitrary subspaces
$V_j\subseteq\mathbb R^m$,
so we must use the entropy bound on $\sigma_B$ to
show that $V_j=\{0\}$ on a positive proportion of scales.

\smallskip
\noindent
\emph{Step 2.1: The multidimensional inverse theorem
(compare \cite[(4.29)--(4.30)]{Wu2025}).}
This is the first point where the adaptation to $\mathbb R^m$ requires a new argument.
Set
$\eta:=\kappa^2 / 100$,
so that
$\eta+\sqrt{\eta}<\kappa/4$,
and
choose $\rho>0$ sufficiently small that $\rho<2^{-2T-3}$ and $\sqrt\rho<\eta/10$.

Apply Hochman's inverse theorem
\cite[Theorem 2.8]{Hochman2017} to $\sigma_B$ as the first
measure and $\sigma_A$ as the second. After the translation in Step 1.2, both measures are supported
on $[-R,R]^m$, where $R:=2R_*+1$.
For the fixed parameters
$m,R,\rho$, and $T$, let
$\delta_H>0$ be the entropy growth threshold in the theorem.
Thus, for all sufficiently large $n$, the theorem applies whenever
\[
 H(\sigma_A\ast\sigma_B,\mathcal D_n)
 -
 H(\sigma_B,\mathcal D_n)
 <
 \delta_H n.
\]
Choose $\delta_0>0$ so small that
$\tau(\delta)<\delta_H/2$ for every $0<\delta<\delta_0$.
By 
\eqref{eq:convolution-bound},
this entropy-growth hypothesis is then satisfied.
The theorem therefore yields
subspaces
$V_j\subseteq\mathbb R^m$
such that, when $j$ is chosen uniformly
from $\{0,\ldots,n\}$ and the corresponding components are sampled independently
according to $\sigma_B$ and $\sigma_A$, the probability that the
$\sigma_B$-component is $(V_j,\rho,T)$-saturated and the
$\sigma_A$-component is $(V_j,\rho)$-concentrated is at least
$1-\rho$.

For each $j$, let $e_j$ be the probability that this joint
conclusion fails at scale $j$. Since
$$
\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^n e_j\leq\rho,
$$
Markov's inequality shows that $e_j\leq\sqrt{\rho}$ for all but at
most $\sqrt{\rho}\,n+O(1)$ scales. At each remaining scale, a set
of $\sigma_B$-mass at least $1-\sqrt{\rho}$ has
$(V_j,\rho,T)$-saturated components, while a set of
$\sigma_A$-mass at least $1-\sqrt{\rho}$ has
$(V_j,\rho)$-concentrated components.



\smallskip
\noindent
\emph{Step 2.2: Zero subspaces at sufficiently many scales
(compare
\cite[(4.31)--(4.34)]{Wu2025}).}
In Wu's one-dimensional argument, the only possible subspaces are
$\{0\}$ and $\mathbb R$, so the entropy bound directly forces point
concentration at many scales. In $\mathbb R^m$ we must also exclude
non-zero proper
subspaces.

Hochman's local entropy average identity
\cite[Lemma 3.5]{Hochman2017} gives
$$
 \frac1nH(\sigma_B,\mathcal D_n)
 =
 \frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
 \int
 \frac1T H(\sigma_B^{x,j},\mathcal D_T^0)
 \,d\sigma_B(x)
 +O_{m,R_*}(T/n).
$$
At each remaining scale with $V_j\ne\{0\}$, saturation implies
normalized block entropy at least $1-\rho$ on a set of
$\sigma_B$-mass at least $1-\sqrt\rho$. Hence the average
contribution of that scale is at least
 $(1-\sqrt\rho)(1-\rho)$.
Since
$H(\sigma_B,\mathcal D_n)/n\le1-\kappa$,
the number of such scales is at most
$$
 \frac{(1-\kappa)n+O_{m,R_*}(T)}
 {(1-\sqrt\rho)(1-\rho)}.
$$
By the choice of $\rho$, after accounting for the discarded
scales, we obtain at least $(\kappa-\eta)n$ scales with
$V_j=\{0\}$, for all large $n$.
At each such scale $j$, a set of $\sigma_A$-mass at least
$1-\sqrt\rho$ consists of points $x$ for which
$\sigma_A^{x,j}$ assigns mass at least $1-\rho$ to a ball
of radius $\rho$, whose center may depend on $x$ and $j$. 
This is the only new issue in the inverse theorem part compared
with Wu.

Let $J$ be the set of these scales satisfying
$j\le(s-2)T$. Then
$|J|\ge(\kappa-\eta)n-O(T)$.
For each $j\in J$, there is $F_j\subset A''$ with
$\sigma_A(F_j)\ge1-\eta$ such that, for every $x\in F_j$,
the component $\sigma_A^{x,j}$ gives mass at least $1-\eta$
to a ball of radius $\rho$.
The restriction $j\le(s-2)T$ ensures that the depth-$2T$
descendants used in Step 3.1 remain within the regularized blocks.

\smallskip
\noindent
{\bf Step 3: From point concentration to unique descendants.}
Once point concentration has been obtained, we return to Wu's
extraction argument. The only dimensional change is the branching
bound: a small ball may meet up to $2^m$ dyadic cubes, leading to
the constant $N_m=8\cdot2^m$ in place of the one-dimensional bound.


\smallskip
\noindent
\emph{Step 3.1: The multidimensional branching bound
(compare \cite[(4.34)--(4.40)]{Wu2025}).}
Fix $j\in J$ and $x\in F_j$.
Wu's argument now extracts a bounded number of descendants from
point concentration. The only dimensional change is that a small
ball may meet several dyadic cubes.

Since $\rho<2^{-2T-3}$, the concentration ball meets at most
$2^m$ cubes at relative depth $2T$. Hence some
level-$(j+2T)$ descendant
$Q_*\subset\mathcal D_j(x)$ satisfies
$\sigma_A(Q_*)
 \ge
 (1-\eta)2^{-m}\sigma_A(\mathcal D_j(x))$.
Set
$r:=\lfloor j/T\rfloor+1$,
so that $rT$ is the first block level strictly greater than $j$.
Since $(r+1)T\le j+2T$, the level-$(r+1)T$ ancestor $Q^*$
of $Q_*$ satisfies
$ \sigma_A(Q^*)\ge\sigma_A(Q_*)$.

All level-$(r+1)T$ cubes inside $\mathcal D_j(x)$ which meet
$A''$ share the level-$(r-1)T$ ancestor of $x$.
By the two-block comparison from Step 1.3, each such cube $Q$
satisfies
$$
 \sigma_A(Q)
 \ge
 \frac14\sigma_A(Q^*)
 \ge
 \frac{1-\eta}{4\cdot2^m}
 \sigma_A(\mathcal D_j(x)).
$$
They are disjoint, so their number is at most
$(4\cdot2^m)/(1-\eta)
 \le
 N_m=8\cdot2^m$.
This is the multidimensional counterpart of the bound on the number
of occupied descendant intervals in \cite[(4.40)]{Wu2025}.

\smallskip
\noindent
\emph{Step 3.2: Unique descendant scales
(see \cite[(4.41)--(4.45)]{Wu2025}).}
From this point Wu's tree argument is unchanged, with $N_m$
in place of the one-dimensional branching bound.

Along the chain of dyadic cubes containing $x$, call a level
\emph{branching} if the cube has at least two immediate children
meeting $A''$. Between levels $rT$ and $(r+1)T-1$, there are at
most $N_m-1$ branching levels, since each branching creates an
additional level-$(r+1)T$ descendant meeting $A''$.
Each branching level can make the unique descendant property fail
for at most $\ell$ starting scales. Allowing also for windows
crossing the end of the block, at least
 $T-\ell N_m-\ell$
 starting scales in this block have the unique descendant property
relative to $A''$.

We now select points for which this happens in sufficiently many
blocks, exactly as in Wu's passage from (4.42) to (4.45).
Since
$$
 \int
 \#\{j\in J:x\notin F_j\}
 \,d\sigma_A(x)
 \le
 \eta n,
$$
Markov's inequality gives $E\subset A''$ with
$\sigma_A(E)\ge1-\sqrt\eta$ such that every $x\in E$ belongs to
$F_j$ for at least
 $(\kappa-\eta-\sqrt\eta)n-O(T)$
indices $j\in J$.
At most $T$ indices $j$ correspond to the same following block
$[rT,(r+1)T)$. Hence every $x\in E$ has at least
$
 (\kappa-\eta-\sqrt\eta)n/{T} -O(1)$
good following blocks. By \eqref{eq:block-length-choice} and our choice of $\eta$,
we have
$$
T-\ell N_m-\ell>\frac{3T}{4}
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
\eta+\sqrt\eta<\frac{\kappa}{4}.
$$
Thus, for all large $n$, every $x\in E$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:unique descendant} 
on at least
$\kappa n/2$ scales.
Indeed, since $E\subset A''$, at most one level-$(j+\ell)$
descendant can meet $E$; the descendant containing $x$ does
meet $E$, since $x\in E$.

\smallskip
\noindent
\emph{Step 3.3: Returning to the original measure
(compare the estimates
after \cite[(4.46)]{Wu2025}).}
It remains to show that $E$ has sufficiently large $\omega$-mass.

Since $\sigma_A$ is uniform on $A''$ and
$\sigma_A(E)\ge1-\sqrt\eta>1/2$, we have
$|E|\ge|A''|/2$.
The point masses of $\omega$ on $A_0$ differ by at most
$2^{n/T}$, hence
$$
 \omega(E)
 \ge
 2^{-n/T}\frac{|E|}{|A_0|}\omega(A_0).
$$
Using \eqref{eq:bsg-output}, 
$ {|A'|}/{|A_0|}\ge2^{-\tau n}$,
and the regularization estimate
$ {|A''|}/{|A'|}\ge2^{-n\xi_T}$,
we obtain
$$
 \omega(E)
 \ge
 C_{m,R_*,T}^{-1}
 2^{-n(1/T+\tau+\xi_T)}.
$$
By 
the second condition in
\eqref{eq:block-length-choice}, after shrinking $\delta_0$
further so that $\tau<c_0/3$, we have
$1/T+\tau+\xi_T<2c_0/3$.
For all sufficiently large $n$ this gives
$\omega(E)\ge2^{-c_0n}$.
Undoing the translation from Step 1.2 gives a subset $E$ of the
original $A$, with the same unique descendant counts and $\omega$-mass bound.
This proves the assertion.
