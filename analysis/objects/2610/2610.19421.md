\label{thm:affine-boundary}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:affine-seed} and
\ref{ass:invertibility} hold and \(\phi=1\), so that
\(\alpha=1-\beta>0\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(\omega<0\), the state recursion $(M_t)$, as well as the process
$(X_t)$, has a unique stationary distribution satisfying $\Pr(M_0>y)\sim Cy^{-\kappa},\qquad y\to\infty,$ where $C$ is a positive constant and \(\kappa:=-2\omega/(\alpha^2\sigma^2)\).   
For every \(s>0\), the moments \(\mathbb{E} M_0^s\) and \(\mathbb{E} X_0^s\) are finite
if and only if \(s<\kappa\).  Furthermore,
\begin{itemize}
\item $(X_t)$ is not exponentially \(\beta\)-mixing.
\item If
\(\kappa>1\), then for any exponent \(r\) satisfying \(1<r<\kappa\),
\(j^r\beta_X^{\mathrm{mix}}(j)\) converges to zero as \(j\) increases to infinity.
\item The process is covariance stationary if and only if \(\kappa>2\).
\end{itemize}
\item If \(\omega\geq0\), the process $(X_t)$ has no stationary distribution.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           
\end{enumerate}

\label{thm:kappa-two-three-long-memory}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:affine-seed} and
\ref{ass:invertibility} hold, with \(\phi=1\) and \(\omega<0\), and let
\(\kappa\) be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:affine-boundary}.

\item If \(\kappa>3\), then \((\rho_X(j))\) is absolutely summable.
\item If \(2<\kappa\leq3\) and \(\beta\geq0\), then \((X_t)\) has long memory:

\label{eq:kappa-two-three-acf-divergence}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:general-link-condition}
The specific form of softplus is not essential for
Theorems~\ref{thm:affine-boundary}--\ref{thm:affine-supercritical}.
A convenient sufficient condition under which all their conclusions remain
valid is to replace \(s_c\) by a \(C^1\) strictly increasing bijection
\(\ell:\mathbb R\to(0,\infty)\) satisfying
\[
 0<\ell'(x)\leq1,
 \qquad
 \ell(x)-x=O(x^{-A})\quad(x\to\infty)
 \quad\text{for every }A>0.
\]
Nonexpansiveness and the second condition imply \(\ell(x)\geq x\).  The
link correction is therefore negligible in the high-state increment
expansions, so the boundary moment index \(\kappa\) is unchanged.
Monotonicity supplies the association argument in the long-memory regime,
whereas nonexpansiveness supplies filter contraction, coupling, and the
high-state persistence bounds.  Strict positivity prevents the state from
being absorbed at zero.  The smooth bijection condition is used for the
change of variables and ensures differentiability of the likelihood function.
The ReLU limit \(\max(x,0)\), however, lies outside this class because it is
neither strictly positive nor a \(C^1\) bijection.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:softplus-supercritical-fixed-points}
Let \(\phi>1\) and consider the deterministic map
\[
 \Psi_\phi(m)=s_c(\omega+\phi m),\qquad m>0.
\]
Define
\[
 \omega_{\mathrm{SN}}(\phi)
 =c[(\phi-1)\log(\phi-1)-\phi\log\phi],
 \qquad
 m_{\mathrm{SN}}(\phi)
 =c\log\left(\frac{\phi}{\phi-1}\right),
\]
where ``SN'' means saddle-node. We have  $\omega_{\mathrm{SN}}(\phi)<0$, and furthermore, if \(\omega<\omega_{\mathrm{SN}}(\phi)\), then \(\Psi_\phi\) has exactly two
positive fixed points \(m_-<m_{\mathrm{SN}}(\phi)<m_+\), with the lower (resp. upper) fixed
point being locally attracting (resp. repelling); if \(\omega=\omega_{\mathrm{SN}}(\phi)\), there is a unique, double fixed point \(m_{\mathrm{SN}}(\phi)\); if
\(\omega>\omega_{\mathrm{SN}}(\phi)\), there is no positive fixed point.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:affine-qmle}
{
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:affine-seed} and \ref{ass:invertibility}
hold, the observations come from a stationary and ergodic solution of
\eqref{eq:affine-softplus-model} at \(\theta_0\), and \(\theta_0\) is an
interior point of the compact set \(\Theta_Q\) defined above.  Put
\(\phi_0=\alpha_0+\beta_0\), and suppose either
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \(\phi_0<1\) and \(\alpha_0\neq0\); or
\item[(ii)] \(\phi_0=1\), \(\omega_0<0\), and
\(\kappa_0:=-2\omega_0/(\alpha_0^2\sigma_0^2)>1\).
\end{enumerate}
Then the conditional-mean filter is identified at \(\theta_0\) over
\(\Theta_Q\), \(I_0\) is positive definite,
\(\widehat\theta_n\to\theta_0\) almost surely,
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_0)
 \Rightarrow N(0,\sigma_0^2I_0^{-1}),
\]
and \(\widehat\sigma_n^2\to\sigma_0^2\) and
\(\widehat I_{Q,n}\to I_0\) in probability.
}

<!-- head-1: body_md -->
Here \(\sigma_0^2=1\), and \(Q_n-\sum_{t=1}^n\log(X_t!)\) is the exact
conditional log-likelihood.  Thus the QMLE is the conditional MLE, with
first-order covariance \(I_0^{-1}/n\), where \(I_0\) is the conditional
Fisher information.  At a stationary unit-root truth, the boundary moment
index is \(\kappa(\omega,\alpha)=-2\omega/\alpha^2\).  Its gradient at
\(\theta_0\) is \(g_0=(-2/\alpha_0^2,4\omega_0/\alpha_0^3,0)^\top\), so the
delta method gives
\[
 \sqrt n(\kappa(\widehat\omega_n,\widehat\alpha_n)-\kappa_0)
 \Rightarrow
 N(0,g_0^\top I_0^{-1}g_0).
\]

\label{thm:rootn-local-power}
Suppose that the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:affine-qmle} hold at
\(\theta_0\), the conditional distribution is Poisson (hence \(\sigma_0^2=1\)),
and \(\kappa_0>1\).
For each sufficiently large \(n\), let
\(\{(M_{n,t},X_{n,t}):t\in\mathbb Z\}\) be a two-sided stationary row at
\(\theta_n\), and let \(\pi_n\) be the invariant law of its state chain;
thus \(M_{n,0}\sim\pi_n\).  Let \(\pi_0\) denote the invariant law of the
state chain at \(\theta_0\).  This data-generating initialization is
distinct from the fixed, parameter-independent filter initialization used to
compute the QMLE in \eqref{eq:affine-qmle-criterion}.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \pi_n\Rightarrow\pi_0.
 \label{eq:rootn-local-invariant-convergence}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)
 \Rightarrow N(0,I_0^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \widehat I_{Q,n}\xrightarrow{p}I_0,
 \qquad
 \widehat\sigma_n^2\xrightarrow{p}1.
 \label{eq:rootn-local-estimator}
\end{equation}
Then the Wald statistic defined in Section~\ref{sec:unit-root-test} satisfies
\begin{equation}
 T_n\Rightarrow
 N\left(-\frac{h}{\tau_{\phi,0}},1\right),
 \label{eq:rootn-local-wald}
\end{equation}
where the scale defined in Section~\ref{sec:unit-root-test} satisfies
\(\tau_{\phi,0}^2=e_\phi^\top I_0^{-1}e_\phi\) because \(\sigma_0^2=1\).
Consequently, the level-\(\eta\) test that rejects when
\(T_n<z_\eta\) has limiting power
\begin{equation}
 \Phi\left(z_\eta+\frac{h}{\tau_{\phi,0}}\right).
 \label{eq:rootn-local-power}
\end{equation}

\label{lem:qmle-initialization}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:affine-qmle}, let stationary and
initialized candidate filters be driven by the same observations.  On
every compact subset \(\mathcal K\subseteq\Theta_Q\),
\[
 \sum_{t=1}^\infty\sup_{\theta\in \mathcal K}
 \left[
 |\widetilde\ell_t^Q(\theta)-\ell_t^Q(\theta)|
 +\|\widetilde S_t(\theta)-S_t(\theta)\|
 +\|\widetilde K_t(\theta)-K_t(\theta)\|
 \right]<\infty
 \quad\text{a.s.}
\]
These conclusions require only
\(\mathbb{E} X_0<\infty\).

<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
The proof follows the perturbed-recursion strategy of
\citet[Section~2]{straumannMikosch2006}, retaining explicit bounds to obtain
all three summability conclusions.
In this proof, filter quantities are evaluated at a generic
\(\theta\in\mathcal K\), whose argument is suppressed.
Untilded quantities use the stationary candidate filter, and tildes denote
its fixed-initialization counterpart.  Since \(\widetilde M_0>0\) is
independent of \(\theta\), \(\widetilde D_0=0\); subsequent derivatives
are obtained by differentiating the same forward recursion.
Let
\[
 B_0=1+\sup_{\theta\in \mathcal K}\left[
 |\widetilde M_0(\theta)-M_0(\theta)|
 +\|\widetilde D_0(\theta)-D_0(\theta)\|\right].
\]
The stationary envelopes and fixed initialization give \(B_0<\infty\) almost
surely.
Lemma~\ref{lem:unrestricted-candidate-filter} gives
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in \mathcal K}|\widetilde M_t-M_t|\leq B_0\rho_\beta^t,
 \qquad
 \sup_{\theta\in \mathcal K}|\widetilde\eta_t-\eta_t|
 \leq B_0\rho_\beta^t.
\]
The \(1/c\)-Lipschitz property of \(\log s_c\) and the one-Lipschitz
property of \(s_c\) give
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in \mathcal K}|\widetilde\ell_t^Q-\ell_t^Q|
 \leq CB_0\rho_\beta^t(1+X_t).
\]

Use the level envelope \(Z_t\) from \eqref{eq:qmle-level-envelope}, with
\(\bar\rho\in(\sqrt{\rho_\beta},1)\).  Since
\(\mathbb{E} Z_0=(1+\mathbb{E} X_0)/(1-\bar\rho)<\infty\), this envelope requires only
the assumed first moment.
Subtracting their recursions, using boundedness of \(s_c''\), and iterating
gives
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\theta\in \mathcal K}
 [\|\widetilde A_t-A_t\|+\|\widetilde D_t-D_t\|]
 \leq CB_0\rho_\beta^t\sum_{j=1}^t(1+Z_j).
 \label{eq:qmle-initialized-first-derivative}
\end{equation}
Use \(p_c,r_c,j_c\) as defined in Lemma~\ref{lem:softplus-calculus}.  Since
\[
 S_t(\theta)=(X_t r_c(\eta_t)-p_c(\eta_t))A_t,
 \qquad
 K_t(\theta)=j_c(\eta_t)A_tA_t^\top,
\]
the preceding bounds and \(\sup_{\theta\in \mathcal K}\|A_t(\theta)\|\leq CZ_t\)
give
\begin{align}
 \sup_{\theta\in \mathcal K}\|\widetilde S_t-S_t\|
 &\leq CB_0\rho_\beta^t(1+X_t)
       \left[1+Z_t+\sum_{j=1}^t(1+Z_j)\right],
 \label{eq:qmle-score-initialization-bound}\\
 \sup_{\theta\in \mathcal K}\|\widetilde K_t-K_t\|
 &\leq C\left[
 B_0\rho_\beta^t(1+Z_t)
 \left(1+Z_t+\sum_{j=1}^t(1+Z_j)\right)
 +B_0^2\rho_\beta^{2t}
   \left(\sum_{j=1}^t(1+Z_j)\right)^2
 \right].
 \label{eq:qmle-information-initialization-bound}
\end{align}
If a stationary nonnegative process \((Y_t)\) satisfies
\(\mathbb{E}\log(1+Y_0)<\infty\), then \(b_{\mathrm{BC}}^{-t}Y_t\to0\) almost surely for every
\(b_{\mathrm{BC}}>1\): the tail-sum formula gives
\(\sum_t\Pr\{\log(1+Y_t)>t\log b_{\mathrm{BC}}\}<\infty\), so Borel--Cantelli applies.
Since \(X_t\) and the level envelope \(Z_t\) are integrable, this applies to
both.  Increase \(\rho_\beta\) slightly, without changing notation, and choose
\(b_{\mathrm{BC}}>1\) with \(\rho_\beta b_{\mathrm{BC}}^2<1\).  Almost surely, eventually,
\[
 1+X_t\leq Cb_{\mathrm{BC}}^t,\qquad 1+Z_t\leq Cb_{\mathrm{BC}}^t,
 \qquad \sum_{j=1}^t(1+Z_j)\leq Cb_{\mathrm{BC}}^t,
\]
with finite random \(C\).  Substitution into the criterion bound and
\eqref{eq:qmle-score-initialization-bound}--
\eqref{eq:qmle-information-initialization-bound} gives summable geometric
majorants because \(\rho_\beta b_{\mathrm{BC}}^2<1\); the factors \(B_0,B_0^2\) are almost
surely finite.  This proves all three assertions under a first moment.

\label{lem:qmle-rare-relative-innovation}
Let \((M_t,X_t)\) be the stationary solution under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:affine-seed} and \ref{ass:invertibility}, with
\(\phi_0=1\), \(\omega_0<0\), and \(\mathbb{E} M_t<\infty\).
For the innovations \(U_t=X_t-M_t\), define
\[
 \mathsf V_t(r)=1+\sum_{j=0}^\infty r^j|U_{t-1-j}|,\qquad 0<r<1.
\]
Fix \(\varrho\in(0,1)\), and write \(\mathsf V_t=\mathsf V_t(\varrho)\).
Then \(\mathsf V_t\in L^2\).  For every \(\chi\geq1\), there is
\(C_{0,\chi}<\infty\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf V_t^{\chi}
 \mathbf1_{\{\mathsf V_t>C_{0,\chi}(1+M_t)\}}\right]<\infty.
 \label{eq:qmle-rare-relative-moment}
\end{equation}
The assertion remains valid after increasing \(C_{0,\chi}\).

<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
At the unit root, \(\alpha_0=1-\beta_0>0\) and
\(M_{t+1}=s_c(\omega_0+M_t+\alpha_0U_t)\).
The affine conditional variance gives
\(\mathbb{E} U_t^2=\sigma_0^2\mathbb{E} M_t<\infty\), so Minkowski's inequality gives
\(\mathsf V_t\in L^2\).

Choose \(\varrho_0\in(\varrho,1)\) and then \(\epsilon>0\) so that
\(1-\alpha_0\epsilon>\varrho_0\).  Set
\[
 B_t=\mathbf1_{\{|U_t|>\epsilon(1+M_t)\}}.
\]
Since \(s_c(x)\geq x\), on \(\{B_t=0\}\),
\[
 1+M_{t+1}\geq(1-\alpha_0\epsilon)(1+M_t)+\omega_0
             \geq \varrho_0(1+M_t)+\omega_0.
\]
After enlarging fixed constants, the following inequality is therefore
valid on both \(\{B_t=0\}\) and \(\{B_t=1\}\):
\begin{equation}
 1+M_t\leq \varrho_0^{-1}(1+M_{t+1})+K
 +C(1+M_t+|U_t|)B_t.
 \label{eq:qmle-backward-good-bad}
\end{equation}
Define
\[
 \Sigma_t=\sum_{j\geq0}\varrho^j(1+M_{t-1-j}),
 \qquad
 \Xi_t=\sum_{j\geq0}\varrho^j
 (1+M_{t-1-j}+|U_{t-1-j}|)B_{t-1-j}.
\]
Multiplying \eqref{eq:qmle-backward-good-bad} at time \(t-1-j\) by
\(\varrho^j\) and summing gives
\[
 \Sigma_t\leq \varrho_0^{-1}(1+M_t+\varrho\Sigma_t)
 +\frac K{1-\varrho}+C\Xi_t,
\]
and hence \(\Sigma_t\leq C_\Sigma(1+M_t+\Xi_t)\) for some finite
positive deterministic constant \(C_\Sigma\), depending only on the fixed
model parameters and the choices of \(\varrho,\varrho_0,\epsilon\).
Set \(C_{\rm rel}=1+\epsilon C_\Sigma\).  Splitting the innovations according
to \(B_{t-1-j}\) yields
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf V_t\leq1+\epsilon\Sigma_t+\Xi_t
 \leq C_{\rm rel}(1+M_t+\Xi_t).
 \label{eq:qmle-V-good-bad}
\end{equation}
If \(C_0>2C_{\rm rel}\), then on
\(\{\mathsf V_t>C_0(1+M_t)\}\),
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf V_t\leq2C_{\rm rel}\Xi_t.
 \label{eq:qmle-V-by-Xi}
\end{equation}

Weighted Jensen's inequality gives, for every \(\chi\geq1\),
\begin{equation}
 \Xi_t^{\chi}\leq C_{\chi,\varrho}\sum_{j\geq0}
 \varrho^{j(\chi+1)/2}
 (1+M_{t-1-j}+|U_{t-1-j}|)^{\chi}B_{t-1-j}.
 \label{eq:qmle-weighted-bad-sum}
\end{equation}
Since \(\mathcal L(U_t\mid M_t=m)=\mathcal L(U_m)\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:affine-concentration} gives, for arbitrary \(p\geq2\),
\[
 \mathbb{E}(|U_t|^p\mid M_t=m)\leq C_p(1+m^{p/2}).
\]
Choose \(p>\chi\) and \(p\geq2(\chi-1)\).  Markov's inequality gives, uniformly
for \(m\geq1\),
\[
 (1+m)^{\chi}\Pr(B_t=1\mid M_t=m)
 +\mathbb{E}(|U_t|^{\chi}B_t\mid M_t=m)
 \leq C_{\chi}(1+m).
\]
The uniform small-state conditional moments in
Lemma~\ref{lem:affine-concentration} give the same bound for \(0<m<1\).
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb{E}[(1+M_t+|U_t|)^{\chi}B_t\mid M_t=m]
 \leq C_{\chi}(1+m).
 \label{eq:qmle-one-bad-moment}
\end{equation}
Taking expectations in \eqref{eq:qmle-weighted-bad-sum} and using
stationarity and \(\mathbb{E} M_t<\infty\) gives \(\mathbb{E}\Xi_t^{\chi}<\infty\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:qmle-V-by-Xi} proves
\eqref{eq:qmle-rare-relative-moment}.

\label{lem:boundary-qmle-hessian-envelope}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:affine-qmle}, suppose that
\(\phi_0=1\) and \(\mathbb{E} M_t<\infty\).  Use the inference notation from
Section~\ref{app:inference-notation}.  There is a compact convex
neighborhood \(\mathcal N\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\Theta_Q)\)
of \(\theta_0\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb{E}\sup_{\theta\in \mathcal N}
 \|\partial_{\theta\theta^\top}^2\ell_t^Q(\theta)\|<\infty,
 \qquad
 \mathbb{E}\sup_{\theta\in \mathcal N}
 \frac{\|D_t(\theta)\|^2}{M_t(\theta)}<\infty.
 \label{eq:qmle-boundary-local-envelopes}
\end{equation}

Use the true state \(M_t\) and the innovations \(U_t\) defined above.
Choose \(\bar\rho\in(\sqrt{\rho_\beta},1)\), where
\(\sup_{\Theta_Q}|\beta|\leq\rho_\beta<1\), and specialize the
innovation envelope to \(\mathsf V_t=\mathsf V_t(\bar\rho)\).
The preceding lemma gives \(\mathsf V_t\in L^2\) and its rare-event moments of
every order.  Since \(s_c(x)\geq x\), the true boundary recursion gives
\[
 M_{t-1}\leq M_t+|\omega_0|+\alpha_0|U_{t-1}|.
\]
Iteration yields, for every \(k\geq1\),
\begin{equation}
 M_{t-k}\leq M_t+k|\omega_0|
       +\alpha_0\sum_{\ell=1}^k|U_{t-\ell}|.
 \label{eq:qmle-backward-level-bound}
\end{equation}
Moreover, \(X_{t-k}\leq M_{t-k}+|U_{t-k}|\).  Recalling the level envelope
\(Z_t\) in \eqref{eq:qmle-level-envelope}, substitute
\eqref{eq:qmle-backward-level-bound} with \(k=j+1\) and interchange the two
nonnegative sums.  Because
\[
 \sum_{j\geq0}\bar\rho^j
       \sum_{\ell=1}^{j+1}|U_{t-\ell}|
 =\frac1{1-\bar\rho}
   \sum_{\ell\geq1}\bar\rho^{\ell-1}|U_{t-\ell}|,
\]
and \(\sum_{j\geq0}(j+1)\bar\rho^j<\infty\), this gives
\begin{equation}
 Z_t\leq C(M_t+\mathsf V_t).
 \label{eq:qmle-boundary-current-state-envelope}
\end{equation}

Using \(X_{t-1}=M_{t-1}+U_{t-1}\) and \(\phi_0=1\), we have
\[
 \eta_t(\theta)-\eta_t(\theta_0)
 = (\omega-\omega_0)+(\alpha-\alpha_0)U_{t-1}
   +(\phi-1)M_{t-1}+\beta \delta_{t-1}(\theta).
\]
For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), define the closed ball
\[
 \mathcal N_\varepsilon
 =\{\theta\in\mathbb R^3:\|\theta-\theta_0\|\leq\varepsilon\}
 \subseteq\operatorname{int}(\Theta_Q).
\]
The one-Lipschitz property of softplus and the uniform bound on \(|\beta|\)
then give
\begin{align}
 \sup_{\theta\in \mathcal N_\varepsilon}|\delta_t(\theta)|
 &\leq C\varepsilon(M_t+\mathsf V_t),
 \label{eq:qmle-local-filter-boundary}\\
 \sup_{\theta\in \mathcal N_\varepsilon}\|A_t(\theta)\|
 &\leq C(M_t+\mathsf V_t),\qquad
 \sup_{\theta\in \mathcal N_\varepsilon}\|J_t(\theta)\|
 \leq C(M_t+\mathsf V_t)^2.
 \label{eq:qmle-local-derivatives-boundary}
\end{align}
Indeed, the preceding identity gives the one-step bound
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in \mathcal N_\varepsilon}|\delta_t(\theta)|
 \leq C\varepsilon(1+M_{t-1}+|U_{t-1}|)
 +\rho_\beta\sup_{\theta\in \mathcal N_\varepsilon}|\delta_{t-1}(\theta)|.
\]
Iteration and \eqref{eq:qmle-backward-level-bound} prove
\eqref{eq:qmle-local-filter-boundary}.  Iterating the derivative recursions
in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:affine-qmle-envelopes}, using
\eqref{eq:qmle-boundary-current-state-envelope}, proves
\eqref{eq:qmle-local-derivatives-boundary}; here
\(\bar\rho^2>\rho_\beta\) controls the quadratic forcing.

We next prove that
\(\sup_{\theta\in \mathcal N_\varepsilon}\|J_t(\theta)\|\) is integrable.
The chain rule gives
\(D_t(\theta)=s_c'(\eta_t(\theta))A_t(\theta)\).  Define
\[
 D_t^*=\sup_{\theta\in \mathcal N_\varepsilon}\|D_t(\theta)\|,
 \qquad
 J_t^*=\sup_{\theta\in \mathcal N_\varepsilon}\|J_t(\theta)\|.
\]
Then \(\mathbb{E} D_t^*<\infty\).  Split the candidates according as
\(M_t(\theta)\geq M_t/2\).  On this set,
\eqref{eq:qmle-sharp-softplus-derivatives} and
\eqref{eq:qmle-local-derivatives-boundary} give the bound below; on its
complement, \eqref{eq:qmle-local-filter-boundary} gives
\(M_t+\mathsf V_t\leq C\mathsf V_t\), and boundedness of \(s_c''\) gives the same bound:
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in \mathcal N_\varepsilon}
 s_c''(\eta_t(\theta))\|A_t(\theta)\|^2
 \leq C(1+\mathsf V_t^2).
\]
The recursion for \(J_t(\theta)\) becomes
\[
 J_t(\theta)=e_\beta D_{t-1}(\theta)^\top
 +D_{t-1}(\theta)e_\beta^\top
 +\beta\bigl[s_c''(\eta_{t-1}(\theta))
 A_{t-1}(\theta)A_{t-1}(\theta)^\top
 +s_c'(\eta_{t-1}(\theta))J_{t-1}(\theta)\bigr].
\]
Taking suprema gives \(J_t^*\leq\rho_\beta J_{t-1}^*+F_{t-1}\) for an integrable stationary
\(F_t\).  Backward iteration gives
\[
 J_t^*\leq\rho_\beta^kJ_{t-k}^*
 +\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\rho_\beta^jF_{t-1-j}.
\]
Stationarity makes the first term converge to zero in probability, hence
almost surely along a deterministic subsequence.  Passing to that
subsequence gives
\(J_t^*\leq\sum_{j\geq0}\rho_\beta^jF_{t-1-j}\) almost surely.  The right side
has expectation \((1-\rho_\beta)^{-1}\mathbb{E} F_0\), and therefore
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb{E} J_t^*<\infty.
 \label{eq:qmle-integrable-J}
\end{equation}

It remains to transfer these predictor bounds to the likelihood Hessian and
information integrand.  Use \(p_c,r_c,r_c'\) from
Lemma~\ref{lem:softplus-calculus}.  Suppressing the generic candidate
argument \(\theta\) in \(\mathcal H_t,K_t,\eta_t,A_t,J_t\), direct
differentiation gives
\[
 \mathcal H_t=-X_t r_c'(\eta_t)A_tA_t^\top
     +s_c''(\eta_t)A_tA_t^\top
     +(p_c(\eta_t)-X_t r_c(\eta_t))J_t,
 \qquad K_t=\frac{p_c(\eta_t)^2}{M_t(\theta)}A_tA_t^\top.
\]
Split \(\mathcal N_\varepsilon\) into
\[
 \mathcal C_t=\{\theta\in \mathcal N_\varepsilon:M_t(\theta)\geq M_t/2\},
 \qquad \mathcal D_t=\mathcal N_\varepsilon\setminus\mathcal C_t.
\]
For \(\mathcal A_t\in\{\mathcal C_t,\mathcal D_t\}\), let
\(\mathcal H_{t,\mathcal A}^*=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal A_t}
\|\mathcal H_t(\theta)\|\) and define \(K_{t,\mathcal A}^*\) analogously,
with an empty supremum equal to zero.  Continuity makes these restricted
suprema measurable (use a measurable maximum argument on \(\mathcal C_t\)
and a fixed countable dense subset on the relatively open \(\mathcal D_t\)).
On \(\mathcal C_t\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:softplus-calculus},
\eqref{eq:qmle-local-derivatives-boundary}, and
\(\mathbb{E}(1+X_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})=1+M_t\) give
\[
 \mathbb{E}(\mathcal H_{t,\mathcal C}^*\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
 +K_{t,\mathcal C}^*
 \leq C\left[\frac{(M_t+\mathsf V_t)^2}{1+M_t}+J_t^*\right].
\]
If \(\mathcal D_t\ne\varnothing\),
\eqref{eq:qmle-local-filter-boundary} gives
\(M_t\leq C\varepsilon\mathsf V_t\) and
\(M_t+\mathsf V_t\leq C\mathsf V_t\).  The globally bounded quantities
\(r_c,r_c',s_c'',p_c\), and \(p_c^2/s_c\) therefore give
\[
 \mathbb{E}(\mathcal H_{t,\mathcal D}^*\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
 +K_{t,\mathcal D}^*
 \leq C(1+M_t)\mathsf V_t^2
       \mathbf1_{\{\mathcal D_t\ne\varnothing\}}.
\]
Choose \(C_0\) from
\eqref{eq:qmle-rare-relative-moment} at power three and then decrease
\(\varepsilon\), if necessary, so that, on \(\{M_t>1\}\),
\(\{\mathcal D_t\ne\varnothing\}\subseteq
\{\mathsf V_t>C_0(1+M_t)\}\).  Hence the last display is bounded by
\[
 C\left[\mathsf V_t^2+
 \mathsf V_t^3\mathbf1_{\{\mathsf V_t>C_0(1+M_t)\}}\right].
\]
Finally,
\((M_t+\mathsf V_t)^2/(1+M_t)
 \leq C(1+M_t+\mathsf V_t^2)\).  Taking expectations and using
\(\mathbb{E} M_t<\infty\), \(\mathsf V_t\in L^2\),
\eqref{eq:qmle-integrable-J}, and
\eqref{eq:qmle-rare-relative-moment} proves both envelopes in
\eqref{eq:qmle-boundary-local-envelopes} with
\(\mathcal N=\mathcal N_\varepsilon\) for this fixed \(\varepsilon\).
