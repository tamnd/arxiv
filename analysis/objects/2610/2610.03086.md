<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
There is an absolute constant $C$ such that, for $n\ge2$ and
$0<\varepsilon<1/2$, every monotone
$f:[0,1]^n\to\{0,1\}$ admits $B\subseteq[n]$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$ with
\[
 |B|\le \frac{Cn}{\varepsilon\log n},
 \qquad \mathbb P[f(b_B,X_{-B})=b]\ge1-\varepsilon.
\]
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
The reduction to the monotone case by coordinatewise compression is
standard; see~\cite{F04,CG}.
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:junta}
There is an absolute constant $c$ such that, if $K(h)\le L$ with $L>0$,
then for every $0<\eta<1$ there are $S\subseteq[n]$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$ with
\[
 |S|\le\exp(cL/\eta),\qquad
 \mathbb P\bigl[h(b_S,X_{-S})=b\bigr]\ge1-\eta.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For $M=2^m$, let $D_M(x)=\lfloor Mx\rfloor/M$ for $x<1$, with
$D_M(1)=(M-1)/M$. Set $h_M(x)=h(D_M(x_1),\ldots,D_M(x_n))$ and regard
this step function as a Boolean function $H_M$ on $nm$ fair bits.
Monotonicity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx}
 \mathbb P[h\ne h_M]\le n/M.
\end{equation}
Indeed, on each small cube both values lie between the values of $h$ at
its lower and upper corners. Summing these corner differences, and
telescoping one coordinate at a time, gives at most $nM^{n-1}$.

With all other blocks fixed, $H_M$ is a threshold in the binary order on
the remaining block. Its $j$th bit has influence at most $2^{-(j-1)}$:
only the unique prefix of length $j-1$ containing the threshold can be
pivotal. Thus a non-constant block section contributes less than $2$ to
the total bit influence $I(H_M)$, where each bit's influence is the
probability that flipping it changes the output. Consequently
\begin{align}
 I(H_M)
 &\le 2\sum_i\mathbb E\bigl[h(1,D_M(X_{-i}))-h(0,D_M(X_{-i}))\bigr]\notag\\
 &\le 2K(h)+\frac{2n(n-1)}{M}.\label{eq:bits}
\end{align}
For the last inequality, apply~\eqref{eq:approx} in dimension $n-1$ to
each endpoint section; the lower-grid average of the upper section is
at most its integral, and that of the lower section is at least its
integral minus $(n-1)/M$.

Take $M$ sufficiently large that the error in~\eqref{eq:approx} is at most
$\eta/2$ and $I(H_M)\le4L$. Friedgut's Boolean junta theorem~\cite{F98}
gives an $\eta/2$-approximation to $H_M$ depending on at most
$\exp(8c_0L/\eta)$ bits, where $c_0$ is its absolute constant.
Let $S$ be the original coordinates containing those bits. The resulting
function $Q(X_S)$ approximates $h$ with error at most $\eta$.
Averaging over $X_S$ gives a fixed assignment $a$ for which
\[
 \mathbb P[h(a,X_{-S})\ne Q(a)]\le\eta.
\]
Set $b=Q(a)$. Moving every coordinate of $a$ to the endpoint $b$ can only
increase the probability of output $b$. This proves the lemma with
$c=8c_0$.
