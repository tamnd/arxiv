<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:phase}
Suppose that Assumptions~\ref{hyp:f}--\ref{hyp:data} hold. For every $\beta\in[0,\infty)$, there is a unique $\xi_\beta\in\mathbb R$ such that, as $t\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-limit}
 \sup_{x\ge0}\big|u^\beta(t,x)-U_*(x-m(t)-\xi_\beta)\big|\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
At $x=0$, the value of $u^\beta$ in~\eqref{eq:phase-limit} is understood as the right-hand trace $u^\beta(t,0^+)$. Define the leading-edge amplitude by $\alpha_\beta=A_*e^{\xi_\beta}>0$. For every $\eta\in(0,1)$, the following limit exists, is finite, and is independent of $\eta$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delay}
 \tau_\beta:=\lim_{R\to\infty}
 \big(T_\eta^\beta(R)-T_\eta^0(R)\big)
 =\frac{\xi_0-\xi_\beta}{2}
 =\frac12\log\frac{\alpha_0}{\alpha_\beta}.
\end{equation}
The maps $\beta\mapsto\alpha_\beta$ and $\beta\mapsto\tau_\beta$ are continuous on $[0,\infty)$, and $\tau_0=0$.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:monotonicity}
Suppose that Assumptions~\ref{hyp:f}--\ref{hyp:concave} hold. The leading-edge amplitude is strictly decreasing in the resistance, whereas the far-field delay is strictly increasing. More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict-order}
 0\le\beta_1<\beta_2<\infty
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \alpha_{\beta_1}>\alpha_{\beta_2},\qquad
 \tau_{\beta_1}<\tau_{\beta_2}.
\end{equation}
Since $\tau_0=0$, it follows that $\tau_\beta>0$ for every $\beta>0$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:small-beta}
Suppose that Assumptions~\ref{hyp:f}--\ref{hyp:concave} hold. The far-field delay is right differentiable at zero resistance and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-delay}
 \tau_\beta=K\beta+o(\beta)\qquad(\beta\downarrow0),\qquad
 K=\tau'_+(0)>\frac14.
\end{equation}
The leading-edge amplitude also has a right derivative at zero, and
\[
 \dot\alpha_0:=\lim_{\beta\downarrow0}
 \frac{\alpha_\beta-\alpha_0}{\beta},\qquad
 K=-\frac{\dot\alpha_0}{2\alpha_0}.
\]
The right first variation
\[
 w=\left.\partial_\beta u^\beta\right|_{\beta=0^+}
   =\lim_{\beta\downarrow0}\frac{u^\beta-u^0}{\beta}
\]
exists, with convergence uniform on each finite time interval in $L^\infty(\mathbb R\setminus\{0\})$, and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-variation-main}
 \begin{cases}
 w_t=w_{xx}+f'(u^0)w,&t>0,\quad x\ne0,\\
 [w](t)=u_x^0(t,0),&t>0,\\
 [w_x](t)=0,&t>0,\\
 w(0,x)=0,&x\ne0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The amplitude derivative is recovered from the leading edge of this first variation: for every $0<\theta<1/3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-derivative-main}
 \dot\alpha_0=\lim_{t\to\infty}
 \frac{e^{t^\theta}w(t,m(t)+t^\theta)}{t^\theta}.
\end{equation}

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:large-beta}
Suppose that Assumptions~\ref{hyp:f}--\ref{hyp:data} hold. As $\beta\to\infty$, the leading-edge amplitude satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:large-amplitude}
 \alpha_\beta=\frac{I}{\sqrt\pi}\frac{\log\beta}{\beta}(1+o(1)).
\end{equation}
Consequently, the far-field delay satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:large-delay}
 \tau_\beta=\frac12\log\beta-\frac12\log\log\beta
 +\frac12\log\frac{\sqrt\pi\alpha_0}{I}+o(1).
\end{equation}

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
For each finite resistance $\beta$, the far-field limit $R\to\infty$ in~\eqref{eq:delay} is taken first to define $\tau_\beta$; Theorem~\ref{thm:large-beta} then describes its behavior as $\beta\to\infty$. If the barrier is made completely impermeable, the receiving side remains at zero under Assumption~\ref{hyp:data}, so no positive level is reached there in finite time. Thus, the expansion concerns the far-field delay for finite resistances and does not directly give the establishment time at a fixed spatial point.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
In the deterministic additive-selection model of~\cite{PB1997}, the barrier strength is $B=[p]/p_x$; after scaling, this gives the resistance in~\eqref{eq:interface}. Their Appendix~A estimates establishment at the barrier from linearized growth. The numerical delay in their Figure~1A is computed from the difference in total allele numbers between solutions with and without a barrier after the wave has crossed, while Figure~1B follows the evolving difference in wave position. Definition~\eqref{eq:delay} instead specifies a limit of distant first-arrival times; Theorem~\ref{thm:phase} identifies this limit with the asymptotic phase difference divided by the wave speed.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:well-posedness}
Under Assumption~\ref{hyp:f}, let $u_0$ be measurable with $0\le u_0\le1$, and fix $0\le\beta<\infty$. There is a unique global bounded mild solution $u^\beta$, characterized by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mild}
 u^\beta(t,x)=\int_\mathbb R P_\beta(t,x,y)u_0(y)\,dy
 +\int_0^t\int_\mathbb R P_\beta(t-s,x,y)f(u^\beta(s,y))\,dy\,ds.
\end{equation}
It satisfies $0\le u^\beta\le1$ and is classical on each side for $t>0$, with one-sided traces satisfying \eqref{eq:interface}. At $\beta=0$, it is the classical homogeneous full-line solution for positive times. As $t\downarrow0$, $u^\beta(t,\cdot)\to u_0$ locally in $L^p(\mathbb R)$ for every $1\le p<\infty$, and pointwise at every continuity point of $u_0$ away from the interface.

For the same fixed $\beta$ and reaction $f$, comparison holds for any two measurable initial data taking values in $[0,1]$: if $u_0\le v_0$ almost everywhere, then their solutions satisfy $u^\beta(t,x)\le v^\beta(t,x)$ for $t>0$, including the corresponding one-sided interface traces.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Extend $f$ to a globally Lipschitz function on $\mathbb R$. The contraction property of the linear semigroup makes the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:mild} a contraction on bounded measurable functions on a sufficiently short time interval. This gives local existence and uniqueness without requiring continuity of the initial data.

To obtain comparison, choose a Lipschitz constant $L$ for the extension, so that $s\mapsto f(s)+Ls$ is nondecreasing. In the equivalent integral formulation with the damped kernel $e^{-Lt}P_\beta(t,x,y)$ and reaction $f(u)+Lu$, positivity of the kernel and Picard iteration preserve the order of the initial data. Comparison with the constant solutions $0$ and $1$ gives $0\le u^\beta\le1$, and hence global continuation and independence of the chosen extension of $f$.

Kernel smoothing, followed by parabolic regularity for the folded Neumann
and Robin problems, gives positive-time classical regularity and the
transmission traces; see~\cite{Lieberman1996} for the scalar boundary
regularity tools. The explicit kernel supplies the interface structure used here. Finally, the kernel has the initial approximation property locally in $L^p$ and at continuity points away from the interface, while the reaction integral in \eqref{eq:mild} is $O(t)$ in $L^\infty$. This proves the asserted initial convergence.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:reflection-comparison}
Under Assumptions~\ref{hyp:f}--\ref{hyp:data}, fix $0\le\beta<\infty$. Then $p_\beta(t,x)>q_\beta(t,x)$ for $t>0$ and $x>0$. For $\beta>0$, this inequality also holds at $x=0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reflection}
 j_\beta(t):=-q_{\beta,x}(t,0)
 =\frac{p_\beta(t,0)-q_\beta(t,0)}\beta>0,
 \qquad t>0.
\end{equation}
Here $j_\beta$ is oriented from the donor side to the receiving side, so $[u^\beta](t)=-\beta j_\beta(t)$ for $\beta>0$. For $\beta=0$, the interface flux satisfies $j_0(t):=-u_x^0(t,0)>0$. Moreover, for every finite $\beta\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:right-monotone}
 q_{\beta,x}(t,x)<0\qquad(t>0,x>0).
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Define
\[
 c_\beta(t,x)=\int_0^1 f'\!\left(q_\beta(t,x)
 +s\bigl(p_\beta(t,x)-q_\beta(t,x)\bigr)\right)\,ds.
\]
Then $(D_\beta)_t=(D_\beta)_{xx}+c_\beta D_\beta$, with
$|c_\beta|\le\|f'\|_{L^\infty([0,1])}$ and
$D_\beta(0,x)=u_0(-x)/2\ge0$ not identically zero. Robin comparison for $\beta>0$, or Dirichlet comparison for $\beta=0$, followed by the strong maximum principle gives $D_\beta(t,x)>0$ for $t>0$ and $x>0$. If $\beta>0$ and $D_\beta(t,0)=0$ at a positive time, the boundary Hopf lemma would give $(D_\beta)_x(t,0)>0$, contradicting \eqref{eq:fold-bc}. Thus $D_\beta(t,0)>0$, and the interface law yields \eqref{eq:reflection}. When $\beta=0$, the Hopf lemma instead gives
$j_0(t)=(D_0)_x(t,0)>0$.

For the receiving-side monotonicity, set $v=q_{\beta,x}$. At positive times,
\[
 v_t=v_{xx}+f'(q_\beta)v,\qquad x>0,
 \qquad v(t,0)=-j_\beta(t)<0.
\]
The zero initial condition for this derivative is justified directly from the kernel, even though $u_0$ is only measurable. Indeed, differentiation of \eqref{eq:barrier-kernel} gives
$\sup_{x>0}\int_\mathbb R|\partial_xP_\beta(t,x,y)|\,dy\le Ct^{-1/2}$.
Since $u_0$ vanishes for $y\ge b$ with $b<0$, the initial term and the bounded reaction term in \eqref{eq:mild} therefore give
\[
 \|q_{\beta,x}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^\infty(0,\infty)}
 \le Ct^{-1/2}e^{-c/t}+C\sqrt t\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(t\downarrow0),
\]
where $c>0$. With $M=\|f'\|_{L^\infty([0,1])}$ and $v^+=\max\{v,0\}$, comparison on $[\varepsilon,T]\times(0,\infty)$ gives
\[
 v(t,x)\le e^{M(t-\varepsilon)}
 \|v(\varepsilon,\cdot)^+\|_{L^\infty(0,\infty)},
 \qquad \varepsilon\le t\le T.
\]
Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ yields $v\le0$. The strong maximum principle and the strictly negative boundary values give $v<0$ in the interior. Neither concavity of $f$ nor monotonicity of $u_0$ is used.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:linear-front}
Fix $0<\rho<1/2$, and set
\[
 a_0=\frac12-\rho,
 \qquad d(\sigma)=e^{-a_0\sigma},
 \qquad \theta_0(\sigma)=\rho d(\sigma)+\frac32e^{-\sigma/2}.
\]
Let $\sigma_0\ge0$, and consider solutions for $\sigma\ge\sigma_0$, $y\ge0$, continuous up to the initial and lateral boundaries and classical in the interior. The initial and boundary values are compatible at $(\sigma_0,0)$, and the solutions are bounded in the respective weighted spaces on every finite time interval.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Suppose that $Q$ solves
\[
 Q_\sigma+\mathcal LQ+\theta_0Q_y=0,\qquad
 Q(\sigma,0)=h(\sigma),
\]
with $Q(\sigma_0,\cdot)\in X_{1/5}$, $h\in C^1([\sigma_0,\infty))$, and
$|h|+|h'|\le M\exp(-c e^{\rho\sigma})$ for fixed $M,c>0$.
If $\sigma_0$ is sufficiently large in terms of $\rho$, then
\begin{equation}\label{front:linear-upper}
 \sup_{\sigma\ge\sigma_0}\|Q(\sigma)\|_{1/5}\le C,
 \qquad
 \sup_{\sigma\ge\sigma_0+1}\sup_{0\le y\le1}|Q_y(\sigma,y)|\le C.
\end{equation}
If the initial and boundary data of $Q$ are nonnegative, then $Q\ge0$, and
$Q(\sigma,2d(\sigma))\le Cd(\sigma)$ for $\sigma\ge\sigma_0+1$.

\item Suppose that $P$, which may change sign, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{front:linear-positive-cut}
 P_\sigma+\mathcal LP+\vartheta(\sigma)P_y+A(\sigma,y)P=0,
 \qquad P(\sigma,0)=b(\sigma),
\end{equation}
where $\vartheta$, $A$, and $b$ are continuous and, for fixed $M,c>0$,
\[
 |\vartheta(\sigma)|\le Md(\sigma),\quad
 |b(\sigma)|\le Md(\sigma),\quad
 \|A(\sigma,\cdot)\|_\infty
       \le M e^{M\sigma-c e^{\rho\sigma}},\quad
 \sup_{\sigma\ge\sigma_0}\|P(\sigma)\|_{1/6}\le M.
\]
Then there is a real number $\alpha$ such that
\begin{align}
 J(\sigma)&:=\int_0^\infty yP(\sigma,y)\,dy
       \longrightarrow2\sqrt\pi\alpha,
       & |J(\sigma)-2\sqrt\pi\alpha|&\le Cd(\sigma),
       \label{front:linear-moment}\\
 \|P(\sigma)-\alpha\phi\|_{1/6}&\le Cd(\sigma).
       \label{front:linear-profile}
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
In part~(i), $C$ depends only on $\rho$, $M$, $c$, and an upper bound for $\|Q(\sigma_0)\|_{1/5}$. In part~(ii), it depends only on $\rho$, $\sigma_0$, $M$, and $c$; no derivative bound for $b$ enters the estimates. Thus families with a fixed starting time and common bounds have common constants.

Choose a fixed smooth, compactly supported function $\chi$ with $\chi(0)=1$,
and lift the boundary data in the first part by $h(\sigma)\chi(y)$.
The function $V=Q-h\chi$ has homogeneous boundary data and satisfies
\[
 V_\sigma+\mathcal LV+\theta_0V_y=F,
 \qquad
 \|F(\sigma)\|_{1/5}\le C e^{-c'e^{\rho\sigma}}.
\]
The drift convolution is controlled by
\begin{equation}\label{front:drift-absorption}
 \sup_{\sigma\ge\sigma_0}\int_{\sigma_0}^{\sigma}
 \bigl[1+(\sigma-s)^{-1/2}\bigr]d(s)\,ds
 \le C_\rho d(\sigma_0).
\end{equation}
Indeed, on $\sigma-s\ge1$ use the integrable tail of $d$, and on $0<\sigma-s<1$ use $d(s)\le d(\sigma_0)$ and the integrability of $(\sigma-s)^{-1/2}$. Since $|\theta_0|\le C_\rho d$, \eqref{front:kernel-bounded}--\eqref{front:kernel-derivative} give, for every finite final time $S$,
\[
 \sup_{\sigma_0\le\sigma\le S}\|V(\sigma)\|_{1/5}
 \le C\|V(\sigma_0)\|_{1/5}
 +C\int_{\sigma_0}^S\|F(s)\|_{1/5}\,ds
 +C e^{-a_0\sigma_0}
       \sup_{\sigma_0\le\sigma\le S}\|V(\sigma)\|_{1/5}.
\]
Taking $\sigma_0$ sufficiently large in terms of $\rho$, we absorb the last term and obtain the Gaussian bound. On $0\le y\le2$, the drift and the lifted forcing are uniformly
bounded. Local parabolic gradient estimates up to the flat boundary, applied to $V$ with homogeneous Dirichlet data on unit time intervals, give the second estimate in \eqref{front:linear-upper}; see~\cite{Lieberman1996} for the standard boundary regularity theory. The bound on $h'$ controls the lifted forcing, and adding back $h\chi$ preserves the gradient estimate.
Consequently, $Q(\sigma,2d)\le h+C(2d)\le Cd$.

The first moment in the second part satisfies the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{front:moment-identity}
 J'=b+\vartheta\int_0^\infty P\,dy
       -\int_0^\infty yAP\,dy.
\end{equation}
The identity can also be obtained from the mild formula using the kernel moment identities above and integration by parts for the drift term. The Gaussian bound justifies the resulting integrals. Thus $|J'|\le Cd$, and integration of the integrable tail gives
\eqref{front:linear-moment}. To prove the profile estimate, separate the
projection \eqref{front:projection} in Duhamel's formula.
The stable part of the initial term is controlled by \eqref{front:kernel-gap}.
For the drift term, use \eqref{front:kernel-derivative} and \eqref{front:kernel-derivative-gap}, interpreting the derivative in the distributional sense.
For the potential term, use
\eqref{front:kernel-bounded}--\eqref{front:kernel-gap}.
Split the boundary term into the most recent unit interval and the preceding
times, and apply the two estimates in \eqref{front:poisson-estimates}.
For $\sigma\ge\sigma_0+1$, the stable terms are bounded in sum by
\[
 C e^{-(\sigma-\sigma_0)}
 +C\int_{\sigma_0}^{\sigma}
 e^{-(\sigma-s)}d(s)\,ds
 +C\int_{\sigma-1}^{\sigma}
 [1+(\sigma-s)^{-1/2}]d(s)\,ds
 \le Cd(\sigma).
\]
Here we use $a_0<1$; the first unit interval is controlled by the assumed weighted bound on $P$, with a constant depending on $\sigma_0$. Hence
$\|P-\Pi P\|_{1/6}\le Cd$, and the first-moment estimate yields
\eqref{front:linear-profile}.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:leading-edge}
Under Assumptions~\ref{hyp:f}--\ref{hyp:data}, for every finite $\beta\ge0$ there exists a leading-edge coefficient $\alpha_\beta>0$, independent of the auxiliary cutoff exponent $\rho$. Section~\ref{sec:phase} identifies it with $A_*e^{\xi_\beta}$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:phase}.
Fix $0<\rho<1/3$. There are constants $C,T$, depending on $f,u_0,\rho$ but independent of $\beta$, such that $0<\alpha_\beta\le C$ and, for $t\ge T$,
\begin{equation}\label{front:nonlinear-profile}
 \left|w_\beta(\log t,\eta)-\alpha_\beta\eta e^{-\eta^2/4}\right|
       \le Ct^{\rho-1/2}e^{-\eta^2/7},
       \qquad\eta\ge t^{\rho-1/2}.
\end{equation}
In particular, locally uniformly for $\eta\in(0,\infty)$,
\[
 t^{-1/2}e^{\sqrt t\eta}
 u^\beta(t,m(t)+\sqrt t\eta)
       \longrightarrow\alpha_\beta\eta e^{-\eta^2/4}.
\]
If $0<\gamma<1/3$, then
\begin{equation}\label{front:intermediate}
 \frac{e^{t^\gamma}u^\beta(t,m(t)+t^\gamma)}{t^\gamma}
       \longrightarrow\alpha_\beta,
 \qquad
 \frac{e^{t^\gamma}u_x^\beta(t,m(t)+t^\gamma)}{t^\gamma}
       \longrightarrow-\alpha_\beta.
\end{equation}
Moreover, with $\ell(t)=m(t)+t^\rho$,
\begin{equation}\label{front:uniform-moment}
 \mathcal J_\beta(t):=e^{-2t}\int_{\ell(t)}^\infty
 (x-\ell(t))e^xu^\beta(t,x)\,dx,
 \qquad
 |\mathcal J_\beta(t)-2\sqrt\pi\alpha_\beta|
       \le Ct^{\rho-1/2}.
\end{equation}

Fix $\rho$, and write $d(\sigma)=e^{-(1/2-\rho)\sigma}$. By \eqref{eq:uniform-gaussian}, at any fixed positive time the weighted right tail has a Gaussian bound uniform in $\beta$. Choose a common starting time $\sigma_*$ sufficiently large for Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-front} and for the negative cutoff $z=-t^\rho$ to lie in $x>0$ for all subsequent times. Write $t_*=e^{\sigma_*}$. This cutoff uses the bound $u^\beta\le1$ to make the boundary input small:
\[
 0\le w_\beta(\sigma,-d(\sigma))
       \le h(\sigma):=e^{-\sigma/2-e^{\rho\sigma}}.
\]
Remove the nonnegative absorption term from \eqref{front:selfsimilar-equation} and construct a common linear supersolution with boundary value $h$. Its initial profile can be chosen continuous, nonnegative, dominating every $w_\beta(\sigma_*,\cdot)$ on the truncated region, with a common $X_{1/5}$ bound after shifting the boundary to zero, and with value $h(\sigma_*)$ at that boundary. Indeed, take the minimum of the bound $t_*^{-1/2}e^{\sqrt{t_*}\eta}$ and a Gaussian upper envelope furnished by \eqref{eq:uniform-gaussian}, enlarged to be at least $h(\sigma_*)$ at the cutoff. This also ensures initial-boundary compatibility.
Move the negative cutoff to zero by setting $y=\eta+d(\sigma)$.
The drift coefficient is exactly
$\theta_0=\rho d+\frac32e^{-\sigma/2}$, so the first part of
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-front} applies; $h$ and $h'$ satisfy its required decay bound.
For $\sigma\ge\sigma_*+1$, the gradient bound for the linear supersolution and comparison yield
\begin{equation}\label{front:positive-cutoff-bound}
 w_\beta(\sigma,d(\sigma))\le Cd(\sigma).
\end{equation}
The point $\eta=d$ corresponds to $y=2d$ in the supersolution coordinates. We increase the threshold time $T$ to include this one-unit waiting interval.

The positive cutoff makes the nonlinear potential uniformly small. Move this cutoff to zero and set
\[
 \mathcal P_\beta(\sigma,y)=w_\beta(\sigma,y+d(\sigma)).
\]
The coefficients in \eqref{front:linear-positive-cut} are now
\begin{equation}\label{front:positive-coefficients}
 \vartheta=-\rho d+\frac32e^{-\sigma/2},\qquad
 A_\beta(\sigma,y)=t\frac{g(u^\beta(t,x))}{u^\beta(t,x)}\ge0,
 \qquad x=m(t)+\sqrt t\,(y+d).
\end{equation}
The Gaussian bound for the common supersolution and \eqref{eq:g} give,
on $z\ge t^\rho$,
\[
 0\le A_\beta\le Ctu^\beta
       \le Ct^{3/2}e^{-t^\rho}.
\]
After the shift, the Gaussian bound remains uniform with the weaker weight
$1/6$. Together with \eqref{front:positive-cutoff-bound}, the second part of
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-front}, started at $\sigma_*+1$, yields a nonnegative amplitude and the profile and first-moment estimates. Positive-time regularity gives continuous coefficients, boundary traces, and compatible initial values at this starting time. The common weighted bound also yields a uniform upper bound for the amplitudes. Returning from $y=\eta-d$ to $\eta$ and weakening the Gaussian
weight slightly once more gives \eqref{front:nonlinear-profile}.
The change of variables also gives the exact identity
\[
 \int_0^\infty y\mathcal P_\beta(\log t,y)\,dy=\mathcal J_\beta(t),
\]
so \eqref{front:uniform-moment} holds with the same common constant. If two different cutoff exponents are used, the resulting profile limits at any fixed $\eta>0$ must agree. Since $\phi(\eta)>0$, their amplitudes coincide; we denote this common value by $\alpha_\beta$.

We now prove strict positivity for each fixed $\beta$. First fix a nonzero function $\psi\in C_c^\infty((1,2))$ with $\psi\ge0$. Choose a time $\sigma_1\ge\sigma_*+1$ sufficiently large that the decaying drift and potential can be absorbed in the homogeneous-boundary Duhamel estimate below and that
\[
 C\|\psi\|_{1/6}\int_{\sigma_1}^\infty
 (|\vartheta|+\|A_\beta\|_\infty)\,d\sigma
 <\frac12\int_0^\infty y\psi(y)\,dy,
\]
where $C$ is the constant in that estimate, independent of sufficiently large $\sigma_1$. Finally, positivity of the transmission kernel and comparison with the linear heat evolution give $\mathcal P_\beta(\sigma_1,y)>0$ for $y>0$. Hence one can choose $\varepsilon>0$ such that $\varepsilon\psi\le \mathcal P_\beta(\sigma_1,\cdot)$.

Let $H$ solve \eqref{front:linear-positive-cut} with the actual coefficients $\vartheta,A_\beta$, homogeneous Dirichlet boundary data, and initial datum $\varepsilon\psi$ at $\sigma_1$. Comparison gives $0\le H\le \mathcal P_\beta$. In Duhamel's formula, \eqref{front:drift-absorption} bounds the drift contribution by $Cd(\sigma_1)$ times the weighted supremum norm. The potential contribution is bounded by $C\int_{\sigma_1}^\infty\|A_\beta(s)\|_\infty\,ds$ times that norm, using \eqref{front:kernel-bounded}. Both coefficients are small for sufficiently large $\sigma_1$. Absorbing these terms gives
\[
 \sup_{\sigma\ge\sigma_1}\|H(\sigma)\|_{1/6}
       \le C\varepsilon\|\psi\|_{1/6}.
\]
By \eqref{front:moment-identity} with zero boundary data, the total variation of its first moment on $[\sigma_1,\infty)$ is at most
\[
 C\varepsilon\|\psi\|_{1/6}
 \int_{\sigma_1}^\infty
 (|\vartheta|+\|A_\beta\|_\infty)\,d\sigma
 <\frac\varepsilon2\int_0^\infty y\psi(y)\,dy.
\]
Thus the limiting first moment of $H$ is positive. Since $H\le \mathcal P_\beta$, this implies $\alpha_\beta>0$. The seed size $\varepsilon$ may depend on $\beta$, so this argument does not assert a positive lower bound uniform over all parameters.

For the intermediate-scale limits, given $\gamma$, choose $0<\rho<\gamma$.

Write $q=q_{\mathrm{aux}}$. Standard parabolic theory, after mapping the moving interval to a fixed interval, gives the bounded solution and positive-time regularity; see~\cite{Lieberman1996}. Comparison gives $0\le q\le1$. All energy calculations below start at $T+1$, so initial corner incompatibilities cause no difficulty. The identities may first be derived for smooth data and then justified by approximation on time intervals separated from $T$.
Recall $g(s)=s-f(s)$ from~\eqref{eq:g}, and set
\[
 G(s)=\int_0^s g(r)\,dr,\qquad V(t,z)=e^zq(t,z).
\]
By \eqref{eq:g}, $0\le g(s)\le Cs^2$, hence $0\le G(s)\le Cs^3$.
The function $V$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{matching:weighted-equation}
 V_t-V_{zz}+a(t)(V_z-V)+P_V(z,V)=0,
 \qquad a(t)=\frac3{2t},
\end{equation}
where
\[
 P(z,V)=e^{2z}G(e^{-z}V),\qquad
 P_V(z,V)=e^zg(e^{-z}V)\ge0.
\]
The right boundary condition becomes $V(t,R(t))=\alpha R(t)$, corresponding to the linear leading-edge growth $V\sim\alpha z$. Its gradient energy on the positive half of the interval has the leading contribution $\alpha^2R(t)/2$. Subtracting this contribution, define
\begin{equation}\label{matching:renormalized-energy}
 E(t)=\int_{-R(t)}^{R(t)}
 \left(\frac12V_z^2+P(z,V)\right)\,dz
 -\frac{\alpha^2}2R(t).
\end{equation}
Using that boundary condition and completing the square gives
\begin{align}\label{matching:coercive-identity}
 E(t)+\alpha
 ={}&\frac12\int_{-R(t)}^0V_z^2\,dz
 +\frac12\int_0^{R(t)}(V_z-\alpha)^2\,dz \notag\\
 &+\int_{-R(t)}^{R(t)}P(z,V)\,dz
 +\alpha[1-V(t,0)].
\end{align}
Since $V(t,0)=q(t,0)\le1$, every term on the right is nonnegative.
In particular, $E\ge-\alpha$.

The energy also controls the norm needed to estimate the drift term.
On the negative half of the interval we use $V\le e^z$; on the positive half we use
\[
 V(t,z)=V(t,0)+\alpha z
 +\int_0^z[V_z(t,s)-\alpha]\,ds.
\]
The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and~\eqref{matching:coercive-identity} give
\begin{equation}\label{matching:drift-control}
 \int_{-R(t)}^{R(t)}(V_z-V)^2\,dz
 \le CR(t)^2[E(t)+\alpha+1]+CR(t)^3.
\end{equation}
The constants below may depend on $\alpha,T,\gamma,f$ and $\|h'\|_{L^\infty([T,\infty))}$, but not on $t$. The resulting energy and dissipation bounds may additionally depend on the initial profile through the finite energy $E(T+1)$.

Differentiate~\eqref{matching:renormalized-energy} and use
\eqref{matching:weighted-equation} to eliminate $V_{zz}-P_V$.
The interior contribution is
\[
 -\int_{-R}^R V_t^2\,dz
 -a(t)\int_{-R}^R(V_z-V)V_t\,dz.
\]
At the moving right endpoint, $V_t+R'V_z=\alpha R'$.
Thus the right boundary contribution, including the renormalization term, is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{matching:right-boundary-flux}
 -\frac{R'}2[V_z(t,R)-\alpha]^2
 +R'P(R,\alpha R).
\end{equation}
Since $P(R,\alpha R)\le C\alpha^3R^3e^{-R}$, its positive part is integrable.
At the left endpoint, write $b(t)=e^{-R(t)}h(t)$. Differentiation along this moving endpoint gives
\[
 V_t(t,-R)-R'V_z(t,-R)=b'(t).
\]
The corresponding boundary contribution is
\begin{equation}\label{matching:left-boundary-flux}
 -V_z(t,-R)b'(t)-\frac{R'}2V_z(t,-R)^2
 +R'P(-R,b).
\end{equation}
Since $R'(t)>0$, Young's inequality gives directly
\[
 -V_z(t,-R)b'-\frac{R'}2V_z(t,-R)^2
 \le\frac{|b'|^2}{2R'}.
\]
Here $b'=e^{-R}(h'-R'h)$ and $P(-R,b)=e^{-2R}G(h)$. Hence the positive part of \eqref{matching:left-boundary-flux} is bounded by
\[
 \frac{|b'|^2}{2R'}+R'P(-R,b)
 \le C\bigl(t^{1-\gamma}+t^{\gamma-1}\bigr)e^{-2t^\gamma},
\]
which is integrable. This polynomial factor can be absorbed into a weaker exponential bound $Ce^{-R(t)}$.

Use Young's inequality to absorb the drift term into the dissipation.
Together with~\eqref{matching:drift-control}, this gives
\begin{align}\label{matching:energy-dissipation}
 E'(t)\le{}&-\frac12\int_{-R(t)}^{R(t)}V_t^2\,dz
 +Ct^{2\gamma-2}[E(t)+\alpha+1] \notag\\
 &+Ct^{3\gamma-2}+C(1+R(t)^3)e^{-R(t)}.
\end{align}
Both $t^{2\gamma-2}$ and $t^{3\gamma-2}$ are integrable, the latter being the term that requires $\gamma<1/3$; the exponential remainder is integrable as well. Apply Gronwall's inequality to $E+\alpha+1\ge1$,
and then integrate~\eqref{matching:energy-dissipation}, to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{matching:finite-dissipation}
 \sup_{t\ge T+1}E(t)<\infty,\qquad
 \int_{T+1}^\infty\int_{-R(t)}^{R(t)}V_t^2\,dz\,dt<\infty.
\end{equation}

We next identify the limits of time translates. Take any sequence $t_n\to\infty$. On fixed compact subsets of space-time, $0\le q\le1$ and interior parabolic estimates give local compactness of $V(t_n+s,z)$. After passing to a subsequence, these functions and their first spatial derivatives converge uniformly on compact subsets. For fixed $K,S>0$, the expanding domain eventually contains $[-K,K]$ throughout $[t_n-S,t_n+S]$, and \eqref{matching:finite-dissipation} yields
\[
 \int_{t_n-S}^{t_n+S}\int_{-K}^K|V_t(t,z)|^2\,dz\,dt
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus every subsequential limit is independent of $s$. Passing to the limit in the equation in the distributional sense, using $a(t_n+s)\to0$ uniformly for bounded $s$, and then applying local regularity, we find that its stationary profile $V_\infty$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{matching:stationary-limit}
 V_\infty''=e^zg(e^{-z}V_\infty),\qquad
 0\le e^{-z}V_\infty\le1.
\end{equation}
By~\eqref{matching:coercive-identity} and the uniform energy bound, we may first pass to the limit on $[0,K]$ and then let $K\to\infty$ to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{matching:tail-slope-control}
 \int_0^\infty[V_\infty'(z)-\alpha]^2\,dz<\infty.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{matching:stationary-limit} shows that $V_\infty'$ is nondecreasing.
Consequently,~\eqref{matching:tail-slope-control} forces
\begin{equation}\label{matching:identified-slope}
 \lim_{z\to\infty}V_\infty'(z)=\alpha,
 \qquad V_\infty(z)=\alpha z+o(z).
\end{equation}
In particular, $V_\infty\not\equiv0$.

It remains to identify this stationary profile as a translated wave. Set $Q=e^{-z}V_\infty$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{matching:wave-ode}
 Q''+2Q'+f(Q)=0,\qquad 0\le Q\le1,\qquad Q(+\infty)=0.
\end{equation}
To identify the limit at the other end, set $F(s)=\int_0^s f(r)\,dr$.
Local ODE estimates give a uniform bound on the derivative of the bounded
solution $Q$, whose mechanical energy satisfies
\[
 \frac d{dz}\left(\frac12Q'^2+F(Q)\right)=-2Q'^2.
\]
The energy has finite limits at both ends, so $Q'\in L^2(\mathbb R)$.
The boundedness of $Q''=-2Q'-f(Q)$ makes $Q'$ uniformly continuous,
and therefore $Q'(\pm\infty)=0$. The boundedness of
$Q'''=-2Q''-f'(Q)Q'$ also makes $Q''$ uniformly continuous. Together with the finite limits of $Q'$ at both ends, this implies $Q''(\pm\infty)=0$: otherwise, uniform continuity would force changes of a fixed size in $Q'$ along intervals escaping to one of the two ends. The ODE therefore gives $f(Q(z))\to0$. For every $0<\delta<1/2$, positivity of $f$ on $[\delta,1-\delta]$ implies that $Q$ eventually avoids this interval at either end. Continuity prevents repeated transitions between the neighborhoods of $0$ and $1$ on either tail. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ shows that each end limit exists and is either $0$ or $1$. If $Q(-\infty)=0$, both limiting
energies vanish, and the energy identity forces $Q'\equiv0$, contradicting
\eqref{matching:identified-slope}. Thus $Q(-\infty)=1$. The strong maximum
principle gives $0<Q<1$. At any point where $Q'=0$, one has
$Q''=-f(Q)<0$, so every critical point would be a strict local maximum. If $Q'$ were positive somewhere, it would remain positive to the left, since a transition from negative to positive would require a local minimum. This contradicts $Q(-\infty)=1$ and $Q<1$. Thus $Q'\le0$, and a zero of $Q'$ would itself force positive values immediately to its left. Hence $Q'<0$.
By the classical uniqueness recalled with~\eqref{eq:wave}, $Q(z)=U_*(z-\xi)$.

Comparison with~\eqref{matching:identified-slope} yields
$\xi=\log(\alpha/A_*)$. Every subsequence of time translates has the
same stationary limit. Hence the full family converges locally uniformly,
as asserted in~\eqref{matching:auxiliary-limit}.

 1-\delta\le u^\beta(t,x)\le1\qquad (|x|\le c_{\rm out}t).
\]
Choose $a_*>0$ so that
\[
 f(s)\ge a_*(1-s),\qquad f'(s)\le-a_*\qquad (1-\delta\le s\le1).
\]
Thus $1-u^\beta$ satisfies $(\partial_t-\partial_{xx}+a_*)(1-u^\beta)\le0$ in this interval. Choose $b_*>0$ such that $b_*^2+b_*c_{\rm out}<a_*$. The even function
\begin{equation}\label{small:saturation-screen}
 \Psi(t,x)=C e^{-a_*(t-T)}+C e^{-b_*c_{\rm out}t}\cosh(b_*x)
\end{equation}
satisfies
\[
 (\partial_t-\partial_{xx}+a_*)\Psi
 =C(a_*-b_*c_{\rm out}-b_*^2)e^{-b_*c_{\rm out}t}\cosh(b_*x)\ge0.
\]
It is continuous at zero with $\Psi_x(t,0)=0$, so it satisfies the interface
conditions for every $\beta$. Choose $T$ above the common starting time.
The first term controls the initial section $t=T$, while
$e^{-b_*c_{\rm out}t}\cosh(b_*c_{\rm out}t)\ge1/2$ at each lateral endpoint.
Thus a common choice of $C$ bounds $1-u^\beta$ on the parabolic boundary.
On $|x|\le c_{\rm in}t$, the spatial term is at most
$Ce^{-b_*(c_{\rm out}-c_{\rm in})t}$. The comparison principle gives
\begin{equation}\label{small:inner-saturation}
 1-u^\beta(t,x)\le Ce^{-\omega t},\qquad |x|\le c_{\rm in}t.
\end{equation}

Let $\mathsf S_\beta(r)$ denote the transmission heat operator, and write
$F_\beta(t)=f(u^\beta(t))$. Differentiating~\eqref{eq:barrier-kernel}
in the heat time, each Gaussian term is controlled after spatial integration
by $\|\partial_rG_r\|_{L^1(\mathbb R)}\le C/r$.
The translated Gaussian terms have the same bound, and their averaging
weight has total mass $\int_0^\infty e^{-s}\,ds=1$.
Consequently, for $r,h>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{small:heat-time-bounds}
 \|\partial_r\mathsf S_\beta(r)\|_{\infty\to\infty}\le \frac Cr,
 \qquad
 \|\mathsf S_\beta(r+h)-\mathsf S_\beta(r)\|_{\infty\to\infty}
 \le C\min\{1,h/r\},
\end{equation}
uniformly in $\beta$. For the second estimate, integrate the first when
$h\le r$, and otherwise use the contraction bound. Apply Duhamel's formula
on $[t-1,t+h]$, keeping the lower endpoint fixed. Boundedness of $u^\beta$
and $F_\beta$ gives, for $t\ge1$ and $0<h\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{small:uniform-time-modulus}
 \begin{split}
 \|u^\beta(t+h)-u^\beta(t)\|_\infty
 &\le Ch+C\int_0^1\min\{1,h/r\}\,dr\\
 &\le Ch(1+|\log h|)\le C_\theta h^\theta,
 \qquad 0<\theta<1.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The Lipschitz continuity of $f$ gives the same temporal modulus for
$F_\beta$. To differentiate at time $t\ge2$, again treat $t-1$ as a fixed
starting time and subtract the terminal reaction value. The resulting
cancellation is
\begin{equation}\label{small:time-cancellation}
 \begin{split}
 u_t^\beta(t)={}&(\partial_r\mathsf S_\beta)(1)u^\beta(t-1)
              +\mathsf S_\beta(1)F_\beta(t)\\
 &+\int_0^1(\partial_r\mathsf S_\beta)(r)
       [F_\beta(t-r)-F_\beta(t)]\,dr.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The last integrand has norm at most $Cr^{\theta-1}$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{small:uniform-time-derivative}
 \sup_{0<\beta\le\beta_*}\sup_{t\ge2}
       \|u_t^\beta(t)\|_\infty\le C.
\end{equation}
These estimates use the invariant bound $0\le u^\beta\le1$ and the boundedness
and Lipschitz continuity of $f$ on $[0,1]$. They require neither spatial
regularity of the initial datum nor concavity.

For each fixed $\beta$, the positive-time differences
$(u^\beta(t+h)-u^\beta(t))/h$ satisfy the same homogeneous interface law,
with reaction coefficient
$\int_0^1f'((1-\theta)u^\beta(t)+\theta u^\beta(t+h))\,d\theta$.
Passing to the limit at positive times yields the linear evolution for
$v_\beta=u_t^\beta$:
\[
 (v_\beta)_t=(v_\beta)_{xx}+f'(u^\beta)v_\beta,\qquad
 [v_\beta]=\beta(v_\beta)_x(t,0^\pm),\qquad [(v_\beta)_x]=0.
\]
The identities hold first in the mild sense on intervals separated from
zero; fixed-parameter parabolic regularity with $f\in C^2$ then gives the
one-sided traces. This regularity is used only to justify the equation and
boundary identities. Since $f'(u^\beta)\le-a_*$ on the outer moving interval,
$\Psi$ from~\eqref{small:saturation-screen} is also a supersolution for
$\partial_t-\partial_{xx}-f'(u^\beta)$ there. Compare both $v_\beta$ and
$-v_\beta$ with $\Psi$. The common global bound
in~\eqref{small:uniform-time-derivative} controls the initial data and
lateral endpoints. Thus, after increasing the common starting time,
\begin{equation}\label{small:inner-time-decay}
 |u_t^\beta(t,x)|\le Ce^{-\omega t},\qquad |x|\le c_{\rm in}t.
\end{equation}
The constants are obtained by comparison and do not depend on a
boundary regularity estimate as $\beta\downarrow0$.

We next control the Robin traces directly. Let $k=2/\beta$, let
$\mathsf T_k(s)$ be the half-line heat operator with condition
$v_x(0)=kv(0)$, and denote its kernel by $K_k$. The
formula~\eqref{order:robin-kernel} and integration by parts give
\begin{equation}\label{small:robin-trace-kernel}
 kK_k(s,0,y)=-2k\int_0^\infty e^{-kr}G'_s(y+r)\,dr\ge0,
 \qquad
 \int_0^\infty kK_k(s,0,y)\,dy\le\frac1{\sqrt{\pi s}}.
\end{equation}
More precisely, for every $R\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{small:robin-trace-tail}
 \int_R^\infty kK_k(s,0,y)\,dy
 =2k\int_0^\infty e^{-kr}G_s(R+r)\,dr
 \le\frac{e^{-R^2/(4s)}}{\sqrt{\pi s}}.
\end{equation}
Here $ke^{-kr}\,dr$ is a probability measure, so the bounds are
independent of $k$.
Set
\[
 N_\beta=\frac{f(p_\beta)-f(q_\beta)}2,\qquad
 M_\beta=\frac{f'(p_\beta)(p_\beta)_t-f'(q_\beta)(q_\beta)_t}2.
\]
Time derivatives are written explicitly as $\partial_t D_\beta$; the dot in $\dot\alpha_0$ denotes the right derivative with respect to resistance.
The folded equations and their positive-time difference quotients yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (D_\beta)_t&=(D_\beta)_{xx}+N_\beta,
 & (D_\beta)_x(t,0)&=kD_\beta(t,0),\\
 (\partial_t D_\beta)_t&=(\partial_t D_\beta)_{xx}+M_\beta,
 & (\partial_t D_\beta)_x(t,0)&=k\partial_t D_\beta(t,0).
 \end{aligned}
\]
In particular, on the most recent unit time interval,
\begin{equation}\label{small:differentiated-robin-mild}
 \begin{split}
 D_\beta(t)&=\mathsf T_k(1)D_\beta(t-1)
       +\int_0^1\mathsf T_k(s)N_\beta(t-s)\,ds,\\
 \partial_t D_\beta(t)&=\mathsf T_k(1)\partial_t D_\beta(t-1)
       +\int_0^1\mathsf T_k(s)M_\beta(t-s)\,ds.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The definitions give
\[
 |N_\beta|\le\|f'\|_\infty|D_\beta|,\qquad
 |\partial_t D_\beta|+|M_\beta|
 \le C\bigl(|(p_\beta)_t|+|(q_\beta)_t|\bigr).
\]
Thus $D_\beta,N_\beta,\partial_t D_\beta,M_\beta$ are bounded by a common constant
on the half-line for sufficiently large times. Moreover,
$|D_\beta|\le((1-p_\beta)+(1-q_\beta))/2$.
By~\eqref{small:inner-saturation} and~\eqref{small:inner-time-decay}, all four
functions are bounded by $Ce^{-\omega t}$ on
$0\le y\le R_t:=c_{\rm in}(t-1)$ throughout $[t-1,t]$, once this interval
lies beyond the common decay threshold.
Multiply the boundary traces in~\eqref{small:differentiated-robin-mild}
by $k$ and split the integrals at $R_t$. Equations
\eqref{small:robin-trace-kernel}--\eqref{small:robin-trace-tail} give
\[
 \begin{split}
 k|D_\beta(t,0)|+k|\partial_t D_\beta(t,0)|
 &\le Ce^{-\omega t}\left(1+\int_0^1s^{-1/2}\,ds\right)
       +Ce^{-R_t^2/4}\\
 &\quad+C\int_0^1s^{-1/2}e^{-R_t^2/(4s)}\,ds
 \le Ce^{-\omega't}.
 \end{split}
\]
For each fixed $\beta$, the positive-time regularity used above also
permits differentiation of the boundary value $D_\beta(t,0)$ in time,
with derivative $\partial_t D_\beta(t,0)$. The Robin condition therefore gives
\[
 d_\beta(t)=-kD_\beta(t,0),\qquad
 d'_\beta(t)=-k\partial_t D_\beta(t,0).
\]
In particular $d_\beta\in C^1((0,\infty))$. The trace estimates prove
\eqref{small:flux-decay} after reducing the common decay rate if necessary;
no uniform estimate for $u_{tx}^\beta$ is needed.

Recall the Neumann heat semigroup $\mathsf N(t)$ on the half-line and put
\[
 b_\beta(t,x)=2\int_0^tG_{t-s}(x)j_\beta(s)\,ds.
\]
The mild formulas with boundary loss and input give
\[
 \begin{split}
 p_N(t)-p_\beta(t)
 &=b_\beta(t)+\int_0^t\mathsf N(t-s)
       [f(p_N(s))-f(p_\beta(s))]\,ds,\\
 q_\beta(t)&=b_\beta(t)+\int_0^t\mathsf N(t-s)f(q_\beta(s))\,ds.
 \end{split}
\]
Since $\|b_\beta(t)\|_\infty\le2\varepsilon\sqrt{t/\pi}$,
contractivity of $\mathsf N$ and the Lipschitz continuity of $f$, with
$f(0)=0$, imply
\[
 \mathcal E_\beta(t):=\|p_N(t)-p_\beta(t)\|_\infty+\|q_\beta(t)\|_\infty
 \le C\varepsilon\sqrt t+\|f'\|_\infty\int_0^t \mathcal E_\beta(s)\,ds.
\]
Gronwall's inequality proves~\eqref{large:finite-time}.
These formulas hold up to the boundary for positive times. The initial
profiles vanish near the interface, so the heat representations also give
$p_\beta(t,0),q_\beta(t,0),h(t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$.
Thus $j_\beta$ has the stated continuous extension at zero. The identity
$j_\beta/\varepsilon=p_\beta(t,0)-q_\beta(t,0)$ now gives
\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}
 \left|\frac{j_\beta(t)}\varepsilon-h(t)\right|\le C_T\varepsilon.
\]

Writing $f(q)=q-g(q)$, the receiving-side formula becomes
\[
 q_\beta(t,x)=2\int_0^t e^{t-s}G_{t-s}(x)j_\beta(s)\,ds
 -\int_0^t e^{t-s}
      \bigl[\mathsf N(t-s)g(q_\beta(s))\bigr](x)\,ds.
\]
By~\eqref{eq:g} and~\eqref{large:finite-time},
$\sup_{s\le T}\|g(q_\beta(s))\|_\infty\le C_T\varepsilon^2$.
Divide the formula by $\varepsilon$ and subtract $v(t,x)$.
The flux error contributes at most
$C_T\varepsilon\int_0^t(t-s)^{-1/2}\,ds$, and the nonlinear term
contributes at most $C_T\varepsilon\int_0^t e^{t-s}\,ds$.
This proves~\eqref{large:linear-input-error} and hence~\eqref{large:seed}.

Continuity and positivity of $v_T$ follow from its integral representation,
$h(s)>0$ for $s>0$, and the integrable bound $G_r(x)\le Cr^{-1/2}$.

 =2e^{-2T}\int_0^T e^{2(T-s)}h(s)\,ds=2I_T,

which proves~\eqref{large:seed-mass}.

 0\le w^\varepsilon(t,x)\le1/C_f\le1\qquad(t\ge0,x\in\mathbb R).

 \liminf_{\beta\to\infty}\frac{\beta\alpha_\beta}{\log\beta}
 \ge\frac I{\sqrt\pi}.
\end{equation}

Under Assumptions~\ref{hyp:f}--\ref{hyp:data}, let $\beta\ge e^2$ and
use the rescaling and weight above. The moment

 \mathcal M(\sigma)=\int_0^\infty W(\sigma,\eta)r(\sigma,\eta)\,d\eta
\]
is finite and locally absolutely continuous for $\sigma\ge0$, with
$\mathcal M(0)=0$. There is a constant $C$, depending only on $f,u_0$,
such that, for all $\sigma\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{large:uniform-first-moment}

 \le\mathcal M(\sigma)\le2IL+C\log(2+L)+C.
\end{equation}

We first bound the mass and boundary trace. Full-space linear comparison
in~\eqref{large:point-source} gives
\[
 Q_\beta(t,X)\le2\int_0^t e^{t-s}G_{t-s}(X)j_\beta(s)\,ds.
\]
Changing variables in the mass integral, enlarging its spatial domain to
$\mathbb R$, and using $\int_\mathbb R e^XG_r(X)\,dX=e^r$ yield
\begin{equation}\label{large:mass-bound}
 \int_0^\infty r(\sigma,\eta)\,d\eta
 \le 2\sqrt{1+t}\int_0^t\frac{j_\beta(s)}\varepsilon e^{-2s}\,ds
 \le2I e^{\sigma/2}.
\end{equation}
For $b(\sigma)=r(\sigma,0)$,~\eqref{large:linear-bound} implies
$b\le C\varepsilon e^t/(1+t)\le Ce^{-3L/4}$ when $t\le L/4$.
For $t\ge L/4$, the bound $Q_\beta\le1$ gives
$b\le(1+t)^{-1/2}$. Since $d\sigma=dt/(1+t)$,
\begin{equation}\label{large:boundary-bound}
 \int_0^\infty b(\sigma)\,d\sigma
 \le Ce^{-3L/4}\log(1+L)+CL^{-1/2}\le CL^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}

Define the source weight without evaluating $W$ at negative arguments:
\[
 \mathcal W_s(\sigma)=
 \begin{cases}
 W(\sigma,\eta_s(t)),&\eta_s(t)>0,\\
 0,&\eta_s(t)\le0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad t=e^\sigma-1.
\]

 \begin{split}

 +k^2e^{-\sigma}\int_0^\infty\psi' r\,d\eta
 -\int_0^\infty WN\,d\eta\\

 \end{split}
\end{equation}

$j_\beta\le\varepsilon$ give

 \le C_T\varepsilon t,\qquad 0\le t\le T,
\]
because $\int_{\mathbb R}(1+|X|)e^XG_s(X)\,dX$ is bounded for

 \qquad(t\downarrow0).
\]

 k^2e^{-\sigma}\int_0^\infty\psi' r\,d\eta
 \le C e^{-\sigma/2},\qquad
