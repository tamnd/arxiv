<!-- th-1: body_md -->
\label{th:main}
Every $\{C_3,C_5\}$-free graph $G$ of order $n\ge1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
q_{\min}(G)<\frac{569n}{10000}=0.0569n.
\end{equation}
<!-- end th-1 -->
<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:local}
For every choice of $\sigma,w$ and every graph $G$ of order $N\ge7$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localrayleigh}
q_{\min}(G)D_\sigma(w;G)
\le (N-6)E_\sigma(w;G)+5D_\sigma(w;G).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For each tuple $R$ inducing $\sigma$, apply $q_{\min}(G)z^Tz\le z^TQz$ to the vector above; this inequality also holds when $z=0$. Substituting \eqref{eq:rootD} and \eqref{eq:rootE} and dividing by $N-5$ gives
\begin{align*}
q_{\min}(G)D_R(w)
&\le (N-6)E_R(w)+\frac1{N-5}\sum_{x\notin R}d_R(x)w_R(x)^2\\
&\le (N-6)E_R(w)+5D_R(w).
\end{align*}
Averaging over all ordered five-tuples, with zero contribution from tuples of the wrong type, proves \eqref{eq:localrayleigh}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:flagsquare}
For every fixed linear combination $L$ of flags of one type, there is a constant $C_L$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flagsquarenonneg}
\left[\!\left[L^2\right]\!\right]_G\ge-\frac{C_L}{N}
\end{equation}
for every admissible graph $G$ of sufficiently large order $N$.
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix roots inducing $\tau$, and let $f_i$ be the probability of obtaining $F_i^\tau$ by choosing a uniformly random $(\ell-k)$-set of other vertices. Independent choices for two such sets $S,T$ give $(\sum_i a_i f_i)^2\ge0$. Moreover,
\[
\Pr(S\cap T\ne\varnothing)\le\frac{(\ell-k)^2}{N-k}=O(N^{-1}),
\]
uniformly in the roots. Conditional on $S\cap T=\varnothing$, the distribution is exactly that of the two flags defined above: every ordered pair of disjoint extension sets has the same number of internal orderings. Since the coefficients are fixed, this conditioning changes the average by $O(N^{-1})$. Averaging over the roots, with zero contribution from an incorrect type, proves the assertion.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lem-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:certificate}
For the four local weights and the $40$ linear combinations specified above, there are rational numbers $\gamma_H>10^{-10}$, $H\in\mathcal H_7$, such that every admissible graph $G$ of order $N\ge7$ satisfies the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flagidentity}
r_0\mathcal D(G)-\mathcal E(G)
=\sum_{u}\left[\!\left[L_u^2\right]\!\right]_G
+\sum_{H\in\mathcal H_7}\gamma_H p(H,G).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:countingbound}
r_0\mathcal D(G)-\mathcal E(G)\ge10^{-10}-O(N^{-1}),
\end{equation}
where the implicit constant is independent of $G$.
<!-- end lem-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We expand all the flag expressions in the common basis $\mathcal H_7$. Index the $40$ columns by $u$ and define
\[
M_{u,H}=\left[\!\left[L_u^2\right]\!\right]_H,\qquad
d_H=\mathcal D(H),\qquad e_H=\mathcal E(H).
\]
The averaging principle, obtained by first choosing a seven-element set and then ordering it, gives
\[
\left(\left[\!\left[L_u^2\right]\!\right]_G\right)_u=M\left(p(H,G)\right)_H,
\quad
\mathcal D(G)=\sum_H d_Hp(H,G),\quad
\mathcal E(G)=\sum_H e_Hp(H,G).
\]
It is therefore enough to make the remainder positive for each $H\in\mathcal H_7$. Multiplying these coordinatewise inequalities by the nonnegative densities $p(H,G)$ and summing will give the required counting inequality. In matrix form, the coefficient comparison is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coordinate}
\mathbf1^TM<r_0d-e,
\end{equation}
where $d=(d_H)_H$ and $e=(e_H)_H$ are row vectors and the inequality is coordinatewise. We give the exact calculation underlying this comparison.

Write $a=569$ and $b=10000$, and let $A$ denote the adjacency matrix of the graph $H$ under consideration. For an ordering $(v_0,\ldots,v_6)$, let $i,j$ be the indices of the two flags over a given type. An incorrect root type contributes zero. Summing over all orderings and all columns gives the integer
\begin{align}\label{eq:xi}
\Xi_H={}&b\sum_{\tau}\sum_{u\text{ column of }U_\tau}
\sum_{\text{orderings of type }\tau}u_i u_j\notag\\
&+\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{c\text{ column of }V_\sigma}
\sum_{\text{orderings of type }\sigma}
\left[b A_{v_5v_6}(c_i^2+c_i c_j)-a c_i^2\right].
\end{align}
To relate the second line to \eqref{eq:flagD}--\eqref{eq:flagE}, fix a spectral column $c$ and sum over orderings of its root type. Exchanging $x=v_5$ and $y=v_6$ interchanges $i,j$ and preserves $A_{xy}$. Hence
\[
\sum A_{xy}(c_i^2+c_ic_j)=\frac12\sum A_{xy}(c_i+c_j)^2,
\qquad
\sum c_i^2=\frac12\sum(c_i^2+c_j^2).
\]
Thus the spectral contribution agrees with the symmetric flag expressions. Each product of weights contributes a factor $s^{-2}$, averaging over seven-vertex orderings contributes $1/7!$, and multiplication by $b$ clears the denominator of $r_0=a/b$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xiscale}
\Xi_H=5040bs^2\left(\sum_uM_{u,H}+e_H-r_0d_H\right).
\end{equation}
For example, let $H=7K_1$. Only the types $\tau_1,\tau_2,\sigma_1$ contribute. The rows for their empty flags in $U_{\tau_1},U_{\tau_2},V_{\sigma_1}$ are, respectively,
\[
(-34,-5,58,-1588,-1747),\quad
(0,0,0,0,37,0,0),\quad
(4299,10700).
\]
The first two rows have squared norms $5578298$ and $1369$, and $e_H=0$. Hence this coordinate of \eqref{eq:coordinate} can be checked directly:
\[
\sum_u M_{u,H}+e_H-r_0d_H
=\frac{5578298+1369-\frac{569}{10000}(4299^2+10700^2)}{10^{16}}
=-\frac{19864057169}{10^{20}}<0.
\]
All other coordinates follow from the same finite expansion. Table~\ref{tab:coefficients} reports the exact integer comparison for all $89$ graphs, grouped by their numbers of edges. For each edge count $m$, its last column gives $\min_{e(H)=m}(-\Xi_H)$. Every value in this column is positive, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificatemax}
\max_{H\in\mathcal H_7}\Xi_H=-75231473686920.
\end{equation}
It follows that \eqref{eq:coordinate} holds with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
\gamma_H=-\frac{\Xi_H}{5040bs^2}
\ge\frac{208976315797}{1400000000000000000000}>10^{-10}.
\end{equation}

The graph list can be obtained by enumerating all bipartitions of seven vertices and all subsets of their crossing edges, taking isomorphism classes, and adding $C_7$. The bipartite enumeration contains $103237$ labelled graphs and $88$ isomorphism classes. Each coefficient is then computed from all $5040$ orderings using \eqref{eq:xi}; the expected coefficients are used only as a separate check. This proves \eqref{eq:flagidentity}. Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:flagsquare} to the $40$ squares and using $\sum_Hp(H,G)=1$ proves \eqref{eq:countingbound}.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Suppose that an admissible graph $G$ of order $n$ satisfies $q_{\min}(G)\ge r_0n$. Let $G^{(t)}$ be its balanced complete blow-up, in which each vertex is replaced by an independent set of size $t$ and each edge by a complete bipartite graph. The graph $G^{(t)}$ is still admissible: an odd cycle of length at most five in it would project to an odd closed walk of that length in $G$, and hence to a forbidden odd cycle.

With vertices ordered by their replacement sets,
\[
Q(G^{(t)})=tD(G)\otimes I_t+A(G)\otimes J_t,
\]
where $I_t$ is the identity matrix and $J_t$ is the all-ones matrix. On vectors constant on each replacement set, this matrix acts as $tQ(G)$. On the subspace supported on the replacement set of $v$ with coordinate sum zero, it acts as multiplication by $td(v)$. These invariant subspaces give all its eigenvalues. Since $q_{\min}(G)\le d(v)$ for every $v$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blowup}
q_{\min}(G^{(t)})=tq_{\min}(G)\ge r_0tn.
\end{equation}
Write $N=tn$. Apply \eqref{eq:spectralreduction} to $G^{(t)}$. Because $\mathcal E\ge0$,
\[
r_0\mathcal D(G^{(t)})-\mathcal E(G^{(t)})
\le\frac{5\mathcal D(G^{(t)})-6\mathcal E(G^{(t)})}{N}
\le\frac{5\mathcal D(G^{(t)})}{N}=O(N^{-1}).
\]
The final bound holds because the four weight vectors are fixed. In contrast, Lemma~\ref{lem:certificate} gives
\[
r_0\mathcal D(G^{(t)})-\mathcal E(G^{(t)})
\ge10^{-10}-O(N^{-1}).
\]
Letting $t$ tend to infinity gives a contradiction. This proves the strict finite-graph inequality in \eqref{eq:main}.
