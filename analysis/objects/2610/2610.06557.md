<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:convex}
The function $f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R$ is twice continuously differentiable and
convex. Thus, for every $x,y\in\mathbb R^d$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convexity}
 f(y)\ge f(x)+\nabla f(x)^T(y-x).
\end{equation}
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- assumption-2: body_md -->
\label{ass:level}
The set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:level-set}
\mathcal C_0=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:f(x)\le f(x^0)\}
\end{equation}
is bounded.
<!-- end assumption-2 -->
<!-- assumption-3: body_md -->
\label{ass:hessian}
There exists a constant $H>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hessian-lipschitz}
 \|\nabla^2f(x)-\nabla^2f(y)\|\le2H\|x-y\|
 \qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathcal C_0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end assumption-3 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:oracles}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:convex}--\ref{ass:hessian}, consider a
nonstationary iterate $x^k$ of Algorithm~\ref{alg:cubic}.
Let the columns of
$Q^k\in\mathbb R^{d\times r_k}$ form a Euclidean orthonormal basis of
$\mathcal S^k$, where $r_k=\dim\mathcal S^k\le3$. Define
\[
 B^k=(Q^k)^T\nabla^2f(x^k)Q^k.
\]
The step is $s^k=Q^k a^k$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced-solution}
 a^k=-(B^k+\lambda_k I)^{-1}(Q^k)^T\nabla f(x^k),
\end{equation}
and $\lambda_k$ is the unique positive solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:secular}
 \lambda_k=H\bigl\|(B^k+\lambda_k I)^{-1}
                                  (Q^k)^T\nabla f(x^k)\bigr\|.
\end{equation}
It satisfies $\lambda_k\le\sqrt{H\|\nabla f(x^k)\|}$.
After initialization, each iteration needs one new gradient and
at most three HVPs, and no function evaluations.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $Q^k$ is orthonormal, substituting $s=Q^k a$ into the model gives
\[
 \nabla f(x^k)^TQ^k a+\tfrac12 a^TB^k a+\tfrac H3\|a\|^3.
\]
Convexity gives $B^k\succeq0$. Since $H>0$, this reduced objective
is strictly convex and coercive, so it has a unique minimizer.
Its first-order condition is
$(B^k+H\|a^k\|I)a^k=-(Q^k)^T\nabla f(x^k)$.
The projected gradient is nonzero because the full gradient is
nonzero and belongs to $\mathcal S^k$. Thus $a^k\ne0$ and
$\lambda_k=H\|a^k\|>0$.
Also $B^k\succeq0$, so $B^k+\lambda_k I$ is invertible.
This proves \eqref{eq:reduced-solution}--\eqref{eq:secular}.

To verify uniqueness directly, diagonalize $B^k$.
The norm on the right of \eqref{eq:secular} is a strictly decreasing
continuous function of positive $\lambda_k$, with positive
(possibly infinite) limit at zero and limit zero at infinity.
The left side increases strictly from zero to infinity, so there
is exactly one intersection. Since
$\|(B^k+\lambda_k I)^{-1}\|\le1/\lambda_k$, the equation also gives
$\lambda_k^2\le H\|(Q^k)^T\nabla f(x^k)\|=H\|\nabla f(x^k)\|$.

The directions defining $\mathcal S^k$ are available before the model
solve. One HVP for each column of $Q^k$ determines
$\nabla^2f(x^k)Q^k$ and hence $B^k$. Forming
$(Q^k)^T\nabla f(x^k)$ uses the current gradient already in memory.
The scalar solve and all reduced linear algebra then use only
these coefficients, with no further objective-oracle calls.
The single new gradient is $\nabla f(x^{k+1})$, used in the
correction and in the next iteration. With the supplied $H$, the
algorithm requires no function-value test.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:invariance}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:convex}--\ref{ass:hessian}, let
$x\in\mathcal C_0$, let $S$ be a linear subspace containing
$\nabla f(x)$, and let $s$ be defined by the cubic subproblem
\eqref{eq:model}.  The minimizer exists and is unique, and it satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-stationarity}
 \Pi_S\bigl(\nabla f(x)+\nabla^2f(x)s+H\|s\|s\bigr)=0.
\end{equation}
Moreover, $[x,x+s]\subseteq\mathcal C_0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-decreases}
 f(x)-f(x+s)
 \ge\tfrac12s^T\nabla^2f(x)s+\tfrac{2H}{3}\|s\|^3.
\end{equation}
If $\nabla f(x)\ne0$, then $s\ne0$ and the decrease is strict.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Convexity gives $\nabla^2f(x)\succeq0$.  Hence the objective in
\eqref{eq:model} is strictly convex and coercive on $S$, owing to the term
$H\|u\|^3/3$.  It has a unique minimizer, and its first-order condition is
\eqref{eq:model-stationarity}.  Taking the inner product of that condition
with $s\in S$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationarity-along-step}
 \nabla f(x)^Ts=-s^T\nabla^2f(x)s-H\|s\|^3.
\end{equation}

If $\nabla f(x)=0$, uniqueness gives $s=0$, and all the conclusions are
immediate.  Suppose therefore that $\nabla f(x)\ne0$.  Because
$-\nabla f(x)\in S$ is a strict descent direction for the cubic objective
at zero, its minimizer is nonzero.  Equation
\eqref{eq:stationarity-along-step} then gives
$\nabla f(x)^Ts<0$, so differentiability implies
$f(x+ts)<f(x)\le f(x^0)$ for all sufficiently small $t>0$.

For every $0<t\le1$, substituting
\eqref{eq:stationarity-along-step} into the full cubic expression at $ts$
gives
\begin{align*}
 &t\nabla f(x)^Ts
 +\frac{t^2}{2}s^T\nabla^2f(x)s
 +\frac{Ht^3}{3}\|s\|^3\\
 &\qquad=-t(1-t/2)s^T\nabla^2f(x)s
          -Ht(1-t^2/3)\|s\|^3<0.
\end{align*}
Suppose that the segment from $x$ to $x+s$ leaves $\mathcal C_0$, and set
\[
 t_{\rm exit}=\inf\{t\in[0,1]:f(x+ts)>f(x^0)\}.
\]
The initial strict decrease gives $t_{\rm exit}>0$.  Continuity gives
$f(x+t_{\rm exit}s)=f(x^0)$, and the segment preceding this point is
contained in $\mathcal C_0$.  We may therefore apply
\eqref{eq:taylor-upper} on that segment.  The preceding strict inequality
then yields
\begin{align*}
 f(x+t_{\rm exit}s)
 &\le f(x)+t_{\rm exit}\nabla f(x)^Ts
   +\frac{t_{\rm exit}^2}{2}s^T\nabla^2f(x)s
   +\frac{Ht_{\rm exit}^3}{3}\|s\|^3\\
 &<f(x)\le f(x^0),
\end{align*}
contradicting the definition of $t_{\rm exit}$.  Thus
$[x,x+s]\subseteq\mathcal C_0$.

The Taylor upper bound is now valid for the full step.  Using
\eqref{eq:stationarity-along-step},
\begin{align*}
 f(x+s)-f(x)
 &\le\nabla f(x)^Ts+\tfrac12s^T\nabla^2f(x)s
                      +\tfrac H3\|s\|^3\\
 &=-\tfrac12s^T\nabla^2f(x)s-\tfrac{2H}{3}\|s\|^3.
\end{align*}
Rearranging proves \eqref{eq:two-decreases}.  Its right-hand side is
strictly positive when $s\ne0$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:defect}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:convex}--\ref{ass:hessian}, let $x$, $S$, and
$s$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:descent-defect}
 \|\Pi_S\nabla f(x+s)\|
 \le2H\|s\|^2
 \le(18H)^{1/3}\bigl(f(x)-f(x+s)\bigr)^{2/3}.
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:gradient-decrease}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:convex}--\ref{ass:hessian}, let $x$, $S$, and
$s$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}.  Suppose
$\nabla f(x^0)\ne0$. Then $L_0+\sqrt{2HL_0D_0}>0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradient-budget}
 f(x)-f(x+s)\ge\frac{\|\nabla f(x)\|^2}{3\bigl(L_0+\sqrt{2HL_0D_0}\bigr)}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For this proof, abbreviate the gradient-decrease coefficient by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decrease-constant}
 \mathcal L:=L_0+\sqrt{2HL_0D_0}.
\end{equation}
The segment from $x^\star$ to $x\in\mathcal C_0$ lies in the convex set
$\mathcal C_0$.  Since $\nabla f(x^\star)=0$, integration of the Hessian
along this segment gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradient-level-bound}
 \|\nabla f(x)\|\le L_0\|x-x^\star\|\le L_0D_0.
\end{equation}
In particular, applying this bound at $x^0$ shows that
$\nabla f(x^0)\ne0$ implies $L_0D_0>0$, and hence $\mathcal L>0$.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}, the Taylor upper bound applies to the
accepted step.  By the minimizing property in \eqref{eq:model}, its full
cubic expression is no larger than the value at
$-\nabla f(x)/\mathcal L\in S$.  Consequently,
\begin{align*}
 f(x+s)-f(x)
 &\le\nabla f(x)^Ts+\tfrac12s^T\nabla^2f(x)s
                      +\tfrac H3\|s\|^3\\
 &\le-\frac{\|\nabla f(x)\|^2}{\mathcal L}
 +\frac{\nabla f(x)^T\nabla^2f(x)\nabla f(x)}{2\mathcal L^2}
 +\frac{H\|\nabla f(x)\|^3}{3\mathcal L^3}\\
 &\le-\frac{\|\nabla f(x)\|^2}{\mathcal L}
 \left(1-\frac{L_0}{2\mathcal L}
          -\frac{H\|\nabla f(x)\|}{3\mathcal L^2}\right).
\end{align*}
The last inequality uses
$\|\nabla^2f(x)\|\le L_0$.  Also $L_0/(2\mathcal L)\le1/2$, and
$\mathcal L^2\ge2HL_0D_0\ge2H\|\nabla f(x)\|$ by
\eqref{eq:gradient-level-bound}, so
$H\|\nabla f(x)\|/(3\mathcal L^2)\le1/6$.  The last parenthesis is
therefore at least $1/3$, which gives
\[
 f(x+s)-f(x)\le-\frac{\|\nabla f(x)\|^2}{3\mathcal L}.
\]
Rearranging proves \eqref{eq:gradient-budget}.  Only the accepted step
uses a Taylor estimate; the comparison point
$x-\nabla f(x)/\mathcal L$ is used solely to compare explicit cubic
expressions and need not belong to $\mathcal C_0$.

\label{lem:reserve}
For all integers $0\le \ell\le k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window-reserve}
\begin{aligned}
 \omega_k^2\Delta_{k+1}
 +\frac34\sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i^2d_i
 &\le \omega_{\ell-1}^2\Delta_\ell
 +(x^0-x^\star)^T(z^k-z^{\ell-1})\\
 &\quad
 -\frac{\|z^k\|^2-\|z^{\ell-1}\|^2}{12\bigl(L_0+\sqrt{2HL_0D_0}\bigr)}
 +D_0\sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i\|e^i\|.
\end{aligned}

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The weight identities in Lemma~\ref{lem:weights} give
$\omega_i=\omega_i^2-\omega_{i-1}^2$.  Summation by parts therefore yields
the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-gap-identity}
 \omega_k^2\Delta_{k+1}
 +\sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i^2d_i
 =\omega_{\ell-1}^2\Delta_\ell+\sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i\Delta_i.
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\[
 \sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i\Delta_i
 =\omega_k^2\Delta_k-\omega_{\ell-1}^2\Delta_\ell
   +\sum_{i=\ell}^{k-1}\omega_i^2(\Delta_i-\Delta_{i+1}),
\]
and substituting $\Delta_k=\Delta_{k+1}+d_k$ gives
\eqref{eq:weighted-gap-identity}.

Convexity at $x^i$ and the decomposition
$\nabla f(x^i)=h^i+e^i$ imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_i
 &\le \nabla f(x^i)^T(x^i-x^\star)\\
 &=(h^i)^T(x^i-x^0)+(h^i)^T(x^0-x^\star)
   +(e^i)^T(x^i-x^\star)\\
 &\le (h^i)^T(x^0-x^\star)+D_0\|e^i\|.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $(h^i)^T(x^i-x^0)=0$ by \eqref{eq:orthogonalities}, and
$\|x^i-x^\star\|\le D_0$ because $x^i\in\mathcal C_0$.  Since
$z^i-z^{i-1}=\omega_i h^i$, summing the last inequality after multiplication
by $\omega_i$ gives
\[
 \sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i\Delta_i
 \le (x^0-x^\star)^T(z^k-z^{\ell-1})
     +D_0\sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i\|e^i\|.
\]

The second orthogonality in \eqref{eq:orthogonalities} gives a Pythagorean
identity at every update:
\[
 \|z^i\|^2
 =\|z^{i-1}+\omega_i h^i\|^2
 =\|z^{i-1}\|^2+\omega_i^2\|h^i\|^2.
\]
Moreover, $h^i$ is the orthogonal-projection residual of
$\nabla f(x^i)$, so $\|h^i\|\le\|\nabla f(x^i)\|$.  Telescoping and using
\eqref{eq:algorithm-gradient} now give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aggregate-interval}
 \|z^k\|^2-\|z^{\ell-1}\|^2
 =\sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i^2\|h^i\|^2
 \le3\mathcal L\sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i^2d_i.
\end{equation}
Split the decrease sum on the left of
\eqref{eq:weighted-gap-identity} into three quarters and one quarter.
By \eqref{eq:aggregate-interval}, the latter quarter is at least
$(\|z^k\|^2-\|z^{\ell-1}\|^2)/(12\mathcal L)$.  Moving this lower bound to
the right proves \eqref{eq:window-reserve}.

For every integer $k\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window-prefix}
\begin{aligned}

 +\frac14\sum_{i=0}^k\omega_i^2d_i

\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window-aggregate-bound}
\begin{aligned}
 \|z^k\|^2
 &\le12\bigl(L_0+\sqrt{2HL_0D_0}\bigr)\bigl(1+\log(k+1)\bigr)\\
 &\quad\cdot\Bigl[3\bigl(L_0+\sqrt{2HL_0D_0}\bigr)
                 \|x^0-x^\star\|^2+\tfrac{512}{3}HD_0^3\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The initial gap and every shifted prefix gap satisfy

\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_0&\le3\bigl(L_0+\sqrt{2HL_0D_0}\bigr)\|x^0-x^\star\|^2,\\

 \quad(\ell\ge1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:reserve} with $\ell=0$.  The conventions
$\omega_{-1}=0$ and $z^{-1}=0$, together with $e^0=0$, reduce its right-hand

\[
 (x^0-x^\star)^Tz^k-\frac{\|z^k\|^2}{12\mathcal L}

Completing the square gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix-complete-square}
 (x^0-x^\star)^Tz^k-\frac{\|z^k\|^2}{12\mathcal L}
 =3\mathcal L\|x^0-x^\star\|^2
  -\frac{\|z^k-6\mathcal L(x^0-x^\star)\|^2}{12\mathcal L}
 \le3\mathcal L\|x^0-x^\star\|^2.
\end{equation}

We use the following elementary scalar inequality.  For
$\alpha\ge0$, $\beta>0$, and $t\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-young}
 \alpha t^{2/3}-\beta t\le\frac{4\alpha^3}{27\beta^2}.
\end{equation}
To verify it, write $t=y^3$ with $y\ge0$.  The derivative of
$\alpha y^2-\beta y^3$ is $y(2\alpha-3\beta y)$, so the maximum is attained
at $y=2\alpha/(3\beta)$ (or at $y=0$ if $\alpha=0$), and its value is the
right-hand side of \eqref{eq:weighted-young}.

\eqref{eq:weighted-young}, with

 =\frac14\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\omega_i^2d_i
  +\frac34\omega_k^2d_k
 \ge\frac14\sum_{i=0}^k\omega_i^2d_i.
\]

The bounds in \eqref{eq:weights} imply, for $i\ge1$,
\[
 \frac{\omega_i^3}{\omega_{i-1}^4}
 \le\frac{(i+1)^3}{((i+1)/2)^4}
 =\frac{16}{i+1}.
\]
For $k\ge1$, monotonicity of $u\mapsto1/u$ gives
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^k\frac1{i+1}
 \le\int_1^{k+1}\frac{du}{u}=\log(k+1);
\]
for $k=0$ both sides of the corresponding empty-sum estimate are zero.

 \le\frac{4C_{\mathrm{global}}(1+\log \ell)}{\ell^2}.
\]
This completes the proof.

\label{lem:window}
Fix an integer $\ell\ge1$, and let $k\ge \ell$ be the first index such that
\[

Then

 \omega_k^2\Delta_{k+1}

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The lower weight bound in \eqref{eq:weights} shows that the defining index
$k$ exists.  Since $\omega_{\ell-1}\ge1$ and the weight recurrence gives the
strict inequality $\omega_\ell<\omega_{\ell-1}+1\le2\omega_{\ell-1}$, we have
$k\ge \ell+1$.  By minimality, $\omega_{k-1}<2\omega_{\ell-1}$.  The increment
bounds in Lemma~\ref{lem:weights} therefore give
\[
 \omega_{\ell-1}+\frac{k-\ell}{2}
 \le\omega_{k-1}<2\omega_{\ell-1},
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad k-\ell<2\omega_{\ell-1}.
\]
For $\ell<i\le k$, we also have
$\omega_{i-1}\ge\omega_{\ell-1}$ and
$\omega_i\le\omega_{i-1}+1\le2\omega_{i-1}$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window-ratio-sum}
 \sum_{i=\ell+1}^k\frac{\omega_i^3}{\omega_{i-1}^4}
 \le\sum_{i=\ell+1}^k\frac{8}{\omega_{i-1}}
 \le\frac{8(k-\ell)}{\omega_{\ell-1}}
 \le16.
\end{equation}

We first bound the aggregate boundary term in Lemma~\ref{lem:reserve}.
The Pythagorean identity in \eqref{eq:aggregate-interval} shows that
$\|z^k\|\ge\|z^{\ell-1}\|$.  Put
$t=\|z^k\|-\|z^{\ell-1}\|\ge0$.  The triangle inequality gives
$\|z^k-z^{\ell-1}\|\le2\|z^{\ell-1}\|+t$, while
\[
 \|z^k\|^2-\|z^{\ell-1}\|^2
 =2\|z^{\ell-1}\|t+t^2.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
 &(x^0-x^\star)^T(z^k-z^{\ell-1})
 -\frac{\|z^k\|^2-\|z^{\ell-1}\|^2}{12\mathcal L}\\
 &\quad\le
 2\|x^0-x^\star\|\,\|z^{\ell-1}\|
 +\left(\|x^0-x^\star\|-\frac{\|z^{\ell-1}\|}{6\mathcal L}\right)t
 -\frac{t^2}{12\mathcal L}\\
 &\quad\le
 2\|x^0-x^\star\|\,\|z^{\ell-1}\|
 +3\mathcal L\|x^0-x^\star\|^2.
\end{align*}
For the last line, the maximum over $t\ge0$ of the last two terms is
\[
 3\mathcal L
 \max\!\left\{\|x^0-x^\star\|-
       \frac{\|z^{\ell-1}\|}{6\mathcal L},0\right\}^2
 \le3\mathcal L\|x^0-x^\star\|^2.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:window-aggregate-bound} with $k=\ell-1$ and
$3\mathcal L\|x^0-x^\star\|^2\le C_{\mathrm{global}}$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window-boundary}
\begin{aligned}
 &(x^0-x^\star)^T(z^k-z^{\ell-1})
 -\frac{\|z^k\|^2-\|z^{\ell-1}\|^2}{12\mathcal L}\\
 &\qquad\le
 4C_{\mathrm{global}}\sqrt{1+\log \ell}+C_{\mathrm{global}}
 \le5C_{\mathrm{global}}\sqrt{1+\log \ell}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The bound for $e^\ell$ uses $d_{\ell-1}$, the decrease immediately
before the current interval.  Since
$d_{\ell-1}\le\Delta_{\ell-1}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:prefix},
$\omega_\ell\le \ell+1\le2\ell$, and \eqref{eq:defect-budget} give
\begin{align*}
 D_0\omega_\ell\|e^\ell\|
 &\le2D_0(18H)^{1/3}\ell
       \left(\frac{4C_{\mathrm{global}}(1+\log \ell)}{\ell^2}\right)^{2/3}\\
 &=2\,4^{2/3}D_0(18H)^{1/3}C_{\mathrm{global}}^{2/3}
       \left(\frac{(1+\log \ell)^2}{\ell}\right)^{1/3}.
\end{align*}
The definition of $C_{\mathrm{global}}$ implies
\[
 \bigl[D_0(18H)^{1/3}\bigr]^3
 =18HD_0^3\le\frac{27}{256}C_{\mathrm{global}}.
\]
The function $v\mapsto(1+\log v)^2/v$ on $[1,\infty)$ has derivative
$(1+\log v)(1-\log v)/v^2$, and hence its maximum is $4/e$, attained at
$v=e$.  Substitution gives the explicit bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-window-defect}
 D_0\omega_\ell\|e^\ell\|
 \le(54/e)^{1/3}C_{\mathrm{global}}<4C_{\mathrm{global}}.
\end{equation}

Every remaining correction is paid for inside the current window.  For
$\ell<i\le k$, the same scalar inequality \eqref{eq:weighted-young} used in
the prefix proof gives
\[
 D_0\omega_i\|e^i\|-\frac12\omega_{i-1}^2d_{i-1}
 \le\frac{32}{3}HD_0^3
       \frac{\omega_i^3}{\omega_{i-1}^4}.
\]
Summing and using \eqref{eq:window-ratio-sum} bounds the unabsorbed part by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:internal-window-defects}
 \frac{32}{3}HD_0^3
 \sum_{i=\ell+1}^k\frac{\omega_i^3}{\omega_{i-1}^4}
 \le\frac{512}{3}HD_0^3\le C_{\mathrm{global}}.
\end{equation}
The absorbed decreases are precisely those indexed from $\ell$ through

 \frac34\sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i^2d_i
 -\frac12\sum_{i=\ell+1}^k\omega_{i-1}^2d_{i-1}
 =\frac14\sum_{i=\ell}^{k-1}\omega_i^2d_i
  +\frac34\omega_k^2d_k
 \ge\frac14\sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i^2d_i.

 5C_{\mathrm{global}}\sqrt{1+\log \ell}+4C_{\mathrm{global}}+C_{\mathrm{global}}
 \le10C_{\mathrm{global}}\sqrt{1+\log \ell}
 \le16C_{\mathrm{global}}\sqrt{1+\log \ell}.
\]
Substitution in \eqref{eq:window-reserve} proves
\eqref{eq:window-gap-recurrence}.

\label{thm:main}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:convex}--\ref{ass:hessian},
Algorithm~\ref{alg:cubic}, with exact model solves and projections,

 U_j=\omega_{k_j-1}^2\Delta_{k_j}.
\]
These intervals cover all positive indices because the weights
increase without bound.

For one such interval, write $\ell=k_j$ and $k=k_{j+1}-1$.
Monotonicity of the weights and telescoping of the decreases give
\[
 \sum_{i=\ell}^k\omega_i^2d_i
 \ge\omega_\ell^2\sum_{i=\ell}^k d_i
 =\omega_\ell^2(\Delta_\ell-\Delta_{k+1}).
\]
Substituting this into \eqref{eq:window-gap-recurrence} yields
\[
 \left(\omega_k^2-\tfrac14\omega_\ell^2\right)\Delta_{k+1}
 \le\left(\omega_{\ell-1}^2-\tfrac14\omega_\ell^2\right)\Delta_\ell
            +16C_{\mathrm{global}}\sqrt{1+\log \ell}.
\]
For the following algebra only, put
\[
 \alpha=\frac{\omega_\ell^2}{\omega_{\ell-1}^2},\qquad
 \beta=\frac{\omega_k^2}{\omega_{\ell-1}^2}.
\]
The weight increment bound and $\omega_{\ell-1}\ge1$ give
$1\le \alpha<4$, while the definition of $k$ gives $\beta\ge4$.
As $U_{j+1}=\omega_k^2\Delta_{k+1}$, the preceding inequality is
\[
 U_{j+1}\le
 \frac{1-\alpha/4}{1-\alpha/(4\beta)}U_j
 +\frac{16C_{\mathrm{global}}\sqrt{1+\log k_j}}{1-\alpha/(4\beta)}.
\]
Both coefficients can be bounded independently of the interval.
Indeed,
\[
 \frac{1-\alpha/4}{1-\alpha/(4\beta)}
 \le\frac{1-\alpha/4}{1-\alpha/16}\le\frac45,
 \qquad
 \frac1{1-\alpha/(4\beta)}\le\frac43.
\]
For the second inequality in the first chain, the rational function
decreases for $1\le \alpha<4$, so its maximum is attained at $\alpha=1$.
We have therefore obtained the contraction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-contraction}
 U_{j+1}\le\frac45U_j+22C_{\mathrm{global}}\sqrt{1+\log k_j}.
\end{equation}

The initial value satisfies
$U_0=\Delta_1\le\Delta_0\le C_{\mathrm{global}}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:prefix}.
Induction in \eqref{eq:endpoint-contraction}, using $k_{j+1}>k_j$,
gives
\[
 U_j\le110C_{\mathrm{global}}\sqrt{1+\log k_j}.
\]
The constant $110$ is chosen so that $(4/5)110+22=110$.
For an arbitrary $K\ge1$, choose the interval containing it,
$k_j\le K<k_{j+1}$. By monotonicity, $\Delta_K\le\Delta_{k_j}$.
The defining minimality of $k_{j+1}-1$ gives
$\omega_{K-1}<2\omega_{k_j-1}$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:weights} gives $\omega_{K-1}\ge(K+1)/2$.
Combining these relations,
\begin{align*}
 \Delta_K
 &\le\frac{U_j}{\omega_{k_j-1}^2}
 \le\frac{16U_j}{(K+1)^2}\\
 &\le\frac{1760C_{\mathrm{global}}\sqrt{1+\log(K+1)}}{(K+1)^2}.
\end{align*}
For $K=0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:prefix} gives $\Delta_0\le C_{\mathrm{global}}$.
Thus the uniform factor $2048$ in \eqref{eq:explicit-rate} covers
all indices. At any terminating index the gap becomes zero, so
constant extension preserves the estimate.

Finally, $\|x^0-x^\star\|\le D_0$ and the elementary inequality
$\sqrt{ab}\le(a+b)/2$ give
\[
 \mathcal L D_0^2
 =L_0D_0^2+\sqrt{2(L_0D_0^2)(HD_0^3)}
 \le\tfrac32L_0D_0^2+HD_0^3.
\]
Absorbing the numerical constants proves \eqref{eq:main-rate}.

$\nabla^2f(x)\succeq(\mu_\star/2)I$. Since
$\nabla f(x^\star)=0$, integrating along the segment from $x^\star$

 f(x)\ge f(x^\star)+\tfrac14\mu_\star\|x-x^\star\|^2.

it a cluster point with that same property. The Hessian bound now
follows from entry into the ball chosen above.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{lem:local-projection}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:convex}--\ref{ass:hessian} and~\ref{ass:local},
let $P^k$ be the orthogonal
projector onto $\mathcal S^k$ for the inner product $v^T\nabla^2f(x^\star)w$.
There exists a finite constant $C_1$, depending on the problem
and the fixed coefficient $H$ but independent of $k$ and the choice
of search space, such that, for all sufficiently large $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-projection}
 \begin{aligned}
 x^{k+1}-x^\star&=(I-P^k)(x^k-x^\star)+w^k,\\
 w^k&\in\mathcal S^k,\qquad
 \|w^k\|_\star\le C_1\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^2.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Consequently, using the distance defined just above the lemma,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-distance-bound}
 \|x^{k+1}-x^\star\|_\star\le\operatorname{dist}_\star(x^k-x^\star,\mathcal S^k)+C_1\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^2.
\end{equation}

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Write $u^k=x^k-x^\star$. The initial sublevel is convex and
contains both $x^k$ and $x^\star$. Assumption~\ref{ass:hessian}
therefore applies along their segment. In particular,
\begin{align}
 \|\nabla f(x^k)-\nabla^2f(x^\star)u^k\|
 &\le\int_0^1 2Ht\|u^k\|^2\,dt
 =H\|u^k\|^2,\label{eq:local-gradient-expansion}\\
 \|\nabla^2f(x^k)-\nabla^2f(x^\star)\|&\le2H\|u^k\|.
 \label{eq:local-hessian-expansion}
\end{align}
The first bound also gives
$\|\nabla f(x^k)\|\le L_\star\|u^k\|+H\|u^k\|^2=O(\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star)$.

Since $s^k\in\mathcal S^k$, taking its inner product with the
projected stationarity equation~\eqref{eq:model-stationarity}
gives
\[
 (s^k)^T\nabla^2f(x^k)s^k+H\|s^k\|^3
 =-\nabla f(x^k)^Ts^k.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-entry} and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 \tfrac12\mu_\star\|s^k\|^2
 \le\|\nabla f(x^k)\|\|s^k\|,
 \qquad
 \|s^k\|\le\frac{2}{\mu_\star}\|\nabla f(x^k)\|=O(\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star).
\]
The last inequality also holds when $s^k=0$.

Define the stationarity remainder
\[
 r^k=\nabla^2f(x^\star)u^k-\nabla f(x^k)
       +(\nabla^2f(x^\star)-\nabla^2f(x^k))s^k-H\|s^k\|s^k.
\]
The two Taylor bounds and the step-length estimate yield
\[
 \|r^k\|
 \le H\|u^k\|^2+2H\|u^k\|\|s^k\|+H\|s^k\|^2
 =O(\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^2).
\]
For every $v\in\mathcal S^k$, equation~\eqref{eq:model-stationarity}
now gives
\[
 v^T\nabla^2f(x^\star)(u^k+s^k)=v^Tr^k.
\]
By the defining property of the projector orthogonal in the norm $\|\cdot\|_\star$,
$P^k(u^k+s^k)=P^k[\nabla^2f(x^\star)]^{-1}r^k$.
Also $(I-P^k)s^k=0$, because $s^k$ lies in the search space.
Together these identities prove~\eqref{eq:local-projection} with
$w^k=P^k[\nabla^2f(x^\star)]^{-1}r^k$. Orthogonal projection does not increase
the norm $\|\cdot\|_\star$, so
\[
 \|w^k\|_\star\le\|[\nabla^2f(x^\star)]^{-1}r^k\|_\star
 \le \mu_\star^{-1/2}\|r^k\|=O(\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^2).
\]
All estimates are uniform for sufficiently large $k$, independently
of the dimension of $\mathcal S^k$ or the angles between its defining directions.
Finally, $(I-P^k)u^k$ is the error of the best approximation from
$\mathcal S^k$ in the norm $\|\cdot\|_\star$. The triangle inequality in
\eqref{eq:local-projection} gives~\eqref{eq:local-distance-bound}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:local}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:convex}, \ref{ass:level},
\ref{ass:hessian}, and~\ref{ass:local}, the iterates converge to $x^\star$. Unless the method terminates
finitely, there is a finite constant $C$ such that, eventually,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-rate}
 \|x^{k+1}-x^\star\|_\star\le\frac{\kappa_\star-1}{\kappa_\star+1} \|x^k-x^\star\|_\star+C\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^2.
\end{equation}
For every $q\in(\frac{\kappa_\star-1}{\kappa_\star+1},1)$, this gives
$\|x^{k+1}-x^\star\|_\star\le q\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star$ for all sufficiently large $k$. In the absence
of finite termination, the objective gaps also satisfy
\[
 \limsup_{k\to\infty}
 \frac{f(x^{k+1})-f_\star}{f(x^k)-f_\star}
 \le\left(\frac{\kappa_\star-1}{\kappa_\star+1}\right)^2.
\]
No strong-convexity parameter, restart, or test for entering a local
phase is supplied to the algorithm.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Convergence follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:local-entry}.
Because $\nabla f(x^k)\in\mathcal S^k$, we can approximate
$u^k=x^k-x^\star$ by
$2\nabla f(x^k)/(L_\star+\mu_\star)$ inside that space.
The approximation error is
\[
 u^k-\frac{2\nabla f(x^k)}{L_\star+\mu_\star}
 =\left(I-\frac{2\nabla^2f(x^\star)}{L_\star+\mu_\star}\right)u^k
   +\frac{2(\nabla^2f(x^\star)u^k-\nabla f(x^k))}{L_\star+\mu_\star}.
\]
The second term has size $O(\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^2)$
in the norm $\|\cdot\|_\star$, by
\eqref{eq:local-gradient-expansion} and equivalence of the norms.
For the first term, diagonalize $\nabla^2f(x^\star)$. A component corresponding to
an eigenvalue $\lambda\in[\mu_\star,L_\star]$ is multiplied by
$1-2\lambda/(L_\star+\mu_\star)$, whose absolute value is at most
$\frac{\kappa_\star-1}{\kappa_\star+1}$. Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{dist}_\star(u^k,\mathcal S^k)
 &\le\left\|u^k-\frac{2\nabla f(x^k)}{L_\star+\mu_\star}\right\|_\star\\
 &\le\frac{\kappa_\star-1}{\kappa_\star+1} \|x^k-x^\star\|_\star+O(\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^2).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Combining this with Lemma~\ref{lem:local-projection} proves
\eqref{eq:local-rate}. Given $q>\frac{\kappa_\star-1}{\kappa_\star+1}$, convergence ensures
$C\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star\le q-\frac{\kappa_\star-1}{\kappa_\star+1}$ eventually; dividing
\eqref{eq:local-rate} by $\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star$ then gives the claimed contraction.

Finally, the Hessian Lipschitz bound on the segment from $x^\star$
to $x^k$ gives the two-sided Taylor estimate
\[
 \left|f(x^k)-f_\star-\tfrac12\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^2\right|
 \le\tfrac H3\|x^k-x^\star\|^3=O(\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^3).
\]
Thus the objective gap is $\tfrac12\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^2(1+O(\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star))$.
Substituting~\eqref{eq:local-rate} into the ratio of consecutive
gaps proves the last assertion.

\label{thm:local-pl}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:convex}--\ref{ass:hessian}, consider
a nonstationary run of the fixed-coefficient model steps, with
search spaces containing the current gradient and with restarts
permitted. Their decrease bounds are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pl-two-budgets}
 \begin{aligned}
 f(x^k)-f(x^{k+1})&\ge
 \frac{\|\nabla f(x^k)\|^2}{3(L_0+\sqrt{2HL_0D_0})},\\
 f(x^k)-f(x^{k+1})&\ge
 \frac{2H}{3}\|x^{k+1}-x^k\|^3.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For an infinite run, suppose an accumulation point $\bar x$ has a
neighborhood on which, for some $r,\mu>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-pl-condition}
 \|\nabla f(x)\|^2\ge2\mu(f(x)-f_\star)
 \quad\text{for all }x\in\mathcal C_0\cap B(\bar x,r).
\end{equation}
Decrease $\mu$ if necessary so that
$0<2\mu<3(L_0+\sqrt{2HL_0D_0})$, and put
\[
 q=1-\frac{2\mu}{3(L_0+\sqrt{2HL_0D_0})}\in(0,1).
\]

for some $k_0$ and every $k\ge k_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-pl-conclusions}
 \begin{aligned}
 f(x^{k+1})-f_\star&\le q(f(x^k)-f_\star),\\
 \sum_{i=k}^\infty\|x^{i+1}-x^i\|
 &\le\frac{(f(x^k)-f_\star)^{1/3}}
              {(2H/3)^{1/3}(1-q^{1/3})},\\
 \|x^k-\bar x\|
 &\le\frac{(f(x^{k_0})-f_\star)^{1/3}}
              {(2H/3)^{1/3}(1-q^{1/3})}\,q^{(k-k_0)/3}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus the gaps converge Q-linearly and the iterates converge
R-linearly to $\bar x$. Neither uniqueness of the minimizer nor a
positive-definite Hessian is required. The PL constant is used only
in the analysis. Finite termination at a stationary point is the
immediate alternative.

\label{thm:oracle-lower-bound}
 
A vector-oracle call returns either $\nabla f(x)$ or
$\nabla^2f(x)v$. Consider algorithms starting at $0$ for which every

evaluation counts, including the initial gradient and any inner calls.

and a unique minimizer $x^\star$, such that

 0\preceq\nabla^2f(x)\preceq3I\qquad(x\in\mathbb R^{M+2}).

\[
 L_0=\max_{x\in\mathcal C_0}\|\nabla^2f(x)\|=1,
 \qquad D_0=\max_{x\in\mathcal C_0}\|x-x^\star\|=1.
\]

 f(x)-f_\star\ge\frac{1}{2\cosh^2(2)(M+1)^2},
 \qquad f_\star=f(x^\star).
\]

number of gradient and HVP calls per outer iteration cannot yield

A vector-oracle call returns either $\nabla f(x)$ or
$\nabla^2f(x)v$. Consider algorithms starting at $0$ for which every
query point, HVP input vector, and output point belongs to the linear
span of previously returned vectors; before the first call this span
is $\{0\}$. Scalar coefficients may depend arbitrarily on the observed
information. Memory, scalar computations, and function-value queries
within the available span are unrestricted. Every gradient or HVP
evaluation counts, including the initial gradient and any inner calls.

For every integer call budget $M\ge1$, there exists a convex nonquadratic
$C^\infty$ function $f:\mathbb R^{M+2}\to\mathbb R$ with compact sublevel sets
and a unique minimizer $x^\star$, such that
\[
 0\preceq\nabla^2f(x)\preceq3I\qquad(x\in\mathbb R^{M+2}).
\]
Its smallest global Hessian Lipschitz constant is exactly $2$,
so $H=1$ is a valid supplied bound. Its third derivative is globally
$32$-Lipschitz in the induced multilinear norm. For
$\mathcal C_0=\{x:f(x)\le f(0)\}$, the level-set constants are
\[
 L_0=\max_{x\in\mathcal C_0}\|\nabla^2f(x)\|=1,
 \qquad D_0=\max_{x\in\mathcal C_0}\|x-x^\star\|=1.
\]
On this single function, every algorithm in the stated class and
every output $x$ obtained after at most $M$ vector-oracle calls satisfy
\[
 f(x)-f_\star\ge\frac{1}{2\cosh^2(2)(M+1)^2},
 \qquad f_\star=f(x^\star).
\]
Monotonicity is not required. In particular, a uniformly bounded
number of gradient and HVP calls per outer iteration cannot yield
a uniform dimension-independent $O(k^{-3})$ guarantee with constants

{Step 2: bound the best point in the available polynomial space.}

\[
 \mathcal K_0=\{0\},\qquad
 \mathcal K_j=\operatorname{span}\{Au^\star,A^2u^\star,\ldots,A^ju^\star\}

degree at most $M$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-polynomial-error}
 u-u^\star=-p(A)u^\star,\qquad p(0)=1.
\end{equation}
Indeed, writing $u=\sum_{j=1}^M a_jA^ju^\star$ gives
$p(t)=1-\sum_{j=1}^M a_jt^j$.

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 1&=\left(\sum_{i=0}^M\ell_i(0)p(\lambda_i)\right)^2\\
  &\le\left(\sum_{i=0}^M\frac{\ell_i(0)^2}{w_i}\right)
        \left(\sum_{i=0}^M w_ip(\lambda_i)^2\right)
   =W_M^2\sum_{i=0}^M w_ip(\lambda_i)^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Consequently, \eqref{eq:lower-polynomial-error} and the choice
of $u^\star$ imply
\[
 q(u)=\frac\mu2\sum_{i=0}^M w_ip(\lambda_i)^2
      \ge\frac{\mu}{2W_M^2}.
\]

It remains to bound $W_M$ by a constant independent of $M$.

$\mathsf T_M(\cos\theta)=\cos(M\theta)$. At the chosen nodes,
\[
 \mathsf T_M\left(\frac{2\lambda_i-1-\mu}{1-\mu}\right)=(-1)^i.
\]
The decreasing order of the nodes gives
$\operatorname{sign}\ell_i(0)=(-1)^{M+i}$: the numerator has
$M$ negative factors and the denominator has $i$ negative factors.

its parity therefore gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}

 &=\mathsf T_M\left(\frac{1+\mu}{1-\mu}\right)\\
 &=\cosh\left(M\log\frac{1+\sqrt\mu}{1-\sqrt\mu}\right)
  =\cosh\left(M\log(1+2/M)\right)

 \end{aligned}
\]

\end{equation}

inside it is selected.

 \le-b-\tfrac56\widehat H\|s\|^3.
\]
Since the current gradient belongs to the space and is nonzero,
$s=0$ cannot satisfy the criterion. Along the segment, the trial
cubic model therefore obeys, for $0<t\le1$,
\[
 m_x(ts)\le-t(1-t/2)b
       -\widehat Ht(5/6-t^2/3)\|s\|^3<0.
\]
When $\widehat H$ is at least the true localized half-Lipschitz
constant, the first-exit argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}
applies to this strict inequality. The segment stays in the initial
sublevel set, and both Taylor certificates hold. At an accepted trial,
the function certificate gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inexact-decrease}
 f(x)-f(x+s)\ge-m_x(s)
 \ge\tfrac12b+\tfrac12\widehat H\|s\|^3.
\end{equation}
Thus the same backtracking thresholds and parameter bounds apply,
and accepted trials remain monotone even below the true constant.

The gradient Taylor certificate and \eqref{eq:inexact-model} imply
\begin{align*}
 \|\Pi_{\mathcal S^k}\nabla f(x+s)\|
 &\le\tfrac{13}{6}\widehat H\|s\|^2\\
 &\le\tfrac{13}{6}\,2^{2/3}\widehat H^{1/3}
              (f(x)-f(x+s))^{2/3}.
\end{align*}
The fixed-coefficient version uses the corresponding Taylor bound.
For the gradient decrease bound, positive semidefiniteness and
$\nabla^2 f(x)\preceq L_0I$ give
\[
 \|\nabla f(x)\|^2
 \le3L_0 b+\tfrac{37}{12}\widehat H^2\|s\|^4.
\]
Indeed, the projected stationarity equation expresses the gradient
as the sum of $-\Pi_{\mathcal S^k}\nabla^2 f(x)s$,
$-\widehat H\|s\|s$, and $r$; the square of its norm is at most
three times the sum of their squared norms.
The same inner-product identity also gives
\[
 \widehat H\|s\|
 \le\sqrt{\tfrac65\widehat H\|\nabla f(x)\|}
 \le\sqrt{\tfrac65\widehat H L_0D_0}.
\]
Combining these estimates with \eqref{eq:inexact-decrease} yields
\[
 \|\nabla f(x)\|^2
 \le7\left(L_0+\sqrt{\tfrac65\widehat H L_0D_0}\right)
              (f(x)-f(x+s)).
\]
The proofs of Lemmas~\ref{lem:reserve}--\ref{lem:window} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} use only these gradient and correction-error bounds.
Substituting the displayed constants gives the same global order;
in the adaptive case use the upper bound on $H_k$.
The same two bounds also give \eqref{eq:epoch-bound} with changed
constants. Increasing the constant in the epoch induction accordingly
preserves the inverse-square rate.

Locally, $\nabla^2f(x)\succeq\mu I$ for some $\mu>0$, and the inner-product identity
gives $\|s\|\le\|\nabla f(x)\|/\mu=O(\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star)$.
For bounded $H_k$, both the model regularization gradient and $r$
are $O(\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^2)$. The local projected Newton decomposition thus retains
an $O(\|x^k-x^\star\|_\star^2)$ remainder in the search space. The proofs of Lemma~\ref{lem:local-projection} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:local} therefore apply unchanged. The displayed
decrease bounds also give the stated PL factor. The step-length
coefficient is $H/2>0$ for fixed coefficients, or $H_{\min}/2>0$
for the adaptive tail under the lower-bound condition in
Proposition~\ref{prop:inexact-model}. Apply the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-pl} to obtain neighborhood invariance,
finite trajectory length, and iterate convergence with the stated
constants, also across restarts.

Finally, for a nonzero current gradient the reduced cubic model is
coercive and strictly convex, with a unique nonzero minimizer.
Along any inner sequence converging to that minimizer, the residual
tends to zero, whereas the right side of \eqref{eq:inexact-model}
tends to a positive number. The criterion is therefore met after
finitely many inner iterations. Its evaluation needs only reduced
matrix-vector arithmetic after the original at most three HVPs
(four with the optional memory direction).

\label{prop:cubic-inner-work}
For a nonstationary trial, retain the notation $Q^k,B^k,\widehat H$
of this appendix. Choose a computable $L\ge\|B^k\|$, for example
$L=\operatorname{tr}B^k$, and define
\[
 a(\lambda)=-(B^k+\lambda I)^{-1}(Q^k)^T\nabla f(x^k),\qquad
 \psi(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{\widehat H\|a(\lambda)\|},
 \qquad \lambda>0.
\]
The unique root of $\psi(\lambda)=1$ lies in $[\ell,U]$, where
\[
 U=\sqrt{\widehat H\|\nabla f(x^k)\|},\qquad
 \ell=\frac{U^2}{L+U}.
\]
Let $J_{\mathrm{inner}}$ denote the number of inner bisections,
which is separate from the accepted-step count $K$. If $L>0$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic-inner-count}
 J_{\mathrm{inner}}=\max\left\{0,\left\lceil
 \log_2\!\left(\frac{2\log(1+L/U)}{\log(7/6)}\right)
 \right\rceil\right\}.
\end{equation}
Starting with $[\ell,U]$, perform $J_{\mathrm{inner}}$ geometric bisections: evaluate
$\psi$ at the geometric mean of the endpoints, replace the lower
endpoint when this value is at most one, and otherwise replace the
upper endpoint. Let $v$ be the final upper endpoint. Then
$s=Q^k a(v)$ satisfies \eqref{eq:inexact-model} with $H_k=\widehat H$.
The procedure uses at most $J_{\mathrm{inner}}+1$ reduced linear solves, including a
final evaluation at $v$, and no additional objective-oracle calls.
If $L=0$, the bracket collapses to $\ell=U$ and one reduced solve
gives the exact model minimizer.

Since the current gradient belongs to the search space,
$\|(Q^k)^T\nabla f(x^k)\|=\|\nabla f(x^k)\|>0$.
The spectral bounds $0\preceq B^k\preceq LI$ imply
\[
 \frac{\|\nabla f(x^k)\|}{L+\lambda}
 \le\|a(\lambda)\|
 \le\frac{\|\nabla f(x^k)\|}{\lambda}.
\]
The function $\psi$ is strictly increasing. The upper norm bound
gives $\psi(U)\ge1$, while the lower bound and $\ell\le U$ give
\[
 \psi(\ell)\le\frac{\ell(L+\ell)}{U^2}
              \le\frac{\ell(L+U)}{U^2}=1.
\]
For any $0<u\le v$, diagonalizing the positive semidefinite matrix
$B^k$ gives
\[
 1\le\frac{\|a(u)\|}{\|a(v)\|}\le\frac vu,
 \qquad
 \frac{\psi(v)}{\psi(u)}\le\left(\frac vu\right)^2.
\]
Every bisection preserves the root bracket and halves its logarithmic
width. If $[u,v]$ is the final interval, then
\[
 1\le\psi(v)\le(v/u)^2
 =\exp\!\left(2^{1-J_{\mathrm{inner}}}\log(U/\ell)\right)\le\frac76,
\]
where $\log(U/\ell)=\log(1+L/U)$ and
\eqref{eq:cubic-inner-count} gives the last inequality.
For $s=Q^k a(v)$, the projected model gradient is exactly
$(\widehat H\|s\|-v)s$. Therefore its norm is
\[
 (\psi(v)-1)\widehat H\|s\|^2
 \le\frac{\widehat H}{6}\|s\|^2,
\]
as required. Each midpoint and the final endpoint require at most
one reduced solve; the initial bracket needs no numerical root test.
If $L=0$, then $B^k=0$ and $U=\ell$ solves the secular equation exactly.
