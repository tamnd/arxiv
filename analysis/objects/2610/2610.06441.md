If $H$ is bounded and continuous and the distortion of~$u$ is bounded on the set where $|u|$ is
    large, then $u$ is locally H{\"o}lder continuous.  If moreover $H$ is locally Lipschitz, then
    $u$ is of class~$1$ and its derivative is locally H{\"o}lder continuous.

\label{mr:notation}
    In this section we fix notation, define weak solutions and the distortion, and prove the
    estimate~\ref{lem:K} on which the argument rests. Throughout, $n \ge 2$ is an integer and
    $U = \mathbf{U}(0,1) \subseteq \mathbf{R}^n$. The notation follows~\cite[pp.~669--671]{Fed69}. In particular
    $\mathscr{P}$ denotes the set of positive integers, $\mathbf{U}(a,r)$ and $\mathbf{B}(a,r)$ are the open and the
    closed ball, $\mathscr{L}^n$ and $\mathscr{H}^k$ are the Lebesgue and the Hausdorff measure, $\mu  \mathop{ \rule[0pt]{.5pt}{6pt} \rule[0pt]{4pt}{0.5pt} }\nolimits  A$
    is the restriction of $\mu$ to~$A$, and $\|T\|$ is the variation measure of a linear
    functional~$T$ on a space of continuous maps with compact support; see~\ref{mr:push}. The
    standard bases of $\mathbf{R}^n$ and $\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ are $e_1, \ldots, e_n$ and $e_1, \ldots, e_{n+1}$.

    For linear maps $A$ and $B$ between Euclidean spaces with the same domain
    and the same codomain we set
    \begin{displaymath}
        A \bullet B = \operatorname{trace} (A^* \circ B) \,, \quad
        |A| = (A \bullet A)^{1/2} \,, \quad
        \|A\| = \sup \{ |A(v)| : |v| \le 1 \} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    Then $\|A\| \le |A|$. If $A$ and $B$ are linear maps between Euclidean
    spaces and the domain of $A$ is the codomain of~$B$, then $|A \circ B|
    \le \|A\| |B|$.

    A vector space and its dual are not identified. For $\omega \in
    \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^k,\mathbf{R})$ and $b \in \mathbf{R}^N$ let $\omega \cdot b \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^k,\mathbf{R}^N)$
    be given by $(\omega \cdot b)(w) = \omega(w) \, b$ for $w \in \mathbf{R}^k$; this is
    Allard's convention, cf.~\cite[2.2, 2.3]{All72}. If $o_1, \ldots, o_k$ is an
    orthonormal basis of $\mathbf{R}^k$ and $Z \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^k,\mathbf{R}^N)$, then
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:notation:allard}
        (\omega \cdot b) \bullet Z
        = {\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}} \omega(o_i) \, b \bullet Z(o_i)
        = {\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}} \omega(o_i) \, Z^*(b) \bullet o_i
        = \omega \bigl( Z^*(b) \bigr) \,.
    \end{equation}
    If $\varphi$ is a real valued function of class~$1$ on an open subset
    of~$\mathbf{R}^k$, then $\operatorname{grad} \varphi(y) \in \mathbf{R}^k$ is defined by
    $\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}\varphi(y)w = \operatorname{grad} \varphi(y) \bullet w$ for $w \in \mathbf{R}^k$. If $g$ takes
    values in $\mathbf{R}^N$ and has class~$1$, then
    $\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}(\varphi g) = \varphi \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g + \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}\varphi \cdot g$, and
    by~\eqref{eq:notation:allard}
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:notation:rank-one}
        \bigl( \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}\varphi(y) \cdot b \bigr) \bullet Z = b \bullet Z \bigl(
        \operatorname{grad} \varphi(y) \bigr)
        \quad \text{for $b \in \mathbf{R}^N$ and $Z \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^k,\mathbf{R}^N)$} \,.
    \end{equation}
    We write $\mathscr{C}^{1}_c(\mathbf{R}^k,\mathbf{R}^N)$ for the vector space of maps
    $g : \mathbf{R}^k \to \mathbf{R}^N$ of class~$1$ with compact support, normed by
    $\sup \operatorname{im} (|g| + |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g|)$.  If $X$ is a Euclidean space and $T \subseteq X$
    is a linear subspace, then we write ${T}_\natural$ for the orthogonal
    projection onto~$T$, cf.~\cite[T.1(9)]{Alm00}. In particular, if $\nu \in X$
    and $|\nu| = 1$, then $(\mathbf{R} \nu)_\natural \in \operatorname{Hom}(X,X)$ denotes the
    orthogonal projection onto $\mathbf{R} \nu$, that is,
    $(\mathbf{R} \nu)_\natural(x) = (x \bullet \nu) \nu$ for $x \in X$.

    For $A \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^n,\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ we have
    $\|{\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{n}} A\| = | {\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{n}} A | = |A e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge A e_n|$,
    the norm on~${\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{n}} \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ being that
    of~\cite[1.7.5]{Fed69}. By~\cite[1.7.5]{Fed69},
    $| {\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{n}} A |^2 = \det (A^* \circ A)$; thus, $| {\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{n}} A |$ is the $n$
    dimensional Jacobian of $A$ of~\cite[3.2.1]{Fed69}. Moreover
    $| {\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{n}} A | = |A v_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge A v_n|$ for every orthonormal
    basis $v_1, \ldots, v_n$ of~$\mathbf{R}^n$. Let
    $\star : {\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{p}} \mathbf{R}^{n+1} \to {\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{n+1-p}} \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ be the Hodge star
    operator denoted $*$ in~\cite[1.7.8]{Fed69}, defined with respect to the
    unit $(n+1)$ vector $e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{n+1}$; the symbol $*$ is
    reserved for adjoints.  Then $\star$ maps each element
    $e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_p}$ of the standard orthonormal basis to
    the complementary one, up to the sign, and thus preserves the inner
    product. Moreover
    $\xi \wedge \star \eta = (\xi \bullet \eta) \, e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge
    e_{n+1}$ for $\xi, \eta \in {\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{p}} \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$, and
    $\star \star \xi = (-1)^{p(n+1-p)} \xi$. For $\zeta \in {\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{n}} \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$
    and $w \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$, the choice $\eta = (-1)^n \star w$ gives
    $\star \eta = w$. Since $\star$ preserves the inner product,
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:notation:hodge}
        \zeta \wedge w = (-1)^n (\zeta \bullet \star w) \, e_1 \wedge \cdots
        \wedge e_{n+1} = (w \bullet \star \zeta) \, e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge
        e_{n+1} \,.
    \end{equation}
    Define
    \begin{displaymath}
        \mathbf{J} (A) = \star (A e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge A e_n)
        \quad \text{for $A \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^n,\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    Then $|\mathbf{J} (A)| = | {\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{n}} A |$ and $\mathbf{J} (A) \bullet A(v) = 0$ for
    $v \in \mathbf{R}^n$. Indeed, $\star$ preserves the inner product, and the $n+1$
    vectors $A e_1, \ldots, A e_n, A(v)$ lie in $\operatorname{im} A$, which has dimension at
    most~$n$, so that $A e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge A e_n \wedge A(v) = 0$; now
    \eqref{eq:notation:hodge} applies.  If $A \ne 0$, we set
    \begin{displaymath}
        Y(A) = |A|^{-2} A \circ A^* \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1}) \,.
    \end{displaymath}

    For $X \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^d,\mathbf{R}^d)$ and $k \in \{1, \ldots, d\}$ let
    \begin{displaymath}
        \sigma_k(X) = \sup \bigl\{ \inf \{ |X(w)| : w \in W,\ |w| = 1 \}
        : W \in \mathbf{G}(d,k) \bigr\} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    By the Courant--Fischer principle~\cite[Problem~III.6.1]{Bha97}, $\sigma_k(X)$ is
    the $k$-th largest singular value of~$X$, see~\cite[section~I.2]{Bha97}, and it is
    the $k$-th largest eigenvalue of $X$ if $X$ is symmetric and non-negative. In particular $\sigma_1(X) = \|X\|$.

\label{mr:separable}
    Suppose $k, N \in \mathscr{P}$. There are
    $g_1, g_2, \ldots \in \mathscr{C}^{1}_c(\mathbf{R}^k,\mathbf{R}^N)$ such that each
    $g \in \mathscr{C}^{1}_c(\mathbf{R}^k,\mathbf{R}^N)$ is the limit of a subsequence of
    $g_1, g_2, \ldots$. Indeed, for each~$j \in \mathscr{P}$, the compact metric space
    $\mathbf{B}(0,j) \times \{1, \ldots, (k+1)N\}$ has a countable base, and the space
    of continuous real valued functions on it, normed by the supremum norm, is
    separable by~\cite[V.8.4]{DS58}. Mapping $g$ onto the function on
    $\mathbf{B}(0,j) \times \{1, \ldots, (k+1)N\}$ whose values at
    $(y,1), \ldots, (y,(k+1)N)$ are the coordinates of
    $g(y), \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g(y)(e_1), \ldots, \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g(y)(e_k)$ with respect to the standard
    basis of~$\mathbf{R}^N$, where $e_1, \ldots, e_k$ is the standard basis of~$\mathbf{R}^k$,
    we embed the subspace
    $\mathscr{C}^{1}_c(\mathbf{R}^k,\mathbf{R}^N) \cap \{ g : \operatorname{spt} g \subseteq \mathbf{B}(0,j) \}$ linearly
    and homeomorphically into that space of functions. Hence,
    by~\cite[I.6.12]{DS58}, this subspace has a countable dense subset. The
    union of these subsets over~$j$ is countable, and each
    $g \in \mathscr{C}^{1}_c(\mathbf{R}^k,\mathbf{R}^N)$ belongs to the closure of one of them.

\label{def:hsys}
    Suppose $0 \le h < \infty$ and $H : \mathbf{R}^{n+1} \to \mathbf{R}$ is a Borel
    function with $|H(y)| \le h$ for $y \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$. We say that $u$ is a
    \emph{weak solution of the $H$-system} if $u \in W^{1,n}(U,\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ and
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:hsys}
        {\textstyle\int_{U}^{}} |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u|^{n-2} \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u \bullet \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}\psi \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^n
        = {\textstyle\int_{U}^{}} H(u(x)) \, \mathbf{J} u(x) \bullet \psi(x) \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^n x
    \end{equation}
    whenever $\psi \in W^{1,n}_0(U,\mathbf{R}^{n+1}) \cap \mathbf{L}_{\infty}(U,\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:hadamard}
    If $A \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^n,\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$, then
    \begin{gather}
        \label{eq:hadamard:amgm}
        |\mathbf{J} (A)| \le n^{-n/2} |A|^n \,,
        \\
        \label{eq:hadamard:smallest}
        |A(v)| \ge |\mathbf{J} (A)| \, |A|^{1-n} |v|
        \quad \text{for $v \in \mathbf{R}^n$, provided $A \ne 0$} \,.
    \end{gather}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $v_1, \ldots, v_n$ be an orthonormal basis of $\mathbf{R}^n$. Hadamard's
    inequality gives $|\mathbf{J} (A)| = |A v_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge A
    v_n| \le |A v_1| \cdots |A v_n|$. Since $|A v_1|^2 + \cdots + |A v_n|^2
    = |A|^2$, the inequality between the geometric and the arithmetic mean
    yields~\eqref{eq:hadamard:amgm}. Suppose $A \ne 0$ and $|v| = 1$. We
    choose $v_1 = v$ and use $|A v_i| \le \|A\| \le |A|$ for $i \ge 2$ to
    obtain $|\mathbf{J} (A)| \le |A(v)| \, |A|^{n-1}$. This
    is~\eqref{eq:hadamard:smallest}.

\label{mr:distortion}
    Let $A \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^n,\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ with ${\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{n}} A \ne 0$. We call
    $|A|^n / |\mathbf{J} (A)|$ the \emph{distortion} of~$A$. By the proof
    of~\ref{lem:hadamard}, it is at least $n^{n/2}$, with equality if and
    only if $A^* \circ A$ is a multiple of the identity. In this case
    $Y(A) = n^{-1} (\operatorname{im} A)_\natural$.

\label{def:K}
    For $n^{n/2} \le \kappa < \infty$ let $\Psi_\kappa$ be the closure
    in $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ of the convex hull of
    \begin{displaymath}
        \bigl\{ Y(A) : A \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^n,\mathbf{R}^{n+1}),\
        0 < |A|^n \le \kappa |\mathbf{J} (A)| \bigr\} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    Then $Y(A) \in \Psi_\kappa$ whenever $A \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^n,\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ and
    $0 < |A|^n \le \kappa |\mathbf{J} (A)|$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:K}
    If $n^{n/2} \le \kappa < \infty$, then $\Psi_\kappa$ is a non-empty
    compact convex set, and each $X \in \Psi_\kappa$ is symmetric and
    satisfies
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:K}
        Xw \bullet w \ge 0 \text{ for $w \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$} \,, \quad
        \operatorname{trace} X = 1 \,, \quad
        \sigma_1(X) \le 1 \,, \quad
        \sigma_n(X) \ge (2 \kappa^2)^{-1} \,.
    \end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Call $N \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ \emph{admissible} if $N$ is
    symmetric, $Nw \bullet w \ge 0$ for $w \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$, and $\operatorname{trace} N = 1$.
    Convex combinations and limits of admissible maps are admissible, and $|N|
    \le \operatorname{trace} N = 1$ for admissible $N$. For a symmetric $X \in
    \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ and an admissible $N$ consider the condition
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:K:S}
        Xw \bullet w \ge \kappa^{-2} \bigl( |w|^2 - Nw \bullet w \bigr)
        \quad \text{for $w \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$} \,.
    \end{equation}

    Suppose $A \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^n,\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ and
    $0 < |A|^n \le \kappa |\mathbf{J} (A)|$. Clearly $\dim \operatorname{im} A = n$. Let
    \begin{displaymath}
        \nu \in (\operatorname{im} A)^{\perp} \,,
        \quad
        |\nu| = 1 \,,
        \quad
        P = (\operatorname{im} A)_\natural \,,
        \quad \text{and} \quad
        s = |\mathbf{J} (A)| \, |A|^{1-n} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    If $z \in \operatorname{im} A$, then $z = A(v)$ for some $v \in \mathbf{R}^n$, $|z| \ge s|v|$
    by~\ref{lem:hadamard}\eqref{eq:hadamard:smallest}, and
    $|A^*(z)| \, |v| \ge A^*(z) \bullet v = |z|^2$; hence $|A^*(z)| \ge s
    |z|$. Since $A^*(\nu) = 0$, $s |A|^{-1} \ge \kappa^{-1}$, and
    $|P(w)|^2 = |w|^2 - (w \bullet \nu)^2$, we infer
    \begin{displaymath}
        Y(A)w \bullet w
        = |A|^{-2} |A^*(P(w))|^2
        \ge \kappa^{-2} \bigl( |w|^2 - (w \bullet \nu)^2 \bigr)
        \quad \text{for $w \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    Since $(\mathbf{R} \nu)_\natural$ is admissible and $(\mathbf{R} \nu)_\natural(w)
    \bullet w = (w \bullet \nu)^2$, the condition~\eqref{eq:K:S} holds for $X
    = Y(A)$ and $N = (\mathbf{R} \nu)_\natural$. Moreover $Y(A)$ is symmetric,
    $Y(A)w \bullet w \ge 0$ for $w \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$, and $\operatorname{trace} Y(A) = |A|^{-2}
    \operatorname{trace} (A \circ A^*) = 1$.

    Let $X$ be a convex combination of finitely many such maps $Y(A_i)$ with
    weights $t_i$, and let $N$ be the convex combination of the
    corresponding projections $N_i = (\mathbf{R} \nu_i)_\natural$ with the same
    weights. Then $N$ is admissible, $X$ is symmetric, $Xw \bullet w \ge
    0$ for $w \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$, $\operatorname{trace} X = 1$, and~\eqref{eq:K:S} holds. If $X
    \in \Psi_\kappa$, then $X$ is the limit of a sequence of such convex
    combinations, with corresponding admissible maps. These maps are
    bounded, and a subsequence converges to some admissible $N$. Hence $X$
    is symmetric, $Xw \bullet w \ge 0$ for $w \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$, $\operatorname{trace} X =
    1$, and~\eqref{eq:K:S} holds. The set $\Psi_\kappa$ is bounded, since
    $|X| \le \operatorname{trace} X$ for symmetric positive-semidefinite~$X$, closed by definition,
    and convex, as the closure of a convex set. The inclusion $A$ of $\mathbf{R}^n$
    into $\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ satisfies $|A|^n = n^{n/2} = n^{n/2} |\mathbf{J} (A)|$;
    hence $Y(A) \in \Psi_\kappa \ne \varnothing$.

    Let $X \in \Psi_\kappa$ and let $N$ be an admissible map as
    in~\eqref{eq:K:S}. The largest eigenvalue of $X$ is at most $\operatorname{trace} X = 1$,
    so $\sigma_1(X) \le 1$. Let $e$ be a unit eigenvector of $N$ for its largest
    eigenvalue $\lambda_1$, and let $W = \{ w : w \bullet e = 0 \}$.  Then
    $N[W] \subseteq W$, and every eigenvalue of $N|W$ is at most
    $\min \{ \lambda_1, 1 - \lambda_1 \} \le 1/2$, because the eigenvalues of
    $N$ are non-negative with sum~$1$. Hence $Nw \bullet w \le 1/2$ and
    $|Xw| \ge Xw \bullet w \ge (2 \kappa^2)^{-1}$ for $w \in W$ with $|w| = 1$.
    Since $W \in \mathbf{G}(n+1,n)$, we obtain $\sigma_n(X) \ge (2 \kappa^2)^{-1}$.

\label{mr:push}
    Suppose $d \in \mathscr{P}$ and $W$ is a finite dimensional Euclidean space. As
    in~\cite[2.5.14]{Fed69}, $\mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d)$ is the space of continuous
    functions $f : \mathbf{R}^d \to \mathbf{R}$ with compact support, and
    $\mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d)^+ = \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d) \cap \{ f : f \ge 0 \}$. Similarly,
    $\mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,W)$ is the space of continuous maps of $\mathbf{R}^d$ into $W$ with
    compact support. Suppose $T : \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,W) \to \mathbf{R}$ is a linear
    functional such that
    \begin{displaymath}
        \|T\|(f) = \sup \bigl\{ T(\xi) : \xi \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,W),\
        |\xi(y)| \le f(y) \text{ for $y \in \mathbf{R}^d$} \bigr\} < \infty
        \quad \text{for $f \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d)^+$} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    We apply~\cite[2.5.12]{Fed69} in the form for $L = \mathscr{K}(X)$ given in
    the last paragraph of~\cite[2.5.14]{Fed69}, with $\mathbf{R}^d$,
    $\mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,W)$, $W$ and the norm of $W$ in place of $X$, $\Omega$,
    $E$ and~$\nu$, as in~\cite[4.1.5]{Fed69}. Hence $\|T\|$ is a monotone
    Daniell integral on $\mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d)^+$. As in~\cite[4.1.5]{Fed69}, we
    denote by $\|T\|$ also the Radon measure over $\mathbf{R}^d$ associated with it,
    and call it the \emph{variation measure} of~$T$. Moreover, there is an
    $\|T\|$ measurable map $\eta$ with values in $\operatorname{Hom}(W,\mathbf{R})$ such that
    $\|\eta(y)\| = 1$ for $\|T\|$ almost all $y$ and
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:stress:polar}
        T(\xi) = {\textstyle\int_{}^{}}\eta(y)(\xi(y)) \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \|T\| y
        \quad \text{for $\xi \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,W)$} \,.
    \end{equation}
    Since $\|T\|(f) = {\textstyle\int_{}^{}}f \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \|T\|$ for $f \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d)^+$, we
    have $T(\xi) \le {\textstyle\int_{}^{}}|\xi| \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \|T\|$ for
    $\xi \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,W)$.  The functions $f_j \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d)^+$ given
    by $f_j(y) = \min \{ 1, \max \{ 0, j - |y| \} \}$ for $y \in \mathbf{R}^d$ and
    $j \in \mathscr{P}$ satisfy $f_j \le f_{j+1} \le 1$ and $f_j(y) = 1$ whenever
    $|y| \le j-1$.  By~\cite[2.4.7]{Fed69},
    $\|T\|(\mathbf{R}^d) = \lim_{j \to \infty} \|T\|(f_j)$.  Since $f_j \le 1$, we infer
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:stress:variation}
        \begin{gathered}
            |T(\xi)| \le {\textstyle\int_{}^{}}|\xi| \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \|T\|
            \quad \text{for $\xi \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,W)$} \,,
            \\
            \|T\|(\mathbf{R}^d) = \sup \bigl\{ T(\xi) : \xi \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,W),\
            |\xi(y)| \le 1 \text{ for $y \in \mathbf{R}^d$} \bigr\} \,.
        \end{gathered}
    \end{equation}

\label{mr:push:measure}
    The linear functionals in~\ref{def:push} do not depend on the choice of the
    representatives. Since $u$ and $\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u$ are Borel maps, the integrands
    in~\ref{def:push} are Borel functions. Since $\mu_{\rho}(f) \ge 0$ for
    $f \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1})^+$, the functional $\mu_{\rho}$ is monotone,
    and~\cite[2.5.13]{Fed69} shows that it is a Daniell integral. Thus
    $\mu_{\rho}$ is a monotone Daniell integral in the sense
    of~\cite[2.5.6]{Fed69}. We denote with the same symbol $\mu_{\rho}$ also the
    $\mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ regular measure associated with it. Since the topology
    of $\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ has a countable base, it is a Radon measure
    by~\cite[2.5.14]{Fed69}, and $\mu_{\rho}(f) = {\textstyle\int_{}^{}}f \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mu_{\rho}$
    for $f \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$.

    Suppose $V \subseteq \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ is open, let $f_j$ be as in~\ref{mr:push}
    with $d = n+1$, and let $\operatorname{dist}(y,\varnothing) = \infty$ for $y \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$. The
    functions $\varphi_j : \mathbf{R}^{n+1} \to \mathbf{R}$ given by
    \begin{displaymath}
        \varphi_j(y) = \min \bigl\{ f_j(y), j \operatorname{dist}(y, \mathbf{R}^{n+1} \!\sim\! V)
        \bigr\}
        \quad \text{for $y \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ and $j \in \mathscr{P}$}
    \end{displaymath}
    belong to $\mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1})^+$ and increase to the characteristic
    function of $V$ as $j \to \infty$. We replace $f$ by $\varphi_j$ in the
    identity
    \begin{displaymath}
        {\textstyle\int_{}^{}}f \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mu_{\rho} = {\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{U}(0,\rho)}^{}} f(u(x)) \, |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u(x)|^n \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}}
        \mathscr{L}^n x
    \end{displaymath}
    and apply~\cite[2.4.7]{Fed69} to both sides. This gives
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:stress:borel}
        \mu_{\rho}(V) = {\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{U}(0,\rho) \cap u^{-1}[V]}^{}} |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u|^n \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^n \,. 
    \end{equation}
    Both sides of~\eqref{eq:stress:borel} are finite and
    additive on Borel sets; hence~\eqref{eq:stress:borel} also holds for
    closed sets~$E$ in place of~$V$. Suppose $E \subseteq \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ is a Borel set.
    By~\cite[2.2.2(1)]{Fed69}, $\mu_{\rho}(E)$ is the supremum of the numbers
    $\mu_{\rho}(C)$ corresponding to closed sets $C \subseteq E$. Hence
    $\mu_{\rho}(E)$ does not exceed the right-hand side
    of~\eqref{eq:stress:borel}. The same holds for $\mathbf{R}^{n+1} \!\sim\! E$ in
    place of~$E$, and~\eqref{eq:stress:borel} with $E = \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$

    \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$.

Suppose $\varrho \in \mathscr{D}(J,\mathbf{R})$ and $g \in
    \mathscr{C}^{1}_c(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$. Define $\omega : \mathbf{R} \to \mathbf{R}$ by
    \begin{displaymath}
        \omega(t) = {\textstyle\int_{\sup \{ t, 0 \}}^{1}} \varrho \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^1
        \quad \text{for $t \in \mathbf{R}$} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    Then $\omega$ is of class~$\infty$, $\omega'(t) = - \varrho(t)$ for
    $t \in J$, $\omega(t) = 0$ for $t \ge \sup \operatorname{spt} \varrho$, and $\omega$ is
    constant on $\{ t : t \le \inf \operatorname{spt} \varrho \}$. Hence the function mapping
    $x \in U$ onto $\omega(|x|)$ is of class~$\infty$ with compact support
    in~$U$, and its gradient at $x$ equals $- \varrho(|x|) |x|^{-1} x$ if
    $x \ne 0$ and $0$ if $x = 0$. One readily verifies that
    $g \circ u \in W^{1,n}(U,\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ and
    $\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}(g \circ u) = \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g(u) \circ \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u$; cf.~\cite[Lemma~7.5]{GT01}.  Therefore
    the map $\psi$ given by $\psi(x) = \omega(|x|) \, g(u(x))$ for $x \in U$
    belongs to $W^{1,n}_0(U,\mathbf{R}^{n+1}) \cap \mathbf{L}_{\infty}(U,\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$.  Since
    $\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u \circ \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u^*$ is symmetric,
    \begin{displaymath}
        \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u \bullet \bigl( \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g(u) \circ \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u \bigr)
        = \operatorname{trace} \bigl( \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g(u) \circ \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u \circ \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u^* \bigr)
        = \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g(u) \bullet \bigl( \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u \circ \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u^* \bigr) \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    Testing~\ref{def:hsys}\eqref{eq:hsys} with $\psi$ and
    using~\ref{mr:notation}\eqref{eq:notation:rank-one}, we obtain
    \begin{multline*}
        {\textstyle\int_{U}^{}} \omega(|x|) \, \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g(u(x)) \bullet \bigl( |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u|^{n-2} \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u
        \circ \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u^* \bigr)(x) \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^n x
        \\
        - {\textstyle\int_{U}^{}} \varrho(|x|) \, |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u(x)|^{n-2} \, g(u(x)) \bullet \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}

        \\
        = {\textstyle\int_{U}^{}} \omega(|x|) \, H(u(x)) \, \mathbf{J} u(x) \bullet g(u(x))
        \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^n x \,.
    \end{multline*}
    Since $\omega(|x|) = {\textstyle\int_{J \cap \{ s : s > |x| \}}^{}} \varrho \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^1$
    for $x \in U$, Fubini's theorem~\cite[2.6.2]{Fed69} transforms the first
    and the last integral into ${\textstyle\int_{J}^{}} \varrho(s) \, \Sigma_s(\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g) \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^1
    s$ and ${\textstyle\int_{J}^{}} \varrho(s) \, \tau_s(g) \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^1 s$. This
    proves~\eqref{eq:sphere:test}.

    \begin{displaymath}
        f_g(t) = {\textstyle\int_{\operatorname{Bdry}{\mathbf{U}(0,t)}}^{}} |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u(x)|^{n-2} \, g(u(x)) \bullet
        \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u(x)(|x|^{-1} x) \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{H}^{n-1} x
    \end{displaymath}
    whenever $t \in J$ and ${\textstyle\int_{\operatorname{Bdry}{\mathbf{U}(0,t)}}^{}} |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u|^{n-1} \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}}
    \mathscr{H}^{n-1} < \infty$. Since $U$ is bounded, $|\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u|^{n-1}$ is $\mathscr{L}^n

    coordinates~\cite[3.2.13]{Fed69}, $f_g$ is $\mathscr{L}^1  \mathop{ \rule[0pt]{.5pt}{6pt} \rule[0pt]{4pt}{0.5pt} }\nolimits  J$ summable,
    and the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:sphere:test} equals ${\textstyle\int_{J}^{}} \varrho
    f_g \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^1$. The function mapping $t \in J$ onto $\Sigma_t(\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g) -
    \tau_t(g)$ is bounded and continuous. Hence~\eqref{eq:sphere:test}
    shows that the regularisations, defined in~\cite[(7.13)]{GT01}, of the
    function $[ J \ni t \mapsto \Sigma_t(\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g) - \tau_t(g) - f_g(t) ]$

    \cite[Lemma~7.2]{GT01} yields

        \label{eq:sphere:ae}
        \Sigma_t(\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g) = \tau_t(g) + f_g(t)
        \quad \text{for $\mathscr{L}^1$ almost all $t \in J$} \,.

    \mathscr{C}^{1}_c(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ such that each $g \in
    \mathscr{C}^{1}_c(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ is the limit of a subsequence of $g_1, g_2,

    $u = \tilde u$ at $\mathscr{H}^{n-1}$ almost all points of $\operatorname{Bdry}{\mathbf{U}(0,t)}$,
    ${\textstyle\int_{\operatorname{Bdry}{\mathbf{U}(0,t)}}^{}} |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u|^n \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{H}^{n-1} < \infty$, and

    $S = \operatorname{Bdry}{\mathbf{U}(0,\rho)}$, and let $v = \tilde u | S$. Since

    ${\textstyle\int_{S}^{}} |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u|^{n-1} \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{H}^{n-1} < \infty$.

    $f_{g_k}(\rho) = \beta_\rho(g_k)$, and $\rho \in T$ gives~\eqref{eq:sphere}
    for $g = g_k$, $k \in \mathscr{P}$. Since $|\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u(x)(|x|^{-1} x)| \le |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u(x)|$,
    we have
    $|\beta_\rho(g)| \le \sup |g| {\textstyle\int_{S}^{}} |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u|^{n-1} \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{H}^{n-1}$ for
    $g \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$. Combining this

    \begin{gather*}
        |\Sigma_\rho(\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g)| \le \mu_{\rho}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1}) \sup |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g| \,,
        \\
        |\tau_\rho(g) + \beta_\rho(g)|
        \le \Bigl( h n^{-n/2} \mu_{\rho}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1}) + {\textstyle\int_{S}^{}} |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u|^{n-1} \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}}
        \mathscr{H}^{n-1} \Bigr) \sup |g| \,.

    \ne 0$, and $|\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u(x)|^n \le \kappa |\mathbf{J} u(x)|$. Then ${\textstyle \boldsymbol{\bigwedge}_{n}}

    $\psi$ on $A$ and $0$ elsewhere, and recalling that $f = u$ on $A$, we

        \label{eq:structure:area}
        {\textstyle\int_{A}^{}} \psi \, |\mathbf{J} u| \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^n

    right-hand side is $\mathscr{H}^n$ measurable; see the proof
    of~\cite[Theorem~12]{Haj00} and~\cite[3.2.3(2)]{Fed69}.

    \end{gather}

    \end{displaymath}

\label{mr:DGPR}
    In this section we combine~\ref{lem:structure} with the inequality of De~Philippis, Gennaioli,
    Pigati, and Rindler~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{DGPR26}. We obtain lower mass ratios, boundedness, and continuity. The
    standing hypotheses of~\ref{mr:stress} and the notation of section~\ref{sec:stress} are
    retained.

    We use~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{DGPR26} in the following form. Suppose $1 \le m \le d$ are integers and
    $\Psi \subseteq \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^d,\mathbf{R}^d)$ is non-empty, compact and convex, with
    \begin{displaymath}
        \lambda = \inf \{ \sigma_m(X) : X \in \Psi \} > 0 \,, \qquad
        \Lambda = \sup \{ \sigma_1(X) : X \in \Psi \} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    Then there is $0 < \Gamma < \infty$, depending only on $d$, $\lambda$ and $\Lambda$, with the
    following property. Suppose $M \subseteq \mathbf{R}^d$ is a Borel set with $\mathscr{H}^m(M) < \infty$,
    $\vartheta : M \to [0, \infty)$ is $\mathscr{H}^m  \mathop{ \rule[0pt]{.5pt}{6pt} \rule[0pt]{4pt}{0.5pt} }\nolimits  M$ summable, $X : M \to \Psi$ is
    $\mathscr{H}^m  \mathop{ \rule[0pt]{.5pt}{6pt} \rule[0pt]{4pt}{0.5pt} }\nolimits  M$ measurable, and $\zeta : \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^d,\mathbf{R}^d)) \to \mathbf{R}$ is the
    linear functional given by
    \begin{displaymath}
        \zeta(F) = {\textstyle\int_{M}^{}} \vartheta \, F \bullet X \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{H}^m
        \quad \text{for $F \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^d,\mathbf{R}^d))$} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    Suppose moreover that $\nu : \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,\mathbf{R}^d) \to \mathbf{R}$ is a linear
    functional with
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:DGPR:sup}
        \sup \bigl\{ \nu(g) : g \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,\mathbf{R}^d),\ |g(y)| \le 1
        \text{ for $y \in \mathbf{R}^d$} \bigr\} < \infty \,.
    \end{equation}
    If $f \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d)^+$, $\sup f > 0$, $\xi \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,\mathbf{R}^d)$
    and $|\xi| \le f$, then $|(\sup f)^{-1} \xi| \le 1$. Hence $\nu$
    satisfies the hypothesis imposed on $T$ in~\ref{mr:push}, with
    $W = \mathbf{R}^d$, and $\|\nu\|(\mathbf{R}^d)$ equals the supremum
    in~\eqref{eq:DGPR:sup} by~\ref{mr:push}\eqref{eq:stress:variation}.
    Suppose finally that
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:DGPR:div}
        \zeta(\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g) = - \nu(g)
        \quad \text{for $g \in \mathscr{D}(\mathbf{R}^d,\mathbf{R}^d)$} \,.
    \end{equation}
    Then
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:DGPR}
        {\textstyle\int_{M}^{}} \vartheta \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{H}^m \le \Gamma \, \mathscr{H}^m(M)^{1/m} \, \|\nu\|(\mathbf{R}^d)
        \,.
    \end{equation}
    For $m = n$, $d = n+1$ and $\Psi = \Psi_\kappa$ with $n^{n/2} \le \kappa
    < \infty$, \ref{lem:K} gives $\lambda \ge (2 \kappa^2)^{-1}$ and $\Lambda
    \le 1$. We write $\Gamma_\kappa$ for the corresponding constant~$\Gamma$;
    it depends only on $n$ and~$\kappa$.

\label{mr:DGPR:language}
    In~\cite{DGPR26}, linear maps are represented by their coefficients with respect to the standard
    basis. In that language $\sigma_k$ is the $k$-th singular value and $\bullet$ is the
    Hilbert--Schmidt product.  Moreover, \cite[Theorem~1.1]{DGPR26} is formulated for vector valued
    measures.  To the functional $\zeta$ of~\ref{mr:DGPR} corresponds the measure with values in
    $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^d,\mathbf{R}^d)$ assigning ${\textstyle\int_{E \cap M}^{}} \vartheta X \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{H}^m$ to each Borel set
    $E \subseteq \mathbf{R}^d$. To $\nu$ corresponds the $\mathbf{R}^d$ valued measure assigning
    ${\textstyle\int_{E}^{}} \eta(y)^*(1) \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \|\nu\| y$ to $E$, where $\eta$ is the map
    of~\ref{mr:push}\eqref{eq:stress:polar} with $T = \nu$. Since
    $\eta(y)^*(1) \bullet w = \eta(y)(w)$ for $w \in \mathbf{R}^d$, \ref{mr:push}\eqref{eq:stress:polar}
    shows that integrating $g \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^d,\mathbf{R}^d)$ against this measure with respect to
    $\bullet$ gives $\nu(g)$. Since $|\eta(y)^*(1)| = \|\eta(y)\| = 1$ for $\|\nu\|$ almost all $y$,
    the total variation measure of this measure is~$\|\nu\|$.  Thus~\ref{mr:DGPR}\eqref{eq:DGPR:div}
    states that the second measure is the divergence of the first in the sense of distributions.

\label{lem:ode}
    Suppose $0 < r < \infty$, $0 < c < \infty$, $0 \le b < \infty$, $f :
    (0, r] \to (0, \infty)$ is non-decreasing, and
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:ode:hyp}
        f(t) \le c \, f(s)^{1/n} \bigl( b f(s) + (s - t)^{-1} (f(s) - f(t))
        \bigr)
        \quad \text{whenever $0 < t < s \le r$} \,.
    \end{equation}
    Then $f(t) \ge \min \{ (2nc)^{-1} t, (2bc)^{-1} \}^n$ for $0 < t \le r$,
    where $(2bc)^{-1} = \infty$ if $b = 0$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $F = f^{1/n}$ and $0 < t \le r$. If $2 b c F(t) > 1$, then $f(t) >
    (2bc)^{-n}$. Suppose $2 b c F(t) \le 1$. We extend $F$ to a
    non-decreasing function on~$\mathbf{R}$ by setting $F(\tau) = 0$ for $\tau \le 0$
    and $F(\tau) = F(r)$ for $\tau > r$. By~\cite[2.9.19]{Fed69}, $F$ has a
    derivative $F'(\tau) \in \mathbf{R}$ at $\mathscr{L}^1$ almost all $\tau \in \mathbf{R}$, and
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:ode:int}
        {\textstyle\int_{0}^{t}} F' \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^1 \le F(t) - F(0) = F(t) \,.
    \end{equation}
    Suppose $0 < \tau < t$ and $F'(\tau) \in \mathbf{R}$. Then $f$ is continuous
    at~$\tau$ and has the derivative $f'(\tau) = n F(\tau)^{n-1}
    F'(\tau)$. Letting $s \downarrow \tau$ in~\eqref{eq:ode:hyp} with $\tau$
    in place of~$t$, we obtain
    \begin{displaymath}
        f(\tau) \le b c F(\tau) f(\tau) + c F(\tau) f'(\tau)
        \le f(\tau) / 2 + n c f(\tau) F'(\tau) \,,
    \end{displaymath}
    where we used $F(\tau) \le F(t)$, $2 b c F(t) \le 1$ and $F(\tau)
    f'(\tau) = n f(\tau) F'(\tau)$. Since $0 < f(\tau) < \infty$, this gives
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:ode:diff}
        F'(\tau) \ge (2nc)^{-1}
        \quad \text{for $\mathscr{L}^1$ almost all $\tau \in (0, t)$} \,.
    \end{equation}
    Combining~\eqref{eq:ode:int} and~\eqref{eq:ode:diff}, we obtain $F(t)
    \ge (2nc)^{-1} t$.

    \end{displaymath}
    Since $\operatorname{spt} \varphi \subseteq \mathbf{U}(a,s)$, \eqref{eq:lower-mass:away}
    and~\ref{lem:structure}\eqref{eq:structure} give
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:lower-mass:rep}
        \zeta(F) = {\textstyle\int_{M \cap \mathbf{U}(a,s)}^{}} \varphi \theta \, F \bullet X \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}}
        \mathscr{H}^n
        \quad \text{for $F \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1}))$}
        \,.
    \end{equation}
    Suppose $g \in \mathscr{D}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$. Then $\varphi g \in
    \mathscr{C}^{1}_c(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$, $\operatorname{spt}(\varphi g) \subseteq \mathbf{U}(a,s)
    \subseteq \mathbf{R}^{n+1} \!\sim\! K_\rho$, and $\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}(\varphi g) = \varphi \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g
    + \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}\varphi \cdot g$. From~\ref{mr:regular}\eqref{eq:regular},
    \ref{mr:notation}\eqref{eq:notation:rank-one},
    \eqref{eq:lower-mass:away} and~\ref{lem:structure}\eqref{eq:structure}
    we infer
    \begin{displaymath}
        \zeta(\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} g)
        = \Sigma_\rho(\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}(\varphi g)) - \Sigma_\rho(\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}\varphi \cdot g)
        = \tau_\rho(\varphi g)
        - {\textstyle\int_{M}^{}} \theta \, g \bullet X(\operatorname{grad} \varphi) \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{H}^n
        = - \nu(g) \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    By~\ref{lem:K}, $\|X(y)\| = \sigma_1(X(y)) \le 1$ for $y \in M$. Suppose
    $g \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ and $|g(y)| \le 1$ for $y \in
    \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$. Then $|g \bullet X(\operatorname{grad} \varphi)| \le |\operatorname{grad} \varphi|$
    on~$M$ and $|\varphi g| \le \varphi$. Combining this
    with~\eqref{eq:lower-mass:grad}, \ref{lem:structure}\eqref{eq:structure}
    and~\ref{mr:push:measure}\eqref{eq:stress:bounds}, and recalling that $0 \le
    \varphi \le 1$ and $\operatorname{spt} \varphi \subseteq \mathbf{U}(a,s)$, we obtain
    \begin{multline*}
        \nu(g)
        \le 2 (s - t)^{-1} {\textstyle\int_{M \cap \{ y : t < |y - a| < s \}}^{}} \theta \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}}
        \mathscr{H}^n + h n^{-n/2} {\textstyle\int_{}^{}}\varphi \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mu_{\rho}
        \\
        \le 2 (s - t)^{-1} \bigl( f(s) - f(t) \bigr) + h n^{-n/2} f(s) \,.
    \end{multline*}
    Hence $\nu$ satisfies~\ref{mr:DGPR}\eqref{eq:DGPR:sup}, and, by~\ref{mr:DGPR},
    $\|\nu\|(\mathbf{R}^{n+1}) \le 2 (s - t)^{-1} ( f(s) - f(t) ) + h n^{-n/2}
    f(s)$. By~\ref{lem:structure}\eqref{eq:structure},
    ${\textstyle\int_{M \cap \mathbf{U}(a,s)}^{}} \theta \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{H}^n = f(s) < \infty$. Since $\theta \ge n^{n/2}$ on $M$,
    we infer $\mathscr{H}^n(M \cap \mathbf{U}(a,s)) \le n^{-n/2} f(s)$. Since $\varphi(y) = 1$
    for $|y - a| \le t$, \ref{lem:structure}\eqref{eq:structure} also gives
    \begin{displaymath}
        {\textstyle\int_{M \cap \mathbf{U}(a,s)}^{}} \varphi \theta \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{H}^n
        \ge {\textstyle\int_{M \cap \mathbf{U}(a,t)}^{}} \theta \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{H}^n = f(t) \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    We apply~\ref{mr:DGPR} with $m = n$, $d = n+1$, $\Psi = \Psi_\kappa$, the
    Borel set $M \cap \mathbf{U}(a,s)$, the function $\vartheta = \varphi \theta | M
    \cap \mathbf{U}(a,s)$, the map $X | M \cap \mathbf{U}(a,s)$, and the linear functionals
    $\zeta$ and~$\nu$. By~\eqref{eq:lower-mass:rep} and the preceding
    estimates, \ref{mr:DGPR}\eqref{eq:DGPR} yields
    \begin{displaymath}
        f(t) \le \Gamma_\kappa n^{-1/2} f(s)^{1/n} \bigl( 2 (s - t)^{-1}
        (f(s) - f(t)) + h n^{-n/2} f(s) \bigr) \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    This is~\ref{lem:ode}\eqref{eq:ode:hyp} with $c = 2 n^{-1/2}
    \Gamma_\kappa$ and $b = h n^{-n/2} / 2$. Since $2nc = 4 n^{1/2}
    \Gamma_\kappa$ and $(2bc)^{-1} = 2 n^{(n+2)/2} (4 n^{1/2} \Gamma_\kappa
    h)^{-1}$, \ref{lem:ode} with $t = r$ yields the conclusion.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:bounded}
    Suppose $0 \le h < \infty$, $H$ and $u$ are as in~\ref{def:hsys}, $0 \le
    R_0 < \infty$, $n^{n/2} \le \kappa < \infty$, and
    \begin{displaymath}
        |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u(x)|^n \le \kappa |\mathbf{J} u(x)|
        \quad \text{for $\mathscr{L}^n$ almost all $x \in U$ with $|u(x)| > R_0$} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    Then $u \in \mathbf{L}_{\infty}(\mathbf{U}(0,\rho), \mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ for $0 < \rho < 1$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $0 < \rho < 1$. By~\ref{lem:sphere} there is a regular $\rho'$ with
    $\rho < \rho' < 1$. Let
    \begin{displaymath}
        c_0 = (4 n^{1/2} \Gamma_\kappa)^{-n} \min \{ 1, h^{-1} \}^n \,, \qquad
        W = \{ y : |y| \le R_0 + 1 \} \cup \{ y : \operatorname{dist}(y, K_{\rho'}) \le 1
        \} \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    Since $K_{\rho'}$ is compact, $W$ is bounded. If $a \in \operatorname{spt} \mu_{\rho'}
    \!\sim\! W$, then $\operatorname{dist}(a, K_{\rho'}) > 1$ and $|a| - R_0 > 1$; hence
    $\mu_{\rho'} \, \mathbf{U}(a,1) \ge c_0$ by~\ref{lem:lower-mass}. If $a_1,
    \ldots, a_N \in \operatorname{spt} \mu_{\rho'} \!\sim\! W$ and $|a_i - a_j| \ge 2$ for
    $i \ne j$, then the balls $\mathbf{U}(a_i,1)$ are disjoint, and
    \begin{displaymath}
        N c_0 \le \mu_{\rho'}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1})
        = {\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{U}(0,\rho')}^{}} |\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u|^n \ensuremath{\,\mathrm{d}} \mathscr{L}^n < \infty \,.
    \end{displaymath}
    Choosing such points with $N$ maximal, we obtain $\operatorname{spt} \mu_{\rho'}
    \!\sim\! W \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^N \mathbf{U}(a_i,2)$. Hence there is $0 <
    R_1 < \infty$ with $\operatorname{spt} \mu_{\rho'} \subseteq \mathbf{B}(0,R_1)$.

    Choose $\gamma : \mathbf{R} \to \mathbf{R}$ of class~$\infty$ with $\gamma(t) = 0$ for $t
    \le R_1$ and $\gamma(t) = 1$ for $t \ge R_1 + 1$, and define $\psi :
    \mathbf{R}^{n+1} \to \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ by $\psi(y) = \gamma(|y|) \, y$ for $y \in
    \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$. Then $\psi$ is of class~$\infty$, $\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}\psi$ is bounded,
    $\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}\psi(y) = 0$ for $|y| \le R_1$, and $\psi(y) = y$ for $|y| \ge R_1 +
    1$. The proof of~\cite[Lemma~7.5]{GT01} applies to vector valued maps and
    shows that $w = \psi \circ u | \mathbf{U}(0,\rho')$ belongs to
    $W^{1,n}(\mathbf{U}(0,\rho'),\mathbf{R}^{n+1})$ and $\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} w = \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}\psi(u) \circ \ensuremath{\mathrm{D}}
    u$. Since $\mu_{\rho'}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1} \!\sim\! \operatorname{spt} \mu_{\rho'}) = 0$
    by~\cite[2.2.5]{Fed69}, \ref{mr:push:measure}\eqref{eq:stress:borel} yields
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:bounded:alt}
        \text{$\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} u(x) = 0$ or $|u(x)| \le R_1$}
        \quad \text{for $\mathscr{L}^n$ almost all $x \in \mathbf{U}(0,\rho')$} \,.
    \end{equation}
    Hence $\ensuremath{\mathrm{D}} w = 0$ at $\mathscr{L}^n$ almost all points of $\mathbf{U}(0,\rho')$.
    Since $\mathbf{U}(0,\rho')$ is convex, the Poincar{\'e}
    inequality~\cite[(7.45)]{GT01}, applied to the components of $w$, yields
    $c \in \mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ such that $w(x) = c$ for $\mathscr{L}^n$ almost all $x \in
    \mathbf{U}(0,\rho')$. Since $u(x) = w(x)$ whenever $|u(x)| \ge R_1 + 1$, we
    obtain $|u(x)| \le \max \{ R_1 + 1, |c| \}$ for $\mathscr{L}^n$ almost all $x \in
    \mathbf{U}(0,\rho')$. Hence $u \in \mathbf{L}_{\infty}(\mathbf{U}(0,\rho), \mathbf{R}^{n+1})$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:continuity}
    Suppose, in addition to the hypotheses of~\ref{thm:bounded}, that $H$ is
    continuous. Then $u$ has a representative which, for every compact subset
    $C$ of~$U$, is H{\"o}lder continuous on $C$ with some positive exponent.
    If moreover $\operatorname{Lip} (H|C) < \infty$ for every compact subset $C$
    of~$\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$, then there is $0 < \alpha < 1$ such that $u$ has a
    representative of class~$1$ whose derivative is H{\"o}lder continuous with
    exponent~$\alpha$ on every compact subset of~$U$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Each compact subset of~$U$ is contained in $\mathbf{U}(0,\rho)$ for some $0 < \rho < 1$; hence, $u$ is
    locally bounded by~\ref{thm:bounded}. Recalling~\ref{def:hsys}\eqref{eq:hsys} one readily
    verifies that $u$ is a weak solution in the sense of~\cite[Definition~1.1]{FKLV18} of the
    system~\cite[(1.1)]{FKLV18} on the bounded Lipschitz domain~$U$, with $H$ replaced by
    $(-1)^n n^{-n/2} H$. This function is bounded and continuous. Under the additional hypothesis,
    its restriction to each compact subset of~$\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$ is Lipschitz.
    Hence~\cite[Proposition~1.1]{FKLV18} yields both conclusions.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:direct}
    After the present work had been completed, F.~Rindler suggested to the author, in a private
    communication, that the method developed by Gennaioli and Rindler~\cite{GR26} for the Vanishing
    Mass Conjecture could be applied to $H$-systems directly, without passing through the
    Michael--Simon-type inequality of~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{DGPR26}. It would be interesting to carry
    this out. The approach presented here is, however, geometric. Through the push-forwards
    $\mu_\rho$ and $\Sigma_\rho$, it regards the image of~$u$ as a ``varifold'' whose first variation
    is controlled by the $H$-system. Boundedness then follows from lower mass ratios, as in the
    regularity theory of varifolds; see~\ref{mr:ode}. The author believes that this point of view is
    of independent interest, because it places the regularity of $H$-systems within geometric
    measure theory.
