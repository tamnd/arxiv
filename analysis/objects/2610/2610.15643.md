Let $p = 4q + 1$ be a prime integer, where $q$ is an odd prime integer. Let $L = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)^+$, and let $K \neq \mathbb{Q}$ be any totally real subfield of $L$. If $U_L^+=U_L^2$ (i.e., every totally positive unit is a square in $L$), then for any non-trivial totally positive unit $1 \neq \varepsilon \in U_K^+$, we have
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon) \ge p - 4.
\end{equation*}
Furthermore, this lower bound is sharp and is attained in the subfield of degree $q$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{1-2}
Let $p = 4q + 1$ be a prime integer, where $q$ is an odd prime integer. Let $L = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)^+$, and let $K \neq \mathbb{Q}$ be any totally real subfield of $L$. If 
\begin{itemize}
    \item $2$ is a primitive root modulo $q$, or
    \item $p=4q+1<2^{22}$ and $p\neq 29$,
\end{itemize}
then for any non-trivial totally positive unit $1 \neq \varepsilon \in U_K^+$, we have
$
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon) \ge p - 4.
$

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{maintheorem2}
Let $p = 4q + 1$ be a prime integer, where $q$ is an odd prime integer. Let  $\mathcal{C}$ be a spherical fusion category with $\dim(\mathcal{C})=p$.  If 
$2$ is a primitive root modulo $q$, or
 $p=4q+1<2^{22}$ and $p\neq 29$, then $\mathcal{C}$ is pointed.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{squares}
Let $q$ be an odd prime and $p = 4q+1$ be a prime. Let $L = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)^+$, and let $K$ be the unique subfield of $L$ with degree $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = q$. If $U_L^+=U_L^2$, then every totally positive unit of $K$ is a square in $K$, i.e.,
$
U_K^+ = U_K^2.
$
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $u \in U_K^+$ be an arbitrary totally positive unit. Since \(K\subset L\) and both fields are totally real, we have
$
U_K^+\subseteq U_L^+.
$
Thus \(u\in U_L^+\).
By hypothesis, every totally positive unit of $L$ is a square in $L$, so there exists a unit $v \in U_L$ such that $v^2 = u$.  We claim that $v$ must belong to $K$.

Suppose $v \notin K$. Since $\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$ with $q$ being an odd prime, the relative extension $L/K$ is quadratic. Thus, $v \notin K$ implies that $L = K(v) = K(\sqrt{u})$.

On the other hand, since $p = 4q+1 \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, the unique quadratic subfield of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})$. Since $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = q$ is odd, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p}) \cap K = \mathbb{Q}$. Furthermore, the compositum $K(\sqrt{p}) \subseteq L$ has degree $[K(\sqrt{p}):\mathbb{Q}] = [K:\mathbb{Q}] [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p}):\mathbb{Q}] = 2q$. Comparing degrees over $\mathbb{Q}$ with $[L:\mathbb{Q}] = (p-1)/2 = 2q$, we obtain $L = K(\sqrt{p})$.


Since two quadratic extensions $K(\sqrt{u})$ and $K(\sqrt{p})$ coincide if and only if $u/p$ is a square in $K^\times$. Therefore, there exists some element $w \in K^\times$ such that
$
u = p \cdot w^2.
$
Taking the norm $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}$ on both sides:
$
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(u) = N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(p \cdot w^2) = p^q \cdot \left(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(w)\right)^2.
$
Since $u \in U_K^+$, $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(u) = 1$. Substituting into the norm equation, we get
$
1 = p^q \cdot \left(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(w)\right)^2.
$
Let $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(w) = \frac{a}{b} \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ with $\gcd(a,b)=1$ for $a, b \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$. Clearing denominators gives
\begin{equation}\label{b2}
b^2 = p^q \cdot a^2.
\end{equation}
We use the $p$-adic valuation $v_p$ \cite[Chapter II 6.1]{Shimura2010} on both sides of Equation \eqref{b2}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For the left-hand side: $v_p(b^2) = 2 v_p(b)$, which is an even integer.
    \item For the right-hand side: $v_p(p^q a^2) = q + 2 v_p(a)$, which is an odd integer.
\end{itemize}
This asserts that an even integer equals an odd integer, which is a contradiction.

Hence, our assumption that $v \notin K$ is false, forcing $v \in K$. Since $v \in U_K$ and $v^2 = u$, we conclude that $u \in U_K^2$, which completes the proof that $U_K^+ = U_K^2$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{trace_matrix_lemma}
For any $1 \le j, k \le 2q$, the trace pairing $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(e_j e_k)$ satisfies:
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(e_j e_k) = 
\begin{cases} 
p - 2, & \text{if } j = k, \\
-2, & \text{if } j \neq k.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Using the  identity $e_j e_k = e_{j+k} + e_{j-k}$, we evaluate the trace.
 When $j = k$, $e_j^2 = e_{2j} + 2$. Since $1 \le 2j < p$,  $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(e_{2j})= \sum_{j=1}^{2q} e_j = -1$. Hence $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(e_j^2) = -1 + 2 [L:\mathbb{Q}] = -1 + 4q = p - 2$.
 When $j \neq k$,  $e_j e_k = e_{j+k} + e_{j-k}$. Since $1 \le |j \pm k| < p$ and $j \neq k$,  $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(e_j e_k) = (-1) + (-1) = -2$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{trace_quadratic_prop}
Let $x = \sum_{j=1}^{2q} c_j e_j \in \mathcal{O}_L$ with $c_j \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{trace_L_formula}
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(x^2) = p \sum_{j=1}^{2q} c_j^2 - 2 \left( \sum_{j=1}^{2q} c_j \right)^2.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Expanding $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(x^2)$ and using Lemma~\ref{trace_matrix_lemma}:
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(x^2) &= \sum_{j=1}^{2q} c_j^2 \operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(e_j^2) + \sum_{j \neq k} c_j c_k \operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(e_j e_k) 
= (p-2) \sum_{j=1}^{2q} c_j^2 - 2 \sum_{j \neq k} c_j c_k \\
&= p \sum_{j=1}^{2q} c_j^2 - 2 \left( \sum_{j=1}^{2q} c_j^2 + \sum_{j \neq k} c_j c_k \right) 
= p \sum_{j=1}^{2q} c_j^2 - 2 \left( \sum_{j=1}^{2q} c_j \right)^2.
\end{align*}
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{fix}
The action of $G$ on the index set $I$ is fixed-point-free for non-identity elements. That is, if $\sigma \in G$ is non-trivial, then $\pi_\sigma(j) \neq j$ for all $j \in I$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For any $\sigma_a \in G \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times / \{\pm 1\}$ and notice that $e_j=\zeta_p^j + \zeta_p^{-j} = 2\cos\frac{2\pi j}{p}$, the condition $\sigma_a(e_j) = e_j$ is equivalent to $\cos(\frac{2\pi aj}{p}) = \cos(\frac{2\pi j}{p})$, which yields $aj \equiv \pm j \pmod p$. Since $\gcd(j, p) = 1$, this forces $a \equiv \pm 1 \pmod p$, meaning $\sigma_a = \mathrm{id}$. Thus, $\mathrm{Stab}_G(e_j) = \{\mathrm{id}\}$, so $e_j$ has $2q$ distinct conjugates under $G$ and is a primitive element for $L/\mathbb{Q}$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
Let $H \le G$ be a subgroup of order $|H| = m$. Under the restricted action of $H$ on $I$, the index set $I$ decomposes into a disjoint union of $n = \frac{2q}{m}$ equicardinal orbits:
\begin{equation*}
I = \mathcal{O}_1 \sqcup \mathcal{O}_2 \sqcup \dots \sqcup \mathcal{O}_n,
\end{equation*}
where each orbit $\mathcal{O}_i$ has cardinality $|\mathcal{O}_i| = |H| = m$. Furthermore, $j, k \in I$ belong to the same orbit $\mathcal{O}_i$ if and only if there exists some $\sigma \in H$ such that $k = \pi_\sigma(j)$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By Lemma \ref{fix}, the stabilizer subgroup $\mathrm{Stab}_H(j)$ is trivial for every $j \in I$. By the Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem, each orbit has cardinality $|\mathcal{O}_j| = |H| / |\mathrm{Stab}_H(j)| = m$. Since the orbits partition $I$, the number of orbits is $n = |I| / m = 2q / m$.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{subfield_trace_formula}
For any element $\eta = \sum_{i=1}^n d_i E_i \in \mathcal{O}_K$, where $E_i$ are the orbit sums of $e_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{master_trace_eq}
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\eta^2) = p \sum_{i=1}^n (d_i - \bar{d})^2 + \frac{T^2}{n}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let $\varepsilon = \eta^2 \in U_K^+$ with $\varepsilon \neq 1$, which implies $\eta \neq \pm 1$. Thus, the integer vector $(d_1, \dots, d_n) \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ cannot have all equal components, so the discrete variance $V = \sum_{i=1}^n (d_i - \bar{d})^2 > 0$. The minimum non-zero value of $V$ over $\mathbb{Z}^n$ is $\frac{n-1}{n}$, attained when $n-1$ components equal $a$ and one component equals $a+1$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Case 1: $n = q$ (The $q$-th degree subfield $K_q$, $m=2$).} \\
In this case, $2m = 4$. Equation \eqref{master_trace_eq} becomes:
$\operatorname{Tr}_{K_q/\mathbb{Q}}(\eta^2) = p \sum_{i=1}^q (d_i - \bar{d})^2 + \frac{T^2}{q}.$
\begin{itemize}
     \item If $T \neq 0$, since $T \in \mathbb{Z}$, $T^2 \ge 1$. Combined with $V \ge \frac{q-1}{q}$, we get
    $$\operatorname{Tr}_{K_q/\mathbb{Q}}(\eta^2) \ge p \left( \frac{q-1}{q} \right) + \frac{1}{q} = \frac{p(q-1)+1}{q} = p - \frac{p-1}{q} = p - 4.$$
    
     \item If $T = 0$,  since not all $d_i$ are equal and $\sum d_i = 0$, there must be at least one positive and one negative integer. Thus $\sum d_i^2 \ge 1^2 + (-1)^2 = 2$. Then
     $
    \operatorname{Tr}_{K_q/\mathbb{Q}}(\eta^2) = p \sum_{i=1}^q d_i^2 \ge 2p > p - 4.
    $
\end{itemize}    
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Case 2: $n = 2q$ (The full field $L$, $m=1$).} 
\begin{itemize}
    \item  If $T \neq 0$,  $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\eta^2) \ge p \left(\frac{2q-1}{2q}\right) + \frac{1}{2q} = p - \frac{p-1}{2q} = p - 2 > p - 4$.
    \item If $T = 0$,  $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\eta^2) \ge 2p > p - 4$.
\end{itemize}
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Case 3: $n = 2$ (The quadratic subfield $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})$, $m=q$).} \\
Let $\varepsilon \in U_K^+$ be a non-trivial totally positive unit. For a prime $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, the fundamental unit of $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})$ has norm $-1$ \cite[Theorem 11.5.4]{AlacaWilliams2003}. Thus, any totally positive unit must be a square, so $\varepsilon = \eta^2$ for some unit $\eta \in \mathcal{O}_K^\times$.
Since $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{p}}{2}\right]$, we can write $\eta = \frac{t + k\sqrt{p}}{2}$ for integers $t, k$ with $t \equiv k \pmod 2$. If $k = 0$, then $\eta \in \mathbb{Z}$, forcing $\eta = \pm 1$ and $\varepsilon = 1$, which contradicts that $\varepsilon$ is non-trivial. Thus, $k \neq 0$.

The unit condition $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\eta) = \pm 1$ yields the Pell equation $t^2 - p k^2 = \pm 4$. Computing the trace of $\varepsilon = \eta^2$ gives:
$
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon) = \operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\left(\frac{t^2 + p k^2 + 2t k\sqrt{p}}{4}\right) = \frac{t^2 + p k^2}{2}.
$
Substituting $t^2 = p k^2 \pm 4$, we obtain $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon) = p k^2 \pm 2$. Since $k^2 \ge 1$, it  follows that:
$
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon) \ge p - 2 > p - 4.
$



Combining all three cases proves $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon) \ge p - 4$ for all non-trivial subfields $K \neq \mathbb{Q}$. The fact that this lower bound is sharp and is attained in the subfield $K_q$  will be proved in Theorem \ref{p-4}. This completes the proof of the theorem.

The explicitly constructed totally positive unit $\varepsilon_q \in U_{K_q}^2$ satisfies $
 \operatorname{Tr}_{K_q/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon_q) = p - 4$.

Let $m = (p+1)/2$, so $2m \equiv 1 \pmod p$. The cyclotomic unit as 
$
1 + \zeta_p = 1 + \zeta_p^{2m} = \zeta_p^m (\zeta_p^{-m} + \zeta_p^m).
$
Let $\varepsilon=(1+\zeta_p)(1+\zeta_p^{-1})$.
Substituting this into $\varepsilon$, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\varepsilon= (1+\zeta_p)(1+\zeta_p^{-1}) = \zeta_p^m \zeta_p^{-m} (\zeta_p^m + \zeta_p^{-m})^2 = (e_m)^2.
\end{equation*}
Since $e_m \in \mathcal{O}_L$, $\varepsilon$ is a square in $\mathcal{O}_L$.  Observe that $\varepsilon_q = \varepsilon \cdot \sigma_a(\varepsilon) = (e_m)^2 (e_{am})^2 = (e_m e_{am})^2$. We must show $\eta_q := e_m e_{am}\in K_q$. Notice that the Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K_q)$ is generated by $\sigma_a$, applying $\sigma_a$ to $\eta_q$ gives
$
\sigma_a(e_m e_{am}) = e_{am} e_{a^2 m}.
$
Since $a^2 \equiv -1 \pmod p$ and $e_{-x} = e_x$ for all $x$, we have $e_{a^2 m} = e_{-m} = e_m$. Thus,
$
\sigma_a(\eta_q) = e_{am} e_m = \eta_q.
$
This shows that $\eta_q$ is fixed by $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K_q)$,  $\eta_q \in \mathcal{O}_{K_q}$. Therefore, $\varepsilon_q = \eta_q^2$ is a square unit in $U_{K_q}^2$ and is totally positive.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{13dimension}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a spherical fusion category of global dimension $13$. If $\mathcal{C}$ is not pointed, then the Frobenius-Perron dimensions of simple objects of $\mathcal{C}$ are $1,u,u,u,u,v,w$, where $
u=\eta_0^{-1},
v=\eta_1\eta_0^{-1},
w=-\eta_2\eta_0^{-1}$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
By Lemma \ref{13ranklemma}, $\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{C})=7$ and $\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=13\alpha$. Write the simple objects as
$
\mathbf1,V_1,\ldots,V_6
$, and  let $
d_i=\operatorname{FPdim}(V_i)$, then we have
$
\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=1+\sum_{i=1}^{6}d_i^2=13\eta_0^{-2}$. Therefore, the positive algebraic integers $
b_i=\eta_0d_i$
satisfy 
\begin{equation}\label{(4)}
\sum_{i=1}^{6}b_i^2=13-\eta_0^2.
\end{equation}

By \cite[Corollary 2.2.1]{Davis1969}, $\{e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4,e_5,e_6\}$ is a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-basis of \(\mathcal{O}_L\). For \(b=\sum_{j=1}^{6}n_je_j\in\mathcal O_L\) with $n_j\in\mathbb{Z}$, by Equation \eqref{trace_L_formula}, after expanding and simplifying, we have
\begin{equation}\label{(5)}
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b^2)
=13\sum_{j=1}^{6}n_j^2
  -2\left(\sum_{j=1}^{6}n_j\right)^2
=\sum_{j=1}^{6}n_j^2
  +2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq6}(n_i-n_j)^2.
\end{equation}
If all \(n_j=m\), then \(b=-m\) and \(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b^2)=6m^2\).
Otherwise, 
there are at least five pairs of unequal coordinates, so $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b^2)\geq11$.
Therefore, for every nonzero element \(b\in\mathcal O_L\),
\begin{equation}\label{(6)}
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b^2)=6\quad\text{if }b=\pm1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b^2)\geq11\quad\text{otherwise}.
\end{equation}
Since \(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\eta_0^2)=18\), 
$\sum_{j=1}^{6}\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b_j^2)=60$ by Equation \eqref{(4)}, so \(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b_j^2)\leq30\) for all $j$.

Let $\tau\in \mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})$ such that $\tau(\zeta_{13})=\zeta_{13}^5$, then $\tau(e_1)=e_5$, $\tau(e_2)=e_3$, $\tau(e_4)= e_6$ and $\tau(\eta_0)=\eta_0$.
 Hence, we obtain
\[
1+\sum_{i=1}^6\tau(d_i)^2
=13\eta_0^{-2}
=1+\sum_{i=1}^6 d_i^2.\]
Note $|\tau(d_j)|\leq d_j$, as the Frobenius-Perron dimension of object are the largest among its Galois conjugates \cite[Proposition 3.3.6]{EGNO},  we have $
\tau(b_j)=\pm b_j$ for all $j$. 
If \(\tau(b_i)=-b_i\) for some $i$, its coefficient vector in the integral

(r,s,-s,t,-r,-t)$,
so Equation \eqref{(5)} gives
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b_i^2)=26(r^2+s^2+t^2),
\end{equation*}
 which would force \(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b_i^2)=26\) as \(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b_i^2)\leq30\).
The other five traces would then sum to \(34\), which then contradicts
Inequality \eqref{(6)}.
Hence,
$
\tau(b_j)=b_j$ for all $1\leq j\leq 6$.

Meanwhile, for any $b\in\mathcal{O}_L$, if $\tau(b)=b$, then 
$b=r\eta_0+s\eta_1+t\eta_2$, $r,s,t\in\mathbb Z$, and 
\begin{equation}\label{(10)}
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b^2)
=
2\left(
r^2+s^2+t^2
+4\bigl((r-s)^2+(s-t)^2+(t-r)^2\bigr)
\right).
\end{equation}
If $\tau(b)=b$, $b\neq \pm1$ and  \(r,s,t\) are not all equal, we have
\begin{equation}\label{(11)}
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(b^2)\geq18,
\end{equation}
with equality occurs precisely for $
b\in\{\pm\eta_0,\pm\eta_1,\pm\eta_2\}$, equivalently
\(r^2+s^2+t^2=1\). Let \(k\) be the number of \(b_j\) different from $1$.
Inequality \eqref{(11)} implies $
60\geq6(6-k)+18k$,
so \(k\leq2\).
The case \(k=0\) would give total trace \(36\).
If \(k=1\), the remaining element would have trace \(30\).
However, by Equation \eqref{(10)}, this would imply $
13(r^2+s^2+t^2)-4(r+s+t)^2=15$,
 hence $(r+s+t)^2\equiv6\pmod{13}$,
which is impossible as \(6\) is not a quadratic residue
modulo \(13\).
Thus \(k=2\), and traces of both these two exceptional elements (denote by $b,c$) are  \(18\). Then the preceding argument shows
$b,c\in\{\pm\eta_0,\pm\eta_1,\pm\eta_2\}$.
Since all \(b_j\) are positive,  a direct computation shows that 
$b=\eta_1,c=-\eta_2
$. Denote
$
u=\eta_0^{-1},
v=\eta_1\eta_0^{-1},
w=-\eta_2\eta_0^{-1}, 
$
the Frobenius-Perron dimensions of the simple objects are $1,u,u,u,u,v,w$, as claimed.

\label{13theorem}
There is no spherical fusion category \(\mathcal C\) satisfying
$
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C)=7, \dim(\mathcal C)=13$ and $\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal C)=13\alpha$. That is, spherical fusion categories of global dimension $13$ are pointed.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
By Proposition \ref{13dimension}, the Frobenius-Perron dimensions of simple objects are $1,u,u,u,u,v,w$. Without loss of generality, assume $\text{FPdim}(V_i)=u$ for $1\leq i\leq 4$, $\text{FPdim}(V_5)=v$ and $\text{FPdim}(V_6)=w$. Let $g(T)=T^3-4T^2+T+1$, then $g(T)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ and $g(u)=0$. Let $u_1,u_2,u_3$ be the three distinct
roots of $g(T)$, and let $\phi_j$ be the homomorphisms that are conjugate to $\mathrm{FPdim}(-)$, then for any simple object $V$ of $\mathcal{C}$, we have $\phi_j(V^*)=\phi_j(V)$ for $1\leq j\leq 3$, also  $\phi_j\neq \dim(-)$ as $\mathcal{C}$ is  not a pseudo-unitary fusion category. 

Let 
$x_i=\dim(V_i)$, $1\leq i\leq 4$, $ y=\dim(V_5),
z=\dim(V_6),
S=\sum_{i=1}^{4}x_i$.
Then \cite[Lemma 8.14.1]{EGNO} shows that $\sum_{V\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\dim(V)\phi_j(V^*)=0$ for $j=1,2,3$, that is,
\begin{equation*}
1+Su_j+y(u_j^2-2u_j-1)+z(u_j^2-u_j)=0,\quad j=1,2,3.
\end{equation*}
Thus the polynomial
$h(T):=
(y+z)T^2+(S-2y-z)T+(1-y)
$
vanishes at the three distinct roots $u_j$ of $g(T)$.
The degree  of $h(T)$ is at most two, so it is identically zero.
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
y=1,\quad z=-1,\quad S=1.
\end{equation*}
Meanwhile,
$\dim(\mathcal{C})=1+\sum_{i=1}^{4}x_i^2+y^2+z^2,
$
hence $\sum_{i=1}^4 x_i^2=10$. Therefore,  
\begin{equation*}
\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq4}x_ix_j
=
\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^4 x_i\right)^2-\sum_{i=1}^4 x_i^2}{2}
=-\frac{9}{2},
\end{equation*}
which is impossible, as the left-hand side is an algebraic integer. Therefore, spherical fusion category of global dimension $13$ is pointed.

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a spherical fusion category of global dimension $p=4q+1$. Assume that $2$ is a primitive root modulo $q$, or
 $p=4q+1<2^{22}$ and $p\neq 29$ below. If $q=3$, this is proved in Theorem \ref{13theorem}. By assumption, we have $q>7$. 

It is proved in \cite[Lemma 5.1, Corollary 5.3]{Schopieray2021} that $\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=p\cdot u_{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}$, where $u_{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}\in \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)^+$ is a totally positive  algebraic unit, then  $d_{\mathrm{FPdim(\mathcal{C})}} \in\{1,2,q,2q\}$.  If  $d_{\mathrm{FPdim(\mathcal{C})}} =1$, then $\mathcal{C}$ is pointed, we are done.
It follows from \cite[Proposition 3.6]{Yu2023} that   $d_{\mathrm{FPdim(\mathcal{C})}} \neq 2,2q$. Assume  $d_{\mathrm{FPdim(\mathcal{C})}} =q$ below. Notice that all the formal codegrees of $\mathcal{C}$ belong to the field $\mathbb{Q}(\dim(X),X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
)$ by \cite[Corollary 2.15]{Ostrik2015},  so the ring homomorphism $\dim(-)$   has at least $q$
Galois conjugates, which means that $\mathcal{C}$ has at least $q$ formal codegrees being $p$, thus the Equation  \eqref{classeqt} implies 
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}(u_{\mathrm{FPdim(\mathcal{C})}})/\mathbb{Q}}(u^{-1}_{\mathrm{FPdim(\mathcal{C})}})\leq p-q.
\end{equation*}
However, $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}(u_{\mathrm{FPdim(\mathcal{C})}})/\mathbb{Q}}(u^{-1}_{\mathrm{FPdim(\mathcal{C})}}) \geq p-4$ by Corollary \ref{1-2}, which is a contradiction as $q > 7$.
