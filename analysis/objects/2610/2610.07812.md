<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Let $n \geq 5$ and let $G$ be a simple graph on $[n]$ with at least two edges. The following
  conditions are equivalent.
  \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item $J_{c}(G)$ is CS for the column grading.
  \item $J_{c}(G)$ has a multilinear universal Gr\"{o}bner  basis.
  \item $G$ is one of the following graph:
    \begin{enumerate}[(a)]
      \item a matching with two edges on $5$ vertices;
      \item $K_{1,n-1}$, a star with $n-1$ leaves;
      \item $K_{1,n-2}+K_1$, consisting of a star with $n-2$ leaves together with one isolated vertex;
      \item $K_{n-1}+K_1$, consisting of a complete graph on $[n]\setminus\{v\}$ together with the isolated vertex $v$;
      \item a complete graph on $[n]\setminus\{v\}$ with $v$ adjacent to exactly one other vertex;
      \item $K_n$ with the edges of a matching removed.
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{definition:Cartwright--Sturmfels ideal}
  A homogeneous ideal  $I$ is \textit{Cartwright--Sturmfels}, or \textit{CS} for short,
  if there exists a squarefree
  multigraded Borel-fixed monomial ideal $J$ such that $\operatorname{HS}(S/I; \mathbf{t}) = \operatorname{HS}(S/J; \mathbf{t})$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:filter}
Let $Q\subset S$ be a squarefree monomial ideal such that
$Q_\mathbf{a}$ is either $0$ or $S_\mathbf{a}$ for each degree
$\mathbf{a}$. Then, for every homogeneous ideal $I$ and every term
order $\preceq$,
\begin{equation*}
 \operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(I\cap Q)=\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(I)\cap Q.
\end{equation*}
Moreover, if $I$ is CS, then $I\cap Q$ is CS.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
In each degree, $(I\cap Q)_{\mathbf{a}}$ is either $I_\mathbf{a}$ or $0$. Therefore, we have
\begin{equation*}
 \operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(I\cap Q)=\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(I)\cap Q.
\end{equation*}
The assumption also implies that $Q$ is
invariant under every coordinate change. Taking a common
sufficiently general change of coordinates gives
\[
 \operatorname{gin}_{\preceq}(I\cap Q)=\operatorname{gin}_{\preceq}(I)\cap Q.
\]
If $I$ is CS, the right-hand side is an intersection of squarefree monomial
ideals and is squarefree.
Thus, $I \cap Q$ is CS.

conditions are equivalent.

    \item $K_n$ with the edges of a matching removed.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:inheritance}
Let $I$ be a homogeneous ideal which has a multilinear universal Gr\"{o}bner  basis.
Then, the following ideals also have a multilinear universal Gr\"{o}bner  basis:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item The elimination ideal $I \cap \Bbbk[Y]$ for a subset $Y \subset X$;
\item The ideal generated by the polynomials obtained from elements of $I$ by
setting the variables in $Y$ equal to zero for a subset $Y \subset X$;
\item The ideal generated by the polynomials obtained from elements of $I$
by setting $t=1$, where $t$ is a variable of degree $\mathbf{e}_i$
for some $i\in[n]$ and no other variable has degree $\mathbf{e}_i$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{U}$ be a multilinear universal Gr\"{o}bner  basis of $I$
consisting of homogeneous polynomials.
We show that each ideal in (a)--(c) has a multilinear
Gr\"{o}bner  basis with respect to an arbitrary monomial order.

For (a), fix a monomial order $\preceq_Y$ on $\Bbbk[Y]$
and a monomial order $\preceq_{X\setminus Y}$ on
$\Bbbk[X\setminus Y]$.
Define the product order $\preceq$ on $\Bbbk[X]$ by first
comparing the $(X\setminus Y)$-parts using
$\preceq_{X\setminus Y}$ and then the $Y$-parts using
$\preceq_Y$.
This is an elimination order for the variables $X \setminus Y$
and restricts to $\preceq_Y$ on $\Bbbk[Y]$.
Since $\mathcal{U}$ is a Gr\"{o}bner  basis of $I$ with respect to
$\preceq$, the elimination theorem implies that
$\mathcal{U}\cap\Bbbk[Y]$ is a Gr\"{o}bner  basis of $I\cap\Bbbk[Y]$
with respect to $\preceq_Y$.
It is a multilinear Gr\"{o}bner  basis.

For (b), put $Z=X\setminus Y$, and let
$\pi:\Bbbk[X]\to\Bbbk[Z]$ be the homomorphism obtained by setting
the variables in $Y$ equal to zero.
Fix a monomial order $\preceq_Z$ on $\Bbbk[Z]$.
Define a monomial order $\preceq$ on $\Bbbk[X]$ by comparing
first total degrees, then degrees in $Z$, then the $Z$-parts
using $\preceq_Z$, and finally the $Y$-parts using any fixed
monomial order on $\Bbbk[Y]$.
This order agrees with $\preceq_Z$ on monomials of the same total degree in $\Bbbk[Z]$.

We claim that the nonzero elements of $\pi(\mathcal{U})$ form
a Gr\"{o}bner  basis of $\pi(I)$ with respect to $\preceq_Z$.
It suffices to consider a nonzero homogeneous polynomial
$f\in\pi(I)$.
Choose a homogeneous polynomial $F\in I$ such that $\pi(F)=f$.
Among the terms of $F$, those that survive under $\pi$
have the largest possible degree in $Z$.
Consequently,
\[
  \operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(F)=\operatorname{in}_{\preceq_Z}(f).
\]
Since $\mathcal{U}$ is a Gr\"{o}bner  basis of $I$ with respect to
$\preceq$, there exists $g\in\mathcal{U}$ such that
\[
  \operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(g)\mid\operatorname{in}_{\preceq_Z}(f).
\]
In particular, $\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(g)$ involves only variables
in $Z$, so $\pi(g)\neq0$ and
\[
  \operatorname{in}_{\preceq_Z}(\pi(g))=\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(g).
\]
This proves the claim.
Every nonzero $\pi(g)$ is homogeneous of the same degree
as $g$, and hence is multilinear.

For (c), put $Y=X\setminus\{t\}$, and let
$\pi:\Bbbk[X]\to\Bbbk[Y]$ be the homomorphism sending $t$ to $1$.
Fix a monomial order $\preceq_Y$ on $\Bbbk[Y]$.
Define a monomial order $\preceq$ on $\Bbbk[X]$ by first
comparing the $Y$-parts using $\preceq_Y$ and then comparing
the $t$-exponents when the $Y$-parts coincide.
Since $t$ is the only variable in its grading block,
each $g\in\mathcal{U}$ has the form $g=t^{b_g}h_g$, where $b_g\ge0$ and $h_g\in\Bbbk[Y]$.

We claim that $\{h_g:g\in\mathcal{U}\}$ is a Gr\"{o}bner  basis
of the ideal $\pi(I)$ with respect to $\preceq_Y$.
Since $\mathcal{U}$ generates $I$, the polynomials $h_g=\pi(g)$
generate $\pi(I)$.
Thus, for any nonzero $f\in\pi(I)$, we may write
\[
  f=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{U}}a_g h_g,
  \qquad a_g\in\Bbbk[Y].
\]
Choose $N$ such that $N\ge b_g$ whenever $a_g\neq0$.
Then
\[
  t^Nf
  =\sum_{\substack{g\in\mathcal{U}\\a_g\neq0}}
    a_g t^{N-b_g}g
  \in I.
\]
Since $\mathcal{U}$ is a Gr\"{o}bner  basis of $I$ with respect to
$\preceq$, there exists $g\in\mathcal{U}$ such that
\[
  \operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(g)\mid\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(t^Nf).
\]
By the definition of $\preceq$,
\[
  \operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(t^Nf)
  =t^N\operatorname{in}_{\preceq_Y}(f),
  \qquad
  \operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(g)
  =t^{b_g}\operatorname{in}_{\preceq_Y}(h_g).
\]
Since neither $h_g$ nor $f$ involves $t$, it follows that
\[
  \operatorname{in}_{\preceq_Y}(h_g)\mid\operatorname{in}_{\preceq_Y}(f).
\]
This proves the claim.
After forgetting the grading coordinate corresponding to $t$,
each $h_g$ remains homogeneous and multilinear.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma:3 times 4}

  Let $Y = (y_{i,j})$ be a $3 \times 4$ generic matrix with $\deg(y_{i,j}) = \mathbf{e}_j$.
  Let $p_1$ and $p_2$ be
  \begin{equation*}
    p_1 = \det Y_{[3], \{2,3,4\}} \qquad p_2 = \det Y_{[3], \{1,3,4\}}.
  \end{equation*}
  Then, the ideal $I = (p_1, p_2)$ does not have any multilinear universal Gr\"{o}bner  basis.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Put $S=\Bbbk[y_{i,j}: 1 \leq i \leq 3,1 \leq j \leq 4]$.
Since $\deg p_1=\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{e}_1$ and $\deg p_2=\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{e}_2$,
the only nonzero multilinear homogeneous components of $I$ are $I_{\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{e}_1}, I_{\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{e}_2}$ and $I_{\mathbf{1}}$.

Let $\preceq$ be the lexicographic order induced by natural order of the variables.
The first $S$-polynomial is
\[
  f:=S_{\preceq}(p_2,p_1)
    =y_{1,2}p_2-y_{1,1}p_1.
\]
A direct expansion gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(p_1)
    &=y_{1,2}y_{2,3}y_{3,4},\\
  \operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(p_2)
    &=y_{1,1}y_{2,3}y_{3,4},\\
  \operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(f)
    &=y_{1,1}y_{1,3}y_{2,2}y_{3,4}.
\end{aligned}
\]
In what follows, the $S$-polynomial computations are carried out in degrees that are not multilinear.
Indeed, the degrees of the next $S$-polynomials $S_{\preceq}(f,p_1)$ and $S_{\preceq}(f,p_2)$ are not multilinear.
Therefore, among the minimal generators of the initial ideal $\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(I)$, the multilinear ones are
$\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(p_1)$, $\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(p_2)$, and $\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(f)$.

Set
\begin{equation*}
  h = S_{\preceq}(f,p_2) = y_{2,3}f - y_{2,2}y_{1,3}p_2.
\end{equation*}
Then, we have $\deg(h) = (1,1,2,1)$ and $\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(h) = y_{1,1}y_{1,3}y_{2,2}y_{2,4}y_{3,3}.$
In particular, $\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(h) \in \operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(I)$ is not multilinear.
However, $\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(h)$ is not divisible by any of
$\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(p_1)$, $\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(p_2)$, and $\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(f)$.
Therefore, among the minimal generators of $\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(I)$, there exists a non-multilinear monomial.
Thus, the ideal $I$ has no multilinear Gr\"{o}bner  basis with respect
to $\preceq$.
In particular, $I$ has no multilinear universal
Gr\"{o}bner  basis.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:graph-classification}
Suppose that $n\geq5$ and $d_G(v)\in\{0,1,n-2,n-1\}$ for all $v$.
Then $G$ has one of the following forms:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item a matching, together with any isolated vertices;
\item $K_{1,n-1}$, a star with $n-1$ leaves;
\item $K_{1,n-2}+K_1$, consisting of a star with $n-2$ leaves together with one isolated vertex;
\item $K_{n-1}+K_1$, consisting of a complete graph on $[n]\setminus\{v\}$ together with the isolated vertex $v$;
\item a complete graph on $[n]\setminus\{v\}$ with $v$ adjacent to exactly one other vertex;
\item $K_n$ with the edges of a matching removed.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Set
\[
  V_{\text{high}}
  =\{v\in V(G):d_G(v)\ge n-2\},
  \qquad
  V_{\text{low}}
  =\{v\in V(G):d_G(v)\le1\},
\]
and write
\[
  h=|V_{\text{high}}|,
  \qquad
  \ell=|V_{\text{low}}|.
\]
By the degree condition, these sets form a partition of $V(G)$.
In particular, $h+\ell=n$.

We first show that $h\le1$ or $\ell\le1$.
Suppose that $h,\ell\ge2$.
Let
\[
  A=
    \{(u,v)\in V_{\text{high}}\times V_{\text{low}}:
      \{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
\]
and $a = |A|$.

Each vertex in $V_{\text{low}}$ has degree at most one.
If a vertex $v \in V_{\text{low}}$ is incident to an edge in $A$,
then there exists exactly one vertex
$u \in V_{\text{high}}$ such that $(u,v) \in A$.
Therefore we have
\[
  a
  =\sum_{v\in V_{\text{low}}}
    |N_G(v)\cap V_{\text{high}}|
  \le\ell.
\]
On the other hand, each vertex in $V_{\text{high}}$
has degree at least $n-2$ and has at most $h-1$
neighbors in $V_{\text{high}}$.
It consequently has at least $(n-2)-(h-1)=\ell-1$
neighbors in $V_{\text{low}}$.
Therefore, if a vertex $u \in V_{\text{high}}$ is incident to an edge in $A$,
there are at least $\ell - 1$ vertices $v \in V_{\text{low}}$ such that $(u,v) \in A$.
We have
\[
  a
  =\sum_{u\in V_{\text{high}}}
    |N_G(u)\cap V_{\text{low}}|
  \ge h(\ell-1).
\]
Combining the two inequalities, we obtain $h(\ell-1)\le a\le\ell$, and hence
\begin{equation*}
  (h-1)(\ell-1)\le1.
\end{equation*}
Since $h,\ell\ge2$ are integers, this forces $h=\ell=2$.
Thus $n=h+\ell=4$. It contradicts $n\ge5$.
Therefore, we have $h\le1$ or $\ell\le1$.

If $h=0$,
every vertex has degree at most one, so no two distinct
edges share a vertex.
Hence $G$ is a matching, with isolated vertices allowed.
Therefore, $G$ is as in case~\textup{(a)}.

We consider the case $h=1$.
Let $V_{\text{high}}=\{v\}$.
Then $d_G(v)$ is either $n-1$ or $n-2$.
Every neighbor of $v$ belongs to $V_{\text{low}}$
and already has an incident edge joining it to $v$.
Thus none of these neighbors can be incident to another edge.

If $d_G(v)=n-1$, it follows that $G$ is as in case~\textup{(b)}.
If $d_G(v)=n-2$, let $w$ be the unique vertex other than $v$
that is not adjacent to $v$.
The vertex $w$ cannot be adjacent to any neighbor of $v$,
since each such neighbor already has degree one.
Consequently, $w$ is isolated, and $G$ is as in case~\textup{(c)}.

We consider the case $\ell=1$.
Let $V_{\text{low}}=\{v\}$.

If $v$ is isolated, every vertex in $V_{\text{high}}$ is not adjacent to $v$.
Since a vertex in $V_{\text{high}}$ has at most one non-neighbor among the
other vertices of $G$, it must be adjacent to every other
vertex in $V_{\text{high}}$.
Therefore, $G$ is as in case~\textup{(d)}.

If $d_G(v)=1$, let $w$ be its unique neighbor.
No vertex $u \in V_{\text{high}}$ with $u\neq w$ is adjacent to $v$,
so it must be adjacent to every other vertex in $V_{\text{high}}$,
including $w$.
This also shows that $w$ is adjacent to all other high vertices.
Therefore, $G$ is as in case~\textup{(e)}.

If $\ell=0$,
every vertex has degree at least $n-2$.
Hence
\[
  d_{\overline G}(v)=n-1-d_G(v)\le1
  \qquad\text{for every }v\in V(G).
\]
Therefore, $\overline G$ is a matching, with isolated vertices
allowed, and $G$ is as in case~\textup{(f)}.

\label{cor:matching-general}
If $n\geq6$ and $G$ is a matching with at least two edges, then
$J_{c}(G)$ does not have any multilinear universal Gr\"{o}bner  basis.
In particular, $J_{c}(G)$ is not CS.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We assume that $J_{c}(G)$ has a multilinear universal Gr\"{o}bner  basis.
If $G$ has exactly two edges, let $W \subset [n]$ be a subset consisting of
their four endpoints and two
isolated vertices. If $G$ has at least three edges, let $W \subset [n]$ be a subset
consisting of
the six endpoints of three of them. In either case the induced six-vertex graph $G[W]$
is, after relabeling, one of the graphs in Lemma~\ref{lemma:matching-six}.
By Lemma~\ref{lemma:induced}, the ideal $J_c(G[W])$ has a multilinear universal Gr\"{o}bner 
basis. However, this contradicts Lemma~\ref{lemma:matching-six}.

\label{lem:five-matching-CS}
If $n=5$ and $G$ is a matching consisting of two edges, then $J_{c}(G)$ is CS.

It suffices to consider the graph $G$ with the edge set $E(G) = \{\{1,2\}, \{3,4\}\}$.
  Then,
  \begin{equation*}
    J_{c}(G) = (\mu_{12}) + (\mu_{34}).
  \end{equation*}
  The degree of $\mu_{12}$ is $\mathbf{e}_3+\mathbf{e}_4+\mathbf{e}_5$.
  The degree of $\mu_{34}$ is $\mathbf{e}_1+\mathbf{e}_2+\mathbf{e}_5$.
  By Proposition~\ref{proposition:connect one column}, the ideal $J_{c}(G)$ is CS.

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
\noindent\textbf{(b)}:
If $G$ is a complete star $K_{1, n-1}$ centered at $v$, then $J_{c}(G)=J_v$,
which is CS.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(c)}:
Suppose that $G$ is a star $K_{1,n-2}$ centered at $v$,
and let $w$ be the unique vertex that is not adjacent to $v$.
Then $J_{c}(G)=(\mu_{vj}:j\in U_v\setminus\{w\}).$

Consider the homogeneous decomposition $J_v=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{a} \in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n}(J_v)_\mathbf{a}.$
Since $\mathfrak c_w$ is generated by all variables in column $w$,
\[
  (\mathfrak c_w)_\mathbf{a}=
  \begin{cases}
    S_\mathbf{a}, & a_w>0,\\
    0, & a_w=0,
  \end{cases}
\]
where $a_w$ is the $w$th component of $\mathbf{a}$.
Consequently,
\[
  J_v\cap\mathfrak c_w
  =
  \bigoplus_{\substack{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\\
                       a_w>0}}
    (J_v)_\mathbf{a}.
\]
Thus $J_v\cap\mathfrak c_w$ is obtained by retaining precisely
the components of $J_v$ whose degree in column $w$ is positive.

We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:almost-star}
  J_{c}(G)=J_v\cap\mathfrak c_w.
\end{equation}
For every $j\in U_v\setminus\{w\}$, the minor $\mu_{vj}$
uses column $w$, so it has degree one in the $w$th column.
Hence every generator of $J_{c}(G)$ belongs to
$J_v\cap\mathfrak c_w$.

For the reverse inclusion, it suffices to take a homogeneous
$f\in(J_v)_\mathbf{a}$ with $a_w>0$.
Since $J_v$ is generated by the homogeneous polynomials
$\mu_{vj}$, we may write
\[
  f=
  \sum_{j\in U_v\setminus\{w\}}h_j\mu_{vj}
  +h_w\mu_{vw},
\]
where each nonzero $h_j$ is homogeneous of degree
$\mathbf{a}-\deg(\mu_{vj})$.
The terms in the first sum belong to $J_{c}(G)$.
Since $\mu_{vw}$ does not use column $w$, the
$w$th component of $\deg(h_w)$ is positive.
Thus $h_w\in\mathfrak c_w$.

At the vertex $v$, the only missing neighbor is $w$.
By~\eqref{eq:one-missing-minor}, we have
\[
  x_{aw}\mu_{vw}\in J_{c}(G)
  \qquad(a\in[m]),
\]
and hence
\[
  \mathfrak c_w\mu_{vw}\subset J_{c}(G).
\]
It follows that $h_w\mu_{vw}\in J_{c}(G)$, and therefore $f\in J_{c}(G)$.
This proves~\eqref{eq:almost-star}.
Since $J_v$ is CS, Lemma~\ref{lem:filter} shows that $J_{c}(G)$ is CS.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(d)}:
Suppose that $G[U_v]$ is complete and $v$ is isolated.
Using the same argument as in the previous case, we prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isolated}
  J_{c}(G)=J\cap\mathfrak c_v
  =
  \bigoplus_{\substack{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\\
                       a_v>0}}
    J_\mathbf{a}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, every generator $\mu_{ij}$ of $J_{c}(G)$ has $i,j\neq v$,
so it uses column $v$.
This proves $J_{c}(G)\subset J\cap\mathfrak c_v$.

Conversely, let $f\in J_\mathbf{a}$ be homogeneous with
$a_v>0$.
Express $f$ as a sum of homogeneous multiples of the maximal
minors of $X$.
The minors not belonging to the generating set of $J_{c}(G)$
are precisely $\mu_{vj}$ with $j\in U_v$.
Since each $\mu_{vj}$ has degree zero in column $v$,
its coefficient in this expression belongs to $\mathfrak c_v$.

For every $j\in U_v$, the only missing neighbor of $j$ is $v$.
Thus~\eqref{eq:one-missing-minor} gives
\[
  x_{av}\mu_{vj}\in J_{c}(G)
  \qquad(a\in[m],\ j\in U_v).
\]
Therefore, $f$ belongs to $J_{c}(G)$,
proving~\eqref{eq:isolated}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:filter}, applied to $J$ and $\mathfrak c_v$,
proves that $J_{c}(G)$ is CS.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(e)}:
Suppose that $G[U_v]$ is complete and $N_G(v)=\{w\}$.
Adding the edge $vw$ to the graph in the preceding case gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pendant}
  J_{c}(G)=(J\cap\mathfrak c_v)+(\mu_{vw}).
\end{equation}
Since $\mu_{vw}\in J$ and $\mu_{vw}$ does not use column $v$,
we obtain
\[
  (J_{c}(G))_\mathbf{a}=
  \begin{cases}
    J_\mathbf{a}, & a_v>0,\\
    ((\mu_{vw}))_\mathbf{a}, & a_v=0.
  \end{cases}
\]

Every coordinate change $\gamma \in \mathcal{G}$ preserves
the grading.
Hence
\[
  (\gamma J_{c}(G))_\mathbf{a}=
  \begin{cases}
    (\gamma J)_\mathbf{a}, & a_v>0,\\
    ((\gamma\mu_{vw}))_\mathbf{a}, & a_v=0.
  \end{cases}
\]
Choosing $\gamma$ sufficiently general,
we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pendant-gin}
  \operatorname{gin}_{\preceq}(J_{c}(G))
  =
  \bigl(\operatorname{gin}_{\preceq}(J)\cap\mathfrak c_v\bigr)
  +\bigl(\operatorname{in}_{\preceq}(\gamma\mu_{vw})\bigr).
\end{equation}

Since $J$ is CS, the first summand is a squarefree monomial ideal.
Moreover, $\gamma\mu_{vw}$ has degree
$\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{e}_v-\mathbf{e}_w$, so its initial monomial is a squarefree monomial.
Thus the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:pendant-gin} is generated
by squarefree monomials.
Therefore, $J_{c}(G)$ is CS.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(f)}:
Let $G=K_n\setminus M$, where $M$ is a matching, and set
\[
  Q_M=\bigcap_{\{i,j\}\in M}(\mathfrak c_i+\mathfrak c_j),
\]
with the empty intersection interpreted as $S$.

For each $\{i,j\}\in M$, we have
\[
  (\mathfrak c_i+\mathfrak c_j)_\mathbf{a}=
  \begin{cases}
    S_\mathbf{a}, & a_i+a_j>0,\\
    0, & a_i=a_j=0.
  \end{cases}
\]
Consequently,
\[
  (Q_M)_\mathbf{a}=
  \begin{cases}
    S_\mathbf{a},
      & a_i+a_j>0
        \text{ for every }\{i,j\}\in M,\\
    0, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\]
and therefore
\[
  J\cap Q_M
  =
  \bigoplus_{\substack{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\\
    a_i+a_j>0\ \text{for every }\{i,j\}\in M}}
    J_\mathbf{a}.
\]
Thus $J\cap Q_M$ retains precisely those components of $J$
whose degrees are positive on at least one endpoint of
every edge in $M$.

We claim that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deleted-matching}
  J_{c}(G)=J\cap Q_M.
\end{equation}
First, let $\mu_{ab}$ be a generator of $J_{c}(G)$, and fix a
edge $\{i,j\}\in M$.
Since $\{a,b\}\neq\{i,j\}$, the column set
$[n]\setminus\{a,b\}$ contains at least one of $i,j$.
Therefore, we have $\mu_{ab}\in\mathfrak c_i+\mathfrak c_j$ for every $\{i,j\} \in M$
and we have $J_{c}(G)\subset J\cap Q_M.$

For the reverse inclusion, we first consider a edge $\{i,j\}\in M$.
Since $M$ is a matching, $j$ is the only non-neighbor of $i$,
and $i$ is the only non-neighbor of $j$.
Applying~\eqref{eq:one-missing-minor} at these two vertices yields
\[
  x_{ai}\mu_{ij}\in J_{c}(G),
  \qquad
  x_{aj}\mu_{ij}\in J_{c}(G)
  \qquad(a\in[m]).
\]
It follows that
\[
  (\mathfrak c_i+\mathfrak c_j)\mu_{ij}\subset J_{c}(G).
\]

Now take a nonzero homogeneous polynomial
$f\in(J\cap Q_M)_\mathbf{a}$.
By the description of $(Q_M)_\mathbf{a}$, its degree satisfies
$a_i+a_j>0$ for every $\{i,j\}\in M.$
Since $f\in J$, we may write
\[
  f=
  \sum_{\{a,b\}\in E(G)}h_{ab}\mu_{ab}
  +
  \sum_{\{i,j\}\in M}h_{ij}\mu_{ij},
\]
choosing each nonzero coefficient to be homogeneous so that
every nonzero summand has degree $\mathbf{a}$.
All summands in the first sum belong to $J_{c}(G)$.

We consider the summands in the second sum. For each $\{i,j\} \in M$,
the minor $\mu_{ij}$ has degree zero in both columns $i$ and $j$.
Thus the $i$th and $j$th components of $\deg(h_{ij})$
are $a_i$ and $a_j$, respectively.
Since $a_i + a_j > 0$,  we have $h_{ij}\in\mathfrak c_i+\mathfrak c_j$.
Thus, we have
\[
  h_{ij}\mu_{ij}\in (\mathfrak c_i+\mathfrak c_j)\mu_{ij}\subset J_{c}(G).
\]
Therefore, every summand belongs to $J_{c}(G)$, proving
$f\in J_{c}(G)$ and establishing~\eqref{eq:deleted-matching}.

Since $Q_M$ satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:filter},
and since $J$ is CS, $J_{c}(G)$ is CS.
