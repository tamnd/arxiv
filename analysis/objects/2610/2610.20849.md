<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{pra2}
Let $x \in X \setminus \{0\}$. For any two norming functionals $T_x, S_x \in T(x)$ and any $v \in \operatorname{span}\{x\}$, we have
\[
T_x(v) = S_x(v).
\]
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Write $v = \lambda x$ for some $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$. Since $T_x(x) = S_x(x) = \|x\|$,
\[
T_x(v) = T_x(\lambda x) = \lambda T_x(x) = \lambda \|x\|
\]
and
\[
S_x(v) = S_x(\lambda x) = \lambda S_x(x) = \lambda \|x\|.
\]
Thus $T_x(v) = S_x(v)$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{pra3}
Let $F \in H(\mathbb{B})$ be normalized and locally biholomorphic, and suppose that
\[
F(x) = g(x)x,
\]
where $g : \mathbb{B} \to \mathbb{C}$ is holomorphic. We require
\begin{equation}\label{pra4}
g(x) \neq 0 \qquad (x \in \mathbb{B}).
\end{equation}
Since $g(0) = 1$ by normalization, this condition is nontrivial only for $x \neq 0$. For $\alpha \in (0,1]$, we say that $F$ belongs to the scalar-profile strongly starlike class of order $\alpha$, denoted by $\mathcal{S}^*_\alpha(\mathbb{B})$, if for every $x \in \mathbb{B} \setminus \{0\}$ and every $T_x \in T(x)$,
\begin{equation}\label{pra5}
\left|
\arg\left(T_x\bigl((DF(x))^{-1}F(x)\bigr)\right)
\right|
< \alpha \frac{\pi}{2}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
\label{pra6}
Condition \eqref{pra4} ensures that the argument in \eqref{pra5} is defined. Indeed, for $F(x) = g(x)x$ with $x \in \mathbb{B} \setminus \{0\}$, differentiation gives
\[
DF(x)x = g(x)x + [Dg(x)x]x = \bigl(g(x) + Dg(x)x\bigr)x.
\]
Since $F$ is locally biholomorphic, $DF(x)$ is invertible, so the eigenvalue $g(x) + Dg(x)x$ is nonzero. Hence
\[
(DF(x))^{-1}x = \frac{1}{g(x) + Dg(x)x}\,x,
\]
and therefore
\[
(DF(x))^{-1}F(x) = g(x)(DF(x))^{-1}x = \frac{g(x)}{g(x) + Dg(x)x}\,x = \lambda(x)\,x
\]
with $\lambda(x) = g(x)/(g(x) + Dg(x)x)$. Since $g(x) \neq 0$ by \eqref{pra4} and the denominator is nonzero, we have $\lambda(x) \neq 0$. Thus $T_x((DF(x))^{-1}F(x)) = \lambda(x)\|x\|$ is nonzero, and its argument is defined.

When $X = \mathbb{C}$ and $\mathbb{B} = \mathbb{D}$, a normalized mapping becomes an analytic function
\[
f(z) = z + \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} a_n z^n.
\]
The unique norming functional is $T_z(v) = v|z|/z$. Hence
\[
T_z\bigl((DF(z))^{-1}f(z)\bigr)
= T_z\left(\frac{f(z)}{f'(z)}\right)
= |z|\frac{f(z)}{z f'(z)}.
\]
Taking arguments, the positive factor $|z|$ cancels, and we recover the classical definition
\[
\left|\arg\left(\frac{z f'(z)}{f(z)}\right)\right| < \alpha \frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
This is the definition of $\mathcal{S}^*_\alpha$.

For the scalar-profile subclass, the vector $(DF(x))^{-1}F(x)$ lies in $\operatorname{span}\{x\}$. Since every $T_x \in T(x)$ satisfies $T_x(x) = \|x\|$, Lemma \ref{pra2} shows that the evaluation on this one-dimensional subspace does not depend on the choice of $T_x$. Thus condition \eqref{pra5} is independent of the particular norming functional selected.

\label{pra7}
If $F(x) = g(x)x$ is normalized, then $DF(0) = I$. Differentiating at $x = 0$ gives
\[
DF(0)v = g(0)v + [Dg(0)v]\,0 = g(0)v.
\]
Since $DF(0) = I$, we obtain $g(0) = 1$. Thus the normalization $g(0) = 1$ is automatic rather than an additional assumption. It is retained below as a convenient explicit normalization.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{pra8}
Let $F(x) = g(x)x$ be a normalized holomorphic mapping on $\mathbb{B}$. Then for every $x_0 \in \partial \mathbb{B}$ and every $n \in \mathbb{N}$,
\begin{equation}\label{pra9}
\frac{1}{n!} D^n F(0)(x_0^n)
=
\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\bigl(D^{n-1} g(0)(x_0^{n-1})\bigr) x_0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $G(x) = x$ be the identity mapping. Then $DG(x) = I$ and $D^k G(x) = 0$ for $k \ge 2$. By the Leibniz rule,
\[
D^n F(0)(x_0^n)
=
\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}
D^{n-k} g(0)(x_0^{n-k}) D^k G(0)(x_0^k).
\]
Since $G(0) = 0$ and $D^k G(0) = 0$ for $k \ge 2$, only the term with $k = 1$ remains. Hence
\[
D^n F(0)(x_0^n)
=
\binom{n}{1} D^{n-1} g(0)(x_0^{n-1}) DG(0) x_0
=
n D^{n-1} g(0)(x_0^{n-1}) x_0.
\]
Dividing by $n!$ gives \eqref{pra9}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{pra10}
Let $F(x) = g(x)x$ be normalized and locally biholomorphic on $\mathbb{B}$, satisfying \eqref{pra4}. Let $x_0 \in \partial \mathbb{B}$ and let $f(\xi) = g(\xi x_0)\xi$. Then for every $\xi \in \mathbb{D} \setminus \{0\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{pra11}
T_{x_0}\bigl((DF(\xi x_0))^{-1} F(\xi x_0)\bigr) = \frac{f(\xi)}{f'(\xi)}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Differentiating $F(x) = g(x)x$ at $x = \xi x_0$ gives
\[
DF(\xi x_0)v = g(\xi x_0)v + [Dg(\xi x_0)v]\xi x_0.
\]
Since $F$ is locally biholomorphic, $DF(\xi x_0)$ is invertible. Let
\[
v = (DF(\xi x_0))^{-1} F(\xi x_0).
\]
Since $T_{x_0}(x_0) = 1$, the kernel and span form a direct sum. Indeed, if $v \in \ker T_{x_0} \cap \operatorname{span}\{x_0\}$, then $v = \mu x_0$ for some $\mu \in \mathbb{C}$ and $0 = T_{x_0}(v) = \mu T_{x_0}(x_0) = \mu$, so $v = 0$. Moreover, every $v \in X$ can be written as
\[
v = \bigl(v - T_{x_0}(v)x_0\bigr) + T_{x_0}(v)x_0,
\]
with $v - T_{x_0}(v)x_0 \in \ker T_{x_0}$ and $T_{x_0}(v)x_0 \in \operatorname{span}\{x_0\}$. Thus $X = \ker T_{x_0} \oplus \operatorname{span}\{x_0\}$, and we may write $v = \lambda x_0 + w$ with $w \in \ker T_{x_0}$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$.

Substituting into
\[
DF(\xi x_0)v = F(\xi x_0) = g(\xi x_0)\xi x_0
\]
gives
\[
g(\xi x_0)w + [Dg(\xi x_0)w]\xi x_0
+ \lambda\bigl(g(\xi x_0) + \xi Dg(\xi x_0)x_0\bigr)x_0
= g(\xi x_0)\xi x_0.
\]

Comparing the two components with respect to the decomposition $X = \ker T_{x_0} \oplus \operatorname{span}\{x_0\}$: the term $g(\xi x_0)w$ lies in $\ker T_{x_0}$, since $w \in \ker T_{x_0}$ and scalar multiplication preserves the kernel. All remaining terms are multiples of $x_0$, hence lie in $\operatorname{span}\{x_0\}$. Projecting onto $\ker T_{x_0}$ gives
\[
g(\xi x_0)w = 0.
\]
Since $g(\xi x_0) \neq 0$ by \eqref{pra4}, we obtain $w = 0$. In particular, $Dg(\xi x_0)w = 0$. Projecting now onto $\operatorname{span}\{x_0\}$ and using $w = 0$,
\[
\lambda\bigl(g(\xi x_0) + \xi Dg(\xi x_0)x_0\bigr) = g(\xi x_0)\xi.
\]

On the other hand, direct differentiation of $f(\xi) = g(\xi x_0)\xi$ gives
\[
f'(\xi) = g(\xi x_0) + \xi Dg(\xi x_0)x_0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
DF(\xi x_0)x_0
=
\bigl[g(\xi x_0) + \xi Dg(\xi x_0)x_0\bigr]x_0
=
f'(\xi)x_0,
\]
so $f'(\xi)$ is the eigenvalue of the invertible operator $DF(\xi x_0)$ on the radial direction $x_0$, and hence $f'(\xi) \neq 0$. Therefore
\[
\lambda = \frac{\xi g(\xi x_0)}{g(\xi x_0) + \xi Dg(\xi x_0)x_0} = \frac{f(\xi)}{f'(\xi)}.
\]
Since $T_{x_0}(v) = \lambda$, the result follows.

\label{pra12}
Let $F \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha(\mathbb{B})$ with $F(x) = g(x)x$, and let $x_0 \in \partial \mathbb{B}$. Then the slice function $f$ belongs to $\mathcal{S}^*_\alpha$, and
\begin{equation}\label{pra13}
A_n = a_n \qquad (n \in \mathbb{N}),
\end{equation}
where $a_n$ are the Taylor coefficients of $f$.
<!-- end theo-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $F \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha(\mathbb{B})$. Fix $x_0 \in \partial \mathbb{B}$. For $\xi \in \mathbb{D} \setminus \{0\}$, the sector condition \eqref{pra5} gives
\begin{equation}\label{pra14}
\left|
\arg\left(
T_{\xi x_0}\bigl((DF(\xi x_0))^{-1} F(\xi x_0)\bigr)
\right)
\right|
< \alpha \frac{\pi}{2}.
\end{equation}
By Lemma \ref{pra10}, the vector $v = (DF(\xi x_0))^{-1} F(\xi x_0)$ lies in $\operatorname{span}\{x_0\}$. Hence, by Lemma \ref{pra2}, the evaluation does not depend on the choice of the norming functional. For any $T_{\xi x_0} \in T(\xi x_0)$, we have
\[
T_{\xi x_0}(v) = \lambda T_{\xi x_0}(x_0) = \lambda \frac{1}{\xi} T_{\xi x_0}(\xi x_0).
\]
Since $T_{\xi x_0}(\xi x_0) = |\xi|$, this becomes
\begin{equation}\label{pra15}
T_{\xi x_0}(v)
=
\lambda \frac{|\xi|}{\xi}
=
\frac{|\xi| g(\xi x_0)}{g(\xi x_0) + \xi Dg(\xi x_0)x_0}.
\end{equation}
Also
\[
\frac{f(\xi)}{\xi f'(\xi)}
=
\frac{g(\xi x_0)}{g(\xi x_0) + \xi Dg(\xi x_0)x_0}.
\]
Thus
\[
T_{\xi x_0}(v) = |\xi| \frac{f(\xi)}{\xi f'(\xi)}.
\]
Since $|\xi| > 0$ is a positive real number, multiplication by $|\xi|$ does not change the argument. Therefore
\[
\left|
\arg\bigl(T_{\xi x_0}(v)\bigr)
\right|
=
\left|
\arg\left(\frac{f(\xi)}{\xi f'(\xi)}\right)
\right|
=
\left|
\arg\left(\frac{\xi f'(\xi)}{f(\xi)}\right)
\right|.
\]
The last identity holds because $|1/w| = 1/|w|$ and $|\arg(1/w)| = |\arg w|$ for any nonzero complex number $w$. Combining with \eqref{pra14},
\[
\left|\arg\left(\frac{\xi f'(\xi)}{f(\xi)}\right)\right| < \alpha \frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
Hence $f \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha$.

Next, write
\[
g(\xi x_0) = 1 + Dg(0)(x_0)\xi + \frac{1}{2!} D^2 g(0)(x_0^2)\xi^2 + \cdots
+ \frac{1}{(n-1)!} D^{n-1} g(0)(x_0^{n-1})\xi^{n-1} + \cdots.
\]
Then
\[
f(\xi) = g(\xi x_0)\xi = \xi + \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} a_n \xi^n,
\]
where
\[
a_n = \frac{1}{(n-1)!} D^{n-1} g(0)(x_0^{n-1}).
\]
By Proposition \ref{pra8} and $T_{x_0}(x_0) = 1$,
\[
A_n = \frac{1}{n!} T_{x_0}\bigl(D^n F(0)(x_0^n)\bigr)
= \frac{1}{(n-1)!} D^{n-1} g(0)(x_0^{n-1}).
\]
This is exactly $a_n$. Hence $A_n = a_n$.

\label{pra16}
Let $F \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha(\mathbb{B})$ with $F(x) = g(x)x$, and fix $x_0 \in \partial \mathbb{B}$. Let $P$ be any polynomial in finitely many complex variables. Then
\[
P(A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_m) = P(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_m),
\]
where the directional coefficients $A_n$ are defined by \eqref{pra1} and $a_n$ are the Taylor coefficients of the slice function $f(\xi) = g(\xi x_0)\xi$. Consequently, every known coefficient inequality for the one-variable class $\mathcal{S}^*_\alpha$ transfers to the corresponding directional functional on the scalar-profile class $\mathcal{S}^*_\alpha(\mathbb{B})$, provided the functional involves only the coefficients $a_n$.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{pra18}
Let $f \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha$, let $u \in \partial \mathbb{B}$, and let $T_u \in T(u)$. Define the analytic function $h : \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{C}$ by
\[
h(z) = \frac{f(z)}{z} \quad (z \neq 0), \qquad h(0) = 1,
\]
and the auxiliary function
\[
\Phi(z) :=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{f(z)}{z f'(z)}, & z \neq 0,\\[2mm]
1, & z = 0.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
F_f(x) := h(T_u(x))\, x, \qquad x \in \mathbb{B},
\]
belongs to $\mathcal{S}^*_\alpha(\mathbb{B})$. Moreover, along the direction $x_0 = u$, the directional coefficients of $F_f$ coincide with the Taylor coefficients of $f$.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Since $f$ is analytic on $\mathbb{D}$ with $f(0) = 0$ and $f'(0) = 1$, the function $h$ extends to an analytic function on $\mathbb{D}$ with $h(0) = 1$. For $x \in \mathbb{B}$, we have $|T_u(x)| \leq \|T_u\|\,\|x\| = \|x\| < 1$, so $T_u(x) \in \mathbb{D}$ and the composition $g_f := h \circ T_u$ is well defined and holomorphic on $\mathbb{B}$. Thus $F_f = g_f \cdot \mathrm{Id}$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb{B}$ with $F_f(0) = 0$ and $DF_f(0) = g_f(0)I = I$. Hence $F_f$ is normalized, and by Remark \ref{pra7} we have $g_f(0) = 1$.

We claim $g_f(x) \neq 0$ for every $x \in \mathbb{B}$. If $T_u(x) \neq 0$, then $g_f(x) = f(T_u(x))/T_u(x)$. Since $f \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha$, the sector condition
\[
\left|\arg\frac{z f'(z)}{f(z)}\right| < \frac{\alpha\pi}{2} \leq \frac{\pi}{2}
\]
for $z \neq 0$ implies $\operatorname{Re}\bigl(z f'(z)/f(z)\bigr) > 0$. In particular, $z f'(z)/f(z)$ is a nonzero complex number, so $f(z) \neq 0$ for $z \neq 0$. Hence $g_f(x) \neq 0$ whenever $T_u(x) \neq 0$. If $T_u(x) = 0$, then $g_f(x) = h(0) = 1 \neq 0$. Thus $g_f$ is nonvanishing, and condition \eqref{pra4} holds.

Next we compute the Fr\'echet derivative of $F_f$. For $x \in \mathbb{B}$ and $v \in X$,
\[
DF_f(x)v = g_f(x)v + [Dg_f(x)v]\,x.
\]
Since $g_f = h \circ T_u$ and $T_u$ is linear, $Dg_f(x)v = h'(T_u(x))\,T_u(v)$. Writing $z = T_u(x)$, we obtain
\[
DF_f(x) = g_f(x)I + h'(z)\,(x \otimes T_u),
\]
where $(x \otimes T_u)(v) := T_u(v)\,x$ denotes the rank-one operator determined by $x$ and $T_u$.

This is a bounded linear operator on $X$ of the form $A + u_0 \otimes \phi$, with $A = g_f(x)I$, $u_0 = h'(z)x$, and $\phi = T_u$. By the Sherman--Morrison formula for rank-one perturbations,
\[
(A + u_0 \otimes \phi)^{-1}
=
A^{-1} - \frac{A^{-1}u_0 \otimes \phi A^{-1}}{1 + \phi(A^{-1}u_0)},
\]
provided the denominator is nonzero. Here
\[
A^{-1}u_0 = \frac{h'(z)}{g_f(x)}x,
\qquad
\phi(A^{-1}u_0) = T_u\!\left(\frac{h'(z)}{g_f(x)}x\right) = \frac{h'(z)z}{g_f(x)},
\]
so
\[
1 + \phi(A^{-1}u_0) = \frac{g_f(x) + h'(z)z}{g_f(x)}.
\]
Since $g_f(x) = h(z)$ and $z = T_u(x)$, we have
\[
g_f(x) + h'(z)z = h(z) + h'(z)z = \frac{d}{dz}\bigl(zh(z)\bigr) = f'(z).
\]
Thus
\[
1 + \phi(A^{-1}u_0) = \frac{f'(z)}{g_f(x)}.
\]
Since $f \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha$ and $z \in \mathbb{D}$, the sector condition gives $\operatorname{Re}\bigl(z f'(z)/f(z)\bigr) > 0$, so $f'(z) \neq 0$. Together with $g_f(x) \neq 0$, the denominator is nonzero, and $DF_f(x)$ is invertible for every $x \in \mathbb{B}$. Hence $F_f$ is locally biholomorphic.

It remains to verify the sector condition. Using the Sherman--Morrison formula and $F_f(x) = g_f(x)x$,
\[
(DF_f(x))^{-1}F_f(x)
=
g_f(x)\left[\frac{1}{g_f(x)}I - \frac{h'(z)}{g_f(x)^2(1 + h'(z)z/g_f(x))}(x \otimes T_u)\right]x.
\]
Since $(x \otimes T_u)(x) = T_u(x)x = zx$, this simplifies to
\[
(DF_f(x))^{-1}F_f(x)
=
x - \frac{h'(z)z}{g_f(x)(1 + h'(z)z/g_f(x))}x
=
x \cdot \frac{g_f(x)}{g_f(x) + h'(z)z}
=
x \cdot \frac{h(z)}{f'(z)}
=
\Phi(z)\,x,
\]
where we have used $g_f(x) = h(z)$ and $g_f(x) + h'(z)z = f'(z)$, together with the definition of $\Phi$. When $z = 0$, the formula reduces to $1 \cdot x$, consistent with $\Phi(0) = 1$.

Applying any $T_x \in T(x)$,
\[
T_x\bigl((DF_f(x))^{-1}F_f(x)\bigr)
=
\Phi(z)\,T_x(x)
=
\Phi(z)\,\|x\|.
\]
Since $\|x\| > 0$ for $x \neq 0$, taking arguments yields
\[
\left|\arg T_x\bigl((DF_f(x))^{-1}F_f(x)\bigr)\right|
=
\left|\arg \Phi(z)\right|
=
\left|\arg\frac{f(z)}{z f'(z)}\right|
=
\left|\arg\frac{z f'(z)}{f(z)}\right|
< \frac{\alpha\pi}{2},
\]
for $z \neq 0$, where the last inequality uses $f \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha$ and $z = T_u(x) \in \mathbb{D}$. When $z = 0$, the left-hand side is $|\arg 1| = 0 < \alpha\pi/2$. Hence $F_f$ satisfies the sector condition and belongs to $\mathcal{S}^*_\alpha(\mathbb{B})$.

Finally, along $x_0 = u$, we have $T_u(u) = \|u\| = 1$ and $g_f(\xi u) = h(\xi) = f(\xi)/\xi$ for $\xi \neq 0$, with $g_f(0) = 1$. Thus
\[
F_f(\xi u) = g_f(\xi u)\xi u = f(\xi)u.
\]
The slice function is $T_u(F_f(\xi u)) = f(\xi)T_u(u) = f(\xi)$. By Theorem \ref{pra12}, the directional coefficients of $F_f$ along $u$ equal the Taylor coefficients of $f$.

<!-- cor-2: body_md -->
\label{pra19}
Let $P$ be any polynomial in finitely many complex variables. Then
\[
\sup_{\substack{F \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha(\mathbb{B}),\\ x_0 \in \partial \mathbb{B},\ T_{x_0} \in T(x_0)}}
|P(A_1, \ldots, A_m)|
=
\sup_{f \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha} |P(a_1, \ldots, a_m)|,
\]
where the directional coefficients $A_n$ are computed for each $F$ along the direction $x_0$ with respect to the functional $T_{x_0}$, and $a_n$ denote the Taylor coefficients of $f$.
<!-- end cor-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For the inequality $\leq$: given $F \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha(\mathbb{B})$, a direction $x_0 \in \partial \mathbb{B}$, and $T_{x_0} \in T(x_0)$, the slice function $f$ belongs to $\mathcal{S}^*_\alpha$ by Theorem \ref{pra12}, and $P(A_1,\ldots,A_m) = P(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ by Corollary \ref{pra16}. Hence
\[
|P(A_1,\ldots,A_m)| \leq \sup_{f \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha} |P(a_1,\ldots,a_m)|.
\]
Taking the supremum over $F$, $x_0$, and $T_{x_0}$ gives the desired bound.

For the inequality $\geq$: given $f \in \mathcal{S}^*_\alpha$, choose any $u \in \partial \mathbb{B}$ and $T_u \in T(u)$. By Proposition \ref{pra18}, the lifting $F_f$ belongs to $\mathcal{S}^*_\alpha(\mathbb{B})$ and has directional coefficients along $u$ equal to the Taylor coefficients of $f$. Hence
\[
|P(a_1,\ldots,a_m)| = |P(A_1,\ldots,A_m)| \leq \sup_{\substack{F, x_0, T_{x_0}}} |P(A_1,\ldots,A_m)|.
\]
Taking the supremum over $f$ gives the reverse inequality.

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
By Theorem \ref{pra12}, the slice function $f(\xi) = g(\xi x_0)\xi$ belongs to $\mathcal{S}^*_\alpha$. The formulas \eqref{pra47}--\eqref{pra49} show that
\[
\Gamma_1 \Gamma_3 - \Gamma_2^2
=
\gamma_1(f)\gamma_3(f) - \gamma_2^2(f).
\]
Using the Carath\'eodory optimization from Theorem 1 of \cite{Kowalczyk2023}, with
\[
\frac{z f'(z)}{f(z)} = (p(z))^\alpha,
\]
one obtains
\[
|\Gamma_1 \Gamma_3 - \Gamma_2^2| \le \frac{1}{4}\alpha^2.
\]
For the symmetric mapping \eqref{pra43}, we have $A_2 = A_4 = 0$ and $A_3 = \alpha$, so
\[
\Gamma_1 = 0, \qquad \Gamma_2 = \frac{\alpha}{2}, \qquad \Gamma_3 = 0.
\]
Hence $\Gamma_1 \Gamma_3 - \Gamma_2^2 = -\alpha^2/4$, and the bound is attained.
