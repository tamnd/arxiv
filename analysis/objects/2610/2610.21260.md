<!-- defia-1: body_md -->
Let $f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{U})$ be such that $f(z)f'(z)/z \neq 0$, $z \in \mathbb U$. Then $f$ is said to be $\alpha$-convex in $\mathbb{U}$ if and only if
\begin{align}\label{Def1}
\Re\left\lbrace J_{\alpha}[f](z)\right\rbrace>0,\quad z\in\mathbb{U},
\end{align}
where
\begin{align*}
J_{\alpha}[f](z)=(1-\alpha)\frac{zf'(z)}{f(z)}+\alpha\left(1+\frac{zf''(z)}{f'(z)}\right).
\end{align*}
<!-- end defia-1 -->
<!-- rema-1: body_md -->
The assumption $f(z)f'(z)/z \neq 0$, $z \in \mathbb U$, in Definition A is redundant. In 1979, Sakaguchi and Fukui \emph{\cite{Sakaguchi-Fukui-1979}} established that $\Re\{J_{\alpha}[f](z)\}> 0$ for $z \in \mathbb U$, implies $f(z)f'(z)/z \neq 0$, $z \in \mathbb U$.
<!-- end rema-1 -->
<!-- theoa-1: body_md -->
\label{Theorem A}\emph{\cite{Miller-Mocanu-Reade-1973}} If $f\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{U})$, then it is starlike and univalent in $\mathbb{U}$. Moreover, if $\alpha\geq 1$, then $f$ is convex for $|z|<1$ and if $\alpha\leq -1$, then $1/f(1/z)$ is convex for $|z| > 1$.
<!-- end theoa-1 -->
<!-- theob-1: body_md -->
\emph{\cite{Todorov-1987}} The function $f\in \mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{U})$ for $\alpha>0$ if and only  if there exists a starlike function $F$ such that
\begin{align}\label{TheoB1}
f(z)=\left\lbrack\frac{1}{\alpha}\int\limits_{0}^{z}F^{1/\alpha}(\xi)\xi^{-1}{\rm d}\xi \right\rbrack^{\alpha}\quad z\in\mathbb{U},
\end{align}
where powers are meant as principal values.
<!-- end theob-1 -->
<!-- theoc-1: body_md -->
\emph{\cite{Miller-1973}} Let $f\in \mathcal{M}_\alpha(\mathbb{U})$ for $\alpha>0$ and be given by $f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_n(\alpha)z^n$. Then $|a_2(\alpha)|\leq 2/(1+\alpha)$. The inequality is sharp.
<!-- end theoc-1 -->
<!-- theod-1: body_md -->
\emph{\cite[Theorem 1]{Sokol-Thomas-2018}} Let $f \in \mathcal{M}_\alpha(\mathbb{U})$, $\alpha \ge 0$, be of the form \eqref{eq1}. Then
\begin{align}\label{TheoD1}
|a_2 a_4 - a_3^2| \le
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2(1 + 8\alpha + 3\alpha^2)}{(1 + 3\alpha)(2 + 15\alpha + 24\alpha^2 + 7\alpha^3)}, & \text{when } \alpha \in [0, 1], \\[10pt]
\dfrac{3 + 35\alpha + 126\alpha^2 + 162\alpha^3 + 79\alpha^4 + 27\alpha^5}{(1 + 2\alpha)^2 (1 + 3\alpha)(3 + 26\alpha + 42\alpha^2 + 18\alpha^3 + 7\alpha^4)}, & \text{when } \alpha \in [1, \infty).
