<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let us consider still the IFS 
$$\Phi = \{x \mapsto f_1(x) = x/2,x \mapsto f_2(x) = x/3 + 2/3\}.$$ 
Write $\xi = s e^{t}$ for some $s \in \mathbb{R}$ and $t > 0$ and consider the words $\mathcal{W}_t$ of potentially \textit{different }lengths where for all $\mathbf{a} \in \mathcal{W}_t$ we have 
$$2^{-k(\mathbf{a})} 3^{-n+k(\mathbf{a})} \approx e^{-t}.$$ 
Thus we replace in \eqref{eq:FourierBound} the average over $\{1,2\}^n$ by a weighted sum over $\mathcal{W}_t$:
\begin{align} \label{eq:FourierBoundStop} |\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|^2 \lesssim \int\hspace{-0.1in}\int\limits_{|x-y| > \delta} \sum_{ \mathbf{a} \in \mathcal{W}_t } 2^{-|\mathbf{a}|} e^{-2\pi i \xi 2^{-k(\mathbf{a})} 3^{-n+k(\mathbf{a})}  (x-y)} \, d\mu(x) \, d\mu(y) + \delta^{\varepsilon_1},
\end{align}
which is possible because the words $\mathcal{W}_t$ form a partition and splitting of the integration was done with some $\delta = \delta(\xi) \to 0$ as $|\xi| \to \infty$. The term $\delta^{\varepsilon_1}$ comes from the fact that $\mu$ is \textit{non-atomic} self-similar measure, so for all $\delta > 0$, we have 
$$\mu \times \mu((x,y) : |x-y| \leq \delta) = \int \mu(B(x,\delta)) \, d\mu(x) \lesssim \delta^{\varepsilon_1}$$ 
for some $\varepsilon_1 > 0$ \cite{FL}. Taking logarithms, this translates the study of the large $|\xi|$ limit in \eqref{eq:FourierBoundStop} to understanding the limit of the distributions 
$$\sum_{k = 1}^{n_{t}} X_k-t$$ 
as $t \to \infty$, where $X_k = \log 2$ with probability $1/2$ and $X_k = \log 3$ with probability $1/2$. Here $n_t := n_t(w)$, $w \in \{1,2\}^\mathbb{N}$, is the \textit{stopping time}, that is, the smallest $n$ such that 
$$2^{-k(w_1 \dots w_n)} 3^{-n+k(w_1\dots w_n)} < e^{-t}.$$ 
In other words, setting 
$$g_{s,x,y}(z) := e^{-2\pi i s  (x-y) e^{-z}}, \quad z \in \mathbb{R},$$ we see in \eqref{eq:FourierBoundStop}, once $x,y$ are fixed, that
$$\sum_{ \mathbf{a} \in \mathcal{W}_t } 2^{-|\mathbf{a}|} e^{-2\pi i \xi 2^{-k(\mathbf{a})} 3^{-n+k(\mathbf{a})}  (x-y)} = \mathbb{E}_t g_{s,x,y}\Big(\sum_{k = 1}^{n_{t}} X_k-t\Big),$$
where $\mathbb{E}_t$ is the expectation associated to the probability measure $\sum_{ \mathbf{a} \in \mathcal{W}_t } 2^{-|\mathbf{a}|}\delta_\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathcal{W}_t$. 

The limiting distribution of $\sum_{k = 1}^{n_{t}} X_k-t$ as $t \to \infty$ can be found using \textit{renewal theory}. In particular, the irrationality of $\frac{\log 2}{ \log 3}$ and the \textit{renewal theorem} for stopping times (e.g. Kesten's renewal theorem \cite{Kesten1974} from 1974) guarantees the limit distribution of $\sum_{k = 1}^{n_{t}} X_k-t$ as $t \to \infty$ is some absolutely continuous measure $\lambda$, so
$$\lim_{t \to \infty} \mathbb{E}_t g_{s,x,y}\Big(\sum_{k = 1}^{n_{t}} X_k-t\Big) = \int g_{s,x,y}(z) \, d\lambda(z).$$
Now, by the definition of $g_{s,x,y}$, the oscillating integral $\int g_{s,x,y}(z) \, d\lambda(z)$ is actually the Fourier transform of push-forward of $\lambda$ under $e^{-z}$ at frequency $s(x-y)$, so if $|s(x-y)|$ is arbitrarily large, Riemann-Lebesgue lemma guarantees 
$$\int g_{s,x,y}(z) \, d\lambda(z) \to 0.$$ 
This leads to $\widehat{\mu}(\xi) \to 0$ as $|\xi| \to \infty$ since $\xi = s e^t$. Now to make this argument more formal, one would need to choose $s$, $t$ and $\delta$ depending on $\xi$ in a way we can take a double limit in this procedure.

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $\mu$ be a non-atomic self-conformal measure with equal weights $1/2$ for the IFS 
$$\Phi_2 = \{x \mapsto f_1(x) = 1/(x+1) , x \mapsto f_2(x) = 1/(x+2) \}.$$ 
Compared to the self-similar case, recalling \eqref{eq:FourierBound}, we can iterate $\mu = \frac{1}{2}f_1(\mu) + \frac{1}{2}f_2(\mu)$ up to some $n = c \log |\xi|$, for some $c > 0$, to obtain

\begin{align} \label{eq:FourierBoundGauss} |\widehat{\mu}(\xi)| \lesssim \int \Big|\frac{1}{2^n} \sum_{ \mathbf{a} \in \{1,2\}^n } e^{-2\pi i \xi f_\mathbf{a}(x)}\Big| \, d\mu(x),
\end{align}
where $f_\mathbf{a}(x) = f_{a_1} \circ \dots \circ f_{a_n}(x)$. Then fixing $\varepsilon > 0$, in \eqref{eq:FourierBoundGauss} we consider only the ``regular'' words $\mathcal{R}_n(\varepsilon)$ formed of those $\mathbf{a} \in \{1,2\}^n$ for which 
$$e^{-(\lambda + \varepsilon) n} \lesssim |f_\mathbf{a}'(x)| \lesssim e^{-(\lambda - \varepsilon) n}$$ 
for all $x \in [0,1]$, where $\lambda = \int \log |G'(x)| \, d\mu(x)$ is the Lyapunov exponent associated to the IFS $B_2$. By the large deviation principle, we know that most words $\mathbf{a}$ satisfy this property, so in particular as $n = c \log |\xi|$ the bound for $ |\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|$ in \eqref{eq:FourierBoundGauss} is bounded by 
$$\int |S_\xi(x)| \, d\mu(x) + O(|\xi|^{-\delta})$$ 
for some $\delta > 0$, where 
$$S_\xi(x) := \frac{1}{2^n} \sum_{ \mathbf{a} \in \mathcal{R}_n(\varepsilon) } e^{-2\pi i \xi f_\mathbf{a}(x)}.$$ 
Compare this to the stopping time bound in the self-similar case \eqref{eq:FourierBoundStop}. 

Then to go from the self-conformal measure $\mu$ to Lebesgue measure, we use an idea motivated by the \textit{large sieve inequalities} in analytic number theory (e.g. (4) in \cite{BakerFirst}), exploiting the Frostman regularity of $\mu$ enable us to bound $\int |S_\xi(x)| \, d\mu(x)$ in terms of the $L^\infty$ norm of $S_\xi'$ and $L^2$ norm of $S_\xi$ in terms of the Lebesgue measure: 
$$\int |S_\xi(x)| \, d\mu(x) \lesssim \|S_\xi\|_2^2 \|S_\xi'\|_\infty^{\varepsilon_1} |\xi|^{\varepsilon_3} + |\xi|^{-\varepsilon_3}$$ 
with suitable choices of constants $\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\varepsilon_3 > 0$. Here $ \|S_\xi'\|_\infty$ grows in $|\xi|$, so in order to get Fourier decay, we need to ensure $\|S_\xi\|_2^2$ decays rapidly enough. This is where the non-linearity of the inverse branches manifests itself.

By expanding the square and using the triangle inequality, we can bound
$$\|S_\xi\|_2^2 \leq 2^{-2n} \sum_{\mathbf{a} \in \mathcal{R}_n(\varepsilon) }\sum_{\mathbf{b} \in \mathcal{R}_n(\varepsilon)}\Big| \int e^{-2\pi i \xi (f_\mathbf{a}(x) - f_\mathbf{b}(x))} \, dx \Big|.$$ 
It is interesting to compare this to the self-similar case, namely \eqref{eq:FourierBoundStop} achieved using the stopping time and Cauchy-Schwarz, where one studied the phases of the form $f_\mathbf{a}(x) - f_\mathbf{a}(y)$ for the \textit{same} word $\mathbf{a}$ but \textit{pairs} of $x$ and $y$, we now use \textit{same} $x$, but \textit{pairs} of words $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$. This creates a conceptual difference in the proofs. Thus we can reduce the problem of bounding $\|S_\xi\|_2^2$ to decay of oscillatory Lebesgue integrals with the \textit{non-linear} phase 
$$x \mapsto \psi_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}(x) := f_\mathbf{a}(x) - f_\mathbf{b}(x),$$ which we can control using a kind of stationary phase approximation, which requires us to bound from below the derivative of the phase function $\psi_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}(x)$. This is the first time the unique properties of the Gauss map appear. Crucially we have:
$$\psi_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}'(x) = \varphi_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}(x) ((q_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}) - q_{n-1}(\mathbf{b})) x + q_n(\mathbf{a}) - q_n(\mathbf{b})), \quad x \in (0,1),$$
where 
$$\varphi_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}(x) := (-1)^{n} \frac{(q_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}) + q_{n-1}(\mathbf{b}))x + q_{n}(\mathbf{a}) + q_{n}(\mathbf{b})}{(q_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}) x + q_{n}(\mathbf{a}) )^2(q_{n-1}(\mathbf{b}) x + q_{n}(\mathbf{b}))^2}$$ 
and $q_n(\mathbf{a})$ is the $n$th \textit{continuant} associated to the word $\mathbf{a}$, that is, writing the continued fraction expansion associated to $\mathbf{a}$, we obtain a rational number $p_n(\mathbf{a})/q_n(\mathbf{a})$, and the continuant is the denominator of this rational number. Thus $q_n(\mathbf{a}) \neq q_n(\mathbf{b})$ and $q_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}) \neq q_{n-1}(\mathbf{b})$, depending on how large $|q_{n}(\mathbf{a}) - q_{n}(\mathbf{b})|$ is compared to $|q_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}) - q_{n-1}(\mathbf{b})|$, we can use the integration by parts to bound 
$$\Big| \int e^{-2\pi i \xi (f_\mathbf{a}(x) - f_\mathbf{b}(x))} \, dx \Big|$$ 
by 
$$|\xi|^{\varepsilon_4-1/2} |q_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}) - q_{n-1}(\mathbf{b})|^{-1/2} \quad \text{ or } \quad |\xi|^{\varepsilon_4-1/2} |q_{n}(\mathbf{a}) - q_{n}(\mathbf{b})|^{-1/2}$$ for some $\varepsilon_4 > 0$.

To then get enough decay when summing over $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$, we just need to bounds for $|q_{j}(\mathbf{a}) - q_{j}(\mathbf{b})|$ for $j = n-1,n$ from below and the number of pairs $(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$ for which $q_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}) = q_{n-1}(\mathbf{b})$ and $q_n(\mathbf{a}) = q_n(\mathbf{b})$. The latter is trivial: it can only happen when $\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{b}$. In the former case, just because the continuants are integers, we always have $|q_{n}(\mathbf{a}) - q_{n}(\mathbf{b})| \geq 1$ or $|q_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}) - q_{n-1}(\mathbf{b})| \geq 1$. These easy bounds are already enough to prove polynomial Fourier decay for self-conformal measures $\mu$ with Hausdorff dimension greater than $2/3$. But performing more careful analysis of the cardinality of the set of pairs $(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$ for which 
$$|q_n(\mathbf{a}) - q_n(\mathbf{b})| \approx e^{k \lambda/2}, \quad k = 1,2,\dots,n/2$$ 
for some $\varepsilon_5 > 0$ (which requires us to use the property that $q_{n}(\mathbf{a}) = q_n(\overline{\mathbf{a}})$ and  $q_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}) = p_{n-1}(\overline{\mathbf{a}})$ for the \textit{reverse} $\overline{\mathbf{a}} = a_n \dots a_1$ of $\mathbf{a} = a_1\dots a_n$), following an argument due to Queffélec and Ramaré \cite{QetR}, we can improve the decay allowing us to include cases of $\mu$ with dimension near $1/2$. This includes the set $B_2$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
If we go below dimension $1/2$ in Theorem \ref{thm:JordanSahlsten}, the methods of Kaufman and Queffélec-Ramaré break down, and we end up having bad estimates from the stationary phase bounds. This highlighted that some deeper multiscale analysis ideas for $p_n(\mathbf{a})/q_n(\mathbf{a})$ were needed that go beyond the Kaufman and Queffélec-Ramaré type method to bound Fourier transforms in the non-linear case. A new method was introduced in the groundbreaking work of Bourgain and Dyatlov \cite{BD1} from 2017 based on the \textit{discretised sum-product theorem}, which we will discuss in the next section. We highlight that C. Stevens in his PhD thesis \cite{Stevens} from 2022, adapting the method of Bourgain and Dyatlov, was able to remove the dimension $1/2$ assumption on Theorem \ref{thm:JordanSahlsten}. Thus Theorem \ref{thm:JordanSahlsten} holds without any assumption on the Hausdorff dimension of $\mu$ except positivity.

<!-- theorem-15: body_md -->
\label{thm:discretised}
For all $\kappa > 0$, there exists $\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2 > 0$ such that the following holds. Let $\mu$ be any probability measure on $[1/2,1]$ and $N \in \mathbb{N}$ large enough. Assume $\mu(B(y,\varrho)) \lesssim \varrho^{\kappa}$ for all $\varrho \in [1/N,1/N^{\varepsilon_1}]$ and $y \in \mathbb{R}$. Then for all $k > 1/\kappa$, $\xi \in \mathbb{R}$, $|\xi| \approx N$, we have
$$|\widehat{\mu^{\odot k}}(\xi)| = \Big|\int e^{-2\pi i \xi x_1 \dots x_k} \, d\mu(x_1) \dots d\mu(x_k)\Big| \lesssim |\xi|^{-\varepsilon_2}.$$
<!-- end theorem-15 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By taking a suitable mollifier $\tau_N$ for $N \approx |\xi|$ where the additive convolution $\tau_N \ast \mu$ satisfies  $\|\tau_N \ast \mu\|_\infty \lesssim N^{1-\kappa}$ and an integer $r \geq 1$, we can bound 
\begin{align}\label{eq:sumprodboundinitial}|\widehat{\mu^{\odot (k+1)}}(\xi)|^r \leq \int |\widehat{\mu^{\odot k}}(\eta \xi)|^r \, d\mu(\eta) \approx \int |\widehat{\mu^{\odot k}}(\eta \xi)|^r (\tau_N \ast \mu)(\eta) \, d\eta.
\end{align}
Thus, as long as we can find integers $k,r \geq 2$ such that
\begin{align}\label{eq:sumprodbound}
\int_{-N}^N |\widehat{\mu^{\odot k}}(\eta)|^r \, d\eta < N^{\kappa/2},
\end{align}
integration by parts with respect to Lebesgue measure produces a term $|\xi|^{-1}$ in front on the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:sumprodboundinitial}, by $\|\tau_N \ast \mu\|_\infty \lesssim N^{1-\kappa}$ we get the required estimate for $|\widehat{\mu^{\odot k}}(\xi)|$ with $k$ replaced by $k+1$. We note in \cite{OSS}, that improved \cite{Bourgain2010}, is that $r$ can be made equal to $2$ in \eqref{eq:sumprodbound}, which would allow us to optimise $k$ in terms of $\kappa$.

To find $k$ and $r$ such that \eqref{eq:sumprodbound} holds one has to study the numbers $\sigma_{k,r} > 0$ defined by the expression 
$$\int_{-N}^N |\widehat{\mu^{\odot k}}(\eta)|^r \, d\eta = N^{\sigma_{k,r}}.$$
The numbers $\sigma_{k,r}$ are essentially decreasing both in $k$ and $r$, so one looks for large enough $k$ and $r$ that we can use. It turns out that the discretised sum-product theorem \cite{Bourgain2010} gives the existence of $\varepsilon_1 > 0$ such that $\sigma_{2k,\overline{r}} < \sigma_{k,2r} - \varepsilon_1/2$ for a suitable choice $\overline r \approx c_3 r^2$, where $c_3 > 0$ is chosen appropriately. Thus iterating this with respect to $k$ and $r$, we can find $k$ and $r$ such that $\sigma_{k,2r} < \kappa/2$ giving \eqref{eq:sumprodbound}. 

We can achieve $\sigma_{2k,\overline{r}} < \sigma_{k,2r} - \varepsilon_1/2$ by studying measures
$$\nu_{r,k} := \frac{1}{2}[(\mu^{\odot k} \ast \mu^{\odot k}_{-})^{\ast r} + (\mu^{\odot 2k} \ast \mu^{\odot 2k}_{-})^{\ast r}],$$ 
where $\mu^{\ast r}$ is the additive self-convolution of $\mu$ and $\mu_{-}$ is the push-forward of $\mu$ under $x \mapsto -x$. Then 
$$\int_{-N}^N |\widehat{\nu}_{r,k}(\eta)|^2 \, d\eta \sim \int_{-N}^N |\widehat{\mu^{\odot k}}(\eta)|^{2r} \, d\eta = N^{\sigma_{k,2r}}.$$ 
Thus if we can find $\varepsilon_1 > 0$ such that there is an $N^{-\varepsilon_1}$ gain:
\begin{align}\label{eq:sumpr}\int_{-N}^N \int |\widehat{\nu}_{r,k}(\eta)|^2 |\widehat{\nu}_{r,k}(y \eta)|^2 d\nu_{r,k}^{\ast 2}(y) \, d\eta \lesssim N^{-\varepsilon_1} \int_{-N}^N |\widehat{\nu}_{r,k}(\eta)|^2 \, d\eta \qquad (\sim N^{-\varepsilon_1 + \sigma_{k,2r}}),\end{align}
then with some effort one can deduce that this estimate gives us $\sigma_{2k,\overline{r}} < \sigma_{k,2r} - \varepsilon_1/2$ by using a covering argument on points $\eta \in \mathbb{R}$ with $|\widehat{\mu}_k(\eta)| > N^{-\kappa_0}$ for $\kappa_0 = 2 c_3^{-1} r^{-2}$.

The estimate \eqref{eq:sumpr} follows from Bourgain's discretised sum-product theorem \cite{Bourgain2010} from 2010. Set $\nu := \nu_{r,k}$. If \eqref{eq:sumpr} fails for $\nu$, then any small $\varepsilon > 0$, we have 
$$\int_{|y| > N^{-\varepsilon_1}}|S \cap y^{-1} S| \, d\nu^{\ast 2}(y) > N^{\sigma_{k,2r}-\varepsilon}$$ 
for a scale $N^{-1}$ discretisation $S = \bigcup_{j = 1}^M B(z_j,N^{-1-\varepsilon})$ of the support of $\nu^{\ast 2}$ for some $1/N$ separated points $z_j$ in the support of $\nu^{\ast 2}$. Thus we can find many $1/N$ separated points $y_k$ from the support of $\nu$ such that $|z_{j_1} - y_k z_{j_k}|$ is large for many pairs $(z_{j_1},z_{j_2})$. This can then be turned into a lower bound on the number of quadruples $(\log |z_{j_1}|,\log |z_{j_2}|,\log |z_{j_3}|,\log |z_{j_4}|) \in (\log |S|)^4$ for which 
$$\log |z_{j_1}|+\log |z_{j_2}| = \log |z_{j_3}| + \log |z_{j_4}|.$$ In other words, $\log |S|$ has a large \textit{additive energy}, so by the Balog-Szemer\'edi-Gowers theorem (see e.g. \cite{Gowers}) there exists a large subset $\mathcal{F}_1$ of $S$ such that $\mathcal{F}_1 \cdot \mathcal{F}_1$ can be covered by at most $N^{\varepsilon_3}|\mathcal{F}_1|$ intervals of size $1/N$ for a suitable $\varepsilon_3> 0$. On the other hand, as $S$ comes from the support of the additive self-convolution $\nu^{\ast 2}$, by summing a mollifier of $\nu^{\ast 2}$ over points in $\mathcal{F}_1 \subset S$, Cauchy-Schwarz implies that $\mathcal{F}_1$ also has large additive energy, so again by Balog-Szemer\'edi-Gowers theorem we can find a further large subset $\mathcal{F}_2 \subset \mathcal{F}_1$ such that $\mathcal{F}_2 + \mathcal{F}_2$ can be covered by at most $N^{\varepsilon_4}|\mathcal{F}_2|$ intervals of size $1/N$ for a suitable $\varepsilon_4 > 0$. As the sets $\mathcal{F}_2 \subset \mathcal{F}_1$ are large subsets of $S$, this is impossible by the discretised sum-product theorem \cite{Bourgain2010}.

<!-- theorem-16: body_md -->
\label{thm:bourgaindyatlov}
If $\mu_\Gamma$ is the Patterson-Sullivan measure on the limit set $\Lambda_\Gamma$ associated to a convex co-compact Fuchsian group $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, then there exists $\alpha = \alpha(\dim_\mathrm{H} \Lambda_\Gamma) > 0$ such that

as $|\xi| \to \infty$.
<!-- end theorem-16 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
When $\Gamma$ is a convex co-compact subgroup, it has an associated \textit{Schottky structure} \cite[Section 15.1]{Bor}, which allows us to code the limit set $\Lambda_\Gamma$ using matrices $\gamma_\mathbf{a} \in \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, which we can identify as M\"obius transformations acting on the hyperbolic plane $\mathbb{H}$ and contracting real analytic maps on the boundary of $\mathbb{H}$. Here $\mathbf{a} \in \mathcal{W}$, where $\mathcal{W} \subset \bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathcal{A}^n$ is the set of allowed finite words from some index set $\mathcal{A}$ that satisfy rules defined by the Schottky structure. This also allows us to realise the Patterson-Sullivan measure $\mu_\Gamma$ as a Gibbs measure associated to a subshift of an IFS.

Instead of now trying to iterate transfer operator up to words of length $n$ as in the case of Gauss map (recall \eqref{eq:FourierBound}), Bourgain and Dyatlov \cite{BD1} instead apply \textit{blocks} of length $k$ and $k+1$ formed of words of lengths $n$. The $k$ is precisely the $k$ that will arise from Theorem \ref{thm:discretised}. Now, fix a suitable $\varepsilon_1 > 0$ such that $\tau \approx |\xi|^{-\varepsilon_1}$ and words $\mathcal{W}(\tau) \subset \mathcal{W}$ such that any $\mathbf{a} \in \mathcal{W}(\tau)$ satisfy that the lengths $|\gamma_\mathbf{a}[0,1]| \approx \tau$. Then, as $\mu_\Gamma$ is Ahlfors-David regular, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (compare to \eqref{eq:FourierBoundStop} in the self-similar case), mean value theorem and the fact that the contractions $\gamma_\mathbf{a}$ exhibit bounded distortions, there exists suitable constants $\varepsilon_2 > 0$ and $C > 0$ such that

\begin{align} \label{eq:FourierBoundFuchsian} |\widehat{\mu}_\Gamma(\xi)|^2 \lesssim \tau^{k\delta} \max_{\mathbf{A} =\mathbf{a}_1 \dots \mathbf{a}_{k+1}} \sup_{\tau^{-1/4} \leq |\eta| \leq C \tau^{-1/2}} \Big| \sum_{\mathbf{b}_1 \dots \mathbf{b}_{k} \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbf{A})} e^{-2\pi i \eta \prod_{j = 1}^k \tau^{-2} \gamma'_{\mathbf{a}_{j-1}'\mathbf{b}_j'}(x_{\mathbf{a}_j})}\Big| + \tau^{\varepsilon_2},
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{A})$ are the collection of blocks of words in $\mathcal{W}(\tau)$ that are allowed in the Schottky structure depending $\mathbf{A}$, words $\mathbf{a} = a_1\dots a_n$ and we use the notations $\mathbf{a}' = a_1\dots a_{n-1}$ and $x_\mathbf{a}$ as the center point of $\gamma_\mathbf{a}[0,1]$. Now we see that the right hand-side of \eqref{eq:FourierBoundFuchsian} is a multiplicative convolution of the discrete measures 
$$\nu_j := \sharp \mathcal{Z}_j^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf{b} \in \mathcal{Z}_j} \delta_{\tau^{-2} \gamma_{\mathbf{a}_j' \mathbf{b}'}' (x_\mathbf{a})}$$ 
for some finite sets $\mathcal{Z}_j$. Thus by adapting Theorem \ref{thm:discretised} to multiplicative convolutions of several $\nu_j$, having polynomial Fourier decay bound reduces by \eqref{eq:FourierBoundFuchsian} to proving the Frostman condition required by Theorem \ref{thm:discretised} for each $\nu_j$. This means that we need to prove that the normalised derivatives $\tau^{-2} \gamma_{\mathbf{a}_j' \mathbf{b}'}' (x_\mathbf{a})$, $\mathbf{b} \in \mathcal{W}(\tau)$, do not concentrate near a point.

Let us now argue the non-concentration of the numbers $\tau^{-2} \gamma_{\mathbf{a}_j' \mathbf{b}'}' (x_\mathbf{a})$, $\mathbf{b} \in \mathcal{W}(\tau)$. Fixing $b \in \mathcal{A}$, we write $I_b = [x_0,x_1]$. Then we can write $\gamma_\mathbf{a}(x) = S_b \gamma_{\alpha_{\mathbf{a},b}}T_{\mathbf{a},b}^{-1}$, where $T_{\mathbf{a},b}$ and $S_b$ are affine maps such that $T_{\mathbf{a},b}([0,1]) = \gamma_\mathbf{a}(I_b)$ and $S_b([0,1]) = I_b$ and
$$\gamma_{\alpha_{\mathbf{a},b}} = \begin{pmatrix} e^{\alpha_\mathbf{a}/2} & 0 \\ e^{\alpha_{\mathbf{a},b}/2} - e^{-\alpha_{\mathbf{a},b}/2} & e^{-\alpha_{\mathbf{a},b}/2}\end{pmatrix}, \quad \text{where}\quad \alpha_{\mathbf{a},b} = \log \frac{\gamma_\mathbf{a}^{-1}(\infty) - x_1}{\gamma_\mathbf{a}^{-1}(\infty) - x_0}$$
is the \textit{distortion} of $\gamma_\mathbf{a}$ in $I_b$. Thus 
$$\gamma_\mathbf{a}'(x) = \gamma_{\alpha_{\mathbf{a},b}}'(T_{\mathbf{a},b}^{-1}(x)) \cdot \frac{|\gamma_\mathbf{a}(I_b)|}{|I_b|}$$ 
so using bounded distortions we can reduce the non-concentration properties of $\tau^{-2} \gamma_{\mathbf{a}_j' \mathbf{b}'}' (x_\mathbf{a})$ to non-concentrations of $\gamma_{\mathbf{a}_j' \mathbf{b}'}^{-1}(\infty)$. As with the Gauss map, if we consider the \textit{reverse} word $\overline{\mathbf{a}}$ of $\mathbf{a}$, the lengths 
$$|\gamma_\mathbf{a}[0,1]| \approx |\gamma_{\overline{\mathbf{a}}}[0,1]|.$$ 
The Schottky structure was defined so that $\gamma_{\mathbf{a}_j' \mathbf{b}'}^{-1}(\infty) = \gamma_{\overline{\mathbf{a}_j' \mathbf{b}'}}(\infty) \in \gamma_{\overline{\mathbf{b}'}}[0,1]$, which has a length approximately the same as $|\gamma_{\mathbf{b}'}[0,1]| \approx \tau$. Given $\mathbf{a}_j$, $y \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\varrho > \tau$, we can thus cover $B(y,\varrho) \cap \{\gamma_{\mathbf{a}_j' \mathbf{b}'}^{-1}(\infty) : \mathbf{b} \in \mathcal{W}(\tau)\}$, by the disjoint intervals $\gamma_{\mathbf{b}'}[0,1]$ that intersect $B(y,\varrho)$. This implies an upper bound for the number of $\gamma_{\mathbf{a}_j' \mathbf{b}'}^{-1}(\infty)$, $\mathbf{b} \in \mathcal{W}(\tau)$, with $\gamma_{\mathbf{a}_j' \mathbf{b}'}^{-1}(\infty) \in B(y,\varrho)$.

\label{thm:FourierSelfSimRd}
Let $\Gamma < \mathrm{SO}(d)$ with $d \geq 3$ be a dense subgroup generated by the rotations $\theta_j$, $j \in \mathcal{A}$, and $\mu$ is a non-atomic self-similar measure associated to the IFS $\Phi = \{f_j = \lambda \theta_j + b_j\}$ with same contraction ratio $0 < \lambda < 1$ and that $f_j$ do not have a common fixed point, then there is a constant $c = c(\mu) > 0$ depending only on $\mu$ such that for any $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $|\xi| > 1$, we have
$$|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)| \lesssim e^{-c(\log |\xi|)^{1/3}}.$$
<!-- end theorem-24 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The idea of the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:FourierSelfSimRd} was communicated to the author by P. Varj\'u.  Write $\xi = R z$ for $R > 1$ and $z \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$. Define the Fourier restriction $\widehat{\mu}_{R} : \mathbb{S}^{d-1} \to \mathbb{C}$,
$$\widehat{\mu}_{R}(z) := \widehat{\mu}(R z), \quad z \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}.$$ 
Let $R_0 := \lambda^{n_0} R$ for some $n_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ we will choose later. Since $\mu = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{A}} p_j f_j(\mu)$ and $f_j = \lambda \theta_j + b_j$, we have 
$$|\widehat{\mu}_R(z)| = \Big|\sum_{j \in \mathcal{A}} p_j \int e^{-2\pi i (Rz) \cdot (\lambda \theta_j x + b_j) } \, d\mu(x)\Big| \leq \sum_{j \in \mathcal{A}} p_j \Big|\int e^{-2\pi i (\lambda R\theta_j^{-1} z) \cdot x } \, d\mu(x) \Big| = T|\widehat{\mu}_{\lambda R}|(z).$$
Thus by iterating this $n_0$ times, we have
\begin{align}\label{eq:selfbound}|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)| = |\widehat{\mu}_R(z)| \leq T^{n_0} |\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}|(z).\end{align}
Note that here we identify $T$ also as an operator on $L^2(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})$ as follows: if $f \in L^2(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})$ and $z \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, then write $Tf(z) := \sum_{j \in \mathcal{A}} p_j f(\theta_j^{-1} z)$. Since for any $z \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, we can always find $\sigma_z \in \mathrm{SO}(d)$, which maps $1$ to $z$, we have $Tf(z) = T \tilde f (\sigma_z)$, where $\tilde f(g) := f(g 1)$, $g \in \mathrm{SO}(d)$. Moreover, $\|\tilde f\|_{L^2(G)} = \|f\|_{L^2(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}$ and 
$$\|T^k\tilde f\|_{L^2(G)} = \|T^kf\|_{L^2(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}$$ 
for any $k \geq 1$.

Now, decompose $L^2(G)$ with respect to the Haar measure $dg$ on $G$ as a direct sum with irreducible representations $V_v < L^2(\mathrm{SO}(d))$ of weight $v$, that is, $L^2(\mathrm{SO}(d)) = \bigoplus_{v} V_v$, where $v=0$ corresponds to constant functions. Let $r \geq 1$, which we will choose later. Then, by Lemma 19 by Varj\'u \cite{Varju3}, there exists a function $h_r \in \bigoplus_{|v| \leq r} V_v$ such that $\int h_r(g) \, dg = 1$, $\|h_r\|_2 \lesssim r^{\dim \mathrm{SO}(d) / 4}$, where $\dim \mathrm{SO}(d) = \frac{d(d-1)}{2}$ and 
$$\int h_R(g) \operatorname{dist}(g,1) \, dg \lesssim_d r^{-1/2}.$$ 
Consider the convolution $|\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| \ast h_r \in L^2(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})$, defined by
$$ |\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| \ast h_r(z) := \int |\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}|(g^{-1} z) h_r(g) \, dg, \quad z \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}.$$
Then we can write
\begin{align}\label{eq:selfbound2}|\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}|(z) =  \int (|\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| \ast h_r)(v) \, dv + H(z) + F(z),\end{align}
where $H(z) = (|\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| \ast h_r)(z) - \int (|\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| \ast h_r)(v) \, dv$ and $F(z) = |\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}|(z) - |\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| \ast h_r(z)$.  Combining \eqref{eq:selfbound2} with \eqref{eq:selfbound}, we have the bound
\begin{align}\label{eq:selfbound3} |\widehat{\mu}(\xi)| \leq \int (|\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| \ast h_r)(v) \, dv  + |T^{n_0}H(z)| + |T^{n_0} F(z)|.\end{align}
To finish the argument, we need to bound the constant term and have an $L^\infty$ bound in $z$ of 
$$G(z) := |T^{n_0}H(z)| + |T^{n_0} F(z)|,$$ 
which is possible by an $L^2$ bound combined with a $\mathrm{Lip}$ norm bound, where the Lipschitz norm $\|G\|_{\mathrm{Lip}} := \|G'\|_\infty$.

For the constant term of \eqref{eq:selfbound3}, it was shown by Mattila \cite{Mattila3} (see Lemma 3.15 of \cite{Mattila95}) that for any Borel measure $\tau$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$, we have 
$$\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}} |\widehat{\tau}(r z)|^2 \, dz \lesssim r^{-\delta} I_{\delta}(\tau),$$ 
where $I_\delta(\tau) = \int\hspace{-0.1in}\int |x-y|^{-\delta} \, d\mu(x) \, d\mu(y)$ for any $0 < \delta < (d-1)/2$. Now, for a self-similar measure $\mu$, we know that $I_{\varepsilon_1}(\mu) < \infty$ for some $\varepsilon_1 > 0$, e.g. following the argument of Feng and Lau \cite{FL} from 2009 as $\mu$ has no atoms. Thus by Cauchy-Schwarz
$$\Big| \int (|\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| \ast h_r)(v) \, dv\Big|  \leq \|\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}\|_2 |\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|^{1/2} \|h_r\|_1 \lesssim R_0^{-\varepsilon_1}.$$

By the $1$-Lipschitz continuity of the exponential, we have:
$$\Big\||\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| - |\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| \ast h_r \Big\|_2 \lesssim_d  R_0 r^{-1/2} \quad \text{and} \quad \Big\||\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| - |\widehat{\mu}_{R_0}| \ast h_r\Big\|_{\mathrm{Lip}} \lesssim_d  R_0.$$
Since $\|H + F\|_{\mathrm{Lip}} \lesssim R_0$ and that $T$ does not increase $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}$, we have $\|G\|_{\mathrm{Lip}} = \|T^{n_0}H + T^{n_0 }F\|_{\mathrm{Lip}} \lesssim R_0$.

For the $L^2$ norm of $G$, since 
$$\int h_R(g) \operatorname{dist}(g,1) \, dg \lesssim_d r^{-1/2},$$ 
using $1$-Lipschitz continuity we have $\|F\|_2 \lesssim R_0 r^{-1/2}$. Thus by triangle inequality 
$$\|T^{n_0} F \|_2 \leq \|F\|_2 \leq R_0 r^{-1/2}.$$

Next, as $h_r \in \bigoplus_{|v| \leq r} V_v$, $\int H = 0$, and as $H$ comes from a convolution with $h_r$, we can use the contraction of $T^n$ in $L^2_0(\mathrm{SO}(d))$ that depends on $r$, i.e. Theorem 6 of Varj\'u \cite{Varju3} from 2013 implying for some $c_0,c_0' > 0$ and $0 < A \leq 2$ such that
$$\|T^{n_0} H\|_2 \lesssim \Big(1-\frac{c_0}{ \log^{A} (c_0' r + 2)}\Big)^{n_0}\|H\|_2 \leq e^{-\frac{n_0 c_0}{ \log^{2} (c_0' r + 2)} }\|H\|_2.$$
Here by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have by the $L^2$ decay on spheres (like we argued for the constant term in \eqref{eq:selfbound3}) that $\|H\|_2 \lesssim R_0^{-\varepsilon_1}$.  

Having bounds for $\|G\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}$ and $\|G\|_{2}$, we obtain $L^\infty$ bound:
$$\|G\|_\infty \lesssim \|G\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}^{1/3}  \|G\|_2^{2/3} \lesssim R_0^{1/3}  (e^{-\frac{n_0 c_0}{ \log^{2} (c_0' r + 2)} }R_0^{-\varepsilon_1} + R_0 r^{-1/2})^{2/3}.$$
Choose now constant $c_1 > 0$ possibly depending on $\lambda$ such that when $R_0 = e^{c_1 (\log R)^{1/3}}$ (which also fixes $n_0$ as $\lambda^{n_0} R = R_0$) and $r = e^{10 c_1(\log R)^{1/3}}$ we have
$$R_0^{-\varepsilon_1} + R_0^{1/3}  (e^{-\frac{n_0 c_0}{ \log^{2} (c_0' r + 2)} }R_0^{-\varepsilon_1} + R_0 r^{-1/2})^{2/3} \leq e^{-c(\log R)^{1/3}}$$ 
for some constant $c > 0$. Thus the proof is complete.

<!-- theorem-26: body_md -->
\label{thm:rapaport}
Any self-similar measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ associated to an affinely irreducible IFS $\Phi = \{r_j \theta_j + b_j : j \in \mathcal{A}\}$ is Rajchman if and only if the following condition does not hold: there exists a subspace $V \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ with $d' = \dim V > 0$ such that $\theta_j (V) = V$ for all $j \in \mathcal{A}$ and an isometry $S : V \to\mathbb{R}^{d'}$ such that
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] If $U_j' \in O(d')$ and $a_j' \in \mathbb{R}^{d'}$ are defined such that 
$$S \circ \pi_V \circ \varphi_j \circ S^{-1} = r_j U_j' + b_j'$$ 
where $\pi_V$ is the orthogonal projection onto $V$, and $H \subset \mathrm{GL}(d',\mathbb{R})$ is the subgroup generated by matrices $r_j U_j'$, $j \in \mathcal{A}$, then $N := H \cap O(d')$ is finite normal subgroup of $H$ and $H / N$ is cyclic.
\item[(2)] If $A \in H$ is contraction such that $\{A^n N : n\in \mathbb{Z}\} = H / N$, then there exists $k \geq 1$, $z_1,\dots,z_k \in \mathbb{C}$ and $\zeta_1,\dots,\zeta_k \in \mathbb{C}^{d'} \setminus \{0\}$ such that
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\{z_1,\dots,z_k\}$ is a P.V. $k$-tuple, that is, $z_i$, $i = 1,\dots,k$, are distinct complex numbers outside the closed unit disk that are roots of an integral monic polynomial whose other roots are all inside the open unit disk;
\item[(ii)] $A^{-1} \zeta_i = z_i \zeta_i$ for $1 \leq i \leq k$;
\item[(iii)] if $j \in \mathcal{A}$ and $V \in N$, there is a polynomial $P_{j,V}$ of rational coefficients such that $\langle V b_j' , \zeta_i \rangle = P_{j,V}(z_i)$ for $1 \leq i \leq k$.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

<!-- theorem-29: body_md -->
\label{thm:discretisedRd}
For all $\kappa > 0$, there exists $\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2 > 0$ and $k \in \mathbb{N}$ such that the following holds. Let $\mu$ be any probability measure on $[1/2,1]^d$ and $N \in \mathbb{N}$ large enough. Assume $\mu(x : v\cdot x \in B(y,\varrho)) \lesssim \varrho^{\kappa}$ for all $\varrho \in [1/N,1/N^{\varepsilon_1}]$, $y \in \mathbb{R}$ and $v \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$. Then for all $\xi \in \mathbb{R}$, $|\xi| \approx N$:
$$\Big|\int e^{2\pi i \xi \cdot x_1 x_2 \dots x_k} \, d\mu(x_1) \dots d\mu(x_k)\Big| \lesssim |\xi|^{-\varepsilon_2}.$$

<!-- theorem-33: body_md -->
\label{thm:KhalilRd}
Let $\mu$ be a compactly supported Borel probability measure on $\mathbb{R}^d$, which is $(C,\alpha)$ uniformly affinely non-concentrated for some $C,\alpha > 1$, that is, for any $\varrho > 0$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $0 < r \leq 1$ and any affine hyperplane $V$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$, we have
$$\mu(\{y \in B(x,r) : \mathrm{dist}(y,V) \leq \varrho r \} ) \leq C\varrho^\alpha \mu(B(x,r)).$$
Then for any $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $\delta > 0$ such that for all $T > 0$:
$$\mathrm{Leb}(\{\xi \in B(0,T) : |\widehat{\mu}(\xi)| > T^{-\delta}\}) = O_\varepsilon(T^\varepsilon).$$

<!-- theorem-34: body_md -->
\label{thm:Demeter}
Suppose $\Gamma$ is a graph of a $C^3$ function with non-zero curvature everywhere. If $\mu$ is a Borel measure on $\Gamma$ satisfying $\mu(B(x,r)) \lesssim r^{\delta}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and $r > 0$ for some constant $\delta > 0$, then there exists $\beta = \beta(\delta) > 0$ such that for any ball $B_R$ of radius $R > 0$ we have
$$\|\widehat{\mu}\|_{L^6(B_R)} \lesssim R^{\frac{2-2\delta - \beta}{6}}.$$
