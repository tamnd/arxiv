<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:oppenheim}
Suppose $P^{\tau}$ is irreducible for all $\tau \in X$ of codimension at least $2$ and that there exists $\gamma > 1 - \frac{1}{n-1}$ such that $\gamma\left({P^{\tau}}\right) \geq \gamma$ for all $\tau \in X(n-2)$. Then for every $2 \leq k \leq n$ and every $\tau \in X(n-k)$,
\begin{align*}
    \gamma\left({P^{\tau}}\right) \geq \frac{1 - (k-1) \cdot (1 - \gamma)}{1 - (k-2) \cdot (1 - \gamma)}.
\end{align*}
Moreover,
\begin{align*}
    \gamma\left({P_{n}^{\bigtriangledown}}\right) \geq \frac{1 - (n-1) \cdot (1 - \gamma)}{n}.
\end{align*}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:LO26-improved}
Let $(X,\mu)$ be a pure $n$-partite complex, and assume $P^{\tau}$ is irreducible for all $\tau \in X$ of codimension at least $2$. Suppose there exists $0 < \epsilon < 1$ such that $\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{I}) \leq 1 - \epsilon$. Then for every $2 \leq k \leq n$ and every $\tau \in X(n-k)$,
\begin{align*}
    \gamma\left({P^{\tau}}\right) \geq 1 - \frac{1 - \epsilon}{1 + \epsilon \cdot (k-2)}.
\end{align*}
Moreover,
\begin{align*}
    \gamma\left({P_{n}^{\bigtriangledown}}\right) \geq \frac{\epsilon}{n}.
\end{align*}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:bochner}
Let $P$ be an irreducible Markov chain that is reversible with respect to its stationary distribution $\mu$, and let $\+L = \mathsf{Id} - P$ be its Laplacian. Then $P$ has spectral gap at least $\gamma$ if and only if $\+L^{2} - \gamma \+L \succeq 0$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The statement that $P$ has spectral gap at least $\gamma$ is equivalent to the spectral inequality $\+L \succeq \gamma \cdot \mathsf{Id}$ restricted to the subspace $\mathbf{1}^{\perp}$ endowed with inner product $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle_{\mu}$. On this inner product space, $\+L$ is positive definite by irreducibility and hence, is invertible. Hence, on this subspace, we have that $\+L \succeq \gamma \cdot \mathsf{Id}$ is equivalent to $\+L^{2} \succeq \gamma \+L$ by conjugating by $\+L^{1/2}$ or $\+L^{-1/2}$ appropriately.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
We note that irreducibility is essential.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:universality}
Suppose $0<\epsilon\leq n$ and $\gamma\left({P_{n}^{\bigtriangledown}}\right) \geq \frac{\epsilon}{n}$. Then $\gamma\left({P^{\emptyset}}\right) \geq 1 - \frac{1-\epsilon}{1 + \epsilon \cdot (n-2)}$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\cite[Section~3.1]{AnariEtAl2024} proved the above with denominator $\epsilon(n-1)$ in place of $1+\epsilon(n-2)$. The above gives a slight sharpening that makes the two claims of \cref{thm:oppenheim} quantitatively consistent with each other. A short proof is provided in \cref{sec:universality-proof} to keep this note self-contained.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- observation-1: body_md -->
\label{obs:L-down-up}
For all $\tau \in X(n-1)$, we have $Q_\tau^2 = Q_\tau$ and $\+L_\tau^2 = \+L_\tau$. Furthermore, $\+L = n\left(\mathsf{Id} - P_n^{\bigtriangledown}\right)$.
<!-- end observation-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The first claim is immediate from the definition of $Q_{\tau}$. For the second claim, we have
\begin{align*}
    (\+L f)(\sigma)
    &= \sum_{\tau \in X(n-1)} (\+L_\tau f)(\sigma) \\
    &= \sum_{\tau \in X(n-1): \tau \subseteq \sigma} \left(f(\sigma) -  \ifthenelse{\isempty{\eta\sim \mu^{\tau}}}
  {\mathbb{E}\left[f(\eta)\right]}
  {\mathbb{E}_{\eta\sim \mu^{\tau}}\left[f(\eta)\right]} \right) \\
    &= n\cdot f(\sigma) - n \cdot \frac{1}{n}\sum_{\tau \in X(n-1): \tau \subseteq \sigma}  \ifthenelse{\isempty{\eta\sim \mu^{\tau}}}
  {\mathbb{E}\left[f(\eta)\right]}
  {\mathbb{E}_{\eta\sim \mu^{\tau}}\left[f(\eta)\right]}  \\
    &= n \left({\left({\mathsf{Id} - P_n^{\bigtriangledown}}\right) f}\right)(\sigma),
\end{align*}
where the second equation follows by $(\+L_\tau f)(\sigma) = 0$ for $\sigma\not\supseteq \tau$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:garland}
    Suppose $n \geq 2$. For every $\alpha \in X(n-2)$, define
    \begin{align*}
        \+L_{\alpha} \overset{\mathsf{def}}{=} \sum_{\tau \in X(n-1) : \tau \supseteq \alpha} \+L_\tau.
    \end{align*}
    Then, it holds that 
    \begin{align*}
        \+L^2 - \+L = \sum_{\alpha \in X(n-2)} \left(\+L_{\alpha}^2 - \+L_{\alpha}\right).
    \end{align*}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By definition of $\+L$, we have
    \begin{align*}
        \+L^2 - \+L
        &= \sum_{\substack{\sigma,\tau \in X(n-1) \\ \left|{\sigma \oplus \tau}\right| \geq 2}} \+L_\sigma \+L_\tau
        = \sum_{\substack{\sigma,\tau \in X(n-1) \\ \left|{\sigma \oplus \tau}\right| = 2}} \+L_\sigma \+L_\tau,
    \end{align*}
    where the last equation follows by the fact that if $\left|{\sigma\oplus \tau}\right| \geq 3$, then there is no $\eta \in X(n)$ such that both $\eta\supseteq \sigma$ and $\eta \supseteq \tau$ hold. Now, note that for every $\sigma,\tau \in X(n-1)$ such that $\left|{\sigma\oplus\tau}\right| = 2$, there is a unique $\alpha \in X(n-2)$ such that $\sigma,\tau \supseteq \alpha$. Hence, it is straightforward to regroup the right hand side by $\alpha \in X(n-2)$ and finish the proof.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:local-expansion}
    Let $\alpha \in X(n-2)$. Assume the down-up walk on $\mu^{\alpha}$ is irreducible.
    The following inequality holds in $L^2(\mu)$ (and restricts to $L^2(\mu^{\alpha})$),
    \begin{align*}
        \+L_{\alpha}^2 \succeq \gamma\left({P^{\alpha}}\right) \cdot \+L_{\alpha}.
    \end{align*}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The block operator vanishes outside facets containing $\alpha$, so we focus on $(X^{\alpha}, \mu^{\alpha})$ and the space $L^2(\mu^{\alpha})$. If $\mu^{\alpha}$ is a point mass, the claim is trivial. Now, let $P_2^{\bigtriangledown}$ denote the down-up walk on this specific link $(X^{\alpha},\mu^{\alpha})$. By \Cref{obs:L-down-up}, $\+L_{\alpha} = 2(\mathsf{Id} - P_2^\bigtriangledown)$ and hence, the target inequality is equivalent to 
    \begin{align*}
        \left({\mathsf{Id} - P_2^\bigtriangledown}\right)^{2} \succeq \frac{\gamma\left({P^{\alpha}}\right)}{2} \cdot \left({\mathsf{Id} - P_2^\bigtriangledown}\right).
    \end{align*}
    Since the down-up walk is irreducible, by \cref{lem:bochner}, it suffices to show that
    \begin{align*}
        \lambda_2\left({P_2^\bigtriangledown}\right) &= \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \lambda_2(P^{\alpha}).
    \end{align*}
    In fact, $P_{2}^{\bigtriangledown}$ and $\frac{1}{2} \cdot \left({\mathsf{Id} + P^{\alpha}}\right)$ have the exact same spectrum up to the multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue. Indeed, let $D^{\alpha}$ denote the transition matrix that encodes the following action: Given $\sigma \in X(n)$ satisfying $\sigma \supseteq \alpha$, remove a uniformly random element in $\sigma \setminus \alpha$ from $\sigma$ to obtain $\tau \in X(n-1)$. Let $U^{\alpha}$ denote the transition matrix of the adjoint action: Given $\tau \in X(n-1)$ satisfying $\tau \supseteq \alpha$, sample a random element from $\mu_{1}^{\tau}$ and add it to $\tau$ to obtain $\sigma \in X(n)$. It is straightforward to verify that $P_{2}^{\bigtriangledown} = D^{\alpha}U^{\alpha}$ and $\frac{1}{2} \cdot \left({\mathsf{Id} + P^{\alpha}}\right) = U^{\alpha}D^{\alpha}$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let us focus first on lower bounding the spectral gap of the global down-up walk $P_{n}^{\bigtriangledown}$. Irreducibility of all local walks $P^{\tau}$ ensures irreducibility of $P_{n}^{\bigtriangledown}$. Hence, by \cref{lem:bochner} and \cref{obs:L-down-up}, it suffices to show that $\+L^2 \succeq (1 - (n-1) \cdot (1 - \gamma)) \cdot \+L$. For this, we have
    \begin{align*}
        \+L^2 - \+L
        &= \sum_{\alpha \in X(n-2)} \left(\+L_{\alpha}^2 - \+L_{\alpha}\right) \tag{\cref{prop:garland}} \\
        &\succeq -(1 - \gamma) \sum_{\alpha \in X(n-2)} \+L_{\alpha} \tag{\cref{prop:local-expansion}} \\
        &= -(n-1) \cdot (1 - \gamma) \cdot \+L,
    \end{align*}
    where in the last equation, we use the fact that for every $\sigma \in X(n-1)$, there are exactly $(n-1)$ distinct $\alpha \in X(n-2)$ with $\alpha \subseteq \sigma$. This proves $\gamma\left({P_{n}^{\bigtriangledown}}\right) \geq \frac{1 - (n-1) \cdot (1 - \gamma)}{n}$ as desired.

    Regarding the first claim on spectral gaps of local walks, fix $\tau \in X(n-k)$ where $2 \leq k \leq n$. Applying the preceding argument mutatis mutandis to the link $(X^{\tau},\mu^{\tau})$ shows that its global down-up walk has spectral gap at least $\frac{1 - (k-1) \cdot (1 - \gamma)}{k}$. Applying \cref{lem:universality} with $\epsilon=1-(k-1)(1-\gamma)\in(0,k]$ then completes the proof.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:LO26-local-mixing}
  For every $u \neq v$ in $[n]$, every $\sigma \in X(n-2)$ such that $u,v \not\in V(\sigma)$, and every function $f$,
  \begin{align*}
    \left\langle \+L_u f, \+L_v f\right\rangle_{\mu^{\sigma}} \geq -\lambda_2(P^{\sigma}) \cdot \left\|{\+L_u f}\right\|_{\mu^{\sigma}, 2} \cdot \left\|{\+L_v f}\right\|_{\mu^{\sigma}, 2}.
  \end{align*}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:LO26-local-mixing}
For every $u \neq v$ in $[n]$ and every function $f$,
\begin{align*}
      \left\langle \+L_{u} f, \+L_{v} f\right\rangle_{\mu} \geq -\mathcal{I}(u,v) \cdot \left\|{\+L_{u}f}\right\|_{\mu,2} \cdot \left\|{\+L_{v}f}\right\|_{\mu,2}.
  \end{align*}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Averaging over $\sigma=\eta\setminus(U_u\cup U_v)$ for $\eta\sim\mu$, we have
  \begin{align*}
    \left\langle \+L_u f, \+L_v f\right\rangle_{\mu}
    &=  \ifthenelse{\isempty{\sigma}}
  {\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle \+L_u f, \+L_v f\right\rangle_{\mu^{\sigma}}\right]}
  {\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}\left[\left\langle \+L_u f, \+L_v f\right\rangle_{\mu^{\sigma}}\right]}  \tag{Law of Total Probability} \\
    &\geq -\mathcal{I}(u,v) \cdot  \ifthenelse{\isempty{\sigma}}
  {\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\+L_u f}\right\|_{\mu^{\sigma}, 2} \cdot \left\|{\+L_v f}\right\|_{\mu^{\sigma}, 2}\right]}
  {\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}\left[\left\|{\+L_u f}\right\|_{\mu^{\sigma}, 2} \cdot \left\|{\+L_v f}\right\|_{\mu^{\sigma}, 2}\right]}  \tag{\cref{lem:LO26-local-mixing}} \\
    &\geq -\mathcal{I}(u,v) \cdot \left\|{\+L_u f}\right\|_{\mu, 2} \cdot \left\|{\+L_v f}\right\|_{\mu, 2}. \tag{Cauchy--Schwarz and $\mathcal{I}(u,v) \geq 0$}
  \end{align*}
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We first bound the spectral gap of the global down-up walk. Observe that for any $f$,
  \begin{align*}
    \left\|{\+L f}\right\|_{\mu,2}^2
    &= \sum_w \left\|{\+L_w f}\right\|_{\mu,2}^2 + \sum_{u \neq v} \left\langle \+L_u f, \+L_v f\right\rangle_{\mu} \\
    &\geq \sum_w \left\|{\+L_w f}\right\|_{\mu,2}^2 - \sum_{u \neq v} \mathcal{I}(u,v) \cdot \left\|{\+L_u f}\right\|_{\mu, 2} \cdot \left\|{\+L_v f}\right\|_{\mu, 2} \tag{\cref{cor:LO26-local-mixing}} \\
    &= \*x^{\intercal} \left({\mathsf{Id} - \mathcal{I}}\right) \*x \tag{where $\*x \in {\mathbb{R}}^{n}$ has $x_{u} = \left\|{\+L_{u} f}\right\|_{\mu,2}$} \\
    &\geq \epsilon \*x^{\intercal} \*x \tag{$\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{I}) \leq 1 - \epsilon$} \\
    &= \epsilon \left\langle f, \+L f\right\rangle_{\mu}. \tag{Using $\+L_{u}^{2} = \+L_{u}$ for all $u \in [n]$}
  \end{align*}
  Thus $\+L^2\succeq\epsilon\+L$. By \cref{lem:bochner} and $\+L = n\left({\mathsf{Id}-P_n^{\bigtriangledown}}\right)$, this proves $\gamma(P_n^{\bigtriangledown})\geq\epsilon/n$ as desired.

  Regarding the first claim on spectral gaps of local walks, fix $\tau \in X(n-k)$ where $2 \leq k \leq n$. For $u,v \notin V(\tau)$, the spectral influence $\mathcal{I}^{\tau}(u,v)$ after conditioning on $\tau$ is upper bounded by the unconditional spectral influence $\mathcal{I}(u,v)$. Hence, by monotonicity of the spectral radius for nonnegative matrices (extending $\mathcal{I}^{\tau}$ by zero on the fixed parts), $\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{I}^{\tau}) \leq \lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{I}) \leq 1 - \epsilon$. Applying the preceding argument mutatis mutandis to the global down-up walk of the conditional measure $\mu^{\tau}$ then yields a spectral gap lower bound of $\epsilon/k$. \cref{lem:universality} then finishes the proof.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:linear-func-sufficiency}
  If \cref{eq:LO26-local-target} holds for all linear functions, then it holds for all functions.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
For an operator $Q$, we use $\mathsf{Ran}\; Q$ and $\mathsf{Ker}\; Q$ to denote the range and kernel of $Q$, respectively. Recalling that $P_{u},P_{v}$ are the heat-bath projections defined above, define $K \overset{\mathsf{def}}{=} \mathsf{Ker}\; P_u \cap \mathsf{Ker}\; P_v$. Since $P_u$ and $P_v$ are self-adjoint with respect to $\left\langle \cdot, \cdot\right\rangle$, the subspaces $K, K^\perp$ are closed under both $P_u$ and $P_v$.

Now, let $f$ be an arbitrary function, and decompose it as $f = f^K + f^{\perp K}$ where $f^K \in K$ and $f^{\perp K} \in K^{\perp}$. If we can establish that every function in $K^{\perp}$ is linear, then
\begin{align*}
  \left\langle \+L_u f, \+L_v f\right\rangle
  &= \left\langle \+L_u f^K, \+L_v f^K\right\rangle + \left\langle \+L_u f^{\perp K}, \+L_v f^{\perp K}\right\rangle \\
  &= \left\|{f^K}\right\|^2 + \left\langle \+L_u f^{\perp K}, \+L_v f^{\perp K}\right\rangle \\
  &\geq \left\langle \+L_u f^{\perp K}, \+L_v f^{\perp K}\right\rangle \\
  &\geq -\lambda_{2}(P^{\sigma}) \cdot \left\|{\+L_{u} f^{\perp K}}\right\| \cdot \left\|{\+L_{v} f^{\perp K}}\right\| \tag{Linearity of $f^{\perp K}$} \\
  &\geq -\lambda_{2}(P^{\sigma}) \cdot \left\|{\+L_{u} f}\right\| \cdot \left\|{\+L_{v} f}\right\| \tag{Using $\lambda_{2}(P^{\sigma}) \geq 0$}
\end{align*}
completes the proof. To show that all functions in $K^{\perp}$ are linear, note that $K^\perp = \mathsf{Ran}\; P_u + \mathsf{Ran}\; P_v$. Hence, if $f \in K^\perp$, there exist functions $\tilde{g}, \tilde{h}$ such that
\begin{align*}
  f = P_{u}\tilde{g} + P_{v}\tilde{h} =  \ifthenelse{\isempty{}}
  {\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{g}(\eta)\mid \eta(v)\right]}
  {\mathbb{E}_{}\left[\tilde{g}(\eta)\mid \eta(v)\right]}  +  \ifthenelse{\isempty{}}
  {\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{h}(\eta) \mid \eta(u)\right]}
  {\mathbb{E}_{}\left[\tilde{h}(\eta) \mid \eta(u)\right]} , \quad \eta \sim \mu^{\sigma}.
\end{align*}
Clearly, $g \overset{\mathsf{def}}{=} P_{u}\tilde{g}$ depends only on coordinate $v$ and $h \overset{\mathsf{def}}{=} P_{v}\tilde{h}$ depends only on coordinate $u$.

Noting that $\+L_{\sigma}=\+L_u+\+L_v$ on this link, \cref{prop:local-expansion} applied to the $2$-partite link $(X^{\sigma},\mu^{\sigma})$ gives
\begin{align*}
    \left\langle f, (\+L_u + \+L_v)^2 f\right\rangle \geq (1-\lambda_2(P^{\sigma})) \cdot \left\langle f, (\+L_u + \+L_v)f\right\rangle.
\end{align*}
Expanding $(\+L_u + \+L_v)^2$ and rearranging gives
\begin{align} \label{eq:Bochner-local-GD}
    \left\langle \+L_uf, \+L_v f\right\rangle \geq -\lambda_2(P^{\sigma}) \cdot \frac{\left\|{\+L_u f}\right\|^2 + \left\|{\+L_v f}\right\|^2}{2},
\end{align}
where we used $\+L_u^2 = \+L_u, \+L_v^2 = \+L_v$ and self-adjointness. We would like to replace the arithmetic mean in the right-hand side with the geometric mean to conclude \cref{eq:LO26-local-target}. By \Cref{lem:linear-func-sufficiency}, we may assume without loss of generality that $f = g + h$, where $g, h$ depend only on coordinates $v$ and $u$, respectively. Noting that $P_u g = g, P_v h = h$ and $\+L_u g = 0, \+L_v h = 0$, \cref{eq:Bochner-local-GD} is equivalent to
\begin{align*}
    \left\langle \+L_uh, \+L_v g\right\rangle \geq -\lambda_2(P^{\sigma}) \cdot \frac{\left\|{\+L_u h}\right\|^2 + \left\|{\+L_v g}\right\|^2}{2}.
\end{align*}
Since this holds for any $g,h$, we have that for every $t > 0$,
\begin{align*}
    \left\langle \+L_uf, \+L_v f\right\rangle = \left\langle \+L_uh, \+L_v g\right\rangle 
    &= \left\langle \+L_u (th), \+L_v (g/t)\right\rangle \\
    &\geq -\lambda_2(P^{\sigma}) \cdot \frac{t^2 \left\|{\+L_u h}\right\|^2 + \left\|{\+L_v g}\right\|^2/t^2}{2} \\
    &= -\lambda_2(P^{\sigma}) \cdot \frac{t^2 \left\|{\+L_u f}\right\|^2 + \left\|{\+L_v f}\right\|^2/t^2}{2}.
\end{align*}
  If either norm is zero, the claim is immediate. Otherwise, taking $t^2 = \left\|{\+L_v f}\right\|/\left\|{\+L_u f}\right\|$ finishes the proof.
