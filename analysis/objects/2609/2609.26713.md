<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:BN_existence}
Let $C$ be a general curve of genus $g$, by which we mean a general point $C \in \mathcal{M}_g$. Then, there exists a non-degenerate map $f\colon C \to \mathbb{P}^r$ of degree $d$ if and only if the \emph{Brill-Noether number}
\[
\rho(g,r,d) := g - (r+1)(g-d+r)
\]
is non-negative.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:degenerate_nice}
Let $C$ be a smooth curve. A map $f\colon C \to \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^s}\mathbb{P}^r$ is \emph{non-degenerate} if it is so after post-composing with both the blow-up morphism $b\colon \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^s}\mathbb{P}^r \to \mathbb{P}^r$ and the projection $\pi\colon \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^s}\mathbb{P}^r \to \mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}$.

We say that $f$ is \emph{nice} if, in addition, $H^1(C,f^*T_{\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^s}\mathbb{P}^r})=0$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:degenerate}
In fact, if \(b\circ f\) is non-degenerate, then \(\pi\circ f\) is automatically non-degenerate as well. However, we have included both requirements in the definition of non-degeneracy of \(f\) for emphasis.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $C$ be a general curve of genus $g$. Let $r,s$ be non-negative integers with $s \leq r-2$. Then, a nice map $f\colon C \to \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^s}\mathbb{P}^r$ of degree $(d,k)$ exists if and only if the following three inequalities hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] $\rho(g,r,d) := g-(r+1)(g-d+r)\ge0$,
\item[(2)] $\rho_{\bullet}(g,d,k;r,s) := \rho(g,r,d)+(r-s)(s+1)-(r-s-1)k\ge0$, and
\item[(3)] $\rho'(g,d,k;r,s) := \rho(g,r-s-1,d-k)\ge0$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:not_nice}
In fact, in characteristic zero, whenever the moduli space of non-degenerate maps $f\colon C \to \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^s}\mathbb{P}^r$ is non-empty, it is known to be pure of the expected dimension (\cite[Theorem 0.1]{farkas}; see also \cite[Corollary 2.3.4]{cl_complete} or Theorem \ref{thm:farkas} below). Thus, conditions (1)-(3) are equivalently necessary and sufficient with the requirement that $f$ be non-degenerate, but not necessarily nice. In positive characteristic, it could be true in principle that non-degenerate, but non-nice maps for which $\rho_{\bullet}<0$ exist, though this would be ruled out with a proof of Theorem \ref{thm:farkas} in arbitrary characteristic. We also do not know, even in characteristic 0, whether any non-degenerate map is automatically nice, which would be an analog of the Gieseker-Petri theorem.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:notation}
We have chosen to diverge in our notation from \cite{farkas}, as follows. We have indexed our invariants with the integers $g,d,k;r,s$, whereas \cite{farkas} uses instead $g,r,d$, in addition to $e=k$ and $f=k-s-1$. The quantity $f$ is natural for the following reason: a non-degenerate curve in $\mathbb{P}^r$ automatically possesses an $(s+1)$-secant $s$-plane, so $f=k-s-1$ measures the extent to which an unexpected secant is present. However, our arguments will be uniform in $k$, allowing in particular $k<s+1$. (See below for a discussion of the case $k=0$, which recovers the classical Brill-Noether theorem.) Moreover, we take the perspective of studying the moduli space of maps to a fixed blow-up $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^s}\mathbb{P}^r$, as opposed to maps to projective space with a fixed excess degree of secancy.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{eg:intro_example}
Let $(g,d,k;r,s)=(8,10,5;3,1)$. Then, Theorem \ref{thm:main} asserts the existence of a map $f\colon C \to \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^1}\mathbb{P}^3$ of degree $(10,5)$ out of a general curve of genus $8$. Equivalently, $f$ is a space curve of degree $10$ with a $5$-secant line. The existence of such an $f$ is not immediately given by \cite[Theorem 0.5]{farkas}.

We prove the existence of such an $f$ as follows. We construct a map
\[
f\colon C' \cup R_1 \cup R_2 \to \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^1}\mathbb{P}^3,
\]
where $C' \to \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^1}\mathbb{P}^3$ is a nice map of degree $(6,3)$ out of a general curve of genus $4$, and the maps $R_i \to \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^1}\mathbb{P}^3$ are maps of degree $(2,1)$ out of rational curves $R_i \cong \mathbb{P}^1$. The rational components are attached to $C'$ at $3$ nodes each. The map $C' \to \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^1}\mathbb{P}^3$ may be regarded as a canonically embedded
\[
C' \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^3,
\]
where the blow-up center $\mathbb{P}^1 \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ is a ruling of the quadric surface $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^3$. An analysis of the restricted tangent bundle $T_{\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^1}\mathbb{P}^3}|_{C'}$ will show that $f$ may be deformed to a nice map of degree $(10,5)$ out of a smooth curve of genus $8$.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\cite[Definition 4.2]{chz}
\label{def:ILS}
Let $C$ be a smooth curve of genus $g$. Fix integers $d \geq k \geq 0$, and $r,s$ with $-1 \leq s \leq r-1$. An \emph{inclusion of linear series} (\emph{ILS}) $(\mathcal{L},D,V \subset W)$ on $C$ consists of the following data.
\begin{itemize}
\item A linear series $(\mathcal{L},W) \in G^r_d(C)$,
\item an effective divisor $D \subset C$ of degree $k$, and
\item a subspace \(V \subset W\) of dimension \(r-s\) whose sections vanish along \(D\), giving a linear series $(\mathcal{L}(-D),V) \in G^{r-s-1}_{d-k}(C)$.
\end{itemize}
The moduli space of ILS on $C$ is denoted $G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:bpf}
An ILS $(\mathcal{L},D,V \subset W) \in G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)$ is \emph{base-point-free} (bpf) if both $(\mathcal{L},W) \in G^r_d(C)$ and $(\mathcal{L}(-D),V) \in G^{r-s-1}_{d-k}(C)$ are. Equivalently, the following two conditions both hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(BPF1)] $(\mathcal{L}(-D),V) \in G^{r-s-1}_{d-k}(C)$ is bpf.
\item[(BPF2)] For all $p \in D$, not every section of $W \subset H^0(C,\mathcal{L})$ vanishes at $p$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:farkas}\cite[Theorem 0.1]{farkas}
Suppose that $\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0$. Then, $G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)$ is pure of dimension
\[
\rho_{\bullet}(g,d,k;r,s) := \rho(g,r,d) + (r-s)(s+1) - (r-s-1)k,
\]
the expected. If $\rho_{\bullet}(g,d,k;r,s)<0$, then $G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)$ is empty.

In particular, $\mathcal{M}_{(d,k)}(C,X_{r,s})^\circ$ is pure of dimension
\[
\rho_{\bullet}(g,d,k;r,s) + \dim(\operatorname{Aut}(X_{r,s})) = \chi(C,f^*T_{X_{r,s}}) = (r+1)d - (r-s-1)k - rg + r,
\]
the expected.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:valid}
Fix $r,s$ with $0 \leq s \leq r-2$. We say that a tuple $(g,d,k;r,s)$ is \emph{valid} if $g,k \geq 0$, and, in addition, the quantities
\[
\rho(g,r,d), \quad \rho_{\bullet}(g,d,k;r,s), \quad \rho'(g,d,k;r,s),
\]
appearing in Theorem \ref{thm:main}, are all non-negative. 

We will often abbreviate these quantities by $\rho,\rho_{\bullet},\rho'$, respectively. The triple $(\rho,\rho_{\bullet},\rho')$ is called the \emph{profile} of $(g,d,k;r,s)$. 

We write $\mathrm{Ex}(g,d,k;r,s)$ for the statement that Theorem \ref{thm:main} holds for $(g,d,k;r,s)$. When $r,s$ are fixed, we will often drop them from the notation, e.g., writing $\mathrm{Ex}(g,d,k)$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rmk:degree-checks}
    We record the following basic observation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho \geq 0 &\implies d \geq r,\\
\rho' \geq 0 &\implies d-k \geq r-s-1.
\end{aligned}
\]
    In particular, if $(g,d,k;r,s)$ is valid, then the inequalities $d \geq r$ and $d-k \geq r-s-1$, which are necessary for non-degenerate maps to exist, are automatic.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\,
\label{prop:inductive_steps_main}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(L)] If \(\mathrm{Ex}(g-1,d-1,k)\) holds, then \(\mathrm{Ex}(g,d,k)\) holds.
    \item[(R)] If \(\mathrm{Ex}(g-(r-s),d-(r-s),k-1)\) holds, then \(\mathrm{Ex}(g,d,k)\) holds.
    \item[\((T_\ell)\)] Let \(\ell\) be an integer with \(0\leq \ell\leq s+1\). If
    \[
    \mathrm{Ex}\bigl(g-(r+1)-\ell(r-s-1),d-r-\ell(r-s-1),k-\ell\bigr)
    \]
    holds, then \(\mathrm{Ex}(g,d,k)\) holds.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:dimension_effects}
In each of the three cases of Proposition~\ref{prop:inductive_steps_main}, passing from \((g,d,k)\) to the predecessor (e.g. \((g-1,d-1,k)\)) has the following effects on the profile \((\rho,\rho_\bullet,\rho')\).
\begin{itemize}
    \item[(L)] \((\rho,\rho_\bullet,\rho')\to(\rho-1,\rho_\bullet-1,\rho'-1)\).
    \item[(R)] \((\rho,\rho_\bullet,\rho')\to(\rho-(r-s),\rho_\bullet-1,\rho')\).
    \item[\((T_\ell)\)] \((\rho,\rho_\bullet,\rho')\to(\rho-(r-s-1)\ell,\rho_\bullet,\rho'-(s+1-\ell))\).
\end{itemize}
Note that the values of \(\rho,\rho_\bullet,\rho'\) can only decrease under each of these decrements, as must be the case for the statements in Proposition~\ref{prop:inductive_steps_main} to be plausible.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:attach_and_smooth}
Let \(f'\colon C'\to X\) be a nice map, and let \(R\) be a connected, nodal curve of arithmetic genus \(0\). Let \(C=C'\cup R\) be a nodal curve given by attaching \(C'\) and \(R\) at nodes \(C'\cap R=\{x_1,\ldots,x_m\}\). Let \(f\colon C\to X\) be any map for which \(f|_{C'}=f'\). Suppose that
\[
H^1\bigl(R,f^*T_X(-x_1-\cdots-x_m)\bigr)=0.
\]
Then, \(f\) deforms to a nice map.

In particular, write \(g'=g(C')\), and \((d',k')\) for the degree of \(f'\). Let \((\delta,\kappa)\) be the degree of \(f|_R\). Then, \(\mathrm{Ex}(g'+m-1,d'+\delta,k'+\kappa)\) holds.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We claim first that \(H^1(C,f^*T_X)=0\). Indeed, write \(\nu\colon C'\sqcup R\to C\) for the normalization map. Then, the short exact sequence
\[
0 \to f^*T_X \to \nu_*\nu^*(f^*T_X)
   \to \bigoplus_{i=1}^m (T_X)_{f(x_i)}
   \to 0.
\]
induces an exact sequence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact_sequence_normalization}
H^0(C',f^*T_X)\oplus H^0(R,f^*T_X)\to \bigoplus_{i=1}^m (T_X)_{f(x_i)}\to H^1(C,f^*T_X)\to H^1(C',f^*T_X)\oplus H^1(R,f^*T_X)=0.
\end{equation}
The vanishing of the last term follows from the assumptions that \(f'\) is nice and that \(H^1\bigl(R,f^*T_X(-x_1-\cdots-x_m)\bigr)=0\). On the other hand, we also have an exact sequence
\[
H^0\bigl(R,f^*T_X(-x_1-\cdots-x_m)\bigr)\to H^0(R,f^*T_X)\to \bigoplus_{i=1}^m (T_X)_{f(x_i)}\to H^1\bigl(R,f^*T_X(-x_1-\cdots-x_m)\bigr)=0,
\]
so the first map in \eqref{eq:exact_sequence_normalization} is surjective. It follows that \(H^1(C,f^*T_X)=0\).

Therefore, \(f\) defines a smooth point of expected dimension for the morphism
\[
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g+m-1,0}(X,(d'+\delta,k'+\kappa))\to \mathfrak{M}_{g+m-1},
\]
where $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g+m-1,0}(X,(d'+\delta,k'+\kappa))$ is the moduli space of stable maps and $\mathfrak{M}_{g+m-1}$ is the moduli space of prestable curves. We conclude that there exists an open neighborhood of \(f\) dominating \(\mathfrak M_{g+m-1}\), and containing nice maps of degree \((d'+\delta,k'+\kappa)\). Note in particular that a general deformation of $f$ remains non-degenerate.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
\label{rem:make_nodal_curve}
In the setting of Lemma~\ref{lem:attach_and_smooth}, suppose the data of \(f'\colon C'\to X\) is given, along with points \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\in C'\). Then, constructing a map \(f\colon C=C'\cup R\to X\) amounts to finding a nodal rational curve \(R\) with smooth points (abusively denoted) \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\in R\), along with a map \(h\colon R\to X\) with \(f'(x_i)=h(x_i)\) for all \(i=1,\ldots,m\). Indeed, a map out of a nodal curve is equivalent to the data of a map out of its normalization, mapping preimages of any given node to the same point. Concretely, one needs to find a rational curve interpolating through (the images under \(f'\) of) the points \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\) in \(X\).

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:curve_linearly_general}
Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\in C'\) be general points and $\Pi_1,\ldots, \Pi_\ell \subseteq \mathbb{P}^r$ be any collection of linear spaces. Then, (the images under \(f'\) of) the points \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\) in \(X\) are in linearly general position with respect to each $\Pi_1,\ldots, \Pi_\ell$.

In particular, if \(m\leq r-s\), then the linear span of the \(x_j\) in \(\mathbb{P}^r\) does not meet the blown-up linear space \(\mathbb{P}^s\subset \mathbb{P}^r\), and if \(m=r-s+1\), then \(\langle x_1,\ldots,x_{r-s+1}\rangle\cap \mathbb{P}^s\) is a single point.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let \(s_i= \mathrm{dim}(\Pi_i)\), and let \(\pi_i: \mathbb{P}^r \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^{r-s_i-1}\) be the projection from \(\Pi_i\), for \(i=1,\ldots, \ell\). The condition of linear generality is manifestly Zariski open, so it suffices to construct one collection of points \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\in C'\) with the desired property. This can be done inductively as follows. At the $j$-th step, choose \(x_j\in C'\) in such a way that it does not lie in the linear span of any \(r\) of the previously chosen points in \(\mathbb{P}^r\), or any \(r-s_i-1\) of the previously chosen points in \(\mathbb{P}^{r-s_i-1}\), for \( i=1,\ldots, \ell\). This is possible exactly because the image of \(f'\) is not contained in any hyperplane in \(\mathbb{P}^r\) or \(\mathbb{P}^{r-s_i-1}\).
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:L}
 If \(\mathrm{Ex}(g-1,d-1,k)\) holds, then \(\mathrm{Ex}(g,d,k)\) holds.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let \(f'\colon C'\to X\) be a nice map of degree \((d-1,k)\), where the genus of \(C'\) is \(g-1\). Let \(x_1,x_2\in C'\) be general points. By Lemma \ref{lem:curve_linearly_general}, the unique line through \(x_1,x_2\) in \(\mathbb{P}^r\) does not meet the blow-up center \(\mathbb{P}^s\subset \mathbb{P}^r\). Therefore, we may take \(h\colon R=\mathbb{P}^1\to X\) to be the inclusion of this line passing through \(x_1,x_2\) in \(X\), of degree \((1,0)\).

The restricted tangent bundle \(h^*T_X\) is the same as the restricted tangent bundle of the inclusion of a line \(h\colon \mathbb{P}^1\to \mathbb{P}^r\), which is
\[
h^*T_{\mathbb{P}^r}\simeq \mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus r-1}\oplus \mathcal{O}(2),
\]
see e.g. \cite[Proposition 3.1]{larson} or Lemma \ref{lem:tangent_degenerate_curve} below. Therefore, taking \(C=C'\cup \mathbb{P}^1\) to be the curve obtained by gluing \(C'\) and \(\mathbb{P}^1\) at nodes \(x_1,x_2\), the maps \(f'\), \(h\) together induce a map \(f\colon C\to X\), satisfying the hypothesis of Lemma \ref{lem:attach_and_smooth}. The conclusion follows.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:R}
If \(\mathrm{Ex}(g-(r-s),d-(r-s),k-1)\) holds, then \(\mathrm{Ex}(g,d,k)\) holds.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let \(f'\colon C'\to X\) be a nice map of degree \((d-(r-s),k-1)\), where the genus of \(C'\) is \(g-(r-s)\). Let \(x_1,\dots,x_{r-s+1}\in C'\) be general points. By Lemma \ref{lem:curve_linearly_general}, the linear span \(\Lambda\cong \mathbb{P}^{r-s}\subset \mathbb{P}^r\) of \(x_1,\dots,x_{r-s+1}\) meets the blow-up center \(\mathbb{P}^s\subset \mathbb{P}^r\) in a unique point \(q\). Moreover, the points \(x_1,\dots,x_{r-s+1},q\in \Lambda\) are themselves in linearly general position, or else the linear span of a strict subset of \(x_1,\dots,x_{r-s+1}\) would meet \(\mathbb{P}^s\) non-trivially, contradicting Lemma \ref{lem:curve_linearly_general}.

Therefore, there exists an embedded rational normal curve \(R\subset \Lambda\), passing through \(x_1,\dots,x_{r-s+1},q\). Moreover, \(R\) meets \(\mathbb{P}^s\subset \mathbb{P}^r\) only in the point \(q\), because \(\Lambda\cap \mathbb{P}^s=\{q\}\). It follows that \(R\subset \Lambda\) lifts to a map \(h\colon R=\mathbb{P}^1\to X\) of degree \((r-s,1)\), whose image contains \(x_1,\dots,x_{r-s+1}\). Furthermore, we have
\[
h^*T_X\cong \mathcal{O}(r-s)^{\oplus r-1}\oplus \mathcal{O}(r-s+1),
\]
by Lemma \ref{lem:tangent_r-s} below. We now conclude by Lemma \ref{lem:attach_and_smooth}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:tangent_r-s}
Let \(h\colon \mathbb{P}^1\to X\) be a map of degree \((r-s,1)\), given by the proper transform of a rational normal curve \(R\subset \Lambda\cong \mathbb{P}^{r-s}\subset \mathbb{P}^r\), where \(\Lambda\cap \mathbb{P}^s=\{q\}\). Then,
\[
h^*T_X\cong \mathcal{O}(r-s)^{\oplus r-1}\oplus \mathcal{O}(r-s+1).
\]
Furthermore, let \(x\in \mathbb{P}^1\) be any point not mapping to the exceptional divisor of $X$. Then, the fiber of the summand \(\mathcal{O}(r-s+1)\subset h^*T_X\) at $x$ corresponds to the tangent direction at \(x\in \mathbb{P}^r\) pointing toward \(q\).
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let \(\widetilde{\Lambda}\cong \operatorname{Bl}_q(\mathbb{P}^{r-s})\subset X\) be the proper transform of \(\Lambda\subset \mathbb{P}^r\). Then, we have a short exact sequence
\[
0\to T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}|_R\to h^*T_X\to N_{\widetilde{\Lambda}/X}|_R\to 0.
\]
Because \(\widetilde{\Lambda}\subset X\) is cut out by the proper transforms of \(s\) general hyperplanes in \(\mathbb{P}^r\), we have
\[
N_{\widetilde{\Lambda}/X}|_R\cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^r}(1)^{\oplus s}|_R\cong \mathcal{O}(r-s)^{\oplus s}.
\]

Next, let \(\pi\colon \widetilde{\Lambda}\to \mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}\) be the projection. Then, we have a short exact sequence
\[
0\to T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}/\mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}}|_R\to T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}|_R\to T_{\mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}}|_R\to 0.
\]
The image of \(R\) in \(\mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}\) is a rational normal curve, and any two rational normal curves are projectively equivalent. We conclude (e.g. by \cite[Proposition 3.2]{larson}) that
\[
T_{\mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}}|_R\cong \mathcal{O}(r-s)^{\oplus r-s-1}.
\]
By comparing with the degree of \(T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}|_R\), we deduce that \(T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}/\mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}}|_R\cong \mathcal{O}(r-s+1)\). Therefore, we have
\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1(T_{\mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}}|_R,T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}/\mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}}|_R)=0
\Rightarrow
T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}|_R\cong \mathcal{O}(r-s)^{\oplus r-s-1}\oplus \mathcal{O}(r-s+1).
\]
We have in turn that
\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1(N_{\widetilde{\Lambda}/X}|_R,T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}|_R)=0
\Rightarrow
h^*T_X\cong \mathcal{O}(r-s)^{\oplus r-1}\oplus \mathcal{O}(r-s+1),
\]
as needed.

Finally, the summand \(\mathcal{O}(r-s+1)\) is precisely the summand coming from the relative tangent bundle \(T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}/\mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}}|_R\), which is naturally identified with the bundle of tangent directions pointing toward \(q\).

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:family_of_maps}
Let \(\Pi\subset X\) be a linear subspace\footnote{By a linear subspace, we mean the proper transform of a linear subspace of \(\mathbb{P}^r\).} of dimension \(m\leq s\), with the property that the image of \(\Pi\) under the projection \(\pi\colon X\to \mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}\) is a point. Let \(B\subset \Pi\) be a finite union of proper linear subspaces. Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_{r-s}\in X\) be points in linearly general position with respect to \(\Pi\) and all linear subspaces comprising \(B\). Fix a rational curve \(\mathbb{P}^1\), and let \(y',p_1,\ldots,p_{r-s},y\in \mathbb{P}^1\) be general points.

Let \(\mathcal{F}\) be the family of maps \(h\colon \mathbb{P}^1\to X\) of degree \((r-s,1)\) satisfying the following properties:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(h(p_j)=x_j\) for \(j=1,\ldots,r-s\), and
\item \(h(y)\in \Pi\setminus B\).
\end{itemize}
Then, we have the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \(\dim(\mathcal{F})=m+1\).
\item[(b)] Varying over all maps \(h\in \mathcal{F}\), the points \(h(y')\in X\) sweep out a subvariety \(\Pi'\setminus B'\subset X\), where \(\Pi'\subset X\) is a linear subspace of dimension \(m+1\), whose image under \(\pi\) is a point, and \(B'\) is a finite union of proper linear subspaces.
\item[(c)] The map \(\mathcal{F}\to \Pi'\setminus B'\) given by evaluation at \(y'\) is an isomorphism of schemes.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Let \(\overline{\mathcal{F}}\) be the family of maps defined in the same way as \(\mathcal{F}\), except that we relax the condition \(h(y)\in \Pi\setminus B\) to \(h(y)\in \Pi\). Clearly, \(\mathcal{F}\subset \overline{\mathcal{F}}\) is an open subset. We will explicitly parametrize \(\overline{\mathcal{F}}\) in terms of local coordinates.

First, recall that a map \(h\colon \mathbb{P}^1\to X\) of degree \((r-s,1)\) is given by a tuple
\[
h=[u_0u_1:\cdots:u_0u_{r-s}:u_{r-s+1}:\cdots:u_{r+1}],
\]
where
\begin{itemize}
\item \(u_0\in H^0(\mathbb{P}^1,\mathcal{O}(1))\), \(u_1,\ldots,u_{r-s}\in H^0(\mathbb{P}^1,\mathcal{O}(r-s-1))\), and \(u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}\in H^0(\mathbb{P}^1,\mathcal{O}(r-s))\),
\item the collections of sections \(u_1,\ldots,u_{r-s}\) and \(u_0,u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}\) both do not simultaneously vanish, and are both taken up to the same simultaneous scaling, and
\item (base-point-freeness) the sections \(u_0u_1,\ldots,u_{r+1}\in H^0(\mathbb{P}^1,\mathcal{O}(r-s))\) share no common factors.
\end{itemize}
For \(j=1,\ldots,r+1\), write \(e_j\in \mathbb{P}^r\) for the point
\[
e_j=[0:\cdots:0:1:0:\cdots:0],
\]
where \(1\) appears in the \(j\)-th coordinate. For \(j<r-s\), the point \(e_j\in \mathbb{P}^r\) may be identified with a point \(e_j\in X\). For \(j>r-s\), the point \(e_j\in \mathbb{P}^r\) lies in the blown-up subspace \(\mathbb{P}^s\subset \mathbb{P}^r\). 

We may assume that the coordinates are chosen in such a way that
\begin{itemize}
\item \(x_j=e_j\), for \(j=1,\ldots,r-s\),
\item \(\Pi\subset X\) is the proper transform of \(\langle [1:\cdots:1],e_{r-s+1},\ldots,e_{r-s+m}\rangle\subset \mathbb{P}^r\).
\end{itemize}
Choose homogeneous coordinates \(z,w\) on \(\mathbb{P}^1\). Identify \(p_1,\ldots,p_{r-s}\in \mathbb{P}^1\) with elements of \(\mathbb{K}\subset \mathbb{P}^1\), taking \(\frac{z}{w}\) to be an affine coordinate, and assume also that \(y'=\infty\) and \(y=0\). From the fact that \(h(p_j)=x_j\) for \(j=1,\ldots,r-s\), and that \(\pi(h(y))=\pi(h(0))=[1:\cdots:1]\in \mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}\), we deduce that
\begin{align*}
u_1 &= p_1(z-p_2w)\cdots(z-p_{r-s}w),\\
u_2 &= p_2(z-p_1w)(z-p_3w)\cdots(z-p_{r-s}w),\\
&\vdots\\
u_{r-s} &= p_{r-s}(z-p_1w)(z-p_2w)\cdots(z-p_{r-s-1}w).
\end{align*}
 In particular, the sections \(u_1,\ldots,u_{r-s}\in H^0(\mathbb{P}^1,\mathcal{O}(r-s-1))\) are determined, as is the point
\[
\pi(h(y'))=\pi(h(\infty))=[p_1:\cdots:p_{r-s}]\in \mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}.
\]

Next,  \(u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}\) must all vanish at \(p_1,\ldots,p_{r-s}\), and \(u_{r-s+m+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}\) must all be equal at \(y=0\). We conclude that we must have
\begin{align*}
u_{r-s+1} &= \gamma_1(z-p_1w)\cdots(z-p_{r-s}w),\\
&\vdots\\
u_{r-s+m} &= \gamma_m(z-p_1w)\cdots(z-p_{r-s}w),\\
u_j &= \gamma(z-p_1w)\cdots(z-p_{r-s}w), \qquad j=r-s+m+1,\ldots,r+1,
\end{align*}
for some \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m,\gamma\in \mathbb{K}\).

Finally, write \(u_0=\alpha z-\beta w\), for some \(\alpha,\beta\in \mathbb{K}\). Then, because 
\[
u_0u_1,\ldots,u_0u_{r-s},u_{r-s+m+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}
\]
must all be equal at \(y=0\), we have
\[
(-\beta)(-1)^{r-s-1}p_1p_2\cdots p_{r-s}
=
\gamma(-1)^{r-s}p_1p_2\cdots p_{r-s}
\Longrightarrow \gamma=\beta.
\]

The base-point-free condition on the sections \(u_j\) amounts to the requirement that \(\alpha,\beta\) are not both \(0\), and furthermore that \(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\neq p_1,\ldots,p_{r-s}\). Therefore, \(\overline{\mathcal{F}}\) is identified (scheme-theoretically) with the open subset of the projective space \(\mathbb{P}^{m+1}\) with homogeneous coordinates
\[
\alpha,\beta,\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m,
\]
where \((\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)\) and \(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\neq p_1,\ldots,p_{r-s}\).

Now, evaluation at \(y'=\infty\) gives a map
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{ev}_\infty\colon \overline{\mathcal{F}} &\to X,\\
[\alpha:\beta:\gamma_1:\cdots:\gamma_m] &\mapsto [\alpha\cdot p_1:\cdots:\alpha\cdot p_{r-s}:\gamma_1:\cdots:\gamma_m:\beta:\cdots:\beta].
\end{align*}
It is clear by inspection that \(\operatorname{ev}_\infty\) is an isomorphism onto its image, which is the complement of a finite union of linear subspaces in a linear space \(\Pi'\subset X\) of dimension \(m+1\). Furthermore, we have \(\pi(\Pi')=[p_1:\cdots:p_{r-s}]\).

It remains to restrict \(\operatorname{ev}_\infty\) to the open subset \(\mathcal{F}\subset \overline{\mathcal{F}}\). However, \(\mathcal{F}\subset \overline{\mathcal{F}}\) is also the complement of a finite union of linear spaces, under the identification of \(\overline{\mathcal{F}}\) with an open subset of \(\mathbb{P}^{m+1}\). Indeed, for any \(u\in \overline{\mathcal{F}}\), we have
\[
h(0)=[\beta\cdot 1:\cdots:\beta\cdot 1:\gamma_1:\cdots:\gamma_m:\beta:\cdots:\beta].
\]
In particular, removing \(h\in \overline{\mathcal{F}}\) with the property that \(h(0)\in B\subset \Pi\) amounts to removing the loci on \(\overline{\mathcal{F}}\) where certain linear equations in \(\beta,\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m\) vanish. All three of the needed statements (a)-(c) follow.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
We now carry out the construction of the desired map \(h\colon R\to X\), outlined at the beginning of this section. Choose points \(x_{\ell 1},\ldots,x_{\ell,r-s+1}\in C'\) in linearly general position. Apply Lemma \ref{lem:family_of_maps} with:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(\Pi=x_{\ell,r-s+1}\) and \(B=\emptyset\),
\item \(x_j=x_{\ell j}\) for \(j=1,\ldots,r-s\),
\item \(\mathbb{P}^1=R_\ell\), and
\item \((y',y)=(y_{\ell-1},y_\ell)\).
\end{itemize}
Let \(\mathcal{F}_\ell\) be the resulting family of maps \(h^\ell\colon R_\ell\to X\), and define \(\Pi_{\ell-1}=\Pi'\), \(B_{\ell-1}=B'\). Proceeding inductively in the order \(R_{\ell-1},\ldots,R_1\), apply Lemma \ref{lem:family_of_maps} with:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(\Pi=\Pi_i\),
\item \(x_j=x_{ij}\) for \(j=1,\ldots,r-s\), where \(x_{ij}\in C'\) are chosen in linearly general position with respect to \(\Pi_i\) and \(B_i\),
\item \(\mathbb{P}^1=R_i\), and
\item \((y',y)=(y_{i-1},y_i)\).
\end{itemize}
Let \(\mathcal{F}_i\) be the resulting family of maps \(h^i\colon R_i\to X\), and define \(\Pi_{i-1}=\Pi'\), \(B_{i-1}=B'\).

Choose \(x_{01},\ldots,x_{0,r-\ell+1}\in C'\) in linearly general position with respect to \(\Pi_0\) and \(B_0\). Let \(\Lambda_0=\langle x_{01},\ldots,x_{0,r-\ell+1}\rangle\cong \mathbb{P}^{r-\ell}\) and let \(x=\Lambda_0\cap(\Pi_0\setminus B_0)\). Then, observe that \(x_{01},\ldots,x_{0,r-\ell+1},x\) must be in linearly general position on \(\Lambda_0\). Indeed, if this were not the case, then there would exist a point \(x\in\Pi_0\) lying on a common \((r-\ell-1)\)-plane with a strict subset of \(x_{01},\ldots,x_{0,r-\ell+1}\), contradicting linear generality. 

Therefore, there exists an \((r-\ell-1)\)-parameter family of embedded rational normal curves \(h^0\colon R_0\hookrightarrow\Lambda_0\) passing through all of the points \(x_{01},\ldots,x_{0,r-\ell+1}\). A general such rational normal curve \(h^0\) misses the proper linear subspace \(\mathbb{P}^s\cap\Lambda_0\), because \(s\leq r-2\). Choose such a general \(h^0\), and define \(p_{01},\ldots,p_{0,r-\ell+1}\in R_0\) in such a way that \(h^0(p_{0j})=x_{0j}\) for \(j=1,\ldots,r-\ell+1\), in addition to \(h^0(y_0)=x\). Then, \(h^0\) lifts to a map \(h^0\colon R_0\hookrightarrow X\) of degree \(r-\ell,0\), as it image does not meet the blown-up subspace \(\mathbb{P}^s\subset \mathbb{P}^r\).

Finally, the point \(x\in\Pi_0\setminus B_0\) uniquely determines a map \(h^1\in\mathcal{F}_1\), which in turn determines a point \(h^1(y_1)\in\Pi_1\setminus B_1\). Continuing in this fashion, we uniquely determine maps \([h^i\colon R_i\to X]\in \mathcal{F}_i\), with the property that
\[
h^i(y_i)=h^{i+1}(y_i)\in\Pi_i\setminus B_i
\]
for each \(i\). In particular, we obtain a map \(h\colon R\to X\) satisfying the needed degree and incidence conditions.

It remains to prove that \(H^1(R,h^*T_X(-\sum p_{ij}))=0\). A straightforward calculation shows that \(\chi(R,h^*T_X(-\sum p_{ij}))=0\), so it suffices to show that \(H^0(R,h^*T_X(-\sum p_{ij}))=0\). This amounts to the statement that the map \(h\) admits no nontrivial first-order deformations, subject to the fixed incidence conditions \(h(p_{ij})=x_{ij}\).

Fix a first-order deformation of \(h\). We show first that the image \(h(y_0)\) deforms trivially to first order. Indeed, \(h(y_0)\) must deform along both \(\Lambda_0\) and \(\Pi_0\), but the intersection \(\Lambda_0\cap\Pi_0=x\) is transverse. Now, by Lemma \ref{lem:family_of_maps}(c), it follows that \(h^1\) also deforms trivially, and therefore that \(h(y_1)=h^1(y_1)\) also deforms trivially. Continuing inductively in this manner, we deduce that all of the restrictions \(h^1,\ldots,h^{\ell}\) deform trivially. Finally, the restriction \(h^0\colon R_0\to X\) deforms to first order, preserving \(r-\ell+2\) incidence conditions \(h^0(p_{i0})=x_{i0}\) and \(h(y_0)=x\). By Lemma \ref{lem:tangent_degenerate_curve} below, the deformation of \(h^0\) must also be trivial. Therefore, \(h\) admits no non-trivial deformations.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:tangent_degenerate_curve}
Let \(h_0\colon R_0=\mathbb{P}^1\to X\) be a curve of degree \((r-\ell,0)\), where \(1\le \ell\le r-1\), given by the proper transform of a rational normal curve \(R_0\subset \Lambda_0\cong \mathbb{P}^{r-\ell}\subset \mathbb{P}^r\). Then, we have
\[
h_0^*T_X\cong \mathcal{O}(r-\ell)^{\oplus \ell}\oplus \mathcal{O}(r-\ell+1)^{\oplus r-\ell}.
\]
Furthermore, the subbundle \(\mathcal{O}(r-\ell+1)^{\oplus r-\ell}\subset h_0^*T_X\) is identified with \(T_{\Lambda_0}|_{R_0}\).
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
We have a short exact sequence
\[
0\to T_{\Lambda_0}|_{R_0}\to h_0^*T_X\to N_{\Lambda_0/X}|_{R_0}\to 0.
\]
Arguing as in the proof of Lemma \ref{lem:tangent_r-s}, we have
\[
h_0^*T_X\cong N_{\Lambda_0/X}|_{R_0}\oplus T_{\Lambda_0}|_{R_0}\cong \mathcal{O}(r-\ell)^{\oplus \ell}\oplus \mathcal{O}(r-\ell+1)^{\oplus r-\ell},
\]
as needed.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
First, assume that \(\ell\ge 1\), as is the case in Situation \ref{sit:curve_many_points}. Let \(f\colon C=C'\cup R\to X\) be the map given by gluing the maps \(f'\colon C'\to X\) and \(h\colon R\to X\) (Proposition \ref{prop:tree_exists}) along the nodes \(x_{ij}\). By the cohomology vanishing in of Proposition \ref{prop:tree_exists}, the criterion of Lemma \ref{lem:attach_and_smooth} applies, and we conclude immediately.

It remains to consider the case \(\ell=0\). In this case, we take \(x_1,\dots,x_{r+2}\in C'\) to be general points, and \(h\colon R\to \mathbb{P}^r\) to be a rational normal curve in \(\mathbb{P}^r\) passing through all of the \(x_i\). Arguing as in the proof of Proposition \ref{prop:tree_exists}, there is an \((r-1)\)-dimensional family of such rational normal curves \(h\), and a general such misses the blow-up center \(\mathbb{P}^s\subset \mathbb{P}^r\). Therefore, \(h\) lifts to a map \(h\colon R\to X\) of degree \((r,0)\). Because
\[
h^*T_X\cong h^*T_{\mathbb{P}^r}\cong \mathcal{O}(r+1)^{\oplus r},
\]
the criterion of Lemma \ref{lem:attach_and_smooth} again applies. This completes the proof.

<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
\label{def:terminal}
We say that a valid tuple \((g,d,k)\) is \emph{terminal} if it lies in one of the following five families.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(Ia)] \((g,d,k)=(r+1,2r,k)\), where
\[
s+1+\frac{1}{r-s}\le k\le s+1+ \frac{s+1}{r-s}
\]

<!-- proposition-6: body_md -->
\label{prop:rho_0}

<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Because

Now, we compute that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho'&=(r-s)(d-k)-(r-s-1)g-(r-s)(r-s-1) \\

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{lem:check_bpf}
Let \(C\) be a general curve of genus \(g\), and fix \(r,s,d,k\) with $s\le r-2$. Assume that, for all \(d',k'\) satisfying $d'\le d$ and $d'-k'\le d-k$, the moduli space of ILS \(G^{r,r-s-1}_{d',k'}(C)\) is pure of expected dimension. Then, the bpf locus in \(G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)\) is dense.
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
The proof is very similar to (but easier than) that of \cite[Proposition 2.6.2]{cl_complete}. Suppose for sake of contradiction that there is an irreducible component \(G\subset G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)\), on which a general point \(\underline{W}\in G\) is not bpf. Fix such a general point \(\underline{W}=(\mathcal{L},D,V\subset W)\). Consider the following two operations on ILS (\cite[Definition 2.5.1]{cl_complete}).
 \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(T1)] If every section of $V\subset H^0(C,\mathcal{L}(-D))$ vanishes at $p\in C$, then replace $D$ with $D+p$, leaving $\mathcal{L},V,W$ the same. We now have $(\mathcal{L},D,V\subset W)\in G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k+1}(C)$.
    
    \item[(T2)] If every section of $W$ vanishes at $p\in \operatorname{Supp}(D)$, then replace $D$ with $D-p$ and $\mathcal{L}$ with $\mathcal{L}(-p)$. The subspaces $V\subset W$ are identified with subspaces of the new $H^0(C,\mathcal{L})$ in the obvious way. We now have $(\mathcal{L},D,V\subset W)\in G^{r,r-s-1}_{d-1,k-1}(C)$.   
\end{enumerate}

We may apply the operation (T1) a total of \(\delta_1\geq 0\) times, at various points \(p\in C\), until property (BPF1) is satisfied, and then apply operation (T2) \(\delta_2\geq 0\) times, until \(\underline{W}\) is bpf. By assumption, \(\delta_1,\delta_2\) are not both \(0\). After possibly passing to an étale neighborhood of \(G\)\footnote{In order to make sense of the operations (T1), (T2) in a neighborhood of \(\underline{W}\), one needs a consistent choice of point \(p\in C\) over which the twisting can be applied. Such a choice may only exist after passing to an étale open neighborhood of \(\underline{W}\); see also the footnote to \cite[Proof of Proposition 2.6.2]{cl_complete}.}, this sequence of operations defines a rational map
\[
\tau\colon G\dashrightarrow G^{r,r-s-1}_{d-\delta_2,k+\delta_1-\delta_2}(C),
\]
dominating the bpf locus. The fibers of \(\tau\) have dimension \(\delta_2\), corresponding to choices of twisted points \(p\in C\) in the operations (T2). Therefore, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dim(G)&\le\delta_2+\dim\bigl(G^{r,r-s-1}_{d-\delta_2,k+\delta_1-\delta_2}(C)\bigr) \\
&=\rho_\bullet-\delta_1(r-s-1)-\delta_2(s+1) \\
&<\rho_\bullet,
\end{aligned}
\]
which is a contradiction.

Recalling that we set \(q=\left\lceil \frac{s+1}{r-s}\right\rceil\), the Serre dual tuple is
\[
(g,\widetilde{d},k;\widetilde{r},\widetilde{s})
=
\bigl(g,(r-s+1)q,s+1+q;q,q-1\bigr),
\]
where we have also applied Lemma \ref{lem:rho_prime_0_profile}. Note in particular that \(\widetilde{s}=\widetilde{r}-1\).

Therefore, the moduli space of ILS \(G^{\widetilde{r},\widetilde{r}-\widetilde{s}-1}_{\widetilde{d},k}(C)=G^{\widetilde{r},0}_{\widetilde{d},k}(C)\) parameterizes the data of:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a point \(\widetilde{W}\in G^{\widetilde{r}}_{\widetilde{d}}(C)\),
    \item an effective divisor \(D\subset C\) of degree \(k=s+1+q\), and
    \item a line \(\ell\subset \widetilde{W}\), consisting of sections vanishing along \(D\).
\end{itemize}
We have
\[
\dim\bigl(G^{\widetilde{r}}_{\widetilde{d}}(C)\bigr)=\rho(g,\widetilde{r},\widetilde{d})=\rho'=0.
\]
Therefore, \(G^{\widetilde{r}}_{\widetilde{d}}(C)\) consists of finitely many smooth points, of which we may choose one, \(\widetilde{W}\in G^{\widetilde{r}}_{\widetilde{d}}(C)\). Then, \(\widetilde{W}\) is bpf, because \(\rho(g,\widetilde{r},\widetilde{d}-1)<\rho(g,\widetilde{r},\widetilde{d})=0\), and complete, because \(\rho(g,\widetilde{r}+1,\widetilde{d})<\rho(g,\widetilde{r},\widetilde{d})=0\). Let \(f\colon C\to \mathbb{P}^{\widetilde{r}}\) be a map with underlying linear series \(\widetilde{W}\). 

The data of a line \(\ell\subset \widetilde{W}\) of sections vanishing along \(D\) is equivalently the data of a hyperplane \(H\subset \mathbb{P}^{\widetilde{r}}\) for which \(D\subset f^{-1}(H)\). Therefore, the open and closed subscheme \(G_{\widetilde{W}}\subset G^{\widetilde{r},\widetilde{r}-\widetilde{s}-1}_{\widetilde{d},k}(C)\), consisting of ILS with underlying \(\widetilde{W}\in G^{\widetilde{r}}_{\widetilde{d}}(C)\), admits the structure of a finite cover
\[
\gamma\colon G_{\widetilde{W}}\to (\mathbb{P}^{\widetilde{r}})^\vee
\]
of the space of hyperplanes \(H\subset \mathbb{P}^{\widetilde{r}}\). In particular, \(G_{\widetilde{W}}\) is pure of dimension \(\widetilde{r}=q=\rho_\bullet\), the expected. The last equality used Lemma \ref{lem:rho_prime_0_profile}.

By Bertini smoothness, a general hyperplane \(H\subset \mathbb{P}^{\widetilde{r}}\) has the property that \(f^{-1}(H)\) is reduced, which implies that every point of \(\gamma^{-1}(H)\) is a smooth point of \(G_{\widetilde{W}}\). In particular, any general point of \(G_{\widetilde{W}}\) is smooth of expected dimension.

Finally, it suffices to show that a general point of \(G_{\widetilde{W}}\) is also complete. This amounts to the statement that \(H\subset \mathbb{P}^{\widetilde{r}}\) is the \emph{unique} hyperplane containing \(D\). Because
\[
k=s+1+q>q=\widetilde{r},
\]
one can arrange for \(D\) to contain a collection of \(\widetilde{r}\) general points on \(C\), which in particular lie on a unique hyperplane. Therefore, we conclude by Corollary \ref{cor:char_0_serre}.

To compute the dimension of \(G^{\widetilde{r},1}_{\widetilde{d},k}(C)\) near \(\underline{W}\), it suffices to fix \(f\). Then, an ILS \(\underline{W}\) is determined by the data of:
\begin{itemize}
\item a line bundle \(\mathcal{L}\in W^{\widetilde{r}}_{\widetilde{d}}(C)\), such that \(\mathcal{L}\otimes \mathcal{N}^\vee=\mathcal{L}\otimes f^*\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1)\) is effective,
\item an effective divisor \(D\in |\mathcal{L}\otimes \mathcal{N}^\vee|\), and
\item a linear series \(W\subset H^0(C,\mathcal{L})\) of rank \(\widetilde{r}+1\), containing \(V\).
\end{itemize}

To understand the space of possible \(\mathcal{L}\in \operatorname{Pic}^{\widetilde{d}}(C)\) satisfying the first condition, we will apply the main dimensional transversality theorem of Hurwitz-Brill-Noether theory, \cite[Theorem 1.2]{hlar}. Namely, consider the vector bundle \(f_*\mathcal{L}\) on \(\mathbb{P}^1\), which has rank \(\widetilde{d}-k=r+2\) and degree
\[
\widetilde{d}-g+1-(r+2)=k-2r-1.
\]
Then, we have a splitting
\[
f_*\mathcal{L}\cong \mathcal{O}(e_1)\oplus \cdots \oplus \mathcal{O}(e_{r+2}),
\]
where \(e_1\leq \cdots \leq e_{r+2}\). 

The Picard variety is stratified by loci \(\Sigma_{\vec e}\subset \operatorname{Pic}^{\widetilde{d}}(C)\) for which \(f_*\mathcal{L}\) has splitting type \(\vec e=(e_1,\ldots,e_{r+2})\). \cite[Theorem 1.2]{hlar} asserts that \(\Sigma_{\vec e}\) is non-empty and pure of dimension
\[
g-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r+2}(e_j-e_i-1),
\]
if this quantity is non-negative, and empty otherwise. In terms of the splitting of \(f_*\mathcal{L}\), the condition that \(h^0(\mathcal{L})\geq \widetilde{r}+1\) is equivalent to
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{r+2}\max\{0,e_i+1\}\geq \widetilde{r}+1,
\]
and the condition that \(\mathcal{L}\otimes f^*\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1)\) is effective is equivalent to simply \(e_{r+2}\geq 1\), by the projection formula.

Now, the dimension of \(G^{\widetilde{r},1}_{\widetilde{d},k}(C)\) near \(\underline{W}\) is equal to
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\vec e}
&:= \dim(\Sigma_{\vec e})+\bigl(h^0(C,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{N}^\vee)-1\bigr)+\dim\bigl(\operatorname{Gr}(\widetilde{r}-1,H^0(C,\mathcal{L})/V)\bigr)\\
&= \dim(\Sigma_{\vec e})+\bigl(h^0(\mathbb{P}^1,f_*\mathcal{L}\otimes \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(-1))-1\bigr)+\dim\bigl(\operatorname{Gr}(\widetilde{r}-1,h^0(\mathbb{P}^1,f_*\mathcal{L})-2)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
All three quantities are in particular determined by the splitting type \(\vec e\). It is now a purely numerical statement that \(\Delta_{\vec e}\leq \rho_{\bullet}\), with equality if and only if
\[
\vec e=(e_1,\ldots,e_{r+s-k+2},0,\ldots,0,1),
\]
where \(e_{r+s-k+2}-e_1\leq 1\). While one can prove this numerical statement directly, we have the following cheaper finish.

Namely, because the non-emptiness and dimensional transversality of the splitting locus \(\Sigma_{\vec e}\subset \operatorname{Pic}^{\widetilde{d}}(C)\) are independent of characteristic, the dimension of \(G^{\widetilde{r},1}_{\widetilde{d},k}(C))\) is also independent of characteristic. Therefore, we may conclude immediately by the characteristic \(0\) statement, Theorem \ref{thm:farkas}.

As explained in \cite{hlar}, the main theorems of Hurwitz-Brill-Noether theory hold for a general point \(f\colon C\to \mathbb{P}^1\) in the Hurwitz space of simply branched covers, lying in the same component as a particular degenerate cover \(f\). <!-- proposition-23: body_md -->
\label{prop:pointed_rho_zero}
Let $(C,p_0)$ be a general pointed curve of genus $g$. Fix $r,d$, and a vanishing sequence $\vec{a}$ such that $\rho(g,r,d,\vec{a})=0$. Let $W$ be a linear series of rank $r$, degree $d$, and vanishing sequence $\vec{a}$ at $p_0$.

Fix an integer $s$ with $-1 \leq s \leq r-2$, and let $V \subset W$ be the unique subspace of sections of dimension $r-s$ with vanishing sequence $a_{s+1}<\cdots<a_r$ at $p_0$. Then, $V$ has no base points on $C$ away from $p_0$.

<!-- proof-35: body_md -->
Fix a degeneration
\[
(C,p_0) \rightsquigarrow (C_0 = E_1 \cup \cdots \cup E_g,p_0)
\]
as above, and let $W_0$ be the limit of $W$ on $C_0$. Let $V_0$ be the limit of the subspace $V \subset W$ on $C_0$, and write $V_1,\ldots,V_g$, with $V_i \subset W_i$, for the aspect of $V_0$ on $E_i$. We claim that, for all $i = 1,\ldots,g$, we have
\[
V_i = \langle \sigma^i_{s+1},\ldots,\sigma^i_r\rangle.
\]
Equivalently, $V_i \subset W_i$ is the unique subspace with vanishing sequences
\[
a^i_{s+1}<\cdots<a^i_r \quad \text{and} \quad b^i_{s+1}>\cdots>b^i_r
\]
at $p_{i-1}$ and $p_i$, respectively. 

We argue by induction on $i$. This is clear for $i = 1$, by the requirement that $V$ have vanishing sequence $a_{s+1}<\cdots<a_r$ at $p_0$. Then, if $V_i = \langle \sigma^i_{s+1},\ldots,\sigma^i_r\rangle$, then in order for $V_i,V_{i+1}$ to satisfy the compatibility condition for limit linear series, the vanishing sequence of $V_{i+1}$ at $p_i$ must be \emph{at least}
\[
(d-b^i_{s+1},\ldots,d-b^i_r) = (a^{i+1}_{s+1},\ldots,a^{i+1}_r).
\]
It follows that we must have $V_{i+1} = \langle \sigma^{i+1}_{s+1},\ldots,\sigma^{i+1}_r\rangle$, as needed.

Now, it suffices to show that each $V_i$ has no base points away from $p_{i-1}$ and $p_i$.\footnote{If so, a base point of $V$ could only specialize to a node of $C_0$, but this is also not possible by standard semi-stable reduction argument.}

Recall that each section $\sigma^i_j$ has at most one point $q^i_j$ of simple vanishing away from $p_{i-1}$ and $p_i$. Moreover, varying over all $j$, the points $q^i_j$ must be pairwise distinct, or else $p_{i-1}-p_i$ is torsion. Because $\dim(V_i)=r-s \geq 2$, the base-point-freeness follows.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:farkas_nice}
Let $(g,d,k;r,s)$ be a terminal tuple of type (II), with $k \leq 2s+1$. Let $\underline{W_0}$ be the limit ILS on the singular curve $C_0 = Y \cup Z$ of genus $g$ constructed in \cite[\S 3]{farkas}. Then, $\underline{W_0}$ smooths to an ILS $\underline{W} \in G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)$ on a smooth curve $C$, which is a smooth point of dimension $0$, the expected, and bpf.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-36: body_md -->
We first recall the needed aspects of Farkas's construction. The curve $C_0$ is given by the nodal union of smooth components $Y,Z$ of genus $k$ and $g-k$, respectively, intersecting at $p$. The pointed curves $(Y,p)$ and $(Z,p)$ are assumed to be general. The limit ILS $\underline{W_0}$ is built from three pieces:
\begin{itemize}
\item a linear series $(\mathcal{A},W_{\mathcal{A}}) \in G^{r-s-1}_{d-k}(Y)$, with \emph{ramification}\footnote{While the results above involving limit linear series on chains of elliptic curves were more easily stated in terms of \emph{vanishing} sequences $a_i=\alpha_i+i$, here we match Farkas's convention to index instead the ramification sequences.} sequence $\alpha=(\alpha_0\leq\cdots\leq\alpha_{r-s-1})$ at $p$, where $\alpha_{r-s-1}-\alpha_0 \leq 1$
\item a linear series $(\mathcal{L},W_{\mathcal{L}}) \in G^s_{k+s}(Y)$, with ramification sequence $\beta=(  \beta_0\leq\cdots\leq\beta_s)$ at $p$, where $\beta_s - \beta_0 \leq 1$, and
\item a linear series $\mathfrak{l}_Z \in G^r_d(Z)$, with ramification sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:ram-seq-l}
\gamma: \quad k-\beta_s \leq \cdots \leq k-\beta_0 \leq d-r-\alpha_{r-s-1} \leq \cdots \leq d-r-\alpha_0
\end{equation}
at $p$.
\end{itemize}


The integers $\alpha_i$ and $\beta_i$ are chosen in the unique possible way such that each of the linear series $W_{\mathcal{A}}$, $W_{\mathcal{L}}$, and $\mathfrak{l}_Z$, together with their ramification data at $p$, defines a smooth, $0$-dimensional point in its respective moduli space of pointed linear series.

Farkas shows that, under the assumption $k \leq 2s+1$, \footnote{The assumption $k \leq 2s+1$ here is equivalent to condition (i) ($2f \leq e-1$) in \cite[Theorem 0.5]{farkas}.} one can furthermore arrange for
\begin{equation}\label{eqn: H0-vanishing-Gabi}
H^0(Y,\mathcal{L}\otimes \mathcal{A}^{\vee}\otimes \mathcal{O}_Y((d-k-s-1)p))=0.
\end{equation}
Therefore, by Riemann-Roch, the degree $k=g(Y)$ line bundle $\mathcal{L}\otimes \mathcal{A}^{\vee}\otimes \mathcal{O}_Y((d-k-s)p)$ on $Y$ has a \emph{unique} effective divisor $D$ in its linear system, disjoint from $p$.

From here, $(\mathcal{A}(D), W_{\mathcal{A}}(D) \oplus W_{\mathcal{L}}((d-k-s)p))$\footnote{One needs that $d \geq k+s$, which holds in terminal family (II). } is a rank $r$, degree $d$ linear series on $Y$, and together with $\mathfrak{l}_Z$, gives a refined limit linear series $W_0$ on $C_0$. Now, write $V_Z$ for the $(r-s)$-dimensional subspace of sections of $\mathfrak{l}_Z$ with ramification sequence
\[
d-r-\alpha_{r-s-1} \leq \cdots \leq d-r-\alpha_0.
\]
Then, $(\mathcal{A},W_{\mathcal{A}})$ and $V_Z(-kp)$ together define a refined sub-limit linear series $V_0 \subset W_0$ of rank $r-s-1$ and degree $d-k$, vanishing along $D \subset Y$. 

We claim that the data of $V_0,W_0,D$ define an isolated reduced point of the moduli space of limit ILS. To see this, consider a first order deformation $(D', \mathcal{M}, V_0' \subseteq W_0)$ of $(D, V_0 \subseteq W_0)$ over $S= \mathrm{Spec}( k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2))$. The curve $Y \cup_p Z$ is constant over $S$, and $V_0', W_0'$ are both refined limit linear series. The $Y$-aspect of $V_0'$ and the $Z$-aspect of $W_0'$ cannot deform, because $G^{r-s-1}_{d-k}(Y,\alpha)$ and $G_d^r(Z,\gamma)$ are 0-dimensional and reduced. By semicontinuity, the evaluation map
\[
\bigg( Y-\text{aspect of } W_0' \bigg) \to H^0((d-k-s)p,  \mathcal{M}|_{(d-k-s)p})
\]
has rank exactly $r-s$, so its kernel $K_S$ is locally free of rank $s+1$. Since $G^{s}_{k+s}(Y,\beta)$ is reduced and $0$-dimensional, the $S$-point $K_S(-(d-k-s)p) \in G^{s}_{k+s}(Y,\beta) (S) $ is constant and equal to $W_{\mathcal{L}}$. 
Also, by semicontinuity, \eqref{eqn: H0-vanishing-Gabi} holds over $S$, so $D$ does not move. Because $W_{\mathcal{A}}(D) $ and $W_{\mathcal{L}}((d-k-s)p) $ are constant over $S$, the $Y$-aspect of $W_0'$ is constant. Finally, the $Z$-aspect of $V_0'$ coincides with the intersection of the $Z$-aspect of $W_0'$ vanishing to order at least $d-r-\alpha_{r-s-1}+s+1$ at $p$, which is also fixed.

Finally, it suffices to check that the respective aspects of $V_0,W_0$ have no base points away from $p$. In the case of $V_0$, this follows from Proposition \ref{prop:pointed_rho_zero}, applied to $(\mathcal{A},W_{\mathcal{A}})$ and $V_Z \subset \mathfrak{l}_Z$. In the case of $W_0$, it again follows from Proposition \ref{prop:pointed_rho_zero}, applied to $(\mathcal{L},W_{\mathcal{L}})$ and all of $\mathfrak{l}_Z$.
