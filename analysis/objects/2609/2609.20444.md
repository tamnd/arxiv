\label{Rem-interface-unknown}
Since ${\cal I}_h\subset{\cal F}_h$, the unknowns attached to the interface faces are
single valued, and they approximate the average $\left\{\underline{u}\right\}$ rather than
either of the two traces; the jump $\left[\underline{u}\right]$ is not an unknown of the
method and is recovered a posteriori as the difference of the traces of the
two adjacent cell unknowns.  The discrete counterpart of the compliancy term
$\left\langle {\bf S}\left[\underline{u}\right],\left[\underline{v}\right]\right\rangle_\Gamma$ of (\ref{weak-form}) is produced by
the interface stabilization $s_{T^\Gamma}$, as the following computation
shows.  Let $F=T_1\cap T_2\in{\cal I}_h$ and assume for simplicity that
$h_{T_1}=h_{T_2}$ and that the two cells carry the same matrix ${\bf S}_h$.
Eliminating the sole interface unknown $\underline{v}_F$ from
$s_{T_1^\Gamma}+s_{T_2^\Gamma}$, i.e.\ minimising
\begin{align*}
  2\left\langle  {\bf S}_h(\underline{v}_F-\Pi_F\underline{v}_{T_1}), \underline{v}_F-\Pi_F\underline{v}_{T_1} \right\rangle_F
  +2\left\langle  {\bf S}_h(\underline{v}_F-\Pi_F\underline{v}_{T_2}), \underline{v}_F-\Pi_F\underline{v}_{T_2} \right\rangle_F
\end{align*}
over $\underline{v}_F\in\mathbb{P}^k(F)^d$, gives
$\underline{v}_F=\frac12\Pi_F(\underline{v}_{T_1}+\underline{v}_{T_2})$ and the value
$\left\langle {\bf S}_h\Pi_F\left[\underline{v}\right],\Pi_F\left[\underline{v}\right]\right\rangle_F$.  The factor $2$ in
(\ref{stabilization-term}) is thus precisely the one for which the elimination
of the interface unknown reproduces the compliancy term of (\ref{weak-form}),
with ${\bf S}$ replaced by its regularisation ${\bf S}_h$.

\label{Rem-limits}
The definition (\ref{penalty-matrix}) of ${\bf S}_h$ is designed so that one single
formulation covers the whole range of compliancies.  When $\alpha=\beta=0$,
i.e.\ for a perfectly bonded interface, we have ${\bf K}=\bf{0}$ and therefore
${\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h={\bf I}$ and
${\bf S}_h={\bf Q}{\bf P}^{-1}{\bf Q}^T =\frac{\mu+\lambda}{h_T}\underline{n}\otimes\underline{n}
+\frac{\mu}{h_T}\sum_{i=1,2}\underline{t}_i\otimes\underline{t}_i$; the interface faces
are then treated as ordinary faces and $s_{T^\Gamma}$ is a Nitsche-type
penalty enforcing the continuity of the displacement, the weights being the
same as those of $s_{T^{\backslash\Gamma}}$ up to the factor $2$ discussed in
Remark \ref{Rem-interface-unknown}.  In the opposite regime
$\alpha,\beta\gg h_T/\mu$, we have ${\bf S}_h\simeq{\bf K}^{-1}$, so that
$s_{T^\Gamma}$ represents the physical interface stiffness, and
${\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h\simeq\bf{0}$, so that the interface contributions to
(\ref{gradient-reconstruct}) and (\ref{Local-aT}) disappear; in the limit
$\alpha,\beta\to+\infty$ the interface unknowns become inactive and the
discrete problem decouples into two independent problems with a traction-free
boundary condition on $\Gamma$, which is the correct behaviour of
(\ref{elasticity-interface}) in that limit.  The perturbation $\bf P$ is what
makes both limits, and the degenerate cases $\alpha=0$ or $\beta=0$, uniformly
accessible; this is confirmed numerically in \S\ref{sec-numerics}.

\label{Lem-Basic-Ineq}
There are $C_{dtr}>0$ and $C_{mtr}>0$, such that the following inequalities hold true:
\begin{equation}\label{Lem-Discrete-trace-Ineq}
  \left\Vertv\right\Vert_F\leq C_{dtr} h_T^{-\frac12}\left\Vertv\right\Vert_T,\quad
    \forall \, v\in \mathbb{P}^l(T), \, \forall\, F\in{\cal F}_{\partial T},\, T\in{\cal T}_h,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{Lem-Multip-trace-Ineq}
  \left\Vertv\right\Vert_{\partial T}\leq  C_{mtr}\big( h_T^{-\frac12}\left\Vertv\right\Vert_T +h_T^{\frac12}\left\Vert\nabla v\right\Vert_T \big), \quad
    \forall\, v\in H^1(T),\, T\in{\cal T}_h.
\end{equation}
For any $T\in{\cal T}_h$, the following Poincar\'e inequality is valid
\begin{equation}\label{Lem-Poincare-Ineq}
  \left\Vertv\right\Vert_T\leq  C_Ph_T \left\Vert\nabla v\right\Vert_T, \quad
    \forall\, v\in H^1(T),\, \int_T v=0.
\end{equation}
Let $RM=\{\underline{v} \in \mathbb{P}^1(T)^d |\ {\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} (\underline{v}) ={\bf 0}\}$, the following second Korn's inequality holds for any $T\in{\cal T}_h, \, \underline{v}\in H^1(T)^d$ satisfying $(\underline{v},\underline{r})_T=0$, $\forall\, \underline{r}\in RM$,
\begin{equation}\label{Lem-Korn-Ineq}
  \left\Vert\nabla \underline{v}\right\Vert_T\leq  C_K \left\Vert{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} (\underline{v})\right\Vert_T.
\end{equation}

\label{Lem-Fce-Korn} Let $k\geq1$. For any $T\in{\cal T}_h$ and any

Let $\Pi_{RM}$ denote the $L^2(T)$-orthogonal projection onto the space
$RM$ of rigid-body motions defined in Lemma \ref{Lem-Basic-Ineq}.  Since
$RM\subset \mathbb{P}^1(T)^d$ and $k\geq1$, the trace on any face $F\in{\cal F}_{\partial T}$ of a
rigid-body motion belongs to $\mathbb{P}^k(F)^d$ so that
$\Pi_{\partial T}\Pi_{RM}(\underline{v}_T)=\Pi_{RM}(\underline{v}_T)$ and the left-hand side of
(\ref{Lem-Fce-Korn-0}) is unchanged if $\underline{v}_T$ is replaced by
$\underline{v}_T-\Pi_{RM}(\underline{v}_T)$.  Setting
$\underline{\psi}:=\underline{v}_T-\Pi_{RM}(\underline{v}_T)$ and using that $\Pi_{\partial T}$ is an
$L^2(\partial T)$-orthogonal projection, we get
\begin{align*}
  h_T^{-1}\left\Vert\underline{v}_T -\Pi_{\partial T}\underline{v}_T \right\Vert^2_{\partial T}
    = h_T^{-1}\left\Vert(I-\Pi_{\partial T} )\underline{\psi} \right\Vert^2_{\partial T}
    \leq h_T^{-1}\left\Vert\underline{\psi} \right\Vert^2_{\partial T}.
\end{align*}
The discrete trace inequality (\ref{Lem-Discrete-trace-Ineq}), the
Poincar\'e inequality (\ref{Lem-Poincare-Ineq}) applied component-wise, which
is licit since $\int_T\underline{\psi}=\underline{0}$, and Korn's inequality
(\ref{Lem-Korn-Ineq}), which is licit since $(\underline{\psi},\underline{r})_T=0$ for all
$\underline{r}\in RM$, then give in turn
\begin{align*}
  h_T^{-1}\left\Vert\underline{\psi} \right\Vert^2_{\partial T}
    &\leq C_{dtr}^2 h_T^{-2}\left\Vert\underline{\psi} \right\Vert^2_T,\\
  h_T^{-2}\left\Vert\underline{\psi} \right\Vert^2_T
    &\leq C_P^2 \left\Vert\nabla\underline{\psi} \right\Vert^2_T,\\
  \left\Vert\nabla\underline{\psi} \right\Vert^2_T
    &\leq C_K^2\left\Vert{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} (\underline{\psi}) \right\Vert^2_T .
\end{align*}
Finally, every element of $RM$ has a vanishing symmetric gradient, so that
${\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} (\underline{\psi})={\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} (\underline{v}_T)$, and collecting the three inequalities
above yields (\ref{Lem-Fce-Korn-0}). $\hfill\Box$

      +\frac12 \left\langle  ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K} {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{v}}_T) \underline{n}, 2\mu{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{v}_T) \underline{n} \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    &-\left\langle  \underline{v}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma} -\Pi_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}\underline{v}_T, 2\mu{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{v}_T) \underline{n} \right\rangle_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}  \nonumber\\
    &-\left\langle  ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h) (\underline{v}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} -\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\underline{v}_T), 2\mu{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{v}_T) \underline{n} \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma},
\end{align}
where we have also used that $2\mu{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{v}_T)\underline{n}$ has its components in
$\mathbb{P}^{k}(F)^d$ on every face $F\in{\cal F}_{\partial T}$, so that the traces
$\underline{v}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}-\underline{v}_T$ and $\underline{v}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\underline{v}_T$ appearing in
(\ref{gradient-reconstruct}) may be replaced by
$\underline{v}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}-\Pi_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}\underline{v}_T$ and
$\underline{v}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\underline{v}_T$, respectively.

<!-- head-4: body_md -->
It remains to bound $h_T^{-1}\left\Vert\underline{v}_{\partial T} -\underline{v}_T\right\Vert^2_{\partial T}$, in
which the {\em trace} of the cell unknown appears, whereas the right-hand side
of (\ref{Lem-equivalent-norm-0}) only involves its $L^2$-projection onto the
face spaces.  Writing
$\underline{v}_{\partial T} -\underline{v}_T=\big(\underline{v}_{\partial T} -\Pi_{\partial T}\underline{v}_T\big)
-\big(I-\Pi_{\partial T}\big)\underline{v}_T$ and recalling the definition
(\ref{stabilization-term}) of the stabilization bilinear form, we only need to
bound $h_T^{-1}\left\Vert(I-\Pi_{\partial T})\underline{v}_T\right\Vert^2_{\partial T}$, and this is precisely
the content of Lemma \ref{Lem-Fce-Korn}:
\begin{align*}
    h_T^{-1}\left\Vert(I-\Pi_{\partial T})\underline{v}_T \right\Vert^2_{\partial T}
    \leq C_{dtr}^2C_P^2C_K^2\left\Vert{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} (\underline{v}_T) \right\Vert^2_T .
\end{align*}
Together with (\ref{Lem-equivalent-norm-3}) and with the bound
$\left\vert{\bf S}_h^{\frac12}\right\vert^2\leq\mu/h_T$ of (\ref{Lem-Sh-2}), which allows us to
compare the ${\bf S}_h$-weighted face term of (\ref{Energy-norm-local}) with
$s_{T^{\Gamma}}$, this gives the inequality
(\ref{Lem-equivalent-norm-0}).$\hfill\Box$

<!-- rem-3: body_md -->
\label{Rem-interpolants}
The reason for using two different sets of projections in (\ref{reduction-map})
is the following. The $L^2$-orthogonal projections are the ones for which the
reconstruction ${\bf E}$ is consistent, in the sense that
${\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{v})$ approximates $\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{v})$ optimally, where $\pi_k$ is the elementwise projection on polynomials of order $k$; this is the
content of Theorem \ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx} below.  They do not, however,
preserve normal traces, and the resulting bound on the {\em divergence} part
$D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{v})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{v})$ would then carry a factor
$\lambda$ instead of $\mu$, which would spoil the robustness in the
quasi-incompressible limit.  The BDM interpolation operator, on the contrary,
satisfies $\big(\underline{v}-\Pi_T\underline{v}\big)\cdot\underline{n}=0$ on every face of $T$
and commutes with the divergence in the sense that
$\nabla\cdot\Pi_T\underline{v}=\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{v})$; the terms in which the
normal component of the interpolation error appears therefore drop out.  Both
operators enjoy the approximation properties of
Lemma \ref{Lem-Interpolation-properties}, which is all that is used below, and
we keep the same symbol $\hat{\underline{I}}_T$ for both, indicating explicitly which one
is meant whenever the distinction matters.  This device is the same as the one
introduced in \cite{DE15} to obtain locking-free estimates; see also
Remark \ref{Rem-lambda-norm}.

\label{Thm-reconstruct-Approx} Let $\underline{u}$ be the solution of (\ref{elasticity-interface}) and assume $\underline{u}\in H^{k+2}(\Omega_1\cup \Omega_2)^d$. For any $T\in{\cal T}_h$, we have
\begin{align}\label{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-0}
  \sqrt{2\mu}\left\Vert{\bf E}\big( \hat{\underline{I}}_T(\underline{u}) \big)-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert_T
    &+\sqrt{\lambda}\left\VertD\big( \hat{\underline{I}}_T(\underline{u}) \big)-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\right\Vert_T \nonumber\\
    &+\left\Vert[({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K}]^\frac12 \big( {\bf G}\big( \hat{\underline{I}}_T(\underline{u}) \big) -\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}) \big)\underline{n}\right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\nonumber\\
   \lesssim& \sqrt{\mu} h_T^{k+1}\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}(T)^d},
\end{align}
where $\pi_k$ is the $L^2(T)$-orthogonal projection on the polynomial function space of degree $k$.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- head-6: body_md -->
Throughout the proof, ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$ is an arbitrary element of
$\mathbb{P}^k(T)^{d\times d}$ and we abbreviate $\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}$ for
$\hat{\underline{I}}_T(\underline{u})$.  Since $\underline{u}$ is discontinuous across $\Gamma$, we
set $\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\underline{u}:=\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\left\{\underline{u}\right\}$, i.e.\ the interface
component of the interpolate approximates the average of the two traces, and
we shall repeatedly use the two interface conditions of
(\ref{elasticity-interface}) in the form
\begin{equation}\label{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-jump}
  \left\{\underline{u}\right\}=\underline{u}-\frac12\left[\underline{u}\right],\qquad
  \left[\underline{u}\right]=-{\bf K}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\underline{n} \qquad \mbox{on }\ \Gamma.
\end{equation}
The proof consists in identifying the residual of the defining relation
(\ref{gradient-reconstruct}) when $\hat{\underline{v}}_T=\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}$ (Steps 1--3),
and in choosing the test function ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$ so that this residual is the quantity
to be estimated (Step 4).

<!-- head-7: body_md -->
By the definition (\ref{gradient-reconstruct}) of the reconstruction operator,
\begin{align}\label{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-2}
  ({\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}})_T &+\frac12\left\langle ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K}{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})\underline{n} ,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    =& ({\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\Pi_T\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}})_T +\left\langle \Pi_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}\underline{u}-\Pi_T\underline{u}, {\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}
       +\left\langle ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h)(\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\left\{\underline{u}\right\}-\Pi_T\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}.
\end{align}
We integrate by parts the volume term of the right-hand side, which produces
the boundary contribution $\left\langle \Pi_T\underline{u},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T}$; the latter
cancels the two terms $-\left\langle \Pi_T\underline{u},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle$ on $\partial T\backslash \Gamma$ and on $\partial T\cap \Gamma$
up to the factor ${\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h$ in the second one.  Since
${\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}|_F\in\mathbb{P}^k(F)^d$ for every $F\in{\cal F}_{\partial T}$, the projections
$\Pi_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}$ and $\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}$ may be removed from the first argument of the
remaining boundary pairings, and we obtain
\begin{align}\label{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-3}
  ({\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}})_T &+\frac12\left\langle ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K}{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})\underline{n} ,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    =& -(\Pi_T\underline{u}, \nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_T +\left\langle \underline{u}, {\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}
       +\left\langle \left\{\underline{u}\right\}, {\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}
      - \left\langle {\bf K}{\bf S}_h (\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\left\{\underline{u}\right\}-\Pi_T\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}.
\end{align}

In (\ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-3}) we may replace $\Pi_T\underline{u}$ by $\underline{u}$
in the volume term, because $\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{P}^{k-1}(T)^d\subset
\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(T)^d$, and integrate by parts backwards.  Using the first identity in
(\ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-jump}) to write
$\left\{\underline{u}\right\}=\underline{u}-\frac12\left[\underline{u}\right]$ in the interface pairing, and
removing once more the projection $\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}$ in the last term, this yields
\begin{align}\label{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-4}
  ({\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}})_T &+\frac12\left\langle ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K}{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})\underline{n} ,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    =& ({\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}})_T -\frac12\left\langle \left[\underline{u}\right], {\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}
       -\left\langle {\bf K}{\bf S}_h (\left\{\underline{u}\right\}-\Pi_T\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}.
\end{align}
The first term of the right-hand side is $(\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u}),{\boldsymbol{\tau}})_T$, since
${\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{P}^k(T)^{d\times d}$.  In the last one we insert again
$\left\{\underline{u}\right\}-\Pi_T\underline{u}=(\underline{u}-\Pi_T\underline{u})-\frac12\left[\underline{u}\right]$, so
that the two terms involving the jump combine into
$-\frac12\left\langle ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h)\left[\underline{u}\right],{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}$, and we use the
second identity in (\ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-jump}) to replace
$-\left[\underline{u}\right]$ by ${\bf K}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\underline{n}$.  This gives
\begin{align}\label{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-5}
  ({\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}})_T &+\frac12\left\langle ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K}{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})\underline{n} ,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    =& (\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}})_T +\frac12\left\langle ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\underline{n}, {\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    &-\left\langle {\bf K}{\bf S}_h (\underline{u}-\Pi_T\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}.
\end{align}

Two simplifications are now available in (\ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-5}).
First, in the interface pairing we may replace ${\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})$ by
$\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})$: indeed, for any symmetric tensor field ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$ with
${\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}|_F\in\mathbb{P}^k(F)^d$ one has
$\left\langle \big({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\big)\underline{n},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}=0$,
because $\mbox{sym}\big(({\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n})\otimes\underline{n}\big)$ belongs to
$\mathbb{P}^k(\partial T\cap \Gamma)^{d\times d}$ and $\underline{n}$ is constant on each interface face.
Second, we decompose ${\bf K}{\bf S}_h(\underline{u}-\Pi_T\underline{u})$ along the normal and the
two tangential directions,
\begin{align*}
  {\bf K}{\bf S}_h (\underline{u}-\Pi_T\underline{u})
    = {\bf K}{\bf S}_h\underline{n}\, (\underline{u}-\Pi_T\underline{u})\cdot\underline{n}
      +\sum_{i=1,2} {\bf K}{\bf S}_h\underline{t}_i\, (\underline{u}-\Pi_T\underline{u})\cdot\underline{t}_i,
\end{align*}
and we observe that the normal contribution vanishes.  Indeed, as recalled in
Remark \ref{Rem-interpolants}, the normal component of the cell interpolate is
the one of the BDM interpolation operator, for which
$(\underline{u}-\Pi_T\underline{u})\cdot\underline{n}=0$ on every face of $T$.  Collecting these
two observations, and writing the tangential contribution in a form that is
adapted to the interface term of the left-hand side by means of the
factorisation ${\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h = {\bf QPQ}^T{\bf S}_h$ of (\ref{Lem-Sh-1}), we arrive
at the residual identity
\begin{align}\label{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-6}
  &({\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\tau}})_T
     +\frac12\left\langle ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K}\big({\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\big)\underline{n} ,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    &\hspace{1cm}= -\sum_{i=1,2}\left\langle \underline{t}_i^T({\bf QPQ}^T)^{-\frac12} {\bf K}^\frac12{\bf S}_h^\frac12\underline{t}_i\,
        (\underline{u}-\Pi_T\underline{u}),
        ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h)^{\frac12} {\bf K}^\frac12{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma},
\end{align}
valid for every ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{P}^k(T)^{d\times d}$.

We now take
${\boldsymbol{\tau}}={\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}) -\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})
=\mathbb{C}\big({\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}) -\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})\big)$, which is an
admissible test function, and we use that $\mathbb{C}$ is symmetric positive
definite with
\begin{align*}
  \big({\bf E} ,\mathbb{C}{\bf E}\big)_T = 2\mu\left\Vert{\bf E}\right\Vert^2_T +\lambda\left\Verttr{\bf E}\right\Vert^2_T .
\end{align*}
The left-hand side of (\ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-6}) then becomes
\begin{align*}
  2\mu\left\Vert{\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert^2_T
    &+\lambda\left\VertD(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\right\Vert^2_T \\

while the right-hand side is bounded by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality.  For
the first factor we use that, in the tangential directions,
$\left\vert({\bf QPQ}^T)^{-\frac12} {\bf K}^\frac12{\bf S}_h^\frac12\right\vert
\leq \big(\frac{\mu}{h_T}\big)^{\frac12}$, which follows from
(\ref{Lem-Sh-2})--(\ref{Lem-Sh-4}); the second factor is one half of the third
term of the left-hand side.  Absorbing the latter, we obtain
\begin{align}\label{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-1}
  2\mu\left\Vert{\bf E}\big( \hat{\underline{I}}_T(\underline{u}) \big)-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert^2_T
    &+\lambda\left\VertD\big( \hat{\underline{I}}_T(\underline{u}) \big)-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\right\Vert^2_T \nonumber\\

      -\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}) \big)\underline{n}\right\Vert^2_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    \leq& \frac{\mu}{h_T}\left\Vert\underline{u}-\Pi_T\underline{u}\right\Vert^2_{\partial T\cap \Gamma},
\end{align}
and Lemma \ref{Lem-Interpolation-properties} bounds the right-hand side by
$\mu h_T^{2(k+1)}\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert^2_{H^{k+2}(T)^d}$, which proves
(\ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-0}).$\hfill\Box$

\label{Lem-traction} Under the assumptions of Theorem \ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx}, for any $T\in{\cal T}_h$ we have
\begin{align}
  \sqrt{\frac{h_T}{\mu+\lambda}} \left\Vert{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}
    &\lesssim \sqrt{\mu} h_T^{k+1}\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}(T)^d},\label{Lem-traction-1}\\
  \sqrt{\frac{h_T}{\mu}} \left\Vert{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert_{\partial T}
    &\lesssim \mu^{-\frac12}h_T^{k+1}\big( \mu\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}(T)^d}
      +\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+1}(T)}\big),\label{Lem-traction-2}\\
  \sqrt{\frac{h_T}{\mu+\lambda}} \left\Vert{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}
    &\lesssim \mu^{-\frac12}h_T^{k+1}\big( \mu\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}(T)^d}

\end{align}

We prove the three bounds in turn.

For (\ref{Lem-traction-1}) we observe that
${\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})
=2\mu\big({\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})\big)
+\lambda\big(D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\big){\bf I}$ is a
polynomial of degree at most $k$ on $T$, so that the discrete trace inequality
(\ref{Lem-Discrete-trace-Ineq}) applies to each of the two contributions.
Using $\lambda\leq\mu+\lambda$ and $2\mu\leq2(\mu+\lambda)$, we get
\begin{align*}
  \sqrt{\frac{h_T}{\mu+\lambda}} \left\Vert{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}
    \lesssim& \left\Vert\sqrt{\mu}\big( {\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u}) \big)\right\Vert_T \\
    &+    \sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\mu+\lambda}}\left\Vert\sqrt{\lambda}\big( D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u}) \big)\right\Vert_T ,
\end{align*}
and (\ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-0}) bounds both terms by
$\sqrt{\mu} h_T^{k+1}\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}(T)^d}$.

The bound (\ref{Lem-traction-2}) is a pure approximation result.  Since
${\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})$ is not a polynomial, we use the multiplicative
trace inequality (\ref{Lem-Multip-trace-Ineq}) and then
${\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})=2\mu{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})+\lambda(\nabla\cdot\underline{u}){\bf I}$ together with
Lemma \ref{Lem-Interpolation-properties}:
\begin{align*}
  \sqrt{\frac{h_T}{\mu}} \left\Vert{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert_{\partial T}
    \lesssim& \mu^{-\frac12}\Big(\mu\big(\left\Vert{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert_T
         +h_T\left\Vert\nabla({\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u}))\right\Vert_T\big)\\
    &+    \lambda\big( \left\Vert\nabla\cdot\underline{u}-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\right\Vert_T
         +h_T\left\Vert \nabla\big(\nabla\cdot\underline{u}-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\big) \right\Vert_T \big)\Big) \\
    \lesssim& \mu^{-\frac12}h_T^{k+1}\big( \mu\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}(T)^d}
    +\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+1}(T)}\big).
\end{align*}
Finally, (\ref{Lem-traction-3}) follows from
${\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})
=\big({\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\big)
-\big({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\big)$, the triangle inequality,
(\ref{Lem-traction-1})--(\ref{Lem-traction-2}) and $\mu\leq\mu+\lambda$.$\hfill\Box$

\label{Pro-energy-Err} Let $k\geq 1$. Assume that the exact solution $\underline{u}$ of (\ref{weak-form}) satisfies $\underline{u}\in H^{k+2}({\cal T}_h)^d, \nabla\cdot\underline{u}\in H^{k+1}({\cal T}_h)$. Let $\hat{\underline{u}}_h$ be the solution of the discrete problem (\ref{discrete-form}), then we have

    +\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+1}({\cal T}_h)}\big).
\end{equation}

<!-- head-12: body_md -->
As in Theorem \ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx}, we set
$\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\underline{u}:= \Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\left\{\underline{u}\right\}$, since $\underline{u}$ is discontinuous
across $\Gamma$, and we write $\hat{\underline{e}}_h=\hat{\underline{u}}_h-\hat{\underline{I}}_h(\underline{u})$.
The proof is organised as follows.  We first reduce the estimate to the
consistency error of the method (Step 1), which we then rewrite in a form in
which only differences between the reconstructed and the exact stress appear
(Step 2).  The five resulting contributions are estimated one by one in
Steps 3--6, and Step 7 concludes.

\smallskip\noindent

<!-- head-13: body_md -->
Since $a_h$ is symmetric positive semi-definite and
$\left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \cdot \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert^2_h=a_h(\cdot,\cdot)$, the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\begin{align}\label{Pro-energy-Err-1}
  \left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{e}}_h \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_h
    \leq&\sup_{\hat{\underline{w}}_h\in\hat{\underline{V}}_{h0},|||\hat{\underline{w}}_h|||_h=1} a_h(\hat{\underline{e}}_h,\hat{\underline{w}}_h)\nonumber\\
    =& \sup_{\hat{\underline{w}}_h\in\hat{\underline{V}}_{h0},|||\hat{\underline{w}}_h|||_h=1} {\cal E}_h(\hat{\underline{w}}_h),
\end{align}
where, by the discrete problem (\ref{discrete-form}),
${\cal E}_h(\hat{\underline{w}}_h)= l(\hat{\underline{w}}_h) -a_h(\hat{\underline{I}}_h(\underline{u}),\hat{\underline{w}}_h)$ is the
consistency error of the method.  Splitting $a_h$ into its local
contributions and $s_T$ into its two parts, we restate it as
\begin{align}\label{Pro-energy-Err-2}
  {\cal E}_h(\hat{\underline{w}}_h) =& \sum_{T\in {\cal T}_h}\big[ l_T(\hat{\underline{w}}_h) -a_T(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}}_h)
      -s_T(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}}_h)\big] \nonumber\\
    =&\sum_{T\in {\cal T}_h}\big[ l_T(\hat{\underline{w}}) -a_T(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}})
      -s_{T^{\Gamma}}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}}) -s_{T^{\backslash \Gamma}}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}})\big],
\end{align}
in which we drop the subscript $h$ on $\hat{\underline{w}}$ for brevity.  The last
contribution is estimated separately in Step 6; we now concentrate on the
first three.

<!-- head-14: body_md -->
On every element $T\in{\cal T}_h$, we use ${\bf G}=\mathbb{C}{\bf E}$, the symmetry of
$\mathbb{C}$ and of $({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K}$, and the equation
$-\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})=\underline{f}$ to write $l_T$ as a volume term, which
gives
\begin{align*}
  a_T(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}})
      &+s_{T^{\Gamma}}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}}) -l_T(\hat{\underline{w}}) \\
    =& 2\mu\big( {\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}) ,{\bf E}(\hat{\underline{w}}) \big)_T
      +\lambda \big( tr{\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}) ,tr{\bf E}(\hat{\underline{w}}) \big)_T \\
    &+\frac12\left\langle  ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K}{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})\underline{n} ,{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{w}})\underline{n} \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}
      +\big(\nabla\cdot {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}), \underline{w}\big)_T +s_{T^{\Gamma}}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}}) \\
    =& \big( {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}) ,{\bf E}(\hat{\underline{w}}) \big)_T
      +\frac12\left\langle  {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})\underline{n} ,({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K}{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{w}})\underline{n} \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \\
    &+\big(\nabla\cdot {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}), \underline{w}\big)_T +s_{T^{\Gamma}}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}}).
\end{align*}
We now apply the definition (\ref{gradient-reconstruct}) of the reconstruction
operator to $\hat{\underline{w}}$, with the admissible test function
${\boldsymbol{\tau}}={\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})$, which transforms the first two terms into a
volume term involving ${\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{w})$ and boundary terms involving the
differences $\underline{w}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}-\underline{w}_T$ and $\underline{w}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\underline{w}_T$; we
integrate by parts the term $\big(\nabla\cdot {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}), \underline{w}\big)_T$,
and we use (\ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-jump}) in the form
$\left\{\underline{u}\right\}-\underline{u}_i=\frac12{\bf K}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\underline{n}_i$, $i=1,2$, to rewrite
$s_{T^{\Gamma}}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}})$.  Summing over the cells, in
which process the traction terms of two neighbouring cells cancel on the
interior faces not lying on $\Gamma$, we deduce
\begin{align}\label{Pro-energy-Err-3}
  \sum_{T\in{\cal T}_h} \big[a_T(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}})
      &+s_{T^{\Gamma}}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u},\hat{\underline{w}}) -l_T(\hat{\underline{w}})\big] \nonumber\\
    =& \sum_{T\in{\cal T}_h} \Big[ \big( {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}) ,{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{w}) \big)_T
      +\left\langle  {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})\underline{n} ,\underline{w}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}-\underline{w}_T \right\rangle_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    &+\left\langle  ({\bf I}-{\bf S}_h{\bf K}) {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})\underline{n} ,\underline{w}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\underline{w}_T \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    &-\big({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}), {\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{w})\big)_T
      -\left\langle  {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\underline{n} ,\underline{w}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}-\underline{w}_T \right\rangle_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}
      -\left\langle  {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\underline{n} ,\underline{w}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\underline{w}_T \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    &+2\left\langle  {\bf S}_h\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\big( \left\{\underline{u}\right\} -\Pi_T\underline{u} \big), \underline{w}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\underline{w}_T \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}  \Big]\nonumber\\
    =& \sum_{T\in{\cal T}_h} \Big[ \big( {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}) ,{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{w}) \big)_T
      +\left\langle  \big( {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}) \big)\underline{n} ,\underline{w}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}-\underline{w}_T \right\rangle_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    &+\left\langle  \big( {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}) \big)\underline{n} ,\underline{w}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\underline{w}_T \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    &-\left\langle  {\bf S}_h{\bf K}\big( {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}) \big)\underline{n},\underline{w}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\underline{w}_T \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    &+2\left\langle  {\bf S}_h( \underline{u} -\Pi_T\underline{u} ), \underline{w}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\underline{w}_T \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}  \Big].
\end{align}
The gain of this rewriting is that every term of the last expression is a
duality pairing between an interpolation error of the stress and a quantity
that is controlled by $\left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{w}} \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T}$.  We estimate the five terms
successively.

We first split the volume term according to
${\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})
=\big({\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\big)
-\big({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\big)$ and we expand the first part
according to the constitutive law, which gives
\begin{align*}
  \big( {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}) ,{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{w}) \big)_T
    =& 2\mu\big( {\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u}) ,{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{w}) \big)_T
      +\lambda\big( D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u}) ,D(\hat{\underline{w}}) \big)_T \\
    &+ \lambda\big( D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u}) ,\nabla\cdot\underline{w}-D(\underline{w}) \big)_T
      -\big( {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}) ,{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{w}) \big)_T .
\end{align*}
The first, second and fourth terms are directly bounded by the
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, since
$\left\Vert\sqrt{2\mu}{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{w})\right\Vert_T\leq\left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{w}} \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_E$ and
$\left\Vert\sqrt{\lambda}D(\underline{w})\right\Vert_T\leq\left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{w}} \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T}$.  The third one
requires an argument, because $\nabla\cdot\underline{w}-D(\underline{w})$ is not
controlled by the energy norm: we use the definition
(\ref{divergence-reconstruct}) of the discrete divergence with the test
function $q=\lambda\big(D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\big)$,
which turns it into three boundary terms,
\begin{align*}
  \lambda\big( D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u}) &,\nabla\cdot\underline{w}-D(\hat{\underline{w}}) \big)_T
    = \frac{\lambda}{2}\left\langle  \big(D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\big)\underline{n},
        ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K} {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{w}})\underline{n} \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \\
    &- \left\langle  \lambda^{\frac12}\big(D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\big)\underline{n},
       \lambda^{\frac12}\underline{n}\otimes\underline{n}(\underline{w}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}-\Pi_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}\underline{w}_T) \right\rangle_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma} \\
    &- \lambda\left\langle  \big(D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\big)\underline{n},
        ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h) (\underline{w}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\underline{w}_T) \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}.
\end{align*}
Each of the three is now bounded by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, the
weights being $\big(\frac{h_T}{\mu+\lambda}\big)^{\frac12}$ for the first and
the third one, by (\ref{Lem-Sh-4}) and (\ref{Lem-equivalent-norm-2}), so that
\begin{align*}
  \lambda\big( D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u}) &,\nabla\cdot\underline{w}-D(\hat{\underline{w}}) \big)_T \\
    \leq& \lambda\sqrt{\frac{h_T}{\mu+\lambda}} \left\VertD(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}
           \left\Vert[({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h){\bf K}]^{\frac12} {\bf G}(\underline{w})\underline{n} \right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \\
    &+  \left\Vert\lambda^{\frac12}\big(D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\big)\right\Vert_T \left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{w}} \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T} \\
    &+  \sqrt{\frac{h_T}{\mu+\lambda}}\left\Vert \lambda\big(D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\big) \right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}
          \left\Vert{\bf S}_h^{\frac12} (\underline{w}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}\underline{w}_T)\right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \\
    \leq& \left\Vert\lambda^{\frac12}\big(D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\big)\right\Vert_T \left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{w}} \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T},
\end{align*}
where the last inequality uses the discrete trace inequality
(\ref{Lem-Discrete-trace-Ineq}) to convert the interface norms of the
polynomial $D(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})$ into cell norms.
Collecting the four contributions and applying
(\ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-0}) to the first two,
Lemma \ref{Lem-Interpolation-properties} to the fourth, we obtain
\begin{align}\label{Pro-energy-Err-4}
  \big( {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u}) ,{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{w}) \big)_T
    \leq& \sqrt{2\mu}\left\Vert{\bf E}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert_T \left\Vert\sqrt{2\mu}{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{w})\right\Vert_T \nonumber\\
    &+  \sqrt{\lambda}\left\VertD(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\right\Vert_T
       \Big( \left\Vert\sqrt{\lambda}D(\underline{w})\right\Vert_T +\left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{w}} \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T}\Big) \nonumber\\
    &+ \mu^{-\frac12}\big( 2\mu\left\Vert{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert_T
           +\lambda\left\Vert\nabla\cdot\underline{u}-\pi_k(\nabla\cdot\underline{u})\right\Vert_T\big)\left\Vert\sqrt{2\mu}{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{w})\right\Vert_T \nonumber\\
    \lesssim& \mu^{-\frac12}h^{k+1}\big( \mu\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}(T)^d}
               +\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+1}(T)}\big)\left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{w}} \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T}.
\end{align}

the third one involves the trace $\underline{w}_T$ instead of its projection, and

We bound the three contributions to (\ref{aT-extended}) separately.  We
shall use that the proof of Theorem \ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx} only relies on
Lemma \ref{Lem-Interpolation-properties}, so that
(\ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx-0}) holds with $h_T^{k+1}$ and

    \leq& \sqrt{2\mu}\left\Vert{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{v})-\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{v})\right\Vert_T

\end{align*}
where the last inequality also uses $\mu\leq\mu+\lambda$.  Collecting the
three bounds gives (\ref{Lem-aT-consistency-0}).$\hfill\Box$

Integrating by parts cell by cell and using the continuity of
${\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})\underline{n}$ across the interior faces, together with the fact that the
face components of $\hat{\underline{e}}_h$ are single valued and vanish on $\partial\Omega$, we
obtain
\begin{align}\label{Pro-L2-Err-1}
  \left\Vert\underline{e}_h\right\Vert^2_\Omega =& \sum_{T\in{\cal T}_h}(\underline{e}_T, -\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta}))_T \nonumber\\
    =& \sum_{T\in{\cal T}_h}\Big[\big({\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{e}_T), {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})\big)_T +\left\langle  \underline{e}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}-\underline{e}_T, {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}
       +\left\langle  \underline{e}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\underline{e}_T, {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \Big].
\end{align}
We now insert ${\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta})$, i.e.\ the reconstructed dual stress, in the
volume term, and we treat the resulting term
$\big({\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{e}_T), {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta}) \big)_T$ with the definition
(\ref{gradient-reconstruct}) of the reconstruction operator applied to
$\hat{\underline{e}}_T$ with the test function ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}={\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta})$, which yields
\begin{align*}
  \big({\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{e}_T), {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta}) \big)_T
    =& a_T( \hat{\underline{e}}_T, \hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta} )
      -\left\langle  \underline{e}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}-\underline{e}_T, {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta})\underline{n} \right\rangle_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma} \\
    &- \left\langle  ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h) (\underline{e}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\underline{e}_T), {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta})\underline{n} \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}.
\end{align*}
Substituting this identity into (\ref{Pro-L2-Err-1}), adding and subtracting
$s_T(\hat{\underline{e}}_T,\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta})$ so as to reconstruct $a_h$, and using the
definition (\ref{ah}), we deduce that
\begin{align}\label{Pro-L2-Err-2}
  \left\Vert\underline{e}_h\right\Vert^2_\Omega =& \sum_{T\in{\cal T}_h}\Big[\big({\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{e}_T), {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})-{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta}) \big)_T
       +\left\langle  \underline{e}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}-\underline{e}_T, \big( {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})-{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta}) \big)\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    &+ \left\langle  \underline{e}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\underline{e}_T, {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}
       -\left\langle  ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h) (\underline{e}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\underline{e}_T), {\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta})\underline{n} \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \nonumber\\
    &- s_T( \hat{\underline{e}}_T, \hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta} ) \Big]
       +a_h( \hat{\underline{e}}_h, \hat{\underline{I}}_h\underline{\zeta} ).
\end{align}
The last term is estimated in Steps 4--5; the sum is estimated in Steps 2--3.

stabilization do not individually have the right structure, and we first
combine them.  Using $\left\{\underline{\zeta}\right\}=\underline{\zeta}-\frac12\left[\underline{\zeta}\right]$ and

Every term of (\ref{Pro-L2-Err-3}) is now a pairing between a component of
$\hat{\underline{e}}_T$, controlled by $\left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{e}}_T \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T}$, and an interpolation error
of the dual solution, which is of order $h_T$ by
Lemma \ref{Lem-Interpolation-properties}, Theorem \ref{Thm-reconstruct-Approx}
and Lemma \ref{Lem-traction} used with $m=1$.  The five estimates are the
exact analogues of (\ref{Pro-energy-Err-4}), (\ref{Pro-energy-Err-5}),
(\ref{Pro-energy-Err-9}), (\ref{Pro-energy-Err-8}) and
(\ref{Pro-energy-Err-11}), with the roles of the primal and the dual solution
interchanged.  For the first one, splitting around $\pi_k{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})$ and
expanding according to the constitutive law as in Step 3 of the proof of
Proposition \ref{Pro-energy-Err},
\begin{align*}
  \big({\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\underline{e}_T), {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})-{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta}) \big)_T
    \leq& \left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{e}}_T \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T} \mu^{-\frac12}h\big(\mu\left\vert\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^2(T)^d}+\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^1(T)}\big).
\end{align*}
For the second one, inserting $\Pi_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}\underline{e}_T$ in the polynomial part
and using the trace inequalities,
\begin{align*}
  \left\langle  \underline{e}_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}-\underline{e}_T, \big( {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})-{\bf G}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta}) \big)\underline{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T\backslash \Gamma}
    \leq& \left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{e}}_T \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T} \mu^{-\frac12}h\big(\mu\left\vert\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^2(T)^d}+\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^1(T)}\big).
\end{align*}
The third one is bounded by moving half of the weight
${\bf QPQ}^T$ onto the test function, as in (\ref{Pro-energy-Err-9}), and by
(\ref{Lem-traction-3}),
\begin{align*}
  &\left\langle ({\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h)(\underline{e}_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}-\underline{e}_T),

  &\hspace{1cm}\leq \left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{e}}_T \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T} \mu^{-\frac12}h\big(\mu\left\vert\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^2(T)^d}
      +\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^1(T)}\big).
\end{align*}
For the fourth one we use $\left\vert{\bf K}{\bf S}_h\right\vert\leq 1$ from (\ref{Lem-Sh-3}), the
orthogonality of $I-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}$ to $\mathbb{P}^k(\partial T\cap \Gamma)^d$,

(\ref{Lem-Multip-trace-Ineq}), which give
\begin{align*}

    \lesssim& \left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{e}}_T \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T} \mu^{-\frac12}h\big(\mu\left\vert\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^2(T)^d}+\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^1(T)}\big).

Remark \ref{Rem-interpolants}, and we conclude as in
(\ref{Pro-energy-Err-11})--(\ref{Pro-energy-Err-13}) that
\begin{align*}

       +s_{T^{\backslash \Gamma}}(\hat{\underline{e}}_T, \hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta})
    \lesssim& \left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{e}}_T \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_{a_T} h\mu^{\frac12}\left\vert\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^2(T)^d}.
\end{align*}

(\ref{Pro-energy-Err-14}) for $\left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{e}}_h \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_h$ and the regularity

  \diamondsuit\lesssim& \left\Vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\left\vert \hat{\underline{e}}_h \right\vert{\hskip -2.6pt}\right\Vert_h \mu^{-\frac12}h\big(\mu\left\vert\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^2({\cal T}_h)^d}+\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^1({\cal T}_h)}\big) \nonumber\\

          \left\Vert\underline{e}_h\right\Vert_\Omega.
\end{align}

$\left[\underline{u}\right]=-{\bf K}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\underline{n}$, which gives
\begin{align*}
  \left\langle  {\bf S}_h(I-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}) \left[\underline{u}\right] ,{\bf K} {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})\underline{n} \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}
    =& \left\langle  {\bf K}{\bf S}_h(I-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}) \left[\underline{u}\right] ,(I-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}){\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})\underline{n} \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \\
    \leq& \left\Vert(I-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma})\underline{u}\right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \left\Vert(I-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}){\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{\zeta})\right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \\
    \lesssim& \mu^{-1}h^{k+2} \mu\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}(T)^d}
          \big(\mu \left\vert\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^2(T)^d}+\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^1(T)}\big),
\end{align*}
and
\begin{align*}
  \left\langle  {\bf S}_h(I-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}) \left[\underline{u}\right] ,\underline{\zeta}-\Pi_T\underline{\zeta} \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}
    =&\left\langle  {\bf S}_h{\bf K}(I-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}) {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\underline{n} ,\underline{\zeta}-\Pi_T\underline{\zeta} \right\rangle_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \\
    \leq& h^{\frac12}\left\Vert(I-\Pi_{\partial T\cap \Gamma}){\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(\underline{u})\right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} h^{-\frac12}\left\Vert\underline{\zeta}-\Pi_T\underline{\zeta}\right\Vert_{\partial T\cap \Gamma} \\
    \lesssim& \mu^{-1}h^{k+2}\big(\mu\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}(T)^d}+\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+1}(T)}\big)
         \mu \left\vert\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^2(T)^d}.
\end{align*}

Inserting the six bounds of Step 5 into (\ref{Pro-L2-Err-6}), and estimating
the two remaining terms of (\ref{Pro-L2-Err-6}) by the orthogonality of
$\Pi_T$, by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for the positive semi-definite form
$s_{T^{\backslash \Gamma}}$ and by Lemma \ref{Lem-Interpolation-properties},
we obtain with the regularity assumption (\ref{Regularity-Asm}) that
\begin{align}\label{Pro-L2-Err-7}
  a_h( \hat{\underline{e}}_h, \hat{\underline{I}}_h\underline{\zeta} ) \leq& \sum_{T\in {\cal T}_h} \Big[ (\underline{f},\Pi_T\underline{\zeta}-\underline{\zeta})_T
      +s_{T^{\backslash \Gamma}}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}, \hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta}) \Big]\nonumber\\
    &+ \mu^{-1}h^{k+2}\big(\mu\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}({\cal T}_h)^d}+\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+1}({\cal T}_h)}\big)
          \big(\mu\left\vert\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^2({\cal T}_h)^d}+\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^1({\cal T}_h)}\big) \nonumber\\
    \lesssim& \sum_{T\in {\cal T}_h} \Big[ (\underline{f}-\Pi_T\underline{f},\Pi_T\underline{\zeta}-\underline{\zeta})_T
      +s_{T^{\backslash \Gamma}}^{\frac12}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u}, \hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{u})
       s_{T^{\backslash \Gamma}}^{\frac12}(\hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta}, \hat{\underline{I}}_T\underline{\zeta}) \Big]\nonumber\\
    &+ \mu^{-1}h^{k+2}\big(\mu\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}({\cal T}_h)^d}+\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+1}({\cal T}_h)}\big)
          \left\Vert\underline{e}_h\right\Vert_\Omega \nonumber\\
    \lesssim& \sum_{T\in {\cal T}_h} \Big[ h^{k+2}\left\vert\underline{f}\right\vert_{H^k(T)^d} \left\vert\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^2(T)^d}
          +h^{k+2} \left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}(T)^d} \mu\left\vert\underline{\zeta}\right\vert_{H^2(T)^d} \Big]\nonumber\\
    &+ \mu^{-1}h^{k+2}\big(\mu\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}({\cal T}_h)^d}+\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+1}({\cal T}_h)}\big)
          \left\Vert\underline{e}_h\right\Vert_\Omega \nonumber\\
    \lesssim& \mu^{-1}h^{k+2}\big(\mu\left\vert\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+2}({\cal T}_h)^d}+\lambda\left\vert\nabla\cdot\underline{u}\right\vert_{H^{k+1}({\cal T}_h) }\big)
          \left\Vert\underline{e}_h\right\Vert_\Omega.
\end{align}
Combining (\ref{Pro-L2-Err-2}), (\ref{Pro-L2-Err-4}) and (\ref{Pro-L2-Err-7}),
and dividing by $\left\Vert\underline{e}_h\right\Vert_\Omega$, deduces (\ref{Pro-L2-Err-0}).
$\hfill\Box$

\label{Rem-geom}
Approximating $\Gamma$ by the chords of the circle, as is done for convenience
in \cite[Sect.~5]{HH04}, perturbs the geometry by $O(h^2)$ and therefore caps
the accuracy of the method, whatever the polynomial degree.  This is
immaterial for the piecewise affine approximation used in \cite{HH04}, but it
would hide the high-order convergence predicted by
Proposition \ref{Pro-energy-Err}--Proposition \ref{Pro-L2-Err} as soon as $k\geq 1$. The above theory requires flat interfaces,
however we included this example to see how fragile the method is. It turns out the perturbation is small enough that optimal convergence is observed.
For a detailed analysis on how to extend the HHO method to the case of curved interfaces we refer to \cite{Yemm24}.

\label{Rem-free}
When $\alpha,\beta\to+\infty$ one has ${\bf I}-{\bf K}{\bf S}_h\to \bf{0}$ and
${\bf S}_h\to\bf{0}$, so that the interface unknowns disappear from
(\ref{gradient-reconstruct}), (\ref{Local-aT}) and (\ref{stabilization-term}),
and the discrete problem degenerates into two independent traction-free
problems set on $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$, as anticipated in
Remark \ref{Rem-limits}.  For very large compliancies the interface unknowns
become numerically inactive -- the corresponding diagonal entries of the
condensed matrix drop below $10^{-12}$ times the largest one -- and are then
simply removed from the linear system; the number of such degrees of freedom
is reported in the last column of Table~\ref{tab-robust}.  On the mesh
sequence used there this occurs for $\alpha=\beta\gtrsim10^{10}$, whereas the
free-interface regime is reached much earlier: for $k=1$ on the second mesh
one obtains $E_{e}=8.6015\cdot10^{-2}$, $E_{h}=7.9552\cdot10^{-2}$ and
$E_{0}=4.2861\cdot10^{-4}$ for every $\alpha=\beta$ between $10^{4}$ and
$10^{16}$, to five significant digits, the two subdomains being then
decoupled.  The row $\alpha=\beta=10^{12}$ of Table~\ref{tab-robust}, for
which all the interface unknowns are inactive, gives errors identical to those
of the row $\alpha=\beta=10^{4}$, for which none is: the elimination is
harmless.
