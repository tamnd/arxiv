<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:top-str-set}
		There is an isomorphism $\mathcal{S}^{\textup{TOP}}(M_{m,n}^k) \cong \mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}$ where $e = 2n$ is the Euler number of $\gamma_{m,n}^k$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:the-forgetful-map-on-str-sets}
		The forgetful map $F_{M_{m,n}^k} \colon \mathcal{S}^{\textup{DIFF}}(M_{m,n}^k) \rightarrow \mathcal{S}^{\textup{TOP}}(M_{m,n}^k)$ has its cokernel isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/g\mathbb{Z}$ where $g$ is given as follows. 
		
		When $n \neq 0$, then $g$= gcd$(e,\theta_k)$ with $e=2n$ the Euler number of the bundle and 
		\[
		\theta_k = \text{numerator}\left(\frac{B_k}{4k}\right)\cdot a_k \cdot 2^{2k-2}\cdot(2^{2k-1}-1)
		\]
		where $B_k$ is $k$-th Bernoulli number and $a_k$ is $1$ or $2$ according to $k$ is even or odd.
		
		When $n=0$, then $g=\theta_k$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:never-surjective}
		The forgetful map $F_{M_{m,n}^k}$ is never surjective.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:any-smooth-is-bundle}
		Any element in the image of $F_{M_{m,n}^k}$ can be realized with the source manifold as an $S^{4k-1}$-bundle over $S^{4k}$ with the structure group $\textup{SO}(4k)$.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:if-n-theta-coprime-then-half-of-elements-are-bundles}
		If gcd$(n,\theta_k)=1$ then half of the elements of $\mathcal{S}^{\textup{TOP}}(M_{m,n}^k)$ can be realized with the source manifold as an $S^{4k-1}$-bundle over $S^{4k}$.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
The proof proceeds by applying the surgery theory described above to the pair $(W_{m,n}^k,M_{m,n}^k)$ of the disk bundle and sphere bundle defined earlier. Note that $\partial W_{m,n}^k=M_{m,n}^k$. The $\pi-\pi$ Theorem (see \cite[Theorem 1.65]{CW2021}) implies that in this case the top horizontal map $\eta$ in~\eqref{bdry} is an isomorphism. Furthermore, primary obstruction to null homotopy implies (see \cite[Theorem 13.11, Chapter VII]{GEB}):
	\begin{equation}\label{idfic}
		[W_{m,n}^{k},\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]\cong H^{4k}(W_{m,n}^k;\mathbb{Z}) \text{ and } [M_{m,n}^k,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}] \cong H^{4k}(M_{m,n}^k;\mathbb{Z}) 
	\end{equation}
	Also note that for any $d>0$, $\pi_{d}(\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}) \cong L_{d}(\mathbb{Z})$. Therefore for $n>0$, we get the following commutative diagram:
	
	\begin{equation}\label{equation:ses}
		\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=1em,column sep=1em]
			\mathcal{S}^{\textup{TOP}}(W_{m,n}^k) \arrow[d,"i^*"] \arrow[r,"\overset{\eta}{\cong}"] & \mathcal{N}^{\textup{TOP}}(W_{m,n}^k) \arrow[d,"i^*"] \arrow[r,"\cong"] & H^{4k}(W_{m,n}^k) \arrow[d,"i^*"] \arrow[r,"\cong"] & \mathbb{Z} \dar  \\
			\mathcal{S}^{\textup{TOP}}(M_{m,n}^k) \arrow[r,"\overset{\eta}{\cong}"] & \mathcal{N}^{\textup{TOP}}(M_{m,n}^k) \arrow[r,"\cong"] & H^{4k}(M_{m,n}^k) \arrow[r,"\cong"] & \mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z},
		\end{tikzcd}
	\end{equation}
	                                                                                                                                                                                               
	The right vertical map is the canonical projection. When $n=0$, we shall have $\mathbb{Z}$ in the bottom right corner, and the right vertical map will be the identity. The maps $i^*$ are induced by restriction to the boundary. The desired statement follows from the bottom line of Diagram \eqref{equation:ses}.  \qed

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The  smooth version $\mathcal{S}^{\textup{DIFF}} (X)$ of the structure set is, in general, harder to determine. Firstly, the $\textup{DIFF}$-version of the surgery exact sequence~\eqref{eqn:cat-ses} is a-priori not an exact sequence of abelian groups, only of pointed sets. Secondly, the set of normal invariants $\mathcal{N}^{\textup{DIFF}}(X) \cong [X,\textup{G}/\textup{O}]$ is harder to determine, since the homotopy type of $\textup{G}/\textup{O}$ is more complicated than that of $\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}$, see~\cite[Ch. 5]{MM}. Finally, although the $L$-groups are the same, the map $\eta$ need not be injective if $X$ is odd-dimensional. For our $X = M^k_{m,n}$ we will be able to overcome some of these difficulties and obtain some information about $\mathcal{S}^{\textup{DIFF}} (M^k_{m,n})$, but the full calculation will remain open.

We first look at some homotopical properties of the total space $M_{m,n}^k$. Since $k,m,n$ are fixed throughout the proof, in order to avoid unnecessary clutter in the  notation, we abbreviate $M = M_{m,n}^{k}$. The total space has the following cell structure (see \cite[Equation 3.3]{JW54}):
\begin{equation}\label{cell}
	M = S^{4k-1} \underset{\alpha}{\cup} e^{4k} \underset{\beta}{\cup} e^{8k-1}	
\end{equation}
where  $\beta:S^{8k-2} \rightarrow S^{4k-1} \underset{\alpha}{\cup} e^{4k}$ and $\alpha = p_{*}(\gamma_{m,n}^k)$ are induced from the projection $p: \textup{SO}(4k) \rightarrow S^{4k-1}$. In general, $p_*$ sends an $\mathbb{R}^q$ bundle over $S^p$, say $\xi$, to $O_{\xi}$, which is an obstruction against finding a section. If $q=p+1$, then this obstruction is just the Euler number, $e$, of the bundle. Therefore, $\alpha = p_*(\gamma_{m,n}^k)=e=2n$; \cite[p. 64]{Levine(1983)}. 

Let us denote by $Y = S^{4k-1} \underset{\alpha}{\cup} e^{4k}$. From \cite[Equation 3.7]{JW54} and \cite[Equation 5.1 and page 5]{JW55}, it follows that after collapsing the fiber, the total space $M$ is stably homotopy equivalent to the wedge sum
\begin{equation}\label{stableeq}
	M/S^{4k-1} \simeq_s S^{4k} \vee S^{8k-1}.	
\end{equation} 
Namely, since $Y/S^{4k-1} \cong S^{4k}$ the composition $\overline{\beta}: S^{8k-2} \xrightarrow{\beta} Y \xrightarrow{collapse}S^{4k}$ satisfies 
\begin{center}
	$\overline{\beta} \simeq_s 0$ by \cite[Equation 5.1]{JW55}
\end{center}
which implies (\ref{stableeq}).

Since in \eqref{stableeq} we have a stable homotopy equivalence, we obtain, for some $p$, a cofibration sequence of the form:
\begin{multline} \label{mcof}
	\Sigma^{p} S^{4k-1} \cong S^{4k-1+p} \rightarrow \Sigma^{p} M \rightarrow \Sigma^{p}(S^{4k-1} \vee S^{8k-1}) \cong S^{4k-1+p} \vee S^{8k-1+p} \rightarrow \\
	\rightarrow \Sigma^{p+1} S^{4k-1} \cong S^{4k+p} \rightarrow \Sigma^{p+1} M \rightarrow \cdots
\end{multline}

Given a spectrum (or an infinite loop space) $K$ and a space $X$, we may consider $K$-cohomology: 
\begin{equation}\label{RH}
	K^{q}(X) = [X,K]_{-q} = \text{colim}_{p}[\Sigma^{p}X,K_{q+p}].
\end{equation}
From \cite[Part 3, Proposition 6.1]{JA73} and \cite[Theorem 4.58]{AH02} we have that the functors $X \mapsto K^{q} (X) = [X, K]_{-q}$, $q \in \mathbb{Z}$, define a reduced cohomology theory on the category of base-pointed $CW$-complexes and base point preserving maps. 

The spaces $\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}$, $\textup{G}/\textup{O}$, $\textup{G}$ are infinite loop spaces \footnote{See also Lemma~\ref{lem:group-structures-on-ni-of-E} below.} and hence each gives us a spectrum. Applying the contravariant functors $[-,\textup{G}/\textup{O}]$ and $[-,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]$ to cofibration (\ref{mcof}) and using suspension and de-suspension isomorphisms in the respective $K$-cohomology, we obtain a commutative diagram as follows:  
\begin{equation}\label{mreln}
	\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=.7em, font=\small]

		{} \arrow[r,"r_2"] & \left[S^{4k-1},\textup{G}/\textup{O}\right] \arrow[r,"e\oplus D_{*}"] \arrow[d] & \left[S^{4k-1}\vee S^{8k-2}, \textup{G}/\textup{O}\right] \arrow[d] \\

We now study this diagram in detail. Topological surgery tells us that $[S^{n},\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}] \cong L_{n}(\mathbb{Z})$ and we have already calculated the normal invariants set $\mathcal{N}^{\textup{TOP}}(M) \cong [M,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}] \cong \mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}$ in Theorem \ref{thm:top-str-set}. We also obtain an improvement:

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:group-structures-on-ni-of-E}
	The two group structures on $\mathcal{N}^{\textup{TOP}}(M) \cong [M,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]$ coming from the two $H$-space structures on $\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}$ agree.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	The two group structures on $[S^{n},\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]$ agree for any $n$ since both of them agree with the group structure obtained from the co-$H$-spaces structure of $S^n$. By the above sequence, $[M,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]$ is the quotient of a homomorphism $[S^{4k},\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}] \rightarrow [S^{4k},\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]$, which proves the claim.
\end{proof}

By \eqref{eqn:pi_4k-of-G-mod-O} we have $[S^{4k},\textup{G}/\textup{O}] \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \Theta_{4k}$. By part 2. of Lemma~\ref{lem:Brumfiel-result-about-zeta_one-two}, the first two vertical maps are given as multiplication by $\theta_k$, which is defined in Notation \eqref{equation:nota}. If we denote by $R$ the cokernel of the map $r$ in the diagram (\ref{mreln}) then by combining it with above diagram we get the another commutative diagram:

\begin{lemma}\label{image}
	The image of the map $\chi$ is the group $\text{im}(\chi) = \langle \text{im}(\chi'),\text{im}(\chi'')\rangle$ generated by images of $\chi'$ and $\chi''$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
	It is easy to see that 
	\begin{center}
		$\langle \text{im}(\chi'),\text{im}(\chi'')\rangle \subseteq \text{im}(\chi)$.
	\end{center}
	Now let us prove inclusion the other way around.
	
	Let $y \in [M,\textup{G}/\textup{O}]$. Assume for a moment that there exists some $z \in [M,G]$ such that $r_2(\delta(z)) = r_2(y)$ in $[S^{4k-1},\textup{G}/\textup{O}]= \pi_{4k-1}(\textup{G}/\textup{O}) = c(J_{4k-1})$. Then we have:
	\begin{align*}
		r_2(\delta(z)-y) = r_2(\delta(z)) - r_2(y) = 0 & \implies \delta(z)-y = \overline{r}(x) \text{ for some } x \in R\\
		& \implies y = \delta(z) - \overline{r}(x)\\
		& \implies \chi(y) = \chi(\delta(z)) - \chi(\overline{r}(x)) = \chi'(z)-\chi''(x).
	\end{align*}
	This proves the other inclusion.
	
	It remains to show the existence of $z \in [M,G]$ that was assumed in the paragraph above. 
	
	Consider the following sequence of maps in which two consecutive arrows form a fibration up to homotopy: 
	\begin{center}
		$\cdots \rightarrow \textup{G} \rightarrow \textup{G}/\textup{O} \rightarrow \textup{BO} \rightarrow BG \rightarrow \cdots$
	\end{center} 
	After applying the covariant functors $[S^{4k-1},-]$ and $[S^{4k-1}\vee S^{8k-2},-]$, we get a commutative diagram of the long exact sequences that has a part looking like

		{[S^{4k-1},\textup{G}/\textup{O}]} \arrow[r,"e \oplus D_*"] \arrow[d,"f_1"] & {[S^{4k-1},\textup{G}/\textup{O}] \oplus [S^{8k-2},\textup{G}/\textup{O}]} \arrow[d,"g_1"] \\
		{[S^{4k-1},\textup{BO}]} \arrow[r,"e \oplus D_*"] & {[S^{4k-1},\textup{BO}] \oplus [S^{8k-2},\textup{BO}]}
	\end{tikzcd}

	Note that all the horizontal maps are different, but they are defined in the same way, so we are abusing the notation. After replacing known terms, see \eqref{eqn:htpy-groups-of-so}, the diagram becomes 
	\[
	\begin{tikzcd}

Also, in the first column, since $[S^{4k-1},\textup{G}/\textup{O}]$ is the cokernel of $J_{4k-1}$, we shall have a splitting in the opposite direction of $f$, say $f'$ (the splitting exists because of \eqref{eqn:splitting-of-coker-ofJ}). Given any $r_2(y) \in [S^{4k-1},\textup{G}/\textup{O}]$ for some $y \in [M,\textup{G}/\textup{O}]$, it is mapped to, say, some $\overline{r_2(y)} \in [S^{4k-1},G]$ under $f'$. By replacing known spaces and maps from the Kervaire-Milnor braid, see part 1. of Lemma~\ref{lem:Brumfiel-result-about-zeta_one-two}, we obtain

	\cdots \arrow[r,"0"]& \mathbb{Z} \arrow[r,"\cdot e"] \arrow[d,"{(j_{k}',t_1)}"] & \mathbb{Z} \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"{(j_{k}',t_1)}"]& {[Y,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]} \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"{\Psi|_{Y}}"] & 0 \arrow[d] \\
	\cdots\arrow[r] & \mathbb{Z} \oplus T \arrow[r,"\cdot e"] & \mathbb{Z} \oplus T \arrow[r] & {[Y,\textup{BTOP}]} \arrow[r] & {[S^{4k-1},\textup{BTOP}]} 
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $j_{k}'$ is defined earlier in (\ref{equation:nota}). Top exact row implies $[Y,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}] \cong \mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}$. The following commutative diagram will help us studying kernel of $\Psi|_{Y}$. 
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
	0 \arrow[r] & {[Y,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]} \arrow[r,"\cong"] \arrow[d,"\Psi|_{Y}'"] & {[Y,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]} \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"\Psi|_{Y}"] & 0 \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & 0 \\
	0 \arrow[r] & W \arrow[r] & {[Y,\textup{BTOP}]} \arrow[r] & W' \arrow[r] & 0  
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $W \cong \mathbb{Z} /e\mathbb{Z} \oplus T'$, with $T'$ being cokernel of restriction of $e$ onto $T$, is the cokernel of the bottom map of previous diagram and $W'$ is the cokernel of the inclusion of $W$ in $[Y,\textup{BTOP}]$.\\

By snake lemma, ker$(\Psi|_{Y}) \cong$ ker$\Psi|_{Y}'$. Let us denote it by $K$. We have 

\begin{center}
	$K = \text{ker}(\pi_{1}\circ \Psi|_{Y}') \cap \text{ker}(\pi_{2}\circ \Psi|_{Y}')$
\end{center}
where $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ are projections onto first and second components.\\


And it is clear that ker$(\pi_{1}\circ \Psi|_{Y}') \cong \mathbb{Z}/h'\mathbb{Z}$ where $h' = \text{gcd}(j_{k}',e)$. Therefore ker$(\Psi|_{Y})$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}/h'\mathbb{Z}$.\\

Finally, it remains to show that there is an injection ker$\Psi \rightarrow$ ker$\Psi|_{Y}$ implying that $\text{im}(\chi') = $ ker$(\Psi)$ is even smaller. The cofibre sequence
\begin{center}
	$Y \rightarrow M \rightarrow S^{8k-1}$	
\end{center} 
induces the commutative diagram where the indicated map in the top row turns out to be an isomorphism:                                                                                                                            
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
	0 \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & {[M,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]} \arrow[r,"\cong"] \arrow[d,"\Psi"] & {[Y,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]} \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"\Psi|_{Y}"] & \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \arrow[r,"\rho"] \arrow[d] & {} \\
	\pi_{8k-1}(\textup{BTOP}) \arrow[r] & {[M,\textup{BTOP}]} \arrow[r,"\Gamma"] & {[Y,\textup{BTOP}]} \arrow[r] & \pi_{8k-2}(\textup{BTOP}) \arrow[r] & {} 
\end{tikzcd}
\]
To show the statement about the map in the top row, we need to consider two cases. When $n \neq 0$ this map is an injective map from the finite cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}$ to itself, and so it is an isomorphism, which immediately gives us another diagram 
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
	0 \arrow[r] &	0 \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & {[M,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]} \arrow[r,"\cong"] \arrow[d,"\Psi"] & {[Y,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]} \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"\Psi|_{Y}"] & 0  \\
	0 \arrow[r] &	\text{ker}(\Gamma) \arrow[r] & {[M,\textup{BTOP}]} \arrow[r,"\Gamma"] & \text{im}(\Gamma) \arrow[r] & 0 
\end{tikzcd}
\]

Now the snake lemma implies that there is an exact sequence 
\begin{center}
	$0 \rightarrow \text{ker}(\Psi) \rightarrow \text{ker}(\Psi|_{Y}) \rightarrow \text{ker}(\Gamma) \rightarrow...$
\end{center}

therefore, the map ker$\Psi \rightarrow$ ker$\Psi|_Y$ is injective. When $n=0$, we can apply the same reasoning, but need to provide an additional reason for why the map $[M,G/TOP]\rightarrow [Y,G/TOP]$ is an isomorphism. Note that in this case it is a map $\mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}$. The argument is made by showing that the map $\rho \colon [S^{8k-2},\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}] \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \rightarrow [\Sigma^{-1} M,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}]$ to the right in the diagram is injective. This, in turn, is provided by considering a larger diagram where this map fits:
\[
\xymatrix{
	\cdots \ar[r] & [S^{8k-2},\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}] \ar[r]^{\rho} & [\Sigma^{-1} M,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}] \ar[r] & \cdots \\
	0 \ar[r] \ar[u] & [S^{8k-2},\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}] \ar[r]_-{\cong} \ar[u]^{\cong} & [S^{4k-1} \vee S^{8k-2} ,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}] \ar[r] \ar[u] & 0 \ar[u] \\
	& & [S^{4k-1} ,\textup{G}/\textup{TOP}] \cong 0 \ar[u] 
}
\]

This finishes the proof of the statement that the map ker($\Psi )\rightarrow$ ker($\Psi|_Y$) is injective.


 Also recall that ker$(\Psi) = \text{im}(\chi')$ which is a subgroup, say $A$, of $\mathbb{Z}/h'\mathbb{Z}$ where $h'=\text{gcd}(j_k',e)$. Therefore $\text{im}(\chi) = \langle A, B \rangle$ where $A = \text{im}(\chi') \le \mathbb{Z}/h'\mathbb{Z}$, $B = \text{im}(\chi'') \cong \theta_k\cdot (\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z})$. Note that $B$ is a cyclic group of order $\frac{e}{g} = \overline{e}$.

\begin{lemma}
	$A = \text{im}(\chi') \subseteq \text{im}(\chi'') = B$
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	We shall show that $h'|\overline{e}$, which means that $A$, is in fact a subgroup of $B$ and therefore $\text{im}(\chi) = \text{im}(\chi'')$. 
	
	Let us recall the integers involved, their relevant properties, and introduce some more notation. The necessary definitions are in and around \eqref{equation:nota} and \eqref{equation:alter}:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item $g = \gcd (e,\theta_k)$;
		\item $h' = \gcd (e,j'_k)$, which is an odd integer, since $j'_k$ is odd;
		\item $\theta_k = 2^{\alpha} \cdot \theta'_k$ for suitable $\alpha$;
		\item $1 = \gcd (j'_k,\theta'_k)$;
		\item $e = 2^{\beta} \cdot e'$, where $e'$ is odd, for suitable $\beta$;
		\item $\gamma = \min (\alpha,\beta)$.
	\end{itemize}
	
	Then $g = 2^{\gamma} \cdot \gcd (e',\theta'_k)$ and $g' = \gcd (e',\theta'_k)$ is odd, since both $e'$ and $\theta'_k$ are odd. Moreover, for $\delta = \max (0,\beta-\alpha)$, we have
	\[
	\overline{e} = \frac{e}{g} = 2^{\delta} \cdot \frac{e'}{g'}.
	\]
	Note that $h'$ divides $j'_k$ and $g'$ divides $\theta'_k$. Since $1 = \gcd (j'_k,\theta'_k)$ we obtain that $1 = \gcd (h',g')$. Also $h'$ is an odd number that divides $e$ and because it is coprime to $g'$ it also divides $\frac{e'}{g'}$.
\end{proof}

As a corollary we get
\begin{corollary}
	$\text{im}(\chi) = \text{im}(\chi'')$
\end{corollary}
Therefore the cokernel of $\chi$ contains $\frac{e}{e'} = g$ many elements.  This completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{fn}. \qed
