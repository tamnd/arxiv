<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{main}
Let $\mathcal V\leq\mathcal A$. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $TR_6\notin\mathcal V$;
\item $\mathcal V$ satisfies \eqref{P};
\item $\mathcal V$ is hereditarily finitely based.
\end{enumerate}
The subvariety lattice of $\mathcal K$ is finite, and every subvariety of $\mathcal K$ has a finite equational basis involving at most ten variables. Moreover, $\mathsf{V}(TR_6)$ is the unique limit subvariety of both $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal M$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{exclude}
For every $\mathcal V\leq\mathcal A$, the following are equivalent:
\[
 TR_6\notin\mathcal V,\qquad \mathcal V\models\eqref{P},\qquad \mathcal V\leq\mathcal K.
\]
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The last equivalence is the definition of $\mathcal K$. In $TR_6$, the substitution
$(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\mapsto(2,6,5,4)$ gives
\[
 x_1x_2+x_2x_3+x_3x_4=3+3+3=3,\qquad x_1x_4=2\cdot4=1.
\]
Since $3+1=1\neq3$, identity \eqref{P} fails in $TR_6$.

Conversely, assume $TR_6\notin\mathcal V$. By \eqref{one-sided}, there is an identity \eqref{failed-id} that holds in $\mathcal V$ and fails in $TR_6$. Choose $A,B$ as in \eqref{partition}, and put
\[
 \mathbf{p}=x_1x_2+x_2x_3+x_3x_4,
\]
where $x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4$ are distinct variables outside $c(\mathbf{u})\cupc(\mathbf{q})$. If a substitution $\varphi$ satisfies
$\mathbf{p}\approx\mathbf{p}+\varphi(\mathbf{u})$ by \eqref{A}, then in $\mathcal V$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{absorb-image}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbf{p}&\approx\mathbf{p}+\varphi(\mathbf{u})\\
 &\approx\mathbf{p}+\varphi(\mathbf{u})+\varphi(\mathbf{q})\qquad\text{by \eqref{failed-id}}\\
 &\approx\mathbf{p}+\varphi(\mathbf{q}).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We distinguish three cases.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Case 1.} $c(\mathbf{q})\nsubseteqc(\mathbf{u})$.
Choose $t\inc(\mathbf{q})\setminusc(\mathbf{u})$ and define
\[
 \varphi(a)=
 \begin{cases}
 x_2,&a\in A,\\
 x_3,&a\in B,\\
 x_1x_4,&a=t,\\
 x_2,&a\in X\setminus(A\cup B\cup\{t\}).
 \end{cases}
\]
Each additive subterm of $\varphi(\mathbf{u})$ is $x_2$, $x_3$ or $x_2x_3$. Hence \eqref{A} gives $\mathbf{p}\approx\mathbf{p}+\varphi(\mathbf{u})$. Also, $x_1x_4$ is a factor of $\varphi(\mathbf{q})$. By \eqref{absorb-image} and \eqref{factor},
\[
 \mathbf{p}\approx\mathbf{p}+\varphi(\mathbf{q})
 \approx\mathbf{p}+\varphi(\mathbf{q})+x_1x_4
 \approx\mathbf{p}+x_1x_4.
\]
Thus \eqref{P} holds.

We may now assume $c(\mathbf{q})\subseteqc(\mathbf{u})$. The recalled characterization of the identities of $TR_6$ gives $\ell(\mathbf{q})\geq2$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Case 2.} At least two occurrences of variables in $\mathbf{q}$ belong to the same part of \eqref{partition}.
Interchanging $A$ and $B$ if necessary, assume this part is $A$. Define $\varphi(a)=x_1$ for $a\in A$ and $\varphi(a)=x_2$ for $a\in B$; let $\varphi$ fix the other variables. Each additive subterm of $\varphi(\mathbf{u})$ is $x_1$, $x_2$ or $x_1x_2$, so $\mathbf{p}\approx\mathbf{p}+\varphi(\mathbf{u})$. Since $x_1^2$ is a factor of $\varphi(\mathbf{q})$, \eqref{absorb-image} and \eqref{factor} give
\begin{equation}\label{square-left}
 \mathbf{p}\approx\mathbf{p}+x_1^2.
\end{equation}
The substitution interchanging $x_1,x_4$ and $x_2,x_3$ fixes $\mathbf{p}$ and yields
\begin{equation}\label{square-right}
 \mathbf{p}\approx\mathbf{p}+x_4^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbf{p}&\approx\mathbf{p}+x_1^2+x_4^2\qquad\text{by \eqref{square-left}, \eqref{square-right}}\\
 &\approx\mathbf{p}+x_1^2+x_4^2+x_1x_4\qquad\text{by \eqref{B}}\\
 &\approx\mathbf{p}+x_1x_4\qquad\text{by \eqref{square-left}, \eqref{square-right}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves \eqref{P}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Case 3.} $\mathbf{q}=st$, where $s\in A$ and $t\in B$.
Since $st$ is not an additive subterm of $\mathbf{u}$, define
\[
 \varphi(a)=
 \begin{cases}
 x_1,&a=s,\\
 x_4,&a=t,\\
 x_3,&a\in A\setminus\{s\},\\
 x_2,&a\in B\setminus\{t\},\\
 a,&a\notin A\cup B.
 \end{cases}
\]
A member of $L_2(\mathbf{u})$ containing $s$ has image $x_1x_2$; one containing $t$ has image $x_3x_4$; one containing neither has image $x_2x_3$. None contains both $s$ and $t$. The image of an additive subterm of length one is one of $x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4$, each of which is a factor of a member of $L_2(\mathbf{p})$. Thus \eqref{A} gives $\mathbf{p}\approx\mathbf{p}+\varphi(\mathbf{u})$, while $\varphi(\mathbf{q})=x_1x_4$. Equation~\eqref{absorb-image} proves \eqref{P}.

Every additive subterm of length at least three belongs to Case~2, as does a square. The three cases therefore exhaust all possibilities.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{basic}
The variety $\mathcal K$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 x^4&\approx x^3,\label{cube-stable}\\
 xyz&\approx x^3+y^3+z^3,\label{triple-sum}\\
 x^3y&\approx x^3+y^3,\label{cube-product}\\
 x^3+y^2&\approx x^3+y^3.\label{cube-square}
\end{align}
If $\mathbf{w}\inX_c^+$ and $\ell(\mathbf{w})\geq3$, then
\begin{equation}\label{long-sum}
 \mathbf{w}\approx\sum_{x\inc(\mathbf{w})}x^3.
\end{equation}

Substitute $(x^2,x,x,x^2)$ for $(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)$ in \eqref{P}. Then
\[
 x^3\approx x^3+x^2
 \approx x^3+x^2+x^4
 \approx x^4,
\]
where the first and last steps use \eqref{A}. This proves \eqref{cube-stable}, and hence $x^k\approx x^3$ for every $k\geq3$.

Substituting $(xy,z,y,x)$ in \eqref{P} gives
\[
 xyz\approx xyz+yz+xy
 \approx xyz+yz+xy+x^2y
 \approx xyz+x^2y.
\]
Here the first and last steps again use \eqref{A}. Replacing $(x,y,z)$ by $(x,x,y)$ in the resulting identity yields $x^2y\approx x^2y+x^3$. Therefore
\[
 xyz\approx xyz+x^2y
 \approx xyz+x^2y+x^3
 \approx xyz+x^3.
\]
Permuting the variables, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{triple-absorb}
 xyz\approx xyz+x^3+y^3+z^3.
\end{equation}
For the reverse direction,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x^3+y^3+z^3
 &\approx x^4+y^4+z^3+z^2\qquad\text{by \eqref{cube-stable}, \eqref{A}}\\
 &\approx x^4+y^4+z^3+z^2+x^2y^2\qquad\text{by \eqref{B}}\\
 &\approx x^4+y^4+z^3+z^2+x^2y^2+xyz\qquad\text{by \eqref{B}}\\
 &\approx x^3+y^3+z^3+xyz\qquad\text{by \eqref{B}, \eqref{cube-stable}, \eqref{A}}\\
 &\approx xyz\qquad\text{by \eqref{triple-absorb}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The two steps adding a product use the substitutions $(x,y)\mapsto(x^2,y^2)$ and $(x,y)\mapsto(xy,z)$ in \eqref{B}. This proves \eqref{triple-sum}.

Now substitute $(x^2,x,y)$ in \eqref{triple-sum}. By \eqref{cube-stable},
\[
 x^3y\approx x^6+x^3+y^3\approx x^3+y^3,
\]
which is \eqref{cube-product}. Also,

\begin{aligned}
 x^3+y^2&\approx x^6+y^2
 \approx x^6+y^2+x^3y\qquad\text{by \eqref{B}}\\
 &\approx x^3+y^2+x^3+y^3\qquad\text{by \eqref{cube-stable}, \eqref{cube-product}}\\
 &\approx x^3+y^3\qquad\text{by \eqref{A}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Finally, prove \eqref{long-sum} by induction on length. The case of length three is \eqref{triple-sum}. If $\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{v} t$, where $t\in X$ and $\ell(\mathbf{v})\geq3$, then

\]

\label{K-lf}

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For a finite variable set $Z$, equation~\eqref{long-sum} expresses every $Z$-term as a nonempty sum of members of the finite set

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{quadratic}
Let $\mathbf{h}$ be a nonempty sum of additive subterms of length two, and let $Y=c(\mathbf{h})$. There are pairwise disjoint sets $C,U_1,V_1,\ldots,U_m,V_m$, where $m\geq0$ and each $U_i,V_i$ is nonempty, such that
\begin{equation}\label{quadratic-rep}
 Y=C\mathbin{\dot\cup}\bigcup_{i=1}^m(U_i\mathbin{\dot\cup}V_i),
 \qquad \mathbf{h}\approx\sum_{x\in C}x^2+\sum_{i=1}^m\boldsymbol{\ell}_{U_i}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{V_i}.
\end{equation}
If $x^2$ occurs in $\mathbf{h}$, then $x\in C$. Each additive subterm $xy$ of $\mathbf{h}$ has either both factors in $C$, or one in $U_i$ and the other in $V_i$ for some $i$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Write $Y=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}$, with the variables $a_i$ pairwise distinct, and put
\[
 \mathcal S=\{a_i a_j:1\leq i\leq j\leq n,
       \ \Sigma_{\mathcal K}\vdash\mathbf{h}\approx\mathbf{h}+a_i a_j\}.
\]
This is a finite set containing every additive subterm of $\mathbf{h}$. By adding its members successively,
\begin{equation}\label{s-sum}
 \mathbf{h}\approx\mathbf{h}+\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal S}\mathbf{w}
 \approx\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal S}\mathbf{w}.
\end{equation}
The second step uses additive idempotence. If $ab,bc,cd\in\mathcal S$, then
\[
 \mathbf{h}\approx\mathbf{h}+ab+bc+cd
 \approx\mathbf{h}+ab+bc+cd+ad\qquad\text{by \eqref{P}}
 \approx\mathbf{h}+ad.
\]
The last step removes $ab,bc,cd$ by the identities defining their membership in $\mathcal S$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{three-products}
 ab,bc,cd\in\mathcal S\quad\Longrightarrow\quad ad\in\mathcal S.
\end{equation}
The variables in this implication need not be distinct. Taking $(a,b,c,d)=(b,a,a,b)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{square-propagation}
 a^2,ab\in\mathcal S\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b^2\in\mathcal S.
\end{equation}
Put $C=\{a\in Y:a^2\in\mathcal S\}$ and $D=Y\setminus C$. By \eqref{square-propagation}, no member of $\mathcal S$ has one factor in $C$ and the other in $D$. The sum of the members with content in $C$ is equivalent to $\sum_{a\in C}a^2$, since \eqref{B} removes each additional product.

Suppose $D\neq\varnothing$. Every variable of $Y$ occurs in $\mathcal S$, so there is $ab\in\mathcal S$ with $a,b\in D$. Necessarily $a\neq b$. Define
\[
 U=\{x\in D:xb\in\mathcal S\},\qquad
 V=\{y\in D:ay\in\mathcal S\}.
\]
These sets are nonempty because $a\in U$ and $b\in V$. If $z\in U\cap V$, then $zb,ba,az\in\mathcal S$, so \eqref{three-products} gives $z^2\in\mathcal S$, contrary to $z\in D$. Hence $U\cap V=\varnothing$. For $x\in U$ and $y\in V$, the products $xb,ba,ay$ belong to $\mathcal S$, so
\begin{equation}\label{complete-products}
 xy\in\mathcal S\qquad(x\in U,\ y\in V).
\end{equation}
Conversely, let $x\in U$ and $xz\in\mathcal S$. Then $z\notin C$ by \eqref{square-propagation}; from $ab,bx,xz\in\mathcal S$, equation~\eqref{three-products} gives $az\in\mathcal S$. Thus $z\in V$. Similarly, if $y\in V$ and $yz\in\mathcal S$, then $ba,ay,yz\in\mathcal S$ give $bz\in\mathcal S$, so $z\in U$. Consequently,
\[
 \{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal S:c(\mathbf{w})\cap(U\cup V)\neq\varnothing\}
 =\{xy:x\in U,\ y\in V\}.
\]
Remove $U\cup V$ from $D$ and repeat the construction. The displayed equality ensures that each remaining variable still occurs with another remaining variable. Since $D$ is finite, the process gives disjoint pairs $U_i,V_i$ exhausting $D$. Their products have sum $\sum_i\boldsymbol{\ell}_{U_i}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{V_i}$, by distributivity. Equation~\eqref{s-sum} now proves \eqref{quadratic-rep}. The final assertions follow because all original additive subterms of $\mathbf{h}$ belong to $\mathcal S$.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{representation}
Every term is equivalent in $\mathcal K$ to a term
\begin{equation}\label{normal}
 \mathbf{u}=\sum_{x\in L}x+\sum_{x\in C}x^r
      +\sum_{i=1}^m\boldsymbol{\ell}_{U_i}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{V_i},
\end{equation}
where $r\in\{2,3\}$, the sets $L,C,U_1,V_1,\ldots,U_m,V_m$ are pairwise disjoint, their union is nonempty, and each $U_i,V_i$ is nonempty.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By \eqref{long-sum}, every term is equivalent to a sum of additive subterms of the forms $x,x^2,x^3,xy$ with $x\neq y$. If an additive subterm $x$ of length one also occurs as a factor of a longer additive subterm, remove $x$ using \eqref{A}. Let $L$ be the set of remaining variables of length one, and let $Q,T$ be the sets of variables whose squares and cubes occur. Let $E$ be the set of the remaining products of distinct variables. Adding the squares of the variables in $T$, by \eqref{A}, gives
\[
 \sum_{x\in L}x+\mathbf{h}+\sum_{x\in T}x^3,
 \qquad
 \mathbf{h}=\sum_{x\in Q\cup T}x^2+\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in E}\mathbf{w}.
\]
If the two indexing sets in $\mathbf{h}$ are empty, the original term is a sum of variables. Otherwise apply Lemma~\ref{quadratic} to $\mathbf{h}$. We obtain
\[
 \sum_{x\in L}x+\sum_{x\in C}x^2+\sum_{x\in T}x^3
       +\sum_{i=1}^m\boldsymbol{\ell}_{U_i}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{V_i},
 \qquad T\subseteq C.
\]
If $T$ is empty, take $r=2$. If $T$ is nonempty, choose $z\in T$ and use \eqref{cube-square} for each $x\in C\setminus\{z\}$:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{x\in C}x^2+\sum_{x\in T}x^3
 &\approx z^3+\sum_{x\in C\setminus\{z\}}x^2
             +\sum_{x\in T\setminus\{z\}}x^3\\
 &\approx z^3+\sum_{x\in C\setminus\{z\}}x^3
             +\sum_{x\in T\setminus\{z\}}x^3
 \approx\sum_{x\in C}x^3.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This yields \eqref{normal} with $r=3$.

<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{original-support}
Let $\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}_1+\cdots+\mathbf{v}_s$ be a term and choose its representation \eqref{normal} by the preceding construction. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a variable in $L$ occurs in $\mathbf{v}$ only as an additive subterm of length one;
\item an additive subterm of $\mathbf{v}$ involving a variable in $U_i\cup V_i$ is either a single variable or a product $ab$ with $a\in U_i$ and $b\in V_i$;
\item if some $\mathbf{v}_j$ has length at least three, the representation has $r=3$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The definition of $L$ proves (i). A variable occurring in a square or in an additive subterm of length at least three has its square in $\mathbf{h}$, and therefore belongs to $C$. Lemma~\ref{quadratic} gives (ii). A subterm of length at least three makes $T$ nonempty, which gives (iii).

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{ten}
Each such identity is equivalent within $\mathcal K$ to one identity involving at most ten variables.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Put $D=c(\mathbf{q})$. Renumber the pairs $U_i,V_i$ so that
\[
 (U_i\cup V_i)\cap D\neq\varnothing\quad(1\leq i\leq k),\qquad
 (U_i\cup V_i)\cap D=\varnothing\quad(k<i\leq m).
\]
Since the sets are pairwise disjoint and $|D|\leq2$, we have $k\leq2$. Choose distinct new variables $a,b,a_i,b_i$ for $1\leq i\leq k$, and $z_1,z_2$, outside $c(\mathbf{u})\cup D$. Define a substitution fixing $D$ by
\[
 \varphi(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 a,&x\in L\setminus D,\\
 b,&x\in C\setminus D,\\
 a_i,&x\in U_i\setminus D,\quad 1\leq i\leq k,\\
 b_i,&x\in V_i\setminus D,\quad 1\leq i\leq k,\\
 z_1,&x\in U_i,\quad k<i\leq m,\\
 z_2,&x\in V_i,\quad k<i\leq m.
 \end{cases}
\]
Other variables are fixed. Put $\mathbf{u}_0=\varphi(\mathbf{u})$. Every pair with $i>k$ has image $z_1z_2$, and $\varphi(\mathbf{q})=\mathbf{q}$. Therefore $\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}$ implies
\begin{equation}\label{reduced-id}
 \mathbf{u}_0\approx\mathbf{u}_0+\mathbf{q},
\end{equation}
which involves at most $|D|+2+2k+2\leq10$ variables.

To prove the converse, define $\psi$ to fix $D$ and set
\[
 \psi(a)=\boldsymbol{\ell}_{L\setminus D},\quad
 \psi(b)=\boldsymbol{\ell}_{C\setminus D},\quad
 \psi(a_i)=\boldsymbol{\ell}_{U_i\setminus D},\quad
 \psi(b_i)=\boldsymbol{\ell}_{V_i\setminus D}\quad(1\leq i\leq k).
\]
Each prescription is used only when the corresponding variable occurs in $\mathbf{u}_0$, so its right-hand side is nonempty. Distributivity restores the first $k$ product parts. For the power part, Lemma~\ref{sum-powers} gives
\[
 \psi\varphi\left(\sum_{x\in C}x^r\right)
 \approx\sum_{x\in C\cap D}x^r+
       \left(\sum_{x\in C\setminus D}x\right)^r
 \approx\sum_{x\in C}x^r.
\]
The part of length one is restored as well.

If $k=m$, then $\psi(\mathbf{u}_0)\approx\mathbf{u}$, and applying $\psi$ to \eqref{reduced-id} proves the assertion. If $k<m$, also set
\[
 \psi(z_1)=\boldsymbol{\ell}_{U_{k+1}},\qquad
 \psi(z_2)=\boldsymbol{\ell}_{V_{k+1}}.
\]
Then $\psi(\mathbf{u}_0)\approx\mathbf{h}$, where
\[
 \mathbf{h}=\sum_{x\in L}x+\sum_{x\in C}x^r
             +\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{U_i}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{V_i}.
\]
Each additive subterm of $\mathbf{h}$, after distributing, occurs in $\mathbf{u}$, so $\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{h}\approx\mathbf{u}$. Since $\psi(\mathbf{q})=\mathbf{q}$, equation~\eqref{reduced-id} yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbf{u}&\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{h}
 \approx\mathbf{u}+\psi(\mathbf{u}_0)\\
 &\approx\mathbf{u}+\psi(\mathbf{u}_0+\mathbf{q})\qquad\text{by \eqref{reduced-id}}\\
 &\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{h}+\mathbf{q}
 \approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \eqref{reduced-id} is equivalent to the original identity within $\mathcal K$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{K-finite-lattice}
The subvariety lattice of $\mathcal K$ is finite. Every subvariety of $\mathcal K$ has a finite equational basis involving at most ten variables.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Fix $Z=\{z_1,\ldots,z_{10}\}$. By Corollary~\ref{K-lf}, the relatively free algebra $F_{\mathcal K}(Z)$ is finite. Choose a finite set $R$ of terms representing its elements, and put
\[
 \Theta=\{\mathbf{r}\approx\mathbf{s}:\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}\in R\}.
\]
Let $\mathcal V=\mathcal K\cap[\Sigma]$ be a subvariety of $\mathcal K$. By \eqref{one-sided}, Proposition~\ref{representation}, and Lemma~\ref{ten}, each identity $\varepsilon\in\Sigma$ is equivalent in $\mathcal K$ to finitely many identities on at most ten variables. Rename their variables into $Z$ and replace each term by its representative in $R$. This gives a subset $\Theta_\varepsilon\subseteq\Theta$ equivalent to $\varepsilon$ within $\mathcal K$.

The set $\Delta=\bigcup_{\varepsilon\in\Sigma}\Theta_\varepsilon$ is a subset of the finite set $\Theta$, and $\mathcal V=\mathcal K\cap[\Delta]$. Hence only finitely many subvarieties of $\mathcal K$ occur. Moreover, $\Sigma_{\mathcal K}\cup\Delta$ is a finite basis for $\mathcal V$. The identities in $\Sigma_{\mathcal K}$ use at most four variables, and those in $\Delta$ use at most ten.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{K-in-M}
The variety $\mathcal K$ is a subvariety of $\mathcal M$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $\mathbf{v}\approx\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{q}$ hold in $\mathbf{N}$, where $\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}_1+\cdots+\mathbf{v}_s$ and $\mathbf{v}_j,\mathbf{q}\inX_c^+$. Assigning $1$ to a variable of $\mathbf{q}$ outside $c(\mathbf{v})$ and $0$ to the other variables proves
\begin{equation}\label{content-N}
 c(\mathbf{q})\subseteqc(\mathbf{v}).
\end{equation}
Put $d=\max_j\ell(\mathbf{v}_j)$, and let $\mathbf{u}$ be the representation \eqref{normal} of $\mathbf{v}$ obtained in Proposition~\ref{representation}. We prove $\mathbf{v}\approx\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{q}$ in $\mathcal K$.

If $\ell(\mathbf{q})=1$, use \eqref{content-N} and \eqref{factor}. If $\mathbf{q}=x^2$, evaluate $x$ at $1$ and all other variables at $0$ in $\mathbf{N}$. Some $\mathbf{v}_j$ must contain $x$ at least twice, so \eqref{factor} again applies.

Suppose $\mathbf{q}=xy$, with $x\neq y$. If $x,y\in C$, the power part of $\mathbf{u}$ absorbs $x^2+y^2$, and then absorbs $xy$ by \eqref{B}. If $x\in U_i$ and $y\in V_i$, or conversely, $xy$ is an additive subterm of $\mathbf{u}$ after distributing.

In any other case, at least one of $x,y$ lies outside $C$; denote it by $x$. Let $D$ be the part $U_i$ or $V_i$ containing $x$, or let $D=\{x\}$ if $x\in L$. If $y\in D$, assign $1$ to every variable in $D$ and $0$ elsewhere. By Corollary~\ref{original-support}, each original $\mathbf{v}_j$ contains at most one occurrence from $D$. Thus $\mathbf{v}\leq1<2=xy$, a contradiction.

If $y\notin D$, it does not lie in the opposite part of the same pair. Choose an integer $N>d$ and evaluate
\[
 t\mapsto\begin{cases}
 N,&t\in D,\\1,&t=y,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
An original additive subterm involving $D$ is a single variable from $D$, or a product whose other variable has value $0$. It has value at most $N$. Every other original additive subterm has value at most $d<N$. Hence $\mathbf{v}\leq N<N+1=xy$, again a contradiction. This settles length two.

Finally, let $\ell(\mathbf{q})\geq3$. Evaluating all variables at $1$ gives $d\geq\ell(\mathbf{q})\geq3$, so Corollary~\ref{original-support} gives $r=3$. Suppose $x\inc(\mathbf{q})\setminus C$, and define $D$ as above. Assign $N>d$ to the variables in $D$ and $1$ to all other variables. The original additive subterms involving $D$ have value at most $N+1$; all others have value at most $d$. But
\[

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{congruences}

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{embeddings}
Let $2\leq k\leq l$. There is an embedding $A_k\hookrightarrow A_l$ if and only if some integer $r\geq1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{embedding-condition}
 (k-1)r<l\leq kr.
\end{equation}
In particular, $A_k$ does not embed in $A_{k+1}$ for $k\geq3$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
The multiplicatively idempotent elements of $A_l$ are $0$ and $l$. If an embedding $f$ sent $0$ to $l$, the equalities $0\cdot a=a$ would force $f(a)=l$ for every $a$. Thus $f(0)=0$. Put $r=f(1)>0$. Since each positive integer $j\leq k$ is a power of numerical $1$,
\[
 f(j)=\min\{jr,l\}\qquad(1\leq j\leq k).
\]
Also, $k$ is multiplicatively idempotent and has nonzero image, so $f(k)=l$. Injectivity is therefore equivalent to \eqref{embedding-condition}.

Conversely, under \eqref{embedding-condition}, the displayed formula and $f(0)=0$ define a strictly increasing map, which preserves maximum. For $a,b\in A_k$, numerical calculation gives
\[
 \min\{\min\{a+b,k\}r,l\}
 =\min\{(a+b)r,l\}
 =\min\{f(a)+f(b),l\}.
\]
Thus it also preserves multiplication. Finally, if $l=k+1$, the inequality $l\leq kr$ forces $r\geq2$, whereas $(k-1)r\geq2k-2\geq k+1$ for $k\geq3$.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{unit-fb}
Let $S$ be a finite commutative ai-semiring satisfying \eqref{A} and possessing a multiplicative identity. Then $S$ is finitely based in the signature $(2,2)$. If $|S|=d+1>1$, a basis can be chosen whose identities, apart from the commutative ai-semiring axioms, involve at most $d+1$ variables.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
The one-element case is immediate. Suppose $|S|=d+1>1$, and let $e$ be its multiplicative identity. For $a\in S$, identity~\eqref{A} gives $e+a=e+ea=ea=a$. Thus $e$ is the least element of the additive order.

We first prove that $S$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{length-reduction}
 x_1\cdots x_{d+1}\approx
 \sum_{i=1}^{d+1}x_1\cdots x_{i-1}x_{i+1}\cdots x_{d+1}.
\end{equation}
For arbitrary $a_1,\ldots,a_{d+1}\in S$, consider
\[
 e\leq a_1\leq a_1a_2\leq\cdots\leq a_1\cdots a_{d+1}.
\]
There are $d+2$ entries and only $d+1$ elements of $S$, so two adjacent entries coincide. Multiplication by the remaining factors shows that deleting the corresponding factor does not change the full product. Each product obtained by deleting a factor is at most the full product, by \eqref{A}. Their sum is therefore the full product, proving \eqref{length-reduction}.

Repeated use of \eqref{length-reduction} reduces every term to a sum of additive subterms of length at most $d$. Fix $d$ variables, and let $R_d$ consist of all such terms on these variables, modulo commutativity and additive idempotence. This set is finite. Let $\Gamma$ be the set of all identities between members of $R_d$ that hold in $S$.

We prove that the commutative ai-semiring axioms, \eqref{A}, \eqref{length-reduction}, and $\Gamma$ form a basis for $S$. By the length reduction and \eqref{one-sided}, it suffices to derive $\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}$ when every additive subterm involved has length at most $d$.

First, $c(\mathbf{q})\subseteqc(\mathbf{u})$. Otherwise assign an element $a\neq e$ to a variable of $\mathbf{q}$ absent from $\mathbf{u}$, and assign $e$ to every other variable. The value of $\mathbf{u}$ is $e$, whereas that of $\mathbf{q}$ is $a^r\geq a>e$, a contradiction.

Delete from each additive subterm of $\mathbf{u}$ the variables outside $c(\mathbf{q})$, and omit any additive subterm all of whose variables are deleted. Denote the resulting nonempty term by $\mathbf{v}$. Evaluating the deleted variables at $e$ in $S$ shows that $S\models\mathbf{v}\approx\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{q}$. This identity involves at most $\ell(\mathbf{q})\leq d$ variables, and its additive subterms have length at most $d$. After renaming variables, it belongs to $\Gamma$. Each additive subterm of $\mathbf{v}$ is a nonempty factor of an additive subterm of $\mathbf{u}$. Hence
\[
 \mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{q}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q},
\]
by \eqref{factor} and $\Gamma$. This proves completeness and the stated variable bound.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{no-first-use}
Let $\Sigma\subseteq\operatorname{Id}(\mathbf{N})$. If
\[
 \Sigma\cup\{x^3+z\approx x^3,\ x^2y^2+z\approx x^2y^2\}
 \vdash\varepsilon_n,
\]
then $\Sigma\vdash\varepsilon_n$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
Suppose $\mathbf{N}\models\mathbf{t}\approx\mathbf{v}_n$. No additive subterm of $\mathbf{t}$ contains a variable three times: assigning $1$ to that variable and $0$ to all others would give $\mathbf{t}\geq3$ and $\mathbf{v}_n\leq2$. Nor can an additive subterm contain two distinct variables each at least twice: assigning $1$ to those two variables and $0$ elsewhere would give $\mathbf{t}\geq4$ and $\mathbf{v}_n\leq3$.

Consider a derivation starting from $\mathbf{v}_n$. Before the first use of either added identity, the current term is equal to $\mathbf{v}_n$ in $\mathbf{N}$, and therefore has the two stated properties. An instance of either side of $x^3+z\approx x^3$ contains $\varphi(x)^3$. Choosing an additive subterm $\mathbf{a}$ of $\varphi(x)$ gives the additive subterm $\mathbf{a}^3$, in which some variable occurs at least three times.

Similarly, an instance of either side of $x^2y^2+z\approx x^2y^2$ contains $\varphi(x)^2\varphi(y)^2$. Choose additive subterms $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ of $\varphi(x)$ and $\varphi(y)$. Its expansion contains $\mathbf{a}^2\mathbf{b}^2$. If $c(\mathbf{a})\capc(\mathbf{b})\neq\varnothing$, some variable occurs at least four times. Otherwise two distinct variables each occur at least twice.

An additive context retains the indicated additive subterm; multiplication by a nonempty term retains a product having it as a factor. Thus the term before the first application would violate one of the two properties. No such first application exists, and the derivation uses only $\Sigma$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{one-greatest}
The following varieties are finitely based:
\begin{align}
 &\mathcal M\cap[x_1\cdots x_r+z\approx x_1\cdots x_r]
       &&(r\geq1),\label{linear-greatest}\\
 &\mathcal M\cap[x^2y_1\cdots y_m+z\approx x^2y_1\cdots y_m]
       &&(m\geq0),\label{single-square-greatest}
\end{align}
where the displayed variables are distinct except for the repeated $x$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
Consider \eqref{single-square-greatest}, and put $d=m+1$. Its defining identity and \eqref{A} make every nonlinear additive subterm of length at least $d+1$ greatest. Indeed, choose two occurrences of one variable for $x^2$, choose $m$ further factors for the $y_i$, and multiply by any remaining factors.

Fix $d$ variables. Let $\Gamma_d$ consist of all identities of $\mathbf{N}$ between the finitely many terms on these variables whose additive subterms have length at most $d$, modulo commutativity and additive idempotence. We prove that the commutative ai-semiring axioms, \eqref{A}, the defining identity in \eqref{single-square-greatest}, and $\Gamma_d$ form a basis.

Since $\mathbf{q}$ has value $\ell(\mathbf{q})$, validity forces $\mathbf{q}$ to be linear, and some linear additive subterm of $\mathbf{u}$ must contain every variable of $\mathbf{q}$. Then \eqref{factor} proves the identity.

Delete the variables outside $c(\mathbf{q})$ from the additive subterms of $\mathbf{u}$, omitting products that become empty, and call the resulting term $\mathbf{v}$. Each additive subterm of $\mathbf{v}$ has length at most $d$: an originally short subterm remains short, and an originally linear one has at most $|c(\mathbf{q})|\leq d$ variables. The identity therefore belongs to $\Gamma_d$ after renaming. Using \eqref{factor},
\[
 \mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{q}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}.

This proves finite basedness in \eqref{single-square-greatest}.

<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{combine}
Assume \eqref{A}, \eqref{B}, and $x^2\approx x^3$. Let $\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}\inX_c^+$ have every variable occurring at most twice. Define a product $\mathbf{w}$ by the following rules: a variable occurs twice in $\mathbf{w}$ if it occurs twice in at least one of $\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}$; otherwise, it occurs once in $\mathbf{w}$ exactly when it occurs in both $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{s}$. If this product is nonempty, then
\begin{equation}\label{combine-id}
 \mathbf{r}+\mathbf{s}\approx\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{w}.
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-31: body_md -->
Delete from $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{s}$ the variables whose multiplicity pairs are $(1,0)$ or $(0,1)$. Denote the products of the variables with multiplicity pairs $(2,0),(2,1),(1,2),(0,2)$ by $\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf c,\mathbf d$, respectively. Let $\mathbf{h}$ collect the variables with pairs $(1,1),(2,2)$, with those common multiplicities. The retained products and the prescribed product are
\[
 \mathbf{r}_0=\mathbf{h}\mathbf{a}^2\mathbf{b}^2\mathbf c,\qquad
 \mathbf{s}_0=\mathbf{h}\mathbf d^2\mathbf{b}\mathbf c^2,\qquad
 \mathbf{w}=\mathbf{h}\mathbf{a}^2\mathbf d^2\mathbf{b}^2\mathbf c^2.
\]
An absent factor in these products is omitted. If $\mathbf{a}^2\mathbf{b}$ has no factors, then $\mathbf{w}$ is a nonempty factor of $\mathbf{s}$. If $\mathbf d^2\mathbf c$ has no factors, then $\mathbf{w}$ is a nonempty factor of $\mathbf{r}$. In either case, \eqref{factor} proves \eqref{combine-id}.

Otherwise $\mathbf{a}^2\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf d^2\mathbf c$ are nonempty substitution terms for \eqref{B}. By $x^2\approx x^3$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbf{r}_0+\mathbf{s}_0
 &\approx\mathbf{h}\mathbf{a}^4\mathbf{b}^3\mathbf c+\mathbf{h}\mathbf d^4\mathbf{b}\mathbf c^3\\
 &=\mathbf{h}\mathbf{b}\mathbf c\bigl((\mathbf{a}^2\mathbf{b})^2+(\mathbf d^2\mathbf c)^2\bigr)\\
 &\approx\mathbf{h}\mathbf{b}\mathbf c\bigl((\mathbf{a}^2\mathbf{b})^2+(\mathbf d^2\mathbf c)^2
                          +(\mathbf{a}^2\mathbf{b})(\mathbf d^2\mathbf c)\bigr)\qquad\text{by \eqref{B}}\\
 &\approx\mathbf{r}_0+\mathbf{s}_0+\mathbf{h}\mathbf{a}^2\mathbf d^2\mathbf{b}^2\mathbf c^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
When $\mathbf{h}\mathbf{b}\mathbf c$ has no factors, the displayed application of \eqref{B} is made without an outer multiplier. Both $\mathbf{r}_0$ and $\mathbf{s}_0$ are now nonempty factors of $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{s}$, respectively. Thus \eqref{factor} gives
\[
 \mathbf{r}+\mathbf{s}\approx\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{r}_0+\mathbf{s}_0
 \approx\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{r}_0+\mathbf{s}_0+\mathbf{w}
 \approx\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{w}.
\]

<!-- theorem-8: body_md -->
\label{P2-basis}
The variety $\mathcal P_2$ is defined by the commutative ai-semiring axioms, \eqref{A}, \eqref{B}, and $x^2\approx x^3$.
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-32: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal U$ be defined by the stated finite set. Then $\mathcal P_2\leq\mathcal U$. We prove that every identity $\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}$ of $\mathbf{N}$ holds in $\mathcal U$.

Apply \eqref{convex} to the original additive subterms of $\mathbf{u}$ and to $\mathbf{q}$. After omitting coefficients equal to zero, there is a nonempty index set $I$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{positive-combination}
 \alpha(\mathbf{q})\leq\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i\alpha(\mathbf{u}_i),
 \qquad\lambda_i>0,\qquad\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i=1.
\end{equation}
Let $\overline{\mathbf{u}}_i$ and $\overline{\mathbf{q}}$ be obtained by replacing every exponent greater than two by two. These replacements are valid in $\mathcal U$ by $x^2\approx x^3$.

If every selected $\mathbf{u}_i$ is linear, \eqref{positive-combination} forces $\mathbf{q}$ to be linear. Each variable of $\mathbf{q}$ occurs in every selected $\mathbf{u}_i$: a positive weighted average of numbers in $\{0,1\}$ is at least one only if all the numbers equal one. Thus $\mathbf{q}$ is a factor of each selected $\mathbf{u}_i$, and \eqref{factor} applies.

Otherwise begin with a selected $\overline{\mathbf{u}}_i$ containing a square, and apply Lemma~\ref{combine} successively with the other selected $\overline{\mathbf{u}}_j$. At every step that square is retained, so the prescribed product is nonempty. The resulting additive subterm $\mathbf{w}$ satisfies
\[
 \sum_{i\in I}\overline{\mathbf{u}}_i
 \approx\sum_{i\in I}\overline{\mathbf{u}}_i+\mathbf{w}.
\]
A variable occurs twice in $\mathbf{w}$ exactly when it occurs at least twice in a selected original $\mathbf{u}_i$. Every remaining variable occurs once in $\mathbf{w}$ exactly when it occurs in all the selected $\mathbf{u}_i$.

If a variable occurs at least twice in $\mathbf{q}$, equation~\eqref{positive-combination} implies that it occurs at least twice in some selected $\mathbf{u}_i$. If it occurs once in $\mathbf{q}$, then either some selected $\mathbf{u}_i$ contains it at least twice, or its selected exponents all belong to $\{0,1\}$. In the latter case they must all equal one. It follows that $\overline{\mathbf{q}}$ is a factor of $\mathbf{w}$. Adding the unselected additive subterms and using \eqref{factor} proves $\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}$ in $\mathcal U$.

Therefore $\mathcal U\leq\mathcal M$ by \eqref{one-sided}. Its defining power identity then gives $\mathcal U=\mathcal P_2$.

<!-- theorem-9: body_md -->
\label{P-classification}
For $k\geq1$, the variety $\mathcal P_k$ is finitely based if and only if $k\leq2$. Moreover,
\[
 \mathcal P_1<\mathcal P_2<\mathcal P_3<\cdots,\qquad
 \bigvee_{k\geq1}\mathcal P_k=\mathcal M.
\]
<!-- end theorem-9 -->
<!-- proof-33: body_md -->
Under $x^2\approx x$, distributivity and \eqref{A} give
\[
 x+y\approx(x+y)^2\approx x+y+xy\approx xy.
\]
Thus $\mathcal P_1$ is the variety with both operations equal to a semilattice operation, and is finitely based. The case $k=2$ is Theorem~\ref{P2-basis}. For $k\geq3$, each member of $\mathcal D$ has all cubes greatest and multiplicatively absorbing. Hence $\mathcal D\leq\mathcal P_k$, and Theorem~\ref{NFB-interval} gives nonfinite basedness.

Multiplying $x^k\approx x^{k+1}$ gives $\mathcal P_k\leq\mathcal P_{k+1}$. The algebra $A_{k+1}$ belongs to $\mathcal P_{k+1}$ but not to $\mathcal P_k$, so each inclusion is strict. Since $A_k\in\mathcal P_k$, equation~\eqref{Ak-chain} proves the join assertion.

The variety $\mathcal M$ has no maximal proper subvariety.
<!-- end corollary-10 -->

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{atoms-exclude}
For every $\mathcal V\leq\mathcal M$,
\begin{align}
 T_2\notin\mathcal V
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal V\models x^2\approx x
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal V\leq\mathsf{V}(M_2),\label{T2-exclude}\\
 M_2\notin\mathcal V
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal V\models x^k+z\approx x^k\text{ for some }k\geq1.\label{M2-exclude}
\end{align}
The only atoms of the subvariety lattice of $\mathcal M$ are $\mathsf{V}(M_2)$ and $\mathsf{V}(T_2)$.
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-35: body_md -->
Suppose $T_2\notin\mathcal V$ and choose an identity $\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}$ of $\mathcal V$ failing in $T_2$. Since every product of length at least two is greatest in $T_2$, all additive subterms of $\mathbf{u}$ have length one. If $\ell(\mathbf{q})\geq2$, identify all variables to get $x\approx x^{\ell(\mathbf{q})}$. Equation~\eqref{A} then gives $x\approx x^2$. If $\mathbf{q}$ has length one, its variable is absent from $\mathbf{u}$; map it to $x^2$ and all variables of $\mathbf{u}$ to $x$, with the same conclusion. Under $x^2\approx x$, the calculation in the proof of Theorem~\ref{P-classification} gives $xy\approx x+y$. Both operations therefore coincide with a semilattice operation. Conversely, $x^2\approx x$ fails in $T_2$, proving \eqref{T2-exclude}.

The identities of $M_2$ are exactly those with the same content on both sides. If $M_2\notin\mathcal V$, choose an identity $\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}$ of $\mathcal V$ and $t\inc(\mathbf{q})\setminusc(\mathbf{u})$. Map $t$ to $z$ and every other variable to $x$. If $k$ is the maximum length of an additive subterm of $\mathbf{u}$, its image reduces to $x^k$ by \eqref{A}. The image of $\mathbf{q}$ has $z$ as a factor, giving
\[
 x^k\approx x^k+\varphi(\mathbf{q})
 \approx x^k+\varphi(\mathbf{q})+z
 \approx x^k+z.
\]
No such identity holds in $M_2$, which proves \eqref{M2-exclude}.

The varieties $\mathsf{V}(M_2)$ and $\mathsf{V}(T_2)$ are minimal nontrivial varieties by~\cite{YSR}. If a subvariety of $\mathcal M$ omitted both, \eqref{T2-exclude} and \eqref{M2-exclude} would imply $x+z\approx x$, making it trivial. Thus there are no other atoms.

<!-- proposition-9: body_md -->
\label{join-atoms}
Relative to the commutative ai-semiring axioms, $\mathcal W$ is defined by \eqref{A} and
\begin{align}
 x^2&\approx x^3,\label{join1}\\
 xy&\approx x^2+y^2,\label{join2}\\
 x^2+y&\approx x^2+y^2.\label{join3}
\end{align}
Its subvariety lattice consists of $\mathcal T$, $\mathsf{V}(M_2)$, $\mathsf{V}(T_2)$ and $\mathcal W$, and $\mathcal W<\mathsf{V}(A_2)$.
<!-- end proposition-9 -->
<!-- proof-36: body_md -->
Both generators satisfy the displayed identities. Equations~\eqref{join1} and \eqref{join2} express every additive subterm of length at least two as the sum of the squares of its content variables. For the induction step, use
\[
 x^2y\approx x^4+y^2\approx x^2+y^2.
\]
If a term has an additive subterm of length at least two, \eqref{join3} also replaces every remaining additive subterm of length one by its square. Every term thus has one of the two forms
\[
 \sum_{x\in C}x,\qquad\sum_{x\in C}x^2,
\]
according as all original additive subterms have length one or at least one has greater length. In $M_2$, equality requires equal contents. In $T_2$, the second form is constantly greatest, whereas the first is not. These observations distinguish any two different represented terms and prove that the displayed identities define $\mathcal W$.

A proper subvariety cannot contain both generators. If it omits $T_2$, equation~\eqref{T2-exclude} places it below $\mathsf{V}(M_2)$. If it omits $M_2$, equation~\eqref{M2-exclude} gives $x^k+z\approx x^k$. For $k=1$ the variety is trivial. For $k\geq2$, \eqref{join1} gives $x^2+z\approx x^2$, so all squares coincide, and \eqref{join2} makes multiplication constant. The standard basis for $T_2$ then places the variety below $\mathsf{V}(T_2)$~\cite{YSR}. This proves the lattice assertion.

The subsets $\{0,2\}$ and $\{1,2\}$ of $A_2$ are isomorphic to $M_2$ and $T_2$, respectively. Hence $\mathcal W\leq\mathsf{V}(A_2)$. Identity~\eqref{join2} fails in $A_2$ at $x=0$, $y=1$, proving strictness.

<!-- corollary-11: body_md -->
\label{fibres}
Let $\mathcal L(\mathcal U)$ denote the subvariety lattice of $\mathcal U$, and let
\[
 \pi:\mathcal L(\mathcal M)\longrightarrow\mathcal L(\mathcal W),\qquad
 \pi(\mathcal V)=\mathcal V\cap\mathcal W.
\]
Then
\begin{align*}
 \pi^{-1}(\mathcal T)&=\{\mathcal T\},&
 \pi^{-1}(\mathsf{V}(M_2))&=\{\mathsf{V}(M_2)\},\\
 \pi^{-1}(\mathsf{V}(T_2))&=\bigcup_{k\geq2}[\mathsf{V}(T_2),\mathcal W_k],&
 \pi^{-1}(\mathcal W)&=[\mathcal W,\mathcal M].
\end{align*}
<!-- end corollary-11 -->
<!-- proof-37: body_md -->
If $\mathcal V\cap\mathcal W=\mathcal T$, then $\mathcal V$ omits both atoms and is trivial. If the intersection is $\mathsf{V}(M_2)$, then $T_2\notin\mathcal V$, so \eqref{T2-exclude} gives $\mathcal V=\mathsf{V}(M_2)$. If the intersection is $\mathsf{V}(T_2)$, then $\mathcal V$ contains $T_2$ and omits $M_2$. By \eqref{M2-exclude}, it lies below some $\mathcal W_k$, necessarily with $k\geq2$. Conversely, each variety in such an interval contains $T_2$ and omits $M_2$. The last equality is immediate from $\pi(\mathcal V)=\mathcal W$ if and only if $\mathcal W\leq\mathcal V$.

<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{descending}
Each $\mathcal B_N$ is finitely based, and
\[
 \mathsf{V}(TR_6)<\cdots<\mathcal B_{N+1}<\mathcal B_N\leq\mathsf{V}(A_2),
 \qquad\bigcap_{N\geq1}\mathcal B_N=\mathsf{V}(TR_6).
\]
For $\mathbf{w}\inX_c^+$ and $z\notinc(\mathbf{w})$, the identity $\mathbf{w}+z\approx\mathbf{w}$ holds in some $\mathcal B_N$ if and only if it holds in all $\mathcal B_N$, if and only if it holds in $TR_6$. These conditions are equivalent to $\ell(\mathbf{w})\geq3$ or $\mathbf{w}$ being nonlinear.
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-38: body_md -->
Finite basedness follows from the finite defining set. The basis in~\cite[Proposition~3.5]{SRG} gives the intersection equality. The proof of~\cite[Proposition~3.6]{SRG} shows that $\mathcal B_N$ fails $\sigma_{N+1}$, so $\mathcal B_{N+1}<\mathcal B_N$. Also $\mathsf{V}(TR_6)<\mathcal B_N$ because the former is nonfinitely based.

In $\mathcal B_N$, every square is greatest, so \eqref{A2-basis1} holds. Identity $\sigma_1$ makes $xy+yz+xz$ greatest, and $xyz\approx x^2$ does the same for triple products. Thus \eqref{A2-basis2} holds, and the cited basis for $A_2$ gives $\mathcal B_N\leq\mathsf{V}(A_2)$.

A nonlinear additive subterm has a square factor, and every product of length at least three is greatest in $\mathcal B_N$. Conversely, a remaining $\mathbf{w}$ is a variable or a product of two distinct variables. In $TR_6$, assign $5$ to the former, or $5,6$ to the factors of the latter, and assign $1$ to $z$. The value of $\mathbf{w}$ is $5$ or $3$, respectively, and $\mathbf{w}+z\approx\mathbf{w}$ fails. Since $TR_6\in\mathcal B_N$ for every $N$, it fails there as well.
