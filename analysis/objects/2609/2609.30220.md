<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:weight_convergence}
\underline{Choice of initialization:} Assume that $\mu_\omega^1 = \sum_{j=1}^p \omega_j^1 \delta_{x_j^1}$ with $\omega_j^1>0$ and $x_j^1\in\mathcal{X}$. 
Moreover, we suppose that there exists disjoint regions $\{\mathcal{X}_j\}_{j=1}^p \subset \mathcal{X}$ such that for all $j=1,\ldots,p$, $x_j^1 \in \mathcal{X}_j$ and $\sup_{\substack{x \in \mathcal{X}_j \,, \\ j=1,\ldots,p}} \mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_j^1,x) \leq r_{\mu_\omega^1,\mathcal{X}}$ for a radius $r_{\mu_\omega^1,\mathcal{X}}>0$. We write $\mathcal{X}_0\mathrel{\resizebox{\widthof{$\mathord{=}$}}{\height}{ $\!\!\resizebox{1.2\width}{0.8\height}{\raisebox{0.23ex}{$\mathop{:}$}}\!\!=\!\!$ }} \mathcal{X} \setminus \left(\cup_{j=1}^p \mathcal{X}_j \right)$.
\\\underline{Choice of location-scale update:} We choose $\mathcal{T}$ and $\beta$ such that for $\mu_\omega^k = \sum_{j=1}^p \omega_j^k \delta_{x_j^k} \in \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X})^+$, $x_j^{k+1}=\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(x_j^k,J_{\mu_\omega^k}')\in \mathcal{X}$ and $\mathfrak{d}_\mathfrak{g}(x_j^k,x_j^{k+1}) \leq C_{\mathcal{T}} \beta$. 
\\\underline{Control of the Cesàro average:}
Let $K \geq 2$. Let $\kappa \leq 1$.
If $\alpha  = \frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}$ and $0 \leq \beta \leq \frac{1}{K^{3/2}C_{\mathcal{T}}}$, then
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:weight_convergence}
\begin{equation}
    J_W\left(\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \mu_\omega^k\right) - J_W(\mu_\omega^\star)
    \lesssim_{\left\lVert\mu_\omega^\star\right\rVert_{\rm TV}, \left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}, \mathcal{X}, \left\lVert\mu_\omega^1\right\rVert_{\rm TV}} \frac{H_{\mu_\omega^1,\mu_\omega^\star} + 1}{\sqrt{K}} + r_{\mu_\omega^1,\mathcal{X}} + \mu_\omega^\star(\mathcal{X}_0)
\label{eq:borne1}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
   H_{\mu_\omega^1,\mu_\omega^\star} = \sum_{\substack{j\in \{1,\ldots, p\} \,, \\ \mu_\omega^\star(\mathcal{X}_j)>0}}\left( -\ln\left(\frac{\omega_j^1}{\mu_\omega^\star(\mathcal{X}_j)} \right)\mu_\omega^\star(\mathcal{X}_j) - \mu_\omega^\star(\mathcal{X}_j) \right) + \left\lVert\mu_\omega^1\right\rVert_{\rm TV} \,. \label{eq:def_H_mu1_mu_star}
\end{equation}
\end{subequations}

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
In Proposition \ref{prop:weight_convergence}, the step size $\beta$ can be taken equal to zero without affecting the bound on $J_W\left(\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \mu_\omega^k\right) - J_W(\mu_\omega^\star)$. The choice of location–scale updates is likewise largely immaterial, as it only needs to satisfy a very mild condition. In particular, one may simply use the trivial update $\mathcal{T}_\beta(x_j^k,J_{\mu_\omega^k}')=x_j^k$. 
This reflects the fact that this convergence analysis depends solely on the behavior of the weights, and not on the evolution of the location–scale parameters.
Consequently, the initialization must place sufficient mass near the support of the solution $\mu_\omega^\star$. More precisely, it requires that there exists $\mathcal{X}_0 \subset \mathcal{X}$ such that $\mu_\omega^\star(\mathcal{X}_0)$ is small, and for every $x\in \operatorname{supp}(\mu_\omega^\star) \setminus \mathcal{X}_0$, $\min_{j=1,\ldots,p} \mathfrak{d}_\mathfrak{g}(x,x_j^1)$ is small.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{remark:construct_init_grid}
The initialization $\mu_\omega^1$ can be specifically designed to control the terms $H_{\mu_\omega^1,\mu_\omega^\star}$, $r_{\mu_\omega^1,\mathcal{X}}$ and $\mu_\omega^\star(\mathcal{X}_0)$ appearing in Proposition \ref{prop:weight_convergence}.
Setting $\mu_\omega^1=\sum_{j=1}^p \frac{1}{p} \delta_{x_j^1}$ where the points $x_j^1$ form a grid and the regions $\mathcal{X}_j$ correspond to the associated cells, then we have $\mu_\omega^\star(\mathcal{X}_0)=0$ and we can expect $r_{\mu_\omega^1,\mathcal{X}}$ to be of order $p^{-1/(2d)}$ (omitting the dependence on the total volume of $\mathcal{X}$). Moreover, recalling \eqref{eq:def_H_mu1_mu_star}, with this choice of $\mu_\omega^1$,
\begin{align*}
H_{\mu_\omega^1,\mu_\omega^\star}
&\leq \max\left\{\left\lVert\mu_\omega^\star\right\rVert_{\rm TV} \ln\left(\frac{\left\lVert\mu_\omega^\star\right\rVert_{\rm TV}}{\min_{j} \omega_j^1}\right) \,, \, 0\right\} - \left\lVert\mu_\omega^\star\right\rVert_{\rm TV} +\mu_\omega^\star(\mathcal{X}_0) + \left\lVert\mu_\omega^1\right\rVert_{\rm TV} \,, \\
&\leq \left\lVert\mu_\omega^\star\right\rVert_{\rm TV} \left( \max\left\{\ln(p \left\lVert\mu_\omega^\star\right\rVert_{\rm TV})\,, \, 0\right\}-1\right)+ 1 \,.
\end{align*}
In particular, choosing $p=\left\lceil K^d \right\rceil$, we get $H_{\mu_\omega^1,\mu_\omega^\star} \lesssim_{\left\lVert\mu_\omega^\star\right\rVert_{\rm TV}} d\ln(K)$ and
\[
    J_W\left(\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \mu_\omega^k\right) - J_W(\mu_\omega^\star)
    \lesssim_{\left\lVert\mu_\omega^\star\right\rVert_{\rm TV}, d, \left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}, \mathcal{X}} \frac{\ln(K)}{\sqrt{K}} \,.
\]
Note that with our choice of initialization, $\left\lVert\mu_\omega^1\right\rVert_{\rm TV}=1$ does not depend on $p$.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
Working on a compact set ensures the existence of a solution to the BLASSO. Moreover, in the absence of prior information on $\mu_\omega^\star$, restricting the problem to a compact set is necessary in order to construct the initialization as described in Remark \ref{remark:construct_init_grid}.

In addition, we used in the proof the explicit form of $K_{\rm norm}$ and in particular the fact that $\inf_{x,x' \in \mathcal{X}} K_{\rm norm}\mathrel{\resizebox{\widthof{$\mathord{=}$}}{\height}{ $\!\!=\!\!\resizebox{1.2\width}{0.8\height}{\raisebox{0.23ex}{$\mathop{:}$}}\!\!$ }} C_{\mathcal{X}}>0$ since $\mathcal{X}$ is compact. This control allows to establish a bound on $\left\lVert\mu_\omega^k\right\rVert_{\rm TV}$. An alternative approach, which does not rely on the positivity of $C_{\mathcal{X}}$, can still yield a bound on $\left\lVert\mu_\omega^k\right\rVert_{\rm TV}$, but this bound then depends on $p$ (see Remark \ref{remark:control_tv_iterates_wo_C_X} in appendix). In particular, without prior information on $\mu_\omega^\star$, one cannot expect convergence as $p$ grows, since the bound on $J_W\left(\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \mu_\omega^k\right) - J_W(\mu_\omega^\star)$ then involves the term $p r_{\mu_\omega^1,\mathcal{X}}$, which may scale as $p^{1-\frac{1}{(2d)}}$ when constructing $\mu_\omega^1$ on a grid.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:norm_covariant_derivatives}
Let $\psi \in \mathcal{C}^2(\mathbb{R}^d \times [u_{\min}, +\infty)^d)$. Let $x,x' \in \mathbb{R}^d \times [u_{\min}, +\infty)^d$.
\\\underline{Covariant derivatives:} Let $v,v' \in \mathbb{R}^{2d}$.
We define \[D_0[\psi](x)=\psi(x)\,, \quad D_1[\psi](x)[v]=v^T \nabla \psi(x)=\left\langle v, \nabla^{\mathfrak{g}} \psi(x) \right\rangle_x\,, \quad D_2[\psi](x)[v,v'] = v^T H^{\mathfrak{g}} \psi(x) v'\,.\]
\underline{Operator norms:} For $j\in\{0,1,2\}$, we define the operator norm \[\left\lVertD_j[\psi](x)\right\rVert_x \mathrel{\resizebox{\widthof{$\mathord{=}$}}{\height}{ $\!\!\resizebox{1.2\width}{0.8\height}{\raisebox{0.23ex}{$\mathop{:}$}}\!\!=\!\!$ }} \sup_{\substack{V=[v_1,\ldots,v_j]\in (\mathbb{R}^{2d})^j \\ \forall l=1,\ldots,j\,, \: \left\lVertv_l\right\rVert_x \leq 1}} D_j[\psi](x)[V]\,.\]
\underline{Kernel derivatives and associated operators:} Let $i,j\in\{0,1,2\}$.
We define the covariant derivative of the kernel of order $i$ with respect to the first variable $x$ and of order $j$ with respect to the second variable $x'$ by \[[Q]K_{\rm norm}^{(ij)}(x,x')[V]=\left\langle D_i[\Psi](x)[Q], D_j[\Psi](x')[V] \right\rangle_\mathbb{L} \quad \forall\, Q \in (\mathbb{R}^{2d})^i\,, V \in (\mathbb{R}^{2d})^j\,.\]
The associated operator norm is \[\left\lVertK_{\rm norm}^{(ij)}(x,x')\right\rVert_{x,x'}\mathrel{\resizebox{\widthof{$\mathord{=}$}}{\height}{ $\!\!\resizebox{1.2\width}{0.8\height}{\raisebox{0.23ex}{$\mathop{:}$}}\!\!=\!\!$ }} \sup_{\substack{Q=[Q_1,\ldots,Q_i]\in (\mathbb{R}^{2d})^i\,,\,V=[V_1,\ldots,V_j]\in (\mathbb{R}^{2d})^j\\ \forall l\, \left\lVertQ_l\right\rVert_x\,, \, \left\lVertV_l\right\rVert_{x'} \leq 1}} [Q]K_{\rm norm}^{(ij)}(x,x')[V] \,.\]

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The bounds $B_{00}, B_{10},  B_{11},  B_{02},  B_{12}$ come from the global controls of the covariant derivatives given in \citep[Lemma J.2]{article_giard_decastro_marteau}. The bound $B_{22}$ can be found in the proof of \citep[Lemma K.1]{article_giard_decastro_marteau}.
To derive the global bounds $B_{02}^e$ and $B_{33}^e$, we follow the strategy used in the proof of \citep[Lemma~J.2]{article_giard_decastro_marteau}.
\\\underline{$B_{02}^e$:} We refer to \texttt{cpgd\_sympy.ipynb} in \citep{repo_zenodo_cpgd} for the formal computations used to derive the bounds presented here. 

Using that the metric tensor $\mathfrak{g}_x$ is diagonal, we have
    \begin{align*}
        \sup_{x,x'\in \mathbb{R}^d \times [u_{\min},+\infty)^d]} \left\lVert\mathfrak{g}_x^{-1/2} \nabla_1^2  K_{\rm norm} (x,x') \mathfrak{g}_{x'}^{-1/2}\right\rVert_2 &\leq 2d \sup_{b_k,b_l',x,x'\in \mathbb{R}^d \times [u_{\min},+\infty)^d]} \mathfrak{g}_{b_k b_k}^{-1/2} \mathfrak{g}_{b_l b_l}^{-1/2}|\partial_{b_k b_l}  K_{\rm norm} (x,x') |\,.
    \end{align*}
If $b_k\in \{t_k,u_k\}$ and $b_l\in \{t_l,u_l\}$ with $k\neq l$ (\emph{i.e}\futurelet\@let@token\ifx\@let@token.\else.\null\fi\xspace the derivatives are not taken in the same dimension), then 
\begin{align*}
\mathfrak{g}_{b_k b_k}^{-1/2} \mathfrak{g}_{b_l b_l}^{-1/2}|\partial_{b_k b_l}  K_{\rm norm} (x,x') | &\leq \mathfrak{g}_{b_k b_k}^{-1/2} |\partial_{b_k}  K_{\rm norm} (x_k,x_k) | \mathfrak{g}_{b_l b_l}^{-1/2}|\partial_{b_l}  K_{\rm norm} (x_l,x_l) | \,, \\
&\leq 1
\end{align*}   
as shown in the proof of \citep[Lemma J.2]{article_giard_decastro_marteau} when deriving $B_{10}$. If $b_k=b_l=t_k$, 
\begin{align*}
\mathfrak{g}_{t_k t_k}^{-1}|\partial_{t_k t_k}  K_{\rm norm} (x,x') | &\leq \mathfrak{g}_{t_k t_k}^{-1}\sqrt{\partial_{t_k t_k}\partial_{t_k' t_k'}  K_{\rm norm} (x_k,x_k)} \,, \\
&= \sqrt{3} \,.
\end{align*}

\label{lemma:taylor_expansions_kernel}
    Let $x,x' \in \mathbb{R}^d \times [u_{\min},+\infty)^d$.
    We have \[\left\lVert\Psi(\delta_{x} - \delta_{x'})\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}^2 \leq B_{02} \mathfrak{d}_\mathfrak{g}(x,x')^2\,.\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Remark that $K_{\rm norm}(x,x) = K_{\rm norm}(x',x') = 1$ and that $\nabla_2 K_{\rm norm}(x,x) = 0$. Writing $\gamma_{x \to x'}$ the Fisher-Rao geodesic such that $\gamma_{x \to x'}(0)=x$, $\gamma_{x \to x'}(1)=x'$, it comes that
  \begin{align*}
      \left\lVert\Psi(\delta_{x} - \delta_{x'})\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}^2 &= 2K_{\rm norm}(x,x)-2K_{\rm norm}(x,x')\,, \\
      &= -2\dot \gamma_{x \to x'}(0)^T \nabla_2 K_{\rm norm}(x,x) - 2\int_0^1 (1-y)\dot \gamma_{x \to x'}(y)^T H_2^\mathfrak{g} K_{\rm norm}(x,\gamma_{x \to x'}(y)) \dot \gamma_{x \to x'}(y) \, \mathrm{d} y \,, \\
      &= -2\int_0^1 (1-y)\dot \gamma_{x \to x'}(y)^T H_2^\mathfrak{g} K_{\rm norm}(x,\gamma_{x \to x'}(y)) \dot \gamma_{x \to x'}(y) \, \mathrm{d} y \,.
  \end{align*}
  From the control on the Riemannian Hessian (Lemma \ref{lemma:controls_kernel}), and as $\gamma_{x\to x'}$ stays in $\mathbb{R}^d \times [u_{\min},+\infty)^d$ (Section \ref{section:geodesics_and_distances}), we have \[\left|\dot \gamma_{x \to x'}(y)^T H_2^\mathfrak{g} K_{\rm norm}(x,\gamma_{x \to x'}(y)) \dot \gamma_{x \to x'}(y) \right| \leq B_{02} \mathfrak{d}_\mathfrak{g}(x,x')^2\,.\]
  So $\left\lVert\Psi(\delta_{x} - \delta_{x'})\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}^2 \leq B_{02} \mathfrak{d}_\mathfrak{g}(x,x')^2.$

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lemma:control_tv_iterates}
Let $\mathcal{X}$ be a compact subset of $\mathbb{R}^d \times [u_{\min},+\infty)^d$ and $C_\mathcal{X} \mathrel{\resizebox{\widthof{$\mathord{=}$}}{\height}{ $\!\!\resizebox{1.2\width}{0.8\height}{\raisebox{0.23ex}{$\mathop{:}$}}\!\!=\!\!$ }} \inf_{x,x' \in \mathcal{X}} K_{\rm norm}(x,x')$. Let $\mu_\omega^1=\sum_{j=1} \omega_j^1 \delta_{x_j^1}$ be the initialization of CPGD, with $\omega_j^1 \geq 0$ for $j=1,\ldots,p$. Let $\mu_\omega^k$ be the kth iterate of the algorithm, with weights updated via $\omega_j^{k+1} = \omega_j^k e^{-\alpha J_{\mu_\omega^k}'(x_j^k)}$, and location-scale parameters verifying $x_j^k \in \mathcal{X}$ for all $j=1,\ldots,p$ and $k\geq 1$.
Then $\mu_\omega^k$ is a nonnegative measure. Moreover, for all $k\geq 1$, \[\left\lVert\mu_\omega^k\right\rVert_{\rm TV} \leq \max\left\{\frac{1}{C_{\mathcal{X}}}(\left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L} - \kappa) e^{\alpha(\left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}-\kappa)} \,, \, \left\lVert\mu_\omega^1\right\rVert_{\rm TV} \right\} \leq \max\left\{\frac{1}{C_{\mathcal{X}}}\left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L} e^{\alpha \left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}} \,, \, \left\lVert\mu_\omega^1\right\rVert_{\rm TV} \right\} \mathrel{\resizebox{\widthof{$\mathord{=}$}}{\height}{ $\!\!=\!\!\resizebox{1.2\width}{0.8\height}{\raisebox{0.23ex}{$\mathop{:}$}}\!\!$ }} B_{\rm TV} \,.\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Note that as our kernel is positive ($\left\langle \Psi \delta_x, \Psi \delta_{x'} \right\rangle> 0$ for $x,x' \in \mathbb{R}^d \times (0,+\infty)^d$) and as $\mathcal{X}$ is compact, $C_{\mathcal{X}}>0$. As $\omega_j^k\geq 0$ for all $j=1,\ldots,p$, it comes that, for $x \in \mathcal{X}$, 
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:Jp_bound_CX}
       J_{\mu_\omega^k}'(x)=\left\langle \Psi \delta_x, \sum_{j=1}^p \omega_j^k \Psi \delta_{x_j^k} - y \right\rangle_\mathbb{L} + \kappa \geq C_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lVert\mu_\omega^k\right\rVert_{\rm TV} - \left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L} + \kappa\,.
   \end{equation}
\\\underline{Case $\left\lVert\mu_\omega^k\right\rVert_{\rm TV} \geq \frac{\left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}-\kappa}{C_{\mathcal{X}}}$:} From \eqref{eq:Jp_bound_CX}, as we assumed that $x_j^k\in\mathcal{X}$, it comes that for all $j=1,\ldots,p$, $J_{\mu_\omega^k}'(x_j^k)\geq 0$. Hence $\omega_j^{k+1}= \omega_j^k e^{-\alpha J_{\mu_\omega^k}'(x_j^k)} \leq \omega_j^k$ and $\left\lVert\mu_\omega^{k+1}\right\rVert_{\rm TV}\leq \left\lVert\mu_\omega^k\right\rVert_{\rm TV}$.
\\\underline{Case $\left\lVert\mu_\omega^k\right\rVert_{\rm TV} \leq \frac{\left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}-\kappa}{C_{\mathcal{X}}}$:} Then $\left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}-\kappa\geq 0$ (because $\left\lVert\mu_\omega^k\right\rVert_{\rm TV}\geq 0$) and from \eqref{eq:Jp_bound_CX} we get $-J_{\mu_\omega^k}'(x_j^k) \leq \left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}-\kappa$, leading to
$\omega_j^{k+1}\leq \omega_j^k e^{\alpha (\left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}-\kappa)}$. It comes that $\left\lVert\mu_\omega^{k+1}\right\rVert_{\rm TV} \leq \frac{1}{C_\mathcal{X}}(\left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L} - \kappa) e^{\alpha(\left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}-\kappa)}$. 

We showed that for all $k\geq 1$, \[\left\lVert\mu_\omega^k\right\rVert_{\rm TV} \leq \max\left\{\frac{1}{C_\mathcal{X}}(\left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L} - \kappa) e^{\alpha(\left\lVerty\right\rVert_\mathbb{L}-\kappa)} \,, \, \left\lVert\mu_\omega^1\right\rVert_{\rm TV}\right\}\,.\]

\begin{align*}
    \eta^\star(x) &= \eta^\star(x_j^\star) + \dot\gamma_{x_j^\star \to x}(0)^T \nabla\eta^\star(x_j^\star) + \int_0^1 (1-y) \dot\gamma_{x_j^\star \to x}(y)^T  H^\mathfrak{g} \eta^\star(\gamma_{x_j^\star \to x}(y)) \dot\gamma_{x_j^\star \to x}(y) \, \mathrm{d} y \,, \\
    &= 1 + \int_0^1 (1-y) \dot\gamma_{x_j^\star \to x}(y)^T  H^\mathfrak{g} \eta^\star(\gamma_{x_j^\star \to x}(y)) \dot\gamma_{x_j^\star \to x}(y) \, \mathrm{d} y \,, \\
    &\leq 1- \frac{\varepsilon_2}{4}\mathfrak{d}_\mathfrak{g}(x,x_j^\star)^2 \,.
\end{align*}

The last item can be deduced using the previous ones, along with Lemma \ref{lemma:bound_semidist_fisher_rao}, taking $r_{\max} \leq \frac{0.3025}{\sqrt{d}}$. For $x\in \mathcal{X}^{\rm far}(r_{\max})$, $\eta^\star(x)\leq 1-\varepsilon_0(r_{\max})$. For $x\in \mathcal{X}^{\rm far}(r)\setminus \mathcal{X}^{\rm far}(r_{\max})$, there exists $j\in \{1,\ldots p^\star\}$ such that $x\in \mathcal{X}_j^{\rm near}(r_{\max})$. So as $\mathpzc{d}(x,x_j^\star)>r$, \[\eta^\star(x)\leq 1- \frac{\varepsilon_2}{4} \mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,x_j^\star)^2 \leq 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_2}{4\bar \varepsilon_3}\mathpzc{d}(x,x_j^\star)^2 \leq 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_2}{ 4\bar\varepsilon_3} r^2\,.\]

\label{remark:dependence_kappa_exp_convergence}
To obtain the lower bound for $G_\mu$, we used several times the inequality $(a+b)^2 \geq \frac{1}{2}a^2 - b^2$. A parameterized version of this inequality holds: for $\delta\in (0,1)$, $(a+b)^2 \geq (1 - \delta)a^2 - \frac{1-\delta}{\delta} b^2$. By optimizing $\delta$ where we use this inequality, we can improve both the condition on the initialization and the convergence rate. Specifically, this allows one to reduce the exponent associated with $\kappa$ in Equations \eqref{eq:condition_initialisation} and \eqref{eq:rate_convergence}.
\\We do not present this approach here in order to keep the exposition simple, since it would require choosing $\delta$ based on results given later in the proof.
