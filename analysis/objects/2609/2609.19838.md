<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
We stress that Algorithm 1 is not completely symmetric with respect to the four candidates. Since the entropy $\@fontswitch\relax\mathcal S$ depends only on 
$\rho$ and $p$, the minimization determines only $\widehat\rho_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}$ and $\widehat p_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}$. If ${\@fontswitch\relax\mathcal S}_1$ or ${\@fontswitch\relax\mathcal S}_2$ is the 
minimum, the selected density and pressure are taken from the same cell, and we therefore use the velocities from that cell. If ${\@fontswitch\relax\mathcal S}_3$ 
or ${\@fontswitch\relax\mathcal S}_4$ is the minimum, the selected density and pressure are taken from different cells, and hence there are no corresponding 
velocity values and we use the arithmetic averages of each of the velocity components.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
We have shown several examples, which illustrate a potential advantage of the ELCD procedure. We have tested many additional examples, both 
one- and two-dimensional ones. In all of the performed numerical experiments, the use of the ELCD-based reconstructions led to either 
(slightly) better results or to the results of the same quantity, which is achieved by the LCD based on the arithmetic averages of the 
primitive variables.
