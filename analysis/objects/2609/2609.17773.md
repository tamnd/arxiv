<!-- problem-1: body_md -->
\label{problem:odd-cycle}
    What is the smallest $L(k)$ such that every $k$-edge-coloring of the complete graph $K_{2^k+1}$ has a monochromatic odd cycle of length at most $L(k)$?
<!-- end problem-1 -->
<!-- problem-2: body_md -->
What is the smallest $L_r(k)$ such that every $k$-edge-coloring of the complete graph $K_{r^k+1}$ has a monochromatic subgraph $H$ of diameter at most $L_r(k)$ with chromatic number $\chi(H) \geqslant r+1$?
<!-- end problem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:ub}
 For all integers $r\geqslant 2$ and $k\geqslant 1$, we have
 \[L_r(k)= O\left(k^{3/2}r^{k/2}\log r\right).\]
 In particular, for fixed $r$, we have $L_r(k)= O_r\left(k^{3/2}r^{k/2}\right)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:ub-gen}
 Let $r\geqslant 2$ and $k\geqslant 1$ be integers, and let $0<\delta \leqslant 1$ such that $n=(1+\delta)r^k$ is an integer. Then in every $k$-edge-coloring of $K_n$ there is a monochromatic subgraph with chromatic number at least $r+1$ and diameter $O(k^{3/2}\delta^{-1/2}\log r)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:lb}
    For all integers $r\geqslant 2$ and $k\geqslant 1$, we have
    \[L_r(k)\geqslant 2^{\Omega(\sqrt{\log k})}.\]
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
For a graph $G$, an \emph{orthonormal representation} $X$ is a set of unit vectors $\{x_v\}_{v\in V(G)}$ in a Euclidean space such that $x_u\cdot x_v = 0$ whenever $u\ne v\in V(G)$ and $uv\not\in E(G)$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
For a graph $G$, the Lov\'asz theta function $\vartheta(G)$ is defined as 
    \[\vartheta(G) := \min_{c,X}\max_{v\in V(G)}(c\cdot x_v)^{-2}\]
    where the minimum is over all unit vectors $c\in \mathbb{R}^{V(G)}$ and orthonormal representations $X$ of $G$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:spectral-theta}
    For a graph $G$, we have $\vartheta(\overline{G})=\max_{M\in\mathcal M(G)}\lambda_1(M)$, where $\overline{G}$ denotes the complement of $G$, and $\lambda_1(M)$ is the largest eigenvalue of $M$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:theta-complete-graph}
    For a complete graph $K_n$ on $n$ vertices, we have $\vartheta(\overline{K}_n) = n$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:submultiplicative}
    For graphs $G_1, G_2$ on the vertex set $[n]$, let $G_1\cup G_2$ denote the graph on vertex set $[n]$ with edge set $E(G_1)\cup E(G_2)$. Then we have $\vartheta(\overline{G_1\cup G_2})\leqslant \vartheta(\overline{G_1})\cdot \vartheta(\overline{G_2})$. Consequently, if $G_1,\dots,G_k$ are graphs on the same vertex set $[n]$, and  $E(G_1)\cup\cdots\cup E(G_k) = E(K_n)$, then $\prod_{i=1}^k \vartheta(\overline{G_i})\geqslant n$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:local-r}
Let $H$ be an induced subgraph of $G$. If $\chi(H)\leqslant r$, then for each $M\in\mathcal M(G)$, we have $\lambda_1(M[H])\leqslant r$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
\cref{lem:spectral-theta} yields $\lambda_1(M[H])\leqslant \vartheta(\overline{H})$, so this result follows immediately from the well-known fact that $\vartheta(\overline{H})\leqslant \chi(H)$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:poly-local}
For a graph $G$, let $M\in\mathcal M(G)$ and let $\rho\geqslant 0$ be an integer. For $v\in V(G)$, let $B_v=B_G(v,\rho)$.
If $p$ is a polynomial of degree at most $\rho$, then we have
\[p(M)e_v=p(M[B_v])e_v,\]
where the right-hand side is extended by zero outside $B_v$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Since $M_{uw}=0$ unless $u=w$ or $uw\in E(G)$, for each positive integer $s$, the vector $M^s e_v$ is supported on the coordinates corresponding to vertices at graph distance at most $s$ from $v$. Thus, $(M^s)_{wv}$ is a weighted sum over length-$s$ walks from $v$ to $w$, and therefore $(M^s)_{wv}=0$ if $\operatorname{dist}_G(v,w)>s$.

If $s\leqslant \rho$, then every walk of length $s$ starting at $v$ remains inside $B_G(v,\rho)$.  Hence we have $M^s e_v=M[B_v]^s e_v$ for every $0\leqslant s\leqslant \rho$. The result follows by linearity for every polynomial of degree at most $\rho$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:trace}
Let $G$ be a graph on $n$ vertices and let $M\in \mathcal M(G)$. Let $p$ be a polynomial of degree at most $\rho$ such that $|p(x)|\leqslant 1$ for all $x\in [0,r]$. Suppose the ball of radius $\rho$ around $v$ for every $v\in V(G)$ is $r$-colorable in $G$. Then we have
\[ \operatorname{tr}\bigl(p(M)^2\bigr)\leqslant n.\]
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Fix $v\in V(G)$ and let $B_v=B_G(v,\rho)$ for convenience. By assumption, $B_v$ is $r$-colorable. Thus by \cref{lem:local-r}, we have $\lambda_1(M[B_v])\leqslant r$.
Furthermore, since $M$ is positive semidefinite, so is $M[B_v]$, so every eigenvalue of $M[B_v]$ lies in $[0,r]$.  Since $|p(x)|\leqslant 1$ for all $x\in [0,r]$, we claim that \[\|p(M[B_v])\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leqslant 1.\] 
Indeed, let $\lambda_1,\dots, \lambda_m\in [0,r]$ be the eigenvalues of $M[B_v]$. Then $p(\lambda_1),\dots, p(\lambda_m)\in [-1,1]$ are the eigenvalues of $p(M[B_v])$. So we have $\|p(M[B_v])\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \max_{i\in [m]} |p(\lambda_i)|\leqslant 1$.
By \cref{lem:poly-local}, we have $p(M)e_v=p(M[B_v])e_v$, and thus $\|p(M)e_v\|_2\leqslant 1$.
Summing over all the vertices, we obtain
\[\operatorname{tr}(p(M)^2)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}e_v^Tp(M)^2e_v=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\|p(M)e_v\|_2^2\leqslant n.\qedhere\]
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:cheb}
Let $G$ be a graph on $n$ vertices. Suppose every ball of radius $\rho$ in $G$ is $r$-colorable. Then we have
\[
\vartheta(\overline{G})\leqslant r + \frac{r}{4}((2\sqrt{n})^{1/\rho}-1)^2.
\]
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Choose
\[p(x)=T_\rho\left(\frac{2x}{r}-1\right).\]
Then $p$ has degree $\rho$. Furthermore, for $x\in[0,r]$, we have $|p(x)|\leqslant 1$, since $\frac{2x}{r}-1\in[-1,1]$.
By \cref{lem:trace}, for every $M\in\mathcal M(G)$ we have
\[\operatorname{tr}\bigl(p(M)^2\bigr)\leqslant n.\]

By \cref{lem:spectral-theta}, we can choose $M\in\mathcal M(G)$ such that $\lambda_1(M)=\vartheta(\overline{G})$. For convenience, let $\lambda=\lambda_1(M)$. If $\lambda\leqslant r$, there is nothing to prove.  Otherwise, suppose $\lambda=r+\eta$ for $\eta > 0$. Then $p(M)$ has an eigenvalue
\[p(r+\eta)=T_\rho\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{r}\right).\]
Hence we must have
\[
T_\rho\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{r}\right)^2\leqslant\operatorname{tr}\bigl(p(M)^2\bigr)\leqslant n.\]
Thus $T_\rho\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{r}\right)\leqslant\sqrt{n}$.
For $x\geqslant 1$, we have 
\[T_\rho(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(x+\sqrt{x^2-1}\right)^\rho+\left(x-\sqrt{x^2-1}\right)^\rho\right)\geqslant \frac{1}{2}\left(x+\sqrt{x^2-1}\right)^\rho.\]
Substituting $x=1+\frac{2\eta}{r}$ gives
\[1+2\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{r}} \leqslant 1+\frac{2\eta}{r}+2\sqrt{(\frac{\eta}{r})^2+\frac{\eta}{r}}\leqslant \left(2T_\rho\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{r}\right)\right)^{1/\rho} \leqslant(2\sqrt{n})^{1/\rho}.\]
Further rearranging yields
\[
\eta \leqslant \frac{r}{4}((2\sqrt{n})^{1/\rho}-1)^2,
\]
as desired.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $N=(1+\delta)r^k$ for convenience. Fix a $k$-edge-coloring of $K_N$ with color classes $G_i$ for $i\in \{1,\dots,k\}$, so $K_N=G_1\cup\cdots\cup G_k$. By \cref{lem:submultiplicative}, we have $\prod_{i=1}^k\vartheta(\overline{G_i})\geqslant N$. Then for some color $j$, we have
\[
\vartheta(\overline{G}_j)\geqslant N^{1/k}.
\]
Without loss of generality, assume $j=1$. Let $\rho\geqslant 0$ be the largest integer such that every ball of radius $\rho$ in $G_1$ is $r$-colorable. Note that $\rho$ is finite because otherwise, in the limit as $\rho\to \infty$, \cref{lem:cheb} yields $\vartheta(\overline{G})\leqslant r<N^{1/k}$, a contradiction. If $\rho=0$, then $G_1$ contains a subgraph of diameter at most $2$ that is not $r$-colorable, and we are done, so assume $\rho>0$. By \cref{lem:cheb}, we have
\[
r+\frac{r}{4}((2\sqrt{N})^{1/\rho}-1)^2 \geqslant N^{1/k}=r(1+\delta)^{1/k}.
\]
Since $\delta \leqslant 1$, we have $r(1+\delta)^{1/k}\geqslant r \exp(\delta/(2k))$. Then we have
\[
\exp(\delta/(2k))\leqslant 1+\frac{1}{4}((2\sqrt{N})^{1/\rho}-1)^2\leqslant \exp(\frac{1}{4}((2\sqrt{N})^{1/\rho}-1)^2).
\]
Rearranging then yields
\[
1+\sqrt{\frac{2\delta}{k}}\leqslant (2\sqrt{N})^{1/\rho},
\]
so
\[
\rho \leqslant \frac{\log (2\sqrt{N})}{\log (1+\sqrt{\frac{2\delta}{k}})}\leqslant \frac{\frac{1}{2} k\log r+O(1)}{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\delta}{k}}}=O(k^{3/2}\delta^{-1/2}\log r).
\]
Then by our definition of $\rho$, taking a ball of radius $\rho+1$ yields a subgraph $H$ of $G_1$ with $\chi(H)\geqslant r+1$ and  $\operatorname{diam}(H)\leqslant 2\rho+2$.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
We define a \emph{bracelet graph} $B_{r,n}$ via the following construction. Starting with a cycle $C_{2n+1}$ with vertices $v_0,\dots,v_{2n}$ in order, we blow up each of the vertices $v_{2i-1}$, where $1\leqslant i\leqslant n$, into a copy of $K_{r-1}$. Concretely, $B_{r,n}$ consists of an alternating sequence of $n+1$ single vertices (the ``little beads'') and $n$ $(r-1)$-cliques (the ``big beads''), such that each of the $(r-1)$-cliques is complete to the single vertices on both sides, and the two vertices on the ends are adjacent to each other.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:induct}
    If $K_{r^k+1}$ admits an $(r;n_1,\dots,n_k)$-RBD, then $K_{r^{k+1}+1}$ admits an $(r;n_1+1,n_2,\dots,n_k,2n_1)$-RBD.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Given an $k$-edge-coloring $c_k$ of $K_{r^k+1}$ that exhibits the $(r;n_1,\dots,n_k)$-RBD, we construct an $(k+1)$-edge-coloring $c_k'$ of $K_{r^{k+1}+1}$ as follows. Let $V(K_{r^k+1})=\{O\}\cup U$, where $O$ is the root of the relevant RBD and $U$ consists of the other $r^k$ vertices. We identify $V(K_{r^{k+1}+1})$ with $\{O\}\cup U_1\cup \cdots \cup U_r$, where each $U_i=\{x^{(i)}:\: x\in U\}$ is a copy of $U$. For all $x,y\in U$ with $x\neq y$, let $c_k'(x^{(i)},y^{(j)})=c_k(x,y)$ for all $i,j\in [r]$. Likewise, let $c_k'(O,x^{(i)})=c_k(O,x)$ for $x\in U$. Finally, let $c_k'(x,x')$ be the new $(k+1)$-st color for all $x\in U$.

The new color, meanwhile, is $r$-partite. We now modify $c_k'$ into a new coloring $c_{k+1}$ that yields the desired $(r;n_1+1,n_2,\dots,n_k,2n_1)$-RBD, as follows. For convenience, call the first color blue, and call the new $(k+1)$-st color red. Fix a vertex partition that exhibits the fact that the blue graph $G_1$ is a (subgraph of a) rooted blowup of $B_{r,n_1}$, i.e. fix an edge-preserving map $\varphi: G_1 \to B_{r,n_1}$ that sends $O$, and no other vertex, to $w_0$; the parts of the partition are the preimages of each vertex of $B_{r,n_1}$. Now for $x,y\in U$ with $x\neq y$ such that the edge $xy$ is blue in $c_k$, and for $i,j\in [r]$, recolor the edge $x^{(i)}y^{(j)}$ from blue to red in $c_{k+1}$ if and only if one of the following is true:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $i\neq j$, and for some $m\in [n_1]$ and $a,b\in [r-1]$, we have $\varphi(x)=w_{(m-1)r+a}, \varphi(y)=w_{(m-1)r+b}$ (i.e. $\varphi(x),\varphi(y)$ are part of the same big bead $K_{r-1}$ in $B_{r,n_1}$).
        \item $i=1$, $j>1$, and for some $m\in [n_1]$ and $a\in [r-1]$, we have $\varphi(x)=w_{mr}$ and $\varphi(y)=w_{(m-1)r+a}$ (i.e. $\varphi(x)$ is the little bead that comes after the big bead in $B_{r,n_1}$ that $\varphi(y)$ is part of).
        \item $i=1$, $j>1$, and for some $m\in [n_1]$ and $a\in [r-1]$, we have $\varphi(x)=w_{(m-1)r+a}$ and $\varphi(y)=w_{(m-1)r}$ (i.e. $\varphi(y)$ is the little bead that comes before the big bead in $B_{r,n_1}$ that $\varphi(x)$ is part of).
    \end{itemize}
    We also recolor the edges between $O$ and $x^{(i)}$ red when either $i=1$ and $\varphi(x)\in \{w_1,\dots,w_{r-1}\}$, or $i>1$ and $\varphi(x)=w_{rn_1}$. See \cref{fig:blowup}.



    Then by construction, the red graph is a subgraph of a blowup of $B_{r,2n_1}$, where the (big and little) beads in the blue color class of the first copy $U_1$ of $U$, as a (subgraph of a) blowup of $B_{r,n_1}$, form the little beads in the red graph (with $O$ serving as the last little bead), while the corresponding beads in the blue color classes of the other copies of $U$ combine together into the big beads in the red graph. Meanwhile, in the blue graph, we can merge all but the first copy of $U$ back together, and identify each vertex $w_{i}$ in $\varphi(U_1)$ with the vertex $w_{i+r}$ in $\varphi(U_j)$ for $2\leqslant j\leqslant r$, to see that the blue graph is a subgraph of a blowup of $B_{r,n_1+1}$. This gives the desired $(r;n_1+1,n_2,\dots,n_k,2n_1)$-RBD of $K_{r^{k+1}+1}$.

We inductively apply \cref{lem:induct}. Note that for $k=1$, $K_{r+1}$ trivially admits an $(r;1)$-RBD, since $B_{r,1}=K_{r+1}$. At each step $k\geqslant 1$, we sort the sequence $n_1\leqslant \cdots\leqslant n_k$ in increasing order and apply \cref{lem:induct} to extend the ``shortest'' color, which corresponds to $n_1$. 

When we iteratively apply the transformation given by \cref{lem:induct}, the sequence $(2n_1+1,\dots,2n_k+1)$ undergoes the same recursive transformation that the sequence $(r_1,\dots,r_k)$ defined in \cite{DayJohnson2017MulticolourRamseyOddCycles} undergoes in \cite[Lemma~5]{DayJohnson2017MulticolourRamseyOddCycles}. Thus, as reasoned in \cite[Corollary~6]{DayJohnson2017MulticolourRamseyOddCycles}, in $2^{O(t^2)}$ steps we can reach a mininum value $n_1$ of $>2^t$, meaning that in $k$ steps we can reach a minimum diameter of $2^{\Omega(\sqrt{\log k})}$ as desired.
