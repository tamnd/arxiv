<!-- defn-1: body_md -->
\thlabel{ADregular}
For a non-empty finite set $A \subseteq [N],\footnote{We use the standard shorthand notation $[N]=\{1,\ldots,N\}$.}$ we say $A$ is \emph{Ahlfors--David regular} with dimension $\alpha$ and regularity constant $M$ if, for every $a \in A$ and every $1 \leq R \leq N$,
\[\frac{1}{M}R^{\alpha}\leq \left\lvert {A \cap [a-R,a+R]} \right \rvert \leq M R^{\alpha}.\]
<!-- end defn-1 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\thlabel{main}
There exists a constant $c>0$ such that for every $N \geq 1$, and every Ahlfors--David regular set $A \subseteq [N]$ of dimension $0 \leq \alpha < 1$ and regularity constant $M \geq 2$,
\[\left\lvert {A+A} \right \rvert\geq \left\lvert {A} \right \rvert^{1+\frac{c\left(1-\alpha\right)}{\log M}}.\]
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- defn-2: body_md -->
Let $0 < \delta \leq 1$. A non-empty compact set $X \subseteq [0,1]$ is said to be $\alpha$-Ahlfors--David regular on scales $[\delta,1]$ with regularity constant $C$ if there is a finite Borel measure $\mu$ supported on $X$ such that
\[C^{-1}r^\alpha \leq \mu(B(x,r)) \leq C r^\alpha\]
for every $\delta \leq r \leq 1$, whenever $x \in X$.
<!-- end defn-2 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\thlabel{continuous}
There is a constant $c > 0$ such that, for any $\alpha$-Ahlfors--David regular set $X\subseteq[0,1]$ on scales $[\delta,1]$ with regularity constant $C \geq 2$ and dimension $0 < \alpha < 1$,
\[N_\delta(X+X)\geq \frac{1}{3}N_\delta(X)^{1+\frac{c\left(1-\alpha\right)}{\log C}}.\]
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\thlabel{good}
Let $0 < \alpha < 1$. Then for every $2 \leq R \leq N$, if $X$ is uniform on an $\left(\alpha,M\right)$-Ahlfors--David regular set $A \subseteq [N]$, then
\[\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert {A \cap I_R\left(X\right)} \right \rvert < \frac{R^{\alpha}}{40M}\right) \leq \frac{1}{10}.\]

Moreover, if $R = qr \leq N$ and $I \in \mathcal{I}_R$ is such that
\[\left\lvert {A \cap I} \right \rvert \geq \frac{R^{\alpha}}{40M},\]
then
\[\max_k \mathbb{P} \left(D_I = k\right) \leq \frac{40M^2}{q^{\alpha}}\]
and
\[\frac{q^{\alpha}}{40M^2} \leq \left\lvert {\operatorname{supp} D_I} \right \rvert \leq 8 M^2 q^{\alpha}.\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{E}=\{I=[nR,\left(n+1\right)R) \in \mathcal{I}_R : A \cap I \neq \emptyset, n \ \text{even}\}$, and define $\mathcal{O}$ similarly for the odd intervals. Without loss of generality, assume that $\left\lvert {\mathcal{E}} \right \rvert \geq \left\lvert {\mathcal{O}} \right \rvert$. For each interval $I \in \mathcal{E}$, choose one point $a_I \in A \cap I$. Then the intervals
    \[\left[a_I - \frac{R}{2},a_I + \frac{R}{2}\right]\]
    are pairwise disjoint, and we have 
    \[\left\lvert {A \cap\left[a_I - \frac{R}{2},a_I + \frac{R}{2}\right]} \right \rvert \geq \frac{1}{M}\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^{\alpha} \geq \frac{R^{\alpha}}{2M},\] 
    by Ahlfors--David regularity. Therefore $\mathcal{E}$ contains at most $2M \left\lvert {A} \right \rvert R^{-\alpha}$ non-empty intervals, and hence
    \[\left\lvert {\{I \in \mathcal{I}_R : A \cap I \neq \emptyset\}} \right \rvert \leq 4 M \left\lvert {A} \right \rvert R^{-\alpha}.\]

   Therefore the number of points which lie in some $I \in \mathcal{I}_R$ with
    \[\left\lvert {A \cap I} \right \rvert < \frac{R^{\alpha}}{40M}\]
    is at most
    \[\left(4 M \left\lvert {A} \right \rvert R^{-\alpha}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{R^{\alpha}}{40M}\right)=\frac{1}{10} \left\lvert {A} \right \rvert.\]
    Since $X$ is uniform on $A$, the first assertion follows.
    \smallbreak
    Now suppose that $R = qr \leq N$ and
    \[\left\lvert {A \cap I} \right \rvert \geq \frac{R^{\alpha}}{40M},\]
    and that $0 \leq k < q$ is such that $A \cap I_k \neq \emptyset$. Choosing some $x \in A \cap I_k$, we have $I_k \subseteq [x-r,x+r]$, and hence by Ahlfors--David regularity
    \[\left\lvert {A \cap I_k} \right \rvert \leq Mr^{\alpha}.\]
    Hence, for any $0 \leq k < q$,
    \[\mathbb{P}\left(D_I = k\right) = \frac{\left\lvert {A \cap I_k} \right \rvert}{\left\lvert {A \cap I} \right \rvert} \leq \frac{Mr^{\alpha}}{\frac{R^{\alpha}}{40M}}=\frac{40M^2}{q^{\alpha}},\]
    and so in particular,
    \[\max_k \mathbb{P} \left(D_I = k\right) \leq \frac{40M^2}{q^{\alpha}}.\]
    For the final part, since
    \[\sum_{0 \leq k < q}\mathbb{P}\left(D_I = k\right) = 1,\]
    we have the lower bound
    \[\left\lvert {\operatorname{supp} D_I} \right \rvert \geq \left(\max_{k}\mathbb{P}\left(D_I = k\right)\right)^{-1} \geq  \frac{q^{\alpha}}{40M^2}.\]
    It remains to prove the corresponding upper bound. Assume without loss of generality that at least half of the non-empty subintervals $I_k$ have $k$ even. If $r \geq 2$, for each such $k$ choose one point $x_k \in A \cap I_k$ and consider the intervals
    \[\left[x_k - \frac{r}{2}, x_k + \frac{r}{2}\right].\]
     These intervals are pairwise disjoint, and we have
     \[\left\lvert {A \cap \left[x_k - \frac{r}{2}, x_k + \frac{r}{2}\right]} \right \rvert \geq \frac{1}{M}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{\alpha} \geq \frac{r^{\alpha}}{2M}.\]
     All of these intervals lie in $[nR-r/2,(n+1)R+r/2]$. Choosing $x_0 \in A \cap I$, if $2R \leq N$ then
     \[[nR-r/2,(n+1)R+r/2] \subseteq [x_0-2R,x_0+2R],\]
     and so 
     \[\left\lvert {A \cap[nR-r/2,(n+1)R+r/2]} \right \rvert \leq \left\lvert {A \cap [x_0-2R,x_0+2R]} \right \rvert \leq M(2R)^{\alpha}.\] If $2R > N$, then $\left\lvert {A} \right \rvert \leq MN^{\alpha} \leq M \left(2R\right)^\alpha$. Summing the disjoint lower bounds therefore yields
    \[\frac{\left\lvert {\operatorname{supp} D_I} \right \rvert}{2}\frac{r^{\alpha}}{2M} \leq 2MR^{\alpha},\]
    and hence
    \[\left\lvert {\operatorname{supp} D_I} \right \rvert \leq 8M^2 q^{\alpha}.\]
    For $r=1$, each half-open interval $[k,k+1)$ contains at most one integer, and
    \[\left\lvert {\operatorname{supp} D_I} \right \rvert = \left\lvert {A \cap I} \right \rvert \leq MR^{\alpha} = Mq^{\alpha} \leq 8M^2 q^{\alpha}.\]

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\thlabel{AP} Let $P \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ be a non-empty finite arithmetic progression, and let
\[L_r = \{\ell \in \mathbb{Z}: A \cap [\ell r,\left(\ell+1\right)r) \neq \emptyset\}\]
be the indices of the intervals in $\mathcal{I}_r$ which contain a point of $A$. Then
\[\left\lvert {P \cap L_r} \right \rvert \leq 6 M^2 \left\lvert {P} \right \rvert^{\alpha}.\]
Consequently, if
\[\left\lvert {P \cap L_r} \right \rvert \geq \delta \left\lvert {P} \right \rvert\]
for some $\delta > 0$, then
\[\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert \leq \left(\frac{6M^2}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}.\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $\left\lvert {P \cap L_r} \right \rvert \leq 2$, the claim is immediate, so assume that $\left\lvert {P \cap L_r} \right \rvert \geq 3$, so in particular $\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert \geq 3$. Let $t>0$ be the common difference of $P$. If $tr>N$, then $P$ contains at most one index from $L_r$, since all occupied indices lie in an interval of length at most $N/r$. We may therefore suppose that $tr \leq N$.
\smallbreak
For each $\ell \in P \cap L_r$, choose
\[a_{\ell} \in A \cap [\ell r, \left(\ell + 1\right)r),\]
and let
\[B_{\ell} = [a_{\ell}-tr, a_{\ell}+tr].\]
By Ahlfors--David regularity,
\[\left\lvert {A \cap B_{\ell}} \right \rvert \geq \frac{\left(tr\right)^{\alpha}}{M}.\]

If $x \in B_{\ell}$, then $a_{\ell} \in [x-tr,x+tr]$, so $\ell$ lies in an interval of length $2t+1$. The indices $\ell$ belong to an arithmetic progression of common difference $t$, so there are at most three possibilities, hence every point of $\mathbb{R}$ belongs to at most three intervals $B_{\ell}$.
\smallbreak
Let $J$ be the smallest interval containing all of the $B_{\ell}$. The largest and smallest indices in $P \cap L_r$ differ by at most $\left(\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert-1\right)t$, and therefore
\[\text{length}\left(J\right) < \left(\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert+2\right)tr \leq 2 \left\lvert {P} \right \rvert tr.\]
If $2\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert tr \leq N$, choose a point $a$ of $A \cap J$ and consider the interval $\left[a-2\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert tr,a+2\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert tr\right]$, which contains $A \cap J$. By Ahlfors--David regularity,
\[\left\lvert {\left[a-2\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert tr,a+2\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert tr\right] \cap A} \right \rvert \leq M \left(2\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert tr\right)^{\alpha},\]
and hence
\[\left\lvert {A \cap J} \right \rvert \leq M \left(2\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert tr\right)^{\alpha} \leq 2M \left(\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert tr\right)^{\alpha}.\]
If $2 \left\lvert {P} \right \rvert tr > N$, then $\left\lvert {A} \right \rvert \leq MN^{\alpha}$, and we have the same bound.
\smallbreak
Summing the lower bounds for the $B_{\ell}$ and using the fact that each point of $\mathbb{R}$ is only contained in at most three intervals $B_{\ell}$, we have
\[\left\lvert {P \cap L_r} \right \rvert\frac{\left(tr\right)^{\alpha}}{M} \leq \sum_{\ell \in P\cap L_r}\left\lvert {A \cap B_{\ell}} \right \rvert \leq 3 \left\lvert {A \cap J} \right \rvert \leq 6M \left(\left\lvert {P} \right \rvert tr\right)^{\alpha},\]
and hence
\[\left\lvert {P \cap L_r} \right \rvert \leq 6M^2 \left\lvert {P} \right \rvert^{\alpha}.\]

The final part follows by combining this inequality with $\left\lvert {P \cap L_r} \right \rvert \geq \delta \left\lvert {P} \right \rvert$ and rearranging.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\thlabel{GMT}
If $X$ and $Y$ are finitely supported integer-valued random variables such that $d[X,Y] \leq \beta$, then there is a non-empty finite set $S \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ such that
\[\left\lvert {S-S} \right \rvert \leq 4 e^{12 \beta}\left\lvert {S} \right \rvert,\]
\[d[U_S,Y] \leq 6\beta + \log 2,\]
where $U_S$ is the discrete uniform distribution on $S$.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- defn-3: body_md -->
A \emph{generalised arithmetic progression} of rank $d$ is a set of the form
\[\mathcal{P}=\{x_0+n_1v_1+\cdots+n_dv_d:0\leq n_i<N_i,\ n_i \in \mathbb{Z}\},\]
where $x_0,v_1,\ldots,v_d\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $N_1,\ldots,N_d$ are positive integers. We say that $\mathcal{P}$ is \emph{proper} if every choice of
\[(n_1,\ldots,n_d)\in [0,N_1)\times\cdots\times[0,N_d)\]
gives a distinct element of $\mathcal{P}$. In this case,
\[|\mathcal{P}|=N_1\cdots N_d.\]
<!-- end defn-3 -->
<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\thlabel{Freiman}
    For every $K \geq 1$, there are $d(K) \in \mathbb{N}$ and $F(K) > 0$ such that every non-empty finite set $S \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ which satisfies
    \[\left\lvert {S-S} \right \rvert \leq K \left\lvert {S} \right \rvert\]
    is contained in a proper generalised arithmetic progression $\mathcal{P}$ with
    \[\text{rank}\ \mathcal{P} \leq d(K), \qquad \left\lvert {\mathcal{P}} \right \rvert \leq F(K) \left\lvert {S} \right \rvert.\]
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\thlabel{GAP}
For any $\beta > 0$, there are constants $c_{\beta},C_{\beta} > 0$ and an integer $d_{\beta} > 0$ such that, if $X$ and $Y$ are finitely supported integer-valued random variables with
\[d[X,Y] \leq \beta,\]
then there is a proper generalised arithmetic progression $\mathcal{P} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ such that
\[\text{rank} \ \mathcal{P} \leq d_{\beta}, \qquad \left\lvert {\mathcal{P}} \right \rvert \leq C_{\beta} e^{H(Y)}, \quad \text{and} \quad\mathbb{P} \left(Y \in \mathcal{P}\right) \geq c_{\beta}.\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By \thref{GMT}, there is a finite set $S \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ such that
    \[d[U_S,Y] \leq 6 \beta + \log 2,\]
    where $U_S$ is the uniform distribution on $S$. Thus,
    \[\left\lvert {\log \left\lvert {S} \right \rvert-H(Y)} \right \rvert \leq 12 \beta + 2 \log 2.\]
    Let $U_S'$ and $Y'$ be independent copies of $U_S$ and $Y$ respectively. Then
    \[H\left(U_S'-Y'\right)\leq 6 \beta + \log 2 + \frac{1}{2}\log\left\lvert {S} \right \rvert+\frac{1}{2}H(Y') \leq \log \left\lvert {S} \right \rvert + 12 \beta + 2 \log 2.\]
There must be some $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that
\[\mathbb{P}\left(U_S'-Y'=n\right) \geq e^{-12\beta - 2\log 2}\left\lvert {S} \right \rvert^{-1}.\]
Since $U_S'$ is uniform on $S$,
\[\mathbb{P}\left(U_S'-Y'=n\right)=\left\lvert {S} \right \rvert^{-1}\mathbb{P}\left(Y' \in S-n\right),\]
and hence
\[\mathbb{P}\left(Y \in S-n\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(Y' \in S-n\right) \geq e^{-12 \beta - 2\log 2}.\]
Let $T=S-n$. Then $T-T=S-S$, and hence by \thref{GMT},
\[\left\lvert {T-T} \right \rvert \leq 4 e^{12\beta}\left\lvert {T} \right \rvert.\]
Thus by \thref{Freiman} with $K=4 e^{12\beta}$, $T$ is contained in a proper generalised arithmetic progression $\mathcal{P}$ with
\[\text{rank}\ \mathcal{P} \leq d_{\beta},\qquad \left\lvert {\mathcal{P}} \right \rvert \leq F_{\beta}\left\lvert {T} \right \rvert,\]
where
\[F_{\beta}=F\left(4e^{12 \beta}\right),\qquad d_{\beta}=d\left(4e^{12 \beta}\right).\]
Since
\[\left\lvert {\log \left\lvert {S} \right \rvert-H(Y)} \right \rvert \leq 12 \beta + 2 \log 2,\]
it follows that
\[\left\lvert {T} \right \rvert=\left\lvert {S} \right \rvert \leq e^{12 \beta + 2 \log 2}e^{H(Y)}.\]
The lemma follows after taking
\[C_{\beta} = F_{\beta}e^{12 \beta + 2 \log 2}, \qquad c_{\beta}=e^{-12 \beta - 2 \log 2}.\]
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\thlabel{local}
For every $\beta>0$, there is a constant $K_{\beta}\geq1$, depending only on $\beta$, such that the following holds for every $0<\alpha<1$. Let
    \[C = \left\lceil \frac{K_\beta}{\alpha \left(1-\alpha\right)} \right\rceil, \qquad q \geq M^C, \qquad R = qr \leq N,\]
    and let $I,J \in \mathcal{I}_R$ satisfy
    \[\min\{\left\lvert {A \cap I} \right \rvert,\left\lvert {A \cap J} \right \rvert\} \geq \frac{R^{\alpha}}{40M}.\]
    Then
    \[d\left[D_I,-D_J\right] > \beta.\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $I=[nR,\left(n+1\right)R)$ and $J = [mR,\left(m+1\right)R)$. Define the translated variables
    \[\widehat{D_I}=D_I + nq, \qquad \widehat{D_J} = D_J + mq.\]
    Then
    \[\operatorname{supp}{\widehat{D_I}}, \operatorname{supp}{\widehat{D_J}} \subseteq L_r,\]
    and since entropic Ruzsa distance is invariant under translating either variable and under simultaneously negating both variables,
    \[d[D_I,-D_J]=d[-D_I,D_J]=d[-\widehat{D_I},\widehat{D_J}].\]
    Suppose for a contradiction that
    \[d\left[D_I,-D_J\right] \leq \beta.\]
    By \thref{GAP}, applied to $-\widehat{D_I}$ and $\widehat{D_J}$, there is a generalised arithmetic progression $\mathcal{P}$ such that
    \[\text{rank}\ \mathcal{P} \leq d_{\beta}, \qquad \mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{D_J} \in \mathcal{P}\right) \geq c_{\beta}\]
    and
    \[\left\lvert {\mathcal{P}} \right \rvert \leq C_{\beta}e^{H(\widehat{D_J})} \leq C_{\beta}\left\lvert {\operatorname{supp} \widehat{D_J}} \right \rvert \leq 8C_{\beta}M^2 q^{\alpha},\]
    where the last inequality follows from \thref{good}.
    \smallbreak
    Let
    \[B = \mathcal{P} \cap \operatorname{supp} \widehat{D_J}.\]
    By \thref{good}, and the fact that $\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{D_J} \in \mathcal{P}\right) \geq c_{\beta}$, we have
    \[\left\lvert {B} \right \rvert \geq \frac{c_{\beta}q^{\alpha}}{40M^2} .\]
    Combining $\left\lvert {\mathcal{P}} \right \rvert \leq 8C_{\beta}M^2 q^{\alpha}$ with the previous inequality, we obtain
    \[\frac{\left\lvert {B} \right \rvert}{\left\lvert {\mathcal{P}} \right \rvert} \geq \frac{c_{\beta}}{320C_{\beta}M^4}.\]
    Choosing $K_{\beta}$ large enough ensures that $\left\lvert {B} \right \rvert \geq 2$ and thus $\mathcal{P}$ has positive rank, write
    \[\mathcal{P} = \{x_0 + n_1v_1 + \cdots + n_dv_d : 0 \leq n_i < N_i\},\]
    and choose $i$ for which $N_i$ is largest. Since $\mathcal{P}$ is proper, we have
    \[N_i \geq \left\lvert {\mathcal{P}} \right \rvert^{1/d} \geq \left\lvert {B} \right \rvert^{1/d_{\beta}}.\]
    Fixing all $n_j$ with $j \neq i$, and allowing $n_i$ to vary, gives $\frac{\left\lvert {\mathcal{P}} \right \rvert}{N_i}$ fibres which partition $\mathcal{P}$, where each is an arithmetic progression of length $N_i$. There must be one fibre $Q$ which satisfies
    \[\left\lvert {Q} \right \rvert \geq \left\lvert {B} \right \rvert^{1/d_{\beta}}, \qquad \frac{\left\lvert {Q \cap B} \right \rvert}{\left\lvert {Q} \right \rvert} \geq \frac{\left\lvert {B} \right \rvert}{\left\lvert {\mathcal{P}} \right \rvert} \geq \frac{c_{\beta}}{320C_{\beta}M^4}.\]

    Since $B \subseteq \operatorname{supp} \widehat{D_{J}} \subseteq L_r$, \thref{AP} with $\delta = \frac{c_{\beta}}{320C_{\beta}M^4}$ gives

\[\left(\alpha C - 2\right)\left(1-\alpha\right)-6d_{\beta} \geq K_{\beta} - 2 - 6d_{\beta}.\]

\[K_{\beta} > 2 + 6 d_{\beta} + \frac{\log \left({\frac{40}{c_{\beta}}} \left(\frac{1920C_{\beta}}{c_{\beta}}\right)^{d_{\beta}}\right)}{\log 2}.\]

\thlabel{block}
    Let $B \geq 2$ be an integer, and let $X,Y$ be finitely supported independent integer-valued random variables. Write
    \[X = BU + \widetilde{X}, \qquad Y = BV + \widetilde{Y}, \qquad 0 \leq \widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y} < B.\]
    Then
    \[d\left[X,-Y\right] \geq d\left[U,-V\right] + \operatorname*{ 
  \text{$\vcenter{\hbox{\scaleobj{2}{\mathbb{E}}}}$} 
}_{u,v} d\left[\widetilde{X} | U=u, -\left(\widetilde{Y}|V=v\right)\right]-\log 2,\]
    where the expectation is with respect to the joint laws of the conditioning variables.

Let
\[C = \left\lfloor \frac{\widetilde{X}+\widetilde{Y}}{B} \right\rfloor \in \{0,1\}\]
record whether or not there is a carry, so that
\[\left\lfloor \frac{X+Y}{B} \right\rfloor = U+V+C.\]

Thus $X+Y$ determines $U+V$ up to at most two possibilities, and hence
\[H\left(U+V|X+Y\right) \leq \log 2.\]
Applying the chain rule for conditional entropy to the joint entropy $H(X+Y,U+V)$ in two different ways and equating the resulting expressions, we have
\[H(X+Y) = H(U+V) + H (X+Y|U+V) - H(U+V| X+Y).\]
We also have
\[H(X+Y|U+V) \geq H\left(X+Y|U,V\right) = H\left(\widetilde{X}+\widetilde{Y}|U,V\right).\]
Combining the previous three displayed equations, we obtain the inequality
\[H\left(X+Y\right) \geq H(U+V) + H\left(\widetilde{X}+\widetilde{Y}|U,V\right) - \log 2.\]
Since the quotient and remainder determine the original integer uniquely,
\[H(X) = H(U) + H(\widetilde{X}|U), \qquad H(Y) = H(V) + H(\widetilde{Y}|V).\]

Since $X$ and $Y$ are independent,
\[d[X,-Y] = H(X+Y) - \frac{1}{2}H(X) - \frac{1}{2}H(Y)\]
\[\geq H(U+V) + H\left(\widetilde{X}+\widetilde{Y}|U,V\right) - \log 2 - \frac{1}{2}\left(H(U) + H(\widetilde{X}|U)\right) - \frac{1}{2}\left(H(V) + H(\widetilde{Y}|V)\right)\]
\[=d[U,-V] + H\left(\widetilde{X}+\widetilde{Y}|U,V\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(H(\widetilde{X}|U)+H(\widetilde{Y}|V)\right) - \log 2.\]
But expanding the conditional entropies as expectations over $U=u$ and $V=v$, we have
\[H\left(\widetilde{X}+\widetilde{Y}|U,V\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(H(\widetilde{X}|U)+H(\widetilde{Y}|V)\right)=\operatorname*{ 
  \text{$\vcenter{\hbox{\scaleobj{2}{\mathbb{E}}}}$} 
}_{u,v} d\left[\widetilde{X} | U=u, -\left(\widetilde{Y}|V=v\right)\right],\]
proving the result.

    \[r = q^j, \qquad R = qr.\]

    \[I(s) = [sR,\left(s+1\right)R), \qquad I(s)_k = [sR + kr, sR + (k+1)r).\]

    \smallbreak

    \[\geq \beta \left(\frac{9}{10}\right)^2 = 2 \log 2,\]

    \[\mathcal{E}_j \geq 2 \log 2.\]

    \[d[U_j,-V_j] \geq d[U_{j+1},-V_{j+1}]+ \log 2\]

\[\mu(B(x_k,\delta))\geq C^{-1}\delta^\alpha.\]
If $y\in B(x_k,\delta)$, then the interval $[k\delta,(k+1)\delta)$ meets $[y-\delta,y+\delta]$, and an interval of length $2\delta$ meets at most three intervals of length $\delta$. Thus every point belongs to at most three of these balls.

\[\left\lvert {x_k-x_a} \right \rvert\leq(R+1)\delta,\]

The upper Ahlfors--David bound also holds for radii larger than $1$, since $X\subseteq B(x_a,1)$ and $\mu$ is supported on $X$, and so
