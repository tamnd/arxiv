<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:upper}
Let $\gamma\in(0,1)$ and $W_T=\lceil T^{1/(2\gamma+1)}\rceil$. There is a constant $C$, depending only on $\gamma$ and the constants of $\mathcal{C}_\gamma$, such that for all $T\ge2W_T$
\[
\sup_{(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{C}_\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\mu,\nu}|\hat\rho-\rho(\nu)|\ \le\ C\,T^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+1)} .
\]

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:minimax}
If $\mathcal{C}_\gamma$ contains a point satisfying~\eqref{eq:nulltail} with $H(r)=r^\gamma$, then $\mathcal R_T(\mathcal{C}_\gamma)\asymp T^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+1)}$.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The same bias--variance argument suggests an extension to envelopes that are regularly varying at $0$ with index in $(0,1)$: with $W_T=1/r_T$, Potter's bounds would control the spectral bias by a multiple of $H(1/W_T)$, while the stochastic error is of order $\sqrt{W_T/T}\asymp H(r_T)$ by~\eqref{eq:rT}. We do not pursue this here.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
In regular short-memory parametric Hawkes models, branching parameters typically admit the usual $T^{-1/2}$ rate under standard ergodicity and regularity conditions \citep{Ogata1978,ClinetYoshida2017}. Corollary~\ref{cor:roott} shows that no such uniform rate is available over long-memory classes, and Corollary~\ref{cor:minimax} identifies the exact rate for power-law envelopes. For power-law envelopes the exponent $\gamma/(2\gamma+1)$ increases from $0$ to $1/3$ as $\gamma$ goes from $0$ to $1$: heavier memory makes the branching ratio harder to estimate. The formal resemblance with nonparametric rates for $\gamma$-smooth functions is misleading, since $\gamma$ here indexes spectral mass near zero rate, not temporal regularity.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
The alternative in the proof has the same mean intensity as the null and differs only by slow mass moved into the baseline. The relevant resolution scale is $\tau^*=1/r_T$, which for power-law envelopes is of order $T^{1/(2\gamma+1)}$, much shorter than $T$. Branching mass carried by relaxation modes slower than $\tau^*$ cannot be reliably separated from immigration, even though their characteristic times are well inside the window.

\[

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $P_1'$ be the law of the point process that coincides with the $P_0$-process on $(-\infty,-L]$ and, on $(-L,\infty)$, has intensity $\mu_1+\int\phi_1(t-s)N(\mathrm{d} s)$, the integral running over the whole past, including points before $-L$.

\emph{Step 1: $P_0$ versus $P_1'$.} Let $\mathcal H_{-L}$ be the history up to time $-L$. The two laws agree on $\mathcal H_{-L}$, so by the chain rule
\[
\mathrm{KL}\big(P_0|_{(-\infty,T]}\,\big\|\,P_1'|_{(-\infty,T]}\big)=\mathbb{E}_0\Big[\mathrm{KL}\big(P_0(\cdot\mid\mathcal H_{-L})\,\big\|\,P_1'(\cdot\mid\mathcal H_{-L})\big)\Big],
\]
where the conditional laws are those of the points in $(-L,T]$. Given $\mathcal H_{-L}$, both conditional laws are described on $(-L,T]$ by intensities with respect to the same full-history filtration, $\lambda_0$ and $\lambda_1\ge\mu_1>0$. By the likelihood formula for point processes \citep{Jacod1975}, the conditional divergence equals $\mathbb{E}_0[\int_{-L}^{T}\varphi(\lambda_0(t),\lambda_1(t))\,\mathrm{d} t\mid\mathcal H_{-L}]$. Taking expectations and using stationarity of $P_0$ gives $(T+L)\,h$. Restricting to $[0,T]$ does not increase the divergence, and Pinsker's inequality gives $\mathrm{TV}(P_0^T,P_1'^T)\le\sqrt{(T+L)h/2}$.

\emph{Step 2: $P_1'$ versus $P_1$.} Both laws follow the $P_1$ dynamics on $(-L,\infty)$. By the cluster representation, the restriction to $[0,T]$ of either process is the superposition of (i) the clusters of immigrants arriving in $(-L,T]$ and (ii) the descendants, through cascades of $\phi_1$, of points located before $-L$. Part (i) has the same law under $P_1'$ and $P_1$ and is coupled identically. Hence the total variation is at most the probability that part (ii) places a point in $[0,T]$ in either process.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:twopoint}(c) and since $r\mapsto r/(r-\delta)$ decreases on $(r_0,\infty)$,
\[
q:=\int_0^\infty\phi_1(t)e^{\delta t}\,\mathrm{d} t=\int\frac{r}{r-\delta}\,\nu_1(\mathrm{d} r)\le\frac{\rho_1\,r_0}{r_0-\delta}=\frac{2\rho_1}{1+\rho_1}<1 .
\]
Thus $\int\Psi_1(u)e^{\delta u}\mathrm{d} u\le q/(1-q)=2\rho_1/(1-\rho_1)$ and, by Markov's inequality, $\Psi_1([v,\infty))\le\frac{2\rho_1}{1-\rho_1}e^{-\delta v}$. Points before $-L$ have mean density $\bar\lambda$ under both laws. Attaching to each of them its full $\phi_1$-cascade over-counts part (ii): some chains pass through times before $-L$, where the process is not driven by $\phi_1$ in $P_1'$, and in $P_1$ a point before $-L$ may be counted both on its own and as a descendant of an earlier point. Over-counting only strengthens the bound, so the expected number of points of part (ii) in $[0,\infty)$ is at most
\[
\bar\lambda\int_L^\infty\Psi_1([v,\infty))\,\mathrm{d} v\le\frac{2\rho_1\bar\lambda}{(1-\rho_1)\delta}\,e^{-\delta L}
\]
for each process. Combining both steps with the triangle inequality proves~\eqref{eq:coupling}. \qedhere

The key property is Lemma~\ref{lem:twopoint}(c): the alternative has no relaxation rates at or below $r_0$ and therefore forgets its past exponentially fast. A two-sided tail condition in the class would force one to keep part of the slow mass in the alternative and would destroy this property. This is why $\mathcal{C}_\gamma$ is defined through the upper bound~\eqref{eq:tailcond} only.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:bias}
There is $B<\infty$, depending only on $\gamma$ and the constants of $\mathcal{C}_\gamma$, such that $|f(\omega)-f(0)|\le B|\omega|^\gamma$ for all $\omega\in\mathbb{R}$ and all $(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{C}_\gamma$. Consequently, for every $W>0$,
\[
\Big|\frac{\mathrm{Var}\,N((0,W])}{W}-f(0)\Big|\le B\,\kappa_\gamma\,W^{-\gamma},\qquad \kappa_\gamma=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb{R}|u|^{\gamma-2}\,4\sin^2(u/2)\,\mathrm{d} u<\infty .
\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Since $\widehat\phi(0)-\widehat\phi(\omega)=\int\nu(\mathrm{d} r)\,i\omega/(r+i\omega)$, we have $|\widehat\phi(0)-\widehat\phi(\omega)|\le\int\nu(\mathrm{d} r)\min(1,|\omega|/r)$. For $|\omega|<r_\star$, split at $r=|\omega|$. The part $r\le|\omega|$ is at most $c_+|\omega|^\gamma$. For the part $r>|\omega|$, integration by parts and~\eqref{eq:tailcond} give
\[
|\omega|\int_{(|\omega|,\infty)}\frac{\nu(\mathrm{d} r)}{r}\le|\omega|\Big(c_+r_\star^{\gamma-1}+c_+\int_{|\omega|}^{r_\star}r^{\gamma-2}\mathrm{d} r+\frac{\rho_{\max}}{r_\star}\Big)\le B_1|\omega|^\gamma .
\]
For $|\omega|\ge r_\star$ the difference is at most $2\rho_{\max}\le(2\rho_{\max}r_\star^{-\gamma})|\omega|^\gamma$. Finally, $|1-\widehat\phi|\ge1-\rho_{\max}$ and $|1-\widehat\phi|\le2$ give
\[
|f(\omega)-f(0)|=\bar\lambda\,\frac{\big||1-\widehat\phi(0)|^2-|1-\widehat\phi(\omega)|^2\big|}{|1-\widehat\phi(\omega)|^2|1-\widehat\phi(0)|^2}\le\frac{4\bar\lambda}{(1-\rho_{\max})^4}\,|\widehat\phi(0)-\widehat\phi(\omega)| .
\]
For the second statement, recall \citep{DaleyVereJones2003} that
\[
\mathrm{Var}\,N((0,W])=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb{R} f(\omega)\,\frac{4\sin^2(\omega W/2)}{\omega^2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega,\qquad \frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb{R}\frac{4\sin^2(\omega W/2)}{\omega^2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega=W .
\]
Hence the left-hand side equals $\frac1{2\pi W}\int(f(\omega)-f(0))\,4\sin^2(\omega W/2)\,\omega^{-2}\mathrm{d}\omega$, and the substitution $u=\omega W$ gives the bound; $\kappa_\gamma<\infty$ because $\gamma\in(0,1)$. \qedhere

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:var}
Let $S$ be the total size of a cluster (immigrant and all descendants) and $s_k=\mu\,\mathbb{E}[S^k]$. With $Y_j=N_j-W\bar\lambda$ and $V_0=T'^{-1}\sum_jY_j^2$,
\[
\mathbb{E} V_0=\frac{\mathrm{Var}\,N((0,W])}{W},\qquad \mathrm{Var}(V_0)\le\frac{2Ws_2^2+s_4}{T'},\qquad \mathrm{Var}(\hat\lambda)\le\frac{s_2}{T'},
\]
\[
\mathbb{E}(\hat\lambda-\bar\lambda)^4\le\frac{3s_2^2}{T'^2}+\frac{s_4}{T'^3},\qquad \mathrm{Var}(\hat V)\le\frac{2(2Ws_2^2+s_4)}{T'}+2W^2\Big(\frac{3s_2^2}{T'^2}+\frac{s_4}{T'^3}\Big)\le\frac{C_VW}{T'},
\]
with $C_V=10s_2^2+4s_4$, which is bounded uniformly over the class. Moreover $\sup_{\rho\le\rho_{\max}}\mathbb{E}[S^k]<\infty$ for every $k$.

\emph{Cluster sizes.} In the cluster representation, $S$ is the total progeny of a Galton--Watson process with Poisson($\rho$) offspring, which has the Borel law $\mathbb{P}(S=n)=e^{-\rho n}(\rho n)^{n-1}/n!$. Since $\rho e^{1-\rho}<1$ for $\rho<1$, Stirling's formula shows that $\mathbb{P}(S=n)$ decays geometrically, so $S$ has moments of all orders. A standard coupling of Galton--Watson trees shows that $S$ is stochastically increasing in $\rho$, hence $\mathbb{E}[S^k]\le\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{\max}}[S^k]<\infty$.

\emph{A moment bound.} Pathwise, $\int_\mathbb{R} N_s(A)\,\mathrm{d} s=\sum_{x}\int\mathbf 1\{s+x\in A\}\mathrm{d} s=S|A|$, the sum running over the relative positions $x$ of the $S$ cluster points; and $N_s(A)\le S$, so $N_s(A)^k\le S^{k-1}N_s(A)$. Hence
\[
\mu\int_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{E}[N_s(A)^k]\,\mathrm{d} s\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}\Big[S^{k-1}\int_\mathbb{R} N_s(A)\,\mathrm{d} s\Big]=|A|\,s_k .
\]
With $k=2$ and~\eqref{eq:clustercum} this gives $\mathrm{Var}\,N(A)\le|A|s_2$, hence the bound on $\hat\lambda$, and $\mathbb{E} V_0=\mathrm{Var}\,N((0,W])/W$ by stationarity.

\emph{Variance of $V_0$.} For centred variables, $\mathrm{Cov}(Y_j^2,Y_k^2)=2\,\mathrm{Cov}(Y_j,Y_k)^2+\kappa(Y_j,Y_j,Y_k,Y_k)$, and cumulants of order at least two are unchanged by centring. By~\eqref{eq:clustercum} all covariances are non-negative, and $\mathrm{Cov}(Y_j,Y_k)\le\max_i\mathrm{Var}\,Y_i\le Ws_2$. Therefore
\[
\sum_{j,k}\mathrm{Cov}(Y_j,Y_k)^2\le Ws_2\sum_{j,k}\mathrm{Cov}(Y_j,Y_k)=Ws_2\,\mathrm{Var}\,N((0,T'])\le WT's_2^2 .
\]
For the fourth-order term, by~\eqref{eq:clustercum},
\[
\sum_{j,k}\kappa(Y_j,Y_j,Y_k,Y_k)=\mu\int\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_jN_s(B_j)^2\Big)^2\Big]\mathrm{d} s\le\mu\int\mathbb{E}\big[N_s((0,T'])^4\big]\mathrm{d} s\le T's_4,
\]
using $\sum_jN_s(B_j)^2\le N_s((0,T'])^2$. Hence $\mathrm{Var}(\sum_jY_j^2)\le2WT's_2^2+T's_4$, and dividing by $T'^2$ gives the bound on $\mathrm{Var}(V_0)$.

\emph{Variance of $\hat V$.} For the count $N((0,T'])$, $\mathbb{E}(N-\mathbb{E} N)^4=3\,\mathrm{Var}(N)^2+\kappa_4(N)\le3T'^2s_2^2+T's_4$ by~\eqref{eq:clustercum} and the moment bound, which gives the bound on $\mathbb{E}(\hat\lambda-\bar\lambda)^4$ after division by $T'^4$. Since $\hat V=V_0-W(\hat\lambda-\bar\lambda)^2$, $\mathrm{Var}(\hat V)\le2\,\mathrm{Var}(V_0)+2W^2\mathbb{E}(\hat\lambda-\bar\lambda)^4$, and $W\le T'$ gives the last inequality. \qedhere

Since $\sum_jY_j=T'(\hat\lambda-\bar\lambda)$, we have $\hat V=V_0-W(\hat\lambda-\bar\lambda)^2$, and $\mathbb{E}[W(\hat\lambda-\bar\lambda)^2]\le Ws_2/T'$. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:bias} and~\ref{lem:var},
\[
\mathbb{E}|\hat V-f(0)|\le B\kappa_\gamma W^{-\gamma}+\sqrt{\frac{2Ws_2^2+s_4}{T'}}+\frac{Ws_2}{T'},\qquad \mathbb{E}|\hat\lambda-\bar\lambda|\le\sqrt{\frac{s_2}{T'}} .
\]
The true values satisfy $\bar\lambda\in[\mu_{\min},\lambda_{\max}]$, $f(0)=\bar\lambda/(1-\rho)^2\in[\mu_{\min},v_{\max}]$ and $\rho\in[0,\rho_{\max}]$, so neither the clipping nor the final projection increases the errors. On the rectangle $[\mu_{\min},\lambda_{\max}]\times[\mu_{\min},v_{\max}]$ the map $(\lambda,v)\mapsto1-\sqrt{\lambda/v}$ is Lipschitz with a constant depending only on the class, and $\rho=1-\sqrt{\bar\lambda/f(0)}$. Hence $\mathbb{E}|\hat\rho-\rho|$ is bounded by a constant times the sum of the two displayed errors. With $W=W_T$ and $T'\ge T/2$, each term is $O(T^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+1)})$, uniformly over $\mathcal{C}_\gamma$. \qedhere
