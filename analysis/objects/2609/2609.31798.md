w(3,k)\ge k^{c\log k}.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:spectrum}
For every sufficiently large \(r\), there is a finite set \(S_{r}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{r}\) such that
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[\textup{(1)}] \(\bm{0}\in S_{r}.\)
    \item[\textup{(2)}] \(|S_{r}|\le \exp(100r).\)
    \item[\textup{(3)}] \(\sum_{\bm{\xi}\notin S_{r}}\widehat\Psi_{r}(\bm{\xi})<\frac{400^{-r}}{100}.\)
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since \(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat\psi(n)^{3/4}<\infty\), this sum is a finite constant. Therefore \(\log\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat\psi(n)^{3/4}\right)\) is finite, and hence \(\frac{100}{4}-\log\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat\psi(n)^{3/4}\right)>\log 400+2\) holds for the fixed constant \(100\). Define \(S_{r}=\{\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}:\widehat\Psi_{r}(\bm{\xi})\ge \exp(-100r)\}.\) If \(\bm{\xi}\notin S_{r}\), then \(\widehat\Psi_{r}(\bm{\xi})\le\exp\left(-\frac{100r}{4}\right)\widehat\Psi_{r}(\bm{\xi})^{3/4}.\) Summing this inequality and using the product structure gives
\[
\sum_{\bm{\xi}\notin S_{r}}\widehat\Psi_{r}(\bm{\xi})
\le \exp\left(-\frac{100r}{4}\right)\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat\psi(n)^{3/4}\right)^{r}
\le \exp\left(-\left(\log 400+2\right)r\right)
<\frac{400^{-r}}{100}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(r\). Since all Fourier coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one, \(|S_{r}|\le \exp(100r)\). Moreover, \(100>\log 400\), so \(\bm{0}\in S_{r}\). This proves the lemma.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:box-image}
If \(0<t<\frac{1}{2}\), then \(m_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(\phi_{V}(Q_{t}))\ge (2t)^{m}\).
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let \(A\) be the \(m\times r\) integer matrix whose rows are \(\bm{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{m}\). Choose a set \(J\) of \(m\) coordinates such that the corresponding square minor \(A_{J}\) is nonsingular, and consider the copy of \((-t,t)^{m}\) in \(Q_{t}\) supported on \(J\). Since \(t<\frac{1}{2}\), this box embeds in \(\mathbb{T}^{r}\), and the restriction of \(\phi_{V}\) to it is \(\bm{u}\to A_{J}\bm{u}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\).

The Euclidean image \(A_{J}(-t,t)^{m}\) has volume \(|\det A_{J}|(2t)^{m}\). The torus endomorphism induced by \(A_{J}\) has kernel of order \(|\det A_{J}|\). Hence every point of \(\mathbb{T}^{m}\) has at most \(|\det A_{J}|\) preimages in \((-t,t)^{m}\). Applying the area formula before and after reduction modulo \(\mathbb{Z}^{m}\) gives \(|\det A_{J}|(2t)^{m}\le |\det A_{J}|m_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(\phi_{V}(Q_{t}))\), which proves the result.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:centers}
For every sufficiently large \(r\), there are \(\exp\left(\frac{r}{10}\right)\) points \(\bm{z}_{i}\in\mathbb{T}^{r}\), indexed by \(i\in[\exp\left(\frac{r}{10}\right)]\), with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[\textup{(1)}] If \(i,j,\ell\in[\exp\left(\frac{r}{10}\right)]\) are not all equal, then \(\bm{z}_{i}-2\bm{z}_{j}+\bm{z}_{\ell}\notin Q_{\frac{1}{25}}\).
    \item[\textup{(2)}] Let \(V\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{r}\) be generated by elements of \(S_{r}\), and suppose that \(\dim V<\frac{r}{1000}\). For every \(\bm{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{r}\), there are \(\exp\left(\frac{r}{100}\right)\) pairwise distinct indices \(i\) and corresponding vectors \(\bm{u}_{i}\in Q_{\frac{1}{400}}\) such that \(\bm{\xi}\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{z}_{i}-\bm{u}_{i})=0\) in \(\mathbb{T}\) for every \(\bm{\xi}\in L_{V}\).
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Choose the \(\exp\left(\frac{r}{10}\right)\) points independently and uniformly at random according to Haar measure on \(\mathbb{T}^{r}\).

\begin{claim}\label{claim:center-separation}
With probability \(1-o(1)\), the first property of Lemma~\ref{lem:centers} holds.
\end{claim}

\begin{poc}
For any \(i,j,\ell\) that are not all equal, the random variable \(\bm{z}_{i}-2\bm{z}_{j}+\bm{z}_{\ell}\) is Haar distributed on \(\mathbb{T}^{r}\). This is still true when \(i=\ell\ne j\), since multiplication by \(2\) keeps Haar measure unchanged. The union bound gives
\[
\mathbb{P} \bigl(\text{the separation property fails}\bigr)\le \exp\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)\left(\frac{2}{25}\right)^{r}=o(1).
\]
\end{poc}

\begin{claim}\label{claim:center-covering}
With probability \(1-o(1)\), the second property of Lemma~\ref{lem:centers} holds for all spaces \(V\) generated by elements of \(S_{r}\) with \(\dim V<\frac{r}{1000}\).
\end{claim}

\begin{poc}
Fix such a space \(V\) of dimension \(m<\frac{r}{1000}\), and put \(W_{V}=\phi_{V}(Q_{\frac{1}{1600}})\). We can choose a maximal finite set \(T_{V}\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{m}\) such that the sets \(\bm{t}+W_V\) and \(\bm{t}'+W_V\) are disjoint for any distinct \(\bm{t},\bm{t}'\in T_V\). This process stops because Lemma~\ref{lem:box-image} gives \(|T_{V}|m_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(W_{V})\le 1\) and \(m_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(W_{V})\ge\left(\frac{1}{800}\right)^{m}\), so \(|T_{V}|\le800^{m}\). For every \(\bm{q}\in\mathbb{T}^{m}\), maximality forces \(\bm{q}+W_{V}\) to meet some \(\bm{t}+W_{V}\). Hence \(\bm{q}\in \bm{t}+(W_{V}-W_{V})\subseteq \bm{t}+\phi_{V}(Q_{\frac{1}{800}})\).

Split the indices into \(\exp\left(\frac{r}{100}\right)\) disjoint blocks, each containing at least \(\exp\left(\frac{r}{20}\right)\) indices when \(r\) is large. Fix one block and one \(\bm{t}_{0}\in T_{V}\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:box-image}, the set \(\bm{t}_{0}-\phi_{V}(Q_{\frac{1}{800}})\) has measure at least \(400^{-m}\). The probability that no center in the block maps into this set is at most \(\exp\left(-\exp\left(\frac{r}{25}\right)\right).\)

Every such \(V\) has a basis contained in \(S_{r}\), so there are at most \(\sum_{0\le j<\frac{r}{1000}}|S_{r}|^{j}=\exp(O(r^{2}))\) such spaces. Taking a union bound over all these spaces, all \(\exp\left(\frac{r}{100}\right)\) blocks and all \(\bm{t}_{0}\in T_{V}\), the total failure probability is still \(o(1)\). We may therefore assume that every block contains a suitable center for every \(V\) and every \(\bm{t}_{0}\in T_{V}\).

Now fix \(\bm{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{r}\). Choose \(\bm{t}_{0}\in T_{V}\) and \(\bm{w}_{1}\in\phi_{V}(Q_{\frac{1}{800}})\) such that \(\phi_{V}(\bm{x})=\bm{t}_{0}+\bm{w}_{1}\). In each block choose a center \(\bm{z}_{i}\) for which \(\phi_{V}(\bm{z}_{i})=\bm{t}_{0}-\bm{w}_{2,i}\) for some \(\bm{w}_{2,i}\in\phi_{V}(Q_{\frac{1}{800}})\). Then \(\phi_{V}(\bm{x}-\bm{z}_{i})=\bm{w}_{1}+\bm{w}_{2,i}\in\phi_{V}(Q_{\frac{1}{400}})\), so there is \(\bm{u}_{i}\in Q_{\frac{1}{400}}\) with \(\phi_{V}(\bm{x}-\bm{z}_{i}-\bm{u}_{i})=0\). The chosen indices lie in different blocks and are therefore distinct. Since the coordinate functions of \(\phi_{V}\) form a basis of \(L_{V}\), the required phase identity follows.
\end{poc}

Claims~\ref{claim:center-separation} and~\ref{claim:center-covering} hold together with positive probability, so a deterministic choice of the centers has both properties.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:fejer}
For every \(t\in\mathbb{T}\),
\[
\widehat\omega(t):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\omega(n){\mathsf e}(nt)=\left|\frac{1}{\exp(200r)}\sum_{j=0}^{\exp(200r)-1}{\mathsf e}(jt)\right|^{2}\ge 0,
\]
and \(\widehat\omega(t)\le1\). If \(t\ne0\) in \(\mathbb{T}\), then \(\widehat\omega(t)\le\frac{1}{\bigl(2\exp(200r)\|t\|_{\mathbb{T}}\bigr)^{2}}\).
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
For the first identity, write \(\omega\) as the convolution of the uniform probability measure on \(\{0,\ldots,\exp(200r)-1\}\) with its reflection. This also gives the trivial upper bound \(1\). If \(t\ne0\) in \(\mathbb{T}\), the geometric sum gives
\[
\left|\frac{1}{\exp(200r)}\sum_{j=0}^{\exp(200r)-1}{\mathsf e}(jt)\right|\le\frac{1}{2\exp(200r)\|t\|_{\mathbb{T}}},
\]
where we used \(\bigl|\sin(\pi t)\bigr|\ge2\|t\|_{\mathbb{T}}\).

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:rotation}
For every sufficiently large \(r\), there is \(\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{T}^{r}\) such that for every \(d\in[\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1000}\right)]\),
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[\textup{(1)}] \(\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}R(d\bm{\theta})<\frac{r}{1000}.\)
    \item[\textup{(2)}]\(\|d\bm{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{T}^{r},\infty}>\exp\left(-\frac{r}{400}\right).\)
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Choose \(\bm{\theta}\) according to Haar measure on \(\mathbb{T}^{r}\). If \(\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}R(d\bm{\theta})\ge\frac{r}{1000}\), then \(R(d\bm{\theta})\) contains \(\frac{r}{1000}\) linearly independent frequencies \(\bm{\xi}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\xi}_{\frac{r}{1000}}\in S_{r}\). For each fixed independent tuple and each fixed \(d\), the homomorphism
\[
\bm{\theta}\longrightarrow\left(\bm{\xi}_{1}\cdot d\bm{\theta},\ldots,\bm{\xi}_{\frac{r}{1000}}\cdot d\bm{\theta}\right)
\]
maps \(\mathbb{T}^{r}\) onto \(\mathbb{T}^{\frac{r}{1000}}\). Indeed, its dual sends an integer vector \(\left(q_{1},\ldots,q_{\frac{r}{1000}}\right)\) to \(d\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{1000}}q_{j}\bm{\xi}_{j}\), and this map is injective. The resulting point of \(\mathbb{T}^{\frac{r}{1000}}\) is therefore Haar distributed. The union bound gives
\[
\begin{split}
&\mathbb{P}\left(\exists d\in\left[\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1000}\right)\right]:\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}R(d\bm{\theta})\ge\frac{r}{1000}\right)\\
&\le\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1000}\right)|S_{r}|^{\frac{r}{1000}}\left(\frac{20^{r+1}}{\exp(200r)}\right)^{\frac{r}{1000}}\\
&\le\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{10000}\right)=o(1).
\end{split}
\]
Here the last estimate follows from \(|S_{r}|\le\exp(100r)\).

For each fixed \(d\), the point \(d\bm{\theta}\) is also Haar distributed. A union bound over \(d\in[\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1000}\right)]\) shows that the probability of \(\|d\bm{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{T}^{r},\infty}\le\exp\left(-\frac{r}{400}\right)\) for some such \(d\) is at most
\[
\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1000}\right)\left(2\exp\left(-\frac{r}{400}\right)\right)^{r}\le\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{1000}\right).
\]
The two bad events have total probability smaller than one for all sufficiently large \(r\), which proves the lemma.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:orbit}
Let \(\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{T}^{r}\), put \(V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}R(\bm{\alpha})\), and suppose that \(\dim V<\frac{r}{1000}\). Let \(\bm{x},\bm{z}\in\mathbb{T}^{r}\). If there is \(\bm{u}\in Q_{\frac{1}{400}}\) such that \(\bm{\xi}\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{z}-\bm{u})=0\) in \(\mathbb{T}\) for every \(\bm{\xi}\in L_{V}\), then there is an integer \(n\) with \(|n|<\exp(200r)\) such that \(\bm{x}+n\bm{\alpha}-\bm{z}\in Q_{\frac{1}{100}}\).
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Consider the nonnegative real number \(S=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\omega(n)\Psi_{r}(\bm{x}+n\bm{\alpha}-\bm{z}-\bm{u})\). Absolute convergence permits termwise Fourier expansion, giving
\[
S=\sum_{\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}\widehat\Psi_{r}(\bm{\xi}){\mathsf e}\bigl(\bm{\xi}\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{z}-\bm{u})\bigr)\widehat\omega(\bm{\xi}\cdot\bm{\alpha}).
\]
The zero frequency contributes \(400^{-r}\). If \(\bm{\xi}\in R(\bm{\alpha})\), then \(\bm{\xi}\in V\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}=L_{V}\), so the phase is one. By Lemma~\ref{lem:fejer}, the remaining factor is nonnegative. If \(\bm{\xi}\in S_{r}\setminus R(\bm{\alpha})\), then Lemma~\ref{lem:fejer} and the definition of \(R(\bm{\alpha})\) give \(|\widehat\omega(\bm{\xi}\cdot\bm{\alpha})|\le400^{-r-1}\).
For \(\bm{\xi}\notin S_{r}\), we only use \(|\widehat\omega(\bm{\xi}\cdot\bm{\alpha})|\le1\). Since \(S\) is real, taking real parts and using Lemma~\ref{lem:spectrum} gives
\[
S\ge400^{-r}-400^{-r-1}\sum_{\bm{\xi}\in S_{r}}\widehat\Psi_{r}(\bm{\xi})-\sum_{\bm{\xi}\notin S_{r}}\widehat\Psi_{r}(\bm{\xi})>400^{-r}\left(1-\frac{1}{400}-\frac{1}{100}\right)>0.
\]
Both \(\omega\) and \(\Psi_{r}\) are nonnegative. Hence \(\Psi_{r}(\bm{x}+n\bm{\alpha}-\bm{z}-\bm{u})>0\) for some integer \(n\) with \(|n|<\exp(200r)\). The positivity set of \(\Psi_{r}\) is \(Q_{\frac{1}{400}}\), so \(\bm{x}+n\bm{\alpha}-\bm{z}-\bm{u}\in Q_{\frac{1}{400}}\). Since \(\bm{u}\in Q_{\frac{1}{400}}\), we obtain \(\bm{x}+n\bm{\alpha}-\bm{z}\in Q_{\frac{1}{200}}\subseteq Q_{\frac{1}{100}}\).
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let \(k\) be sufficiently large, and choose an integer \(r\) such that \(\frac{\log k}{500}\le r\le\frac{\log k}{400}\). In particular, \(2\exp(200r)-1\le k\).

Fix centers \(\bm{z}_{i}\), indexed by \(i\in[\exp\left(\frac{r}{10}\right)]\), as in Lemma~\ref{lem:centers}, and choose \(\bm{\theta}\) as in Lemma~\ref{lem:rotation}. Independently for each \(i\), choose \(s_{i}\) uniformly at random from \(\{0,\ldots,r\cdot\exp\left(\frac{r}{200}\right)-1\}\). Define
\[
A_{i}=\left\{\bm{x}\in Q_{\frac{1}{100}}:s_{i}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{r}{200}\right)\le\|\bm{x}\|_{2}^{2}<(s_{i}+1)\exp\left(-\frac{r}{200}\right)\right\},
\]
and put \(B=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\exp\left(\frac{r}{10}\right)}(\bm{z}_{i}+A_{i})\). Color \(n\in[\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1000}\right)]\) blue if \(n\bm{\theta}\in B\), and red otherwise.

\begin{claim}\label{claim:no-blue}
For every choice of the \(s_{i}\), there is no blue \(3\)-AP.
\end{claim}

\begin{poc}
Suppose that \(n-d,n,n+d\in[\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1000}\right)]\) are blue for some \(d\ge1\). Choose \(i,j,\ell\) and \(\bm{r}_{1}\in A_{i},\bm{r}_{2}\in A_{j},\bm{r}_{3}\in A_{\ell}\) so that \((n-d)\bm{\theta}=\bm{z}_{i}+\bm{r}_{1}\), \(n\bm{\theta}=\bm{z}_{j}+\bm{r}_{2}\), and \((n+d)\bm{\theta}=\bm{z}_{\ell}+\bm{r}_{3}\) in \(\mathbb{T}^{r}\). Taking second differences gives \(\bm{z}_{i}-2\bm{z}_{j}+\bm{z}_{\ell}=-(\bm{r}_{1}-2\bm{r}_{2}+\bm{r}_{3})\in Q_{\frac{1}{25}}\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:centers} gives \(i=j=\ell\). Hence \(\bm{r}_{1}-2\bm{r}_{2}+\bm{r}_{3}\) is an integer vector all of whose coordinates have absolute value less than \(\frac{1}{25}\), and so it is zero. Write \(\bm{r}_{1}=\bm{r}_{2}-\bm{v}\) and \(\bm{r}_{3}=\bm{r}_{2}+\bm{v}\).
Since \(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},\bm{r}_{3}\) lie in the same shell, the parallelogram identity gives
\[
\|\bm{v}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{\|\bm{r}_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\bm{r}_{3}\|_{2}^{2}}{2}-\|\bm{r}_{2}\|_{2}^{2}<\exp\left(-\frac{r}{200}\right).
\]
But \(d\bm{\theta}=\bm{r}_{2}-\bm{r}_{1}=\bm{v}\) in \(\mathbb{T}^{r}\). Since \(\bm{v}\in Q_{\frac{1}{50}}\subseteq\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{r}\), it is the representative of \(d\bm{\theta}\) in \(\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{r}\), and \(\|d\bm{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{T}^{r},\infty}\le\|\bm{v}\|_{2}<\exp\left(-\frac{r}{400}\right)\), contrary to Lemma~\ref{lem:rotation}.
\end{poc}

\begin{claim}\label{claim:no-red}
With positive probability, the coloring has no red AP of length \(2\exp(200r)-1\).
\end{claim}

\begin{poc}
Fix an AP \(P\) of this length, with common difference \(d\ge1\), say
\[
P=\{n_{0}+jd:0\le j\le2\exp(200r)-2\}\subseteq\left[\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1000}\right)\right].
\]
Put \(\bm{x}=(n_{0}+(\exp(200r)-1)d)\bm{\theta}\), \(\bm{\alpha}=d\bm{\theta}\), and \(V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}R(\bm{\alpha})\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:rotation}, \(\dim V<\frac{r}{1000}\), while \(V\) is generated by elements of \(S_{r}\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:centers}, we have \(\exp\left(\frac{r}{100}\right)\) distinct indices \(i\) and vectors \(\bm{u}_{i}\in Q_{\frac{1}{400}}\) such that \(\bm{\xi}\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{z}_{i}-\bm{u}_{i})=0\) for every \(\bm{\xi}\in L_{V}\). For each such \(i\), there exists an integer \(n_{i}\) with \(|n_{i}|<\exp(200r)\) such that\(\bm{q}_{i}:=\bm{x}+n_{i}\bm{\alpha}-\bm{z}_{i}\in Q_{\frac{1}{100}},\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit}. Since \(0\le\exp(200r)-1+n_{i}\le2\exp(200r)-2\), we have \(n_{0}+(\exp(200r)-1+n_{i})d\in P\), and its image on the torus is \(\bm{x}+n_{i}\bm{\alpha}=\bm{z}_{i}+\bm{q}_{i}\).

For each \(i\), fix one such \(n_{i}\), and hence \(\bm{q}_{i}\). None of these choices depends on the variables \(s_{i}\). Since
\[
0\le\|\bm{q}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}<\frac{r}{10000}<r\cdot\exp\left(\frac{r}{200}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{r}{200}\right),
\]
there is a unique \(t_{i}\in\{0,\ldots,r\cdot\exp\left(\frac{r}{200}\right)-1\}\) such that \(t_{i}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{r}{200}\right)\le\|\bm{q}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}<(t_{i}+1)\exp\left(-\frac{r}{200}\right)\).
Now \(s_{i}=t_{i}\) with probability \(\frac{1}{r\cdot\exp\left(\frac{r}{200}\right)}\), and on this event \(P\) contains a blue point. These events are independent, since the indices \(i\) are distinct. Thus
\[
\mathbb{P}(P\text{ is entirely red})\le\left(1-\frac{1}{r\cdot\exp\left(\frac{r}{200}\right)}\right)^{\exp\left(\frac{r}{100}\right)}\le\exp\left(-\frac{\exp\left(\frac{r}{100}\right)}{r\cdot\exp\left(\frac{r}{200}\right)}\right).
\]
The quotient in the exponent is \(\exp(\Omega(r))\), and there are at most \(\exp(O(r^{2}))\) APs in the ambient interval. A union bound therefore shows that, with probability \(1-o(1)\), none is entirely red.
\end{poc}

By Claims~\ref{claim:no-blue} and~\ref{claim:no-red}, there is a choice of the \(s_{i}\) for which the coloring has no blue \(3\)-AP and no red AP of length \(2\exp(200r)-1\). Since this length is at most \(k\), the same coloring has no red \(k\)-AP. The coloring is defined on an interval of length \(\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1000}\right)\), so \(w(3,k)\ge\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}}{2000}\right)\) for all sufficiently large \(r\). Since \(r=\Theta(\log k)\), this gives \(w(3,k)\ge k^{c\log k}\) for some absolute constant \(c>0\), as required.
