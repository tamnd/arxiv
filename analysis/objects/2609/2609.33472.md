<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{assump:centered-symmetric}
The cost law is centrally symmetric about $\bar c$, namely
$c\overset{d}=2\bar c-c$, and $\mathbb{E}\|c\|<\infty$. The deterministic measurable
selection $w^*$ is fixed before population risks are formed.
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:bayes-minimizer}
Under Assumption~\ref{assump:centered-symmetric}, $R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}$ is convex and
$\bar c\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\hat c}R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c)$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
We use ``unique quotient-report identification'' to mean uniqueness of the
quotient Bayes report $[\hat c]$, never uniqueness of an ambient representative. In
the terminology of scoring-function theory this is closely related to strict
consistency and should not be confused with identification-function
terminology for statistical functionals
\citep{Gneiting2011,FisslerHlavinovaRudloff2021}.  The present paper is not a
scoring-rule survey; the distinction matters because a strictly consistent
loss can have a unique quotient Bayes report without implying any particular downstream
decision rule, and conversely decision consistency can hold without unique
identification.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:compact-support}
Absolute continuity does not by itself make a decision-focused surrogate
identify the quotient report. Let $S=[-1/2,1/2]$ and
$C\sim\operatorname{Unif}[1,2]$, so $\bar c=3/2$. Direct evaluation gives
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\hat c}R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c)=[1,\infty),
  \label{eq:compact-support-plateau}
\end{equation}
although every minimizer induces the same singleton decision as the mean.
Indeed, for $c>0$,
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c,c)=\tfrac12|c-2\hat c|-\hat c+\tfrac c2,
  \label{eq:compact-support-loss}
\end{equation}
so the loss vanishes almost surely for $\hat c\geq1$ and is positive with
positive probability for $\hat c<1$. The exact piecewise risk and the same
construction with a smooth compactly supported density are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:counterexamples}.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:strict-crossing}
The triple $(P_c,S,w^*)$ satisfies \emph{optimal-face strict crossing}
(SC) when,
for every effective displacement $\Delta\in L\setminus\{0\}$,
\[
  \Pr\{w^*(c)\notinW^*(c+2\Delta)\}>0.
  \qquad\textup{(SC)}
\]
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:identification-sc}
Under Assumption~\ref{assump:centered-symmetric},
\begin{equation}
  \bar c+N\text{ is the unique minimizer set of }R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \text{SC holds.}
  \label{eq:identification-iff-sc}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The centered excess-risk identity is
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c+\Delta)-R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c)
  =\mathbb{E}\left[G_{\Delta}(c)\right].
  \label{eq:spo-plus-excess-identity}
\end{equation}
By equation~\ref{eq:gap-positive-iff-leaves-face}, the expectation in
equation~\ref{eq:spo-plus-excess-identity} is strictly positive exactly when the SC
event has positive probability. Proposition~\ref{prop:bayes-minimizer} then
gives both directions of equation~\ref{eq:identification-iff-sc}. Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}
records the centered algebra and the full quantifier argument.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The centered representation equation~\ref{eq:spo-plus-excess-identity} and uniqueness
under stronger full-support conditions are known \citep{LiuGrigas2021}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:identification-sc} characterizes their exact equality case
after quotienting invisible directions: zero gap means that the old decision
remains in the shifted optimal face. The distinct geometric and quantitative
consequences are developed below.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:full-support}
Suppose $\operatorname{supp}(P_Lc)=L$. Then SC in Definition~\ref{def:strict-crossing}
holds.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Fix $\Delta\in L\setminus\{0\}$ and set $q_0=-\Delta$. Every
$w\inW^*(q_0)$ maximizes $\Delta^\top w$, whereas
$z^*(q_0+2\Delta)=z^*(\Delta)$. Hence
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^\top w-z^*(\Delta)
  =\max_{u\inS}\Delta^\top u-\min_{u\inS}\Delta^\top u>0.
  \label{eq:directional-width}
\end{equation}
The width is positive because a vector in $L$ that is orthogonal to every
element of $S-S$ must vanish. Put
\[
  g_\Delta(q,w):=(q+2\Delta)^\top w-z^*(q+2\Delta).
\]
Since $z^*$ is continuous, so is $g_\Delta$. At $q=q_0$, uniformly over
$w\inW^*(q_0)$,
\[
  g_\Delta(q_0,w)
  =\max_{u\inS}\Delta^\top u-\min_{u\inS}\Delta^\top u
  =:\delta_\Delta>0.
\]
Compactness and continuity give relative neighborhoods $U_1$ of $q_0$ in $L$
and $V$ of
$W^*(q_0)$ on which $g_\Delta>\delta_\Delta/2$. By the maximum theorem,
$q\mapstoW^*(q)$ is upper hemicontinuous, so some relative neighborhood
$U_2$ of $q_0$ satisfies $W^*(q)\subset V$. Thus, for $U=U_1\cap U_2$,
\[
  W^*(q)\subset V,\qquad
  g_\Delta(q,w)>\delta_\Delta/2\quad\forall w\inW^*(q),
\]
which implies $w^*(q)\notinW^*(q+2\Delta)$. Hence the SC event contains
the preimage of the nonempty relatively open set $U$, and intrinsic full
support gives $\Pr\{P_Lc\in U\}>0$. The appendix gives the reduction and
full argument.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:quotient-space-identification}
Let $L:=\operatorname{span}(S-S)$ and $L^\perp$ be its orthogonal complement.
For any $\Delta\in L^\perp$, the linear form $\Delta^\top w$ is constant on
$S$, and a pointwise calculation gives
$\ell_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c+\Delta,c)=\ell_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c,c)$ for every $\hat c,c$
(Appendix~\ref{app:geometry}).  Hence the ambient SPO+ risk is constant along
$L^\perp$ for \emph{every} distribution, including asymmetric laws.
The identification target is therefore the quotient report
$[\bar c]=\bar c+L^\perp$, or equivalently the effective component
$P_L\bar c$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:identification-sc} accordingly quantifies SC over
$L\setminus\{0\}$ and identifies the quotient report in
$\mathbb{R}^d/L^\perp$; no ambient full-dimensionality is required.
<!-- end remark-3 -->

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:ac-equivalence}
Suppose $P_Lc\ll\lambda_L$. For every fixed $\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^d$,
\begin{equation}
  \Pr(A_\Delta\mathbin{\triangle}B_\Delta)=0.
  \label{eq:ac-disagreement-crossing-equivalence}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The projected tie locus in $L$ is $\lambda_L$-null because the restricted support
function is finite convex. Hence the source and shifted projected costs avoid
it almost surely, and selected-output disagreement is equivalent to departure
from the shifted singleton optimal face. The statement is for each fixed
$\Delta$; the full argument is in Appendix~\ref{app:geometry}.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:quantitative-margin}
For $r,\gamma,\kappa>0$, $\alpha\geq1$, and $\beta\geq0$,
QSC$(\alpha,\beta;\gamma,\kappa,r)$ holds if, for every effective
displacement $\Delta\in L$ with $0<\|\Delta\|\leq r$,
\begin{equation}
  \Pr\{G_{\Delta}(c)\geq\gamma\|\Delta\|^\alpha\}
  \geq\kappa\|\Delta\|^\beta.
  \label{eq:quantitative-strict-crossing}
\end{equation}
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:quantitative-crossing}
Under Assumption~\ref{assump:centered-symmetric}, QSC$(\alpha,\beta;
\gamma,\kappa,r)$ implies
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c+\Delta)-R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c)
  \geq\gamma\kappa\|\Delta\|^{\alpha+\beta}
  \quad\text{for }\Delta\in L,\ 0<\|\Delta\|\leq r.
  \label{eq:qsc-risk-transfer}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:coercivity-qsc}
Under Assumption~\ref{assump:centered-symmetric}, assume the effective space is
nontrivial, $\dim L\geq1$ (equivalently $D_{S}>0$), and suppose there are
$C,r>0$ and $p\geq1$ such that
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c+\Delta)-R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c)
  \geq C\|\Delta\|^p
  \quad\text{for every }\Delta\in L,\ 0<\|\Delta\|\leq r.
  \label{eq:local-coercivity-hypothesis}
\end{equation}
Then QSC$(p,p-1;\tfrac C2,\tfrac{C}{4D_{S}},r)$ holds.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
See Appendix~\ref{app:quantitative-proofs}.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:qsc-coercivity-equivalence}
Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative-crossing} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:coercivity-qsc} form a two-way relationship at the
level of local polynomial moduli, up to transformed exponents and constants:
QSC$(\alpha,\beta)$ yields growth exponent $p=\alpha+\beta$, while a
growth exponent $p\geq1$ yields QSC$(p,p-1)$.  The universal linear envelope
equation~\ref{eq:universal-gap-envelope} forces $p\geq1$ for any nontrivial
polynomial lower bound.  Strict crossing is the qualitative limit: it asserts
$\mathbb{E}[G_{\Delta}]>0$ for every nonzero $\Delta$, but no modulus.
QSC strengthens this by jointly controlling crossing mass and crossing gap on
the effective quotient directions.
<!-- end remark-4 -->

  \tau_{\mathcal E}
  :=\min_{u\in L:\|u\|=1}\max_{e\in\mathcal E}|a_e^\top u|>0.
  \label{eq:direction-covering-constant}
\end{equation}

<!-- assumption-2: body_md -->
\label{assump:polyhedral-boundary-mass}
Let $k=\dim L\geq1$. Let $\mathcal E_0$ be a finite family of edges of
$S$ whose directions span $L$; equivalently $\tau_{\mathcal E_0}>0$ in
equation~\ref{eq:direction-covering-constant}. The law of $P_Lc$ has density $p$
with respect to $\lambda_L$. For
every $e\in\mathcal E_0$, there are a compact patch
$K_e\Subset\operatorname{relint}(F_e)$ of positive $(k-1)$-dimensional measure, a radius
$\rho_e>0$, and a common constant $m>0$.  With
\begin{equation}
  K_e^{[\rho_e]}:=\{y+h:y\in K_e,\ h\in L_e,
  \ \|h\|\leq\rho_e\},
  \label{eq:tangential-facet-neighborhood}
\end{equation}
assume $K_e^{[\rho_e]}\subset\operatorname{relint}(F_e)$ and, for every
$y\in K_e^{[\rho_e]}$,
\begin{align}
  y+\frac{s}{\|a_e\|^2}a_e&\in\operatorname{int}(C_{v_i})
    &&\text{if }-\rho_e<s<0, \\
  y+\frac{s}{\|a_e\|^2}a_e&\in\operatorname{int}(C_{v_j})
    &&\text{if }0<s<\rho_e.
  \label{eq:two-cone-facet-property}
\end{align}
Finally, $p$ admits a Borel version satisfying, for
$(\mathcal H^{k-1}\!\restriction_{K_e})\otimes\lambda_1$-almost every
$(y,s)\in K_e\times[-\rho_e,\rho_e]$,
\begin{equation}
  p\left(y+\frac{s}{\|a_e\|^2}a_e\right)\geq m.
  \label{eq:polyhedral-boundary-mass}
\end{equation}

\label{thm:polyhedral-quadratic-growth}
Let $S$ be a compact polytope. Under
Assumption~\ref{assump:centered-symmetric} and
Assumption~\ref{assump:polyhedral-boundary-mass}, there exist $C,r>0$ such

\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\label{thm:selection-balanced-identification}
Let $\mu=\mathbb{E}[C]$, $C^\circ=2\mu-C$, and $\mathbb{E}\|C\|<\infty$. If
$\mathbb{E}[w^*(C)]=\mathbb{E}[w^*(C^\circ)]$, then
$\operatorname*{arg\,min}_\theta R_{P,\mathrm{SPO+}}(\theta)=\mu+N$ if and only if

<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For fixed $c$, equation~\ref{eq:spo-plus-loss} is convex in $\hat c$, and
\begin{equation}
  g_c:=2\bigl(w^*(c)-w^*(2\bar c-c)\bigr)
  \in\partial_{\hat c}\ell_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c,c).
  \label{eq:spo-plus-subgradient}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\partial_{\hat c}\xi_{S}(c-2\hat c)=-2W^*(2\hat c-c)$, so
$g_c$ is the sum of $-2w^*(2\bar c-c)$ and the derivative $2w^*(c)$ of
the report-dependent term.  By convexity,
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c,c)
  \ge\ell_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c,c)+g_c^\top(\hat c-\bar c)
  \quad\text{for every }\hat c,c.
  \label{eq:pointwise-subgradient-inequality}
\end{equation}
Both sides are integrable: the loss is bounded by a constant multiple of
$\|c\|+\|\hat c\|+1$ because $S$ is compact, and $g_c$ is bounded
for the same reason.  Taking expectations in
equation~\ref{eq:pointwise-subgradient-inequality} gives
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c)
  \geR_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c)+\mathbb{E}[g_c]^\top(\hat c-\bar c).
  \label{eq:integrated-subgradient-inequality}
\end{equation}
Under Assumption~\ref{assump:centered-symmetric}, $c\overset d=2\bar c-c$,
so the deterministic selections satisfy
$\mathbb{E}[w^*(c)]=\mathbb{E}[w^*(2\bar c-c)]$; hence $\mathbb{E}[g_c]=0$.  Therefore
equation~\ref{eq:integrated-subgradient-inequality} shows
$R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c)\geR_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c)$ for every $\hat c$, proving
$\bar c\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\hat c}R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c)$.  Convexity of
$R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}$ follows from convexity of equation~\ref{eq:spo-plus-loss} in $\hat c$
and linearity of the expectation.

Under Assumption~\ref{assump:centered-symmetric}, for every $\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^d$,
\begin{equation}

<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Subtract equation~\ref{eq:spo-plus-loss} at $\bar c$ from its value at
$\bar c+\Delta$.  Before using symmetry, the difference is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}\bigl[\xi_{S}(c-2\bar c-2\Delta)-\xi_{S}(c-2\bar c)
      +2\Delta^\topw^*(c)\bigr].
  \label{eq:pre-symmetry-excess-algebra}
\end{equation}
Since $c-2\bar c\overset d=-c$, symmetry changes the expectation of the
support-function difference into the corresponding expression at $-c$; the
linear term is unchanged.  Thus
\begin{align}
  R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c+\Delta)-R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c)
  &=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\xi_{S}(-c-2\Delta)-\xi_{S}(-c)

  &=\mathbb{E}\bigl[(c+2\Delta)^\topw^*(c)-z^*(c+2\Delta)\bigr].
  \label{eq:appendix-excess-algebra}
\end{align}
For the second equality, use $\xi_{S}(-q)=-z^*(q)$ and
$c^\topw^*(c)=z^*(c)$. This is equation~\ref{eq:appendix-centered-excess-identity}.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{thm:optimal-set-containment}
Let $S\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ be nonempty, compact, and convex, assume
$\mathbb{E}\|c\|<\infty$, and write $\bar c:=\mathbb{E}[c]$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If $c^*\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\hat c}R_{\mathrm{SPO}}^{\mathrm{set}}(\hat c)$,
  then $W^*(c^*)\subseteqW^*(\bar c)$.
  \item Conversely, if $W^*(c^*)$ is a singleton and
  $W^*(c^*)\subseteqW^*(\bar c)$, then
  $c^*\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\hat c}R_{\mathrm{SPO}}^{\mathrm{set}}(\hat c)$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $F:=W^*(\bar c)$.  We first prove the containment.  If $F$ is a
singleton, write $F=\{e\}$ and take $\tilde c_n=\bar c$.  Otherwise choose an
exposed point $e$ of the compact convex set $F$ relative to its affine hull.
Thus there is $d\in\mathbb{R}^d$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{w\in F}d^\top w=\{e\}.
  \label{eq:exposed-point-in-mean-face}
\end{equation}
For $\epsilon_n\downarrow0$, put $\tilde c_n=\bar c+\epsilon_n d$.

We claim that $W^*(\tilde c_n)$ converges to $\{e\}$ in Hausdorff
distance.  Take any sequence $w_n\inW^*(\tilde c_n)$.  Optimality against
$e$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_n(d^\top e-d^\top w_n)
  \geq \bar c^\top w_n-z^*(\bar c)\geq0,
  \label{eq:exposed-perturbation-sandwich}
\end{equation}
so $d^\top w_n\leq d^\top e$.  Every convergent subsequence has a limit
$w\inW^*(\bar c)=F$: this follows by passing to the limit in
$\tilde c_n^\top w_n\leq\tilde c_n^\top u$ for every $u\inS$.  Equation
equation~\ref{eq:exposed-point-in-mean-face} and the preceding inequality force
$w=e$.  Compactness then implies the asserted Hausdorff convergence.

Let $c^*$ minimize $R_{\mathrm{SPO}}^{\mathrm{set}}$.  Since the support
functions of $W^*(\tilde c_n)$ converge pointwise to that of $\{e\}$ and
are dominated by $M\|c\|$, where $M:=\sup_{w\inS}\|w\|$, dominated
convergence yields
\begin{align}
  0
  &\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}
  \left(R_{\mathrm{SPO}}^{\mathrm{set}}(\tilde c_n)
       -R_{\mathrm{SPO}}^{\mathrm{set}}(c^*)\right) \\
  &=\bar c^\top e
    -\mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{w\inW^*(c^*)}c^\top w\right] \\
  &\leq\bar c^\top e
    -\max_{w\inW^*(c^*)}\bar c^\top w
  \leq0.
  \label{eq:set-valued-containment-chain}
\end{align}
The penultimate inequality is Jensen's inequality for the support function of
$W^*(c^*)$; the last uses
$\bar c^\top e=z^*(\bar c)\leq\bar c^\top w$ for every $w\inS$.
Equality throughout equation~\ref{eq:set-valued-containment-chain} implies
$\bar c^\top w=z^*(\bar c)$ for every $w\inW^*(c^*)$, proving
$W^*(c^*)\subseteqW^*(\bar c)$.

For the converse, write $W^*(c^*)=\{w^*\}\subseteqW^*(\bar c)$.  Then
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{SPO}}^{\mathrm{set}}(c^*)
  =z^*(\bar c)-\mathbb{E}[z^*(c)].
\end{equation}
For arbitrary $\hat c$ and any $w\inW^*(\hat c)$,
\begin{align}
  R_{\mathrm{SPO}}^{\mathrm{set}}(\hat c)
  &\geq \mathbb{E}[c^\top w]-\mathbb{E}[z^*(c)] \\
  &=\bar c^\top w-\mathbb{E}[z^*(c)]
  \geqz^*(\bar c)-\mathbb{E}[z^*(c)],
\end{align}
which proves optimality of $c^*$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:affine-hull-obstruction}
Let $L:=\operatorname{span}(S-S)$.  If $L^\perp$ contains a nonzero vector
$\Delta$, then for every $\hat c,c\in\mathbb{R}^d$,
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c+\Delta,c)=\ell_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c,c).
  \label{eq:pointwise-affine-invariance}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c+\Delta)=R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c)$ for
\emph{every} distribution satisfying the integrability needed to define the
risk, and
\begin{equation}
  W^*(c+2\Delta)=W^*(c)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  G_{\Delta}(c)=0
  \qquad\text{for every }c\in\mathbb{R}^d.
  \label{eq:affine-hull-invisible-direction}
\end{equation}
Thus SC and ambient-space unique identification are impossible for every
distribution.  In particular, full dimensionality is necessary for a
distribution-free sufficient condition that covers all nonzero ambient
directions.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
For $\Delta\in L^\perp$, there is a constant $k=\Delta^\top w$ independent of
$w\inS$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
  \xi_{S}(q-2\Delta)=\xi_{S}(q)-2k
  \quad\text{for every }q\in\mathbb{R}^d.
  \label{eq:support-shift-constant}
\end{equation}
Applying this with $q=c-2\hat c$ and adding the linear report shift,
\begin{align}
  \ell_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c+\Delta,c)
  &=\xi_{S}(c-2\hat c-2\Delta)
    +2(\hat c+\Delta)^\topw^*(c)-z^*(c) \\
  &=\xi_{S}(c-2\hat c)-2k+2\hat c^\topw^*(c)+2k-z^*(c) \\
  &=\ell_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c,c).
\end{align}
This proves equation~\ref{eq:pointwise-affine-invariance}.

To prove optimizer invariance, fix $c\in\mathbb{R}^d$.  For any $w,u\inS$,
$\Delta\in L^\perp$ gives $\Delta^\top(w-u)=0$, hence
\begin{equation}
  (c+2\Delta)^\top(w-u)=c^\top(w-u).
  \label{eq:pairwise-ordering-shift}
\end{equation}
Thus $c$ and $c+2\Delta$ induce exactly the same pairwise objective
differences on $S$, so $W^*(c+2\Delta)=W^*(c)$.  Since
$w^*(c)\inW^*(c)=W^*(c+2\Delta)$, the crossing gap vanishes by
equation~\ref{eq:gap-positive-iff-leaves-face}.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:normal-fan-crossing-representation}
Suppose $S$ is a compact polytope and $P_Lc\ll\lambda_L$. For every fixed
$\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^d$,
\begin{equation}
  \Pr(B_\Delta)
  =\Pr\!\left(
    \bigcup_{v\in V}
    \left\{P_Lc\in\operatorname{int}_L(C_v):
    P_Lc+2P_L\Delta\notin C_v\right\}
  \right).
  \label{eq:normal-fan-crossing-event}
\end{equation}
The union is disjoint up to a null set.  Thus, away from tie strata, strict
crossing is exactly positive probability mass leaving the closed normal cone
of the formerly optimal vertex; landing on a shared boundary does not count
when that vertex remains optimal.

  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad

\end{equation}
Taking the union over the almost-surely unique source cone proves

      p\bigl(T_e(y,s)\bigr)\,

  \label{eq:prism-mass}
\end{equation}

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:local-parameter-recovery}
Suppose that, for some $C,r,p>0$,
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c+\Delta)-R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c)
  \geq C\|\Delta\|^p
  \quad\text{for every }\ \Delta\in L
  \text{ with }0<\|\Delta\|\leq r.
  \label{eq:generic-local-growth}
\end{equation}
Write
$\overline B_L(\bar c,r):=\{\bar c+\Delta:\Delta\in L,
\ \|\Delta\|\leq r\}$ for the canonical effective slice.  Then every
$\hat c\in\overline B_L(\bar c,r)$ obeys
\begin{equation}
  \|\hat c-\bar c\|
  \leq C^{-1/p}\bigl(R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c)-R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c)\bigr)^{1/p}.
  \label{eq:local-inverse-risk}
\end{equation}
Let $\widehat R_n$ be an empirical criterion and let a (possibly
random) $\hat c_n\in\overline B_L(\bar c,r)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \widehat R_n(\hat c_n)\leq\widehat R_n(\bar c)+\eta_n.
  \label{eq:approximate-local-erm}
\end{equation}
On any event on which
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{\theta\in\overline B_L(\bar c,r)}
  |\widehat R_n(\theta)-R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\theta)|\leq\varepsilon_n,
  \label{eq:local-uniform-deviation}
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}
  \|\hat c_n-\bar c\|
  \leq\left(\frac{2\varepsilon_n+\eta_n}{C}\right)^{1/p}.
  \label{eq:local-erm-parameter-rate}
\end{equation}
This statement is deterministic conditional on the local uniform-deviation
event equation~\ref{eq:local-uniform-deviation} and on local membership
$\hat c_n\in\overline B_L(\bar c,r)$; it is not a standalone finite-sample
rate theorem.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
If $\hat c=\bar c$, equation~\ref{eq:local-inverse-risk} is immediate. Otherwise,
apply equation~\ref{eq:generic-local-growth} with $\Delta=\hat c-\bar c$ and take
$p$th roots. On the event equation~\ref{eq:local-uniform-deviation},
\begin{align}
  R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\hat c_n)-R_{\mathrm{SPO+}}(\bar c)
  &\leq \widehat R_n(\hat c_n)-\widehat R_n(\bar c)+2\varepsilon_n \\
  &\leq \eta_n+2\varepsilon_n.
\end{align}
Apply equation~\ref{eq:local-inverse-risk} to obtain
equation~\ref{eq:local-erm-parameter-rate}.

\label{thm:fenchel-transition-identity}
Suppose $c\overset d=2\bar c-c$ and, for every $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^m$, each of

  \label{eq:fenchel-transition-identity}
\end{equation}
where $D_{\Phi}(x,y;s(y)):=\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)-s(y)^\top(x-y)$.
