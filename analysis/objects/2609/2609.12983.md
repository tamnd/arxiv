<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:intro_main}
Fix a lattice path $\nu$, and let $L_1$ and $L_2$ be two alt~$\nu$-Tamari lattices. Then rowmotion behaves the same for $L_1$ and $L_2$. That is, there is a bijection $\Phi\colon L_1 \to L_2$ such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Phi( \operatorname{Row} (x) ) = \operatorname{Row} (\Phi (x))$ for all $x \in L_1$;
\item $\sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\geq 0\}} \operatorname{ddeg}(y) = \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\geq 0\}} \operatorname{ddeg}(\Phi(y))$ for all $x \in L_1$.
\end{itemize}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:intervals}
Let $L$ be a semidistributive and extremal lattice. Then any interval in $L$ is also a semidistributive and extremal lattice.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
All sublattices of a semidistributive lattice are semidistributive by the definition of semidistributivity in terms of meets and joins. In particular, any interval of a semidistributive lattice is also semidistributive. By \cite[Theorem~1.4]{thomas2019rowmotion}, a finite semidistributive lattice is extremal if and only if it is \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{trim}}; moreover, \cite[Theorem~3.6]{thomas2019rowmotion} asserts that any interval of a finite trim lattice is again trim.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
Note crucially however that if $L'$ is an interval of $L$, then $\operatorname{Row} \colon L' \to L'$ is \emph{not} just the restriction of $\operatorname{Row} \colon L \to L$; indeed, rowmotion on $L'$ is basically unrelated to rowmotion on $L$.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
Let $L$ be a semidistributive and extremal lattice, and let $z_{N+1}\lessdot z_{N}\lessdot \cdots z_2 \lessdot z_1$ be a maximum-length chain of $L$, where $N=\ell(L)=|\mathcal J_L|=|\mathcal M_L|$. Then
\[\operatorname{Row}=\tau_{j_N}\circ\cdots\circ\tau_{j_1},\]
where $j_i \coloneqq j_{z_{i+1},z_{i}}$ for $i=1,\ldots,N$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
Consider a distributive lattice $L$, which we identify with the lattice $J(P)$ of order ideals of a poset $P$. For each $p\in P$, let $\Lambda(p)=\{q\in P:q\leq p\}$ be the order ideal generated by~$p$. The map $p\mapsto \Lambda(p)$ is a bijection from $P$ to $\mathcal J_L$. If $u\lessdot v$ is a cover relation in $L$, then there is a unique $r\in P$ such that $v=u\sqcup\{r\}$, and the join-irreducible element $j_{u,v}$ is $\Lambda(r)$. Thus, the toggles~$\tau_p \coloneqq \tau_{\Lambda(p)}\colon L \to L$ for $p \in P$ are given by 
\[\tau_{p}(I)=\begin{cases}
    I\triangle\{p\} & \text{if } I\triangle \{p\} \in J(P); \\
    I & \text{otherwise}, 
\end{cases}\]
where $\triangle$ denotes symmetric difference. And the slow motion description of rowmotion on $L$ is~$\operatorname{Row} = \tau_{p_N} \circ \cdots \circ \tau_{p_1}$, where $p_N, \ldots, p_1$ is any linear extension of $P$ \cite{cameron1995orbits}. This ``row-by-row'' way to compute rowmotion is where the name comes from; see~\cite{striker2012rowmotion}.
<!-- end example-1 -->

There are four join-irreducible elements $j_1,j_2,j_3,j_4$, and we have labeled each Hasse diagram edge with its canonical join-irreducible label. The indexing of the join-irreducible elements comes from the maximum-length chain along the left side of the figure: the labels of the edges going up the chain are $j_4,j_3,j_2,j_1$ (in this order). \begin{align*}
\operatorname{Row}(j_2)&=(\tau_{j_4}\circ\tau_{j_3}\circ\tau_{j_2}\circ\tau_{j_1})(j_2) \\
 &=(\tau_{j_4}\circ\tau_{j_3}\circ\tau_{j_2})(j_1) \\ 
 &=(\tau_{j_4}\circ\tau_{j_3})(j_1) \\ 
 &=\tau_{j_4}(j_1) =\hat 1. 
\end{align*}

<!-- example-3: body_md -->
\label{ex:stanley_tamari}
Let $n \geq 1$. Recall from~\cref{sec:intro} that the \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{Stanley lattice}}, $\operatorname{Stan}({n})$, is the lattice of Dyck paths of length $2n$ ordered by nesting. It is a distributive lattice on $\operatorname{Cat}(n)$-many elements, where $\operatorname{Cat}(n) \coloneqq \frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}$ is the $n$th \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{Catalan number}}. Meanwhile, the \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{Tamari lattice}}, $\operatorname{Tam}({n})$, is another lattice on $\operatorname{Cat}(n)$-many elements, whose cover relations can be described in terms of flips in triangulations or rotations of trees. For a precise definition of $\operatorname{Tam}({n})$, see \cref{sec:alt} below. It is known that $\operatorname{Tam}({n})$ is a semidistributive and extremal lattice, so it has a rowmotion operation defined on it, and in fact one that can be computed in slow motion. As mentioned in~\cref{sec:intro}, rowmotion has the same orbit structure on $\operatorname{Stan}({n})$ and $\operatorname{Tam}({n})$; for~$n=3$, this is depicted in~\cref{fig:5-element-lattices}. One way to relate rowmotion on $\operatorname{Stan}({n})$ and $\operatorname{Tam}({n})$ is via another operation on a third Catalan lattice. Namely, the \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{noncrossing partition lattice}} is the collection of set partitions of~$\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$ whose blocks are noncrossing when the numbers $1,2,\ldots,n$ are arranged clockwise around a circle, partially ordered by refinement. It is known that this lattice is complemented, and one particular choice of complement, whose square is rotation, is the \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{Kreweras complement}}. It is known that both rowmotion on the Stanley lattice~\cite{armstrong2013uniform}, and rowmotion on the Tamari lattice~\cite{barnard2019canonical}, have the same orbit structure as Kreweras complement of noncrossing partitions. This is also depicted, in the case $n=3$, in \cref{fig:5-element-lattices}.
<!-- end example-3 -->
<!-- example-4: body_md -->
The case of $\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta)$ where $\delta_i = \nu_i$ for all $1 \leq i \leq n$ recovers the $\nu$-Tamari lattice~$\operatorname{Tam}({\nu})$ of Pr\'eville-Ratelle and Viennot~\cite{preville2017nutamari}. (In turn, for appropriate choices of $\nu$, the $\nu$-Tamari lattices $\operatorname{Tam}({\nu})$ recover the \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{$m$-Tamari lattices}}~\cite{bergeron2012higher} and \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{rational Tamari lattices}}; see also~\cref{sec:rational} below.) On the other hand, setting $\delta_i = 0$ for all $i$, $\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta)$ becomes the distributive lattice of $\nu$-Dyck paths ordered by nesting, sometimes called the $\nu$-Dyck lattice. In particular, when~$\nu=(NE)^n$ is the staircase path, we get the classical Tamari and Stanley lattices, $\operatorname{Tam}({n})$ and $\operatorname{Stan}({n})$, respectively. Moreover, for this staircase $\nu$, the alt~$\nu$-Tamari lattices provide $2^{n-1}$ semidistributive lattices on Catalan many elements interpolating between the Tamari and Stanley lattices; see~\cref{fig:alt_tamaris_14}.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:rowmotion_slow_motion}
Label the boxes of~$F_{\delta,\nu}$ with the numbers $1,2,\dots,\ell$ from top to bottom and right to left, i.e., in ``Arabic/Hebrew reading order.'' Then we have that rowmotion on the alt $\nu$-Tamari lattice $\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta)$ is the following composition of $\delta$-toggles: 
\[ \operatorname{Row}(T)=\tau_\ell \circ \dots \circ \tau_2 \circ \tau_1(T). \]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
It suffices to see that there is a chain of maximal length whose sequence of labels reads $\ell, \ell - 1, \dots, 1$ from bottom to top.
    
Recall that the smallest $(\delta, \nu)$-tree corresponds to the path $\nu$ under the flushing bijection. Each north step of $\nu$ is followed by $\nu_i$ east steps, and we have $\delta_i \leq \nu_i$. Let $k$ be the index of the first non-initial north step of $\nu$, that is, the leftmost north step of $\nu$ involved in a valley. The rotation of $\nu$ at this leftmost valley will be equivalent to changing this valley into a peak. In this case, the area under the path has increased by exactly $1$.

Moreover, for each $i \geq k$, the $i$-th north step is still followed by at least $\delta_i$ east steps. Hence, we can further proceed to rotate at the leftmost valley of the resulting path, and we obtain a maximal chain from the bottom element $\nu$ to the top element of the lattice, where each cover relation increases the area by $1$. Thus, this is a chain of length $\ell$, hence of maximal length. Finally, under the flushing bijection, the sequence of box labels along this chain is precisely $\ell, \ell - 1, \dots, 1$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Our description of $\varphi$ is purely combinatorial. Nevertheless, we would like to point out that its discovery was motivated by the underlying geometric structure of the alt $\nu$-Tamari lattice. Its Hasse diagram is the edge graph of a polytopal complex induced by an arrangement of tropical hyperplanes~\cite{ceballos2024canonical}. When $\delta=(\delta_1,\dots,\delta_n)$ with $\delta_{n-1}\neq 0$, decreasing the last nonzero entry~$\delta_{n-1}$ by one corresponds to ``moving'' one of the hyperplanes in the arrangement. The map $\varphi$ is then obtained by relabeling the vertices accordingly. Two examples are illustrated in \cref{fig:tropical_varphi,fig:tropical_varphi2}, where the node labels represent rowmotion orbits (cf.~\cref{fig:alt_tamaris_14}). This yields the combinatorial description of~$\varphi$ in this particular case, which we subsequently extended to the general setting.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
In the case of a staircase lattice path $\nu$, it is also possible to think of the map $\varphi$ as a ``piece'' of the map $D$ (or rather its inverse) from Striker--Williams~\cite[Theorem 5.4]{striker2012rowmotion} that conjugates rowmotion to promotion.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:row_varphi_commute}
We have $\widetilde \operatorname{Row} \circ \varphi = \varphi \circ \operatorname{Row}$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:varphi_widetilde_varphi}
We have $ \varphi = \operatorname{flush} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}} = \widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{up}} \circ \operatorname{flush}$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $\varphi \coloneqq \operatorname{flush} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}$ and $\widetilde \varphi \coloneqq \widetilde \tau^{\operatorname{up}} \circ \operatorname{flush}$. Let $T$ be a $(\delta,\nu)$-tree. Observe that the nodes of $T$ lying strictly below the line separating $F^{\operatorname{down}}$ and $F^{\operatorname{up}}$ are unaffected by both $\operatorname{flush}$ and $\tau^{\operatorname{up}}$. Thus, it suffices to compare the actions of $\varphi$ and $\widetilde{\varphi}$ on the nodes contained in $F^{\operatorname{up}}$.

Since $F^{\operatorname{up}}$ terminates on the right with a full vertical step, the restriction of $T$ to $F^{\operatorname{up}}$ behaves as in the classical $\nu$-Dyck lattice setting, except that certain columns are forbidden. More precisely, for every node of $T$ in the lower part, the column directly above it must be empty unless the node is the leftmost node in its row. Ignoring these forbidden columns, the nodes of $T$ contained in $F^{\operatorname{up}}$ form a northwest lattice path from the lower-right corner to the upper-left corner.

Under this identification, the action of $\tau^{\operatorname{up}}$ agrees with rowmotion on a $\nu$-Dyck lattice. In this case, rowmotion exchanges valleys and peaks: a northwest path has a valley at $(i,j)$ if and only if its image under rowmotion has a peak at $(i+1,j+1)$.

On the other hand, the map $\operatorname{flush}$ keeps track of the forbidden columns and moves each node to the first available position to its left. This operation does not change the underlying northwest path; it only changes the columns in which the nodes are placed. Consequently, $\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{up}}(\operatorname{flush}(T))$ is obtained from $\tau^{\operatorname{up}}(T)$ by shifting each node to the first available position to its left (equivalently, applying $\operatorname{flush}$). This is exactly the action of $\varphi$, and therefore $\varphi(T)=\widetilde{\varphi}(T)$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:varphi_tau_up_commute}
We have $\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{up}} \circ \varphi = \varphi \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By \cref{lem:varphi_widetilde_varphi}, we have $\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{up}} \circ \operatorname{flush} = \operatorname{flush} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}$. Therefore,
\begin{align*}
\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{up}} \circ \varphi
&= \widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{up}} \circ (\operatorname{flush} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}) \\
&= (\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{up}} \circ \operatorname{flush}) \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}} \\
&= (\operatorname{flush} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}) \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}} \\
&= \varphi \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}. \qedhere
\end{align*}
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:varphi_map} 
Let $T$ be a $(\delta,\nu)$-tree and let $\widetilde T \coloneqq \varphi(T)$. Then~$T$ and $\widetilde T$ coincide on the vertical part $F^{\operatorname{vert}}=\widetilde F^{\operatorname{vert}}$, except for the nodes belonging to the upper part. More precisely, for each $i\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}$ and every node of $T$ at height $h_i$, there is a node at height $h_{i-1}+1$ in $\widetilde T$ within the same column.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The map $\varphi=\operatorname{flush} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}$ is obtained by applying rowmotion to the northwest path determined by the nodes of $T$ in $F^{\operatorname{up}}$, and then shifting each resulting node to the first available non-forbidden position to its left.

Under rowmotion, a valley of the path at height $h_i$ is transformed into a peak one unit above and one unit to the right. Therefore, the subsequent left shift places the corresponding node in the same column and at height $h_{i}+1$. In~\cref{fig:varphi_map}, the resulting path with peaks at these positions is shown in blue. Consequently, for every peak at height $h_i$ in $T$ there is a peak at height $h_{i-1}+1$ in $\widetilde T$ within the same column. This property also holds for the positions of the nodes on the horizontal lines of the restriction of $T$ and $\widetilde T$ to the vertical part. The other nodes (coming from north steps) are completely determined.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:taudown_varphi}
Let $T$ be a $(\delta,\nu)$-tree and let $\widetilde T\coloneqq \varphi(T)$. Set $T'\coloneqq \tau^{\operatorname{down}}(T)$ and $ \widetilde T' \coloneqq \widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}}(\widetilde T)$. Then $T'$ and $\widetilde T'$ coincide on the vertical part $F^{\operatorname{vert}} =\widetilde F^{\operatorname{vert}}$, except for the nodes belonging to the upper part. More precisely, for each $i\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}$ and every node of $T'$ at height $h_i$, there is a node at height $h_{i-1}+1$ in $\widetilde T'$ within the same column.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
For simplicity, let ($\star$) denote the property stated in the lemma, relating a pair of trees~$(S,\widetilde S)$. By \cref{lem:varphi_map}, the pair $(T,\widetilde T)$ satisfies ($\star$). Since $\tau^{\operatorname{down}}$ and $\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}}$ are compositions of toggles associated with boxes in the lower part, it suffices to show that ($\star$) is preserved by a single toggle.

If a toggle is the identity, then property ($\star$) is clearly preserved. If not, we consider two cases. If a toggle does not involve any node in the upper part, then it acts identically on both trees and~($\star$) is clearly preserved.

Otherwise, the toggle exchanges the lower-left and upper-right corners of a rectangle $R$ whose bottom lies strictly in the lower part and whose top lies at height $h_i$ in the upper part, for some $i$. The corresponding toggle on $\widetilde T$ exchanges the corners of a rectangle $\widetilde R$ whose bottom coincides with that of $R$ and whose top lies at height $h_{i-1}+1$. Since ($\star$) precisely states that nodes at height $h_i$ in one tree correspond to nodes at height $h_{i-1}+1$ in the other, the effect of the toggle is compatible with the correspondence. Hence, if $(T,\widetilde T)$ satisfies ($\star$), then so does $(\tau(T),\widetilde{\tau}(\widetilde T))$.

It follows by induction over the sequence of toggles defining $\tau^{\operatorname{down}}$ and $\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}}$ that $(T',\widetilde T')$ satisfies property~($\star$).
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:varphi_Tprime}
Let $T$ be a $(\delta,\nu)$-tree and let $\widetilde T \coloneqq \varphi(T)$. Set $T' \coloneqq \tau^{\operatorname{down}}(T)$ and $\widetilde T' \coloneqq \widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}}(\widetilde T)$. Then $\varphi(T')$ and $\widetilde T'$ coincide on the vertical part $F^{\operatorname{vert}}=\widetilde F^{\operatorname{vert}}$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
By~\cref{lem:taudown_varphi}, the pair $(T',\widetilde T')$ satisfies the same displacement property as $(T,\widetilde T)$: for each~$i\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}$, every node of $T'$ at height $h_i$ corresponds to a node of $\widetilde T'$ at height $h_{i-1}+1$ in the same column. Applying \cref{lem:varphi_map} to $T'$ therefore moves every node of $T'$ at height $h_i$ precisely to height $h_{i-1}+1$. Hence the image $\varphi(T')$ coincides with $\widetilde T'$ on $F^{\operatorname{vert}}=\widetilde F^{\operatorname{vert}}$.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:varphi_taudown_commute_verticalpart}
Let $T$ be a $(\delta,\nu)$-tree. Then $\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \varphi(T)$ and $ \varphi \circ \tau^{\operatorname{down}}(T)$ coincide on the vertical part $F^{\operatorname{vert}} = \widetilde F^{\operatorname{vert}}$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Let $\widetilde T\coloneqq \varphi(T)$, $T'\coloneqq \tau^{\operatorname{down}}(T)$, and $\widetilde T'\coloneqq\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}}(\widetilde T)$. By \cref{lem:varphi_Tprime}, the trees $\varphi(T')=\varphi\circ\tau^{\operatorname{down}}(T)$ and $\widetilde T' = \widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \varphi(T)$ coincide on the vertical part $F^{\operatorname{vert}}=\widetilde F^{\operatorname{vert}}$.
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:varphi_tau_down_commute}
We have $\widetilde\tau^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \varphi = \varphi \circ \tau^{\operatorname{down}}$.
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $T$ be a $(\delta,\nu)$-tree. By \cref{lem:varphi_taudown_commute_verticalpart}, the trees $\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \varphi(T)$ and $\varphi \circ \tau^{\operatorname{down}}(T)$ coincide on the vertical part. It therefore remains to compare the nodes outside the vertical part. Since~$\tau^{\operatorname{down}}$ and~$\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}}$ are compositions of toggles supported in the lower part, neither map affects any node outside the vertical part. Moreover, the action of $\varphi$ outside the vertical part is independent of whether~$\tau^{\operatorname{down}}$ has been applied beforehand. Consequently, the two compositions also coincide outside the vertical part. We conclude that $\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \varphi(T) =
\varphi \circ \tau^{\operatorname{down}}(T)$, which proves the proposition.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
We have
\begin{align*}
\widetilde{\operatorname{Row}} \circ \varphi &= (\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{up}}) \circ \varphi \\
&= \widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}} \circ (\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{up}} \circ \varphi) \\
&= \widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}} \circ (\varphi \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}) \qquad\text{by \cref{prop:varphi_tau_up_commute}} \\
&= (\widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \varphi) \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}} \\
&= (\varphi \circ \tau^{\operatorname{down}}) \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}} \qquad\text{by \cref{prop:varphi_tau_down_commute}} \\
&= \varphi \circ (\tau^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}) \\
&= \varphi \circ \operatorname{Row}. \qedhere
\end{align*}
<!-- end proof-11 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
Note that the flush bijection, by itself, does \emph{not} intertwine rowmotion, since  
\[\widetilde{\operatorname{Row}} \circ \operatorname{flush} = \widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \widetilde{\tau}^{\operatorname{up}} \circ \operatorname{flush} = \operatorname{flush} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{down}} \neq \operatorname{flush} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}} = \operatorname{flush} \circ \operatorname{Row}.\]
Here we are using the fact that $\tau^{\operatorname{up}}$ and $\tau^{\operatorname{down}}$ do not commute in general.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:varphi_udeg}
The bijection $\varphi\colon \operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta) \to \operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\widetilde{\delta})$ preserves orbit averages of the up-degree statistic. More precisely,
\[ \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(y) = \sum_{y \in \{\widetilde{\operatorname{Row}}^j(\varphi(x))\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(y) \]
for every $x \in \operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta)$.
<!-- end theorem-5 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:varphi_ddeg}
The bijection $\varphi\colon \operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta) \to \operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\widetilde{\delta})$ preserves orbit averages of the down-degree statistic. More precisely,
\[ \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{ddeg}(y) = \sum_{y \in \{\widetilde{\operatorname{Row}}^j(\varphi(x))\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{ddeg}(y) \]
for every $x \in \operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta)$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
By \cref{thm:varphi_udeg}, it suffices to use the identity $\operatorname{ddeg}(y)=\operatorname{udeg}(\operatorname{Row}^{-1}(y))$, which holds for every element $y$. Indeed,
\begin{align*}
\sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{ddeg}(y) &= \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(\operatorname{Row}^{-1}(y)) \\
&= \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(y),
\end{align*}
since the orbit of $y$ coincides with the orbit of $\operatorname{Row}^{-1}(y)$. Applying \cref{thm:varphi_udeg}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(y) &= \sum_{y \in \{\widetilde{\operatorname{Row}}^j(\varphi(x))\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(y) \\
&= \sum_{y \in \{\widetilde{\operatorname{Row}}^j(\varphi(x))\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(\widetilde{\operatorname{Row}}^{-1}(y)) \\
&= \sum_{y \in \{\widetilde{\operatorname{Row}}^j(\varphi(x))\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{ddeg}(y),
\end{align*}
where the last equality follows from the analogous identity $\operatorname{ddeg}(y)= \operatorname{udeg}(\widetilde{\operatorname{Row}}^{-1}(y))$ on $\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\widetilde{\delta})$. This proves the claim.
<!-- end proof-12 -->
<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:udeg_invariance}
The following statements hold for every $(\delta,\nu)$-tree $T$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \label{item:udeg_invariance_down} The maps $\operatorname{flush}$ and $\tau^{\operatorname{up}}$ preserve the lower up-degree:
\[\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}(T) = \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}(\operatorname{flush}(T)),\]
and
\[\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}(T) = \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}(\tau^{\operatorname{up}}(T)).\]
\item \label{item:udeg_invariance_up} The maps $\operatorname{flush}$ and $\tau^{\operatorname{down}}$ preserve the upper up-degree:
\[\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}(T) = \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}(\operatorname{flush}(T)),\]
and
\[\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}(T) = \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}(\tau^{\operatorname{down}}(T)).\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
The nodes of $T$ that can be increasingly flipped are precisely the leftmost nodes in rows containing at least two nodes, excluding the top row. Since $\operatorname{flush}$ preserves the number of nodes at each height, it preserves the number of rows whose leftmost node can be increasingly flipped. Therefore, $\operatorname{flush}$ preserves both $\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}$ and $\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}$.

Next, $\tau^{\operatorname{up}}$ is a composition of toggles corresponding to boxes in $F^{\operatorname{up}}$. Such toggles do not affect the configuration of nodes strictly below the separation line between $F^{\operatorname{up}}$ and $F^{\operatorname{down}}$. Therefore, $\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}$ is preserved under $\tau^{\operatorname{up}}$.

Similarly, $\tau^{\operatorname{down}}$ is a composition of toggles corresponding to boxes in $F^{\operatorname{down}}$. It therefore suffices to show that each such toggle preserves $\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}$. A toggle in $F^{\operatorname{down}}$ either acts trivially on $F^{\operatorname{up}}$ or adds or removes a node that is not the leftmost node of its row. Consequently, it does not affect the set of increasingly flippable nodes in $F^{\operatorname{up}}$, and hence preserves $\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}$.
<!-- end proof-13 -->
<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{prop:udeg_split}
For every $(\delta,\nu)$-tree $T$, the map $\varphi$ preserves the lower up-degree, while the upper up-degree is transformed according to rowmotion:
\[\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}(\varphi(T)) = \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}(T); \qquad \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}(\varphi(T)) = \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}(\operatorname{Row}(T)).\]
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
By definition, $\varphi(T) = \operatorname{flush}(\tau^{\operatorname{up}}(T))$. Both $\tau^{\operatorname{up}}$ and $\operatorname{flush}$ preserve the lower up-degree by~\cref{lem:udeg_invariance}\eqref{item:udeg_invariance_down}. Hence,
\[\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}(\varphi(T)) = \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}(T).\]
Moreover, using that rowmotion decomposes as $\operatorname{Row} = \tau^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\varphi(T) &= \operatorname{flush}(\tau^{\operatorname{up}}(T)) \\
&= \operatorname{flush} \circ (\tau^{\operatorname{down}})^{-1} \bigl(\tau^{\operatorname{down}} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}(T)\bigr) \\
&= \operatorname{flush} \circ (\tau^{\operatorname{down}})^{-1}(\operatorname{Row}(T)).
\end{align*}
Both $(\tau^{\operatorname{down}})^{-1}$ and $\operatorname{flush}$ preserve the upper up-degree by \cref{lem:udeg_invariance}\eqref{item:udeg_invariance_up}. Therefore,
\[\operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}(\varphi(T)) = \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}(\operatorname{Row}(T)),\]
as desired.
<!-- end proof-14 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Since $\varphi$ intertwines rowmotion by \cref{thm:row_varphi_commute}, we have
\[\sum_{y \in \{\widetilde{\operatorname{Row}}^j(\varphi(x))\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(y) = \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(\varphi(y)).\]
Moreover, 
\begin{align*}
\sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(\varphi(y)) &= \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}(\varphi(y)) + \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}(\varphi(y)) \\
&= \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}(y) + \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}(\operatorname{Row}(y)) \\
&= \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{down}}(y) + \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}^{\operatorname{up}}(y) \\
&= \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(y),
\end{align*}
where in the second equality we used \cref{prop:udeg_split}, and in the third equality we reindexed the orbit of rowmotion for the second term. Combining the two displayed identities yields
\[\sum_{y \in \{\widetilde{\operatorname{Row}}^j(\varphi(x))\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(y) = \sum_{y \in \{\operatorname{Row}^j(x)\colon j\ge 0\}} \operatorname{udeg}(y), \]
as required.
<!-- end proof-15 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
A careful analysis of our proof of \cref{cor:varphi_ddeg} shows that $\varphi$ preserves not just the rowmotion orbit sums of the down-degree statistic, but also the rowmotion orbit sums of the refined statistics where we keep track of the number of descent boxes in each row separately.
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Let $L_1$ and $L_2$ be two alt $\nu$-Tamari lattices; say $L_1=\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta_1)$ and $L_2=\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta_2)$. Let $\delta_{\min} \coloneqq (0,0,\ldots,0)$, so that $\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta_{\min})$ is the distributive $\nu$-Dyck lattice. Define a sequence $\delta_1=\delta^1,\delta^2,\ldots,\delta^m=\delta_{\min}$ of increment vectors so that $\delta^{i+1}$ is obtained from $\delta^{i}$ by decrementing by one the last nonzero entry, and let $\varphi_i\colon \operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta^i)\to\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta^{i+1})$ be the corresponding $\varphi$ from above. Then define $\Phi_1\colon\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta_1)\to\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta_{\min})$ as the composition $\Phi_1\coloneqq \varphi_{m-1}\circ \cdots \circ \varphi_1$. Similarly, define $\Phi_2\colon\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta_2)\to\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta_{\min})$. Finally, let $\Phi\colon L_1=\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta_1) \to \operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta_2) = L_2$ be $\Phi\coloneqq (\Phi_2)^{-1}\circ\Phi_1$. Each of the constituent $\phi$'s (and their inverses) intertwines rowmotion by~\cref{thm:row_varphi_commute} and has the required interaction with the down-degree statistic by~\cref{cor:varphi_ddeg}. So $\Phi$ does as well, proving the theorem.

<!-- remark-7: body_md -->
The proof we have given of \cref{thm:intro_main} requires us to compose many of the $\varphi$'s (and their inverses) to define the bijection $\Phi$ between two alt $\nu$-Tamari lattices $L_1$ and $L_2$. It could be useful to have a simpler description of $\Phi$. We believe this is sometimes possible. For example, suppose that $\widetilde{\delta}$ is obtained from $\delta$ by decrementing \emph{some} nonzero entry by one, but not necessarily the last nonzero entry. Then we believe that basically the same definition of $\varphi\colon \operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\delta)\to\operatorname{Tam}_{\nu}(\widetilde\delta)$ as $\varphi\coloneqq \operatorname{flush} \circ \tau^{\operatorname{up}}$ should work, as long as one carefully and correctly defines the upper-part $F^{\operatorname{up}}$. The reason we preferred to consider only the case where $\widetilde{\delta}$ is obtained from $\delta$ by decrementing the \emph{last} nonzero entry is because then the $\delta$-toggles corresponding to boxes in $F^{\operatorname{up}}$ behave essentially as distributive lattice toggles, which are easier to analyze. Nevertheless, the more general $\varphi$'s we sketched in this remark could be useful when considering the extension to the cross Tamari case (see \cref{sec:cross}).

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
For any coprime integers $a<b$, the order of rowmotion acting on any $(a,b)$ rational alt Tamari lattice $L$ divides $a+b-1$. Moreover, the triple $(L,\mathbb{Z}/(a+b-1)\mathbb{Z},\operatorname{Cat}_q(a,b))$ exhibits cyclic sieving, where the generator of $\mathbb{Z}/(a+b-1)\mathbb{Z}$ acts on $L$ as $\operatorname{Row}$.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
By \cref{thm:intro_main}, it is enough to prove this in the distributive case, i.e., for the lattice of~$(a,b)$-rational Dyck paths ordered by nesting. The proof of the desired cyclic sieving result for this distributive lattice is basically explained in~\cite[\S7.5]{thomas2019rowmotion}, but let us give more details here.

As explained in \cite[\S5.1]{armstrong2013rational}, the set of $(a,b)$-rational Dyck paths is in bijection with a certain collection of set partitions of $[a+b-1]=\{1,2,\ldots,a+b-1\}$ called \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{$(a,b)$-homogeneous noncrossing set partitions}}. Let us review this construction. Let $D$ be an $(a,b)$-rational Dyck path. Label its steps $1,2,\ldots,a+b$ in order from the lower-left to upper-right corners of the $a\times b$ rectangle. Slightly above each north step, draw a diagonal line with the same slope as the line connecting the lower-left and upper-right corners of the $a\times b$ rectangle. This divides the labels $1,2,\ldots,a+b-1$ into a noncrossing set partition $\mu$ with exactly $a$ blocks (we ignore the label $a+b$). See \cref{fig:rational_dyck_path} for an example in the case $(a,b)=(5,8)$. In~\cite[\S5.1]{armstrong2013rational} it is proved that this map $D\mapsto \mu$ is injective, and the image is called the set of $(a,b)$-homogeneous noncrossing set partitions. (Note that, in the classical Catalan case $(a,b)=(n,n+1)$, the $(a,b)$-homogeneous noncrossing set partitions are exactly the \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{noncrossing perfect matchings}} of $[2n]$.)

Moreover, in~\cite[\S5.2]{armstrong2013rational}, Armstrong, Rhoades, and Williams show that \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{promotion}} of the rational Dyck path $D$ corresponds to \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{rotation}} of the homogeneous noncrossing partition $\mu$. Here promotion of Dyck paths is a certain ``left-to-right'' composition of the distributive lattice toggles. The by now well-understood technique of Striker and Williams~\cite{striker2012rowmotion} allows one to show that this ``left-to-right'' composition of toggles (i.e., promotion) is conjugate, in the group generated by toggles, to the ``top-to-bottom'' composition of toggles (i.e., rowmotion). Hence, rowmotion in the distributive lattice of rational Dyck paths is in equivariant bijection with rotation of homogeneous noncrossing partitions. This implies that the order of rowmotion divides $a+b-1$.

Finally, we conclude by noting that Bodnar and Rhoades~\cite[Theorem 5.4]{bodnar2016rational} proved that rotation of $(a,b)$ homogeneous noncrossing partitions exhibits cyclic sieving with the sieving polynomial being the rational $q$-Catalan number $\operatorname{Cat}_q(a,b)$. This completes the proof.

<!-- theorem-7: body_md -->
\label{thm:homomesy}
For any $n,m\geq 1$, and for any alt $m$-Tamari lattice $L$, the down-degree statistic $\operatorname{ddeg}$ is homomesic with respect to the action of rowmotion on $L$, with average $\frac{m(n-1)}{m+1}$.
<!-- end theorem-7 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
As indicated, this is an extension of \cite[Theorem 5.14]{defant2024tamari}, which addressed the case of the~$m$-Tamari lattice. By \cref{thm:intro_main}, this case implies all the other cases. However, here we supply an alternative proof, where we reduce instead to the distributive case, i.e., the lattice of $(n,nm+1)$ rational Dyck paths ordered by nesting. In this case, \cite[Corollary 3.11]{chan2017expected} implies the desired rowmotion homomesy result, because these rational Dyck paths are exactly those inside the partition shape $\lambda=(m(n-1),m(n-2),\ldots,m)$, which is a \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{balanced}} shape in the sense of that paper.
<!-- end proof-18 -->
<!-- conjecture-1: body_md -->
\label{conj:homomesy}
For any coprime integers $a<b$ and any $(a,b)$-rational alt Tamari lattice $L$, the down-degree statistic $\operatorname{ddeg}$ is homomesic with respect to the action of rowmotion on $L$, with average $\frac{(a-1)(b-1)}{a+b-1}$.
<!-- end conjecture-1 -->
<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
The proof we gave of \cref{thm:homomesy} does not work for \cref{conj:homomesy}, because, outside of the Fuss setting, the partition shapes corresponding to rational Dyck paths are not balanced. Moreover, the technique of~\cite{chan2017expected} (which was further developed in \cite{defant2023homomesy}), in which one writes the down-degree statistic as a (constant plus a) linear combination of \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{toggleability statistics}}~\cite{striker2015tog}, cannot possibly work in the general rational setting, because at this level of generality the down-degree statistic is \emph{not} a (constant plus a) linear combination of toggleability statistics.
<!-- end remark-8 -->
<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
It is well-known that the Hasse diagram of the classical Tamari lattice is the one-skeleton of a simple convex polytope, namely, the associahedron. In particular, this means that the Hasse diagram of the classical Tamari lattice is regular. Hence, for any element $x\in \operatorname{Tam}({n+1})$, we will have that $\operatorname{ddeg}(\operatorname{Row}(x))+\operatorname{ddeg}(x)=n$, since the up-degree of an element is the down-degree of its image under rowmotion. This yields a very quick proof of down-degree homomesy for rowmotion of the classical Tamari lattice. But this argument does not extend to the rational Tamari lattices, which do not have regular Hasse diagrams.

<!-- remark-10: body_md -->
Unlike the class of alt~$\nu$-Tamari lattices, the class of cross Tamari lattices is closed under duality: the dual of the cross Tamari lattice corresponding to a given moon polyomino is the one corresponding to its $180^\circ$ rotation. It is clear that a (say, semidistributive) lattice $L$ and its dual $L^*$ have the same behavior of rowmotion. For this reason, the cross Tamari setting would be a more conceptually satisfying context in which to establish invariance of rowmotion results.

<!-- remark-11: body_md -->
For a semidistributive lattice $L$, we recall that its (undirected) \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{Galois graph}} can be defined as the complement graph of the one skeleton of its canonical join complex;  see~\cite{barnard2019canonical, thomas2019rowmotion, thomas2019independence}. In the case where $L=J(P)$ is distributive, then its Galois graph is the \textcolor{Turquoise}{\emph{comparability graph}} of $P$. As mentioned in \cref{sec:intro}, in~\cite[Proposition~4.10]{hopkins2022minuscule} it was shown that if $P$ and $Q$ have isomorphic comparability graphs, then $J(P)$ and $J(Q)$ have the same rowmotion behavior. As an extension of this, one might optimistically hope that if two semidistributive lattices $L$ and $L'$ have isomorphic Galois graphs, then they have the same rowmotion behavior.\footnote{But note that already $\operatorname{Stan}({4})$ and $\operatorname{Tam}({4})$ have \emph{non}-isomorphic Galois graphs; so this optimistic hope is anyway orthogonal to our main interests in this paper.} (Again, semidistributive lattices with isomorphic Galois graphs have the same number of elements, so this is a reasonable hope.) However, this is not true: for example, the two semidistributive lattices in \cref{fig:framing_lattices} have isomorphic Galois graphs, but different rowmotion behavior.

\label{conj:pl}
Fix a lattice path $\nu$, and let $L_1$ and $L_2$ be two alt~$\nu$-Tamari lattices. Then piecewise-linear rowmotion behaves the same for $L_1$ and $L_2$. \end{itemize}
where $\operatorname{sum}(x)$ is the sum-of-coordinates (the piecewise-linear analogue of the down-degree statistic).
