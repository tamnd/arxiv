<!-- maintheorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:A}
Let $n\ge 2$ be an integer. Every countable group embeds in a group of type $\mathrm{FP}_{n}$.
<!-- end maintheorem-1 -->
<!-- maintheorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:B}
Let $n\ge 2$ be an integer. There is a group of type $\mathrm{F}_{n}$ that contains a copy of every recursively presented group.
<!-- end maintheorem-2 -->
<!-- maincorollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:C}
Let $n\ge2$ be an integer. A countable group embeds in a group of type $\mathrm{F}_{n}$ if and only if it embeds in a finitely presented group. In particular, a finitely generated group embeds in a group of type $\mathrm{F}_{n}$ if and only if it is recursively presented.
<!-- end maincorollary-1 -->
<!-- maintheorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:O}
Every countable group embeds in a group of type $\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}$.
<!-- end maintheorem-3 -->
<!-- maintheorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:raising}
Let $m\ge 1$, let $B$ be a group of type $\mathrm{FP}_{m}$ and let $\varphi\colon B\to B$ be an injective endomorphism. Suppose that some chain lift $\Phi$ of $\varphi$ satisfies $\Phi(Z)\subseteq M$ for a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z} B$-submodule $M\subseteq Z$. Then $B$ embeds in the group
\[
E=\langle B,t\colon t^{-1}bt=\varphi(b),\ b\in B\rangle,
\]
and $E$ is of type $\mathrm{FP}_{m+1}$.
<!-- end maintheorem-4 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:lift}
Let $\theta$ be an endomorphism of $B$. A \emph{chain lift} of $\theta$ is a family of $\theta$-semilinear maps $f_i\colon F_i\to F_i$, $0\le i\le m$, such that $d_if_i=f_{i-1}d_i$ for $1\le i\le m$ and $\varepsilon f_0=\varepsilon$. Its restriction to $Z$, which it maps into $Z$, is denoted by the same letter.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:comparison}
If $f$ and $g$ are chain lifts of the same endomorphism $\theta$ of $B$, then $(f-g)(Z)$ is contained in a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z} B$-submodule of $Z$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $\varepsilon(f_0-g_0)=0$ and $\ker\varepsilon=d_1(F_1)$, choose a $\theta$-semilinear map $\sigma_0\colon F_0\to F_1$ with $f_0-g_0=d_1\sigma_0$. Inductively, for $1\le i<m$, construct $\theta$-semilinear maps $\sigma_i\colon F_i\to F_{i+1}$ satisfying
\[
f_i-g_i=d_{i+1}\sigma_i+\sigma_{i-1}d_i.
\]
Indeed, once the preceding maps have been constructed, the chain-map identities give
\[
d_i(f_i-g_i-\sigma_{i-1}d_i)
=(f_{i-1}-g_{i-1}-d_i\sigma_{i-1})d_i=0.
\]
For $i=1$ the last equality follows from the choice of $\sigma_0$, and for $i>1$ from $d_{i-1}d_i=0$. Exactness at $F_i$ lets us lift the values through $d_{i+1}$ and extend $\theta$-semilinearly.

Now $c=f_m-g_m-\sigma_{m-1}d_m$ satisfies $d_mc=0$, so $c(F_m)\subseteq Z$, and $c$ agrees with $f_m-g_m$ on $Z$. Since $c$ is $\theta$-semilinear and $F_m$ is finitely generated, $c(F_m)$ lies in a finitely generated submodule of $Z$ by~\eqref{eq:semilinear}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:resolution-independence}
The hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:raising} is also independent of the chosen finite free partial resolution. Let $F'_*$ be another such resolution through degree $m$, put $Z'=\ker d'_m$, and choose comparison maps $u\colon F_*\to F'_*$ and $v\colon F'_*\to F_*$ lifting $\operatorname{id}_\mathbb{Z}$. If $\Phi$ is a chain lift of $\varphi$ with $\Phi(Z)\subseteq M$, then $u\Phi v$ is a chain lift of $\varphi$ on $F'_*$ and maps $Z'$ into the finitely generated submodule $u_m(M)\subseteq Z'$. Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison}, applied to $F'_*$, gives the same conclusion for every chain lift on $F'_*$. Interchanging the two resolutions proves independence.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:triples}
  An \emph{oriented triple system} ${\mathscr{T}}$ on a finite set $V$ is a finite set of triples $\ell=(i,j)\to k$ of distinct elements of $V$; we call $i,j$ the \emph{inputs} and $k$ the \emph{output} of $\ell$, and the order of the inputs is immaterial. The elements of $V$ are called \emph{indices}.

  The \emph{incidence graph} $\Gamma({\mathscr{T}})$ is the bipartite graph with vertex set $V\sqcup{\mathscr{T}}$ in which $v\in V$ is joined to $\ell\in{\mathscr{T}}$ when $v$ is an entry of $\ell$. The \emph{row} of $\ell=(i,j)\to k$ is $W_\ell=e_i+e_j-e_k\in\mathbb{Z}^V$, where $(e_v)_{v\in V}$ is the standard basis, and $W$ is the ${\mathscr{T}}\times V$ integer matrix with rows $W_\ell$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:certificate}
There is an oriented triple system ${\mathscr{T}}$ with $23$ indices and $15$ triples satisfying~\eqref{eq:girth} and~\eqref{eq:row}, with $\nu\in\{\pm1\}^{\mathscr{T}}$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let the points of the Fano plane be the elements of $\mathbb{Z}/7$ and its lines the sets $L_j=\{j,j+1,j+3\}$, $j\in\mathbb{Z}/7$. Its flags are the pairs $f_{j,d}=(j+d,L_j)$ with $j\in\mathbb{Z}/7$ and $d\in\{0,1,3\}$; the flags through the point $p$ are $f_{p,0}$, $f_{p-1,1}$ and $f_{p-3,3}$. Let $V$ consist of the twenty flags other than $f_{0,3}=(3,L_0)$ and of three further indices $a$, $b$ and $0$, and let ${\mathscr{T}}$ consist of the fifteen triples
\begin{align*}
\pi_p&=(f_{p,0},f_{p-1,1})\to f_{p-3,3}\quad(p\ne3), & \pi_3&=(f_{3,0},f_{2,1})\to b,\\
\lambda_j&=(f_{j,0},f_{j,1})\to f_{j,3}\quad(j\ne0), & \lambda_0&=(f_{0,0},f_{0,1})\to a,\\
\gamma&=(b,0)\to a.
\end{align*}
Thus $\pi_p$ consists of the flags through $p$ and $\lambda_j$ of the flags on $L_j$, except that the flag $(3,L_0)$ is replaced by $b$ in $\pi_3$ and by $a$ in $\lambda_0$.

Put $\nu=1$ on the triples $\pi_p$ and $\gamma$, and $\nu=-1$ on the triples $\lambda_j$. In $\nu W=\sum_pW_{\pi_p}-\sum_jW_{\lambda_j}+W_\gamma$, a flag $f_{j,0}$ or $f_{j,1}$ is an input of one triple $\pi_p$ and of one triple $\lambda_j$, and contributes $1-1=0$; a flag $f_{j,3}$ with $j\ne0$ is the output of one $\pi_p$ and one $\lambda_j$, and contributes $-1+1=0$; the index $a$ contributes $1$ from $\lambda_0$ and $-1$ from $\gamma$, the index $b$ contributes $-1$ from $\pi_3$ and $1$ from $\gamma$, and the index $0$ contributes $1$ from $\gamma$. Hence $\nu W=e_0$, which is~\eqref{eq:row}.

Every index other than $0$ lies in exactly two triples, and $0$ lies in $\gamma$ only. Let $H$ be the graph with vertex set ${\mathscr{T}}$ and, for each index other than $0$, an edge joining the two triples that contain it. Then $\Gamma({\mathscr{T}})$ is obtained from $H$ by subdividing every edge and attaching the pendant vertex $0$ to $\gamma$, so its girth is twice that of $H$. The graph $H$ is the incidence graph of the Fano plane, with $\pi_p$ corresponding to $p$ and $\lambda_j$ to $L_j$, in which the edge joining $3$ to $L_0$ is subdivided by the vertex $\gamma$; see Figure~\ref{fig:triple-graph}. The incidence graph of a projective plane is bipartite and contains no cycle of length $4$, since two points lie on a unique line; hence it has girth at least $6$, and subdividing an edge does not shorten any cycle. Therefore $\Gamma({\mathscr{T}})$ has girth at least $12$, which is~\eqref{eq:girth}.

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:flow}
The construction is an instance of the following principle. Let $H$ be a finite graph in which every vertex has degree $3$, one of the edges being a half-edge $0$ with a single endpoint, and orient $H$ so that no vertex is a source or a sink and the half-edge $0$ is directed outwards. Taking the triples to be the sets of edges at the vertices, declare the output at each vertex to be the edge whose orientation there differs from that of the other two, and put $\nu=1$ or $\nu=-1$ at vertices with one or two incoming edges respectively. Then the matrix with rows $\nu_\ell W_\ell$ is the oriented incidence matrix of $H$, with entry $1$ at the tail and $-1$ at the head of each edge, so $\nu W$ vanishes on every edge with two endpoints and equals $1$ on the half-edge: this is~\eqref{eq:row}. Dually, a solution of the equations $x_k=x_i+x_j$ is a conserved flow on $H$, and conservation forces the flow $x_0$ through the half-edge to vanish. Condition~\eqref{eq:girth} holds as soon as $H$ has girth at least $6$. In the proof above, $H$ is oriented from points to lines along the flags $f_{j,0}$, $f_{j,1}$ and from lines to points along the flags $f_{j,3}$, the subdivided flag $(3,L_0)$ runs from $L_0$ through $\gamma$ to $3$, and the half-edge $0$ leaves $\gamma$.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:injective}
If $\operatorname{girth}\Gamma({\mathscr{T}})\ge 12$, then $\iota_v$ is injective for every $v\in V$ and every group $G$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{P}$ be the poset with elements $o$, the elements of $V$ and the elements of ${\mathscr{T}}$, ordered by $\ell<v<o$ whenever $v$ is an entry of $\ell$. The monomorphisms of the complex of groups defined below go from larger to smaller elements of $\mathcal{P}$, that is, along $o\to v\to\ell$ and $o\to\ell$. Its geometric realisation $|\mathcal{P}|$ is the cone with apex $o$ on $\Gamma({\mathscr{T}})$, whose $2$-simplices are the triangles $\{o,v,\ell\}$ with $v$ an entry of $\ell$. In particular, $|\mathcal{P}|$ is contractible.

Let $G(\mathcal{P})$ be the simple complex of groups over $\mathcal{P}$, in the sense of~\cite[II.12]{BH}, with local groups $G_o=1$, $G_v=G$ for $v\in V$ and $G_\ell=G\times G$ for $\ell\in{\mathscr{T}}$, and with the following monomorphisms: those out of $G_o$ are trivial, and for $\ell=(i,j)\to k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-maps}
G_i\to G_\ell,\ g\mapsto(g,1);\qquad G_j\to G_\ell,\ g\mapsto(1,g);\qquad G_k\to G_\ell,\ g\mapsto(g,g).
\end{equation}
The compatibility condition on composites is vacuous, because every chain of length two in $\mathcal{P}$ begins at $o$. The direct limit of $G(\mathcal{P})$ is generated by the groups $G_v$ and $G_\ell$ subject to the identifications~\eqref{eq:local-maps}. Since $G\times G$ is generated by $G\times 1$ and $1\times G$ subject only to the relations that these subgroups commute, eliminating the groups $G_\ell$ yields the presentation~\eqref{eq:KSigma}. As $|\mathcal{P}|$ is simply connected, this direct limit is the fundamental group of $G(\mathcal{P})$. It therefore suffices to show that $G(\mathcal{P})$ is developable, since the local groups of a developable complex of groups embed in its fundamental group~\cite[II.12]{BH}.

Make every triangle $\{o,v,\ell\}$ of $|\mathcal{P}|$ a Euclidean triangle with side lengths $|ov|=\sqrt3$, $|v\ell|=1$ and $|o\ell|=2$. Its angles are $\pi/6$ at $o$, $\pi/2$ at $v$ and $\pi/3$ at $\ell$, and edges of the same type have the same length, so this is a piecewise Euclidean structure with a single shape. By~\cite[II.12.28]{BH} (see also~\cite[III.C.4.17]{BH}), $G(\mathcal{P})$ is developable if it is non-positively curved, that is, if its local developments are locally $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$. For a $2$-dimensional piecewise Euclidean complex this is the link condition: every injective loop in the link of a vertex has length at least $2\pi$~\cite[II.5]{BH}. The links at vertices of the local developments are as follows.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
vertex & local group & link & edge length & girth \\ \hline
$o$ & $1$ & $\Gamma({\mathscr{T}})$ & $\pi/6$ & $\ge 12$\\
$v$ & $G$ & $G*\operatorname{Inc}(v)$ & $\pi/2$ & $\ge 4$\\
$\ell$ & $G\times G$ & point--coset incidence graph & $\pi/3$ & $\ge 6$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At $o$ the local group is trivial and the link is $\Gamma({\mathscr{T}})$. At $v$, put $\operatorname{Inc}(v)=\{\ell\in{\mathscr{T}}\colon v\text{ is an entry of }\ell\}$. The link is the simplicial join $G*\operatorname{Inc}(v)$ of two discrete sets: the set $G_v/G_o\cong G$ of directions towards lifts of $o$, and the set of triples containing $v$. Thus it is a complete bipartite graph.

At $\ell=(i,j)\to k$, let $H_1=G\times1$, $H_2=1\times G$ and let $H_3$ be the diagonal. The link is the bipartite graph with point vertices $z\in G\times G$ and tagged coset vertices $(a,zH_a)$, $a\in\{1,2,3\}$. Each point is joined to the three tagged cosets containing it. Distinct cosets of the same subgroup are disjoint. If $a\ne b$ and two cosets of $H_a$ and $H_b$ meet at $z$, their intersection is $z(H_a\cap H_b)=\{z\}$. Consequently, two distinct tagged cosets have at most one common point. This link has no cycle of length $4$, so its girth is at least $6$. In each case every injective loop has length at least $2\pi$, and the link condition holds.

Thus $G(\mathcal{P})$ is non-positively curved, hence developable, and every $\iota_v$ is injective.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:presentations}
The construction $G\mapsto K_{\mathscr{T}}(G)$ is functorial. If $G=\langle X\colon\mathcal{R}\rangle$ with $X$ finite, then $K_{\mathscr{T}}(G)$ is presented by the generators $x_v$ ($x\in X$, $v\in V$) and the relators
\[
r_v\ (r\in\mathcal{R},\ v\in V),\qquad [x_i,y_j],\ \ x_k^{-1}x_ix_j\ \ \bigl(x,y\in X,\ (i,j)\to k\in{\mathscr{T}}\bigr),
\]
where $r_v$ denotes $r$ written in the letters $x_v$. Consequently, $K_{\mathscr{T}}$ preserves finite generation, finite presentability and, for finitely generated groups, $\mathcal{O}$-recursive presentability.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Functoriality is clear from~\eqref{eq:KSigma}. In the group given by the displayed presentation, the relators $[x_i,y_j]$ make the images of $G_i$ and $G_j$ commute, so $g\mapsto g_ig_j$ is a homomorphism on $G$. It agrees with $g\mapsto g_k$ on $X$, hence on $G$. So all relators in~\eqref{eq:KSigma} follow from the displayed ones.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:product}
Let $\alpha,\beta$ be endomorphisms of $B$ such that $\alpha(B)$ and $\beta(B)$ commute elementwise, and let $\delta(b)=\alpha(b)\beta(b)$. For any chain lifts $\Phi_\alpha,\Phi_\beta,\Phi_\delta$ of $\alpha,\beta,\delta$, there is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z} B$-submodule $M\subseteq Z$ such that
\[
(\Phi_\delta-\Phi_\alpha-\Phi_\beta)(Z)\subseteq M.
\]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $Q_*=F_*\otimes_\mathbb{Z} F_*$ with differential $d(x\otimes y)=dx\otimes y+(-1)^{|x|}x\otimes dy$, and with $\mathbb{Z}[B\times B]=\mathbb{Z} B\otimes_\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{Z} B$ acting factorwise. Each $Q_n=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}F_p\otimes_\mathbb{Z} F_q$ is a finitely generated free $\mathbb{Z}[B\times B]$-module. Write $\varepsilon_Q=\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon\colon Q_0\to\mathbb{Z}$ for its augmentation. Let $\rho_1=\operatorname{id}\otimes\varepsilon$ and $\rho_2=\varepsilon\otimes\operatorname{id}$, where $\varepsilon$ is regarded as a chain map from $F_*$ to $\mathbb{Z}$ concentrated in degree $0$. These are chain maps $Q_*\to F_*$, semilinear with respect to the two projections $B\times B\to B$, and $\rho_1(x\otimes y)=0$ whenever $y$ has positive degree (similarly for $\rho_2$). The groups $F_p$ are free abelian and $H_0(F_*)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, so the Künneth formula has no $\mathrm{Tor}$ terms in degrees $\le m$, and gives $H_0(Q_*)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, $H_n(Q_*)=0$ for $0<n<m$, and an isomorphism
\[
(\rho_{1*},\rho_{2*})\colon H_m(Q_*)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }Z\oplus Z .
\]
In particular:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kunneth}
\text{a cycle $w\in Q_m$ with $\rho_1(w)=\rho_2(w)=0$ lies in $d(Q_{m+1})$.}
\end{equation}

\emph{The product.} Since the images commute, $\eta(b,c)=\alpha(b)\beta(c)$ is a homomorphism $B\times B\to B$. Choose $h_0$ on a basis so that $\varepsilon h_0=\varepsilon_Q$. Since the $Q_n$ are free and the augmented complex $F_*\to\mathbb{Z}$ is exact at $F_0,\dots,F_{m-1}$, this extends degree by degree to $\eta$-semilinear maps $h_n\colon Q_n\to F_n$, $0\le n\le m$, commuting with the differentials. Put
\[
M_0=\mathbb{Z} B\cdot h_m d(Q_{m+1}).
\]
We have $d_mh_md=h_{m-1}dd=0$, so $M_0\subseteq Z$. Since $h_md$ is $\eta$-semilinear and $Q_{m+1}=\bigoplus_{p=1}^{m}F_p\otimes_\mathbb{Z} F_{m+1-p}$ is finitely generated, $M_0$ is finitely generated by~\eqref{eq:semilinear}. The chain maps $i_1(x)=x\otimes1$ and $i_2(x)=1\otimes x$ from $F_*$ to $Q_*$, where $1\in F_0=\mathbb{Z} B$, are semilinear with respect to $b\mapsto(b,1)$ and $b\mapsto(1,b)$. Hence $f=hi_1$ and $g=hi_2$ are chain lifts of $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

\emph{The diagonal.} Let $Q^{\mathrm{brut}}_{\le m}$ denote the brutal truncation of $Q_*$: its terms in degrees $0,\dots,m$ are unchanged and its terms in higher degrees are zero. Using $\varepsilon_Q$ in degree $0$ and exactness of the augmented complex at $Q_0,\dots,Q_{m-1}$, construct a chain map $\Delta\colon F_*\to Q^{\mathrm{brut}}_{\le m}$ with $\varepsilon_Q\Delta_0=\varepsilon$, semilinear with respect to the diagonal $B\to B\times B$. We continue to use the full complex $Q_*$, including $Q_{m+1}$, when applying~\eqref{eq:kunneth}. Then $h\Delta$ is a chain lift of $\delta$, and $\tau_a=\rho_a\Delta$ ($a=1,2$) is a $\mathbb{Z} B$-linear chain lift of $\operatorname{id}_B$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison} there are finitely generated submodules $Y_1,Y_2\subseteq Z$ with $(\tau_a-\operatorname{id})(Z)\subseteq Y_a$. For $z\in Z$, the element
\[
w=\Delta(z)-i_1\tau_1(z)-i_2\tau_2(z)
\]
is a cycle, and $\rho_1(w)=\tau_1(z)-\tau_1(z)-0=0$ because $\rho_1i_1=\operatorname{id}$ and $\rho_1i_2$ vanishes in positive degrees; likewise $\rho_2(w)=0$. By~\eqref{eq:kunneth}, $w\in d(Q_{m+1})$, so $h_m(w)\in M_0$. Since $hi_1\tau_1(z)=f(z)+f\bigl((\tau_1-\operatorname{id})(z)\bigr)$ and similarly for $g$, we obtain
\[
(h\Delta-f-g)(Z)\subseteq M_0+\mathbb{Z} B\,f(Y_1)+\mathbb{Z} B\,g(Y_2),
\]
and the right-hand side is a finitely generated submodule of $Z$ by~\eqref{eq:semilinear}.

\emph{Arbitrary lifts.} Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison} to the pairs $(\Phi_\delta,h\Delta)$, $(\Phi_\alpha,f)$ and $(\Phi_\beta,g)$, and adding the resulting finitely generated submodules to $M_0+\mathbb{Z} B\,f(Y_1)+\mathbb{Z} B\,g(Y_2)$, gives $M$.

<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:row}
Let $(\alpha_v)_{v\in V}$ be endomorphisms of $B$ such that, for every $(i,j)\to k\in{\mathscr{T}}$, the images of $\alpha_i$ and $\alpha_j$ commute elementwise and $\alpha_k(b)=\alpha_i(b)\alpha_j(b)$ for all $b\in B$. Then every chain lift $\Phi_0$ of $\alpha_0$ maps $Z$ into a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z} B$-submodule of $Z$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
Choose chain lifts $\Phi_v$ of $\alpha_v$ for $v\ne0$. For each $\ell=(i,j)\to k$, Lemma~\ref{lem:product} gives a finitely generated submodule $M_\ell\subseteq Z$ containing $(\Phi_i+\Phi_j-\Phi_k)(Z)$; let $M=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathscr{T}}}M_\ell$. By~\eqref{eq:row}, applied to the abelian group of additive maps $Z\to Z$,
\[
\Phi_0(z)=\sum_{\ell=(i,j)\to k}\nu_\ell\,\bigl(\Phi_i(z)+\Phi_j(z)-\Phi_k(z)\bigr)\in M\qquad(z\in Z).\qedhere
\]
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
By Britton's lemma, $B$ embeds in $E$. Since $\mathbb{Z} E$ is a free right $\mathbb{Z} B$-module, the functor $\mathbb{Z} E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}-$ is exact. Put $P_i=\mathbb{Z} E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}F_i$ and $Z_E=\mathbb{Z} E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}Z$. Then $P_m\to\cdots\to P_0\to\mathbb{Z}[E/B]\to0$ is exact, the $P_i$ are finitely generated free $\mathbb{Z} E$-modules, and $Z_E$ is the kernel of $d_m\colon P_m\to P_{m-1}$.

Choose a chain lift $\Phi$ of $\varphi$ satisfying $\Phi(Z)\subseteq M$ for a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z} B$-submodule $M\subseteq Z$, and define $T_i\colon P_i\to P_i$ by
\[
T_i(a\otimes x)=at\otimes\Phi_i(x).
\]
This is well defined: for $b\in B$ we have $bt=t\varphi(b)$ in $E$, so $abt\otimes\Phi_i(x)=at\varphi(b)\otimes\Phi_i(x)=at\otimes\Phi_i(bx)$. The maps $T_i$ are $\mathbb{Z} E$-linear, commute with the differentials, and induce $T(gB)=gtB$ on $\mathbb{Z}[E/B]=\mathbb{Z} E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}\mathbb{Z}$ because $\varepsilon\Phi_0=\varepsilon$. Let $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_r$ generate $M$. If $z\in Z$ and $\Phi_m(z)=\sum_p\lambda_p\mu_p$ with $\lambda_p\in\mathbb{Z} B$, then $T_m(a\otimes z)=\sum_pat\lambda_p\otimes \mu_p$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TinM}
T_m(Z_E)\subseteq\mathbb{Z} E\,(1\otimes \mu_1)+\dots+\mathbb{Z} E\,(1\otimes \mu_r).
\end{equation}

The sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coset}
0\to\mathbb{Z}[E/B]\xrightarrow{\,1-T\,}\mathbb{Z}[E/B]\xrightarrow{\,\varepsilon\,}\mathbb{Z}\to0
\end{equation}
is exact. Indeed, the height is constant on each coset $gB$ and $T$ raises it by one, so the terms of minimal height of a nonzero element of $\mathbb{Z}[E/B]$ survive in its image under $1-T$. The cokernel of $1-T$ is the quotient of $\mathbb{Z}[E/B]$ by the relations $gB=gtB$; together with $gbB=gB$ for $b\in B$, these identify $gB$ with $gxB$ for every $x\in B\cup\{t^{\pm1}\}$, hence identify all cosets, since $B$ and $t$ generate $E$.

The idea is that, modulo $(1-T_m)Z_E$, every $\zeta\in Z_E$ is congruent to $T_m\zeta$, which lies in the finitely generated submodule~\eqref{eq:TinM}. We make this precise with a mapping cone.

Let $C_*$ be the brutal truncation in degrees $\le m$ of the mapping cone of $1-T$: $C_0=P_0$, $C_j=P_j\oplus P_{j-1}$ for $1\le j\le m$, with differentials
\begin{align*}
D_1(x,y)&=d_1x+(1-T_0)y,\\
D_j(x,y)&=\bigl(d_jx+(1-T_{j-1})y,\,-d_{j-1}y\bigr)\qquad(2\le j\le m),
\end{align*}
augmented by the composite $C_0=P_0\to\mathbb{Z}[E/B]\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathbb{Z}$. Since $T$ commutes with the differentials, $D_{j-1}D_j=0$. Let $\xi_1,\dots,\xi_s$ be a basis of $F_m$. We show that $C_*$ is exact in degrees $<m$ and that $\ker D_m$ is generated by the elements
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generators}
(1\otimes \mu_p,\,0)\quad(1\le p\le r),\qquad\bigl((1-T_m)(1\otimes\xi_q),\,-d_m(1\otimes\xi_q)\bigr)\quad(1\le q\le s).
\end{equation}
Mapping a free $\mathbb{Z} E$-module of rank $r+s$ onto $\ker D_m$ then extends $C_*$ to a partial resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$ by finitely generated free $\mathbb{Z} E$-modules in degrees $0,\dots,m+1$, so $E$ is of type $\mathrm{FP}_{m+1}$.

\emph{Degree $0$.} Let $x\in P_0$ have augmentation $0$. By~\eqref{eq:coset}, its image in $\mathbb{Z}[E/B]$ is $(1-T)\bar y$ for the image $\bar y$ of some $y\in P_0$. Then $x-(1-T_0)y$ lies in the kernel of $P_0\to\mathbb{Z}[E/B]$, which is $d_1(P_1)$, so $x\in D_1(C_1)$.

\emph{Degrees $1\le j\le m$.} Let $(x,y)\in C_j$ with $D_j(x,y)=0$. We first show that $y=d_jz$ for some $z\in P_j$. If $j=1$, mapping $D_1(x,y)=0$ to $\mathbb{Z}[E/B]$ gives $(1-T)\bar y=0$, so $\bar y=0$ by~\eqref{eq:coset} and $y\in d_1(P_1)$. If $j\ge2$, then $d_{j-1}y=0$ and $y$ is a boundary because $H_{j-1}(P_*)=0$. Now $d_j\bigl(x+(1-T_j)z\bigr)=0$. If $j<m$, then $x+(1-T_j)z=d_{j+1}w$ for some $w$, and $(x,y)=D_{j+1}(w,-z)$. If $j=m$, then $\zeta=x+(1-T_m)z$ lies in $Z_E$ and
\[
(x,y)=(\zeta,0)+\bigl((1-T_m)(-z),\,-d_m(-z)\bigr).
\]
The elements $\bigl((1-T_m)x',-d_mx'\bigr)$ with $x'\in P_m$ form the image of a $\mathbb{Z} E$-linear map $P_m\to C_m$, which is generated by the second family in~\eqref{eq:generators}. For $\zeta\in Z_E$ we have $d_m\zeta=0$, hence
\[
(\zeta,0)=(T_m\zeta,0)+\bigl((1-T_m)\zeta,\,-d_m\zeta\bigr),
\]
where the first term lies in the span of the first family in~\eqref{eq:generators} by~\eqref{eq:TinM}, and the second in the span of the second family.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:HNN}
Every countable group with an $\mathcal{O}$-r.e.\ presentation embeds in a two-generator $\mathcal{O}$-recursively presented group $G^*$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Write $G=\langle x_1,x_2,\dots\colon\mathcal{R}\rangle$, repeating generators if necessary, and let $L=\langle a,b\rangle$ be free of rank two. In $G*L$ put
\[
u_0=b,\quad u_i=a^{-i}ba^i;\qquad v_0=a,\quad v_i=x_ib^{-i}ab^i\qquad(i\ge1).
\]
The $u_i$ freely generate a free subgroup of $L$. So do the $v_i$, since the retraction $G*L\to L$ killing $G$ maps them to the free basis $\{b^{-i}ab^i\colon i\ge0\}$ of a subgroup of $L$. Hence $G*L$, and so $G$, embeds in
\[
G^*=\langle G*L,\,t\colon t^{-1}u_it=v_i,\ i\ge0\rangle .
\]
The defining relations give $a=t^{-1}bt$ and $x_i=t^{-1}a^{-i}ba^it\,b^{-i}a^{-1}b^i$ for $i\ge1$. Eliminating $a$ and the $x_i$ presents $G^*$ on $b,t$ with relators obtained from $\mathcal{R}$ by substituting these words, which is an $\mathcal{O}$-r.e.\ set.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:initial}
There is a two-generator $\mathcal{O}$-recursively presented group $B_{1,\mathcal{O}}$ that contains a copy of every finitely generated $\mathcal{O}$-recursively presented group. In particular, $B_{1,\mathcal{O}}$ is of type $\mathrm{FP}_{1}$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
For $k\ge1$ and an oracle Turing machine $\mathcal{M}$, let $G_{k,\mathcal{M}}$ be the group generated by $x_1,\dots,x_k$ subject to those outputs of $\mathcal{M}$ with oracle $\mathcal{O}$ that are words in $x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_k^{\pm1}$. Every finitely generated $\mathcal{O}$-recursively presented group is isomorphic to some $G_{k,\mathcal{M}}$. Using disjoint alphabets and dovetailing the enumerations, the free product $\Omega_\mathcal{O}$ of all these groups has an $\mathcal{O}$-r.e.\ presentation and contains each of them. By Lemma~\ref{lem:HNN}, $\Omega_\mathcal{O}$ embeds in a two-generator $\mathcal{O}$-recursively presented group $B_{1,\mathcal{O}}$. Finite generation is equivalent to type $\mathrm{FP}_{1}$.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:oracle}
For every oracle $\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ and every integer $n\ge1$ there is an $\mathcal{O}$-recursively presented group $B_{n,\mathcal{O}}$ of type $\mathrm{FP}_{n}$ that contains a copy of every finitely generated $\mathcal{O}$-recursively presented group. These groups can be chosen so that $B_{1,\mathcal{O}}\le B_{2,\mathcal{O}}\le\cdots$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The group $B_{1,\mathcal{O}}$ is given by Lemma~\ref{lem:initial}. Suppose that $B=B_{m,\mathcal{O}}$ has been constructed for some $m\ge1$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:presentations}, $K_{\mathscr{T}}(B)$ is finitely generated and $\mathcal{O}$-recursively presented, so there is an embedding $\kappa\colon K_{\mathscr{T}}(B)\to B$. Put $\alpha_v=\kappa\circ\iota_v$ for $v\in V$. The relations~\eqref{eq:KSigma} show that the family $(\alpha_v)$ satisfies the hypothesis of Corollary~\ref{cor:row}, and $\alpha_0$ is injective by Lemma~\ref{lem:injective}. By Corollary~\ref{cor:raising},
\[
B_{m+1,\mathcal{O}}=\langle B,\,t_m\colon t_m^{-1}bt_m=\alpha_0(b),\ b\in B\rangle
\]
is of type $\mathrm{FP}_{m+1}$ and contains $B$, hence every finitely generated $\mathcal{O}$-recursively presented group. It is $\mathcal{O}$-recursively presented by Subsection~\ref{sec:oracle}.
<!-- end proof-10 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Let $G$ be a countable group and choose $\mathcal{O}$ such that $G$ has an $\mathcal{O}$-r.e.\ presentation (see Subsection~\ref{sec:oracle}). By Lemma~\ref{lem:HNN}, $G$ embeds in a finitely generated $\mathcal{O}$-recursively presented group, hence in $B_{1,\mathcal{O}}\le B_{n,\mathcal{O}}$, which is of type $\mathrm{FP}_{n}$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle}.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:infinity}
For $n=0$ every group is of type $\mathrm{FP}_{0}$, and for $n=1$ the required embedding follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:HNN}. <!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:Fn}
A finitely presented group of type $\mathrm{FP}_{n}$ is of type $\mathrm{F}_{n}$.\qed
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $\Omega_\emptyset$ be the recursively presented free product constructed in Lemma~\ref{lem:initial}. By Lemma~\ref{lem:HNN} and Higman's embedding theorem~\cite{Hig}, it embeds in a finitely presented group $U_2$. Every countable recursively presented group embeds, by Lemma~\ref{lem:HNN}, in a finitely generated recursively presented group, and the latter is isomorphic to a free factor of $\Omega_\emptyset$. Thus $U_2$ contains a copy of every countable recursively presented group.

Suppose that $U_m$ is finitely presented, of type $\mathrm{F}_{m}$, and contains $U_2$. In particular, it contains a copy of every finitely presented group. By Lemma~\ref{lem:presentations}, $K_{\mathscr{T}}(U_m)$ is finitely presented and hence embeds in $U_m$. As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle}, composing this embedding with the maps $\iota_v$ gives endomorphisms satisfying the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:raising}, and the resulting ascending HNN extension $U_{m+1}$ is of type $\mathrm{FP}_{m+1}$. It is finitely presented, being obtained from a finite presentation of $U_m$ by adding one generator and finitely many relators, so it is of type $\mathrm{F}_{m+1}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:Fn}. It contains $U_m$, hence $U_2$, hence a copy of every countable recursively presented group.

A group of type $\mathrm{F}_{n}$ with $n\ge2$ is finitely presented, so every subgroup of such a group embeds in a finitely presented group. <!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{lem:two-generators}
Let $n\ge2$. Every finitely generated group of type $\mathrm{FP}_{n}$ embeds in a two-generator group of type $\mathrm{FP}_{n}$. If the original group is of type $\mathrm{F}_{n}$, the two-generator group can also be chosen of type $\mathrm{F}_{n}$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
Let $G=\langle x_1,\dots,x_k\rangle$, with $k\ge1$, and use the construction of Lemma~\ref{lem:HNN} with only the indices $0,\dots,k$. The subgroups generated by $u_0,\dots,u_k$ and by $v_0,\dots,v_k$ are free of rank $k+1$. Thus
\[
J=\langle G*\langle a,b\rangle,\,t\colon t^{-1}u_it=v_i,\ 0\le i\le k\rangle
\]
contains $G$ by Britton's lemma and is generated by $b,t$, by the same elimination formulas as before.

We recall why the finite-rank edge groups preserve type $\mathrm{FP}_{n}$. For a group $\Gamma$ that is the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups, the augmented cellular chain sequence of its Bass--Serre tree is
\[
0\to\bigoplus_e\mathbb{Z}\Gamma\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} G_e}\mathbb{Z}
\to\bigoplus_v\mathbb{Z}\Gamma\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} G_v}\mathbb{Z}
\to\mathbb{Z}\to0,
\]
where one edge is chosen from each unoriented edge orbit. Induce free resolutions of $\mathbb{Z}$ over the vertex and edge groups to $\mathbb{Z}\Gamma$, lift the first map in this sequence to a chain map, and take its mapping cone. Exactness of induction and the homology sequence of the cone show that this is a free resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$. Its degree-$j$ term is the sum of the induced degree-$j$ vertex terms and degree-$(j-1)$ edge terms. Hence $\Gamma$ is of type $\mathrm{FP}_{n}$ if its vertex groups are of type $\mathrm{FP}_{n}$ and its edge groups are of type $\mathrm{FP}_{n-1}$.

Apply this first to the free product $G*\langle a,b\rangle$, whose edge group is trivial, and then to the HNN extension defining $J$, whose edge group is free of finite rank. This proves that $J$ is of type $\mathrm{FP}_{n}$. If $G$ is of type $\mathrm{F}_{n}$, it is finitely presented, and so is $J$: the construction adds finitely many generators and relators. Lemma~\ref{lem:Fn} then shows that $J$ is of type $\mathrm{F}_{n}$.

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{app:contraction-def}
Let $\theta$ be an endomorphism of $B$, let $A$ be a bound in $B$ and let $n\ge0$. A \emph{controlled $n$-contraction} of $\theta$ over $A$ is a family of controlled additive maps $h_q\colon C^A_q(B)\to C_{q+1}(B)$, $0\le q\le n$, such that, with $h_{-1}=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{app:contraction-eq}
\partial h_q+h_{q-1}\partial=\theta_*-c_*\quad\text{on }C^A_q(B)\qquad(0\le q\le n).
\end{equation}

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{app:EZ}
There are additive maps $\Psi\colon C_q(B)\to C_{q+1}(B\times B)$, $q\ge0$, such that
\[
\partial\Psi+\Psi\partial=\Delta_*-\nabla\,\mathrm{AW}
\]
and $\Psi\bigl(C^A_*(B)\bigr)\subseteq C^{A\times A}_{*+1}(B\times B)$ for every bound $A$.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
For $x\in B\times B$ and a chain $u$, let $x*u$ be obtained by prepending $x$ to every tuple; then $\partial(x*u)=u$ whenever $u$ is a cycle of positive degree. In degree $0$, both $\Delta_*$ and $\nabla\,\mathrm{AW}$ map $(b)$ to $\bigl((b,b)\bigr)$; put $\Psi=0$ there. Let $q\ge1$, and suppose that $\Psi$ has been defined in degrees $<q$, satisfies the identity there, and maps every tuple to a chain whose entries are pairs of entries of that tuple. Let $s=(b_0,\dots,b_q)$ be a nondegenerate tuple with set of entries $W$. The chain $u_s=\Delta_*s-\nabla\,\mathrm{AW}(s)-\Psi\partial s$ has entries in $W\times W$, and is a cycle by the identity in degree $q-1$. Put $\Psi(s)=(b_0,b_0)*u_s$. If $s\in C^A_q(B)$, then $b^{-1}b'\in A$ for all $b,b'\in W$, whence the last assertion.

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{app:additivity}
Let $n\ge1$, let $A$ be a bound in $B$, and let $\theta$ be an endomorphism of $B$ admitting a controlled $(n-1)$-contraction over $A$. Let $\alpha,\beta$ be endomorphisms of $B$ whose images commute elementwise, and put $\delta(b)=\alpha(b)\beta(b)$. Then there is a bound $A'$ such that, for every cycle $z\in C^A_n(B)$,
\[
(\delta\theta)_*z-(\alpha\theta)_*z-(\beta\theta)_*z\in\partial C^{A'}_{n+1}(B).
\]
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
Let $(h_q)_{q<n}$ be the contraction, put $h_q=0$ for $q\ge n$, and let $\Theta=\theta_*-\partial h-h\partial$ on $C^A_*(B)$. This is a chain map, with $\Theta_0=c_*$, $\Theta_q=0$ for $0<q<n$, and $\Theta_n=\theta_*-h_{n-1}\partial$; in particular $\Theta_nz=\theta_*z$ for every cycle $z$. On $C^A_*(B)\otimes C^A_*(B)$ define $\Xi$, of degree $1$, by
\[
\Xi(x\otimes y)=h(x)\otimes\theta_*(y)+(-1)^{|x|}\,\Theta(x)\otimes h(y).
\]
Since $\partial h+h\partial=\theta_*-\Theta$, direct expansion gives $\partial\Xi+\Xi\partial=\theta_*\otimes\theta_*-\Theta\otimes\Theta$. Put $\Lambda=\Psi\theta_*+\nabla\,\Xi\,\mathrm{AW}$ on $C^A_*(B)$. Lemma~\ref{app:EZ} and the naturality $\mathrm{AW}\,\theta_*=(\theta_*\otimes\theta_*)\mathrm{AW}$ give
\[
\partial\Lambda+\Lambda\partial=\Delta_*\theta_*-\nabla(\Theta\otimes\Theta)\mathrm{AW}.
\]
Let $A_1$ be a bound containing $\theta(A)$ and the images of $h_q$ ($q<n$) and $\Theta_q$ ($q\le n$) on $C^A_*(B)$. Only these maps enter $\Lambda$ on $C^A_n(B)$, so $\Lambda$ maps $C^A_n(B)$ into $C^{A_1\times A_1}_{n+1}(B\times B)$.

Let $z\in C^A_n(B)$ be a cycle. In $(\Theta\otimes\Theta)\mathrm{AW}(z)$, the terms of bidegree $(p,n-p)$ with $0<p<n$ vanish, and the two remaining terms sum to $\theta_*z\otimes(1)+(1)\otimes\theta_*z$. By~\eqref{app:shuffle-point},
\[
\partial\Lambda z=\Delta_*\theta_*z-(i_1)_*\theta_*z-(i_2)_*\theta_*z.
\]
Since the images of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ commute, $\mu(x,y)=\alpha(x)\beta(y)$ defines a homomorphism $\mu\colon B\times B\to B$ with $\mu\Delta=\delta$, $\mu i_1=\alpha$ and $\mu i_2=\beta$. Hence
\[
\partial\mu_*\Lambda z=(\delta\theta)_*z-(\alpha\theta)_*z-(\beta\theta)_*z.
\]
Finally, $A'=\alpha(A_1)\beta(A_1)$ is a bound, symmetric because the images commute, and $\mu_*$ maps $C^{A_1\times A_1}_*(B\times B)$ into $C^{A'}_*(B)$, since $\mu(x,y)^{-1}\mu(x',y')=\alpha(x^{-1}x')\,\beta(y^{-1}y')$.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{app:contraction}
For every $n\ge0$ and every bound $A$ in $B$, some power $\varphi^r$ with $r\ge1$ admits a controlled $n$-contraction over $A$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
For $n=0$, fix a finite symmetric generating set $S$ of $B$ containing $1$. For each $u\in B$, choose $s_1,\dots,s_\ell\in S$ with $\varphi(u)=s_1\cdots s_\ell$, and put
\[
h_0(u)=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(s_1\cdots s_{k-1},\,s_1\cdots s_k)\in C^S_1(B).
\]
Then $\partial h_0(u)=(\varphi(u))-(1)$, so $(h_0)$ is a controlled $0$-contraction of $\varphi$ over every bound. The lengths $\ell$ need not be bounded.

Let $n\ge1$, and assume the assertion in degree $n-1$ for all bounds. Let $(h_q)_{q<n}$ be a controlled $(n-1)$-contraction of $\varphi^r$ over $A$. For each nondegenerate tuple $s$ in $C^A_n(B)$, the chain $w_s=\varphi^r_*s-h_{n-1}\partial s$ is a cycle, since
\[
\partial w_s=\varphi^r_*\partial s-(\varphi^r_*-c_*-h_{n-2}\partial)\partial s=c_*\partial s=0;
\]
indeed $c_*$ vanishes in positive degrees and $\varepsilon(\partial s)=0$. The cycles $w_s$ all lie in $C^{A_1}_n(B)$ for one bound $A_1$, because $\varphi^r_*$ and $h_{n-1}$ are controlled. By hypothesis, some $\varphi^{r'}$ admits a controlled $(n-1)$-contraction over $A_1$. Apply Lemma~\ref{app:additivity} with $\theta=\varphi^{r'}$ to $(\alpha,\beta)=(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$ for each triple $(i,j)\to k$ of ${\mathscr{T}}$, and let $A'$ be the union of the resulting bounds. For a cycle $w\in C^{A_1}_n(B)$ put $y_v=(\alpha_v\varphi^{r'})_*w$. Then $y_i+y_j-y_k\in\partial C^{A'}_{n+1}(B)$ for every $(i,j)\to k$, and~\eqref{eq:row}, applied in the abelian group $C_n(B)$, gives
\[
\varphi^{r'+1}_*w=y_0=\sum_{\ell=(i,j)\to k}\nu_\ell\,(y_i+y_j-y_k)\in\partial C^{A'}_{n+1}(B).
\]
In particular, $\varphi^{r'+1}_*w_s=\partial y_s$ for some $y_s\in C^{A'}_{n+1}(B)$. Extend $s\mapsto y_s$ additively to $h_n\colon C^A_n(B)\to C^{A'}_{n+1}(B)$. Then $(\varphi^{r'+1}_*h_0,\dots,\varphi^{r'+1}_*h_{n-1},h_n)$ is a controlled $n$-contraction of $\varphi^{r+r'+1}$ over $A$: in degrees $<n$ this was observed after Definition~\ref{app:contraction-def}, and in degree $n$
\[
\partial h_n(s)+\varphi^{r'+1}_*h_{n-1}\partial s=\varphi^{r'+1}_*(w_s+h_{n-1}\partial s)=\varphi^{r+r'+1}_*s.\qedhere
\]

<!-- lemma-11: body_md -->
\label{app:hnn-sequence}
For every right $\mathbb{Z} E$-module $N$ there is a long exact sequence
\[
\cdots\to H_q(B,N)\xrightarrow{1-T}H_q(B,N)\to H_q(E,N)\to H_{q-1}(B,N)\xrightarrow{1-T}\cdots.
\]
<!-- end lemma-11 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
The complex $P_*=\mathbb{Z} E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C_*(B)$ is a free $\mathbb{Z} E$-resolution of $\mathbb{Z}[E/B]$, and $\tilde T(g\otimes\sigma)=gt\otimes\varphi_*\sigma$ is a $\mathbb{Z} E$-linear chain endomorphism of $P_*$ lifting $T(gB)=gtB$. By the exactness of~\eqref{eq:coset}, the mapping cone of $1-\tilde T$ is a free $\mathbb{Z} E$-resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$. Tensoring with $N$ identifies $N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} E}P_*$ with $N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C_*(B)$ and $1\otimes\tilde T$ with $T$; the long exact sequence of the cone is the assertion.

<!-- lemma-12: body_md -->
\label{app:product-vanishing}
Let $I$ be a set and $N=(\mathbb{Z} E)^I$. Then $T$ is locally nilpotent on $H_q(B,N)$ for every $q\ge1$. Consequently $H_q(E,N)=0$ for every $q\ge2$.
<!-- end lemma-12 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
For every bound $A$ and every $p$, the module $C^A_p(B)$ is free on the finite set $\mathcal{S}_p(A)$, and finite direct sums commute with products, so the coordinate map
\begin{equation}\label{app:finite-types}
\pi\colon N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C^A_p(B)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\prod_{\lambda\in I}\mathbb{Z} E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C^A_p(B)
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism, compatible with $\partial$, with $T$ and with enlarging $A$. Moreover $N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C^A_*(B)$ is a subcomplex of $N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C_*(B)$, because $C^A_p(B)$ is a direct summand of $C_p(B)$.

Let $q\ge1$, let $z$ be a $q$-cycle, and choose a bound $A$ with $z\in N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C^A_q(B)$. By Proposition~\ref{app:contraction}, some $\varphi^r$ admits a controlled $q$-contraction $(h_p)_{p\le q}$ over $A$; let $A'\supseteq A\cup\varphi^r(A)$ be a bound containing the images of the $h_p$. Fix representatives of the cosets $E/B$. As a right $\mathbb{Z} B$-module, $\mathbb{Z} E$ is the direct sum of the submodules $g\mathbb{Z} B$, so every element of $\mathbb{Z} E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C^A_p(B)$ can be written uniquely as $\sum_gg\otimes x_g$ with $x_g\in C^A_p(B)$. Put
\[
H\Bigl(\sum_gg\otimes x_g\Bigr)=\sum_ggt^r\otimes h_p(x_g)\in\mathbb{Z} E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C^{A'}_{p+1}(B)\qquad(0\le p\le q).
\]
Since $\partial$ acts componentwise and $c_*$ vanishes in positive degrees,~\eqref{app:contraction-eq} gives $\partial H+H\partial=T^r$ on $\mathbb{Z} E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C^A_p(B)$ for $1\le p\le q$. Applying $H$ in every coordinate and using~\eqref{app:finite-types} for $A$ and $A'$, we obtain maps $H^I\colon N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C^A_p(B)\to N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C^{A'}_{p+1}(B)$ satisfying $\partial H^I+H^I\partial=T^r$ in the same degrees, since both sides lie in $N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z} B}C^{A'}_p(B)$ and agree in every coordinate. In particular $T^rz=\partial H^Iz$, so $T^r[z]=0$.

For a locally nilpotent $T$, the map $1-T$ is bijective, with inverse $x\mapsto\sum_{k\ge0}T^kx$. For $q\ge2$, the maps $1-T$ in degrees $q$ and $q-1$ of the sequence of Lemma~\ref{app:hnn-sequence} are therefore bijective, and $H_q(E,N)=0$.

<!-- lemma-13: body_md -->
\label{app:syzygies}
Let $m\ge1$, and let $\Gamma$ be a group of type $\mathrm{FP}_{m}$ such that $H_q(\Gamma,(\mathbb{Z}\Gamma)^I)=0$ for every set $I$ and every $q\ge m$. Then $\Gamma$ is of type $\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}$.
<!-- end lemma-13 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
Write $R=\mathbb{Z}\Gamma$, and let $P_*$ be a free resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$ whose terms $P_0,\dots,P_m$ have finite rank~\cite[VIII.4.3]{Bro}. Let $Z_m=\ker(d_m\colon P_m\to P_{m-1})$, let $I$ be its underlying set, and put $N=R^I$. Since $P_m$ and $P_{m-1}$ have finite rank, $N\otimes_RP_q\cong P_q^I$ for $q=m-1,m$, compatibly with $1\otimes d_m$. The family $\xi=(z)_{z\in Z_m}$ is therefore a cycle in $N\otimes_RP_m$, and $H_m(\Gamma,N)=0$ gives $\xi=(1\otimes d_{m+1})(y)$ for some $y=\sum_{a=1}^sx_a\otimes p_a$ with $x_a\in N$ and $p_a\in P_{m+1}$. In the coordinate indexed by $z$, this reads $z=\sum_a(x_a)_z\,d_{m+1}p_a$, so $Z_m$ is generated by $d_{m+1}p_1,\dots,d_{m+1}p_s$. Replacing $P_{m+1}$ by a free module of rank $s$ mapping onto $Z_m$, and the higher terms by a free resolution of its kernel, yields a free resolution that agrees with $P_*$ in degrees $\le m$ and has finite rank in degree $m+1$. Iterating produces a single free resolution of finite rank in every degree.
<!-- end proof-21 -->
