         C_{1k}^T & C_{2k}^T & C_{3k}^T & \cdots & M_k
        \end{bmatrix}\] be a real symmetric matrix, where $M_j$ is an $n_j\times n_j$ symmetric matrix and $C_{ij}$ is a matrix of appropriate order. Suppose there exist nonzero vectors ${\bf w}_j\in\mathbb{R}^{n_j},$ $1\leq j\leq k,$ such that 
        \[M_j{\bf w}_j=\lambda_j{\bf w}_j, \quad   C_{ij}{\bf w}_j=\alpha_{ij}{\bf w}_i, \quad \text{and}\quad C_{ij}^T{\bf w}_i=\beta_{ij}{\bf w}_j  \quad \text{for all}\quad 1\leq i< j\leq k.    \]
        Define ${\bf u}_j=[0,\ldots,0,{\bf w}_j,0,\ldots,0]^T,$ where ${\bf w}_j$ occupies the $jth$ block.
        Then the following holds.
        \begin{enumerate}
            \item $W=span\{{\bf u}_1,{\bf u}_2,\ldots,{\bf u}_k\}$ is an invariant subspace of $M.$ 
            \item If $\mathcal{Q}=[{\bf u}_1~~ {\bf u}_2~~ \cdots~~ {\bf u}_k],$ then $M\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}H,$ where $H=(h_{ij})_{i,j=1}^k$ and  \[h_{ij}=\left\{ \begin{array}{rcl}
            
 \alpha_{ij}, & \text{if\quad $i<j$,} \\
 \lambda_j, & \text{if \quad$i=j$,}\\

 \end{array}\right.\]

For each $j,$ the $ith$ block of $M{\bf u}_j$ is given by
   \[(M{\bf u}_j)_i=\left\{ \begin{array}{rcl}
 C_{ij}{\bf w}_j, & \text{if} \quad i<j,\\ 
 M_j{\bf w}_j, & \text{if} \quad i=j, \\
 C_{ji}^T{\bf w}_j, & \text{if} \quad i>j.
 \end{array}\right.\]

denotes the $jth$ column of $H,$ then the expression for $M{\bf u}_j$ shows that
$M{\bf u}_j=\mathcal{Q}{\bf h}_j,$
for every $j,$ and hence $M\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}H.$

\label{6t1}
    Suppose the premise of Lemma \ref{6l1} holds with each ${\bf u}_j$ being a unit vector. Then the graph associated with matrix $H$ exhibits fractional revival from vertex $a$ to $b$ if and only if the graph associated with matrix $M$ exhibits fractional revival from ${\bf u}_a$ to ${\bf u}_b.$
<!-- end thm-5 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By Lemma \ref{6l1}(2), we have  $M\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}H,$ which implies $M^k\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}H^k$ for all $k\geq 0.$ Therefore, for all $t\in\mathbb{R},$ we have $U_M(t)\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}U_H(t).$ Since $\mathcal{Q}{\mathbf e}_a={\bf u}_a,$ we obtain $U_M(t){\bf u}_a=U_M(t)\mathcal{Q}{\mathbf e}_a=\mathcal{Q}U_H(t){\mathbf e}_a.$ As $\mathcal{Q}^T\mathcal{Q}=I,$ it follows that, for some complex scalars $\alpha, \beta$ with $\beta\neq 0,$ $U_H(t){\mathbf e}_a=\alpha{\mathbf e}_a+\beta{\mathbf e}_b$ if and only if $U_M(t){\bf u}_a=\alpha{\bf u}_a+\beta{\bf u}_b.$

<!-- thm-15: body_md -->
Let $a$ and $b$ be two vertices in the different partite set of a complete bipartite graph $K_{n,n}$ with $n>2.$ Then the complementary prism $K_{n,n}\overline{K_{n,n}}$ exhibits Laplacian perfect state transfer between $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}({\mathbf e}_a+{\mathbf e}_b)$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}({\mathbf e}_{a'}+{\mathbf e}_{b'})$ if and only if $n$ is a multiple of $4.$ If $n=2,$ then $K_{2,2}\overline{K_{2,2}}$ exhibits perfect state transfer between $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_1+{\mathbf e}_3\right)$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_{2'}+{\mathbf e}_{4'}\right).$

Let ${\bf u}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_a+{\mathbf e}_b\right)$ and ${\bf v}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_{a'}+{\mathbf e}_{b'}\right).$ The eigenvalue support of ${\bf u}$ contains $0,2,n+2$ and $n.$ One may observe that  $0,n\in\sigma_{{\bf u},{\bf v}}^+(L)$ and $2,n+2\in \sigma_{{\bf u},{\bf v}}^-(L)$ for $n>2.$ Let $\lambda_1=n.$ By \cite[Corollary 5.7(3)]{god7}, PST occurs between ${\bf u}$ and ${\bf v},$ if and only if 
    \[\nu_2(n)>\nu_2(n-2)=\nu_2(-2)=1.\] 
    Now, $\nu_2(n-2)=1$ if and only if $n\equiv 0 \pmod {4}.$
    
For $n=2,$ the states ${\bf u}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_1+{\mathbf e}_3\right)$ and ${\bf w}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_{2'}+{\mathbf e}_{4'}\right)$ are strongly cospectral with $0,4\in \sigma_{{\bf u},{\bf w}}^+(L)$ and $2\in \sigma_{{\bf u},{\bf w}}^-(L).$ Now consider $\lambda_1=4.$ Since $v_2(4)>v_2(2),$  by \cite[Corollary 5.7(3)]{god7}, we have PST between $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_1+{\mathbf e}_3\right)$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_{2'}+{\mathbf e}_{4'}\right).$

<!-- thm-16: body_md -->
\label{6t13}
    There is no perfect plus state transfer in the complementary prism of a complete bipartite graph $K_{n,n}$ with respect to the adjacency matrix.

<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
For $n=1,$ the complementary prism $K_{n,n}\overline{K_{n,n}}$ is isomorphic to $P_4,$ and $P_4$ does not admit plus PST relative to $A$ \cite[Corollary 7.5]{god7}. For $n\geq 2,$
   the eigenvalues of $K_{n,n}\overline{K_{n,n}}$ are $\beta_{\pm}(2n,n)=\frac{2n-1\pm\sqrt{5}}{2},$ $\alpha_{\pm}(-n)=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{(1-2n)^2+4}}{2},$ and $\alpha_{\pm}(0)=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{5}}{2},$ with eigenvectors as given in Theorem \ref{5cor1}. Let $a,b$ and $a',b'$ be the vertices in $K_{n,n}$ and $\overline{K_{n,n}},$ respectively. If $a$ and $b$ are in the different partite set of $K_{n,n},$ then the eigenvalue support of $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_a+{\mathbf e}_b\right)$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_{a'}+{\mathbf e}_{b'}\right)$ contain $\beta_{\pm}(2n,n)$ and $\alpha_{\pm}(0).$ If $a$ and $b$ are in the same partite set, then the eigenvalue supports of plus states $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_a+{\mathbf e}_b\right)$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_{a'}+{\mathbf e}_{b'}\right)$ contain $\beta_{\pm}(2n,n)$ and $\alpha_{\pm}(-n).$ Lastly, for any $a,b$ in $K_{n,n},$ the eigenvalue support of $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf e}_a+{\mathbf e}_{b'}\right)$ contains $\beta_{\pm}(2n,n)$ and $\alpha_{\pm}(-n).$ Therefore none of these states are periodic by Theorem \ref{6t3}, resulting in no plus PST in $K_{n,n}\overline{K_{n,n}}.$
