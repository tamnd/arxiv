\label{k-PCA convergence}
    Fix $\delta>0$ and let $\left\{ \eta_t; \, 1 \leq t \leq n \right\}$ be an arbitrary sequence of positive learning rates such that

.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The bound \eqref{eq:eca-final} is a finite-sample bound that holds with no assumption on $R$ beyond continuity; in particular
it applies when $\mathbb E[|R|^\gamma]=\infty$, for every $\gamma>0$. This property is inherited from the sign transformation, which makes the transformed data bounded, so that the boundedness assumption of Section~\ref{sec:k-PCA-convergence} holds with universal constants. This is also the same principle as in \cite{han2014scale,han2018eca}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The eigengap in \eqref{eq:eca-transformed-spectrum} is approximately the same as the eigengap of $\bm\Sigma$ itself. Indeed, in high dimensions, we expect $D$ to  concentrate around
$\operatorname{tr}(\bm\Sigma)/p$, so that
\[
\tilde\lambda_i \approx \frac{\lambda_i}{\operatorname{tr}(\bm\Sigma)},
\qquad
\tilde\sigma_{ij}^2 \approx \frac{\lambda_i\lambda_j}{\operatorname{tr}(\bm\Sigma)^2},
\]
using $\mathbb E[U_i^2U_j^2]=\{p(p+2)\}^{-1}$ for $i\ne j$. The factors of
$\operatorname{tr}(\bm\Sigma)$ cancel, and the leading term becomes
\[
\frac{\ell_\delta}{n}
\sum_{i=1}^k\sum_{j=k+1}^p
\frac{\lambda_i\lambda_j}{\left( \lambda_i-\lambda_j \right)^2},
\]
which is exactly the asymptotic variance of the maximum likelihood estimator in the Gaussian model \cite{anderson1963asymptotic}. Thus nothing is lost by discarding the radial component, which is the same phenomenon observed in \cite{han2018eca}.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Let us discuss the total cost and memory storage of Algorithm \ref{alg:eca}. Below are the costs of the steps in the algorithm:
\begin{itemize}

\label{prop:inner-product}
    For decomposable elements $\bm u, \bm v \in \bigwedge^k \mathbb R^p$, we have
    \[
    \langle \bm u, \bm v \rangle_{\wedge} = \mbox{det} \left( \bm U^\top \bm V \right)
    \]
    where $\bm U=[\bm u_1,\ldots,\bm u_k]$ and $\bm V=[\bm v_1,\ldots,\bm v_k]$, with $\bm u=\bm u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\bm u_k$ and $\bm v=\bm v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\bm v_k$.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- head-1: body_md -->
With $\bm u= \bm u_1 \wedge \bm u_2\wedge \dots \wedge \bm u_k$ and  $\bm v= \bm v_1 \wedge \bm v_2\wedge \dots \wedge \bm v_k$, write 
\[
\bm U:= \left( \bm u_1,\dots, \bm u_k \right), \qquad \bm V:= \left( \bm v_1,\dots, \bm v_k \right).
\]
                                                
Observe that
\begin{align*}
\bm u &= \left( \sum_{j_1=1}^p u_{1,j_1} \cdot  \bm e_{j_1} \right) \wedge \dots \wedge \left(  \sum_{j_k=1}^p u_{k,j_k} \cdot  \bm e_{j_k} \right) \\
&= \sum_{1\leq j_1,\dots, j_k \leq p} u_{1,j_1} \, u_{2,j_2}\dots u_{k,j_k} \cdot \bm e_{j_1} \wedge \dots \wedge \bm e_{j_k} = \sum_{I \in \mathcal I_k} \mbox{det} \left( \bm U_I^\top \right) \, \bm e_I. 
                                                                          
\end{align*}
Here $\bm U_I$ and $\bm V_I$ are the $k\times k$ submatrices of $\bm U$ and $\bm V$ consisting of the rows indexed by $I$.
Similarly, $\bm v =  \sum_{I \in \mathcal I_k} \mbox{det} \left( \bm V_I \right) \, \bm e_I$. Thus the Cauchy--Binet formula yields 
\[
\langle \bm u, \bm v \rangle_{\wedge} = \sum_{I \in \mathcal I_k}  \mbox{det} \left( \bm U_I^\top \right) \cdot  \mbox{det} \left( \bm V_I \right) = \det \left( \bm U^\top \bm V \right).
\]
$\hfill$ $\square$

\label{Bernstein's inequality}
 Suppose $\bm Y_1,\ldots,\bm Y_q$ are martingale differences in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space $(\mathbb H, \|.\|)$   with respect to some filtration $\left\{  \mathcal F_t; t \geq 1\right\}$. Assume that
\[
\|\bm Y_s\|\le b, \qquad \text{and} \qquad 
\sum_{s=1}^q
\mathbb E\left[
\|\bm Y_s\|^2
\mid\mathcal F_{s-1}
\right]
\le v.
\]
Then, for every $x>0$, with probability at least $1-2e^{-x}$,
\begin{equation*}
\left\| \sum_{s=1}^q\bm Y_s \right\| \le C\left( \sqrt{vx}+bx\right)
\end{equation*}
for some universal constant $C>0$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- head-11: body_md -->
Let $\bm S_j:= \sum_{s=1}^j \bm Y_s$. Theorem 3.4 in \cite{pinelis1994optimum} yields
\[
\mathbb P \left( \max_{1\leq j \leq q} \| \bm S_j \| \geq t \right) \leq 2 \exp \left\{ -\frac{v}{b^2} \cdot h \left( \frac{bt}{v} \right) \right\}
\]
where $h(u):= (1+u) \log (1+u) - u$.

By using the elementary inequality
\[
h(u) \geq \frac{u^2}{2(1+u/3)}, \qquad u\geq 0,
\]
this implies
\[
\mathbb P \left( \max_{1\leq j \leq q} \| \bm S_j \| \geq t \right) \leq 2 \cdot \exp \left\{ - \frac{t^2}{2(v+bt/3)} \right\}.
\]
Consequently, with probability at least $1-2e^{-x}$,
\[
 \| \bm S_q \|  \leq \max_{1\leq j \leq q} \| \bm S_j \| \leq \sqrt{2vx} + \frac{2bx}{3}. 
\]
Note that this is stronger than what we need. The proof is completed. $\hfill$ $\square$

Decompose 
$$\bm O = \bm M + \bm K$$ 
with

\bm M := \frac12 \left( \bm O + \bm O^\top \right) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \bm K := \frac12 \left( \bm O - \bm O^\top \right).

=

+

Independence is obvious because the pairs
$(\bm X_{2t-1},\bm X_{2t})$, $t=1,\ldots,n$, are disjoint.

\emph{(i)} By \eqref{elliptical model}, we have
$$\bm X_{2t}-\bm X_{2t-1} \stackrel{d}{=} \bm\Sigma^{1/2}\bm W$$ 
with
$\bm W := R_{2t}\bm U_{2t}-R_{2t-1}\bm U_{2t-1}$; the mean $\bm\mu$ cancels.


For any orthogonal $\bm O$ we have
$$\bm O\bm W = R_{2t}\bm O\bm U_{2t}-R_{2t-1}\bm O\bm U_{2t-1}
\stackrel{d}{=}\bm W,$$ 
so $\bm W$ is spherically symmetric. 

Hence $\bm W\stackrel{d}{=}\|\bm W\|\cdot\bm U$ with $\bm U\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\mathbb
S^{p-1})$ independent of $\|\bm W\|$. Since $R$ is continuous,
$\bm W\ne\bm 0$ almost surely. The positive scalar $\|\bm W\|$ therefore
cancels between numerator and denominator of \eqref{eq:eca-preprocessing}.

\emph{(ii)} By \emph{(i)}, $\bm{\mathcal X}_1\stackrel{d}{=}-\bm{\mathcal X}_1$, whence
$\mathbb E\bm{\mathcal X}_1=\bm0$; and $\|\bm{\mathcal X}_1\|=1$ by construction. Both
$\bm{\mathcal A}_1$ and $\widetilde{\bm\Sigma}$ are positive semi-definite with
operator norm at most one, so
$-\widetilde{\bm\Sigma}\preceq\bm{\mathcal A}_1-\widetilde{\bm\Sigma}\preceq
\bm{\mathcal A}_1$ gives $\|\bm{\mathcal A}_1-\widetilde{\bm\Sigma}\|_{\rm op}\le1$.
Hence $M=1$ and $L=\tilde\lambda_1+M$.

\emph{(iii)} Recall $D$ in \eqref{eq:eca-transformed-spectrum}. By \emph{(i)},
$\bm u_i^\top\bm{\mathcal X}_1=\sqrt{\lambda_i}\,U_i/\sqrt D$, so
$$\bm u_i^\top \, \widetilde{\bm\Sigma} \, \bm u_j=\mathbb E \left[\sqrt{\lambda_i\lambda_j} \cdot \frac{U_iU_j}{D} \right].$$ 
For $i\ne j$ the map $U_i\mapsto-U_i$ preserves the law of $\bm U$
and fixes the value of $D$, while flipping the sign of $U_iU_j$. Hence the off-diagonal
entries vanish and the diagonal ones are the $\tilde\lambda_i$. 
Summing gives
$$\sum_i\tilde\lambda_i=\mathbb E\|\bm{\mathcal X}_1\|^2=1.$$
For the ordering, fix $i\ne j$ and condition on $\left\{ U_\ell:\ell\ne i,j \right\}$ and
on $U_i^2+U_j^2$. Conditionally, $(a,b):=(U_i^2,U_j^2)$ is exchangeable.
Define
\[
c:=\sum_{\ell\ne i,j}\lambda_\ell U_\ell^2, \qquad 
Q:=c+\lambda_ib+\lambda_ja.
\]
By the distributional identity $(a,b)\stackrel{d}{=}(b,a)$, we have 
\[
2\left( \tilde\lambda_i-\tilde\lambda_j \right)
=
\mathbb E\left[
\frac{(\lambda_ia-\lambda_jb)Q+(\lambda_ib-\lambda_ja)D}{DQ}
\right] .
\]
Expanding the numerator,
\[
(\lambda_ia-\lambda_jb)Q+(\lambda_ib-\lambda_ja)D
=
\left( \lambda_i-\lambda_j \right)
\left[ c(a+b)+2\left( \lambda_i+\lambda_j \right) ab \right] ,
\]
which is non-negative when $\lambda_i\ge\lambda_j$.

\emph{(iv)} The first two bounds follow from $\sum_i\tilde\lambda_i=1$. For the
variance, $\bm u_j^\top\widetilde{\bm\Sigma}\bm u_i=0$ for $i\ne j$ by
\emph{(iii)}, so
$$\sigma_k^2=\sum_{i\le k}\sum_{j>k}\mathbb E[(\bm u_i^\top\bm{\mathcal X}_1)^2
(\bm u_j^\top\bm{\mathcal X}_1)^2]=\sum_{i\le k}\sum_{j>k}\tilde\sigma_{ij}^2.$$

\[
\sum_{i=1}^k\sum_{j=k+1}^p \frac{\lambda_i\lambda_jU_i^2U_j^2}{D^2}
\le
\left( \sum_{i=1}^p \frac{\lambda_iU_i^2}{D} \right)^2
=1 ,
\]
and taking expectations gives $\sigma_k^2\le1$. $\hfill\square
