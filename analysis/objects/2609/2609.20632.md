<!-- head-1: body_md -->
\label{ex:SL1}
As a running example, we consider $n$~coupled identical Stuart--Landau oscillators.
Identify $\mathbb{C} \equiv \mathbb{R}^2$ and write $z_k = x_k+iy_k \equiv (x_k,y_k)$ for the complex coordinates with $i=\sqrt{-1}$ denoting the imaginary unit.
An (isolated) Stuart--Landau oscillator, whose state $z_k\in\mathbb{C}$ evolves according to $\dot z_k = F_k(z_k)$ with
\[F_k(z_k)=(a+ib)z_k+(c+id)\lvert z_k \rvert^2z_k,\]
and parameters $a>0$ and $c<0$ has a stable hyperbolic limit cycle $\Gamma(t) = \{\lvert z_k \rvert=R\}=R\mathrm{e}^{i\omega t}$, with radius $R = \sqrt{-\frac{a}{c}}$, frequency $\omega=b-\frac{ad}{c}$, and Floquet exponent $\lambda = -2a$. The oscillators being identical, the frequency $\omega$ is the same for each isolated system.
A network of $n$ coupled Stuart--Landau oscillators now has a normally hyperbolic invariant torus~$\Gamma^n$ that persists for $\varepsilon>0$. $\square$\\
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- head-2: body_md -->
Parameterizing the unperturbed limit cycle~$\Gamma$ of the Stuart--Landau oscillators in the example above by
\[e^{(0)}_k = R\mathrm{e}^{i\vartheta_k}\]
yields the trivial zeroth order phase reduction with a uniformly rotating phase $\dot\vartheta_k = \omega = 1$.
<!-- end head-2 -->
<!-- head-3: body_md -->
For the Stuart--Landau oscillators in Example 1, the projections $\Pi, \mathbf{1}-\Pi$ at phase $\vartheta_k = 0$ ($z_k = 0$) of a vector $w = u+iv \equiv (u,v)$ onto~$\mathbf{T}_k$ and~$\mathbf{N}_k$ are
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:Pi0}
\begin{align}
\Pi_k(0)(u+iv) &=i\left(v-\left(\frac{d}{c}\right)u\right),\\
(1-\Pi_k(0))(u+iv) &=u+iu\left(\frac{d}{c}\right).
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The rotational symmetry allows to compute the corresponding projections by rotating~$w$ to zero phase, applying $\Pi_k(0)$ and rotating back, that is,
\begin{align*}
\Pi_k(\vartheta_k)(u+iv) &= \mathrm{e}^{i\vartheta_k}\Pi_k(0)(u+iv)\mathrm{e}^{-i\vartheta_k},\\
(1-\Pi_k(\vartheta_k))(u+iv) &= \mathrm{e}^{i\vartheta_k}(1-\Pi_k(0))(u+iv)\mathrm{e}^{-i\vartheta_k}.
\end{align*}
For the system of $n$~oscillators, the projections, which determine the right hand side of~\eqref{eq:conj_order}, are given by the matrices
\begin{align*}
\Pi(\vec{\theta}) &= \operatorname{diag}(\mathrm{e}^{i\vartheta_k}\Pi_k(0)\mathrm{e}^{-i\vartheta_k})\\
\mathbf{1}-\Pi(\vec{\theta}) &= \operatorname{diag}(\mathrm{e}^{i\vartheta_k}(1-\Pi_k(0))\mathrm{e}^{-i\vartheta_k}).
\end{align*} $\square$
<!-- end head-3 -->
<!-- head-4: body_md -->
For the Stuart--Landau oscillators in the above Example 1, the pseudo inverses of $\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{N}$ are given by
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{T}^+(\vartheta) &= \operatorname{diag}((iR)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-i\vartheta_k}),&
\mathbf{N}^+(\vartheta) &= \operatorname{diag}((c+id)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-i\vartheta_k}).
\end{align*}
This is all the information needed to compute first and second order phase approximations for specific linear and nonlinear coupling~$\vec G$ between oscillators. $\square$\\
