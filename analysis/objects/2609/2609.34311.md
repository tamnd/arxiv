<!-- head-1: body_md -->
Does the
$L\log L$ endpoint bound for the rough commutator persist uniformly over
all truncation parameters, without strengthening the
$L(\log L)^2$ hypothesis on $\Omega$?
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
	Let $d\geq 2$. Suppose that $\Omega$ is homogeneous of degree zero,
	has mean zero on $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, and belongs to
	$L(\log L)^2(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})$. If
	$b\in\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$, then, for every $\lambda>0$,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-endpoint}
		\bigl|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:T^{*}_{\Omega,b} f(x)>\lambda\}\bigr|
		\lesssim_{\Omega,b}
		\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
		\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,\,\mathrm{d} x.
	\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- head-2: body_md -->
To demonstrate our novelty, 
we now explain a little bit of the main ideas in the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}.

We begin by replacing arbitrary truncations with dyadic ones and linearizing
the resulting maximal operator. A Calder\'on-Zygmund decomposition of $f$
then separates the good and bad contributions. The good part is controlled
by $L^2$ estimates. For a bad function supported on a cube $Q$, we use
\[
	b(x)-b(y)
	=
	\bigl(b(x)-b_Q\bigr)-\bigl(b(y)-b_Q\bigr).
\]
The term containing $b(y)-b_Q$ is reduced to Lai's estimate
\eqref{eq:Lai-maximal-weak11}, applied to a modified bad function. The term
with $b(x)-b_Q$ outside the singular integral is the principal new
difficulty.

Let $s$ measure the separation between the scale of a bad cube and the scale
of the kernel. At each $s$, we split
\[
	\Omega
	=
	\Omega\chi_{\{|\Omega|>2^{\eta s}\}}
	+
	\Omega\chi_{\{|\Omega|\leq 2^{\eta s}\}}.
\]
The large part is handled in $L^1$ by combining Orlicz estimates with the
John-Nirenberg inequality. The summability in $s$ is exactly where the
$L(\log L)^2$ hypothesis enters. For the bounded part of the kernel, a
Rademacher-Menshov linearization and Lai's dyadic organization reduce the
argument to an $L^2$ estimate with decay in $s$.

To obtain this decay, we regularize the spatial cutoffs and separate the
boundary, low-frequency, and high-frequency contributions. The first two are
controlled by localized $L^1$ and $L^3$ estimates and interpolation. For the
high-frequency term, a microlocal projection $G_\nu$ yields the identity
\[
	(b-b_Q)u
	=
	G_\nu\bigl[(b-b_Q)u\bigr]
	+[b,G_\nu]u
	+(b-b_Q)(I-G_\nu)u,
\]
where $u$ denotes a localized high-frequency piece. This decomposition is
the main point at which the commutator argument departs from the proof for
$T^{*}_{\Omega}$. The principal microlocal term is treated by a $TT^*$ argument, the
middle term by analytic conjugation and weighted interpolation, and the
remainder by localized interpolation. Their combined decay is summable in
$s$ and closes the endpoint estimate.
\vspace{0.3cm}

Section \ref{sec:preliminaries} records the Orlicz and BMO estimates,

Section \ref{sec:reduction-linearization} reduces Theorem \ref{thm:main} to a

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:young-function}
	A continuous function $\Psi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ is called a
	Young function if it is convex and increasing, $\Psi(0)=0$, and
	$\Psi(t)\to\infty$ as $t\to\infty$.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:orlicz-norm}
	Let $Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$ be a cube.  For a measurable function $f$ on $Q$, its
	localized Luxemburg norm is defined by
	\begin{equation*}
		\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Psi(L),Q}
		\coloneqq \inf\left\{
			\lambda>0:
			\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
			\int_Q\Psi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert}{\lambda}\Big)\,\,\mathrm{d} x
			\leq1
		\right\}.
	\end{equation*}
	We denote the corresponding local Orlicz space by $\Psi(L)(Q)$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:orlicz-holder}
	Let $\Psi$ be a Young function, with complementary function $\overline{\Psi}$.
	For every cube $Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and all measurable functions $f,g$ on $Q$,
	\begin{equation*}
		\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
		\int_Q\left\lvert f(x)g(x)\right\rvert\,\,\mathrm{d} x
		\leq
		2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Psi(L),Q}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\overline{\Psi}(L),Q}.
	\end{equation*}
	Moreover,
	\begin{equation*}
		\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Psi(L),Q}
		\approx
		\sup_{\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\overline{\Psi}(L),Q}\leq1}
		\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
		\int_Q\left\lvert f(x)g(x)\right\rvert\,\,\mathrm{d} x,
	\end{equation*}
	with absolute comparison constants.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:orlicz-local-interpolation}
	Let $Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$ be a cube, let $1<p<\infty$ and $\alpha\geq0$, and
	let $0<C_1\leq1$.  Suppose that $f$ is measurable on $Q$ and
	\begin{equation*}
		\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
		\int_Q\left\lvert f(y)\right\rvert\,\,\mathrm{d} y
		\leq C_1,
		\quad
		\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^p(\log L)^{-\alpha},Q}\leq1.
	\end{equation*}
	Fix $1<q<p$, and let $r\in(0,1)$ be determined by
	\begin{equation*}
		\frac{1}{q}=r+\frac{1-r}{p}.
	\end{equation*}
	Then, for every $0<\varepsilon<r$,
	\begin{equation*}
		\left(
			\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
			\int_Q\left\lvert f(y)\right\rvert^q\,\,\mathrm{d} y
		\right)^{1/q}
		\lesssim_{p,q,\alpha,\varepsilon}
		C_1^{\varepsilon}.
	\end{equation*}

<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def:BMO}
	Let $b\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})$.  For a cube $Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$, set
	\begin{equation*}
		b_Q\coloneqq\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}\int_Q b(x)\,\,\mathrm{d} x.
	\end{equation*}
	The BMO seminorm of $b$ is
	\begin{equation*}
		\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}
		\coloneqq \sup_Q
		\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
		\int_Q\left\lvert b(x)-b_Q\right\rvert\,\,\mathrm{d} x,
	\end{equation*}
	where the supremum is taken over all cubes $Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$.  We write
	$b\in\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$ when $\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}<\infty$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:john-nirenberg}
	There exist constants $c_d,C_d>0$ such that, for every
	$b\in\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$, every cube $Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$, and every $t>0$,
	\begin{equation*}
		\left\lvert \left\{x\in Q:\left\lvert b(x)-b_Q\right\rvert>t\right\}\right\rvert
		\leq
		C_d \exp\left(-\frac{c_dt}{\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}}\right)
		\left\lvert Q\right\rvert.
	\end{equation*}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:bmo-intexp}
	There exist constants $c_d,C_d>0$ such that, for every
	$b\in\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$, every cube $Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$, and every $s\geq0$ satisfying
	$s\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}\leq c_d$,
	\begin{equation*}
		\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
		\int_Q\exp\left(s\left\lvert b(x)-b_Q\right\rvert\right)\,\,\mathrm{d} x
		\leq 1+C_ds\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}.
	\end{equation*}
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:bmo-expL}
	For every $b\in\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$ and every cube $Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$,
	\begin{equation*}
		\left\lVert b-b_Q\right\rVert_{\exp L,Q}
		\lesssim
		\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}.
	\end{equation*}
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:bmo-Lp}
	For every $1\leq p<\infty$, every $b\in\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$, and every cube
	$Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$,
	\begin{equation*}
		\left(
			\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
			\int_Q\left\lvert b(x)-b_Q\right\rvert^p\,\,\mathrm{d} x
		\right)^{1/p}
		\lesssim_{d,p} \left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}.
	\end{equation*}

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor:bmo-subset-integral}
	Let $R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$ be a cube, and let $E\subset R$ be measurable with
	$0<\left\lvert E\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert R\right\rvert$.  Then, for every $b\in\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$,
	\begin{equation*}
		\int_E\left\lvert b(x)-b_R\right\rvert\,\,\mathrm{d} x
		\lesssim_d
		\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}\left\lvert E\right\rvert
		\left(1+\log\frac{\left\lvert R\right\rvert}{\left\lvert E\right\rvert}\right).
	\end{equation*}
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Set $N=\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}$.  The assertion is immediate if $N=0$.
	Otherwise, the layer-cake representation and Theorem
	\ref{thm:john-nirenberg} give, for every $t_0>0$,
	\begin{align*}
		\int_E\left\lvert b(x)-b_R\right\rvert\,\,\mathrm{d} x
		&=\int_0^\infty
		\left\lvert E\cap\left\{x\in R:\left\lvert b(x)-b_R\right\rvert>t\right\}\right\rvert\,\,\mathrm{d} t\\
		&\leq \left\lvert E\right\rvert t_0
		+C_d\left\lvert R\right\rvert\int_{t_0}^\infty
		\exp\left(-\frac{c_dt}{N}\right)\,\,\mathrm{d} t\\
		&=\left\lvert E\right\rvert t_0
		+\frac{C_dN}{c_d}\left\lvert R\right\rvert
		\exp\left(-\frac{c_dt_0}{N}\right).
	\end{align*}
	Taking
	\begin{equation*}
		t_0=\frac{N}{c_d}
		\log\left(\frac{C_d\left\lvert R\right\rvert}{\left\lvert E\right\rvert}\right)
	\end{equation*}
	proves the claim.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:bmo-orlicz}
	For every $b\in\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$, every cube $Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$, and every locally

	\begin{equation*}
		\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
		\int_Q\left\lvert b(x)-b_Q\right\rvert\left\lvert h(x)\right\rvert\,\,\mathrm{d} x

	\end{equation*}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:bmo-exponential-weight}
	Let $1<p<\infty$.  There exist constants $c_{d,p},C_{d,p}>0$ such that,
	whenever $b\in\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$ and $z\in\mathbb C$ satisfy
	\begin{equation*}
		\left\lvert z\right\rvert\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}\leq c_{d,p},
	\end{equation*}
	the weight
	\begin{equation*}                          
		w_z(x)\coloneqq e^{p\operatorname{Re}(z)b(x)}
	\end{equation*}
	belongs to $A_p$ and satisfies $[w_z]_{A_p}\leq C_{d,p}$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For each cube $Q$, the constant factor
	$e^{p\operatorname{Re}(z)b_Q}$ cancels from the $A_p$ expression.  Hence
	\begin{align*}
		&\left(\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
		\int_Qe^{p\operatorname{Re}(z)b(x)}\,\,\mathrm{d} x\right)
		\left(\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
		\int_Qe^{-p'\operatorname{Re}(z)b(x)}\,\,\mathrm{d} x\right)^{p-1}\\
		&\quad=
		\left(\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
		\int_Qe^{p\operatorname{Re}(z)(b-b_Q)}\,\,\mathrm{d} x\right)
		\left(\frac{1}{\left\lvert Q\right\rvert}
		\int_Qe^{-p'\operatorname{Re}(z)(b-b_Q)}\,\,\mathrm{d} x\right)^{p-1}.
	\end{align*}
	If $c_{d,p}$ is sufficiently small, Corollary
	\ref{cor:bmo-intexp}, applied with $s=p\left\lvert z\right\rvert$ and $s=p'\left\lvert z\right\rvert$,
	bounds both factors uniformly in $Q$.  Taking the supremum over $Q$
	proves the assertion.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:L1-L3-interpolation}
	Let $T$ be a linear operator on $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ such that
	\begin{equation*}
		\left\lVert Tf\right\rVert_{L^1}\leq A_1\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^1},
		\quad
		\left\lVert Tf\right\rVert_{L^3}\leq A_3\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^3}.
	\end{equation*}
	Then
	\begin{equation*}
		\left\lVert Tf\right\rVert_{L^2}
		\leq
		A_1^{1/4}A_3^{3/4}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^2}.
	\end{equation*}

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Apply the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem with interpolation parameter
	$\theta=3/4$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:stein-weiss}
	Let $T$ be a linear operator, and let $v_i,w_i$ be positive measurable
	weights for $i=0,1$.  Suppose that
	\begin{equation*}                                 
		\left\lVert Tf\right\rVert_{L^{p_i}(w_i)}
		\leq
		M_i\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p_i}(v_i)},
		\quad
		1\leq p_i<\infty.
	\end{equation*}
	For $0<\theta<1$, define $p_\theta$ by
	\begin{equation*}                                
		\frac{1}{p_\theta}
		=\frac{1-\theta}{p_0}+\frac{\theta}{p_1},
	\end{equation*}
	and define
	\begin{align*}
		w_\theta
		& \coloneqq w_0^{p_\theta(1-\theta)/p_0}
		w_1^{p_\theta\theta/p_1},
		\label{eq:stein-weiss-output-weight}\\
		v_\theta
		& \coloneqq v_0^{p_\theta(1-\theta)/p_0}
		v_1^{p_\theta\theta/p_1}.
		                                    
	\end{align*}
	Then
	\begin{equation*}                                  
		\left\lVert Tf\right\rVert_{L^{p_\theta}(w_\theta)}
		\leq
		M_0^{1-\theta}M_1^\theta
		\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p_\theta}(v_\theta)}.
	\end{equation*}
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:stein-weiss-p2}

			& \qquad \left\|\Omega_m(\cdot-y)\right\|_{L\log L,J} \\

			\right\}

	\end{align*}

	\coloneqq

	\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{J-s}}

	\Big].
\end{equation*}
We prove the required decay by a $TT^*$ argument.  First decompose $\mathcal{E}_{s,n,\iota}$
into directionally localized terms $\mathcal{E}_{s,n,\iota}^\nu$.


Recall that
\begin{equation*}
	\widehat{G_\nu  u}(\xi)
	=\Psi\Big(
	2^{s\gamma}
	\Big\langle e_\nu,\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\Big\rangle
	\Big)\widehat u(\xi).
\end{equation*}
The support of $\Psi$ implies that
\begin{equation*}
	\operatorname{dist}\Big(
	e_\nu,\big(\xi/|\xi|\big)^\perp
	\Big)
	\lesssim 2^{-s\gamma}.
\end{equation*}
Since the directions $\{e_\nu\}_\nu$ are $2^{-s\gamma-4}$-separated, this
spherical band contains at most $C_d2^{s\gamma(d-2)}$ such points.  Hence
\begin{equation*}
	\sup_{\xi\neq0}
	\sum_{\nu}
	\Big|
	\Psi\Big(
	2^{s\gamma}
	\Big\langle e_\nu,\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\Big\rangle
	\Big)
	\Big|^2
	\lesssim2^{s\gamma(d-2)}.
\end{equation*}
Plancherel's theorem yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma44-G-square-function}
	\sum_{\nu}
	\|G_\nu u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^d)}^2
	\lesssim
	2^{s\gamma(d-2)}
	\|u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^d)}^2.
\end{equation}
For each fixed $\nu$, set
\begin{equation*}
	\mathcal E_{s,n,\iota}^\nu
	\coloneqq 
	\sum_{J\in\mathcal I_{s,\iota}^{\#,n}}
	\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{J-s}}

	\varepsilon_J\big(T_{j,\nu} h_Q\big)
	\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}
	\Big].
\end{equation*}
For any $u \in L^2(\mathbb R^d)$, the Cauchy-Schwarz
inequality and \eqref{eq:lemma44-G-square-function} give
\begin{equation*}                                     
	\begin{aligned}
		\big|\langle\mathcal E_{s,n,\iota},u \rangle\big|
		&=\Big|
		\sum_{\nu}
		\langle\mathcal E_{s,n,\iota}^\nu,G_\nu  u \rangle \Big|\\

\end{equation}

\end{equation*}
Hence,

localization cube, with $\ell(I)=2^i$.  Expanding the square in

	\begin{aligned}
		\|\mathcal E_{s,n,\iota}^\nu\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^d)}^2 = & \sum_{J\in\mathcal I_{s,\iota}^{\#,n}}

	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The sum is symmetric in $(J,Q)$ and $(I,R)$.  After taking absolute values,
we may therefore restrict to $\ell(I)\leq\ell(J)$.  The support of
$L_{J,Q}^\nu(x,y)$ then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma44-TTstar-reduction}
	\begin{aligned}
		\|\mathcal E_{s,n,\iota}^\nu\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^d)}^2
		\lesssim{}&

		\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{J-s}}
		\int_Q|h_Q(y)|\\
		&\quad\times
		\int_{\mathbb R^d}
		| L_{J,Q}^\nu(x,y)|
		|b(x)-b_Q|
		S_J^\nu(x,y)dxdy,
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma44-SJ-definition}

				\ell(I)\leq\ell(J)}}
		\sum_{R\in\mathfrak Q_{I-s} }

		&\quad\times

A comparison of supports shows that $R$ meets

	\lesssim

\end{equation}

\begin{equation*}

	\lesssim2^{id-s\gamma(d-1)}.
\end{equation*}

		&\lesssim

\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:lemma44-composed-kernel-bound},
\eqref{eq:lemma44-local-bad-mass}, and
\eqref{eq:lemma44-bmo-on-sector} into
\eqref{eq:lemma44-SJ-definition}, we obtain

For $J\in\mathcal I_{s,\iota}^{\#,n}$ and
$Q\in\mathfrak Q_{J-s}$, define
\begin{equation*}

	\coloneqq 
	\big(I-P_{j-s\kappa}\big)\Big[
	\varepsilon_J\big(T_{j,\nu} h_Q\big)
	\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}
	\Big]
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}                       
	u_\nu
	\coloneqq 
	\sum_{J\in\mathcal I_{s,\iota}^{\#,n}}
	\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{J-s}}
	u_{J,Q,\nu}.
\end{equation*}
The expression in Lemma \ref{lem:sec42-commutator-microlocal} is therefore
\begin{equation*}                             
	\mathcal E_{s,n,\iota}
	\coloneqq 
	\sum_{J\in\mathcal I_{s,\iota}^{\#,n}}
	\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{J-s}}
	\sum_\nu [b,G_\nu] u_{J,Q,\nu}.
\end{equation*}


Recall that the multiplier of $G_\nu $ is
\begin{equation*}
	m_\nu(\xi)
	\coloneqq 
	\Psi\left(
	2^{s\gamma}
	\left\langle e_\nu,\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\right\rangle
	\right).
\end{equation*}
Thus $\widehat{G_\nu g}(\xi)=m_\nu(\xi)\widehat g(\xi)$.  We use the
analytic-family method of Coifman, Rochberg, and Weiss
\cite{CoifmanRochbergWeiss1976}, together with the quantitative weighted
bounds of Hyt\"onen and P\'erez \cite{HytonenPerez2013}, to prove the following
square-function estimate.
\begin{lemma}
	\label{lem:lemma45-commutator-micro}
	For every $u\in L^2(\mathbb{R}^{d})$,
	\begin{equation*}                                              
		\sum_\nu
		\|[b,G_\nu ]u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^d)}^2
		\lesssim
		2^{s\gamma(d-\frac32)}
		\|b\|_{\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb R^d)}^2
		\|u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^d)}^2.
	\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
	
	We first obtain a weighted $L^2$ square-function estimate for $G_\nu$ by
	Stein-Weiss interpolation.  The analytic-family argument then gives the
	corresponding estimate for the commutators.  We begin with the unweighted
	bound.
	
	If $m_\nu(\xi)\neq0$, then
	\begin{equation*}
		\left|
		\left\langle e_\nu,\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\right\rangle
		\right|
		\lesssim2^{-s\gamma}.
	\end{equation*}
	Since the directions $\{e_\nu\}_{\nu}$ are
	$2^{-s\gamma-4}$-separated, for each fixed $\xi\neq0$ there are at most
	$C_d2^{s\gamma(d-2)}$ directions for which $m_\nu(\xi)\neq0$.
	Hence,
	\begin{equation*}
		\sup_{\xi\neq0}
		\sum_\nu|m_\nu(\xi)|^2
		\lesssim2^{s\gamma(d-2)}.
	\end{equation*}
	By Plancherel's theorem,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma45-unweighted-G-square-function}
		\sum_\nu
		\|G_\nu  u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^d)}^2
		\lesssim
		2^{s\gamma(d-2)}
		\|u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^d)}^2.
	\end{equation}
	
	For the weighted estimate, set
	\begin{equation*}
		N \coloneqq d.
	\end{equation*}
	By the chain rule, for every
	$|\alpha|\leq N$ we have
	\begin{equation*}
		|\partial_\xi^\alpha m_\nu(\xi)|
		\lesssim_{\alpha}
		2^{s\gamma|\alpha|}|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}
		\lesssim_{d}
		2^{s\gamma N}|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}.
	\end{equation*}
	Thus, for every weight $w\in A_2$, the weighted Mikhlin multiplier theorem gives
	\begin{equation*}
		\|G_\nu u\|_{L^2(w)}
		\lesssim_{[w]_{A_2}}
		2^{s\gamma N}\|u\|_{L^2(w)}.
	\end{equation*}
	Using also
	$ \operatorname{card} \{e_\nu\} \lesssim 2^{s\gamma(d-1)}$, we obtain
	\begin{equation}	\label{eq:lemma45-weighted-rough-square-function}
		\begin{aligned}
			\sum_\nu\|G_\nu u\|_{L^2(w)}^2
			&\lesssim_{[w]_{A_2}}
			2^{s\gamma(d-1+2N)}
			\|u\|_{L^2(w)}^2 \\
			&\lesssim_{[w]_{A_2}}
			2^{s\gamma(3d-1)}
			\|u\|_{L^2(w)}^2.
		\end{aligned}
	\end{equation}
	
	
	We interpolate between \eqref{eq:lemma45-unweighted-G-square-function} and
	\eqref{eq:lemma45-weighted-rough-square-function}.  Set
	\begin{equation*}
		a_0 \coloneqq 4d+4,
		\qquad
		\theta_0 \coloneqq \frac{1}{1+a_0}=\frac{1}{4d+5}.
	\end{equation*}
	Suppose that $w^{1+a_0}\in A_2$, and regard
	$g\mapsto\{G_\nu g\}_\nu$ as an operator with values in $\ell^2$.  Apply the
	Stein-Weiss theorem to the unweighted bound and the weighted bound with
	weight $w^{1+a_0}$.  Since
	\begin{equation*}
		\big(w^{1+a_0}\big)^{\theta_0}=w,
	\end{equation*}
	we obtain
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma45-interpolated-weighted-square-function}
		\sum_\nu\|G_\nu u\|_{L^2(w)}^2
		\lesssim_{[w^{1+a_0}]_{A_2}}
		2^{s\gamma a_*}
		\|u\|_{L^2(w)}^2,
	\end{equation}
	where
	\begin{align*}
		a_*
		& \coloneqq (d-2)(1-\theta_0)+(3d-1)\theta_0\\
		&=d-2+\frac{2d+1}{4d+5}
		<d-\frac32.
	\end{align*}
	
	We now pass to the commutators.  By Lemma
	\ref{lem:bmo-exponential-weight}, there exists $c_d>0$ such that, whenever
	\begin{equation*}
		|z| \leq \rho
		\coloneqq 
		\frac{c_d}{\|b\|_{\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb R^d)}},
	\end{equation*}
	the weight
	\begin{equation*}
		w_z(x) \coloneqq e^{2\operatorname{Re}(z)b(x)}
	\end{equation*}
	satisfies $w_z^{1+a_0} \in A_2$ with a uniform bound, namely,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma45-exponential-weight}
		[w_z^{1+a_0}]_{A_2}\lesssim 1.
	\end{equation}
	For each $\nu$, define the analytic family
	\begin{equation*}
		F_\nu(z)  \coloneqq e^{zb}G_\nu (e^{-zb}u).
	\end{equation*}
	Then
	\begin{equation*}
		\frac{d}{dz}F_\nu(z) \bigg|_{z=0} = [b,G_\nu ]u.
	\end{equation*}
	By Cauchy's integral formula,
	\begin{equation*}
		[b,G_\nu]u
		=\frac{1}{2\pi i}
		\int_{|z|=\rho}
		\frac{F_\nu(z)}{z^2}\,dz.
	\end{equation*}
	Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the contour integral and summing over $\nu$ yields
	\begin{align*}
		\sum_\nu\|[b,G_\nu ]u\|_2^2
		&\lesssim
		\rho^{-3}
		\int_{|z|=\rho}
		\sum_\nu\|F_\nu(z)\|_2^2\,|dz|\\
		&=
		\rho^{-3}
		\int_{|z|=\rho}
		\sum_\nu
		\|G_\nu (e^{-zb}u)\|_{L^2(w_z)}^2\,|dz|.
	\end{align*}
	By \eqref{eq:lemma45-interpolated-weighted-square-function} and
	\eqref{eq:lemma45-exponential-weight},
	\begin{equation*}
		\sum_\nu
		\|G_\nu (e^{-zb}u)\|_{L^2(w_z)}^2

	\end{equation*}

	\end{equation*}
	Consequently,
	\begin{align*}

		&\lesssim
		2^{s\gamma(d-\frac32)}

	\end{align*}

We first obtain a weighted $L^2$ square-function estimate for $G_\nu$ by
	Stein-Weiss interpolation.  The analytic-family argument then gives the
	corresponding estimate for the commutators.  We begin with the unweighted
	bound.
	
	If $m_\nu(\xi)\neq0$, then
	\begin{equation*}
		\left|
		\left\langle e_\nu,\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\right\rangle
		\right|
		\lesssim2^{-s\gamma}.
	\end{equation*}
	Since the directions $\{e_\nu\}_{\nu}$ are
	$2^{-s\gamma-4}$-separated, for each fixed $\xi\neq0$ there are at most
	$C_d2^{s\gamma(d-2)}$ directions for which $m_\nu(\xi)\neq0$.
	Hence,
	\begin{equation*}
		\sup_{\xi\neq0}
		\sum_\nu|m_\nu(\xi)|^2
		\lesssim2^{s\gamma(d-2)}.
	\end{equation*}
	By Plancherel's theorem,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:lemma45-unweighted-G-square-function}
		\sum_\nu
		\|G_\nu  u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^d)}^2
		\lesssim
		2^{s\gamma(d-2)}
		\|u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^d)}^2.
	\end{equation}
	
	For the weighted estimate, set
	\begin{equation*}
		N \coloneqq d.
	\end{equation*}
	By the chain rule, for every
	$|\alpha|\leq N$ we have
	\begin{equation*}
		|\partial_\xi^\alpha m_\nu(\xi)|
		\lesssim_{\alpha}
		2^{s\gamma|\alpha|}|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}
		\lesssim_{d}
		2^{s\gamma N}|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}.
	\end{equation*}
	Thus, for every weight $w\in A_2$, the weighted Mikhlin multiplier theorem gives
	\begin{equation*}
		\|G_\nu u\|_{L^2(w)}
		\lesssim_{[w]_{A_2}}
		2^{s\gamma N}\|u\|_{L^2(w)}.
	\end{equation*}
	Using also
	$ \operatorname{card} \{e_\nu\} \lesssim 2^{s\gamma(d-1)}$, we obtain
	\begin{equation}	\label{eq:lemma45-weighted-rough-square-function}
		\begin{aligned}

			&\lesssim_{[w]_{A_2}}
			2^{s\gamma(d-1+2N)}
			\|u\|_{L^2(w)}^2 \\
			&\lesssim_{[w]_{A_2}}
			2^{s\gamma(3d-1)}

	\eqref{eq:lemma45-weighted-rough-square-function}.  Set

	\end{equation*}

		\big(T_{j,\nu} h_Q\big)
		\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}
		\right](x)
		\\
		&\qquad=:
		\int_Q D_{j,k,\nu}(x,y)h_Q(y)\,\,\mathrm{d} y,
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:lemma45-high-kernel-action}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation*}
	D_{j,k,\nu}(x,y)
	=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle x-w,\xi\rangle}M_{j,k,\nu}(\xi)
	\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal K_{j,\nu}(w-y)
	\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(w)\,\,\mathrm{d} w\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi.
\end{equation*}
Making the change of variables $w-y=r\theta$ with $\theta\in E_\nu$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		D_{j,k,\nu}(x,y)
		=C
		\int_{E_\nu}\Omega(\theta)
		\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_0^\infty
		&e^{ i\langle x-y-r\theta,\xi\rangle}
		M_{j,k,\nu}(\xi)
		\\
		&\times
		\frac{\varphi_j(r)}{r}
		\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y+r\theta)
		\,\,\mathrm{d} r\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(\theta).
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:lemma46-D}
\end{equation}



We shall also use the following weighted spatial-kernel estimate.
\begin{lemma}
	\label{lem:sec6-BMO-kernel}
	Let $y\in Q$ and $r\approx2^j$.  Suppose that either $z=y+r\theta$ or
	$z=y_t+r\theta$, where
	\begin{equation*}
		y_t=ty+(1-t)y_Q,
		\quad 0\leq t\leq1.
	\end{equation*}
	Then
	\begin{equation*}
		\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
		\frac{\left\lvert b(x)-b_Q\right\rvert}
		{\left(1+2^{-2k}\left\lvert x-z\right\rvert^{2}\right)^N}
		\,\,\mathrm{d} x
		\lesssim
		2^{kd}\big(s+\left\lvert j-k\right\rvert\big)\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}.
	\end{equation*}	
\end{lemma}


\begin{proof}
	Let $R=R_k(z)$ be the cube centered at $z$ with side length comparable to
	$2^k$.
	Choose a cube $S$ such that
	\begin{equation*}
		Q\cup R\subset S,
		\qquad
		\ell(S)\approx2^{\max\{j,k\}}.
	\end{equation*}
	Since $\ell(Q)=2^{j-s}$, we have
	\begin{equation*}
		\begin{aligned}
			\left\lvert b_Q-b_R\right\rvert
			&\leq
			\left\lvert b_Q-b_S\right\rvert+\left\lvert b_S-b_R\right\rvert
			\\
			&\lesssim
			\big(s+\left\lvert j-k\right\rvert\big)\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}.
		\end{aligned}
		                             
	\end{equation*}
	A dyadic annular decomposition centered at $R$, together with $2N>d$, gives
	\begin{equation*}
		\begin{aligned}
			&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
			\frac{\left\lvert b(x)-b_R\right\rvert}
			{\left(1+2^{-2k}\left\lvert x-z\right\rvert^{2}\right)^N}
			\,\,\mathrm{d} x
			\\
			&\quad\lesssim
			\sum_{m=0}^\infty
			2^{-2Nm}
			\int_{2^{m+1}R}\left\lvert b(x)-b_R\right\rvert\,\,\mathrm{d} x
			\\
			&\quad\lesssim
			\sum_{m=0}^\infty
			2^{-2Nm}(m+1)2^{md}\left\lvert R\right\rvert\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}
			\\
			&\quad\lesssim
			2^{kd}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}.
		\end{aligned}
		                            
	\end{equation*}
	Combining this with
	\begin{equation*}
		\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
		\left(1+2^{-2k}\left\lvert x-z\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{-N}\,\,\mathrm{d} x
		\lesssim2^{kd}
	\end{equation*}
	and
	$\left\lvert b-b_Q\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert b-b_R\right\rvert+\left\lvert b_R-b_Q\right\rvert$,
	proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
















We first consider the high-frequency contribution
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\mathcal E_{s,n,\iota}^{\mathrm{high}}
		\coloneqq  \sum_{J,Q} \sum_\nu
		\sum_{k\leq j-s\varepsilon}
		&(b-b_Q)
		\big(I-P_{j-s\kappa}\big)
		\Lambda_k^2
		(I-G_\nu) \big[\varepsilon_J(T_{j,\nu} h_Q)\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota} \big].
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:lemma45-high-definition}
\end{equation}
We combine the three Fourier multipliers and estimate the kernel of their
composition directly.  Minkowski's inequality gives
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		\left\lVert \mathcal E_{s,n,\iota}^{\mathrm{high}}\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
		\leq
		\sum_{J,Q} \sum_\nu
		\sum_{k\leq j-s\varepsilon}
		\left\lVert 
			(b-b_Q)		\big(I-G_\nu\big) 	\Lambda_k^2 \big(I-P_{j-s\kappa}\big) \big[ (T_{j,\nu} h_Q)\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota} \big]
		\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.
	\end{aligned}
	                                  
\end{equation*}



Let $N_1$ be a sufficiently large positive integer.  Integrating by parts
$N_1$ times in $r$ in \eqref{eq:lemma46-D} gives
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\int_0^\infty
		e^{- i r\langle\theta,\xi\rangle}
		\frac{\varphi_j(r)}{r}
		\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y+r\theta)\,\mathrm{d} r
		\\
		&\quad=
		\big( i\langle\theta,\xi\rangle\big)^{-N_1}
		\int_0^\infty
		e^{- i r\langle\theta,\xi\rangle}
		\partial_r^{N_1}
		\left[
		\frac{\varphi_j(r)}{r}
		\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y+r\theta)
		\right]\,\mathrm{d} r.
	\end{aligned}
	                              
\end{equation*}






The Leibniz rule gives
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\partial_r^{N_1}
		\left[
		\frac{\varphi_j(r)}{r}
		\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y+r\theta)
		\right]
		\\
		&\quad=
		\sum_{a=0}^{N_1}
		\binom{N_1}{a}
		\partial_r^a
		\left[\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y+r\theta)\right]
		\partial_r^{N_1-a}
		\left[\frac{\varphi_j(r)}{r}\right].
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:lemma45-high-Leibniz-r}
\end{equation}
Since $r \approx 2^j$, the derivative bounds for the smooth cutoff give 
\begin{equation*}
	\left|
	\partial_r^a
	\left[\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y+r\theta)\right]
	\right|
	\lesssim_a
	2^{-a(j-s\kappa)},
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
	\left|
	\partial_r^{N_1-a}
	\left[\frac{\varphi_j(r)}{r}\right]
	\right|
	\lesssim_{N_1}
	2^{-j(N_1-a+1)}.
\end{equation*}
Thus \eqref{eq:lemma45-high-Leibniz-r} yields
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\left|
		\partial_r^{N_1}
		\left[
		\frac{\varphi_j(r)}{r}
		\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y+r\theta)
		\right]
		\right|
		&\lesssim_{N_1}
		\sum_{a=0}^{N_1}
		2^{-a(j-s\kappa)}
		2^{-j(N_1-a+1)}
		\\
		&\lesssim_{N_1}
		2^{s\kappa N_1}2^{-j(N_1+1)}.
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:lemma45-high-r-derivative}
\end{equation}

The support of $\psi_k$ implies that $|\xi|\approx2^{-k}$.  Integrating by
parts in $\xi$ gives
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		& \quad \int_{|\xi| \approx 2^{-k}} e^{i \langle x-y-r\theta,\xi \rangle} \big( i \langle \theta,\xi \rangle \big)^{-N_1} M_{j,k,\nu}(\xi)\,\,\mathrm{d} \xi \\
		& = \int_{|\xi| \approx 2^{-k}} e^{i \langle x-y-r\theta,\xi \rangle} \frac{\big( I - 2^{-2k} \Delta_\xi\big)^N \big[ \big( i \langle \theta,\xi \rangle \big)^{-N_1} M_{j,k,\nu}(\xi) \big] }{\big( 1+2^{-2k} |x-y-r\theta|^2 \big)^N }\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi,
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
where $N = d$.

By the support properties of $1-\Psi$, 
\begin{equation*}
	\left|
	\left\langle e_\nu,\frac{\xi}{\left\lvert \xi\right\rvert}\right\rangle
	\right|
	\geq 2^{1-s\gamma}.
\end{equation*}
Since $\theta\in E_\nu$ implies
$\left\lvert \theta-e_\nu\right\rvert\leq2^{-s\gamma-2}$, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
	\left|
	\left\langle\theta,\frac{\xi}{\left\lvert \xi\right\rvert}\right\rangle
	\right|
	\geq
	\left|
	\left\langle e_\nu,\frac{\xi}{\left\lvert \xi\right\rvert}\right\rangle
	\right|
	-\left\lvert \theta-e_\nu\right\rvert
	\gtrsim2^{-s\gamma}.
	                                           
\end{equation*}
The support of $\psi_k$ gives $|\xi|\approx2^{-k}$.  Hence
\begin{equation*}
	\left\lvert \langle\theta,\xi\rangle\right\rvert
	\gtrsim2^{-s\gamma-k}.
	                                          
\end{equation*}
Furthermore, for every multi-index $|\alpha| \leq 2N$, 
\begin{equation*}
	\left|
	\partial_\xi^\alpha
	\left(\langle\theta,\xi\rangle^{-N_1}\right)
	\right|
	\lesssim
	2^{(s\gamma+k)(N_1+\left\lvert \alpha\right\rvert)}.
	                                         
\end{equation*}
Together with Lemma \ref{lem:sec6-M-derivative-bound}, this yields
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\left|
		\big(I-2^{-2k}\Delta_\xi\big)^N
		\left[
		\langle\theta,\xi\rangle^{-N_1}
		M_{j,k,\nu}(\xi)
		\right]
		\right| \lesssim 2^{(s\gamma+k)N_1}2^{2Ns\gamma}.
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:lemma45-high-xi-derivative}
\end{equation}







Substituting \eqref{eq:lemma45-high-r-derivative} and
\eqref{eq:lemma45-high-xi-derivative} into
\eqref{eq:lemma46-D}, we obtain the pointwise kernel bound
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\left\lvert D_{j,k,\nu}(x,y)\right\rvert
		\lesssim &
		2^{-j(N_1+1)}
		2^{s\kappa N_1}
		2^{(s\gamma+k)N_1}
		2^{2Ns\gamma}
		\\
		&\times
		\int_{E_\nu}\left\lvert \Omega(\theta)\right\rvert
		\int_{r \approx 2^j}
		\int_{\left\lvert \xi\right\rvert \approx 2^{-k}}
		\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d} r\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(\theta)}
		{\left(
			1+2^{-2k}\left\lvert x-y-r\theta\right\rvert^{2}
			\right)^N}.
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:lemma45-high-pointwise-kernel}
\end{equation}

By \eqref{eq:lemma45-high-kernel-action} and Fubini's theorem,
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\left\lVert 
			(b-b_Q) (I-G_\nu)  \Lambda_k^2  
			(I-P_{j-s\kappa}) \big[(T_{j,\nu} h_Q)\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota} \big]
		\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
		\\
		&\quad\leq
		\int_Q\left\lvert h_Q(y)\right\rvert
		\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
		\left\lvert b(x)-b_Q\right\rvert
		\left\lvert D_{j,k,\nu}(x,y)\right\rvert
		\,\mathrm{d} x\,\,\mathrm{d} y.
	\end{aligned}
	                                     
\end{equation*}
Insert \eqref{eq:lemma45-high-pointwise-kernel} and apply Lemma \ref{lem:sec6-BMO-kernel}. Since $|\xi| \approx 2^{-k}$, integration in frequency contributes $2^{-kd}$. Similarly, $r \approx 2^j$ means that integration in $r$ contributes $2^j$. Hence,
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\left\lVert 
			(b-b_Q)
			\big(I-P_{j-s\kappa}\big)
			\Lambda_k^2(I-G_\nu)\big[(T_{j,\nu} h_Q)\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota} \big]
		\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
		\\
		&\quad\lesssim 
		\big(s+j-k\big)
		\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}
		\left\lVert h_Q\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
		\\
		&\qquad\times
		2^{-(j-k)N_1}
		2^{s\kappa N_1+s\gamma N_1+2Ns\gamma}
		\int_{E_\nu}\left\lvert \Omega(\theta)\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(\theta).
	\end{aligned}
	                                                 
\end{equation*}
Recall that $\left\lVert \Omega\right\rVert_{L^\infty(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\leq2^{s\eta}$ and
$\left\lvert E_\nu\right\rvert\lesssim2^{-s\gamma(d-1)}$.  It follows that
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\left\lVert 
			(b-b_Q)
			\big(I-P_{j-s\kappa}\big)
			\Lambda_k^2(I-G_\nu)\big[(T_{j,\nu} h_Q)\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota} \big]
		\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
		\\
		&\quad\lesssim 
		\big(s+j-k\big)
		\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}
		\left\lVert h_Q\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
		\\
		&\qquad\times
		2^{-s\gamma(d-1)}
		2^{-(j-k)N_1}
		2^{s\kappa N_1+s\gamma N_1+2Ns\gamma+s\eta}.
	\end{aligned}
	                                     
\end{equation*}
Summing first over $k$ and using the high-frequency condition $k\leq j-s\varepsilon$ gives
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\sum_{k\leq j-s\varepsilon}
		\left\lVert 
			(b-b_Q) (I-G_\nu) \Lambda_k^2 \big(I-P_{j-s\kappa}\big) \big[(T_{j,\nu} h_Q)\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota} \big]
		\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
		\\
		&\quad\lesssim
		\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}
		\left\lVert h_Q\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
		2^{-s\gamma(d-1)}2^{-s(N_1(\varepsilon-\kappa-\gamma)
			-2N\gamma-\eta)}.
	\end{aligned}
	                                            
\end{equation*}
Summation in $\nu$ cancels the factor $2^{-s\gamma(d-1)}$.  Summing next in
$J,Q$ and applying Lemma \ref{lem:layered-bad-mass}, we obtain
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		\left\lVert \mathcal E_{s,n,\iota}^{\mathrm{high}}\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
		\lesssim 2^{-2n}2^{-s[N_1(\varepsilon-\kappa-\gamma)
			-2N\gamma-\eta]}
		\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}
		\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:lemma45-high-L1-before-absorption}
\end{equation}
If $\varepsilon>\kappa+\gamma$, choosing $N_1$ sufficiently large yields the
required decay for the high-frequency contribution.


We next turn to the low-frequency contribution.  Fix the parameters in the
order
\begin{equation*}
	0<\eta\ll\gamma\ll\kappa<\varepsilon\ll1.
	                                       
\end{equation*}
Define
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		\mathcal E_{s,n,\iota}^{\mathrm{low}}
		\coloneqq  \sum_{J,Q} \sum_\nu
		\sum_{k>j-s\varepsilon}
		&(b-b_Q)
		\big(I-P_{j-s\kappa}\big)
		\Lambda_k^2
		\big(I-G_\nu\big)
		\big[\varepsilon_J (T_{j,\nu} h_Q)\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota} \big].
	\end{aligned}
	                                  
\end{equation*}
By Minkowski's inequality,
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		\left\lVert \mathcal E_{s,n,\iota}^{\mathrm{low}}\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
		\leq \sum_{J,Q} \sum_\nu
		\sum_{k>j-s\varepsilon}
		\left\lVert 
			(b-b_Q)
			\big(I-P_{j-s\kappa}\big)
			\Lambda_k^2
			\big(I-G_\nu\big)
			\big[(T_{j,\nu} h_Q)\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota} \big]
		\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.
	\end{aligned}
	                                 
\end{equation*}
Integration by parts in $\xi$ in \eqref{eq:lemma46-D} gives
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		D_{j,k,\nu}(x,y)
		=C_d
		\int_{E_\nu}\Omega(\theta)
		\int_0^\infty\frac{\varphi_j(r)}{r}
		\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
		&e^{ i\langle x-y-r\theta,\xi\rangle}
		\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y+r\theta)
		\\
		&\times
		\frac{M_{j,k,\nu}^{[2N]}(\xi)}
		{\left(1+2^{-2k}\left\lvert x-y-r\theta\right\rvert^{2}\right)^N}
		\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\,\mathrm{d} r\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(\theta),
	\end{aligned}
	                                   
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
	M_{j,k,\nu}^{[2N]}(\xi)
	\coloneqq 
	(I-2^{-2k} \Delta_\xi)^N
	M_{j,k,\nu}(\xi).
	\label{eq:lemma45-low-L}
\end{equation*}




Let $y_Q$ be the center of $Q$.  Since $h_Q$ has mean zero,
\begin{equation*}
	\int_QD_{j,k,\nu}(x,y)h_Q(y)\,\,\mathrm{d} y
	=
	\int_Q
	\left[
	D_{j,k,\nu}(x,y)-D_{j,k,\nu}(x,y_Q)
	\right]
	h_Q(y)\,\,\mathrm{d} y.
	                                    
\end{equation*}
For brevity, write
\begin{equation*}
	W(x-y-r\theta)
	\coloneqq 
	\left(1+2^{-2k}\left\lvert x-y-r\theta\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{-N}.
\end{equation*}
Decompose
\begin{equation*}
	D_{j,k,\nu}(x,y)-D_{j,k,\nu}(x,y_Q)
	=
	D_{j,k,\nu}^{(1)}(x,y)
	+D_{j,k,\nu}^{(2)}(x,y)
	+D_{j,k,\nu}^{(3)}(x,y),
	                                   
\end{equation*}
where the first term records the difference of phases:
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		D_{j,k,\nu}^{(1)}(x,y)
		\coloneqq C_d
		& \int_{E_\nu}\Omega(\theta)
		\int_0^\infty\frac{\varphi_j(r)}{r}
		\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}} e^{ i\langle x-r\theta,\xi\rangle}
		\left(
		e^{- i\langle y,\xi\rangle}
		-e^{- i\langle y_Q,\xi\rangle}
		\right)
		\\
		& \quad \times
		\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y+r\theta)
		W(x-y-r\theta)
		M_{j,k,\nu}^{[2N]}(\xi)
		d \xi d r d \sigma(\theta);
	\end{aligned}
	                          
\end{equation*}
the second term records the difference of the smooth cutoffs:
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		D_{j,k,\nu}^{(2)}(x,y)
		&  \coloneqq C_d
		\int_{E_\nu}\Omega(\theta)
		\int_0^\infty\frac{\varphi_j(r)}{r}
		\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}} e^{ i\langle x-y_Q-r\theta,\xi\rangle}
		\\
		&\qquad \quad \times
		\left[
		\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y+r\theta)
		-\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y_Q+r\theta)
		\right] \\ 
		&  \qquad \quad \times W(x-y-r\theta)
		M_{j,k,\nu}^{[2N]}(\xi)
		d \xi d r d \sigma(\theta);
	\end{aligned}
	                          
\end{equation*}
and the third term records the difference in the centers of the denominators:
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		D_{j,k,\nu}^{(3)}(x,y)
		&  \coloneqq C_d
		\int_{E_\nu}\Omega(\theta)
		\int_0^\infty\frac{\varphi_j(r)}{r}
		\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}} e^{ i\langle x-y_Q-r\theta,\xi\rangle}
		\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y_Q+r\theta)
		\\
		& \qquad \quad \times
		\left[
		W(x-y-r\theta)-W(x-y_Q-r\theta)
		\right]
		M_{j,k,\nu}^{[2N]}(\xi)
		d \xi d r d \sigma(\theta).
	\end{aligned}
	                          
\end{equation*}




For $D_{j,k,\nu}^{(1)}$, the inequalities
$\left\lvert e^{iu}-e^{iv}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert u-v\right\rvert$,
$\left\lvert y-y_Q\right\rvert\lesssim2^{j-s}$, and $\left\lvert \xi\right\rvert\approx2^{-k}$ give
\begin{equation*}
	\left|
	e^{- i\langle y,\xi\rangle}
	-e^{- i\langle y_Q,\xi\rangle}
	\right|
	\lesssim
	2^{j-s-k}.
	                                        
\end{equation*}
The size bound for the kernel and the microlocal decomposition give
\begin{equation}
	\int_{E_\nu}\left\lvert \Omega(\theta)\right\rvert d \sigma(\theta)
	\lesssim
	2^{-s\gamma(d-1)+s\eta}.
	\label{eq:lemma45-low-angular-mass}
\end{equation}
Combining Lemmas \ref{lem:sec6-M-derivative-bound} and \ref{lem:sec6-BMO-kernel}, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
		\left\lvert b(x)-b_Q\right\rvert
		\left\lvert D_{j,k,\nu}^{(1)}(x,y)\right\rvert d x
		\\
		&\quad\lesssim
		\big(s+\left\lvert j-k\right\rvert\big)
		\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\operatorname{BMO}}
		2^{j-s\kappa-k}2^{2Ns\gamma}
		2^{-s\gamma(d-1)+s\eta}
		2^{j-s-k}.
	\end{aligned}
	                                   
\end{equation*}
Here integration over the frequency region contributes $O(2^{-kd})$, while
Lemma \ref{lem:sec6-BMO-kernel} contributes $2^{kd}$;
these factors cancel. Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
	\int_{r\approx2^j}\frac{\left\lvert \varphi_j(r)\right\rvert}{r}d r
	\lesssim1.
\end{equation*}


For $D_{j,k,\nu}^{(2)}$, the mean value theorem and
\begin{equation*}
	\left\lVert \nabla\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}\right\rVert_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
	\lesssim2^{-j+s\kappa},
\end{equation*}
we obtain
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		&\left|
		\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y+r\theta)
		-\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota}(y_Q+r\theta)
		\right|
		\\

		\left(1+2^{-2k}\left\lvert x-z-r\theta\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{-N}

		{\left(1+2^{-2k}\left\lvert x-z-r\theta\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{N+1}} \\
		& \lesssim \frac{2^{-k}}{\left(1+2^{-2k}\left\lvert x-z-r\theta\right\rvert^{2}\right)^N}.

		\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
		\\

		\left\lVert h_Q\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

		& \qquad \times \left[

	\end{aligned}
	                                       
\end{equation*}

		\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak Q_{J-s}}
		\sum_\nu \sum_{k > j-s\varepsilon} \left\lVert 
			(b-b_Q)

			\Lambda_k^2(I-G_\nu)\big[(T_{j,\nu} h_Q)\widetilde\chi_{J^\iota} \big]

		\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.
	\end{aligned}
	                                         
\end{equation*}

\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		& 1+\kappa-2\varepsilon-\eta-2N\gamma>0, \\
		& \ 1-\varepsilon-\eta-2N\gamma>0,
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

		\| \mathcal{E}_{s,n,\iota} \|_{L^1} & \lesssim 2^{-2n} \|b\|_{\operatorname{BMO}} \|f\|_{L^1} \\
		& \quad\times\big[
		2^{-s[N_1(\varepsilon-\kappa-\gamma)-2N\gamma-\eta]}
		+2^{-s(1+\kappa-2\varepsilon-\eta-2N\gamma)}
		+2^{-s(1-\varepsilon-\eta-2N\gamma)}\big].
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent{\bf Interpolation.}
Interpolating \eqref{eq:lemma46-L3} with \eqref{eq:lemma46-L1}, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
	\begin{aligned}
		\|\mathcal{E}_{s,n,\iota}\|_{L^2} & \lesssim \| \mathcal{E}_{s,n,\iota} \|_{L^1}^{1/4} \| \mathcal{E}_{s,n,\iota} \|_{L^3}^{3/4} \\
		& \lesssim 2^{-n} (2^{-s\theta_1} + 2^{-s \theta_2} + 2^{-s \theta_3}) \|b\|_{\operatorname{BMO}} \|f\|_{L^1}^{1/2},
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{align*}
	\theta_1 & = \frac{1}{4} \big[ N_1(\varepsilon-\kappa-\gamma)-2N\gamma-\eta \big] - \frac{3}{4} \big[ \gamma(N+d-1) + \eta \big], \\
	\theta_2 & = \frac{1}{4} \big[ 1+\kappa-2\varepsilon-\eta-2N\gamma \big] - \frac{3}{4} \big[ \gamma(N+d-1) + \eta \big], \\
	\theta_3 & = \frac{1}{4} \big[ 1-\varepsilon-\eta-2N\gamma \big] - \frac{3}{4} \big[ \gamma(N+d-1) + \eta \big]. \\
\end{align*}
Choose $\varepsilon$ and $\kappa$ sufficiently small with
$0<\kappa<\varepsilon$, then choose $\gamma$ and $\eta$ successively smaller,
and finally take $N_1$ sufficiently large.  With these choices,
$\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3>0$, proving Lemma
\ref{lem:sec42-remainder-microlocal}.

		\qquad

			&\leq
