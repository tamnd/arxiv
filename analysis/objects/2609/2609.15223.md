<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:complementarity-map}
Let $\gamma>0$ and $a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}$. Then
\begin{equation}
a = \frac{1}{\gamma}\left[\gamma a - b\right]_+
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
a\geq 0,\quad b\geq 0,\quad ab = 0
\label{eq:scalar-complementarity-equivalence}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The right-hand side is nonnegative, so $a<0$ is impossible. For $a>0$ the
identity holds if and only if $\gamma a-b=\gamma a$, that is, $b=0$, and for
$a=0$ it holds if and only if $[-b]_+=0$, that is, $b\geq 0$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:reynolds-well-posedness}
Let $d^3\in L_\infty(\Omega)$ with $d^3\geq d_0^3>0$ and let
$f\in L_2(\Omega)$. Then the obstacle problem of finding $P\in K$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reynolds-variational-inequality}
a(P,v-P)\geq (f,v-P)_\Omega\qquad\forall v\in K
\end{equation}
has exactly one solution. If moreover
$\lambda := -\nabla\cdot(d^3\nabla P)-f$ belongs to $L_2(\Omega)$, then for
every $\gamma>0$ the pair $(P,\lambda)$ solves
\eqref{eq:reynolds-augmented-primal}--\eqref{eq:reynolds-augmented-multiplier}, and conversely every solution of
\eqref{eq:reynolds-augmented-primal}--\eqref{eq:reynolds-augmented-multiplier} is of this form. In particular the
solution set of \eqref{eq:reynolds-augmented-primal}--\eqref{eq:reynolds-augmented-multiplier} does not depend on
$\gamma$.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The form $a(\cdot,\cdot)$ is bounded and, by the Poincar\'e inequality and
$d^3\geq d_0^3$, coercive on $H^1_0(\Omega)$, and $K$ is nonempty, closed
and convex, so existence and uniqueness is the theorem of Lions and
Stampacchia \cite{KiSt80}. If $(P,\lambda)$ solves
\eqref{eq:reynolds-augmented-primal}--\eqref{eq:reynolds-augmented-multiplier},
Lemma~\ref{lem:complementarity-map} gives $P\geq 0$, $\lambda\geq 0$ and
$\lambda P = 0$ almost everywhere, and \eqref{eq:reynolds-augmented-primal}
reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reynolds-kkt-equilibrium}
a(P,q)-(\lambda,q)_\Omega = (f,q)_\Omega\qquad\forall q\in H^1_0(\Omega)
\end{equation}
so that $a(P,v-P)-(f,v-P)_\Omega=(\lambda,v)_\Omega\geq 0$ for $v\in K$.
Conversely, if $P$ solves \eqref{eq:reynolds-variational-inequality} and
$\lambda := -\nabla\cdot(d^3\nabla P)-f\in L_2(\Omega)$, taking $v=P+\varphi$
with $\varphi\in K$ gives $\lambda\geq 0$, and $v=0$ and $v=2P$ give
$(\lambda,P)_\Omega=0$, hence $\lambda P=0$ almost everywhere;
Lemma~\ref{lem:complementarity-map} then returns
\eqref{eq:reynolds-augmented-multiplier}. Neither step involves $\gamma$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- rem-1: body_md -->
The requirement $\lambda\in L_2(\Omega)$ is a regularity assumption on the
obstacle problem. If $f\in L_2(\Omega)$ and $d^3\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)$
is uniformly positive, interior regularity gives
$\lambda\in L_{2,{\rm loc}}(\Omega)$; see \cite{KiSt80}. Global
$L_2$ regularity of $\lambda$, and the $H^2$ regularity used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:reynolds-error}, require a corresponding global obstacle
regularity result, including assumptions on the domain, coefficient, data,
and compatibility at the boundary of the contact set. We therefore state
$P\in H^2(\Omega)$ and $\lambda\in L_2(\Omega)$ explicitly whenever those
properties are used. They allow the multiplier to be sought in
$L_2(\Omega)$ rather than only in $H^{-1}(\Omega)$.
<!-- end rem-1 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:reynolds-stability}
Let $d\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)$ with $d\geq d_0>0$, let $\mathcal{T}$ be
shape regular with $h_T\leq 1$, and let $\gamma\vert_T = \gamma_0h_T^2$.
Then there is a constant $C_I$, depending only on the shape regularity of
$\mathcal{T}$, on the polynomial degree $k$ and on
$\|d\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reynolds-frozen-coercivity}
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{A}}(v_h,v_h)\geq
\left(d_0^3-\gamma_0C_I^2\right)\|\nabla v_h\|^2_{\Omega}
\qquad\forall v_h\in V_h
\end{equation}
for every choice of $\mathcal{A}$. If $\gamma_0 < d_0^3C_I^{-2}$ then
$\mathfrak{F}_h$ is strictly convex on $V_h$, so \eqref{eq:reynolds-discrete-functional} has exactly
one solution, and every linear system arising in the active set iteration
is symmetric and positive definite, uniformly in $h$.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Write $Lv := \nabla\cdot(d^3\nabla v) = d^3\Delta v + 3d^2\nabla d\cdot\nabla v$.
The first term obeys the inverse estimate for polynomials and the second is
bounded outright, so that $h_T\|Lv_h\|_T\leq C_I\|\nabla v_h\|_T$ with
$C_I$ depending only on the shape regularity, on $k$ and on
$\|d\|_{W^{1,\infty}}$. Summing over the elements with
$\gamma\vert_T=\gamma_0h_T^2$ bounds the last term of \eqref{eq:reynolds-frozen-form} by
$\gamma_0C_I^2\|\nabla v_h\|^2_\Omega$, the middle term is nonnegative and
$a(v_h,v_h)\geq d_0^3\|\nabla v_h\|^2_\Omega$, which is \eqref{eq:reynolds-frozen-coercivity}. The
remaining term of $\mathfrak{F}_h$ is convex, being the composition of
$[\,\cdot\,]_+^2$ with an affine map, so under the stated bound $\mathfrak{F}_h$
is strictly convex.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- rem-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:reynolds-parameter-threshold}
The threshold of Theorem~\ref{thm:reynolds-stability} can be located exactly. Since the middle term of
\eqref{eq:reynolds-frozen-form} is nonnegative,
$\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{A}}\geq\mathcal{B}_\emptyset$ for every
$\mathcal{A}$: the empty set, which is also the first pass of the
iteration, is the worst case, and a threshold computed for it serves for
the whole iteration. Now $\mathcal{B}_\emptyset$ is
positive definite if and only if $\gamma_0<1/\Lambda_{\max}$, where
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{\max} := \sup_{v_h\in V_h\setminus\{0\}}\
\frac{\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}h_T^2\|\nabla\cdot d^3\nabla v_h\|_T^2}
{a(v_h,v_h)} \ \leq\ \frac{C_I^2}{d_0^3}
\label{eq:reynolds-threshold-eigenvalue}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:reynolds-frozen-coercivity}, $\Lambda_{\max}$ is bounded
under refinement. Section~\ref{subsec:reynolds-stabilized-results} computes
it for $\mathbb{P}_2$ and shows that above the resulting threshold the
frozen systems are indefinite and the iteration does not converge. Thus $\gamma$, arbitrary in the continuous formulation by
Theorem~\ref{thm:reynolds-well-posedness}, is in
\eqref{eq:reynolds-discrete-functional} a local, mesh-dependent parameter
with a computable admissible range.
<!-- end rem-2 -->
<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:reynolds-nodal-structure}
Let $V_h$ consist of continuous piecewise linears vanishing on
$\partial\Omega$ and let $A$ be the corresponding stiffness matrix, which
is symmetric and positive definite. Then, for every choice of
$\mathcal{A}$, the matrix in \eqref{eq:reynolds-nodal-system} is nonsingular. Its
solution satisfies $P_i = 0$ on $\mathcal{A}$ and $\lambda_i = 0$ off it,
so that the product $\lambda_iP_i$ vanishes at every node, for every
$\mathcal{A}$. The test argument $\gamma\lambda_i-P_i$ reduces to
$\gamma\lambda_i$ on $\mathcal{A}$ and to $-P_i$ on $\mathcal{I}$.
At a fixed point, membership therefore gives $\lambda_i\geq0$ on
$\mathcal{A}$ and $P_i>0$ on $\mathcal{I}$, because the test fails there.
Neither the frozen solutions nor the fixed point depend on $\gamma$, and
the fixed point satisfies the discrete Kuhn--Tucker conditions exactly.
Finally, the limit is the unique
minimiser of $\tfrac12a(v_h,v_h)-(f,v_h)_\Omega$ over
$K_h := \{v_h\in V_h:\ v_h\geq 0\ \text{in}\ \Omega\}$, and $K_h\subset K$.
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
With zero right-hand side the second block row gives $P_i=0$ on
$\mathcal{A}$ and $\lambda_i=0$ on $\mathcal{I}$, since $m_i>0$. The first
row at $i\in\mathcal{I}$ is then $(AP)_i=0$, and since $P$ vanishes on
$\mathcal{A}$, $P^{\rm T}AP=0$, so $P=0$; the first row on $\mathcal{A}$
then gives $\lambda=0$. The same computation with the actual right-hand side
gives the stated structure, and with it the reduction of the test argument
and the independence of $\gamma$. A piecewise linear function attains its
extrema at the nodes, so the nodal constraints describe $K_h$ exactly, and
$K_h\subset K$. At a fixed point the conditions $\lambda_i\geq 0$ and $P_i=0$
on $\mathcal{A}$ and $P_i>0$ and $\lambda_i=0$ on $\mathcal{I}$ are the
Kuhn--Tucker conditions of $\min\{\tfrac12v^{\rm T}Av-F^{\rm T}v:\ v\geq 0\}$,
which are sufficient because $A$ is positive definite.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- thm-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:reynolds-error}
Let $P$ and $\lambda$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:reynolds-well-posedness} and let $P_h$ solve the
nodal method. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reynolds-quasi-optimal-error}
\|P-P_h\|_{H^1(\Omega)}\leq C\inf_{v_h\in K_h}
\left(\|P-v_h\|_{H^1(\Omega)}
+\|\lambda\|_{L_2(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|P-v_h\|_{L_2(\Omega)}^{1/2}\right)
\end{equation}
with $C$ depending only on $d_0$, on $\|d^3\|_{L_\infty}$ and on $\Omega$.
If in addition $P\in H^2(\Omega)$ then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reynolds-h1-rate}
\|P-P_h\|_{H^1(\Omega)}\leq C h
\left(\vert P\vert_{H^2(\Omega)}
+\|\lambda\|_{L_2(\Omega)}^{1/2}\vert P\vert_{H^2(\Omega)}^{1/2}\right)
\end{equation}

\label{rem:outflow-flux}
The parts $\Gamma^{\mathrm{N}}$ and $\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}$, on which the normal
velocity is free, are not merely a convenience. Since
$\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}\geq 0$ almost everywhere by \eqref{eq:stokes-complementarity}, the divergence
theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outflow-flux-balance}
0\leq \int_\Omega \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega = \int_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}}\boldsymbol{g}\cdot{\boldsymbol n}\,\mathrm{d}s + \int_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{N}}\cup\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}}\boldsymbol{u}\cdot{\boldsymbol n}\,\mathrm{d}s
\end{equation}
so the volumetric expansion of the lubricant, integrated over the film,
equals the net outward flux through the boundary. If $\Gamma^{\mathrm{N}}\cup\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}=\emptyset$ and the Dirichlet data
satisfy the usual compatibility condition $\int_{\partial\Omega}\boldsymbol{g}\cdot{\boldsymbol n}\,\mathrm{d}s = 0$,
then \eqref{eq:outflow-flux-balance} forces $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u} = 0$ almost everywhere, the constraint
is saturated everywhere, and the model becomes equality-constrained
incompressible Stokes flow. The pressure is then an equality multiplier,
determined only modulo constants unless it is normalised. Thus an outflow
boundary through which the lubricant can expand is a prerequisite for
nontrivial cavitation in the present inequality formulation, for strict
feasibility, and for taking the unnormalised pressure space in
\eqref{eq:stokes-spaces} to be all of $L_2(\Omega)$.

\label{lem:continuous-divergence-range}
For the space $V$ in \eqref{eq:stokes-spaces},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divergence-surjectivity}

\label{thm:stokes-well-posedness}
Let the assumptions above hold, let $\vert\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}\vert>0$, and
assume that the only conformal Killing field satisfying the homogeneous
essential conditions in \eqref{eq:stokes-spaces} is zero. A sufficient
condition used here is that $\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}$ contain a relatively open
planar patch. Let $\mu>0$ be constant and let
$\mathcal{K} := \{\boldsymbol{v}\in V_{\boldsymbol{g}}:\ \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\geq 0
\text{ a.e.\ in }\Omega\}$, which is nonempty by the construction in
Remark~\ref{rem:strictly-feasible-velocity} below. Then the problem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stokes-constrained-minimization}
\boldsymbol{u} = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{K}}
\left\{\tfrac12a(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v})-L(\boldsymbol{v})\right\}
\end{equation}
has exactly one solution, there is exactly one $p\in Q$ with
$p\geq 0$ such that $(\boldsymbol{u},p)$ satisfies \eqref{eq:stokes-complementarity} together with
$a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})-(p,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})_\Omega = L(\boldsymbol{v})$ for all
$\boldsymbol{v}\in V$, and this pair solves \eqref{eq:stokes-augmented-form} for every $\gamma>0$.
Conversely, every solution of \eqref{eq:stokes-augmented-form} is this pair, so that, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:reynolds-well-posedness}, the solution set of \eqref{eq:stokes-augmented-form} does not depend on
$\gamma$.
<!-- end thm-5 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
$\mathcal{K}$ is closed and convex, and $a(\cdot,\cdot)$ is coercive on
$V$. For $n=2$ this is elementary: since
$(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})^2\leq 2\vert\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v})\vert^2$ pointwise,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stokes-deviatoric-coercivity}
a(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}) = 2\mu\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v})\|^2_\Omega
-\tfrac{2\mu}{3}\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\|^2_\Omega
\geq \tfrac{2\mu}{3}\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v})\|^2_\Omega
\end{equation}
and we can apply Korn's inequality, available because the full velocity trace vanishes
on $\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}$ of positive measure.

For $n=3$ the form is $2\mu\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\rm dev}(\boldsymbol{v})\|_\Omega^2$, and
coercivity on $V$ follows from the trace-free Korn inequality of Dain
\cite{Da06} and the kernel-elimination hypothesis, by the compactness
argument of Peetre and Tartar; Lemma~\ref{lem:continuous-trace-free-korn}
gives the version used here. The functional in \eqref{eq:stokes-constrained-minimization} is then strictly
convex and coercive on $V_{\boldsymbol{g}}$, which gives a unique $\boldsymbol{u}$.

For the multiplier we need a constraint qualification. Slater's condition
is unavailable, since the cone of nonpositive functions has empty interior
in $L_2(\Omega)$. Robinson's condition holds instead: writing
$\boldsymbol{v} = \boldsymbol{w}_0+\boldsymbol{z}$ with $\boldsymbol{w}_0\in V_{\boldsymbol{g}}$ fixed and
$\boldsymbol{z}\in V$, \eqref{eq:divergence-surjectivity} gives
$\{-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{z}-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{w}_0 :\boldsymbol{z}\in V\} = L_2(\Omega)$, so
$0\in{\rm int}\{-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{z}-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{w}_0+c:\ \boldsymbol{z}\in V,\ c\geq 0\}$. Convex duality in the form of the Zowe--Kurcyusz theorem \cite{ZoKu79}
then provides $p$ in the dual of $L_2(\Omega)$, which is $L_2(\Omega)$
itself, lying in the dual of the nonnegative cone, which is again the
nonnegative cone, so that $p\geq 0$; and it provides
$(p,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u})_\Omega=0$ together with
$a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})-(p,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})_\Omega = L(\boldsymbol{v})$ for
$\boldsymbol{v}\in V$. These are \eqref{eq:stokes-complementarity}. Uniqueness of $p$ is
\eqref{eq:divergence-surjectivity} once more. Lemma
\ref{lem:complementarity-map} applied pointwise to \eqref{eq:stokes-complementarity} gives
\eqref{eq:stokes-complementarity-map}, and substituting it in the equilibrium equation returns
\eqref{eq:stokes-augmented-form}; no step involves $\gamma$.

For the converse, taking $\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{0}$ in \eqref{eq:stokes-augmented-form}
shows that $\gamma^{-1}[\gamma p-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}]_+=p$ almost everywhere,
so \eqref{eq:stokes-complementarity} holds by
Lemma~\ref{lem:complementarity-map}, and taking $q=0$ returns the
equilibrium equation. For $\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{K}$,
$a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})-L(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})=(p,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})_\Omega\geq 0$,
so $\boldsymbol{u}$ solves \eqref{eq:stokes-constrained-minimization}, and uniqueness
identifies the pair.

\label{rem:strictly-feasible-velocity}
A strictly feasible field exists. Let $\tilde{\boldsymbol{w}}\in[H^1(\Omega)]^n$
extend $\boldsymbol{g}$, and choose $\boldsymbol{z}_1,\boldsymbol{z}_2\in V$ with
$\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{z}_1 = -\nabla\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{w}}$ and
$\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{z}_2 = c_0$ for a constant $c_0>0$, by
\eqref{eq:divergence-surjectivity}. Then
$\boldsymbol{w} := \tilde{\boldsymbol{w}}+\boldsymbol{z}_1+\boldsymbol{z}_2\in V_{\boldsymbol{g}}$ has
$\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{w} = c_0$. If $\Gamma^{\mathrm{N}}\cup\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}=\emptyset$,
the range of the divergence on $V$ consists of the functions with vanishing
mean, which never contains $c_0$, so \eqref{eq:divergence-surjectivity} and
Robinson's condition fail; this is the analytical counterpart of
Remark~\ref{rem:outflow-flux}. The problem is then incompressible Stokes
flow, with the pressure in a normalised or quotient space. Without an
essential boundary condition that removes the rigid or conformal Killing
kernel, coercivity and uniqueness of $\boldsymbol{u}$ are lost as well.

\label{thm:stokes-exact-complementarity}
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be any subset of the elements and let $(\boldsymbol{u}^h,p^h)$
be any solution of \eqref{eq:stokes-frozen-momentum}--\eqref{eq:stokes-frozen-multiplier}. Then
\begin{equation}
(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^h)_T = 0 \ \ \text{for }T\in\mathcal{A},
\qquad
p^h_T = 0 \ \ \text{for }T\notin\mathcal{A}
\label{eq:stokes-active-set-structure}
\end{equation}
so that the product $p^h\,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^h$ vanishes identically, on
every element and for every $\mathcal{A}$. The test argument
$\gamma p^h_T-(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^h)_T$ reduces to $\gamma p^h_T$ on
$\mathcal{A}$ and to $-(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^h)_T$ off it. At a fixed point,
membership therefore gives $p^h_T\geq0$ on $\mathcal{A}$ and
$(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^h)_T>0$ off it, because the test fails there. Neither
the frozen solutions nor the fixed point depend on $\gamma$, and the fixed
point satisfies the discrete Kuhn--Tucker
conditions $p^h\geq 0$, $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^h\geq 0$,
$p^h\,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^h = 0$ exactly, elementwise; in particular its
pressure lies in the continuous constraint set $Q_+$.

$\mathcal{A}$, failure of the test, which with $p^h_T = 0$ there reads

\label{thm:stokes-consistency}

satisfies \eqref{eq:stokes-discrete-momentum} up to the nonconformity term
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stokes-consistency-residual}
\begin{aligned}
E_h(\boldsymbol{v}) := {}&\sum_{F}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u},p)\cdot{\boldsymbol n}
-\overline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u},p)\cdot{\boldsymbol n}},\ \llbracket \boldsymbol{v}\rrbracket\right)_F
\\
&+\sum_{F\subset\Gamma^{\mathrm{S}}}\left(
{\boldsymbol n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u},p)\cdot{\boldsymbol n}
-\overline{{\boldsymbol n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u},p)\cdot{\boldsymbol n}},\
\ \boldsymbol{v}\cdot{\boldsymbol n}\right)_F
\\
&+\sum_{F\subset\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}}\left(
({\boldsymbol I}-{\boldsymbol n}\otimes{\boldsymbol n})\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u},p){\boldsymbol n}
-\overline{({\boldsymbol I}-{\boldsymbol n}\otimes{\boldsymbol n})\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u},p){\boldsymbol n}},\
({\boldsymbol I}-{\boldsymbol n}\otimes{\boldsymbol n})\boldsymbol{v}\right)_F
\\
&+2\mu\gamma_1\sum_{F\subset\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}}
\left(h_F^{-1}\left(\boldsymbol{g}-\overline{\boldsymbol{g}}\right),\llbracket \boldsymbol{v}\rrbracket\right)_F
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
for $\boldsymbol{v}\in\vec{V}^h$, the first sum running over the interior faces
and those on $\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}$, the bar denoting the mean over the face.
The second and third sums are the traction fluctuations on $\Gamma^{\mathrm{S}}$
and $\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}$, and the last term is the penalty evaluated at the
data; it vanishes
whenever $\boldsymbol{g}$ is constant on each face of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}$, as it is
in every pit computation of Section~\ref{sec:numerical-results}. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stokes-boundary-data-oscillation}
G_h(\boldsymbol{g}) := \left(\sum_{F\subset\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}}
h_F^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{g}-\overline{\boldsymbol{g}}\|_F^2\right)^{1/2}
\end{equation}
Then, in the norm of \eqref{eq:cr-coercivity},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stokes-consistency-bound}
|E_h(\boldsymbol{v})| \leq
\left[Ch\left(|\boldsymbol{u}|_{H^2(\Omega)}+|p|_{H^1(\Omega)}\right)
+2\mu\gamma_1G_h(\boldsymbol{g})\right]\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_h
\end{equation}

holds when the Dirichlet data are constant on each boundary face.

$\|\boldsymbol{u}-I_h\boldsymbol{u}\|_h\leq Ch|\boldsymbol{u}|_{H^2(\Omega)}$. Subtracting
\eqref{eq:stokes-discrete-momentum}, in which the positive part equals
$p^h$ by \eqref{eq:stokes-discrete-multiplier}, from the identity of
Theorem~\ref{thm:stokes-consistency} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stokes-error-equation}
a_h(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^h,\boldsymbol{v})-(p,\nabla_h\cdot\boldsymbol{v})_\Omega
+(p^h,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})_\Omega = E_h(\boldsymbol{v})
\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\vec{V}^h
\end{equation}
Put $\boldsymbol{e}^h := I_h\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^h$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:stokes-exact-complementarity} and
\eqref{eq:cr-interpolant-divergence},
$(p^h,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{e}^h)_\Omega=(p^h,\Pi_0\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u})_\Omega\geq 0$.
Since $p\geq 0$, $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^h\geq 0$ and
$(p,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u})_\Omega=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stokes-error-sign}
(p,\nabla_h\cdot\boldsymbol{e}^h)_\Omega\leq(p,\Pi_0\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u})_\Omega
=(\Pi_0p-p,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}-\Pi_0\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u})_\Omega
\leq Ch^2|p|_{H^1(\Omega)}|\boldsymbol{u}|_{H^2(\Omega)}
\end{equation}
Taking $\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{e}^h$ in \eqref{eq:stokes-error-equation} and writing
$\boldsymbol{e}^h=(I_h\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u})+(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^h)$, the coercivity
\eqref{eq:cr-coercivity}, the boundedness of $a_h(\cdot,\cdot)$ in
$\|\cdot\|_h$ and \eqref{eq:stokes-consistency-bound} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stokes-error-energy}
c(\gamma_1)\|\boldsymbol{e}^h\|_h^2\leq
\left[Ch\left(|\boldsymbol{u}|_{H^2(\Omega)}+|p|_{H^1(\Omega)}\right)
+2\mu\gamma_1G_h(\boldsymbol{g})\right]\|\boldsymbol{e}^h\|_h
+Ch^2|p|_{H^1(\Omega)}|\boldsymbol{u}|_{H^2(\Omega)}
\end{equation}
and the velocity bound follows by Young's inequality and the triangle
inequality. For the pressure, \eqref{eq:stokes-error-equation} reads
$(\Pi_0p-p^h,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})_\Omega
= a_h(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^h,\boldsymbol{v})-E_h(\boldsymbol{v})$ for $\boldsymbol{v}\in\vec{V}^h$. The
Crouzeix--Raviart pair satisfies a discrete inf--sup condition uniformly in
$h$ \cite{BrFo91}, with $I_h$ as Fortin operator and
Lemma~\ref{lem:continuous-divergence-range} as its continuous counterpart,
so $\|\Pi_0p-p^h\|_\Omega$ is bounded by the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:stokes-error-estimate}. Finally
$\|p-\Pi_0p\|_\Omega\leq Ch|p|_{H^1(\Omega)}$.

\label{rem:deviatoric-stability}
For fields vanishing on the whole boundary,
$2\mu(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}))_\Omega
=\mu(\nabla\boldsymbol{u},\nabla\boldsymbol{v})_\Omega+\mu(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u},\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})_\Omega$,
so the usual viscous forms agree on divergence-free fields and differ only
on the cavitated set, where the choice is one of bulk viscosity. With
$\vert\Gamma^{\mathrm{N}}\cup\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}\vert>0$ the choice also changes the
natural boundary condition, as for `do nothing' outflow conditions. The full-gradient form needs only the Poincar\'e inequality for coercivity, whereas
\eqref{eq:deviatoric-cauchy-stress} needs Korn's inequality, through
\eqref{eq:stokes-deviatoric-coercivity} for $n=2$ and in the trace-free form
of Dain \cite{Da06} for $n=3$. With $\tfrac12$ in place of $\tfrac13$ in
plane flow the bound \eqref{eq:stokes-deviatoric-coercivity} would
degenerate, so the physically correct factor is also the one for which
coercivity is elementary.

\label{lem:smoothed-complementarity}
Let $\gamma,s>0$ and $a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}$. Then
\begin{equation}
a = \frac{1}{\gamma}\varphi_s\left(\gamma a-b\right)
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
a>0,\quad b>0,\quad ab = \frac{s}{\gamma}
\label{eq:smoothed-complementarity-equivalence}
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-4 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
By construction $\varphi_s(w)$ is the unique positive root of
$z^2-wz-s = 0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothing-identities}
\varphi_s(w)-w = \varphi_s(-w),\qquad
\varphi_s(w)\,\varphi_s(-w) = s
\end{equation}
the first by inspection and the second because the product telescopes to
$(w^2/4+s)-w^2/4$. Suppose $a = \gamma^{-1}\varphi_s(w)$ with
$w=\gamma a-b$. Then $b = \gamma a-w = \varphi_s(w)-w = \varphi_s(-w)$, so
$a$ and $b$ are positive, and $\gamma ab = \varphi_s(w)\varphi_s(-w) = s$.
Conversely, let $a,b>0$ with $ab = s/\gamma$ and put $w = \gamma a-b$. Then
$(\gamma a)^2-w(\gamma a)-s = \gamma ab-s = 0$ and $\gamma a>0$, so
$\gamma a = \varphi_s(w)$.

\label{lem:conformal-field-plane}
A conformal Killing field vanishing on an open subset of a plane vanishes
identically.

<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
The restriction of \eqref{eq:conformal-killing-fields} to a plane is polynomial in two
variables, so vanishing on an open subset of the plane is vanishing on
all of it. Since $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\rm dev}$ transforms tensorially under rigid
motions, the class \eqref{eq:conformal-killing-fields} is invariant under them, and we may
take the plane to be $x_3=0$. The quadratic part of the third
component of \eqref{eq:conformal-killing-fields} there is $-(x_1^2+x_2^2)\zeta_3$, which forces
$\zeta_3=0$; the quadratic part of the first component is then
$\zeta_1x_1^2+2\zeta_2x_1x_2-\zeta_1x_2^2$, which forces $\zeta_1=\zeta_2=0$, and the field
is linear, $\boldsymbol{w}(x) = \boldsymbol{a}+\mathsf{R}x$ with

tangent directions in the kernel of $\mathsf{R}$, while the eigenvalues
of $\mathsf{R}$ are $\vartheta$ and $\vartheta\pm i\vert\boldsymbol{b}\vert$; a two-dimensional
kernel requires zero to be an eigenvalue of algebraic multiplicity at
least two, which forces $\vartheta=0$ and $\boldsymbol{b}=\boldsymbol{0}$, whence
$\mathsf{R}=0$, and evaluating at any point of the plane,
$\boldsymbol{a}=\boldsymbol{0}$.

<!-- proof-16: body_md -->

$\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{H^1(\Omega)}\leq
C(\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\rm dev}(\boldsymbol{u})\|_\Omega+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_\Omega)$, proved for
bounded Lipschitz domains in \cite{Da06}, together with
the finite dimensionality of the kernel \eqref{eq:conformal-killing-fields} and the

$\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_k\|_\Omega\to 1$ and, by the trace inequality,
$\|\boldsymbol{w}_k\|_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}}\leq
\|\boldsymbol{u}_k\|_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}}+C\|\boldsymbol{r}_k\|_{H^1(\Omega)}\to 0$. On the
finite-dimensional space $CK$ the expression
$(\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}\|_\Omega^2+\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}}^2)^{1/2}$ is a
norm, since its kernel consists of constant fields vanishing on
$\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}$, so the sequence $\boldsymbol{w}_k$ is bounded in $CK$ and a
subsequence converges to some $\boldsymbol{w}$ with

$\boldsymbol{w} = \boldsymbol{0}$, a contradiction.

\label{lem:oswald-averaging}
Let $\boldsymbol{w}$ be piecewise linear on the mesh and let $I_h\boldsymbol{w}$ be the
continuous piecewise linear field whose value at each mesh vertex is the
average of the values of $\boldsymbol{w}$ on the elements meeting that vertex.
If the elements meeting any one vertex form a face connected set, as they
do for the meshes used here, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oswald-estimate}
\sum_{T}\left(h_T^{-2}\|\boldsymbol{w}-I_h\boldsymbol{w}\|_T^2
+\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{w}-I_h\boldsymbol{w})\|_T^2\right)
\lesssim \sum_{F\ {\rm interior}}h_F^{-1}\|\llbracket \boldsymbol{w}\rrbracket\|_F^2
\end{equation}
with a constant depending only on the shape regularity; cf.\
\cite{KaPa03} for the general piecewise polynomial case.
<!-- end lem-7 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
On $T$ the error $\boldsymbol{w}-I_h\boldsymbol{w}$ is linear, so equivalence of norms on
shape-regular elements gives
$\|\boldsymbol{w}-I_h\boldsymbol{w}\|_T^2\lesssim
h_T^3\sum_{p}\vert(\boldsymbol{w}\vert_T-I_h\boldsymbol{w})(p)\vert^2$, the sum running
over the four vertices of $T$. Fix a vertex $p$ and write $\omega_p$ for
the set of elements meeting it, of cardinality bounded by the shape
regularity. By the definition of the average,
$(\boldsymbol{w}\vert_T-I_h\boldsymbol{w})(p)$ is a mean of the differences
$(\boldsymbol{w}\vert_T-\boldsymbol{w}\vert_{T'})(p)$ over $T'\in\omega_p$, and each
difference telescopes along a chain of face neighbours within $\omega_p$,
each step being the value at $p$ of the jump across an interior face
containing $p$. For a linear function on a face, equivalence of norms
again gives
$\vert\llbracket \boldsymbol{w}\rrbracket(p)\vert^2\lesssim h_F^{-2}\|\llbracket \boldsymbol{w}\rrbracket\|_F^2$.
Combining, $h_T^{-2}\|\boldsymbol{w}-I_h\boldsymbol{w}\|_T^2\lesssim
h_T\sum h_F^{-2}\|\llbracket \boldsymbol{w}\rrbracket\|_F^2\approx
\sum h_F^{-1}\|\llbracket \boldsymbol{w}\rrbracket\|_F^2$, the sums running over the interior
faces of the patches of the vertices of $T$; summing over $T$, each face
appears a bounded number of times, which is the first part of
\eqref{eq:oswald-estimate}. The gradient part follows from the inverse inequality
$\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{w}-I_h\boldsymbol{w})\|_T\lesssim h_T^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{w}-I_h\boldsymbol{w}\|_T$
for linear functions.

\label{thm:broken-trace-free-korn}
Under the domain, mesh-connectivity, and boundary-kernel assumptions stated
above, let $\boldsymbol{v}$ be piecewise linear on the mesh; in particular
$\boldsymbol{v}$ may be any member of $\vec{V}^h$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:broken-trace-free-korn}
\|\nabla_h\boldsymbol{v}\|_\Omega^2\leq C\,\Xi(\boldsymbol{v})^2
\end{equation}
with $C$ depending only on the shape regularity of the mesh, on $\Omega$
and on $\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}$.
<!-- end thm-11 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
\emph{Step 1: elementwise similarity split.} On each $T$ the gradient
$\mathsf{G}_T := \nabla\boldsymbol{v}\vert_T$ is a constant matrix. Put
$\mathsf{S}_T := {\rm skew}\,\mathsf{G}_T
+\tfrac13({\rm tr}\,\mathsf{G}_T)\mathsf{I}$ and define the piecewise
linear field $\boldsymbol{w}$ by
$\boldsymbol{w}\vert_T(x) := \bar{\boldsymbol{v}}_T+\mathsf{S}_T(x-x_T)$, with $x_T$ the
centroid of $T$ and $\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}_T$ the mean of $\boldsymbol{v}$ over $T$. Each
$\boldsymbol{w}\vert_T$ is a similarity field, so
$\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\rm dev}(\boldsymbol{w}) = \boldsymbol{0}$ elementwise, and
\begin{equation}
\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w})\vert_T
= \mathsf{G}_T-\mathsf{S}_T
= {\rm sym}\,\mathsf{G}_T-\tfrac13({\rm tr}\,\mathsf{G}_T)\mathsf{I}
= \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\rm dev}(\boldsymbol{v})\vert_T
\label{eq:similarity-split}
\end{equation}
an exact identity, special to piecewise linears. Since
$\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w}$ has zero mean on $T$, the Poincar\'e inequality gives
$\|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w}\|_T\lesssim h_T\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\rm dev}(\boldsymbol{v})\|_T$, and the
discrete trace inequality on shape-regular elements then
$\|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w}\|_F^2\lesssim h_F^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w}\|_T^2
\lesssim h_F\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\rm dev}(\boldsymbol{v})\|_T^2$ for every face
$F\subset\partial T$. Consequently, on every face,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:similarity-jump-bound}
h_F^{-1}\|\llbracket \boldsymbol{w}\rrbracket\|_F^2
\lesssim h_F^{-1}\|\llbracket \boldsymbol{v}\rrbracket\|_F^2
+\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\rm dev}(\boldsymbol{v})\|_{T^+\cup T^-}^2
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 2: averaging.} Let $I_h\boldsymbol{w}$ be the vertex averaged field of
Lemma~\ref{lem:oswald-averaging}. The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:oswald-estimate} is
$\lesssim\Xi(\boldsymbol{v})^2$ by \eqref{eq:similarity-jump-bound}.

\emph{Step 3: the continuous inequality.} Lemma~\ref{lem:continuous-trace-free-korn} applied
to $I_h\boldsymbol{w}\in[H^1(\Omega)]^3$ gives
$\|\nabla I_h\boldsymbol{w}\|_\Omega\lesssim
\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\rm dev}(I_h\boldsymbol{w})\|_\Omega
+\|I_h\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}}$. For the first term,

$\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\rm dev}(I_h\boldsymbol{w})\|_\Omega

\leq\|\nabla_h(I_h\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{w})\|_\Omega\lesssim\Xi(\boldsymbol{v})$, by
\eqref{eq:oswald-estimate}. For the second, on each face $F\subset\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}$,
\begin{equation}

\|I_h\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{w}\|_F+\|\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_F+\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_F
\label{eq:averaged-boundary-control}
\end{equation}
each square is $h_F$ times a quantity that sums to
$\lesssim\Xi(\boldsymbol{v})^2$: the first through the trace inequality and
\eqref{eq:oswald-estimate}, the second by Step 1, and the third because
$\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_F = \|\llbracket \boldsymbol{v}\rrbracket\|_F$ there; since
$h_F\lesssim{\rm diam}\,\Omega$, the sum over $\Gamma^{\mathrm{D}}$ is
$\lesssim\Xi(\boldsymbol{v})^2$.

\lesssim \Xi(\boldsymbol{v})
\label{eq:broken-korn-assembly}
\end{equation}

The restriction to piecewise linears is what makes
Step 1 an identity; for piecewise $H^1$ fields the elementwise kernel
includes the quadratic members of \eqref{eq:conformal-killing-fields} and
the exact similarity split is no longer available. The corresponding
piecewise $H^1$ and $H^2$ theory, with projected jump controls, is given
in \cite{WiHo24}.
The restriction to $n=3$ is
essential, not technical: in two
dimensions the pointwise kernel of the plane deviatoric
$\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}-\tfrac12(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}){\boldsymbol I}$ is infinite-dimensional, so no
inequality of the type \eqref{eq:broken-trace-free-korn} can hold for it; the two-dimensional
form of this paper keeps the three-dimensional factor $\tfrac13$, is
bounded below elementwise through \eqref{eq:stokes-deviatoric-coercivity}, and is covered by
\cite{Br04} directly, as noted in Section~\ref{sec:stokes-cavitation}. Alternatively,
the four steps above reprove that case for piecewise linears verbatim,
with $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ in place of $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\rm dev}$ and the rigid motions, the
linear kernel of $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$, in place of the similarity fields, a rigid
motion vanishing on a boundary segment of positive length being zero.
