\label{160310}
For a point $(u+\varepsilon,\,\varepsilon)$ to be a tip of a left herringbone in the strip $\Omega_\varepsilon,$ the following conditions must be satisfied\textup:
\begin{enumerate}
\item
\label{140301}
$D_+(u,\varepsilon)=0;$
\item
\label{140302}
$f''_+(u+\varepsilon)-f''_-(u-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f'''_+(u+\varepsilon)\ge0;$
\item
\label{140303}
$f'''_+(u+\varepsilon)\ge0.$
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end th-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
As usual for the study of left herringbones, we consider field~(5.3), one of whose integral curves, when shifted to the right by $\varepsilon$, gives the spine of a herringbone. Since at any nonsingular point of field~(5.3) the integral curve intersects the upper boundary of the strip with slope $-1$, while the spine of any left horizontal herringbone intersecting the upper boundary must have slope in the interval from zero to one, we conclude that the point $(u,\varepsilon)$ must be a stationary point of field~(5.3), that is, condition~\eqref{140301} holds.

The Jacobi matrix at such a point was already examined in Section~5 (see~\cite{First}). 
For the reader's convenience, we reproduce formulas (5.17) and (5.18), adopting the 
notation introduced in formula (8.20) (\cite{Second}):
\begin{equation}\label{150301} 
J(u,\varepsilon)=\varepsilon\big(f_+''(u+\varepsilon)-f_-''(u-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f_+'''(u+\varepsilon)\big)
\begin{pmatrix}
\phantom{i}1&1-3s
\\
-1&s-1
\end{pmatrix},
 \end{equation}
where $s=1+\varepsilon\kappa_+^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm L}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{150302} 
\kappa_+^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm L}=\frac{2f_+'''(u+\varepsilon)}{f_+''(u+\varepsilon)-f_-''(u-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f_+'''(u+\varepsilon)}\,.
 \end{equation}
We recall that, in this context, the point $(u,\varepsilon)$ is assumed to lie on the curve $D_+=0$.

We have already computed the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the matrix in~\eqref{150301} on several occasions; they are the roots of the polynomial $\lambda^2-s\lambda-2s$, namely
\begin{equation}\label{150303} 
\lambda=\frac{s\pm\sqrt{s^2+8s}}2\quad\text{and the corresponding vectors}\quad
\begin{pmatrix}
s-1-\lambda
\\
1
\end{pmatrix}.
 \end{equation}
We are not interested in non-real eigenvalues, since in that case $(u,\varepsilon)$ is a spiral stationary point, and no integral curve approaching it with monotonically increasing slope in the interval $[0,1]$ can exist—--and it is precisely such curves that are of interest to us. Furthermore, we disregard the eigenvalue in which the square root is taken with the plus sign, because then $s-\lambda=\tfrac12(s-\sqrt{s^2+8s})\le0$, and $s-1-\lambda\le-1$. Thus the corresponding eigenvector has negative slope.

Thus, we shall compare the integral curve approaching the point $(u,\varepsilon)$ with slope
\begin{equation}\label{150304} 
\frac1{s-1-\lambda}=\frac2{s-2+\sqrt{s^2+8s}}\,,
 \end{equation}
and the curve $D_+=0$, whose slope at this point equals
\begin{equation}\label{150305} 
\frac1{2s-1}\,.
 \end{equation}
The latter assertion has been verified on numerous occasions, but we shall repeat it once more. It follows simply by differentiating the identity \hbox{$2x_2D_+=0$:}
\begin{equation}\label{150306} 
\big[f''_+-f''_--2x_2f'''_+\big]dx_1-\big[f''_+-f''_-+2x_2f'''_+\big]dx_2=0.
 \end{equation}

As already mentioned, the slope of our integral curve must lie in the interval $[0,1]$, which, in view of formula~\eqref{150304}, can be rewritten as
$$
s-2+\sqrt{s^2+8s}\ge2\,.
$$
This condition is in turn equivalent to the inequality $s\ge1$, or, what is the same, $\kappa_+^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm L}\ge0$.

Let us also note that for $s>1$ the strict inequality
$$
\frac2{s-2+\sqrt{s^2+8s}}>\frac1{2s-1}
$$
holds, meaning that the slope of the integral curve of interest at the point $(u,\varepsilon)$ is strictly greater than the slope of the curve $D_+=0$. This implies that, in a neighbourhood of $(u,\varepsilon)$, our integral curve enters the strip $\Omega_\varepsilon$ to the right of the curve $D_+=0$.

Let us now turn to the conditions~\eqref{140302} and~\eqref{140303}, which we have left aside for the moment. Suppose that condition~\eqref{140302} is not satisfied, i.\,e., the strict inequality
\begin{equation}\label{150307} 
\frac{\partial D_+}{\partial x_1}(u,\varepsilon)<0
 \end{equation}
holds. This implies, in particular, first, that the quantities $\kappa_+^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm L}$ and $s$ are well-defined, and second, that to the right of the curve $D_+=0$ lies the domain where $D_+<0$. Consequently, the integral curve of interest passing through that domain cannot give rise to the spine of a left herringbone. Therefore, condition~\eqref{140302} must necessarily be fulfilled.

If the inequality in~\eqref{140302} is strict, then the quantity $\kappa_+^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm L}$ is well-defined by formula~\eqref{150302}, and the proved inequality $\kappa_+^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm L}\ge0$ is equivalent to condition~\eqref{140303}.

It remains to consider the degenerate case
\begin{equation}\label{230701} 
f_+''(u+\varepsilon)-f_-''(u-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f_+'''(u+\varepsilon)=0\,.
 \end{equation}
Suppose that condition~\eqref{140303} does not hold, that is, $f'''_+(u+\varepsilon)<0$.
In this case, equality~\eqref{230701} implies the relation
\begin{equation}\label{150308} 
f_+''(u+\varepsilon)<f_-''(u-\varepsilon)\,,
 \end{equation}
whence it follows that
\begin{align*}
2\varepsilon D_-(u,\varepsilon)&=f'_+(u+\varepsilon)-f'_-(u-\varepsilon)-
2\varepsilon f''_-(u-\varepsilon)
\\
&<f'_+(u+\varepsilon)-f'_-(u-\varepsilon)-
2\varepsilon f''_+(u+\varepsilon)=2\varepsilon D_+(u,\varepsilon)=0\,.
\end{align*}
Thus, the necessary condition $D_-(u,\varepsilon)\ge0$ fails, and consequently the point $(u+\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ cannot be a tip of a herringbone. This verifies the necessity of condition~\eqref{140303}.

Let $f'_+(u_0)=f'_-(u_0),$ $f''_+(u_0)=f''_-(u_0),$ $f'''_+(u_0)>0$
and $f'''_+(u_0)>f'''_-(u_0)$. Then, for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$\textup, there exists a root $u_+$ of the equation $D_+(u,\varepsilon)=0$ such that one can construct a left herringbone extending from the point $(u_0+\varepsilon,\,0)$ to the point $(u_+\!+\varepsilon,\,\varepsilon).$

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Consider the behavior of the function $D_+$ in a neighborhood of the point $(u_0,0)$. To this end, write $x_1=u_0+(\alpha+1)x_2$ and expand the resulting function in powers of $x_2$, treating $\alpha$ as a fixed parameter:
\begin{equation}\label{230201} 
\begin{aligned}
2x_2 D_+(x_1,x_2)&=f'_+(u_0+(\alpha+2)x_2)-f'_-(u_0+\alpha x_2)
-2x_2 f''_+(u_0+(\alpha+2)x_2)
\\
&=\Big[\tfrac12\alpha^2\big(f'''_+(u_0)-f'''_-(u_0)\big)-2f'''_+(u_0)\Big]x_2^2
+O(x_2^3)\,.
\end{aligned}
 \end{equation}
We see that the isocline $X_1$ ($D_+=0$) has two branches in a neighborhood of $(u_0,0)$. They intersect at this point and have slopes
\begin{equation}\label{230202} 
\frac1{1\pm\alpha_0},\qquad \text{where}\quad 
\alpha_0=2\;\sqrt\frac{f'''_+(u_0)}{f'''_+(u_0)-f'''_-(u_0)}\,,
 \end{equation}
Thus, on the boundary $x_2=\varepsilon$ the function $D_+$ has two roots in a neighborhood of $u_0$. Choose the larger one:
\begin{equation}\label{230203} 
u_+=u_0+(\alpha_0+1)\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)\,,
 \end{equation}
and verify the conditions of Theorem~\ref{170301} for the point $u=u_+$. Condition~\eqref{170302} holds by the definition of $u_+$. Condition~\eqref{170304} holds for small $\varepsilon$ since $f'''_+(u_0)>0$. Finally, condition~\eqref{170303} is the condition $\frac{\partial}{\partial x_1}D_+(u_+,\varepsilon)>0$, which holds because the function $D_+(x_1,\varepsilon)$ is negative to the left of the simple root $u_+$ and positive to the right of it.

It remains to verify that the resulting herringbone can be continued up to the point $(u_0+\varepsilon,0)$. In other words, we need to trace that the integral curve $\ell_\varepsilon$ defining the spine of the herringbone passes through the domain where $D_+>0$ and $D_->0$ and terminates at the point $(u_0,0)$. As before, we consider the subdomain $\omega$ in the upper half-strip bounded by the line $x_1=u_+$ and that part of the isocline $D_+=0$ which connects the points $(u_0,0)$ and $(u_+,\varepsilon)$.

We shall essentially repeat the arguments used in the proof of Proposition~6.1 (see~\cite{First}). First, note that for small $\varepsilon$ the slope of the chosen branch of the isocline is close to $1/(1+\alpha_0)$, while the integral curves have slope equal to $1$ at points of the isocline. Therefore, the curve $\ell_\varepsilon$ in question, starting downward from the point $(u_+,\varepsilon)$ to the right of the isocline, cannot intersect it at any point of the upper half-strip. Nor can the curve $\ell_\varepsilon$ leave the domain $\omega$ through the line $x_2=0$ except through the stationary point $(u_0,0)$. Second, in $\omega$ we have $D_+>0$ and $D_->0$. The first inequality holds because, by construction, $\omega$ lies to the right of the chosen segment of the isocline $D_+=0$, on which the function $D_+$ changes sign from minus to plus when we intersect this isocline from left to right. We now verify the second inequality by examining the values of $D_-$ on the rays $x_1=u_0+(\alpha+1)x_2$:
\begin{equation}\label{260201} 
\begin{aligned}
2x_2 D_-(x_1,x_2)&=f'_+(u_0+(\alpha+2)x_2)-f'_-(u_0+\alpha x_2)
-2x_2 f''_-(u_0+\alpha x_2)
\\
&=\Big[(\tfrac12\alpha^2+2\alpha)\big(f'''_+(u_0)-f'''_-(u_0)\big)
+2f'''_+(u_0)\Big]x_2^2+O(x_2^3)\,.
\end{aligned}
 \end{equation}
Since $\alpha>0$ for points in $\omega$, this expression is strictly positive in $\omega$. Thus we have verified that the curve $\ell_\varepsilon$ starting from $(u_+,\varepsilon)$ moves leftward and downward inside the domain where $D_+>0$ and $D_->0$, eventually reaching the point $(u_0,0)$. Consequently, one can construct a left herringbone extending from the point $(u_0+\varepsilon,0)$ to the point $(u_++\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$, as required.

Since $f'''_+(u_0)>0$ and $f'''_-(u_0)<0$, the conditions of Lemmas~\ref{220201} and~\ref{260204} are satisfied simultaneously. Thus we can immediately construct two herringbones: a left one from the point $(u_0+\varepsilon,\,0)$ and a right one from the point $(u_0-\varepsilon,\,0)$. The proof is completed by applying Proposition~11.1 (see~\cite{Second}), whose conditions are satisfied with $C=(u_0,0)$. Namely, the two herringbones constructed can be joined by a rectangle, which in this case is a square.

As in the previous proof, using Lemma~\ref{220201}, we construct a left herringbone from the point $(u_0+\varepsilon,\,0)$ to the point $(u_+\!+\varepsilon,\,\varepsilon)$. Now we need to continue it to the left up to the second intersection with the upper boundary. In other words, we need to continue the curve $\ell_\varepsilon$ to the left from the point $(u_0,0)$ so that it runs in the upper half-strip up to the upper boundary, remaining in the domain where $D_+>0$ and $D_->0$. To this end, we need to study in more detail the behavior of the isoclines of the integral curves in a neighborhood of $(u_0,0)$.

Substituting the expansion
\begin{equation}\label{270201} 
x_2=k(x_1-u_0)+O(x_2^2)
 \end{equation}
into the equations of our field \textup{\bf(}e.\,g., into formula~(5.3)\textup{\bf)}, we obtain the following equation for the slope coefficient $k$:
\begin{equation}\label{280201} 
k=\frac{2k}{1-k^2}\cdot
\frac{(1+k)f'''_+(u_0)-(1-k)f'''_-(u_0)}{f'''_+(u_0)-f'''_-(u_0)}\,.
 \end{equation}
All integral curves except two correspond to the value $k=0$, i.\,e., they are tangent to the axis $x_1$. The two distinguished integral curves, which are separatrices, have slopes
\begin{equation}\label{280202} 
k_\pm=-\frac{\sqrt{f'''_+(u_0)}\pm\sqrt{f'''_-(u_0)}}
{\sqrt{f'''_+(u_0)}\mp\sqrt{f'''_-(u_0)}}\,.
 \end{equation}
The sector between the separatrices is foliated by integral curves that do not pass through $(u_0,0)$. The behavior of the extremal curves in a neighborhood of this point is shown in Figure~\ref{280203}.
\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[scale = 0.3]{2order_fixpoint.pdf}
    \caption{Integral curves of the field in a neighborhood of the stationary 
    point $(u_0,0)$.}
    \label{280203}
\end{figure}

Thus, our integral curve $\ell_\varepsilon$ is tangent to the axis $x_1$ from the right at the point $(u_0,0)$, and we can continue it by any integral curve tangent to the axis $x_1$ at $(u_0,0)$ from the left in order to obtain a $C^1$-smooth curve. This curve suplies us with the spine of a herringbone, provided that it passes through the strip in the domain where $D_+>0$ and $D_->0$. We need not worry about the inequality $D_->0$ at all, since under the conditions stated in Proposition~\ref{130804} it holds in some neighborhood of $(u_0,0)$ in the upper half-plane. To verify this, it suffices to rewrite equality~\eqref{260201} in the form
\begin{equation}\label{010301} 
2x_2 D_-(x_1,x_2)=\Big[\tfrac12(\alpha+2)^2\big(f'''_+(u_0)-f'''_-(u_0)\big)
+2f'''_-(u_0)\Big]x_2^2+O(x_2^3)\,.
 \end{equation}
The fact that for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ we can always find an integral curve tangent to the axis $x_1$ at $(u_0,0)$ from the left on which the condition $D_+>0$ holds follows simply by choosing the initial point $v_+^l$ of this curve on the boundary $x_2=\varepsilon$ to the left of the left root of the equation $D_+(u,\varepsilon)=0$.
