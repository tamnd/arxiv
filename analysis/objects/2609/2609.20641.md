\label{ex:pde}
    A classical example is the boundary value problem
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:ellPDE}
    \begin{aligned}
        -\operatorname*{div}(A\nabla u) &= f \; &&\mathrm{in}\;\Omega,\\
        u &= g_{\mathrm{bc}} &&\mathrm{on}\;\partial\Omega,
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    where $A\in L^\infty(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$ is symmetric and, for constants $0<\alpha\le\beta$, satisfies
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:propA}
        \alpha|\eta|^2
        \le
        (A(x)\eta)\cdot\eta
        \le
        \beta|\eta|^2
    \end{equation}
    for almost all $x\in\Omega$ and every $\eta\in\mathbb{R}^d$. Here,
    $|\bullet|$ denotes the Euclidean norm in $\mathbb{R}^d$.
    In the strong formulation~\eqref{eq:PDEstrong}, one takes
    $D=-\operatorname*{div}(A\nabla\bullet)$ and $B=\gamma$ on a sufficiently regular
    space $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{str}}\subset H^1(\Omega)$ such that
    $D[u]\in L^2(\Omega)$ and the trace $\gamma u$ is well defined.
    For homogeneous Dirichlet data, the corresponding weak formulation is
    naturally posed on $H_0^1(\Omega)$, where the elliptic operator is instead
    understood as a map into $H^{-1}(\Omega) =
    (H_0^1(\Omega))'$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:architecture}
Based on the \emph{hyperparameters}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $L\in\mathbb{N}$, called the \emph{depth} of the network,
  \item $(l_i)_{0\le i\le L}\in\mathbb{N}^{L+1}$, called
  the \emph{widths} of the layers,
  \item $(\chi_i)_{1\le i\le L}\in\prod_{i=1}^{L}W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{l_i};\mathbb{R}^{l_i})$, called the \emph{activation functions},
  \item $p\in\mathbb{N}$ with $1\le p\le \sum_{i=1}^L l_i (l_{i-1}+1)$, called the \emph{number of parameters},
  \item $\psi\in C^1\left(\mathbb{R}^p;\prod_{i=1}^{L}\mathbb{R}^{l_i\times l_{i-1}}\times \prod_{i=1}^{L}\mathbb{R}^{l_i}\right)$,
  called the \emph{parameterization map},
\end{itemize}
a \emph{multi-layer perceptron (MLP) architecture} is a mapping $v \colon \mathbb{R}^p \to C^0(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{l_L})$ for some subdomain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^{l_0}$, defined by
\begin{equation}
    \theta \mapsto\circ_{k=1}^L \chi_k\big(\psi_k(\theta)\bullet+\psi_{L+k}(\theta)\big),
  \label{eq:architecture-map}
\end{equation}
where $\psi_k(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_k \times l_{k-1}}$ and $\psi_{L+k}(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{l_k}$ denote
the \emph{weight matrix} and \emph{bias vector} of the $k$th layer.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Note that we restrict our presentation to MLPs. Many other neural network architectures have been proposed, such as convolutional neural networks~\cite{lecunGradientbasedLearningApplied1998,goodfellowDeepLearning2016}, recurrent neural networks, variants thereof~\cite{elmanFindingStructureTime1990,hochreiterLongShorttermMemory1997,choPropertiesNeuralMachine2014}, and transformers~\cite{vaswaniAttentionAllYou2017}. From the perspective adopted in this work, these architectures can often be viewed as particular parameterizations obtained by imposing structural constraints on a general neural network model. Since our analysis only relies on the resulting parametric map~\eqref{eq:architecture-map}, focusing on MLPs entails no significant loss of generality.
