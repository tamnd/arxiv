<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{K}$ be a 3-dimensional simplicial sphere. There is a ring isomorphism $H^*(\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}) \cong H^*(M_1\#\cdots\# M_k)$ where each $M_i$ is a product of spheres, if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied: 
\begin{itemize} 
\item[(a)] $\mathcal{K}=S^0*S^0*S^0*S^0$ (the boundary of a $4$-dimensional cross-polytope); 
\item[(b)] $\mathcal{K}^1$ is a chordal graph; 
\item[(c)] $\mathcal{K}^1$ has exactly two missing edges and they are not adjacent to each other (i.\,e. form a chordless $4$-cycle). 
\end{itemize}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{K}$ be a 3-dimensional starshaped simplicial sphere with only two missing edges which are not adjacent to each other. Then there is a diffeomorphism

\] 
where $M_1\cong S^3\times S^3\times S^{m-2}$, and each $M_i$ with $i\geqslant 2$ is a product of two spheres.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{retract} 
Let $\mathcal{K}_J$ be the full subcomplex of a simplicial complex $\mathcal{K}$ on $[m]$. Then $\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_J}$ is a retract of $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}$ and $H^*(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_J})$ is a subring of $H^*(\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K})$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- solution-1: body_md -->
Consider the canonical inclusion $i\colon \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K} \hookrightarrow (D^2)^m$, and let $q\colon (D^2)^m \rightarrow (D^2)^{|J|}$ be the map that omits the coordinates corresponding to $[m] \setminus J$. Then $r = q \circ i\colon \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K} \rightarrow \mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_J}$ is the required retraction, and it induces an injective homomorphism $H^*(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_J})\to H^*(\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K})$ in cohomology.
<!-- end solution-1 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{Hochster} 
There are isomorphisms of groups 
\[ 
H^l(\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}) \cong \underset{J \subset [m]}{\bigoplus} \widetilde{H}^{l - |J| - 1}(\mathcal{K}_J)  
\] 
These isomorphisms combine to form a ring isomorphism  
\[ 
H^*(\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}) \cong \underset{J \subset [m]}{\bigoplus} \widetilde{H}^*(\mathcal{K}_J), 
\] 
where the ring structure on the right hand side is given by the canonical maps 
\[ 
H^{k - |I| - 1}(\mathcal{K}_I)  \otimes H^{l - |J| - 1}(\mathcal{K}_J) \longrightarrow H^{k + l - |I| - |J| - 1}(\mathcal{K}_{I \cup J}) \,, 
\] 
which are induced by the simplicial maps $\mathcal{K}_{I \cup J} \rightarrow \mathcal{K}_I * \mathcal{K}_J$ for $I \cap J = \varnothing$ and zero otherwise.

<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{manifold} 
Let $\mathcal{K}$ be a simplicial sphere of dimension $n - 1$ with $m$ vertices. Then $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}$ is a closed topological manifold of dimension $m+n$. 
If $P$ is a simple $n$-dimensional polytope with $m$ facets, then $\mathcal{Z}_P$ is a smooth manifold of dimension $m+n$.

<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{T4.6.12} 
Let $P$ be a dual stacked $n$-dimensional polytope with $m > n + 1$ facets. Then the corresponding moment-angle manifold is diffeomorphic to a connected sum of products of pairs of spheres, namely, 
\[ 
  \mathcal{Z}_P \cong \underset{k=3}{\overset{m-n+1}{\#}} (S^k \times S^{m+n-k})^{\#(k-2)\binom{m-n}{k-1}}. 
\]

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
Let $P=P(\mathrm A,b)$ be a polyhedron given by a generic system. Then 
\begin{itemize} 
\item[(1)] 
  $P$ is simple and has full dimension $n=\dim W$; 
\item[(2)] 
  if the inequality $\langle a_i,w\rangle+b_i\geqslant0$ is redundant in the system $(\mathrm A,b)$, then $\langle a_i,w\rangle+b_i>0$ for all $w\in P$ (i.\,e. the hyperplane corresponding to the redundant inequality does not intersect~$P$). 
\end{itemize}
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
To prove (1), take any vertex $w\in P$. It belongs to exactly $n$ facets (to at most $n$ since the system is generic, and to at least $n$ since $\dim W=n$). Without loss of generality assume that $\langle a_i,w\rangle+b_i=0$ for $i=1,\ldots,n$ and $\langle a_i,w\rangle+b_i>0$ for $i>n$, so $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ is a basis of $W^*$. Let $w_1,\ldots,w_n$ be the dual basis of~$W$. Define $w'=w+\varepsilon_1w_1+\cdots+\varepsilon_n w_n$ where $\varepsilon_i>0$. Then we have 
\[ 
  \langle a_i,w'\rangle+b_i=\langle a_i,w\rangle+b_i+ 
  \varepsilon_1\langle a_i,w_1\rangle+\cdots+\varepsilon_n\langle a_i,w_n\rangle, 
\] 
which is positive for every $i=1,\ldots,m$ if $\varepsilon_i$ are sufficiently small. Hence $w'$ belongs to the interior of the polyhedron $P(\mathrm A,b)$ and therefore it has full dimension. 
 
\smallskip 
 
To prove (2), suppose that the hyperplane $\langle a_i,w\rangle+b_i=0$ corresponding to a redundant inequality intersects $P$. Then it intersects $P$ along some face. This face contains a vertex, which is also a vertex of the polyhedron~$P$ and is contained in its $n$ facets by claim~(1). Together with the redundant hyperplane $\langle a_i,w\rangle+b_i=0$, we obtain $n+1$ hyperplanes containing this vertex. A contradiction.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- constr-1: body_md -->
\label{galed} 
For a configuration $\mathrm A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}$ spanning the linear space   $W^*\cong\mathbb{R}^n$, consider the linear map $A\colon\mathbb{R}^m\to W^*$ given by $e_i\mapsto a_i$, where $e_i$ is the $i$-th vector of the standard basis in~$\mathbb{R}^m$. The map $A$ is surjective by assumption, we complete it to the exact sequence 
\[ 
  0\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^m\stackrel{A}\longrightarrow W^*  \longrightarrow 0, 
\] 
and consider the dual exact sequence 
\[ 
  0\longrightarrow W\stackrel{A^*}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^m 
  \stackrel\varGamma\longrightarrow 
  V^*\longrightarrow 0, 
\] 
where $A^*(w)=(\langle a_1,w\rangle,\ldots,\langle a_m,w\rangle)$. 
 
Define $\gamma_i=\varGamma(e_i)$, then $\Gamma=\{\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m\}$ is a spanning configuration of $m$ vectors in the space~$V^*\cong\mathbb{R}^{m-n}$. The configuration $\Gamma$ is called the \emph{Gale dual} to $\mathrm A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}$. If we choose bases in $W^*$ and $V^*$, then the maps $A$ and $\varGamma$ are given by matrices of sizes $n\times m$ and $(m-n)\times m$ respectively, and the relation $\varGamma A^*=0$ expresses the fact that the rows of the matrix $\varGamma$ form a basis in the space of linear relations among the vectors $a_1,\ldots,a_m$. The following standard property of Gale dual configurations holds.
<!-- end constr-1 -->
<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\label{dspan} 
For any $I\subset[m]$ the subconfiguration $\mathrm A_I = \{a_i\colon i\in I\}$ is linearly independent in $W^*$ if and only if the subconfiguration $\Gamma_{\widehat I}=\{\gamma_j\colon j\notin I\}$ spans~$V^*$.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- constr-2: body_md -->
For a polyhedron~\eqref{ptope} given by a system of linear inequalities, consider the injective affine map 
\[ 
  i_{\mathrm A,b}\colon W\to\mathbb{R}^m,\quad 
  w\mapsto A^*w+b=(\langle a_1,w\rangle+b_1,\ldots,\langle a_m,w\rangle+b_m). 
\] 
The image $i_{\mathrm A,b}(P)$ is the intersection of the nonnegative orthant 
\[ 
  \mathbb{R}^m_\geqslant=\{y=(y_1,\ldots,y_m)\in\mathbb{R}^m\colon y_i\geqslant0,\;i=1,\ldots,m\} 
\] 
with the $n$-dimensional plane 
\begin{equation}\label{iAb} 
\begin{aligned} 
  i_{\mathrm A,b}(W) &= \{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon y=A^{*}w+b\quad\text{for some }w\in W\} \\ 
  &= \{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon \varGamma y=\varGamma b\}. 
\end{aligned} 
\end{equation} 
                                                                                                                                                    
 
Consider the standard moment map 
\[ 
  \mu\colon\mathbb{C}^m\to\mathbb{R}^m,\quad(z_1,\ldots,z_m)\mapsto 
  (|z_1|^2,\ldots,|z_m|^2) 
\] 
and define the space $\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm A,b}$ from the pullback square 
\[ 
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=small] 
  \mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm A,b} \ar{r}{i_{\mathcal{Z}}} \ar{d} & \mathbb{C}^m \ar{d}{\mu}\\ 
  P \ar{r}{i_{\mathrm A,b}} & \mathbb{R}_{\geqslant}^m 
\end{tikzcd} 
\] 
Replacing $y_k$ by $|z_k|^2$ in the equations defining the affine plane~\eqref{iAb} we obtain that $\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm A,b}$ embeds into $\mathbb{C}^m$ as the set of common zeros of $m-n$ quadratic equations (\textit{hermitian quadrics}): 
\begin{equation}\label{intqu} 
  i_\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm A,b}) =\mu^{-1}(i_{\mathrm A,b}(P))= \bigl\{ z \in \mathbb{C}^m \colon 
  \gamma_1|z_1|^2+\cdots+\gamma_m|z_m|^2=\delta\bigr\}, 
\end{equation} 
where $\delta=\varGamma b=b_1\gamma_1+\cdots+b_m\gamma_m\in V^*$. 
The torus $T^m$ acts on $\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm A,b}$ with quotient $P$ and $i_\mathcal{Z}$ is a $T^m$-equivariant embedding.
<!-- end constr-2 -->
<!-- thm-4: body_md -->
\label{zpqua}\ 
\begin{itemize} 
\item[(1)] The intersection of quadrics~\eqref{intqu} is nonempty and nondegenerate if and only if the system $(\mathrm A,b)$ is generic. 
 
\item[(2)] If, in addition to the conditions of~\emph{(1)}, the polyhedron $P=P(\mathrm A,b)$ is bounded (i.\,e., is a simple polytope), then the intersection of quadrics~\eqref{intqu} is $T^m$-equi\-va\-ri\-ant\-ly homeomorphic to the product $\mathcal Z_P\times T^r$, where $r$ is the number of redundant inequalities in the system $(\mathrm A,b)$. 
\end{itemize} 
Thus, the moment-angle manifold $\mathcal{Z}_P$ embeds as a smooth real submanifold into $\mathbb C^m$ with trivial normal bundle.
<!-- end thm-4 -->
<!-- prop-3: body_md -->
\label{ftriv} 
Let $G$ be a face of a simple polytope $P=P(\mathrm A,b)$ given by a generic system of inequalities~\eqref{ptope}. Then $\mathcal{Z}_G$ embeds as a smooth submanifold in $\mathcal{Z}_P$ with trivial normal bundle.
<!-- end prop-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The face $G$ is given by setting some of the inequalities in \eqref{ptope} to equalities. We may assume $0\in G$; this can always be achieved by shifting the polytope $P$. Then for some  
$I=\{i_1,\ldots,i_k\}\subset[m]$ we have 
\[ 
  G=\{w\in P\colon \langle a_i,w\rangle=0 
  \text{ for }i\in I\}. 
\] 
Therefore $G$ is given by the generic system of inequalities 
\[ 
  (\mathrm A_{\widehat I},b_{\widehat I})=\{\langle a_j,w\rangle+b_j\geqslant0,\; j\notin I\} 
\]   
in the space $W_{\widehat I}=\{w\in P\colon \langle a_i,w\rangle=0  \text{ for }i\in I\}$ of dimension $n-|I|$.  
 
Since the vectors $\mathrm A_I$ are linearly independent, the Gale dual configuration to $\mathrm A_{\widehat I}$ in $W_{\widehat I}$ is $\Gamma_{\widehat I}$ in the same space $V^*$. The intersection of quadrics~\eqref{intqu}, corresponding to the polyhedron $G=P(\mathrm A_{\widehat I},b_{\widehat I})$ is 
\begin{equation}\label{Gquad} 
  \bigl\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{\widehat I}\colon \sum_{j\in\widehat I}\gamma_j|z_j|^2=\delta\bigr\}, 
\end{equation} 
where $\mathbb{C}^{\widehat I}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^m\colon z_i=0\text{ for }i\in I\}$ and $\delta=\sum_{j\notin I}b_j\gamma_j$ since $b_i=0$ for $i\in I$. 
By Theorem~\ref{zpqua} the nondegenerate intersection of quadrics~\eqref{Gquad} is homeomorphic to $\mathcal Z_G\times T^{r_G}$, where $r_G$ is the number of redundant inequalities in the system~$(\mathrm A_{\widehat I},b_{\widehat I})$.  
 
On the other hand, the system of quadrics~\eqref{Gquad} is obtained from~\eqref{intqu} by adding the conditions $z_i=0$ for $i\in I$. Therefore, the system 
\[ 
  \{\gamma_1|z_1|^2+\cdots+\gamma_m|z_m|^2=\delta\}\cup 
  \{z_i=0,\; i\in I\} 
\] 
of $m-n+|I|$ hypersurfaces in $\mathbb{C}^m$ defining $\mathcal Z_G\times T^{r_G}$ is nondegenerate. Hence each of the embeddings in the diagram 
\[ 
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=small] 
  \mathcal{Z}_G\times T^{r_G}\cong 
  \mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm A_{\widehat I},b_{\widehat I}}  
  \ar[hook]{r} \ar[hook,shift left=1cm]{d} & 
  \mathbb{C}^{\widehat I} \ar[hook]{d}\\ 
  \mathcal{Z}_P\times T^r \cong  
  \mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm A,b} \ar[hook]{r} & \mathbb{C}^m 
\end{tikzcd} 
\] 
has trivial normal bundle. Since every redundant inequality in the system $(\mathrm A,b)$ is also redundant in the system $(\mathrm A_{\widehat I},b_{\widehat I})$ we also obtain an embedding $\mathcal{Z}_G\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}_G\times T^{r_G-r}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}_P$ with trivial normal bundle.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- prop-4: body_md -->
\label{qtriv} 
Let $L$ be a transverse plane to the polytope $P=P(\mathrm A,b)$ given by a generic system of inequalities~\eqref{ptope} and $Q=L\cap P$ is nonempty. Then $\mathcal{Z}_Q$ embeds as a smooth submanifold in $\mathcal Z_P$ with trivial normal bundle.
<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We proceed by induction on the codimension $k$ of the plane $L$. 
 
Let $k=1$. Then the hyperplane $L$ is given by an equation $\langle a_{m+1},w\rangle+b_{m+1}=0$ with nonzero $a_{m+1}\in W^*$. Let $P'$ be the polytope in $W$ given by the system of inequalities $\langle a_i,w\rangle+b_i\geqslant0$, $i=1,\ldots,m+1$. Since $L$ is transverse, this is a generic system. Hence 
$P'$ is a simple polytope and $Q$ is one of its facets. Shifting the origin to a point of $Q$ we may assume that $b_{m+1}=0$. By Proposition~\ref{ftriv}, $\mathcal{Z}_Q$ embeds into $\mathcal{Z}_{P'}$ with trivial normal bundle. Let us look at the intersection of quadrics in $\mathbb{C}^{m+1}$ realizing this embedding. A basis of the space $V'$ of linear relations on the vectors $a_1,\ldots,a_{m+1}$ is obtained from a basis of the space $V$ of linear relations on the vectors $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ by adding one relation $r_1a_1+\cdots+r_ma_m+a_{m+1}=0$. Therefore the intersection of quadrics~\eqref{intqu} corresponding to the polytope~$P'$ is 
\[ 
  \bigl\{ z \in \mathbb{C}^{m+1} \colon 
  \gamma_1|z_1|^2+\dots+\gamma_m|z_m|^2=\delta,\quad 
  r_1|z_1|^2+\dots+r_m|z_m|^2+|z_{m+1}|^2=\delta_{m+1} \bigr\}, 
\] 
where $\{\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m\}$ is the Gale dual to  
$\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}$, $\delta=b_1\gamma_1+\dots+b_m\gamma_m$ and $\delta_{m+1}=b_1r_1+\dots+b_mr_m$. 
By Proposition~\ref{ftriv}, adding the condition $z_{m+1}=0$ to the above equations yields a nondegenerate system of quadrics defining the manifold $\mathcal{Z}_Q\times T^{r_Q}$ (where $r_Q$ is the number of hyperplanes $\langle a_i,w\rangle+b_i=0$ that do not intersect~$Q$). On the other hand, this same system of quadrics is obtained from the system $\gamma_1|z_1|^2+\dots+\gamma_m|z_m|^2=\delta$ defining $\mathcal{Z}_P$ in $\mathbb{C}^m$ by adding one equation $r_1|z_1|^2+\dots+r_m|z_m|^2=\delta_{m+1}$. Hence $\mathcal{Z}_Q\times T^{r_Q}$ is a codimension-one submanifold of $\mathcal{Z}_P$ with trivial normal bundle and therefore $\mathcal{Z}_Q$ also has trivial normal bundle in~$\mathcal{Z}_P$. 
 
Now let $L$ be a transverse plane of codimension $k$. Take a hyperplane $H$ containing $L$ and transverse to $P$ (i.\,e. not intersecting the vertices of~$P$). Let $Q=L\cap P$ and $S=H\cap P$. By the previous argument, $\mathcal{Z}_S$ embeds into $\mathcal{Z}_P$ with trivial normal bundle. At the same time, $L$ is a transverse plane to $S$ of codimension $(k-1)$. Therefore, by the induction hypothesis, $\mathcal{Z}_Q$ embeds into $\mathcal{Z}_S$ with trivial normal bundle.

<!-- thm-7: body_md -->
\label{h-cob} 
Let $\mathcal Q$ be a compact smooth manifold of dimension $d+1 \geqslant 6$ with boundary $\6\mathcal Q$, satisfying the following: 
\begin{enumerate} 
\item $\mathcal Q$ and $\6\mathcal Q$ are simply connected and $H_i(\mathcal Q) = 0$ for $i \geqslant d - 1$. 
   
\item There is a finite collection $\{X_j\}$ of disjointly embedded closed smooth submanifolds inside $\mathcal Q$ with trivial normal bundles, such that the embedding $\bigsqcup_j X_j \to\mathcal Q$ induces isomorphisms of integral homology groups of positive dimensions. 
\end{enumerate} 
Then $\mathcal Q$ is diffeomorphic to the boundary connected sum 
$\coprod_j (X_j \times D^{d+1 - \dim{X_j}})$, 
and therefore $\6\mathcal Q$ is diffeomorphic to the connected sum $\#_j (X_j \times S^{d - \dim{X_j}})$.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let the polytope $P$ be given by a system~\eqref{ptope} without redundant inequalities, so that $P$ has $m$ facets. We identify $\mathcal{Z}_P$ with the nondegenerate intersection of quadrics 
\[ 
  \bigl\{ z \in \mathbb{C}^m \colon 
  \gamma_1|z_1|^2+\dots+\gamma_m|z_m|^2=\delta\bigr\}, 
\] 
see~\eqref{intqu}. The proof is based on the application of the Theorem~\ref{h-cob}, for this we need to provide a manifold $\mathcal Q$ satisfying its conditions, with boundary $\6 \mathcal{Q} = \mathcal{Z}_P$ and a collection of submanifolds $\{X_j\}$. 
 
Following~\cite[\S 1]{gi-lo13}, define 
\begin{equation}\label{define-Q} 
  \mathcal Q=\bigl\{ (x,z) \in \mathbb{R}_\geqslant\times\mathbb{C}^m \colon 
  \gamma_1(x^2+|z_1|^2)+|z_2|^2+\dots+\gamma_m|z_m|^2 
  =\delta\bigr\}. 
\end{equation} 
Then $\mathcal Q$ is a smooth manifold with boundary $\6\mathcal Q =\mathcal  Q \cap \{x = 0\} = \mathcal{Z}_P$. Also note that $\mathcal Q \cap \{x = c\} \cong \mathcal{Z}_P$ for small $c\geqslant0$. Denote by $i\colon \mathcal{Z}_P \hookrightarrow\mathcal  Q$ the embedding of the boundary and by $i_c$ the embedding $\mathcal{Z}_P \cong\mathcal  Q \cap \{x = c\} \hookrightarrow\mathcal  Q$. The normal bundles of these embeddings are obviously trivial. 
     
Let $\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_P$. Similarly to homeomorphism $\mathcal Z_P\cong (D^2,S^1)^\mathcal{K}$ we have a homeomorphism $\mathcal Q \cong (\bm{X}, \bm{A})^\mathcal{K}$, where $(\bm{X}, \bm{A})^\mathcal{K}$ is a polyhedral product (\cite[Construction~4.2.1]{BP}) corresponding to the collection of pairs $(X_1, A_1) = (D^3_+, S^2_+)$ and $(X_j, A_j) = (D^2, S^1)$ for $j >1$. Here $D^3_+=\{(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}_\geqslant\times\mathbb{C}\colon x^2+|z|^2\leqslant1\}$ and $S^2_+=\{(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}_\geqslant\times\mathbb{C}\colon x^2+|z|^2=1\}$. Since the pair $(D^3_+, S^2_+)$ is contractible, there is a homotopy equivalence $\mathcal Q \simeq (D^2,S^1)^{\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1}}=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1}}$. We also have a retraction $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1}}$, where $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}=\partial\mathcal Q$ and $\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1}}\simeq\mathcal Q$. Therefore, the embedding of the boundary $i\colon\partial\mathcal Q\hookrightarrow\mathcal Q$ is surjective on the homology groups.  
     
Condition (1) of Theorem~\ref{h-cob} is satisfied since the moment-angle complexes $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}=\partial\mathcal Q$ and $\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1}}\simeq\mathcal Q$ are two-connected (\cite[Proposition~4.3.5]{BP}), and Poincar\'e duality holds for $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}$. 
         
To satisfy condition (2) of Theorem~\ref{h-cob} it is sufficient to provide a set of submanifolds $\{X_j\}$ in $\mathcal{Z}_P$ with trivial normal bundles, such that the embedding $\bigsqcup_j X_j \to \mathcal{Z}_P \overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathcal Q$ induces isomorphisms of integral homology groups of positive dimensions. (The submanifolds $X_j$ can be assumed to be parwise disjoint in $\mathcal Q$ by considering the embeddings $ X_j \to \mathcal{Z}_P \overset{i_{c_j}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathcal Q$ with pairwise different small constants~$c_j$.) 
         
Now we analyse the homology groups $H_*(\mathcal Q)$. We have $\mathcal{H}_{l,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1})=0$ for $l\geqslant 3$, since $\mathcal{K}$ is a 3-dimensional sphere. That is, 
\[ 
  \widetilde H_*(\mathcal Q) \cong \mathcal{H}_{0,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1})\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1})\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1}). 
\]   
   
By assumption $\mathcal{K}$ has exactly two missing edges which form a single chordless $4$-cycle $C$. Without loss of generality we assume that $\{1\} \notin C$. Then $\mathcal{H}_{2,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1}) = 0$.  
Indeed, if there exist $I \subset [m]\setminus 1$ such that $\mathcal{H}_{2,I} \neq 0$ then we have $\mathcal{H}^{0,[m] \setminus I} \cong \mathcal{H}_{2,I} \neq 0$ due to Poincar\'e duality~\eqref{aldua}, but $1 \in [m] \setminus I$ and hence $\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus I}$ is connected and $\mathcal{H}^{0,[m] \setminus I} = 0$, a contradiction.  
     
The group $\widetilde{H}_0(\mathcal{K}_I)$ is nonzero is and only if $I$ is one of the two missing edges, since otherwise the full subcomplex $\mathcal{K}_I$ is connected. Therefore $\mathcal{H}_{0,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1}) \cong \mathcal{H}_{0,*}(C) \cong \mathbb{Z}\langle a_1, a_2 \rangle$, where $a_1$ and $a_2$ correspond to the missing edges of~$C$. 
 
From Poincar\'e duality we obtain $\mathcal{H}_{1,I}=\widetilde{H}_1(\mathcal{K}_I) \cong \widetilde{H}^1(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus I})$ for any $I\subset[m]$, hence the group $\mathcal{H}_{1,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1})$ is torsion-free. Also $\widetilde{H}_1(C) \cong \mathbb{Z}\langle c \rangle$, and for $\mathcal{K}_I \neq C$ every nonzero cycle $\gamma \in \widetilde{H}_1(\mathcal{K}_I)$ can be written as $\gamma = \lambda_1 \gamma_1 + \cdots + \lambda_k\gamma_k$, where each $\gamma_i$ corresponds to a missing 2-face of $\mathcal{K}_I$ and $\lambda_i\in\mathbb Z$. 
Hence, $\mathcal{H}_{1,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus 1}) \cong \mathbb{Z}\langle c, b_\alpha \colon \alpha \in A\rangle$, where the classes $\{b_\alpha\colon\alpha \in A\}$ correspond to missing 2-faces. Finally we obtain 
\[ 
  \widetilde H_*(\mathcal Q) = \mathbb{Z}\langle a_1, a_2, c \rangle \oplus \mathbb{Z}\langle b_\alpha \colon \alpha \in A \rangle \cong  
  \widetilde H_*\bigl( S^3 \times S^3 \bigr) \oplus  
  \widetilde H_*\Bigl( \bigvee_{\alpha \in A} S^{n_\alpha} \Bigr). 
\] 
 
Set one of the submanifolds $X_j$ equal to $S^3 \times S^3 = \mathcal{Z}_C$, it corresponds to the full subcomplex $C$. The embedding $\mathcal{Z}_C \hookrightarrow \mathcal{Q}$ has trivial normal bundle by Lemma~\ref{by-Iriye} below and induces a natural isomorphism between $\widetilde H_*(\mathcal{Z}_C)$ and the subgroup of $H_*(\mathcal Q)$ generated by the elements $a_1, a_2$ and $c$. 
 
The remaining generators $b_\alpha$ of the group $\widetilde H_*(\mathcal Q)$ correspond to missing 2-faces of $\mathcal{K}_P$. They are represented by embedded spheres with trivial normal bundles by Lemma~\ref{normal_bundles} below.

The embedding $f_j \colon D^2_j \times T^{J\setminus j}\hookrightarrow V \subset \mathcal{Z}_P$ and the embedding $T^J\cdot U\subset V\cap(T^J\cdot X)\subset\mathcal{Z}_P$ agree on the intersection $Y_{j,J}\cap (T^J\cdot U)=T^J\cdot p_j$. Therefore they can be extended to an embedding 
\[ 
  f'_j\colon D^{2k-1} \times D^2_j \times T^{J \setminus j} \hookrightarrow V, 
\]   
where $f'_j(D^{2k-1} \times D^2_j \times T^{J \setminus j})\cong U\times Y_{j,J}$.  
 
Then we have an embedding 
\begin{equation}\label{bordism} 
  b\colon (S^{2k-1} \times S^1_j \cup_{D^{2k-1} \times S^1} D^{2k-1} \times D^2_j)  
  \times T^{J\setminus j} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{Z}_P 
\end{equation} 
with image $(T^J\cdot X)\cup_{T^J\cdot U} (U\times Y_{j,J})$, 
where the restriction of $b$ to $S^{2k-1}\times S^1_j\times T^{J\setminus j}= S^{2k-1}\times T^J$ coincides with $i\colon S^{2k-1} \times T^{J} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{Z}_P$, and the restriction of $b$ to $D^{2k-1} \times D^2_j\times T^{J\setminus j}$ coincides with $f'_j$. Restricting the embedding $b$ to the gyration $\mathcal{G}(S^{2k-1})$ we obtain an embedding 
\begin{equation}\label{g'} 
\begin{gathered} 
  g'\colon \mathcal{G}(S^{2k-1})\times T^{J\setminus j}=\bigl((S^{2k-1}\setminus D^{2k-1})\times S^1\cup_{S^{2k-2}\times S^1}S^{2k-2}\times D^2\bigr) \times T^{J\setminus j} \xrightarrow{\cong}\\  
  \xrightarrow{\cong} \bigl(T^J\cdot (X\setminus U)\bigr)\cup_{T^J\cdot \partial U}  
  \bigl(\6 U\times Y_{j,J}\bigr) 
  \subset (T^J\cdot X)\cup_{T^J\cdot U} (U\times Y_{j,J})\subset\mathcal{Z}_P. 
\end{gathered} 
\end{equation} 
However, this embedding is not smooth along $ T^J\cdot \partial U$ and needs to be smoothened (see Fig.~\ref{fig:GX-angles}), and we also need to glue together the trivial normal bundles over the parts $T^J\cdot (X\setminus U)$ and $\6 U\times Y_{j,J}$ in~$\mathcal{Z}_P$. 
 
Since $T^J\cdot (X\setminus U)\subset T^J\cdot X=i(S^{2k-1} \times T^J)$, the normal bundle to $T^J\cdot (X\setminus U)$ in $\mathcal{Z}_P$ is the restriction of the trivial normal bundle $\nu(i\colon S^{2k-1} \times T^J \hookrightarrow \mathcal{Z}_P)$.                                
First consider the case $J=\varnothing$, i.\,e. the normal bundle of the sphere $X=\mathcal Z_Q\cong S^{2k-1}$ in $\mathcal Z_P$. This embedding of $S^{2k-1}$ into $\mathcal Z_P$ is a restriction of the embedding $i\colon S^{2k-1} \times T^{[m]\setminus I} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{Z}_P$ from Lemma~\ref{normal-mf-submanifold}, so we have a decomposition 
\[ 
  \nu(i\colon S^{2k-1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}_P)\cong\tau^{[m] \setminus I}\oplus \nu(i\colon S^{2k-1} \times T^{[m]\setminus I}  
  \hookrightarrow \mathcal{Z}_P)|_X=:\tau^{[m] \setminus I}\oplus\nu', 
\] 
where $\tau^{[m] \setminus I}$ is the trivial tangent bundle along the orbits of the action of the torus~$T^{[m] \setminus I}$ and $\nu'$ is the trivial bundle of dimension $m+n-(2k-1)-(m-k)=n-k+1$, which is identified with the normal bundle of $Q\cong\Delta^{k-1}$ in~$P$. Now for arbitrary $J\subset[m]\setminus I$ we have 
\[ 
  \nu(i\colon S^{2k-1} \times T^J \hookrightarrow \mathcal{Z}_P) = \tau^{[m] \setminus (I \cup J)} \oplus \nu', 
\] 
Choose a decomposition $\nu'=\eta^1\oplus\nu''$ into the sum of a one-dimensional trivial subbundle $\eta^1$, which at the point $p_j = X\cap Y_{j,J}\in\partial Y_{j,J}$ is generated by the normal to the boundary $\partial Y_{j,J}\cong T^J$ in $Y_{j,J}\cong D^2_j \times T^{J \setminus j}$, and a trivial subbundle $\nu''$ of rank $n-k$.  
Spreading by the action of the torus $T^J$ we obtain a decomposition 
\begin{equation}\label{nu-S-T} 
  \nu(T^J\cdot (X\setminus U)\subset\mathcal{Z}_P)=\nu(i\colon S^{2k-1} \times T^J \hookrightarrow \mathcal{Z}_P)|_{T^J\cdot (X\setminus U)} = \tau^{[m] \setminus (I \cup J)} \oplus\eta^1\oplus \nu''. 
\end{equation} 
     
Now consider the normal bundle of $\6 U\times Y_{j,J}$ in~$\mathcal{Z}_P$, which is the normal bundle of the embedding $f'_j\colon S^{2k-2} \times D^2_j \times T^{J \setminus j} 
  \hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}_P$. 
We have $\6 U\times Y_{j,J}\subset V\subset\mathcal{Z}_P$, where $V \cong U\times Y_{j,J}\times D^{m+n-2k-l}$. Then we have a decomposition 
\begin{equation}\label{nu-dU-Y} 
  \nu(\6 U\times Y_{j,J}\subset\mathcal{Z}_P)= 
  \nu(f'_j\colon S^{2k-2} \times D^2_j \times T^{J \setminus j} 
  \hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}_P)= 
  \widetilde{\eta}^1 \oplus \xi 
  \oplus \tau^{[m] \setminus (I \cup J)} , 
\end{equation} 
where $\widetilde{\eta}^1=\nu(\6 U\times Y_{j,J} \subset U\times Y_{j,J})$ is a trivial one-dimensional bundle and $\xi$ is some bundle of rank~$n-k$. 
 
From formulas~\eqref{nu-S-T} and~\eqref{nu-dU-Y} we obtain two decompositions of the normal bundles over two parts of the embedding~\eqref{g'}: 
\begin{equation}\label{twoparts} 
\begin{aligned} 
  \nu(g')|_{T^J\cdot(X \setminus U)} &=  \eta^1 \oplus \nu''\oplus 
  \tau^{[m] \setminus (I \cup J)},\\ 
  \nu(g')|_{\6 U \times Y_{j,J}} &= \widetilde{\eta}^1 \oplus \xi 
   \oplus \tau^{[m] \setminus (I \cup J)}. 
\end{aligned} 
\end{equation} 
Moreover, the bundles $\nu''$ and $\xi$ are identified over the intersection $(T^J\cdot(X \setminus U))\cap(\6 U \times Y_{j,J})=T^J\cdot\6U$ due to the natural identification of all other bundles: 
\begin{multline*} 
  \tau(\mathcal{Z}_P)|_{T^J \cdot\6U} = \tau(T^J \cdot U)|_{T^J\cdot\6U} \oplus  
  \nu\bigl(T^J\cdot (X\setminus U)\subset \mathcal{Z}_P\bigr)\big|_{T^J\cdot\6U} =\\=  
  \bigl( \tau^J \oplus \tau(\6 U) \oplus \widetilde{\eta}^1\bigr) \oplus  
  \bigl( \eta^1 \oplus \nu''\oplus \tau^{[m] \setminus (I \cup J)} \bigr), 
\end{multline*} 
and at the same time 
\begin{multline*} 
  \tau(\mathcal{Z}_P)|_{T^J \cdot \6 U} = 
  \tau(\6 U \times Y_{j,J})|_{T^J \cdot\6U} \oplus  
  \nu(\6 U \times Y_{j,J} \subset \mathcal{Z}_P)|_{T^J \cdot\6U}  =\\ 
  = \bigl(\tau(\6 U) \oplus \tau^J \oplus \eta^1\bigr) \oplus \bigl(\widetilde{\eta}^1 \oplus \xi \oplus \tau^{[m] \setminus (I \cup J)}\bigr). 
\end{multline*} 
Note that the decomposition $\tau(Y_{j,J})|_{\6Y_{j,J}} = \tau^J \oplus \eta^1$ used in the last formula cannot be extended to the whole $Y_{j,J}\cong D^2\times T^{J\setminus j}$, but we do not need this. 
 
Since the bundles $\nu''$ and $\xi$ in~\eqref{twoparts} are identified over $T^J\cdot\6 U$ it remains only to smoothen the corners of the gyration $\mathcal{G}(S^{2k-1})$ at $S^{2k-2} \times S^1$ and to glue the bundles $\widetilde{\eta}^1$ and $\eta^1$ over $T^J\cdot\6 U$. 
 
Consider the embedding of a collar neighbourhood $U_1\cong S^{2k-2} \times T^J \times [-\varepsilon,0]$ of the boundary $T^J\cdot\6 U$ of the manifold $T^J\cdot(X\setminus U)$: 
\[ 
  i_1\colon S^{2k-2} \times T^J \times [-\varepsilon,0]\to T^J\cdot(X\setminus U), \quad 
  (s,t,r) \mapsto t\cdot \exp(r\widetilde\eta^1)\, i(s). 
\] 
where $\exp(r\widetilde\eta^1)\, i(s)$ is the shift of the point $i(s)\in\6 U\subset X$ by $r$ along the trajectory of the tangent vector field $\widetilde\eta^1$.  
 
Consider also the embedding of a collar neighbourhood $U_2\cong S^{2k-2} \times T^J \times [0, \varepsilon]$ of the boundary $T^J\cdot\6 U$ of the manifold $\6 U \times Y_{j,J}$: 
\[ 
  i_2\colon S^{2k-2} \times T^J \times [0, \varepsilon]\to \6 U \times Y_{j,J}, \quad 
  (s,t,r) \mapsto t\cdot \exp(r\eta^1)\, i(s). 
\] 
where $\exp(r\eta^1)\, i(s)$ is the shift of the point $i(s)\in\6 U\subset X$ by $r$ along the trajectory of the tangent vector field $\eta^1$.  
 
Then the gyration $\mathcal{G}(S^{2k-1})$ can be decomposed as 
\begin{equation}\label{gyrdec} 
\begin{aligned} 
   \mathcal{G}(S^{2k-1}) &\cong \bigl((S^{2k-1}\setminus D^{2k-1})\times S^1 \bigr) \cup_{S^{2k-2} \times S^1 \times \{-\varepsilon\}} S^{2k-2} \times S^1 \times [-\varepsilon, 0] \\ 
   &\cup_{S^{2k-2} \times S^1 \times \{0\}} S^{2k-2} \times S^1 \times [0, \varepsilon] \cup_{S^{2k-2} \times S^1 \times \{\varepsilon\}} \bigl( S^{2k-2}\times D^2 \bigr), 
    \end{aligned} 
\end{equation}     
         
                                                                                                                                                                
         
and now we regard $T^J \cdot \6 U = U_1 \cap U_2$ as separated from $T^J \cdot (X \setminus U)$ and $\6 U \times Y_{j,J}$. In other words, after taking collars we can regard $T^J \cdot (X \setminus U)$ and $\6 U \times Y_{j,J}$ as disjoint subsets in $g'(\mathcal{G}(S^{2k-2}))$. By gluing the segments $[-\varepsilon,0]$ and $[0, \varepsilon]$ we obtain $U_1 \cup_{T^J \cdot \6 U} U_2 \cong S^{2k-2} \times T^J \times [-\varepsilon, \varepsilon]$, but there is a corner at $r = 0$ that needs to be smoothened. 
 
We replace the nonsmooth embedding~\eqref{g'} by a smooth one by shifting the image of $g'(\mathcal{G}(S^{2k-1})\times T^{J \setminus j})$ along the trajectories of a smooth vector field that coincides with $\eta^1$ on $T^J\cdot (X\setminus U)$ and with $\widetilde\eta^1$ on $\6U\times Y_{j,J}$ (see Fig.~\ref{fig:GX-angles}). Explicitly, the new embedding $g\colon \mathcal{G}(S^{2k-1}) \times T^{J \setminus j} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{Z}_P$ is given by the following formulas on the parts of the union~\eqref{gyrdec}:   
\begin{align*} 
  &  g|_{(S^{2k-1} \setminus D^{2k-1}) \times T^J}(x,t) = \exp(\varepsilon \eta^1)\,i(x,t) ,\\ 
  &  g|_{S^{2k-2}\times T^J\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]}(s,t,r)  =  \exp\biggl(\varepsilon\sqrt{1 - \frac{r + \varepsilon}{2\varepsilon}}\eta^1 + \varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{r + \varepsilon}{2\varepsilon}}\widetilde{\eta}^1 \biggr)\, i(s,t),\\ 
  &  g|_{S^{2k-2} \times D^2 \times T^{J \setminus j}}(s,y) = \exp(\varepsilon\widetilde{\eta}^1) f'_j(s,y), 
\end{align*} 
where $x \in S^{2k-1} \setminus D^{2k-1}$, $t \in T^J$, $s \in S^{2k-2}$ and $y \in D^2 \times T^{J \setminus j}$. This is a smooth embedding, as $i$ and $f'_j$ agree on $T^J \cdot \6 U$.

Suppose the data $\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm A\}$ defines a complete simplicial fan, and let $I$ be a missing face in $\mathcal{K}$ with $|I|=k$.  
Then there exists a smooth embedding $S^{2k-1}\times T^{[m]\setminus I}\hookrightarrow \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}$ with trivial normal bundle, whose restriction to $S^{2k-1}\times T^J$ for any $J\subset[m]\setminus I$ is a smooth submanifold representing the homology class in $H_{2k-1+|J|}(\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal K)$ corresponding to the missing face~$I$.

To construct a smooth embedding, we need to consider two cases. 
 
\textit{Case 1:} the vectors $\mathrm A_I$ are linearly dependent. 
For each $i \in I$ the vectors $\mathrm A_{I \setminus i}$ are linearly independent since $I \setminus \{i\}\in \mathcal{K}$. Hence, the vectors $\mathrm A_I$ satisfy exactly one linear relation $\sum_{i \in I} \lambda_i a_i = 0$. 
                                                                
                                                         
Let $\widetilde W^*=\mathbb{R}\langle\mathrm A_I\rangle$ be the subspace in $W^*$ spanned by the vectors $\mathrm A_I$, then $\dim \widetilde W^*=k-1$. Choose a projection $p\colon W^*\to\widetilde W^*$ and consider the configuration $\widetilde{\mathrm A}=p(\mathrm A)$ of $m$ vectors in~$\widetilde W^*$; note that $\widetilde a_i=a_i$ for $i\in I$. Consider the short exact sequence 
\[ 
  0\longrightarrow\widetilde V\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^m\stackrel{\widetilde A}\longrightarrow  
  \widetilde W^*  \longrightarrow 0, 
\] 
where $\widetilde A(e_i)=\widetilde a_i$ and $\widetilde V\cong\mathbb{R}^{m+1-k}$ is the space of linear relations among the vectors~$\widetilde{\mathrm A}$. We have $\widetilde A=p\circ A$, which implies $\ker A\subset\ker\widetilde A$ and hence $\widetilde{V}=V \oplus V'$, where $V$ is the space of linear relations on $\mathrm A$ and $V'$ is a complementary subspace with $\dim V' = n+1-k$. Let $\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]}$ be the full subcomplex of $\mathcal{K}$ corresponding to the missing face~$I$, regarded as a simplicial complex on~$[m]$, i.\,e. $[m]\setminus I$ is the set of its ghost vertices. Then the data $\{\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]},\widetilde{\mathrm A}\}$ define a complete simplicial fan in~$\widetilde W^*$. By Theorem~\ref{zkquo}, $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}\cong U(\mathcal{K})/V$ and $\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]}} \cong U(\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]})/\widetilde{V}$. Moreover, $U(\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]})$ is a Zariski open subset of $U(\mathcal{K})$, i.\,e. $U(\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]})=U(\mathcal{K})\setminus R$, where $R$ is a closed subset (the arrangement of coordinate subspaces corresponding to $J\in\mathcal{K}$, $J\notin\mathcal{K}_I$). We have 
\[ 
  S^{2k-1}\times T^{[m]\setminus I} \cong \mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]}} \cong U(\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]}) / \widetilde{V} = \bigl(U(\mathcal{K})\setminus R\bigr)/(V\oplus V')\cong 
  \bigl(\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}\setminus R'\bigr)/V', 
                                                                                                                                   
\] 
where $R'\cong R/V$ is a closed subset of $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}$. Since the principal $V'$-bundle $(\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K} \setminus R')\to S^{2k-1}\times T^{[m]\setminus I}$ is trivial, its smooth section provides the required embedding $S^{2k-1}\times T^{[m]\setminus I}\hookrightarrow \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}$ with trivial normal bundle.

\textit{Case 2}: the vectors $\mathrm A_I$ are linearly independent. As in Case~1 we define $\widetilde W^*=\mathbb{R}\langle\mathrm A_I\rangle$, the configuration $\widetilde{\mathrm A}=p(\mathrm A)$, the space of linear relations $\widetilde{V}$ and the decomposition $\widetilde{V}=V \oplus V'$, but now $\dim \widetilde W^*=k$, $\dim \widetilde V^*=m-k$ and $\dim V' = n-k$. The data $\{\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]},\widetilde{\mathrm A}\}$ define an \textit{incomplete} simplicial fan in the space $\widetilde W^*$ so the action of $\widetilde{V}$ on $U(\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]})$ is proper, but the quotient $U(\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]})/\widetilde{V}$ is noncompact. Denote by $\widetilde{\Gamma}$ the Gale dual to $\widetilde{\mathrm A}$ configuration. Since $\widetilde{\mathrm A}_I$ is a basis of $\widetilde{W}^*$ then $\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\widehat{I}}$ is a basis of $\widetilde{V}^*$ (Proposition~\ref{dspan}). We have 
\[ 
  U(\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]}) / \widetilde{V} = \big( (\mathbb{C}^I \setminus \{0\}) \times (\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\})^{[m]\setminus I} \big) / \widetilde{V} \cong (\mathbb{C}^I \setminus \{0\}) \times T^{[m]\setminus I}, 
\] 
since for any point $(z_1, \ldots, z_m) \in U(\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]})$ there exists $v \in \widetilde{V}$ such that $\langle \gamma_j, v \rangle = -\ln{|z_j|}$ for all $j \notin I$.

\[ 
(\mathbb{C}^I \setminus \{0\}) \times T^{[m]\setminus I} \cong \mathbb{R} \times S^{2k-1}\times T^{[m]\setminus I} \cong \mathbb{R} \times \mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{I,[m]}}. 
\] 
Accordingly, defining $R$ and $R'$ as in Case~1, we obtain

\]

<!-- thm-10: body_md -->
\label{connected-starshaped} 
Let $\mathcal{K}$ be a 3-dimensional starshaped sphere on $[m]$ such that $\mathcal{K}^1$ is a chordal graph and there is a vertex $v \in [m]$ adjacent to all other vertices. Then $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}$ is diffeomorphic to a connected sum of products of pairs of spheres.
<!-- end thm-10 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
The proof is based on the application of Theorem~\ref{h-cob} and follows the same scheme as the proof of Theorem~\ref{diffeo4}. 
 
                                                                                                                                      
Define $\mathcal Q = (\bm{X}, \bm{A})^\mathcal{K}$ as the polyhedral product corresponding to the collection of pairs $(X_v, A_v) = (D^3_+, S^2_+)$ and $(X_i, A_i) = (D^2, S^1)$ for $i \neq v$. 
                                                                                        
Since $\mathcal{K}$ arises from a complete simplicial fan, the quotient construction by the exponential action shows that $\mathcal Q$ is a smooth manifold with boundary $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               
Since the pair $(D^3_+, S^2_+)$ is contractible we obtain $\mathcal Q \simeq (D^2,S^1)^{\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus v}} = \mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus v}}$. 
 
Next we provide a collection of embedded smooth submanifolds $X_j \subset \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}$ with trivial normal bundles, required by Theorem~\ref{h-cob}. 
 
Since $\mathcal{K}^1$ is a chordal graph then the groups $\mathcal{H}_{0,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus v})$ and $\mathcal{H}_{1,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus v})$ are generated by classes corresponding to missing one-dimensional and two-dimensional faces of~$\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus v}$, respectively. 
 
We prove that $\mathcal{H}_{k,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus v}) = 0$ for $k \geqslant 2$. Indeed, $\mathcal{H}_{k,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus v}) = 0$ for $k \geqslant 3$, since $\mathcal{K}$ is a 3-dimensional sphere. Furthermore, suppose that for some $I \subset [m] \setminus v$ we have $\mathcal{H}_{2,I}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus v}) = \mathcal{H}_{2,I}(\mathcal{K}) \neq 0$, then by Poincar\'e duality~\eqref{aldua} we get $\mathcal{H}^{0,[m] \setminus I}(\mathcal{K}) \cong \mathcal{H}_{2,I}(\mathcal{K})$. However $v \in [m] \setminus I$ so the full subcomplex $\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus I}$ is connected and we obtain $\mathcal{H}^{0,[m] \setminus I}(\mathcal{K}) = 0$ --- a contradiction.  
     
It follows that  
\[ 
  H_*(\mathcal Q) \cong \mathcal{H}_{*,*}(\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus v}) \cong \mathbb{Z} \langle a^0_\alpha \colon \alpha \in A \rangle \oplus \mathbb{Z}\langle b^1_\beta \colon \beta \in B \rangle, 
\] 
where the classes $a^0_\alpha$ correspond to missing edges, and the classes $b^1_\beta$ correspond to missing triangles. All of them are represented by spheres with trivial normal bundles by Lemma~\ref{normal-mf-starshaped}. 
     
Therefore, we can apply Theorem~\ref{h-cob} which gives us that $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}=\6\mathcal Q$ is diffeomorphic to a connected sum of products of spheres.

Let $\mathcal{K}$ be a starshaped neighbourly simplicial sphere of odd dimension $n-1$ with $\mathcal{K}\ne\partial\Delta^n$. Then there is a diffeomorphism $\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K} \cong M_1\#\cdots\# M_k$, where each $M_i$ is a product of two spheres.

Set $q=\frac{n-2}{2}$. As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{connected-starshaped} we construct a manifold $\mathcal Q$ with boundary $\partial\mathcal Q\cong\mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{K}$ and $\mathcal Q \simeq \mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{[m] \setminus \{1\}}}$.

\label{bbexa} 
The Barnette sphere is a well-known example of a three-dimensional simplicial sphere that is not polytopal. Its construction (see, e.g.,~\cite[Construction 2.5.5]{BP}) starts with a configuration of three tetrahedra $abde$, $acdf$ and $bcef$ in Fig.~\ref{fig:Barnette-Bruckner}, left. Then one adds the vertex $p$ and the tetrahedra $pabc$, $pdef$, $pabd$, $pbde$, $pbce$, $pcef$, $pacf$ and $padf$ inside, as well as the vertex $p'$ and the tetrahedra $p'abc$, $p'def$, $p'abe$, $p'ade$, $p'acd$, $p'cdf$, $p'bcf$ and $p'bef$ outside, as in Fig.~\ref{fig:Barnette-Bruckner}, right. The result is a simplicial triangulation of a 3-dimensional sphere with $8$ vertices and $19$ tetrahedra, which we denote by~$\mathcal{K}_\mathrm{Ba}$. 
The Br\"uckner sphere is obtained from $\mathcal{K}_\mathrm{Ba}$ by a bistellar flip along the edge $pp'$, as the result of which the two tetrahedra $pabc$ and $p'abc$ are replaced by the three tetrahedra $pp'ab$, $pp'bc$ and $pp'ac$ (see Fig.~\ref{fig:Barnette-Bruckner}). We denote the corresponding simplicial complex by $\mathcal{K}_\mathrm{Br}$; it is neighbourly, has $8$ vertices and $20$ tetrahedra. The Barnette sphere and the Br\"uckner sphere are nonpolytopal, but they are starshaped (see~\cite[Example~2.5.8]{BP}). Therefore, the corresponding moment-angle complexes $\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_\mathrm{Ba}}$ and $\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_\mathrm{Br}}$ are smooth manifolds.
