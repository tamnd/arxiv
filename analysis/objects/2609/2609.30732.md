<!-- head-1: body_md -->
We use $\|\cdot\|$ to denote the $\ell_2$ norm for vectors and the operator norm for matrices. Let $I$ be the identity matrix and $\0$ the zero vector or matrix, with dimensions clear from the context. For the constraints $c(\bm{x}): \mathbb{R}^d\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^m$, we let $G(\bm{x}) \coloneqq \nabla c(\bm{x}) \in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$ denote its Jacobian matrix and $c^i(\bm{x})$ denote the $i$-th constraint for $1\leq i\leq m$. Define $P(\bm{x}) \coloneqq I-G(\bm{x})^{\top}[G(\bm{x})G(\bm{x})^{\top}]^{-1}G(\bm{x})$ as the projection matrix onto the null space of $G(\bm{x})$. Then, we let $Z(\bm{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times (d-m)}$ form an orthonormal basis of $\text{ker}(G(\bm{x}))$ such that $Z(\bm{x})^{\top} Z(\bm{x})=I$ and $Z(\bm{x})Z(\bm{x})^{\top}=P(\bm{x})$. For any iteration index $k$, we write $\bm{c}_k \coloneqq c(\bm{x}_k)$ and $G_k \coloneqq G(\bm{x}_k) = \nabla c(\bm{x}_k)$ (similarly, $P_k \coloneqq P(\bm{x}_k)$, etc.).

\label{lemma:Ful_cauchy}
For any $k\geq 0$, the Cauchy reduction of Problem \eqref{eq:Byrd_tangential} satisfies
\begin{equation*}
q_k(\bm{u}_k^{CP})-q_k(\0) \leq-\|Z_k^{\top}(\nabla f_k+B_k\bm{w}_k)\| \Delta_k^t +\frac{1}{2}\|B_k\| \left(\Delta_k^t\right)^2,
\end{equation*}
where $\Delta_k^t \coloneqq \sqrt{\Delta_k^2 - \Vert \bm{w}_k \Vert^2}$ is the trust-region radius for the tangential step.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Our method specifies a parameter $\theta\in(0,1)$ to split the trust-region radius $\Delta_k$ into $\theta\Delta_k$ and $\sqrt{1-\theta^2}\Delta_k$ for normal and tangential steps, respectively. It is worth mentioning that a recent work \cite{Fang2024Fully} proposed a parameter-free radius decomposition, in which $\theta$ is chosen adaptively at each step based on feasibility and optimality residuals. Our analysis can be readily extended to this more sophisticated decomposition scheme; however, we consider specifying any $\theta\in(0,1)$ for clarity and simplicity. First, it is more efficient to implement. Second, our goal is not to design a new relaxation mechanism, but to develop a new theory for trust-region methods under heavy-tailed~noise. The present scheme has captured the essential features of radius decomposition.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
We note that the normal step $\bm{w}_k$ reduces the feasibility residual while the tangential step $\bm{t}_k$ reduces the optimality residual. To see this clearly, we recall that $\Delta\bm{x}_k=\bm{w}_k+\bm{t}_k$ with $\bm{t}_k\in\text{ker}(G_k)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constraint_violation}
\|\bm{c}_k+G_k\Delta\bm{x}_k\|-\|\bm{c}_k\|=\|\bm{c}_k+G_k\bm{w}_k\|-\|\bm{c}_k\| \stackrel{\eqref{eq:Sto_normal_step}}{=}-\gamma_k\|\bm{c}_k\|\leq 0,
\end{equation}
where the inequality is strict whenever $\bm{c}_k\neq \0$. Furthermore, define the least-squares Lagrange multiplier as $\bm{\lambda}_k \coloneqq -[G_k G_k^{\top}]^{-1} G_k \nabla f_k$, then $ \nabla_{\bm{x}} \mathcal{L}_k = \nabla f_k + G_k^{\top} \bm{\lambda}_k = P_k \nabla f_k$. Using $Z_k^{\top} Z_k = I$, $Z_k Z_k^{\top} = P_k$, and $P_k^2 = P_k$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\|Z_k^{\top} (\nabla f_k+B_k\bm{w}_k)\|^2 & =  (\nabla f_k+B_k\bm{w}_k)^{\top} Z_k Z_k^{\top} (\nabla f_k+B_k\bm{w}_k) \\
& =(\nabla f_k+B_k\bm{w}_k)^{\top} P_k^2 (\nabla f_k+B_k\bm{w}_k) = \|\nabla_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{L}_k + P_k B_k\bm{w}_k\|^2.
\end{align*}
Consequently, Lemma \ref{lemma:Ful_cauchy} can be equivalently written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ful_Cauchy_2}
q_k(\bm{u}_k^{CP}) - q_k(\0) \leq  -\|\nabla_{\bm{x}} \mathcal{L}_k + P_k B_k \bm{w}_k\| \Delta_k^t +\frac{1}{2}\|B_k\| \left(\Delta_k^t\right)^2.
\end{equation}

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:2.4}
Unlike existing line-search-based SSQP methods \citep{Berahas2021Sequential, Berahas2023Accelerating, Na2022adaptive, Na2025Statistical, Na2023Inequality, Berahas2024Stochastic}, we do not require $B_k$ to be positive definite in the null space $\text{ker}(G_k)$, i.e., $Z_k^{\top}B_kZ_k\succ \0$. Our analysis only requires $B_k$ to be upper bounded. This advantage stems from the trust-region scheme; more precisely, the trust-region constraint ensures \eqref{eq:Byrd_tangential} to be bounded below even without convexity. This flexibility allows us to construct different choices of $B_k$ when forming the subproblem. In Section \ref{sec:experiment}, we consider several options: the identity matrix, quasi-Newton updates, estimated Hessians, and averaged estimated Hessians. In contrast, the aforementioned literature set $B_k=I$ without any~curvature~information in practice.

<!-- assumption-1: body_md -->
\label{ass:regularity}
Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ be an open convex set containing the iterates $\{\bm{x}_k\}$. We assume that the objective $f(\bm{x})$ is continuously differentiable and bounded below by $f_{\inf}$ on $\Omega$, and $\nabla f(\bm{x})$ is Lipschitz continuous on $\Omega$ with constant $L_{\nabla f}>0$. The constraint $c(\bm{x})$ is also continuously differentiable with $L_G$-Lipschitz continuous Jacobian $G(\bm{x}) = \nabla c(\bm{x})$ on $\Omega$.
Moreover, there exist positive constants $\kappa_B$, $\kappa_c$, $\kappa_{\nabla f}$, $\kappa_{1,G}$, $\kappa_{2,G}$ such that for all $k\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regularity_bounds}
\|B_k\|\leq \kappa_B,\qquad \|\bm{c}_k\|\leq \kappa_c,\qquad \|\nabla f_k\|\leq \kappa_{\nabla f},\qquad \kappa_{1,G} I\preceq G_kG_k^\top\preceq \kappa_{2,G} I.
\end{equation}
<!-- end assumption-1 -->
<!-- assumption-2: body_md -->
\label{ass:oracle}
For any $k \geq 0$, the samples $\{\xi_k^i\}_{i=1}^{N_k}\sim\mathcal{P}$ are i.i.d. conditional on $\mathcal{F}_{k-1}$ and are unbiased: $\mathbb{E}[\nabla F(\bm{x}_k;\xi_k^i)\mid\mathcal{F}_{k-1}] = \nabla f_k$ for $1\leq i \leq N_k$. Moreover, there exist $\mathfrak p\in(1,2]$ and constants $\sigma_0 \geq 0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{equ:heavy_tail}
\mathbb{E} [\|\nabla F(\bm{x}_k;\xi_k^i)-\nabla f_k\|^\mathfrak p\mid\mathcal{F}_{k-1}] \leq \sigma_0^\mathfrak p \quad\; \text{ for } \;\; 1\leq i\leq N_k.
\end{equation}
<!-- end assumption-2 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:normal_tangent_contribution}
Under Assumption \ref{ass:regularity}, there exist constants $\kappa_{\mathrm n}, \kappa_{\mathrm t}>0$ such that, for any $k \geq 0$,
\begin{align}
\text{normal:}\quad\quad & - \nabla f_k^{\top}\bm{w}_k + \mu\left(\|\bm{c}_k\|-\|\bm{c}_k+G_k\bar\Delta\bm{x}_k\|\right) \geq \kappa_{\mathrm n}\Delta_k\|\bm{c}_k\|, \label{eq:normal_contribution} \\
\text{tangential:}\quad\quad  & - \nabla f_k^{\top}\bar\bm{t}_k \geq \kappa_{\mathrm t}\Delta_k\|\bar\bm{r}_k\| - \Delta_k\|\bm{e}_k\| - 3\kappa_B\Delta_k^2. \label{eq:tangential_contribution}
\end{align}

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:model_reduction_recursion}
Under Assumption \ref{ass:regularity}, there exists a constant $\kappa_{\ell} > 0$ such that, for any $k\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{equ:local_model_reduction_bound}
\Delta \ell_{\mu}(\bar\Delta\bm{x}_k; \bm{x}_k, \nabla f_k) \geq \kappa_{\ell}\Delta_k\|\bar\nabla\mathcal{L}_k\| - \Delta_k\|\bm{e}_k\| - 3\kappa_B\Delta_k^2.
\end{equation}
Moreover, there exist constants $\kappa_{\phi,1}, \kappa_{\phi,2}, \kappa_{\phi,3}$ such that, for any $k\geq0$,
\begin{multline}\label{eq:lyapunov_recursion}
\phi_{\mu}(\bm{x}_{k+1})-\phi_{\mu}(\bm{x}_k) \leq
- \Delta \ell_{\mu}(\bar\Delta\bm{x}_k; \bm{x}_k, \nabla f_k) + \frac{L_{\nabla f}+\mu L_G}{2}\Delta_k^2 \\
\leq - \kappa_{\phi,1}\Delta_k\|\nabla\mathcal{L}_k\| + \kappa_{\phi,2}\Delta_k\|\bm{e}_k\| + \kappa_{\phi,3}\Delta_k^2.    
\end{multline}

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:tracking_error_summability}
Under Assumptions \ref{ass:regularity} and \ref{ass:oracle}, for any $k \geq 1$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tracking_error_bound_general_batch}
\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{e}_k\|] \leq \Pi_{1:k}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{e}_0\|] + L_{\nabla f}\sum_{s=1}^{k} \Pi_{s:k}\Delta_{s-1} + 2\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_0 \left(\sum_{s=1}^{k}\frac{\nu_s^\mathfrak p \Pi_{s+1:k}^\mathfrak p}{N_s^{\mathfrak p-1}}\right)^{1/\mathfrak p},
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_{s:t} \coloneqq \prod_{j=s}^{t}(1-\nu_j)$ for $s\leq t$ and $\Pi_{s:t}\coloneqq 1$ for $s>t$. Moreover, suppose the sequences $\{\Delta_k,\nu_k,N_k\}_{k\geq 0}$ (cf. \eqref{equ:seq}) are chosen with exponents satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tracking_schedule_general_batch}
	0.5 < a_1 \leq 1, \qquad 0 < a_2 < 2 a_1 - 1, \qquad a_3 \geq 0, \qquad a_1 + (a_2 + a_3) (\mathfrak p-1) / \mathfrak p > 1,
\end{equation}
then we have $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \Delta_k\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{e}_k\|] < \infty$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Under Assumptions \ref{ass:regularity} and \ref{ass:oracle} and supposing  the sequences $\{\Delta_k,\nu_k,N_k\}_{k\geq 0}$ are chosen to satisfy \eqref{eq:tracking_schedule_general_batch}, then we have
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{k \rightarrow \infty} \left(\|\nabla f_k + G_k^{\top} \bm{\lambda}_k\| + \|\bm{c}_k\|\right) = 0 \quad\quad \text{almost surely}.
\end{equation*}

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rem:3.6}

For any fixed moment index $\mathfrak p\in(1,2]$ and batch size exponent $a_3 \geq 0$, condition \eqref{eq:tracking_schedule_general_batch} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tracking_schedule_general_batch_2}
\max\left\{0.5, \, \frac{2\mathfrak p-1}{3\mathfrak p-2} - \frac{\mathfrak p-1}{3\mathfrak p-2}a_3\right\} < a_1 \leq 1, \quad \max\left\{0, \, \frac{\mathfrak p(1-a_1)}{\mathfrak p-1}-a_3\right\} < a_2 < 2a_1-1.
\end{equation}
This form makes the role of $a_3$ transparent. Increasing $a_3$ reduces the heavy-tailed martingale contribution in \eqref{eq:tracking_error_bound_general_batch}. Thus, larger $a_3$ relaxes the requirements on the trust-region radius and momentum sequences, as reflected in \eqref{eq:tracking_schedule_general_batch_2}. This gives a \textit{trade-off} between per-iteration sampling cost and noise control: larger mini-batches require more samples per iteration, but they reduce the heavy-tailed martingale effect and allow the trust-region radius sequence $\{\Delta_k\}_{k}$ to decay more slowly. Indeed,~a~slower~decay of $\{\Delta_k\}_{k\ge0}$ can be advantageous in practice, as it leads to less conservative trial steps.

If $a_3$ is chosen such that $a_3 \geq \mathfrak p / [2(\mathfrak p-1)]$, then the lower bound on $a_2$ in \eqref{eq:tracking_schedule_general_batch_2} becomes $0$ for any $a_1 \in (0.5, 1]$. In this case, the conditions in \eqref{eq:tracking_schedule_general_batch_2} reduce to $0.5 < a_1 \leq 1$, $0 < a_2 < 2a_1-1$. Note that the threshold for $a_3$ depends on $\mathfrak p$; this simplification only indicates that once $a_3$ is large enough, the admissible region for $(a_1,a_2)$ no longer explicitly depends on $\mathfrak p$.

When $a_3 = 0$, the batch size is fixed: $N_k \equiv \lceil N_0\rceil$. In particular, $N_0=1$ is allowed, so our theory covers online trust-region methods that use only a single stochastic gradient sample per iteration. In this fixed-batch setting, even if the moment index $\mathfrak p$ is unknown, one can still adopt the universal choice $a_1 = 1$ and $a_2 \in (0, 1)$, which satisfies \eqref{eq:tracking_schedule_general_batch_2} for all $\mathfrak p\in(1,2]$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\citep[Lemma~3]{Leluc2023Asymptotic}\label{lem:polynomial_product_rate}
Let $\{\nu_k\}_{k\geq1}$ be a nonnegative sequence converging to zero, and let $\lambda>0$, $m\geq1$, and $q\geq0$. Suppose two nonnegative sequences $\{A_k\}_{k\geq0}$ and $\{\varepsilon_k\}_{k\geq1}$ satisfy $A_k=(1-\lambda\nu_k)^mA_{k-1}+\nu_k^{q+1}\varepsilon_k$ for all sufficiently large $k$.

If $\nu_k=\nu_0/(k+1)^a$ with $\nu_0>0$ and $a\in(0,1)$, then $\limsup_{k\to\infty}A_k/\nu_k^q\leq(m\lambda)^{-1}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\varepsilon_k$. If instead $\nu_k=\nu_0/(k+1)$ and $q<m\lambda\nu_0$, then $\limsup_{k\to\infty}A_k/\nu_k^q\leq\nu_0(m\lambda\nu_0-q)^{-1}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\varepsilon_k$.
