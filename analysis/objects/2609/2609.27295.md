Let $(\mathcal{C}, \varphi: \operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{C}} \Rightarrow ()^{\vee \vee})$ be a pivotal category. For any object $c \in \mathcal{C},$ we have
\begin{align*}
    &\mathbf{1} \xrightarrow{\operatorname{coev}_{c^\vee}} c^\vee \otimes c^{\vee \vee} \xrightarrow{ \operatorname{id}_{c^\vee} \otimes \varphi_c^{-1}} c^{\vee} \otimes c\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}_{c}} \mathbf{1} = d^L_c \cdot \operatorname{id}_\mathbf{1}
    \\
  &\mathbf{1} \xrightarrow{\operatorname{coev}_c} c \otimes c^\vee \xrightarrow{\varphi_c \otimes \operatorname{id}_{c^\vee}} c^{\vee \vee} \otimes c^\vee \xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}_{c^\vee}} \mathbf{1} = d^R_c \cdot \operatorname{id}_\mathbf{1}
\end{align*}
for some scalars $d^L_c, d^R_c \in \mathbb{C}.$ Then $\lambda_c, \rho_c$ are the \textbf{left/right quantum dimensions} of $c,$ respectively, denoted $\dim_L^\varphi(c) \coloneq d^L_c,$ and $\dim_R^\varphi(c) \coloneq d^R_c.$

<!-- defn-5: body_md -->
(c.f. \cite[2.1]{etingof2019semisimplificationtensorcategories})
   For $\mathcal{C}$ a linear monoidal category, a \textbf{tensor ideal} $I$ in $\mathcal{C}$ is a collection of subspaces $I(x,y) \subset \mathcal{C}(x,y)$ for all $x,y \in \mathcal{C}$ such that for all $x,y,z,t \in \mathcal{C}$:
   \begin{enumerate}
       \item \label{i1} for $f \in I(x,y)$ and $g \in \mathcal{C}(y,z)$, $h \in \mathcal{C}(z,x)$, we have $f \circ h \in I (z,y)$ and $g \circ f \in I(x,z)$;
       \item \label{i2} for $f \in I(x,y),$ $g \in \mathcal{C}(z,t)$, we have $f \otimes g \in I(x \otimes z, y \otimes t)$ and $g \otimes f \in I(z \otimes x , t \otimes y)$.
   \end{enumerate}
   When $I$ is a tensor ideal, the quotient $\mathcal{C}/I$ defines a linear monoidal category: objects of $\mathcal{C}/I$ are the objects of $\mathcal{C};$ morphism space $\mathcal{C}/I(x,y) \coloneq \mathcal{C}(x,y)/I(x,y);$ composition and tensor product of morphisms are inherited from $\mathcal{C}.$

\label{lem: small face}
     Any trivalent diagram on a disk $D^2$ satisfying the following conditions has a pentagon or smaller face. \\
     (i). It has exactly two open edges intersecting the boundary of $D^2$.\\
     (ii). Every face in it is an $n$-gon for $n \geq 2$.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $s$ be such a diagram. We connect the source and target strands and embed this closed diagram on a sphere: by doing so we created two faces.

 
\[
\vcenter{\hbox{\begin{tikzpicture}[]
\draw[thick](0,.2) -- (0,.8) -- (-.4,1.2)--(0,1.6)--(-.4,2)--(0,2.5) ;
\draw[thick](0,.8) -- (.4,1.2)--(0,1.6);
\draw[thick](-.4,1.2)--(-.4, 2);
\draw[thick](.4,1.2) -- (0,2.5) -- (0,3.1);
\draw[thick,  dotted] (0,.2) to[bend right=90] (1.5,.2) -- (1.5, 3.1) to[bend right=90] (0,3.1);
\draw[thick, red,dotted] (-.6,.3) -- (.7,.3) -- (.7,3) -- (-.6,3)--(-.6,.3);
\node[black] at (0,1.2) {$\scriptstyle \cdots$};
\node[red] at (-.8,0.5){$\scriptstyle s$};

                     \end{tikzpicture}}}
     
\]


Denote the number of vertices by $|V|,$ the number of edges by $|E|,$ the number of faces by $|F|,$ the number of faces with $n$ edges by $|F_n|.$ By Euler's characteristic, $|V|-|E|+|F|=2,$ we have 
\[|F| =  \sum_n  |F_n|,\hspace{.3cm} |E| = \frac{1}{2}  \sum_n  n|F_n|,\hspace{.3cm}  |V| = \frac{1}{3}  \sum_n  n|F_n|,\] since each edge is shared by 2 faces and each vertex is shard by 3 faces. Thus the Euler's characteristic yields
\[
\sum_n \left( 1 - \frac{n}{6} \right) |F_n| = 2.
\]
We have $|F_1|=0$ by assumption. Each $n$-gon for $2 \leq n \leq 5$ contributes a positive weight of at most $\frac{2}{3}$ to this sum. Each hexagon contributes 0 weight. Each $n$-gon for $n \geq 7$ contributes a negative weight. Thus there are at least 3 pentagon or smaller faces in this closed diagram, i.e., there is at least one such face in $s.$
