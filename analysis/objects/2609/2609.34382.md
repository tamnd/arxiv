<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:intro_main}
Let $L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ be a linear space with loopless matroid $M=\operatorname{M}(L)$ and let $k\geq 2$. The generalized matroid determinant $E_{L,-k}$ decomposes into irreducible factors as
\[
E_{L,-k} = \prod_{F \textup{ conn.\ flat of }M} \Delta(L_{F}^{-k+1})^{m_F}, \qquad m_F = k^{\operatorname{rk}(M/F)-c(M)+1}\mu^+(M/F).
\]
The multiplicity $m_F$ further equals the local multiplicity of $L^{-k+1}$ along the stratum indexed by $F$. In particular, \cite[Conjecture 7.1]{MatsubaraHeoTelen2026} holds true.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $X \subset \mathbb{P}^n$ be an irreducible projective variety. Its \emph{dual variety} is the irreducible variety
\[
    X^\vee \coloneq \overline{\Set{H \in (\mathbb{P}^n)^* | \exists x \in X_{\text{reg}} \text{ such that } H \supseteq T_xX}}.
\]
If $\operatorname{codim}(X^\vee) = 1$, then there exists a unique, up to scaling, irreducible polynomial $\Delta_X$ such that $X^\vee = \mathcal{V}(\Delta_X)$ called the \emph{$X$-discriminant}. If $\operatorname{codim} X^\vee >1$ then we set $\Delta_X \coloneq 1$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
The \emph{$X$-resultant} is $\operatorname{Res}(X) \coloneq r^*\operatorname{Ch}(X) \in \mathbb{K}[u_{ij} \mid 0 \leq i \leq d, 0 \leq j \leq n]$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $A \in \mathbb{Z}^{(d+1)\times(n+1)} $ be of rank $d+1$, such that $(1,\dots,1) \in \operatorname{Row}(A)$. Let $a_0,\dots,a_n \in \mathbb{K}^{d+1}$ be the columns of $A$. The projective toric variety $X_A$ associated to $A$ is the closure of the image of the map
\[
  (\mathbb{K}^\times)^{d+1} \to  \mathbb{P}^n , \qquad x \mapsto  [x^{a_0}:\dots:x^{a_n}]
\]
where $x^{a_i} \coloneq x_0^{a_{i,0}}\dotsm x_d^{a_{i,d}}$ are the Laurent monomials with exponent vectors $a_i$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
For $z \in \mathbb{K}^{n+1}$ consider the matrix $A_z \coloneq A\cdot\operatorname{diag}(z) \in \mathbb{K}^{(d+1)\times(n+1)}$ and let $i\colon \mathbb{K}^{n+1} \to \mathbb{K}^{(d+1)\times(n+1)}$ be the map linear map $z \mapsto A_z$. The \emph{principal $A$-determinant} is 
\[
    E_A \coloneq i^*\operatorname{Res}(X_A) = i^*r^*\operatorname{Ch}(X_A) \in \mathbb{K}[z_0,\dots,z_n].
\]
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{th: ppalAdet}
The principal A-determinant has degree 
$
\deg(E_A) = (d+1)\mathrm{Vol}(P_A).
$
It factors as
\[
E_A = \prod_{Q \leq P_A} \Delta(X_{A|_Q})^{m_Q}
\]
where $Q$ ranges over the faces of $P_A$, and $m_Q = [P_A:Q]\mathrm{Vol}(Q)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
Let $k \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$, and let $L \subset \mathbb{P}^n$ be a linear space not contained in any coordinate hyperplane. The \emph{$k$-th power map} is 
\[
\phi_k \colon L \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^n, \qquad x \mapsto x^k \coloneq [x_0^k:\dots:x_n^k].
\]
Define $L^k \coloneq \overline{\phi_k(L)}$: we say that $L^{-1}$ is a \emph{reciprocal linear space}.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
\label{def:matroid_det}
Consider again the map $i\colon \mathbb{K}^{n+1} \to \mathbb{K}^{(d+1)\times(n+1)}$, $z \mapsto  A\cdot\text{diag}(z)$. For $k \geq 2$, the \emph{$k$-th generalized matroid determinant} is 
\[
    E_{L,-k} \coloneq i^*\operatorname{Res}(L^{-k}) = i^*r^*\operatorname{Ch}(L^{-k}).
\]
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The behavior of $i^*r^*\operatorname{Ch}(L^{k})$ for $k = -1$ or $k \geq 1$ is very different to that of the principal matroid determinant $E_{L,-k}$, so we decided \emph{not} to include those cases in the definition of the generalized matroid determinant, although the definition still makes sense. We include some results and open questions for this range of $k$ in \Cref{sec:otherMulties}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{th: ppalmatdet}
Let $k \geq 2$.
The $k$-th generalized matroid determinant has degree 
$
\deg(E_{L,-k}) =
              
    (d+1)k^{d-c(M)+1}\mu^+(M).
                               
                                              
            
$
It factors into irreducible factors as
\[
E_{L,-k} =
              
    \prod_{F\text{ conn.\ flat of }M} \Delta(L_F^{-k+1})^{m_F}                           
                                            
            
\]
for some integers $m_F \geq 1$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The claim about the degree follows by exactly the same argument as \cite[Theorem 3.5]{MatsubaraHeoTelen2026}, simply replacing their second powers by $k$-th powers. As mentioned, the factorization follows closely the proof of \cite[Theorem 3.11]{MatsubaraHeoTelen2026}.
Consider the incidence variety
\begin{align*}
Y \coloneq \Set{(x,z)\in \mathbb{P}^n\times (\mathbb{P}^n)^* | A_z\cdot x=0\text{ and }x\in L^{-k}}.
\end{align*}
The proof of \cite[Lemma 3.7]{MatsubaraHeoTelen2026} does not require smoothness on the torus and therefore by exactly the same argument we obtain that the zero locus $E_{L,-k}$ is the image of $Y$ under the second projection $\pi_2\colon \mathbb{P}^n\times(\mathbb{P}^n)^*\to (\mathbb{P}^n)^*$.
On the other hand $Y$ can be stratified according to the decomposition of $L^{-k}$ into flat strata:
\begin{align}
\label{eq: Ystrat}
Y=\bigsqcup_{F\in \mathcal{F}(M)}\underbrace{Y\cap(T_F\times (\mathbb{P}^n)^*)}_{\eqcolon Y_F }
\end{align}
We proceed in three steps to complete the proof:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \label{1} For any flat $F$ we have $(L_F^{-k+1})^\vee\subseteq \overline{\pi_2(Y_F)}$.
\item \label{2} If $F$ is a connected flat of $M$ then equality holds in \ref{1} and $\overline{\pi_2(Y_F)}$ is a hypersurface.
\item \label{3} If $F$ is a disconnected flat and $F'$ is a component of $F$ then $\overline{\pi_2(Y_F)}\subseteq \overline{\pi_2(Y_F')}$.
\end{enumerate}
Once these three claims are proven the result follows: Since by the above $\pi_2(Y)$ is the vanishing locus of $E_{L,-k}$ while by \eqref{eq: Ystrat} we also have
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{V}(E_{L,-k})=\pi_2(Y)=\bigcup_{F\in \mathcal{F}(M)}\overline{\pi_2(Y_F)}\overset{\ref{3}}{=} \bigcup_{F\text{ conn.\ flat of }M}\overline{\pi_2(Y_F)}\overset{\ref{2}}{=}\bigcup_{F\text{ conn.\ flat of }M}(L_F^{-k+1})^\vee.
\end{align*}
This yields all the irreducible factors of $E_{L,-k}$, as the dual hypersurfaces $(L_F^{-k+1})^\vee = V(\Delta(L_F^{-{k+1}}))$ on the right hand side are irreducible. It remains to prove  \ref{1}-\ref{3}.
\begin{enumerate}[wide]
\item Write $\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_n$ for the linear forms on $\mathbb{K}^{d+1}$ whose coefficients in the standard basis are the columns of the matrix $A$, that is, $\ell(x) = x\cdot A$. Then $L_F^{-k+1}$ is rationally parametrized by the map $\ell^{-k+1}_F \colon \mathbb{P}^d \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^n$ whose $i$-th coordinate is $\ell_i^{-k+1}$ if $i\in F$ and $0$ otherwise. Similarly, $L_F^{-k}$ is rationally parametrized by $\ell^{-k}_F$. Assume $t\in \mathbb{P}^d$ is such that $\ell^{-k+1}_F$ is defined at $t$ and $\ell^{-k+1}_F(t)$ is a smooth point of $L_F^{-k+1}$. Notice that in particular $\phi_{-k+1}$ is unramified at $t$, so we can use the parametrization $\ell^{-k+1}_F$ to compute the tangent space to $L_F^{-k+1}$ at $\ell^{-k+1}_F(t)$. The proof of \cite[Lemma 3.6]{MatsubaraHeoTelen2026} then shows that the pair $(\ell^{-k}_F(t),z)$ belongs to $Y_F$ if and only if the hyperplane defined by $z$ contains the tangent space to $L^{-k+1}_F$ at $\ell^{-k+1}_F(t)$. This implies that $(L_F^{-k+1})^\vee\subseteq \overline{\pi_2(Y_F)}$. 
\item Notice that $A_z\cdot x=A_x\cdot z$ and hence the fiber in $Y$ over a fixed point $x\in L^{-k}$ is a linear space $\Lambda_x$. The dimension of $\Lambda_x$ is $n-\operatorname{rk}(A_x)$ and hence it only depends on which coordinates of $x$ vanish, since a non-zero rescaling of columns of $A$ does not alter the rank. More precisely we have $\operatorname{rk}(A_x)=\operatorname{rk}_M(F)$ whenever $x\in T_F$. In particular, $Y_F$ the total space is a vector bundle over $L^{-k}\cap T_F$ and therefore irreducible of dimension 
\[
\dim Y_F = \dim L_F^{-k+1}+\dim \Lambda_x =\operatorname{rk}_M(F)-1+n-\operatorname{rk}_M(F)=n-1.
\]
In particular $\overline{\pi_2(Y_F)}$ is an irreducible variety of dimension at most $n-1$. On the other hand, assuming $F$ is connected, we have that $(L_F^{-k+1})^\vee$ is a hypersurface by \cite[Theorem 4.8]{briand2025}. Hence equality must hold in \ref{1} in that case.
\item Suppose $F$ is disconnected and write $F=F'\sqcup G$ where $F'$ is a connected flat of $M$. By an invertible change of coordinates in $\operatorname{GL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{K})$ we can assume that the columns of $A$ whose index belongs to $F$ are of block shape, i.e.\ $A$ is of the form displayed below where the first columns belong to $F'$, the second set of columns belongs to $G$ and the last set is in $F^c$. This does not change $Y$.
\[
    A=
\begin{bNiceArray}{rrr|rrr|rrr}[margin=4pt]
\Block{2-3}{A_1} & & & \Block{2-3}{0} & & & \Block{2-3}{A_3} & & \\
& & & & & & & & \\
\hline
\Block{2-3}{0} & & & \Block{2-3}{A_2} & & & \Block{2-3}{A_4} & & \\
& & & & & & & & \\
\CodeAfter
  \UnderBrace{4-1}{4-3}{F'}[shorten]
  \UnderBrace{4-4}{4-6}{G}[shorten]
   \UnderBrace{4-7}{4-9}{F^c}[shorten]
\end{bNiceArray}\vspace{3ex}
\]
Now suppose $z\in \pi_2(Y_F)$, then there exists $x\in T_F\cap L^{-k+1}$ such that $A_z\cdot x=0$. But then setting the entries $x_i$ with $i\in G$ to zero we obtain a point $x'\in L^{-k+1}\cap T_{F'}$ which still satisfies $A_z\cdot x'=0$ due to the block shape of $A$. Hence $z\in \pi_2(Y_{F'})$ which finishes the proof. \qedhere
\end{enumerate}

<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
The multiplicity of the $\mathfrak{m}_R$-primary ideal $I \subset R$, denoted by $e(I,R) \in \mathbb{N}_{>0}$, is the normalized leading coefficient of the Hilbert polynomial
\[
\operatorname{HP}_{I,R}(t) = \frac{e(I,R)}{(n-1)!}t^{n-1} + O(t^{n-2}).
\]
Equivalently, writing the Hilbert series $\sum_{i\geq 0} \chi_R^I(i)t^i$ as $\frac{Q(t)}{(1-t)^n}$, then $e(I,R) = Q(1)$.

For any Noetherian ring $R$, $I\subseteq R$ a $\mathfrak{p}$-primary ideal, we set $e(I,R)\coloneq e(IR_\mathfrak{p},R_\mathfrak{p})$.

If $X$ is a variety, or more generally an algebraic $\mathbb{K}$-scheme, and $Z \subseteq X$ a closed irreducible subset, then the \emph{local multiplicity} of $X$ along $Z$ is $\operatorname{mult}_Z(X) \coloneq e(\mathfrak{m}_{X,Z},\mathcal{O}_{X,Z})$ where $\mathfrak{m}_{X,Z}$ denotes the maximal ideal of the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{X,Z}$ associated to $Z$.

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm: ZariskiSamuel}
Let $R$ be a local ring, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_R$ and consider an inclusion of rings $R \hookrightarrow S$ which makes the semi-local ring $S$ a finite $R$-module. Let $J$ be an ideal of $R$ which is $\mathfrak{m}_R$-primary. Let $\mathfrak{m}_{S,1},\dots,\mathfrak{m}_{S,r}$ be the maximal ideals of $S$ and let $I_i$ be the $\mathfrak{m}_{S,i}$-primary component of $JS$. Suppose no nonzero element of $R$ is a zero divisor in $S$ and for all $i=1,\ldots,s$, the local ring $S_{\mathfrak{m}_{S,i}}$ has the same dimension as $R$. 
Then,
\[
[S:R]e(J, R) = \sum_{i=1}^r [S/\mathfrak{m}_{S,i}:R/\mathfrak{m}_R] e(I_i, S).
\]

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem: simplifications}
By \cite[Theorem 4.8]{briand2025}, for $k \geq 2$, $\Delta(L_F^{-k+1})$ is dual-defective if and only if $F$ is not a connected flat, in which case it does not contribute to the factorization of $E_{L,-k}$. Therefore, the product in the factorization of $E_{L,-k}$ in \Cref{thm:intro_main} may be taken either  over connected flats only or over all flats where the disconnected flats simply contribute a factor of 1.
In particular, the component $\Delta(L^{-1})$ appears non-trivially in the factorization if and only if $\operatorname{M}(L)$ is connected. It corresponds to the flat $F = E$, and so it appears with multiplicity
\[
m_E = k^{\operatorname{rk}(M/E)}\mu^+(M/E) = 1
\]
as $M/E$ is the empty matroid.

<!-- example-4: body_md -->
Let $L \subset \mathbb{P}^n$ be a generic linear space of dimension $d$. The matroid associated to $L$ is the uniform matroid of rank $d+1$ on $n+1$ elements $U_{d+1,n+1}$.
The only connected flats of $U_{d+1,n+1}$ are the entire ground set $E$ and the singletons. Therefore,
\[
E_{L,-k} = \Delta(L^{-k+1})^{m_E}\prod_{i \in [n]} \Delta(L_{\{i\}}^{-k+1})^{m_i}.
\]
Now, $L_{\{i\}}^{-k+1} = L_{\{i\}}$ is the $i$-th coordinate point. Its dual variety is the set of hyperplanes containing that point, so $\Delta(L_{\{i\}})=z_i \in \mathbb{K}[z]$.
By \Cref{thm:intro_main}, 
\[
m_i = k^{\operatorname{rk}(M/\{i\})-c(M)+1} \mu^+(M/\{i\}) = k^{d} \mu^+(U_{d+1,n}) = k^{d}\binom{n-1}{d-1}.

    or equivalently the vector $(x_iz_i)_{i\in F}$ belongs to $L_F^\perp=\{y\in \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{K}^F)\mid \forall w\in L_F:\sum_{i\in F}y_iw_i=0\}$. Notice that for a fixed $z\in (\mathbb{P}^n)^*$ with the property that not all $z_i=0$ for $i\in F$, there exists $x\in L^{-1}\cap T_F$ with the above property if and only if $(z_i)_{i\in F}\in (L_F\cap T_F)\star L_F^\perp$. This shows that $\overline{\pi_2(Y_F)}=(L_F\star L_F^\perp)\vee \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{K}^{F^c})$ where $\vee$ denotes the join of two projective varieties.


    
                                                                                                                                                                                                                           

    Now, the stratification of $L^{-1}$ by flats induces the stratification
    \[
    Y \coloneq \bigsqcup_{F \in \mathcal{F}(M)} Y_F \subset \mathbb{P}^n \times (\mathbb{P}^n)^*
    \]
    whose image under the projection $\pi_2:\mathbb{P}^n \times (\mathbb{P}^n)^* \rightarrow (\mathbb{P}^n)^*$ is the vanishing locus of $E_{L,-1}$ by the same reasoning as in \cite[Lemma 3.7]{MatsubaraHeoTelen2026}. The above argument shows that
    \[
    \mathcal{V}(E_{L,-1}) = \pi_2\big(\bigsqcup_{F \in \mathcal{F}(M)} Y_F\big) = \bigcup_{F \in \mathcal{F}(M)}(\overline{\pi_2(Y_F)}) = \bigcup_{F \in \mathcal{F}(M)} (L_F \star L_F^\perp)\vee \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{K}^{F^c}).
    \]
    Now, by \cite[Proposition 4.6]{MatsubaraHeoTelen2026} and \cite[Theorem 2.11]{briand2025}, $\dim(L_F \times L_F^\perp) = |F|-2$ if and only if $F$ is a connected flat, and in that case, it is the hyperplane given by $\sum_{i \in F} z_i = 0$. This completes the argument.

<!-- example-5: body_md -->
For $k=1$, one can deduce \Cref{conj: positivek} from elementary considerations. A direct calculation shows that 
\[
\operatorname{Res}(L)(u) = \det(UA^{\textsf{T}}), \qquad E_{L,1}(z) = \det(A\operatorname{diag}(z) A^{\textsf{T}}) = \sum_{S \in \binom{[n+1]}{d+1}} \det(A_S)^2 z^S,
\]
where the last formula is the Cauchy--Binet formula. This expansion shows that $S$ is in the support of $E_{L,1}$ if and only if $S$ is a basis of the matroid $M=\operatorname{M}(L)$. If $M$ is connected, then such a polynomial must be irreducible, see \cite[Proposition 4.6]{Choe2004}. As $\Delta(L^2) \mid E_{L,1}$, these two polynomials must be equal up to scaling.
