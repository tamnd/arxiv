<!-- thma-1: body_md -->
For every $k\geqslant 0$, restriction to the supersingular locus induces a Hecke-equivariant injective map $W_k\longrightarrow{\mathcal M}_k(\Omega)$.
  If $k>p+1$, this is an isomorphism.
<!-- end thma-1 -->
<!-- thmb-1: body_md -->
For every $k\in\mathbf{Z}$ there are explicit Hecke-equivariant isomorphisms
  \begin{equation*}
    {\mathcal M}_k(\Omega)\cong {\mathcal M}_{k+p^2-1}(\Omega),\quad
    {\mathcal M}_k(\Omega)\cong {\mathcal M}_{pk}(\Omega),\quad
    {\mathcal M}_k(\Omega)[\chi]\cong {\mathcal M}_{k+p+1}(\Omega),
  \end{equation*}
  where $\chi$ denotes the mod $p$ cyclotomic character.
<!-- end thmb-1 -->
<!-- corc-1: body_md -->
Let $(a_{\ell})\in\displaystyle\prod_{\ell\nmid p\delta N} \overline{\mathbf{F}}_p$ be a system of Hecke eigenvalues.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $(a_\ell)$ arises from $M_k$, then it arises from ${\mathcal M}_{k'}(\Omega)$ for some $0\leqslant k'\leqslant k$ such that $k'\equiv k\pmod{p-1}$.
    \item If $(a_\ell)$ arises from ${\mathcal M}_k(\Omega)$, then it arises from $M_{k'}$ for every $k'>p+1$ such that $k'\equiv k,pk\pmod{p^2-1}$.
  \end{enumerate}
  In particular, the systems of Hecke eigenvalues arising from $\displaystyle\bigoplus_k M_k$ and from ${\mathcal M}(\Omega)$ coincide.
<!-- end corc-1 -->
<!-- defn-1: body_md -->
A false elliptic curve over a scheme $S$ (on which $\delta$ is invertible) is an abelian surface $A$ over $S$, together with an injective ring homomorphism $\iota\,\colon\; {\mathcal O}_B \hookrightarrow \mathsf{End}_S(A)$. An isogeny $f\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow A_0$ of false elliptic curves is an isogeny of abelian surfaces over $S$ that commutes with the actions of ${\mathcal O}_B$; in other words we have the commutative diagram
                
                                                                                     
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      A \arrow[r, "f"] \arrow[d, "\iota(b)"'] & A_0 \arrow[d, "\iota_0(b)"] \\
      A \arrow[r, "f"'] & A_0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  for every $b \in {\mathcal O}_B$.
<!-- end defn-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The term ``fausse courbe elliptique'' is attributed to Serre by Deligne and Rapoport \cite[p. 155]{DR}.
  David Kohel points out that translating this as ``fake elliptic curve'' does a better job of capturing its essence.
  We decided to stick with ``false elliptic curve'' as it is employed by all the references we are using.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- defn-2: body_md -->
Define level $V(N)$ structure on a false elliptic curve $A/S$ to be an isomorphism
  \begin{equation*}
    \alpha\,\colon\; ({\mathcal O}_B \otimes \mathbf{Z}/N\mathbf{Z})_S \cong A[N]
  \end{equation*}
  of group schemes over $S$ with left action of ${\mathcal O}_B$.
  Define level $V_1(N)$ structure to be an inclusion
  \begin{equation*}
    \alpha\,\colon\; (\mathbf{Z}/N\mathbf{Z} \times \mathbf{Z}/N\mathbf{Z})_S  \hookrightarrow A[N]
  \end{equation*}
  of group schemes over $S$ with left action of ${\mathcal O}_B$  (defined on $\mathbf{Z}/N\mathbf{Z} \times \mathbf{Z}/N\mathbf{Z}$ by the identification \\ ${\mathcal O}_B \otimes \mathbf{Z}/N\mathbf{Z} \cong \mathsf{M}_2(\mathbf{Z}/N\mathbf{Z})$).
<!-- end defn-2 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
For $N \geqslant4$, the functor from the category of schemes over $\mathbf{Z}[1/N\delta]$ to the category of sets, assigning to any scheme $S$ the set of isomorphism classes of false elliptic curves over $S$ with level $V(N)$ structure, is representable by a smooth proper curve $\mathbf{X}(N)$ over $\mathbf{Z}[1/N\delta]$. This is the Shimura curve of level $V(N)$ (associated to $B$). The same is true replacing $V(N)$ with $V_1(N)$, and we denote the Shimura curve by $\mathbf{X}_1(N)$.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
See \cite{DT1} or \cite{Buz2}.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
In contrast to the theory of modular curves, one does not need to add cusps in the construction of the Shimura curves.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- notn-1: body_md -->
For our fixed choice of nonzero idempotents $e_1, e_2=1-e_1$ in $\mathsf{M}_2(\mathbf{F}_p)$, define for any false elliptic curve $s\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow S$ the line bundle on $S$
  \begin{equation*}
    \underline{\omega}_{A/S} := e_2 \cdot s_* \Omega_{A/S}^1.
  \end{equation*}
  In the case of the universal false elliptic curve $\pi\,\colon\; \mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathbf{X}$, we write simply $\underline{\omega}$ for $\underline{\omega}_{\mathcal{A}/\mathbf{X}}$.
  Note that for a general $A\longrightarrow S$, $\underline{\omega}_{A/S}$ is the pullback under the associated morphism $S \longrightarrow \mathbf{X}$ of $\underline{\omega}$.
<!-- end notn-1 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{KS2}
  We have the Kodaira--Spencer isomorphism $\Omega_{\mathbf{X}}^1 \cong \underline{\omega}^{\otimes 2}$.
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
\cite[Lemma 7]{DT1}.

<!-- defn-3: body_md -->
A modular form with respect to $B^\times$, of weight $k \geqslant 0$ and level $V=V(N),V_1(N)$ over a ring $R_0$ on which $\delta N$ is invertible, is a rule assigning a value $f(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega) \in R$ to each isomorphism class of quadruples $(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega)$, where $R$ is an $R_0$-algebra, $(A,\iota,\alpha)$ is a false elliptic curve over $R$ with level $V$ structure, and $\omega$ is a basis for $\underline{\omega}_{A/R}$, satisfying:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item $f(A/R,\iota,\alpha,\mu\omega) = \mu^{-k}f(A/R,\iota,\alpha,\omega)$ for any $\mu \in R^\times$.
    \item (Base change) If $\phi\,\colon\; R \longrightarrow R'$ is an $R_0$-algebra homomorphism, then $f(A/R',\iota,\alpha ,\omega_{R'}) = \phi(f(A/R,\iota,\alpha,\omega))$.
  \end{enumerate}
  Equivalently, $f$ is a global section of $H^0(\mathbf{X}_{R_0} , \underline{\omega}^{\otimes k})$.
<!-- end defn-3 -->

\label{Hecke def 2}
  For a prime $\ell$ (recall, not dividing $p\delta N$), we define the $\ell$-th Hecke operator $T_\ell$ on $M_k$ by the following formula:
  \begin{equation*}T_\ell f (A,\iota,\alpha,\omega) = \frac{1}{\ell}\cdot \sum\limits_{\parbox{8em}{\scriptsize$\eta\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow A/G$ \\ $G \cong \mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z} \times \mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z}$ \\ as ${\mathcal O}_B \otimes \mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z}$-modules }} f(A/G, \iota, \alpha_\eta, (\eta^t)^*\omega).
  \end{equation*}
                                                                                 
  Here $\eta^t$ was earlier defined as the composition of the dual isogeny $\eta^\vee$ with the principal polarisations, and we set $\alpha_{\eta}=\eta \circ \alpha$, which is a level structure on $A/G$ because $\ell$ is coprime to $N$. Note that $(\eta^t)^* \omega \in \underline{\omega}_{A/G}$ because $\eta^t$ commutes with ${\mathcal O}_B$, and in particular with the idempotents $e_1,e_2$.

<!-- defn-5: body_md -->
Define the {\it Hasse invariant} $\mathbf{H}$, a function on triples $(A,\iota,\omega)$, by setting $\mathbf{H}(A,\iota,\omega) \in R$ determined by
  \begin{equation*}
    F_{\text{abs}}^*(\omega^\vee) = \mathbf{H}(A,\iota,\omega) \omega^\vee \in H^1(A,{\mathcal O}_A).
  \end{equation*}
  Note that $F_{\text{abs}}^*(\omega^\vee) \in e_2 \cdot H^1(A,{\mathcal O}_A)$ because $F_{\text{abs}}$ commutes with ${\mathcal O}_B$.
<!-- end defn-5 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
The Hasse invariant $\mathbf{H}$ is a modular form over $\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p$ with respect to $B^\times$, of weight $p-1$ and level 1.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We compute for $(A,\iota)$ defined over an $\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p$-algebra $R$ and $\mu \in R^\times$,
  \begin{align*}\mathbf{H}(A,\iota,\mu \omega) \cdot \mu^{-1} \omega^\vee & = F_{\text{abs}}^*(\mu^{-1} \omega^\vee)                  \\
                                                                 & = \mu^{-p} F_{\text{abs}}^*(\omega^\vee)                  \\
                                                                 & = \mu^{-p} \mathbf{H}(A,\iota,\omega) \cdot \omega^\vee
  \end{align*}
  so $\mathbf{H}(A,\iota,\mu \omega) = \mu^{1-p}\mathbf{H}(A,\iota,\omega)$.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{Hasse quotient 2}
  Let $\mathbf{H}$ be the Hasse invariant mod $p$ with respect to $B^\times$. Let $f$ be a weight $k$ mod $p$ modular form  with respect to $B^\times$ of level $V$. Then $f$ is a Hecke eigenform if and only if $\mathbf{H} f$ is a Hecke eigenform. In this case, they have the same Hecke eigenvalues.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The Hecke operator $T_\ell$ is defined in terms of quadruples $(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega)$ by the formula
  \begin{equation*}
    T_\ell f (A,\iota,\alpha,\omega) = \frac{1}{\ell}\cdot \sum\limits_{\parbox{8em}{\scriptsize$\eta\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow A/G$ \\ $G \cong \mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z} \times \mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z}$ \\ as ${\mathcal O}_B \otimes \mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z}$-modules }} f(A/G, \iota, \alpha_\eta, (\eta^t)^*\omega)
  \end{equation*}
  and so acts on $\mathbf{H} f$ by
  \begin{equation*}
    T_\ell (\mathbf{H} f) (A,\iota,\alpha,\omega) = \frac{1}{\ell}\cdot \sum\limits_{\parbox{8em}{\scriptsize$\eta\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow A/G$ \\ $G \cong \mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z} \times \mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z}$ \\ as ${\mathcal O}_B \otimes \mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z}$-modules }} f(A/G, \iota, \alpha_\eta, (\eta^t)^*\omega) \cdot \mathbf{H}(A/G, \iota, (\eta^t)^*\omega).
  \end{equation*}
                                                                                                                                          
  We claim that
  \begin{equation}\label{eqn}
    \mathbf{H}(A/G, \iota, (\eta^t)^*\omega)=\mathbf{H}(A,\iota,\omega) \text{ for any isogeny $\eta\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow A/G$ of the form above. }
  \end{equation}
  Given \eqref{eqn}, if $f$ is an eigenvector for $T_\ell$, we see that $\mathbf{H} f$ is an eigenvector for $T_\ell$ with the same eigenvalue. For the converse, if $\mathbf{H} f$ is an eigenvector for $T_\ell$ with eigenvalue $a_\ell$, then for any quadruple $(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega)$ we have, given the claim \eqref{eqn},
  \begin{equation*}
    a_\ell \cdot \mathbf{H}(A,\iota,\omega)f(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega) = T_\ell(\mathbf{H} f)(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega) = \mathbf{H}(A,\iota,\omega) \cdot T_\ell f(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega).
  \end{equation*}
  We deduce that $T_\ell f$ and $a_\ell f$ agree away from the zeros of the Hasse invariant. Since the Hasse invariant is nonzero there can only be finitely many of them, so $T_\ell f$ and $a_\ell f$ agree on a dense subset of the Shimura curve, so must coincide. It remains to prove the claim \eqref{eqn} of the Hasse invariant; we can express it as the requirement that the following diagram commutes
                                                                                                                                    
                                                                                       
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
      e_2 \cdot H^1(A,{\mathcal O}_{A}) \arrow[r, "(\eta^t)^*"] \arrow[d, "F_{\text{abs}}^*"] & e_2 \cdot H^1(A/G,{\mathcal O}_{A/G}) \arrow[d, "F_{\text{abs}}^*"] \\
      e_2 \cdot H^1(A,{\mathcal O}_{A}) \arrow[r, "(\eta^t)^*"] & e_2 \cdot H^1(A/G,{\mathcal O}_{A/G}).
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}

  This follows from the fact that the absolute Frobenius morphisms $F_{\text{abs}}$ commute with any isogeny, including $\eta^t$ (the absolute Frobenius is the identity on points, and the $p$-power map on structure sheaves commutes with morphisms of sheaves).

  \begin{equation*}

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{dihedral}
  For any $\mu\in\mathcal{O}_p^\times$, we have
  \begin{equation*}
    \pi^{-1}\mu\pi\in\mathcal{O}_p^\times\qquad\text{and}\qquad \pi^{-1}\mu\pi\mu^{-p}\in\mathcal{O}_p^\times(\pi).
  \end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
The first statement is clear since $\mathsf{Nm}_p(\pi^{-1}\mu\pi)=\mathsf{Nm}_p(\mu)$.

  For the second, write $\varphi\,\colon\;\mathcal{O}_p^\times\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}_{p^2}^\times$ for the reduction modulo $\pi$.
  We want to show that
  \begin{equation*}
    \varphi(\pi^{-1}\mu\pi)=\varphi(\mu)^p\qquad\text{in }\mathbf{F}_{p^2}.
  \end{equation*}
  In the above basis, we have
  \begin{equation*}
    \mu=a+bi+c\pi+di\pi,\qquad a,b,c,d\in\mathbf{Z}_p,
  \end{equation*}
  therefore
  \begin{equation*}
    \pi^{-1}\mu\pi=a-bi+c\pi-di\pi.
  \end{equation*}
  After reduction modulo $\pi$, we have
  \begin{equation*}
    \varphi(\mu)=a+bi\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi(\pi^{-1}\mu\pi)=a-bi,
  \end{equation*}
  where $a,b\in\mathbf{F}_p$ and $\pm i$ are the two roots of the irreducible quadratic polynomial $x^2-e$.
  Hence $\varphi(\mu)$ and $\varphi(\pi^{-1}\mu\pi)$ are nontrivial Galois conjugates in $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}$, but the Galois conjugation $i\mapsto -i$ is raising to the power $p$ in $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}$.

\label{MM_dihedral}
  The map $f\mapsto \iota_p(\pi)\cdot f$ defines a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism
  \begin{equation*}
    {\mathcal M}_k(\Omega)\longrightarrow {\mathcal M}_{pk}(\Omega).
  \end{equation*}
<!-- end prop-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $f\in {\mathcal M}_k(\Omega)$ and let $g=\iota_p(\pi)\cdot f$.
  For any $\mu\in\mathcal{O}_p^\times$, let $\kappa=\pi^{-1}\mu\pi\mu^{-p}$, then \autoref{dihedral} tells us that $\kappa\in\mathcal{O}_p^\times(\pi)$, so
  \begin{align*}
    g(\iota_p(\mu)x) & =f\big(\iota_p(\pi^{-1}\mu)x\big)                           \\
                     & =f\big(\iota_p(\kappa\mu^p\pi^{-1})x\big)                   \\
                     & =f\big(\iota_p(\kappa)\iota_p(\mu^p)\iota_p(\pi)^{-1}x\big) \\
                     & =[\mu]^{-pk}f\big(\iota_p(\pi)^{-1}x\big)                   \\
                     & =[\mu]^{-pk}g(x),
  \end{align*}
  where we used the $\mathcal{O}_p^\times(\pi)$-invariance of $f$, and the fact that $f$ has weight $k$.

  The linearity of the map is clear.
  For the bijectivity, note first that the map is injective, since it is the restriction of a bijective map ${\mathcal M}(\Omega)\longrightarrow {\mathcal M}(\Omega)$ to the subspace ${\mathcal M}_k(\Omega)$.
  Therefore the composition
  \begin{equation*}
    {\mathcal M}_k(\Omega)\longrightarrow {\mathcal M}_{pk}(\Omega)\longrightarrow {\mathcal M}_{p^2k}(\Omega)={\mathcal M}_k(\Omega)
  \end{equation*}
  is an injective endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space, hence bijective.

  The Hecke equivariance is simply due to the fact that for any prime $\ell\nmid p\delta$, the action of the Hecke operator $T_\ell$ is defined by working entirely in the $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$ component of $\mathbf{A}_f$ via $\iota_\ell$, and the action of $\pi$ is defined by working entirely in the $\mathbf{Q}_p$ component via $\iota_p$, hence these two actions commute.

\label{hecke_prod}
  If $f$ is a locally constant function on $D^\times(\mathbf{A}_f)/D^\times(\mathbf{Q})$, then
  \begin{equation*}
    T_\ell\big(f \psi_D\big) = \psi(\ell)  \psi_D T_\ell(f)
  \end{equation*}
  for all primes $\ell$ coprime to $p\delta N$ and the level of $f$.

  In particular, $\psi_D$ is an eigenfunction of $T_\ell$ for all primes coprime to $p\delta N$:
  \begin{equation*}
    T_\ell \psi_D = \big(1+\ell^{-1}\big)\psi(\ell) \psi_D.
  \end{equation*}

    \mathsf{GL}_2(\mathbf{Z}_\ell)\begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & \ell\end{pmatrix}\mathsf{GL}_2(\mathbf{Z}_\ell)=\bigsqcup_g \mathsf{GL}_2(\mathbf{Z}_\ell)g,
  \end{equation*}

    \psi_D\big(\iota_\ell(g)\big)=\psi^{-1}\left(\theta\big(\iota_\ell(\mathsf{Nm}_\ell(g))\big)\right)=\psi^{-1}\left(\theta(\iota_\ell(\ell))\right)=\psi^{-1}(\ell^{-1})=\psi(\ell).

    (T_\ell (\psi_D f))(x)

<!-- defn-6: body_md -->
We say that a false elliptic curve $(A,\iota)$ over $\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p$ is supersingular if $A$ is isogenous to the square of a supersingular elliptic curve.
<!-- end defn-6 -->
<!-- prop-7: body_md -->
\label{super false prop}
  We have the following properties:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item The isogeny class of any supersingular false elliptic curve consists exactly of the supersingular false elliptic curves.
    \item The endomorphism algebra $\mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q}$ of a supersingular false elliptic curve $(A,\iota)$ is isomorphic to the quaternion algebra $D$.
    \item Every supersingular false elliptic curve $(A,\iota)$ has a canonical $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}$-structure where the $p^2$ Frobenius endomorphism acts by multiplication by $[-p]$. In other words, $(A,\iota)$ is isomorphic (over $\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p$) to (the base change to $\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p$ of) a supersingular false elliptic curve $(A',\iota')$ defined over $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}$ on which the $p^2$ Frobenius endomorphism acts by $[-p]$.
    \item The Hasse invariant $\mathbf{H}$ with respect to $B^\times$ has (simple) zeros exactly at the supersingular false elliptic curves.
  \end{enumerate}
<!-- end prop-7 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item This follows from the analogous fact for supersingular elliptic curves, as well as the fact that being isogenous is an equivalence relation on false elliptic curves.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  
    \item In more generality see \cite[Proposition 5.2]{Mil1}. Suppose $(A,\iota)$ is isogenous to $E^2$ for some supersingular elliptic curve $E$. Then $E$ has endomorphism algebra $D'$, where $D'$ is the unique quaternion algebra over $\mathbf{Q}$ ramified at $\{p,\infty\}$, and so we have $\mathsf{End}(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q} \cong \mathsf{M}_2(D')$. Applying the double centraliser theorem to $B \subset \mathsf{End}(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q}$ and its centraliser $\mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q}$, we deduce that
      \begin{equation*}
        B \otimes_{\mathbf{Q}} (\mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q}) \cong \mathsf{M}_2(D').
      \end{equation*}
      In other words, in the Brauer group $\mathsf{Br}(\mathbf{Q})$ we have $[B] \cdot [\mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q}] = [D']$. Thus $\mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q}$ is the unique definite quaternion algebra over $\mathbf{Q}$ ramified at the primes dividing $p\delta $.
    \item Our argument is based on the proof of \cite[Lemma 3.2.1]{BGJGP1}. Let $E$ be any supersingular elliptic curve with canonical $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}$-structure (in fact by Honda-Tate theory, one can find such an $E$ defined over $\mathbf{F}_p$). Then $E_{\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p}^2$ is a supersingular false elliptic curve under any choice of embedding ${\mathcal O}_B \hookrightarrow \mathsf{M}_2({\mathcal O}_D)$. There exists an isogeny $\phi\,\colon\; E_{\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p}^2 \longrightarrow A$ of supersingular false elliptic curves by part (i). By the proof of \cite[Lemma 9]{DT1} we can assume that $\phi$ has degree coprime to $p$. Then the kernel $K$ of $\phi$ is stable both under the action of ${\mathcal O}_B \subset \mathsf{End}(E_{\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p}^2)$ (by definition of $\phi$ as an isogeny of false elliptic curves) and also under $[-p]$, the square of the Frobenius. Hence $K$ is defined over $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}$ and $(A' = E^2/K,\iota)$ is a false elliptic curve over $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}$ with $A_{\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p}' \cong A$ an isomorphism of supersingular false elliptic curves (we require $\phi$ to be separable for the induced map $A_{\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p}' \longrightarrow A$ to be an isomorphism). The $p^2$ Frobenius acts on $A'$ by $[-p]$.


    \item By definition, the Hasse invariant with respect to $B^\times$ vanishes at a false elliptic curve $(A,\iota)$ if and only if $F_{abs}^*$ is the zero map $e_2 \cdot H^1(A,{\mathcal O}_A) \longrightarrow e_2 \cdot H^1(A,{\mathcal O}_A)$. Since the idempotents $e_1,e_2$ are conjugate, this occurs if and only if $F_{abs}^*$ is zero on $H^1(A,{\mathcal O}_A)$. Now $A$ is isogenous to $E^2$ for some elliptic curve $E$, and since the absolute Frobenius commutes with isogenies, we see that $F_{abs}^*$ is zero on $H^1(A,{\mathcal O}_A)$ if and only if it is zero on $H^1(E^2,{\mathcal O}_{E^2})$. Then this is zero if and only if $E$ is supersingular by the Kunneth formula. The zeros are simple by \cite[Lemma 5.2]{Kas1}.\qedhere
                                                                                           
  \end{enumerate}

<!-- prop-9: body_md -->
The endomorphism ring ${\mathcal O} = \mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A_0)$ of a supersingular false elliptic curve $(A_0,\iota_0)$ is a maximal order in the quaternion algebra $D$.
<!-- end prop-9 -->
<!-- proof-17: body_md -->
We may assume that $(A_0,\iota_0)$ is given by its canonical $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}$-structure. It is equivalent to prove that $\mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A_0) \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell$ is a maximal order in $D \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell$ for all finite primes $\ell$.

  We first consider the case where $\ell \neq p$, in which case \autoref{isog thm false} tells us that $\mathsf{End}(A_0) \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell \cong \mathsf{M}_4(\mathbf{Z}_\ell)$, and we fix this isomorphism.

  It is well known (see for example \cite[Lemma 7.5.4]{Voi1}) that if $C$ is a central simple $F$-algebra of dimension $d$, then $C \otimes C^{\mathrm{op}} \cong \mathsf{End}_{F}(C) \cong \mathsf{M}_d(F)$ via the isomorphism sending $\sum \alpha_i \otimes \beta_i \mapsto (\mu \mapsto \sum\alpha_i\mu\beta_i)$. Under this isomorphism, the transpose on $\mathsf{End}_F(C)$ sending $(\mu \mapsto \sum\alpha_i\mu\beta_i) \mapsto (\mu \mapsto \sum\beta_i\mu\alpha_i)$ induces an isomorphism of algebras $C \otimes 1 \leftrightarrow 1 \otimes C^{\mathrm{op}}$.

  When $\ell \neq p$, we can apply this to the quaternion algebra $B \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell$ over $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$, where $D \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell \cong B \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell$ by definition of the ramification in $D$, and $B \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell \cong (B \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell)^{\mathrm{op}}$ under the involution map (quaternionic conjugate). So we have an isomorphism $(B \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell) \otimes (D \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell) \cong M_4(\mathbf{Q}_\ell)$ such that the transpose on $M_4(\mathbf{Q}_\ell)$ induces an isomorphism $(B \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell) \otimes 1 \cong 1 \otimes (D \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell)$. Because the transpose on $M_4(\mathbf{Q}_\ell)$ stabilises $M_4(\mathbf{Z}_\ell)$, we have an isomorphism
  \begin{equation*}
    \big((B \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell) \otimes 1\big) \bigcap M_4(\mathbf{Z}_\ell)\quad \cong \quad \big(1 \otimes (D \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell)\big) \bigcap M_4(\mathbf{Z}_\ell).
  \end{equation*}
  Consequently, if the left hand side is a maximal order in $B\otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell$ then the right hand side is a maximal order in $D \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell$. Because $(A_0,\iota_0)$ is a false elliptic curve, we have an inclusion ${\mathcal O}_B \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell \subset \mathsf{End}(A_0) \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell \cong \mathsf{M}_4(\mathbf{Z}_\ell)$, so that the left hand side is indeed a maximal order. The right hand side can be identified with
  \begin{equation*}
    \big(\mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A_0) \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell\big) \bigcap \big( \mathsf{End}(A_0) \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell\big)  = {\mathcal O} \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell.
  \end{equation*}
  Hence ${\mathcal O} \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell$ is a maximal order in $D \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell$ for $\ell \neq p$.


  At the prime $\ell = p$, we need to show that ${\mathcal O} \otimes \mathbf{Z}_p$ is the valuation ring of the division algebra $D \otimes \mathbf{Q}_p$. Here the valuation $v_p$ of an endomorphism of $(A_0,\iota_0)$ is defined to be half the $p$-adic valuation of its false degree (so the valuation of $[p]$ is 1) - one sees that this extends the usual $p$-adic valuation on $\mathbf{Q}_p$ to $ D \otimes \mathbf{Q}_p$ and hence is the unique such valuation. It is equivalent to show that ${\mathcal O}_{(p)}$ consists exactly of the rational endomorphisms of $(A_0,\iota_0)$ whose false degree has nonnegative $p$-adic valuation. Indeed, we can write any rational endomorphism of $(A_0,\iota_0)$ as $a \phi$ for $a \in \mathbf{Q}$ and $\phi \in {\mathcal O}$, and moreover assume that $\phi$ is not an integer multiple of an endomorphism of $(A_0,\iota_0)$ (else change $a$ and $\phi$). By \cite[Lemma 9]{DT1}, $\phi$ has positive valuation $v_p(\phi) \geqslant r/2$ if and only if $\phi$ factors through the $r$-th power of the Frobenius morphism. Since the square of the Frobenius morphism is $[-p]$, our assumption on $\phi$ implies that $0 \leqslant v_p(\phi) \leqslant \frac{1}{2}$. Thus $v_p(a\phi) \geqslant 0$ implies $v_p(a) \geqslant -\frac{1}{2}$, and so $a \in \mathbf{Z}_p$. This completes the proof that ${\mathcal O}$ is a maximal order at all primes, and so is a maximal order in $D$.

  \end{equation*}

For $H$ a finite subgroup scheme of $A_0$ stable under ${\mathcal O}_B$, define

  \end{equation*}

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{HI inverse}
  If $H$ is any finite subgroup scheme of $A_0$ stable under ${\mathcal O}_B$, then $H(I(H))=H$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
By the connected-\'{e}tale exact sequence, $H$ factors as $H^0 \times H^{\text{\'{e}t}}$ where $H^0$ is the connected component of the identity and $H^{\text{\'{e}t}}$ is an \'{e}tale finite group scheme. The subgroup scheme $H^0$ can be interpreted as the kernel of a certain power of the absolute Frobenius morphism. Since the Frobenius commutes with ${\mathcal O}_B$, the decomposition is compatible with the action of ${\mathcal O}_B$, and we have $H(I(H)) = H(I(H^0)) \times H(I(H^{\text{\'{e}t}}))$. So we may assume that $H$ is either the kernel of the $r$-th power of the Frobenius for some $r$, or is \'{e}tale. In the first case, the result is obvious as $I(H)$ is the ideal generated by the $r$-th power of the Frobenius. In the case that $H$ is \'{e}tale, if $H$ is properly contained in $H(I(H))$ then the same is true of the base changes to $\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p$, which are constant group schemes. We can identify these group schemes with subgroups of the group $A_0[N]$ where $N$ is the rank of $H$. Note that $N$ is coprime to $p$ because $A_0[p]$ is the kernel of the square of the Frobenius by \autoref{super false prop}. By \autoref{isog thm false}, we have the isomorphism ${\mathcal O}/N{\mathcal O} \cong \mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A_0[N])$. Hence if $H$ is properly contained in $H(I(H))$ as subgroups of $A_0[N]$, then there exists $\alpha \in {\mathcal O}$ annihilating $H$ but not $H(I(H))$. This contradicts the fact that $I(H)=I(H(I(H)))$, coming from applying \autoref{wat1} to the ideal $I=I(H)$.

<!-- thm-7: body_md -->
\label{false deuring}
  Fix a supersingular false elliptic curve $(A_0,\iota_0)$ with respect to $B$ and let ${\mathcal O} = \mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A_0)$, a maximal order in the quaternion algebra $D$. There is a bijection between the set of isomorphism classes of supersingular false elliptic curves and the set of isomorphism classes of invertible left ${\mathcal O}$-ideals (the left class set $\mathsf{Cls}_L{\mathcal O}$). The map is given by sending $(A,\iota)$ to the isomorphism class of the ideal $\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)\theta$, for any isogeny $\theta\,\colon\; (A_0,\iota_0) \longrightarrow (A,\iota)$.
<!-- end thm-7 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
We construct the inverse by sending the isomorphism class of an invertible left ${\mathcal O}$-ideal $I$ to the isomorphism class of the supersingular false elliptic curve $(A_0/H(I),\iota_0)$. By \autoref{wat1} and \autoref{wat2}, this is a well-defined injection. It remains to show that any supersingular false elliptic curve $(A,\iota)$ is isomorphic to $(A_0/H(J),\iota_0)$ for some invertible left ${\mathcal O}$-ideal $J$. By \autoref{super false prop}, for any supersingular $(A,\iota)$, there exists an isogeny $\theta\,\colon\; A_0 \longrightarrow A$, which we may assume is separable after factoring out a power of the Frobenius. Then $(A,\iota)$ is isomorphic to $(A_0/\mathsf{ker}\,\theta,\iota_0)$. By \autoref{HI inverse}, $\mathsf{ker}\,\theta = H(I(\mathsf{ker}\,\theta))$ so we can take $J=I(\mathsf{ker}\,\theta)$. Since $\theta$ is separable, we in fact have $I(\mathsf{ker}\,\theta) = \mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)\theta$, whose isomorphism class as an ${\mathcal O}$-ideal does not depend on $\theta$, so that the bijection we have defined relates $(A,\iota)$ to $J=\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)\theta$.
<!-- end proof-21 -->
<!-- lemma-8: body_md -->
\label{insep}
  Suppose $\phi\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow A_0$ is an isogeny of supersingular false elliptic curves, with ${\mathcal O}= \mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A_0)$, and let $\omega$ be a basis for $\underline{\omega}_{A_0}$. Then $(\phi^t)^*\omega=0$ if and only if $\phi \in \pi \mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)$.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-22: body_md -->
Let $\theta\,\colon\; A_0 \longrightarrow A$ be any separable isogeny. Then $0 \neq \phi\theta \in {\mathcal O} = \mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A_0)$. By definition of $\pi$ as a uniformiser of ${\mathcal O}_p$, we see that $x \in \pi{\mathcal O}$ if and only if the false degree of $x$ as an endomorphism of $A_0$ is divisible by $p$. Now $(\phi^t)^*\omega = 0$ if and only if the false degree of $\phi$ is divisible by $p$, since $A_0$ is supersingular. This occurs if and only if $\phi\theta$ has false degree divisible by $p$ (since $\theta$ has false degree coprime to $p$), and we have seen that this is equivalent to $\phi\theta \in \pi{\mathcal O}$. The statement $\phi\theta \in \pi{\mathcal O}$ implies $\phi \in \pi \mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)$ by precomposing with $\theta^t$, and the converse is immediate.

<!-- lemma-9: body_md -->
\label{crt}[Chinese remainder theorem for ideals of ${\mathcal O}$]
  Let $I$ be an invertible left ${\mathcal O}$-module. The natural map $I/\pi N I \longrightarrow I/\pi I \times I/NI$ is an isomorphism.
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-23: body_md -->
The map is injective because $p$ and $N$ are coprime, and comparing the size of each side, it is equivalent to show that
  \begin{equation*}
    \# ({\mathcal O} /\pi N {\mathcal O}) = \# ({\mathcal O} / \pi {\mathcal O}) \times  \#({\mathcal O} / N{\mathcal O}).
  \end{equation*}
  But this just follows from the fact that for any $a \in {\mathcal O}$, $\# ({\mathcal O} / a {\mathcal O})$ is the norm (not reduced norm) $N_{D/\mathbf{Q}}(a)$ (\cite[Lemma 9.6.3]{Voi1}) and that the norm is multiplicative.

<!-- thm-8: body_md -->
\label{enrich false deuring}
  Fix a supersingular false elliptic quadruple $(A_0,\iota_0,\alpha_0,\omega_0)$ of level $V$. For any supersingular false elliptic quadruple $(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega)$, there exists an isogeny $\phi\,\colon\; (A,\iota) \longrightarrow (A_0,\iota_0)$ such that $(\phi^t)^*\omega = \omega_0$ and $\alpha_\phi = \alpha_0$. Let ${\mathcal O} = \mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A_0)$, a maximal order in $D$, so that $\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)$ is an invertible left ${\mathcal O}$-module. We have the following uniqueness results:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Over all such isogenies $\phi$, the reduction of $\phi \mod \pi \mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)$ is unique.
    \item For $V=V(N)$, the reduction of $\phi \mod N\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)$ is unique.
    \item For $V=V_1(N)$, for some isomorphism $({\mathcal O}/N{\mathcal O})^\times \cong \mathsf{GL}_2(\mathbf{Z}/N \mathbf{Z})$, the reduction of $\phi \mod N\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)$ is unique up to left multiplication by the subgroup of $\mathsf{GL}_2(\mathbf{Z}/N\mathbf{Z})$ consisting of matrices of the form $\begin{psmallmatrix} 1&* \\0&*\end{psmallmatrix}$.
  \end{itemize}
<!-- end thm-8 -->
<!-- proof-24: body_md -->
By \autoref{crt}, if we can construct isogenies $\phi_\pi, \phi_N\,\colon\; (A,\iota) \longrightarrow (A_0,\iota_0)$ such that
  \begin{equation*}
    (\phi_\pi^t)^*\omega = \omega_0\quad\text{and}\quad \alpha_{\phi_N} = \alpha_0,
  \end{equation*}
  then there exists an isogeny $\phi\,\colon\; (A,\iota) \longrightarrow (A_0,\iota_0)$ congruent to $\phi_\pi \mod \pi \mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)$, and congruent to $\phi_N \mod N\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)$, which then satisfies the desired properties.

  We begin with the construction of $\phi_\pi$. Start with any separable isogeny $\psi\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow A_0$. Then $(\psi^t)^* \omega$ is a basis for $\underline{\omega}_{A_0}$, rational over $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}$ (since all isogenies between supersingular false elliptic curves with canonical structure are defined over $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}$). By composing with an endomorphism of $A_0$, it suffices to prove that ${\mathcal O}$ acts transitively by pullback on the set of nonzero elements of $\underline{\omega}_{A_0}$ rational over $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}$ (which can be identified with $\mathbf{F}_{p^2}^\times$ by picking a basis). <!-- thm-9: body_md -->
\label{main thm 2}
  For $V = V(N),V_1(N)$, let $\Omega=\Omega^D(N),\Omega^D_1(N)$, respectively.
  There exists a natural bijection between the set of isomorphism classes of supersingular false elliptic quadruples of level $V$, and $\Omega$.
<!-- end thm-9 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
Fix a supersingular false elliptic quadruple $(A_0,\iota_0,\alpha_0,\omega_0)$ of level $V$ and let ${\mathcal O}=\mathsf{End}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A_0)$. For any supersingular false elliptic quadruple $(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega)$, fix some separable isogeny $\theta\,\colon\; A_0 \longrightarrow A$. Let $\phi\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow A_0$ be an isogeny as in the statement of \autoref{enrich false deuring}. Then $I=\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)\theta$ is an invertible left ${\mathcal O}$-ideal containing the element $\phi\theta \in I$, whose reduction modulo $\pi N$ is a basis for $I/\pi N I$ as an ${\mathcal O}/\pi N {\mathcal O}$-module. Since $I$ is invertible, it is locally principal (\cite[Theorem 16.6.1]{Voi1}), and we can pick local generators $x_\ell \in D^\times_\ell$ so that $I \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell = {\mathcal O}_\ell x_\ell$ for all $\ell$, and also $\phi\theta \equiv x_\ell \mod \pi N (I \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell)$ for all $\ell$ (note that almost all of these congruences are vacuous). The class $[x_\ell]$ of $(x_\ell)$ in $\Omega$ depends only on the isomorphism class of the supersingular false elliptic quadruple $(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega)$ by the uniqueness properties of $\phi$ in \autoref{enrich false deuring}. So we have a well defined map between the set of supersingular quadruples of level $V$ and the set $\Omega$.

  We construct an inverse to this map. For any $[x_\ell] \in \Omega$, the $(x_\ell) \in D^\times (\mathbf{A}_f)$ are local generators for an invertible left fractional ${\mathcal O}$-ideal $J$. We can pick a representative $I$ of the left class set of $J$ that is an ${\mathcal O}$-ideal of reduced norm coprime to $pN$. Replacing $J$ by $I$ corresponds to multiplication of $(x_\ell)$ on the right by an element of $D^\times(\mathbf{Q})$. So assume that $(x_\ell)$ are local generators for $I$. There also exists some $\psi \in I$ congruent to each $x_\ell$ mod $\pi N I_\ell$ by \autoref{crt}. From \autoref{false deuring}, we have the supersingular false elliptic curve $A = A_0/H(I)$ and a separable isogeny $\theta\,\colon\; A_0 \longrightarrow A$ of false degree $\mathsf{Nm}(I)$ (\autoref{wat1}) coprime to $pN$, such that $\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)\theta = I$. Hence we can express $\psi\in I$ as $\phi\theta$ for some isogeny $\phi\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow A_0$. The desired inverse map is given by sending $[x_\ell] \in \Omega$ to the isomorphism class of the supersingular false elliptic quadruple $(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega)$, for $\alpha, \omega$ some differential and level structure satisfying $(\phi^t)^*\omega = \omega_0$ and $(\alpha)_\phi = \alpha_0$. Such $\alpha$ and $\omega$ exist: we can take $\omega = \frac{1}{\mathrm{falsedeg}\phi} \phi^* \omega_0 \neq 0$ and $\alpha = (\alpha_0)_{\phi^{-1}}$, where $\phi^{-1}$ denotes an inverse to $\phi$ mod $N$ ($\mathrm{falsedeg}(\phi)$ is coprime to $N$ since $\psi$ is a basis for $I/\pi N I$ and the reduced norm of $I$ is coprime to $pN$; consequently $\phi$ induces an isomorphism $A[N] \cong A_0[N]$). This proves the theorem.

<!-- thm-10: body_md -->
\label{thm:hecke_equiv}
  The bijection $Q_k \cong {\mathcal M}_k(\Omega)$ from \autoref{weight action} is Hecke-equivariant.
<!-- end thm-10 -->
<!-- proof-27: body_md -->
We show equivariance for an arbitrary Hecke operator $T_{\ell_0}$.

  We recall how the bijection of \autoref{weight action} and \autoref{main thm 2} was constructed. Fix a supersingular false elliptic quadruple $(A_0,\iota_0,\alpha_0,\omega_0)$ of level $V$, and consider another quadruple $(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega)$. Fix an isogeny $\theta\,\colon\; (A_0,\iota_0) \longrightarrow (A,\iota)$. Then the image $[x_\ell] \in \Omega$ of $(A,\iota,\alpha,\omega)$ is determined by the conditions:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item $\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0) \theta \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell = {\mathcal O}_\ell x_\ell$.
    \item If $\phi \in \mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)$ satisfies $(\phi^t)^* \omega = \omega_0$ and $\alpha_\phi = \alpha_0$, then for all $\ell$,
      \begin{equation*}
        \phi \theta \equiv x_\ell \mod \pi N {\mathcal O}_\ell x_\ell.
      \end{equation*}
  \end{enumerate}
  Let $\eta\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow A/G$ be an isogeny with $G \cong \mathbf{Z}/\ell_0\mathbf{Z} \times \mathbf{Z}/\ell_0\mathbf{Z}$ as $\mathcal{O}_B \otimes \mathbf{Z}/\ell_0\mathbf{Z}$-modules. We want to determine the image of $(A/G,\iota,\alpha_\eta, (\eta^t)^*\omega)$ in $\Omega$.
                                                                                                                       
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      A/G \arrow[rr, shift left=1ex, "\eta^t"] && A \arrow[ll, shift left=1ex, "\eta"] \arrow[rr, shift left=1ex, "\phi"] && A_0 \arrow[ll, shift left=1ex, "\theta"]
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  Now $\phi \eta^t \in \mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A/G,A_0)$ satisfies $((\phi \eta^t)^t)^* (\eta^t)^* \omega = \ell_0 \omega_0$ and $\alpha_{\phi \eta^t \eta} = \ell_0 \alpha_0$. Let $m$ be an integer that is an inverse of $\ell_0$ mod $p N$. Then $\phi_G := m \phi \eta^t \in \mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A/G,A_0)$ satisfies $(\phi_G^t)^* (\eta^t)^*\omega = \omega_0$ and $\alpha_{\phi_G \eta} = \alpha_0$. Hence the image $[y_\ell] \in \Omega$ of $(A/G,\iota,\alpha_\eta, (\eta^t)^*\omega)$ is determined by the conditions:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item $\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A/G,A_0)\eta \theta \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell = {\mathcal O}_\ell y_\ell$.
    \item $\phi_G \eta\theta \equiv \phi\theta \equiv y_\ell \mod \pi N {\mathcal O}_\ell y_\ell.$
  \end{enumerate}
  Because $\eta$ has false degree $\ell_0$, we see that for any $\ell \neq \ell_0$,
  \begin{equation*}
    \mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A/G,A_0)\eta\theta \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell = \mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)\theta \otimes \mathbf{Z}_\ell.
  \end{equation*}
  Consequently, we can take $y_\ell$ to be $x_\ell$ for all $\ell \neq \ell_0$. At the $\ell_0$ place we only need to consider condition (i) since $\ell_0 \nmid pN$.

  Fixing a basis for $\mathsf{Ta}_{\ell_0}(A)$, for each $\eta\,\colon\; A \longrightarrow A/G$ we can find a basis for $\mathsf{Ta}_{\ell_0}(A/G)$ such that the linear map $\mathsf{Ta}_{\ell_0}(A) \cong \mathsf{M}_2(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell_0}) \longrightarrow \mathsf{Ta}_{\ell_0}(A/G) \cong \mathsf{M}_2(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell_0})$ induced by $\eta$ is given by right multiplication by a matrix $g_G^T$ of the form $\begin{psmallmatrix}1&0\\i&\ell_0\end{psmallmatrix}$ or $\begin{psmallmatrix} \ell_0 &0 \\0 &1 \end{psmallmatrix}$. Each matrix of this form occurs exactly once as we range over all $\eta$ so the $g_G$ are the coset representatives of $\mathsf{GL}_2(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell_0}) \backslash \mathsf{GL}_2(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell_0}) \begin{psmallmatrix} 1&0 \\0 & \ell_0 \end{psmallmatrix} \mathsf{GL}_2(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell_0})$. Recall that we identify $\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0) \otimes \mathbf{Z}_{\ell_0} \cong \mathsf{M}_2(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell_0})$ by taking the transpose of the matrices acting by right multiplication. Hence, with respect to our choice of basis for the Tate module $\mathsf{Ta}_{\ell_0}(A)$, we see that (for any basis of $\mathsf{Ta}_{\ell_0}(A/G)$)
  \begin{equation*}
    \mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A/G,A)\eta \otimes \mathbf{Z}_{\ell_0} = \mathsf{M}_2(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell_0}) g_G.
  \end{equation*}


  Since $x_{\ell_0}$ was defined by $\mathsf{Hom}_{{\mathcal O}_B}(A,A_0)\theta \otimes \mathbf{Z}_{\ell_0} = {\mathcal O}_{\ell_0}x_{\ell_0}$, we see that $y_{\ell_0}$ can be given by $g_G \cdot x_{\ell_0}$ for $g_G$ corresponding to $G$. This proves the theorem.

<!-- prop-10: body_md -->
\label{arise quotient 2}
  The systems of Hecke eigenvalues arising from the $M_k$ as $k$ ranges over the nonnegative integers, are the same as those arising from all the $W_k$.
<!-- end prop-10 -->
<!-- proof-28: body_md -->
If $f \in M_k$ is a Hecke eigenform with eigenvalues $(a_\ell)$, then the image $\bar{f}$ of $f$ in the quotient $W_k$ also satisfies $T_\ell(\bar{f}) = a_\ell \bar{f}$ for all $\ell \nmid p\delta N$. To show that the system of eigenvalues $(a_\ell)$ arises from one of the quotients $W_{k'}$, we need to show that there exists a Hecke eigenform $g \in M_{k'}$, also with eigenvalues $(a_\ell)$, such that $\bar{g} \in W_{k'}$ is nonzero. Let $r \geqslant 0$ be the largest degree to which $f$ vanishes at all the supersingular points on the Shimura curve $\mathbf{X}$. Then $f$ factors as $f= \mathbf{H}^r \cdot g$ for some $g \in M_{k-r(p-1)}$, where now $g$ is not a multiple of the Hasse invariant, so has nonzero image in $W_{k-r(p-1)}$. By \autoref{Hasse quotient 2}, $g$ is a Hecke eigenform with the same Hecke eigenvalues $(a_\ell)$. This implies that any system of Hecke eigenvalues $(a_\ell)$ arising from an $M_k$ also arises from some $W_{k'}$, where moreover we can choose $k' \equiv k \mod p-1$.

  Conversely, if $(a_\ell)$ is a system of Hecke eigenvalues arising from a quotient $W_k$, then \cite[Proposition 1.2.2]{AS1} tells us (since we are working over the algebraically closed field $\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p$) that $(a_\ell)$ also arises from $M_k$. We remark that while the system of eigenvalues `lifts' from $W_k$ to $M_k$, the eigenvectors need not all lift.

\label{Heckesuper}
  The systems of Hecke eigenvalues arising from the $M_k$ as $k$ ranges over the nonnegative integers, are the same as those arising from all the $Q_k$.

Suppose $(a_\ell)$ arises from some $M_k$. By \autoref{arise quotient 2}, $(a_\ell)$ arises from some $W_{k'}$. Conversely, if $(a_\ell)$ arises from some $Q_k$, we can replace $k$ by $k+(p^2-1)$ by the previous lemma to assume that $k > p+1$.
