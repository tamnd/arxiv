<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:energy_dissipation} The minimizing-movements scheme satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(\phi^{n+1}) + \frac{1}{2\tau}\left\| \phi^{n+1} - \phi^n \right\|^2 \leq \mathcal{F}(\phi^n).
\end{equation}
In particular, $\mathcal{F}(\phi^{n+1}) \leq \mathcal{F}(\phi^n)$ for all $n \geq 0$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By definition, $\phi^{n+1}$ minimizes the functional $\Phi_\tau^n$ of \eqref{eq:minimizing_movement}. Since $\Phi_\tau^n(\phi)=\mathcal{F}(\phi)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\| \phi-\phi^n \right\|^2$, evaluating at $\phi=\phi^n$ as a competitor gives
\[
\mathcal{F}(\phi^{n+1}) + \frac{1}{2\tau}\left\| \phi^{n+1} - \phi^n \right\|^2 = \Phi_\tau^n(\phi^{n+1}) \leq \Phi_\tau^n(\phi^n) = \mathcal{F}(\phi^n),
\]
which is the stated inequality. Discarding the nonnegative metric term yields $\mathcal{F}(\phi^{n+1})\leq\mathcal{F}(\phi^n)$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:first_order}
Let $\mathcal{X}_h\simeq\mathbb{R}^N$ be equipped with a discrete inner product, and let $\mathcal{F}_h\in C^1(\mathcal{X}_h)$ be the corresponding discrete energy. Let $\phi_h\in C^2([0,T];\mathcal{X}_h)$ solve
\[
\partial_t\phi_h(t)=-\nabla\mathcal{F}_h(\phi_h(t)).
\]
Assume that $\nabla\mathcal{F}_h$ is Lipschitz continuous on a set $B_h\subset\mathcal{X}_h$ that contains the exact trajectory $\{\phi_h(t):0\le t\le T\}$ and the discrete iterates $\{\phi_h^n\}$,
\[
\left\| \nabla\mathcal{F}_h(u)-\nabla\mathcal{F}_h(w) \right\| \le L_h\,\left\| u-w \right\|
\qquad \text{for all } u,w\in B_h.
\]
Let $\{\phi_h^n\}$ be generated from $\phi_h^0=\phi_h(0)$ by the discrete minimizing-movements step
\[
\phi_h^{n+1}\in
\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\phi\in\mathcal{X}_h}
\left\{
\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\| \phi-\phi_h^n \right\|^2+\mathcal{F}_h(\phi)
\right\},
\]
assuming that a minimizer exists at every step. Then, for every $\tau>0$ satisfying $\tau L_h\le 1/2$,
\begin{equation}
\max_{0\le n\le \lfloor T/\tau\rfloor}\,
\left\| \phi_h(t_n)-\phi_h^n \right\|
\le
\frac{M_{2,h}\,T\,e^{2L_hT}}{2}\,\tau,
\qquad
M_{2,h}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\| \partial_{tt}\phi_h(t) \right\| .
\label{eq:first-order-bound}
\end{equation}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The Euler--Lagrange equation of the discrete step,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\phi_h^{n+1}-\phi_h^n}{\tau}
+\nabla\mathcal{F}_h(\phi_h^{n+1})=0,
\label{eq:euler-lagrange}
\end{equation}
identifies the scheme as implicit Euler.

Since $\phi_h^{n+1}$ minimizes the differentiable discrete functional over $\mathcal{X}_h$, it is a critical point, and the Euler--Lagrange equation \eqref{eq:euler-lagrange} holds exactly.

A Taylor expansion with integral remainder about $t_{n+1}$ gives
\[
\phi_h(t_n)=\phi_h(t_{n+1})-\tau\,\partial_t\phi_h(t_{n+1})+R_h^n,
\qquad
\left\| R_h^n \right\|\le\frac{\tau^2}{2}\,M_{2,h},
\]
and substituting $\partial_t\phi_h(t_{n+1})=-\nabla\mathcal{F}_h(\phi_h(t_{n+1}))$ yields
\[
\phi_h(t_{n+1})
=
\phi_h(t_n)-\tau\,\nabla\mathcal{F}_h(\phi_h(t_{n+1}))-R_h^n .
\]

Set $\xi^n:=\phi_h(t_n)-\phi_h^n$. Subtracting \eqref{eq:euler-lagrange} from the identity above gives
\[
\xi^{n+1}
=
\xi^n
-\tau\Bigl(\nabla\mathcal{F}_h(\phi_h(t_{n+1}))
-\nabla\mathcal{F}_h(\phi_h^{n+1})\Bigr)
-R_h^n .
\]
The Lipschitz bound on $B_h$ then gives
\[
\left\| \xi^{n+1} \right\|
\le
\left\| \xi^n \right\|+\tau L_h\left\| \xi^{n+1} \right\|
+\frac{\tau^2}{2}M_{2,h}.
\]
For $\tau L_h\le 1/2$, we have $(1-\tau L_h)^{-1}\le 1+2\tau L_h\le e^{2\tau L_h}$, hence
\[
\left\| \xi^{n+1} \right\|
\le
e^{2\tau L_h}
\Bigl(\left\| \xi^n \right\|+\frac{\tau^2}{2}M_{2,h}\Bigr).
\]
Since $\xi^0=0$, iterating gives, for $n\tau\le T$,
\[
\left\| \xi^n \right\|
\le
\frac{\tau^2}{2}M_{2,h}\sum_{k=1}^{n} e^{2k\tau L_h}
\le
\frac{\tau^2}{2}M_{2,h}\,n\,e^{2L_hT}
\le
\frac{M_{2,h}\,T\,e^{2L_hT}}{2}\,\tau,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:first-order-bound}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:total-error}
Theorem~\ref{thm:first_order} controls the temporal error of exact minimizing-movements iterates for a fixed spatial discretization. To distinguish the other contributions, let $R_h\phi(t_n)$ denote the exact PDE solution sampled on the comparison grid, let $\phi_h(t_n)$ be the exact solution of a compatible semidiscrete gradient system, let $\phi_h^n$ be its exact time-discrete iterate, and let $\phi_{\theta,h}^{\,n}$ be the sampled network iterate. The triangle inequality gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\left\| R_h\phi(t_n)-\phi_{\theta,h}^{\,n} \right\|_h
\le{}\underbrace{\left\| R_h\phi(t_n)-\phi_h(t_n) \right\|_h}_{\text{spatial discretization}}
+\underbrace{\left\| \phi_h(t_n)-\phi_h^n \right\|_h}_{\text{temporal discretization}}
+\underbrace{\left\| \phi_h^n-\phi_{\theta,h}^{\,n} \right\|_h}_{\text{network approximation}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:total-error}
\end{equation}
Here $\left\| \cdot \right\|_h$ denotes the discrete $L^2$ comparison norm. At fixed spatial resolution, equivalence of finite-dimensional norms transfers the first-order estimate in the flow metric to this comparison norm, so Theorem~\ref{thm:first_order} bounds the middle term for the first-order scheme.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:equiv-coordinate}
Fix \(s>0\) and \(c\in\mathbb{R}^d\), and let the first layer of \(\mathrm{NN}_\theta\) be an affine map \(z\mapsto Wz+b\) with unconstrained weights \(W\) and bias \(b\). Then the trial classes
\[
\mathcal{A}
=\bigl\{x\mapsto \mathrm{NN}_\theta(x)\bigr\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathcal{A}_{s,c}
=\Bigl\{x\mapsto \mathrm{NN}_\theta\bigl(\tfrac{x-c}{s}\bigr)\Bigr\}
\]
contain the same functions.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Given an unscaled first layer \(W_0x+b_0\), the scaled first layer with \(W_s=sW_0\) and \(b_s=b_0+W_0c\) satisfies \(W_s\frac{x-c}{s}+b_s=W_0x+b_0\). Conversely, a scaled first layer \(W_s\frac{x-c}{s}+b_s\) equals the unscaled layer with \(W_0=W_s/s\) and \(b_0=b_s-W_sc/s\). All subsequent layers are shared.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:k_stage}
Starting from $U_0=\phi^n$, a $k$-stage scheme computes intermediate states $U_1,\ldots,U_k$ via the variational problems
\begin{equation}
U_m
\in
\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\phi\in\mathcal{X}}
\left(
\mathcal{F}(\phi)
+
\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}
\frac{\gamma_{m,i}}{2\tau}
\left\| \phi-U_i \right\|^2
\right),
\qquad m=1,\ldots,k,
\label{eq:k_stage_problem}
\end{equation}
and the updated solution is taken as $\phi^{n+1}=U_k$. The lower-triangular matrix $\gamma=(\gamma_{m,i})$ collects the scheme coefficients. The column index is zero-based, so row $m$ of a displayed matrix lists $\gamma_{m,0},\ldots,\gamma_{m,m-1}$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:multistage_stability}
Given the coefficients $\gamma_{m,i}$ of Definition~\ref{def:k_stage}, define auxiliary quantities by the backward recursion (from $m=k$ down to $m=1$)
\begin{equation}
\tilde\gamma_{m,i} = \gamma_{m,i} - \sum_{j=m+1}^{k} \tilde\gamma_{j,i}\,\frac{\tilde S_{j,m}}{\tilde S_{j,j}},
\qquad
\tilde S_{j,m} = \sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tilde\gamma_{j,i},
\label{eq:stability-aux}
\end{equation}
where the sum over $j$ is understood to be empty for $m=k$. If
\begin{equation}
\tilde S_{m,m} > 0 \qquad \text{for } m=1,\ldots,k,
\label{eq:stability-cond}
\end{equation}
then the $k$-stage scheme is unconditionally energy-stable. For every $\tau>0$ and every $n\ge 0$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(\phi^{n+1}) \le \mathcal{F}(\phi^n),
\end{equation}
provided each stage minimization in \eqref{eq:k_stage_problem} is solved exactly.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:high_order}
Let $U_0=\phi^n$, let $\phi$ denote the exact solution of the gradient flow with $\phi(t_n)=U_0$, and assume that $\phi$ and the energy $\mathcal{F}$ are sufficiently smooth and that each stage minimization in Definition~\ref{def:k_stage} is solved exactly along a smooth, locally unique stage branch. If the coefficient matrix $\gamma=(\gamma_{m, i})$ satisfies the stability condition \eqref{eq:stability-cond} and the order conditions above through second order, then the one-step error satisfies $\left\| U_k-\phi(t_n+\tau) \right\|=O(\tau^{3})$ with $k=3$ stages. If $\gamma$ satisfies the order conditions through third order, then $\left\| U_k-\phi(t_n+\tau) \right\|=O(\tau^{4})$ with $k=6$ stages. The corresponding exact-stage schemes therefore have formal temporal orders of 2 and 3, respectively.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:stability-network}
Theorem~\ref{thm:multistage_stability} controls the energy only at full time steps; intermediate stages need not decrease it monotonically. In the sampled network implementation, define
\[
\mathcal G_h(U):=\mathcal{F}_h(U)+P_{\mathrm{BC},h}(U),\qquad
J_{m,h}(U):=\mathcal G_h(U)+\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{\gamma_{m,i}}{2\tau}\left\| U-U_i \right\|_{\mathcal{X},h}^2,
\]
where all states are mean-projected in the Cahn--Hilliard cases. Provided the same admissible trial class, quadrature, metric, and boundary penalty are used at every stage, the preceding stage is an admissible competitor. If the computed stages satisfy
\[
J_{m,h}(U_m)\le J_{m,h}(U_{m-1}),\qquad m=1,\ldots,k,
\]
the algebraic argument of~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{Zaitzeff2020} gives $\mathcal G_h(U_k)\le\mathcal G_h(U_0)$ without requiring global minimization. For the single-stage scheme, the required check is
\[
\mathcal G_h(U_1)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\| U_1-U_0 \right\|_{\mathcal{X},h}^2\le\mathcal G_h(U_0).
\]
When the boundary penalty is absent or vanishes identically, $\mathcal G_h = \ E_h$.
