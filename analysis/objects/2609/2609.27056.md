\label{thm-lex}
\label{universal-point}
    Let $\mathfrak{Lex}_{0}$ be the 2-category of  finitely complete categories admitting an initial object, left-exact functors between such categories and natural transformations between such functors. Let $\mathfrak{Lex}_{\ast}$ be the full 2-subcategory of $\mathfrak{Lex}_{0}$ generated by  pointed finitely complete categories.

    The assignment
    \[
    \mathfrak{Lex}_{0}\ni\mathscr{A}\longmapsto(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0_{\mathscr{A}}})\in\mathfrak{Lex}_{\ast}
    \]
    can be extended to a pseudofunctor
    \[
    \textnormal{S}\colon\mathfrak{Lex}_{0}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{Lex}_{\ast}.
    \]
    This pseudofunctor is left bi-adjoint to the inclusion $\textnormal U\colon\mathfrak{Lex}_{\ast}\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement = \&, cramped, sep=1.05em]
      {}\arrow[r, shook] \&{}
  \end{tikzcd}\mathfrak{Lex}_{0}$. The component along $\mathscr{A}\in\mathfrak{Lex}_{0}$ of the unit of this adjunction is given by the pullback functor \(p^\ast\colon\mathscr{A}\to(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})\), with $p\colon 0_\mathscr{A}\to1_\mathscr{A}$.

<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:propointed}
     Let $\mathscr{A}$ be a finitely complete category. We say that $\mathscr{A}$ is \emph{pro\-point\-ed{}} if $\mathscr{A}$ admits an initial object and the unique morphism $0\to1$ in $\mathscr{A}$ is a universal extremal epimorphism (that is, a pullback-stable extremal epimorphism).
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:propointed}
     Let $\mathscr{A}$ be a finitely complete category. The following are equivalent.
     \begin{enumerate}[(1)]
         \item \label{propointed-1}$\mathscr{A}$ is pro\-point\-ed{};
         \item\label{propointed-2} there exists a conservative right-adoint functor $U\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}$ with $\mathscr{X}$ pointed and finitely complete;
        \item\label{propointed-3} $\mathscr{A}$ admits an initial object and there exists a conservative left-exact functor $U\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}$ with $\mathscr{X}$ pointed and finitely complete.
        \end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The implication \ref{propointed-1}$\implies$\ref{propointed-2} is essentially an application of descent theory. If $p\colon 0\to 1$ is a universal extremal epimorphism in $\mathscr{A}$, take $U=p^\ast\colon(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{1})\to(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0}) $, and recall that $(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{1})\simeq \mathscr{A}$ and that $(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})$ is pointed. Moreover, recall that $p^\ast$ admits a left adjoint $p_!$. Since $p\colon 0\to 1$ is a universal extremal epimorphism, $p^\ast$ is conservative (see \cite[Proposition in 1.3]{JANELIDZE-THOLEN-ALG}; see also \cite{FACETS-I}). 
    
     The implication \ref{propointed-2}$\implies$\ref{propointed-3} is immediate. Indeed any right-adoint functor is left-exact and, if $F\colon \mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{A}$ is a left adjoint to $U$, then $F(0_{\mathscr{X}})$ is an initial object in $\mathscr{A}$. 
     
     Finally, assume \ref{propointed-3}. Fix a functor $U\colon \mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}$ as in \ref{propointed-3} and let $p$ denote the unique morphism from $0$ to $1$ in $\mathscr{A}$. Since $U$ is left-exact and $\mathscr{X}$ is pointed, Theorem~\ref{universal-point} yields a (left-exact) functor $V\colon (\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})\to \mathscr{X}$ such that the following diagram commutes up to isomorphism.
     \begin{equation}
     \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=5em]
         \mathscr{A}\arrow[d, "U"']\arrow[r, "p^\ast"]&(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})\arrow[dl, "V", bend left]
         \\
         \mathscr{X}
     \end{tikzcd}
     \end{equation}
Since $U$ is conservative, it follows that $p^\ast$ is conservative as well. Hence, by \cite[Proposition in 1.3]{JANELIDZE-THOLEN-ALG}, $p$ is a universal extremal epimorphism, proving \ref{propointed-1}.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
The prefix \emph{pro-} in the term \emph{propointed} is to be understood in the sense of `in place of'. Accordingly, a pro\-point\-ed{} category is not itself pointed; rather, it carries additional structure that, as we shall see, allows many constructions usually carried out in the pointed setting to be developed in this more general context.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
A given pro\-point\-ed{} category may admit several `forgetful functors' as in Theorem~\ref{thm:propointed}, each of which may be useful in different situations. Nevertheless, every pro\-point\-ed{} category is always equipped with the canonical pullback functor $p^\ast\colon \mathscr{A}\to(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})$
associated with the unique morphism $p\colon0\to1$, which satisfies the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:propointed}.\ref{propointed-2}.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{faithfulness}
    Every functor satisfying the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:propointed}.\ref{prot-propointed-3} is faithful. Indeed, this follows immediately from left exactness and  conservativity.
<!-- end remark-3 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{rmk:quasi-pointed}
     Pro\-point\-ed{}ness may be viewed as complementary to the notion of quasi-pointedness in the sense of \cite{BOURN-QUASI-POINTED}, where the morphism $0\to1$ is required to be a monomorphism. Indeed, a category is pointed if and only if it is both quasi-pointed and pro\-point\-ed{}.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
Condition~\ref{prot-propointed-3}  of Theorem~\ref{thm:propointed} is closely related to the notion of a \emph{basic setting} introduced in \cite{RELATIVE-IDEALS} when the target category is homological. We shall return to this in Section~\ref{sec:prohom}.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
In the upcoming sections, we will be often interested in working with regular categories, where the situation becomes fairly simpler. 
    In a regular category, universal extremal epimorphisms coincide with regular epimorphisms. Hence, a regular category is pro\-point\-ed{} if and only if it admits an initial object and $0\to1$ is a regular epimorphism.
    Moreover, slices of a regular category are again regular, and change-of-base functors between them are regular functors. Consequently, Theorem~\ref{thm:propointed} can be specialised to the regular context as follows.
<!-- end remark-6 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:reg-propointed}
    For a regular category $\mathscr{A}$, the following are equivalent.
    \begin{enumerate}[(1)]
        \item \label{reg-propointed-1}$\mathscr{A}$ is pro\-point\-ed{};
         
        \item\label{reg-propointed-3} there exists a regular conservative right-adoint functor $U\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}$ with $\mathscr{X}$ pointed and regular;
        \item\label{reg-propointed-4} $\mathscr{A}$ admits an initial object and there exists a regular conservative functor $U\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}$ with $\mathscr{X}$ pointed and regular.
        \end{enumerate}

A finitely complete (respectively, regular) category with an initial object is pro\-point\-ed{} if and only if, for every object $X$, the product projection $X\times 0\to X$ is an extremal epimorphism (respectively, a regular epimorphism).

\label{example-forgetful-variety}

<!-- example-6: body_md -->
The dual of the category of semigroups is pro\-point\-ed{}. Indeed, for any semigroup $X$, the coproduct $X+1$ in the category of semigroups is given by the set of non-empty words formed by alternating elements of $X$ and of $1=\{\ast\}$, with the obvious multiplication. The coproduct injections
    \( i\colon X\to X+1\)
     and 
    \( j\colon 1\to X+1\)
    assign to each $a\in X$ or $a\in 1$ the single-letter word $(a)$. 
    We show that $i$ is a regular monomorphism in the category of semigroups. 
    
    Let $\mathbb{Z}_2$ be the set $\{0,1\}$ with multiplication modulo 2, and consider the maps
    \(p_0\colon X+1\to\mathbb{Z}_2\)
    and 
    \(p_1\colon X+1\to\mathbb{Z}_2\)
    defined by
    \[
    \mcomp{i,p_0}=\mcomp{i,p_1}=\Delta_{1},\quad \mcomp{j, p_0}=\Delta_{0},\quad\mcomp{j,p_1}=\Delta_{1},
    \]
    where $\Delta_{b}$ denotes the constant map at $b\in\mathbb{Z}_2$ with the appropriate domain (note that a constant function at an idempotent element is always a semigroup homomorphism). One readily checks that 
    \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
        X\arrow[r,"i"]&X+1\arrow[r, "p_0", yshift=.3em]\arrow[r, "p_1"',yshift=-.3em]&\mathbb{Z}_2
    \end{tikzcd}
    \]
    is an equaliser diagram.

        Note that the category of semigroups is not coregular (\cite[Section~3]{KELLY69}).

<!-- example-7: body_md -->
The dual of a variety with no constants is \emph{not} pro\-point\-ed{} in general. Consider, for instance, the variety of semigroups satisfying $x^2=y^2$. Every algebra $X$ in this variety has at most one idempotent, and therefore admits at most one morphism $1\to X$. It follows that $\varnothing\to1$ is an epimorphism. Since it is not an isomorphism, it cannot be a regular monomorphism.

<!-- example-8: body_md -->
Coslices of pro\-point\-ed{} categories are again pro\-point\-ed{}. Indeed, let $\mathscr{A}$ be a pro\-point\-ed{} category, and let $A$ be an object in $\mathscr{A}$. The morphism $p\colon 0\to1$ in $\mathscr{A}$ may be factored as 
\begin{ildiag}
    0\rar{\mathord{!}_{A}}&A\rar{!^{A}} &1.
\end{ildiag}
Since $p$ is a universal extremal epimorphism, so is $!^{A}$. Consequently, $!^{A}$ is a stably extremal epimorphism when regarded as a morphism in $({A}\downarrow\mathscr{A})$ from $0_{({A}\downarrow\mathscr{A})}= (A,\textnormal{id}_{A})$ to $1_{({A}\downarrow\mathscr{A})}=(1,!^{A})$.

\label{example-slice-coslice}

Consider the category $\mathscr{A}=({(E,p)}\downarrow{(\mathscr{E}\downarrow{B})})$ obtained as the coslice under $(E,p)$ of the slice of $\mathscr{E}$ over $B$. The objects of $\mathscr{A}$ can be described as triples $(X,x_1,x_2)$ with $X$ an object in $\mathscr{E}$ and $x_1\colon E\to X$ and $x_2\colon X\to B$ morphisms in $\mathscr{E}$ such that $x_2\cdot x_1=p$, as in the diagram below on the left.      \[
     \begin{tikzcd}[row sep =1.em]
         &&&&X\arrow[dr, "x_2", bend left]\arrow[dd, "f" description]
         \\
         E\arrow[r, "x_1"']\arrow[rr, bend left, "p"]&X\arrow[r, "x_2"'] &B & E\arrow[ur,"x_1", bend left]\arrow[dr, "y_1"', bend right] && B
         \\
         &&&&Y\arrow[ur, "y_2"', bend right]
     \end{tikzcd}
     \]

The unique morphism $0_{\mathscr{A}}\to 1_{\mathscr{A}}$ is $p$ itself. The slice $(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0_{\mathscr{A}}})$ is just the category $\mathsf{Pt}_{E}({\mathscr{E}})$ of points of $\mathscr{E}$ over $E$ (in the sense of \cite{BBBOOK}), and $p^\ast\colon\mathscr{A}\to(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})$ takes $(X,x_1,x_2)\in\mathscr{A}$ to the split epimorphism $(f,s)\in\mathsf{Pt}_{E}({\mathscr{E}})$ described by the following pullback diagram in $\mathscr{E}$.

         \\[-2em]

         \\
        & X\arrow[r, "x_2"'] &B
     \end{tikzcd}

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:exact-sequence}
    Let $\mathscr{A}$ denote a category with an initial object.

    A \emph{complex} in $\mathscr{A}$ is a triple of morphisms 
    \begin{equation}
    \label{complex-def-diagram}
    \begin{tikzcd}
        0&A\arrow[l, "e"'] \arrow[r, "f"]&B\arrow[r, "g"]&C
    \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
    in $\mathscr{A}$ such that the following diagram is commutative.
    \begin{equation}
    \label{complex-def-square}
    \begin{tikzcd}
        A\arrow[r,"e"]\arrow[d,"f"']&0\arrow[d, "\mathord{!}_{C}"]
        \\
        B\arrow[r,"g"']&C
    \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
    Given two complexes
    \[ 
    \begin{tikzcd}
        0&A\arrow[l, "e"'] \arrow[r, "f"]&B\arrow[r, "g"]&C,
    \end{tikzcd}\qquad
    \begin{tikzcd}
        0&A'\arrow[l, "e'"'] \arrow[r, "f'"]&B'\arrow[r, "g'"]&C'
    \end{tikzcd}
    \]
    a \emph{morphism of complexes} $(e,f,g)\to (e',f',g')$ is triple of morphisms
    \[
    \alpha\colon A\to A',\quad \beta\colon B\to B',\quad\gamma\colon C\to C'
    \]
    making the following diagram commutative.
    \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
        &A\arrow[r, "f"]\arrow[ld, "e"']\arrow[dd,"\alpha"]&B\arrow[dd,"\beta"]\arrow[r, "g"]&C\arrow[dd,"\gamma"]
        \\[-2em]
        0
        \\[-2em]
        &A' \arrow[ul, "e'"]\arrow[r, "f'"']&B'\arrow[r, "g'"']&C'
    \end{tikzcd}
    \]
    Complexes in $\mathscr{A}$ and morphisms between them form a category which we denote by $\mathsf{Cpx}({\mathscr{A}})$.

    
    We say that the complex in \ref{complex-def-diagram} is 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item a \emph{left-exact sequence} if the corresponding Diagram~\ref{complex-def-square} is a pullback;
        \item a \emph{right-exact sequence} if the corresponding Diagram~\ref{complex-def-square} is a pushout;
        \item a \emph{(short) exact sequence} if the corresponding Diagram~\ref{complex-def-square} is a both pullback and a pushout.
    \end{itemize}
    When \ref{complex-def-diagram} is a left-exact sequence, we also say that $(A,e,f)$ is the \emph{kernel} of $g$ (in symbols, $(A,e,f)=\operatorname{ker}(()g)$).
    
    A morphism of complexes $(e,f,g)\to(e',f',g')$ is called a \emph{morphism of (left-\slash right-)exact sequences} if the complexes $(e,f,g)$ and $(e',f',g')$ are (left-\slash right-)exact sequences.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- remark-8: body_md -->
All the notions introduced in Definition~\ref{def:exact-sequence} reduce to the usual ones when the ambient category is pointed. In this case, the morphism $e$ to the initial object appearing in the data of a complex $(e,f,g)$ or of a kernel $(A,e,f)$ becomes redundant and is omitted.
<!-- end remark-8 -->
<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
The notion of kernel of Definition~\ref{def:exact-sequence} can be viewed as an instance of a \emph{homotopy kernel} with respect to the nullhomotopy structure arising from the coreflective subcategory of initial objects (see \cite{GRANDIS97,HTT}). Note that, in general, the map $f$ underlying a kernel $(A,e,f)$ is not necessarily a monomorphism. It becomes one when the category is quasi-pointed (see Remark~\ref{rmk:quasi-pointed} and \cite{BOURN-QUASI-POINTED}).
<!-- end remark-9 -->
<!-- remark-10: body_md -->
Given a complex 
    \begin{ildiag}
        0&A\arrow[l, "e"'] \arrow[r, "f"]&B\arrow[r, "g"]&C
    \end{ildiag}
     as in Definition~\ref{def:exact-sequence}, if it is a right-exact sequence, then
    \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
        A\arrow[phantom,rr, "0",yshift=.3em]\arrow[r, yshift=.3em, "e"]\arrow[rr, yshift=-.3em, "f"'] & {}\arrow[r, yshift=.3em] & B\arrow[rr, "g"] && C
    \end{tikzcd}
    \]
    is a coequaliser diagram. In particular, it yields a \emph{star coequaliser} (in the sense of \cite{GRAN12}) with respect to the ideal of morphisms factoring through 0. 
    
    When the complex is a left-exact sequence, however, the corresponding cofork diagram above is not necessarily a \emph{star-kernel} (again in the sense of \cite{GRAN12}), since $(f,\mathord{!}_{B}\cdot e)$ are not jointly monic in general.
<!-- end remark-10 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{def-normal-epi}
    In a category with an initial object, a morphism $g\colon B\to C$ is called a \emph{normal epimorphism} if the pullback of $g$ along $0\to C$ exists and is a pushout.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- remark-11: body_md -->
\label{remark-on-normal-epis}
    In a category with an initial object, a map $g$ in a right-exact sequence $(e,f,g)$ is always a normal epimorphism, and hence a regular epimorphism (being the pushout of the split epimorphism $e$). Clearly, a triple of morphisms $(e,f\colon A\to B,g)$ is an exact sequence if and only if $(A, e,f)$ is the kernel of $g$ and $g$ is a normal epimorphism.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{complex-functor}
Let $U\colon \mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{B}$ be a left-exact functor between finitely complete categories admitting an initial object. Then $U$ induces a functor 
\[
\mathord{\mathsf{Cpx}}{U}\colon\mathsf{Cpx}({\mathscr{A}})\to\mathsf{Cpx}({\mathscr{B}})
\]
between the corresponding categories of complexes. The image of a complex
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
    0 & A\rar{f}\arrow[l,"e"'] & B\rar{g} &C
\end{tikzcd}
\]
in $\mathscr{A}$ is the complex 
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep = 3em]
    0_{\mathscr{B}} &H\rar{U(f)\cdot h}\arrow[l, "\ell"']&U(B)\rar{U(g)}&U(C)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with $(H,\ell, h)=\operatorname{ker}(U)(e)$, as in the following diagram.
\[

\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
{A}\arrow[r, "{e}"]\arrow[d,"{f}"']\&{0_{\mathscr{A}}}\arrow[d, "{}"]
\\
{B}\arrow[r, "{g}"']\&{C}
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad\longmapsto\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}
    H\arrow[r,"\ell"] \arrow[d,"h"']&0_{\mathscr{B}}\dar
    \\
    U(A)\arrow[r, "U(e)"]\arrow[d,"U(f)"']
    & U(0_{\mathscr{A}})\dar
    \\
    U(B)\arrow[r, "U(g)"'] & U(C)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
In particular, $\mathord{\mathsf{Cpx}}{U}$ preserves left-exact sequences.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The image via $\mathord{\mathsf{Cpx}}{U}$ of a morphism of complexes $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\colon (e,f,g)\to(e',f',g')$ in $\mathscr{A}$ is defined as 
    
    \[\bigl(\eta, U(\beta),U(\gamma)\bigr)\colon (\ell, h\cdot U(f), U(g))\to(\ell', h'\cdot U(f'), U(g')),\] with $\eta$ the unique map such that $\ell'\cdot \eta=\ell$ and $h'\cdot \eta=U(\alpha)\cdot h$. The preservation of left-exact sequences follows from the left-exactness of $U$ and the usual pullback composition property.

In the opposite category $\mathsf{HComp}^{\mathsf{op}}$ of the category $\mathsf{HComp}$ of compact Hausdorff spaces, short exact sequences admit a particularly simple description. \label{thm:prot-propointed}
    Let $\mathscr{A}$ be a finitely complete category. The following are equivalent.
    \begin{enumerate}[(1)]
        \item\label{prot-propointed-1} $\mathscr{A}$ is pro\-point\-ed{} and pro\-to\-mod\-u\-lar{}.
        \item\label{prot-propointed-2} $\mathscr{A}$ is pro\-point\-ed{} and $(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})$ is pro\-to\-mod\-u\-lar{}.
        \item\label{prot-propointed-3} There exists a conservative right-adoint functor $U\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}$ with $\mathscr{X}$ finitely complete, pointed and pro\-to\-mod\-u\-lar{}.
        \item\label{prot-propointed-4} $\mathscr{A}$ admits an initial object and there exists a left-exact conservative functor $U\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}$ with $\mathscr{X}$ finitely complete, pointed and pro\-to\-mod\-u\-lar{}.
    \end{enumerate}

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:ex-seq-prot}
    Let $\mathscr{A}$ be a pro\-point\-ed{} pro\-to\-mod\-u\-lar{} finitely complete category, and let $U\colon \mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}$ be a left-exact conservative functor with $\mathscr{X}$ pointed  pro\-to\-mod\-u\-lar{} and finitely complete (as in Theorem~\ref{thm:prot-propointed}.\ref{prot-propointed-3}). The functor induced on the corresponding categories of complexes
    \[
    \bar U\coloneqq\mathord{\mathsf{Cpx}}{U}\colon\mathsf{Cpx}({\mathscr{A}})\to\mathsf{Cpx}({\mathscr{X}})
    \]
    preserves and reflects left-exact sequences. Moreover, it preserves and reflects monomorphisms and isomorphisms componentwise: for a morphism of complexes $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ in $\mathscr{A}$, if

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:split-5}
    Let $\mathscr{A}$ be a pro\-point\-ed{} finitely complete category. The following are equivalent.
    \begin{enumerate}[(1)]
        \item\label{split-5-1} $\mathscr{A}$ is pro\-to\-mod\-u\-lar{}.
        \item\label{split-5-2} The split short five lemma holds in $\mathscr{A}$, that is, for any commutative diagram $\mathscr{A}$
        \begin{equation}
        \label{split-5-diagram}
    \begin{tikzcd}
        &A\arrow[r, "f"]\arrow[ld, "e"']\arrow[dd,"\alpha"]&B\arrow[dd,"\beta"]\arrow[r, "g", yshift=.3em]&C\arrow[dd,"\gamma"]\arrow[l, yshift=-.3em,, "s"]
        \\[-2em]
        0
        \\[-2em]
        &A' \arrow[ul, "e'"]\arrow[r, "f'"']&B'\arrow[r, "g'", yshift=.3em]&C'\arrow[l, "s'", yshift=-.3em]
    \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
        where $(e,f,g)$ and $(e',f',g')$ are left-exact sequences, $g\cdot s=\textnormal{id}_{C}$ and $g'\cdot s'=\textnormal{id}_{C'}$, if $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ are isomorphisms, then so is $\beta$.
    \end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
If $\mathscr{A}$ is pro\-to\-mod\-u\-lar{}, Item~\ref{split-5-2} follows immediately from Theorem~\ref{thm:ex-seq-prot}.
    
    Conversely, assume \ref{split-5-2} holds. Since $\mathscr{A}$ is pro\-point\-ed{}, the change-of-base functor $\mathscr{A}\to(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})$ along $0\to1$ is left-exact and conservative. By \cite[Example~3.1.9]{BBBOOK}, it suffices to show that $(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})$ is pro\-to\-mod\-u\-lar{}. Since $(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})$ is pointed, it is enough to verify that the split short five lemma holds in $(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})$. Now, the zero object of $(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})$ is $(0,\textnormal{id}_{0})$, and pullbacks in $(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})$ are computed as in $\mathscr{A}$. It follows that applying the forgetful functor $(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})\to\mathscr{A}$ to a morphism of left-exact sequences in $(\mathscr{A}\downarrow{0})$ yields a morphism of left-exact sequences in $\mathscr{A}$, and the result follows.

<!-- theorem-5: body_md -->
\label{thm:ex-seq-homol}
    Let $\mathscr{A}$ be a pro\-ho\-mo\-lo\-gi\-cal{} category, and let $U\colon \mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}$ be a regular conservative functor with $\mathscr{X}$ homological (as in Proposition~\ref{thm:homol-propointed}.\ref{homol-propointed-3}).     \(
    \mathord{\mathsf{Cpx}}{U}\colon\mathsf{Cpx}({\mathscr{A}})\to\mathsf{Cpx}({\mathscr{X}})
    \)
    preserves and reflects short exact sequences. Moreover, it preserves and reflects monomorphisms, regular epimorphisms and isomorphisms componentwise (in the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:ex-seq-prot}).

<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Recall from Remark~\ref{remark-on-normal-epis} that a complex $(e,f,g)$ in a category with an initial object is a short exact sequence if and only if it is a left-exact sequence and $g$ is a normal epimorphism. Recall further that a regular conservative functor preserves and reflects regular epimorphisms. The preservation and reflection of short exact sequence then follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:ex-seq-prot} and Lemma~\ref{lemma-reg=norm}. 

    The componentwise preservation and reflection of monomorphisms and isomorphisms was established in Theorem~\ref{thm:ex-seq-prot}. The corresponding statement for regular epimorphisms can be proved by following the proof of the isomorphism case in Theorem~\ref{thm:ex-seq-prot}, using again the fact that $U$ preserves and reflects regular epimorphisms together with  \cite[Lemma~4.2.5.4]{BBBOOK}.

In the presence of pullbacks and pushouts, normal spans can be seen as generalisations of normal subgroups in the sense of Mac Lane (\cite{MACLANE50}): a span $(H, e\colon H\to 0,h\colon H\to A)$ is normal when, for any other span $(S, u\colon S\to 0, s\colon S\to A)$, if $H$ \emph{dominates} $S$ (that is, for all $f\colon A\to B$ such that $f\cdot h=\mathord{!}_{B}\cdot e$, it follows that $f\cdot s=\mathord{!}_{B}\cdot u$), then $H$ \emph{contains} $S$ (that is, there exists a morphism $S\to H$ of spans).

\label{thm:ideals}
    Let $\mathscr{A}$ be a pro\-ho\-mo\-lo\-gi\-cal{} category and $U\colon \mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{X}$ a regular conservative functor with $\mathscr{X}$ homological. Then, for any object $A\in\mathscr{A}$, the poset of normal spans on $A$ is isomorphic to the poset of $U$-ideals of $A$ (with the usual order on subobjects of $U(A)$).
<!-- end theorem-8 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
To a normal span $(H,e,h)=\operatorname{ker}((f\colon A\to B))$ on $A$ we assign a kernel of $U(f)$ in $\mathscr{X}$. One easily checks that this assignment is well defined and  yields an order-preserving surjection from the poset of normal spans on $A$ to the poset of $U$-ideals of $A$. It remains to show that this assignment reflects the order.

    Let $(H,h,e)$ and $(H', h',e')$ be normal spans on $A$, and let $I\equiv(I,i)$ and $I'\equiv(I',i')$ be the corresponding $U$-ideals. Assume $I\le I'$. We prove that $H\le H'$.
    \[
    \begin{tikzcd}[column sep =2em]
        &H\arrow[r, "h"]\arrow[dd, dashed]\arrow[ld, "e"']&A\arrow[dd,equal]\arrow[r, "f"]&B\arrow[dd, dashed, "\beta"]&&
        I\arrow[r, "i"]\arrow[dd] & U(A)\arrow[dd, equal]\arrow[r, "U(f)"] &[1em]U(B)\arrow[dd, dashed, "\tilde\beta"]
        \\[-2.8em]
        0&&&&\longmapsto
        \\[-2.8em]
        &H' \arrow[ul, "e'"]\arrow[r, "h'"']&A\arrow[r, "f'"']&B'&&
        I'\arrow[r, "i'"'] &U(A)\arrow[r, "U(f')"']&U(B')
    \end{tikzcd}
    \]
    Choose morphisms $f\colon A\to B$ and $f'\colon A\to B'$ in $\mathscr{A}$ such that $H$ and $H'$ are the kernels (in $\mathscr{A}$) of $f$ and $f'$, respectively, so that $I$ and $I'$ are the kernels (in $\mathscr{X}$) of $U(f)$ and $U(f')$, respectively. By Theorem~\ref{Noether-1}, we may assume that $f$ and $f'$ are regular epimorphisms. Since $U$ is a regular functor, $U(f)$ and $U(f')$ are regular epimorphisms; since $\mathscr{X}$ is homological, they are the cokernels of $i$ and $i'$, respectively (see \cite[Proposition~3.1.23]{BBBOOK}). Therefore, by the universal property of cokernels and the fact that $I\le I'$, it immediately follows that there exists a map $\tilde\beta\colon U(B)\to U(B')$ such that $U(f')=\tilde\beta\cdot U(f)$. Let $(R,r_0,r_1)$ be the kernel pair of $f$. Then
    \[
    U(f'\cdot r_0)=\mcomp{U(r_0),U(f),\tilde\beta}=\mcomp{U(r_1),U(f),\tilde\beta}=U(f'\cdot r_1).
    \]
    Since $U$ is faithful (Remark~\ref{faithfulness}), it follows that $f'\cdot r_0=f'\cdot r_1$. As $f$ is the coequaliser of its kernel pair, there exists a unique morphism $\beta\colon B\to B'$ such that $\beta\cdot f=f'$. By the universal property of kernels, $\beta$ induces a morphism of normal spans $H\to H'$, showing that $H\le H'$.

<!-- remark-15: body_md -->
The previous theorem shows that every object of a pro\-ho\-mo\-lo\-gi\-cal{} category carries an  `absolute' poset of ideals. This poset may be described extrinsically, via any regular conservative forgetful functor as in Proposition~\ref{thm:homol-propointed}, as a poset of genuine normal subobjects in the pointed category, or intrinsically, in terms of normal spans. Either description may be useful, depending on the situation.

    This phenomenon does not occur in general, not even for regular pro\-point\-ed{} categories. For example, in the (regular pro\-point\-ed{}) category $\mathsf{Set_{\ast\ast}}$ of doubly pointed sets, the object $(\{0,1,2\},0,1)$ admits five normal spans but only four $U$-ideals, where $U\colon\mathsf{Set_{\ast\ast}}\to\mathsf{Set_{\ast}}$ is the (regular monadic) forgetful functor that forgets the second distinguished point.
<!-- end remark-15 -->
<!-- remark-16: body_md -->
A simpler situation arises when there exists a sufficiently well-behaved forgetful functor $U$ to a pointed category such that, for every object $A$ in the base category, \emph{every} normal subobject of $U(A)$ is a $U$-ideal (in the sense of \cite{RELATIVE-IDEALS}). This is the case in the ideally exact setting and, more generally, in the ideally regular setting. As \cite[Remark~3.6]{IDR} shows, however, a fundamental requirement for this phenomenon is that $U$ be \emph{monadic}. 
Pro\-ho\-mo\-lo\-gi\-cal{} categories do not, in general, admit a monadic forgetful functor to a homological category. Indeed, such a functor would force the morphism $0\to 1$ to be \emph{effective for descent}, a condition that is not satisfied in general -- see \cite[Remark~2.7, Example~1.4.c]{IDR}.

\label{Noether-2}
    Let $\mathscr{A}$ denote an ideally exact category. Then, for any pair of normal spans $H_1$ and $H_2$ over an object $A$ the following hold.

        \item the dual of the lattice of quotient objects of $U(A)$ in $\mathscr{X}$;
        \item the lattice of normal subobjects of $U(A)$ in $\mathscr{X}$.

\label{example:OrdRing}
    Let $\mathsf{OrdRing}$ denote the category of preordered unital rings, that is, unital rings $R$ equipped with a preorder $\le$ such that
    \begin{equation}
    \label{ordring-conditions}
          0\le1, \qquad a\le b\implies a+c\le b+c, \qquad 0\le a,b\implies 0\le a\cdot b
    \end{equation}
    for all $a,b,c\in R$. Morphisms are monotone unital ring homomorphisms.  Equivalently, $\mathsf{OrdRing}$ may be described as the category of pairs $(R,S)$, where $R$ is a unital ring and $S\subseteq R$ is a unital subsemiring, and morphisms are unital ring homomorphisms compatible with the subsemirings. The correspondence sends the preordered ring $(R,\leq)$ to the pair $(R,R^+)$, where $R^+=\{a\in R\mid 0\leq a\}$, and the pair $(R,S)$ to the ring $R$ with the preorder defined by $a\leq b$ if and only if $b-a\in S$. Consequently $\mathsf{OrdRing}$ can be seen as a two-sorted quasivariety. Thus, limits are computed sortwise in $\mathsf{Set}$, while regular epimorphisms are precisely the sortwise surjective morphisms. Its initial object is $(\mathbb Z,\mathbb N)$, and one readily checks that $\mathsf{OrdRing}$ is pro\-nor\-mal{} (this essentially follows from the fact that the category of unital rings is pro\-nor\-mal{}). It is, however, not protomodular. This can be seen by observing that there is a pullback-preserving conservative functor $\mathsf{OrdAb}\to \mathsf{OrdRing}$ 
    sending a preordered abelian group $(A,M)$ (see \cite{CLEMENTINO19}) to the preordered ring $(A\times \mathbb Z,M\times\mathbb N)$, equipped with the evident operations. If $\mathsf{OrdRing}$ were protomodular, so would be $\mathsf{OrdAb}$, which is not the case (see again \cite{CLEMENTINO19}).

\label{ex-pronorm-seq}
Let $\mathsf{OrdRng}$ be the category of (not necessarily unital) rings $R$ equipped with a preorder $\le$ satisfying the second and the third condition in \ref{ordring-conditions}. Morphisms are monotone ring homomorphisms. One readily checks that $\mathsf{OrdRng}$ is a normal category. There is an obvious forgetful functor $U\colon \mathsf{OrdRing}\to\mathsf{OrdRng}$, which satisfies Condition~\ref{pronormal-4} of Theorem~\ref{thm:pronormal}. Nevertheless, the induced functor between the corresponding categories of complexes (as in Proposition~\ref{complex-functor}) neither reflects left-exact sequences nor is conservative. Indeed, consider the complex in $\mathsf{OrdRing}$
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
    \mathbb Z & H\arrow[l, "e"'] \arrow[r, "h"] & \mathbb Z\times \mathbb Z\arrow[r, "\pi_1"]&\mathbb Z.
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Here $\mathbb Z$ is equipped with its usual order and $\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$ with the product order; $\pi_1$ is the first product projection; $H=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$ is endowed with the preorder defined by
\[
(a,b)\preceq(a',b')
\iff0\le a'-a\le b'-b;
\] 
$h$ is the identity map and $e$ is again the first projection. This is clearly a complex, but not a left-exact sequence. On the other hand, the kernels of $U(e)$ and $U(\pi_1)$ coincide, so the associated complex in $\mathsf{OrdRng}$ is left exact (cf.\ Proposition~\ref{complex-functor}).
