<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
    For all $t\leqslant n^k$, there exists a binary $t$-list decodable $k$-deletion code $\mathcal C\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ with size $|\mathcal C|\geqslant \Omega_k\left(\frac{2^n t\log^{1/t} n}{n^{k(1+1/t)}}\right)$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
The optimal size of a $t$-list decodable $k$-deletion code is $\Theta_k(2^nt/n^k)$ for all $\Omega(k\log n)\leqslant t\leqslant n^k$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $u$ and $v$ be strings. We call a common supersequence $w$ of $u$ and $v$ a \emph{minimal common supersequence} of $u$ and $v$ if no proper subsequence of $w$ is a common supersequence of $u$ and $v$. Let $\text{LCS}(u,v)$ denote the length of the longest common subsequence of $u$ and $v$ and $\text{SCS}(u,v)$ denote the length of the shortest common supersequence of $u$ and $v$. Let $m_{\text{LCS}(u,v)}$ and $m_{\text{SCS}(u,v)}$ denote the number of common subsequences or supersequences of length $\text{LCS}(u,v)$ or $\text{SCS}(u,v)$, respectively. For all $r\geqslant 0$, let $m_{\text{LCS}(u,v),r},m_{\text{SCS}(u,v),r}$ denote the number of common subsequences or supersequences of length $\text{LCS}(u,v)-r$ or $\text{SCS}(u,v)+r$, respectively.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:genmult}
    Let $n,a,b$ be natural numbers such that $n\geqslant a+b.$ If $u$ and $v$ are words of length $n-a$ and $n-b$, respectively, and $\text{LCS}(u,v)=n-a-b$, then for all $r\geqslant 0$, $m_{\text{LCS}(u,v),r}\leqslantm_{\text{SCS}(u,v),r}$. Furthermore, if $u$ and $v$ are binary strings, then
    \begin{align*}
         m_{\text{LCS}(u,v),r}\leqslantm_{\text{SCS}(u,v),r}\leqslant \sum_{s=0}^r {a+b+2s\choose a+s}\sum_{i=0}^{r-s}{n+r\choose i}\,.
    \end{align*}
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
We call $\mathcal C\subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ a \emph{$t$-list decodable $k$-deletion code} if for all $y\in \{0,1\}^{n-k}$, there exist at most $t$ distinct $x\in\mathcal C$ containing $y$ as a subsequence.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
We define the $(k,t)$-deletion hypergraph $\Gamma_{n,k,t}$ to be the $(t+1)$-uniform hypergraph with vertex set $V(\Gamma_{n,k,t})\coloneqq \{0,1\}^n$ and edge set $E(\Gamma_{n,k,t})$ such that for all $C\subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ with $|C|=t+1$, we have $C\in E(\Gamma_{n,k,t})$ if there exists some $y\in \{0,1\}^{n-k}$ which is a subsequence of all $x\in C$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal H$ be a $k$-uniform hypergraph. Define $F_\mathcal H(j)$ to be the number of unordered pairs $C,C'\in \mathcal H$ such that $|C\cap C'|=j$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- fact-1: body_md -->
(\cite[Theorem~3]{DLR95})\label{fact:indnum}
    Let $3\leqslant k\leqslant O((\log t/\log\log t)^{1/3})$ and $\mathcal H$ be a $k$-uniform hypergraph with $n$ vertices with maximum degree $\leqslant t^{k-1}$. If $F_\mathcal H(j)\leqslant nt^{2k-j-1-\varepsilon}$ for some $\varepsilon\geqslant \exp(-O(k))$ for all $2\leqslant j\leqslant k-1$, then $\alpha(\mathcal H)=\Omega(\frac{n}{t}\log^{1/(k-1)}t)$.
<!-- end fact-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\cite{DLR95} states an $\alpha(\mathcal H)=\Omega_{k,\varepsilon}(\frac{n}{t}\log^{1/(k-1)}t)$ lower bound; the dependence on $\varepsilon,k$ is a factor of $(\varepsilon/k)^{1/(k-1)}$ which is $\Omega(1)$ for $\varepsilon\geqslant \exp(-O(k))$.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:mincount}
    Let $n, a, b$ be natural numbers with $n \geqslant a + b$ and let $r\geqslant 0$. If $u$ and $v$ are words with length $n - a$ and $n - b$ (respectively) and $\text{SCS}(u, v)= n$, then the number of minimal common supersequences of $u$ and $v$ of length $n+r$ is at most ${a+b+2r\choose a+r}$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We will prove the result for $r\geqslant -1$. If $a=0$ or $b=0$, then there is at most one minimal common supersequence, while when $a,b\geqslant 1$ and $r= -1$, there are no minimal common supersequences of length $n-1$. We induct on $a+b+2r$ with the aforementioned base cases. Assume without loss of generality that $u_1\neq v_1$; if $u$ and $v$ have a common prefix, then every minimal common supersequence of $u$ and $v$ must have the same prefix, so we can ignore the prefix. Then every minimal common supersequence is either of the form $u_1x$ or $v_1y$, where $x$ is a minimal common supersequence of $u_{[2,n-a]}$ and $v$, and $y$ is a minimal common superseqeunce of $u$ and $v_{[2,n-b]}$. Note that $x$ has length $n+r-1$ and $\text{SCS}(u_{[2,n-a]},v)$ is either $n-1$ or $n$; if it is equal to $n-1$, then $a$ and $r$ are unchanged but $b$ decreases by $1$, and if it is $n$, then $a$ increases by $1$, $b$ is unchanged, and $r$ decreases by $1$. Either way, there are at most ${a+b+2r-1\choose a+r}$ total choices for $x$ by induction. A similar analysis shows that there are at most ${a+b+2r-1\choose a+r-1}$ choices for $y$. Thus by Pascal's Identity, there are at most ${a+b+2r-1\choose a+r}+{a+b+2r-1\choose a+r-1}={a+b+2r\choose a+r}$ length $n+r$ minimal common supersequences.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:upperbound}
    Let $n,a,b$ be natural numbers such that $n\geqslant a+b.$ If $u\in \{0,1\}^{n-a}$ and $v\in \{0,1\}^{n-b}$ such that $\text{SCS}(u,v)=n$, then
    \begin{align*}
         m_{\text{SCS}(u,v),r}\leqslant \sum_{s=0}^r {a+b+2s\choose a+s}\sum_{i=0}^{r-s}{n+r\choose i}\,.
    \end{align*}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $x$ be a length $\text{SCS}(u,v)+r=n+r$ common supersequence of $u$ and $v$. Then there exists $y\prec x$ such that $y$ is a minimal common supersequence of $u$ and $v$. Note that we can count the number of possible $x$ by first picking the length of some minimal common supersequence, which must be $n+s$ for some $0\leqslant s\leqslant r$. Then by \Cref{lem:mincount} there are up to ${a+b+2s\choose a+s}$ minimal common supersequences $y$ of that length, and finally, we generate a length $n+r$ common supersequence $x\succ y$, which can be done in $\sum_{i=0}^{r-s}{n+r\choose i}$ ways.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:weakalphabound}
 For all $0<t\leqslant n^k$ and $k\geqslant 2$, we have $\alpha(\Gamma_{n,k,t})\geqslant\Omega(\frac{2^nt}{n^{k+k/t}})$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $p=t/en^{k+k/t}$ and let $\mathcal C\subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ such that each $x\in \{0,1\}^n$ is included in $\mathcal C$ independently with probability $p$. Then ${\varmathbb E}|\mathcal C|=2^nt/en^{k+k/t}$. Note that every $y\in \{0,1\}^{n-k}$ has at most $\sum_{i=0}^k{n\choose i}\leqslant n^k$ length-$n$ supersequences and thus $|E(\Gamma_{n,k,t})|\leqslant 2^{n-k}{n^k\choose t+1}\leqslant 2^{n-k}n^{k(t+1)}/(t+1)!$. Given $\mathcal C$, we can delete one $x\in C$ from $\mathcal C$ from every $C\in E(\Gamma_{n,k,t})$ where $C\subseteq \mathcal C$ to create an independent set. The expected size of such an independent set is at least
    \begin{align}
        \frac{2^nt}{en^{k+k/t}}-\frac{2^{n-k}n^{k(t+1)}t^{t+1}}{\left(n^{k+k/t}\right)^{(t+1)}e^{t+1}(t+1)!}\geqslant \frac{2^nt}{en^{k+k/t}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k+1/2}\sqrt{\pi t}}\right)\,.
    \end{align}
Thus we have $\alpha(\Gamma_{n,k,t})\geqslant\Omega_k(\frac{2^nt}{n^{k+k/t}})$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:alphabound}
 For all $2\leqslant t\leqslant O((\log n/\log \log n)^{1/3})$, we have $\alpha(\Gamma_{n,k,t})\geqslant\Omega_k(\frac{2^nt}{n^{k+k/t}}\log^{1/t}n)$.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $N=2^n$ and $T=An^{k+k/t}/t$, where $A=O_k(1)$ will be chosen later. Pick an arbitrary $x\in V(\Gamma_{n,k,t})$. Then for every $C\in E(\Gamma_{n,k,t})$ such that $x\in C$, there exists some $y\in \{0,1\}^{n-k}$ such that $y\prec x'$ for all $x'\in C$. Then given $x$, there are ${n\choose k}\leqslant n^k$ ways to choose such a $y\prec x$. There are at most $2^k{n\choose k}\leqslant 2n^k$ total $x'\in \{0,1\}^n$ with $x'\succ y$, so we upper bound the number of ways to choose the remaining elements of some $C\ni x$ by ${2n^k\choose t}\leqslant 2^tn^{kt}/t!$. Thus $x$ has degree at most $n^k2^tn^{kt}/t!\leqslant (2en^{k+k/t}/t)^t\leqslant T^t$, where we ensure that we choose $A\geqslant 2e$.

    We now upper bound $F_{\Gamma_{n,k,t}}(j)$, after which the result follows from \Cref{fact:indnum}. For any $2\leqslant j\leqslant t$, we upper bound the number of ways to count pairs $C,C'\in \Gamma_{n,k,t}$ such that $|C\cap C'|=j$. To do so, we first pick $y,y'$ such that $y\prec x$ and $y'\prec x'$ for all $x\in C,x'\in C'$, counting only $y,y'$ pairs such that there exists $x\in \{0,1\}^n$ with $y,y'\prec x$. Then we choose $C\cap C'$ and finally picking $C\setminus C'$ and $C'\setminus C$.

    Start by picking $y\in \{0,1\}^{n-k}$, which can be done in $2^{n-k}$ ways. Note that if we have $y', y\in \{0,1\}^{n-k}$ such that there exists some $x\in \{0,1\}^{n}$ satisfying $y,y'\prec x$, then $n-k\leqslant\text{SCS}(y,y')\leqslant n$. We pick $y'$ by first picking $n-k\leqslant\ell\leqslant n$, then picking a length-$\ell$ supersequence, which can be done in at most $2^{\ell-(n-k)}{\ell\choose \ell-(n-k)}=O_k(n^{\ell-(n-k)})$ ways, and finally some $y'$ satisfying $\text{SCS}(y,y')=\ell$ as a length-$(n-k)$ subsequence of the aforementioned supersequence. This can be done in up to ${\ell\choose \ell-(n-k)}=O_k(n^{\ell-(n-k)})$ ways. Then by \Cref{lem:upperbound}, the total number of length-$n$ common supersequences of $y,y'$ is at most 
    \begin{align*}
        m_{\text{SCS}(y,y'),n-\ell}\leqslant\sum_{s=0}^{n-\ell}{2k+2s\choose k+s}\sum_{i=0}^{n-\ell-s}{n\choose i}\leqslant O_k(n^{n-\ell})\,,
    \end{align*}
    so there are at most ${O_k(n^{n-\ell})\choose j}\leqslant \frac{B^j}{j!}\cdot O_k(n^{j(n-\ell)})$ ways to choose $C\cap C'=\{x_1, \cdots, x_j\}$ such that $y,y'\prec x_1, \cdots, x_j$, where $B=O_k(1)$. Therefore, the total number of ways to choose $y,y',x_1, \cdots, x_j$ is
    \begin{align*}
        \frac{B^j}{j!}2^{n-k}\sum_{\ell=n-k}^{n}O_k(n^{j(n-\ell)+2(\ell-(n-k))})
        =\frac{B^j}{j!}2^{n-k}\sum_{\ell=n-k}^nO_k(n^{jk-(j-2)(\ell+k-n)})=\frac{B^j}{j!}2^{n-k}O_k(n^{jk})\,.
    \end{align*}    
    Finally, we upper bound the number of ways to pick $C\setminus C'$ and $C'\setminus C$ by the number of ways to choose $t+1-j$ distinct $x,x'\in \{0,1\}^n$ with $y\prec x$ and $y'\prec x'$, respectively. There are at most $\sum_{i=0}^k{n\choose i}=O_k(n^k)$ choices for each, so there are at most ${O_k(n^k)\choose t+1-j}^2\leqslant \frac{D^{2(t+1-j)}}{((t+1-j)!)^2}O_k(n^{2k(t+1-j)})$ total ways to choose the remaining elements of $C$ and $C'$, where $D=O_k(1)$. Therefore, letting $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{t+1}$ and picking $A=9e^2(B+D+O_k(1))^2=O_k(1)$, we have
    \begin{align*}
        F_{\Gamma_{n,k,t}}(j)
        &\leqslant 2^{n-k}\frac{B^jD^{2(t+1-j)}}{j!((t+1-j)!)^2}O_k(n^{jk+2k(t+1-j)})
        \\&\leqslant \frac{(B+D+O_k(1))^{2t+2-j}}{j!((t+1-j)!)^2}\cdot 2^nn^{(k+k/t)(2t-jt/(t+1))}
        \\&\leqslant \frac{(3eB+3eD+O_k(1))^{2t+2-j}}{t^{2t+2-j}} 2^nn^{(k+k/t)(2t+1-j-1/(t+1))}
        \\&\leqslant N\left(\frac{A n^{(k+k/t)}}{t}\right)^{2t+1-j-1/(t+1)}
        \leqslant NT^{2t+1-j-\varepsilon}\,.
    \end{align*}

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The $t=1$ case is known from \cite{ABGHK}. For the $k=1$ case, the \cite{VarshamovTenengolts65} construction gives $n+1$ mutually disjoint $1$-deletion codes of size $\Theta(2^n/n)$, and the union of any $t$ of these is a $t$-list decodable $1$-deletion code of size $\Theta(2^nt/n)\geqslant \Omega(2^nt\log^{1/t}n/n^{1+1/t})$. When $k,t\geqslant 2$, since every independent set in $\Gamma_{n,k,t}$ is a $t$-list decodable $k$-deletion code, so if $t\leqslant O(\log \log n)$, this directly follows from \Cref{lem:alphabound}. When $t=\Omega(\log \log n)$, we note that $\log^{1/t}n=O(1)$, so the result follows from \Cref{lem:weakalphabound}.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
Let $n,a,b$ be natural numbers such that $n\geqslant a+b.$ If $u$ and $v$ are words of length $n-a$ and $n-b$, respectively, and $\text{LCS}(u,v)=n-a-b$, then for any $r\geqslant 0$, $m_{\text{LCS}(u,v),r}\leqslantm_{\text{SCS}(u,v),r}$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We show the existence of an injective mapping $\phi$ from the set of common subsequences of length $\text{LCS}(u,v)-r$ of $u$ and $v$ to the set of common supersequences of length $\text{SCS}(u,v)+r$. For any common subsequence $w$ of length $\text{LCS}(u,v)-r$, consider the left-most copy of $w$ in $u$ and $v$, and let $i_1< \cdots< i_{\text{LCS}(u,v)-r}$ be the corresponding coordinates of $u$ and $j_1< \cdots< j_{\text{LCS}(u,v)-r}$ be the corresponding coordinates of $v$. Letting $i_0=j_0=0$, for all $k=1, \cdots, \text{LCS}(u,v)-r$, we print all $u_{[i_{k-1}+1,i_k-1]}$, followed by $v_{[j_{k-1}+1,j_k-1]}$, followed by $w_k$. Finally, we end by printing $u_{[i_{\text{LCS}(u,v)-r}+1,n-a]}$ followed by $v_{[j_{\text{LCS}(u,v)-r}+1,n-b]}$. We let $\phi(w)$ be the resulting length-$(n+r)$ supersequence.  

    Let $x=\phi(w)$ and $x'=\phi(w')$ for some length-$(n-a-b-r)$ common subsequences $w\neq w'$. We will show that $x\neq x'$. Let $i_1<\cdots<i_{\text{LCS}(u,v)-r}$ and $j_1<\cdots<j_{\text{LCS}(u,v)-r}$ be the coordinates of the leftmost copy of $w$ in $u$ and $v$, and $i'_1<\cdots<i'_{\text{LCS}(u,v)-r}$ and $j'_1<\cdots<j'_{\text{LCS}(u,v)-r}$ be the coordinates of the leftmost copy of $w'$ in $u$ and $v$. Let $k$ be the smallest coordinate such that $w_k\neq w'_k$. Then $i_\ell=i'_\ell$ and $j_\ell=j'_\ell$ for all $\ell < k$, and $x$ and $x'$ begin with the same first $i_{k-1}+j_{k-1}-(k-1)$ coordinates. 
    
    If $i_k+j_k=i'_k+j'_k$, then $x_{i_k+j_k-k}=w_k\neq w'_k=x'_{i'_k+j'_k-k}$. Otherwise, we assume without loss of generality that $i_k+j_k<i'_k+j'_k$. Suppose that $x=x'$. If $i_k\geqslant i'_k$, then $x_{i'_{k}+j'_{k-1}-(k-1)}=u_{i'_k}=w'_k$. Then if $x'_{i'_k+j'_{k}-(k-1)}=w'_k$, this implies that either there exists some $i'_{k-1}+1\leqslant i\leqslant i'_k-1$ such that $u_i=w'_k$, or some $j'_{k-1}+1\leqslant j\leqslant j'_k-1$ such that $v_j=w'_k$, both of which contradict that $u_{i'_k},v_{j'_k}$ are the coordinates of $w'_k$ in the leftmost copy of $w'$. Thus we have $i_k<i'_k$. Similarly, we have $j_k<j'_k$. 

    Now let $p, q\geqslant 0$ such that $i_{k+p}\leqslant i'_k< i_{k+p+1}$ and $j_{k+q}< j'_k\leqslant j_{k+q+1}$, where we define $i_{\text{LCS}(u,v)-r+1}=n-a+1$ and $j_{\text{LCS}(u,v)-r+1}=n-b$. If $p\leqslant q$, then $x_{i'_k+j_{k+p}-(k+p)}=u_{i'_k}=w'_k$, and $i'_k+j_{k+p}-(k+p) < i'_k+j'_k-k$. On the other hand, if $p>q$, then $x_{i_{k+q+1}+j'_k-(k+q+1)}=v_{j'_k}=w'_k$, and $i_{k+q+1}+j'_k-(k+q+1)<i'_k+j'_k-k$. Either way, there exists some $i_{k-1}+j_{k-1}-(k-1) < t < i'_k+j'_k-k$ such that $x_t=w'_k$. Now suppose that $x'=x$, so $x'_t=w'_k$. But then as before there exists some $i'_{k-1}+1\leqslant i\leqslant i'_k-1$ such that $u_i=w'_k$, or some $j'_{k-1}+1\leqslant j\leqslant j'_k-1$ such that $v_j=w'_k$, again contradicting that $u_{i'_k},v_{j'_k}$ are the coordinates of $w_k'$ in the leftmost $w'$ in $u$ and $v$. 
    
    Thus we have $x'\neq x$ for all $w\neq w'$, so $\phi$ is an injective map from length $\text{LCS}(u,v)-r$ subsequences to length $\text{SCS}(u,v)+r$ supersequences, so $m_{\text{LCS}(u,v),r}\leqslantm_{\text{SCS}(u,v),r}$.
