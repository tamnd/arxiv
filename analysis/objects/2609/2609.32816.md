<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
In classical algebraic topology, the $n$-th symmetric product of a topological space $X$ is the following quotient space:
    \begin{equation}
        \operatorname{SP}^nX:\equiv X^n/\Sigma_n\equiv X^n/(x_1,\dots,x_n)\sim(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(n)}),\sigma\in\Sigma_n
    \end{equation}
    In other words, $\operatorname{SP}^nX$ is the space of unordered $n$-tuples of elements of $X$ with a quotient topology inherited from $X^n$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
In Homotopy Type Theory, a pushout of $f:A\to B$ and $g:A\to C$ written as:
    \begin{equation}
        \begin{tikzcd}
            A \arrow[r, "f"] \arrow[d, "g"] & B \arrow[d] \\
            C \arrow[r] & D \arrow[ul, pos=0, phantom, "\ulcorner"]
        \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
    is the higher inductive type $D$ given by constructors:
    \begin{equation}
        \begin{cases}
            \operatorname{in}_B:B\to D\\
            \operatorname{in}_C:C\to D\\
            \operatorname{glue}:\prod_{a:A}\operatorname{in}_B(f(a))=\operatorname{in}_C(g(a))
        \end{cases}.
    \end{equation}

<!-- fact-1: body_md -->
\label{f:joincontr}
    If $A$ or $B$ is contractible, then so is $A*B$. If $B$ is a proposition and $a:A$, then $A*B$ is contractible if and only if there exists an identification $\prod_{a':A}\operatorname{in}_Aa=_{A*B}\operatorname{in}_Aa'$.

The type $B\Sigma_n$ is the type of (small) types merely equivalent to $\mathbf{n}$:
    \begin{equation}
        B\Sigma_n:\equiv\sum_{X:\mathcal{U}}\left\lVert X\simeq \mathbf{n}\right\rVert_{},

    \begin{equation}
        X^n\mathbin{/\!/} \Sigma_n:\equiv\sum_{A:B\Sigma_n}(\mathrm{proj}_1A\to X)
    \end{equation}
    or the type of tuples over $X$ indexed by unordered $n$-element types.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{t:sp2set}
    If $X$ is a set, then $\operatorname{SP}^2X$ is also a set.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
It suffices to show that loop spaces in $\operatorname{SP}^2X$ are contractible for a model point $z_0$ such that $z_0=q(w_0)$ and $w_0=(\mathbf{2},(x_0,x_1))$ for $x_0,x_1: X$. Indeed, by a pushout induction, for each $x:\operatorname{SP}^2X$ there merely exists a path between $x$ and a model point, and since $\operatorname{Contr}$ is a proposition, we can then prove that the loop space based at $x$ is contractible.

    We use an encode-decode strategy through a family $Q:\operatorname{SP}^2X\to\mathcal{U}$, with the goal of proving that $Q(z)$ is the type $z_0=z$, and then proving contractibility of $Q(z_0)$. We set $P:\equiv x_0=x_1$ and:
    \begin{equation}
        \begin{cases}
            Q(q(K,f))=(w_0=(K,f))*(P\times(f=\lambda\_.x_0)),\\
            Q(s(x))=P\times (x=x_0)
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
    To finish this definition, we need to define the glue case, $\mathit{ap}_Q(\operatorname{glue}(K,x)):Q(q(K,\lambda\_.x))=_\mathcal{U} Q(s(x))$ for $K:B\Sigma_2$ and $x:X$. We will prove that both sides of the equality are propositions. For the right side it is easy, for the left side we assume it has an element and prove it is contractible. If $w_0=(K,\lambda\_.x)$, then $x_0=x_1=x$, so any inhabitant of the join implies $P\times(\lambda\_.x=\lambda\_.x_0)$, which contracts the join. 

    We will now prove that $D:\equiv \sum_{z:\operatorname{SP}^2(X)}Q(z)$ is contractible. By the fundamental theorem of identity types (5.8.2 in \cite{book}), this will give us the desired relation $Q(z)\simeq(z_0=z)$. By the flattening lemma (6.12.2 in \cite{book}), we get a pushout diagram:
    \begin{equation}
        \begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&] \sum_{v:B\Sigma_2\times X}P\times (\mathrm{proj}_2v=x_0) \arrow[r]\arrow[d] \& \sum_{w:X^2\mathbin{/\!/} \Sigma_2}Q(q(w)) \arrow[d]\\ \sum_{x:X}P\times (x=x_0) \arrow[r] \& D \arrow[ul, pos=0, phantom, "\ulcorner"] \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
    Now we can use the fact that $\sum_{s:S}(s_0=s)$ is contractible for any $S:\mathcal{U},s_0:S$ (lemma 3.11.8 in \cite{book}) and commutativity of sigma-types to simplify the corners of the pushout. We get the diagram:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:pushoutD}
        \begin{tikzcd}
            B\Sigma_2\times P \arrow[r, "\mathrm{proj}_2"] \arrow[d] & P \arrow[d]\\
            \sum_{w:X^2\mathbin{/\!/} \Sigma_2}Q(q(w)) \arrow[r] & D \arrow[ul, pos=0, phantom, "\ulcorner"]
        \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
    where the vertical map is $(K,p)\mapsto((K,\lambda\_.x_0),\operatorname{inr}(p,\operatorname{refl}))$. Denote the lower left corner as $R$, by commutativity of sigma-types and pushouts (and the same contractibility argument as above) we get: 
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:pushoutR}
        \begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&] P \arrow[r]\arrow[d] \& B\Sigma_2\times P \arrow[d]\\ \mathbf{1} \arrow[r] \& R \arrow[ul, pos=0, phantom, "\ulcorner"] \end{tikzcd}
    \end{equation}
    where the horizontal map is $p\mapsto(\mathbf{2},p)$. 

    To finish the proof of contractibility of $D$, we show that $d=\operatorname{in}_R(\operatorname{in}_{\mathbf1}(*))$ for any $d:D$ by double induction on the pushout of \eqref{eq:pushoutD} and of \eqref{eq:pushoutR}.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item For the $p:P$ case, we have $\operatorname{glue}(\mathbf{2},p):\operatorname{in}_Pp=\operatorname{in}_R(\operatorname{in}_{B\Sigma_2\times P}(\mathbf{2},p))$ and $\mathit{ap}_{\operatorname{in}_R}(\operatorname{glue} p):\linebreak\operatorname{in}_R(\operatorname{in}_{B\Sigma_2\times P}(\mathbf{2},p))=\allowbreak\operatorname{in}_R(\operatorname{in}_{\mathbf1}(*))$, composing the two paths gives the desired path.
        \item For the $R$ case, we proceed by a second layer of induction.
        \begin{itemize}
            \item For the $(K,p):B\Sigma_2\times P$ case, we have $\operatorname{glue}_D(K,p)^{-1}:\operatorname{in}_R(\operatorname{in}_{B\Sigma_2\times P}(K,p))=\operatorname{in}_Pp$, $\operatorname{glue}_D(\mathbf{2},p):\operatorname{in}_Pp=\operatorname{in}_R(\operatorname{in}_{B\Sigma_2\times P}(\mathbf{2},p))$ and $\mathit{ap}_{\operatorname{in}_{R}}(\operatorname{glue}_R p):\operatorname{in}_R(\operatorname{in}_{B\Sigma_2\times P}(\mathbf{2},p))=\linebreak\operatorname{in}_R(\operatorname{in}_{\mathbf1}(*))$, composing the three paths gives the desired paths.
            \item For the $\mathbf{1}$ case, the path is $\operatorname{refl}$.
            \item For the glue case, we have to prove an equality of form $(\mathit{ap}_f(r_2))^{-1}=r_1^{-1}\cdot r_1\cdot\mathit{ap}_f(r_2^{-1})$, which is true by path induction.
        \end{itemize}
        \item The glue case follows by commutativity of action on paths and the path inverse.
    \end{itemize}

    Now all that is left is to prove that $Q(z_0)$ is contractible. Since the right side is a proposition, it suffices to prove that $\operatorname{inl}\operatorname{refl}_{w_0}=_{Q(z_0)}\operatorname{inl} p$ for any $p:w_0=w_0$. Now we can treat an inhabitant of $w_0=w_0$ as an equivalence $\mathbf{2}\simeq \mathbf{2}$ commuting with the labelling functions. If this equivalence is the identity, $p$ is exactly $\operatorname{refl}_{w_0}$. If it is the unique non-identity equivalence (the swap), by the commutation relation we get $x_0=x_1$, which inhabits $P$ and contracts the join.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{l:coleq}
    If $\operatorname{col}(a,b)=\operatorname{col}(a',b')$, then $a=a'$ and $b=b'$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The path $\operatorname{col}(a,b)=\operatorname{col}(a',b')$ in $X^3\mathbin{/\!/} \Sigma_3$ is an equivalence $e:\mathbf{2}\sqcup\mathbf{1}\simeq\mathbf{2}\sqcup\mathbf{1}$ commuting with the labelling functions. If $e(\operatorname{inr}*)=\operatorname{inr}*$, then $e$ sends $\operatorname{inl}\cdot$ to $\operatorname{inl}\cdot$, and by its commutation we get $b=b'$ and $a=a'$. If $e(\operatorname{inr}*)=\operatorname{inl} y$ for some $y:\mathbf{2}$, we get $b=a'$. However, there exists an $x:\mathbf{2}$ such that $e(\operatorname{inl} x)=\operatorname{inr}*$, which gives $a=b'$, and $e(\operatorname{inl}(\neg x))=\operatorname{inl}(\neg y)$, which gives $a=a'$.

\end{equation}
Therefore:
\begin{equation}

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{l:PtoD}

By path induction, assume $x_1\equiv x_2\equiv x_0$. Then $w_0=\operatorname{col}(x_0,x_0)$, so $\operatorname{cb}(w_0)$, $J(w_0)$ \linebreak and $\operatorname{ColR}$ all hold. <!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{l:Wprop}
    Assume $w_0=\operatorname{col}(x,y)$. Then $W:X^3\mathbin{/\!/} \Sigma_3\to\mathcal{U}$ defined as:
    \begin{equation}
        W(u):\equiv\left(\sum_{K:B\Sigma_2}\psi(K,x,y)=u\right)*\left((x=y)\times (\mathrm{proj}_2u=\lambda\_.x)\right)
    \end{equation}
    is proposition-valued.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Assume for $u:X^3\mathbin{/\!/} \Sigma_3$ we have a $w:W(u)$, we prove $W(u)$ is contractible by induction on $w$, omitting the glue case. The right side of the join is a proposition, so if it is inhabited, the whole join is contractible. Now say we have a $(K,p):\sum_{K:B\Sigma_2}\psi(K,x,y)=u$, by induction on $p$ we can assume $\psi(K,x,y)\equiv u$. We set the center of contraction to $\operatorname{inl}(K,\operatorname{refl})$, and we just need to find:
    \begin{equation}
        \prod_{(K',p'):\sum_{K':B\Sigma_2}\psi(K',x,y)=\psi(K,x,y)}\operatorname{inl}(K',p')=_{W(u)}\operatorname{inl}(K,\operatorname{refl})
    \end{equation}
    Now $p'$ can be seen as an equivalence $e:K'\sqcup\mathbf{1}\simeq K\sqcup\mathbf{1}$ commuting with labelling functions. If $e(\operatorname{inr}*)=\operatorname{inr}*$, then $e$ restricts to a path $q:K'=K$ such that $s:\mathit{ap}_{\psi(\cdot,x,y)}(q)=p'$. We get the claim by generalizing over $q$ and $s$ and performing path induction on them. If $e(\operatorname{inr} *)=\operatorname{inl} k$ for some $k:K$, by the commutation relations, similarly to the proof of lemma \ref{l:coleq}, we get $x=y$. Then the right side of the join holds, so the join is contractible by fact \ref{f:joincontr} and we have a path.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{l:colToD}
    Assume $w_0=\operatorname{col}(x,y)$. Then $D$ is contractible.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
For $(u,v):\sum_{u:X^3\mathbin{/\!/} \Sigma_3}\operatorname{cb}(u)$, we can prove that $W(u)$ holds. Indeed, $W(u)$ is a proposition, so we can assume the untruncated $\operatorname{cb}(u)$, which gives us $u=\operatorname{col}(x,y)$, which lies in the left side of the join for $K=\mathbf{2}$. Furthermore, $\operatorname{cb}(w_0)$, $\operatorname{ColR}$ and $J(w_0)$ all hold, so $\rho$ is contractible and $\Lambda\simeq\sum_{u:X^3\mathbin{/\!/} \Sigma_3}J(u)$ by \eqref{eq:stage3}, the equivalence preserves the first coordinate. Composing that with the contraction of $\rho$ centered at the point $(x,y,\dots)$, we get an equivalence of pushout diagrams, presenting the space $L$ as the mapping cone of $F$. We will now prove the contractibility of $D$, choosing the vertex of $L$ as the center. Let $d:D$, proceed by pushout induction on $d$ as an element of the pushout in \eqref{eq:stage1}. The only nontrivial case is when $d$ comes from $L$, because otherwise $P$ holds and by lemma \ref{l:PtoD} $D$ is contractible, so we have a path. Now we need to find a path
    \begin{equation}
        p:\prod_{t:\sum_{u:X^3\mathbin{/\!/} \Sigma_3} \operatorname{cb}(u)}\left(\operatorname{in}_L(\operatorname{in} t)=_D\operatorname{in}_L*\right)
    \end{equation}
    such that $p(F(K))$ is the gluing path. For a given $t=(u,v)$, we proceed by pushout induction on the inhabitant of $W(u)$. Once again, the only nontrivial case is the left one, since otherwise $P$ holds. We therefore have a given $K$ and a path $\psi(K,x,y)=u$. Since $\operatorname{cb}(u)$ is a proposition, we have a path $q:F(K)=t$. By path induction on $q$, we can assume $F(K)\equiv t$ and define $p$ as the gluing path on $F(K)$.

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{l:Dcontr}
    The type $D$ is contractible.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Express $D$ as a pushout resulting from diagrams \eqref{eq:stage1}, \eqref{eq:stage2} and \eqref{eq:stage3}. The center of contraction will then be $c:\equiv \operatorname{in}_L(\operatorname{in}_{\Lambda}(\operatorname{in}_{\mathbf{1}}*))$. We pick a $d:D$ and find paths $c=d$ by iterated pushout induction:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item For the $P$ and the glue case, since $P$ holds, the claim follows by lemma \ref{l:PtoD}.
        \item Let $d=\operatorname{in}_Ll$.

            \item Let $l=\operatorname{in}_\rho r$. Once again the $P$ and the glue case are easy. In the $\operatorname{ColR}$ case, we have $(x,y,c):\operatorname{ColR}$, and once again we can prove $D$ is contractible by eliminating the truncation of $c$ and using lemma \ref{l:colToD}. The glue case for $l$ is analogous, since it also makes $\rho$ hold.
            \item Let $l=\operatorname{in}_\Lambda L$.

                \item Let $L=\operatorname{in}_{\sum_{u:X^3\mathbin{/\!/} \Sigma_3}J(u)}(u,p)$. Then $p:\operatorname{cb}(u)*(P\times \mathrm{proj}_2u=\lambda\_.x_0)$.                 \item Let $L=\operatorname{in}_{\mathbf{1}}*$. Then the path is $\operatorname{refl}$.
            \end{itemize}
        \end{itemize}
    \end{itemize}
    Since in all cases $D$ is contractible or the path is $\operatorname{refl}$, we have constructed a uniform path $\prod_{d:D}c=d$.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{l:Qz0contr}
    The type $Q(z_0)\equiv (w_0=w_0)*(J(w_0))$ is contractible.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The type $Q(z_0)$ is inhabited by $\operatorname{inl}\operatorname{refl}$. Notice $J(w_0)=\operatorname{cb}(w_0)*P$. Since it is a proposition, by fact \ref{f:joincontr} it suffices to prove that for $h:w_0=w_0$, $\operatorname{inl}\operatorname{refl}=_{Q(z_0)}\operatorname{inl} h$. We can interpret $h$ as an equivalence $e:\mathbf{3}\simeq\mathbf{3}$ commuting with the labelling functions. There are three kinds of equivalences, we consider cases based on them.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item If $e$ is an identity, $h=\operatorname{refl}$.
        \item If $e$ is a 3-cycle, by the commutation with labelling we get $x_0=x_1=x_2$, which gives us $P$ and $J(w_0)$.
        \item If $e$ is a transposition, say for $a,b,c:\mathbf{3}$ we have $e(a)=b,e(b)=a,e(c)=c$ and $x_m:\equiv (\mathrm{proj}_2w_0)(m)$ for $m=a,b,c$. Then by the commutation relation we have $x_a=x_b$ and $\lVert w_0=\linebreak\operatorname{col}(x_a,x_c)\rVert$ holds, which gives us $\operatorname{cb}(w_0)$ and $J(w_0)$.\qedhere
    \end{itemize}
