<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:typeI}
If $\mathfrak{u}$ is an ultrafilter on a non-empty collection $D$ of pairwise  disjoint doubletons, then $\mathfrak{D}_\mathfrak{u}$ is a type-I parultrafilter.
Every type-I parultrafilter{} is of this form.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathfrak{u}$ be an ultrafilter on $D$.  
                                                        
Suppose $F \notin \mathfrak{D}_\mathfrak{u}$, i.e.\
$\Sigma_F \notin \mathfrak{u}$.  Then $B := D \setminus \Sigma_F \in \mathfrak{u}$, and 
$F \wedge D_B = \Delta$: each doubleton in $B$ is split by $F$, hence is a
sub-section of $F$, and the singleton cells of $D_B$ are trivially
sub-sections.  Thus $\mathfrak{D}_\mathfrak{u}$ is maximal.  To see that $\mathfrak{D}_\mathfrak{u}$ is of
type~I, choose a section $a$ of $D$ and note that $\{a, a^c\} \wedge D_A = \Delta$ for every $A \subseteq D$, so the finite partition $\{a, a^c\}$ does not belong to $\mathfrak{D}_\mathfrak{u}$.

Conversely, suppose $\mathfrak{F}$ is a type-I parultrafilter.  If a parfilter{}
contains every two-element partition $\{a, a^c\}$ of $X$, then it contains
all finite partitions.  Thus there exist $a \subseteq X$ and $E \in \mathfrak{F}$ such
that $E \wedge \{a, a^c\} = \Delta$.  Then no cell of $E$ has more than two
elements.  Let $D \subseteq E$ be the set of $E$'s non-singleton cells.


{Now, for any partition $F$ of $X$, 
let $\phi(F) = (F \wedge E ) \cap D$. That is, $\phi(F)$ is the set of doubletons of $E$ contained in a cell of $F$. If $F \in \mathfrak{F}$, then as 
$F \cap E \not = \Delta$, such doubletons exist, 
and so $\phi(F) \not = \emptyset$. It is easy to check 
that $\phi(F_1 \wedge F_2) = \phi(F_1) \cap \phi(F_2)$, 
so the image of $\mathfrak{F}$ under $\phi$ is the base for a filter, $\mathfrak{u}$, on $D$. We claim that $\mathfrak{u}$ is an 
ultrafilter on $D$. For any $A \subseteq D$ and $F \in \mathfrak{F}$, we have $D_{A} \wedge F = \Delta$ iff $A \cap \phi(F) = \emptyset$. {Now suppose $A \subseteq D$
with $A \notin \mathfrak{u}$.  Then $D_A \notin \mathfrak{F}$: for $\phi(D_A) = A$ (the
doubletons of $E$ contained in a cell of $D_A$ are exactly those of $A$),
so $D_A \in \mathfrak{F}$ would put $A$ in the base of $\mathfrak{u}$.  By the maximality
criterion of Section~1 there is then some $F \in \mathfrak{F}$ with
$D_A \wedge F = \Delta$, i.e., $A \cap \phi(F) = \emptyset$.} Similarly, if 
$A^{c} = D \setminus A \not \in \mathfrak{u}$, there is some 
$F' \in \mathfrak{F}$ with $D_{A^{c}} \wedge F' = \Delta$. 
But now all the doubletons of $D$ contained 
in cells of $F$ lie in $A^{c}$, while those of 
$F'$ lie in $A$; accordingly, $F \wedge F' \wedge E = \Delta$, a contradiction.}
{Thus $\mathfrak{u}$ is an ultrafilter.  Finally, for every $F \in \mathfrak{F}$ we
have $\phi(F) \in \mathfrak{u}$ and $\phi(F) \subseteq \Sigma_F$, so
$\Sigma_F \in \mathfrak{u}$ and $F \in \mathfrak{D}_\mathfrak{u}$.  Hence
$\mathfrak{F} \subseteq \mathfrak{D}_\mathfrak{u}$, and since $\mathfrak{F}$ is maximal and $\mathfrak{D}_\mathfrak{u}$ is
proper, $\mathfrak{F} = \mathfrak{D}_\mathfrak{u}$.}
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem:uniqueness}
{The representing pair $(D, \mathfrak{u})$ is unique up to restriction. Suppose $\mathfrak{D}_\mathfrak{u} = \mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{u}'}$ for 
ultrafilters $\mathfrak{u}, \mathfrak{u}'$ on sets $D, D'$ of doubletons. 
Then $D \cap D'$ is non-empty, else 
$\Delta = D_{D} \wedge D_{D'} \in \mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{u}} = \mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{u}'}$, a contradiction. 
Let $A \subseteq D$ with $A \in \mathfrak{u}$. Then 
$D_{A} \in \mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{u}} = \mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{u}'}$, so 
for some $B \subseteq D'$ with $B \in \mathfrak{u}'$, 
we have $D_{A} \geq D_{B}$. But then every 
doubleton in $B$ is also a doubleton in $A$, 
i.e., $B \subseteq A$, whence, $A \cap D' \in \mathfrak{u}'$. 
Similarly, every $B \in \mathfrak{u}'$ has 
$B \cap D \in \mathfrak{u}$. Thus, $\mathfrak{u}$ and $\mathfrak{u}'$ have 
the same trace on $D \cap D'$. }
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:heart}
If $\mathfrak{F}$ is a type-II parultrafilter, then $h(\mathfrak{F})$ is a non-principal
ultrafilter on $X$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $a \subseteq X$.  Then $\{a, a^c\} \in \mathfrak{F}$.  Suppose that neither
$\widehat{a}$ nor $\widehat{a^c}$ belongs to $\mathfrak{F}$.  Since $\mathfrak{F}$ is maximal,
there exist partitions $F_1, F_2 \in \mathfrak{F}$ with
$\widehat{a} \wedge F_1 = \widehat{a^c} \wedge F_2 = \Delta$.
But then $\{a, a^c\} \wedge (F_1 \wedge F_2) = \Delta$: each cell of
$F_1 \wedge F_2$ meets $a$ at most once and meets $a^c$ at most once, so its
intersections with the two cells of $\{a,a^c\}$ are singletons or empty.
This contradicts the properness of $\mathfrak{F}$.  
{
Finally, since $\widehat{\{x\}} = \Delta \not \in \mathfrak{F}$ 
for all $x \in X$, $h(\mathfrak{F})$ is non-principal.}
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:splitting}
For any partition $F$, {$\widehat{\mathrm{sing}(F)} \wedge F = \Delta$.}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
The cells of $\widehat s \wedge F$ are the sets $s \cap c$ ($c$ a cell of $F$) together with singletons drawn from $s^c$.  If $c$ is a singleton cell then
$c \subseteq s$ and $s \cap c = c$ is a singleton; if $c$ is not a singleton then $c \cap s = \emptyset$.  So every cell of $\widehat s \wedge F$ is a singleton.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:coloring}
Let $\mathfrak{F}$ be a type-II parultrafilter{} with heart $\mathfrak{u}$, and let $F \in \mathfrak{F}$.  Then
for every $n \geq 1$, $\mathrm{sing}_n(F) \notin \mathfrak{u}$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
First the case $n = 1$.  If $s = \mathrm{sing}(F) \in \mathfrak{u}$ then
$\widehat s \in \mathfrak{F}$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:splitting},
$\widehat s \wedge F = \Delta$ with both meetands in $\mathfrak{F}$, contradicting
properness.

Now suppose $\mathrm{sing}_n(F) \in \mathfrak{u}$ for some $n \geq 2$.  Color the points of
each cell of $F$ of size at most $n$ injectively with the colors
$1, \ldots, n$, and let $E$ be the finite partition of $X$ into the $n$
color classes together with $X \setminus \mathrm{sing}_n(F)$ (omitted if empty).
Since $\mathfrak{F}$ is of type II, $E \in \mathfrak{F}$, so $G := F \wedge E \in \mathfrak{F}$.  {Every cell of $G$ 
meets $\mathrm{sing}_{n}(F)$ in a singleton} 
                                                   
(a cell of size $\leq n$
meets each color class at most once).  Hence
$\mathrm{sing}(G) \supseteq \mathrm{sing}_n(F) \in \mathfrak{u}$, so $\mathrm{sing}(G) \in \mathfrak{u}$,
contradicting the case $n = 1$ applied to $G$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
Let $\mathfrak{u}$ be a non-principal ultrafilter on $X$ and $F$ a partition.  Say $F$ is \textbf{$\mathfrak{u}$-bounded} if $\mathrm{sing}_n(F) \in \mathfrak{u}$ for some $n$, and
\textbf{$\mathfrak{u}$-unbounded} otherwise.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:unbounded}
Every member of a type-II parultrafilter{} is $\mathfrak{u}$-unbounded, where $\mathfrak{u}$ is the
heart.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:compat}
Let $\mathfrak{u}$ be a non-principal ultrafilter on $X$ and $F$ a partition.  Then
$F$ belongs to some type-II parultrafilter{} with heart $\mathfrak{u}$ if and only if no
member of $\mathfrak{u}$ is a sub-section of $F$.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
For $a \subseteq X$ non-empty, $\widehat{a} \wedge F = \Delta$ iff every cell of
$F$ meets $a$ at most once, i.e.\ iff $a$ is a sub-section of $F$.  
{
In this case, no proper parfilter contains both $\widehat{a}$ and $F$. Thus, if $F$ has a subsection $a \in \mathfrak{u}$, no parfilter extending $\widehat{\mathfrak{u}}$, and so, 
no type-II parultrafilter{} with heart $\mathfrak{u}$, contains $F$. 
}

Conversely, 
if no member of $\mathfrak{u}$ is a sub-section of $F$, then $\widehat a \wedge F \neq \Delta$ for every $a \in \mathfrak{u}$. 
{Hence the parfilter{} generated by $\widehat\mathfrak{u} \cup \{F\}$ is proper (its
general element coarsens some $\widehat a \wedge F \neq \Delta$), and by Zorn's
Lemma it extends to an parultrafilter.} {Thus, $F$ belongs to a parultrafilter{} $\mathfrak{F}$ that 
contains $\widehat{\mathfrak{u}}$ (and is thus type~II), 
and $h(\mathfrak{F}) \supseteq \mathfrak{u}$, whence $h(\mathfrak{F}) = \mathfrak{u}$. 
}

\label{ex:fubini}
Let $X = \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$, let $\mathfrak{p}$ and $\mathfrak{q}$ be non-principal ultrafilters
on $\mathbb{N}$, and let $\mathfrak{u} = \mathfrak{p} \otimes \mathfrak{q}$ be their Fubini product:
$a \in \mathfrak{u}$ iff $\{m \mid \{n \mid (m,n) \in a\} \in \mathfrak{q}\} \in \mathfrak{p}$.  Let
$V = \bigl\{\{m\} \times \mathbb{N} \mid m \in \mathbb{N}\bigr\}$ (columns) and
$R = \bigl\{\mathbb{N} \times \{n\} \mid n \in \mathbb{N}\bigr\}$ (rows).  A sub-section
$a$ of $V$ meets each column at most once, so each set
$\{n \mid (m,n) \in a\}$ has at most one element and is not in $\mathfrak{q}$;
hence $a \notin \mathfrak{u}$.  A sub-section $a$ of $R$ meets each row at most
once, so $a = \{(g(n), n) \mid n \in \operatorname{dom} g\}$ for a partial
function $g$ {from row indices to column indices}; for each $m$ the trace of $a$ on the $m$-th column is $\{(m,n) \mid n \in g^{-1}(m)\}$, and the sets $g^{-1}(m)$ are pairwise disjoint, at most one of which can lie in $\mathfrak{q}$, so $\{m \mid \{n \mid (m,n) \in a\} \in \mathfrak{q}\}$ has at most one element and is not in $\mathfrak{p}$; hence $a \notin \mathfrak{u}$.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:compat}, both $V$ and $H$ belong to type-II parultrafilter{}s
with heart $\mathfrak{u}$; but $V \wedge R = \Delta$, so no parultrafilter{} contains both.
(One readily checks that the pushforwards of $\mathfrak{u}$ along $V$ and $R$, in the sense introduced next, are $\mathfrak{p}$ and $\mathfrak{q}$ respectively.)

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:minimal}
A non-principal ultrafilter $\mathfrak{u}$ on $X$ is of minimal type if and only if
$\langle\widehat{\mathfrak{u}}\rangle$ is an parultrafilter.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
First observe that for any partition $F$ with canonical surjection $f$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $F \in \langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle$ iff $F \geq \widehat a$ for some
  $a \in \mathfrak{u}$ iff some cell of $F$ belongs to $\mathfrak{u}$ iff $\beta f(\mathfrak{u})$ is
  principal;
\item for $a \in \mathfrak{u}$, $\widehat a \wedge F = \Delta$ iff $a$ is a sub-section of
  $F$ iff $f$ is injective on $a$.
\end{enumerate}
Since every element of $\langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle$ coarsens some $\widehat a$
($a \in \mathfrak{u}$), and since $G \geq \widehat a$ and $F \wedge G = \Delta$ force
$F \wedge \widehat a = \Delta$, the maximality criterion of Section~1 reads:
$\langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle$ is an parultrafilter{} iff for every partition $F$, either
$\beta f(\mathfrak{u})$ is principal or some $a \in \mathfrak{u}$ is a sub-section of $F$.
By the standard result quoted above, the latter alternative says exactly
that $\beta f(\mathfrak{u})$ has the same type as $\mathfrak{u}$.  
{Thus, $\langle \widehat{\mathfrak{u}} \rangle$ is an 
parultrafilter{} iff every ultrafilter below $\mathfrak{u}$ in the Rudin-Keisler order is either principal or equivalent 
to $\mathfrak{u}$ --- that is, iff $\mathfrak{u}$ is minimal.}

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
\label{rem:selective}
For ultrafilters on a countable set, minimality is equivalent to
selectivity (the Ramsey property); see Booth~\cite{booth} and
\cite{comfort-negrepontis}.  Selective ultrafilters exist under the
Continuum Hypothesis \cite{booth, comfort-negrepontis}, while Kunen showed
that after adding $\aleph_2$ random reals to a model of CH there are none
(see \cite{jech}); so the existence of hearts that determine their
parultrafilter{}s is, for countable $X$, independent of ZFC.  For general $X$: if a
minimal $\mathfrak{u}$ is countably incomplete, then some countable partition of
$X$ has no cell in $\mathfrak{u}$, its pushforward is a non-principal ultrafilter
on a countable set lying below $\mathfrak{u}$, and minimality makes $\mathfrak{u}$
isomorphic to it; so every countably incomplete minimal ultrafilter is
isomorphic to a selective ultrafilter on $\mathbb{N}$.  The countably complete
case is bound up with measurable cardinals, and we do not pursue it here.
In uniform language, Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal} says that the filter
uniformity of $\mathfrak{u}$ is an atom of the lattice of \emph{zero-dimensional}
uniformities iff $\mathfrak{u}$ is {Rudin-Keisler} minimal; compare
\cite[Proposition~2.3]{prrs1}, where the same uniformity is shown to be
an atom of the lattice of \emph{all} uniformities, for $X$ of
non-measurable cardinality, iff $\mathfrak{u}$ is selective in the partition sense
used there.

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
\label{rem:baumgartner}
We record here, without proof, two assertions that we believe deserve
further study: if a type-II parultrafilter{} $\mathfrak{F}$ is recovered from
$\langle\widehat{h(\mathfrak{F})}\rangle$ by adjoining a single partition, then
$h(\mathfrak{F})$ dominates a minimal ultrafilter with no type strictly between;
and, as James Baumgartner observed to us, this situation occurs when
$h(\mathfrak{F})$ is the product of a minimal ultrafilter with itself.  Under CH, the situation contemplated here does
occur: for any prescribed ultrafilter $\mathcal G$ on $\omega$,
Pelant--Reiterman--R\"odl--Simon construct hearts above $\mathcal G$ in
the Rudin--Keisler order admitting exactly $s$ type-II parultrafilter{}s
($1 \leq s < \omega$), each obtained from $\langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle$ by
adjoining a single partition \cite[Theorem~4.1]{prrs2}; a heart admitting
exactly one, which is \emph{not} so obtainable
\cite[Theorem~4.2]{prrs2}; and a heart admitting $2^{\mathfrak c}$ of
them \cite[Theorem~4.3]{prrs2}.  (Their counts concern atoms of the full
lattice of uniformities, but since the atoms in question are
zero-dimensional and every parultrafilter, viewed as a uniformity, is refined by
some atom, the counts transfer to parultrafilter{}s.)

<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:trichotomy}
Let $\mathfrak{u} \in X^{*}$ and let $F$ be a partition of $X$, with
$\mathfrak{p} = \beta f(\mathfrak{u})$ and $K = f^*(\mathfrak{u})$.  Exactly one of the following
holds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\alph*)}]
\item Some cell $c$ of $F$ belongs to $\mathfrak{u}$.  Then $c$ is infinite,
  $K = c^*$, and $K$ has no isolated points.
\item No cell of $F$ belongs to $\mathfrak{u}$, and $F$ is $\mathfrak{u}$-bounded.  Then
  $K = T$ is finite; if $\mathfrak{p}$-almost all cells of $F$ have exactly $k$ elements, then $\operatorname{\#}(K) = k$.  In particular
  $K = \{\mathfrak{u}\}$ iff $\mathrm{sing}(F) \in \mathfrak{u}$.
\item No cell of $F$ belongs to $\mathfrak{u}$, and $F$ is $\mathfrak{u}$-unbounded.  Then
  $T$ is infinite and discrete, its points all of type $\mathfrak{p}$, and
  $K = \overline{T}$; the isolated points of $K$ are exactly $T$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
(a) Here $\mathfrak{p}$ is principal {at some cell} $c \in F$, {and as $c \in \mathfrak{u}$ with $\mathfrak{u}$ non-principal, $c$ is infinite.} 
                                                             
An ultrafilter $\mathfrak{v}$ agrees with $\mathfrak{u}$ on $F$ iff
$c \in \mathfrak{v}$, so $K = c^*$.  For any $\mathfrak{v} \in c^*$ and $a \in \mathfrak{v}$, the set
$a \cap c$ lies in $\mathfrak{v}$ and is infinite; splitting it into two infinite 
halves produces two distinct points of $c^*$ inside $\overline a$, so no point is isolated.

For (b) and (c), $\mathfrak{p}$ is non-principal and the lemmas above apply. 

(b) Choose $n$ and $A_0 \in \mathfrak{p}$ with every cell in $A_0$ of size at most
$n$; label the points of each such cell injectively with
$1, \ldots, n$.  Each section $j$ {of $F$ [?] then}  determines a label function
$A_0 \to \{1, \ldots, n\}$. Since $\mathfrak{p}$ is an ultrafilter, exactly one 
label value $i$ occurs on a $\mathfrak{p}$-large set of cells, and two sections with
the same such value agree (as selections) on a $\mathfrak{p}$-large set of cells,
whence $\mathfrak{k}[j] = \mathfrak{k}[j']$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:threefaces}.  Thus
$\operatorname{\#}(T) \leq n$. Being finite, $T$ is closed, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:density}, $K = \overline{T} = T$.  If the sizes {of the cells in $A_o$} are exactly
$k$ $\mathfrak{p}$-almost everywhere, the $k$ constant-label sections pairwise disagree on a $\mathfrak{p}$-large set, giving $\operatorname{\#}(K) = k$ exactly.  Finally
$\mathrm{sing}(F) \in \mathfrak{u}$ iff the cells {in $A_o$} are singletons $\mathfrak{p}$-almost everywhere
iff $k = 1$ iff $K = \{\mathfrak{u}\}$ ({noting} $\mathfrak{u} \in K$ always).

(c) $\mathfrak{u}$-unboundedness says that for each $m$, the set $N_m$ of cells with at least $m$ elements lies in $\mathfrak{p}$.  Fix $m$ and choose sections
$j_1, \ldots, j_m$ pairwise disagreeing on every cell of $N_m$ (possible since such cells have at least $m$ points).  For $i \neq l$, the agreement
set of $j_i, j_l$ misses $N_m \in \mathfrak{p}$, so by
Proposition~\ref{prop:threefaces} the $\mathfrak{k}[j_i]$ are pairwise distinct. 
Hence $\operatorname{\#}(T) \geq m$ for all $m$: $T$ is infinite.  Discreteness, common type, $K = \overline T$ (density plus closedness of $K$), and the
identification of the isolated points are
Lemmas~\ref{lem:sections},~\ref{lem:density} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:threefaces}.

only if $\mathrm{sing}(F) \in \mathfrak{u}$.

<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A set $K \subseteq X^{*}$ is \textbf{sparse} iff $K$ is the closure of an
infinite discrete set of ultrafilters of a common type.  (We avoid the word ``rare,'' which has an established, different meaning for ultrafilters \cite[1.6]{prrs2}.)

<!-- corollary-3: body_md -->
\label{cor:members-sparse}
Suppose no cell of $F$ lies in $\mathfrak{u}$ and $F$ is $\mathfrak{u}$-unbounded.  Then {the fiber} $f^*(\mathfrak{u})$ is sparse.  In particular, if $\mathfrak{F}$ is a type-II parultrafilter{} with heart $\mathfrak{u}$ and $F \in \mathfrak{F}$, then either $F \in \langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle$ (equivalently, some cell of $F$ lies in $\mathfrak{u}$) and $f^*(\mathfrak{u}) = c^*$ for
that cell, or $f^*(\mathfrak{u})$ is sparse.
<!-- end corollary-3 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
The first statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}(c) together with the
definition of sparseness.  For the second: the equivalence
$F \in \langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle \iff$ some cell of $F$ lies in $\mathfrak{u}$ was
observed in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal}; when it fails, $F$ is
$\mathfrak{u}$-unbounded by Corollary~\ref{cor:unbounded}, and the first statement
applies.  (We take no position on whether the growths $c^*$ arising in the
first alternative are themselves sparse.)

\label{prop:sparse-not-fiber}

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:positive}
Let $\mathfrak{F}$ be a type-II parultrafilter{} with heart $\mathfrak{u}$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\arabic*)}]
  \item $(f \wedge g)^*(\mathfrak{u}) \subseteq f^*(\mathfrak{u}) \cap g^*(\mathfrak{u})$ for all
    $F, G$; consequently $\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{F})$ is downward directed under
    inclusion.
  \item For all $f \in \mathfrak{F}$, $f^*(\mathfrak{u}) \neq \{\mathfrak{u}\}$.
  \item $\bigcap \mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{F}) = \{\mathfrak{u}\}$: the family $\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{F})$ converges
    properly to the heart, which is therefore recoverable from it.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-14: body_md -->
(1) If $\mathfrak{v}$ agrees with $\mathfrak{u}$ on $F \wedge G$ it agrees with $\mathfrak{u}$ on
every coarsening of $F \wedge G$, in particular on $F$ and on $G$
(pushforwards factor through the canonical surjections
$F \wedge G \to F$, $F \wedge G \to G$).  Directedness follows since
$f \wedge g \in \mathfrak{F}$ whenever $f, g \in \mathfrak{F}$.

(2) By Lemma~\ref{lem:coloring}, $\mathrm{sing}(F) \notin \mathfrak{u}$; apply
Corollary~\ref{cor:singleton-fiber}.

(3) For $a \in \mathfrak{u}$ we have $\widehat a \in \mathfrak{F}$, and the fiber of $\widehat a$ at
$\mathfrak{u}$ is computed directly: any ultrafilter containing $a$ has pushforward
along $\widehat a$ equal to the principal ultrafilter at the cell $a$, while
an ultrafilter omitting $a$ has a pushforward omitting $\{a\}$; since
$a \in \mathfrak{u}$, an ultrafilter $\mathfrak{v} \in X^{*}$ agrees with $\mathfrak{u}$
on $\widehat a$ iff $a \in \mathfrak{v}$.  So
$\widehat a^*(\mathfrak{u}) = a^*$, and
$\bigcap \mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{F}) \subseteq \bigcap_{a \in \mathfrak{u}} a^* = \{\mathfrak{v} \in X^{*} \mid
\mathfrak{u} \subseteq \mathfrak{v}\} = \{\mathfrak{u}\}$.  Conversely, $\mathfrak{u}$ lies in every fiber $f^*(\mathfrak{u})$.

$u, \sigma(u)$ lies in $\mathfrak{w}$; if $u \in \mathfrak{w}$ then, as $\sigma$ is an
involution, $u \in \sigma\mathfrak{w}$ would mean $\sigma(u) \in \mathfrak{w}$, {which is} impossible; and symmetrically if $\sigma(u) \in \mathfrak{w}$.  Finally
$V \wedge R = \Delta$, and $\Delta^*(\mathfrak{w}) = \{\mathfrak{w}\}$ by
Corollary~\ref{cor:singleton-fiber} (every cell of $\Delta$ is a
singleton).

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:conditionR}
The configuration of Proposition~\ref{prop:meet-vs-cap} is tied to a
property of ultrafilters isolated in \cite[Section~3]{prrs1}: say that
$\mathfrak{u}$ satisfies \emph{condition (R)} if any two self-maps of $X$ with the
same pushforward along $\mathfrak{u}$ agree on a member of $\mathfrak{u}$.  The ultrafilter
$\mathfrak{w}$ above violates (R) as badly as possible: the two coordinate
projections (composed with a fixed bijection $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N} \times \{0\}
\subseteq X$, say) have the same pushforward $\mathfrak{q}$, yet agree only on the
diagonal, which is not in $\mathfrak{w}$.  Pelant--Reiterman--R\"odl--Simon show
(R) to be strictly weaker than selectivity and equivalent to
$0$-proximal fineness of the associated atom \cite[Section~3]{prrs1}.
Whether Problem~\ref{prob:injective} below has a positive answer for
hearts satisfying (R) --- for which the pathology above cannot arise in
this form --- we do not know.

\label{lem:containment}
Let $F, G$ be partitions and $\mathfrak{u} \in X^{*}$, with $\mathfrak{p} = \beta f(\mathfrak{u})$

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:generators}
For $[g] \in M$ the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)}]
  \item $[g]$ is $\mathfrak{u}$-injective;
  \item the substructure generated by $[g]$ is all of $M$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, a substructure of $M$ is proper if and only if it contains
no $\mathfrak{u}$-injective class.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-18: body_md -->
The substructure generated by $[g]$ is $\{[k \circ g] \mid k \colon X^{n}
\to X, ~{n \in \mathbb{N}}\}$: indeed $h \circ (g, \ldots, g) = (h \circ \Delta_n) \circ g$,
where $\Delta_n$ is the diagonal map $x \mapsto (x, \ldots, x)$.  If $g$
is injective on $a \in \mathfrak{u}$, then given any $h \colon X \to X$ define
$k$ on $g[a]$ by $k(g(x)) = h(x)$ and arbitrarily elsewhere; then
$k \circ g = h$ on $a$, so $[h] = [k \circ g]$.  As every element of $M$
is the class of a self-map, this proves (ii).  Conversely, if (ii)
holds, then $[\mathrm{id}] = [k \circ g]$ for some $k$, so
$\mathrm{id} = k \circ g$ on some $a \in \mathfrak{u}$, forcing $g$ to be
injective on~$a$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:welldef}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\arabic*)}]
  \item If {$[g] = [g']$,} 
                                  
  then for every parfilter{} $\mathfrak{F}$
    with $\widehat\mathfrak{u} \subseteq \mathfrak{F}$:\; $G_g \in \mathfrak{F}$ iff $G_{g'} \in \mathfrak{F}$.
  \item If $G_g = G_{g'}$, then for every substructure $S$ of $M$,\;
    $[g] \in S$ iff $[g'] \in S$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-19: body_md -->
(1) Say $g = g'$ on $a \in \mathfrak{u}$.  The cells of $G_g \wedge \widehat a$ are
the non-empty fibers of $g{\restriction}a$ together with singletons drawn
from $a^c$; hence $G_g \wedge \widehat a = G_{g'} \wedge \widehat a$.  Since
$\widehat a \in \mathfrak{F}$, we have $F \in \mathfrak{F}$ iff $F \wedge \widehat a \in \mathfrak{F}$, for
every partition $F$ (upward closure one way, closure under meets the
other). Apply this to $F = G_g$ and to $F = G_{g'}$.

(2) If $g, g'$ have the same fibers, {let $\tau_o : \operatorname{im}(g) \rightarrow \operatorname{im}(g')$ be the bijection taking $y \in \operatorname{im}(g)$ to the unique element of $g'(g^{-1}(y))$)} 
                                                                                                                  
Extending $\tau_0$ arbitrarily to
$\tau \colon X \to X$ gives $g' = \tau \circ g$, whence
{if $[g] \in S$,} $[g'] = \tau^M([g]) \in S$.

<!-- theorem-6: body_md -->
\label{thm:ultrapower}
Let $\mathfrak{u}$ be a non-principal ultrafilter on the infinite set $X$.  The
assignments
\begin{align*}
  S(\mathfrak{F}) &\;=\; \bigl\{[g] \in M \mid G_g \in \mathfrak{F}\bigr\},\\
  \mathfrak{F}(S) &\;=\; \bigl\{F \in \mathbb{P}(X) \mid [g] \in S
    \text{ for some (equivalently, every) realization $g$ of } F\bigr\}
\end{align*}
are mutually inverse bijections between the type-II parultrafilter{}s $\mathfrak{F}$ with
heart $\mathfrak{u}$ and the maximal proper substructures $S$ of
$M = X^X\!/\mathfrak{u}$.
<!-- end theorem-6 -->
<!-- proof-20: body_md -->
Both assignments are well defined by Lemma~\ref{lem:welldef} (part (1)
applies since $\mathfrak{u}$ is the heart of $\mathfrak{F}$; part (2) gives the
parenthetical equivalence).

\emph{$S(\mathfrak{F})$ is a proper substructure.}  If $[g_1], \ldots, [g_n] \in
S(\mathfrak{F})$ and $h \colon X^n \to X$, put $g = h \circ (g_1, \ldots, g_n)$;
two points identified by every $g_i$ are identified by $g$, so
$G_g \geq G_{g_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge G_{g_n} \in \mathfrak{F}$ and {thus} 
$[g] \in S(\mathfrak{F})$.               
{To see that $S(\mathfrak{F})$ is proper, note 
that} if {$S(\mathfrak{F}) = M$, then by Lemma~\ref{lem:generators}, some} $[g] \in S(\mathfrak{F})$ 
{would be}                                 
injective on some $a \in \mathfrak{u}$. {But} then $a$ would be a sub-section of
$G_g$, so $\widehat a \wedge G_g = \Delta$ with both meetands in $\mathfrak{F}$, {which is impossible}. 
                                                        

\emph{$S(\mathfrak{F})$ is maximal.}  Let $S'$ be a substructure with $S' \supsetneq S(\mathfrak{F})$, and pick $[g] \in S' \setminus S(\mathfrak{F})$.  Then $G_g \notin \mathfrak{F}$, so by maximality of $\mathfrak{F}$ there is {some} $E \in \mathfrak{F}$ with $E \wedge G_g = \Delta$.  Fix a realization $e$ of $E$ (so $[e] \in S(\mathfrak{F}) \subseteq S'$) and a pairing bijection $p \colon X \times X \to X$ (one exists, $X$ being infinite).  Since $E \wedge G_g = \Delta$, the map
$(e, g) {: X \to X \times X}$ is injective, hence so is $p \circ (e, g)$; and
$[p \circ (e, g)] = p^M([e], [g]) \in S'$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:generators}, $S' = M$.  Thus no proper substructure strictly contains $S(\mathfrak{F})$.

\emph{$\mathfrak{F}(S)$ is a proper parfilter{} extending $\widehat\mathfrak{u}$.}  Upward closure: 
if $F \leq F'$ and $g$ realizes $F$, choose for each cell of $F'$ a
representative and let $\sigma$ send each value $g(x)$ to the
representative of the $F'$-cell of $x$ (well defined since $F$ refines
$F'$, and extended arbitrarily off $\operatorname{im} g$); then
$\sigma \circ g$ realizes $F'$ and
$[\sigma \circ g] = \sigma^M([g]) \in S$.  Meets: if $g_F, g_G$ realize
$F, G$, then $p \circ (g_F, g_G)$  realizes $F \wedge G$, and its class lies in $S$.  Properness: a realization of $\Delta$ is injective, and $S$,
being proper, contains no $\mathfrak{u}$-injective class
(Lemma~\ref{lem:generators}).  Non-emptiness: constants realize 
$\{X\}$.  The heart: for $b \in \mathfrak{u}$, {any}     
map fixing $X \setminus b$
pointwise and collapsing $b$ to a point of $b$ realizes $\widehat b$ and is
$\mathfrak{u}$-almost-everywhere constant, so its class is a constant class and lies in $S$; hence $\widehat b \in \mathfrak{F}(S)$.  For $b \notin \mathfrak{u}$, the same map
is $\mathfrak{u}$-almost-everywhere the identity, so $\widehat b \in \mathfrak{F}(S)$ would
put $[\mathrm{id}]$ in $S$, {contradicting 
the properness of $S$.}  Thus $\{a \mid \widehat a \in \mathfrak{F}(S)\} = \mathfrak{u}$, and since every finite partition has a cell in $\mathfrak{u}$, $\mathfrak{F}(S)$ contains
$\mathbb{P}_0(X)$.


\emph{$\mathfrak{F}(S)$ is maximal} (hence a type-II parultrafilter{} with heart $\mathfrak{u}$, by the preceding paragraph).  Suppose $G \notin \mathfrak{F}(S)$, with realization
$g$, so $[g] \notin S$.  The substructure generated by $S \cup \{[g]\}$
consists of the classes $h^M([e_1], \ldots, [e_m], [g], \ldots, [g])$ with $[e_1], \ldots, [e_m] \in S$ (a single application of an operation suffices, since
compositions of operations are operations); by maximality of $S$ it is
improper, hence equals $M$ and contains $[\mathrm{id}]$:
\[
  \mathrm{id} \;=\; h \circ (e_1, \ldots, e_m, g, \ldots, g)
  \quad\text{on some } a \in \mathfrak{u},
\]
with $[e_i] \in S$ (possibly $m = 0$).  On $a$, then, the point $x$ is
determined by the values $e_1(x), \ldots, e_m(x), g(x)$; that is, $a$ is
a sub-section of $E_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge E_m \wedge G$, where
$E_i := G_{e_i} \in \mathfrak{F}(S)$.  Consequently
$(\widehat a \wedge E_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge E_m) \wedge G = \Delta$, and the
left meetand belongs to $\mathfrak{F}(S)$.  By the maximality criterion of
Section~1, $\mathfrak{F}(S)$ is an parultrafilter.


\emph{Mutual inverses.}  $\mathfrak{F}(S(\mathfrak{F})) = \mathfrak{F}$ is immediate from the
definitions, and $S(\mathfrak{F}(S)) = S$ follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:welldef}(2).

<!-- corollary-4: body_md -->
\label{cor:diagonal}
                          
The canonical (diagonal) copy of $X$ in its
ultrapower            
{is} a maximal proper substructure of $X^X\!/\mathfrak{u}$ if and
only if $\mathfrak{u}$ is minimal in the Rudin--Keisler order.
<!-- end corollary-4 -->
<!-- proof-21: body_md -->
The formula defining $S(\cdot)$ makes sense for any parfilter{} extending
$\widehat\mathfrak{u}$, and for $\langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle$ it yields exactly the constant
classes: $G_g \in \langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle$ iff some cell of $G_g$ lies in

$g$ is constant on a member of $\mathfrak{u}$ iff $[g]$ is a constant class.  If
the constants form a maximal proper substructure $S$, then
$\mathfrak{F}(S) = \langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle$ by the same computation, so
$\langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle$ is an parultrafilter{} by
Theorem~\ref{thm:ultrapower}, and $\mathfrak{u}$ is minimal by
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal}.  Conversely, if $\mathfrak{u}$ is minimal, then
$\langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle$ is an parultrafilter{} and
$S(\langle\widehat\mathfrak{u}\rangle)$ --- the constants --- is a maximal proper
substructure.
