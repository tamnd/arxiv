\label{prop:scalar}
Let \(\alpha\colon X{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig} Y\), \(\beta\colon Y{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig} Z\), and \(\gamma\colon X{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig} Z\) be arbitrary \(Q\)-relations. For all \(q\in Q\), the following identities hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label={\rm(\arabic*)}]
\item \((q\&\beta)\circ\alpha=\beta\circ(q\&\alpha)=q\&(\beta\circ\alpha)\);
\item \((q\&\beta)\searrow\gamma=q\rightarrow(\beta\searrow\gamma)=\beta\searrow(q\rightarrow\gamma)\);
\item \(\gamma\swarrow(q\&\alpha)=q\rightarrow(\gamma\swarrow\alpha)=(q\rightarrow\gamma)\swarrow\alpha\).
\end{enumerate}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:yoneda}
For any \(Q\)-category \(\mathbb{A}\), all objects \(x\in\mathbb{A}_0\), presheaves \(\lambda\in\mathcal{P}\mathbb{A}\), and copresheaves \(\rho\in\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\mathbb{A}\),
\begin{equation*}
\lambda(x)=\mathcal{P}\mathbb{A}(Y_{\mathbb{A}}x,\lambda),\qquad
\rho(x)=\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}\mathbb{A}(\rho,Y_{\mathbb{A}}^\dagger x).
\end{equation*}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->

The correspondence \((\mathbb{A}\xrightarrow{f}\mathbb{B})\mapsto(\mathbb{B}^\mathrm{op}\xrightarrow{f^\natural}\mathbb{A}^\mathrm{op})\) yields an isomorphism between \({Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}\) and its opposite category \({Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}^\mathrm{op}\). \label{ex:discrete}
For any set \(X\), all maps \(\lambda\colon X\to Q\) serve simultaneously as presheaves and copresheaves on the discrete \(Q\)-category \(\delta_X\). The associated cocomplete \(Q\)-categories \(\mathcal{P}\delta_X\) and \(\mathcal{P}^\dagger\delta_X\) share the same underlying set \(Q^X\) but carry dual hom structures and orderings. \label{prop:adjoint-char}

\begin{enumerate}[label={\rm(\arabic*)}]
\item \(F\) is a left adjoint if and only if it preserves scalar actions \(F(q\cdot x)=q\cdot F(x)\) and arbitrary joins.

\label{prop:qrel-enriched}
The quantaloid \(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel}\) of \(Q\)-relations is a \({Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}\)-enriched category.
<!-- end proposition-6 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
As a quantaloid, \(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel}\) is naturally \(\mathbf{Sup}\)-enriched: each hom-set \(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel}(X,Y)\) constitutes a complete lattice, and relational composition preserves arbitrary joins in both variables. To establish \({Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}\)-enrichment, it suffices to equip every hom-set with a compatible \(Q\)-module action.

We endow each \(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel}(X,Y)\) with the standard scalar multiplication action \(q\cdot\alpha = q\,\&\,\alpha\). For any composable \(Q\)-relations \(\alpha\colon X{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig} Y\), \(\beta\colon Y{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig} Z\) and any scalar \(q\in Q\), direct computation yields
\[
\beta\circ(q\cdot\alpha) =\beta\circ(q\,\&\,\alpha) = q\,\&\,\left(\beta\circ\alpha\right) = q\cdot\left(\beta\circ\alpha\right).
\]
The symmetric identity \((q\cdot\beta)\circ\alpha = q\cdot(\beta\circ\alpha)\) holds by identical reasoning.

Therefore, relational composition commutes with the scalar \(Q\)-module action on both sides, which verifies that \(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel}\) is a \({Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}\)-enriched category.

<!-- proposition-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:b-enriched}
The category $\mathbf{B}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})$ is ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-enriched.
<!-- end proposition-8 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
As a quantaloid, $\mathbf{B}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})$ is inherently $\mathbf{Sup}$-enriched: every hom-set forms a complete lattice, and the composition operation preserves arbitrary local joins in both arguments. To upgrade this enrichment to ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-enrichment, it suffices to equip each hom-set with a compatible $Q$-action and verify that composition commutes with this action in both variables.

First, we define a canonical $Q$-action on the hom-sets of $\mathbf{B}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})$. For any morphism $f\in\mathbf{B}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})(\alpha,\beta)$ and any scalar $q\in Q$, we set
\[
q\cdot f=q\rightarrow f,
\]
where $\rightarrow$ denotes the scalar implication operation on $Q$-relations. We now confirm that $q\cdot f$ is indeed a morphism in $\mathbf{B}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})(\alpha,\beta)$. Since there exist $Q$-relations $u,v$ such that $f=\alpha\swarrow u=v\searrow\beta$, applying the compatibility between scalar implication and meets of $Q$-relations (Proposition \ref{prop:scalar}), we compute
\[
\alpha\swarrow(q\& u)=q\rightarrow(\alpha\swarrow u)=q\cdot f
=q\rightarrow(v\searrow\beta)=(q\& v)\searrow\beta.
\]
This shows that $q\cdot f$ is a back diagonal morphism. Thus, the assignment $(q,f)\mapsto q\cdot f$ yields a well-defined $Q$-action on each hom-set of $\mathbf{B}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})$, endowing every hom-set with the structure of a cocomplete $Q$-category.

Next, we prove that the composition operation $\bullet$ preserves the $Q$-action in both arguments, making it a ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-bimorphism. Consider composable morphisms $f\in\mathbf{B}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})(\alpha,\beta)$ and $g\in\mathbf{B}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})(\beta,\gamma)$. Direct calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(q\cdot g)\bullet f
&=(\beta\swarrow f)\searrow(q\cdot g)\\
&=(\beta\swarrow f)\searrow(q\rightarrow g)\\
&=(q\&(\beta\swarrow f))\searrow g\\
&=q\rightarrow((\beta\swarrow f)\searrow g)\\
&=q\cdot(g\bullet f).
\end{aligned}
\]
By duality, the identity $g\bullet(q\cdot f)=q\cdot(g\bullet f)$ also holds. These identities confirm that composition commutes with the scalar $Q$-action on both sides. Therefore, $\mathbf{B}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})$ is a ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-enriched category as desired.

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
For any $Q$-context $(X,Y,\alpha)$, there exists a canonical isomorphism of $Q$-categories
\[
\mathfrak{B}(X,Y,\alpha)\cong\mathfrak{B}\alpha.
\]
Consequently, the formal concept lattice construction $(X,Y,\alpha)\mapsto\mathfrak{B}(X,Y,\alpha)$ is functorial over the category ${\mathbf{B}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}^\mathrm{op}$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $(\lambda,\sigma)$ be any formal concept of $(X,Y,\alpha)$. Direct computation yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
((\tau\swarrow\sigma)\searrow\tau)(x)
&=\bigwedge_{q\in Q} 
   {\hbox{$\left(\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}(\sigma(x)\rightarrow\tau(q))\rightarrow\tau(q) 
   {\hbox{$\left)\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}\\
&=\bigwedge_{q\in Q} 
   {\hbox{$\left(\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}(\sigma(x)\rightarrow q)\rightarrow q 
   {\hbox{$\left)\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}\\
&=\sigma(x)
\end{aligned}
\]
for all $x\in X$, which establishes the identity $(\tau\swarrow\sigma)\searrow\tau=\sigma$. By the formal concept conditions $\sigma=\alpha\swarrow\lambda$ and $\lambda=\sigma\searrow\alpha$, we obtain $\sigma=\alpha\swarrow(\sigma\searrow\alpha)$. Thus, $\sigma\in\mathfrak{B}\alpha$.

Conversely, for any $\sigma\in\mathfrak{B}\alpha$, define $\lambda=\sigma\searrow\alpha$. The fixed-point condition for $\mathfrak{B}\alpha$ guarantees $\sigma=\alpha\swarrow\lambda$, so that $(\lambda,\sigma)$ is a formal concept of $(X,Y,\alpha)$. This yields a bijection between the objects of $\mathfrak{B}(X,Y,\alpha)$ and $\mathfrak{B}\alpha$.

For all \(\sigma,\rho\in\mathfrak{B}\alpha\), the hom function of \(\mathfrak{B}\alpha\) is given by
\[
\mathfrak{B}\alpha(\sigma,\rho)
=\bigvee\{q\in Q\mid q\rightarrow\sigma\geq\rho\}
=\rho\searrow\sigma,
\]
which coincides with the hom function of the \(Q\)-category \(\mathfrak{B}(X,Y,\alpha)\).

The object bijection together with identical hom functions induces a canonical $Q$-category isomorphism $\mathfrak{B}(X,Y,\alpha)\cong\mathfrak{B}\alpha$. The functoriality of the formal concept lattice construction follows immediately from the functoriality of $\mathfrak{B}$.

<!-- proposition-10: body_md -->
\label{prop:d-enriched}
The category $\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})$ is ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-enriched.
<!-- end proposition-10 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
As a quantaloid, $\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})$ is $\mathbf{Sup}$-enriched, with complete lattice hom-sets and bilaterally join-preserving composition. To establish ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-enrichment, we verify the existence of a compatible $Q$-action on all hom-sets and confirm that composition preserves this action bilaterally.

For any morphism $f\in\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})(\alpha,\beta)$ and scalar $q\in Q$, we define the $Q$-action by
\[
q\cdot f=q\& f,
\]
i.e., the scalar multiplication of $q$ and $f$. We verify that $q\cdot f$ remains a diagonal morphism. Using the morphism factorization property, there exist $Q$-relations $u,v$ such that $f=v\circ\alpha=\beta\circ u$. By Proposition \ref{prop:scalar}(1), we compute
\[
(q\& v)\circ\alpha=q\&(v\circ\alpha)=q\& f=q\&(\beta\circ u)=\beta\circ(q\& u).
\]
This factorization confirms that $q\cdot f$ satisfies the diagonal morphism condition, so the assignment $(q,f)\mapsto q\cdot f$ defines a valid $Q$-action on each hom-set of $\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})$, equipping each hom-set with a cocomplete $Q$-category structure.

Next, we verify the compatibility of composition with the $Q$-action. For composable morphisms $f\in\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})(\alpha,\beta)$ and $g\in\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})(\beta,\gamma)$, direct calculation yields
\[
(q\cdot g){\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,} f=(q\& g)\circ(\beta\searrow f)
=q\&(g\circ(\beta\searrow f))=q\cdot(g{\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,} f).
\]
The dual identity
\[
g{\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,}(q\cdot f)=(g\swarrow\beta)\circ(q\& f)
=q\&((g\swarrow\beta)\circ f)=q\cdot(g{\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,} f)
\]
holds symmetrically. These equalities demonstrate that the diagonal composition operation commutes with the scalar $Q$-action in both arguments. Therefore, $\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})$ is a ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-enriched category as desired.

\label{prop:faithful}

<!-- proposition-13: body_md -->
\label{prop:tensor-compat}
Let $\alpha\colon X{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig} Y$, $\beta\colon Y{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig} Z$ and $\alpha'\colon X'{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig} Y'$, $\beta'\colon Y'{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig} Z'$ be composable $Q$-relations. The pointwise tensor product commutes with relational composition, i.e.,
\[
(\beta\circ\alpha)*(\beta'\circ\alpha')
=(\beta*\beta')\circ(\alpha*\alpha').
\]
<!-- end proposition-13 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
This identity follows from direct pointwise computation, together with the fact that the commutative monoidal operation $\&$ in $Q$ distributes over arbitrary suprema. For all $(x,x')\in X\times X'$ and $(z,z')\in Z\times Z'$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\bigl((\beta*\beta')\circ(\alpha*\alpha')\bigr)\bigl((x,x'),(z,z')\bigr)\\
&\quad=\bigvee_{y\in Y,\,y'\in Y'}
\bigl(\beta(y,z)\mathbin{\&}\beta'(y',z')\bigr)
\mathbin{\&}\bigl(\alpha(x,y)\mathbin{\&}\alpha'(x',y')\bigr)\\
&\quad=\Bigl(\bigvee_{y\in Y}\beta(y,z)\mathbin{\&}\alpha(x,y)\Bigr)
\mathbin{\&}\Bigl(\bigvee_{y'\in Y'}\beta'(y',z')\mathbin{\&}\alpha'(x',y')\Bigr)\\
&\quad=(\beta\circ\alpha)(x,z)\mathbin{\&}(\beta'\circ\alpha')(x',z').
\end{aligned}
\]
The right-hand side exactly matches the pointwise definition of $(\beta\circ\alpha)*(\beta'\circ\alpha')$, which completes the proof.

<!-- proposition-14: body_md -->
\label{prop:tensor-diag-compat}
Let \(f\colon\alpha\to\beta\) and \(f'\colon\alpha'\to\beta'\) be morphisms in \({\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}\). Then the pointwise tensor product \(f\ast f'\) defines a morphism in \({\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}(\alpha\ast\alpha',\beta\ast\beta')\). Furthermore, for any additional morphisms \(g\colon\beta\to\gamma\) and \(g'\colon\beta'\to\gamma'\) in \({\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}\), the pointwise tensor product and composition of diagonal morphisms commute in the sense that
    \[
    (g\ast g'){\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,} (f\ast f')=(g{\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,} f)\ast(g'{\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,} f').
    \]
<!-- end proposition-14 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
We first verify that \(f\ast f'\) is a well-defined morphism in \({\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}(\alpha\ast\alpha',\beta\ast\beta')\). Since \(f\in{\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}(\alpha,\beta)\) and \(f'\in{\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}(\alpha',\beta')\) are diagonal morphisms, there exist \(Q\)-relations \(u,v\) and \(u',v'\) satisfying the characteristic factorisation conditions of \({\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}\)-morphisms:
    \[
    \beta\circ u=f=v\circ\alpha,\qquad \beta'\circ u'=f'=v'\circ\alpha'.
    \]
By virtue of Proposition \ref{prop:tensor-compat}, the tensor product operation preserves such relational factorisations, yielding
    \[
    (\beta\ast\beta')\circ(u\ast u')=f\ast f'=(v\ast v')\circ(\alpha\ast\alpha').
    \]
This two-sided factorisation property implies \(f\ast f'\in{\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}(\alpha\ast\alpha',\beta\ast\beta')\).
    
We now prove the commutative identity relating tensor products and diagonal composition. By the definition of the diagonal composition \({\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,}\) on \({\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}\), we have
    \[
    (g\ast g'){\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,} (f\ast f')=(g\ast g')\circ\bigl((\beta\ast\beta')\searrow (f\ast f')\bigr).
    \]
    Since \(g\colon\beta\to\gamma\) and \(g'\colon\beta'\to\gamma'\) are morphisms in \({\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}\), each diagonal morphism admits the standard decomposition \(g=(g\swarrow\beta)\circ\beta\) and \(g'=(g'\swarrow\beta')\circ\beta'\). Applying Proposition \ref{prop:tensor-compat}, the tensor product distributes over relational composition, so that
    \[
    g\ast g'= 
   {\hbox{$\left(\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}(g\swarrow\beta)\circ\beta 
   {\hbox{$\left)\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}\ast 
   {\hbox{$\left(\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}(g'\swarrow\beta')\circ\beta' 
   {\hbox{$\left)\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}= 
   {\hbox{$\left(\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}(g\swarrow\beta)\ast(g'\swarrow\beta') 
   {\hbox{$\left)\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}\circ(\beta\ast\beta').
    \]
    Substituting this decomposition into the previous formula yields the following equational derivation:
    \begin{align*}
    (g\ast g'){\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,} (f\ast f')
    &= 
   {\hbox{$\left(\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}(g\swarrow\beta)\ast(g'\swarrow\beta') 
   {\hbox{$\left)\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}\circ(\beta\ast\beta')\circ\bigl((\beta\ast\beta')\searrow (f\ast f')\bigr)\\
    &= 
   {\hbox{$\left(\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}(g\swarrow\beta)\ast(g'\swarrow\beta') 
   {\hbox{$\left)\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}\circ(f\ast f')  \\
    &= 
   {\hbox{$\left(\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}(g\swarrow\beta)\circ f 
   {\hbox{$\left)\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}\ast 
   {\hbox{$\left(\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}(g'\swarrow\beta')\circ f' 
   {\hbox{$\left)\vcenter to\@ne\big@size{}\right.\n@space$}}\\
    &=(g{\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,} f)\ast(g'{\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,} f').
    \end{align*}
The second step holds due to the fact $f\ast f'\in{\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}(\beta\ast\beta',\gamma\ast\gamma')$.   The third step follows directly from Proposition \ref{prop:tensor-compat}. The final equality is exactly the definition of ${\,{\scriptstyle\odot}\,}$.

<!-- proposition-16: body_md -->
\label{prop:idm-morph}
Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be idempotent $Q$-relations. For any $Q$-relation $f$,
\[
f\in{\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}(\alpha,\beta)
\iff f=\beta\circ f\circ\alpha.
\]
<!-- end proposition-16 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
The sufficiency is trivial. For necessity, assume $f\in{\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}(\alpha,\beta)$. By the definition of diagonal morphisms, $(f\swarrow\alpha)\circ\alpha=f=\beta\circ(\beta\searrow f)$. Using the idempotency of $\beta$, we obtain
\[
\beta\circ f=\beta\circ\bigl(\beta\circ(\beta\searrow f)\bigr)
=\beta\circ(\beta\searrow f)=f.
\]
Similarly, the idempotency of $\alpha$ implies
\[
f\circ\alpha=\bigl((f\swarrow\alpha)\circ\alpha\bigr)\circ\alpha
=(f\swarrow\alpha)\circ\alpha=f.
\]
Combining the two identities yields $\beta\circ f\circ\alpha=f$.

<!-- proposition-17: body_md -->
\label{prop:tensor-regular}
The pointwise tensor product of two regular (respectively, idempotent) $Q$-relations is regular (respectively, idempotent).
<!-- end proposition-17 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
First, let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be regular $Q$-relations, so that $\alpha=\alpha\circ\overleftarrow{\alpha}\circ\alpha$ and $\beta=\beta\circ\overleftarrow{\beta}\circ\beta$. Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:tensor-compat} twice, we have
\[
\alpha*\beta
=(\alpha\circ\overleftarrow{\alpha}\circ\alpha)*(\beta\circ\overleftarrow{\beta}\circ\beta)
=(\alpha*\beta)\circ(\overleftarrow{\alpha}*\overleftarrow{\beta})\circ(\alpha*\beta),
\]
which verifies the regularity of $\alpha*\beta$.

Next, suppose $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are idempotent. Again by Proposition~\ref{prop:tensor-compat},
\[
(\alpha*\beta)\circ(\alpha*\beta)
=(\alpha\circ\alpha)*(\beta\circ\beta)=\alpha*\beta,
\]
so $\alpha*\beta$ is idempotent. This completes the proof.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be regular $Q$-relations. There exists a natural isomorphism
\[
\mathfrak{D}\alpha\otimes\mathfrak{D}\beta\cong\mathfrak{D}(\alpha*\beta)
\]
in the category ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$. In particular, the tensor product of two constructively completely distributive cocomplete $Q$-categories remains constructively completely distributive.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Let $\mathbb{A}=\mathfrak{D}\alpha$ and $\mathbb{B}=\mathfrak{D}\beta$, so that both $\mathbb{A}$ and $\mathbb{B}$ are ccd cocomplete $Q$-categories. As noted above, every ccd $Q$-category is isomorphic to the $\mathfrak{D}$-image of its associated idempotent totally-below distributor. Accordingly, there exist idempotent distributors $\theta_{\mathbb{A}}$ and $\theta_{\mathbb{B}}$ (the totally below relations of $\mathbb{A}$ and $\mathbb{B}$) such that $\mathfrak{D}\theta_{\mathbb{A}}\cong\mathbb{A}$ and $\mathfrak{D}\theta_{\mathbb{B}}\cong\mathbb{B}$. It suffices to verify $\mathfrak{D}(\theta_{\mathbb{A}}*\theta_{\mathbb{B}})\cong\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}$.

By the symmetric monoidal closed structure of ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$, we have the canonical isomorphism
\[
\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}\cong(\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B})\otimes \mathbb{Q}\cong{Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}(\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B},\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{op}},
\]
which identifies the objects of $\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}$ with ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-bimorphisms $\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{op}}$. We show that such bimorphisms bijectively correspond to the elements of $\mathfrak{D}(\theta_{\mathbb{A}}*\theta_{\mathbb{B}})$.

Set $\theta=\theta_{\mathbb{A}}*\theta_{\mathbb{B}}$. For any ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-bimorphism $u\colon\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{B}\to \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{op}}$ and all $x\in\mathbb{A}_0$, $y\in\mathbb{B}_0$, the ccd property of $\mathbb{A}$ and $\mathbb{B}$ guarantees $x=S_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{\mathbb{A}}x)$ and $y=S_{\mathbb{B}}(T_{\mathbb{B}}y)$. Expanding these adjoint representations gives
\begin{align*}
u(x,y)&=u\bigl(S_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{\mathbb{A}}x),S_{\mathbb{B}}(T_{\mathbb{B}}y)\bigr)\\
&=u\biggl(\bigvee_{x'\in\mathbb{A}_0}T_{\mathbb{A}}x(x')\cdot x',\,
\bigvee_{y'\in\mathbb{B}_0}T_{\mathbb{B}}y(y')\cdot y'\biggr)\\
&=\bigwedge_{x'\in\mathbb{A}_0}\theta_{\mathbb{A}}(x',x)\rightarrow
\biggl(\bigwedge_{y'\in\mathbb{B}_0}\theta_{\mathbb{B}}(y',y)\rightarrow u(x',y')\biggr)\\
&=\bigwedge_{x'\in\mathbb{A}_0,\,y'\in\mathbb{B}_0}
\bigl(\theta_{\mathbb{A}}(x',x)\mathbin{\&}\theta_{\mathbb{B}}(y',y)\bigr)\rightarrow u(x',y')\\
&=(u\swarrow\theta)(x,y).
\end{align*}
The third equality follows from the bimorphism property of $u$: the hom functor on $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{op}}$ interchanges joins and meets and converts the scalar action $q\cdot z$ into scalar implication $q\rightarrow z$. This computation implies $u=u\swarrow\theta$, so $u$ lies in the image of the lifting operation $(-)\swarrow\theta\colon Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel}(\mathbb{A}_0\times\mathbb{B}_0,{\{\star\}})\toQ\text{-}\mathbf{Rel}(\mathbb{A}_0\times\mathbb{B}_0,{\{\star\}})$.

Conversely, for any $Q$-relation $f\colon\mathbb{A}_0\times\mathbb{B}_0{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig}{\{\star\}}$, define $u=f\swarrow\theta\colon\mathbb{A}_0\times\mathbb{B}_0{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig}{\{\star\}}$. By definition of the lifting operator,
\[
(f\swarrow\theta)(x,y)=f\swarrow(T_\mathbb{A}(x)\ast T_\mathbb{B}(y)).
\]
This induces the commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{B}
\arrow{rr}{u=f\swarrow\theta} \arrow{dr}[swap]{T_{\mathbb{A}}(-)*T_{\mathbb{B}}(-)} &&
\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{op}} \\
&\mathcal{P}\delta_{\mathbb{A}_0\times\mathbb{B}_0}
\arrow{ur}[swap]{f\swarrow(-)} &
\end{tikzcd}
\]

Here, $T_{\mathbb{A}}(-)*T_{\mathbb{B}}(-)$ is a ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-bimorphism as the pointwise tensor product of adjoint functors, while $f\swarrow(-)$ is a ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-morphism. Their composite $u$ is therefore a ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-bimorphism.

The above arguments demonstrate that the set $\mathbf{bi}\text{-}{Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}(\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{B},\mathbb{Q}^\mathrm{op})^\mathrm{op}$ of all ${Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}$-bimorphisms from $\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{B}$ to $\mathbb{Q}^\mathrm{op}$ coincides exactly with the image of the $Q$-functor $(-)\swarrow\theta$. Since $(-)\swarrow\theta$ is right adjoint to $(-)\circ\theta$, its image is isomorphic to $\mathfrak{D}\theta$. We thus obtain the natural isomorphism
\[
\mathfrak{D}(\theta_{\mathbb{A}}*\theta_{\mathbb{B}})=\mathfrak{D}\theta
\cong\mathbf{bi}\text{-}{Q\text{-}\mathbf{Sup}}(\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{B},\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{op}}
\cong\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}.
\]

By Proposition \ref{prop:idm-hom}, $\theta_\mathbb{A}\cong\alpha$ and $\theta_\mathbb{B}\cong\beta$ in ${\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}_\mathrm{reg}$, which yields
\[
\mathfrak{D}\alpha\otimes\mathfrak{D}\beta=\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}\cong\mathfrak{D}(\theta_\mathbb{A}\ast\theta_\mathbb{B})\cong\mathfrak{D}(\alpha*\beta).
\]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:tensor-regular}, $\alpha*\beta$ is regular, so $\mathfrak{D}(\alpha*\beta)$ is ccd. Consequently, the tensor product of two ccd cocomplete $Q$-categories remains ccd.

Let \(Q\) be a Girard quantale. By the complementation isomorphism between \({\mathbf{D}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}\) and \({\mathbf{B}(Q\text{-}\mathbf{Rel})}\) (Lemma~\ref{lem:girard-b}), we have \(\mathfrak{B}\alpha=\mathfrak{D}(\neg\alpha)\) for every \(Q\)-relation \(\alpha\). Hence, for a \(Q\)-relation \(\alpha\colon X{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig} Y\), the formal concept lattice \(\mathfrak{B}\alpha\) is ccd if and only if \(\neg\alpha\) is regular.

If both \(\neg\alpha\) and \(\neg\beta\) are regular, then
\[
\mathfrak{B}\alpha\otimes\mathfrak{B}\beta
\cong\mathfrak{D}(\neg\alpha)\otimes\mathfrak{D}(\neg\beta)
\cong\mathfrak{D}((\neg\alpha)*(\neg\beta))
= \mathfrak{B}(\neg((\neg\alpha)*(\neg\beta))).
\]
In particular, in the classical case \(Q=\{0,1\}\), the formal concept lattice \(\mathfrak{B}(X,Y,\alpha)\) is ccd if and only if \(\neg\alpha\) is regular. For contexts \((X,Y,\alpha)\) and \((X',Y',\beta)\),
\[
((x,y),(x',y'))\in \neg((\neg\alpha)*(\neg\beta)) 
\iff (x,y)\in\alpha \text{ or } (x',y')\in\beta.
\]
Thus \((X\times X',Y\times Y',\neg((\neg\alpha)*(\neg\beta)))\) is the direct product of \((X,Y,\alpha)\) and \((X',Y',\beta)\) (see \cite[Section 1.3, Definition 32]{Ganter2024}). Therefore, Theorem \ref{thm:main} recovers Theorem 33 in \cite[Section 4.4]{Ganter2024}.

<!-- example-3: body_md -->
Let $Q=([0,1],\leq,\&)$ denote the standard Łukasiewicz quantale, with monoidal and implication operations defined by
\[
x\mathbin{\&} y=\max\{x+y-1,0\},\quad x\rightarrow y=\min\{1-x+y,1\}.
\]

Consider the singleton-valued $Q$-relation
\[
\alpha\colon{\{\star\}}{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig}{\{\star\}},\qquad \alpha(\star,\star)=0.5.
\]
Direct verification confirms that $\alpha$ is non-regular. We first characterize the associated $Q$-category $\mathfrak{D}\alpha$. A $Q$-relation $\lambda\colon{\{\star\}}{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig}{\{\star\}}$ belongs to $\mathfrak{D}\alpha$ if and only if it satisfies the fixed-point condition
\[
\lambda(\star,\star)=\bigl(\alpha(\star,\star)\rightarrow\lambda(\star,\star)\bigr)\,\&\, \alpha(\star,\star)
=\min\bigl\{\lambda(\star,\star),\,0.5\bigr\}.
\]
This forces $\lambda(\star,\star)\in[0,0.5]$, so we identify $\mathfrak{D}\alpha$ with the interval $[0,0.5]$. Its enriched hom-operation and scalar $Q$-action are given by
$\mathfrak{D}\alpha(p,q)=p\rightarrow q=\min\{1-p+q,\,1\}$,
 $q\cdot p=q\mathbin{\&} p=\max\{q+p-1,\,0\}$ and $p^{q}=q\rightarrow p=\min\{1-q+p,\,0.5\}$.


The objects of the tensor product $\mathfrak{D}\alpha\otimes\mathfrak{D}\alpha$ correspond bijectively to left-adjoint $Q$-functors $F\colon\mathfrak{D}\alpha\to(\mathfrak{D}\alpha)^{\mathrm{op}}$, which are uniquely determined by two conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item $F(q\mathbin{\&} x)=q\rightarrow F(x)$ for all $q\in Q$ and $x\in[0,0.5]$;
\item $F(\sup X)=\inf F(X)$ for all subsets $X\subseteq[0,0.5]$.
\end{enumerate}

Every such functor admits a piecewise explicit form
\[
F_a(t)=
\begin{cases}
0.5, & 0\leq t\leq a,\\
a+0.5-t, & a<t\leq 0.5,
\end{cases}
\]
parameterized by $a\in[0,0.5]$. This yields the object isomorphism
\[
(\mathfrak{D}\alpha\otimes\mathfrak{D}\alpha)_0\cong\{F_a\mid 0\leq a\leq 0.5\}.
\]

On the other hand, the pointwise self-tensor product of $\alpha$ satisfies
\[
(\alpha*\alpha)(\star,\star)=\alpha(\star,\star)\mathbin{\&}\alpha(\star,\star)
=\max\{0.5+0.5-1,\,0\}=0.
\]
A $Q$-relation $\lambda\colon{\{\star\}}{\bfig\morphism<180,0>[\mkern-4mu`\mkern-4mu;]\place(86,0)[\shortmid]\efig}{\{\star\}}$ lies in $\mathfrak{D}(\alpha*\alpha)$ if and only if $\lambda=(\lambda\swarrow(\alpha*\alpha))\circ(\alpha*\alpha)$. Since $\alpha*\alpha$ is the zero $Q$-relation, the only feasible solution is $\lambda(\star,\star)=0$, so that $\mathfrak{D}(\alpha*\alpha)\cong\{0\}$.

We therefore obtain a strict non-isomorphism
\[
\mathfrak{D}\alpha\otimes\mathfrak{D}\alpha\not\cong\mathfrak{D}(\alpha*\alpha),
\]
which demonstrates that the tensor product isomorphism established in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} fails for non-regular $Q$-relations, validating the necessity of the regularity assumption.
