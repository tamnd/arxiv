<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $K$ be a number field of degree $5$ and let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $K$. Then
$E(K)_\mathrm{tors}$ is isomorphic to one of the following $35$ groups:
\begin{alignat*}{2}
&\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, \qquad && n=1,\dots,22,\ 24,\ 25,\ 28,\ 30,\\
&\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2n\mathbb{Z}, \qquad && n=1,\dots, 9.
\end{alignat*}
Equivalently, $\Phi(5)=\Phi^\infty(5)\cup\{\mathbb{Z}/28\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}/30\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}\}$. Each of the
groups in $\Phi^\infty(5)$ is the torsion group of infinitely many pairs $(K,E)$, whereas for
the three exceptional groups there are $1$, $2$ and $1$ isomorphism classes of pairs $(K,E)$,
respectively. The corresponding curves have quintic $j$-invariants and hence give
$5$, $10$ and $5$ $\overline\mathbb{Q}$-isomorphism classes. They are listed explicitly
in \Cref{thm:sporadic}.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:sporadic}
Let $K$ be a number field of degree $5$ and let $E/K$ be an elliptic curve such that
$E(K)_\mathrm{tors}$ is not in $\Phi^\infty(5)$. Then, up to isomorphism of the pair $(K,E)$, exactly
one of the following holds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $E(K)_\mathrm{tors}\simeq \mathbb{Z}/28\mathbb{Z}$, $K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ with
      $\alpha^5-\alpha^4-2\alpha^3-\alpha^2+2\alpha+2=0$, and $E$ is the curve
      \eqref{eq:E28} in Section \ref{sec:sporadic}.
\item $E(K)_\mathrm{tors}\simeq \mathbb{Z}/30\mathbb{Z}$, $K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ with
      $\alpha^5+\alpha^4-3\alpha^3+3\alpha+1=0$, and $E$ is one of the two curves
      \eqref{eq:E30a}, \eqref{eq:E30b} in Section \ref{sec:sporadic}, which are $2$-isogenous over $K$.
\item $E(K)_\mathrm{tors}\simeq \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}$, $K=\mathbb{Q}(w)$ with $w^5-2w^4+2w^3-3w^2-w+4=0$, and $E$ is the
      curve \eqref{eq:E218} in Section \ref{sec:sporadic}.
\end{enumerate}
In all three cases $K$ has signature $(3,1)$ and class number $1$ and the Galois group of its
normal closure is $S_5$. Each of the four curves is without complex multiplication and has a
$j$-invariant of degree $5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. The discriminants, LMFDB labels and conductors are
displayed in \Cref{table:fields}.

\label{prop:tools}
Let $m\in\{1,2\}$, $m\mid n$, and let $q\nmid 2n$ be a prime.
\begin{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:additive}
Let $mn>4$, let $K$ be a number field, let $E/K$ be an elliptic curve with
$\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\subseteq E(K)$, and let $\wp$ be a prime of $K$ above a rational prime $p\nmid n$.
Then $E$ does not have additive reduction at $\wp$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
This is contained in the proof of \cite[Proposition 5.2]{DN}. If $E$ had additive reduction at
$\wp$, the special fibre of the N\'eron model would have $a\cdot\#\mathbb{F}_\wp$ points over $\mathbb{F}_\wp$,
where $a\leq4$ is the order of the component group and $\#\mathbb{F}_\wp$ is a power of $p$. Reduction is
injective on the prime-to-$p$ torsion of $E(K)$, so $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ embeds into that group and
$mn\mid a\cdot\#\mathbb{F}_\wp$. As $m\mid n$ and $p\nmid n$ we have $\gcd(mn,p)=1$, so $mn\mid a\leq4$,
contradicting $mn>4$.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:global}
Let $n$ be an integer with $3\nmid n$, and let $a\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ be such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\chi_f(a)=1$ for every newform $f$ of weight $2$ and level $n'$ dividing $n$ with
      $L(f,1)=0$, where $\chi_f$ denotes the nebentypus of $f$, evaluated at the image of $a$

\item $b(n)>5$, where $b(n)$ is as in \eqref{eq:bn} with $q=3$ and with $k_3$ determined by

\item $\operatorname{ord}\langle a\rangle\nmid 5$ in $\Delta(n)=(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times/\{\pm1\}$;
\item $d_{\mathrm{CM}}X_1(n)>5$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $X_1(n)$ has no non-cuspidal point of degree $5$. In particular, $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is not a

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->

Galois orbits of newforms of level $n'$, and this decomposition is equivariant for the diamond
operators, $\langle a\rangle$ acting on $A_{[f]}$ through $\chi_f(a)\in\operatorname{End}(A_{[f]})\otimes\mathbb{Q}$. For an $f$
with $L(f,1)\neq0$ the group $A_{[f]}(\mathbb{Q})$ is finite by Kato's theorem
\cite[Corollary 14.3]{kato}, and for the remaining $f$ we have $\chi_f(a)=1$ by (1), so $\langle a\rangle-1$
annihilates $A_{[f]}$. Hence $(\langle a\rangle-1)J_1(n)(\mathbb{Q})$ is contained in the image of a finite group
under an isogeny, and is finite. The rest is
\cite[Proposition 4.3]{DN} and the corollary following it, applied with $d=5$, $q=3$,
$H=\{\pm1\}$, whose hypotheses are (2), (3) and (4).

<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem:CC}
Let $X=X_1(n)$ with $n>4$, and let $p\nmid 2n$ and $q\nmid 2pn$ be primes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $L\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ be a subfield in which both $p$ and $q$ split completely, and
let $C$ be a cuspidal divisor on $X_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ that is the reduction of a cuspidal divisor on
$X_L$. Then $[A_q\psi(C)]=0$ in $J(\mathbb{F}_p)$ for every $\psi\in\mathbb{Z}[\Delta(n)]$.
\item Let $C=\sum_i a_iC_i$ be a cuspidal divisor on $X_{\mathbb{F}_p}$, the $C_i$ being pairwise
distinct closed points. If $q\bmod n$ lies in $H_i$ for every $i$, then $[A_q\psi(C)]=0$ for
every $\psi\in\mathbb{Z}[\Delta(n)]$. This holds in particular whenever $q\equiv p^j\pmod n$ for some
$j\geq0$.
\end{enumerate}
The case $\psi=1$ of \textup{(2)} is \cite[Lemma 5.10]{DN}.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
(1) Let $c$ be a cuspidal divisor on $X_L$ reducing to $C$ and put $e:=\deg\psi(c)$. The
diamond operators are defined over $\mathbb{Q}$ and permute the cusps, so $\psi(c)$ is again a cuspidal
divisor rational over $L$, and $\psi(c)-ec_0$ is a cuspidal divisor of degree $0$ on $X_L$. Its
class lies in $J(L)_\mathrm{tors}$ by Manin--Drinfeld. As $q$ splits completely in $L$,
\Cref{prop:tools}(2) gives $[A_q(\psi(c)-ec_0)]=0$, and $A_q(c_0)=0$ by \Cref{prop:tools}(3),
so $[A_q\psi(c)]=0$ in $J(L)$. Reducing modulo a prime of $L$ above $p$, whose residue field is
$\mathbb{F}_p$ because $p$ splits completely in $L$, and using that $A_q$ and $\psi$ commute with
reduction, gives $[A_q\psi(C)]=0$.

(2) Both $\psi$ and $A_q$ are linear on divisors, so $[A_q\psi(C)]=\sum_i a_i\,[A_q\psi(C_i)]$ and it suffices to prove $[A_q\psi(C_i)]=0$ for all $i$. Fix $i$ and apply (1) with $L=L_i$ and $C=C_i$: the divisor $c_i$ is cuspidal, defined over $L_i$ and reduces to $C_i$, the prime $p$ splits completely in $L_i$ because $D_p\subseteq H_i$, and $q$ splits completely in $L_i$ because $q\bmod n\in H_i$ by hypothesis.
For the last assertion, $q\equiv p^j\pmod n$ means $q\bmod n\in D_p\subseteq H_i$ for every $i$.

Since $A_q$ commutes with $\mathbb{Z}[\Delta(n)]$ and $A_q(C)$ has degree $0$, we have
$[A_q\psi(C)]=\psi\bigl([A_q(C)]\bigr)$, so the case $\psi=1$ implies the general statement.
<!-- end proof-9 -->
<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
Only the closed points that actually occur in a cuspidal complement matter, so in practice one
only has to impose $q\bmod n\in H_i$ for the closed cuspidal points $C_i$ of degree at most
$5-d'$. Moreover $H_i=(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ (so that $C_i$ imposes no condition on $q$) as soon as
the $D_p$-orbit $O_i$ is a full $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$-orbit, i.e.\ as soon as the corresponding
Galois orbit of cusps over $\mathbb{Q}$ stays irreducible modulo $p$. Our code compares the degrees of
the Galois orbits of cusps over $\mathbb{Q}$ with the degrees of their reductions to find the orbits
that split modulo $p$. If a cuspidal complement can contain a point coming from such an orbit,
it only uses $q$ with $q\bmod n\in D_p$, which suffices as $D_p\subseteq H_i$.

Suppose the opposite. By \Cref{lem:additive} the reduction of $E$ at every prime of $K$ above $p$ is
multiplicative, so, fixing a prime $\wp$ of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ above $p$, the reduction of $x^{(5)}$
modulo $\wp$ is an effective divisor of degree $5$ supported on the cusps. As in the proof of \cite[Proposition 5.2]{DN}, it lifts to an effective cuspidal divisor $C$ of
degree $5$ on $X_1(n)_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)}$, since the cuspidal subscheme is finite flat over
$\mathbb{Z}[1/n]$ and every cusp of $X_1(n)$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$-rational.

Let $y:=(\langle a\rangle-1)[x^{(5)}-C]\in J_1(n)(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n))$. Writing
$[x^{(5)}-C]=[x^{(5)}-5c_0]+[5c_0-C]$ and using that $[x^{(5)}-5c_0]\in J_1(n)(\mathbb{Q})$, that
$(\langle a\rangle-1)J_1(n)(\mathbb{Q})$ is finite, and that $[5c_0-C]$ is torsion by Manin--Drinfeld, we see that
$y$ is torsion. By construction $y$ reduces to $0$ modulo $\wp$. If $p>2$, reduction is
injective on $J_1(n)(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n))_\mathrm{tors}$ by \cite[Appendix]{katz}, so $y=0$. If $p=2$ and $n$ is odd, the kernel of reduction has exponent at most $2$ by \cite[Proposition 2.4]{parent}, so $2y=0$. In either case $\varepsilon\, y=0$, i.e.
\[
\varepsilon(\langle a\rangle-1)(x^{(5)}-C)=\operatorname{div}(f)
\]
for some $f\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)(X_1(n))$.

If $f$ is non-constant then, separating the positive and negative parts,
$\deg f\leq \varepsilon\deg\bigl(\langle a\rangle x^{(5)}+C\bigr)=10\varepsilon$, contradicting (2).

So $f$ is constant, i.e.\ $\varepsilon(\langle a\rangle-1)(x^{(5)}-C)=0$ as a divisor. The diamond
operators preserve the set of cusps and its complement, so
$\langle a\rangle x^{(5)}=x^{(5)}$ and $\langle a\rangle C=C$. Now $\langle a\rangle$ permutes the five distinct
geometric points in the support of $x^{(5)}$. If every one of them had trivial stabilizer in
the cyclic group $\langle\langle a\rangle\rangle$, all orbits would have length $\operatorname{ord}\langle a\rangle$ and hence
$\operatorname{ord}\langle a\rangle\mid 5$, contradicting (1). So some conjugate $x^\sigma$ satisfies
$\langle b\rangle x^\sigma=x^\sigma$ with $b\not\equiv\pm1\pmod n$. Writing $x^\sigma=(E',P')$, this means
$(E',P')\simeq(E',bP')$, so $E'$ has an automorphism $\zeta\neq\pm1$, hence $j(E')\in\{0,1728\}$
and $x^\sigma$ is a CM point of degree $5$, contradicting (3).

<!-- proposition-12: body_md -->
\label{prop:direct}
Let $X=X_1(m,n)$ with $m\in\{1,2\}$, let $p\nmid 2n$ be a prime, and assume that $\operatorname{rk} J(\mathbb{Q})=0$
and $J(\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{Cl}^{c}_\mathbb{Q} X$.
Let $c_1,\dots,c_r$ be the Galois orbits of cusps over $\mathbb{Q}$, ordered so that $c_1$ is a
$\mathbb{Q}$-rational cusp, and let $\varphi:\mathbb{Z}^r\to \operatorname{Cl}(X_{\mathbb{F}_p})$ be the homomorphism sending the
$i$-th generator to the class of $\overline{c_i}-\deg(c_i)\,\overline{c_1}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] $\mathbb{Z}^r/\ker\varphi\simeq J(\mathbb{Q})$ canonically, and reduction modulo $p$ is
      injective on $J(\mathbb{Q})$ with image $\operatorname{im}\varphi$;
\item[\textup{(b)}] if $D\in X^{(5)}(\mathbb{Q})$ then $\operatorname{red}_p\bigl([D-5c_1]\bigr)+[5\overline{c_1}]$
      lies in the set $S_p$ of classes of effective divisors of degree $5$ on $X_{\mathbb{F}_p}$;
\item[\textup{(c)}] for every $\gamma\in J(\mathbb{Q})$ the effective divisors of degree $5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$
      in the class of $5c_1+\gamma$ are exactly the elements of the complete linear system
      $|5c_1+\gamma|$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end proposition-12 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
Reduction modulo $p$ is injective on $J(\mathbb{Q})=J(\mathbb{Q})_\mathrm{tors}$ by \cite[Appendix]{katz}, since $p>2$
is a prime of good reduction of $X$. By hypothesis the map $\mathbb{Z}^r\to J(\mathbb{Q})$ sending the
$i$-th generator to $[c_i-\deg(c_i)c_1]$ is surjective, and $\varphi$ is its composite with the
injective map $\operatorname{red}_p$. Hence $\ker\varphi$ is the kernel of $\mathbb{Z}^r\to J(\mathbb{Q})$, giving (a). Part
(b) holds because $\operatorname{red}_p$ commutes with the Abel--Jacobi map and $D$ reduces to an effective
divisor of degree $5$ on $X_{\mathbb{F}_p}$. Part (c) is the definition of the linear system.
<!-- end proof-15 -->
<!-- remark-9: body_md -->
\Cref{prop:direct} turns the determination of $X^{(5)}(\mathbb{Q})$ into a finite computation, and this
is how we use it. We compute $\operatorname{Cl}(X_{\mathbb{F}_p})$ and all closed points of $X_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ of degree at
most $5$, and construct $S_p$. By (a) the group $J(\mathbb{Q})$ is the finite group $\mathbb{Z}^r/\ker\varphi$, so
we enumerate its elements $\gamma$ and discard those failing the condition in (b). Using several primes $p$ and keeping the $\gamma$ that survive at each of them shortens the list further. For each surviving $\gamma$ we compute the Riemann--Roch space $L(5c_1+\gamma)$ over
$\mathbb{Q}$, whose projectivization is $|5c_1+\gamma|$.
By (b) every element of $X^{(5)}(\mathbb{Q})$ is found in this way.

<!-- remark-10: body_md -->
\label{rem:basecusp}
The choice of base point in \Cref{prop:direct} matters: $c_1$ must be the reduction of a
$\mathbb{Q}$-rational cusp, not merely an $\mathbb{F}_p$-rational cusp of the model. At a prime $p$ at which
some Galois orbit of cusps of degree $>1$ splits into $\mathbb{F}_p$-rational points, an arbitrary

$10584$ \cite[Theorem 3.1(3), Proposition 4.16 and Table 3]{DEHMZ}. The gonality of
$X_1(2,18)$ is $4$, so we cannot argue as in \Cref{prop:28,prop:30}. Instead we use the fact, proved by Derickx and Sutherland \cite[Proposition 5.4]{DS}, that $\mathbb{Q}(X_1(2,18))$ contains no function of degree \emph{exactly} $5$.

We run \Cref{prop:direct} at $p=5$. There are $15$ Galois orbits of cusps over $\mathbb{Q}$, of degrees
$1^9,2^3,3^3$, and we \href{https://github.com/F-Najman/quintic-torsion/blob/main/direct_analysis_X11.m}{compute} that the image of $\operatorname{Cl}^{c}_\mathbb{Q}$ in $\operatorname{Cl}(X_{\mathbb{F}_5})$ has invariant factors $[2,42,126]$. and its $6273$ effective divisors of degree $5$ fall into $6138$ classes. Exactly $1944$ of the
$10584$ classes of $J(\mathbb{Q})$ satisfy condition (b) of \Cref{prop:direct}.

By \eqref{eq:cuspgf} there are $1980$ effective $\mathbb{Q}$-rational cuspidal divisors of degree $5$. As the gonality is $4$, they need not lie in distinct classes, and we compute that they fall into $1926$ classes, all among the $1944$ candidates.

We claim that a class containing an effective cuspidal divisor $C$ of degree $5$ contains no
irreducible non-cuspidal divisor. Suppose $D\sim C$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ with $D$ an irreducible
non-cuspidal divisor of degree $5$, and let $f\in\mathbb{Q}(X_1(2,18))^\times$ satisfy
$\operatorname{div}(f)=D-C$. The supports of $D$ and of $C$ are disjoint, since $D$ is non-cuspidal and $C$
is cuspidal, so no cancellation occurs and the polar divisor of $f$ is exactly $C$. Hence $f$ has degree $\deg C=5$. This
contradicts \cite[Proposition 5.4]{DS}.

There remain $1944-1926=18$ classes. For each of them we \href{https://github.com/F-Najman/quintic-torsion/blob/main/direct_analysis_X11.m}{compute} the Riemann--Roch space $L(5c_1+\gamma)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Each is one-dimensional, so the class contains a unique effective divisor, and we compute that this divisor is an irreducible non-cuspidal closed point of degree $5$. All $18$ have the same
minimal polynomial of the $j$-invariant, of degree $5$. Since every non-cuspidal closed point
of degree $5$ has its class among the $1944$ candidates, and cannot lie in one of the $1926$
cuspidal classes, these $18$ points are all of them.

<!-- proposition-18: body_md -->
\label{prop:28_30_218}
We use the notation of \Cref{prop:sporadiccurves}. Let $K$ be a quintic field and $E/K$ an elliptic curve.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $E(K)$ contains a point of order $28$, then $(K,E)\simeq (K_{28},E_{28})$ and
      $E(K)_\mathrm{tors}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/28\mathbb{Z}$.
\item If $E(K)$ contains a point of order $30$, then $(K,E)$ is isomorphic to
      $(K_{30},E_{30})$ or to $(K_{30},E_{30}')$, and $E(K)_\mathrm{tors}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/30\mathbb{Z}$.
\item If $E(K)$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}$, then

\end{enumerate}
In particular no group properly containing $\mathbb{Z}/28\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/30\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}$ is a
quintic torsion group.

We prove (1) and (2) together, and then (3), where an extra step is needed. As in
\Cref{sec:groups}, a point of order $28$ on $E(K)$ gives a non-cuspidal closed point $\bar x$
of $Y_1(28)$ of degree $5$, so that $\mathbb{Q}(\bar x)\simeq K$. By \Cref{prop:28} $\bar x$ is one of the six
diamond translates $\langle a\rangle x_0$, whose residue field is $K_{28}$ in every case. As $28>4$,
$X_1(28)$ is a fine moduli space, so the universal pair over $\mathbb{Q}(\bar x)$ is $(E_{28},aP)$ for
the corresponding $a$, and after identifying $K$ with $K_{28}$ we get $E\simeq E_{28}$.
Finally $E_{28}(K)_\mathrm{tors}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/28\mathbb{Z}$ by \Cref{prop:sporadiccurves}. Case (2) is the same,
using \Cref{prop:30} in place of \Cref{prop:28}.

For (3), put $E:=E_{2,18}$ and $K:=K_{2,18}$. We show that the level structures on $E$ account for all $18$ closed points. The extra step is needed because \Cref{prop:2_18} reports only the
number of the quintic points and the minimal polynomial of their $j$-invariants, which leaves
open the possibility of distinct quadratic twists over $K$. On $E$ there are
$18$ points $Q$ of order $18$, and for each of them two points $P$ of order $2$ outside
$\langle Q\rangle$. Since $\operatorname{Aut}(E)=\{\pm1\}$ and $(P,Q)$ and $(-P,-Q)=(P,-Q)$ give isomorphic triples, these $36$ pairs give exactly $18$ level structures $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow E(K)$ up to
$K$-isomorphism. Note that they define $18$ \emph{distinct} closed points of $Y_1(2,18)$: if $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ carried one to another, it would fix $j(E)$, hence $K=\mathbb{Q}(j(E))$, hence $E$ and all its $K$-rational points, so it would fix the level structure. By \Cref{prop:2_18} there are exactly $18$ non-cuspidal closed points of degree $5$
on $X_1(2,18)$, so these are all of them.

$E(K)_\mathrm{tors}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}$ by \Cref{prop:sporadiccurves}.

and realized as $E(K)_\mathrm{tors}$ would in particular contain that group, forcing $E$ to be one of
the four curves and hence $G$ to be its exact torsion group.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:fasthecke}
Let $q\nmid pn$ be a prime and let $z$ be a non-cuspidal closed point of $X_1(n)_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ of
degree $k$, corresponding to a pair $(E,P)$ over $\kappa:=\mathbb{F}_{p^k}$. Let $\mathcal O$ be the set
of orbits of $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\kappa}/\kappa)$ on the set of the $q+1$ subgroups of order $q$ of
$E[q]$. For $O\in\mathcal O$ pick $C\in O$, let $L$ be the field of definition of $C$, and let
$y_O$ be the closed point of $X_1(n)_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ containing $(E/C,P\bmod C)$. Then
\[
T_q(z)=\sum_{O\in\mathcal O}\frac{[L:\kappa]\cdot k}{\deg y_O}\;y_O ,
\]
and $[L:\kappa]=\#O$. Furthermore, $L$ is the field generated over $\kappa$ by the coefficients of
the kernel polynomial $g_C=\prod_{Q\in (C\setminus\{0\})/\pm}\bigl(T-x(Q)\bigr)$, where $x(Q)$
denotes the $x$-coordinate of $Q$ in a fixed Weierstrass model of $E$.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-25: body_md -->
By definition $T_q(z)=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\kappa/\mathbb{F}_p)}\sum_{C}\sigma\bigl(E/C,P\bmod C\bigr)$,
where $C$ ranges over all $q+1$ subgroups of order $q$ of $E[q]$. Grouping the $k(q+1)$ geometric points by the closed point that contains them, and using that Galois acts
transitively on the geometric points of a closed point, gives
$T_q(z)=\sum_y (m_y k/\deg y)\,y$ with $m_y=\#\{C:(E/C,P\bmod C)\in y\}$. This is the formula
implemented in \cite{mdmagma}. Now $E$ and $P$ are $\kappa$-rational, so for
$C'=F^i(C)$ with $F$ the $\kappa$-Frobenius we have
$(E/C',P\bmod C')=F^i\bigl(E/C,P\bmod C\bigr)$, and these two geometric points lie in the same
closed point of $X$. Hence $m_y=\sum_{O:\,y_O=y}\#O$, and it suffices to run over one $C$ per
orbit together with $\#O$. By the orbit--stabilizer theorem $\#O=[L:\kappa]$ where $L$ is the
fixed field of the stabilizer of $C$. Finally a subgroup of order $q$ is determined by, and
determines, the set of $x$-coordinates of its non-zero points, so the stabilizer of $C$ equals
the stabilizer of $g_C$, i.e.\ $L=\kappa(\text{coefficients of }g_C)$.

<!-- lemma-7: body_md -->
\label{lem:fastorbits}
Let $x,x'$ be non-cuspidal closed points of $X_1(n)_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ of the same degree $k$, represented
by $(E_1,P_1)$ over $\kappa_1$ and $(E_2,P_2)$ over $\kappa_2$. Then $x'=\langle a\rangle x$ for some
$a\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ if and only if there exist a field isomorphism $\kappa_2\to\kappa_1$, a
power $\sigma$ of the Frobenius of $\kappa_1$ and an isomorphism
$\iota:E_2^{\sigma}\to E_1$ defined over $\kappa_1$ such that $\iota(P_2^{\sigma})$ generates
$\langle P_1\rangle$.
<!-- end lemma-7 -->
<!-- proof-26: body_md -->
The diamond operators commute with the Galois action, so $x'=\langle a\rangle x$ if and only if the
geometric point $(E_1,aP_1)$ is $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p)$-conjugate to $(E_2,P_2)$, i.e. if and only if there are $\sigma$ and an isomorphism of pairs
$\iota:(E_2,P_2)^{\sigma}\to (E_1,aP_1)$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$. Such an $\iota$ is
automatically defined over $\kappa_1$, since otherwise $\iota^{F}\iota^{-1}$, where $F$ is the
$\kappa_1$-Frobenius $x\mapsto x^{p^k}$, would be a
non-trivial automorphism of $E_1$ fixing the point $aP_1$, which is $\kappa_1$-rational and of
order $n\geq5$. That is impossible, because for a non-trivial automorphism $\zeta$ of an
elliptic curve $\ker(\zeta-1)$ has at most four geometric points, the bound being attained by
$\zeta=[-1]$, for which $\ker(\zeta-1)=E[2]$.
