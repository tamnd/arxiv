<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{theorem:intro}
Every John domain is conformally equivalent to a circle domain.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- conjecture-1: body_md -->
Every domain in the Riemann sphere is conformally equivalent to a circle domain.
<!-- end conjecture-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{definition:john}
Let $\Omega\subset \mathbb C$ be a domain and let $L\geq 1$.  We say that $\Omega$ is an \textit{$L$-John domain} if for every pair of distinct points $z,w\in \Omega$ there exists a curve $\gamma\colon [0,1]\to \Omega$ such that  $\gamma(0)=z$, $\gamma(1)=w$, and
\begin{align}\label{def:john}
\min\{\ell (\gamma|_{[0,t]}), \ell(\gamma|_{[t,1]} )\} \leq L \operatorname{dist}(\gamma(t),\partial \Omega)\,\,\, \text{for all $t\in [0,1]$.}
\end{align}
We say that $\Omega$ is a John domain if it is an $L$-John domain for some $L\ge 1$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{theorem:exhaustion}
A domain $\Omega\subset \widehat \mathbb C$ satisfies Koebe's conjecture if and only if there exist an exhaustion $\{\Omega_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ of $\Omega$ and a Jordan region $U\subset \widehat \mathbb C$ that contains $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus \Omega$ such that for every $ q\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$ we have
$$\lim_{\substack{z\to  q\\z\in \Omega}} \limsup_{j\to\infty} \operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j} \mathcal G^*(z,q_j(q); U)=0,$$
where $\mathcal G^*(z,q_j(q);U)$ is the family of curves in $\pi_{\Omega_j}(\overline U)$ with endpoints on $\partial U$ that separate $z$ and $q_j(q)$ in $\pi_{\Omega_j}(U)$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemma:crosscut_connected}
If $G\subset \widehat \mathbb C$ is a simply connected domain and $C$ is a crosscut of $G$, the following statements are true.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\normalfont(\arabic*)]
\item\label{lemma:crosscut_connected:1} The set $G\setminus C$ has exactly two components $G_1,G_2$ such that
$$G\cap \partial G_1=G\cap \partial G_2=C.$$
\item\label{lemma:crosscut_connected:2} The set $\partial G_i\setminus C$ is connected for $i=1,2$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
By the Riemann mapping theorem there is a conformal map $\phi$ from $\mathbb D$ onto $G$. By \cite{Pommerenke:conformal}*{Prop.\ 2.14}, $\phi^{-1}(C)$ is an open Jordan arc $J\subset \mathbb D$ with distinct endpoints on $\partial \mathbb D$. By the Jordan curve theorem, $\mathbb D\setminus J$ is the union of two Jordan regions $\Omega_1,\Omega_2$. We set $G_i=\phi(\Omega_i)$ for $i=1,2$ and \ref{lemma:crosscut_connected:1} follows. Fix $i\in \{1,2\}$. The set $\partial G_i\setminus C$ is precisely the set of accumulation points of $\phi(z)$ as $z\to E=\partial \Omega_i\setminus J$. There exists a decreasing sequence of Jordan regions $U_n\subset \Omega_i$, $n\in \mathbb N$, such that $\partial U_n$ is the union of $E$ with an arc in $\Omega_i$ that has the same endpoints as $E$ for each $n\in \mathbb N$. We have
$$\partial G_i\setminus C= \bigcap_{n=1}^\infty \overline{\phi(U_n)},$$
so $\partial G_i\setminus C$ is the intersection of a decreasing sequence of continua. Thus, it is a continuum and \ref{lemma:crosscut_connected:2} follows.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{theorem:bounded_turning}
Let $\Omega\subset \mathbb C$ be an $L$-John domain for some $L\geq 1$. Then each component of $\mathbb C\setminus \Omega$ has $(L+1)$-bounded turning and in particular, it is arcwise connected.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $P$ be a component of $\mathbb C\setminus \Omega$. First, we consider $w_1,w_2\in  P$ such that the crosscut $C=(w_1,w_2)$ is disjoint from $P$. Since $P$ is a component of $\mathbb C\setminus \Omega$, we conclude that $\mathbb C\setminus P$ is connected. Also, if $P$ is bounded, then it is compact, and $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus  \overline P$ is a simply connected domain. If $P$ is unbounded, then $\overline P=P\cup\{\infty\}$. The domain $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus \overline P =\mathbb C\setminus P$ is again simply connected. In both cases, by Lemma \ref{lemma:crosscut_connected} the crosscut $C=(w_1,w_2)$ separates $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus \overline P$ into two components $U,V$ such that $\partial U\setminus \overline P=\partial V\setminus \overline P=C$. In particular, $w_1,w_2$ lie in both $\partial U$ and $\partial V$.

Let $w_0=(w_1+w_2)/2$ and $r=|w_1-w_2|/2$. Suppose that $U,V$ are not contained in $\mathbb D(w_0,(L+1)r)$. Since $C$ intersects the boundaries of both $U,V$ and is contained in $\mathbb D(w_0,r)$, we conclude that both $U,V$ intersect $\mathbb D(w_0,(L+1)r)$. By connectedness, since $U,V$ are not contained in $\mathbb D(w_0,(L+1)r)$, both must intersect the circle $\mathbb S(w_0,(L+1)r)$. Since $\Omega$ is a John domain, there exists a curve $\gamma\colon [0,1]\to \Omega$ with $\gamma(0)\in \mathbb S(w_0,(L+1)r)\cap U$, $\gamma(1)\in \mathbb S(w_0,(L+1)r)\cap V$, and  
$$\min\{\ell(\gamma|_{[0,t]}),\ell(\gamma|_{[t,1]})\} \leq L\operatorname{dist}(\gamma(t),\partial \Omega)\,\,\, \text{for all $t\in [0,1]$}.$$
Since $C=\partial U\setminus \overline P=\partial V\setminus \overline P$,  there exists $t_0\in (0,1)$ such that $\gamma(t_0)\in C\subset \mathbb D(w_0,r)$. We have $\ell(\gamma|_{[0,t_0]})> Lr$, $\ell(\gamma|_{[t_0,1]})> Lr$, and $\operatorname{dist}(\gamma(t_0),\partial \Omega)\leq \ell(C)/2=r$. Thus,
$$Lr< \min\{\ell(\gamma|_{[0,t_0]}),\ell(\gamma|_{[t_0,1]})\}\leq L\operatorname{dist}(\gamma(t_0),\partial \Omega)\leq  Lr,$$
a contradiction. Therefore, one of the sets $U,V$ is contained in $\mathbb D(w_0,(L+1)r)$. Without loss of generality, we suppose that $V\subset \mathbb D(w_0,(L+1)r)$. By Lemma \ref{lemma:crosscut_connected} \ref{lemma:crosscut_connected:2}, the set $E=\partial V\setminus C$ is connected. Also, $E\subset P\cap \overline \mathbb D(w_0,(L+1)r)$, it contains $w_1,w_2$, and $\operatorname{diam} E\leq 2(L+1)r =(L+1)|w_1-w_2|$.

Now, suppose that $w_1,w_2\in P$ are arbitrary distinct points. If $[w_1,w_2]\subset P$ there is nothing to show, so we suppose that $[w_1,w_2]\setminus P\neq \emptyset$. The set $[w_1,w_2]\setminus P$ has countably many components $\{C_i\}_{i\in I}$ each of which is a segment in $\mathbb C\setminus P$ with endpoints in $\partial P$. By the above, for each $i\in I$ there exists a continuum $E_i\subset P$ that contains the endpoints of $C_i$ and satisfies $\operatorname{diam} E_i\leq (L+1)\operatorname{diam} C_i$. By replacing each subsegment $C_i$ of $[w_1,w_2]$ with $E_i$ we obtain a continuum $E\subset P$ that contains $w_1,w_2$ and satisfies $\operatorname{diam} E\leq (L+1)|w_1-w_2|$. This completes the proof.

\item\label{prop:overlap:1} For each $z_0\in \mathbb C$ and $r>0$ the number of components $p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$ that intersect both complementary components of the annulus $\mathbb A(z_0;r/2,r)$ is bounded above by $L$.

\end{align*}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{align*}
|z-z_p|\leq |z-z_q|+|z_q-z_p|<Lr_q+ (r_p+r_q)/2= (L+1/2)r_q+r_p/2,
\end{align*}
which implies that

LB_q\subset \mathbb D(z_p,(L+1/2)r_q+r_p/2).
\end{align}

\begin{align*}
(L+1/2)r_q\geq r_p/2.

<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
\label{prop:shadows} 
Let $\Omega\subset \mathbb C$ be an $L$-John domain for some $L\geq 1$. Let $\mathcal C$ be the set of non-degenerate components $p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$ that do not contain $\infty$.  Then there is a constant $k=k(L)\ge 1$ such that for each $p\in \mathcal C$ there exists a ball $B_p\subset \Omega$ with
\begin{align}\label{prop:shadow:conclusion}
\operatorname{diam} B_p \le k\operatorname{diam} p, \quad p\subset kB_p, \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{p\in \mathcal C}\chi_{B_p}\leq k.
\end{align}
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let $p\in \mathcal C$ and $d_p=\operatorname{diam} p$. Suppose first that $\Omega$ is bounded. By Lemma \ref{diamanddistbounded} there is a point $z_0\in \Omega$ such that for $r_0=\operatorname{dist}(z_0,\partial \Omega)$ we have
\begin{align}\label{r0d}
\frac{1}{4L}\operatorname{diam} \Omega\le r_0 \le \operatorname{diam}\Omega.
\end{align}
Let $x_p\in \partial p$ and $z_p\in \Omega$ such that $|x_p-z_p|< \frac{d_p}{8L}$. There is a curve $\gamma_p$ in $\Omega$ connecting $z_0$ to $z_p$ as in Definition \ref{definition:john}. We observe that $|x_p-z_0|\ge r_0$ and 
\begin{align}\label{distdiam}
\operatorname{diam} |\gamma_p|&\ge |z_p-z_0| \ge \big||z_p-x_p|-|x_p-z_0|\big|> r_0-\frac{d_p}{8L}\geq \frac{d_p}{8L},
\end{align}
where the last inequality follows from \eqref{r0d} and the fact that $d_p\leq \operatorname{diam} \Omega$.

Now, suppose that $\Omega$ is unbounded and our goal is to show a similar inequality as \eqref{distdiam}.  There is $R_p>0$ such that $p\subset \mathbb D(0,R_p)$ and hence $d_p=\operatorname{diam} p \in (0,2R_p)$. Since $\Omega$ is an unbounded domain, there is a point $z_0\in \Omega\cap \mathbb{S}(0,6R_p)$. Let $x_p\in\partial p$ and $z_p\in \Omega$ such that $|z_p-x_p|< \frac{d_p}{8L} < \frac{d_p}{2}< R_p$. Then there exists a curve $\gamma_p$ in $\Omega$ connecting $z_0$ to $z_p$ as in Definition \ref{definition:john}. Note that $z_p\in \mathbb D(0,2R_p)$, so  
\begin{align}\label{distdiam:unbounded}
\operatorname{diam} |\gamma_p|\ge |z_p-z_0|> 4R_p> 2d_p > \frac{d_p}{8L}.
\end{align}
That is, inequality \eqref{distdiam} is also true in this case.

Hence, in both cases there is a point $w_p\in |\gamma_p|$ with $|w_p-z_p|=\frac{d_p}{16L}$. So, we have
\begin{align}\label{assumdistance}
|w_p-x_p| \le |w_p-z_p|+|z_p-x_p|< \frac{d_p}{16L}+ \frac{d_p}{8L}=\frac{3d_p}{16L}.
\end{align}
Moreover, as we proved in \eqref{distdiam} and \eqref{distdiam:unbounded}, we have
\[|z_p-z_0|> \frac{d_p}{8L} \]
and thus
\begin{align}\label{forjpro}
|w_p-z_0|\ge \big||w_p-z_p|-|z_p-z_0|\big|> \frac{d_p}{8L}- \frac{d_p}{16L}=\frac{d_p}{16L}.
\end{align}

Let $B_p=\mathbb D(w_p,r_p)$, where $r_p=\operatorname{dist} (w_p,\partial \Omega)$, so $B_p\subset \Omega$. By \eqref{assumdistance} we infer that
\[ r_p\le |w_p-x_p|< \frac{3d_p}{16L}. \]
Hence,
\begin{align}\label{ann1}
\operatorname{diam} B_p< \frac{3}{8L}\operatorname{diam} p<\operatorname{diam} p.
\end{align}
Moreover, by \eqref{forjpro} and the John property we deduce that
\begin{align}\label{assumjohn}
Lr_p&=L\operatorname{dist}(w_p,\partial \Omega)\geq \min \{|w_p-z_0|,|w_p-z_p|\}= \frac{d_p}{16L}.
\end{align}
Now, let $z\in p$. By \eqref{assumdistance} and \eqref{assumjohn} we have
\[|z-w_p|\le |z-x_p|+|x_p-w_p|< d_p+\frac{3d_p}{16L}< \frac{3d_p}{2}\le 24L^2r_p.\]
Hence, $z\in \mathbb D(w_p,24L^2r_p)=24L^2B_p$.  So, we have proved that $p\subset 24L^2B_p$. 

We now apply Proposition \ref{prop:overlap} in order to obtain the final inequality in \eqref{prop:shadow:conclusion} as follows. Condition \ref{prop:overlap:1} in Proposition \ref{prop:overlap} is a consequence of Proposition \ref{prop:packing} and Lemma \ref{lemma:count}. We have shown that the balls $B_p$, $p\in \mathcal C$, satisfy $\operatorname{diam} B_p\leq \operatorname{diam} p$ (see \eqref{ann1}) and $p\subset 24L^2B_p$, as required in Proposition \ref{prop:overlap} \ref{prop:overlap:2}. Hence, by Proposition \ref{prop:overlap}, we have
$$\sum_{p\in \mathcal C} \chi_{\frac{1}{2}B_p}\leq N(L).$$
Finally, for $p\in \mathcal C$ we set $B_p'=\frac{1}{2}B_p$ and note that $B_p'\subset \Omega$, $\operatorname{diam} B_p'\leq \operatorname{diam} p$, and $p\subset 48L^2B_p'$. We have proved the desired \eqref{prop:shadow:conclusion} for $k=\max\{48L^2,N(L)\}$.

\item\label{theorem:general_caratheodory:1} There exists a sequence of domains $\{\Omega_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ such that each compact subset of $\Omega$ is contained in $\Omega_j$ for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$ and for each $j\in \mathbb N$ there exists $q_j\in \mathcal C(\Omega_j)$ with $q\subset q_j$.
\item\label{theorem:general_caratheodory:2} There exists a Jordan region $U\supset q$ with  $\partial U\subset \Omega$ such that 
$$\lim_{\substack{z\to  q\\z\in \Omega}} \limsup_{j\to\infty} \operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j} \mathcal G^*(z,q_j; U)=0,$$
where $\mathcal G^*(z,q_j;U)$ is the family of curves in $\pi_{\Omega_j}(\overline U)$ with endpoints on $\partial U$ that separate $z$ and $q_j$ in $\pi_{\Omega_j}(U)$. 
\item\label{theorem:general_caratheodory:3} For each $j\in \mathbb N$, there exists a conformal map $f_j$ from $\Omega_j$ onto a $\tau$-cofat domain $D_j\subset \widehat \mathbb C$ such that $\{f_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ converges locally uniformly in $\Omega$ to a conformal map $f$ from $\Omega$ onto a domain $D\subset \widehat \mathbb C$.
\end{enumerate}
Then, for any Hausdorff limit $E$ of the sequence $\{\hat f_j(q_j)\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$, the set $\hat f(q)$ is the complement of the component of $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus E$ that contains $D$.

By passing to a subsequence, suppose that $\{\hat f_j(q_j)\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ converges to a connected compact set $E$ in the Hausdorff sense. We prove that $D\cap E=\emptyset$. Let $z\in \Omega$ and $S\subset \Omega$ be a Jordan curve that separates $z$ and $q$; the existence of $S$ can be justified with the aid of Zoretti's theorem \cite{Whyburn:topology}*{Corollary VI.3.11}. By assumption \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:1}, for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$ we have $S\subset \Omega_j$ and $q\subset q_j$, so the set $q_j$ lies in the component of $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus S$ that contains $q$. Thus, the Jordan curve $f_j(S)$ separates $\hat f_j(q_j)$ and $f_j(z)$. These three sequences of sets converge in the Hausdorff sense as $j\to\infty$ to $f(S)$, $E$, and $f(z)$, respectively. Therefore, the point $f(z)$ lies in a component of $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus f(S)$ that is disjoint from $E$. Since $z\in \Omega$ is arbitrary, we conclude that $D\cap E=\emptyset$. Let $p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)\setminus \{q\}$. We show that $\hat f(p)\subset V$. Consider a Jordan curve $S\subset \Omega$ that separates $p$ and $q$. By the above argument, $f(z)$ lies in a component of $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus f(S)$ that is disjoint from $E$. By the choice of $z$, the set $\hat f(p)$ lies in the same component of $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus f(S)$ as $f(z)$, so $\hat f(p)\cap E=\emptyset$. Thus, $\hat f(p)$ lies in a component of $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus E$.  Since $\partial \hat f(p)\subset \overline D$, we see that $\hat f(p)\subset V$, as claimed. $$\widehat \mathbb C\setminus \hat f(q)=D\cup \bigcup_{p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)\setminus \{q\}} \hat f(p) \subset V.$$
Equivalently, if we set $Q=\widehat \mathbb C\setminus V$, then $Q\subset \hat f(q)$. Our goal is to show the reverse inclusion, namely $\hat f(q)\subset Q$.  

We suppose, instead, that $\hat f(q)\setminus Q\neq \emptyset$, so $\hat f(q)\cap V\neq \emptyset$, as in Figure \ref{fig:general_caratheodory:V}. \begin{align}\label{theorem:general_caratheodory:loewner}

\end{align}
Assuming this, by the conformal invariance of transboundary modulus, we have
$$\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j}\mathcal G^*(z_n,q_j;U)\geq C_0$$
for each $n\geq N$ and for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$. Taking the $\limsup$ as $j\to\infty$ and then the limit as $n\to\infty$ we obtain a contradiction to assumption \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:2}.

\label{lemma:domains_converge}
Let $\Omega\subset \widehat \mathbb C$ be a domain. Suppose that the following conditions hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\normalfont(\arabic*)]
	\item\label{lemma:domains_converge:1} There exists a sequence of domains $\{\Omega_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ such that each compact subset of $\Omega$ is contained in $\Omega_j$ for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$.
	\item\label{lemma:domains_converge:2} For each $j\in \mathbb N$, there exists a conformal map $f_j$ from $\Omega_j$ onto a domain $D_j\subset \widehat \mathbb C$ such that $\{f_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ converges locally uniformly in $\Omega$ to a conformal map $f$ from $\Omega$ onto a domain $D\subset \widehat \mathbb C$.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\begin{enumerate}[label=\normalfont(\arabic*)]\setcounter{enumi}{2}
	\item\label{lemma:domains_converge:3} each Hausdorff limit of a sequence $p_j\in \mathcal C(\Omega_j)$, $j\in \mathbb N$, is contained in some $p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$ and
	\item\label{lemma:domains_converge:4} conditions \ref{lemma:domains_converge:1} and \ref{lemma:domains_converge:3} hold for the domain $D$ and the sequence $\{D_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$. 	
\end{enumerate}

Let $E$ be the Hausdorff limit of a sequence $p_{j_k}\in \mathcal C(\Omega_{j_k})$, $k\in \mathbb N$. If there exists a point $z_0\in E\cap \Omega$, then there exists a compact neighborhood $U$ of $z_0$ that lies in $\Omega$ and hence in $\Omega_j$ for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$ by assumption \ref{lemma:domains_converge:1}. However, $U\cap p_{j_k}\neq \emptyset$ for all sufficiently large $k\in \mathbb N$, a contradiction. Thus, $E\subset \widehat \mathbb C\setminus \Omega$. Since $E$ is connected, we have $E\subset p$ for some $p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$.

For \ref{lemma:domains_converge:4} it suffices to verify \ref{lemma:domains_converge:1} for $D$ and $\{D_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$. Let $z_0\in \Omega$ and  $w_0=f(z_0)\in D$. Consider a ball $B \subset \overline B\subset \Omega$ centered at $z_0$, so $\overline B\subset \Omega_j$ for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$. By the argument principle and the uniform convergence of $\{f_j|_{\overline B}\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ to $f|_{\overline B}$, there exists a neighborhood $U$ of $w_0$ that is contained in $f_j(\overline B)$ for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$. Thus, $U\subset D_j$ for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$. Now, given a compact set $K\subset D$, we can cover it by finitely many such neighborhoods, so we have $K\subset D_j$ for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$, as desired.

\label{lemma:potential}
Suppose that $\Omega,q$ and $\Omega_j,q_j$, $j\in \mathbb N$, are as in Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory} \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:1} and that for each $z_0\in \partial q$ and $R\in (0,\pi)$ we have 
$$ \lim_{r\to 0^+} \limsup_{j\to\infty}\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j} \Gamma(\pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,r)), \pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,R)); \hat\Omega_j\setminus \{q_j\}) =0.$$
Then condition \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:2} of Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory} is satisfied.

Consider a Jordan region $U\supset q$ with $\partial U\subset \Omega$. Also, let $z_n\in \Omega$, $n\in \mathbb N$, such that $z_n\to z_0$ for some $z_0\in \partial q$. Let  $R>0$ such that $\overline B(z_0,R)\subset U$ and let $r\in (0,R)$. Let $n_0\in \mathbb N$ such that $z_n\in B(z_0,r)$ for $n\geq n_0$. We fix $n\geq n_0$. For sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$, say for $j\geq j(n)$, we have $z_n\in \Omega_j$, so $z_n\notin q_j$. We fix $j\geq j(n)$.

We claim that each curve in $\mathcal G^*(z_n,q_j;U)$ has a subcurve in 
$$\Gamma(r,j)=\Gamma(\pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,r)), \pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,R)); \hat\Omega_j\setminus \{q_j\}).$$
Let $\gamma\in \mathcal G^*(z_n,q_j;U)$. Then $\pi_{\Omega_j}^{-1}(|\gamma|)$ is a continuum that intersects $\partial U$, so it is not contained in $B(z_0,R)$. Also, $\pi_{\Omega_{j}}^{-1}(|\gamma|)$ intersects $B(z_0,r)$, because $\gamma$ separates $z_n$ and $q_j$ in $\pi_{\Omega_j}(\overline U)$. Thus, $\pi_{\Omega_j}^{-1}(|\gamma|)$ intersects both $S(z_0,r)$ and $S(z_0,R)$. Equivalently, $|\gamma|$ intersects both $\pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,r))$ and $\pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,R))$. The claim follows. Therefore, for all $j\geq j(n)$ we have
$$\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j}\mathcal G^*(z_n,q_j;U) \leq \operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j}\Gamma(r,j)$$
This implies that 
$$\limsup_{j\to\infty }\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j} \mathcal G^*(z_n,q_j;U) \leq \limsup_{j\to\infty}\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j}\Gamma(r,j)$$
for all $n\geq n_0$. By assumption, the right-hand side converges to $0$ as $r\to 0^+$. Hence, letting $n\to \infty$ and then $r\to 0^+$ shows that
$$\lim_{\substack{z\to  q\\z\in \Omega}} \limsup_{j\to\infty} \operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j} \mathcal G^*(z,q_j; U)=0.$$
Thus, condition \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:2} of Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory} holds.

\label{theorem:koebe_nondegenerate}
In the setting of Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory}, if the condition of Lemma \ref{lemma:potential} is satisfied for $q$, then $q$ is a singleton if and only if $\hat f(q)$ is a singleton.

<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Let $f_j\colon \Omega_j\to D_j$, $j\in \mathbb N$, be a sequence of conformal maps that converges locally uniformly in $\Omega$ to a conformal map $f\colon \Omega\to D$, as in Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory} \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:3}. If $E$ is a Hausdorff limit of $\{\hat f_j(q_j)\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$, then the set $\hat f(q)$ is the complement of the component of $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus E$ that contains $D$, as in the conclusion of Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory}. In order to prove the current theorem, it suffices to show that $q$ is a singleton if and only if $E$ is a singleton.  Let $U\supset q$ be a Jordan region such that $\partial U\subset \Omega$ as in Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory} \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:2}. 

Suppose that $E$ is non-degenerate. As $j\to\infty$ we have
\begin{align*}
\Delta(\hat f_j(q_j), f_j(\partial U))=\frac{\operatorname{dist}(\hat f_j(q_j), f_j(\partial U))}{\min\{\operatorname{diam} \hat f_j(q_j), \operatorname{diam} f_j(\partial U)\}} \to \frac{\operatorname{dist}(E,f(\partial U))}{\min\{\operatorname{diam} E,\operatorname{diam} f(\partial U)\}}
\end{align*}
so there exists $\Delta>0$ such that $\Delta(\hat f_j(q_j), f_j(\partial U))\leq \Delta$ for each $j\in \mathbb N$. By the Loewner property of $\widehat \mathbb C$ \cite{HeinonenKoskela:qc}*{Section 6}, there exists $\delta>0$ such that 
$$\operatorname{Mod} \Gamma( \hat f_j(q_j), f_j(\partial U);\widehat \mathbb C) \geq \delta\,\,\, \text{for each $j\in \mathbb N$.}$$
By the modulus comparison given in Proposition \ref{proposition:comparison}, we have
$$\operatorname{Mod}_{D_j} \Gamma( \hat f_j(q_j), f_j(\partial U);\hat D_j)\geq  c(\tau)^{-1}\min\{1,\delta\}\eqqcolon C_0\,\,\, \text{for each $j\in \mathbb N$.}$$
The conformal invariance of transboundary modulus implies that
$$\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j} \Gamma( q_j, \partial U;\hat \Omega_j) \geq C_0\,\,\, \text{for each $j\in \mathbb N$.}$$
Suppose that $q$ consists of a single point $z_0$ and let $0<r<R$ such that $\overline B(z_0,R)\subset U$. By Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory} \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:1} we have $q_j\to q$ as $j\to\infty$, so for sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$ we have $q_j\subset B(z_0,r)$, say for $j\geq j_0$. We note that each curve in $\Gamma( q_j, \partial U;\hat \Omega_j)$ contains a subcurve in 
$$\Gamma(r,j)=\Gamma(\pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,r)), \pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,R));  \hat \Omega_j\setminus \{q_j\}).$$
Thus, for $j\geq j_0$ we have
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j}&\Gamma(\pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,r)), \pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,R));  \hat \Omega_j\setminus \{q_j\})\geq \operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j} \Gamma( q_j, \partial U;\hat \Omega_j) \geq C_0.
\end{align*}
Letting $j\to\infty$ and then $r\to 0^+$ contradicts the condition of Lemma \ref{lemma:potential}. Therefore, $q$ is non-degenerate.


                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      


Next, suppose that $E$ is a single point $w_0$. By passing to a subsequence, suppose that $\hat f_j(q_j)\to w_0$ as $j\to\infty$. We conclude that $\hat f(q)=w_0$ is a complementary component of $D$. Consider $R_1>0$ such that the ball $\overline B(w_0,R_1)$ is contained in the component of $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus f(\partial U)$ that contains $w_0$. We let $A_1=A(w_0;R_1/2,R_1)$. Since $w_0\in \mathcal C(D)$, there exists $R_2<R_1/2$ such that no $p\in \mathcal C(D)$ intersects both annuli $\overline A_1$ and $\overline A_2$, where $A_2=A(w_0;R_2/2,R_2)$.  Inductively, for each $i\in \mathbb N$ we may find an annulus $A_i=A(w_0;R_i/2,R_i)$ with $R_{i+1}<R_i/2$ for $i\in \mathbb N$ such that no $p\in \mathcal C(D)$ intersects both $\overline A_i$ and $\overline A_{i+1}$. As a consequence, for each fixed $i\in \mathbb N$ and for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$ no component $p\in \mathcal C(D_j)$ intersects both annuli $\overline A_i$ and $\overline A_{i+1}$. Indeed, if there exists $p_j\in \mathcal C(D_j)$ that intersects $\overline A_i$ and $\overline A_{i+1}$ for infinitely many $j\in \mathbb N$, then there exists a Hausdorff limit $F$ of $\{p_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ that intersects both $\overline A_i$ and $\overline A_{i+1}$. By Lemma \ref{lemma:domains_converge} \ref{lemma:domains_converge:4}, there exists $p\in \mathcal C(D)$ such that $F\subset p$. Thus, $p$ intersects both $\overline A_i$ and $\overline A_{i+1}$, a contradiction.

Let $N\in \mathbb N$. For all sufficiently large $j\in\mathbb N$, no $p\in \mathcal C(D_j)$ intersects two of the annuli $A_i$, $i\in \{1,\dots,N\}$. Also, there exists $C_0>0$ such that
$$\operatorname{Mod} \Gamma^*(A_i) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \log \frac{\tan(R_i/2)}{\tan(R_i/4)}\geq C_0 \,\,\, \text{for all $i\in \{1,\dots,N\}$}.$$
By the modulus comparison from Proposition \ref{proposition:comparison}, we have 
$$\operatorname{Mod}_{D_j}\pi_{D_j}(\Gamma^*(A_i)) \geq c(\tau)^{-1} \min\{ 1, C_0\}\eqqcolon C_1\,\,\, \text{for all $j\in \mathbb N$}.$$
For all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$ we have $\hat f_j(q_j) \subset B(w_0, R_N/2)$. This implies that $\pi_{D_j}(\Gamma^*(A_i))\subset \Gamma^*(\hat f_j(q_j),  f_j(\partial U);\hat D_j)$ for each $i\in \{1,\dots,N\}$. Let $\rho\colon \hat D_j\to [0,\infty]$ be admissible for the transboundary modulus of $\Gamma^*(\hat f_j(q_j),  f_j(\partial U);\hat D_j)$ and for $i\in \{1,\dots,N\}$ we define 
$$\widetilde \rho_i(z)=\begin{cases}\rho(z)\cdot \chi_{A_i\cap D_j}(z),& z\in D_j\\
\rho(z), & \text{$z=p$ for some $p\in \mathcal C(D_j)$ with $p\cap A_i\neq \emptyset$}\\
0,& \text{$z=p$ for some $p\in \mathcal C(D_j)$ with $p\cap A_i=\emptyset$}
\end{cases}.$$
Then $\widetilde \rho_i$ is admissible for $\pi_{D_j}(\Gamma^*(A_i))$ for each $i\in \{1,\dots,N\}$. Since the annuli $A_1,\dots,A_N$ are disjoint and each component $p\in \mathcal C(D_j)$ can intersect at most one of these annuli for sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\int_{D_j} \rho^2\, d\Sigma+\sum_{p\in \mathcal C(D_j)}\rho(p)^2 &\geq \sum_{i=1}^N \left(\int_{D_j} \widetilde\rho_i^2\, d\Sigma+\sum_{p\in \mathcal C(D_j)} \widetilde \rho_i(p)^2\right) \\
&\geq \sum_{i=1}^N\operatorname{Mod}_{D_j}\pi_{D_j}(\Gamma^*(A_i))\geq C_1N.
\end{align*}
Infimizing over $\rho$ gives
$$\operatorname{Mod}_{D_j}\Gamma^*(\hat f_j(q_j),  f_j(\partial U); \hat D_j) \geq C_1N.$$ 
By letting $j\to\infty$ and then $N\to\infty$, we obtain
$$\lim_{j\to\infty}\operatorname{Mod}_{D_j}\Gamma^*(\hat f_j(q_j),  f_j(\partial U);\hat D_j)=\infty.$$
By the conformal invariance of transboundary modulus, we have
\begin{align}\label{theorem:koebe_nondegenerate:infinity}
\lim_{j\to\infty}\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j}\Gamma^*(q_j,  \partial U;\hat \Omega_j)=\infty.
\end{align}


For the sake of contradiction, suppose that $q$ is non-degenerate. Let $z_0\in \partial q$ and fix $R>0$ such that $\overline B(z_0,R)\subset U$ and $q\not\subset B(z_0,R)$. Also, by the condition of Lemma \ref{lemma:potential}, there exist $r\in (0,R)$ and $j_0\in \mathbb N$ such that 
$$\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j} \Gamma(j)<1\,\,\, \text{for $j\geq j_0$},$$
where $\Gamma(j)=\Gamma(\pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,r)), \pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,R));  \hat \Omega_j\setminus \{q_j\})$. If $S(z_0,r)\subset \widehat \mathbb C\setminus \Omega$, then $S(z_0,r)\subset q$, so $\overline B(z_0,r)\subset q$, which contradicts the assumption that $z_0\in \partial q$. Thus $S(z_0,r)\cap \Omega\neq \emptyset$. Consider disjoint arcs $S,T$ in $\Omega$ connecting $S(z_0,r)$ and $\partial U$. Also, let $\delta>0$ such that $\overline{N_\delta(T)}\subset \Omega$ and $S\cap {N_\delta(T)}= \emptyset$, where
$$N_\delta(T)= \{z\in \widehat \mathbb C: \operatorname{dist}(z,T)<\delta\}.$$
See Figure \ref{fig:potential} for an illustration. For all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$, we have $S\cup \overline {N_\delta(T)}\subset \Omega_j$ by Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory} \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:1}. Let $\rho_j$ be an admissible density for $\Gamma(j)$ with total mass bounded above by $1$. We define $\widetilde \rho_j=\rho_j$ in the complement of $\Omega_j$ and $\widetilde \rho_j=\rho_j+ \delta^{-1}\chi_{N_\delta(T)}$ in $\Omega_j$. Note that 
$$\int_{\Omega_j} \widetilde \rho_j^2\, d\Sigma+ \sum_{p\in \mathcal C(\Omega_j)}\widetilde \rho_j(p)^2\leq (1+\delta^{-1}\Sigma(N_\delta(T))^{1/2})^2\eqqcolon C_2$$

$$\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j}\Gamma^*(q_j,  \partial U;\hat \Omega_j)\leq C_2 $$
for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$. This contradicts \eqref{theorem:koebe_nondegenerate:infinity}, so $q$ must be a singleton.

\label{lemma:potential_sufficient}
Suppose that $\Omega,q$ and $\Omega_j,q_j$, $j\in \mathbb N$, are as in Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory} \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:1} and that the following conditions hold.

\item\label{lemma:potential_sufficient:3} For each $z_0\in \partial q$ there exists a sequence of annuli $\{A_i=\widehat \mathbb C\setminus (E_i\cup F_i)\}_{i\in \mathbb N}$ that separate $z_0$ from a fixed point of $\Omega$, converge to $z_0$ in the Hausdorff sense, and
$$\sup_{i\in \mathbb N} \left( \limsup_{j\to\infty}\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j} \Gamma(\pi_{\Omega_j}(E_i),\pi_{\Omega_j}(F_i); \pi_{\Omega_j}(\overline A_i)) \right)<\infty.$$

Let $R\in (0,\pi)$, $z_0\in \partial q$, and $N\in \mathbb N$. By assumption \ref{lemma:potential_sufficient:3}, there exist annuli $A_{i}$, $i\in \mathbb N$, that separate $z_0$ from a fixed base point $z_\infty\in \Omega$,  shrink to $z_0$ as $i\to\infty$, and satisfy
\begin{align}\label{assumption:pointwise}
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j} \Gamma(i,j) < C(z_0)\,\,\, \text{for all $i\in \mathbb N$},
\end{align}
where $\Gamma(i,j)=\Gamma(\pi_{\Omega_j}(E_i),\pi_{\Omega_j}(F_i); \pi_{\Omega_j}(\overline A_i))$. We let $i_1\in \mathbb N$ such that $A_{i_1}\subset B(z_0,R)$. $$\operatorname{Mod}_{\Omega_j}\Gamma(\pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,r)), \pi_{\Omega_j}(S(z_0,R)); \hat \Omega_j\setminus \{q_j\})\leq \frac{C(z_0)}{N}$$
for every $r\in (0,\delta)$ and for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$. By letting $j\to\infty$, $r\to 0^+$, and $N\to\infty$, we obtain the condition of Lemma \ref{lemma:potential}, as desired.

\label{lemma:characteristics}
Let $\Omega\subset \widehat \mathbb C$ be a domain and $A=\mathbb A(z_0;t/2,t)$ be an annulus, where $z_0\in \widehat \mathbb C$ and $t>0$, satisfying the following conditions for some $L\geq 1$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\normalfont(\arabic*)]
	\item\label{lemma:characteristics:1} The number of components $p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$ that intersect both complementary components of the annulus $\mathbb A(z_0;t,2t)$ is bounded above by $L$. 
	\item\label{lemma:characteristics:2} There exists a set $\mathcal C\subset \mathcal C(\Omega)$ with $\mathcal C(\Omega)\setminus \mathcal C$ having at most $L$ non-degenerate elements such that for each $p\in \mathcal C$ there exists a ball $B_p\subset \mathbb C$ with
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{diam} B_p \leq L\operatorname{diam} p,\quad   p\subset LB_p,\quad \text{and}\quad \sum_{p\in \mathcal C}\chi_{B_p}\leq L.
\end{align*}
\end{enumerate}
Then 
$$\sum_{\substack{p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)\\ p\cap A\neq \emptyset}} w_{A} (p)^2 \leq C(L).$$
<!-- end lemma-9 -->
<!-- proof-16: body_md -->
Consider the annulus $A=\mathbb A(z_0;t/2,t)$. If $z_0=\infty$, then $A= \mathbb A(\infty;t/2,t)=\mathbb A(0;t^{-1},2t^{-1})$, so it suffices to consider the case that $z_0\in \mathbb C$. 

Let $p\in \mathcal C$ such that $p\cap A\neq \emptyset$. By \ref{lemma:characteristics:2} we have $p\subset LB_p$. Note that there exists a ball in $A\cap (LB_p)$ of diameter equal to $R_A(LB_p)-r_A(LB_p)$. Hence,
\begin{align}\label{lemma:characteristics:ineq1}
(R_A(p)-r_A(p))^2 &\leq (R_A(LB_p)-r_A(LB_p))^2 \leq \frac{4}{\pi} m_2( A\cap (LB_p)).
\end{align}
If $p\subset \mathbb D(z_0,2t)$, then $\operatorname{diam} p<4t$, so by assumption \ref{lemma:characteristics:2} we have $\operatorname{diam} B_p\leq L\operatorname{diam} p<4Lt$ and $\operatorname{diam}(LB_p)< 4L^2t$. Since $\mathbb D(z_0,t)\cap p\neq \emptyset$, we have $\mathbb D(z_0,t)\cap (LB_p)\neq \emptyset$, which implies that
$$B_p\subset LB_p\subset \mathbb D(z_0,Mt),$$
where $M=1+4L^2$. Thus, 
\begin{align}\label{lemma:characteristics:ineq2}
m_2( A\cap (LB_p))\leq m_2(LB_p)= L^2 \int_{\mathbb D(z_0,Mt)} \chi_{B_p}\, dm_2.
\end{align} 
By combining \eqref{lemma:characteristics:ineq1} and \eqref{lemma:characteristics:ineq2}, we conclude that
\begin{align*}
\sum_{\substack{p\in \mathcal C\\ p\cap A\neq \emptyset, \,p\subset \mathbb D(z_0,2t)}} (R_A(p)-r_A(p))^2&\leq  \frac{4L^2}{\pi}\sum_{p\in \mathcal C}\int_{\mathbb D(z_0,Mt)} \chi_{B_p}\, dm_2 \\
&=  \frac{4L^2}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb D(z_0,Mt)} \sum_{p\in \mathcal C} \chi_{B_p}\, dm_2 \leq 4L^3M^2 t^2;
\end{align*}
the last inequality follows from assumption \ref{lemma:characteristics:2}, which implies that $\mathcal C$ is countable and $\sum_{p\in \mathcal C}\chi_{B_p}$ is measurable and bounded above by $L$. Note that
$$w_A(p)=\log \frac{R_A(p)}{r_A(p)}\leq \frac{R_A(p)-r_A(p)}{r_A(p)}\leq 2\frac{R_A(p)-r_A(p)}{t}.$$
We conclude that
\begin{align}\label{lemma:characteristics:ineq3}
\sum_{\substack{p\in \mathcal C\\ p\cap A\neq \emptyset,\,p\subset \mathbb D(z_0,2t)}} w_A(p)^2\leq 16L^3M^2.
\end{align}

Suppose that $p\cap A\neq\emptyset $ and $p\not\subset \mathbb D(z_0,2t)$. Such components intersect both complementary components of $\mathbb A(z_0;t,2t)$. By assumption \ref{lemma:characteristics:1}, there exist at most $L$ such components $p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$. Also, we have  $w_A(p)\leq \log 2$ for each $p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$. Thus,
\begin{align}\label{lemma:characteristics:ineq4}
\sum_{\substack{p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)\\ p\cap A\neq \emptyset,\, p\not\subset \mathbb D(z_0,2t)}} w_A(p)^2\leq  L(\log2)^2.
\end{align}
Finally, by assumption \ref{lemma:characteristics:2} the set $\mathcal C(\Omega)\setminus \mathcal C$ has at most $L$ non-degenerate elements.   Since $w_A(p)=0$ for degenerate components $p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$, we have 
\begin{align}\label{lemma:characteristics:ineq5}
\sum_{p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)\setminus \mathcal C} w_A(p)^2\leq  L(\log2)^2.
\end{align}
By combining \eqref{lemma:characteristics:ineq3}, \eqref{lemma:characteristics:ineq4}, and \eqref{lemma:characteristics:ineq5}, we conclude that
$$\sum_{\substack{p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)\\ p\cap A\neq \emptyset}} w_{A} (p)^2 \leq 16L^3M^2+ 2L(\log2)^2.$$
This completes the proof.

\label{theorem:john:restate}
Let $\Omega\subset \mathbb C$ be a John domain. Then there exists a conformal map $f$ from $\Omega$ onto a circle domain. Moreover for each $q\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$ the set $\hat f(q)$ is a singleton if and only if $q$ is a singleton.

By Lemma \ref{lemma:diameter}, for each $j\in \mathbb N$ at most finitely many components of $\mathcal C(\Omega)$ have spherical diameter greater than $j^{-1}$. Let $\Omega_j'$ be the finitely connected domain such that $\mathcal C(\Omega_j')$ consists of these components. By the Koebe uniformization theorem, for $j\in \mathbb N$ there exists a conformal map $f_j$ from $\Omega_j'$ onto a circle domain $D_j'$, which we normalize so that it fixes three points of $\Omega$. By a normality criterion \cite{LehtoVirtanen:quasiconformal}*{Theorem II.5.1}, and by passing to a subsequence, we may assume that $\{f_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ converges locally uniformly in $\Omega$ to a conformal map from $\Omega$ onto a domain $D\subset \widehat \mathbb C$. Our goal is to show that $D$ is a circle domain. For this, it suffices to show that Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory} is applicable for each $q\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$. Indeed, then for any Hausdorff limit $E$ of $\{\hat f_j(q_j)\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$, which is necessarily a disk or a point, we have $\hat f(q)=E$, so $\hat f(q)$ is a disk or a point.

We first verify conditions \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:1} and \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:3} of Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory}. For $j\in \mathbb N$ we set $\Omega_j=\Omega_j'\setminus q$ and $D_j=f_j(\Omega_j)=D_j'\setminus f_j(q)$. Proposition \ref{prop:packing} and Lemma \ref{lemma:count} imply that condition \ref{lemma:characteristics:1} of Lemma \ref{lemma:characteristics} holds for $\Omega$. Moreover, Proposition \ref{prop:shadows} shows that condition \ref{lemma:characteristics:2} of Lemma \ref{lemma:characteristics} holds; here $\mathcal C$ is taken to be the collection of non-degenerate components $p\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$ that do not contain $\infty$, so $\mathcal C(\Omega)\setminus \mathcal C$ has at most one non-degenerate element. Condition \ref{prop:geometric_sufficient:1} of Proposition \ref{prop:geometric_sufficient} also holds, by Lemma \ref{lemma:diameter}. Thus, condition \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:2} of Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory} is true, as desired. <!-- proof-18: body_md -->

By Koebe's uniformization theorem, there exists a sequence of conformal maps $f_j$ from $\Omega_j$ onto a circle domain $D_j$, $j\in \mathbb N$. By appropriate normalizations, we may assume that there exist three points of $\Omega$ that are fixed by $f_j$ for each $j\in \mathbb N$. Thus, by a normality criterion \cite{LehtoVirtanen:quasiconformal}*{Theorem II.5.1}, there exists a subsequence of $\{f_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ that converges locally uniformly to a conformal map $f$ from $\Omega$ onto a domain $D$.  Note that for each $q\in \mathcal C(\Omega)$ the set $\hat f_j(q_j(q))$ is a disk for each $j\in \mathbb N$. Thus, all Hausdorff limits of $\{\hat f_j(q_j(q))\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ are disks or points. Let $E$ be such a Hausdorff limit. By Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory}, the set $\hat f(q)$ is the complement of the component of $\widehat \mathbb C\setminus E$ that contains $D$. Hence, $\hat f(q)=E$, the set $\hat f(q)$ is a disk or a point, and
$$\lim_{j\to\infty}\hat f_j(q_j(q)) =\hat f(q).$$
Therefore, $D$ is a circle domain. Thus, Koebe's conjecture is true for $\Omega$. Note that the last part of the theorem follows from the above argument. 

Conversely, suppose that Koebe's conjecture is true for $\Omega$, so there exists a conformal map $f$ from $\Omega$ onto a circle domain $D$. Without loss of generality, suppose that $\infty\in \Omega$ and $f(\infty)=\infty$. By \cite{NtalampekosRajala:exhaustion}*{Theorem 2.1} there exists an exhaustion $\{D_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ of $D$ that is $K_0$-\textit{quasiround} for $K_0=43$, meaning that for each $j\in \mathbb N$ and $q\in \mathcal C(D_j)$ there exists a ball $B_q$ in $\mathbb C$ such that
\begin{align}\label{theorem:exhaustion:quasiround}
B_q\subset q\subset K_0B_q.
\end{align}
Let $\{\Omega_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ be the preimage of this exhaustion under $f$. We show \eqref{theorem:exhaustion:claim} for this exhaustion.  Consider a ball $B$ in $\mathbb C$ such that $B\supset \widehat \mathbb C\setminus D$ and let $U$ be the Jordan region that is bounded by $f^{-1}(\partial B)$ and does not contain $\infty$. By the conformal invariance of transboundary modulus, it suffices to prove that 
$$\lim_{\substack{w\to q\\w\in D}} \limsup_{j\to\infty} \operatorname{Mod}_{D_j} \mathcal G^*(w,q_j(q);B) =0$$
for every $q\in \mathcal C(D)$. That is, we wish to verify condition \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory:2} of Theorem \ref{theorem:general_caratheodory} for the domain $D$, the component $q\in \mathcal C(D)$, the sequence $\{D_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$, and the components $q_j=q_j(q)\in \mathcal C(D_j)$. It suffices to verify the condition of Lemma \ref{lemma:potential}. For this, it is enough to establish condition \ref{prop:geometric_sufficient:3} of Proposition \ref{prop:geometric_sufficient} for $A_r=\mathbb A(z_0;r/2,r)$, where $z_0\in \partial q$ and $r>0$. 

We verify the assumptions of Lemma \ref{lemma:characteristics} for the annulus $A_r$ and the domain $D_j$ when $j$ is large enough. If a ball $B$ intersects both complementary components of $A_{2r}$, then there exists a ball $B'\subset B\cap A_{2r}$ of radius $r/2$. Thus, $m_2(A_{2r}\cap B)\geq \pi r^2/4$. Hence, if a collection of $N$ disjoint balls intersect both complementary components of $A_{2r}$, then
$$N \frac{\pi r^2}{4} \leq m_2(A_{2r})= 3\pi r^2.$$
That is, at most $12$ elements of $\mathcal C(D)$ can intersect both boundary components of $A_{2r}$. Since $\{D_j\}_{j\in \mathbb N}$ is an exhaustion of $D$, we conclude that for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$ at most $12$ components $p\in \mathcal C(D_j)$ intersect both boundary components of $A_{2r}$. Thus, condition \ref{lemma:characteristics:1} of Lemma \ref{lemma:characteristics} holds for the domain $D_j$ for all sufficiently large $j\in \mathbb N$ with constant $L=12$. Also, condition \ref{lemma:characteristics:2} of Lemma \ref{lemma:characteristics} holds automatically with constant $K_0$ by \eqref{theorem:exhaustion:quasiround}. By Lemma \ref{lemma:characteristics}, we conclude that
$$\limsup_{j\to\infty}\sum_{\substack{p\in \mathcal C(D_j)\\ p\cap A_r\neq \emptyset}} w_{A_r} (p)^2 \leq C$$
for a universal constant $C>0$, independent of $z_0$ and $r$. Therefore,
$$\liminf_{r\to 0^+}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\sum_{\substack{p\in \mathcal C(D_j)\\ p\cap A_r\neq \emptyset}} w_{A_r} (p)^2 <\infty.$$
This verifies condition \ref{prop:geometric_sufficient:3} of Proposition \ref{prop:geometric_sufficient}, as desired.
