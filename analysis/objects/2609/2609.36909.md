<!-- conj-1: body_md -->
\label{Generalized Franchetta Conjecture}
     Assume $g\ge3$. For each closed point $x\in \mathcal{F}_g'$, denote $S_x:=\pi^{-1}(x)$. Then, for any class $a\in \operatorname{CH}^2(\mathcal{S}_g')$ and any closed point $x\in \mathcal{F}_g'$, the restriction~$a|_{S_x}$ is a multiple of the Beauville--Voisin class $o_{S_x}$.
<!-- end conj-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Suppose that $g\ge3$ and $\mathcal{S}_g'$ is rationally connected. Then the generalized Franchetta conjecture holds for genus $g$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:genus22}
Conjecture \ref{Generalized Franchetta Conjecture} holds for $g=22$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
For $3\le g\le10$ and $g=12,13,16,18,20$, Mukai models give unirational
parametrizations of the universal K3 surfaces \cite[Section 2]{PSY}. For $g=14$, the unirationality of the universal K3 surface follows from
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{FV14}. For $g=11$, Mukai's program \cite{Muk} induces a birational map between a projective bundle on $\mathcal{S}_{11}'$ and $\mathcal{C}_{11}$, the universal family of smooth curves of genus $11$, and $\mathcal{C}_{11}$ is unirational by \cite[Corollary 2]{Bar}. Therefore, Theorem \ref{thm:main} recovers the
previously known cases of Conjecture \ref{Generalized Franchetta Conjecture}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm: cohomological GFC}
Assume $g\ge3$. For every $\alpha\in\operatorname{CH}^2(\mathcal{S}_g')$, there exists
$t\in\mathbb{Q}$ and a finite union $D\subset\mathcal{F}_g'$ of Noether--Lefschetz
divisors such that, setting $V:=\mathcal{F}_g'\setminus D$ and
$\mathcal{S}_V:=\pi^{-1}(V)$, one has
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathcal{S}_V}\bigl((\alpha-tc_2(T_\pi))|_{\mathcal{S}_V}\bigr)
 =0\in H^4(\mathcal{S}_V,\mathbb{Q}).
\]

Throughout this paper, $\operatorname{CH}^*(X)_{\hom}$ is the group of integrally homologically trivial cycles.
<!-- end head-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:lifting}
Let $X$ be a smooth quasi-projective variety, and let
$j:X\hookrightarrow\overline X$ be a smooth projective compactification such
that $D:=\overline X\setminus X$ is a simple normal crossings divisor.
Then for every homologically trivial cycle $\alpha\in\operatorname{CH}^2(X)_{\mathrm{hom}}$, there exist an integer $N>0$
and a homologically trivial cycle $\overline\alpha\in\operatorname{CH}^2(\overline X)_{\mathrm{hom}}$ such that
\[
 j^*\overline\alpha=N\alpha.
\]
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Write $D=\bigcup_{i\in I}D_i$ as its irreducible components, and let
$\iota_i:D_i\hookrightarrow\overline X$ be the inclusions. Each $D_i$ is
smooth and projective. Deligne's weight spectral sequence
\cite{Del} gives the exact sequence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weight-four}
 \bigoplus_{i\in I}H^2(D_i,\mathbb{Q})(-1)
 \xrightarrow{\ f\ }H^4(\overline X,\mathbb{Q})
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Gr}^W_4H^4(X,\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
where $f=\sum_i(\iota_i)_*$ is the sum of the Gysin maps, and $\operatorname{Gr}^W_4H^4(X,\mathbb{Q})$ is the graded piece of the weight filtration on $H^*(X,\mathbb{Q})$.

The localization exact sequence for Chow groups allows us to choose
$\alpha'\in\operatorname{CH}^2(\overline X)$ with $j^*\alpha'=\alpha$. Put
$c:=\operatorname{cl}_{\overline X}(\alpha')\in H^4(\overline X,\mathbb{Q})$. Since $\alpha$ is
homologically trivial, $j^*c=0$ in $H^4(X,\mathbb{Q})$.

By the definition of the weight filtration, we have that $\operatorname{Gr}_4^WH^4(X,\mathbb{Q})$ is the image of 
$$j^*:H^4(\overline X, \mathbb{Q})\to H^4(X,\mathbb{Q}).$$
Therefore, the exact sequence \ref{eq:weight-four} implies that $c\in \operatorname{Im} f$.

The map $f$ is a morphism of polarizable rational Hodge structures.
Since the category of polarizable rational Hodge structures is semisimple, there exists a splitting morphism between rational Hodge structures
\[
 h:\operatorname{Im} f\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i\in I}H^2(D_i,\mathbb{Q})(-1)
\]
such that 
$$f\circ h=\mathrm{id}_{\operatorname{Im} f}.$$
Write $h(c)=(c_i(-1))_{i\in I}$, with each $c_i\in H^2(D_i,\mathbb{Q})$. Since $h$ preserves rational Hodge structure, each $c_i$
is a rational Hodge class of type $(1,1)$ on $D_i$. By the Lefschetz $(1,1)$-theorem, there exists an integer
$N_0>0$ and divisors $\gamma_i\in\operatorname{CH}^1(D_i)$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_{D_i}(\gamma_i)=N_0c_i\qquad\text{in }H^2(D_i,\mathbb{Q}).
\]
Consequently, the class
\[
 \beta:=N_0\alpha'-\sum_{i\in I}(\iota_i)_*\gamma_i
 \in\operatorname{CH}^2(\overline X)
\]
has zero rational cohomology class and satisfies $j^*\beta=N_0\alpha$.

Using that cycle class maps commutes with proper pushforwards, we have
$$\operatorname{cl}_{\overline X}(\beta)=N_0\operatorname{cl}_{\overline X}(\alpha')-\sum_{i\in I} (\iota_i)_*\operatorname{cl}_{D_i}(\gamma_i)=N_0c-N_0\sum_{i\in I} (\iota_i)_*c_i=0.$$
Therefore, taking $\overline \alpha=m\beta$ for some positive integer $m$, such that $\overline\alpha$ is integrally homologically trivial satisfies the requirement.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:open-vanishing}
Let $X$ be a smooth, rationally connected, quasi-projective variety. Suppose that
\[
 \operatorname{Gr}^W_3H^3(X,\mathbb{Q})=0.
\]
Then the group $\operatorname{CH}^2(X)_{\mathrm{hom}}$ of homologically trivial cycles is torsion. In particular, the rational
cycle-class map
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_X:\operatorname{CH}^2(X)_\mathbb{Q}\longrightarrow H^4(X,\mathbb{Q})
\]
is injective.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By resolution of singularities \cite{Hir}, we can choose a smooth projective
compactification $j:X\hookrightarrow\overline X$ for which
$D:=\overline X\setminus X$ is a simple normal crossings divisor. The variety
$\overline X$ is rationally connected. Write
$D=\bigcup_{i\in I}D_i$ as its irreducible components, and denote $\iota_i:D_i\hookrightarrow\overline X$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:lifting}.
                                                                      
                                                                       
                                                                  
                                                                      
                                                                

Deligne's weight spectral sequence \cite{Del} gives an exact sequence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weight-three}
 \bigoplus_{i\in I}H^1(D_i,\mathbb{Q})(-1)
 \xrightarrow{\ \varphi\ }H^3(\overline X,\mathbb{Q})
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Gr}^W_3H^3(X,\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
where $\varphi=\sum_i(\iota_i)_*$ is the sum of Gysin pushforwards. The last term is zero by assumption, so $\varphi$ is surjective. 

Since the Gysin map preserves integral Hodge structures, we have that $\varphi$ maps
\[
 \bigoplus_{i\in I}H^1(D_i,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{free}}(-1)
 \quad\text{into}\quad H^3(\overline X,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{free}}.
\]
Here $H^*(\overline X,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{free}}:=H^*(\overline X,\mathbb{Z})/H^*(\overline X,\mathbb{Z})_\mathrm{tor}$, similarly for $D_i$.

Denote
$$J^3(\overline X):=H^3(\overline X,\mathbb{C})/(H^3(\overline X,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{free}}\oplus F^2H^3(\overline X,\mathbb{C}))$$
then we have the Abel–Jacobi map \cite{VoiII}
$$\operatorname{AJ}_{\overline X}: \operatorname{CH}^2(\overline X)_{\hom}\to J^3(\overline X).$$
We find that $\varphi$ induces a homomorphism of complex tori
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:boundary-jacobian}
 \psi:\prod_{i\in I}\operatorname{Pic}^0(D_i)\longrightarrow J^3(\overline X).
\end{equation}
Surjectivity of $\varphi$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, and hence over $\mathbb{C}$, implies that $\psi$ is surjective.

Therefore, we have the following diagram
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:boundary-aj-diagram}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
 \displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in I}\operatorname{CH}^1(D_i)_{\mathrm{hom}}
   \arrow[r,"{\sum_i(\iota_i)_*}"]
   \arrow[d,"{\bigoplus_i\operatorname{AJ}_{D_i}}"']
 &\operatorname{CH}^2(\overline X)_{\mathrm{hom}}\arrow[d,"\operatorname{AJ}_{\overline X}"]\\
 \displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}\operatorname{Pic}^0(D_i)
   \arrow[r,"\psi"']
 &J^3(\overline X)
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
and from the definition of Gysin pushforward and Abel–Jacobi map, we can check it is commutative.

For any $\alpha\in\operatorname{CH}^2(X)_{\mathrm{hom}}$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:lifting},
there exists $m>0$ and $\alpha'\in\operatorname{CH}^2(\overline X)_{\mathrm{hom}}$ such that
$j^*\alpha'=m\alpha$. By \cite[Theorem 1(ii)]{BS}, we may replace $\alpha'$ with a multiple such that $\alpha'$ is algebraically trivial. The surjectivity of $\psi$
and the commutative diagram \eqref{eq:boundary-aj-diagram} give classes
$\beta_i\in\operatorname{CH}^1(D_i)_{\hom}$ with
\[
 \operatorname{AJ}_{\overline X}(\alpha')
 =\operatorname{AJ}_{\overline X}\left(\sum_{i\in I}(\iota_i)_*\beta_i\right).
\]
Set
\[
 \gamma:=\alpha'-\sum_{i\in I}(\iota_i)_*\beta_i
 \in\operatorname{CH}^2(\overline X)_{\hom}.
\]
Then $\operatorname{AJ}_{\overline X}(\gamma)=0$. 

By \cite[Theorem 1(i)]{BS} and \cite[Theorem 1.9]{Mur}, the restriction of $\operatorname{AJ}_{\overline X}$ to $\operatorname{CH}^2(\overline X)_{\mathrm{alg}}$ is an isomorphism. Since homological equivalence and algebraic equivalence coincide on divisors, we have $\beta_i$ is algebraically trivial. Therefore, $\gamma\in \operatorname{CH}^2(\overline X)_{\mathrm{alg}}$. Hence $\gamma=0$. 

The restriction of $\gamma$ on $X$ is $m\alpha$. Hence $\alpha$ is torsion.

<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:universal-weight}
For every dense open subset $V\subset\mathcal{F}_g'$, denote $\mathcal{S}_V:=\mathcal{S}_g'\times_{\mathcal{F}_g'} V$, one has
\[
\operatorname{Gr}^W_3H^3(\mathcal{S}_V,\mathbb{Q})=0.
\]
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
By the property of weight filtration, it is enough to
prove the lemma for $V=\mathcal{F}_g'$.
Consider the Leray spectral sequence for
$\pi:\mathcal{S}_g'\to\mathcal{F}_g'$:
\[
E_2^{p,q}=H^p(\mathcal{F}_g',R^q\pi_*\mathbb{Q})
\ \Longrightarrow\ H^{p+q}(\mathcal{S}_g',\mathbb{Q}).
\]
Since a K3 surface $S$ satisfies $H^1(S,\mathbb{Q})=H^3(S,\mathbb{Q})=0$, the only
terms that could contribute to $H^3(\mathcal{S}_g',\mathbb{Q})$ are
\[
H^3(\mathcal{F}_g',\mathbb{Q})
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
H^1(\mathcal{F}_g',R^2\pi_*\mathbb{Q}).
\]
Compatibility of the Leray filtration with mixed Hodge structures
shows that the only terms that could contribute to $\operatorname{Gr}^W_3H^3(\mathcal{S}_g',\mathbb{Q})$ are 
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Gr}^W_3H^3(\mathcal{F}_g',\mathbb{Q})
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{Gr}^W_3H^1(\mathcal{F}_g',R^2\pi_*\mathbb{Q}).
\end{equation}
Here, $R^2\pi_*\mathbb{Q}$ carries its natural variation of Hodge structure
of weight two.

Following the argument in \cite[Section 3]{BL}, we can take an orthogonal
arithmetic quotient $Y$ of type $\mathrm{SO}(2,19)$ and its dense open subset $\mathcal{F}^l$, together with a finite surjective morphism $p:\mathcal{F}^l\to \mathcal{F}_g'$. Here, the variety $\mathcal{F}^l$ is a dense open subset of a connected component of the moduli stack of polarized K3 surfaces of genus $g$ with a full level structure. Following \cite{BL}, the pullback $p^*R^2\pi_*\mathbb{Q}$ is isomorphic to the local system corresponding to the universal family on $\mathcal{F}^l$. Hence, it could be extended into an automorphic local system $\mathbf E$ on $Y$.

                                                                     
                                                                     
                                                                
                                                                       
                                                                   
Apply \cite[Proposition~6.2.1]{BL} to the constant system $\mathbb Q$ and to $\mathbf E$, we have that
\begin{equation}
H^3(Y,\mathbb{Q})=0,
\qquad H^1(Y,\mathbf E)=0.
\end{equation}
We carry a weight $2$ variation of Hodge structure on $\mathbf E$. Using the property of weight filtration, we have that 
\begin{align}
W_3H^3(\mathcal{F}^l,\mathbb{Q})
&\subseteq\operatorname{Im}\bigl(H^3(Y,\mathbb{Q})
\longrightarrow H^3(\mathcal{F}^l,\mathbb{Q})\bigr),\\
W_3H^1(\mathcal{F}^l,p^*R^2\pi_*\mathbb{Q})
&\subseteq\operatorname{Im}\bigl(H^1(Y,\mathbf E)
\longrightarrow H^1(\mathcal{F}^l,p^*R^2\pi_*\mathbb{Q})\bigr).
\notag
\end{align}
Therefore, 
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Gr}^W_3H^3(\mathcal{F}^l,\mathbb{Q})=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{Gr}^W_3H^1(\mathcal{F}^l,p^*R^2\pi_*\mathbb{Q})=0.
\end{equation}
The two maps
$$p_*:H^*(\mathcal{F}^l,\mathbb{Q})\to H^*(\mathcal{F}_g',\mathbb{Q})$$
$$p^*:H^*(\mathcal{F}_g',\mathbb{Q})\to H^*(\mathcal{F}^l,\mathbb{Q})$$
both preserve the weight filtration, and $p_*p^*=(\deg p)\operatorname{id}$, we have that 
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Gr}^W_3H^3(\mathcal{F}_g',\mathbb{Q})=0.
\end{equation}
Similarly, we have that
$$\operatorname{Gr}^W_3H^1(\mathcal{F}_g',R^2\pi_*\mathbb{Q})=0.$$
Therefore, we have $\operatorname{Gr}_3^WH^3(\mathcal{S}_g',\mathbb{Q})=0$.

<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:franchetta}
Suppose that $g\ge3$ and that $\mathcal{S}_g'$ is rationally connected. Then Conjecture \ref{Generalized Franchetta Conjecture} holds for genus $g$.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Fix a cycle $\alpha\in \operatorname{CH}^2(\mathcal{S}_g')$. By a standard spread-out argument \cite[Section~1.1.2]{Voi}, it suffices to prove the result for $\alpha$ for a general closed point $x\in \mathcal{F}_g'$. In addition, since the Chow group of a complex K3 surface is torsion-free \cite{Roi}, it suffices to prove the result up to torsion.

By Bergeron--Li's cohomological generalized Franchetta theorem (Theorem \ref{thm: cohomological GFC}), there exists a rational number $t$ such that the
cohomology class of
\[
 \alpha-tc_2(T_\pi)
\]
is supported on the inverse image of a finite union of
Noether--Lefschetz divisors on $\mathcal{F}_g'$ under $\pi:\mathcal{S}_g'\to \mathcal{F}_g'$. Consequently, there exists a dense open subset
$V\subset\mathcal{F}_g'$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cohomological-relation}
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathcal{S}_V}\bigl((\alpha-tc_2(T_\pi))|_{\mathcal{S}_V}\bigr)=0
 \in H^4(\mathcal{S}_V,\mathbb{Q}).
\end{equation}

Take an integer $m>0$ such that $mt$ is an integer. Since $\mathcal{S}_V$ is rationally connected, combining Lemma \ref{lem:universal-weight} and Proposition \ref{prop:open-vanishing}, we have that
$$m(\alpha-tc_2(T_\pi))|_{\mathcal{S}_V}\in \operatorname{CH}^2(\mathcal{S}_V)$$
is torsion.

From the property of Beauville–Voisin class \cite{BV}, for any closed point $x\in V$, we have that $c_2(S_x)=24o_{S_x}$. Therefore, the restriction of $\alpha$ of $S_x$ is a multiple of Beauville–Voisin class $o_{S_x}$ up to torsion. Hence we proved the theorem.
