<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:main-inertial-mhd-instability}
Let $k\geq2$ be an integer and let $a\in\mathbb{R}$ satisfy
\begin{equation} \label{eq:parameter-condition}
a^2(1+k^2)<2.
\end{equation}
Then the steady state $\psi_\ast=\sin(kx_1)$, $\Phi_\ast=a\sin(kx_1)$ is linearly unstable. More precisely, its linearization has a nonzero smooth eigenfunction in the transverse Fourier mode $e^{\mathrm{i} x_2}$ with a positive real eigenvalue.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:positive-symmetric-part}
Under \eqref{eq:parameter-condition}, the symmetric part of $M_j^{-1}$ is positive definite, uniformly in $j\geq1$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Write $M_j=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_j&\beta_j\\\gamma_j&1\end{pmatrix}$, where $\alpha_j=1-k^2/d_j$, $\beta_j=a(d_j-k^2)/(d_j+1)$, and $\gamma_j=a(1+k^2)/d_j$. The symmetric part of $M_j^{-1}$ is
\[ (\det M_j)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}1&-(\beta_j+\gamma_j)/2\\-(\beta_j+\gamma_j)/2&\alpha_j\end{pmatrix}. \]
It is positive definite precisely when $4\alpha_j>(\beta_j+\gamma_j)^2$. A simplification gives
\[ \beta_j+\gamma_j=a\left(1+\frac{1+k^2}{d_j(d_j+1)}\right). \]
As $j$ increases, $\left\lvert \beta_j+\gamma_j\right\rvert$ is nonincreasing and $\alpha_j$ increases, so it is enough to check $j=1$. By \eqref{eq:parameter-condition},
\[ (\beta_1+\gamma_1)^2 = a^2 \left(\frac{k^2+3}{k^2+2}\right)^2 <\frac{2}{1+k^2}\left(\frac{k^2+3}{k^2+2}\right)^2<\frac{4}{1+k^2}=4\alpha_1, \]
where the second strict inequality holds for $k\geq2$. Since $\det M_j\leq1$ and $4\alpha_j-(\beta_j+\gamma_j)^2\geq4\alpha_1-(\beta_1+\gamma_1)^2>0$, the symmetric part of $M_j^{-1}$ is uniformly positive definite in $j$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:stable-graph}
For every $\mu>0$, the initial pairs $(b_0,b_1)$ generating exponentially decaying solutions of the tail recurrence in \eqref{eq:mixed-recurrence} form a two-dimensional subspace. This subspace is the graph of a unique real matrix $R(\mu)$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:stable-graph}
b_1=R(\mu)b_0,
\end{equation}
and $R:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ is continuous.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
We first examine the constant recurrence obtained by replacing $M_j$ with $M_\infty$. Its exponentially decaying solutions are $b_j=R_\infty^jc$, where
\begin{equation} \label{eq:constant-tail-ratio}
R_\infty=\begin{pmatrix}\rho&\sigma\\0&\rho\end{pmatrix},\qquad \rho=\frac{\sqrt{\mu^2+4}-\mu}{2}\in(0,1),\qquad \sigma=\frac{a(1-\rho^2)}{\mu+2\rho}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\mu R_\infty+M_\infty R_\infty^2=M_\infty$. For every $\theta\in(\rho,1)$, one has $\lVert R_\infty^j \rVert \leq C_\theta\theta^j$, so the limiting recurrence relation has a two-dimensional stable space.

Write the recurrence \eqref{eq:mixed-recurrence} as $\mathbf{b}_{j+1}=\mathcal A_j(\mu)\mathbf{b}_j$ for $\mathbf{b}_j=(b_j,b_{j-1})^{\mathsf T}$, where
\[ \mathcal A_j(\mu)=\begin{pmatrix}-\mu M_{j+1}^{-1}&M_{j+1}^{-1}M_{j-1}\\I&0\end{pmatrix}. \]
The limiting matrix $\mathcal A_\infty(\mu) = \begin{pmatrix}-\mu M_\infty^{-1} & I\\I&0\end{pmatrix}$ is hyperbolic: its characteristic roots are $\rho$ and $-\rho^{-1}$, each with algebraic multiplicity two, and its stable generalized eigenspace is the graph $b_j=R_\infty b_{j-1}$. Since $\sum_{j\geq1}\left\lVert \mathcal A_j-\mathcal A_\infty\right\rVert<\infty$ by \eqref{eq:matrix-limit}, roughness of discrete exponential dichotomies~\cite[Chapter~3]{Coppel1978} gives, for all sufficiently large $J$, an exact two-dimensional stable space at level $J$; it consists of all solutions that decay exponentially as $j\to\infty$.

We now identify this space by a convergent matrix continued fraction. For a matrix $R$, set $T_j(R)=(\mu I+M_{j+1}R)^{-1}M_{j-1}$. The identities $b_j=R_jb_{j-1}$ satisfy the recurrence \eqref{eq:mixed-recurrence} exactly when $R_j=T_j(R_{j+1})$. Use the vector norm $\left\lvert (x,y)\right\rvert_\delta=\max\{\left\lvert x\right\rvert,\delta\left\lvert y\right\rvert\}$ with $\delta=\max\{1, \frac{2\left\lvert \sigma\right\rvert}{1-\rho}\}$. Its induced matrix norm satisfies $\left\lVert R_\infty\right\rVert_\delta\leq \frac{1+\rho}{2}<1$. Choose $\theta\in(\frac{1+\rho}{2},1)$. By \eqref{eq:matrix-limit}, there are $\epsilon>0$ and $J$ such that, whenever $\left\lVert R-R_\infty\right\rVert_\delta\leq \epsilon$ and $j\geq J$,
\[ \begin{aligned} \left\lVert (\mu I+M_{j+1}R)^{-1}M_{j+1}\right\rVert_\delta&\leq\theta,\qquad &\left\lVert (\mu I+M_{j+1}R)^{-1}M_{j-1}\right\rVert_\delta&\leq\theta,\\ \left\lVert T_j(R_\infty)-R_\infty\right\rVert_\delta&\leq(1-\theta^2)\epsilon. \end{aligned} \]
The resolvent identity gives
\begin{equation} \label{eq:tail-exact-difference}
T_j(X)-T_j(Y)=(\mu I+M_{j+1}X)^{-1}M_{j+1}(Y-X)(\mu I+M_{j+1}Y)^{-1}M_{j-1}.
\end{equation}
Hence $T_j$ maps the closed $\epsilon$-ball about $R_\infty$ into itself and contracts there by the factor $\theta^2$. For $N\geq J$, start with $R_{N+1}^{(N)}=R_\infty$ and define $R_j^{(N)}=T_j(R_{j+1}^{(N)})$ backward. If $N'>N$, repeated contraction gives
\[ \lVert R_j^{(N')}-R_j^{(N)} \rVert_\delta\leq \epsilon\theta^{2(N-j+1)},\qquad J\leq j\leq N. \]
Thus $R_j^{(N)}$ converges for each fixed $j\geq J$, its limit satisfies the backward equation, and $R_j\to R_\infty$ as $j\to\infty$. These ratios generate a two-dimensional family of exponentially decaying solutions and therefore give the stable subspace. Propagating the stable subspace backward by the linear recurrence is legitimate through $j=1$ because $M_0,M_1,\ldots$ are invertible by \eqref{eq:matrix-determinants}.

It remains to show that this stable plane is a graph over $b_0$. Put $p_j=M_jb_j$. Then
\begin{equation} \label{eq:p-recurrence}
p_{j+1}=p_{j-1}-\mu M_j^{-1}p_j,\qquad j\geq1.
\end{equation}
If an exponentially decaying solution has $b_0=0$, multiply \eqref{eq:p-recurrence} by $p_j^{\mathsf T}$ and sum from $j=1$ to $N$. The nearest-neighbor terms cancel and give $\mu\sum_{j=1}^Np_j^{\mathsf T}M_j^{-1}p_j=p_1^{\mathsf T}p_0-p_N^{\mathsf T}p_{N+1}$. Passage to the limit gives $\sum_{j\geq1}p_j^{\mathsf T}M_j^{-1}p_j=0$, and Lemma \ref{lem:positive-symmetric-part} forces every $p_j$ to vanish. The projection of the constructed two-dimensional stable plane onto $b_0\in\mathbb{R}^2$ is therefore injective and hence bijective, proving the graph representation. This identity also proves that the constructed plane contains every decaying solution: choose a vector in the constructed plane with the same $b_0$ component as any such solution and apply the same uniqueness argument to their difference.

Finally, on each compact interval of $\mu>0$ the exponential dichotomy, tail contraction, and finite backward propagation are uniform. A continuous basis of the stable plane at level $J$ therefore propagates to a continuous basis at level zero. Its projection onto the $b_0$ coordinate is an invertible $2\times2$ matrix by the preceding transversality argument, so inversion of that projection shows that $R(\mu)$ is continuous.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:endpoints}
There is a constant $C\geq1$ such that
\begin{equation} \label{eq:endpoint-asymptotics}
\left\lVert M_1R(\mu)-M_0\right\rVert\leq C\mu\quad(0<\mu\leq C^{-1}),\qquad \left\lVert R(\mu)-\mu^{-1}M_0\right\rVert\leq C\mu^{-3}\quad(\mu\geq C).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
For the small-$\mu$ estimate, use the Euclidean operator norm and put
\[ \mathsf K=\sup_{j\geq1}\lVert M_j^{-1}\rVert,\qquad \kappa=\inf_{\substack{j\geq1\\\left\lvert z\right\rvert=1}}z^{\mathsf T}M_j^{-1}z>0. \]
The positivity of $\kappa$ follows from Lemma \ref{lem:positive-symmetric-part}. Introduce $p_j=M_jb_j$, $\xi_j=(p_j+p_{j-1})/2$, and $\eta_j=(p_j-p_{j-1})/2$. Equation \eqref{eq:p-recurrence} becomes
\[ \xi_{j+1}=\xi_j-\frac{\mu}{2}M_j^{-1}(\xi_j+\eta_j),\qquad \eta_{j+1}=-\eta_j-\frac{\mu}{2}M_j^{-1}(\xi_j+\eta_j). \]
If the stable planes at levels $j+1$ and $j$ are respectively $\eta_{j+1}=X\xi_{j+1}$ and $\eta_j=Y\xi_j$, their exact relation is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:small-exact-graph}
\left(I+\frac{\mu}{2}M_j^{-1}-\frac{\mu}{2}XM_j^{-1}\right)Y=-X-\frac{\mu}{2}M_j^{-1}+\frac{\mu}{2}XM_j^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Denote the resulting map by $Y=\Gamma_j(X)$. Wherever the inverse exists, \eqref{eq:small-exact-graph} gives
\[ I+\Gamma_j(X)=\left[I+\frac{\mu}{2}(I-X)M_j^{-1}\right]^{-1}(I-X). \]
Differentiation and the resolvent identity therefore give
\begin{equation} \label{eq:small-exact-derivative}
D\Gamma_j(X)[Z]=-\left[I+\frac{\mu}{2}(I-X)M_j^{-1}\right]^{-1}Z\left[I+\frac{\mu}{2}M_j^{-1}(I-X)\right]^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Choose $0<\varepsilon<1$ so that $2\mathsf K\varepsilon\leq\kappa$. If $\left\lVert X\right\rVert\leq\varepsilon$, then the symmetric parts of both $(I-X)M_j^{-1}$ and $M_j^{-1}(I-X)$ are bounded below by $\frac{\kappa}{2}I$. For a real matrix $B$ whose symmetric part is at least $dI$, one has
\[ \left\lvert (I+\frac{\mu}{2}B)z\right\rvert^2\geq(1+\mu d)\left\lvert z\right\rvert^2. \]
Thus both inverses in \eqref{eq:small-exact-derivative} exist throughout this ball, and
\begin{equation} \label{eq:small-contraction}
\left\lVert D\Gamma_j(X)\right\rVert\leq\left(1+\frac{\kappa\mu}{2}\right)^{-1}\leq1-c\mu,\qquad \left\lVert X\right\rVert\leq\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
for sufficiently small $\mu>0$, with $c>0$ independent of $j$. This estimate requires no bound on $\Gamma_j(X)$. The limiting stable subspace for $(\xi_j,\eta_j)$ is the graph of $S_\infty=(R_\infty-I)(R_\infty+I)^{-1}$. From \eqref{eq:constant-tail-ratio}, we get $\left\lVert S_\infty\right\rVert\leq C\mu$. Since $S_\infty$ solves the limiting equation,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:small-coefficient-identity}
\Gamma_j(S_\infty)-S_\infty=-\frac{\mu}{2}\left\{I+\frac{\mu}{2}(I-S_\infty)M_j^{-1}\right\}^{-1}(I-S_\infty)(M_j^{-1}-M_\infty^{-1})(I+S_\infty).
\end{equation}
Hence $\left\lVert \Gamma_j(S_\infty)-S_\infty\right\rVert\leq C\mu j^{-2}$.

Choose $\mu>0$ sufficiently small that
\[ \left\lVert S_\infty\right\rVert+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert \Gamma_\ell(S_\infty)-S_\infty\right\rVert\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. \]
The backward iteration from infinity is made precise by finite cutoffs. For $N\geq1$, define $S_{N+1}^{(N)}=S_\infty$ and $S_j^{(N)}=\Gamma_j(S_{j+1}^{(N)})$ for $j=N,\ldots,1$. By backward induction, \eqref{eq:small-contraction} applies on the segment joining $S_\infty$ to each preceding iterate and gives
\begin{equation} \label{eq:small-cutoff-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\left\lVert S_j^{(N)}-S_\infty\right\rVert&\leq\sum_{\ell=j}^N(1-c\mu)^{\ell-j}\left\lVert \Gamma_\ell(S_\infty)-S_\infty\right\rVert\\
&\leq C\mu\sum_{\ell=j}^{\infty}\ell^{-2},\qquad 1\leq j\leq N.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the first bound and the choice of $\mu$ imply $\left\lVert S_j^{(N)}\right\rVert\leq\varepsilon/2$, so all iterates and the required segments remain inside the ball on which \eqref{eq:small-contraction} holds. If $N'>N$, the terminal discrepancy at level $N+1$ is bounded uniformly, and repeated contraction gives $\left\lVert S_j^{(N')}-S_j^{(N)}\right\rVert\leq C(1-c\mu)^{N-j+1}$. Thus $S_j^{(N)}$ converges for each fixed $j$ to a graph $S_j$ satisfying $\left\lVert S_j-S_\infty\right\rVert\leq C\mu\sum_{\ell=j}^{\infty}\ell^{-2}$. In particular, this indexed tail bound proves $S_j\to S_\infty$ as $j\to\infty$.

This limiting graph corresponds to the stable subspace from Lemma \ref{lem:stable-graph} after returning to the original variables $b_j$ and $R_j$. Indeed, define
\[ R_j=M_j^{-1}(I+S_j)(I-S_j)^{-1}M_{j-1}. \]
Then $b_j=R_jb_{j-1}$ solves the recurrence. Since $S_j\to S_\infty$ and $M_\infty$ commutes with $R_\infty$, we have $R_j\to M_\infty^{-1}R_\infty M_\infty=R_\infty$. Hence these solutions decay exponentially, and uniqueness in Lemma \ref{lem:stable-graph} identifies their level-one graph with $R(\mu)$. In particular, \eqref{eq:small-cutoff-bound} gives $\left\lVert S_1\right\rVert\leq C\mu$. On the stable subspace, $p_0=(I-S_1)\xi_1$ and $p_1=(I+S_1)\xi_1$, so
\[ M_1R(\mu)=(I+S_1)(I-S_1)^{-1}M_0. \]
This proves $\left\lVert M_1R(\mu)-M_0\right\rVert\leq C\mu$.

For the large-$\mu$ estimate, let $\mathsf M=\sup_{j\geq0}\left\lVert M_j\right\rVert$. The backward maps $T_j$ preserve the ball $\left\lVert R\right\rVert\leq2\mathsf M/\mu$. By \eqref{eq:tail-exact-difference}, they are contractions with factor $4\mathsf M^2/\mu^2$ when $\mu$ is large. Repeating the construction in the proof of Lemma \ref{lem:stable-graph} produces a sequence $R_j$ in the ball $\left\lVert R\right\rVert\leq2\mathsf M/\mu$. Since $R(\mu)=T_1(R_2)$, the resolvent identity gives
\[ R(\mu)-\mu^{-1}M_0=-\mu^{-1}(\mu I+M_2R_2)^{-1}M_2R_2M_0, \]
which is $O(\mu^{-3})$.

By Lemma \ref{lem:stable-graph}, a decaying tail satisfies the zeroth-mode equation in \eqref{eq:mixed-recurrence} if and only if
\[ \{\mu I+M_1R(\mu)\}b_0=0. \]
Define $f(\mu)=\det\{\mu I+M_1R(\mu)\}$. This is a continuous real-valued function. The small-$\mu$ estimate in Lemma \ref{lem:endpoints}, together with $\det M_0<0$, gives $f(\mu)<0$ for sufficiently small $\mu>0$. The large-$\mu$ estimate gives
\[ \mu I+M_1R(\mu)=\mu\left[I+\mu^{-2}M_1M_0+O(\mu^{-4})\right], \]
so $f(\mu)>0$ for sufficiently large $\mu$. The intermediate value theorem supplies $\mu_\ast>0$ with $f(\mu_\ast)=0$.

Exponential decay of Fourier coefficients makes the eigenfunction smooth.

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:one-mode-spectrum}
For each fixed $n\neq0$, the operator $\mathscr L_{k,n}$ is compact on $X_n^s$ for any $s\geq 0$. The nonzero spectral values of $\mathscr L_{k,n}$ are precisely the roots of
\[ z^2=-n^2k^2\kappa,\qquad \kappa\in\sigma(K_{k,n})\setminus\{0\}. \]
Moreover, the algebraic multiplicity of $z$ for $\mathscr L_{k,n}$ is identical to that of $\kappa$ for $K_{k,n}$. In addition, an eigenfunction of $\mathscr L_{k,n}$ with nonzero eigenvalue is smooth.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Compactness on $X_n^s$ follows directly from the derivative balance: $P:H^s\to H^s\hookrightarrow H^{s-1}$ and $Q:H^{s-1}\to H^{s+1}\hookrightarrow H^s$, where the displayed embeddings are compact. We first prove the spectral correspondence for $s=0$, so that $PQ$ acts on $H^{-1}$ and $QP=D_{k,n}A_{k,n}$ acts on $L^2$. For bounded maps $S:Y\to X$ and $T:X\to Y$, the identity
\[ (z-TS)^{-1}=z^{-1}\bigl[I+T(z-ST)^{-1}S\bigr],\qquad z\neq0, \]
and its counterpart with $S,T$ interchanged show that $\sigma(ST)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma(TS)\setminus\{0\}$. The intertwining identities $T(ST-z)^m=(TS-z)^mT$ and $S(TS-z)^m=(ST-z)^mS$, or equivalently the corresponding Riesz projections, show that Jordan block dimensions agree for any $z\neq0$. Apply this first to $P:L^2\to H^{-1}$ and $Q:H^{-1}\to L^2$, and then on $L^2$ with $S=D_{k,n}^{1/2}$ and $T=D_{k,n}^{1/2}A_{k,n}$. This gives
\[ \sigma(PQ)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma(QP)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma(K_{k,n})\setminus\{0\}. \]
Spectral mapping in \eqref{eq:squared-mode-operator} gives the asserted relation between $z$ and $\kappa$.

If $\kappa$ has algebraic multiplicity $m$ for $K_{k,n}$, then it has multiplicity $m$ for each diagonal product in \eqref{eq:squared-mode-operator}; hence $-n^2k^2\kappa$ has multiplicity $2m$ for $\mathscr L_{k,n}^2$. The involution $J=\operatorname{diag}(I,-I)$ satisfies $J\mathscr L_{k,n}J=-\mathscr L_{k,n}$, so the generalized eigenspaces at the two roots $z$ and $-z$ have equal dimension. Their direct sum is the generalized eigenspace of $\mathscr L_{k,n}^2$ for $z^2$, and each root therefore has algebraic multiplicity $m$.

It remains to justify that this description is independent of $s$. We claim that every generalized eigenvector of $\mathscr L_{k,n}$ at $z\neq0$ is smooth. Indeed, $\mathscr L_{k,n}^2$ gains two derivatives on both components, while its restriction to the finite-dimensional generalized eigenspace at $z$ is invertible; the inverse on that space is a polynomial in $\mathscr L_{k,n}^2$. Thus $U=(\mathscr L_{k,n}^2|_E)^{-1}\mathscr L_{k,n}^2U$ gains two derivatives, and iteration gives $U\in C^\infty$. Consequently the generalized eigenspaces, their dimensions, and all nonzero spectral values are the same on every $X_n^s$.

<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:negative-index}
The number of negative eigenvalues of $K_{k,n}$, counted with multiplicity, is
\[ N_{k,n}=\#\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:m^2+n^2<k^2\}. \]
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
For every $g\in L^2(\mathbb T_{x_1})$,
\[ \langle K_{k,n}g,g\rangle=\langle A_{k,n}D_{k,n}^{1/2}g,D_{k,n}^{1/2}g\rangle. \]
The negative subspace $E_-$ of $A_{k,n}$ is spanned by the modes with $m^2+n^2<k^2$ and has dimension $N_{k,n}$. If a subspace $G$ is strictly negative for $K_{k,n}$, then $D_{k,n}^{1/2}G$ is strictly negative for $A_{k,n}$ and has the same dimension because $D_{k,n}^{1/2}$ is injective. Hence the negative index of $K_{k,n}$ is at most $N_{k,n}$. Conversely, since $E_-$ is finite-dimensional, there is a constant $c>0$ such that $\langle A_{k,n}f,f\rangle\leq-c\left\lVert f\right\rVert^2$ on $E_-$. The range of $D_{k,n}^{1/2}$ is dense because the operator is self-adjoint and injective. Approximate a basis of $E_-$ sufficiently closely by vectors in this range; continuity of the quadratic form preserves strict negativity and linear independence. Pulling those approximants back through $D_{k,n}^{1/2}$ gives an $N_{k,n}$-dimensional negative subspace for $K_{k,n}$. For a compact self-adjoint operator, this negative index coincides with the number of negative eigenvalues counted with multiplicity.

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:full-unstable-spectrum}
The spectrum of $\mathscr L_k$ in the open right half-plane is a finite set of isolated positive real eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity. More precisely,
\[ \sigma(\mathscr L_k)\cap\{\operatorname{Re}z>0\}=\bigcup_{0 < |n| < k}\{\lambda_{k,n,j}:1\leq j\leq N_{k,n}\}. \]
The union involves only the finitely many transverse frequencies $0<|n|<k$. Thus the unstable spectrum of the full operator is discrete and no additional unstable spectrum is created by the direct sum over high transverse modes.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The $n=0$ block vanishes. If $|n|\geq k$, then $A_{k,n} \succeq 0$, hence $K_{k,n}\succeq 0$ and Proposition \ref{prop:one-mode-spectrum} excludes unstable spectrum. For $0<|n|<k$, Proposition \ref{prop:one-mode-spectrum} and Lemma \ref{lem:negative-index} give exactly the finite list in the theorem.

It remains to rule out additional spectrum arising from the infinite direct sum over the transverse frequency $n$. For $|n|\geq k+1$, set
\[ E_n(\omega,F)=\langle B_{k,n}\omega,\omega\rangle_{H^1 \times H^{-1}} +\langle A_{k,n}F,F\rangle_{L^2 \times L^2}. \]
Write $\omega=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\omega_me^{\mathrm{i} mx_1}$ and $F=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}F_me^{\mathrm{i} mx_1}$. Then
\[ E_n(\omega,F)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\frac{1+k^2}{m^2+n^2}|\omega_m|^2+\frac{m^2+n^2-k^2}{m^2+n^2+1}|F_m|^2\right\}. \]
Recall that
\[ \left\lVert (\omega,F)\right\rVert_{X_n^0}^2=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\frac{|\omega_m|^2}{1+m^2+n^2}+|F_m|^2\right\}. \]
For $r=m^2+n^2\geq(k+1)^2$, one has
\[ 1+k^2\leq(1+k^2)\frac{1+r}{r}\leq(1+k^2)\left(1+\frac{1}{(k+1)^2}\right),\qquad \frac{2k+1}{k^2+2k+2}\leq\frac{r-k^2}{r+1}<1. \]
Consequently there are constants $c,C>0$, depending only on $k$ and independent of $n$, such that
\[ c\left\lVert (\omega,F)\right\rVert_{X_n^0}^2\leq E_n(\omega,F)\leq C\left\lVert (\omega,F)\right\rVert_{X_n^0}^2,\qquad |n|\geq k+1. \]
Let $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{E_n}$ denote the inner product obtained by polarizing $E_n$. The bounded operator $\mathscr L_{k,n}$ is skew-adjoint in this equivalent Hilbert norm, so $z-\mathscr L_{k,n}$ is invertible whenever $\operatorname{Re}z\neq0$. Given $V\in X_n^0$, set $U=(z-\mathscr L_{k,n})^{-1}V$. Taking the $E_n$ inner product of $(z-\mathscr L_{k,n})U=V$ with $U$ and then taking real parts gives
\[ |\operatorname{Re}z|E_n(U)=\left|\operatorname{Re}\langle V,U\rangle_{E_n}\right|\leq E_n(V)^{1/2}E_n(U)^{1/2}. \]
Hence, uniformly for $|n|\geq k+1$,
\[ \left\lVert (z-\mathscr L_{k,n})^{-1}\right\rVert_{X_n^0\to X_n^0}\leq\frac{C}{|\operatorname{Re}z|},\qquad |n|\geq k+1. \]
For an orthogonal direct sum, $z\in\rho(\bigoplus_n\mathscr L_{k,n})$ if and only if $z\in\rho(\mathscr L_{k,n})$ for every $n$ and $\sup_n\left\lVert (z-\mathscr L_{k,n})^{-1}\right\rVert<\infty$. The preceding estimate is uniform on all high blocks $|n|\geq k+1$, and only finitely many blocks satisfy $|n|\leq k$. The two boundary blocks $|n|=k$ are not covered by the coercive energy because the symbol of $A_{k,n}$ vanishes at $m=0$; nevertheless, they are compact, and $A_{k,n}\succeq0$ implies $K_{k,n}\succeq0$, so Proposition \ref{prop:one-mode-spectrum} places their nonzero spectrum on the imaginary axis. Their resolvent norms are therefore finite for each fixed $z$ with $\operatorname{Re}z>0$. The same is immediate for the zero block, while the remaining finitely many blocks $0<|n|<k$ have precisely the positive eigenvalues listed in \eqref{eq:unstable-eigenvalues}. Thus every spectral point with positive real part comes from one of these finitely many compact blocks, and every such point is an isolated eigenvalue of finite algebraic multiplicity for the full operator.

<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Write $X^0=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}X_n^0$ and $\mathscr L_k=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr L_{k,n}$. Although every fixed block is compact, the full direct sum is compact only if $\left\lVert \mathscr L_{k,n}\right\rVert_{X_n^0\to X_n^0}\to0$. This necessary condition fails. Indeed, with unit vectors $U_n=(0,e^{\mathrm{i} nx_2})$ in $X^0$,
\[ \mathscr L_kU_n=\left(-\mathrm{i} kn\frac{n^2-k^2}{n^2+1}\cos(kx_1)e^{\mathrm{i} nx_2},0\right),\qquad \left\lVert \mathscr L_kU_n\right\rVert_{X^0}^2=\frac{k^2n^2}{2(1+k^2+n^2)}\left(\frac{n^2-k^2}{n^2+1}\right)^2\longrightarrow\frac{k^2}{2}. \]
The vectors $\mathscr L_kU_n$ are mutually orthogonal for distinct $n$, so they have no convergent subsequence and $\mathscr L_k$ is not compact. The blockwise compact operators $K_{k,n}$ vary with $n$, and the spectral relation contains the compensating factor $n^2k^2$; accordingly, the full operator has a nontrivial essential spectrum even though each fixed block is compact.

Essential spectrum may be produced by the loss of a uniform resolvent bound as $|n|\to\infty$, even though every fixed block has discrete nonzero spectrum. This high-frequency mechanism gives the imaginary interval in Proposition \ref{prop:sobolev-semigroup} but does not affect the unstable half-plane.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:sobolev-semigroup}
The spectrum of $\mathscr L_k$ is the same on every $X^s$ for $s\geq0$. With the Fredholm definition of essential spectrum,
\[ \sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathscr L_k)=\mathrm{i}[-k\sqrt{1+k^2},k\sqrt{1+k^2}]. \]
If $Y^s\subset X^s$ is a closed invariant subspace defined by Fourier modes or the reflections used below, and if the restricted operator has spectral bound $\Lambda$, then for every $s\geq0$ and $\eta>0$ there is a constant $C_{k,Y,s,\eta}$ such that
\begin{equation} \label{eq:full-semigroup-bound}
\left\lVert e^{t\mathscr L_k}\right\rVert_{Y^s\to Y^s}\leq C_{k,Y,s,\eta}e^{(\Lambda+\eta)t},\qquad t\geq0.
\end{equation}
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Extend $\Delta^{-1}$ by zero on the constant Fourier mode and let $\Lambda_0=(1-\Delta)^{1/2}$. This defines a classical pseudodifferential realization on all of $L^2\times L^2$ after the identification $(\omega,F)\mapsto(\Lambda_0^{-1}\omega,F)$. Restriction to the invariant mean-zero vorticity space, or any other choice on the constant mode, changes only a finite-dimensional summand and does not alter the Fredholm essential spectrum or the index. The resulting order-zero system $\widetilde{\mathscr L}_k$ has principal symbol
\[ -\mathrm{i} kC_k(x_1)\frac{\xi_2}{|\xi|}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1+k^2&0\end{pmatrix}, \]
whose eigenvalues range over the interval $I_k=\mathrm{i}[-k\sqrt{1+k^2},k\sqrt{1+k^2}]$ on the cosphere bundle.

If $z\notin I_k$, then $z-\widetilde{\mathscr L}_k$ is elliptic. Standard order-zero pseudodifferential Fredholm theory~\cite{Shvydkoy2006} gives $G_z\in\Psi^0$ such that
\[ G_z(z-\widetilde{\mathscr L}_k)=I-R_{z,1},\qquad (z-\widetilde{\mathscr L}_k)G_z=I-R_{z,2},\qquad R_{z,j}\in\Psi^{-1}. \]
The remainders are compact on $L^2\times L^2$ because they map into $H^1$. By Atkinson's theorem, a two-sided inverse modulo compact operators makes $z-\widetilde{\mathscr L}_k$ Fredholm. Its index is zero: the index is locally constant on the connected set $\mathbb{C}\setminus I_k$, and the operator is invertible for large $|z|$.

If $z\in I_k$, choose a characteristic point $(x^0,\xi^0)$ and a null vector $v$ of the principal symbol of $z-\widetilde{\mathscr L}_k$. Normalized coherent states of the form $h^{-1/2}\chi((x-x^0)/\sqrt h)e^{\mathrm{i}(x-x^0)\cdot\xi^0/h}v$, including at the endpoints of the symbol range, satisfy
\[ u_h\rightharpoonup0,\qquad \left\lVert (z-\widetilde{\mathscr L}_k)u_h\right\rVert_{L^2}=O(\sqrt h). \]
A Fredholm operator $T$ satisfies $\left\lVert u\right\rVert\leq C\left\lVert Tu\right\rVert+\left\lVert \Pi u\right\rVert$, where $\Pi$ projects onto its finite-dimensional kernel. Since $\Pi u_h\to0$, this estimate contradicts $\left\lVert u_h\right\rVert=1$. Projecting $u_h$ onto mean-zero vorticity changes it by $o(1)$ and gives the same contradiction on $X^0$. Hence $z-\widetilde{\mathscr L}_k$ is not Fredholm, proving $\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathscr L_k)=I_k$.

For $s\geq0$, identify $X^s$ with $L^2\times L^2$ by $(\omega,F)\mapsto(\Lambda_0^{s-1}\omega,\Lambda_0^sF)$. The corresponding realization differs from $\widetilde{\mathscr L}_k$ by a commutator term of order $-1$, schematically
\[ [\Lambda_0^s,\widetilde{\mathscr L}_k]\Lambda_0^{-s}\in\Psi^{-1}, \]
hence by a compact operator. Thus Fredholmness, index, and the essential spectrum are unchanged. Outside $I_k$, elliptic regularity shows that every kernel element is smooth, so the realizations of $\mathscr L_k-\lambda I$ on $X^s$ and $X^{s'}$ have the same kernel for any $0\leq s<s'$. Since their index is zero, they are simultaneously invertible. Points of $I_k$ belong to the essential spectrum on every realization, so the full spectrum is independent of $X^s$.

Finally, let $A=\mathscr L_k|_{Y^s}$. The operator $A$ is bounded. If its spectral bound is $\Lambda$, the spectral mapping theorem and the spectral-radius formula give $r(e^A)=e^\Lambda$ and, for each $\eta>0$, a constant $C_\eta$ such that $\left\lVert e^{mA}\right\rVert\leq C_\eta e^{(\Lambda+\eta)m}$ for every integer $m\geq0$. Writing $t=m+\tau$ with $0\leq\tau<1$ and using $\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq1}\left\lVert e^{\tau A}\right\rVert<\infty$ proves \eqref{eq:full-semigroup-bound}. The Fourier and reflection projections commute with the Sobolev identifications above, so the same regularity argument makes $\Lambda$ independent of $s$ for the subspaces used in this article.

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label{prop:continued-fraction}
For every $\mu>0$ and $j\geq2$, the finite continued fractions defined by $r_{N+1}^{(N)}=0$ and
\[ r_j^{(N)}=\frac{c_{j-1}}{\mu+c_{j+1}r_{j+1}^{(N)}},\qquad 2\leq j\leq N, \]
converge as $N\to\infty$ to a positive continuous function $r_j(\mu)$ satisfying
\begin{equation} \label{eq:general-tail-ratios}
r_j(\mu)=\frac{c_{j-1}}{\mu+c_{j+1}r_{j+1}(\mu)}.
\end{equation}
The recurrence \eqref{eq:coefficient-recurrence} has a nonzero square-summable solution if and only if
\begin{equation} \label{eq:general-matching-condition}
G_{k,n}(\mu):=\mu+c_2r_2(\mu)-\frac{-c_0c_1}{\mu}=0.
\end{equation}
Consequently, $G_{k,n}$ has exactly one positive root $\mu_{k,n}$, and
\begin{equation} \label{eq:branch-eigenvalue}
\lambda_{k,n}=\frac{nk\mu_{k,n}}2.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:branch-bounds}
\max\{0,-c_0c_1-c_1c_2\}\leq\mu_{k,n}^2<-c_0c_1.
\end{equation}

\[ \left\lvert r_j^{(N')}-r_j^{(N)}\right\rvert\leq\frac{C}{\mu}\prod_{\ell=j}^{N}\frac{c_{\ell-1}c_{\ell+1}}{\mu^2}. \]

Since $0<r_2(\mu)\leq c_1/\mu$, this identity gives \eqref{eq:branch-bounds}.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem:nonlinear-energy}
Let $s\geq4$ be an integer. The perturbation equation \eqref{eq:nonlinear-perturbation-equation} is locally well-posed on the real subspace of $X^s_{\mathrm{sym}}$, with solutions in $C([0,T];X^s_{\mathrm{sym}})\cap C^1([0,T];X^{s-1}_{\mathrm{sym}})$. These solutions continue as long as their $X^s$ norm remains bounded. For every integer $r\geq2$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:quadratic-tame-bound}
\left\lVert \mathcal B(U,V)\right\rVert_{X^r}\leq C_r\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{X^{r+1}}\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{X^{r+1}}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, suppose that smooth real-valued functions $V,W,R$ satisfy
\begin{equation} \label{eq:error-equation-general}
\partial_tW=\mathscr L_kW+2\mathcal B(V,W)+\mathcal B(W,W)+R.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation} \label{eq:nonlinear-energy-estimate}
\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq M_s\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}+C_s\bigl(\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{X^{s+1}}+\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}\bigr)\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}+\left\lVert R\right\rVert_{X^s},
\end{equation}
where $M_s$ and $C_s$ depend only on $s$ and the fixed equilibrium. The same inequality holds in the integrated sense for strong $X^s$ solutions obtained by regularization.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-15: body_md -->
The elliptic relations give
\[ \left\lVert u_U\right\rVert_{H^r}\leq C_r\left\lVert \omega\right\rVert_{H^{r-1}},\qquad \left\lVert b_U\right\rVert_{H^{r+1}}\leq C_r\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{H^r}. \]
Since $H^r(\mathbb T^2)$ is an algebra for $r\geq2$,
\[ \left\lVert u_U\cdot\nabla\omega_V\right\rVert_{H^{r-1}}+\left\lVert b_U\cdot\nabla F_V\right\rVert_{H^{r-1}}+\left\lVert u_U\cdot\nabla F_V\right\rVert_{H^r}\leq C_r\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{X^{r+1}}\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{X^{r+1}}, \]
and the symmetric terms satisfy the same bound. This proves \eqref{eq:quadratic-tame-bound}.

Writing components explicitly gives
\[ \partial_t\omega_W=(\mathscr L_kW)_\omega-u_V\cdot\nabla\omega_W-u_W\cdot\nabla\omega_V-u_W\cdot\nabla\omega_W-b_V\cdot\nabla F_W-b_W\cdot\nabla F_V-b_W\cdot\nabla F_W+R_\omega, \]
\[ \partial_tF_W=(\mathscr L_kW)_F-u_V\cdot\nabla F_W-u_W\cdot\nabla F_V-u_W\cdot\nabla F_W+R_F. \]
For real divergence-free $v$, $\operatorname{Re}\langle v\cdot\nabla\partial^\alpha f,\partial^\alpha f\rangle=0$, so the self-transport terms contribute only commutators. Use
\[ \left\lVert [\partial^\alpha,v\cdot\nabla]f\right\rVert_{L^2}\leq C_s\bigl(\left\lVert \nabla v\right\rVert_{L^\infty}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{s-1}}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^s}\left\lVert \nabla f\right\rVert_{L^\infty}\bigr),\qquad |\alpha|=s-1, \]
and its $|\alpha|=s$ analogue. The remaining terms contain either a derivative of $V$ or $b_W$, which is one derivative smoother than $F_W$. Sobolev embedding and boundedness of $\mathscr L_k$ give
\[ \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert \omega_W\right\rVert_{H^{s-1}}^2+\left\lVert F_W\right\rVert_{H^s}^2\right)\leq M_s\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}^2+C_s\left(\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{X^{s+1}}+\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}\right)\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}^2+\left\lVert R\right\rVert_{X^s}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}. \]
Division by $\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}$ proves \eqref{eq:nonlinear-energy-estimate}; at zero, use the upper right derivative. Applying the estimate to Friedrichs regularizations and then using strong convergence in lower norms and lower semicontinuity extends its integrated form to the strong-solution class.

For local existence, solve the Fourier--Galerkin system $\partial_tU_N=J_N\mathscr L_kU_N+J_N\mathcal B(J_NU_N,J_NU_N)$, where $J_N$ is the $L^2$ orthogonal projection onto the Fourier modes in $[-N,N]^2\cap\mathbb{Z}^2$. The same estimate gives
\[ \frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert U_N\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq M_s\left\lVert U_N\right\rVert_{X^s}+C_s\left\lVert U_N\right\rVert_{X^s}^2. \]
Scalar comparison yields an $N$-independent existence time and $X^s$ bound; the equation bounds $\partial_tU_N$ in $X^{s-1}$. The compact embedding $X^s\hookrightarrow X^{s-1}$ and the Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem give, along a subsequence, strong convergence in $C([0,T];X^{s-1})$ and weak-* convergence in $L^\infty([0,T];X^s)$. These convergences are sufficient to pass to the transport products in $C([0,T];X^{s-2})$, recover the initial value, and construct a solution in $L^\infty([0,T];X^s)\cap W^{1,\infty}([0,T];X^{s-1})$.

We prove uniqueness using an estimate one derivative lower than the preceding $X^s$ error estimate. If $U$ and $Z$ are two solutions and $W=U-Z$, the same commutator calculation at level $s-1$ gives
\begin{equation} \label{eq:lower-difference-estimate}
\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^{s-1}}\leq C_s\bigl(1+\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{X^s}+\left\lVert Z\right\rVert_{X^s}\bigr)\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^{s-1}}.
\end{equation}
Gronwall's inequality gives uniqueness and therefore convergence of the whole Galerkin sequence.

For continuity of the solution map, the standard Bona--Smith argument now applies: solve from smoothed initial data, use \eqref{eq:lower-difference-estimate} for the low-norm difference, and combine the uniform $X^s$ energy bound with persistence estimates for the smoothed solutions to control the high-frequency tails. This yields strong continuity in $X^s$ and continuous dependence on the initial data in $X^s$. Since the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:nonlinear-perturbation-equation} is continuous from $X^s$ to $X^{s-1}$, the solution then belongs to $C^1([0,T];X^{s-1})$. Restarting the uniform energy estimate gives continuation while the $X^s$ norm stays bounded. Since $J_N$ commutes with the defining symmetries and preserves real-valued fields, the limit remains in the real subspace of $X^s_{\mathrm{sym}}$.

\label{thm:nonlinear-instability}
Let $k\geq2$ and let $s\geq4$ be an integer. Choose a real eigenfunction $U_\ast\in\mathcal E_k\cap C^\infty$ satisfying
\[ \mathscr L_kU_\ast=\Lambda_kU_\ast,\qquad \left\lVert U_\ast\right\rVert_{X^s}=1. \]
There are constants $\delta>0$, $\epsilon_0>0$, and $C>0$, depending on $k$, $s$, and $U_\ast$ but not on $\epsilon$, such that for every $0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0$ the nonlinear perturbation equation has a solution $U^\epsilon\in X^s_{\mathrm{sym}}$ with
\[ U^\epsilon(0)=\epsilon U_\ast, \]
on the interval $[0,T_\epsilon]$, where
\begin{equation} \label{eq:escape-time}
T_\epsilon=\frac{1}{\Lambda_k}\log\frac{\delta}{\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
This solution satisfies
\begin{equation} \label{eq:nonlinear-departure}
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_\epsilon}\left\lVert U^\epsilon(t)\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq C\delta,\qquad \left\lVert U^\epsilon(T_\epsilon)\right\rVert_{X^s}\geq\frac{\delta}{2}.
\end{equation}
In particular, the purely magnetic equilibrium is nonlinearly unstable in $X^s_{\mathrm{sym}}$, and an initial perturbation of size $\epsilon$ produces a departure of size independent of $\epsilon$ in time $O(|\log\epsilon|)$.

The gap gives
\[ e^{t\mathscr L_k}=e^{\Lambda_kt}P_k+e^{t\mathscr L_k}(I-P_k),\qquad \left\lVert e^{t\mathscr L_k}(I-P_k)\right\rVert\leq C_se^{(\Lambda_k- \frac{\gamma_k}{2})t}, \]
and hence \eqref{eq:leading-semigroup-bound}, even if leading branches tie.

\begin{equation} \label{eq:choice-of-expansion-order}
(N+1)\Lambda_k>M_s + 1.
\end{equation}

To absorb the derivative loss, set $U_1(t)=e^{\Lambda_kt}U_\ast$ and define $U_2,\ldots,U_N$ by

\end{equation}
Smoothness of $U_\ast$ permits this recursion. With $s_j=s+N-j+1$, induction gives

\left\lVert U_j(t)\right\rVert_{X^{s_j}}\leq C_j e^{j\Lambda_kt},\qquad 1\leq j\leq N,\quad t\geq0.

Indeed, for $p+q=j$ one has $s_p,s_q\geq s_j+1$, so \eqref{eq:quadratic-tame-bound} bounds the forcing by $Ce^{j\Lambda_kt}$. Duhamel's formula gives

\[ R^{\mathrm{app}}=-\sum_{\substack{1\leq p,q\leq N\\N+1\leq p+q\leq2N}}\epsilon^{p+q}\mathcal B(U_p,U_q). \]

\begin{equation} \label{eq:approximate-solution-bounds}
\left\lVert U^{\text{app}}(t)\right\rVert_{X^{s+1}}\leq C_{\mathrm{app}}\epsilon e^{\Lambda_kt},\qquad \left\lVert R^{\text{app}}(t)\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq C_{\mathrm{res}}(\epsilon e^{\Lambda_kt})^{N+1}.
\end{equation}

For $W=U^\epsilon-U^{\text{app}}$, one has $W(0)=0$ and
\[ \partial_tW=\mathscr L_kW+2\mathcal B(U^{\text{app}},W)+\mathcal B(W,W)-R^{\text{app}}. \]

\[ C_\ast=\frac{C_{\mathrm{res}}}{(N+1)\Lambda_k-M_s - 1}, \]
and choose $0<\delta_0<1$ so small that
\[ C_s(C_{\mathrm{app}}+1)\delta_0\leq1,\qquad C_\ast\delta_0^N\leq\frac12. \]

\[ T_{\delta_0,\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\Lambda_k}\log\frac{\delta_0}{\epsilon},\qquad \tau_\epsilon=\sup\left\{0<T<\min\{T_{\max}^\epsilon,T_{\delta_0,\epsilon}\}:\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert W(t)\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq\delta_0\right\}. \]

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}&\leq M_s\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}+C_s\bigl(C_{\mathrm{app}}\epsilon e^{\Lambda_kt}+\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}\bigr)\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}+C_{\mathrm{res}}(\epsilon e^{\Lambda_kt})^{N+1}\\ &\leq (M_s + 1)\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{X^s}+C_{\mathrm{res}}\epsilon^{N+1}e^{(N+1)\Lambda_kt}. \end{aligned} \]

\[ \left\lVert W(t)\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq C_{\mathrm{res}}\epsilon^{N+1}\int_0^te^{(M_s+1)(t-\sigma)}e^{(N+1)\Lambda_k\sigma}\,d\sigma\leq C_\ast(\epsilon e^{\Lambda_kt})^{N+1}\leq\frac{\delta_0}{2}. \]
This is a strict improvement of the bootstrap assumption. Moreover,
\[ \sup_{0\leq t<\tau_\epsilon}\left\lVert U^\epsilon(t)\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq\sup_{0\leq t<\tau_\epsilon}\left(\left\lVert U^{\text{app}}(t)\right\rVert_{X^s}+\left\lVert W(t)\right\rVert_{X^s}\right)\leq\left(C_{\mathrm{app}}+\frac12\right)\delta_0. \]

\[ \left\lVert W(t)\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq C_\ast(\epsilon e^{\Lambda_kt})^{N+1},\qquad 0\leq t\leq T_{\delta_0,\epsilon}. \]

Choose a fixed $0<\delta\leq\delta_0$ so small that
\[ C\delta^2+C\delta^{N+1}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}, \]
and take $\epsilon_0<\delta$. Then
\[ \left\lVert U^{\text{app}}(T_\epsilon)-\delta U_\ast\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq\sum_{j=2}^NC_j\delta^j\leq C\delta^2,\qquad \left\lVert W(T_\epsilon)\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq C\delta^{N+1}. \]
Therefore we obtain the lower bound in \eqref{eq:nonlinear-departure}.

Use the same $N$, set $U_1(t)=e^{t\mathscr L_k}V$, and define the higher profiles by \eqref{eq:approximate-profile-recursion}. Since $P_kV=U_\ast$,
\[ U_1(t)=e^{\Lambda_kt}U_\ast+e^{t\mathscr L_k}(I-P_k)V,\qquad \left\lVert U_1(t)\right\rVert_{X^{s+N}}\leq C_Re^{\Lambda_kt}. \]
The preceding profile and error estimates are uniform for $\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{X^{s+N}}\leq R$:
\[ \left\lVert U_j(t)\right\rVert_{X^{s+N-j+1}}\leq C_Re^{j\Lambda_kt},\qquad \left\lVert W(t)\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq C_R\bigl(\epsilon e^{\Lambda_kt}\bigr)^{N+1}. \]
After decreasing $\delta$ in terms of $R$, the same bootstrap extends the solution through $T_\epsilon$. At that time,
\[ \left\lVert \epsilon e^{T_\epsilon\mathscr L_k}(I-P_k)V\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq C_R\epsilon e^{(\Lambda_k-\gamma_k/2)T_\epsilon}=C_R\delta\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\delta}\right)^{\gamma_k/(2\Lambda_k)}. \]
The remaining profiles and the exact error satisfy
\[ \left\lVert \sum_{j=2}^N\epsilon^jU_j(T_\epsilon)+W(T_\epsilon)\right\rVert_{X^s}\leq C_R\sum_{j=2}^N\delta^j+C_R\delta^{N+1}\leq C_R\delta^2. \]
These estimates prove \eqref{eq:robust-simple-escape}. Choose first $\delta$ and then $\epsilon_0$ so that the two terms on its right-hand side are at most $\delta/4$; the lower bound follows.
