\label{theorem_1} Let $d=1$, $\lambda>0$ and $\frac{2}{\sqrt{7}}<\sigma<1$. Define
$$
\nu_*(\sigma)=
\begin{cases}
\frac{4-3\sigma^2}{2\sigma(2\sigma-1)}&\text{if}\quad \frac{2}{\sqrt7}\le\sigma\le\frac{2+4\sqrt3}{11},\\
2 &\text{if}\quad \frac{2+4\sqrt3}{11}\le\sigma\le\frac 67,\\
\frac{4-3\sigma}{2\sigma-1}&\text{if}\quad \frac 67\le \sigma\le1.
\end{cases}
$$
Then, for any $u_+ \in  \mathcal FH^{\nu}({\mathbb{R}})$ with $\nu_*(\sigma)<\nu<\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1/2}$, there exists a unique global solution $u\in C({\mathbb{R}}, L^2({\mathbb{R}}))$ to  
\hyperref[{NLS}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{NLS}}}} 
 satisfying $e^{-it\Delta/2}u\in C({\mathbb{R}}, \mathcal F H^1({\mathbb{R}}))$ and, for any $\delta>0$,
\begin{align}
&\|xe^{-it\Delta/2}\{u(t)-u_{\mathrm p,+}(t)\}\|_{L^2}+t^{\sigma/2-\delta/2}\left\|u(t)-u_{\mathrm p,+}(t)\right\|_{L^2}\nonumber\\
&\quad +t^{-\sigma/2+3/2}\left\|\left|\widehat{u_+}\left(\frac xt\right)\right|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\overline{u_{\mathrm p,+}(t)}\{u(t)-{u_{\mathrm p,+}(t)}\}\right]\right\|_{L^2}
                                                                                                                          
\lesssim t^{-\beta+\delta}
\label{theorem_1_1}
\end{align}
uniformly with respect to $t\ge1$, where
\begin{align}
\beta=\beta(\sigma,\nu)=\begin{cases}
\nu(\sigma-1/2)+{3\sigma}/{2}-2&\text{if}\quad 1\le \nu<2,\\
\nu(\sigma-1/2)+\sigma/2-1&\text{if}\quad 2\le \nu<\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1/2}.
\end{cases}
\label{beta}
\end{align}
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               
As a consequence, the following modified wave operator is well-defined: $$W_{\sigma,+}:{\mathcal{F}} H^{\nu}({\mathbb{R}})\ni u_+\mapsto u(0)\in \mathcal F H^1({\mathbb{R}}).$$

\label{remark_1}
By  
\hyperref[{theorem_1_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{theorem_1_1}}}} 
, the solution $u$ satisfies
\begin{align}
\|xe^{-it\Delta/2}\{u(t)-u_{\mathrm p,+}(t)\}\|_{L^2}&\lesssim t^{-\beta+\delta},\nonumber\\
\left\|u(t)-u_{\mathrm p,+}(t)\right\|_{L^2}&\lesssim t^{-\beta-\sigma/2+3\delta/2},\nonumber\\
\left\|\left|\widehat{u_+}\left(\frac xt\right)\right|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\overline{u_{\mathrm p,+}(t)}\{u(t)-{u_{\mathrm p,+}(t)}\}\right]\right\|_{L^2}&\lesssim t^{-\beta-(3-\sigma)/2+\delta}. \label{remark_1_1}
\end{align}
Combining with the estimates $\|e^{it\Delta/2}(1+|x|)^{-1}\|_{L^2\to L^\infty}\lesssim t^{-1/2}$ and $\|u_{\mathrm p,+}(t)\|_{L^\infty}\lesssim t^{-1/2}$, we also have
\begin{align}
\label{remark_1_2}
\|u(t)-u_{\mathrm p,+}(t)\|_{L^\infty}\lesssim t^{-1/2-\beta+\delta},\quad \|u(t)\|_{L^\infty}\lesssim t^{-1/2}. 
\end{align}
These decay estimates, except for  
\hyperref[{remark_1_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{remark_1_1}}}} 
, have been usually obtained in the small-data scattering result for the critical case $\sigma=1$; see e.g. \cite{Ozawa_1991,Ginibre_Ozawa_1993,Hayashi_Naumkin_2006}.  
\hyperref[{remark_1_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{remark_1_1}}}} 
 was implicitly obtained in the previous work \cite{KaMi} again in the critical case $\sigma=1$. This additional information is not only a consequence, but also important for proving Theorem \ref{theorem_1} itself. More precisely, the last term of the LHS of  
\hyperref[{theorem_1_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{theorem_1_1}}}} 
 plays a crucial role in our argument.

\label{remark_2}
It is easy to check that $\nu_*(\sigma)$ is continuous and non-increasing, $\nu_*(1)=1$ and $$\nu_*(2/\sqrt{7})=\sigma/(\sigma-1/2)|_{\sigma=2/\sqrt7}=(16+4\sqrt{7})/9<3.$$ Thus, as a direct corollary of Theorem \ref{theorem_1}, the modified wave operator $W_{\sigma,+}:{\mathcal{F}} H^3({\mathbb{R}})\to {\mathcal{F}} H^1({\mathbb{R}})$ exists for any $2/\sqrt7<\sigma<1$. 

$\nu_*(\sigma)$ and the lower bound $2/\sqrt7$ of the admissible range of $\sigma$  are determined as follows; see Section \ref{subsection_existence_3} for the details. Define $\mu(\sigma,\nu):=\min\{\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3\}$, where
\begin{align*}
\mu_1(\sigma,\nu)&=\frac{\sigma^2}{2}+2\sigma-2+2\sigma \beta(\sigma,\nu),\\
\mu_2(\sigma,\nu)&=\frac{\sigma^2}{2}+\frac{(\min\{\nu,3/2\}+1)\sigma}{2}-1+(2\sigma-1)\beta(\sigma,\nu),\\
\mu_3(\sigma,\nu)&=\frac{5\sigma}{4}-1+\beta(\sigma,\nu).
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{align*}
2/\sqrt7&=\inf\{\sigma\in (1/2,1)\ |\ \mu(\sigma,\sigma/(\sigma-1/2))>0,\ \beta(\sigma,\sigma/(\sigma-1/2))>0\},\\
\nu_*(\sigma)&:=\inf\{\nu\in (1,\sigma/(\sigma-1/2))\ |\ \mu(\sigma,\nu)>0,\ \beta(\sigma,\nu)>0\}.
\end{align*}
More precisely, if $\nu=\sigma/(\sigma-1/2)$, then $\mu(\sigma,\sigma/(\sigma-1/2))=\mu_1(\sigma,\sigma/(\sigma-1/2))=7\sigma^2/2-2$ and $\beta(\sigma,\sigma/(\sigma-1/2))=3\sigma/2-1$. Thus the lower bound $2/\sqrt{7}$ is determined as the positive root of $\mu(\sigma,\sigma/(\sigma-1/2))=0$. 
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   
This lower bound is due to technical limitations of our method. In view of \cite{HKN} and by analogy with modified scattering for the Hartree equations, when $d=1$, $\sigma=1/2$ should be the optimal lower bound for modified scattering with the above asymptotic profile  
\hyperref[{u_p}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{u_p}}}} 
; see also Remark \ref{remark_SeWu} below. We however stress that our argument is completely different from that of \cite{HKN}. In particular, neither structural conditions nor an analyticity are required for scattering data $u_+$. Moreover, as mentioned above, it is difficult to apply directly the existing methods for the Hartree equations based on the smoothing property of the convolution to  
\hyperref[{NLS}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{NLS}}}} 
.

<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
\label{remark_SeWu}
During the preparation of this article, the very recent preprint \cite{SeWu} appeared, which established the existence of modified wave operators for  
\hyperref[{NLS}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{NLS}}}} 
 with $d\ge1$, $0<\sigma<1/d$,  $\lambda\in {\mathbb{R}}$ and sufficiently small, non-vanishing scattering data $u_+$ in an analytic function space. More precisely, let $A^6_\rho$ be a weighted analytic function space defined through the norm 
$$
\|f\|_{A^6_\rho}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^d}e^{6\<\xi\>}\<\xi\>^\rho |\widehat f(\xi)|d\xi. 
$$
The scattering data in \cite{SeWu} is assumed to be of the form $u_+=e^{\Psi}$ with smooth $\Psi$ satisfying $u_+\in L^2\cap A^6_\rho$, $\nabla \Psi\in A^6_\rho$ and the smallness condition $\|u_+\|_{L^2\cap A^6_\rho}+\|e^{\sigma \mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\Psi}\|_{A^6_\rho}\ll1$ with some $\rho>0$. In particular, $u_+$ is nowhere vanishing. Then they showed the modified scattering in $L^2$, thereby constructing the modified wave operator $W_{\sigma,+}(u_+)\in L^2$.                                                                                                                            

 The method in \cite{SeWu} successfully constructs the modified wave operators for all $d\ge1$, $0<\sigma<1/d$ and $\lambda\in {\mathbb{R}}$ under the above assumptions on the scattering data. Notably,  they establish the relevant asymptotic profile for $0<\sigma\le 1/(2d)$ and a mechanism to determine it, both of which were previously unknown in the literature.
                                                                                                        
 Compared with their result, our admissible range of $\sigma$ is narrow and we consider the one-dimensional defocusing case only. However, the advantage of our result is that we treat general scattering data $u_+\in \mathcal F H^\nu({\mathbb{R}})$ without either smallness or non-vanishing conditions, besides we construct the modified wave operator not only on $L^2({\mathbb{R}})$ but also on $\mathcal F H^1({\mathbb{R}})$ which particularly yields the sharp decay estimates  
\hyperref[{remark_1_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{remark_1_2}}}} 
. Furthermore, we deal with the difficulty caused by  the low regularity at $u=0$ of the subcritical nonlinearity $|u|^{2\sigma}u$ which does not appear under non-vanishing conditions. 
 
 The two approaches pursue fundamentally different objectives and employ distinct methodologies in the construction of modified wave operators for subcritical nonlinearities. While the approach of \cite{SeWu} succeeds in treating the full range of exponents and general space dimensions at the cost of imposing above restrictions on the scattering data, our approach, by restricting the admissible dimensions and exponent range, enables us to handle a broader class of large scattering data. Thus, the two works address different aspects of the problem.

We list further remarks on Theorem \ref{theorem_1}. 
\label{remark_theorem_1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Precisely speaking, the uniqueness in Theorem \ref{theorem_1} means that if $u_1,u_2\in C({\mathbb{R}}, L^2({\mathbb{R}}))$ are two solutions to  
\hyperref[{NLS}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{NLS}}}} 
 satisfying $e^{-it\Delta/2}u_j\in C({\mathbb{R}}, \mathcal F H^1({\mathbb{R}}))$ for $j=1,2$ and  
\hyperref[{theorem_1_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{theorem_1_1}}}} 
 for any $\delta>0$ with the same asymptotic profile $u_{\mathrm p,+}(t)$, then $u_1\equiv u_2$. 
\item By the time reversal symmetry of  
\hyperref[{NLS}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{NLS}}}} 
, the analogous statement also holds for the negative time direction $t\to -\infty$. Namely, for any $u_-\in {\mathcal{F}} H^{\nu}({\mathbb{R}})$ there exists a unique global solution $u\in C({\mathbb{R}},L^2({\mathbb{R}}))$ to  
\hyperref[{NLS}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{NLS}}}} 
 satisfying $e^{-it\Delta/2}u\in C({\mathbb{R}}, \mathcal F H^1({\mathbb{R}}))$ and  
\hyperref[{theorem_1_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{theorem_1_1}}}} 
 with $u_{\mathrm p,+}$ replaced by $u_{\mathrm p,-}$ as $t\to -\infty$. In particular, the negative time modified wave operator $W_{\sigma,-}:{\mathcal{F}} H^{\nu}({\mathbb{R}})\ni u_-\mapsto u(0)\in \mathcal F H^1({\mathbb{R}})$  exists.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                
        
\item NLS  
\hyperref[{NLS}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{NLS}}}} 
 is invariant under the scaling 
$
(0,\infty)\ni \rho\mapsto u_\rho(t,x)=\rho^{\frac1\sigma}u(\rho^2 t,\rho x),
$  
that is, $u$ satisfies  
\hyperref[{NLS}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{NLS}}}} 
 if and only if $u_\rho$ does. Moreover, we have $
\|{\mathcal{F}}(f_\rho) \|_{L^\infty}=\rho^{\frac1\sigma-1}\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^\infty}
$. Thus, it could be possible to assume without loss of generality that $\|\widehat{u_+}\|_{L^\infty}$ is sufficiently small as long as $\sigma\neq 1$. However, this does not reduce at all the difficulty of the subcritical case $\sigma<1$. To explain the difficulty, we observe that $u-u_{\mathrm p,+}$ satisfies, at least formally, 
$$
i\partial_t (u-u_{\mathrm p,+})+\frac12\Delta (u-u_{\mathrm p,+})=\lambda |u|^{2\sigma} u-\lambda |u_{\mathrm p,+}|^{2\sigma}u_{\mathrm p,+}+\text{(error)}
$$
with some error term \text{(error)} depending only on $u_{\mathrm p}$ which we neglect in the following observation for simplicity. Since $|u_{\mathrm p,+}|\le t^{-1/2}\|\widehat{u_+}\|_{L^\infty}$, one can expect
\begin{align*}
\frac{d}{ds}\|u-u_{\mathrm p,+}\|_{L^2}^2
&\sim 2\lambda \mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\<|u|^{2\sigma} u-|u_{\mathrm p,+}|^{2\sigma}u_{\mathrm p,+},u-u_{\mathrm p,+}\>\\
&\lesssim t^{-\sigma}|\lambda|\|\widehat{u_+}\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma}\|u-u_{\mathrm p,+}\|^2+\|u-u_{\mathrm p,+}\|_{L^{4\sigma+4}}^{2\sigma+2},
\end{align*}
where the first term $ t^{-\sigma}\|\widehat{u_+}\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma}\|u-u_{\mathrm p,+}\|^2$ is expected to be the worst part regarding the decay rate in $t$ as we seek a solution satisfying $\|u-u_{\mathrm p,+}\|_{H^1}\lesssim t^{-\beta}$ and $\|u\|_{L^\infty}\lesssim t^{-1/2}$ as $t\to \infty$. If either that $\sigma>1$ or that $\sigma=1$ and $|\lambda|\|\widehat{u_+}\|_{L^\infty}^2$ is small enough, then it is in fact possible to construct  a unique global solution $u$ satisfying $\|u-u_{\mathrm p,+}\|_{L^2}\lesssim t^{-\beta}$ with some $\beta>0$ by using this formulation (\cite{Ozawa_1991}). We will give a brief explanation on the argument for the small-data critical case in the beginning of Section \ref{subsection_energy}. However, this  argument does not work for the subcritical case as the first term is no longer a small perturbation regardless of the size of $\|\widehat{u_+}\|_{L^\infty}$ since the decay rate $t^{-\sigma}$ is too weak. Overcoming this difficulty is the most novel aspect of the present paper.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             
\item It would be interesting to investigate whether a strategy similar to the one developed in the present paper can be applied to establish modified scattering for other subcritical nonlinear dispersive equations, in particular, for the NLS  
\hyperref[{NLS}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{NLS}}}} 
 in higher dimensions and for the Hartree  equation in $d=1$ with the nonlinearity $(|x|^{-\sigma}*|u|^2)u$ for some $\sigma<1$. 
\end{enumerate}

\label{lemma_PC}
Suppose $f\in C([t_1,\infty),L^2({\mathbb{R}}))$ and $e^{-it\Delta/2}f\in C([t_1,\infty),{\mathcal{F}} H^1({\mathbb{R}}))$. Then
\begin{align}
\label{lemma_PC_1}
\|f(t)\|&=\|\mathop{\mathrm{PC}}[f](s)\|,\\
\label{lemma_PC_2}
\|xe^{-it\Delta/2}f(t)\|&=\|\nabla \mathop{\mathrm{PC}}[f](s)\|,\\
\label{lemma_PC_3}
\left\|\left|\varphi\left(\frac xt\right)\right|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{u_{\mathrm p}(t)} f(t)]\right\|&=s^{1/2}\left\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}(s)}\mathop{\mathrm{PC}}[f](s)\right\|,
                                                                     
\end{align}
where $s=t^{-1}$. 
In particular, $\mathop{\mathrm{PC}}[f]\in C((0,s_1],H^1({\mathbb{R}}))$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
\hyperref[{lemma_PC_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{lemma_PC_1}}}} 
 follows by the unitarity of $\mathcal M(t)$ and $\mathcal D(t)$ on $L^2({\mathbb{R}})$.  
\hyperref[{lemma_PC_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{lemma_PC_2}}}} 
 follows from  
\hyperref[{Dollard}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{Dollard}}}} 
  as
$$
\| xe^{-it\Delta/2}f(t)\|=\|x\mathcal M(t)^{-1}\mathcal F^{-1}\mathcal D(t)^{-1}\mathcal M(t)^{-1}f(t)\|=\|\nabla \mathop{\mathrm{PC}}[f](s)\|.
$$
To prove  
\hyperref[{lemma_PC_3}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{lemma_PC_3}}}} 
, we let $f(t)=\mathcal M(t)\mathcal D(t)\overline g(t)$ and $u_{\mathrm p}(t)=\mathcal M(t)\mathcal D(t)\overline{u_1}(t)$ for short. Since
$$
\overline{\mathcal M(t)}=e^{-\frac{i|x|^2}{2t}},\quad \overline{\mathcal D(t)h}=\overline{(it)^{-1/2}h(x/t)}=i^{1/2}t^{-1/2}\overline{h(x/t)}
$$
for any $h\in L^2({\mathbb{R}})$, we can write
\begin{align*}
&\left\|\left|\varphi\left(\frac xt\right)\right|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{u_{\mathrm p}(t)}f(t)]\right\|\\
                                                                                                                                                                                             
&=\left\|\left|\varphi\left(\frac xt\right)\right|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[e^{-\frac{i|x|^2}{2t}}i^{1/2}t^{-1/2}u_1\left(\frac xt\right)e^{\frac{i|x|^2}{2t}}(it)^{-1/2}\overline g\left(\frac xt\right)\right]\right\|\\
&=t^{-1/2}\left\|\mathcal D(t)|\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[u_1(t)\overline{g(t)}\right]\right\|
                                                                                     
=s^{1/2}\left\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}(s)}\mathop{\mathrm{PC}}[f](s)\right]\right\|.
\end{align*}
This completes the proof.

\label{lemma_propagator}
Suppose $\sigma>0$ and $\varphi\in H^1({\mathbb{R}})$. Then there exists a unique propagator 
$$\mathcal  U(s,r):(0,1]\ni s,r\mapsto \mathbb B(H^1({\mathbb{R}})^2)$$ generated by $\mathcal H(s)$ satisfying the following properties:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] $\mathcal  U(s,r)=\mathcal  U (s,s')\mathcal  U (s',s)$ and $\mathcal  U (s,s)=I$ for all $0< r,s,s' \le1$. 
\item[(2)] $(0,1]^2\ni (s,r)\mapsto \mathcal  U(s,r)$ is strongly continuous on $H^1({\mathbb{R}})^2$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
\item[(3)] For all $\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}_0\in  H^1({\mathbb{R}})^2$ and $s,r\in (0,1]$, $\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}(s,x)=\mathcal  U(s,r)\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}_0(x)$ solves  
\hyperref[{linearized_equation}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{linearized_equation}}}} 
 in $H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})^2$. 
\item[(4)] If $\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}_0=\vv{\psi_0}$ with $\psi_0\in H^1({\mathbb{R}})$, then $\mathcal  U(s,r)\vv{\psi_0}=\vv \psi(s)$, where $\psi\in C((0,1],H^1({\mathbb{R}}))$ satisfies 
\begin{equation}
\label{psi}
\left\{\begin{aligned}
&i\partial_s\psi +\frac12\Delta \psi=\lambda  s^{\sigma-2}\left(\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma}\psi +\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}^2\overline{\psi}\right),\quad 0< s\le1,\\
&\psi(r)=\psi_0.
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
The proof is essentially the same as that of \cite[Lemma 2.2]{KaMi}, so we give its brief outline only. 
Observe that $v_{\mathrm p}= \varphi e^{-\frac{i\lambda}{\sigma-1}|\varphi|^{2\sigma} s^{\sigma-1}}$ satisfies
\begin{align*}
|\varphi|=|\varphi|,\quad |\nabla v_{\mathrm p}|\lesssim (1+ s^{\sigma-1}|\varphi|^{2\sigma})|\nabla\varphi|,
\end{align*}
which implies                                                             
\begin{align}
\nonumber
\|\mathcal  V(s)\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}_0\|_{H^1}
&\lesssim \|\mathcal  V(s)\|_{L^\infty}\|\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}_0\|_{H^1}+\|\nabla \mathcal  V(s)\|\|\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}_0\|_{L^\infty}\\
&\lesssim (1+ s^{\sigma-1})\|\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}_0\|_{H^1}. 
\label{lemma_propagator_proof_2}
\end{align}
With this bound and the unitarity of $e^{-it\mathcal H_0}$ on $H^1({\mathbb{R}})^2$ at hand, one can construct a unique solution $\mathcal  U(s,r)\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}_0$ to  
\hyperref[{linearized_equation}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{linearized_equation}}}} 
 by solving the Duhamel integral equation
\begin{align}
\label{lemma_propagator_proof_3}
\mathcal  U(s,r)\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}_0=e^{-i(s-r)\mathcal H_0}\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}_0-i\lambda \int_r^s \tau^{\sigma-2}e^{-i(s-\tau)\mathcal H_0}\mathcal  V(\tau,x)\mathcal  U(\tau,r)\mbox{\boldmath $\Psi$}_0d\tau
\end{align}
in $C((0,1],H^1({\mathbb{R}})^2)$ via the standard successive approximation. The items (1)--(4) then follow from this Duhamel formula and the uniqueness of the solution to  
\hyperref[{linearized_equation}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{linearized_equation}}}} 
.

<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{proposition_energy}
Let $\lambda>0$, $1\le \nu\le 3/2$, $4/(3+2\nu)<\sigma\le1$, $\varphi\in H^1({\mathbb{R}})$ and $\nabla\varphi\in L^{\frac{2}{3-2\nu}}({\mathbb{R}})$. Suppose $\delta>0$ is so small that $(3+2\nu)\sigma-4-(2\nu-1)\delta>0$.  Then, there exists $s_0>0$ such that
\begin{align}
\label{proposition_energy_estimate_1}
Q\big[\mathcal  U(s,r)\vv{\psi_0}\big](s)\lesssim Q[\psi_0](r),\quad \vv{\psi_0}=(\psi_0,\overline{\psi_0})^{\mathrm T}
\end{align}
uniformly in  $\psi_0\in H^1({\mathbb{R}})$ and $0<r\le s\le s_0$. In particular, for sufficiently small $\delta>0$,  
\hyperref[{proposition_energy_estimate_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_energy_estimate_1}}}} 
 holds for $4/5<\sigma \le1$ if $\varphi\in H^1({\mathbb{R}})$ and for $2/3<\sigma\le4/5$ if in addition $\nabla \varphi \in L^\infty({\mathbb{R}})$.

By Lemma \ref{lemma_propagator} (4), it suffices to prove
\begin{align}
\label{proposition_energy_proof_0}
Q[\psi(s)](s)\lesssim Q[\psi_0](r),
\end{align}
where  $\psi\in C((0,1],H^1({\mathbb{R}}))$ is the solution to  
\hyperref[{psi}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{psi}}}} 
. 
                                                                                                                                                                                          
The key ingredient is the following identity: 
\begin{align}
\nonumber
\frac{d}{ds}\Big( Q[\psi](s)\Big)^2&=-(\sigma-\delta)  s^{-\sigma-1+\delta}\|\psi\|^2-(2-\sigma)\sigma\lambda  s^{\sigma-3} \||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\|^2\\
\nonumber
&\quad -4\sigma\lambda  s^{-2+\delta}\<|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\>\\
\label{eq_E_ep}
&\quad-\sigma \lambda  s^{\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\<\nabla \psi,|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{\varphi}\nabla\varphi]\psi\>.
\end{align}
To prove this identity, we observe that the first equation of  
\hyperref[{psi}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{psi}}}} 
 is written in the form
\begin{align}
\label{proposition_energy_proof_2}
i\partial_s\psi +\frac12\Delta\psi
=\lambda  s^{\sigma-2}\left\{|\varphi|^{2\sigma}\psi+2\sigma  |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]v_{\mathrm p}\right\}.
\end{align}
Then  
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep}}}} 
 is obtained by the combination of the following three identities: 
\begin{align}
&\frac14\frac{d}{ds}\|\nabla \psi\|^2+\lambda  s^{\sigma-2} \frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac12\||\varphi|^{\sigma}\psi\|^2+\sigma\left\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\right\|^2\right)\nonumber\\
&\quad =-2\sigma\lambda^2  s^{2\sigma-4} \<|\varphi|^{4\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\>,\label{eq_E_ep_1}\\
&\frac{d}{ds}( s^{-\sigma+\delta}\|\psi\|^2)
=-(\sigma-\delta)  s^{-\sigma-1+\delta}\|\psi\|^2-4\sigma\lambda  s^{-2+\delta}\<|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\>\label{eq_E_ep_1_1},\\
&\frac{\lambda s^{\sigma-2}}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\||\varphi|^\sigma \psi\|^2
                  
=\sigma \lambda s^{\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\<\nabla \psi,|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{\varphi}\nabla\varphi]\psi\>\nonumber\\
&\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-2\sigma\lambda^2 s^{2\sigma-4}\<|\varphi|^{4\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\>\label{eq_E_ep_1_2}.
\end{align}
Indeed,  
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep}}}} 
 is obtained by calculating both sides of  
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1}}}} 
+ 
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1_1}}}} 
- 
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1_2}}}} 
 as follows: 
\begin{align*}
&\text{LHS of } 
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1}}}} 
+ 
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1_1}}}} 
- 
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1_2}}}} 
\\
&=\frac14\frac{d}{ds}\|\nabla \psi\|^2
+\sigma\lambda  s^{\sigma-2} \frac{d}{ds}\left\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\right\|^2
+\frac{d}{ds}( s^{-\sigma+\delta}\|\psi\|^2)\\
&=\frac{d}{ds}\Big( Q[\psi](s)\Big)^2-(\sigma-2)\sigma\lambda s^{\sigma-3}\left\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\right\|^2
\end{align*}
and
\begin{align*}
&\text{RHS of } 
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1}}}} 
+ 
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1_1}}}} 
- 
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1_2}}}} 
\\
&=
-(\sigma-\delta)  s^{-\sigma-1+\delta}\|\psi\|^2-4\sigma\lambda  s^{-2+\delta}\<|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\>\\
&\quad -\sigma \lambda s^{\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\<\nabla \psi,|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{\varphi}\nabla\varphi]\psi\>.
\end{align*}
 Identities  
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1_1}}}} 
 and  
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1_2}}}} 
 are obtained by direct calculations. Precisely, we have 
\begin{align*}
\frac{d}{ds}( s^{-\sigma+\delta}\|\psi\|^2)
=-(\sigma-\delta)  s^{-\sigma-1+\delta}\|\psi\|^2
+2s^{-\sigma+\delta}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\<\partial_s\psi,\psi\>,
\end{align*}
where $\<\cdot,\cdot\>$ is identified with the duality coupling $\<\cdot,\cdot\>_{H^{-1},H^1}$ and  
\hyperref[{proposition_energy_proof_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_energy_proof_2}}}}

\begin{align*}
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\<\partial_s\psi,\psi\>
&=2\sigma \lambda s^{\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\<|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]v_{\mathrm p},\psi\>\\
&=-2\sigma \lambda s^{\sigma-2}\<|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\>.
\end{align*}
Similarly, 
\begin{align*}
&\frac{\lambda s^{\sigma-2}}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\||\varphi|^\sigma \psi\|^2\\
&=\frac{\lambda s^{\sigma-2}}{2}\left(-\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\<\Delta \psi,|\varphi|^{2\sigma}\psi\>
+4\sigma \lambda s^{\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\<|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]v_{\mathrm p},|\varphi|^{2\sigma}\psi\>\right)\\
&=\sigma\lambda s^{\sigma-2} \mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\<\nabla \psi,|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{\varphi}\nabla \varphi]\psi\>
-2\sigma \lambda^2 s^{2\sigma-4}\<|\varphi|^{4\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\>.
\end{align*}

\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1}}}}

\hyperref[{proposition_energy_proof_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_energy_proof_2}}}} 
,\partial_s \psi\>=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits \<\text{RHS of } 
\hyperref[{proposition_energy_proof_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_energy_proof_2}}}} 
,\partial_s \psi\>.$$
Indeed, we obtain
\begin{align*}
&\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits \<i\partial_s\psi +\frac12\Delta\psi,\partial_s \psi\>
                                                                                                                      
=-\frac14\frac{d}{ds}\left\|\nabla \psi\right\|^2
\end{align*}
and, by using  
\hyperref[{NLS_v_p}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{NLS_v_p}}}} 
, 
\begin{align*}
&\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\< |\varphi|^{2\sigma}\psi+2\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],\partial_s\psi\>\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\||\varphi|^{\sigma}\psi\|^2+2\sigma \mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\< |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],\partial_s(\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi)\>\\
&\quad -2\sigma\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\< |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],i\lambda s^{\sigma-2}|\varphi|^{2\sigma}\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi \>\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\||\varphi|^{\sigma}\psi\|^2
+\sigma \frac{d}{ds}\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\|^2
+2\sigma\lambda s^{\sigma-2}\< |\varphi|^{4\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi] \>. 
\end{align*}
Identity  
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep_1}}}} 
 thus follows. 
Note however that the term $\<i\partial_s\psi +\frac12\Delta\psi,\partial_s \psi\>$ does not make sense if $\psi\in C((0,1].H^1({\mathbb{R}}))\cap C^1((0,1],H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}))$ only. To be more precise, if we set $\psi^m=(I-m^{-1}\Delta)^{-1}\psi$, then

\hyperref[{proposition_energy_proof_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_energy_proof_2}}}} 
,\partial_s \psi^m\>,\label{eq_E_ep_2}
\end{align}
and  taking $m\to \infty$. This is possible since $(I-m^{-1}\Delta)^{-1}$ commutes with $i\partial_s+\frac12\Delta$. Since the computation is rather involved, we give the technical details in Appendix \ref{appendix_A} below. 


Now we prove  
\hyperref[{proposition_energy_proof_0}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_energy_proof_0}}}} 
. Note that the first two terms of the RHS of  
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep}}}} 
 are negative and thus good terms. Other two terms  are not sign definite, but can be controlled by the first two terms as follows. For the third term, taking $s_0>0$ further small if necessary, we obtain
\begin{align}
\nonumber
&\left|-4\sigma\lambda  s^{-2+\delta}\<|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi],\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\>\right|\\
\nonumber
&\le 4\sigma\lambda  s^{\frac{\delta}{2}}\|\varphi\|_{L^\infty}^{\sigma}
\cdot  s^{-\frac{\sigma+1-\delta}{2}}\|\psi\|
\cdot  s^{\frac{\sigma-3}{2}}\|\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\|\\
\label{proposition_energy_estimate_1_proof_4}
&\le \frac{(\sigma-\delta)  s^{-\sigma-1+\delta}\|\psi\|^2}{2}+\frac{(2-\sigma)\sigma\lambda  s^{\sigma-3} \||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\psi]\|^2}{2}
\end{align}
for all $s\in (0,s_0]$ with sufficiently small $s_0=s_0(\sigma,\lambda,\delta,\varphi)$. 
To deal with the last term of  
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep}}}} 
, we use Gagliardo--Nirenberg's  inequality
\begin{align}
\|f\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\nu-1}}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim \|\nabla f\|_{L^2({\mathbb{R}})}^{\frac32-\nu}\|f\|_{L^2({\mathbb{R}})}^{\nu-\frac12},\quad 1\le \nu\le 3/2,
\label{GN}
\end{align}
and Young's inequality \begin{align}
ab\le \frac{a^p}{p}+\frac{b^q}{q}
\label{Young}
\end{align} with $p=\frac{2}{5/2-\nu}$, $q=\frac{2}{\nu-1/2}$, $a=\sqrt{m}^{-(\nu-1/2)}\|\nabla \psi\|^{5/2-\nu}$ and $b=(\sqrt{m}\|\psi\|)^{\nu-1/2}$ to obtain\begin{align*}|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\<\nabla \psi,|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{\varphi}\nabla\varphi]\psi\>|&\lesssim \|\nabla \psi\|_{L^2}\|\varphi\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma-1}\|\nabla \varphi\|_{L^{\frac{2}{3-2\nu}}}\|\psi\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\nu-1}}}\\&\lesssim \|\nabla \psi\|^{\frac52-\nu}\|\psi\|^{\nu-\frac12}\\&\le C_0 (m^{-\frac{\nu-1/2}{5/2-\nu}}\|\nabla \psi\|^2+m\|\psi\|^2)\end{align*}with some $C_0>0$ uniformly in $m>0$. By choosing $$m=\frac{(\sigma-\delta) s^{1-2\sigma+\delta}}{2C_0 \sigma\lambda}$$so that $C_0\sigma\lambda m s^{\sigma-2}=(\sigma-\delta) s^{-\sigma-1+\delta}/2$ and $\sigma\lambda C_0 s^{\sigma-2}m^{-\frac{\nu-1/2}{5/2-\nu}}\sim  s^{\frac{(3+2\nu)\sigma-4-(2\nu-1)\delta}{5-2\nu}-1}$, we have
\begin{align}
\nonumber
&|\sigma \lambda  s^{\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\<\nabla \psi,|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{\varphi}\nabla\varphi]\psi\>|\\
\label{proposition_energy_estimate_1_proof_5}
&\le C s^{\frac{(3+2\nu)\sigma-4-(2\nu-1)\delta}{5-2\nu}-1}\|\nabla \psi\|^2+\frac{(\sigma-\delta)  s^{-\sigma-1+\delta}\|\psi\|^2}{2}
\end{align}
with some $C>0$. It follows from  
\hyperref[{eq_E_ep}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{eq_E_ep}}}} 
,  
\hyperref[{proposition_energy_estimate_1_proof_4}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_energy_estimate_1_proof_4}}}} 
 and  
\hyperref[{proposition_energy_estimate_1_proof_5}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_energy_estimate_1_proof_5}}}} 
 that
\begin{align*}
\frac{d}{ds}\Big(Q[\psi](s)\Big)^2
\lesssim  s^{\frac{(3+2\nu)\sigma-4-(2\nu-1)\delta}{5-2\nu}-1}\|\nabla \psi\|^2
                                                          
\lesssim  s^{\frac{(3+2\nu)\sigma-4-(2\nu-1)\delta}{5-2\nu}-1}Q[\psi](s)^2.
\end{align*}
Assuming $(3+2\nu)\sigma-4-(2\nu-1)\delta>0$, we obtain  
\hyperref[{proposition_energy_proof_0}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_energy_proof_0}}}} 
 as follows: 
 
$$Q[\psi](s)^2\le Q[\psi_0](r)^2\exp\left(C \int_r^s \tau^{\frac{(3+2\nu)\sigma-4-(2\nu-1)\delta}{5-2\nu}-1}d\tau\right)\lesssim Q[\psi_0](r)^2$$uniformly in $0< r<s\le s_0$.

We compute $\nabla\Phi =2\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{\varphi}\nabla\varphi]$ and 
$$
\|\nabla (e^{-i\gamma \Phi }f)\|^2=\|\nabla f-i\gamma f \nabla \Phi\|^2=\|\nabla f\|^2+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\<\nabla f,-i\gamma f\nabla \Phi\>+\gamma^2\|f \nabla \Phi \|^2.
$$
It follows from the same computations as in the proof of  
\hyperref[{proposition_energy_estimate_1_proof_5}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_energy_estimate_1_proof_5}}}} 
 that
\begin{align*}
|\<\nabla f,f \nabla \Phi \>|
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 
\lesssim m^{-\frac{\nu-1/2}{5/2-\nu}}\|\nabla f\|^2+m\|f\|^2
\end{align*}
for all $m>0$. Choosing $m=s^{1-2\sigma+\delta_1}$ with some $\delta_1>\delta$ sufficiently close to $\delta$ so that $\gamma m\sim s^{-\sigma+\delta_1}$ and $\gamma m^{-\frac{\nu-1/2}{5/2-\nu}}\sim s^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha=\frac{(3+2\nu)\sigma-4+(2\nu-1)\delta_1}{5-2\nu}>0$ and $s_0$ is small enough, we have
\begin{align*}
2\gamma |\<\nabla f, f \nabla \Phi\>|
\le C s^{\alpha} \|\nabla f\|^2+s^{-\sigma+\delta_1}\|f\|^2
\le \frac14(\|\nabla f\|^2+s^{-\sigma+\delta}\|f\|^2)
\end{align*}
for any $s\in (0,s_0]$. Note that $s_0$ can be taken uniformly in $s,f$. Similarly, we use  
\hyperref[{GN}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{GN}}}} 
 and  
\hyperref[{Young}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{Young}}}} 
 with $p=\frac{2}{3-2\nu}$, $q=\frac{2}{2\nu-1}$, $a=\sqrt{m}^{-(2\nu-1)}\|\nabla f \|^{3-2\nu}$ and $b=(\sqrt{m}\|f\|)^{2\nu-1}$ to obtain
\begin{align*}
\|f\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\nu-1}}}^2
\le \|\nabla f\|^{3-2\nu}\|f\|^{2\nu-1}\lesssim m^{-\frac{2\nu-1}{3-2\nu}}\|\nabla f\|^2+m\|f\|^2.
\end{align*}
Choosing $m=s^{2-3\sigma+\delta_1}$ and $\delta_1>\delta$ sufficiently close to $\delta$ so that $\gamma^2m\sim s^{-\sigma+\delta_1}$ and $\gamma^2m^{-\frac{2\nu-1}{3-2\nu}}\sim s^\alpha$, we have
\begin{align*}
\gamma^2\|f \nabla \Phi \|^2
&\lesssim \gamma^2\|\varphi\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma-1}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{2}{3-2\nu}}}^2\|f\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\nu-1}}}^2\\
&\le Cs^{\alpha} \|\nabla f\|^2+s^{-\sigma+\delta_1}\|f|^2
\le \frac14(\|\nabla f\|^2+s^{-\sigma+\delta}\|f\|^2).
\end{align*}
Hence, $\widetilde Q[f](s)\lesssim  Q[f](s)$. Conversely, if we set $g=e^{-i\gamma\Phi}f$, then
 $$\|\nabla f\|^2=\|\nabla (e^{i\gamma\Phi }g)\|^2=\|\nabla g\|^2+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\<\nabla g,i\gamma g\nabla \Phi\>+\gamma^2\|g\nabla \Phi\|^2.$$
By the same argument with $f$ replaced by $g$ then yields $ Q[f](s)\lesssim  \widetilde Q[f](s)$ for $s\in (0,s_0]$.

Note that $Q$ can be replaced by $\widetilde Q$ by the above lemma. The following elementary inequalities are used frequently: 
\begin{align}
\label{proposition_nonlinear_proof_1}
\big||z_1|^{2\sigma-k} z_1^k-|z_2|^{2\sigma-k} z_2^k\big|
&\lesssim |z_1-z_2|^{2\sigma}+|z_2|^{2\sigma-1}|z_1-z_2|,\\
\big||z_1|^{2\sigma-k-1} z_1^k-|z_2|^{2\sigma-k-1} z_2^k\big|
                                   
\label{proposition_nonlinear_proof_2}
&\lesssim |z_1-z_2|^{2\sigma-1}
\end{align}
for $z_1,z_2\in {\mathbb{C}}$ and $k=0,1,2,...$. We also use the following estimates: 
\begin{align*}
\|w_{\varepsilon}\|&                                      
\lesssim  s^{\sigma/2-\delta/2}\widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s),\\
                                                           
\|\nabla (e^{-i\gamma \Phi}w_{\varepsilon})\|&\lesssim \widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s),\\
\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^\infty}&\lesssim \|w_{\varepsilon}\|^{1/2}\|\nabla (e^{-i\gamma \Phi}w_{\varepsilon})\|^{1/2}\lesssim  s^{\sigma/4-\delta/4 }\widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s),\\
                                                                                                                                                     
\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}w_{\varepsilon}]\|&\lesssim  s^{1-\sigma/2}\widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s).
\end{align*}
These follow from the definition of the energy norm $\widetilde Q[w]$. Let $G=G(w_{\varepsilon},v_{\mathrm p})$ for short. To prove  
\hyperref[{proposition_nonlinear_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_nonlinear_2}}}}

Recall that $G$ is given by $\int_0^1G_{1,\theta}d\theta,
$
where 
$$
G_{1,\theta}= (\sigma+1)w_{\varepsilon}\left(|w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma}-|\varphi|^{2\sigma}\right)
+\sigma \overline{w_{\varepsilon}}\left(|w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-2}w_{[\theta]}^2-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}^2\right)
$$
and $w_{[\theta]}=v_{\mathrm p}+\theta w_{\varepsilon}$. We start with dealing with $\|G\|$. It follows from  
\hyperref[{proposition_nonlinear_proof_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_nonlinear_proof_1}}}} 
 and $|v_{\mathrm p}|=|\varphi|$ that 
\begin{align*}
\left||w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-k}w_{[\theta]}^k-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-k}v_{\mathrm p}^k\right|
                                                                                           
&\lesssim |w_{[\theta]}-v_{\mathrm p}|^{2\sigma}+|\varphi|^{2\sigma-1}|w_{[\theta]}-v_{\mathrm p}|\\
&\lesssim |w_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma}+\|\varphi\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma-1}|w_{\varepsilon}|
\end{align*}
and hence
$
|G_{1,\theta}|\lesssim |w_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma+1}+|w_{\varepsilon}|^2
$ uniformly in $\theta\in [0,1]$. Then
\begin{align}

 s^{-\sigma/2+\delta/2}\|G\|
                                                                                                    
&\lesssim   s^{-\sigma/2+\delta/2}\|w_{\varepsilon}\|(\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma}+\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^\infty})\\
&\lesssim  s^{\sigma^2/2-\sigma\delta/2}\widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s)^{2\sigma+1}+ s^{\sigma/4-\delta/4}\widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s)^{2}.
\label{proposition_nonlinear_proof_3}
                                                                             
\end{align}

For the term $|\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}G]$, the same argument implies a rough bound $$|\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}G]\lesssim |\varphi|^{2\sigma-1}|w_{\varepsilon}|^2++|\varphi||w_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma+1}. $$
However, this is not enough to prove  
\hyperref[{proposition_nonlinear_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_nonlinear_2}}}} 
. Instead, we utilize the structure of $\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}G]$ to show \begin{align}

\end{align}
which implies the desired estimate as

\nonumber
&\lesssim  s^{\sigma/2-1}\|\varphi\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma-1}\left(\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}w_{\varepsilon}]\|\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^\infty}
          
+\|w_{\varepsilon}\|\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma}\right)\\
                                                                                                 
                                      
&\lesssim   s^{\sigma/4-\delta/4}\widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s)^2+ s^{\sigma^2/2+\sigma-1-(\sigma+1)\delta/2}\widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s)^{2\sigma+1}.
\label{proposition_nonlinear_proof_4}
\end{align}
In order to show  
\hyperref[{proposition_nonlinear_proof_4_0}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_nonlinear_proof_4_0}}}} 
, we first calculate 
\begin{align*}
\partial_z|z|^{2\sigma}&=\sigma |z|^{2\sigma-2}\overline z,\quad  
\partial_{\overline{z}}|z|^{2\sigma}=\sigma |z|^{2\sigma-2} z,\\
\partial_z(|z|^{2\sigma-2}z^2)&=(\sigma+1) |z|^{2\sigma-2}z,\quad
\partial_{\overline z}(|z|^{2\sigma-2}z^2)=(\sigma-1) |z|^{2\sigma-4}z^3,
\end{align*}
and use the Taylor expansions of $|w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma}$ and $|w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-2}w_{[\theta]}^2$ around $v_{\mathrm p}$ to observe
\begin{align*}
                                      
\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}G_{1,\theta}
&=(\sigma+1)  \overline{v_{\mathrm p}}w_{\varepsilon}\left\{\sigma|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\theta w_{\varepsilon}
+\sigma|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}\theta \overline{w_{\varepsilon}}\right\}\\
&\quad +\sigma \overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\overline{w_{\varepsilon}}\left\{(\sigma+1) |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}\theta w_{\varepsilon}+(\sigma-1)|\varphi|^{2\sigma-4}v_{\mathrm p}^3\theta \overline{w_{\varepsilon}}\right\}+\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}G_{2,\theta}\\
&=\theta (\sigma+1)\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}^2w_{\varepsilon}^2
+2\theta (\sigma+1)\sigma|\varphi|^{2\sigma}|w_{\varepsilon}|^2\\
&\quad +\theta \sigma(\sigma-1)|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}^2\overline{w_{\varepsilon}}^2
+\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}G_{2,\theta}
\end{align*}
where, setting $w_{[\theta,\rho]}=v_{\mathrm p}+\rho (w_{[\theta]}-v_{\mathrm p})=v_{\mathrm p}+\rho\theta w_{\varepsilon}$ for short, $G_{2,\theta}$ is given by
\begin{align*}
G_{2,\theta}
&=\theta (\sigma+1)\sigma w_{\varepsilon}^2 \int_0^1 \left(|w_{[\theta,\rho]}|^{2\sigma-2}\overline{w_{[\theta,\rho]}}-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\right)d\rho\\
&\quad+2\theta (\sigma+1)\sigma |w_{\varepsilon}|^2 \int_0^1 \left(|w_{[\theta,\rho]}|^{2\sigma-2}w_{[\theta,\rho]}-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}\right)d\rho
\\
                                                                                                                                                       
&\quad+\theta \sigma(\sigma-1) \overline{w_{\varepsilon}}^2 \int_0^1 \left(|w_{[\theta,\rho]}|^{2\sigma-4}w_{[\theta,\rho]}^3-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-4}v_{\mathrm p}^3\right)d\rho. 
\end{align*}
Since $\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits \overline z=-\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits z$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[z^2]=2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits z\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits z$, we have 
\begin{align*}
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}G_{1,\theta}]
=4\theta\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}w_{\varepsilon}]\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}w_{\varepsilon}]+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}G_{2,\theta}].
                                                                                                                                            
\end{align*}
Moreover,  
\hyperref[{proposition_nonlinear_proof_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_nonlinear_proof_2}}}} 
 implies
$
|G_{2,\theta}|\lesssim |w_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma+1}
$ 
and thus  
\hyperref[{proposition_nonlinear_proof_4_0}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_nonlinear_proof_4_0}}}} 
 follows. 

It remains to deal with $\|\nabla G\|$. To this end, we first calculate, for any $h\in H^1({\mathbb{R}})$ 
\begin{align}
                                      
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 
\nabla (|h|^{2\sigma-2}h^{2})
\label{proposition_nonlinear_proof_6}
                                                                                                                                                                   
&=(\sigma+1) |h|^{2\sigma-2}h\nabla h+(\sigma-1)|h|^{2\sigma-4} h^3\overline{\nabla h}.
\end{align}
For short we set $\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}=e^{-i\gamma \Phi}w_{\varepsilon}$. Then 
$$
e^{-i\gamma \Phi}G_{1,\theta}=(\sigma+1)\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}\left(|\widetilde w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma}-|\varphi|^{2\sigma}\right)
+\sigma \overline{\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\widetilde w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-2}\widetilde w_{[\theta]}^2-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\varphi^2\right),
$$
where 
$\nabla \{\overline {\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}}(|\widetilde w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-2}\widetilde w_{[\theta]}^2-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\varphi^2)\}$ is given by a linear combination of seven terms
\begin{align*}
&(\overline{\nabla  \widetilde w_{\varepsilon}})\left(|\widetilde w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-2}w_{[\theta]}^{2}-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\varphi^{2}\right),\\
&\overline{\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\widetilde w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-2}\widetilde w_{[\theta]}-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\varphi\right)\nabla \varphi,\\
&\overline{\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\widetilde w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-4}\widetilde w_{[\theta]}^3-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-4}\varphi^3\right)\overline{\nabla \varphi},\\
&\overline{\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\widetilde w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-2}\widetilde w_{[\theta]}-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\varphi\right)\nabla (\widetilde w_{[\theta]}-\varphi),\\
&\overline{\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\widetilde w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-4}\widetilde w_{[\theta]}^3-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-4}\varphi^3\right)\overline{\nabla (\widetilde w_{[\theta]}-\varphi)},\\
&\overline{\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}}|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\varphi\nabla (\widetilde w_{[\theta]}-\varphi),\\
&\overline{\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}}|\varphi|^{2\sigma-4}\varphi^3\overline{\nabla (\widetilde w_{[\theta]}-\varphi)}.
\end{align*}
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  
                                                                                                                                                                                                                       
Applying  
\hyperref[{proposition_nonlinear_proof_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_nonlinear_proof_1}}}} 
,  
\hyperref[{proposition_nonlinear_proof_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_nonlinear_proof_2}}}} 
 to these terms shows
\begin{align*}
&|\nabla \{\overline {\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}}(|\widetilde w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-2}\widetilde w_{[\theta]}^2-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\varphi^2)\}|\\
&\lesssim |\nabla \widetilde w_{\varepsilon}|\left(|w_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma}+\|\varphi\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma-1}|w_{\varepsilon}|\right)+|w_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma}|\nabla \varphi|
+|w_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|+\|\varphi\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma-1}|w_{\varepsilon}||\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|\\
&\lesssim |\nabla \widetilde w_{\varepsilon}|(|w_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma}+|w_{\varepsilon}|)+ |\nabla \varphi||w_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma}.
\end{align*}
By a similar argument, we also obtain the same estimate for $|\nabla \{ {\widetilde w_{\varepsilon}}(|\widetilde w_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma}-|\varphi|^{2\sigma})\}|$. 
Therefore, 
\begin{align}
\nonumber
\|\nabla   ( e^{-i\gamma \Phi}G)\|
&\lesssim \|\nabla \widetilde w_{\varepsilon}\|\left(\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma}+\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^\infty}\right)
+ \left\||\nabla\varphi||w_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma}\right\|
\end{align}
By H\"older's inequality $L^{\frac{2}{3-2\nu}}({\mathbb{R}})\cdot L^{\frac{1}{\nu-1}}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^2({\mathbb{R}})$, Sobolev's embedding $H^{s_\nu}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^{\frac{2\sigma}{\nu-1}}({\mathbb{R}})$ with $s_\nu=1/2-(\nu-1)/(2\sigma)=(\sigma-\nu+1)/(2\sigma)\in (0,1)$ and the interpolation, we obtain
\begin{align}
\left\||\nabla\varphi||w_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma}\right\|
&\le \|\nabla \varphi\|_{L^{\frac{2}{3-2\nu}}}\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{2\sigma}{\nu-1}}}^{2\sigma}\nonumber\\
&\lesssim \left(\|w_{\varepsilon}\|^{1-s_\nu}\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^1}^{s_\nu}\right)^{2\sigma}\nonumber\\
&\lesssim s^{(\sigma+\nu-1)(\sigma-\delta)/2}\widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s)^{2\sigma}.
\label{proposition_nonlinear_proof_7}
\end{align}
Thus
\begin{align*}
\|\nabla (  e^{-i\gamma \Phi}G)\|
\lesssim  s^{\sigma^2/2-\sigma\delta/2}\widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s)^{2\sigma+1}+ s^{ \sigma/4-\delta/4}
\widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s)^2+s^{(\sigma+\nu-1)(\sigma-\delta)/2}\widetilde Q[w_{\varepsilon}](s)^{2\sigma}.
\end{align*}
Since $\sigma/2^2-\sigma\delta/2>\sigma^2/2+\sigma-1-(\sigma+1)\delta/2$ for $0<\sigma<1$ and $\delta>0$, this estimate, combined with  
\hyperref[{proposition_nonlinear_proof_3}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_nonlinear_proof_3}}}} 
 and  
\hyperref[{proposition_nonlinear_proof_4}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_nonlinear_proof_4}}}} 
, imply  
\hyperref[{proposition_nonlinear_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_nonlinear_2}}}} 
. 
This completes the proof.

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lemma_Holder}
Let $m$ be an integer, $F(z)=|z|^{\alpha-m}z^m$ and $f\in H^\beta({\mathbb{R}})$. Then 
$$
\|F(\varphi)f\|_{H^\beta}\lesssim \begin{cases}\|\varphi\|_{H^1}^\alpha \|f\|_{H^\beta}&\text{if}\quad 0\le \beta\le1<\alpha,\ \varphi\in H^1({\mathbb{R}}),\\\|\varphi\|_{H^{3/2}}^\alpha \|f\|_{H^\beta}&\text{if}\quad 0\le \beta<\alpha<1,\ \varphi\in H^{3/2}({\mathbb{R}}).\end{cases}
$$
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
We first observe $\|F(\varphi)f\|\lesssim \|\varphi\|_{H^1}^{\alpha}\|f\|$ by the embedding $H^1({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^\infty({\mathbb{R}})$. When $\alpha>1$ and $\beta>1/2$ in which case $F\in C^1({\mathbb{C}})$ and $H^\beta({\mathbb{R}})$ is algebra, we have
\begin{align*}
\|F(\varphi)f\|_{\dot H^\beta}
\lesssim \|F'(\varphi)\|_{L^\infty}\|\varphi\|_{H^\beta}\|f\|_{H^\beta}
&\lesssim \|\varphi\|_{H^\beta}^\alpha\|f\|_{H^\beta},
                                                                                         
\end{align*}
where  $F'=(\partial_zF,\partial_{\overline z}F)$. In order to prove $F(\varphi)f\in \dot H^\beta({\mathbb{R}})$ for $\alpha>1$ and $\beta\le1/2$ or $0\le \beta<\alpha<1$, we first employ the fractional Leibniz rule by Kenig--Ponce--Vega  \cite{KPV} to observe
$$
\|F(\varphi)f\|_{\dot H^\beta}\lesssim \|f|\nabla|^\beta F(\varphi)\|_{L^2}+\|F(\varphi)|\nabla|^\beta f\|_{L^2}+\||\nabla|^{\beta-\beta_1}F(\varphi)\|_{L^{p_1}}\||\nabla|^{\beta_1}f\|_{L^{p_2}},
$$
where $1<p_1,p_2<\infty$, $1/2=1/p_1+1/p_2$ and $0<\beta_1<\beta$. Let $p>\max\{2,1/\beta\}$ be specified later. For the first term $\|f|\nabla|^\beta F(\varphi)\|_{L^2}$, H\"older's inequality $L^p({\mathbb{R}})\cdot L^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^2({\mathbb{R}})$ and Sobolev's embedding $H^\beta({\mathbb{R}}) \subset L^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}({\mathbb{R}})$ imply
\begin{align*}
\|f|\nabla|^\beta F(\varphi)\|_{L^2}\le \|f\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}}\||\nabla|^\beta F(\varphi)\|_{L^p}\lesssim \|f\|_{H^\beta} \||\nabla|^\beta F(\varphi)\|_{L^p}.
\end{align*}
The second term $\|F(\varphi)|\nabla|^\beta f\|_{L^2}$ is simply dominated by
$ \|\varphi\|_{H^1}^{\alpha}\|f\|_{H^\beta}$. 
For the third term, if we take $p_1>p$ close to $p$ so that $\beta_1:=1/p-1/p_1\in (0,\beta)$, then
$$
\||\nabla|^{\beta-\beta_1}F(\varphi)\|_{L^{p_1}}\||\nabla|^{\beta_1}f\|_{L^{p_2}}\lesssim \||\nabla|^\beta F(\varphi)\|_{L^p}\||\nabla|^{\beta_1+1/p_1}f\|_{L^2}\lesssim \||\nabla|^\beta F(\varphi)\|_{L^p}\|f\|_{H^\beta}
$$
by the embeddings $\dot W^{\beta_1,p}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^{p_1}({\mathbb{R}})$ and $\dot H^{1/p_1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^{p_2}({\mathbb{R}})$. 

It remains to deal with $\||\nabla|^\beta F(\varphi)\|_{L^p}$. When $\alpha>1$ and $\beta\le1/2$, we use the fractional chain rule by Christ--Weinstein \cite[Proposition 3.1]{ChWe} to obtain
$$
\||\nabla|^\beta F(\varphi)\|_{L^p}\lesssim \|F'(\varphi)\|_{L^{q_1}}\||\nabla|^\beta \varphi\|_{L^{q_2}}\lesssim \|\varphi\|_{L^{(\alpha-1)q_1}}^{\alpha-1}\||\nabla|^\beta \varphi\|_{L^{q_2}},
$$
where $1/p=1/q_1+1/q_2$ and $1<q_1,q_2<\infty$. If $(\alpha-1)q_1>2$, then
$$
\|\varphi\|_{L^{(\alpha-1)q_1}}^{\alpha-1}\||\nabla|^\beta \varphi\|_{L^{q_2}}\lesssim \|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}}^{\alpha-1}\|\varphi\|_{H^{\beta+1/2}}\lesssim \|\varphi\|_{H^1}^\alpha.
$$
On the other hand, when $0\le \beta<\alpha<1$,  the fractional chain rule for H\"older continuous  functions by Visan \cite[Proposition A.1]{Visan} implies
$$
\||\nabla|^\beta F(\varphi)\|_{L^p}\lesssim \|\varphi\|_{L^{(\alpha-\beta/s)q_1}}^{\alpha-\beta/s}\||\nabla|^s \varphi\|_{L^{{\beta q_2}/{s}}}^{\beta/s}
$$
provided $\beta/\alpha<s<1$, $1<p<\infty$, $1/p=1/q_1+1/q_2$ and $(1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha s})q_1>1$. Fixing such an $s$, we can choose  $p>\max\{2,1/\beta\}$ sufficiently large so that $(\alpha-{\beta}/{ s})q_1>2$ and $\beta q_2/s>2$. Then
$$
\|\varphi\|_{L^{(\alpha-\beta/s)q_1}}^{\alpha-\beta/s}\||\nabla|^s \varphi\|_{L^{{\beta q_2}/{s}}}^{\beta/s}\lesssim \|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}}^{\alpha-\beta/s}\|\varphi\|_{H^{s+1/2}}^{\beta/s}\lesssim \|\varphi\|_{H^{3/2}}^{\alpha}.
$$
This completes the proof.

In the following argument, $C_\nu$ denotes constants depending on $\|\varphi\|_{H^\nu}$ but not on ${\varepsilon},s$, which may vary line to line.

 for $1\le \nu<2$. Lemma \ref{lemma_Holder} with $\alpha=2\sigma>1$ and $\beta=\nu-1<1$ implies

\lesssim s^{\nu/2}\left(s^{\sigma-1}\|e^{i\gamma \Phi}\varphi\nabla \Phi\|_{\dot H^{\nu-1}}+\|e^{i\gamma \Phi}\nabla \varphi\|_{\dot H^{\nu-1}}\right)

 for $e_1$ as

&\le C_\nu s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)}(s^{-1/2}+s^{-\sigma/2+\delta/2}
+s^{\sigma/2-1}
)\\
&\le C_\nu s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)+\sigma/2-1}.
\end{align*}
To deal with $e_2$, we first rewrite  
\hyperref[{e2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e2}}}} 
 as
\begin{align}

&=\left(\Phi \mathcal R-\mathcal R\Phi \right)v_{\mathrm p}+2\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\mathcal Rv_{\mathrm p}\right]
\label{e_6}
\end{align}
and compute
\begin{align}
\nonumber
\nabla e_2
&=\left\{(\nabla \Phi )\mathcal R-\mathcal R(\nabla \Phi )\right\}v_{\mathrm p}+\left(\Phi \mathcal R-\mathcal R\Phi \right)\nabla v_{\mathrm p}
+2\sigma \nabla(|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p})\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\mathcal Rv_{\mathrm p}\right]\\
&\quad +2\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\overline{\nabla v_{\mathrm p}}\mathcal Rv_{\mathrm p}\right]
+2\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\mathcal R\nabla v_{\mathrm p}\right]. 
\label{e_7}
\end{align}
Since $\Phi \varphi\in H^{\nu}({\mathbb{R}})$ and $\varphi\nabla\Phi ,\varphi\Phi \nabla\Phi ,\Phi \nabla\varphi \in H^{\nu-1}({\mathbb{R}})$ by Lemma \ref{lemma_Holder}, the same argument as above based on Lemmas \ref{lemma_R} and \ref{lemma_v_p} implies
\begin{align*}
\|\mathcal R\Phi v_{\mathrm p}\|
&\le C_\nu   s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)},\\
\|\mathcal R(\nabla \Phi )v_{\mathrm p}\|
&\lesssim s^{(\nu-1)/2}\|e^{i\gamma \Phi }\varphi\nabla \Phi \|_{\dot H^{\nu-1}}
\le C_\nu   s^{(\nu-1)(\sigma-1/2)},\\
\|\mathcal R\nabla v_{\mathrm p}\|
&\lesssim s^{(\nu-1)/2}\left(s^{\sigma-1}\|e^{i\gamma \Phi }\varphi\nabla \Phi \|_{\dot H^{\nu-1}}+\|e^{i\gamma \Phi }\nabla \varphi\|_{\dot H^{\nu-1}}\right)
\le C_\nu   s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)-1/2},\\
\|\mathcal R\Phi \nabla v_{\mathrm p}\|
&\lesssim s^{(\nu-1)/2}\left(s^{\sigma-1}\|e^{i\gamma \Phi }\varphi|\nabla \Phi |^2\|_{\dot H^{\nu-1}}+\|e^{i\gamma \Phi }\Phi \nabla \varphi\|_{\dot H^{\nu-1}}\right)
\le C_\nu   s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)-1/2},\\
\|\mathcal Rv_{\mathrm p}\|_{L^\infty}
&\lesssim \|\mathcal Rv_{\mathrm p}\|^{1/2}\|\mathcal R\nabla v_{\mathrm p}\|^{1/2}\le C_\nu   s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)-1/4}.
\end{align*}
Hence
\begin{align*}
\|e_2\|
&\lesssim \|\Phi \|_{L^\infty}\|\mathcal Rv_{\mathrm p}\|+\|\mathcal R\Phi v_{\mathrm p}\|\le C_\nu   s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)},\\
\|\nabla e_2\|
&\lesssim \|\nabla\Phi \|\|\mathcal Rv_{\mathrm p}\|_{L^\infty}+\|\mathcal R(\nabla \Phi )v_{\mathrm p}\|\\
\nonumber
&\quad
+\|\Phi \|_{L^\infty}\|\mathcal R\nabla v_{\mathrm p}\|+\|\mathcal R\Phi \nabla v_{\mathrm p}\|+
\|\nabla(|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p})\varphi\| \|\mathcal Rv_{\mathrm p}\|_{L^\infty}\\
&\le C_\nu  (s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)-1/4}+s^{(\nu-1)(\sigma-1/2)}+s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)-1/2}+s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)+\sigma-5/4})\\
&\le C_\nu  s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)-1/2}
\end{align*}
provided $3/4<\sigma<1$. Then  
\hyperref[{proposition_e_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_e_1}}}} 
 for $e_2$ follows as in the case of $e_1$. 

Next we assume $\varphi\in H^2({\mathbb{R}})$ and prove  
\hyperref[{proposition_e_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_e_2}}}} 
. To obtain the desired bound for $\|e_1\|$,  
\hyperref[{e_5}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_5}}}} 
 with $\nu=2$ is sufficient. 
To deal with the term $|\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}e_1]$, we decompose $\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}e_1]$ into $\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\mathcal R_1v_{\mathrm p}]$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\frac{is}{2}\Delta v_{\mathrm p}]$, where $\mathcal R_1=\mathcal R-\frac{is}{2}\Delta$. For the first term,  
\hyperref[{e_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_2}}}} 
 with $f=\varphi$ implies
\begin{align}
v_{\mathrm p}
&=\nabla^{-4}e^{i\gamma\Phi}\left\{\gamma^4 \varphi(\nabla\Phi)^4-i\gamma^3\nabla\left( \varphi(\nabla\Phi)^3\right)\right\}\nonumber
 -\gamma^2\nabla^{-3}e^{i\gamma\Phi}\nabla\left(\varphi(\nabla\Phi)^2\right)\nonumber\\
&\quad +\nabla^{-2}e^{i\gamma\Phi}\left\{2i\gamma (\nabla \varphi)\nabla \Phi+i\gamma \varphi\Delta\Phi+\Delta \varphi\right\}.
\label{e_8}
\end{align}
Here all of $\varphi(\nabla\Phi)^4$, $\nabla( \varphi(\nabla\Phi)^3)$, $\nabla(\varphi(\nabla\Phi)^2)$, $(\nabla \varphi)\nabla \Phi$ and $\varphi\Delta\Phi$ belong to $L^2({\mathbb{R}})$. Indeed, since 
\begin{align}
\nabla \Phi&=2\sigma|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline \varphi \nabla \varphi],\nonumber\\
\Delta \Phi&=2\sigma(2\sigma-2)|\varphi|^{2\sigma-4}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline \varphi \nabla \varphi]\right)^2+2\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^2+\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline \varphi \Delta \varphi]\right),
\label{e_8_0}
\end{align}
we have 
\begin{align*}
\|\nabla \Phi\|_{L^\infty}&\lesssim  \|\varphi\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma-1}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^\infty}\le C_2,\\
\|\varphi\Delta\Phi\|&\lesssim \|\varphi\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma-1}\||\nabla\varphi|^2\|+\|\varphi\|_{L^\infty}^{2\sigma}\|\Delta\varphi\|\le C_2,\\
\|\varphi(\nabla\Phi)^4\|
&\lesssim \|\varphi\|\|\nabla \Phi\|_{L^\infty}^4\le C_2,\\
\|\nabla\left( \varphi(\nabla\Phi)^j\right)\|
&\lesssim \|\nabla\varphi\|\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^\infty}^j+\|\nabla \Phi\|_{L^\infty}^{j-1}\|\varphi\Delta\Phi\|\le C_2,\quad j=2,3.
\end{align*}
Moreover, 
$
\|\mathcal R_1\nabla^{-j}\|_{L^2\to L^2}\lesssim s^{j/2}$ for $ 2\le j\le 4$ by Lemma \ref{lemma_R}. 
Hence, these estimates and  
\hyperref[{e_8}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_8}}}} 
 imply
\begin{align}
                                                                                                          
\|\mathcal R_1v_{\mathrm p}\|
&\lesssim s^2\left(s^{4(\sigma-1)}\|\varphi(\nabla \Phi)^4\|+s^{3(\sigma-1)}\|\nabla(\varphi (\nabla \Phi)^3)\|\right)+s^{3/2+2(\sigma-1)}\|\nabla(\varphi (\nabla \Phi)^2)\|\nonumber\\
&\quad+s\left(s^{\sigma-1}(\|(\nabla\varphi)\nabla\Phi\|+\|\varphi\Delta\Phi\|)+\|\Delta\varphi\|\right)\nonumber\\
&\le C_1  s^{4\sigma-2}+C_2s^{3\sigma-1}+C_2 s^{2\sigma-1/2}+C_2s^\sigma
                                                              
                                                                                                                     
\le C_2 s^\sigma
\label{e_9}
\end{align}
provided $2/3\le\sigma\le1$. 
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             
Besides, the second term $\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\frac{is}{2}\Delta v_{\mathrm p}]$ can be computed explicitly as
\begin{align}
-\frac{s}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\Delta v_{\mathrm p}]
&=-\frac{s}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\left[-\gamma^2|\varphi|^2|\nabla\Phi |^2+2i\gamma\overline\varphi\nabla\varphi\nabla\Phi +i\gamma|\varphi|^2\Delta\Phi +\overline\varphi\Delta\varphi\right]\nonumber
\\
&=-s\gamma\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline\varphi\nabla\varphi\nabla\Phi]-\frac{s\gamma}{2}|\varphi|^2\Delta\Phi-\frac s2\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline \varphi\Delta\varphi],
\label{e_10}
\end{align}
which implies
\begin{align}
\label{e_11}
\left\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\frac{is}{2}\Delta v_{\mathrm p}\right]\right\|\le C_2   s^{\sigma}.
\end{align}
These estimates  
\hyperref[{e_9}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_9}}}} 
 and  
\hyperref[{e_11}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_11}}}} 
 imply
\begin{align}
 s^{\sigma/2-1}\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}e_1]\|\le C_2  s^{3\sigma/2-1}.                          
\label{e_12}
\end{align}
                                                                                                 
                                                                        
To estimate $\|\nabla e_1\|$, we use the formula  
\hyperref[{e_4}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_4}}}} 
 with $f=\varphi$ and Lemma \ref{lemma_R} to observe
\begin{align}
\|\nabla e_1\|
\le  C_1s^{1+3(\sigma-1)}+C_2s^{1+2(\sigma-1)}+C_2s^{1/2+\sigma-1}+C_2s^{1/2}
                                                                                                                                                  
                                                               
\label{e_13}
\end{align}
Now  
\hyperref[{proposition_e_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_e_2}}}} 
 follows from  
\hyperref[{e_5}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_5}}}} 
 with $s=2$,  
\hyperref[{e_12}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_12}}}} 
 and  
\hyperref[{e_13}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_13}}}} 
 since
$$\min\left\{2\sigma-1-\frac \sigma2+\frac\delta2,\ \frac{3\sigma}{2}-1,\ 2\sigma-1,\ \sigma-\frac12, \frac12\right\}=\frac{3\sigma}{2}-1$$
for $2/3<\sigma<1$ and $\delta>0$. Note that the decay rate $s^{\sigma}$ in  
\hyperref[{e_11}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_11}}}} 
 is optimal due to the formula  
\hyperref[{e_10}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_10}}}} 
, so is the rate $s^{3\sigma/2-1}$ in  
\hyperref[{proposition_e_2}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_e_2}}}} 
. 

Finally, we prove  
\hyperref[{proposition_e_3}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{proposition_e_3}}}} 
. 
To show the desired estimates for $e_2$ and $|\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}i e_2]$, we write
\begin{align*}
e_2
&=\left(\Phi \frac{is}{2}\Delta-\frac{is}{2}\Delta\Phi \right)v_{\mathrm p}
+\left(\Phi \mathcal R_1-\mathcal R_1\Phi \right)v_{\mathrm p}\\
&\quad -s\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits \left[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}} \Delta v_{\mathrm p}\right]
+2\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\mathcal R_1v_{\mathrm p}\right]\\
&=:\mathrm{I}_1+\mathrm{I}_2+\mathrm{I}_3+\mathrm{I}_4.
\end{align*}
The first term $\mathrm{I}_1=\left(\Phi \frac{is}{2}\Delta-\frac{is}{2}\Delta\Phi \right)v_{\mathrm p}$ is further  calculated as
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{I}_1
=-is(\nabla\Phi )\nabla v_{\mathrm p}-\frac{is}{2}(\Delta\Phi )v_{\mathrm p}
=se^{i\gamma \Phi }\left(\gamma \varphi|\nabla\Phi |^2-i(\nabla\Phi) \nabla \varphi\right)-\frac{is}{2}(\Delta\Phi )v_{\mathrm p},
\end{align*}
which implies 
$\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\mathrm{I}_1]=-s\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[i\overline{\varphi}\nabla\Phi \nabla \varphi]-\frac s2\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[i|\varphi|^2\Delta\Phi ]$. Hence
\begin{align}
\|\mathrm{I}_1\|
&\lesssim
s\left(s^{\sigma-1}\|\varphi|\nabla\Phi |^2\|+\|(\nabla\Phi )\nabla\varphi\|\right)
+s\|\varphi\Delta\Phi \|
\le C_2  s^{\sigma},\label{e_14}\\
\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\mathrm{I}_1]\|
&\lesssim s\left(\||\varphi|^\sigma|\nabla\Phi |\nabla\varphi\|+\||\varphi|^{\sigma+1}\Delta\Phi \|\right)
\le C_2  s.
\label{e_15}
\end{align}
By  
\hyperref[{e_10}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_10}}}} 
, the third term $\mathrm{I}_3=-s\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits \left[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}} \Delta v_{\mathrm p}\right]$ satisfies 
\begin{align}
\|\mathrm{I}_3\|\le C_2  s^\sigma,\quad \mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits [\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\mathrm{I}_3]=0.
\label{e_16}
\end{align}
For the fourth term $\mathrm{I}_4=2\sigma |\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\mathcal R_1v_{\mathrm p}\right]$,  
\hyperref[{e_9}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_9}}}} 
 implies
\begin{align}
\label{e_17}
\|\mathrm{I}_4\|\le C_2 s^\sigma,\quad \mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits [\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\mathrm{I}_4]=0.
\end{align}
To deal with the second term $\mathrm{I}_2=\left(\Phi \mathcal R_1-\mathcal R_1\Phi \right)v_{\mathrm p}$, we observe by  
\hyperref[{e_8}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_8}}}} 
 and  
\hyperref[{e_9}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_9}}}} 
 that
\begin{align*}
\|\mathcal R_1v_{\mathrm p}\|\le C_2(s^{4\sigma-2}+s^{3\sigma-1}+s^{2\sigma-1/2}+s)+\gamma \|\mathcal R_1\nabla^{-2}e^{i\gamma\Phi}\left(2 (\nabla\varphi)\nabla\Phi+\varphi\Delta\Phi\right)\|,
\end{align*}
where the term $C_2(s^{4\sigma-2}+s^{3\sigma-1}+s^{2\sigma-1/2}+s)$ is dominated by $C_2s$ provided $\sigma\ge 3/4$. On the other hand, by  
\hyperref[{e_8_0}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_8_0}}}} 
, the term $2 (\nabla\varphi)\nabla\Phi+\varphi\Delta\Phi$  can  be written in the form
\begin{align*}
&|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\left\{a_1\overline \varphi (\nabla\varphi)^2+a_2\varphi|\nabla\varphi|^2\right\}
+a_3|\varphi|^{2\sigma-4}\varphi^3(\overline{\nabla\varphi})^2
+a_4|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\varphi\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{\varphi}{\Delta\varphi}]
                                   
\end{align*}
with some constants $a_1,...a_4$. For short, we set 
$$
\mathrm{I}_{21}:=|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\left\{a_1\overline \varphi (\nabla\varphi)^2+a_2\varphi|\nabla\varphi|^2\right\}
+a_3|\varphi|^{2\sigma-4}\varphi^3(\overline{\nabla\varphi})^2,\quad 
\mathrm{I}_{22}:=a_4|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\varphi\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{\varphi}{\Delta\varphi}],
$$
which satisfy
$
|\mathrm{I}_{21}|\lesssim |\varphi|^{2\sigma-1}|\nabla\varphi|^2$ and $|\mathrm{I}_{22}|\lesssim |\varphi|^{2\sigma}|\Delta\varphi|
$,  and write
\begin{align*}
e^{i\gamma\Phi}\left(2 (\nabla\varphi)\nabla\Phi+\varphi\Delta\Phi\right)
=\mathrm{I}_{21}+(e^{i\gamma\Phi}-1)\mathrm{I}_{21}+e^{i\gamma\Phi}\mathrm{I}_{22}.
\end{align*}
Since $0<2-2\sigma<2\sigma-1<1$ if $3/4<\sigma<1$, by Lemmas \ref{lemma_R} and \ref{lemma_Holder} with $(\alpha,\beta)=(2\sigma-1,2-2\sigma)$, the first term $\mathrm{I}_{21}$ satisfies
\begin{align}
\gamma \|\mathcal R_1\nabla^{-2}\mathrm{I}_{21}\|
&\lesssim s^{\sigma-1+(2-\sigma)}\|\mathrm{I}_{21}\|_{\dot H^{2-2\sigma}}\nonumber\\
&\le s\|\varphi\|_{H^{3/2}}^{2\sigma-1}\left(\||\nabla \varphi|^2\|_{H^{2-2\sigma}}+\|(\nabla\varphi)^2\|_{H^{2-2\sigma}}\right)
\le C_2s.
\label{e_18}
\end{align}
For the term $(e^{i\gamma\Phi}-1)\mathrm{I}_{21}$, thanks to the bound $|e^{i\gamma\Phi}-1|\lesssim \gamma\Phi$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
|\nabla\{(e^{i\gamma\Phi}-1)\mathrm{I}_{21}\}|
\lesssim \gamma\Phi|\nabla \mathrm{I}_{21}|+\gamma |\nabla \Phi||\mathrm{I}_{21}|
\lesssim s^{\sigma-1}\left(|\varphi|^{4\sigma-1}|\nabla\varphi||\Delta\varphi|+|\varphi|^{4\sigma-2}|\nabla\varphi|^3\right)
\end{align*}
and hence
\begin{align}
\gamma \|\mathcal R_1\nabla^{-2}(e^{i\gamma\Phi}-1)\mathrm{I}_{21}\|
\lesssim s^{\sigma-1+3/2}\|\nabla\{(e^{i\gamma\Phi}-1)\mathrm{I}_{21}\}\|\le C_2 s^{2\sigma-1/2}\le C_2s
\label{e_19}
\end{align}
whenever $\sigma\ge 3/4$. 
The term $e^{i\gamma\Phi}\mathrm{I}_{22}$ is estimated by using Lemma  \ref{lemma_R} and \ref{lemma_v_p} as 
\begin{align}
\gamma \|\mathcal R_1\nabla^{-2}e^{i\gamma\Phi}\mathrm{I}_{22}\|\lesssim s^{\sigma-1+\nu/2}\|e^{i\gamma\Phi}\mathrm{I}_{22}\|_{\dot H^{\nu-2}}\le                                             
C_\nu s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)+1-\sigma}
\label{e_20}
\end{align}
for $2\le \nu\le 3$. In particular, if we choose $\nu=\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1/2}$, which belongs to $[2,3]$ if $3/4\le \sigma\le1$, then 
$$
\gamma \|\mathcal R_1\nabla^{-2}e^{i\gamma\Phi}\mathrm{I}_{22}\|\le C_{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1/2}}s.$$
By  
\hyperref[{e_18}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_18}}}} 
-- 
\hyperref[{e_20}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_20}}}} 
, we obtain
\begin{align}
\|\mathcal R_1v_{\mathrm p}\|\le C_{\nu}s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)+1-\sigma},\quad 2\le \nu\le \frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1/2}.
\label{e_21}
\end{align}
Since $\Phi\varphi\in H^2({\mathbb{R}})$, the same argument also provides the same bound for $\|\mathcal R_1\Phi v_{\mathrm p}\|$. Hence
\begin{align}
\|\mathrm{I}_2\|\le C_{\nu}s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)+1-\sigma},\quad 2\le \nu\le \frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1/2}.
\label{e_22}
\end{align}
It follows from  
\hyperref[{e_14}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_14}}}} 
-- 
\hyperref[{e_17}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_17}}}} 
 and  
\hyperref[{e_21}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_21}}}} 
 that, for $2\le \nu\le {\sigma}/{(\sigma-1/2)}$, 
\begin{align}
s^{-\sigma/2+\delta/2}\|e_2\|+s^{\sigma/2-1}\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}e_2]\|\le C_\nu s^{\nu(\sigma-1/2)-\sigma/2}. 
\label{e_23}
\end{align}

It remains to deal with $\nabla e_2$ given by the formula  
\hyperref[{e_7}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_7}}}} 
. For the term $\left\{(\nabla \Phi )\mathcal R-\mathcal R(\nabla \Phi )\right\}v_{\mathrm p}$ in  
\hyperref[{e_7}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_7}}}} 
, we use  
\hyperref[{e_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_1}}}} 
 with $f=\varphi,\nabla\Phi$ to observe
\begin{align}
&\|\left\{(\nabla \Phi )\mathcal R-\mathcal R(\nabla \Phi )\right\}v_{\mathrm p}\|\nonumber\\
&\lesssim \|\nabla \Phi\|_{L^\infty}\|\mathcal R\nabla^{-1}\|_{\dot H^1\to L^2}\left(s^{\sigma-1}e^{i\gamma \Phi}\varphi\nabla \Phi\|_{\dot H^1}+\|e^{i\gamma \Phi}\nabla\varphi\|_{\dot H^1}\right)\nonumber\\
&\quad +s^{\sigma-1}\|\mathcal R\nabla^{-1}\|_{\dot H^1\to L^2}\|e^{i\gamma \Phi}\varphi(\nabla \Phi)^2\|_{\dot H^1}+\|\mathcal R\nabla^{-1}\|_{L^2\to L^2}\|e^{i\gamma \Phi}\nabla(\varphi\nabla\Phi)\|
\nonumber\\
&\le C_2 (s^{1+2(\sigma-1)}+s^{1+\sigma-1}+s^{1+2(\sigma-1)}+s^{1/2})
\le C_2s^{\sigma/2}
\label{e_24}
\end{align}
provided $\sigma\ge 2/3$. 
We next compute 
\begin{align*}
\left(\Phi \mathcal R-\mathcal R\Phi \right)\nabla v_{\mathrm p}
=\left(\Phi \mathcal R-\mathcal R\Phi \right)e^{i\gamma\Phi }(i\gamma \varphi\nabla\Phi +\nabla\varphi),
\end{align*}
where the term $\left(\Phi \mathcal R-\mathcal R\Phi \right)e^{i\gamma\Phi }\nabla\varphi$ satisfies the same bound as  
\hyperref[{e_24}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_24}}}} 
. Moreover, the term $i\gamma \left(\Phi \mathcal R-\mathcal R\Phi \right)e^{i\gamma\Phi }\varphi\nabla\Phi $ is further calculated as
\begin{align*}
&i\gamma \Phi \mathcal R(\nabla+1)^{-1}(\nabla+1)e^{i\gamma\Phi }\varphi\nabla\Phi 
-i\gamma \mathcal R(\nabla+1)^{-1}(\nabla+1)\Phi e^{i\gamma\Phi }\varphi\nabla\Phi \\
&=-\gamma^2 \left(\Phi \mathcal R(\nabla+1)^{-1}-\mathcal R(\nabla+1)^{-1}\Phi \right)e^{i\gamma\Phi } \varphi|\nabla\Phi |^2\\
&\quad +i\gamma \Phi \mathcal R(\nabla+1)^{-1}e^{i\gamma\Phi }(\nabla+1)(\varphi\nabla\Phi )
-i\gamma \mathcal R(\nabla+1)^{-1}e^{i\gamma\Phi }(\nabla+1)(\varphi\Phi \nabla\Phi )\\
&=\mathrm{II}_1+\mathrm{II}_2+\mathrm{II}_3,
\end{align*}
where we set for short
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{II}_1&=-\gamma^2 \left(\Phi \frac{is}{2}\Delta(\nabla+1)^{-1}-\frac{is}{2}\Delta(\nabla+1)^{-1}\Phi \right)e^{i\gamma\Phi } \varphi|\nabla\Phi |^2,\\
\mathrm{II}_2&=-\gamma^2 \left(\Phi \mathcal R_1(\nabla+1)^{-1}-\mathcal R_1(\nabla+1)^{-1}\Phi \right)e^{i\gamma\Phi } \varphi|\nabla\Phi |^2,\\
\mathrm{II}_3&=i\gamma \Phi \mathcal R(\nabla+1)^{-1}e^{i\gamma\Phi }(\nabla+1)(\varphi\nabla\Phi )
-i\gamma \mathcal R(\nabla+1)^{-1}e^{i\gamma\Phi }(\nabla+1)(\varphi\Phi \nabla\Phi ).
\end{align*}
Since 
$$
\Delta(\nabla+1)^{-1}=\nabla(\nabla+1-1)(\nabla+1)^{-1}=\nabla-\nabla(\nabla+1)^{-1}=\nabla-1+(\nabla+1)^{-1},
$$
we have, for any $f\in L^2({\mathbb{R}})$ and $g\in H^1({\mathbb{R}})$ satisfying $\nabla g\in L^\infty({\mathbb{R}})$,                                                                                                  
\begin{align}
\left\{g\Delta(\nabla+1)^{-1}-\Delta(\nabla+1)^{-1}g\right\}f
=-(\nabla g)f+[g,(\nabla+1)^{-1}]f.
                                                     
\label{e_25}
                                                                                  
                                                                                                     
                                                               
\end{align}
Since $\|[g,(\nabla+1)^{-1}]\|_{L^2\to L^2}\le 2\|g\|_{L^\infty}$, applying this formula with $g=\Phi$ to $\mathrm{II}_1$ shows
\begin{align}
\|\mathrm{II}_1\|\lesssim s^{2\sigma-1}\left(\|\nabla\Phi \|_{L^\infty}+\|\Phi \|_{L^\infty}\right)\|e^{i\gamma\Phi } \varphi|\nabla\Phi |^2\|\le C_2 s^{2\sigma-1}.
\label{e_26}
\end{align}
For the term $\mathrm{II}_2$, since $|\varphi||\nabla\Phi |^2\lesssim |\varphi|^{4\sigma-1}|\nabla\varphi|^2$ and thus $\varphi|\nabla\Phi |^2\in H^1({\mathbb{R}})$, we can rewrite it as
\begin{align}
\mathrm{II}_2
&=-\gamma^2 \Phi \mathcal R_1(\nabla+1)^{-2}e^{i\gamma\Phi }\left\{i\gamma \varphi|\nabla\Phi |^3+(\nabla+1)(\varphi|\nabla\Phi |^2)\right\}\nonumber\\
&\quad 
+\gamma^2 \mathcal R_1(\nabla+1)^{-2}e^{i\gamma\Phi }\left\{i\gamma \Phi \varphi|\nabla\Phi |^3+(\nabla+1)(\Phi \varphi|\nabla\Phi |^2)\right\}. 
\label{e_27}\end{align}
Then Lemmas \ref{lemma_R} and \ref{lemma_v_p} imply
\begin{align*}
\|\mathrm{II}_2\|
&\lesssim 
s^{3(\sigma-1)+3/2}\left(\|e^{i\gamma\Phi }\varphi|\nabla\Phi |^3\|_{\dot H^1}+\|e^{i\gamma\Phi }\Phi \varphi|\nabla\Phi |^3\|_{\dot H^1}\right)\nonumber\\
&\quad +s^{2(\sigma-1)+1}\left(\|e^{i\gamma\Phi }(\nabla+1)(\varphi|\nabla\Phi |^2)\|+\|e^{i\gamma\Phi }(\nabla+1)(\Phi \varphi|\nabla\Phi |^2)\|\right)\nonumber\\
&\le C_2(s^{4\sigma-5/2}+s^{2\sigma-1}).
                                                      
                 
\end{align*}
To deal with the term $\mathrm{II}_3$, we observe by  
\hyperref[{e_8_0}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{e_8_0}}}} 
 that $(\nabla+1)(\varphi\nabla\Phi )$ is a linear combination of 
\begin{align*}
&|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\overline\varphi(\nabla\varphi)^2,\quad 
|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\varphi|\nabla\varphi|^2,\quad 
|\varphi|^{2\sigma-4}\varphi^3(\overline{\nabla\varphi})^2,\\
&|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}\varphi\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline\varphi\Delta\varphi],\quad

&\quad +2 (\sigma+1)\sigma \mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[ W\overline w] \int_0^1 \left(|w_\theta|^{2\sigma-2}w_{\theta}-|v_{\mathrm p}|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}\right)d\theta
\\
                                                                                                                                                       
&\quad+ \sigma(\sigma-1) \overline{Ww} \int_0^1 \left(|w_\theta|^{2\sigma-4}w_\theta^3-|v_{\mathrm p}|^{2\sigma-4}v_{\mathrm p}^3\right)d\theta. 
\end{align*}
Since $\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[z_1z_2]=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[z_1]\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[z_2]+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[z_1]\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[z_2]$, we have
\begin{align*}
|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\widetilde G(W)]|\lesssim |\varphi|^{2\sigma-1}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}W]|w|+|W|\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}w]\right)+|\varphi||W||w|^{2\sigma}.
\end{align*}
The same argument as above then shows
$$
s^{\frac\sigma2-1}\||\varphi|^{\sigma-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits[\overline{v_{\mathrm p}}\widetilde G(W)]\|
\lesssim s^{\frac\sigma4+\beta-}\widetilde Q[W](s)+s^{\frac{\sigma^2}{2}+\sigma-1+2\sigma\beta-}\widetilde Q[W](s).
$$
Finally, the desired estimate for $\|\nabla (e^{-i\gamma \Phi}\widetilde G(W))\|$ is obtained by completely the same argument as that for  
\hyperref[{p_u_p_4}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{p_u_p_4}}}} 
. 
Indeed, $\nabla\{e^{-i\gamma \Phi}\overline W(|v_1|^{2\sigma-2}v_1^2-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}^2)$ is a linear combination of the same operators mentioned above as that for $\nabla (\overline{\widetilde W}(|\widetilde W_{[\theta]}|^{2\sigma-2}\widetilde W_{[\theta]}^2-|\widetilde v_1|^{2\sigma-2}\widetilde v_1^2))$ with $\widetilde W_{[\theta]}$ replaced by $\varphi$. Thus, the same argument as above yields
\begin{align*}
&\|\nabla\{e^{-i\gamma \Phi}\overline W(|v_1|^{2\sigma-2}v_1^2-|\varphi|^{2\sigma-2}v_{\mathrm p}^2)\|\\

\end{align*}
where, in order to bound $\| W|v_1-v_{\mathrm p}|^{2\sigma-1}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\nu_1-1}}}$ by $s^{\frac{\sigma^2}{2}+\frac{(\nu_1-1)\sigma}{2}+(2\sigma-1)\beta-}\widetilde Q[W](s)$, we interpolate between the following two cases $\nu_1=1,3/2$ obtained by  
\hyperref[{theorem_v_1}]{\textup{\tagform@{\!\!\ref*{theorem_v_1}}}}

This completes the proof of
