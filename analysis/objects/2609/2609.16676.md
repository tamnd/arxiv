Assume (a). The Laplace transform is defined outside $\mathcal W(h_\phi)^\dagger$ by \cite{beauduin2026b}
    \begin{equation}
        \Phi(p) = \int_0^{\infty e^{i\theta}} \phi(s) e^{-ps} \odif s, \quad \operatorname{Re}(p e^{i\theta}) > h_\phi(\theta), \quad \theta\in \bra{-\frac\pi2,\frac\pi2}.
    \end{equation}
    To obtain decay towards $\operatorname{Re} p=-\infty$, fix $\gamma \in (c,0)$ and set
    \[
    \Phi_\gamma(p) := \Phi(p)-\int_0^\gamma\phi(s)e^{-ps}\odif s.
    \]
    Shifting the integration ray gives, for $\operatorname{Re}(\pm ip)>h_\phi(\pm\frac\pi2)$,
    \[
    \Phi_\gamma(p) = \int_\gamma^{\gamma\pm i\infty}\phi(s)e^{-ps}\odif s.
    \]
    On each smaller closed half-plane, this yields
    \[
    |\Phi_\gamma(p)| \le \int_0^\infty |\phi(\gamma\pm it)|e^{-\operatorname{Re}(p(\gamma\pm it))}\odif t \ll e^{-\gamma\operatorname{Re} p}.
    \]
    The correction is entire and tends to zero as $\operatorname{Re} p\IfValueTF{-}{
    \IfValueTF{#2}{\xrightarrow[#2]{-}}{\xrightarrow{-}}
  }{
    \IfValueTF{#2}{\xrightarrow[#2]{}}{\rightarrow}
  }
\infty$, so it changes neither the finite singularities nor those towards $-\infty$.

    Work locally near any $z\notin\exp(-K)\cup\{0\}$ and choose a branch of $\log$. The contour theorem of \cite{beauduin2026b} gives a contour $\Gamma$ around $K$ that leaves the poles of $(1-ze^p)^{-1}$ outside for all $z$ in this neighborhood. This construction also covers disconnected and purely infinite sets $K$.

    The unbounded parts of $\Gamma$ lie in half-planes where the preceding estimate holds. On the compact remainder, the estimate follows by enlarging the constant. Laplace inversion gives the absolutely convergent integral
    \begin{equation}
        \phi(s) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_{\Gamma}\Phi_\gamma(p)e^{ps}\odif p,
        \quad \operatorname{Re} s>\gamma,
    \end{equation}
    which we sum at $s=n$ with weights $z^n$ for small $|z|$, using Fubini's theorem. Analytic continuation and the substitution $p\mapsto p+\log z$ then give
    \[
    f(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_{\Gamma+\log z}\frac{\Phi_\gamma(p-\log z)}{1-e^p}\odif p.
    \]
    Where two such formulas apply, deforming one contour into the other without crossing singularities shows that they give the same value of $f(z)$. Together with the original power series near $0$, they give the continuation to $\mathbb C\setminus\exp(-K)$.

    It remains to bound $f$ at infinity. The estimate for $\Phi_\gamma$ gives
    \[
    |f(z)|\ll |z|^\gamma\oint\limits_{\Gamma+\log z}\frac{e^{-\gamma\operatorname{Re} p}}{|1-e^p|}\abs{\odif p}.
    \]
    Fix a closed set of admissible directions. As $z$ tends to infinity in these directions, the contours can be chosen uniformly so that their translates by $\log z$ stay separated from $2\pi i\mathbb Z$. The denominator is therefore bounded away from zero when $\abs{\operatorname{Re} p}\le1$.

    On the remaining parts, use $x=\operatorname{Re} p$ with $\abs{\odif p}\ll\abs{\odif x}$. The integrand is bounded by a constant times $e^{-\gamma x}$ for $x\le-1$ and $e^{-(\gamma+1)x}$ for $x\ge1$. Both bounds are integrable since $-1<\gamma<0$. Thus $f(z)=O(|z|^\gamma)$ for every $\gamma\in(c,0)$, proving (b).

    \medskip

    Conversely, assume (b) and define
    \[
    \phi(s) := \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_\Gamma f(e^{-u}) e^{us} \odif u,
    \]
    where $\Gamma$ encircles $K$ in a horizontal strip of width less than $2\pi$, with horizontal tails towards $\operatorname{Re} u=-\infty$. The growth assumption on $f$ gives, for every $\gamma \in (c, 0)$,
    \begin{align*}
        |\phi(s)|
        &\le \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint\limits_\Gamma |f(e^{-u})|e^{\operatorname{Re}(us)}\abs{\odif u} \\
        &\le \frac{C_\gamma}{2\pi}\oint\limits_\Gamma
        e^{-\gamma\operatorname{Re} u}e^{\operatorname{Re} u\operatorname{Re} s-\operatorname{Im} u\operatorname{Im} s}\abs{\odif u}.
    \end{align*}
    Since $\operatorname{Im} u$ is bounded on $\Gamma$, the integral converges absolutely and locally uniformly for $\operatorname{Re} s>\gamma$ for every $\gamma > c$. The same estimate gives an exponential bound on every smaller half-plane, which shows that $\phi$ is analytic and of exponential type for $\operatorname{Re} s>c$.

    For $\operatorname{Re} p$ sufficiently large, Fubini's theorem gives
    \[
    \Phi(p) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_\Gamma\frac{f(e^{-u})}{p-u}\odif u,
    \]
    which gives the continuation of $\Phi$ to $\bar\mathbb C\setminus K$ after deforming $\Gamma$ towards $K$.
    
    To verify interpolation, use $z=e^{-u}$, which is injective on the strip containing $\Gamma$. This gives
    \[
    \phi(s) = -\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\limits_{\partial U}\frac{f(z)}{z^{s+1}}\odif z,
    \]
    where $U$ is a neighborhood of $\exp(-K)$ whose boundary tails are radial and whose closure avoids $0$. Choose $\epsilon>0$ so that $\mathbb D_\epsilon$ does not intersect $U$. For all sufficiently large $R$ and every $n\in\mathbb N$, Cauchy's integral formula gives
    \[
    \oint\limits_{\partial(U\cup\mathbb D_R)}\frac{f(z)}{z^{n+1}}\odif z = \oint\limits_{\partial\mathbb D_\epsilon}\frac{f(z)}{z^{n+1}}\odif z+\oint\limits_{\partial U}\frac{f(z)}{z^{n+1}}\odif z = 2\pi i(c_n-\phi(n)),
    \]
    whose left-hand side tends to zero. Indeed, for any $\gamma\in(c,0)$,
    \begin{align*}
        \abs{\,\oint\limits_{\partial(U\cup\mathbb D_R)}\frac{f(z)}{z^{n+1}}\odif z}
        &\le \oint\limits_{\partial(U\cup\mathbb D_R)}\frac{|f(z)|}{|z|^{n+1}}\abs{\odif z} \\
        &\ll R^{\gamma-n}+\int_R^\infty r^{\gamma-n-1}\odif r \\
        &\ll R^{\gamma-n}\IfValueTF{_}{
    \IfValueTF{#2}{\xrightarrow[#2]{_}}{\xrightarrow{_}}
  }{
    \IfValueTF{#2}{\xrightarrow[#2]{}}{\rightarrow}
  }
{R\IfValueTF{\infty}{
    \IfValueTF{#2}{\xrightarrow[#2]{\infty}}{\xrightarrow{\infty}}
  }{
    \IfValueTF{#2}{\xrightarrow[#2]{}}{\rightarrow}
  }
} 0.
    \end{align*}
    Hence $\phi(n)=c_n$.
