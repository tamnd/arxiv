<!-- thma-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:A}
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item The space \(\operatorname{BARI}_{\operatorname{swap},\underline{\mathrm{il}}}\) is a Lie algebra with the uri bracket, i.e.\ we have
\[
 \operatorname{uri}(\operatorname{BARI}_{\operatorname{swap},\underline{\mathrm{il}}},\operatorname{BARI}_{\operatorname{swap},\underline{\mathrm{il}}})\subseteq\operatorname{BARI}_{\operatorname{swap},\underline{\mathrm{il}}}.
\]
\item The space \(\mathfrak B\) is a weight-graded Lie subalgebra of \(\operatorname{BARI}_{\operatorname{swap},\underline{\mathrm{il}}}\).
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end thma-1 -->
<!-- thma-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:B}
The space \(\mathfrak{bm}_0\) is a weight-graded Lie algebra under the bracket \(\{\ ,\ \}_{\mathrm b}\), and
\[
 \Phi:(\mathfrak{bm}_0,\{\ ,\ \}_{\mathrm b})
 \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}(\mathfrak B,\operatorname{uri})
\]
is an isomorphism of weight-graded Lie algebras.
<!-- end thma-2 -->
<!-- dfn-1: body_md -->
\label{def:uri}
For \(a,b\in\operatorname{BARI}^{\mathrm{loc}}\), set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uri}
 \operatorname{uri}(a,b)=T\bigl(\operatorname{ari}(T^{-1}a,T^{-1}b)\bigr).
\end{equation}
<!-- end dfn-1 -->
<!-- dfn-2: body_md -->
\label{def:tilde}
For a normalized bimould \(A\), define \(\widetilde A_0=1\) and, for \(r\geq1\),
\begin{align}\label{eq:tilde-recursion}
 \widetilde A_r={}&-A_r
 -\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}A^{(0)}\langle1,i\rangle
 \widetilde A^{(0)}\langle i+1,r\rangle\notag\\
 &+\sum_{i=1}^r
 \frac{A^{(0)}\langle1,i-1\rangle
 \widetilde A^{(0)}\langle i+1,r\rangle
 -A^{(X_i)}\langle1,i-1\rangle
 \widetilde A^{(X_i)}\langle i+1,r\rangle}{X_i}.
\end{align}
<!-- end dfn-2 -->
<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:tilde-regular}
The recursion \eqref{eq:tilde-recursion} defines a unique normalized power-series bimould.  If \(A\) is polynomial, then \(\widetilde A\) is polynomial.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
For fixed \(i\), put
\[
 F_i(t)=A^{(t)}\langle1,i-1\rangle
 \widetilde A^{(t)}\langle i+1,r\rangle.
\]
The two blocks omit the index \(i\), so \(X_i\) enters \(F_i(t)\) only through \(t\), and therefore \(F_i(0)-F_i(X_i)\) is divisible by \(X_i\).  Every quotient in \eqref{eq:tilde-recursion} is therefore a formal power series.  Induction on the depth proves existence and uniqueness.  The same argument in the polynomial ring proves polynomiality.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- dfn-3: body_md -->
\label{def:diamond}
For normalized power-series bimoulds \(A,B\), define
\begin{align}\label{eq:diamond-direct}
 (A\diamond B)_r
 \genfrac{(}{)}{0pt}{}{X_1,\ldots,X_r}{Y_1,\ldots,Y_r}
 :={}&\sum_{\substack{0\leq j\leq r\\
 0=r_0<n_1\leq r_1<\cdots<n_j\leq r_j\leq r}}
 Q^A_{\boldsymbol n,\boldsymbol r}
 B_j\genfrac{(}{)}{0pt}{}{X_{n_1},\ldots,X_{n_j}}{Y_{1:r_1},Y_{r_1+1:r_2},\ldots,Y_{r_{j-1}+1:r_j}},
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diamond-left-factor}
 Q^A_{\boldsymbol n,\boldsymbol r}
 =\left(\prod_{i=1}^j
 A^{(X_{n_i})}\langle r_{i-1}+1,n_i-1\rangle
 \widetilde A^{(X_{n_i})}\langle n_i+1,r_i\rangle\right)
 A^{(0)}\langle r_j+1,r\rangle.
\end{equation}
For \(j=0\), the displayed lists and product are empty and \(r_j=r_0=0\).
<!-- end dfn-3 -->
<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:translation}
For normalized power-series bimoulds,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diamond-gaxit}
 A\diamond B=\operatorname{mu}\bigl(\operatorname{gaxit}_{A,\widetilde A}(B),A\bigr).
\end{equation}
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Expand the right-hand side using \eqref{eq:mu} and \eqref{eq:gaxit}.  Write \(n_i=p_i\) and \(r_i=q_i\) for the marked positions and their endpoints.  The final split in \(\operatorname{mu}\) gives the factor \(A^{(0)}\langle r_j+1,r\rangle\).  The remaining factors and lower arguments are exactly those in \eqref{eq:diamond-direct} and \eqref{eq:diamond-left-factor}.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:tilde-graph}
Let \(A\) be normalized, either as a power-series bimould or as a localized bimould.  A bimould \(K\in\operatorname{BIMU}^{\mathrm{loc}}\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilde-graph}
 \operatorname{gaxit}_{A,K}(\jmath)=\operatorname{mu}(A,\jmath,K)
\end{equation}
if and only if \(K=\widetilde A\).
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Only decompositions with one marked letter contribute to \(\operatorname{gaxit}_{A,K}(V)\), and
\[
 \operatorname{gaxit}_{A,K}(V)(w_1\cdots w_n)
 =\sum_{i=1}^n
 \frac{A^{(X_i)}\langle1,i-1\rangle
 K^{(X_i)}\langle i+1,n\rangle}{X_i}.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
 \operatorname{mu}(A,\jmath,K)(w_1\cdots w_n)
 =\sum_{i=0}^nA^{(0)}\langle1,i\rangle
 K^{(0)}\langle i+1,n\rangle
 -\sum_{i=1}^nA^{(0)}\langle1,i-1\rangle
 \frac{K^{(0)}\langle i+1,n\rangle}{X_i}.
\]
Substituting these formulas in \eqref{eq:tilde-graph} and solving for \(K_n\) gives \eqref{eq:tilde-recursion}.  Uniqueness finishes the proof.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:diamond-associative}
The operation \(\diamond\) is associative on normalized power-series bimoulds and has unit \(\mathbf 1\).
<!-- end thm-2 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
The unit follows directly from \eqref{eq:diamond-direct}.  Put \(\Gamma(A)=(A,\widetilde A)\).  Multiplicativity and \eqref{eq:gaxi}, together with \cref{lem:translation,lem:tilde-graph}, give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:graph-product}
 \operatorname{gaxi}(\Gamma(A),\Gamma(B))=\Gamma(B\diamond A).
\end{equation}
To see this, we apply the operator corresponding to the left-hand pair to \(\jmath\) and get
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{gaxit}_{B,\widetilde B}
 \bigl(\operatorname{gaxit}_{A,\widetilde A}(\jmath)\bigr)
 &=\operatorname{gaxit}_{B,\widetilde B}\bigl(\operatorname{mu}(A,\jmath,\widetilde A)\bigr)\\
 &=\operatorname{mu}\bigl(\operatorname{gaxit}_{B,\widetilde B}(A),
 \operatorname{mu}(B,\jmath,\widetilde B),
 \operatorname{gaxit}_{B,\widetilde B}(\widetilde A)\bigr).
\end{align*}
Write the GAXI product as \((C,L)\).  Its first component is \(C=B\diamond A\), and the last display is \(\operatorname{mu}(C,\jmath,L)\).  By \cref{lem:tilde-graph}, we have \(L=\widetilde C\), which proves \eqref{eq:graph-product}.  Associativity now follows from associativity of GAXI and injectivity of \(\Gamma\).
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:diamond-conjugation}
For a normalized \(A\) with poles, put \(Q=T^{-1}(A)\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:garit-conjugation}
 T\circ\operatorname{garit}_Q\circ T^{-1}=\operatorname{gaxit}_{A,\widetilde A}.
\end{equation}
Therefore, for normalized power-series bimoulds \(A,B\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diamond-gari}
 A\diamond B=T\bigl(\operatorname{gari}(T^{-1}B,T^{-1}A)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The order on the right is reversed.
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
The GAXI composition law shows that the operator on the left of \eqref{eq:garit-conjugation} is \(\operatorname{gaxit}_{A,K}\), where
\[
 K=\operatorname{mu}\bigl(\operatorname{pic},\operatorname{invmu}(A),T(\operatorname{garit}_Q(\operatorname{poc}))\bigr).
\]
Using \eqref{eq:flexion-unit-identities}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{gaxit}_{A,K}(\jmath)
 &=T\bigl(\operatorname{garit}_Q(T^{-1}(\jmath))\bigr)
 =T\bigl(\operatorname{garit}_Q(\operatorname{poc})\bigr)\\
 &=\operatorname{mu}\bigl(A,\operatorname{invmu}(A),T(\operatorname{garit}_Q(\operatorname{poc}))\bigr)
 =\operatorname{mu}(A,\jmath,K).
\end{align*}
The last equality uses \(\operatorname{mu}(\jmath,\operatorname{pic})=\mathbf 1\).  By \cref{lem:tilde-graph}, we have \(K=\widetilde A\), which proves \eqref{eq:garit-conjugation}.  Together with \cref{lem:translation}, this gives
\begin{align*}
 A\diamond B
 &=\operatorname{mu}\bigl(T(\operatorname{garit}_Q(T^{-1}B)),T(Q)\bigr)\\
 &=T\bigl(\operatorname{mu}(\operatorname{garit}_Q(T^{-1}B),Q)\bigr),
\end{align*}
which is \eqref{eq:diamond-gari}.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:diamond-symmetril}
If \(A\) and \(B\) are normalized symmetril bimoulds, then \(A\diamond B\) is symmetril.
<!-- end thm-3 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
The symmetral--symmetril transport for \(T\) and its inverse is \cite{Ko}*{Theorem~3.24}.  Normalized symmetral bimoulds form a GARI subgroup by \cite{Ko}*{Theorem~A.7}.  The proofs of both results use only finitely many shuffle and flexion identities in each depth.  These identities remain valid after base change to the localization \eqref{eq:poles}.  Therefore, the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:diamond-gari} is symmetril.  The formula \eqref{eq:diamond-direct} shows that if $A$ and $B$ are power-series bimoulds, then \(A\diamond B\) is a power-series bimould as well.
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- thm-4: body_md -->
\label{thm:regularity}
The uri bracket maps power-series bimoulds to power-series bimoulds in every depth and componentwise polynomial bimoulds to componentwise polynomial bimoulds, and it preserves \(\operatorname{BARI}^{\mathrm{pol}}\).
<!-- end thm-4 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Let
\[
 S=R[\varepsilon,\eta]/(\varepsilon^2,\eta^2),\qquad
 A=\mathbf 1+\varepsilon a,\qquad B=\mathbf 1+\eta b.
\]
The operation \(\diamond\) is defined over \(S\).  The mixed coefficients of \(A\diamond B\) and \(B\diamond A\) are power series, and they are polynomials whenever the components of \(a\) and \(b\) are polynomials.

Equation \eqref{eq:diamond-gari} and the definition \eqref{eq:gari-tangent} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-uri}

 =-T\bigl(\operatorname{ari}(T^{-1}a,T^{-1}b)\bigr)
 =-\operatorname{uri}(a,b).
\end{equation}
This proves power-series and componentwise polynomial closure.

For the last statement, give a monomial in depth \(r\) and ordinary degree \(d\) the weight \(r+d\). The weight is therefore preserved, and the mixed coefficient has weight equal to the sum of the input weights.  In weight \(k\), a depth- \(r\) component has ordinary degree \(k-r\) and hence vanishes for \(r>k\).  Every finite-depth polynomial bimould is a finite sum of weight-homogeneous bimoulds.  Its bracket therefore has finite depth support.

Fix an odd $k\geq3$ and put $h=\{f_k,f_1\}_{\mathrm b}$.  The construction of $\theta$ in \cite{Bu1}*{Theorem~4.28} splits the support of $f_k$ into words in $b_0,b_1$ and words containing no $b_0$.  The first part has zero bracket with $b_1$ by \cite{Bu1}*{Lemma~3.15(i)}, while the defining derivation formula preserves the subspace of words containing no $b_0$.  Hence $h$ contains no $b_0$.

By \cref{thm:B}, $h\in\mathfrak{bm}_0$, so it is primitive and its projection is $\tau$-invariant.  Since $h$ contains no $b_0$, $\Pi_0(h)=h$.  Formula \eqref{eq:balanced-tau} sends every word without $b_0$ other than a power of $b_1$ to a word containing $b_0$.  So a homogeneous $\tau$-invariant element of weight $k+1$ containing no $b_0$ is a multiple of $b_1^{k+1}$.  Since $b_1^{k+1}$ is not primitive, $h=0$.  This is the argument of \cite{Bu1}*{Proposition~4.56}, whose closure hypothesis is now provided by \cref{thm:B}.  Finally, \cref{lem:b-bracket-uri-comparison} gives \(\operatorname{uri}(\xi_1,\xi_k)=0\).  Equation \eqref{eq:differentiated-eisenstein-relations} follows because $\delta$ is a derivation of the uri bracket.
