<!-- maintheorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:complex-main}
There exist a complex projective K3 surface \(X\) and an automorphism
\(T \colon X \to X\) with the following properties.
The automorphism \(T\) has positive topological entropy and a non-empty
Fatou set. More precisely, \(\operatorname{Fat}(T)\) contains a \(T\)-invariant biannulus.
On this domain, \(T\) is holomorphically conjugate to a Diophantine rotation.
<!-- end maintheorem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label[definition]{dfn:diophantine-condition}
Let \(\gamma>0\) and \(\tau\geq2\).
The set \(\operatorname{DC}(\gamma,\tau)\subset{\mathbb R}^2\) consists of the
vectors \(\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\) satisfying the following bound.
For every \(\boldsymbol{k}=(k_1,k_2)\in{\mathbb Z}^2\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}\), we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diophantine}
\operatorname{dist}(k_1\alpha_1+k_2\alpha_2,2\pi{\mathbb Z})
\geq\gamma\,\left\lVert\boldsymbol{k}\right\rVert_\infty^{-\tau}.
\end{equation}
Here \(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{k}\right\rVert_\infty=\max\{\left\lvertk_1\right\rvert,\left\lvertk_2\right\rvert\}\).
We call \(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\) \emph{Diophantine} if it belongs to
\(\operatorname{DC}(\gamma,\tau)\) for some \(\gamma>0\) and \(\tau\geq2\).
The condition depends only on \([\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\in{\mathbb T}^2\).
We also write \(\operatorname{DC}(\gamma,\tau)\) for its image in \({\mathbb T}^2\).
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- maintheorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:real-main}
There exist a smooth projective surface \(X_{{\mathbb R}}\) over \({\mathbb R}\) and an
\({\mathbb R}\)-automorphism
\(T_{{\mathbb R}} \in \operatorname{Aut}_{{\mathbb R}}(X_{{\mathbb R}})\) with the following
properties. Write \(X\) and \(T\) for their complexifications.
Then \(X\) is a complex projective K3 surface and
\(T \in \operatorname{Aut}(X)\).
The Picard number satisfies \(\rho(X)\geq4\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item\textup{\textbf{Real dynamics.}}
The real locus is the disjoint union of two tori:
\[
X_{{\mathbb R}}({\mathbb R}) = M_0 \sqcup M_1,
\qquad M_\ell \simeq ({\mathbb S}^1)^2 \quad \text{for } \ell=0,1.
\]
There are a constant \(\gamma>0\) and a vector
\(\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\in\operatorname{DC}(\gamma,3)\).
For each \(\ell = 0,1\), there is a
real-analytic diffeomorphism
\(j_{\ell,{\mathbb R}} \colon ({\mathbb S}^1)^2 \longrightarrow M_\ell\)
that conjugates the real dynamics to a rotation:
\[
T_{{\mathbb R}} \circ j_{\ell,{\mathbb R}}
= j_{\ell,{\mathbb R}} \circ R_{(-1)^\ell\boldsymbol{\alpha}},
\qquad \text{for } \ell = 0,1.
\]
Thus \(T_{{\mathbb R}}\) is conjugate to a rotation on each
component of the real locus. In particular,
\[
h_{\mathrm{top}}\bigl(T_{{\mathbb R}}|_{X_{{\mathbb R}}({\mathbb R})}\bigr) = 0.
\]
\item\textup{\textbf{Complex dynamics.}} The topological entropy of the holomorphic dynamical system \((X,T)\)
is
\[
h_{\mathrm{top}}(T)
= \log(7 + 4\sqrt3).
\]
\item\textup{\textbf{Invariant biannuli.}}
For some \(r>0\), each map \(j_{\ell,{\mathbb R}}\) extends to a holomorphic embedding
\(j_\ell \colon {\mathcal A}_r \longrightarrow X\).
The extension satisfies \(j_\ell|_{({\mathbb S}^1)^2} = j_{\ell,{\mathbb R}}\) and
\[
T \circ j_\ell = j_\ell \circ R_{(-1)^\ell\boldsymbol{\alpha}}.
\]
The open sets \(W_\ell \coloneqq j_\ell({\mathcal A}_r)\) are disjoint.
They satisfy
\[
X_{{\mathbb R}}({\mathbb R}) \subset W_0 \cup W_1 \subset \operatorname{Fat}(T).
\]
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label[proposition]{prop:fatou-components-and-coupling}
For the pair \((X,T)\) constructed in \Cref{thm:real-main}, let
\(U_\ell\) be the Fatou component containing \(M_\ell\), for \(\ell=0,1\).
Then \(U_0\ne U_1\). In particular, \(\operatorname{Fat}(T)\) is disconnected.

There is an involution \(A\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\), defined over
\({\mathbb R}\), such that
\[
A(M_0)=M_1,\qquad A(U_0)=U_1,\qquad
A\circ T\circ A=T^{-1}.
\]
The restriction \(A|_{M_0}\) conjugates \(T|_{M_0}\) to
\(T^{-1}|_{M_1}\).
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\Cref{thm:real-main} also shows that the real locus is non-empty and
lies entirely in the Fatou set. Although the complex automorphism has
positive entropy, its restriction to the real locus has entropy zero.
These two properties answer two questions of Cantat affirmatively
\cite{Cantat2018}*{Questions~3.5(2) and (3)}.
<!-- end remark-1 -->
<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
The disjoint \(T\)-invariant open sets \(W_0\) and \(W_1\) in
\Cref{thm:real-main} imply that no \(T\)-orbit is dense.
On each \(W_\ell\), the automorphism \(T\) is conjugate to a rotation
without periodic points. Hence all periodic points lie in the complement of
\(W_0 \cup W_1\), and the set of periodic points is not dense either.
<!-- end remark-2 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label[corollary]{cor:canonical-volume-nonergodic}
For the pair \((X,T)\) obtained in \Cref{thm:real-main}, the canonical
volume \(\mathrm{vol}_X\) is not ergodic with respect to \(T\). More precisely,
the disjoint \(T\)-invariant open sets \(W_0\) and \(W_1\) have positive
canonical volume, so
\[
0 < \mathrm{vol}_X(W_\ell) < 1
\qquad \text{for } \ell=0,1.
\]
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label[corollary]{cor:maximal-entropy-support}
For the pair \((X,T)\) obtained in \Cref{thm:real-main}, the support of
the measure of maximal entropy \(\mu_T\) does not have full Lebesgue measure.
More precisely, the open sets \(W_0\) and \(W_1\) satisfy
\[
\operatorname*{Supp}(\mu_T)\cap(W_0\cup W_1)=\varnothing.
\]

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label[lemma]{lem:diophantine-choice}
The union \(\bigcup_{\gamma>0}\operatorname{DC}(\gamma,3)\) has full
Haar measure in \({\mathbb T}^2\).
In particular, every nonempty open subset of \({\mathbb R}^2\) contains a vector
\(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\operatorname{DC}(\gamma,3)\) for some \(\gamma>0\).
The constant \(\gamma\) may depend on the chosen vector.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label[theorem]{thm:herman}
\label[theorem]{prop:continuous-herman-counterterm}
Fix \(\gamma>0\), \(\tau\geq2\), and
\(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\operatorname{DC}(\gamma,\tau)\). For every
\(\eta>0\), there are an integer \(q\geq1\) and a \(C^q\)-open neighborhood
\({\mathcal U}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\subset\operatorname{Diff}^{\omega}_0({\mathbb T}^2)\) of
\(\operatorname{Rot}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\) with the following properties.
There is a continuous map for the \(C^q\)-topology
\[
\vartheta_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\colon{\mathcal U}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\longrightarrow(-\eta,\eta)^2.
\]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item\textup{\textbf{Normal form.}}
For every \(g\in{\mathcal U}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\), there is a
\(\psi\in\operatorname{Diff}^{\omega}_0({\mathbb T}^2)\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:herman-normal-form}
g=\operatorname{Rot}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\circ\psi\circ\operatorname{Rot}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\circ\psi^{-1},
\qquad \boldsymbol{\theta}=\vartheta_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(g).
\end{equation}
\item\textup{\textbf{Nearby rotations.}}
There is a radius \(r_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in(0,\pi)\) with the following property.
If \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\in{\mathbb R}^2\) satisfies
\(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right\rVert_\infty<r_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\), then
\(\operatorname{Rot}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in{\mathcal U}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:herman-rotation-counterterm}
\vartheta_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\operatorname{Rot}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}})=\boldsymbol{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Each \(g\) sufficiently \(C^0\)-close to \(\operatorname{Rot}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\) has a unique
real-analytic displacement
\(d_g\colon{\mathbb T}^2\to(-\pi,\pi)^2\) such that
\[
g([\boldsymbol{u}])=[\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}+d_g([\boldsymbol{u}])].
\]
Define a lift of \(g\) by
\[
G_g(\boldsymbol{u})\coloneqq \boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}+d_g([\boldsymbol{u}]).
\]
This lift depends continuously on \(g\) in the \(C^q\)-topology.
This is the local correspondence between lifts and torus maps described in
\cite{Herman1979}*{Annex, Section~1, p.~202}.

The vector \(\boldsymbol{\alpha}/(2\pi)\) has Diophantine constant
\(\gamma/(2\pi)\) and exponent \(\tau\) in Herman's period-\(1\)
normalization. After rescaling to our period-\(2\pi\) coordinates,
\cite{Herman1979}*{Annex, Theorem~2.2, p.~203} applies to
\(G_g\) near \(\boldsymbol{u}\mapsto \boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}\). Choose an integer \(q\) sufficiently
large. The continuity and analytic-regularity clauses give a
continuous correction term \(\boldsymbol{\theta}(g)\) and a real-analytic lift
\(\Psi_g\) of a diffeomorphism
\(\psi_g\in\operatorname{Diff}^{\omega}_0({\mathbb T}^2)\), which satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:herman-lifted-normal-form}
G_g\bigl(\Psi_g(\boldsymbol{u})\bigr)=\Psi_g(\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{\alpha})+\boldsymbol{\theta}(g).
\end{equation}
Projecting \eqref{eq:herman-lifted-normal-form} to the torus gives
\eqref{eq:herman-normal-form}.

If \(g=\operatorname{Rot}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\) with \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\) close to \(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\) in \({\mathbb R}^2\),
then \(G_g(\boldsymbol{u})=\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{\beta}\). Iterating
\eqref{eq:herman-lifted-normal-form} gives
\[
\Psi_g(\boldsymbol{u}+n\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\Psi_g(\boldsymbol{u})+n\bigl(\boldsymbol{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\theta}(g)\bigr).
\]
Since \(\psi_g\) is isotopic to the identity, the displacement
\(\Psi_g-{\rm id}\) is periodic and hence bounded. Dividing the last
identity by \(n\) yields \(\boldsymbol{\theta}(g)=\boldsymbol{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}\).
In particular, \(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\operatorname{Rot}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})=\boldsymbol{0}\). By continuity, we may shrink
the neighborhood \({\mathcal U}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\) so that
\(\boldsymbol{\theta}(g)\in(-\eta,\eta)^2\) throughout it. Choose \(r_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in(0,\pi)\)
small enough that all rotations with
\(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right\rVert_\infty<r_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\) lie in this neighborhood.
Taking \(\vartheta_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\boldsymbol{\theta}\) proves the theorem.

\label[proposition]{prop:herman-ring-complexify-torus}

\(M \subset Y\) be a compact maximally totally real real-analytic torus.

f \circ j_{{\mathbb R}} = j_{{\mathbb R}} \circ R_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}.
\]
Then \(j_{{\mathbb R}}\) extends to a holomorphic embedding
\(j \colon {\mathcal A}_r \to Y\) for some \(r > 0\). This extension satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstract-complex-conjugacy}
f \circ j = j \circ R_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}.
\end{equation}
Set \(W \coloneqq j({\mathcal A}_r)\). Then \(W\) is invariant and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-integer-iterates}
f^n|_W = j \circ R_{n\boldsymbol{\beta}} \circ j^{-1},
\qquad \text{for } n \in {\mathbb Z}.
\end{equation}
In particular, \(W \subset \operatorname{Fat}(f)\).

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Regard \(({\mathbb S}^1)^2\) as the standard maximally totally real real-analytic
submanifold of \((\mathbb {C}^*)^2\).
We first extend \(j_{{\mathbb R}}\) to a biholomorphism
\(J\colon V\xrightarrow{\sim}V'\) between neighborhoods of \(({\mathbb S}^1)^2\)
and \(M\).
This extension is used without proof in
\cite{Moncet2013}*{Proposition~1.2}; we recall the standard argument.
Near each point, maximal total reality gives holomorphic coordinates in
which the torus is \({\mathbb R}^2\subset\mathbb {C}^2\).
In such coordinates, the convergent power series of \(j_{{\mathbb R}}\) define
local holomorphic extensions.
By the identity theorem, these extensions are unique and agree near the
torus on overlaps, so they glue.
The same construction applies to \(j_{{\mathbb R}}^{-1}\).
The two compositions are the identity on the tori, hence near them.
Shrinking the neighborhoods gives \(J\).
Compactness gives an \(r>0\) such that
\(\overline{{\mathcal A}_r}\subset V\). Set
\[
j\coloneqq J|_{{\mathcal A}_r}\colon{\mathcal A}_r\xrightarrow{\sim}W,
\qquad W\coloneqq J({\mathcal A}_r).
\]

The holomorphic maps \(f\circ j\) and \(j\circ R_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\) agree on
\(({\mathbb S}^1)^2\), so analytic continuation gives
\eqref{eq:abstract-complex-conjugacy} on \({\mathcal A}_r\). Since
\(R_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}({\mathcal A}_r)={\mathcal A}_r\), the set \(W\) is invariant, and
iteration yields \eqref{eq:all-integer-iterates}.

The vectors \(\bigl(e^{in\beta_1},e^{in\beta_2}\bigr)\) lie in the compact

iterates of \(f\) on \(W\). By the Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem, the family of
integer iterates is equicontinuous on \(W\), so \(W\subset\operatorname{Fat}(f)\).

<!-- proposition-4: body_md -->
\label[proposition]{prop:k3-entropy}
For every \(s \in {\mathcal G}\), \(X_s\) is a smooth projective complex K3
surface with Picard number at least \(4\). For every non-vanishing holomorphic
\(2\)-form \(\Omega_s \in \operatorname{H}^0(X_s,K_{X_s})\), the deck involutions
and their composition satisfy
\[
A_s^*\Omega_s=B_s^*\Omega_s=-\Omega_s,
\qquad
T_s^*\Omega_s = \Omega_s.
\]
The algebraic automorphism \(T_s\) has entropy
\[
h_{\mathrm{top}}(T_s) = \log(7 + 4\sqrt3).
\]
<!-- end proposition-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We first verify the K3 property.
We then compute the intersection form on a \(T_s^*\)-invariant rank-four
sublattice of the N\'eron--Severi group.
Finally, we determine the involution actions and deduce the assertions for
\(T_s\).

\noindent\textbf{Step 1: the K3 property.}
Since \(s\in{\mathcal G}\), the real surface \(X_{s,{\mathbb R}}\) is smooth, and hence so
is its complexification \(X_s\). Moreover, \(X_s\) is projective because it
is closed in \(P\).

Adjunction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjunction}
K_{X_s} = (K_P + 2L)|_{X_s} \simeq {\mathcal O}_{X_s}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, \(X_s\) is a smooth complete intersection of two ample divisors
in \(P=(\mathbb{P}^1)^4\).  The Lefschetz hyperplane theorem therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{H}^1(X_s,\mathbb {C}) \simeq \operatorname{H}^1(P,\mathbb {C})=0.
\]
Hence \(\operatorname{H}^1(X_s,{\mathcal O}_{X_s})=0\) by Hodge decomposition. Together with
\eqref{eq:adjunction}, this proves that \(X_s\) is a K3 surface.

\noindent\textbf{Step 2: the intersection form.}
We determine the part of the N\'eron--Severi lattice of \(X_s\) coming
from the ambient product. Pull back the class of \({\mathcal O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1)\) from each of
the four factors of \(P\). Let \(H_x,H_y,H_z,H_w\) denote the restrictions
of these classes to \(X_s\).
Write \(V_s\coloneqq\operatorname{span}_{{\mathbb R}}\{H_x,H_y,H_z,H_w\}\) for their real span.
Intersection in \(P\) gives
\[
H_i^2 = 0,
\qquad H_iH_j = 2 \quad \text{for } i \ne j.
\]
The Gram matrix has eigenvalues \(6,-2,-2,-2\), so the four classes are
independent in \(\operatorname*{NS}(X_s)\) and span a rank-four lattice of signature
\((1,3)\). In particular, \(\rho(X_s)\geq4\) for every \(s\in{\mathcal G}\).


\noindent\textbf{Step 3: the involution actions.}
To compute the action on divisor classes, we first recall two push--pull
identities for a finite double cover \(\pi\colon Z\to Y\) of smooth
complex surfaces. Let \(\tau\) be its deck involution.
For divisor classes \(E\) on \(Y\) and \(D\) on \(Z\), one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:double-cover-push-pull}
\pi_*\pi^*E=2E,
\qquad
\pi^*\pi_*D=D+\tau^*D.
\end{equation}
The first identity is the degree formula for a finite locally free morphism
\cite{StacksProject}*{\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02RH}
{Tag~02RH}}. For the second, \(\pi\) realizes \(Y\) as the quotient
\(Z/G\), where \(G=\{{\rm id},\tau\}\).
The push--pull formula for a finite group quotient gives
\(\pi^*\pi_*D=\sum_{g\in G}g_*D\)
\cite{Fulton1998}*{Example~1.7.6}.
Since \(\tau^{-1}=\tau\), we have \(\tau_*D=\tau^*D\).
This proves the second identity.

Apply these identities to \(\pi_{yw}\).
Let \(h_y,h_w\) be the pullbacks of the class of \({\mathcal O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1)\)
from the two factors of \(P_{yw}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^1_y\times\mathbb{P}^1_w\).
Then \(H_y=\pi_{yw}^*h_y\) and \(H_w=\pi_{yw}^*h_w\), so
\(A_s^*\) fixes \(H_y,H_w\).
To compute its action on \(H_x\), write
\((\pi_{yw})_*H_x=a h_y+b h_w\).
Since \(h_y^2=h_w^2=0\) and \(h_yh_w=1\), the projection formula and
the intersection numbers above give
\[
\begin{aligned}
b&=((\pi_{yw})_*H_x)\mathbin{\cdot}h_y
    =H_x\mathbin{\cdot}\pi_{yw}^*h_y
    =H_x\mathbin{\cdot}H_y=2,\\
a&=((\pi_{yw})_*H_x)\mathbin{\cdot}h_w
    =H_x\mathbin{\cdot}\pi_{yw}^*h_w
    =H_x\mathbin{\cdot}H_w=2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The same computation applies to \(H_z\). Thus
\[
(\pi_{yw})_*H_x = 2h_y + 2h_w,
\qquad
(\pi_{yw})_*H_z = 2h_y + 2h_w.
\]
The second identity in \eqref{eq:double-cover-push-pull} gives
\[
H_x+A_s^*H_x=\pi_{yw}^*(\pi_{yw})_*H_x=2H_y+2H_w,
\qquad
H_z+A_s^*H_z=\pi_{yw}^*(\pi_{yw})_*H_z=2H_y+2H_w.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-action}
\begin{aligned}
A_s^*H_x &= -H_x + 2H_y + 2H_w,&
A_s^*H_z &= -H_z + 2H_y + 2H_w,\\
A_s^*H_y &= H_y,& A_s^*H_w &= H_w.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Applying the same argument to \(\pi_{xz}\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-action}
\begin{aligned}
B_s^*H_y &= -H_y + 2H_x + 2H_z,&
B_s^*H_w &= -H_w + 2H_x + 2H_z,\\
B_s^*H_x &= H_x,& B_s^*H_z &= H_z.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These formulas show that \(A_s^*\) and \(B_s^*\) preserve \(V_s\).
We use the ordered basis \((H_x,H_y,H_z,H_w)\) and let matrices act
on coefficient column vectors.
The matrices of \(A_s^*|_{V_s}\) and \(B_s^*|_{V_s}\) are
\[
M_A=\begin{pmatrix}
-1&0&0&0\\
2&1&2&0\\
0&0&-1&0\\
2&0&2&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_B=\begin{pmatrix}
1&2&0&2\\
0&-1&0&0\\
0&2&1&2\\
0&0&0&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
We also determine the action on the holomorphic two-form.
Since \(A_s\) has order \(2\), we have either
\(A_s^*\Omega_s = \Omega_s\) or \(A_s^*\Omega_s = -\Omega_s\). We show
that \(\Omega_s\) is not invariant under \(A_s\). If a holomorphic
two-form were invariant under \(A_s\), it would descend to
\(\mathbb{P}^1_y \times \mathbb{P}^1_w\).
To check descent across the branch curve, choose local coordinates in
which the cover is \((u,v)\mapsto(u,t=v^2)\).
Invariance of \(f(u,v)\,du\wedge dv\) under \(v\mapsto-v\) says that
\(f\) is odd in \(v\). Thus \(f(u,v)=v g(u,v^2)\) for a holomorphic
function \(g\), and the form is the pullback of
\(\tfrac12 g(u,t)\,du\wedge dt\) on the base \(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1\).
Since
\(\operatorname{H}^0(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1,K_{\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1}) = 0\), \(\Omega_s\)
cannot be \(A_s\)-invariant. Thus \(A_s^*\Omega_s = -\Omega_s\).
The same argument gives \(B_s^*\Omega_s = -\Omega_s\).

\noindent\textbf{Step 4: the action of \(T_s\).}
Since \(T_s^*=A_s^*B_s^*\), its matrix on \(V_s\) in this convention is
\[
M_T=M_AM_B=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&-2&0&-2\\
2&7&2&8\\
0&-2&-1&-2\\
2&8&2&7
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its characteristic polynomial is \((t+1)^2(t^2-14t+1)\).
Thus its spectral radius is \(7+4\sqrt3\).
The orthogonal complement of \(V_s\) in \(\operatorname{H}^{1,1}(X_s,{\mathbb R})\) is
negative definite, by the Hodge index theorem, and \(T_s^*\)-invariant.
The action there is an isometry, so its eigenvalues have modulus one.
Thus the first dynamical degree is exactly
\(\lambda_1(T_s)=7+4\sqrt3\), and the Gromov--Yomdin theorem gives
\[
h_{\mathrm{top}}(T_s) = \log\lambda_1(T_s) = \log(7 + 4\sqrt3).
\]

Both involutions \(A_s\) and \(B_s\) pull back \(\Omega_s\) to \(-\Omega_s\), so their composition satisfies
\[
T_s^*\Omega_s = \Omega_s.
\]

\label[lemma]{lem:phase-selection}
Let \(D\subset{\mathbb R}^2\) be an open disk, let \(\delta>0\), and let
\(\Theta\colon\overline D\times[-\delta,\delta]\to{\mathbb R}^2\) be continuous.

zero \(\boldsymbol{t}_0\) in \(\overline D\), which lies in \(D\).

Then there is \(0<\delta_1\leq\delta\) with the following property.
For every \(\lambda\) with \(\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert<\delta_1\), there exists
\(\boldsymbol{t}_\lambda\in D\) such that \(\Theta(\boldsymbol{t}_\lambda,\lambda)=\boldsymbol{0}\).

We show that \(\Theta(\,\cdot\,,0)\) has nonzero degree on \(D\) and that
small perturbations keep this degree.
Since \(\boldsymbol{t}_0\) is the only zero and it is nondegenerate, \(\boldsymbol{0}\) is a
regular value of \(\Theta(\,\cdot\,,0)\) on \(D\).
By the regular-value formula, \(\deg(\Theta(\,\cdot\,,0),D,\boldsymbol{0})\) is
the sign of the Jacobian determinant at \(\boldsymbol{t}_0\), hence \(\pm1\).
The boundary values at \(\lambda=0\) are separated from zero by
\[
m\coloneqq\min_{\boldsymbol{t}\in\partial D}\left\lvert\Theta(\boldsymbol{t},0)\right\rvert>0.
\]
Uniform continuity on the compact set \(\overline D\times[-\delta,\delta]\)
gives \(0<\delta_1\leq\delta\) such that
\[
\left\lvert\Theta(\boldsymbol{t},\lambda)-\Theta(\boldsymbol{t},0)\right\rvert<m
\qquad\text{for } \boldsymbol{t}\in\partial D \text{ and } \left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert<\delta_1.
\]
Fix such a \(\lambda\).
The straight-line homotopy from \(\Theta(\,\cdot\,,0)\) to
\(\Theta(\,\cdot\,,\lambda)\) therefore avoids zero on \(\partial D\).
By homotopy invariance of the degree, we have
\[
\deg(\Theta(\,\cdot\,,\lambda),D,\boldsymbol{0})
=\deg(\Theta(\,\cdot\,,0),D,\boldsymbol{0})\ne0.
\]
Hence \(\Theta(\,\cdot\,,\lambda)\) has a zero \(\boldsymbol{t}_\lambda\in D\).

\label[theorem]{thm:rotation-domain-structure}
Let \(f\) be an automorphism of a compact complex surface \(Y\) that
preserves a smooth positive volume form.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item\textup{\textbf{Rotation domains.}}

\item\textup{\textbf{Group action.}}

The construction in \Cref{thm:prescribed-rotation-smoothing} gives the
real deck involution \(A\coloneqq A_s\) with
\[
A(M_0)=M_1,\qquad ATA=T^{-1}.
\]
Thus \(AT^nA=T^{-n}\) for every \(n\in{\mathbb Z}\), so \(A\) maps \(\operatorname{Fat}(T)\) onto
itself. Hence \(A\) permutes the Fatou components, and \(A(U_0)=U_1\).
Restricting \(AT=T^{-1}A\) to \(M_0\) gives the stated conjugacy.

Suppose, for contradiction, that \(U_0=U_1=:U\).
The component \(U\) is \(T\)-invariant because it contains the invariant
biannulus \(W_0\). The automorphism \(T\) preserves the canonical volume
\(\mathrm{vol}_X\) in \eqref{eq:canonical-volume}, which is given by a smooth
positive volume form.
Thus \Cref{thm:rotation-domain-structure}\textup{(i)} shows that \(U\) is
a rotation domain.
By \Cref{thm:rotation-domain-structure}\textup{(ii)}, the closure
\[
G\coloneqq\overline{\{T^n|_U:n\in{\mathbb Z}\}}
\subset\operatorname{Aut}(U)
\]
is a compact abelian Lie group acting real-analytically on \(U\).

We first identify \(G\) with the rotation group of \({\mathcal A}_r\), which is \(({\mathbb S}^1)^2\).
Each \(g\in G\) is a locally uniform limit of iterates \(T^{n_j}|_U\).
On \({\mathcal A}_r\), we have \(T^{n_j}\circ j_0=j_0\circ R_{n_j\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\).
Passing to a subsequence with \(R_{n_j\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\to R_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\) gives
\(g\circ j_0=j_0\circ R_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\).
Hence \(g\mapsto j_0^{-1}\circ g|_{W_0}\circ j_0\) is a continuous
homomorphism from \(G\) to the rotation group \(({\mathbb S}^1)^2\) of \({\mathcal A}_r\).
Its image is compact and contains the dense subgroup
\(\{R_{n\boldsymbol{\alpha}}:n\in{\mathbb Z}\}\), so it is surjective.
The identity theorem gives injectivity.
Since \(G\) is compact, this map is an isomorphism of topological groups.
For \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\in{\mathbb R}^2\), let \(g_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in G\) be the element with
\(g_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\circ j_0=j_0\circ R_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\) on \({\mathcal A}_r\).
Let \(\xi_1\) and \(\xi_2\) be the vector fields on \(U\) generating the
flows \(t\mapsto g_{(t,0)}\) and \(t\mapsto g_{(0,t)}\), respectively.
They are holomorphic because each element of \(G\) is holomorphic.

Choose a nonzero holomorphic two-form \(\Omega\) on \(X\).
By \Cref{prop:k3-entropy}, we have \(T^*\Omega=\Omega\) and
\(A^*\Omega=-\Omega\).
Passing to the limit in \((T^{n_j})^*\Omega=\Omega\) shows that \(G\)
preserves \(\Omega\).
Since \(G\) is abelian, the generators satisfy
\[
[\xi_1,\xi_2]=0,
\qquad
\mathcal L_{\xi_1}\Omega=\mathcal L_{\xi_2}\Omega=0.
\]
Define the holomorphic function \(h\colon U\to\mathbb {C}\) by
\(h\coloneqq\Omega(\xi_1,\xi_2)\).
Since \(\Omega\) is closed, Cartan's formula
\(\mathcal L_{\xi_i}=d\iota_{\xi_i}+\iota_{\xi_i}d\) gives
\(d(\iota_{\xi_i}\Omega)=\mathcal L_{\xi_i}\Omega=0\) for \(i=1,2\).
Combining this with the identity
\(\iota_{[\xi_1,\xi_2]}=\mathcal L_{\xi_1}\iota_{\xi_2}
-\iota_{\xi_2}\mathcal L_{\xi_1}\), we obtain
\[
0=\iota_{[\xi_1,\xi_2]}\Omega
=\mathcal L_{\xi_1}(\iota_{\xi_2}\Omega)
=d(\iota_{\xi_1}\iota_{\xi_2}\Omega)
+\iota_{\xi_1}d(\iota_{\xi_2}\Omega)
=-dh.
\]
Since \(U\) is connected, \(h\) is constant.
The flows \(t\mapsto R_{(t,0)}\) and \(t\mapsto R_{(0,t)}\) are generated
by \(iz_1\partial_{z_1}\) and \(iz_2\partial_{z_2}\).
Hence \(\xi_1\) and \(\xi_2\) are their pushforwards under \(j_0\) on
\(W_0\).
They are therefore linearly independent over \(\mathbb {C}\) at every point of
\(W_0\).
The form \(\Omega\) is nowhere vanishing, so this constant is nonzero.

Since \(A(U)=U\), the relation \(AT^nA=T^{-n}\) extends by continuity to
\(AgA=g^{-1}\) for every \(g\in G\).
In particular, \(Ag_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}A=g_{-\boldsymbol{\beta}}\) for every \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\in{\mathbb R}^2\).

\[
dA_x\bigl(\xi_i(x)\bigr)=-\xi_i(Ax),
\qquad \text{for } x\in U \text{ and } i=1,2.
\]
Since \(\Omega\) is bilinear, the two minus signs cancel.
For every \(x\in U\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
h(Ax)
&=\Omega_{Ax}\bigl(\xi_1(Ax),\xi_2(Ax)\bigr)\\
&=(A^*\Omega)_x\bigl(\xi_1(x),\xi_2(x)\bigr)
=-h(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
This contradicts the fact that \(h\) is a nonzero constant.
Therefore \(U_0\ne U_1\).

\left\{s\in{\mathcal V}:

The Jacobian criterion and properness of the ambient products show that
these conditions define a Zariski-open subscheme of
\(\mathbb A^{32}_{{\mathbb R}}\). Its complexification is nonempty by the preceding
generic smoothness statement. Since \({\mathbb R}^{32}\) is Zariski dense in
\(\mathbb A^{32}_{{\mathbb R}}\), its real locus \({\mathcal G}\) is nonempty as well.
For \(s\in{\mathcal G}\), the surface \(X_{s,{\mathbb R}}=X_{1234,s}\) is smooth by
definition, proving~\textup{(i)}.

To prove~\textup{(ii)}, we first show that every coordinate projection
has zero-dimensional geometric fibers by excluding a common curve in
its two defining equations. Fix distinct \(i,j\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), and write
\(\{i,j,k,l\}=\{1,2,3,4\}\). Over a geometric point \((v_i,v_j)\),
the fiber of \(p_{ij}\) is cut out on
\(\mathbb{P}(V_k^\vee)\times\mathbb{P}(V_l^\vee)\) by two \((1,1)\)-forms
\(g_0,g_1\). A positive-dimensional fiber would give a common divisor
of bidegree \((1,0)\), \((0,1)\), or \((1,1)\).
A common \((1,0)\)-factor gives a point \(v_k\) at which
\[
s(v_i,v_j,v_k,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)=0.
\]
This point is a rank singularity of \(X_{ijk,s}\).
A common \((0,1)\)-factor gives a rank singularity of
\(X_{ijl,s}\). A common \((1,1)\)-factor makes \(g_0,g_1\) linearly
dependent, so there is a \(v_5\in\mathbb{P}(V_5^\vee)\) for which
\(s(v_i,v_j,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,,v_5)\) is the zero matrix. This gives
a rank singularity of \(X_{ij5,s}\). All three cases contradict
\(s\in{\mathcal G}\).

Thus \(p_{ij}\) is projective with zero-dimensional geometric fibers,
and hence finite. Each fiber is a proper intersection of two
\((1,1)\)-divisors, so its scheme-theoretic length is calculated by the intersection number
\[
(1,1)\mathbin{\cdot}(1,1)=2.
\]
The source is smooth, hence Cohen--Macaulay, and the target is a regular
