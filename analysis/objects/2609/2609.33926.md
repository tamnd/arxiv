<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
\label{rem: bounded_dual}     
Assumption {\SFbf (A3)} implies relatively complete recourse; i.e., $\mathcal{Y}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega})$ is feasible for any $\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{X}$ and $\omega \in [N]$. Thus, $ h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_\omega)$ is bounded from above and $\Set*{\boldsymbol{\pi} \ge \boldsymbol{0}}{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\top} \boldsymbol{D}_{\omega} \le \boldsymbol{0}^{\top}, \ \boldsymbol{\pi}^{\top} (\boldsymbol{B}_{\omega} \boldsymbol{x} + \boldsymbol{b}_{\omega}) \ge \boldsymbol{0}} =\{\boldsymbol{0}\}$ by Farkas' Lemma. Hence, an optimal solution to the corresponding dual problem of \eqref{eq: recourse} is attained at an extreme point, showing that $ h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega})$ is bounded from below (by weak duality). In addition, given that we have a complete recourse for every $\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$, then $\Set*{\boldsymbol{\pi} \ge \boldsymbol{0}}{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\top} \boldsymbol{D}_{\omega} \le \boldsymbol{0}^{\top}}=\{\boldsymbol{0}\}$; so, the set of optimal dual solutions is bounded. 
\hfill	$\blacksquare$

<!-- remark-2: body_md -->
When integer variables are present in the first stage, i.e., $n_{1}>0$, a branch-and-cut procedure can be employed, where a linear relaxation of the master problem is solved using a standard branch-and-bound algorithm. Then, a globally valid optimality cut is obtained whenever an integer feasible solution to the restricted master problem violates any of the previously generated optimality cuts. By the finiteness of the branch-and-bound, it is straightforward to prove the finite convergence of this adjusted version of Algorithm \ref{alg: DD-nonconvex-recourse}. 
Moreover, while Algorithm \ref{alg: DD-nonconvex-recourse} is presented for a DRO problem with a decision-dependent ambiguity set, it can be used to solve DRO problems with a decision-independent ambiguity set, i.e., $\vartheta(\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{\vartheta}$. In this case, problem \eqref{eq: reform-recourse} reduces to a two-stage stochastic MIP. 
Thus, Algorithm \ref{alg: DD-nonconvex-recourse} needs to be modified by just changing $\vartheta(\boldsymbol{x})$ to $\boldsymbol{\vartheta}$. \hfill	$\blacksquare$

<!-- remark-3: body_md -->
When the recourse function $h(\cdot,\boldsymbol{\xi})$, defined  in \eqref{eq: recourse}, is a convex function, e.g., when $\psi_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{y}, \boldsymbol{\xi})=\boldsymbol{q}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}$, one can obtain Benders-type cut for $G_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\lambda})$, defined in \eqref{eq: Q_k}. More precisely, it can be shown that $G_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\lambda})$ is convex on $(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\lambda})$ for $\omega \in [N]$. Consequently, by exploiting the convex structure of $G_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\lambda})$, one can iteratively obtain its outer approximations using subgradient inequality. 
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              
As a result, the standard finite-convergence argument of two-stage SPs, combined with Assumption {\SFbf (A4)}, leads to convergence to an $\epsilon$-optimal solution to  \eqref{eq: DRO_Obj}  in a finite number of iterations. We skip the details for brevity. \hfill	$\blacksquare$

<!-- head-2: body_md -->
{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm: simple-moment-inner-lag-dual}}
    Let us define $\mathcal{M}:=\Set*{\boldsymbol{p} \ge \boldsymbol{0}}{ \underline{p}_{\omega} \le  p_{\omega} \le \overline{p}_{\omega}, \; \omega \in [N]}$.
    For a fixed $\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{X}$, by dualizing the second set of constraints in \eqref{eq: simple-moment}, a Lagrangian function of problem $\max_{\boldsymbol{p} \in \mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{x})} \ \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{p}} \left[ h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}) \right]$  can be written as: 
                      
        $\mathfrak{L}(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{p}, \boldsymbol{\lambda})= \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top} \boldsymbol{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{x})+ \sum_{\omega \in [N]} p_{\omega} \Big(h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}) - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top}\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega})\Big)$.
                    
    Hence, the Lagrangian dual of $\max\limits_{\boldsymbol{p} \in \mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{x}) }
    \ \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{p}} \left[ h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}) \right]$ is 
                      
     $   \min\limits_{ \boldsymbol{\lambda} \ge \boldsymbol{0}}   \max\limits_{ \boldsymbol{p} \in \mathcal{M}}  \ \mathfrak{L}(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{p}, \boldsymbol{\lambda})$.
                        
We have    \begin{align*}
         - & \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top} \boldsymbol{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{x}) +  \max_{ \boldsymbol{p} \in \mathcal{M}}  \mathfrak{L}(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{p}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) \\
        =& \sum_{\omega \in [N]} \overline{p}_{\omega} \Big(h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}) - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top} \boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega})\Big)_{+}   -   \sum_{\omega \in [N]} \underline{p}_{\omega} \Big(-h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}) + \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top} \boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}) \Big)_{+} \\
        = & \sum_{\omega \in [N]} \overline{p}_{\omega} \Big(h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}) - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top} \boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega})\Big)_{+}  - \underline{p}_{\omega} \Big(-h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}) + \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top} \boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}) \Big)_{+} \\
        = &  \sum_{\omega \in [N]} \varphi_{\omega} \Big[ h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}) - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top} \boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}) \Big] = \sum_{\omega \in [N]} G_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}).  
    \end{align*}
                                                                                                                                                                                                                         
    The second equality above is due to the fact that either $\Big(h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}) - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top} \boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega})\Big)_{+}$ or $\Big(-h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}) + \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top} \boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega})\Big)_{+}$ is positive. Now, because $\max_{\boldsymbol{p} \in \mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{x}) }
    \ \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{p}} \left[ h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}) \right]$ is an LP and by Assumption {\SFbf (A2)}, there is no duality gap. Combining the resulting dual problem with the outer minimization problem yields the reformulation stated in the theorem. \hfill \mbox{\quad$\square$}

<!-- head-6: body_md -->
{Proof of Lemma \ref{lem: boundedness_dual}}
    For a fixed $\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{X}$, following the proof of Theorem \ref{thm: simple-moment-inner-lag-dual}, $\max_{\boldsymbol{p} \in \mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{x})}  \linebreak  \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{p}} \left[ h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}) \right]$ can be reformulated as the minimization problem $\min_{\boldsymbol{\lambda} \ge \boldsymbol{0}} F_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})$, where
                      
        $F_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}):= \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{x})  + \sum_{\omega \in [N]} G_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{\lambda})$.
                    
    Let $\mathcal{M}:=\Set*{\boldsymbol{p} \in \mathbb{R}^{N}}{\underline{p}_{\omega} \le p_{\omega} \le \overline{p}_{\omega}, \; \omega \in [N]}$, $\boldsymbol{h}_{\boldsymbol{x}}:=(h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega}))_{\omega \in [N]}$, and $\boldsymbol{A}:=[\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1}) \ \cdots \ \boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{N})]$. Then
                      
        $F_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\max_{\boldsymbol{p} \in \mathcal{M}} \left\{\boldsymbol{h}_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{x})-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{p}\right)\right\}$.
                    
    Suppose $\boldsymbol{d}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{s}$ satisfies
                      
        $\max_{\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{M}} \ \boldsymbol{d}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{x})-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{p}\right)=0$.
                    
    Then, for every $t\ge 0$ and every optimal multiplier $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$,
    \begin{align*}
        F_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}+t\boldsymbol{d})
        &=\max_{\boldsymbol{p} \in \mathcal{M}} \left\{\boldsymbol{h}_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{x})-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{p}\right)+t\boldsymbol{d}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{x})-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{p}\right)\right\} \\
        & = \max_{\boldsymbol{p} \in \mathcal{M}} \left\{\boldsymbol{h}_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{x})-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{p}\right) \right\} + t \max_{ \boldsymbol{p} \in \mathcal{M}}\boldsymbol{d}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{x})-\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{p}\right)\\
        & \le F_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}).
    \end{align*}
    Hence, by optimality of $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$, equality holds. Therefore, unbounded rays of optimal multipliers, if present, do not change the objective value, and it suffices to select a bounded representative optimal multiplier.
    To construct such a representative uniformly over $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X}$, consider the equivalent LP dual
    \begin{equation*}
        \min_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\mu}}\left\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{x})+\sum_{\omega\in[N]}\overline{p}_{\omega}\gamma_{\omega}-\sum_{\omega\in[N]}\underline{p}_{\omega}\mu_{\omega}:\;
        \boldsymbol{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\lambda}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}-\boldsymbol{\mu}=\boldsymbol{h}_{\boldsymbol{x}},\; \boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\mu}\ge\boldsymbol{0}\right\}.
    \end{equation*}
    Eliminating $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ and $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ recovers $\min_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\ge\boldsymbol{0}}F_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})$. Since $\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{x})$ is nonempty by Assumption {\SFbf (A2)} and $\mathcal{M}$ is bounded, the primal LP is feasible and bounded; thus, the above dual has a finite optimal extreme point. Every such extreme point is a basic feasible solution of the system with coefficient matrix $[\boldsymbol{A}^{\top} \ I \ -I]$ and right-hand side $\boldsymbol{h}_{\boldsymbol{x}}$. 
    Let $\overline{h}:=\sup\{|h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega})|: \boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X},\; \omega\in[N]\} < \infty$, where finiteness follows from the boundedness of $h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega})$ (implied by Assumption {\SFbf A3}) and the compactness of $\mathcal{X}$.  
    Because $|h(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\omega})|\le \overline{h}$ for all $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X}$ and $\omega\in[N]$, and because there are finitely many bases of $[\boldsymbol{A}^{\top} \ I \ -I]$, there is a constant $\overline{\lambda}<\infty$ that bounds the $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$-components of these optimal basic feasible solutions for all $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X}$. Therefore, for every $\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X}$, the minimization of $F_{\boldsymbol{x}}$ admits an optimal solution in $\mathcal{L}$, and restricting $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ to $\mathcal{L}$ does not change the optimal value of \eqref{eq: reform-simple-moment}.  \hfill \mbox{\quad$\square$}

{Proof of Lemma \ref{lem: G_properties}}

\begin{equation*}

{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm: DD-finite-convergence-nonconvex}}
    To prove the finite convergence of Algorithm \ref{alg: DD-nonconvex-recourse}, we need to show that the ``while" loop terminates in a finite number of iterations, generating an $\epsilon$-optimal solution to \eqref{eq: DRO_Obj}. 

    By contradiction, suppose that the ``while" loop does not terminate in a finite number of iterations. Let $\{(\boldsymbol{x}^{t}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{t}, \boldsymbol{\theta}^{t})\}$ be the sequence of iterates generated in Line \ref{line: iterates} and $\{(\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\omega}^{t}, \sigma_{\omega}^{t}) \in \mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{x}^t,\boldsymbol{\lambda}^t)\}$, $\omega \in [N]$, be the sequence of dual multipliers  generated in Line \ref{line: duals}. 
    We show that  $\theta_{\omega}^{t} \ge G_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{x}^{t}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{t}) - \frac{\epsilon}{2}$, $\omega \in [N]$, for all sufficiently large $t$.

		+ \sum_{\omega \in [N] } \theta_{\omega}^{t} \ge \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{t} \boldsymbol{\vartheta}(\boldsymbol{x}^{t}) 
		+ \sum_{\omega \in [N]} G_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{x}^{t},\lambda^{t}) - \frac{\epsilon}{2} \ge \mathrm{UB} - \boldsymbol{c}^{\top} \boldsymbol{x}^t - (\boldsymbol{x}^t)^{\top} \boldsymbol{C} \boldsymbol{x}^t  -\frac{\epsilon}{2}$,
                    
    contradicting that the ``while'' loop does not terminate in a finite number of iterations. 
    Given Assumption {\SFbf (A4)}, we have $\mathrm{LB} \le z^{t} + \frac{\epsilon}{2}$.  Thus, Algorithm \ref{alg: DD-nonconvex-recourse} returns the $\epsilon$-optimal value $\textrm{UB}$ because $\textrm{UB} \le z^{t} + \epsilon$, with the corresponding $\epsilon$-optimal solution $(\boldsymbol{x}^{t}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{t})$.

    Let $(\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}, \overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}, \overline{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) \in \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{L}$ (by closedness) and $(\overline{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\omega}, \overline{\sigma}_{\omega})$, $\omega \in [N]$,  be a limit point of $\{(\boldsymbol{x}^{t}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{t}, \boldsymbol{\theta}^{t})\}$ and $\{(\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\omega}^{t}, \sigma_{\omega}^{t})\}$, $\omega \in [N]$, on $\mathcal{K}^{\prime}$, respectively.          Now, given that $(\overline{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\omega}, \overline{\sigma}_{\omega}) \in \mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\overline{\boldsymbol{x}},\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})$, $\omega \in [N]$, by strong duality we have $G_{\omega}(\overline{\boldsymbol{x}},\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})=\mathfrak{L}_{\omega}(\overline{\boldsymbol{x}},\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\overline{\boldsymbol{y}}_{\omega},  \overline{\sigma}_{\omega},\overline{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\omega}; \overline{\boldsymbol{x}})$. Hence, $\overline{\theta}_{\omega}  \ge G_{\omega}(\overline{\boldsymbol{x}},\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})$. Finally, because $G_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\lambda})$ is l.s.c. by Lemma \ref{lem: G_properties}.\ref{G}, we have $\theta_{\omega}^{t} \ge G_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{x}^{t}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{t}) - \frac{\epsilon}{2}$, $\omega \in [N]$, for all sufficiently large $t$. This completes the proof. \hfill \mbox{\quad$\square$}
