\label{lemma:An}
    For all integers $k \geqslant 0$ and $\ell \geqslant 3$, the tournament $A_{k\ell}$ contains a subdigraph $H \subseteq A_{k\ell}$ with $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(H) > k$ and $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\operatorname{girth}}}(H) > \ell$.

We proceed by induction on $k$. When $k=0$, we let $H = A_0$ be a one-vertex digraph.
    Let $k \geqslant 1$ and suppose the tournament $A_{(k-1)\ell}$ contains a subdigraph $H$ with $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(H) > k-1$ and $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\operatorname{girth}}}(H) > \ell$.
    The tournament $A_{k\ell}$ is made from $k\ell$ copies $T_1, \dots, T_{k\ell}$ of $A_{k\ell-1}$ and $k\ell+1$ additional vertices $v_0, \dots, v_{k\ell}$ such that for $0 \leqslant i < j \leqslant n$, we have $v_i \Leftarrow v_j$ and $v_i \Rightarrow T_j$, and for $1 \leqslant i < j \leqslant n$, we have $T_i \Rightarrow v_j$ and $T_i \Rightarrow T_j$.

    As $(k-1)\ell \leqslant k\ell-1$, each copy $T_i$ of $A_{k\ell-1}$ contains a copy of $A_{(k-1)\ell}$ and thus a subdigraph $H_i \subseteq T_i$ such that $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(H_i) > k-1$ and $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\operatorname{girth}}}(H_i) > \ell$, using the induction hypothesis. For each $i < t \leqslant j$, as $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(H_t) > k-1$, there is a vertex $u_t \in H_t$ with colour $c$. Then, $v_i u_{i+1} \cdots u_jv_j$ form a monochromatic directed cycle, a contradiction. Hence $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(H) > k$.

<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lemma:main}
    For all integers $h,k,\ell,c \geqslant 0$, there exists an integer $g(h,k,\ell,c) \geqslant 0$ such that every tournament $T$ with $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(T) \geqslant g(h,k,\ell,c)$ and $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\omega}}(T) \leqslant h$ contains a subdigraph $H \subset T$ with $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\operatorname{girth}}}(H) > \ell$ and such that for every subset of vertices $X \subset V(T)$ with $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(T[X]) \leqslant c$, we have $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(H - X) > k$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
We proceed by induction on $h+k$. When $h=0$, the fact that $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\omega}}(T) \leqslant 0$ implies that $T$ is empty, so it suffices to set $g(h,k,\ell,c)=1$. When $k=0$, we set $g(h,k,\ell,c)=c+1$, and take $H=(V(T),\varnothing)$. Now suppose $h,k \geqslant 1$ and set
    \[
         g' := g(h-1,k,\ell,c),\quad g'' := g \big( h, k-1, \ell, 2g'+c \big), \quad\text{and}\quad g(h,k,\ell,c) := \ell k g'' + c+1.
    \]
    
    Fix a $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\omega}}$-ordering $\prec$ of $T$. For each vertex $v \in V(T)$, as $\omega(T^\prec)=h$, the neighborhood of $v$ in $T^\prec$ contains no $h$-clique, so $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\omega}}(N_{T^\prec}(v)) \leqslant h-1$. By induction hypothesis, if $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(N_{T^\prec}(v)) \geqslant g' = g(h-1,k,\ell,c)$ then $N_{T^\prec}(v)$ contains a subdigraph $H$ satisfying the desired result. Therefore, we may suppose that
    \[
        \operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(N_{T^\prec}(v))< g' \qquad \text{ for all } v \in V(T).
    \]

    We construct bags of vertices $B_0, B_1, \dots$ by adding vertices to $B_0$, in the order given by $\prec$, until $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(B_0) = g''$, then adding the following vertices to $B_1$ until $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(B_1) = g''$, and so on until no more vertices remain. We obtain a partition $B_0 \prec \cdots \prec B_t$ of the vertices such that $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(B_i) = g''$ for all $0 \leqslant i < t$, and $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(B_t) \leqslant g''$.

    For each $0 \leqslant i < t$, as $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(B_i) = g'' = g \big( h, k-1, \ell, 2g'+c \big)$, by induction hypothesis, the tournament $T[B_i]$ contain a subdigraph $H_i \subseteq T[B_i]$ with $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\operatorname{girth}}}(H_i) > \ell$ and such that for every subset of vertices $X \subset V(T)$ with $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(X) \leqslant 2g'+c$, we have $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(H_i - X) > k-1$.
    Let $H$ be the digraph made from the subgraphs $H_i$ for $0 < i < t$, every (forward) arc from $B_i$ to $B_{i+1}$ for $0 \leqslant i < t$, and all (backward) arcs from $B_j$ to $B_i$ for $i<j$ and $i \equiv j \pmod \ell$. In what follows, we refer to this last type of arcs as \emph{long} arcs.

    We claim that $H$ satisfies the desired result.
    The proof that $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\operatorname{girth}}}(H) > \ell$ is basically the same as in \Cref{lemma:An}.
    Let $X \subset V(T)$ be a subset of vertices with $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(T[X])\leqslant c$, and toward a contradiction, suppose that $H-X$ has a $k$-dicoloring.
    We distinguish two cases depending on whether there is a monochromatic long arc.
    
    \medskip

    If there is a monochromatic long arc, then we claim that there is a monochromatic directed cycle. 
    First, note that for $v_{t-1} \in B_{t-1}$ and $v_{t+1} \in B_{t+1}$, as $v_{t-1} \prec B_t \prec v_{t+1}$, every in-neighbor of $v_{t-1}$ in $B_t$ is adjacent to $v_{t-1}$ in the backedge graph $T^\prec$, that is $(N^-(v_{t-1}) \cap B_t) \subseteq N_{T^\prec}(v_{t-1})$, and likewise we have $(N^+(v_{t+1}) \cap B_t) \subseteq N_{T^\prec}(v_{t+1})$. Recall that $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(N_{T^\prec}(v)) \leqslant g'$ for all vertices $v \in V(T)$, so $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(X') \leqslant 2g'+c$ where $X' = (N^-(v_{t-1}) \cap B_t) \cup (N^+(v_{t+1}) \cap B_t) \cup X$, and thus $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(H_t-X') > k-1$.
    
    Now let $u_ju_i$ be a long arc with $u_i \in B_i$ and $u_j \in B_j$ ($i<j$) both having color $\alpha$. We iteratively build a monochromatic path $u_i u_{i+1} \cdots u_{j-2}$. For $i < t < j-1$, denote $X_t = (N^-(u_{t-1}) \cap B_t) \cup X$, then as $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(H_t - X_t) > k-1$, there exists a vertex $u_t \in H_t - X_t$ with color $\alpha$. Last, denote $X_{j-1} = (N^-(u_{j-2}) \cap B_{j-1}) \cup (N^+(u_{j}) \cap B_{j-1}) \cup X$, then as $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(H_{j-1} - X_{j-1}) > k-1$, there exists a vertex $u_{j-1} \in H_{j-1} - X_{j-1}$ with color $\alpha$. Now $u_i \cdots u_j$ is a monochromatic cycle in $H-X$, a contradiction.

    \medskip

    If there is no monochromatic long arc, then we claim that $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(T-X) \leqslant \ell k g''$ which contradicts $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(T) \geqslant \ell k g''+c+1$ as $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(T[X]) \leqslant c$.
    We partition the bags in $\ell$ classes according to their index modulo $\ell$, that is, for each $i \in [\ell]$, we denote $C_i = \bigcup_{i \equiv j \pmod{\ell}} B_j$. Observe that every backward arc between two different bags of $C_i$ is a long arc and thus bichromatic. For each index $i \in [\ell]$ and color $\alpha \in [k]$, let $T_{i,k}$ be the subtournament of $T-X$ induced by all vertices of color $\alpha$ in $C_i$. Note that there is no arc in $T_{i,k,}$ from $B_j$ to $B_{j'}$ with $j>j'$, so we have $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(T_{i,k}) \leqslant \max_j \operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(B_j) = g''$. It follows that $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(T-X) \leqslant \ell k g''$, a contradiction. Therefore we have $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(H-X) \geqslant k$.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:ineq}
    Let $T$ be a tournament. For every ordering $\prec$ of $T$, we have:
    \[
        \operatorname{\chi}(T^\prec) \leqslant \operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(T) \cdot \operatorname{\omega}(T^\prec).
    \]
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Let $k\geqslant 0$ and $\ell \geqslant 3$.
    Suppose there exists an integer $f(k,\ell) \geqslant 0$ that satisfies Conjecture \ref{conj:EHT}.
    Fix a graph $H_{k,\ell}$ with $\operatorname{girth}(H_{k,\ell}) > \ell$ and $\operatorname{\chi}(H_{k,\ell}) > k$, which exists by \cite{Erdos}, and let $G$ be a graph with $\operatorname{\chi}(G) \geqslant |V(H_{k,\ell})| \cdot f(k,\ell)$. If $G$ contains a $|V(H_{k,\ell})|$-clique, then $H_{k,\ell}$ is a subgraph of $G$ and we're done, so $\omega(G) < |V(H_{k,\ell})|$.
    
    Let $T$ be a tournament that admits an ordering $\prec$ such that $T^\prec = G$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:ineq}, we have $\operatorname{\chi}(G) \leqslant \operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(T) \cdot \omega(G)$ which implies $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(T) \geqslant f(k,\ell)$. By our assumption, the tournament $T$ contains a subdigraph $S \subseteq T$ such that $\operatorname{girth}(S) > \ell$ and $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(S) > k$. Then, $S^\prec$ is a subgraph of $T^\prec = G$ with $\operatorname{girth}(S^\prec) > \ell$ and $\operatorname{\chi}(S^\prec) > k$.

<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
For every integers $k \geqslant 0$ and $\ell \geqslant 3$, there exists an integer $f(k,\ell) \geqslant 0$ such that $A_{f(k,\ell)}$ contains a subdigraph $S \subseteq A_{f(k,\ell)}$ with $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(S) > k$ and $\operatorname{girth}(S) > \ell$.
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
We proceed by induction on $k$. When $k=0$, we let $f(k,\ell)=0$ and take $S = A_0$.
    Let $k \geqslant 1$ and suppose $f(k-1,\ell)$ is well-defined.
    Denote $r = |V(A_{f(k-1,\ell)})|$ and let $H$ be a $(r\ell)$-uniform hypergraph $H$ with $\operatorname{\chi}(H) > k$ and $\operatorname{girth}(H) > \ell$, which exists by \Cref{thm:Lov}.
    Denote $n=|V(H)|$ and $m=|E(H)|$ and set $f(k,\ell) = n \cdot m$.
    
    The tournament $A_{f(k,\ell)}$ is made of $f(k,\ell)$ copies $T_1, \dots, T_{f(k,\ell)}$ of $A_{f(k,\ell)-1}$ and $f(k,\ell)+1$ vertices $v_0, \dots, v_{f(k,\ell)}$ such that for $0 \leqslant i < j \leqslant f(k,\ell)$, we have $v_i \Leftarrow v_j$ and $v_i \Rightarrow T_j$, and for $1 \leqslant i < j \leqslant f(k,\ell)$, we have $T_i \Rightarrow v_j$ and $T_i \Rightarrow T_j$. As $f(k,\ell)-1 \geqslant f(k-1,\ell)$, each copy $T_i$ of $A_{f(k,\ell)-1}$ contains a copy of $A_{f(k-1,\ell)}$, and thus a subdigraph $S_i \subseteq T_i$ with $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(S_i) > k-1$ and $\operatorname{girth}(S_i) > \ell$. Without loss of generality we assume that $|V(S_i)|=r=|V(A_{f(k-1,\ell)})|$ for every $S_i$ (by keeping isolated vertices).

    Identify the $n$ vertices $v_{m}, v_{2m}, \dots, v_{nm}$ of $A_{f(k,\ell)}$ with the vertices $u_1, \dots, u_{n}$  of $H$. Denote $E(H)= \{e_1, \dots, e_{m}\}$.
    To each hyperedge $e_i = \{u_{i_1}, \dots, u_{i_{r\ell}} \}$ with $i_1 < \cdots < i_{r\ell}$, we associate a subdigraph $S'_i = S_{i_r \cdot m+i}$.
    Note that each hyperedge is associated to different subdigraph, and that we have $\{ u_{i_1}, \dots, u_{i_{r}} \} \Leftarrow S_i' \Leftarrow \{ u_{i_{r(\ell-1)+1}}, \dots, u_{i_{r\ell}} \}$.

    We claim that this subdigraph $S \subseteq A_{f(k,\ell)}$ satisfies $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(S) > k$ and $\operatorname{girth}(S) > \ell$. Suppose $S$ has a $k$-dicoloring. As the hypergraph $H$ is not $k$-colorable, there exists an hyperedge $e_i = \{u_{i_1}, \dots, u_{i_{r\ell}} \}$ whose vertices all have the same color $c$. Consider the associated subdigraph $S'_i$. As $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(S_i') > k-1$, there is a vertex $w^i_j \in S_i'$ of color $c$. Then $C^i_j$ is a monochromatic directed cycle, a contradiction. Henceforth $\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\chi}}(S) > k$.

   
    Let $C$ be a (not necessarily directed) cycle $C$ in $S$. Consider the hypergraph $H'$ with vertex set $V(S)$ and hyperedges $\{ e_i \cup S_i' : i \in [m]\}$. As the subdigraphs $S_i'$ are disjoint, we have $\operatorname{girth}(H') = \operatorname{girth}(H)$. Now every arc of $S$ is included in an hyperedge of $H'$, hence the cycle $C$ induces a closed trail in $H'$. If this trail is trivial (only one edge appears in the sequence defining the trail), that is $C$ is included in $S_i' \cup e_i$ for some $i \in [m]$, then either $C$ in contained in $S_i'$ and $|C| \geqslant \operatorname{girth}(S_i') > \ell$, or $C$ contains a cycle of the form $C^i_j$ and $|C| \geqslant |C^i_j| = \ell+1$. Otherwise the trail contains a non-trivial cycle of $H'$, thus $|C| \geqslant \operatorname{girth}(H') > \ell$.
