<!-- problem-1: body_md -->
\label{prob:main}
	Given a premaniplex $X$, does there exist a maniplex $M$ with $\operatorname{STG}(M)\cong X$?
  Can $M$ be chosen to be polytopal (that is, can $M$ be the flag graph of a polytope)?
<!-- end problem-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{th:main}
	Every finite premaniplex of rank $3$ or $4$ is the symmetry type graph of a finite maniplex.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:quotient}
	Let $X$ be a premaniplex of rank $n$. Then the following hold:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item $X\cong\mathcal U_n/\mathcal N$ for some subgroup $\mathcal N\le W_n$,
		\item $\mathcal U_n/\mathcal N$ is a maniplex if and only if $\mathcal N$ contains no
			conjugate of $r_i$ and no conjugate of $r_ir_j$, for all $i\ne j$.
	\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:aut}
	Let $\mathcal N\le W_n$. Then every automorphism of $\mathcal U_n/\mathcal N$ lifts to an automorphism of $\mathcal U_n$, 
  the lift of $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal U_n/\mathcal N)$ is equal to $\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)$. More precisely,
    $\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)$ acts on $\mathcal U_n/\mathcal N$ by $\pi(g): w\mathcal N\mapsto wg\mathcal N$. 
    This action has kernel $\mathcal N$, and the induced action of  $\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)/\mathcal N$ on
     $\mathcal U_n/\mathcal N$ is permutationally isomorphic to
    $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal U_n/\mathcal N)$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
\label{lem:cover}
	Let $\mathcal P\trianglelefteq\mathcal N\le W_n$ and let
	$\wp:\mathcal U_n/\mathcal P\rightarrow\mathcal U_n/\mathcal N$ be the map defined by $w\mathcal P\mapsto w\mathcal N$. Then
	$\wp$ is a regular covering projection with $\operatorname{CT}(\wp)=\mathcal N/\mathcal P$.
	The largest subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal U_n/\mathcal N)$ that lifts along $\wp$ is
			\[
				\bigl(\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)\cap\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal P)\bigr)/\mathcal N.
			\]
	Its lift is $\bigl(\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)\cap\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal P)\bigr)/\mathcal P$.
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
It is straightforward to check that $\wp$ is a regular covering projection with the group of covering
transformations $\mathcal N/\mathcal P$.

Assume first that $g\mathcal N \in (\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)\cap\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal P))/\mathcal N$. Then by \cref{lem:aut}, $g\mathcal N$ is an automorphism of $\mathcal U_n/\mathcal N$
and $g\mathcal P$ is an automorphism of $\mathcal U_n/\mathcal P$. Even more, by the definition of $g\mathcal N$ and $g\mathcal P$, the former is the projection
of the latter along $\wp$.


Conversely let $g\mathcal N$ be an automorphism of $\mathcal U_n/\mathcal N$ that lifts to an automorphism $h\mathcal P$ of $\mathcal U_n/\mathcal P$. 
By \cref{lem:aut},
it follows that $g \in \mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)$ and that $h \in \mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal P)$. Since $h\mathcal P$ is a lift of $g\mathcal N$ along
$\wp$, it follows that $g\mathcal N$ must map $\wp(\mathcal P) = \mathcal N$ into $\wp(g\mathcal P) = g\mathcal N$, or 
equivalently that $g\mathcal N = h\mathcal N$. Therefore $h \in \mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)$. Hence $g\mathcal N = h\mathcal N \in (\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)\cap\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal P))/\mathcal N$,
which proves the first claim.
Note that we showed that $h\mathcal P$ is a lift of $h\mathcal N$ for every $h \in \mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)\cap\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal P)$. This shows that the lift of $(\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)\cap\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal P))/\mathcal N$ is
$(\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal N)\cap\mathbf N_{W_n}(\mathcal P))/\mathcal P$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem:cover2}
Let $X$ be a finite premaniplex of rank $n$. Then there exist a finite maniplex $M$ and a
regular covering projection $\wp\colon M\rightarrow X$ along which every automorphism of $X$
lifts.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
Let $G=\mathbb{Z}_2^n$ with standard basis $e_0,\dots,e_{n-1}$, and let $\zeta$ assign to every
	dart of colour $i$ of $X$ the voltage $e_i$. Since every $e_i$ is an involution, this is
	a voltage assignment. Let $M$ 
be a connected component of the derived graph. Then $M$ is a regular cover of $X$. We claim that $M$ is a maniplex. Since $M$ is a cover of 
  $X$, it is a properly $n$-edge coloured graph. It contains no parallel edges,
  since such edges have different colour, and edges of different colours have
  voltage whose product is not $0$. Since no dart has trivial voltage, $M$ contains no semiedges. Since every $i,j$-walk $W$ for $|i-j|\ge2$ in $X$ of length $4$
  is closed and has voltage $0$, its lift $\tilde W$ is also closed in $M$. Additionally, since
  no proper non-empty subwalk of $W$ has voltage $0$, it follows that $\tilde W$ is a cycle. This proves that $M$ is a maniplex.

	Finally, let $g$ be an automorphism of $X$. As $g$ preserves the colours of
	darts and the voltage of a dart depends only on its colour, $\zeta(W^g)=\zeta(W)$, for
	every walk $W$ of $X$. Therefore $g$ lifts to an automorphism of $M$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:lifting5}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, let ${T}$ be an infinite tree, let
$\mathcal G\le\operatorname{Aut}({T})$, let $\mathcal N$ be a
non-identity normal subgroup of $\mathcal G$ of finite index such that
$\mathcal N_x=1$ for every vertex and for every edge $x$ of ${T}$,
and let $\mathcal H=\mathbf N_{\operatorname{Aut}({T})}(\mathcal N)$.
If $\mathcal H/\mathcal N$ acts faithfully on
$\mathrm{H}_1({T}/\mathcal N;\mathbb{Z})$, then there exists a normal
subgroup $\mathcal P$ of $\mathcal N$ of finite index such that
$\mathbf N_{\mathcal H}(\mathcal P)=\mathcal G$ and $\mathcal N/\mathcal P$
is a $p$-group.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{rem:H1}
	Let $\Gamma$ be a finite connected graph without semiedges in which every vertex has
	valency at least $2$ and which is not a cycle. Then $\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ acts faithfully on
	$\mathrm{H}_1(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})$.
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
It is enough to show that there
  exists no nonidentity $g\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ that fixes every cycle
  setwise, as well as their direction. Suppose that such a $g$ exists.
  
                                      
                                                      
  Note also that $g$ cannot fix all vertices and only move some dart $x$, since then either $x$ is a loop, or the edges of $x$ and $x^g$, have the same
  endvertices. In both case $g$ does not fix all cycles together with their directions.

  We first aim to show that there exists at least one vertex that is moved by $g$ and lies on a
  cycle. Suppose that there exists no such vertex. Let $u$ be a vertex that is moved by $g$ and lies on
  no cycles. Observe that it is a cut-vertex, and that each of the at least two connected
  components of $\Gamma - u$ contains a cycle. Choose vertices $a$ and $b$ lying on cycles in two different components
  of $\Gamma-u$. Then $u$ lies on every path from $a$ to $b$. By our assumption $a$ to $b$ are fixed by $g$.
The vertex $u^g$ also lies on every path from $a$ to $b$, and $u$ and $u^g$ are at the same distance from $a$.
Therefore they
  lie at the same position on any shortest path from $a$ to $b$. This implies that $u^g=u$, which is a contradiction.
  We choose $v$ to be
  a vertex that is moved by $g$ and lies on a cycle.

  Now let $C$ be the cycle containing $v$. Since $g$ fixes the cycles
  setwise, and their directions, $C$ also contains $v^g$, and $g$ induces
  a nontrivial rotation on $C$. Let $\Gamma \setminus C$ denote the graph obtained from $\Gamma$ by
  deleting the edges of $C$. Each of its components contains a vertex of $C$.
  Now we separate two options. Either there exist a walk $W$
  in $\Gamma \setminus C$ connecting two distinct vertices in $C$, or each component of
  $\Gamma \setminus C$ contains exactly one vertex of $C$. In the first case, there exists a cycle $C'$ containing $W$
  and an arc of $C$. Since $g$ also fixes $C'$ setwise, and preserves its direction,
  it fixes that arc, and hence its endvertices, contradicting that it acts on $C$ as a nontrivial rotation.
  In the second case, each component of
  $\Gamma \setminus C$
  contains a cycle whenever it contains at least two vertices. Such a component exists because
  $\Gamma$ is not a cycle. The
  components are permuted by $g$, and none of them is fixed, hence $g$ moves a cycle.

  In both cases we arrive to a contradiction, which completes the proof.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:lifting5'}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, let $W\in \{W_3, W_4\}$, let $\mathcal G\le W$ and let $\mathcal N$ be a non-identity
normal subgroup of $\mathcal G$ of finite index in $W$ that contains none of the
elements $r_i$, $r_ir_j$ for $i \ne j$,
 or their conjugates. 
 Let
$\mathcal H'=\mathbf N_{W}( \mathcal N)$. Then there exists a normal
subgroup $\mathcal P$ of $\mathcal N$ of finite index such that
$\mathbf N_{\mathcal H'}(\mathcal P)=\mathcal G$ and $\mathcal N/\mathcal P$ is a
$p$-group.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Let $T$ be the Bass-Serre tree of $W$ that is described in \cref{sec:tree}. In both cases it
	is an infinite tree without semiedges. The action of $W$ on $T$ is faithful, since its kernel is
	contained in the intersection of the vertex stabilisers, which is trivial in both cases. In particular,
	$W\le\mathrm{Aut}(T)$. Let $\mathcal H$ be the
	normaliser of $\mathcal N$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(T)$. We will check the hypotheses of \cref{thm:lifting5}
	for the tree $T$ and the group $\mathcal N\trianglelefteq\mathcal G$.

	The index of $\mathcal N$ in $\mathcal G$ is finite by assumption. Every non-trivial element of a vertex
	stabiliser of $T$ is a conjugate of some $r_i$ or of some $r_ir_j$ with $i\ne j$, so the
	vertex stabilisers, and hence also the edge stabilisers, intersect $\mathcal N$ trivially.
	Therefore $\mathcal N_x=1$ for every vertex and for every edge $x$ of $T$.

	It remains to check that $\mathcal H / \mathcal N$ acts faithfully on
	$\mathrm{H}_1(T/ \mathcal N; \mathbb{Z})$. Since $\mathcal N$ acts freely on the
	vertices of $T$, and hence on its darts, the valencies are preserved. 
  Therefore every vertex of
	$T/\mathcal N$ has valency $2$ or $4$ and at least one has valency $4$.
   Since there are no edges between vertices
   in the same $W$-orbit on $T$, $T/\mathcal N$ contains no semiedges. Note that it is connected. 
   Since $\mathcal N$ has finite
    index in $W$,
	which has finitely many orbits on the vertices and on the edges of $T$,
	 $T/ \mathcal N$ is finite.
	The group $\mathcal H/\mathcal N$ is a subgroup of the full automorphism group of the
	graph $T/\mathcal N$, hence it suffices to show that $\mathrm{Aut}(T/\mathcal N)$ acts
	faithfully on $\mathrm{H}_1(T/ \mathcal N ; \mathbb{Z})$, which it does by \cref{rem:H1}.

By \cref{thm:lifting5} there exists a normal subgroup $\mathcal P$ of $\mathcal N$ of
finite index with $\mathcal N/\mathcal P$ a $p$-group and
$\mathbf N_{\mathcal H}(\mathcal P)=\mathcal G$. Finally note that $\mathcal H'=\mathcal H\cap W$,
so $\mathbf N_{\mathcal H'}(\mathcal P)=\mathbf N_{\mathcal H}(\mathcal P)\cap W
=\mathcal G\cap W$. Since $\mathcal G\le W$, it follows that $\mathbf N_{\mathcal H'}(\mathcal P) = \mathcal G$.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- theorem-4: body_md -->
\label{th:lift}
	Let $X$ be a finite premaniplex of rank $3$ or $4$ and let $G\le\mathrm{Aut}(X)$. Then there
	exist a finite maniplex $M$ and a regular covering projection $\wp\colon M\rightarrow X$
	such that
	such that $\mathrm{Aut}(M)$ is the lift of $G$ along $\wp$.
<!-- end theorem-4 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
By \cref{lem:cover2}, there exists a cover $\wp': M_1 \rightarrow X$, where $M_1$ is a maniplex, such that every automorphism of $X$ lifts to $M_1$. Let $\hat G = \wp'^{-1}(G)$. We aim to show that the maniplex $M_1$ admits a finite regular cover $\wp'': M \rightarrow M_1$, such that $\mathrm{Aut}(M) = \wp''^{-1}(\hat G)$. 

    Let $W$ and $\mathcal U$ be the universal string Coxeter group and the universal maniplex of the
	same rank as $X$.  
    Let $\mathcal N$ be a subgroup of $W$ such that 
  $\mathcal U/\mathcal N \cong M_1$, let $\mathcal G$ be the lift of $\hat G$ to $\mathbf N_W(\mathcal N)$ (so that
  $\mathcal G/\mathcal N=\hat G$), let $f:=[W:\mathcal N]$ be the number of flags of $M_1$, let $p$ be an odd prime with $p>f$, and
	let $\mathcal H':=\mathbf N_{W}(\mathcal N)$. The subgroup $\mathcal N$ is normal in $\mathcal G$ and of finite index $f$ in
	$W$, and it is non-trivial because $W$ is infinite. Since $\mathcal U/\mathcal N\cong M_1$ is a maniplex,
	$\mathcal N$ contains none of the elements $r_i$, $r_ir_j$ for $i\ne j$, or their conjugates by
	\cref{lem:quotient}. By \cref{thm:lifting5'} there exists a normal
	subgroup $\mathcal P$ of $\mathcal N$ of finite index with $\mathbf N_{W}(\mathcal P) \cap \mathbf N_{W}(\mathcal N)= \mathbf N_{\mathcal H'}(\mathcal P) = \mathcal G$ and 
     $\mathcal N/\mathcal P$ a $p$-group. Let $M = \mathcal U/\mathcal P$ and $\wp'': M \rightarrow M_1$ be the regular covering projection as defined in \cref{lem:cover}. Note that the largest automorphism group that lifts along $\wp''$ is 
     \[(\mathbf N_W(\mathcal N) \cap \mathbf N_W(\mathcal P))/\mathcal N =  \mathcal G/\mathcal N = \hat G.\] 
     
     Next we need to show that that the lift of $\hat G$ along $\wp''$ is the whole automorphism group of $M$, or equivalently that  \[(\mathbf N_W(\mathcal N) \cap \mathbf N_W(\mathcal P))/\mathcal P =  \mathbf N_W(\mathcal P)/\mathcal P.\]
     Let $Q = \mathbf N_W(\mathcal P)$. Since $\mathcal N$ has index $f$ in $W$, and $f < p$, it has index less than $p$ in $Q$. Recall that $\mathcal N/\mathcal P$ is a $p$-group, therefore it is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $Q/\mathcal P$. We use this in a similar way to \cite{PotocnikSpiga2019}.
     Let $n_p$ denote the number of
	Sylow $p$-subgroups of $Q/\mathcal P$. Then $n_p$ is congruent to $1$ modulo $p$, and it divides
	the index of $\mathcal N/ \mathcal P$ in $Q/\mathcal P$. Since this index is smaller than $p$, it follows that $n_p$ is $1$. Hence $\mathcal N/\mathcal P$ is the unique Sylow $p$-subgroup of $Q/\mathcal P$. In particular, $\mathcal N/\mathcal P \trianglelefteq Q/\mathcal P$.  Hence by the third isomorphism theorem, $N \trianglelefteq \mathbf N_W(\mathcal P) = Q$. This implies that $\mathbf N_W(\mathcal P) \le \mathbf N_W(\mathcal N)$, and 
    hence $\mathbf N_W(\mathcal N) \cap \mathbf N_W(\mathcal P))/\mathcal P =  \mathbf N_W(\mathcal P)/\mathcal P$.

    We showed that the largest group of automorphisms of $M$ that lifts along $\wp''$ is $\hat G$, and that every automorphism of $M$ projects to $M_1$. Let $\wp := \wp'\circ \wp''$. A composition of covering projections is a covering projection. Since $\wp'{-1}({\mathrm{id}}) \le \hat G$, it lifts along $\wp''$. Hence $\operatorname{CT}(\wp)$ acts regularly on the fibres of the projection, and $\wp$ is regular. We have shown that $\mathrm{Aut}(M) = \wp''^{-1}(\hat G) = \wp^{-1}(G)$. This completes the proof.
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- remark-1: body_md -->
Note that the same method does not work for higher ranks.
  We could generalize \cref{thm:lifting5'} to the statement:
  let $p$ be
	an odd prime, let $W$ be a group that acts faithfully on an infinite tree $T$ that is not
	a line and in which at least one vertex has degree at least $3$, and every vertex has degree at least $2$, with finite vertex- and edge-
	stabilisers and with finitely many orbits on the vertices and on the edges. Let
	$\mathcal G\le W$, let $\mathcal N$ be a torsion-free normal subgroup of $\mathcal G$ of finite index in $W$ and
	let $\mathcal H=\mathbf N_{W}(\mathcal N)$. Then there exists a normal subgroup $\mathcal P$ of $\mathcal N$ of finite index
	such that $\mathbf N_{\mathcal H}(\mathcal P)=\mathcal G$ and $\mathcal N/\mathcal P$ is a $p$-group. 

  However, the groups $W_n$ for $n \ge 5$ are not virtually free, and
  therefore do not act on a tree in the required way.
