<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A manifold $(M,g_{ab})$ is called a \textbf{conformal Ricci soliton} (CRS) if there exists a conformal change of metric $\tilde{g}_{ab}=e^{2u}g_{ab}$, with $u\in C^{\infty}(M)$, such that the conformal metric satisfies the RS equation \eqref{ricci-soliton} \cite{Catino1}. The quintuple $(M,g_{ab},u,y^a,\lambda)$ is called the CRS structure.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
A rank--two conformal Killing Yano (CKY) tensor on an $n$--dimensional pseudo--Riemannian manifold is a 2--form $Y_{ab}$ satisfying \cite{Kashiwada1,Kashiwada2}
\begin{align}\label{cky-eq}
\nabla_cY_{ab}&=2g_{c[a}\xi_{b]}+z_{abc},\nonumber\\
z_{abc}&=z_{[abc]}=(dY)_{abc},\\
\xi_a&=\frac{1}{n-1}\nabla^bY_{ba}.\nonumber
\end{align}
The equation \eqref{cky-eq} is the (rank 2) CKY equation, and the vector field $\xi_a$ obtained from the divergence of $Y_{ab}$ will be called the CKY vector field. The notation $\xi[Y]$ will sometimes be used to indicate $\xi^a$ is the vector field associated to the CKY 2--form $Y_{ab}$. Furthermore, since $\xi^a$ is the divergence of a 2--form, it is divergence--free.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{cky-rs-structure}
A sextuple $(M,g_{ab},u,Y_{ab},\xi^a[Y],\lambda)$ is called a \textbf{CKY Ricci soliton} (CKY--RS or CKY--RS structure) if the CRS structure $(M,g_{ab},u,y^a,\lambda)$ is sourced by the CKY vector field $y^a=\xi^a[Y]$ of the CKY 2--form. That is, the underlying vector field is geometrically fixed by $Y_{ab}$. The CKY--RS will be called trivial if $u$ is constant, i.e. the conformal transformation is homothetic.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{th2}
Let $(M,g_{ab},u,Y_{ab},\lambda)$ be a 4--dimensional Lorentzian CKY--RS spacetime with a non-degenerate CCKY 2--form $Y_{ab}$. Let the CKY vector field $\xi^a$ be a CKV for $g_{ab}$, and suppose
\begin{align}
du\wedge Y=0.\label{compa1}
\end{align}
Then, $\tilde g_{ab}$ is locally Kerr--NUT--(A)dS, and the Weyl tensor of $g_{ab}$ is of Petrov type D or O.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemma-4}
Let $g_{ab}$ belong locally to the 4--dimensional Lorentzian Kerr--NUT--(A)dS class and let $Y_{ab}$ denote its principal CCKY 2-form. Then there exists no non-trivial conformal deformation $\tilde g_{ab}=e^{2u}g_{ab}$ for which $ \tilde Y=e^{3u}Y $ is again a principal CCKY 2--form.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Since $\tilde Y_{ab}$ is closed,
\begin{align*}
0=d\tilde Y=d(e^{3u}Y)=3e^{3u}du\wedge Y,
\end{align*}
where we have used $dY=0$. Hence $du\wedge Y=0$. Since $Y_{ab}$ is non-degenerate, and dimension is four, the map $\beta\mapsto\beta\wedge Y$, for a 1-form $\beta$, is an isomorphism. Hence, $du=0$ and $u$ is constant. Hence the CKY--RS structure is trivial.\hfill \nobreak \ifvmode \relax \else

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->

Let $g_{ab}$ belong locally to the 4--dimensional Lorentzian Kerr--NUT--(A)dS class and let $Y_{ab}$ denote its principal CCKY 2-form. Any CKY--RS structure with soliton vector field $\xi[Y]$ is necessarily trivial.

<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{cky-rs-flow}
Let $(M,g_{ab})$ be an Einstein manifold and $Y_{ab}$ a CKY 2--form on $(M,g_{ab})$ with associated vector field $\xi$. Define the set
\begin{align*}
\Sigma_{\xi}:=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}:\exists \psi>0, \rm d\psi\not\equiv0,\mathcal{L}_\xi\psi=\alpha\psi\}.
\end{align*}
Then any CKY--RS structure compatible with $Y$ produces a pair $(\alpha,\psi)\in \Sigma_{\xi}$, with $\alpha=c-\lambda$.
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
Let $(M,g_{ab})$ be an Einstein manifold and $Y_{ab}$ a CKY 2--form on $(M,g_{ab})$ with associated vector field $\xi$. If a non-trivial CKY--RS structure compatible with $Y_{ab}$ exists with $\widetilde g=\phi^{-2}g$, with $\phi>0$, then there exists a constant $\alpha\in\mathbb R$ such that $\mathcal{L}_\xi\phi=\alpha\phi$. Consequently, if the $\mathcal{L}_{\xi}$ admits no positive non-constant eigenfunction for any real eigenvalue, then $g_{ab}$ admits no non-trivial CKY--RS structure compatible with $Y_{ab}$.
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proposition-3: body_md -->
et $(M,g_{ab},u,Y_{ab},\lambda)$ be a CKY--RS structure with $g$ Einstein. Then,
\begin{align}
K&=\frac{\Lambda}{n-1}-\frac{q}{\phi},\label{K-form}\\
(n-1)|\nabla\phi|^2&=-(\Lambda\phi^2+c)+2(n-1)q\phi,\label{first-int-1}
\end{align}
for some constant $q$.
<!-- end proposition-3 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
For a Hessian equation of the form $\phi_{ab}=\psi g_{ab}$, for some function $\psi$, commuting derivatives and using the Ricci identities gives
\begin{align*}
R_{cabd}^{\ \ \ \ d}\phi_d=g_{ab}\psi_c-g_{cb}\psi_a.
\end{align*}
Tracing over $c$ and $b$ and using $R_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab}$ gives $\psi_a=-(\Lambda/(n-1))\phi_a$, which integrates to
\begin{align}
\psi=-\frac{\Lambda}{(n-1)}\phi +q,
\end{align}
for some constant $q$. Setting $\psi=-K\phi$ gives \eqref{K-form}. Substituting \eqref{K-form} into \eqref{bec-2} and using the definition of $\mathcal{G}$ gives \eqref{first-int-1}.\hfill\nobreak \ifvmode \relax \else

<!-- proposition-5: body_md -->
\label{c-c}
Let $(M,g_{ab},u,Y_{ab},\lambda)$ be a Einstein CKY--RS structure, and let $\phi=e^{-u}$. If $d\phi\neq0$, then $\mathcal{W}_{abc}^{\ \ \ d}\phi_d=0$.
<!-- end proposition-5 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Commute derivatives on the Hessian BEC to get
\begin{align}
R_{abc}^{\ \ \ d}\phi_d=K(g_{ac}\phi_b-g_{bc}\phi_a).\label{reduced-0}
\end{align} 
Since $R_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab}$, using the decomposition of the Riemann tensor into its Weyl and Ricci parts we get
\begin{align}
R_{abc}^{\ \ \ d}\phi_d=\mathcal{W}_{abc}^{\ \ \ d}\phi_d+\frac{\Lambda}{(n-1)}(g_{ac}\phi_b-g_{bc}\phi_a).
\end{align}
Comparing this to \eqref{reduced-0} gives the Weyl-alignment condition $\mathcal{W}_{abc}^{\ \ \ d}\phi_d=0$.\hfill\nobreak \ifvmode \relax \else

<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{th-17}
Let $(M,g_{ab},u,Y_{ab},\lambda)$ be a Einstein CKY--RS structure, and suppose $d\phi\neq0$. Set $\alpha=c-\lambda$. Then, either 
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\alpha=0$, in which case $\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi=0$ and $\xi^a$ is a KV for both $g_{ab}$ and $\tilde g_{ab}$; or
\item $\alpha\neq0$, in which case $q=c=0$, $\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\tilde g_{ab}=-2\alpha \tilde g_{ab}$, and $\tilde g_{ab}$ is Ricci-flat. 
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Differentiating the eigenvalue/compatibility equation $\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi=\alpha\phi$  and using the BEC gives
\begin{align*}
(\nabla_a\xi_b)\phi^b=\alpha\phi_a+K\phi\xi_a.\label{funda-new-1}

<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm-compact}
Let $(M,g_{ab},u,Y_{ab},\lambda)$ be a compact Riemannian Einstein CKY--RS structure, and let $d\phi\neq0$. Then $\alpha=0$, $\Lambda>0$, $(M,g_{ab})$ is isometric to the round sphere of radius $r=\sqrt{(n-1)/\Lambda}$, and
\begin{align*}
\phi=\bar q+\bar\phi,\quad \bar\phi_{ab}=-r^{-2}\bar\phi g_{ab},\quad \bar q>\bar\phi_{\rm {max}}, \quad c=\Lambda(\bar q^2-\bar\phi^2_{\rm {max}}).
\end{align*}
Consequently, the conformal metric $\tilde g_{ab}$ is the round sphere.

Since $\xi^a$ is Killing, it is volume preserving and hence  
\begin{align*}
\int_M\xi(\phi)\,\mbox{dVol}_g=\alpha\int_M\phi\mbox{dVol}_g=0.
\end{align*}
Thus, $\alpha=0$. If $\Lambda=0$, $\Delta\phi=nb$. Integrating gives $q=0$, and therefore $\Delta\phi=0$, which gives $\phi$ is constant, contradicting $d\phi\neq0$. If $\Lambda\neq0$, define
\begin{align*}
\bar q=\frac{(n-1)}{\Lambda}q,\quad\bar\phi=\phi-\bar q.
\end{align*} 
This brings the BEC into the Obata form
 \begin{align*}
\bar\phi_{ab}=-\frac{\Lambda}{n-1}\bar\phi g_{ab}. 
\end{align*}
If $\Lambda<0$, then 
 \begin{align*}
\Delta\bar\phi=\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right)|\Lambda|\bar\phi,
\end{align*}
and the maximum principle forces $\bar\phi\equiv0$, contradicting $d\phi\neq0$. If $\Lambda>0$, then by Obata's theorem \cite{Obata1} $(M,g_{ab})$ is isometric to the round sphere with radius $r=\sqrt{(n-1)/\Lambda}$. Since

|\nabla\bar\phi|^2=r^{-2}(\bar\phi^2_{\rm {max}}-\bar\phi^2).
\end{align*}

\label{th-108}
Let $(M,g_{ab},u,Y_{ab},\lambda)$ be an Einstein CKY--RS structure, and suppose $d\phi\neq0$ on a neighborhood $U$ of a point $p\in M$. 
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $|\nabla\phi|^2\neq0$ at $p$, define $\epsilon=\operatorname{sgn}(|\nabla\phi|^2)\in\{\pm1\}$. Then there exists a coordinate $\tau$ with $|\nabla\tau|^2=\epsilon$ and $\phi=\phi(\tau),\dot{\phi}>0$ such that locally,
\begin{align*}
g&=\epsilon\,d\tau^2+\bar\gamma(\tau)^2h,\quad c_0\bar\gamma=\dot{\phi},\quad\ddot{\phi}=\epsilon\left(q-\frac{\Lambda}{n-1}\phi\right),\\
R^h_{AB}&=\mu h_{AB},\quad \mu=\frac{\epsilon}{c_0^{2}}(n-2)\left(q^2-\frac{\Lambda c}{(n-1)^2}\right),\quad \dot{\phi}=\frac{d\phi}{d\tau},
\end{align*}
with $c_0$ a non-zero constant.

\item $\Lambda=0$: for $q=0$, $\bar\gamma$ is constant and $g=\epsilon d\tau^2+h$ with $h_{AB}$ Ricci-flat; for $q\neq0$, $\bar\gamma=\tau$ after a shift of $\tau$ and $c_0=\epsilon q$, so $g=\epsilon d\tau^2+\tau^2h$ with $R^h_{AB}=\epsilon(n-2)h_{AB}$ (Ricci-flat cone).

\item $\Lambda\neq0$: set $k=\sqrt{|\Lambda|/(n-1)}$ and $\mathcal{D}:=\bar q^2-c/\Lambda$. Then, for $\dot{\phi}>0$,
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
$\epsilon\Lambda>0$ & $\quad\bar\phi=\mathcal{A}_1\cos k\tau+\mathcal{A}_2\sin k\tau=\sqrt{\mathcal{D}}\cos k(\tau-\tau_0)$, & $\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{A}_1^2+\mathcal{A}_2^2$\\
$\epsilon\Lambda<0$ & $\quad\bar\phi=\mathcal{B}_1e^{k\tau}+\mathcal{B}_2e^{-k\tau}$, & $\mathcal{D}=4\mathcal{B}_1\mathcal{B}_2$\\

\end{tabular}

\item If $\alpha\neq0$ and $|\nabla\phi|^2\neq0$, then $q=c=0$, $\epsilon\Lambda<0$, $\phi=\phi_0e^{k\tau}$, $g=\epsilon d\tau^2+e^{2k\tau}h$ with $h_{ab}$ Ricci--flat, and $\tilde g=\phi_0^{-2}(\epsilon\,ds^2+h)$, where $s=-(1/k)e^{-k\tau}$. \textit{1.} The metric splitting follows from standard arguments. (See the references \cite{Brinkmann2, Tashiro1, Kuhnel1}, adapted to the Riemannian setting. We provide a short proof here for completeness, covering the Lorentzian setting as well.)

Let $\tau$ be a function and define $\sqrt{||\nabla\phi|^2|}d\tau=d\phi$. Then $|\nabla\tau|^2=\epsilon$ and $\phi=\phi(\tau)$ with $\dot{\phi}=\sqrt{||\nabla\phi|^2|}>0$, and hence $|\nabla\phi|^2=\epsilon\dot{\phi}^2$. Now, since $\nabla_a\tau$ has constant norm, it is geodesic. Since 
\begin{align*}
\phi_{ab}=\ddot{\phi}\,\tau_a\tau_b+\dot{\phi}\,\tau_{ab},
\end{align*}
using $\phi_{ab}=\psi g_{ab}$ with $\psi=-(\Lambda/(n-1))\phi+q$,  and contracting with the product $\tau^a\tau^b$ gives $\ddot{\phi}=\epsilon\psi$. Thus
\begin{align*}
\mbox{Hess}(\tau)=\frac{\psi}{\dot{\phi}}(g-\epsilon\,d\tau^2),
\end{align*}
from which it follows that the level sets of $\tau$ are totally umbilic with mean curvature $\psi/\dot{\phi}$ being $\tau$-dependent only. Therefore $g_{ab}$ splits as
\begin{align}
g=\epsilon d\tau^2+\bar\gamma(\tau)^2h,\label{split-met}
\end{align}
for some function $\bar\gamma(\tau)$. This metric \eqref{split-met} has $\Gamma^\tau_{AB}=-\epsilon \bar\gamma\dot{\bar\gamma}h_{AB}$, which gives
\begin{align*}
\phi_{AB}=-\dot{\phi}\Gamma^\tau_{AB}=\epsilon \bar\gamma\dot{\bar\gamma}\dot{\phi}h_{AB}.
\end{align*}
From the BEC 
\begin{align*}
\phi_{AB}=\psi g_{AB}=\psi \bar\gamma^2h_{AB}=\epsilon\ddot{\phi}\bar\gamma^2h_{AB},
\end{align*}
it follows $\dot{\bar\gamma}/\bar\gamma=\ddot{\phi}/\dot{\phi}$. Hence, $c_0\bar\gamma=\dot{\phi}$ for constant $c_0$.

The components of the Ricci tensor o metric of this form \eqref{split-met} are computed explicitly as \cite[Corollary 7.43]{ONeill1}
\begin{align*}
R_{\tau\tau}=-(n-1)\frac{\ddot{\bar\gamma}}{\bar\gamma},\qquad R_{AB}=R^h_{AB}-\epsilon(\bar\gamma\ddot{\bar\gamma}+(n-2)\dot{\bar\gamma}^2)h_{AB}.
\end{align*}
Since $R_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab}$, 
\begin{align*}
R_{\tau\tau}=\epsilon\Lambda h_{AB},\quad R_{AB}=\Lambda\bar\gamma^2h_{AB},
\end{align*}
from which we obtain $(n-1)\ddot{\bar\gamma}=-\epsilon\Lambda \bar\gamma$ and 
\begin{align*}
R^h_{AB}&=(\Lambda \bar\gamma^2+\epsilon\,(\bar\gamma\ddot{\bar\gamma}+(n-2)\dot{\bar\gamma}^2))h_{AB}\\
&=(n-2)\left(\frac{\Lambda}{n-1}\bar\gamma^2+\frac{\epsilon}{c_0^2}\psi^2\right)h_{AB}\\
&=\frac{\epsilon}{c_0^2}(n-2)\left(\frac{\Lambda}{n-1}\frac{c_0^2\bar\gamma^2}{\epsilon}+\psi^2\right)h_{AB}\\
&=\frac{\epsilon}{c_0^2}(n-2)\left(\frac{\Lambda}{n-1}\frac{\dot{\phi}^2}{\epsilon}+\psi^2\right)h_{AB}\\
&=\frac{\epsilon}{c_0^2}(n-2)\left(\frac{\Lambda}{n-1}|\nabla\phi|^2+\psi^2\right)h_{AB}\\
&=\frac{\epsilon}{c_0^2}(n-2)\left(q^2-\frac{\Lambda}{(n-1)^2}c\right)h_{AB}.
\end{align*}
where in the last line we have also used the first integral \eqref{first-int-1}.

Y=r\epsilon_{AB},\label{unique-ccky}

\xi^A=-\epsilon^{AB}\nabla_B r,\quad \xi^i=0.\label{kodoma}
