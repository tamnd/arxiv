<!-- thm-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:split_semiab}
Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a split semiabelian variety of dimension $n\ge 2$ and let $\mathcal{C} \subseteq \mathcal{G}$ be an irreducible curve defined over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ and suppose that $\mathcal{C}$ is not contained in a proper algebraic subgroup.
Consider the set $\mathcal{C}^{[1, sing]}$ of points $P \in \mathcal{C}$ such that there is a proper algebraic subgroup $H$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $P$ lies in $\mathcal{C} \cap H$;
\item the tangent space $T_P\mathcal{C}$ of $\mathcal{C}$ at $P$ is included in the tangent space $T_P H$ of $H$ at $P$ (namely, the curve $\mathcal{C}$ is tangent to $H$ at $P$).
\end{enumerate}
Then, $\mathcal{C}^{[1, sing]}$ is a finite set.
<!-- end thm-1 -->
<!-- thm-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:split_semiab_reformulated}
Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a split semiabelian variety of dimension $n \ge 2$, and let $\mathcal{C}$ be an irreducible subvariety of $\mathcal{G}$ of dimension 1, not contained in any proper algebraic subgroup, where everything is defined over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.
Let $\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}$ and, for any $W \subseteq \mathcal{G}$ smooth subvariety, $\widetilde{W}$ be as above.
Then, the set
\[
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{[2]}:= \bigcup_{\substack{H < \mathcal{G}\\ \dim H \leq \dim \mathcal{G} - 1}} \widetilde{\mathcal{C}} \cap \widetilde{H} \subseteq \mathbb{P} T\mathcal{G},
\]
where $H$ varies through the proper algebraic subgroups of $\mathcal{G}$, is a finite set.

\label{conj:sing_inters_const_higher_dim}
Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a semiabelian variety, and let $V$ be an irreducible smooth subvariety of $\mathcal{G}$, not contained in any proper algebraic subgroup, where everything is defined over $\mathbb{C}$.

<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lemma:invariant_isogeny}
Let $\mathcal{G}$ and $\mathcal{G}'$ be split semiabelian varieties, and let $f: \mathcal{G}' \to \mathcal{G}$ be an isogeny.
Moreover, let $\mathcal{C} \subseteq \mathcal{G}$ be a curve satisfying the assumptions of Theorem \ref{thm:split_semiab}.
Then, if the claim of Theorem \ref{thm:split_semiab} holds for all irreducible components of $f^{-1}(\mathcal{C})$, it holds for $\mathcal{C}$.

<!-- thm-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:main}
Let $G$ be $\mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}$ or $E$ for some elliptic curve $E$ defined over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.
Let $n\ge 2$ be an integer, $\mathcal{G} = G^n$, and let $\mathcal{C} \subseteq \mathcal{G}$ be a curve defined over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ and suppose that $\mathcal{C}$ is not contained in a proper algebraic subgroup.
Consider the set $\mathcal{C}^{[1, sing]}$ of points $P \in \mathcal{C}$ such that there is a one-codimensional algebraic subgroup $H$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $P$ is in $\mathcal{C} \cap H$;
\item the tangent space $T_P\mathcal{C}$ of $\mathcal{C}$ at $P$ is included in the tangent space $T_PH$ of $H$ at $P$ (namely, the curve $\mathcal{C}$ is tangent to $H$ at $P$).
\end{enumerate}
Then, $\mathcal{C}^{[1, sing]}$ is a finite set.

<!-- prop-1: body_md -->
\
\begin{enumerate}
\item All the algebraic subgroups of $\mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}^n$ of codimension $d$ are of the form $\ker(\varphi_A)$ for some $A \in \operatorname{Mat}_{d\times n}(\mathbb{Z})$ of maximal rank.
\item Let $H \subseteq \mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}^n$ be an irreducible component of an algebraic subgroup. Then, $H=\omega T$, where $T$ is an irreducible algebraic subgroup of $\mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}^n$ and $\omega$ is a torsion point.
\item Let $W \subseteq \mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}^n$ be an irreducible component of an algebraic coset, i.e. a translate of an algebraic subgroup. Then, $W = c T$,  where $T$ is an irreducible algebraic subgroup and $c$ is any point in $\mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}^n$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end prop-1 -->
<!-- defn-1: body_md -->
We say that $H \subseteq \mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}^n$ is a \emph{special subvariety} or \emph{torsion coset} if it is an irreducible component of an algebraic subgroup of $\mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}^n$;
we say that $W \subseteq \mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}^n$ is a \emph{weakly special subvariety} if it is an irreducible component of an algebraic coset.
Note that weakly special subvarieties are exactly the irreducible algebraic cosets.
<!-- end defn-1 -->
<!-- prop-2: body_md -->
\
\label{prop:elliptic_subgroups_structure}
\begin{enumerate}
\item All algebraic subgroups of $E^n$ of codimension $d$ are \emph{contained} in subgroups of the same codimension of the form $\ker(\varphi_A)$ for some $A \in \operatorname{Mat}_{d \times n}(\operatorname{End}(E))$ of maximal rank.
\item Let $H \subseteq E^n$ be an irreducible component of an algebraic subgroup. 
Then, $H=\omega + A$, where $A$ is an irreducible abelian subvariety and $\omega$ is a torsion point.
\item Let $W \subseteq E^n$ be an irreducible component of an algebraic coset, i.e. a translate of an algebraic subgroup. 
Then, $W = c + A$,  where $A$ is an irreducible abelian subvariety and $c$ is any point in $E^n$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end prop-2 -->
<!-- defn-2: body_md -->
We say that $H \subseteq E^n$ is a \emph{special subvariety} or \emph{torsion coset} if it is an irreducible component of algebraic subgroup of $E^n$;
we say that $W \subseteq E^n$ is a \emph{weakly special subvariety} if it is an irreducible component of an algebraic coset.
Note again that weakly special subvarieties are exactly the irreducible algebraic cosets.

\label{coroll:tangentplane}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a curve in $\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}^n$ or $\mathcal{G}=E^n$ for some elliptic curve $E$, all defined over a number field $K$, 
and let $W=\Hvar{p}[c]$ be a one-codimensional coset.

Then, $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (P)$ is contained in the hyperplane $L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{n-1}= \mathbb{P} T_e \mathcal{G}$ given by the equation:

\[

\]
where $z_i=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}$ are the (usual) coordinates of $\mathbb{P} T_e \mathcal{G}$.

<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Up to removing a finite number of points, we may assume $\mathcal{C}$ to be a smooth curve, so that $\gamma_\mathcal{C}$ is defined everywhere.
Up to enlarging $W$, we may assume that $W=\Hvar{p}[c]$ for some $\underline{p} \in \operatorname{End}(G)^n$ and $c \in G(\overline{K})$.
We will denote by $L$ the hyperplane cut by $p_1 z_1+\cdots + p_n z_n = 0$ in $\mathbb{P} T_0 \mathcal{G} = \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$.

Notice that the image $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$ cannot be contained in $L$.
Indeed, consider the map $\varphi_{\underline{p}}:\mathcal{G} \to G$ given by
\[
\varphi_{\underline{p}}: (x_1, \dots, x_n) \mapsto x_1^{p_1} \cdots x_n^{p_n}
\]
in the toric case
(and, respectively, $p_1 x_1 + \dots + p_n x_n$ in the elliptic case) and its differential $\text{d}{\varphi}$.
If by contradiction $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C}) \subseteq L$, we have that $\text{d}{\varphi}$ is constantly zero on $\mathcal{C}$, and this means that $\varphi \raisebox{-.5ex}{$|$}_{\mathcal{C}}$ is constant. 
Hence $\mathcal{C}$ is contained in the 
algebraic coset $\Hvar{p}[c']$, for some $c' \in G$, which is in contradiction with the assumption that $\mathcal{C}$ is not contained into any proper coset.
\medskip

Now, let $P \in \mathcal{C} \cap W$ be a point such that $\mathcal{C}$ is tangent to $W$ at $P$. 
Then $\gamma_\mathcal{C}(P)$ is in $L \cap \gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$ and the latter is a finite set, as $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$ is not contained in $L$.
We have two possibilities:

\begin{enumerate}

\item $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$ is a point: this means that $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$ is exactly $\{\gamma_\mathcal{C} (P)\}$ and therefore $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$ is contained in $L$;
as already remarked, this cannot happen under our assumptions.

\item $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$ is a curve: observe that in this case there is an upper bound on the cardinality $\abs{L \cap \gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})}$ given by Bezout's Theorem;
such an upper bound does not depend on $L$, and is exactly the degree $d$ of $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$ with respect to the line bundle $O(1)$ (and the following holds: any hyperplane intersects $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$ in at most $d$ points and a generic hyperplane intersects $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$ in exactly $d$ points).

As $L$ is defined over $K$ (since by assumption $\operatorname{End}(G) \subseteq K$) and $\gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$ is defined over $K$, this means that:
\[
[K(\gamma_\mathcal{C} (P)):K] \le \abs{L \cap \gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})} \le d.
\]
It follows that $[K(P):K] \le d \cdot \deg{\gamma_\mathcal{C}}$, where $\deg{\gamma_\mathcal{C}}$ is the degree of the morphism of curves given by $\gamma_\mathcal{C}: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \gamma_\mathcal{C} (\mathcal{C})$.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- prop-8: body_md -->
\label{prop:hyperplaneheight}

Let $s$ be a positive integer and $\mathcal{C}' \subseteq \mathbb{P}^s$ be an irreducible curve defined over a number field $K$. Then, there is a constant $\delta'>0$, depending only on $\mathcal C'$, such that, for every hyperplane $L_{\underline{p}}$  given by the equation:
\[
p_0 z_0+p_1 z_1+\cdots+p_s z_s=0,
\]
with $\underline{p}=(p_0,p_1,\ldots,p_s) \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{s+1}\setminus \{\underline{0}\}$, not containing $\mathcal C'$, and for every point $P \in \mathcal{C}' \cap L$, one has
\[
h(P) \le \delta'(1+h(\underline{p})),
\]
where $h(\underline{p})$ is the projective height of $\underline{p} \in \mathbb{P}^s$.
<!-- end prop-8 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Recall that the Grassmannian $(\mathbb{P}^{s})^\vee$ parameterizing hyperplanes in $\mathbb{P}^s$, also known as its dual, is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^s$.
This isomorphism is induced by the choice of a projective basis: we choose the canonical one, and denote by $z_0, \dots z_s$ the coordinates (with respect to this basis) of the starting $\mathbb{P}^s$ and with $p_0,\ldots,p_s$ the coordinates in the Grassmanian $(\mathbb{P}^{s})^\vee$ with respect to the dual basis.
In other words, the point $[p_0 : \dots : p_s] \in (\mathbb{P}^{s})^\vee$ represents the hyperplane $L_{\underline{p}}$ of $\mathbb{P}^s$ given by the equation $p_0 z_0+p_1 z_1+\cdots+p_s z_s=0$.
\medskip

Let $V \subseteq (\mathbb{P}^{s})^\vee$ be the subset consisting of $\underline{p} \in (\mathbb{P}^{s})^\vee$ such that $L_{\underline{p}}$ does not contain $\mathcal{C}'$; 
$V$ is an open subset of $(\mathbb{P}^{s})^\vee$ (since the requirement of containing a prescribed point is a closed condition and therefore $(\mathbb{P}^{s})^\vee \setminus V$ is an intersection of closed sets).

Our strategy consists in constructing some morphisms which behave well with respect to height in order to bound the height of $P$ from the height of $\underline{p}$.
\medskip

Let us consider $Sym^d(\mathcal{C}') = (\mathcal{C}')^d/S_d$ the $d$-th symmetric power of $\mathcal{C}'$.
From \cite[Prop IV.1.5, p.180]{Knutson71}, $Sym^d(\mathcal{C}')$ is a quasi-projective variety, so we can assume that it is embedded into a projective space, from which it inherits a height function. \footnote{A priori the constants that appear from now on will, and do in fact, depend on the choice of such projective embedding. However, the proof by Knutson construct an explicit projective embedding, and we may assume to have chosen that one.}


We can suppose that $\mathcal{C}'$ is closed in $\mathbb{P}^s$ (this would just enlarge $\mathcal{C}' \cap L_{\underline{p}}$). 
By Bézout's Theorem, there is a positive integer $d$ such that, for any $\underline{p} \in V$, we have that $L_{\underline{p}}$ intersects $\mathcal{C}'$ in precisely $d$ points (counted with multiplicity). This gives rise to the following morphism, defined over $K$:
\[
\begin{matrix}
\phi_1: &V &\rightarrow &Sym^d(\mathcal{C}') \\
&(\underline{p}) & \mapsto &\mathcal{C}' \cap_m L_{\underline{p}},
\end{matrix}
\]

where $\mathcal{C}' \cap_m L_{\underline{p}}$ denotes the schematic intersection of $\mathcal{C}'$ and $L_{\underline{p}}$, taking into account multiplicities.
Since $Sym^d(\mathcal{C}')=(\mathcal{C}')^d/S_d$ is the quotient of $(\mathcal{C}')^d$ by the finite group $S_d$, the quotient map defines a finite morphism, defined over $K$:
\[
\begin{matrix}
\phi_2:&(\mathcal{C}')^d &\rightarrow  &Sym^d(\mathcal{C}')\;\;\\
&(P_1,\ldots,P_d) &\mapsto &(P_1,\ldots,P_d)_{no}
\end{matrix}
\]

where the $d$-uple is ordered in the LHS and non-ordered in the RHS (we use the subscript $\text{}_{no}$ to denote this).
Finally, we consider the projection on the first coordinate:
\[\begin{matrix}
\phi_3: &(\mathcal{C}')^d  &\rightarrow &\mathcal{C}' \\
&(P_1,\ldots,P_d) &\mapsto& P_1
\end{matrix}\]

To summarize, we have the following diagram:
\[\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=-0.5em]
	& {\,(\mathcal{C}')^d\,} && {\hspace{-0.5 em} V\subseteq(\mathbb{P}^{s})^\vee} \\
	{\quad\mathcal{C}'\quad} && {\hspace{-1 em}Sym^d(\mathcal{C}') \hspace{-1em}}
	\arrow["{\phi_3}"', from=1-2, to=2-1]
	\arrow["{\phi_2}", from=1-2, to=2-3]
	\arrow["{\phi_1}"', from=1-4, to=2-3]
\end{tikzcd}\]




We will now see that these maps behave well with respect to height.

Since $V$ is an open set of $(\mathbb{P}^{s})^\vee$, the map $\phi_1$ extends (formally) to a rational map between projective spaces.

Therefore, by Proposition \ref{prop:heightUpperBound}, there exists a constant $\eta_1>0$ such that, for all $\underline{p} \in V(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$, we have
\[
h(\phi_1(\underline{p}))\leq \eta_1(1+h(\underline{p})).
\]
If we consider $(\mathcal{C}')^d \subseteq (\mathbb{P}^s)^d \subseteq \mathbb{P}^{(s+1)^d-1}$, then the map $\phi_3$ can also be extended to a rational map between projective spaces such that it is defined on $(\mathcal{C}')^d$, and in a similar way we get that there exists a constant $\eta_3>0$ such that, for all $(P_1, \dots, P_d) \in (\mathcal{C}')^d (\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$,
\[
h(P_1) = h(\phi_3((P_1,\ldots,P_{d})))\leq \eta_3(1+h((P_1,\ldots,P_{d})).
\]

\medskip
Let us now focus on $\phi_2$; this is the quotient map by a finite group, so
$(\mathcal{C}')^d$ and $Sym^d(\mathcal{C}')$ have the same dimension, $\phi_2$ is surjective, and all the fibers are finite; 
in particular, all the points $P \in (\mathcal{C}')^d$ are isolated in their fiber.
We can then apply Propostition \ref{prop:heightLowerBound} with $U=(\mathcal{C}')^d$, which gives us a constant $\eta_2>0$ such that
\[
h((P_1,\ldots,P_{d}))\leq\eta_2(1+h(\phi_2((P_1,\ldots,P_{d})))=\eta_2(1+h((P_1,\ldots,P_{d})_{no})).
\]
 
Let now be $P \in \mathcal{C}' \cap L_{\underline{p}}$. 
We can choose points $Q_1,\ldots,Q_{d-1}$ in $\mathcal{C}'$ such that the element $(P,Q_1,\ldots,Q_{d-1})_{no}=\mathcal{C}' \cap_m L_{\underline{p}}$. We have:
\begin{align*}
\phi_1(\underline{p})&=(P,Q_1,\ldots,Q_{d-1})_{no} \\
\phi_2(P,Q_1,\ldots,Q_{d-1})&=(P,Q_1,\ldots,Q_{d-1})_{no} \\
\phi_3(P,Q_1,\ldots,Q_{d-1})&=P. 
\end{align*}
We thus have
\begin{align*}
h(P)& \le \eta_3 (1+h((P, Q_1, \ldots, Q_{d-1})))\\
& \le\eta_3(1 + \eta_2(1 + h((P, Q_1, \ldots, Q_{d-1})_{no})))\\
& \le \eta_3(1 + \eta_2(1 + \eta_1(1 + h(\underline{p}))))
\end{align*}
 
Putting $\delta' = (\eta_1 + 1)(\eta_2 +1)(\eta_3+1)$ we conclude.

\label{thm:toric_divisibility_seq}
Let $G= \mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}$, let $S$ be a smooth curve defined over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ and consider the constant scheme $\mathcal{G} := G \times S$ with structural morphism $\pi: \mathcal{G} \to S$.

<!-- example-1: body_md -->
The conclusion of Theorem \ref{thm:toric_divisibility_seq} is very natural, and in some cases can be proved in elementary terms, as this example shows. 

Let $f(t)\in \mathbb{C}[t]$ be a nonconstant polynomial; then, $f(t)\in \mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}(\mathbb{C}(t))$ is a rational function. For every $n \ge 1$ we can then define the divisor $D_{f^n}=\sigma_{f^n}^*(1_{\mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}})$, where $\sigma_{f^n}$ denotes the section of $\mathcal G$ corresponding to the rational function $f^n$ and $1_{\mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}}$ is the identity section corresponding to the point $1 \in \mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}(\mathbb{C}(t))$.
In this setting, the divisor $D_{f^n}$ is reduced if and only if the polynomial $f(t)^n-1$ has only simple roots.

We notice that, for every $n\ge 1$, we can factorize $f(t)^n-1$ as
$$ f(t)^n-1= \prod_{j=0}^{n-1} (f(t)-\zeta_n^j), $$
with $\zeta_n=e^{2\pi i/n}$. 
It is easy to see that for every $j \neq j'$, the factors $f(t)-\zeta_n^j$ and $f(t)-\zeta_n^{j'}$ do not have common roots, hence $f(t)^n-1$ has a multiple root $t_0 \in \mathbb{C}$ if and only if $t_0$ is a multiple root of one of the factors. 
On the other hand, if $t_0$ is a multiple root of some $f(t)-\zeta_n^j$, then $f'(t_0)=0$. 
Consequently, the multiple roots of the polynomials of the form $f(t)^n-1$ are exactly the $t_0\in \mathbb{C}$ such that $f'(t_0)=0$ and there exists $n\ge 1$ and $0 \le j \le n-1$ such that $f(t_0)=e^{2\pi j/n}$. 
Notice that these elements are $\le \deg f-1$. For each of these elements, denote by $m_{t_0}$ the smallest $m \ge 1$ such that $f'(t_0)=0$ and $f(t_0)=e^{2\pi j/m_{t_0}}$ with $0 \le j \le m_{t_0}$; then, $t_0$ will be a multiple root of $f(t)^n-1$ for every $n$ which is a positive multiple of $m_{t_0}$. 
This means that $D_{nP}$ is reduced if and only if $n$ is not divisible by any of these $m_{t_0}$, as implied by Theorem \ref{thm:toric_divisibility_seq}.

<!-- thm-6: body_md -->
\label{Application_thm_1}
Let $G= \mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}$ or $G=E$ for an elliptic curve $E$ defined over a number field $K$, let $S$ be a smooth curve and consider the constant scheme $\mathcal{G} := G \times S$ with structural morphism $\pi: \mathcal{G} \to S$. 
Let $P, Q$ be sections $S \to \mathcal{G}$.
If there exist infinitely many $s \in S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ for which there exists $m_s \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $m_s P(s) = Q(s)$ tangentially, then, for some $m \in \mathbb{Z}$, we have that $mP=Q$ identically on $S$.
<!-- end thm-6 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
Consider $(s_n)_n$ an infinite sequence of pairwise distinct $s_n \in S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ for which there exists $m_{s_n} \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{tangent_intersection_section_eqn}
m_{s_n} P(s_n) = Q(s_n) \text{  tangentially}.
\end{equation}

We have two cases: either the $m_{s_n}$ are bounded, or they are unbounded.
We will treat these two cases separately.

If the $m_{s_n}$ are bounded, then, up to taking a subsequence, we have that for a fixed $m$ there exist infinitely many $s \in S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ where $m P(s) = Q(s)$, from which the claim follows.


The case in which the $m_{s_n}$ are unbounded is left to prove, so we will be assuming to be in that case.
From Theorem \ref{thm:main} it follows that, for some $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$ not both zero,
\begin{equation}
\label{relation_identical_section_eqn}
a P = b Q
\end{equation}
identically on $S$.
Combining this with \eqref{tangent_intersection_section_eqn}, we find that $P(s_n)$ is torsion for infinitely many $n$, since we have
\begin{equation}
\label{p_section_is_torsion_eqn}
O(s_n)=a P(s_n) - b Q(s_n) = a P(s_n) - b m_n P(s_n) = (a-b m_n)P(s_n)
\end{equation}
and $(a- b m_n)$ is non-zero infinitely often, as $m_n$ is unbounded.
Moreover, since \eqref{tangent_intersection_section_eqn} is true tangentially and \eqref{relation_identical_section_eqn} is true identically, we get that \eqref{p_section_is_torsion_eqn} also holds tangentially.
Again by Theorem \ref{thm:main}, $P$ must be identically torsion, and so the same is true for $Q$, by \eqref{relation_identical_section_eqn}.
Since by assumption there is a certain $s \in S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ and $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $m P(s) = Q$, such relation must hold for all $s \in S(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$, and hence identically on $S$.
<!-- end proof-7 -->
<!-- remark-4: body_md -->
Notice that in the case $G=\mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}$, the result by Marché and Maurin \cite{Maurin23} is sufficient to prove the previous theorem.
<!-- end remark-4 -->
<!-- corollary-2: body_md -->
\label{coroll_finite_set}
    Let $G, S, \mathcal{G}, \pi$ be as in the previous theorem, and let $P, Q$ be sections $S \to \mathcal{G}$ such that no multiple of $P$ is identically equal to $Q$.
    Let $S'$ be the set of $s \in S$ such that, for some $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $2s \leq D_{nP, Q}$.
    Then, $S'$ is a finite set.
<!-- end corollary-2 -->
<!-- thm-7: body_md -->
\label{thm:elliptic_div}
Let $G= \mathbb{G}_\mathrm{m}$ or $G=E$ for an elliptic curve $E$ defined over a number field $K$, let $S$ be a smooth curve and consider the constant scheme $\mathcal{G} := G \times S$ with structural morphism $\pi: \mathcal{G} \to S$. 
Let $P, Q$ be sections $S \to \mathcal{G}$ such that no multiple of $P$ is identically equal to $Q$.
Let $D_{nP, Q}$ be as in \eqref{D_nP_Q_eqation}.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item there exists a divisor $D$ of $S$, independent of $n$, such that $D_{nP, Q}^{\mathrm{nonRed}} \leq D$;
\item the set 
\[
\Xi = \{ n \in \mathbb{N} \operatorname{\; | \;} D_{nP, Q} \text{ is not reduced} \}
\]
is the union of a finite set and a finite union of arithmetic progressions.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end thm-7 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
For $s \in \mathcal{C}$ let $\Xi_s = \{ n \in \mathbb{N} \operatorname{\; | \;} 2s \leq D_{nP, Q} \}$, that is, the set of $n\in \mathbb{N}$ such that $nP$ and $Q$ intersect tangentially in $s$.
Clearly, $\Xi = \bigcup_{s \in \mathcal{C}} \Xi_s$.
Moreover, $\Xi_s$ is nonempty exactly whenever $s$ lies in the finite set $S'$ of Corollary \ref{coroll_finite_set}. 
In particular, the support of $D_{nP, Q}^{\mathrm{nonRed}}$ is contained in such $S'$.
To get part (1), it is now enough to apply \cite[Lemma 4.5]{Ghioca2018} (see also \cite[Lemma 4.4]{BCT2024}).

For part (2), we need to show that, for each $s \in S$, we have that $\Xi_s$ is either empty, a singleton, or a finite union of arithmetic progressions.
Suppose that $\Xi_s$ is nonempty, and let $n_0$ be its smallest element. For any other $n \in \Xi_s$ we have that both $n_0 P$ and $n P$ intersect $Q$ tangentially over $s$. Hence, $(n - n_0) P$ intersects the zero section tangentially over $s$. 
 
By \cite[Thm 1.4]{UU21} in the elliptic case, and Theorem \ref{thm:toric_divisibility_seq}, we can deduce that $\Xi_s - n_0$ is a finite union of arithmetic progressions starting from 0, so $\Xi_s$ itself is a finite union of arithmetic progressions starting from $n_0$.
<!-- end proof-8 -->
<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
The result of part (1) of the previous theorem also holds in the case of non-isotrivial elliptic scheme, with the same proof, although it is not stated explicitly in \cite{Ottolini}.
<!-- end remark-5 -->
<!-- remark-6: body_md -->
We point out that the result of part (2) of Theorem \ref{thm:elliptic_div} is weaker than what Ulmer and Urz{\'u}a get in the special case $Q = O$ (\cite[Thm 1.4]{UU21}) (see also Theorem \ref{thm:toric_divisibility_seq}). 
There, the authors prove that $\{ n \in \mathbb{N} \operatorname{\; | \;} D_{nP} \text{ is not reduced} \}$ is the finite union of arithmetic progressions \emph{starting from 0}, implying that if $D_{P}$ is reduced, then $D_{nP}$ is reduced infinitely often.
In our case, the arithmetic progressions may not start from 0, so Theorem \ref{thm:elliptic_div} does not provide an easy method to check whether $D_{nP, Q}$ is reduced infinitely often.
