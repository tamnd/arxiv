<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
A \emph{frame} is a pair $\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R})$, where $\mathcal{W}$ is a nonempty set whose elements are called \emph{worlds} and $\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{W}$ is a binary relation called the \emph{accessibility relation}. A \emph{model} based on $\mathcal{F}$ is a triple
$\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{V})$, equivalently $(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V})$, where
$\mathcal{V}:\mathcal{W}\to 2^{\mathsf{Prop}}$ is a function called \emph{valuation}.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{d:Sigma-system}
A \emph{$\Sigma$-system}\relax{Note that $\Sigma$-systems are a restricted form of semi-Thue systems~\cite{Pos47}.} is a set $\mathcal S$ of productions
$z\mapsto\beta$, where $z\in\Sigma$ and $\beta\in\Sigma^*$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
\label{d:semi-thue-deriv-lang}
Let $\mathcal S$ be a $\Sigma$-system. We write
$\beta\mapsto_{\mathcal S}\gamma$ if there are
$\rho,\tau\in\Sigma^*$ and a production $z\mapsto\eta\in\mathcal S$
such that $\beta=\rho z\tau$ and $\gamma=\rho\eta\tau$.
In this case, $\gamma$ is derived from $\beta$ in \emph{one step}. A \emph{derivation} of $\gamma$ from $\beta$, written $\beta \mapsto_{\mathcal{S}}^\ast \gamma$, is a
finite sequence of one-step derivations
\[
\beta=\delta_0\mapsto_{\mathcal S}\delta_1
\mapsto_{\mathcal S}\cdots
\mapsto_{\mathcal S}\delta_n=\gamma,
\]
whose length is $n$, where $\beta = \gamma$ for $n = 0$. Finally, put
$\mathcal L_{\mathcal S}(\beta)
\coloneqq\{\gamma\mid\beta\mapsto_{\mathcal S}^*\gamma\}$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
Let $\alpha\in\Sigma^*$. A frame $\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R})$ satisfies the
\emph{generalized path condition generated by $\alpha$}, denoted by
$\mathbf{G}(\alpha)$, if
\[
\mathcal{R}_\beta\subseteq\mathcal{R}
\qquad\text{for every }
\beta\in\mathcal L_{\mathcal S_\alpha}(x).
\]
For a finite set $\mathbf{G}$ of generalized path conditions, a frame is a
\emph{$\mathbf{G}$-frame} if it satisfies every member of $\mathbf{G}$. We write $\mathscr{F}_\mathbf{G}$ for the class of all $\mathbf{G}$-frames and put
\[
\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})
\coloneqq
\bigcup_{\mathbf{G}(\alpha)\in\mathbf{G}}\mathcal S_\alpha.
\]
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:word-relations}
Let $X$ be a set, let $Q,P\subseteq X\times X$, and let $\beta,\gamma\in\Sigma^*$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Q_{\beta\gamma}=Q_\gamma\circ Q_\beta$.
\item $Q_{\beta^{-1}}=(Q_\beta)^{-1}$.
\item If $Q\subseteq P$, then $Q_\beta\subseteq P_\beta$.
\item Suppose that $\delta=\rho z\tau$,
$\eta=\rho\gamma\tau$, and
$Q_\gamma\subseteq Q_z$, where $z\in\Sigma$.
Then $Q_\eta\subseteq Q_\delta$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
The concatenation identity follows by induction on the length
of $\gamma$. The converse and monotonicity claims follow by
induction on the length of the relevant word. For the final
claim, use the concatenation identity twice to obtain
\[
Q_\eta=Q_\tau\circ Q_\gamma\circ Q_\rho
\subseteq
Q_\tau\circ Q_z\circ Q_\rho
=Q_\delta
\]
and monotonicity of relational composition.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- lemma-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:gpc-finite-basis}
Let $\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R})$ be a frame and let $\alpha\in\Sigma^*$.
Then
\[
\mathcal{F}\models\mathbf{G}(\alpha)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal{R}_\alpha\subseteq\mathcal{R}.
\]
Consequently, $\mathcal{F}$ is a $\mathbf{G}$-frame if and only if
$\mathcal{R}_\alpha\subseteq\mathcal{R}$ for every
$\mathbf{G}(\alpha)\in\mathbf{G}$.
<!-- end lemma-2 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
If $\mathcal{F}\models\mathbf{G}(\alpha)$, then
$\alpha\in\mathcal L_{\mathcal S_\alpha}(x)$, because
$x\mapsto\alpha$ is a production of $\mathcal S_\alpha$.
Hence $\mathcal{R}_\alpha\subseteq\mathcal{R}$.
Conversely, suppose that $\mathcal{R}_\alpha\subseteq\mathcal{R}$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:word-relations},
\[
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha^{-1}}
=(\mathcal{R}_\alpha)^{-1}
\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{-1}
=\mathcal{R}_{\overline{x}}.
\]
Thus both productions of $\mathcal S_\alpha$ replace a letter
by a word whose relation is contained in the relation of that
letter. Lemma~\ref{lem:word-relations}(4), followed by
induction on derivation length, gives
\[
x\mapsto_{\mathcal S_\alpha}^*\beta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal{R}_\beta\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{x}=\mathcal{R}.
\]
This is exactly the condition $\mathbf{G}(\alpha)$.
The final assertion follows by applying this equivalence to
each member of $\mathbf{G}$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- example-1: body_md -->
By Lemma~\ref{lem:gpc-finite-basis}, the following
correspondences are immediate:
\[
\setlength{\arraycolsep}{2pt}
\begin{array}{@{}rcl@{\quad}rcl@{}}
\mathbf{G}(\epsilon) &\Longleftrightarrow&
\mathcal{R}_\epsilon\subseteq\mathcal{R} \;\text{(reflexivity)},
& \mathbf{G}(xx) &\Longleftrightarrow&
\mathcal{R}^2\subseteq\mathcal{R} \;\text{(transitivity)},\\
\mathbf{G}(\overline{x}) &\Longleftrightarrow&
\mathcal{R}^{-1}\subseteq\mathcal{R} \;\text{(symmetry)},
& \mathbf{G}(\overline{x}x) &\Longleftrightarrow&
\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{R}^{-1}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
\;\text{(right Euclideanness)}.
\end{array}
\]
Note that seriality is not expressible by GPCs.
<!-- end example-1 -->
<!-- definition-5: body_md -->
For a finite set $\mathbf{G}$ of GPCs, the logic $\mathbf{NCL}_\mathbf{G}$ is defined to be the set of formulae valid over $\mathscr{F}_\mathbf{G}$.
<!-- end definition-5 -->
<!-- lemma-3: body_md -->
For every finite set $\mathbf{G}$ of GPCs, the logic $\mathbf{NCL}_{\mathbf{G}}$ is non-normal:
\[
\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}}(p\to q)\to(\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}} p\to\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}} q)
\notin\mathbf{NCL}_\mathbf{G}.
\]
<!-- end lemma-3 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Let $\mathcal{W}=\{w,v,u\}$, let $\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{W}$, and let
$\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{V})$, where
$\mathcal{V}(w)=\mathcal{V}(v)=\emptyset$ and $\mathcal{V}(u)=\{q\}$. 
Since $\mathcal{R}$ is universal, $\mathcal{R}_\beta\subseteq\mathcal{R}$ for every
$\beta\in\Sigma^*$; hence $(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R})$ is a $\mathbf{G}$-frame for every
$\mathbf{G}$.
The atom $p$ is false everywhere, so $p\to q$ is true everywhere.
Thus $\mathcal{M},w\models\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}}(p\to q)$ and $\mathcal{M},w\models\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}} p$.
However, $q$ is false at $v$ and true at $u$, and both are
$\mathcal{R}$-successors of $w$. Hence $\mathcal{M},w\not\models\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}} q$.
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- definition-6: body_md -->
A sequent $\sigma$ is \emph{true in $\mathcal{M}$ under $\lambda$}, written
$\mathcal{M},\lambda\Vdash\sigma$, if $\mathcal{M},\lambda\Vdash\mathsf{x} \mathrel{\mathsf{R}} \mathsf{y}$ for every $\mathsf{x} \mathrel{\mathsf{R}} \mathsf{y} \in \sigma_c$ and $\mathcal{M},\lambda\Vdash \mathsf{x}: \varphi$ for every $\mathsf{x}: \varphi\in\sigma_L$ imply that $\mathcal{M},\lambda\Vdash \mathsf{y}: \psi$ for some $\mathsf{y}: \psi \in\sigma_R$. It is \emph{valid over} a frame class $\mathscr{F}$ if $\sigma$ is true in every
model based on a frame in $\mathscr{F}$ under every assignment; otherwise $\sigma$ is
\emph{falsifiable over $\mathscr{F}$}.
<!-- end definition-6 -->
<!-- definition-7: body_md -->
Let $\mathbf{G}$ be a finite set of GPCs. The calculus $\mathrm{NCL}_\mathbf{G}$
consists of the initial sequents and rules displayed in
Tables~\ref{tab:rules1} and~\ref{tab:rules2}.

<!-- definition-8: body_md -->
Let $\sigma=\Omega\dashv\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta$ be a sequent.  The
\emph{induced frame} $\mathcal{F}_\sigma=(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R})$ of $\sigma$ is defined as
follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathcal{W}\coloneqq\mathsf{Var}(\sigma)$;
  \item $\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathcal{R}}\mathsf{y}$ if and only if $\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y}\in\Omega$.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-8 -->
<!-- lemma-4: body_md -->
\label{lem: induced-frame}
If $\mathsf{Var}(\sigma)\neq\emptyset$, then the induced frame $\mathcal{F}_\sigma$
is a frame.
<!-- end lemma-4 -->
<!-- definition-9: body_md -->
Let $\sigma$ be a sequent with induced frame
$\mathcal{F}_\sigma=(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R})$, and let $\mathcal S$ be a $\Sigma$-system.
The \emph{closure} of $\mathcal{F}_\sigma$ under $\mathcal S$ is given as $C_{\mathcal S}(\mathcal{F}_\sigma) \coloneqq \bigl(\mathcal{W},C_{\mathcal S}(\mathcal{R})\bigr)$, where
\[
  C_{\mathcal S}(\mathcal{R})
  \coloneqq
  \bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal L_{\mathcal S}(x)}
  \mathcal{R}_\alpha.
\]
<!-- end definition-9 -->
<!-- lemma-5: body_md -->
\label{lem: closure-G-frame}
Let $\mathbf{G}$ be a finite set of GPCs, let
$\mathcal S=\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})$, and let $\sigma$ be a sequent such
that $\mathsf{Var}(\sigma)\neq\emptyset$.  Then
$C_{\mathcal S}(\mathcal{F}_\sigma)$ is a $\mathbf{G}$-frame.  Moreover, the
closure is idempotent:
\[
  C_{\mathcal S}\bigl(C_{\mathcal S}(\mathcal{F}_\sigma)\bigr)
  =
  C_{\mathcal S}(\mathcal{F}_\sigma).
\]
<!-- end lemma-5 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Each edge of a path in the closed relation can be expanded into a possibly empty path in the original relation; converse productions handle backward edges. Composing the corresponding grammar derivations shows that the closure satisfies the GPCs and that closing it again adds no edges. Full details are given in the arXiv version.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- definition-10: body_md -->
Let $\sigma=\Omega\dashv\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta$ be a sequent and $\mathbf{G}$ be a finite set of GPCs. The set
$\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})(\Omega)$ is defined as follows. For all $\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y}\in\mathsf{Var}$:
\begin{center}
  $\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y}\in\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})(\Omega)$ if and only if
  $\mathsf{x}\mathrel{C_{\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})}(\mathcal{R})}\mathsf{y}$ in
  $C_{\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})}(\mathcal{F}_\sigma)$.
\end{center}
<!-- end definition-10 -->
<!-- example-2: body_md -->
(1) If $\mathbf{G}=\emptyset$, then $\mathcal L_{\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})}(x)=\{x\}$,
    
                                               
    
and hence $C_{\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})}(\mathcal{F}_\sigma)=\mathcal{F}_\sigma$. (2) If $\mathbf{G}=\{\mathbf{G}(\epsilon)\}$, then $\mathcal L_{\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})}(x)
  =
  \{x,\epsilon\}$,
    
                                      
     
                     
    
so $C_{\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})}(\mathcal{R})
  =
  \mathcal{R}_{x}\cup\mathcal{R}_\epsilon$.
    
                            
     
                            
    
Thus, read bottom-up, the rule $\mathsf r_\mathbf{G}$ does not alter the control in case (1) and adds
$\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{x}$ to the control for every $\mathsf{x}\in\mathsf{Var}(\sigma)$ in case (2). For a specific set $\mathbf{G}$ of GPCs, we can therefore replace $\mathsf r_\mathbf{G}$ by its specific instantiation, which simplifies the sequent calculus while it retains all properties proven for the general case. For example for (2) $\mathsf r_\mathbf{G}$ is equivalently
presented as
\begin{prooftree}
  \AxiomC{$\Omega,\{\,\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{x}\,\}_{\mathsf{x}\in\mathsf{Var}(\Omega\dashv\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta)}
    \dashv\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta$}
  \RightLabel{$\mathsf{refl}$}
  \UnaryInfC{$\Omega\dashv\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta$}
\end{prooftree}

<!-- definition-11: body_md -->

<!-- lemma-6: body_md -->
\label{lem: local-soundness1}
Let $\mathbf{G}$ be a finite set of GPCs.  The rule
$\mathsf r_\mathbf{G}$ preserves validity over the class of
$\mathbf{G}$-frames.
<!-- end lemma-6 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We argue contrapositively.  Suppose that
$\sigma=\Omega\dashv\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta$ is falsified in a
model $\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{V})$ based on a $\mathbf{G}$-frame, under an assignment
$\lambda$.  Then $\lambda$ satisfies $\Omega$ and $\Gamma$ and
falsifies $\Delta$.  Since $\lambda$ satisfies $\Omega$, it maps
every path in $\mathcal{F}_\sigma$ to a path with the same label in the
frame of $\mathcal{M}$.  Thus, if
$\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y}\in\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})(\Omega)$, then $\lambda(\mathsf{x})\mathrel{\mathcal{R}_\beta}\lambda(\mathsf{y})$ for some
  $\beta\in\mathcal L_{\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})}(x)$.
As $\mathcal{M}$ is based on a $\mathbf{G}$-frame, the finite-basis lemma and
induction on $\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})$-derivations give
$\mathcal{R}_\beta\subseteq\mathcal{R}$.  Hence $\lambda$ satisfies
$\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})(\Omega)$.  Since the body is unchanged, the same
model and assignment falsify the premise.

<!-- definition-12: body_md -->
\label{def:gpc-saturation}
Let $\sigma=\Omega\dashv\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta$ be a sequent.
The control $\Omega$ is \emph{$\mathbf{G}$-saturated in $\sigma$} if $\Omega=\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})(\Omega)$,
where the right-hand side is evaluated in the induced frame $\mathcal{F}_\sigma$, and hence over the label domain $\mathsf{Var}(\sigma)$.
<!-- end definition-12 -->
<!-- definition-13: body_md -->
\label{def:formula-saturation}
Let $\sigma=\Omega\dashv\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta$ be a sequent. An
occurrence of a labelled formula $\mathsf{x}: \varphi$ in $\Gamma$ or $\Delta$ is
\emph{formula-saturated in $\sigma$} if $\varphi = p \in \mathsf{Prop}$, or $\varphi = \bot$, or if $\mathsf{x}: \varphi$ satisfies the corresponding clause below:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item if $\mathsf{x}:\varphi\to\psi\in\Gamma$, then
        $\mathsf{x}:\varphi\in\Delta$ or $\mathsf{x}:\psi\in\Gamma$;
  \item if $\mathsf{x}:\varphi\to\psi\in\Delta$, then
        $\mathsf{x}:\varphi\in\Gamma$ and $\mathsf{x}:\psi\in\Delta$;
  \item if $\mathsf{x}:\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}}\varphi\in\Gamma$, then either
        $\mathsf{y}:\varphi\in\Gamma$ for every $\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y}\in\Omega$, or
        $\mathsf{y}:\varphi\in\Delta$ for every $\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y}\in\Omega$;
  \item if $\mathsf{x}:\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}}\varphi\in\Delta$, then there are distinct labels
        $\mathsf{y}$ and $\mathsf{z}$ such that $\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y},\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{z}\in\Omega$,
        $\mathsf{y}:\varphi\in\Gamma$, and $\mathsf{z}:\varphi\in\Delta$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end definition-13 -->
<!-- definition-14: body_md -->
Let $\mathbf{G}$ be a finite set of GPCs. A sequent
$\sigma=\Omega\dashv\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta$ is \emph{saturated} if $\sigma$ is not an initial sequent, its control is $\mathbf{G}$-saturated in $\sigma$ and every labelled formula in its body is formula-saturated in $\sigma$.

<!-- definition-15: body_md -->
\label{def:proof-search}
Let $\mathbf{G}$ be a finite set of GPCs and let $\sigma$ be a sequent. A
\emph{proof-search tree} for $\sigma$ in $\mathrm{NCL}_\mathbf{G}$ is a possibly
infinite rooted tree whose nodes are labelled by sequents and which
satisfies the following conditions.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The root is labelled by $\sigma$.
  \item Every internal node is the conclusion of a preserving rule
        instance, and its children are exactly the premises of that
        instance.
  \item The rule $\mathsf r_\mathbf{G}$ may be applied at a sequent
        $\tau$ only if its control is not $\mathbf{G}$-saturated in $\tau$.
        A logical rule may be applied only if its principal left- or
        right-hand occurrence is not formula-saturated in its
        conclusion.
  \item A node is a leaf if and only if it is labelled by an initial sequent or
        by a saturated sequent.
  \item Every branch is \emph{fair}. Unless it terminates at an axiom,
        every formula occurrence or control that is unsaturated at a
        node is eventually \emph{discharged}: a formula occurrence either
        becomes formula-saturated or, while still unsaturated, is
        principal in a rule applied at a descendant; a control either
        becomes $\mathbf{G}$-saturated or, while still unsaturated, is
        processed by $\mathsf r_\mathbf{G}$ at a descendant.
\end{enumerate}

<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:proof-search}
Let $\mathbf{G}$ be a finite set of GPCs, let
$\sigma=\Omega\dashv\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta$ be a sequent, and let
$\pi$ be a proof-search tree for $\sigma$ in $\mathrm{NCL}_\mathbf{G}$. Then either
$\pi$ is a $\mathrm{NCL}_\mathbf{G}$-proof of $\sigma$, or $\sigma$ is falsifiable
over the class of $\mathbf{G}$-frames.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-8: body_md -->
Suppose $\pi$ is not a proof. Then $\pi$ must contain a `bad' branch: either a finite branch which ends in a saturated leaf or an infinite branch. Thus let $\rho=(\sigma_i)_{i<\ell}$ be a finite or infinite bad branch, where $\ell\in\mathbb N^+\cup\{\omega\}$, and let
$\sigma_i=\Omega_i\dashv\Gamma_i\Rightarrow\Delta_i$. We define
\[
  X_\rho=\bigcup_{i<\ell}\mathsf{Var}(\sigma_i),\qquad
  \Omega_\rho=\bigcup_{i<\ell}\Omega_i,\qquad
  \Gamma_\rho=\bigcup_{i<\ell}\Gamma_i,\qquad
  \Delta_\rho=\bigcup_{i<\ell}\Delta_i.
\]
Along $\rho$, preservation gives
$\Omega_i\subseteq\Omega_j$, $\Gamma_i\subseteq\Gamma_j$, and
$\Delta_i\subseteq\Delta_j$ whenever $i\leq j$.
Since $\rho$ contains no initial sequent, we also have
\begin{equation}\label{eq: completeness}
\mathsf{x}:\bot\notin\Gamma_\rho \text{ for every label } \mathsf{x} \text{ and }
\Gamma_\rho\cap\Delta_\rho=\emptyset.
\end{equation}
    

Indeed, a left-hand bottom
formula would already give a $\bot$-initial sequent, while persistence would
place any formula in both unions at a common node, giving an
$\mathsf{id}$-initial sequent.

We define a countermodel for $\sigma$ as follows. Choose $\star\notin X_\rho$ and let
\[
  \mathcal{W}_\rho=X_\rho\mathbin{\cup}\{\star\},\qquad
  \mathcal{R}_\rho=
  \{(\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y})\in X_\rho^2\mid \mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y}\in\Omega_\rho\}
  \cup\{(\star,\star)\}.
\]
For $\mathsf{x}\in X_\rho$, let
\[
  \mathcal{V}_\rho(\mathsf{x})=\{p\in\mathsf{Prop}\mid \mathsf{x}:p\in\Gamma_\rho\},
  \qquad \mathcal{V}_\rho(\star)=\emptyset,
\]
and define $\lambda_\rho:\mathsf{Var}\to\mathcal{W}_\rho$ by
$\lambda_\rho(\mathsf{x})=\mathsf{x}$ if $\mathsf{x}\in X_\rho$, and
$\lambda_\rho(\mathsf{x})=\star$ otherwise. Finally, set
$\mathcal{M}_\rho=(\mathcal{W}_\rho,\mathcal{R}_\rho,\mathcal{V}_\rho)$.

The point $\star$ ensures that $\mathcal{W}_\rho$ is non-empty and $\lambda_\rho$ total.
It is an isolated reflexive component; hence, once the relation on
$X_\rho$ is shown below to satisfy $\mathbf{G}$, adjoining $\star$ preserves
the $\mathbf{G}$-frame condition.

We now verify the frame condition. If $X_\rho=\emptyset$, the model
consists only of the reflexive point $\star$, so the claim is
immediate. Suppose $X_\rho\neq\emptyset$, fix
$\mathbf{G}(\alpha)\in\mathbf{G}$, and write
$\mathcal{R}_\rho^0=\mathcal{R}_\rho\cap X_\rho^2$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:gpc-finite-basis}, it is enough to prove
\[
  (\mathcal{R}_\rho^0)_\alpha\subseteq\mathcal{R}_\rho^0.
\]
Suppose $(\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y})\in(\mathcal{R}_\rho^0)_\alpha$. By the definition of $\mathcal{R}_\rho^0$, every edge of the witnessing $\alpha$-path corresponds to a relational atom in $\Omega_\rho$, and every label on the path belongs to $X_\rho$. Since the path is finite, all its labels and relational atoms have appeared by some stage of the branch; by persistence, they therefore occur together in some $\sigma_i$. Since $x\mapsto_{\mathcal S(\mathbf{G})}\alpha$, closing the control of $\sigma_i$ adds the endpoint atom $\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y}$.

If $\rho$ is finite, persistence puts this witness in its terminal
sequent, whose control is saturated; hence
$\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y}\in\Omega_\rho$. If $\rho$ is infinite and the endpoint
atom is not already present at $\sigma_i$, its control is
unsaturated. Fairness eventually makes that control saturated or
processes it by $\mathsf r_\mathbf{G}$; in either case
$\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y}$ occurs in a later control and therefore belongs to
$\Omega_\rho$. Thus $(\mathcal{R}_\rho^0)_\alpha\subseteq\mathcal{R}_\rho^0$.
Since $\mathbf{G}(\alpha)$ was arbitrary, $(X_\rho,\mathcal{R}_\rho^0)$ is a
$\mathbf{G}$-frame. Together with the isolated reflexive point $\star$,
this shows that $(\mathcal{W}_\rho,\mathcal{R}_\rho)$ is a nonempty
$\mathbf{G}$-frame.

Every label occurring in $\Gamma_\rho\cup\Delta_\rho$ belongs to
$X_\rho$, and hence $\lambda_\rho(\mathsf{x})=\mathsf{x}$. We prove simultaneously
by induction on $\varphi$ that
\[
  \mathsf{x}:\varphi\in\Gamma_\rho
  \Longrightarrow \mathcal{M}_\rho,\mathsf{x}\models\varphi,
  \qquad
  \mathsf{x}:\varphi\in\Delta_\rho
  \Longrightarrow \mathcal{M}_\rho,\mathsf{x}\not\models\varphi.
\]

\noindent\textsc{Case for $\varphi=\bot$.}
The left implication is vacuous by observation (\ref{eq: completeness}) while the right implication follows from the semantics of $\bot$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textsc{Case for $\varphi=p\in\mathsf{Prop}$.}
The left implication follows from the definition of $\mathcal{V}_\rho$. For
the right implication, $\mathsf{x}:p\in\Delta_\rho$ and observation (\ref{eq: completeness}) gives
$\mathsf{x}:p\notin\Gamma_\rho$; hence $p\notin\mathcal{V}_\rho(\mathsf{x})$ and
$\mathcal{M}_\rho,\mathsf{x}\not\models p$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textsc{Case for $\varphi=\psi\to\gamma$.}
For a finite branch, the terminal sequent is saturated; for an
infinite branch, fairness eventually saturates or processes each
persistent implication occurrence. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{x}:\psi\to\gamma\in\Gamma_\rho
  &\Longrightarrow
  \mathsf{x}:\psi\in\Delta_\rho
  \text{ or }\mathsf{x}:\gamma\in\Gamma_\rho,\\
  \mathsf{x}:\psi\to\gamma\in\Delta_\rho
  &\Longrightarrow
  \mathsf{x}:\psi\in\Gamma_\rho
  \text{ and }\mathsf{x}:\gamma\in\Delta_\rho.
\end{aligned}
\]
In the first case, the induction hypothesis makes $\psi$ false or
$\gamma$ true at $\mathsf{x}$, and therefore
$\mathcal{M}_\rho,\mathsf{x}\models\psi\to\gamma$. In the second, it makes $\psi$
true and $\gamma$ false at $\mathsf{x}$, and therefore
$\mathcal{M}_\rho,\mathsf{x}\not\models\psi\to\gamma$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textsc{Case for $\varphi=\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}}\psi$.}
Suppose $\mathsf{x}:\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}}\psi\in\Gamma_\rho$. Every finite set $F$ of
$\Omega_\rho$-successors of $\mathsf{x}$ is assigned a common polarity.
Indeed, choose a node containing the modal formula and all atoms
$\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y}$ with $\mathsf{y}\in F$. Terminal saturation in the finite case,
or fairness in the infinite case, makes the occurrence saturated or
processes it by $\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}}\mathsf L$. Since $F$ persists, either
$\mathsf{y}:\psi\in\Gamma_\rho$ for every $\mathsf{y}\in F$, or
$\mathsf{y}:\psi\in\Delta_\rho$ for every $\mathsf{y}\in F$.

If $\mathsf{x}$ has no $\Omega_\rho$-successor, both alternatives are
vacuous. Otherwise fix one successor $\mathsf{y}_0$. The preceding
observation puts $\mathsf{y}_0:\psi$ on one side; suppose it belongs to
$\Gamma_\rho$, the other case being symmetric. For any other
successor $\mathsf{y}$, apply the observation to $F=\{\mathsf{y}_0,\mathsf{y}\}$. Its common
polarity cannot be right-hand, since that would put
$\mathsf{y}_0:\psi$ in both branch unions, contradicting (\ref{eq: completeness}). Hence
\[
  \forall\mathsf{y}\,
  (\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y}\in\Omega_\rho\Rightarrow\mathsf{y}:\psi\in\Gamma_\rho),
\]
or symmetrically the same statement holds with $\Delta_\rho$ in
place of $\Gamma_\rho$. The induction hypothesis therefore makes
$\psi$ uniformly true or uniformly false at all successors of
$\mathsf{x}$, and so $\mathcal{M}_\rho,\mathsf{x}\models\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}}\psi$.

Now suppose $\mathsf{x}:\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}}\psi\in\Delta_\rho$. Terminal saturation or
fairness supplies distinct labels $\mathsf{y},\mathsf{z}$ such that
\[
  \mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{y},\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{z}\in\Omega_\rho,\qquad
  \mathsf{y}:\psi\in\Gamma_\rho,\qquad
  \mathsf{z}:\psi\in\Delta_\rho.
\]
By the induction hypothesis, $\psi$ is true at $\mathsf{y}$ and false at
$\mathsf{z}$. Hence $\mathcal{M}_\rho,\mathsf{x}\not\models\mathop{\raisebox{-0.2ex}{\scalebox{0.85}{\rotatebox{90}{$\nabla$}}}}\psi$.

This completes the induction. By preservation,
$\Omega\subseteq\Omega_\rho$, $\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma_\rho$, and
$\Delta\subseteq\Delta_\rho$. Hence $\mathcal{M}_\rho,\lambda_\rho$
satisfy the control of the root sequent which is $\sigma$ and every formula in its antecedent, while
every formula in its consequent is false. Therefore $\mathcal{M}_\rho,\lambda_\rho\not\Vdash\sigma$. Since $\mathcal{M}_\rho$ is based on a $\mathbf{G}$-frame, $\sigma$ is falsifiable
over the class of $\mathbf{G}$-frames.

$\sigma$ is valid over the class of $\mathbf{G}$-frames, then
$\mathrm{NCL}_\mathbf{G}\vdash\sigma$.
