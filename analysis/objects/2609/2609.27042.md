<!-- conjecture-1: body_md -->
Let $G$ be a finite group, $p$ a prime and $P$ a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$. Then there is a bijection \[\varepsilon:\mathrm{Irr}_{p'}(G)\rightarrow\operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(P))\]
such that $\varepsilon(\chi)(1)\leq\chi(1)$ for all $\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{p'}(G)$.
<!-- end conjecture-1 -->
<!-- problem-1: body_md -->
\label{inductive-condition}
\label{prob:mckay}
Let $S$ be a sporadic simple group with universal cover $\widehat{S}$, let $p$ be a prime, and let $P \in \mathrm{Syl}_p(\widehat{S})$. Does 
there exist an explicit bijection 
\[
{}^{*} \colon \operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(\widehat{S}) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(\operatorname{N}_{\widehat{S}}(P))
\]
satisfying the following four conditions?
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textnormal{(C\arabic*)}]
  \item \textbf{Equivariance.} $(\chi^*)^\alpha = (\chi^\alpha)^*$ for every $\alpha \in \operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S})_P$.
  \item \textbf{Non-increasing degree.} $\chi^*(1) \le \chi(1)$.
  \item \textbf{Central character (Schur).} $\chi_{\operatorname{Z}(\widehat{S})} = \chi(1) \lambda \iff (\chi^*)_{\operatorname{Z}(\widehat{S})} = \chi^*(1) \lambda$ for the same $\lambda \in 
  \operatorname{Irr}(\operatorname{Z}(\widehat{S}))$.
  \item \textbf{$p$-part of the conductor.} $C(\chi)_p = C(\chi^*)_p$.
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end problem-1 -->
<!-- definition-1: body_md -->
\label{def:universal-cover}
Let $S$ be a finite non-abelian simple group. Its \emph{universal cover} $\widehat{S}$ is the unique (up to isomorphism) finite group satisfying the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $\widehat{S}$ is perfect;
  \item $\widehat{S} / \operatorname{Z}(\widehat{S}) \cong S$;
  \item $\widehat{S}$ is the largest group satisfying (i) and (ii).
\end{enumerate}
The order of the center, $m(S) := |\operatorname{Z}(\widehat{S})|$, is called the \emph{Schur multiplier} of $S$. For sporadic simple groups, it is known that $m(S) \in 
\{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12\}$.
<!-- end definition-1 -->
<!-- definition-2: body_md -->
\label{def:conductor}
For a character $\chi \in \operatorname{Irr}(G)$, let $\mathbb{Q}(\chi)$ be its field of values, which is the smallest subfield of $\mathbb{C}$ containing $\chi(g)$ for all 
$g \in G$. By a theorem of Brauer, $\mathbb{Q}(\chi)$ is contained in some cyclotomic field. The \emph{conductor} $C(\chi)$ is defined as the smallest positive 
integer $n$ such that $\mathbb{Q}(\chi) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$, where $\zeta_n$ is a primitive $n$-th root of unity. Furthermore, the \emph{$p$-part of the 
conductor}, denoted $C(\chi)_p$, is the largest power of the prime $p$ dividing $C(\chi)$.
<!-- end definition-2 -->
<!-- definition-3: body_md -->
Let $P \in \mathrm{Syl}_p(\widehat{S})$. The relevant outer quotient for our problem is defined as $\Gamma_P := \operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S})_P / \operatorname{Inn}(\widehat{S})_P$, where $\operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S})_P = \{ \alpha 
\in \operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S}) : \alpha(P) = P \}$ and $\operatorname{Inn}(\widehat{S})_P = \operatorname{Inn}(\widehat{S}) \cap \operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S})_P$.
<!-- end definition-3 -->
<!-- lemma-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:accion-outer}
Let $\alpha, \beta \in \operatorname{Aut}(G)$ lie in the same coset modulo $\operatorname{Inn}(G)$. Then $\chi^\alpha = \chi^\beta$ for every $\chi \in \operatorname{Irr}(G)$. In particular, the action of 
$\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ on $\operatorname{Irr}(G)$ factors through $\operatorname{Aut}(G)/\operatorname{Inn}(G)$.
<!-- end lemma-1 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
Write $\beta = \iota_g \circ \alpha$ for some $g \in G$. Since irreducible characters are class functions, $\chi^{\iota_g} = \chi$. Thus, evaluating the action 
yields $\chi^\beta = \chi^{\iota_g \circ \alpha} = (\chi^\alpha)^{\iota_g} = \chi^\alpha$.
<!-- end proof-1 -->
<!-- theorem-1: body_md -->
\label{thm:gamma-P}
For every sporadic simple group $S$, every prime $p$, and every $P \in \mathrm{Syl}_p(\widehat{S})$, the group $\Gamma_P$ has order at most $2$.
<!-- end theorem-1 -->
<!-- proof-2: body_md -->
By the Frattini argument, since all Sylow $p$-subgroups of $\widehat{S}$ are conjugate, we have $\operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S}) = \operatorname{Inn}(\widehat{S})\operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S})_P$. By the Second Isomorphism Theorem, 
the quotient $\Gamma_P = \operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S})_P / \operatorname{Inn}(\widehat{S})_P$ is exactly isomorphic to $\operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S})/\operatorname{Inn}(\widehat{S}) = \operatorname{Out}(\widehat{S})$. For sporadic groups, it is well known from the ATLAS that $|\operatorname{Out}(S)| \in \{1, 2\}$. Since the universal cover satisfies $\operatorname{Out}(\widehat{S}) \cong \operatorname{Out}(S)$, it follows that $\Gamma_P$ has order at most $2$.
<!-- end proof-2 -->
<!-- corollary-1: body_md -->
\label{cor:un-alpha}
If $|\Gamma_P| = 1$, condition (C1) is automatic. If $|\Gamma_P| = 2$, choose any $\alpha \in \operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S})_P$ whose image is the unique non-trivial element in 
$\Gamma_P$. Then (C1) is equivalent to the single equation $(\chi^*)^\alpha = (\chi^\alpha)^*$ for all $\chi \in \operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(\widehat{S})$.
<!-- end corollary-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Every $\beta \in \operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S})_P$ is either inner, making (C1) automatic by Lemma \ref{lem:accion-outer}, or can be written as $\beta = \alpha \circ \sigma$ for 
some $\sigma \in \operatorname{Inn}(\widehat{S})_P$. By Lemma \ref{lem:accion-outer}, $\sigma$ acts trivially, so $(\chi^*)^\beta = (\chi^*)^\alpha$ and $(\chi^\beta)^* = 
(\chi^\alpha)^*$. The equation $(\chi^*)^\alpha = (\chi^\alpha)^*$ thus implies (C1).
<!-- end proof-3 -->
<!-- proposition-1: body_md -->
\label{prop:independence_sylow}
  The type associated with these characters is independent of the chosen Sylow $p$-subgroup.
<!-- end proposition-1 -->
<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
If $P' = P^g$ is another Sylow $p$-subgroup of $\widehat{S}$ with $g \in \widehat{S}$, then $\alpha' := \iota_g \circ \alpha \circ \iota_g^{-1}$ stabilizes $P'$ and represents a non-trivial class of $\Gamma_{P'}$. By Lemma \ref{lem:accion-outer}, the corresponding partition of $\operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(\widehat{S})$ is the same.
<!-- end proof-4 -->
<!-- proposition-2: body_md -->
\label{prop:clasificacion-A-indice-n}
Let $A = \widehat{S} \rtimes \langle \alpha \rangle$, where $\alpha$ represents the non-trivial class of $\Gamma_P$, and let $\theta \in \operatorname{Irr}(\widehat{S})$.
Then $\theta$ is of \emph{Type 1} if and only if there exists $\psi \in \operatorname{Irr}(A)$ that restricts to $\theta$ (\emph{Pattern a}), 
and it is of \emph{Type 2} if and only if there exists $\psi \in \operatorname{Irr}(A)$ that restricts to $\theta + \theta^\alpha$ (\emph{Pattern b}).
<!-- end proposition-2 -->
<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
Because $\overline{\alpha}$ has order 2 in $\operatorname{Out}(\widehat{S})$ and $\alpha \notin \operatorname{Inn}(\widehat{S})$, the order $n$ of $\alpha$ must be
 even, with $\alpha^2 \in \operatorname{Inn}(\widehat{S})$. Let $B = \widehat{S} \langle \alpha^2 \rangle$. Since $A/\widehat{S} \cong C_n$ is cyclic and $n$ is even, $B/\widehat{S}$ is the
 \emph{unique} subgroup of index 2 in $A/\widehat{S}$, and $B$ is a maximal subgroup of $A$.

By Lemma \ref{lem:accion-outer}, $\alpha^2 \in \operatorname{Inn}(\widehat{S})$ acts trivially on $\operatorname{Irr}(\widehat{S})$. Therefore, every $\langle \alpha \rangle$-orbit on $\operatorname{Irr}(\widehat{S})$ 
has size at most 2, and $B \le A_\theta$ for every $\theta \in \operatorname{Irr}(\widehat{S})$.

If $\theta$ is of Type 1 ($\theta^\alpha = \theta$), it is fully $A$-invariant. Since the quotient $A/\widehat{S}$ is cyclic, standard Clifford theory dictates that 
$\theta$ extends to $A$. Thus, there exists $\psi \in \operatorname{Irr}(A)$ with $\psi_{\widehat{S}} = \theta$, establishing Pattern (a).

If $\theta$ is of Type 2 ($\theta^\alpha \neq \theta$), then $\alpha \notin A_\theta$. We have the chain of subgroups $\widehat{S} \le B \le A_\theta \subsetneq A$. 
Because $B$ is maximal in $A$, the inertia subgroup is exactly $A_\theta = B$. Because $B/\widehat{S}$ is cyclic, $\theta$ extends to some $\xi \in \operatorname{Irr}(B)$, 
satisfying $\xi(1) = \theta(1)$. By the Clifford correspondence, $\psi := \xi^A$ is irreducible in $A$. Furthermore, standard induction degrees yield $\psi(1) 
= [A : B]\xi(1) = 2\theta(1)$. 

By Clifford's restriction theorem applied to $\widehat{S} \trianglelefteq A$, $\psi_{\widehat{S}} = e(\theta + \theta^\alpha)$ for some multiplicity $e \ge 1$. Checking degrees, 
we have $\psi(1) = e(2\theta(1))$. Substituting $\psi(1) = 2\theta(1)$ forces $e = 1$. Thus, $\psi_{\widehat{S}} = \theta + \theta^\alpha$, establishing Pattern (b).
<!-- end proof-5 -->
<!-- definition-4: body_md -->
\label{def:witnesses}
Let $\alpha \in \operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{S})_P$ represent the non-trivial class in $\Gamma_P$, and let $A = \widehat{S} \rtimes \langle \alpha \rangle$. For any irreducible character 
$\theta \in \operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(\widehat{S})$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A character $\psi \in \operatorname{Irr}(A)$ satisfying $\psi_{\widehat{S}} = \theta$ is called a \textbf{Type 1 witness} for $\theta$.
    \item A character $\psi \in \operatorname{Irr}(A)$ satisfying $\psi_{\widehat{S}} = \theta + \theta^\alpha$ is called a \textbf{shared Type 2 witness} for the orbit 
    $\{\theta, \theta^\alpha\}$.
\end{itemize}
<!-- end definition-4 -->
<!-- theorem-2: body_md -->
\label{thm:atajo-S}
Let $H$ be a finite group with $\widehat{S} \trianglelefteq H$ and $[H : \widehat{S}] = 2$, such that conjugation by elements of $H \setminus \widehat{S}$ induces the 
non-trivial outer class $\overline{\alpha} \in \Gamma_P$. The restriction map $\operatorname{Irr}(H) \to \operatorname{Char}(\widehat{S})$ correctly produces the
Type 1 / Type 2 partition with witness patterns (a) and (b), identical to those produced by $A = \widehat{S} \rtimes \langle \alpha \rangle$.
<!-- end theorem-2 -->
<!-- proof-6: body_md -->
By Lemma \ref{lem:accion-outer}, the action of $H/\widehat{S}$ on $\operatorname{Irr}(\widehat{S})$ is exactly the action of $\overline{\alpha}$. Let $\theta 
\in \operatorname{Irr}(\widehat{S})$. Because $[H : \widehat{S}] = 2$, the inertia group $H_\theta$ must be strictly either $H$ or $\widehat{S}$.

If $\theta$ is of Type 1 ($\theta^\alpha = \theta$), then $H_\theta = H$. Since the quotient $H/\widehat{S} \cong C_2$ is cyclic, standard 
Clifford theory dictates that $\theta$ extends to $H$. Thus, there exists $\psi \in \operatorname{Irr}(H)$ such that $\psi_{\widehat{S}} = \theta$, which exactly matches Pattern (a).

If $\theta$ is of Type 2 ($\theta^\alpha \neq \theta$), then $H_\theta = \widehat{S}$. By the Clifford correspondence, $\theta$ induces to an irreducible 
character $\psi = \theta^H \in \operatorname{Irr}(H)$. Standard induction degrees give $\psi(1) = [H : \widehat{S}]\theta(1) = 2\theta(1)$. By Clifford's restriction theorem 
applied to $\widehat{S} \trianglelefteq H$, we know $\psi_{\widehat{S}} = e(\theta + \theta^\alpha)$ for some multiplicity $e \ge 1$. Comparing degrees yields $2\theta(1) = 
e(2\theta(1))$, which strictly forces $e = 1$. Thus, $\psi_{\widehat{S}} = \theta + \theta^\alpha$, matching Pattern (b).
<!-- end proof-6 -->
<!-- theorem-3: body_md -->
\label{thm:biyeccion_correcta}
For a given sporadic group $S$, Problem \ref{prob:mckay} is positive if and only if the algorithm terminates without reporting a violation.
<!-- end theorem-3 -->
<!-- proof-7: body_md -->
The map ${}^*$ is a bijection, since $|\mathcal{B}_{\widehat{S}}| = |\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{N}}|$ is checked for all respective sub-blocks, and it pairs elements 
bijectively within each partition.

\textbf{Condition (C1):} By Corollary \ref{cor:un-alpha} and Section \ref{sec:clifford}, equivariance is equivalent to preserving types and mapping 
$\langle \alpha \rangle$-orbits to $\langle \alpha \rangle$-orbits. The algorithm explicitly partitions characters by type ($t \in \{1, 2\}$) and maps Type 1 
singletons to Type 1 singletons, and Type 2 pairs to Type 2 pairs. Hence, (C1) is satisfied.

\textbf{Condition (C2):} In Step 11, characters within each sub-block are ordered by descending degree. Step 15 explicitly verifies that $\chi^*(1) \le \chi(1)$. 
Since the algorithm terminates without reporting a violation, (C2) is strictly maintained.

\textbf{Condition (C3):} Characters are partitioned into blocks based on their central character behavior $c$. For Type 1 characters, $c = \lambda_\chi$. 
For Type 2 characters, $c = \{\lambda_\chi, \lambda_{\chi^\alpha}\}$. Step 14 ensures that within a Type 2 orbit, the mapping is oriented such that 
$\lambda_\chi = \lambda_{\chi^*}$. Consequently, for all $\chi$, the associated linear character $\lambda$ is preserved, satisfying (C3).

\textbf{Condition (C4):} Characters are further partitioned by the $p$-part of the conductor, $v = C(\chi)_p$. Characters are only paired if they belong 
to the same sub-block, meaning $C(\chi)_p = C(\chi^*)_p$ by definition. This satisfies (C4).
<!-- end proof-7 -->
