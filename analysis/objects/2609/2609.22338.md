A dynamical system of particular interest to us which is a special case of \eqref{eqn:general_dynamics} is a partially-observed bilinear dynamical systems with linear state observation (BDS-PO). 
It has the state-space representation
\begin{equation} \label{eqn:BDS-PO}
    \begin{aligned}
         x_{t+1} &= A_0 x_t + \sum_{k=1}^{d_u} (u_t)_k A_k x_t + B u_t +  w_t, \\
         y_t &= C x_t + D u_t + v_t,
    \end{aligned}
    \tag{BDS-PO}
\end{equation}
where $A_0, A_1, \dots, A_{d_u}$ denote the $d_u+1$ state matrices taking values in $\mathbb{R}^{d_x \times d_x}$, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_x \times d_u}$ denotes the input matrix, $C \in \mathbb{R}^{d_y \times d_x}$, and $D \in \mathbb{R}^{d_y \times d_u}$ denote the observation/measurement matrices.
All of these matrices constitute the parameter $\theta=(A_0,...,A_{d_u},B,C,D)$.
We are interested both in learning the input-output behavior of unknown bilinear systems from a single trajectory of input-output samples $\lbrace(u_t,y_t)\rbrace_{t=0}^T$

<!-- proof-1: body_md -->

    Let $u_\text{LQG}= \mathcal A^{-1}  \mathcal B \hat x$ and suppose that $C(u_\text{LQG})=0$.

    Depending on their relative magnitudes, this can result in a local maximum for their sum $f=f_{LQG}+g$.
    Concretely, in the scalar case $n=m=p=1$, we may compute the second derivative of $f$ and evaluate it at $u_\text{LQG}$:

In the scalar setting, one may further characterize all critical points of $f$ as the roots of a degree 5 polynomial. <!-- remark-4: body_md -->
For the special case $F(x) = A_0 x$ and $G(x) = B$ (i.e., an LTI system), any dictionary $\Psi$ that includes the identity ($\psi_k(x) = x_k$) as observables recovers an exact linear representation with $\hat{A}_k = 0$ for $k \geq 1$ and $\hat{B} = B$.
    In particular, LTI systems are a degenerate special case of the bilinear Koopman surrogate~\eqref{eq:bilinear-koopman} without bilinear coupling terms.
    Any richer nonlinear control-affine system requires the bilinear terms $(u_t)_k\hat{A}_k z_t$ for a finite-dimensional representation, which underscores why the bilinear setting is the right level of generality for Koopman-based control.
    We emphasize that the state-independent control matrix $\hat{B}$ is crucial for the expressiveness of the approximation, i.e., a linear system could not be represented by~\eqref{eq:bilinear-koopman} without this term.

<!-- remark-5: body_md -->
A richer dictionary (larger $N$) reduces the closure error and, hence, tightens $Q_\Psi$, yielding less conservative stability certificates.
    However, a larger dictionary increases the state dimension $N$ of the lifted bilinear model, which affects the complexity of the SDP associated with the controller design problem.
    The size of the LMI design scales as $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ and the SOS design as $\mathcal{O}(N^{2\alpha})$.
    Practitioners therefore face a trade-off between model fidelity (larger $N$, smaller $Q_\Psi$) and computational tractability.
    For control applications, the LMI approach of \S\ref{sec:control-bilinear-LMI-overapprox} typically scales better to large dictionaries, while SOS methods are preferable when a tight ROA certificate is needed with a moderate dictionary.

<!-- proof-2: body_md -->

\begin{align}
	\mathbb{E}\left[ X_\phi^\top X_\phi\right] = \mathbb{E} \left[\sum_{t = 1}^{T} \phi_t \phi_t^{\top}\right] &= \sum_{t = 1}^{T} \mathbb{E} \left[\phi_t \phi_t^\top\right] = \sum_{t = 1}^{T} I_{d{\phi}} = T I_{d{\phi}}.
\end{align}
With a slight abuse of notation, let $v \in \mathcal{S}^{d_{\phi}-1}$, and consider the quantity $v^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi}v = \sum_{t = 1}^{T} (v^\top\phi_t)^2$, which can be viewed as a summation of the random process $\{(v^\top\phi_t)^2\}_{t = 1}^{T}$. In the following, we will derive a one-sided concentration bound for this random process.

\medskip
\noindent $\bullet$ {\bf Step 1) Blocking:} We begin by using blocking technique to get independent samples as follows,
\begin{align}
	\sum_{t = 1}^{T} (v^\top\phi_t)^2 = \sum_{k = 1}^{\tau} \sum_{\ell = 0}^{T/\tau-1} (v^\top\phi_{\ell \tau  + k})^2,
\end{align}
where we make the simplifying assumption that $T$ can be divided by $\tau$. \emph{This goes without loss of generality, and we assume it for the sake of clarity. It can be easily avoided by noting that $
\sum_{t=1}^{T} \phi_t \phi_t^\top \succeq \sum_{t=1}^{ \tau\lfloor {T}/{\tau} \rfloor } \phi_t \phi_t^\top, 
$ where $\lfloor\cdot \rfloor$ denotes the floor operator. We can then analyze everything with $T_0 =  \tau\lfloor {T}/{\tau} \rfloor$, and note that $ T-\tau <  T_0 \le T$.}

\medskip
\noindent $\bullet$ {\bf Step 2) Bernstein's inequality for non-negative random variables:} From Assumption~\ref{assump:phi_t distribution}, we have $\mathbb{E}[(v^\top \phi_t)^4] \le \gamma\, \mathbb{E}[(v^\top \phi_t)^2]^2$, for all $v \in \mathcal{S}^{d_\phi-1}$, and all $t \in [1,T]$. Hence, we can use one-sided Bernstein's inequality for non-negative random variables~\cite[Proposition 2.14]{wainwright2019high}, also stated in Proposition~\ref{corr:onesided_bernstein} in Section~\ref{sec:onesided} to obtain a lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of $\sum_{t=1}^{T} \phi_t \phi_t^\top$. Specifically, we have,
\begin{align}
	&\mathbb{P} \left( \sum_{\ell = 0}^{T/\tau-1} \left((v^\top\phi_{ \ell \tau  + k})^2 - \mathbb{E}[(v^\top\phi_{\ell \tau  + k})^2]\right)  \leq - \frac{T}{\tau}\zeta \right) \leq \exp \left( \frac{-(T/\tau) \zeta^2}{\frac{2}{T/\tau}\sum_{\ell=0}^{T/\tau-1} \mathbb{E}[(v^\top\phi_{\ell \tau  + k})^4] }\right). 
\end{align}
From Assumption~\ref{assump:phi_t distribution}, we know that $\{\phi_{\ell \tau  + k}\}_{\ell=0}^{T/\tau-1}$ are i.i.d. for each $k \in [1,\tau]$. Therefore, we have
\begin{align}
	\mathbb{P} \left( \sum_{\ell = 0}^{T/\tau-1} \left((v^\top\phi_{\ell \tau + k})^2 - \mathbb{E}[(v^\top\phi_{\ell \tau + k})^2]\right)  \leq - \frac{T}{\tau}\zeta~ \mathbb{E}[(v^\top\phi_{\tau})^2]\right) &\leq \exp \left( \frac{-(T/\tau) \zeta^2~\mathbb{E}[(v^\top\phi_{\tau})^2]^2}{2~ \mathbb{E}[(v^\top\phi_{\tau })^4] }\right), \nonumber \\
    &\leq \exp \left( \frac{-T \zeta^2}{2 \tau \gamma }\right).
\end{align}
This further implies that
\begin{align}
	\mathbb{P} \left( \sum_{\ell = 0}^{T/\tau-1} (v^\top\phi_{\ell \tau + k})^2  \leq \frac{T}{\tau}(1-\zeta) \right) 
    &\leq \exp \left( \frac{-T \zeta^2}{2 \tau\gamma }\right), \label{eqn:onesided_bound_k}
\end{align}
where we use the fact that $\mathbb{E}[(v^\top\phi_{\tau})^2] = \mathbb{E}[v^\top\phi_{\tau} \phi_{\tau}^\top v] = v^\top I_{d_{\phi}} v = 1$. Note that, if $\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\tau} \phi_{\tau}^\top] = \Sigma_{\phi}$ instead of identity, we can do a change of variable by setting $\phi_t' := \Sigma_{\phi}^{-1/2} \phi_t$, and work with $\phi_t'$, and later convert our final results in terms of $\phi_t$. To proceed, union bounding over $\tau$ events of the form \eqref{eqn:onesided_bound_k}, we get the following,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
	\mathbb{P} \left( \sum_{k = 1}^{\tau}\sum_{\ell = 0}^{T/\tau-1} (v^\top\phi_{\ell \tau + k})^2  \leq T(1-\zeta) \right) 
    &\leq \tau \exp \left( \frac{-T \zeta^2}{2 \tau \gamma}\right),\\
    \implies \quad \mathbb{P} \left( \sum_{t = 1}^{T} (v^\top\phi_t)^2  \leq T - \sqrt{2 \tau T \gamma \log(\tau/\delta)}\right) 
    &\leq \delta \label{eqn:onesided_union_k}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where we get the last inequality by setting,
\begin{align}
	\tau \exp\left(-\frac{T\zeta^2}{2\tau \gamma}\right) &= \delta, \nonumber \\
	\iff \frac{T\zeta^2}{2\tau \gamma} &= \log(\tau /\delta), \nonumber \\
	\impliedby \zeta &= \sqrt{\frac{\tau}{T}2 \gamma\log(\tau/\delta)}. 
\end{align}


\noindent $\bullet$ {\bf Step 3) Covering with ${\delta}/{(8d_{\phi})}$-net:} Next, we use a covering argument as follows: Let $\mathcal{N}_\epsilon := \{v_1, v_2, \dots, v_{|\mathcal{N}_\epsilon|} \} \subset \mathcal{S}^{d_{\phi}-1}$ be the $\epsilon$-net of $\mathcal{S}^{d_{\phi}-1}$ such that for any $v \in \mathcal{S}^{d_{\phi}-1}$, there exists $v_i \in \mathcal{N}_\epsilon$ such that $\left\|{v - v_i}\right\|_{\ell_2} \leq \epsilon$. From Lemma 5.2 of \cite{vershynin2010introduction}, we have $|\mathcal{N}_\epsilon| \leq (1 + 2/ \epsilon)^{d_{\phi}}$. 

Let us choose $v \in \mathcal{S}^{d_{\phi}-1}$ for which $\lambda_{\min} (X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi}) = v^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v$, and choose $v_i \in \mathcal{N}_\epsilon$ which approximates $v$ as $\left\|{v - v_i}\right\|_{\ell_2} \leq \epsilon$. By triangle inequality, we have
\begin{align}
	|v^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v  - v_i^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v_i| &= |v^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} ( v - v_i) + (v - v_i)^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v_i|, \nonumber \\
	& \leq \lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op} \left\|{v}\right\|_{\ell_2} \left\|{v - v_i}\right\|_{\ell_2} + \lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op} \left\|{v_i}\right\|_{\ell_2} \left\|{v - v_i}\right\|_{\ell_2}, \nonumber \\
	& \leq 2 \epsilon \lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op}, \nonumber \\
    \implies  v^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v &\geq  v_i^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v_i - 2 \epsilon \lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op}, \nonumber \\
     \implies \lambda_{\min}(X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} ) &\geq \inf_{v_i \in \mathcal{N}_\epsilon} v_i^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v_i - 2 \epsilon \lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op}.
\end{align}
Hence, in order to lower bound $\lambda_{\min}(X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} )$, we also need an upper bound on $\lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op}$. This can be done as follows: First, we have 
\begin{align}
	\mathbb{E}[\lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op}] = \mathbb{E}[\lambda_{\max}(X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi})] &\leq \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{tr}(X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi})]= \operatorname{tr}\big(\mathbb{E}[X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi}]\big), \nonumber \\
    & = \operatorname{tr}\big(T I_{d_{\phi}}\big) = d_{\phi} T.
\end{align}
Hence, using Markov's inequality, we get
\begin{align}
	\mathbb{P}\left( \lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op} > \frac{d_{\phi}T}{\delta}\right) \leq  \frac{\mathbb{E}[ \lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op} ]}{d_{\phi}T} \delta \leq \delta.
\end{align}
Let  $\mathcal{E} := \{\lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op}  \leq \frac{2d_{\phi}T}{\delta}\}$ denote the event that $\lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op}$ is upper bounded by a specified threshold. Then, it is straightforward to see that $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}) \geq 1 - \delta/2$. This further implies,
\begin{align}
	\mathbb{P}&\left(\lambda_{\min}(X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi}) < T(1/2-\zeta)\right) \nonumber \\
    &\leq \mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\lambda_{\min}(X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi}) < T(1/2-\zeta)\right\} \bigcap \mathcal{E} \right) + \mathbb{P} (\mathcal{E}^c), \nonumber \\
	&\leq \mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\inf_{v_i \in \mathcal{N}_\epsilon} v_i^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v_i - 2 \epsilon \lVert X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} \rVert_\textup{op} < T(1/2-\zeta)\right\} \bigcap \mathcal{E} \right) + \delta/2, \nonumber \\
	&\leq \mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{v_i \in \mathcal{N}_\epsilon} v_i^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v_i  < T(1/2-\zeta) +  4 \epsilon\frac{d_{\phi}T}{\delta}\right) + \delta/2, \nonumber \\
    &= \mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{v_i \in \mathcal{N}_\epsilon} v_i^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v_i  < T(1/2- \zeta +  \frac{4 \epsilon d_{\phi}}{\delta})\right) + \delta/2, \nonumber \\
	&= \mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{v_i \in \mathcal{N}_\epsilon} v_i^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v_i  < T(1-\zeta )\right) + \delta/2, \label{eqn:before_union_bound}
\end{align}
where we obtain the last inequality by choosing $\epsilon = \frac{\delta}{8d_{\phi}}$. Using \eqref{eqn:onesided_union_k}, with union bounding over all the elements in $\mathcal{N}_\epsilon$, we obtain,
\begin{align}
	 \mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{v_i \in \mathcal{N}_\epsilon} v_i^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v_i  < T(1-\zeta )\right) &\leq |\mathcal{N}_\epsilon| \tau \exp\left(-\frac{T\zeta^2}{2\tau \gamma}\right).
\end{align}
 From Lemma 5.2 of \cite{vershynin2010introduction}, we have $|\mathcal{N}_\epsilon| \leq (1 + 2/ \epsilon)^{d_{\phi}}$. Hence, we have
\begin{align}
    &\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{v_i \in \mathcal{N}_\epsilon} v_i^\top X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi} v_i  < T(1-\zeta )\right) \leq \left(1 + \frac{16 d_{\phi}}{\delta}\right)^{d_{\phi}} \tau \exp\left(-\frac{T\zeta^2}{2\tau \gamma }\right), \nonumber \\
    \implies \quad &\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\min}(X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi}) < T/2- \sqrt{2\tau \gamma T\left(\log\left(\frac{2\tau }{\delta}\right) +d_{\phi} \log\left(1 + \frac{16 d_{\phi}}{\delta}\right)\right)}\right) \leq \delta,
\end{align}
where we get the last inequality by setting,
\begin{align}
	&\left(1 + \frac{16 d_{\phi}}{\delta}\right)^{d_{\phi}} \tau \exp\left(-\frac{T\zeta^2}{2\tau \gamma }\right) = \delta/2, \nonumber \\
	 \iff &\exp\left(-\frac{T\zeta^2}{2\tau \gamma }\right) = \delta/(2\tau) (1 + \frac{16 d_{\phi}}{\delta})^{-d_{\phi}}, \nonumber \\
	 \iff &\frac{T\zeta^2}{2\tau \gamma } = \log(2\tau /\delta) + d_{\phi} \log\left(1 + \frac{16 d_{\phi}}{\delta}\right), \nonumber \\
	\impliedby &\zeta = \sqrt{\frac{2\tau \gamma }{T}\left(\log\left(\frac{2\tau }{\delta}\right) + d_{\phi} \log\left(1 + \frac{16 d_{\phi}}{\delta}\right)\right)}.
\end{align}
Finally, choosing the trajectory length via
\begin{align}
	T/4 &\geq \sqrt{2\tau \gamma T\left(\log\left(\frac{2\tau }{\delta}\right) +d_{\phi} \log\left(1 + \frac{16 d_{\phi}}{\delta}\right)\right)},\nonumber \\
	 \iff T/16 &\geq 2\tau \gamma \left(\log\left(\frac{2\tau }{\delta}\right) +d_{\phi} \log\left(1 + \frac{16 d_{\phi}}{\delta}\right)\right), \nonumber \\
	 \impliedby T &\geq 32\tau \gamma \left(\log\left(\frac{2\tau }{\delta}\right) +d_{\phi} \log\left(1 + \frac{16 d_{\phi}}{\delta}\right)\right),
\end{align}
we obtain the following persistence of excitation result, 
\begin{align}
	\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\min}(X_{\phi}^\top X_{\phi}) \geq T/4 \right) \geq 1 - \delta.  
\end{align}
This completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:persistence}.

Let $v \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u(d_u+1)(\tau - 1)}$ be an arbitrary vector, and $V := \operatorname{\mathsf{mtx}}(v) \in \mathbb{R}^{ d_u(\tau-1) \times (d_u+1)}$ is the corresponding matrix. Then, the second moment of the random variable $v^\top \phi_t^{}$ is given by,
    \begin{align}
        \mathbb{E} \left[(v^\top \phi_t^{})^2\right] = \mathbb{E} \left[\left((\tilde{u}_t^\top \otimes \bar{u}_{t-1}^\top) v\right)^2\right]  &= \mathbb{E} \left[\left(\bar{u}_{t-1}^\top V \tilde{u}_t\right)^2\right], \nonumber \\ 
        &= \mathbb{E} \left[\mathbb{E} \left[\left(\bar{u}_{t-1}^\top V \tilde{u}_t\right)^2 {~\big |~} \bar{u}_{t-1}\right] \right], \nonumber \\
        &= \mathbb{E} \left[ \bar{u}_{t-1}^\top V V^\top \bar{u}_{t-1}\right], \nonumber \\
        &= \operatorname{tr} \left( V V^\top \mathbb{E}[\bar{u}_{t-1}\bar{u}_{t-1}^\top]\right), \nonumber \\
        &= \operatorname{tr} \left( V V^\top\right), \nonumber \\
        &= \left\|{V}\right\|_{F}^2. 
        \label{eqn:lds_hyper_second_mom_app}
    \end{align}
    Next, we will upper bound the fourth moment of the random variable $v^\top \phi_t^{}$ as follows:
    \begin{align}
         \mathbb{E} \left[(v^\top \phi_t^{})^4\right] = \mathbb{E} \left[\left((\tilde{u}_t^\top \otimes \bar{u}_{t-1}^\top) v\right)^4\right]  &= \mathbb{E} \left[\left(\bar{u}_{t-1}^\top V \tilde{u}_t\right)^4\right], \nonumber \\ 
         &= \mathbb{E} \left[\left(\tilde{u}_t^\top V^\top \bar{u}_{t-1}\right)^4\right], \nonumber \\
         &= \mathbb{E} \left[\mathbb{E} \left[\left(\tilde{u}_t^\top V^\top \bar{u}_{t-1}\right)^4 {~\big |~} \tilde{u}_t\right] \right], \nonumber \\
         &\stackrel{\text{(i)}}{\leq} 3 \mathbb{E} \left[ \left\|{V \tilde{u}_t}\right\|_{\ell_2}^4\right], \nonumber \\
         &= 3 \mathbb{E} \left[ (\tilde{u}_t^\top V^\top V \tilde{u}_t)^2\right], \nonumber \\
         & \stackrel{\text{(ii)}}{\leq} 9 \left\|{V}\right\|_{F}^4, 
        \label{eqn:lds_hyper_fourth_mom_app}
    \end{align}
where we get (i) from the observation that, for any fixed $q = [q_1^\top~~q_2^\top~~\cdots~~q_{\tau-1}^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u(\tau - 1)}$, using Assumption~\ref{assump:input+noise}(\textbf{a}), we have
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E} \left[ (q^\top \bar{u}_{t-1})^4\right] &= \mathbb{E} \left[ \left(\sum_{k=1}^{\tau-1}q_k^\top u_{t-k}\right)^4\right], \nonumber \\
    &= \sum_{k=1}^{\tau-1} \mathbb{E} \left[ (q_k^\top u_{t-k})^4\right] + 3\sum_{k=1}^{\tau-1}\sum_{\substack{\ell = 1 \\ \ell \neq k}}^{\tau-1}  \mathbb{E} \left[ (q_k^\top u_{t-k})^2\right]  \mathbb{E} \left[ (q_\ell^\top u_{t-\ell})^2\right], \nonumber \\
    &\leq \sum_{k=1}^{\tau-1} 3\left\|{q_k}\right\|_{\ell_2}^4 + 3\sum_{k=1}^{\tau-1}\sum_{\substack{\ell = 1 \\ \ell \neq k}}^{\tau-1} \left\|{q_k}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2 \left\|{q_\ell}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2, \nonumber \\
    &= 3 \left(\sum_{k=1}^{\tau-1} \left\|{q_k}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2\right)^2, \nonumber \\
    &= 3 \left\|{q}\right\|_{\ell_2}^4.
\end{align}
Moreover, we get (ii) in \eqref{eqn:lds_hyper_fourth_mom_app} from the application of Lemma~\ref{lemma:hyper_to_moment_app} along-with the observation that $\tilde{u}_t$ is $(4,2,3)$-hypercontractive as follows: for any fixed $b = [b_0~~b_1] \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u+1}$, using Assumption~\ref{assump:input+noise}(\textbf{a}), we have
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E} \left[ (b^\top \tilde{u}_t)^4\right] &= \mathbb{E} \left[ \left(b_0 + b_1^\top u_t\right)^4\right], \nonumber \\
    &= b_0 ^4 + 6 b_0^2 \mathbb{E} \left[(b_1^\top u_t)^2\right] + \mathbb{E} \left[(b_1^\top u
    _t)^4\right], \nonumber \\
    &\leq b_0^4 + 6 b_0^2\left\|{b_1}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2 + 3 \left\|{b_1}\right\|_{\ell_2}^4, \nonumber \\
    &\leq 3 \left(b_0^2 + \left\|{b_1}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2\right)^2, \nonumber \\
    &= 3 \left\|{b}\right\|_{\ell_2}^4, \nonumber \\
    &= 3 \mathbb{E} \left[ (b^\top \tilde{u}_t)^2\right]^2.
\end{align}
Finally, combining \eqref{eqn:lds_hyper_second_mom_app} and \eqref{eqn:lds_hyper_fourth_mom_app}, we get the statement of Lemma~\ref{lemma:hyper_phi_lds_app} as follows:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E} \left[(v^\top \phi_t^{})^4\right] \leq 9 \left\|{V}\right\|_{F}^4 = 9\, \mathbb{E} \left[(v^\top \phi_t^{})^2\right]^2.
\end{align}
This completes the proof.

<!-- proof-4: body_md -->
Throughout this proof, for notational convenience, we use $\Gamma_x$ instead of $\Gamma_x^{(\tau-1)}$ to denote the covariance of $x_{t,\tau}$. To begin, consider the isotropic covariates as follows: 
\begin{align}
    \phi_{t,\tau}^{'{}}  = \begin{bmatrix} u_{t} \\ \Gamma_x^{-1/2} x_{t,\tau} \\
    u_{t} \otimes \Gamma_x^{-1/2} x_{t,\tau} \end{bmatrix} =: 
    \begin{bmatrix} u_{t} \\ x_{t,\tau}' \\
    u_{t} \otimes  x_{t,\tau}' \end{bmatrix}. \label{eqn:isotropic_truncated_covariates_def}
\end{align}
 Let $v = [v_1^\top~~v_2^\top~~v_3^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u+ (d_u+1)d_x}$ be an arbitrary vector. Then, the second moment of the random variable $v^\top \phi_{t,\tau}^{'{}}$ is given by,
\begin{align}
   \mathbb{E} [(v^\top \phi_{t,\tau}^{'{}})^2] = \mathbb{E} \left[v^\top \phi_{t,\tau}^{'{}} (\phi_{t,\tau}^{'{}})^\top v\right] = \left\|{v}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2. \label{eqn:second_moment_bds_app}
\end{align}
 Next, we will upper bound the fourth moment of the random variable $v^\top \phi_{t,\tau}^{'{}}$ as follows:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E} [( v^\top \phi_{t,\tau}^{'{}})^4]^{1/4}  &= \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(v_1^\top u_t + v_2^\top x_{t,\tau}' + v_3^\top (u_{t} \otimes x_{t,\tau}') \right)^4 \right]^{1/4}, \nonumber \\
    &= \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(v_1^\top u_t + v_2^\top x_{t,\tau}' + u_{t}^\top V_3 x_{t,\tau}' \right)^4 \right]^{1/4}, \nonumber \\
    & \stackrel{\text{(a)}}{\leq}  \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(v_1^\top u_t\right)^4 \right]^{1/4} +  \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(v_2^\top x_{t,\tau}'\right)^4 \right]^{1/4} +  \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(u_{t}^\top V_3 x_{t,\tau}' \right)^4 \right]^{1/4}, \label{eqn:fourth_moment_bds_app_v1}
\end{align}
where we get (a) from using Minkowski's inequality. In the following, we will upper bound each term in \eqref{eqn:fourth_moment_bds_app_v1} separately. The first term can be easily upper bounded as follows:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(v_1^\top u_t\right)^4 \right]^{1/4} \leq \left(\frac{3d_u}{d_u+2}\right)^{1/4} \left\|{v_1}\right\|_{\ell_2} \leq (3)^{1/4} \left\|{v_1}\right\|_{\ell_2}. \label{eqn:firt_term_bds_app}
\end{align}
Next, using Minkowski's inequality along-with Assumptions~\ref{assump:input+noise} and \ref{assump:stability}, the second term in \eqref{eqn:fourth_moment_bds_app_v1} can be upper bounded as follows:
\begin{align}
    &\mathbb{E}\left[ \left(v_2^\top x_{t,\tau}'\right)^4 \right]^{1/4} \nonumber \\
    &=  \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(v_2^\top \Gamma_{x}^{-1/2}\sum_{\ell = 1}^{\tau-1} \left(\prod_{k = 0}^{\ell-1} (u_{t-k} \circ A)\right) B u_{t-\ell} + v_2^\top\Gamma_{x}^{-1/2} \sum_{\ell = 1}^{\tau-1} \left(\prod_{k = 0}^{\ell-1} (u_{t-k} \circ A)\right)  w_{t-\ell}\right)^4 \right]^{1/4}, \nonumber \\
    &\stackrel{\text{(b)}}{\leq} \sum_{\ell= 1}^{\tau-1} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(v_2^\top \Gamma_{x}^{-1/2} \left(\prod_{k = 0}^{\ell-1} (u_{t-k} \circ A)\right) B u_{t-\ell}\right)^4 \right]^{1/4}, \nonumber \\
    &+ \sum_{\ell= 1}^{\tau-1} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left( v_2^\top\Gamma_{x}^{-1/2} \left(\prod_{k = 0}^{\ell-1} (u_{t-k} \circ A)\right)  w_{t-\ell}\right)^4 \right]^{1/4}, \nonumber \\
    &\leq  \sum_{\ell= 1}^{\tau - 1} (3)^{1/4} \left\lVert v_2^\top \Gamma_{x}^{-1/2} \left(\prod_{k = 0}^{\ell-1} (u_{t-k} \circ A)\right) B \right\rVert_{\ell_2} + \sum_{\ell= 1}^{\tau-1} (3)^{1/4} \sigma \left\lVert v_2^\top \Gamma_{\tilde{x}}^{-1/2} \left(\prod_{k = 0}^{\ell-1} (u_{t-k} \circ A)\right) \right\rVert_{\ell_2}, \nonumber \\
    & \leq (3)^{1/4}\left\|{v_2}\right\|_{\ell_2} \left\lVert \Gamma_{x}^{-1/2} \right\rVert_{\textup{op}}(\sigma+\lVert B \rVert_\textup{op})\sum_{\ell = 1}^{\tau-1}\left\lVert \prod_{k = 0}^{\ell-1} (u_{t-k} \circ A) \right\rVert_\textup{op}, \nonumber \\
    & \stackrel{\text{(c)}}{\leq} (3)^{1/4}\left\|{v_2}\right\|_{\ell_2} \left\lVert \Gamma_{x}^{-1/2} \right\rVert_{\textup{op}}(\sigma+\lVert B \rVert_\textup{op})\sum_{\ell = 1}^{L-1} \kappa \rho^{\ell-1}, \nonumber \\
    & \leq (3)^{1/4}\left\|{v_2}\right\|_{\ell_2} \left\lVert \Gamma_{x}^{-1/2} \right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\frac{\kappa(\sigma+\lVert B \rVert_\textup{op})}{1-\rho}, \label{eqn:second_term_bds_app}
\end{align}
where we get (b) from using Minkowski's inequality and (c) from Assumption~\ref{assump:stability}. Finally, using the law of total expectation along-with \eqref{eqn:firt_term_bds_app} and \eqref{eqn:second_moment_bds_app}, the third term in \eqref{eqn:fourth_moment_bds_app_v1} can be upper bounded as follows:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(u_{t}^\top V_3 x_{t,\tau}' \right)^4 \right]^{1/4} \leq (9)^{1/4}\left\|{v_3}\right\|_{\ell_2} \left\lVert \Gamma_{x}^{-1/2} \right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\frac{\kappa(\sigma+\lVert B \rVert_\textup{op})}{1-\rho}. \label{eqn:third_term_bds_app}
\end{align}
Hence, plugging in \eqref{eqn:firt_term_bds_app}, \eqref{eqn:second_term_bds_app}, and \eqref{eqn:third_term_bds_app} into \eqref{eqn:fourth_moment_bds_app_v1}, we get the following:
\begin{align}
      \mathbb{E} [( v^\top \phi_{t,\tau}^{'{}})^4]^{1/4} &\leq (3)^{1/4} \left\|{v_1}\right\|_{\ell_2} + (3)^{1/4}\left\|{v_2}\right\|_{\ell_2} \left\lVert \Gamma_{x}^{-1/2} \right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\frac{\kappa(\sigma+\lVert B \rVert_\textup{op})}{1-\rho} \nonumber \\
     &+ (9)^{1/4}\left\|{v_3}\right\|_{\ell_2} \left\lVert \Gamma_{x}^{-1/2} \right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\frac{\kappa(\sigma+\lVert B \rVert_\textup{op})}{1-\rho} \nonumber \\
     &\stackrel{\text{(d)}}{\leq}  \left(3^{1/2} + (3^{1/2}+ 3)\left\lVert \Gamma_{x}^{-1} \right\rVert_{\textup{op}} \frac{\kappa^2(\sigma+\lVert B \rVert_\textup{op})^2}{(1-\rho)^2} \right)^{1/2} \left(\left\|{v_1}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2 + \left\|{v_2}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2 + \left\|{v_3}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2 \right)^{1/2}, \nonumber \\
     & = \left(3^{1/2} + (3^{1/2}+ 3)\left\lVert \Gamma_{x}^{-1} \right\rVert_{\textup{op}} \frac{\kappa^2(\sigma+\lVert B \rVert_\textup{op})^2}{(1-\rho)^2} \right)^{1/2} \left\|{v}\right\|_{\ell_2}, \label{eqn:fourth_moment_bds_app_v2}
\end{align}
where we get (d) by using Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Combining this with \eqref{eqn:second_moment_bds_app}, we have
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E} [( v^\top \phi_{t,\tau}^{'{}})^4] \leq \left(3^{1/2} + (3^{1/2}+ 3)\left\lVert \Gamma_{x}^{-1} \right\rVert_{\textup{op}} \frac{\kappa^2(\sigma+\lVert B \rVert_\textup{op})^2}{(1-\rho)^2} \right)^{2} \mathbb{E} [( v^\top \phi_{t,\tau}^{'{}})^2]^2.
\end{align}
This completes the proof.

<!-- proof-5: body_md -->
We begin the proof by upper bounding the Euclidean distance between $\phi_t^{}$ and $\phi_{t,\tau}^{}$ as follows:     

\begin{align}
    \left\|{\phi_t^{} - \phi_{t,\tau}^{}}\right\|_{\ell_2} &= \left\lVert \begin{bmatrix} u_{t} \\ x_t \\
    u_{t} \otimes x_{t}\end{bmatrix} -\begin{bmatrix} u_{t} \\ x_{t,\tau} \\
    u_{t} \otimes x_{t,\tau}\end{bmatrix}  \right\rVert_{\ell_2} ,\nonumber \\
    & = \sqrt{1 + d_u} \left\|{x_t - x_{t,\tau}}\right\|_{\ell_2}, \nonumber \\
    &= \sqrt{1 + d_u} \left\lVert \left( \prod_{\ell = 1}^{\tau-1} (u_{t-\ell}\circ A )  \right) x_{t-\tau+1} \right\rVert_{\ell_2}, \nonumber \\
    & \leq \sqrt{1 + d_u} \left\lVert \left( \prod_{\ell = 1}^{\tau-1} (u_{t-\ell}\circ A )  \right) \right\rVert_\textup{op} \left\|{x_{t-\tau+1}}\right\|_{\ell_2}, \nonumber \\
    & \leq \sqrt{1 + d_u}\, \kappa \rho^{\tau-1} \left\|{x_{t-\tau+1}}\right\|_{\ell_2}. \label{eqn:phi_t_minus_truncated_bds_app_v1}
\end{align}
Hence, we need to upper bound the Euclidean norm of the states in \eqref{eqn:BDS-FO}. Under Assumptions~\ref{assump:input+noise} and \ref{assump:stability}, for all $t \in [1,T]$, we have
\begin{align}
    \left\|{x_t}\right\|_{\ell_2} &= \left\|{\sum_{\ell = 1}^{t} \left(\prod_{k = 1}^{\ell-1} (u_{t-k} \circ A)\right) B u_{t-\ell} + \sum_{\ell = 1}^t \left(\prod_{k = 1}^{\ell-1} (u_{t-k} \circ A)\right)  w_{t-\ell}}\right\|_{\ell_2}, \nonumber \\
    & \leq \sum_{\ell = 1}^{t} \left\|{\prod_{k = 1}^{\ell-1} (u_{t-k} \circ A)}\right\|_{\textup{op}}  \left\|{B u_{t-\ell} + w_{t-\ell}}\right\|_{\ell_2}, \nonumber \\
    & \stackrel{\text{(a)}}{\leq} \sum_{\ell = 1}^{t} \kappa\rho^{\ell-1} \sqrt{\sigma^2 + \left\|{B}\right\|_{\textup{op}}^2}\sqrt{d_x + \log(T/\delta)}, \nonumber \\
&\leq \frac{\kappa(1-\rho^t)}{1-\rho} \sqrt{\sigma^2 + \left\|{B}\right\|_{\textup{op}}^2}\sqrt{d_x + \log(T/\delta)}, \nonumber \\
    &\leq \frac{\kappa\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \left\|{B}\right\|_{\textup{op}}^2}}{1-\rho} \sqrt{d_x + \log(T/\delta)} =: \beta_{x,\delta} \label{eqn:x_t_bound_bds_app}
\end{align}
with probability at least $1-\delta$, where we obtain (a) from the observation that $B u_{t-\ell} + w_{t-\ell}$ is a $(\sigma^2 + \left\|{B}\right\|_{\textup{op}}^2)$-subgaussian random vector in $\mathbb{R}^{d_x}$. Hence, plugging \eqref{eqn:x_t_bound_bds_app} into \eqref{eqn:phi_t_minus_truncated_bds_app_v1}, with probability at least $1-\delta$ we have

\begin{align}
    \left\|{\phi_t^{} - \phi_{t,\tau}^{}}\right\|_{\ell_2} & \leq \sqrt{1 + d_u}\, \kappa \rho^{\tau-1} \frac{\kappa\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \left\|{B}\right\|_{\textup{op}}^2}}{1-\rho} \sqrt{d_x + \log(T/\delta)}, \nonumber \\
    &\leq \frac{\kappa^2\rho^{\tau-1} \sqrt{\sigma^2 + \left\|{B}\right\|_{\textup{op}}^2}}{1-\rho} \sqrt{(d_u+1)(d_x + \log(T/\delta))} \label{eqn:phi_t_minus_truncated_bds_app_v2}
\end{align}
Moreover, using \eqref{eqn:x_t_bound_bds_app}, we can also upper bound the Euclidean norm of the covariates $\phi_t^{}$ as follows:
\begin{align}
\left\|{\phi_t^{}}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2 &= \left\|{u_t}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2 + \left\|{x_t}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2 + \left\|{u_t \otimes x_t}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2, \nonumber \\
&= \left\|{u_t}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2 + \left(\left\|{u_t}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2+1\right)\left\|{x_t}\right\|_{\ell_2}^2, \nonumber \\
& \leq d_u + \frac{\kappa^2(\sigma^2 + \left\|{B}\right\|_{\textup{op}}^2)}{(1-\rho)^2} (d_u+1)(d_x + \log(T/\delta)). \label{eqn:phi_t_bound_bds_app}
\end{align}
Finally, using \eqref{eqn:phi_t_minus_truncated_bds_app_v2} and \eqref{eqn:phi_t_bound_bds_app}, with probability at least $1-\delta$, we have

\begin{align}
  \left\lVert \sum_{t=1}^T \phi_t^{} (\phi_t^{})^\top - \sum_{t=1}^T \phi_{t,\tau}^{} (\phi_{t,\tau}^{})^\top \right\rVert_\textup{op}   &\leq  \sum_{t=1}^T \left\lVert \phi_t^{} (\phi_t^{})^\top - \phi_{t,\tau}^{} (\phi_{t,\tau}^{})^\top \right\rVert_\textup{op}, \nonumber \\
  &\leq \sum_{t=1}^T \left(\left\|{\phi_{t}^{}}\right\|_{\ell_2} + \left\|{\phi_{t,\tau}^{}}\right\|_{\ell_2} \right) \left\|{\phi_{t}^{} - \phi_{t,\tau}^{}}\right\|_{\ell_2}, \nonumber \\
  &\leq \sum_{t=1}^T 2\left(\sqrt{d_u} + \sqrt{d_u +1}\left\|{x_t}\right\|_{\ell_2} \right)\sqrt{d_u+1}\, \kappa \rho^{\tau-1} \left\|{x_{t-\tau+1}}\right\|_{\ell_2} \nonumber \\
&\leq 2 T \kappa\rho^{\tau-1} \bigg( \frac{\kappa\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \left\|{B}\right\|_{\textup{op}}^2}}{1-\rho} \sqrt{d_u(d_u+1)(d_x + \log(T/\delta))}\nonumber \\
  & + \frac{\kappa^2(\sigma^2 + \left\|{B}\right\|_{\textup{op}}^2)}{(1-\rho)^2} (d_u+1)(d_x + \log(T/\delta))\bigg), \nonumber \\
  &\leq 2 T \kappa^2\rho^{\tau-1} \frac{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \left\|{B}\right\|_{\textup{op}}^2}}{1-\rho} \bigg(  \sqrt{d_u(d_u+1)(d_x + \log(T/\delta))}\nonumber \\
  & + \frac{\kappa\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \left\|{B}\right\|_{\textup{op}}^2}}{(1-\rho)} (d_u+1)(d_x + \log(T/\delta))\bigg), \nonumber \\
& \leq \frac{T(1 \land \lambda_{\min}(\Gamma_x^{(\tau-1)}))}{8},
\end{align}
where we get the last inequality by choosing $\tau$ via
\begin{align}

    \impliedby & \tau \geq 1+ \log \left( \frac{32 \kappa \left( \sqrt{d_u(d_u+1)}\beta_{x,\delta} + (d_u+1)\beta_{x,\delta}^2\right)}{(1 \land \lambda_{\min}(\Gamma_x^{(\tau-1)}))}\right) \bigg/ (1-\rho),

\begin{align}

\end{align}
This completes the proof.

First, using the variational form of the operator norm, we see that:
\begin{align*}
   \left \Vert \sum_{t=\tau}^T \phi_t  z_t^\top   \right\Vert_\textup{op}  = \sup_{\theta \in \mathcal{S}^{d_{\phi} - 1}, \lambda \in \mathcal{S}^{d_y - 1}}   \sum_{t=\tau}^T (\theta^\top\phi_t) (\lambda^\top z_t). 
\end{align*}
We use a $1/4$-net argument to bound the supremum. Let $\mathcal{M}$ (resp. $\mathcal{N}$) be $1/4$-nets with minimal cardinality of the sphere $\mathcal{S}^{d_{\phi} - 1}$ (resp. $\mathcal{S}^{d_y - 1}$). Thus, using Lemma \ref{lem:net argument}   we obtain: for all $r > 0$: 
\begin{align}\label{eqn:eq net argument}
    \mathbb{P}\left(    \left \Vert \sum_{t=\tau}^T \phi_t  z_t^\top   \right\Vert_\textup{op}  > 2 r \right) \le 9^{d_{\phi}+ d_y}\max_{\theta \in \mathcal{M}, \lambda \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{P}\left(  \sum_{t=\tau}^T (\theta^\top\phi_t) (\lambda^\top z_t) > r \right).
\end{align}
It remains to bound $\sum_{t=\tau}^T (\theta^\top\phi_t) (\lambda^\top z_t)$ with high probability uniformly over the unit spheres. Let $\theta \in \mathcal{S}^{d_{\phi} - 1}$, and $\lambda \in \mathcal{S}^{d_y - 1}$. We have: 
    \begin{align*}

    \end{align}
    where we introduce in $(c)$ the functions $f_s(\cdot)$. Specifically, the right side of \eqref{eq:martingale bias} is a sum of martingale differences when the indices are revealed in reverse order. \label{lem:stability notion}
    Let $\mathcal{U}$ be a bounded and non-empty subset of $\mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ such that $\rho(\mathcal{U} \circ A) < 1$, then for all $ \rho \in (\rho(\mathcal{U} \circ A), 1)$, there exists $\kappa \ge 1$ such that the  system is $(\mathcal{U} , \kappa, \rho)$-uniformly stable.
<!-- end lemma-8 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
Let $\rho \in (\rho(\mathcal{U} \circ A), 1)$. We recall that the joint spectral radius of $\mathcal{U} \circ A$ is defined as follows: 
    \begin{align}
        \rho(\mathcal{U} \circ A ) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \sup_{M_1, \dots, M_k \in \mathcal{U} \circ A} \Vert M_1 M_2 \cdots M_k \Vert_\textup{op}^{1/k}. 
    \end{align}
    Since by assumption the limit exists, we have by definition that, 
    \begin{align*}
         \forall \; \epsilon > 0, \; \exists \; k_0 \ge 1: \;  \forall \; k \ge k_0, \quad  \left\vert \sup_{M_1, \dots, M_k \in \mathcal{U} \circ A }\Vert M_1 M_2 \cdots M_k \Vert_\textup{op}^{1/k} - \rho(\mathcal{U} \circ A) \right\vert < \epsilon \rho(\mathcal{U} \circ A).  
ˇ    \end{align*}
    Thus choosing $\epsilon > 0$, such that $\rho \ge  (1+\epsilon) \rho(\mathcal{U} \circ A)$, we can find $k_0$ such that, \begin{align*}
        \forall \; k \ge k_0, \qquad \sup_{M_1, \dots, M_k \in \mathcal{U} \circ A }\Vert M_1 M_2 \cdots M_k \Vert_\textup{op} <  \rho^k.
    \end{align*}
    Now, we can further define 
    \begin{align}
        \kappa = \max\left\lbrace 1, \sup_{1 \le k \le k_0}\sup_{ M_1, \dots, M_k \in \mathcal{U} \circ A }\frac{\Vert M_1 M_2 \cdots M_k \Vert_\textup{op}^{1/k}}{\rho}  \right\rbrace.
    \end{align}
    We note that $\kappa$ is well defined because $\mathcal{U} \circ A$ is a bounded set of matrices since $\mathcal{U}$ is bounded. Indeed, for all $M \in \mathcal{U} \circ A$, we have $\Vert M\Vert_\textup{op} \le \max\lbrace 1, \sup_{u \in \mathcal{U} }\Vert u \Vert_{\ell_\infty}\rbrace (\Vert A_0 \Vert_\textup{op}  + \dots + \Vert A_{d_u} \Vert_\textup{op} )$. This concludes the proof.

\label{lem:upper bound stability}
Suppose Assumption \ref{assump:stability} holds and 
let $(u_t)_{t \ge 0}$ be a sequence of inputs taking values in $\sqrt{d_u} \cdot\mathcal{S}^{d_u-1}$, and let $F_t =  \begin{bmatrix}
     I_{d_y} &
    C   &
    C \,  (u_{t-1}\circ A )  &
\cdots &
    C \prod_{\ell = 1}^{\tau-2} (u_{t-\ell}\circ A ) 
 \end{bmatrix}$. We have

\end{align}
Consequently, we have \begin{align}\label{eq:stability second bound}
    \left\Vert F_t  \right\Vert_\textup{op} \le   1 + \frac{\kappa \Vert C \Vert_\textup{op}  }{1- \rho}
\end{align}

<!-- lemma-10: body_md -->
\label{lemma:hyper_to_moment_app}
     Let $Q, R \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u \times d}$, and $\gamma >0$. Let $u$ be an isotropic and $(4,2,\gamma)$-hypercontractive random vector taking values in $\mathbb{R}^{d_u}$. Then, we have
    \begin{align}
        \mathbb{E}[(u^\top Q Q^\top u)(u^\top R R^\top u)] \leq \gamma \lVert Q \rVert_F^2 \lVert R \rVert_F^2 
    \end{align}
<!-- end lemma-10 -->
<!-- proof-12: body_md -->
Note that since $u$ is isotropic and $(4,2, \gamma)$-hypercontractive, therefore, for any $Q = [q_1~~q_2~~ \cdots~~q_d] \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u \times d}$ and $R = [r_1~~r_2~~ \cdots~~r_d] \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u \times d}$, we have 
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{E}[(u^\top Q Q^\top u)(u^\top R R^\top u)] & = \mathbb{E}\left[\left( \sum_{i=1}^d (u^\top q_i)^2 \right) \left( \sum_{i=1}^d (u^\top r_i)^2 \right)  \right], \\
    & =  \sum_{1 \le i,j \le d} \mathbb{E}\left[(u^\top q_i)^2(u^\top r_j)^2  \right], \\
    & \stackrel{\text{(a)}}{\leq} \sum_{1 \le i,j \le d}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[ (u^\top q_i)^4   \right]} \sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[  (u^\top r_j)^4  \right]}, \\
    & \stackrel{\text{(b)}}{\leq} \sum_{1 \le i,j \le d} \sqrt{\gamma} \mathbb{E}\left[ (u^\top q_i)^2   \right] \sqrt{\gamma}\mathbb{E}\left[  (u^\top r_j)^2  \right], \\
    & \le \gamma \sum_{1 \le i,j \le d}  \lVert q_i \rVert_{\ell_2}^2  \lVert r_j \rVert_{\ell_2}^2,  \\
    & \le \gamma  \lVert Q \rVert_F^2 \lVert R \rVert_F^2 ,  
\end{align*}
where we obtain (a) by using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and (b) from the $(4,2, \gamma)$-hypercontractivity assumption. This completes the proof.

    Fix a realization of $W$ such that $\lambda_{\min}(W)<\nu$. Since $W$
    is symmetric, there exists $u\in\mathcal{S}^{d-1}$ such that
    \[
        u^\top W u=\lambda_{\min}(W)<\nu.

    \[
        x^\top W x-u^\top W u
        =(x-u)^\top W x+u^\top W(x-u),

        \left|x^\top W x-u^\top W u\right|

        +\|u\|_2\|W(x-u)\|_2 \\
        &\leq
        2\epsilon\|W\|_{\textup{op}}.
    \end{align*}

        x^\top W x
        \leq
