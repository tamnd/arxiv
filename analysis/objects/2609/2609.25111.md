The paper is organized as follows.
In Section~2, we recall the basic definitions of finite graphs, ramified coverings, and \(G\)-covers. 
In Section~3, we describe ramified covers in terms of voltage assignments with inertia subgroups and prove a connectedness criterion for the derived graphs.
In Section~4, we introduce multiplicity spaces and \(h\)-functions for \(G\)-covers. We prove a ramified Hashimoto's formula, the spanning tree factorization, and the additivity and induction properties of the \(h\)-functions.
In Section~5, we prove the main theorems.

A \textit{graph} \(X=(V_X,E_X,o,t,\iota)\) consists of two sets \(V_X\) and \(E_X\), two maps
\(o,t\colon E_X\to V_X\), and a fixed-point-free involution \(\iota\colon E_X\to E_X\)

Let \(X\) be a finite graph.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The \textit{degree matrix} \(D_X\) of \(X\) is the diagonal matrix indexed by \(V_X\) with
\[
(D_X)_{v,v}\coloneq \deg_{X}(v).
\]
\item The \textit{adjacency matrix} \(A_X\) of \(X\) is the symmetric matrix indexed by \(V_X\) with

\]\item The \textit{Laplacian matrix} \(L_X\) of \(X\) is defined by
\[
L_X\coloneq D_X-A_X.
\]
\end{enumerate}

\label{thm:mattree}
Let \(X\) be a finite connected graph. Then the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\((\arabic*)\)]
\item \(\det(L_X(v))=\kappa(X)\) for every \(v\in V_{X}\), where \(L_X(v)\) denotes the matrix obtained from \(L_X\) by deleting the row and column indexed by \(v\),
\item \([t]\det(tI-L_X)=(-1)^{|V_X|-1}|V_X|\,\kappa(X)\),
\item \(\operatorname{adj}(L_{X})=\kappa(X)\ J\), where \(J\) is the \(|V_{X}|\times |V_{X}|\) matrix all of whose entries are \(1\).
\end{enumerate}

<!-- dfn-3: body_md -->
Let \(Y=(V_Y, E_Y)\) and \(X=(V_X, E_X)\) be graphs. A \textit{morphism} \(f=(f_V,f_E)\colon Y\to X\) of graphs consists of maps \(f_V\colon  V_Y\rightarrow V_X\) and \(f_E\colon E_Y\rightarrow E_X\) satisfying the following conditions, for all \(e\in E_{Y}\),
 \begin{enumerate}
	\item \(f_V(o(e))=o(f_E(e))\),
	\item \(f_V(t(e))=t(f_E(e))\),
	\item \(\overline{f_E(e)}=f_E(\overline{e})\).
\end{enumerate}
A morphism \(f\) is an \textit{isomorphism} if \(f_V\) and \(f_E\) are bijective. In this case, \(Y\) and \(X\) are said to be \textit{isomorphic}. When no confusion can arise, we write \(f\) instead of \(f_V\) and \(f_E\).
<!-- end dfn-3 -->
<!-- dfn-4: body_md -->
\label{dfn:ram}
Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be finite connected graphs. A morphism
\(\pi=(\pi_V,\pi_E)\colon Y\to X\) is called a \textit{cover} if the following
conditions are satisfied.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The maps
\(\pi_V\colon V_Y\to V_X\)
and
\(\pi_E\colon E_Y\to E_X\)
are surjective.
\item For each \(w\in V_Y\), there exists a positive integer
\(m_w(Y/X)\) such that 
\(\pi_{E}\colon E_{Y}^{w}\to E_{X}^{\pi_{V}(w)}\)
is \(m_{w}(Y/X)\)-to-\(1\).
\end{enumerate}

The integer \(m_w(Y/X)\) is called the \textit{ramification index} of
\(\pi\) at \(w\).
The cover \(\pi\) is called \textit{unramified} if
\(m_w(Y/X)=1\) for every \(w\in V_Y\), and \textit{ramified} otherwise.
<!-- end dfn-4 -->
<!-- rmk-1: body_md -->
Our convention for the empty map \(\emptyset\to\emptyset\) ensures that the ramification index is uniquely determined when \(E_X^{\pi_V(w)}=\emptyset\).
This occurs only when both \(Y\) and \(X\) consist of a single vertex and have no edges.

<!-- dfn-5: body_md -->
\label{dfn:Galois}
	Let \(G\) be a finite group. A cover \(\pi\colon Y\to X\) is called a \textit{\(G\)-cover} if \(G\) acts faithfully on \(Y\) by graph automorphisms and the following conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Each fiber of \(\pi_{V}\) and \(\pi_{E}\) is \(G\)-stable,
	\item \(G\) acts transitively on each fiber of \(\pi_{V}\),
	\item \(G\) acts freely and transitively on each fiber of \(\pi_{E}\).
\end{enumerate}

\label{dfn:vol}
Let \(X\) be a finite connected graph, 
let \(G\) be a finite group, 
let \(\alpha\colon E_X\to G\) be a voltage assignment,
and \(\mathcal{I}=\{I_v\}_{v\in V_X}\) a family of subgroups of \(G\). 
The quadruple
\[
(X,G,\alpha,\mathcal{I})
\]
is called a \textit{voltage graph}.
Its \textit{derived graph} \(X(\alpha,\mathcal{I})\) is defined by
\[
V_{X(\alpha,\mathcal{I})}\coloneq \bigsqcup_{v\in V_X}\bigl((G/I_v)\times\{v\}\bigr),
\qquad
E_{X(\alpha,\mathcal{I})}\coloneq G\times E_X.
\]
For \((\sigma,e)\in E_{X(\alpha,\mathcal{I})}\), we define
\[
o((\sigma,e))\coloneq (\sigma I_{o(e)},o(e)),\quad
t((\sigma,e))\coloneq (\sigma\alpha(e)I_{t(e)},t(e)),\quad
\overline{(\sigma,e)}\coloneq (\sigma\alpha(e),\overline{e}).
\]
If \(I_v=\{1_G\}\) for every \(v\in V_X\), we write \(X(\alpha)\) instead of \(X(\alpha,\mathcal{I})\).

\label{prp:connectedness}
Let \((X,G,\alpha,\mathcal{I})\) be a voltage graph.
Fix a vertex \(v_0\in V_X\). For each \(v\in V_X\), choose a path \(p_v\) in \(X\) from \(v_0\) to \(v\),
with \(p_{v_0}\) equal to the trivial path at \(v_0\), 
and put \(g_v\coloneq \alpha(p_v)\).
Then \(X(\alpha,\mathcal{I})\) is connected if and only if
\[
G=
\left\langle
\alpha(C),\ g_v\sigma g_v^{-1}
\ \middle|\
C \text{ is a closed path at }v_0,\ 
v\in V_X,\ \sigma\in I_v
\right\rangle.
\]
In particular, \(X(\alpha)\) is connected if and only if  
\(
G=
\left\langle
\alpha(C)
\ \middle|\
C \text{ is a closed path at }v_0\right\rangle\).
<!-- end prp-3 -->
<!-- proof-1: body_md -->
We set
\(
H\coloneq
\left\langle
\alpha(C),\ g_v\sigma g_v^{-1}
\ \middle|\
C \text{ is a closed path based at }v_0,\ 
v\in V_X,\ \sigma\in I_v
\right\rangle
\)
and \(Y\coloneq X(\alpha,\mathcal{I})\).
We define a subset \(R\) of \(G\)
\[
R\coloneq
\{\,r\in G\mid \text{ there exists a path from } (I_{v_0},v_0) \text{ to } (rI_{v_0},v_0) \text{ in } Y \, \}.
\]
Then \(R\) is a subgroup of \(G\).
Indeed, the trivial path at \((I_{v_0},v_0)\) shows that \(1_G\in R\).
If \(r,s\in R\), let \(P\) and \(Q\) be paths from
\((I_{v_0},v_0)\) to \((rI_{v_0},v_0)\) and
\((sI_{v_0},v_0)\), respectively. Then the concatenation of
\(P\) and \(rQ\) shows that \(rs\in R\). Similarly,
\(r^{-1}\overline{P}\) shows that \(r^{-1}\in R\).

Suppose first that \(H=G\).
If \(C\) is a closed path based at \(v_0\), 
then there is a lift of \(C\) starting at
\((I_{v_0},v_0)\) and ending at
\((\alpha(C)I_{v_0},v_0)\). Hence \(\alpha(C)\in R\).
Moreover, for \(v\in V_X\) and \(\sigma\in I_v\), there is a lift of
\(p_v\) from \((I_{v_0},v_0)\) to
\((g_vI_v,v)=(g_v\sigma I_v,v).\)
Starting from this vertex, there is a lift of \(\overline{p_v}\)
ending at
\((g_v\sigma g_v^{-1}I_{v_0},v_0).\)
Thus \(g_v\sigma g_v^{-1}\in R\), and hence \(H\subseteq R\).
Since \(H=G\), we have \(R=G\).

Let \((\tau I_v,v)\in V_Y\). Since
\(\tau g_v^{-1}\in R\), there is a path from
\((I_{v_0},v_0)\) to
\(
(\tau g_v^{-1}I_{v_0},v_0).
\)
Following this path by a lift of \(p_v\), we reach
\(
(\tau g_v^{-1}g_vI_v,v)=(\tau I_v,v).
\)
Therefore \(Y\) is connected.

Conversely, suppose that \(Y\) is connected, and let \(\tau\in G\).
Choose a path in \(Y\) from
\((I_{v_0},v_0)\) to \((\tau I_{v_0},v_0)\), with successive edges
\((\sigma_1,e_1),\ldots,(\sigma_n,e_n).\)
Put
\(v_j\coloneq t(e_j)\)
and
\(w_j\coloneq t((\sigma_j,e_j))\)
for
\(1\leq j\leq n\),
and \(w_0\coloneq(I_{v_0},v_0)\).

We claim that, for each \(j=0,\dots,n\), there exists \(h_j\in H\) such that
\[
w_j=(h_jg_{v_j}I_{v_j},v_j).
\]
This is clear for \(j=0\), since \(g_{v_0}=1_G\).
Suppose that the claim holds for some \(0\leq j<n\).
Since
\((\sigma_{j+1}I_{v_{j}},v_{j})=w_j=(h_jg_{v_j}I_{v_j},v_j),\)
there exists \(\sigma\in I_{v_j}\) such that
\(\sigma_{j+1}=h_jg_{v_j}\sigma.\)
Hence we have
\begin{align*}
w_{j+1}
&=
(h_jg_{v_j}\sigma\alpha(e_{j+1})I_{v_{j+1}},v_{j+1})\\
&=
\bigl(
h_j
(g_{v_j}\sigma g_{v_j}^{-1})
\alpha(p_{v_j}e_{j+1}\overline{p_{v_{j+1}}})
g_{v_{j+1}}I_{v_{j+1}},
v_{j+1}
\bigr).
\end{align*}
Both
\(g_{v_j}\sigma g_{v_j}^{-1}\)
and
\(\alpha(p_{v_j}e_{j+1}\overline{p_{v_{j+1}}})\)
belong to \(H\), so the claim follows by induction.

In particular,
\[
(\tau I_{v_0},v_0)=w_n=(h_ng_{v_0}I_{v_0},v_0)=(h_nI_{v_0},v_0)
\]
for some \(h_n\in H\).
Since \(I_{v_0}\subseteq H\), we obtain \(\tau\in H\).
As \(\tau\in G\) was arbitrary, \(G=H\).

The subgroup \(H\) in \cref{prp:connectedness} is independent of the
choice of the paths \(p_v\), and is independent of the base vertex
\(v_0\) up to conjugation in \(G\).

left multiplication by \(\alpha(p_v'\overline{p_v})\), which is the voltage of a closed path
based at \(v_0\), and hence does not change the subgroup in \cref{prp:connectedness}.
If the base vertex is changed from \(v_0\) to \(v_1\), then
the subgroup \(H\) in \cref{prp:connectedness} is replaced by
\(\alpha(r)^{-1}H\alpha(r)\), where \(r\) is a path
from \(v_0\) to \(v_1\).
Thus the condition in \cref{prp:connectedness} is independent of these choices.

<!-- lem-1: body_md -->
\label{lem:dimension-multiplicity}
Let \(Y/X\) be a \(G\)-cover. For each \(v\in V_X\), choose
\(w_v\in\pi_V^{-1}(v)\).
Then we have the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\((\arabic*)\)]
\item For every representation \(\rho\) of \(G\), we obtain
\[
s_\rho=\sum_{v\in V_X}\dim V_\rho^{\operatorname{Stab}_G(w_v)}.
\]
In particular, we have \(s_{\mathbf{1}_G}=|V_X|\).
\item The number of vertices of \(Y\) is given by
\[
|V_Y|=\sum_{\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}d_\rho s_\rho.
\]
\end{enumerate}
<!-- end lem-1 -->
<!-- proof-3: body_md -->
Since \(G\) acts transitively on each fiber \(\pi_V^{-1}(v)\) with
stabilizer \(I_v\coloneq \operatorname{Stab}_G(w_v)\), we have
\[
\mathbb{C}[V_Y]
\cong
\bigoplus_{v\in V_{X}}
\mathbb{C}[\pi^{-1}(v)]
\cong
\bigoplus_{v\in V_{X}}
\mathbb{C}[G/I_{v}]
\cong
\bigoplus_{v\in V_X}
\operatorname{Ind}_{I_v}^{G}(\mathbf{1}_{I_v}).
\]
as \(\mathbb{C}[G]\)-modules.
Hence, by Frobenius reciprocity,
\begin{align*}
s_\rho
&=
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G(V_\rho,\mathbb{C}[V_Y])\\
&=
\sum_{v\in V_X}
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G
\bigl(
V_\rho,\operatorname{Ind}_{I_v}^{G}(\mathbf{1}_{I_v})
\bigr)\\
&=
\sum_{v\in V_X}
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{I_v}
\bigl(
V_\rho,\mathbf{1}_{I_v}
\bigr)\\
&=
\sum_{v\in V_X}\dim V_\rho^{I_v},
\end{align*}
which proves (1).
We remark that the right-hand side of (1) is independent of the choice of \(w_v\), since the stabilizers of two vertices in the same fiber are conjugate in \(G\).

Finally, the evaluation maps induce a \(G\)-equivariant isomorphism
\[
\mathbb{C}[V_Y] \cong \bigoplus_{\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)} V_\rho\otimes\mathcal{M}_\rho(Y).
\]
Taking dimensions yields
\[
|V_Y|=\sum_{\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}d_\rho s_\rho,
\]
which proves (2).

\label{thm:ramified-hashimoto}
For a \(G\)-cover \(Y/X\),
\[
h_{Y/X}'(\mathbf{1}_{G},1)=-\frac{2\chi(Y)\kappa(X)}{|G|}\prod_{v\in V_X}m_{v}(Y/X).

In particular, if \(Y/X\) is unramified, then \(h_{Y/X}'(\mathbf{1}_{G},1)=-2\chi(X)\kappa(X).\)

=

\]
as desired.

<!-- lem-2: body_md -->
\label{lem:main}
Let \(Y/X\) be a \(G\)-cover, and let \(H\) be a subgroup of \(G\).
For each \(\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}\), let
\(a_\rho^H\) be the non-negative integer determined by
\[
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}(\mathbf{1}_{H})=\mathbf{1}_{G}\oplus \bigoplus_{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}\rho^{\oplus a_{\rho}^{H}}
\]
Then we have
\[
[G:H]\kappa(X_H)
=
f_{H}(Y/X)
\kappa(X)
\hspace{-4mm}
\prod_{\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}
h_{Y/X}(\rho,1)^{a_\rho^H}.
\]
<!-- end lem-2 -->
<!-- proof-9: body_md -->
Applying \cref{main2} to the \(G\)-cover
\(Y\to X\) and the \(H\)-cover \(Y\to X_H\), respectively, gives
\[
\kappa(Y)
=
\frac{\kappa(X)}{|G|}
\prod_{v\in V_{X}}m_{v}(Y/X)\prod_{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus \{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}h_{Y/X}(\rho,1)^{d_{\rho}},
\]
\[
\kappa(Y)
=
\frac{\kappa(X_H)}{|H|}
\prod_{x\in V_{X_{H}}}m_{x}(Y/X_{H})\prod_{\tau\in \operatorname{Irr}(H)\setminus \{\mathbf{1}_{H}\}}h_{Y/X_{H}}(\tau,1)^{d_{\tau}}.
\]
Equating these two expressions for \(\kappa(Y)\) and using the definition of \(f_H(Y/X)\), we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c}
[G:H]\kappa(X_{H})=f_{H}(Y/X)\kappa(X)
\frac{
\displaystyle
\prod_{\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_G\}}
h_{Y/X}(\rho,1)^{d_\rho}
}{
\displaystyle
\prod_{\tau\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_H\}}
h_{Y/X_H}(\tau,1)^{d_\tau}
}.
\end{equation}
Since \(\operatorname{Ind}_H^G(\operatorname{reg}_H)\cong\operatorname{reg}_G\), it follows from
\cref{prp:additivity-h-function,thm:induction-h-function} that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a}
\prod_{\tau\in \operatorname{Irr}(H)}h_{Y/X_{H}}(\tau,u)^{d_{\tau}}=\prod_{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)}h_{Y/X}(\rho,u)^{d_{\rho}}.
\end{equation}
Since \(\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}(\mathbf{1}_{H})=\mathbf{1}_{G}\oplus \bigoplus_{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}\rho^{\oplus a_{\rho}^{H}}\),
the induction property and the multiplicativity of \(h\)-functions give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b}
h_{Y/X_{H}}(\mathbf{1}_{H},u)=h_{Y/X}(\mathbf{1}_{G},u)\prod_{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}h_{Y/X}(\rho,u)^{a_{\rho}^{H}}.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:a} and \eqref{eq:b},
\begin{align*}
\frac{
\displaystyle
\prod_{\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_G\}}
h_{Y/X}(\rho,u)^{d_\rho}
}{
\displaystyle
\prod_{\tau\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_H\}}
h_{Y/X_H}(\tau,u)^{d_\tau}
}
&\overset{\eqref{eq:a}}{=}
\frac{
h_{Y/X_{H}}(\mathbf{1}_{H},u)
}{
h_{Y/X}(\mathbf{1}_{G},u)
}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:b}}{=}
\prod_{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}h_{Y/X}(\rho,u)^{a_{\rho}^{H}}.
\end{align*}
Therefore, by \eqref{eq:c} we conclude that
\[
[G:H]\kappa(X_H)
=
f_{H}(Y/X)
\kappa(X)
\hspace{-4mm}
\prod_{\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}
h_{Y/X}(\rho,1)^{a_\rho^H},
\]
as desired.

<!-- cor-2: body_md -->
\label{cor:normal-intermediate-complexity}
Let \(Y/X\) be a \(G\)-cover, and let \(H\) be a normal subgroup of \(G\).
Then
\[
[G:H]\kappa(X_H)
=
f_H(Y/X)\kappa(X)
\prod_{\substack{
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_G\}\\
H\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\rho
}}
h_{Y/X}(\rho,1)^{d_\rho}.
\]
<!-- end cor-2 -->
<!-- proof-10: body_md -->
For each \(\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_G\}\), let
\(a_\rho^H\) denote the multiplicity of \(\rho\) in
\(\operatorname{Ind}_H^G(\mathbf{1}_H)\), as in \cref{lem:main}.
By Frobenius reciprocity, the multiplicity \(a_\rho^H\) is
\[
a_\rho^H
=
\dim \operatorname{Hom}_G(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_H,\rho)
=
\dim \operatorname{Hom}_H(\mathbf{1}_H,\operatorname{Res}_H^G\rho)
=
\dim V_\rho^H.
\]
Since \(H\) is normal in \(G\), the subspace \(V_\rho^H\) is
\(G\)-stable. Hence, by the irreducibility of \(\rho\),
\[
V_\rho^H=
\begin{cases}
V_\rho,& H\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\rho,\\
0,& H\not\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\rho.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus
\[
a_\rho^H=
\begin{cases}
d_\rho,& H\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\rho,\\
0,& H\not\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\rho.
\end{cases}
\]
The assertion follows from \cref{lem:main}.

<!-- dfn-12: body_md -->
\label{dfn:mob}
Let \(\mathscr{L}\) be a finite partially ordered set. The \textit{M\"{o}bius function} of \(\mathscr{L}\) is the function 
\(\mu\colon \mathscr{L} \times \mathscr{L} \to \mathbb{Z}\) satisfying the following condition 
\[
\mu(x,y)=\begin{cases}
	\hfill 1 \hfill & \text{if \(x=y\),}\\
	\displaystyle{-\sum_{\substack{z\in \mathscr{L} \\ x\leq z< y}}\mu(x,z)} & \text{if \(x<y\),}\\
	\hfill 0 \hfill & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases} 
\]

<!-- prp-7: body_md -->
\label{Moinv}
Let \(\mathscr{L}\) be a finite partially ordered set. Let \(f,g\colon\mathscr{L}\to A\) be two maps, where \(A\) is an abelian group. Then we have
\[
\text{\(g(x)=\sum_{\substack{y\in \mathscr{L}\\y\geq x}}f(y)\) if and only if \(f(x)=\sum_{\substack{y\in \mathscr{L}\\y\geq x}}\mu(x,y) g(y)\)}
\]

\label{dfn:h}
For a finite group \(G\), we define

\mathcal{H}_{G}\coloneq \{\, H\mid H=\operatorname{Ker}(\rho) \text{ for some }\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\,\}.

<!-- thm-6: body_md -->
\label{main3}
Let \(G\) be a finite group, and let \(\underline{\mathcal{H}}_{G}\) be the partially ordered set defined in \cref{dfn:h}. Let
\(
\mu\colon \underline{\mathcal{H}}_{G}\times \underline{\mathcal{H}}_{G}\to \mathbb{Z}
\)
be the M\"obius function of \(\underline{\mathcal{H}}_{G}\) (cf.~\cref{dfn:mob}). 
Then, for any \(G\)-cover \(Y/X\), we have
\[
\kappa(Y)
=
\frac{1}{|G|}
\left(\prod_{v\in V_X}m_v(Y/X)\right)
\prod_{H\in \mathcal{H}_G}
\left(
\frac{[G:H]\kappa(X_H)}{f_{H}(Y/X)}
\right)^{-\mu(\emptyset,H)}.
\]
<!-- end thm-6 -->
<!-- proof-11: body_md -->
Define two functions \(f,g\colon \underline{\mathcal{H}}_{G}\to \mathbb{C}^{*}\) by 
\[
f(H)\coloneq
\begin{cases}
\hfill \kappa(X) \hfill & \text{if \(H=G,\)}\\
\displaystyle{\prod_{\substack{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\} \\ H=\operatorname{Ker}\rho}}h_{Y/X}(\rho,1)^{d_{\rho}}} & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
\[
g(H)
\coloneq 
\kappa(X)
\hspace{-4mm}
\prod_{\substack{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\} \\ H\subseteq \operatorname{Ker}\rho}}
h_{Y/X}(\rho,1)^{d_{\rho}}.
\]
By the definition of \(f\) and \(g\), for every \(H\in\underline{\mathcal{H}}_G\), we have
\[
g(H)
=
\prod_{\substack{K\in \underline{\mathcal{H}}_{G} \\ H\subseteq K}}
f(K).
\]
By the Möbius inversion formula (see \cref{Moinv}), we have
\[
f(H)
=
\prod_{\substack{K\in \underline{\mathcal{H}}_{G} \\ H\subseteq K}}
g(K)^{\mu(H,K)}.
\]
Since \(f(\emptyset)=1\), we have 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mob}
\prod_{K\in \underline{\mathcal{H}}_{G}}
g(K)^{\mu(\emptyset,K)}
=
1.
\end{equation}
Hence we obtain
\begin{align}\label{eq:5.2}
\frac{|G|\kappa(Y)}{\prod_{v\in V_X}m_{v}(Y/X)}
&\overset{\mathclap{\ref{main2}}}{=}\notag
\kappa(X)
\prod_{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}
h_{Y/X}(\rho,1)^{d_{\rho}}\\
&=\notag
g(\emptyset)\\
&\overset{\mathclap{\eqref{eq:mob}}}{=}
\prod_{H\in \mathcal{H}_{G}}
g(H)^{-\mu(\emptyset,H)}.
\end{align}
Moreover, by \cref{cor:normal-intermediate-complexity}, we have 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:5.3}
g(H)=\frac{[G:H]\kappa(X_H)}{f_{H}(Y/X)}.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:5.2} and \eqref{eq:5.3}, we conclude that
\[
\kappa(Y)
=
\frac{1}{|G|}
\left(\prod_{v\in V_X}m_v(Y/X)\right)
\prod_{H\in \mathcal{H}_G}
\left(
\frac{[G:H]\kappa(X_H)}{f_{H}(Y/X)}
\right)^{-\mu(\emptyset,H)},
\]
as desired.

\label{eg:elementary-abelian-branched}
Let \(\pi\colon Y\to X\) be a \(G\)-cover with
\(
G=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^m
\)
with \(m\geq 2\).
The group \(G\) has \(2^m-1\) subgroups
\(
H_1,\ldots,H_{2^m-1}
\)
of index \(2\), and we have
\[
\underline{\mathcal{H}}_G
=
\{\emptyset,H_1,\ldots,H_{2^m-1},G\}.
\]
The M\"obius function \(\mu\) of \(\underline{\mathcal{H}}_G\) satisfies
\(
\mu(\emptyset,G)=2^m-2,
\)
and 
\(
\mu(\emptyset,H_i)=-1
\)
for all
\(
i\in \{1,\dots,2^{m}-1\}.
\)
Write
\(m_v(Y/X)=2^{r_v}\)
with \(0\leq r_v\leq m\).
Let \(X_i\) denote the intermediate graph corresponding to \(H_i\).

Since \(f_G(Y/X)=1\), applying \cref{main3} gives
\[
\kappa(Y)
=\frac{2^{2^m-m-1}}{\kappa(X)^{2^m-2}}
\frac{2^{\sum_{v\in V_X}r_v}}
     {\prod_{i=1}^{2^m-1}f_{H_i}(Y/X)}
\prod_{i=1}^{2^m-1}\kappa(X_i).
\]
For each \(v\in V_X\), choose a vertex \(w_v\) above \(v\) and put
\(I_v\coloneq\operatorname{Stab}_G(w_v)\), so that \(|I_v|=2^{r_v}\).
Since \(G\) is abelian, \(I_v\) is independent of the choice of
\(w_v\). Let \(\pi_i\colon X_i\to X\) be the natural map.

If \(I_v\subseteq H_i\), then \(\pi_i^{-1}(v)\) consists of two
vertices, each with ramification index \(2^{r_v}\) in \(Y/X_i\).
If \(I_{v}\nsubseteq H_{i}\), \(I_vH_i=G\) and \(|I_v\cap H_i|=2^{r_v-1}\), so
\(\pi_i^{-1}(v)\) consists of one vertex, whose ramification index
in \(Y/X_i\) is \(2^{r_v-1}\). Thus
\[
\prod_{x\in\pi_i^{-1}(v)}m_x(Y/X_i)
=\begin{cases}
2^{2r_v},& I_v\subseteq H_i,\\
2^{r_v-1},& I_v\not\subseteq H_i.
\end{cases}
\]
By the definition of \(f_{H_i}(Y/X)\), it follows that
\[
f_{H_i}(Y/X)
=\prod_{\substack{v\in V_X\\I_v\subseteq H_i}}2^{-r_v}
 \prod_{\substack{v\in V_X\\I_v\not\subseteq H_i}}2.
\]

The subgroups of index \(2\) in \(G\) containing \(I_v\) correspond
to the subgroups of index \(2\) in
\(G/I_v\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{m-r_v}\).
Hence exactly \(2^{m-r_v}-1\) of the subgroups \(H_i\) contain
\(I_v\), while exactly \(2^m-2^{m-r_v}\) of them do not contain \(I_{v}\).
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\prod_{i=1}^{2^m-1}f_{H_i}(Y/X)
&=2^{\sum_{v\in V_X}
\bigl(-r_v(2^{m-r_v}-1)+2^m-2^{m-r_v}\bigr)}\\
&=2^{\sum_{v\in V_X}
\bigl(r_v+2^m-(r_v+1)2^{m-r_v}\bigr)}.
\end{align*}
Substituting this expression into the preceding formula for
\(\kappa(Y)\), we obtain
\begin{equation*}\tag{\(\varheartsuit\)}
\kappa(Y)
=\frac{2^{2^m-m-1+
\sum_{v\in V_X}\bigl((r_v+1)2^{m-r_v}-2^m\bigr)}}
{\kappa(X)^{2^m-2}}
\prod_{i=1}^{2^m-1}\kappa(X_i).
\end{equation*}

In particular, if \(Y/X\) is unramified, then \(r_v=0\) for every
\(v\in V_X\), and hence
\(
(r_v+1)2^{m-r_v}-2^m=0
\)
for every \(v\in V_X\).
Thus \((\varheartsuit)\) reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HMSV}
\kappa(Y)
=
\frac{2^{2^m-m-1}}
{\kappa(X)^{2^m-2}}
\prod_{i=1}^{2^m-1}\kappa(X_i).
\end{equation}
\eqref{eq:HMSV} recovers the unramified formula of Hammer, Mattman, Sands, and Valli\`eres \cite[Theorem~3.6 and Remark~3.7]{HMSV24},
and see also \cite[Example~4.5]{Miz26}.
Therefore, \((\varheartsuit)\) may be regarded as a ramified generalization of \eqref{eq:HMSV}.

\label{artinn}
Let $\chi$ be a rational-valued character of $G$. For every cyclic subgroup $C$ of $G$, let 
\[
a_{\chi}(C)\coloneq \frac{1}{[G:C]}\sum_{\substack{B\in \mathcal{C}_{G} \\ C\subseteq B}}\mu_{\mathrm{cl}}([B:C])\chi(g_B),
\] 
where $g_{B}$ is a generator of $B$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{cl}}\colon \mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}\to \{-1,0,1\}$ is the classical M\"{o}bius function. Then we have $$\chi=\sum_{\substack{C\in \mathcal{C}_{G}}}a_{\chi}(C)\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{C}(\mathbf{1}_{C})}. $$

\label{main4}
Let \(G\) be a finite group, and let \(\mathcal{C}_{G}\) be the set defined in \cref{dfn:c}. 
Then, for any \(G\)-cover \(Y/X\), we have
\[
\kappa(X)
=
\prod_{C\in \mathcal{C}_{G}}
\left(
\frac{[G:C]\kappa(X_{C})}
{f_{C}(Y/X)}
\right)^{a(C)}.
\]
<!-- end thm-8 -->
<!-- proof-13: body_md -->
Let \(a^{C}_{\rho}\) be the integers satisfying 
\[
\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{C}\mathbf{1}_{C}}=\chi_{\mathbf{1}_{G}}+\sum_{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus \{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}a_{\rho}^{C}\chi_{\rho}
\] 
for each \(C\in \mathcal{C}_{G}\).
By \cref{cor:c}, we have 
\begin{align*}
\chi_{\mathbf{1}_{G}}
&\overset{\mathclap{\ref{cor:c}}}{=}
\sum_{C\in \mathcal{C}_{G}}
\left(
a(C)
\left(
\chi_{\mathbf{1}_{G}}
+
\sum_{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus \{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}
a^{C}_{\rho}\chi_{\rho}
\right)
\right)
\\
&=
\left(
\sum_{C\in \mathcal{C}_{G}} a(C)
\right)
\chi_{\mathbf{1}_{G}}
+
\sum_{\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus \{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}
\left(
\sum_{C\in \mathcal{C}_{G}}a(C)a^{C}_{\rho}
\right)
\chi_{\rho}.
\end{align*}
Since the irreducible characters of \(G\) are linearly independent,
we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aC}
\sum_{C\in \mathcal{C}_G} a(C)=1,
\qquad
\sum_{C\in \mathcal{C}_G}a(C)a^C_\rho=0
\end{equation}
for any \(\rho\in \operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}\).
Then we have
\begin{align*}
&\prod_{C\in \mathcal{C}_{G}}([G:C]\kappa(X_{C}))^{a(C)}\\
&\overset{\mathclap{\ref{lem:main}}}{=}
\prod_{C\in \mathcal{C}_{G}}
\left(
f_{C}(Y/X)
\cdot
\kappa(X)
\hspace{-4mm}
\prod_{\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}
h_{Y/X}(\rho,1)^{a_\rho^C}
\right)^{a(C)}
\\
&=
\kappa(X)^{\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_G}a(C)}
\prod_{C\in \mathcal{C}_{G}}
{f_{C}(Y/X)}^{a(C)}
\prod_{\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\setminus\{\mathbf{1}_{G}\}}
h_{Y/X}(\rho,1)^{
\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_G}a(C)a^C_\rho
}
\\
&\overset{\mathclap{\eqref{eq:aC}}}{=}
\kappa(X)
\prod_{C\in \mathcal{C}_{G}}
{f_{C}(Y/X)}^{a(C)},
\end{align*}
as desired.

<!-- rmk-4: body_md -->
We make a few remarks on the main results.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Let \(\overline{\mathcal{C}}_G\coloneq\mathcal{C}_G\sqcup\{\infty\}\) be the
partially ordered set obtained by adjoining a greatest element
\(\infty\) to \(\mathcal{C}_G\), and let
\(\mu\colon\overline{\mathcal{C}}_G\times\overline{\mathcal{C}}_G\to\mathbb{Z}\)
be its M\"obius function.
Then, for every \(C\in\mathcal{C}_G\),
\[
a(C)=-\frac{1}{[G:C]}\mu(C,\infty).
\]
Thus \cref{main4} can equivalently be written as
\[
\kappa(X)
=\prod_{C\in\mathcal{C}_G}
\left(
\frac{[G:C]\kappa(X_C)}{f_C(Y/X)}
\right)^{-\frac{1}{[G:C]}\mu(C,\infty)}.
\]
This is the ramified analogue of \cite[Theorem~4.11]{Miz26}.

\item
Suppose that \(Y/X\) is unramified. Then \(m_v(Y/X)=1\) for
every \(v\in V_X\), and hence \(f_H(Y/X)=1\) for every subgroup
\(H\subseteq G\).
Therefore, \cref{main3,main4} specialize to the Kuroda and
Brauer--Kuroda relations for unramified \(G\)-covers obtained
in \cite[Theorem~4.3 and 4.11]{Miz26}.

\item
A finite group \(G\) is called \textit{irreducibly represented}
if it admits a faithful irreducible complex representation.
Every cyclic group has this property.
If \(G\) is irreducibly represented, then \cref{main3} reduces
to an identity.
Indeed, we have \(\{1_G\}\in\mathcal{H}_G\).
For the M\"obius function \(\mu\) of \(\underline{\mathcal{H}}_G\),
it follows that
\(\mu(\emptyset,\{1_G\})=-1\)
and
\(\mu(\emptyset,H)=0\)
for every \(H\in\mathcal{H}_G\setminus\{\{1_G\}\}\).
Thus \cref{main3} reduces to the identity \(\kappa(Y)=\kappa(Y)\).
A list of groups of order at most \(24\), indicating which
are irreducibly represented, is given in
\cite[Table~A.1]{Miz26}.

\item
Suppose that \(G\) is cyclic in \cref{main4}.
Then every subgroup of \(G\) is cyclic, and
\[
a(C)=\begin{cases}
1,& \text{if \(C=G\)},\\
0,& \text{if \(C\neq G\)}.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus \cref{main4} reduces to the identity
\(\kappa(X)=\kappa(X)\).
If \(G\) is noncyclic, then \(G\notin\mathcal{C}_G\), so
\(\kappa(X)\) does not appear on the right-hand side of the
formula in \cref{main4}. Hence the formula gives a nontrivial
relation in this case.

\item
A relation of the form 
\[
\sum_{H\subseteq G}n_{H}\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{H}=0
\]
is called a \textit{Brauer relation}, where \(n_{H}\in \mathbb{Z}\). 
Artin's induction theorem (=\cref{artinn}) is an example of a Brauer relation.
Brauer relations in finite groups are studied and classified in \cite{BD15}.
Given a Brauer relation for \(G\), an argument similar to that in the proof of
\cref{main4} gives
\[
\prod_{H\subseteq G}\left(\frac{[G:H]\kappa(X_{H})}{f_{H}(Y/X)} \right)^{n_{H}}=1
\]
A related observation in the unramified setting appears in
\cite[Remark~4.13]{Miz26}.

\item
In \cite[Theorem~4.22]{Miz26}, we proved that no nontrivial monomial-type spanning tree formula exists for unramified \(G\)-covers when \(G\) is a cyclic group.
Thus, for cyclic groups, the triviality described in (3) and (4) reflects a nonexistence result already present in the unramified setting.
\end{enumerate}

\label{eg:elementary-abelian-artin}
Let \(G=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^m\), with \(m\geq2\), and let \(Y/X\) be a \(G\)-cover.
We show that (\(\varheartsuit\)) in \cref{eg:elementary-abelian-branched} also follows from \cref{main4}.
Let \(C_1,\ldots,C_{2^m-1}\) be the subgroups of \(G\) of order two, and let \(H_1,\ldots,H_{2^m-1}\) be its subgroups of index two.

Applying \cref{main4} to \(Y/X\) and taking the power \(2^{m-1}\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:el2-direct-original}
  \kappa(X)^{2^{m-1}}
  =
  \left(
    \frac{2^m\kappa(Y)}{f_{\{1_G\}}(Y/X)}
  \right)^{1-2^{m-1}}
  \prod_{i=1}^{2^m-1}
  \frac{2^{m-1}\kappa(X_{C_i})}{f_{C_i}(Y/X)}.
\end{equation}
Applying \cref{main4} to the \(H_j\)-cover \(Y/X_{H_j}\) and 
raising both sides to the power \(2^{m-2}\), we obtain
\[
\kappa(X_{H_j})^{2^{m-2}}
=
\left(
\frac{2^{m-1}\kappa(Y)}
{f_{\{1_G\}}(Y/X_{H_j})}
\right)^{1-2^{m-2}}
\prod_{\substack{C\subseteq H_j\\ |C|=2}}
\frac{2^{m-2}\kappa(X_C)}
{f_C(Y/X_{H_j})}.
\]
By the definition of the ramification factor, we have
\[
f_{\{1_G\}}(Y/X_{H_j})
=
\frac{f_{\{1_G\}}(Y/X)}{f_{H_j}(Y/X)}.
\]
Thus, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:el2-direct-hyperplane}
\left(
\frac{2\kappa(X_{H_j})}{f_{H_j}(Y/X)}
\right)^{2^{m-2}}
=
\left(
\frac{2^m\kappa(Y)}{f_{\{1_G\}}(Y/X)}
\right)^{1-2^{m-2}}
\prod_{\substack{C\subseteq H_j\\ |C|=2}}
\frac{2^{m-1}\kappa(X_C)}{f_C(Y/X)}.
\end{equation}

Each \(C_i\) is contained in \(2^{m-1}-1\) of the
subgroups \(H_j\), since the subgroups \(H_{j}\) containing \(C_{i}\) correspond to the
index-two subgroups of \(G/C_i\). 
Thus, multiplying \eqref{eq:el2-direct-hyperplane} over \(j=1,\ldots,2^m-1\),
we obtain
\[
\prod_{j=1}^{2^m-1}
\left(
\frac{2\kappa(X_{H_j})}{f_{H_j}(Y/X)}
\right)^{2^{m-2}}
=
\left(
\frac{2^m\kappa(Y)}{f_{\{1_G\}}(Y/X)}
\right)^{(2^m-1)(1-2^{m-2})}
\prod_{i=1}^{2^m-1} \left( \frac{2^{m-1}\kappa(X_{C_i})}{f_{C_i}(Y/X)} \right)^{2^{m-1}-1}.
\]
Substituting \eqref{eq:el2-direct-original} into this equation, we obtain
\[
  \left(
    \prod_{j=1}^{2^m-1}
    \frac{2\kappa(X_{H_j})}{f_{H_j}(Y/X)}
  \right)^{2^{m-2}}
  =
  \left(
    \frac{2^m\kappa(Y)}{f_{\{1_G\}}(Y/X)}
    \kappa(X)^{2^m-2}
  \right)^{2^{m-2}}.
\]
Hence,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:el2-direct-factor-form}
  \kappa(Y)
  =
  \frac{2^{\,2^m-m-1}}{\kappa(X)^{\,2^m-2}}
  \frac{f_{\{1_G\}}(Y/X)}
       {\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{2^m-1}f_{H_j}(Y/X)}
  \prod_{j=1}^{2^m-1}\kappa(X_{H_j}).
\end{equation}

For each \(v\in V_X\), write \(m_v(Y/X)=2^{r_v}\).
The ramification-factor calculation in
\cref{eg:elementary-abelian-branched} gives
\[
  \frac{f_{\{1_G\}}(Y/X)}
       {\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{2^m-1}f_{H_j}(Y/X)}
  =
  2^{\sum_{v\in V_X}
       \left((r_v+1)2^{m-r_v}-2^m\right)}.
\]
Substituting this into \eqref{eq:el2-direct-factor-form}, we obtain
\[
  \kappa(Y)
  =
  \frac{
    2^{2^m-m-1+
       \sum_{v\in V_X}\left((r_v+1)2^{m-r_v}-2^m\right)}
  }{\kappa(X)^{\,2^m-2}}
  \prod_{j=1}^{2^m-1}\kappa(X_{H_j}),
\]
which is \((\varheartsuit)\).
